A Martingale Kernel Independence Test
Felix Laumann, Zhaolu Liu, Mauricio Barahona

Abstract
The Hilbert–Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC) and its joint-independence extension d ​ HSIC d\mathrm{HSIC} are degenerate V V -statistics whose data-dependent weighted- χ 2 \chi^{2} null limits force a permutation calibration that multiplies the per-test cost by the number of permutations, in practice two orders of magnitude. Adapting the recent martingale MMD construction for two-sample testing to the (joint) independence problem, we introduce two studentised statistics whose null distributions are standard normal regardless of the data law, so that a single normal-quantile lookup replaces the permutation step entirely. The first, m ​ HSIC m\mathrm{HSIC} , is a self-normalised lower-triangular sum of the Hadamard product of two empirically centred Gram matrices. Under independence and bounded-fourth-moment kernels it converges to a standard normal. It is consistent against every fixed alternative, and runs at quadratic cost in the sample size without any sample split, matching the biased HSIC V V -statistic. The naive direct-product extension to d d variables fails because the empirical-centring bias compounds across the d d factors and breaks finite-sample calibration once the number of variables exceeds the square root of the sample size. Our second statistic, m ​ d ​ HSIC md\mathrm{HSIC} , repairs this with a single half-sample split: the centring is estimated on one half and the lower-triangular self-normalised martingale is run on the other, shrinking the conditional-mean residual to a quantity that is exponentially small in d d , so the statistic is asymptotically standard normal at every fixed number of jointly tested variables, with a per-test cost that grows only linearly in d d . On synthetic data with per-variable input dimension from 1 1 to 500 500 and between 2 2 and 10 10 jointly tested variables, both statistics match the empirical type-I error rate and test power of permutation-calibrated baselines while running 25 25 to 60 × 60\times faster.

Background

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Polish space and
k:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}

a positive-definite, bounded, characteristic kernel with associated
RKHS
\mathcal{H}_{k}
and feature map
\phi(x)=k(\cdot,x)

embedding of a probability measure

\mu_{P}=\mathbb{E}_{X\sim P}[\phi(X)]

\bar{k}(x,x^{\prime})\;=\;\langle\phi(x)-\mu_{P},\,\phi(x^{\prime})-\mu_{P}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}\;=\;k(x,x^{\prime})-\mu_{P}(x)-\mu_{P}(x^{\prime})+\langle\mu_{P},\mu_{P}\rangle,

(3)

with
\mu_{P}(x):=\mathbb{E}[k(X,x)]

\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of size
n

K\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
for the
Gram matrix with entries
K_{ij}:=k(X_{i},X_{j})
,
I_{n}
for the

n\times n
identity matrix,
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for the vector of all ones,
and
H:=I_{n}-\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
for the corresponding
empirical centring matrix. The empirically centred Gram matrix has
entries

(HKH)_{ij}=k(X_{i},X_{j})-\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{a}k(X_{a},X_{i})-\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{a}k(X_{a},X_{j})+\tfrac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{a,b}k(X_{a},X_{b})
.

Given paired observations
\{(X_{i},Y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
with bounded
characteristic kernels
k_{X},k_{Y}
, RKHSs

\mathcal{H}_{X}:=\mathcal{H}_{k_{X}}
,

\mathcal{H}_{Y}:=\mathcal{H}_{k_{Y}}

5
)

\mathrm{HSIC}(P_{XY})\;=\;\|C_{XY}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{HS}},\qquad C_{XY}\;:=\;\mathbb{E}\!\left[(\phi(X)-\mu_{X})\otimes(\psi(Y)-\mu_{Y})\right],

(4)

the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm of the cross-covariance operator

, with
\otimes
denoting

\mathrm{HSIC}(P_{XY})=0
if and only if
X\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\perp}Y
.
The biased empirical estimator is (
1
); under

-statistic. The

(
7
)

which equals zero iff

The martingale HSIC

4.1
Definition of the statistic

The starting point is that the HSIC
V
-statistic in (
1
)
can be read entrywise as a sum of products of two empirically centred
kernel evaluations,

\widehat{\mathrm{HSIC}}_{n}=\tfrac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j}(HK_{X}H)_{ij}\,(HK_{Y}H)_{ij}
.
At the population level, this is a
V
-statistic in the bivariate kernel

\Phi^{\mathrm{HSIC}}(z_{i},z_{j})\;:=\;\bar{k}_{X}(X_{i},X_{j})\,\bar{k}_{Y}(Y_{i},Y_{j}),\qquad z_{i}=(X_{i},Y_{i}),

(6)

with
\bar{k}_{X},\bar{k}_{Y}
the population-centred kernels
of (
3
). The next lemma is the central building block
of our definition of the martingale test statistic for independence.

Lemma 1

(Conditionally centred independence kernel)
.

Under
H_{0}:X\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\perp}Y
, for every
j\leq i-1
,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Phi^{\mathrm{HSIC}}(Z_{i},Z_{j})\,\big|\,\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\right]\;=\;0.

(7)

Proof.

For
j\leq i-1
,
Z_{j}
is part of
\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
while
Z_{i}
is
independent of
\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
. Therefore

\mathbb{E}[\Phi^{\mathrm{HSIC}}(Z_{i},Z_{j})\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]=\mathbb{E}_{Z_{i}}[\bar{k}_{X}(X_{i},X_{j})\,\bar{k}_{Y}(Y_{i},Y_{j})]
. Under
H_{0}
,
Z_{i}

has the product law
P_{X}\otimes P_{Y}
and the inner expectation
factorises as
\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}}[\bar{k}_{X}(X_{i},X_{j})]\cdot\mathbb{E}_{Y_{i}}[\bar{k}_{Y}(Y_{i},Y_{j})]
.
By the definition of the centred kernel, each factor is zero
(
\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}}[k_{X}(X_{i},X_{j})]=\mu_{X}(X_{j})
exactly cancels the
-\mu_{X}(X_{j})

term, and the remaining two centring constants cancel against each
other), so the product vanishes.
∎

In analogy with (
2
), define

\widehat{T}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC}}\;=\;\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=2}^{n}\frac{1}{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}(HK_{X}H)_{ij}\,(HK_{Y}H)_{ij},

(8)

i.e., the lower-triangular sum of the Hadamard product of the two
empirically centred Gram matrices.
Its self-normalised counterpart is

\eta_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC}}\;:=\;\frac{\widehat{T}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC}}}{\widehat{\sigma}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC}}},\quad\bigl(\widehat{\sigma}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC}}\bigr)^{2}\;=\;\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=2}^{n}\Bigl(\frac{1}{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}(HK_{X}H)_{ij}\,(HK_{Y}H)_{ij}\Bigr)^{2}.

(9)

The empirical centring breaks the strict martingale-difference property
of
\Phi^{\mathrm{HSIC}}
because
HKH
is computed from
all
of the
sample, not the past only.
The replacement is asymptotically negligible because
the gap
(HK_{X}H)_{ij}-\bar{k}_{X}(X_{i},X_{j})=O_{P}(1/\sqrt{n})
uniformly
in
(i,j)
by the
\sqrt{n}
-consistency of the empirical mean
embedding for bounded characteristic kernels

(
10
, Theorem 27)
, and the resulting bias in

\widehat{T}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC}}
is
O_{P}(1/n)
, contributing
o_{P}(1)
to the
studentised statistic.
We make this precise in Theorem
3

Null distribution and consistency

Assumption 2

(Bounded fourth moments)
.

The kernels

are bounded characteristic kernels and the

\mathbb{E}[\Phi^{\mathrm{HSIC}}(Z_{1},Z_{2})^{2}]\in(0,\infty)

Theorem 3

(Null distribution of
m\mathrm{HSIC}
)
.

Under Assumption
2
and
H_{0}:X\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\perp}Y
,

\eta_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC}}\;\xrightarrow{d}\;\mathcal{N}(0,1)\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty,

(10)

regardless of the joint distribution
P_{XY}
.

Proof sketch.

(i) Population centring.
Replace
(HK_{X}H)_{ij}
by
\bar{k}_{X}(X_{i},X_{j})
and likewise for
Y
, and call the resulting statistic

\eta_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC},\star}
. By Lemma

form a

\mathcal{F}_{i-1}

,
\Phi^{\mathrm{HSIC}}
is symmetric,
bounded and has finite fourth moment. The Berry–Esseen bounds for

3
, reproduced in
the proof of
2

, yield
\eta_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC},\star}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.

(ii) Empirical centring is asymptotically negligible.
Define

\Delta_{ij}^{X}:=(HK_{X}H)_{ij}-\bar{k}_{X}(X_{i},X_{j})
and likewise

\Delta_{ij}^{Y}
, and write
\hat{\mu}_{X}^{(n)}:=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{a}\phi(X_{a})\in\mathcal{H}_{X}

, contributing

o_{P}(1)

(iii) Slutsky.
The denominator

O(n^{2})
, and the studentisation at
O(n)
, for a total
per-test cost of
O(n^{2})
, which is identical to the biased HSIC

V
-statistic, but
without
the
O(B)
permutation
resampling step.

5
The martingale d-variable HSIC

5.1
Empirical-centring bias of the direct-product analogue

The natural
d
-variable analogue of
m\mathrm{HSIC}
would replace the
centred-kernel pair
\bar{k}_{X}\,\bar{k}_{Y}
by the
d
-fold centred-kernel
product

\Phi^{d\mathrm{HSIC},\,\star}(z_{i},z_{j})\;:=\;\prod_{k=1}^{d}\bar{k}_{k}(X_{i}^{k},X_{j}^{k}),\qquad z_{i}=(X_{i}^{1},\ldots,X_{i}^{d}),

(11)

with
\bar{k}_{k}
the
population
centred kernel of the
k
-th
marginal. Under joint independence, this bivariate kernel is
conditionally centred given the past for every
j\leq i-1
:

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Phi^{d\mathrm{HSIC},\,\star}(Z_{i},Z_{j})\,\big|\,\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\right]\;=\;\prod_{k}\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}^{k}}[\bar{k}_{k}(X_{i}^{k},X_{j}^{k})]\;=\;0,

(12)

because
(X_{i}^{1},\ldots,X_{i}^{d})\sim\prod_{k}P_{X^{k}}
under
H_{0}

factorises and each factor integrates to zero by definition of

\bar{k}_{k}
. Substituting this directly into the
m\mathrm{HSIC}
scheme of
Algorithm
1
yields a statistic with the right
population limit but
incorrect finite-sample calibration at

d\geq 3
: the population kernel
\bar{k}_{k}
is unknown and has to be
replaced by an empirically centred one,
(HK_{k}H)_{ij}
, and the
resulting per-variable bias
\Delta_{ij}^{k}=(HK_{k}H)_{ij}-\bar{k}_{k}(X_{i}^{k},X_{j}^{k})=O_{P}(1/\sqrt{n})
multiplies
across the
d
variables. The aggregate bias is
O_{P}(d/\sqrt{n})
, which
dominates the
\sqrt{n}
studentised scale once
d\gtrsim\sqrt{n}
,
breaking the standard-normal calibration of the direct-product
statistic in that regime. This formal obstruction motivates the
split-martingale construction below.

5.2
Definition of the split-martingale statistic

Our proposed solution is to estimate the centring from a disjoint subsample, in
the spirit of the cross-statistic
xd\mathrm{HSIC}
of
6
(and the
earlier two-sample
x\mathrm{HSIC}
of
8
), but to retain the
lower-triangular martingale pairing of
m\mathrm{HSIC}
on the remaining
subsample. Concretely, fix

m\;:=\;\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\qquad\mathcal{S}_{1}:=\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m}\},\qquad\mathcal{S}_{2}:=\{Z_{m+1},\ldots,Z_{n}\},

(13)

and for each kernel
k
let

\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\;:=\;\tfrac{1}{m}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\phi_{k}(X_{l}^{k}),\qquad\bar{k}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}(x,x^{\prime})\;:=\;\langle\phi_{k}(x)-\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}},\,\phi_{k}(x^{\prime})-\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}

(14)

be the empirical mean embedding and the associated
\mathcal{S}_{1}
-centred kernel. The bivariate kernel that we substitute into the
martingale construction is the centred-kernel product evaluated with
this
\mathcal{S}_{1}
-centring:

\Phi^{d\mathrm{HSIC}}(z_{i},z_{j})\;:=\;\prod_{k=1}^{d}\bar{k}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}(X_{i}^{k},X_{j}^{k}),\qquad i,j\in\{m+1,\ldots,n\}.

(15)

The studentised statistic is the
m\mathrm{HSIC}
lower-triangular
self-normalised martingale restricted to
\mathcal{S}_{2}
:

\widehat{T}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC}}\;:=\;\frac{1}{n-m}\sum_{i=m+2}^{n}\frac{1}{i-m}\sum_{j=m+1}^{i-1}\Phi^{d\mathrm{HSIC}}(Z_{i},Z_{j}),

(16)

\bigl(\widehat{\sigma}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC}}\bigr)^{2}\;:=\;\frac{1}{(n-m)^{2}}\sum_{i=m+2}^{n}\Bigl(\frac{1}{i-m}\sum_{j=m+1}^{i-1}\Phi^{d\mathrm{HSIC}}(Z_{i},Z_{j})\Bigr)^{2},

(17)

\eta_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC}}\;:=\;\widehat{T}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC}}\,/\,\widehat{\sigma}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC}}.

(18)

The asymptotic
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
calibration at every
d\geq 2

rests on the following lemma.

Lemma 4

(Conditional mean of the split-centred product kernel)
.

Assume that, when the kernels
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}
depend on tunable
hyperparameters (e.g. the bandwidth
\sigma_{k}
of a Gaussian or
Laplace kernel
k_{k}
), those hyperparameters

\{\sigma_{k}\}_{k=1}^{d}
are deterministic functions of
\mathcal{S}_{1}

alone, so that
\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
and

\bar{k}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
are determined by
\mathcal{F}_{m}=(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m})
. Under joint independence, for every

m+1\leq j<i
,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Phi^{d\mathrm{HSIC}}(Z_{i},Z_{j})\,\big|\,\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\right]\;=\;\prod_{k=1}^{d}\,\bigl\langle\mu_{k}-\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}},\,\phi_{k}(X_{j}^{k})-\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\bigr\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}\;=\;O_{P}(m^{-d/2}).

(19)

Proof.

Z_{j}
and
\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
are determined by
\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
,
and under joint independence
(X_{i}^{1},\ldots,X_{i}^{d})\sim\prod_{k}P_{X^{k}}
factorises. Evaluating one factor at a time:

\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}^{k}}\bigl[\bar{k}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}(X_{i}^{k},X_{j}^{k})\bigr]=\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}^{k}}\bigl[\langle\phi_{k}(X_{i}^{k})-\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}},\phi_{k}(X_{j}^{k})-\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\rangle\bigr]=\langle\mu_{k}-\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}},\phi_{k}(X_{j}^{k})-\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\rangle,

which multiplies over
k
to the displayed product. The

\sqrt{m}
-consistency of
\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
for bounded
characteristic kernels
(
10
, Theorem 27)
gives

\|\mu_{k}-\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}=O_{P}(1/\sqrt{m})

and boundedness of
k_{k}
gives

\|\phi_{k}(X_{j}^{k})-\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}=O_{P}(1)
,
so each factor is
O_{P}(1/\sqrt{m})
and the product is
O_{P}(m^{-d/2})
.
∎

Lemma
4
is the core of the split-martingale
construction. The per-variable empirical-centring bias is still

O_{P}(1/\sqrt{m})
, but because it enters the inner expectation

multiplicatively
across the
d
variables, the aggregate residual
is
O_{P}(m^{-d/2})
: exponentially small in
d
for any
m\geq 2

and negligible next to the studentised scale for any
d\geq 2
. Under
the direct-product construction of
Section
5.1
this same bias enters

additively
across the
d
variables (via a product expansion with
a
\sqrt{n}
-scale leading term) and grows to
O_{P}(d/\sqrt{n})
, which
is why the direct construction fails.

7
Discussion

We presented two permutation-free kernel statistics,
m\mathrm{HSIC}
and

md\mathrm{HSIC}
: studentised lower-triangular sums of conditionally
centred bivariate kernels that converge to
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
under
the relevant null regardless of the data law, at a per-test cost of

O(n^{2})
and
O(d\,n^{2})
respectively, matching the corresponding
biased
V
-statistics without the permutation factor

.
Consistency against any fixed alternative follows from a calculation
parallel to
2
.
md\mathrm{HSIC}
adopts
the half-sample split of

(

)
for centring
only, then runs the martingale over the second half.

Appendix A
Proof of asymptotic normality of the martingale statistic

We give the full argument behind Theorem
3
.

Step 1: Population-centring statistic is a self-normalised
martingale-difference sum.

With
\Phi^{\mathrm{HSIC}}
defined in (
6
), set

\widehat{T}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC},\star}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=2}^{n}\frac{1}{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\Phi^{\mathrm{HSIC}}(Z_{i},Z_{j}),\qquad\bigl(\widehat{\sigma}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC},\star}\bigr)^{2}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=2}^{n}\Bigl(\frac{1}{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\Phi^{\mathrm{HSIC}}(Z_{i},Z_{j})\Bigr)^{2}.

(21)

By Lemma
1
, under
H_{0}
the inner sum

\xi_{i}=\tfrac{1}{i}\sum_{j<i}\Phi^{\mathrm{HSIC}}(Z_{i},Z_{j})
is a martingale
difference w.r.t.
\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
. This is exactly the structure of

m\mathrm{MMD}
’s
T_{n}
in (
2
) with
\Phi^{m\mathrm{MMD}}
replaced by
\Phi^{\mathrm{HSIC}}
.
By Assumption
2
,
\Phi^{\mathrm{HSIC}}
is symmetric,
bounded, and has finite fourth moment. The Berry–Esseen bound for
self-normalised martingales of
3
(used in identical
form in the proof of
2
) yields

\widehat{T}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC},\star}/\widehat{\sigma}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC},\star}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.

Step 2: Empirical-centring bias.

Let
\hat{\mu}_{X}^{(n)}:=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{a}\phi(X_{a})\in\mathcal{H}_{X}

be the empirical mean embedding (cf. Section
4.2
)
and
e_{X}:=\hat{\mu}_{X}^{(n)}-\mu_{X}
the corresponding centring
error. By
10
for bounded
characteristic kernels,
\|e_{X}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{X}}=O_{P}(1/\sqrt{n})
, and
the same holds for
e_{Y}\in\mathcal{H}_{Y}
. Expanding

(HK_{X}H)_{ij}=\langle\phi(X_{i})-\hat{\mu}_{X}^{(n)},\,\phi(X_{j})-\hat{\mu}_{X}^{(n)}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{X}}
and

\bar{k}_{X}(X_{i},X_{j})=\langle\phi(X_{i})-\mu_{X},\,\phi(X_{j})-\mu_{X}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{X}}
,

\Delta_{ij}^{X}:=(HK_{X}H)_{ij}-\bar{k}_{X}(X_{i},X_{j})=-\langle\phi(X_{i})-\mu_{X},e_{X}\rangle-\langle\phi(X_{j})-\mu_{X},e_{X}\rangle+\|e_{X}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{X}}.

(22)

Writing
C_{X}:=\sup_{x}\sqrt{k_{X}(x,x)}
for the kernel-induced
feature-norm bound (finite by boundedness of
k_{X}
),
Cauchy–Schwarz gives
|\Delta_{ij}^{X}|\leq 2C_{X}\|e_{X}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{X}}+\|e_{X}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{X}}=O_{P}(1/\sqrt{n})
uniformly in
(i,j)
,
and likewise
|\Delta_{ij}^{Y}|=O_{P}(1/\sqrt{n})
.

The product expansion is

(HK_{X}H)_{ij}(HK_{Y}H)_{ij}=\bar{k}_{X}\bar{k}_{Y}+\bar{k}_{X}\Delta_{ij}^{Y}+\bar{k}_{Y}\Delta_{ij}^{X}+\Delta_{ij}^{X}\Delta_{ij}^{Y}.

(23)

The term
\Delta_{ij}^{X}\Delta_{ij}^{Y}=O_{P}(1/n)
contributes
O_{P}(1/n)

to
\widehat{T}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC}}-\widehat{T}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC},\star}
and

\sqrt{n}\cdot O_{P}(1/n)=o_{P}(1)
to the studentised statistic. The
two cross terms
\bar{k}_{X}\Delta^{Y}
and
\bar{k}_{Y}\Delta^{X}
are individually

O_{P}(1/\sqrt{n})
in absolute value but, when summed over the
lower-triangular indices and divided by
n
, telescope to

O_{P}(1/n)
contributions: applying the analogous expansion

\Delta_{ij}^{Y}=-\langle\phi(Y_{i})-\mu_{Y},e_{Y}\rangle-\langle\phi(Y_{j})-\mu_{Y},e_{Y}\rangle+\|e_{Y}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{Y}}
(with

e_{Y}:=\hat{\mu}_{Y}^{(n)}-\mu_{Y}
) and using

\mathbb{E}[\bar{k}_{X}(X_{i},X_{j})\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{i-1},X_{j}]=0
from
Lemma
1
, the conditional expectation of each cross
term given
\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
vanishes to leading order in
e_{Y}
, so
the cross-term contribution to the lower-triangular sum is itself
a martingale-difference sum whose magnitude is

O_{P}(1/n)
. Combining,

\widehat{T}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC}}-\widehat{T}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC},\star}=O_{P}(1/n)
.

Step 3: Variance is asymptotically equivalent.

The same expansion gives
(\widehat{\sigma}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC}})^{2}-(\widehat{\sigma}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC},\star})^{2}=O_{P}(1/n)
, because the cross
terms entering the variance estimator are squared versions of the cross
terms in the numerator and so are at most
O_{P}(1/n)
.

Step 4: Slutsky.

Combining Steps 1–3,

\eta_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC}}=\frac{\widehat{T}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC},\star}+O_{P}(1/n)}{\widehat{\sigma}_{n}^{m\mathrm{HSIC},\star}\bigl(1+o_{P}(1)\bigr)}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,1)

(24)

by Slutsky’s theorem. The result follows.

Why the lower-triangular structure.

The lower-triangular pairing makes the inner sum

\xi_{i}=\tfrac{1}{i}\sum_{j<i}\Phi^{\mathrm{HSIC}}(Z_{i},Z_{j})
a martingale
difference: by Lemma
1
,
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{i}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]=\tfrac{1}{i}\sum_{j<i}\mathbb{E}[\Phi^{\mathrm{HSIC}}(Z_{i},Z_{j})\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]=0
. Had we summed over all
n^{2}
Hadamard entries (as in the standard
biased
V
-statistic of HSIC), the contributions sharing the index
i

would be doubly counted across diagonals
(i,j)
and
(j,i)
, the
diagonal entries
\Phi^{\mathrm{HSIC}}(Z_{i},Z_{i})\neq 0
would inflate the
variance, and the standard non-degenerate
U
-statistic CLT under
H_{0}

would be inapplicable due to the degeneracy of the kernel
\Phi^{\mathrm{HSIC}}

under the null. The lower-triangular restriction selects the
n(n-1)/2

pairs that become a martingale-difference sum, and the per-row
normalisation
1/i
restores the
O(n^{2})
asymptotic scale.

Appendix B
Proof of asymptotic normality of the split-martingale d-variable statistic

We give the full argument behind Theorem
6
. Let

m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor
,
n_{2}=n-m
,

\mathcal{S}_{1}=\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m}\}
,

\mathcal{S}_{2}=\{Z_{m+1},\ldots,Z_{n}\}
, and (continuing the
shorthand of Section
3
)
\mathcal{F}_{i}:=(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{i})
. Write

\xi_{i}\;:=\;\frac{1}{i-m}\sum_{j=m+1}^{i-1}\Phi^{d\mathrm{HSIC}}(Z_{i},Z_{j}),\qquad i=m+2,\ldots,n,

(25)

with
\Phi^{d\mathrm{HSIC}}
defined in (
15
), so

\widehat{T}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC}}=(n_{2})^{-1}\sum_{i=m+2}^{n}\xi_{i}
and

(\widehat{\sigma}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC}})^{2}=(n_{2})^{-2}\sum_{i=m+2}^{n}\xi_{i}^{2}
.

Step 1: Approximate martingale-difference decomposition.

Write
\xi_{i}=\xi_{i}^{\star}+r_{i}
where

\xi_{i}^{\star}\;:=\;\xi_{i}-\mathbb{E}[\xi_{i}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{i-1}],\qquad r_{i}\;:=\;\mathbb{E}[\xi_{i}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{i-1}].

(26)

By construction
(\xi_{i}^{\star})_{i=m+2}^{n}
is a martingale
difference sequence adapted to
(\mathcal{F}_{i})
; by
Lemma
4
,

|r_{i}|\;\leq\;\prod_{k=1}^{d}\|\mu_{k}-\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}\cdot\|\phi_{k}(X_{j}^{k})-\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}\;=\;O_{P}(m^{-d/2}),

(27)

uniformly in
i>m
, where the
\|\mu_{k}-\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}=O_{P}(1/\sqrt{m})
bound is

10
and the other factor is
O_{P}(1)
by
boundedness of
k_{k}
. Summing,

\widehat{T}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC}}\;=\;\frac{1}{n_{2}}\sum_{i=m+2}^{n}\xi_{i}^{\star}+\frac{1}{n_{2}}\sum_{i=m+2}^{n}r_{i}\;=\;\widehat{T}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC},\star}+O_{P}(m^{-d/2}).

(28)

For every fixed
d\geq 2
and
m\geq n/2
,
O_{P}(m^{-d/2})=o_{P}(n^{-1/2})
,
so the residual is negligible after studentisation.

Step 2: Self-normalised martingale CLT for the leading term.

By Step 1,
\widehat{T}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC},\star}=(n_{2})^{-1}\sum_{i}\xi_{i}^{\star}

is a self-normalised sum of martingale differences. Under
Assumption
5
,
\Phi^{d\mathrm{HSIC}}
is symmetric,
bounded, and has finite fourth moment conditional on
\mathcal{F}_{m}

(boundedness and finite fourth moment of each
\bar{k}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
transport to the product by Hölder, since the
L^{p}
-norm of a
product of bounded zero-mean random variables is bounded by the
product of the
L^{p}
-norms). The same Berry–Esseen bound for
self-normalised martingales of
3
used in the proof
of Theorem
3
applies conditionally on
\mathcal{F}_{m}
, yielding

\widehat{T}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC},\star}\,/\,\widehat{\sigma}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC},\star}\;\xrightarrow{d}\;\mathcal{N}(0,1)\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty,

(29)

where
(\widehat{\sigma}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC},\star})^{2}=(n_{2})^{-2}\sum_{i}(\xi_{i}^{\star})^{2}
.

Step 3: Variance is asymptotically equivalent.

Expanding
\xi_{i}^{2}=(\xi_{i}^{\star}+r_{i})^{2}=(\xi_{i}^{\star})^{2}+2\xi_{i}^{\star}r_{i}+r_{i}^{2}
and summing, the cross-term is a
martingale-difference sum of magnitude
O_{P}(1/\sqrt{n_{2}}\cdot m^{-d/2})=o_{P}(n^{-1})
and the
r_{i}^{2}
term is
O_{P}(m^{-d})
. Both are
dominated by
(\widehat{\sigma}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC},\star})^{2}
. Hence

(\widehat{\sigma}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC}})^{2}=(\widehat{\sigma}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC},\star})^{2}(1+o_{P}(1))
.

Step 4: Slutsky.

Combining Steps 1–3,
\eta_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC}}=(\widehat{T}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC},\star}+o_{P}(n^{-1/2}))/(\widehat{\sigma}_{n}^{md\mathrm{HSIC},\star}(1+o_{P}(1)))\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,1)
by Slutsky’s theorem.

Necessity of the half-sample split.

Without the half-sample split, the centring would be the full-sample
empirical mean embedding
\hat{\mu}_{k}^{(n)}:=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{a=1}^{n}\phi_{k}(X_{a}^{k})\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
, which is not
determined by
\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
. The product expansion then produces
d

cross-terms of the form
\bar{k}_{1}\cdots\bar{k}_{k-1}\,\Delta_{ij}^{k}\,\bar{k}_{k+1}\cdots\bar{k}_{d}
with

\Delta_{ij}^{k}:=(HK_{k}H)_{ij}-\bar{k}_{k}(X_{i}^{k},X_{j}^{k})=O_{P}(1/\sqrt{n})
. When summed over the lower-triangular index set and divided by

n
, each cross term individually contributes
O_{P}(1/n)
(by the same
conditional-mean-zero argument as in
Appendix
A
), but the aggregate over all
d
such
cross terms is
O_{P}(d/n)
, which after
\sqrt{n}
-scaling yields

O_{P}(d/\sqrt{n})
, dominating the studentised scale for
d\gtrsim\sqrt{n}
. The sample split replaces the full-sample centring by the
first-half empirical mean
\hat{\mu}_{k}^{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
, which is
determined by
\mathcal{F}_{m}
and reduces the conditional-mean
residual to the product of
d
inner products in (
27
),
hence to
O_{P}(m^{-d/2})
, which is exponentially small in
d
.
