Six- α \alpha cluster Bose-Einstein condensation and supersolid 12 C( OPEN 0 2 + ) 0_{2}^{+}) + 12 C( OPEN 0 2 + ) 0_{2}^{+}) molecular structure in 24 Mg
S. Ohkubo, J. Takahashi, Y. Yamanaka

Abstract
We show for the first time that the low-spin ( J ≤ 4 + J\leq 4^{+} ) six- α \alpha condensate candidate states in 24 Mg, recently reported by Fujikawa et al. [Phys. Lett. B 848, 138384 (2024)], are well described by the superfluid α \alpha -cluster model (SCM). This is achieved by a rigorous treatment of the Nambu-Goldstone (NG) zero mode as the order parameter of condensation in the finite six- α \alpha system. We find that a roton rotational band with a large moment of inertia is built on the first excited NG 0 + 0^{+} state, analogous to the roton bands observed in three-, four-, and five- α \alpha condensates in 12 C, 16 O, and 20 Ne, respectively. Remarkably, our calculated roton band reproduces the well-known molecular resonance with a 12 C( 0 2 + 0_{2}^{+} )+ 12 C( 0 2 + 0_{2}^{+} ) structure ( 16 + 16^{+} ) observed at E c . m . = 32.5 E_{\rm c.m.}=32.5 MeV in inelastic 12 C+ 12 C scattering. This result provides a unified description of both the low-spin six- α \alpha condensate states and the high-spin 12 C( 0 2 + 0_{2}^{+} )+ 12 C( 0 2 + 0_{2}^{+} ) molecular resonance. Analysis of the wave functions reveals a large overlap between the SCM states and a geometrical 12 C( 0 2 + 0_{2}^{+} )+ 12 C( 0 2 + 0_{2}^{+} ) configuration. This dual nature —the coexistence of superfluidity and crystallinity— identifies these states as a signature of a supersolid.

The traditional
\alpha

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\int d{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}})\left(-\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2m}+V_{\mathrm{ex}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}})-\mu\right)\hat{\psi}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}})

\displaystyle\,\,+\frac{1}{2}\int d{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}}d{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}^{\prime}}}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}})\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}}^{\prime})U(|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}}^{\prime}|)\hat{\psi}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}}^{\prime})\hat{\psi}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bf r}})\,.

(1)

Here, where

\alpha

is introduced phenomenologically

and the
\alpha

U(|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|)=V_{r}e^{-\mu_{r}^{2}|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{2}}-V_{a}e^{-\mu_{a}^{2}|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{2}}\,.

We set

throughout this paper.

When BEC of
\alpha
clusters occurs, i.e.
the global phase symmetry of
\hat{\psi}
is spontaneously broken, we decompose
\hat{\psi}

as
\hat{\psi}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}})=\xi({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}})+\hat{\varphi}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}})

\xi(r)=\bra{0}\hat{\psi}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}})\ket{0}

To obtain the excitation spectrum, we need to solve
three coupled equations, which are the Gross–Pitaevskii (GP) equation, Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) equations, and Nambu-Goldstone zero-mode equation

The GP equation determines the order parameter

\left\{-\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2m}+V_{\mathrm{ex}}(r)-\mu+V_{H}(r)\right\}\xi(r)=0\,,

V_{H}(r)=\int d{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}^{\prime}}}U(|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}^{\prime}}}|)\xi^{2}(r^{\prime})
.

\xi
is normalized

N_{0}

\displaystyle\int d{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}}|\xi(r)|^{2}=N_{0}\,.

(3)

The superfluidity density is given by
\rho(r)
=
|\xi(r)|^{2}/N_{0}
.
The BdG equations describe the collective motions on the condensate
by

\displaystyle\int d{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}^{\prime}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{L}&\mathcal{M}\\
-\mathcal{M}^{*}&-\mathcal{L}^{*}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{\bm{n}}\\
v_{\bm{n}}\end{array}\right)=\omega_{\bm{n}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{\bm{n}}\\

\mathcal{M}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}^{\prime}}})=U(|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}^{\prime}}}|)\xi(r)\xi(r^{\prime}),\,\mathcal{L}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}^{\prime}}})=\delta{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}^{\prime}}})\left\{-\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2m}+V_{\mathrm{ex}}(r)-\mu+V_{H}(r)\right\}+\mathcal{M}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}^{\prime}}})\,.

\displaystyle u_{\bm{n}}(\bm{r})=\mathcal{U}_{n\ell}(r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi),\,

\displaystyle v_{\bm{n}}(\bm{r})=\mathcal{V}_{n\ell}(r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi).

(10)

(\xi(r),-\xi(r))^{t}

\hat{\varphi}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}})=-i{\hat{Q}}\xi(r)+{\hat{P}}\eta(r)+\sum_{\bm{n}}\left\{{\hat{a}}u_{\bm{n}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}})+{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}v^{\ast}_{\bm{n}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bm{r}}})\right\}\,,

[{\hat{a}}_{\bm{n}}\,,\,{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}_{\bm{n}^{\prime}}]=\delta_{\bm{n}\bm{n}^{\prime}}

{\hat{Q}}

{\hat{P}}

and
{\hat{P}}

The coefficients in

I=\frac{\partial\mu}{\partial N_{0}}

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{u}^{QP}\ket{\Psi_\nu}=E_{\nu}\ket{\Psi_\nu}\qquad(\nu=0,1,\cdots)\,.

We discuss why a deformed rotational band,

, emerges as the BdG excitation of a superfluid vacuum. Among the BdG excited states with
J^{\pi}
, states with even parity are lower than those with odd parity because the
\alpha

Among the many even-parity BdG states, those that belong to rotational excitations have the lowest energy, which approaches zero for extremely large systems due to the NG mode caused by the SSB of rotational invariance. Thus, the lowest states with
finite
angular momentum (
J^{\pi}\neq 0
) belong to a rotational band with a deformation. As the deformation becomes larger, the excitation energy becomes smaller. This explains why the BdG states with angular momentum forms a deformed rotational band.

On the other hand, from the geometrical picture, it has been systematically shown by Freer and his collaborators
Freer1995A
;
Freer1997
that even though the deformation costs energy, molecular and cluster structures are favored from the shell model point of view. They demonstrated this by exploring the symmetries of the deformed harmonic oscillator (DHO) for both prolate and oblate deformations, visually demonstrating how the degeneracies of the DHO lead to the spatial nucleon densities expected for
\alpha
-cluster structures
Freer2007
;
Freer2020

6
Summary

We have
demonstrated,
for the first time, that the recently observed candidate states for a six-
\alpha
condensate with low spins (
J\leq 4^{+}
) in
24
Mg
are
well described by the superfluid
\alpha
-cluster model. This model rigorously treats the Nambu-Goldstone (NG) zero mode as the order parameter of condensation for the finite six-
\alpha
system. It is found that a roton rotational band with a large moment of inertia is built on the first excited NG
0^{+}
state,
analogous
to the roton bands observed in three-, four-, and five-
\alpha
condensates in
12
C,
16
O, and
20
Ne, respectively. The calculated roton band successfully reproduces the
well-known
molecular resonance with a
12
C(
0_{2}^{+}
)+
12
C(
0_{2}^{+}
) structure at
E_{\rm c.m.}=32.5
MeV, characterized by a spin of
16^{+}
, as observed in inelastic
12
C+
12
C scattering.

Consequently,
the low-spin six-
\alpha
condensate states located slightly above the vacuum and the
12
C(
0_{2}^{+}
)+
12
C(
0_{2}^{+}
) molecular resonances are now understood within
a single, unified framework
.

Furthermore, the roton band is found to exhibit a duality of superfluidity and crystallinity, being dominantly composed of a two-cluster Hoyle-like
12
C(
0_{2}^{+}
)+
12
C(
0_{2}^{+}
) structure. This coexistence of properties is a definitive signature of a supersolid.
These results suggest that the duality of crystallinity and superfluidity may be a common feature across multi-
\alpha
systems, encouraging further studies on supersolidity in both light and heavy nuclei.
The present discovery suggests a new link between nuclear cluster physics and the universal study of quantum liquids, such as ultracold atomic systems, opening future prospects for exploring supersolidity across different energy scales.
