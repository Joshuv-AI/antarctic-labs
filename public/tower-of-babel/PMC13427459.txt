Increased risk of cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma in patients with hidradenitis suppurativa or psoriasis: A multicenter retrospective cohort study
Authors: Chi-Ya Yang, Matthew J. Yan, Chase Yonamine, Shih-Yu Yang, Kevin Sheng-Kai Ma, Steven T. Chen, Madeline E. DeWane
Published: 2026

To the Editor: Hidradenitis suppurativa (HS) is a chronic inflammatory skin disorder associated with cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (cSCC).1,2 Although burn scars are well-established risk factors for cSCC, real-world data comparing risk among patients with HS, psoriasis, and burns remain limited. We aimed to assess the risk and anatomical distribution of cSCC among patients with HS compared with those with psoriasis and burns.3

We conducted a retrospective cohort study using the TriNetX Global Collaborative Network (January 2005-May 2025), including adults aged ≥18 years without prior cSCC. Three cohorts (HS, psoriasis, burns) were identified and 1:1 propensity score matched on age, sex, race, body mass index, tobacco use, diabetes mellitus, and immunosuppressant use. After matching, we have 148,185 patients in each of the HS versus psoriasis cohorts, 178,439 patients in each of the HS and burns cohort, and 440,377 patients in each of the burns versus psoriasis cohorts. The primary outcome was incident cSCC occurring ≥3 months after index diagnosis (Supplementary Table I and Fig 1, available via Mendeley at https://data.mendeley.com/preview/zzp8m2sr33?a=7dd82476-4ae8-451e-8572-e330529eecb1).

Patients with HS demonstrated elevated risk of cSCC compared with burns (Hazard Ratio [HR] 1.41; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.20-1.66) and psoriasis (HR 1.16; 95% CI, 1.02-1.32). Psoriasis patients independently carried an elevated risk of cSCC relative to burns (HR 1.43; 95% CI, 1.36-1.50) (Table I, Fig 1). Subgroup analysis stratified by anatomical site demonstrated that HS was associated with elevated cSCC risk across all body sites. Compared with burns, HS conferred the greatest regional risk in the lower limbs (HR 1.65; 95% CI, 1.21-2.25) and trunk (HR 1.64; 95% CI, 1.25-2.16). Compared with psoriasis, HS demonstrated particularly increased risk in the anal (HR 1.99; 95% CI, 1.51-2.63) and trunk regions (HR 1.79; 95% CI, 1.35-2.37). Psoriasis was also associated with increased regional risk relative to burns, most notably in the lower limbs (HR 1.81; 95% CI, 1.61-2.03) and upper limbs (HR 1.62; 95% CI, 1.48-1.77) (Table I, Fig 1). The increased risk of cSCC in HS may reflect sustained inflammation, repeated tissue injury, and scarring in mechanically stressed regions.3 While burn scars are established risk factors for skin malignancy, these findings suggest that chronic inflammatory dermatoses may confer comparable or greater risk. Psoriasis-associated risk may similarly reflect long-standing inflammation, phototherapy exposure, or immunomodulatory treatment.3,4 The elevated risk of cSCC in HS patients with recurrent lesions and scarring highlights the importance of cSCC surveillance, particularly in high-risk anatomical regions.4,5 This study is limited by potential miscoding and lack of granular data on disease severity. Nonetheless, the large, matched cohorts and anatomical stratification strengthen the validity of these findings. Ultimately, these findings highlight the importance of clinical vigilance for cSCC development, particularly in high-risk anatomical regions and in poorly controlled disease. Further mechanistic studies are needed to elucidate inflammation-driven carcinogenesis and inform future risk-stratified screening strategies.
