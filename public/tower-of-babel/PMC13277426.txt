Young Man With Sudden Quadriplegia After Neck Massage
Authors: Xiujiao Wei, Qinghong Duan
Published: 2026

Case Presentation

A previously healthy 33-year-old man presented to the emergency department with acute quadriplegia. He reported a 7-day history of neck pain that intensified after 3 days of neck massage. Neurologic examination revealed flaccid paralysis of the lower extremities (0/5), graded weakness in the upper extremities (2-4/5), and complete sensory loss below the T4 dermatome. Diagnosis: Spontaneous Cervical Spinal Epidural Hematoma

Emergency magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was diagnostic, demonstrating a posterior epidural hematoma (Fig 2, star) spanning C5 to T1 with significant spinal cord compression and edema (Fig 2, arrows). The patient underwent urgent surgical decompression.Figure 2Emergency spinal magnetic resonance imaging scan, T2-weighted (sagittal). This definitive study reveals a posterior epidural hematoma (star) extending from C5 to T1, causing compression and edema of the spinal cord (arrows).

Spontaneous spinal epidural hematoma is a rare neurologic emergency where timely diagnosis is critical to prevent permanent deficit.1 First, for progressive myelopathy, a normal CT—especially in the artifact-prone cervicothoracic region—does not rule out compressive pathology. MRI is the definitive study and should be obtained urgently when clinical suspicion is high.2 Second, although rare, neck massage can be a potential trigger for spontaneous cervical spinal epidural hematoma (SSEH),3 emphasizing the need for caution in patients with acute or worsening neck pain. SSEH is a neurosurgical emergency. Upon confirmation of the diagnosis, urgent surgical decompression is required to optimize the potential for neurologic recovery.
