A Continuous-Time and State-Space Relaxation of the Linear Threshold Model with Nonlinear Opinion Dynamics
Ian Xul Belaustegui, Himani Sinhmar, Ling-Wei Kong, Andrew Michael Hein, Naomi Ehrich Leonard

Abstract
The Linear Threshold Model (LTM) is widely used to study the propagation of collective behaviors as complex contagions. However, its dependence on discrete states and timesteps restricts its ability to capture the multiple time-scales inherent in decision-making, as well as the effects of subthreshold signaling. To address these limitations, we introduce a continuous-time and state-space relaxation of the LTM based on the Nonlinear Opinion Dynamics (NOD) framework. By replacing the discontinuous step-function thresholds of the LTM with the smooth bifurcations of the NOD model, we map discrete cascade processes to the continuous flow of a dynamical system. We prove that, under appropriate parameter choices, activation in the discrete LTM guarantees activation in the continuous NOD relaxation for any given seed set. We establish computable conditions for equivalence: by sufficiently bounding the social coupling parameter, the continuous NOD cascades exactly recover the cascades of the discrete LTM. We then illustrate how this NOD relaxation provides a richer analytical framework than the LTM, allowing for the exploration of cascades driven by strictly subthreshold inputs and the role of temporally distributed signals.



I
Introduction

The propagation of collective behaviors, from the adoption of social norms
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, to coordinated escape responses in animal groups
[
4
,
5
]
, is frequently formalized as a complex contagion
[
3
]
and analyzed using the Linear Threshold Model (LTM)
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
. Although they are foundational, standard discrete-state threshold models possess fundamental limitations when applied to real-world biological and engineered systems. These models can explain many aspects of behavioral spread, but they cannot be used to explore questions of how cascades emerge in the first place. Nodes in a fully inactive system cannot transition to an active state, excluding the possibility of spontaneous false alarms that are often observed in nature. Furthermore, their reliance on discrete timesteps abstracts away the intrinsically continuous timescales of individual decision-making processes
[
9
]
, preventing the study of how internal cognitive dynamics interact with external stimuli over time.

To address these limitations, we introduce a continuous-time and state-space framework based on Nonlinear Opinion Dynamics (NOD)
[
10
,
11
]
. By considering the cascade process through the lens of nonlinear dynamical systems, we provide a mathematically rigorous bridge between discrete contagion models and continuous networked dynamics. We then show how the NOD framework, unlike the LTM, provides the means to explore emergence of cascades from subthreshold inputs and the role of temporally distributed inputs.

Previous approaches have introduced continuous relaxations of the LTM across different disciplines. Notably, Continuous-Time Linear Threshold Models (CT-LTM) introduce continuous random waiting times for activation, yet the state space itself remains strictly binary
[
12
,
13
]
. While allowing for richer temporal structures of cascades, this approach does not capture the continuous, dynamical evolution of agent opinions. Most closely related to our approach, the Continuous Threshold Model (CTM) replaces the discrete step function of the LTM with a smooth sigmoidal activation function, yielding a smooth flow
[
14
]
. In the CTM, a positive sign of the continuous state corresponds to activation, and the exact LTM equivalence is recovered only in the limit as the slope of the sigmoid approaches infinity, forcing the continuous function back into a strict step-function. This is fundamentally different from our approach, where activation corresponds to an agent’s opinion converging to one, and the equivalence of cascades with LTM is exactly recovered for a computable range of parameter values.

Our main contributions are as follows:

(i)

We establish a formal procedure for producing a Nonlinear Opinion Dynamics (NOD) relaxation of any given Linear Threshold Model (LTM). By replacing the discontinuous step-function thresholds of the LTM with the smooth bifurcations of the NOD model, we map the discrete cascade process to the continuous flow of a dynamical system.

(ii)

We prove that, under appropriate parameter choices, activation in the discrete LTM guarantees activation in the continuous NOD relaxation for any given seed set.

(iii)

We prove asymptotic equivalence between the two models: by sufficiently bounding the social coupling parameter, the continuous NOD dynamics exactly recover the discrete cascade of the LTM for any given seed set.

(iv)

We show how the NOD relaxation expands on the LTM and, unlike the LTM, can be used to explore important facets of cascade processes: 1) the possibility of cascades from only subthreshold inputs and 2) the role of temporal separation of distributed inputs.

III
The Linear Threshold Model

We consider the deterministic (persistent) LTM as a discrete-time dynamical system defined in the following way
[
6
,
2
]
. Consider a directed graph
G=(V,E)
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
nodes, and non-negative edge weights given by a weighted adjacency matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq}^{n\times n}
, with zero diagonal entries. We assume for simplicity that
V=[n]
. We also define a threshold vector
\boldsymbol{\tau}=\begin{bmatrix}\tau_{1}&\dots&\tau_{n}\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in\mathbb{R}_{>}^{n}
, where each node has an associated threshold
\tau_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{>}
. We denote the state of the network at time
t\in\mathbb{N}
as
\boldsymbol{\zeta}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}\zeta_{1}(t)&\dots&\zeta_{n}(t)\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
. Each node
i\in[n]
has a binary state
\zeta_{i}(t)\in\{0,1\}
, and we say that node
i
is active at time
t
if
\zeta_{i}(t)=1
, and inactive if
\zeta_{i}(t)=0
. For each node, the time evolution of its state is determined by

\zeta_{i}(t+1)=\zeta_{i}(t)\,\vee\,\mathbb{I}\left(((A\boldsymbol{\zeta}(t))_{i}>\tau_{i})\right).

(1)

That is, an agent will become active in the next timestep if and only if its state is already active, or the weighted sum of its active neighbors exceeds its threshold. Note that we adopt a strict inequality for the activation threshold, a slight variation from standard LTM formulations, which simplifies the correspondence with the relaxation later on. Notice also that the dynamics of the LTM are completely determined by the weighted adjacency matrix and the threshold vector, so we will refer to a tuple
(A,\boldsymbol{\tau})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{R}_{>}^{n}
where the diagonals of
A
are zero, as an
instance
of the LTM.

A fundamental problem in this setting is characterizing the terminal state
\boldsymbol{\zeta}(\infty)
as a function of the initial condition
\boldsymbol{\zeta}(0)

[
6
,
7
]
. That is, given a seed set of nodes
\mathcal{I}\subseteq V
, we set the initial states to be

\zeta_{i}(0)=\mathbb{I}(i\in\mathcal{I})

and define the cascade (or spreading, or contagion) size as
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbf{1}^{T}\zeta(t)
. A full (or large) cascade (i.e.,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbf{1}^{T}\boldsymbol{\zeta}(t)\approx n
) is often of particular interest
[
2
]
. Notice that, since the state space is finite and the dynamics are monotone, it follows that the dynamics reach a steady state in at most
n
steps. Thus, the cascade size is given by
\mathbf{1}^{T}\boldsymbol{\zeta}(n)
.

Definition 1

Given a LTM instance
(A,\boldsymbol{\tau})
, and a seed set
\mathcal{I}\subseteq[n]
, we define the LTM cascade set

C_{LTM}(\mathcal{I}):=\{i\in[n]\mid\lim_{t\to\infty}\zeta_{i}(t)=1\},

(2)

where state
\boldsymbol{\zeta}
evolves according to
1
with initial condition
\zeta_{i}(0)=\mathbb{I}(i\in\mathcal{I})
.

A particular subclass of the LTM model is the fractional contagion model (FCM)
[
2
]
. In this case, given a fixed threshold
\tau\in(0,1]
, the individual thresholds for the LTM are chosen such that
\tau_{i}=\tau\,(A\mathbf{1})_{i}
. Given this, the FCM implements a decision rule where each node becomes active if and only if it was previously active, or if the fraction of social input that it observes is greater than
\tau
. Decision rules that appear to be well approximated by FCM have been observed in empirical studies of human and animal social systems (e.g.,
[
4
,
15
]
but see
[
5
]
).

For simplicity, throughout the following discussion we assume that the information graph
G
of the LTM is strongly connected, meaning that there is a path from any node to any other. This is equivalent to the weighted adjacency matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq}^{n}
being irreducible. This condition allows us to assume that
A
has a simple real dominant eigenvalue
\lambda_{\max}>0
.

IV
The NOD Framework for Continuous Cascades

IV-A

Model Definition and Parameter Space

We define an opinion dynamics model based on the nonlinear opinion dynamics (NOD) model
[
10
]
in the following way. Consider
n
agents, and a vector of opinion states
\boldsymbol{z}\in[0,1]^{n}
. The opinions have continuous time dynamics

\dot{\boldsymbol{z}}=-\boldsymbol{z}+\textbf{sat}(\boldsymbol{\mu}\odot\boldsymbol{z}+k(\boldsymbol{z}\odot\boldsymbol{z})+\gamma A\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{b}),

(3)

where
\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq}^{n}
is a vector of heterogeneous gain on linear self-reinforcement,
k\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq}
is a gain on nonlinear self-reinforcement,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq}
is a rescaling of social interactions,
A
is the matrix of social information weights of the LTM, and
\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq}^{n}
is a vector of inputs or biases. We denote a tuple
(A,\boldsymbol{\mu},k,\gamma,\boldsymbol{b})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{R}_{>}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq}^{n}
to be an instance of the NOD model. Notice that, in contrast with previous work, our NOD definition is not smooth everywhere, but is piecewise-smooth and continuous. However, the bifurcation we describe below always occur away from the switching manifold, and in a region where the dynamics are locally smooth, so we still describe the NOD as smooth. Additionally, the saturation function can be approximated arbitrarily by smooth functions for which all the results in
[
10
,
11
]
apply directly.

As we will show in Section
V
, the condition for
3
(with input
\boldsymbol{b}=\boldsymbol{0}
) to correspond to the LTM is the following:

\displaystyle\tau_{i}=\frac{(1-\mu_{i})^{2}}{4\gamma k},

\displaystyle i\in[n].

(4)

This condition implies that the relaxation of LTM to NOD is not unique, since there is some freedom in choosing
\gamma
and
k
. We only consider heterogeneity in the linear gain
\mu_{i}
across agents, and so we can rewrite the condition
4

\mu_{i}=1-2\sqrt{\gamma k\tau_{i}}
. We require
\forall i\in[n],\mu_{i}>0
, which restricts the set of valid parameters to those satisfying
\forall i\in[n],\gamma k\tau_{i}<1/4
. As we will see, in order for the resulting NOD model to reasonably agree with the LTM, in the sense that the origin is stable and the active agents stay active, there are further restrictions that must be made on the parameters.

IV-B

Dynamical Properties of the NOD Model

In this section we discuss essential properties of the dynamics in
3
, and characterize the threshold of each agent in terms of the model parameters.

We have defined the opinions in
3
to be in the unit hypercube
S:=[0,1]^{n}
. Indeed, the set
S
is invariant for the dynamics. To see this, simply notice that for
i\in[n]
, if
z_{i}=1
then
\textbf{sat}(x)\leq 1
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
, implies
\dot{z}_{i}\leq 0
; and if
z_{i}=0
then
\dot{z}_{i}\geq 0
, since all terms inside saturation function are non-negative. Thus, the dynamics are well defined in
S
.

We make two important remarks on the opinion dynamics.

Remark 1

The neutral equilibrium
\boldsymbol{z}=\boldsymbol{0}
of the no-input system is asymptotically stable if
\lVert\boldsymbol{\mu}\rVert_{\infty}<1-\gamma\lambda_{\max}
.

Remark 2

For almost every initial state
\boldsymbol{z}\in S
, there exists a unique point
\bar{\boldsymbol{z}}\in S
, such that
\boldsymbol{z}(t)\to_{t\to\infty}\bar{\boldsymbol{z}}
.

Remark
1
follows immediately from ensuring the Jacobian of
3
at the origin,
J=-I+\text{diag}(\boldsymbol{\mu})+\gamma A
, is Hurwitz. This sufficient condition is quite conservative, but a tight characterization of the condition on
\boldsymbol{\mu}
for stability of the neutral equilibrium can be given (using the theory of Metzler matrices). However, for our purposes this is enough. This is consistent with the wider work on the indecision-breaking bifurcation in the NOD model
[
10
,
11
]
. Remark
2
is a consequence of the system being a monotone cooperative dynamical system
[
16
]
, with some minor consideration for the fact that
sat
is continuous but not everywhere differentiable. This tells us that we need not worry about complex behavior such as limit cycles or complex attractors, as almost every initial state will be in the basin of attraction of a stable equilibrium. We say that an agent or node is active if it converges to
1
in forward time. The following Proposition tells us that if an agent’s opinion value starts close enough to a value of
1
, then it will stay active.

Proposition 1

Let
(A,\boldsymbol{\mu},k,\gamma,\boldsymbol{b})
be an instance of NOD. If
k\geq 1
, then for any
i\in[n]
, the subset
S_{z_{i}=1}:=\{\boldsymbol{z}\in S\mid z_{i}=1\}
is invariant. Furthermore,
S_{z_{i}=1}
is asymptotically stable.

Proof:

First, if
k\geq 1
, then the saturation is active in the
i
th coordinate at
z_{i}=1
, meaning
\textbf{sat}(\mu_{i}z_{i}+kz_{i}^{2}+{(A\boldsymbol{z})_{i}}+b_{i})\rvert_{z_{i}=1}=1
, which implies
\left.{\dot{z}_{i}}\right\rvert_{z_{i}=1}=0
. So
S_{z_{i}=1}
is invariant. Then, since by definition of NOD instance we have
\mu_{i}>0
, it follows that the saturation is actually strictly active at
z_{i}=1
, meaning for every point
\boldsymbol{z}\in S_{z_{i}=1}
, there exists
\varepsilon_{z}>0
, such that for every
\tilde{\boldsymbol{z}}\in S
, if
\lVert\boldsymbol{z}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{z}}\rVert<\varepsilon_{z}
, then the saturation function is active. Since
S_{z_{i}=1}
is compact, we have
\varepsilon:=\min\{\varepsilon_{z}\mid\boldsymbol{z}\in S_{z_{i}=1}\}>0
. Thus, for every
\boldsymbol{z}\in S
, if
\lvert 1-z_{i}\rvert<\varepsilon
, then
\dot{z}_{i}=-z_{i}+1
, which converges to
z_{i}=1
.
∎

From the proof of Proposition
1
it is clear that condition
k\geq 1
is not the tightest possible to guarantee our desired behavior, but for the theorems in Section
V
we will consider that
k\geq 1
for simplicity, as it does not affect the set of behaviors achievable.

As an immediate consequence of Proposition
1
, it follows that the full cascade (
\boldsymbol{z}=\mathbf{1}
) is an equilibrium (as it is the unique intersection
\cap_{i\in[n]}S_{z_{i}=1}=\{\mathbf{1}\}
), and it is asymptotically stable. This suggests a new way of posing the question raised in Section
III
about understanding initial conditions that lead to full cascades, which in the context of NOD becomes that of understanding which points are in the basin of attraction of the stable equilibrium
\boldsymbol{z}=\mathbf{1}
. We do not explore this question here. We give the following definition in close correspondence with Definition
1
.

Definition 2

Given a NOD instance
(A,\boldsymbol{\mu},k,\gamma,\boldsymbol{b})
, and a seed set
\mathcal{I}\subseteq[n]
, we define the NOD cascade set

C_{NOD}(\mathcal{I}):=\{i\in[n]\mid\lim_{t\to\infty}z_{i}(t)=1\},

(5)

where state
\boldsymbol{z}
evolves according to
3
with initial condition
z_{i}(0)=\mathbb{I}(i\in\mathcal{I})
. (Dependence of the cascade on the particular NOD instance is left implicit.)

We state the threshold condition for activation of an agent in the NOD model.

Proposition 2

Let
(A,\boldsymbol{\mu},k,\gamma,\boldsymbol{b})
be an instance of NOD, with
\lVert\boldsymbol{\mu}\rVert_{\infty}<1
, and an initial condition
\boldsymbol{z}(0)\in S
. For
i\in[n]
, denote
b_{i}^{*}:=\frac{(1-\mu_{i})^{2}}{4k}
. If there exists
t^{*}\geq 0
and
\varepsilon>0
, such that
\forall t>t^{*}
, we have
\gamma(A\boldsymbol{z})_{i}+b_{i}>b_{i}^{*}+\varepsilon
, then
i\in C_{NOD}(\mathcal{I})
.

Proof:

Let
i\in[n]
. Suppose
\boldsymbol{z}\in S
such that
\gamma(A\boldsymbol{z})_{i}+b_{i}>b_{i}^{*}+\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
, and
z_{i}<1
. There are two cases. First, if the saturation function is active on the
i
th coordinate, then
\dot{z}_{i}=-z_{i}+1>0
. Otherwise, if the saturation is inactive, then

\displaystyle\dot{z}_{i}

\displaystyle=-z_{i}+\mu_{i}z_{i}+kz_{i}^{2}+\gamma\cdot(A\boldsymbol{z})_{i}+b_{i}

\displaystyle>kz_{i}^{2}+(\mu_{i}-1)z_{i}+b_{i}^{*}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle=k\left(z_{i}-\frac{(1-\mu_{i})}{2k}\right)^{2}+\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon>0.

In any case, we have that
\dot{z}_{i}>\min(-z_{i}+1,\varepsilon)>0
. Thus, if there exists a
t^{*}\geq 0
such that
\forall t>t^{*}
, we have
\gamma(A\boldsymbol{z})_{i}+b_{i}>b_{i}^{*}+\varepsilon
, then the previous analysis implies
\lim_{t\to\infty}z_{i}(t)=1
, and so
i\in C_{NOD}(\mathcal{I})
.
∎

2
we require the combined social and external input to remain above the threshold for all time, it would only need to be maintained long enough for the state to enter the basin of a fixed point in
S_{z_{i}=1}
. One way to understand where the threshold

comes from is to regard it as a tipping point (saddle-node bifurcation) occurring with respect to parameter

V
An NOD-based relaxation of LTM

In this section we show how, given an instance of an LTM, it is possible to produce an instance of the NOD model defined above, such that this new model can be seen as a relaxation of the LTM model. By replacing the discontinuous step-function thresholds of the LTM with the smooth bifurcations of the NOD model, we map the discrete cascade process to the continuous flow of a dynamical system.

V-A

Mapping LTM Thresholds to NOD Bifurcations

Given an instance
(A,\boldsymbol{\tau})
of the LTM as defined in Section
III
, we show how to produce an instance of the NOD model that is a relaxation of the LTM to a continuous flow. In Proposition
2
we obtained an expression for the threshold (
b^{*}_{i}
) of each agent in terms of
k
and
\mu_{i}
. If there is no exogenous input (
\boldsymbol{b}=0
in
3
), then this tells us that if after any time the social input is maintained strictly above an agent’s threshold, then that agent will become active. Thus, the condition for switching in the LTM model,
(A\boldsymbol{\zeta})_{i}>\tau_{i}
, corresponds to the condition
(A\boldsymbol{z})_{i}>\frac{b_{i}^{*}}{\gamma}=\frac{(1-\mu_{i})^{2}}{4\gamma k}
. In order for the two models to match in behavior, we choose the
\mu_{i}
in the NOD model so that
\tau_{i}=\frac{b_{i}^{*}}{\gamma}=\frac{(1-\mu_{i})^{2}}{4\gamma k}
. This results in the expression

\mu_{i}=1-2\sqrt{\gamma k\tau_{i}},

(6)

where we assume
\gamma
and
k
are chosen such that
\mu_{i}>0
and also satisfy the other conditions described earlier. We can see that the constraints that we have found tell us that the valid values of the parameters require that
k
is large enough and
\gamma
is small enough. With this relation, we have the following result that relates the cascades in both models.

Theorem 1

Let
(A,\boldsymbol{\tau})
be an instance of the LTM. Consider the NOD instance
(A,\boldsymbol{\mu},k,\gamma,0)
, where
\gamma,k\in\mathbb{R}_{>}
are chosen to satisfy
k\geq 1
,
\gamma k\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}\rVert_{\infty}<1/4
, and for each
i\in[n],
we choose
\mu_{i}=1-2\sqrt{\gamma k\tau_{i}}
. Then for any
\mathcal{I}\subseteq[n],
we have that
C_{LTM}(\mathcal{I})\subseteq C_{NOD}(\mathcal{I})
.

Proof:

Denote the state
\boldsymbol{\zeta}(\ell)
evolving according to the LTM dynamics (
1
) in discrete time
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Define,
C^{(\ell)}:=\{i\in[n]\mid\zeta_{i}(\ell)=1\}
. Then
C^{(0)}=\mathcal{I}
and
C^{(n)}=C_{LTM}(\mathcal{I})
. We show that for each
\ell\in[n],

C^{(\ell)}\subseteq C_{NOD}(\mathcal{I})
, by induction on
\ell
.
First, by definition, for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
,
z_{i}(0)=1
, so by Proposition
1
we conclude that
C^{(0)}=\mathcal{I}\subseteq C_{NOD}(\mathcal{I})
.

For the inductive step, take
\ell\in[n-1]
, and assume
C^{(\ell)}\subseteq C_{NOD}(\mathcal{I})
. Letting
j\in C^{(\ell+1)}
, we need to show that
j\in C_{NOD}(\mathcal{I})
. From the LTM dynamics, there are two cases: either
j\in C^{(\ell)}
or
\exists
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{>}
such that
(A\boldsymbol{\zeta}(\ell))_{j}=\tau_{j}+\delta
. In the former case we are done by the inductive hypothesis. In the latter case, our inductive hypothesis
C^{(\ell)}\subseteq C_{NOD}(\mathcal{I})
implies that
\forall i\in C^{(\ell)}
,
z_{i}\to_{t\to\infty}1
, which in turn implies

lim_t→∞ (A
z
(t))_j≥(A
ζ
(ℓ))_j=
τ
_j+
δ
=

b

j

*

γ

+
δ
.

Multiplying by
\gamma
on both sides we get
\lim_{t\to\infty}\gamma(A\boldsymbol{z}(t))_{j}\geq b_{j}^{*}+\gamma\delta
. By definition of the limit, the trajectory must eventually remain strictly closer to its limit than any positive value. In particular, taking
\frac{\gamma\delta}{2}>0
, we have that there exists
t^{*}>0
such that for all
t\geq t^{*}
,
\gamma(A\boldsymbol{z}(t))_{j}>(b_{j}^{*}+\gamma\delta)-\frac{\gamma\delta}{2}=b_{j}^{*}+\frac{\gamma\delta}{2}
. Using Proposition

we conclude that

. Thus,
C^{(\ell+1)}\subseteq C_{NOD}(\mathcal{I})
.
By induction we conclude that
C_{LTM}(\mathcal{I})=C^{(n)}\subseteq C_{NOD}(\mathcal{I})
.
∎

It is in this sense that we say that NOD is a relaxation of LTM, since the thresholds for each node match, meaning activation in LTM implies activation in NOD for the same seed set. An NOD relaxation always exists, but is not unique, and its properties can be tuned by parameters
\gamma
and
k
. An example of the preceding discussion is presented in Fig.

. This figure shows a cascade starting from
4

\gamma
(B) and one with large
\gamma
(C). The cascade in the LTM is partial, and takes only three time-steps to complete. We can see that the cascade set in both NOD relaxations contains the LTM cascade set, and in fact it perfectly matches it in the case of small
\gamma
(this is formalized in Thm.

The parameter
k
modulates the strength of the nonlinear self-reinforcement. Higher values of

produce steeper, more switch-like activation dynamics.

From Remark

\boldsymbol{z}=\boldsymbol{0}

V-B

Qualitative Differences and Asymptotic Convergence

The fact that NOD dynamics evolve in continuous state and time, means that the particular evolution of the cascade, while related as shown before to that of the LTM, evolves in a quite different way. For example, while any input above threshold will cause activation of the node, the speed with which this happens also depends on the magnitude of the input, with stronger inputs leading to faster responses. This means a seed set that is just slightly above the threshold for propagation can take considerably longer to cascade than a seed set producing an initial input well above the threshold of its neighbors, even when this difference is not noticeable in the LTM. In addition to this temporal difference, another consequence of the opinion state being continuous is that there is subthreshold signaling. That is, even when the input an agent receives is below its threshold, it will make its resting state positive, which in turn feeds back into the network. This fact implies that the relation in the previous section does not hold in the opposite direction, that is, we do
not
have in general that
C_{NOD}(\mathcal{I})\subseteq C_{LTM}(\mathcal{I})
. This is illustrated in Fig.
2
, where we can see that the cascade set of the NOD relaxation with high
\gamma
leads to a full cascade, which is a strict super-set of the LTM cascade set in A. However, as we show in the following, we can guarantee exact correspondence when
\gamma
is made small enough.

Intuitively, this follows from the fact that as the social coupling
\gamma
decreases, the opinion value at the activation threshold (the value
z_{i}^{*}
in Fig.
1

The following theorem shows that by sufficiently bounding the social coupling parameter, the continuous NOD relaxation recovers the exact discrete cascade of the LTM, independent of the initial seed set. VI
The NOD relaxation expands on the LTM

The behavior captured by the continuous dynamics of the NOD model is much richer than that of the LTM. In defining the NOD relaxation we have taken the bias or external information term
\boldsymbol{b}
to be zero. In the LTM, this term is not necessary because it would produce the same effect as reducing the thresholds. However, in the NOD model, a constant non-zero
\boldsymbol{b}
produces a persistent subthreshold signal. One consequence of this is that it is possible to have input vectors
\boldsymbol{b}
that are subthreshold for all individual agents, but which produce a cascade at the group level. An example of this is presented in Fig.
3
, where for an NOD relaxation of an LTM (subplot
a
) with constant threshold (leading to a constant threshold in the NOD) we apply an equal time-varying input
\boldsymbol{b}(t)
as a square wave with an amplitude less than half that of the threshold for a short interval, and this produces an almost full cascade (subplot
b
). This is possible due to subthreshold effects feeding back through the network.

We can also use the NOD model to consider time-varying inputs
\boldsymbol{b}
, and how the temporal structure of the information received by the agents affects cascade outcomes. An example of this is shown in Fig.
3
c
, where the same square time-varying input is applied to all agents, but with a delay selected uniformly randomly to be no greater than the duration of the square wave. We can see that this change produces a subthreshold response in the agents, but it is not enough to drive the group beyond the collective tipping point to a cascade, even though there is a non-zero interval where all the inputs overlap. This emphasizes the large role that the temporal structure of inputs can play in determining cascade outcomes, which are not captured in the LTM.

VII
Conclusion

We have established a rigorous mathematical bridge between discrete contagion models and continuous networked dynamics by introducing a continuous-time and continuous-state relaxation of the Linear Threshold Model (LTM). By mapping the discontinuous step-function thresholds of the LTM directly to the smooth saddle-node bifurcations of the Nonlinear Opinion Dynamics (NOD) model, we retain previous results on cascade properties in the classical threshold model while embedding it within a rich, differentiable state-space. We formally proved that activation in the discrete LTM guarantees activation in the NOD relaxation for any given seed set, and established computable parameter bounds on the social coupling under which the continuous flow exactly recovers the discrete cascade.

Beyond the exact equivalence regime, the true utility of this NOD relaxation lies in its ability to capture network phenomena that are not captured by discrete models. By allowing subthreshold signaling, the continuous framework shows how distributed, weak inputs can feed back through the network to trigger global cascades. Furthermore, it reveals the effects on collective decision-making of the temporal structure and delay of external signals. Future work will leverage this continuous formulation to systematically study the effects of time-varying inputs, structured network heterogeneity, and mixed feedback motifs on the emergence of collective behavior in both biological and engineered systems.
