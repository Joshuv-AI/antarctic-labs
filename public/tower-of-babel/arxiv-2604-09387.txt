Asymptotic rigidity of codimension-1 isometric immersions via quantitative estimates
Mert Baştuğ

Abstract
We offer an alternative approach to the asymptotic rigidity of codimension-1 isometric immersions via quantitative rigidity estimates. We show that an immersion between compact manifolds M M and N N of dimensions d d and d + 1 d+1 , respectively, with small stretching plus bending energy is close to an isometric immersion. In this way, we recover the results of Alpern, Kupferman, and Maor [ 1 , 2 ] . In contrast to their intrinsic approach, we reduce the problem to the equidimensional Euclidean setting and apply the Friesecke–James–Müller [ 8 ] rigidity estimate to obtain quantitative results. This yields an elementary proof based on Euclidean techniques. The rigidity estimates are of independent interest.



1
Introduction

A classical theorem due to Liouville says that a map
u\in C^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, satisfying
Du(x)\in\SO(d)
for all
x\in U
must have a constant derivative if
U
is connected, that is,
Du\equiv R\in\SO(d)
. Liouville’s theorem is the starting point for a large body of research concerning the rigidity of isometric maps. Such theorems play a crucial role in the theory of elasticity and differential geometry. An important generalization of Liouville’s theorem is the celebrated Friesecke-James-Müller quantitative rigidity estimate
[
8
]
(see also
[
5
, Section 2.4]
). Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. It states that for all
u\in W^{1,p}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined on a Lipschitz domain
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists
R\in\SO(d)
such that

\|Du-R\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\dist(Du,\SO(d))\|_{L^{p}},

(1.1)

where
C
depends only on
U
and
p
. The estimate (
1.1
) was a milestone in the rigorous derivation of lower-dimensional models such as plate and shell theories
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
12
]
.

Questions of rigidity naturally lend themselves to generalization in the Riemannian setting, since a map
u\in C^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying
Du(x)\in\SO(d)
for all
x\in U
is an isometric embedding of
U
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Such generalizations are not only interesting from a mathematical viewpoint but also appear in non-Euclidean theory of elasticity (see
[
10
, Section 1.3]
for some applications). Let
(M,g)
and
(N,h)
be compact, oriented,
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifolds. Kupferman, Maor and Schachar
[
10
]
proved the asymptotic rigidity of isometric immersions from
M
to
N
: If
u_{k}\in W^{1,p}(M;N)
and

\int_{M}\dist_{g,h}^{p}(d(u_{k})_{x},\SO((T_{x}M,g_{x}),(T_{u_{k}(x)}N,h_{u_{k}(x)}))\,d\vol_{g}(x)\to 0

(see Section
2.1
for an explanation of the notation), then, up to a subsequence,
(u_{k})
converges in
W^{1,p}
to a smooth isometric immersion. A quantitative version was obtained recently for self-maps by Conti, Dolzmann and Müller
[
4
]
. Essentially, they prove that for every
u\in W^{1,p}(M;M)
, there exists an orientation preserving isometry
\phi
such that the
W^{1,p}
-distance between
u
and
\phi
is bounded by the “elastic energy”

\int_{M}\dist_{g}^{p}(du_{x},\SO(T_{x}M,g_{x}))\,d\vol_{g}(x).

We refer the reader to
[
2
, Figure 1]
for a summary of the literature.

The rigidity property enjoyed by isometric embeddings between equidimensional Euclidean spaces fails when the codomain has higher dimension, as illustrated by unit-speed curves in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The reason is that the derivative reflects local stretching properties but does not capture the degree of bending in the ambient space. The latter information is encoded by the second fundamental form or the shape operator. In the following, we consider
M
and
N
to be
d
and
(d+1)
-dimensional, respectively. Given an immersion
u:M\rightarrow N
and
x\in M
, let
\nu_{u}(x)\in T_{u(x)}N
be the unit normal to
du_{x}(T_{x}M)
that is consistent with the orientation induced by
du_{x}
. We denote by
S_{u}:TM\rightarrow TM
the shape operator induced by
u
(see Section
2.2
for the definition). We consider an elastic energy that measures the bending as well as the stretching energies. We set

E(u):=\int_{M}\dist_{g,h}^{p}(du_{x},\Ort((T_{x}M,g_{x}),(T_{u(x)}N,h_{u(x)})))\,d\vol_{g}(x)+\int_{M}|du_{x}\circ(S_{u}(x)-S(x))|_{g,h}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}(x).

Here
S:TM\rightarrow TM
is a reference shape operator on
M
. The natural class of functions on which the energy is well-defined is the space of Sobolev immersions given by

\Imm_{p}(M;N):=\{u\in W^{1,p}(M;N):\rank du_{x}=d\text{ for a.e. }x\in M,\,\nu_{u}\in W^{1,p}(M;TN)\}.

Alpern, Kupferman, and Maor proved in
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
that for compact manifolds
M
and
N
, any sequence
(u_{k})\in\Imm_{p}(M;N)
with
E(u_{k})\to 0
admits a subsequence converging in
W^{1,p}
to a smooth isometric immersion
u\in\Imm_{p}(M;N)
. Moreover, the associated unit normals
(\nu_{u_{k}})
converge to
\nu_{u}
in
W^{1,p}
, and
S=S_{u}
. This result builds on their earlier work
[
1
]
, where the target manifold
N
was additionally assumed to have constant sectional curvature.

The purpose of this work is to provide an alternative proof of the asymptotic rigidity theorem for codimension-1 isometric immersions based on local quantitative rigidity estimates. In comparison to the Young measure approach used in
[
2
]
, we rely only on the FJM estimate (
1.1
) and elementary methods. We briefly describe the main idea behind our local rigidity estimate in the special case
N=\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Let
u
be a Sobolev immersion with small elastic energy
E(u)
. Then the small bending energy implies that the normal field
\nu_{u}
varies little on sufficiently small scales. As a consequence, the image of
u
is locally close to a
d
-dimensional affine subspace. Since
M
is locally Euclidean, this allows us to view
u
, after restriction to small regions, as an approximately equidimensional map. The Euclidean rigidity estimate then shows that
u
is locally well approximated by a rotation. When
N
is an arbitrary compact manifold, we embed it isometrically in a Euclidean space. See Theorem
3.1
for the precise statement of the result. Once we have the local rigidity estimate, we can recover the asymptotic rigidity result by a compactness argument similar to
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
.

In a companion paper
[
3
]
, we extend the result by Alpern, Kupferman and Maor to complete manifolds
N
, dropping the compactness assumption. In this setting, it becomes more natural to work with the intrinsic definition of manifold-valued Sobolev maps, which requires technical refinements of the previous argument. The approach through embedding the target manifold isometrically into a Euclidean space offers a simplification and yields an elementary proof of Alpern, Kupferman and Maor. The rigidity estimate is also of independent interest.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces some basic notation and the main class of functions we work with, the Sobolev immersions. In Section
3
, we prove the local quantitative rigidity estimate stated in Theorem
3.1
. In the final section of the paper, we recover the result by Alpern, Kupferman and Maor, given in Theorem
4.1
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we define the main space of functions we work with. The first subsection introduces notation that we use frequently in the paper.

2.1
Notation

Let
V
be a vector space endowed with an inner product or, equivalently, a constant metric
g_{0}
. If
v,v^{\prime}\in V
, then we simply write
(v,v^{\prime})_{g_{0}}
for
g_{0}(v,v^{\prime})
and
|v|_{g_{0}}
for
\sqrt{g_{0}(v,v)}
. Let
W
be another vector space with a constant metric
h_{0}
. The set of linear maps from
V
to
W
is denoted by
\mathcal{L}(V,W)
. We denote the subset of isometries in
\mathcal{L}(V,W)
by
\Ort((V,g_{0}),(W,h_{0}))
, and the set of orientation preserving isometries is denoted by
\SO((V,g_{0}),(W,h_{0}))
. Let
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})
be an orthonormal basis of
V
. We define the Frobenius norm of
T\in\mathcal{L}(V,W)
by

|T|_{g_{0},h_{0}}:=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|Tv_{i}|_{h_{0}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

If
(v_{1}^{\prime},\dots,v_{d}^{\prime})
is another orthonormal basis, then

\sum_{i=1}^{d}|Tv_{i}|_{h_{0}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|(v_{i},v_{j}^{\prime})_{g_{0}}|^{2}|Tv_{j}^{\prime}|_{h_{0}}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}|Tv_{j}^{\prime}|_{h_{0}}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}|(v_{i},v_{j}^{\prime})_{g_{0}}|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}|Tv_{j}^{\prime}|_{h_{0}}^{2}.

Hence, the Frobenius norm is well-defined. If
V=W
and
g_{0}=h_{0}
, then we simply write
|T|_{g_{0}}
. If
\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{L}(V,W)
, then we define their distance by

\dist_{g_{0},h_{0}}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S}):=\inf\{|T-S|_{g_{0},h_{0}}:T\in\mathcal{T},S\in\mathcal{S}\}.

If
V_{1},V_{2}
are orthogonal subspaces of
V
and
V_{1}+V_{2}=V
, then we write
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
. In this case,
V_{2}
is the orthogonal complement of
V_{1}
, written as
V_{1}^{\perp}=V_{2}
.

We denote the standard basis vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
(e_{1},\dots,e_{d})
. The Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
\e_{d}
. In the notation introduced above, we suppress the metric when all relevant vector spaces are Euclidean and write
(v,v^{\prime})
,
|T|
,
\dist(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S})
etc. Let
g_{0}
and
g_{0}^{\prime}
be constant metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We define their distance by

|g_{0}-g_{0}^{\prime}|:=\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|(e_{i},e_{j})_{g_{0}}-(e_{i},e_{j})_{g_{0}^{\prime}}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

If
h_{0}
is a constant metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then we use the short-hand notations
\Ort(g_{0},h_{0})
and
\SO(g_{0},h_{0})
instead of
\Ort((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{0}),(\mathbb{R}^{n},h_{0}))
and
\SO((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{0}),(\mathbb{R}^{n},h_{0}))
. When
g_{0}=h_{0}
, the notation is further shortened to
\Ort(g_{0})
and
\SO(g_{0})
.

Let
(M,g)
and
(N,h)
be Riemannian manifolds. We denote the differential of a smooth map
f:M\rightarrow N
by
df:TM\rightarrow TN
. If
N=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then we can identify
T\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
. Hence, for every
x\in M
, there exists a map
Df(x):T_{x}M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

df_{x}(v)=(f(x),Df(x)v).

We call
Df:TM\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
the total derivative of
f
. When
f
is only weakly differentiable, the notations
df
and
Df
are used in analogy with the smooth case. We denote the volume form on
M
by
\vol_{g}
.

2.2
Sobolev immersions

The following definitions are based on
[
1
, Sections 2.4, 2.5]
. Let
(M,g)
be a
d
-dimensional oriented Riemannian manifold and let
(N,h)
be a
(d+1)
-dimensional oriented compact Riemannian manifold. Let
1<p<\infty
. By Nash’s embedding theorem, there exists a smooth isometric embedding
\iota:N\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{D}
for some
D\geq d+1
. We write
u\in W^{1,p}(M;N)
if
\iota\circ u\in W^{1,p}(M;\mathbb{R}^{D})
and we set
\bar{u}:=\iota\circ u
. The compactness of
N
ensures that the definition of
W^{1,p}(M;N)
is independent of the embedding
\iota
. It is not difficult to show that for a.e.
x\in M
,
d\bar{u}_{x}(T_{x}M)
is contained in
d\iota_{u(x)}(T_{u(x)}N)
. This allows us to define a weak differential
du_{x}:T_{x}M\rightarrow T_{u(x)}N
for a.e.
x\in M
by setting
du_{x}:=(d\iota_{u(x)})^{-1}\circ d\bar{u}_{x}
. The definition of the weak differential does not depend on
\iota
. Throughout the paper, we do not identify Sobolev functions that agree almost everywhere. It is possible to define manifold-valued Sobolev maps intrinsically as well (see
[
6
]
). This is the approach we take in
[
3
]
.

If
u\in W^{1,p}(M;N)
and
\rank du_{x}=d
, then there exists a unique vector
\nu_{u}(x)\in T_{u(x)}N
satisfying the following conditions:

1.

|\nu_{u}(x)|_{h}=1
,

2.

(\nu_{u}(x),du_{x}(v))_{h}=0
for all
v\in T_{x}M
,

3.

if
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})
is a positively oriented basis of
T_{x}M
, then
(du_{x}(v_{1}),\dots,du_{x}(v_{d}),\nu_{u}(x))
is a positively oriented basis of
T_{u(x)}N
.

We call
\nu_{u}(x)
the oriented unit normal at
x
. If
\rank du_{x}<d
, we set
\nu_{u}(x):=0\in T_{u(x)}N
. We define
\bar{\nu}_{u}(x):=D\iota(u(x))(\nu_{u}(x))
. The space of Sobolev immersions is given by

\Imm_{p}(M;N):=\{u\in W^{1,p}(M;N):\rank du_{x}=d\text{ for a.e. }x\in M,\,\bar{\nu}_{u}\in W^{1,p}(M;\mathbb{R}^{D})\}.

Given
u\in\Imm_{p}(M;N)
, we let
P_{u}(x)
be the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{R}^{D}
onto the subspace that is canonically isomorphic to
T_{\bar{u}(x)}\iota(N)
. Differentiating the identity
(\bar{\nu}_{u}(x),\bar{\nu}_{u}(x))=1
shows that
\bar{\nu}_{u}(x)
is orthogonal to
D\bar{\nu}_{u}(x)(T_{x}M)
for a.e.
x\in M
. Thus,
P_{u}(x)\circ D\bar{\nu}_{u}(x)
maps
T_{x}M
into
D\bar{u}(x)(T_{x}M)
for a.e.
x\in M
. Consequently, there exists a unique linear map
S_{u}(x):T_{x}M\rightarrow T_{x}M
satisfying

D\bar{u}(x)\circ S_{u}(x)=P_{u}(x)\circ D\bar{\nu}_{u}(x).

(2.1)

We call
S_{u}:TM\rightarrow TM
the induced shape operator.

If we view
u\in\Imm_{p}(M;N)
as a deformation of
M
, then we can measure its stretching and bending energies with the following functionals:

\displaystyle E_{s}(u)

\displaystyle:=\int_{M}\dist_{g,h}^{p}(du_{x},\Ort((T_{x}M,g_{x}),(T_{u(x)}N,h_{u(x)})))\,d\vol_{g}(x),

(2.2)

\displaystyle E_{b}(u)

\displaystyle:=\int_{M}|du_{x}\circ S_{u}(x)|_{g,h}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}(x)=\int_{M}|P_{u}(x)\circ D\bar{\nu}_{u}(x)|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}(x).

(2.3)

The integrand of
E_{s}(u)
quantifies how far
du_{x}
is from being length preserving, whereas the integrand of
E_{b}(u)
measures how much the deformation of
M
curves within the ambient space
N
. If
M
has a pre-assigned “shape”, then we can also measure the deviation from the original shape after the deformation. To make this precise, we assume that
M
is equipped with a smooth symmetric 2-tensor field
b
. For each
x\in M
we let
S(x):T_{x}M\rightarrow T_{x}M
be the unique linear map defined by

(S(x)v,w)_{g_{x}}=b(v,w)\quad\text{for all }v,w\in T_{x}M.

(2.4)

We call the resulting map
S:TM\rightarrow TM
the reference shape operator. The modified bending energy is given by

E_{b}^{S}(u):=\int_{M}|du_{x}\circ(S_{u}(x)-S(x))|_{g,h}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}(x).

(2.5)

Clearly,
E_{b}(u)=E_{b}^{S}(u)
if
S\equiv 0
. Finally, we also introduce the quantity

\mathcal{E}(u):=E_{b}(u)+\int_{M}|du_{x}|_{g,h}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}(x),

(2.6)

which will appear frequently in our estimates.

3
Local quantitative rigidity estimate

We fix an open and bounded cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
endowed with a metric
g
such that

\frac{1}{\lambda}\e_{d}\leq g\leq\lambda\e_{d}\quad\text{in }Q

(3.1)

for some
\lambda>0
. Let
(N,h)
be an oriented compact manifold of dimension
d+1
and fix a smooth isometric embedding
\iota:N\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{D}
. We also fix
p\in(1,\infty)
. In this section, we prove the following quantitative rigidity result for functions in
\Imm_{p}(Q;N)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u\in\Imm_{p}(Q;N)
. There exist
x_{0}\in Q
and
R\in\Ort(g_{x_{0}},\e_{D})
such that

\int_{Q}|D\bar{u}-R|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(|Q|\left(\osc_{Q}g\right)^{p}+E_{s}(u)+\diam^{p}(Q)\mathcal{E}(u)\right).

The constant
C
depends only on
d
,
p
,
\lambda
,
N
and
\iota
.

Our proof is based on the celebrated Euclidean rigidity estimate due to Friesecke, James and Müller
[
8
]
(see
[
5
, Section 2.4]
for
p\neq 2
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a Lipschitz domain and let
u\in W^{1,p}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then there exists
R\in\SO(\e_{d})
such that

\int_{U}|Du-R|^{p}\,dx\leq C_{U}\int_{U}\dist^{p}(Du,\SO(\e_{d}))\,dx

(3.2)

with
C_{U}
depending only on
p
and
U
.

Remark 3.2.1
.

It is easy to see that the rigidity constant
C_{U}
is invariant under scaling and translations. Furthermore, if
T:U\rightarrow V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bi-Lipschitz map, then
C_{V}
can be estimated in terms of
C_{U}
and the Lipschitz constants of
T
and
T^{-1}

[
11
, Theorem 2.3]
.

We would like to generalize Theorem
3.2
to domains endowed with non-Euclidean metrics. In order to do this, we need to add pointwise dependence to the set of rotations in the bounding term, since the rotations depend on the metric. We shall do this with a simple error estimate.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
x,y\in Q
. Then

\dist(\SO(g_{x},\e_{d}),\SO(g_{y},\e_{d}))\leq\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{2}|g_{x}-g_{y}|.

Proof.

Define the matrices
G_{x}
and
G_{y}
by
(G_{x})_{ij}:=(e_{i},e_{j})_{g_{x}}
,
(G_{y})_{ij}:=(e_{i},e_{j})_{g_{y}}
. Let
R_{x}
and
R_{y}
be the positive square roots of
G_{x}
and
G_{y}
, respectively. An easy computation reveals that
R_{x}\in\SO(g_{x},\e_{d})
and
R_{y}\in\SO(g_{y},\e_{d})
. Hence,

\dist(\SO(g_{x},\e_{d}),\SO(g_{y},\e_{d}))\leq|R_{x}-R_{y}|.

We bound
|R_{x}-R_{y}|
by
|R_{x}^{2}-R_{y}^{2}|=|G_{x}-G_{y}|
. Let
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})
be a Euclidean orthonormal basis consisting of eigenvectors of
R_{x}-R_{y}
with
(R_{x}-R_{y})v_{i}=\mu_{i}v_{i}
. Then

((G_{x}-G_{y})v_{i},v_{i})=(R_{x}(R_{x}-R_{y})v_{i},v_{i})+((R_{x}-R_{y})R_{y}v_{i},v_{i})=\mu_{i}\left((R_{x}v_{i},v_{i})+(R_{y}v_{i},v_{i})\right)\geq\frac{2\mu_{i}}{\sqrt{\lambda}},

where we used (
3.1
) in the final inequality. Since
|\mu_{i}|=|(R_{x}-R_{y})v_{i}|
, we obtain

|G_{x}-G_{y}|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|(G_{x}-G_{y})v_{i}|^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d}((G_{x}-G_{y})v_{i},v_{i})^{2}\geq\frac{4}{\lambda}\sum_{i=1}^{d}|(R_{x}-R_{y})v_{i}|^{2}=\frac{4}{\lambda}|R_{x}-R_{y}|^{2}.

∎

We also require the following lemma on the uniform equivalence of the Frobenius norms with respect to
g
and
\e_{d}
in
Q
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
W
be a vector space endowed with a constant metric
h_{0}
. If
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d},W)
, then

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}|T|_{g_{x},h_{0}}\leq|T|_{\e_{d},h_{0}}\leq\sqrt{\lambda}|T|_{g_{x},h_{0}}\quad\text{for all }x\in Q.

(3.3)

Proof.

Fix
x\in Q
. Define the matrix
G
by
G_{ij}:=(e_{i},e_{j})_{g_{x}}
. Let
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})
be a Euclidean orthonormal basis consisting of eigenvectors of
G
. Set
\lambda_{i}=(v_{i},v_{i})_{g_{x}}
and
\tilde{v}_{i}:=v_{i}/\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}
. Then
(\tilde{v}_{1},\dots,\tilde{v}_{d})
is an orthonormal basis relative to
g_{x}
. Consequently,

|T|_{\e_{d},h_{0}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|Tv_{i}|_{h_{0}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}|T\tilde{v}_{i}|_{h_{0}}^{2}.

By (
3.1
), we know that
1/\lambda\leq\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda
for all
i
. Since
|T|_{g_{x},h_{0}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|T\tilde{v}_{i}|_{h_{0}}^{2}
, the claim follows immediately.
∎

Compare the following result with
[
11
, Theorem 2.3]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

For every
u\in W^{1,p}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
x_{0}\in Q
, there exists
R\in\SO(g_{x_{0}},\e_{d})
such that

\int_{Q}|Du-R|_{g,\e_{d}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(|Q|\left(\osc_{Q}g\right)^{p}+\int_{Q}\dist_{g,\e_{d}}^{p}(Du,\SO(g,\e_{d}))\,dx\right).

The constant
C
depends only on
p
,
d
and
\lambda
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in Q
and
u\in W^{1,p}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. If
T\in\SO(g_{x_{0}},\e_{d})
, then
v:=u\circ T^{-1}\in W^{1,p}(T(Q);\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By Theorem
3.2
, there exists
\bar{R}\in\SO(\e_{d})
such that

\int_{T(Q)}|Dv-\bar{R}|^{p}\,dx\leq C_{T(Q)}\int_{T(Q)}\dist^{p}(Dv,\SO(\e_{d}))\,dx.

(3.4)

Due to Remark
3.2.1
, we can replace
C_{T(Q)}
by another constant
C
depending only on
p
,
d
and
\lambda
, since the Lipschitz constants of
T
and
T^{-1}
can be bounded in terms of
\lambda
. Using the equalities

\displaystyle\int_{Q}|Du-\bar{R}T|_{g_{x_{0}},\e_{d}}^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{Q}|Du\,T^{-1}-\bar{R}|^{p}\,dx,

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\dist_{g_{x_{0}},\e_{d}}^{p}(Du,\SO(g_{x_{0}},\e_{d}))\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{Q}\dist^{p}(Du\,T^{-1},\SO(\e_{d}))\,dx

and changing variables in (
3.4
), we obtain

\int_{Q}|Du-\bar{R}T|_{g_{x_{0}},\e_{d}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\int_{Q}\dist_{g_{x_{0}},\e_{d}}^{p}(Du,\SO(g_{x_{0}},\e_{d}))\,dx.

Therefore, by Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
, we have

\int_{Q}|Du-\bar{R}T|_{g,\e_{d}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\int_{Q}|Du-\bar{R}T|_{g_{x_{0}},\e_{d}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\int_{Q}\dist_{g_{x_{0}},\e_{d}}^{p}(Du,\SO(g_{x_{0}},\e_{d}))\,dx\\
\leq C\left(|Q|\left(\osc_{Q}g\right)^{p}+\int_{Q}\dist_{g,\e_{d}}^{p}(Du,\SO(g,\e_{d}))\,dx\right).

Since
\bar{R}T\in\SO(g_{x_{0}},\e_{d})
, we are done.
∎

Given
u\in\Imm_{p}(Q;N)
, we can compose
\bar{u}
with a projection map onto a
d
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{D}
. Then the composition is a map between
d
-dimensional domains, to which we can apply the rigidity estimate in Theorem
3.5
. In order to estimate the error coming from the projection, we need to bound the

-norm of
D\bar{\nu}_{u}
. We do this in the next proposition in terms of the bending energy of

.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
u\in\Imm_{p}(Q;N)
. Then for all
j=1,\dots,d
,

|\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{\nu}_{u}|^{2}\leq|P_{u}(\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{\nu}_{u})|^{2}+C|\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{u}|^{2}\quad\text{a.e. in }Q.

(3.5)

In particular,

\int_{Q}|D\bar{\nu}_{u}|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\mathcal{E}(u).

The constant
C
depends only on
d
,
p
,
\lambda
,
N
and
\iota
.

Proof.

Set
r:=D-d-1
. If
q\in\iota(N)
, then
T_{q}\iota(N)
is canonically isomorphic to a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{D}
which we denote by
V_{q}
. For each
q\in\iota(N)
, we can find an open neighborhood
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
and vector fields
n_{i}\in C^{\infty}(U;\mathbb{R}^{D})
for
i=1,\dots,r
such that
(n_{1}(q^{\prime}),\dots,n_{r}(q^{\prime}))
is an orthonormal basis of
V_{q^{\prime}}^{\perp}
for all
q^{\prime}\in U\cap\iota(N)
, and
|Dn_{i}|
is uniformly bounded in
U
for all
i
.

We choose representatives for
\bar{u}
and
\bar{\nu}_{u}
that are absolutely continuous on almost all line segments in
Q
parallel to the coordinate axes. We fix
q\in\iota(N)
and an integer
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
. Assume
\bar{u}
and
\bar{\nu}_{u}
are absolutely continuous on
\ell:=Q\cap\{x+te_{j}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
. Then
I:=\ell\cap\bar{u}^{-1}(U)
is relatively open in
\ell
. Hence, we can differentiate the relation
(\bar{\nu}_{u},n_{i}\circ\bar{u})=0
in

to get

(\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{\nu}_{u},n_{i}\circ\bar{u})=-(\bar{\nu}_{u},\partial_{x_{j}}(n_{i}\circ\bar{u}))=-(\bar{\nu}_{u},Dn_{i}\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{u})\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}\text{-a.e. in }I.

Recall that
P_{u}(x)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{D};\mathbb{R}^{D})
is the orthogonal projection onto
V_{\bar{u}(x)}
. The orthogonal decomposition of
\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{\nu}_{u}
yields

\displaystyle|\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{\nu}_{u}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|P_{u}(\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{\nu}_{u})|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}|(\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{\nu}_{u},n_{i}\circ\bar{u})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|P_{u}(\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{\nu}_{u})|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}|Dn_{i}\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{u}|^{2}\leq|P_{u}(\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{\nu}_{u})|^{2}+C|\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{u}|^{2}\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}\text{-a.e. in }I,

where
C
is a uniform bound for
|Dn_{i}|
in
U
. Since
\ell
was an arbitrary line segment, we conclude, using Fubini’s theorem for measurable sets, that

|\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{\nu}_{u}|^{2}\leq|P_{u}(\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{\nu}_{u})|^{2}+C|\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{u}|^{2}\quad\text{a.e. in }u^{-1}(U).

(3.6)

We can cover
\iota(N)
with finitely many open sets
U_{1},\dots,U_{m}
such that the estimate (
3.6
) holds on each
u^{-1}(U_{j})
with a uniform constant depending only on

and
\iota
. Thus,

|\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{\nu}_{u}|^{2}\leq|P_{u}(\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{\nu}_{u})|^{2}+C|\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{u}|^{2}\quad\text{a.e. in }Q.

(3.7)

Finally, we sum (
3.7
) over all
k
and integrate in

. We need the following lemma to conclude the proof.

Since
g
is comparable to the Euclidean metric, integrals with respect to the Riemannian volume form
\vol_{g}
can be bounded by those with respect to the Lebesgue measure and vice versa. The bounding factors depend only on
\lambda
and
d
. The result is summarized in the next lemma, which we shall use implicitly most of the time. We omit its proof, since it is elementary.

Lemma 3.7
.

If
f:Q\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is measurable, then

\frac{1}{\lambda^{d/2}}\int_{Q}f\,dx\leq\int_{Q}f\,d\vol_{g}\leq\lambda^{d/2}\int_{Q}f\,dx.

We introduce some definitions. Let
\Pi\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
be a
d
-dimensional oriented subspace. A natural orientation on
\Pi^{\perp}
is defined by declaring an ordered basis
(w_{1},\dots,w_{D-d})
of
\Pi^{\perp}
to be positively oriented, if
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d},w_{1},\dots,w_{D-d})
is a positively oriented basis of
\mathbb{R}^{D}
for any positively oriented basis
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})
of
\Pi
. If
\Pi^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is another
d
-dimensional oriented subspace, then the distance between
\Pi
and
\Pi^{\prime}
is defined by

|\Pi-\Pi^{\prime}|:=\inf\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|v_{i}-v_{i}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the infimum ranges over all positively oriented bases
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})
and
(v_{1}^{\prime},\dots,v_{d}^{\prime})
of
\Pi
and
\Pi^{\prime}
, respectively.
The orthogonal projection from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
onto
\Pi
is denoted by
P_{\Pi}
.

The next proposition bounds the distance of the projection of a linear map from rotations in the projection plane by how far the map itself is from arbitrary rotations. The proof depends on two lemmas, which we state at the end.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\Pi_{0},\Pi\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
be
d
-dimensional oriented subspaces and let
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Pi)
. Then

|P_{\Pi_{0}}T-T|_{g_{x},\e_{D}}\leq|T|_{g_{x},\e_{D}}|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|\quad\text{for all }x\in Q.

(3.8)

If
T
is orientation preserving, then

\dist_{g_{x},e_{D}}\left(P_{\Pi_{0}}T,\SO\left((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{x}),(\Pi_{0},\e_{D})\right)\right)\leq\dist_{g_{x},e_{D}}(T,\Ort(g_{x},\e_{D}))+C|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|\quad\text{for all }x\in Q,

(3.9)

where
C
depends only on
d
and
D
.

Proof.

Fix
x\in Q
. Set
r=D-d
. Let
(v_{1},\dots,v_{r})
and
(w_{1},\dots,w_{r})
be oriented orthonormal bases of
\Pi_{0}^{\perp}
and
\Pi^{\perp}
, respectively. If
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then

|P_{\Pi_{0}}Tv-Tv|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(Tv,v_{j})^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(Tv,v_{j}-w_{j})^{2}\leq|Tv|^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}|v_{j}-w_{j}|^{2}.

Taking the infimum over all positively oriented orthonormal bases of
\Pi_{0}^{\perp}
and
\Pi^{\perp}
yields
|P_{\Pi_{0}}Tv-Tv|\leq|Tv||\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|
which implies
|P_{\Pi_{0}}T-T|_{g_{x},\e_{D}}\leq|T|_{g_{x},\e_{D}}|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|
.

To prove (
3.9
), assume first that
P_{\Pi_{0}}T
is orientation preserving. By Lemma
3.9
, there exists
R\in\Ort((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{x}),(\Pi,\e_{D}))
such that
|T-R|_{g_{x},\e_{D}}=\dist_{g_{x},e_{D}}(T,\Ort(g_{x},\e_{D}))
. Using Lemma
3.9
again, we have

\dist_{g_{x},e_{D}}(P_{\Pi_{0}}T,\SO((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{x}),(\Pi_{0},\e_{D})))=\dist_{g_{x},e_{D}}(P_{\Pi_{0}}T,\Ort(g_{x},e_{D}))\\
\leq|P_{\Pi_{0}}T-R|_{g_{x},\e_{D}}\leq|P_{\Pi_{0}}(T-R)|_{g_{x},e_{D}}+|P_{\Pi_{0}}R-R|_{g_{x},\e_{D}}\leq\dist_{g_{x},e_{D}}(T,\Ort(g_{x},\e_{D}))+|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|\sqrt{d}.

Lemma
3.10
implies that there exists
\varepsilon>0
, depending only on
d
and
D
, such that
P_{\Pi_{0}}T
is orientation preserving if
|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|<\varepsilon
. Therefore, to finish the proof, we need to handle the case
|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|\geq\varepsilon
. Let
R\in\Ort(g_{x},\e_{D})
satisfy
|T-R|_{g_{x},\e_{D}}=\dist_{g_{x},\e_{D}}(T,\Ort(g_{x},\e_{D}))
. Using the triangle inequality, it easily follows that

\dist_{g_{x},e_{D}}(P_{\Pi_{0}}T,\SO((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{x}),(\Pi_{0},\e_{D})))\leq|P_{\Pi_{0}}T|_{g_{x},\e_{D}}+\sqrt{d}\leq|P_{\Pi_{0}}(T-R)|_{g_{x},\e_{D}}+2\sqrt{d}\\
\leq\dist_{g_{x},e_{D}}(T,\Ort(g_{x},\e_{D}))+2\sqrt{d}\leq\dist_{g_{x},e_{D}}(T,\Ort(g_{x},\e_{D}))+\frac{2\sqrt{d}}{\varepsilon}|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|.

Finally, we set
C:=\sqrt{d}\max\{1,2/\varepsilon\}
.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\Pi\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
be a
d
-dimensional oriented subspace and
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\Pi)
. Then

\dist_{g_{x},e_{D}}(T,\Ort(g_{x},e_{D}))=\dist_{g_{x},e_{D}}(T,\Ort((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{x}),(\Pi,\e_{D})))\quad\text{for all }x\in Q.

(3.10)

Moreover, if
T
is orientation preserving, then

\dist_{g_{x},e_{D}}(T,\Ort(g_{x},e_{D}))=\dist_{g_{x},e_{D}}(T,\SO((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{x}),(\Pi,\e_{D})))\quad\text{for all }x\in Q.

(3.11)

Proof.

Fix
x\in Q
. To begin with, we assume
g_{x}=\e_{d}
. By the singular value decomposition, there exist
Q\in\Ort(\e_{d})
,
S\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
R\in\Ort((\mathbb{R}^{d},\e_{d}),(\Pi,e_{D}))
and positive real numbers
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}
such that
T=RSQ
and
Se_{i}=\lambda_{i}e_{i}
for all
i
. If
L\in\Ort(\e_{d},e_{D})
, then

|L-RS|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|Le_{i}-RSe_{i}|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}1+\lambda_{i}^{2}-2\lambda_{i}(Le_{i},Re_{i})\\
\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d}1+\lambda_{i}^{2}-2\lambda_{i}(Re_{i},Re_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|Re_{i}-RSe_{i}|^{2}=|R-RS|^{2}.

Since
LQ^{-1}\in\Ort(\e_{d},e_{D})
, we also have

|L-T|=|LQ^{-1}-RS|\geq|R-RS|=|RQ-T|.

Hence,
\dist(T,\Ort(\e_{d},\e_{D}))=|RQ-T|
. As
R\in\Ort((\mathbb{R}^{d},\e_{d}),(\Pi,e_{D}))
, we conclude that

\dist(T,\Ort((\mathbb{R}^{d},\e_{d}),(\Pi,e_{D})))\leq|RQ-T|=\dist(T,\Ort(\e_{d},\e_{D}))\leq\dist(T,\Ort((\mathbb{R}^{d},\e_{d}),(\Pi,e_{D}))).

Now assume
T
is orientation preserving. Because
S
is positive definite, it is orientation preserving as well. Therefore,
RQ
must be orientation preserving, and we obtain

\dist(T,\SO((\mathbb{R}^{d},\e_{d}),(\Pi,e_{D})))=\dist(T,\Ort(\e_{d},\e_{D})).

If
g_{x}\neq\e_{d}
, we let
L\in\Ort(\e_{d},g_{x})
. Then
TL\in\Ort(\e_{d},\e_{D})
. We deduce from the previous step that

\dist_{g_{x},e_{D}}(T,\Ort(g_{x},e_{D}))=\dist(TL,\Ort(\e_{d},e_{D}))\\
=\dist(TL,\Ort((\mathbb{R}^{d},\e_{d}),(\Pi,\e_{D})))=\dist_{g_{x},e_{D}}(T,\Ort((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{x}),(\Pi,\e_{D}))).

The claim (
3.11
) follows similarly.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\Pi_{0},\Pi\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
be
d
-dimensional oriented subspaces. There exists
\varepsilon>0
depending only on
d
and
D
such that the restriction of
P_{\Pi_{0}}
to
\Pi
is orientation preserving if
|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Let
(v_{d+1},\dots,v_{D})
and
(w_{d+1},\dots,w_{D})
be positively oriented orthonormal bases of
\Pi_{0}^{\perp}
and
\Pi^{\perp}
, respectively, such that
|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|^{2}=\sum_{j=d+1}^{D}|v_{j}-w_{j}|^{2}
. Using an orthogonal transformation, we may assume
w_{j}=e_{j}
for
j=d+1,\dots,D
so that
\Pi=\lspan(e_{1},\dots,e_{d})
. We denote the projection map simply by
P
. Our goal is to prove that
(Pe_{1},\dots,Pe_{d},v_{d+1},\dots,v_{D})
is a positively oriented basis of
\mathbb{R}^{D}
if
|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|
is sufficiently small. We start with the proof of linear independence. For
i=1,\dots,d
we have

|e_{i}-Pe_{i}|^{2}=\sum_{j=d+1}^{D}|(e_{i},v_{j})|^{2}=\sum_{j=d+1}^{D}|(e_{i},v_{j}-e_{j})|^{2}\leq\sum_{j=d+1}^{D}|v_{j}-e_{j}|^{2}=|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|^{2}.

(3.12)

Therefore,

|(Pe_{i},Pe_{j})-\delta_{ij}|=|(Pe_{i}-e_{i},Pe_{j})+(e_{i},Pe_{j}-e_{j})|\leq 2|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|\quad i,j=1,\dots,d.

If
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}Pe_{i}\Big|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|a_{i}|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{i}a_{j}((Pe_{i},Pe_{j})-\delta_{ij}).

If
\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}Pe_{i}=0
, then

\sum_{i=1}^{d}|a_{i}|^{2}\leq 2|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|a_{i}|\Big)^{2}\leq 2d|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|\sum_{i=1}^{d}|a_{i}|^{2}.

Hence, we conclude that
a_{i}=0
for all
i
, and
(Pe_{1},\dots,Pe_{d})
is linearly independent if
|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|<1/2d
. We set
v_{i}=Pe_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,d
. It now follows that
(v_{1},\dots,v_{D})
is a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{D}
and (
3.12
) implies
\sum_{i=1}^{D}|v_{j}-e_{j}|^{2}\leq(d+1)|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|^{2}
. Since
(e_{1},\dots,e_{D})
is positively oriented, so is
(v_{1},\dots,v_{D})
granted
|\Pi_{0}^{\perp}-\Pi^{\perp}|
is sufficiently small.

Proof of Theorem

.

Roughly speaking, our strategy will be to project
\bar{u}

\int_{Q}\int_{Q}|\Pi_{x}^{\perp}-\Pi_{y}^{\perp}|^{p}\,dx\,dy\leq C\diam^{p}(Q)|Q|\mathcal{E}(u).

Since

\int_{Q}\int_{Q}|\bar{\nu}_{u}(x)-\bar{\nu}_{u}(y)|^{p}\,dx\,dy\leq C\diam^{p}(Q)|Q|\int_{Q}|D\bar{\nu}_{u}|^{p}\,dx\leq C\diam^{p}(Q)|Q|\mathcal{E}(u).

By Chebyshev’s inequality applied to (

\displaystyle|D\bar{u}(x)-R|_{g_{x},\e_{D}}

\dist_{g_{x},\e_{d}}(Dv(x),\SO(g_{x},\e_{d}))=\dist_{g_{x},\e_{D}}(P_{\Pi_{x_{0}}}D\bar{u}(x),\SO((\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{x}),(\Pi_{x_{0}},\e_{D}))\\

for a.e.

4
Asymptotic rigidity

In this section, we prove our main result. Compare with
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(M,g)
and
(N,h)
be oriented compact Riemannian manifolds of dimensions
d
and
d+1
, respectively. Let
\iota:N\to\mathbb{R}^{D}
be a smooth isometric embedding. Assume
(u_{k})\subset\Imm_{p}(M;N)
satisfies

\lim_{k\to\infty}E_{s}(u_{k})=0,\quad\limsup_{k\to\infty}E_{b}(u_{k})<\infty.

(4.1)

Then there exists a subsequence
(u_{k_{j}})
and

such that

\bar{u}_{k_{j}}\to\bar{u}\text{ in }W^{1,p}(M;\mathbb{R}^{D}),\quad du_{x}\in\Ort((T_{x}M,g_{x}),(T_{u(x)}N,h_{u(x)}))\text{ for a.e. }x\in M.

(4.2)

Furthermore, if
S:TM\rightarrow TM
is the reference shape operator with
|S|_{g}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(M)
, where
p^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
, and

\lim_{k\to\infty}E_{b}^{S}(u_{k})=0,

(4.3)

then
S=S_{u}
a.e. in
M
.

Note that, in contrast to
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
, we do not prove the regularity of the limiting map. Since the main difficulty of Theorem
4.1
is establishing the existence of an isometric limit, we prove this as a separate lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(M,g)
,
(N,h)
and
\iota
be as in Theorem
4.1
. Assume
(u_{k})\subset\Imm_{p}(M;N)
satisfies (
4.1
). Then there exists a subsequence
(u_{k_{j}})
and
u\in W^{1,p}(M;N)
such that

\bar{u}_{k_{j}}\to\bar{u}\text{ in }W^{1,p}(M;\mathbb{R}^{D}),\quad du_{x}\in\Ort((T_{x}M,g_{x}),(T_{u(x)}N,h_{u(x)}))\text{ for a.e. }x\in M.

Proof.

Given
q\in M
, let
(U,\varphi)
be a chart on
M
with
q\in M
such that
Q:=\varphi(U)
is a bounded open cube and set
v_{k}:=u_{k}\circ\varphi^{-1}
. We denote the pushforward of
g
by
\varphi
by the same letter, and we assume without loss of generality that

\frac{1}{\lambda}\e_{d}\leq g\leq\lambda\e_{d}\quad\text{in }Q

for some
\lambda>0
and that
g
is Lipschitz continuous in
Q
. We prove the claim in
U
. Since
q
is arbitrary and
M
is compact, the general result follows easily.

We briefly outline the proof. In its essentials, the proof is very similar to the proof of the compactness result in
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
. We use the rigidity theorem
3.1
to approximate
D\bar{v}_{k}
by an almost isometric piecewise constant map
G_{k}
. Then, using the hypotheses (
4.1
), we show that
(G_{k})
satisfies the Fréchet-Kolmogorov theorem and, therefore, prove that it has a convergent subsequence in
L^{p}(Q;\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{D}))
, denoted by
G
. Finally, we show that the corresponding subsequence of
(\bar{v}_{k})
converges to a Sobolev map
\bar{v}
with
D\bar{v}=G
. The desired limit is then given by
u:=\iota^{-1}\circ\bar{v}\circ\varphi
.

Let
l
be the side length of
Q
. We partition
Q
into identical open cubes with side lengths
l/t_{k}
, where
t_{k}
is a natural number to be determined. We denote the partition by
\mathcal{P}_{k}
. If
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
, then, by Theorem
3.1
, there exist
x_{0}\in Q^{\prime}
and
R\in\Ort(g_{x_{0}},\e_{D})
such that

\int_{Q^{\prime}}|D\bar{v}_{k}-R|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(|Q^{\prime}|\left(\osc_{Q^{\prime}}g\right)^{p}+E_{s}(v_{k},Q^{\prime})+\diam^{p}(Q^{\prime})\mathcal{E}(v_{k},Q^{\prime})\right),

(4.4)

where
E_{s}(v_{k},Q^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{E}(v_{k},Q^{\prime})
denote the energies of
v_{k}
restricted to
Q^{\prime}
. We set
G_{k}(x):=R
for all
x\in Q^{\prime}
. By applying the rigidity theorem to
\bar{v}_{k}
in each cube in
\mathcal{P}_{k}
, we obtain a piecewise constant map
G_{k}:Q\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{D})
.

For
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
, we denote by
3Q^{\prime}
the concentric cube with three times the side length. We also denote the center of
Q^{\prime}
by
c_{Q^{\prime}}
. Let
Q^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
and assume
Q^{\prime\prime}\subset 3Q^{\prime}\subset Q
. We shall estimate
|G_{k}(c_{Q^{\prime}})-G_{k}(c_{Q^{\prime\prime}})|
. We apply the rigidity estimate to
\bar{v}_{k}
in
3Q^{\prime}
to obtain an approximation
R^{\prime}
for
D\bar{v}_{k}
. Then

\int_{Q^{\prime}}|G_{k}(c_{Q^{\prime\prime}})-R^{\prime}|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(\int_{Q^{\prime\prime}}|G_{k}(c_{Q^{\prime\prime}})-D\bar{v}_{k}|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx+\int_{3Q^{\prime}}|R^{\prime}-D\bar{v}_{k}|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx\right)\\
\leq C\left(|3Q^{\prime}|\left(\osc_{3Q^{\prime}}g\right)^{p}+E_{s}(v_{k},3Q^{\prime})+\diam^{p}(3Q^{\prime})\mathcal{E}(v_{k},3Q^{\prime})\right).

Clearly, we can also take
Q^{\prime\prime}=Q^{\prime}
. Consequently, by the triangle inequality,

\int_{Q^{\prime}}|G_{k}(c_{Q^{\prime\prime}})-G_{k}(c_{Q^{\prime}})|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(|3Q^{\prime}|\left(\osc_{3Q^{\prime}}g\right)^{p}+E_{s}(v_{k},3Q^{\prime})+\diam^{p}(3Q^{\prime})\mathcal{E}(v_{k},3Q^{\prime})\right).

If
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies
\|\zeta\|_{\ell_{\infty}}\leq l/t_{k}
, then the previous estimate implies more generally

\int_{Q^{\prime}}|G_{k}(x+\zeta)-G_{k}(x)|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\sum_{Q^{\prime\prime}\subset 3Q^{\prime}}\int_{Q^{\prime}}|G_{k}(c_{Q^{\prime\prime}})-G_{k}(c_{Q^{\prime}})|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx\\
\leq C\left(|3Q^{\prime}|\left(\osc_{3Q^{\prime}}g\right)^{p}+E_{s}(v_{k},3Q^{\prime})+\diam^{p}(3Q^{\prime})\mathcal{E}(v_{k},3Q^{\prime})\right).

(4.5)

Next, we let
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be arbitrary and set
m:=\lfloor\|(t_{k}/l)\zeta\|_{\ell_{\infty}}\rfloor
. Define

\mathcal{G}_{k}(\zeta):=\{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}:3Q^{\prime}\cup(\zeta+3Q^{\prime})\subset Q\}.

If
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
, then there exist vectors
\zeta_{0},\dots,\zeta_{m+1}
with
\|\zeta_{j}-\zeta_{j-1}\|_{\ell_{\infty}}\leq l/t_{k}
such that
\zeta_{0}=0
,
\zeta_{m+1}=\zeta
, and
c_{Q^{\prime}}+\zeta_{j}
is the center of some cube
Q^{\prime}_{j}
in
\mathcal{P}_{k}
for
j=1,\dots,m
. Setting
Q^{\prime}_{0}:=Q^{\prime}
and applying the triangle inequality gives

\int_{Q^{\prime}}|G_{k}(x+\zeta)-G_{k}(x)|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx\leq(m+1)^{p-1}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\int_{Q^{\prime}}|G_{k}(x+\zeta_{j+1})-G_{k}(x+\zeta_{j})|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx\\
\leq C(m+1)^{p-1}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\left(|3Q_{j}^{\prime}|\left(\osc_{3Q_{j}^{\prime}}g\right)^{p}+E_{s}(v_{k},3Q_{j}^{\prime})+\diam^{p}(3Q_{j}^{\prime})\mathcal{E}(v_{k},3Q_{j}^{\prime})\right).

(4.6)

Let
\mathcal{Q}_{k}(\zeta)
be the union of all cubes in
\mathcal{G}_{k}(\zeta)
. We sum (
4.6
) over all
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{k}(\zeta)
and note that, as
Q^{\prime}
ranges in
\mathcal{G}_{k}(\zeta)
, any cube in
\mathcal{P}_{k}
appears at most
C(m+1)
times on the right-hand side of the inequality, where
C
depends only on
d
. Hence,

\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{k}(\zeta)}|G_{k}(x+\zeta)-G_{k}(x)|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx\\
\leq C(m+1)^{p}\left(|Q|\max_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\left(\osc_{3Q^{\prime}}g\right)^{p}+E_{s}(v_{k},Q)+\max_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\diam^{p}(3Q^{\prime})\mathcal{E}(v_{k},Q)\right).

We observe that

\osc_{3Q^{\prime}}g\leq CL\frac{l}{t_{k}},\quad\diam(3Q^{\prime})\leq C\frac{l}{t_{k}}\quad\text{for all }Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{k},

(4.7)

where
C
depends only on
d
, and
L
is the Lipschitz constant of
g
. As a result,

\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{k}(\zeta)}|G_{k}(x+\zeta)-G_{k}(x)|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(\|\zeta\|_{\ell_{\infty}}+\frac{l}{t_{k}}\right)^{p}\left(|Q|L^{p}+\left(\frac{t_{k}}{l}\right)^{p}E_{s}(v_{k},Q)+\mathcal{E}(v_{k},Q)\right).

(4.8)

On the other hand, extending
G_{k}
by 0 to
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Q
, we see that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{Q}_{k}(\zeta)}|G_{k}(x+\zeta)-G_{k}(x)|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx\leq C|Q\setminus\mathcal{Q}_{k}(\zeta)|.

(4.9)

It is clear that

\lim_{\|\zeta\|_{\ell_{\infty}}\to 0}\limsup_{k\to\infty}|Q\setminus\mathcal{Q}_{k}(\zeta)|=0.

As
\varphi:(U,g)\rightarrow(Q,g)
is an isometry, we have

E_{s}(v_{k},Q)=E_{s}(u_{k},U),\quad\mathcal{E}(v_{k},Q)=\mathcal{E}(u_{k},U).

Now, we choose a sequence
(t_{k})
tending to infinity such that
\limsup_{k\to\infty}t_{k}^{p}E_{s}(u_{k},U)<\infty
. It follows from (
4.1
) that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}|Q|L^{p}+\left(\frac{t_{k}}{l}\right)^{p}E_{s}(v_{k},Q)+\mathcal{E}(v_{k},Q)<\infty.

Consequently, (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) imply

\lim_{\|\zeta\|_{\ell_{\infty}}\to 0}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|G_{k}(x+\zeta)-G_{k}(x)|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx=0.

Hence, by the Fréchet-Kolmogorov theorem, there exists a subsequence
(G_{k_{j}})
and a map
G:Q\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{D})
such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{Q}|G_{k_{j}}-G|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx=0.

From (
4.1
) and (
4.4
) we get
|D\bar{v}_{k}-G_{k}|_{g,\e_{D}}\to 0
in
L^{p}(Q)
. Therefore,
|D\bar{v}_{k_{j}}-G|_{g,\e_{D}}\to 0
in
L^{p}(Q)
as
j\to\infty
. Clearly,
G(x)\in\Ort(g_{x},\e_{D})
for a.e.
x\in Q
.

In order to prove the convergence of the sequence
(\bar{v}_{k_{j}})
we assume that the averages
(\bar{v}_{k_{j}})_{Q}
converge in
\iota(N)
. As
\iota(N)
is compact, there is no loss of generality. By the Poincaré inequality,

\int_{Q}|\bar{v}_{k_{j}}-\bar{v}_{k_{i}}|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(\int_{Q}|(\bar{v}_{k_{j}}-\bar{v}_{k_{i}})-(\bar{v}_{k_{j}}-\bar{v}_{k_{i}})_{Q}|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}+|(\bar{v}_{k_{j}}-\bar{v}_{k_{i}})_{Q}|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,dx\right)\\
\leq C\left(\int_{Q}|D\bar{v}_{k_{j}}-D\bar{v}_{k_{i}}|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}dx+|Q||(\bar{v}_{k_{j}})_{Q}-(\bar{v}_{k_{i}})_{Q}|^{p}\right).

Hence,
(\bar{v}_{k_{j}})
is a Cauchy sequence. Let
\bar{v}
be its limit. Clearly,
\bar{v}\in W^{1,p}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{D})
and
D\bar{v}=G
. Since
\bar{v}(x)\in\iota(N)
for a.e.
x\in Q
,
v:=\iota^{-1}\circ\bar{v}
is well-defined. It follows that
v\in W^{1,p}(Q;N)
and
dv_{x}\in\Ort((T_{x}\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{x}),(T_{u(x)}N,h_{u(x)}))
for a.e.
x\in Q
. Setting
u:=v\circ\varphi
concludes the proof.
∎

We are ready to prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

By Lemma
4.2
, there exist a subsequence of
(u_{k})
, not relabeled, and a Sobolev map
u\in W^{1,p}(M;N)
such that

\bar{u}_{k}\to\bar{u}\text{ in }W^{1,p}(M;\mathbb{R}^{D}),\quad du_{x}\in\Ort((T_{x}M,g_{x}),(T_{u(x)}N,h_{u(x)}))\text{ for a.e. }x\in M.

Since
\rank du_{x}=d
a.e. in
M
, the oriented unit normal
\nu_{u}
is well-defined a.e. in
M
. We show that
\bar{\nu}_{u}\in W^{1,p}(M;\mathbb{R}^{D})
, which proves that
u\in\Imm_{p}(M;N)
. Without loss of generality, we assume
\bar{u}_{k}\to\bar{u}
and
D\bar{u}_{k}\to D\bar{u}
pointwise a.e. in
M
.

We denote the subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{D}
canonically isomorphic to
T_{q}\iota(N)
by
V_{q}
. Set
r:=D-d-1
. Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
be open and let
n_{i}\in C^{\infty}(U;\mathbb{R}^{D})
for
i=1,\dots,r
such that
(n_{1}(q),\dots,n_{r}(q))
is a positively oriented orthonormal basis of
V_{q}^{\perp}
for all
q\in U\cap\iota(N)
. Assume

\bar{u}_{k}(x)\to\bar{u}(x),\quad D\bar{u}_{k}(x)\to D\bar{u}(x),\quad D\bar{u}(x)\in\Ort(g_{x},\e_{D})

(4.10)

for some
x\in\bar{u}^{-1}(U)
. Then

\lim_{k\to\infty}(\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}(x),n_{i}(\bar{u}(x))-n_{i}(\bar{u}_{k}(x)))=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}(x),n_{i}(\bar{u}_{k}(x)))=0\implies\lim_{k\to\infty}(\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}(x),n_{i}(\bar{u}(x)))=0.

On the other hand, if
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then

\lim_{k\to\infty}(\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}(x),D(\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{k})(x)v)=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}(x),D\bar{u}_{k}(x)v)=0\implies\lim_{k\to\infty}(\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}(x),D\bar{u}(x)v)=0.

Let
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})
be an orthonormal basis of
(\mathbb{R}^{d},g_{x})
. From
V_{\bar{u}(x)}=\lspan(\bar{\nu}_{u}(x))\oplus D\bar{u}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we obtain

1=|\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}(x)|^{2}=|(\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}(x),\bar{\nu}_{u}(x))|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}|(\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}(x),n_{i}(\bar{u}(x)))|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}|(\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}(x),D\bar{u}(x)v_{i})|^{2}\\
\implies\lim_{k\to\infty}|(\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}(x),\bar{\nu}_{u}(x))|=1.

Since
(D\bar{u}_{k}(x)v_{1},\dots,D\bar{u}_{k}(x)v_{d},\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}(x))
and
(D\bar{u}(x)v_{1},\dots,D\bar{u}(x)v_{d},\bar{\nu}_{u}(x))
are both positively oriented and
D\bar{u}_{k}(x)\to D\bar{u}(x)
, we get
(\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}(x),\bar{\nu}_{u}(x))\to 1
. This proves that
|\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}(x)-\bar{\nu}_{u}(x)|\to 0
. As (
4.10
) is satisfied for a.e.
x\in\bar{u}^{-1}(U)
, we conclude by the dominated convergence theorem that
\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}\to\bar{\nu}_{u}
in
L^{p}(\bar{u}^{-1}(U))
. Convergence in
L^{p}(M)
follows from the arbitrariness of
U
. Furthermore, by covering
M
with finitely many charts and applying Proposition
3.6
locally, we deduce

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{M}|D\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}C\mathcal{E}(u_{k})<\infty.

Hence,
\bar{\nu}_{u}\in W^{1,p}(M;\mathbb{R}^{D})
and
D\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}\rightharpoonup D\bar{\nu}_{u}
in
L^{p}
.

To conclude the proof, assume
\lim_{k\to\infty}E_{b}^{S}(u_{k})=0
. Let
W\subset M
be open and let
v_{i}\in C^{\infty}(W;TM)
for
i=1,\dots,d
such that
(v_{1}(x),\dots,v_{d}(x))
is a basis of
T_{x}M
, that is,
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})
is a frame in
W
. We show that
D\bar{u}(Sv_{i})=D\bar{u}(S_{u}v_{i})
a.e. in
W
. By Hölder’s inequality, we have
D\bar{u}_{k}\circ S\to D\bar{u}\circ S
in
L^{1}
. Thus, the hypothesis

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{M}|D\bar{u}_{k}\circ(S_{u_{k}}-S)|_{g,\e_{D}}^{p}\,d\vol_{g}=0

yields
P_{u_{k}}\circ D\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}=D\bar{u}_{k}\circ S_{u_{k}}\to D\bar{u}\circ S
in
L^{1}
. Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(W;\mathbb{R}^{D})
be an arbitrary vector field. Then

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{W}(P_{u_{k}}(D\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}v_{i}),\eta)\,d\vol_{g}=\int_{W}(D\bar{u}(Sv_{i}),\eta)\,d\vol_{g}

(4.11)

On the other hand,

\int_{W}(P_{u_{k}}(D\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}v_{i}),\eta)\,d\vol_{g}=\int_{W}(D\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}v_{i},P_{u_{k}}\eta)\,d\vol_{g}.

Since
\bar{u}_{k}\to\bar{u}
pointwise a.e. in
M
,
P_{u_{k}}\eta\to P_{u}\eta
in
L^{q}
by the dominated convergence theorem. Thus, the weak convergence of
D\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}
implies

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{W}(P_{u_{k}}(D\bar{\nu}_{u_{k}}v_{i}),\eta)\,d\vol_{g}=\int_{W}(D\bar{\nu}_{u}v_{i},P_{u}\eta)\,d\vol_{g}\\
=\int_{W}(P_{u}(D\bar{\nu}_{u}v_{i}),\eta)\,d\vol_{g}=\int_{W}(D\bar{u}(S_{u}v_{i}),\eta)\,d\vol_{g}.

Hence, from (
4.11
) and the arbitrariness of
\eta
, we obtain
D\bar{u}(Sv_{i})=D\bar{u}(S_{u}v_{i})
a.e. in
W
. As
D\bar{u}
is injective a.e. in
W
, we conclude that
S=S_{u}
a.e. in
W
. Every point in
M
has a neighborhood with a frame, so that
S=S_{u}
holds a.e. in
M
.
∎
