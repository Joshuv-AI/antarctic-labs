Effective schemes for fusion of hyperentangled W W states
Wen-Xiu Zhang 1 , Wen-Qiang Liu 2 , and Hai-Rui Wei 1,

Abstract
Hyperentangled states are fascinating resources in quantum information processing as they can significantly increase the channel capacity and enhance noise resistance. We explore a hyperfusion mechanism to fuse one n n -photon hyper- W W state and one m m -photon hyper- W W state into a large-scale ( n + m − 2 ) (n+m-2) -photon hyper- W W state. Another mechanism to fuse one n n -photon hyper- W W state, one m m -photon hyper- W W state, and one t t -photon hyper- W W state into an ( n + m + t − 3 ) (n+m+t-3) -photon hyper- W W state is also proposed. These two hyperfusion mechanisms are constructed employing only polarizing beam splitters, balanced beam splitters, half-wave plates, single-photon detectors, and cross-Kerr nonlinearities. Conditional quantum gates, path couplers, and ancillary photons are not required in our constructions. Moreover, our fused W W states are hyperentangled in the polarization and spatial degrees of freedom of single-photon systems. The presence of only one garbage output state demonstrates that high eﬃciency can be achieved in our schemes.

IV.1
Success probabilities of the schemes

In this section, we evaluate the success probabilities of our two-fusion and three-fusion protocols.
Based on Sec.
II
, one can see that the success probability of the desired two-fused large-scale hyper-
W
state
|W_{n+m-2}\rangle_{P}\otimes|W_{n+m-2}\rangle_{S}
is

\displaystyle P_{\text{S}}=\frac{(n+m-2)^{2}}{n^{2}m^{2}}.

(25)

The failure probability of the two-fusion protocol (the garbage state) is

\displaystyle P_{\text{F}}=\frac{1}{n^{2}m^{2}}.

(26)

The success probability of the two-fusion partially successful state and partially recyclable states is given by

\displaystyle P_{\text{PS,PR}}=1-\frac{(n+m-2)^{2}}{n^{2}m^{2}}-\frac{1}{n^{2}m^{2}}.

(27)

Based on Sec.
III
, we can see that the success probability of the desired large-scale hyper-
W
state
|W_{n+m+t-3}\rangle_{P}\otimes|W_{n+m+t-3}\rangle_{S}
is

\displaystyle P_{\text{S}}^{\prime}=\frac{(n+m+t-3)^{2}}{n^{2}m^{2}t^{2}}.

(28)

The failure probability of the three-fusion protocol is

\displaystyle P_{\text{F}}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{n^{2}m^{2}t^{2}}.

(29)

The success probability of the three-fusion partially successful state and partially recyclable states is

\displaystyle P_{\text{PS,PR}}^{\prime}=1-\frac{(n+m+t-3)^{2}}{n^{2}m^{2}t^{t}}-\frac{1}{n^{2}m^{2}t^{2}}.

(30)

IV.2
Imperfections arising from linear optics

BSs, PBSs, HWPs, and single-photon detectors are the necessary ingredients in our two hyper-
W
fusion schemes.
Hence, in practical applications, the imperfections arising from these linear optical elements will cause the real output states to deviate from their ideal counterpart, and they will also slightly reduce the fidelity of the fused hyper-
W
states.
The transformations of the imperfect PBS can be given by
imperfection

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} U_{\text{PBS}}|H\rangle=&\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+r}}[(\cos\theta-\sqrt{r}\sin\theta)|H\rangle\\
&-(\sin\theta+\sqrt{r}^{*}\cos\theta)|V\rangle],\end{aligned}

(31)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} U_{\text{PBS}}|V\rangle=&\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+r}}[(\sin\theta+\sqrt{r}\cos\theta)|H\rangle\\
&+(\cos\theta-\sqrt{r}^{*}\sin\theta)|V\rangle],\end{aligned}

(32)

where
\theta
is the deviation of the mirror mounts and
r
is the polarization extinction ratio of the PBS.

The imperfection in the transmission ratio of the balanced BS, denoted by
\epsilon
modifies the transformations of the 50:50 BS as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} U_{\text{BS}}|\textbf{0}\rangle_{S}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}+2\epsilon+2}}[(1+\epsilon)|\textbf{0}\rangle_{S}+|\textbf{1}\rangle_{S}],\end{aligned}

(33)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} U_{\text{BS}}|\textbf{1}\rangle_{S}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}+2\epsilon+2}}[|\textbf{0}\rangle_{S}-(1+\epsilon)|\textbf{1}\rangle_{S}].\end{aligned}

(34)

In additional to the BS and PBS, the detector dark count (1.2%), two-photon events (1.2%), and imperfections in the polarization HWP plate (0.8%) also effect the fidelity. We believe that these issues will be mitigated by future developments.

V
conclusion

In conclusion, we have proposed two practical hyperfusion schemes for hyper-
W
states via weak cross-Kerr nonlinearities.
The computation qubits are encoded in the polarization and spatial DOFs of single-photon systems.
The two-fusion protocol can fuse one
n
-photon hyper-
W
state and one
m
-photon hyper-
W
state into an
(n+m-2)
-photon hyper-
W
state,
while the three-fusion protocol can fuse one
n
-photon hyper-
W
state, one
m
-photon hyper-
W
state, and one
t
-photon hyper-
W
state into an
(n+m+t-3)
-photon hyper-
W
state.
Unlike previous fusion schemes, which are limited to single-DOF systems such as polarization or spin,
our fused
W
states are hyperentangled in both polarization and spatial DOFs.
Our schemes are more efficient, producing only one output garbage state.
The constructions of the two-fused and three-fused hyper-
W
states employ PBSs, BSs, HWPs, single-photon detectors, and cross-kerr nonlinearities;
quantum conditional controlled gates and additional single photons are not required.
Designing schemes to fuse four, five, or more hyper-
W
states simultaneously, as well as developing schemes to fuse hyper-
W
states without qubits loss, will be addressed in our future work.
