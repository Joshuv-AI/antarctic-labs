Spatiotemporal evolution and influencing factors of the coupling coordination between Agricultural Digitalization and Agricultural New Quality Productive Forces
Authors: Yuan Wang, Xiaoyuan Zhao, Chengxue Yang, Min Su
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe mutually reinforcing and coordinated development of Agricultural Digitalization (AD) and Agricultural New Quality Productive Forces (ANQPF) is an important path to achieving the modernization of agriculture and rural areas. Utilizing panel data from 31 provinces spanning 2012 to 2023, this study employs an Improved Coupling Coordination Degree Model (ICCDM), an Obstacle Degree Model (ODM), and a Random Effects Panel Tobit Model to investigate the spatiotemporal evolution patterns and the internal and external determinants of the Coupling Coordination Degree (CCD) between AD and ANQPF. The results indicate that: (1) From 2012 to 2023, both AD and ANQPF exhibited a steady upward trend, yet their overall development levels remained relatively low. (2) During this period, the CCD between the two systems evolved from moderately uncoordinated development to barely coordinated development, forming a distinct spatial pattern characterized by “the Eastern region leading, the Central region tackling key challenges, and the Western region catching up.” (3) Internet penetration rate, leisure agriculture, and other factors are key obstacles affecting the coupling coordination, displaying heterogeneous characteristics across different regions. (4) Economic Development Level, Agricultural Development Level, Rural Consumption Level, Fiscal Support Intensity, Openness To Foreign Trade, and Agricultural Security Level significantly improve the CCD between AD and ANQPF, whereas Industrial Development Level has a significant negative effect on the CCD. Furthermore, external influencing factors demonstrated notable regional variations. Consequently, implementing targeted policies and region-targeted guidance represents an imperative pathway for fostering the coordinated development of AD and ANQPF.Keywords: Agricultural Digitalization, Agricultural New Quality Productive Forces, Improved Coupling Coordination Degree Model, Random effects panel Tobit model, Targeted policySubject terms: Development studies, Environmental social sciences

Introduction

Currently, China is at the “Lewis Turning Point” of the dualistic development structure1. The development paradigm of agriculture and rural areas urgently needs to be transformed from the spillover paradigm relying on non-farming activities to the paradigm of autonomous endogenous development. It is also necessary to introduce higher-level modernization elements from non-farming parts of the rural economy and from urban areas2. In the digital era, the deep integration of digital technologies into the agricultural sector has become essential to promote the economic and social transformation of agriculture and rural areas. In recent years, China’s central government has introduced policy documents such as the Strategic Plan for Digital Rural Development and the Plan for Digital Agriculture and Rural Development (2019–2025). These policies embed Agricultural Digitalization (AD) within the broader agenda of agricultural modernization. They aim to deploy information and intelligent technologies to improve rural development conditions3, raise agricultural productivity, and promote industrial integration4. In doing so, they help foster new growth drivers for the agricultural economies5. Nevertheless, the foundation of AD in China remains underdeveloped. According to the China Digital Economy Development Report (2024) released by the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology, the digital economy penetration rate of the primary industry stood at only 10.78% in 2023, significantly lagging behind the industrial and service sectors. In this context, the 2025 China’s Central Government Document No. 1 explicitly calls for leveraging technological innovation to drive the agglomeration of production factors and fostering Agricultural New Quality Productive Forces (ANQPF) in a region-specific manner. ANQPF catalyzes new industries and business models through revolutionary technological breakthroughs6 and enhances resource allocation efficiency via innovative factor allocation7. By driving qualitative, efficiency, and dynamic transformations, ANQPF fosters an efficient, sustainable, and inclusive modern agricultural system8, thereby facilitating the full penetration of digital technologies across rural areas. On the one hand, AD deeply embeds core digital technologies, such as the Internet of Things and artificial intelligence, into agricultural production processes. It empowers agricultural labor and means of production, enables optimal factor allocation and innovation in production modes5, and thereby fosters the formation and development of ANQPF. On the other hand, ANQPF reshapes the foundation of productive forces through technological change, optimizes production relations via institutional innovation, and activates industrial ecosystems through systemic synergy. This provides the material basis required for the substantive advancement of digitalization. Accordingly, investigating the dynamic interplay between AD and ANQPF and examining the spatiotemporal evolution of their coupling coordination and its determinants not only deepens the understanding of their reciprocal relationship, but also offers important theoretical insights and practical guidance. Such efforts strengthen AD’s contribution to ANQPF and support the construction of a new system for high-quality agricultural development in China.

The extant literature on AD has predominantly focused on its strategic value, prevailing constraints, driving forces, and corresponding policy implications, yielding substantial scholarly outcomes. Specifically, AD not only reshapes the operational paradigms of traditional industries9 but also enhances rural endogenous development momentum, thereby accelerating the modernization of agriculture and rural areas10. In practice, the digital transformation of agriculture continues to face multiple constraints, including weak interest linkage mechanisms11, structurally imbalanced income distribution12, and the marginalization of smallholders13–15. Scholars widely recognize that policy support for digital transformation11,16,17, agricultural value chain integration18, and the emergence of new business entities19 constitute pivotal drivers in steering AD toward higher quality and efficiency. Aligned with the strategic imperatives of precision and smart agriculture20, advancing AD requires the synchronous enhancement of infrastructure11, institutional refinement21, entrepreneurial cultivation22, and the delivery of efficient socialized services23, ultimately steering integrated actors toward scale-appropriate intensification. In contrast to the well-established discourse on AD, ANQPF remains a nascent concept. While extant scholarship has extensively examined ANQPF across corporate operations24–28, marine ecosystems29, and urban-regional innovation systems30,31, its application to agriculture remains conspicuously underdeveloped. Current inquiries are largely confined to delineating its conceptual boundaries and identifying key drivers. ANQPF embodies a dual logic encompassing both material and social attributes. It represents a multi-dimensional breakthrough characterized by the coordination of the material and spiritual spheres, as well as the interplay between natural transformation and social progress, reflecting a systemic shift in the synergistic evolution of production technologies and organizational models32. Scholars have operationalized the conceptual framework of ANQPF into quantitative metrics. Drawing on the classic tripartite factor model of production, Cao et al. construct a measurement index system comprising three dimensions: new-quality labor, new-quality means of labor, and new-quality objects of labor33. Alternatively, adopting a contemporary lens, Li et al. develop a comprehensive evaluation system centered on technological advancement, green development, and digitalization34. Existing studies primarily explore the impact of industrial agglomeration35, digital financial inclusion36,37, urban-rural integration38, and the diffusion of digital technologies39, which shape the emergence and growth of ANQPF. However, the nexus between AD and ANQPF remains underexplored in the extant literature, with empirical inquiries largely confined to causal identification strategies. On the one hand, AD significantly fosters the development of ANQPF by driving agricultural modernization40. On the other hand, ANQPF reciprocally empowers the evolution of AD41. Furthermore, the synergistic development of AD and greening promotes the upgrading of ANQPF through the optimization of industrial structure, stimulation of technological innovation, and enhancement of resource allocation efficiency42,43.

In summary, while prior scholarship provides valuable references for this study, there is a paucity of literature that systematically measures the Coupling Coordination Degree (CCD) between AD and ANQPF, as well as its underlying driving factors. Accordingly, this study employs a combined entropy-AHP method to assess the development levels of AD and ANQPF across 31 Chinese provinces from 2012 to 2023. An Improved Coupling Coordination Degree Model (ICCDM) is utilized to analyze the spatiotemporal evolution of their coupling coordination. Furthermore, the Obstacle Degree Model (ODM) and the Random Effects Panel Tobit Model are applied to identify internal barriers within heterogeneous regional CCD systems and external influencing factors, respectively, aiming to provide policy insights for achieving synergistic upgrading between AD and ANQPF across diverse regions.

The potential marginal contributions of this study are as follows: (1) Extending the research scope of New Quality Productive Forces from urban and firm levels to the agricultural sector, shifting the analytical focus from mere mechanism analysis to an in-depth exploration of influencing factors.(2) Diverging from conventional studies that predominantly emphasize causal inference between AD and ANQPF, this study proposes a dual-interaction logic between the two. It elucidates the underlying mechanisms by integrating the Theory of Transforming Traditional Agriculture with the Rural Innovation Systems theory, offering a holistic examination of their synergistic co-evolution in dynamic development, thereby addressing the limitations of unilateral causal inference in the existing literature. (3) By diagnosing the spatiotemporal distribution characteristics of the CCD between AD and ANQPF and identifying the influencing factors across heterogeneous regions, this study provides practical guidance and scientific references for the current national agenda of “deeply implementing the strategies of regional coordinated development, major regional initiatives, and functional zoning, while optimizing the layout of major productive forces.”

Theoretical analysis

Conceptualization of Agricultural Digitalization, Agricultural New Quality Productive Forces, and their coupling

AD, as a product of the deep integration of information technology and the agricultural industry44,45, specifically encompasses the construction of digital infrastructure, the transformation of production and operation models, the evolution of development concepts, the restructuring of industrial organizations, and the upgrading of labor skills. One of its core connotations lies in shaping a precise and scientific agricultural production model oriented toward visualization, quantification, and datafication46. By enabling precise control over critical agricultural processes such as fertilization, irrigation, and sowing, AD disrupts the traditional smallholder production paradigm dominated by vague experience, thereby enhancing total factor productivity in agriculture44. Consequently, the digital transformation of agriculture can be fundamentally characterized as a paradigm shift from experience-based to precision-based decision-making. ANQPF represent the extension of the concept of new quality productive forces into the agricultural sector and serve as a key driver of agricultural modernization and rural revitalization. Characterized by the application of advanced agricultural technologies and oriented toward high quality, high efficiency, and high performance47, ANQPF enhance agricultural total factor productivity through the optimized recombination of three fundamental elements, new-quality labor, new-quality means of labor, and new-quality objects of labor48. Specifically, new-quality labor entails a transition from experience-based farmers to highly skilled, knowledge-intensive agricultural workers; new-quality means of labor, enabled by disruptive innovations such as big data and the Internet of Things, allow for precise, intelligent production processes; and new-quality objects of labor extend beyond traditional natural resources to include data assets and synthetic biological materials, evolving toward greener and more sustainable forms48,49. The coupling relationship between AD and ANQPF refers to the dynamic transformation and interaction formed by the two subsystems through factor mobility, information transmission, and energy conversion. The coordination relationship denotes a benign and orderly state of dynamic development shaped by this coupled interaction, which further drives the integrated and coordinated development of the two systems, ultimately realizing the optimization and upgrading of overall system functions.

Analysis of the coupling and coordination mechanism between Agricultural Digitalization and Agricultural New Quality Productive Forces

Agricultural Digitalization promotes the development of Agricultural New Quality Productive Forces

Productive forces are the primary drivers of human social development and the ultimate factors underlying all social and political transformations50. ANQPF represents an advanced stage in the evolution of agricultural productive forces, the formation and development of which require adaptive adjustments in the relations of production and institutional arrangements. The theory of transforming traditional agriculture posits that the technological stagnation inherent in traditional agriculture dictates the introduction of modern productive factors as a prerequisite for achieving agricultural modernization51. Accordingly, this study elucidates the theoretical logic through which digitalization promotes the development of ANQPF from the perspectives of infrastructure, the enabling development environment, green development, and human resources. First, digital infrastructure serves as the foundational support for the development of ANQPF39. As Rostow argued, infrastructure constitutes a prerequisite for economic growth and broader social transformation52. Thus, the digital development of infrastructure broadens information and communication channels and accelerates the dissemination and diffusion of innovative technologies and advanced knowledge in rural areas53. Next, the development environment provides direction for ANQPF. AD builds digital development environments, including online sales platforms, logistics systems, and live-streaming channels. Through the integration of internal and external rural resources, it expands farmers’ livelihood space and fosters the growth of new quality productivity54. Thirdly, green development constitutes a basic requirement for ANQPF. The digital transformation of agriculture can facilitate breakthroughs in green technologies and the dissemination and adoption of green innovations, thereby propelling agriculture toward green, low-carbon, and circular development55, enhancing agricultural green total factor productivity, and further promoting the development of ANQPF56. Finally, a rich reservoir of human capital constitutes the pivotal agent in the formation and evolution of ANQPF47. Marx notes in Capital that “productivity is, of course, always the productivity of useful, concrete labor.”57 The realization of productive capacity necessarily presupposes human participation and agency, expressed through “useful, concrete labor” by which humans mediate material transformations with nature. Digital talents, equipped with strong digital literacy, can diffuse skills and practices to traditional farmers and cultivate a new cohort of skilled farmers58. This, in turn, deepens the integration of digital concepts and technologies into agricultural development and strengthens the human resources foundation for the advancement of ANQPF56.

Agricultural New Quality Productive Forces Exerts a feedback-driven effect on Agricultural Digitalization

According to rural innovation systems theory59,60, rural revitalization requires the formulation of holistic innovation policies. These policies coordinate agricultural technological innovation, institutional and managerial innovation, as well as community networks and intermediary platform innovation, aiming to enhance the overall capacity of the rural innovation system and promote agricultural modernization. Within the context of rural innovation and development, ANQPF systematically reshapes the structure and allocation patterns of traditional agricultural production factors through the deep coupling of new-quality labor, new-quality means of labor, and new-quality objects of labor. This restructuring occurs through the deep coupling of new-quality labor, new-quality means of production, and new-quality objects of labor, representing an important manifestation of the rural innovation system. Consequently, ANQPF enables agriculture to achieve a transition from “old-quality” to “new-quality” development, strengthens the agricultural system’s capacity to absorb, integrate, and apply digital technologies, and thereby enhances the level of AD41. Drawing on the “three elements” approach to productive forces, this study explains how ANQPF can propel AD through changes in laborers, means of labor, and objects of labor. First, new-quality labor serves as a core driver of AD by strengthening innovation-oriented social networks and organizations, representing the modernization of agricultural production agents. ANQPF improves skill formation and helps attract young workers back to rural areas61. This helps cultivate talents’ innovation and entrepreneurial capabilities and contributes to a sufficient, high-quality talent pool for agricultural digital transformation. Second, as an important carrier of technological innovation, new-quality means of labor provide the material foundation for AD62. They include new production tools and new input factors63. On the one hand, agricultural technological innovation, guided by the goal of green and sustainable development, facilitates the application of emerging technologies in agricultural production, such as biological breeding, remote sensing monitoring, and crop growth diagnosis. These technologies improve agricultural production efficiency while reducing excessive reliance on chemical inputs, including pesticides. On the other hand, the innovative development of new-quality means of labor promotes the research, development, and application of new agricultural machinery. This provides essential material support for large-scale, intensive, and intelligent agricultural production, thereby accelerating the digital transformation of agricultural production systems. Finally, ANQPF promotes the transformation of new-quality objects of labor from traditional natural resources to digital resources, brand resources, and data factors. As the practical foundation of AD, new-quality objects of labor represent the expansion of traditional agricultural production objects into a data-driven production factor system under digitalization. This transformation enhances the dependence of agricultural production activities on digital platforms, information platforms, and data platforms. Meanwhile, through intelligent management of water, fertilizers, climate, soil, and other elements, these objects enable precision agricultural operations64, strengthen digital and smart farmland management, and further accelerate agricultural digital transformation. The two thus form a synergistic evolution mechanism characterized by spiral advancement (see Fig. 1).

Research design

Indicator system construction and data sources

Establishment of the Agricultural Digitalization evaluation system

Drawing on the theory of traditional agricultural transformation, and integrating relevant studies65–68, this study constructs a measurement system based on the principles of scientific rigor, comprehensiveness, data availability, and independence. The evaluation framework encompasses four dimensions: agricultural digital infrastructure, development environment, green development, and human resources. Among these, agricultural digital infrastructure serves as the foundation for AD. It primarily captures the status of telecommunications infrastructure and information accessibility in rural areas, encompassing metrics such as internet penetration, mobile phone ownership, and optical cable construction. The agricultural digital development environment serves as a prerequisite for digital transformation. It encompasses the foundational ecosystem supporting information technology services, scientific research, and rural development. This dimension is operationalized through five indicators: e-commerce development, digital payment adoption, meteorological observation capabilities, and the application of advanced technologies and equipment. It is worth noting that meteorological observation, as an important sensing node of the agricultural Internet of Things, can collect agricultural environmental data such as temperature and precipitation in real time, providing fundamental data support for precision agriculture. However, this indicator mainly reflects the construction of environmental sensing infrastructure at the early stage of agricultural production and has limited coverage of IoT deployment in the midstream and downstream stages of agricultural production. Green development serves as the enduring safeguard for the high-quality advancement of AD, determining the sustainability of agricultural production activities. This dimension addresses environmental performance, specifically proxied by the intensity of pesticide, chemical fertilizer, and plastic film application. Human resources constitute a pivotal safeguard for AD, underpinned by local educational investment and the size of the agricultural labor force. Accordingly, this study develops a comprehensive evaluation index system for agricultural digital transformation, comprising 13 indicators nested within the aforementioned 4 dimensions (see Table 1).

Establishment of the Agricultural New Quality Productive Forces evaluation system

Grounded in Marxist production theory, this study develops an evaluation index system. Drawing on prior studies49,56,69, the framework conceptualizes agricultural digital transformation through the lens of ANQPF, specifically encompassing three core elements: new-quality laborers, new-quality means of production, and new-quality subjects of labor. Grounded in the technological revolution, labor capacity has been significantly augmented. In particular, new-quality laborers equipped with specialized expertise and innovation capabilities act as pivotal leaders in advancing ANQPF. Consequently, this dimension is operationalized through a composite measure of labor quality and labor capability. Means of production represent the integration of technological elements, manifesting as both physical natural entities and immaterial scientific advancements and applications. Consequently, this dimension is evaluated across two domains: the material means of production driven by technological progress, such as roads, water conservancy, and energy infrastructure, and the intangible means of production encompassing R&D and digital application. Random effects panel Tobit model

The dependent variable, the CCD between AD and ANQPF, is a continuous non-negative variable truncated at 0 and 1. Given that the coupling coordination degree is a bounded continuous variable, several models, including OLS, Beta panel models, and Tobit models, can be employed for estimation. Although Beta regression is specifically designed for continuous variables restricted to the interval (0,1), its application in empirical research remains relatively limited, especially for panel data analysis. In contrast, OLS models do not adequately consider the bounded characteristics of the dependent variable, which may affect the reliability of parameter estimates. Therefore, this study employs the Tobit model, which is more appropriate for handling limited dependent variables, to examine the influencing factors of the coupling coordination between AD and ANQPF. Given this data structure, the panel Tobit model is typically employed to obtain consistent estimators72. To circumvent the issue that the fixed-effects panel Tobit model fails to yield consistent and unbiased estimates73. Considering previous studies69,74 and the current state of rural development in China, this study selects influencing factors from four dimensions: economy, society, environment, and nature. The specific variables are selected as follows: (1) Economic Development Level. Economic foundations directly influence the investment capacity in AD and the provision of public services. Therefore, this study uses the logarithm of per capita regional GDP as a proxy variable to measure the resource support capacity for rural digital development. (2) Industrial Development Level. Rural industrial development reflects the level of local industrial upgrading and serves as an important driver of ANQPF development. Therefore, this study uses the ratio of the secondary industry’s added value to regional GDP as a proxy variable for Industrial Development Level. (3) Agricultural Development Level. Rural agricultural development directly reflects the improvement of ANQPF under the application of digital technologies. Therefore, this study uses the ratio of the primary industry’s added value to regional GDP as a proxy variable for Agricultural Development Level. (4) Urban-rural Income Gap. The income gap affects the integration of production factors in agricultural development and AD by constraining the balanced allocation of urban and rural resources. Therefore, this study uses the logarithm of the ratio of urban per capita income to rural per capita income to measure the Urban-rural Income Gap. (5) Urbanization Rate. The process of urbanization promotes the integration of agriculture, culture, and tourism through population mobility and the upgrading of consumption demand, thereby facilitating the development of ANQPF. Therefore, this study uses the ratio of urban population to total population to measure the Urbanization Rate. (6) Rural Consumption Level. As one of the three major drivers of economic growth, consumption is an important indicator reflecting the expansion of rural market demand and the improvement of living standards. Therefore, this study uses the logarithm of per capita living consumption expenditure of rural residents to measure the Rural Consumption Level. (7) Fiscal Support Intensity. Government policy guidance influences the development direction of AD and ANQPF. Therefore, this study uses the logarithm of general public budget expenditure to reflect the intensity of government fiscal investment. (8) Openness To Foreign Trade. Openness to foreign trade facilitates the development of AD and ANQPF by promoting the adoption of advanced foreign technologies and management practices and attracting high-quality talent. Therefore, this study uses the ratio of total imports and exports to regional GDP to measure the level of openness to foreign trade. (9) Agricultural Security Level. As an important component of the modern agricultural risk management system, agricultural insurance provides support for the development of ANQPF through risk diversification and loss compensation mechanisms. (10) Agricultural Disaster Rate. Agricultural production and development are highly dependent on the natural environment. Therefore, this study uses the ratio of disaster-affected area to cultivated land area to measure the Agricultural Disaster Rate. The specific indicators are presented in Table 4.

Data sources and processing

To ensure data continuity and comparability, this study adopts panel data from China’s 31 provinces, municipalities, and autonomous regions (excluding Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan) spanning 2012 to 2023. Beginning with the pivotal policy moment in 2012 when China’s “Document No. 1” explicitly proposed for the first time the comprehensive advancement of rural and agricultural informatization. The Rural Digital Inclusive Finance Mobile Payment Index and Regional Innovation and Entrepreneurship Index are sourced from the Digital Finance Research Center and Big Data Research Center at Peking University. The remaining data are sourced from the China Statistical Yearbook, China Rural Statistical Yearbook, China Fiscal Statistical Yearbook, China Water Resources Statistical Yearbook, China Social Statistical Yearbook, the EPS database, and provincial statistical yearbooks. For a small portion of missing data, linear interpolation or averaging is used to fill in the gaps.

Empirical results analysis

Spatiotemporal evolution analysis of Agricultural Digitalization

By normalizing the raw data of the indicator system and applying the combined entropy-AHP method to measure the AD levels of China and 31 provinces (municipalities), this study obtains the temporal evolution results for the national and three major regional levels (see Fig. 2) and the provincial temporal dynamics (see Fig. 3). For the provincial temporal analysis, four time points were selected: 2012, 2016, 2020, and 2023. China’s AD level exhibits an overall upward trend, with the index rising from 0.204 in 2012 to 0.387 in 2023, representing an average annual growth rate of 8.16%. This change is largely driven by the implementation of the Digital Village Pilot Policy and national policy support for AD. However, constrained by insufficient penetration of agricultural digital technologies, limited technology absorption capacity of business entities, and the unbalanced allocation of urban-rural factor resources, the overall growth margin of AD remains to be further improved.

In terms of spatial distribution, China’s AD exhibits an evolutionary pattern characterized by “overall improvement coupled with gradient differentiation.” Although the inclusive effect of regional digital development has gradually emerged, the stepwise disparity pattern of “Eastern > Central > Western” persists through 2023. (1) Benefiting from a robust economic foundation, well-established digital infrastructure, and optimized resource allocation, the Yangtze River Delta region has formed a dual-core structure led by Jiangsu and Zhejiang, radiating outward to drive the eastern coastal areas and adjacent central provinces into the medium-to-high echelon of AD development. (2) The issuance of the Several Opinions of the State Council on Vigorously Implementing the Strategy for the Rise of the Central Region in 2012 has facilitated the modernization of agriculture in the central region. By establishing modern agricultural demonstration zones, the region pursues the development of high-yield, high-quality, efficient, eco-friendly, and safe agriculture. Empowered by supportive policies and technological advances, the central region’s AD level ranks second only to that of the eastern region. Henan Province, by implementing the Broadband Central Plains strategy and absorbing the spillover effects from the Yangtze River Delta, achieved rapid progress in AD starting from 2012 and extended its influence to neighboring provinces by 2016, demonstrating the positive driving role of national strategic initiatives in regional AD development.(3) Although the western region has made notable progress, constrained by factors such as geographical location, population agglomeration, and industrial upgrading, the AD levels in some provinces such as Tibet and Ningxia have remained persistently low. An overall spatial gradient of “Eastern leadership, Central quality enhancement, and Western catch-up” has formed. This reflects the agglomeration patterns of digital factors while highlighting the critical roles of policy intervention and geographical location in narrowing regional disparities, indicating that the development of AD involves both notable achievements and persistent challenges.

Spatiotemporal evolution analysis of Agricultural New Quality Productive Forces

By applying the combined entropy-AHP method to calculate the ANQPF level, this study obtains the temporal evolution results for the national and three major regional levels (see Fig. 4) and the provincial temporal dynamics for 2012, 2016, 2020, and 2023 (see Fig. 5). China’s ANQPF exhibits an overall upward trend, with the index rising from 0.106 in 2012 to 0.237 in 2023, registering an average annual growth rate of 11.23%. However, the overall development level remains relatively low. On one hand, although the level of AD and smartification has improved and the coverage of agricultural science and technology has expanded, the current situation remains characterized by broad yet shallow and extensive yet imprecise application. Consequently, the core competitiveness of ANQPF warrants further enhancement. On the other hand, China’s agricultural development is constrained by structural issues, including a high proportion of smallholder farming, lagging digital awareness among business entities, a fragile rural economic foundation, and insufficient application of agricultural technology. Addressing these constraints requires regions to formulate tailored strategies based on local conditions to accelerate the cultivation and development of ANQPF.

In terms of spatial distribution, ANQPF exhibits a pronounced spatially imbalanced pattern of “Eastern > Central > Western.” Although ANQPF levels have improved across all regions, the developmental advantage of certain eastern coastal provinces has been continuously reinforced, with a significant Matthew effect observed. (1) In the eastern region, ANQPF shows a clear overall upward trend, except for a temporary plateau during 2020–2021 caused by the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic. Notably, a clear bipolar divergence in ANQPF development is evident within the region. Jiangsu and Shandong have consistently maintained their leading positions, possessing greater resources for agricultural R&D, technology extension, and infrastructure construction, which more effectively drives the advancement of agricultural modernization. By contrast, in cities such as Shanghai, affected by the shift in the focal point of urban economic development, agricultural functions have gradually transitioned toward an urban-oriented model, resulting in a relatively low level of ANQPF. (2) Located primarily on the Central Plains, the central region benefits from favorable agricultural resources and a high degree of scale-based, organized farming. Supported by substantial fiscal and financial assistance for agriculture, it enables large-scale intelligent mechanization, integrated agricultural IoT deployment, and online marketing of agricultural products. Consequently, the region demonstrates relatively strong endogenous momentum for the development of ANQPF. In recent years, with the implementation of the Central Plains Granary Initiative and the Digital Farmland Infrastructure Project, the central region has witnessed a marked acceleration in ANQPF development, exhibiting distinct regional inclusive characteristics. (3) Characterized predominantly by mountainous terrain and plateaus, the western region faces a high degree of fragmented and decentralized agricultural production, where mechanized operations are severely restricted, resulting in a relatively low level of ANQPF. However, leveraging unique natural and geographical conditions, the southeastern periphery of the region has developed competitive industries in grain, livestock, and fruits, promoting the refinement of agriculture and deep processing, thereby significantly enhancing the level of ANQPF.

Spatiotemporal characteristics of coupling coordination degree between Agricultural Digitalization and Agricultural New Quality Productive Forces

This study employs an ICCDM to calculate the coupling coordination level between AD and ANQPF, mapping the temporal evolution trends for the national and three major regional levels from 2012 to 2023 (see Fig. 6), as well as the provincial temporal dynamics for the years 2012, 2016, 2020, and 2023 (see Fig. 7). As the study period progressed, the average CCD between AD and ANQPF in China increased from 0.351 in 2012 to 0.523 in 2023, marking a transition from moderate uncoordinated to barely coordinated development. Based on its evolutionary trajectory, the overall development of the CCD can be divided into two distinct stages. The first stage is the slow-growth period (2012–2016), during which the CCD rose from 0.351 to 0.406, with an average annual growth rate of approximately 3.88%. The second stage is the accelerated-growth period (2016–2023), during which the degree increased from 0.420 to 0.523, with an average annual growth rate of approximately 3.91%. The second stage’s average annual growth rate exceeds that of the first stage. This is attributed to the issuance of the National Agricultural Modernization Plan (2016–2020) and the “Internet +” Modern Agriculture Three-Year Action Implementation Plan in 2016. These documents provided a systemic framework for AD and high-quality development, placing them on an institutionalized development track. Consequently, this effectively bolstered the development of ANQPF, leading to a significant enhancement in the synergy level between the two sectors from that point onward. Nevertheless, some provinces remain in a state of uncoordinated development. This may be attributed to the fact that their agriculture still relies heavily on primary production and initial processing, lacking sufficient digital penetration. Consequently, these regions fail to effectively guide the innovative integration and development of technologies, business models, and production factors, preventing the realization of a virtuous interaction between the two systems.

In terms of spatial distribution, the coupling coordination level exhibits an overall gradient pattern of progressive decline from east to west and from coastal to inland areas. By 2023, the regional development landscape had evolved into a hierarchical ranking of “Eastern > Central > Western,” forming a developmental dynamic characterized by “the Eastern region leading, the Central region tackling key challenges, and the Western region catching up.” (1) Benefiting from a robust rural economy, sophisticated digital infrastructure, advanced industrial structures, and mature market mechanisms, the eastern region maintains a leading position in coupling coordination levels, with this dominance being particularly prominent in provinces such as Shandong, Zhejiang, and Jiangsu. These provinces entered a coordinated development stage as early as 2016 and have consistently served as exemplars of the synergistic development between AD and ANQPF. This suggests the presence of Myrdal’s circular cumulative causation effect in the coupled coordination of the two systems within the region, accompanied by pronounced path dependence. Consequently, areas with initially high coordination levels continue to lead other parts of the country. (2) The CCD in the central region has risen steadily. Starting in 2020, the gap with the eastern region has gradually narrowed. The driving force behind this rapid development relies primarily on the concentrated release of policy dividends and locational advantages. Provinces such as Henan and Hunan have witnessed significant growth in their CCD, with their development even surpassing that of major eastern cities in certain years. This is attributed to the fact that, driven by the Poverty Alleviation and Digital Village Pilot Policy, the central region has made substantial progress in addressing deficiencies in agricultural digital infrastructure. Coupled with the strategic dividends of the Rise of Central China initiative, this has enabled the region to effectively undertake industrial transfers from the eastern region, leverage its locational and transportation advantages alongside its abundant agricultural resource endowments, and thereby provide a solid foundation for the coupled and coordinated development of AD and ANQPF. (3) The western region exhibits a relatively low level of coupling coordination, with most provinces remaining in a state of uncoordinated development over the long term. However, its growth potential and catch-up mechanisms are gradually becoming evident. Supported by multiple policies including the Western Development strategy, Poverty Alleviation, and Rural revitalization strategy, Sichuan and Yunnan entered the coordinated development stage in 2020 and 2023 respectively, demonstrating high development resilience and upward potential. This is largely due to targeted national investment. It has effectively addressed funding gaps and technological bottlenecks. Meanwhile, local natural resource endowments have been fully activated. As a result, new quality industries have flourished, and support systems for rural innovation and entrepreneurship have rapidly improved.

Internal obstacle analysis

Diagnostic analysis of obstacle factors in the coupling coordination development of Agricultural Digitalization and Agricultural New Quality Productive Forces

Based on the above findings and drawing on methods used in previous research75, this study calculates the obstacle degrees influencing AD and ANQPF in China for the period 2012–2023. From a national perspective, the obstacle factors influencing the CCD of AD and ANQPF remained relatively stable throughout the sample period, showing little change (see Fig. 8). This suggests that the determinants of the two systems’ integrated development are relatively stable and exhibit inflexible characteristics (Fig. 8).

Average obstacle factors and their degrees: (a) AD; (b) ANQPF.

Regarding the AD system, X8 (development momentum), X1 (internet penetration rate), X2 (optical cable construction), X5 (level of rural online payments), and X12 (education investment) ranked as the top five obstacle factors from 2012 to 2023. Among them, the average annual obstacle degrees of X8 (development momentum) and X1 (internet penetration rate) both exceeded 23%, making them the most significant barriers. Efforts to promote AD should improve agricultural mechanization, enhance market activity and talent development, and strengthen digital infrastructure in rural areas. These actions will help promote the growth of CCD. Specifically, (1) the obstacle degree of X8 (development momentum) increased year by year, rising from second place to first place. This may be partly because China’s crop cultivation and harvesting have achieved a comprehensive mechanization rate of 75.64%, signaling a shift toward high-quality agricultural mechanization. However, compared with the core requirements of digital agriculture, challenges remain, including insufficient autonomy in core technologies, weak coordination among key technologies, and a shortage of agricultural machinery professionals. These issues objectively constrain the advancement of digitalization. (2) From 2012 to 2023, the obstacle degrees of X1 (internet penetration rate) and X2 (optical cable construction) showed a declining trend. In 2018, China’s No.1 Central Document first proposed the Digital Rural Strategy, calling for accelerated broadband network coverage and the expansion of fourth-generation mobile communication networks in rural areas. In the same year, X1 (internet penetration rate) dropped from the primary obstacle factor to the second position. Internet penetration and optical cable construction are critical infrastructure for AD. They mainly function to accelerate the dissemination of agricultural information, reshape agricultural product circulation and trading channels, and provide a foundation for the adoption of new agricultural technologies and modern agricultural management concepts. If these indicators fail to meet standards, it will directly constrain information flow, market integration, and technology diffusion. Therefore, these indicators remain a key focus for AD development. (3) The barrier levels for X5 (level of rural online payments) and X12 (educational investment) show negligible changes, representing significant obstacles hindering the CCD of AD and ANQPF. This indicates that the agricultural digital market and human capital supply are two critical issues requiring urgent attention in AD.

Regarding the ANQPF system, Y14 (leisure agriculture), Y9 (energy consumption), Y2 (skill level), Y8 (water conservancy construction), and Y11 (R&D investment) ranked as the top five obstacle factors from 2012 to 2023. Among them, the average annual obstacle degrees of Y14 (leisure agriculture) exceeded 20%, making it the most significant barrier. This indicates that developing ANQPF should focus on enhancing scientific and technological innovation capabilities, cultivating talent, increasing agricultural energy supply, accelerating the development of new quality industries, and improving infrastructure construction. These efforts will enhance the coordination between AD and ANQPF. Specifically, (1) Y14 (leisure agriculture) has remained largely unchanged and has consistently been a significant obstacle in the ANQPF system. This is because leisure agriculture serves as an important carrier for the deep integration of agriculture with tourism, culture, and the digital economy. It also represents a crucial pathway for extending the agricultural industrial chain, increasing agricultural value-added, and fostering new agricultural business formats. Therefore, its development level directly affects the formation and enhancement of ANQPF. However, leisure agriculture currently suffers from severe homogenization across China. For example, the rapid proliferation of small-scale rural agritainment businesses without sufficient differentiation has increased market competition. Once affected by declining demand, intensified competition, or technological changes, these risks can quickly spread to related sectors, triggering waves of business closures, increasing rural economic risks, and constraining the development of ANQPF. Therefore, improving the supporting policy system for leisure agriculture, formulating development plans according to local conditions, and optimizing financial support mechanisms are of great significance for promoting ANQPF. In response, China has proposed the development of “small but beautiful” agriculture-culture-tourism integration models, strengthening the empowerment of digital technologies and brand building, thereby fully leveraging the positive role of leisure agriculture in enhancing ANQPF. (2) From 2012 to 2023, the obstacle degrees of Y9 (energy consumption) and Y8 (water conservancy construction) both increased. These two indicators belong to the dimension of material production inputs. They reflect the importance of infrastructure for ANQPF development. Agricultural production relies on a stable electricity supply and sound water conservancy facilities. Unstable electricity supply affects the use of mechanized and digital equipment. Inadequate water conservancy facilities can reduce agricultural production efficiency. These problems hinder the development of ANQPF. (3) The obstacle degrees of Y2 (skill level) and Y11 (R&D investment) were relatively stable in the early stage. Y2 (skill level) began to decline after 2018, while Y11 (R&D investment) began to decline after 2019. This change reflects the impact of the rural revitalization strategy. The strategy increased investment in agricultural technological innovation and strengthened agricultural skills training. It also promoted the cultivation of new agricultural business entities. These efforts helped promote the development of ANQPF.

Heterogeneity analysis

To examine the differentiated effects of the influencing factors under different regional conditions, regressions were conducted for the eastern, central, and western regions (see Table 9). (1) Economic Development Level has a positive effect on the coordinated development of AD and ANQPF in the eastern, central, and western regions. However, it is statistically significant only in the western region at the 1% level, while it is insignificant in the central and eastern regions. This indicates that the coordinated development of AD and ANQPF in the western region exhibits a stronger dependence on economic development. In contrast, the eastern and central regions have relatively higher levels of economic development, resulting in lower marginal contributions. (2) Industrial Development Level exhibits a significantly negative effect only in the western region. This may be because rural development in most provinces of the western region remains relatively lagging. The continuous advancement of industrialization attracts a large amount of rural labor, which generates a significant crowding-out effect on agricultural economic development to some extent, thereby intensifying rural hollowing. (3) As a direct manifestation of the coordinated development between AD and ANQPF, Agricultural Development Level exerts a significantly positive effect on all three regions. (4) Urban-rural Income Gap has a significantly positive effect in the eastern region, while it shows a negative but insignificant effect in the central and western regions. This may be because the eastern region generally has higher per capita income levels, more developed digital infrastructure, and a higher degree of industrial integration. In this context, the Urban-rural Income Gap may reflect economic development levels and market vitality to a greater extent. Higher-income groups can stimulate agricultural product consumption and investment in modern agriculture, thereby promoting AD. In contrast, in the central and western regions, the Urban-rural Income Gap mainly reflects imbalanced urban-rural development, which is unfavorable for the coordinated development of AD and ANQPF. (5) Urbanization Rate has a significantly positive effect on the coordinated development of AD and ANQPF in the eastern and central regions, while its impact is insignificant in the western region. This may be because factors such as relatively underdeveloped rural economies, excessive outflow of labor, and low-quality urbanization in the western region weaken the accumulation of agricultural human capital and the capacity of urban areas to support rural development. To some extent, these factors constrain the coordinated development of AD and ANQPF. (6) Rural Consumption Level exhibits a positive effect in all three regions, although the effects are statistically insignificant. This may be because the consumption capacity of rural residents in the western region has continuously improved, while the demand for high-quality agricultural products from the western region through e-commerce platforms, both locally and nationwide, has gradually increased. The demand-side driving effect has become more evident; however, due to a certain time lag, its impact is not significant in the short term. In contrast, the central and eastern regions have relatively mature rural consumer markets, resulting in weakened marginal promotion effects. (7) Fiscal Support Intensity has a significantly positive effect in the western region, while its effect is insignificant in the central and eastern regions. Compared with the central and eastern regions, the western region has a relatively weaker foundation for agricultural modernization and urgently requires government fiscal investment to play an important guiding role in public service provision and digital technology promotion. Therefore, Fiscal Support Intensity significantly promotes the coupling coordination development between AD and ANQPF in the western region. This indicates that the “timely assistance” effect of fiscal support is greater than its “icing on the cake” effect. (8) Openness To Foreign Trade has a significantly positive effect on the coordinated development of AD and ANQPF in the central region, while its effect is insignificant in the eastern and western regions. This may be because the eastern region has a high level of economic development, strong technological innovation capacity, and well-established digital infrastructure, resulting in strong endogenous development momentum. Therefore, the marginal effect of foreign trade openness on high-quality agricultural development has gradually declined. In contrast, rural areas in the western region are constrained by geographical location and transportation conditions, leading to relatively limited connections with external markets and a lower level of foreign trade openness. Meanwhile, rural areas mainly rely on traditional agricultural economies, and this economic structure may be less sensitive to changes in external markets. (9) Agricultural Security Level has a significantly positive effect on the coordinated development of AD and ANQPF in the central and eastern regions, while it exhibits a negative effect in the western region. This may be because agricultural operations in the central and eastern regions are characterized by a higher degree of scale and a relatively well-developed agricultural insurance system, which can effectively diversify agricultural production risks. In contrast, agricultural production conditions in the western region are more complex, with a higher frequency of natural disasters. Therefore, agricultural insurance mainly serves a compensatory and risk-mitigation function, providing basic protection rather than promoting digital transformation and technological innovation. Meanwhile, agricultural production in the western region is largely dominated by small-scale farmers and cooperatives, with relatively weak foundations for digital technology application. Even with expanded agricultural insurance coverage, it is difficult to directly transform insurance protection into investment in digital technologies and the development of ANQPF, thereby resulting in a negative effect. (10) Agricultural Disaster Rate has a significantly negative effect on the coordinated development of AD and ANQPF in the eastern region, while its effect is insignificant in the central and western regions. This may be because agricultural production in the eastern region is characterized by a higher degree of intensification, making natural disasters exert greater impacts on agricultural industrial chains and supply chains, thereby reducing the transformation efficiency of ANQPF. In contrast, agricultural production in the central and western regions is more dominated by household-based farming, where natural disaster risks are relatively dispersed. Therefore, the impact of Agricultural Disaster Rate on the coupling coordination between AD and ANQPF is relatively limited.

Overall, significant regional differences exist in the external influencing factors of the coordinated development between AD and ANQPF. Specifically, the eastern region mainly relies on its endogenous advantages in agricultural industrial foundations, urbanization, and agricultural security, indicating that its coordinated development depends more on well-established agricultural development foundations and urban-rural integration conditions. The central region, by contrast, relies more on factors such as agricultural industrial foundations, Openness To Foreign Trade, and Agricultural Security Level. As it is at an important stage of undertaking industrial transfer and promoting agricultural modernization, expanding foreign trade openness and advancing agricultural modernization are important pathways for promoting the coordinated development between AD and ANQPF. The western region relies more heavily on factors such as Economic Development Level, Fiscal Support Intensity, and agricultural industrial foundations. Meanwhile, Industrial Development Level exhibits a certain resource crowding-out effect in this region. This indicates that the western region remains at a relatively lower stage of coordinated development between AD and ANQPF, with a high dependence on external resource inputs and a strong reliance on economic development.

Conclusions and recommendations

Conclusions

Based on the evaluation of AD and ANQPF, this study primarily analyzes their spatiotemporal characteristics and internal and external driving factors. The main conclusions are as follows:

From 2012 to 2023, AD exhibited a steady upward trend featuring a spatial gradient of Eastern leadership, Central quality enhancement, and Western catch-up; ANQPF maintained a positive growth trajectory with a significant non-equilibrium pattern of Eastern > Central > Western.

AD and ANQPF exhibit a positive trend of synergistic development, with continuously strengthening coupling coordination effects. The system currently operates within the transition interval from moderate uncoordinated to barely coordinated, indicating substantial room for improvement before reaching a level of favorable coordination. Temporally, 2012–2016 constituted a period of slow growth in coupling coordination, followed by an accelerated upward trajectory from 2016 to 2023. Spatially, the distribution pattern has evolved into a hierarchical structure of “Eastern > Central > Western,” forming a developmental landscape characterized by “the Eastern region leading, the Central region tackling key challenges, and the Western region catching up.”

Recommendations

Building on these findings, this study quantitatively identifies the varying degrees of influence on the coupling coordination between AD and ANQPF across different regions and pinpoints the critical internal and external determinants. Accordingly, targeted policy recommendations are proposed, tailored to local conditions:

The eastern region should intensify agricultural technological innovation and optimize the industrial structure. Specifically, it is imperative to sustain the dual-core radiation effect of Jiangsu and Zhejiang within the Yangtze River Delta to drive the synergistic development of surrounding areas. In implementation, region-specific strategies must be prioritized. For regions with high ANQPF levels such as Jiangsu and Shandong, efforts should focus on refining rural market service systems and fully activating market dynamism alongside strengthened policy support. Additionally, adhering to market-oriented principles is crucial to accelerating agricultural scientific and technological innovation and application. For metropolitan-oriented cities like Shanghai, leveraging urban consumer demand is essential to cultivate specialized entities such as new-type agricultural operators and rural stewards, with a strategic emphasis on developing “small but beautiful” leisure agriculture, premium boutique agriculture, and interactive cultural tourism initiatives. Furthermore, the linchpin for advancing the synergy between AD and ANQPF in the east is to harness urbanization to revitalize rural areas. While absorbing the spillover of urban industries to create jobs, rural regions must concurrently foster proprietor economies such as housing rentals to bolster rural residents’ property and wage incomes, thereby actualizing the dynamic of urban-led rural development and industry-supported agriculture. Concurrently, rural areas in the east should also systematically improve disaster prevention and mitigation infrastructure and refine the agricultural insurance system to achieve full coverage, thereby mitigating the adverse impacts of natural disasters on agriculture.

For regions with a weak foundation in AD, ANQPF, and coupling coordination, such as Tibet and Ningxia, it is imperative to proceed from local natural conditions and resource endowments. Efforts should focus on soil amelioration and ecological governance, coupled with promoting the widespread application of intelligent monitoring equipment in agricultural production. It is essential to clarify the functional positioning of agricultural universities, research institutes, and technology enterprises to establish a comprehensive, high-efficiency, and all-encompassing agricultural science and technology innovation system. Limitations and future research directions

This study has limitations in terms of sample scope, indicator selection, research perspective, and the generalizability of the conclusions. (1) Regarding the sample scope, the data used in this study are provincial-level panel data, and the research scale is rather coarse, making it difficult to reveal the differences at the prefectural-level city or county levels. Future research can utilize municipal or county-level data for measurement to explore more detailed temporal and spatial evolutions. (2) Regarding indicator selection, some indicators in the AD and ANQPF evaluation systems were measured using total values due to data availability constraints. However, such indicators may not fully capture the actual development conditions of different regions, and some indicators, such as agricultural meteorological observation stations and annual income from leisure agriculture, may introduce certain biases into the research results. Future studies should further improve the evaluation systems of AD and ANQPF by incorporating more representative and comparable indicators to enhance the accuracy of regional development assessments. (3) Regarding the research perspective, this study focuses mainly on China’s AD and ANQPF, lacking comparative analysis with other countries. Although regional heterogeneity analysis was conducted by dividing China into eastern, central, and western regions, this classification may not fully capture the internal differences within each region. The three regions still exhibit substantial variations in geographical conditions, resource endowments, and policy environments, which may lead to differentiated development mechanisms. Future research can adopt more refined spatial classifications and spatial analysis methods to further explore the heterogeneity of AD and ANQPF development. (4) Regarding research conclusions, findings based on China may not be applicable to other countries. Future research can utilize data from other countries to explore the CCD between AD and ANQPF, as well as internal barriers and external influencing factors.
