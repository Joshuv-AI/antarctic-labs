Index Modulation for Modulation on Conjugate-Reciprocal Zeros (IM-MOCZ)
Aidan Corbett, Ebrahim Bedeer

Abstract
This paper investigates the application of Index Modulation (IM) to Modulation on Conjugate-Reciprocal Zeros (MOCZ) to enhance spectral efficiency (SE) in short packet communications. The proposed IM-MOCZ scheme splits an N N -bit message into two streams: N − K N-K bits select one of 2 N − K 2^{N-K} uniquely designed codebooks, while the remaining K K bits are transmitted with conventional MOCZ using the selected codebook. At the receiver, Root Finding Minimum Distance (RFMD) and Direct Zero-Testing (DiZeT) detectors evaluate all candidate codebooks and compute penalty metrics, with a majority-vote rule selecting the most confident codebook and recovering the transmitted message. The proposed IM-MOCZ provides higher SE gains than conventional MOCZ, and simulations demonstrate improved bit error rate (BER) performance for larger K K relative to N N .

I
Introduction

Fig. 1
:
A block diagram of the proposed baseband equivalent IM-MOCZ system.

Emerging wireless applications such as ultra-reliable low-latency communication (URLLC) and the Internet of Things (IoT) require the reliable transmission of short packets under strict latency and reliability constraints. In short-packet communications (SPCs), traditional coherent methods become inefficient, as the overhead required for channel estimation can rival or exceed the payload itself, significantly degrading spectral efficiency (SE) and system performance. Modulation on Conjugate-Reciprocal Zeros (MOCZ) addresses this challenge as a non-coherent modulation scheme specifically designed for SPC scenarios
[
1
]
. By eliminating the need for pilot-based channel estimation, MOCZ reduces overhead while maintaining reliable detection, making it particularly attractive for sporadic, low-latency transmissions.

Since its introduction, MOCZ has been the subject of extensive research aimed at expanding its capabilities and optimizing performance. The foundational work by Walk et al. established the MOCZ framework and highlighted its suitability for non-coherent SPCs
[
1
]
. Subsequent studies by Walk et al. in
[
2
]
have explored extensions to multi-user settings to enable simultaneous transmissions, and work by Eren in
[
3
]
utilized dynamic codebooks to further improve throughput and resource management in multi-user scenarios. In addition, Sahin et al. incorporated majority vote rulings that enhanced performance in over-the-air computation applications, demonstrating improvements in computation error rate
[
4
]
. Other efforts, such as those by Siddiqui et al., have focused on improving the SE of MOCZ by integrating faster-than-Nyquist signalling techniques
[
5
,
6
]
. Collectively, these contributions continue to broaden the theoretical understanding and practical applicability of MOCZ in wireless systems.

In this paper, we propose an index modulation (IM) based MOCZ scheme, named IM-MOCZ, to improve SE of SPCs. A message of length
N
bits is divided into two streams:
N-K
bits select one of multiple unique codebooks, while the remaining
K
bits are transmitted using conventional MOCZ with the selected codebook. With the codebooks known at both the transmitter and receiver, Root Finding Minimum Distance (RFMD) and Direct Zero-Testing (DiZeT) detectors evaluate the received complex zeros across all candidate codebooks, and a penalty matrix is computed to quantify the confidence of each codebook. A majority vote rule is applied to determine the most probable codebook to recover the message. The proposed IM-MOCZ design achieves significant SE gains when compared to MOCZ, and simulation results show improved BER performance for high values of
K
compared to
N
.

This paper is organized as follows: Section
II
introduces the IM-MOCZ system model, while Section
III
outlines the design and integration of IM components. Simulation results are presented in Section
IV
and Section
V

II
System Model

Fig.
1
presents the block diagram of the proposed IM-MOCZ communication system. The message to be transmitted, of length
N
, is divided into two bit streams. The first stream, consisting of the first
N-K
bits (
b_{1},\dots,b_{N-K}
), is used to select a unique codebook
\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,2^{N-K}
, from a set of predefined codebooks
\mathcal{C}
using IM.
The second bit stream consists of the remaining
K
bits (
b_{N-K+1},\dots,b_{N}
) transmitted using conventional MOCZ according to the selected codebook
\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}
. In other words, each of the
K
bits of the second stream is mapped to a unique complex zero
\{a_{i,k},a^{-1}_{i,k}\}\in\mathbb{C}
,
k=1,\dots,K
, in the
z
-domain according to codebook
\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}
. A logical 0 is mapped to the inner zero
a^{-1}_{i,k}
, while a logical 1 is mapped to the outer zero
a_{i,k}
. Then, the
K+1
polynomial coefficients
\mathbf{x}=[x_{1},\dots,x_{k},\dots,x_{K+1}]^{\rm T}\in\mathbb{C}^{K+1}
, corresponding to the
K
transmitted complex zeros, are constructed according to the modified Toeplitz iterator method
[
5
]
. The proposed IM-MOCZ system transmits
N
bits using
K+1
(
K\leq N
) polynomial coefficients.

The received signal,
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{C}^{(K+L_{\rm{ch}})}
, is expressed as
[
1
]

\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{x}*\mathbf{h}+\mathbf{w},

(1)

where
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{C}^{L_{\text{ch}}}
is the time-invariant multipath channel consisting of
L_{\rm{ch}}
taps and
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{C}^{(K+L_{\rm{ch}})}
is the additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) with a one-sided power spectral density of
N_{0}
. The received sampled signal
\mathbf{y}
is processed by a detection block employing RFMD or DiZeT detection schemes
[
1
]
to estimate the complex zeros
\{\hat{a}_{i,k},\hat{a}^{-1}_{i,k}\}
associated with each candidate codebook
\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}
. The resulting estimates are collected in the matrix
\hat{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathbb{C}^{2^{N-K}\times K}
, while the corresponding penalty metrics (defined in Section
III
) for each estimated complex zero are stored in
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{N-K}\times K}
. The transmitted message is recovered by identifying the most likely codebook
\mathbf{\hat{\zeta}}_{i}
using a majority vote decision. The index of the recovered codebook determines the implicitly transmitted bits (
\hat{b}_{1},\dots,\hat{b}_{N-K}
). The remaining explicitly transmitted bits are then recovered by selecting the detected complex zeros from
\hat{\mathbf{A}}
corresponding to
\mathbf{\hat{\zeta}}_{i}
, yielding (
\hat{b}_{N-K+1},\dots,\hat{b}_{N}
).

III
The Proposed IM-MOCZ

As discussed in Section
II
, the transmission requires
K+1
polynomial coefficients generated from the
K

a_{i,k}
,
k=1,...,K

\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}

III-A

IM-MOCZ Codebook Construction

In the proposed IM-MOCZ system, each candidate codebook
\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}
is generated by applying a phase rotation to a common conventional MOCZ codebook, denoted as
\mathbf{\zeta}_{1}
. Specifically, the
i
-th codebook is obtained by applying the phase offset

\theta_{i}=\frac{2\pi(i-1)}{K2^{N-K}},\quad i=1,\dots,2^{N-K},

(2)

such that each complex zero location in the common conventional MOCZ codebook is mapped according to

\displaystyle\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle\mathbf{\zeta}_{1}e^{j\theta_{i}},\quad i=1,\dots,2^{N-K}.

(3)

This produces
2^{N-K}
phase-rotated codebooks that are uniformly spaced.
Each codebook index
i
is associated with a unique binary sequence. In this work, the sequence is defined by the
(N-K)
-bit binary representation of
i-1
, with the most significant bit corresponding to
b_{1}
. Selecting a codebook implicitly conveys the first
N-K
information bits without increasing the number of polynomial coefficients transmitted. An example is illustrated in Fig.

III-B

Per-Sector Detection and Penalty Metrics

For each candidate codebook
\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}
and sector
k\in\{1,\dots,K\}
, the detector produces an estimated complex zero
\{\hat{a}_{i,k},\hat{a}^{-1}_{i,k}\}\in\mathbb{C}
together with an associated penalty metric
p_{i,k}\in\mathbb{R}
. The penalty metric quantifies how well the received polynomial zeros match the expected codebook zero in a given sector. In this work, two detection schemes are considered: RFMD and DiZeT detection
[
1
]
. Both detectors additionally produce a scalar penalty value and an estimated received complex zero for each sector and candidate codebook, enabling a majority vote decision rule. The received polynomial
\mathbf{y}
produces
M=K+L_{\text{ch}}-1
complex zeros, denoted
\tilde{a}_{m}
.

III-B
1
RFMD Penalty Metric

For RFMD detection, a detector block is applied to each candidate codebook
\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}
across all
K
sectors. Given the received polynomial coefficients
\mathbf{y}
and codebook
\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}
, the RFMD block returns, for each sector
k
, a detected complex zero
\hat{\mathbf{A}}_{i,k}
together with an associated penalty metric
p_{i,k}=\mathrm{d}(\tilde{a}_{m},\hat{\mathbf{A}}_{i,k})
, where
\mathrm{d}(\tilde{a}_{m},\hat{\mathbf{A}}_{i,k})
corresponds to the smallest Euclidean distance between the received complex zero
\tilde{a}_{m}
in sector
k
of
\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}
and
\hat{\mathbf{A}}_{i,k}\in\{a_{i,k},a^{-1}_{i,k}\}
which is the codebook complex zero that achieves this minimum. This operation is expressed as

\big(\hat{\mathbf{A}}_{i,k},\,p_{i,k}^{\text{(RFMD)}}\big)=\text{RFMD}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}).

(4)

Smaller values of
p_{i,k}
indicate a closer match between the received and detected codebook complex zeros, and therefore a higher confidence in detection. These penalty metrics are used directly in the majority vote codebook detection procedure.

2
DiZeT Penalty Metric

For DiZeT detection, a detector block is applied to each candidate codebook
\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}
across all
K
sectors. Given the received polynomial coefficients
\mathbf{y}
and a candidate codebook
\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}
, the DiZeT detector evaluates, for each sector
k
, a detected complex zero
\hat{\mathbf{A}}_{i,k}
and corresponding penalty metric
p^{\text{(DiZeT)}}_{i,k}
. This is achieved by substituting the received complex zeros
\tilde{a}_{m}
into the expected complex zero locations
a_{i,k}
associated with
\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}

[
1
]
. This is expressed as

\big(\hat{\mathbf{A}}_{i,k},\,p_{i,k}^{\text{(DiZeT)}}\big)=\text{DiZeT}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}).

(8)

During detection, the DiZeT detector must determine whether the received zero corresponds to the inner or outer zero of the codebook pair
\{a_{i,k},a^{-1}_{i,k}\}
. Accordingly, two candidate penalty metrics are computed: an outer penalty
p^{\text{(out)}}_{i,k}
and an inner penalty
p^{\text{(in)}}_{i,k}

[
1
]
. These are defined as

\displaystyle p_{i,k}^{\text{(out)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg|\prod_{m=1}^{M}(a_{i,k}-\tilde{a}_{m})\Bigg|,

(9)

\displaystyle p_{i,k}^{\text{(in)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{M}\Bigg|\prod_{m=1}^{M}(a_{i,k}^{-1}-\tilde{a}_{m})\Bigg|,

(10)

where
R
denotes the radius of the outer codebook zeros. Smaller penalty values indicate a closer agreement between the received complex zeros and the associated codebook complex zero locations, and therefore higher confidence in detection.

The DiZeT penalty metric for codebook
\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}
in sector
k
is then defined as the minimum of the inner and outer penalties,

p_{i,k}^{\text{(DiZeT)}}=\min\big(p^{\text{(in)}}_{i,k},p_{i,k}^{\text{(out)}}\big).

(11)

In the event that
p^{\text{(in)}}_{i,k}=p^{\text{(out)}}_{i,k}
, the DiZeT penalty metric is selected as either value with equal probability, and the detected complex zero
\hat{\mathbf{A}}_{i,k}
is selected as
a^{-1}_{i,k}
or
a_{i,k}
, respectively.

III-C

Majority Vote Codebook Detection

Once all penalty values

p_{i,k}\in\{p_{i,k}^{\text{(RFMD)}},p_{i,k}^{\text{(DiZeT)}}\}

have been computed for every candidate codebook
\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}
and collected in the penalty matrix
\mathbf{P}\in\{\mathbf{P}^{\text{(RFMD)}},\mathbf{P}^{\text{(DiZeT)}}\}
, the final codebook is detected using a majority vote across sectors.
The correct codebook is expected to produce consistently lower penalty values across most sectors.

For each sector
k
, the codebook index that minimizes the penalty metric is determined as

\hat{i}_{k}=\arg\min_{i}p_{i,k}.

(16)

Each sector casts one vote for the codebook that minimizes the penalty in that sector.
The total number of votes for each candidate codebook is given by the number of sectors that select it as the minimum-penalty codebook:

w_{i}=\left|\{k\in\{1,\dots,K\}\mid\hat{i}_{k}=i\}\right|.

(17)

The final codebook decision is made by selecting the codebook with the majority of votes:

\hat{i}=\arg\max_{i}w_{i}.

(18)

In the event of a tie, the detected codebook is chosen uniformly at random from the set of tied candidates.
Once
\mathbf{\hat{\zeta}}_{i}
is selected, the implicitly transmitted bits

(\hat{b}_{1},\dots,\hat{b}_{N-K})
are recovered directly from
\hat{i}
.
The explicitly transmitted bits

(\hat{b}_{N-K+1},\dots,\hat{b}_{N})
are then recovered by selecting the detected zeros
\hat{a}_{i,k}
associated with
\mathbf{\hat{\zeta}}_{i}
from the matrix
\hat{\mathbf{A}}
and applying the standard MOCZ demapping procedure.

III-D

Complexity Analysis of IM-MOCZ

When analyzing the complexity of the IM-MOCZ receiver, let the detector block have complexity
V\in\{V^{\text{(RFMD)}},V^{\text{(DiZeT)}}\}
per transmission. Since the proposed IM-MOCZ receiver performs
2^{N-K}
detections (one per codebook
\mathbf{\zeta}_{i}
), the detector block requires
O\big(2^{N-K}V\big)
. The majority voting stage computes
K
minimum operations over
2^{N-K}
values, resulting in
O\!\left(2^{N-K}K\right)
. The remaining indexing and demapping operations require
O(K)
, however they are dominated by the previous
2^{N-K}
terms. Therefore, the overall complexity of the proposed IM-MOCZ receiver is
O\big(2^{N-K}(V+K)\big)
. This complexity is suitable for SPC applications, provided that
N-K
remains small.

V
Conclusions

In this paper, we incorporate index modulation into the non-coherent MOCZ sheme, enabling additional information to be conveyed implicitly through a conventional MOCZ codebook and thereby achieving significant SE gains. For example, at
N=10
, and
L_{\text{ch}}=3

K=8
and
K=6

K=6
when compared to conventional MOCZ where

K
