Truncated Grassmannians, blow-ups along Schubert varieties and collineations
Evgeny Feigin

Abstract
. Truncated Grassmannians are defined as closures of orbits of abelian unipotent groups acting on the degree truncations of projectivized wedge powers. We show that such truncations in a more general setup show up in the description of the blow-ups of general flag varieties along Schubert subvarieties. We work out the case of Grassmannians in detail. In particular, we show that our blow-ups are members of a larger family of varieties projecting onto Grassmannians, and describe the fibers of these projections via the spaces of collineations.



Introduction

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a simple Lie algebra over the filed of complex numbers and let
G
be the
corresponding simple simply connected Lie group. For a standard parabolic subgroup
P
,
let

be a generalized flag variety.
We denote by

the Schubert variety in

attached to the Weyl group element

In this paper we describe

for certain Schubert varieties in the complex Grassmannians

Not much is known about the blow-ups of flag varieties along Schubert subvarieties in general,
although some partial results (mostly in the smooth case)
are available
[
BS25
,
HKLS25
,
KP21
,
LSi21
]
.
We formulate a general approach, based on the truncated flag varieties,
and work out the construction in detail for the Grassmannians. We provide more details below.

Let

be a vector space of dimension
n
and let us fix

;
to simplify the notation, we assume that
d\leq n-d
.
We fix
a decomposition

fixing two subspaces of

of dimensions

d
and

.
Let

\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
be the Grassmannian of
d
-dimensional subspaces in

can be realized as the quotient

SL_{n}/P_{d}
, where
P_{d}
is the maximal parabolic subgroup corresponding to the

d
-th simple root; in particular,
P_{d}
preserves
V_{d}
. Let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{d}
be the Lie
algebra of

and let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}

be the opposite abelian unipotent radical, i.e.
{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{d}=\mathfrak{sl}(V)

The group
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
acts on the Grassmannian and
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
is the closure
of the orbit
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})V_{d}
.

The Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
admits the Plücker embedding

\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\subset{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}V)

[

]
. Let us fix a basis
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
of
V
such that
the first
d
vectors form a basis of
V_{d}
and the last
n-d
form a basis of
V_{n-d}
.
The wedge power
\Lambda^{d}V
admits the standard basis of the form
v_{I}
consisting of
wedge monomials, where
I
is a cardinality
d
subset of the set
\{1,\dots,n\}
.
We define the degree
\deg I
as the number of elements of
I
that are larger than
d
.
For a number
r=0,\dots,d
, we introduce the space
(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r}
, which is the
quotient of
\Lambda^{d}V
by the space spanned by all vectors

of degree larger than

r
. Then
(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r}
inherits the cyclic action of
{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}
with the cyclic vector

,
the image of the cyclic vector of
\Lambda^{d}V
.

By definition, the truncated Grassmannian
X_{r}
is a subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}((\Lambda^{d}V)_{r})

defined as the closure of the
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
orbit of the line
[w_{r}]
.
One easily sees that

admits an action of a larger group
P_{d}^{-}
, where

P_{d}^{-}
is the (opposite) maximal parabolic subgroup attached to the
d
-th simple root.
In particular,

X_{1}
is the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}^{d(n-d)}
and
X_{d}=\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
. By definition, all

X_{r}
are pairwise birationally isomorphic
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{d(n-d)}
varieties
[
A11
,
HT99
,
Fe12
]
.
The closure
of the graph of the birational isomorphism
X_{1}\to X_{d}
was studied in
[
FSS25
,
BSS25
]

(see also
[
Fe24
,
Fe25
]
). Our goal is to study the graph closures
of maps between various truncated
Grassmannians and to provide a link with the blow-ups of the classical Grassmann varieties.

For
r=1,\dots,d
let
S_{r}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
be the Schubert subvariety whose points
U

satisfy the condition
\dim U\cap V_{n-d}\geq r
. In particular, the codimension of

S_{r}
is
r^{2}
and
S_{r}
exhaust all the

P_{d}^{-}
invariant Schubert varieties. Our first theorem is as follows.

Theorem A
.

The blow-up
\mathrm{Bl}_{S_{r}}\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
is isomorphic to the closure of the graph
of the birational map
X_{r-1}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
. In particular,
\mathrm{Bl}_{S_{r}}\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
admits
the action of
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
with an open dense orbit.

We can restate the theorem in the following form:
\mathrm{Bl}_{S_{r}}\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
admits
a closed embedding into the product
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\times X_{r-1}
with the image being the closure
of the
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
orbit of the point
V_{d}\times[w_{r-1}]
.
This is a particular case of a general statement (Theorem
C
); we give a
separate proof in the case of Grassmannians since here we can explicitly compute
the Koszul type resolution of the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}}
.

All the varieties
X_{r}
and
\mathrm{Bl}_{S_{r}}\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
are birationally isomorphic with the
isomorphism provided by the ”common” open
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
orbit. We define a larger family
of pairwise birationally isomorphic
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{d(n-d)}
varieties,
which includes all the above examples. More precisely, let
{\bf r}
be a collection of
numbers
1<r_{1}<\dots<r_{m}\leq d
. We define
\mathrm{Bl}_{\bf r}
as the multi-projective blow-up
with respect to the varieties
S_{r_{1}},\dots,S_{r_{m}}
.

\mathrm{Bl}_{\bf r}
can be also described as the closure
of the
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
orbit through the point

V_{d}\times[w_{r_{1}-1}]\times\dots\times[w_{r_{m}-1}]
inside the product of
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
and
the corresponding truncated Grassmannians

. To describe the fibers of the natural
projection map

\mathrm{Bl}_{\bf r}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
we consider the partial collineations

[

,
LAT89
,
Vain84
]
.

Let
E_{1},E_{2}
be two spaces of dimension

{\bf s}=(1\leq s_{1}<\dots<s_{m}<N)

Then

to be smooth
[
Vain84
,
M20
,
Th99
]
. We prove the following theorem (see precise statement in

4.6
).

Theorem B
.

The fiber of the projection map
\mathrm{Bl}_{\bf r}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
over a subspace

to a collineation variety depending on
\dim U\cap V_{n-d}
. The complete mixed blow-up
\mathrm{Bl}_{(2,\dots,d)}
is smooth
and serves as a desingularization for all other

the literature. First, in
[

,
FW25
,
FZ21
]

They define the generalized Kausz compactifications

\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
(and

) symmetry.

Finally, let us describe the general case.
Let
G
be a simple simply-connected Lie group with the flag variety
G/B

(the case of arbitrary
G/P
is similar).
Let
\lambda
be a regular dominant integral weight and let
L(\lambda)
be the corresponding
irreducible highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
module with the highest weight vector
v(\lambda)
.
We fix the projective embedding
G/B\subset{\mathbb{P}}(L(\lambda))
with the image being the closure
of the
B_{-}
orbit through the highest weight line
[v(\lambda)]
.
Let
\mathcal{O}(\lambda)
be the line bundle on
G/B
obtained as a pull back of
\mathcal{O}(1)
on

{\mathbb{P}}(L(\lambda))
.
For a Weyl group element
\sigma
let
v(\sigma\lambda)\in L(\lambda)
be the corresponding
extremal weight vector. The (opposite) Demazure module
D(\sigma\lambda)\subset L(\lambda)

is the
\mathrm{U}({\mathfrak{b}}_{-})
span of
v(\sigma\lambda)
. The (opposite) Schubert variety

sits inside
{\mathbb{P}}(D(\sigma\lambda))
as the closure of the
B_{-}
orbit of
the line
[v(\sigma\lambda)]
.

We define the truncated
{\mathfrak{b}}_{-}
module
L_{\sigma}(\lambda)
as the quotient
L(\lambda)/D(\sigma\lambda)

and denote the image of

as

; note that the truncated module

is cyclic with cyclic vector

. We also define the

as the closure
of
B_{-}[v_{\sigma}(\lambda)]
.

Theorem C
.

The blow-up of
G/B
along the Schubert variety
S_{\sigma}
admits a closed embedding
into
G/B\times X_{\sigma}(\lambda)
as the closure of the
B_{-}
orbit through the product

[v(\lambda)]\times[v_{\sigma}(\lambda)]
.

As in the case of Grassmann varieties, it is natural to consider the mixed blow-ups.
More precisely, one starts with a collection of the Weyl group elements

\underline{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m})
and define
F_{\underline{\sigma}}

inside
\prod_{i=1}^{m}X_{\sigma_{i}}(\lambda)
as the closure of the
B_{-}
orbit through the
product of highest weight lines
[v_{\sigma_{i}}(\lambda)]
. It is tempting to conjecture
that if
\underline{\sigma}=W
(i.e.
m=|W|
), then
F_{\underline{\sigma}}
is smooth.

Our paper is organized in the following way. In Section
1
we introduce
the main objects of study and collect the preliminary material.
In Section
2
we describe the blow-ups along the Schubert varieties

as orbit closures inside the products of classical and truncated Grassmannians.
In Section

we construct a resolution for the ideal sheaf of

In Section

In Section

Appendix
A

Conventions

Throughout the paper we work over the field of complex numbers. We fix two positive
integers
d<n
and always assume that
d\leq n-d
.
For a vector
u
in a vector space
U
we denote by
[u]\in{\mathbb{P}}(U)
the line spanned
by
u
.
All the Demazure modules and Schubert
varieties we consider are the opposite Demazure modules and opposite Schubert varieties.
Hence, the Demazure modules are generated from the extremal weight vectors by the
action of the Chevalley generators corresponding to the negative roots and the Schubert
varieties are invariant with respect to the negative Borel subgroup.

1.2.
Representations

In what follows we use simultaneously representations of algebras

1.4.
Grassmannians and Schubert varieties

Let
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
be the Grassmannian of
d
-dimensional subspaces of
V
.
The Plücker embedding realizes
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
as a projective algebraic subvariety
of
{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}V)
of dimension
d(n-d)
.
The Grassmannians admit the transitive action of
SL_{n}
and
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\simeq SL_{n}/P_{d}
.
The action of the abelian unipotent subgroup
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
is not transitive, but
admits an open dense orbit through the highest weight line containing
v_{[d]}
;
this orbit is an affine cell
{\mathbb{A}}^{d(n-d)}
.

More generally, for a collection

I\in\binom{[n]}{d}
the open (opposite) Schubert variety
S^{\circ}_{I}
is defined as the

B_{-}
orbit through the line
[v_{I}]\in{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}V)
. This orbit is an affine cell and
its closure is the (opposite) closed Schubert variety. Hence, the Grassmannian is the disjoint
union of the affine cells – the open Schubert varieties. We denote by
S_{I}
the closure
of
S^{\circ}_{I}
and by
p_{I}\in S_{I}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
the point corresponding to
[v_{I}]
. Explicitly,

p_{I}
is spanned by the vectors
v_{i}
with
i\in I
.

In what follows we use the special family
S_{r}=S_{I(r)}
,
r=0,\dots,d

of opposite Schubert varieties.

Lemma 1.9
.

A
d
-dimensional subspace
U\subset V
belongs to
S_{r}
if and only if

\dim U\cap V_{n-d}\geq r
.

Proof.

For the torus fixed point
p_{I(r)}
one has
\dim p_{I(r)}\cap V_{n-d}=r
. Since

B_{-}
preserves
V_{n-d}
, we conclude that
\dim U\cap V_{n-d}\geq r
for any
U\in S_{r}
.

Now assume
\dim U\cap V_{n-d}\geq r
. Then
U
belongs to a Schubert cell
S_{J}^{\circ}

for some
J
subject to the condition
|\{j\in J:\ j>d\}|\geq r
. Hence
I(r)\geq J

(componentwise, being ordered from smaller to larger entries) and
S_{I(r)}\supset S_{J}
. ∎

One has the following list of basic properties of the Schubert varieties
S_{r}
.

Lemma 1.10
.

The varieties
S_{r}
,
0\leq r\leq d
satisfy the following properties:

(i)

S_{0}\supset S_{1}\supset\dots\supset S_{d}
,

(ii)

S_{0}=\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
,
S_{d}=\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V_{n-d})
,

(iii)

S_{1}
is the divisor in
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
,
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\setminus S_{1}=\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})[v_{[d]}]
,

(iv)

\mathrm{codim}S_{r}=r^{2}
.

Remark 1.11
.

The Schubert varieties inside Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
are often parametrized by partitions

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})
with
n-d\geq\lambda_{1}
and
k\leq d
(i.e. the corresponding Young
diagram fits into the
d\times(n-d)
box). In particular, the dimension of the Schubert variety
attached to

is the sum of all
\lambda_{i}
.
In this parametrization the Schubert variety

corresponds to the partition

(n-d)^{d-r}(n-d-r)^{r}
. Here is a picture for
n=10,d=4
and

.

Remark 1.12
.

The previous remark implies that the varieties
S_{r}
are singular for
r\neq 0,d
, since
the smooth Schubert varieties correspond to rectangular partitions
[
LSh84
]
.

Lemma 1.13
.

A Schubert variety in
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
is
P^{-}_{d}
invariant if and only if it coincides
with one of
S_{r}
. The variety
S_{r}
consists of points of
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
contained
in the projectivization of the Demazure module
\mathrm{U}({\mathfrak{n}}_{-})v_{I(r)}
.

Proof.

The maximal parabolic subgroup
P^{-}_{d}
preserves
V_{n-d}
and hence by Lemma
1.9

all
S_{r}
are
P^{-}_{d}
invariant. Now let
S_{I}
be a
P^{-}_{d}
invariant Schubert variety.
Let
I=I_{<}\sqcup I_{>}
, where
I_{<}=I\cap[d]
,
I_{>}=\{i\in I:\ i>d\}
, and let

r=|I_{>}|
. Then there exists an element
g\in P^{-}_{d}
such that
gp_{I}=p_{I(r)}
for

I(r)
defined by (
1.2
). Hence
S_{I}=S_{I(r)}
, because
S_{r}
is
P^{-}_{d}

invariant. The last claim is the standard link between a Schubert variety and the corresponding
Demazure module (see e.g.
[
Kum02
]
), but can be easily proved explicitly in this case.
∎

1.5.
Vector bundles on Grassmannians

Let us consider the tautological vector bundle
\mathcal{U}_{d}
on
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
, i.e.
\mathcal{U}_{d}

is a rank
d
subbundle of
V(\mathcal{O})=V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)}
, whose fiber at a point
U

is
U
itself. We denote by
\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp}
the vector bundle

(V(\mathcal{O})/\mathcal{U}_{d})^{*}
. Let
\mathcal{O}(1)
be the line bundle on
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
, which is the positive
generator of the
Picard group of
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
. In particular,
\mathcal{O}(1)
is the inverse image of
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}V)}(1)

with respect to the standard Plücker embedding.

For a collection of integers
\mu=(\mu_{1}\geq\dots\geq\mu_{k})
and a rank
k
vector
bundle
\mathcal{L}
, let
\Sigma_{\mu}\mathcal{L}
be the Schur functor applied to
\mathcal{L}
(see e.g.
[
W03
]
).
In particular,

\Sigma_{(1,\dots,1)}\mathcal{U}_{d}^{*}\simeq\Sigma_{(-1,\dots,-1)}\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp}\simeq\mathcal{O}(1).

We note also that for any vector bundle
\mathcal{L}
and a composition
\mu
one has

(1.3)

\Sigma_{(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{k})}\mathcal{L}\simeq\Sigma_{(-\mu_{k},\dots,-\mu_{1})}\mathcal{L}^{*}.

In what follows we denote
(-\mu_{k},\dots,-\mu_{1})
by
\mu^{*}
.

For two collections
\mu=(\mu_{1}\geq\dots\geq\mu_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
and

\nu=(\nu_{1}\geq\dots\geq\nu_{n-d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n-d}
,
the cohomologies of the vector bundles
\Sigma_{\mu}\mathcal{U}_{d}\otimes\Sigma_{\nu}\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp}
are computed
via the Borel-Weil-Bott theorem as follows (see
[
D76
,
Kap84
,
Kap88
]
). Let
\rho=(n,n-1,\dots,1)
be
the half sum of positive roots
and let us denote by
\ell(\sigma)
the length of a permutation
\sigma
. Also, let

\kappa=(\mu,\nu)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Then all the cohomologies of
\Sigma_{\mu}\mathcal{U}_{d}\otimes\Sigma_{\nu}\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp}

vanish unless
\kappa+\rho
has no coinciding entries. If this condition holds, then

(1.4)

H^{k}(\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V),\Sigma_{\mu}\mathcal{U}_{d}\otimes\Sigma_{\nu}\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp})=\begin{cases}\Sigma_{\sigma(\kappa+\rho)-\rho}V^{*},&k=\ell(\sigma),\\
0,&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\sigma(\kappa+\rho)
is strictly decreasing.

1.6.
Truncated Grassmannians and their relatives

Recall the cyclic vector
w_{r}\in(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r}
– the image of the highest weight vector

v_{[d]}\in\Lambda^{d}V
.

Definition 1.14
.

For
r=1,\dots,d
we define

X_{r}=\overline{\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d}).[w_{r}]}\subset{\mathbb{P}}((\Lambda^{d}V)_{r}).

So,
X_{r}
is the closure of the orbit of the abelian unipotent group
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{d(n-d)}
.

Lemma 1.15
.

All varieties
X_{r}
are birationally isomorphic and admit the action of the parabolic
group
P_{d}^{-}
. The boundary members of the family are

X_{1}={\mathbb{P}}^{d(n-d)}
and
X_{d}=\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
.

Proof.

All the varieties
X_{r}
share the same open part
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d}).[w_{r}]

isomorphic to
{\mathbb{A}}^{d(n-d)}
. The truncated spaces
(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r}
admit the action of

P_{d}^{-}\supset\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
and
[w_{r}]
is invariant with respect ot the Levi part

SL(V_{d})\times SL(V_{n-d})
. Hence the
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
action on
X_{r}
extends to the
action of
P_{d}^{-}
.

For
r=1
the space
(\Lambda^{d}V)_{1}
is spanned by
w_{1}
and
the images of vectors of the form
v_{I}
with
I=[d]\setminus\{j\}\cup\{i\}
for some

1\leq j\leq d
,
d+1\leq i\leq n
. Hence, we identify
(\Lambda^{d}V)_{1}
with
{\mathbb{K}}w_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}
.
The
{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}
action on
(\Lambda^{d}V)_{1}
is trivial on the second summand and the action
on
w_{1}
is induced
by the isomorphism
{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}\otimes{\mathbb{K}}w_{1}\simeq{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}
. Hence for
a\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}
one has

\exp(a)[w_{1}]=[w_{1}+a]
and
X_{1}={\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}V)_{1}={\mathbb{P}}^{d(n-d)}
.
Finally,
(\Lambda^{d}V)_{d}=\Lambda^{d}V
and hence
X_{d}=\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
.
∎

Remark 1.16
.

Identifying
{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}
with
{\mathbb{A}}^{d(n-d)}
, one gets the maps
{\mathbb{A}}^{d(n-d)}\to X_{r}
. In particular,
for
r=d
one gets the so called Landsberg-Manivel map
{\mathbb{A}}^{d(n-d)}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)

(see
[
LM02
,
FW25
]
).

In
[
BSS25
,
FSS25
]
the authors considered the closure
of the graph
of the birational map
{\mathbb{P}}^{d(n-d)}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
, induced
by the Landsberg-Manivel map. This graph closure
\mathrm{G}_{d}(V)
can be described as

(1.5)

\mathrm{G}_{d}(V)=\overline{\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d}).\left([w_{1}]\times[w_{d}]\right)}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{d(n-d)}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)=X_{1}\times X_{d},

where
[w_{d}]=[v_{[d]}]
is the highest weight line in
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)=X_{d}
.
It is proved in
[
Fe24
]
that all fibers of the natural projection
\pi:\mathrm{G}_{d}(V)\to\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)

are projective spaces. By definition, the fibers are subvarieties of the projective space

{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}(V)_{1})\simeq{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{K}}w_{1}\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(V_{d},V_{n-d}))
.
The following is proved in
[
Fe24
]
:

(i)

if

, then
\pi^{-1}U
is a single point,

(ii)

\pi^{-1}U\subset{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{Hom}(V_{d},V_{n-d}))
for

such that

,

(iii)

if

, then

\pi^{-1}U\simeq{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{Hom}(V_{d}/\mathrm{pr}(U),U\cap V_{n-d})),

where
\mathrm{pr}:V\to V_{d}
is a projection along
V_{n-d}
. In particular,
if the dimension of
U\cap V_{n-d}
is equal to

, then
\pi^{-1}U\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{m^{2}-1}
.

Example 1.17
.

Let
n=4
,

. Then the preimage of the point
p_{\{3,4\}}
is isomorphic to

{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
. Outside of this point the map

is one-to-one.

Remark 1.18
.

The varieties

admit the following explicit Plücker type realization. Let

be auxiliary variables with

,

2.
Truncated Grassmannians and blow-ups

The goal of this section is to describe the truncated Grassmannians and blow-ups
\mathrm{Bl}_{S_{r}}\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
for the
P^{-}_{d}

invariant Schubert varieties
S_{r}
. To simplify the notation, in this section
we denote the blow-up
\mathrm{Bl}_{S_{r}}\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
by
\mathrm{Bl}_{r}
.

2.1.
Truncated Grassmannians

Recall the truncated wedge powers from subsection
1.3

(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r-1}\simeq\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-1}\mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda^{i}V_{d},\Lambda^{i}V_{n-d})

and the truncated Grassmannians
X_{r-1}
sitting inside
{\mathbb{P}}((\Lambda^{d}V)_{r-1})

(Definition
1.14
).
In order to describe the geometric structure of
X_{r-1}
we prepare the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

The birational projection map

{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}V)\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}((\Lambda^{d}V)_{r-1})

restricted to
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
is well defined embedding outside
S_{r-1}
.

Proof.

We note that the complement
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\setminus S_{r-1}
is the union of Schubert cells
(open Schubert varieties)

S^{\circ}_{J}
with
J\in\binom{[n]}{d}
,
\deg J\leq r-2
. The affine coordinates on
S^{\circ}_{J}

are
a_{i,j}
with

j\in J
,
i\notin J
and
i>j
; for
J=(j_{1}<\dots<j_{d})
the Plücker coordinates
\Delta_{I}
of a
point
(a_{i,j})
are the minors of the matrix of the map sending
v_{s}
,
s\in[d]
to

v_{j_{s}}+\sum_{i>j}a_{i,j}v_{i}
.

Now let
(a_{i,j})
be coordinates of
U\in\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\setminus S_{r-1}
. Under the projection

{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}V)\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r-1}
one forgets all the Plücker coordinates

\Delta_{I}(U)
such that
\deg I\geq r
. We need to check that no information is lost
under this projection for
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\setminus S_{r-1}
. To this end, we take a pair

i>j
,
j\in J
,
i\notin J
and
consider the collection
J\setminus\{j\}\cup\{i\}
. Then

\Delta_{J\setminus\{j\}\cup\{i\}}(U)=\pm a_{i,j}\ \text{ and }\ \deg J\setminus\{j\}\cup\{i\}\leq r-1.

Hence
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\setminus S_{r-1}
embeds into
{\mathbb{P}}((\Lambda^{d}V)_{r-1})
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

The truncated Grassmannian
X_{r-1}
contains
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\setminus S_{r-1}
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
r=2
(the
r=1
case is trivial, since
S_{0}=\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
). Then Corollary above says that

X_{1}\supset\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\setminus S_{1}
, which is the open Schubert cell in the Grassmannian
(the
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
orbit of the highest weight line). One easily sees that
X_{1}
is simply
the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}(V)_{1})\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{d(n-d)}
.

Now let us describe the complement of
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\setminus S_{r-1}
in
X_{r-1}
.
Let
R_{r-1;d,n-d}
be the algebra generated by all
(r-1)
minors of a
d\times(n-d)
matrix
inside a polynomial ring in variables
z_{i,j}
,
d+1\leq i\leq n
,
1\leq j\leq d

[
BV88
,
BCV13
]
.
Then
\mathrm{Proj}(R_{r-1;d,n-d})
is
isomorphic to the closure in
{\mathbb{P}}\mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda^{r-1}V_{d},\Lambda^{r-1}V_{n-d})
of the
set of elements of the form
[\Lambda^{r-1}\varphi]
,
\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}(V_{d},V_{n-d})
.
By definition, we have the embedding
\mathrm{Proj}(R_{r-1;d,n-d})\subset{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r-1}

(since
(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r-1}\supset\mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda^{r-1}V_{d},\Lambda^{r-1}V_{n-d})
).

Proposition 2.4
.

X_{r-1}
is the union of
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\setminus S_{r-1}
and
\mathrm{Proj}(R_{r-1;d,n-d})
.

Proof.

The complement of
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\setminus S_{r-1}
in
X_{r-1}
is equal to the intersection

X_{r-1}\cap{\mathbb{P}}\mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda^{r-1}V_{d},\Lambda^{r-1}V_{n-d})
. We claim that this
intersection coincides with
\mathrm{Proj}(R_{r-1;d,n-d})
. In fact, by definition,
X_{r-1}
is
the closure inside
{\mathbb{P}}((\Lambda^{d}V)_{r-1})
of the open
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
orbit of
[w_{r-1}]
,
which is naturally identified with
{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(V_{d},V_{n-d})
.
Let us denote the matrix of a homomorphism
\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}(V_{d},V_{n-d})
by
z_{i,j}
,

i=d+1,\dots,n
,
j=1,\dots,d
.
The
I
-th Plücker coordinate of the point corresponding
to
\varphi
is the minor
\Delta_{I}
of the submatrix of the
n\times d
matrix

z_{0}\mathrm{Id}_{d\times d}+\varphi
supported on rows from
I
(here
z_{0}
is an auxiliary
variable). Hence,
\Delta_{I}
is a polynomial in
z_{i,j}
of total degree
\deg I
multiplied
by
z_{0}^{d-\deg I}
; note that possible values of
\deg I
for
(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r-1}
are from

0
to
r-1
.

Now assume we have a polynomial in Plücker variables vanishing at all points of the open orbit.
We are interested in all the solutions such that
\Delta_{I}=0
for
\deg I<r-1
.
We claim that these are exactly the zeroes of all the polynomials involving only the maximal
degree terms (i.e. only
\Delta_{I}
with
\deg I=r-1
). In fact, all the relations for the
points of the orbit are degree-homogeneous. Hence, if a monomial of a relation involves
\Delta_{I}

with
\deg I<r-1
as a factor, then any other monomial should also have a factor

\Delta_{I^{\prime}}
with
\deg I^{\prime}<r-1
. Since we are looking at the zeroes of the
Plücker coordinates of degree less than
r-1
, such a relation automatically holds true.

Finally, let us look at the coordinate
\Delta_{I}
with
\deg I=r-1
.
There exist
\alpha=(1\leq\alpha_{1}<\dots<\alpha_{r-1}\leq d)
and

\beta=(d+1\leq\beta_{1}<\dots<\beta_{r-1}\leq n)
, such that
\Delta_{I}(\varphi)
is the

(r-1)
minor of the matrix
\varphi
supported on rows
\alpha
and columns
\beta
(multiplied
by
z_{0}^{d-r+1}
). Hence the relations between such
\Delta_{I}
are exactly the relations in

R_{r-1;d,n-d}
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

The scheme
\mathrm{Proj}(R_{r-1;d,n-d})
is isomorphic to the the closure of the image of
the natural birational map between the projective homomorphism spaces

{\mathbb{P}}\mathrm{Hom}(V_{d},V_{n-d})\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda^{r-1}V_{d},\Lambda^{r-1}V_{n-d})
sending

[\varphi]
to
[\Lambda^{r-1}\varphi]
. These schemes are normal, Cohen-Macaulay and have
rational singularities (see
[
BV88
,
BCRV22
]
).

2.2.
Fibers of the blow-ups

Let
\pi_{r}:\mathrm{Bl}_{r}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)

be the projection map; our goal here is to describe the fibers
\pi_{r}^{-1}(U)
.
Let
J\in\binom{[n]}{d}
be a cardinality
d
subset and let
p_{J}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
be the
corresponding point in the Grassmannian.
Let
A_{J}
be the standard affine cell containing
p_{J}
, i.e.

A_{J}\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(p_{J},V/p_{J})\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{span}(v_{j},j\in J),\mathrm{span}(v_{j},j\notin J)).

To a homomorphism
\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}(p_{J},V/p_{J})
we attach
the subspace
U_{\varphi}=\mathrm{span}(v_{j}+\varphi v_{j},j\in J)\in\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
; in particular,
for
\varphi=0
one gets
U_{0}=p_{J}
.

We want to describe the intersection
A_{J}\cap S_{r}
. Let
m=\deg J

be the cardinality of
J_{>d}
. Let
X_{\varphi}
be the
m\times m
submatrix of
\varphi

supported on columns from
J_{>d}
and rows from
[d]\setminus J_{\leq d}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

A point
U_{\varphi}
belongs to the intersection
A_{J}\cap S_{r}
if and only if
the rank of
X_{\varphi}
is at most
m-r
.

Proof.

The condition
\mathrm{rk}X_{\varphi}\leq m-r
is equivalent to

\dim\bigl(\mathrm{span}\{v_{j}+\varphi(v_{j}),\ j\in J_{>d}\}\cap V_{n-d}\bigr)\geq r,

which in turn is equivalent to
\dim U_{\varphi}\cap V_{n-d}\geq r
.
∎

Let
Y_{N,c}\subset\mathrm{Mat}_{N\times N}
be the determinantal variety consisting
of matrices of rank at most
c

[
Eis95
]
.
The variety
Y_{N,c}
is the vanishing set of the prime ideal generated by all
(c+1)\times(c+1)

minors of an
N\times N
matrix.

Corollary 2.7
.

Assume
m=|J_{>d}|\geq r
. Then the intersection
A_{J}\cap S_{r}
is isomorphic to a product of a determinantal variety and
an affine space.

Proof.

The condition
\mathrm{rk}X_{\varphi}\leq m-r
is equivalents to the vanishing of all

m-r+1
minors of
X_{\varphi}
. Since there are no other conditions on the
entries of the matrix of
\varphi
, we arrive at the following isomorphism:

A_{J}\cap S_{r}\simeq Y_{m,m-r}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{d(n-d)-m^{2}}

(here
d(n-d)-m^{2}
is equal to the number of entries of the matrix
\varphi
which are not
in
X_{\varphi}
).
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Here are several extreme cases:

•

if
r=1
, then
A_{J}\cap S_{r}
is cut out by a single equation
\det X_{\varphi}=0
;

•

if
r=2
, then
A_{J}\cap S_{r}
is the common vanishing set of all maximal minors of
X_{\varphi}
;

•

if
r=m
, then
A_{J}\cap S_{r}
is just the affine space
{\mathbb{A}}^{d(n-d)-r^{2}}
;

•

if
r>m
, then
A_{J}\cap S_{r}
is empty.

Now let us consider the entries of the matrix
X_{\varphi}
as variables.
Let
R_{m,r}
be the subring of the polynomial ring in entries of
X_{\varphi}
generated
by all
m-r+1
minors
\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}

for all

\alpha,\beta\in\binom{m}{m-r+1}
. The projective spectrum of the ring
R_{m,r}
is isomorphic to
the space of partial collineations. Namely, let

(2.1)

E_{1}=\mathrm{span}\{v_{j}:\ j\in J_{>d}\},\ E_{2}=\mathrm{span}\{v_{j}:\ j\in[d]\setminus J_{\leq d}\}

(in particular,
X_{\varphi}\in\mathrm{Hom}(E_{1},E_{2})
).
We consider the birational map

{\mathbb{P}}\mathrm{Hom}(E_{1},E_{2})\to{\mathbb{P}}\mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda^{m-r+1}E_{1},\Lambda^{m-r+1}E_{2}),\ f\mapsto\Lambda^{m-r+1}f

and define partial collineations
\mathrm{Col}_{m-r+1}(E_{1},E_{2})
as the closure of the image of this map.
We will also denote this space by
\mathrm{Col}_{m-r+1}(m)
, where
m=\dim E_{1}=\dim E_{2}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

One has
\mathrm{Col}_{m-r+1}(m)\simeq\mathrm{Proj}(R_{m,r})
.

Proof.

By definition, the variety
\mathrm{Col}_{m-r+1}(m)
sits in the projective space

{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{m-r+1}E_{1},\Lambda^{m-r+1}E_{2})

with the coordinates labeled by pairs
\alpha,\beta\in\binom{[m]}{m-r+1}
.
The defining relations cutting out
\mathrm{Col}_{m-r+1}(m)
are exactly the (homogeneous) algebraic
relations between the minors
\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}
. ∎

Now let
\pi_{r}
be the standard projection from the blow-up of
A_{J}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
along
A_{J}\cap S_{r}
to
A_{J}
.
We arrive at the following proposition, where
E_{1},E_{2}
are defined in (
2.1
).

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
m=|J_{>d}|
. Then
\pi_{r}^{-1}(p_{J})\simeq\mathrm{Col}_{m+1-r}(E_{1},E_{2})
.

Proof.

By definition, the blow-up
\mathrm{Bl}_{A_{J}\cap S_{r}}A_{J}
sits inside the product of

A_{J}
and
{\mathbb{P}}\mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda^{m-r+1}E_{1},\Lambda^{m-r+1}E_{2})
as the closure of the
set of points of the form
(\varphi,[\Lambda^{m-r+1}X_{\varphi}])
.
Now
p_{J}\in A_{J}
corresponds to
\varphi=0
and hence the fiber over
p_{J}
is exactly
\mathrm{Proj}(R_{m,r})\simeq\mathrm{Col}_{m-r+1}(m)
, which is
realized as
\mathrm{Col}_{m-r+1}(E_{1},E_{2})
.
∎

Let
\mathrm{pr}:V\to V_{d}
be the projection sending
V_{n-d}
to zero.

Corollary 2.11
.

For a point
U\in\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
such that
\dim U\cap V_{n-d}=m
, one has

\pi_{r}^{-1}U\simeq\mathrm{Col}_{m-r+1}(U\cap V_{n-d},V_{d}/\mathrm{pr}(U))\simeq\mathrm{Col}_{m-r+1}(m).

Proof.

Follows from Proposition
2.10
by shifting the center of the cell
A_{J}
from
p_{J}
to
U
. ∎

Example 2.12
.

Let
r=2
. Then all the fibers of the blow-up
\mathrm{Bl}_{S_{2}}\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
are projective spaces.
In fact, the algebra
R_{m,2}
is generated by all the maximal minors (of size
(m-1)
),
which are algebraically independent. So the fiber over a point

is isomorphic to

, where

.

Remark 2.13
.

We note that the defining relations between the generating minors

in

3.
Blow-ups of Grassmannians and ideal sheaves

The goal of this section is to construct a resolution of the ideal sheaf of
S_{r}
.
As a consequence we derive the embedding of the blow-up

\mathrm{Bl}_{S_{r}}\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
into
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\times X_{r-1}
.
The existence of such an embedding also follows from the general
construction from Section
5
.

We consider the desingularization
[
BK05
,
D74
]

\widetilde{S}_{r}=\{(U_{r},U_{d})\in\mathrm{Gr}_{r}(V_{n-d})\times\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V):\ U_{r}\subset U_{d}\}.

Clearly,
\widetilde{S}_{r}
is smooth, as fibration over
\mathrm{Gr}_{r}(V_{n-d})
with smooth fibers, and admits
a natural projection
q:\widetilde{S}_{r}\to S_{r}
sending
(U_{r},U_{d})
to
U_{d}

(since
U_{d}\supset U_{r}\subset V_{n-d}
, one gets
\dim U_{d}\cap V_{n-d}\geq r
).
For a point
(U_{r},U_{d})\in\widetilde{S}_{r}
one has the following sequence

(3.1)

U_{r}\hookrightarrow V_{n-d}\subset V\twoheadrightarrow V/U_{d}\to 0

and hence
\widetilde{S}_{r}
is identified with the scheme of zeroes of a section of the following vector
bundle on
\mathrm{Gr}_{r}(V_{n-d})\times\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{r}
be the tautological
r
-dimensional
vector bundle on
\mathrm{Gr}_{r}(V_{n-d})
and let
\mathcal{U}_{d}
be the tautological
d
-dimensional
vector bundle on
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
. Then (
3.1
) implies that
\widetilde{S}_{r}
is
the scheme of zeroes of an element of
H^{0}(\mathcal{U}_{r}^{*}\otimes V(\mathcal{O})/\mathcal{U}_{d})
.
We thus arrive at the following lemma
(we use the standard notation
\mathcal{U}^{\perp}_{d}=(V(\mathcal{O})/\mathcal{U}_{d})^{*}
).

Lemma 3.1
.

The ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{\widetilde{S}_{r}}
admits the following Koszul resolution

(3.2)

\dots\to\Lambda^{2}(\mathcal{U}_{r}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp})\to\mathcal{U}_{r}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp}\to\mathcal{I}_{\widetilde{S}_{r}}\to 0.

Our next goal is to compute the resolution of the sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}}
. To this end we
use the decomposition

(3.3)

\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{U}_{r}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp})\simeq\bigoplus_{\lambda}\Sigma_{\lambda}\mathcal{U}_{r}\otimes\Sigma_{\lambda^{t}}\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp},

where the sum ranges over partitions

(3.4)

\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{r}),\ \lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{r}=k,\ n-d\geq\lambda_{1}

and
\Sigma
is the Schur functor. Recall that
S_{r}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
is a Schubert variety and thus has rational singularities
[
BK05
,
Kum02
]
. Hence
we can use the desingularization
q:\widetilde{S}_{r}\to S_{r}
in order to produce
the resolution for the sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}}
.
More precisely, we compute the direct image of (
3.2
) with respect to the map
q_{*}
.
To this end, we need to compute the cohomologies of the sheaves
\Sigma_{\lambda}\mathcal{U}_{r}
along the
fibers of the map
q
.

For a tuple
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})
we use the notation
\lambda^{*}=(-\lambda_{r},\dots,-\lambda_{1})
and
|\lambda|=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}
.
We also denote by
\rho
the collection

(n-d,\dots,1)
and if
\lambda^{*}+\rho
has no coinciding entries, we let

\sigma\in S_{n-d}
denote the permutation reshuffling
\lambda^{*}+\rho

into a decreasing order (see subsection
1.5
).

Lemma 3.2
.

One has the following complex of sheaves on
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
:

0\to\mathcal{F}_{r^{2}}\to\dots\to\mathcal{F}_{1}\to\mathcal{F}_{0}\to\mathcal{O}_{S_{r}}\to 0,

with

\mathcal{F}_{k}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}n-d\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{r}\geq 0\\
|\lambda|-\ell(\sigma)=k\end{subarray}}\Sigma_{\sigma(\lambda^{*}+\rho)-\rho}V_{n-d}^{*}\otimes\Sigma_{\lambda^{t}}\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp},

where the tuples
\lambda
which show up satisfy the condition that
\lambda^{*}+\rho
has no coinciding entries.

Proof.

We use the Koszul resolution (
3.2
), the decomposition (
3.3
) and the Borel-Weil-Bott formula (
1.4
).
Let us show that the maximal possible value for
|\lambda|-\ell(\sigma)
is equal to
r^{2}
. By definition,

(3.5)

\lambda^{*}+\rho=(n-d-\lambda_{r},\dots,n-d-r+1-\lambda_{1},n-d-r,\dots,1).

Let

\mu_{1}=n-d-\lambda_{r},\ \mu_{2}=n-d-1-\lambda_{r-1},\dots,\mu_{r}=n-d-r+1-\lambda_{1}.

We have the following properties:

•

n-d\geq\mu_{1}>\dots>\mu_{r}\geq-r+1
,

•

\mu_{i}\notin\{n-d,\dots,1\}
,
i=1,\dots,r
.

We conclude that there are a total of
\binom{2r}{r}
terms (tensor products) in all
\mathcal{F}_{k}
.

Let
a
be the number of non-positive
\mu_{i}
’s. Then for the permutation

\sigma\in S_{n-d}
such that
\sigma(\lambda^{*}+\rho)
is non-increasing
one has
\ell(\sigma)=a(n-d-r)
.
Our goal is to show that the possible values of
N=|\lambda|-\ell(\sigma)
are from zero to
r^{2}
.
Note that if
a=r
, then
\mu_{i}=-i+1
,
\lambda_{i}=n-d
for all
i
and hence
N=r^{2}
.
Now if one passes from
a
to
a-1
, then the value of
\ell(\sigma)
becomes smaller by
n-d-r
.

Let
M_{a}(\lambda)
be the maximal possible value of
|\lambda|
for a fixed
a
; in particular,
M_{r}(\lambda)=r(n-d)
.
Also let
m_{a}(\mu)
be the minimal possible value of
|\mu|
for a fixed
a
; in particular,
M_{r}(\lambda)+m_{r}(\mu)
does not depend on
a
.
One easily sees
that
m_{a}(\mu)
is attained at

\mu=(n-d+r-a,\dots,n-d+1,-r+a,\dots,-r+1).

Hence,
m_{a-1}(\mu)=m_{a}(\mu)+n-d-r+2(r-a)+1
and

M_{a-1}(\mu)=m_{a}(\mu)-(n-d-r+2(r-a)+1).

So the maximal possible value of
N=|\lambda|-\ell(\sigma)
becomes
smaller by
2(r-a)+1
when we pass from
a
to
a-1
. Hence the maximal
value is
r^{2}
showing up for
a=r
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

As shown in the proof above, there are totally
\binom{2r}{r}
terms in all
\mathcal{F}_{k}
,
k=0,\dots,r^{2}
. Each term is a tensor product of an
irreducible finite-dimensional representation of
\mathfrak{gl}(V_{n-d})
and
a sheaf on
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
which is equal to a Schur functor applied to
\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp}
.

Example 3.4
.

One has

(i)

\mathcal{F}_{0}=\mathcal{O}
, the term corresponds to
\lambda=0
,

(ii)

\mathcal{F}_{1}=\Sigma_{(0^{r-1}(-1)^{n-d-r+1})}V_{n-d}^{*}\otimes\Sigma_{(1^{n-d-r+1}0^{r-1})}\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp}
, the term corresponds to
\lambda=(n-d-r+1,0^{r-1})
,

(iii)

\mathcal{F}_{r^{2}}=\mathcal{O}(-r)
, the term corresponds to
\lambda=((n-d)^{r})
.

Our next goal is to compute the sections of the sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}}(1)
.
We first formulate the following direct consequence of Lemma
3.2

(recall that
\mathcal{F}_{0}\simeq~\mathcal{O}
).

Corollary 3.5
.

The sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}}(1)
admits the following resolution

0\to\mathcal{F}_{r^{2}}(1)\to\dots\to\mathcal{F}_{1}(1)\to\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}}(1)\to 0.

Let us introduce the following notation

\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}=\Sigma_{\sigma(\lambda^{*}+\rho)-\rho}V_{n-d}^{*}\otimes\Sigma_{\lambda^{t}}\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp}.

In particular,

\mathcal{F}_{k}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}n-d\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{r}\geq 0\\
|\lambda|-\ell(\sigma)=k\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

H^{0}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(1))\neq 0
if and only if
\lambda=(n-d-m,0^{r-1})
for
m=0,\dots,r-1
. The sheaf
\mathcal{T}_{(n-d-m,0^{r-1})}
is a summand of

\mathcal{F}_{r-m}
. One has

H^{0}(\mathcal{T}_{(n-d-m,0^{n-d-1})}(1))\simeq\Sigma_{(0^{r-1},(-1)^{n-d-r},m-r)}V^{*}_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{m}V.

Proof.

By definition
H^{0}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(1))\neq 0
if and only if
H^{0}(\Sigma_{\lambda^{t}}\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp}(1))\neq 0
. Recall that
\mathcal{O}(1)=\Sigma_{(-1,\dots,-1)}\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp}
. Hence

(3.6)

\Sigma_{\lambda^{t}}\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp}(1)=\Sigma_{\lambda^{t}-(1,\dots,1)}\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp}.

By (
1.4
) for a collection
\mu=(\mu_{1}\geq\dots\geq\mu_{d})
the space of sections of
\Sigma_{\mu}\mathcal{U}^{\perp}
is
non-zero if and only if
\mu_{1}\leq 0
. Hence the right hand side of (
3.6
) admits a non-trivial
section if and only if all the entries of
\lambda^{t}
are at most one, so

\lambda
has a single non-trivial part
\lambda_{1}
(not exceeding
n-d
).
The condition

n-d-r+1-\lambda_{1}\notin[n-d-r]
implies
\lambda_{1}\geq n-d-r+1
.

The subscript
k
of the sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{k}
containing the term
\mathcal{T}_{(n-d-m,0^{n-d-1})}
is
computed as
k=|\lambda|-\ell(\sigma)=n-d-m-(n-d-r)=r-m
, which proves the second claim of the proposition. Finally, let us compute the corresponding spaces of sections.

For
\lambda=(n-d-m,0^{r-1})
one has
\sigma(\lambda^{*}+\rho)-\rho=(0^{r-1},(-1)^{n-d-r},m-r)
.
Therefore

\Sigma_{\sigma(\lambda^{*}+\rho)-\rho}V_{n-d}^{*}\simeq\Sigma_{(0^{r-1},(-1)^{n-d-r},m-r)}V^{*}_{n-d}.

Computing the sections of the corresponding sheaf one gets

H^{0}(\Sigma_{\lambda^{t}-(1,\dots,1)}\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp})=H^{0}(\Sigma_{0^{d-m},(-1)^{m}}\mathcal{U}_{d}^{\perp})\simeq\Lambda^{m}V.

∎

In Proposition
A.2
(see Appendix
A
) we use Lemma
3.6

to identify the dual
space of sections of
\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}}(1)
with the truncated wedge power
\Lambda^{d}(V)_{r}
.
Recall the truncated Grassmannians

X_{r}=\overline{\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})[w_{r}]}\subset{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}(V)_{r}).

Theorem 3.7
.

The blow-up
\mathrm{Bl}_{r}
admits a closed embedding into
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\times X_{r-1}
.
The image of the embedding is the closure of the
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
orbit of the product of

V_{d}
and
[w_{r-1}]
.

Proof.

We want to construct the
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
equivariant embedding

\mathrm{Bl}_{r}
into
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\times{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r-1}
.
Let
\mathcal{I}_{r}
be the ideal sheaf of the subvariety
S_{r}
.
We use the following standard construction involving the relative
\mathrm{Proj}
construction.
Let

(3.7)

A=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}S^{k}(W^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-1))=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}S^{k}(W^{*})\otimes\mathcal{O}(-k).

Then
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\times{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r-1}\simeq\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)}A
, because
{\mathbb{P}}(W)=\mathrm{Proj}\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}S^{k}(W^{*})
and twisting by
\mathcal{O}(-k)
we get the
desired result.

Recall that by definition

\mathrm{Bl}_{r}=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)}\left(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}}\oplus(\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}})^{2}\oplus\dots\right).

Hence in order to obtain the desired embedding

\mathrm{Bl}_{r}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\times{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r-1}
it suffices to
construct a surjective homomorphism of algebras

(3.8)

\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}S^{k}((\Lambda^{d}V)_{r-1}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-1))\to\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}})^{k}.

Using Proposition
A.2
we obtain the surjective map

(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r-1}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-1)\to\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}}
, which implies the desired embedding.

To finalize the proof we note that
\mathrm{Bl}_{r}
is realized inside

\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\times{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r}
and admits the action of the group
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})

with an open dense orbit. In fact, the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
contains an open
dense orbit
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})V_{d}
, which does not intersect with
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
invariant
S_{r}
.
Therefore, the preimage
of
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})V_{d}
in the blow-up is an open dense
\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
orbit, since the blow-up is
irreducible. Hence it suffices to prove that the preimage of
V_{d}
in
\mathrm{Bl}_{r}

is exactly
V_{d}\times[w_{r}]
.

To this end we restrict the surjection (
3.8
) to the open cell

C=\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})V_{d}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
; in particular,
C
is identified with
{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}
.
Since
S_{r}
does not intersect this cell, the right hand
side is the direct sum
\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{O}(C)
and the left hand side is given by

\mathcal{O}[C]\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}S^{k}(\Lambda^{d}V_{r})^{*}
(here
\mathcal{O}(C)
is just the algebras of functions on the
affine space
C
). The surjection between these restrictions is
\mathcal{O}(C)
linear and hence
determined by
the
{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}
-equivariant map
(\Lambda^{d}V_{r})^{*}\to\mathcal{O}(C)
(coming from the
k=1
part).
The image of this map consists of functions of degree at most one with

(\Lambda^{d}V_{r})^{*}\subset W^{*}
mapping to the constants and
\mathrm{Hom}(V_{n-d},V_{d})\subset(\Lambda^{d}V_{r})^{*}

mapping to the space of linear functions, i.e.
\xi
is mapped to the function on
C

taking value
\xi(w+c)
for a point
c\in C
(recall that
W\simeq{\mathbb{K}}w\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}
).
We conclude that the preimage of the point
V_{d}

in the blow-up is mapped to itself times
[w_{r}]
inside
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\times{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r}
.
∎

4.
Mixed case and collineations

In this section we introduce a family of pairwise birationally isomorphic
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{d(n-d)}

varieties which include the truncated Grassmannians
X_{r}
and the blow-ups
\mathrm{Bl}_{r}
.
In particular, the ”largest” member of the family is smooth
and serves as a desingularization for all other members. We first recall the construction
of (partial) collineations
[
L88
,
Th99
,
Vain84
]
.

4.1.
Collineations

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be two vector spaces of dimension
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
(in what follows
we will only need the case
d_{1}=d_{2}
). Let
{\bf s}=(1\leq s_{1}<\dots<s_{k}\leq\min(d_{1},d_{2}))

be a collection of integers.
Let
\mathrm{Hom}^{\circ}(E_{1},E_{2})
be the full rank homomorphisms from
E_{1}
to
E_{2}
.
For
\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}^{\circ}(E_{1},E_{2})

we consider the corresponding (non-zero) elements

\Lambda^{s_{i}}\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda^{s_{i}}E_{1},\Lambda^{s_{i}}E_{2})
(the coordinates
of
\Lambda^{s_{i}}
are the minors of
\varphi
). We thus obtain the birational map

F_{\bf s}:{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{Hom}(E_{1},E_{2}))\dashrightarrow\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda^{s_{i}}E_{1},\Lambda^{s_{i}}E_{2})).

The space of partial collineations
\mathrm{Col}_{\bf s}(E_{1},E_{2})
is defined as the closure
of the image of
F_{\bf s}
:

\mathrm{Col}_{\bf s}(E_{1},E_{2})=\overline{\{([\Lambda^{s_{1}}\varphi],\dots,[\Lambda^{s_{k}}\varphi]),\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}^{\circ}(E_{1},E_{2})\}}

(as usual, for a vector
\varphi
we denote by
[\varphi]
the corresponding line in the
projective space).

Example 4.1
.

Here are several examples of the collineation varieties.

(i)

If
k=1
,
s_{1}=1
, then
\mathrm{Col}_{\bf s}(E_{1},E_{2})={\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{Hom}(E_{1},E_{2}))
.

(ii)

If
k=1
,
s_{1}>1
, then

\mathrm{Col}_{\bf s}(E_{1},E_{2})
is the projective spectrum of the algebra generated by the
s_{1}
minors
of a
d_{1}\times d_{2}
matrix
[
BV88
,
BC01
,
dCEP80
]
.

(iii)

If
k=1
,
d_{1}<d_{2}
and
s_{1}=d_{1}
, then
\mathrm{Col}_{\bf s}(E_{1},E_{2})\simeq\mathrm{Gr}_{d_{1}}(E_{2})
.

(iv)

If
k=1
,
d_{1}=d_{2}
,
s_{1}=d_{1}-1
, then
\mathrm{Col}_{\bf s}(E_{1},E_{2})\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{d_{1}^{2}-1}
, since
the
(d_{1}-1)\times(d_{1}-1)
minors of a matrix are algebraically independent.

(v)

If
k
is arbitrary,
s_{1}=1
, then there is a natural projection map
from
\mathrm{Col}_{\bf s}(E_{1},E_{2})
to
{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{Hom}(E_{1},E_{2}))
.

(vi)

If
d_{1}=d_{2}
,
k=d_{1}-1
,
{\bf s}=(1,\dots,d_{1}-1)
, then
\mathrm{Col}_{\bf s}(E_{1},E_{2})
is smooth
and projects onto all other collineation varieties.

4.2.
Mixed truncated Grassmannians

As above, we fix
d
and
n
such that
d\leq n-d
and consider
a collection
{\bf r}=(1<r_{1}<\dots<r_{k}\leq d)
.
Recall the chain of embedded Schubert varieties
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\supset S_{2}\supset\dots\supset S_{d}
.
We define the blow-up

\mathrm{Bl}_{\bf r}\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
as follows. Let
\pi_{r}:\mathrm{Bl}_{r}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)

be the standard projection (as above,
\mathrm{Bl}_{r}=\mathrm{Bl}_{S_{r}}\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
).

Definition 4.2
.

The mixed blow-up
\mathrm{Bl}_{\bf r}\subset\prod_{r\in{\bf r}}\mathrm{Bl}_{r}

is the closure of the following set

(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}):\ \pi_{r_{1}}(x_{1})=\dots=\pi_{r_{k}}(x_{k}),\ \pi_{r_{i}}(x_{i})\notin\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{r_{i}}.

Remark 4.3
.

Since
r_{1}<\dots<r_{k}
, the union
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{r_{i}}
is equal to
S_{r_{1}}
.

The varieties
\mathrm{Bl}_{\bf r}
admit the following description in terms of truncated

Recall that

All the blow-ups

Theorem 4.4
.

The variety

(4.1)

\mathrm{Bl}_{\bf r}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\times X_{r_{1}-1}\times\dots\times X_{r_{k}-1};

\mathrm{Bl}_{\bf r}\simeq\overline{\exp({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})(V_{d}\times[w_{r_{1}-1}]\times\dots\times[w_{r_{k}-1}])}.

Proof.

By Theorem

∎

Corollary 4.5
.

All varieties
\mathrm{Bl}_{\bf r}
share the same open part (the open

The following proposition describes the fibers of the natural projection map

\pi_{\bf r}:\mathrm{Bl}_{\bf r}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
.
Let
U\in\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
satisfies
\dim U\cap V_{n-d}=m

We denote by
\mathrm{pr}
the projection map
V\to V_{d}

whose kernel is

.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
U\in\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
and
\dim U\cap V_{n-d}=m
. Then
\pi_{\bf r}^{-1}U
is isomorphic to the space of partial collineations

\mathrm{Col}_{(m+1-r_{j},\dots,m+1-r_{1})}(U\cap V_{n-d},V_{d}/\mathrm{pr}(U)).

Proof.

By definition, the mixed blow-up
\mathrm{Bl}_{\bf r}
sits inside the product of the Grassmannian

\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
and the blow-ups
\mathrm{Bl}_{r_{i}}
. For
U\in\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
as above, let
A_{U}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)

be the standard open affine cell containing
U
,
A_{U}\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(U,V/U)
.
Definition
4.2
implies that
\pi_{\bf r}^{-1}(A_{U})
is the closure of the set
of points of the form
(x,\pi_{r_{1}}^{-1}(x),\dots,\pi^{-1}_{r_{m}}(x))
for

x\in A_{U}\setminus S_{r_{1}}
. We note that if
i>j
, i.e.
r_{i}>m
, then
A_{U}\cap S_{r_{i}}=\emptyset

(since for any

one has

).
We conclude that the fiber
\pi_{\bf r}^{-1}U
sits inside the product of fibers

\pi_{{\bf r}_{i}}^{-1}U
for
i=1,\dots,j
. Now
Proposition
2.10
claims that each
\pi_{{\bf r}_{i}}^{-1}U
is
isomorphic
\mathrm{Col}_{(m+1-r_{i})}U\cap V_{n-d},V_{d}/\mathrm{pr}(U)
and this description emerges
from the closure of the map
\varphi\mapsto\Lambda^{m+1-r_{i}}\varphi
(for
\phi
of high
enough rank). Since
\mathrm{Bl}_{\bf r}
is defined as the closure of the diagonally embedded

, we arrive ate the desired statement.
∎

Now let us consider the maximal (complete) blow-up
\mathrm{Bl}_{(2,\dots,d)}
. By definition,
one gets the projection map
\mathrm{Bl}_{(2,\dots,d)}\to\mathrm{Bl}_{\bf r}
for any collection
{\bf r}
.
The following proposition shows that this map is a desingularization.
To simplify the notation, we denote
\mathrm{Bl}_{(2,\dots,d)}
by
\mathrm{Bl}_{\max}
,

Proposition 4.7
.

The complete blow-up
\mathrm{Bl}_{\max}
is smooth.

Proof.

Consider the
P^{-}_{d}
equivariant projection
\pi_{\max}:\mathrm{Bl}_{\max}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
.
If
\mathrm{Bl}_{\max}
is singular, then there exists a point
U
such that
\dim U\cap V_{n-d}=d

and a singularity shows up over
U
, i.e. in
\pi_{\max}^{-1}U
. In fact, for any point in
the Grassmannian the closure of its

orbit intersects with the Schubert variety
S_{d}

(note that
S_{d}=\{U\in\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V):\ U\subset V_{n-d}\}
).

Now for a point
U\in S_{d}
let us describe the preimage
\pi_{\max}^{-1}A_{U}
of the standard
cell
A_{U}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
containing
U
. The cell
A_{U}
is identified with the space of linear maps
\mathrm{Hom}(U,V/U)
. For
\psi\in\mathrm{Hom}(U,V/U)
we denote by
\varphi
the composition
\mathrm{pr}\circ\psi
;
in particular,
\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}(U,V_{d})

\mathrm{Hom}(U,V/U)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(U,V_{d})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(U,V_{n-d}/U)

.
Now by Proposition
2.10

the preimage

sits inside

A_{U}\times\prod_{s=1}^{d-1}{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda^{s}U,\Lambda^{s}V_{d}))
as the closure
of the set of points
(\psi,[\varphi],[\Lambda^{2}\varphi],\dots,[\Lambda^{d-1}\varphi])

Let us consider the projection map

\pi_{\max}^{-1}A_{U}\to\mathrm{Col}_{(1,\dots,d-1)}(U,V_{d})

.
The fiber over

(p_{1},\dots,p_{d-1})

is
isomorphic to

∎

5.
The general case

In this section we describe the general picture for the blow-ups of flag varieties along Schubert
subvarieties. To simplify the exposition, we concentrate on the case of complete flag
varieties. One can similarly work out the case of Schubert varieties inside partial flags.
In particular, in the previous sections we considered the case of Grassmannians.

Let
G
be a simple simply-connected Lie group. We fix a Borel subgroup
B
and the maximal
torus
T\subset B
.
Let
F=G/B
be the corresponding flag variety. Let
B_{-}\subset G
be the opposite
Borel subgroup, which acts on
F
with an open
dense orbit through the class of identity.
Let
W
be the Weyl group of
G
; for
\sigma\in W
, let
p_{\sigma}\in F
be the corresponding

T
fixed point in the flag variety. We denote by
S^{\circ}(\sigma)\subset S(\sigma)\subset F

the (opposite)
open and closed Schubert varieties (in particular,
\dim S(\sigma)=\dim F-\ell(\sigma)
,
where
\ell(\sigma)
is the length of
\sigma
). Hence,
S^{\circ}(\sigma)={B_{-}p_{\sigma}}
,
S(\sigma)=\overline{S^{\circ}(\sigma)}
.
Each open Schubert variety is an open affine cell and
F=\sqcup_{\sigma\in W}S^{\circ}(\sigma)
.
We are interested in the blow-ups
\mathrm{Bl}_{S(\sigma)}F
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be the Lie algebra of
G
with the Cartan decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{n}}_{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}

and let
{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{n}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}
,
{\mathfrak{b}}_{-}={\mathfrak{n}}_{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}
be the Borel subalgebras.
For a dominant weight
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
,
we denote by
L(\lambda)
the irreducible highest
weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
module with a highest weight vector
v(\lambda)\in L(\lambda)
. In particular,

L(\lambda)=\mathrm{U}({\mathfrak{n}}_{-})v(\lambda)
and
{\mathfrak{n}}.v(\lambda)=0
.
For an element
\sigma\in W
we fix an extremal weight vector
v(\sigma\lambda)\in L(\lambda)
of
weight
\sigma\lambda
(the space of such vectors is one-dimensional for every
\sigma
).
Let
D(\sigma\lambda)
be the (opposite) Demazure module
D(\sigma\lambda)=\mathrm{U}({\mathfrak{n}}_{-})v(\sigma\lambda)
.

Now assume that
\lambda
is regular, i.e. all fundamental weights show up as summands of
\lambda
.
One has the following
G
-equivariant closed embeddings

\displaystyle F=G/B\subset{\mathbb{P}}(L(\lambda)),\ eB\mapsto[v(\lambda)],\qquad S(\sigma)\subset{\mathbb{P}}(D(\sigma\lambda)),\ p_{\sigma}\mapsto[v(\sigma\lambda)].

The following Lemma is standard (see e.g.
[
BK05
,
Kum02
]
), but important for the following discussion.

Lemma 5.1
.

One has
S(\sigma)=F\cap{\mathbb{P}}(D(\sigma\lambda))\subset{\mathbb{P}}(L(\lambda))
.

We introduce the following notation for the truncated representations

(5.1)

L_{\sigma}(\lambda)=L(\lambda)/D(\sigma\lambda).

We denote the image of the highest weight vector
v(\lambda)
in
L_{\sigma}(\lambda)
by
v_{\sigma}(\lambda)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}(\lambda)
be the line bundle on
F
obtained as the pull
back of
\mathcal{O}(1)
with respect to the embedding
F\subset{\mathbb{P}}(L(\lambda))
.
We use the same notation
\mathcal{L}(\lambda)
for the restriction of
\mathcal{L}(\lambda)
to
S(\sigma)
.
Also let
\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
be the ideal sheaf (on
F
) of
S(\sigma)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

L_{\sigma}(\lambda)
is a cyclic
{\mathfrak{b}}_{-}
module with the cyclic vector
v_{\sigma}(\lambda)
.
One has the isomorphism
L_{\sigma}(\lambda)^{*}\simeq H^{0}(F,\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\lambda))
.

Proof.

The first claim follows from the surjection of
{\mathfrak{b}}_{-}
modules
L(\lambda)\to L_{\sigma}(\lambda)
,
sending
v(\lambda)
to
v_{\sigma}(\lambda)
. This implies that
L_{\sigma}(\lambda)=\mathrm{U}({\mathfrak{b}}_{-})v_{\sigma}(\lambda)
.

To prove the second claim, we recall the isomorphisms

L(\lambda)^{*}\simeq H^{0}(F,\mathcal{L}(\lambda)),\ D(\sigma\lambda)^{*}\simeq H^{0}(S(\sigma),\mathcal{L}(\lambda)).

Hence
L_{\sigma}(\lambda)^{*}
is realized as kernel of the restriction map

H^{0}(F,\mathcal{L}(\lambda))\to H^{0}(S(\sigma),\mathcal{L}(\lambda))
. By definition, the sections of

\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\lambda)
are exactly the sections of
\mathcal{L}(\lambda)
which vanish on
the Schubert variety
S(\sigma)
. Hence, we obtain the second claim.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

The blow-up of
{\mathbb{P}}(L(\lambda))
along
{\mathbb{P}}(D(\sigma\lambda))
is isomorphic to the product

{\mathbb{P}}(L(\lambda))\times{\mathbb{P}}(L_{\sigma}(\lambda))
. The blow-up consists of pairs of lines
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})

such that
\ell_{1}
projects to
\ell_{2}
under the projection map

L(\lambda)\to L_{\sigma}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

We use the standard construction of the blow-up of a projective space along a subspace
(see e.g.
[
Vak25
]
) and definition of
L_{\sigma}(\lambda)
.
∎

In order to describe the restriction of the above blow-up, we define the truncated
flag variety

(5.2)

X_{\sigma}(\lambda)=\overline{B_{-}v_{\sigma}(\lambda)}\subset{\mathbb{P}}(L_{\sigma}(\lambda)).

We define the variety
F_{\sigma}(\lambda)
inside the product
F\times X_{\sigma}(\lambda)

of the flag variety and the truncated flag variety by

F_{\sigma}(\lambda)=\overline{B_{-}\bigl([v(\lambda)]\times[v_{\sigma}(\lambda)]}\bigr)\subset F\times X_{\sigma}(\lambda)\subset{\mathbb{P}}(L(\lambda))\times{\mathbb{P}}(L_{\sigma}(\lambda)).

By definition, there is a canonical projection
F_{\sigma}(\lambda)\to F=G/B
.

Theorem 5.4
.

The blow-up of
F
along
S(\sigma)
is embedded into
{\mathbb{P}}(L(\lambda))\times{\mathbb{P}}(L_{\sigma}(\lambda))

as a strict transform of
F\subset{\mathbb{P}}(L(\lambda))
. The image of the embedding is equal
to
F_{\sigma}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
we know that the intersection of
F
with
{\mathbb{P}}(D(\sigma\lambda))

is
S(\sigma)
. Hence
\mathrm{Bl}_{S(\sigma)}F
is the closure of the preimage of

F\setminus S(\sigma)
inside
{\mathbb{P}}(L(\lambda))\times{\mathbb{P}}(L_{\sigma}(\lambda))
under the natural
projection map from
\mathrm{Bl}_{{\mathbb{P}}(D(\sigma\lambda))}{\mathbb{P}}(L(\lambda))
to
{\mathbb{P}}(L(\lambda))
.

Now let us prove the second claim. The orbit
B_{-}\bigl([v(\lambda)]\times[v_{\sigma}(\lambda)]\bigr)

is contained in the blow-up of
{\mathbb{P}}(L(\lambda))
along
{\mathbb{P}}(D(\sigma\lambda))
. Since the
B_{-}

orbit of
[v(\lambda)]
is open in
F
and does not intersect the Schubert variety
S(\sigma)
,
the orbit
B_{-}\bigl([v(\lambda)]\times[v_{\sigma}(\lambda)]\bigr)
is contained in
\mathrm{Bl}_{S(\sigma)}F
.
Since the blow-up is irreducible, we conclude that
F_{\sigma}(\lambda)
coincides with
the blow-up of
F
along
S(\sigma)
.
∎

It is natural to generalize the construction above to the case of multiple permutations.
Namely, for a collections
\underline{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m})\in W^{m}

we define the orbit closure

F_{\underline{\sigma}}(\lambda)=\overline{B_{-}\times_{i=1}^{m}[v_{\sigma_{i}}(\lambda)]}\subset\prod_{i=1}^{m}X_{\sigma_{i}}(\lambda).

It is tempting to conjecture that if
\underline{\sigma}
exhausts the set of all Weyl group elements,
then
F_{\underline{\sigma}}(\lambda)
is smooth.

Remark 5.5
.

One can similarly consider the case of a partial flag variety
F=G/P
for a standard
parabolic subgroup
P
. The difference is that the weights
\lambda
providing the
embedding
F\subset{\mathbb{P}}(L(\lambda))
do not have to be regular (only
P
-regular) and
there are less Schubert varieties (since part of the Weyl group stabilizes the
point corresponding to the identity).

Finally, let us establish a correspondence between the objects studied in the previous sections
for the Grassmannians and the general objects introduced above. The relevant flag variety

F
is
SL_{n}/P_{d}
,
SL_{n}=SL(V)
, for a maximal parabolic
P_{d}
. We fix
\lambda=\omega_{d}
; then

L(\lambda)=\Lambda^{d}(V)
and the corresponding projective
embedding
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)\subset{\mathbb{P}}(L(\lambda))
is the classical Plücker embedding.
For
r=1,\dots,d
we fix permutation
\sigma_{r}
such that

\sigma_{r}([d])=\{1,\dots,d-r,d+1,\dots,d+r\}=I(r)
(see (
1.2
)).
Then the Demazure module
D(\sigma_{r}\omega_{d})\subset\Lambda^{d}V
is the span of wedge products
v_{I}

such that
\deg I\geq r
(i.e.
|I_{>d}|\geq r)
; the cyclic vector of
D_{r}
is
v_{I(r)}
.
The Schubert variety
S_{r}=S(\sigma_{r})
consists of
subspaces
U\in\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
such that
\dim U\cap V_{n-d}\geq r

(see Lemma
1.9
).
Now the truncated representation
L_{\sigma_{r}}(\omega_{d})=L(\omega_{d})/D(\sigma_{r}\omega_{d})
is
exactly
(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r-1}
and
X_{r-1}=F_{\sigma_{r}}(\omega_{d})
.

Appendix A
Twisted ideal sheaf

In this section we provide explicit computation of the space of sections of the twisted
ideal sheaf of the Schubert variety
S_{r}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V)
.
We identify
H^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}}(1))
with
the dual of the truncated module
(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r}
. We prepare the following lemma.

Lemma A.1
.

The
{\mathfrak{p}}^{-}_{d}
module
\Lambda^{r}V_{n-d}^{*}\otimes\Lambda^{r}V
surjects onto
(\Lambda^{d}V)^{*}_{r}
.

Proof.

Let us show that there exists an embedding
(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r}\subset\Lambda^{r}V_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{r}V^{*}
. We rewrite

\Lambda^{r}V_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{r}V^{*}\simeq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{r}\left(\Lambda^{r}V_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{k}V_{n-d}^{*}\right)\otimes\Lambda^{r-k}V_{d}^{*}.

The tensor product
\Lambda^{r}V_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{k}V_{n-d}^{*}
contains

\Lambda^{r-k}V_{n-d}
as a direct summand in the decomposition into irreducible

\mathfrak{sl}(V_{n-d})
modules.
Hence we obtain a
\mathfrak{sl}(V_{n-d})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(V_{d})
submodule

\bigoplus_{k=0}^{r}\Lambda^{r-k}V_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{r-k}V_{d}^{*}
, which is
isomorphic to the restriction of
(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r}
from
{\mathfrak{p}}^{-}_{d}
to its Levi subalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}(V_{n-d})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(V_{d})
of
(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r}
. Hence its suffices to prove that

\bigoplus_{k=0}^{r}\Lambda^{r-k}V_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{r-k}V_{d}^{*}
is a
{\mathfrak{p}}^{-}_{d}
submodule of

\Lambda^{r}V_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{r}V^{*}
.

Let us consider the trivial subrepresentation inside
\Lambda^{r}V_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{r}V_{d}^{*}

(which sits inside
\Lambda^{r}V_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{r}V^{*}
). We denote by
u_{r}
a generator of this
one-dimensional
\mathfrak{sl}(V_{n-d})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(V_{d})
module. Since
(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r}
is cyclic

{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}
module, we need to show that the universal enveloping algebra
\mathrm{U}({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})
generate
from
u_{r}
a submodule isomorphic to
(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r}
.

First note that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}
preserves
V_{n-d}
and maps (via the dual action)
V_{n-d}^{*}
to
V_{d}^{*}
.
Hence

{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}:\Lambda^{r}V_{n-d}\otimes\left(\Lambda^{k}V_{n-d}^{*}\otimes\Lambda^{r-k}V_{d}^{*}\right)\to\Lambda^{r}V_{n-d}\otimes\left(\Lambda^{k-1}V_{n-d}^{*}\otimes\Lambda^{r-k+1}V_{d}^{*}\right).

Since
{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}
is commutative, the universal enveloping algebra of
{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}
is just the
polynomial ring. Let us show that applying degree
k
part
\mathrm{U}({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})_{k}

(the degree
k
polynomials in a basis of
{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}
)
to
u_{r}
we get exactly the
\mathfrak{sl}(V_{n-d})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(V_{d})
module

\Lambda^{k}V_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{k}V_{d}^{*}
.

Let us consider the tensor product
\Lambda^{r}V_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{r-k}V_{n-d}^{*}
. The

\mathfrak{gl}(V_{n-d})
module
\Lambda^{r}V_{n-d}
corresponds to the partition
(1^{r})
; the module

\Lambda^{r-k}V_{n-d}^{*}
corresponds to the partition
(1^{n-d-r+k})
. By the
Littelwood-Richardson rule the tensor product of these modules decomposes into several summands
with one of them corresponding to the partition
(2^{k}1^{n-d})
. All other summands are labeled
by partitions whose number of parts is strictly less than
n-d
and the sum of parts
equal to
n-d+k
. We note that non of these summands tensored by
\Lambda^{k}V_{d}^{*}

may show up in the decomposition of
\mathrm{U}({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})_{k}u_{r}
into irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}(V_{n-d})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(V_{d})

modules, since
{\mathfrak{a}}_{d}\simeq V_{n-d}\otimes V_{d}^{*}
and
u_{r}
generates a trivial module; hence
in the decomposition of
\mathrm{U}({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})_{k}u_{r}
into irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}(V_{n-d})
modules one sees
only representations labeled by partitions with the sum of the parts equal to
k
.
Now getting back to the module corresponding to
(2^{k}1^{n-d})
, we see that as
\mathfrak{sl}(V_{n-d})

module it is isomorphic to
\Lambda^{k}V_{n-d}
. Hence

\mathrm{U}({\mathfrak{a}}_{d})u_{r}\simeq(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r}\subset\Lambda^{r}V_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{r}V^{*}.

∎

Now we are ready to prove the following proposition.

Proposition A.2
.

The sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}}(1)
is globally generated and one has an isomorphism of
{\mathfrak{p}}^{-}_{d}
modules

H^{0}(\mathrm{Gr}_{d}(V),\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}}(1))\simeq(\Lambda^{d}V)^{*}_{r}.

Proof.

Corollary
3.5
and Example
3.4
give a surjection

\mathcal{F}_{1}(1)\simeq\mathcal{T}_{(n-d-r+1,0^{r-1})}(1)\simeq\\
\Sigma_{(0^{r-1},(-1)^{n-d-r+1})}V_{n-d}^{*}\otimes\Sigma_{(0^{r-1},(-1)^{n-d-r+1})}\mathcal{U}^{\perp}_{d}\to\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}}(1).

Since
\Sigma_{(0^{r-1},(-1)^{n-d-r+1})}\mathcal{U}^{\perp}_{d}
is globally generated, the twisted ideal sheaf
is globally generated as well. We also get a surjection on the level of sections

(A.1)

H^{0}(\mathcal{F}_{1})\simeq\Lambda^{r-1}V^{*}_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{r-1}V\to H^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}}(1)),

since
(0^{r-1},(-1)^{n-d-r+1})=((-1)^{n-d})+(1^{r-1},0^{n-d-r+1})
and adding
weight
((-1)^{n-d})
does not change the restriction of the corresponding
\mathfrak{gl}(V_{n-d})
module to
\mathfrak{sl}(V_{n-d})
.
Lemma
A.1
implies that in order to complete the proof it suffices
to show that the kernel of the map
(
A.1
) coincides with the kernel of map

\Lambda^{r-1}V^{*}_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{r-1}V\to(\Lambda^{d}V)^{*}_{r-1}

Lemma
3.6
and Corollary
3.5
imply that there exists the following exact sequence

0\to\Sigma_{(0^{r-1},(-1)^{n-d-r},1-r)}V^{*}_{n-d}\otimes V\to\Sigma_{(0^{r-1},(-1)^{n-d-r},2-r)}V^{*}_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{2}V\to\\
\dots\to\Sigma_{(0^{r-1},(-1)^{n-d-r+1})}V^{*}_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{r-1}V\to H^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}}(1))\to 0.

The
\mathfrak{gl}(V_{n-d})
module
\Sigma_{(0^{r-1},(-1)^{n-d-r},s-r)}V^{*}_{n-d}
is isomorphic to
the irreducible representation
\Sigma_{((r-s),1^{n-d-r})}V_{n-d}
.
Hence the terms in the above exact sequence are written as (here
s=0,\dots,r-1
)

\Sigma_{((r-s),1^{n-d-r})}V_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{s}V\simeq\bigoplus_{m=0}^{s}\left(\Sigma_{((r-s),1^{n-d-r})}V_{n-d}\otimes\Lambda^{s-m}V_{n-d}\right)\otimes\Lambda^{m}V_{d}.

Let us denote the right hand side by
M_{s}
; hence,
M_{s}
are
\mathfrak{sl}(V_{n-d})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(V_{d})

modules.
We want to compute the Euler characteristic of
[M_{s}]
(starting from
s=r-1
), where
for a
\mathfrak{sl}(V_{n-d})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(V_{d})
module
M
we denote by
[M]
its element
in the Grothendieck ring, i.e. a formal linear combination of its irreducible summands.
Our goal is to show that

(A.2)

\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}[M_{s}](-1)^{r-1-s}=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{r-1}\Lambda^{m}V_{n-d}^{*}\otimes\Lambda^{m}V_{d}.

Clearly, each
M_{s}
is a direct sum of terms
R_{m}\otimes\Lambda^{m}V_{d}
for some
\mathfrak{sl}(V_{n-d})

representations
R_{m}
. One has

(A.3)

[R_{m}]=[\Sigma_{(1^{n-d-r+1})}\otimes\Sigma_{(1^{r-m-1})}]-[\Sigma_{(21^{n-d-r})}\otimes\Sigma_{(1^{r-m-2})}]+\\
[\Sigma_{(31^{n-d-r})}\otimes\Sigma_{(1^{r-m-3})}]-\dots+(-1)^{r-m+1}[\Sigma_{((r-m),1^{n-d-r})}\otimes\Sigma_{(0)}],

where all the Schur functors are applied to
V_{n-d}
and we omit zeroes at the end of partitions
(to simplify the formula).
We compute (
A.3
) using the Littelwood-Richardson rule (to be precise, we only need
the Pieri formulas).

All the summands showing up in (
A.3
) are the tensor products of a thin hook and
a one column partition; in particular, one of the factors is always a wedge power of
V_{n-d}
.
Hence, applying the Pieri formula, one gets a linear combination of
(classes of) representations labeled by partitions of the form
(N,2^{a},1^{b})
(as above,
zeroes at the end are omitted). For each
(N,a,b)
with
N>1
the corresponding summand
shows up twice in (
A.3
) in two adjacent terms and hence cancels. For
N=1
(and, hence,

a=0
) the corresponding term show up only in the tensor product

[\Sigma_{(1^{n-d-r+1})}\otimes\Sigma_{(1^{r-m-1})}],\ n-d-m=b+1

with a positive sign. We conclude that
[R_{m}]=[\Sigma_{(1^{n-d-m},0^{m})}V_{n-d}]=[\Lambda^{m}V_{d}^{*}]
and hence
H^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{S_{r}}(1))\simeq(\Lambda^{d}V)_{r}^{*}
.
∎
