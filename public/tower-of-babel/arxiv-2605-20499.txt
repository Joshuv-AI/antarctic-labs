Beyond minimal coupling for charged scalars? Modified electrodynamics and London-penetration tests
F. Minotti, G. Modanese

Abstract
While standard minimal coupling works well for Dirac fermions, its application to scalar fields features a known “peculiarity”: the term linear in A μ A_{\mu} does not coincide with the conserved Noether current of the interacting theory. We recently proposed choosing a different principle for electromagnetic interactions, namely a linear coupling A μ ​ J μ A_{\mu}J^{\mu} with J μ J^{\mu} a (globally) conserved current, accepting the consequence that one must abandon full local gauge invariance in the electromagnetic sector and adopt an extended electrodynamics (of Aharonov–Bohm type) that can couple consistently to non-locally-conserved currents. We first review the standard theory (scalar QED and the role of the A 2 ​ | ϕ | 2 A^{2}|\phi|^{2} term), then present the physical motivations offered for proposing the modified coupling and discuss general consequences of reducing gauge invariance. We then focus on the central condensed-matter claim: for bosonic charged condensates, the modified framework predicts a rescaled magnetic penetration depth λ → λ / 2 \lambda\to\lambda/\sqrt{2} , while leaving other key qualitative features of superconducting electrodynamics and the type-I/type-II distinction unchanged (up to an equivalent rescaling of the GL parameter). Finally, we analyze experimental data for a London-length consistency check based on independent measurements of the ratio n s / m ∗ n_{s}/m^{\ast} between carrier density and effective mass. We compare for five materials an “optical” penetration depth λ opt \lambda_{\mathrm{opt}} inferred from IR/THz superfluid spectral weight with a “magnetic” depth λ mag \lambda_{\mathrm{mag}} obtained independently (LE- μ \mu SR, TF- μ \mu SR, microwave methods, etc.). Data for Nb, YBCO and Ba(Fe,Co) 2 As 2 confirm the hypothesis λ opt > λ mag \lambda_{\mathrm{opt}}>\lambda_{\mathrm{mag}} , with a ratio not far from 1.4; data for Pb are inconclusive while data for MgB 2 indicate λ opt ≃ λ mag \lambda_{\mathrm{opt}}\simeq\lambda_{\mathrm{mag}} as predicted by the standard theory.



I
Introduction

Local gauge invariance (LGI) and minimal coupling are cornerstone principles of modern electrodynamics
and of the Standard Model.
In the familiar fermionic case (Dirac QED), replacing
\partial_{\mu}\to\partial_{\mu}+i(q/\hbar)A_{\mu}

produces an interaction term of the form
-J_{\mu}A^{\mu}
with
J^{\mu}=q\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi
,
which coincides with the conserved Noether current associated with global phase symmetry.
This harmony among (i) LGI, (ii) strict local charge conservation, and (iii) the physical meaning of
J^{\mu}A_{\mu}

as interaction energy is aesthetically and technically compelling.

For scalar fields, minimal coupling also works and produces a consistent gauge-invariant theory,
but the bookkeeping of currents is less transparent.
Expanding the covariant-derivative Lagrangian yields a term linear in
A_{\mu}
proportional to the
free

Noether current, plus a quadratic term
A^{2}|\phi|^{2}
.
The conserved source current that enters the gauge-field equations is the derivative of the full action
with respect to
A_{\mu}
, and it includes contributions from the
A^{2}|\phi|^{2}
term.
This is a standard feature of derivative couplings and of gauging scalar fields.

Nevertheless, in Ref.
15
we argued that one can adopt an alternative principle:
electromagnetic interaction energy should always be expressible as
J^{\mu}A_{\mu}
where
J^{\mu}
is the conserved current
resulting from global gauge invariance of the full Lagrangian. For scalar systems, enforcing this principle implies
renouncing LGI in the electromagnetic sector and adopting an extended electrodynamics compatible with
possible local nonconservation of charge. In that modified framework, new terms appear in the effective dynamics
of charged bosons, and this has potentially observable consequences in superconductors,
including a characteristic rescaling of the magnetic penetration depth by a factor
1/\sqrt{2}
.

This article develops the conceptual background of the alternative coupling proposed, and frames a concrete experimental test strategy.

II
Standard scalar QED and the status of the conserved current

Consider a free complex scalar field
\phi
:

\mathcal{L}_{0}=\partial_{\mu}\phi^{\ast}\partial^{\mu}\phi-m^{2}\phi^{\ast}\phi.

(1)

The global phase symmetry
\phi\to e^{i\alpha}\phi
yields the Noether current

j^{\mu}_{\mathrm{free}}=iq\left(\phi^{\ast}\partial^{\mu}\phi-(\partial^{\mu}\phi^{\ast})\phi\right),\qquad\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{\mathrm{free}}=0\ \ \text{(on-shell for the free equations).}

(2)

Minimal coupling introduces the covariant derivative
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+iqA_{\mu}
and the matter Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mat}}=(D_{\mu}\phi)^{\ast}(D^{\mu}\phi)-m^{2}\phi^{\ast}\phi.

(3)

Expanding,

(D_{\mu}\phi)^{\ast}(D^{\mu}\phi)=\partial_{\mu}\phi^{\ast}\partial^{\mu}\phi+iqA_{\mu}\left(\phi^{\ast}\partial^{\mu}\phi-(\partial^{\mu}\phi^{\ast})\phi\right)+q^{2}A_{\mu}A^{\mu}\,\phi^{\ast}\phi.

(4)

The linear term involves
j^{\mu}_{\mathrm{free}}
from Eq. (
2
), but the quadratic

q^{2}A^{2}|\phi|^{2}
term is essential for gauge invariance.
In perturbation theory it yields the familiar “seagull” vertex for scalar QED.

The electromagnetic source is obtained by varying the full Lagrangian with respect to
A_{\mu}
:

J^{\mu}\equiv\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial A_{\mu}}=iq\!\left[\phi^{\ast}D^{\mu}\phi-(D^{\mu}\phi)^{\ast}\phi\right]=j^{\mu}_{\mathrm{free}}-2q^{2}A^{\mu}|\phi|^{2}.

(5)

This current is locally conserved as a consequence of gauge invariance and the structure of the field equations.
Hence, the standard view is that scalar QED has no internal inconsistency; rather, it illustrates that
“naively” identifying the coefficient of the linear term with the source current is incorrect for scalars.

As stated in
15
, this is “not an inconsistency, just a feature” ensuring
that the correct conserved current is the source of the gauge field.

II.1
Why insist on a linear
A_{\mu}J^{\mu}
coupling with conserved
J^{\mu}
?

The proposal in
15
can be read as an attempt to elevate to a principle
what is commonly true in fermionic QED and in classical electrodynamics: the interaction energy density
is
J^{\mu}A_{\mu}
, and gauge invariance of this interaction term in a transformation
A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}f
is closely tied to current conservation
(since, by integration by parts,
J^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}f\to-f\,\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}
).

The fact that the interaction energy is not gauge invariant in standard scalar QED appears as a weak point of the theory in a semiclassical limit, where a proper expression for this energy is expected to be gauge-invariant and independent from whether, given a current, it is made of fermions or bosons.

In ordinary Maxwell electrodynamics with strict local charge conservation, gauge transformations are
allowed and the potentials are not unique.
However, Aharonov and Bohm famously argued that potentials can have physical significance even in regions
where
F_{\mu\nu}=0
, as evidenced by the (gauge invariant) AB phase.
In extended electrodynamics frameworks that reduce gauge freedom,
A_{\mu}
can be treated more directly
as a physical field (modulo residual gauge transformations), and then
J^{\mu}A_{\mu}
becomes a natural
candidate for a physically meaningful interaction energy density.
Ref.
15
emphasizes that if one wants to treat
J^{\mu}A_{\mu}
as a well-defined physical quantity,
one is led to link it to a conserved
J^{\mu}
.

Maxwell theory with gauge invariance supports two transverse propagating photon polarizations.
Reduced gauge invariance can allow additional longitudinal modes.
In AB-type extended electrodynamics, an additional scalar field (or scalar-like degree of freedom in the potentials)
can appear, namely
S=\partial_{\mu}A^{\mu}
, sourced by the local nonconservation measure

I\equiv\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J},

as discussed in the AB-electrodynamics literature
13
;
12
.

The extended Aharonov-Bohm electrodynamics with reduced gauge invariance can also be applied to
effective theories with apparent local nonconservation.
In many-body quantum mechanics and effective condensed-matter models, one sometimes encounters
nonlocal potentials, coarse-graining, or open-system descriptions where the usual local continuity equation
for an effective current can appear violated (even if the underlying microscopic theory is conservative).
Ref.
15
connects this to the possibility that strict local charge conservation could fail
in certain effective quantum models, and that an electromagnetic theory able to couple to such currents
would be required (or at least meaningful as an effective description).
Related discussions appear in our broader program on Aharonov–Bohm extended electrodynamics and
“gauge waves”
13
;
12
;
14
.

A recent complementary perspective is provided by Lenzi
et al.
, who study a generalized (fractional) Schrödinger
dynamics combining (i) a temporal memory kernel, (ii) a fractional (Riesz) spatial operator, and (iii) an explicitly
nonlocal interaction term written as an integral operator acting on the wavefunction
20
.
In this framework, the quantity
\rho(x,t)=|\Psi(x,t)|^{2}
obeys a generalized continuity equation
rather than the standard local form
\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\!\cdot\!{\bf J}=0
.
Lenzi
et al.
explicitly show that (a) the memory kernel introduces additional terms that act as sources/sinks
for the local density, and (b) the spatially nonlocal term generates a redistribution that further invalidates a strictly
local continuity equation
20
.

Lenzi
et al.
connect this effective nonconservation to a standard physical mechanism:

openness
of the reduced system.
They emphasize that when the dynamics is non-Markovian (with memory), probability can be temporarily stored in
“memory channels” and later flow back from the environment to the system, leading to an effective nonunitary evolution
at the level of the reduced description
20
.
From the viewpoint adopted in Ref.
15
, such examples are relevant because they illustrate
concrete, mathematically controlled situations in which the effective current associated with a reduced model
does not satisfy the naive local continuity equation even when the underlying microscopic theory is conservative.
This supports the motivation for considering (at least as an effective theory) an electromagnetic sector capable of
coupling consistently to currents that are not locally conserved in the reduced description.

III
Renouncing full gauge invariance: possible consequences

If full local gauge invariance is abandoned (or restricted),
the electromagnetic field theory is no longer uniquely Maxwellian,
and several general consequences follow.

As it was explicitely stated in Ref.
15
, the “alternative coupling” is intended as an effective model
for bosonic condensed-matter systems, while some crucial scattering-theory properties (soft-photon factorization, KLN cancellations)
fail in this framework, placing particle-physics scattering outside scope.
In this sense, the proposal is not a competitor to the Standard Model as a UV-complete theory,
but an effective phenomenological framework for certain macroscopic bosonic systems.

III.1
Landau levels for integer-spin charged bosons vs. observability of the modified coupling

A basic quantum-mechanical arena where the modified bosonic coupling has in principle a clean signature is the spectrum in a uniform
magnetic field. In Maxwell electrodynamics, the minimal-coupling Hamiltonian yields the usual Landau problem and its
macroscopic degeneracy. In the AB-extended framework, however, the vector potential must be treated as a
source-generated dynamical field rather than a pure gauge choice, and the modified bosonic coupling effectively doubles
the coefficient of the diamagnetic term. The detailed derivation is given in Appendix A, where we show that the resulting
effective Hamiltonian in the uniform-
B
bulk limit takes a Fock–Darwin form with an oscillator frequency

\Omega=\sqrt{2}\,\omega_{c}
(with
\omega_{c}=|q|B/m
) and eigenvalues

E_{n_{r},l}=\hbar\Omega(2n_{r}+|l|+1)-\frac{\hbar\omega_{c}}{2}\,l,\qquad n_{r}=0,1,2,\dots,\;\;l\in\mathbb{Z},

(6)

(Eqs. (
21
)–(
22
).
In particular, the modified coupling introduces an effective quadratic confinement
\propto r^{2}
already in the
uniform-
B
limit, which removes the standard Landau macroscopic degeneracy; see Appendix A.

From the standpoint of potential tests, the relevant question is whether such spectral changes can be observable.
For an electron the cyclotron scale
\hbar\omega_{c}
is large enough that Landau quantization is routinely accessible;
however, the electron case is excluded here because the modified coupling under discussion is formulated for integer-spin
bosons. In the present context, the physically motivated candidates are ions or composite bosons.
For ions with masses of order the atomic mass scale,
m\sim A\,m_{u}
with
A\gtrsim 10
–
100
, the cyclotron frequency
is suppressed by
m^{-1}
, and therefore the characteristic energy scale is extremely small:

\hbar\omega_{c}=\hbar\,\frac{|q|B}{m}\simeq 6\times 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{eV}\,\left(\frac{|q|}{e}\right)\left(\frac{B}{10\,\mathrm{T}}\right)\left(\frac{m_{p}}{m}\right).

(7)

Even for
m=m_{p}
(proton mass) this gives sub-
\mu
eV energies, and for a typical ion with
m\sim 100\,m_{p}
the scale
drops to the few-neV range. The modified coupling changes the spectrum through
\Omega=\sqrt{2}\,\omega_{c}
, implying
energy shifts of order
\Delta E\sim(\sqrt{2}-1)\hbar\omega_{c}\simeq 0.41\,\hbar\omega_{c}
, which are far below the
resolution of realistic condensed-matter or atomic measurements for heavy bosons. Hence, while the Landau-level analysis
is conceptually useful (and provides an unambiguous signature at the level of the single-particle Hamiltonian),
it does not offer a practical experimental test for integer-spin bosons of atomic mass.

III.2
Possible astrophysical and cosmological implications

Once one modifies electrodynamics and its coupling,
one naturally asks: could compact astrophysical objects or cosmological environments provide constraints or signatures?

Impact is likely limited for standard cosmological scalar fields.
Many cosmological scalar fields invoked in inflation, quintessence, axion-like models, or fuzzy dark matter
are electrically neutral; the modified electromagnetic coupling would not apply directly.
Thus, broad cosmological consequences are not automatic.

A more relevant arena is charged scalar configurations coupled to electromagnetism and gravity,
notably charged boson stars described by the Einstein–Maxwell–Klein–Gordon system.
These models are explicitly locally
U(1)
gauge invariant, and they involve a conserved Noether current
that includes the
-2qA_{\mu}|\phi|^{2}
term, as in Eq. (
5
). See refs.
9
;
6
.

From the viewpoint of the modified-coupling proposal, such systems would be altered at a basic level if one
replaced Maxwell electrodynamics by AB extended electrodynamics and enforced a different coupling principle. Possible changes would be the following:
(i)
equilibrium configurations (field profiles, charge distributions) would shift;
(ii) stability boundaries and maximal masses could change due to altered electromagnetic energy and stress;
(iii) radiative properties could change if additional modes propagate or couple.

In principle, experimental astrophysical constraints could arise if
additional electromagnetic modes propagate over astrophysical distances,
or if the coupling to macroscopic charged condensates changes magnetic screening or field structure,
or if compact-object electrodynamics is modified in measurable ways.
In practice, however, the strongest constraints would likely come from laboratory condensed-matter tests,
because superconductors provide controlled environments where the relevant bosonic condensate is real and tunable.

We will address this point in the next sections.

IV
Superconductors as the key laboratory: why
\lambda
is a clean signature

The Ginzburg–Landau functional for a superconducting order parameter
\psi
contains the covariant gradient term

\frac{1}{2m^{\ast}}\left|\left(-i\hbar\nabla-q\mathbf{A}\right)\psi\right|^{2},

(8)

which yields a supercurrent containing both a paramagnetic (gradient) part and a diamagnetic part
proportional to
-|\psi|^{2}\mathbf{A}
.
This structure underlies the London equation and the Meissner effect.
In standard electrodynamics, this term is the condensed-matter counterpart of the seagull term
in scalar QED.

In the London limit, the magnetic penetration depth is equal to
\lambda_{L}
, which is defined by the relation

\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}_{L}}=\mu_{0}q^{2}\frac{n_{s}}{m^{\ast}},

(9)

showing its connection to the ”superfluid stiffness”, i.e. the ratio between the density of superconducting carriers
n_{s}
and their effective mass
m^{\ast}
.

IV.1
Key predictions of the modified coupling for superconductors

IV.1.1
Modified penetration depth:
\lambda\to\lambda/\sqrt{2}

A central result of
15
is that in the modified bosonic framework,
the magnetic penetration depth
\lambda_{mag}
acquires a factor
1/\sqrt{2}
:

\lambda_{\mathrm{mag}}=\frac{\lambda_{L}}{\sqrt{2}}.

(10)

Experimentally, it is often difficult to measure
n_{s}
and
m^{\ast}
separately, but the ratio

n_{s}/m^{\ast}
can be accessed through optical/THz measurements, extracting the condensate spectral weight and converting it into an equivalent
\lambda_{\mathrm{opt}}
defined exactly as
\lambda_{L}
in (
9
).

Therefore, a natural test of London electrodynamics (and a potential test bed for modified coupling
ideas) is to compare two independent determinations of the same stiffness:
(a) an optical/THz determination of
\lambda_{opt}
and
(b) a magnetic determination of
\lambda_{\mathrm{mag}}
from Meissner profiling
or vortex-lattice field distributions.

IV.1.2
AC electrodynamics

Ref.
15
analyzes frequency-dependent electrodynamics
through the calculation of the surface impedance
Z_{s}(\omega)
.
For a transverse electromagnetic wave in the superconducting medium, a dispersion relation is obtained
with the key difference that a numerical factor
2
multiplies the superfluid term compared to the Maxwell/Lorentz-gauge case.
This leads precisely to the scaling (
10
) as the net physical difference in the penetration length,
rather than a qualitatively new form of AC response.

In other words, within the approximations used (two-fluid normal+superfluid decomposition, transverse mode),
the structure of electrodynamics remains the same, but the ”effective stiffness” (hence
\lambda
) is rescaled.

IV.1.3
Type I vs Type II superconductors: unchanged classification

A potential worry is that modifying
\lambda
might alter the type-I/type-II boundary or flux quantization.
Ref.
15
shows that fluxoid quantization is not modified because the expression of the conserved current is not changed,
and that the usual type-I/type-II criterion in terms of
\kappa_{\mathrm{GL}}
remains effectively the same.
It is noted that in the modified theory one can regard
\kappa
as scaled by
1/\sqrt{2}
, such that the sign change
of surface energy occurs at
\kappa=1/\sqrt{2}
which corresponds to
\kappa_{\mathrm{GL}}=1
in the original theory
15
. Thus the qualitative distinction between type I and type II is not changed.

VI
Conclusions

We have revisited the long-standing observation that, for charged scalar fields, the term linear in
A_{\mu}
obtained by
expanding the minimally coupled Lagrangian does not coincide with the fully conserved electromagnetic source current
of the interacting theory. In standard scalar QED this is not an inconsistency: the conserved source current is obtained
by varying the full action with respect to
A_{\mu}
and necessarily includes the “seagull” contribution proportional to

A^{2}|\phi|^{2}
. The alternative viewpoint advocated in Ref.
15
is to elevate to a guiding
principle the requirement that electromagnetic interaction energy be expressible as
J^{\mu}A_{\mu}
with
J^{\mu}
the
(conserved) Noether current of the full matter sector, even in effective models where a naive local continuity equation
may fail. This choice leads naturally to a reduced-gauge electromagnetic sector (AB-type extended electrodynamics) that
can couple consistently to currents which are not locally conserved at the level of the reduced description.

On the phenomenological side, the most concrete consequence for superconductors is a simple and testable scaling:
in the modified bosonic framework the magnetic penetration depth is predicted to satisfy

\lambda_{\mathrm{mag}}=\lambda_{L}/\sqrt{2}
,
while the qualitative structure of AC electrodynamics and the type-I/type-II classification remain unchanged up to an
equivalent parameter mapping. The Landau-level analysis in Appendix A provides an additional quantum-mechanical
signature of the doubled diamagnetic term, but we have emphasized that for integer-spin bosons of atomic mass the
associated energy shifts are far too small to be experimentally accessible.

We have then implemented a London-length “consistency check” by comparing independent determinations of the same
superfluid stiffness
n_{s}/m^{\ast}
: optical/THz measurements provide an effective length
\lambda_{\mathrm{opt}}
defined exactly as
\lambda_{L}
, while
magnetic probes (LE-
\mu
SR Meissner profiling, TF-
\mu
SR in the vortex state, or calibrated microwave methods) provide

\lambda_{\mathrm{mag}}
. For Nb, YBCO and Ba(Fe,Co)
2
As
2
, currently available datasets support the trend

\lambda_{\mathrm{opt}}>\lambda_{\mathrm{mag}}
, with ratios ranging from
\sim 1.2
–
1.3
(YBCO) up to
\sim 1.4

(Ba(Fe,Co)
2
As
2
) depending on sample matching and definitions.
This is intriguing, since there are many possible parasitic effects which can weaken the response of supercurrents to an external field and so increase the effective penetration depth, but a decrease needs a more fundamental explanation.
For Pb the situation remains inconclusive because
type-I materials require sizable nonlocal corrections and different experimental routes yield a spread of effective values,
while MgB
2
provides the standard result
\lambda_{\mathrm{opt}}\simeq\lambda_{\mathrm{mag}}
.

The main methodological conclusion is that future progress hinges on improved consistent comparisons:
(i) optical and magnetic determinations should be performed on the same specimen (or on specimens from the same growth
batch with tightly controlled doping and disorder), (ii) tensor components must be matched directly in anisotropic
materials, and (iii) uncertainty budgets should be propagated uniformly so that the significance of any observed offset
can be assessed quantitatively. A definitive verification (or refutation) of the
\sqrt{2}
scaling will likely require
such coordinated measurements with fully documented systematics.

Appendix A
Spectrum of an integer spin particle in a uniform magnetic field

In ordinary electromagnetism (Maxwell theory with full local gauge redundancy) it is possible to impose the Lorenz gauge condition, which for static configurations with time-independent scalar potential
\phi
reduces to
\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf{A}=0
. For the case of a uniform magnetic field this is satisfied both by the symmetric gauge
\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{r}
and by Landau gauges (e.g.
\mathbf{A}=(0,Bx,0)
for
\mathbf{B}=B\hat{z}
).

However, if we work consistently within the extended Aharonov–Bohm electrodynamics the four-potential
A^{\mu}
must be treated as a fundamental dynamical field obeying a wave equation sourced by a four-current, namely
\square A^{\mu}=\mu_{0}J^{\mu}
. Therefore we need the solution generated by a physically specified source (an ideal long solenoid), and only then use that potential in the bosonic Hamiltonian with the modified (“doubled”) diamagnetic term.

In the magnetostatic regime relevant to a long current-carrying solenoid (time-independent currents,
\partial_{t}=0
, with
\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf{J}=0
), the wave equation reduces to a Poisson equation for the spatial vector potential:

\nabla^{2}\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r})=-\mu_{0}\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}).

(11)

Consider an ideal infinitely long solenoid of radius
R
aligned with the
z
-axis, carrying an azimuthal surface current density

\mathbf{K}=K\,\hat{\bm{\varphi}},

(12)

which produces (in the ideal limit) a uniform axial magnetic field inside the solenoid and a vanishing field outside. By cylindrical symmetry one may take

\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r})=A_{\varphi}(r)\,\hat{\bm{\varphi}}.

(13)

A standard magnetostatic solution of (
11
) is

A_{\varphi}(r)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{B}{2}\,r,&r<R,\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{BR^{2}}{2r},&r>R,\end{cases}\qquad B=\mu_{0}K,

(14)

which yields

B_{z}(r)=\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\Big(rA_{\varphi}(r)\Big)=\begin{cases}B,&r<R,\\
0,&r>R.\end{cases}

(15)

Inside the solenoid, and in particular in the bulk region far from the boundary (
r\ll R
), one has

A_{\varphi}(r)=\frac{B}{2}\,r,

(16)

which in Cartesian coordinates is precisely the symmetric gauge potential for a uniform magnetic field:

\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{r}=\frac{B}{2}(-y,\;x,\;0),\qquad\mathbf{B}=B\hat{z},\qquad(r<R).

(17)

We stress that eq. (
17
) satisfies the condition
\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf{A}=0
, but is not introduced by gauge choice alone: it is the interior limit of the source-generated solution (
14
). This provides a source-consistent route to the “uniform-
B
” vector potential within the AB-extended framework.

Now let us look at the modified quantum dynamics in this field.

The modified bosonic coupling includes an additional diamagnetic contribution compared to minimal coupling, which effectively doubles the coefficient multiplying
\mathbf{A}^{2}
in the Schrödinger-like Hamiltonian. A convenient effective form (with
\phi=0
and restricting to planar motion) is

H_{\rm AB,bos}=\frac{1}{2m}\big(\mathbf{p}-q\mathbf{A}\big)^{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\,\mathbf{A}^{2},

(18)

so that the total coefficient of
\mathbf{A}^{2}
becomes
q^{2}/m
instead of the standard
q^{2}/(2m)
.

Substituting the bulk solenoid potential (
17
) gives

\mathbf{A}^{2}=\frac{B^{2}}{4}(x^{2}+y^{2})=\frac{B^{2}}{4}\,r^{2}.

(19)

In the symmetric gauge one may rewrite the standard minimal-coupling Hamiltonian as

H_{\rm std}=\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}m\left(\frac{\omega_{c}}{2}\right)^{2}r^{2}-\frac{\omega_{c}}{2}L_{z},\qquad\omega_{c}=\frac{|q|B}{m},

(20)

where
L_{z}=xp_{y}-yp_{x}
.
The additional term in (
18
) contributes a second copy of the harmonic piece, yielding

H_{\rm AB,bos}=\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2m}+m\left(\frac{\omega_{c}}{2}\right)^{2}r^{2}-\frac{\omega_{c}}{2}L_{z}=\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}m\Omega^{2}r^{2}-\frac{\omega_{c}}{2}L_{z},\qquad\Omega=\frac{\omega_{c}}{\sqrt{2}}.

(21)

This is a two-dimensional isotropic oscillator coupled to
L_{z}
(Fock–Darwin form). Its eigenvalues can be written as

E_{n_{r},l}=\hbar\Omega\,(2n_{r}+|l|+1)-\frac{\hbar\omega_{c}}{2}\,l,\qquad n_{r}=0,1,2,\ldots,\quad l\in\mathbb{Z}.

(22)

Unlike the standard Landau problem, the spectrum (
22
) depends on the angular-momentum quantum number
l
and therefore does not exhibit the macroscopic Landau degeneracy associated with the guiding-center degree of freedom. Equivalently, the doubled diamagnetic term induces an effective quadratic confinement
\propto r^{2}
already in the bulk uniform-
B
limit, qualitatively modifying the level structure.

The “uniform-
B
” treatment is justified when the relevant wavefunctions are well localized within the solenoid interior, so that the boundary at
r=R
is not probed. A natural scale is the magnetic length

\ell_{B}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{|q|B}},

(23)

and one requires
R\gg\ell_{B}
(and similarly for excited states with larger extent).
