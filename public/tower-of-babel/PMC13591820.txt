Obsessive-compulsive disorders (OCD) and job performance among Iranian healthcare workers
Authors: Soheila Majidi, Amin Rouhi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundObsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD) is one of the psychiatric disorders which characterized by unwanted thoughts (obsessions) and repetitive behaviors (compulsions). Mental health problems can affect job performance of healthcare workers. This study was conducted with the aim of investigating OCD and job performance of healthcare workers in the Primary Health Care (PHC) setting of Hamadan, Iran.MethodsThis study was descriptive-correlational research. Of 403 eligible healthcare workers were selected using convenience sampling method in 50 Urban Community Health Centers (UCHC) and Health Posts (HP) in the city of Hamadan in Iran. The study instruments included the demographic information form, Patterson’s Job Performance Questionnaire, and Yale–Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (YBOCS). Data were analyzed using SPSSv.26 software and the descriptive statistics, Pearson’s correlation test, independent t-test, One-way ANOVA, and Multiple linear regression.ResultsMean scores of OCD and job performance of the healthcare workers were 0.87 ± 0.63 and 2.63 ± 0.35, respectively. Up to 51.2% and 97% employees experienced sub-threshold OCD and better job performance, respectively. The results showed that the total score of OCD had not a significant relationship with job performance (p > 0.05). There was a statistically significant relationship between gender and educational levels with OCD (P < 0.05). The significant predictors of job performance were gender (ʙ= -2.402, p = 0.006) and employment status (ʙ =2.264, p = 0.035).ConclusionsThe findings showed that OCD is more common in women with lower education. It is recommended to implement incentive policies, provide psychological services to alleviate mental health issues, and offer training courses for employees.

Introduction

The health system is a set of organizations, institutions and resources whose main purpose is to improve health [1]. Primary health care (PHC) enables health systems to move towards universal health coverage by providing prevention, treatment, management and rehabilitation services through skilled human resources [2]. Human resources are one of the most important inputs of any health system and have a major impact on the performance of health centers [1, 3]. Evidence shows that healthcare workers are vulnerable to mental health problems. These problems not only have a long-term impact on their mental health, but also negatively affect their decision-making [4]. Mental health disorders are estimated to account for about 14% of the global burden of disease [5]. Healthcare workers are more exposed than others to psychosocial risk factors in their work environment that can lead to adverse health effects, such as stress, fatigue, burnout, and obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) [6]. OCD is a psychiatric disorder [7] characterized by unwanted thoughts (obsessions) and repetitive behaviors (compulsions) [8–11]. While many people engage in repetitive actions in their daily lives, for people with OCD, these thoughts and behaviors are more frequent and intense, often significantly affecting their quality of life and social functioning [8, 12, 13]. This disorder is one of the most common brain disorders [10] and causes significant discomfort and anxiety in social, educational, occupational and other important areas of a person’s life [10], ultimately leading to significant functional problems such as increased unemployment, financial problems and weak social relationships [14]. Contamination, ordering, repetition or checking of topics is very common in OCD disorder [15]. OCD symptoms occur in the context of everyday anxieties and problems, and recur in response to stress caused by external or environmental factors [16].

The prevalence of OCD varies depending on factors such as age, location, and other factors. People with OCD may have different types of symptoms [8]. These symptoms and behaviors can be irregular, fluctuate over time, or change over the course of the illness. People may also deny or do not accept their symptoms and behaviors because of the stigma associated with them [8]. OCD is the fourth most common mental disorder in the world [17], affecting 1.6 to 3% of the world’s population [12]. The World Health Organization (WHO) ranks OCD among the top 10 most disabling disorders worldwide, highlighting its significant impact on global health [18].

Evidence shows that mental health problems can affect the efficiency and satisfaction of healthcare workers in health systems [1, 19]. A study found that healthcare workers exhibit higher levels of OCD symptoms compared to others. Under stressful working conditions, such as during the COVID-19 pandemic, these symptoms especially contamination fears and work-related compulsive behaviors, can negatively affect their mental health and their clinical performance [20]. As a result, OCD leads to increased absenteeism among healthcare workers, weakening the job performance and subsequently reducing the quality of health service delivery [21, 22].

Job performance refers to the performance and achievements of health professionals in their work, which is influenced by personal factors (level of knowledge, skills and experience of health professionals), organizational factors (work environment and resources) and external environmental factors (policies and regulations, competition and social demands) [21, 23]. In fact, job performance is employees’ capability to carry out assigned tasks as well as the motivation required to perform these tasks effectively; both ability and motivation are essential to ensure optimal employee productivity [24].

The job performance of health professionals is crucial to the achievement of organizational goals [25]. Therefore, improving their job performance helps to improve the quality of healthcare services and communication between healthcare employees and patients, which plays an essential role in the sustainable development of healthcare centers [23]. To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to investigate the relationship between OCD and job performance of healthcare workers in Iran. Therefore, considering the possible impact of mental health problems on the job performance of employees, it is hoped that by determining the prevalence of OCD symptoms among healthcare workers, an effective step will be taken in order to improve job performance and subsequently improve the quality and efficiency of healthcare services. This study was conducted with the aim of investigating the relationship between OCD and job performance among healthcare workers in the PHC of Hamadan, Iran.

Methods

Research design, setting and participants

This was a descriptive-correlational study and its statistical population consisted of all employees (n = 650) who work in PHC setting of Hamadan, one of the largest and historic districts in West of Iran, from January to April 2023. A district health center, in Iran’s health care system has responsibility of providing health care services. This center handles urban and rural health centers and staff workers in this are responsible for planning, monitoring, and evaluating the care provided to people. The district health center composed of 50 Urban Community Health Centers (UCHC) and Health Posts (HP).

The study population encompasses all technical healthcare workers who delivered primary health care services, including doctors, nutritionists, psychologists, disease control experts, healthcare providers, midwifery experts, nurses, environmental and professional health experts, and dentists. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (a) people who possess a minimum of an associate degree, b) people with a minimum of one year of experience working in the mentioned centers, c) people employed in the relevant centers during the study period. job resignations or employee dismissals was considered the exclusion criterion. The participants were selected using a convenience sampling method. There were 403 eligible people to enter into the study. The sampling approach may introduce selection bias, which should be considered when interpreting the findings. Although the sample reflects key characteristics of the target population, it may not fully represent individuals with specific characteristics.

Data collection

Data were collected using a demographic information form, Patterson’s Job Performance Questionnaire, and Yale–Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (YBOCS).

Demographic information form

The demographic information form inquired about age, gender, marital status, work experience, educational level, workplace, employment and job.

Patterson’s job performance questionnaire

Patterson’s job performance questionnaire includes 15 items rated on a 0–3 Likert scale (0 = rarely, 3 = always). Total scores range from 0 to 45, with scores above 22 indicating better job performance [26]. Arshadi et al. translated Patterson’s job performance questionnaire into Persian and confirmed the reliability of the questionnaire to be 0.82 [27]. Aslanpour et al. also confirmed the reliability of the questionnaire with Cronbach’s alpha of 0.88 [28]. This study confirmed the Cronbach’s alpha of Patterson’s Job Performance Questionnaire was 0.89 in a pilot study.

Yale–Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (YBOCS)

The Yale- Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (YBOCS) includes 10 items graded from 0 (no symptoms) to 4 (extreme symptoms). The total score reflects overall OCD severity, categorized as: 0–7 (sub-threshold), 8–15 (mild), 16–23 (moderate), 24–31 (severe), and 32–40 (extremely severe) [29]. The reliability coefficient of the Persian version of this questionnaire was measured among 140 patients (0.99) [30]. The reliability of this scale was reported to be higher than 0.80. For the Internal consistency coefficient of this scale, an alpha ranging from 0.69 to 0.91 was also reported [31]. Dadfar et al. reported the reliability of this scale (0.98), Internal consistency coefficient (0.89), and the retest coefficient (0.84) [32].

Data collection

At first, the researchers got permission to conduct the study from the Health Deputy of Hamedan University of Medical Sciences. After obtaining approval, the researchers visited the study centers in Hamedan city. Prior to distributing the questionnaire, they explained the aims of the research to the healthcare workers, ensuring confidentiality of information, and encouraging volountary participation. Participants completed the questionaire on their own, and they were provided with a two-week period to do so. Afterwards, the researchers collected the completed questionnaires for review. In case of any incomplete questionnaire, healthcare workers were asked to fill them out entirely. Participants who did not cooperate were excluded from the study.

Data analysis

The collected data were analyzed using SPSS26 software. The percentage, mean, and standard deviation were calculated to describe the demographic variables. The one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) test and the independent t-test were used to compare the mean difference of OCD based on the demographic variables. Pearson’s correlation coefficient was utilized for evaluating the relationships of OCD and job performance. Multiple linear regression analysis was used to examine the predictive roles of demographic characteristics and OCD in job performance. In all analyses, OCD severity was assessed using the total YBOCS score. All tests were performed at the 0.05 significance level.

Discussion

The mental health of healthcare workers in the healthcare system is crucial, as stress and work pressure can cause they become mentally and physically weakened. Finally, these factors impact the quality of services provided and job performance. Maintaining the mental health of employees is not only beneficial for the workers but also directly affects the health and well-being of the community [19, 21, 22]. The purpose of the present study was to investigate the relationship between OCD and job performance among healthcare workers. Our findings show that there was no significant relationship between total score of OCD and job performance. This may be due to cultural differences and methodological limitations such as sample size and measurement tools. On the other hand, it can be said, the effects of OCD on job performance are strongly influenced by individual differences and the coping strategies of individuals. In environments such as the healthcare sector, the presence of strict protocols, as well as the adaptive skills of employees, can reduce the impact of OCD symptoms on performance; therefore, the total OCD score does not necessarily reflect negative or positive effects on job performance [33].

The current findings indicate that the majority of employees experienced sub-threshold levels of OCD, indicating that the prevalence of OCD among healthcare workers is low, which is consistent with the study by Rintala et al. ‘s study. where almost less than 1% of people experienced OCD [34]. Soleimanvandi Azar and colleagues, also showed that the prevalence of OCD among healthcare workers was 29% during the covid-19 pandemic [7]. A study conducted among medical students reported a prevalence of OCD at 14.4% [35], which demonstrates a relatively low. In contrast, other studies have reported a medium or high prevalence of OCD among employees [5, 36], which contradicts the findings of our study. The possible reason for these differences in the level of OCD can be attributed to the cultural and social issues, differences in individual characteristics of people, features of the work environment, protection and safety guidelines, lack of training and social support, and covid-19 pandemic [7, 19].

Based on the findings of this study, a statistically significant relationship was found between gender and the level of OCD, indicating that women experienced the highest level of OCD. Kurhan et al. reported the higher prevalence of OCD in women, which can usually occur in them during the postpartum or puerperium period (16). In the study by Bigdeli Shamloo et al., the mean score of OCD symptoms was significantly higher in women [10]. In another study by Darvishi et al. it was reported that the prevalence of students’ OCD was slightly higher in women than the men [37], which is consistent with the results of our study. In contrast, in the study by Abba-Aji et al., the highest level of OCD was found in men [36], which is in contrast to the present finding. In another study, there was no association between OCD and gender [38]. In general, OCD usually develops gradually, mainly in early adolescence or early adulthood, after a stressful situation such as pregnancy, childbirth, and problems caused by emotional and work relationships [10].

The results show a statistically significant relationship was found between education level and OCD. In other words, OCD is more common among healthcare workers with lower levels of education. Liu et al. reported that healthcare workers with lower levels of education were associated with increased rates of OCD [5]. Other studies also have reported that there is an association between the level of OCD and low levels of education [34, 36, 39], which is consistent with our findings and in contrast to previous studies that indicated there was no association between the level of OCD and levels of education [10, 38]. It can be said that those with a lower level of education may not have the tools and ability to acquire relevant knowledge to better manage and improve their emotions [5].

Our findings show that women had better job performance, which is consistent with a previous study [1]. In contrast, another study has reported that women have lower job performance [19], which contradicts the findings of our study. The reason for the high job performance of women in this study may be the more of women than men. It can also be said that women tend to perform more effectively in their tasks due to traits such as accuracy, high responsibility, and strong motivation to maintain a work-life balance. On the other hand, factors like social expectations, inequalities, and unequal access to opportunities may also influence the higher performance observed among women.

Another noteworthy finding of this study indicates that healthcare workers with permanent employment status had better job performance, which is in line with previous study [40]. The evidence shows that permanent employees have greater job security compared to others. Lack of job security negatively impacts the mental health of healthcare workers [40]. Since increasing job security among employees and changing their status can improve their performance, providing efficient employment policies is suggested.

Limitation of study

Notwithstanding important findings, this study had limitations that need to be considered. First, this study was conducted only in one city, which limits the ability to generalize the findings to the entire country. Therefore, further studies in other regions are recommended. Second, since the questionnaire was self-reported, there may be biased in the results. It is suggested that future studies use additional measurement methods and tools, such as interviews, to achieve more accurate results. A key limitation of this study was the 62% response rate, which may cause nonresponse bias if non-respondents differ from respondents, potentially limiting result generalizability. Therefore, findings should be interpreted cautiously. Future studies can address this by increasing follow-ups, using statistical weighting, or conducting sensitivity analyses to improve data accuracy and representativeness. Another limitation of this study was the inability to measure and adjust for certain key confounding variables, such as psychiatric history and work-related stress, due to incomplete information. However, the main focus of the research was on examining preliminary relationships and identifying potential patterns, which can serve as a foundation for future studies with more precise control of these variables. Although no specific measures were taken in this study to reduce model complexity and prevent overfitting, this issue is considered an important limitation of the study and it is recommended that future research optimize the model using more advanced techniques.

Conclusions

The findings showed that OCD is more common in women with lower education. Also, there was more job performance between women healthcare workers with permanent employment status. Taking these factors into account, it is recommended to implement incentive policies, address the needs of healthcare workers, and provide psychological services to alleviate mental health issues. Additionally, enhancing communication, increasing job security, and offering training courses for employees are crucial steps. By doing so, organizations can improve overall job performance among healthcare workers.
