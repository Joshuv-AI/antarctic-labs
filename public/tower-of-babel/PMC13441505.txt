Endoscopic retrieval of a fragile foam foreign body at the rectosigmoid junction using an improvised lithotripter-guidewire retrieval device
Authors: Qiutong Lu, Ling Gong, Beibei Liu, Zhilong Chen, Raymond S.Y. Tang, Junbo Hong
Published: 2026

Rectal foreign bodies are most often voluntarily inserted for sexual stimulation, primarily in men. 1​ ​2​ Although various extraction techniques are used clinically, 3​ ​4​ conventional methods sometimes fail with objects of specific characteristics. In this report, we present a case of successful endoscopic retrieval of a rectal foreign body using a simple homemade device constructed from a lithotripter outer sheath and a guidewire ( Video 1 ).

Video 1 Endoscopic retrieval of a 5-cm fragile foam foreign body at the rectosigmoid junction using an improvised lithotripter-guidewire device.

A 51-year-old man presented with a rectal foreign body retained for 24 hours. Prior abdominal computed tomography at a local hospital showed partial bowel dilation with gas and minor air-fluid levels, but no distinct high-density radio-opaque object. Patient was referred to our institution after a failed endoscopic retrieval attempt at a community hospital. Urgent flexible sigmoidoscopy identified a large foam-like object at the rectosigmoid junction. Initial extraction with a standard snare failed due to its soft, slippery plastic surface ( Fig. 1a ). Subsequent retrieval attempts with oval forceps also failed due to poor grasp.

Side-by-side comparison of endoscopic retrieval attempts. ( a ) A standard snare slipping over the object due to its soft, slippery plastic surface. ( b ) The improvised lithotripter-guidewire device achieving a firm grasp on the friable foreign body.

In order to maximize the chance of the en-bloc removal of the potentially fragile foreign body, we improvised to create a retrieval device by combining the basket mechanical lithotripter outer sheath with an endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography guidewire. This combined device achieved a firm grasp and intact removal of the large, fragile foreign body ( Fig. 1b ). To construct this device, the guidewire was folded and threaded through the metal sheath to form a distal loop. The device was then inserted via the working channel, and the loop diameter could be easily adjusted by pushing or pulling the external ends of the guidewire ( Fig. 2 ). The rigid outer sheath provided solid anatomical support, while the flexible guidewire securely looped around the object. The retrieved object was a square synthetic foam measuring approximately 5 cm in length ( Fig. 3 ). Repeat endoscopic examination revealed significant mucosal swelling and multiple ulcers covered with white exudate at the rectum and rectosigmoid junction that were managed conservatively ( Fig. 4 ).

Assembly of the improvised lithotripter-guidewire retrieval device. ( a ) The two ends of the guidewire were inserted into the lithotripter sheath. ( b ) The guidewire was threaded through the sheath to exit the proximal end, forming an adjustable distal loop. ( c ) The loop was narrowed to facilitate insertion through the working channel of the endoscope. ( d ) The assembled device. The yellow, white, and red arrows indicate the endoscope, lithotripter sheath, and guidewire loop, respectively.
