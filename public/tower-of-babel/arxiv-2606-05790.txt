Abstract
. We show that for any uncountable cardinal κ \kappa , there is a coloring c : [ κ ] 2 → ω c:[\kappa]^{2}\to\omega such that c ′′ ​ A ⊗ B = ω c^{\prime\prime}A\otimes B=\omega for any A , B ⊆ κ A,B\subseteq\kappa of order type ω 1 \omega_{1} that are stationary in their common supremum. In particular, the stationary version of Erdős-Rado theorem and the higher dimensional Friedman’s property are both inconsistent. We demonstrate that the theorem is optimal in various ways.



1.
Introduction

Ever since the work by Sierpinski
[
Sie33
]
, it has been known that Ramsey theorem fails for the first uncountable cardinal
\omega_{1}
, namely, there is
c:[\omega_{1}]^{2}\to 2
without uncountable homogeneous subset. Later, works by Laver
[
Lav75
]
, Galvin and Shelah
[
GS73
]
, increase the number of colors to 3 and 4 respectively. These methods are based on the existence of certain uncountable linear orderings, which makes generating more colors difficult. In
[
Tod81
]
, Todorcevic came up with the method of oscillation and was able to produce a coloring
c:[\omega_{1}]^{2}\to\omega
such that for any
stationary

A,B\subseteq\omega_{1}
, it is the case that
c^{\prime\prime}A\otimes B=\omega
. Here
A\otimes B=_{\mathrm{def}}\{(\alpha,\beta):\alpha<\beta,\alpha\in A,\beta\in B\}
. In
[
Tod87
]
, the method of minimal walks on ordinals was introduced, which was combined with the previous method of oscillation to produce a coloring
c:[\omega_{1}]^{2}\to\omega
such that for any
uncountable

A\subseteq\omega_{1}
,
c^{\prime\prime}[A]^{2}=\omega
. Finally, the rectangular version was proved by Moore
[
Moo06
]
(see also
[
RT13
]
for generalizations to other cardinals), by oscillating lower traces that result from walks on ordinals.

In this note, we apply the method of oscillation to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every uncountable cardinal
\kappa
, there exists a coloring
c:[\kappa]^{2}\to\omega
such that
c^{\prime\prime}A\otimes B=\omega
for any
A,B\subseteq\kappa
of order type
\omega_{1}
that are stationary in
\sup A=\sup B
.

Recall that an instance of Erdős-Rado theorem
[
ER56
]
gives that if
\kappa=(2^{\omega})^{+}
, then for any
c:[\kappa]^{2}\to\omega
, there exists
A\subseteq\kappa
of order type
\omega_{1}
such that
c\restriction[A]^{2}
is constant. Theorem
1.1
shows that it is inconsistent to strengthen the conclusion requiring
A
to be stationary in its supremum.

Recall that (a version of)
Friedman’s property
states: for any regular
\kappa\geq\omega_{2}
, for any
c:\kappa\to\omega
, there exists a closed
d
of order type
\omega_{1}
such that
c\restriction d
is constant. Martin’s Maximum
[
FMS88
]
implies that Friedman’s property holds. Theorem
1.1
implies that the 2-dimensional Friedman’s property, namely when the given coloring is
c:[\kappa]^{2}\to\omega
, fails in a strong way.

Let us briefly discuss how the proof compares with those of the previous theorems. In general, using the method of elementary submodels, we are able to control the pattern of oscillation on final segments of two given sets. This leaves us with a task of removing initial segments whose oscillating behavior cannot be controlled in general. When
\kappa
is
\omega_{1}
, such a problem can be easily solved due to the fact that any countable elementary submodel contains an initial segment of
\omega_{1}
. When
\kappa>\omega_{1}
, a way to remove initial segments was invented in
[
Tod94
]
, however, at the cost of using a third coordinate. The main innovation in this note is to come up with a binary function,
\Delta^{\prime}
(see Definition
2.2
), that serves to remove undesirable initial segments in an economic way.

We finish the introduction with some definitions and notations that will be used in the proof. Let
\kappa
and
\theta
be two fixed ordinals for the rest of the section.

3.
Optimality

First we demonstrate that the stationarity requirement is necessary.

. Apply Martin’s Maximum again to find a closed
\Sigma\subseteq S
of order type
\omega_{1}
. It is now easy to check that
c^{\prime\prime}\Gamma\otimes\Sigma=\{i\}
.
∎

Finally, we show that the number of colors is optimal in terms of results provable in
\mathrm{ZFC}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

It is consistent relative to the existence of suitable large cardinals that for any
c:[\omega_{2}]^{2}\to\omega_{1}
, there exists
A\subseteq\omega_{2}
of order type
\omega_{1}
that is stationary in
\sup A
such that
|c^{\prime\prime}[A]^{2}|=\aleph_{0}
.

Proof.

This follows from a classical construction due to Kunen
[
Kun78
]
. See
[
GZ21
, Lemma 2.20]
for a proof.
∎

The conclusion of Proposition
3.3
has large cardinal strength since by
[
Tod81
]
,
\square_{\omega_{1}}
implies there exists a coloring
c:[\omega_{2}]^{2}\to\omega_{1}
, for any
A,B\subseteq\omega_{2}
of order type
\omega_{1}
that are stationary in
\sup A=\sup B
, it is the case that
c^{\prime\prime}A\otimes B=\omega_{1}
.

4.
A consequence regarding set-systems

Recall the following definitions from
[
EGH75
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

\mathcal{I}
is a
set-system with set of vertices
V
if for any
A\in\mathcal{I}
,
A\subseteq V
and
|A|\geq 2
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a set-system with set of vertices
V
. The
chromatic number
of
\mathcal{I}
is the smallest cardinal
\kappa
for which there is a function
f:V\to\kappa
such that for any
\xi<\kappa
and
A\in\mathcal{I}
,
A\not\subseteq f^{-1}(\{\xi\})
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\{\mathcal{I}_{\nu}:\nu<\lambda\}
be a collection of set-systems with the same set of vertices
V
. The system is said to have
simultaneous chromatic number

\kappa
if
\kappa
is the smallest cardinal such that there is a function
f:V\to\kappa
such that for any
\xi<\kappa
, there is
\nu<\lambda
such that for any
A\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}
,
A\not\subseteq f^{-1}(\{\xi\})
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a set-system with set of vertices
V
.
P(\mathcal{I},\lambda,\kappa)
is said to hold if there is a partition of
\mathcal{I}
into a union of
\lambda
disjoint set-systems
\mathcal{I}_{\nu}
(
\nu<\lambda
) in such a way that this system has simultaneous chromatic number
\geq\kappa
.

Erdős, Galvin and Hajnal
[
EGH75
]
showed that
\omega_{1}\not\to[stat(\omega_{1})]^{2}_{\omega}
implies that
P([\omega_{1}]^{2},\aleph_{0},\aleph_{1})
holds. Theorem
1.1
gives rise to the validity of the following variant.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a set-system with set of vertices
V
and let
V^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(V)
.
P(\mathcal{I},\lambda,\kappa,V^{\prime})
is said to hold if there is a partition of
\mathcal{I}
into a union of
\lambda
disjoint set-systems
\mathcal{I}_{\nu}
(
\nu<\lambda
) in such a way that for any
A\in V^{\prime}
,
\langle\mathcal{I}_{\nu}\restriction A:\nu<\lambda\rangle
has simultaneous chromatic number
\geq\kappa
.

Corollary 4.6
.

P([\kappa]^{2},\aleph_{0},\aleph_{1},\kappa\cap\cof(\omega_{1}))
holds for any cardinal
\kappa\geq\omega_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
c:[\kappa]^{2}\to\omega
be given by Theorem
1.1
. For each
n\in\omega
, let
\mathcal{I}_{n}=c^{-1}(\{n\})
. For any
\gamma\in\kappa\cap\cof(\omega_{1})
and for any
f:\gamma\to\omega
, there is some
m\in\omega
and
A\subseteq\gamma
of order type
\omega_{1}
that is stationary in
\gamma
such that
f^{\prime\prime}A=\{m\}
. By the property of
c
, for all
n\in\omega
, there are
\alpha<\beta\in A
such that
c(\alpha,\beta)=n
, namely,
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\restriction\gamma
. Hence,
\langle\mathcal{I}_{n}\restriction\gamma:n\in\omega\rangle
has simultaneous chromatic number
\geq\aleph_{1}
, as desired.
∎
