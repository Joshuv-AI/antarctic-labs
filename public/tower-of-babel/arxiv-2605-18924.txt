REMARKS ON PRIMITIVE REGULATION
Milan Rosko

Abstract
We prove, and mechanize in Rocq , an abstract obstruction theorem for closure predicates C : 𝖥𝗈𝗋𝗆 → 𝖯𝗋𝗈𝗉 C:\mathsf{Form}\to\mathsf{Prop} over the closed implication-falsity fragment A , B : := ⊥ ∣ A → B A,B::=\bot\mid A\to B . Evaluation completeness, 𝖤𝗏𝖺𝗅 ⁡ ( C ) \mathsf{Eval}(C) , says that every formula-valued behavior of codes is represented up to closure equivalence, where A ≃ C B A\simeq_{C}B abbreviates C ⁡ ( A → B ) ∧ C ⁡ ( B → A ) C(A\to B)\land C(B\to A) . For any C C closed under modus ponens and satisfying consistency, this completeness principle is incompatible with the internal excluded-middle schema 𝖫𝖤𝖬 ⁡ ( C ) \mathsf{LEM}(C) , namely ∀ A , C ⁡ ( A ) ∨ C ⁡ ( ¬ A ) \forall A,C(A)\lor C(\neg A) . Thus 𝖤𝗏𝖺𝗅 ⁡ ( C ) \mathsf{Eval}(C) , 𝖬𝖯 ⁡ ( C ) \mathsf{MP}(C) , 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗌 ⁡ ( C ) \mathsf{Cons}(C) , and 𝖫𝖤𝖬 ⁡ ( C ) \mathsf{LEM}(C) cannot hold jointly. The proof uses evaluation completeness to obtain a formula B B such that B ≃ ¬ C ⁡ B B\simeq_{C}\lnot B . Applying 𝖫𝖤𝖬 ⁡ ( C ) \mathsf{LEM}(C) to this B B , either alternative gives C ⁡ ( ⊥ ) C(\bot) by detachment, contradicting consistency. Consequently, any Boolean decision procedure for C C induces the obstructed excluded-middle schema. Mere refutation behaves differently: its false branch carries no closure condition, and is therefore inhabited by the always-false classifier.

1 Introduction

1.1 Background

Limitative results are typically established within their native semantics:
Incompleteness

[
Whitehead and Russell 1927
;
Gödel 1931
;
Rosser 1936
]
relies on the arithmetization of syntax;
Undefinability

[
Tarski 1933
]
proceeds by diagonalizing against a hypothesized truth predicate;
Semidecidability

[
Church 1936
;
Turing 1937
;
Kleene 1952
;
Rice 1953
]
presupposes a fixed model of computation;
Löb’s Theorem

[
Löb 1955
]
depends on the modal derivability conditions. The present result isolates the diagonal obstruction shared by these results under a minimal set of propositions.

1.2 Intuition

Prior to the formal definitions, let
C
denote a primitive acceptance predicate

\mathrm{regulator}\;C,

(1)

on the closed implication–falsity fragment. Thus
C(A)
means only that the formula
A
is accepted. We imagine three closure-level conditions. First,
C
supports
detachment
: if
C(A\to B)
and
C(A)
, then
C(B)
. Second,
C
is
consistent
: it does not accept
\bot
. Third,
C
is
total
at the object level: for every formula
A
, either
C(A)
or
C(\lnot A)
.

The diagonal step requires one further ingredient: an evaluation interface whose indices produce formulas when applied to indices. If this interface is complete enough to name formula-valued behaviours up to closure equivalence, then
C
can be tested against the behaviour that sends an index
x
to the negation of the formula obtained by applying
x
to itself. Self-application yields a formula
B
such that
B\simeq_{C}\lnot B
, meaning that
C
accepts both implications between
B
and
\lnot B
. The formal argument below makes this pressure precise at the diagonal formula
B
.

Our result is close enough to
Curry’s Paradox

[
Curry and Feys 1958
;
Sørensen and Urzyczyn 2006
]
to require separation. In the Curry pattern, one begins with a formula satisfying

B\leftrightarrow(B\to\bot)

(2)

and derives
\bot
by exploiting the conditional behaviour of that very formula. The collapse is therefore driven by a formula that says, in effect, that its own acceptance entails absurdity.

The present argument has a different locus. The diagonal formula is not a syntactic fixed point, it satisfies only

B\simeq_{C}\lnot B,

(3)

that is,
C
accepts the two implications between
B
and
\lnot B
. It is obtained from top-level
C
-facts:
\mathsf{LEM}(C)
supplies one of
C(B)
and
C(\lnot B)
, and
\mathsf{MP}(C)
detaches along the accepted implications. Thus the obstruction is not a collapse of the conditional. A preliminary intuition is that the question

“True or false?
True or false
holds for every formula.”

already pressures the very form of answer under examination. To answer internally with
yes
is to accept unrestricted closure-level bivalence. To answer internally with
no
would also be a closure-level verdict about the totality principle itself.

2 Substrate

2.1 Object syntax

Definition 2.1.1

(Syntax)
.

A constructive theory
[
Troelstra and van Dalen 1988
]
, e.g.
Calculus of Constructions

[
Coquand and Huet 1988
]
, isolates our object language: a minimal propositional language, containing only falsity and implication.

A,B\;::=\;\bot\quad\mid\quad A\to B,\quad\lnot A\;\mathrel{\triangleq}\;A\to\bot.

(4)

We write
\mathsf{Form}
for the type of formulas; formulas are finite trees over
\{\bot,\to\}
.

Lemma 2.1.2

(No formula is its own negation)
.

For every formula,

\forall A\in\mathsf{Form},\;A\neq A\to\bot.

(5)

\lnot A=A\to\bot
has
A
as a proper subterm. A finite tree cannot be a proper subterm of itself.
∎

Remark 2.1.3
.

Lemma
2.1.2
forces every fixed-point claim below to be stated via
\simeq_{C}
, never as a syntactic identity of formulas.

2.2 Closure predicates

Definition 2.2.1

(Primitive regulator with closure predicate)
.

A
primitive regulator
on the formula language is a predicate

C\;:\;\mathsf{Form}\to\mathsf{Prop}.

(6)

We write
C(A)
, read “
C
accepts
A
.” Primitive regulation is the bare acceptance layer: no semantic truth, model satisfaction, arithmetic provability, or model of computation is implied, and
C(A)
is treated as a mere proposition. We also call
C
a
closure predicate
when emphasizing closure properties. Every later result is parametric in
C
and never inspects its definition.

Remark 2.2.2
.

Throughout, omitted parameters in
\mathsf{MP}(C){}
,
\mathsf{Cons}(C){}
,
\mathsf{LEM}(C){}
,
\mathsf{Dec}(C){}
,
\mathsf{Ref}(C){}
,
\mathsf{Eval}(C){}
, and
\mathsf{NegFP}_{C}(B)
refer to the ambient closure predicate
C
.

Definition 2.2.3

(Closure equivalence)
.

For formulas
A
and
B
, closure equivalence means mutual acceptance of the corresponding implications. We define

A\simeq_{C}B\;\mathrel{\triangleq}\;C(A\to B)\;\land\;C(B\to A).

(7)

Thus
A
and
B
are equivalent relative to
C
when
C
accepts both directions between them.

Remark 2.2.4
.

\simeq_{C}
records the two accepted implications between
A
and
B
. By Lemma
2.1.2
,
A=\lnot A
never holds, whereas
A\simeq_{C}\lnot A
may, depending on
C
.

Definition 2.2.5

(Modus ponens, consistency, and internal excluded middle)
.

The structural properties of
C
are

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathsf{MP}(C)&\mathrel{\triangleq}&\forall A,B:\mathsf{Form},\;\bigl(C(A\to B)\land C(A)\bigr)\to C(B),\\[4.30554pt]
\mathsf{Cons}(C)&\mathrel{\triangleq}&C(\bot)\to\bot,\\[4.30554pt]
\mathsf{LEM}(C)&\mathrel{\triangleq}&\forall A:\mathsf{Form},\;\;C(A)\;\lor\;C(\lnot A).\end{array}

(8)

Remark 2.2.6

(Internal versus metatheoretic excluded middle)
.

The schema
\mathsf{LEM}(C)
is an internal totality condition on the closure predicate:

\forall A:\mathsf{Form},\;C(A)\lor C(\lnot A).

(9)

It should not be confused with the metatheoretic excluded middle instance

\forall A:\mathsf{Form},\;C(A)\lor\neg C(A),

(10)

which may be available in a classical ambient theory.

Definition 2.2.7

(Decision and refutation)
.

A
decision
for
C
is a Boolean classifier whose two branches are sound:

\mathsf{Dec}(C)\;\mathrel{\triangleq}\;\exists d:\mathsf{Form}\to\{\mathsf{tt},\mathsf{ff}\},\quad\begin{aligned} &\forall A:\mathsf{Form},\;d(A)=\mathsf{tt}\to C(A),\\
&\forall A:\mathsf{Form},\;d(A)=\mathsf{ff}\to C(\lnot A).\end{aligned}

(11)

A
refutation
is the negative-only counterpart, with no constraint on the false branch:

\mathsf{Ref}(C)\;\mathrel{\triangleq}\;\exists r:\mathsf{Form}\to\{\mathsf{tt},\mathsf{ff}\},\quad\forall A:\mathsf{Form},\;r(A)=\mathsf{tt}\to C(\lnot A).

(12)

2.3 Evaluation frame and concrete regulators

Definition 2.3.1

(Evaluation frame)
.

Fix an arbitrary type
\mathsf{Code}
of
codes
. An
evaluation frame for

C
is a record with two fields: an evaluation operation

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathsf{eval}\;:\;\mathsf{Code}\to\mathsf{Code}\to\mathsf{Form},\\[4.30554pt]
(c,x)\mapsto\mathsf{eval}(c,x),\end{array}

(13)

and a completeness field, stated up to the relation
\simeq_{C}
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathsf{Eval}(C)\;\mathrel{\triangleq}\;\forall f:\mathsf{Code}\to\mathsf{Form},\\[4.30554pt]
\exists c\;\forall x\;\mathsf{eval}(c,x)\simeq_{C}f(x).\end{array}

(14)

The two fields are not independent hypotheses:
\mathsf{Eval}(C)
quantifies over
\mathsf{eval}(\cdot,\cdot)
, so the frame is a single bundled datum, and supplying a frame means supplying both at once.

Remark 2.3.2
.

The theorems of Section
3
take an evaluation frame as structure and project the needed field, so
\mathsf{Eval}(C)
is not a free-standing axiom.
\mathsf{Eval}(C)
places no definability or computability condition on
f
, and
\mathsf{Code}
is arbitrary. Categorically,
\mathsf{Eval}(C)
has the form of point-surjectivity with equality weakened to
\simeq_{C}
.

Remark 2.3.3
.

If evaluation completeness were stated with syntactic equality rather than closure equivalence, diagonalization would produce a formula
B
such that
B=\lnot B
. This contradicts Lemma
2.1.2
. The relaxation to
\simeq_{C}
is therefore not cosmetic: it is what prevents the evaluation interface from being immediately impossible.

Remark 2.3.4
.

Section
3
uses only a special case of the full evaluation frame. For a fixed goal formula
G
, it suffices to name the single behaviour

x\mapsto\mathsf{eval}(x,x)\to G.

(15)

We write this goal-restricted completeness principle as

\mathsf{Eval}_{G}(C)\;\mathrel{\triangleq}\;\exists c\;\forall x\;\mathsf{eval}(c,x)\simeq_{C}\mathsf{eval}(x,x)\to G.

(16)

A full frame yields
\mathsf{Eval}_{G}(C)
for every
G
by instantiating
\mathsf{Eval}(C)
at

f:=x\mapsto\mathsf{eval}(x,x)\to G
.

Definition 2.3.5

(Regulator Theory)
.

A
Regulator Theory
is a syntactic theory that generates a concrete pair

R\;\mathrel{\triangleq}\;(\pi,\operatorname{T}),

(17)

where
\pi
is a regulator logic and
\operatorname{T}
is an axiom set of object formulas. A context
\Gamma
supplies local assumptions but is not part of the theory itself. Together,
R
and
\Gamma
induce the closure predicate

C_{R,\Gamma}(A)\;\mathrel{\triangleq}\;\Gamma\vdash_{R}A,

(18)

where
\vdash_{R}
denotes checked derivability in
R
. Hence a
Regulator Theory
serves as a checked syntactic source of accepted formulas, rather than as a model, truth predicate, or semantic environment.

3 Obstruction

3.1 Fixed-point

Lemma 3.1.1

(Generic closure fixed-point)
.

Under
\mathsf{Eval}(C)
, for every transformer
g:\mathsf{Form}\to\mathsf{Form}
there exists
B:\mathsf{Form}
with

B\simeq_{C}g(B).

(19)

Apply
\mathsf{Eval}(C)
to
f:=x\mapsto g(\mathsf{eval}(x,x))
to obtain a code

\forall x,\;\mathsf{eval}(c,x)\simeq_{C}g(\mathsf{eval}(x,x)).

(20)

By self-application
[
Curry and Feys 1958
]

B\mathrel{\triangleq}\mathsf{eval}(c,c)

B\simeq_{C}g(B)

∎

Definition 3.1.2

(Negation fixed-point inside
C

.

A formula

is a

for

\mathsf{NegFP}_{C}(B)\;\mathrel{\triangleq}\;B\simeq_{C}\lnot B.

(21)

Remark 3.1.3
.

The negation instance uses only the goal-restricted frame

of Remark
2.3.4
. The full frame is stronger than needed.

Theorem 3.1.4

(Negation fixed-point)
.

Under
\mathsf{Eval}(C)
,

\exists B:\mathsf{Form},\;\mathsf{NegFP}_{C}(B).

(22)

Apply Lemma
3.1.1
to the transformer

. The resulting

satisfies

, which is

by Definition
3.1.2
.
∎

Remark 3.1.5
.

This is the only fixed-point construction used below. The proof is a single application of the fixed-point lemma followed by the unfolding

\lnot B\mathrel{\triangleq}B\to\bot.

(23)

The proof uses neither
\mathsf{LEM}(C)
,
\mathsf{MP}(C)
, nor
\mathsf{Cons}(C)
; moreover, by Lemma
2.1.2
, the fixed point is necessarily relative to

5 Discussion

5.1 More Intuition

Formally,
\mathsf{LEM}(C)
has no side condition:

\forall A,\;C(A)\lor C(\lnot A).

(31)

It does not require the
A/\lnot A
alternative to be admissible by any further criterion.
\mathsf{Eval}(C)
supplies the code
c
naming the behavior

x\mapsto\mathsf{eval}(x,x)\to\bot.

(32)

Self-application produces the formula

B\;\mathrel{\triangleq}\;\mathsf{eval}(c,c).

(33)

By the representing property of
c
, this
B
satisfies

B\simeq_{C}\lnot B.

(34)

Equivalently, unfolded, since
\lnot B
abbreviates
B\to\bot
, this says

C\bigl(B\to(B\to\bot)\bigr)\land C\bigl((B\to\bot)\to B\bigr).

(35)

The formal source is the interaction between
B\simeq_{C}\lnot B
and the unrestricted instance of
\mathsf{LEM}(C)
at
B
. The collapse arises because
\mathsf{LEM}(C)
has no admissibility condition on
A
. Once
B
exists, the proof is mechanical: instantiate
\mathsf{LEM}(C)
at
B
, use the two implications in
B\simeq_{C}\lnot B
, and detach to obtain
C(\bot)
.

This clarifies the intuitionistic restraint. The constructive refusal is not a competing answer to
\mathsf{LEM}(C)
, it is the refusal to assert the schema uniformly. An external metatheorist may conclude that a consistent
C
must withhold both disjuncts at the diagonal formula
B
:

“The regulator must not accept either branch at the diagonal formula if it is to remain consistent.”

That diagnosis is not a
C
-fact unless it is imported into
C
. The obstruction begins precisely when such verdicts are required internally and uniformly. The collapse does not arise from “too much” semantics, but from too little semantic discrimination: a regulator treats excluded-middle completeness as a purely formal closure demand, with no criterion that can exclude the diagonal case.

5.2 Comparisons and Outlook

Evaluation completeness plays the role of a weak point-surjectivity condition, while the diagonal behavior produces a fixed-point. The fixed point is not an equality:

B\simeq_{C}\lnot B\quad\text{meaning}\quad C(B\to\lnot B)\land C(\lnot B\to B).

(36)

Substructural treatments sharpen the comparison. Roberts’ refinement of
Lawvere’s fixed-point theorem

[
Roberts 2023
]
shows that diagonal arguments can survive after substantial weakening of the surrounding structural assumptions, close to the present economy. Reuse occurs outside the object language, at the level of
C(A)
-facts, not as a structural rule internal to the object language.

The result should also be contrasted with universal-schema presentations of diagonalization, such as
Yanofsky 2003
. Such presentations treat many paradoxes and limitative theorems as instances of a common diagonal-point pattern. The present theorem belongs to this tradition, but the fixed point is formed at the level of
\simeq_{C}
. Unlike those presentations, the relevant point here is not syntactic, semantic, or provable equality, but closure equivalence alone:

B\simeq_{C}\lnot B.

(37)

This also explains why the comparison with
Curry’s paradox
is limited. The latter collapse is usually sensitive to structural assumptions governing the conditional, especially contraction. Here the object language is only the closed implication–falsity fragment, and the collapse does not rely on an internal contraction rule of the object logic. The theorem is therefore a diagonal obstruction for
\mathsf{LEM}(C)
, not a contraction-sensitive Curry collapse.

The comparison with the
Second Incompleteness Theorem

[
Gödel 1931
]
and reflection principles
[
Feferman 1962
]
is therefore indirect: those results concern systems that internalize derivability, whereas
\mathsf{Eval}(C)
supplies only the propositional-core analogue, naming formula-valued evaluative behavior up to closure equivalence.

A future extension could define reductions between nested closure predicates or
Regulator Theories
and ask which hypotheses are preserved under such reductions. This would turn the obstruction from a single incompatibility theorem into a structural map: some translations may preserve
\mathsf{MP}(C){}
and
\mathsf{Cons}(C){}
while weakening evaluation completeness, others may preserve the evaluation interface while breaking
\mathsf{LEM}(C)
, still others may fail to preserve the relevant closure equivalence itself. Charting these cases would clarify which parts of the obstruction are invariant under changes of regulator and which part depends on the particular presentation of acceptance.

5.3 Summary

The obstruction uses none of arithmetization, a fixed computational model, a truth predicate, or a categorical evaluation structure. It uses only a predicate
C
over the implication–falsity fragment,
\simeq_{C}
,
\mathsf{MP}(C)
,
\mathsf{Cons}(C)
, and the evaluation interface. This keeps the result portable across settings where richer machinery is unavailable or deliberately withheld.
