A Diminutive Colonic Lesion of Poorly Differentiated Adenocarcinoma With Submucosal Invasion
Authors: Takuro Tomino, Kensuke Shinmura, Atsushi Inaba, Yukiko Sasahara, Tomonori Yano
Published: 2026

CASE REPORT

A 75-year-old man with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) underwent a screening colonoscopy that revealed a 4 mm slightly elevated lesion with a subtly depressed central area and submucosal tumor-like protrusion—classified as Paris classification 0-IIa+IIc—in the transverse colon (Figure 1). Magnifying narrow-band imaging revealed an irregular distribution of vessels and obscure surface patterns, consistent with the Japan narrow-band imaging Expert Team classification type 2B (Figure 1). Given suspected high-grade dysplasia, a hot-snare polypectomy was performed. Histopathologic examination revealed poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma (pT1b, 1800 µm) with positive lymphatic invasion, negative venous invasion, budding grade 1, and negative resection margins (Figure 2). Tumor cells were predominantly located in the submucosa, with only a small component exposed on the surface of the depressed area (Figure 2). Negative staining for chromogranin A, synaptophysin, CD56, and hepatocyte antigen excluded the possibility of neuroendocrine tumors or metastasis from the HCC (Figure 2). Additional surgery was not performed because of HCC lung metastases. His serum carcinoembryonic antigen level before resection was 25.4 ng/mL.

Colonoscopy revealed a 4 mm slightly elevated lesion with a subtly depressed central area and submucosal tumor-like protrusion in the transverse colon. (A) White light imaging. (B) Narrow-band imaging. (C) Magnifying narrow-band imaging. Irregular distribution of vessels and obscure surface patterns were shown.

Histological examination of the resected specimen. (A) Hematoxylin and eosin staining (5×). The lesion did not have any components of adenoma or differentiated adenocarcinoma. (B) Magnified image of the mucosal layer (20×). Tumor cells were predominantly located in the submucosa, with only a small component exposed on the surface of the depressed area. (C) D2-40 staining (35×) revealed positive lymphatic invasion. (D) The tumor was negative for hepatocyte staining (5×).

A diminutive lesion composed entirely of poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma is extremely rare.1 Even in diminutive lesions, suspected early colorectal cancer should be managed with endoscopic mucosal resection or endoscopic submucosal dissection.2
