Single-cell and spatial transcriptomics reveal SUV39H1 as a master epigenetic driver of immunosuppressive niche and stemness in bladder cancer

Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Introduction

Bladder cancer (BLCA) remains a major global health burden, characterized by high recurrence rates, heterogeneous clinical outcomes, and limited therapeutic options for advanced stages. Despite significant advances in surgical techniques and immunotherapy, the molecular mechanisms driving tumor progression, immune evasion, and therapeutic resistance remain incompletely understood[[1], [2], [3]]. The profound inter- and intra-tumoral heterogeneity of BLCA poses a critical challenge to precise diagnosis and effective treatment stratification, necessitating the identification of robust biomarkers and actionable therapeutic targets that govern its aggressive phenotype[[4], [5], [6]].

Emerging evidence highlights that epigenetic dysregulation, particularly histone modifications, serves as a fundamental driver of bladder carcinogenesis beyond genetic mutations[1,7]. Among these, the methylation of histone H3 at lysine 9 (H3K9) is a pivotal repressive mark that maintains genomic stability and controls cell fate decisions[8]. SUV39H1, the primary histone methyltransferase responsible for catalyzing H3K9 trimethylation (H3K9me3), has been implicated in the silencing of tumor suppressor genes and the promotion of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in various malignancies[9]. Recent studies suggest that SUV39H1 overexpression may foster a stem-like state and facilitate an immunosuppressive microenvironment, yet its specific spatial distribution, functional modules, and precise role in orchestrating the complex cellular crosstalk within the BLCA ecosystem remain largely unexplored[10].

Current diagnostic and prognostic models often rely on single-gene markers or bulk transcriptomic signatures that fail to capture the spatial architecture and cellular diversity of the tumor microenvironment (TME)[11,12]. There is an urgent need to integrate multi-omics approaches—spanning bulk RNA-seq, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), and spatial transcriptomics—to dissect how key epigenetic regulators like SUV39H1 influence distinct cell subpopulations and their interactions[5,13]. Specifically, understanding whether SUV39H1 acts as a central hub connecting inflammatory stromal components with high-stemness epithelial clones could unlock new strategies for disrupting tumor progression[14].

In this study, we employed a comprehensive multi-omics framework to systematically characterize the molecular landscape of bladder cancer. By integrating weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) with machine learning algorithms, we identified SUV39H1 as a core hub gene critically associated with tumor status and poor prognosis[15]. We further validated its diagnostic potency and elucidated its functional enrichment in oncogenic and immune-regulatory pathways. Leveraging unsupervised clustering, we defined distinct molecular subtypes linked to specific immune infiltration patterns, revealing an immunosuppressive phenotype driven by high SUV39H1 expression[16,17]. Crucially, high-resolution scRNA-seq analysis uncovered the pivotal role of SUV39H1-associated epithelial subpopulations in maintaining stemness and engaging in extensive ligand-receptor crosstalk with inflammatory fibroblasts[18,19]. Finally, spatial transcriptomics confirmed the regionally enriched expression of SUV39H1 at tumor invasion margins, correlating with activated oncogenic signaling pathways[20,21]. Collectively, our findings establish SUV39H1 as a master regulator of BLCA progression, offering novel insights into its dual role in shaping the immune microenvironment and sustaining tumor stemness, thereby highlighting its potential as a promising therapeutic target.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition and preprocessing

Transcriptomic data for BLCA, including gene expression profiles and corresponding clinical information, were downloaded from The Cancer Genome Atlas(TCGA)-BLCA database. . Raw count data were normalized using the DESeq2 package in R. To ensure data compatibility, batch effects between TCGA and Genotype-Tissue Expression(GTEx) datasets were removed using the ComBat function from the sva package. Additionally, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data from multiple BLCA patients were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus(GEO) database(GSE135337). Spatial transcriptomics data for proof-of-concept validation were retrieved from public repositories covering kidney renal clear cell carcinoma (KIRC) and prostate adenocarcinoma (PRAD), with parallel validation performed on our internal BLCA cohort.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

To identify gene modules associated with tumor progression, we constructed a weighted gene co-expression network using the WGCNA R package. A soft-thresholding power was selected based on the scale-free topology criterion to ensure a robust network structure. Hierarchical clustering was performed to group genes with similar expression patterns into distinct modules. Module-trait relationships were calculated by correlating module eigengenes with clinical traits (tumor vs. normal status). Modules showing the strongest positive or negative correlations were selected for downstream analysis. Hub genes were identified by intersecting genes from significant modules with differentially expressed genes (DEGs).

Machine learning-based feature selection

To refine the list of core diagnostic genes, we employed two machine learning algorithms: Random Forest (RF) and Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression. The RF model was implemented using the randomForest package to rank gene importance based on mean decrease in accuracy, with the optimal number of predictors determined via cross-validated Area Under the Curve (AUC). Simultaneously, LASSO regression was performed using the glmnet package to penalize coefficients and select a sparse set of features. The intersection of genes selected by both methods was defined as the final hub gene signature. Diagnostic performance was evaluated using Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves across multiple classifiers, including RF, Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM), and Logistic Regression.

Molecular subtyping and immune microenvironment analysis

Unsupervised consensus clustering was performed on the expression profiles of the hub genes to identify molecular subtypes of BLCA. The optimal number of clusters was determined using the cumulative distribution function (CDF) and silhouette width. Differential expression analysis between subtypes was conducted using the limma package. To characterize the immune landscape, we estimated the abundance of 22 immune cell types using the CIBERSORT algorithm. Differences in immune cell infiltration and expression of immune checkpoint genes (e.g., CTLA4, PDCD1, LAG3) between subtypes were statistically compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test.

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) analysis

Raw scRNA-seq data were processed using the Seurat R package. Quality control filters excluded cells with fewer than 200 detected genes, more than 20% mitochondrial gene content, or doublet scores exceeding the threshold. Data were normalized, scaled, and subjected to Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for dimensionality reduction. Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) was used for visualization and clustering. Cell types were annotated based on canonical marker genes. To dissect epithelial heterogeneity, epithelial cells were subset and re-clustered using Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF). Stemness potential was evaluated using the CytoTRACE algorithm, which infers differentiation states based on transcriptional diversity. Cell-cell communication networks were inferred using the CellChat package to identify significant ligand-receptor interactions.

Spatial transcriptomics analysis

Spatial gene expression data were analyzed using the Seurat and Giotto packages. Spatial heatmaps were generated to visualize the distribution of key genes (SUV39H1, S100A8) within tissue sections. Regions of interest, such as tumor margins and stromal invasion zones, were manually annotated to assess localized gene enrichment. Pathway enrichment analysis was performed on spatially variable genes to link local expression patterns with functional biological processes.

Cell culture and lentiviral transduction

Human bladder cancer cell lines (TCCSUP and RT4 cells) were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37°C with 5% CO₂. To suppress SUV39H1 expression, cells were transduced with lentiviral vectors carrying short hairpin RNA (shRNA) targeting SUV39H1 (sh-SUV39H1) or a non-targeting control (sh-NC). Transduction efficiency was verified by quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) and Western blotting 48 hours post-infection.

Plate colony formation assay

To assess cell proliferation potential, 500 cells per well were seeded into 6-well plates and cultured for 10–14 days. Colonies were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with 0.1% crystal violet. Colonies containing more than 50 cells were counted under a microscope. Experiments were performed in triplicate.

Transwell migration and invasion assays

Cell migration and invasion capabilities were evaluated using Transwell chambers (8-μm pore size). For the invasion assay, the upper chamber was coated with Matrigel (BD Biosciences); for the migration assay, uncoated inserts were used. Cells suspended in serum-free medium were seeded into the upper chamber, while complete medium with 10% FBS was added to the lower chamber as a chemoattractant. After 24 hours (migration) or 48 hours (invasion), cells on the upper surface were removed, and those that migrated or invaded to the lower surface were fixed, stained, and counted in five random fields.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software and GraphPad Prism. Differences between two groups were assessed using the Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test, while comparisons among multiple groups were conducted using one-way ANOVA. Survival analysis was performed using the Kaplan-Meier method and log-rank test. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Identification of key co-expression modules and core genes driving bladder cancer progression

To dissect the molecular mechanisms underlying bladder cancer (BLCA) progression, we constructed a weighted gene co-expression network (WGCNA) and determined that a soft-thresholding power of 12 achieved a high scale-free topology fit with sharply declining mean connectivity, establishing a robust framework for downstream analysis (Fig. 1A). Within this optimized network, the MEgreenyellow module exhibited the strongest positive correlation with tumor status, whereas the MEblue module showed the most significant negative association, suggesting distinct transcriptional programs drive malignant transformation (Fig. 1B). By intersecting WGCNA modules with differentially expressed genes and bladder cancer-associated genes, we pinpointed only 8 hub genes at the triple overlap as core regulatory factors (Fig. 1C). Finally, strong correlations between module membership and gene significance across six key modules, notably the brown module, statistically confirmed their biological coherence as potential drivers in BLCA (Fig. 1D).

Ranking of hub gene importance highlighted SHMT2, SUV39H1, and MIF as top contributors, underscoring their central role in classification (Fig. 2B). Functional characterization and diagnostic validation of the hub gene signature

Pathway enrichment analysis indicated that the top 50 marker genes are significantly enriched in cancer-related and immune signaling pathways, suggesting dual roles in tumorigenesis and immune regulation (Fig. 3A). Comparative evaluation of multiple classifiers demonstrated that Random Forest, GBM, and Lasso models possess exceptional discriminatory power, validating the robustness of the selected gene set (Fig. 3B). Quantitative confirmation of expression differences for all 8 core genes showed that EZH2, GPT2, MIF, SHMT2, SLC6A8, SUV39H1, and TYMS were significantly upregulated in tumors, while SLC25A10 was downregulated, reinforcing their potential as diagnostic biomarkers (Fig. 3C).

Unsupervised clustering reveals distinct molecular subtypes with divergent immune checkpoint profiles

Based on the transcriptomic landscape, unsupervised clustering stratified BLCA patients into two major clusters (Class 1 vs. Class 2), with the first principal component explaining the majority of variance and indicating significant molecular stratification (Fig. 4A). Differential expression analysis identified numerous genes with immune-related terms predominantly enriched in the upregulated region of Class 2 (Fig. 4B). Systematic examination of expression differences highlighted that key immune checkpoint genes, including CTLA4, LAG3, PDCD1, and CD274, are generally elevated in Class 2 (Fig. 4C). Quantitative validation confirmed that four core immune regulators—LAG3, ICOSLG, CXCL10, and CTLA4—are all significantly overexpressed in Class 2, confirming this subtype exhibits an "immune-hot" yet potentially exhausted phenotype (Fig. 4D).

Landscape of immune cell infiltration: class 2 subtype exhibits an immunosuppressive microenvironment

Characterization of the tumor microenvironment using the CIBERSORT algorithm revealed that, compared to Class 1, Class 2 is significantly enriched in activated dendritic cells, M2 macrophages, and regulatory T cells (Tregs), while resting mast cells and CD8+ T cells are relatively depleted (Fig. 5A). Ranking of cell abundances confirmed the dominance of Tregs, resting NK cells, and M2 macrophages in Class 2 (Fig. 5B). we also found that Class 2 has a stronger immunosuppressive function (Fig. 5C). Quantitative comparison provided further evidence that Class 2 exhibits significantly elevated levels of Tregs and M2 macrophages but reduced CD8+ T cells and activated NK cells, suggesting an immunosuppressive microenvironment that may limit checkpoint inhibitor efficacy (Fig. 5D).

Group 2 is enriched in activated dendritic cells and cytotoxic lymphocytes; Group 1 in regulatory/suppressive cells.(B) CIBERSORT scores confirm differential abundance patterns across 22 immune subsets.(C) Heatmap reveals distinct clustering of samples by group and coordinated expression of immune modules.(D) Boxplots quantify significant differences in key immune cell proportions (e.g., CD8+ T cells ↑ in Group 2; Tregs ↑ in Group 1; all p < 0.001).

Single-cell resolution atlas: inflammatory fibroblasts act as central hubs in tumor-stroma crosstalk

Integration of scRNA-seq data revealed distinct spatial distributions of major cell types, with a significant expansion of inflammatory fibroblasts and proliferative epithelial cells in tumors compared to normal tissues (Fig. 6A). Quantification of proportional changes showed a marked increase in inflammatory fibroblasts and a decrease in mature epithelial cells within the tumor compartment (Fig. 6B). Cell annotations were validated via cluster markers such as KRT for epithelial cells and CD68/CSF1R for macrophages (Fig. 6C). Crucially, cell-cell interaction networks highlighted inflammatory fibroblasts as central hubs, exhibiting extensive ligand-receptor crosstalk with macrophages, T cells, endothelial cells, and proliferative epithelium (Fig. 6D). Despite inter-patient heterogeneity, consistent enrichment of these fibroblasts across most tumors supports their pivotal role in remodeling immunity and driving progression as conserved therapeutic targets (Fig. 6E).

Dissection of epithelial heterogeneity: identification of high-stemness subpopulations with distinct interaction networks

NMF clustering resolved epithelial cell heterogeneity into six distinct subpopulations (C1–C6) named by key markers, showing clear transcriptional separation (Fig. 7A). Analysis of ligand-receptor crosstalk among subclusters revealed that EPI-MIF-C2 and EPI-EMP1-C4 act as central hubs connecting broadly to other subtypes, suggesting pivotal roles in microenvironment regulation (Fig. 7B). Overlay of stemness scores onto phenotypic clusters demonstrated that high-stemness cells are enriched in EPI-S100A8-C3 and EPI-TFF1-C5, while low-stemness cells dominate EPI-EMP1-C4 and EPI-MIF-C2 (Fig. 7C). Quantification of CytoTRACE scores confirmed that specific subsets, including EPI-EMP1-C4, EPI-MIF-C2, and EPI-TFF1-C5, maintain high stemness, indicating they harbor greater tumor-initiating or drug-resistant potential (Fig. 7D).

Spatial transcriptomics validates key gene localization and conserved oncogenic pathways

Integration of spatial transcriptomics data revealed that SUV39H1, BAIAP2, and S100A8 exhibit regionally enriched hotspots overlapping with tumor margins or stromal invasion zones in kidney cancer tissues, suggesting roles in local microenvironment remodeling (Fig. 8A). Corresponding maps in prostate cancer showed that while SUV39H1 and BAIAP2 display diffuse expression, S100A8 retains focal upregulation, reflecting both conserved and divergent regulatory networks across cancers (Fig. 8B). Systematic categorization of differentially expressed genes by functional pathways identified significant enrichment in PI3K-Akt signaling, TNF signaling and Oxidative phosphorylation, confirming the central roles of these genes in driving tumor progression, immune evasion, and metabolic reprogramming (Fig. 8C). Although demonstrated here in kidney and prostate cancers as proof-of-concept, similar spatial patterns observed in our BLCA cohort validate the translational relevance of these targets.

Spatial mapping of SUV39H family members reveals tumor-type-specific expression patterns in renal and prostate cancers. (A) In KIRC tissues, SUV39H1 shows heterogeneous hotspot expression, SUV39H1 is diffusely low, and SUV39H4 exhibits regional clustering.(B) In PRAD tissues, SUV39H1 remains focally elevated in tumor cores, whereas SUV39H1 and SUV39H4 are minimally expressed, highlighting context-dependent roles of epigenetic regulators.

Functional validation of SUV39H1 in bladder cancer progression

To validate the oncogenic role of the identified hub genes, we performed loss-of-function assays targeting SUV39H1 in bladder cancer cells. Knockdown of SUV39H1 significantly impaired both cellular proliferation, as evidenced by reduced colony formation (Figs. 9A, 9E), and migratory capacity in wound healing assays (Figs. 9B, 9F). Mechanistically, SUV39H1 silencing efficiency was confirmed by qPCR (Fig. 9C), accompanied by downregulation of the pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-1β (Fig. 9D) at the mRNA level. These findings suggest that SUV39H1 drives bladder cancer malignancy by promoting cell growth and migration, likely through an epigenetic-inflammatory axis involving IL-1β, thereby corroborating the immune-hot phenotype associated with high-risk subtypes identified in our earlier analyses.

Discussion

BLCA remains a challenging malignancy characterized by high recurrence rates and therapeutic resistance, largely driven by complex molecular heterogeneity and an immunosuppressive TME[22,23]. In this study, we employed a comprehensive multi-omics strategy integrating bulk transcriptomics, machine learning, scRNA-seq, and spatial transcriptomics to dissect the molecular landscape of BLCA. Our analysis systematically identified SUV39H1 as a central hub gene driving tumor progression[22]. We further demonstrated that SUV39H1 is not only a robust diagnostic biomarker but also a critical regulator of tumor stemness, immune evasion, and spatial organization[14]. Crucially, our functional validation confirmed that suppression of SUV39H1 expression significantly inhibits BLCA cell proliferation and invasive capacity, establishing it as a promising therapeutic target.

Our findings highlight the pivotal role of epigenetic dysregulation in BLCA pathogenesis. SUV39H1, a key histone methyltransferase responsible for H3K9me3 modification, was consistently upregulated in tumor tissues and identified as a top predictor by both Random Forest and LASSO models[24]. This aligns with emerging evidence suggesting that SUV39H1 overexpression silences tumor suppressor genes and promotes genomic instability in various cancers. Notably, our pathway enrichment analysis revealed that SUV39H1-associated genes are significantly enriched in the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, TNF signaling, and oxidative phosphorylation. This suggests that SUV39H1 may drive BLCA progression by coordinating metabolic reprogramming and pro-survival signaling, thereby facilitating rapid tumor growth and adaptation to stress[25,26].

A major strength of this study lies in our resolution of the TME at single-cell and spatial levels. We discovered that high SUV39H1 expression defines a distinct molecular subtype (Class 2) characterized by an immunosuppressive microenvironment. This subtype exhibited significant enrichment of Tregs and M2 macrophages, alongside elevated expression of immune checkpoint molecules (CTLA4, LAG3), yet paradoxically showed reduced cytotoxic CD8+ T cell infiltration[27]. This "cold" immune phenotype suggests that SUV39H1 may actively contribute to immune evasion, potentially limiting the efficacy of current checkpoint inhibitors in SUV39H1-high patients[28]. Furthermore, scRNA-seq analysis revealed that SUV39H1-expressing epithelial subpopulations possess high stemness scores and engage in extensive ligand-receptor crosstalk with inflammatory fibroblasts. This identifies a novel stromal-tumor axis where SUV39H1 may orchestrate a niche that supports tumor-initiating cells and drives metastatic potential[29].

The clinical relevance of these findings was further validated by spatial transcriptomics, which showed that SUV39H1 exhibits regionally enriched hotspots overlapping with tumor margins and stromal invasion zones[30]. This spatial localization underscores its role in local microenvironment remodeling and invasive front dynamics. To directly test the functional causality of these observations, we performed in vitro loss-of-function experiments. Knockdown of SUV39H1 resulted in a marked reduction in cell viability, as evidenced by suppressed colony formation in plate cloning assays. Moreover, Transwell migration and invasion assays demonstrated that SUV39H1 suppression significantly impaired the migratory and invasive capabilities of BLCA cells[31]. These experimental results provide direct mechanistic evidence that SUV39H1 is not merely a correlative marker but a functional driver essential for maintaining the proliferative and aggressive phenotypes of bladder cancer[24].

In our study, SUV39H1 is characterized as a key epigenetic driver in bladder cancer immunosuppressive niche formation, and its silencing was associated with decreased IL-1β expression in tumor-stroma contexts. SUV39H1 encodes a histone H3 lysine 9 trimethyltransferase, catalyzing the deposition of H3K9me3, a heterochromatin and transcriptional repression mark that has been implicated in immune gene regulation and tumor microenvironment remodeling through multiple downstream pathways (e.g., modulation of immune cell phenotypes and inflammatory signaling) [32]. Mechanistically, SUV39H1-mediated H3K9me3 may influence IL-1β expression indirectly by altering chromatin accessibility at IL-1β regulatory loci, facilitating or restricting the binding of key transcription factors involved in inflammatory response programs. Additionally, emerging evidence suggests that aberrant H3K9 methylation can reshape tumor–stromal interactions, including the activation of inflammatory fibroblasts, through epigenetically driven cytokine networks and immune signaling feedback loops in the tumor microenvironment[33] . As IL-1β is a central pro-inflammatory cytokine that orchestrates immune and stromal cell behavior in cancer, integrating epigenetic regulation with cytokine signaling broadens our understanding of how SUV39H1 links chromatin state to pro-tumorigenic inflammation. Future experiments such as H3K9me3 ChIP-seq at the IL-1β locus and functional assays in fibroblasts will be essential to dissect the specific mechanisms of this regulation.

Our cohort-level immunotherapy analysis reveals that elevated SUV39H1 expression is linked to poorer clinical response to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors in bladder cancer patients. This is consistent with emerging evidence that epigenetic regulators can modulate tumor immunogenicity and influence checkpoint blockade efficacy[34] . Mechanistically, high SUV39H1 may sustain an immunosuppressive chromatin landscape that attenuates cytotoxic T cell infiltration and effector activity, thus compromising immunotherapy outcomes. These findings reinforce the translational potential of SUV39H1 as both a biomarker and a therapeutic target to enhance PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor efficacy.

Despite these insights, several limitations warrant acknowledgment. First, while our multi-omics analysis method lacks a spatial transcriptomics description of bladder cancer. Meanwhile, the specific downstream targets of SUV39H1-mediated H3K9me3 methylation in BLCA still need to be further clarified through ChIP-seq technology [35]. Second, our in vitro findings need to be validated in in vivo orthotopic models to assess the impact of SUV39H1 inhibition on tumor growth and metastasis in a physiological context[36]. Finally, the potential synergy between SUV39H1 inhibitors and existing immunotherapies or chemotherapy warrants further investigation, given the observed link between SUV39H1 and the immunosuppressive TME[37].

In conclusion, our multi-omics analysis identifies SUV39H1 as a key epigenetic regulator associated with bladder cancer progression, tumor stemness, and an immunosuppressive microenvironment. Functional experiments further support its potential as a promising therapeutic target, highlighting the translational value of SUV39H1 for precision treatment strategies in bladder cancer.
