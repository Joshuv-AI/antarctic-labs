Free product of bi-orderable group is bi-orderable: A simple dynamical proof
Juan Alonso and Cristóbal Rivas

Abstract
. We give a new proof of Vinogradov’s theorem asserting that free product of biorderable groups is biorderable. Our proof relies on a simple dynamical construction that provides a bi-ordering of the free product that extends the bi-orderings on the factors. Furthermore, if the bi-orders of the factors are invariant under some automorphisms, then the bi-ordering on the product turns out to be invariant under the product automorphism.



1.
Introduction

We say that a group is
left-orderable
if it supports a total ordering which is invariant under left multiplication and
bi-orderable
if the ordering is also invariant under right multiplication (see §
2
for precisions). We use the term
orderable
when there is no harm in the ambiguity. In 1949, Vinogradov
[
23
]
used groups of positive units in ordered rings to show that the free product of two bi-ordered groups admits a bi-ordering that extends the bi-orders of the factors. This approach was latter simplified Johnson
[
13
]
in 1972, and even further by Bergman
[
1
]
in 1990 who considered specific rings of matrices over groups-rings (see also
[
7
, §2.1.2]
).
Recently, Muliarchyk
[
15
]
obtained the same result but with a completely different method which considers group actions by homeomorphisms of the real line. Unfortunately, Muliarchyk’s approach dependes heavily on the intricate embedding of a (countable) bi-ordered group into the group of orientation preserving homeomorphisms of the real line build by Dovhyi and Muliarchyk in
[
9
]
, together with a delicate perturbation argument to show that the bi-ordering obtained is in fact an ordering on the free product and not only on a quotient of it.

The aim of this note is to give another proof of Vinogradov’s theorem. Our approach is also dynamical, yet it uses the usual
dynamical realization
of an ordered group as a group of homeomorphisms of the line (see, for instance,
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
10
,
16
]
, or §
2.3
) and (modifications of) ping-pong partitions to ensure faithfulness of the representation. Furthermore, with our construction we get that if the bi-orders of the factors are invariant under some automorphisms, then the bi-ordering on the product turns out to be invariant under the product automorphisms, thus recovering a result from Rolfsen
[
21
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
(A,\preceq_{A})
and
(B,\preceq_{B})
are biordered groups, then
A*B
admits a biorder
\preceq^{*}
that extends
\preceq_{A}
and
\preceq_{B}
. Moreover if
f:A\to A
and
g:B\to B
are automorphisms that preserves the bi-orderings
\preceq_{A}
and
\preceq_{B}
, then
\preceq^{*}
is invariant under the induced automorphism
f*g
.

Remark 1.2
.

As pointed out by Passman
[
18
]
Vinogradov’s approach also shows that the free product of left-orderable groups is left-orderable. This is also the case for our approach as we point out in Corollary
3.5
. In
[
8
]
Dicks and Šunić describe a very nice geometrical method to produce left-orderings on free product of groups out of actions on oriented trees.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
is organized in three steps. We first show in §
3
that the free product
A*B
of two
countable
bi-ordered groups admits a bi-order that extends the bi-orders on the factors. For this we modify a natural ping-pong action of
A*B
on the line to produce an action on the real line by homeomorphisms whose supports are bounded from below, and with the property that each homeomorphism has a well defined sign at the infimum of its support (see Proposition
3.6
). This behaviour is much like that of piecewise analytic homeomorphisms with support bounded from below, compare with
[
9
,
15
]
. Then, in §
3.1
, we exploit the fact that an order preserving automorphisms of a countable ordered group can always be implemented by conjugation of its corresponding dynamical realizations (see Proposition
2.1
) to produce a conjugation of our special action of
A*B
that implements our given automorphism
f*g
from Theorem
1.1
. This allows us to finish the proof in the countable case. Finally, in §
4
, we prove Theorem
4.1
which is a variation of Burns and Hale theorem
[
4
]
that enable us to pass from the countable case of Theorem
1.1
to the general one.

Remark 1.3
.

The construction of our special action of
A*B
on the line is much inspired in
[
7
, §1.2.3]
, where an action of the free group
F_{2}
on the line is constructed aiming to show its bi-orderability. The main difference between both constructions, is that for the case of
F_{2}
one can assume that the action is given by piecewise linear automorphism, and hence the elements of
F_{2}
certainly have a well defined sign around its infimum of support. In our case, we cannot assume that the initial actions of
A
and
B
have any degree of regularity and so we have to elaborate a little bit in order to prove Proposition
3.6
.

2.
Preliminars

Let us fix some basic notations. Throughout this work, if
X\subset\mathbb{R}
we denote by
int(X)
its interior and
cl(X)
its closure. The identity of group will be denoted by
\mathsf{id}
.

2.1.
Bi-orders, partial bi-orders and positive cones

An order relation
\preceq
(not necessarily total) on a group

G
is said to be a
partial bi-ordering
if for every

g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}
in
G
, we have that

g_{1}\prec g_{2}
implies
g_{3}g_{1}\prec g_{3}g_{2}
and
g_{1}g_{3}\prec g_{2}g_{3}
. An element
g\in G
is called

\preceq
-positive if
\mathsf{id}\prec g
, where
\mathsf{id}
denotes the identity of
G
. The subset of
\preceq
-positive
elements, usually called the
positive cone
for
\preceq
, will
be denoted by
P
. Clearly
P
satisfies

(o1)

PP\subseteq P\;
and
gPg^{-1}=P
for every
g\in G
; that
is,
P
is a normal sub semigroup,

(o2)

P\cap P^{-1}=\varnothing
, where

P^{-1}=\{g^{-1}\in G\mid g\in P\}=\{g\in G\mid g\prec id\}
.

If in addition
\preceq
is a total order, we will simply say that

\preceq
is a
bi-ordering
. In this case, the set of

\preceq
-positive elements also satisfies

(o3)

G=P\cup P^{-1}\cup\{\mathsf{id}\}
.

Conversely, given any subset
P\subseteq\Gamma
satisfying the
conditions
(o1)
,
(o2)
and
(o3)
(resp.
(o1)
and
(o2)
)
above, we can define a bi-ordering (resp. a partial bi-ordering)

\preceq
by letting
f\prec g
if and only if
f^{-1}g\in P
.
We will usually identify
\preceq
with
P
.

2.2.
Compactness of the space of partial bi-orders

Given a group
G
(of arbitrary cardinality), we denote the set
of all partial bi-orderings on
G
by

\mathcal{PBO}(G)
. This set has a natural topology first
exploited by Sikora for the case of (total orderings on) countable
groups
[
22
]
. This topology can be defined by identifying

P\in\mathcal{PBO}(G)
with its characteristic function

\chi_{P}\in\{0,1\}^{G}
. In this way, we can view

\mathcal{PBO}(G)
as embedded in
\{0,1\}^{G}
. This latter
space, with the product topology, is a Hausdorff, totally
disconnected, and compact space. It is not hard to see that (the
image of)
\mathcal{PBO}(G)
is closed, and hence
compact as well (see
[
16
,
19
,
7
,
6
]
for details).
In the same way, for a bi-orderable group
G
, the space of
all bi-orderings, here denoted
\mathcal{BO}(G)
, is closed
in
\mathcal{PBO}(G)
, hence compact as well.

2.3.
Left-orderings and dynamical realization

A total ordering
\preceq
on a group
G
is called a left-orderings if
f\preceq g
implies that
hf\preceq hg
for all
f,g,h\in G
. It is a classical fact that a countable group is left orderable if and only if it embeds into
\homeo_{+}(\mathbb{R})
, the group of orientation preserving homeomorphisms of the real line (see
[
7
,
10
,
16
]
).

One direction of the proof consists in building a
dynamical realization
of a countable left-ordered group. To explain this let
(G,\preceq)
be a countable and left-ordered group and consider, first, an order preserving injection
i:(G,\preceq)\to(\mathbb{R},\leq)
, that is well behaved in the sense that

(1)

The image
i(G)
is unbounded in both directions of the line,

(2)

Every connected component
I
of the complement of
cl(i(G))
satisfies that its boundary
\partial I\subset i(G)
.

Concrete examples of well behaved injections
i
can be easily be build algorithmically (see for instance
[
10
,
16
]
), but it requires
G
to be countable (for instance
\omega_{1}
, the first uncountable ordinal, cannot be orderly embedded into
\mathbb{R})
. Other than that any bijection
h:i(G)\to i(G)
that preserves the order
\leq
can be extended to an increasing homeomorphisms of the real line by first extending it to the closure of
i(G)
and then taking linear interpolation on the complements. In fact, it is the whole group of order preserving bijections of
i(G)
that extends to a group of homeomorphisms. This is implicit in
[
10
,
16
,
7
,
6
]
and is explicit in
[
3
, Lemma 2.2.14]
. In particular, since
G
induces an order preserving bijection of
i(G)
via

g.(i(x))=i(g\cdot x),

we obtain an homomorphic embedding
\rho:G\to\homeo_{+}(\mathbb{R})
, that represents
G
as a group of order preserving homeomorphisms of the line. This is what we call a
dynamical realization
of
(G,\preceq)
. We say that a point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
is a
reference point
of the dynamical realization if it satisfies that

id\prec g\Leftrightarrow x_{0}<\rho(g)(x_{0}).

Reference points may not be unique but certainly
x_{0}=i(\mathsf{id})
is a reference point.

Analogously, any order-preserving automorphism
\varphi\in Aut(G,\preceq)
defines an order preserving bijection of
i(G)
via

\varphi(i(x))=i(\varphi(x))
. Hence we obtain

Proposition 2.1
.

For every order-preserving automorfism of a countable left-ordered group
\varphi\in Aut((G,\preceq))
, there is an increasing homeomorphism
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

h\circ\rho(g)\circ h^{-1}(x)=\rho(\varphi(g))(x)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
and every
g\in G
.

Remark 2.2
.

In a dynamical realization of
\rho:G\to\homeo_{+}(\mathbb{R})
, the set of fixed points of a non-trivial
g\in G
has empty interior. Indeed any point in the image of
i(G)
has free orbit under the action of
\rho(G)
, and, although fixed points may appear when passing to the action in its closure
1
1

1

In fact, an important result of Hölder
[
12
]
states that a group acting freely by homeomorphisms of the line must be abelian. See, for instance,
[
7
]
for a modern proof.
, outside this closure the
\rho
-action is also free. To see this note that for
I
a (open) complementary interval of
\mathbb{R}\smallsetminus cl(i(G))
, we have that
\partial I\subseteq i(G)
and hence
I
is a
wandering
interval for
\rho(G)
, meaning that
\rho(g)(I)\cap I=\varnothing
for any
g\neq\mathsf{id}
.

Remark 2.3
.

In the special case where
\preceq
is a bi-ordering of
G
, then
\mathsf{id}\prec g
implies that
x\prec g\cdot x
for every
x\in G
, and hence every
\preceq
-positive element
g\in G
satisfies that

x\leq\rho(g)(x)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
. See for instance
[
16
]
. We will use this last fact in the construction of our bi-order of
A*B
.

2.4.
Free product and ping pong configurations

A ping pong configuration is a simple dynamical mechanism that produces a faithful action of a free product of groups. Early applications of this mechanism can be traced back to Klein and Tits. See
[
11
, §II]
and the references therein. For our purpose we use the following formalization.

Let
\phi:G\to Sym(X)
be an action of a group
G
by bijections of a set
X
, and let
A
and
B
be subgroups of
G
. We say that a
ping pong configuration
for the action of
\phi(A)
and
\phi(B)
is a collection of disjoint subsets
X_{A}
and
X_{B}
of
X
such that
X\smallsetminus(X_{A}\cup X_{B})\neq\varnothing
and

\phi(a)(X\smallsetminus X_{A})\subseteq X_{A}\text{ and }\phi(b)(X\smallsetminus X_{B})\subseteq X_{B}

for every
a\in A\smallsetminus\{\mathsf{id}\}
and every
b\in B\smallsetminus\{\mathsf{id}\}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

With the notations above, if
X_{A}
and
X_{B}
is a ping pong configuration for the action of
\phi(A)
and
\phi(B)
, then
\langle\phi(A),\phi(B)\rangle
, the group generated by
\phi(A)
and
\phi(B)
, is isomorphic to the free product
A*B
. Moreover, any point in
X\smallsetminus(X_{A}\cup X_{B})\neq\varnothing
has free orbit under
\langle\phi(A),\phi(B)\rangle
.

Proof.

Clearly the group
\langle\phi(A),\phi(B)\rangle
is an homomorphic image of the free product
A*B
. Furthermore, the image of any point
x\in X\smallsetminus(X_{A}\cup X_{B})
under
\phi(w)
belongs to
X_{A}\cup X_{B}
for any non-trivial
w\in A*B
. Hence, the natural homomorphisms
\phi:A*B\to\langle\phi(A),\phi(B)\rangle
is an isomorphism, and the points in
X\smallsetminus(X_{A}\cup X_{B})
have free orbit.
\hfill\Box

3.
Proof in the countable case

In this section we show the countable version of our main result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(A,\preceq_{A})
and
(B,\preceq_{B})
be countable biordered groups. Then

i)

The free product
A*B
admits a biorder
\preceq^{*}
that extends
\preceq_{A}
and
\preceq_{B}
.

ii)

If
f:A\to A
and
g:B\to B
are automorphisms that preserves the bi-orderings
\preceq_{A}
and
\preceq_{B}
, then
\preceq^{*}
is invariant under the induced automorphism
f*g
.

Our proof consists in building a special action of the free product
A*B
on the real line. Its construction requires three steps.

Let
\preceq_{A}
and
\preceq_{B}
be biorders of
A
and
B
respectively.

Step 1:

Preparing the factors: a blow-up on the dynamical realizations
.

For convenience we will consider dynamical realizations of
(A,\preceq_{A})
and
(B,\preceq_{B})
to be inside
\homeo_{+}([0,1])
instead of
\homeo_{+}(\mathbb{R})
. This can be done since the interval
(0,1)
is orderly-homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
and every increasing homeomorphism of
\mathbb{R}
fixes
+\infty
and
-\infty
.

So let
\rho_{A}:A\to Homeo_{+}([0,1])
be a dynamical realization of
(A,\preceq)
and assume that
x_{A}=1/2
is a reference point. We now make a
blow-up
of the point
x_{A}
to create a
new
interval on which
A
still acts. For this we first replace each point
o
in the
A
-orbit of
x_{A}
by a bounded and closed interval
I_{o}
. Since the orbit is countable we can choose the length of the intervals
I_{o}
to ensure that their reunion has finite Lebesgue measure, so that we end up with a
new
compact interval
J
. Denote
J^{\prime}
the set of points of
J
that are complementary to the reunion of
\{I_{o}\}
. Note that on
J^{\prime}
the group
A
acts via
\rho_{A}
and this action preserves the order of
J^{\prime}
coming from the line. In particular (following §
2.3
) this
A
action on
J^{\prime}
can be extended to an action by homeomorphisms of the whole interval
J
.

We call
\rho_{A}^{blow}:A\to\homeo_{+}(J)
the resulting representation of
A
. After conjugation we can (and will) assume that
\rho_{A}^{blow}
takes
A
into
Homeo_{+}([0,1])
and that the interval
I_{x_{A}}=[1/5,4/5]
. Remark that for

\rho_{A}^{blow}:A\to\homeo_{+}([0,1])

the interior of the interval
I_{x_{A}}
is
wandering
, meaning that
int(I_{x_{A}})
is moved disjointly by any non-trivial element
a\in A
. Furthermore, any point
x\in int(I_{x_{A}})
is a reference point for
\preceq
in the sense that
\mathsf{id}\prec a
if and only if
x<\rho_{A}^{blow}(a)(x)
.

For
(B,\preceq_{B})
we do the analogous construction to end up with
\rho_{B}^{blow}:B\to Homeo_{+}([0,1])
and
int(I_{x_{B}})=(1/5,4/5)
a wandering interval made of reference points of
\preceq_{B}
.

Remark 3.2
.

In the representation
\rho_{A}^{blow}:A\to\homeo_{+}([0,1])
, the set of fixed points of a non-trivial
a\in A
has empty interior. Indeed, from Remark
2.2
we know this to be true for the original dynamical realization
\rho_{A}
, and the procedure of blowing up only introduces wandering intervals, namely the intervals in the orbit of
int(I_{x_{A}})
. The same holds for the representation
\rho_{B}^{blow}:B\to\homeo_{+}([0,1])
.

Remark 3.3
.

Proposition
2.1
also holds when the action is a blow-up of a dynamical realization. In fact we can define
h_{A}\in\homeo_{+}([0,1])
so that

h_{A}\circ\rho_{A}^{blow}(a)\circ h_{A}^{-1}(x)=\rho_{A}^{blow}(\varphi(a))(x)

and
h_{A}
maps
\rho_{A}^{blow}(a)I_{x_{A}}\to\rho_{A}^{blow}(\varphi(a))I_{x_{A}}
by an affine map for every
a\in A
. In particular
h_{A}
is the identity on
I_{x_{A}}
. The same can be done for
B
.

Step 2:

Building a ping-pong.

For
r
a real number let
T_{r}:x\mapsto x+r
be the traslation by
r
. We take
\widetilde{\rho}_{A}:A\to Homeo_{+}(\mathbb{R})
so that the restriction of
\widetilde{\rho}_{A}
to
[0,1]
equals
\rho_{A}^{blow}
and, for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the restriction of
\widetilde{\rho}_{A}
to
[n,n+1]
equals
T_{n}\circ\rho_{A}^{blow}\circ T_{n}^{-1}
. In other words
\widetilde{\rho}_{A}
is the periodic repetition of
\rho_{A}^{blow}
. For
\widetilde{\rho}_{B}
we do similarly, namely
\widetilde{\rho}_{B}:B\to Homeo_{+}(\mathbb{R})
is build such that on the interval
[n,n+1]+1/2
the representation
\widetilde{\rho}_{B}
equals
T_{n+1/2}\circ\rho_{B}^{blow}\circ T_{n+1/2}^{-1}
. We call

\widetilde{\rho}:A*B\to Homeo_{+}(\mathbb{R})

the induced action of
A*B
from
\widetilde{\rho}_{A}
and
\widetilde{\rho}_{B}
. In Proposition
3.4
below, we show that
\widetilde{\rho}
is a faithful representation by exhibiting a
ping pong
configuration for the action. For its proof (and statement) we introduce some notations. These notations will be also needed in the proof of Lemma
3.12
.

For
j\in\mathbb{Z}
let
I_{A}^{j}=(1/5,4/5)+j
and
I_{B}^{j}=(1/5,4/5)+j+1/2
. These are wandering intervals under
\widetilde{\rho}(A)
and
\widetilde{\rho}(B)
respectively. Put

\mathcal{I}_{A}=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{A}^{j}\;,\quad\mathcal{I}_{B}=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{B}^{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{A}\cap\mathcal{I}_{B}.

Remark that
\mathcal{I}\neq\varnothing
. Also let
C_{A}^{j}=[-1/5,1/5]+j
, which is the connected component of
\mathbb{R}\smallsetminus\mathcal{I}_{A}
lying between
I_{A}^{j-1}
and
I_{A}^{j}
, and
C_{B}^{j}=[-1/5,1/5]+j+1/2
. Define

\mathcal{C}_{A}=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}C_{A}^{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{C}_{B}=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}C_{B}^{j}\,.

By construction we get that
\mathcal{C}_{A}=\mathbb{R}\smallsetminus\mathcal{I}_{A}
and
\mathcal{C}_{B}=\mathbb{R}\smallsetminus\mathcal{I}_{B}
. More precisely
C_{A}^{j}\subset I_{B}^{j-1}
and
C_{B}^{j}\subset I_{A}^{j}
for each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
A^{+}
and
A^{-}
be the positive and negative cones for the order
\preceq_{A}
and define
B^{+}
and
B^{-}
analogously. By construction of the action

(3.1)

X^{j}_{A}:=\bigcup_{a\in A^{+}}\widetilde{\rho}(a)I_{A}^{j-1}\cup\bigcup_{a\in A^{-}}\widetilde{\rho}(a)I_{A}^{j}\subset C_{A}^{j}

where the left side of the union lies in the open left half of
C^{j}_{A}
, i.e.
(j-1/5,j)
, and vice-versa. Define

(3.2)

U^{j}_{A}:=C^{j}_{A}\smallsetminus cl(X^{j}_{A})\;,\quad\widehat{X}_{A}^{j}=cl(X_{A}^{j})\smallsetminus X_{A}^{j}\;,\quad X_{A}=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}X_{A}^{j}\;,\quad\mathcal{U}_{A}=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}U_{A}^{j}

and notice that
\mathcal{U}_{A}
is a union of intervals that are wandering under
\widetilde{\rho}(A)
, which are translates of the intervals mentioned in Remark
3.2
. Of course, the same definitions and remarks are made for
B
.

Proposition 3.4
.

The action
\widetilde{\rho}:A*B\to Homeo_{+}(\mathbb{R})
is faithful and any point inside
\mathcal{I}
has free orbit under
A*B
. Moreover the set of fixed points of any non-trivial
w\in A*B
has empty interior, and if
\widetilde{\rho}(w)(1/2)=1/2
, then
w\in B
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{I}_{A}=\mathbb{R}\smallsetminus\mathcal{C}_{A}
(respectively
\mathcal{I}_{B}=\mathbb{R}\smallsetminus\mathcal{C}_{B}
) is a reunion of wandering intervals separated by global fixed points of
\widetilde{\rho}(A)
(respectively
\widetilde{\rho}(B)
), we get that

\widetilde{\rho}(a)(\mathcal{\mathbb{R}}\smallsetminus\mathcal{C}_{A})\subset\mathcal{C}_{A}\quad\text{ and }\quad\widetilde{\rho}(b)(\mathcal{\mathbb{R}}\smallsetminus\mathcal{C}_{B})\subset\mathcal{C}_{B}

for every
a\in A\smallsetminus\{\mathsf{id}\}
and every
b\in B\smallsetminus\{\mathsf{id}\}
. Since the sets
\mathcal{C}_{A}
and
\mathcal{C}_{B}
are disjoint and
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{A}\cap\mathcal{I}_{B}=\mathbb{R}\smallsetminus(\mathcal{C}_{A}\cup\mathcal{C}_{B})
is non empty, we get that
\mathcal{C}_{A}
,
\mathcal{C}_{B}
is a poing pong configuration of
\widetilde{\rho}(A)
and
\widetilde{\rho}(B)
. By Lemma
2.4
, we obtain that
\widetilde{\rho}:A*B\to\homeo_{+}(\mathbb{R})
is faithful and every point in
\mathcal{I}
has a free orbit under
\widetilde{\rho}(A*B)
.

We now show that the stabilizer of
1/2
is the subgroup
B
. Certainly we have that
\widetilde{\rho}(b)(1/2)=1/2
for every
b\in B
. Conversely, let
w=a_{0}b_{0}\cdots a_{n}b_{n}\in A\ast B
be a word where all letters are non trivial except -perthaps- the first and last one. If
n>0
we see that
\widetilde{\rho}(a_{n}b_{n})(1/2)\in\mathcal{C}_{A}\subset\mathcal{I}_{B}
. On the other hand

\widetilde{\rho}(a_{j}b_{j})\mathcal{I}_{B}\subset\widetilde{\rho}(a_{j})\mathcal{C}_{B}\subset\widetilde{\rho}(a_{j})\mathcal{I}_{A}\subset\mathcal{C}_{A}\subset\mathcal{I}_{B}

for every
j=1,\ldots,n-1

To show density, observe that we have a disjoint union

(3.3)

I_{B}^{j+1}=(\mathcal{I}\cap I_{B}^{j+1})\cup C_{A}^{j}=(\mathcal{I}\cap I_{B}^{j+1})\cup U^{j}_{A}\cup\left(\bigcup_{a\in A^{+}}\widetilde{\rho}(a)I_{A}^{j-1}\cup\bigcup_{a\in A^{-}}\widetilde{\rho}(a)I_{A}^{j}\right)\cup\widehat{X}^{j}_{A}

Proof.

For

To obtain a the similar statement for the biorderable case we need to perform one last modification to

Step 3:

Cutting the ping-pong.

Proof of Theorem

item

Define

3.1.

and
\preceq_{B}

For this we first show in Lemma

Lemma 3.12

There is an increasing homeomorphism

Proof.

By Remark
3.3

, so

4.
The uncountable case

It is well known that (left or bi) orderability is a local property. This principle is manifested in different theorems relating the orderability of an ambient group with the orderability of its finitely generated subgroup or even its finitely generated sub-semigroups. See the result of Burns and Hale
[
4
]
for the former case and the results of Loś
[
14
]
and Onishi
[
17
]
for the latter. Burns-Hale theorem from
[
4
]
has been generalize to several of other settings related to orderability, see the work of Clay
[
5
]
for an account of some of them, and the technique from Loś and Onishi led the second author to obtain a local criterion for
not allowing an isolated ordering

[
19
]
. In
[
16
]
, Navas interpreted these classical results as a consecuence of the compactness of the space of orderings.

In this section we provide another variation of this general principle by showing that a group supports an ordering invariant under a given automorphisms if and only if all its countable subgroups supports an ordering invariant under the automorphism.

To be more precise, given a group
G
, a subgroup
H\leq G
and an automorphisms
\varphi:G\to G
, we denote by
H^{\varphi}=\langle\varphi^{n}(H)\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\rangle
the group generated by
H
and all its images under
\varphi
. Note that
H^{\varphi}
is countable whenever
H
is countable. Clearly, every
\varphi
-invariant bi-order of
G
induces a
\varphi
-invariant bi-order on every subgroup
H^{\varphi}
of
G
. For the converse we have

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
be a group, and
\varphi:G\to G
be an automorphism.
Assume that for every countable subgroup
H\leq G
, there is a bi-order of
H^{\varphi}
that is invariant under
\varphi
. Then
G
supports a bi-order that is inveriant under
\varphi
.

Proof.

This follows by compactness of
\mathcal{PBO}(G)
, the set of partial bi-orderings on
G
(see §
2.2
). Indeed, for every countable
H\leq G
, consider
\chi(H^{\varphi})
to be the set of all partial bi-orderings on
G
whose restriction to
H^{\varphi}
is a total and
\varphi
-invariant bi-ordering. The set
\chi(H^{\varphi})
is a closed subset of
\mathcal{PBO}(G)
which is non-empty by hypothesis. In addition, a finite intersection

\chi(H_{1}^{\varphi})\cap\ldots\cap\chi(H_{n}^{\varphi})
of these subsets is non-empty since it contains
\chi(\langle H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}\rangle^{\varphi})
, where
\langle H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}\rangle
is the (countable) group generated by
H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}
. By the finite intersection property of compact sets, we get that
\chi:=\bigcap_{H}\chi(H^{\varphi})
, where
H
run over all countable subgroups of
G
, is non-empty. Happily, any element in
\chi
is a total bi-order of
G
which, moreover, is
\varphi
-invariant.
\hfill\Box

With Theorem
4.1
at hand, we can finish the proof of our main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:
Let
G=A*B
be the free product of two bi-orderable group
A
and
B
, and let
H\leq A*B
be a countable subgroup. Clearly
H
is contained in the free product of two countable subgroup
A_{count}*B_{count}
: it is enough to take
A_{count}
to be the group generated by all the
A
-letters in the words
w\in H
, and
B_{count}
the analog group but for
B
-letters. Furthermore, if
\varphi=f*g:G\to G
is the product of the automorphisms
f:A\to A
and
g:B\to B
, then

H^{f*g}\leq(A_{count}*B_{count})^{f*g}=A_{count}^{f}*B_{count}^{g}.

Now if
\preceq_{A}
and
\preceq_{B}
are bi-orderings of
A
and
B
that are invariant under
f
and
g
respectively, then Theorem
3.1
implies that
A^{f}_{count}*B^{g}_{count}
admits a bi-orderings that is invariant under
f*g
. Hence the same holds fof
H^{f*g}
. Thus, applying Theorem
4.1
we obtain that
A*B
supports a bi-ordering that is invariant under
f*g
.
\hfill\square
