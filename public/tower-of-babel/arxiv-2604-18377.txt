1.2
Compactified universal Jacobians

A classical problem in algebraic geometry is to extend the family

\pi:\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{d}\to\mathcal{M}_{g,n}

over the moduli space of
stable curves
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

while preserving as many properties (flatness, properness, modular interpretation) as possible. Many such extensions have been found, see
[
14
,
21
,
40
,
48
,
55
,
22
,
41
,
42
,
36
,
47
,
58
,
25
,
24
]
. In our main results, we will discuss the family of
smoothable fine compactified Jacobians
defined by Pagani–Tommasi
[
47
]
. These compactified Jacobians are highly non-unique, for fixed
(g,n,d)
, and depend on the choice of a
stability condition

\sigma
. We write

\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{g,n}^{d}(\sigma)\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\,,

for the compactified Jacobian associated to the stability condition
\sigma
; it is a proper nonsingular Deligne–Mumford stack.
Note that e.g. by
[
36
, Corollary 6.19]
, even for
g=1
, there are examples
\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{1,n}^{d}(\sigma)
,
\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{1,n}^{d}(\sigma^{\prime})
of compactified Jacobians for different stability conditions
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
of equal degree which are not isomorphic as Deligne–Mumford stacks.

Stability conditions
\sigma
as above do not always exist. When
n=0
, a fine compactified Jacobian extending
\mathcal{J}_{g}^{d}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
exists if and only if
\gcd(g-1+d,2g-2)=1
. When
n>0
, fine compactified Jacobians exist for all
g
and
d
, but it is not always the case that an
S_{n}
-equivariant stability condition exists
[
24
, Remark 7.6]
.

In light of the discussion in
Section

1.1
, it is natural to ask to what extent the cohomology of
\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{g,n}^{d}(\sigma)
depends on
d
and on
\sigma
. The surprising answer is:
not at all!

Theorem 1.2
.

For all
2g-2+n>0
, all
d,d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and all choices of Pagani–Tommasi stability conditions
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
, there exists an isomorphism

H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{g,n}^{d}(\sigma);\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{g,n}^{d^{\prime}}(\sigma^{\prime});\mathbb{Q})

of
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structures. If the stability conditions
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
are both invariant for a subgroup
S_{\lambda}=S_{n_{1}}\times\dots\times S_{n_{p}}
, with
n_{1}+\dots+n_{p}=n
, then the isomorphism may be chosen to be equivariant with respect to the natural actions of the group
S_{\lambda}
.

Theorem

1.2
is an immediate consequence of a much more general theorem of Migliorini–Shende–Viviani
[
44
]
— which concerns the supports in the Decomposition Theorem for a family of compactified Jacobians associated to a family of “
H
-smooth” nodal curves — together with the arguments for
Proposition

1.1
. See
[
4
, Section 2]
for more details.

From the point of view of the boundary combinatorics of these moduli spaces,
Theorem

1.2
is rather surprising. The space
\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{g,n}^{d}(\sigma)
admits a stratification, in which each stratum is a torus bundle over a product of smaller moduli spaces
\mathcal{J}_{g_{i},n_{i}}^{d_{i}}
, modulo a finite group. One could imagine calculating (say) the Hodge–Deligne polynomial for the space
\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{g,n}^{d}(\sigma)
by adding up the Hodge–Deligne polynomials for all of the individual strata.
Proposition

1.1
implies that the Hodge–Deligne polynomial of every such stratum is independent of the tuple of degrees
(d_{i})
. But this does not immediately prove
Theorem

1.2
, because the sets of strata for different stability conditions
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
are different!
Theorem

1.2
thus predicts that there should be some kind of bijection between strata that makes the end result independent of the stability condition.

A Pagani–Tommasi stability condition is essentially a purely combinatorial object. It is natural to ask for a direct combinatorial proof of
Theorem

1.2
, by explicitly adding up contributions from all strata and arguing that the terms in these sums for different stability conditions
\sigma
,
\sigma^{\prime}
, can be paired in such a way that the end result is manifestly independent of the stability condition. The main goal of this paper is to give such a proof.

\sigma

At the moment, full knowledge of

2
Cohomology of universal Jacobians

2.1
Rigidity

We will use the following Rigidity Theorem for variations of Hodge structure proven by Schmid
[
53
, (7.24)]
, extending previous results of Grothendieck, Griffiths, and Deligne. All schemes and stacks here are separated and of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
.

Theorem 2.1

(Rigidity Theorem)
.

Let
B
be a smooth connected algebraic space,
x\in B
a point. Suppose
V
and
W
are polarizable variations of Hodge structure over
B
. Suppose given an isomorphism
\phi:V_{x}\to W_{x}
of Hodge structures with an action of
\pi_{1}(B,x)
. Then there is a unique isomorphism
V\to W
of variations of Hodge structure extending
\phi
.

We will need an extension of the Rigidity Theorem to stacks. The following will be sufficient for our purposes.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that
B
is a smooth connected Artin stack, such that there exists an atlas
U\to B
with connected fibers. Then the conclusion of the Rigidity Theorem holds for
B
. Explicitly, suppose
V
and
W
are polarizable variations of Hodge structure over
B
, and let
x
be a point of
B
. Suppose given an isomorphism
\phi:V_{x}\to W_{x}
of Hodge structures with an action of
\pi_{1}(B,x)
. Then there is a unique isomorphism
V\to W
of variations of Hodge structure extending
\phi
.

Proof.

Let
U^{k}
be the
k
-fold fibered product
U\times_{B}U\times_{B}\dots\times_{B}U
. Let
y\in U
be a preimage of
x
. From the choice of
y
we get a compatible system of basepoints on all powers
U^{k}
. Let
f_{k}:U^{k}\to B
be the projection. The fiber at the basepoint of
f^{\ast}_{k}V
is canonically identified with
V_{x}
, for all
k
, and similarly for
W
. Since each
U^{k}
is smooth and connected, the Rigidity Theorem furnishes a compatible system of isomorphisms
f^{\ast}_{k}V\to f^{\ast}_{k}W
for all
k\geq 1
. These isomorphisms descend uniquely to an isomorphism
V\to W
.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a morphism of Artin stacks, with
Y
satisfying the conditions of
Proposition

2.2
. Assume that
\pi_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}(Y)
is surjective. If
V
and
W
are variations of Hodge structure on
Y
such that
f^{\ast}(V)
is isomorphic to
f^{\ast}(W)
, then
V
and
W
are isomorphic.

Proof.

By
Proposition

2.2
, it suffices to find a point
y\in Y
such that there exists an isomorphism
V_{y}\to W_{y}
of Hodge structures equipped with an action of
\pi_{1}(Y)
. Now if
x\in X
then
f^{\ast}(V)_{x}\cong V_{f(x)}
and the action of
\pi_{1}(X)
is through the surjection to
\pi_{1}(Y)
, so the result follows.
∎

2.2
Universal Jacobians

Let
\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{d}
be the degree
d
universal Jacobian over
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
, and let

\pi^{d}:\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{d}\to\mathcal{M}_{g,n}

be the projection (with the degree
d
now explicitly labelled).

Proposition 2.4
.

For any integers
d,d^{\prime}
, and any
g
,
n
and
q
satisfying
2g-2+n>0
, there is an isomorphism

R^{q}\pi^{d}_{\ast}\mathbb{Z}\cong R^{q}\pi_{\ast}^{d^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}

of
S_{n}
-equivariant polarizable variations of Hodge structures on
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
.

Proof.

The result is trivial when
g=0
, since in this case all maps
\pi^{d}
are isomorphisms, and when
n=1
, since
\mathcal{J}_{g,1}^{d}\cong\mathcal{J}_{g,1}^{d^{\prime}}
for any two
d,d^{\prime}
. The result for
n>1
follows from the result for
n=0
, since both sheaves
\smash{R^{q}\pi^{d}_{\ast}\mathbb{Z}}
and
\smash{R^{q}\pi_{\ast}^{d^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}}
are pulled back from
\mathcal{M}_{g}
, and
S_{n}
acts fiberwise. (Indeed, the family
\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{d}\to\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
is pulled back from
\mathcal{M}_{g}
for all
d
and
n
.) But the result for
n=0
follows from the result for
n=1
, and Corollary
2.3
. Indeed:
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1})\to\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g})
is surjective, since a fiber bundle with connected fibers induces a surjection on fundamental groups, using the long exact sequence in homotopy associated to a fibration. Moreover,
\mathcal{M}_{g}
admits an atlas with connected fibers: if
g>0
, we may take the atlas to be the space
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{1}
parametrizing curves with a marked point and a nonzero tangent vector at that point; a curve of positive genus cannot have an automorphism fixing a point and a nonzero tangent vector in characteristic zero.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

For any integers
d,d^{\prime}
there are isomorphisms

H^{\ast}(\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{d},\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{\ast}(\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{d^{\prime}},\mathbb{Q})\quad\text{and}\quad H^{\ast}_{c}(\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{d},\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{\ast}_{c}(\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{d^{\prime}},\mathbb{Q})\,,

both compatible with mixed Hodge structure, the action of
S_{n}
, and the cup-product.

Proof.

Suppose that
p:X\to Y
is a smooth proper family. There is an isomorphism

Rp_{\ast}\mathbb{Q}\cong\bigoplus_{q}R^{q}p_{\ast}\mathbb{Q}[-q]\,.

(3)

The fact that there is such an isomorphism in the derived category of constructible sheaves is Deligne’s degeneration theorem
[
17
]
. Saito
[
52
]
promoted this result to an isomorphism in his derived category of mixed Hodge modules. It follows that there are isomorphisms of mixed Hodge structures

H^{k}(X)\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=k}H^{p}(Y,R^{q}p_{\ast}\mathbb{Q})\quad\text{and}\quad H^{k}_{c}(X)\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=k}H^{p}_{c}(Y,R^{q}p_{\ast}\mathbb{Q})\,.

(4)

(When
Y
is smooth, these isomorphisms are interchanged under Poincaré duality.) Now suppose
p
is an abelian scheme. Then the isomorphism (
3
) can be chosen to be compatible with cup-product, so that both isomorphisms (
4
) are isomorphisms of rings. (This is a very rare property.) In fact, for an abelian scheme, the natural map

\bigwedge R^{1}p_{\ast}\mathbb{Q}[-1]\longrightarrow Rp_{\ast}\mathbb{Q}

(5)

induced by cup-product is an isomorphism; indeed, its mapping cone is acyclic. The same holds for torsors under abelian schemes, by the same argument.

After the above considerations, it suffices to prove that for the various projections
p=\pi^{d}:\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{d}\to\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
we have isomorphisms
\smash{R^{1}\pi^{d}_{\ast}\mathbb{Q}\cong R^{1}\pi_{\ast}^{d^{\prime}}\mathbb{Q}}
. But this was proven in
Proposition

2.4
.∎

Remark 2.7

The construction of the morphism (

R^{1}p_{*}\mathbb{Q}[-1]
. Since
Rp_{\ast}\mathbb{Q}
is a commutative algebra object, the universal property of
\bigwedge
says that the linear map
R^{1}p_{*}\mathbb{Q}[-1]\to Rp_{*}\mathbb{Q}
in the derived category is adjoint to a ring homomorphism
\bigwedge R^{1}p_{*}\mathbb{Q}[-1]\to Rp_{*}\mathbb{Q}
, and this is how (
5

Rp_{*}\mathbb{Q}
as being a commutative algebra object, or of
\bigwedge
having a universal property, we are obliged to treat the derived category as a stable
\infty

\infty
-category compatibly with six operations was recently constructed by Tubach
[
57

3
Boundary stratifications

3.1
Hodge Euler characteristics

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a finite type Deligne–Mumford stack over
\mathbb{C}
. We define the
Hodge Euler characteristic
to be the element

\chi_{c}(X):=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}[H^{i}_{c}(X,\mathbb{Q})]

in the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathsf{MHS})
of
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structures (always assumed to be graded-polarizable).

\lozenge

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
X
and
Y
are both smooth and proper. Then
\chi_{c}(X)=\chi_{c}(Y)
if and only if
H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{i}(Y,\mathbb{Q})
as Hodge structures for all
i
.

Proof.

By purity of weights, the class of
H^{i}(X;\mathbb{Q})
in
K_{0}(\mathsf{MHS})
can be recovered from
\chi_{c}(X)
by taking all terms of weight
i
. But this determines the Hodge structure
H^{i}(X;\mathbb{Q})
completely, since the category of pure polarizable Hodge structures is semisimple.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X=\bigcup X_{\alpha\in A}
be a stratification into pairwise disjoint locally closed substacks. Then
\chi_{c}(X)=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\chi_{c}(X_{\alpha})
.

Proof.

The proof reduces by induction to the case
|A|=2
, in which case one uses the long exact sequence

\dots\to H^{i}_{c}(U,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{i}_{c}(X,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{i}_{c}(X\setminus U,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{i+1}_{c}(U,\mathbb{Q})\to\dots

associated to an open immersion
U\subset X
.
∎

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on
X
. Then
\chi_{c}(X)
admits an equivariant refinement
\chi_{c}^{G}(X)
, valued in
K_{0}(\mathsf{MHS}^{G})
, where
\mathsf{MHS}^{G}
denotes the category of
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structures with an action of
G
. The evident analogue of
Lemma

3.2
is true in the equivariant setting, with the same argument. Moreover, the same argument as
Lemma

3.3
implies that
\chi_{c}^{G}(-)
is additive over
G
-invariant stratifications. The following lemma gives a generalization in the case where
G
is allowed to permute the strata.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X=\bigcup_{\alpha\in A}X_{\alpha}
be a stratification into pairwise disjoint locally closed substacks. Suppose given a finite group
G
acting on
X
, in such a way that for all
g\in G
and
\alpha\in A
,
gX_{\alpha}=X_{\beta}
for some
\beta\in A
. Then

\chi_{c}^{G}(X)=\sum_{\alpha\in A/G}\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathrm{Stab}(\alpha)}^{G}\chi_{c}^{\mathrm{Stab}(\alpha)}(X_{\alpha}),

where the sum runs over any set of orbit representatives of
G
acting on
A
.

Proof.

Since
\chi_{c}^{G}(-)
is additive over
G
-invariant stratifications, we may treat one
G
-orbit of strata at a time, and assume that
G
acts transitively on

. Then necessarily
X=\coprod_{\alpha\in A}X_{\alpha}
is actually a disjoint union, and

for any choice of

.
∎

The above discussion provides a first reduction of

1.2
. Firstly, it suffices by

to prove that the Hodge Euler characteristic

is independent of

and
\sigma
. Secondly, by

we may compute
\chi_{c}^{S_{\lambda}}(\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{g,n}^{d}(\sigma))
by adding up contributions from individual strata. In analyzing the individual strata, we use that

(6)

for any

and

3.2
Stratification of the universal stack

Recall that a prestable curve is said to be
quasi-stable
if all its unstable components are disjoint, and that a line bundle on a quasi-stable curve is
admissible
if its degree on each unstable component is
1
. If
C
is a quasi-stable curve and
\mathcal{L}
is an admissible line bundle on
C
, then
|\mathrm{Aut}(C,\mathcal{L})/\mathbb{G}_{m}|<\infty
if and only if the union of all stable components of
C
is connected, and we shall assume this in what follows. Let
\mathfrak{J}_{g,n}
be the highly nonseparated Deligne–Mumford stack parametrizing such pairs
(C,\mathcal{L})
. Stabilization defines a natural morphism
\mathfrak{J}_{g,n}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.

In this section we will describe a stratification of
\mathfrak{J}_{g,n}
, analogous to the stratification of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
by topological type. In these terms, a stability condition
\sigma
will then simply be the data of a finite collection of strata in
\mathfrak{J}_{g,n}
, satisfying a short list of combinatorial conditions which ensure that this union of strata defines an open substack

\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{g,n}^{d}(\sigma)\subset\mathfrak{J}_{g,n}

which is proper over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. The stratification of
\mathfrak{J}_{g,n}
is indexed by triples

(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d})

(7)

where
\Gamma
is a stable graph of type
(g,n)
,
\Gamma_{0}\subseteq\Gamma
is a connected subgraph containing all vertices, and
\underline{d}:V(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Z}
is a function. Geometrically, this stratum parametrizes quasi-stable curves whose dual graph is obtained from
\Gamma
by subdividing each edge
not
in
\Gamma_{0}
once, equipped with an admissible line bundle whose degree on each stable component is specified by the function
\underline{d}
. It will be convenient in what follows to write
e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}}=|E(\Gamma)\setminus E(\Gamma_{0})|
for the number of unstable components of the quasi-stable curve corresponding to
(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0})
.

Let us recall something about the structure of the Jacobian of a nodal curve. Let
f:C\to S
be a flat proper family of nodal curves, and assume that the dual graph
\gamma
is constant in the family. Let
\nu:C^{\nu}\to C
be its fiberwise normalization, and let
i:C^{\mathrm{sing}}\to C
be the fiberwise singular locus. The exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\times}\to\nu_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C^{\nu}}^{\times}\to i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C^{\mathrm{sing}}}^{\times}\to 0
gives an exact sequence on
S

0\to f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\times}\to(f\circ\nu)_{*}\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{C^{\nu}}\to(f\circ i)_{*}\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{C^{\mathrm{sing}}}\to R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\times}\to R^{1}(f\circ\nu)_{*}\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{C^{\nu}}\to 0,

or more explicitly

0\to\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}\to\bigoplus_{v\in V(\gamma)}\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}\to\bigoplus_{e\in E(\gamma)}\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}\to R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\times}\to R^{1}(f\circ\nu)_{*}\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{C^{\nu}}\to 0.

(8)

On the level of the group schemes representing these étale sheaves, this says that
\mathrm{Pic}_{C/S}
is fibered over
\mathrm{Pic}_{C^{\nu}/S}
, with kernel the algebraic torus
\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1}(\gamma,\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{G}_{m})
, which represents the cokernel of the map
\bigoplus_{v\in V(\gamma)}\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}\to\bigoplus_{e\in E(\gamma)}\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}
.

We are now ready to describe the stratification of
\mathfrak{J}_{g,n}
by topological type. Let
(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d})
be a triple as in (
7
). Let
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d})
denote the subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
of graph automorphisms which send the subgraph
\Gamma_{0}
to itself, and which preserve the function
\underline{d}:V(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Z}
. We then have the following, which is the analogue of the familiar stratification

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}=\bigcup_{[\Gamma]}[\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}/\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)]

of the Deligne–Mumford compactification.

Proposition 3.5
.

For each
(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d})
as in (
7
), there is a torus bundle

\mathcal{J}_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d}}\longrightarrow\prod_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\mathcal{J}_{g(v),n(v)}^{\underline{d}(v)}

which is an
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d})
-equivariant torsor for the torus
T_{\Gamma_{0}}:=\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1}(\Gamma_{0}),\mathbb{G}_{m})\cong\mathbb{G}_{m}^{b_{1}(\Gamma_{0})}
. The stratum in
\mathfrak{J}_{g,n}
corresponding to
(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d})
is isomorphic to
[\mathcal{J}_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d}}/\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d})]
, so that set-theoretically there is a disjoint union

\mathfrak{J}_{g,n}=\bigcup_{[\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d}]}[\mathcal{J}_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d}}/\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d})].

Proof.

Let
\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}}
be the graph obtained from
\Gamma
by subdividing each edge not in
\Gamma_{0}
once. Set

\ \mathcal{M}_{\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}}}=\prod_{v\in V(\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}})}\mathcal{M}_{g(v),n(v)}\cong\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}\times(B\mathbb{G}_{m})^{e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}}}.

Then
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}}}
is the moduli space of quasi-stable curves equipped with an isomorphism of their dual graph with
\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}}
. We extend the function
\underline{d}
to
V(\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}})
by setting
\underline{d}(v)=1
for all
v\in V(\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}})\setminus V(\Gamma)
. Let
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}}}
be the universal quasi-stable curve. We let
\mathcal{J}_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d}}
be the component of the relative Jacobian of
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}}}
, parametrizing admissible line bundles of multidegree
\underline{d}
. There are now natural maps

\mathcal{J}_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}\prod_{v\in V(\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}})}\mathcal{J}_{g(v),n(v)}^{\underline{d}(v)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\longrightarrow}}\prod_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\mathcal{J}_{g(v),n(v)}^{\underline{d}(v)}

given by restricting a line bundle on the quasi-stable curve to its normalization, and forgetting the unstable components, respectively. The map
f
is a torus bundle with fiber
\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1}(\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}},\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{G}_{m})
, as we saw from (
8
). The map
g
is a trivial bundle with fiber
(B\mathbb{G}_{m})^{e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}}}
, since
\mathcal{J}_{0,2}^{d}\cong\mathcal{M}_{0,2}\cong B\mathbb{G}_{m}
for all
d
. Consequently, the fiber of
(g\circ f)
is the quotient torus

\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1}(\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}},\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{G}_{m})/\mathbb{G}_{m}^{e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}}}.

The naturality of the sequence (
8
) shows that
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}}}
acts in the evident way on
\bigoplus_{v\in V(\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}})}\mathbb{G}_{m}
and
\bigoplus_{e\in E(\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}})}\mathbb{G}_{m}
, which allows us to identify the quotient torus with
\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1}(\Gamma_{0},\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{G}_{m})
. Since
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}}}
parametrizes quasi-stable curves together with an identification of their dual graph with
\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}}
, the corresponding locus in the moduli stack of prestable curves is obtained by forgetting this identification, giving the quotient stack
[\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma^{\mathrm{qs}}}/\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0})]
. Similarly the locus in
\mathfrak{J}_{g,n}
corresponding to
\mathcal{J}_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d}}
is
[\mathcal{J}_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d}}/\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d})]
. ∎

.

3.3
Pagani–Tommasi stability conditions

Given a stable graph
\Gamma
, we denote by

\mathrm{Pic}(\Gamma)=\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}/\mathrm{Twist}_{\Gamma}

(9)

the
Picard group of
\Gamma
. Here
\mathrm{Twist}_{\Gamma}
is the group of multidegrees obtained by twisting by some vertex of
\Gamma
, see
[
7
]
for further details. We have that
\mathrm{Pic}(\Gamma)=\coprod_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\Gamma)
decomposes into fibers according to the total degree of a divisor on
\Gamma
(i.e. the quantity
d=\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\underline{d}(v)
), and each
\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\Gamma)
is a torsor under the finite abelian group
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(\Gamma)
.

We will not need to use the full definition of a Pagani–Tommasi stability condition. The only thing we shall need to know is that a Pagani–Tommasi stability condition
\sigma
over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
of degree
d
consists of a collection of triples
(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d})
as in (
7
), which in particular must satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

For every pair
(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0})
, the set

\{\underline{d}:(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d})\in\sigma\}

is a minimal set of coset representatives for
\operatorname{Pic}^{d-e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}}}(\Gamma_{0})
.

(ii)

If
f:\Gamma^{\prime}\to\Gamma
is an isomorphism of stable graphs, then

(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d})\in\sigma\iff(\Gamma^{\prime},f^{-1}\Gamma_{0},\underline{d}\circ f)\in\sigma.

The full definition of a Pagani–Tommasi stability condition is not much more complicated than this: it involves also a compatibility condition under the operation of growing
\Gamma_{0}
(while keeping
\Gamma
fixed), and a compatibility condition under the operation of contracting a subgraph of
\Gamma
. These conditions will however play no role in our arguments. For such a Pagani–Tommasi stability condition
\sigma
we have

\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{g,n}^{d}(\sigma)=\bigcup_{[\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d}]\in\sigma}[\mathcal{J}_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d}}/\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d})]\subset\mathfrak{J}_{g,n}.

(10)

We claim that at this point, the main result of the paper has been reduced to the following combinatorial claim.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
be Pagani–Tommasi stability conditions of degrees
d
and
d^{\prime}
, for fine compactified universal Jacobians over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. Assume moreover that
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
are invariant under a subgroup
S_{\lambda}=S_{n_{1}}\times\dots\times S_{n_{p}}\subseteq S_{n}
. Let
\Gamma
be a stable graph of type
(g,n)
with
n_{k}
legs labeled by
k
, for all
k=1,\dots,p
(so some legs may be indistinguishable), and let
\Gamma_{0}\subset\Gamma
be a connected subgraph containing all vertices. There exists an
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0})
-equivariant bijection between the sets

\{\underline{d}:(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d})\in\sigma\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\{\underline{d}^{\prime}:(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d}^{\prime})\in\sigma^{\prime}\}.

(The assumption that
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
are
S_{\lambda}
-invariant implies in particular that
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0})
acts on both sets.)

Before proceeding to the proof of
Lemma

3.7
, let us explain how it implies
Theorem

1.2
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

Consider first the non-equivariant situation; that is,

is trivial. It was observed in Section

that it suffices to show that

d
and
\sigma
.
By (
10
) and

3.5
we have

\chi_{c}(\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{g,n}^{d}(\sigma))=\sum_{[\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d}]\in\sigma}\chi_{c}\big(\mathcal{J}_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d}}/\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0},\underline{d})\big).

Consider our fixed Pagani–Tommasi stability conditions
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
. For each pair
(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0})
, let

be the bijection of

for all

\mathrm{Pic}^{d-e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}}}(\Gamma_{0}^{\prime})
and
\mathrm{Pic}^{d^{\prime}-e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}}}(\Gamma_{0}^{\prime})
. We consider
\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}
as having groups of
n_{1},\dots,n_{p},2e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}}
indistinguishable legs.

We assumed that
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
were invariant under
S_{\lambda}
. According to
[
24
, Remark 7.6]
, such invariant Pagani–Tommasi stability conditions exist only for
d
and
d^{\prime}
satisfying the condition

\gcd(g-1+d,2g-2,n_{1},\dots,n_{p})=1.

The condition we need to verify, to apply
Lemma

3.8
, is that

\gcd(g(\Gamma_{0}^{\prime})-1+(d-e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}}),2g(\Gamma_{0}^{\prime})-2,n_{1},\dots,n_{p},2e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}})=1.

The result follows once we note that
g(\Gamma_{0}^{\prime})=g-e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}}
.
∎

3.5
Computation

The results of this paper do not only prove that the cohomology groups
H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{g,n}^{d}(\sigma),\mathbb{Q})
are independent of
d
and
\sigma
, they also provide an algorithm for computing these cohomology groups, assuming that we know all of the equivariant Euler characteristics
\chi_{c}^{S_{n^{\prime}}}(\mathcal{J}_{g^{\prime},n^{\prime}})
of the uncompactified moduli spaces, with
g^{\prime}\leq g
, and
2g^{\prime}-2+n^{\prime}\leq 2g-2+n
.

\bullet
How does one compute the Hodge numbers of
\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{g,n}^{d}(\sigma)
?

What we need to do is to enumerate all isomorphism classes of pairs
(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0})
, where
\Gamma
is a stable graph of type
(g,n)
, and
\Gamma_{0}
is a connected subgraph containing all vertices, and for each such pair calculate the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0})
and the Picard torsor
\mathrm{Pic}^{d-e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}}}(\Gamma_{0})
. For each pair
(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0})
, the union of the corresponding strata in
\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{g,n}^{d}(\sigma)
has compactly supported cohomology groups

\Big(H^{*}_{c}(T_{\Gamma_{0}},\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{Pic}^{d-e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}}}(\Gamma_{0})]\otimes\bigotimes_{v\in V(\Gamma)}H^{*}_{c}(\mathcal{J}_{g(v),n(v)},\mathbb{Q})\Big)^{\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0})}.

Here
\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{Pic}^{d-e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}}}(\Gamma_{0})]
denotes the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space spanned by the finite set
\mathrm{Pic}^{d-e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}}}(\Gamma_{0})
, thought of as a trivial Hodge structure of weight
0
, but with a nontrivial action of the group
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0})
. From this, we can compute the Hodge–Deligne polynomial of each such union of strata in terms of the
S_{n(v)}
-equivariant Hodge–Deligne polynomials of the factors
\mathcal{J}_{g(v),n(v)}
; we need the equivariance to know how
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma,\Gamma_{0})
acts.

It would be interesting if this procedure could be carried out systematically in terms of symmetric functions, akin to Getzler–Kapranov’s
[
28
, Theorem 8.13]
, which solves the analogous problem when there is no
\Gamma_{0}
, and the factors
H^{*}_{c}(T_{\Gamma_{0}},\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{Pic}^{d-e_{\Gamma,\Gamma_{0}}}(\Gamma_{0})]
are omitted.

\bullet
How does one compute
\chi_{c}^{S_{n}}(\mathcal{J}_{g,n})
?

Suppose first
g\geq 2
. The forgetful map
\mathcal{J}_{g,n}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
is a fiber bundle with fiber
F(X,n)\times J(X)
, where
X
is a fixed curve of genus
g
, and
F(X,n)
denotes the configuration space of
n
distinct ordered points on
X
. We may think of the cohomologies
H^{*}_{c}(F(X,n),\mathbb{Q})
and
H^{*}_{c}(J(X),\mathbb{Q})
as variations of mixed Hodge structure on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. Let us write
\chi_{\mathcal{M}_{g},c}^{S_{n}}(F(X,n))
for the corresponding Euler characteristic, now taken in the Grothendieck group of polarizable variations of Hodge structure over
\mathcal{M}_{g}
with an action of
S_{n}
. Getzler (see
[
30
, Theorem 4.5]
or
[
29
]
) has given an explicit formula for
\chi_{\mathcal{M}_{g},c}^{S_{n}}(F(X,n))
in terms of standard variations of Hodge structure
\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}
on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
associated to irreducible representations of the symplectic group, indexed by partitions. This formula for
\chi_{\mathcal{M}_{g},c}^{S_{n}}(F(X,n))
includes precisely those
\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}
with
|\lambda|\leq n
. The Euler characteristic
\chi_{\mathcal{M}_{g},c}(J(X))
is straightforwardly
\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}[\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{V}_{1}]
. Multiplying these two together gives an expression for

\chi_{\mathcal{M}_{g},c}^{S_{n}}(F(X,n)\times J(X))

in terms of classes of local systems
\mathbb{V}_{\mu}
with
|\mu|-\ell(\mu)\leq n
. (One can show that these
\mu
are precisely the representations of the symplectic group which occur as a summand in
\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}\otimes\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{V}_{1}
for some
\lambda
with
|\lambda|\leq n
and some
k
.) We then obtain a formula for
\chi_{c}^{S_{n}}(\mathcal{J}_{g,n})

by “integrating over
\mathcal{M}_{g}
”, i.e. substituting each occurrence of
\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}
in
\chi_{\mathcal{M}_{g},c}^{S_{n}}(F(X,n)\times J(X))
for the Euler characteristic of its cohomology,
\chi_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{g},\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})
. In particular,
\chi_{c}^{S_{n}}(\mathcal{J}_{g,n})
can be expressed as a sum of terms
\chi_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{g},\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})
with
|\lambda|-\ell(\lambda)\leq n
.

The situation when
g=1
is similar and even a little simpler: the forgetful map
\mathcal{J}_{g,n}\to\mathcal{M}_{1,1}
has fiber
F(E,n)
, where
E
is an elliptic curve, and one similarly expresses
\chi_{c}^{S_{n}}(\mathcal{J}_{g,n})
in terms of
\chi_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{1,1},\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})
. When
g=0
,
\mathcal{J}_{0,n}=\mathcal{M}_{0,n}
, whose cohomology was calculated
S_{n}
-equivariantly by Getzler
[
27
]
.

The cohomology groups
H^{*}_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{1,1},\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})
are calculated classically by Eichler and Shimura. See Zucker
[
60
]
or
[
30
, Theorem 5.3]
for the mixed Hodge structure. In genus two, a formula for
\chi_{c}(\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})
was conjectured in
[
23
]
and proven in
[
51
]
, where in fact
H^{*}_{c}(\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})
is calculated (as a real Hodge structure, or
\ell
-adic Galois representation). Using the branching rule of
[
50
, Proposition 3.4]
one can then compute
\chi_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{2},\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})
for any
\lambda
. In genus
3
,
\chi_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{3},\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})
is known in the Grothendieck group of Galois representations unconditionally for
|\lambda|\leq 7

[
9
]
, and for
|\lambda|\leq 14
conditionally on standard conjectures in the Langlands program
[
10
]
. When
|\lambda|
is even, one can calculate
\chi_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{3},\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})
in terms of
\chi_{c}(\mathcal{A}_{3},\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})
. A formula for the latter was conjectured in
[
11
]
and proven in
[
56
]
. In genus
4
,
\chi_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{4},\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})
is known for
|\lambda|\leq 3

[
12
]
, which, however, is not enough information to determine even
\chi_{c}(\mathcal{J}_{4})
.

See also
[
35
, Proposition E]
for related work, expressing the Euler characteristics
\chi_{c}^{S_{n}}(\mathcal{J}_{g,n})
in terms of the Euler characteristics
\chi_{c}^{S_{n}}(\mathcal{C}_{g}^{n})
, for fixed
g
, where
\mathcal{C}_{g}^{n}
is the
n
-fold fibered power of the universal curve over
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. The weight-zero and integer-valued Euler characteristics of
\mathcal{J}_{g,n}
were studied in
[
34
]
.

Theorem A.1

(Jeremy Feusi)
.

For
g\geq 1
and
2g-2+n>0
, the universal Jacobians

\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{d}
and

\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{d^{\prime}}
are
S_{n}
-equivariantly isomorphic over
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}

if and only if

d\equiv d^{\prime}\quad\text{or}\quad-d\equiv d^{\prime}\mod\gcd(2g-2,n)\,.

Proof.

The case
n=0
was covered in
[
14
, Lemma 8.1]
, so from now on we assume
n>0
. Then, to prove the claim, one shows a stronger claim:

(
\dagger
) Any isomorphism
\varphi:\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{d}\to\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{d^{\prime}}
over
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
is given by
a morphism

T_{\textbf{a},\epsilon}(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},\mathcal{L})=(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},\mathcal{L}^{\otimes\epsilon}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}\omega_{C}^{\otimes a}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}p_{i}))\,,

where
\textbf{a}=(a,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
and
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
.

Assuming claim (
\dagger
) for now, we next want to argue that
T_{\textbf{a},\epsilon}
being
S_{n}
-equivariant then actually forces all
a_{1}=\ldots=a_{n}=:b
to be equal. Indeed, we must have

\mathcal{L}^{\otimes\epsilon}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}\omega_{C}^{\otimes a}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}p_{i})\cong\mathcal{L}^{\otimes\epsilon}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}\omega_{C}^{\otimes a}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}p_{\tau(i)})

(13)

for any permutation
\tau\in S_{n}
. Here we use that the action
S_{n}\curvearrowright\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{d}
actually leaves the line bundle itself unchanged. Tensoring with the dual of the line bundle on the left-hand side of (
13
), this equality is equivalent to

\mathcal{O}_{C}\cong\mathcal{O}_{C}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(p_{i}-p_{\tau(i)}))\,.

(14)

For any
i\geq 2
, let
\tau
be the transposition of
1
and
i
. Then we get the equality
\mathcal{O}_{C}\cong\mathcal{O}_{C}((a_{1}-a_{i})(p_{i}-p_{1}))
.
Since
g\geq 1
, we know that generically
\mathcal{O}_{C}(p_{i}-p_{1})
is not a torsion point. Hence it follows that
a_{1}=a_{i}
.

To show the stronger claim, use
[
15
, Lemma 3.2.3]
, stating that for a general smooth curve
C
of genus
g\geq 1
, any isomorphism
\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(C)\to\mathrm{Pic}^{d^{\prime}}(C)
is of the form
\mathcal{L}\mapsto\mathcal{L}^{\otimes\epsilon}\otimes\mathcal{M}
for some
\mathcal{M}\in\mathrm{Pic}^{d^{\prime}-\epsilon\cdot d}
for
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
. Thus restricting any isomorphism
\varphi
as in (
\dagger
) over a general point
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
, it must be of this shape (translation, possibly preceded by taking the dual). Continuity forces the sign
\epsilon=\epsilon(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
to be locally constant, and thus constant.
5
5

5

For a formal argument, consider the fiber dimensions of the maps
\mathcal{L}\mapsto\mathcal{L}\otimes\varphi(\mathcal{L})
and
\mathcal{L}\mapsto\mathcal{L}\otimes\varphi(\mathcal{L})^{\vee}
. Then
\epsilon=-1
implies the first map having positive-dimensional fibers, and
\epsilon=1
the same for the second map. By standard semicontinuity results on fiber dimensions, we have that the loci with fixed
\epsilon
are both open, and so connectedness of
\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{d}
(for
d\neq 0
) and
\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{0}\setminus\{\mathcal{O}\}
(for
d=0
, removing the only point where
\epsilon
is not uniquely determined) gives the claimed result.

So after possibly composing
\varphi
with
T_{\textbf{0},-1}
, we can
assume that
\varphi(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})}
for some collection of line bundles
\mathcal{N}_{(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})}\in\mathrm{Pic}^{d^{\prime}-d}(C)
. Next, we claim that the map

\mathcal{N}:\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\to\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{d^{\prime}-d},\quad(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\mapsto(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},\mathcal{N}_{(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})})

is actually a morphism. And indeed, it is just given by the composition

\mathcal{N}(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})=\mathcal{O}_{C}(-d\cdot p_{1})\otimes\varphi(\mathcal{O}_{C}(d\cdot p_{1}))

of universal sections of
\mathcal{J}_{g,n}^{\pm d}
with multiplication maps and
\varphi
. Then by the (strong) Franchetta property
[
54
]
, the section
\mathcal{N}
must be of the form

\mathcal{N}(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})=(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},\omega_{C}^{\otimes a}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}p_{i}))\,,

which completes the proof.

Theorem A.2

(Qizheng Yin)
.

Fix an integer
g\geq 22
and let
d,\widehat{d}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

[\mathcal{J}_{g}^{d}]=[\mathcal{J}_{g}^{\widehat{d}}]\quad\text{in}\quad K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})

if and only if
\widehat{d}\equiv\pm d\mod{2g-2}
.

For the proof of this theorem, we want to use that by Larsen–Lunts
[
37
, Sect. 2.5]
, there is a ring homomorphism

\Phi_{\mathrm{sb}}:K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{SB}]

to the monoid ring on stable birational equivalence classes of smooth projective varieties
X
, which sends the class
[X]\in K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})
of such a smooth projective variety to
1\cdot(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{SB}]
. To extend it to arbitrary varieties
U
, we choose a smooth projective birational model
X
of
U
with a common open
V\subset U,V\subset X
, and obtain
\Phi_{\mathrm{sb}}([U])=(X)-\Phi_{\mathrm{sb}}([X\setminus V])+\Phi_{\mathrm{sb}}([U\setminus V])
. Here the last two terms are defined inductively (since they are of smaller dimension). This process yields in particular an expression

\Phi_{\mathrm{sb}}([U])\;=\;(X)\;+\;\sum_{i}n_{i}(V_{i})\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{SB}]\,,

(15)

where each
V_{i}
is smooth projective with
\dim V_{i}<\dim X
.

To use this tool in the proof of
Theorem

A.2
, we will apply the following lemma.

Lemma A.3

(No cancellation of the top stable birational class for
\kappa\geq 0
)
.

Let
U
be an irreducible complex variety, and let
X
be a smooth projective birational model of
U
.
Assume
\kappa(X)\geq 0
(or weaker, that
X
is not uniruled).
Then no term
(V_{i})
in the expression (
15
) is equal to
(X)
in
\mathrm{SB}
, hence the coefficient of
(X)
is well-defined and equal to
1
.

If
U^{\prime}
is another irreducible variety admitting a smooth projective birational model
X^{\prime}
, with
\dim U=\dim U^{\prime}
and
\Phi_{\mathrm{sb}}([U])=\Phi_{\mathrm{sb}}([U^{\prime}])
in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{SB}]
,
then
(X)=(X^{\prime})
, i.e.
X
and
X^{\prime}
are stably birational; in fact
X
and
X^{\prime}
are birational.

Proof.

We first show that
(X)
cannot coincide with any
(V_{i})
.
Assume for contradiction that
(X)=(V_{i})
in
\mathrm{SB}
for some
i
. Set
d:=\dim X
and
e:=\dim V_{i}
; by construction
e<d
, and we write
r:=d-e>0
. The equality
(X)=(V_{i})
shows that
X
and
V_{i}\times\mathbb{P}^{r}
are smooth projective varieties that are stably birational and of the same dimension. But since
X
is non-uniruled, Liu–Sebag’s Corollary
[
38
, Cor. 1(1)]
yields that
X
and
V_{i}\times\mathbb{P}^{r}
are actually birational. This contradicts the uniruledness of
V_{i}\times\mathbb{P}^{r}
.

Consequently no cancellation of the basis element
(X)
occurs in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{SB}]
,
and the coefficient of
(X)
in
\Phi_{\mathrm{sb}}([U])
is well-defined and equal to
1
.

For the final assertion, write (using (
15
)) expansions

\Phi_{\mathrm{sb}}([U])=(X)+\sum_{i}n_{i}(V_{i})\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi_{\mathrm{sb}}([U^{\prime}])=(X^{\prime})+\sum_{j}m_{j}(W_{j})\,,

where
\dim V_{i},\dim W_{j}<\dim X=\dim X^{\prime}
for all
i,j
. Moreover, we have
(X)\neq(V_{i})
for all
i
, and repeating the argument above we also have
(X)\neq(W_{j})
for all
j
.
Now recall that
\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{SB}]
is, by definition, the free abelian group on the set

\mathrm{SB}
(equivalently, the monoid ring of
\mathrm{SB}
), so every element has a unique
expression as a finite
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination of the basis symbols
(T)\in\mathrm{SB}
.
Thus, if
\Phi_{\mathrm{sb}}([U])=\Phi_{\mathrm{sb}}([U^{\prime}])
, then the coefficient of the basis
element
(X)
on the left (which is
1
) must equal its coefficient on the right.
Hence it must be
that
(X)=(X^{\prime})
in
\mathrm{SB}
, i.e.
X
and
X^{\prime}
are stably birational.

Finally, since
X
and
X^{\prime}
are smooth projective varieties that are stably birational and of the same dimension, and since
X
is non-uniruled,
[
38
, Cor. 1(1)]
again yields that
X
and
X^{\prime}
are birational.
∎
