Multiomics integration uncovers metabolic control of H3K27ac in bovine inner cell masses†
Authors: Aldcejam Martins da Fonseca Junior, Jessica Ispada, Camila Bruna de Lima, Danilo Trabuco do Amaral, Pablo Juan Ross, Haja N Kadarmideen, Marcella Pecora Milazzotto
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe epigenetic reprogramming that occurs during the early stages of embryonic development has been described as crucial for the initial events of cell specification and differentiation, with possible long-term consequences to the offspring. This investigation explores the link between pyruvate metabolism and epigenetic regulation by culturing bovine embryos from Day 5 in the presence of sodium dichloroacetate (DCA), an organic acid that enhances the conversion of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA, and sodium iodoacetate (IA), which inhibits glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH), leading to the inhibition of glycolysis. Both treatments led to changes in the levels of H3K27 acetylation as well as their resulting binding patterns across the chromatin in the inner cell masses of blastocysts. An integrative analysis was performed between the molecular data and the results of chromatin state for H3K27ac. DCA led to a more robust association with biological processes related to lipid metabolism, while IA presented a higher binding activity linked to processes such as apoptosis and catabolism. The results discussed here corroborate previous evidence of the relationship between energy metabolism and epigenetic reprogramming in preimplantation bovine embryos, pointing to H3K27ac as an important mark for the immediate response of embryos to environmental changes in culture systems.Keywords: CUT&Tag, RNA sequencing, metaboloepigenetics, pyruvate metabolism, glycolysis

Introduction

The interaction of metabolic processes and epigenetic modifications provides a critical framework to understand gene regulation and cellular differentiation [1]. Metabolic pathways, beyond their primary roles in energy production, act as pivotal determinants of the epigenomic landscape. This is particularly evident during embryonic development, when fluctuations in metabolic states can exert profound influences on epigenetic markers, such as histone modifications, thereby directing critical gene expression patterns for development [2, 3].

A clear example of this metabolic–epigenetic dialogue emerges when we map how pyruvate is routed in the developing embryo. Pyruvate metabolism fundamentally impacts the early bovine embryo, acting beyond energy supply; it serves as a critical crossroads in the metabolic–epigenetic interface [4]. Pyruvate’s conversion to acetyl-CoA in the mitochondria not only fuels the tricarboxylic acid cycle (TCA) but also supplies acetyl groups for histone acetylation, altering the chromatin structure and transcriptional accessibility, which is imperative for the orchestration of gene expression patterns during development [5, 6].

Prior contributions from our research team revealed that perturbing pyruvate metabolism triggers adaptive changes that reshape global H3K9 and H3K27 acetylation and H3K27 trimethylation patterns. These changes lead to downstream alterations in metabolic regulation, epigenetic reprogramming, gene expression, and cell-cycle control, ultimately impacting cell number and differentiation in bovine embryos [4].

Although acetyl-CoA is synthesized within mitochondria via the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (PDC), it cannot directly cross the mitochondrial membrane. Citrate acts as an intermediate metabolite; it is produced in mitochondria and can be exported to the cytosol through the mitochondrial citrate carrier SLC25A1 (Solute carrier family 25 member 1). In the cytosol, citrate is cleaved by ATP citrate lyase (ACL), generating acetyl-CoA that can be used for histone acetylation, including H3K27ac [5–7]. Acetyl-CoA can also be synthesized directly inside the nucleus via PDC. In this case, PDC can translocate to the nucleus in certain conditions, such as mitochondrial stress, where it contributes to the synthesis of acetyl-CoA for histone acetylation [8].

Epigenetic remodeling through histone modifications can affect gene expression along with the regulation of chromatin accessibility to transcription factors (TFs) and RNA polymerase II [2, 9, 10]. Acetylation of lysine 27 at histone 3 (H3K27ac) is a marker of both active promoters and enhancers [11] and is key to understanding epigenetic dynamics in the developing bovine embryo.

Acetyl residues are added to H3K27 by two histone acetyltransferases (HATs) CBP (CREB-binding protein) and p300 (EP300 Lysine Acetyltransferase), affecting the accessibility of chromatin and playing a crucial role in cell fate [6, 12–15]. H3K27ac patterns are dynamically affected during preimplantation development, liable to a delicate crosstalk with diverse posttranslational modifications and coordinate genome activation in mammalian embryos through metabolic control [7, 16, 17].

In this work, we report a comprehensive data integration between the transcriptome and chromatin states related to H3K27ac of inner cell masses from metabolically modulated bovine blastocysts. These blastocysts were cultured in the presence of sodium dichloroacetate (DCA), an organic acid that increases pyruvate-to-acetyl-CoA conversion, or sodium iodoacetate (IA), which inhibits glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH), leading to glycolysis inhibition (Fig. 1).

This schematic representation explains the mechanisms of action for both modulators and their multifaceted context. After glucose uptake, glycolysis converts it to pyruvate (center). Pyruvate either feeds the TCA cycle in mitochondria (right), or may be converted in acetyl-CoA in the nucleus for histone acetylation. Citrate can exit mitochondria to supply nuclear acetyl-CoA for histone acetylation (left). DCA inhibits Pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase (PDK) to keep Pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH) active and boost pyruvate-to-acetyl-CoA flux, while IA irreversibly blocks GAPDH early in glycolysis, cutting off downstream pyruvate (and lactate) production.

Supplementation with DCA indirectly boosts the activity of pyruvate dehydrogenase enzymatic complex, through targeted suppression of the enzyme pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase (PDK). As a result, pyruvate oxidation is enhanced, leading to greater integration of the metabolite into the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle and, consequently, increased mitochondrial membrane potential and ATP production [18, 19]. However, previous findings reported diminished mitochondrial membrane potential in bovine blastocysts exposed to DCA, contrasting with the transient increase observed after 8 h of incubation at the morula stage, suggesting an adaptive mitochondrial overload response in later stages [4].

Conversely, iodoacetate produces an irreversible inactivation of GAPDH [20, 21], leading to glycolysis impairment and lactate depletion [22], together with changes in acetylation levels in distinct cellular types such as embryonic stem cells, sperm, and bovine embryos [3, 4, 23] and a consistent response in regard to posttranslational modifications and transcriptomics [24]. By performing a thorough bioinformatics analysis across datasets generated within these treatments, we unravel the relationships between H3K27ac enrichment and the molecular status of these embryos and give insights to the epigenomic reprogramming process in the developing bovine embryo.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

All the procedures were conducted in accordance with the Ethical Principles in Animal Research adopted by the Ethic Committee in the Use of Animals of Federal University of ABC, and were approved in the meeting of 2 March 2020, under the number CEUAx 6786040220 (ID 8402160218).

In vitro embryo production and inner cell mass isolation

In vitro bovine embryos were produced following the standard protocol for oocyte maturation, fertilization (pool of two thawed doses [0.5 mL] from different bulls), and first stages of culturing. From Day 5 of culture, three experimental groups were designed: synthetic oviductal fluid with amino acids (SOFaa) + 4% bovine serum albumin (Supplementary Material 1); control (no additional supplementation); and DCA (addition of 2 mM of DCA) or IA (addition of 2 μM of IA). The doses were defined according to the work of Fonseca Jr et al. (2023) [4] and the blastocyst rates are presented in Supplementary Material 1. Blastocysts were collected on Day 7 and their inner cell masses (ICMs) were isolated by immunosurgery.

Briefly, zona pellucidae were removed in pronase droplets (10 mg/mL) and SOFaa-HEPES with 10% fetal bovine serum was used to interrupt the reaction. The structures were then incubated with 20% anti-bovine serum antibody (B8270-SIGMA) and 20% guinea pig complement (Innovative Grade, IGP-COMPL-21249) before the trophectoderm (TE) and inner cell masses were mechanically separated by capillary micropipetting [25]. ICMs were submitted to RNA-Seq and CUT&Tag procedures.

Global transcriptomic profiling by RNA-Seq

Since the metabolic and molecular profile of ICM and TE cells are quite different at the blastocyst stage, we opted for isolating ICMs for RNA-Seq analysis. Briefly, RNA from three ICMs per group (n = 3 biological replicates) was extracted using the PicoPure RNA Isolation Kit, including DNAse treatment, following the manufacturer’s instructions, but with a modified RNA elution step. The RNAs (7 μL per sample; average of 1.6 ng) were used for cDNA synthesis and amplification by the Ovation RNA-seq V1 kit (NuGen, San Carlos, CA, USA) following the manufacturer’s recommendation. At the end, the samples were quantified again and an average of 1 μg of each sample was used to construct the cDNA libraries.

The Bioanalyzer 2100 (Agilent) was used for quality control. The samples were sonicated to produce DNA fragments of an average of 300 base pairs. Libraries were generated by the NEBNext Ultra II DNA Library Prep Kit for the Illumina system. Libraries were considered adequate with fragments averaging 350 bp in size (resulting from fragments and adapters). Samples were sequenced at the UC Davis Genome Sequencing Center by the Illumina Genome Analyzer IIx with 150 bp reads. Quality control of reads and alignment with the Bos taurus ARS-UCD1.2 genome (GCF_002263795.3) was performed using Genomics CLC Workbench 4.7 software (CLC bio, Aarhus, Denmark) and samples were checked for transcript quality and quantification and read alignment.

CUT&Tag of chromatin state related to H3K27 acetylation

CUT&Tag (Cleavage Under Targets and Tagmentation) of the chromatin state related to H3K27 acetylation was performed for DCA- and IA-treated embryos and their respective control groups, as shown in Figure 4. For this purpose, inner cell masses (10 ICM/sample [3 replicates]) were subjected to the procedure. DNA cleavage and library preparation were performed in a single step and the resulting fragments were sequenced. In brief, for both libraries and samples, the ICMs were incubated with concanavalin-coated magnetic beads for 20 min in a thermomixer at 400 rpm at room temperature (RT). Samples were then incubated with a primary antibody anti-H3K27ac (Cat# C15410196, Diagenode, 1:100) overnight at 4°C on a nutator.

Euler diagrams showing the unique symbols and matches among the DiffBind, peak scores, and DEG datasets for both treatment groups in comparison with the control group, with the consistency list with 273 symbols for DCA (corresponding to 31.30% of DEGs) and 262 for IA (corresponding to 25.15% of DEGs) (A, B).

They were incubated with secondary antibody (Cat# ABIN101961, Antibodies-online, 1:100) for 1 h on a nutator at RT and subsequently incubated with the hypoactive pA-Tn5 transposon for 1 h on a nutator at RT. To perform targeted digestion, 300 μL of labeling buffer was then added, and samples were incubated at 37°C for 1 h. The reaction was interrupted by adding 10 μL of 0.5 M Ethylenediamine tetraacetic acid (EDTA), 3 μL of 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS), and 2.5 μL of 20 mg/mL proteinase K to each tube, which was then incubated at 50°C for 1 h. DNA extraction was performed by centrifuging for 3 min at room temperature at 16 000 × g. Then, 300 μL of chloroform was added followed by centrifugation for 3 min at room temperature at 16 000 × g. The aqueous phase was removed and transferred to a 1.5 mL tube, to which 750 μL of 100% ethanol was added; the tube was inverted 10 times and then centrifuged for 30 min at 4°C at 16,000 × g. The supernatant was removed and 1 mL of 100% ethanol was added, followed by centrifugation for 5 min at 4°C at 16 000 × g. The supernatant was removed and the tube was then left to air dry. TE (Tris, EDTA) buffer (20 μL) was added and Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) was performed using NEBNext High-Fidelity 2X PCR Master Mix (New England Biolabs, M0541) as follows: 58°C for 5 min, 72°C for 5 min, 98°C for 45 s, followed by 20 cycles of 98°C for 15 s and 60°C for 10 s, with a final extension at 72°C for 1 min. Purification of PCR products was performed using Ampure XP beads (Bechman Coulter, A63881), and libraries were pooled for sequencing on the Illumina NextSeq platform to generate 40 bp paired-end reads.

Bioinformatic analysis

First, the samples were checked for the quality and quantification of the transcripts and the alignment of reads. Normalization and differential expression measurements of RNA-Seq were performed by DESeq2 (v3.20) in R (v4.3.0).

Genes with P < 0.005, Padj < 0.07, and log2 fold change (FC) > 1 or <−1 were considered differentially expressed between comparisons. DEGs (differentially expressed genes) between comparisons were ordered by adjusted p-value. Functional annotation was done using the DAVID (The Database for Annotation, Visualization, and Integrated Discovery) functional annotation tool (https://david.ncifcrf.gov//). These thresholds were selected to balance statistical rigor and biological relevance, ensuring that detected DEGs displayed both strong statistical support and meaningful changes in expression levels.

CUT&Tag results were analyzed in an R environment (v4.4.0) with Bioconductor packages (v3.19) specified here: quality control performed with FastQC (v0.12.0), sequence trimming by Trimmomatic (v0.39), genome alignment with Bowtie2 (v2.5.4) (B. taurus ARSUCD1.2, GCF_002263795.3). CUT&Tag was evaluated for H3K27ac-associated regions. Peak calling (identification of associated regions) was performed by MACS2 (Model-based Analysis of ChIP-Seq, v2.2.7.1) using an effective genome size of 2 983 314 115 bp, with a q-value threshold of 0.05 (p-value adjusted for multiple testing based on the false discovery rate). A round of ChIPseeker was performed to annotate the genic, intergenic, intron, exon, upstream, downstream, and distal intergenic regions.

Data integration

The datasets analyzed in this study included chromatin binding data processed through DiffBind (v3.16.0). This provided fold changes representing differential binding events in control versus DCA and control versus IA. It was also included DEGs derived from DESeq2. This yielded log2FCs indicating differential expression between the control and treatments.

The datasets also included peak scores representing the binding strength of chromatin-associated modification (H3K27ac). These scores were assessed individually for DCA, IA, and their control groups. Fold changes were extracted from the DiffBind analysis and log2FCs from the DESeq2 analysis. The peak scores were aggregated across samples within each condition (control, DCA, and IA) to derive average scores representative of those conditions. This aggregation provided a single representative score for unique associated genes in both the DCA and IA conditions. By doing so, it was ensured that the peak score data could be meaningfully compared with the fold changes from DiffBind and the log2 fold changes from DESeq2, despite the differences in how these data were initially generated.

Subsequently, each dataset was filtered to retain only those genes consistently present across all datasets. This filtering allowed us to identify common elements that could be directly compared across conditions. The consistency of signal direction was assessed—whether upregulation or downregulation—across DiffBind, DEGs, and peak scores. To visually represent the overlaps and consistency of signals across these datasets, the Eulerr package [26] was employed to create Euler diagrams. This methodology provides a comprehensive framework for analyzing complex genomic datasets.

Integration of transcriptome and chromatin state

To investigate the association between H3K27ac chromatin binding and gene expression changes, we integrated CUT&Tag and RNA-seq datasets, focusing on the most statistically and biologically relevant regions. Integration considered both peak scores, representing H3K27ac binding strength [27], and differential binding analysis results [28], alongside DEGs. Common official gene symbols were used as identifiers to link the datasets and identify consistent regulatory patterns.

Combining DiffBind results and peak scores allowed the identification of genomic regions consistently modified by treatment, which were subsequently integrated with DEGs. For DCA-treated embryos, 273 official gene symbols were consistently found across all three datasets (DiffBind, peak scores, and DEGs), corresponding to 31.30% of DEGs showing associated H3K27ac modifications (Figure 3A). For IA, 262 consistent symbols were identified, representing 25.15% of the DEGs (Figure 4B).

These results suggest that integrating chromatin state data (binding strength and differential binding) before correlating with gene expression provides a more robust framework to detect significant biological changes induced by metabolic treatments.

The consistency results assessed for functional annotation by DAVID (https://david.ncifcrf.gov//) found acetylation as the main posttranslational modification in both groups ([UP_KW_PTM] DCA – count: 56, p-value: 8.8E-8. IA – count: 75 p-value: 1,2E-16), with biological processes related to positive regulation of transcription by RNA polymerase II, translation, cell division, and chromatin remodeling, irrespective of the treatment group.

Regarding the unique symbols found in the integration for the DCA group, the modulation aimed at enhancing pyruvate oxidation was associated with a broad spectrum of biological processes, including those related to lipid metabolism and cellular signaling. The 10 most significant were: positive regulation of the canonical Wnt signaling pathway; camera-type eye development; positive regulation of phospholipid efflux; intracellular signal transduction; mRNA export from the nucleus; protein import; isoleucine transport; negative regulation of the canonical Wnt signaling pathway; fat cell differentiation; and magnesium ion homeostasis (Figure 4B).

The most prominent cluster of biological processes identified in the IA consistency list encompassed a diverse set of regulatory functions, including the control of protein localization, rDNA heterochromatin formation, K27-linked protein ubiquitination, and modulation of apoptosis. Additional enriched terms involved ubiquitin-dependent protein catabolism, transcriptional regulation by RNA polymerases II and III, mitochondrial calcium ion homeostasis, suppression of viral transcription, and regulation of protein secretion (Figure 5D).

The genome-wide distribution of those consistent genes across all chromosomes is represented in circos plots (Figure 5A and C), illustrating that treatment-specific differences are spread broadly across the genome rather than being restricted to isolated loci.

Among the consistent sets (DCA, n = 273; IA, n = 262), several gene symbols correspond to chromatin remodelers or transcription factors. In the DCA set, we identified CHD1 (Chromodomain Helicase DNA Binding Protein 1), GATAD2A (GATA Zinc Finger Domain Containing 2A), BRD3 (Bromodomain Containing 3), and the transcription factor TCF4 (Transcription Factor 4) (Figure 5G). In the IA set, remodelers included SMARCA5 (SWI/SNF related, matrix associated, actin dependent regulator of chromatin, subfamily A, member 5) (Figure 5F) and SETDB2 (histone lysine N-methyltransferase SETDB2) (Figure 5H), while BRD3 and GATAD2A were again present, highlighting common remodeler components across treatments. The recurrence of these regulators in both sets underlines that the metabolic interventions converge on core elements of chromatin remodeling and transcriptional control.

Discussion

This comprehensive analysis reveals significant insights into how the alterations induced by metabolic modulation of pyruvate shape the remodeling of H3K27ac in bovine embryos. The integration of transcriptome and chromatin binding data in this scenario highlights the complex regulatory networks affected in this metaboloepigenetics approach. Even though the selection and standardization of computational methods and bioinformatics strategies are still a challenge currently, relying on technological power, statistical temperance, and biological context seems to be a fair course of action [29, 30].

In general, the distribution of scores across peaks showed diminished levels for IA when compared to the control and DCA, however, showing higher number of peaks associated with gene symbols, which means extensive, but low levels of acetylated histones were observed in this treatment. The consistency list for IA highlights a distinct metabolic and epigenetic response to stress conditions induced by the treatment, indicating a compensatory shift in glycolytic metabolism, ensuring that glucose metabolism remains active despite the inhibition of GAPDH. This suggests that embryos under IA treatment prioritize maintaining energy homeostasis through alternative pathways, likely to support key processes such as histone acetylation and cellular adaptation under stress. Additionally, chromatin remodelers act to regulate accessibility and safeguard essential transcriptional programs, including under conditions where metabolic signaling pathways are perturbed [31, 32].

More specifically, the consistency list for DCA revealed key insights into the metabolic reprogramming and cellular functions shaped by H3K27ac. Notably, it captured symbols involved in glucose and phosphate transport, mitochondrial activity, and translational regulation, all of which contribute to supporting the energy demands and developmental requirements of the embryos. This points to a metabolic strategy based on mitochondrial respiration and efficient energy production, with indicators of reduced dependence on glycolysis. The enrichment of translation initiation factors and transcriptional regulators indicates the importance of maintaining protein synthesis and stress resilience, critical processes for early development.

The glucose transporter SLC2A8 (solute carrier family 2 member 8) was up-represented in the DCA consistency list, suggesting the action of H3K27ac in adaptative changes during the regulation of glucose consumption by these embryos. The upregulation of phosphate transporters (SLC35C2 [solute carrier family 35 member C2] and SLC34A2 [solute carrier family 34 member 2]) together with the mitochondrial electroneutral antiporter SLC25A1, which exports citrate from the mitochondria into the cytosol in exchange for malate, could help maintain the ATP production through oxidative phosphorylation instead of relying solely on glycolysis, while the acetyl-CoA could be generated by breaking down the accumulated fatty acids, corroborating their increased levels in DCA [4]. In addition, the increased export of citrate from the mitochondria to the cytosol could explain the higher levels of H3K27ac observed in the DCA group, since the acetyl-CoA derived from citrate conversion is described as the main source of acetyl groups for histone acetylation [5–7] (Supplementary Material 1).

The consistency list for DCA also presented symbols corresponding to eukaryotic translational initiation factors, including EIF2S2 (eukaryotic translation initiation factor 2 subunit beta), that maintain mitochondrial activity and prevent cellular stress in mice oocytes [33] as well as EIF3E (eukaryotic translation initiation factor 3 subunit E), essential for embryonic development and cell proliferation in the mouse and present in competent in vitro produced bovine embryos [34, 35], along with TCF4 (transcription factor 4), required for cell differentiation and specification during mouse embryogenesis [36]. These findings corroborate previous evidence, when DCA was capable of impacting the mitochondrial membrane potential and total cell number [4].

The positive regulation of ADPGK (ADP-dependent glucokinase) in the IA consistency list reveals an interesting response to the targeted action of iodoacetate, as this enzyme can maintain glycolytic activity when GAPDH is inhibited, phosphorylating glucose to glucose-6-phosphate using ADP (adenosine diphosphate), keeping glucose metabolism active, providing essential intermediates for alternative pathways in early development, and ensuring energy supply for processes like H3K27ac acetylation under stress conditions [7, 37, 38].

The chromatin remodeler SMARCA5, a chromatin remodeler that plays a crucial role in regulating chromatin structure and accessibility by repositioning nucleosomes [39], was up-represented in the IA group. In IA-treated embryos, this enzyme may drive the activation of stress-response genes or sustain essential transcriptional programs that support cellular survival and adaptation under metabolic stress, with H3K27ac acting as a key epigenetic regulator of these processes. SMARCA5 also plays a role in DNA damage repair, cell cycle regulation, and genome stability maintenance, particularly under stressful conditions [40, 41], correlating to gene ontology findings in IA, associated with the regulation of apoptotic and catabolic activities.

In line with this, SETDB2 (histone lysine N-methyltransferase SETDB2) illustrates the interplay between H3K27ac and other histone modifications, acting as a key mediator of repressive chromatin states through the deposition of H3K9me2/me3 marks associated with gene silencing [42, 43]. Its positive representation in the IA-treated embryos might indicate a stress-induced transcriptional repression due to the impact on pyruvate metabolism. SETDB2 has also been linked to cellular stress responses and the control of differentiation [44–46], suggesting that its methyltransferase activity may act as a protective mechanism in IA-treated embryos. This interpretation is supported by previous observations in which reduced H3K27 acetylation was associated with a decrease in total cell number and a lower proportion of inner cell mass (ICM) cells in embryos exposed to IA. The suggested transcriptional repression denotes the interplay between acetylation and methylation in modulating transcription, potentially as a protective mechanism under adverse conditions. The balance between maintaining genome stability, managing cellular stress, and promoting necessary differentiation pathways is reflected in these findings.

These observations align with the notion that H3K27ac plays a role beyond simple transcriptional activation, actively participating in the metabolic adjustments needed during early embryonic development. The presence of mitochondrial transporters and chromatin remodelers across the consistency lists suggests that metabolic flexibility is closely associated with epigenetic regulation, allowing embryos to balance energy production through oxidative phosphorylation while managing stress. Additionally, the involvement of enzymes such as ADPGK in glycolytic maintenance under IA treatment reflects how embryos dynamically respond to metabolic inhibitors, safeguarding energy metabolism through alternative pathways. This capacity to reprogram cellular activity under metabolic stress appears to be crucial for maintaining chromatin accessibility and transcriptional programs, with differential acetylation patterns influencing developmental outcomes. Such findings suggest that the embryos modulate histone acetylation to align with metabolic conditions, underlying H3K27ac as a joint operator and indispensable for the early bovine embryo reprogramming, regardless of the modulations employed in this work.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study reveals the interplay between metabolic modulation and chromatin remodeling in bovine embryos, with both enhancement (DCA) and inhibition (IA) of key metabolic pathways reshaping H3K27ac patterns and gene expression. DCA treatment appears to enhance lipid metabolism, redirecting energy supply toward oxidative phosphorylation as an adaptive mechanism. The resulting lipid accumulation supports sustained ATP production and aligns with the transcriptomic profile and the increased levels of fatty acids observed in metabolomic analyses. IA-treated embryos seem to have been facing metabolic stress, compromising key metabolic pathways related to transcription activity and presenting a predominance of biological processes linked to apoptosis and catabolism. These findings provide valuable insights into how metabolic pathways shape the epigenetic landscape, contributing to early developmental processes and cellular adaptation.
