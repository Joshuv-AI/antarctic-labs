Semiparametric Efficient Bilevel Gradient Estimation
Fares El Khoury ∗, Houssam Zenati ∗, Nathan Kallus, Michael Arbel, Aurélien Bibaut

Abstract
Functional bilevel methods estimate a lower-level function and plug it into a hypergradient, but this plug-in gradient can retain first-order bias when the lower-level problem is learned nonparametrically. To remove this bias, we develop a semiparametric debiasing theory for population bilevel gradients based on the efficient influence function. This perspective leads to a cross-fitted orthogonal hypergradient estimator for which we establish asymptotic normality together with uniform control over the outer parameter. Under quadratic losses, the estimator reduces to a simple doubly robust score based on conditional mean nuisances. On synthetic bilevel benchmarks with known ground truth, the method tracks the oracle efficient-gradient benchmark and improves over plug-in functional hypergradients and regularized kernel bilevel baselines.

Introduction

Bilevel optimization provides a natural framework for problems in which one learning task is constrained by the solution of another. This hierarchical structure appears across machine learning, including hyperparameter optimization
[
43
,
39
,
36
]
, meta-learning
[
20
,
18
,
45
]
, inverse problems and optimal control
[
31
,
1
]
, reinforcement learning
[
25
]
, domain adaptation
[
35
]
, and instrumental variable regression
[
41
,
50
,
49
]
. In these applications, the outer parameter is typically updated using gradient-based methods, so the quality of the resulting bilevel gradient directly affects both optimization and statistical performance.

Most existing theory for bilevel optimization has been developed in finite-dimensional parametric settings, often under strong convexity of the lower-level problem
[
21
,
27
,
29
,
61
]
. This assumption gives a unique inner solution and makes implicit differentiation stable
[
43
,
36
]
. It is also convenient for algorithmic convergence and stability analyses
[
9
,
23
,
40
]
. However, these assumptions do not fully capture modern learning problems where the inner-level object is a flexible prediction function. This is the case, for instance, in kernel methods, which are naturally viewed as function estimators in reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHS)
[
48
,
51
,
10
]
, and in overparameterized neural networks, including those analyzed via the neural tangent kernel regime, which also admit function-space interpretations whose effective dimension may grow with the sample size
[
26
,
32
,
3
]
.

This limitation has motivated a functional view of bilevel optimization. Functional bilevel optimization treats the lower-level variable as a prediction function, rather than as the parameters of a model, and applies implicit differentiation in function space
[
44
]
. Its algorithms estimate the lower-level function and an adjoint sensitivity function, then plug these estimates into a hypergradient formula
[
44
]
. Kernel bilevel optimization instantiates this program in an RKHS
[
14
]
, where the representer theorem yields finite-dimensional empirical solutions
[
48
,
6
]
. Its analysis gives empirical-process bounds for fixed-regularization bilevel values and gradients
[
57
,
30
,
14
]
. Thus, existing functional approaches provide plug-in estimators and generalization guarantees for regularized bilevel gradients, but they do not characterize efficient estimation of the unregularized population gradient.

We study this statistical target directly. Although this gradient is finite-dimensional, it depends on infinite-dimensional nuisance functions: the lower-level population solution and its sensitivity with respect to the outer parameter. This places the problem in semiparametric inference, where low-dimensional functionals of nonparametric distributions are analyzed through pathwise differentiability and influence functions
[
8
,
42
,
58
,
30
]
. The efficient influence function gives the first-order expansion of the target under perturbations of the data law and determines the semiparametric efficiency bound for regular asymptotically linear estimators
[
8
,
58
,
52
]
. In this setting, plug-in hypergradients can retain first-order bias from estimating the lower-level nuisance functions, especially when these functions are learned nonparametrically or with regularization
[
42
,
11
,
28
]
. Orthogonal scores and cross-fitting remove this first-order nuisance sensitivity and yield asymptotically normal estimators under product-rate conditions on the nuisance errors
[
46
,
56
,
11
]
.

We bring this semiparametric viewpoint to functional bilevel gradient estimation. Recent work has extended orthogonal debiasing beyond classical finite-dimensional parameters, using learned Riesz representers, automatic debiased machine learning for nonparametric
M
-estimands, and Hilbert-valued one-step corrections
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
28
,
19
,
54
,
37
]
. Closest in spirit are recent problem-specific uses of this machinery for doubly robust kernel-embedding functionals, functional policy-gradient learning, and semiparametric efficient tests
[
62
,
7
,
38
,
63
]
.

Bilevel gradients are not covered by these developments: the target here is a finite-dimensional functional of a nonparametric population optimizer and of the optimizer’s derivative with respect to the outer parameter. The efficient correction must therefore account for perturbations of both the inner solution and its Jacobian. Instead, existing functional and kernel bilevel methods provide plug-in hypergradients and generalization guarantees for regularized objectives
[
44
,
14
]
, and not an efficient influence function, no semiparametric efficiency bound, nor orthogonal estimator for the unregularized population hypergradient. To our knowledge, this is the first semiparametric efficiency theory for population bilevel gradient estimation.

Contributions.

We make four contributions. First, we derive the efficient influence function for the population bilevel gradient and identify the correction terms missing from plug-in hypergradients. This shows that differentiating a fitted bilevel objective is generally not efficient: it retains first-order nuisance bias from estimating the lower-level solution and its derivative. Second, we construct a cross-fitted orthogonal estimator. In the quadratic inner-loss case, the score reduces to a simple doubly robust form based on conditional-mean nuisances, yielding second-order bias, asymptotic normality, and coordinate-wise confidence intervals. Third, we prove a uniform empirical-process bound for the debiased gradient over the outer parameter space, enabling its use as a statistical gradient oracle. Finally, experiments with known ground truth show that the estimator tracks the oracle efficient-gradient benchmark, gives calibrated inference, and exposes the fixed-regularization bias of KBO when the target is the unregularized population gradient.

2
Problem Statement

Let
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}
,
\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}
,
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{z}}
, and
O=(X,Y,Z)\sim P
, where
P
is a probability distribution supported on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Z}
, with
P_{X}
,
P_{Y}
, and
P_{Z}
denoting the marginal distributions of
X
,
Y
, and
Z
, respectively. For any integrable function
f
, we use the shorthand
Pf\coloneqq\int f(o)\,dP(o)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[f(O)]
. This notation makes explicit that all population quantities below are functionals of the unknown data-generating law
P
. We consider the following Functional Bilevel Optimization (
FBO
) problem
[
44
]
:

\min_{\omega\in\Omega}\mathcal{F}_{P}(\omega)\quad\text{such that}\quad\mathcal{F}_{P}(\omega)\coloneqq P\,\ell^{out}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})\quad\text{and}\quad h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\in\argmin_{h\in\mathcal{H}}P\,\ell^{in}_{\omega}(h),

(FBO)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space of functions defined on
\mathcal{X}
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{q}
, and for any
\omega\in\Omega
and
h\in\mathcal{H}
,
\ell^{in}_{\omega}(h):\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\ell^{out}_{\omega}(h):\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}
are the inner and outer pointwise losses, respectively. If the lower-level argmin is not unique,
h^{\star}_{\omega,P}
denotes the minimum-
\mathcal{H}
-norm solution whenever this selection exists. We write the population solution of the outer problem
\omega^{\star}_{P}\in\argmin_{\omega\in\Omega}\mathcal{F}_{P}(\omega)
. While this solution motivates the bilevel problem, it is not the object of inference in this paper. Although the framework (
FBO
) accommodates general losses, squared losses occupy a central place in machine learning: they arise naturally in regression and causal inference, and they yield closed-form inner solutions that make the bilevel structure analytically tractable. We therefore take
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathcal{X},P_{X};\mathbb{R}^{q})
(abbreviated by
L^{2}(P_{X})
), the space of square-integrable
\mathbb{R}^{q}
-valued functions on
\mathcal{X}
, equipped with inner product
\langle h,h^{\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}=\mathbb{E}_{P_{X}}[h(X)^{\top}h^{\prime}(X)]
and induced norm
\|h\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{P_{X}}[\|h(X)\|^{2}]
, and specialize to quadratic losses:

\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(X,Z)=\frac{1}{2}\|h(X)-g_{\omega}(Z)\|^{2},\quad\ell^{\mathrm{out}}_{\omega}(h)(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\|Y-h(X)\|^{2},

(1)

where
g_{\omega}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
is a parametric map encoding how the hyperparameter
\omega
enters the inner problem. The inner loss penalizes the discrepancy between the prediction
h(X)
and a
\omega
-dependent target
g_{\omega}(Z)
, while the outer loss measures prediction error against the observed response
Y
. Under this choice, the
unique
inner minimizer
h^{\star}_{\omega,P}(X)=\mathbb{E}_{P_{Z}}[g_{\omega}(Z)\mid X]
is the conditional mean of
g_{\omega}(Z)
given
X
in
\mathcal{H}
.

2.1
Examples of applications

We provide examples of applications of the (
FBO
) problem with such a specialization.

Instrumental variable regression.

A canonical instance is the nonparametric instrumental variable (IV) regression. Here
Z
is an instrument,
X
is the treatment, and
Y
is the outcome. Under the quadratic losses (
1
), the unique inner minimizer projects the instrument-driven signal
g_{\omega}(Z)
onto the treatment
X
, so that the outer objective becomes:

\mathcal{F}_{P}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{P_{X},P_{Y}}\bigl[\|Y-\mathbb{E}_{P_{Z}}[g_{\omega}(Z)\mid X]\|^{2}\bigr].

The bilevel problem thus learns the structural signal
g_{\omega}(Z)
and evaluates it through a downstream prediction criterion on the outcome
Y
, connecting to nonparametric IV and modern deep IV procedures
[
41
,
24
,
5
,
49
]
.

Fitted Bellman regression in reinforcement learning.

Quadratic inner losses arise naturally in fitted value and fitted Q-function methods. Let
X=(S,A)
be the state-action pair and
Z=(S,A,R,S^{\prime})
the full transition tuple. The Bellman target
g_{\omega}(Z)
is parameterized by
\omega
; for instance, in fitted value iteration
g_{\omega}(Z)=R+\gamma V_{\omega}(S^{\prime})
, where
V_{\omega}
is a parameterized value function, and in fitted-Q control
g_{\omega}(Z)=R+\gamma\max_{a^{\prime}}Q_{\omega}(S^{\prime},a^{\prime})
, where
Q_{\omega}
is a parameterized Q-function and
\gamma\in(0,1)
is the discount factor. Under the quadratic losses (
1
), the unique inner minimizer is the projected Bellman backup

h^{\star}_{\omega,P}(S,A)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[g_{\omega}(Z)\mid S,A],

and the outer objective fits this projected value or Q-function to returns or policy-performance targets. This covers the regression step underlying fitted Q iteration and least-squares Bellman methods
[
15
,
2
,
16
]
.

Conditional equilibrium response learning.

Consider a game or market where agents have observable characteristics
X
, the environment is subject to exogenous shocks
Z
, and the equilibrium outcome, such as a price, allocation, or action vector, is determined by a solver with primitives
\omega
and takes the form
g_{\omega}(Z)
. Since
g_{\omega}(Z)
is not directly observable from
X
alone, the inner problem under (
1
) learns the conditional equilibrium response
\mathbb{E}_{P}[g_{\omega}(Z)\mid X]
from observable data. The outer objective then selects
\omega
to match observed market outcomes, while the equilibrium computation remains encapsulated in
g_{\omega}
. This connects to differentiable game solving and end-to-end learning in games
[
34
,
33
]
.

2.2
Target functional

Fix
\omega\in\Omega
and define the target functional

\Psi_{\omega}(P)\coloneqq\nabla\mathcal{F}_{P}(\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

a finite-dimensional statistical functional mapping the unknown law
P
to the hypergradient of the population outer value at
\omega
. Computing
\Psi_{\omega}(P)
requires differentiating through the inner solution
h^{\star}_{\omega,P}
. Assuming that
\omega^{\prime}\mapsto h^{\star}_{\omega^{\prime},P}
is Fréchet differentiable at the fixed
\omega
, we denote the partial derivatives of the inner solution by

j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\coloneqq D_{\omega_{k}}h^{\star}_{\omega,P}:X\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{P_{Z}}[\partial_{\omega_{k}}g_{\omega}(Z)\mid X],\qquad k=1,\ldots,d,

and collect them as
j^{\star}_{\omega,P}=(j^{\star}_{1,\omega,P},\ldots,j^{\star}_{d,\omega,P})\in L^{2}(P_{X})^{d}
. Let
\partial_{\omega_{k}}\ell^{\mathrm{out}}_{\omega}
and
\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{out}}_{\omega}
denote the first-order partial derivatives of
\ell^{\mathrm{out}}
with respect to
\omega_{k}
and
h
, while
\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}
and
\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}
denote the second-order partial derivative of
\ell^{\mathrm{in}}
with respect to
h
and its mixed derivative with respect to
\omega_{k}
and
h
. For each coordinate
k=1,\ldots,d
, define

\psi_{k,P}(h,v)\coloneqq P\,\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{out}}_{\omega}(h)(v),\qquad h,v\in L^{2}(P_{X}),

The chain rule then yields the
direct form
of the target functional

\Psi_{k,\omega}(P)=\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}).

This form requires computing
j^{\star}_{\omega,P}
, which involves solving a
d
-dimensional system in
L^{2}(P_{X})
. An equivalent
adjoint form
avoids this by introducing a single adjoint variable
a^{\star}_{\omega,P}\in L^{2}(P_{X})
solving

P\,\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(a^{\star}_{\omega,P},u)+P\,\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{out}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(u)=0,\qquad\forall u\in L^{2}(P_{X}).

(2)

The target functional then takes the following adjoint form

\Psi_{k,\omega}(P)=P\,\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(a^{\star}_{\omega,P}).

Stacking these coordinates yields the full gradient
\Psi_{\omega}(P)=(\Psi_{1,\omega}(P),\ldots,\Psi_{d,\omega}(P))^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which is the standard hypergradient of functional implicit differentiation
[
44
]
. A natural approach to estimating
\Psi_{\omega}(P)
is to plug in empirical estimates of
h^{\star}_{\omega,P}
and
a^{\star}_{\omega,P}
directly
[
44
,
14
]
. However, such plug-in estimators induce a first-order bias due to the nonparametric estimation of these nuisance functions
[
8
,
58
]
; Appendix
E.2
gives the exact plug-in bias decomposition in the quadratic specialization. The efficient influence function of
\Psi_{\omega}
, derived via a functional von Mises expansion
[
59
,
22
,
17
]
, provides a principled correction for this bias
[
42
,
11
]
. We make this precise in
Section

3
.

3
Functional von Mises Expansion and Efficient Influence Function

The target functional
\Psi_{\omega}(P)
depends on
P
through two infinite-dimensional nuisances: the inner solution
h^{\star}_{\omega,P}
and its derivative
j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}
, both of which must be estimated from data. To understand how
\Psi_{\omega}(P)
changes under perturbations of
P
and identify the correction terms needed to debias plug-in hypergradients, we proceed via a functional von Mises expansion that linearizes
\Psi_{\omega}
around
P
. This yields the efficient influence function of
\Psi_{\omega}
, which is the canonical object governing the first-order bias of plug-in estimators and the semiparametric efficiency bound, and identifies the correction terms needed for consistent estimation. The expansion requires the nuisances to vary smoothly along parametric submodels, which we formalize in the following assumption.

Assumption A.

(Differentiability of the nuisances) Along every regular parametric submodel
P_{\epsilon}
through
P
, the maps
\epsilon\mapsto h^{\star}_{\omega,P_{\epsilon}}
and
\epsilon\mapsto j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P_{\epsilon}}
are differentiable at
\epsilon=0
in
L^{2}(P_{X})
.

The von Mises expansion requires correcting for perturbations of both nuisances. Each correction is encoded by a Riesz representer:
\alpha_{1,k,P}
corrects for perturbations of
h^{\star}_{\omega,P}
, and
\alpha_{2,P}
corrects for perturbations of
j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}
. They are defined by

\displaystyle\left\langle\alpha_{1,k,P},u\right\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})[u],\qquad\forall u\in L^{2}(P_{X}),

(3)

\displaystyle\left\langle\alpha_{2,P},u\right\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}

\displaystyle=\partial_{2}\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})[u],\qquad\forall u\in L^{2}(P_{X}),

(4)

where
\partial_{1}
and
\partial_{2}
denote the Fréchet derivatives of
\psi_{k,P}
with respect to its first and second arguments respectively. These are the bilevel analogues of the Riesz representers used in semiparametric debiasing for nonparametric
M
-estimands
[
54
]
. Two simplifications follow from the quadratic structure. First, the right-hand side of (
4
) does not depend on
k
, since

\partial_{2}\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})[u]=P\,\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{out}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(u),

so the same
\alpha_{2,P}
is shared across all
d
coordinates. Second, we have
\partial_{1}\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})[u]=-\langle j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P},u\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}
, so (
3
) gives

\alpha_{1,k,P}=-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}.

Third, comparing (
4
) with the adjoint equation (
2
) shows that

\alpha_{2,P}=-a^{\star}_{\omega,P}.

Therefore,
\alpha_{1,k,P}
and
\alpha_{2,P}
are
j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}
and the adjoint
a^{\star}_{\omega,P}
, respectively, up to sign. The analysis throughout requires the following mild boundedness condition.

Assumption B.

(Almost sure boundedness) There exist finite constants
A,B,D>0
such that
\|Y\|\leq A
,
\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\|g_{\omega}(Z)\|\leq B
, and
\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|\partial_{\omega_{k}}g_{\omega}(Z)\|^{2}\leq D
almost surely.

We now derive the efficient influence function of
\Psi_{k,\omega}
: the canonical first-order derivative of the target under regular perturbations of
P
. This object encodes the correction terms needed to remove first-order plug-in bias and attain the semiparametric efficiency bound
[
8
,
42
,
58
,
52
,
30
]
. Recall that
\Psi_{k,\omega}
is
pathwise differentiable
at
P
if, for every regular parametric submodel
(P_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
with score
S
, the map
\epsilon\mapsto\Psi_{k,\omega}(P_{\epsilon})
is differentiable at
\epsilon=0
and its derivative takes the form

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\Psi_{k,\omega}(P_{\epsilon})\right|_{\epsilon=0}=\left\langle\chi_{k,P,\omega},S\right\rangle_{L^{2}(P)}

for some fixed
\chi_{k,P,\omega}
that does not depend on the choice of submodel, which is called the
efficient influence function
of
\Psi_{k,\omega}
.

Theorem 3.1

(Efficient influence function)
.

Under
A
and
B
, the functional
\Psi_{k,\omega}
is pathwise differentiable at
P
with efficient influence function

\displaystyle\chi_{k,P,\omega}(O)

\displaystyle=\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{out}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(O)(j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})-\Psi_{k,\omega}(P)-\partial_{h}\ell^{in}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(O)(\alpha_{1,k,P})-\partial_{\omega_{k},h}^{2}\ell^{in}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(O)(\alpha_{2,P})

(5)

\displaystyle-\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{in}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(O)(j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P},\alpha_{2,P}).

Consequently,
\chi_{P,\omega}\coloneqq(\chi_{1,P,\omega},\ldots,\chi_{d,P,\omega})^{\top}
is the vector-valued efficient influence function for
\Psi_{\omega}(P)
in the nonparametric model.

The proof is deferred to Appendix
A
. The next result gives the corresponding von Mises expansion. It quantifies the error made when the oracle nuisances are replaced by candidate functions, and shows explicitly why the Riesz-corrected score is orthogonal.

Theorem 3.2

(Functional von Mises expansion)
.

Under
A
and
B
, for each
k=1,\ldots,d
there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{N}_{k}
of
(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})
such that, for every
(h,v)\in\mathcal{N}_{k}
and every
\alpha_{1,k},\alpha_{2}\in L^{2}(P_{X})
,

\displaystyle\psi_{k,P}(h,v)-\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})-P\,\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(\alpha_{1,k})-P\,\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(\alpha_{2})-P\,\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(v,\alpha_{2})

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha_{1,k,P}-\alpha_{1,k},h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}+\langle\alpha_{2,P}-\alpha_{2},v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}+\mathrm{Rem}_{k}(h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P},v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}),

(6)

where

|\mathrm{Rem}_{k}(h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P},v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}^{2}+\|v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}^{2}\right).

(7)

The proof is deferred to Appendix
B
. Equation (
3.2
) is the key orthogonality identity: once the Riesz corrections are subtracted from the plug-in score, the residual has no first-order dependence on the nuisance estimation errors
h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}
and
v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}
. The only remaining terms are cross-products of Riesz-estimation errors
\|\alpha_{1,k,P}-\alpha_{1,k}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}
and
\|\alpha_{2,P}-\alpha_{2}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}
with nuisance-estimation errors
\|h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}
and
\|v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}
, plus a second-order remainder controlled by (
7
).

4
Debiased Estimation and Inference

We now use the efficient influence function derived in the previous section to construct a
\sqrt{N}
-consistent estimator for
\Psi_{\omega}(P)
. The construction follows the standard cross-fitting principle: estimate the nuisance functions on one fold, evaluate the orthogonal score on the held-out fold, and average
[
8
,
42
,
11
]
.

Let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
be two independent folds of i.i.d. observations from
P
, each of size
n
, and set
N=2n
. Write
P_{n,r}
for the empirical measure on
S_{r}
,
r=1,2
. For each fold
r
, let
\hat{\eta}_{\omega}^{(-r)}=(\hat{h}_{\omega}^{(-r)},\hat{j}_{\omega}^{(-r)},\hat{\alpha}_{1,\omega}^{(-r)},\hat{\alpha}_{2,\omega}^{(-r)})
denote nuisance estimates trained on the opposite fold
S_{3-r}
, where
\hat{j}_{\omega}^{(-r)}=(\hat{j}_{1,\omega}^{(-r)},\ldots,\hat{j}_{d,\omega}^{(-r)})\in L^{2}(P_{X})^{d}
and
\hat{\alpha}_{1,\omega}^{(-r)}=(\hat{\alpha}_{1,1,\omega}^{(-r)},\ldots,\hat{\alpha}_{1,d,\omega}^{(-r)})\in L^{2}(P_{X})^{d}
. For a nuisance tuple
\eta=(h,j,m)
with
j=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{d})\in L^{2}(P_{X})^{d}
and
m\in L^{2}(P_{X})
an estimate of the regression function
m^{\star}_{P}(X)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{P}[Y\mid X]
, define the pseudo-outcome

\varphi_{k,\omega}(O;\eta)\coloneqq\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{out}}_{\omega}(h)(O)(j_{k})-\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(O)(\alpha_{1,k})-\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(O)(\alpha_{2})-\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(O)(j_{k},\alpha_{2}),

where
\alpha_{1,k}=-j_{k}
and
\alpha_{2}=-(m-h)
follow from the quadratic structure. Under the quadratic specialization (
1
), this reduces to the explicit form

\varphi_{k,\omega}(O;\eta)=-\langle Y-g_{\omega}(Z),j_{k}(X)\rangle-\langle\partial_{\omega_{k}}g_{\omega}(Z),m(X)-h(X)\rangle+\langle j_{k}(X),m(X)-h(X)\rangle.

(8)

The pseudo-outcome combines a plug-in score
-\langle Y-g_{\omega}(Z),j_{k}(X)\rangle
with two bias-correction terms that vanish at the oracle nuisances
h=h^{\star}_{\omega,P}
,
j_{k}=j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}
, and
m=m^{\star}_{P}
. Indeed, at the oracle nuisance tuple
\eta^{\star}_{\omega}=(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{\omega,P},m^{\star}_{P})
,
\mathbb{E}_{P}[\varphi_{k,\omega}(O;\eta^{\star}_{\omega})]=\Psi_{k,\omega}(P)
exactly. The nuisance estimates trained on fold
S_{3-r}
are therefore
\hat{\eta}_{\omega}^{(-r)}=(\hat{h}_{\omega}^{(-r)},\hat{j}_{\omega}^{(-r)},\hat{m}^{(-r)})
, where
\hat{m}^{(-r)}
is an estimate of
m^{\star}_{P}
trained on
S_{3-r}
. Let
\varphi_{\omega}(O;\eta)=(\varphi_{1,\omega}(O;\eta),\ldots,\varphi_{d,\omega}(O;\eta))^{\top}
. Consequently, the two-fold cross-fitted doubly robust estimator is

\widehat{\Psi}^{\,DR}_{\omega}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{2}P_{n,r}\,\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\hat{\eta}_{\omega}^{(-r)}).

(9)

For any nuisance tuple
\eta
, define the centered score

\chi_{\eta,\omega}(O)\coloneqq\varphi_{\omega}(O;\eta)-P\,\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\eta),

so that
\chi_{P,\omega}:=\chi_{\eta^{\star}_{\omega},\omega}
is the oracle efficient influence function from Theorem
3.1
. The centering by
P
is used only for the analysis; the estimator (
9
) is fully empirical. Since
\widehat{\Psi}^{\,DR}_{\omega}
is an empirical average of the pseudo-outcome (
8
), its asymptotic distribution is governed by the oracle efficient influence function
\chi_{P,\omega}
via a central limit theorem. For this to hold, the estimated score
\chi_{\hat{\eta}_{\omega}^{(-r)},\omega}
must be close enough to the oracle
\chi_{P,\omega}
, and the nuisance estimation errors must vanish at a sufficient rate. The following two assumptions formalize these requirements.

Assumption C.

(Nuisance rates)
For
r=1,2
,

\|\hat{h}_{\omega}^{(-r)}-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}=o_{p}(N^{-1/4}),\quad\|\hat{j}_{\omega}^{(-r)}-j^{\star}_{\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})^{d}}=o_{p}(N^{-1/4}),\quad\|\hat{m}^{(-r)}-m^{\star}_{P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}=o_{p}(N^{-1/4}).

These rates can be verified by standard least-squares regression arguments under appropriate approximation and entropy or Rademacher-complexity conditions; see Appendix
E.1
.

Remark 4.1

(Score stability)
.

Under the quadratic specialization (
1
) and
B
, the score stability condition
\|\chi_{\hat{\eta}_{\omega}^{(-r)},\omega}-\chi_{P,\omega}\|_{L^{2}(P)}=o_{p}(1)
is implied by
C
. Indeed, the explicit form (
8
) shows that the score difference
\chi_{\hat{\eta}_{\omega}^{(-r)},\omega}-\chi_{P,\omega}
is a sum of inner products involving the nuisance errors
\hat{h}_{\omega}^{(-r)}-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}
,
\hat{j}_{\omega}^{(-r)}-j^{\star}_{\omega,P}
, and
\hat{m}^{(-r)}-m^{\star}_{P}
. By Cauchy-Schwarz and the almost sure bounds of
B
, each term is bounded in
L^{2}(P)
by a finite constant times one of these errors, which are all
o_{p}(N^{-1/4})=o_{p}(1)
by
C
.

Let
\Sigma_{P,\omega}\coloneqq P[\chi_{P,\omega}\chi_{P,\omega}^{\top}]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
denote the covariance matrix of the oracle efficient influence function. The following theorem provides the asymptotic normality of the cross-fitted debiased estimator.

Theorem 4.2

(Asymptotic normality of the cross-fitted debiased estimator)
.

Under
B
and
C
,

\sqrt{N}\left(\widehat{\Psi}^{\,DR}_{\omega}-\Psi_{\omega}(P)\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{r=1}^{2}\sum_{O_{i}\in S_{r}}\chi_{P,\omega}(O_{i})+o_{p}(1).

(10)

Consequently,

\sqrt{N}\left(\widehat{\Psi}^{\,DR}_{\omega}-\Psi_{\omega}(P)\right)\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{P,\omega}).

The proof of Theorem
4.2
is given in Appendix
C
. The expansion separates the statistical error into three parts: the oracle empirical average in (
10
), a stochastic equicontinuity term, and a population bias remainder controlled by Assumption
C
. The correction terms make the estimating function first-order insensitive to nuisance perturbations at the oracle nuisances, so only product-rate and quadratic remainders remain.

Confidence intervals and gradient certificates.

The asymptotic covariance
\Sigma_{P,\omega}
is estimated by the empirical covariance of the cross-fitted pseudo-outcomes:

\widehat{\Sigma}_{P,\omega}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{2}P_{n,r}\left[\left(\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\hat{\eta}_{\omega}^{(-r)})-\widehat{\Psi}^{\,DR}_{\omega}\right)\left(\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\hat{\eta}_{\omega}^{(-r)})-\widehat{\Psi}^{\,DR}_{\omega}\right)^{\top}\right].

Under
\widehat{\Sigma}_{P,\omega}\xrightarrow{p}\Sigma_{P,\omega}
, an asymptotically valid
(1-\alpha)
Wald confidence interval for the
k
-th coordinate of
\Psi_{\omega}(P)
is

\left[\widehat{\Psi}^{\,DR}_{\omega,k}\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\widehat{\Sigma}_{P,\omega,kk}}{N}}\right],

(11)

where
z_{1-\alpha/2}
is the
(1-\alpha/2)
quantile of the standard normal distribution. Beyond pointwise inference, these intervals provide uncertainty quantification for the bilevel gradient coordinates themselves: they can be used to certify whether a candidate point is statistically distinguishable from stationarity, or to assess whether an apparent descent direction exceeds the sampling noise, in the spirit of inexact-gradient optimization
[
47
]
.

We now control the debiased gradient uniformly over
\omega\in\Omega

\Omega

[

\hat{h}_{\omega}

\widehat{\Psi}_{\omega,2}^{DR}\coloneqq P_{n,2}\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\hat{h}_{\omega},\hat{j}_{\omega},\hat{m}).

(12)

Decomposition.

Adding and subtracting the oracle pseudo-outcome yields

\displaystyle\widehat{\Psi}_{\omega,2}^{DR}-\Psi_{\omega}(P)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{(P_{n,2}-P)\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{\omega,P},m^{\star}_{P})}_{\text{oracle empirical process}}

(13)

\displaystyle+\underbrace{(P_{n,2}-P)\left[\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\hat{h}_{\omega},\hat{j}_{\omega},\hat{m})-\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{\omega,P},m^{\star}_{P})\right]}_{\text{nuisance empirical process}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{P\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\hat{h}_{\omega},\hat{j}_{\omega},\hat{m})-\Psi_{\omega}(P)}_{\text{population bias}}.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{\rm tr}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})

Empirical process complexity.

Since
\widehat{\Psi}_{\omega,2}^{DR}-\Psi_{\omega}(P)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mathbb{R}^{d}

(\omega,u)\in\Omega\times S^{d-1}

\mathcal{F}_{A}\coloneqq\left\{u^{\top}\left(\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{\omega,P},m^{\star}_{P})-\Psi_{\omega}(P)\right):\omega\in\Omega,\ u\in S^{d-1}\right\}.

To control the nuisance empirical process, we require the learned nuisance paths to lie in well-controlled function classes, which we now formalize.

Assumption D.

(Training-conditional nuisance classes)Conditionally on
S_{1}

\mathcal{H}_{n}\subset L^{2}(P_{X})
,
\mathcal{J}_{n}\subset L^{2}(P_{X})^{d}

\{\hat{j}_{\omega}:\omega\in\Omega\}\subset\mathcal{J}_{n}

\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\|h(X)\|\leq\bar{H}_{n},\qquad\sup_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|j_{k}(X)\|^{2}\leq\bar{J}_{n}^{2},\qquad\sup_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\|m(X)\|\leq\bar{M}_{n}.

Kernel ridge regression, sieve estimators, and norm-controlled neural networks satisfy assumptions of this form under the usual envelope and entropy controls; representative sufficient conditions are collected in Appendix

The nuisance empirical process is then controlled by the localized nuisance-difference class

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{B,n}^{\rm loc}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})\coloneqq\bigl\{

(14)

\displaystyle\omega\in\Omega,\ u\in S^{d-1},\ h\in\mathcal{H}_{n},\ j\in\mathcal{J}_{n},\ m\in\mathcal{M}_{n},

which localizes the nuisance functions to a ball of radius
(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})
around the oracle, conditionally on
S_{1}

\mathfrak{C}_{B,n}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{B,n}^{\rm loc}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})}|(P_{n,2}-P)f|\,\middle|\,S_{1}\right].

(15)

Assumption E.

(Entropy of population classes)
Define the population function classes indexed by

Under

(Polynomial-entropy instantiation)

Under the conditions of Theorem

(17)

Optimization with the debiased gradient oracle.

The uniform bound of Corollary
5.2
has a direct optimization consequence. By the triangle inequality, if an algorithm returns
\widehat{\omega}\in\Omega
with
\|\widehat{\Psi}_{\widehat{\omega},2}^{DR}\|\leq\tau_{N}

\|\Psi_{\widehat{\omega}}(P)\|\leq\tau_{N}+\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\|\widehat{\Psi}_{\omega,2}^{DR}-\Psi_{\omega}(P)\|.

(18)

Thus empirical stationarity for the debiased gradient implies population stationarity up to the uniform statistical error. This is the same role played by deterministic error control in inexact-gradient methods
[
47
]

D.4

7
Conclusion

We studied the population bilevel gradient
\Psi_{\omega}(P)
as a statistical functional and used the efficient influence function to construct OBiGrad, a cross-fitted orthogonal estimator that removes first-order nuisance bias via the efficient influence function correction (
5
). The experiments show that plug-in hypergradients retain first-order nuisance bias, as identified by the von Mises expansion (
3.2
), while fixed-regularization kernel methods target a regularized gradient
\Psi_{\omega,\lambda}(P)
rather than
\Psi_{\omega}(P)
; OBiGrad instead estimates the unregularized population gradient directly. Limitations remain: our guarantees concern gradient estimation and approximate stationarity, not a full end-to-end analysis of arbitrary bilevel algorithms, and they rely on nuisance learners satisfying the consistency, product-rate, and entropy conditions of
C
and
F
. Verifying these conditions for highly adaptive neural learners and extending the method to infinite-horizon reinforcement learning, meta-learning, and other structured bilevel problems remain natural next steps.

Appendix A
Efficient Influence Function

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Fix
k\in\{1,\dots,d\}
. Let
(P_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
be a regular parametric submodel through
P
with score
S\in L_{0}^{2}(P)
, meaning that
S
satisfies
PS=0
and
PS^{2}<\infty
. Recall the score identity: for any square-integrable function
f
,

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}P_{\epsilon}f\right|_{\epsilon=0}=P[fS],

which follows by differentiating under the integral sign. Since
P_{\epsilon}
is a probability distribution for all
\epsilon
, differentiating
\int\mathrm{d}P_{\epsilon}=1
at
\epsilon=0
gives
PS=0
, so the score is always mean-zero under
P
.

Our goal is to find
\chi_{k,P,\omega}\in L^{2}_{0}(P)
such that

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\Psi_{k,\omega}(P_{\epsilon})\right|_{\epsilon=0}=\langle\chi_{k,P,\omega},S\rangle_{L^{2}(P)},\qquad\forall S\in L^{2}_{0}(P).

In the nonparametric model, the tangent space is all of
L^{2}_{0}(P)
, so this condition uniquely determines
\chi_{k,P,\omega}
, which is the efficient influence function.

Decomposition.

For any
h,v\in L^{2}(P_{X})
, define
\Gamma_{k,\omega}(h,v)\coloneqq\partial_{h}\ell^{out}_{\omega}(h)(v)
. We differentiate

\Psi_{k,\omega}(P_{\epsilon})=P_{\epsilon}\,\Gamma_{k,\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P_{\epsilon}},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P_{\epsilon}}).

There are three sources of
\epsilon
-dependence: the measure
P_{\epsilon}
, the inner solution
h^{\star}_{\omega,P_{\epsilon}}
, and the Jacobian
j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P_{\epsilon}}
. Denote their derivatives at
\epsilon=0
by

\dot{h}\coloneqq\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}h^{\star}_{\omega,P_{\epsilon}}\right|_{\epsilon=0},\qquad\dot{j}_{k}\coloneqq\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P_{\epsilon}}\right|_{\epsilon=0}.

By the chain rule,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\Psi_{k,\omega}(P_{\epsilon})\right|_{\epsilon=0}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}P_{\epsilon}\,\Gamma_{k,\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})\right|_{\epsilon=0}}_{\text{(A): measure perturbation}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{P\,\partial_{1}\Gamma_{k,\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})[\dot{h}]}_{\text{(B): effect on }h^{\star}_{\omega,P_{\epsilon}}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{P\,\partial_{2}\Gamma_{k,\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})[\dot{j}_{k}]}_{\text{(C): effect on }j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P_{\epsilon}}}.

Term (A): measure perturbation.

By the score identity,

A=\left\langle\Gamma_{k,\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}),S\right\rangle_{L^{2}(P)}.

This is the plug-in term: the direct contribution from the measure shifting.

Term (B): effect of
h^{\star}_{\omega,P_{\epsilon}}
changing.

Since
h^{\star}_{\omega,P_{\epsilon}}
minimizes
P_{\epsilon}\,\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)
for all
\epsilon
, it satisfies the first-order condition

P_{\epsilon}\,\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P_{\epsilon}})(g)=0,\qquad\forall g\in L^{2}(P_{X}).

Differentiating this identity with respect to
\epsilon
at
\epsilon=0
, and applying the score identity to the
P_{\epsilon}
-dependence and the chain rule to the
h^{\star}_{\omega,P_{\epsilon}}
-dependence, gives

\left\langle\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(g),S\right\rangle_{L^{2}(P)}+P\,\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(\dot{h},g)=0.

This expresses
P\,\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(\dot{h},g)
as a score inner product. To connect this to term (B), we use the Riesz representer
\alpha_{1,k,P}
, defined by

P\,\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(\alpha_{1,k,P},u)=P\,\partial_{1}\Gamma_{k,\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})[u],\qquad\forall u\in L^{2}(P_{X}).

Setting
g=\alpha_{1,k,P}
in the differentiated first-order condition and using symmetry of the Hessian bilinear form,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=P\,\partial_{1}\Gamma_{k,\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})[\dot{h}]

\displaystyle=P\,\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(\alpha_{1,k,P},\dot{h})

\displaystyle=P\,\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(\dot{h},\alpha_{1,k,P})

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(\alpha_{1,k,P}),S\right\rangle_{L^{2}(P)}.

Term (C): effect of
j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P_{\epsilon}}
changing.

The first-order condition holds along the submodel for all
\epsilon
:

P_{\epsilon}\,\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P_{\epsilon}})(g)=0,\qquad\forall g\in L^{2}(P_{X}).

Differentiating with respect to
\omega_{k}
at the fixed
P
, and using the chain rule with
j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}=D_{\omega_{k}}h^{\star}_{\omega,P}
, gives

P\,\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(g)+P\,\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P},g)=0,\qquad\forall g\in L^{2}(P_{X}).

Now differentiate this identity along
P_{\epsilon}
at
\epsilon=0
. There are three sources of
\epsilon
-dependence:
P_{\epsilon}
,
h^{\star}_{\omega,P_{\epsilon}}
, and
j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P_{\epsilon}}
. The third-order derivative terms arising from differentiating through
h^{\star}_{\omega,P_{\epsilon}}
vanish identically under the quadratic inner loss, since
\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}
is quadratic in
h
and all its derivatives of order three or higher are zero. Applying the score identity to the
P_{\epsilon}
-dependence and the chain rule to
j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P_{\epsilon}}
, we obtain

\left\langle\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(g),S\right\rangle_{L^{2}(P)}+\left\langle\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P},g),S\right\rangle_{L^{2}(P)}+P\,\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(\dot{j}_{k},g)=0.

This expresses
P\,\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(\dot{j}_{k},g)
as a score inner product. Setting
g=\alpha_{2,P}
and using symmetry of the Hessian,

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=P\,\partial_{2}\Gamma_{k,\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})[\dot{j}_{k}]

\displaystyle=P\,\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(\alpha_{2,P},\dot{j}_{k})

\displaystyle=P\,\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(\dot{j}_{k},\alpha_{2,P})

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(\alpha_{2,P})+\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P},\alpha_{2,P}),S\right\rangle_{L^{2}(P)}.

Combining and centering.

Adding terms (A), (B), and (C),

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\Psi_{k,\omega}(P_{\epsilon})\right|_{\epsilon=0}=\langle\tilde{\chi}_{k,P,\omega},S\rangle_{L^{2}(P)},

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\chi}_{k,P,\omega}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{k,\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})-\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(\alpha_{1,k,P})

\displaystyle-\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(\alpha_{2,P})-\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P},\alpha_{2,P}).

Since
PS=0
, we may subtract any constant from
\tilde{\chi}_{k,P,\omega}
without changing the inner product with
S
. Subtracting
\Psi_{k,\omega}(P)
centers the function and ensures
P\chi_{k,P,\omega}=0
, giving the canonical gradient

\displaystyle\chi_{k,P,\omega}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{k,\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})-\Psi_{k,\omega}(P)-\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(\alpha_{1,k,P})

\displaystyle-\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(\alpha_{2,P})-\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P},\alpha_{2,P}).

By Assumption
B
,
\chi_{k,P,\omega}\in L^{2}_{0}(P)
. Since the nonparametric tangent space is all of
L^{2}_{0}(P)
, the identity
\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\Psi_{k,\omega}(P_{\epsilon})\right|_{\epsilon=0}=\langle\chi_{k,P,\omega},S\rangle_{L^{2}(P)}
holds for every
S\in L^{2}_{0}(P)
, and
\chi_{k,P,\omega}
is the unique element of
L^{2}_{0}(P)
satisfying this, hence it is the efficient influence function. Stacking coordinates,
\chi_{P,\omega}=(\chi_{1,P,\omega},\ldots,\chi_{d,P,\omega})^{\top}
is the vector-valued efficient influence function for
\Psi_{\omega}(P)
.
∎

Appendix B
Functional von Mises Expansion

Proposition B.1
.

Under the quadratic specialization (
1
), for each
k=1,\ldots,d
, the map
(h,v)\mapsto\psi_{k,P}(h,v)
is Fréchet differentiable on all of
L^{2}(P_{X})\times L^{2}(P_{X})
, with globally Lipschitz Fréchet derivative of constant
1
.

Proof.

Fix
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. Under the quadratic outer loss
\ell^{\mathrm{out}}_{\omega}(h)(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\|Y-h(X)\|^{2}
, we have

\psi_{k,P}(h,v)=P\,\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{out}}_{\omega}(h)(v)=-\mathbb{E}_{P}[\langle Y-h(X),v(X)\rangle].

We can write this as

\psi_{k,P}(h,v)=-\mathbb{E}_{P}[\langle Y,v(X)\rangle]+\mathbb{E}_{P}[\langle h(X),v(X)\rangle],

which is affine in
h
and linear in
v
.

Fréchet differentiability.

For any
\delta h\in L^{2}(P_{X})
,

\psi_{k,P}(h+\delta h,v)-\psi_{k,P}(h,v)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[\langle\delta h(X),v(X)\rangle]\eqqcolon\partial_{1}\psi_{k,P}(h,v)[\delta h],

with remainder zero, so
\psi_{k,P}
is exactly linear in
h
. The functional
\partial_{1}\psi_{k,P}(h,v)
is bounded since by Cauchy-Schwarz,

|\partial_{1}\psi_{k,P}(h,v)[\delta h]|\leq\|v\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}\|\delta h\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}.

For any
\delta v\in L^{2}(P_{X})
,

\psi_{k,P}(h,v+\delta v)-\psi_{k,P}(h,v)=-\mathbb{E}_{P}[\langle Y-h(X),\delta v(X)\rangle]\eqqcolon\partial_{2}\psi_{k,P}(h,v)[\delta v],

with remainder zero, so
\psi_{k,P}
is exactly linear in
v
. The functional
\partial_{2}\psi_{k,P}(h,v)
is bounded since by Cauchy-Schwarz,

|\partial_{2}\psi_{k,P}(h,v)[\delta v]|\leq\|Y-h(X)\|_{L^{2}(P)}\|\delta v\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}.

Since both partial Fréchet derivatives exist and are bounded,
\psi_{k,P}
is Fréchet differentiable on all of
L^{2}(P_{X})\times L^{2}(P_{X})
.

Lipschitz derivative.

For any
(h_{1},v_{1}),(h_{2},v_{2})\in L^{2}(P_{X})\times L^{2}(P_{X})
, by Cauchy-Schwarz,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{1}\psi_{k,P}(h_{1},v_{1})-\partial_{1}\psi_{k,P}(h_{2},v_{2})\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{2}\psi_{k,P}(h_{1},v_{1})-\partial_{2}\psi_{k,P}(h_{2},v_{2})\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\|h_{1}-h_{2}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}.

Combining,

\|D\psi_{k,P}(h_{1},v_{1})-D\psi_{k,P}(h_{2},v_{2})\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\|(h_{1}-h_{2},v_{1}-v_{2})\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})\times L^{2}(P_{X})},

so the Fréchet derivative is globally Lipschitz with constant
1
.
∎

Lemma B.2

(Coordinatewise Taylor expansion)
.

Fix
k\in\{1,\dots,d\}
. Under the quadratic specialization (
1
), for all
h,v\in L^{2}(P_{X})
,

\displaystyle\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P}+h,j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}+v)-\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})[h]+\partial_{2}\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})[v]+\mathrm{Rem}_{k}(h,v),

where

|\mathrm{Rem}_{k}(h,v)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|h\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}^{2}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}^{2}\right).

Proof.

Let
z^{\star}=(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})
and
r=(h,v)
. By Proposition
B.1
,
\psi_{k,P}
is Fréchet differentiable on all of
L^{2}(P_{X})\times L^{2}(P_{X})
with globally Lipschitz derivative of constant
1
. By the integral form of Taylor’s theorem,

\psi_{k,P}(z^{\star}+r)-\psi_{k,P}(z^{\star})=\int_{0}^{1}D\psi_{k,P}(z^{\star}+tr)[r]\,\mathrm{d}t.

Adding and subtracting
D\psi_{k,P}(z^{\star})[r]
gives

\psi_{k,P}(z^{\star}+r)-\psi_{k,P}(z^{\star})=D\psi_{k,P}(z^{\star})[r]+\int_{0}^{1}\left\{D\psi_{k,P}(z^{\star}+tr)-D\psi_{k,P}(z^{\star})\right\}[r]\,\mathrm{d}t,

where the first term equals

D\psi_{k,P}(z^{\star})[r]=\partial_{1}\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})[h]+\partial_{2}\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})[v].

For the remainder, the Lipschitz bound from Proposition
B.1
gives

\left|\int_{0}^{1}\left\{D\psi_{k,P}(z^{\star}+tr)-D\psi_{k,P}(z^{\star})\right\}[r]\,\mathrm{d}t\right|\leq\int_{0}^{1}\|D\psi_{k,P}(z^{\star}+tr)-D\psi_{k,P}(z^{\star})\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|r\|\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{1}t\|r\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{2}\|r\|^{2},

so

|\mathrm{Rem}_{k}(h,v)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|(h,v)\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})\times L^{2}(P_{X})}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|h\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}^{2}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}^{2}\right).

∎

Lemma B.3
.

Under the quadratic inner-loss specialization (
1
), for all
h,f,g\in L^{2}(P_{X})
,

P\,\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h+f)(g)=P\,\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(g)+P\,\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(f,g).

Proof.

This follows immediately from the fact that
\ell^{in}_{\omega}
is quadratic in
h
.
∎

Lemma B.4
.

Under the quadratic inner-loss specialization (
1
), for all
h,f,g\in L^{2}(P_{X})
and all
k=1,\dots,d
,

P\,\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h+f)(g)=P\,\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(g).

Proof.

Under the quadratic inner loss, the mixed derivative is

\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(g)(X,Z)=-\langle\partial_{\omega_{k}}g_{\omega}(Z),g(X)\rangle,

which does not depend on
h
. Hence
\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h+f)(g)=\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(g)
.
∎

We now prove Theorem
3.2
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Fix
k\in\{1,\dots,d\}
.

Step 1: First-order expansion of
\psi_{k,P}
.

By Lemma
B.2
, applied at the base point
(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})
,

\displaystyle\psi_{k,P}(h,v)-\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})[h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}]+\partial_{2}\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})[v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}]+\mathrm{Rem}_{k}(h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P},v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}),

where

|\mathrm{Rem}_{k}(h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P},v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})|\leq\frac{1}{2}(\|h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}^{2}+\|v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}^{2}).

By the definitions of
\alpha_{1,k,P}
and
\alpha_{2,P}
, the first-order terms equal
\langle\alpha_{1,k,P},h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}
and
\langle\alpha_{2,P},v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}
respectively, so

\displaystyle\psi_{k,P}(h,v)-\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})

(19)

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha_{1,k,P},h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}+\langle\alpha_{2,P},v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}+\mathrm{Rem}_{k}(h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P},v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}).

Step 2: Expanding the correction terms.

We now express each correction term on the left-hand side of (
3.2
) in terms of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}
.

First correction:
P\,\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(\alpha_{1,k})
.

By Lemma
B.3
,

P\,\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(\alpha_{1,k})=P\,\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(\alpha_{1,k})+\langle h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P},\alpha_{1,k}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}.

Since
h^{\star}_{\omega,P}
satisfies the first-order condition, the first term vanishes, giving

P\,\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(\alpha_{1,k})=\langle h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P},\alpha_{1,k}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}.

(20)

Second and third corrections:
P\,\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(\alpha_{2})
and
P\,\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(v,\alpha_{2})
.

By Lemma
B.4
, the mixed derivative does not depend on

, so

P\,\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(\alpha_{2})=P\,\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(\alpha_{2}).

Since

Differentiating the first-order condition with respect to

P\,\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P})(u)+\langle j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P},u\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}=0,\qquad\forall u\in L^{2}(P_{X}).

Taking
u=\alpha_{2}
and combining the two corrections,

P\,\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(\alpha_{2})+P\,\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(v,\alpha_{2})=\langle v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P},\alpha_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}.

(21)

Step 3: Assembling the expansion.

Subtracting (
20
) and (
21
) from (

), and using symmetry of

\displaystyle\psi_{k,P}(h,v)-\psi_{k,P}(h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})-P\,\partial_{h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(\alpha_{1,k})-P\,\partial^{2}_{\omega_{k},h}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(\alpha_{2})-P\,\partial_{h}^{2}\ell^{\mathrm{in}}_{\omega}(h)(v,\alpha_{2})

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha_{1,k,P}-\alpha_{1,k},h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}+\langle\alpha_{2,P}-\alpha_{2},v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}+\mathrm{Rem}_{k}(h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P},v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}).

Appendix C
Asymptotic Normality

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

For
r=1,2
, write
\hat{\eta}_{r}\coloneqq\hat{\eta}_{\omega}^{(-r)}
for brevity. By definition of the two-fold estimator and since
P\,\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\eta^{\star}_{\omega})=\Psi_{\omega}(P)
,

\widehat{\Psi}^{\,DR}_{\omega}-\Psi_{\omega}(P)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{2}\left\{P_{n,r}\,\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\hat{\eta}_{r})-P\,\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\eta^{\star}_{\omega})\right\}.

Adding and subtracting
P\,\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\hat{\eta}_{r})
and
P_{n,r}\,\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\eta^{\star}_{\omega})
for each fold
r
yields the decomposition

\widehat{\Psi}^{\,DR}_{\omega}-\Psi_{\omega}(P)=A_{N}+B_{N}+R_{N},

where

\displaystyle A_{N}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{2}(P_{n,r}-P)\chi_{P,\omega},

\displaystyle B_{N}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{2}(P_{n,r}-P)\left(\chi_{\hat{\eta}_{r},\omega}-\chi_{P,\omega}\right),

\displaystyle R_{N}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{2}\left\{P\,\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\hat{\eta}_{r})-P\,\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\eta^{\star}_{\omega})\right\}.

We show that
\sqrt{N}\,A_{N}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{P,\omega})
and
B_{N},R_{N}=o_{p}(N^{-1/2})
.

Term
A_{N}
.

Since the two folds are independent and each has size
n=N/2
,

A_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{r=1}^{2}\sum_{O_{i}\in S_{r}}\chi_{P,\omega}(O_{i}).

Under
B
, each term of the efficient influence function (
5
) is square-integrable. Indeed, the almost sure bounds
\|Y\|\leq A
,
\sup_{\omega}\|g_{\omega}(Z)\|\leq B
, and
\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|\partial_{\omega_{k}}g_{\omega}(Z)\|^{2}\leq D
imply that
h^{\star}_{\omega,P}
,
j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}
, and
\alpha_{1,k,P}=-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}
are all bounded in
L^{2}(P_{X})
, while
\alpha_{2,P}=-a^{\star}_{\omega,P}\in L^{2}(P_{X})
follows from the adjoint equation (
2
). Together these imply
P\|\chi_{P,\omega}\|_{2}^{2}<\infty
. Therefore the multivariate central limit theorem gives

\sqrt{N}\,A_{N}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{r=1}^{2}\sum_{O_{i}\in S_{r}}\chi_{P,\omega}(O_{i})\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{P,\omega}).

Term
B_{N}
.

Fix
r\in\{1,2\}
and condition on the opposite fold
S_{3-r}
, so that
\hat{\eta}_{r}
is fixed and
S_{r}
is independent of
S_{3-r}
. Since both scores are mean-zero under
P
,

P\left[\chi_{\hat{\eta}_{r},\omega}-\chi_{P,\omega}\right]=0.

By the conditional variance bound,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\sqrt{n}(P_{n,r}-P)\left(\chi_{\hat{\eta}_{r},\omega}-\chi_{P,\omega}\right)\right\|^{2}\,\middle|\,S_{3-r}\right]\leq\left\|\chi_{\hat{\eta}_{r},\omega}-\chi_{P,\omega}\right\|_{L^{2}(P)}^{2}=o_{p}(1),

where the last equality follows from Remark
4.1
. Therefore
(P_{n,r}-P)(\chi_{\hat{\eta}_{r},\omega}-\chi_{P,\omega})=o_{p}(n^{-1/2})
, and summing over both folds gives
B_{N}=o_{p}(N^{-1/2})
.

Term
R_{N}
.

We decompose the population remainder coordinatewise. For each
r=1,2
and
k=1,\dots,d
, Theorem
3.2
gives

P\,\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\hat{\eta}_{r})-P\,\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\eta^{\star}_{\omega})=C_{r}+D_{r}+E_{r},

where
C_{r}=(C_{r,1},\dots,C_{r,d})^{\top}
,
D_{r}=(D_{r,1},\dots,D_{r,d})^{\top}
,
E_{r}=(E_{r,1},\dots,E_{r,d})^{\top}
, and

\displaystyle C_{r,k}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\langle\alpha_{1,k,P}-\hat{\alpha}_{1,k,\omega}^{(-r)},\hat{h}_{\omega}^{(-r)}-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})},

\displaystyle D_{r,k}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\langle\alpha_{2,P}-\hat{\alpha}_{2,\omega}^{(-r)},\hat{j}_{k,\omega}^{(-r)}-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})},

\displaystyle E_{r,k}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathrm{Rem}_{k}\left(\hat{h}_{\omega}^{(-r)}-h^{\star}_{\omega,P},\hat{j}_{k,\omega}^{(-r)}-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\right).

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\|C_{r}\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\hat{\alpha}_{1,\omega}^{(-r)}-\alpha_{1,P}\right\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})^{d}}\left\|\hat{h}_{\omega}^{(-r)}-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\right\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}=o_{p}(N^{-1/2}),

\displaystyle\|D_{r}\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\hat{\alpha}_{2,\omega}^{(-r)}-\alpha_{2,P}\right\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}\left\|\hat{j}_{\omega}^{(-r)}-j^{\star}_{\omega,P}\right\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})^{d}}=o_{p}(N^{-1/2}),

by the first two lines of Assumption
C
. For the remainder, Lemma
B.2
gives

\|E_{r}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|\hat{h}_{\omega}^{(-r)}-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\right\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}^{2}+\left\|\hat{j}_{\omega}^{(-r)}-j^{\star}_{\omega,P}\right\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})^{d}}^{2}\right)=o_{p}(N^{-1/2}),

by the third line of Assumption
C
. Summing over both folds gives
R_{N}=o_{p}(N^{-1/2})
.

Conclusion.

Combining the three terms,

\sqrt{N}\left(\widehat{\Psi}^{\,DR}_{\omega}-\Psi_{\omega}(P)\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{r=1}^{2}\sum_{O_{i}\in S_{r}}\chi_{P,\omega}(O_{i})+o_{p}(1).

The central limit theorem applied to
A_{N}
and Slutsky’s theorem give

\sqrt{N}\left(\widehat{\Psi}^{\,DR}_{\omega}-\Psi_{\omega}(P)\right)\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{P,\omega}).

∎

D.1
Auxiliary empirical-process lemmas

Throughout this subsection, write
\|f\|_{Q,2}\coloneqq(\int\|f\|^{2}\,dQ)^{1/2}
for the
L^{2}(Q)
norm of a function
f
, and
N(\delta,\mathcal{C},L^{2}(Q))
for the
\delta
-covering number of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to the
L^{2}(Q)
metric,
i.e.
, the minimum number of
L^{2}(Q)
-balls of radius
\delta
needed to cover
\mathcal{C}
. Its logarithm
\log N(\delta,\mathcal{C},L^{2}(Q))
is called the metric entropy of
\mathcal{C}
. For a class
\mathcal{C}
with envelope
C
satisfying
\sup_{f\in\mathcal{C}}\|f\|\leq C
pointwise, define

\bar{H}(\varepsilon;\mathcal{C},C)\coloneqq\sup_{Q}\log N(\varepsilon\|C\|_{Q,2},\mathcal{C},L^{2}(Q)),\qquad 0<\varepsilon\leq 1,

where the supremum is over all finitely supported probability measures
Q
.

Lemma D.1

(Entropy of sums and differences)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
be classes of measurable functions taking values in the same finite-dimensional normed space, with nonnegative envelopes
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
. Then
C_{1}+C_{2}
is an envelope for
\mathcal{C}_{1}\pm\mathcal{C}_{2}=\{f_{1}\pm f_{2}:f_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{1},\ f_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}\}
, and

\bar{H}(\varepsilon;\mathcal{C}_{1}\pm\mathcal{C}_{2},C_{1}+C_{2})\leq\bar{H}(\varepsilon/2;\mathcal{C}_{1},C_{1})+\bar{H}(\varepsilon/2;\mathcal{C}_{2},C_{2}).

Proof.

Fix
Q
. Let
\{f_{1,i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{1}}
and
\{f_{2,j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{2}}
be minimal
L^{2}(Q)
-covers of
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
at radii
(\varepsilon/2)\|C_{1}\|_{Q,2}
and
(\varepsilon/2)\|C_{2}\|_{Q,2}
, respectively, so that

N_{1}=N\!\left(\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\|C_{1}\|_{Q,2},\mathcal{C}_{1},L^{2}(Q)\right),\qquad N_{2}=N\!\left(\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\|C_{2}\|_{Q,2},\mathcal{C}_{2},L^{2}(Q)\right).

For any
f=f_{1}\pm f_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\pm\mathcal{C}_{2}
, find the closest cover elements
f_{1,i}
and
f_{2,j}
. By the triangle inequality,

\|f_{1}\pm f_{2}-(f_{1,i}\pm f_{2,j})\|_{Q,2}\leq\|f_{1}-f_{1,i}\|_{Q,2}+\|f_{2}-f_{2,j}\|_{Q,2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|C_{1}\|_{Q,2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|C_{2}\|_{Q,2}.

Since
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are nonnegative,
C_{1}+C_{2}\geq C_{1}
and
C_{1}+C_{2}\geq C_{2}
pointwise, so
\|C_{1}+C_{2}\|_{Q,2}\geq\|C_{1}\|_{Q,2}
and
\|C_{1}+C_{2}\|_{Q,2}\geq\|C_{2}\|_{Q,2}
. Adding these two inequalities gives

\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|C_{1}\|_{Q,2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|C_{2}\|_{Q,2}\leq\varepsilon\|C_{1}+C_{2}\|_{Q,2},

so
\{f_{1,i}\pm f_{2,j}\}
covers
\mathcal{C}_{1}\pm\mathcal{C}_{2}
at radius
\varepsilon\|C_{1}+C_{2}\|_{Q,2}
, confirming that
C_{1}+C_{2}
is an envelope for
\mathcal{C}_{1}\pm\mathcal{C}_{2}
. Since this cover has at most
N_{1}\cdot N_{2}
elements,

\log N\!\left(\varepsilon\|C_{1}+C_{2}\|_{Q,2},\mathcal{C}_{1}\pm\mathcal{C}_{2},L^{2}(Q)\right)\leq\log N_{1}+\log N_{2}.

Taking the supremum over all finitely supported
Q
on both sides gives

\bar{H}(\varepsilon;\mathcal{C}_{1}\pm\mathcal{C}_{2},C_{1}+C_{2})\leq\bar{H}(\varepsilon/2;\mathcal{C}_{1},C_{1})+\bar{H}(\varepsilon/2;\mathcal{C}_{2},C_{2}).

∎

Lemma D.2

(Entropy of bounded products)
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
be vector-valued function classes such that, for finite constants
\bar{U},\bar{V}
,
\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\|u(O)\|\leq\bar{U}
and
\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\|v(O)\|\leq\bar{V}
for all
O
. Define

\mathcal{U}\odot\mathcal{V}\coloneqq\{O\mapsto\langle u(O),v(O)\rangle:u\in\mathcal{U},\ v\in\mathcal{V}\}.

Then
\bar{U}\bar{V}
is an envelope for
\mathcal{U}\odot\mathcal{V}
, and for every
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\sup_{Q}\log N(\varepsilon\bar{U}\bar{V},\mathcal{U}\odot\mathcal{V},L^{2}(Q))

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{Q}\log N\!\left(\tfrac{\varepsilon\bar{U}}{2},\mathcal{U},L^{2}(Q)\right)+\sup_{Q}\log N\!\left(\tfrac{\varepsilon\bar{V}}{2},\mathcal{V},L^{2}(Q)\right).

Proof.

The envelope bound follows from Cauchy-Schwarz: for all
O
,

|\langle u(O),v(O)\rangle|\leq\|u(O)\|\|v(O)\|\leq\bar{U}\bar{V}.

For the entropy bound, fix
Q
and let
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{1}}
and
\{v_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{2}}
be minimal
L^{2}(Q)
-covers of
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
at radii
\varepsilon\bar{U}/2
and
\varepsilon\bar{V}/2
respectively. For any
u\in\mathcal{U}
and
v\in\mathcal{V}
, find the closest cover elements
u_{i}
and
v_{j}
. By the triangle inequality and the pointwise bounds,

\displaystyle\|\langle u,v\rangle-\langle u_{i},v_{j}\rangle\|_{Q,2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\langle u-u_{i},v\rangle\|_{Q,2}+\|\langle u_{i},v-v_{j}\rangle\|_{Q,2}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{V}\|u-u_{i}\|_{Q,2}+\bar{U}\|v-v_{j}\|_{Q,2}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{V}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon\bar{U}}{2}+\bar{U}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon\bar{V}}{2}=\varepsilon\bar{U}\bar{V}.

So
\{u_{i}\odot v_{j}\}
covers
\mathcal{U}\odot\mathcal{V}
at radius
\varepsilon\bar{U}\bar{V}
with at most
N_{1}\cdot N_{2}
elements. Taking logarithms and the supremum over
Q
gives the result.
∎

Lemma D.3

(Entropy of block linear combinations)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a class of block-vector-valued functions
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})
with values in
(\mathbb{R}^{q})^{d}
, equipped with the block norm
\|a(O)\|_{2,d}\coloneqq(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|a_{k}(O)\|^{2})^{1/2}
, and suppose
\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\|a(O)\|_{2,d}\leq\bar{A}
for all
O
. Define

\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{A})\coloneqq\left\{O\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{d}u_{k}a_{k}(O):a\in\mathcal{A},\ u\in S^{d-1}\right\}.

Then
\bar{A}
is an envelope for
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{A})
, and for every
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
,

\sup_{Q}\log N(\varepsilon\bar{A},\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{A}),L^{2}(Q))\leq d\log(1+4/\varepsilon)+\sup_{Q}\log N(\varepsilon\bar{A}/2,\mathcal{A},L^{2}(Q)).

Proof.

The envelope bound follows from Cauchy-Schwarz: for all
O
and
u\in S^{d-1}
,

\left|\sum_{k=1}^{d}u_{k}a_{k}(O)\right|\leq\|u\|_{2}\|a(O)\|_{2,d}\leq\bar{A}.

For the entropy bound, fix
Q
. Let
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{1}}
be a minimal
L^{2}(Q)
-cover of
\mathcal{A}
at radius
\varepsilon\bar{A}/2
in the block norm, and let
\{u_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N_{2}}
be an
(\varepsilon/2)
-cover of
S^{d-1}
in Euclidean norm, which exists with cardinality
N_{2}\leq(1+4/\varepsilon)^{d}
by a standard volumetric argument
[
57
]
. For any
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
u\in S^{d-1}
, choose the closest cover elements
a_{i}
and
u_{\ell}
. By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{d}u_{k}a_{k}-\sum_{k=1}^{d}(u_{\ell})_{k}(a_{i})_{k}\right\|_{Q,2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{d}u_{k}(a_{k}-(a_{i})_{k})\right\|_{Q,2}+\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{d}(u_{k}-(u_{\ell})_{k})(a_{i})_{k}\right\|_{Q,2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{2}\|a-a_{i}\|_{Q,2,d}+\|u-u_{\ell}\|_{2}\bar{A}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon\bar{A}}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\bar{A}=\varepsilon\bar{A}.

So
\{u_{\ell}\odot a_{i}\}
covers
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{A})
at radius
\varepsilon\bar{A}
with at most
N_{1}\cdot N_{2}
elements. Taking logarithms and the supremum over
Q
gives

\sup_{Q}\log N(\varepsilon\bar{A},\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{A}),L^{2}(Q))\leq\log N_{2}+\sup_{Q}\log N_{1}\leq d\log(1+4/\varepsilon)+\sup_{Q}\log N(\varepsilon\bar{A}/2,\mathcal{A},L^{2}(Q)).

∎

For a class
\mathcal{G}
of real-valued measurable functions, write
\operatorname{star}(\mathcal{G})\coloneqq\{\alpha g:\alpha\in[0,1],\ g\in\mathcal{G}\}
for its star hull around zero. For
\delta>0
, define the conditional local Rademacher complexity

\mathfrak{R}_{n}(\mathcal{G},\delta\mid S_{1})\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon,S_{2}}\left[\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{G}\\
\|g\|_{L^{2}(P)}\leq\delta\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in S_{2}}\varepsilon_{i}g(O_{i})\right|\,\middle|\,S_{1}\right],

where
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}
are i.i.d. Rademacher random variables independent of
S_{2}
, and the expectation is over both
\varepsilon
and
S_{2}
conditionally on
S_{1}
.

Lemma D.4

(Localized OSL maximal inequality)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a pointwise measurable real-valued class with envelope
G<\infty
and
\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\|g\|_{L_{2}(P)}\leq\rho
. Define
\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{0}=\{g/G:g\in\mathcal{G}\}\cup\{0\}
. Suppose
\delta_{n}
satisfies

\mathfrak{R}_{n}\left(\operatorname{star}(\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{0}),\delta_{n}\right)\leq\delta_{n}^{2}.

Then there exist universal constants
C,c<\infty
such that

\mathbb{E}\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}|(P_{n}-P)g|\leq C\left\{\delta_{n}\rho+G\delta_{n}^{2}+Ge^{-cn\delta_{n}^{2}}\right\}.

The same statement holds conditionally on
S_{1}
for an
S_{1}
-measurable class and envelope, with the expectation taken over the evaluation fold.

Proof.

Apply Lemma 14 in
[
19
]
to
\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{0}
with identity loss
L_{f}=f
and comparator
f^{\star}=0
. The identity loss is linear, so the lower bound on
\delta_{n}^{2}
in Lemma 14 is not required. Here the dimension parameter in Lemma 14 is one, because
\mathcal{G}
is real-valued; the outer-gradient dimension has already been absorbed by scalarization over
u\in S^{d-1}
. Then, with probability at least
1-Ce^{-cn\delta_{n}^{2}}
,

|(P_{n}-P)\bar{g}|\leq C\delta_{n}(\|\bar{g}\|_{L_{2}(P)}+\delta_{n}),\qquad\forall\bar{g}\in\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{0}.

Multiplying by
G
and using
\|g\|_{L^{2}(P)}\leq\rho
gives

|(P_{n}-P)g|\leq C\{\delta_{n}\|g\|_{L^{2}(P)}+G\delta_{n}^{2}\}\leq C\{\delta_{n}\rho+G\delta_{n}^{2}\}

on the good event. On the complement, which has probability at most
Ce^{-cn\delta_{n}^{2}}
, the supremum is bounded by
2G
since
|(P_{n}-P)g|\leq 2\|g\|_{\infty}\leq 2G
for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
. Taking expectations and combining gives the result.
∎

57
, Chapter 2]
; see also
[
30
, Chapter 8]
.

Lemma D.5

(Uniform entropy maximal inequality)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a pointwise measurable class of real-valued functions with envelope
F\in L^{2}(P)
. Define the uniform entropy integral

J(1,\mathcal{F},F)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\sup_{Q}\sqrt{1+\log N\bigl(\varepsilon\|F\|_{Q,2},\mathcal{F},L^{2}(Q)\bigr)}\,d\varepsilon,

where the supremum is over all finitely supported probability measures
Q
. If
J(1,\mathcal{F},F)<\infty
, then there exists a universal constant
0<C<\infty
such that

\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}|(P_{n}-P)f|\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}J(1,\mathcal{F},F)\|F\|_{L^{2}(P)}.

Corollary D.6

(Polynomial entropy maximal inequality)
.

Suppose that, for some
p\in(0,2)
and
K<\infty
,

\sup_{Q}\log N\bigl(\varepsilon\|F\|_{Q,2},\mathcal{F},L^{2}(Q)\bigr)\leq K\varepsilon^{-p},\qquad 0<\varepsilon\leq 1.

Then there exists a constant
C_{p}<\infty
depending only on
p
such that

\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}|(P_{n}-P)f|\leq\frac{C_{p}}{\sqrt{n}}\|F\|_{L^{2}(P)}\sqrt{1+K}.

Proof.

Since
\varepsilon\leq 1
implies
1\leq\varepsilon^{-p}
, we have
1+K\varepsilon^{-p}\leq(1+K)\varepsilon^{-p}
, and therefore

J(1,\mathcal{F},F)\leq\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{1+K\varepsilon^{-p}}\,d\varepsilon\leq\sqrt{1+K}\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{-p/2}\,d\varepsilon=\frac{2\sqrt{1+K}}{2-p}<\infty,

where finiteness uses
p<2
. Applying Lemma
D.5
with
C_{p}\coloneqq 2C/(2-p)
gives the claim.
∎

D.2
Proof of Theorem
5.1

Proof.

The proof proceeds in the following 6 steps.

Step 1: population identities.

Under the quadratic specialization (
1
), the unique population inner minimizer is
h^{\star}_{\omega,P}(X)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[g_{\omega}(Z)\mid X]
and
j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}(X)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[\partial_{\omega_{k}}g_{\omega}(Z)\mid X]
for
k=1,\dots,d
. The outer objective and its gradient are

\mathcal{F}_{P}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{P}\|Y-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}(X)\|^{2},\qquad[\Psi_{\omega}(P)]_{k}=\mathbb{E}_{P}[\langle h^{\star}_{\omega,P}(X)-Y,j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}(X)\rangle].

By the conditional Jensen inequality and
B
,

\|h^{\star}_{\omega,P}(X)\|\leq B,\qquad\|m^{\star}_{P}(X)\|\leq A,\qquad\|j^{\star}_{\omega,P}(X)\|_{2,d}\leq D,

where
\|a\|_{2,d}\coloneqq(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|a_{k}\|^{2})^{1/2}
for
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})\in(\mathbb{R}^{q})^{d}
.

Step 2: exact bias identity.

At the oracle nuisances,
P\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{\omega,P},m^{\star}_{P})=\Psi_{\omega}(P)
. Indeed, for each coordinate
k
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}\varphi_{k,\omega}(O;h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{\omega,P},m^{\star}_{P})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}\langle g_{\omega}(Z)-Y,j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}(X)\rangle

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{P}\langle\partial_{\omega_{k}}g_{\omega}(Z)-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}(X),h^{\star}_{\omega,P}(X)-m^{\star}_{P}(X)\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}\langle h^{\star}_{\omega,P}(X)-Y,j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}(X)\rangle=[\Psi_{\omega}(P)]_{k},

where the second term vanishes because
j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}(X)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[\partial_{\omega_{k}}g_{\omega}(Z)\mid X]
, so
\mathbb{E}_{P}[\partial_{\omega_{k}}g_{\omega}(Z)-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}(X)\mid X]=0
and the tower property applies.

For arbitrary
h\in L^{2}(P_{X})
,
j\in L^{2}(P_{X})^{d}
, and
m\in L^{2}(P_{X})
, the tower property gives

\displaystyle P\varphi_{k,\omega}(\cdot;h,j,m)-[\Psi_{\omega}(P)]_{k}

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}_{P}\langle j_{k}(X)-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}(X),h(X)-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}(X)\rangle

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{P}\langle j_{k}(X)-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}(X),m(X)-m^{\star}_{P}(X)\rangle.

By Cauchy-Schwarz, summing over
k
and using
\|u\|_{2}=1
,

\bigl\|P\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;h,j,m)-\Psi_{\omega}(P)\bigr\|\leq\|j-j^{\star}_{\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})^{d}}\left(\|h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}+\|m-m^{\star}_{P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}\right).

(22)

Step 3: estimator decomposition.

Write
\varphi^{\star}_{\omega}\coloneqq\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{\omega,P},m^{\star}_{P})
and
\hat{\varphi}_{\omega}\coloneqq\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\hat{h}_{\omega},\hat{j}_{\omega},\hat{m})
. Then

\widehat{\Psi}_{\omega,2}^{DR}-\Psi_{\omega}(P)=(P_{n,2}-P)\varphi^{\star}_{\omega}+(P_{n,2}-P)(\hat{\varphi}_{\omega}-\varphi^{\star}_{\omega})+\bigl(P\hat{\varphi}_{\omega}-\Psi_{\omega}(P)\bigr).

We work throughout on the event
\mathcal{E}_{\rm tr}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})
. On this event, the product-bias bound (
22
) gives

\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\bigl\|P\hat{\varphi}_{\omega}-\Psi_{\omega}(P)\bigr\|\leq r_{j}(r_{h}+r_{m}).

Using the dual representation
\|v\|_{2}=\sup_{u\in S^{d-1}}u^{\top}v
, where
S^{d-1}=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|u\|_{2}=1\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{tr}}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})}\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\|\widehat{\Psi}_{\omega,2}^{DR}-\Psi_{\omega}(P)\|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{tr}}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{A}}|(P_{n,2}-P)f|

\displaystyle+\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{tr}}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{B,n}^{\rm loc}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})}|(P_{n,2}-P)f|

\displaystyle+r_{j}(r_{h}+r_{m}),

where

\mathcal{F}_{A}\coloneqq\left\{u^{\top}\left(\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{\omega,P},m^{\star}_{P})-\Psi_{\omega}(P)\right):\omega\in\Omega,\ u\in S^{d-1}\right\}

is the oracle score class and
\mathcal{F}_{B,n}^{\rm loc}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})
is the localized nuisance-difference class (
14
).

Step 4: envelopes.

For the oracle score, by Cauchy-Schwarz and the bounds of Step 1,

\displaystyle\|\varphi^{\star}_{\omega}(O)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|g_{\omega}(Z)-Y\|\|j^{\star}_{\omega,P}(X)\|_{2,d}+\|\partial_{\omega}g_{\omega}(Z)-j^{\star}_{\omega,P}(X)\|_{2,d}\|h^{\star}_{\omega,P}(X)-m^{\star}_{P}(X)\|

\displaystyle\leq(A+B)D+2D(A+B)=3(A+B)D,

where
\|\partial_{\omega}g_{\omega}(Z)-j^{\star}_{\omega,P}(X)\|_{2,d}\leq\|\partial_{\omega}g_{\omega}(Z)\|_{2,d}+\|j^{\star}_{\omega,P}(X)\|_{2,d}\leq 2D
by the triangle inequality and
B
. Since
\Psi_{\omega}(P)=P\varphi^{\star}_{\omega}
, Jensen’s inequality gives
\|\Psi_{\omega}(P)\|_{2}\leq 3(A+B)D
. Therefore
F_{A}\coloneqq 6(A+B)D
is an envelope for
\mathcal{F}_{A}
.

For the nuisance-difference class, let
\mathcal{F}_{B,n}
denote the ambient class obtained from
\mathcal{F}_{B,n}^{\rm loc}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})
by dropping the radius constraints. Fix
\omega\in\Omega
,
u\in S^{d-1}
,
h\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
,
j\in\mathcal{J}_{n}
, and
m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Write
\Delta h=h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}
,
\Delta j=j-j^{\star}_{\omega,P}
, and
\Delta m=m-m^{\star}_{P}
. The score difference expands as

\displaystyle\varphi_{k,\omega}(O;h,j,m)-\varphi^{\star}_{k,\omega}(O)

\displaystyle=\langle g_{\omega}(Z)-Y,\Delta j_{k}(X)\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\partial_{\omega_{k}}g_{\omega}(Z)-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}(X),\Delta h(X)-\Delta m(X)\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\Delta j_{k}(X),h(X)-m(X)\rangle.

By
B
and
D
,

\|\Delta j(X)\|_{2,d}\leq\bar{J}_{n}+D,\qquad\|\Delta h(X)-\Delta m(X)\|\leq\bar{H}_{n}+\bar{M}_{n}+A+B,

and

\|h(X)-m(X)\|\leq\bar{H}_{n}+\bar{M}_{n},\qquad\|\partial_{\omega}g_{\omega}(Z)-j^{\star}_{\omega}(X)\|_{2,d}\leq 2D.

Thus

F_{B,n}\coloneqq(A+B)(\bar{J}_{n}+D)+2D(\bar{H}_{n}+\bar{M}_{n}+A+B)+(\bar{J}_{n}+D)(\bar{H}_{n}+\bar{M}_{n})

is an
S_{1}
-measurable envelope for
\mathcal{F}_{B,n}
, and hence also for
\mathcal{F}_{B,n}^{\rm loc}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})
. Moreover, every
f\in\mathcal{F}_{B,n}^{\rm loc}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})
satisfies

\|f\|_{L^{2}(P)}\leq(A+B+\bar{H}_{n}+\bar{M}_{n})r_{j}+2D(r_{h}+r_{m})\eqqcolon\rho_{B,n},

where the three terms in the score difference contribute
(A+B)r_{j}
,
2D(r_{h}+r_{m})
, and
(\bar{H}_{n}+\bar{M}_{n})r_{j}
respectively.

Step 5: entropy transfer.

We transfer the polynomial entropy bounds of
E
and
F
to the score classes
\mathcal{F}_{A}
and
\mathcal{F}_{B,n}
via Lemmas
D.1
,
D.2
, and
D.3
. Throughout, we use the notation

\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{A})\coloneqq\left\{O\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{d}u_{k}a_{k}(O):a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})\in\mathcal{A},\ u\in S^{d-1}\right\}

for the scalarized class associated with a block-vector class
\mathcal{A}
.

Oracle score class
\mathcal{F}_{A}
.
Define
\mathcal{U}_{g}\coloneqq\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}-Y
,
\mathcal{V}^{\star}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}^{\star}-m^{\star}_{P}
,
\mathscr{J}^{\star}\coloneqq\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{J}^{\star})
,

, and
\Delta\dot{\mathscr{G}}^{\star}\coloneqq\dot{\mathscr{G}}-\mathscr{J}^{\star}
. Reading off the three terms in the oracle score, we obtain the inclusion

\mathcal{F}_{A}\subset(\mathcal{U}_{g}\odot\mathscr{J}^{\star})+(\Delta\dot{\mathscr{G}}^{\star}\odot\mathcal{V}^{\star})-\mathcal{C}_{\Psi},

where
\mathcal{C}_{\Psi}\coloneqq\{O\mapsto u^{\top}\Psi_{\omega}(P):\omega\in\Omega,\ u\in S^{d-1}\}

d\log(1/\varepsilon)

K\varepsilon^{-p}
for any
p>0
. Applying Lemmas

D.2

D.3

K_{A}<\infty
depending only on the population entropy constants such that

\sup_{Q}\log N(\varepsilon F_{A},\mathcal{F}_{A},L^{2}(Q))\leq K_{A}\varepsilon^{-p},\qquad 0<\varepsilon\leq 1.

Nuisance-difference class
\mathcal{F}_{B,n}
.

Condition on
S_{1}
and define
\mathscr{J}_{n}\coloneqq\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{J}_{n})
,
\Delta\mathscr{J}_{n}\coloneqq\mathscr{J}_{n}-\mathscr{J}^{\star}

\Delta\mathcal{H}_{n}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}_{n}-\mathcal{H}^{\star}

\Delta\mathcal{V}_{n}\coloneqq\Delta\mathcal{H}_{n}-\Delta\mathcal{M}_{n}

\mathcal{W}_{n}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}_{n}-\mathcal{M}_{n}

\displaystyle u^{\top}\bigl(\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;h,j,m)-\varphi^{\star}_{\omega}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\left\langle g_{\omega}-Y,\sum_{k=1}^{d}u_{k}(j_{k}-j^{\star}_{k,\omega})\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\sum_{k=1}^{d}u_{k}(\partial_{\omega_{k}}g_{\omega}-j^{\star}_{k,\omega}),(h-h^{\star}_{\omega})-(m-m^{\star}_{P})\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\left\langle\sum_{k=1}^{d}u_{k}(j_{k}-j^{\star}_{k,\omega}),h-m\right\rangle,

which gives the inclusion

\mathcal{F}_{B,n}\subset(\mathcal{U}_{g}\odot\Delta\mathscr{J}_{n})+(\Delta\dot{\mathscr{G}}^{\star}\odot\Delta\mathcal{V}_{n})-(\Delta\mathscr{J}_{n}\odot\mathcal{W}_{n}).

Applying the same three lemmas together with

S_{1}

Since
\mathcal{F}_{B,n}^{\rm loc}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})\subset\mathcal{F}_{B,n}
, the same bound holds for the localized class. Finally, adding the zero function and passing to the star hull each change the covering number by at most a constant depending only on

\mathcal{F}_{B,n}^{{\rm loc},0}\coloneqq\left\{f/F_{B,n}:f\in\mathcal{F}_{B,n}^{\rm loc}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})\right\}\cup\{0\}

satisfies the same polynomial entropy bound, up to increasing

by a constant depending only on

.

Step 6: empirical process bounds and conclusion.

By Corollary
D.6

\mathcal{F}_{A}

∎

.

Corollary D.7

(Stationarity certificate)
.

On
\mathcal{E}_{\rm tr}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})
, for any possibly data-dependent

Corollary D.8

(Gradient descent convergence)
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}_{P}
is
L
-smooth on
\Omega
,
F_{\inf}\coloneqq\inf_{\omega\in\Omega}\mathcal{F}_{P}(\omega)>0
, and the iterates

\omega_{t+1}=\omega_{t}-\eta\widehat{\Psi}_{\omega_{t},2}^{DR},\qquad 0<\eta\leq\frac{1}{L},

remain in
\Omega
. Then, for every
T\geq 1
, on
\mathcal{E}_{\rm tr}(r_{h},r_{j},r_{m})
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\min_{0\leq t<T}\|\Psi_{\omega_{t}}(P)\|\,\middle|\,S_{1}\right]\leq\left(\frac{2\{\mathcal{F}_{P}(\omega_{0})-F_{\inf}\}}{\eta T}\right)^{1/2}+\mathfrak{s}_{N}.

Proof.

Write
e_{t}\coloneqq\widehat{\Psi}_{\omega_{t},2}^{DR}-\Psi_{\omega_{t}}(P)
, so that
\|e_{t}\|_{2}\leq\Delta_{N,2}
. By
L
-smoothness and
\omega_{t+1}-\omega_{t}=-\eta(\Psi_{\omega_{t}}(P)+e_{t})
,

\mathcal{F}_{P}(\omega_{t+1})\leq\mathcal{F}_{P}(\omega_{t})-\eta\|\Psi_{\omega_{t}}(P)\|^{2}-\eta\langle\Psi_{\omega_{t}}(P),e_{t}\rangle+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\|\Psi_{\omega_{t}}(P)+e_{t}\|^{2}.

Using
\eta\leq 1/L
, Young’s inequality
-\langle a,b\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|a\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|b\|^{2}
, and
\|a+b\|^{2}\leq 2\|a\|^{2}+2\|b\|^{2}
, we obtain

\mathcal{F}_{P}(\omega_{t+1})\leq\mathcal{F}_{P}(\omega_{t})-\frac{\eta}{2}\|\Psi_{\omega_{t}}(P)\|^{2}+\frac{\eta}{2}\|e_{t}\|^{2}.

Summing over
t=0,\dots,T-1
and using
\|e_{t}\|\leq\Delta_{N,2}
,

\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\|\Psi_{\omega_{t}}(P)\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{F}_{P}(\omega_{0})-F_{\inf}+\frac{\eta T}{2}\Delta_{N,2}^{2}.

Dividing by
\eta T/2
and taking the minimum over
t
,

\min_{0\leq t<T}\|\Psi_{\omega_{t}}(P)\|^{2}\leq\frac{2\{\mathcal{F}_{P}(\omega_{0})-F_{\inf}\}}{\eta T}+\Delta_{N,2}^{2}.

Taking square roots and using
\sqrt{a+b}\leq\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}
,

\min_{0\leq t<T}\|\Psi_{\omega_{t}}(P)\|\leq\left(\frac{2\{\mathcal{F}_{P}(\omega_{0})-F_{\inf}\}}{\eta T}\right)^{1/2}+\Delta_{N,2}.

Taking conditional expectations and applying Theorem
5.1
gives the result.
∎

Appendix E
Nuisance Learning and Plug-in Hypergradients

E.1
Nuisance learning

This appendix records standard sufficient conditions under which the nuisance assumptions used in Sections
4
and
5
can be verified. In the quadratic specialization, the nuisance targets are conditional expectations:

h_{\omega,P}^{\star}(X)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[g_{\omega}(Z)\mid X],\qquad j_{k,\omega,P}^{\star}(X)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[\partial_{\omega_{k}}g_{\omega}(Z)\mid X],\qquad m^{\star}_{P}(X)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[Y\mid X].

Thus nuisance learning reduces to standard regression. For example, define

R_{\omega}^{h}(h)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{P}\|h(X)-g_{\omega}(Z)\|^{2},\qquad R_{\omega}^{j}(j)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{P}\|j(X)-\partial_{\omega}g_{\omega}(Z)\|_{2,d}^{2},

and
R^{m}(m)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{P}\|m(X)-Y\|^{2}
. Their minimizers are respectively
h_{\omega,P}^{\star}
,
j_{\omega,P}^{\star}
, and
m^{\star}_{P}
.

A generic verification route is the following. Let
\mathcal{H}_{n},\mathcal{J}_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n}
be the classes used to estimate
h_{\omega,P}^{\star}
,
j_{\omega,P}^{\star}
, and
m^{\star}_{P}
. Let
h_{n,\omega}^{\circ}
,
j_{n,\omega}^{\circ}
, and
m_{n}^{\circ}
denote the corresponding population risk minimizers over these classes. For squared loss,

R_{\omega}^{h}(h)-R_{\omega}^{h}(h_{\omega}^{\star})=\frac{1}{2}\|h-h_{\omega}^{\star}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}^{2},

and similarly for
j
and
m
. Hence an oracle inequality such as
R_{\omega}^{h}(\hat{h}_{\omega})-R_{\omega}^{h}(h_{n,\omega}^{\circ})=O_{p}(\delta_{h,n}^{2})
implies

\|\hat{h}_{\omega}-h_{\omega}^{\star}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}=O_{p}(a_{h,n}+\delta_{h,n}),\qquad a_{h,n}:=\|h_{n,\omega}^{\circ}-h_{\omega}^{\star}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}.

Uniformly over
\omega
, the same argument gives

\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\|\hat{h}_{\omega}-h_{\omega}^{\star}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}=O_{p}(a_{h,n}+\delta_{h,n})

whenever the oracle inequality is uniform in
\omega
. Analogous bounds hold for
\hat{j}_{\omega}
and
\hat{m}
. Such oracle inequalities are standard consequences of empirical-process bounds for least-squares estimators over entropy- or Rademacher-controlled classes
[
57
,
53
,
30
,
60
]
.

If an empirical nuisance risk has multiple minimizers, the estimator is understood as a measurable selection satisfying the stated
L^{2}(P_{X})
rate. The theory requires convergence to the selected population nuisance; arbitrary empirical selections that oscillate among distinct minimizers are not covered.

The rate
\delta_{n}
depends on the size of the regression class. A common formulation uses a localized critical radius. If
\delta_{n}
satisfies a local Rademacher complexity inequality of the form

\mathfrak{R}_{n}\bigl(\mathcal{F}_{n}\cap\{f:\|f-f_{n}^{\circ}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}\leq\delta_{n}\}\bigr)\lesssim\delta_{n}^{2},

then least-squares ERM over
\mathcal{F}_{n}
typically satisfies
\|\hat{f}-f^{\star}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}=O_{p}(a_{n}+\delta_{n})
, up to standard boundedness and measurability conditions
[
4
,
60
,
55
]
. For classes with metric entropy
\log N(\varepsilon,\mathcal{F}_{n},L_{2})\lesssim\varepsilon^{-p}
,
p\in(0,2)
, the corresponding nonparametric norm rate is typically
n^{-1/(2+p)}
modulo approximation error. Since
p<2
gives
1/(2+p)>1/4
, such rates are compatible with the second-order conditions in Assumption
C
, provided the approximation terms are negligible.

For fixed-
\omega
inference, the required conditions are the score stability and product rates in Assumptions
4.1
–
C
. In the quadratic score,

\bigl\|P\varphi_{\omega}(\cdot;\hat{h}_{\omega},\hat{j}_{\omega},\hat{m})-\Psi_{\omega}(P)\bigr\|_{2}\leq\|\hat{j}_{\omega}-j_{\omega,P}^{\star}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})^{d}}\bigl(\|\hat{h}_{\omega}-h_{\omega,P}^{\star}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}+\|\hat{m}-m^{\star}_{P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}\bigr).

Thus, if

\|\hat{j}_{\omega}-j_{\omega,P}^{\star}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})^{d}}\bigl(\|\hat{h}_{\omega}-h_{\omega,P}^{\star}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}+\|\hat{m}-m^{\star}_{P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}\bigr)=o_{p}(N^{-1/2}),

and the quadratic remainder condition

\|\hat{h}_{\omega}-h_{\omega,P}^{\star}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}+\|\hat{j}_{\omega}-j_{\omega,P}^{\star}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})^{d}}=o_{p}(N^{-1/4})

holds, then the CLT in Theorem
4.2
applies. If all nuisance errors have the same order, this requires
o_{p}(N^{-1/4})
rates, not merely
O_{p}(N^{-1/4})
. This is the usual rate requirement in orthogonal and doubly robust estimation: orthogonality removes first-order nuisance bias, while second-order remainders must still be negligible
[
11
,
19
,
54
]
.

For the uniform result in Section
5
, one also needs complexity control of the score classes generated by the learned nuisance paths. Assumptions
D
and
F
give a sufficient condition: conditionally on the training fold, the learned nuisances lie in bounded classes with polynomial entropy. For kernel ridge regression with bounded kernel
K(x,x)\leq\kappa^{2}
and ridge parameter
\lambda_{n}>0
, comparison with the zero function gives
\lambda_{n}\|\hat{f}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{K}}^{2}\leq C^{2}
when the regression target is bounded by
C
. Hence
\|\hat{f}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{K}}\leq C/\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}
and
\|\hat{f}\|_{\infty}\leq\kappa C/\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}
. Entropy or Rademacher bounds for the induced RKHS ball then yield the required class-complexity control
[
48
,
51
,
10
]
. For neural-network classes with bounded depth, width, activation Lipschitz constants, and weight norms, standard covering-number bounds give entropy of order
s_{n}\log(C_{n}/\varepsilon)
, where
s_{n}
is the number of effective parameters and
C_{n}
depends on the norm constraints. If the architecture grows with
n
, these constants grow accordingly and must be small enough for the localized critical-radius terms in Theorem
5.1
to vanish.

For nonquadratic inner problems, additional Riesz or adjoint correction nuisances may appear. The same verification principle applies: one needs oracle inequalities for the additional nuisance learners and product-rate conditions matching the corresponding von Mises remainder
[
54
]
.

E.2
Plug-in hypergradient estimation

This section records what the direct plug-in hypergradient estimates in the quadratic specialization, and why it should not be expected to deliver semiparametric-efficient inference. Functional implicit differentiation and KBO both lead to plug-in hypergradients in this broad sense: one estimates the inner solution, or an adjoint/sensitivity representation of it, and substitutes the estimate into a gradient formula
[
44
,
14
]
. This is natural for optimization. It is not, by itself, an orthogonal statistical estimator.

Throughout this subsection we use the quadratic inner loss and squared outer loss,
\ell^{in}_{\omega}(h)(O)=\frac{1}{2}\|h(X)-g_{\omega}(Z)\|^{2}
and
\ell^{out}_{\omega}(h)(O)=\frac{1}{2}\|Y-h(X)\|^{2}
. Thus
h^{\star}_{\omega,P}(X)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[g_{\omega}(Z)\mid X]
,
j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}(X)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[\partial_{\omega_{k}}g_{\omega}(Z)\mid X]
, and
m^{\star}_{P}(X)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[Y\mid X]
.

For clarity, we analyze the sample-split plug-in estimator. Let
\hat{h}_{\omega}
and
\hat{j}_{\omega}=(\hat{j}_{1,\omega},\dots,\hat{j}_{d,\omega})
be trained on
S_{1}
and evaluated on the independent fold
S_{2}
:

\widehat{\Psi}^{\,\mathrm{PI}}_{\omega,2,k}\coloneqq P_{n,2}\big[\langle\hat{h}_{\omega}(X)-Y,\hat{j}_{k,\omega}(X)\rangle\big],\qquad k=1,\dots,d.

(23)

The full-sample version replaces
P_{n,2}
by
P_{n}
, but then the empirical-process term involves a function estimated on the same sample on which it is averaged. The sample-split version isolates the statistical issue cleanly.

Exact plug-in bias.

For arbitrary
h,v\in L^{2}(P_{X})
, define

\varphi^{\mathrm{PI}}_{k,\omega}(O;h,v)\coloneqq\langle h(X)-Y,v(X)\rangle.

Since
h(X)
and
v(X)
are functions of
X
,

P\,\varphi^{\mathrm{PI}}_{k,\omega}(\cdot;h,v)=\langle h-m^{\star}_{P},v\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}.

Also,

[\Psi_{\omega}(P)]_{k}=\langle h^{\star}_{\omega,P}-m^{\star}_{P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}.

Therefore

\displaystyle P\,\varphi^{\mathrm{PI}}_{k,\omega}(\cdot;h,v)-[\Psi_{\omega}(P)]_{k}

(24)

\displaystyle=\langle h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}+\langle h^{\star}_{\omega,P}-m^{\star}_{P},v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}+\langle h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P},v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}.

The first two terms are first-order nuisance errors. Thus the plain plug-in hypergradient is not Neyman-orthogonal with respect to either
h^{\star}_{\omega}
or
j^{\star}_{k,\omega}
.

In particular, if
\|j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}<\infty
and
\|h^{\star}_{\omega,P}-m^{\star}_{P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\big|P\,\varphi^{\mathrm{PI}}_{k,\omega}(\cdot;h,v)-[\Psi_{\omega}(P)]_{k}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}\|h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}

(25)

\displaystyle+\|h^{\star}_{\omega,P}-m^{\star}_{P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}\|v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}

\displaystyle+\|h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}\|v-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}.

Proposition E.1

(Rate of the sample-split plug-in estimator)
.

Fix
\omega\in\Omega
and
k\in\{1,\dots,d\}
. Suppose
\hat{h}_{\omega}
and
\hat{j}_{k,\omega}
are trained on
S_{1}
, and suppose

P\big[\varphi^{\mathrm{PI}}_{k,\omega}(\cdot;\hat{h}_{\omega},\hat{j}_{k,\omega})^{2}\big]=O_{p}(1).

If
\|\hat{h}_{\omega}-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}=O_{p}(n^{-\alpha})
and
\|\hat{j}_{k,\omega}-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}=O_{p}(n^{-\beta})
, then

\widehat{\Psi}^{\,\mathrm{PI}}_{\omega,2,k}-[\Psi_{\omega}(P)]_{k}=O_{p}\left(n^{-1/2}+n^{-\alpha}+n^{-\beta}+n^{-(\alpha+\beta)}\right).

Proof.

Conditionally on
S_{1}
, the function
\varphi^{\mathrm{PI}}_{k,\omega}(\cdot;\hat{h}_{\omega},\hat{j}_{k,\omega})
is fixed, and the evaluation observations in
S_{2}
are independent. Hence

(P_{n,2}-P)\varphi^{\mathrm{PI}}_{k,\omega}(\cdot;\hat{h}_{\omega},\hat{j}_{k,\omega})=O_{p}(n^{-1/2}).

The population bias is bounded by (
25
). Substituting the assumed nuisance rates gives the result.
∎

This proposition should be read literally. For the direct plug-in estimator, the generic bias is not a product of nuisance errors. Root-
n
consistency requires the first-order terms in (
24
) to be
O_{p}(n^{-1/2})
or smaller; asymptotic linearity centered at
\Psi_{\omega}(P)
requires them to be
o_{p}(n^{-1/2})
. Under generic nuisance rates, this means that both
\hat{h}_{\omega}
and
\hat{j}_{k,\omega}
must be estimated at essentially parametric accuracy. A product-rate condition such as
\alpha+\beta>1/2
is not enough for the plain plug-in estimator unless the two linear terms in (
24
) vanish for additional, problem-specific reasons.

Asymptotic distribution and efficiency.

If the plug-in bias in (
24
) is
o_{p}(n^{-1/2})
and

\bigl\|\varphi^{\mathrm{PI}}_{k,\omega}(\cdot;\hat{h}_{\omega},\hat{j}_{k,\omega})-\varphi^{\mathrm{PI}}_{k,\omega}(\cdot;h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})\bigr\|_{L^{2}(P)}=o_{p}(1),

then the sample-split plug-in estimator is asymptotically linear with influence function

\varphi^{\mathrm{PI}}_{k,\omega}(\cdot;h^{\star}_{\omega,P},j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P})-[\Psi_{\omega}(P)]_{k}.

Consequently,

\sqrt{n}\left(\widehat{\Psi}^{\,\mathrm{PI}}_{\omega,2,k}-[\Psi_{\omega}(P)]_{k}\right)\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}\left(0,\operatorname{Var}_{P}\left[\langle h^{\star}_{\omega,P}(X)-Y,j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}(X)\rangle\right]\right).

This is not the semiparametric efficiency bound in general. The efficient influence function from Theorem
3.1
subtracts additional correction terms. Unless those correction terms are identically zero, the plug-in influence function is not the canonical gradient and its variance is not
\Sigma_{P,\omega,kk}
.

Contrast with the debiased estimator.

In the same quadratic specialization, the debiased score used in Section
4
is

\varphi_{k,\omega}(O;h,j,m)=\langle g_{\omega}(Z)-Y,j_{k}(X)\rangle+\langle\partial_{\omega_{k}}g_{\omega}(Z)-j_{k}(X),h(X)-m(X)\rangle.

For arbitrary
h,j,m
, its population bias satisfies

P\,\varphi_{k,\omega}(\cdot;h,j,m)-[\Psi_{\omega}(P)]_{k}=-\langle j_{k}-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P},h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}+\langle j_{k}-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P},m-m^{\star}_{P}\rangle_{L^{2}(P_{X})}.

(26)

Thus

\left|P\,\varphi_{k,\omega}(\cdot;h,j,m)-[\Psi_{\omega}(P)]_{k}\right|\leq\|j_{k}-j^{\star}_{k,\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}\left(\|h-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}+\|m-m^{\star}_{P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}\right).

The first-order terms present in the plug-in bias (
24
) are absent. This is the practical content of orthogonality: the estimator can be root-
n
and asymptotically normal under product-rate nuisance conditions, such as

\|\hat{j}_{k,\omega}-j^{\star}_{k,\omega}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}\left(\|\hat{h}_{\omega}-h^{\star}_{\omega,P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}+\|\hat{m}-m^{\star}_{P}\|_{L^{2}(P_{X})}\right)=o_{p}(n^{-1/2}),

rather than requiring each nuisance to be estimated at a parametric rate. Rates at the
n^{-1/4}
boundary are not enough as big-
O
statements; the product must be little-
o(n^{-1/2})
for the centered CLT in Theorem
4.2
.

This is the core distinction. The plug-in hypergradient estimates the correct population gradient only if the learned lower-level solution and sensitivity are accurate enough to make the first-order bias negligible. The debiased estimator changes the score so that those first-order terms cancel, leaving only second-order nuisance products and the empirical average of the efficient influence function.

F.1
Instrumental-variable experiments

IV DGP.

The IV experiments use a two-stage conditional-projection design, following the IV example of Section
2
. Here
X
is the treatment,
Z
is the instrument, and the lower-level problem projects
g_{\omega}(Z)
onto functions of
X
. We draw
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{p})
with
p=3
, set

Z=2\sum_{j=1}^{p}X_{j}+\eta,\qquad\eta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{Z}^{2}),\qquad\sigma_{Z}^{2}=0.1,

and define
\phi_{\ell}(z)=\sin(z+\ell)
,
\ell=1,\ldots,d
, with
d=4
and
g_{\omega}(Z)=\omega^{\top}\phi(Z)
. The structural parameter is
\omega^{\star}=(1,2,3,4)^{\top}/\sqrt{30}
. For the gradient estimation and inference experiments,
Y=\omega^{\star\top}\phi(Z)+\varepsilon_{Y}
with
\varepsilon_{Y}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,0.25^{2})
, which isolates gradient estimation and calibration. For the KBO regularization experiment, we use
Y=\omega^{\star\top}\phi(Z)+0.5\eta
, which preserves
\mathbb{E}_{P}[Y\mid X]
and hence
\Psi_{\omega}(P)
, but introduces correlation between the outcome noise and
Z
. We evaluate the population gradient at the fixed non-stationary point

\omega_{0}=\omega^{\star}+0.35\frac{(1,1/3,-1/3,-1)^{\top}}{\|(1,1/3,-1/3,-1)\|},

chosen away from
\omega^{\star}
to measure gradient-estimation error at a non-stationary point.

The following analytic expressions are used only to compute the ground-truth population target
\Psi_{\omega}(P)
for evaluation; they are not used by the estimator:

j^{\star}_{\ell}(X)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[\phi_{\ell}(Z)\mid X]=\exp(-\sigma_{Z}^{2}/2)\sin\!\left(2\sum_{j=1}^{p}X_{j}+\ell\right),

with
h^{\star}_{\omega}(X)=j^{\star}(X)^{\top}\omega
and
m^{\star}_{P}(X)=j^{\star}(X)^{\top}\omega^{\star}
. Hence

\Psi_{\omega}(P)=A(\omega-\omega^{\star}),\qquad A_{k\ell}=\frac{\exp(-\sigma_{Z}^{2})}{2}\left\{\cos(k-\ell)-\exp(-8p)\cos(k+\ell)\right\},

where the term
\exp(-8p)
is negligible for
p=3
. Feasible IV nuisance learners use ridge regression on Fourier features of
\sum_{j}X_{j}
, with frequencies
1,\ldots,8
, intercept included, and ridge parameter
10^{-6}
. Two-fold cross-fitting is used throughout.

-regression experiments

Fitted
Q
-regression DGP.

The fitted
Q
-regression experiments use a projected Bellman-backup design. Let
X=(S,A)
and
Z=(S,A,R,S^{\prime})
. The state is
S\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, the action is binary
A\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(0.5)
, and the next state follows

S^{\prime}=\rho S+\tau A+\xi,\qquad\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{S}^{2}),

with
\rho=0.7
,
\tau=0.5
, and
\sigma_{S}=0.2
. The reward is

R=\sin(S)+0.5A+0.25SA+\varepsilon_{R},\qquad\varepsilon_{R}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,0.1^{2}).

For a continuation-value model
V_{\omega}(s^{\prime})=\omega^{\top}\phi(s^{\prime})
, the Bellman target is
g_{\omega}(Z)=R+\gamma V_{\omega}(S^{\prime})
, and the lower-level loss is the quadratic Bellman-regression loss

\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{P}\bigl[(h(S,A)-R-\gamma V_{\omega}(S^{\prime}))^{2}\bigr],

whose population solution is the projected Bellman backup

h^{\star}_{\omega,P}(S,A)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[R+\gamma V_{\omega}(S^{\prime})\mid S,A],

as in the FQE example of Section
2
.

For the gradient-estimation and KBO experiments, we use

\phi(s^{\prime})=(\sin s^{\prime},\cos s^{\prime},s^{\prime},(s^{\prime})^{2})^{\top},\qquad\omega^{\star}=(0.55,-0.35,0.25,0.15)^{\top},

\gamma=0.8
, and

Y=R+\gamma V_{\omega^{\star}}(S^{\prime})+\varepsilon_{Y},\qquad\varepsilon_{Y}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,0.25^{2}).

The evaluation point is

\omega_{0}=\omega^{\star}+0.35\frac{(1,-0.5,0.35,-0.25)^{\top}}{\|(1,-0.5,0.35,-0.25)\|}.

For the Wald experiment, we use the same structural design with a state-dependent behavior policy
P(A=1\mid S)=\sigma(0.5S)
and
\gamma=0.9
. For the root experiment, we use the two-dimensional feature map
\phi(s^{\prime})=(\sin s^{\prime},\cos s^{\prime})^{\top}
with
\omega^{\star}=(0.65,-0.45)^{\top}
, which gives a well-conditioned population stationary point.

The analytic nuisances are available because
S^{\prime}\mid S,A
is Gaussian. Writing
\mu(S,A)=\rho S+\tau A
,

\mathbb{E}_{P}[\sin(S^{\prime})\mid S,A]=e^{-\sigma_{S}^{2}/2}\sin\{\mu(S,A)\},\qquad\mathbb{E}_{P}[\cos(S^{\prime})\mid S,A]=e^{-\sigma_{S}^{2}/2}\cos\{\mu(S,A)\},

and
\mathbb{E}_{P}[S^{\prime}\mid S,A]=\mu(S,A)
,
\mathbb{E}_{P}[(S^{\prime})^{2}\mid S,A]=\mu(S,A)^{2}+\sigma_{S}^{2}
. Hence

j^{\star}_{\omega,P}(S,A)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[\gamma\phi(S^{\prime})\mid S,A],

h^{\star}_{\omega,P}(S,A)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[R\mid S,A]+j^{\star}_{\omega,P}(S,A)^{\top}\omega,\qquad m^{\star}_{P}(S,A)=\mathbb{E}_{P}[R\mid S,A]+j^{\star}_{\omega,P}(S,A)^{\top}\omega^{\star}.

Feasible nuisance learners use only the observed covariates
(S,A)
, with ridge regression on an observable nonlinear basis in
(S,A)
as the default. Oracle conditional expectations and transition parameters are used only for evaluation and for the oracle DR benchmark.
