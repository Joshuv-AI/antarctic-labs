On Controllability of a Class of N N -dimensional Hyperbolic Equations with Internal Single-point Degeneracy
Donghui Yang, Weijia Wu

Abstract
This paper explores the controllability of a class of N N -dimensional hyperbolic equations featuring a single interior degenerate point. Firstly, we establish the well-posedness of the equation through the application of the Hardy inequality. Following this, we primarily utilize the Carleman estimate method to derive the observability inequality. By leveraging the equivalence between observability and controllability, we deduce the exact controllability of the equation. It is worth noting that our selected control region includes the degenerate null point. In the Carleman estimate, we adopt a unique approach to construct the weight function, effectively negating the influence of the degenerate region. Keywords : Degenerate hyperbolic equations, Carleman estimate, controllability, observability. AMS subject classifications: 35L25, 93B05, 93B07, 93C20.

Introduction

Controllability, a basic concept in dynamical systems, holds utmost importance in understanding and influencing the trajectories of systems under specified control inputs. In recent times, the study of controllability within the framework of degenerate equations has sparked extensive and profound mathematical inquiries. This heightened scrutiny is attributed to the unique capabilities of these equations in depicting specific behaviors pertinent to natural and physical phenomena. For instance, the renowned Crocco equation in meteorology exemplifies a degenerate parabolic equation (
[
19
]
), which finds extensive application in fields such as hypersonic aerodynamics and fluid dynamics. Similarly, the Black-Scholes equation (
[
21
]
), ubiquitous in finance, presents a degenerate form, further emphasizing the versatility and applicability of degenerate equations across diverse disciplines.

Unlike classical equations, degenerate equations violate the uniform ellipticity condition, thereby complicating the establishment of solution existence, uniqueness, and regularity. The degeneration of coefficients may result in significant singularities in certain regions, posing additional hurdles in proving controllability.

Compared to research on the controllability of degenerate parabolic equations (
[
6
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
14
]
), there has been comparatively less exploration into the controllability of degenerate hyperbolic equations, with current focus primarily on one-dimensional cases. In
[
18
]
, the author employed nonharmonic Fourier series to investigate sharp observability estimates for a class of one-dimensional degenerate wave equations, with control applied to degenerate boundaries. In contrast,
[
22
]
used the multiplier method to study the persistent regional null controllability of a class of degenerate wave equations, where control acts internally. In
[
18
]
, control is observed to act on the degenerate boundary, whereas in
[
22
]
, control acts internally but still involves boundary degeneracy. Differing from both
[
18
]
and
[
22
]
, the work presented in
[
5
]
explores the exact controllability of a one-dimensional degenerate wave equation, with control applied to the boundary but the degenerate point located within the interior of the domain. In
[
5
]
, both strong and weak degeneracy cases are considered, and exact controllability is proven under weak degeneracy. However, when control is applied only at one boundary point, exact controllability under strong degeneracy cannot be achieved. Unlike
[
5
]
,
[
3
]
considers not only the degenerate case but also the singular case. Despite significant progress in studying one-dimensional degenerate hyperbolic equations (
[
2
,
4
,
18
,
22
,
23
]
), the application of Carleman estimates in this context remains relatively limited.

The primary approach in Carleman estimates involves constructing a weight function to derive an estimate for the solution. In the context of degeneration, constructing this weight function becomes more complex. In this paper, we introduce a novel weight function that distinguishes itself from those utilized for equations satisfying the uniform ellipticity condition (as seen in
[
7
,
15
]
) and also from those employed in other studies focusing on degenerate Carleman estimates (such as
[
1
,
2
,
16
]
). This innovative approach enables us to effectively mitigate the influence of degenerate points and attain the desired weighted Carleman estimate.

In this paper, we study the controllability problem for a class of high-dimensional internal single-point degenerate hyperbolic equations. Our primary strategy involves leveraging the Carleman estimate method, coupled with the introduction of suitable weight functions, to achieve
exact controllability for this specific class of problems. Our work stands out from existing research, such as
[
5
,
18
,
22
]
, as it focuses on the high-dimensional case and presents distinctive features in both the control domain and the methodology employed. Furthermore, to our knowledge, there has been no prior research on the controllability of high-dimensional degenerate hyperbolic equations, making this paper a first attempt in this topic.

The remaining structure of this paper is outlined as follows. Section

2
Problems and main results

In this paper, we examine the following single-point interior degenerate system:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Div\left(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u\right)=\chi_{\omega}f,&\mbox{in }Q,\\
u=0,&\mbox{on }\Sigma,\\
u(0)=u_{0},\partial_{t}u(0)=u_{1},&\mbox{in }\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a smooth domain with
N\geq 1
being an integer and
0\in\Omega
. Here,
Q=\Omega\times(0,T)
, with
T
being a specific time that will be detailed later in Chapter
5
. Additionally,
\Sigma=\partial Q=\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
,
\omega\subseteq\Omega
is a given control domain that will be specified later,
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
(with
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
defined subsequently), and

u_{1}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
represent the initial data. The control function
f
belongs to
f\in L^{2}(Q)
. Let
\nu
denote the outer normal vector to
\partial\Omega
, and define

\Gamma_{+}:=\left\{x\in\partial\Omega\bigm|x\cdot\nu(x)\geq 0\right\},

where
\Gamma_{+}
is an open subset of
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
. Set

\mathcal{O}(\Gamma_{+},\delta)=\left\{x\in\Omega\bigm|\dist(x,\Gamma_{+})<\delta\right\},

and assume that

\mathcal{O}(\Gamma_{+},3\delta)\subseteq\omega,

(2.2)

where
\delta>0
is a given constant, and

\dist(x,\Gamma_{+})=\inf_{y\in\Gamma_{+}}|x-y|.

Define

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\left|\ \!|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\right.\right\},

with the norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega)}=\left\||x|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

and

\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)=\overline{C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega)}}.

It is evident that there exists a solution

u\in C([0,T];\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\cap C^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))

to the equation (
2.1
) for any
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
. This will be proven using the classic Galerkin method in Section
3
.

As is classical in controllability problems (HUM), we introduce the nonhomogeneous adjoint problem for (
2.1
):

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}z-\Div(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla z)=F,&\mbox{in }Q,\\
z=0,&\mbox{on }\Sigma,\\
z(0)=z_{0},\partial_{t}z(0)=z_{1},&\mbox{in }\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.3)

where
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
z_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, and
z_{1}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

The main results of this paper are the Carleman inequality and observability inequality stated in Theorem
2.1

following.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\omega\subset\Omega
be given as in (
2.2
). Assume
0\in\omega
. For any solution
z
to (
2.3
), there exist positive constants
C=C(\omega,\Omega)
, such that for sufficiently large constants

\begin{split}&\iint_{Q}e^{2s\varphi}s\gamma\varphi\left(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla z\cdot\nabla z+|z_{t}|^{2}+s^{2}\gamma^{2}\varphi^{2}|z|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq C\iint_{\omega\times(0,T)}e^{2s\varphi}s\gamma\varphi\left(z_{t}^{2}+|x|^{\alpha}\nabla z\cdot\nabla z+s^{2}\gamma^{2}\varphi^{2}|z|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C\iint_{Q}e^{2s\varphi}F^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

(2.4)

where
\varphi
is the weight function defined in (
4.2
).

Using the duality between controllability and observability (
[
20
,
17
]
), proving controllability is equivalent to establishing an observability property for the adjoint system. From this, we can deduce the unique continuation and exact controllability of (
2.1

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\omega
be given as in (
2.2
) and
0\in\omega
. If
z
is a solution of (
2.3
) with
F=0
(or, equivalently, if
u
is a solution of (
2.1
) with

), and

on

, then

in

.

After establishing unique continuation, we can further deduce controllability.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\omega\subseteq\Omega
be given as in (
2.2
).
Assume
0\in\omega
. Then the system (
2.1
) is exactly controllable, i.e., for every
(u_{0},u_{1}),(w_{0},w_{1})\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, there exists a control

2.1

and
u_{t}(T)=w_{1}
.

3
Well-posedness

In this section, we discuss the well-posedness of (
2.1
). To set the stage, we present some preliminary results.

Consider
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,d(0,\Gamma))
, where

d(0,\Gamma)=\inf_{y\in\Gamma}|y|
, and for a small constant
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\varepsilon_{0}\right]
, let

\Omega^{\varepsilon}=\left\{x\in\Omega\mid|x|>\varepsilon\right\},\ \Omega_{\varepsilon}=\left\{x\in\Omega\mid|x|<\varepsilon\right\}\mbox{ and }S(\varepsilon)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\bigm||x|=\varepsilon\right\}\,.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
N\geq 2
and
\alpha\in(0,2)
, it holds that
|x|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
for all
u\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, satisfying the inequality

(N-2+\alpha)\left\||x|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)},

(3.1)

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
u
. Additionally, if
u\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
then
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

For
u\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, its restriction belongs to
W^{1,2}\left(\Omega^{\varepsilon}\right)
,
and its trace represents a bounded linear map into
L^{2}\left(\partial\Omega^{\varepsilon}\right)
. Then,
since the trace of
u
is zero on
\partial\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}|x|^{\alpha-2}u(x\cdot\nabla u)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}|x|^{\alpha-2}x\cdot\nabla\left(u^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{S(\varepsilon)}|x|^{\alpha-1}u^{2}ds-\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}(N-2+\alpha)|x|^{\alpha-2}u^{2}dx,

and

\displaystyle(N-2+\alpha)\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}|x|^{\alpha-2}u^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq-2\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}|x|^{\alpha-2}u(x\cdot\nabla u)dx\leq 2\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}\left(|x|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}|u|\right)\left(|x|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}|\nabla u|\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\{\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}|x|^{\alpha-2}u^{2}dx\right\}^{1/2}\left\{\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u\cdot\nabla udx\right\}^{1/2}.

Finally, (
3.1
) follows by letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
due to
|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.
∎

The inequality (
3.1
) plays a pivotal role and will be essential in the subsequent proof of compact embedding. Now, let us establish that the solution space is indeed a Hilbert space.

Lemma 3.2
.

The space
(\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)})
is a Hilbert space.

Proof.

Firstly, it is straightforward to verify that
(\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)})
is an inner product space. Next, we prove that

(\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)})
is a Hilbert space. For simplicity, we define the weighted space

L_{\alpha}^{2}(\Omega):=\left\{w\left|\ \!w:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}\mbox{ is a measurable function},\mbox{ and }\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\alpha}w\cdot wdx<\infty\right.\right\}.

It is easily checked that
L_{\alpha}^{2}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space with the inner product

(w_{1},w_{2})=\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\alpha}w_{1}\cdot w_{2}dx=\int_{\Omega}\left(|x|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}w_{1}\right)\cdot\left(|x|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}w_{2}\right)dx.

Let
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
be a Cauchy sequence. Then there exist
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
g=(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{N})\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{N}
such that

v_{n}\rightarrow v\mbox{ strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega),\mbox{and }|x|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\nabla v_{n}\rightarrow g\mbox{ strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega)^{N}.

To conclude, we need to show that
\nabla v=|x|^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g
. For this purpose, since

v_{n}\rightarrow v
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have
\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\rightarrow\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}

in the sense of distributions for
i=1,2,\cdots,N
, and the distributional limit is unique. Therefore, it suffices to prove that

\nabla v_{n}\rightarrow|x|^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g\mbox{ strongly in }L_{\alpha}^{2}(\Omega)\Rightarrow\nabla v_{n}\rightarrow|x|^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g\mbox{ strongly in }(C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega))^{\prime}.

Indeed,
for all
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}(\nabla v_{n}-|x|^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g)\varphi dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla v_{n}-|x|^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g\right|dx\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\left||x|^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right|\left||x|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\nabla v_{n}-g\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\int_{\Omega}|x|^{-\alpha}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left||x|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\nabla v_{n}-g\right|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla v_{n}-|x|^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g\right\|_{L_{\alpha}^{2}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0,\ n\rightarrow\infty,

where
C
denotes different constants depending on the context.
∎

Below, we will prove a significant compact embedding theorem, which is vital for our application of the Galerkin method to demonstrate the existence of solutions.

Theorem 3.1
.

\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is compactly embeded in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

To establish the compactness of the embedding it is suffices to show that if
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is a sequence converging weakly to zero in
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, then
\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
by abstract subsequence.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
L^{2}(\Omega)
,
L^{2}(\Omega)^{*}\subset

\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{*}
and hence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
converges weakly to zero in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Consider
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\varepsilon_{0}\right]
. If the sequence

\left\{u_{n}\right\}
does not converge weakly to zero in
W^{1,2}\left(\Omega^{\varepsilon}\right)
,
then there exist
f\in W^{1,2}\left(\Omega^{\varepsilon}\right)^{*}
, a subsequence

\left\{u_{n_{k}}\right\}
and
\delta>0
such that

\left|f\left(u_{n_{k}}\right)\right|\geq\delta

for all
n_{k}
. By passing to a further subsequence if necessary, we can assume that

\left\{u_{n_{k}}\right\}
converges weakly to an element
v
in
W^{1,2}\left(\Omega^{\varepsilon}\right)
. Consequently,

\left\{u_{n_{k}}\right\}
also converges weakly to
v
in
L^{2}\left(\Omega^{\varepsilon}\right)
. Since
\left\{u_{n}\right\}

converges weakly to zero in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and hence also in
L^{2}\left(\Omega^{\varepsilon}\right)
,
it follows that
v=0
almost everywhere on
\Omega^{\varepsilon}
. However, this implies that

f\left(u_{n_{k}}\right)\rightarrow f(v)=f(0)=0
as
n_{k}\rightarrow\infty
, which contradicts the choice of
\delta
. Therefore,

\left\{u_{n}\right\}
must converge weakly to zero in
W^{1,2}\left(\Omega^{\varepsilon}\right)
and, consequently,

\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega^{\varepsilon}\right)}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\left|u_{n}\right|^{2}dx.

(3.2)

However, from
[
12
]
and Lemma
3.1
, we have

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)},\ 1\leq q\leq\frac{2N}{N-2+\alpha},

(3.3)

and by taking
q>2
, we obtain

\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\left|u_{n}\right|^{2}dx\leq\left(\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}|1|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}dx\right)^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\left(\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\left(|u_{n}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}\leq|B(0,\varepsilon)|^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\left\|u_{n}\right\|^{2}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}.

The weak convergence of
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
in
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and (
3.3
) imply that this sequence is bounded in

L^{q}(\Omega)
. Since
q>2
, we have

\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\left|u_{n}\right|^{2}dx\leq C|B(0,\varepsilon)|^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\left\|u_{n}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(B(0,\varepsilon))}\leq C|B(0,\varepsilon)|^{\frac{q-2}{q}}.

Letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
in (
3.2
) shows that
\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, completing the proof.
∎

Next, we will focus on proving the existence of solutions to Problem (

Theorem 3.2
.

Given
T>0
, suppose that

\chi_{\omega}f\in L^{2}(Q),\quad u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\ \mbox{ and }u_{1}\in L^{2}(\Omega).

Then, there exists a unique solution
u
to (

) such that

u\in C([0,T];\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\cap C^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)),

and there exists a constant

\|u\|_{C([0,T];\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\chi_{\omega}f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\right).

(3.4)

Furthermore,

\partial_{\nu}u\in L^{2}(\Sigma),

and there exists a constant
C=C(T,\Omega)>0
such that

\|\partial_{\nu}u\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\chi_{\omega}f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\right).

(3.5)

Proof.

The proof will be divided into two major steps.

Step 1:
Utilizing the well-known result on the unique existence of weak solutions for abstract evolution equations presented in
[
13
]
or the Galerkin method, we derive

u\in C([0,T];\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\cap C^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)).

Multiplying the first equation of (

) by

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\Big(|u_{t}(t)|^{2}+|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u(t)\cdot\nabla u(t)\Big)\mathrm{d}x=2\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\omega}f(x,t)u_{t}(t)\mathrm{d}x.

Define

E(t)=\left(\left\||x|^{\alpha}\nabla u(t)\cdot\nabla u(t)\right\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E^{2}(t)\leq C\left(\|\chi_{\omega}f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}E(t)+E^{2}(t)\right),\ t\in(0,T),

E^{\prime}(t)\leq C\left(\|\chi_{\omega}f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+E(t)\right),\ t\in(0,T).

By applying Gronwall’s inequality and integrating over

, we get

E(t)\leq C_{T}\left(E(0)+\|\chi_{\omega}f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\right),\ t\in(0,T).

This implies (

Step 2:
We define a mapping

\Lambda:H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(Q)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Sigma),\ (u_{0},u_{1},\chi_{\omega}f)\mapsto\partial_{\nu}u,

where

is a bounded linear operator, i.e.,

(3.7)

Given the density of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

and a density argument, it suffices to demonstrate the result for
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega),u_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
and
\chi_{\omega}f\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
. Select a function
\zeta\in C^{3}(\overline{\Omega})
that satisfies

0\leq\zeta\leq 1\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad\zeta\equiv 0\mbox{ on }B(0,\varepsilon),\quad\zeta\equiv 1\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N}-B(0,2\varepsilon),\ \mbox{ and }B(0,3\varepsilon)\subseteq\Omega.

Replace
V
by
\zeta V
in Lemma
3.3
(still denoting
\zeta V
by

C^{2}
-vector field
V
on
\overline{\Omega}
that satisfies

V(x)=\nu(x),\ x\in\partial\Omega;\quad|V(x)|\leq 1,\ x\in\Omega;\quad\supp V\subseteq\Omega\setminus B(0,\varepsilon).

Multiplying the first equation of (
2.1
) by
V\cdot\nabla u
and integrating over
Q
, we obtain

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{Q}\chi_{\omega}f(x,t)(V\cdot\nabla u)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{Q}u_{tt}(V\cdot\nabla u)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-\iint_{Q}(V\cdot\nabla u)\Div\left(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=:H_{1}+H_{2}.

Calculating

\displaystyle H_{1}

4
Carleman estimate

To achieve controllability results, it is necessary to employ a Carleman estimate, which is a fundamental mathematical tool in the realm of partial differential equations. By utilizing appropriately chosen weight functions, we can investigate how external control influences the behavior of a system. The weighted integral aids in amplifying contributions from specific regions within the domain, enabling the extraction of information that may otherwise be difficult to obtain. Carleman estimates are pivotal in establishing unique continuation properties, which are crucial for proving controllability. The primary objective of this section is to derive Carleman estimates, and we will prove Theorem

The proof follows a similar approach to the proof of Theorem

Since

\displaystyle-\iint_{Q}\Div(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla w)(g\cdot\nabla w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\iint_{Q}\Div\left(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla w(g\cdot\nabla w)\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{Q}|x|^{\alpha}\nabla w\cdot\nabla(g\cdot\nabla w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\iint_{\Sigma}|x|^{\alpha}(\nabla w\cdot\nu)(g\cdot\nabla w)\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{Q}|x|^{\alpha}\left((Dg\nabla w)\cdot\nabla w+\frac{1}{2}g\cdot\nabla|\nabla w|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\iint_{\Sigma}|x|^{\alpha}\varphi|\partial_{\nu}w|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{Q}|x|^{\alpha}(Dg\nabla w)\cdot\nabla w\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 12.80373pt+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{Q}\Div\left(|x|^{\alpha}g|\nabla w|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-\frac{1}{2}\iint_{Q}\Div(|x|^{\alpha}g)|\nabla w|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

&\leq C\iint_{\omega\times(0,T)}\varphi\left(w_{t}^{2}+|x|^{\alpha}\nabla w\cdot\nabla w\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C\iint_{Q}\varphi F^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

(4.4)

Observability inequality and exact controllability

In this section, we derive the observability inequality for the system described by (

(u_{0},u_{1})\in L^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique solution to (
2.1
) that starts from

(u_{0},u_{1})
and satisfies the terminal conditions:

u(\cdot,T)=0\ \mbox{and}\ u_{t}(\cdot,T)=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega.

First, we introduce the operator
J
defined as

J:\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}

where
J
maps
(z_{0},z_{1})
to:

J(z_{0},z_{1}):=\left(u_{t}(\cdot,0),-u(\cdot,0)\right).

Here,
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
denotes the dual space of
\mathcal{H}
, with the duality pairing between

\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, denoted by
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}
. Note that we identify the dual of

L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
with itself.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.
.

We demonstrate the controllability of the system on the domain

\omega
.

Assume
0\in\omega
. According to Theorem
3.2
, the following system:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Div(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u)=\chi_{\omega}f,&\mbox{in }Q,\\
u(\cdot,T)=0,\partial_{t}u(\cdot,T)=0,&\mbox{in }\partial Q,\\
u=0,&\mbox{on }\Sigma,\end{cases}

admits a unique solution
u
that satisfies:

u(\cdot,0)\in L^{2}(\Omega)\ \mbox{and}\ u_{t}(\cdot,0)\in\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\Omega),

By setting
f=\chi_{\omega}z
, and multiplying
z
to the first equation of (
2.1
), we derive

\displaystyle\iint_{Q}\left(\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Div(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u)\right)z\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\iint_{Q}\chi_{\omega}z^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(zu_{t}-z_{t}u\right)\mathrm{d}x\bigg|_{0}^{T}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}^{2}z-\Div(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla z)u\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{\Sigma}u|x|^{\alpha}\nabla z\cdot\nu-z|x|^{\alpha}\nabla u\cdot\nu\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t

\begin{split}\left\langle J(z_{0},z_{1}),(z_{0},z_{1})\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}=\iint_{Q}\chi_{\omega}z^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

7
Concluding Remarks

In this study, we establish the controllability for a class of high-dimensional hyperbolic equations characterized by a single interior degenerate point. This is achieved by employing the Carleman estimate method to derive the corresponding observability inequality. It is important to note that the selection of the multiplier is highly contingent upon the position of the degenerate point. In scenarios involving two or more interior degenerate points, the methodology for constructing an appropriate multiplier remains ambiguous. A plausible approach is to isolate the degenerate interior points, thereby enabling the energy of the degenerate hyperbolic equation to be controlled through both the boundary control domain and the isolated domains surrounding the interior degeneracies. Nevertheless, the rigorous implementation of this strategy remains an open question.

Future research may investigate the case where the weight function
w
features countably many interior degenerate points
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. In such cases, we select a control domain
\omega
such that

\omega\supset\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}B(x_{n},r_{n}),

where
B(x_{n},r_{n})\cap B(x_{m},r_{m})=\emptyset
for all distinct
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Alternatively, the set
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
could represent all rational points on
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\setminus\Omega
. Despite these considerations, the challenge of constructing an appropriate multiplier persists as an unresolved issue.
