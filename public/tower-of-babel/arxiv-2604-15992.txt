Solving Fuzzy Satisfiability via Mixed-Integer Non-Linear Programming Thanks:
Pablo F. Castro

Abstract
This paper introduces SATFuL , a SAT solver for fuzzy logics. In contrast to the Boolean case, for which numerous SAT solvers exist, the SAT problem for fuzzy logics has attracted less attention, even though these tools have interesting applications. Unlike existing SAT solvers for fuzzy logics, SATFuL uses MINLP (Mixed Integer Non-Linear Programming) solvers to check the satisfiability of fuzzy formulas. This approach offers certain benefits; for instance, our tool can handle all major variations of fuzzy propositional logic, whereas other fuzzy solvers are usually tailored to specific versions of fuzzy logic. We conduct some experiments and demonstrate that the performance of our tool is comparable with state-of-the-art fuzzy solvers for Łukasiewicz logic, and outperforms available solvers for Product logic. The approach is sound and complete and can be easily extended to accommodate new fuzzy operators.

Boolean SAT solvers

There are several approaches to solving the SAT problem for fuzzy logics.

Furthermore, the performance of existing fuzzy SAT solvers remains far behind that of Boolean SAT solvers, which can typically handle formulas several orders of magnitude larger. In this paper, we build on the initial ideas proposed in

Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the concepts and notation required for the remainder of the paper.
We focus on fuzzy logics, i.e., many-valued logics in which the truth value is taken from the interval
[0,1]
. Most fuzzy logics are constructed upon the concept of
t-norm
. A t-norm is a binary operator
\otimes:[0,1]^{2}\rightarrow[0,1]
that satisfies
commutativity
,
associativity
,
monotonicity
, and
identity
.
There are different choices for
\otimes
; each of them gives a different logic. In this paper, we concentrate on the following logics.

Łukasiewicz Logic.

This logic is obtained by considering
x\otimes y=\max(0,x+y-1)
as the
t-norm. Hence, the usual operators are defined as follows:

•

x\lukand y=\max(0,x+y-1)
,

•

x\lukor y=\min(1,x+y)
,

•

x\lukimplies y=(x\leq y){?}1{:}(1-x)+y

•

\luknot x=1-x
.

Product Logic.

Product logic is obtained by using the product as t-norm, in this case, we obtain the following operators:

•

x\productand y=x*y
,

•

x\productor y=x+y-x*y
,

•

x\productimplies y=(x\leq y)?1{:}y/x
,

•

\productnot x=(x=0)?1{:}0
.

Gödel Logic.

This logic is obtained by using the t-norm
\min
as the conjunction and defining the logical operators as follows:

•

x\godeland y=\min(x,y)
,

•

x\godelor y=\max(x,y)
,

•

x\godelimplies y=(x\leq y)?1{:}y
,

•

\godelnot x=(x=0)?1{:}0
.

Mixed Integer Linear Programming.

Given a collection

Linear programming is in P, MILP is NP-complete, while MINLP is NP-hard and decidable for bounded problems. Given a problem

) such that satisfies the inequalities

, an

. Given an assignment

Related Approaches.

It is worth comparing Algorithm

In

uses a tableau procedure together with MILP solvers (

It is worth stressing that in previous works only Łukasiewicz and similar logics are reduced to MILP; reductions of Product logic to arithmetic constraints are not discussed therein.
Furthermore, it is worth noting that Theorem

Conclusions

We introduced the tool
SATFuL
, an open-source Python-based SAT solver for fuzzy logics that translates fuzzy clauses into MINLP problems via a recursive algorithm. We have shown that
SATFuL
outperforms
MNiBLoS
, one of the few SAT solvers for Product logic. Furthermore, for Łukasiewicz logic, its performance is aligned with the state-of-the-art
fuzzySAT
solver for SAT formulas, and outperforms this tool for UNSAT formulas.
We aimed to provide a maintainable SAT solver for fuzzy logic that is easy to use and extend, following the successful path of Boolean SAT solvers. There are simple extensions for the SAT solver that were not included in this paper. For instance, adding support for stochastic variables and relational operators. We leave them for future work.

f(x_{\phi})=f|_{\mathcal{X}}(\phi)
. If
f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})>f(x_{\psi^{\prime}})
, then
f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})-f(x_{\psi^{\prime}})>0
and then
f(x)=1
and also
f(x_{\phi}^{\prime})>0
, and by the equations in
P_{\psi}
we have
f(x^{\prime})*f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})=f(x)*f(x_{\psi^{\prime}})
and then
f(x^{\prime})*f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})=f(x_{\psi^{\prime}})
, since
f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})
is not
0
we get that
f(x^{\prime})=f(x_{\psi^{\prime}})/f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})
and so also
f(x_{\phi})=f(x_{\psi^{\prime}})/f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})
, i.e., by induction
f(x_{\phi})=f|_{\mathcal{X}}(\psi^{\prime})/f|_{\mathcal{X}}(\phi^{\prime})=f|_{\mathcal{X}}(\phi)
.

Case
\phi=\phi^{\prime}\productor\psi^{\prime}
. By definition

f(x_{\phi})=f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})+f(x_{\psi^{\prime}})-f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})*f(x_{\psi^{\prime}})
and by induction we get:
f(x_{\phi})=f|_{\mathcal{X}}(\phi^{\prime})+f|_{\mathcal{X}}(\psi^{\prime})-f|_{\mathcal{X}}(\phi^{\prime})*f|_{\mathcal{X}}(\psi^{\prime})=f|_{\mathcal{X}}(\phi)
.

Case
\phi=\productnot\phi^{\prime}
. We proceed by cases. If
f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})=0
, then

f(x)=0
since
f(x)\geq f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})
and
x
is a variable to minimize, therefore

f(x_{\phi})=1-f(x)=1
. On the other hand, by induction we have
f|_{\mathcal{X}}(\phi^{\prime})=0
and so
f|_{\mathcal{X}}(\phi)=1=f(x_{\phi})
. If
f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})>0
then

f(x)=1
, because it is an integer variable and
f(x)\geq f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})
, and then

f(x_{\phi})=0
. We also have
f|_{\mathcal{X}}(\phi^{\prime})>0
and by definition of

\productnot
we have
f|_{\mathcal{X}}(\phi)=0=f(\phi)
.

Our next lemma states that any valuation of fuzzy variables can be extended to an optimal assignment. Note that here we work on fuzzy formulas (no clauses), which can always be assigned a value. Intuitively, this lemma states that the systems of equations constructed by Algorithm
2
are not overly restrictive.

Lemma 2

Let
\phi
be a fuzzy formula and let
\mathcal{X}=\mathit{Var}(\phi)
, then:

\forall v\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{X}}:\exists!f\in\mathcal{O}(P_{\phi}):v=f|_{\mathcal{X}}.

Proof

The proof is by induction on
\phi
.

Base case.

If
\phi=k
then
\mathit{Var}(\phi)=\emptyset
, and the property holds trivially.
If
\phi=x
for a fuzzy variable, then the unique equation in
P_{\phi}
is
0\leq x\leq 1
that is satisfied by assigning
f(x)=v(x)
no other equations need to be satisfied and no variable needs to be optimized then
f\in\mathcal{O}(P_{\phi})
.

Inductive Case.

We proceed by cases:

Case
\phi=\phi^{\prime}\productand\psi^{\prime}
. Let
v
be a valuation over
\mathit{Var}(\phi)
,
by induction we have unique assignment
g
over the variables in
\mathcal{O}(P_{\phi^{\prime}})
and
h
over the variables in
\mathcal{O}(P_{\psi^{\prime}})
with
v(x)=g(x)
for
x\in\mathit{Var}(\phi^{\prime})
and
v(x)=h(x)
for
x\in\mathit{Var}(\psi^{\prime})
thus they must coincide in the variables in
\mathit{Var}(P_{\phi^{\prime}})\cap\mathit{Var}(P_{\psi^{\prime}})
. Then, we define an assignment
f
as follows: if
x\in\mathit{Var}(P_{\phi^{\prime}})
then

f(x)=g(x)
, if
x\in\mathit{Var}(P_{\psi^{\prime}})
then
f(x)=h(x)
, and for
x_{\phi^{\prime}\productand\psi^{\prime}}
we define

f(x_{\phi^{\prime}\productand\psi^{\prime}})=f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})*f(x_{\psi^{\prime}})
, this is the unique way of satisfying the corresponding equation in
P_{\phi}
, therefore
f|_{\mathcal{X}}=v
and it is unique.

Case
\phi=\phi^{\prime}\productor\psi^{\prime}
. The proof is analogous to the case above.

Case
\phi=\phi\lukimplies\psi^{\prime}
. As in the case
\phi=\phi^{\prime}\productand\psi^{\prime}
we consider assignments
g
over the variables in
\mathcal{O}(P_{\phi^{\prime}})
and
h
over the variables in
\mathcal{O}(P_{\psi^{\prime}})
. We define
f
as follows: if
x\in\mathit{Var}(P_{\phi^{\prime}})
then

f(x)=g(x)
, if
x\in\mathit{Var}(P_{\psi^{\prime}})
then
f(x)=h(x)
, and for
f(x_{\phi^{\prime}\lukimplies\psi^{\prime}})
, we consider cases, if
f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})\leq f(x_{\psi^{\prime}})
then we set
f(x_{\phi^{\prime}\lukimplies\psi^{\prime}})=1
and
f(x)=0
which satisfies the equations and minimizes the integer variable
x
, note that the possible values for
x
and
x_{\phi}
are unique. If
f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})>f(x_{\psi^{\prime}})
then we set
f(x)=1
and
f(x_{\phi})=1-f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})+f(x_{\psi^{\prime}})
note that
0\leq f(x_{\phi})\leq 1
(since the last assumption) and also these values are the unique possible and satisfy the equations.

Case
\phi=\phi^{\prime}\productimplies\psi^{\prime}
. As above consider assignment
g
over the variables in
\mathcal{O}(P_{\phi}^{\prime})
and
h
over the variables in
\mathcal{O}(P_{\psi}^{\prime})
. We define
f
as follows: if
x\in\mathit{Var}(P_{\phi^{\prime}})
then

f(x)=g(x)
, if
x\in\mathit{Var}(P_{\psi^{\prime}})
then
f(x)=h(x)
, and we
define
f(x_{\phi})
by cases. If
f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})\leq f(x_{\psi^{\prime}})
, then we set

f(x)=0
(this variable has to be minimized), necessarily
f(x^{\prime})=0

(since we have the inequation
x^{\prime}\leq x
), then we have to set
f(x_{\phi})=1
; this satisfies the equation
x^{\prime}*x_{\phi^{\prime}}=x*x_{\psi^{\prime}}
, and is the unique assignment possible that minimizes
x
. Note that for the case
x_{\phi^{\prime}}=0
this assignment is well-defined since
x=x^{\prime}=0
.
If
f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})>f(x_{\psi^{\prime}})
then we set
f(x)=1
,
f(x)=0
is not possible because we have the inequation
x>x_{\phi^{\prime}}-x_{\psi^{\prime}}
, thus we have
f(x^{\prime})=f(x_{\psi}^{\prime})/f(x_{\phi}^{\prime})
which is well defined and satisfies the equations.

Case
\phi=\productnot\phi^{\prime}
. Consider an assignment
g
over the variables
\mathcal{O}(P_{\phi}^{\prime})
such that
v(x)=g(x)
for all
x\in\mathit{Var}(\phi^{\prime})
which exists by induction. We define
f
as follows,
f(x)=g(x)
if
x\in\mathit{Var}(\phi^{\prime})
. For
x_{\phi}
we define
f
by cases.
If
f(x_{\phi^{\prime}})=0
, then we define
f(x)=0
, and then
f(x_{\phi})=1
, which satisfies the equations, since
x
needs to be minimized, this is the optimal assignment to it. If

x_{\phi^{\prime}}\geq 0
then necessarily
f(x)=1
and so
f(x_{\phi})=0
which is the unique assignment that satisfies the equation.

Now we prove the main theorems.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We prove the theorem for one clause; it is straightforward to extend this proof to several clauses. Without loss of generality we assume
\mathcal{X}=\mathit{Var}(\phi)
.
Let
\ell\leq\phi\leq u
be a fuzzy clause. Let
v\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{X}}
be a valuation such that
v\vDash_{\infty}\ell\leq\phi\leq u
, that is,

\ell\leq v(\phi)\leq u
, by Lemma
2
there is a
f\in\mathcal{O}(P_{\phi})
such that
f|_{\mathcal{X}}=v
, and so by Lemma
1

we get
f(x_{\phi})=v(\phi)
, then
\ell\leq f(x_{\phi})\leq u
which proves
\{v\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{X}}\mid v\vDash_{\infty}\ell\leq\phi\leq u\}\subseteq\{f|_{\mathcal{X}}\mid f\in\mathcal{O}(P_{\Phi})\}
. Now, let
f|_{\mathcal{X}}\in\{f|_{\mathcal{X}}\mid f\in\mathcal{O}(P_{\Phi})\}
, then

\ell\leq f(x_{\phi})\leq u
, and so by Lemma
1
we get
\ell\leq f|_{\mathcal{X}}(\phi)\leq u
which implies that

f|_{\mathcal{X}}\in\{v\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{X}}\mid v\vDash_{\infty}\ell\leq\phi\leq u\}
and therefore:

\{f|_{\mathcal{X}}\mid f\in\mathcal{O}(P_{\Phi})\}\subseteq\{v\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{X}}\mid v\vDash_{\infty}\ell\leq\phi\leq u\}
which proves the theorem.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

The result follows from Theorem
3.1
. If Alg.
2
returns “SAT” then there is a
f\in\mathcal{O}(P_{\Phi})
such that

\ell\leq f(x_{\phi})\leq u
, but then by Theorem
3.1
we have a valuation
v
such that
\ell\leq v(\phi)\leq u
and the formula is SAT.
If there is a valuation
v
such that
\ell\leq v(\phi)\leq u
, then by Lemma
2
we have that there is an assignment
f\in\mathcal{O}(P_{\Phi})
such that
f|_{\mathcal{X}}=v
and so
\ell\leq f(x_{\phi})\leq u
, then Alg.
2
returns “SAT” given that the solver used is complete.
