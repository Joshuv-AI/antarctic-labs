Don’t Let a Few Network Failures Slow the Entire AllReduce
Peiqing Chen, Jiedong Jiang, Nengneng Yu, Yuefeng Wang, Sixian Xiong, Wei Wang, Zaoxing Liu, [4pt] University of Maryland, College Park

Abstract
Network failures are among the most frequent hardware faults in large-scale GPU clusters and a leading cause of training-job interruptions. Modern collective communication libraries such as NCCL mitigate network failures by rerouting traffic through surviving NICs on the same server, trading reduced inter-node bandwidth for uninterrupted training. However, the degraded server remains on the critical path of the standard ring algorithm, slowing the entire collective. We present the first information-theoretic lower bound on AllReduce completion time under asymmetric network bandwidth and show that when the straggler retains at least half of its original bandwidth, the unavoidable overhead relative to the fault-free optimum is only O ⁡ ( 1 / p ) O(1/p) for p p GPUs. We then design OptCC , a four-stage pipelined AllReduce algorithm that approaches this lower bound. Experiments on SimAI confirm that OptCC closes the gap left by existing fault-tolerant schemes: under practical network failures with up to 50% bandwidth loss, OptCC completes AllReduce within 2–6% of NCCL’s fault-free ring performance, whereas the state-of-the-art incurs up to 57% overhead.

Single straggler, one GPU per server in DP group.

Algorithm Design

Our algorithm partitions each GPU’s
n
-element vector into many small
segments and, for every segment, decomposes the AllReduce into four
logical stages: (1) reduce-scatter among the
p{-}1
healthy GPUs,
(2) upload partial sums to the straggler, (3) download global sums from
the straggler, and (4) allgather among the healthy GPUs. Stages 2
and 3 use only the slow straggler link while Stages 1 and 4 use fast links between healthy
GPUs—these are
disjoint hardware resources
. We
exploit this by pipelining segments so that the slow Stages 2/3 of one
segment run in parallel with Stages 1/4 of other segments, effectively
hiding the straggler overhead.
We present the algorithm for the single-straggler, one-GPU-per-server
setting in this section; the algorithm designs for multiple stragglers
(Appendix
D
) and multi-GPU-per-server
(Appendix
E
) are deferred to the appendix.

4.1
Four-Stage Decomposition

We partition the
n

k

p-1

For each segment, the AllReduce proceeds through four stages

We define a

as a point-to-point transfer of one section between
two GPUs, and enforce that each NIC receives at most one flow at a time;
overlapping incoming flows would contend for bandwidth and cause unpredictable

Stage 2: Upload to the straggler.

s

\ell\cdot s

Each segment has

Once a section’s global sum returns to a healthy server via Stage 3, it is

p-2

Stage ordering and correctness.

For a single section, the four stages must respect data dependencies: Stage 1
produces the partial sum consumed by Stage 2, Stage 2 feeds Stage 3, and
Stage 3 produces the global sum consumed by Stage 4. Two orderings satisfy
these constraints:
(1) S1
{}\to{}
S2
{}\to{}
S3
{}\to{}
S4, and
(2) S3

S1
{}\to{}
S4

In ordering (1), the healthy servers first reduce-scatter among

global sum and downloads it for allgather. In ordering (2), the straggler
uploads its local value
first

their reduce-scatter (which now produces the global sum directly), perform the
allgather, and finally send the result back to the straggler. Both orderings compute a correct AllReduce.

6
Conclusion

Network failures are an operational reality in large GPU clusters, yet
their impact on collective communication has lacked a principled
theoretical treatment.
This paper closes that gap for AllReduce: we derive tight
information-theoretic lower bounds showing that, when the straggler
retains at least half of its bandwidth, the unavoidable overhead is
only
O(1/p)
—fundamentally negligible in large clusters—and we
design OptCC, an algorithm that achieves these bounds.
The key insight is that healthy GPUs carry enough spare bandwidth to
absorb a straggler’s deficit through careful pipeline interleaving,
so that near-optimal performance is attainable
without any
hardware redundancy, job restart, or offline pre-computation
.
SimAI-based evaluation confirms 2–6% overhead relative to the
fault-free NCCL ring, compared with up to 57% for existing schemes.
Extending the bounds to latency-sensitive settings and dynamic
bandwidth fluctuations is left to future work.

T\geq\frac{F_{\mathrm{h}\to\mathrm{h}}+\ell F_{\mathrm{h}\to\mathrm{s}}}{p-1}=\frac{F-F_{\mathrm{s}\to\mathrm{h}}+(\ell-1)F_{\mathrm{h}\to\mathrm{s}}}{p-1}.

Using
F\geq 2(p-1)n
and substituting

F_{\mathrm{s}\to\mathrm{h}}=xn
,
F_{\mathrm{h}\to\mathrm{s}}=yn
yields

\frac{T}{n}\geq 2+\frac{(\ell-1)y-x}{p-1}.

(9)

Similarly, the total
receiving
work across healthy GPUs is at least

F_{\mathrm{h}\to\mathrm{h}}+\ell F_{\mathrm{s}\to\mathrm{h}}
, which implies

\frac{T}{n}\geq 2+\frac{(\ell-1)x-y}{p-1}.

(10)

Min–max form and symmetry reduction.

Combining (
7
), (
8
),
(
9
), and (
10
), for all

x\geq 1,y\geq 1
,

\frac{T}{n}\geq\max\!\left\{2+\frac{(\ell-1)y-x}{p-1},\;2+\frac{(\ell-1)x-y}{p-1},\;\ell x,\;\ell y\right\}.

Let
g(x,y)
denote the right-hand side. It is convex (a max of affine
functions) and symmetric under swapping

and
y
, hence the minimum over

x\geq 1,y\geq 1
is attained on the diagonal
x=y=z\geq 1
. Substituting
x=y=z
,

\frac{T}{n}\geq\min_{z\geq 1}\max\!\left\{\,2+\frac{(\ell-2)z}{p-1},\;\ell z\,\right\}.

Solving the 1-D minimization.

If
\ell\geq 2
, both branches are nondecreasing in

, so the minimum is at

and
\frac{T}{n}\geq\ell
.
If
1<\ell\leq 2
, the minimum is attained at the intersection

2+\frac{(\ell-2)z}{p-1}=\ell z
, i.e.,

z^{\star}=\frac{2(p-1)}{\ell(p-2)+2},\qquad\frac{T}{n}\geq\ell z^{\star}=\frac{2\ell(p-1)}{\ell(p-2)+2}.

Therefore,

T\;\geq\;n\cdot\begin{cases}\ell,&\ell\geq 2,\\[4.0pt]
\dfrac{2\ell(p-1)}{\ell(p-2)+2},&1<\ell\leq 2.\end{cases}

Equivalently,

T\;\geq\;\max\!\left\{\,\dfrac{2\ell(p-1)}{\ell(p-2)+2},\;\ell\,\right\}\cdot n.

\square

Lemma 7

(Total traffic bound)

Proof.

Identical to Lemma

.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Write

(by Lemma

We derive

Straggler bottlenecks.

Each straggler

The

Combining.

Taking the maximum of all

Remark 9
.

Unlike the other two subsections in this section, the refinement of the
healthy-side bottleneck does not extend directly to the present setting with
multiple bad servers. The reason is that, once the inter-server traffic is
decomposed according to healthy and bad endpoints, one now obtains four directed

rather than three. In particular, the additional bad-to-bad traffic

is not captured, so the
same strengthening trick used in the previous two subsections cannot be applied
here in a direct way.

Consistency with the single-straggler bound.

Setting

recovers

,
which coincides with Theorem
1
.

Multi-GPU per Server Case

Finally, we prove the bounds for the multi-GPU server setting with a single straggler: Theorem

Write

for the number of servers. We treat each server as a supernode.
Inter-server communication uses NICs; intra-server communication uses NVLink.
After normalization, each healthy NIC has unit rate, so each healthy server has
aggregate NIC capacity

Let

the inter-server NIC traffic,

NIC-time model.

We count

Theorem
3
(restated)
.

Any correct AllReduce algorithm satisfies

T\;\geq\;\frac{n}{g}\cdot\max\!\left\{\frac{2\ell(q-1)}{1+\ell(q-1)},\;\ell\right\}.

Lemma 10

(Inter-group traffic per coordinate via supernodes)
.

Partition the

\mathcal{G}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{G}_{q}
.
Count only

to

to some GPU in

with

.

Proof.

Fix a coordinate

and let

.

Supernode flow graph.

For each time

, define a directed multigraph

on vertex set

(the groups as

that completes by time

.

For a group index

and time

can compute

at time

whose local state at time

(equivalently, there exists a decoding function of its

for all inputs).

Let

and define the set of
roots at the first completion time

A_{j}\;=\;\{r\in[q]:\text{group $r$ can compute $s_{j}$ at time }t_{j}\},\qquad a_{j}=|A_{j}|\ (\geq 1).

Key reachability property.

Fix
r\in A_{j}
. Then every group

must have a directed path

in

; otherwise the state within group

is

(for coordinate

at time

.
In particular, for any chosen

, all

reach
r_{0}
.
Choose an arbitrary

in

“Last-hop at

” into each root and disjointness.

For every
r\in A_{j}
, there exists an inter-group edge
e_{r}
completing

exactly

whose head is

would receive

at time

The edges

are pairwise distinct (different heads).

Any

whose head is not

must complete at time

(otherwise information could not continue onward to reach

by time

Therefore,

Hence the number of inter-group

Edges after

.

For any group

.
If

Per-coordinate bound and summation.

Let

be the number of inter-group element-transfers

Summing over

.
∎

Remark 14

(Dropping the NVLink term)
.

In addition to the NIC constraints, we also have the NVLink-time constraint

since the

. Using the global

where we used

Intuitively, in

denotes the new

(1+(2{-}\ell)/\ell)\cdot\ell\,s^{\prime}=2s^{\prime}
, matching the ring slot
exactly.

Data accounting.

Each group of four segments (one per pattern) processes two kinds of data:

•

Ring path:

(p{-}1)
sections of size
s^{\prime}
, totalling

(p{-}1)\,s^{\prime}
elements.

•

P2P path:

(2{-}\ell)\,s^{\prime}/\ell
additional elements
allreduced via the straggler bubbles.

With
k/4
groups (i.e.
k
segments total) the constraint

n=k\bigl[(p{-}1)\,s^{\prime}+(2{-}\ell)\,s^{\prime}/\ell\bigr]
gives

s^{\prime}\;=\;\frac{n\,\ell}{k\bigl[(p{-}2)\ell+2\bigr]}\,.

Total time.

Because every slot now has duration
2s^{\prime}
, each middle body takes

2(p{-}1)\,s^{\prime}
. The straggler’s serialized recv chain across all

k{+}1
bodies takes
2k(p{-}1)\,s^{\prime}
(the HEAD bub-up and TAIL Stage 2
flows are shorter than middle Stage 2 flows, but their durations sum to
exactly
2k(p{-}1)\,s^{\prime}
). In addition, ring-flow dependencies at the
HEAD and TAIL add a startup/drain overhead of

2(\ell{-}1)(p{-}1)\,s^{\prime}
(at
\ell=1
the HEAD/TAIL flows are short
enough to hide entirely within the straggler chain; at
\ell=2
they
contribute a full extra body). Hence

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=\bigl[2k+2(\ell{-}1)\bigr]\,(p{-}1)\,s^{\prime}=\frac{2(p{-}1)\,\ell\,n}{(p{-}2)\ell+2}\,\frac{k+\ell-1}{k}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\;\frac{2(p{-}1)\,\ell\,n}{(p{-}2)\ell+2}\,.

Comparing with Theorem
6
, we see that our algorithm
achieves bandwidth optimality without requiring
p\to\infty
, confirming its optimality even for small clusters.

Overhead bound.

Compared with the non-degraded baseline
T_{0}=2(p{-}1)\,n/p
(single
GPU per server,
g{=}1
),

\frac{T}{T_{0}}=\frac{p\,\ell}{(p{-}2)\ell+2}\,.

This ratio is increasing in
\ell
on
[1,2]
and attains its maximum

p/(p{-}1)=1+1/(p{-}1)
at
\ell=2
, so the overhead is at most

1+1/(p{-}1)
for all
\ell\in[1,2]
.

D.3
Time Analysis

Each segment has
p{-}m
sections of size

s=n/(k(p{-}m))
.
In each parallel body, the healthy GPUs occupy
p{-}1

of
2s
each, for a total of
2(p{-}1)\,s
.
Each straggler

p{-}m
flows, each of

\ell_{i}\,s
, giving a straggler chain of

\ell_{i}(p{-}m)\,s
. In the current schedule, additional
2(m-1)
sections between healthy servers will be sent after the healthy servers finish communication with stragglers. The body duration is therefore

T_{\mathrm{body}}\;=\;\max\!\left\{\,2(p{-}1)\,s,\;\;\left(\ell_{1}(p{-}m)+2(m-1)\right)\,s\,\right\}\,.

With
k/4
groups (each spanning four patterns) plus startup/drain
equivalent to one body, the total time is

T\;=\;\left(\frac{k}{4}+1\right)\cdot 4\,T_{\mathrm{body}}\;=\;(k+4)\,T_{\mathrm{body}}\,.

Case
\ell_{1}<2
(ring-bottlenecked).

The body is determined by the healthy GPUs:

T_{\mathrm{body}}=2(p{-}1)\,s
. Substituting
s
:

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=(k+4)\cdot 2(p{-}1)\,s=\frac{2(p{-}1)}{p{-}m}\,n\,\frac{k+4}{k}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\;\frac{2(p{-}1)}{p{-}m}\,n\,.

Case
\ell_{1}\geq 2
(straggler-bottlenecked).

The body is determined by the worst straggler:

T_{\mathrm{body}}=\left(\ell_{1}(p{-}m)+2(m-1)\right)\,s

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=(k+4)\cdot\left(\ell_{1}(p{-}m)+2(m-1)\right)\,s=\frac{\ell_{1}(p{-}m)+2(m-1)}{p-m}\,n\,\frac{k+4}{k}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\;\left(\ell_{1}+\frac{2(m-1)}{p-m}\right)\,n\,.

From Theorem
2
, the lower bound (for

T\geq\max\{2(p{-}1)/(p{-}m{+}\sum 1/\ell_{i}),\;\ell_{1}\}\cdot n
.
Our algorithm achieves

T\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\max\left\{\frac{2(p{-}1)}{p{-}m},\;\ell_{1}+\frac{2(m-1)}{p-m}\right\}\cdot n.

In both cases, the ratio of the achieved time to the lower bound approaches

As outlined in Section
4.4

the total number of servers. Each GPU within a server has its own dedicated NIC for inter-server communication, and the
g

Notation.

We adopt the same symbols as Section
4

n

g

of size

n/g

k

segments

q{-}1

of size

s\;=\;\frac{n}{g\,k\,(q{-}1)}

s

(g{-}1)\times
NIC speed) takes
s/(g{-}1)

E.2

configuration commonly deployed in practice:

•

Each GPU has its own pair of NVLink up/down links, with
per-direction bandwidth equal to
(g{-}1)

of its
g{-}1

•

Different GPUs’ NVLink lanes do not interfere, so all

(g{-}1)\times

We extend our algorithm to this setting in a way that closely

Recall that one healthy NIC hop on a section of

The remaining accounting then follows the single-GPU analysis of
Section

Counting bodies is now straightforward (using the head/tail

Case
\ell\geq 2
(straggler-bottlenecked).

Each parallel body takes
\ell\,(q{-}1)\,s=\ell n/(gk)

T\;\leq\;\ell\,(q{-}1)\,s\,(k+5.5)\;=\;\frac{\ell\,n\,(k+5.5)}{g\,k}\;\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\;\frac{\ell\,n}{g}\,,

The bubble-filling technique of Section
