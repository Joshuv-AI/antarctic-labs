Safe Control of Feedback-Interconnected Systems via Singular Perturbations
Stefano Di Gregorio, Guido Carnevale, Giuseppe Notarstefano

Abstract
Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) have emerged as a powerful tool in the design of safety-critical controllers for nonlinear systems. In modern applications, complex systems often involve the feedback interconnection of subsystems evolving at different timescales, e.g., two parts from different physical domains (e.g., the electrical and mechanical parts of robotic systems) or a physical plant and an (optimization or control) algorithm. In these scenarios, safety constraints often involve only a portion of the overall system. Inspired by singular perturbations for stability analysis, we develop a formal procedure to lift a safety certificate designed on a reduced-order model to the overall feedback-interconnected system. Specifically, we show that under a sufficient timescale separation between slow and fast dynamics, a composite CBF can be designed to certify the forward invariance of the safe set for the interconnected system. As a result, the online safety filter only needs to be solved for the lower-dimensional, reduced-order model. We numerically test the proposed approach on: (i) a robotic arm with joint motor dynamics, and (ii) a physical plant driven by an optimization algorithm.



I
Introduction

Autonomous and complex systems operating in real world
scenarios require the design of control algorithms capable of enforcing
strict safety operational constraints.
This challenging requirement has given rise to intense
research efforts in the control literature, as reviewed in the recent survey

[
1
]
.

Among existing methods, Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) have emerged as a
powerful tool for designing safety-critical controllers for nonlinear
systems (see the comprehensive
references
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
). Their versatility has been demonstrated across different domains, including
robotics
[
5
]
, autonomous
vehicles
[
6
]
, and human-robot
interaction
[
7
]
.
A major structural limitation arises when the control input does not directly affect the safety constraint, leading to a high relative degree. To address this, High-Order CBFs (HOCBFs)
[
8
]
have been proposed to propagate safety via successive differentiations of the constraint.
Despite its effectiveness, this approach requires a uniform relative degree across the safe set.
This limitation has motivated a shift toward more flexible architectures, such as leveraging input-output feedback linearization (see, e.g.,
[
9
]
), rectified CBFs
[
10
]
, or designs based on the backstepping technique
[
11
]
.

Parallel to these developments, the integration of safety with other objectives has been widely explored. The combination of Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs) and CBFs, allows for the simultaneous pursuit of safety and stability objectives.
To this end, authors in
[
12
]
unify them under a single optimization-based framework.
This unification has been further generalized by the development of a universal formula for safe stabilization
[
13
]
, providing an analytical construction for the control law.
To handle practical uncertainties, these formulations have been extended to enforce safety and stability under input disturbances using ISSf-CBFs (input-to-state safe CBFs,
[
14
]
).
The conflict between reaching a target state and maintaining safety has been addressed in
[
15
]
, where a hybrid CLF-CBF formulation is proposed to overcome intrinsic topological deadlocks.
Recently, an extension to model uncertainties
[
16
]
is derived via an adaptive approach obtained by combining of a CBF, a CLF and a parameter error term.

Beyond structural control design, a second major research direction focuses on the direct integration of CBFs into online dynamic optimization algorithms. In this framework, safety-critical control is reformulated as a constrained nonlinear program, where gradient flows are augmented with CBF-based inputs to ensure forward invariance of the feasible set
[
17
]
. This approach has been further extended to the context of feedback optimization (see, e.g.,
[
18
,
19
,
20
]
). Specifically, authors of
[
21
]
propose a safe feedback optimization method that enforces state constraints at all times.

However, the increasing complexity of modern control architectures leads to nontrivial and/or unknown interconnected dynamics, for which designing CBFs is challenging or even impossible.
To mitigate this issue, there is a growing effort to develop
approaches that synthesize CBFs for reduced-order models (see the
recent survey
[
22
]
).
Several of these approaches rely on specific system structures. For instance, when the system is in strict-feedback form, safety can be propagated from a virtual controller to the actual input via backstepping
[
23
]
.
Alternative strategies include energy-based augmentations
[
24
]
or the model-free tracking of safe reference velocities
[
25
]
.
Closer to our proposed methodology, the work
[
26
]
addresses safety in multi-agent systems by resorting to the system structure typically used in singular perturbations.

Despite these advancements, a systematic framework to guarantee the
safety of generally interconnected systems, especially when dealing
with reduced-order models embedded within complex feedback loops,
remains an open challenge.

The main contribution of this paper is the establishment of a formal framework, based on singular perturbation (or timescale separation) theory, to guarantee the safety of feedback-interconnected systems.
Specifically, we provide sufficient conditions lifting a CBF designed for a reduced-order model to the overall nonlinear, feedback-interconnected system.
By adapting techniques from singular perturbation analysis, we show that if the neglected subsystem evolves on a sufficiently faster timescale than the reduced-order model, then a composite CBF can be constructed to certify the forward invariance of the safe set for the full interconnected system.
Notably, our approach applies to broad classes of nonlinear dynamics without requiring a strict cascade structure or restrictive inversion-based feedback laws.
From an implementation perspective, the proposed framework allows safe controllers to be synthesized solely for the lower-dimensional reduced-order model. This significantly simplifies the safety filter design and reduces the computational load, while still providing formal safety guarantees for the overall interconnection.
This timescale separation arises naturally in modern applications, such as the interaction between physical domains evolving at different rates (e.g., the electrical and mechanical components of robotic systems), or the interconnection of physical systems with online optimization algorithms.

II
Preliminaries on Control Barrier Functions

We start by recalling the theoretical foundations of Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) for continuous-time nonlinear systems as

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=f\left(x({t}),u({t})\right),

(1)

where
x({t})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the system state at time
{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
u({t})\in\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the control input at time
{t}
, and
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
describes the system dynamics.
The next definition introduces the notion of zero-superlevel set of a function.

Definition 1

Given
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, the zero-superlevel set of
h
is defined as
\mathcal{C}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid h(x)\geq 0\}
and its boundary is
\partial\mathcal{C}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid h(x)=0\}
.

\square

Then, let us
introduce
\mathcal{S}_{x}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
as the safe set of system (
1
).
Accordingly, we say that system (
1
) is safe with respect to
\mathcal{S}_{x}
if there exists a set
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{x}
that is forward invariant for system (
1
).
A popular tool for constructively certifying the safety of system (
1
) is given by CBFs
[
12
,
2
,
22
]
.

Definition 2

A continuously differentiable function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
with zero-superlevel set
\mathcal{C}
is said to be a CBF for system (
1
) on
\mathcal{C}
if there exists a class-
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
function
\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\nabla h(x)^{\top}f(x,u)\geq-\alpha(h(x)),

for all
x\in\mathcal{C}
.
\square

With this definition at hand, we can state the following result.

Lemma 1

[
22
, Thm. 4]

Consider a continuously differentiable function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
with zero-superlevel set
\mathcal{C}
.
If a locally Lipschitz feedback controller
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathcal{U}
satisfies

\displaystyle\nabla h(x)^{\top}f(x,\pi(x))\geq-\alpha(h(x)),

for all
x\in\mathcal{C}
, then
\mathcal{C}
is forward invariant for the closed-loop system

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=f(x({t}),\pi(x({t}))).

\square

III-A

Reduced-Order Safety Certificates

We are interested in designing safety certificates on reduced-order
models and then lift them to the overall feedback-interconnected
system. Purposely, we rely on the following three
requirements.

First, the safety specifications are defined solely in terms of the slow subsystem state
x
in (
2a
).
More in detail, we introduce

\mathcal{S}_{x}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
as the safe set for the slow subsystem (
2a
) and, accordingly, the safe set for the overall

2
) is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}:=\{(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\mid x\in\mathcal{S}_{x}\}.

(3)

Second, the fast subsystem (
2b
) admits an
equilibrium function
parametrized in
x

Assumption 2

There exists a continuously differentiable function

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=g(z^{\text{eq}}(x,u),x,u).

for all
x\in\mathcal{S}_{x}
and

\square

Third, we assume the availability of a robustly-safe controller
for a
reduced-order model

of (
2a

2
) on the manifold

\{(x,z,u)\in\mathcal{S}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathcal{U}\mid z=z^{\text{eq}}(x,u)\}

Namely, we introduce the reduced dynamics

f_{\text{rs}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined as

\displaystyle f_{\text{rs}}(x,u):=f(x,z^{\text{eq}}(x,u),u).

(4)

For the reduced system we let the following assumption hold.

Assumption 3

There exist a continuously differentiable function

h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
with a compact zero-superlevel set

\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{x}
, a
L_{\pi}
-Lipschitz continuous and
differentiable function

, a

-Lipschitz continuous function

\displaystyle\nabla h(x)^{\top}f_{\text{rs}}(x,\pi(x))

\displaystyle\geq-\alpha(h(x))+\eta,

for all
x\in\mathcal{C}
.
\square

The goal of our paper is to establish sufficient conditions to lift

3

2

IV-B

Suboptimal Safe Control

Second, we consider the case in which the slow subsystem (
2a
) is a given plant, while the fast one (
2b

We formulate this scenario by introducing, for all

\displaystyle\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\quad\ell(u,x({t}))

\displaystyle=f(x({t}),a(z({t})))

.

V
Singular Perturbations for Safety:

Main Result

In this section, we provide a safety certificate for the interconnected system (

h
and the stability properties of the boundary layer system (

To this end, we introduce the composite CBF function

\displaystyle V(x,z):=h(x)-U(z-z^{\text{eq}}(x,\pi(x))),

(15)

Theorem 1

Consider system (
2

1
,
2
,
3

Then, there exists
\bar{\epsilon}>0
such that, for all
\epsilon\in(0,\bar{\epsilon})

\mathcal{C}_{V}
is forward invariant for the closed-loop system

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=f(x({t}),z({t}),\pi(x({t})))

\displaystyle\epsilon\dot{z}({t})

\displaystyle=g(z({t}),x({t}),\pi(x({t}))).

\square

Timescale Separation and Safety

Consistently with singular perturbation results for stability, Theorem
1
captures the timescale separation required to guarantee safety.
In the ideal case
\epsilon\to 0
, the fast state would instantaneously converge to the equilibrium
z^{\text{eq}}(x,\pi(x))
, thus allowing the slow system to behave exactly as the ideal reduced one and, thus, to preserve safety by Assumption
3
.
However, for
\epsilon\neq 0

Here, the strict safety margin
\eta
plays a fundamental role, acting as a designed robustness buffer against the perturbation terms due to
z({t})\neq z^{\text{eq}}(x({t}),\pi(x({t})))

As shown in the time-domain plot of

Conversely, if

VII
Conclusions

In this paper, we developed a singular perturbation framework for safety, which is the first of its kind in the literature.
More specifically, we considered feedback interconnections between two subsystems, referred to as the slow and fast subsystems, respectively.
We then provided sufficient conditions to guarantee safety of the overall interconnected system, based on reduced-order safety properties of the slow subsystem and stability properties of the fast one.
We illustrated the applicability of our theoretical results in two settings.
The first is the safe control of a robotic arm with joint motors.
The second is a suboptimal safe control scenario in which a physical plant interacts with an optimization algorithm that iteratively solves an optimization problem encoding safety constraints.
