Timelike Meridian Surfaces of hyperbolic type in the Minkowski 4-Space
Victoria Bencheva, Velichka Milousheva

Abstract
. We consider a special class of timelike surfaces in the four-dimensional Minkowski space which are one-parameter systems of meridians of rotational hypersurfaces with spacelike axis and call them meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type. We show that all timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type are surfaces with flat normal connection and give the complete classification of those surfaces with constant Gauss curvature. We also classify all minimal timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type and all timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with non-zero constant mean curvature (CMC-surfaces). We show that there are no timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with parallel mean curvature vector field other than CMC surfaces lying in a hyperplane. Finally, we describe the class of timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field, but non-parallel mean curvature vector.

Primary 53B30, Secondary 53A35, 53B25

1.
Introduction

One of the basic problems in the contemporary
differential geometry of surfaces and hypersurfaces in standard model spaces such as the Euclidean space
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the pseudo-Euclidean space
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}_{k}
(with arbitrary dimension
\displaystyle n
and arbitrary index
\displaystyle k
) is the study of the main invariants characterizing the surfaces.
Curvature invariants are the most fundamental and natural invariants in Riemannian and pseudo-Riemannian geometry and also play key roles in physics.
The basic intrinsic curvature invariant of a surface in Euclidean or pseudo-Euclidean space is the
Gauss curvature and one basic extrinsic invariant is the curvature of the normal
connection. So, a common problem is to investigate various important classes of surfaces which are characterized by conditions on their main curvature invariants. For example, flat surfaces, surfaces with constant Gauss curvature, surfaces with flat normal connection, etc.

The mean curvature vector field is the most fundamental normal vector field on an immersed surface, capturing its extrinsic shape.
So, a fundamental question is also to investigate different classes of surfaces characterized by conditions on the mean curvature vector field, such as surfaces with constant mean curvature (CMC surfaces), surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector field or parallel normalized mean curvature vector field, and to find examples of surfaces belonging to these classes.

On the other hand, a basic source of examples of many geometric classes of surfaces in Riemannian and pseudo-Riemannian geometry are the rotational surfaces and hypersurfaces.
In the present paper, we consider a special class of timelike surfaces in the 4-dimensional Minkowski space
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
which are one-parameter systems of meridians of rotational hypersurfaces with spacelike axis and call them meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type. They are analogous to the timelike meridian surfaces of elliptic type, which are constructed as one-parameter systems of meridians of rotational hypersurfaces with timelike axis and are studied in
[
3
]
. Depending on the type of the spheres in the 3-dimensional subspace
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}

and the casual character of the spherical curves, we distinguish two different kinds of timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
.

We show that all timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type are surfaces with flat normal connection and classify completely the flat meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type
(Theorem
5.1
and Theorem
5.2
) and the meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with
constant non-zero Gauss curvature (Theorem
5.3
and Theorem
5.4
).

In Theorem
6.1
and Theorem
6.2
we classify all minimal timelike first or second kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type.
Then we describe all timelike first or second kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with non-zero constant mean curvature (CMC-surfaces), see Theorem
7.1
and Theorem
7.2
. We also consider timelike first or second kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with parallel mean curvature vector field and prove that there are no timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with parallel mean curvature vector field other than CMC-surfaces lying in a hyperplane of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
.

Finally, we study the class of timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field and classify them in Theorem
9.1
and Theorem
9.2
. The results in these two theorems can be used to find explicit solutions to the background systems of natural partial differential equations describing the timelike surfaces with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
, see
[
2
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

We consider the four-dimensional Minkowski space
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
endowed with the standard flat metric
of signature
\displaystyle(3,1)
given in local coordinates by

\widetilde{g}=dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}+dx_{3}^{2}-dx_{4}^{2},

where
\displaystyle(x_{1};x_{2};x_{3};x_{4})
is a rectangular coordinate system in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
.
Denote by
\displaystyle\langle,\rangle
the indefinite inner scalar product associated with
\displaystyle\widetilde{g}
.
Since
\displaystyle\widetilde{g}
is an
indefinite metric, a vector
\displaystyle v\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
can have one of the three
casual characters:
spacelike
if
\displaystyle\langle v,v\rangle>0

or
\displaystyle v=0
,
timelike
if
\displaystyle\langle v,v\rangle<0
, and
null
(
lightlike
) if
\displaystyle\langle v,v\rangle=0
and
\displaystyle v\neq 0
.

We use the following standard denotations:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\mathbb{S}^{3}_{1}(1)=\left\{V\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}:\langle V,V\rangle=1\right\};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\mathbb{H}^{3}_{1}(-1)=\left\{V\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}:\langle V,V\rangle=-1\right\}.\end{array}

The space
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{3}_{1}(1)
is known as the de Sitter space, and the
space
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{3}_{1}(-1)
is known as the hyperbolic space (or the anti-de Sitter space)
[
17
]
.

Given a surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
, we denote by
\displaystyle g
the induced metric
of
\displaystyle\widetilde{g}
on
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
. A surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
is called
timelike
(or
Lorentz
) if the induced metric
\displaystyle g
on
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
is Lorentzian.
In the present paper
we study timelike surfaces, so at each point

\displaystyle p\in\mathcal{M}^{2}
we have the following decomposition

\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}=T_{p}\mathcal{M}^{2}\oplus N_{p}\mathcal{M}^{2}

with the property that the restriction of the metric onto the tangent space
\displaystyle T_{p}\mathcal{M}^{2}
is of signature
\displaystyle(1,1)
, and the restriction of the metric onto the
normal space
\displaystyle N_{p}\mathcal{M}^{2}
is of signature
\displaystyle(2,0)
.

Let
\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}
and
\displaystyle\nabla
be the Levi Civita connections on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
, respectively. Then, for any tangent vector fields
\displaystyle x
,
\displaystyle y
and any normal vector field
\displaystyle\xi
we have the
following formulas of Gauss and Weingarten:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{x}y=\nabla_{x}y+\sigma(x,y);\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{x}\xi=-A_{\xi}x+D_{x}\xi,\end{array}

which define the second fundamental tensor
\displaystyle\sigma
, the normal connection
\displaystyle D

and the shape operator
\displaystyle A_{\xi}
with respect to
\displaystyle\xi
.

The mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
is defined as

\displaystyle H=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{tr}\,\sigma}
, i.e. in the case of a timelike surface,

\displaystyle H=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\left(\sigma(x,x)-\sigma(y,y)\right)}
, where
\displaystyle\{x,y\}

is a local orthonormal frame of the tangent bundle, such that
\displaystyle\langle x,x\rangle=1
,
\displaystyle\langle y,y\rangle=-1
.

A surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
is called
minimal
if its
mean curvature vector vanishes identically, i.e.
\displaystyle H=0
. If
\displaystyle\langle H,H\rangle=const\neq 0
, then the surface
is said to have constant mean curvature (or
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
is a
CMC-surface
).
A normal vector field
\displaystyle\xi
on
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
is called
parallel in the normal bundle
(or simply
parallel
) if
\displaystyle D{\xi}=0
.
If the mean curvature vector
\displaystyle H
is parallel, i.e.

\displaystyle DH=0
, then the surface is said to have
parallel mean curvature vector field

[
8
]
. Surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector field in Riemannian
space forms were classified by Chen
[
5
]
and Yau
[
19
]
in the early 1970s.
Lorentz surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector field in arbitrary pseudo-Euclidean space
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}_{k}

were studied in
[
7
]
and
[
10
]
.

Surfaces for which the mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
is non-zero, i.e.
\displaystyle\langle H,H\rangle\neq 0
, and the unit vector field in the
direction of
\displaystyle H
is parallel in the normal bundle, are called surfaces with
parallel normalized mean
curvature vector field

[
5
]
. It can easily be seen that if
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
is a
surface with non-zero parallel mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
(i.e.
\displaystyle H\neq 0
and
\displaystyle DH=0
), then
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
is a surface with parallel normalized mean curvature vector
field, but the converse is true only in the case
\displaystyle\|H\|=\mathrm{const}

[
6
]
.

3.
Construction of timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}

Meridian surfaces in the Euclidean 4-space
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}
we defined first by G. Ganchev and the second author in
[
11
]
as 2-dimensional surfaces lying on a rotational hypersurface in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}
. As these surfaces are one-parameter systems of meridians of
the rotational hypersurface, they are called
meridian surfaces
.
The classification of meridian surfaces with constant Gauss curvature, with constant mean curvature, Chen meridian surfaces and meridian surfaces with parallel normal bundle was given in
[
11
]
and
[
13
]
.

In
[
12
]
, the idea from the Euclidean case was used to construct special families of spacelike surfaces lying on rotational hypersurfaces with timelike or spacelike axis in the Minkowski space
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
and these surfaces were called spacelike meridian surface of elliptic or hyperbolic type, respectively. A local classification of marginally trapped meridian surfaces in the Minkowski 4-space was given in
[
12
]
and meridian surfaces with pointwise 1-type Gauss map in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
were classified in
[
1
]
.

In
[
3
]
, the authors studied timelike meridian surfaces of elliptic type in the Minkowski 4-space – these are 2-dimensional timelike surfaces
which lie on rotational hypersurfaces with timelike axis. The following basic classes of timelike meridian surfaces of elliptic type were studied:
with constant Gauss curvature, with constant mean curvature, with parallel mean curvature vector field, with parallel normalized mean curvature
vector field.

In the present paper, we develop the same idea and consider timelike surfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
which are one-parameter systems of meridians of rotational hypersurfaces with spacelike axis and call them meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type. Depending on the type of the spheres in the 3-dimensional subspace
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}

and the casual character of the spherical curves, we distinguish different kinds of timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
.

Bellow we give the construction of these different kinds of meridian surfaces.

Let
\displaystyle Oe_{1}e_{2}e_{3}e_{4}
be the standard orthonormal frame in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
, i.e.
\displaystyle\langle e_{1},e_{1}\rangle=\langle e_{2},e_{2}\rangle=\langle e_{3},e_{3}\rangle=1
,
\displaystyle\langle e_{4},e_{4}\rangle=-1
. We shall
consider a rotational hypersurface with spacelike axis
\displaystyle Oe_{1}
. Of course, similarly one
can consider a rotational hypersurface with axis
\displaystyle Oe_{2}
or
\displaystyle Oe_{3}
.
Since in the three-dimensional Minkowski subspace
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{3}_{1}=\mathrm{span}\left\{e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\right\}
there exist two types of spheres, namely the pseudo-sphere
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{2}_{1}(1)=\left\{V\in\mathbb{E}^{3}_{1}:\langle V,V\rangle=1\right\}
and the pseudo-hyperbolic space
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{2}(-1)=\left\{V\in\mathbb{E}^{3}_{1}:\langle V,V\rangle=-1;V^{3}>0\right\}
(where
\displaystyle V^{3}
is the third coordinate of
\displaystyle V
), we can consider two kinds of rotational hypersurfaces about the axis
\displaystyle Oe_{1}
.

Let
\displaystyle f=f(u),\,\,g=g(u)
,
\displaystyle u\in I
be smooth functions, defined in an
interval
\displaystyle I\subset\mathbb{R}
, such that
\displaystyle\dot{f}^{2}(u)+\dot{g}^{2}(u)\neq 0
,
\displaystyle f(u)>0,\,\,u\in I
.

The first kind rotational hypersurface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
obtained by the rotation of the
meridian curve
\displaystyle m:u\rightarrow(f(u),g(u))
about the
\displaystyle Oe_{1}
-axis, is
parametrized as follows:

\mathcal{M}^{I}:Z(u,w^{1},w^{2})=g(u)e_{1}+f(u)\left(\cosh w^{1}\cos w^{2}e_{2}+\cosh w^{1}\sin w^{2}e_{3}+\sinh w^{1}e_{4}\right).

Note that

l^{I}(w^{1},w^{2})=\cosh w^{1}\cos w^{2}\,e_{2}+\cosh w^{1}\sin w^{2}\,e_{3}+\sinh w^{1}\,e_{4}

is the unit position vector of the pseudo-sphere
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{2}_{1}(1)
(de Sitter space)
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{3}_{1}
centered at the origin
\displaystyle O
. Then, the parametrization of the rotational hypersurface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}
can be written as:

\mathcal{M}^{I}:Z(u,w^{1},w^{2})=g(u)e_{1}+f(u)l^{I}(w^{1},w^{2}).

Now, we will construct surfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{4}_{1}
which are one-parameter systems of meridians of the hypersurface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}
.
Let
\displaystyle w^{1}=w^{1}(v),\,w^{2}=w^{2}(v),\,\,v\in J,\,J\subset\mathbb{R}
and consider a smooth timelike curve
\displaystyle\textbf{c}:l=l(v)=l^{I}(w^{1}(v),w^{2}(v)),\,\,v\in J,\,J\subset\mathbb{R}

on
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{2}_{1}(1)
parametrized by the arc-length, i.e.
\displaystyle\langle l\,^{\prime},l\,^{\prime}\rangle=-1
. Now, we consider the two-dimensional surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
parametrized by:

\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}:z(u,v)=Z(u,w^{1}(v),w^{2}(v)),\quad u\in I,\,v\in J.

The parametrization of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
can also be written in the following form:

(3.1)

\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}:z(u,v)=f(u)\,l(v)+g(u)\,e_{1},\quad u\in I,\,v\in J.

Obviously,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is a one-parameter system of
meridians of the hypersurface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}
, and for this reason we call
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
a

first kind meridian surface of hyperbolic type
.

The second kind rotational hypersurface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
obtained by the rotation of the
meridian curve
\displaystyle m:u\rightarrow(f(u),g(u))
about the
\displaystyle Oe_{1}
-axis, is given by the following parametrization:

\mathcal{M}^{II}:Z(u,w^{1},w^{2})=g(u)e_{1}+f(u)\left(\sinh w^{1}\cos w^{2}e_{2}+\sinh w^{1}\sin w^{2}e_{3}+\cosh w^{1}e_{4}\right).

Note that

l^{II}(w^{1},w^{2})=\sinh w^{1}\cos w^{2}\,e_{2}+\sinh w^{1}\sin w^{2}\,e_{3}+\cosh w^{1}\,e_{4}

is the unit position vector of the hyperbolic sphere
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{2}(-1)
(anti de Sitter space)
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{3}_{1}=\mathrm{span}\left\{e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\right\}
centered at the origin
\displaystyle O
. So, the parametrization of

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}
can be written as:

\mathcal{M}^{II}:Z(u,w^{1},w^{2})=g(u)\,e_{1}+f(u)l^{II}(w^{1},w^{2}).

Meridian surfaces lying on the second kind rotational hypersurface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}
can be constructed as follows. Let
\displaystyle\textbf{c}:l=l(v)=l^{II}(w^{1}(v),w^{2}(v))
be a smooth curve on the hyperbolic sphere
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{2}(-1)
, where
\displaystyle w^{1}=w^{1}(v)
,
\displaystyle w^{2}=w^{2}(v),\,\,v\in J,\,J\subset\mathbb{R}
. Then, the two-dimensional surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
defined by

(3.2)

\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}:z(u,v)=f(u)\,l(v)+g(u)\,e_{1},\quad u\in I,\,v\in J

is a one-parameter system of meridians of the second kind rotational hypersurface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}
, which we call a
second kind meridian surface of hyperbolic type
.

Spacelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type in the Minkowski 4-space were studied in
[
14
]
where the classification of such surface with constant Gauss curvature or with constant mean curvature was given.

In this paper we will study the timelike case of meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type.

4.
Main invariants of timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type

In this section we will study some main invariants of the two kinds of timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type.
Note that the first kind meridian surface of hyperbolic type
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is generated by a smooth timelike curve
c
lying on the pseudo-sphere
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{2}_{1}(1)
, while the second kind meridian surface of hyperbolic type
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
is generated by a smooth timelike curve
c
lying on the hyperbolic sphere
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{2}(-1)
.

For convenience, we use the notation
\displaystyle\dot{f}(u)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}
for differentiation with respect to the parameter
\displaystyle u
, and we use
\displaystyle l\,^{\prime}(v)=\frac{\partial l}{\partial v}
for differentiation with respect to the parameter
\displaystyle v
.

4.1.
First kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
be the first kind meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (
3.1
). The Frenet formulas for the timelike curve
c
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{2}_{1}(1)
are given bellow:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptl\,^{\prime}=t;\\
\vskip 5.69054ptt\,^{\prime}=\varkappa\,n+l;\\
\vskip 5.69054ptn\,^{\prime}=\varkappa\,t,\end{array}

where
\displaystyle\varkappa=\varkappa(v)
is the spherical curvature of
c
, i.e.
\displaystyle\varkappa(v)=\langle t^{\prime}(v),n(v)\rangle
, and
\displaystyle\{l(v),t(v),n(v)\}
is an orthonormal frame field in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
, such that
\displaystyle\langle l,l\rangle=1
,
\displaystyle\langle t,t\rangle=-1
,
\displaystyle\langle n,n\rangle=1
.

Then, the tangent vector fields
\displaystyle z_{u}
and
\displaystyle z_{v}
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
are expressed as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptz_{u}=\dot{f}(u)\,l(v)+\dot{g}(u)\,e_{1};\\
\vskip 5.69054ptz_{v}=f(u)\,t(v),\end{array}

so, the coefficients of the first fundamental form of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
are

E=\langle z_{u},z_{u}\rangle=\dot{f}^{2}(u)+\dot{g}^{2}(u);\quad F=\langle z_{u},z_{v}\rangle=0;\quad G=\langle z_{v},z_{v}\rangle=-f^{2}(u).

Obviously,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is a timelike surface (
\displaystyle E>0
and
\displaystyle G<0
).

The curvature
\displaystyle\varkappa_{m}
of the meridian curve
\displaystyle m
is given by
\displaystyle\varkappa_{m}(u)=\dot{f}(u)\ddot{g}(u)-\dot{g}(u)\ddot{f}(u)
.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
\displaystyle\dot{f}(u)^{2}+\dot{g}(u)^{2}=1
. Then, the coefficients of first fundamental form are:

E=1;\quad F=0;\quad G=-f^{2}(u),

and hence,
\displaystyle EG-F^{2}=-f^{2}(u)
.
We consider the following orthonormal tangent frame field:

(4.1)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptX=z_{u};\\
\vskip 5.69054ptY=\displaystyle\frac{z_{v}}{f}=t.\end{array}

Therefore,
\displaystyle\langle X,X\rangle=1,\,\,\langle X,Y\rangle=0,\,\,\langle Y,Y\rangle=-1
.
We consider the following orthonormal frame field of the normal space:

(4.2)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptN_{1}=n(v);\\
\vskip 5.69054ptN_{2}=\dot{g}(u)\,l(v)-\dot{f}(u)\,e_{1}.\end{array}

Obviously,

\displaystyle\langle N_{1},N_{1}\rangle=1,\,\,\langle N_{1},N_{2}\rangle=0,\,\,\langle N_{2},N_{2}\rangle=1
.
Calculating the second derivatives of the vector function
\displaystyle z(u,v)
we obtain:

(4.3)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptz_{uu}=\ddot{f}\,l+\ddot{g}\,e_{1};\\
\vskip 5.69054ptz_{uv}=\dot{f}\,l^{\prime}=\dot{f}\,t;\\
\vskip 5.69054ptz_{vv}=f\,t^{\prime}=f\varkappa\,n+f\,l,\end{array}

which imply that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054pt\langle z_{uu},N_{1}\rangle=0;&\qquad\langle z_{uu},N_{2}\rangle=-\varkappa_{m};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\langle z_{uv},N_{1}\rangle=0;&\qquad\langle z_{uv},N_{2}\rangle=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\langle z_{vv},N_{1}\rangle=f\varkappa;&\qquad\langle z_{vv},N_{2}\rangle=f\dot{g}.\end{array}

Using that
\displaystyle X=z_{u}
,
\displaystyle Y=\displaystyle\frac{z_{v}}{f}
and formulas (
4.3
), we obtain the following derivative formulas for the orthonormal frame field
\displaystyle\{X,Y,N_{1},N_{2}\}
:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}X=\ddot{f}\,l+\ddot{g}\,e_{1};&\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}N_{1}=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}Y=0;&\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}N_{1}=\displaystyle\frac{\varkappa}{f}\,t;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}X=\displaystyle\frac{\dot{f}}{f}\,t;&\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}N_{2}=\ddot{g}\,l-\ddot{f}\,e_{1};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}Y=\displaystyle\frac{\varkappa}{f}\,n+\displaystyle\frac{1}{f}\,l;&\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}N_{2}=\displaystyle\frac{\dot{g}}{f}\,t.\end{array}

Now, having in mind (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), the formulas above imply:

(4.4)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}X=\quad\quad\qquad\qquad-\varkappa_{m}\,N_{2};&\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}\,N_{1}=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}Y=0;&\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}\,N_{1}=\quad\quad\displaystyle\frac{\varkappa}{f}\,Y;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}X=\qquad\displaystyle\frac{\dot{f}}{f}\,Y;&\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}\,N_{2}=\varkappa_{m}\,X;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}Y=\displaystyle\frac{\dot{f}}{f}\,X\quad+\frac{\varkappa}{f}\,N_{1}+\frac{\dot{g}}{f}\,N_{2};&\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}\,N_{2}=\quad\qquad\displaystyle\frac{\dot{g}}{f}\,Y.\end{array}

Equations (
4.4
) are the derivative formulas of the surface with respect to the parameters
\displaystyle(u,v)
.
Using these formulas we will calculate the Gauss curvature
\displaystyle K
, the curvature of the normal connection
\displaystyle K^{\bot}
, and the mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
of the first kind meridian surface of hyperbolic type
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
.

The Gauss curvature
\displaystyle K
is determined by the following formula:

K=\frac{\langle\sigma(X,X),\sigma(Y,Y)\rangle-\langle\sigma(X,Y),\sigma(X,Y)\rangle}{\langle X,X\rangle\langle Y,Y\rangle-\langle X,Y\rangle^{2}}.

Hence, using (
4.4
), we get that the Gauss curvature of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is expressed as follows:

(4.5)

K=-\frac{\ddot{f}(u)}{f(u)}.

The above formula shows that the Gauss curvature depends only on the parameter
\displaystyle u
since it is expressed in terms of the function
\displaystyle f(u)
determining the meridian curve
\displaystyle m
.

Using (
4.4
) we obtain that the normal mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
depends on both parameters
\displaystyle u
and
\displaystyle v
and is given by the formula:

(4.6)

H=-\frac{\varkappa(v)}{2f(u)}\,N_{1}\pm\frac{f(u)\ddot{f}(u)+\dot{f}^{2}(u)-1}{2f(u)\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}(u)}}\,N_{2}.

The curvature of the normal connection of the surface is defined by:

K^{\bot}=\frac{\langle R^{D}(X,Y)N_{1},N_{2}\rangle}{\langle X,X\rangle\langle Y,Y\rangle-\langle X,Y\rangle^{2}},

where

R^{D}(X,Y)N=D_{X}D_{Y}N-D_{Y}D_{X}N-D_{[X,Y]}N.

Using equalities (
4.4
), we calculate that the curvature of the normal connection of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is:

K^{\bot}=0.

The above formula implies the next statement.

Proposition 4.1
.

The first kind meridian surface of hyperbolic type
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
, defined by (
3.1
), is a surface with flat normal connection.

Remark 4.2
.

Each timelike first kind meridian surface of hyperbolic type is determined by a meridian curve
\displaystyle m
and a timelike curve
c
lying on the pseudo-sphere
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{2}_{1}(1)
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
. So, the geometry of the first kind meridian surface and all its invariants are expressed in terms of the curvature
\displaystyle\varkappa_{m}(u)
of the meridian curve
\displaystyle m
and the spherical curvature
\displaystyle\varkappa(v)
of
c
.

Remark 4.3
.

(i) If the spherical curvature of
c
is
\displaystyle\varkappa=0
, then from (
4.4
) it follows that
\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}N_{1}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}N_{2}=0
and hence, the normal vector field
\displaystyle N_{1}
is constant. So, the meridian surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
lies in the
constant 3-dimensional space
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}=\mathrm{span}\{X,Y,N_{2}\}
of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{1}^{4}
.

(ii) If
\displaystyle\varkappa_{m}=0
, i.e. the meridian curve
\displaystyle m
is part of a straight line, then both the Gauss curvature and the normal curvature are zero, which implies that
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is a flat surface with flat normal connection. Since in this case the surface consists only of inflection points, then it is either developable or lies in a 3-dimensional space
[
15
]
.

4.2.
Second kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
be the second kind meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (
3.2
).
The Frenet formulas for a curve
c
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{2}_{1}(-1)
are given bellow:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptl\,^{\prime}=t;\\
\vskip 5.69054ptt\,^{\prime}=\varkappa\,n+l;\\
\vskip 5.69054ptn\,^{\prime}=-\varkappa\,t,\end{array}

where
\displaystyle\varkappa=\varkappa(v)
is the spherical curvature of
c
, i.e.
\displaystyle\varkappa(v)=\langle t\,^{\prime}(v),n(v)\rangle
, and
\displaystyle\{l(v),t(v),n(v)\}
is an orthonormal frame field in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
, such that
\displaystyle\langle l,l\rangle=-1
,
\displaystyle\langle t,t\rangle=1
,
\displaystyle\langle n,n\rangle=1
.

Then, the tangent vector fields
\displaystyle z_{u}
and
\displaystyle z_{v}
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
are expressed as:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptz_{u}=\dot{f}(u)\,l(v)+\dot{g}(u)\,e_{1};\\
\vskip 5.69054ptz_{v}=f(u)\,t(v).\end{array}

Then, the coefficients of the first fundamental form of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
are:

E=\langle z_{u},z_{u}\rangle=-\dot{f}^{2}(u)+\dot{g}^{2}(u);\quad F=\langle z_{u},z_{v}\rangle=0;\quad G=\langle z_{v},z_{v}\rangle=f^{2}(u).

Since we are interested in timelike surfaces, we assume that
\displaystyle-\dot{f}^{2}(u)+\dot{g}^{2}(u)<0,\,\,u\in I
.
Without loss of generality we may assume that
\displaystyle\dot{f}(u)^{2}-\dot{g}(u)^{2}=1
. Then, the coefficients of the first fundamental form are:

E=-1;\quad F=0;\quad G=f^{2}(u).

Again we have
\displaystyle EG-F^{2}=-f^{2}(u)
.
Let us consider the following orthonormal tangent frame field:

(4.7)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptX=z_{u};\\
\vskip 5.69054ptY=\displaystyle\frac{z_{v}}{f}=t.\end{array}

Therefore,
\displaystyle\langle X,X\rangle=-1,\,\,\langle X,Y\rangle=0,\,\,\langle Y,Y\rangle=1
.
We consider the following orthonormal frame field of the normal space:

(4.8)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptN_{1}=n(v);\\
\vskip 5.69054ptN_{2}=\dot{g}(u)\,l(v)+\dot{f}(u)\,e_{1}.\end{array}

Obviously,

\displaystyle\langle N_{1},N_{1}\rangle=1,\,\,\langle N_{1},N_{2}\rangle=0,\,\,\langle N_{2},N_{2}\rangle=1
.
Calculating the second derivatives of the vector function
\displaystyle z(u,v)
we obtain:

(4.9)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptz_{uu}=\ddot{f}\,l+\ddot{g}\,e_{1};\\
\vskip 5.69054ptz_{uv}=\dot{f}\,l^{\prime}=\dot{f}\,t;\\
\vskip 5.69054ptz_{vv}=f\,t^{\prime}=f\varkappa\,n+f\,l,\end{array}

which imply that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054pt\langle z_{uu},N_{1}\rangle=0;&\qquad\quad\langle z_{uu},N_{2}\rangle=\varkappa_{m};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\langle z_{uv},N_{1}\rangle=0;&\qquad\quad\langle z_{uv},N_{2}\rangle=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\langle z_{vv},N_{1}\rangle=f\varkappa;&\qquad\quad\langle z_{vv},N_{2}\rangle=-f\dot{g}.\end{array}

Using that
\displaystyle X=z_{u}
,
\displaystyle Y=\displaystyle\frac{z_{v}}{f}
and formulas (
4.9
), we obtain the following derivative formulas for the frame field
\displaystyle\{X,Y,N_{1},N_{2}\}
:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}X=\ddot{f}l+\ddot{g}e_{1};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}Y=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}X=\displaystyle\frac{\dot{f}}{f}t;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}Y=\displaystyle\frac{\varkappa}{f}n+\displaystyle\frac{1}{f}l;\end{array}\qquad\quad\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}N_{1}=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}N_{1}=-\displaystyle\frac{\varkappa}{f}t;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}N_{2}=\ddot{g}l+\ddot{f}e_{1};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}N_{2}=\displaystyle\frac{\dot{g}}{f}t.\end{array}

Now, having in mind (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), the formulas above imply:

(4.10)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}X=\quad\quad\qquad\qquad\varkappa_{m}N_{2};&\qquad\quad\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}N_{1}=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}Y=0;&\qquad\quad\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}N_{1}=\quad\quad\displaystyle-\frac{\varkappa}{f}Y;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}X=\qquad\displaystyle\frac{\dot{f}}{f}Y;&\qquad\quad\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}N_{2}=-\varkappa_{m}X;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}Y=\displaystyle-\frac{\dot{f}}{f}X\quad+\frac{\varkappa}{f}N_{1}-\frac{\dot{g}}{f}N_{2};&\qquad\quad\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}N_{2}=\quad\qquad\displaystyle\frac{\dot{g}}{f}Y.\end{array}

Equations (
4.10
) are the derivative formulas of the surface with respect to the parameters
\displaystyle(u,v)
.
Using these formulas we can calculate the Gauss curvature
\displaystyle K
, the curvature of the normal connection
\displaystyle K^{\bot}
, and the mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
of the second kind meridian surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
.

The Gauss curvature of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
is expressed as follows:

K=\frac{\ddot{f}(u)}{f(u)},

which means that the Gauss curvature depends only on the parameter
\displaystyle u
since it is expressed in terms of the function
\displaystyle f(u)
determining the meridian curve
\displaystyle m
.

Using (
4.10
) we find that the normal mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
depends on both parameters
\displaystyle u
and
\displaystyle v
and is given by the formula:

(4.11)

H=\frac{\varkappa(v)}{2f(u)}\,N_{1}\mp\frac{f(u)\ddot{f}(u)+\dot{f}^{2}(u)-1}{2f(u)\sqrt{\dot{f}^{2}(u)-1}}\,N_{2}.

The curvature of the normal connection of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
is:

K^{\bot}=0.

The above formula implies the following statement.

Proposition 4.4
.

The second kind meridian surface of hyperbolic type
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
, defined by (
3.2
), is a surface with flat normal connection.

Remark 4.5
.

Each timelike second kind meridian surface of hyperbolic type is determined by a meridian curve
\displaystyle m
and a spacelike curve
c
lying on the hyperbolic sphere
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{2}(-1)
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
. So, the geometry of the second kind meridian surface and all its invariants are expressed in terms of the curvature
\displaystyle\varkappa_{m}(u)
of the meridian curve
\displaystyle m
and the curvature
\displaystyle\varkappa(v)
of
c
.

Remark 4.6
.

(i) If
\displaystyle\varkappa=0
, then from (
4.10
) it follows that
\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}N_{1}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}N_{2}=0
and hence, the normal vector field
\displaystyle N_{1}
is constant. So, the meridian surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
lies in the
constant 3-dimensional space
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}=\mathrm{span}\{X,Y,N_{2}\}
of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{1}^{4}
.

(ii) If
\displaystyle\varkappa_{m}=0
, i.e. the meridian curve
\displaystyle m
is part of a straight line, then both the Gauss curvature and the normal curvature are zero, which implies that
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
is a flat surface with flat normal connection, which is either developable or lies in a 3-dimensional space.

5.
Timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with constant Gauss curvature

In this section we describe all timelike first or second kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
with constant Gauss curvature. First we shall consider the case when the Gauss curvature is zero, i.e. the surface is flat.

The flat timelike first kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type are characterized in the next theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
be a timelike first kind meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (
3.1
). Then,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is a flat surface if and only if the meridian curve
\displaystyle m
is given by:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptf(u)=a\,u+b;\quad a=\mathrm{const},\,b=\mathrm{const};\\
\vskip 5.69054ptg(u)=\pm\sqrt{1-a^{2}}\,u+c;\quad c=\mathrm{const}.\end{array}

Proof.

If
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is defined by (
3.1
), then the Gauss curvature
\displaystyle K
is expressed by formula (
4.5
).
Hence,
\displaystyle K=0
if and only if
\displaystyle\ddot{f}(u)=0
. The last equality implies that the function
\displaystyle f(u)
is given by

(5.1)

f(u)=a\,u+b

for some constants
\displaystyle a
and
\displaystyle b
.
Since we assume that
\displaystyle\dot{f}(u)^{2}+\dot{g}(u)^{2}=1
, the function
\displaystyle g(u)
is determined by
\displaystyle\dot{g}(u)=\pm\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}(u)}
, and hence, from (
5.1
) we obtain

g(u)=\pm\sqrt{1-a^{2}}\,u+c,

where
\displaystyle c=\mathrm{const}
.
∎

The flat timelike second kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type are characterized in the following theorem.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
be a timelike meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (
3.2
). Then,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
is a flat surface if and only if the meridian curve
\displaystyle m
is given by:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptf(u)=a\,u+b;\quad a=\mathrm{const},\,b=\mathrm{const};\\
\vskip 5.69054ptg(u)=\pm\sqrt{a^{2}-1}\,u+c;\quad c=\mathrm{const}.\end{array}

Proof.

The proof goes similarly to the proof of Theorem
5.1
. In the case of timelike second kind meridian surface of hyperbolic type, we assume that
\displaystyle\dot{f}(u)^{2}-\dot{g}(u)^{2}=1
, so the
function
\displaystyle g(u)
is determined by
\displaystyle\dot{g}(u)=\pm\sqrt{\dot{f}^{2}(u)-1}
and we get

g(u)=\pm\sqrt{a^{2}-1}\,u+c,

where
\displaystyle c=\mathrm{const}
.
∎

In the next theorem we describe all timelike first kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with constant non-zero Gauss curvature.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
be a timelike meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (
3.1
). Then,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
has constant non-zero Gauss curvature
\displaystyle K
if and only if the meridian curve
\displaystyle m
is given by:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptf(u)=a_{1}\sin\sqrt{K}\,u+a_{2}\cos\sqrt{K}\,u,\quad\text{if}\,\,K>0;\\
\vskip 5.69054ptf(u)=a_{1}\sinh\sqrt{-K}\,u+a_{2}\cosh\sqrt{-K}\,u,\quad\text{if}\,\,K<0,\end{array}

where
\displaystyle a_{1}
and
\displaystyle a_{2}
are constants and the function
\displaystyle g(u)
is determined by
\displaystyle\dot{g}(u)=\pm\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}(u)}
.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
be a timelike meridian surface, defined by (
3.1
). Then, the Gauss curvature
\displaystyle K
is given by (
4.5
), which implies that
\displaystyle K
is constant if and only if the function
\displaystyle f(u)
satisfies the following differential equation:

\ddot{f}(u)+Kf(u)=0.

The general solution of this equation is given by the formula:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptf(u)=a_{1}\sin\sqrt{K}\,u+a_{2}\cos\sqrt{K}\,u,\quad\text{in the case}\,\,K>0;\\
\vskip 5.69054ptf(u)=a_{1}\sinh\sqrt{-K}\,u+a_{2}\cosh\sqrt{-K}\,u,\quad\text{in the case}\,\,K<0,\end{array}

where
\displaystyle a_{1}
and
\displaystyle a_{2}
are arbitrary constants. Since
\displaystyle\dot{f}(u)^{2}+\dot{g}(u)^{2}=1
, the function
\displaystyle g(u)
is determined by
\displaystyle\dot{g}(u)=\pm\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}(u)}
.
∎

In a similar way, it is easy to prove that all timelike second kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with constant non-zero Gauss curvature are described as follows.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
be a timelike meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (
3.2
). Then,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
has constant non-zero Gauss curvature
\displaystyle K
if and only if the meridian curve
\displaystyle m
is given by:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptf(u)=a_{1}\cos\sqrt{-K}\,u+a_{2}\sin\sqrt{-K}\,u,\quad\text{if}\,\,K<0;\\
\vskip 5.69054ptf(u)=a_{1}\cosh\sqrt{K}\,u+a_{2}\sinh\sqrt{K}\,u,\quad\text{if}\,\,K>0,\end{array}

where
\displaystyle a_{1}
and
\displaystyle a_{2}
are constants and the function
\displaystyle g(u)
is determined by
\displaystyle\dot{g}(u)=\pm\sqrt{\dot{f}^{2}(u)-1}
.

6.
Minimal timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type

In this section we classify all minimal timelike first or second kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
be a timelike meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (
3.1
). Then,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is minimal if and only if the curve

c
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{2}_{1}(1)
has zero spherical curvature and the meridian curve
\displaystyle m
is given by:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptf(u)=\pm\sqrt{u^{2}+2au+b};\\
\vskip 5.69054ptg(u)=\pm\sqrt{b-a^{2}}\ln|u+a+\sqrt{u^{2}+2au+b}|+c,\end{array}

where
\displaystyle a=\mathrm{const}
,
\displaystyle b=\mathrm{const}
,
\displaystyle c=\mathrm{const}
,
\displaystyle b-a^{2}>0
.

Proof.

Using that the mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is given by formula (
4.6
), we get that the first kind meridian surface of hyperbolic type is minimal if and only if the curvature of
c
is
\displaystyle\varkappa=0
and the function
\displaystyle f(u)
satisfies the following differential equation

f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1=0.

The general solution of this differential equation is given by the formula:

f(u)=\pm\sqrt{u^{2}+2au+b},\quad a=\mathrm{const},\,b=\mathrm{const}.

Using that
\displaystyle\dot{g}=\pm\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}}
, we get the following equation for
\displaystyle g(u)
:

\dot{g}=\pm\frac{\sqrt{b-a^{2}}}{\sqrt{u^{2}+2au+b}},

and after integrating we obtain

g(u)=\pm\sqrt{b-a^{2}}\ln|u+a+\sqrt{u^{2}+2au+b}|+c,\quad c=const.

∎

.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
be a timelike meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
is minimal if and only if the curve

c
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{2}(-1)

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle b=\mathrm{const}

,
\displaystyle a^{2}-b>0

6.1
.

7.
Timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with constant mean curvature

In the next theorem we classify all timelike first kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with non-zero constant mean curvature (CMC-surfaces).

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
be a timelike meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (
3.1
). Then,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
has constant mean curvature vector field, i.e.
\displaystyle||H||=a=\mathrm{const},a\neq 0
, if and only if the curve
c
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{2}_{1}(1)
has constant non-zero spherical curvature
\displaystyle\varkappa=\mathrm{const}=b,\,b\neq 0
and the meridian curve
\displaystyle m
is given by
\displaystyle\dot{f}=\varphi(f)
, where

\varphi(t)=\pm\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{t^{2}}\left(c\mp\frac{t}{2}\sqrt{4a^{2}t^{2}-b^{2}}\pm\frac{b^{2}}{4a}\ln|2at+\sqrt{4a^{2}t^{2}-b^{2}}|\right)^{2}},\quad c=\mathrm{const},

and the function
\displaystyle g(u)
is defined by
\displaystyle\dot{g}=\pm\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}}
.

Proof.

Using that the mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
of the meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (
3.1
), is given by formula (
4.6
), we conclude that
\displaystyle||H||=\mathrm{const}=a
if and only if the following equality holds:

\varkappa^{2}=\frac{4a^{2}f^{2}(1-\dot{f}^{2})-(f\ddot{f}-1+\dot{f}^{2})^{2}}{1-\dot{f}^{2}}.

The left-hand side of the above equality is a function of the parameter
\displaystyle v
, while the right-hand side is a function of
\displaystyle u
, which implies that

(7.1)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\varkappa=\mathrm{const}=b,\,\,b\neq 0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt4a^{2}f^{2}(1-\dot{f}^{2})-(f\ddot{f}-1+\dot{f}^{2})^{2}=b^{2}(1-\dot{f}^{2}).\end{array}

The first equality of (
7.1
) means that the curve
c
has constant spherical curvature
\displaystyle\varkappa=b
. The second equality of (
7.1
) implies:

(7.2)

(f\ddot{f}-1+\dot{f}^{2})^{2}=(1-\dot{f}^{2})(4a^{2}f^{2}-b^{2}).

The solutions to the above differential equation can be found by setting

\displaystyle\dot{f}=\varphi(f)
in equation (
7.2
). Then we obtain that the function
\displaystyle\varphi=\varphi(t)
is a solution to the following differential equation:

1+\varphi^{2}+\frac{t}{2}(\varphi^{2})^{\prime}=\pm\sqrt{\varphi^{2}+1}\sqrt{4a^{2}t^{2}-b^{2}},

whose general solution is given by the formula:

(7.3)

\varphi(t)=\pm\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{t^{2}}\left(c\mp\frac{t}{2}\sqrt{4a^{2}t^{2}-b^{2}}\pm\frac{b^{2}}{4a}\ln|2at+\sqrt{4a^{2}t^{2}-b^{2}}|\right)^{2}},

where
\displaystyle c=\mathrm{const}
. The function
\displaystyle f
is determined by
\displaystyle\dot{f}=\varphi(f)
and formula (
7.3
), and the function
\displaystyle g
is defined by
\displaystyle\dot{g}=\pm\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}}
.

∎

Similarly to the proof of Theorem
7.1
, we obtain the classification of all timelike second kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with non-zero constant mean curvature.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
be a timelike meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (
3.2
). Then,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
has constant mean curvature vector field, i.e.
\displaystyle||H||=a=\mathrm{const},a\neq 0
, if and only if the curve
c
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{2}(-1)
has constant non-zero curvature
\displaystyle\varkappa=\mathrm{const}=b,\,b\neq 0
and the meridian curve
\displaystyle m
is given by
\displaystyle\dot{f}=\varphi(f)
, where

\varphi(t)=\pm\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{t^{2}}\left(c\pm\frac{t}{2}\sqrt{4a^{2}t^{2}-b^{2}}\mp\frac{b^{2}}{4a}\ln|2at+\sqrt{4a^{2}t^{2}-b^{2}}|\right)^{2}},\quad c=\mathrm{const},

and the function
\displaystyle g(u)
is defined by
\displaystyle\dot{g}=\pm\sqrt{\dot{f}^{2}-1}
.

8.
Timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with parallel mean curvature vector field

In this section, we consider non-minimal surfaces,
i.e. we assume that
\displaystyle H\neq 0
.
The mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
of a timelike first kind meridian surface of hyperbolic type is expressed by formula (
4.6
).
Using the derivative formulas (
4.4
) we calculate
\displaystyle D_{X}H
and
\displaystyle D_{Y}H
and obtain the following equalities:

(8.1)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69046ptD_{X}H=\frac{\varkappa\dot{f}}{2f^{2}}\,N_{1}\pm\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\left(\frac{f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1}{2f\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}}}\right)\,N_{2};\\
\vskip 5.69046ptD_{Y}H=-\frac{\varkappa^{\prime}}{2f^{2}}\,N_{1}.\end{array}

Recall that the mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
is parallel in the normal bundle if and only if
\displaystyle D_{X}H=D_{Y}H=0
.

In the next theorem we describe all timelike first kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with parallel mean curvature vector field.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
be a timelike meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (
3.1
). Then,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
has parallel mean curvature vector field, if and only if one of the following cases holds:

(i)
c
has zero spherical curvature and the meridian curve

\displaystyle m
is determined by
\displaystyle\dot{f}=\varphi(f)
where

\varphi(t)=\pm\frac{1}{t}\sqrt{t^{2}-(c-a\,t^{2})^{2}},\quad a=const\neq 0,\quad c=const,

\displaystyle g(u)
is defined by
\displaystyle\dot{g}=\pm\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}}
. In this case,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is a non-flat CMC-surface lying in a hyperplane of
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{4}_{1}
.

(ii)
c
has non-zero constant spherical curvature and the
meridian curve
\displaystyle m
is determined by
\displaystyle f(u)=a
,
\displaystyle g(u)=\pm u+b
, where
\displaystyle a=const\neq 0
,
\displaystyle b=const
. In this case,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is a flat CMC-surface lying in
a hyperplane of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
.

Proof.

Using formulas (
8.1
), we get that the surface has parallel mean curvature
vector field if and only if the following conditions are fulfilled:

(8.2)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\varkappa^{\prime}(v)=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\varkappa\dot{f}=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\frac{{f\ddot{f}}+\dot{f}^{2}-1}{2f\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}}}=const.\end{array}

The first equality of (
8.2
) implies that
\displaystyle\varkappa=\mathrm{const}
. The second equality of (
8.2
) shows that
there are two possible cases:

Case (i): The curvature of
c
is
\displaystyle\varkappa=0
. In this case,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}N_{1}=0,\;\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}N_{1}=0
, which implies that the surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
lies in the constant 3-dimensional space
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}=\mathrm{span}\{X,Y,N_{2}\}
.
From the third equality of (
8.2
) we get that the function
\displaystyle f(u)
is a solution to the following differential equation:

f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1=2af\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}},

where
\displaystyle a=const
,
\displaystyle a\neq 0
.
Setting
\displaystyle\dot{f}=\varphi(f)
, we obtain that the function
\displaystyle\varphi=\varphi(t)
is a solution to the equation:

(8.3)

\frac{t}{2}\,(\varphi^{2})^{\prime}+\varphi^{2}-1=2at\sqrt{1-\varphi^{2}}.

Now, by denoting
\displaystyle z(t)=\sqrt{1-\varphi^{2}(t)}
, the last equation is transformed into the equation:

z^{\prime}(t)+\frac{1}{t}\,z(t)=-2a,

whose general solution is given by the formula
\displaystyle z(t)=\frac{c-at^{2}}{t}
,
\displaystyle c=const
. Finally, we obtain that the general solution of (
8.3
) is

\varphi(t)=\pm\frac{1}{t}\sqrt{t^{2}-(c-a\,t^{2})^{2}}.

In this case,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is a CMC-surface, since the mean curvature vector field is expressed as
\displaystyle H=\pm a\,N_{2}
, which implies
\displaystyle\langle H,H\rangle=a^{2}=const
. Calculating the Gauss curvature
\displaystyle K
we obtain
\displaystyle K=\mp\frac{c^{2}-a^{2}t^{4}}{t^{4}}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is a non-flat CMC-surface lying in a constant hyperplane of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
.

Case (ii): The curvature of
c
is non-zero, i.e.
\displaystyle\varkappa\neq 0
. Then, from the second equality of (
8.2
) we obtain that the function
\displaystyle f(u)
has the following form:

f(u)=a,\quad a=const\neq 0.

Now, having in mind that
\displaystyle\dot{f}^{2}+\dot{g}^{2}=1
, we get
\displaystyle g(u)=\pm u+b
,
\displaystyle b=const
. In this case, the mean curvature vector field is expressed as:

H=-\frac{\varkappa}{2a}\,N_{1}\mp\frac{1}{2a}\,N_{2},

which implies that
\displaystyle\langle H,H\rangle=\frac{1+\varkappa^{2}}{4a^{2}}=const
. Hence,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is a CMC-surface.
Moreover, for the Gauss curvature we obtain
\displaystyle K=0
, i.e.
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is a flat surface. We will prove that the surface lies in a constant 3-dimensional space. Indeed, let us consider the normal vector fields

\bar{N}_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}+1}}(N_{1}\pm\varkappa\,N_{2});\quad\bar{N}_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}+1}}(\mp\varkappa\,N_{1}+N_{2}).

By use of equalities (
4.4
) we obtain
\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}\bar{N}_{1}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}\bar{N}_{1}=0
, which implies that
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
lies in the constant 3-dimensional space
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}=\mathrm{span}\{X,Y,\bar{N}_{2}\}
.
Consequently, in this case the meridian surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is a flat CMC-surface lying in a hyperplane of
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{4}_{1}
.

Conversely, if one of the cases (i) or (ii) in the theorem
holds true, then by direct computations we get that
\displaystyle D_{X}H=D_{Y}H=0
, which means that
the surface has parallel mean curvature vector field.

∎

In a similar way, we can describe all timelike second kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with parallel mean curvature vector field.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
be a timelike second type meridian surface of hyperbolic type. Then, its mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
is expressed by (
4.11
). Using (
4.10
) we obtain the following expressions for
\displaystyle D_{X}H
and
\displaystyle D_{Y}H
:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69046ptD_{X}H=-\frac{\varkappa\dot{f}}{2f^{2}}\,N_{1}\mp\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\left(\frac{f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1}{2f\sqrt{\dot{f}^{2}-1}}\right)\,N_{2};\\
\vskip 5.69046ptD_{Y}H=\frac{\varkappa^{\prime}}{2f^{2}}\,N_{1}.\end{array}

The timelike second type meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with parallel mean curvature vector field are classified in the next theorem.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
be a timelike meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (
3.2

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}

has zero curvature and the meridian curve

\displaystyle m
is determined by
\displaystyle\dot{f}=\varphi(f)
, where

\varphi(t)=\pm\frac{1}{t}\sqrt{t^{2}+(C+a\,t^{2})^{2}},\quad a=const\neq 0,\quad C=const,

\displaystyle g(u)
is defined by
\displaystyle\dot{g}=\pm\sqrt{\dot{f}^{2}-1}
. In this case,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{4}_{1}
.

(ii)

has non-zero constant curvature and the

\displaystyle f(u)=a
,
\displaystyle g(u)=\pm u+b
, where
\displaystyle a=const\neq 0
,
\displaystyle b=const
. In this case,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}

a hyperplane of
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{4}_{1}

.

There are no timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with parallel mean curvature vector field other than CMC-surfaces lying in a hyperplane of

9.
Timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field

This section is devoted to the class of timelike meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field, but non-parallel
\displaystyle H
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
be a timelike meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (
3.1
), and assume that
\displaystyle\langle H,H\rangle\neq 0
and
\displaystyle H
is not parallel. Then, the normalized mean curvature vector field of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
is defined as
\displaystyle H_{0}=\frac{H}{\sqrt{\langle H,H\rangle}}
. Having in mind (
4.6
), we obtain:

(9.1)

H_{0}=-\frac{\varkappa\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}}}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}(1-\dot{f}^{2})+(f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1)^{2}}}\,N_{1}\pm\frac{f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}(1-\dot{f}^{2})+(f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1)^{2}}}\,N_{2}.

Using (
4.4
) and (
9.1
) we get the following expressions for the vector fields
\displaystyle D_{X}H_{0}
and
\displaystyle D_{Y}H_{0}
:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69046ptD_{X}H_{0}\!=\!-\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\!\left(\!\!\frac{\varkappa\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}}}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}(1-\dot{f}^{2})+(f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1)^{2}}}\!\!\right)\!\!N_{1}\pm\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\!\left(\!\!\frac{f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}(1-\dot{f}^{2})+(f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1)^{2}}}\!\!\right)\!\!N_{2};\\
\vskip 5.69046ptD_{Y}H_{0}\!=\!-\frac{1}{f}\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\!\left(\!\!\frac{\varkappa\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}}}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}(1-\dot{f}^{2})+(f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1)^{2}}}\!\!\right)\!\!N_{1}\pm\frac{1}{f}\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\!\left(\!\!\frac{f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}(1-\dot{f}^{2})+(f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1)^{2}}}\!\!\right)\!\!N_{2}.\end{array}

The last formulas imply that
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
has parallel normalized mean curvature vector field (
\displaystyle D_{X}H_{0}=D_{Y}H_{0}=0
) if and only if the following two equalities hold true:

(9.2)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69046pt\frac{\varkappa\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}}}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}(1-\dot{f}^{2})+(f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1)^{2}}}=\mathrm{const}=\alpha;\\
\vskip 5.69046pt\frac{f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}(1-\dot{f}^{2})+(f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1)^{2}}}=\mathrm{const}=\beta,\end{array}

for some constants
\displaystyle\alpha
and
\displaystyle\beta
.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
be a timelike meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (
3.1
). Then,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
has parallel normalized mean curvature vector field, but non-parallel mean curvature vector, if and only if one of the following cases holds:

(i)
\displaystyle\varkappa\neq 0
and the meridian curve
\displaystyle m
is defined by

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptf(u)=\pm\sqrt{u^{2}+2au+b},\\
\vskip 5.69054ptg(u)=\pm\sqrt{b-a^{2}}\,\ln|(u+a)+\sqrt{u^{2}+2au+b}|+c,\end{array}

where
\displaystyle a=\mathrm{const}
,
\displaystyle b=\mathrm{const}
,
\displaystyle c=\mathrm{const}
,
\displaystyle b-a^{2}>0
.

(ii) the curve
c
has non-zero constant spherical curvature and the meridian curve
\displaystyle m
is determined by

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptg(t)=\frac{at^{2}}{2}-ct+b,\\
\vskip 5.69054ptf(t)=\pm\left[\frac{\sqrt{1-(at-c)^{2}}(2at-c)}{4a}+\frac{1}{2a}\arcsin(at-c)\right],\end{array}

where
\displaystyle a=\mathrm{const}
,
\displaystyle b=\mathrm{const}
,
\displaystyle c=\mathrm{const}\neq 0,\;c^{2}\neq\varkappa^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{I}_{m}
be a timelike first kind meridian surface of hyperbolic type with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field, i.e.
\displaystyle D_{X}H_{0}=D_{Y}H_{0}=0
, or equivalently, equalities (
9.2
) hold true. We will consider the following two possible cases.

Case (i):
\displaystyle f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1=0
. In this case, from (
9.1
) it follows that the normalized mean curvature vector field is
\displaystyle H_{0}=N_{1}
and the mean curvature vector field is

\displaystyle H=-\frac{\varkappa}{2f}\,N_{1}
. Hence,
\displaystyle\varkappa\neq 0
(otherwise the surface is minimal). The general solution to the differential equation

\displaystyle f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1=0
is given by

f(u)=\pm\sqrt{u^{2}+2au+b},

where
\displaystyle a=\mathrm{const}
,
\displaystyle b=\mathrm{const}
. Using that
\displaystyle\dot{g}=\pm\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}}
, after integration we obtain that the function
\displaystyle g(u)
is expressed as follows:

g(u)=\pm\sqrt{b-a^{2}}\,\ln|(u+a)+\sqrt{u^{2}+2au+b}|+c,

where
\displaystyle c=\mathrm{const}
,
\displaystyle b-a^{2}>0
.

Case (ii):
\displaystyle f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1\neq 0
in a sub-interval
\displaystyle I_{0}\subset I\subset\mathbb{R}
. In this case, formulas (
9.2
) imply

(9.3)

\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\,\varkappa=\frac{f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1}{\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}}},\quad\alpha\neq 0,\;\beta\neq 0.

Since the left-hand side of (
9.3
) is a function of
\displaystyle v
, while the right-hand side is a function of
\displaystyle u
, it follows that

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\frac{f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1}{\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}}}=c,\quad c=\mathrm{const}\neq 0;\\
\varkappa=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\,c=\mathrm{const}.\end{array}

In this case, for the mean curvature vector field we have
\displaystyle\langle H,H\rangle=\frac{\varkappa^{2}+c^{2}}{4f^{2}}\neq 0
.
To find the solutions of the differential
equation:

(9.4)

f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1=c\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}},

we set
\displaystyle\dot{f}=\varphi(f)
and obtain
that the function
\displaystyle\varphi=\varphi(t)
satisfies

(9.5)

\frac{t}{2}\,(\varphi^{2})^{\prime}+\varphi^{2}-1=c\sqrt{1-\varphi^{2}}.

By setting
\displaystyle z(t)=\sqrt{1-\varphi^{2}(t)}
, the last equation is transformed to

z^{\prime}-\frac{1}{t}\,z=\frac{c}{t},

whose solution is given by the formula
\displaystyle z(t)=at-c
,
\displaystyle a=\mathrm{const}
.
Consequently, the general solution of (
9.5
) is given by the formula

\varphi(t)=\pm\sqrt{1-(at-c)^{2}}.

Using that
\displaystyle\dot{f}=\varphi
and
\displaystyle\dot{g}=\sqrt{1-\dot{f}^{2}}
, from the above formula we obtain:

g=\frac{at^{2}}{2}-ct+b,\,\,b=\mathrm{const},

f=\pm\int{\sqrt{1-(at-c)^{2}}}\,\rm{d}t=\pm\left[\frac{\sqrt{1-(at-c)^{2}}(2at-c)}{4a}+\frac{1}{2a}\arcsin(at-c)\right].

Conversely, if one of the cases (i) or (ii) in the theorem holds true, then by direct computations we obtain that

\displaystyle D_{X}H_{0}=D_{Y}H_{0}=0
, which means that the surface has parallel normalized mean curvature vector field.
Moreover, in case (i) we get

D_{X}H=\frac{\varkappa\dot{f}}{2f^{2}}\,N_{1};\qquad D_{Y}H=-\frac{\varkappa^{\prime}}{2f^{2}}\,N_{1},

and in case (ii) we get

D_{X}H=\frac{\varkappa\dot{f}}{2f^{2}}\,N_{1}\pm\frac{C\dot{f}}{2f^{2}}\,N_{2};\qquad D_{Y}H=0.

Hence, in both cases the mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
is not parallel in the normal bundle, since
\displaystyle\varkappa\neq 0
and
\displaystyle\dot{f}\neq 0
.

∎

In a similar way, we can describe all timelike second kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
be a timelike meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (
3.2
), and the mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
is non-parallel,
\displaystyle\langle H,H\rangle\neq 0
. The normalized mean curvature vector field is
\displaystyle H_{0}=\frac{H}{\sqrt{\langle H,H\rangle}}
. Using (
4.11
) we get:

(9.6)

H_{0}=\frac{\varkappa\sqrt{\dot{f}^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}(\dot{f}^{2}-1)+(f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1)^{2}}}\,N_{1}\mp\frac{f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}(\dot{f}^{2}-1)+(f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1)^{2}}}\,N_{2}.

The surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
has parallel normalized mean curvature vector field if and only if

\displaystyle D_{X}H_{0}=D_{Y}H_{0}=0
.
Using (
4.10
) and (
9.6
) we obtain:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69046ptD_{X}H_{0}=\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\!\left(\!\!\frac{\varkappa\sqrt{\dot{f}^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}(\dot{f}^{2}-1)+(f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1)^{2}}}\!\!\right)\!N_{1}\mp\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\!\left(\!\!\frac{f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}(\dot{f}^{2}-1)+(f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1)^{2}}}\!\!\right)\!N_{2};\\
\vskip 5.69046ptD_{Y}H_{0}=\frac{1}{f}\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\!\left(\!\!\frac{\varkappa\sqrt{\dot{f}^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}(\dot{f}^{2}-1)+(f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1)^{2}}}\!\!\right)\!N_{1}\mp\frac{1}{f}\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\!\left(\!\!\frac{f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}(\dot{f}^{2}-1)+(f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1)^{2}}}\!\!\right)\!N_{2}.\end{array}

Hence,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
has parallel normalized mean curvature vector field if and only if the following equalities hold true:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69046pt\frac{\varkappa\sqrt{\dot{f}^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}(\dot{f}^{2}-1)+(f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1)^{2}}}=\mathrm{const}=\alpha;\\
\vskip 5.69046pt\frac{f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1}{\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}(\dot{f}^{2}-1)+(f\ddot{f}+\dot{f}^{2}-1)^{2}}}=\mathrm{const}=\beta,\end{array}

for some constants
\displaystyle\alpha
and
\displaystyle\beta
.

The timelike second kind meridian surfaces of hyperbolic type with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field are described in the next theorem.

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}
be a timelike meridian surface of hyperbolic type, defined by (
3.2

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{II}_{m}

(i)

and the meridian curve

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptf(u)=\pm\sqrt{u^{2}+2au+b},\\
\vskip 5.69054ptg(u)=\pm\sqrt{a^{2}-b}\,\ln|(u+a)+\sqrt{u^{2}+2au+b}|+c,\end{array}

where
\displaystyle a=\mathrm{const}
,
\displaystyle b=\mathrm{const}
,
\displaystyle c=\mathrm{const}
,
\displaystyle a^{2}-b>0
.

c
has non-zero constant curvature and the meridian curve
\displaystyle m

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptg(t)=a\ln t+ct+b,\\
