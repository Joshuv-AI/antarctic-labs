Stop Suppressing the Tail: Causal Inference for Extreme Events
Eichi Uehara

Abstract
Estimating how an outcome responds, on average, to a continuous treatment — the Average Dose-Response Function (ADRF) — is a core causal-inference primitive. When outcomes are heavy-tailed (financial returns, climate losses, drug-dosage adverse events), standard robust double machine learning (DML) deliberately suppresses the tail to stabilise the bulk average. Yet in exactly these high-stakes settings the tail is the target quantity: the 1 1 -in- 1000 1000 loss, not the bulk mean, determines the decision, so a bulk-only estimate omits the quantity of interest. A natural alternative — reading the tail off the model’s residuals — is circular: the inferred Generalised-Pareto shape ξ ^ \widehat{\xi} shifts from − 0.42 -0.42 (bounded) to + 0.28 +0.28 (heavy) on one dataset solely as a function of switching the core estimator between Huber and Welsch. We propose an ADRF estimator that emits a structured tail-shape output alongside the point estimate, without suppressing the extremes. Its tail diagnostic (PDHTE+JK) estimates the per-treatment tail shape from the outcome centred by a pilot median — not from core-estimator residuals — which breaks the circular dependence and renders the diagnostic invariant to the choice of core method. The output comprises four quantities: treatment-conditional tail shape ξ ^ ​ ( t ) \widehat{\xi}(t) , deep-tail return levels Q ^ α ​ ( t ) \widehat{Q}_{\alpha}(t) , conditional shortfalls S ^ α ​ ( t ) \widehat{S}_{\alpha}(t) , the recovered mean ADRF, and an explicit refusal that declines extrapolation when the data do not support extreme-value modelling. Against kernel-weighted quantile regression (QR), the proposed estimator reduces deep-tail ( α = 0.001 \alpha=0.001 ) return-level MAE by 11 % 11\% and conditional-shortfall MAE by 25.5 % 25.5\% across a 10 10 -DGP heavy-tailed panel, and by 20 20 – 29 % 29\% in the sample-scarce regime ( n ≤ 2000 n\leq 2000 ). On real freMTPL2 motor-insurance claims (excess kurtosis 1667 1667 ) it explicitly refuses on the log-claim scale — an output neither QR nor loss-only DML produces. Keywords: double machine learning, dose-response, heavy tails, extreme value theory, causal inference.

Threshold Selection and GPD Tail Inference

The tail algorithm consumes post-DML residuals
\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}

and emits a triple
(\widehat{u}^{\star},\widehat{\xi},\widehat{\sigma})
that
feeds the core estimator (
Section

4
) and the post-core
component (
Section

5
). When no candidate threshold satisfies a
joint goodness-of-fit and exceedance-budget gate, the algorithm

refuses
(returns
u^{\star}=\emptyset
) and the pipeline
falls back to ordinary local-linear DML on the full sample.

The remainder of the section instantiates this template: a
likelihood-based rule for picking
\widehat{u}^{\star}
, the PWM
estimator that then produces
(\widehat{\xi},\widehat{\sigma})
from the
exceedances, and the conditions under which the rule refuses.

The strictly proper scoring rule we adopt is the

composite log-likelihood
of a Laplace-bulk + GPD-tail
splice density on
\mathbb{R}
:

\widehat{f}(r;u,b,\xi,\sigma)=\begin{cases}(1-p_{\rm tail})\,\dfrac{e^{-|r|/b}}{2b(1-e^{-u/b})}&|r|\leq u,\\[10.0pt]
p_{\rm tail}\,\tfrac{1}{2}\,g_{\xi,\sigma}(|r|-u)&|r|>u,\end{cases}

(1)

where
b=\sigma_{\rm core}/\sqrt{2}
is the Laplace scale,

p_{\rm tail}=n_{\rm exc}/n
, and
g_{\xi,\sigma}
is the GPD
density. The truncation factor
(1-e^{-u/b})
in the bulk makes
the bulk integrate to
1-p_{\rm tail}
, so
\widehat{f}
integrates
to one.

Definition 1

(Composite-likelihood threshold)
.

Given residuals
\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, the threshold

\widehat{u}^{\star}
is the maximiser of the held-out composite
log-likelihood subject to two gates:

\widehat{u}^{\star}=\arg\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\log\widehat{f}(r_{i};u,\widehat{b}(u),\widehat{\xi}(u),\widehat{\sigma}(u))

(2)

with

\mathcal{U}=\big[q_{0.5}(|r|),\,q_{1-n_{\min}/n}(|r|)\big]
,

n_{\min}=30
, gates:

•

Exceedance budget
:
n_{\rm exc}(u)\geq n_{\rm min,exc}

where

is a user-chosen floor (default
30
;

\geq 100
when stability matters more than coverage of the

\widehat{\xi}

•

GPD goodness-of-fit

p

(default
0

If no
u\in\mathcal{U}
satisfies both gates, the rule

refuses

\widehat{u}^{\star}=\emptyset

The two gates address a known systematic downward bias on

\widehat{\xi}
from the unconstrained maximiser of

Equation

2

dominates the joint likelihood and pulls

exceedances are contaminated with sub-tail bulk observations. The

the GPD asymptotic holds and where the exceedance sample is large
enough that PWM is not dominated by small-sample variance. The
lower bound
q_{0.5}(|r|)
separately enforces

\Pr(|r|\leq u^{\star})\geq 1/2

definitional requirement of the splice-density framing.

Treating
Definition

1

regularity conditions deliver consistency. With

L(u)=\mathbb{E}_{r}\log\widehat{f}(r;u,\cdot)

(

With

parameters

Weighted Moments on the exceedances. Let

\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n_{\rm exc}}=\{|r_{i}|-\widehat{u}^{\star}:|r_{i}|>\widehat{u}^{\star}\}

(3)

The tail output

\mathcal{T}=(\widehat{u}^{\star},\widehat{\xi}^{\rm PWM},[\widehat{\xi}_{\ell},\widehat{\xi}_{h}],\widehat{\sigma}^{\rm PWM},\widehat{p}_{\rm KS},\widehat{\rm regime},\widehat{r}_{q})

For

\widehat{r}_{q}=\widehat{u}^{\star}+\frac{\widehat{\sigma}^{\rm PWM}}{\widehat{\xi}^{\rm PWM}}\!\left[\!\left(\frac{n_{\rm exc}}{qn}\right)^{\!\widehat{\xi}^{\rm PWM}}-1\right].

(4)

A central open problem in continuous-treatment ADRF estimation
under heavy tails is the
path-dependence
of the tail-shape
estimate
\widehat{\xi}
on the choice of core method, which we
document empirically:
on synthetic data with one-sided Gaussian outliers
(

DGP,

applied to post-DML residuals gives

•

\widehat{\xi}^{\rm Standard}=-0.41
(bounded-tail/Weibull domain)

•

\widehat{\xi}^{\rm Huber}=+0.28
(Fréchet domain — heavy tail)

•

\widehat{\xi}^{\rm Welsch}=-0.42
(bounded-tail/Weibull domain)

The same data, the same observed
Y
, but the inferred tail
behaviour flips sign with the choice of robust core method. No
existing approach in the heavy-tail causal-inference literature
(
11
,
28
,
27

16
) addresses this circular dependence.
The reason is structural: estimating
\widehat{\xi}
from

r=Y-\widehat{\theta}(T)
entangles tail behaviour with the choice
of
\widehat{\theta}
. Robust core methods (Huber) leave asymmetric
outliers in the residuals, where PWM fits a heavy GPD; OLS-type
methods absorb the bias of the asymmetry into
\widehat{\theta}
,
leaving symmetric-looking residuals where PWM fits a bounded GPD.

We resolve the circular dependence by estimating
\xi(t)

within kernel-weighted
T
-bands using only the outcome
Y

centred by a
pilot kernel-weighted median
— a fixed,
core-method-independent location estimate, never a fitted

\widehat{\theta}

residual, but since it does not depend on the downstream core
method, the circular path-dependence
(

4

Replacing
\widehat{\theta}(T)
with the pilot median trades one
source of error for another: the pilot median itself is
estimated, and that estimation error propagates into the
log-spacings driving the Hill and DEdH statistics. We bound this
propagation under a mild regularity condition. The pilot
median is a
bulk
quantile, estimated at the non-parametric rate

\tilde{Y}(t_{0})-m(t_{0})=O_{P}((nh_{T})^{-1/2})=O_{P}(n^{-2/5})

(Silverman bandwidth). A location shift perturbs the

\delta=\tilde{Y}-m
and

the

-quantile threshold. We assume the
regularity condition

\delta/u_{\kappa}=o_{P}(\mathrm{sd}(\widehat{\xi}^{\rm DEdH}))
—
pilot error negligible against the tail-estimator’s sampling
error; a formal lemma propagating
\delta

Section

7

Method-invariance establishes that PDHTE’s output does not depend
on the choice of core estimator, but it does not by itself imply
that the output is a
causal
quantity. The next step is to
relate the observational law that PDHTE consumes to the causal
target.
PDHTE estimates the tail of the
observational
law

Y\mid T=t
. The causal target is the tail of the potential
outcome
Y(t)
, whose law is
\mathbb{E}_{X}[F_{Y\mid T=t,X}]
under
unconfoundedness, whereas
Y\mid T=t
has law

\mathbb{E}_{X\mid T=t}[F_{Y\mid T=t,X}]
. These coincide when

T\perp X
— which holds by construction in the synthetic
panel (
Section

6
:
T
is drawn independently of

X
), so every synthetic result estimates the causal tail
directly. Two facts govern the confounded (
T\not\perp X
) case:

Proposition 2

(Tail index is confounding-robust)
.

The tail index of a mixture equals the maximum of the component
indices. Hence whenever positivity holds — every
X
-stratum
has positive density of every
t
— the index of

\mathbb{E}_{X\mid T=t}[F_{Y\mid T=t,X}]
equals that of

\mathbb{E}_{X}[F_{Y\mid T=t,X}]
: confounding shifts mixture

proportions
but not the maximum. The per-
T

\widehat{\xi}(t)
is therefore a causal quantity even under
confounding.

The return level
\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}(t)
and shortfall

\widehat{S}_{\alpha}(t)
depend on mixture proportions and
are

confounded. We apply a
first-order partial deconfounding
:
stabilized generalized-propensity-score (GPS) weights

sw_{i}=\widehat{f}_{T}(T_{i})/\widehat{f}_{T\mid X}(T_{i}\mid X_{i})
multiply the
kernel weights, restoring the marginal
X
-distribution in each

T
-band. Because
\widehat{f}_{T\mid X}
is a treatment-side nuisance
shared by all core methods, method-invariance
(
Proposition

1
) is preserved.

This GPS correction is not a free lunch. It is consistent only
when overlap holds and the propensity is correctly specified,
and continuous-treatment IPW is intrinsically high-variance —
constraints we now make explicit.
The correction requires positivity/overlap (
\widehat{f}_{T\mid X}

bounded away from zero on the reported
t
-grid) and a correctly
specified GPS, here a Gaussian propensity from a cross-fit

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[T\mid X]
. Continuous-treatment IPW is high-variance,
amplified in the tail where the weighted quantile rests on few
heavily-reweighted exceedances; we winsorise and hard-clip the
weights and restrict the
t
-grid to the adequate-overlap
interior. The result is a
partial
correction:

Section

6
verifies a
63\%
reduction in
causal-return-level bias, leaving
\sim 37\%
. We do
not

claim
\sqrt{n}
-consistency of the GPS-weighted hybrid estimator;
a doubly-robust or trimmed-weight refinement removing the
residual bias is future work (
Section

7
).

Taking together the core estimator of

\widehat{\xi}(t)

(

back into the Welsch

is empirically worse than a constant

on the

Under asymmetric residuals

\mathbb{E}[Y(t)]
. With sign-conditional exceedance sets

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[Y(t)]=\widehat{\theta}_{W}(t)\,(1-\widehat{p}_{+}-\widehat{p}_{-})+\big(\widehat{\theta}_{W}(t)+\widehat{u}^{\star}+\tfrac{\widehat{\sigma}_{+}}{1-\widehat{\xi}_{+}}\big)\widehat{p}_{+}-\big(\widehat{\theta}_{W}(t)-\widehat{u}^{\star}-\tfrac{\widehat{\sigma}_{-}}{1-\widehat{\xi}_{-}}\big)\widehat{p}_{-}.

on

to

Definition 7

7
Discussion

We have argued that the standard practice of suppressing the
heavy tail to stabilise an ADRF point estimate discards the
quantity that drives high-stakes decisions, and that reading the
tail off post-DML residuals reintroduces the choice of core
estimator as a confounder of the tail diagnostic. The proposed
estimator answers both concerns: a constant-clipping Welsch core
(
TailWelsch-DML
) preserves the standard Neyman-orthogonal DML rate, and a
plateau-detection diagnostic (PDHTE+JK) on the pilot-median-
centred outcome produces a per-
T
tail shape that is invariant
to the choice of core method. The resulting tail-conditional
output — treatment-conditional shape, return level,
conditional shortfall, sign-conditional mean recovery, and an
explicit refusal — is produced at
O(n\log n)
cost on the
residuals already computed. Empirically the estimator reduces
deep-tail return-level MAE by
11\%
and conditional-shortfall
MAE by
25.5\%
against the quantile-regression baseline, and by

20
–
29\%
for
n\leq 2000
; on freMTPL2 motor-insurance
claims it refuses on the log-claim scale, indicating that the
data do not support EVT extrapolation.

Two scope conditions qualify these results. First,
TailWelsch-DML
targets a
Welsch M-functional that equals
\mathbb{E}[Y(t)]
only under symmetric
residuals; under asymmetric contamination it approximates the
structural
\theta(t)
, and the sign-conditional recovery
(
Equation

11
) is the additional step for the mean ADRF
including the tail. For
\xi\geq 1
the mean is undefined and
only the M-functional is sensible. Second, under
T
-
X

confounding the tail
index
remains causal by max-stability
(
Proposition

2
); the return level and shortfall are
confounded and are corrected by GPS-stabilised weighting, which
removes
63\%
of the bias but is not
\sqrt{n}
-consistent.

Within this scope, several limitations remain.
(i) Per-
T

\widehat{\xi}(t)
retains
\sim 0.05
residual bias after
jackknife, carried into
\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}(t)
at deep
\alpha
;
ordering across
t
is reliable below
n\sim 1500
but absolute
level is not.
(ii)
T
-localised contamination defeats
TailWelsch-DML
, Standard, and Huber
alike.
(iii) Only the conditional shortfall is independently validated
against an oracle; the causal-tail effect inherits its accuracy
from
\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}
but is not separately benchmarked.
(iv) The estimator targets univariate residual tails under weak
temporal dependence; the Laplace bulk is a swappable default.
(v) Three asymptotic gaps remain open: pilot-median error
propagation through the DEdH limit (
Section

4
),
GPS-weighted return-level consistency
(
Section

4
), and MSE-optimal jackknife damping
(
Equation

10
). Closing these and extending to
multivariate residual tails are the natural next steps.

A.1
Consistency of the Composite-Likelihood Threshold

Let
L(u)=\mathbb{E}_{r}[\log\widehat{f}(r;u,b(u),\xi(u),\sigma(u))]

denote the population composite log-likelihood of the
Laplace-bulk + GPD-tail splice (
Equation

1
) as a function
of the threshold
u
. The sample analogue is

L_{n}(u)=n^{-1}\sum_{i}\log\widehat{f}(r_{i};u,\widehat{b}(u),\widehat{\xi}(u),\widehat{\sigma}(u))
. Under the gates of
Definition

1

(KS-validity, minimum exceedance budget), let
\mathcal{U}
denote
the gated parameter space and define

u^{\star}_{0}=\arg\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}L(u)
.

Sketch.

Standard M-estimation consistency

(
31
, Theorem 5.7)
. Required
ingredients:

1.

\mathcal{U}
is a closed and bounded subset of the original
search range
[q_{0.5}(|r|),q_{1-n_{\min}/n}(|r|)]
(the gates
are upper semi-continuous in
u
).

2.

L
is continuous in
u
on
\mathcal{U}
and has a unique
maximiser in the interior of
\mathcal{U}
. Uniqueness follows from
the strictly proper scoring rule property of the splice density:
the population maximiser is unique when the residual density is
absolutely continuous and the splice family is identifiable at

u
.

3.

\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}|L_{n}(u)-L(u)|\xrightarrow{P}0
(uniform convergence). The integrands are
continuous in
u
and bounded on compact
u
-sets because the GPD
density is continuous in
(\xi,\sigma)
at the PWM estimates,
which are continuous in
u
for
\xi
bounded away from
1/2

(PWM identification breaks down at
\xi=1/2
; in practice we
clip).

Then
\widehat{u}^{\star}\xrightarrow{P}u^{\star}_{0}
by the standard
argmax-consistency theorem.
∎

Sketch of method-invariance (
Proposition

1
).

(Y,T)

A.3
Sketch: Welsch local-linear DML consistency

Sketch.

TailWelsch-DML
uses the Welsch weight
w(r)=\exp(-\gamma(r/\sigma)^{2})
at a

constant

\gamma
— there is no data-dependent tuning in
the second-stage loss. The consistency argument is the standard
two-step DML construction.

Step 1: nuisance step.
Cross-fit DML conditions

(
6
)
give
\widehat{g}
converging in

L^{2}(P)
at rate
o_{P}(n^{-1/4})
. Then

r^{Y}_{i}=Y_{i}-\widehat{g}(X_{i})
is a
L^{2}
-consistent estimate of the
true residual
r^{Y,\star}_{i}=Y_{i}-g_{0}(X_{i})
.

Step 2: local-linear step.
Because
\gamma
is fixed, the
Welsch population score
\psi(r)=r\,e^{-\gamma(r/\sigma)^{2}}
is
a fixed bounded, Lipschitz, odd function; its derivative at the
truth is mean-zero in the first-stage nuisance, so the
Neyman-orthogonality condition of
Chernozhukov et al. 2018

holds without modification. Standard local-linear M-estimator
theory
(
14
)
then gives, at interior
t_{0}
under
twice-differentiability of
\theta_{W}
in
t
and a positive
bounded second derivative of the population score,

\widehat{\theta}_{W}(t_{0})-\theta_{W}(t_{0})=O_{P}(n^{-2/5})
.

Remark (data-dependent
\gamma
).
An earlier variant tied

\gamma
to a data-driven
\widehat{\xi}
. There
\gamma
is a tuning
parameter of the
target functional
, not a first-stage
nuisance:
\widehat{\xi}
enters the loss, and provided

\widehat{\xi}\xrightarrow{P}\xi_{0}
the estimand stabilises to

\theta_{W}(t;\gamma(\xi_{0}))
, with orthogonality w.r.t. the
first-stage nuisances
g,m
unaffected (the Welsch score’s
nuisance-derivative is mean-zero for every fixed
\gamma
).
We nonetheless report the constant-
\gamma
estimator, which has
no such subtlety and is empirically no worse
(
Section

6
).
∎

A.4
Sign-Conditional Recovery of
\mathbb{E}[Y(t)]

Let
F_{r\mid T=t}
denote the conditional density of the
residual
r
given treatment
t
, with support on
\mathbb{R}
. Suppose
the right tail
F_{r\mid T=t}\big(\cdot\mid r>u^{\star}\big)
is
GPD with shape
\xi_{+}
, scale
\sigma_{+}
and left tail

F_{-r\mid T=t}\big(\cdot\mid r<-u^{\star}\big)
is GPD with
shape
\xi_{-}
, scale
\sigma_{-}
. Let
p_{\pm}(t)=\Pr(r\gtrless\pm u^{\star}\mid T=t)
and
p_{b}(t)=1-p_{+}(t)-p_{-}(t)
.

Decomposing
\mathbb{E}[Y(t)]=\theta_{W}(t)+\mathbb{E}[r\mid T=t]
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[r\mid T=t]

\displaystyle=p_{b}\,\mathbb{E}[r\mid|r|\leq u^{\star},T=t]+p_{+}\,\mathbb{E}[r\mid r>u^{\star},T=t]+p_{-}\,\mathbb{E}[r\mid r<-u^{\star},T=t].

The first term is approximately zero under the Welsch fit (the
M-functional centres the bulk). The second term, by the GPD
mean-excess identity (valid for
\xi_{+}<1
), is

u^{\star}+\sigma_{+}/(1-\xi_{+})
. The third term is its mirror:

-(u^{\star}+\sigma_{-}/(1-\xi_{-}))
. Substituting gives

Equation

11
.

For symmetric tails (
p_{+}=p_{-}
,
\xi_{+}=\xi_{-}
,

\sigma_{+}=\sigma_{-}
), the second and third terms cancel exactly
and
\mathbb{E}[r\mid T=t]=0
, so
\mathbb{E}[Y(t)]=\theta_{W}(t)
. For
one-sided positive contamination (
p_{-}\approx 0
), only the
second term contributes and the recovered
\mathbb{E}[Y(t)]
shifts above

\theta_{W}(t)
by the GPD positive-tail mean times
p_{+}
.
