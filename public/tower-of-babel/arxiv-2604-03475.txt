Scheduling Electricity Production Units to Mitigate Severe Weather Impact: An Efficient Computational Implementation
Yongzheng Dai, Antonio J. Conejo, Feng Qiu

Abstract
In the electric system, extreme weather events can cause trips or physical damage to transmission lines, leading to large-scale load shedding. To mitigate power shedding, we propose a framework that pre-positions the commitment of production units—particularly slow-start units—to cope with transmission topologies that may result from such events. Our goal is to minimize load shedding under the worst-case scenario. The novel contributions of this paper are twofold: (1) a more precise description of the physical laws than those used in previous works reported in the literature, and (2) a highly efficient solution algorithm compared to state-of-the-art, off-the-shelf solvers. We formulate this framework as a two-stage robust optimization model. In the first stage, generation units are scheduled, and in the second stage, power dispatch decisions are made to minimize load shedding under the worst-case scenario. Convexified AC power flow constraints are incorporated to ensure system reliability and security. The resulting formulation is a tri-level mixed-integer nonlinear optimization problem. To address the computational challenges, we propose a problem-specific outer approximation algorithm embedded within a column-and-constraint generation framework. Computational results demonstrate that our model and algorithm can produce solutions within a standard optimality gap in reasonable time for moderately large instances.

1
Introduction

1.1
Motivation, formulation and solution technique

Extreme weather events result in substantial economic loss worldwide.
Campbell and Lowry 2012
indicated that major power outages, mainly caused by storm-related damage to electricity transmission lines, were estimated to cost about $20–$55 billion annually in lost orders, spoiled inventory, delayed production, and other disruptions. On a global scale, extreme weather events including hurricanes, floods, droughts, and storms imposed more than $2 trillion dollars over the past decade, with the United States accounting for nearly half of this total
(
International Chamber of Commerce and Oxera 2024
)
.

Severe weather also inflicts significant damage on energy infrastructure. For example, hurricanes Katrina and Rita damaged or destroyed thousands of miles of transmission lines and hundreds of substations; in Southeast Louisiana and Mississippi, 1.1 million customers lost power after Katrina
(
United States Senate Committee on Energy and Natural
Resources 2005
)
. In 2017, Hurricane Maria caused power outage affecting almost all the customers in Puerto Rico until September 20, 2017
(
Infrastructure Security and Energy
Restoration 2017
)
. More recently, Hurricane Ida (2021) forced the complete failure of all eight transmission lines supplying New Orleans, cutting power to nearly 895,000 customers in Southeastern Louisiana
(
News-Record 2021
)
. These examples highlight how transmission line failures due to wind, flooding, or structural collapse can cascade through the system, requiring emergency redispatch of generation or involuntary load shedding to preserve system stability.

To reduce the negative impact of extreme weather events, many strategies have been studied and adopted, including infrastructure hardening, operational improvements
(
Abdelmalak and Benidris 2022
;
U.S. Department of Energy 2018
)
, and robust planning
(
Sang et al. 2019
;
Mohammadi and Sahraei-Ardakani 2020
)
. There have been significant investments to harden the infrastructure, e.g., $3 billion spent by Florida Power and Light (FPL)
(
Florida Power & Light Company 2017
)
. At the operations level, researchers have investigated both proactive and emergency control actions that change generator commitments and dispatches in anticipation (i.e. redispatch) to component outages
(
Wang et al. 2016
;
Abdelmalak and Benidris 2022
)
.

In this paper, we focus on providing an AC-based robust power generation schedule against the impact of severe weather on transmission lines. Our main purpose is imposing on this scheduling problem robust security against major weather events, which result in an adaptive robust optimization (tri-level) model involving three interrelated problems. Such approach is important to ensure appropriate security against increasingly frequent weather events. We optimize the scheduling of power production units (unit commitment problem) representing the transmission network via AC constraints in second-order-conic format. As it is customary, we slightly relax this formulation to make it convex, which may result in minor imprecisions in the final solution. Such imprecisions typically affect flow and production values, not the commitment of the units.

The first-level problem of the three-level adaptive robust optimization model determines the actual unit commitment of the generating units seeking minimum cost and anticipating the worst possible weather event. Given the commitment identified by the first-level problem, the second-level problem detects the worst-case weather event that results in maximum cost, anticipating the best possible operation (minimum cost) of the system by the system operator. Finally, the third-level problem operates the system seeking minimum cost once the worst weather event (as determined by the second-level problem) materializes with the schedule from the first-level problem. We note that this is a complicated min-max-min optimization problem, with conic, not linear constraints.

Since the third-level problem is a multi-period optimal power flow problem, which is second-order-conic and convex, we can replace it by its dual problem without approximation, in turn, merged with the second-level problem, which returns in a two-level problem. This two-level problem can be solved using the column-and-constraint generation algorithm first proposed by
Zeng and Zhao 2013
.

The column-and-constraint generation algorithm involves a master problem whose size dramatically increases with the number of iterations and that is mixed-integer second-order-conic, and a subproblem that is second-order-conic continuous and whose size is moderate. Since the master problem is the computational bottleneck, to alleviate its computational burden an effective problem-specific outer approximation technique is employed.

1.2
Literature Review

Second-order-conic (SOC) relaxation for unit commitment problem are proposed in
(
Bai et al. 2015
;
Liu et al. 2018
)
. While the SOC relaxation convexifies the continuous network laws, commitment decisions need to be made, resulting in a computationally challenging in mixed-integer SOC Problem
(
Bonami et al. 2011
)
due to the high cost of solving conic subproblems within branch-and-bound framework. To mitigate these issues, some authors have proposed strengthening the SOCP relaxation using valid inequalities and bound tightening
(
Kocuk et al. 2016
;
Coffrin et al. 2015
)
, as well as decomposition algorithms to separate commitment decisions and AC feasibility
(
Bai et al. 2015
;
Constante-Flores et al. 2022
;
Tuncer and Kocuk 2022
)
.

Despite being substantially harder to solve than DC unit commitment problem formulated as a mixed-integer linear program
(
Carrión and Arroyo 2006
;
Conejo and Baringo 2018
)
, SOC-relaxed AC unit commitment provides commitment that are more consistent with physical network constraints. DC unit commitment relies on linearized DC power flow, ignoring reactive power, voltage magnitudes, and losses
(
Wood et al. 2013
)
, and may therefore yield commitment schedules that are infeasible or require significant corrective actions when evaluated under full AC laws
(
Bai et al. 2015
;
Molzahn et al. 2019
)
. In contrast, SOC-based AC unit commitment captures key AC feasibility aspects while maintaining convexity in the continuous relaxation, producing tighter lower bounds and more reliable commitment decisions even if the relaxation is not exact
(
Jabr 2006
)
. As a result, SOC AC unit commitment is widely viewed as a principled compromise between the scalability of DC unit commitment and the accuracy of full nonconvex AC unit commitment
(
Tuncer and Kocuk 2022
)
.

Developing commitment schedules that are robust against contingencies has become increasingly important in modern power system operations. Robust network-constrained unit commitment models aim to identify commitment decisions that remain feasible and cost-effective under the worst-case scenario of uncertain parameters
(
Jiang et al. 2011
;
Wang et al. 2025
;
Bertsimas et al. 2012
;
Amjady et al. 2016
)
.
An and Zeng 2014
deployed column-and-constraint generation to solve this problem. More recently, distributionally robust optimization has been introduced to unit commitment formulations, offering protection against ambiguity in probability distributions. Applications span both linear
(
Zhao and Jiang 2017
;
Duan et al. 2017
)
and nonlinear settings
(
Dehghan et al. 2021
)
. More specifically, stochastic unit commitment with DC-based formulations have been extended to incorporate weather-driven contingencies: for example, DC-based formulations that consider probabilistic transmission line disconnections based on weather forecasts
(
Sang et al. 2019
;
Mohammadi and Sahraei-Ardakani 2020
)
. Additionally, data-driven methods have emerged, with machine learning being applied to improve both scenario generation and solution quality
(
Mohammadi et al. 2021
)
. In addition to traditional robust and stochastic formulations, resilience can be integrated into the unit commitment framework to address the challenges posed by extreme weather events.
(
Zhao et al. 2020
;
Yang et al. 2024
)

Security-constrained unit commitment commonly incorporates the
N-1
security criterion, requiring the system to remain feasible following the outage of any single critical component, such as a generator or transmission line
(
Sundar et al. 2019
)
. Extensions to the more general
N-k
criterion, which considers simultaneous outages of up to
k
components, have been studied to address extreme events and correlated failures, but they significantly increase computational complexity due to the combinatorial growth in the number of contingencies
(
Street et al. 2010
;
Bienstock et al. 2014
)
. As a result, practical implementations often rely on screening, contingency selection, or cutting-plane and Benders-type decomposition methods to identify only the most critical contingencies during the solution process.

In this work, we focus on robust decision that provide protection against the failure of transmission lines due to hurricanes, and the number of failure lines is not given, dislike the model for
N-k
criterion. We emphasize that severe weather is the leading reason of power outages in the United States
(
Campbell and Lowry 2012
;
Sang et al. 2018
)
.

1.3
Contributions

Considering the above literature review, the contributions of this paper are threefold:

1)

Within an adaptive robust optimization framework, we model the operation problem (multi-period optimal power flow) as a second-order-conic problem, not as a linear one. Most works reported in the literature use linear formulations.

2)

We model hurricane trajectories (and the subsequent transmission lines disabled) to be incorporated within the proposed adaptive robust framework.

3)

To ease the computational burden of the master problem of the column-and-constraint generation algorithm, we use a novel problem-specific outer-linearization technique, which results in significant computational savings. This is the major contribution of our work. We conduct extensive computational simulations to show its relevance.

Paper organization

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section

2
Formulation of the network-constrained unit commitment problem

2.1
Problem statements and model assumptions

In this section, we detail the formulation of the considered unit commitment problem with the following features:

1.

Flows through lines are represented using relaxed SOC expressions.

2.

Lines can be disabled due to hurricane trajectories and adopt a mixed-integer linear formulation similar to that proposed by
Kocuk et al. 2017
regarding line switching.

3.

Our formulation includes unserved active and reactive power (slack variables), which prevents infeasibility.

On line 4, we detect violating inequalities of that form

On lines 5 and 6, we select the inequalities with the top

On line 7, we add new cuts if they are not
\epsilon_{par}
-parallel. This step prevents adding too many “too parallel” cuts, which can slow down the progress to the solution.

On line 9, extensive computational experiments reveal that

On lines 10 and 11, we adopt an active-set strategy that only considers binding inequalities based on the solution of the inner problem. Such an active-set strategy can reduce the number of added cuts and reduce the solution time on line 15.

6
Conclusions

Considering the proposed robust model for the scheduling of electricity production units, and the computational analysis carried out, the observations below are in order:

1.

The methodology proposed, which guarantees robust security against major weather events and embodies a detailed second-order-conic representation of the power flow equations, effectively identifies optimal schedules that are robust.

2.

The column-and-constraint-generation algorithm used to address the considered min-max-min problem relies on solving moderate size subproblems (continuous and convex) and a master problem (mixed-integer convex) that is effectively solved using an outer-linearization algorithm.

3.

Solving the mixed-integer second-order-conic master problem, which constitutes the bottleneck of the technique proposed, by using a tailored outer approximation technique results in moderate solution times, which are comparable with the timeframe required by unit commitment problems.

4.

A simple example is used to illustrate the methodology proposed, and an case study is used to demonstrated its real-world applicability.

5.

The methodology proposed is novel in two respects: (i) it used second-order-conic convex models, no linear ones as the available literature does, and (ii) it embodied a particularly effective outer approximation technique to solve the master problem of the column-and-constraint-generation algorithm, the bottleneck of most work reported in the literature.
