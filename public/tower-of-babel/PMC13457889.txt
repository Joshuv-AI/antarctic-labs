Making an Impact with the Indian Journal of Critical Care Medicine
Authors: Jigeeshu V Divatia
Published: 2026

The Indian Journal of Critical Care Medicine (IJCCM) has received its Impact Factor (IF) for 2025—it is now 2.0, an improvement over the 2024 IF of 1.5. This IF is the ratio of the number of IJCCM article citations (534) in the year 2025 to the number of citable articles (264) published in the years 2023–2024.

The IF is considered a key performance indicator (KPI) for a scientific journal. It assumes that if the journal publishes high-quality articles, it will receive a greater number of citations. There could be many other quality and performance indicators; however, the IF is the most recognized and visible KPI on which the IJCCM will be rated and compared with other journals. How can we improve the IF even more? The IJCCM must obviously publish high-quality articles that address relevant questions and contribute novel information, data, and knowledge. The only way to get researchers and authors to submit their best works to the IJCCM, rather than to other journals, is to improve the review process and facilitate timely publication of high-quality articles. High-quality articles deal with relevant research questions, conform to ethical and regulatory norms, have a rigorous study design, meticulous study execution, and analysis of the data, and are prepared in accordance with standard reporting guidelines. This is, however, the chicken-and-egg situation. The IJCCM will get higher-quality articles if we have a higher IF; but to get a higher IF, we need to receive and publish higher-quality articles!

Once a manuscript is submitted to the IJCCM, it is assessed for compliance with the instructions and screened by the Editor-in-Chief (EIC) or an Associate Editor. Articles that pass this screening will be sent on for external peer review. Reviewers critically appraise the manuscripts, raise queries, make comments to improve the manuscript, and help the Associate Editors and the EIC decide on the suitability of articles for publication. An article can undergo one or more revisions, with a review of each revised manuscript. If an article is accepted for publication, it goes through a series of steps, including approval of article proofs by the authors. Timely publication requires that all editors, reviewers, and authors stick to the set timelines.

In the last year, the IJCCM has seen some changes. The cover page has a new, dynamic design, with the highlights of each issue printed on the dynamic section. Every original article now comes with a visual abstract. This is not only more appealing but can be used to insert into a slide presentation. I urge our authors and readers to use this feature to include data published in the IJCCM into their presentations. Authors should cite articles published in the IJCCM, especially when they send articles to other journals. We also encourage authors to submit articles in new formats: Succinct Review, Basic Science Note, and Historical Note. All these should contain no more than 1,000 words in the main manuscript, with one table or figure (preferred), and about 10 references.
