Constitutional On-Policy Safe Distillation
Ming Wen 1,2,3,†, Yuxuan Liu 3,4,†, Kun Yang 3,4, Yunhao Feng 3, Zhuoer Xu 3, Yuhao Sun 3, Shiwen Cui 3, Xiang Zheng 5, Guoyu Wang 6, Xingjun Ma 1,2,∗, Yu-Gang Jiang 1,∗

Abstract
On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) has emerged as an efficient post-training paradigm by using a teacher conditioned on privileged information to provide dense token-level supervision. Prior work has shown that OPSD can collapse in verifiable reasoning tasks, but safety alignment differs in that it is guided by high-level constitutions rather than explicit target answers, making it a natural setting to revisit dense distillation. However, our pilot study show that safety OPSD still suffers from severe collapse: constitutional conditioning contracts the teacher distribution toward short and overly conservative responses, and Reverse KL further amplifies this contraction into reduced expressiveness. We formalize this effect as geometric leakage under safety boundaries in a non-orthogonal semantic space, where safety pressure transfers into the expressiveness dimension. Based on this analysis, we propose Constitutional On-Policy Safe Distillation (COPSD) , which first calibrates the teacher through a Cross-SFT cold-start and then performs constitution-conditioned on-policy distillation. Experiments on 12 benchmarks show that COPSD achieves a consistently stronger safety–helpfulness trade-off than baselines while substantially reducing the safety tax on general reasoning ability.

Introduction

On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD)
[
47
,
40
,
16
]
efficiently aligns foundation models by utilizing a temporary teacher augmented with privileged information to generate token-level reinforcement learning rewards. Despite its empirical success, recent studies have identified a severe vulnerability of this framework in reasoning tasks, manifested as abruptly truncated generations and catastrophic distributional collapse
[
20
,
9
]
. This degradation arises because the student exploits superficial correlations between the teacher’s privileged cues and the final output, rather than internalizing the underlying reasoning process
[
39
]
. As a result, the student minimizes the divergence penalty by collapsing onto low-entropy shortcuts that are statistically dominant but logically deficient.

Safety alignment appears to be a particularly promising setting for OPSD for two reasons. First, unlike reasoning tasks, safety tasks typically lack explicit reference answers and instead rely on high-level constitutions or principles
[
5
,
10
,
42
]
. Conditioning the teacher on such principles may encourage the student to internalize genuine safety boundaries, rather than exploit answer-specific shortcuts. Second, mitigating the safety tax
[
14
]
requires a precise balance between harmlessness and helpfulness
[
26
,
38
]
. OPSD’s dense token-level supervision offers a potentially suitable mechanism for providing the fine-grained calibration needed to navigate this trade-off. From this perspective, the safety domain provides a natural setting for examining whether OPSD can deliver efficient alignment without inducing distributional collapse.

To evaluate this hypothesis, we study standard OPSD and three variants on the Qwen3 model family
[
3
]
, including token-level credit assignment and entropy-aware regularization. However, even in the absence of answer-based shortcuts, the student still exhibits severe response truncation and distributional collapse. Our pilot study in Section
3
reveals that this failure does not arise from shortcut recitation: the lexical overlap between privileged information and generated responses remains consistently low across variants. Instead, the collapse originates from the constitution-conditioned teacher itself. Once conditioned on safety constitution, the teacher distribution contracts into a manifold of short, cautious responses, with substantial reductions in entropy, response length, and generative diversity.

To explain this observation more precisely, Section
3.3
models the constitution-conditioned teacher as an Energy-Based distribution composed of an intrinsic linguistic prior and an external safety potential. We analyze the induced update dynamics in a non-orthogonal semantic subspace whose two principal directions correspond to safety and expressiveness.Unlike verifiable domains, where privileged information specifies an explicit target to be reached, safety constitutions primarily define a boundary to avoid.
Because these two directions are geometrically coupled, the gradient leaks into the expressiveness dimension, producing a passive contraction of response length, entropy, and diversity. This perspective explains why safety OPSD collapses even without shortcut copying: the model is not pulled toward a better target response, but pushed away from unsafe regions in a geometry where safety pressure spills over into expressive behavior.

Building on this diagnosis, we propose
COPSD
(Constitutional On-Policy Safe Distillation), a two-stage framework designed to reduce the geometric entanglement between safety and expressiveness. In the first stage, a Cross-SFT Cold-Start calibrates the teacher on a mixed corpus of constitution-guided and expressiveness-preserving trajectories, so as to better anchor the linguistic prior under safety constitution. In the second stage, the recalibrated teacher is integrated into an on-policy distillation pipeline, where it provides dense token-level supervision on the student’s rollouts under constitutional conditioning. Extensive experiments on 12 benchmarks show that COPSD achieves a consistently better safety–helpfulness trade-off than state-of-the-art alignment baselines such as GRPO and OPD, while preserving general capabilities.

In summary, our main contributions are as follows:

•

We show that OPSD fails differently in safety alignment than in verifiable reasoning tasks, with the latter collapsing toward explicit target response patterns and the former collapsing through boundary-induced geometric leakage into expressiveness under a non-orthogonal representation geometry.

•

We propose COPSD, a two-stage framework that uses Cross-SFT Cold-Start to calibrate the teacher and mitigate safety–expressiveness entanglement prior to constitution-conditioned on-policy distillation.

•

Experiments across 12 benchmarks show that COPSD achieves a consistently stronger safety–helpfulness trade-off than competitive baselines, while substantially reducing the safety tax on general capabilities.

Related Work

On-policy distillation.

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR)
[
11
,
48
,
15
]
has emerged as a prominent approach for post-training large language models. Using algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)
[
29
]
and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)
[
31
]
, RLVR significantly enhances reasoning performance in domains such as mathematics and coding. However, RLVR is fundamentally constrained by sparse sequence-level supervision: rewards are typically assigned only to the final response, providing little token-level feedback on exactly where the generation succeeds or fails
[
46
,
12
]
. To address this, recent work explores on-policy distillation
[
23
,
1
,
19
,
21
]
, which trains on the student’s own rollouts while replacing sparse outcome rewards with dense, token-level supervision from a teacher. A notable variant is on-policy self-distillation (OPSD)
[
40
,
47
,
16
]
, where the same model acts as both teacher and student under varying contexts, improving token efficiency without relying on an external teacher. Nevertheless, recent analyses
[
20
]
indicate that self-distillation with a richer teacher context can inadvertently suppress epistemic verbalization, encouraging overly compressed and overconfident reasoning that degrades out-of-distribution generalization. Furthermore, existing OPSD frameworks have predominantly been explored in text-only reasoning tasks, leaving a critical gap in multimodal grounding.

Safety Alignment

Safety alignment ensures that models adhere to human values, with SFT, ICL, and RLHF being the most prominent paradigms
[
6
,
35
,
28
,
8
]
. These approaches share a fundamental limitation: they provide only sequence-level sparse supervision that cannot distinguish which tokens contribute to safety versus helpfulness, forcing the model to navigate this trade-off at a coarse granularity and leading to over-refusal or residual unsafety
[
37
,
36
,
4
]
. Constitutional approaches attempt to provide denser guidance by conditioning a teacher on safety principles
[
5
]
, yet this exposes a deeper issue: the safety and helpfulness objectives are geometrically coupled in the representation space, causing the conditioned teacher to collapse onto low-diversity, over-cautious outputs. Existing paradigms thus face a dilemma: they either lack token-level precision or trigger distributional collapse when attempting to provide it
[
39
]
. COPSD resolves this dilemma by first decoupling the competing objectives via Cross-SFT, then performing on-policy distillation on the recalibrated teacher.

The standard paradigm,
OPSD (Golden Answer)

[

, uses the ground-truth response

as privileged information. It aligns the student policy
P_{S}
to a conditioned teacher

by minimizing the token-level reverse KL divergence along the student’s on-policy rollouts:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{Gold}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{x,r_{\text{gold}}}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim P_{S}(\cdot\mid x)}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{|y|}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(P_{S}(\cdot\mid x,y_{<t})\parallel P_{T}(\cdot\mid x,r_{\text{gold}},y_{<t})\big)\right].

(2)

Entropy-Aware OPSD

[
19
]
augments constitution-conditioned OPSD with a mean-seeking regularizer to mitigate the collapse tendency of reverse KL. It applies a forward KL term only when the teacher entropy
\mathcal{H}(P_{T})
exceeds a diversity threshold
\tau

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{EOPD}}(\theta)=\mathcal{L}_{C\text{-}\mathrm{safe}}(\theta)+\alpha\,\mathbb{E}_{y\sim P_{S}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{|y|}\mathbb{1}_{\{\mathcal{H}(P_{T})>\tau\}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(P_{T}(\cdot\mid x,c_{\text{safe}},y_{<t})\parallel P_{S}(\cdot\mid x,y_{<t})\big)\right].

(3)

Self-Distilled RL with Verified Rewards (RLVR)

[
39
]
leverages the teacher’s dense signal for token-level credit assignment. Defining the discrepancy as
\Delta_{t}=\log P_{T}(y_{t}\mid x,y_{<t})-\log P_{S}(y_{t}\mid x,y_{<t})
, it modulates the sequence-level advantage
A
via a direction-aware exponential weight to derive the clipped token-level advantage
\hat{A}_{t}
:

\hat{A}_{t}=A\cdot\mathrm{clip}\Big(\exp\big(\mathrm{sign}(A)\cdot\Delta_{t}\big),\,1-\epsilon_{w},1+\epsilon_{w}\Big).

(4)

The policy is then optimized using a standard clipped surrogate objective:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RLVR}}(\theta)=-\mathbb{E}_{y\sim P_{S}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{|y|}\min\Big(\rho_{t}\hat{A}_{t},\,\mathrm{clip}(\rho_{t},1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\hat{A}_{t}\Big)\right],

(5)

where
\rho_{t}
is the importance sampling ratio.

3.2
Empirical Diagnosis: Entanglement within the Linguistic Prior

To understand these failures, we examine how the constitution-conditioned teacher interacts with the choice of KL divergence.

As shown in Figure
2
, constitutional conditioning shifts the teacher toward shorter, less diverse, and more refusal-like outputs. Standard OPSD then amplifies this contraction through Reverse KL (Eq.
1
), which strongly penalizes tokens outside the teacher’s support. As a result, the student is pushed toward a compressed short-and-safe pattern, leading to shorter answers, less explanation, and lower helpfulness. This suggests that safety pressure does not stay confined to refusal behavior, but also spills over into the model’s expressiveness.

Entropy-Aware OPSD tries to counter this effect by adding a Forward KL term in high-entropy regions. This helps preserve diversity, but it also weakens the sharp exclusion effect needed for robust safety control. As a result, the model remains more expressive, yet fails to enforce strong constitutional constraints. Section
3.3
formalizes this asymmetric trade-off as geometric leakage from safety into expressiveness.

3.3
Theoretical Diagnosis: Geometric Leakage under Safety Boundaries

To formalize the collapse observed in Section
3.2
, we model the constitution-conditioned teacher as an energy-based distribution over a continuous semantic representation space
[
7
,
2
]
.

Definition 1

(Energy-Based Teacher Distribution in Continuous Representation Space)
.

Given an input
x
and auxiliary cues
c
, let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote a continuous semantic representation of a generated response in a local latent space. We model the teacher distribution
P_{T}(y\mid x,c)
as

P_{T}(y\mid x,c)=\frac{1}{Z}\exp\!\left(-\left[\alpha E_{base}(y\mid x)+\beta U_{cues}(y\mid x,c)\right]\right),

(6)

where
E_{base}
denotes the intrinsic linguistic energy,
U_{cues}
is a cue-dependent task potential,
Z
is the normalizing constant, and
\alpha,\beta>0
control their relative strength.

Under this model, the reverse-KL objective
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{\theta}\parallel P_{T})
can be written as

\mathcal{J}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim P_{\theta}}\left[\alpha E_{base}(y\mid x)+\beta U_{cues}(y\mid x,c)\right]-\mathbb{H}(P_{\theta})+\log Z,

(7)

where
\log Z
is constant with respect to
\theta
.

Under a first-order approximation, the update can be viewed as following the negative gradient of the total energy

E_{total}(y\mid x,c)=\alpha E_{base}(y\mid x)+\beta U_{cues}(y\mid x,c),

(8)

so that

\Delta y\propto-\nabla_{y}E_{total}(y\mid x,c).

(9)

We project the local representation manifold onto a two-dimensional non-orthogonal semantic subspace. This subspace is spanned by an
Expressiveness
direction
\mathbf{e}_{H}
, associated with response length and information density, and a
Task Constraint
direction
\mathbf{e}_{S}
, associated with alignment pressure. The local geometry is described by the metric tensor

G_{ij}=\langle\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{j}\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}1&\rho\\
\rho&1\end{pmatrix},

(10)

where
\rho\in(-1,1)
captures the empirical coupling between expressiveness and task constraint.

Theorem 1

(Coupled Expressiveness Drift)
.

Under the local metric
G
, the steepest-descent direction in representation space is given by the natural gradient

v=\frac{dy}{dt}=-G^{-1}\nabla_{y}E_{total}(y).

(11)

Writing the two coordinates as
(y^{H},y^{S})
, the corresponding drift components satisfy

v^{H}=-\frac{1}{1-\rho^{2}}\left[\left(\alpha\frac{\partial E_{base}}{\partial y^{H}}+\beta\frac{\partial U_{cues}}{\partial y^{H}}\right)-\rho\left(\alpha\frac{\partial E_{base}}{\partial y^{S}}+\beta\frac{\partial U_{cues}}{\partial y^{S}}\right)\right],

(12)

v^{S}=-\frac{1}{1-\rho^{2}}\left[\left(\alpha\frac{\partial E_{base}}{\partial y^{S}}+\beta\frac{\partial U_{cues}}{\partial y^{S}}\right)-\rho\left(\alpha\frac{\partial E_{base}}{\partial y^{H}}+\beta\frac{\partial U_{cues}}{\partial y^{H}}\right)\right].

(13)

Remark 1

(Geometric Perspective of the Safety Tax)
.

In an ideal alignment regime, the update should reduce safety violations without shrinking expressiveness, i.e.,
v^{S}<0
while
v^{H}\geq 0
. However,
v^{H}<0
can arise even when the primary task pressure is applied along the safety axis. This happens because the update contains not only a direct term
\partial U_{cues}/\partial y^{H}
, but also an off-diagonal leakage term

-\frac{\rho}{1-\rho^{2}}\frac{\partial U_{cues}}{\partial y^{S}},

through which safety pressure spills into the expressiveness direction when
\rho>0
.

Assumption 1

(Task-Gradient Domination)
.

During alignment, the task-induced gradients are much steeper than the background linguistic prior. We therefore assume

\|\beta\nabla_{y}U_{cues}\|\gg\|\alpha\nabla_{y}E_{base}\|,

(14)

so that the leading-order behavior is dominated by the cue-dependent potential.

We now specify the task potential
U_{cues}
for safety and verifiable settings.

Definition 2

(Safety Task Potential)
.

In safety alignment, the external cues define a one-sided boundary along the safety axis, optionally combined with a softer penalty on the expressiveness axis. We model this as

U_{cues}^{safe}(y)=\frac{\lambda}{2}\left(\max(0,y^{S}-y^{S}_{limit})\right)^{2}+\gamma\Omega(y^{H}),

(15)

where
\lambda>0
controls the barrier strength and
\gamma>0
controls the direct penalty on expressiveness. The resulting gradients are

\frac{\partial U_{cues}^{safe}}{\partial y^{S}}=\lambda\,\mathrm{ReLU}(y^{S}-y^{S}_{limit}),\qquad\frac{\partial U_{cues}^{safe}}{\partial y^{H}}=\gamma\omega(y^{H}),

(16)

where
\omega(y^{H})=\partial\Omega(y^{H})/\partial y^{H}
.

Corollary 1

(Metric-Induced Passive Collapse in Safety)
.

Under Assumption
1
, suppose the boundary term dominates the direct expressiveness penalty, i.e.,
\lambda\gg\gamma
. Then the drift along the expressiveness axis is approximated by

v^{H}_{safe}\approx-\frac{\rho\beta\lambda}{1-\rho^{2}}\mathrm{ReLU}(y^{S}-y^{S}_{limit}).

(17)

Thus, the contraction of expressiveness in safety alignment is driven primarily by a geometric leakage effect: the repulsive force from the safety boundary transfers into the expressiveness axis through the non-orthogonal coupling
\rho>0
.

By contrast, verifiable tasks define an explicit target to approach rather than a boundary to avoid.

Definition 3

(Verifiable Task Potential)
.

For verifiable tasks, the privileged cues specify an optimal target footprint
y^{*}=(L^{*},S^{*})
. We model this target attractor using the Mahalanobis distance induced by
G
:

U_{cues}^{ver}(y)=\frac{\gamma}{2}(y-y^{*})^{T}G(y-y^{*})=\frac{\gamma}{2}\left[(y^{H}-L^{*})^{2}+(y^{S}-S^{*})^{2}+2\rho(y^{H}-L^{*})(y^{S}-S^{*})\right],

(18)

where
\gamma>0
controls the attraction strength. In particular,

\frac{\partial U_{cues}^{ver}}{\partial y^{H}}=\gamma(y^{H}-L^{*})+\gamma\rho(y^{S}-S^{*}).

(19)

Corollary 2

(Active Target Collapse in Verifiable Tasks)
.

Under Assumption
1
, the metric inverse
G^{-1}
cancels the cross-coupling induced by the Mahalanobis form, yielding

v^{H}_{ver}\approx-\beta\gamma(y^{H}-L^{*}).

(20)

Unlike the safety case, the contraction here is target-driven. The explicit attractor pulls the representation directly toward the desired expressiveness footprint
L^{*}
, rather than indirectly shrinking expressiveness through boundary pressure.

Taken together, these results explain why safety OPSD and verifiable OPSD fail differently. In verifiable tasks, collapse is driven by attraction toward an explicit target footprint. In safety alignment, by contrast, collapse emerges because a sharp safety boundary acts on a non-orthogonal geometry, causing safety pressure to leak into the expressiveness dimension.

Constitutional On-Policy Safe Distillation

Figure 5

Section
3
shows that safety OPSD fails because that constitutional conditioning pushes the teacher toward overly short and conservative responses, which are further amplified by Reverse KL. This motivates
Constitutional On-Policy Safe Distillation (COPSD)
, a two-stage framework that recalibrates the teacher through Cross-SFT and then performs constitution-conditioned on-policy distillation.

4.1
Cross-SFT Cold-Start

The Cross-SFT cold-start is a teacher SFT stage before on-policy distillation. Its goal is to build a constitution-conditioned teacher that improves safety without collapsing the base model’s original response style. Specifically, we construct the SFT data from two sources.

The first source keeps the original queries and original responses, while adding the safety constitution
c_{safe}
, so that the teacher learns to preserve the base model’s expressiveness under constitutional conditioning. The second source is built from constitution-conditioned responses generated by frontier models. After filtering with LLM-Judges, these responses are rewritten by the base model and sampled to match the original response-length distribution. This second source strengthens safety supervision, while the first anchors the teacher to the base model’s native response profile. Together, they produce a constitution-conditioned teacher that is safer while less prone to becoming uniformly short or over-cautious, providing a better initialization for the subsequent on-policy distillation stage.

Following manifold decoupling, the recalibrated teacher is integrated into the On-Policy Distillation (OPD) framework. The teacher evaluates the student’s on-policy rollouts while conditioned on both the original query
x
and the safety constitution
c_{safe}
.

Formally, we define the token-level distillation signal as the log-probability difference between the student policy and the constitution-conditioned teacher:

r_{t}^{(i,t)}=\log P_{S}(y_{t}^{(i)}\mid x,y_{<t}^{(i)})-\log P_{T}(y_{t}^{(i)}\mid x,c_{safe},y_{<t}^{(i)}).

(21)

Based on this signal, we consider two variants for advantage estimation, depending on whether an external outcome-level reward model is available.

Standard Token-Level Variant.

Without a sequence-level reward model, we rely entirely on dense distillation signals. For a sampled group of
G
trajectories, we first compute the return-to-go

R_{t}^{(i)}=\sum_{k=t}^{|y^{(i)}|}\gamma^{k-t}r_{k}^{(i,k)}
.
We then compute the group mean
\mu_{t}
and standard deviation
\sigma_{t}
at each time step, and obtain the normalized token-level advantage

A_{\text{std}}^{(i,t)}=\frac{R_{t}^{(i)}-\mu_{t}}{\sigma_{t}},

(22)

which provides relative token-level feedback with reduced variance.

Hybrid Outcome-Weighted Variant.

When an outcome-level reward model is available, we combine the dense token-level signal with a sequence-level reward. Let
R_{o}^{(i)}
denote the sequence-level outcome reward,
\hat{R}_{o}^{(i)}
its group-normalized advantage, and
\hat{r}_{t}^{(i,t)}
the normalized token-level distillation signal. We define

A_{\text{hyb}}^{(i,t)}=\hat{R}_{o}^{(i)}\cdot\left(1+\lambda\cdot\operatorname{sgn}(\hat{R}_{o}^{(i)})\cdot\hat{r}_{t}^{(i,t)}\right),

(23)

where
\lambda
controls the strength of the token-level correction. In this variant, the outcome-level reward enforces global safety preferences, while the token-level signal refines the local credit assignment along the sampled trajectory. Given these token-level advantages, we then follow the standard GRPO training pipeline, with a KL penalty to control excessive drift from the reference model.

Safety Performance and the Safety Tax.

As shown in Table
2
, COPSD variants consistently achieve a superior safety-utility balance. While reinforcement learning baselines like GRPO and Safe-RLHF-V demonstrate competitive safety gains, they incur a severe
safety tax
—improving safety at the cost of a drastic drop in helpfulness (e.g., Safe-RLHF-V’s helpfulness on BeaverTails falls to
25.43\%
). In contrast, COPSD-Hybrid maintains robust effectiveness while reaching a higher safety frontier. Furthermore, SFT and OPD exhibit a rigid dependency on the teacher’s inherent limits, struggling to surpass the teacher’s safety boundaries in situational benchmarks like SIUO and SPA-VL.

Reasoning-Centric Alignment Tax.

Table
3
indicates that safety alignment primarily affects reasoning-intensive benchmarks (
e.g.
, MathVista, LLaVA-Wilder), while simple VQA tasks (ScienceQA, GQA) remain largely unaffected. This suggests that safety constraints and complex reasoning trajectories are often non-orthogonal in the latent space, where rigid alignment collapses the diversity required for deduction. COPSD effectively mitigates this degradation by decoupling safety manifolds from the model’s generative footprint. For instance, on MathVista, COPSD limits the reasoning loss to a marginal
2.1\%
, whereas GRPO suffers a catastrophic
17.5\%
drop.

A key advantage of COPSD is its ability to surpass the performance of its own supervisor. Specifically, the COPSD teacher (

Impact of Teacher SFT Epochs.

We first investigate the sensitivity of COPSD to the SFT training duration of the teacher model. As illustrated in Figure

Importance of Self-Generated Data Distribution.

We further examine the role of data composition by removing self-generated trajectories from the SFT (

alignment tax
: without the inclusion of self-generated distributions, the model tends to over-fit to external safety patterns that may be geometrically misaligned with its internal reasoning manifolds. This optimization conflict forces the model to sacrifice general reasoning utility to meet safety constraints, underscoring the necessity of self-generated data in maintaining a coherent generative footprint during alignment.

These observations are consistent with the different optimization behaviors of the two baselines: GRPO tends to contract the output distribution more aggressively, whereas OPSD more directly amplifies short-response behavior under constitution-conditioned reverse-KL matching.

In contrast, COPSD stabilizes quickly after a brief initial transition. Once aligned, COPSD maintains a consistently higher entropy and more stable response length than both baselines, confirming its capacity to sustain safety without sacrificing expressiveness. 6
Conclusion

This paper studies on-policy self-distillation in safety alignment and identifies a distinct collapse mechanism from that in verifiable tasks. We show that safety OPSD collapses because constitutional conditioning contracts the teacher distribution toward short and overly conservative responses, and Reverse KL amplifies this contraction into reduced expressiveness. We formalize this as geometric leakage under safety boundaries and propose COPSD, a two-stage framework that combines a Cross-SFT cold-start with constitution-conditioned on-policy distillation. Experiments demonstrate that COPSD delivers a superior safety–helpfulness trade-off while preserving reasoning capabilities, highlighting the necessity of calibrating teacher distributions for stable and effective safe distillation.

Training Details

8.1
SFT Data Curation

The SFT data follows the same overall composition as the training corpus described in the main text, including safety-oriented samples from BeaverTails-V, SPA-VL, and MM-RLHF Safety, general-domain samples from MMPR and MM-RLHF General, and a small set of over-refusal queries.

For general-domain data, including MMPR and MM-RLHF General, we use the policy model to generate responses under the default constitution policy. This branch is used to preserve the base model’s native response style under constitutional conditioning. For safety-related data, we query multiple frontier models, including
Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct
,
GPT-5.4
, and
Gemini 3.1
, with the corresponding constitutional prompts. Their responses are then compared by an LLM judge in a pairwise manner, and the selected responses are required to be both safer and more effective. For over-refusal data, we leverage the multimodal scenario taxonomy and pipeline from CSR-Bench
[
22
]
to construct paired descriptions of text queries and corresponding image contents. Specifically, for each pair, the text query is designed to appear risky when read in isolation, while the paired image description specifies visual content that, when combined with the text query, renders the full input clearly benign under cross-modal understanding. We then generate the corresponding images from these image descriptions using the
Seedream 4.5
text-to-image model, followed by manual quality inspection to ensure that each image-text pair is genuinely safe under joint interpretation while remaining deceptive in the text-only modality.

The selected frontier responses are subsequently rewritten by the policy model to better match the policy model’s own style. We further resample these rewritten responses to approximately match the original response-length distribution of the policy model on the same queries. This procedure produces a mixed SFT corpus that strengthens safety supervision while preserving expressive characteristics.

8.2
RL Training Details

We implement RL training with the VERL framework. For both Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, we train for 3 epochs with a global batch size of 128. The maximum prompt length and maximum response length are both set to 2048. The actor learning rate is set to
2\times 10^{-6}
, the PPO mini-batch size is 32, and the micro-batch size per GPU is 8. Rollouts are generated with
vLLM
using stochastic sampling, with 16 rollouts per prompt.

For the standard COPSD variant, token-level rewards are computed from the on-policy log-probability discrepancy between the student and the constitution-conditioned teacher. For the hybrid variant, we combine the token-level signal with an outcome-level reward as in Eq.
23
, and set
\lambda=0.3
. All other hyperparameters remain unchanged.

8.3
Baseline Training Details

We train OPD, GRPO, and Safe-RLHF-V under the same basic optimization setup as COPSD whenever applicable. All baselines are trained for 3 epochs with a global batch size of 128, maximum prompt and response lengths of 2048, PPO mini-batch size 32, micro-batch size 8 per GPU, actor learning rate
2\times 10^{-6}
, and KL loss coefficient 0.005 with the low-variance KL formulation. We use stochastic rollout generation with 16 samples per prompt and disable explicit entropy regularization.
For GRPO, we use the standard
grpo
advantage estimator and replace the token-level distillation reward with an external reward model. The reward model is trained based on
[
36
]
, a multi-modal reward model that provides both helpfulness and safety scores.
Safe-RLHF-V is trained with the same reward-model framework as GRPO, except that we enable the self-RLHF-V mode by setting
is_self_rlhfv=True
. To ensure a fair comparison, we perform a small grid search over the safety trade-off coefficient
lambda_value
in
\{1,10,20\}
and report the best-performing configuration. The remaining reward-model hyperparameters are identical to those used in GRPO.
