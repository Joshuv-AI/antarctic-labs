Three-Year Efficacy and Safety of Lebrikizumab in Patients with Moderate-to-Severe Atopic Dermatitis: A Long-Term Extension (ADjoin)
Authors: Emma Guttman-Yassky, Alan D Irvine, Melinda Gooderham, Stephan Weidinger, Lynda Spelman, Jonathan I Silverberg, Heidi Crane, Hany ElMaraghy, Louise DeLuca-Carter, Maria Lucia Buziqui Piruzeli, Georgia Martimianaki, Evangeline Pierce, Helena Agell, Diamant Thaçi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionWe report efficacy and safety of lebrikizumab up to 152 weeks (W) of continuous treatment with/without topical corticosteroids in the ADjoin long-term extension study.MethodsADvocate1&2 W16 lebrikizumab responders (Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI) 75 or Investigator’s Global Assessment (IGA) 0/1 without rescue) were re-randomized 2:2:1 lebrikizumab (LEB) 250 mg Q2W, Q4W, or placebo (lebrikizumab withdrawal, not presented herein) to the 36W maintenance period before entering ADjoin. ADhere W16 lebrikizumab responders were re-randomized 2:1 LEBQ2W/Q4W upon entering ADjoin. Data are reported as observed for W16 lebrikizumab responders from ADvocate1&2 (N = 181) and ADhere (N = 86) who received treatment up to ADjoin W100 (152W/116W for ADvocate1&2/ADhere). The Supplement reports additional populations.ResultsIn participants who achieved IGA 0/1 at W16, 84.0% (Q4W) and 82.9% (Q2W) from ADvocate1&2 maintained IGA 0/1 at W152; 91.7% and 86.7% from ADhere maintained response at W116. Among participants achieving EASI 75 at W16, 94.1% (Q4W) and 90.5% (Q2W) from ADvocate1&2 maintained EASI 75 at W152; 90.9% and 94.9% from ADhere maintained EASI 75 at W116. Most adverse events (AEs) were mild (29.2%) or moderate (33.3%) in severity; 3.7% reported serious AEs; 2.6% reported AEs leading to treatment discontinuation.ConclusionLebrikizumab maintained efficacy up to 3 years of continuous treatment in LEB 250 mg Q2W and Q4W W16 lebrikizumab per-protocol responders. The safety profile was consistent with previous studies.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov identifier, NCT04392154.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s13555-026-01810-1.Keywords: Atopic dermatitis, Lebrikizumab, Efficacy, Safety, Long-term

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Atopic dermatitis (AD) is a chronic, relapsing inflammatory disease associated with substantial symptom burden and impaired quality of life.Sustained improvement and long-term disease control are critical treatment goals to reduce disease burden and improve patients’ quality of life.Lebrikizumab has demonstrated efficacy and safety for up to 2 years of continuous treatment in patients with moderate-to-severe AD; this study evaluates outcomes through 3 years of treatment. What was learned from this study? In patients with moderate-to-severe AD who achieved protocol-defined response at week 16, lebrikizumab treatment resulted in durable improvement in signs and symptoms through 3 years, with a safety profile consistent with previously reported data.These long-term data demonstrate that lebrikizumab supports long-term disease control and may help to inform clinical practice in this chronic and relapsing disease.

Introduction

Atopic dermatitis (AD) is a chronic inflammatory, relapsing skin disease occurring in 2% to 7% of adults globally [1–4]. Symptoms include skin pain and intense itching, which contribute to the high disease burden and disability associated with AD [5–9]. Achieving a durable response that is maintained long-term is a key treatment goal to improve patient quality of life.

Current treatments for AD include topical therapies such as emollients, topical corticosteroids (TCS), topical calcineurin inhibitors (TCI), phosphodiesterase 4 inhibitors, tapinarof (an aryl hydrocarbon receptor agonist), and Janus kinase inhibitors (JAKi) [10–12]. Advanced systemic therapy is recommended as monotherapy or in combination with topical treatments for moderate-to-severe AD, with conditional recommendation for phototherapy and immunosuppressants [10]. Included in the most recent American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) (2023) and European Guideline (2025) treatment guidelines for moderate-to-severe AD are oral JAKi and biologic therapies, such as dupilumab and tralokinumab [10, 13–15]. Lebrikizumab was included in the European Guideline (EuroGuiDerm) (2025) and the AAD Focused Update (2025) [16–18]. While current biologics provide robust efficacy and positive benefit–risk profile, AD remains uncontrolled in many patients who do not achieve sustained long-term clinical benefit [13, 19].

Lebrikizumab is a novel monoclonal antibody that binds to interleukin (IL)-13 with high affinity and slow off-rate, thereby blocking the downstream effects of IL-13 with high potency [15]. Lebrikizumab was recently approved in the USA, European Union, UK, Japan, and several other countries for the treatment of moderate-to-severe AD in adults and adolescents 12 years and older with a body weight of at least 40 kg who are candidates for systemic therapy [20].

Long-term efficacy and safety of lebrikizumab in patients with moderate-to-severe AD were established with up to 2 years of continuous treatment [20]. Here, we describe the results of up to 3 years of continuous treatment with lebrikizumab for patients with moderate-to-severe AD from three originating phase 3 studies (ADvocate1, ADvocate2, and ADhere), focusing on long-term maintenance of response in participants who achieved response (either IGA 0/1 or EASI 75 separately) at W16 in their parent studies. This manuscript also reports data from (1) ADjoin Overall, which includes all participants in ADjoin: from parent studies ADvocate1 (NCT04146363), ADvocate2 (NCT04178967), ADhere (NCT04250337), ADore (a 52-week open-label adolescent safety study, NCT04250350), and ADopt-VA (a 16-week vaccine study, NCT04626297), as well as participants who entered ADjoin directly without having completed a parent study; and (2) participants who achieved per-protocol response at W16 (IGA 0/1 or EASI 75 combined) from ADvocate1, ADvocate2, and ADhere.

The aim of this analysis is to describe 3-year outcomes of continuous lebrikizumab treatment in patients with moderate-to-severe AD from the ADvocate1, ADvocate2, and ADhere studies, focusing on long-term maintenance of response among patients who achieved week 16 response, with separate presentations of W16 IGA 0/1 responders and W16 EASI 75 responders.

Methods

Study Design

ADjoin is a 100-week phase 3 long-term extension study evaluating the long-term safety and efficacy of lebrikizumab in patients with moderate-to-severe AD. Participants were eligible to enroll in ADjoin following completion of specific studies in the lebrikizumab program, including ADvocate1, ADvocate2, and ADhere (Table S1). The study design of these parent studies and the subsequent entry of participants into the long-term extension study ADjoin was described previously [21–23].

Briefly, ADvocate1 and ADvocate2 were identically designed 52-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, phase 3 trials that evaluated the efficacy and safety of lebrikizumab monotherapy in adult and adolescent participants with moderate-to-severe AD. Each trial had a 16-week induction period followed by a 36-week maintenance period. Participants were randomized 2:1 at baseline to receive lebrikizumab 250 mg (with a loading dose of 500 mg administered at baseline and week 2) or placebo every 2 weeks (Q2W). Participants who achieved the per-protocol response of Investigator’s Global Assessment 0 or 1 (IGA 0/1) with 2-point improvement from baseline or a 75% reduction from baseline in the Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI 75) without any rescue medication use (including TCS/TCI) through the 16-week induction period were re-randomized 2:2:1 to receive lebrikizumab 250 mg Q2W, lebrikizumab 250 mg every 4 weeks (Q4W), or placebo Q2W (lebrikizumab withdrawal, not presented herein) for the 36-week double-blind maintenance period. Use of topical or systemic treatments for AD was not permitted during the 16-week induction period. During the 36-week maintenance period, participants were allowed to apply rescue TCS and TCI. Participants who completed study treatments and the last treatment visit (week 52) could enroll in ADjoin. Use of topical rescue therapy during the maintenance period was permitted per protocol and did not constitute treatment failure; in contrast, during the induction period, rescue medication use was not permitted for response classification. Participants who were re-randomized to lebrikizumab 250 mg Q2W or Q4W in the maintenance phase and remained on that dose at W52 of ADvocate1 and ADvocate2 continued this dose regimen in ADjoin.

ADhere was a multicenter, double-blinded, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, 16-week, phase 3 randomized clinical trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of lebrikizumab in combination with low-to-mid-potency TCS in participants with moderate-to-severe AD. Participants were randomized 2:1 at baseline to receive either lebrikizumab 250 mg Q2W (with a loading dose of 500 mg administered at baseline and week 2) or placebo Q2W. All participants were instructed at baseline to use low-to-mid-potency TCS for AD symptoms. High potency TCS or systemic treatments were permitted for participants experiencing intolerable clinical worsening of AD symptoms and were considered rescue therapy. Participants receiving systemic rescue treatment were discontinued from the study drug. Participants who completed last treatment visit (week 16) of ADhere were eligible to enroll in ADjoin. Lebrikizumab responders at ADhere W16 (participants who achieved IGA 0/1 with 2-point improvement from baseline or EASI 75 without rescue medication use) who received lebrikizumab 250 mg Q2W were randomized 2:1 to receive lebrikizumab 250 mg Q2W or Q4W in ADjoin.

Treatment regimen assignment remained blinded during the long-term extension study until unblinding in 2022 to support regulatory submission. Topical and short-term systemic rescue medications were allowed in ADjoin. Participants requiring long-term systemic treatment (> 30 days) for symptoms of AD (non-responders) were discontinued from ADjoin. Participants not achieving EASI 50 by W16 of ADjoin, maintaining an EASI 50 response, or not achieving clinical benefit based on principal investigator discretion were also terminated from the study.

Participant Population

Participants eligible for enrolment in the parent studies ADvocate1, ADvocate2, and ADhere were adults and adolescents aged at least 12 years old and weighing at least 40 kg, with moderate-to-severe chronic AD, and a history of inadequate response to topical therapies. Participants were required to have an EASI ≥ 16, IGA score ≥ 3 , and ≥ 10% body surface area involvement at baseline. Topical and short-term systemic rescue medications were permitted in ADjoin. Participants who required long-term systemic treatment of symptoms of AD (non-responders) were discontinued from the study. The population of interest in this analysis are participants who achieved per-protocol response to lebrikizumab at week 16 of ADvocate1, ADvocate2, or ADhere and received continuous treatment with lebrikizumab into ADjoin. Results for W16 IGA 0/1 responders are presented separately from W16 EASI 75 responders. The Supplement includes results from the ADjoin Overall population and combined data for W16 IGA 0/1 and W16 EASI 75 responders from ADvocate1&2 and ADhere (Table S2).

The ADvocate1, ADvocate2, ADhere, ADjoin, ADore, and ADopt-VA clinical trials were undertaken in accordance with ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences, and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. All investigation sites received approval from the appropriate authorized institutional review board or ethics committee. Informed consent was obtained from all participants before study procedures were initiated. For participants considered to be minors, written consent of the parent or legal guardian, as well as the assent of the minor, was obtained. Intent-to-treat (ITT) population refers to all patients who were assigned to a treatment, regardless of whether they received the assigned treatment, received the correct treatment, or adhered to the study protocol. Analysis was conducted based on the treatment group to which each patient was originally assigned. Following the findings of directed site audits, data from participants at two sites were excluded from analyses, resulting in the creation of a modified intent-to-treat (mITT) population and a modified safety population, data from which were used for the efficacy and safety analyses of the ADjoin Overall population.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint of ADjoin was the proportion of participants discontinued from study treatment due to adverse events (AEs). Secondary endpoints included the proportion of participants with a response of IGA 0/1, and the proportion of participants achieving response of EASI 75 from baseline of the parent study.

In this analysis of responder participants, efficacy was measured by maintenance of IGA 0/1 response (in lebrikizumab participants who achieved IGA 0/1 at W16 of parent study) and maintenance of EASI 75 response (in lebrikizumab participants who achieved EASI 75 at W16 of parent study). Secondary efficacy outcomes included the achievement of EASI 90 (in W16 EASI 75 lebrikizumab responders) and maintenance of patient symptoms as reported in Patient Oriented Eczema Measure (POEM) (in lebrikizumab responders achieving EASI 75 or IGA 0/1 at W16 of parent study). POEM is reported in the absence of Pruritus Numeric Rating Scale data up to 3 years to include patient-reported outcome data in addition to clinician-reported data. Safety was assessed by monitoring vital signs, AEs, concomitant medication use, and laboratory testing. AEs of special interest included conjunctivitis, reported as conjunctivitis cluster containing MedDRA preferred terms of conjunctivitis, conjunctivitis allergic, conjunctivitis bacterial, conjunctivitis viral, and giant papillary conjunctivitis; herpes infection or herpes zoster; and parasitic infection.

Statistical Analyses

The responder populations used to assess the outcome measures (mentioned above) were part of the analysis plan before the database lock and are consistent with the populations previously published [24]. This analysis includes (1) participants who achieved IGA 0/1 at week 16 of ADvocate1, ADvocate2 and ADhere and received continuous lebrikizumab treatment into ADjoin; and (2) participants who achieved EASI 75 at week 16 of ADvocate1, ADvocate2, and ADhere and received continuous lebrikizumab treatment into ADjoin. In the Supplement we also report participants who achieved per-protocol response (IGA 0/1 or EASI 75 combined) at week 16 of (1) ADvocate1 and ADvocate2 combined and (2) ADhere (pre-specified population, see Supplement Figs. S2–S4).

Efficacy outcomes for participants entering ADjoin from ADvocate1&2 were assessed during the ADvocate1&2 maintenance period (weeks 16 to 52) and then for 100 weeks in ADjoin (weeks 52 to 152). For participants entering ADjoin from ADhere, efficacy outcomes were assessed for 100 weeks in ADjoin (weeks 16 to 116). The study design for ADvocate1&2 and ADhere studies was previously described [21–23]. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize demographics and disease characteristics, efficacy and safety results. For categorical variables, the number and percentage of participants in each category were presented. For continuous variables, descriptive statistics, the number of participants, mean, standard deviation (SD), median, minimum, and maximum were presented. To handle missing efficacy data in ADjoin (weeks 52 to 152 for ADvocate1&2 W16 lebrikizumab responders and weeks 16 to 116 for ADhere W16 lebrikizumab responders), the following two methods were used: (1) “As observed analysis”, that is, only observed data (data from completers) at each post-baseline visit were relevant; and (2) non-responder imputation/multiple imputation (NRI/MI) per-protocol analysis. Participants who discontinued treatment due to lack of efficacy during ADjoin had values set to their baseline value subsequent to this time through week 100, and MI was used to handle the remaining missing data. Participants who discontinued treatment for any other reasons had values set to missing subsequent to this time through to ADjoin week 100 and MI was used to handle the missing data. For each imputation process, 25 datasets with imputations were calculated using Rubin’s rules. Data are reported up to the data cutoff 25 February 2025. Last observation carried forward (LOCF) was used in supportive estimand of POEM endpoint, imputing all missing values after treatment discontinuation.

Results

ADvocate1&2 and ADhere W16 lebrikizumab responder populations (hereafter referred to as ADvocate RES and ADhere RES, where RES indicates W16 responders). The ADjoin Overall results are presented in the Supplement and include all participants in the ADjoin study: participants entering from parent studies ADvocate1&2, ADhere, ADore, ADopt-VA, and participants entering ADjoin directly.

Participants

Baseline demographics and disease characteristics of W16 lebrikizumab responders entering ADjoin from ADvocate1, ADvocate2, and ADhere studies were well balanced among treatment groups (Table 1). The mean (SD) age of Q4W participants was 35.8 (17.2) years and 35.5 (16.2) years for Q2W participants from ADvocate1&2, with 14.1% and 13.4% of the population being adolescents, respectively. In ADhere, the mean (SD) age was 29.8 (15.9) years for Q4W participants and 37.0 (19.9) years for Q2W participants; 34.5% and 26.3% of the population were adolescents, respectively. Similar percentages of male and female participants were included, with 60.6% of lebrikizumab Q4W participants and 51.2% of lebrikizumab Q2W participants from ADvocate1&2 being female. In ADhere, this was 51.7% and 47.4%, respectively. Most participants were white; 75.8% (lebrikizumab Q4W) and 72.0% (lebrikizumab Q2W) in ADvocate1&2 and 75.9% (lebrikizumab Q4W) and 61.4% (lebrikizumab Q2W) in ADhere.

Safety

Safety outcomes are reported below separately, firstly for ADvocate RES and ADhere RES and secondly for ADjoin Overall.

During 100 weeks of ADjoin, 67.7% of lebrikizumab Q4W and 72.0% of lebrikizumab Q2W participants from ADvocate RES reported treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs). Most were mild (25.3% [LEBQ4W]; 34.1% [LEBQ2W]) or moderate (36.4% [LEBQ4W]; 34.1% [LEBQ2W]) in severity. Serious AEs were reported by 3.0% of lebrikizumab Q4W and 3.7% of lebrikizumab Q2W participants. For the primary endpoint, in ADvocate RES, 3.0% (n = 3) and 2.4% (n = 2) participants discontinued treatment due to an AE in lebrikizumab Q4W and Q2W, respectively (Table 2).

In ADhere RES, 58.6% of lebrikizumab Q4W and 61.4% of lebrikizumab Q2W participants reported TEAEs, most were mild (41.4% [Q4W]; 22.8% [Q2W]) or moderate (13.8% [Q4W]; 36.8% [Q2W]) in severity. Serious AEs were reported by 6.9% of lebrikizumab Q4W and 3.5% of lebrikizumab Q2W participants. In ADhere RES, 3.5% (n = 2) lebrikizumab Q2W participants discontinued treatment due to an AE.

Among the ADvocate1&2 and ADhere lebrikizumab responders, one participant died of natural causes on ADjoin study day 348 (462 days of total lebrikizumab exposure) from the Q2W arm of ADhere RES. The participant had a medical history of hypertension, cardiac ablation, AD, insomnia, and gastroesophageal reflux and the event was assessed by the Investigator to be unrelated to study treatment.

Conjunctivitis cluster events were reported by 5.1% and 3.7% of participants receiving lebrikizumab 250 mg Q4W and Q2W, respectively, in ADvocate RES and 10.3% and 14.0% of participants receiving lebrikizumab 250 mg Q4W and Q2W, respectively, in ADhere RES.

ADjoin Overall included 1153 participants (modified safety population) from ADvocate1&2, ADhere, ADore, ADopt-VA, and ADjoin direct entry (Table S3). In ADjoin Overall, 63.1% of lebrikizumab Q4W and 63.8% of lebrikizumab Q2W participants reported an AE (Table S3). Most were mild (27.7% [Q4W]; 28.8% [Q2W]) or moderate (30.5% [Q4W]; 31.1% [Q2W]) in severity. Serious AEs were reported by 3.5% of lebrikizumab Q4W participants and 4.1% of lebrikizumab Q2W participants. A total of 49 participants (4.2%) had an AE leading to permanent discontinuation from study treatment, of whom 3 participants (2.1%) were reported in the lebrikizumab Q4W group and 46 participants (4.5%) were reported in the lebrikizumab Q2W group. Overall, 7 participants (0.6%) discontinued due to conjunctivitis and 4 participants (0.3%) discontinued due to conjunctivitis allergic. Two opportunistic infections were confirmed (herpes zoster with reversible facial paralysis and oesophageal candidiasis). Both were treated and resolved. Two deaths occurred in the ADjoin Overall modified safety population, of which one is described above (ADhere RES). One additional death was reported in a participant in the lebrikizumab 250 mg Q2W arm of ADhere (non-responder) who died due to pancreatic cancer on ADjoin study day 496 (608 days of total lebrikizumab exposure). The participant had a medical history of coronary artery disease and myocardial infarction status post coronary artery bypass, hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, hyperuricemia, duodenal ulcer, benign prostatic hyperplasia, and basal cell carcinoma. Both deaths were considered not related to lebrikizumab treatment. Seven other participants in ADjoin Overall modified safety population reported malignancies other than NMSC (Table S3) with no specific pattern identified.

Efficacy

Efficacy is presented below for ADvocate RES and ADhere RES. Efficacy outcomes for the ADjoin Overall mITT population are presented in Table S4. Efficacy outcomes for the per-protocol W16 lebrikizumab responders (either IGA 0/1 or EASI 75, combined) from ADvocate1&2 and ADhere populations are presented in Fig. S2–S4.

IGA 0/1

Using as observed analysis, IGA 0/1 was maintained at W152 (ADjoin W100) by 84.0% (42/50) of lebrikizumab Q4W participants and 82.9% (34/41) of lebrikizumab Q2W participants from ADvocate RES who achieved IGA 0/1 at ADvocate W16 (Fig. 1a). Discussion

Due to the chronic and relapsing nature of AD, safe and efficacious systemic treatment options are important for long-term management of disease. Here, we investigate the long-term efficacy and safety of lebrikizumab in the population of participants who received continuous treatment for up to 152 weeks after achieving clinical response on lebrikizumab at W16. Participants with moderate-to-severe AD treated with lebrikizumab 250 mg Q2W through 16 weeks induction, followed by either 250 mg Q4W or Q2W, maintained a durable response up to 152/116 weeks as demonstrated by IGA 0/1, EASI 75, EASI 90, and POEM. Most participants maintained efficacy without the use of topicals as over 86% of ADvocate W16 lebrikizumab responders receiving lebrikizumab Q4W and Q2W did not require rescue therapy up to 152 weeks. These results confirm long-term efficacy of lebrikizumab (previously described up to 2 years [20], now extended to 3 years) and suggest that after an induction period of Q2W lebrikizumab, the less frequent dosing of Q4W lebrikizumab is sufficient to maintain numerically similar response to Q2W in most ADvocate W16 lebrikizumab responders with moderate-to-severe AD for 136 weeks without the need of rescue medication. The maintenance dosage of 250 mg Q4W provides continuous efficacy with fewer administrations. Convenient dosing is an important consideration for long-term control as it can promote adherence over topicals or daily treatment. The low TCS use and the convenient Q4W maintenance dose indicate that lebrikizumab is able to maintain long-term disease control.

The safety profile of lebrikizumab in ADjoin is consistent with previous lebrikizumab studies in participants with moderate-to-severe AD across 11 clinical trials, and there are no new safety concerns [21–23]. The proportion of participants who discontinued ADjoin due to an AE was low; within the W16 lebrikizumab responders, 5 participants (2.8%) who enrolled into ADjoin from ADvocate1&2 and 2 participants (2.3%) who enrolled into ADjoin from ADhere discontinued due to an AE.

Conjunctivitis was considered an AE of special interest as it was expected to be reported more frequently in patients with moderate-to-severe AD treated with lebrikizumab compared to placebo, similar to other drugs in the class that inhibit IL-13 [25]. During ADjoin, conjunctivitis cluster events were reported by a similar proportion of participants in Q4W and Q2W treatment groups in both ADvocate1&2 and ADhere W16 lebrikizumab responders. However, we note that the frequency with which conjunctivitis was reported by participants originating from ADhere is numerically higher. In ADvocate1&2 W16 lebrikizumab responders, conjunctivitis cluster events were reported by 5.1% and 3.7% of participants receiving lebrikizumab 250 mg Q4W and Q2W, respectively. In ADhere W16 lebrikizumab responders, conjunctivitis cluster events were reported by 10.3% and 14.0% of participants receiving lebrikizumab 250 mg Q4W and Q2W, respectively, during the ADjoin study. In the previously published data on 2415 patients across 11 AD clinical trials who received at least 1 dose of lebrikizumab at any time, the proportion of patients reporting conjunctivitis was 14.3% [26]. The proportion of participants reporting events of conjunctivitis differs between ADvocate1&2 and ADhere. The noted difference may reflect geographic differences between the study populations, although other unmeasured factors cannot be excluded.

Other targeted biologic therapies also reported long-term efficacy for patients with AD. For example, dupilumab reported efficacy up to week 204 in an open-label study with both once-weekly and Q2W dosing [27]. Tralokinumab also reported efficacy for up to 2 years in an open-label study with Q2W dosing [28, 29]. However, as a result of the different study designs, direct comparisons between the results of these studies and ADjoin should not be drawn.

The limitations of this analysis relate to the nature of long-term extension study design and include the decreasing participant population, which is typically seen in long-term extension studies. In addition, the analyses had no comparator or placebo arm because this work focused on efficacy and safety in participants who achieved clinical response following 16 weeks of lebrikizumab treatment. The safety profile of lebrikizumab was shown to be consistent across 11 clinical trials [26]. The total patient-years of exposure during ADjoin Overall was 1846.8.

Conclusion

In patients with moderate-to-severe AD, lebrikizumab treatment results in durable improvement in signs and symptoms from week 16 up to week 152, with a safety profile consistent with previously reported data. Efficacy outcomes were maintained up to 3 years of continuous lebrikizumab treatment in W16 lebrikizumab responders, with or without TCS, in both the lebrikizumab 250 mg Q4W and Q2W dose regimens, with most participants not using rescue therapy. The safety profile was consistent with ADvocate1&2, ADhere, and other lebrikizumab studies in patients with moderate-to-severe AD, and the data does not show new safety findings. Lebrikizumab achieves statistically significant and clinically meaningful response on skin and itch measures with a short-term induction period of Q2W that is maintained long-term, and comparable efficacy with the less frequent dosing regimen of Q4W. These long-term data demonstrate that lebrikizumab provides disease control over time and may help to inform clinical practice in this chronic and relapsing disease.
