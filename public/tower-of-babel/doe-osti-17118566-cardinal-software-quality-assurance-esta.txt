SEPTEMBER 2026

 Summary

This report is the culmination of a multi-year effort, beginning with the Cardinal SQA gap analysis
and progressing through the FY25 development of the SQAP and NekRS test integration [1, 2].
It documents the FY26 implementation of that framework and establishes the NQA-1 compliance
basis for Cardinal, including the capabilities of NekRS, Cardinal’s high-fidelity thermal-hydraulics
module. The work is conducted under the approved Cardinal Software Quality Assurance Plan
(SQAP) [3] and is evaluated against the applicable ASME NQA-1-2022 controls, with emphasis on
Part I Requirement 3, Design Control, and Requirement 11, Test Control. The report summarizes
the SQAP, the associated implementation activities, and the detailed SQAP-to-NQA-1 crosswalk.

 The FY26 activities placed the Cardinal SQAP into effect, established and trained the Change
Control Board, issued the initial controlled baseline release, improved links between test cases and
design documentation, and obtained the non-safety software quality-level designation. Cardinal
was also updated to integrate NekRS v26, including changes to the wrapper, data-transfer inter-
faces, host-side array ownership, passive-scalar handling, input files, and regression tests. These
activities provide the configuration, review, traceability, and records-management context in which
the technical evidence is interpreted.

 The NekRS verification and validation suite is organized around documented software re-
quirements and controlled acceptance criteria. Verification cases compare numerical solutions with
analytical or manufactured solutions and exercise the selected flow regimes, scalar and energy trans-
port, solver modes, preconditioners, subcycling, moving meshes, dealiasing, and related capabilities.
The selected NekRS solvers and turbulence models are tested against experimental or high-fidelity
computational reference data, providing comprehensive coverage of the physical models in scope.
The cases and their evidence are integrated with Cardinal’s software-quality documentation system
and linked to the requirements and SQAP controls.

 Together, the requirements, integration testing, verification and validation evidence, independent-
review process, configuration controls, and NQA-1 crosswalk establish a traceable compliance basis
for the NekRS scope addressed in this report. The conclusion is intentionally bounded by the
requirements and active qualifying cases documented here; release-specific compliance depends on
execution, review, approval, and retention of the associated records. The report also establishes
a baseline SQA roadmap that can support a future extension of the thermal-hydraulics suite or
application to other Cardinal modules.

1 Introduction

Cardinal is an open-source multiphysics application developed at Argonne National Laboratory
(ANL) under the U.S. Department of Energy – Office of Nuclear Energy’s Nuclear Energy Advanced
Modeling and Simulation (NEAMS) program [4]. It brings high-fidelity thermal-hydraulics and
continuous-energy neutron transport into a common environment for nuclear reactor analysis, with
particular emphasis on simulations that require feedback among physics represented on dissimilar
spatial discretizations.

 The application integrates the GPU-accelerated spectral-element Computational Fluid Dy-
namics (CFD) solver NekRS [5] and the Monte Carlo particle-transport code OpenMC [6] with
the Multiphysics Object-Oriented Simulation Environment (MOOSE) computational framework
[7]. Cardinal manages the interfaces and data transfers among these components, allowing coupled
fields to be exchanged without requiring conformal, one-to-one meshes. This modular arrangement
permits NekRS and OpenMC to participate in workflows with other MOOSE applications while
retaining the distributed-memory, shared-memory, and GPU parallelism of the underlying solvers.

 MOOSE provides Cardinal with reusable infrastructure for multiphysics execution, meshing
through libMesh, linear and nonlinear solution through PETSc, postprocessing, and stochastic
analysis. Cardinal adds the coupling objects, transfers, and application-specific controls needed
to assemble that infrastructure with NekRS and OpenMC. For software quality assurance, this
separation is important: the capabilities of each external solver and the interfaces through which
Cardinal exercises them must be stated, tested, and traceable. Thus, implementing the Cardinal
Software Quality Assurance Plan (SQAP) and establishing compliance with Nuclear Quality Assur-
ance Level 1 (NQA-1) for its high-fidelity thermal-hydraulics module calls for consistent, traceable
documentation of NekRS requirements, implementation controls, and verification and validation
evidence.

 NekRS [5] serves as Cardinal’s high-fidelity thermal-hydraulics module. Available NekRS
capabilities include incompressible, low-Mach, and Stokes flow solvers; energy and passive-scalar
transport in fluid and solid domains; conjugate heat transfer; user-defined source terms; turbulence
models including Large Eddy Simulation (LES), the k-τ Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS)
model and its Shear Stress Transport (SST) and hybrid RANS/LES variants, including Delayed
Detached Eddy Simulation (DDES) and Improved Delayed Detached Eddy Simulation (IDDES);
variable thermophysical properties; moving meshes or Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian (ALE); and
the Boussinesq buoyancy formulation. Within coupled Cardinal simulations, these capabilities
support volumetric power, temperature, and density feedback; 1-D/3-D coupling to systems codes;
and forward uncertainty quantification through MOOSE’s stochastic tools. For neutron transport,
Cardinal supports OpenMC cell and mesh tallies on Constructive Solid Geometry (CSG) and
DAGMC Computer-Aided Design (CAD) geometries and transfers the resulting fields through the
same multiphysics framework.

1.1 NQA-1 Compliance Effort

The Cardinal Software Quality Assurance (SQA) program has been developed over a multi-year
effort to establish a robust and sustainable quality framework appropriate for nuclear analysis
software. In FY23, a comprehensive gap analysis was performed to identify deficiencies in Cardinal’s
Quality Assurance (QA) processes with respect to the NQA-1 standard [1]. The gaps were prioritized
across three primary areas: software quality assurance planning, configuration control, and test
control, including verification testing, code coverage, and documentation.

 In FY25, significant progress was made in resolving the identified gaps [2]. A Cardinal SQAP
was drafted based on the MOOSE SQAP framework [8], adapted to reflect Cardinal’s status as
non-safety software while maintaining full NQA-1 compliance. Additionally, the NekRS Continuous
Integration (CI) test suite was partially ported into Cardinal’s automated test harness, with the
initial cases demonstrating how the broader suite could be exercised through Cardinal on each pull
request.

 In FY26, the approved Cardinal SQAP [3] was placed into effect and the remaining implemen-
tation activities were advanced through establishment and training of the Change Control Board
(CCB), issuance of the initial controlled baseline release, improved links between test cases and
design documentation, and formal designation of Cardinal as Quality Level D (QL-D) non-safety
software; these activities are summarized in Section 3. This report also completes the current
verification and validation basis for NekRS as Cardinal’s high-fidelity thermal-hydraulics module by
documenting the requirements and test evidence in Cardinal’s SQA system, extending the suite to
additional solver modes and physical models, and updating Cardinal for NekRS v26, as described in
Section 4.

2 Overview of the Cardinal SQAP

The Cardinal SQAP delineates the software quality assurance program framework by describing
program activities, organization, and documentation and by defining the interconnections among
program elements [3]. Its scope includes the Cardinal code library, associated documentation, and
Cardinal’s dependencies on MOOSE, OpenMC, and NekRS [3]. It is aligned with the Argonne
Quality Assurance Program Plan [3], which was designed to align with U.S. Department of Energy
(DOE) Order 414.1D [10], and with revision 10 of the MOOSE and MOOSE-based applications
SQAP [8].

 The Cardinal development team is responsible for implementing the software quality assurance
requirements for software under its control; the SQAP states that these requirements are necessary
for compliance with DOE Order 414.1D [10] and American Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME)
NQA-1-2022 [9]. Cardinal is designated QL-D non-safety software and is managed accordingly under
Argonne’s graded approach [3]. The application of Cardinal to end-user needs is outside the scope
of the SQAP; users are responsible for determining whether the software is sufficient for a specified
task and for applying the software quality assurance measures required by their organizations [3].

2.1 NQA-1 Alignment and Relevance

The Cardinal SQAP establishes the controls and processes used by the development team to satisfy
the applicable software quality assurance requirements of ASME NQA-1-2022 [9], as implemented
through the plan’s procedures and records [3]. These controls address the applicable requirements
of Part I together with the software-specific provisions of Part II, Subpart 2.7; Appendix A maps
the Cardinal SQAP to those requirements.

 For NekRS, Cardinal’s high-fidelity thermal-hydraulics module, this report applies that frame-
work by focusing on ASME NQA-1-2022 [9] Part I Requirement 3, Design Control, and Part I
Requirement 11, Test Control, because the requirements, traceability, verification, validation, and
test evidence developed for NekRS most directly demonstrate these controls. They are not pre-
sented as the complete basis for Cardinal’s NQA-1 compliance; the documentation, configuration
management, independent review, change-control, and release controls established by the Cardinal
SQAP remain applicable and are summarized in Section 2.2.

 5

2.1.1 Design Control (NQA-1 Part I, Requirement 3)

NQA-1 Part I, Requirement 3 establishes design-control requirements, while Part II, Subpart 2.7
provides software-specific design and verification provisions [9]. The Cardinal SQAP implements
these controls by requiring software requirements to be recorded in the repository and made available
in the Software Requirements Specification (SRS), traceability through design and testing to be
maintained in the Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM), and design documents to be stored in
the repository and referenced by test cases [3]. An independent CCB reviewer evaluates each pull
request for the technical adequacy and internal completeness, consistency, clarity, and correctness
of the software design and confirms traceability to the software requirements [3]. For the work
documented here, the NekRS requirements and their links to design information and test evidence
provide the corresponding design-control record. The NekRS studies are presented in Section 4, while
Appendix A maps the governing Cardinal SQAP controls to the applicable NQA-1 requirements.

2.1.2 Test Control (NQA-1 Part I, Requirement 11)

NQA-1 Part I, Requirement 11 establishes test-control requirements, supplemented for computer
software by Part II, Subpart 2.7 [9]. The Cardinal SQAP implements these controls through
its software verification, validation, and testing process, which covers test planning, automated
execution, evaluation by an independent reviewer, and retention of test results [3]. Each test
case must identify the requirement it satisfies, reference one or more design documents, record its
provenance, and define acceptance criteria; the resulting test records are accessible through the
RTM and are evaluated during independent review of the pull request [3]. Sections 4.4 and 4.5
document how these controls are applied to the verification and validation evidence for NekRS.

2.2 Implementation of the Cardinal SQAP

The Cardinal SQAP is implemented through controlled software-development activities and the
reviews, test results, records, and release artifacts produced by those activities [3]. For the
NekRS work addressed here, Section 3 summarizes the program-level implementation activities,
Section 4 presents the technical verification and validation evidence, and Appendix A relates the
governing SQAP controls to the applicable NQA-1 requirements. Together, these elements document
implementation of the Cardinal SQAP for the scope of this report; release-specific compliance
additionally depends on completion and retention of the reviews, records, and approvals prescribed
by the plan [3].

4 Thermal-Hydraulics Module (NekRS) V&V

4.1 Overview

Software V&V is a fundamental component of the Cardinal SQA program. The V&V strategy for
Cardinal’s thermal hydraulics solver, NekRS, is to demonstrate the suitability of NekRS against
its functional requirements by maintaining a curated set of verification and validation test cases
that run automatically within Cardinal’s CI pipeline upon each pull request. This approach ensures
that any change to the codebase is continuously evaluated against a documented set of acceptance
criteria, providing an auditable record of NekRS’s software quality.

 The test suite covers verification cases, in which numerical solutions are compared against
analytical or manufactured solutions, and validation cases, in which solutions are compared against
experimental or high fidelity computational reference data. Acceptance criteria for verification
cases require that solution errors converge spectrally under h/p spatial refinement toward machine
precision. Acceptance criteria for validation cases are based on quantitative comparison with
experimental measurements or high fidelity Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS) and LES reference
data.

 The suite exercises all major NekRS solver modules, physical models, and supporting modules
identified in the software requirements; these capabilities are listed in Section 4.2. The cases
also exercise alternative solver modes, adaptive time stepping, preconditioner configurations, and
dimensional formulations. Together, these cases provide comprehensive coverage of the NekRS
capabilities selected for Cardinal and generate documented acceptance evidence for the requirements,
verification, and validation controls of the Cardinal SQAP, supporting the NQA-1 compliance basis
for this module. All cases are documented in Cardinal’s MooseDocs SQA documentation system.

4.3 Cardinal and NekRS v26 Integration

Updating Cardinal to NekRS version 26 (v26) required coordinated changes to the Cardinal wrapper,
data-transfer objects, NekRS input files, tutorials, and regression tests. The principal compatibility
change was a shift in ownership of host-side arrays used for data exchange: arrays that were hosted
by NekRS in the v23 integration are now allocated and managed by Cardinal’s wrapper. This
section summarizes those implementation changes, the associated test and tutorial updates, and the
acceptance testing performed.

4.3.1 Integration Scope

The update of NekRS to v26 required changes to all NekRS input files used throughout the Cardinal
test suite; a comprehensive summary of the changes can be found at https://github.com/
neams-th-coe/cardinal/pull/1340. Since a deep review was needed to update to v26,
we took the opportunity to also simplify tests where possible by removing extraneous syntax.
The majority of the existing tests passed without issue; only four tests required re-golding with
justifications described in the pull request and summarized here:

 • cht/nondimensional test: This test checks for conjugate heat transfer solutions in non-
 dimensional form, to ensure that they match an equivalent dimensional setup. In v26, if these
 files run to the pseudo steady state, they diverge from unreasonable CFL after the first data
 transfer from MOOSE. This test previously passed because only one time step was simulated
 in the test – the case was set up with Re=3000, which is turbulent but uses a fairly coarse mesh
 that would not have been suitable for a turbulent flow. This test was revised to simultaneously
 make the case smaller but with more suitable wall resolution, allowing more time steps and
 obtain a more meaningful test result. Some non-determinism (different results when running
 with different number of ranks) was observed; by reverting the repository history, it was
 confirmed that this same non-determinism existed prior to v26, but it never manifested due
 to the particular parallel settings used in the CIVET run (which would only ever run the case
 with a single set of rank choices). However, the non-determinism differences were on the order
 of 1e-5 or smaller and do not grow in time if running a very long pseudo-transient; hence,
 the test tolerances were selected to account for this indeterminism (which is to be expected
 whenever parallel communication happens, though in the other tests the magnitude of this
 indeterminism happens to be lower).

 • cht/sfr pincell test: This test is the dimensional version of the prior listing; for con-
 sistency, this test was updated in the same manner as described in the preceding bullet
 point.

 12

 • lowMach test: This test runs the lowMach NekRS CI test; small differences were observed,
 most likely due to inherent changes in the NekRS solver itself. The purpose of this test is
 not to act as a NekRS CI case itself, but rather as a Cardinal case and it still meets that
 purpose. It was confirmed that the postprocessors in Cardinal are functioning correctly, based
 on looking at the max/min console output from NekRS (the differences in this test were from
 the max p and min p postprocessors).

 • userobjects/side/nondimensional test: This test is checking that integrals and aver-
 ages performed over binned sidesets in NekRS are performed correctly. This test uses a sodium
 fast reactor 7-pin bundle, and the original mesh used happens to have a small floating point
 indeterminism as to identifying which bin in the averaging process each element is assigned to.
 This was confirmed by locating the failing elements and mapping them in Paraview to the
 subchannels. Several elements in the original mesh were nearly perfectly straddling one of the
 boundaries of the userobject edges. The mesh was re-generated to have better quality and
 re-golded, but still confirmed that the values look identical to the prior test and still match
 the dimensional version for which the purpose of this test exists.

 In addition to the tests, all tutorials were updated and several improved. The load from -
exodus test previously used the ethier CI case, which is overly complicated and introduces
technical debt whenever NekRS source code changes. This tutorial was modified so that a much
simpler NekRS case is used, which for the purposes of the tutorial is sufficient. In addition, the
subchannel test was modified to a CSV-diff test to have a quantitative check rather than the
qualitative check that would simply run the input and catch execution errors.

4.5 Validation Studies

The validation studies compare NekRS predictions with experimental measurements or high-fidelity
computational reference data to assess the accuracy of the physical models exercised by the Cardinal
test suite. For the NQA-1 evidence basis, each validation input defines a quantitative comparison
and a final-time pass criterion. The criterion and the reference source are stated below for every case,
with the resulting verification evidence retained and reviewed under the Cardinal SQAP controls
described in Sections 2.1–3. Because these cases are intended to provide practical, repeatable
regression tests, their meshes are deliberately kept modest; the resulting pass criteria are therefore
tied to the selected mesh resolutions and associated discretization errors. A refined mesh could
support tighter criteria, but would increase computational cost, which is undesirable for routine
regression testing, and would require a new controlled regression baseline.

5 Conclusion and Future Outlook

This report establishes the quality-assurance and verification-and-validation basis for the high-
fidelity thermal-hydraulics module of Cardinal, NekRS, within the scope defined by the Cardinal
Software Quality Assurance Plan (SQAP). The SQAP summary, its alignment with the applicable
ASME NQA-1-2022 provisions, and the detailed crosswalk in Appendix A provide the governing
process framework. The report consequently treats NQA-1 compliance as the result of coordinated
requirements definition, design control, configuration control, independent review, test control, and
records management, rather than as a claim based on numerical results alone.

 The report defines the NekRS capabilities in scope and connects them to Cardinal verification
and validation cases. The v26 integration work addressed changes in the Cardinal wrapper, data-
transfer interfaces, host-side array ownership, passive-scalar handling, input files, and regression
tests. The verification suite compares computed solutions with analytical or manufactured solutions
and exercises the selected flow regimes, scalar and energy transport, moving-mesh formulations,
solver modes, preconditioners, subcycling, dealiasing, and related numerical configurations. The
validation suite compares turbulence, conjugate-heat-transfer, backward-facing-step, periodic-hill,
and buoyancy predictions with experimental or high-fidelity reference data. Each case has a

 33

documented quantitative acceptance criterion, and the associated convergence plots and reference
comparisons provide supporting evidence for the correctness and applicability of the implemented
capabilities.

 Taken together, the requirements, controlled test cases, acceptance criteria, SQAP activities,
and NQA-1 crosswalk establish a traceable compliance basis for the NekRS work addressed here,
particularly for ASME NQA-1 Part I Requirement 3, Design Control, and Requirement 11, Test
Control. The determination remains bounded by the requirements and cases explicitly identified
in Section 4; capabilities outside that scope are not implicitly qualified. Release-specific compli-
ance continues to depend on execution of the approved tests, independent review of the results,
configuration of the tested baseline, and retention of the associated records as required by the SQAP.

 Looking ahead, this report and the activities described herein establish a baseline process for
applying software quality assurance within Cardinal. The present implementation is focused on
NekRS, but the same roadmap, including requirements definition, SQAP alignment, traceability,
controlled verification and validation, independent review, and records retention, can be extended
to other Cardinal modules or to a further extended thermal-hydraulics suite as their scope and
quality needs are defined.
