Abstract
. In this article we are concerned with the existence of blow-up solutions to the following boundary value problem − Δ ​ v = λ ​ V ​ ( x ) ​ | x | 2 ​ e v ​ in ​ B 1 , v = 0 ​ on ​ ∂ B 1 , -\Delta v=\lambda V(x)|x|^{2}e^{v}\;\hbox{ in }B_{1},\quad v=0\;\hbox{ on }\partial B_{1}, where B 1 B_{1} is the unit ball in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} centered at the origin, V ⁡ ( x ) V(x) is a positive smooth potential, and λ > 0 \lambda>0 is a small parameter. We find necessary and sufficient conditions on the potential V V for the existence of a blow-up sequence of solutions tending to infinity near the origin as λ → 0 + \lambda\to 0^{+} . In particular, we obtain a second-order classification of the coefficient function V V for which (simple) blow-up occurs at the origin. Introduction

Singular Liouville-type equations play a prominent role in the study of problems in conformal geometry and mathematical physics.
The well-known prescribing Gauss curvature equation, the mean field equation, the Chern–Simons self-dual vortices, the statistical mechanics of two-dimensional turbulence,
as well as systems of equations of Toda type, provide a few representative examples of their applications (

In this paper we consider a smooth and bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
containing the origin, and we study the following singular Liouville equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta v=\lambda|x|^{2\alpha}V(x)e^{v}&\hbox{ in }&\Omega,\\
&\ v=0&\hbox{ on }&\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

Here
\lambda
is a small positive parameter,

V
is a positive smooth function, and
\alpha>0
.

The analysis of this class of equations is usually challenging, since the exponential nonlinear term is closely related to the lack of compactness in the variational approach. One important feature of these equations is the blow-up phenomenon: indeed, when a sequence of solutions tends to infinity near a blow-up point, the asymptotic behavior of the solutions near such a point carries important information that is crucial for some applications.
To this purpose, refined blow-up techniques have been developed to describe the local asymptotic profile of solution sequences around a blow-up point, where bubbling phenomena occur; we refer to the following works as a partial collection of references:

The analysis of the blow-up behaviour at points away from

v_{k}
having a single blow-up point different from
0
. More precisely, Li showed that the difference between
v_{k}
and a suitably scaled sequence of “standard bubbles” is only of order
O(1)

Later, Chen and Lin (

One of the main difficulties in the study of blow-up solutions to (
1.1
) arises when the blow-up point happens to be the origin, i.e. when it coincides with the location of a singularity.
It is known from the works of Kuo and Lin (
[
23
]
) and Bartolucci and Tarantello (
[
2
]
), later refined in
[
1
]
, that if
\alpha\not\in\mathbb{N}
(the set of natural numbers), then blow-up solutions satisfy a spherical Harnack inequality around the singular point
0
. Moreover, by using an improved argument based on the Pohozaev identity, they established a result similar
to Li’s, concerning
the quantization phenomena for blow-up solutions: more precisely, the profile of the solutions
differs from global solutions of a Liouville type equation only by a uniformly
bounded term. Naturally, their method also works when
\alpha=0
and provides an alternative proof of Li’s result. Subsequently, the paper
[
45
]
further improves their result and establishes an expansion of the solutions near the blow-up points with a sharp error estimate.

However, when the strength of the singular source is an integer, i.e.
\alpha=N\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that
0
is a quantized singular source, and the so called “non-simple blow-up” phenomenon does occur. This means that the bubbling solutions may not satisfy the spherical Harnack inequality, and multiple local maxima near the singular source may appear. More precisely, let us consider a sequence
\lambda_{k}\to 0^{+}
and a corresponding sequence
v_{k}
of solutions to the following perturbed problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta v_{k}=\lambda_{k}|x|^{2N}V(x)e^{v_{k}}&\hbox{ in }&\Omega,\\
&\ v_{k}=0&\hbox{ on }&\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

where
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
For a sequence of blow-up solutions
v_{k}
, it is standard
to assume a uniform bound on the total mass (see
[
4
]
): there
exists
C>0
, independent of
k
, such that

\lambda_{k}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2N}V(x)e^{v_{k}}dx\leq C.

(1.3)

We also suppose that
v_{k}
admits the origin as its only blow-up point in
\Omega
; in other words,

\max_{\Omega}v_{k}(x)\to+\infty,

(1.4)

and
for any compact set
K\subset\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{0\}
there exists a constant
C(K)
(depending on
K
) such that

\max_{K}v_{k}\leq C(K).

(1.5)

When
N\in\mathbb{N}
, two cases may occur for bubbling phenomena:
v_{k}
satisfies the simple blow-up property at
0
, or
v_{k}
is not simply blowing up at
0
. More precisely, one of the following two possibilities occurs.

I CASE

We say that
v_{k}
has the
simple blow-up
property if

\sup_{\overline{\Omega}}\Big(v_{k}(x)+2(N+1)\log|x|+\log\lambda_{k}\Big)\leq C.

(1.6)

In fact, the simple blow-up property is a necessary and sufficient condition for the validity of the asymptotic behavior of the sequence
v_{k}
, after scaling, as a “single” bubble around
0

; we refer to
[
35
]
.

II CASE

We say that
v_{k}
is
not-simply blowing up
at
0
if

\max_{\overline{\Omega}}\Big(v_{k}(x)+2(N+1)\log|x|+\log\lambda_{k}\Big)\to+\infty

(1.7)

which is equivalent to saying that the spherical Harnack inequality does not hold for
v_{k}
.

In the latter case,
it was established by
[
2
]
and
[
23
]
that
if
v_{k}
exhibits a non-simple blow-up profile (
1.7
), then there are
N+1
local maximum points of
v_{k}
, denoted by
\beta_{k,0},\ldots,\beta_{k,N}
, and they are asymptotically evenly distributed on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
after scaling according to their magnitude: more precisely, along a subsequence,

\lim_{k}\frac{\beta_{k,i}}{|\beta_{k,0}|}=e^{{\rm i}(\theta_{0}+\frac{2\pi i}{N+1})},\quad i=0,\ldots,N.

It follows from the analysis in
[
35
]
that the simple blow-up property always holds when
N\not\in\mathbb{N}
. On the other hand, for
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the origin may become a non-simple blow-up point, due to the richer structure of the solution set of the planar “singular” Liouville equation:

-\Delta U=|x|^{2N}e^{U}\quad\hbox{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{2},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x|^{2N}e^{U(x)}dx<+\infty,

(1.8)

which arises as the so called “limiting” problem, in the sense that the profile describing
v_{k}
after blow-up is given by a solution of (
1.8
).
In view of the classification result in
[
30
]
, we know that, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
, all solutions of problem (
1.8
) are given, in complex notation, by the three-parameter family of functions

U_{\tau,b}=\log\frac{8(N+1)^{2}\tau}{(\tau+|x^{N+1}-b|^{2})^{2}}\quad\tau>0,\,b\in\mathbb{C}.

(1.9)

Note that, in contrast to the case
N\not\in\mathbb{N}
(where (
1.9
) holds only with
b=0
), the solution
U_{\tau,b}
need not be radial, and may attain its maximum value at a point different from the origin (in fact, at
N+1
points corresponding to the
N+1
complex roots of
b
). Thus, when
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the non-simple blow-up phenomenon may occur.

However, the question on the existence of
blow-up sequences satisfying (
1.2
)–(
1.5
)
is far from being completely settled, and
only partial results are currently known. The construction of solutions exhibiting a
blow-up profile at the origin has been carried out for problem (
1.2
)–(
1.5
) with
V=1
in the following cases: for any fixed integer
N\geq 1
provided that the singular point is shifted in a

\lambda
-dependent way, namely as
|x-p_{\lambda}|^{2N}
(see
[
17
]
); or if
\Omega
is the unit ball and the weight of the source is a positive number
N=N_{\lambda}
approaching an integer
N
from the right (see
[
11
]
). On the other hand, in
[
15
]
, an analogous result is obtained for any fixed positive integer
N
when the potential
V=V_{\lambda}
is symmetric and
\lambda
-dependent. To our knowledge, the existence of non-simple blow-up phenomenon for (
1.2
)–(
1.5
), with a fixed potential
V
and a fixed integer
N
independent of
\lambda
, is still an open problem, even in the case of the ball: the only known example is constructed in

[
14
]
for a special class of potentials.
In this paper we address this case and we shall contribute in this direction.

In particular, we are interested in finding
conditions on the coefficient function

V

under which there exists a sequence of solutions blowing up at
0
(either simple or non simple blow-up). More precisely, we investigate the situation when

N=1
and
\Omega
is the unit ball
B_{1}
centered at the origin:

B_{1}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,|x|<1\},

so that problems (
1.2
)–(
1.5
) become

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta v_{k}=\lambda_{k}V(x)|x|^{2}e^{v_{k}}&\hbox{ in }&B_{1},\\
&\ v_{k}=0&\hbox{ on }&\partial B_{1},\\
&\lambda_{k}\int_{B_{1}}V(x)|x|^{2}e^{v_{k}}dx\leq C,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.10)

and we assume that
v_{k}
admits the origin as its only blow-up point in
B_{1}
:

\max_{B_{1}}v_{k}\to+\infty,\quad\sup_{\varepsilon\leq|x|\leq 1}v_{k}<C(\varepsilon)\quad\forall\varepsilon>0.

(1.11)

In addition, we impose standard assumptions on
V
:

V\in C^{3}(\overline{B}_{1}),\quad\min_{\overline{B}_{1}}V>0,

(1.12)

and

V(0)=1,\qquad\nabla V(0)=0,\qquad 0\hbox{ is a nondegenerate critical point of }V.

(1.13)

We point out that the hypothesis on the vanishing of the first derivatives of the potential
V
at
0
is a necessary condition for the validity of a blow-up behaviour at
0
: indeed, it has been shown in
[
40
]
that even in the case when
blow-up solutions violate the
spherical Harnack inequality
near a singular source, the first derivatives of the coefficient function must tend to zero.

must satisfy when

is the unique blow-up point in a neighborhood of the origin. In particular it has been proved in

Theorem 1.1

(
[

Assume that

V
satisfies (

\Delta V(0)=0.

We also mention that in

The key point of the proof is the use of a radial coefficient function, which allows one to reduce the problem to the radial case and avoid kernel functions in the linearized equation associated with the quantized setting.

The main contribution of this paper is to strengthen the conclusions of

Indeed, we establish that a necessary and sufficient condition on the potential

at the origin.
More specifically, using assumptions (

admits the following expansion in a small neighborhood of the origin:

V(x)=1+\frac{\gamma_{1}x_{1}^{2}+\gamma_{2}x_{2}^{2}}{2}+O(|x|^{3})\hbox{ as }x\to 0.

(1.14)

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that

V
satisfies (
1.12
)–(
1.13
) and that, up to a rotation, (

) holds.
Then, a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a blow-up sequence

satisfying (
1.10

1.11

\operatorname{det}D^{2}V(0)=\gamma_{1}\cdot\gamma_{2}>0.

(1.15)

Furthermore,

satisfies the simple blow-up property (

) and the limiting profile of

, after a suitable scaling, is a bubble (

and

b_{1}>0\text{ if }|\gamma_{1}|<|\gamma_{2}|,\qquad b_{1}<0\text{ if }|\gamma_{1}|>|\gamma_{2}|,\qquad b_{1}=0\text{ if }\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1.2
proves that if
0
is a nondegenerate critical point of
V
, then not only are non-simple blow-up situations ruled out, in agreement with
[
13
]
-
[
41
]
, but simple blow-up can occur if and only if the eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix of
V
at
0
have the same sign. Moreover, the corresponding vector
b
is an eigenvector;
in particular, if
V
is radial at the leading order–namely when the eigenvalues coincide–then the sequence is radially symmetric in its first order approximation and behaves as a bubble around the maximum point
0
.

The proof of the necessary condition relies on the derivation of a Pohozaev-type identity, obtained by suitably choosing
the test functions. In particular, we exploit the invariance of the non-radially symmetric solutions for the limiting problem (
1.8
) with respect to inversion
x\mapsto\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}
,
and we employ a test function singular at
0
to derive
a Pohozaev identity in the punctured domain
B_{1}\setminus\{|x|\leq\varepsilon\}
; we then use the estimates for bubbling solutions obtained in
[
3
]
and
[
39
]
to
handle the terms arising on the inner boundary
|x|=\varepsilon
in the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
A delicate analysis is required to address the boundary terms, for which
it is crucial
to consider the case
\Omega=B_{1}
in order to prove that the integral terms on the outer boundary
\partial B_{1}
vanish in the limit
\lambda\to 0^{+}
. Moreover, the method fails for
N\geq 2
, since in the expansion of the Pohozaev identity we no longer capture information on the Hessian matrix of
V
(see Remark
2.8
for technical details).

The main tool we use to construct the blow-up solutions (sufficient condition) is the Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction,
which is widely employed in many planar critical problems. Roughly speaking,
we look for a solution to (
1.10
) in a small neighborhood of the first approximation provided by the bubble (
1.9
), with a suitable choice of the parameters
\tau,b
. As is quite standard in singular perturbation theory, a crucial ingredient is the nondegeneracy of the explicit
limit
family of solutions (
1.9
) to the limiting Liouville problem (
1.8
), in the sense that all bounded elements in the kernel of the linearized operator correspond to variations along the parameters of
the family, as established in
[
17
]
. By carrying out the reduction process, the problem of constructing blow-up families of solutions to (
1.10
)–(
1.11
) is reduced to the problem of finding critical points of a functional depending on the parameter
b
. More specifically, the reduced functional turns out to be
uniformly close to a limiting functional
{\mathcal{F}}
, which admits
0
as a nondegenerate critical point; consequently, the persistence of a critical point is guaranteed for sufficiently small uniform perturbations. This allows us to detect a family of bubbling solutions to (
1.10
)–(
1.11
).

The organization of the article is as follows. In section 2, we prove the necessary condition for the validity of a blow-up behaviour at
0
. We first derive two Pohozaev identities for problem (
1.10
)–(

In section 3, we present a construction of bubbling solutions based on the Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction method. We first estimate the error up to which the approximating solution solves problem (
1.10

the invertibility of the linearized operator, and finally we address the solvability of the reduced
problem by a contraction argument. This completes the proof of Theorems

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2
Part I: Necessary Condition for Blow-Up

In this section, we derive Pohozaev-type identities for solutions of the singular Liouville equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta v=\lambda|x|^{2}V(x)e^{v}&\hbox{ in }&B_{1}\\
&v=0&\hbox{ on }&\partial B_{1}\end{aligned}\right..

(2.1)

We then exploit these
integral identities to provide the necessary condition on the potential
V
stated in Theorem
1.2
for the existence of associated families of
blow-up solutions.

2.1.
Pohozaev-type identities.

Throughout this section, we assume that
the potential
V>0
belongs to the class
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
.
We then develop some basic properties of solutions
v\in C(\overline{B}_{1})
to the boundary value problem (
2.1
)

The following proposition establishes an integral representation for the first derivatives.

Proposition 2.1
.

Any solution
v
of (
2.1
) satisfies the following Pohozaev identity:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\bigg|\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\bigg|^{2}x_{i}d\sigma+\lambda\int_{\partial B_{1}}V(x)x_{i}d\sigma+2\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}x_{i}d\sigma=4\pi\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}(0)+\lambda\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2}\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{i}}(x)e^{v}dx

for
i=1,2,
where
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
stands for the unit outward normal.

Proof.

Let us multiply both sides of equation in (
2.1
) by
\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}
and integrate on
B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}
for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small:

\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}(-\Delta v)\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}dx=\lambda\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}V(x)e^{v}x_{1}dx.

(2.2)

Taking into account that
\frac{x_{i}}{|x|^{2}}
is a harmonic function for
x\neq 0
, by applying Gauss-Green formula we derive

\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}(-\Delta v)\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}dx=-\int_{\partial B_{1}\cup\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}d\sigma+\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\bigg(\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)v\,d\sigma.

(2.3)

Let us examine separately the boundary integrals over
\partial B_{\varepsilon}
: first

-\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}d\sigma=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}x_{1}d\sigma=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}x_{1}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}x_{2}\bigg)x_{1}d\sigma.

(2.4)

Since
v
is two times differentiable by standard regularity theory,
we get

\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}x_{1}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}x_{2}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}(0)x_{1}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}(0)x_{2}+O(|x|^{2}).

Using that
\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}x_{1}x_{2}d\sigma=0
by oddness and
\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}x_{i}^{2}d\sigma=\pi\varepsilon^{3}
,
by (
2.4
) we deduce

-\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}d\sigma=\pi\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}(0)+o(1)

(2.5)

as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
Let us pass to examine the second boundary term in (
2.3
):

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\bigg(\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)v\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\bigg(\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)x_{1}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\bigg(\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)x_{2}\bigg)v(x)\,d\sigma

(2.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}x_{1}v(x)d\sigma.

Since

x_{1}v(x)=x_{1}v(0)+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}(0)x_{1}^{2}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}(0)x_{1}x_{2}+O(|x|^{3}),

by (
2.6
) we derive

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\bigg(\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)v\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\pi\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}(0)+o(1)

(2.7)

as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
By inserting (
2.5
) and (
2.7
) into (
2.3
) we obtain

\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}(-\Delta v)\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}dx=2\pi\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}(0)-\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}x_{1}d\sigma+o(1)

as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
by which, letting
\varepsilon
go to
0^{+}
in (
2.2
), we conclude

\lambda\int_{B_{1}}V(x)e^{v}x_{1}dx=2\pi\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}(0)-\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}x_{1}d\sigma.

(2.8)

Next, let us multiply both sides of the equation in (
2.1
) by
\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}
and integrating on
B_{1}
we get

\int_{B_{1}}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\bigg)dx-\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}d\sigma=\int_{B_{1}}\lambda|x|^{2}V(x)e^{v}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}dx.

(2.9)

Taking into account that

\nabla v\cdot\nabla\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\bigg)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\Big(|\nabla v|^{2}\Big),\qquad e^{v}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}=\frac{\partial e^{v}}{\partial x_{1}},

after integration by parts (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{1}}|\nabla v|^{2}x_{1}d\sigma-\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}d\sigma

\displaystyle=-2\lambda\int_{B_{1}}x_{1}V(x)e^{v}dx-\lambda\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2}\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{1}}(x)e^{v}dx+\lambda\int_{\partial B_{1}}V(x)x_{i}d\sigma

where we have used the homogeneous boundary condition
v=0
on
\partial B_{1}
. Since on
\partial B_{1}
one has
\nabla v=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\,\nu=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\,x
, the above identity can be rewritten as

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\bigg|\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\bigg|^{2}x_{1}d\sigma=-2\lambda\int_{B_{1}}x_{1}V(x)e^{v}dx-\lambda\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2}\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{1}}(x)e^{v}dx+\lambda\int_{\partial B_{1}}V(x)x_{1}d\sigma

Combining the last identity with (

)
we get the thesis for
i=1
. The identity for

can be deduced similarly.
∎

We are going to provide two crucial Pohozaev identities satisfied by solutions of (

).

Proposition 2.2
.

Any solution
v

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\lambda e^{v}

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{1}}x_{1}-\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{2}}x_{2}\bigg)dx

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{\partial B_{1}}V(x)(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})d\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\bigg|\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\bigg|^{2}(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})d\sigma-\pi\bigg|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}(0)\bigg|^{2}+\pi\bigg|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}(0)\bigg|^{2},

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\lambda e^{v}\bigg(\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{1}}

\displaystyle x_{2}+\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{2}}x_{1}\bigg)dx=2\lambda\int_{\partial B_{1}}V(x)x_{1}x_{2}d\sigma+\int_{\partial B_{1}}\bigg|\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\bigg|^{2}x_{1}x_{2}d\sigma-2\pi\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}(0)\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}(0),

where
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
stands for the unit outward normal.

Proof.

Let us multiply both sides of the equation in (
2.1
) by

\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}};

using that

by standard regularity theory and integrating on

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}(-\Delta v)\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}

(2.10)

By applying Gauss Green formula, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}(-\Delta v)\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)dx-\int_{\partial B_{1}\cup\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)d\sigma.

\displaystyle\nabla v\cdot\nabla\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}(|\nabla v|^{2})\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}(|\nabla v|^{2})\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}-|\nabla v|^{2}\frac{x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}}{|x|^{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\bigg(|\nabla v|^{2}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\bigg(|\nabla v|^{2}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg),

by which, applying again Gauss-Green formula,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)=\int_{\partial B_{1}\cup\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{2}\bigg(\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\nu_{1}-\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\nu_{2}\bigg)d\sigma.

Hence we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}(-\Delta v)\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{1}\cup\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{2}\bigg(\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\nu_{1}-\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\nu_{2}\bigg)d\sigma

(2.11)

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial B_{1}\cup\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)d\sigma.

Let us examine separately the boundary integrals over
\partial B_{1}
and over
\partial B_{\varepsilon}
:
taking into account of the homogeneous boundary condition in problem (

) we have that
\nabla v=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\,\nu=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\,x
on
\partial B_{1}
, which implies

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{2}\bigg(\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\nu_{1}-\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\nu_{2}\bigg)d\sigma-\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)d\sigma=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\bigg|\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\bigg|^{2}(x_{2}^{2}-x_{1}^{2})d\sigma.

On the other hand,

on

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{2}\bigg(\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\nu_{1}-\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\nu_{2}\bigg)d\sigma-\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\bigg(\bigg|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\bigg|^{2}-\bigg|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\bigg|^{2}\bigg)d\sigma

\displaystyle=\pi\bigg|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}(0)\bigg|^{2}-\pi\bigg|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}(0)\bigg|^{2}+o(1)

as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
By inserting the above two boundary estimates into (
2.11
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}(-\Delta v)\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}

\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)dx

(2.12)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\bigg|\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\bigg|^{2}(x_{2}^{2}-x_{1}^{2})d\sigma+\pi\bigg|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}(0)\bigg|^{2}-\pi\bigg|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}(0)\bigg|^{2}+o(1)

as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

Now let us pass to examine the right hand side of (
2.10

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\lambda V(x)e^{v}\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}x_{1}-\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}x_{2}\bigg)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{1}}\lambda e^{v}\bigg(\frac{\partial V(x)}{\partial x_{1}}x_{1}-\frac{\partial V(x)}{\partial x_{2}}x_{2}\bigg)dx+\int_{\partial B_{1}}\lambda V(x)e^{v}\Big(x_{1}\nu_{1}-x_{2}\nu_{2}\Big)d\sigma

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{1}}\lambda e^{v}\bigg(\frac{\partial V(x)}{\partial x_{1}}x_{1}-\frac{\partial V(x)}{\partial x_{2}}x_{2}\bigg)dx+\int_{\partial B_{1}}\lambda V(x)(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})d\sigma,

where we have used the homogeneous boundary condition

Inserting (

Similarly let us multiply both sides of the equation in (
2.1
) by

\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}};

integrating on

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}(-\Delta v)\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)dx=\lambda\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}V(x)e^{v}\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}x_{2}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}x_{1}\bigg)dx.

(2.14)

Gauss-Green formula gives

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}(-\Delta v)\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)dx-\int_{\partial B_{1}\cup\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)d\sigma.

We compute

\displaystyle\nabla v\cdot\nabla\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}(|\nabla v|^{2})\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}(|\nabla v|^{2})\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}-|\nabla v|^{2}\frac{2x_{1}x_{2}}{|x|^{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\bigg(|\nabla v|^{2}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\bigg(|\nabla v|^{2}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg),

which implies, by using again Gauss-Green formula,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)=\int_{\partial B_{1}\cup\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{2}\bigg(\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\nu_{1}+\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\nu_{2}\bigg)d\sigma.

Therefore we rewrite the left hand side of (

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}(-\Delta v)\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{1}\cup\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{2}\bigg(\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\nu_{1}+\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\nu_{2}\bigg)d\sigma

(2.15)

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial B_{1}\cup\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)d\sigma.

Let us focus on the boundary terms:
the homogeneous boundary condition in (

) yields
\nabla v=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\,\nu=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\,x
on

; accordingly

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{2}\bigg(\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\nu_{1}+\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\nu_{2}\bigg)d\sigma-\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)d\sigma=-\int_{\partial B_{1}}\bigg|\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\bigg|^{2}x_{1}x_{2}d\sigma.

on

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{2}\bigg(\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}\nu_{1}+\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\nu_{2}\bigg)d\sigma-\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)d\sigma

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla v|^{2}x_{1}x_{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}x_{1}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}x_{2}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}x_{2}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}x_{1}\bigg)d\sigma

as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
By combining the above two boundary estimates with (
2.15

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}(-\Delta v)\bigg(

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)dx

(2.16)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial B_{1}}\bigg|\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\bigg|^{2}x_{1}x_{2}d\sigma+2\pi\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}(0)\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}(0)+o(1)

as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\lambda V(x)e^{v}\bigg(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}x_{2}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}x_{1}\bigg)dx

(2.17)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\lambda V(x)\bigg(\frac{\partial e^{v}}{\partial x_{1}}x_{2}+\frac{\partial e^{v}}{\partial x_{2}}x_{1}\bigg)dx

2.16
) and (

2.2.
Uniform behaviour of blowing up solutions

In this section, we derive the asymptotic behaviour of blow-up solutions
v_{k}
for the problem (
1.10
)–(
1.11
). This result is a direct consequence of a uniform estimate established in
[
3
]
for bubbling solutions to the singular Liouville equation without boundary conditions and standard elliptic estimates theory.
Let
G
denote the Green’s function which takes for the unit ball the form

G(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{1}{|x-y|}+\frac{1}{4\pi}\log\big(1+|x|^{2}|y|^{2}-2\langle x,y\rangle\big)\hbox{ for }x,y\in B_{1},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let the potential
V>0
belong to the class
C^{1}(\overline{B}_{1})
and let
v_{k}\in C(\overline{B}_{1})
be a sequence of blow-up solutions to problem (
1.10
) satisfying (
1.11
). Then,
along a subsequence

\lambda_{k}|x|^{2}V(x)e^{v_{k}}\rightharpoonup 16\pi{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{0}

(2.18)

weakly in the measure sense, where
{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{0}
denotes the Dirac delta measure concentrated at the origin. Moreover,

v_{k}\longrightarrow 16\pi G(x,0)=8\log\frac{1}{|x|}

in
C^{1}(K)
for every compact set
K\subset\overline{B_{1}}\setminus\{0\}
.

2.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2
Part I: The necessary condition

In this section, we exploit the integral identities obtained in Propositions
2.1
-
2.2
in order to derive necessary conditions for the presence of bubbling solutions at
0
for problem (
1.10
)–(
1.11
).

In the following we assume that
v_{k}
is a sequence satisfying (
1.10
)–(
1.11
) and the potential
V
verifies (
1.12
)–(
1.13
).

We first establish a vanishing estimate for the gradient.

Proposition 2.4
.

The following holds:

\frac{\partial v_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}(0)=o(1),\quad i=1,2

as
k\to+\infty
.

Proof.

According to Proposition
2.3

v_{k}\longrightarrow 8\log\frac{1}{|x|}\quad\text{in }C^{1}(\partial B_{1}).

Then we deduce that

\frac{\partial v_{k}}{\partial\nu}(x)\to-8\hbox{ unif. for }x\in\partial B_{1}.

(2.19)

We have thus proved that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{1}}\bigg|\frac{\partial v_{k}}{\partial\nu}\bigg|^{2}x_{i}d\sigma

\displaystyle=64\int_{\partial B_{1}}x_{i}+o(1)=o(1).

Similarly

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{\partial v_{k}}{\partial\nu}x_{i}d\sigma

\displaystyle=-8\int_{\partial B_{1}}x_{i}d\sigma+o(1)=o(1).

Moreover, by (
2.18
) and using that
\nabla V(0)=0
(see (
1.12
)), we have

\lambda_{k}\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2}\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{i}}(x)e^{v_{k}}dx=o(1).

By inserting the above estimates into the identity provided by Proposition
2.1
we deduce that
\frac{\partial v_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}(0)=o(1)
for
i=1,2
.
∎

The next result is a further consequence of the Pohozaev identities established in Proposition
2.2
and, combined with the previous proposition, provides alternative integral estimates for blow-up solutions.

Proposition 2.5
.

The following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\int_{B_{1}}e^{v_{k}}\bigg(\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{1}}x_{1}-\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{2}}x_{2}\bigg)dx=o(1),

(2.20)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\int_{B_{1}}e^{v_{k}}\bigg(\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{1}}x_{2}+\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{2}}x_{1}\bigg)dx=o(1)

(2.21)

as
k\to+\infty
.

Proof.

Using the convergence (
2.19
) as

we get

\int_{\partial B_{1}}\bigg|\frac{\partial v_{k}}{\partial\nu}\bigg|^{2}(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})d\sigma=64\int_{\partial B_{1}}(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})d\sigma+o(1)=o(1)

thanks to
\int_{\partial B_{1}}(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})d\sigma=0
. On the other hand

\lambda_{k}\int_{\partial B_{1}}V(x)(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})d\sigma=O(\lambda_{k})=o(1).

Inserting the above two estimates into the first Pohozaev identity of Proposition
2.2
written for

and

we find

\int_{B_{1}}\lambda_{k}e^{v_{k}}\bigg(\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{1}}x_{1}-\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{2}}x_{2}\bigg)dx=-\pi\bigg|\frac{\partial v_{k}}{\partial x_{1}}(0)\bigg|^{2}+\pi\bigg|\frac{\partial v_{k}}{\partial x_{2}}(0)\bigg|^{2}.

Combining this identity with Proposition
2.4

(
2.20
).

The proof of (
2.21
) follows analogously from the second Pohozaev
identity in Proposition
2.2
.

∎

Now we are in the position to give the proof of the first part of Theorem
1.2
, concerning the necessary condition.

According to the expansion (
1.14
),

\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{1}}(x)x_{1}-\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{2}}(x)x_{2}=\gamma_{1}x_{1}^{2}-\gamma_{2}x_{2}^{2}+O(|x|^{3}),

and

\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{1}}(x)x_{2}+\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{2}}(x)x_{2}=(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})x_{1}x_{2}+O(|x|^{3}),

by which, using Proposition
2.3
, we derive

\lambda_{k}\int_{B_{1}}e^{v_{k}}\bigg(\frac{\partial V(x)}{\partial x_{1}}x_{1}-\frac{\partial V(x)}{\partial x_{2}}x_{2}\bigg)dx=\lambda_{k}\int_{B_{1}}(\gamma_{1}x_{1}^{2}-\gamma_{2}x_{2}^{2})e^{v_{k}}dx+o(1),

Therefore Proposition
2.5
applies and yields

\lambda_{k}\int_{B_{1}}(\gamma_{1}x_{1}^{2}-\gamma_{2}x_{2}^{2})e^{v_{k}}dx=o(1),

(2.22)

\lambda_{k}(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})\int_{B_{1}}x_{1}x_{2}e^{v_{k}}dx=o(1).

(2.23)

We claim that

\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}\neq 0.

(2.24)

Otherwise, by Proposition
2.3
we would obtain

\lambda_{k}\int_{B_{1}}(\gamma_{1}x_{1}^{2}-\gamma_{2}x_{2}^{2})e^{v_{k}(x)}dx=\gamma_{1}\lambda_{k}\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{2}e^{v_{k}}dx=16\pi\gamma_{1}+o(1).

Combining this estimate with (

), we would deduce
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}=0
, which contradicts the nondegeneracy at the critical point
0
.

Hence (
2.24
) holds, or, equivalently,
\Delta V(0)\neq 0
, which implies, by (
2.23
),

\int_{B_{1}}{\rm Im}(x^{2})e^{v_{k}}dx=2\int_{B_{1}}x_{1}x_{2}e^{v_{k}}dx=o(1).

(2.25)

Moreover, according to Theorem
1.1
, the sequence
v_{k}
must satisfy the simple blow-up property (
1.6
).
In this case, following the approach in
[
20
]
and
[
44
]
, one can perform a refined analysis of blow-up solutions based on Green’s representation formula and maximum principle argument, in order to sharpen the result given in Proposition
2.3
.
In particular one can prove that the solution

v_{k}
differs from a sequence of suitably scaled “standard bubbles” (
1.9

o(1)
term.
More precisely, let
\beta_{k}\in B_{1}
be the maximum point of

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and define

I(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{z_{1}}{|z|}\,\frac{1}{(1+|z-\xi|^{2})^{2}}\,dz.

Then
I\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

I
is odd and

I(\xi)=0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\xi_{1}=0,

and moreover

I(\xi_{1},0)\to\pi\ \text{ as }\xi_{1}\to+\infty,\qquad\partial_{\xi_{1}}I(\xi_{1},0)>0\;\;\forall\xi_{1}\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

We can write

I(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}z_{1}\,F(z_{1})\,dz_{2},

where

F(z_{1}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}}}\,\frac{1}{\bigl(1+(z_{1}-\xi_{1})^{2}+(z_{2}-\xi_{2})^{2}\bigr)^{2}}\,dz_{2}>0.

By symmetry we obtain

I(\xi)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}z_{1}\,[F(z_{1})-F(-z_{1})]\,dz_{1}.

Assume now that
\xi_{1}>0
. For every
z_{1}>0
and every
z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
we have

(z_{1}-\xi_{1})^{2}<(-z_{1}-\xi_{1})^{2},

which implies

\frac{1}{\bigl(1+(z_{1}-\xi_{1})^{2}+(z_{2}-\xi_{2})^{2}\bigr)^{2}}>\frac{1}{\bigl(1+(-z_{1}-\xi_{1})^{2}+(z_{2}-\xi_{2})^{2}\bigr)^{2}}.

Integrating with respect to
z_{2}
, we deduce that

F(z_{1})>F(-z_{1})\qquad\text{for every }z_{1}>0.

Therefore,

I(\xi)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}z_{1}\,[F(z_{1})-F(-z_{1})]\,dz_{1}>0.

The case
\xi_{1}<0
follows by the same argument with reversed inequalities,
while if
\xi_{1}=0
the function
F
is even and hence
I(\xi)=0
.
Let us consider

I(\xi_{1},0):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{z_{1}}{|z|}\,\frac{1}{(1+|z-(\xi_{1},0)|^{2})^{2}}\,dz=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{z_{1}+\xi_{1}}{|z+(\xi_{1},0)|}\,\frac{1}{(1+|z|^{2})^{2}}\,dz.

So by dominated convergence we get

I(\xi_{1},0)\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{(1+|z|^{2})^{2}}\,dz=\pi\hbox{ as }\xi_{1}\to+\infty.

Moreover

\partial_{\xi_{1}}I(\xi_{1},0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{z_{2}^{2}}{|z-(\xi_{1},0)|^{3}}\frac{1}{(1+|z|^{2})^{2}}\,dz>0.

Remark 2.8
.

We point out that if one attempts to apply our technique to
N\geq 2
, a major difficulty arises
from the role of the Pohozaev identity. Indeed, the analogous of Proposition
2.5
would yield

\lambda_{k}\int_{B_{1}}e^{v_{k}}\bigg(\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{1}}{\rm Re}\,(x^{N})-\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{2}}{\rm Im}(x^{N})\bigg)dx=o(1),

Observe that only in the case

can the above integrals be estimated effectively, whereas for

the limited information provided by the Pohozaev

This explains why our method, based on the combination of two Pohozaev identities,
fails to provide the necessary condition stated in Theorems

for
