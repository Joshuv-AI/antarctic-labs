Mesogenesis through the Ephemeral Dark Decay of Beauty
Hooman Davoudiasl, Rachel Houtz, Seyda Ipek

Abstract
Mesogenesis provides a path for generating the baryon asymmetry of the Universe, using only the CP violation furnished by the Standard Model in the decay of B B mesons. While this is an intriguing possibility, it is largely constrained by the data on B B meson branching fractions into baryons and missing energy carried into the dark sector. We point out that it is possible to make this branching fraction dominant only in the early Universe, through an ultralight scalar coupled to the dark sector and the Standard Model leptons. A scenario is examined where the thermal density of muons in the early Universe temporarily lowers the mass of a dark fermion, allowing for efficient B B meson decays. This ‘dark’ decay channel is shut off later when the muon number density falls, making the scenario compatible with flavor data. Our model can be consistent with the LHC constraints on color-charged heavy bosons required to implement Mesogenesis; such states may be discovered in the future runs as their masses cannot be far above the current bounds. We also outline other possible signals, which can arise in future flavor data, long range force searches, and observations of neutron star binary mergers.

II
Mesogenesis: a brief outline

We start with a brief summary of the Mesogenesis mechanism
Elahi et al. 2022
;
Elor et al. 2019
;
Alonso-Álvarez et al. 2020
. In general terms, Mesogenesis can be described by dim-6 effective operators between SM quarks and a new dark fermion
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
carrying anti-baryon number,
\mathcal{B}=-1
, giving rise to a (
\mathcal{B}
-conserving) effective operator:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{d_{k},u_{i}d_{j}}=\mathcal{C}_{d_{k},u_{i}d_{j}}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(\bar{\psi}_{\mathcal{B}}d_{k}^{\alpha})(\bar{d}_{j}^{c\beta}u_{i}^{\gamma})\,,

(2)

where
i,j
and
\alpha,\,\beta,\,\gamma
are flavor and color indices, respectively. Note that in order to kinematically forbid proton decay through the
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}udd
interaction, we require all dark sector baryons to have a mass
\gtrsim 1\,\text{GeV}

A straightforward UV model that gives rise to

2

\mathcal{Y}
carrying electric charge
-1/3

\mathcal{B}=-2/3

\mathcal{Y}

\displaystyle\hskip-13.00806pt\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{Y}}=-\sum_{i,j}y_{u_{i}d_{j}}\mathcal{Y}^{\star}\bar{u}_{iR}d_{jR}^{c}-\sum_{k}y_{\psi d_{k}}\bar{\psi}_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{Y}d_{kR}^{c}+\text{h.c.}

(3)

Of course, just as squarks, any color-triplet scalar is typically constrained by the LHC to be well above the weak scale
\sim 100
GeV. Let us first explain the generation of the baryon asymmetry. Afterwards, we will discuss the collider and
B
-physics constraints which will then guide us in choosing which specific operators to use among
\mathcal{O}_{d_{k},u_{i}d_{j}}

The model first requires a scalar particle
\Phi

T_{d}<T_{\rm QCD}\sim 100

2

\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
decays into the stable dark matter (DM). This is discussed in
Section

III

;
Alonso-Álvarez et al. 2020

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!Y_{\mathcal{B}}\simeq 5\times 10^{-5}\,\sum_{i=d,s}\bigl[\text{Br}\left(B_{i}^{0}\rightarrow\bar{\psi}_{\mathcal{B}}\,\mathcal{B}_{\rm SM}\right)A_{sl}^{i}\bigr]\alpha_{i}(T_{d})\,,

(4)

where

\displaystyle A_{sl}^{d}|_{\rm SM}=(-4.7\pm 0.4)\times 10^{-4}\,,

(5a)

\displaystyle A_{sl}^{s}|_{\rm SM}=(2.1\pm 0.2)\times 10^{-5}\,,

(5b)

are the semileptonic asymmetries in
B^{0}

Lenz and Tetlalmatzi-Xolocotzi 2020

B

\Phi

B
-meson density matrix in the early Universe

From

1

eq.

4

5

\mathcal{C}_{d_{k},u_{i}d_{j}}

One way to utilize the SM CP violation in Mesogenesis while still being safe from branching fraction constraints is to only allow the new
B

between

\displaystyle M_{\mathcal{Y}}\lesssim 620~{\rm GeV}\sqrt{y_{\psi s}y_{cb}}~~~{\rm(successful~Mesogenesis)}\,,

(6)

\mathcal{Y}

-triplet scalar could be allowed.

III
A model for cosmological evolution of dark baryon mass

In this section, we provide a simple model for making the mass of the dark sector fermion
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
dependent on the ambient SM particle density. In particular, we will make
m_{\psi}
dependent on the number density of charged leptons
\ell=e,\mu,\tau
. Given the typical temperatures for Mesogenesis
T_{\rm M}\sim\mathcal{O}{(\rm 10~MeV)}
, this is mostly achieved via the
e^{\pm}
and
\mu^{\pm}
thermal populations. To do this, we introduce an ultralight scalar
\varphi
of mass
m_{\varphi}
that couples to
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
and charged leptons

(m_{\psi}^{0}+g_{\psi}\,\varphi)\bar{\psi}_{\mathcal{B}}\psi_{\mathcal{B}}+\sum_{\ell=e,\mu,\tau}g_{\ell}\,\varphi\,\bar{\ell}\ell\,,

(9)

where
g_{\psi}
and
g_{\ell}
are the couplings of
\varphi
to
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
and
\ell
, respectively. Here,
m_{\psi}^{0}
is the mass of
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
in vacuum, corresponding to
\varphi=0
. Given the above setup, the mass of
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
would have a dependence on
\varphi
via

m_{\psi}=m_{\psi}^{0}+g_{\psi}\,\varphi\,.

(10)

We would like to lower the mass of
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
for
T_{\rm M}\sim 10
MeV so that
\text{Br}\left(B_{i}^{0}\rightarrow\bar{\psi}_{\mathcal{B}}\,\mathcal{B}_{\rm SM}\right)
can proceed effectively during Mesogenesis, but the mass later reaches its larger vacuum value, shutting off these decays after Mesogenesis is over. To achieve a reduction of the
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
mass, we assume
g_{\psi},\,g_{\ell}>0
. We note that
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
eventually decays to DM particles – a scalar
\phi
and a fermion
\xi
– via an interaction

y_{\psi}\,\phi\,\bar{\psi}_{\mathcal{B}}\,\xi+{\rm h.c.}

(11)

Through these interactions,
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
can decay to the dark sector faster than washout processes that would repopulate
B
mesons. To see this, note that the Hubble rate is given by
H(T)=\sqrt{8\pi^{3}g_{*}(T)/90}\,T^{2}/M_{\rm P}
, where
g_{*}(T)
is the number of relativistic degrees of freedom at temperature
T
and
M_{\rm P}\approx 1.22\times 10^{19}
GeV is the Planck mass.
T\sim 10
MeV corresponds to timescales of
\mathcal{O}{(\rm 0.01~s)}
. Hence, for a
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
of mass
\mathcal{O}{(\rm GeV)}
, it only needs to couple with a strength
y_{\psi}\gg 10^{-10}
to DM final states in order to decay fast on the relevant cosmological time scales. Note that these interactions also can facilitate
B
meson decays into the dark sector today, through an off-shell
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
. This will be discussed in
Section

IV
.

The initial value of
\varphi
is sourced by the lepton thermal population and once the Hubble rate falls roughly below
m_{\varphi}/3
, the scalar begins to oscillate and its amplitude redshifts as
T^{3/2}
. As
\varphi
changes, the mass of the dark fermion
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
also changes. Since we require that
m_{\psi}
stays small until
T\lesssim 10
MeV, we need the
\varphi
oscillations to be delayed until after Mesogenesis as well. Therefore, the mass of
\varphi
should generally be
m_{\varphi}\sim O(10^{-13}~{\rm eV})
, making
\varphi
an ultralight scalar, with a Compton wavelength exceeding
\sim 10^{3}
km. For lighter
\varphi
, the oscillations start later and hence the increase in
m_{\psi}
happens later as well. This might result in a period of time where
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
becomes lighter than the proton allowing proton decay. This effect can give a lower bound on the
\varphi
mass. To be conservative, we will take
m_{\varphi}\sim O(10^{-13}~{\rm eV})
as a safe benchmark value.

Thermal evolution of
\varphi
is governed by its coupling to particles in the primordial plasma. In our case, these are charged leptons
\ell
. In the regime where
\ell
is relativistic, the evolution of
\varphi
is given by (see, for example, Ref.

(12)

where

M_{*}\equiv\frac{M_{P}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{90}{8\pi^{3}g_{*}(T)}}\,.

(13)

The corresponding equation for a non-relativistic lepton
\ell
is

\frac{T^{6}}{4M_{*}^{2}}\,\frac{d^{2}\varphi}{dT^{2}}+\left[m_{\varphi}^{2}-\frac{g_{\ell}^{2}n_{0\ell}}{T}\left(1-\frac{3T}{2m_{\ell}}\right)\right]\varphi+g_{\ell}\,n_{0\ell}=0\,,

(14)

where

n_{0\ell}=g\,\left(\frac{m_{\ell}T}{2\pi}\right)^{3/2}e^{-m_{\ell}/T}\,,

(15)

with
g=4
for a Dirac fermion and its anti-fermion conjugate (
\ell,\bar{\ell})
. Technically, in these equations of motion,
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
interactions and decays could also be included. However, as discussed above,
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
decays to DM particles very fast compared to the other time scales. After these decays, there will be a non-thermal density of
\xi

\phi

IV
Possible Signals

In this section we discuss possible signals for this mechanism beyond LHC searches for color-triplet scalars. We focus instead on signals for the dark sector, such as missing energy in
B
meson decays, as well as long-range muonic forces arising from the ultralight scalar. Athough we will not discuss it here, another signal can come from the physics facilitating the annihilation of the symmetric population of DM.

IV.1
Exotic
B

In previous works on Mesogenesis, where
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
is light, exotic decays of
B
mesons were extensively studied. The branching fraction that can be constrained is given as
Elor et al. 2019

\displaystyle{\rm Br}

\displaystyle(B\to\psi_{\mathcal{B}}+{\rm baryon})\simeq

(18)

\displaystyle 0.5\,(y_{cb}y_{\psi s})^{2}\left(\frac{m_{B}-m_{\psi}}{3~{\rm GeV}}\right)^{4}\left(\frac{600~{\rm GeV}}{m_{\mathcal{Y}}}\right)^{4}\,.

Belle II has the potential to probe branching fractions
{\rm Br}(B\to\psi_{\mathcal{B}}+{\rm baryon})\sim 3\times 10^{-6}

Alonso-Álvarez et al. 2021
.

When the mass of the
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
is too heavy to be a final state in a
B\to{\rm inv.}+\mathcal{B}_{c}
decay, there is still a possibility of the three-body
B\to\psi_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}+\mathcal{B}_{c}\to\xi\,\phi+\mathcal{B}_{c}
, where the
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}
is off-shell. (Here
\mathcal{B}_{c}
is a charm baryon.) Compared to the above estimate, this decay rate will be suppressed by
y_{\psi}^{2}
, an extra phase space factor of
8\pi^{2}
and a factor of
(m_{B}/m_{\psi}^{0})^{2}\lesssim 1
. [Note also that the mass difference
(m_{B}-m_{\psi})
will be replaced by
m_{B}-(m_{\xi}+m_{\phi})
.] Although these suppressions are quite significant, for
y_{\psi}\gtrsim 10^{-2}
, the aforementioned 3-body decays could be measurable in the future.

We note that, for values of
y_{\psi}\lesssim 1
, one may need to consider a different set of benchmark parameters such that induced
\varphi
couplings to DM in

17
are sufficiently small, in order to avoid a large back-reaction on the misalignment of
\varphi
. To see this, let us assume the initial decaying modulus
\Phi
, which produced the required
B

T_{\rm M}\sim 10

\text{Br}(\Phi\to B\bar{B})
=1. We then roughly have

m_{B}\,n_{B}\sim T_{M}^{4}\Rightarrow n_{B}\sim 10^{-3}\,T_{M}^{3}\,.

(19)

Given that we want an

fraction of
B

n_{\xi}\sim 10^{-3}\,T_{M}^{3}

To make sure that the initial population of DM (before annihilation down to its asymmetric relic density) does not dominate the misalignment of
\varphi

g_{\psi}\,\delta\varphi\lesssim m_{\psi}^{0}\Rightarrow\frac{g_{\psi}\,g_{\xi}\,n_{\xi}}{m_{\varphi}^{2}}\lesssim m_{\psi}^{0}\,,

where

eV and

19

g_{\psi}\,y_{\psi}\lesssim 10^{-17}\,.

Hence, for

IV.2
Long Range Muonic Force Probes

For values of
g_{\mu}\sim 10^{-18}
, experiments searching for new long range forces can in principle be sensitive to our parameter space
Bergé et al. 2018
;
Fayet 2018
;
Touboul et al. 2022
;
Davoudiasl and Giardino 2019
.
An intriguing possibility is that a long-range muonic force can be probed by gravitational wave signal resulting from neutron star mergers
Dror et al. 2020
;
Liu and Tang 2025
. This is due to the presence of large numbers of muons,
N_{\mu}\sim 10^{55}
(see, for example, Ref.
Garani and Heeck 2019
) in the dense environment of a neutron star. It is estimated that LIGO could probe long-range muonic forces with couplings below
10^{-20}
, but a robust bound is subject to LIGO analysis.

A question related to the above setup is to what extent one can expect the mass of
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
to be lowered within a neutron star due to the significant muon abundance in the stellar environment. This could in principle lead to proton decay, if
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
becomes lighter than a nucleon. A simple estimate for the background value of the scalar in the star is

\varphi_{\rm NS}\sim\frac{g_{\mu}\,N_{\mu}}{4\pi R}\sim g_{\mu}10^{37}~\text{MeV}\,,

(22)

where
R\sim 10
km is a typical length scale for the neutron star.

Since the mass of
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
is given by
m_{\psi}^{0}+g_{\psi}\varphi
, we see that for
m_{\psi}^{0}=7
GeV, we need

g_{\mu}g_{\psi}\lesssim 10^{-33}\,,

(23)

assuming
g_{\mu},\,g_{\psi}>0
, in order to have
m_{\psi}\gtrsim 1
GeV. Therefore, our benchmark values, used in
fig.

2
, would not lead to the possibility of nucleon decay in a neutron star.

V
Conclusions

What is the SM CP violation good for? Although one of the fundamental conditions for creating a baryon asymmetry is the existence of CP violation in a theory, the effects of the CKM phase get suppressed in electroweak baryogenesis models.
Mesogenesis with a morphing mediator
model
Elor et al. 2025
is the only model that utilizes the observed SM CP violation in
B
meson systems in order to create the baryon asymmetry of the Universe.

Mesogenesis models mainly rely on effective operators given in
eq.

2
which can arise from the UV interactions in
eq.

3
between SM quarks and a dark sector fermion
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
, which carries baryon number, mediated by an
SU(3)
triplet scalar
\mathcal{Y}
. On the one hand, in order for the SM CP violation to be enough, the effective interactions need to be sufficiently large. On the other hand, large enough interactions with quarks are heavily constrained by the LHC and, specifically for Mesogenesis, by
B
factories. In order to alleviate these constraints, previous work
Elor et al. 2025
explored a mechanism where the mediator mass changes from
\sim\!600
GeV to a TeV after the baryon asymmetry is created. However, this realization of Mesogenesis, where the baryon asymmetry is created in certain domains with a light mediator between the SM and a dark sector, is argued to suffer from BBN constraints due to inhomogeneities
Bagherian et al. 2026
.

In this work, we introduced an alternative mechanism to realize Mesogenesis using still only the SM CP violation. Instead of focusing on the mediator, we developed a model in which the dark sector fermion
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
, in vacuum, is heavier than the
B
meson but becomes temporarily light enough to allow for Mesogenesis in the early Universe. In this scenario, since
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
is heavy currently, exotic
B
meson decay channels are shut off at colliders. This mass evolution is realized via couplings to an ultralight scalar whose initial field value is driven by the thermal number density of SM charged leptons
Croon et al. 2022a
. As benchmark values, we show that a scalar with mass
\mathcal{O}{(10^{-13}~{\rm eV})}
and coupling constants of
10^{-10}-10^{-24}
are able to realize this scenario. In this work, we focused on muon-induced thermal misalignment, but one could alternatively employ electrons, for different choices of parameters.

Here, we also included multiple terms from the Mesogenesis Lagrangian in
eq.

3
in order to dilute the branching fraction of the mediator
\mathcal{Y}
into quark and missing energy final states. Successful Mesogenesis, if we insist on using only the SM
CP
violation, requires
M_{\mathcal{Y}}\lesssim 600
GeV. We examined several relevant LHC searches to argue that with couplings
y_{cb}\sim 1,y_{tb}\sim 5
such a light
SU(3)
triplet scalar could still be viable. These are very sizable couplings, giving rise to a broad resonance. It is interesting to explore further search strategies at the LHC for such a scenario, which we leave for future work. However, we expect that the underlying physics mediator
\mathcal{Y}
would be within the reach of the LHC, as more data get analyzed and accumulated.

Finally, even though our model shuts off direct
B
meson decays to the dark fermion
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
+ baryons, there can still be three body decays via off-shell
\psi_{\mathcal{B}}
decays. This possibility would require adopting benchmark parameters different from those considered here. Hence, there still can be relevant searches in
B
factories to probe this scenario. The ultralight scalar can also be uncovered in terrestrial searches for long-range forces as well as neutron star mergers.
