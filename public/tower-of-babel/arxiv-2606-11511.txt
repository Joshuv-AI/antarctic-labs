Convergence of a Critical Multitype Bellman–Harris Process with One Infinite-Mean Lifetime
J.H. Ramírez-González, Fabio Prates Machado

Abstract
. We study a critical multitype Bellman–Harris branching particle system in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} with a finite type space 𝐊 = { 1 , … , K } \mathbf{K}=\{1,\dots,K\} . Particles of type i i move according to a symmetric α i \alpha_{i} -stable process and reproduce according to a critical offspring law whose mean matrix is irreducible and stochastic. The lifetime distribution of type 1 1 is assumed to have infinite mean with regularly varying tail 1 − F 1 ​ ( t ) ∼ c 1 ​ t − γ , 0 < γ < 1 , 1-F_{1}(t)\sim c_{1}t^{-\gamma},\qquad 0<\gamma<1, whereas the remaining lifetime distributions satisfy polynomial upper-tail bounds F ¯ i ( t ) ≤ C t − η i , i = 2 , … , K , η i > 1 , η := min 2 ≤ i ≤ K η i . \overline{F}_{i}(t)\leq Ct^{-\eta_{i}},\qquad i=2,\dots,K,\qquad\eta_{i}>1,\qquad\eta:=\min_{2\leq i\leq K}\eta_{i}. The branching mechanism is assumed to be in the domain of attraction of a ( 1 + β ) (1+\beta) -stable law, with β ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \beta\in(0,1] . Under the space–lifetime condition ρ := ( η − 1 ) ∧ N α 1 > γ β , \rho:=\left(\eta-1\right)\wedge\frac{N}{\alpha_{1}}>\frac{\gamma}{\beta}, and a local increment condition on the heavy lifetime distribution, we prove convergence of the system to a Poisson random measure concentrated on the infinite-mean type.

1.
Introduction and previous work

This paper studies the long-time behaviour of critical branching particle
systems, viewed as random measures. Let
E
be a locally compact state space,
and let
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be an
\mathcal{M}(E)
-valued particle system, where

\mathcal{M}(E)
denotes the space of nonnegative Radon measures on
E
, endowed
with the vague topology. For
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(E)
and a measurable function

\varphi:E\to\mathbb{R}
, we use the notation

\langle\mu,\varphi\rangle:=\int_{E}\varphi\,d\mu.

We say that the system survives in the limiting sense if

X_{t}\Rightarrow X_{\infty},\qquad t\to\infty,

for some random measure
X_{\infty}\in\mathcal{M}(E)
satisfying

\mathbb{P}\{X_{\infty}\neq 0\}>0.

We say that the system undergoes local extinction if

\langle X_{t},\varphi\rangle\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{P}0,\qquad\varphi\in C_{c}^{+}(E).

When the limiting random measure preserves the initial mean intensity, the
system is usually said to persist with full intensity.

For monotype critical branching particle systems in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, a basic reference
point is the Markovian model studied by
Gorostiza and Wakolbinger 1991
. In that model, the initial
population is a homogeneous Poisson random measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with intensity
proportional to Lebesgue measure. Each particle moves according to a symmetric

\alpha
-stable process,
0<\alpha\leq 2
, branches after an exponential
lifetime, and has offspring generating function

H(s)=s+\frac{1}{2}(1-s)^{1+\beta},\qquad 0<\beta\leq 1.

Here
\alpha
is the index of the spatial motion and
\beta
is the
offspring parameter appearing in the branching law. For this system,
Gorostiza and Wakolbinger 1991

proved local extinction when

N\leq\frac{\alpha}{\beta},

and convergence to a nonzero equilibrium of full intensity when

N>\frac{\alpha}{\beta}.

The case of general non-arithmetic lifetimes was treated by
Vatutin and Wakolbinger 1999
. In that
setting, the initial population is again a homogeneous Poisson random measure
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Particles move according to a symmetric
\alpha
-stable process,
but their lifetimes are no longer exponential. In the finite-mean case, the
same critical dimension
N=\alpha/\beta
appears. If the lifetime tail is
regularly varying with exponent
0<\gamma\leq 1
, the corresponding threshold is

N=\frac{\alpha\gamma}{\beta}.

Thus, in the monotype model, the lifetime tail changes the critical dimension
through the exponent
\gamma
.

We now recall the multitype setting. Consider a branching population in

\mathbb{R}^{N}
with finite type space

\mathbf{K}=\{1,\dots,K\}.

A particle of type
i
moves according to a symmetric
\alpha_{i}
-stable
motion, has lifetime distribution
F_{i}
, and branches according to a
multitype offspring generating function
f_{i}
. Let

M=(m_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{K}

be the mean offspring matrix, with entries

m_{i,j}:=\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial s_{j}}(\mathbf{1}).

Throughout the paper,
M
is assumed to be irreducible and stochastic. Hence
the system is critical,
u=\mathbf{1}
is the right Perron–Frobenius eigenvector
corresponding to the eigenvalue
1
, and the left eigenvector
v
is the
stationary distribution of
M
.

For multitype Bellman–Harris systems, the extinction side was studied in

Kevei and López-Mimbela 2011
. In that work, the mean offspring matrix is irreducible and
stochastic, and the offspring mechanism satisfies

x-\langle v,1-\mathbf{f}(1-ux)\rangle\sim x^{1+\beta}L(x),\qquad x\downarrow 0,

where
L
is slowly varying at zero and
0<\beta\leq 1
. Under this
assumption, together with suitable assumptions on the lifetime tails,

Kevei and López-Mimbela 2011
proves local extinction in the dimensional regimes considered
there. These results include the finite-mean case and mixed cases in which one
type has a heavy-tailed lifetime and the remaining types have finite mean.

The persistence side in the finite-mean multitype case was treated in

Kolkovska et al. 2026
. There, all lifetime distributions are non-arithmetic and
have finite mean, the spatial motions may depend on the type, and the offspring
law satisfies the same regular-variation condition. The initial population is a
Poisson random measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbf{K}
, with homogeneous spatial intensity
and type weights given by the stationary occupation proportions of the
associated finite-mean type process. Under an occupation-time estimate, it is
proved that the system converges to a nonzero limiting random measure and
preserves the initial spatial intensity. This gives a persistence result
corresponding to the finite-mean extinction theorem.

The present paper considers the mixed case in which one lifetime has infinite
mean. We assume that type
1
has a regularly varying lifetime tail,

1-F_{1}(t)\sim c_{1}t^{-\gamma},\qquad t\to\infty,\qquad c_{1}>0,\quad 0<\gamma<1,

while the lifetimes of types
2,\dots,K
have finite mean and satisfy
polynomial upper-tail bounds. In this setting, the limiting population is
concentrated on the infinite-mean type, rather than distributed among all types
as in the finite-mean case.

Our main theorem proves convergence for homogeneous Poisson initial populations
with arbitrary nonnegative type weights. More precisely, the initial population
is a Poisson random measure on

S=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbf{K}

with intensity

\Lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{K}a_{i}\,\lambda\otimes\delta_{i},

where
a_{i}\geq 0
and
\lambda
denotes Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We write

A:=\sum_{i=1}^{K}a_{i}.

For the finite-mean lifetimes we assume

\overline{F}_{i}(t)\leq Ct^{-\eta_{i}},\qquad i=2,\dots,K,\qquad\eta_{i}>1,

where
\overline{F}_{i}(t):=1-F_{i}(t)
, and set

\eta:=\min_{2\leq i\leq K}\eta_{i}.

Under the condition

\rho:=(\eta-1)\wedge\frac{N}{\alpha_{1}}>\frac{\gamma}{\beta},

and under a local regularity assumption on the heavy lifetime distribution, we
prove that

X_{t}\Rightarrow X_{\infty},\qquad t\to\infty.

The limit is a Poisson random measure on
S
with intensity

A\lambda\otimes\delta_{1}.

Thus the initial type distribution is not preserved. Instead, the limiting
measure is supported on the infinite-mean type and has total spatial intensity

A\lambda
. In particular, if
A>0
, then the limiting population is nonzero
almost surely. This result complements the mixed-regime extinction results of

Kevei and López-Mimbela 2011
.

The proof uses a renewal theorem for matrix renewal systems with one
infinite-mean component. The matrix system is reduced to the scalar renewal
process obtained from return cycles of the embedded type chain to type
1
.
The length of one return cycle has the same tail as the type-
1
lifetime,
whereas excursions through the finite-mean types are of lower order. This
reduction gives the limiting behavior of the renewal system and identifies the
type supporting the limiting measure.

The renewal estimates depend on the value of
\gamma
. If
\gamma>1/2
, the
polynomial remainder estimates needed in the proof follow from the
infinite-mean renewal theorem of
Anderson and Athreya 1987
. If
0<\gamma\leq 1/2
, these
estimates are not a consequence of regular variation alone. In that range, we
assume a renewal-negligibility condition for the return-cycle renewal measure,
as in strong renewal theory; see
Caravenna and Doney 2019
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
defines the mixed
Bellman–Harris system, states the local regularity assumption on the heavy
lifetime distribution, and presents the convergence theorem together with a
lifetime-level sufficient condition for the additional renewal-negligibility
condition required in the range
0<\gamma\leq 1/2
. Section
3

proves the convergence theorem and the sufficient condition for the return-cycle
renewal measure. Section
4
contains the auxiliary renewal
results, including the local hitting estimate, the Stieltjes convolution
estimates, and the mixed finite–infinite mean renewal theorem. Finally,
Section
5
gives a density criterion implying
Assumption
2.1
and provides examples of lifetime
distributions satisfying the assumptions.

2.
The model and main results

2.1.
The model

Let
K\geq 2
and set

\mathbf{K}:=\{1,\dots,K\},\qquad S:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbf{K}.

The population at time
t\geq 0
is represented by a locally finite counting
measure

X_{t}=\sum_{k}\delta_{(X_{t}^{k},K_{t}^{k})}\in\mathcal{M}(S),

where
X_{t}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the position of the
k
-th particle alive at time

t
, and
K_{t}^{k}\in\mathbf{K}
is its type.

2.1.1.
Motion

A particle of type
i\in\mathbf{K}
moves according to a symmetric
\alpha_{i}
-stable
process in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, started from its birth position. The indices satisfy

\alpha_{i}\in(0,2],\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

The motions of distinct particles are independent, and are also independent of
lifetimes and offspring variables.

2.1.2.
Lifetimes

The lifetime of a type-
i
particle has distribution function
F_{i}
on

[0,\infty)
with
F_{i}(0)=0
,
i=1,...,K
. We write

\overline{F}_{i}(t):=1-F_{i}(t),\qquad t\geq 0.

The type-
1
lifetime is assumed to have infinite mean and a regularly varying
tail:

(2.1)

\overline{F}_{1}(t)\sim c_{1}t^{-\gamma},\qquad t\to\infty,\qquad c_{1}>0,\quad 0<\gamma<1.

The remaining lifetimes have finite mean and polynomial upper tails. More
precisely, for
i=2,\dots,K
,

(2.2)

\overline{F}_{i}(t)\leq Ct^{-\eta_{i}},\qquad t>0,

where
\eta_{i}>1
. We set

(2.3)

\eta:=\min_{2\leq i\leq K}\eta_{i}>1.

and define

(2.4)

\rho:=\left(\eta-1\right)\wedge\frac{N}{\alpha_{1}}.

All lifetimes are independent of each other and of all motions and offspring
variables.

2.1.3.
Branching mechanism

At the end of its lifetime, a type-
i
particle is replaced at its death
position by an offspring vector

\zeta_{i}=(\zeta_{i,1},\dots,\zeta_{i,K})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{K},

where
\zeta_{i,j}
is the number of type-
j
children. Given their types and
birth positions, all offspring particles evolve independently of one another and
independently of the past. The generating function of the offspring vector of a
type-
i
particle is

f_{i}(s):=\mathbb{E}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{K}s_{j}^{\zeta_{i,j}}\right),\qquad s=(s_{1},\dots,s_{K})\in[0,1]^{K}.

Let
\mathbf{f}:=(f_{1},\dots,f_{K})
, and let
M=(m_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{K}
be the mean
offspring matrix,

m_{i,j}:=\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial s_{j}}(\mathbf{1}),\qquad\mathbf{1}=(1,\dots,1).

We assume that
M
is irreducible and stochastic, and that

(2.5)

m_{i,i}>0,\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

Thus the system is critical. Let
\pi=(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{K})
be the stationary
distribution of
M
:

\pi M=\pi,\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{K}\pi_{i}=1,\qquad\pi_{i}>0.

Since
M
is stochastic, we set

u:=\mathbf{1},\qquad v:=\pi.

Then
Mu=u
,
vM=v
, and
\langle v,u\rangle=1
. We assume that, for some

\beta\in(0,1]
and some positive slowly varying function
L
at zero,

(2.6)

x-\langle v,1-\mathbf{f}(1-ux)\rangle\sim x^{1+\beta}L(x),\qquad x\downarrow 0.

2.1.4.
Initial configuration

We consider a Poisson initial population on
S
with homogeneous spatial
intensity and arbitrary nonnegative type weights:

(2.7)

\Lambda:=\sum_{i=1}^{K}a_{i}\,\lambda\otimes\delta_{i},\qquad a_{i}\geq 0,

where
\lambda
denotes Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The initial population is
independent of all motions, lifetimes, and offspring variables. We write

(2.8)

A:=\sum_{i=1}^{K}a_{i}.

2.1.5.
Regularity of the heavy lifetime

The tail asymptotic (
2.1
) alone controls only the total mass
of
F_{1}
beyond time
t
. In the nonlinear renewal equation we also need to
control Stieltjes convolutions against
F_{1}
. For this reason we impose the
following local regularity condition.

Assumption 2.1
.

The type-
1
lifetime distribution
F_{1}
is non-arithmetic and satisfies
(
2.1
). Moreover, there exist constants
C>0
and
t_{0}>0

such that

(2.9)

F_{1}(x+y)-F_{1}(x)\leq Cy(1+x)^{-1-\gamma},\qquad x\geq t_{0},\qquad 0\leq y\leq x.

2.1.6.
Mixed renewal framework

The following assumption records the renewal framework associated with the
embedded type chain. In the range
0<\gamma\leq 1/2
, it also includes the
additional renewal-negligibility condition required for the return-cycle
renewal measure.

Assumption 2.2
.

Let
M=(m_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{K}
be an irreducible stochastic matrix satisfying
(
2.5
). Let
F_{1},\dots,F_{K}
be proper distribution
functions on
[0,\infty)
such that

F_{i}(0)=0,\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

Define the matrix of renewal kernels by

F_{i,j}(t):=m_{i,j}F_{i}(t),\qquad i,j=1,\dots,K.

Assume that
F_{1}
is non-arithmetic and satisfies

\overline{F}_{1}(t):=1-F_{1}(t)\sim ct^{-\gamma},\qquad t\to\infty,

for some
c>0
and
0<\gamma<1
. Assume also that, for
i=2,\dots,K
,

\overline{F}_{i}(t):=1-F_{i}(t)\leq Ct^{-\eta_{i}},\qquad t>0,

where
\eta_{i}>1
. Set
\eta=\min_{2\leq i\leq K}\eta_{i}
.

Let
(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be the Markov chain on
\mathbf{K}
with transition matrix

M
. Conditional on
(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, let the holding times

(\tau_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be independent, and let
\tau_{n}
have distribution

F_{Y_{n}}
. Define

S_{0}:=0,\qquad S_{n}:=\tau_{0}+\cdots+\tau_{n-1},\qquad n\geq 1.

Under
\mathbb{P}_{1}
, that is, with
Y_{0}=1
, define

\sigma_{0}:=0,\qquad\sigma_{n+1}:=\inf\{m>\sigma_{n}:Y_{m}=1\},\qquad n\geq 0,

and define the first return-cycle length to type
1
by

C:=S_{\sigma_{1}}.

Let
U_{C}
be the renewal measure associated with i.i.d. cycle lengths

C_{1},C_{2},\ldots
, each distributed as
C
under
\mathbb{P}_{1}
. If
0<\gamma\leq 1/2
, assume that,
for every
\kappa>\gamma
,

(2.10)

\int_{[0,t]}(1+t-s)^{-\kappa}\,U_{C}(ds)\longrightarrow 0,\qquad t\to\infty.

2.1.7.
Laplace functionals

For
\varphi\in C_{c}^{+}(S)
, define

W_{t}(x,i):=\mathbb{E}_{(x,i)}\left(e^{-\langle X_{t},\varphi\rangle}\right),\qquad V_{t}(x,i):=1-W_{t}(x,i).

We also write

(2.11)

G_{i}(t):=\|V_{t}(\cdot,i)\|_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V_{t}(x,i)\,dx,\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

By the Poisson exponential formula,

(2.12)

\mathbb{E}\left(e^{-\langle X_{t},\varphi\rangle}\right)=\exp\left\{-\sum_{i=1}^{K}a_{i}G_{i}(t)\right\}.

2.2.
Main Results

Theorem 2.3
.

Consider the critical multitype Bellman–Harris branching particle system
specified in Section
2
, and assume in particular that

F_{i}(0)=0,\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

Assume (
2.1
), (
2.2
),
(
2.5
), and (
2.6
). Assume also
that the type-
1
lifetime satisfies
Assumption
2.1
. Let
C
and
U_{C}
be the
return-cycle length and the associated renewal measure defined in
Assumption
2.2
. If
0<\gamma\leq 1/2
, assume in
addition the renewal-negligibility condition (
2.10
). If

\gamma>1/2
, no additional renewal-negligibility condition is imposed.
Finally, assume the space–lifetime condition

(2.13)

\rho>\frac{\gamma}{\beta}.

Then, for every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{+}(S)
,

(2.14)

\lim_{t\to\infty}G_{i}(t)=G_{1}(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(1-e^{-\varphi(x,1)}\right)dx,\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

Consequently, for the Poisson initial population with intensity
(
2.7
),

(2.15)

\mathbb{E}\left(e^{-\langle X_{t},\varphi\rangle}\right)\longrightarrow\exp\left\{-A\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(1-e^{-\varphi(x,1)}\right)dx\right\},\qquad t\to\infty,

where
A=\sum_{i=1}^{K}a_{i}
. In particular,

X_{t}\Rightarrow X_{\infty},\qquad t\to\infty,

where
X_{\infty}
is a Poisson random measure on
S
with intensity

A\lambda\otimes\delta_{1}.

If
A>0
, then
\mathbb{P}\{X_{\infty}\neq 0\}=1
.

The next proposition gives a lifetime-level sufficient condition for the
return-cycle renewal-negligibility condition required in the range

0<\gamma\leq 1/2
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume
0<\gamma\leq 1/2
. Let
M=(m_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{K}
be an irreducible
stochastic matrix satisfying (
2.5
). Let

T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(K)}
be lifetime random variables with distribution
functions
F_{1},\dots,F_{K}
, respectively, such that
F_{i}(0)=0
,

i=1,\dots,K
.

Assume that the type-
1
lifetime distribution satisfies
Assumption
2.1
, and that the finite-mean lifetimes
satisfy (
2.2
). Assume in addition that the finite-mean
lifetimes have a common positive exponential moment; that is, there exists

\theta_{0}>0
such that

(2.16)

\mathbb{E}\bigl(e^{\theta_{0}T^{(i)}}\bigr)<\infty,\qquad i=2,\dots,K.

Let
(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be the Markov chain with transition matrix
M
, started
from
Y_{0}=1
, and, conditional on
(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, let the holding times
have distributions
F_{Y_{n}}
. Let
C
be the first return-cycle length to
type

, and let
U_{C}
be the renewal measure associated with i.i.d.

. Then, for

,

(2.17)

Remark 2.5
.

Consider the critical multitype Bellman–Harris system of Section
2
. Assume the mixed lifetime conditions (
2.1
), (
2.2
), and (
2.3
), the branching assumptions (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), and the Poisson initial condition (
2.7
). Let
A
be given by (
2.8
), and set
\alpha:=\min_{1\leq i\leq K}\alpha_{i}
.

Theorem
2.3
gives survival in the limiting sense, provided
A>0
, under Assumption
2.1
, the definition (
2.4
), and the space–lifetime condition (
2.13
), namely

\rho:=\left(\eta-1\right)\wedge\frac{N}{\alpha_{1}}>\frac{\gamma}{\beta},

with the additional renewal-negligibility condition (
2.10
) imposed when
0<\gamma\leq 1/2
. In particular, the condition (
2.13
) implies
N>\alpha_{1}\gamma/\beta
. We now compare this survival region with the extinction regions in
(
Kevei and López-Mimbela 2011
, Theorems 2–4)
.

Case A in
Kevei and López-Mimbela 2011
corresponds to the situation in which the infinite-mean type is also the most mobile type, that is,
\alpha=\alpha_{1}
. In this case, under the extinction-side condition
\eta-1>N/\alpha
, local extinction holds for
N<\alpha_{1}\gamma/\beta
. Hence, under the respective hypotheses of the extinction theorem and of Theorem
2.3
, Case A is determined except at the critical dimension

N=\frac{\alpha_{1}\gamma}{\beta}.

Case B in
Kevei and López-Mimbela 2011
corresponds to the situation in which the infinite-mean type is not the most mobile type, that is,
\alpha<\alpha_{1}
. This case splits into two subregimes. If
\alpha\geq\alpha_{1}\gamma
, then, under the extinction-side condition
\gamma\eta>N/\alpha+1
, local extinction holds for
N<\alpha_{1}\gamma/\beta
. Since Theorem
2.3
gives survival for
N>\alpha_{1}\gamma/\beta
, under its stated assumptions, this subregime is also determined except at the critical dimension
N=\alpha_{1}\gamma/\beta
.

It remains to consider the subregime
\alpha_{1}\gamma>\alpha
. In this case, under the extinction-side condition
\gamma\eta>N/\alpha+1
, local extinction holds for

N<N_{+},\qquad N_{+}:=\frac{\gamma\alpha\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{1}(\beta+1)\gamma-\alpha}.

On the other hand, Theorem
2.3
gives survival for
N>\alpha_{1}\gamma/\beta
, under its stated assumptions. Therefore, in this subregime, the only dimensional region not covered by the available extinction and survival results is

N_{+}\leq N\leq\frac{\alpha_{1}\gamma}{\beta}.

Remark 2.6
.

The tail asymptotic

\overline{F}_{1}(t):=1-F_{1}(t)\sim c_{1}t^{-\gamma},\qquad t\to\infty,

does not imply the local increment condition (
2.9
).
Regular variation of the tail controls the total mass beyond a large level,
but it does not prevent the distribution from placing small atoms in short
intervals far out in the tail.

For example, let
T_{0}
be a Pareto-type random variable such that

\mathbb{P}(T_{0}>t)=t^{-\gamma},\qquad t\geq 1,

and let
n_{k}:=2^{2^{k}}
. Set

q_{k}:=\frac{n_{k}^{-\gamma}}{k^{2}},\qquad Q:=\sum_{k\geq 1}q_{k}<\infty,

and let
D
be a discrete random variable with

\mathbb{P}(D=n_{k})=\frac{q_{k}}{Q},\qquad k\geq 1.

For fixed
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, define a mixture
T
by

\mathbb{P}(T\in\cdot)=(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{P}(T_{0}\in\cdot)+\varepsilon\mathbb{P}(D\in\cdot).

Since
n_{k}
increases superexponentially,

\mathbb{P}(D>t)=o(t^{-\gamma}),\qquad t\to\infty.

Hence

\mathbb{P}(T>t)\sim(1-\varepsilon)t^{-\gamma},\qquad t\to\infty.

Thus
T
has a regularly varying tail with index
-\gamma
. However, for

x_{k}:=n_{k}-1
,

F_{T}(x_{k}+1)-F_{T}(x_{k})=\mathbb{P}(x_{k}<T\leq x_{k}+1)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{Q}\frac{n_{k}^{-\gamma}}{k^{2}}.

This cannot be bounded by
C(1+x_{k})^{-1-\gamma}
, since

\frac{n_{k}^{-\gamma}/k^{2}}{n_{k}^{-1-\gamma}}=\frac{n_{k}}{k^{2}}\longrightarrow\infty.

Therefore (
2.9
) is not a consequence of the tail asymptotic (
2.1
).

This condition is used in Lemma
4.4
to control Stieltjes
convolutions with respect to
dF_{1}
. It is also used in the proof of
Proposition
2.4
to obtain the fixed-window
estimate

\mathbb{P}(x<T^{(1)}\leq x+h)\leq C_{h}x^{-1-\gamma},\qquad x\geq 1,

which is then transferred to the return-cycle distribution and used to verify
the local hypothesis in Lemma
4.3
.

3.
Proof of the main results

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{+}(S)
, and define

H_{i}^{\psi}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathbb{E}_{(x,i)}\bigl(\langle X_{t},\psi\rangle\bigr)\,dx,\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

The same first-step decomposition used in the proof of
Proposition 2.1 of
Kolkovska et al. 2026
, followed by integration over

\mathbb{R}^{N}
and invariance of Lebesgue measure under the stable semigroups, gives

(3.1)

H_{i}^{\psi}(t)=\overline{F}_{i}(t)\|\psi_{i}\|_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{K}m_{i,j}\int_{[0,t]}H_{j}^{\psi}(t-s)\,dF_{i}(s),\qquad i=1,\dots,K,

where
\psi_{i}(x):=\psi(x,i)
. Thus
(H_{i}^{\psi})_{i=1}^{K}
solves the linear
renewal system with

z_{1}^{\psi}(t)=\overline{F}_{1}(t)\|\psi_{1}\|_{1},\qquad z_{i}^{\psi}(t)=\overline{F}_{i}(t)\|\psi_{i}\|_{1},\quad i=2,\dots,K.

For
i\geq 2
, the function
z_{i}^{\psi}
is nonincreasing, bounded, tends to
zero, and is integrable, since
\eta_{i}>1
. Hence
z_{i}^{\psi}
is directly
Riemann integrable and satisfies condition (N1) of
Theorem
4.6
.

We also need that the probabilistic vector
(H_{i}^{\psi})_{i=1}^{K}
is the
locally bounded solution of (
3.1
). This follows from
the same local contraction argument used in the proof of
Proposition 2.1 of
Kolkovska et al. 2026
. Indeed, on a sufficiently small
interval
[0,h]
, chosen so that
\max_{i}F_{i}(h)<1
, the renewal operator
associated with (
3.1
) is a contraction on the space of
bounded Borel maps from
[0,h]
into
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{K}
, endowed with the
supremum norm. The argument uses only
F_{i}(0)=0
, right-continuity of the
distribution functions, and local boundedness of

z_{i}^{\psi}(t)=\overline{F}_{i}(t)\|\psi_{i}\|_{1}.

It therefore does not require finite lifetime means. Repeating the argument
on consecutive intervals gives local boundedness on every compact time
interval. Hence
(H_{i}^{\psi})_{i=1}^{K}
is the locally bounded solution of
(
3.1
).

Applying Theorem
4.6
with

a=\|\psi_{1}\|_{1},\qquad r_{1}\equiv 0,

yields

(3.2)

H_{i}^{\psi}(t)\longrightarrow\|\psi_{1}\|_{1},\qquad t\to\infty,\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

In particular,

(3.3)

\sup_{t\geq 0}\sum_{i=1}^{K}H_{i}^{\psi}(t)<\infty.

Taking
\psi=\varphi
and using
1-e^{-y}\leq y
, we obtain

(3.4)

\sup_{t\geq 0}\sum_{i=1}^{K}G_{i}(t)<\infty.

Let
D_{\varphi}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be compact and satisfy

\operatorname{supp}\varphi\subset D_{\varphi}\times\mathbf{K}.

Since

1-e^{-\langle X_{t},\varphi\rangle}\leq\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}(D_{\varphi}\times\mathbf{K})>0\}},

Lemma
4.1
gives

(3.5)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\ i\in\mathbf{K}}V_{t}(x,i)\leq C_{\varphi}(1+t)^{-\rho},\qquad t\geq 0.

For
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{K})\in[0,1]^{K}
, define

(3.6)

R_{i}(y):=\sum_{j=1}^{K}m_{i,j}y_{j}-\bigl(1-f_{i}(1-y)\bigr),\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

We use the estimates for
R_{i}
proved in the proof of
Theorem 2.3 of
Kolkovska et al. 2026
. Namely, for each

i=1,\ldots,K
, the function
R_{i}
is nonnegative and coordinatewise
nondecreasing on
[0,1]^{K}
. Moreover, for every

\delta\in(0,\beta)
, after increasing the constant if necessary, there
exists
C_{\delta}>0
such that

(3.7)

R_{i}(y)\leq C_{\delta}\|y\|_{\infty}^{1+\beta-\delta},\qquad y\in[0,1]^{K},\quad i=1,\ldots,K.

The proof of these facts uses only the stochasticity of
M
, the
normalization
u=\mathbf{1}
,
v=\pi
, the branching assumption
(
2.6
), and Potter’s bound for
L
. In particular, it is
independent of the lifetime distributions.

Define

(3.8)

B_{i}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}R_{i}\bigl(V_{t}(x,1),\dots,V_{t}(x,K)\bigr)\,dx.

By (
3.7
), (
3.5
), and (
3.4
),

\begin{split}B_{i}(t)&\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq K}V_{t}(x,j)\right)^{1+\beta-\delta}dx\\
&\leq C\left(\sup_{x,j}V_{t}(x,j)\right)^{\beta-\delta}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\|V_{t}(\cdot,j)\|_{1}\\
&\leq C(1+t)^{-\rho(\beta-\delta)}.\end{split}

By the space–lifetime condition (
2.13
), choose

\delta\in(0,\beta)
so small that

(3.9)

\kappa:=\rho(\beta-\delta)>\gamma.

Then

(3.10)

B_{i}(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-\kappa},\qquad t\geq 0,\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

The first-step decomposition
for the Laplace functional gives

\begin{split}V_{t}(x,i)&=(1-F_{i}(t))P_{t,\alpha_{i}}V_{0}(\cdot,i)(x)\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}P_{s,\alpha_{i}}\left(1-f_{i}\bigl(1-V_{t-s}(\cdot,1),\dots,1-V_{t-s}(\cdot,K)\bigr)\right)(x)\,dF_{i}(s).\end{split}

Integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, using invariance of Lebesgue measure under the stable
semigroup, and using the definition of
R_{i}
, we obtain

(3.11)

G_{i}(t)=z_{i}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{K}m_{i,j}\int_{0}^{t}G_{j}(t-s)\,dF_{i}(s),

where

(3.12)

z_{i}(t):=\overline{F}_{i}(t)G_{i}(0)-\int_{0}^{t}B_{i}(t-s)\,dF_{i}(s).

We verify the assumptions of Theorem
4.6
. For
i=1
, write

z_{1}(t)=G_{1}(0)\overline{F}_{1}(t)+r_{1}(t),\qquad r_{1}(t):=-\int_{0}^{t}B_{1}(t-s)\,dF_{1}(s).

By (
3.10
), Lemma
4.4
implies that there
exist
C>0
and
\kappa_{1}^{\prime}>\gamma
such that

|r_{1}(t)|\leq C(1+t)^{-\kappa_{1}^{\prime}},\qquad t\geq 0.

Thus
r_{1}
satisfies condition (N2) of
Theorem
4.6
.

For
i=2,\dots,K
, the first term in (
3.12
) satisfies

\overline{F}_{i}(t)G_{i}(0)\leq C(1+t)^{-\eta_{i}},

with
\eta_{i}>1>\gamma
. By (
3.10
) and
Lemma
4.5
, the convolution term in
(
3.12
) is bounded by
C(1+t)^{-\kappa_{i}^{\prime}}
for some

\kappa_{i}^{\prime}>\gamma
. Hence each
z_{i}
,
i\geq 2
, satisfies condition
(N2) of Theorem
4.6
.

Theorem
4.6
, applied to (
3.11
) with

a=G_{1}(0)
, gives

G_{i}(t)\longrightarrow G_{1}(0),\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

This proves (
2.14
).

By the Poisson formula
(
2.12
),

\mathbb{E}\left(e^{-\langle X_{t},\varphi\rangle}\right)=\exp\left\{-\sum_{i=1}^{K}a_{i}G_{i}(t)\right\}.

Using (
2.14
), we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{K}a_{i}G_{i}(t)\longrightarrow\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}a_{i}\right)G_{1}(0)=A\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(1-e^{-\varphi(x,1)}\right)dx.

This proves (
2.15
). The right-hand side is the Laplace
functional of a Poisson random measure with intensity
A\lambda\otimes\delta_{1}
.

It remains only to justify tightness. Let
K_{0}\subset S
be compact, and choose

\psi\in C_{c}^{+}(S)
such that
\psi\geq\mathbf{1}_{K_{0}}
. By Markov’s inequality and
(
3.3
),

\mathbb{P}\{X_{t}(K_{0})>b\}\leq\mathbb{P}\{\langle X_{t},\psi\rangle>b\}\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\langle X_{t},\psi\rangle}{b}\leq\frac{C}{b}.

Thus

\lim_{b\to\infty}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\{X_{t}(K_{0})>b\}=0.

By the tightness criterion for random Radon measures, see for instance

(
Kallenberg 1986
, Lemma 4.5)
, the family
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is tight in the vague
topology.

We finally prove convergence in the space of Radon measures.
Recall that
S=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbf{K},
where
\mathbf{K}=\{1,\dots,K\}
is finite. Hence
S
is a locally compact second countable Hausdorff space. Therefore the convergence criterion for random measures in the vague topology, Theorem 4.2 of
Kallenberg 1986
, applies on
S
.

By (
2.15
), for every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{+}(S)
,

\mathbb{E}\exp\{-\langle X_{t},\varphi\rangle\}\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}\exp\{-\langle X_{\infty},\varphi\rangle\}.

Thus the Laplace functionals converge on the class
C_{c}^{+}(S)
of nonnegative
continuous functions with compact support. By Theorem 4.2 of
Kallenberg 1986
,
X_{t}\Rightarrow X_{\infty},\,t\to\infty,
in the space of Radon measures on
S
endowed with the vague topology. If
A>0
, the limiting Poisson
random measure has infinite total mass on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\{1\}
, and in particular
is nonzero almost surely. The proof is complete.

∎

Proof of Proposition
2.4
.

Let
(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
,
(\tau_{n})_{n\geq 0}
,
(S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
,

(\sigma_{n})_{n\geq 0}
,
C
, and
U_{C}
be defined as in
Assumption
2.2
, with the chain started from

Y_{0}=1
. Thus

C=S_{\sigma_{1}}.

Write

T^{(1)}:=\tau_{0},\qquad E:=\sum_{n=1}^{\sigma_{1}-1}\tau_{n},

with the convention that the sum is zero when
\sigma_{1}=1
. Then

C=T^{(1)}+E.

Moreover,
T^{(1)}
is independent of
E
, because the initial type-
1

lifetime is independent of the future embedded chain and of the holding times
used after the first transition.

By the finite-state irreducibility argument in the proof of
Theorem
4.6
, specifically the argument leading to
(
4.13
), there exist
r\geq 1
and
p_{0}>0
such that

(3.13)

\mathbb{P}_{1}(\sigma_{1}>nr)\leq(1-p_{0})^{n},\qquad n\geq 0.

Consequently,
\sigma_{1}
has a finite exponential moment of sufficiently small
order.

We now show that the excursion time
E
has an exponential moment. For

0<\theta\leq\theta_{0}
, set

M_{\theta}:=\max_{2\leq i\leq K}\mathbb{E}\bigl(e^{\theta T^{(i)}}\bigr).

Then
M_{\theta}<\infty
, and
M_{\theta}\downarrow 1
as

\theta\downarrow 0
. By (
3.13
), there exists
a>1
such
that

\mathbb{E}_{1}(a^{\sigma_{1}})<\infty.

Choose
\theta>0
so small that
M_{\theta}<a
. Since, before the first return
to type
1
, the chain visits only types in
\{2,\dots,K\}
, conditioning on
the embedded chain gives

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{1}(e^{\theta E})&=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\exp\left\{\theta\sum_{n=1}^{\sigma_{1}-1}\tau_{n}\right\}\Bigm|(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}\right)\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\prod_{n=1}^{\sigma_{1}-1}\mathbb{E}\bigl(e^{\theta T^{(Y_{n})}}\bigr)\right]\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}_{1}\left(M_{\theta}^{\sigma_{1}}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}_{1}(a^{\sigma_{1}})<\infty.\end{split}

Thus there exist constants
A,c>0
such that

(3.14)

\mathbb{P}_{1}(E>t)\leq Ae^{-ct},\qquad t\geq 0.

By Assumption
2.1
, the type-
1
lifetime
distribution
F_{1}
is non-arithmetic and satisfies

\mathbb{P}(T^{(1)}>t)=\overline{F}_{1}(t)\sim c_{1}t^{-\gamma},\qquad t\to\infty.

We also record a consequence of the local increment bound. Fix
h>0
. By
Assumption
2.1
, there exist constants
C>0
and

t_{0}>0
such that

F_{1}(x+y)-F_{1}(x)\leq Cy(1+x)^{-1-\gamma},\qquad x\geq t_{0},\quad 0\leq y\leq x.

If
x\geq\max\{t_{0},h\}
, then taking
y=h
gives

\mathbb{P}(x<T^{(1)}\leq x+h)=F_{1}(x+h)-F_{1}(x)\leq Ch(1+x)^{-1-\gamma}\leq Chx^{-1-\gamma}.

On the remaining interval
1\leq x<\max\{t_{0},h\}
, the probability is bounded
by
1
. Therefore, after increasing the constant, there exists
C_{h}>0
such
that

(3.15)

\mathbb{P}(x<T^{(1)}\leq x+h)\leq C_{h}x^{-1-\gamma},\qquad x\geq 1.

By (
3.14
),

\mathbb{P}_{1}(E>t)=o(t^{-\gamma}).

Since
C=T^{(1)}+E
and
T^{(1)}
is independent of
E
, the same comparison
argument used in the proof of Theorem
4.6
, leading to
(
4.14
), gives

(3.16)

\mathbb{P}_{1}(C>t)\sim\mathbb{P}(T^{(1)}>t)\sim c_{1}t^{-\gamma},\qquad t\to\infty.

Furthermore, the non-arithmeticity argument following
(
4.14
) applies, since
m_{1,1}>0
and
T^{(1)}
is
non-arithmetic. Hence
C
is non-arithmetic.

It remains to verify the local bound required in
Lemma
4.3
. Fix
h>0
. Since
T^{(1)}
and
E
are
independent,

\mathbb{P}_{1}(x<C\leq x+h)=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\mathbb{P}(x-E<T^{(1)}\leq x+h-E\mid E)\right].

On the event
\{E\leq x/2\}
, one has
x-E\geq x/2
. Hence, by
(
3.15
), for
x\geq 2
,

\mathbb{P}(x-E<T^{(1)}\leq x+h-E\mid E)\leq C_{h}(x-E)^{-1-\gamma}\leq C_{h}^{\prime}x^{-1-\gamma}.

On the event
\{E>x/2\}
, the conditional probability is bounded by
1
.
Therefore, using (
3.14
),

\mathbb{P}_{1}(x<C\leq x+h)\leq C_{h}x^{-1-\gamma}+\mathbb{P}_{1}(E>x/2)\leq C_{h}^{\prime}x^{-1-\gamma},\qquad x\geq 2.

Thus
C
is non-arithmetic, satisfies the tail asymptotic
(
3.16
), and satisfies the local bound required in
Lemma
4.3
. The latter lemma gives, for every

\kappa>\gamma
,

\int_{[0,t]}(1+t-s)^{-\kappa}\,U_{C}(ds)\longrightarrow 0,\qquad t\to\infty.

This proves (
2.17
), and therefore verifies
the renewal-negligibility condition in Assumption
2.2
.
∎

4.
Auxiliary Results

Section
4
contains the auxiliary renewal results, including
the local hitting estimate, the Stieltjes convolution estimates, and the mixed
finite–infinite mean renewal theorem.

Lemma 4.1
.

Consider the branching particle system described in Section 2, and assume that
the lifetime distributions satisfy (
2.1
) and
(
2.2
). Then, for every compact set

D\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there exists
C_{D}>0
such that

(4.1)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\ i\in\mathbf{K}}\mathbb{P}_{(x,i)}\{X_{t}(D\times\mathbf{K})>0\}\leq C_{D}(1+t)^{-\rho},\qquad t\geq 0.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma 11 of
Kevei and López-Mimbela 2011
, applied with initial age
0
. That
lemma gives, uniformly in the initial position and type,

\mathbb{P}_{(x,i)}\{X_{t}(D\times\mathbf{K})>0\}\leq C_{D}\bigl(t^{-N/\alpha_{1}}+t^{1-\eta}\bigr),\qquad t\geq 1.

By the observation made in Section 4.3 of
Kevei and López-Mimbela 2011
, the exponent

-N/\alpha_{1}
remains valid also in the case where the long-living type is not
the most mobile type. Since

t^{-N/\alpha_{1}}+t^{1-\eta}\leq C(1+t)^{-\rho},\qquad\rho=\left(\eta-1\right)\wedge\frac{N}{\alpha_{1}},

after increasing
C_{D}
, we obtain (
4.1
).
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Although Lemma 11 of
Kevei and López-Mimbela 2011
is stated under the normalized assumption

1-\Gamma_{1}(t)\sim t^{-\gamma}
, the normalization constant is immaterial. The
argument uses only that the lifetime tail of the long-living type is regularly
varying with index
-\gamma
. Hence the same proof applies when

\overline{F}_{1}(t)\sim c_{1}t^{-\gamma},\qquad c_{1}>0.

The constant
c_{1}
is absorbed into the unspecified constants of the estimate.
Thus the local hitting estimate in Lemma
4.1
follows
from Lemma 11 of
Kevei and López-Mimbela 2011
without any change in the decay exponent.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
X
be a nonnegative, non-arithmetic random variable with distribution

F
, renewal measure
U
, and tail

\overline{F}(t):=F((t,\infty))=\mathbb{P}(X>t)\sim ct^{-\gamma},\qquad t\to\infty,\qquad 0<\gamma<1.

Assume that, for every fixed
h>0
, there exists
C_{h}>0
such that

(4.2)

\mathbb{P}(x<X\leq x+h)\leq C_{h}x^{-1-\gamma},\qquad x\geq 1.

Then the strong renewal estimate holds:

(4.3)

m_{X}(t)\bigl(U(t+h)-U(t)\bigr)\longrightarrow\bigl[\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(2-\gamma)\bigr]^{-1}h,\qquad t\to\infty,\qquad h>0,

where

m_{X}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{P}(X>s)\,ds.

Moreover, for every
\kappa>\gamma
,

(4.4)

\int_{[0,t]}(1+t-s)^{-\kappa}\,U(ds)\longrightarrow 0,\qquad t\to\infty.

Proof.

We first prove (
4.3
). Since

\overline{F}(x)\sim cx^{-\gamma},\qquad x\to\infty,

the notation of
Caravenna and Doney 2019
corresponds to choosing a regularly
varying function
A
such that

A(x)\sim c^{-1}x^{\gamma}.

We verify the local sufficient condition in Proposition 1.7 of

Caravenna and Doney 2019
. Taking
h=1
in (
4.2
), there
exists
C_{1}>0
such that

\mathbb{P}(a<X\leq a+1)\leq C_{1}a^{-1-\gamma},\qquad a\geq 1.

Let
x\geq 2
and
1\leq y\leq x/2
. Put
n:=\lceil y\rceil
. Then

(x-y,x]\subset\bigcup_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(x-y+\ell,x-y+\ell+1].

Since
x-y+\ell\geq x-y\geq x/2
, it follows that

\begin{split}F((x-y,x])&=\mathbb{P}(x-y<X\leq x)\\
&\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{P}(x-y+\ell<X\leq x-y+\ell+1)\\
&\leq C_{1}n(x/2)^{-1-\gamma}\leq Cyx^{-1-\gamma}.\end{split}

Using
A(x)\sim c^{-1}x^{\gamma}
, after increasing the constant and taking

x
sufficiently large, we obtain

F((x-y,x])\leq\frac{C}{A(x)}\left(\frac{y}{x}\right),\qquad x\geq x_{0},\quad 1\leq y\leq x/2.

Thus condition
(1.16)
of
Caravenna and Doney 2019
is satisfied with local
exponent
1
.
If
\gamma>1/2
, Theorem 1.4 of
Caravenna and Doney 2019
gives the strong
renewal theorem without any additional assumption. If
0<\gamma\leq 1/2
, then
Proposition 1.7 of
Caravenna and Doney 2019
applies, since

1>1-2\gamma.

This gives the strong renewal
theorem also in the range
0<\gamma\leq 1/2
. Hence, for every fixed
h>0
,

U(t+h)-U(t)\sim\frac{h}{c\,\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(1-\gamma)}t^{\gamma-1},\qquad t\to\infty.

By Karamata’s theorem,

m_{X}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\overline{F}(s)\,ds\sim\frac{c}{1-\gamma}t^{1-\gamma}.

Therefore

(4.5)

m_{X}(t)\bigl(U(t+h)-U(t)\bigr)\longrightarrow\frac{h}{(1-\gamma)\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(1-\gamma)}=\frac{h}{\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(2-\gamma)}.

This proves (
4.3
).
We now prove (
4.4
). Let

Q(u):=(1+u)^{-\kappa},\qquad u\geq 0.

Assume first that
\gamma<\kappa<1
. Then
Q
is nonincreasing and
regularly varying with index
-\kappa
. By (
4.5
), the
fixed-interval renewal estimate corresponding to Lemma 1(iii) in

Anderson and Athreya 1987
holds for the present renewal measure, with tail exponent

\gamma
. Therefore the estimate proved in Theorem 1 of
Anderson and Athreya 1987
,
applied to
Q
, gives

(U*Q)(t):=\int_{[0,t]}Q(t-s)\,U(ds)=O\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{t}Q(u)\,du}{\int_{0}^{t}\overline{F}(u)\,du}\right).

Here the only point in which Anderson and Athreya require

\gamma>1/2
is the derivation of their fixed-interval renewal estimate;
in the present argument this estimate has already been obtained from
(
4.5
).
Now

\int_{0}^{t}Q(u)\,du=O(t^{1-\kappa}),\qquad t\to\infty,

whereas, by Karamata’s theorem,

\int_{0}^{t}\overline{F}(u)\,du\sim\frac{c}{1-\gamma}t^{1-\gamma}.

Consequently,

(U*Q)(t)=O(t^{\gamma-\kappa})\longrightarrow 0,\qquad t\to\infty,

because
\kappa>\gamma
. Since

(U*Q)(t)=\int_{[0,t]}(1+t-s)^{-\kappa}\,U(ds),

this proves (
4.4
) when
\gamma<\kappa<1
.
If
\kappa\geq 1
, choose
\kappa_{0}
such that

\gamma<\kappa_{0}<1.

Then, for all
u\geq 0
,

(1+u)^{-\kappa}\leq(1+u)^{-\kappa_{0}}.

By the case already proved,

\int_{[0,t]}(1+t-s)^{-\kappa_{0}}\,U(ds)\longrightarrow 0.

Therefore,

\int_{[0,t]}(1+t-s)^{-\kappa}\,U(ds)\leq\int_{[0,t]}(1+t-s)^{-\kappa_{0}}\,U(ds)\longrightarrow 0.

This proves (
4.4
) for every
\kappa>\gamma
.
The proof is complete.
∎

The next lemma is the precise estimate needed in the proof of the main theorem.

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume Assumption
2.1
. Let
\kappa>\gamma
, and let

h:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a Borel function such that

(4.6)

h(t)\leq C_{h}(1+t)^{-\kappa},\qquad t\geq 0.

Then there exist constants
C_{h}^{\prime}>0
and
\kappa_{h}^{\prime}>\gamma
such that

(4.7)

\int_{0}^{t}h(t-s)\,dF_{1}(s)\leq C_{h}^{\prime}(1+t)^{-\kappa_{h}^{\prime}},\qquad t\geq 0.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the estimate for all sufficiently large
t
; the range
of bounded
t
is absorbed by increasing the constant. Put

I(t):=\int_{0}^{t}h(t-s)\,dF_{1}(s).

We split

I(t)=I_{1}(t)+I_{2}(t),

where

I_{1}(t):=\int_{0}^{t/2}h(t-s)\,dF_{1}(s),\qquad I_{2}(t):=\int_{t/2}^{t}h(t-s)\,dF_{1}(s).

For
0\leq s\leq t/2
, one has
1+t-s\geq(1+t)/2
. Hence

I_{1}(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-\kappa}F_{1}(t/2)\leq C(1+t)^{-\kappa}.

Since
\kappa>\gamma
, this term already has the required order.
It remains to estimate
I_{2}(t)
. We first control the contribution from

[t-1,t]
. By (
4.6
) and
(
2.9
), for all large
t
,

\int_{t-1}^{t}h(t-s)\,dF_{1}(s)\leq C\bigl(F_{1}(t)-F_{1}(t-1)\bigr)\leq C(1+t)^{-1-\gamma}.

For the remaining part, decompose according to dyadic intervals in

u=t-s
. Let

M:=\left\lfloor\log_{2}(t/2)\right\rfloor.

For
m=0,\dots,M-1
, define

A_{m}(t):=\{s\in[t/2,t]:2^{m}\leq t-s<2^{m+1}\}.

On
A_{m}(t)
, one has
h(t-s)\leq C(1+2^{m})^{-\kappa}
. Moreover,

A_{m}(t)\subset(t-2^{m+1},t-2^{m}],

and, since
t-2^{m+1}\geq t/2
, Assumption
2.1

gives

F_{1}(t-2^{m})-F_{1}(t-2^{m+1})\leq C2^{m}(1+t)^{-1-\gamma}.

It remains to handle the last possible interval

A_{M}(t):=\{s\in[t/2,t]:2^{M}\leq t-s\leq t/2\}.

On this interval,
h(t-s)\leq C(1+2^{M})^{-\kappa}
. Since

2^{M}\leq t/2<2^{M+1}
, the corresponding interval in the
s
-variable has
length at most
2^{M}
, and Assumption
2.1
gives

\int_{A_{M}(t)}dF_{1}(s)\leq C2^{M}(1+t)^{-1-\gamma}.

Consequently,

\begin{split}I_{2}(t)&\leq C(1+t)^{-1-\gamma}+C(1+t)^{-1-\gamma}\sum_{0\leq m\leq M}2^{m}(1+2^{m})^{-\kappa}\\
&\leq C(1+t)^{-1-\gamma}\sum_{0\leq m\leq M}(1+2^{m})^{1-\kappa}.\end{split}

If
0<\kappa<1
, then the last sum is bounded by

C(1+t)^{1-\kappa}
, and therefore

I_{2}(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-\gamma-\kappa}.

If
\kappa=1
, then

I_{2}(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-1-\gamma}\log(1+t)\leq C(1+t)^{-1}

for all sufficiently large
t
. If
\kappa>1
, then

\sum_{0\leq m\leq M}(1+2^{m})^{1-\kappa}\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-m(\kappa-1)}<\infty,

and consequently

I_{2}(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-1-\gamma}.

Combining the bounds for
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, we obtain
(
4.7
). For instance, one may take

\kappa_{h}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}\kappa,&\gamma<\kappa<1,\\
1,&\kappa=1,\\
\min\{\kappa,1+\gamma\},&\kappa>1.\end{cases}

In each case
\kappa_{h}^{\prime}>\gamma
. The proof is complete.
∎

We also need a simpler convolution estimate for the finite-mean types.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
F
be a distribution function on
[0,\infty)
such that

1-F(t)\leq C_{F}(1+t)^{-\eta},\qquad t\geq 0,

for some
\eta>\gamma
. Let
h:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a Borel function
such that

h(t)\leq C_{h}(1+t)^{-\kappa},\qquad t\geq 0,

with
\kappa>\gamma
. Then there exist constants
C^{\prime}>0
and

\kappa^{\prime}>\gamma
such that

(4.8)

\int_{0}^{t}h(t-s)\,dF(s)\leq C^{\prime}(1+t)^{-\kappa^{\prime}},\qquad t\geq 0.

One may take
\kappa^{\prime}=\min\{\kappa,\eta\}
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the estimate for all sufficiently large
t
, since the
estimate on bounded intervals can be absorbed into the constant. Define

I(t):=\int_{0}^{t}h(t-s)\,dF(s).

We decompose

I(t)=I_{1}(t)+I_{2}(t),

where

I_{1}(t):=\int_{0}^{t/2}h(t-s)\,dF(s),\qquad I_{2}(t):=\int_{t/2}^{t}h(t-s)\,dF(s).

For
0\leq s\leq t/2
, one has

1+t-s\geq\frac{1+t}{2}.

Therefore, by the assumed bound on
h
,

I_{1}(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-\kappa}F(t/2)\leq C(1+t)^{-\kappa}.

It remains to estimate
I_{2}(t)
. We first consider the part where

0\leq t-s<1
. For all sufficiently large
t
,

\begin{split}\int_{\{s\in[t/2,t]:\,0\leq t-s<1\}}h(t-s)\,dF(s)&\leq C\bigl(F(t)-F(t-1)\bigr)\\
&\leq C\bigl(1-F(t-1)\bigr)\\
&\leq C(1+t)^{-\eta}.\end{split}

We now consider the part where
1\leq t-s\leq t/2
. For
m\geq 0
, set

A_{m}(t):=\{s\in[t/2,t]:\,2^{m}\leq t-s<2^{m+1}\}.

Only finitely many of these sets are nonempty. If
s\in A_{m}(t)
, then

h(t-s)\leq C(1+2^{m})^{-\kappa}.

Moreover,
A_{m}(t)\subset[t/2,t]
. Hence, for all sufficiently large
t
,

\int_{A_{m}(t)}dF(s)\leq\mathbb{P}\{T\geq t/2\}\leq\mathbb{P}\{T>t/2-1\}\leq C(1+t)^{-\eta},

where
T
has distribution function
F
.
Therefore,

\begin{split}\int_{\{s\in[t/2,t]:\,1\leq t-s\leq t/2\}}h(t-s)\,dF(s)&\leq C(1+t)^{-\eta}\sum_{m\geq 0}(1+2^{m})^{-\kappa}.\end{split}

Since
\kappa>0
,

\sum_{m\geq 0}(1+2^{m})^{-\kappa}\leq\sum_{m\geq 0}2^{-m\kappa}<\infty.

Thus

I_{2}(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-\eta}.

Combining the estimates for
I_{1}(t)
and
I_{2}(t)
, we obtain

I(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-\kappa}+C(1+t)^{-\eta}\leq C^{\prime}(1+t)^{-\min\{\kappa,\eta\}}.

Since
\kappa>\gamma
and
\eta>\gamma
, the exponent

\kappa^{\prime}:=\min\{\kappa,\eta\}

satisfies
\kappa^{\prime}>\gamma
. Therefore

\int_{0}^{t}h(t-s)\,dF(s)\leq C^{\prime}(1+t)^{-\kappa^{\prime}},\qquad t\geq 0,

after increasing
C^{\prime}
if necessary to cover bounded values of
t
. This
proves the lemma.
∎

4.1.
A mixed finite–infinite mean renewal theorem

This section proves the renewal theorem used in the proof of
Theorem
2.3
. Throughout the section, the notation and
assumptions of Assumption
2.2
are in force.

Theorem 4.6
.

Assume that the mixed renewal framework of
Assumption
2.2
holds. Let

z_{1},\dots,z_{K}
be real-valued Borel measurable functions, locally bounded on

[0,\infty)
, and extended by zero to
(-\infty,0)
. Assume that there exists

a\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(4.9)

z_{1}(t)=a\overline{F}_{1}(t)+r_{1}(t),\qquad t\geq 0.

Assume that each function among

r_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{K}

satisfies one of the following two conditions:

(N1)

it is bounded, tends to zero at infinity, and is directly Riemann
integrable on
[0,\infty)
, in the real-valued sense;

(N2)

there exist constants
C_{h}>0
and
\kappa_{h}>\gamma
such that

|h(t)|\leq C_{h}(1+t)^{-\kappa_{h}},\qquad t\geq 0.

Then the renewal system

(4.10)

H_{i}(t)=z_{i}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{K}\int_{0}^{t}H_{j}(t-s)\,dF_{i,j}(s),\qquad i=1,\dots,K,

has a unique locally bounded solution
H=(H_{1},\dots,H_{K})
. Moreover,

(4.11)

\lim_{t\to\infty}H_{i}(t)=a,\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

Proof.

Since
F_{i}(0)=0
for every
i
, the matrix

F(0):=(F_{i,j}(0))_{i,j=1}^{K}

is the zero matrix. Hence
p(F(0))=0<1
.
We first record a local finiteness estimate for the renewal measure. Let

\tau^{(i)}
denote a random variable with distribution
F_{i}
. Since

F_{i}(0)=0
and
F_{i}
is a proper distribution function on
[0,\infty)
,
for each
i
there exists
\varepsilon_{i}>0
such that

P(\tau^{(i)}>\varepsilon_{i})>0.

Because the type space is finite, there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
p>0

such that

P(\tau^{(i)}>\varepsilon)\geq p,\qquad i=1,\ldots,K.

Conditional on the embedded chain
(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, the events

A_{n}:=\{\tau_{n}>\varepsilon\},\qquad n\geq 0,

are independent and satisfy

P(A_{n}\mid(Y_{m})_{m\geq 0})\geq p.

Therefore, for each fixed
T>0
, if
S_{n}\leq T
, then

\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\mathbf{1}_{A_{r}}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{T}{\varepsilon}\right\rfloor.

Conditionally on the embedded chain, the sum on the left stochastically
dominates a binomial random variable with parameters
n
and
p
. Hence
there exist constants
C_{T},c_{T}>0
such that

P_{i}(S_{n}\leq T)\leq C_{T}e^{-c_{T}n},\qquad n\geq 1,\quad i=1,\ldots,K.

Consequently,

\sum_{n\geq 0}P_{i}(S_{n}\leq T)<\infty,\qquad i=1,\ldots,K,

and the matrix renewal measure associated with
F=(F_{i,j})
is finite on
compact time intervals. In particular,
S_{n}\to\infty
almost surely.

We now construct the renewal representation. Define, for
i=1,\ldots,K
,

\widehat{H}_{i}(t):=E_{i}\left[\sum_{n\geq 0}z_{Y_{n}}(t-S_{n})\mathbf{1}_{\{S_{n}\leq t\}}\right],\qquad t\geq 0.

Since the matrix renewal measure is finite on compact time intervals and the
functions
z_{j}
are locally bounded, the above expectation is finite for
each
t\geq 0
, and
\widehat{H}_{i}
is locally bounded. Conditioning on the first
holding time and the first transition gives

\begin{split}\widehat{H}_{i}(t)&=z_{i}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{K}m_{i,j}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{j}\left[\sum_{n\geq 0}z_{Y_{n}}(t-s-S_{n})\mathbf{1}_{\{S_{n}\leq t-s\}}\right]dF_{i}(s)\\
&=z_{i}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{K}\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{H}_{j}(t-s)\,dF_{i,j}(s),\end{split}

which is exactly (
4.10
). Thus

\widehat{H}=(\widehat{H}_{1},\dots,\widehat{H}_{K})
is a locally bounded solution.

We now prove uniqueness in the class of locally bounded solutions. Let
H
and

\widetilde{H}
be two locally bounded solutions of
(
4.10
), and set

D_{i}(t):=H_{i}(t)-\widetilde{H}_{i}(t),\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

Then
D
satisfies the homogeneous renewal system

D_{i}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{K}\int_{0}^{t}D_{j}(t-s)\,dF_{i,j}(s),\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

Iterating this identity
n
times gives

D_{i}(t)=\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[D_{Y_{n}}(t-S_{n})\mathbf{1}_{\{S_{n}\leq t\}}\right].

Fix
T>0
. Since
D
is locally bounded, there exists
M_{T}<\infty
such
that

|D_{j}(u)|\leq M_{T},\qquad 0\leq u\leq T,\quad j=1,\dots,K.

Thus, for
0\leq t\leq T
,

|D_{i}(t)|\leq M_{T}\,\mathbb{P}_{i}(S_{n}\leq T).

By the non-explosion property proved above,
S_{n}\to\infty
almost surely.
Therefore

P_{i}(S_{n}\leq T)\longrightarrow 0,\qquad n\to\infty.

It follows that
D_{i}(t)=0
for every
0\leq t\leq T
. Since
T>0
is
arbitrary,
D_{i}(t)=0
for all
t\geq 0
and all
i=1,\dots,K
. Hence the
locally bounded solution is unique. Therefore the solution
H
satisfies

(4.12)

H_{i}(t)=\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\sum_{n\geq 0}z_{Y_{n}}(t-S_{n})\mathbf{1}_{\{S_{n}\leq t\}}\right],\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

We next analyze the return cycles to type
1
. Since
M
is irreducible on
the finite state space
\mathbf{K}
, for each initial type
i
there exists a
path from
i
to
1
with positive probability. Hence, because
\mathbf{K}

is finite, there exist an integer
r\geq 1
and a constant
p_{0}>0
such that

\mathbb{P}_{i}\{Y_{m}=1\text{ for some }1\leq m\leq r\}\geq p_{0},\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

For
i=1
, this also follows from
m_{1,1}>0
, since the chain can return to
type
1
in one step with positive probability.

By the Markov property, on each block of
r
consecutive steps, as long as
type
1
has not yet been reached, the probability of hitting type
1
during
the next block is at least
p_{0}
. Consequently,

(4.13)

\mathbb{P}_{i}(\sigma_{1}>nr)\leq(1-p_{0})^{n},\qquad n\geq 0,\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

In particular,
\sigma_{1}<\infty
almost surely. Moreover,

\mathbb{E}_{i}(\sigma_{1})=\sum_{m\geq 0}\mathbb{P}_{i}(\sigma_{1}>m)\leq r\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{P}_{i}(\sigma_{1}>nr)\leq r\sum_{n\geq 0}(1-p_{0})^{n}=\frac{r}{p_{0}}<\infty.

We prove that the return-cycle length
C
has the same tail as the type-
1

holding time:

(4.14)

\mathbb{P}_{1}(C>t)\sim ct^{-\gamma},\qquad t\to\infty.

Write

C=\tau_{0}+E,

where
\tau_{0}
is the initial holding time at type
1
, and
E
is the
duration of the remaining part of the excursion before the next return to type

1
. By the construction of the holding times,
\tau_{0}
is independent of the
future embedded chain and of the holding times used after time
S_{1}
. Hence

\tau_{0}
is independent of
E
.

The random variable
E
is a sum of holding times whose types belong to

\{2,\dots,K\}
. More precisely,

E=\sum_{n=1}^{\sigma_{1}-1}\tau_{n},

with the convention that the sum is zero if
\sigma_{1}=1
. Thus the number of
holding times appearing in
E
is

N_{E}=\sigma_{1}-1.

From (
4.13
), there exist constants
A_{0},b_{0}>0
such
that

(4.15)

\mathbb{P}_{1}(N_{E}>n)\leq A_{0}e^{-b_{0}n},\qquad n\geq 1.

We now show that

(4.16)

\mathbb{P}_{1}(E>t)=o(t^{-\gamma}).

Let
0<\varepsilon<1
. On the event
\{N_{E}\leq t^{\varepsilon}\}
, if

E>t
, then at least one of the holding times in the excursion is larger than

t/\lfloor t^{\varepsilon}\rfloor
. Indeed, otherwise the sum of at most

\lfloor t^{\varepsilon}\rfloor
holding times would be at most
t
. Therefore,
conditioning on the embedded chain and using the union bound over the holding
times in the excursion, for all large
t
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}_{1}(E>t)&\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}(N_{E}>t^{\varepsilon})+\mathbb{P}_{1}(E>t,\,N_{E}\leq t^{\varepsilon})\\
&\leq A_{0}e^{-b_{0}t^{\varepsilon}}+\lfloor t^{\varepsilon}\rfloor\max_{2\leq i\leq K}\mathbb{P}\left(\tau^{(i)}>\frac{t}{\lfloor t^{\varepsilon}\rfloor}\right)\\
&\leq A_{0}e^{-b_{0}t^{\varepsilon}}+Ct^{-\eta+\varepsilon(1+\eta)}.\end{split}

Since
\eta>1>\gamma
, we can choose
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small so
that

-\eta+\varepsilon(1+\eta)<-\gamma.

The exponential term is
o(t^{-\gamma})
, and the polynomial term is also

o(t^{-\gamma})
by this choice of
\varepsilon
. This proves
(
4.16
).

Since
C=\tau_{0}+E
and
E\geq 0
,

\mathbb{P}_{1}(C>t)\geq\mathbb{P}_{1}(\tau_{0}>t)=\overline{F}_{1}(t).

For the upper bound, fix
\delta\in(0,1)
. Then

\{C>t\}\subseteq\{\tau_{0}>(1-\delta)t\}\cup\{E>\delta t\}.

Therefore

\mathbb{P}_{1}(C>t)\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}(\tau_{0}>(1-\delta)t)+\mathbb{P}_{1}(E>\delta t).

Dividing by
\overline{F}_{1}(t)
, using

\overline{F}_{1}(t)\sim ct^{-\gamma}
, and using
(
4.16
), we get

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{1}(C>t)}{\overline{F}_{1}(t)}\leq(1-\delta)^{-\gamma}.

Letting
\delta\downarrow 0
, and combining this upper bound with the lower
bound

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{1}(C>t)}{\overline{F}_{1}(t)}\geq 1,

gives

\frac{\mathbb{P}_{1}(C>t)}{\overline{F}_{1}(t)}\longrightarrow 1.

Since
\overline{F}_{1}(t)\sim ct^{-\gamma}
, (
4.14
)
follows. In particular,

\mathbb{E}_{1}(C)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{1}(C>t)\,dt=\infty,

because
0<\gamma<1
.

The distribution of
C
is non-arithmetic. Suppose, by contradiction, that

C
is arithmetic. Then there exist
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
h>0
such that

\mathbb{P}_{1}(C\in a+h\mathbb{Z})=1.

Let
A:=\{Y_{1}=1\}
. Since
m_{1,1}>0
,
\mathbb{P}_{1}(A)=m_{1,1}>0
. On
A
,
one has
\sigma_{1}=1
, and therefore
C=S_{1}=\tau_{0}
. Hence

A\cap\{\tau_{0}\notin a+h\mathbb{Z}\}\subseteq\{C\notin a+h\mathbb{Z}\}.

Consequently,

0=\mathbb{P}_{1}(C\notin a+h\mathbb{Z})\geq\mathbb{P}_{1}(A,\tau_{0}\notin a+h\mathbb{Z}).

The random variable
\tau_{0}
is independent of
A
, because lifetimes are
independent of the embedded chain. Therefore

\mathbb{P}_{1}(A,\tau_{0}\notin a+h\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{P}_{1}(A)\mathbb{P}_{1}(\tau_{0}\notin a+h\mathbb{Z}).

Since
\mathbb{P}_{1}(A)>0
, it follows that

\mathbb{P}_{1}(\tau_{0}\in a+h\mathbb{Z})=1.

Thus
F_{1}
is arithmetic, contradicting the assumption that
F_{1}
is
non-arithmetic. Hence
C
is non-arithmetic.

We now prove the renewal-negligibility estimate that will be used in the cycle
decomposition. Let
U_{C}
denote the renewal measure associated with the
return-cycle distribution, including the atom at the origin, namely

U_{C}:=\delta_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{C}^{*n},

where
F_{C}
denotes the distribution of
C
. Let
h
be a locally bounded
Borel function on
[0,\infty)
, extended by zero to
(-\infty,0)
, and
suppose that
h
satisfies either (N1) or (N2). We claim that

(4.17)

(U_{C}*h)(t):=\int_{[0,t]}h(t-s)\,U_{C}(ds)\longrightarrow 0,\qquad t\to\infty.

First assume that
h
satisfies (N1). Since the return-cycle
distribution is non-arithmetic and has infinite mean, its limiting renewal
rate is
\lambda=0
. Hence, applying the key renewal theorem for nonlattice
renewal processes to
h
, which are
directly Riemann integrable under (N1), yields

\int_{[0,t]}h(t-s)\,U_{C}(ds)\longrightarrow 0,\qquad t\to\infty;

see
(
Daley and Vere-Jones 2003
, Theorem 4.4.II)
. This proves
(
4.17
) under (N1).

Assume now that
h
satisfies (N2). Then there exist constants

C_{h}>0
and
\kappa_{h}>\gamma
such that

|h(t)|\leq C_{h}(1+t)^{-\kappa_{h}},\qquad t\geq 0.

Choose
\kappa_{0}
such that

\gamma<\kappa_{0}<1,\qquad\kappa_{0}\leq\kappa_{h}.

This is possible because
\gamma<1
and
\kappa_{h}>\gamma
. After increasing
the constant if necessary, we have

|h(t)|\leq C(1+t)^{-\kappa_{0}},\qquad t\geq 0.

Consequently,

|(U_{C}*h)(t)|\leq C\int_{[0,t]}(1+t-s)^{-\kappa_{0}}\,U_{C}(ds).

If
\gamma>1/2
, Theorem 1 of
Anderson and Athreya 1987
, applied to

Q(u)=(1+u)^{-\kappa_{0}}
and to the renewal distribution of
C
, gives

\int_{[0,t]}(1+t-s)^{-\kappa_{0}}\,U_{C}(ds)\longrightarrow 0.

Indeed,
Q
is nonincreasing and regularly varying with index

-\kappa_{0}
. Moreover,

\int_{0}^{t}Q(u)\,du=O(t^{1-\kappa_{0}}),

whereas, by (
4.14
) and Karamata’s theorem,

\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{P}_{1}(C>u)\,du\sim\frac{c}{1-\gamma}t^{1-\gamma}.

Since
\kappa_{0}>\gamma
, the ratio tends to zero. If

0<\gamma\leq 1/2
, the same conclusion is exactly the additional
renewal-negligibility assumption (
2.10
). Hence
(
4.17
) holds also under (N2).

We shall also use that
(U_{C}*h)(t)
is bounded on
[0,\infty)
. Indeed, it
is bounded on compact intervals because
U_{C}
is finite on compact intervals
and
h
is locally bounded. For large
t
, it is bounded because it converges
to zero by (
4.17
).

For the process started from type
1
, define the reward accumulated during
one return cycle by

(4.18)

q(t):=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\sigma_{1}-1}z_{Y_{n}}(t-S_{n})\mathbf{1}_{\{S_{n}\leq t\}}\right],\qquad t\geq 0.

Let
R_{k}:=S_{\sigma_{k}}
,
k\geq 0
. Under
\mathbb{P}_{1}
, the increments

R_{k+1}-R_{k}
,
k\geq 0
, are independent and identically distributed with
common distribution equal to that of
C
. Hence
U_{C}
is the renewal measure
of the renewal epochs
(R_{k})_{k\geq 0}
.

Using (
4.12
), we decompose the sum into return
cycles:

\begin{split}H_{1}(t)&=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{n=\sigma_{k}}^{\sigma_{k+1}-1}z_{Y_{n}}(t-S_{n})\mathbf{1}_{\{S_{n}\leq t\}}\right].\end{split}

For fixed
t
, this rearrangement is justified because only finitely many
terms contribute almost surely, and the local boundedness of the functions

z_{j}
, together with the compact finiteness of the renewal measure, gives
absolute integrability.

By the strong Markov property at
\sigma_{k}
, conditionally on
R_{k}
, the
expected contribution of the
k
-th cycle is

q(t-R_{k})\mathbf{1}_{\{R_{k}\leq t\}}.

Therefore

H_{1}(t)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[q(t-R_{k})\mathbf{1}_{\{R_{k}\leq t\}}\right].

Since

U_{C}(\cdot)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathbb{P}(R_{k}\in\cdot),

we obtain

(4.19)

H_{1}(t)=\int_{[0,t]}q(t-s)\,U_{C}(ds).

We now decompose
q
. The term corresponding to
n=0
in
(
4.18
) is
z_{1}(t)
, since
Y_{0}=1
and
S_{0}=0
.
Therefore, by (
4.9
),

z_{1}(t)=a\overline{F}_{1}(t)+r_{1}(t).

For
j=2,\dots,K
, define the measure

\mu_{j}(B):=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\sigma_{1}-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_{n}=j,\ S_{n}\in B\}}\right],\qquad B\in\mathcal{B}([0,\infty)).

This measure is finite. Indeed,

\mu_{j}([0,\infty))=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\sigma_{1}-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_{n}=j\}}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{1}(\sigma_{1})<\infty.

The contribution of the terms with
n\geq 1
in
(
4.18
) can be written as

\sum_{j=2}^{K}\int_{[0,t]}z_{j}(t-s)\,\mu_{j}(ds).

Consequently,

(4.20)

q(t)=a\overline{F}_{1}(t)+r_{1}(t)+\sum_{j=2}^{K}\int_{[0,t]}z_{j}(t-s)\,\mu_{j}(ds).

Substituting (
4.20
) into
(
4.19
) gives

\begin{split}H_{1}(t)&=a\int_{[0,t]}\overline{F}_{1}(t-s)\,U_{C}(ds)+(U_{C}*r_{1})(t)\\
&\quad+\sum_{j=2}^{K}\int_{[0,t]}\left(\int_{[0,t-s]}z_{j}(t-s-u)\,\mu_{j}(du)\right)U_{C}(ds).\end{split}

For fixed
t
, both
U_{C}
and
\mu_{j}
are finite on
[0,t]
, while

z_{j}
is bounded on
[0,t]
. Hence Fubini’s theorem applies on

[0,t]^{2}
, and the last term may be written as

\int_{[0,t]}(U_{C}*z_{j})(t-u)\,\mu_{j}(du).

Thus

(4.21)

\begin{split}H_{1}(t)&=a\int_{[0,t]}\overline{F}_{1}(t-s)\,U_{C}(ds)+(U_{C}*r_{1})(t)\\
&\quad+\sum_{j=2}^{K}\int_{[0,t]}(U_{C}*z_{j})(t-s)\,\mu_{j}(ds).\end{split}

By (
4.17
),

(U_{C}*r_{1})(t)\longrightarrow 0.

Similarly, for each
j=2,\dots,K
,

(U_{C}*z_{j})(t)\longrightarrow 0.

Moreover, each function
U_{C}*z_{j}
is bounded on
[0,\infty)
, as shown
above. Since
\mu_{j}
is a finite measure, dominated convergence gives

\int_{[0,t]}(U_{C}*z_{j})(t-s)\,\mu_{j}(ds)\longrightarrow 0.

Indeed, extending
U_{C}*z_{j}
by zero to
(-\infty,0)
, the integrand

(U_{C}*z_{j})(t-s)
converges to zero for every fixed
s\geq 0
, and it is
dominated by a finite constant. Therefore

(4.22)

H_{1}(t)=a\int_{[0,t]}\overline{F}_{1}(t-s)\,U_{C}(ds)+o(1).

It remains to identify the limit of the remaining renewal convolution. We prove
that

(4.23)

\int_{[0,t]}\overline{F}_{1}(t-s)\,U_{C}(ds)\longrightarrow 1.

Let

\overline{F}_{C}(t):=\mathbb{P}_{1}(C>t).

The elementary renewal identity gives

(4.24)

\int_{[0,t]}\overline{F}_{C}(t-s)\,U_{C}(ds)=1,\qquad t\geq 0.

To verify this identity, let
C_{1},C_{2},\dots
be i.i.d. copies of
C
, and set

R_{0}=0
,
R_{n}=C_{1}+\cdots+C_{n}
for
n\geq 1
. Since
C>0
almost surely,
the renewal epochs satisfy
R_{n}\to\infty
almost surely. Hence, for each fixed

t\geq 0
, exactly one of the events

\{R_{n}\leq t<R_{n+1}\},\qquad n\geq 0,

occurs. Also,

U_{C}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\in\cdot).

Therefore

\begin{split}\int_{[0,t]}\overline{F}_{C}(t-s)\,U_{C}(ds)&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{F}_{C}(t-R_{n})\mathbf{1}_{\{R_{n}\leq t\}}\right]\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\leq t<R_{n+1})\\
&=1.\end{split}

This proves (
4.24
).

By (
4.14
),

\frac{\overline{F}_{1}(t)}{\overline{F}_{C}(t)}\longrightarrow 1.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Choose
A>0
such that

(1-\varepsilon)\overline{F}_{C}(u)\leq\overline{F}_{1}(u)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\overline{F}_{C}(u),\qquad u\geq A.

Using (
4.24
), for
t>A
we obtain

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{[0,t]}\overline{F}_{1}(t-s)\,U_{C}(ds)-1\right|\\
&\quad\leq\int_{[0,t]}|\overline{F}_{1}(t-s)-\overline{F}_{C}(t-s)|\,U_{C}(ds)\\
&\quad\leq\int_{[0,t-A]}|\overline{F}_{1}(t-s)-\overline{F}_{C}(t-s)|\,U_{C}(ds)\\
&\qquad+\int_{(t-A,t]}\bigl(\overline{F}_{1}(t-s)+\overline{F}_{C}(t-s)\bigr)\,U_{C}(ds).\end{split}

On the interval
[0,t-A]
, one has
t-s\geq A
. Hence the choice of
A

implies

|\overline{F}_{1}(t-s)-\overline{F}_{C}(t-s)|\leq\varepsilon\overline{F}_{C}(t-s).

Therefore, by (
4.24
),

\int_{[0,t-A]}|\overline{F}_{1}(t-s)-\overline{F}_{C}(t-s)|\,U_{C}(ds)\leq\varepsilon\int_{[0,t]}\overline{F}_{C}(t-s)\,U_{C}(ds)=\varepsilon.

On the interval
(t-A,t]
, both tails are bounded by
1
. Hence

\int_{(t-A,t]}\bigl(\overline{F}_{1}(t-s)+\overline{F}_{C}(t-s)\bigr)\,U_{C}(ds)\leq 2\bigl(U_{C}(t)-U_{C}(t-A)\bigr).

Since
C
is non-arithmetic and has infinite mean, applying the same
infinite-mean key renewal theorem to the directly Riemann integrable function

g_{A}(u):=\mathbf{1}_{\{0<u\leq A\}}

gives, for every fixed
A>0
,

U_{C}(t)-U_{C}(t-A)=\int_{[0,t]}g_{A}(t-s)\,U_{C}(ds)\longrightarrow 0,\qquad t\to\infty.

Consequently,

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\left|\int_{[0,t]}\overline{F}_{1}(t-s)\,U_{C}(ds)-1\right|\leq\varepsilon.

Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
proves
(
4.23
). Combining this with
(
4.22
), we obtain

H_{1}(t)\longrightarrow a.

It remains to treat initial types different from
1
. Fix

i\in\{2,\dots,K\}
, and define

\rho_{i}:=\inf\{n\geq 0:Y_{n}=1\},\qquad T_{i}:=S_{\rho_{i}}.

Since the embedded chain is irreducible on a finite state space,

\mathbb{P}_{i}(\rho_{i}<\infty)=1,\qquad\mathbb{E}_{i}(\rho_{i})<\infty.

Moreover,
T_{i}<\infty
almost surely, because
\rho_{i}<\infty
almost surely
and all holding times are finite almost surely. Before the chain hits state

1
, it only visits states in
\{2,\dots,K\}
. Define

b_{i}(t):=\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\rho_{i}-1}z_{Y_{n}}(t-S_{n})\mathbf{1}_{\{S_{n}\leq t\}}\right].

For
j=2,\dots,K
, set

\nu_{i,j}(B):=\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\rho_{i}-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_{n}=j,\ S_{n}\in B\}}\right],\qquad B\in\mathcal{B}([0,\infty)).

The measure
\nu_{i,j}
is finite, since

\nu_{i,j}([0,\infty))=\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\rho_{i}-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_{n}=j\}}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{i}(\rho_{i})<\infty.

Moreover,

b_{i}(t)=\sum_{j=2}^{K}\int_{[0,t]}z_{j}(t-s)\,\nu_{i,j}(ds).

Each
z_{j}
,
j=2,\dots,K
, satisfies either (N1) or
(N2). In both cases
z_{j}
is bounded and

z_{j}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. Extending
z_{j}
by zero to

(-\infty,0)
, we have, for every fixed
s\geq 0
,

z_{j}(t-s)\longrightarrow 0,\qquad t\to\infty.

Since
\nu_{i,j}
is finite and
z_{j}
is bounded, dominated convergence
yields

\int_{[0,t]}z_{j}(t-s)\,\nu_{i,j}(ds)=\int_{[0,\infty)}z_{j}(t-s)\,\nu_{i,j}(ds)\longrightarrow 0.

Thus

(4.25)

b_{i}(t)\longrightarrow 0.

By the strong Markov property at the hitting time
T_{i}
, or equivalently by
splitting the representation (
4.12
) at the
first time at which the embedded chain reaches type
1
, we obtain

(4.26)

H_{i}(t)=b_{i}(t)+\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[H_{1}(t-T_{i})\mathbf{1}_{\{T_{i}\leq t\}}\right].

Since
H_{1}
is locally bounded and
H_{1}(t)\to a
, it follows that

\sup_{u\geq 0}|H_{1}(u)|<\infty.

Moreover,
T_{i}<\infty
almost surely. Hence, for almost every trajectory,

T_{i}\leq t
for all sufficiently large
t
, and
t-T_{i}\to\infty
. Therefore

H_{1}(t-T_{i})\mathbf{1}_{\{T_{i}\leq t\}}\longrightarrow a\qquad\text{almost surely}.

The integrand is dominated by
\sup_{u\geq 0}|H_{1}(u)|<\infty
. By dominated
convergence,

\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[H_{1}(t-T_{i})\mathbf{1}_{\{T_{i}\leq t\}}\right]\longrightarrow a.

Combining this with (
4.25
) and
(
4.26
), we conclude that

H_{i}(t)\longrightarrow a,\qquad i=2,\dots,K.

Together with the already proved convergence of
H_{1}(t)
, this proves

H_{i}(t)\longrightarrow a,\qquad i=1,\dots,K.

The proof is complete.
∎

5.
Examples

This section records explicit lifetime distributions satisfying
Assumption
2.1
. The examples illustrate that the
density sufficient condition in Lemma
5.1
is easily
verified in standard heavy-tailed models.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that the type-
1
lifetime distribution
F_{1}
is non-arithmetic and
absolutely continuous on
[0,\infty)
, with density
f_{1}
. Assume that
(
2.1
) holds and that there exist constants
C>0
and

t_{0}>0
such that

(5.1)

f_{1}(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-1-\gamma},\qquad t\geq t_{0}.

Then
F_{1}
satisfies Assumption
2.1
.

Proof.

Let
x\geq t_{0}
and
0\leq y\leq x
. By
absolute continuity,

F_{1}(x+y)-F_{1}(x)=\int_{x}^{x+y}f_{1}(r)\,dr.

Using (
5.1
), and the fact that
r\geq x
on the interval

[x,x+y]
, we obtain

F_{1}(x+y)-F_{1}(x)\leq C\int_{x}^{x+y}(1+r)^{-1-\gamma}\,dr\leq Cy(1+x)^{-1-\gamma}.

This is (
2.9
).
∎

Remark 5.2
.

In the following examples, when
0<\gamma\leq 1/2
, we shall assume in
addition that the finite-mean lifetimes have a common positive exponential
moment; that is, there exists
\theta_{0}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\bigl(e^{\theta_{0}T^{(i)}}\bigr)<\infty,\qquad i=2,\dots,K.

Under this additional condition,
Proposition
2.4
applies and therefore
verifies the renewal-negligibility condition in
Assumption
2.2
. Equivalently, for every

\kappa>\gamma
,

\int_{[0,t]}(1+t-s)^{-\kappa}\,U_{C}(ds)\longrightarrow 0,\qquad t\to\infty.

The exponential-moment condition is satisfied, for instance, by exponential and
Gamma lifetimes, by bounded lifetimes, and by Weibull lifetimes with shape
parameter at least one. It is stronger than the polynomial tail assumption
imposed in the general theorem, but it gives a simple lifetime-level criterion
ensuring the additional renewal condition required when
0<\gamma\leq 1/2
.

Example 5.3
.

Let
0<\gamma<1
. Let
Z_{\gamma}
be a positive
\gamma
-stable random
variable with Laplace transform

\mathbb{E}(e^{-sZ_{\gamma}})=e^{-s^{\gamma}},\qquad s\geq 0,

and let
Y
be an exponential random variable with parameter
\lambda>0
,
independent of
Z_{\gamma}
. Define

T^{(1)}:=Z_{\gamma}Y^{1/\gamma}.

Then

\mathbb{E}(e^{-sT^{(1)}})=\mathbb{E}(e^{-s^{\gamma}Y})=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+s^{\gamma}}.

Thus
T^{(1)}
has the Mittag–Leffler waiting-time distribution with
parameters
(\gamma,\lambda)
; see, for instance,

(
Cahoy and Polito 2013
, (1.3), (1.5), and (2.1), (2.2), (2.4) with
\delta=1
)
. Its survival function and density are
given by

\mathbb{P}(T^{(1)}>t)=E_{\gamma}(-\lambda t^{\gamma}),\qquad f_{1}(t)=\lambda t^{\gamma-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma}(-\lambda t^{\gamma}),\qquad t>0.

Using the asymptotic representations for the Mittag–Leffler renewal
survival function and density, see
(
Mainardi et al. 2005
, (4.5)–(4.6))
,

E_{\gamma}(-\lambda t^{\gamma})\sim\frac{1}{\lambda\Gamma(1-\gamma)}t^{-\gamma},\qquad t\to\infty,

and

\lambda t^{\gamma-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma}(-\lambda t^{\gamma})\sim\frac{\gamma}{\lambda\Gamma(1-\gamma)}t^{-1-\gamma},\qquad t\to\infty.

Therefore

\mathbb{P}(T^{(1)}>t)\sim\frac{1}{\lambda\Gamma(1-\gamma)}t^{-\gamma},\qquad f_{1}(t)\sim\frac{\gamma}{\lambda\Gamma(1-\gamma)}t^{-1-\gamma}.

Since
F_{1}
is absolutely continuous, it is non-arithmetic. Moreover, the
density asymptotic implies that there exist constants
C>0
and
t_{0}>0

such that

f_{1}(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-1-\gamma},\qquad t\geq t_{0}.

Hence the hypotheses of Lemma
5.1
are satisfied.
Consequently,
F_{1}
satisfies Assumption
2.1
.

Example 5.4
.

Let
0<\gamma<1
. The Pareto distribution with scale
x_{0}>0
,

\overline{F}_{1}(t)=\left(\frac{x_{0}}{t}\right)^{\gamma},\qquad t\geq x_{0},

satisfies

f_{1}(t)=\gamma x_{0}^{\gamma}t^{-1-\gamma},\qquad t\geq x_{0}.

Hence, by Lemma
5.1
,
Assumption
2.1
holds.

The Lomax distribution with scale
\lambda>0
,

\overline{F}_{1}(t)=\left(1+\frac{t}{\lambda}\right)^{-\gamma},\qquad t\geq 0,

satisfies

\overline{F}_{1}(t)\sim\lambda^{\gamma}t^{-\gamma},\qquad f_{1}(t)=\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\left(1+\frac{t}{\lambda}\right)^{-1-\gamma}\leq C(1+t)^{-1-\gamma}.

Hence, by Lemma
5.1
, Assumption
2.1
holds.

Example 5.5
.

Let
a,b,\lambda>0
, and consider the Burr XII tail

\overline{F}_{1}(t)=\left(1+\left(\frac{t}{\lambda}\right)^{a}\right)^{-b},\qquad t\geq 0.

If
ab=\gamma\in(0,1)
, then

\overline{F}_{1}(t)\sim\lambda^{\gamma}t^{-\gamma}.

Moreover, its density is asymptotic to a constant multiple of

t^{-1-\gamma}
, and therefore satisfies

f_{1}(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-1-\gamma}

for all sufficiently large
t
. Hence, by Lemma
5.1
, Assumption
2.1
holds.
