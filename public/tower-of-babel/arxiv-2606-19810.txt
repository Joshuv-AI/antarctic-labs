Analytic continuation of weighted H H -harmonic Bergman spaces
Matěj Moravík

Abstract
We provide a partial answer to Problems 1 and 2 raised in the recent article by Blaschke et al., concerning the analytic continuation of weighted H H -harmonic Bergman spaces. These are spaces of functions annihilated by the Möbius-invariant Laplacian on the unit ball. More precisely, we identify some of the discrete Wallach sets and show, among others, that structure depends on the parity of the dimension.



1
Introduction

Let
B^{n}
be the open unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n>2
. The orthogonal transformations

x\mapsto Ux\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\;U\in O(n),

map
B^{n}
and its boundary
\partial B^{n}
(unit sphere) onto themselves, and so do Möbius transformations

\phi_{a}(x):=\frac{a|x-a|^{2}+(1-|a|^{2})(a-x)}{1-2\langle x,a\rangle+|x|^{2}|a|^{2}}

interchanging the origin
\textbf{0}\in B^{n}
with the point
a\in B^{n}
; here
\langle x,y\rangle
denotes the usual scalar product.

The group
G
generated by the
\phi_{a},\;a\in B^{n},
and
U\in O(n)
is called the Möbius group of
B^{n}
.
Let
\mu
denote the normalized Lebesgue measure on
B^{n}
,
i.e.

\mu(B^{n})=1
. Similarly,
\sigma
will denote the surface measure normalized so that
\sigma(\partial B^{n})=1.

The hyperbolic Laplacian is then defined as

\Delta_{h}f(x):=\Delta(f\>\circ\>\phi_{x})(0),\quad f\in C^{2}(B^{n}),\>x\in B^{n};

(1.1)

it follows from a direct calculation that

\Delta_{h}f(x)=(1-|x|^{2})[(1-|x|^{2})\Delta f(x)+2(n-2)\langle x,\nabla f(x)\rangle],

where
\Delta f=\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}+...+\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{n}^{2}}
and
\nabla f=\big(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}},...,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}\big)
.
Functions on
B^{n}
annihilated by
\Delta_{h}
are called hyperbolic harmonic, or
H
-harmonic for short. The vector space of all
H
-harmonic functions on the unit ball
B^{n}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{H}
.
For any
s>-1
, one can consider the
weighted
H
-harmonic Bergman space

\mathcal{H}_{s}:=\{f\in L^{2}(B^{n},d\rho_{s}),\quad f\text{ is $H$-harmonic}\},

(1.2)

where

d\rho_{s}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+s+1)}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma(s+1)}(1-|x|^{2})^{s}d\mu(x)

is a rotation invariant probability measure.

We consider an analytic continuation and the “residue spaces”
\mathcal{H}_{s}

for
s\leq-1
, in a sense that will be made precise below.

We first recall the analogous situation for weighted Bergman spaces of
holomorphic
, rather than
H
-harmonic, functions on the unit ball
\mathbf{B}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Let

\mathcal{A}_{s}:=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbf{B}^{n},d\rho_{s}):f\text{ is holomorphic on }\mathbf{B}^{n}\},

where the measure
d\rho_{s}
is the same as above. It is well known, that the spaces
\mathcal{A}_{s}
are reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces and the reproducing kernels are given by

K_{s}^{\mathrm{hol}}(x,y)=(1-\langle x,y\rangle)^{-n-1-s}.

(1.3)

Weighted Bergman kernels
K_{s}^{hol}(x,y)
,
s>-1,
continue to be positive definite in the sense of Aronszajn
[
12
]
for all
s\geq-n-1
, thus yielding an analytic continuation of
\mathcal{A}_{s},
for
s\in[-n-1,+\infty)
. One calls the interval
[-n-1,+\infty)
the
Wallach set
of
\mathbf{B}^{n}
.
For general case of holomorphic functions defined on bounded symmetric domains, see
[
13
]
.

When
s=-n-1
, the (
1.3
) reduces to constant and the corresponding Hilbert space is trivial.
However, we can consider the space that arises as the “residue” of
\mathcal{A}_{s}
at
s=-n-1
, namely,

\lim_{s\rightarrow-n-1}\frac{K^{hol}_{s}(x,y)-1}{s+n+1}=\ln{\frac{1}{1-\langle x,y\rangle}},

which is a positive definite function on
\textbf{B}^{n}\times\textbf{B}^{n},
and the associated reproducing kernel Hilbert space becomes the
Dirichlet space
on
B^{n}
; we say that
-n-1
belongs to the
discrete Wallach set
.

We now define the analogues of residue spaces in the case of
H
-harmonic functions on the real ball.

For an integer
m\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{H}^{m}
be the space of restrictions to the unit sphere
\partial B^{n}
of harmonic homogeneous polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of degree
m
. Then we have the following Peter-Weyl decomposition

L^{2}(\partial B^{n},d\sigma)=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{m}

(1.4)

under the action of the orthogonal group
O(n)
of rotations of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

[
6
, Chapter 5]
.
Similarly the Bergman space of
H
-harmonic functions
\mathcal{H}_{s}
admits a Peter-Weyl decomposition given by

\mathcal{H}_{s}=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\textbf{{H}}^{m},

(1.5)

where
\textbf{H}^{m}
is the space of “solid harmonics”

H^{m}=\{f\in C(\overline{B^{n}}):f\text{ is }H\text{-harmonic on }B^{n}\text{ and }f|_{\partial B^{n}}\in\mathcal{H}^{m}\}

and the norm of
f=\sum_{m}f_{m}
,
f_{m}\in\textbf{H}^{m}
, is given by

\|f\|_{s}^{2}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}I_{m}(s)\|f_{m}\|^{2}_{\partial B^{n}},

(1.6)

where the coefficients
I_{m}(s)
are equal to

I_{m}(s):=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+s+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma(s+1)}\int_{0}^{1}t^{m+\frac{n}{2}-1}(1-t)^{s}S_{m}(t)^{2}\,dt,

(1.7)

with

S_{m}(t):=\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)_{m}}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}m,\,1-\frac{n}{2}\\
m+\frac{n}{2}\end{matrix};\,t\right),

and

f_{m}(r\eta)=S_{m}(r^{2})r^{m}f_{m}(\eta),\qquad f_{m}|_{\partial B^{n}}\in\mathcal{H}^{m},\qquad 0\leq r\leq 1,\ \eta\in\partial B^{n},

where
{}_{2}F_{1}
denotes the Gauss hypergeometric function and
(a)_{m}:=(a)(a+1)\cdots(a+m-1)
is the usual Pochhammer symbol.
I_{m}(s)=1
corresponds to the classical Hardy space.

For a detailed exposition of the facts stated above, the reader is referred to
[
7
, Chapter 10]
or
[
10
]
.

In
[
8
, Proposition 1]
it was shown that
I_{m}(s)
admits an analytic continuation for
s\leq-1
, more precisely, the authors showed that
(\Gamma(n+s)\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1+s)\Gamma(2n-1+s)^{2})^{-1}I_{m}(s)
extends to an analytic function on the entire complex plane.

Suppose that
s_{0}
is a pole of
I_{m}(s)
of order
k
. Then we can consider the following space of
H
-harmonic functions on
B^{n}
:

\mathcal{H}^{\#s_{0}}_{r}=\left\{f=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}f_{m}\ \text{$H$-harmonic on $B^{n}$}:\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{r}^{\#s_{0}}}^{2}<\infty\right\},

(1.8)

where

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{r}^{\#s_{0}}}^{2}=\pm\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c_{-r,m}(s_{0})\,\|f_{m}\|_{\partial B^{n}}^{2},

(1.9)

Here the sign before the sum is chosen so that the resulting quantity defines a seminorm,

c_{-r,m}(s_{0})
denotes the
(-r)
-th coefficient in the Laurent expansion of
I_{m}(s)
at
s=s_{0}
,
and
f_{m}\in\mathbf{H}^{m}
is the
m
-th term in the Peter–Weyl decomposition of
f
.
The expression in (
1.9
) defines a seminorm whenever

c_{-r,m}(s_{0})\geq 0
for all
m\geq 0
, or

c_{-r,m}(s_{0})\leq 0
for all
m>0
.
This leads to the following definition of the discrete Wallach sets.

\mathcal{W}^{r}_{d}:=\{s\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,\text{$I_{m}(s)$ has pole at $s$, and $c_{-r,m}(s)$ are all nonnegative or all nonpositive, $\forall m\geq 0$}\}.

For example it was shown in
[
8
, Proposition 1]
that
-n,\,-n-1\in\mathcal{W}_{1}^{d},
furthermore
\mathcal{H}^{\#-n}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\#-n-1}_{1}
correspond to the spaces with unique Möbius invariant semi-inner product.

For an analogous situation in the case of
M
-harmonic functions see
[
11
]
.

In
[
9
]
the following estimate for
I_{m}(s)
, was established:

c_{s}<m^{s+1}I_{m}(s)<C_{s}\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}\quad\text{and $s\geq-1$},

(1.10)

for some absolute constants
C_{s}>c_{s}>0.

However, somewhat surprisingly, it was shown in
[
8
]
that

I_{m}(-2)=1
for all
m\geq 0
. Hence, in the case
s=-2
,
the space
\mathcal{H}_{s}
reduces to the Hardy space, and the estimate
(
1.10
) fails. This is a situation that has no parallel in the case of holomorphic or
M
-harmonic functions.
This observation led the authors of
[
8
]
to introduce the spaces

\mathcal{H}_{\#s}:=\left\{f=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}f_{m}\,:\,f\text{ is }H\text{-harmonic on }B^{n}\text{ and }\|f\|_{\#s}^{2}<\infty\right\},

(1.11)

where

\|f\|_{\#s}^{2}:=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)^{-s-1}\|f_{m}\|_{\partial B^{n}}^{2}.

Here again
f=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}f_{m}
denotes the Peter–Weyl decomposition of
f
.
They then asked for which
s<-1
the spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\#s}
and

\mathcal{H}_{s}
coincide, or, equivalently, for which
s<-1
the estimate (
1.10
) holds (with
I_{m}(s)
possibly replaced by
-I_{m}(s)).

In this article, we derive an analytic continuation of
I_{m}(s)
in terms
of the generalized hypergeometric series
{}_{4}F_{3}
.
This allows us to give a partial answer to the questions raised in
[
8
]
concerning the
characterisation of
\mathcal{W}_{k}^{d}
.
We show that
\mathcal{W}_{k}^{d}

depends on the parity of
n
, which is again a situation with no
counterpart in the holomorphic or
M
-harmonic case.
We show that, for even
n\geq 6
, one has

-n+1,-n+2\in\mathcal{W}_{1}^{d}
, and that

\mathcal{W}_{r}^{d}=\emptyset
for
r>1
. For
n=4
, only
-3\in\mathcal{W}_{1}^{1}.

For odd
n
, we show that

-n+\frac{1}{2},-n-\frac{1}{2}\in\mathcal{W}_{1}^{d}
,

\mathcal{W}_{2}^{d}=\{-2n+1-k:\ k=0,1,2,\dots\},

and
\mathcal{W}_{r}^{d}=\emptyset
for
r>2
.

In the same article, the authors asked about the behaviour of
I_{m}(s)

for
s<-1
as
m\to\infty
. We prove that, for odd
n
,

m^{s+1}I_{m}(s)
converges to a nonzero limit as
m\to\infty
whenever

s+n-1\geq 0
,
s\notin\{-2,-3,\ldots\}
, and

-\frac{n}{2}-s\neq 0,1,2,\dots
. We also show that the condition

s+n-1\geq 0
can be omitted when
n
is even.

2
Notation and preliminaries

The notation
{}_{p}F_{q}
denotes the generalized hypergeometric series, defined as

{}_{p}F_{q}\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{p}\\[3.0pt]
b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{q}\end{matrix}\;;\;z\right)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1})_{\ell}(a_{2})_{\ell}\cdots(a_{p})_{\ell}}{(b_{1})_{\ell}(b_{2})_{\ell}\cdots(b_{q})_{\ell}}\frac{z^{\ell}}{\ell!},

provided that no
b_{j}
is a non-positive integer,
and
(a)_{\ell}=\frac{\Gamma(a+\ell)}{\Gamma(a)}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+\ell-1)
denotes the usual Pochhammer symbol (rising factorial).

The
{}_{p+1}F_{p}
is absolutely convergent for
z=1
, provided that
\sum_{j=1}^{p}b_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{p+1}a_{i}>0
.

We also encounter the following hypergeometric series:

{}_{p}F_{q}\left(\begin{matrix}-m,a_{2},\dots,a_{p}\\[3.0pt]
-n,b_{2},\dots,b_{q}\end{matrix}\;;\;z\right).

For positive integers
m
and
n
with
m<n
, this will always denote, without explicitly mentioning it, the finite sum from
\ell=0
to
m
.

The hypergeometric series
{}_{p+1}F_{p}
is called
balanced
(
Saalschützian
) when
\sum_{j=1}^{p}b_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{p+1}a_{i}=1
.
We also make use of the following identity:

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\epsilon\,{}_{p}F_{q}\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1},\dots,a_{p}\\[3.0pt]
b_{1},\dots,b_{q-1},-m+\epsilon\end{matrix}\;;\;z\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{(-1)^{m}z^{m+1}}{m!(m+1)!}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(a_{i})_{m+1}}{\prod_{j=1}^{q-1}(b_{j})_{m+1}}\,{}_{p}F_{q}\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1}+m+1,\dots,a_{p}+m+1\\
b_{1}+m+1,\dots,b_{q-1}+m+1,m+2\end{matrix}\;;\;z\right),

(2.1)

provided that no
a_{i}
is a negative integer greater than
-m
.
This can be deduced from the fact that
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\epsilon}{(-m+\epsilon)_{\ell}}
equals
0
for
0\leq\ell\leq m
, and
\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!\,(\ell-m-1)!}
for
\ell>m
.

We also need a two-variable generalization of the hypergeometric series,
namely the
Kampé de Fériet
function:

F^{p:q;r}_{s:t;u}\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1},\dots,a_{p}:b_{1},\dots,b_{q};b^{\prime}_{1},\dots,b^{\prime}_{r}\\[3.0pt]
c_{1},\dots,c_{s}:d_{1},\dots,d_{t};d^{\prime}_{1},\dots,d^{\prime}_{u}\end{matrix};x,y\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(a_{i})_{m+\ell}}{\prod_{i=1}^{s}(c_{i})_{m+\ell}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{q}(b_{i})_{m}}{\prod_{i=1}^{t}(d_{i})_{m}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}(b^{\prime}_{i})_{\ell}}{\prod_{i=1}^{u}(d^{\prime}_{i})_{\ell}}\frac{x^{m}y^{\ell}}{m!\ell!}.

(2.2)

In particular, we will need the special case

F^{0:3;3}_{1:1;1}\left(\begin{matrix}-:a\,,b\,,c\,;\,a^{\prime}\,,b^{\prime}\,,c^{\prime}\\[3.0pt]
d\,:e\,;e^{\prime}\end{matrix};x,y\right).

This series is absolutely convergent at
x=y=1
, provided that

d+e-a-b-c>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad d+e^{\prime}-a^{\prime}-b^{\prime}-c^{\prime}>0,

see
[
4
]
.

We shall also use the big-
\mathcal{O}
notation. We write

a_{m}=\mathcal{O}(b_{m})

if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|a_{m}|\leq C|b_{m}|

for all sufficiently large
m
.

In order to make the notation more compact, we shall sometimes use the abbreviation

\frac{\Gamma(a_{1})\cdots\Gamma(a_{n})}{\Gamma(b_{1})\cdots\Gamma(b_{m})}=\Gamma\!\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\\
b_{1},\dots,b_{m}\end{matrix}\right).

3
Analytic continuation of
I_{m}(s)

Proposition 3.1
.

When
n
is even we have:

\displaystyle I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=(n-1)_{m}^{2}\Gamma\!\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\;s+2n-1,\;m+\frac{n}{2}-1,\;s+n\\
m,\;m+n-1,\;s+\frac{3n}{2},\;m+s+\frac{3n}{2}-1\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\,s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1,\,1-\frac{n}{2},\,s+n\\
s+\frac{3n}{2},\,2-m-\frac{n}{2},\,s+\frac{n}{2}+1\end{matrix};1\right),

(3.1)

for
m>0.

When
n
is odd we have

\displaystyle I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)_{m}^{2}}{(n+s)_{m}}\,\Gamma\!\left(\begin{matrix}s+\frac{n}{2}+1,\;s+2n-1,\;1-m-\frac{n}{2}\\
s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1,\;1-\frac{n}{2},\;m+2n+s-1\end{matrix}\right)

(3.2)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}n+s,\,m+n-1,\,m+s+\frac{3n}{2}-1,\,m\\
m+n+s,\,m+\frac{n}{2},\,m+2n+s-1\end{matrix};1\right)

\displaystyle+(n-1)_{m}^{2}\Gamma\!\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\;s+2n-1,\;m+\frac{n}{2}-1,\;s+n\\
m,\;m+n-1,\;s+\frac{3n}{2},\;m+s+\frac{3n}{2}-1\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\,s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1,\,1-\frac{n}{2},\,s+n\\
s+\frac{3n}{2},\,2-m-\frac{n}{2},\,s+\frac{n}{2}+1\end{matrix};1\right),

for
m>0.

Proof.

Recall that

I_{m}(s):=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+s+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(s+1)}\int_{0}^{1}t^{m+\frac{n}{2}-1}(1-t)^{s}\,S_{m}(t)^{2}\,dt,

where

S_{m}(t):=\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{(\frac{n}{2})_{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(m)_{k}(1-\frac{n}{2})_{k}}{(m+\frac{n}{2})_{k}k!}t^{k}.

For
m=0
, we have
I_{m}(s)=1
for all
s\in\mathbb{C}
.
Hence, from now on, we assume that
m>0
; moreover, for the absolute convergence of various sums, we may assume that
s>-1
.

Expanding each
S_{m}(t)
into sum, changing order of summation and integration and using

\int_{0}^{1}t^{\beta}(1-t)^{\alpha}\,dt=\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)\,\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+2)},\quad\text{for $\alpha>-1,\;\beta>-1$},

to integrate term by term we obtain:

\displaystyle I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n}{2}+s+1\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma(s+1)}\left(\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)_{m}}\right)^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(m)_{k}\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)_{k}}{\left(m+\frac{n}{2}\right)_{k}\,k!}\frac{(m)_{j}\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)_{j}}{\left(m+\frac{n}{2}\right)_{j}\,j!}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{0}^{1}t^{m+\frac{n}{2}-1+k+j}(1-t)^{s}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n}{2}+s+1\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma(s+1)}\left(\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)_{m}}\right)^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(m)_{k}\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)_{k}}{\left(m+\frac{n}{2}\right)_{k}\,k!}\frac{(m)_{j}\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)_{j}}{\left(m+\frac{n}{2}\right)_{j}\,j!}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{\Gamma\!\left(m+\frac{n}{2}+k+j\right)\Gamma(s+1)}{\Gamma\!\left(m+\frac{n}{2}+k+j+s+1\right)}.

After cancelling the factor
\Gamma(s+1)
and rewriting

\Gamma\left(m+\frac{n}{2}+k+j\right)
and
\Gamma\left(m+\frac{n}{2}+k+j+s+1\right)
as

\Gamma\left(m+\frac{n}{2}+j\right)\left(m+\frac{n}{2}+j\right)_{k}
and

\Gamma\left(m+\frac{n}{2}+s+1+j\right)\left(m+\frac{n}{2}+s+1+j\right)_{k}
,
the sum over
k
in (
3.3
) can be expressed as a hypergeometric series
{}_{3}F_{2}
:

\displaystyle I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+s+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}\left(\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{(\frac{n}{2})_{m}}\right)^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(m)_{j}(1-\frac{n}{2})_{j}}{(m+\frac{n}{2})_{j}j!}\left(\frac{\Gamma(m+\frac{n}{2}+j)}{\Gamma(m+\frac{n}{2}+s+1+j)}\right)

\displaystyle\times{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}m,\;1-\frac{n}{2},\;m+\frac{n}{2}+j\\
m+\frac{n}{2},\;m+\frac{n}{2}+s+1+j\end{matrix};\;1\right).

(3.4)

Now, by applying Thomae’s identity (
[
2
, p. 14, eq. 2]
):

\frac{\Gamma(a)}{\Gamma(e)\Gamma(f)}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}a,\;b,\;c\\
e,\;f\end{matrix};\,1\right)=\frac{\Gamma(\sigma)}{\Gamma(\sigma+b)\Gamma(\sigma+c)}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}\sigma,\;e-a,\;f-a\\
\sigma+b,\;\sigma+c\end{matrix};\,1\right),

(3.5)

with parameters

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=m,\quad b=1-\frac{n}{2},\quad c=m+\frac{n}{2}+j,

\displaystyle e

\displaystyle=m+\frac{n}{2},\quad f=m+\frac{n}{2}+s+j+1,

where
\sigma
denotes the
excess parameter

\sigma:=e+f-a-b-c=n+s
, we see that

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}m,\;1-\frac{n}{2},\;m+\frac{n}{2}+j\\[2.0pt]
m+\frac{n}{2},\;m+\frac{n}{2}+s+1+j\end{matrix};1\right)=\frac{\Gamma\!\left(m+\frac{n}{2}\right)\,\Gamma\!\left(m+\frac{n}{2}+s+1+j\right)\,\Gamma(n+s)}{\Gamma\!\left(m+\frac{n}{2}+j\right)\,\Gamma(m+n+s)\,\Gamma\!\left(s+\frac{n}{2}+1\right)}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\;\times{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}n+s,\;-j,\;s+1\\[2.0pt]
m+n+s,\;s+\frac{n}{2}+1\end{matrix};1\right).

We then apply Sheppard’s transformation:

{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}-\ell,\ \alpha_{1},\ \alpha_{2}\\
\beta_{1},\ \beta_{2}\end{matrix};1\right)=\frac{(\beta_{2}-\alpha_{1})_{\ell}}{(\beta_{2})_{\ell}}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}-\ell,\ \alpha_{1},\ \beta_{1}-\alpha_{2}\\
\beta_{1},\ 1-\beta_{2}+\alpha_{1}-\ell\end{matrix};1\right),

for terminating
{}_{3}F_{2}
(see
[
3
, Appendix, formula (I)]
).
Thus, (
3.6
) is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma\!\left(m+\frac{n}{2}\right)\,\Gamma\!\left(m+\frac{n}{2}+s+1+j\right)\,\Gamma(n+s)}{\Gamma\!\left(m+\frac{n}{2}+j\right)\,\Gamma(m+n+s)\,\Gamma\!\left(s+\frac{n}{2}+1\right)}\,\frac{\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)_{j}}{\left(s+\frac{n}{2}+1\right)_{j}}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\qquad\times{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}-j,\;n+s,\;m+n-1\\[2.0pt]
m+n+s,\;\frac{n}{2}-j\end{matrix};1\right).

Substituting (
3.7
) into (
3.4
) and cancelling Gamma functions we obtain:

\displaystyle I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{n}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{\left(\tfrac{n}{2}\right)_{m}}\right)^{2}\frac{\Gamma\!\left(m+\tfrac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma(n+s)}{\Gamma(m+n+s)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(m)_{j}\left(1-\tfrac{n}{2}\right)_{j}}{\left(m+\tfrac{n}{2}\right)_{j}\,j!}\frac{\left(1-\tfrac{n}{2}\right)_{j}}{\left(s+\tfrac{n}{2}+1\right)_{j}}\;{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}-j,\ n+s,\ m+n-1\\[2.0pt]
m+n+s,\ \tfrac{n}{2}-j\end{matrix};1\right).

(3.8)

Expanding
{}_{3}F_{2}
and rearranging the terms, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{n}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{\left(\tfrac{n}{2}\right)_{m}}\right)^{2}\frac{\Gamma\!\left(m+\tfrac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma(n+s)}{\Gamma(m+n+s)}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{r=0}^{j}\frac{(m)_{j}\left(1-\tfrac{n}{2}\right)_{j}^{2}}{\left(m+\tfrac{n}{2}\right)_{j}\left(s+\tfrac{n}{2}+1\right)_{j}\,j!}\,\frac{(-j)_{r}\,(n+s)_{r}\,(m+n-1)_{r}}{(m+n+s)_{r}\,\left(\tfrac{n}{2}-j\right)_{r}\,r!}.

Making the substitution
j=k+r
, changing the order of summation, and using the identities:

(-j)_{k}=(-1)^{k}\frac{j!}{(j-k)!},\qquad\left(\tfrac{n}{2}-j\right)_{k}=(-1)^{k}(1-\tfrac{n}{2}+r)_{k}=\frac{(-1)^{k}\left(1-\tfrac{n}{2}\right)_{r+k}}{\left(1-\tfrac{n}{2}\right)_{r}},

we obtain

\displaystyle I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{n}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{\left(\tfrac{n}{2}\right)_{m}}\right)^{2}\frac{\Gamma\!\left(m+\tfrac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma(n+s)}{\Gamma(m+n+s)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(m)_{k+r}\,\left(1-\tfrac{n}{2}\right)_{k+r}\,\left(1-\tfrac{n}{2}\right)_{r}}{\left(m+\tfrac{n}{2}\right)_{k+r}\,\left(s+\tfrac{n}{2}+1\right)_{k+r}}\;\frac{(n+s)_{k}\,(m+n-1)_{k}}{(m+n+s)_{k}\,k!}\;\frac{1}{r!}

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{n}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{\left(\tfrac{n}{2}\right)_{m}}\right)^{2}\frac{\Gamma\!\left(m+\tfrac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma(n+s)}{\Gamma(m+n+s)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(n+s)_{k}\,(m+n-1)_{k}}{(m+n+s)_{k}\,k!}\frac{(m)_{k}\left(1-\tfrac{n}{2}\right)_{k}}{\left(m+\tfrac{n}{2}\right)_{k}\left(s+\tfrac{n}{2}+1\right)_{k}}

\displaystyle\quad\times{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}m+k,\ 1-\tfrac{n}{2}+k,\ 1-\tfrac{n}{2}\\
m+\tfrac{n}{2}+k,\ s+\tfrac{n}{2}+1+k\end{matrix};1\right).

Using the estimate

\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+m)}{\Gamma(\beta+m)}=m^{\alpha-\beta}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)\right)

(3.10)

(see
[
1
]
, Eq. (4), p. 47), we see that (
3.9
) is bounded, up to a multiplicative constant, by

\displaystyle\sum_{k,r=1}^{\infty}(k+r)^{-s-\frac{3n}{2}}r^{-\frac{n}{2}}k^{n-2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,r\geq 1\\
r\leq k\end{subarray}}(k+r)^{-s-\frac{3n}{2}}r^{-\frac{n}{2}}k^{n-2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,r\geq 1\\
k<r\end{subarray}}(k+r)^{-s-\frac{3n}{2}}r^{-\frac{n}{2}}k^{n-2}

\displaystyle<\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,r\geq 1\\
r\leq k\end{subarray}}k^{-s-\frac{3n}{2}}r^{-\frac{n}{2}}k^{n-2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,r\geq 1\\
k<r\end{subarray}}r^{-s-\frac{3n}{2}}r^{-\frac{n}{2}}k^{n-2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-s-\frac{n}{2}-2}\sum_{r=1}^{k}r^{-\frac{n}{2}}+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}r^{-s-2n}\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}k^{n-2}

\displaystyle=C\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-s-\frac{n}{2}-2}+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}r^{-s-n-1}\right),

which converges absolutely for

. Hence, changing the order of summation in (
3.9
) is justified.

Applying one more Thomae’s transform (
3.5
) with parameters:

\displaystyle a=m+k,\;\;\quad b=k-\tfrac{n}{2},\;\;\quad c=1-\tfrac{n}{2},

\displaystyle e=m+\tfrac{n}{2}+k,\;\;\quad f=s+\tfrac{n}{2}+k+1,

we obtain

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}m+k+1,\ k+1-\tfrac{n}{2},\ 1-\tfrac{n}{2}\\[4.0pt]
m+\tfrac{n}{2}+k+1,\ s+\tfrac{n}{2}+k+2\end{matrix};\,1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma\!\left(m+\tfrac{n}{2}+k+1\right)\,\Gamma\!\left(s+\tfrac{n}{2}+k+2\right)\,\Gamma(s+2n-1)}{\Gamma(m+k+1)\,\Gamma\!\left(s+\tfrac{3n}{2}+k\right)\,\Gamma\!\left(s+\tfrac{3n}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\times{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}\tfrac{n}{2},\ s-m+\tfrac{n}{2}+1,\ s+2n-1\\[4.0pt]
s+\tfrac{3n}{2}+k,\ s+\tfrac{3n}{2}\end{matrix};\,1\right).

After substituting this into the sum and simplifying the Pochhammer symbols, we obtain:

\displaystyle I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})(n-1)_{m}^{2}\Gamma(n+s)\Gamma(s+\frac{n}{2}+1)\Gamma(s+2n-1)}{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(m+n+s)\Gamma(s+\frac{3n}{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(n+s)_{k}(m+n-1)_{k}\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)_{k}}{(m+n+s)_{k}\,k!}\;\frac{\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)_{j}\left(s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1\right)_{j}\left(s+2n-1\right)_{j}}{\left(s+\frac{3n}{2}\right)_{k+j}\left(s+\frac{3n}{2}\right)_{j}\;j!}.

The double sum can be identified as a special case of the Kampé de Fériet function:

\displaystyle I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})(n-1)_{m}^{2}\Gamma(n+s)\Gamma(s+\frac{n}{2}+1)\Gamma(s+2n-1)}{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(m+n+s)\Gamma(s+\frac{3n}{2})^{2}}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\quad\times F^{0:3;3}_{1:1;1}\!\left(\begin{matrix}-:n+s,\;m+n-1,\;1-\frac{n}{2};\,\frac{n}{2},\;s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1,\;s+2n-1\\[3.0pt]
s+\frac{3n}{2}:m+n+s\,;\,s+\frac{3n}{2}\end{matrix};1,1\right).

Note that (
3.11
) implies that

\bigl(\Gamma(n+s)\Gamma(s+\tfrac{n}{2}+1)\Gamma(s+2n-1)\bigr)^{-1}I_{m}(s)

is an analytic function on the entire complex plane.

Since

depends continuously on the parameter

. We can then apply the following reduction formula (see

]

\displaystyle F^{0:3;3}_{1:1;1}\!\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-:a,\,b,\,c\,;\,d-a,\,d-b\,c^{\prime}\\

\displaystyle=\Gamma\!\left(\begin{matrix}d,\;e^{\prime},\;a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}-d,\;d+e^{\prime}-a^{\prime}-b^{\prime}-c^{\prime}\\[3.0pt]
a^{\prime},\;b^{\prime},\;e^{\prime}-c^{\prime},\;d+e^{\prime}-a^{\prime}-b^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right)

e,1+d-a^{\prime}-b^{\prime},d+e^{\prime}-a^{\prime}-b^{\prime}\end{matrix};1\right)

\displaystyle+\Gamma\!\left(\begin{matrix}d,\;e,\;a+b-d,\;d+e-a-b-c\\[3.0pt]
a,\;b,\;e-c,\;d+e-a-b\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}a^{\prime},b^{\prime},e^{\prime}-c^{\prime},d+e-a-b-c\\[3.0pt]
e^{\prime},1+d-a-b,d+e-a-b\end{matrix};1\right),

with
a=n+s
and

and we obtain

\displaystyle F^{0:3;3}_{1:1;1}\!

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-:n+s,\,m+\epsilon+n-1,\,1-\frac{n}{2};\,\frac{n}{2},\,s-m-\epsilon+\frac{n}{2}+1,\,s+2n-1\\[3.0pt]
s+\frac{3n}{2}\,:m+\epsilon+n+s\,;\,s+\frac{3n}{2}\end{array};1,1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(s+\frac{3n}{2})^{2}\Gamma(1-m-\epsilon-\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(m+\epsilon)}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(s-m-\epsilon+\frac{n}{2}+1)\Gamma(1-\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(s+m+\epsilon+2n-1)}

\displaystyle\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}n+s,\,m+\epsilon+n-1,\,m+\epsilon+s+\frac{3n}{2}-1,\,m+\epsilon\\[3.0pt]
m+\epsilon+n+s,\,m+\epsilon+\frac{n}{2},\,s+m+\epsilon+2n-1\end{matrix};1\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Gamma(s+\frac{3n}{2})\Gamma(m+\epsilon+n+s)\Gamma(m+\epsilon+\frac{n}{2}-1)\Gamma(s+n)}{\Gamma(n+s)\Gamma(m+\epsilon+n-1)\Gamma(m+\epsilon+s+\frac{3n}{2}-1)\Gamma(s+\frac{n}{2}+1)}

\displaystyle\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\,s-m-\epsilon+\frac{n}{2}+1,\,1-\frac{n}{2},\,s+n\\[3.0pt]
s+\frac{3n}{2},\,2-m-\epsilon-\frac{n}{2},\,s+\frac{n}{2}+1\end{matrix};1\right).

When

is even, the first term vanishes due to the factor

Therefore, letting

Proposition 3.2

(3.15)

)

Substituting (

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)_{m}}\right)^{2}\frac{\Gamma\left(m+\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma(n+s)}{\Gamma(m+n+s)}

the inner sum in (

\displaystyle I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)_{m}^{2}}{\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)_{m}^{2}}\frac{\Gamma\left(m+\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma(n+s)}{\Gamma(m+n+s)}\frac{\Gamma(n-1)\Gamma\left(m+\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{2}\,\Gamma(m+n-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{(n+s)_{m}}\;{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}m,\;1-\frac{n}{2},\;n+s,\;s+1\\[4.0pt]

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
n\geq 6
is even, then

\lim_{s\to-n+1}(s+n-1)I_{m}(s)=-\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{(1)_{m}}\,\frac{\left(\frac{n}{2}-1\right)_{m}}{\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)_{m}}

and

\lim_{s\to-n+2}(s+n-2)I_{m}(s)=-\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{(2)_{m}}\left(\frac{m(m+1)n\left(\frac{n}{2}-1\right)\left(\frac{n}{2}-2\right)}{2\left(m+\frac{n}{2}-2\right)\left(m+\frac{n}{2}-1\right)\left(m+\frac{n}{2}\right)}\right).

In particular, we have
-n+1,\,-n+2\in\mathcal{W}^{d}_{1}
. For
n=4
, we have
-3\in\mathcal{W}^{d}_{1}.

Proof.

As
n\geq 6
the function
I_{m}(s)
is singular for
s=-n+1
and
s=-n+2
. From Proposition
3.2
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to-n+1}(s+n-1)I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to-n+1}(s+n-1)\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{(n+s)_{m}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\;{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}m,\;1-\frac{n}{2},\;n+s,\;s+1\\[4.0pt]
m+n+s,\;\frac{n}{2}+s+1,\;2-n\end{matrix}\;;\;1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{(1)_{m}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\lim_{s\to-n+1}\sum_{l=0}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{(m)_{l}\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)_{l}(n+s)_{l}(s+1)_{l}}{(m+n+s)_{l}\left(\frac{n}{2}+s+1\right)_{l}(2-n)_{l}}\frac{1}{l!}.

The only non-zero term in the sum is the one corresponding to
l=\frac{n}{2}-1
. Using the fact

\lim_{s\to-n+1}\,\frac{(s+n-1)}{\left(\frac{n}{2}+s+1\right)_{\frac{n}{2}-1}}=\frac{(-1)^{\frac{n}{2}-2}}{\left(\frac{n}{2}-2\right)!}

we obtain

\lim_{s\to-n+1}(s+n-1)I_{m}(s)=-\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{\Gamma(m)}\,\frac{\left(\frac{n}{2}-1\right)_{m}}{\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)_{m}}.

Similarly using

\lim_{s\to-n+2}\frac{(s+n-2)}{\left(\frac{n}{2}+s+1\right)_{\frac{n}{2}-1}}=\frac{(-1)^{\frac{n}{2}-3}}{\left(\frac{n}{2}-3\right)!},\qquad\lim_{s\to-n+2}\frac{(s+n-2)}{\left(\frac{n}{2}+s+1\right)_{\frac{n}{2}-2}}=\frac{(-1)^{\frac{n}{2}-3}}{\left(\frac{n}{2}-3\right)!}

(4.1)

we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to-n+2}(s+n-2)I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{(2)_{m}}\lim_{s\to-n+2}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{(s+n-2)\,(m)_{l}\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)_{l}(n+s)_{l}(s+1)_{l}}{(m+n+s)_{l}\left(\frac{n}{2}+s+1\right)_{l}(2-n)_{l}}\frac{1}{l!}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{(2)_{m}}\left(-\frac{m(m+1)n\left(\frac{n}{2}-1\right)\left(\frac{n}{2}-2\right)}{4\left(m+\frac{n}{2}-2\right)\left(m+\frac{n}{2}-1\right)}+\frac{m(m+1)n\left(\frac{n}{2}-1\right)\left(\frac{n}{2}-2\right)}{4\left(m+\frac{n}{2}-1\right)\left(m+\frac{n}{2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{(2)_{m}}\left(\frac{m(m+1)n\left(\frac{n}{2}-1\right)\left(\frac{n}{2}-2\right)}{2\left(m+\frac{n}{2}-2\right)\left(m+\frac{n}{2}-1\right)\left(m+\frac{n}{2}\right)}\right).

Hence, we conclude that

-n+1,\,-n+2\in\mathcal{W}^{d}_{1}.

When
n=4
, only
-3
belongs to
\mathcal{W}_{1}^{d}
, since
I_{m}(s)
is regular at
s=-2
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

It was shown in
[
8
, Proposition 1]
that for
m>0

\lim_{s\rightarrow-n}(n+s)\,I_{m}(s)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{(n-1)_{m}}{\Gamma(m)},&\text{if }n\text{ is even},\\[8.61108pt]
\dfrac{2(n-1)_{m}}{\Gamma(m)},&\text{if }n\text{ is odd},\end{cases}

(4.2)

with the residue at
s=-n-1
being identical.
We can recover this result from Proposition
3.1
. Suppose that
n
is odd. Then, from Proposition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)_{m}^{2}}{(n+s)_{m}}\,\Gamma\!\left(\begin{matrix}s+\frac{n}{2}+1,\;s+2n-1,\;1-m-\frac{n}{2}\\
s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1,\;1-\frac{n}{2},\;m+2n+s-1\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}n+s,\,m+n-1,\,m+s+\frac{3n}{2}-1,\,m\\
m+n+s,\,m+\frac{n}{2},\,m+2n+s-1\end{matrix};1\right)

\displaystyle+(n-1)_{m}^{2}\Gamma\!\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\;s+2n-1,\;m+\frac{n}{2}-1,\;s+n\\
m,\;m+n-1,\;s+\frac{3n}{2},\;m+s+\frac{3n}{2}-1\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\,s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1,\,1-\frac{n}{2},\,s+n\\
s+\frac{3n}{2},\,2-m-\frac{n}{2},\,s+\frac{n}{2}+1\end{matrix};1\right).

Using the facts

\lim_{s\rightarrow-n}\frac{(s+n)}{(s+n)_{m+k}}=\frac{1}{(m+k-1)!},\quad\lim_{s\rightarrow-n}(s+n)\Gamma(s+n)=1,

together with

\lim_{s\rightarrow-n-1}\frac{s+n+1}{(s+n)_{m+k}}=-\frac{1}{(m+k-2)!}\quad\text{for $m+k\geq 1$},\quad\lim_{s\rightarrow-n-1}(s+n+1)\Gamma(s+n)=-1

one can see, from a direct calculation, that each term in the residue of
I_{m}(s)
at both
s=-n
and
s=-n-1
is equal to

\frac{(n-1)_{m}}{\Gamma(m)}.

As the first term vanishes when
n
is even, our conclusion follows.

Theorem 4.4
.

For
n
odd, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow-n+\frac{1}{2}}(s+n-\frac{1}{2})\,I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=-\Gamma\!\left(\begin{matrix}m+n-1,\;m-\frac{1}{2},\;\frac{n}{2},\;\frac{n}{2},\;n-\frac{1}{2},\;n-\frac{1}{2}\\[3.0pt]

\displaystyle\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2},&\frac{n}{2},&n-\frac{1}{2},&1-m\\
\frac{n+1}{2},&n,&\frac{3}{2}-m\end{matrix}\;;\;1\right),

and

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow-n-\frac{1}{2}}(s+n+\frac{1}{2})\,I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=-\Gamma\!\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{3}{2},\;m+n-1,\;\frac{n}{2},\;n-\frac{3}{2},\;n+\frac{1}{2},\;m+\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2},\;m-\frac{3}{2}\\[3.0pt]

\displaystyle\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{3}{2},\;\frac{n}{2},\;n+\frac{1}{2},\;1-m\\[3.0pt]
\frac{n+3}{2},\;\frac{5}{2}-m,\;n\end{matrix};1\right).

In particular, we have
-n-\frac{1}{2},\,-n+\frac{1}{2}\in\mathcal{W}^{d}_{1}

Proof.

When
n
is odd, from Proposition
3.1
we have

\displaystyle I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)_{m}^{2}}{(n+s)_{m}}\,\Gamma\!\left(\begin{matrix}s+\frac{n}{2}+1,\;s+2n-1,\;1-m-\frac{n}{2}\\

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}n+s,\,m+n-1,\,m+s+\frac{3n}{2}-1,\,m\\
m+n+s,\,m+\frac{n}{2},\,m+2n+s-1\end{matrix};1\right)

\displaystyle+(n-1)_{m}^{2}\Gamma\!\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\;s+2n-1,\;m+\frac{n}{2}-1,\;s+n\\
m,\;m+n-1,\;s+\frac{3n}{2},\;m+s+\frac{3n}{2}-1\end{matrix}\right)

(4.4)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\,s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1,\,1-\frac{n}{2},\,s+n\\
s+\frac{3n}{2},\,2-m-\frac{n}{2},\,s+\frac{n}{2}+1\end{matrix};1\right).

We first compute residue at
s=-n+\frac{1}{2}.

For

each Gamma function in (

) is regular at

. For

we have

\lim_{s\rightarrow-n+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(s+\frac{n}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1)}=(-1)^{m}\,m!,

hence the first prefactor is regular for each

Also the prefactor of (
4.4
) is regular, thus

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow-n+\frac{1}{2}}(s+n-\frac{1}{2})\,I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=G_{m}\lim_{s\rightarrow-n+\frac{1}{2}}(s+n-\frac{1}{2})\,{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\,s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1,\,1-\frac{n}{2},\,s+n\\
s+\frac{3n}{2},\,2-m-\frac{n}{2},\,s+\frac{n}{2}+1\end{matrix};1\right)

where

G_{m}=(n-1)_{m}^{2}\,\Gamma\!\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\;n-\frac{1}{2},\;m+\frac{n}{2}-1,\;\frac{1}{2}\\
m,\;m+n-1,\;\frac{n+1}{2},\;m+\frac{n}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\end{matrix}\right).

Using (
2

\displaystyle{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\,s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1,\,1-\frac{n}{2},\,s+n\\
s+\frac{3n}{2},\,2-m-\frac{n}{2},\,s+\frac{n}{2}+1\end{matrix};1\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Gamma(m+\frac{n-1}{2})\Gamma\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(m)\,\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma(n)}\frac{\Gamma\!\left(2-m-\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{3}{2}-m\right)}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\times_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2},&\frac{n}{2},&n-\frac{1}{2},&1-m\\
\frac{n+1}{2},&n,&\frac{3}{2}-m\end{matrix}\;;\;1\right),

(4.6)

here we rewrote Pochhammer symbols as Gamma functions.
Using Legendre’s formula

\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi z)},

(4.7)

we get

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow n+\frac{1}{2}}(s+n-\frac{1}{2})\,I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=-\Gamma\!\left(\begin{matrix}m+n-1,\;m-\frac{1}{2},\;\frac{n}{2},\;\frac{n}{2},\;n-\frac{1}{2},\;n-\frac{1}{2}\\[3.0pt]

\displaystyle\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2},&\frac{n}{2},&n-\frac{1}{2},&1-m\\

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow-n-\frac{1}{2}}(s+n+\frac{1}{2})\,I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=-\Gamma\!\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{3}{2},\;m+n-1,\;\frac{n}{2},\;n-\frac{3}{2},\;n+\frac{1}{2},\;m+\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2},\;m-\frac{3}{2}\\[3.0pt]

\displaystyle\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{3}{2},\;\frac{n}{2},\;n+\frac{1}{2},\;1-m\\[3.0pt]
\frac{n+3}{2},\;\frac{5}{2}-m,\;n\end{matrix};1\right),

thus
-n-\frac{1}{2}\in\mathcal{W}_{1}^{d}
and our conclusion follows.
∎

{}_{4}F_{3}
is positive for each
m
. Consequently (
4.14
) does not change sign and the claim follows.
∎

5
Asymptotic expansion of
I_{m}(s)

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose that
n
is odd,
s+n-1\geq 0
, and

s\neq-\frac{n}{2}-k
for all
k=0,1,2\dots
.

Then

\lim_{m\to\infty}m^{s+1}I_{m}(s)=\Gamma\!\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\;s+2n-1,\;1+\frac{s+n}{2},\;s+\frac{n}{2}+1,\;\frac{s+n}{2}\\[4.0pt]
n-1,\;n-1,\;s+n+1,\;n+\frac{s}{2},\;1+\frac{s}{2}\end{matrix}\right).

(5.1)

If
n
is even, the same limit holds without the additional assumption
s+n-1\geq 0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
n
is odd and
s+n-1\geq 0
, then we know, from Proposition
3.1
that
I_{m}(s)
is equal to

\displaystyle I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(n-1+m)^{2}\,\Gamma(n+s)\,\Gamma(s+\frac{n}{2}+1)\,\Gamma(s+2n-1)\,\Gamma(1-m-\frac{n}{2})}{\Gamma(n-1)^{2}\,\Gamma(m+n+s)\,\Gamma(s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1)\,\Gamma(1-\frac{n}{2})\,\Gamma(m+2n+s-1)}

(5.2)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}n+s,\,m+n-1,\,m+s+\frac{3n}{2}-1,\,m\\[4.0pt]
m+n+s,\,m+\frac{n}{2},\,m+2n+s-1\end{matrix};1\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\,\Gamma(n-1+m)\,\Gamma(s+2n-1)\,\Gamma(m+\frac{n}{2}-1)\,\Gamma(s+n)}{\Gamma(m)\,\Gamma(n-1)^{2}\,\Gamma(s+\frac{3n}{2})\,\Gamma(m+s+\frac{3n}{2}-1)}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\,s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1,\,1-\frac{n}{2},\,s+n\\[4.0pt]
s+\frac{3n}{2},\,2-m-\frac{n}{2},\,s+\frac{n}{2}+1\end{matrix};1\right).

We first examine the behaviour of the first factor. We may use (
4.7
) to write

\frac{\Gamma\!\left(1-m-\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma\!\left(s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1\right)}=-\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma\!\left(m-s-\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\pi\,\Gamma\!\left(m+\frac{n}{2}\right)}\,\sin\!\left(\pi\left(s+\frac{n}{2}\right)\right),

(5.4)

thus the prefactor of (
5.2
) is equal to

\displaystyle\sin\!\left(\pi\left(s+\frac{n}{2}\right)\right)\frac{\Gamma(n-1+m)^{2}\,\Gamma(n+s)\,\Gamma(s+\frac{n}{2}+1)\,\Gamma(s+2n-1)\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma\!\left(m-s-\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\pi\Gamma(n-1)^{2}\,\Gamma(m+n+s)\,\Gamma(m+2n+s-1)\,\Gamma\!\left(m+\frac{n}{2}\right)}.

Now, using (
3.10
) the prefactor of (
5.2
) is, for large
m
, equal to

\sin\!\left(\pi\left(s+\frac{n}{2}\right)\right)\frac{\Gamma(n+s)\,\Gamma\!\left(s+\frac{n}{2}+1\right)\,\Gamma(s+2n-1)\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\pi\,\Gamma(n-1)^{2}}\,m^{-2n-3s-1}+\,\mathcal{O}(m^{-2n-3s-2}).

To analyse the asymptotic behaviour of the
{}_{4}F_{3}
in (
5.2
) we invoke the following asymptotic expansion for large parameters (see
[
5
]
, Eq. (16.11.10), p. 412):

{}_{{p+1}}F_{p}\!\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1}+r,\ldots,a_{k-1}+r,a_{k},\ldots,a_{p+1}\\
b_{1}+r,\ldots,b_{k}+r,b_{k+1},\ldots,b_{p}\end{matrix};z\right)\\
=\sum_{i=0}^{\alpha-1}\frac{(a_{1}+r)_{i}\cdots(a_{k-1}+r)_{i}(a_{k})_{i}\cdots(a_{p+1})_{i}}{(b_{1}+r)_{i}\cdots(b_{k}+r)_{i}(b_{k+1})_{i}\cdots(b_{p})_{i}}\frac{z^{i}}{i!}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\right),

(5.5)

valid for any non-negative integer
\alpha
and
k
from 1 to
p
. Applying this to (
5.2
), with
p=3
and
\alpha=1
we see that the
{}_{4}F_{3}
is equal to
\mathcal{O}(1)
.
Thus (
5.2
) is equal to
\mathcal{O}(m^{-2n-3s-1})
.

Again, using estimate (
3.10
) we see that the Gamma prefactor of (
5.3
) is approximately equal to

\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma(s+2n-1)\Gamma(s+n)}{\Gamma(n-1)^{2}\Gamma\!\left(s+\frac{3n}{2}\right)}\,m^{-s-1}\biggl(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)\biggr).

Now, the second hypergeometric function is equal to

\displaystyle{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\,s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1,\,1-\frac{n}{2},\,s+n\\
s+\frac{3n}{2},\,2-m-\frac{n}{2},\,s+\frac{n}{2}+1\end{matrix};1\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)_{k}\left(s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1\right)_{k}\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)_{k}(s+n)_{k}}{\left(s+\frac{3n}{2}\right)_{k}\left(2-m-\frac{n}{2}\right)_{k}\left(s+\frac{n}{2}+1\right)_{k}}\frac{1}{k!}.

(5.6)

We now provide an upper bound, independent of
m
, for (
5.6
).

Notice that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\left(s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1\right)_{k}}{\left(2-m-\frac{n}{2}\right)_{k}}\right|

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\left|1+\frac{s+n-1}{j+2-m-\frac{n}{2}}\right|

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq k-1\\
j+2-m-\frac{n}{2}>0\end{subarray}}\Bigg|1+\frac{s+n-1}{j+2-m-\frac{n}{2}}\Bigg|\,\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq k-1\\
j+2-m-\frac{n}{2}<0\end{subarray}}\Bigg|1+\frac{s+n-1}{j+2-m-\frac{n}{2}}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\Bigg|1+\frac{s+n-1}{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg|\,\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq k-1\\
j+2-m-\frac{n}{2}<0\end{subarray}}\Bigg|1+\frac{s+n-1}{j+2-m-\frac{n}{2}}\Bigg|.

Using the estimate
\log(1+x)<x
for
x>0
, we get

\displaystyle\log\prod_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\left(1+\frac{s+n-1}{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\log\left(1+\frac{s+n-1}{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(s+n-1)\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(s+n-1)\log(1+k)+C,

for
s+n-1\geq 0
. Thus, the first product can be bounded by

C(s,n)(1+k)^{s+n-1}.

Now

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq k-1\\
j+2-m-\frac{n}{2}<0\end{subarray}}\Bigg|1+\frac{s+n-1}{j+2-m-\frac{n}{2}}\Bigg|=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq k-1\\
j+2-m-\frac{n}{2}<0\end{subarray}}\Bigg|1-\frac{s+n-1}{\left|j+2-m-\frac{n}{2}\right|}\Bigg|<C^{\prime}(n,s).

Indeed, whenever

s+n-1<\left|j+2-m-\frac{n}{2}\right|,

the corresponding factor is bounded by
1
. Hence, it remains only to consider those indices
j
for which

\left|j+2-m-\frac{n}{2}\right|\leq s+n-1.

There are only finitely many such indices, at most

2\lceil s+n-1\rceil+1
.

Hence, (
5.6
) can be uniformly bounded from above by

C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Bigg|\frac{\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)_{k}\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)_{k}(s+n)_{k}}{\left(s+\frac{3n}{2}\right)_{k}\left(s+\frac{n}{2}+1\right)_{k}}\frac{(1+k)^{s+n-1}}{k!}\Bigg|.

(5.7)

Using (
3.10
), it follows that this sum is absolutely convergent.

Thus, from Tannery’s theorem
[
14
, p. 136, §49]
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\,s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1,\,1-\frac{n}{2},\,s+n\\
s+\frac{3n}{2},\,2-m-\frac{n}{2},\,s+\frac{n}{2}+1\end{matrix};1\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left(s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1\right)_{k}}{\left(2-m-\frac{n}{2}\right)_{k}}\frac{\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)_{k}\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)_{k}(s+n)_{k}}{\left(s+\frac{3n}{2}\right)_{k}\left(s+\frac{n}{2}+1\right)_{k}}\frac{1}{k!}

\displaystyle={}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\;1-\frac{n}{2},\;s+n,\\[4.0pt]
s+\frac{3n}{2},\;s+\frac{n}{2}+1\;\end{matrix};1\right),

as

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left(s-m+\frac{n}{2}+1\right)_{k}}{\left(2-m-\frac{n}{2}\right)_{k}}=1.

Therefore

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}m^{s+1}I_{m}(s)=\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma(s+2n-1)\Gamma(s+n)}{\Gamma(n-1)^{2}\Gamma\!\left(s+\frac{3n}{2}\right)}{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\;1-\frac{n}{2},\;s+n,\\
s+\frac{3n}{2},\;s+\frac{n}{2}+1\;\end{matrix};1\right).

(5.8)

Using Dixon’s formula (see
[
1
]
, Eq. (6), p. 189)

{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}a,\ b,\ c\\
1+a-b,\ 1+a-c\end{matrix};1\right)=\frac{\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{a}{2}\right)\Gamma(1+a-b)\Gamma(1+a-c)\Gamma\!\left(1-b-c+\frac{a}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(1+a)\Gamma\!\left(1-b+\frac{a}{2}\right)\Gamma\!\left(1-c+\frac{a}{2}\right)\Gamma(1+a-b-c)},

with
a=s+n
,
b=1-\frac{n}{2}
and
c=\frac{n}{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}m^{s+1}I_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma(s+2n-1)\Gamma(s+n)}{\Gamma(n-1)^{2}\Gamma\!\left(s+\frac{3n}{2}\right)}{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{n}{2},\;1-\frac{n}{2},\;s+n,\\[4.0pt]
s+\frac{3n}{2},\;s+\frac{n}{2}+1,\;\end{matrix};1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma(s+2n-1)\Gamma(s+n)}{\Gamma(n-1)^{2}\Gamma\!\left(s+\frac{3n}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{s+n}{2}\right)\Gamma\!\left(s+\frac{3n}{2}\right)\Gamma\!\left(s+\frac{n}{2}+1\right)\Gamma\!\left(\frac{s+n}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(s+n+1)\,\Gamma\!\left(n+\frac{s}{2}\right)\,\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{s}{2}\right)\,\Gamma(s+n)}\right).

When
n
is even, the same argument shows that
m^{s+1}I_{m}(s)
is equal to (
) without the restriction that
s+n-1\geq 0
as the term (
5.2
) is missing and (
5.3
) is uniformly convergent, thus the claim follows.
∎
