Algorithmic Barriers to Detecting and Repairing Structural Overspecification in Adaptive Data-Structure Selection
Faruk Alpay, Levent Sarioglu

Abstract
We study algorithmic barriers to detecting and repairing a systematic form of structural overspecification in adaptive data-structure selection. An input instance induces an implied workload signature—ordering, sparsity, dynamism, locality, or substring structure—and candidate implementations may be preferred because they match that full signature even when the measured workload evidence supports only a strict subset of it. Working under a model in which pairwise evaluators favor implementations that realize the implied signature, we show that this preference propagates through both benchmark aggregation and Bradley–Terry–Luce fitting. We then establish two results. First, a sharp decidability boundary: determining whether a representation-selection pipeline exhibits structural commitment beyond measured warrant is undecidable on unbounded input domains (by reduction from the halting problem) but decidable with exponential enumeration cost on finite domains. Second, a fixed-point barrier: under a conservative program-transformation constraint—the repair operator must leave already evidence-aligned pipelines unchanged—any total computable repair operator admits an overspecified fixed point, constructed via Kleene’s recursion theorem. These barriers are qualitatively distinct from classical lower bounds in data-structure design: cell-probe and dynamic-graph lower bounds constrain the efficiency of answering operations on finite workloads, while our results constrain the possibility of uniformly detecting and repairing overspecification across pipeline families.

Selecting an implementation or representation for a workload—choosing between adjacency lists and adjacency matrices, balanced search trees and hashing, suffix arrays and suffix trees, or static and dynamic graph primitives—is a central task in data structures and algorithm design

,

Modern systems increasingly make such choices from traces, benchmark outcomes, or learned cost models, often using pairwise comparisons between candidate implementations and then aggregating those outcomes into a single score or ranking
[

,

,

The complexity of data-structure design is well studied:
dynamic data structures admit update/query trade-offs
[

,

[

,

.
These results are

This paper identifies a different type of barrier—a
computability barrier
—that arises when the representation-selection process is systematically overspecified.
Specifically, we formalize a structural-preference channel:
when an input instance suggests a workload signature, evaluators may prefer implementations that realize the full implied signature even when the measured evidence only warrants a smaller subset.
For example, a sparse graph workload may be mapped to aggressively dynamic graph machinery without evidence of adversarial updates; a string-processing task may trigger heavy suffix-based indexing from weak locality cues; a dictionary workload may be assigned ordered search structures from incidental sortedness hints.
Under standard pairwise aggregation models, this preference provably propagates into the learned scoring function (Propositions
3.2
and
3.4
).
The algorithmic questions are then: can one
detect
such overspecification, and can one
repair
it uniformly?

Our contributions.

We establish two main results, each with explicit scope audits.

(i)

Decidability boundary (Section
5
).

The problem of deciding whether a representation-selection pipeline exhibits structural commitment beyond measured warrant is undecidable on unbounded input domains (Theorem
5.6
), via reduction from the halting problem.
On finite domains, the same predicate is decidable by exhaustive enumeration at exponential cost (Proposition
5.9
).
This is a sharp computability boundary.

(ii)

Fixed-point barrier (Section
6
).

Under a conservative constraint on program transformers—the repair operator must leave evidence-aligned pipelines unchanged—any total computable repair operator admits an overspecified fixed point (Theorem
6.6
), constructed via Kleene’s recursion theorem.
No conservative repair operator can uniformly eliminate overspecification across all pipelines.

These barriers are qualitatively distinct from classical data-structure lower bounds:
cell-probe and dynamic-graph lower bounds constrain time and space trade-offs on finite workloads;
our results are computability barriers that no amount of computation can overcome on unbounded pipeline families.

2
Formal framework

2.1
Instances, implementations, and computable selection pipelines

Fix a finite alphabet
\Sigma
.
Let
\Sigma^{\ast}=\Sigma^{\ast}
denote the set of all finite strings over
\Sigma
.
We model workload instances as strings
x\in\Sigma^{\ast}
and candidate implementations or representations as strings
y\in\Sigma^{\ast}
.

Definition 2.1

(Representation-selection pipeline class)
.

A
representation-selection pipeline class

\mathcal{F}
is a recursively enumerable set of total computable functions

f:\Sigma^{\ast}\to\Sigma^{\ast}
.
Equivalently, there exists an effective enumeration
\{f_{e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{N}}

such that
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{e}:e\in\mathbb{N}\}
and each
f_{e}
halts on every input.

Remark 2.2
.

Restricting the domain to
\Sigma^{\leq n}
makes it finite and many existential predicates decidable.
This is critical for the decidability boundary in Section
5
.

2.2
Workload signatures, measured warrant, and structural compatibility

Definition 2.3

(Workload-signature extractor)
.

A
workload-signature extractor
is a total computable function

S:\Sigma^{\ast}\to 2^{\Sigma^{\ast}}

mapping each instance
x
to a finite set
S(x)
of structural feature strings.
These features may encode properties such as sorted access, sparsity, online updates, locality, or substring reuse.

Definition 2.4

(Measured-warrant extractor)
.

A
measured-warrant extractor
is a total computable function

W:\Sigma^{\ast}\to 2^{\Sigma^{\ast}}

such that
W(x)\subseteq S(x)
for all
x
,
with
W(x)
interpreted as the structural features actually supported by the observed trace, benchmark, or specification.

Definition 2.5

(Structural compatibility relation and scores)
.

A
structural compatibility relation
is a total computable function

V:\Sigma^{\ast}\times\Sigma^{\ast}\to\{-1,0,+1\}
.
The
structural compatibility score
is

v(x,y)=\sum_{s\in S(x)}V(y,s)
.
The
beyond-warrant overspecification score
is

v_{\mathrm{bw}}(x,y)=\sum_{s\in S(x)\setminus W(x)}V(y,s).

(1)

A positive value indicates that implementation
y
instantiates structure suggested by the full signature of
x
but not warranted by measured evidence.

Remark 2.6
.

Any property of the form “property of the input/output behavior of a total computable function”
falls under Rice-style limits on unbounded domains (Section
5
).

2.3
Benchmark aggregation and pairwise score fitting

Definition 2.7

(Benchmark profile and implementation aggregation)
.

Fix
k
evaluators, an instance
x
, and candidate set
Y_{x}
.
A
benchmark profile
is
\pi=(\succ_{1},\dots,\succ_{k})
, where each
\succ_{i}
ranks candidate implementations.
An
implementation aggregation rule

A
maps such profiles to aggregate orders.

Definition 2.8

(Decisive benchmark family)
.

C\subseteq[k]
is
decisive for
(y,y^{\prime})

if
(\forall i\in C:y\succ_{i}y^{\prime})\Rightarrow y\succ_{A}y^{\prime}
,
regardless of the preferences of the remaining evaluators.

Definition 2.9

(Logistic pairwise-implementation likelihood)
.

Let
\sigma(t)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-t}}
.
A scoring function
R:\Sigma^{\ast}\times\Sigma^{\ast}\to\mathbb{R}
induces

\Prb[y\succ y^{\prime}\mid x]=\sigma(R(x,y)-R(x,y^{\prime}))
.
Bradley–Terry–Luce fitting provides a standard pairwise scoring model for such outcomes
[
13
,
14
]
.

3
Overspecification model: Inheritance through benchmark aggregation

This section specifies the structural overspecification model under which our algorithmic results operate.
The propositions here are not our main contributions; they formalize the precondition that unwarranted structural preference
does
propagate through standard benchmark aggregation, establishing that detection and repair are problems worth studying.

3.1
Deterministic inheritance under decisiveness

Definition 3.1

(Signature-monotone evaluator)
.

An evaluator ranking
\succ_{i}
is
signature-monotone on
(y,y^{\prime})
if

v(x,y)>v(x,y^{\prime})\Rightarrow y\succ_{i}y^{\prime}
.

Proposition 3.2

(Inheritance under decisive benchmark families)
.

Fix
x
,
Y_{x}
with
|Y_{x}|\geq 3
, and an implementation aggregation rule
A
.
If there exists a family
C
decisive for
(y,y^{\prime})

with every
i\in C
signature-monotone on
(y,y^{\prime})
,
then for every benchmark profile
\pi
:

v(x,y)>v(x,y^{\prime})\Rightarrow y\succ_{A}y^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Signature-monotonicity forces each
i\in C
to rank
y\succ_{i}y^{\prime}
; decisiveness forces
y\succ_{A}y^{\prime}
.
∎

3.2
Statistical inheritance under pairwise score fitting

Definition 3.3

(Random-utility evaluators)
.

For evaluator
i
, latent utility is

u_{i}(x,y)=q_{i}(x,y)+\alpha_{i}\,v(x,y)
,
where
q_{i}
is structural-preference-independent and
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
is signature sensitivity.
Pairwise outcomes follow
\Prb_{i}[y\succ y^{\prime}\mid x]=\sigma(u_{i}(x,y)-u_{i}(x,y^{\prime}))
.

Proposition 3.4

(Pairwise-fit inheritance under quality control)
.

Assume Definition
3.3
with
q_{i}(x,y)=q_{i}(x,y^{\prime})
for all
i
,

\alpha_{i}\geq 0
, and
\bar{\alpha}>0
.
Let
\Delta v=v(x,y)-v(x,y^{\prime})
.
If
\Delta v>0
, then

\Prb[y\succ y^{\prime}\mid x]>\tfrac{1}{2}

and the probability is strictly increasing in
\Delta v
.

Proof.

Under quality control,

\Prb[y\succ y^{\prime}\mid x]=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i}\sigma(\alpha_{i}\Delta v)
.
Each term
\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
; at least one is strict. Averaging gives
>\tfrac{1}{2}
.
Strict monotonicity:

\frac{d}{d\Delta v}\sigma(\alpha_{i}\Delta v)=\alpha_{i}\sigma(\alpha_{i}\Delta v)(1-\sigma(\alpha_{i}\Delta v))\geq 0
,
strict for
\alpha_{i_{0}}>0
.
∎

Corollary 3.5

(Learned scores inherit structural overspecification)
.

Under Proposition
3.4
’s conditions,
any consistent pairwise score estimator satisfies

\Delta v>0\Rightarrow\widehat{R}(x,y)>\widehat{R}(x,y^{\prime})

in the large-sample limit.

Proof.

Consistency gives
\widehat{p}\to p^{\star}>\tfrac{1}{2}
; the logistic link converts this to a positive score difference.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Proposition
3.4
can fail under mixed-sign
\alpha_{i}
, mismeasured workload traces, insufficient pair coverage, or explicit regularizers penalizing structural complexity.

4
Overspecification model: Asymmetric underprovision penalties

The model is completed by noting that missing structure and adding extra structure need not be penalized symmetrically.
In algorithm engineering, underprovision is often treated as more costly than overprovision:
failing to provide dynamic updates or locality support can be penalized more heavily than carrying redundant machinery.
This asymmetry helps explain why repair is harder than detection:
a repair operator must undo a direction-dependent distortion, not merely subtract a constant.

Definition 4.1

(Piecewise-linear regret weighting)
.

Evaluator
i
uses
w_{i}(\delta)=\alpha_{i}\delta
for
\delta\geq 0
and
w_{i}(\delta)=-\lambda_{i}\alpha_{i}|\delta|
for
\delta<0
,
where
\alpha_{i}>0
and
\lambda_{i}\geq 1

[
19
,
18
]
.
With
u_{i}(x,y)=q_{i}(x,y)+w_{i}(v(x,y))
,
define the population score
R(x,y)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i}u_{i}(x,y)
.

Proposition 4.2

(Sensitivity-weighted underprovision ratio)
.

Let
y^{+},y^{0},y^{-}
have structural compatibility scores
+\delta,0,-\delta
with equal baseline quality.
Define
\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}:=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\alpha_{i}/\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}
.
Then

R(x,y^{0})-R(x,y^{-})=\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}\cdot(R(x,y^{+})-R(x,y^{0}))
,
with
\min_{i}\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}\leq\max_{i}\lambda_{i}
.

Proof.

Under equal baseline quality:

R(x,y^{+})-R(x,y^{0})=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\delta

and

R(x,y^{0})-R(x,y^{-})=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\alpha_{i}\delta
.
Dividing gives
\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}
, a convex combination of
\lambda_{i}
.
∎

5
Main result I: Decidability boundary for overspecification detection

We now present our first main result: a sharp decidability boundary for detecting structural overspecification in representation-selection pipelines.

5.1
The overspecification predicate

Definition 5.1

(Beyond-warrant overspecification predicate)
.

For a total computable pipeline
f:\Sigma^{\ast}\to\Sigma^{\ast}
,

B_{\mathrm{bw}}(f)=\begin{cases}1,&\exists x\in\Sigma^{\ast}:\;v_{\mathrm{bw}}(x,f(x))>0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(2)

Definition 5.2

(Index-set form)
.

Fix an effective enumeration
\{f_{e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{N}}
of total computable pipelines.
The corresponding index set is

\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{bw}}:=\{e\in\mathbb{N}:B_{\mathrm{bw}}(f_{e})=1\}.

Lemma 5.3

(Extensionality)
.

If
f,g:\Sigma^{\ast}\to\Sigma^{\ast}
are total computable and
f(x)=g(x)
for all
x\in\Sigma^{\ast}
,
then
B_{\mathrm{bw}}(f)=B_{\mathrm{bw}}(g)
.
Equivalently, if
f_{e}=f_{e^{\prime}}
extensionally, then

e\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{bw}}\Leftrightarrow e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{bw}}
.

Proof.

By pointwise equality, for every
x
we have

v_{\mathrm{bw}}(x,f(x))=v_{\mathrm{bw}}(x,g(x))
.
Hence the existential condition in (
2
) is identical for
f
and
g
.
∎

Lemma 5.4

(Non-triviality)
.

Assume there exist
x_{0}\in\Sigma^{\ast}
,
s_{0}\in\Sigma^{\ast}
,
y^{+}\in\Sigma^{\ast}
, and a padding symbol
\#\in\Sigma

such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

s_{0}\in S(x_{0}\#^{n})\setminus W(x_{0}\#^{n})\quad\text{and}\quad V(y^{+},s_{0})=+1.

Then
B_{\mathrm{bw}}
is non-trivial:

f_{1}\equiv y^{+}
gives
B_{\mathrm{bw}}(f_{1})=1
;

f_{2}\equiv\epsilon
(with
V(\epsilon,s)=0
for all
s
) gives
B_{\mathrm{bw}}(f_{2})=0
.

Proof.

For
f_{1}\equiv y^{+}
, evaluate at
x_{0}
:

v_{\mathrm{bw}}(x_{0},f_{1}(x_{0}))=\sum_{s\in S(x_{0})\setminus W(x_{0})}V(y^{+},s)\geq V(y^{+},s_{0})=1>0,

so
B_{\mathrm{bw}}(f_{1})=1
.
For
f_{2}\equiv\epsilon
, by assumption
V(\epsilon,s)=0
for all
s
, hence

v_{\mathrm{bw}}(x,f_{2}(x))=0
for every
x
, so
B_{\mathrm{bw}}(f_{2})=0
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

The padding condition in Lemma
5.4
is a technical device
for the halting reduction: it provides an unbounded
“clock”
family

x_{0}\#^{n}
while preserving a fixed beyond-warrant witness
s_{0}
.
It holds whenever
S
and
W
are invariant under this padding.

5.2
Undecidability on unbounded domains

Theorem 5.6

(Undecidability of overspecification detection)
.

Under the conditions of Lemma
5.4
,
there is no Turing machine that decides
B_{\mathrm{bw}}(f)
for all total computable
f
.

Proof.

Let
H=\{\langle M,w\rangle:M\text{ halts on input }w\}
.
Fix witnesses
x_{0},s_{0},y^{+},\epsilon,\#
from Lemma
5.4
.

For each pair
\langle M,w\rangle
, define a total computable function
f_{M,w}:\Sigma^{\ast}\to\Sigma^{\ast}
by

f_{M,w}(x)=\begin{cases}y^{+},&x=x_{0}\#^{n}\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }M(w)\text{ halts within }n^{2}\text{ steps},\\
\epsilon,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Totality is immediate because bounded simulation to
n^{2}
steps always halts.
By the
s
-
m
-
n
theorem, there is a total computable map

r:\langle M,w\rangle\mapsto e_{M,w}
such that
f_{e_{M,w}}=f_{M,w}
.

We now verify correctness of the reduction.

If
\langle M,w\rangle\in H
:

suppose
M(w)
halts in exactly
t
steps.
Choose
n\geq\lceil\sqrt{t}\rceil
; then
n^{2}\geq t
, hence

f_{M,w}(x_{0}\#^{n})=y^{+}
.
Therefore

v_{\mathrm{bw}}(x_{0}\#^{n},f_{M,w}(x_{0}\#^{n}))=\sum_{s\in S(x_{0}\#^{n})\setminus W(x_{0}\#^{n})}V(y^{+},s)\geq V(y^{+},s_{0})=1>0,

so
B_{\mathrm{bw}}(f_{M,w})=1
.

If
\langle M,w\rangle\notin H
:

no bounded simulation ever observes halting, so
f_{M,w}(x)=\epsilon
for all
x
.
Hence
v_{\mathrm{bw}}(x,f_{M,w}(x))=0
for all
x
, and
B_{\mathrm{bw}}(f_{M,w})=0
.

Thus,

\langle M,w\rangle\in H\iff B_{\mathrm{bw}}(f_{M,w})=1\iff e_{M,w}\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{bw}}.

If
B_{\mathrm{bw}}
were decidable on all total computable pipelines,
then
H
would be decidable, contradiction.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

Alternatively,
B_{\mathrm{bw}}
is a non-trivial semantic property of total computable functions, so undecidability follows from Rice’s theorem
[
17
]
.
The explicit reduction additionally shows
B_{\mathrm{bw}}
is
\Sigma_{1}^{0}
-hard.

Proposition 5.8

(Semi-decidability of positive instances)
.

Under the promise that input indices denote total computable pipelines,
the language
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{bw}}
is recursively enumerable.

Proof.

Fix an effective enumeration
(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\Sigma^{\ast}
.
Given an index
e
, run a dovetailing procedure over stage
t=0,1,2,\dots
:
for each
j\leq t
, simulate
f_{e}(x_{j})
for
t
steps.
Whenever a simulation halts with output
y
, compute
v_{\mathrm{bw}}(x_{j},y)
.
If the value is positive, accept.

If
e\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{bw}}
, there exists some witness
x_{j}
with

v_{\mathrm{bw}}(x_{j},f_{e}(x_{j}))>0
;
since
f_{e}
is total, that computation halts in finite time,
so the dovetailer eventually accepts.
If
e\notin\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{bw}}
, no accepting witness exists, so the machine runs forever.
Hence
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{bw}}
is recursively enumerable.
∎

5.3
Decidability on finite domains

Proposition 5.9

(Finite-domain decidability with exponential cost)
.

Fix maximum input length
n
and define

\mathcal{D}_{n}:=\Sigma^{\leq n}
,

N_{n}:=|\mathcal{D}_{n}|=\sum_{j=0}^{n}|\Sigma|^{j}
.
Let
T_{f}(n)
be the worst-case time to evaluate
f(x)
for
|x|\leq n
,
and
T_{\mathrm{bw}}(n)
the worst-case time to evaluate

v_{\mathrm{bw}}(x,y)
for
|x|\leq n
and
y=f(x)
.
Then
B_{\mathrm{bw}}(f)
on
\mathcal{D}_{n}
is decidable in

O\bigl(N_{n}\cdot(T_{f}(n)+T_{\mathrm{bw}}(n))\bigr)=O\bigl(|\Sigma|^{n}\cdot(T_{f}(n)+T_{\mathrm{bw}}(n))\bigr).

Proof.

Because
\mathcal{D}_{n}
is finite, exhaustive search terminates.
Algorithm:
enumerate all
x\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
;
for each
x
, compute
y=f(x)
and then

\delta_{x}:=v_{\mathrm{bw}}(x,y)
.
Return
1
iff some
\delta_{x}>0
.

Correctness follows directly from Definition
5.1
with domain restricted to
\mathcal{D}_{n}
.
Running time is at most
N_{n}
iterations, each costing

O(T_{f}(n)+T_{\mathrm{bw}}(n))
.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

Theorem
5.6
and Proposition
5.9
establish a sharp boundary:
overspecification detection is decidable iff the input domain is finite.
This is structurally different from cell-probe or amortized lower bounds for dynamic data structures
[
12
,
4
]
: those are complexity barriers on finite workloads, while our result is a computability barrier on selector families.

6
Main result II: Fixed-point barrier to conservative repair

A trivial repair operator can force
v=0
everywhere by outputting a generic baseline implementation, but this collapses the selection pipeline.
We formalize a
conservative
constraint and show that overspecified fixed points necessarily exist.

6.1
Conservative program transformers

Definition 6.1

(Computable repair operator)
.

A
repair operator
is a total computable function
\Phi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}

mapping pipeline indices to pipeline indices.

Definition 6.2

(Conservativeness)
.

\Phi
is
conservative
if

B_{\mathrm{bw}}(f_{e})=0\Rightarrow\Phi(e)=e
.

Remark 6.3
.

Conservativeness encodes
“do not modify an already evidence-aligned program”
—the program-transformation analogue of minimal repair in consistent query answering
[
15
,
16
]
.

Definition 6.4

(Uniform elimination guarantee)
.

A repair operator
\Phi
has
uniform elimination
if

\forall e\in\mathbb{N},\quad B_{\mathrm{bw}}(f_{\Phi(e)})=0.

6.2
Overspecified fixed points via Kleene’s recursion theorem

Lemma 6.5

(Self-referential gadget family)
.

Fix a total computable repair operator
\Phi
and witnesses
x_{0},s_{0},y^{+},\epsilon,\#
from Lemma
5.4
.
Then there exists a total computable map
G:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that, for every
e\in\mathbb{N}
,

f_{G(e)}(x)=\begin{cases}y^{+},&\Phi(e)=e\text{ and }x=x_{0}\#^{n}\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N},\\
\epsilon,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Define the computable predicate

P(e,x):=\bigl[\Phi(e)=e\bigr]\wedge\bigl[\exists n\in\mathbb{N}:x=x_{0}\#^{n}\bigr].

Because
\Phi
is total computable,
\Phi(e)
is computable for each
e
;
membership in
\{x_{0}\#^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is decidable by a linear-time scan.
Hence the program

Q(e,x):=\begin{cases}y^{+},&P(e,x)=1,\\
\epsilon,&P(e,x)=0,\end{cases}

is total computable in both arguments.
By the
s
-
m
-
n
theorem, there is a total computable index map
G
with

f_{G(e)}(x)=Q(e,x)
for all
e,x
.
∎

Theorem 6.6

(Overspecified fixed point under conservative repair)

Theorem C.2

(Kleene recursion theorem)
.

For every total computable

,
