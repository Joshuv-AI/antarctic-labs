Addendum to: Bounds for survival probabilities in supercritical Galton-Watson processes and applications to population genetics T
Reinhard Bürger

Abstract




Abstract.
In this addendum we extend Theorem 4.6 on the negative binomial distribution in
[
1
]
. We prove that the fractional linear lower bound to the negative binomial generating function derived there is indeed valid for every
x\in[0,1]
, and not only for
x\in[0,P_{\rm{NB}}^{\infty}]
, where
P_{\rm{NB}}^{\infty}
is the extinction probability of the associated Galton-Watson process.

Keywords:
Extinction probability, negative binomial offspring distribution, fractional linear generating function

Mathematics Subject Classification.
Primary: 60J80; Secondary: 92D10, 92D15, 60J85

We assume that the reader is familiar with the content and notation in
[
1
]
. Here is the generalized version of Theorem 4.6 in
[
1
]
.

Theorem 1
.

For every
r\geq 2
and every
\zeta\in(0,1)
, the pgfs
\varphi_{\rm{NB}}
and
\varphi_{\rm{FL}}
satisfy

\varphi_{\rm{FL}}(x;\pi_{\rm{NB}},\rho_{\rm{NB}})\leq\varphi_{\rm{NB}}\Bigl(x;r,\frac{\zeta(1-\zeta^{r})}{1-\zeta^{r+1}}\Bigr)\;\text{ for every }x\in[0,1]\,.

(1)

Equality holds if and only if
x=P_{\rm{NB}}^{\infty}
or
x=1
.

In the following, we first repeat with trivial adaptations the proof of the above theorem for
x\in[0,P_{\rm{NB}}^{\infty}]
, as given in Appendix B in
[
1
]
. In particular, we retain the equation numbering from
[
1
]
until eq. (B.11). Then the new proof starts.

We recall that
\zeta^{r}=P_{\rm{NB}}^{\infty}\in(0,1)
and define

{\tilde{\zeta}}=\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}\zeta^{k}\;\text{ and }\;y(x)=\frac{\zeta^{r}-x}{{\tilde{\zeta}}}\,.

(B.1)

Using eq. (A.2) in
[
1
]
, we observe that

y(0)=\frac{\zeta^{r}}{{\tilde{\zeta}}}<\frac{1}{r}\zeta^{(r+1)/2}<\frac{1}{r}\,,\;y(\zeta^{r})=0\,,\;y(1)=\frac{\zeta^{r}-1}{{\tilde{\zeta}}}=-(1-\zeta)\,.

(B.2)

With these abbreviations, we can express the negative binomial generating function
\varphi_{\rm{NB}}
(defined in eq. (4.23) in
[
1
]
) and the (prospective) bounding fractional linear generating function
\varphi_{\rm{FL}}
(defined in eq. (2.9) in
[
1
]
), where
\pi_{\rm{NB}}
and
\rho_{\rm{NB}}
are given in eq. (4.25) in
[
1
]
, as follows:

\varphi_{\rm{NB}}\Bigl(x;r,\frac{\zeta(1-\zeta^{r})}{1-\zeta^{r+1}}\Bigr)=\zeta^{r}\,\left(\frac{1}{1+y(x)}\right)^{r}

(B.3)

and

\varphi_{\rm{FL}}(x;\pi_{\rm{NB}},\rho_{\rm{NB}})=\zeta^{r}\,\frac{1-x-ry(x)}{1-x-r\zeta^{r}y(x)}\,.

(B.4)

We note that numerator and denominator are always positive.

We define

f_{\rm{NB}}(x;r,\zeta):=\zeta^{r}\left(1/\varphi_{\rm{FL}}(x;\pi_{\rm{NB}},\rho_{\rm{NB}})-1/\varphi_{\rm{NB}}\Bigl(x;r,\frac{\zeta(1-\zeta^{r})}{1-\zeta^{r+1}}\Bigr)\right)

(B.5)

and

g_{\rm{NB}}(y(x);r,\zeta):=f_{\rm{NB}}(x;r,\zeta)\frac{1-x-ry(x)}{(1-\zeta)^{2}}\,,

(B.6)

where
1-x-ry(x)>0
.

Proof of the inequality (
1
) if
x\in[0,P_{\rm{NB}}^{\infty}]
(this was already proved in
[
1
]
) .

Proving this inequality is equivalent to showing that
g_{\rm{NB}}(y(x);r,\zeta)>0
for
0<y(x)<\zeta^{r}/{\tilde{\zeta}}
,
r\geq 2
, and
0<\zeta<1
.
In the following we write
y=y(x)
. Using the transformation
x=\zeta^{r}-y\frac{1-\zeta^{r}}{1-\zeta}
, we obtain

g_{\rm{NB}}(y;r,\zeta)=f_{\rm{NB}}\Bigl(\zeta^{r}-y\frac{1-\zeta^{r}}{1-\zeta};r,\zeta\Bigr)\frac{(1-\zeta)(1-\zeta^{r})+y(1-\zeta^{r}-r(1-\zeta))}{(1-\zeta)^{3}}\,,

(B.7)

which we can rewrite as

g_{\rm{NB}}(y)=\frac{y\bigl((1+y)^{r}-1\bigr)\bigl(r(1-\zeta)-(1-\zeta^{r})\bigr)-(1-\zeta)(1-\zeta^{r})\Bigl(\bigl((1+y)^{r}-1\bigr)-ry\Bigr)}{(1-\zeta)^{3}}\,.

(B.8)

By the binomial expansion
(1+y)^{r}-1=y\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\binom{r}{j+1}y^{j}
and after collection of coefficients of
y^{j}
, we obtain

\displaystyle g_{\rm{NB}}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{y^{2}}{(1-\zeta)^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}y^{j}\left(\binom{r}{j+1}\Bigl(r-\frac{1-\zeta^{r}}{1-\zeta}\Bigr)-\binom{r}{j+2}(1-\zeta^{r})\right)

(B.9a)

\displaystyle=y^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}y^{j}\binom{r}{j+1}\frac{1}{(1-\zeta)^{2}}\left(\Bigl(r-\frac{1-\zeta^{r}}{1-\zeta}\Bigr)-\frac{r-j-1}{j+2}(1-\zeta^{r})\right)\,.

(B.9b)

Finally, expansion in terms of
\zeta
yields after appropriate rearrangement

g_{\rm{NB}}(y;r,\zeta)=y^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}y^{j}\binom{r}{j+1}c_{g}(r,j,\zeta)\,,

(B.10)

where

c_{g}(r,j,\zeta)=\frac{1}{2(j+2)}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{r-2}\zeta^{k}(k+1)[2r(1+j)-(2+j)k-2]+\frac{\zeta^{r-1}}{1-\zeta}r(r+1)j\right)\,.

(B.11)

Because
2r(1+j)-(2+j)k-2\geq 2+j(2r-k)\geq 2
, we obtain
c_{g}(r,j,\zeta)>0
for every
0\leq j\leq r-1
and every
\zeta\in(0,1)
. Therefore,
g_{\rm{NB}}(y;r,\zeta)>0
if
y>0
and
f_{\rm{NB}}(x;r,\zeta)>0
if
0\leq x<\zeta^{r}
.
∎

Proof of the inequality (
1
) if
x\in(P_{\rm{NB}}^{\infty},1]
.

We use the transformation
y=u-(1-\zeta)
which, by (
B.2
), is equivalent to
u=(1-x)\frac{1-\zeta}{1-\zeta^{r}}
. Then,
0\leq u\leq 1-\zeta
if
P_{\rm{NB}}^{\infty}=\zeta^{r}\leq x\leq 1
.
We use series expansion of

\tilde{g}_{\rm NB}(u;r,\zeta):=\frac{1-\zeta}{(u-(1-\zeta))^{2}}g_{\rm{NB}}(u-(1-\zeta);r,\zeta)

(2)

in
u
and
\zeta
to prove that
\tilde{g}_{\rm NB}(u;r,\zeta)\geq 0
if
0\leq u\leq 1-\zeta
and
0<\zeta<1
. The key element of the proof is the subsequent lemma.

Lemma 1
.

The following representation holds:

\tilde{g}_{\rm NB}(u;r,\zeta)=\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}\sum_{n=0}^{2r-3-k}u^{k}\zeta^{n}c_{\tilde{g}}(r,k,n)\,,

(3)

where

\displaystyle c_{\tilde{g}}(r,k,n)=

\displaystyle\textstyle k\binom{n+k+2}{k+2}

if
0\leq n\leq r-1-k
,

(4a)

\displaystyle c_{\tilde{g}}(r,k,n)=

\displaystyle\textstyle\binom{r}{k+1}\frac{(2r-k)(k+1)-(n+1)(k+2)}{k+2}

if
r-k\leq n\leq r-1
,

(4b)

\displaystyle c_{\tilde{g}}(r,k,n)=

\displaystyle\textstyle\binom{r}{k+1}\frac{(2r-k)(k+1)-(n+1)(k+2)}{k+2}

\displaystyle c_{\tilde{g}}(r,k,n)=

\displaystyle\textstyle\quad+\binom{k+(n-r)+1}{k+1}\frac{(2r-k)(k+1)-(n+1)k}{k+2}

if
r\leq n\leq 2r-3-k
.

(4c)

We will prove this lemma below. Obviously, the expression in (
4a
) is positive.
The expression
(2r-k)(k+1)-(n+1)(k+2)
in (
4b
) and (
4c
) is positive if
r-k\leq n\leq r-1
. However, it can be negative if
r\leq n\leq 2r-k-3
.

To finish the proof of inequality (
1
) if
P_{\rm{NB}}^{\infty}<x<1
, it remains to show that the sum of the expressions in (
4c
) is positive. We start by noting that this case applies only if
r\geq 4
and
k\leq r-3
.
Two special cases emerging readily from (
4c
) are

\displaystyle c_{\tilde{g}}(r,k,r)

\displaystyle=\binom{r}{k+1}\frac{(r-k-2)k-2}{k+2}+r-k\,,

\displaystyle c_{\tilde{g}}(r,k,2r-3-k)

\displaystyle=\binom{r}{k+1}\frac{(k+1)k}{r(r-1)}=\binom{r-2}{k-1}\,.

Both are positive because
1\leq k\leq r-3
and
r\geq 4
. We note in passing that
c_{\tilde{g}}(r,k,2r-i-k)=0
if
i\in\{0,1,2\}
.

We observe that the term
c_{\tilde{g},a}(r,k,n):=\binom{r}{k+1}\frac{(2r-k)(k+1)-(n+1)(k+2)}{k+2}
is (linearly) decreasing in
n
(and becomes negative for large
n)
.
Straightforward algebra shows that the term
c_{\tilde{g},b}(r,k,n):=\binom{k+(n-r)+1}{k+1}\frac{(2r-k)(k+1)-(n+1)k}{k+2}
is positive and increasing in
n
under the given constraints. Moreover, the second-order forward difference is computed to

c_{\tilde{g},b}(r,k,n+2)-c_{\tilde{g},b}(r,k,n)-2c_{\tilde{g},b}(r,k,n+1)=\binom{k+(n-r)+1}{k}\frac{(2r-n-k-2)k}{n-r+2}\,.

(5)

This is positive if
r\leq n\leq 2r-k-3
, equals 0 if
n=2r-k-2
, and is negative for larger
n
.
We obtain that (i)
c_{\tilde{g},b}(r,k,n)
is convex, increasing, and positive if
r\leq n\leq 2r-k-3
, (ii)
c_{\tilde{g},a}(r,k,n)
is linear (and decreasing), (iii) the first-order differences of
c_{\tilde{g},b}
reach their maximum at
n=2r-k-2
, where they equal
\binom{r}{k+1}
, which is the negative of the first-order difference of
c_{\tilde{g},a}
, and (iv)
c_{\tilde{g}}(r,k,r)>0
and
c_{\tilde{g}}(r,k,2r-k-3)>0
. Therefore, we conclude that
c_{\tilde{g}}(r,k,n)>0
holds in this range.

As consequence,
\tilde{g}_{\rm NB}(u;r,\zeta)
is strictly positive if
0<u<1-\zeta
. By (
2
) and (
B.7
), this implies the desired positivity of
f_{\rm{NB}}(x)
if
P_{\rm{NB}}^{\infty}<x<1
.
Pending the proof of Lemma
1
, this finishes the proof of inequality (
1
) for
x\in(P_{\rm{NB}}^{\infty},1]
and hence the proof of Theorem
1
.
∎

Of course, the expressions given in Lemma

yield those in eq. (12) in

]
for
r=2,3,4,5
.

Proof of Lemma
1
.

It follows readily from the definition of
\tilde{g}_{\rm NB}

STEP 1. We derive the representation

c_{\tilde{g}}(r,k,n)=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{r-1-k}\binom{m+k}{k}\binom{r}{m+k+1}c_{\tilde{g}_{1}}(r,k,n,m)&\text{ if }0\leq n\leq r-1\,,\\
\sum\limits_{m=n-r+1}^{r-1-k}\binom{m+k}{k}\binom{r}{m+k+1}c_{\tilde{g}_{2}}(r,k,n,m)&\text{ if }r\leq n\leq 2r-3-k\,,\end{cases}

(6a)

c_{\tilde{g}_{1}}(r,k,n,m)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{m}\Bigl(r-\frac{r+1}{m+k+2}\Bigr)&\text{ if }n=0\,,\\

(6b)
