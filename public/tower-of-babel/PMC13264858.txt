Effects of Erbium:YAG laser combined with vaginal estriol therapy in postmenopausal women with Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause: protocol for a randomised, double-blind, controlled trial

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionGenitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM) is a chronic, oestrogen-deficient condition that is frequently underdiagnosed and undertreated. Although low-dose vaginal estriol improves epithelial trophism and microbial balance, a substantial proportion of women report persistent symptoms. High-quality randomised evidence evaluating combined therapeutic strategies remains scarce. Energy-based modalities, including the erbium:YAG (Er:YAG) laser (λ=2940 nm), have been proposed as adjunctive treatments. This trial aims to assess the efficacy of Er:YAG laser therapy combined with vaginal estriol compared with estriol alone in postmenopausal women with GSM.Methods and analysisThis is a single-centre, randomised, double-blind, controlled clinical trial. Postmenopausal women aged 45–70 years with vaginal pH ≥5.0 and at least one moderate GSM symptom (Visual Analogue Scale ≥4) will be eligible. Exclusion criteria include current systemic or local hormone therapy, previous vaginal energy-based treatment, abnormal cervical cytology and body mass index ≥35 kg/m2. All participants will receive vaginal estriol cream (0.5 mg per dose) daily for 14 days, followed by twice-weekly administration for 16 weeks. Participants will be randomised (1:1) to receive either estriol plus sham Er:YAG laser or estriol plus active Er:YAG laser. Three laser sessions will be delivered at approximately 4-week intervals. Assessments will occur at baseline, monthly during treatment and 4 months after the final session. The primary outcome is the Vulvovaginal Health Index, with the primary endpoint defined as the change from baseline to 4 months post-treatment, reflecting sustained effect. Secondary outcomes include GSM symptom severity, vaginal microbiome composition (16S rRNA sequencing), quality of life (Menopause Rating Scale) and sexual function (Female Sexual Function Index). Data will be analysed using repeated-measures analysis of variance or appropriate non-parametric tests, with significance set at p<0.05.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval has been obtained from the Human Research Ethics Committee of UNINOVE. Written informed consent will be obtained. Findings will be disseminated via peer-reviewed journals and scientific meetings.Trial registration numberNCT06873971.Keywords: Genitourinary medicine, Community gynaecology, GYNAECOLOGY

Introduction

Genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM) is a frequently under-recognised and undertreated condition resulting from the progressive decline in oestrogen levels.1 This prevalent postmenopausal disorder involves a wide range of vulvovaginal and lower urinary tract symptoms, including vaginal dryness, burning, irritation, dyspareunia, urinary urgency, recurrent infections and overactive bladder, thereby adversely affecting urogenital health and quality of life.

Multicentre studies have reported that the prevalence of GSM among menopausal women ranges from 13% to 80%.2,4 In Brazil, the estimated prevalence is approximately 56%.3 Divergences of opinion are found in clinical practice regarding when to initiate treatment. It is unclear whether management should be guided primarily by patient-reported symptoms or by specialist clinical assessment, particularly given that GSM is frequently underdiagnosed and inadequately managed.2

The vaginal microbiome plays a central role in maintaining genital tract health. A Lactobacillus-dominant flora and an acidic vaginal pH contribute to natural defence mechanisms and are strongly influenced by oestrogen.5 6 Oestrogen deficiency leads to microbial dysbiosis, characterised by reduced Lactobacillus and increased opportunistic bacteria, which has been associated with both the presence and severity of GSM, particularly genital and sexual symptoms.7 Emerging evidence also suggests that hormonal changes may also lead to gut microbial dysbiosis and an increased risk of gynaecological infections after menopause.8

Optimal management of GSM depends on the degree of genital atrophy, symptom severity, overall clinical status and patient preference regarding hormonal therapy. Treatment should therefore be individualised to achieve optimal clinical outcomes.9 10

Topical low-dose hormone therapy, such as vaginal estriol (E3) cream, is widely used to improve vaginal trophism, reduce pH and promote the re-establishment of a Lactobacillus-dominant vaginal microbiota.11 This type of therapy is effective for many women but does not achieve complete symptom relief.

Energy-based interventions, including CO2 and ER:YAG lasers and radiofrequency, have been proposed as minimally invasive alternatives for the management of GSM.12,15 These devices have controlled thermal or ablative effects on tissue, stimulating collagen remodelling, promoting neovascularisation and epithelial thickening, alleviating symptoms and potentially improving vaginal health.13 16 17 While preliminary studies suggest that Er:YAG laser therapy may be safe and beneficial for postmenopausal women, comprehensive randomised controlled trials remain limited.18 This raises the question of whether laser therapy will provide additional benefit in women already receiving topical estriol.1219,21

With increasing female longevity, accompanied by evolving values and lifestyles, greater emphasis is being placed on self-esteem and sexual well-being.22 This context underscores the need for safe and effective interventions, whose integration into clinical practice should be guided by robust, well-designed clinical studies.

Therefore, the primary objective of this study is to assess the effects of combining Er:YAG laser therapy with vaginal estriol cream, compared with topical estriol therapy alone, in women with GSM. The secondary objectives are to:

Assess changes in GSM symptoms, including vaginal dryness, burning, dyspareunia and persistent leucorrhoea.

Investigate changes in the composition and diversity of the vaginal microbiome.

Participants

Postmenopausal women aged 45–70 years, residing in São Paulo, Brazil, will be recruited through gynaecology outpatient services, menopause clinics affiliated with Universidade Nove de Julho and institutional or local advertisements at the Women’s Health Clinic.

Prior to enrolment, written informed consent will be obtained from all eligible participants by a trained member of the research team. Participants who meet the eligibility criteria will then be randomly allocated to the study groups.

Patient and public involvement

Participants and the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this study due to the methodological nature of the protocol development. Upon completion of the data analysis, participants will be invited to attend a meeting to receive and discuss the study findings. The informed consent document obtained at enrolment specifies that strict confidentiality will be maintained throughout the research process.

Recruitment

Participants will be recruited through referrals from gynaecologists attending at the Women’s Health Clinic and affiliated gynaecology outpatient clinics of UNINOVE, as well as through announcements disseminated via social media platforms targeting women experiencing menopausal symptoms. Potentially eligible individuals will be contacted by a trained member of the research team and, if interested, will undergo eligibility screening and provide written informed consent prior to enrolment.

Randomisation

Eligible participants will be randomly allocated in a 1:1 ratio to one of two groups, with 30 participants in each group. A computer-generated randomisation sequence (www.sealedenvelope.com; Randomness and Integrity Services, London, UK) will be used to assign participants to the study groups. Randomisation will be performed in blocks of six.

Allocation concealment will be ensured using sequentially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes. The randomisation list and allocation codes will be stored in a password-protected file accessible only to the unblinded study coordinator responsible for configuring the laser device. Unblinding will occur only after the database lock, unless earlier unblinding is required for safety reasons.

Participants will be randomised into two study arms:

Group 1: topical oestrogen therapy with E3 combined with sham Er:YAG laser. The laser will be delivered at a minimal fluence of 0.5 J/cm2, which is substantially below the therapeutic range typically reported for clinically meaningful tissue remodelling GSM. Effective intravaginal Er:YAG laser treatments generally employ fluences ranging from 6 to 10 J/cm2, which are required to induce thermal stimulation, collagen remodelling and epithelial thickening.14 18 19 At the selected fluence, no histological or clinically relevant effects on vaginal tissue have been demonstrated in previous studies.25 Although not entirely biologically inert, this low fluence is insufficient to generate meaningful thermal gradients within the tissue and is therefore unlikely to produce clinically relevant biological effects, supporting its use as an appropriate comparator condition.26 27 To maintain participant blinding, the device will be operated below the threshold required to elicit a significant biological response, while closely replicating the procedural and sensory characteristics of the active intervention. This will include identical auditory output, device handling and treatment duration, consistent with the procedures applied in the active intervention group. These measures are intended to ensure effective blinding of both participants and laser operator throughout the trial. This approach is consistent with prior sham-controlled trials in GSM, where low-energy settings are used to preserve blinding while minimising tissue response.

Blinding

Blinding procedures have been designed to ensure that both participants and the laser operator will remain blinded to group allocation throughout the study. Outcome assessments were standardised and based on objective or validated measures to minimise observer bias

The researcher laser operator, who will be responsible for administering the therapeutic procedures and conducting the follow-up assessments, will remain blinded for the duration of the trial. To maintain this blinding, a second member of the research team, who will not be blinded, will be exclusively responsible for the pre-configuration of the laser device parameters. This researcher will programme the device according to the participant’s allocated group and will subsequently conceal the equipment such that the parameters are not visible. This process will be completed prior to the entry of both the participant and the laser operator into the treatment room, thereby ensuring that neither is aware of the intervention administered.

Participant blinding will be ensured by standardising the operational characteristics of the laser across both groups, including light emission, device sounds and duration of the procedure. As a result, no perceptible differences will be present that could enable participants to infer their group allocation.

Unblinding will occur only when clinically justified, such as in the event of a serious adverse event where knowledge of the allocated intervention is necessary for appropriate clinical management. All instances of unblinding will be formally documented, including the rationale, timing and personnel involved, and will be reported in the final study report.

Intervention

Topical estriol therapy: all participants in both study arms will receive concomitant low-dose vaginal estriol therapy (estriol 1 mg/g cream) as background treatment. Following written informed consent, estriol will be administered intravaginally using disposable, single-use, precalibrated applicators delivering a fixed dose of 0.5 g per application (equivalent to 0.5 mg of estriol per dose). The intervention will consist of a predefined induction phase followed by a maintenance phase.The induction phase will involve once-daily intravaginal administration of estriol at bedtime for 14 consecutive days. Participants will then enter the maintenance phase, during which estriol cream will be administered intravaginally twice weekly (Mondays and Thursdays) for a total duration of 16 weeks. Maintenance treatment will continue until 30 days after the third laser session.

Dosimetric laser parameters

The intravaginal stage will be performed at a fluence of 9 J/cm2 and a pulse repetition frequency of 2 Hz, with circumferential irradiation along the full length of the vaginal canal, delivered in three passes using a PS03 fractional handpiece mounted on a 360-degree adaptor. Laser application will begin at the cervical end and proceed in uniform 5 mm withdrawal increments to ensure homogeneous mucosal exposure.

The vulvar vestibule stage will subsequently be performed using the same PS03 handpiece coupled with an intraoral adaptor, targeting the vulvar vestibule, including the periurethral region. This stage will also consist of three passes, delivered at a fluence of 6 J/cm2 and a pulse repetition frequency of 2 Hz.

Intervention delivery and standardisation

Estriol cream will be supplied in 30-day treatment courses and dispensed after each laser session to ensure protocol adherence and continuity of treatment. All applicators will be identical, pre-calibrated to deliver the recommended dose and individually packaged to standardise administration across participants and study sites.

Participants will receive a written information leaflet containing standardised instructions on the correct application technique, timing of administration, storage conditions and management of missed doses.

Outcome measures

Assessments will be conducted at baseline, 30 days after each laser session and 4 months following the final treatment. The Vulvovaginal Health Index (VHI) is defined as the primary outcome of this study. It is a 25-point scale used to assess genitourinary atrophy and is widely applied in longitudinal follow-up.

The secondary outcomes include the VAS for four GSM symptoms (burning, irritation, dyspareunia and persistent leucorrhoea); vaginal microbiological sampling for DNA sequencing using the 16S rRNA gene amplification, specifically targeting the V3/V4 region; and assessment of quality of life and sexual function using two validated questionnaires: the Menopause Rating Scale (MRS) and the Female Sexual Function Index (FSFI).

Sample size calculation

The sample size was calculated based on the primary outcome, the VHI, using repeated-measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) to assess the group-by-time interaction.

An a priori sample size calculation was performed assuming a significance level of 5% and a statistical power of 95%, with two groups and five repeated measurements. Effect size estimated from prior trials, acknowledging potential uncertainty (f=0.42), was assumed, based on clinically meaningful differences observed in previous randomised controlled trials evaluating the VHI, including the study by Cruz et al,24 which reported substantial improvements in VHI scores following laser-based interventions compared with topical estriol therapy.24

This effect size was considered appropriate given the expected additive or synergistic effect of combining Er:YAG laser therapy with vaginal estriol, compared with estriol therapy alone.

Based on these assumptions, the minimum required sample size was estimated at 25 participants per group. To account for an anticipated 20% loss to follow-up, the sample size was increased to 30 participants per group.

If the assumption of sphericity is violated, appropriate corrections (eg, Greenhouse-Geisser) will be applied during the statistical analysis.

Data management

Data will be collected using coded identifiers and stored on a secure, password-protected institutional server with restricted access. Regular data back-ups will be performed. Access to identifiable data will be limited to authorised members of the research team. De-identified data will be made available upon reasonable request, subject to appropriate ethical approval and completion of a data sharing agreement. Further details are available via the study repository on the Open Science Framework: https://osf.io/dwgvx/.

Adherence strategies

To minimise non-adherence and preserve the methodological integrity of the trial, adherence to both topical and laser-based interventions will be actively supported and monitored. Participants will receive individualised reminders on scheduled days of vaginal estriol administration and will be instructed to record each application in a treatment diary. Adherence will be further assessed at follow-up visits through review of used and unused applicators.

Adherence to the laser intervention will be ensured through predefined appointment windows and completion of all three treatment sessions. Intervention fidelity will be monitored using standardised procedural checklists completed after each session, documenting applied laser parameters, treatment sequence, session duration and any protocol deviations.

Concomitant care and restrictions

No additional local or systemic hormonal therapies for GSM will be permitted during the study period. The use of vaginal moisturisers, lubricants or other intravaginal products will be prohibited unless medically indicated and approved by the study investigators.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses will be conducted using SPSS (V.22.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, New York, USA) or equivalent validated statistical software. For continuous outcomes, repeated-measures ANOVA will be applied; where assumptions are not met, appropriate non-parametric tests for repeated measures (eg, the Friedman test) will be used.

The primary outcome is the change in the VHI. Although VHI will be assessed at multiple time points (baseline, 30 days after the third laser session and 4 months after the third session), the primary endpoint for analysis will be the change from baseline to 4 months after completion of the treatment protocol, reflecting the sustained effect of the intervention

Additionally, changes from baseline to 30 days after the third laser session will be analysed as a secondary time point, representing the immediate post-intervention effect. Furthermore, a longitudinal analysis including all three time points (baseline, post-treatment and follow-up) will be performed using repeated-measures models to explore group-by-time interactions.

Secondary continuous outcomes, including VAS symptom scores, MRS scores and FSFI scores, will be analysed using similar mixed-effects modelling strategies. Where appropriate, results will be presented as estimated marginal means with 95% CIs.

Microbiome data: changes in the composition and diversity of the vaginal microbiome, assessed through 16S rRNA gene sequencing (V3-V4 region), will be analysed using appropriate statistical methods. Alpha diversity indices will be compared across groups and time points using parametric or non-parametric tests, as appropriate. Beta diversity will be evaluated using ordination techniques (eg, principal coordinates analysis) and permutational multivariate ANOVA. Differential abundance of taxa will be tested with appropriate corrections for multiple comparisons.

Missing data: the extent, nature and patterns of missing data will be examined and reported descriptively for all study variables. The underlying mechanism of missingness will be explored to assess whether data are missing completely at random, missing at random or not missing at random. Sensitivity analyses will be performed to evaluate the robustness of the findings under alternative assumptions regarding missing data, including complete-case analyses and where relevant different imputation strategies. The impact of missing data on the primary outcome (VHI) and key secondary outcomes will be specifically assessed. All procedures for handling missing data will be prespecified in the statistical analysis plan and implemented in accordance with current methodological recommendations for randomised controlled trials with repeated measures.

Safety

A topical anaesthetic cream will be applied to the vestibular region prior to the procedure. Analgesics (paracetamol 500 mg) will be offered if required to control post-procedure discomfort. Laser energy settings will adhere to the manufacturer’s specifications to minimise the risk of thermal injury.

Participants will be monitored throughout the procedure for discomfort, adverse reactions and tissue sensitivity. All adverse events will be systematically recorded and monitored. Criteria for discontinuation and procedures for reporting serious adverse events have been predefined. In addition, explicit criteria for treatment discontinuation and standardised procedures for the identification and reporting of serious adverse events have been incorporated into the protocol.

Post-procedural care instructions will be provided, including avoidance of sexual intercourse and vaginal moisturisers for 72 hours following treatment. Expected transient effects, such as mild oedema, warmth or local discomfort, will be explained to participants in advance.

Participants may withdraw from the study at any time without providing a reason, if necessary for safety or protocol-related reasons.

Discussion

This randomised, double-blind, controlled clinical trial is designed to address a critical evidence gap in the management of GSM. Although topical E3 remains the standard hormonal therapy for restoring vaginal trophism, its gradual onset of action and reliance on continuous use underscore the need for complementary strategies.11 Energy-based interventions, particularly Er:YAG laser therapy, have shown promise in improving symptoms and mucosal integrity.1314 18,20 25 However, the incremental benefit of combining laser therapy with estriol cream has not been rigorously evaluated.11 12 18 24

By integrating clinical, symptomatic, microbiological and patient-reported outcomes, this study aims to determine whether Er:YAG laser therapy provides additional therapeutic benefit beyond estriol alone.9 15 18 The inclusion of high-resolution vaginal microbiome analysis adds a novel dimension to the investigation, potentially enabling the identification of microbial signatures associated with symptom improvement and tissue recovery.7

If a synergistic effect is confirmed, the findings may contribute to refining the management of GSM by supporting a more comprehensive, durable and individualised therapeutic approach. This may be particularly relevant for postmenopausal women who respond suboptimally to hormonal therapy alone. The results of this protocol may therefore inform clinical practice, guide future research and strengthen evidence-based strategies to improve quality of life.
