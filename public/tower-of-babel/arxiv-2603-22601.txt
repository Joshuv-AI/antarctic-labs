On the combinatorial structure of graphs with a spectral idempotent of small dual diameter
Edwin R. van Dam, Giusy Monzillo, Safet Penjić

Abstract
Let Γ \Gamma be a connected regular graph with an eigenvalue λ \lambda and corresponding idempotent E λ E_{\lambda} . Let ℰ λ = ⟨ J , E λ ⟩ ∘ {\cal E}_{\lambda}=\langle J,E_{\lambda}\rangle^{\circ} be the algebra generated by J J and E λ E_{\lambda} with respect to the entrywise-Hadamard product, where J J is the all- 1 1 matrix. We study the combinatorial structure of a graph Γ \Gamma for which ℰ λ {\cal E}_{\lambda} has dimension 2 2 , giving a combinatorial characterization of such graphs in terms of equitable partitions. We present many examples and classify the distance-regular graphs with this property, as well as graphs that generate a 3 3 -class association scheme. We also study the graphs that have two eigenvalues λ \lambda for which dim ⁡ ( ℰ λ ) = 2 {\rm dim}({\cal E}_{\lambda})=2 and determine all such graphs with four distinct eigenvalues.

1
Introduction

A vector space of symmetric matrices that is closed under both ordinary and entrywise multiplications, containing the identity matrices for both multiplications, i.e.,
I
and
J
, is known to be the Bose-Mesner algebra of an association scheme.

If
\Gamma
is a relation in a
d
-class association scheme with adjacency algebra
{\mathcal{A}}=\langle I,A\rangle^{\cdot}=\langle I,A,A^{2},\ldots\rangle
, then
\Gamma
generates the entire scheme if
\dim{\mathcal{A}}=d+1
. On the other end, if
\Gamma
is a regular graph with
\dim{\mathcal{A}}=2
, then
\Gamma
must be a disjoint union of cliques. In addition, if
\Gamma
is a relation in an association scheme, this gives us relatively little information, as we can only conclude that the scheme is imprimitive.

Here we study a dual version of the above. We consider a regular graph
\Gamma
with an idempotent
E
(for the ordinary product) in the adjacency algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
, and the “idempotent algebra”
{\cal E}=\langle J,E\rangle^{\circ}=\langle J,E,E\circ E,E^{\circ 3},\ldots\rangle
, where
\circ
denotes entrywise (Hadamard) multiplication. In particular, we consider the case
\dim{\cal E}=2
and study the combinatorial structure that can be obtained from this (note that this is again the other end of dimension
d
, which in the case of a
d
-class scheme gives all combinatorial information).

Before we present our main results, we define what an indubitable partition is.

Definition 1.1

A partition
\Pi
of the vertex set
X
of a connected graph
\Gamma
is called
indubitable
with parameters
(a,b)
whether for each cell
{\mathcal{P}}\in\Pi
of the partition and each vertex
x
of
\Gamma
the number of neighbors of
x
in
{\mathcal{P}}
equals
a
if
x\in{\mathcal{P}}
, and
b
otherwise.

If
\Pi
is an indubitable partition with parameters
(a,b)
and
r+1
cells of (the vertex set of) a connected
k
-regular graph
\Gamma
, then its quotient matrix equals
aI+b(J-I)
, which has eigenvalues
k=a+rb
(with multiplicity
1
) and
\lambda=a-b
(with multiplicity
r
), which yields that
a=\lambda+\frac{k-\lambda}{r+1}
and
b=\frac{k-\lambda}{r+1}
. It is well known (see Section
2.2
) that these eigenvalues form a sub(multi)set of the spectrum of
\Gamma
. Of particular interest (and the topic of research of this paper) is the case when the number of cells of the indubitable partition is equal to
m+1
, where
m
is the multiplicity of the eigenvalue
\lambda
;
we then say that the indubitable partition is
full
. In this case, all eigenvectors for
\lambda
can be obtained from the quotient.

Our main characterization of full indubitable partitions is precisely in terms of the idempotent algebra of the spectral idempotent
E_{\lambda}
(the projection matrix of the related eigenspace) having dimension
2
.

Theorem 1.2

Let
\Gamma
be a connected regular graph on
v
vertices that has an eigenvalue
\lambda
with multiplicity
m
and corresponding spectral idempotent
E_{\lambda}
. Let

{\cal E}={\cal E}_{\lambda}=\langle J,E_{\lambda}\rangle^{\circ}
. Then
{\cal E}
has dimension
2
if and only if
\Gamma
has a full indubitable partition corresponding to
\lambda
.
Moreover, if
\dim{\cal E}=2
, then
E_{\lambda}=\frac{m+1}{v}K-\frac{1}{v}J
,
where

K
is permutation-similar to
I_{m+1}\otimes J_{v/(m+1)}
.

This paper is further organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notation and further background.
In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.2
in a series of steps. We also show, in Proposition
3.2
, that if there is an idempotent
E\in{\mathcal{A}}
of rank
m
such that
EJ=0
and
\dim\langle J,E\rangle^{\circ}=2
, then
\Gamma
has an equitable partition into
m+1
parts.
In Section
4
, we present examples of graphs with full indubitable partitions. These include bipartite graphs (Section
4.1
) and some other graphs with a simple eigenvalue as characterized in Proposition
4.1
. We also determine all distance-regular graphs with a full indubitable partition in Proposition
4.2
and characterize the examples in a
3
-class association scheme in Proposition
4.3
. We finish this section by discussing the so-called uniform schemes in Section
4.2.3
and a general product construction in Section
4.3
.
In the final Section
5
, we consider graphs with two full indubitable partitions. In Theorem
5.2
, we obtain that the only such graphs with four distinct eigenvalues are the Cartesian products of two complete graphs (that is, grid graphs), which we also characterize combinatorially as certain co-edge-regular graphs. We conclude by determining the bipartite graphs with five distinct eigenvalues and two full indubitable partitions in Theorem
5.5
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Graphs and matrices

In this paper, we consider simple undirected graphs
\Gamma

with the standard
01
-adjacency matrix
A
.
By the eigenvalues of
\Gamma
we mean the eigenvalues of
A
. For an eigenvalue
\lambda
, we let
E_{\lambda}
be the corresponding
spectral idempotent
, that is, the matrix representing the projection onto the eigenspace
\ker(\lambda I-A)
.
The
adjacency algebra

{\mathcal{A}}
of
\Gamma
is the vector space
\{f(A)\mid f\mbox{ is a polynomial}\}
over
{\mathbb{R}}
spanned by the powers of
A
. We note that
{\mathcal{A}}=\Span\{E_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\mbox{ is an eigenvalue of }\Gamma\}
.
For more general background and notation on spectra of graphs, we refer to
[
3
]
.

Dual to the adjacency algebra, for an idempotent
E
, we let
{\cal E}
be the
idempotent
\circ
-algebra

\{f(E)\mid f\mbox{ is a polynomial}\}
, where the multiplication
\circ
is entrywise, in other terms,

{\cal E}=\langle J,E,E\circ E,E\circ E\circ E,\dots\rangle
.

Matrices
A
and
B
are called
permutation-similar
(denoted by
A\sim B
) if there is a permutation matrix
P
such that
B=P^{\top}AP
. In other words,
B
can be obtained from
A
by simultaneously permuting rows and columns. Two graphs are
isomorphic
if their adjacency matrices are permutation-similar. Matrices
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are simultaneously permutation-similar to
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
if there is a permutation matrix
P
such that
B_{1}=P^{\top}A_{1}P
and
B_{2}=P^{\top}A_{2}P
.

For matrices
C=(c_{ij})\in\mbox{\rm Mat}_{m\times n}({\mathbb{R}})
and
B\in\mbox{\rm Mat}_{p\times q}({\mathbb{R}})
, the
Kronecker product

C\otimes B\in\mbox{\rm Mat}_{mp\times nq}({\mathbb{R}})
is the (block) matrix obtained by replacing each entry
c_{ij}
of
C
with the block
c_{ij}B
.

The
Cartesian product
of two graphs with adjacency matrices
A
and
B
(on
v
and
v^{\prime}
vertices, respectively) is the graph with adjacency matrix
A\otimes I_{v^{\prime}}+I_{v}\otimes B
.
Its eigenvalues are all possible sums of eigenvalues of
A
and
B

[
3
, §1.4.6]
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph with adjacency matrix
A
. Then the
bipartite double
of
\Gamma
is the graph with adjacency matrix
(J_{2}-I_{2})\otimes A
.

2.2
Some properties of the quotient matrix of an equitable partition

An
equitable partition
of a graph
\Gamma
is a partition
\Pi=\{{\mathcal{P}}_{0},{\mathcal{P}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}_{s}\}
of its vertex set, such that for all integers
i,j

(0\leq i,j\leq s)
the number of neighbors in
{\mathcal{P}}_{j}
of a vertex
x\in{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
depends only on
i
and
j
(and not on
x
). We denote the corresponding parameter by
q_{ij}
, i.e., for every
x\in{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
we have
|\Gamma_{1}(x)\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{j}|=q_{ij}
. The matrix
Q=(q_{ij})_{(s+1)\times(s+1)}
is called the
quotient matrix
of the equitable partition
\Pi
of
\Gamma
. If
P
is a matrix having the characteristic vectors of the cells of the partition as columns, then we have that
AP=PQ
. In fact, we have the following characterization.

Lemma 2.1
(
[
9
, §9.3]
)

Let
P
be a matrix whose columns are the characteristic vectors of the cells of a partition
\Pi
of a graph
\Gamma
with adjacency matrix
A
. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

\Pi
is an equitable partition of
\Gamma
.

(ii)

There is a matrix
Q
such that
AP=PQ
.

(iii)

\col P
is invariant under
A
.

It is well known and easy to see that if
x
is an eigenvector for
Q
with eigenvalue
\lambda
, then
Px
is an eigenvector for
A
. This gives rise to eigenvectors that are constant on the cells of the equitable partition. In fact, the other eigenvectors are orthogonal to
P
(see
[
9
, §9.3]
). As mentioned in the introduction, in case the indubitable partition is full corresponding to an eigenvalue
\lambda
, then all eigenvectors of
A
for
\lambda
are constant on the cells of the partition.

We note that an indubitable partition as defined in the introduction is equitable, in fact, it is uniformly equitable, i.e., there is a
01
-matrix
\widetilde{A}
with zero diagonal such that
Q=aI+b\widetilde{A}
(as defined in
[
10
, §4]
and
[
2
, §11.1]
)).

Finally, we observe that from
AP=PQ
, it follows that
f(A)P=Pf(Q)
for any polynomial
f
, and hence it follows that an equitable partition for
\Gamma
is equitable for any matrix in the adjacency algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
of
\Gamma
.
(Of particular interest are
01
-matrices within the adjacency algebra.)
Moreover, if
\Gamma
has an indubitable partition with parameters
(a,b)
, then
Q=aI+b(J-I)
, and it follows that the partition is indubitable for any matrix in
{\mathcal{A}}
as well.

2.3
Association schemes and distance-regular graphs

A (symmetric)
d
-class
association scheme
is a partition of the edge set of the complete graph on
v
vertices into spanning subgraphs
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\dots,\Gamma_{d}
(that are called the non-trivial relations of the scheme) with adjacency matrices
B_{1},B_{2},\dots,B_{d}
such that the vector space
\langle B_{0},B_{1},B_{2},\dots,B_{d}\rangle
(with
B_{0}=I
) is an algebra, that is, it is closed under matrix multiplication. This algebra is called the
Bose-Mesner algebra
of the association scheme, and it is important to note that it is also closed under entrywise multiplication
\circ
(by construction). It also has a dual basis
\{E_{0}=\frac{1}{v}J,E_{1},\dots,E_{d}\}
of primitive idempotents. For background and notation on association schemes, we refer to
[
1
,
2
]
.

2.3.1
Imprimitivity

An assocation scheme is called imprimitive if at least one of its relations is disconnected. For us, it is relevant (cf. Theorem
1.2
) that an association scheme is imprimitive if and only if there are sets of indices
{\mathcal{I}}
and
{\cal J}
(both containing
0
) such that

\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}B_{i}=\tfrac{v}{m+1}\sum_{j\in{\cal J}}E_{j}\sim I_{m+1}\otimes J_{v/(m+1)}

for some
m
with
0<m<v-1
(see, for example,
[
6
, §2]
or
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
).
In this case, let
K=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}B_{i}\sim I_{m+1}\otimes J_{v/(m+1)}
and let
\Pi
be the partition of the vertex set whose characteristic vectors are the distinct columns of
K
.
It then follows from Lemma
2.1
(iii) (cf. the proof of Proposition
3.2
) that
\Pi
is an equitable partition for all relations in the association scheme (since
\im K=\im\sum_{j\in{\cal J}}E_{j}
). In fact, if there is an edge in
\Gamma_{h}
between two fixed cells of
\Pi
, then
h\notin{\mathcal{I}}
and it is not hard to see that every vertex in one of the two cells has
b_{h}:=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}p^{h}_{hi}

\Gamma_{h}
-neighbors in the other cell. This yields that the partition
\Pi
is uniformly equitable, i.e., its quotient matrix
Q_{h}
equals
b_{h}\widetilde{B_{h}}
for some symmetric
01
-matrix
\widetilde{B_{h}}
with zero diagonal. The set
\{\widetilde{B_{h}}\mid h\notin{\mathcal{I}}\}
is the set of adjacency matrices of the non-trivial relations of the so-called quotient scheme
[
2
, §2.4]
). Note that some of the
\widetilde{B_{h}}
are typically identical and we should therefore not interpret the above set of adjacency matrices as a multiset. Moreover, it follows that for
j\in{\cal J}
, the primitive idempotents
E_{j}
are constant on the cells of the partition (since each eigenvector for
\lambda_{j}
is constant on the cells of the partition, cf. Section
2.2
), and their quotient matrices (appropriately scaled so that they are idempotent) form the primitive idempotents of the quotient scheme (and hence
|{\cal J}|-1
is its number of classes). Note also that the ranks of the primitive idempotents in the quotient scheme are the same as the corresponding ones in the original association scheme.

Further, the above uniformly equitable partition for
\Gamma_{h}
is indubitable when
\widetilde{B_{h}}=J-I
, which is the case precisely if the quotient scheme is trivial (i.e., there is just one relation, the complete graph). In this case it follows that the partition is indubitable for all
h
. (Note that if
h\notin{\mathcal{I}}
, the parameter
b
of the indubitable partition is nonzero, while for
h\in{\mathcal{I}}
,
b=0
).
Moreover, then
{\cal J}
has size
2
, so there is a primitive idempotent
E_{j}
such that
\tfrac{v}{m+1}(E_{0}+E_{j})
is permutation-similar to
I_{m+1}\otimes J_{v/(m+1)}
(cf. Theorem
1.2
.)

For completeness (but quite irrelevant for us), we mention that when we restrict the matrices in
\{B_{i}\mid i\in{\mathcal{I}}\}
to a fixed cell of the partition, then this induces a so-called subscheme on that cell
[
1
, §2.7.4]
).

2.3.2
Distance-regular graphs

Assume that
\Gamma
is a connected graph of diameter
d
.
If
\Gamma_{i}
denotes the distance-
i
graph of
\Gamma
(that is, vertices are adjacent in
\Gamma_{i}
if their distance in
\Gamma
is
i
), then
\Gamma
is called
distance-regular
if

\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\dots,\Gamma_{d}
form an association scheme. In this case, the primitive idempotents of the scheme are the same as the spectral idempotents of
\Gamma
.

A distance-regular graph is called
imprimitive
if the corresponding association scheme is imprimitive. A distance-regular graph
\Gamma
of diameter
d
is called
antipodal
if its distance-
d
graph
\Gamma_{d}
is a disjoint union of cliques.
An imprimitive distance-regular graph with valency at least
3
is bipartite or antipodal (or both)
[
2
, Theorem 4.2.1]
.

Moreover, if
\Gamma
is bipartite, then the corresponding “imprimitivity system” is given by
{\mathcal{I}}=\{0,2,4,\dots,2\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor\}
and
{\cal J}=\{0,d\}
(with the standard ordering of primitive idempotents). On the other hand, if
\Gamma
is antipodal, then
{\mathcal{I}}=\{0,d\}
and
{\cal J}=\{0,2,4,\dots,2\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor\}
.
We will use this in Section
4.2
.

3
The indubitable partition

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.2
.
Let
{\cal E}=\langle J,E\rangle^{\circ}
for some idempotent matrix
E
and assume that
\dim{\cal E}=2
. Since this means that
E
has just two distinct entries,
\theta_{0}
and
\theta_{1}
say, we can write
E=\theta_{0}K+\theta_{1}(J-K)
for some
01
-matrix
K
. We will now determine the structure of this matrix
K
, which in the end produces the required indubitable partition. We first focus on idempotent matrices in the adjacency algebra of a regular graph.

3.1
Idempotents in the adjacency algebra

Lemma 3.1

Let
E
be a symmetric
v\times v
idempotent matrix of rank
m
such that
EJ=\OO
. Let
{\cal E}=\langle J,E\rangle^{\circ}
. If
\dim{\cal E}=2
, then
E=\frac{m+1}{v}K-\frac{1}{v}J
,
where

K
is permutation-similar to
I_{m+1}\otimes J_{v/(m+1)}
.

Proof.
Let

E=\theta_{0}K+\theta_{1}(J-K),

(1)

where
K
is a
01
-matrix and
\theta_{0}>\theta_{1}
.

From
EJ=\OO
(i.e.,
E
has row sums
0
), it follows that
\theta_{0}>0>\theta_{1}
and that
K
has constant row sums
-v\theta_{1}/(\theta_{0}-\theta_{1})
. Furthermore,
E\circ I=\theta_{0}I
as
E
is positive semidefinite, and hence
\theta_{0}=m/v
because
\trace E=m
.

It now follows that
E
is the Gram matrix of
v
vectors of length
\sqrt{m/v}
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with inner products
m/v
and
\theta_{1}
. But such vectors have inner product
m/v
only if they are equal, hence inner product being
m/v
is an equivalence relation.

Because
K
has constant row sums and rank
m+1
, it now follows that
K
is permutation-similar to
I_{m+1}\otimes J_{v/(m+1)}
. Moreover, the row sums imply that

-v\theta_{1}/(\theta_{0}-\theta_{1})=v/(m+1)
, which together with
\theta_{0}=m/v
yields that
\theta_{1}=-1/v
.

Observe that from the above representation of
E
as a Gram matrix we have
m+1
distinct vectors of length
\sqrt{m/v}
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with mutual angles
\arccos(-1/m)
, that is, a regular simplex. For example, for
m=2
, we have the vertices of an equilateral triangle in the plane (the three vectors have mutual angle
2\pi/3
).

We next consider idempotents
E
in the adjacency algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
of a connected regular graph
\Gamma
and characterize those for which
\dim\langle J,E\rangle^{\circ}=2
. We also show that the cliques in the above proof are the cells of an equitable partition.
Note that because the spectral idempotents of
\Gamma
form a basis of
{\mathcal{A}}
, an idempotent is in
{\mathcal{A}}
if and only if it is a sum of spectral idempotents.

Proposition 3.2

Let
\Gamma
be a connected regular graph on
v
vertices, with adjacency algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Then
{\mathcal{A}}
contains a matrix
K
that is permutation-similar to
I_{m+1}\otimes J_{v/(m+1)}
if and only if there is an idempotent
E\in{\mathcal{A}}
of rank
m
such that
EJ=\OO
and
\dim\langle J,E\rangle^{\circ}=2
.

Moreover, if so, then the distinct columns of
K
are the characteristic vectors of an equitable partition of
\Gamma
into
m+1
parts of equal size.

Proof.

Let
K
be permutation-similar to
I_{m+1}\otimes J_{v/(m+1)}
and define

E=\frac{m+1}{v}K-\frac{1}{v}J
. Then it is easy to show that
E
is idempotent of rank
m
, with
EJ=\OO
and
\dim\langle J,E\rangle^{\circ}=2
.
Moreover, if
K\in{\mathcal{A}}
, then
E\in{\mathcal{A}}
. The reverse follows from Lemma
3.1
.

If indeed
K\in{\mathcal{A}}
, then by the above,
\frac{m+1}{v}K
is an idempotent of rank
m+1
, hence it is a sum of spectral idempotents, say
\sum_{j\in{\cal J}}E_{j}
, with
\dim\bigoplus_{j}\col E_{j}=\sum_{j\in{\cal J}}\rank E_{j}=m+1=\rank K
. Now
\col K=\col\sum_{j\in{\cal J}}E_{j}\subseteq\bigoplus_{j}\col E_{j}
, but because the dimensions are equal, it follows that
\col K=\bigoplus_{j}\col E_{j}
. The latter is clearly invariant under
A
(in fact, under any matrix in
{\mathcal{A}}
), so by Lemma
2.1
, the distinct columns of
K
are the characteristic vectors of an equitable partition.

A degenerate example of the above is
E=I-\frac{1}{v}J
, which is in the adjacency algebra of any connected regular graph of order
v
, and which corresponds to the trivial equitable partition into
v
parts.

Note that in general, an equitable partition into equal parts does not imply that the corresponding
K
is in
{\mathcal{A}}
.
For example, consider the complete bipartite graph
K_{4,4}
with color classes
{\mathcal{P}}_{0}=\{a,b,c,d\}
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}=\{a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime},d^{\prime}\}
.
Note that
\spec(K_{4,4})=\{[4]^{1},[0]^{6},[-4]^{1}\}
and that
\{{\mathcal{P}}_{0},{\mathcal{P}}_{1}\}
is a full indubitable partition. Moreover,
K_{4,4}
has two equitable partitions into
4
parts of size
2
(one is
\{\{a,a^{\prime}\},\{b,b^{\prime}\},\{c,c^{\prime}\},\{d,d^{\prime}\}\}
, which is indubitable, but not full, and the other is
\{\{a,b\},\{c,d\},\{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\},\{c^{\prime},d^{\prime}\}\}
, which is not indubitable).
Both have
K
permutation-similar to
I_{4}\otimes J_{2}
, which is not in
\langle I,J,(J_{2}-I_{2})\otimes J_{4}\rangle
, so
K\notin{\mathcal{A}}
.

3.2
Spectral idempotents

Next, we further specialize and consider spectral idempotents.

Proposition 3.3

Let
\Gamma
be a connected
k
-regular graph on
v
vertices that has an eigenvalue
\lambda
with multiplicity
m
and corresponding spectral idempotent
E_{\lambda}
. Let
{\cal E}=\langle J,E_{\lambda}\rangle^{\circ}
.
If
\dim{\cal E}=2
, then
\Gamma
has a full indubitable partition corresponding to
\lambda
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
,
E_{\lambda}=\frac{m+1}{v}K-\frac{1}{v}J
,
where

K
is permutation-similar to
I_{m+1}\otimes J_{v/(m+1)}
. By Proposition
3.2
, the
m+1
distinct columns of
K
are the characteristic vectors of an equitable partition of the vertex set of
\Gamma
. Let
P
be the
v\times(m+1)
characteristic matrix of this partition (i.e., its columns are the distinct columns of
K
). Let
Q=\lambda I_{m+1}+\frac{k-\lambda}{m+1}J_{m+1}
.
It then remains to show that
Q
is the quotient matrix of the partition, i.e., that
AP=PQ
(see Lemma
2.1
).

Because
K=\frac{v}{m+1}(E_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{v}J)
, we obtain that

AK=\tfrac{v}{m+1}(\lambda E_{\lambda}+\tfrac{k}{v}J)=\tfrac{v}{m+1}\big(\lambda(\tfrac{m+1}{v}K-\tfrac{1}{v}J)+\tfrac{k}{v}J\big)=\lambda K+\tfrac{k-\lambda}{m+1}J.

Since the columns of
P
are columns of
K
and
J_{v\times(m+1)}=PJ_{m+1}
, it thus follows that
AP=\lambda P+\tfrac{k-\lambda}{m+1}J_{v\times(m+1)}=PQ
.

The final step in proving Theorem
1.2
is to show the reverse of the above proposition by constructing the spectral idempotent
E
from the full indubitable partition.

Proposition 3.4

Let
\Gamma
be a connected
k
-regular graph on
v
vertices that has an eigenvalue
\lambda
with multiplicity
m
and corresponding spectral idempotent

4
Some graphs with full indubitable partitions

In this section, we give some examples of graphs having a full indubitable partition.
We note that if a graph has a full indubitable partition, then this partition is also full indubitable for its complement (assuming that both the graph and its complement are connected).

4.1
Bipartite graphs and simple eigenvalues

It is well known that a connected

-regular graph is bipartite if and only if it has
-k

-regular graph has an indubitable partition with two cells (i.e., the color classes) with parameters

Moreover, by Theorem
1.2
, the spectral idempotent

corresponding to eigenvalue

\frac{1}{v}(2I_{2}-J_{2})\otimes J_{v/2}

Also walk-regular graphs with a simple eigenvalue

Proposition 4.1

Let

On the other hand, assume that

4.2

4.2.3
Uniform association schemes

In his paper on
4
-class imprimitive association schemes, Higman
[
11
]
defined uniform association schemes.
Informally, an association scheme is called uniform if it is imprimitive with a partition of the vertices into cells such that all intersection numbers are spread uniformly over the cells. In particular, this means that the partition is indubitable for all (connected) relations of the scheme. All bipartite schemes and all cometric Q-antipodal schemes are uniform, for example. Van Dam, Martin, and Muzychuk
[
6
]
showed that in a uniform association scheme on

such that

is permutation-similar to
I_{m+1}\otimes J_{v/(m+1)}
for some
m
(among other results about the other primitive idempotents), and hence the indubitable partition is full for all relations that generate the scheme. Prominent examples are obtained from so-called linked systems of symmetric designs and linked systems of strongly regular designs (such as a linked system of Van Lint-Schrijver partial geometries). For many more examples, we refer to
[
6
]
.

5.1
Four eigenvalues

Terwilliger

\Gamma
must be the bipartite double of a complete multipartite graph. Before doing so, we first introduce some notation and make some general observations.

In particular, we will first show that no bipartite graph can have a full indubitable partition corresponding to eigenvalue
0
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected regular bipartite graph on
v
vertices. Let
N
be the incidence matrix of
\Gamma
, that is, we can write its adjacency matrix
A
(after a suitable ordering of the vertices) as

A=\begin{bmatrix}O&N\\
N^{\top}&O\end{bmatrix}.

Using the corresponding bipartition of
\Gamma
and
L_{2}:=J_{2}-I_{2}
, we also introduce the matrices

F=\begin{bmatrix}J_{v/2}&-J_{v/2}\\
-J_{v/2}&J_{v/2}\end{bmatrix}\text{~and~}L=J-F=\begin{bmatrix}O&J_{v/2}\\
J_{v/2}&O\end{bmatrix}=L_{2}\otimes J_{v/2}.

It is well-known that eigenvectors of
\lambda
can easily be transformed into eigenvectors of
-\lambda
and vice versa using the bipartition of
\Gamma
. It follows that
E_{-\lambda}=F\circ E_{\lambda}
for each eigenvalue
\lambda
.

Proposition 5.4

Let
\Gamma
be a connected bipartite graph with an eigenvalue
0
. Then
\Gamma
does not have a full indubitable partition corresponding to
0
.

Proof.
Take
\lambda=0
. From the above, we find that
E_{\lambda}=F\circ E_{\lambda}
, hence
L\circ E_{\lambda}=0
, which implies that
E_{\lambda}
has an entry zero. Thus,
E_{\lambda}
must have at least three distinct entries, which proves the result.

Let
\Gamma
now be a connected
k
-regular bipartite graph on
v
vertices with five distinct eigenvalues. Because bipartite graphs have a symmetric spectrum, the distinct eigenvalues are
\pm k,\pm\lambda
, and
0
, with
\pm\lambda
having multiplicity
m
, say. The spectral decomposition of
A
now gives that

A=kE_{k}+\lambda E_{\lambda}-\lambda E_{-\lambda}-kE_{-k}=L\circ(\frac{2k}{v}J_{v}+2\lambda E_{\lambda}).

Theorem 5.5

Let
\Gamma
be a connected
k
-regular bipartite graph on
v
vertices with five distinct eigenvalues. If
\Gamma
has a full indubitable partition corresponding to an eigenvalue
\lambda\neq-k
with multiplicity
m
, then
\lambda=-k/m
and
\Gamma
is the bipartite double of a complete
(m+1)
-partite graph.

Proof.

First we note that
\lambda\neq 0
by Proposition
5.4
. Again, it follows from Section
4.1
that
\Gamma
has a second full indubitable partition for the eigenvalue
-k
with multiplicity
m^{\prime}=1
, and so Proposition
5.1
yields that

E_{\lambda}=\frac{m+1}{v}K-\frac{1}{v}J_{v}
and
E_{-k}=\frac{2}{v}K^{\prime}-\frac{1}{v}J_{v}
, where we reorder the vertices such that

K=J_{2}\otimes I_{m+1}\otimes J_{\ell},\qquad\text{and}\qquad K^{\prime}=I_{2}\otimes J_{m+1}\otimes J_{\ell}

(with
\ell=v/(2(m+1))
).
Note that we were careful to have the same ordering of vertices (that is, according to the bipartition) as above, so that
E_{-k}=\frac{2}{v}K^{\prime}-\frac{1}{v}J_{v}=\frac{1}{v}F
. Thus, from the spectral decomposition, we obtain that

\displaystyle vA

\displaystyle=L\circ(2kJ_{v}+2v\lambda E_{\lambda})=L\circ(2(k-\lambda)J_{v}+2\lambda(m+1)K)

\displaystyle=L\circ(2(k-\lambda)J_{v}+2\lambda(m+1)J_{2}\otimes I_{m+1}\otimes J_{\ell})

\displaystyle=(L_{2}\otimes J_{v/2})\circ(J_{2}\otimes[2(k-\lambda)J_{m+1}+2\lambda(m+1)I_{m+1}]\otimes J_{\ell})

\displaystyle=L_{2}\otimes[2(k-\lambda)J_{m+1}+2\lambda(m+1)I_{m+1}]\otimes J_{\ell}.

(The line above is due to the mixed-product property
(A\otimes B)\circ(C\otimes D)=(A\circ C)\otimes(B\circ D)
, for matrices
A,B,C
, and
D
such that
A
has same size as
C
and
B
has same size as
D
).
Because
A
is a
01
-matrix, and
\lambda\notin\{0,-k\}
, it follows that
2(k-\lambda)=v
and
2\lambda(m+1)=-v
, which yields
\lambda=-k/m
.

Now
A
can be further simplified as
A=L_{2}\otimes(J_{m+1}-I_{m+1})\otimes J_{\ell}
, i.e.,
\Gamma
is the bipartite double of a complete
(m+1)
-partite graph.

Note that, similarly to Theorem
5.2
, it can easily be shown that
\langle I,J,F,E_{\lambda},E_{-\lambda}\rangle
is closed under entrywise multiplication, and hence it is the Bose-Mesner algebra of a
4
-class association scheme. Needless to say that this scheme is generated by
\Gamma
(but the other relations are not used in the above proof).

Note also that the case
\ell=1
is degenerate and gives the graph of Corollary
5.3
.
