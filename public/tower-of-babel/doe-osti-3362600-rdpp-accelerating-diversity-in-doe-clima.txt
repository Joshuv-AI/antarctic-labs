1.0 Executive Summary
The scope of the project was set out to accelerate the inclusion of diversity into the Department of
Energy (DOE) Earth and Environmental Systems Sciences Division (EESSD) relevant climate science
and resilience research to inclusively advance solutions. The Project Objectives were to usher in
equitable use-inspired climate-related research with underrepresented Minorities of which this project
helped fund 7 HU graduate students work with the DOE (three of which will graduate in Spring 2025).
The two key aims underpinning that core goal were AIM1: developing partnerships (18 organized
engaged, see partners list) and AIM2: Developing capabilities (3 visits to DOE facilities, 5 DOE
partners visits to HU, increased visiting faculty participation in BNL-DOE lab, secured 5 grants together
totaling 1.2 million in funds for HU). The project objectives were highly successful as they were
designed to ambitiously pull together DOE lab researchers with the long-standing and successful
transdisciplinary climate science research programs of the PI and local DC groups. The major outcomes
of this RDPP program will be in the new fundamentally inclusive partnerships with DOE and HU tasked
to understand the urban-rural impacts due to climate change in the US, Eastern South Atlantic (ESA)
Region, related to energy issues driven by heat stress and the water cycle. Overall, this project
contributes to the DOE and science community vision for catalyze connections for project-ready
underrepresented minorities (URMs) at a prominent HBCU to DOE projects supported by the Biological
and Environmental research (BER) Program; particularly, the Earth and Environmental Systems
Sciences Division (EESSD).

2.0 Background
HU has been a powerhouse year-over-year for producing qualified workforce-ready underrepresented
minorities and connecting them with climate positions. For the past two decades, in total, 54% of the
African Americans gaining a Ph.D. in Atmospheric Sciences have come from HU. HU, an Historically
Black College or University (HBCU), located in Washington, DC, is uniquely positioned to spearhead
this RDPP connection. Since the early 1990’s, HU has demonstrated leadership in the environmental
science sector by developing a program that continually fosters trust, nurtures new connections, and
provides resources to URM students to accelerate diversity and inclusion in climate science. From 2000
to 2021, HU environmental science related programs, with its combined partners, have produced 34
Ph.D and 37 M.S. Under-represented minority graduates. Many HU long-standing partnerships have
expressed urgency in developing the future’s diverse workforce, e.g., the National Oceanic and
Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA).
The PI, Dr. Wilkins, has proven ability to lead projects of this stature with previous and current
successful multi-year transdisciplinary partnerships of climate impacts to human health. Wilkins Vision
remain steadfast in accelerating the expansion of the global network of minorities in atmospheric
science, by increasing retention with scalable intention, to propel workforce-ready students for future
climate challenges.

 7. AGU special session (2023 and 2024) GH007- Compound Climate Hazards and Health
 Risks: Floods, Fires, Heat, and Beyond:
 Climate-related hazards present a growing health threat for populations around the world.
 Although often examined separately, hazards that occur at the same place and/or time have the
 potential for synergistic impacts on the environment and society (e.g. co-occurring flooding and
 high wind events or extreme heat and wildfire smoke exposure). Characterizing population
 vulnerability and public health outcomes associated with both individual and compound climate-
 related hazards is challenged by complex climate-related co-exposure dynamics along with
 underlying social and health disparities. Recent improvements in methods for measuring
 climate-related exposures, including remote sensing and modeling approaches, allow for
 greater spatio-temporal specificity in the assessment of individual and combined impacts from
 co-occurring hazards and separation from other extreme weather events. This session aims to
 explore the distribution of past and/or future individual or compound climate hazards, public
 health impacts, vulnerable populations, and the effectiveness of adaptation strategies.

6.0 Lessons Learned
We are highly gracious of the DOE support. We recognize that having strong committed partners such
as BNL DOE was for us during this project are invaluable to the success. Without our key partners
being heavily involved such as John Hill and Noel Blackburn from BNL, most of this project would have
been impossible. Similarly, at PNNL, Samson Hagos and Jermone Fast were paramount to our
success. We Learned that the individuals were really what helped and having them dedicated to our
project really made all the difference in our results and ability to train and retain students. Some of the
key lessons learned were around staffing and internal administration issues. The project was able to be
highly successful however we could have done a lot more if we could have received a no cost
extension and if that paperwork was processed in time. The group wanted to Travel to PNNL during the
time frame of the project however that was not possible due to administrative lag time in processing

 Page 9 of 10

travel. HU has hired a new travel team as a result, and this should prevent this in the future.
Additionally, Howard has worked to restructure its grants support office for pre and post award, and this
should help streamline issues we faced for future grants. Related to the NCE not being approved, for
cases at small institutions with less help, the PI would suggest that for key paperwork such as NCE,
that it be a case where the PI themselves can directly request to submit those, as opposed to waiting
for the grants office to get around to it. As many grants offices at smaller institutions struggle due to
volume of task and may miss key deadlines.
