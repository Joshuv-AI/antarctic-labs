Study on the diagnostic value of lipid and systemic inflammatory markers in colorectal adenomatous polyps and the construction of a diagnostic model
Authors: Ning Yang, Shui Jin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveColorectal adenomatous polyps are well-recognized precancerous lesions of colorectal cancer. Early identification and clinial intervention are crucial for reducing the risk of colorectal cancer. This study aimed to systematically investigate the diagnostic value of lipid and systemic inflammatory markers in colorectal adenomatous polyps and to construct a predictive diagnostic model, thereby providing assistance for early clinical identification.MethodsThis retrospective study includes 220 patients with colorectal adenomatous polyps who underwent endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR) or endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) in the Department of Gastroenterology at the Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University between January 1, 2023 and December 31, 2023. Concurrently, 220 healthy controls undergoing physical examinations at the hospital's physical examination center during the same period are enrolled. This study aimed to analyze the diagnostic value of lipid indicators (cholesterol, triglycerides, high-density lipoprotein and low-density lipoprotein) and systemic inflammatory markers (NLR, PLR, MLR, SII, SIRI and AISI) in colorectal adenomatous polyps and to construct a diagnostic model to facilitate clinical diagnosis.ResultsBaseline data analysis revealed statistically significant differences between the adenoma group and the control group in terms of gender, age, body mass index (BMI), cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), high-density lipoprotein (HDL), low-density lipoprotein (LDL) and systemic inflammatory markers (NLR, PLR, MLR, SII, SIRI and AISI). The diagnostic model integrating metabolic and inflammatory markers exhibits certain diagnostic predictive efficacy,which can serve as providing auxiliary support for non-invasive, early screening of colorectal adenomatous polyps.Keywords: Lipid, Systemic inflammatory markers, Colorectal adenomatous polyps, Diagnostic value, Diagnostic model

Introduction

Colorectal cancer is a globally prevalent malignant tumor.its development often follows the “normal mucosa-adenoma-carcinoma” sequence [1]. Approximately 85% of colorectal cancer cases evolve from adenomatous polyps and this prolonged precancerous process provides a critical window for early intervention [2]. However, although colonoscopy is the current gold standard for diagnosis, its invasive nature leads to a screening compliance rate of less than 50% in the population, along with risks of complications such as perforation and bleeding [3]. Therefore, identifying convenient and accurate serological biomarkers for early screening of colorectal adenomas has become a priority research area.

In recent years, the association between dyslipidemia and colorectal tumors has garnered increasing attention. Multiple studies have shown that dyslipidemia, including elevated triglycerides, elevated low-density lipoprotein cholesterol and reduced high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, is not only associated with cardiovascular diseases but may also promote abnormal proliferation of colorectal epithelial cells through mechanisms such as alterations in cell membrane fluidity, bile acid metabolism and insulin-like growth factor signaling pathways [4]. Meanwhile, chronic low-grade inflammation is considered the “seventh hallmark” of tumorigenesis [5]. Systemic inflammatory markers derived from complete blood counts, such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) and systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), can holistically reflect the systemic inflammatory and immune status [6–8].

Previous studies have extensively examined the association between dyslipidemia and colorectal cancer, which has garnered significant interest, but its impact on adenomatous polyps, precancerous lesions, remains poorly understood [6]. For example, some studies have indicated that high cholesterol and triglyceride levels may promote adenoma development. However, the specific mechanisms of different lipid components require further elucidation [9, 10]. Additionally, inflammatory markers such as NLR and PLR have shown significant predictive potential in the early diagnosis of colorectal cancer, but their role in adenomatous polyps remains incompletely characterized [11]. Furthermore, most existing studies rely on single-indicator analyses and lack systematic multi-indicator evaluations that integrate multiple indicators, thereby hindering a comprehensive understanding of polyp pathogenesis and compromising the accuracy of clinical diagnosis. Therefore, a comprehensive analysis of lipid profiles and systemic inflammatory markers to evaluate their diagnostic potential in colorectal adenomatous polyps holds substantial scientific and clinical significance.

This study focuses on lipid (including cholesterol, triglycerides, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol) and systemic inflammatory markers (such as NLR and PLR) to systematically analyze their diagnostic value in colorectal adenomatous polyps. By integrating metabolic and immune-inflammatory dimensions, this study aims to offer novel insights into early screening strategies. Furthermore, constructing a diagnostic prediction model based on multiple indicators can offer a quantitative auxiliary tool for clinical practice, enhancing diagnostic sensitivity and specificity. Compared with previous studies focusing on single biomarkers, the comprehensive analysis of this study will expand research on colorectal adenomatous polyps and fill current gaps.

In summary, this study investigates the diagnostic value of lipid and systemic inflammatory markers in colorectal adenomatous polyps, aiming to provide a scientific basis and effective tools for early identification of colorectal adenomatous polyps, with the ultimate goal of facilitating early prevention and intervention strategies for colorectal cancer in clinical practice and advancing personalized medicine.

Methods

Study design

This study adopted a retrospective case-control design to evaluate lipid, including cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL) and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL), by comparing patients with colorectal adenomatous polyps to healthy controls recruited during routine physical examinations. Additionally, systemic inflammatory indicators such as NLR, PLR, MLR, SII, SIRI and AISI were analyzed. Based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, we screened 220 patients who underwent their first colonoscopy at the Department of Gastroenterology, Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University, from January 1, 2023 to December 31, 2023, and were subsequently diagnosed with colorectal adenomatous polyps. Concurrently, 220 healthy controls were selected from individuals undergoing routine physical examinations at the hospital’s Health Examination Center during the same period. Data collected from both the colorectal adenomatous polyp group and the healthy control group included age, sex, weight, height, body mass index (BMI), lipid (cholesterol, triglyceride, high-density lipoprotein and low-density lipoprotein), and systemic inflammatory indices (NLR, PLR, MLR, SII, SIRI and AISI). Among these, NLR = absolute neutrophil count/absolute lymphocyte count; PLR = platelet count/absolute lymphocyte count; MLR = absolute monocyte count/absolute lymphocyte count; SII = (absolute neutrophil count × platelet count)/absolute lymphocyte count; SIRI = (absolute neutrophil count × absolute monocyte count)/absolute lymphocyte count; AISI = (absolute neutrophil count × platelet count × absolute monocyte count)/absolute lymphocyte count.

Colonoscopy

Prior to the procedure, participants were instructed to follow a liquid diet for 24 h and fast for at least 8 h. The standard bowel preparation protocol adopted polyethylene glycol to ensure adequate cleansing. Subsequently, a complete colonoscopy was performed by experienced physicians, with the visible lesions resected via endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR) or endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD). Biopsies were also performed. Histopathological diagnosis is confirmed by qualified pathologists through microscopic examination.

Model construction and evaluation

This study adopted stratified random sampling (random seed 42) to partition the study subjects into a training set and a validation set at a 7:3 ratio. Using the training set, variables were initially screened using univariate logistic regression (P < 0.1), and independent predictors among the variables examined were subsequently identified through multivariate logistic regression (P < 0.05) to construct a diagnostic nomogram. The diagnostic efficacy of independent predictors among the variables examined was evaluated using a random forest plot, and a diagnostic nomogram is constructed based on these factors. Internal validation of the model was performed using 1000 bootstrap resampling to correct for optimism bias; the fixed model was applied to the validation set, and its 95% confidence interval was calculated via bootstrap. Finally, the model’s discrimination, calibration and clinical utility were comprehensively evaluated using ROC curves, calibration curves and decision curve analysis (DCA).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using R (4.2.1). This study employs stratified random sampling, where all study samples were stratified by outcome event proportion and then randomly divided into a training set and a validation set at a 7:3 ratio, with a fixed random seed set to ensure reproducibility. All variable screening, model construction and machine learning analysis were performed only in the training set, whereas the validation set was kept blinded throughout to assess the model’s generalizability.

Initially, potential predictive factors were screened in the training set using univariate logistic regression, with variables yielding P < 0.1 included in the subsequent multivariate logistic regression analysis. Independent predictors among the variables examined for disease occurrence are ultimately identified, and a visual diagnostic nomogram model was constructed based on regression coefficients. Simultaneously, the importance of each included variable was ranked using the random forest machine learning algorithm based on the training set data.

To assess the internal stability of the model and correct for overfitting bias, 1000 bootstrap resampling was performed for internal validation within the training set, calculating the original C-index, corrected C-index and model optimism. Model calibration was evaluated using bootstrap calibration plots and the Hosmer–Lemeshow test, while clinical net benefit was assessed via decision curve analysis.

To further validate the model’s generalizability, the final predictive model derived from the training set was directly applied to the validation set without additional variable screening or model reconstruction. The model’s discrimination ability, calibration degree and clinical application value were externally evaluated using ROC curves, calibration curves and decision curve analysis, with 1000 Bootstrap resampling used to obtain the 95% confidence interval of the ROC curve in the validation set.

Results

Baseline data

According to the inclusion and exclusion criteria, a total of 220 patients with colorectal adenomatous polyps (adenomatous polyp group) and 220 healthy individuals (non-adenomatous polyp group) who were undergoing physical examinations were enrolled. As shown in Fig. 1, in the comparison between the adenomatous polyp group and the non-adenomatous polyp group, sex, age, weight, height, BMI, TC, TG, HDL, LDL, NLR, PLR, MLR, SII, SIRI and AISI showed statistically significant differences (P < 0.05).

Baseline characteristics of patients with colorectal adenomatous polyps and healthy controls. Among them, the units of TC, TG, HDL and LDL are mmol/L

Univariate logistic regression analysis and multivariate logistic regression analysis

This study adopted a stratified random sampling method. All study samples are stratified according to the proportion of outcome events and then randomly divided into a training set and a validation set at a ratio of 7:3. A fixed random seed (42) was set to ensure result reproducibility. In the training set, potential predictive factors were initially screened using univariate logistic regression. Variables with P < 0.1 were included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis to identify candidate variables and determine independent predictors among the variables examined. As shown in Fig. 2A, the results of the univariate logistic regression analysis indicated that male, age, BMI, TC, TG, HDL, LDL, NLR, PLR and SII were all statistically significant (P < 0.05). Variables with P < 0.1 were included in the multivariate logistic regression for further screening. As shown in Fig. 2B, the results of the multivariate logistic regression analysis indicated that male, age, TC, TG and NLR were statistically significant (P < 0.05) and served as independent predictors among the variables examined for the diagnosis of colorectal adenomatous polyps.

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis of patients with colorectal adenomatous polyps. Figure A shows univariate logistic regression analysis and Figure B shows multivariate logistic regression analysis. 0 represents male, 1 represents female

Random forest plot

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses identified male, age, TC, TG and NLR as independent predictors among the variables examined. The relative importance of these independent predictors among the variables examined was assessed using the random forest algorithm. As shown in Fig. 3, the random forest plot indicated that the importance of the independent predictors among the variables examined, from high to low, was: TG, NLR, TC, age and gender.

A random forest plot of independent predictors among the variables examined in colorectal adenomatous polyps

Diagnostic nomogram

Based on the multivariate regression results, a diagnostic prediction model for colorectal adenomatous polyp growth was developed. The formula is: Logit(P) = -8.5366 + 0.75549*Gender (male = 1, female = 0) + 0.032293*Age + 0.48906*TC + 2.1745*TG + 0.60184*NLR. The “rms” package in R software (version 4.2.1) was used to visualize the prediction model as a nomogram. The results are shown in Fig. 4. The first row of the nomogram, the Points scale, represents the risk weight scores of each independent predictor. Rows two to six correspond to the five independent predictors among the variables examined: male, age, TC, TG and NLR. The laboratory measurement value of each factor can be projected vertically onto the Points scale in the first row to obtain its risk score. The sum of these scores (Total Points) is mapped to the ‘Risk’ scale in the final row, yielding the individualized predicted probability. A higher value indicates a higher probability of developing colorectal adenomatous polyps. The variance inflation factors (VIF) for male, age, TC, TG and NLR were 1.0448, 1.0461, 1.0429, 1.0532 and 1.0341, respectively. Since the VIF for all five independent predictors among the variables examined is less than 4, it was considered that there was no multicollinearity.

Nomogram illustrating the diagnostic prediction model for colorectal adenomatous polyps. For the gender variable, 0 denotes male and 1 denotes female

ROC Curve of the diagnostic model

To evaluate the internal stability of the model and correct for overfitting bias, 1000 Bootstrap resampling iterations were performed within the training set for internal validation. The original C-index, corrected C-index and model optimism are calculated. As shown in Fig. 5A, the ROC curve for the diagnostic nomogram in the training set is plotted using the Bootstrap method (1000 resampling iterations, sample size n = 308). The original C-statistic (AUC) of the model is 0.847, the corrected C-statistic was 0.847, and the optimism was only 0.000002. The ROC curve was substantially above the chance line (dashed line), indicating that the model exhibited robust discriminatory ability in the training set. The negligible difference between the uncorrected and corrected AUC values suggests minimal overfitting and stable performance within the training set. As shown in Fig. 5B, the ROC curve for the validation set was also plotted using the Bootstrap method (1000 resampling iterations, sample size n = 132). The 95% confidence interval for the model’s AUC was [0.782, 0.909]. The ROC curve was also substantially above the chance line, indicating that the model still maintained good discriminatory ability in the validation set. The fact that the confidence interval did not contain 0.5 indicated statistical significance.

ROC curve of the diagnostic nomogram. Figure A shows the ROC curve of the training set and Figure B shows the ROC curve of the validation set

Calibration curve

As illustrated in Fig. 6, calibration curves were employed to assess the model’s goodness of fit by depicting the agreement between predicted and observed probabilities. The plot includes the Apparent curve (original predictions), the Bias-corrected curve (calibrated predictions) and the Ideal curve (perfect prediction). Proximity to the diagonal line indicates a superior model fit. The calibration curve for the training set (Fig. 6A) and the calibration curve for the validation set (Fig. 6B) showed that the prediction curve was close to the diagonal line, indicating a good fit for the diagnostic model.

Calibration curves of the diagnostic model. Figure A shows the calibration curve of the training set and Figure B shows the calibration curve of the validation set

Decision curve analysis (DCA) plot

Figure 7 displays the Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) results, which evaluate the net benefit of using the model for decision-making across a range of threshold probabilities. The DCA plots indicated a high net benefit for the training set across the threshold probability range of 0.1–0.93 (Fig. 7A) and for the validation set across the range of 0.07–0.85 (Fig. 7B).

DCA plot of the diagnostic model. Figure A shows the DCA plot of the training set and Figure B shows the DCA plot of the validation set

Discussion

As colorectal adenomatous polyps are significant precursors to colorectal cancer, their early identification is crucial for reducing the incidence of this disease [12]. Colorectal cancer is one of the malignant tumors with high morbidity and mortality worldwide, posing a great challenge to public health and clinical treatment [13, 14]. Although colonoscopy is widely recognized as the gold standard for diagnosing colorectal adenomas, its invasive nature and associated complications limit its application in large-scale screening, leading to poor patient compliance and affecting the effectiveness of early diagnosis [15, 16]. Therefore, exploring non-invasive blood biomarkers with high sensitivity and specificity has become an urgent need to improve the early diagnosis of colorectal adenomatous polyps.

This study, employing a retrospective case-control design, systematically evaluated the potential role of dyslipidemia and systemic inflammatory status in the occurrence of colorectal adenomatous polyps, aiming to construct an effective diagnostic prediction model. Unlike previous studies focusing on single indicators, this study comprehensively examines multiple biomarkers, including cholesterol, triglycerides, inflammation-related indicators and the systemic immune-inflammation index (SII). The results not only revealed multiple independent predictors among the variables examined but also demonstrated robust diagnostic performance via model validation, providing novel insights and methods for clinical screening. Furthermore, these findings establish a foundation for future in-depth investigations into the roles of lipid and inflammatory indicators in the pathological mechanisms underlying colorectal adenomatous polyps and their clinical management.

This study identified male and age as independent predictors among the variables examined for colorectal adenomatous polyps, suggesting that sex hormones and aging-related metabolic changes may contribute to adenoma development. Existing studies indicated that androgen levels are closely associated with intestinal epithelial cell proliferation and the tumor microenvironment, correlating with the significantly higher incidence of colorectal adenomas observed in men compared to women [17]. Advancing age is characterized by a decline in immune function and chronic low-grade inflammation, promoting gene mutations in intestinal mucosal cells and tumorigenesis [18]. These factors interact to create a conducive microenvironment that promotes adenoma formation, reflecting the comprehensive impact of metabolic and endocrine factors on the risk of colorectal adenomas.

Abnormal lipid indicators, especially elevated TC and TG, were significantly associated with the occurrence of colorectal adenomatous polyps, suggesting that lipid metabolism disorders may be involved in the mechanism of adenoma formation [19]. Excess lipids can alter the structure of cell membrane lipid rafts, affect receptor signal transduction, promote cell proliferation and inhibit apoptosis [20]. Furthermore, elevated triglycerides may induce intestinal mucosal damage and local chronic inflammation by stimulating abnormal bile acid metabolism, thereby promoting polyp formation [21]. An elevated systemic inflammatory indicator NLR reflected an activated inflammatory state in the body. This chronic low-grade inflammation, through the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, activated intestinal immune cells, driving intestinal epithelial cell proliferation and gene mutations, thereby promoting adenoma development [22].

In recent years, inflammation-based hematological indicators, immunonutritional parameters and blood risk models have received widespread attention in the early diagnosis and risk assessment of colorectal cancer (CRC) and precancerous lesions (such as colorectal adenomatous polyps) [23]. Notably, systemic inflammatory markers such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) can reflect the dysregulation of systemic immune and inflammatory responses and serve as significant indicators in the initiation and progression of colorectal tumors [24]. Studies have shown that these indicators are strongly correlated with the risk of malignant transformation of colorectal adenomas, especially demonstrated robust diagnostic utility in distinguishing high-risk adenomas from low-risk lesions [25].

The high AUC value of the diagnostic model in the statistical analysis reflects the effectiveness of the combination of lipid and inflammatory indicators in the early identification of adenomatous polyps. The robust performance demonstrated by the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve indicated that the multivariate model can accurately integrate biomarker information, thereby mitigating the limited sensitivity inherent in single-indicator approaches [22]. Furthermore, the model construction adopted systematic multivariate analysis, enhancing the stability and generalizability of the prediction, which is beneficial for the development of clinical screening tools. This methodological innovation, combining lipid and inflammatory indicators, demonstrated the application potential of multi-dimensional data integration in disease prediction models.

First, the limitations of this study were mainly reflected in the small sample size and the lack of multi-center data validation, which may affect the general applicability of the results. Although 220 patients provided a basis for preliminary analysis, the small sample size limits the in-depth exploration of potential differences between different subgroups. Significant between-group imbalances in baseline variables (age, gender and BMI) were observed in this study. Despite adjustment for the above confounders via multivariate logistic regression to reduce confounding bias, intrinsic baseline discrepancies could not be fully resolved given the study design. Selection bias therefore remained a potential concern and might distort the effect size estimates of risk factors. Second, the absence of in vitro or in vivo experimental validation introduces uncertainty regarding the clinical translatability of these biomarkers, potentially compromising the reliability of the results and limiting the broader applicability of the conclusions. In addition, this study did not characterize polyp size, location, histological subtype, nor differentiate between high-grade and non-high-grade adenomas, which limited the clinical applicability of the model. Finally, this study did not adjust for several key confounding factors, including smoking, alcohol consumption, physical activity, dietary habits, diabetes, hypertension, metabolic syndrome, fatty liver disease, family history of colorectal cancer and long-term use of aspirin or metformin.

In summary, this study highlighted the significant association of lipid and systemic inflammatory indicators with the occurrence of colorectal adenomatous polyps and successfully constructed a relevant diagnostic model, showcasing promising clinical utility. Future research should consider expanding the sample size, multi-center collaboration and increasing the study indicators to further enhance the credibility of the research results. Meanwhile, combined in vitro or in vivo experimental validation of biomarkers will help further develop it into a potential tool for auxiliary risk stratification, thereby providing a more solid foundation for the prevention and personalized medicine of colorectal cancer.

Conclusion

This retrospective case-control study demonstrated that disorders of lipid metabolism and systemic inflammatory markers are closely associated with the occurrence of colorectal adenomatous polyps. Multivariate logistic regression and random forest analysis identified male, age, TC, TG and NLR as independent predictors among the variables examined of colorectal adenomatous polyps, with TG demonstrated the highest diagnostic value. The diagnostic nomogram model integrating these five indicators exhibited good discrimination, calibration and favourable clinical net benefit in both the training and validation sets. In conclusion, these findings suggested that the combined detection of serum lipid profile and systemic inflammatory markers offers clinical utility for the early diagnosis of colorectal adenomatous polyps.
