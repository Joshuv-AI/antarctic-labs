Cardiac sympathetic afferent denervation improves cardiac post-ischemic remodeling by maintaining autonomic tone
Authors: Xin Lai, Shun Wang, Hui Chen, Zhuo Wang, Xuejun Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe autonomic imbalance can negatively affect left ventricular (LV) remodeling following a myocardial infarction (MI). Ganglionated plexi (GP) play a crucial role in autonomic nervous system imbalance. Cardiac sympathetic afferent denervation (CSAD) has been shown to have beneficial effects in addressing this imbalance.ObjectivesThis study aimed to evaluate the effects of the ablation of cardiac sympathetic sensory neurons on LV remodeling. Resiniferatoxin (RTX), a neurotoxic transient receptor potential vanilloid (TRPV1) antagonist, was used to achieve it.Methods and ResultsA total of 31 beagle dogs were randomly assigned to three groups: MI group (n = 12), CSAD group (MI with CSAD; n = 10), and Control group (sham surgery without CSAD; n = 9). In the CSAD group, RTX was injected into the anterior right ganglionated plexi (ARGP) only once. After a 4-week follow-up period, CSAD treatment resulted in a significant decrease in left stellate ganglion (LSG) activity, improved LSG remodeling, reduced LV dilation, and improved LV function. Furthermore, CSAD treatment significantly attenuated LV remodeling in post-MI LV tissue. The treatment also showed positive effects on high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP), norepinephrine (NE), N-terminal prohormone of brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) and Neuropeptide Y (NPY) levels.ConclusionsIn conscious dogs with healed MI, CSAD led to a reduction in cardiac sympathetic tone and an improvement in ventricular remodeling. This suggests that targeting cardiac sympathetic sensory neurons through CSAD may have therapeutic benefits in managing LV remodeling after MI.Keywords: Autonomic nervous system, Cardiac sympathetic afferent denervation, Myocardial infarction, Heart failure, Remodeling

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is a common, economically burdensome, and potentially life-threatening condition. It is associated with an autonomic imbalance, characterized by a sustained increase in sympathetic drive and a withdrawal of parasympathetic activity, which contributes to the progression of chronic HF [1], [2]. The persistent elevation of sympathetic tone negatively impacts the failing heart by increasing cardiac workload, promoting myocardial remodeling, and exacerbating HF symptoms. Consequently, inhibiting excessive sympathetic activation holds promise as a potential approach to prevent the progression of remodeling and the development of HF [3].

The sympathetic tone can be regulated through the cardiac sympathetic afferent reflex. Sensory afferent terminals express transient receptor potential vanilloid 1 (TRPV1), which plays a crucial role in modulating cardiac sympathetic tone [4]. Research has demonstrated that blocking TRPV1 can lead to a reduction in sympathetic tone. Resiniferatoxin (RTX) is widely described as an ultrapotent TRPV1 agonist that functionally ablates TRPV1 and primary sensory afferents\ [5]. In various studies, RTX administration has been utilized in different areas, including the left ganglion stellate, epicardium, and spinal cord, with the aim of effectively inhibiting sympathetic tone [6].

The anterior right ganglionated plexus (ARGP)is situated close to the right atrium. This ganglionated plexus consists of efferent and afferent parasympathetic and sympathetic neurons, interneurons and others. The entire intrinsic cardiac nervous system, including all the GPs and sub-plexi [7]. TRPV1-expressing sensory neurons have been identified within the cardiac ganglionated plexi, including the ARGP, by immunohistochemical staining in canine models. Tavares et al. demonstrated that RTX injection into the ARGP leads to cytoplasmic microvacuolization and apoptosis of GP cells, confirming the presence of targetable TRPV1+ afferents. [8]. However, it is currently unclear whether administering RTX to the ARGP can improve left ventricular (LV) remodeling by attenuating cardiac sympathetic tone. Based on our previous studies [9], [10], LV remodeling models of beagle dogs were suitable and proven technique for this research. Many institutions investigated autonomic nerve remodeling in beagle dogs for decades. Hence, our study was designed to investigate whether RTX administration in the ARGP could ameliorate LV remodeling with male adult's beagle dogs.

Methods

Animal preparations

All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the institutional guidelines of the Animal Care and Use Committee of Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University, and the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals published by the US National Institutes of Health (NIH Publication No. 85–23, Revised 1996). All surgery was performed under sodium pentobarbital anesthesia, and every effort was made to minimize suffering. All surgical procedures were conducted under sodium pentobarbital anesthesia, with utmost care taken to minimize any potential discomfort or suffering. The dogs were anesthetized using a dosage of 40 mg/kg of sodium pentobarbital and ventilated with room air using a positive pressure respirator (MAO01746, Harvard Apparatus Holliston, USA). Maintenance doses of 2 mg/kg of sodium pentobarbital were additionally administered at the end of each hour throughout the procedure. Normal saline was infused at a rate of 50 to 100 mL/h to compensate for spontaneous fluid loss. Throughout the entire procedure, a body surface electrocardiogram (ECG) was recorded using subcutaneous needle electrodes, employing a computer-based Lab System (Lead 2000B, Jinjiang, Inc., China). To ensure a stable core body temperature, a heating pad was utilized, maintaining the dogs' temperatures at 36.5 ± 1.5 °C. The thoracotomy was performed by accessing the left fifth intercostal space. Beagles were intravenous injection of sodium pentobarbital (150 mg/kg) for euthanasia at the end of experiment.

Experiment protocol

The experiment involved 31 adult male beagle dogs, obtained from the experimental animal center of the Medical College of Wuhan University, with a body weight ranging from 20 to 25 kg. The dogs were randomly assigned to three groups: MI group (n = 12), CSAD group (n = 10), and Control group (n = 9). In the MI group, MI was induced by ligation of the left anterior descending artery. The CSAD group underwent MI induction along with the injection of RTX, a potent TRPV1 agonist, into the ARGP to induce CSAD. The Control group underwent a sham surgery without the induction of CSAD. No additional background therapy was provided to any of the groups.

MI induction

To establish a myocardial infarction (MI) model, a Harris two-stage occlusion approach was adopted for the left anterior descending (LAD) coronary artery. A 3–0 silk suture was carefully placed above the first diagonal branch. Moreover, all the major diagonal branches were ligated to effectively minimize the collateral blood flow reaching the infarct area. Initially, the LAD artery was partially occluded for 20 min. This partial occlusion served as a pre-treatment phase. Subsequently, the artery was completely ligated. The occlusion of the LAD artery was clearly indicated on the electrocardiogram (ECG) by the elevation of the ST segment, which is a well - recognized sign of myocardial ischemia. After a three - hour observation period during which the physiological responses of the model were closely monitored, the chest was closed in layers. Simultaneously, the pneumothorax that might have occurred during the procedure was promptly resolved. To prevent potential infections, antibiotics were administered for three consecutive days after the surgery.

RTX administration

To desensitize cardiac afferents, a solution of RTX (1 mg; Sigma Aldrich) was prepared by dissolving it in 2 mL of ethanol and mixing it with 18 mL of Tween in isotonic saline. RTX was administered immediately into the ARGP fat pad after MI induction. This resulted in a stock solution of RTX at a concentration of 50 μg/mL. A total volume of 200 μL (containing 10 μg of RTX) was then slowly injected into the ARGP fat pad over a duration of 1 min using a 20G needle, creating a wheal over the ARGP fat pad area. The ARGP were identified depending on the anatomical landmarks (epicardial fat pad surrounded by the superior vena cava and right atrium) and heart rate slowing phenomenon under high-frequency stimulation. High-frequency electrical stimulation (20 Hz, 0.1 ms duration, square waves, 0.6–6.0 V) was performed using a bipolar electrode probe through an electrophysiology stimulator (SEN-7103, Japan). Vagal reflex was defined as a ≥ 50% increase in mean R–R interval or when complete atrio-ventricular (AV) block occurred. The solution was then slowly injected into the ARGP over a duration of 1 min using a 20G needle, creating a wheal over the ARGP area. The dosage of RTX was determined based on a previous study, which showed almost complete ablation of TRPV1afferents cardiac nerves in GPs [8].

Neural recording in left stellate ganglion activity

In the canine model, neural activity originating from the left stellate ganglion (LSG) was recorded for a period of 5 min during the baseline stage prior to the induction of ischemia and again at 4 weeks after myocardial infarction (MI). These measurements were performed prior to tissue collection, during the terminal experiment at 4 weeks. At each of these time points, the most representative 1-min segment was carefully chosen for subsequent analysis. Tungsten-coated microelectrodes were meticulously inserted into the fascia of the LSG, while a ground lead was attached to the chest wall to ensure stability. The electrical signals emitted by the LSG were captured using a Power Lab data acquisition system (product 8/35, AD Instruments, Sydney, Australia) and then amplified (product DP-304, Warner Instruments, Hamden, Connecticut). The band-pass filters were configured to operate within the range of 300 to 1 kHz, and the amplification factor was set between 30 and 50 times. The neural activity, as characterized by the recorded amplitude and frequency, was defined as deflections possessing a signal-to-noise ratio exceeding 3:1. These were manually identified, adhering to the procedures detailed in previous study [9].

Measurements of heart rate variability

Heart rate variability (HRV) serves as a well-established measure for assessing sympathetic and vagal tone. All HRV measurements were performed under general anesthesia, both at baseline and at the 4-week follow-up time point. Specifically, BS recordings were obtained under general anesthesia immediately prior to the initial surgical procedure, before any experimental intervention. Follow-up recordings at 4 weeks were also performed under general anesthesia prior to tissue harvest. Thus, the phrase “before harvest” refers to measurements obtained immediately before euthanasia, while the animals were still under stable general anesthesia. The LF component of HRV reflects sympathetic tone, the HF component represents parasympathetic activity, and the LF/HF ratio indicates the sympathovagal balance. In this study, HRV was measured using the Power Lab System (8/35, AD Instruments, Bella Vista, Australia). Electrodes were affixed to the surface of all four legs of each canine participant. All recordings were performed in a dedicated conscious testing room, where dogs were lying on a coach in a quiet, dim-lighted environment, without any sedation or anesthesia. After a stabilization period of 15 min, limb leads were then connected, enabling the recording of their body surface electrocardiogram (ECG) signals for a minimum duration of 10 min. Subsequently, HRV analysis was conducted, focusing on three power spectral indices: the normalized units of low-frequency power (LFnu), the normalized units of high-frequency power (HFnu), and the ratio of LF/HF using the software integrated into the system [LFnu = LF/(LF + HF) and HFnu = HF/(LF + HF)] [14]. The units for LF and HF are denoted as “ms2.”

Echocardiographic evaluation of LV function

Transthoracic Doppler echocardiography was conducted on conscious dogs at both baseline and 4 weeks after MI. The high-resolution imaging system Vivid E9 (GE healthcare, USA) was used for the echocardiographic assessment. Continuous ECG monitoring was utilized during the Doppler echocardiography procedure, and a 3.5-MHz electronic probe was used, like previous methods [10]. To assess left ventricular (LV) function comprehensively, the end - systolic LV volumes (ESV), end - diastolic LV volumes (EDV), and ejection fraction (LVEF) were precisely calculated by employing the Simpson's biplane equation. This well- established equation provides a reliable means to quantify these crucial LV parameters. In addition to these volume - related metrics, further evaluation was conducted on the mitral inflow velocities. Specifically, the peak mitral inflow velocity during early diastole, denoted as (E), and the peak mitral inflow velocity during left atrial contraction, labeled as (A), were carefully measured from the velocity waveforms. Subsequently, the E/A ratio, an important indicator of LV diastolic function, was determined based on these measured velocities. The measurement protocol was designed to ensure accuracy and representativeness. Measurements were carried out on at least three randomly selected end - expiratory beats. This random selection process helps to capture the natural variability of the cardiac cycle and obtain a more comprehensive picture of LV function.

All the obtained measurements were then subjected to evaluation by an experienced echocardiographer. This echocardiographer was not involved in the actual echocardiographic recording process. Moreover, the echocardiographer was kept blinded to the intervention, which effectively eliminates potential bias and ensures the objectivity and reliability of the evaluation results.

Blood sampling

Blood sample collection took place in a specialized conscious testing room. This room was meticulously set up to provide a calm and soothing environment for the dogs. The dogs were gently placed on a plush and comfortable couch, surrounded by a quiet ambiance and dim lighting, which helped them relax. After allowing a 15 - min stabilization period for the dogs to acclimate to the setting, venous blood samples were carefully collected in cephalic vein of the dog's forearm. Ice – chilled tubes pre - coated with EDTA (BD Vacutainer K2E, BD Diagnostics, NJ, USA) were used for this purpose. The blood collection was carried out on three distinct occasions: one day before the surgery, and on the 1st and 28th days after the surgery. These specific points were strategically chosen to monitor the physiological changes in the dogs at different stages of the surgical process. Once the blood samples were collected, they were promptly processed. The samples were centrifuged at a speed of 3000 rpm for 15 min at a temperature of 4 °C. This centrifugation step was crucial as it effectively separated the plasma from the other components of the blood. The separated plasma samples were then stored at −80 °C. This ultra - low temperature storage was employed to preserve the integrity of the plasma and prevent any degradation of the substances of interest until further analysis could be performed. To determine the levels of high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP), norepinephrine (NE), N-terminal prohormone of brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) and Neuropeptide Y (NPY) in the plasma samples, canine - specific high - sensitivity enzyme - linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits were utilized. These kits were sourced from the Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute in Nanjing, China, and ELK Biotechnology CO., Ltd. in Wuhan, China. The use of these specialized kits ensured accurate and reliable measurement of the target substances, providing valuable insights into the physiological state of the dogs.

Western blot

A myocardial tissue specimen approximately 1 cm2 in size was carefully excised from the non - infarcted area of the left ventricular (LV) free wall. To process this tissue, it was homogenized using a radioimmunoprecipitation assay lysis buffer (Rockland, Gilbertsville, PA) fortified with a proteinase inhibitor (Roche, Basel, Switzerland). This addition of the proteinase inhibitor was crucial to prevent protein degradation during the subsequent steps. Following homogenization, the tissue homogenate underwent a two - step centrifugation process. First, it was centrifuged at 4 °C and 2000 g for 10 min. This initial centrifugation helped to separate larger debris from the mixture. The supernatant obtained from this step was then subjected to a second, more rigorous centrifugation at 12,000 g for 20 min. This further separation was aimed at obtaining a relatively pure protein - containing supernatant. The protein concentration in each supernatant sample was accurately determined using a DC protein assay kit (BioRad, Hercules, CA). This step was essential to ensure that equal amounts of protein were used in the subsequent analyses. After determining the protein concentration, equal quantities of protein were carefully loaded onto a 15% sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS – PAGE) gel (Bio - Rad). The proteins were then electrophoretically separated on the gel based on their molecular weights. Subsequently, the separated proteins were transferred onto an Immobilon - P membrane (Millipore, Billerica, MA). To prevent non - specific binding of antibodies on the membrane, BlockAce (Dainippon pharmaceutical, Japan) was used for blocking. This created a clean background for the subsequent antibody - protein interactions. The study employed a set of primary antibodies for the detection of specific proteins. These included a polyclonal antibody for matrix metallopeptidase 9 (MMP - 9; TheBinding Site, Birmingham, UK), antibodies against transforming growth factor β1 (TGF - β1), collagen I, and collagen III (Abcam, Cambridgeshire, England), as well as an antibody against β - Actin (Santa Cruz Biotechnology), which served as an internal control. The appropriate concentration of each primary antibody was strictly determined according to the manufacturer's instructions to ensure optimal binding and accurate detection. Enhanced chemiluminescence (Invitrogen) was used as the detection method. After the antibodies bound to their respective target proteins, the chemiluminescent signals were generated. The resulting bands were then scanned and analyzed using an ECL detection system (GE Healthcare). To ensure the reliability and reproducibility of the results, all experiments were conducted in triplicate and repeated biologically at least three times. This multiple - repetition approach helped to minimize experimental errors and provided more robust data for analysis.

Data analysis

Continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Non-parametric data should be presented as median with IQRs. To compare data between two groups that followed a normal distribution, a student's t-test was employed. For multi-group comparisons, one-way or two-way ANOVA analysis was utilized. Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to test if the continuous variables are distributed normally. The statistical analysis was carried out using SPSS 22.0 software (IBM Corp, New York, USA). All statistical tests were two-sided, and a significance level of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

In this study, ventricular fibrillation resulting in fatalities was observed in 3 dogs from the MI group and 1 dog from the CSAD group, out of a total of 31 dogs. It is important to emphasize that these dogs were not included in the study.

CSAD attenuated the activation of LSG neural activity

Fig. 1 illustrates the LSG neural activity recorded at baseline and at the 4-week follow-up across three groups. At baseline, no significant differences were observed among the groups. However, a clear increase was evident in the MI group compared to baseline after 4 weeks (p < 0.05). Furthermore, there was a noticeable increase in the MI group compared to the Control group at 4 weeks (p < 0.05). Nevertheless, following CSAD treatment, a significant decrease was observed in the MI group compared to the 4-week measurement (p < 0.05).

Effect of CSAD on HRV

HRV has been widely used as an indirect measure of autonomic activity, as depicted in Fig. 2. The LF component of HRV reflects sympathetic tone, the HF component represents parasympathetic activity, and the LF/HF ratio indicates the sympatho-vagal balance. In the Control group, no significant alterations were observed in LF, HF, or the LF/HF ratio between baseline and the 4-weeks. However, following MI induction in the MI group dogs, there was a notable increase in LFnu and LF/HF ratio, accompanied by a slight decrease in HFnu, when compared to the Control group (all p < 0.05). Conversely, treatment with CSAD effectively mitigated the increasing trends in the LF/HF ratio and enhanced HF power (all p < 0.05). These findings suggest that MI significantly elevated sympathetic activity and inhibited parasympathetic nervous activity, while CSAD administration effectively countered these effects of MI.

Discussion

Major findings

The aim of the present study was to evaluate whether CSAD in GPs improved LV remodeling by balancing the dysfunction of cardiac autonomic activity in post-MI dogs. The significant findings were as follows: (1) CSAD treatment attenuated the over-activation of LSG neural activity; (2) CSAD treatment attenuated LV dilation and improved both systolic and diastolic cardiac functions; (3) CSAD treatment reduced the cardiac fibrosis.

The cardiac autonomic nervous system (CANS) encompasses both the extrinsic (central) and intrinsic components that innervate the heart. The extrinsic CANS includes ganglia located in the brain or along the spinal cord, housing the cell bodies of neurons, as well as their axons that project towards the heart [11], [12]. The intrinsic CANS comprises a complex network of nerve axons, interconnecting neurons, and clusters of autonomic ganglia called ganglionated plexi (GP). GPs are embedded within epicardial fat pads. GPs, forming an extensive epicardial neural network within the heart, connect the extrinsic autonomic nervous system and intrinsic autonomic nervous system [7]. GP contain both sympathetic and parasympathetic elements, as well as a variety of neuropeptides and neuromodulators, including calcitonin gene-related peptide, vasoactive intestinal polypeptide, substance P and nitric oxide. GPs are connected with each other by interconnecting neurons and create an extensive and complicated epicardial neural network [13]. The function of GP is to modulate the autonomic interactions between the extrinsic and intrinsic ANS, which affects the functions of heart. Sensory neurons in GPs play a crucial role in mediating an autonomic nervous reflex. Sympathetic afferents receive stimulation from heart, deliver information through both the stellate ganglia and the spinal cord, and finally terminate in the nucleus of the solitary tract, resulting in an augmentation of sympathetic outflow from the central nervous system. Therefore, GPs are an important target in regulating cardiac autonomic nervous system.

While catheter or surgical GP ablation is a commonly used approach for treating heart diseases, it can inadvertently cause damage to various nerve structures [14]. Additionally, ablation procedures can also harm the myocardium and other non-neuronal structures, leading to the release of neurotrophic factors that promote nerve sprouting. Nerve sprouting can begin as early as 2 h after injury and persist for more than a month. The reinnervation patterns following ablation can be highly heterogeneous, contributing to heart remodeling. Given that adrenergic and cholinergic nerves are extensively localized in these areas, selectively targeting cardiac sympathetic afferents for ablation is a reasonable approach [15], [16]. While we did not perform direct TRPV1 immunohistochemistry in the present study, prior work by Tavares et al. in the same canine model has established the presence of TRPV1-expressing neurons in the ARGP and demonstrated their susceptibility to RTX ablation. In our study, we observed that selective ablation of cardiac sympathetic afferents using RTX significantly inhibited the excessive activation of the sympathetic nervous system and mitigated the autonomic remodeling induced by MI. Furthermore, we found that CSAD notably improved LV remodeling caused by sympathetic over-activation.

The strategy of inhibiting sympathetic tone through CSAD offers several potential benefits over the use of beta-blockers in managing conditions like HF. Firstly, Cardiac ganglionated plexi are clusters of nerve cells within the heart that play a significant role in regulating cardiac function. Targeting cardiac GP allows for a highly localized intervention, addressing sympathetic activity at its source within the heart. Beta-blockers, on the other hand, have a systemic effect on beta-adrenergic receptors throughout the body. Secondly, Beta-blockers can have systemic side effects, including fatigue, bronchoconstriction, and sexual dysfunction, due to their broad impact on beta receptors. By specifically targeting cardiac GP, the denervation approach may lead to fewer systemic side effects, making it more tolerable for some patients. Thirdly, in some individuals with chronic HF, there can be a decrease in beta-receptor sensitivity or even resistance to beta-blockers over time. In such cases, alternative strategies like CSAD may provide a complementary therapeutic option to manage sympathetic overactivity.

Clinical application

Recently, both basic and clinical evidence demonstrated that sympathetic overactivation contributes to the LV remodeling after MI. GP plays a crucial role in regulating cardiac sympathetic tone. GP was regarded as a therapeutic target in treating cardiovascular diseases. However, both catheter and surgical GP ablation can inadvertently damage nervous structures and aggravate heart remodeling. Therefore, CSAD by RTX emerges as a promising approach. By regulating cardiac autonomic nervous activity, CSAD provides a more precise and controlled intervention.

Limitations

A limitation of this study is that we did not directly quantify TRPV1-expressing neurons in the ARGP before and after RTX administration. However, prior work by Tavares et al. (2019) has demonstrated the presence of TRPV1+ afferent neurons in the canine ARGP using immunohistochemistry and has shown that RTX injection induces apoptosis of GP cells, supporting the validity of our approach. Nonetheless, we acknowledge that direct confirmation in our own tissue samples would strengthen causality. Firstly, only male animals were included and a limitation of this study is that TRPV1-expressing neurons in the ARGP were not directly quantified before and after RTX administration, precluding definitive confirmation of neuronal targeting and causality. Secondly, we did not measure the associated signal transduction pathway in brain, which may elucidate the detail mechanism that underlines the treatment effects. Thirdly, although we did assess the LV remodeling in function and structure, the downstream mechanisms are still required to further study. Infarct size was not directly measured, although comparable myocardial enzyme levels among groups suggest similar initial injury. Lastly, the animal model used in this study is a chronic model and Pentobarbital can significantly alter autonomic tone and should not be used for neural recordings, therefore, future studies are needed to investigate the acute effects of CSAD in cardiovascular diseases.

Conclusions

CSAD by RTX has emerged as a promising strategy for cardiac autonomic modulation and the treatment of LV remodeling induced by MI. This approach offers a more precise and controlled intervention in comparison to other methods.
