Hadamard product of convex functions and Jackson operator
Krzysztof Piejko 1 , Janusz Sokół 2,∗ , Katarzyna Tra̧bka-Wiȩc ̵law 3

Abstract
. In this paper we consider some properties of Jackson’s difference operator for convex univalent functions in | z | < 1 |z|<1 with complex parameter q q as a Hadamard product of two power series. Jackson in 1908 introduced for a real q q , q ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) q\in[0,1) , the difference operator d q ​ f ​ ( z ) {\rm d}_{q}f(z) for an analytic function f f in the unit disc | z | < 1 |z|<1 in the complex plane. Thanks to this operator, many mathematicians have extended the theory of functions in q q -theory. The q q -theory has found many applications in theory of hypergeometric series, special functions, combinatorics, number theory, fluid mechanics, quantum mechanics and physics.

1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{C}}
denote the open complex plane and
\mathbb{D}
be the unit disc,

{{\mathbb{D}}}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:\ |z|<1\}
. In this paper we will use
the following well-known definitions and notations. Let
{\mathcal{H}}
denote the
class of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
. Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be
the subclass of
{\mathcal{H}}
consisting of functions normalized by

f(0)=0
,
f^{\prime}(0)=1
, i.e.,

(1.1)

f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}

and let
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{A}
be the class of functions
which are univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}
. Let
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
,

\alpha\in[0,1)
, denote the class of functions
f\in\mathcal{A}
such
that

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}>\alpha,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Functions belonging to the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
are called

starlike univalent functions of order

\alpha
, while
\mathcal{S}^{*}=\mathcal{S}^{*}(0)
is called the class of
starlike univalent
functions (with respect to the origin)
. This is because if

f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
, then
f(\mathbb{D})
is a starlike set with
respect to the origin. Recall that a set
E\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is
said to be starlike with respect to a point
w_{0}\in E
, if and only
if, the linear segment joining
w_{0}
to any other point
w\in E

lies entirely in
E
. In
[
7
]
Robertson extended
\mathcal{S}^{*}
to
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
, but the geometric interpretation of

f(\mathbb{D})
in this class is still an open problem. Let
\mathcal{K}
denote the class of functions
f\in\mathcal{A}
such that

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right\}>0,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Functions belonging to the class
\mathcal{K}
are called

convex univalent functions
because

f\in\mathcal{K}\Rightarrow[f\in\mathcal{S}\quad{\rm and}\quad f(\mathbb{D})~{\rm is~a~convex~set}].

It is noted that
f\in\mathcal{K}\Leftrightarrow zf^{\prime}(z)\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
.
For
f(z)=a_{0}+a_{1}z+a_{2}z^{2}+\ldots
and
g(z)=b_{0}+b_{1}z+b_{2}z^{2}+\ldots

the Hadamard product (or convolution) is defined by
(f\ast g)(z)=a_{0}b_{0}+a_{1}b_{1}z+a_{2}b_{2}z^{2}+\ldots
. In
[
9
]
it was
proved the famous result that for all
f,g\in\mathcal{K}
and
h\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
we have

(1.2)

f\ast g\in\mathcal{K}\quad{\rm{and}}\quad f\ast h\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha).

In this paper we replace in some formulas the derivative

f^{\prime}
with the derivative operator
{\rm d}_{\zeta}f
which is described in the
next section. We compare some results for
f^{\prime}
with
analogous results for
{\rm d}_{\zeta}f
.

2.
\zeta
-derivative operator

For given
\zeta
,
|\zeta|\leq 1
, we consider the function

h_{\zeta}
of the form

h_{\zeta}(z)=\frac{z}{(1-\zeta z)(1-z)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-\zeta^{n}}{1-\zeta}\ z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then

(2.1)

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{zh^{\prime}_{\zeta}(z)}{h_{\zeta}(z)}\right\}=\mathfrak{Re}\left\{1+\frac{z}{1-z}+\frac{\zeta z}{1-\zeta z}\right\}>1-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{|\zeta|}{1+|\zeta|}=\frac{1-|\zeta|}{2(1+|\zeta|)}\geq 0,\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

so
h_{\zeta}
is starlike for all complex
\zeta
,
|\zeta|\leq 1
. It
is easy to check that if
\zeta=1
, then the function
h_{\zeta}

becomes the well-known Koebe function

h_{1}(z)=\frac{z}{(1-z)^{2}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nz^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

For each
f\in\mathcal{A}
we can express its derivative in terms of
convolution and the Koebe function as

(2.2)

f^{\prime}(z)=\frac{1}{z}\left\{f(z)*h_{1}(z)\right\}=\frac{1}{z}\left\{f(z)*\frac{z}{(1-z)^{2}}\right\},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

In
[
5
]
the
following generalization of (
2.2
) for
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
,

|\zeta|\leq 1
, was defined

(2.3)

{\rm d}_{\zeta}f(z)=\frac{1}{z}\left\{f(z)*h_{\zeta}(z)\right\}=\frac{1}{z}\left\{f(z)*\frac{z}{(1-\zeta z)(1-z)}\right\}.

For
\zeta=1
convolution operator (
2.3
) becomes the
derivative
f^{\prime}
. For
f
given by (
1.1
) we have:

(2.4)

\displaystyle{\rm d}_{\zeta}f(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z}\left\{f(z)*\frac{z}{(1-\zeta z)(1-z)}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z}\left\{\left(z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\right)*\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-\zeta^{n}}{1-\zeta}\ z^{n}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1-\zeta^{n}}{1-\zeta}\ a_{n}z^{n-1}=1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}[n]_{\zeta}a_{n}z^{n-1},

where

[n]_{\zeta}=\frac{1-\zeta^{n}}{1-\zeta},\quad n=2,3,\ldots\ .

A natural question is what kind of results on
f^{\prime}
hold also for

\zeta
-derivative operator
{\rm d}_{\zeta}f
. It is known that if

f
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and for some real
\alpha
we have

\mathfrak{Re}\{\exp(i\alpha)f^{\prime}(z)\}>0,\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

then
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. The unit disc may be replaced
with any convex domain too. This is the well-known
Noshiro-Warschawski univalence condition,
[
4
,
11
]
.
Such condition does not hold for
{\rm d}_{\zeta}f
instead of
f^{\prime}
,
apart the case
\zeta=1
when
{\rm d}_{\zeta}f=f^{\prime}
. It is known that

f(z)=z+az^{2}
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
, if and only if,
|a|\leq 1/2
. Therefore, for given
\zeta
,
|\zeta|<1
, the function

f(z)=z+\frac{1}{1+\zeta}z^{2}

is not univalent in
\mathbb{D}
, although

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{{\rm d}_{\zeta}f(z)\right\}=\mathfrak{Re}\left\{1+z\right\}>0,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Jackson in
[
2
,
3
]
introduced and studied the

q
-derivative,
0\leq q<1
, as

(2.5)

{\rm d}_{q}f(z)=\frac{f(qz)-f(z)}{(q-1)z},\quad z\neq 0\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm d}_{q}f(0)=f^{\prime}(0).

Thus, from (
2.5
) for a function
f

given by (
1.1
) we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle z{\rm d}_{q}f(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}[n]_{q}a_{n}z^{n},

where

[n]_{q}=\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q},\quad n=2,3,\ldots\ .

It is clear that for
\zeta=q
,
0\leq q<1
, the
\zeta
-derivative
operator given in (
2.4
), becomes the Jackson
q
-derivative of

f
defined in (
2.5
). Therefore, we can also look at Jackson’s

q
-derivative as a special case of convolution operator (
2.3
).
In fact, it follows that the considered convolution operator is an
analytic extension of the Jackson derivative from the segment to the disk.

Definition.
[
5
,
6
]
Let
f\in\mathcal{A}
. For a given
\zeta
,
|\zeta|\leq 1
, we say that
f
is in the
class
\mathcal{R}(\zeta,\alpha)
,
0\leq\alpha<1
, if

(2.7)

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{{\rm d}_{\zeta}f(z)}\right\}>\alpha,\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

where the operator
{\rm d}_{\zeta}
is defined in (
2.3
).

Remark 1.
It is easy to see that
\mathcal{R}(1,\alpha)=\mathcal{A}
.

Remark 2.
For the function
h(z)=z/(1-z)
, we have

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{h^{\prime}(z)}{{\rm d}_{\zeta}h(z)}\right\}=\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{1-\zeta z}{1-z}\right\}>\frac{1+|\zeta|}{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

so for a given
\zeta
,
|\zeta|\leq 1
, the function
h\in\mathcal{R}(\zeta,(1+|\zeta|)/2)
.

Remark 3.
For
f
given by (
1.1
) condition (
2.7
) becomes

(2.8)

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}na_{n}z^{n-1}}{1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}[n]_{\zeta}a_{n}z^{n-1}}\right\}>\alpha,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

3.
Main results

Theorem 3.1
.

If
f
is in the class
\mathcal{K}
of convex univalent functions,
then

(3.1)

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{\zeta{\rm d}_{\zeta}f(z)}-1\right)\right\}>\frac{1}{2}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\zeta
,
|\zeta|\leq 1
. The result is
the best possible.

Proof.

First we prove that for each
f\in\mathcal{K}
the following
inequality

(3.2)

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\left(\frac{e^{ia}}{1-e^{ia}}-\frac{e^{ib}}{1-e^{ib}}\right)\frac{f(e^{ib}z)-f(z)}{f(z)-f(e^{ia}z)}\right\}>0

holds for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and for all real
a,b
such that

0<b<a<\pi
. We have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{e^{ia}}{1-e^{ia}}-\frac{e^{ib}}{1-e^{ib}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{2}\cot\frac{a}{2}\right)-\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{2}\cot\frac{b}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}\left(\cot\frac{a}{2}-\cot\frac{b}{2}\right).

In this way we obtain

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{e^{ia}}{1-e^{ia}}-\frac{e^{ib}}{1-e^{ib}}\right\}=0\quad{\rm and}\quad\mathfrak{Im}\left\{\frac{e^{ia}}{1-e^{ia}}-\frac{e^{ib}}{1-e^{ib}}\right\}<0.

Therefore, to calculate the real part in (
3.2
), we need the
imaginary part of the second factor in (
3.2
). The curve

f(w=|z|)
is convex so as
x
runs the interval
[b,a]
then the
vector
f(e^{ix}z)-f(z)
turns around
f(z)
in the counter-clock
vise direction no more than
\pi
. In the other words,

\arg\{f(e^{ix}z)-f(z)\}
increases no more than
\pi
. This gives

(3.4)

\arg\frac{f(e^{ib}z)-f(z)}{f(e^{ia}z)-f(z)}\in(-\pi,0).

So we have

\mathfrak{Im}\left\{\frac{f(e^{ib}z)-f(z)}{f(e^{ia}z)-f(z)}\right\}<0.

From this we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\left(\frac{e^{ia}}{1-e^{ia}}-\frac{e^{ib}}{1-e^{ib}}\right)\frac{f(e^{ib}z)-f(z)}{f(z)-f(e^{ia}z)}\right\}

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{Im}\left\{\frac{e^{ia}}{1-e^{ia}}-\frac{e^{ib}}{1-e^{ib}}\right\}\mathfrak{Im}\left\{\frac{f(e^{ib}z)-f(z)}{f(z)-f(e^{ia}z)}\right\}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{Im}\left\{\frac{e^{ia}}{1-e^{ia}}-\frac{e^{ib}}{1-e^{ib}}\right\}\mathfrak{Im}\left\{\frac{f(e^{ib}z)-f(z)}{f(e^{ia}z)-f(z)}\right\}

\displaystyle>0

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and for all real
a,b
such that

0<b<a<\pi
. This proves (
3.2
). Inequality (
3.2
) may
be extended to the case for
-\pi<a<b<0
(in this case both
imaginary parts in (
3
) becomes positive). Therefore
(
3.2
) holds for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and for all real
a,b
,

0<|b|<|a|<\pi
.

Inequality (
3.2
) may be written in the following equivalent
form

(3.6)

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{f(e^{ib}z)-f(z)}{e^{ib}z-z}\frac{z(e^{ib}-e^{ia})}{(1-e^{ia})(f(z)-f(e^{ia}z))}\right\}>0.

If
b\rightarrow 0
, then (
3.6
) becomes

(3.7)

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{f^{\prime}(z)\frac{z}{f(z)-f(e^{ia}z)}\right\}>0

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and for all real
-\pi<a<\pi
,
a\neq 0
. It
is clear that (
3.7
) holds for
a=\pi
. We shall consider the
function

h(\zeta)=\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\zeta){\rm d}_{\zeta}f(z)}-\frac{1+\zeta}{2(1-\zeta)},

with
f\in\mathcal{K}
. If
f
is of the form (
1.1
), then we see
that

\displaystyle h(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\zeta}\left\{\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{{\rm d}_{\zeta}f(z)}-1\right\}+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\zeta}\frac{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(n-(\zeta^{n-1}+\cdots+\zeta+1)\right)a_{n}z^{n-1}}{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\zeta^{n-1}+\cdots+\zeta+1)a_{n}z^{n-1}}+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\zeta^{n-2}+2\zeta^{n-3}+3\zeta^{n-4}+\cdots+(n-2)\zeta+n-1\right)a_{n}z^{n-1}}{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+\zeta+\cdots+\zeta^{n-1})a_{n}z^{n-1}}+\frac{1}{2},

so the function
h(\zeta)
is analytic in
|\zeta|\leq 1
.
Furthermore, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Re}\left\{h(1)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(n(n-1)/2\right)a_{n}z^{n-1}}{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}na_{n}z^{n-1}}+\frac{1}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n(n-1)a_{n}z^{n-1}}{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2na_{n}z^{n-1}}+\frac{1}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{2f^{\prime}(z)}+\frac{1}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0,

because
\mathfrak{Re}\{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)/2f^{\prime}(z)\}>-1/2
for all
f\in\mathcal{K}
. We have also

\mathfrak{Re}\{h(0)\}=\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{-zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(0)-f(z)}-\frac{1}{2}\right\}=\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}-\frac{1}{2}\right\}>0

because convex
f
is starlike of order
1/2
too. But

\mathfrak{Re}\{h(|\zeta|<r)\}
,
r\in(0,1)
, attains its minimum on

|\zeta|=r
. On the unit circle
|\zeta|=1
, we have by (
3.8
)
that
\mathfrak{Re}\left\{h(1)\right\}>0
and for
\zeta=e^{ia}
,

\zeta\neq 1
, we have by (
3.7
)

\mathfrak{Re}\{h(e^{ia})\}=\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)-f(e^{ia}z)}-\frac{1+e^{ia}}{2(1-e^{ia})}\right\}=\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)-f(e^{ia}z)}\right\}>0.

This shows that
\mathfrak{Re}\left\{h(\zeta)\right\}>0
on

|\zeta|=1
. Therefore, we finally obtain

(3.9)

\mathfrak{Re}\{h(\zeta)\}>0

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
|\zeta|\leq 1
. This and equality

h(\zeta)=\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\zeta){\rm d}_{\zeta}f(z)}-\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}-\frac{1}{2}

give (
3.1
). If we take the convex function
g(z)=z/(1-z)
,
then

\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}\left(\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{\zeta{\rm d}_{\zeta}g(z)}-1\right)=\frac{1}{1-z}

maps
\mathbb{D}
onto the half-plane
\mathfrak{Re}\{w\}>1/2
, so
(
3.1
) is the best possible in the class
\mathcal{K}
.
∎

Note that (
3.1
) does not hold in the class of starlike
functions. The function
f(z)=z+z^{2}/2
is in
\mathcal{S}^{*}
but
(
3.1
) becomes

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}\left(\frac{1+z}{\zeta(1+(1+\zeta)z/2)}-1\right)\right\}>\frac{1}{2}

which evidently does not hold for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\zeta
,

|\zeta|\leq 1
.

A simple calculation yields another form of
(
3.1
) in the following corollary.

Corollary 3.2
.

If
f
is in the class
\mathcal{K}
of convex univalent functions,
then

(3.10)

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\zeta){\rm d}_{\zeta}f(z)}\right\}>\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{1+\zeta}{2(1-\zeta)}\right\}>0

and

(3.11)

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{1}{1-\zeta}\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{{\rm d}_{\zeta}f(z)}-1\right)\right\}>-\frac{1}{2}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\zeta
such that
|\zeta|\leq 1
. The
results are the best possible.

In
[
6
, Lemma 2]
it was proved that for
f\in\mathcal{K}

(3.12)

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\zeta){\rm d}_{\zeta}f(z)}\right\}>0

for all
z,\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
. Therefore, (
3.10
) improves
earlier result (
3.12
).

Corollary 3.3
.

If
f
is in the class
\mathcal{K}
of convex univalent functions,
then

(3.13)

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\zeta){\rm d}_{\zeta}f(z)}\right\}>\frac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{2(1-2\mathfrak{Re}\zeta+|\zeta|^{2})}=\frac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{2|1-\zeta|^{2}}.

The result is the best possible.

Proof.

Inequality (
3.13
) follows from (
3.10
) and from the
known fact that

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{1+\zeta}{2(1-\zeta)}\right\}=\frac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{2(1-2\mathfrak{Re}\zeta+|\zeta|^{2})}

for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
. For convex function
h(z)=z/(1-z)
, we
have

\left.\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{h^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\zeta){\rm d}_{\zeta}h(z)}\right\}\right|_{z=-1}=\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{1+\zeta}{2(1-\zeta)}\right\}=\frac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{2(1-2\mathfrak{Re}\zeta+|\zeta|^{2})}.

This shows that (
3.13
) is the best possible.
∎

q
instead of

Corollary 3.5
.

If
f
is in the class
\mathcal{K}
of convex univalent functions
and
q\in[0,1)
, then there exists an analytic function
p\in\mathcal{H}
,

(3.16)

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{p(z)\right\}>0,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{\frac{2f^{\prime}(z)}{{\rm d}_{q}f(z)}-(1+q)}{1-q}\right\}>0,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Therefore, the function in the brackets is an analytic function
p

with positive real part in
\mathbb{D}
with normalization

.
This gives (
3.16
).
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

If
f
is in the class
\mathcal{K}
of convex univalent functions,
then

(3.17)

\frac{1+qr}{1+r}\leq\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{{\rm d}_{q}f(z)}\right\}\leq\frac{1-qr}{1-r}

and

(3.18)

\frac{1+qr}{1+r}\leq\left|\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{{\rm d}_{q}f(z)}\right|\leq\frac{1-qr}{1-r}

for all
z,q
such that
|z|=r<1
,
q\in[0,1)
. The result is
sharp.

Proof.

From (
3.14
), we have

\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{{\rm d}_{q}f(z)}\prec\frac{1+(1-2\beta)z}{1-z},\quad\beta=\frac{1+q}{2}.

This gives

(3.19)

\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{{\rm d}_{q}f(z)}\prec\frac{1-qz}{1-z}.

It is known that

\frac{1+qr}{1+r}\leq\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{1-qz}{1-z}\right\}\leq\frac{1-qr}{1-r}

for all
z,q
,
|z|=r<1
,
q\in[0,1)
. This shows (
3.17
).
For convex function
h(z)=z/(1-z)
, we have

\frac{h^{\prime}(z)}{{\rm d}_{q}h(z)}=\frac{1-qz}{1-z}.

This shows the sharpness of (
3.17
). The same argumentation
shows that subordination (
3.19
) implies (
3.18
).
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

If
f
is in the class
\mathcal{K}
of convex univalent functions,
then

(3.20)

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{f(z)}{\left[\log\frac{1}{1-z}\right]*z{\rm d}_{q}f(z)}\right\}>\frac{1+q}{2}

for all
z,q
such that
z\in\mathbb{D}
,
q\in[0,1)
.

Proof.

It is known, see
[
1
, p.171]
, that if
N\in\mathcal{H}
,
D\in\mathcal{H}
,
N(0)=D(0)=0
and
D
is starlike univalent in

\mathbb{D}
, then

(3.21)

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{N^{\prime}(z)}{D^{\prime}(z)}\right\}>\alpha\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{N(z)}{D(z)}\right\}>\alpha

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. From (
3.14
) we have

(3.22)

\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{{\rm d}_{q}f(z)}\right\}>\frac{1+q}{2}.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle{\rm d}_{q}f(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[n]_{q}a_{n}z^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[n]_{q}a_{n}z^{n}*\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}z^{n}\right\}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{z{\rm d}_{q}f(z)*\log\frac{1}{1-z}\right\}^{\prime},

where
z\mapsto\log\frac{1}{1-z}
is convex and
z{\rm d}_{q}f(z)=f(z)*h_{q}(z)
is starlike by (
1.2
) and
(
2.1
). Now, applying (
3.21
) and (
3.22
) give us
(
3.20
).
∎
