1.
Introduction

Consider the function

\mathfrak{m}(x,y,z):=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}-3xyz.

Given a non-negative integer
m
, we say that an ordered triple
(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{N}^{3}

is a
Markoff
m
-triple
if

\mathfrak{m}(a,b,c)=m.

This equation, called the Markoff
m
-equation, generalises the classical Diophantine equation introduced by A. A. Markoff, corresponding to the case
m=0

[
12
,
13
]
.
In that classical setting, all ordered solutions are connected via a unique infinite tree, generated by the three
Vieta transformations
:

\displaystyle\nu_{1}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=(b,c,3bc-a),

(1.1)

\displaystyle\nu_{2}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=(a,c,3ac-b),

\displaystyle\nu_{3}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ord}(3ab-c,a,b),

where
\operatorname{ord}
denotes the operation of rearranging a triple into non-decreasing order. By Lemma 2.1 of
[
18
]
, one has
3ab-c<b
, whereas
3ab-c
may be either smaller or larger than
a
.

If
m>0
, the above Vieta transformations are still valid and give rise to trees of solutions. As shown in
[
18
]
, when
m>0
the number of distinct
m
-trees is equal to the number of
minimal Markoff
m
-triples
, namely those ordered triples
(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
such that
\nu_{3}(a,b,c)
has a non-positive component. Equivalently, a Markoff
m
-triple
(a,b,c)
is minimal if

c\geq 3ab.

An
infinite path
is an infinite sequence of
Markoff
m
-triples
\{(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n})\}_{n\geq 0}
such that each triple is obtained
from the previous one by applying one of the Vieta transformations
\nu_{1}
or
\nu_{2}
.
Equivalently, for every
n\geq 1,
we have

\nu_{3}(a_{n+1},b_{n+1},c_{n+1})=(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n}).

In particular, we denote by
\mathcal{B}(a_{0},b_{0},c_{0})
the
branch rooted at
(a_{0},b_{0},c_{0})
,
namely, the infinite path consisting exclusively of

\nu_{2}
-steps, i.e.

\mathcal{B}(a_{0},b_{0},c_{0}):=\bigl\{\nu_{2}^{\,n}(a_{0},b_{0},c_{0})\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\bigr\}.

Two classical examples in the case
m=0
are, the branch
\mathcal{B}(1,1,2)
, whose components are Fibonacci numbers of odd index, and the branch
\mathcal{B}(2,2,12)
, whose components are Pell numbers of odd index.

In this article, we deal with
k
-Fibonacci numbers, defined for any positive integer
k
by

\left\{\begin{aligned} F_{k}(0)&=0,\\
F_{k}(1)&=1,\\
F_{k}(n)&=kF_{k}(n-1)+F_{k}(n-2),\quad\text{for }n\geq 2.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

In the notation of
[
9
]
,
F_{k}(n)
corresponds to a Lucas-type sequence
U_{n}
with parameters
P=k
and
Q=-1
. There is an extensive literature on branches of
m
-trees whose three elements are
k
-Fibonacci components: the classical Markoff equation (
m=0
) was studied for Fibonacci numbers (
k=1
) by Luca and the fifth author in
[
11
]
, for Pell numbers (
k=2
) by Kafle, Togbe and Srinivasan in
[
17
]
, for
k>2
by Gómez, Gómez and Luca in
[
8
]
, and for Lucas sequences in
[
3
,
19
]
. By contrast, the literature on the Markoff equation for
m>0
is comparatively scarce: the case of Fibonacci triples (
k=1
) was treated in
[
1
]
, and that of
k
-Fibonacci triples with
k\geq 2
in
[
2
]
. Finally, Luca
[
10
]
analysed Markoff triples (
m=0
) with two Fibonacci components
(k=1)
, describing the branch
\mathcal{B}(1,1,2)
and its image under
\nu_{1}
, and proving that only finitely many such triples can occur outside of these instances.

In this paper, we extend Luca’s result by describing the infinite paths of Markoff
m
-triples with at least two
k
-Fibonacci components and
m>0
.

Our first result classifies all non-minimal Markoff
m
-triples whose
last two entries are
k
-Fibonacci numbers.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(a,b,c)
be a non-minimal Markoff
m
-triple with
m>0
. Then
b
and
c
are
k
-Fibonacci numbers if and only if

(a,b,c)=\left(\alpha_{k,r},\,F_{k}(N-r),\,F_{k}(N+r)\right),

where
\alpha_{k,r}=\frac{F_{k}(4r)}{3F_{k}(2r)},

r
is an odd integer,
3\nmid k
, and

; moreover, if
k\in\{1,2\}
, then

must be odd.

The triples appearing in Theorem
1.1
naturally give rise to branches in the corresponding

-trees. More precisely, we call

\mathcal{B}\bigl(\alpha_{k,r},\,F_{k}(N-r),\,F_{k}(N+r)\bigr)

a

(2,k)
-Fibonacci branch
.
Our second result describes how these principal branches are distributed among distinct
m
-trees.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix an odd integer
r\geq 1
and assume
3\nmid k
.
Then the family of Markoff triples

\bigl(\alpha_{k,r},\,F_{k}(\ell),\,F_{k}(\ell+2r)\bigr),\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{N},\

with

\begin{cases}\ell>2r,&\text{if }k>2,\\[3.0pt]
\ell\geq 2r\text{ and }\ell\ \text{even},&\text{if }k\in\{1,2\},\end{cases}

is distributed among exactly

(2,k)
-Fibonacci branches, described as follows:

(1)

For each even
\ell_{0}\in\{2,4,\dots,2r\}
, the triple

(F_{k}(\ell_{0}),\alpha_{k,r},F_{k}(\ell_{0}+2r))
is minimal. The corresponding branch is

\mathcal{B}\big(\nu_{1}(F_{k}(\ell_{0}),\alpha_{k,r},F_{k}(\ell_{0}+2r))\big)=\mathcal{B}\big(\alpha_{k,r},F_{k}(\ell_{0}+2r),F_{k}(\ell_{0}+4r)\big).

(2)

For each odd

, the triple

\nu_{3}(F_{k}(\ell_{0}),\alpha_{k,r},F_{k}(\ell_{0}+2r))
is minimal. The corresponding branch is

\mathcal{B}\big(\nu_{1}(F_{k}(\ell_{0}),\alpha_{k,r},F_{k}(\ell_{0}+2r))\big)=\mathcal{B}\big(\alpha_{k,r},F_{k}(\ell_{0}+2r),F_{k}(\ell_{0}+4r)\big).

Finally, we prove that these principal branches exhaust all
possible infinite paths with at least two
k
-Fibonacci components.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
m>0
and
k\geq 1
. Every infinite path of Markoff

-triples
with at least two

-Fibonacci components is contained in a principal
(2,k)
-Fibonacci branch.

2.
Uniqueness of non-minimal Markoff
m
-triples
(a,b,c)
with fixed
b,c

In this section, we prove a general uniqueness statement for non-minimal
Markoff
m
-triples when the last two components are fixed.
This auxiliary result will be used in Section
4
.

Let
(a,b,c)
be a Markoff
m
-triple that is not minimal, meaning
c<3ab
. We define
a_{q}=\frac{c}{3b}
. Let
a_{p}
be the smaller solution in
x
of the equation
\mathfrak{m}(x,b,c)=0,

with
b
and
c
fixed.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that
a_{q}=\frac{c}{3b}
provides the threshold between minimal and non-minimal
Markoff
m
-triples with fixed last two components. Indeed, a Markoff
m
-triple

(x,b,c)
is minimal if and only if
c\geq 3xb
, which is equivalent to
x\leq a_{q}
. Moreover, it is easy to show that
a_{q}<c
and
a_{p}\leq c
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The inequality
a_{q}<a_{p}
holds.

Proof.

The function
\mathfrak{m}(x,b,c)
is a quadratic in
x
with its vertex at
x=\frac{3}{2}bc
. Since
\frac{3}{2}bc>c
, the vertex lies to the right of
c
, so the function is strictly decreasing for
x\leq c
. We note that both
a_{q}
and
a_{p}
are smaller than or equal to
c
and, consequently, in order to prove that
a_{q}<a_{p}
, it suffices to show:

\mathfrak{m}(a_{q},b,c)>\mathfrak{m}(a_{p},b,c)=0.

We compute

\mathfrak{m}\!\left(\frac{c}{3b},b,c\right)=\left(\frac{c}{3b}\right)^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}-3\cdot\frac{c}{3b}\cdot b\cdot c=\left(\frac{c}{3b}\right)^{2}+b^{2}.

This expression is clearly positive, hence

\mathfrak{m}(a_{q},b,c)>0=\mathfrak{m}(a_{p},b,c),

which implies
a_{q}<a_{p}
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

If
(a,b,c)
is a non-minimal Markoff
m
-triple, then

a_{p}-a_{q}<\frac{b}{c}.

Proof.

If
a_{q}+\frac{b}{c}\geq c
, then the inequality
a_{p}-a_{q}<\frac{b}{c}
holds trivially, since
a_{p}\leq c
and
a_{q}<c
. Otherwise, by a similar argument to that of Lemma
2.2
, it suffices to show that

\mathfrak{m}\!\left(a_{q}+\frac{b}{c},\,b,\,c\right)<\mathfrak{m}(a_{p},b,c)=0.

Indeed, we compute:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}\!\left(a_{q}+\frac{b}{c},b,c\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{c}{3b}+\frac{b}{c}\right)^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}-3bc\left(\frac{c}{3b}+\frac{b}{c}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{c}{3b}\right)^{2}+\frac{b^{2}}{c^{2}}+\frac{2}{3}+b^{2}+c^{2}-3bc\left(\frac{c}{3b}+\frac{b}{c}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{c}{3b}\right)^{2}+\frac{b^{2}}{c^{2}}+\frac{2}{3}-2b^{2}.

Since
(a,b,c)
is non-minimal and ordered, then
a>\frac{c}{3b}
and
b\leq c
, so that

\mathfrak{m}\!\left(a_{q}+\frac{b}{c},b,c\right)<a^{2}+\frac{5}{3}-2b^{2}<0,

where the last inequality always holds except when
a=b=1
. For the triple
(1,1,c)
to be non-minimal, it follows that
c<3ab=3
. We check both cases,
c=1
and
c=2
:

•

If
(a,b,c)=(1,1,1)
, then

a_{q}=\dfrac{1}{3}
,
a_{p}
is the left root of
x^{2}-3x+2=(x-1)(x-2)
, hence

a_{p}=1
.
Therefore
a_{p}-a_{q}=\dfrac{2}{3}<\dfrac{b}{c}=1
.

•

If
(a,b,c)=(1,1,2)
, then

a_{q}=\dfrac{2}{3}
,
a_{p}
is the left root of
x^{2}-6x+5=(x-1)(x-5)
, hence

a_{p}=1
.
Therefore
a_{p}-a_{q}=\dfrac{1}{3}<\dfrac{b}{c}=\dfrac{1}{2}
.

∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
a_{q}=\frac{c}{3b}
, and let
a_{p}
be the left root of the parabola
\mathfrak{m}(x,b,c)=0
. Then, for any fixed pair
(b,c)
of positive integers, with
b\leq c
, the open interval
(a_{q},a_{p})
contains at most one integer
a^{*}
. If such an integer exists, then
(a^{*},b,c)
is the unique ordered non-minimal Markoff
m
-triple with
m>0
and second and third entries equal to
b
and
c
, respectively.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, the interval
(a_{q},a_{p})
is non-empty. Since we are assuming that
b\leq c
, Proposition
2.3
yields that
a_{p}-a_{q}<1
and thus the interval
(a_{q},a_{p})
contains at most one integer.

Moreover, since
\mathfrak{m}(x,b,c)
is strictly decreasing on this interval, any integer
a^{*}\in(a_{q},a_{p})
satisfies

\mathfrak{m}(a^{*},b,c)>\mathfrak{m}(a_{p},b,c)=0,

and hence defines a non-minimal Markoff
m
-triple.
∎

k
-Fibonacci and
k

In this section, we include several useful results related to the
k
-Fibonacci and
k
-Lucas numbers. Many of these have been previously proved in the literature, and we will refer the reader to the relevant sources when needed.

Throughout this paper, we denote

\phi_{k}=\frac{k+\sqrt{k^{2}+4}}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\phi}_{k}=\frac{k-\sqrt{k^{2}+4}}{2},

so that for each
n\geq 0
, the
n
-th
k
-Fibonacci number can be written through Binet’s formula as

F_{k}(n)=\frac{\phi_{k}^{\,n}-\overline{\phi}_{k}^{\,n}}{\sqrt{k^{2}+4}}\,.

(3.1)

Since
|\overline{\phi}_{k}|<1
, it follows immediately from (
3.1
) that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\phi_{k}^{n}}{D_{k}F_{k}(n)}=1,

(3.2)

where, for convenience, we write

D_{k}=\sqrt{k^{2}+4}.

The following lemma and corollary are proved in
[
2
, Lemma 2.1 and Corollary 2.2]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Generalization of Vajda’s Identity for
k
-Fibonacci numbers)
.

For any integers
a,b
,
k
and
n
, the following identity holds:

F_{k}(n+a)F_{k}(n+b)-F_{k}(n)F_{k}(n+a+b)=(-1)^{n}F_{k}(a)F_{k}(b).

Corollary 3.2
.

The following identities hold for any positive integers
a,b,n:

\displaystyle F_{k}(a+b)=F_{k}(a+1)F_{k}(b)+F_{k}(a)F_{k}(b-1),\,

(3.3)

\displaystyle F_{k}(a)\leq\frac{1}{k}F_{k}(a+1),\,

(3.4)

\displaystyle F_{k}(a)F_{k}(b)\leq F_{k}(a+b-1),\,

(3.5)

\displaystyle F_{k}(a+b-1)\leq F_{k}(a)F_{k}(b)\left(1+\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right),\,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\text{(D’Ocagne; $b\geq a$)}\ \,\,(-1)^{a}F_{k}(b-a)=F_{k}(b)F_{k}(a+1)-F_{k}(b+1)F_{k}(a),\,

(3.7)

\displaystyle(\text{Catalan})\,\,F_{k}(n)^{2}=F_{k}(n+r)F_{k}(n-r)+(-1)^{n-r}F_{k}(r)^{2},\,

(3.8)

\displaystyle(\text{Simson})\,\,F_{k}(n)^{2}=F_{k}(n+1)F_{k}(n-1)-(-1)^{n}\,.

(3.9)

Moreover, equality holds in the following cases:

(1)

The equality in (
3.4
) is only attained if
a=1
.

(2)

The equality in (
3.5
) is only attained if either
a=1
or
b=1
.

(3)

The equality in (
3.6
) is only attained if
a=b=2
.

The following lemma provides a lower bound for the product of two

-Fibonacci numbers, depending on the value of

, and was proved in

and

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
1\leq a\leq b\leq c
be integers. Then

(i)

Case
k=1
.

(a)

If
a,b\geq 2
, then
F_{1}(c)\leq 3\,F_{1}(a)F_{1}(b)\ \text{if and only if}\ c\leq a+b,\\
F_{1}(c)>3\,F_{1}(a)F_{1}(b)\ \text{if and only if}\ c\geq a+b+1,

and equality holds if and only if

.

(b)

F_{1}(c)\leq 3\,F_{1}(1)F_{1}(1)\ \text{if and only if}\ c\leq 4,\\
F_{1}(c)>3\,F_{1}(1)F_{1}(1)\ \text{if and only if}\ c\geq 5,

and equality holds if and only if
c=4
.

(c)

If
a=1
with

, then

F_{1}(c)\leq 3\,F_{1}(1)F_{1}(b)\ \text{if and only if}\ c\leq a+b+1;\,\,F_{1}(c)>3\,F_{1}(1)F_{1}(b)\ \text{if and only if}\ c\geq a+b+2,

(ii)

Case

.

.

(iii)

Case

.
One has

and equality occurs only at

As remarked after (

), the

-Fibonacci numbers form a Lucas-type sequence.
We will also make use of its companion, the

-Lucas sequence
(see, e.g.,

\left\{\begin{aligned} L_{k}(0)&=2,\\

(3.10)

For us, it will be relevant to study when

and

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
F_{k}(\ell)
and

be the

•

If

\begin{cases}3\mid F_{k}(\ell)\text{ if and only if }4\mid\ell,\\

•

If

\begin{cases}3\mid F_{k}(\ell)\text{ if and only if }2\mid\ell,\\

Proof.

The statement for the

-Fibonacci numbers is an immediate consequence of general results on linear recurrence sequences modulo primes; see, for instance,

Indeed, applying that result with

and

\alpha(3)=4

.
The corresponding assertions for the

∎

4.
Principal
(2,k)
-Fibonacci branches

4.1.
Existence and positivity of
m

In this subsection, we study principal
(2,k)
-Fibonacci branches arising from Markoff
m
-triples of the form

\left(a,F_{k}(n-r),F_{k}(n+r)\right).

Our aim is to determine when such triples exist and when the corresponding Markoff parameter
m
is positive. For clarity, we distinguish between the cases
3\mid k
and
3\nmid k
.

4.1.1.
The case where
3\mid k

In this case, we show that no such configurations can occur.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
k
and
m
be positive integers with
3\mid k
and
m>0
. Then there are no branches of
m
-Markoff triples of the form
(a,F_{k}(n-r),F_{k}(n+r))
.

Proof.

Suppose
(a,F_{k}(n-r),F_{k}(n+r))
is a Markoff
m
-triple. Then it satisfies the identity

m=a^{2}+F_{k}(n-r)^{2}+F_{k}(n+r)^{2}-3aF_{k}(n-r)F_{k}(n+r).

By using Binet’s formula (
3.1
), we compute

\displaystyle F_{k}(n\pm r)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k^{2}+4}}\right)^{2}\left(\phi_{k}^{n\pm r}-\overline{\phi}_{k}^{\,\,n\pm r}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{2}+4}\left(\phi_{k}^{2n\pm 2r}+\overline{\phi}_{k}^{\,\,2n\pm 2r}-2(-1)^{n\pm r}\right),

\displaystyle F_{k}(n-r)F_{k}(n+r)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k^{2}+4}}\right)^{2}\left(\phi_{k}^{\,n-r}-\overline{\phi}_{k}^{\,\,n-r}\right)\left(\phi_{k}^{n+r}-\overline{\phi}_{k}^{\,\,n+r}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{2}+4}\left(\phi_{k}^{2n}+\overline{\phi}_{k}^{\,\,2n}-(-1)^{n-r}(\phi_{k}^{2r}+\overline{\phi}_{k}^{\,\,2r})\right).

Substituting these expressions in the Markoff identity, and multiplying through by
(k^{2}+4)
, we obtain

\displaystyle(k^{2}+4)m

\displaystyle=(k^{2}+4)a^{2}+\phi_{k}^{2n-2r}+\overline{\phi}_{k}^{\,\,2n-2r}+\phi_{k}^{2n+2r}+\overline{\phi}_{k}^{\,\,2n+2r}

\displaystyle\quad-2(-1)^{n-r}-2(-1)^{n+r}-3a\left(\phi_{k}^{2n}+\overline{\phi}_{k}^{\,\,2n}-(-1)^{n-r}(\phi_{k}^{2r}+\overline{\phi}_{k}^{\,\,2r})\right).

The right-hand side of this expression contains exponential terms in
\phi_{k}^{2n}
and
\overline{\phi}_{k}^{\,\,2n}
, which grow unbounded as
n\to\infty
unless their coefficients vanish. Since we are looking for solutions in a branch, there have to be infinitely many, and since
(k^{2}+4)m
is bounded, it follows that the coefficients of
\phi_{k}^{2n}
and
\overline{\phi}_{k}^{\,\,2n}
must both vanish. In other words,

\phi_{k}^{2r}+\frac{1}{\phi_{k}^{2r}}-3a=0\Longrightarrow\quad a=\frac{1}{3}\left(\phi_{k}^{2r}+\phi_{k}^{-2r}\right).

Using Binet’s formula again, we obtain:

a=\frac{1}{3}\left(\phi_{k}^{2r}+\phi_{k}^{-2r}\right)=\frac{1}{3}\left((k^{2}+4)F_{k}(r)^{2}+2(-1)^{r}\right).

Since
a
is an integer, the numerator must be divisible by 3. Therefore, it follows that

(k^{2}+4)F_{k}(r)^{2}+2(-1)^{r}\equiv 0\pmod{3}.

As we are assuming
k\equiv 0\pmod{3}
, it follows that
k^{2}+4\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, and hence the previous identity reduces to

F_{k}(r)^{2}\equiv(-1)^{r}\pmod{3}.

(4.1)

If
3\mid k
, Lemma
3.4
implies that
F_{k}(r)\equiv 0\pmod{3}
if
2\mid r
and
F_{k}(r)^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
if
2\nmid r
. This contradicts (
4.1
).

Therefore, in all cases, the condition for boundedness fails. Consequently, no such triple
(a,F_{k}(n-r),F_{k}(n+r))
can form a branch of a Markoff
m
-triple when
k\equiv 0\pmod{3}
.
∎

\alpha_{k,r}=\frac{F_{k}(4r)}{3F_{k}(2r)}=\frac{L_{k}(2r)}{3}.

We begin by applying identity (

\displaystyle F_{k}(2r+1)

Proposition 4.4

Let

\mathfrak{m}(\alpha_{k,r},F_{k}(n-r),F_{k}(n+r))=\alpha_{k,r}^{2}+(-1)^{n-r}F_{k}(r)^{2}(F_{k}(r+1)+F_{k}(r-1))^{2}.

F_{k}(n)^{2}-F_{k}(n-r)\,F_{k}(n+r)=(-1)^{\,n-r}\,F_{k}(r)^{2}.

(4.5)

We add the identities (

) and (

) (with

and

and

and

(F_{k}(r+1)+F_{k}(r-1))\,F_{k}(n)=F_{k}(n+r)+(-1)^{r}F_{k}(n-r).

(4.6)

Next, multiplying (

\bigl(F_{k}(r+1)+F_{k}(r-1)\bigr)^{2}
and substituting

using (

Proposition 4.5
.

Let

be odd and

\mathfrak{m}\left(\alpha_{k,r},F_{k}(n-r),F_{k}(n+r)\right)>0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad k\geq 3,

\mathfrak{m}\left(\alpha_{k,r},F_{k}(n-r),F_{k}(n+r)\right)=0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad 1\leq k\leq 2\text{ and }r=1.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.4
,

\mathfrak{m}\left(\alpha_{k,r},F_{k}(n-r),F_{k}(n+r)\right)=\alpha_{k,r}^{2}-F_{k}(r)^{2}\bigl(F_{k}(r+1)+F_{k}(r-1)\bigr)^{2}.

Since

), the inequality

\alpha_{k,r}>F_{k}(r)\bigl(F_{k}(r+1)+F_{k}(r-1)\bigr).

As

, equation (

) gives

\alpha_{k,r}=\frac{\bigl(F_{k}(r+1)+F_{k}(r-1)\bigr)^{2}+2}{3},

F_{k}(r+1)+F_{k}(r-1)+\frac{2}{F_{k}(r+1)+F_{k}(r-1)}>3F_{k}(r).

This holds for

by

), and the extra fraction is positive.
For

and

and

F_{k}(r+1)+F_{k}(r-1)+\frac{2}{F_{k}(r+1)+F_{k}(r-1)}\leq 3F_{k}(r)-1+\frac{1}{2}<3F_{k}(r),

so the inequality fails. Therefore

\mathfrak{m}(\alpha_{k,r},F_{k}(n-r),F_{k}(n+r))>0

.

Finally, by Proposition
4.4
again,

\mathfrak{m}\left(\alpha_{k,r},F_{k}(n-r),F_{k}(n+r)\right)=0

\alpha_{k,r}=F_{k}(r)\bigl(F_{k}(r+1)+F_{k}(r-1)\bigr).

By Lemma
4.3
we also have
\alpha_{k,r}=F_{k}(4r)/(3F_{k}(2r))
, hence
the previous equality is equivalent to

F_{k}(4r)=3F_{k}(2r)\,F_{k}(r)\bigl(F_{k}(r+1)+F_{k}(r-1)\bigr).

Using (

) with
(a,b)=(2r,2r)
gives

F_{k}(4r)=F_{k}(2r)\bigl(F_{k}(2r+1)+F_{k}(2r-1)\bigr)
, and using (

) with

(a,b)=(r,r)
and
(a,b)=(r-1,r)
gives

F_{k}(2r)=F_{k}(r)\bigl(F_{k}(r+1)+F_{k}(r-1)\bigr)
. Cancelling the common factor

, we get the equivalent condition

F_{k}(2r+1)+F_{k}(2r-1)=3F_{k}(2r).

With the recurrence
F_{k}(2r+1)=kF_{k}(2r)+F_{k}(2r-1)

(3-k)F_{k}(2r)=2F_{k}(2r-1),

which forces
k\leq 2
, and for

it forces

because

F_{k}(2r)=kF_{k}(2r-1)+F_{k}(2r-2)>2F_{k}(2r-1)

Conversely, if

\mathfrak{m}(\alpha_{k,1},F_{k}(n-1),F_{k}(n+1))=0

∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose that

∎

.

If

4.2.1.

We now show that the right-hand side is always strictly smaller than

Since

[

\frac{F_{1}(n)}{F_{1}(n+\ell)}\leq\frac{1}{4}.

First suppose that

Now suppose that

. Then again by
[

,

\frac{F_{1}(n-\ell)}{F_{1}(n)}\leq\frac{1}{4}.

Hence

\frac{F_{1}(n-\ell)}{3F_{1}(n)}\leq\frac{1}{12}.

Since

\frac{F_{1}(n)}{F_{1}(n+\ell)}<\frac{1}{4},

and thus

We conclude that

On the other hand, when

Lemma 4.11
.

There is no ordered non-minimal Markoff
m
-triple (
m>0
) of the form

\bigl(a,\,F_{1}(n),\,F_{1}(n+1)\bigr).

Proof.

Assume that such a triple exists and write
x:=F_{1}(n)
and
y:=F_{1}(n+1)
, so
y\leq 2x
and
y\geq x\geq 1
.
From the Markoff equation
a^{2}+x^{2}+y^{2}=3axy+m
, taking the smaller root in
a
(as the triple is ordered), we obtain

a=\frac{3xy-\sqrt{9x^{2}y^{2}-4x^{2}-4y^{2}+4m}}{2}.

Since we assume that
m>0
, the discriminant of the previous quadratic increases with
m
, so the expression for
a
decreases. Therefore, we define

A(x,y):=\frac{3xy-\sqrt{9x^{2}y^{2}-4x^{2}-4y^{2}}}{2},

and conclude that

a<A(x,y).

We claim that
A(x,y)\leq 1
, for all consecutive Fibonacci numbers.
Indeed,
A(x,y)\leq 1
if and only if

3xy-2\leq\sqrt{9x^{2}y^{2}-4x^{2}-4y^{2}}

and squaring (both sides are positive since
x,y\geq 1
) and manipulating the equations gives that

x^{2}+y^{2}-3xy+1\leq 0.

Since
x\geq 1
and
x\leq y\leq 2x
, then

x^{2}+y^{2}-3xy+1=(x-y)^{2}-xy+1\leq x^{2}-xy+1\leq 0.

The left-hand side is non-positive, thereby proving the claim.

Thus
a<A(x,y)\leq 1
, a contradiction.

∎

Lemma 4.12
.

There are no ordered non-minimal Markoff

-triples
(a,F_{1}(n),F_{1}(n+\ell))

Assume that such a triple exists. For

This implies, applying the argument of the proof of Lemma

4.2.2.
The general case

With the preparatory results established, we are now in a position to state the main theorem of this section, which provides a complete characterisation of ordered non-minimal triples whose last two components are
k
-Fibonacci numbers.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
r
be odd and
k\geq 1
. Consider the triple

\left(\frac{F_{k}(4r)}{3F_{k}(2r)},\,F_{k}(N-r),\,F_{k}(N+r)\right).

Then the following hold:

(1)

The triple is ordered if and only if

\begin{cases}N>3r,&\text{if }k>2,\\[3.0pt]
N\geq 3r,&\text{if }k\in\{1,2\}.\end{cases}

(2)

If the triple is ordered, then it is minimal if and only if
N=3r
, and non-minimal if and only if
N>3r
.

Proof.

We first determine when the triple is ordered. By monotonicity of the
k
-Fibonacci sequence,

F_{k}(N-r)\leq F_{k}(N+r),\qquad\text{for all }N>r.

Thus, it suffices to compare the first two entries. Since

\frac{F_{k}(4r)}{3F_{k}(2r)}=\frac{L_{k}(2r)}{3},
orderedness is equivalent to

L_{k}(2r)\leq 3F_{k}(N-r).

If
k>2
, this inequality holds for all
N>3r
, while it fails at
N=3r
, since then it becomes

L_{k}(2r)\leq 3F_{k}(2r),

which is false for all
r\geq 1
. Indeed, for
r=1
,

L_{k}(2)=k^{2}+2>3k=3F_{k}(2),\qquad(k>2),

and the inequality
L_{k}(2r)>3F_{k}(2r)

is preserved by the recurrence relations (
1.2
) and (
3.10
). If
k\in\{1,2\}
, the inequality already holds at
N=3r
, since

L_{k}(2r)\leq 3F_{k}(2r)\qquad\text{for all }r\geq 1.

Therefore, the triple is ordered if and only if

\begin{cases}N>3r,&\text{if }k>2,\\[3.0pt]
N\geq 3r,&\text{if }k\in\{1,2\}.\end{cases}

Assume now that

(x,y,z)=\left(\frac{F_{k}(4r)}{3F_{k}(2r)},\,F_{k}(N-r),\,F_{k}(N+r)\right)

is ordered. Since minimality is equivalent to
z\geq 3xy
, and

3xy=\frac{F_{k}(4r)}{F_{k}(2r)}\,F_{k}(N-r),
the triple is minimal if and only if

F_{k}(N+r)\geq\frac{F_{k}(4r)}{F_{k}(2r)}\,F_{k}(N-r).

Using Lemma
3.5
with
a=b=2r
, namely

F_{k}(4r)=F_{k}(2r)L_{k}(2r),

this is equivalent to

F_{k}(N+r)\geq L_{k}(2r)\,F_{k}(N-r).

Using
L_{k}(2r)=F_{k}(2r+1)+F_{k}(2r-1)
and the addition formula (
3.3
), we get

\displaystyle L_{k}(2r)F_{k}(N-r)-F_{k}(N+r)

\displaystyle=F_{k}(2r-1)F_{k}(N-r)-F_{k}(2r)F_{k}(N-r-1).

Therefore, the sign is determined by

\frac{F_{k}(2r-1)}{F_{k}(2r)}-\frac{F_{k}(N-r-1)}{F_{k}(N-r)}.

Since the sequence
\frac{F_{k}(2n-1)}{F_{k}(2n)}
is strictly decreasing in
n
and converges to
\frac{1}{\phi_{k}}
, whereas the sequence
\frac{F_{k}(2n)}{F_{k}(2n+1)}
is strictly increasing in
n
and also converges to
\frac{1}{\phi_{k}}
, it follows that, if
N
is odd and
N-r>2r
, then

\frac{F_{k}(N-r-1)}{F_{k}(N-r)}<\frac{F_{k}(2r-1)}{F_{k}(2r)},

so the difference is positive. If
N
is even, then

\frac{F_{k}(2r-1)}{F_{k}(2r)}-\frac{F_{k}(N-r-1)}{F_{k}(N-r)}>\frac{1}{\phi_{k}}-\frac{1}{\phi_{k}}=0.

Therefore,

\frac{F_{k}(2r-1)}{F_{k}(2r)}-\frac{F_{k}(N-r-1)}{F_{k}(N-r)}\begin{cases}=0,&\text{if }N-r=2r,\\[3.0pt]
>0,&\text{if }N-r>2r.\end{cases}

Hence,

L_{k}(2r)F_{k}(N-r)-F_{k}(N+r)\begin{cases}=0,&\text{if }N=3r,\\[3.0pt]
>0,&\text{if }N>3r.\end{cases}

Equivalently,

F_{k}(N+r)\begin{cases}=3xy,&\text{if }N=3r,\\[3.0pt]
<3xy,&\text{if }N>3r.\end{cases}

Hence, the ordered triple is minimal if and only if
N=3r
, and non-minimal if and only if
N>3r
.
∎

n\geq 1,
we have

\nu_{3}(a_{n+1},b_{n+1},c_{n+1})=(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n}).

(6.1)

We say that an infinite path is
(2,k)
-Fibonacci if each of its triples contains
at least two
k
-Fibonacci components. Finally, recall that a
principal

(2,k)
-Fibonacci branch is a branch of the form

\mathcal{B}\bigl(\frac{F_{k}(4r)}{3F_{k}(2r)},\,F_{k}(N-r),\,F_{k}(N+r)\bigr),

where
\bigl(\frac{F_{k}(4r)}{3F_{k}(2r)},F_{k}(N-r),F_{k}(N+r)\bigr)
is an ordered
Markoff
m
-triple with
m>0
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\{(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n})\}
be an infinite
(2,k)
-Fibonacci path which is not contained
in any principal
(2,k)
-Fibonacci branch. Then it contains only finitely many triples
for which
a_{n}
is not a
k
-Fibonacci number, and only finitely many triples having
three
k
-Fibonacci components.

Proof.

If
a_{n}
is not a
k
-Fibonacci number for some
n
, then by Theorem
1.1
the triple must be of the form
(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n})=\left(\frac{F_{k}(4r)}{3F_{k}(2r)},F_{k}(N-r),F_{k}(N+r)\right)
.

From the discussion in Section
5
, we have generically

(a_{n-1},b_{n-1},c_{n-1})=\nu_{3}(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n})=\left(\frac{F_{k}(4r)}{3F_{k}(2r)},F_{k}(N-3r),F_{k}(N-r)\right)

except maybe for
n=2
, where the expressions for
a_{1}
and
b_{1}
might be switched in the minimal element
(a_{1},b_{1},c_{1})
.

This implies that if
a_{n}
is not
k
-Fibonacci for some
n
, then
a_{j}
is also not
k
-Fibonacci for all
j
with
1<j\leq n
. As a consequence, if there is an infinite number of
n
such that
a_{n}
is not
k
-Fibonacci, then
a_{n}
is not
k
-Fibonacci for every
n>1
, and the whole path
would eventually coincide with a principal
(2,k)
-Fibonacci branch, contrary to
our assumption.

Finally, by
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
, if the path contains an infinite number of non-minimal triple
with three
k
-Fibonacci components, then it must be of the form

(F_{2}(2)=2,F_{2}(2n),F_{2}(2n+2)),

which is also of the form given by Theorem
1.1
, taking
r=1
and
N=n+1
. Thus, repeating the previous argument, we can see that it only contains a finite number of these triples. As the path, by construction, can have at most one minimal triple, the result follows.
∎

Using the previous lemma, we can explicitly describe the structure of an infinite

(2,k)
-Fibonacci path which is not contained in any principal branch.
Let
\{(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n})\}
be such a path. By Lemma
6.1
, after removing
finitely many initial triples if necessary, we may assume without loss of generality
that
a_{n}
is always a
k
-Fibonacci number, that for every
n
at least one of

b_{n}
or
c_{n}
is not a
k
-Fibonacci number, and that all triples are non-minimal.

First observe that if
c_{n}
is not a
k
-Fibonacci number for some
n
, then both
possible children of
(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n})
have
c_{n}
as their second entry, so

(a_{n+1},b_{n+1},c_{n+1})
has a non-
k
-Fibonacci component in

b_{n+1}=c_{n}
. Thus the path contains infinitely many triples for which
b_{n}

is not a
k
-Fibonacci number.

Suppose now that
b_{n}
is not a
k
-Fibonacci number for some
n
. Then, by
construction,
\nu_{1}(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n})
has
b_{n}
as its smallest entry. Since

a_{n+1}
is a
k
-Fibonacci number, it follows that

(a_{n+1},b_{n+1},c_{n+1})\neq\nu_{1}(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n}).

Therefore,

(a_{n+1},b_{n+1},c_{n+1})=\nu_{2}(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n})=(a_{n},c_{n},3a_{n}c_{n}-b_{n}),

and, since
a_{n}
and
c_{n}
are
k
-Fibonacci numbers, the entry
c_{n+1}
is not
a
k
-Fibonacci number. In other words, the path must eventually alternate between
a triple
(a,b,c)
where
b
is not a
k
-Fibonacci number and a triple

(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})=\nu_{2}(a,b,c)
where
c^{\prime}
is not a
k
-Fibonacci number.

Therefore, after shifting the initial point of the path and reindexing if necessary, we may assume without loss of generality that such a path has the form

\displaystyle(a_{2n},b_{2n},c_{2n})

\displaystyle=(F_{k}(u_{n}),x_{n},F_{k}(v_{n})),

(6.2)

\displaystyle(a_{2n+1},b_{2n+1},c_{2n+1})

\displaystyle=(F_{k}(u_{n}),F_{k}(v_{n}),x_{n+1}),

where

x_{n+1}=3F_{k}(u_{n})F_{k}(v_{n})-x_{n}.

Moreover, the next even-indexed triple is given by either

(a_{2n+2},b_{2n+2},c_{2n+2})=\nu_{1}(a_{2n+1},b_{2n+1},c_{2n+1})=(F_{k}(u_{n+1}),x_{n+1},F_{k}(v_{n+1})),

with
u_{n+1}=v_{n}
, or

(a_{2n+2},b_{2n+2},c_{2n+2})=\nu_{2}(a_{2n+1},b_{2n+1},c_{2n+1})=(F_{k}(u_{n+1}),x_{n+1},F_{k}(v_{n+1})),

with
u_{n+1}=u_{n}
.

Let us now find some lower and upper bounds for the non-
k
-Fibonacci terms
x_{n}
in one such sequence.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\{(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n})\}_{n\geq 0}
be a path of the form (
6.2
)
which is not contained in any principal
(2,k)
-Fibonacci branch. Then
\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is non-decreasing, whereas

\{x_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\{v_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
are increasing. Moreover, for all sufficiently
large
n
, the following hold:

(1)

If
k=1
or
k=2
, then
x_{n}\in\bigl(F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n}-1),\,F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n})\bigr).

(2)

If
k=1
or
k=2
, then

x_{n+1}\in\bigl(F_{k}(u_{n}+v_{n}),\,F_{k}(u_{n}+v_{n}+1)\bigr).

(3)

If
k\geq 3
, then

x_{n}\in\bigl(F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n}),\,F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n}+1)\bigr).

(4)

If
k\geq 3
, then

x_{n+1}\in\bigl(F_{k}(u_{n}+v_{n}-1),\,F_{k}(u_{n}+v_{n})\bigr).

In particular, all the intervals above are open, since

x_{n}
and
x_{n+1}
are not
k
-Fibonacci numbers.

Proof.

a_{n}
is not decreasing and
b_{n}
and
c_{n}
are increasing due to the construction using repeated application of the Vieta transformations
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
, and, thus, so are the indices
u_{n}
,
x_{n}
and
v_{n}
.

Suppose first that
k=1
or
k=2
. By Lemma
3.3
, we know that for sufficiently large
n
it is impossible that
x_{n}<F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n}-1)
since this would imply that
c_{2n}=F_{k}(v_{n})>3F_{k}(u_{n})F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n}-1)>3a_{2n}b_{2n}
, contradicting that the triple is non-minimal. Thus, we have that
x_{n}>F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n}-1)
.

On the other hand, by
[
1
, Proposition 3.1]
in the case
k=1
or by
[
2
, Lemma 3.1.(1) and Lemma 3.2]
in the case
k=2
we have that
\mathfrak{m}(F_{k}(u_{n}),F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n}),F_{k}(v_{n}))\leq 0
. As
\mathfrak{m}(x,y,z)
is monotonically decreasing in
y
and
\mathfrak{m}(F_{k}(u_{n}),x_{n},F_{k}(v_{n}))=m>0
, this implies that
x_{n}<F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n})
.

We can now apply a similar argument for
(a_{2n+1},b_{2n+1},c_{2n+1})=(F_{k}(u_{n}),F_{k}(v_{n}),x_{n+1})
. By Lemma
3.3
, it is impossible that
x_{n+1}>F_{k}(u_{n}+v_{n}+1)
since this would imply that
x_{n+1}>3F_{k}(u_{n})F_{k}(v_{n})
and the triple would be non-minimal. Consequently, we have that
x_{n+1}<F_{k}(u_{n}+v_{n}+1)
. Similarly, by
[
1
, Proposition 3.1]
and
[
2
, Lemma 3.1.(1) and Lemma 3.2]
, we have that
\mathfrak{m}(F_{k}(u_{n}),F_{k}(v_{n}),F_{k}(u_{n}+v_{n}))<0
for sufficiently large
n
and
\mathfrak{m}(F_{k}(u_{n}),F_{k}(v_{n}),x_{n+1})=m>0
, and since
\mathfrak{m}(x,y,z)
is monotonically decreasing in
z
, we have
x_{n+1}>F_{k}(u_{n}+v_{n})
.

Analogously, for
k\geq 3
, non-minimality of the triple
(F_{k}(u_{n}),x_{n},F_{k}(v_{n}))
and Lemma
3.3
(iii) imply that
x_{n}>F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n})
and
x_{n+1}<F_{k}(v_{n}+u_{n}+1)
and
[
2
, Lemma 3.1.(2)]
implies that
\mathfrak{m}(F_{k}(u_{n}),F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n}+1),F_{k}(v_{n}))\leq 0
and
\mathfrak{m}(F_{k}(u_{n}),F_{k}(v_{n}),F_{k}(u_{n}+v_{n}))<0
, so
x_{n}<F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n}+1)
and
x_{n+1}>F_{k}(u_{n}+v_{n})
.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

For a path of the form (
6.2
) which is not contained in any principal
(2,k)
-Fibonacci branch, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}(v_{n}-u_{n})=\infty
.

Proof.

Assume first that
k=1
or
k=2
. By Lemma
6.2
for all
n
sufficiently large we have that

x_{n+1}\in(F_{k}(u_{n}+v_{n}),F_{k}(u_{n}+v_{n}+1)),

and applying
6.2
(
1
) for the index
n+1,
we also have

x_{n+1}\in(F_{k}(v_{n+1}-u_{n+1}-1),F_{k}(v_{n+1}-u_{n+1})),

and, since both are intervals of consecutive
k-
Fibonacci numbers, we must have that

v_{n+1}-u_{n+1}-1=u_{n}+v_{n},

(6.3)

but then

v_{n+1}-u_{n+1}=u_{n}+v_{n}+1>v_{n}-u_{n}

for each sufficiently large
n
. Thus,
\lim_{n\to\infty}(v_{n}-u_{n})=\infty
. Analogously, if
k\geq 3
, by Lemma
6.2
(
4
),

x_{n+1}\in(F_{k}(u_{n}+v_{n}-1),F_{k}(u_{n}+v_{n})),

and by Lemma
6.2
(
3
) applied to the index
n+1
,

x_{n+1}\in(F_{k}(v_{n+1}-u_{n+1}),F_{k}(v_{n+1}-u_{n+1}+1)),

and, by the same reasoning,

v_{n+1}-u_{n+1}=u_{n}+v_{n}-1>v_{n}-u_{n}.

(6.4)

Hence
\lim_{n\to\infty}(v_{n}-u_{n})=\infty
.

∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Recall that
D_{k}=\sqrt{k^{2}+4}
. Assume that
k=1
or
k=2
. Then for any path of the form (
6.2
) as above, there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
N>0
such that one of the following holds:

i)

x_{n}\in\left(\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}-u_{n}-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon},F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n})\right)\,,\quad\forall n\geq N
.

ii)

x_{n}\in\left(F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n}-1),\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}-u_{n}-\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}\right)\,,\quad\forall n\geq N.

Analogously, if
k\geq 3
there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
N>0
such that one of the following holds:

iii)

x_{n}\in\left(\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}-u_{n}+\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon},F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n}+1)\right)\,,\quad\forall n\geq N
.

iv)

x_{n}\in\left(F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n}),\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}-u_{n}+\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}\right)\,,\quad\forall n\geq N
.

Proof.

Let us denote
A=\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}
. Since
u_{n}
is non-decreasing by Lemma
6.2
, then
u_{n}
is either eventually constant and equal to some integer
A>0
or it diverges and
A=\infty
. We will begin by showing that the limit

L_{k,A}(\Delta):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{F_{k}(v_{n})^{2}}\,\,\mathfrak{m}\!\left(F_{k}(u_{n}),\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}-u_{n}+\Delta},F_{k}(v_{n})\right)

is always finite and it only depends on
k
,
A
and
\Delta
. Expanding the expression of
\mathfrak{m}(a,b,c)
we have

\frac{1}{F_{k}(v_{n})^{2}}\,\,\mathfrak{m}\!\left(F_{k}(u_{n}),\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}-u_{n}+\Delta},F_{k}(v_{n})\right)=\frac{F_{k}(u_{n})^{2}}{F_{k}(v_{n})^{2}}+1+\frac{\phi_{k}^{2v_{n}-2u_{n}+2\Delta}}{D_{k}^{2}F_{k}(v_{n})^{2}}-3\frac{F_{k}(u_{n})\phi_{k}^{v_{n}-u_{n}+\Delta}}{D_{k}F_{k}(v_{n})}.

Now, since
v_{n}-u_{n}\to\infty
by Corollary
6.3
, together with (
3.2
), we have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{F_{k}(u_{n})^{2}}{F_{k}(v_{n})^{2}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi_{k}^{-2(v_{n}-u_{n})}=0.

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\phi_{k}^{2v_{n}-2u_{n}+2\Delta}}{D_{k}^{2}F_{k}(v_{n})^{2}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi_{k}^{2\Delta-2u_{n}}=\begin{cases}\phi_{k}^{2\Delta-2A}&A<\infty,\\
0&A=\infty.\end{cases}

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{F_{k}(u_{n})\phi_{k}^{v_{n}-u_{n}+\Delta}}{D_{k}F_{k}(v_{n})}=\begin{cases}\frac{F_{k}(A)}{\phi_{k}^{A}}\phi_{k}^{\Delta}&A<\infty,\\
\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{\Delta}&A=\infty.\end{cases}

Putting the previous computations together,

L_{k,A}(\Delta)=\begin{cases}1+\phi_{k}^{2\Delta-2A}-\frac{3F_{k}(A)}{\phi_{k}^{A}}\phi_{k}^{\Delta}&A<\infty,\\
1-\frac{3}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{\Delta}&A=\infty,\end{cases}

which is indeed always a finite real number. Now, we observe that if
\Delta=\frac{1}{2}
or
\Delta=-\frac{1}{2}
then the limit
L_{k,A}(\Delta)
can never be zero. To show this, assume the contrary. Suppose that
L_{k,A}(1/2)=0
. This is impossible if
A=\infty
, since
L_{k,\infty}(1/2)=0
would imply

\phi_{k}^{1/2}=D_{k}/3
, and hence
\phi_{k}=(k^{2}+4)/9
, contradicting the irrationality of
\phi_{k}
.

If
A<\infty
, then

1+\phi_{k}^{1-2A}-3\frac{F_{k}(A)}{\phi_{k}^{A}}\phi_{k}^{1/2}=0,

and therefore

\sqrt{\phi_{k}}=\frac{(1+\phi_{k}^{1-2A})\phi_{k}^{A}}{3F_{k}(A)}=\frac{\phi_{k}^{A}+\phi_{k}^{1-A}}{3F_{k}(A)}.

Since
A
is an integer, the right-hand side belongs to
\mathbb{Q}(\phi_{k})
. Thus

\sqrt{\phi_{k}}\in\mathbb{Q}(\phi_{k})
, so
\phi_{k}
is a square in the quadratic field

\mathbb{Q}(\phi_{k})
. However, the field norm from
\mathbb{Q}(\phi_{k})
to
\mathbb{Q}
satisfies

N_{\mathbb{Q}(\phi_{k})/\mathbb{Q}}(\phi_{k})=\phi_{k}\overline{\phi}_{k}=-1,

whereas the norm of a square is always a square in
\mathbb{Q}
. This is impossible.
Therefore
L_{k,A}(1/2)\neq 0
.

Assume first that
L_{k,A}(1/2)>0
. By continuity of
L_{k,A}(\Delta)
, there exists

\varepsilon>0
such that
L_{k,A}(1/2+\varepsilon)>0
. Since
F_{k}(v_{n})\to\infty
, it follows that

\mathfrak{m}\!\left(F_{k}(u_{n}),\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}-u_{n}+\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon},F_{k}(v_{n})\right)\to\infty.

Hence there exists
N>0,
such that

\mathfrak{m}\!\left(F_{k}(u_{n}),\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}-u_{n}+\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon},F_{k}(v_{n})\right)>0\,,\qquad\forall n\geq N.

Since
\mathfrak{m}(x,y,z)
is decreasing in
y
, we deduce that

x_{n}>\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}-u_{n}+\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\,,\qquad\forall n\geq N.

Similarly, if
L_{k,A}(1/2)<0,
then

x_{n}<\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}-u_{n}+\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}\,,\qquad\forall n\geq N.

If
k\geq 3
, by Lemma
6.2
(
3
) we know that

x_{n}\in\bigl(F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n}),\,F_{k}(v_{n}-u_{n}+1)\bigr).

Combining this with the previous inequalities, alternatives (iii) and (iv) follow.

Then, alternatives (i) and (ii) follow analogously from Lemma
6.2
(
1
), repeating the previous argument with
\Delta=-\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

Theorem 6.5

(Theorem
1.3
of the Introduction)
.

Let
m>0
and
k\geq 1
. Every infinite path of Markoff
m
-triples
with at least two
k
-Fibonacci components is contained in a principal
(2,k)
-Fibonacci branch.

Proof.

Suppose, on the contrary, that there exists an infinite
(2,k)
-Fibonacci path

\{(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n})\}_{n\geq 0}

which is not contained in any principal
(2,k)
-Fibonacci branch.
By the previous discussion, after removing finitely many initial triples if necessary,
we may assume that it has the form (
6.2
) for some sequences

u_{n}
,
x_{n}
and
v_{n}
. Suppose first that
k=1
or
k=2
. By Lemma
6.4
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
and
N>0
such that either

x_{n}>\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}-u_{n}-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\,,\quad\forall n\geq N,

or

x_{n}<\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}-u_{n}-\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}\,,\quad\forall n\geq N.

(6.5)

First, assume the former. Then, by (
6.3
), for all
n\geq N
we also have that

x_{n+1}>\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n+1}-u_{n+1}-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}+u_{n}+\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\,,\quad\forall n\geq N.

Thus,

x_{n}x_{n+1}>\frac{1}{D_{k}^{2}}\phi_{k}^{2v_{n}+2\varepsilon}.

Since
(a_{2n},b_{2n},c_{2n})=(F_{k}(u_{n}),x_{n},F_{k}(v_{n}))
and
(a_{2n+1},b_{2n+1},c_{2n+1})=(F_{k}(u_{n}),F_{k}(v_{n}),x_{n+1})
, it follows that
x_{n}
and
x_{n+1}
are the two solutions to the quadratic equation

x^{2}-3F_{k}(u_{n})F_{k}(v_{n})x+F_{k}(u_{n})^{2}+F_{k}(v_{n})^{2}-m=0,

so
x_{n}x_{n+1}=F_{k}(u_{n})^{2}+F_{k}(v_{n})^{2}-m
. This means that

1=\frac{F_{k}(u_{n})^{2}+F_{k}(v_{n})^{2}-m}{x_{n}x_{n+1}}<\frac{D_{k}^{2}(F_{k}(u_{n})^{2}+F_{k}(v_{n})^{2}-m)}{\phi_{k}^{2v_{n}}}\phi_{k}^{-2\varepsilon}\,,\quad\forall n\geq N,

but, as
\lim_{n\to\infty}(v_{n}-u_{n})=\infty
by Corollary
6.3
, together with (
3.2
),
the limit of the right hand side is
\phi_{k}^{-2\varepsilon}
. Since
\varepsilon>0
, this limit is strictly less than
1
, which is impossible. Analogously, if (
6.5
) holds, then

x_{n+1}<\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}+u_{n}+\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon},

and we would have

1=\frac{F_{k}(u_{n})^{2}+F_{k}(v_{n})^{2}-m}{x_{n}x_{n+1}}>\frac{D_{k}^{2}(F_{k}(u_{n})^{2}+F_{k}(v_{n})^{2}-m)}{\phi_{k}^{2v_{n}}}\phi_{k}^{2\varepsilon}\,,\quad\forall n\geq N,

which is impossible, since the limit of the right-hand side is
\phi_{k}^{2\varepsilon}>1
,
again by Corollary
6.3
and (
3.2
). The result is completely analogous if
k\geq 3
. In this case, either

x_{n}>\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}-u_{n}+\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\,,\quad\forall n\geq N,

or

x_{n}<\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}-u_{n}+\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}\,,\quad\forall n\geq N,

and using (
6.4
) we have respectively

x_{n+1}>\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}+u_{n}-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\,,\quad\forall n\geq N,

or

x_{n+1}<\frac{1}{D_{k}}\phi_{k}^{v_{n}+u_{n}-\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}\,,\quad\forall n\geq N.

Thus we have
x_{n}x_{n+1}>\frac{1}{D_{k}^{2}}\phi_{k}^{2v_{n}+2\varepsilon}
or
x_{n}x_{n+1}<\frac{1}{D_{k}^{2}}\phi_{k}^{2v_{n}-2\varepsilon}
respectively, and we can apply the same argument as before.
∎

-Fibonacci branches in different Markoff trees, illustrating the results proved in the previous sections.

Example 7.1

(The case
r=1
)
.

Consider the family of triples

(\alpha_{k,1},F_{k}(\ell),F_{k}(\ell+2))
with
\ell\geq 2,
and
3\nmid k
. In this case there are two branches, determined by the initial triples

(F_{k}(2),\alpha_{k,1},F_{k}(4))=\left(k,\frac{k^{2}+2}{3},k^{3}+2k\right)\quad(F_{k}(1),\alpha_{k,1},F_{k}(3))=\left(1,\frac{k^{2}+2}{3},k^{2}+1\right).

When
m>0
, these are the corresponding minimal triples.

The even-parity branch, corresponding to even values of
\ell\geq 2
, satisfies

\mathfrak{m}(\alpha_{k,1},F_{k}(\ell),F_{k}(\ell+2))=\mathfrak{m}\!\left(k,\frac{k^{2}+2}{3},k^{3}+2k\right)=\frac{1}{9}(k^{4}+13k^{2}+4).

The odd-parity branch, corresponding to odd values of
\ell\geq 1
, satisfies

\mathfrak{m}(\alpha_{k,1},F_{k}(\ell),F_{k}(\ell+2))=\mathfrak{m}\!\left(1,\frac{k^{2}+2}{3},k^{2}+1\right)=\frac{1}{9}(k^{4}-5k^{2}+4).
