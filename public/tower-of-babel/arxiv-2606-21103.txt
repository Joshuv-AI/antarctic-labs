On the Reachability Problem on Monoid-Labelled Undirected Graphs A preliminary version of the paper appeared in the proceedings of the 22nd International Conference Relational and Algebraic Methods in Computer Science (RAMiCS 2026)
Nagashri Krishnakumar, Harshil Mittal, Jayalal Sarma

Abstract
The labelled reachability problem for undirected graphs where the edges are labelled with elements from a monoid M M (more generally, other groupoids or magmas) is known to capture space-bounded complexity classes 𝖫 \mathsf{L} and 𝖭𝖫 {\mathsf{NL}} . Given a labelled graph G ⁡ ( V , E ) G(V,E) (labelled with ϕ : E → M \phi~\colon E\to M ), s , t ∈ V s,t\in V and an accepting subset F ⊆ M F\subseteq M , the problem asks to test whether there is a walk P P from s s to t t in G G where ϕ ⁡ ( P ) ∈ F \phi(P)\in F . When the accepting element is also part of the input, the problem has been studied by Ramaswamy et al (2019) [ 19 ] for the case when the monoid is aperiodic and the case when the monoid is a group. Motivated by the success in designing space-bounded algorithms for the graph reachability problem in the undirected case, we study the labelled reachability problem when the accepting set is also fixed. This allows us to reveal finer complexity upper and lower bounds, and dichotomies for the problem using the structure of the underlying monoid and the accepting set. In fact, when the monoid M M is a group or belongs to the monoid pseudovariety 𝖣𝖠 \sf DA , the previous results imply a deterministic logspace algorithm for the undirected labelled reachability problem over the monoid M M for any finite accepting subset as well. We prove the following: • For any finite monoid M M , there is a deterministic logspace algorithm for solving the above problem when the accepting element is the identity of M M . In contrast, if the accepting element is an idempotent element, under suitable constraints, the problem is 𝖭𝖫 {\mathsf{NL}} -hard. • Considering the more general case of the problem when the accepting set is an arbitrary subset of M M , we show a deterministic logspace algorithm for a commutative monoid M M for all accepting sets F ⊆ M F\subseteq M . • Further, we prove that for any finite ℒ \mathcal{L} -commutative or ℛ \mathcal{R} -commutative union-of-groups monoid M M , there is a deterministic logspace algorithm for all accepting sets F ⊆ M F\subseteq M . We also design deterministic logspace algorithms for the union-of-groups case that are neither ℒ \mathcal{L} -commutative nor ℛ \mathcal{R} -commutative, under certain constraints. • For the specific monoids 𝖡𝖠 𝟤 \sf BA_{2} and 𝖴 \sf U , which are the building blocks of non-commutative aperiodic monoids, we prove a dichotomy theorem with respect to the accepting subsets by showing that for all subsets F ⊆ M F\subseteq M , the reachability problem is either 𝖭𝖫 {\mathsf{NL}} -complete or is in 𝖫 \mathsf{L} . To obtain our results, we critically exploit the relationships between Green’s equivalence relations in the union-of-groups monoids and the properties of the product graph (a graph introduced by [ 19 ] ).

4

The most interesting general case of this problem is when the accepting subset can be an arbitrary subset of the monoid

. Our results in this direction are (a) for all commutative monoids (Section

), thus generalising the logspace algorithm for all commutative aperiodic monoids in

]
, (b) for certain subclasses of union-of-groups
(Section

), thus generalising the known algorithm for the case when the monoid is a group by itself, and (c) for the monoids

), which are the building blocks of non-commutative aperiodic monoids.

4.1
Logspace Algorithms for Commutative Monoids

In the following theorem, we show a logspace algorithm for all commutative monoids with arbitrary accepting subsets.

See
1.3

Proof.

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}
denote the elements of

. Let us describe a logspace algorithm that solves

is finite, this sequence must have a repeated element; consider the least

such that the

G^{\prime}
exits the part
V\times H
,
P
cannot re-visit
V\times H
. We then show that
G^{\prime}[V\times H]
is an Eulerian subgraph of
G^{\prime}
, and hence the reachability within the subgraph can be tested in deterministic logspace.

Lemma 4.2
.

Once a path
P
in
G^{\prime}
exits the part
V\times H
for any
\mathcal{H}
-class
H
,
P
cannot re-visit
V\times H
.

Proof.

For the sake of contradiction, assume that
P
exits the part
V\times H
(say, at vertex
(v,h)
), enters the part
V\times\tilde{H}
for another
\mathcal{H}
-class
\tilde{H}
(say, at vertex
(w,\tilde{h})
), and then eventually re-enters the part
V\times H
(say, at vertex
(v^{\prime},h^{\prime})
) (see fig.
3
). Now, as
(w,\tilde{h})
is reachable from
(v,h)
in
G^{\prime}
, there exists an
m\in M
such that
h\cdot m=\tilde{h}
and so,
\tilde{h}\leq_{\mathcal{R}}h
. Also, as
(v^{\prime},h^{\prime})
is reachable from
(w,\tilde{h})
in
G^{\prime}
, there exists an
m^{\prime}\in M
such that
\tilde{h}\cdot m^{\prime}=h^{\prime}
; so,
\tilde{h}\cdot(m^{\prime}\cdot((h^{\prime})^{-1}\cdot h))=h
and thus,
h\leq_{\mathcal{R}}\tilde{h}
.

Therefore,
h~\mathcal{R}~\tilde{h}
and so, as the
\mathcal{R}
classes of
M
coincide with its
\mathcal{H}
classes (from Lemma
4.1
), we get
h~\mathcal{H}~\tilde{h}
. However,
h
and
\tilde{h}
are from different
\mathcal{H}
-classes (i.e.,
H

\tilde{H}
respectively), a contradiction. This proves Lemma
4.2
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
H
be any group (i.e.,
\mathcal{H}
-class) of
M
. Then,
G^{\prime}[V\times H]
is an Eulerian subgraph of
G^{\prime}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the in-degree of any vertex matches its out-degree in
G^{\prime}[V\times H]
. Consider any
(v,h)\in V\times H
. Note that the only edges of
G
due to which edges get added (in the product graph
G^{\prime}
) at the vertex
(v,h)\in V\times H
are the ones incident to the vertex
v
in
G
. Also, for any two such distinct edges (say,
\{v,w\}
and
\{v,w^{\prime}\}
, where
w
and
w^{\prime}
are distinct neighbors of
v
in
G
), the corresponding added edges at
(v,h)
would be such that their other endpoints would be of the form
(w,\_)
and
(w^{\prime},\_)
respectively; that is, the first component of these endpoints differs. So,
\{v,w\}
and
\{v,w^{\prime}\}
cannot be the cause of the same edge incident at
(v,h)
. Thus, it now suffices to individually argue, for each edge
e=\{v,w\}\in E
(say, with label
\lambda\in M
) incident to
v
in
G
, that the numbers of in-edges and out-edges added (in
G^{\prime}[V\times H]
) at vertex
(v,h)
due to edge
e
are equal.

Case 1:
h\lambda\in H
:

Then, due to
e
, there’s clearly exactly one out-edge added to
(v,h)
in
G^{\prime}[V\times H]
; namely, from
(v,h)
to
(w,h\lambda)
. Also, due to
e
, there’s at least one in-edge added to
(v,h)
in
G^{\prime}[V\times H]
; namely, from
(w,h\cdot(h\lambda)^{-1}\cdot h)
to
(v,h)
respectively. Now, let us argue that this is the only in-edge added to
(v,h)
in
G^{\prime}[V\times H]
due to
e
. For contradiction, suppose there exists
\mu\neq h\cdot(h\lambda)^{-1}\cdot h
in
H
such that there’s an edge from
(w,\mu)
to
(v,h)
added due to
e
in
G^{\prime}[V\times H]
. Then,
\mu\cdot\lambda=h
. Writing
\mu
as
\mu\cdot h^{-1}\cdot h
, we get
(\mu\cdot h^{-1})\cdot(h\cdot\lambda)=h
. Post-multiplying both sides by
(h\cdot\lambda)^{-1}\cdot h
, we get
\mu=h\cdot(h\lambda)^{-1}\cdot h
, a contradiction.

Case 2:
h\lambda\not\in H
:

Then, due to
e
, there’s clearly no out-edge added to
(v,h)
in
G^{\prime}[V\times H]
. Let’s argue that due to
e
, no in-edge is added either to
(v,h)
in
G^{\prime}[V\times H]
. For contradiction, assume that due to
e
, there’s an edge from
(w,\mu)
to
(v,h)
added in
G^{\prime}[V\times H]
. Then,
\mu\cdot\lambda=h
. Pre-multiplying both sides by
h\cdot\mu^{-1}
gives
h\cdot\lambda=h\cdot\mu^{-1}\cdot h
, which
\in H
, a contradiction.

Thus, in both cases, the contributions to in-degree and out-degree of
(v,h)
in
G^{\prime}[V\times H]
are equal.
This proves Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
using Lemma
4.2
and
4.3
:

Now, the graph
G
has an
s
-to-
t
walk of yield
\in F
if and only if its product graph
G^{\prime}
has an
(s,id)
to
(t,m)
path for some
m\in F
, where
id
denotes the monoid identity of
M
. Due to Lemma
4.2
, this path (if existent) must be such that for every
\mathcal{H}
-class
H
, it either never visits the part
V\times H
, or enters/leaves the part
V\times H
only once. Guess (i.e., iterate over all possible choices of) the
\mathcal{H}
-classes
H
whose corresponding
V\times H
parts are visited, and also guess the edges involved in the jumps across these parts. As the number of
\mathcal{H}
-classes is
\mathcal{O}(1)
, there are only
n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
guesses (and so, the counter needed to iterate over them uses only
\mathcal{O}(\log n)
space). For each such guess, the task now remains to check for each visited part
V\times H
, whether there is a path in
G^{\prime}[V\times H]
from the vertex at which the guessed edge entering into
V\times H
lands, to the vertex at which the guessed edge exiting
V\times H
departs from; this can be done using the logspace algorithm for Directed Reachability on Eulerian graphs because
G^{\prime}[V\times H]
is Eulerian, as shown in Lemma
4.3
. This proves Theorem
1.4
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

From Lemma
4.1
, we know that for any union-of-groups monoid
M
,
\mathcal{L}
-commutativity implies coincidence of
\mathcal{R}
-classes with
\mathcal{H}
-classes. In fact, the converse is also true. That is, if
\mathcal{R}
-classes of
M
coincide with its
\mathcal{H}
-classes, then we can show that
M
is
\mathcal{L}
-commutative as follows: Suppose this is not the case. Then, there exist
a,b\in M
such that
ab
and
ba
belong to different
\mathcal{L}
-classes (say
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
) respectively. Let
H_{1}\subseteq L_{1}
and
H_{2}\subseteq L_{2}
denote the
\mathcal{H}
-classes containing
ab
and
ba
respectively.
Note that
((ab)^{-1}\cdot a)\cdot(bab)=ab
and thus,
ab\leq_{\mathcal{L}}bab
. Also, as
b\cdot ab=bab
, we have
bab\leq_{\mathcal{L}}ab
. Thus,
bab
belongs to the same
\mathcal{L}
class as
ab
(i.e.,
L_{1}
). Next, as
(bab)\cdot(a\cdot(ba)^{-1})=ba
, we get
ba\leq_{\mathcal{R}}bab
. Also, as
ba\cdot b=bab
, we get
bab\leq_{\mathcal{R}}ba
. Therefore,
bab
and
ba
belong to the same
\mathcal{R}
-class. So, as
\mathcal{R}
-classes coincide with
\mathcal{H}
-classes, both
bab
and
ba
belong to the same
\mathcal{H}
-class (i.e.,
H_{2}
). However,
bab\in L_{1}
is inconsistent with
bab\in H_{2}
, a contradiction.

Example 4.1
.

Consider the full transformation monoid of
\{1,2\}
, i.e., the monoid consisting of all maps from
\{1,2\}
to itself with function composition as the monoid operation. This union-of-groups monoid is
\mathcal{L}
-commutative because it has the following same set of
\mathcal{R}
-classes and
\mathcal{H}
-classes:

\bigg\{\Big\{a=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\mapsto 2\\
2\mapsto 1\end{array}\right),a^{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\mapsto 1\\
2\mapsto 2\end{array}\right)\Big\},\Big\{e=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\mapsto 1\\
2\mapsto 1\end{array}\right)\Big\},\Big\{ae=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\mapsto 2\\
2\mapsto 2\end{array}\right)\Big\}\bigg\}.

Remark 4.2
.

It is known that Clifford monoids are both
\mathcal{L}
-commutative and
\mathcal{R}
-commutative (see Theorem 3.13 in
[
14
]
). We can show that any Clifford monoid
M
is
\mathcal{L}
-commutative (and by a similar argument, also
\mathcal{R}
-commutative) as follows: Suppose this is not the case. Then, as per Remark
4.1
,
M
has an
\mathcal{R}
-class that contains two of its distinct
\mathcal{H}
-classes
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
, say with idempotent elements (same as group identities)
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
respectively. As
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
belong to the same
\mathcal{R}
-class, there exist
x,y\in M
such that
e_{1}\cdot x=e_{2}
and
e_{2}\cdot y=e_{1}
. Since the idempotent elements
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
commute with all elements of
M
(because
M
is a Clifford monoid), we can rewrite these as
x\cdot e_{1}=e_{2}
and
y\cdot e_{2}=e_{1}
. So, apart from being
\mathcal{R}
-related,
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are also
\mathcal{L}
-related (and thus,
\mathcal{H}
-related) to each other, a contradiction.

We now handle some interesting special cases where

Proof.

Suppose that the accepting set

There is an

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
: Suppose that there is an edge

with label
\in M\setminus\tilde{G}
such that there is a walk

of yield

satisfying

to one endpoint of

because

and right-multiplying it with any element of

always gives an element of

(due to

being a right ideal).

(\Rightarrow)
: Suppose that there is an
s
-
t
walk
P
of yield
\in R
. Note that
P
must have at least one edge with label
\in M\setminus\tilde{G}
(as any product of elements from just
\tilde{G}
can never land outside
\tilde{G}
). Let
e
be the first edge in

which is labelled by an element of
M\setminus\tilde{G}
; that is, all edges before

in
P
are labelled by elements of
\tilde{G}
. Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
denote the subwalks of

that appear before edge

and after edge
e
, respectively. We have
\phi(P_{1})\cdot\phi(e)\cdot\phi(P_{2})\in R
. It suffices to show that
\phi(P_{1})\cdot\phi(e)\in R
. For the sake of contradiction, assume that
\phi(P_{1})\cdot\phi(e)\notin R
.

Case 1
:
\phi(P_{1})\cdot\phi(e)\in\tilde{G}
.

As

’s edges’ labels are only from

, we have

Then,

∎

Now, we get the desired log-space algorithm by simply iterating over all edges

with labels from

(i.e., see Theorem

),

5
Discussion and Conclusion

In this paper, we presented complexity-theoretic upper and lower bounds for
\textrm{{\sc Reach}}_{M,F}
for various subclasses of monoids. The main technical ingredient is the study of the product graph’s structure, which is established by exploiting the algebraic properties of the underlying monoid. The general goal of this work is to completely classify the difficulty of the labelled reachability problem based on the structure of the monoid from which the elements are used for labelling. While this target may be far away, one immediate question is whether one can establish a deterministic logspace algorithm for the reachability problem when the underlying monoid
M
is a union-of-groups and the accepting subset
F
is an arbitrary subset of
M
.
