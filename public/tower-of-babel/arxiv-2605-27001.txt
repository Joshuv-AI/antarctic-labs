Introduction and Main results

Let
f
be a nonconstant holomorphic curve from
{\mathbb{C}}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
and
H
be a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. The defect of
H
for
f
is defined by

\delta^{k}_{f}(H)=1-\underset{r\longrightarrow+\infty}{\mathrm{lim\ sup}}\frac{N^{[k]}_{f}(r,H_{i})}{T_{f}(r)},

where
T_{f}(r)
is the characteristic function of
f
and
N^{[k]}_{f}(r,H)
is the truncated (to level
k
) counting function of
f^{-1}(H)
(see
[
21
]
for the definitions). We see that if
f
intersects
H
with only a finite number of times then
\delta^{k}_{f}(H)=1
.

Let
\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
be a family of hyperplanes of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
in general positon, i.e.,
\bigcap_{0\leq j\leq n}H_{i_{j}}=\varnothing
for any
1\leq i_{0}<\cdots<i_{n}\leq q
. From the second main theorem in Nevanlinna theory (due to H. Cartan
[
2
]
and E. Nochka
[
20
]
) we get the defect relation that

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\delta^{k}_{f}(H_{i})\leq 2n-k+1

if the image of
f
is contained in a linear subspace of
k
-dimension of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
but not in any subspace with dimension less than
k
.
Therefore, if
f
intersects
q
hyperplanes
\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
with only a finite number of times then
f
is linearly degenerate for the case
q\geq n+2
, and
f
must be constant for the case
q\geq n+2.

In the late 1990s, H. Fujimoto extended these results to the Gauss maps of complete minimal surfaces by introducing the concept of the modified defect for holomorphic curves on minimal Riemann surfaces in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(see
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
), which serves as an analogue to the classical defect relation in Nevanlinna theory. Fujimoto established several modified defect relations for the Gauss maps of minimal surfaces and used them to investigate various properties of these maps. For instance, he showed that if the Gauss map of a nonflat complete minimal surface immersed in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, whose total curvature is infinite, intersects
q
hyperplanes in general position of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
with only a finite number of times then
q\leq n+1+\frac{n(n+1)}{2}
(see
[
13
, Corollary 1.4]
). Subsequently, many authors have extended the results of Fujimoto to the case where the Gauss maps are ramified over families of hyperplanes, meaning the multiplicity of the intersection points between the Gauss maps and the hyperplanes exceeds a certain threshold. For further details, we refer the reader to the papers
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
24
]
and the references therein. Especially, recently the first author
[
22
]
generalized all these results to the case of the Gauss maps and the families hypersurfaces in subgeneral position. In this paper, we will study the Gauss maps of complete minimal surfaces under the condition of intersection with only a single Fermat hypersurface. Under this condition, we will derive more refined estimates compared to the results in the previous paper. In order to state the main results, we first recall the following essential notions (see
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
and
[
22
]
).

A. Nevanlinna functions.
For each

\Delta_{s,\infty}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\ |\ s\leq|z|<\infty\}.

For a divisor
\nu

\Delta_{s,\infty}

N^{[p]}(r,s,\nu)=\int\limits_{s}^{r}\dfrac{n^{[p]}(t,s)}{t}dt\quad(s<r<\infty).

Let
\varphi
be a meromorphic function on
\Delta_{s,\infty}
. We will write
N_{\varphi}^{[p]}(r,s)

N^{[p]}(r,s,\nu^{0}_{\varphi}),
where
\nu^{0}_{\varphi}
is the zero divisor of
\varphi

The proximity function of
\varphi
(with respect to the point
\infty

m(r,s,\varphi)=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\log^{+}|\varphi(re^{i\theta})|\dfrac{d\theta}{2\pi}-\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\log^{+}|\varphi(se^{i\theta})|\dfrac{d\theta}{2\pi}\ (s<r<\infty),

where
\log^{+}x=\log(\max\{0,x\})
for a real number

. The Nevanlinna’s characteristic function of

T(r,s,\varphi):=m(r,s,\varphi)+N_{1/\varphi}(r,s).

Throughout this paper, we fix a homogeneous coordinates system
(x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n})
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. Let

on

\|F\|=(|f_{0}|^{2}+\cdots+|f_{n}|^{2})^{1/2}.

The characteristic function of

T_{f}(r,s)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\|F(re^{i\theta})\|\dfrac{d\theta}{2\pi}-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\|F(se^{i\theta})\|\dfrac{d\theta}{2\pi}\ (s<r<R).

For a meromorphic function
\varphi
, which is regarded as a holomorphic curve into
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})

T_{\varphi}(r,s)=T(r,s,\varphi)+O(1).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
be
n+1
hypersurfaces of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
in general position of the same degree
p
and
Q
a hypersurface given by
Q=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}Q_{i}^{d}
, where
a_{i}\ (0\leq i\leq n)
are nonzero constants and
d
is a positive integer. Let
f
be a holomorphic mapping of
\Delta_{s,\infty}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
with a reduced representation
F=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
such that
Q_{0}^{d}(F),\ldots,Q_{n}^{d}(F)
are linearly independent. Then, we have

\biggl\|\ \left(1-\frac{n(n+1)}{d}\right)T_{f}(r,s)\leq\frac{1}{pd}N^{[n]}_{Q(f)}(r,s)+o(T_{f}(r,s))+O(\log r).

Proof.

Let
\tilde{f}
be the holomorphic curve with the reduced representation
(Q_{0}^{d}(F),\ldots,Q_{n}^{d}(F))
. Then
\tilde{f}
is linearly nondegenerate. By the second main theorem for hyperplanes, we have

\displaystyle\bigl\|\ T_{\tilde{f}}(r,s)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n}N^{[n]}_{Q_{i}^{d}(f)}(r,s)+N^{[n]}_{Q(f)}(r,s)+o(T_{\tilde{f}}(r,s))+O(\log r)

\displaystyle\leq n\sum_{i=0}^{n}N_{Q_{i}(f)}(r,s)+N^{[n]}_{Q(f)}(r,s)+o(T_{\tilde{f}}(r,s))+O(\log r)

\displaystyle\leq n(n+1)pT_{f}(r,s)+N^{[n]}_{Q(f)}(r,s)+o(T_{\tilde{f}}(r,s))+O(\log r).

Since
T_{\tilde{f}}(r)=dpT_{f}(r,s)+O(1)
, the above inequality implies that

\biggl\|\ \left(1-\frac{n(n+1)}{d}\right)T_{f}(r,s)\leq\frac{1}{pd}N^{[n]}_{Q(f)}(r,s)+o(T_{f}(r,s))+O(\log r).

The theorem is proved.
∎

. Then for an arbitrarily given

\displaystyle\nu_{h}(z_{0})\geq\min\{n,\nu_{H_{n+1}(F)}(z_{0})\}.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nu_{F_{n}}(z_{0})&-\nu_{H_{n+1}(F)}(z_{0})-(1-\frac{n}{d})\sum_{j=0}^{n}\nu_{H_{j}(F)}(z_{0})\\

\displaystyle-\nu_{H_{n+1}(F)}(z_{0})-(1-\frac{n}{d})\sum_{j=0}^{n}\nu_{H_{j}(F)}(z_{0})\biggl)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\epsilon}{\sigma_{n}+\epsilon\tau_{n}}\min\{n,\nu_{H_{n+1}(F)}(z_{0})\}\geq 0.

. By the inequality (

\displaystyle\begin{split}&dd^{c}\log\eta^{2}\geq\dfrac{\gamma-\epsilon\left(\sigma_{n+1}+\theta\right)}{\sigma_{n}+\epsilon\tau_{n}}dd^{c}\log|F_{0}|^{2}+\dfrac{\epsilon}{2(\sigma_{n}+\epsilon\tau_{n})}dd^{c}\log\left(|F_{0}|^{2}\cdots|F_{n-1}|^{2}\right)\\

4.

In this section, we will prove the main theorems of the paper. Firstly, we need the following essential lemma.

Lemma 4.1

(cf.
[

)

Let
d\sigma^{2}
be a conformal flat metric on an open Riemann surface

Let

From the assumption of the theorem, we have

a divisor

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Suppose contrarily that

\delta^{S,n}_{g,S}(Q)>\frac{n(n+1)}{d}+\frac{n(n+1)}{2\ell d}.

Let
G=(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})
be a global reduced representation of
g
. Then, there are a constant
\theta
and a continuous subharmonic functions
u

S
satisfying conditions
(D_{1}),(D_{2})
with respect to the number

Let
f
be the holomorphic curve from
S
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
with the reduced representation
F=(Q_{0}^{d}(G),\ldots,Q_{n}^{d}(G))
. We may assume that
\|G\|^{d\ell}\leq\|F\|.
We use the same hyperplanes
H_{0},\ldots,H_{n+1}
as similar as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Set:

\gamma:=(1-\theta)-\frac{n(n+1)}{d}>\frac{\sigma_{n}}{d\ell}.

Then, we may choose a rational number
\epsilon\ (>0)
such that

\gamma-\epsilon\sigma_{n+1}=\frac{\sigma_{n}+\epsilon\tau_{n}}{d\ell}.

On a local holomorphic chart
(U,z)

\lambda_{z}=\left(\dfrac{|F_{0}|^{\gamma-\epsilon\sigma_{n+1}}e^{u}|(F_{n})_{z}|\prod_{p=0}^{n}|(F_{p})_{z}|^{\epsilon}}{|F(H_{n+1})|\prod_{j=0}^{n}(|F(H_{j})|^{1-\frac{n}{d}}\prod_{j,p}\log(\delta/\varphi_{z,p}(H_{j})))}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}+\epsilon\tau_{n}}},

\varphi_{z,p}(H_{j})=\dfrac{|(F_{p})_{z}(H_{j})|}{|(F_{p})_{z}|},j=0,\ldots,n+1,

3.1
with
F,\varphi_{z,p}(H_{j})

.

We define a pseudo-metric
d\tau^{2}:=\lambda_{z}^{2}|dz|^{2}
on each chart
(U,z)
of

. We will show that

(F_{p})_{z}=(\xi^{\prime}_{z})^{\sigma_{p}}\cdot(F_{p})_{\xi}\ (0\leq p\leq n+1),

\displaystyle\lambda_{z}

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{|F_{0}|^{\gamma-\epsilon\sigma_{n+1}}e^{u}|(F_{n})_{z}|\prod_{p=0}^{n}|(F_{p})_{z}|^{\epsilon}}{|F(H_{n+1})|\prod_{j=0}^{n}(|F(H_{j})|^{1-\frac{n}{d}}\prod_{j,p}\log(\delta/\varphi_{z,p}(H_{j})))}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}+\epsilon\tau_{n}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{|F_{0}|^{\gamma-\epsilon\sigma_{n+1}}e^{u}|(F_{n})_{\xi}|\prod_{p=0}^{n}|(F_{p})_{\xi}|^{\epsilon}|\xi_{z}^{\prime}|^{\sigma_{n}+\epsilon\tau_{n}}}{|F(H_{n+1})|\prod_{j=0}^{n}(|F(H_{j})|^{1-\frac{n}{d}}\prod_{j,p}\log(\delta/\varphi_{\xi,p}(H_{j})))}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}+\epsilon\tau_{n}}}
