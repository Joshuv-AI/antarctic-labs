Mini-Batch Stochastic Halpern Algorithm for Nonexpansive Fixed point Problems
Hideaki Iiduka

Abstract
The Halpern algorithm is a powerful fixed point approximation method for finding the closest point in the fixed point set of a nonexpansive mapping to the initial point. However, in practice, it is not necessarily true that this algorithm can be applied to large-scale fixed point problems, since the computation of the nonexpansive mapping is expensive. In this paper, we present mini-batch stochastic Halpern algorithm to resolve the issue caused by the computational difficulty of the mapping. We preform a convergence analysis demonstrating that the algorithm with diminishing step sizes and increasing batch sizes converges in mean square to the closest point in the fixed point set to the initial point. We also perform a convergence rate analysis demonstrating that convergence speed of the algorithm depends on the settings of the diminishing step sizes.



keywords
Halpern algorithm, Mini-batch stochastic Halpern algorithm, Nonexpansive fixed point problem

Introduction

1.1
Fixed point approximation methods

The nonexpansive fixed point problem
(
Goebel and Kirk 1990
, Chapter 4)
,
(
Goebel and Reich 1984
, Chapter 1)
,
(
Bauschke and Combettes 2017
, Chapter 4)
,
(
Takahashi 2000
, Chapter 3)
, which is to find fixed points of a nonexpansive mapping, lies at the core of fixed point problems. It includes the convex feasibility problem
Bauschke and Borwein 1996
, monotone variational inequality problems
(
Kinderlehrer and Stampacchia 2000
, Chapter III)
, and convex minimization problems
(
Hiriart-Urruty and Lemaréchal 1993
, Chapter II)
.

The Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm
Krasnosel’skiĭ 1955
;
Mann 1953
is a well-known fixed point approximation method for finding a fixed point
\bm{x}
of a nonexpansive mapping
T
such that
\bm{x}=T(\bm{x})
. When the current point is the
k
-th approximate fixed point
\bm{x}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
of a nonexpansive mapping
T
, the Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm updates its iterates in the direction
\bm{d}_{k}^{\mathrm{KM}}=T(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}_{k}
with a step size
\alpha_{k}\in(0,1)
:

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{k+1}=\bm{x}_{k}+\alpha_{k}\bm{d}_{k}^{\mathrm{KM}}=\bm{x}_{k}+\alpha_{k}(T(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}_{k})=(1-\alpha_{k})\bm{x}_{k}+\alpha_{k}T(\bm{x}_{k}).

(1)

Convergence of the Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm (
1
) to a fixed point of
T
is guaranteed if
\alpha_{k}
satisfies
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})=+\infty

(
Bauschke and Combettes 2017
, Theorem 5.15)
,
(
Groetsch 1972
, Corollaries 1-3)
. Moreover, the algorithm satisfies that
\|\bm{x}_{K}-T(\bm{x}_{K})\|=O(1/\sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})})

(
Cominetti et al. 2014
, Theorem 1)
.

While the Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm (
1
) can find a certain fixed point of a nonexpansive mapping
T
, the Halpern algorithm
Halpern 1967
can find the global solution of the following convex minimization problem over the fixed point set
\mathrm{Fix}(T)\coloneqq\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon T(\bm{x})=\bm{x}\}
:

\displaystyle\text{Minimize }f_{0}(\bm{x})\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{0}\|^{2}\text{ subject to }\bm{x}\in\mathrm{Fix}(T).

(2)

The Halpern algorithm updates its iterates in the direction
\bm{d}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}=-\nabla f_{0}(T(\bm{x}_{k}))=\bm{x}_{0}-T(\bm{x}_{k})
with a step size
\alpha_{k}\in(0,1)
:

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{k+1}=T(\bm{x}_{k})+\alpha_{k}\bm{d}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}=T(\bm{x}_{k})+\alpha_{k}(\bm{x}_{0}-T(\bm{x}_{k}))=\alpha_{k}\bm{x}_{0}+(1-\alpha_{k})T(\bm{x}_{k}).

(3)

Convergence of the Halpern algorithm (
3
) to the global minimizer
\bm{x}^{\star}
of
f_{0}
over
\mathrm{Fix}(T)
is guaranteed if
\alpha_{k}
satisfies
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\alpha_{k}=0
,
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}=+\infty
, and
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}|\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha_{k}|<+\infty

(
Wittmann 1992
, Theorem 2)
. The solution
\bm{x}^{\star}
of the convex minimization problem (
2
) is the closest point in the fixed point set
\mathrm{Fix}(T)
to the initial point
\bm{x}_{0}
. Accordingly, using the metric projection
P_{\mathrm{Fix}(T)}
onto
\mathrm{Fix}(T)
leads to
\bm{x}^{\star}=P_{\mathrm{Fix}(T)}(\bm{x}_{0})
.

1.2
Mini-batch stochastic fixed point approximation methods

As indicated above, the Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann and Halpern algorithms can in theory find fixed points of a nonexpansive mapping. However, in practice, it is not necessarily true that they can be applied to any nonexpansive fixed point problem. This is because their computation strongly depends on the computability of
T
.

For example, let us consider finding a fixed point of the following
T
defined by the mean of
n
nonexpansive mappings
T_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

(i=1,2,\cdots,n)
on a
d
-dimensional Euclidean space:

\displaystyle T=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathrm{DU}(n)}[T_{\xi}]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i},

where
\xi\sim\mathrm{DU}(n)
denotes that a random variable
\xi
follows a discrete uniform distribution on
\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[X]
is the expectation of
X
with respect to
\xi
. When
n
and
d
are very large, the computation of
T
is expensive. Hence, the existing methods (
1
) and (
3
) may not function properly depending on the computing environment.

To resolve the issue caused by the computational difficulty of the nonexpansive mapping
T=(1/n)\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}
, we set a (mini-)batch size
b_{k}
that is the number of samples and sample
b_{k}
nonexpansive mappings that are randomly chosen from
\{T_{1},T_{2},\cdots,T_{n}\}
at the
k
-th iteration. Here, let
\xi_{k,i}
be a random variable generated by the
i
-th sampling in the
k
-th iteration and let
\bm{\xi}_{k}=(\xi_{k,1},\xi_{k,2},\cdots,\xi_{k,b_{k}})^{\top}
comprise
b_{k}
independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) variables. Then, we can write a nonexpansive mapping for the
i
-th sampling in the
k
-th iteration as
T_{\xi_{k,i}}
, and hence, we can define the following mini-batch stochastic mapping that is the mean of
T_{\xi_{k,1}},T_{\xi_{k,2}},\cdots,T_{\xi_{k,b_{k}}}
: for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x})\coloneqq\frac{1}{b_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{b_{k}}T_{\xi_{k,i}}(\bm{x}).

(4)

Despite its dependence on the computing environment,
T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x})
remains computationally feasible with a properly chosen batch size
b_{k}
. When
T_{\xi_{k,i}}
is an unbiased estimator of
T
, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{k,i}}[T_{\xi_{k,i}}(\bm{x})]=T(\bm{x})
for all
\bm{x}
that are independent of
\xi_{k,i}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}[T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x})]=\frac{1}{b_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{b_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{k,i}}[T_{\xi_{k,i}}(\bm{x})]=T(\bm{x}).

Hence, we can expect that methods using (
4
) will converge to fixed points of
T
.

1.2.1
Mini-batch stochastic Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm

The mini-batch stochastic Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm
Iiduka 2026
can be obtained by replacing
T(\bm{x}_{k})
in (
1
) with
T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})
defined by (
4
) as follows:

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{k+1}=\bm{x}_{k}+\alpha_{k}(T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}_{k})=(1-\alpha_{k})\bm{x}_{k}+\alpha_{k}T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k}).

(5)

Reference
Iiduka 2026
proved that the mini-batch stochastic Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm (
5
) converges almost surely to a fixed point of
T
if
\alpha_{k}
and
b_{k}
satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})=+\infty\text{ and }\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{k}}}<+\infty.

(6)

Moreover, algorithm (
5
) satisfies that
\min_{k\in[0:K-1]}\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|]=O(1/\sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})})

(
Iiduka 2026
, Theorem 2)
. The condition on
b_{k}
in (
6
) implies that increasing batch sizes are essential for guaranteeing almost-sure convergence of algorithm (
5
). In sampling with replacement, even if the batch size
b_{k}
exceeds
n
,
T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\neq T
holds in general. Hence, to examine the convergence of mini-batch algorithms under sampling with replacement, we can use
b_{k}\to+\infty

(k\to+\infty)
.

Nevertheless, when using an increasing batch size
b_{k}
, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
k\geq k_{0}
,
b_{k}\geq n
. This implies that, for all
k\geq k_{0}
, computing
T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})=(1/b_{k})\sum_{i=1}^{b_{k}}T_{\xi_{k,i}}(\bm{x}_{k})
would be difficult in practice. However, the previous numerical results in
Umeda and Iiduka 2025
;
Oowada and Iiduka 2025
showed that increasing the batch size a finite number of times (e.g., for training ResNet-34 on ImageNet with
n=1,281,167
,
b_{0}=2^{5},b_{1}=2^{6},b_{2}=2^{7}
,
b_{3}=2^{8}
, and
b_{4}=2^{9}
were used in Section A.5 in
Umeda and Iiduka 2025
) accelerates mini-batch stochastic gradient descent, which is an example of algorithm (
5
). From a practical standpoint, finitely increasing the batch size within a computable range leads to faster convergence of algorithm (
5
).

1.2.2
Mini-batch stochastic Halpern algorithm

The mini-batch stochastic Halpern algorithm can be obtained by replacing
T(\bm{x}_{k})
in (
3
) with
T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})
defined by (
4
) as follows:

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{k+1}=T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})+\alpha_{k}(\bm{x}_{0}-T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k}))=\alpha_{k}\bm{x}_{0}+(1-\alpha_{k})T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k}).

(7)

In this paper, we prove that algorithm (
7
) with
(\alpha_{k})
and
(b_{k})
satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lim_{k\to+\infty}\alpha_{k}=0,\text{ }\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}=+\infty,\text{ }\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}|\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha_{k}|<+\infty,\\
&\frac{1}{b_{k}}\leq\alpha_{k}^{2}\text{ }(k\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}),\text{ and }\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{k}}}<+\infty\end{split}

(8)

converges in mean square to
\bm{x}^{\star}=P_{\mathrm{Fix}(T)}(\bm{x}_{0})
(Theorem
3.1
) through three lemmas (Lemmas
3.1
,
3.2
, and
3.3
). The condition on
b_{k}
in (
8
) implies that increasing batch sizes are essential for guaranteeing mean-square convergence of algorithm (
7
). This is similar to the trend for guaranteeing almost-sure convergence of algorithm (
5
) with (
6
).

We also show that, under certain conditions, algorithm (
7
) has the following convergence rate (Theorem
3.2
):

\displaystyle\min_{k\in[0:K-1]}\mathbb{E}\left[f_{0}(\bm{x}_{k})\right]=f_{0}^{\star}+\begin{cases}O\left(\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}^{2}}{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}}\right)&\text{ }\left(\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}^{2}=o\left(\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}\right)\right)\\
O\left(\frac{1}{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}}\right)&\text{ }\left(\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}^{2}<+\infty\right),\end{cases}

where
f_{0}^{\star}=f_{0}(\bm{x}^{\star})
is the optimal value of
f_{0}
over
\mathrm{Fix}(T)
. For example, when a diminishing step size
\alpha_{k}=1/\sqrt{k+1}
and an increasing batch size
b_{k}
are used, we have

\displaystyle\min_{k\in[0:K-1]}\mathbb{E}\left[f_{0}(\bm{x}_{k})\right]=f_{0}^{\star}+O\left(\frac{\log K}{\sqrt{K}}\right).

This
O(\log K/\sqrt{K})
convergence rate of algorithm (
7
) with a diminishing step size
\alpha_{k}=1/\sqrt{k+1}
is comparable to that of the existing methods (see, e.g.,
(
Hazan 2022
, Chapter 2)
).

Notation and definitions

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of natural numbers and let
[n]\coloneqq\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
and
[0:n]\coloneqq\{0,1,\cdots,n\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Furthermore, let
\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
d
-dimensional Euclidean space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and norm
\|\cdot\|
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}\coloneqq\{\bm{x}=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon x_{i}\geq 0\text{ }(i\in[n])\}
. The identity mapping on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
\mathrm{Id}
(i.e.,
\mathrm{Id}(\bm{x})\coloneqq\bm{x}
for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
). The closed ball with center
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and radius
r>0
is denoted by
B_{r}(\bm{x})\coloneqq\{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\|\bm{y}-\bm{x}\|\leq r\}
.

For a mapping
T\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the set of all fixed points of
T
is defined by
\mathrm{Fix}(T)\coloneqq\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\bm{x}=T(\bm{x})\}
. We call it the fixed point set of
T
.
T
is said to be nonexpansive if
\|T(\bm{x})-T(\bm{y})\|\leq\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|
for all
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The fixed point set of a nonexpansive mapping is closed and convex
(
Goebel and Kirk 1990
, Proposition 5.3)
.
T
is said to be firmly nonexpansive if
\|T(\bm{x})-T(\bm{y})\|^{2}\leq\langle T(\bm{x})-T(\bm{y}),\bm{x}-\bm{y}\rangle
for all
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality ensures that a firmly nonexpansive mapping
T
satisfies
\|T(\bm{x})-T(\bm{y})\|^{2}\leq\|T(\bm{x})-T(\bm{y})\|\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|
, which implies that firm nonexpansivity is stronger than the nonexpansivity. The metric projection
P_{C}
onto a nonempty, closed convex set
C

(\subset\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
P_{C}(\bm{x})\in C
and
\|\bm{x}-P_{C}(\bm{x})\|=\inf_{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|
.
P_{C}
is firmly nonexpansive with
\mathrm{Fix}(P_{C})=C

(
Takahashi 2000
, Theorem 3.1.4(i))
,
(
Bauschke and Borwein 1996
, p.371)
.

\mathrm{P}(A)
denotes the probability of event
A
.
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\bm{X}(\xi)]
denotes the expectation of a random variable
\bm{X}(\xi)
with respect to a random variable
\xi
. The variance of
\bm{X}(\xi)
with respect to
\xi
is defined by
\mathbb{V}_{\xi}[\bm{X}(\xi)]\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|\bm{X}(\xi)-\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\bm{X}(\xi)]\|^{2}]
.
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\bm{X}(\xi)|\bm{Y}]
(resp.
\mathbb{V}_{\xi}[\bm{X}(\xi)|\bm{Y}]
) denotes the expectation (resp. the variance) of
\bm{X}(\xi)
conditioned on
\bm{Y}
. In the case of the independence of
\bm{\xi}_{0},\bm{\xi}_{1},\cdots,\bm{\xi}_{k}
, we define the total expectation
\mathbb{E}
by
\mathbb{E}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{0}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{1}}\cdots\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}
. We write
\xi\sim\mathrm{DU}(n)
when
\xi
follows a discrete uniform distribution on
[n]
.

2
Stochastic Nonexpansive Fixed Point Problem

Let us consider the following problem
(
Iiduka 2026
, Problem 1.1 and Assumption 2.1)
.

Problem 2.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
T_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
i\in[n]
). Furthermore, let
\xi
be a random variable taking values in
[n]
. A stochastic mapping
T_{\xi}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is randomly chosen in
\{T_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and a mapping
T\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

\displaystyle T(\bm{x})\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[T_{\xi}(\bm{x})],

(9)

where
\xi
is independent of
\bm{x}
. We assume that:

(A1)

[Nonexpansivity]
T_{i}

(i\in[n])
is nonexpansive, i.e.,
\|T_{i}(\bm{x})-T_{i}(\bm{y})\|\leq\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|

(\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

(A2)

[Boundedness of variance of stochastic mapping] There exists
\sigma\geq 0
such that, for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{\xi}[T_{\xi}(\bm{x})]\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\big[\|T_{\xi}(\bm{x})-\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[T_{\xi}(\bm{x})]}_{T(\bm{x})}\|^{2}\big]\leq\sigma^{2};

(A3)

[Nonemptiness of fixed point set]
\mathrm{Fix}(T)\neq\emptyset
.

Accordingly, we would like to find a fixed point
\bm{x}^{\star}
of
T
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{\star}=T(\bm{x}^{\star})=\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[T_{\xi}(\bm{x}^{\star})].

2.1
Discussion of assumptions and examples

Assumption (A1) guarantees the nonexpansivity of
T
defined by (
9
). In fact, the definition (
9
) of
T
and the property of
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}
ensure that

\displaystyle\|T(\bm{x})-T(\bm{y})\|

\displaystyle=\|\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[T_{\xi}(\bm{x})]-\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[T_{\xi}(\bm{y})]\|=\|\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[T_{\xi}(\bm{x})-T_{\xi}(\bm{y})]\|,

which, together with Jensen’s inequality and (A1), implies that

\displaystyle\|T(\bm{x})-T(\bm{y})\|\leq\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|T_{\xi}(\bm{x})-T_{\xi}(\bm{y})\|]\leq\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|.

(10)

Here, we give two examples of
T_{\xi}
and
T
satisfying Assumption (A2)
(
Iiduka 2026
, Example 2.1)
. The first is a convex feasible problem of finding the intersection
C
of many closed convex sets
C_{i}
s. Here, we define a mapping
T
for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

\displaystyle T(\bm{x})=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathrm{DU}(n)}[\underbrace{P_{\xi}(\bm{x})}_{T_{\xi}(\bm{x})}]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}(\bm{x}),

(11)

where
T_{i}=P_{i}
is the metric projection onto
C_{i}
. Then, under
C\coloneqq\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Fix}(T_{i})\neq\emptyset
, Proposition 4.47 in
Bauschke and Combettes 2017
and (
11
) ensure that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fix}(T)=\mathrm{Fix}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}\right)=\mathrm{Fix}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}\right)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Fix}(T_{i})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}=C.

(12)

The triangle inequality and the firm nonexpansivity of
P_{i}

(i\in[n])
imply that, for all
\bm{x}^{\star}\in C
and all
\bm{x}\in B_{r}(\bm{x}^{\star})
,

\displaystyle\|P_{i}(\bm{x})\|\leq\|P_{i}(\bm{x})-P_{i}(\bm{x}^{\star})\|+\|\bm{x}^{\star}\|\leq\|\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|+\|\bm{x}^{\star}\|\leq r+\|\bm{x}^{\star}\|.

(13)

Using (
13
) leads to the finding that

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{\xi\sim\mathrm{DU}(n)}[P_{\xi}(\bm{x})]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathrm{DU}(n)}\left[\|P_{\xi}(\bm{x})\|^{2}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|P_{i}(\bm{x})\|^{2}\mathrm{P}(\xi=i)\leq\left(r+\|\bm{x}^{\star}\|\right)^{2}.

The second example is a convex minimization problem of finding a global minimizer of
f
defined by the mean of many convex functions
f_{i}
s. Let
\nabla f_{\xi}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a stochastic gradient of
f\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}
such that

(C1)

[Unbiasedness of stochastic gradient] For all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathrm{DU}(n)}[\nabla f_{\xi}(\bm{x})]=\nabla f(\bm{x})
;

(C2)

[Boundedness of variance of stochastic gradient] There exists
\sigma_{g}\geq 0
such that, for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathbb{V}_{\xi\sim\mathrm{DU}(n)}[\nabla f_{\xi}(\bm{x})]\leq\sigma_{g}^{2}
.

We define a mapping
T
for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

\displaystyle T(\bm{x})=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathrm{DU}(n)}[\underbrace{(\mathrm{Id}-\eta\nabla f_{\xi})(\bm{x})}_{T_{\xi}(\bm{x})}]=\bm{x}-\eta\nabla f(\bm{x}),

(14)

where
\eta>0
. Then, from (
14
) and the convexity of
f
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fix}(T)=\operatornamewithlimits{argmin}_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\bm{x})=\operatornamewithlimits{argmin}_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\bm{x})\neq\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\operatornamewithlimits{argmin}_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{i}(\bm{x})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Fix}(T_{i}).

(15)

Here, we remark that
\mathrm{Fix}(T)=\operatornamewithlimits{argmin}_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\bm{x})
in (
15
) holds without assuming
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Fix}(T_{i})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\operatornamewithlimits{argmin}_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{i}(\bm{x})\neq\emptyset
, in contrast to the convex feasibility problem considered in (
12
). Using (C1) and (C2) leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{\xi\sim\mathrm{DU}(n)}[\bm{x}-\eta\nabla f_{\xi}(\bm{x})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathrm{DU}(n)}[\|(\bm{x}-\eta\nabla f_{\xi}(\bm{x}))-(\bm{x}-\eta\nabla f(\bm{x}))\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathrm{DU}(n)}[\|\nabla f_{\xi}(\bm{x})-\nabla f(\bm{x})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\eta^{2}\mathbb{V}_{\xi\sim\mathrm{DU}(n)}[\nabla f_{\xi}(\bm{x})]\leq(\eta\sigma_{g})^{2}.

It is guaranteed that, when
\eta
is suitably restricted,
T_{i}\coloneqq\mathrm{Id}-\eta\nabla f_{i}
is nonexpansive
(
Iiduka 2011
, Proposition 2.3)
.

2.2
Mini-batch stochastic mapping

Let
b\in\mathbb{N}
be the batch size (number of samples), and let
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{b})^{\top}
comprise
b
i.i.d. variables. Here, we define the
mini-batch stochastic mapping
of
T
obeying (
9
) for any
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
as follows:

\displaystyle T_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})\coloneqq\frac{1}{b}\sum_{i=1}^{b}T_{\xi_{i}}(\bm{x}),

(16)

where
\bm{x}
is independent of
\bm{\xi}
. The following proposition demonstrates that the mini-batch stochastic mapping
T_{\bm{\xi}}
inherits useful properties of the stochastic mapping
T_{\xi}
, such as unbiasedness ((
9
) in Problem
2.1
) and boundedness of variance (Assumption (A2)). The proof of Proposition
2.1
is given in
Iiduka 2026
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Consider Problem
2.1
and let
T_{\bm{\xi}}
be the mini-batch stochastic mapping defined by (
16
). Further, let
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be independent of
\bm{\xi}
. Then, the following hold.

(i)

[Unbiasedness of mini-batch stochastic mapping]
\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}[T_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})]=T(\bm{x})}
;

(ii)

[Boundedness of variance of mini-batch stochastic mapping]
\displaystyle{\mathbb{V}_{\bm{\xi}}[T_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b}}
.

Moreover, (i) and (ii) lead to the finding that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})-T(\bm{y})\|^{2}\right]\leq\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b}.

(17)

Inequality (
17
) implies that, when the batch size
b
is large enough,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})-T(\bm{x})\|^{2}\right]\leq\|\bm{x}-\bm{x}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b}\approx 0,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})-T(\bm{y})\|^{2}\right]\leq\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b}\approx\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|^{2},

which implies that
T_{\bm{\xi}}
with a large
b
approximates a nonexpansive mapping
T
(see (
10
) for the nonexpansivity of
T
).

3
Mini-Batch Stochastic Halpern Algorithm

Let
\xi_{k,i}
be a random variable generated by the
i
-th sampling in the
k
-th iteration. Since the
k
-th iterate
\bm{x}_{k}
is computed before
\bm{\xi}_{k}=(\xi_{k,1},\xi_{k,2},\cdots,\xi_{k,b_{k}})^{\top}
is sampled,
\bm{x}_{k}
is independent of
\bm{\xi}_{k}
. Hence, by referring to (
16
), we can define the mini-batch stochastic mapping of
T
at the
k
-th iteration by

\displaystyle T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x})\coloneqq\frac{1}{b_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{b_{k}}T_{\xi_{k,i}}(\bm{x}),

(18)

where
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is independent of
\bm{\xi}_{k}
. The pseudo-code of the mini-batch stochastic Halpern Algorithm is listed below.

Algorithm 1

Mini-Batch Stochastic Halpern Algorithm

1:

\bm{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(initial point),
\alpha_{k}\in(0,1]
(step size),
b_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
(batch size),
K\in\mathbb{N}
(steps).

2:

\bm{x}_{K}

3:

for

k=0,1,\cdots,K-1

do

4:

\bm{\xi}_{k}=(\xi_{k,1},\xi_{k,2},\cdots,\xi_{k,b_{k}})^{\top}

5:

6:

\bm{x}_{k+1}\coloneqq T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})+\alpha_{k}(\bm{x}_{0}-T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k}))=\alpha_{k}\bm{x}_{0}+(1-\alpha_{k})T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})

7:

k\leftarrow k+1

8:

end

for

We may in theory assume sampling with replacement. In sampling with replacement, even if the batch size
b_{k}
exceeds
n
,
T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\neq T
holds in general. Hence, to examine the convergence of mini-batch algorithms under sampling with replacement, we can use
b_{k}\to+\infty

(k\to+\infty)
.

3.1
Convergence

First, we show that the sequence
(\bm{x}_{k})
is bounded in the sense of the expected squared norm.

Lemma 3.1
.

The sequence
(\bm{x}_{k})
generated by Algorithm
1
for Problem
2.1
satisfies, for all
k\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
and all
\bm{x}^{\star}\in\mathrm{Fix}(T)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\alpha_{k}\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b_{k}},

where
\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\coloneqq\{\bm{\xi}_{0},\bm{\xi}_{1},\cdots,\bm{\xi}_{k-1}\}
. This implies that, if

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b_{k}}\leq\alpha_{k},

(19)

for all
k\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
, then there exists
M\geq\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\sigma^{2}
such that, for all
k\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]\leq M\text{ and }\mathbb{E}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]\leq M+\sigma^{2}.

Proof.

The convexity of
\|\cdot\|^{2}
and the definition of
\bm{x}_{k+1}
(Step 4 of Algorithm
1
) imply that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\alpha_{k}(\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star})+(1-\alpha_{k})(T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}^{\star})\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]+(1-\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right].

Since Proposition
2.1
with
\bm{y}=\bm{x}^{\star}=T(\bm{x}^{\star})
ensures that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]\leq\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b_{k}},

(20)

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{k}\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+(1-\alpha_{k})\left(\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\alpha_{k}\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b_{k}}.

The total expectation
\mathbb{E}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{0}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{1}}\cdots\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k-1}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}
for the above inequality, together with the condition
1/b_{k}\leq\alpha_{k}
, implies that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]&\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]+\alpha_{k}\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b_{k}}\\
&\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]+\alpha_{k}\left(\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\sigma^{2}\right).\end{split}

(21)

Here, we can set
M\geq 0
such that
M\geq\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\sigma^{2}
and then show by mathematical induction that
\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}]\leq M
holds for all
k\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
. Since (
21
) with
k=0
leads to the finding that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{0})\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\alpha_{0}\left(\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\sigma^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{0})M+\alpha_{0}M=M.

We assume that
\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{m}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}]\leq M
holds for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, from (
21
) with
k=m
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{m+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{m})\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{m}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]+\alpha_{m}\left(\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\sigma^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{m})M+\alpha_{m}M=M,

which implies that
\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}]\leq M
for all
k\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
. From (
20
), we have, for all
k\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b_{k}}\leq M+\sigma^{2}\alpha_{k}\leq M+\sigma^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we show that the distance between
\bm{x}_{k}
and
\bm{x}_{k+1}
converges to
0
in the sense of the expected norm.

Lemma 3.2
.

The sequence
(\bm{x}_{k})
generated by Algorithm
1
under (
19
) for Problem
2.1
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}_{k}\|]\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}_{k-1}\|\right]+M_{1}|\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1}|+\sigma\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{k}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{k-1}}}\right),

for all
k\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
, where
M_{1}\coloneqq\|\bm{x}_{0}\|+\sqrt{2(M+\|\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\sigma^{2})}
, which implies that, if

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}=+\infty,\text{ }\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}|\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha_{k}|<+\infty,\text{ and }\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{k}}}<+\infty,

(22)

then

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}_{k}\|]=0.

Proof.

The definition of
\bm{x}_{k+1}
(Step 4 of Algorithm
1
) and the triangle inequality imply that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}_{k}\|\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\left\|\left(\alpha_{k}\bm{x}_{0}+(1-\alpha_{k})T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})\right)-\left(\alpha_{k-1}\bm{x}_{0}+(1-\alpha_{k-1})T_{\bm{\xi}_{k-1}}(\bm{x}_{k-1})\right)\right\|\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\big[\big\|(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1})\bm{x}_{0}+(1-\alpha_{k})(T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-T_{\bm{\xi}_{k-1}}(\bm{x}_{k-1}))

\displaystyle\quad+(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha_{k})T_{\bm{\xi}_{k-1}}(\bm{x}_{k-1})\big\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\big]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-T_{\bm{\xi}_{k-1}}(\bm{x}_{k-1})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]}_{X_{k}}+|\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1}|(\|\bm{x}_{0}\|+\underbrace{\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k-1}}(\bm{x}_{k-1})\|}_{Y_{k}}).

From the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle X_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k-1})\|\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]}_{X_{k,1}}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k-1})-T(\bm{x}_{k-1})\|\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]}_{X_{k,2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\|T(\bm{x}_{k-1})-T_{\bm{\xi}_{k-1}}(\bm{x}_{k-1})\|}_{X_{k,3}}.

The triangle inequality, Assumption (A1), and the mutual independence of
\bm{\xi}_{k}
,
\bm{x}_{k}
, and
\bm{x}_{k-1}
ensure that

\displaystyle X_{k,1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\left\|\frac{1}{b_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{b_{k}}(T_{\xi_{k,i}}(\bm{x}_{k})-T_{\xi_{k,i}}(\bm{x}_{k-1}))\right\|\Bigg|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{b_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{b_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{k,i}}\left[\|T_{\xi_{k,i}}(\bm{x}_{k})-T_{\xi_{k,i}}(\bm{x}_{k-1})\|\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]\leq\frac{1}{b_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{b_{k}}\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}_{k-1}\|=\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}_{k-1}\|.

Proposition
2.1
and Jensen’s inequality imply

\displaystyle X_{k,2}\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k-1})-T(\bm{x}_{k-1})\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]}=\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k-1})\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]}\leq\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{b_{k}}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k-1}}\left[X_{k,3}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-2]}\right]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{\bm{\xi}_{k-1}}\left[T_{\bm{\xi}_{k-1}}(\bm{x}_{k-1})\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-2]}\right]}\leq\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{b_{k-1}}}.

Accordingly, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X_{k}]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}_{k-1}\|\right]+\sigma\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{k}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{k-1}}}\right).

(23)

Moreover, Jensen’s inequality and the relation
\|\bm{x}+\bm{y}\|^{2}\leq 2\|\bm{x}\|^{2}+2\|\bm{y}\|^{2}

(\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d})
ensure that, for all
\bm{x}^{\star}\in\mathrm{Fix}(T)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y_{k}]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k-1}}(\bm{x}_{k-1})\|^{2}\right]}=\sqrt{2\mathbb{E}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k-1}}(\bm{x}_{k-1})-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]+2\|\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}},

which, together with Lemma
3.1
, implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y_{k}]\leq\sqrt{2(M+\|\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\sigma^{2})}.

(24)

Therefore, from (
23
) and (
24
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}_{k}\|]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}[X_{k}]+|\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1}|(\|\bm{x}_{0}\|+\mathbb{E}[Y_{k}])

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}_{k-1}\|]+M_{1}|\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1}|+\sigma\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{k}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{k-1}}}\right),

where
M_{1}\coloneqq\|\bm{x}_{0}\|+\sqrt{2(M+\|\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\sigma^{2})}
. Hence, for all
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k+l+1}-\bm{x}_{k+l}\|]

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j=l}^{k+l-1}(1-\alpha_{j+1})\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{l+1}-\bm{x}_{l}\|]+M_{1}\sum_{j=l}^{k+l-1}|\alpha_{j+1}-\alpha_{j}|+\sigma\sum_{j=l}^{k+l-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{j+1}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{j}}}\right).

Since
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}=+\infty
implies that
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\prod_{j=l}^{k+l-1}(1-\alpha_{j+1})=0
, we have that, for all
l\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}_{k}\|]

\displaystyle=\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k+l+1}-\bm{x}_{k+l}\|]

\displaystyle\leq M_{1}\sum_{j=l}^{+\infty}|\alpha_{j+1}-\alpha_{j}|+\sigma\sum_{j=l}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{j+1}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{j}}}\right).

Moreover,
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}|\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha_{k}|<+\infty
and
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}1/\sqrt{b_{k}}<+\infty
imply that
\lim_{l\to+\infty}\sum_{j=l}^{+\infty}|\alpha_{j+1}-\alpha_{j}|=0
and
\lim_{l\to+\infty}\sum_{j=l}^{+\infty}(1/\sqrt{b_{j+1}}+1/\sqrt{b_{j}})=0
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}_{k}\|]\leq 0,\text{ i.e., }\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}_{k}\|]=0,

which completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|]=0.

The definition of
\bm{x}_{k+1}

(Step 4 of Algorithm
1
) and the relation
\|\bm{x}+\bm{y}\|^{2}=\|\bm{x}\|^{2}+2\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle+\|\bm{y}\|^{2}

(\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d})
imply that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\alpha_{k}(\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star})+(1-\alpha_{k})(T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}^{\star})\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}^{2}\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+2\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\langle\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star},T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}^{\star}\rangle\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\alpha_{k})^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right],

which, together with (
20
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\langle\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star},T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}^{\star}\rangle\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle=\left\langle\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star},\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]-\bm{x}^{\star}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star},T(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}^{\star}\rangle,

\alpha_{k}\in(0,1)
, and
1/b_{k}\leq\alpha_{k}^{2}
, implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{k}^{2}\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+2\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})\langle\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star},T(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}^{\star}\rangle+(1-\alpha_{k})^{2}\left(\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+2\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})\langle\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star},T(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}^{\star}\rangle+\alpha_{k}^{2}\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\alpha_{k}^{2}\sigma^{2}.

Accordingly, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]+2\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})\underbrace{\left\langle\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star},\mathbb{E}\left[T(\bm{x}_{k})\right]-\bm{x}^{\star}\right\rangle}_{B_{k}}+\alpha_{k}\underbrace{(M_{2}\alpha_{k})}_{C_{k}},

where
M_{2}\coloneqq\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\sigma^{2}
. The property of the limit superior of
B_{k}
ensures that there exists a subsequence
(\bm{x}_{k_{i}})
of
(\bm{x}_{k})
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to+\infty}B_{k}=\lim_{i\to+\infty}\left\langle\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star},\mathbb{E}\left[T(\bm{x}_{k_{i}})\right]-\bm{x}^{\star}\right\rangle.

(29)

Moreover, from Lemma
3.1
, there exists a subsequence
(\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}})
of
(\bm{x}_{k_{i}})
which converges in mean. Let
\bar{\bm{x}}
be the convergent random variable of
(\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}})
. From the triangle inequality and Assumption (A1) (which implies the nonexpansivity of
T
; see also (
10
)),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{\bm{x}}-T(\bar{\bm{x}})\|]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bar{\bm{x}}-\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}}\right\|\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}}-T(\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}})\right\|\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|T(\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}})-T(\bar{\bm{x}})\right\|\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}}-\bar{\bm{x}}\right\|\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}}-T(\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}})\right\|\right].

Hence, Lemma
3.3
leads to the finding that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{\bm{x}}-T(\bar{\bm{x}})\|]=0,\text{i.e., }\bar{\bm{x}}\in\mathrm{Fix}(T)\text{ a.s.},

which, together with the closedness and convexity of
\mathrm{Fix}(T)
, implies that
\mathbb{E}[\bar{\bm{x}}]\in\mathrm{Fix}(T)
. From (
29
), the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to+\infty}B_{k}

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\left\langle\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star},\mathbb{E}\left[T(\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}})\right]-\bm{x}^{\star}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\left\langle\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star},\mathbb{E}\left[T(\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}})-\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}}-\bar{\bm{x}}\right]+\mathbb{E}[\bar{\bm{x}}]-\bm{x}^{\star}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|\lim_{j\to+\infty}\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[T(\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}})-\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}}\right]\right\|+\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}}-\bar{\bm{x}}\right]\right\|

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star},\mathbb{E}[\bar{\bm{x}}]-\bm{x}^{\star}\right\rangle,

which, together with Jensen’s inequality, Lemma
3.3
,
\lim_{j\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k_{i_{j}}}-\bar{\bm{x}}\|]=0
, and the property of
P_{\mathrm{Fix}(T)}
(i.e.,
\langle\bm{x}_{0}-P_{\mathrm{Fix}(T)}(\bm{x}_{0}),\bm{x}-P_{\mathrm{Fix}(T)}(\bm{x}_{0})\rangle\leq 0
for all
\bm{x}\in\mathrm{Fix}(T)
), implies that

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to+\infty}B_{k}\leq\left\langle\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star},\mathbb{E}[\bar{\bm{x}}]-\bm{x}^{\star}\right\rangle\leq 0.

Hence, together with
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\alpha_{k}=0
,
for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle B_{k}\leq\epsilon\text{ and }C_{k}\leq\epsilon

for all
k\geq k_{0}
. Therefore, for all
k\geq k_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]+2\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})\epsilon+\alpha_{k}\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]+3\alpha_{k}\epsilon

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]+3\{1-(1-\alpha_{k})\}\epsilon,

which implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]\leq\prod_{j=k_{0}}^{k}(1-\alpha_{j})\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k_{0}}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]+3\epsilon\left\{1-\prod_{j=k_{0}}^{k}(1-\alpha_{j})\right\}.

Since
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}=+\infty
implies that
\prod_{j=k_{0}}^{+\infty}(1-\alpha_{j})=0
, we have that, for all
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]\leq 3\epsilon,\text{ i.e., }\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

An increasing batch size

Lemma 3.4

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\left\langle\bm{x}_{k}-T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}^{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{k}),\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\right\rangle\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]\geq(1-\lambda)\left\{\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\left\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}_{k}\right\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b_{k}}\right\}.

\displaystyle G_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f_{0}\left(T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}^{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{k})\right),\bm{x}^{\star}-T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}^{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{k})\right\rangle\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle G_{k,1}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\left\|\nabla f_{0}\left(T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}^{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{k})\right)-\nabla f_{0}(\bm{x}_{k})\right\|\left\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}^{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}_{k}\right\|\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+2\alpha_{k}\left\{f_{0}^{\star}-f_{0}(\bm{x}_{k})+(1-\lambda)^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\left\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}_{k}\right\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]\right\}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b_{k}}

\displaystyle\quad+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\left\|\nabla f_{0}\left(T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}^{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{k})\right)\right\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]+(1-\lambda)(1-2\lambda)\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\left\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}_{k}\right\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle=\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+2\alpha_{k}\left(f_{0}^{\star}-f_{0}(\bm{x}_{k})\right)+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\left\|\nabla f_{0}\left(T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}^{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{k})\right)\right\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b_{k}}

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\lambda)\left\{2(1-\lambda)\alpha_{k}+(1-2\lambda)\right\}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\left\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}_{k}\right\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right],

which, together with
\alpha_{k}\leq(2\lambda-1)/(2(1-\lambda))
, leads to the assertion in Lemma
3.4
.
∎

Lemma
3.4
enables us to conduct the following convergence rate analysis on algorithm (
35
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(\bm{x}_{k})
be the sequence generated by (
35
) for Problem
2.1
with
(\alpha_{k})
and
(b_{k})
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b_{k}}\leq\alpha_{k}\leq\frac{2\lambda-1}{2(1-\lambda)}\text{ }(k\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}),\text{ }\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}=+\infty,\text{ and }\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{b_{k}}<+\infty.

Then, for all
K\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\min_{k\in[0:K-1]}\mathbb{E}\left[f_{0}(\bm{x}_{k})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}\mathbb{E}[f_{0}(\bm{x}_{k})]

\displaystyle\leq f_{0}^{\star}+\frac{1}{2\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}}\left(\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+M_{3}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}^{2}+\sigma^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\frac{1}{b_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=f_{0}^{\star}+\begin{cases}O\left(\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}^{2}}{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}}\right)&\text{ }\left(\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}^{2}=o\left(\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}\right)\right)\\
O\left(\frac{1}{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}}\right)&\text{ }\left(\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}^{2}<+\infty\right),\end{cases}

where
M_{3}\coloneqq 4\{(M+\sigma^{2})+\|\nabla f_{0}(\bm{x}^{\star})\|^{2}\}
and
M
is defined as in Lemma
3.1
.

Proof.

Let
\bm{x}^{\star}=P_{\mathrm{Fix}(T)}(\bm{x}_{0})
. From the relation
\|\bm{x}+\bm{y}\|^{2}\leq 2\|\bm{x}\|^{2}+2\|\bm{y}\|^{2}

(\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\left\|\nabla f_{0}\left(T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}^{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{k})\right)\right\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\left\|\nabla f_{0}\left(T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}^{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{k})\right)-\nabla f_{0}(\bm{x}^{\star})\right\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]+2\left\|\nabla f_{0}(\bm{x}^{\star})\right\|^{2},

which, together with the
1
-Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla f_{0}
, implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\left\|\nabla f_{0}\left(T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}^{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{k})\right)\right\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\left\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}^{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}^{\star}\right\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]+2\left\|\nabla f_{0}(\bm{x}^{\star})\right\|^{2}.

A similar argument to the one proving Lemma
3.1
leads to the finding that algorithm (
35
) with
1/b_{k}\leq\alpha_{k}

(k\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N})
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}^{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}^{\star}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq M+\sigma^{2}.

Accordingly, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\left\|\nabla f_{0}\left(T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}^{\lambda}(\bm{x}_{k})\right)\right\|^{2}\Big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]\leq 2\left(M+\sigma^{2}\right)+2\left\|\nabla f_{0}(\bm{x}^{\star})\right\|^{2}\eqqcolon\frac{M_{3}}{2}.

Lemma
3.4
thus ensures that, for all
k\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle 2\alpha_{k}\mathbb{E}[f_{0}(\bm{x}_{k})-f_{0}^{\star}]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]+M_{3}\alpha_{k}^{2}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b_{k}}.

Let
K\in\mathbb{N}
. Summing the above inequality from
k=0
to
K-1
guarantees that

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}\mathbb{E}[f_{0}(\bm{x}_{k})]-f_{0}^{\star}\leq\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}-\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{K}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]+M_{3}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}^{2}+\sigma^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\frac{1}{b_{k}},

which, together with
\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{K}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}]\geq 0
, implies that

\displaystyle\min_{k\in[0:K-1]}\mathbb{E}[f_{0}(\bm{x}_{k})]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}\mathbb{E}[f_{0}(\bm{x}_{k})]

\displaystyle\leq f_{0}^{\star}+\frac{\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}}{2\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}}+\frac{M_{3}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}}+\frac{\sigma^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}b_{k}^{-1}}{2\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.1
Concrete convergence rates

The diminishing step size
\alpha_{k}
defined by (
30
) may be modified to

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}=\frac{2\lambda-1}{2(1-\lambda)(k+1)^{a}}\leq\frac{2\lambda-1}{2(1-\lambda)},

(38)

where
a\in(0,1)
. From (
31
),
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}=+\infty
holds. Using an increasing batch size
b_{k}
defined by (
32
) satisfies
1/b_{k}\leq\alpha_{k}
for a sufficiently large
k
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}^{2}\leq\frac{(2\lambda-1)^{2}}{2^{2}(1-\lambda)^{2}}\times\begin{cases}\frac{K^{1-2a}}{1-2a}&\text{ }\left(a\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\\
1+\log K&\text{ }\left(a=\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
\frac{2a}{2a-1}&\text{ }\left(a\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)\right).\end{cases}

(39)

Theorem
3.2
with (
31
) and (
39
) thus indicates that algorithm (
35
) using a diminishing step size defined by (
38
) and an increasing batch size defined by (
32
) has the following convergence rate:

\displaystyle\min_{k\in[0:K-1]}\mathbb{E}[f_{0}(\bm{x}_{k})]=f_{0}^{\star}+\begin{cases}O\left(\frac{1}{K^{a}}\right)&\text{ }\left(a\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\\
O\left(\frac{\log K}{\sqrt{K}}\right)&\text{ }\left(a=\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
O\left(\frac{1}{K^{1-a}}\right)&\text{ }\left(a\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)\right)\\
O\left(\frac{1}{\log K}\right)&\text{ }\left(a=1\right).\end{cases}

(40)

When we use a diminishing step size
\alpha_{k}
, we cannot use a constant batch size
b_{k}=b
, since
\alpha_{k}
satisfying
1/b_{k}=1/b\leq\alpha_{k}
does not converge to
0
. Hence, Theorem
3.2
says that the use of constant batch sizes does not guarantee convergence of
\min_{k\in[0:K-1]}f_{0}(\bm{x}_{k})
to
f_{0}^{\star}
, as is evident as well from Theorem
3.1
.

4
Conclusion and Future Work

This paper considered a stochastic fixed point problem for nonexpansive mappings and presented a convergence analysis of the mini-batch stochastic Halpern algorithm for solving it. The analysis showed that the algorithm using a diminishing step size and an increasing batch size converges in mean square to the closest point in the fixed point set to the initial point. This paper also presented convergence rates when the algorithm converges to the closest point under certain conditions.

While this paper assumed the boundedness of the variance of a stochastic mapping (Assumption (A2)), the previously reported results in
Simsekli et al. 2019
;
Garg et al. 2021
;
Battash et al. 2024
;
Ahn et al. 2024
showed that stochastic noise in practical machine learning may exhibit heavy-tailed behavior, violating the bounded-variance assumption. Hence, we should also verify whether the mini-batch stochastic Halpern algorithm converges under heavy-tailed noise.
