Identification of the collecting lymphatic vessel responsible for dermal backflow using indocyanine green lymphography-guided contrast-enhanced ultrasound
Authors: Takahiro Hirayama, Kiyoka Omoto, Tamami Watanabe, Shinobu Matsubara, Naoto Yamamoto
Published: 2026

In lymphedema diagnosis and treatment, dermal backflow (DBF) is an important finding. DBF is considered to arise from impaired lymphatic flow, with reflux into precollectors and avalvular dermal lymphatics.1 Although indocyanine green (ICG) lymphography readily demonstrates DBF, identifying the collecting lymphatic vessel anatomically connected to the DBF remains difficult. We present an image-based protocol combining ICG lymphography and contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS) study to visualize the superficial lymphatic network and identify the collecting lymphatic vessel responsible for DBF.

ICG lymphography was performed in patients with lymphedema. The earliest focal site at which fluorescence was first detected and subsequently spread as DBF was defined as the first DBF emergence point and marked on the skin (A, circle). CEUS study was then performed using Canon Aplio i800 (Canon Medical Systems Corporation) after subcutaneous injection of perfluorobutane microbubble contrast agent (2-3 μm in diameter) at the same sites as ICG. With minimal probe pressure over the first DBF emergence point, CEUS study visualized the superficial lymphatic network corresponding to the DBF region (B/Cover; Supplementary Video 1, online only; different patient than in A). Vertical channels consistent with precollecting and superficial lymphatics were seen arising from a collecting lymphatic vessel that appeared responsible for DBF. Some microbubbles refluxed into the dermis, seemingly demonstrating DBF in real time. The responsible collecting lymphatic vessel could then be traced. (C; Supplementary Video 2, online only; same patient as in A).

Lymphaticovenular anastomosis is generally considered more effective when performed on collecting lymphatic vessels with higher intralymphatic pressure. The collecting lymphatic vessel responsible for the first DBF emergence point may represent the vessel with the highest intralymphatic pressure within the local lymphatic network. Therefore, identifying the responsible lymphatic vessel using our method may help select a more rational target for lymphaticovenular anastomosis. Additional cases are currently being accumulated to validate this approach.
