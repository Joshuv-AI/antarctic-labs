Project Overview

Thermal-induced polarization effects in high-energy, high-average-power lasers can reduce
usable energy, degrade beam quality, lower frequency-conversion efficiency, and limit
repetition rate and operational lifetime. Existing passive methods for correcting these effects
can be sensitive to alignment errors, changing thermal conditions, and inaccuracies in
predictive models. We have developed an adaptive system that measures the polarization
distribution of a laser beam and actively corrects it to a uniform linear polarization state using
programmable spatial light modulators (SLMs).
The project demonstrated closed-loop polarization correction with 250 x 250 programmable
pixels, more than a 10x resolution improvement over the current state-of-the-art to the best of
our knowledge. Using commercial liquid crystal on silicon SLMs, the system reduced residual
depolarization loss from 30 percent to less than 1 percent. Closed-loop shaping was also
performed with optically addressable light valves, which have better aperture scalability and
higher damage thresholds for demanding applications. The prototype light valves achieved less
than 3 percent residual depolarization, and future production devices with better substrate and
fabrication quality would be expected to further reduce the residual energy loss. These results
establish a foundation for real-time polarization control in high-power laser systems and
demonstrate a flexible alternative to passive birefringence compensation approaches.

Mission Impact

This work advances LLNL’s capabilities in laser and optical science and technology and supports
high-energy-density science, inertial fusion research, and high-average-power laser
development. The technology may enable new experimental regimes for inertial fusion and
other high-energy-density applications, while supporting Department of Energy and National
Nuclear Security Administration missions to develop science and technology tools for future
national security challenges.
