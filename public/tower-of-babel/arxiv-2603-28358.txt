Abstract
. This paper investigates p p -harmonic functions on infinite, connected, and locally finite weighted graphs. We focus on the concept of p p -massiveness, establishing its equivalent characterization with the non-uniqueness of bounded solutions to the Dirichlet boundary value problem. Furthermore, for graphs satisfying the volume doubling condition and the weak ( 1 , p ) (1,p) -Poincaré inequality, we establish a Wiener-type criterion at infinity to determine the p p -massiveness of an infinite set.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the
p
-harmonic functions on weighted graphs. Let
G=(V,E)
be an infinite, connected, locally finite graph. Here
V
denotes the vertex set, and
E
denotes the edge set.
If there exists an edge connecting
x
and
y
, we denote it by
x\sim y
.

Let
\mu:V\times V\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be an edge weight, and denote it by
\mu_{xy}:=\mu(x,y)
.
Note
\mu_{xy}>0
if and only if
x\sim y
. Moreover,
\mu_{xy}=\mu_{yx}
. And the weight of vertex is denoted by
m>0
.

Then, for
p>1
, the
p
-Laplacian operator
\Delta_{p}
on the graph is defined as

\Delta_{p}u(x):=\frac{1}{m(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}\mu_{xy}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{xy}u,

where
\nabla_{xy}u=u(y)-u(x)
.

Let
\ell(V)
denote the set of all real functions on
V
,
\ell_{0}(V)
the subset of functions with finite support, and
\mathcal{B}(V)
the set of bounded functions.
We call a function
u\in\ell(\Omega)

p
-subharmonic in
\Omega
if
\Delta_{p}u\geq 0
for all
x\in\Omega
, and
p
-superharmonic if
\Delta_{p}u\leq 0
. A function
u
is called
p
-harmonic
in
\Omega
if it is both
p
-subharmonic and
p
-superharmonic, that is
\Delta_{p}u=0
.

Clearly,
m
does not affect the harmonicity of functions, we more often use a special vertex measure,

\mu(x)=\sum_{y\sim x}\mu_{xy}.

Under this edge and vertex weights, such weighted graph
(V,E,\mu)
is simplified as
(V,\mu)
.

Let
\Omega^{c}=V\setminus\Omega
, we define the vertex boundary set
\partial\Omega
and the edge boundary set
\partial_{e}\Omega
of a subset
\Omega\subset V
by

\partial\Omega=\{x\in\Omega^{c}\mid\exists y\in\Omega\text{ such that }x\sim y\},

and

\partial_{e}\Omega=\{xy\in E\mid x\in\Omega\text{ and }y\in\partial\Omega\},

and let
\overline{\Omega}:=\Omega\cup\partial\Omega
.

For an infinite subset
\Omega\subset V
and a bounded boundary function
f\in\mathcal{B}(\partial\Omega)
, we investigate the uniqueness of bounded solutions to the following Dirichlet boundary value problem:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}u=0,&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=f,&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

This Dirichlet problem has strong connections to the massive sets:

Definition 1.1
.

A subset
\Omega\subset V
is said to be
p
-massive
if there exists a function
u\in\ell(V)
such that

\displaystyle 0<u<1\quad\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}u=0\quad\text{on }\Omega,

\displaystyle u=1\quad\text{on }\Omega^{c}.

If, in addition,
u
satisfies
D_{p}(u)<\infty
, then
\Omega
is called
D_{p}
-massive
, where the
p
-Dirichlet energy of a function
f
is defined by

(1.2)

D_{p}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in V}\mu_{xy}|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(V,\mu)
be an infinite, connected, and locally finite graph. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\Omega\subset V
is a
p
-massive (resp.,
D_{p}
-massive) set.

(2)

For some (equivalently, all)
f\in\mathcal{B}(\partial\Omega)
, the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) admits more than one bounded solution (resp., with finite
p
-energy).

Furthermore, we characterize the existence of non-trivial global
p
-harmonic functions. A space is called
p
-Liouville (resp.,
D_{p}
-Liouville) if every bounded
p
-harmonic function (resp., with finite
p
-energy) is constant. It is known that the failure of the
p
-Liouville (resp.,
D_{p}
-Liouville) property is equivalent to the existence of two disjoint
p
-massive (resp.,
D_{p}
-massive) subsets. This equivalence was proved for Riemannian manifolds by A. Grigor’yan
[
8
]
for
p=2
and I. Holopainen
[
13
]
for
p>1
. In the discrete setting, I. Holopainen and P. M. Soardi
[
12
]
established the
D_{p}
-Liouville counterpart for unweighted graphs with bounded degree. In this paper, we extend their results to general weighted graphs.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an infinite, connected, and locally finite graph. Then
G
admits a nonconstant bounded
p
-harmonic function (resp., with finite
p
-energy) if and only if there exist two disjoint
p
-massive (resp.,
D_{p}
-massive) subsets of
V
.

Next, we establish a Wiener-type criterion to determine whether an infinite set is
p
-massive. For
p=2
, the massiveness of a set has a direct probabilistic interpretation, characterizing whether the random walk starting in the set has a strictly positive probability of escaping to infinity without hitting the boundary. Using probabilistic methods, K.Itô and H.P.McKean
[
14
]
established a Wiener criterion for this
p=2
case on the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with simple weight, (see
[
4
,
16
]
for relevant probabilistic developments). In this paper, we employ analytic methods to derive a Wiener-type criterion for any
p>1
on a broader class of weighted graphs satisfying the volume doubling condition
(VD)
and the Poincaré inequality
(P_{p})
.

Let us introduce the notion of
p
-capacity. For subsets
K\subset U
, it is defined as

cap_{p}(K,U):=\inf\{D_{p}(f)\mid f\in\ell_{0}(U),f=1\text{ on }K\},

where
D_{p}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y}\mu_{xy}|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}
denotes the
p
-Dirichlet energy (see Section
2
for details).

To obtain the Wiener criterion, some other geometric conditions on graph are also needed.

Definition 1.4
.

We say that the weighted graph
(V,\mu)
satisfies
(p_{0})
condition if for some
p_{0}>0
,

(
p_{0}
)

\frac{\mu_{xy}}{\mu(x)}\geq\frac{1}{p_{0}},\quad\mbox{when $y\sim x$}.

Definition 1.5
.

We say that the weighted graph
(V,\mu)
satisfies volume doubling condition
(VD)
, if there exists a constant
C_{D}>0
such that

(
VD
)

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C_{D}\mu(B(x,r)),

for every ball

.

Definition 1.6
.

We say that the weighted graph
(V,\mu)
admits the weak
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality
(P_{p})
, if there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that for every ball
B=B(x,r)
and all

,

(

)

\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\sum_{x\in B}|f(x)-f_{B}|\mu(x)\leq C_{p}r\left(\frac{1}{\mu(2B)}\sum_{x,y\in 2B}|f(y)-f(x)|^{p}\mu_{xy}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where

and the average
f_{B}
is defined as

f_{B}=\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\sum_{y\in B}f(y)\mu(y).

As a consequence of
(P_{p})
, for any function
u
vanishing outside
B
, we have the following version,

\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\sum_{x\in B}|u(x)|\mu(x)\leq C_{p}^{\prime}\left(\frac{r^{p}}{\mu(2B)}\sum_{x,y\in 2B}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}\mu_{xy}\right)^{1/p}.

2.
Preliminaries

For
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\Omega\subseteq V
, the
p
-Dirichlet energy of a function
f\in\ell(\Omega)
is defined by

(2.1)

D_{p}(f;\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\Omega}\mu_{xy}|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}.

Then, we introduce the set of admissible functions of
(B_{1},B_{2};\Omega)
, denoted by
\mathcal{A}(B_{1},B_{2};\Omega)
:

(2.2)

\mathcal{A}(B_{1},B_{2};\Omega):=\{f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\mid f|_{B_{1}}=1,f|_{B_{2}}=0,\text{ and }D_{p}(f;\Omega)<\infty\}.

The
p
-effective conductance between
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
in
\Omega
is then given by

(2.3)

C_{p}(B_{1},B_{2};\Omega)=\inf_{f\in\mathcal{A}(B_{1},B_{2};\Omega)}D_{p}(f;\Omega).

If
\mathcal{A}(B_{1},B_{2};\Omega)=\emptyset
, we set
C_{p}(B_{1},B_{2};\Omega)=\infty
. Clearly,
C_{p}(B_{1},B_{2};\Omega)=C_{p}(B_{2},B_{1};\Omega)
. Furthermore, when
\Omega=V
, we simplify the notation to
C_{p}(B_{1},B_{2})
.

We further introduce a related quantity. For subsets
K\subset U
, the
p
-capacity of a finite set
K
relative to
U
in
\Omega
is defined as

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K,U;\Omega)=\inf\{D_{p}(f;\Omega)\mid f\in\ell_{0}(U),f=1\text{ on }K\}.

When
U
is finite, it naturally follows that

(2.4)

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K,U;\Omega)=C_{p}(K,U^{c};\Omega).

If
U
is infinite and
U\neq\Omega
, let
\{B_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an exhaustion of
\Omega
. The following equalities hold:

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K,U;\Omega)=\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{p}(K,(B_{n}\cap U)^{c};\Omega)=C_{p}(K,U^{c};\Omega).

Finally, we define

C_{p}(K,\infty;\Omega):=\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{p}(K,B_{n}^{c};\Omega),

from which it follows that
C_{p}(K,\infty;\Omega)=\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K,\Omega;\Omega)
. For convenience, when
\Omega=V
, we simplify the notation to
\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K)
.

We say that a subset
\Omega\subset V
is
p
-parabolic if
C_{p}(K,\infty;\Omega)=0
for some (and consequently, any) finite subset
K\subset\Omega
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(V,\mu)
be an infinite, connected, and locally finite weighted graph. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

(V,\mu)
is
p
-parabolic.

(2)

For any finite non-empty subset
A\subset V
,
\operatorname{cap}_{p}(A)=0
.

(3)

Every positive
p
-superharmonic function on
V
is constant.

Proof.

The equivalence of these statements is a well-known result in nonlinear potential theory on graphs; see
[
17
]
, or
[
1
]
for a more general setting.

\square

Remark 2.2
.

A subset
\Omega
being
p
-parabolic is equivalent to the induced subgraph
(\Omega,\mu|_{\Omega\times\Omega})
being a
p
-parabolic graph.

While the
p
-effective conductance
C_{p}(K,U;\Omega)
is more general than
\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K,U^{c};\Omega)
, as it does not require
K
to be finite, we will sometimes use these two notations interchangeably in the subsequent text, even when
K
is infinite.

An immediate consequence of the definition is:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
B_{1},B_{2}
be disjoint non-empty subsets of
\Omega
. The
p
-effective conductance satisfies the following properties:

(i)

If
B_{1}\subseteq B_{1}^{\prime}
,
B_{2}\subseteq B_{2}^{\prime}
, and
\Omega\subseteq\Omega^{\prime}
, then
C_{p}(B_{1},B_{2};\Omega)\leq C_{p}(B_{1}^{\prime},B_{2}^{\prime};\Omega^{\prime})
.

(ii)

C_{p}(B_{1},B_{2};\Omega)=C_{p}(\partial B^{o}_{1},\partial B^{o}_{2};\Omega)
, where
U^{o}
denotes the interior of a set
U
.

Next, we introduce some well known properties:

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose that
C_{p}(B_{1},B_{2};\Omega)<\infty
. Then:

(i)

There exists a unique function
u\in\mathcal{A}(B_{1},B_{2};\Omega)
such that
E_{p}(u;\Omega)=C_{p}(B_{1},B_{2};\Omega)
. We call this unique minimizer the
p
-potential of
(B_{1},B_{2};\Omega)
.

(ii)

The function
u
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem:

(2.5)

\begin{cases}u(x)=1,&\text{for }x\in B_{1},\\
u(x)=0,&\text{for }x\in B_{2},\\
\Delta_{\Omega,p}u(x)=0,&\text{for }x\in\Omega\setminus(B_{1}\cup B_{2}).\end{cases}

Proof.

See
[
3
, Theorem 2.17]
for the case
p=2
. The proof extends directly to general
p
by observing that while the space of real-valued functions on
\Omega
equipped with the norm
\|f\|_{o,p}:=(D_{p}(f;\Omega)+|f(o)|^{p})^{1/p}
is no longer a Hilbert space, it remains uniformly convex and therefore reflexive. For analogous arguments, we refer the reader to
[
18
,
11
,
1
]
.

\square

Lemma 2.5

(Green’s formula)
.

Let
(V,\mu)
be a locally finite weighted graph, and let
\Omega\subset V
be a non-empty finite subset, Then, for any two functions
f,g
on
V
, the following identity holds:

(2.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\Omega}\Delta_{p}f(x)g(x)\mu(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\Omega}|\nabla_{xy}f|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}f)(\nabla_{xy}g)\mu_{xy}

\displaystyle+\sum_{x\in\Omega}\sum_{y\in\partial\Omega}|\nabla_{xy}f|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}f)g(x)\mu_{xy}.

Proof.

The proof follows from a direct computation.

\square

Lemma 2.6

(Uniqueness of the Dirichlet problem)
.

Let
(V,\mu)
be a connected, locally finite weighted graph. Let
\Omega
be a finite subset of
V
such that
\Omega^{c}\neq\emptyset
. Then, for any
f\in\ell(\partial\Omega)
, the following Dirichlet problem admits a unique solution:

(2.7)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{\mu}u(x)=0&\text{for all }x\in\Omega,\\
u(x)=f(x)&\text{for all }x\in\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.7

(Comparison principle)
.

Let
\Omega\subset V
be a finite set. If
u
is
p
-superharmonic and
v
is
p
-subharmonic in
\Omega
such that
u\geq v
on
\partial\Omega
, then
u\geq v
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Lemmas
2.6
and
2.7
correspond to Theorem 3.11 and Theorem 3.14 in
[
12
]
. While the original results were stated for unweighted graphs, the proofs extend directly to the weighted setting.

\square

3.
Lemmas

In this section, we provide some lemmas on
p
-capacity. We begin with a simple upper and lower bound estimates of the
p
-capacity of balls.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
0<r<R
, define
B_{1}=B(x_{0},r)
and
B_{2}=B(x_{0},R)
, then,

(3.1)

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(B_{1},B_{2})\leq\frac{\mu(B_{2})}{(R-r)^{p}}.

Proof.

Define a cut-off function
\eta
such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
as follows:

\eta(x):=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }d(x,x_{0})\leq r,\\
0,&\text{if }d(x,x_{0})>R,\\
\frac{R-d(x,x_{0})}{R-r},&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

By construction,
\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{1}
,
\operatorname{supp}(\eta)\subset B_{2}
, and
|\nabla_{xy}\eta|\leq 1/(R-r)
. From the definition of
p
-capacity, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}_{p}(B_{1},B_{2})\leq\sum_{x,y\in V}|\nabla_{xy}\eta|^{p}\mu_{xy}\leq\frac{\mu(B_{2})}{(R-r)^{p}}.

\square

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume condition
(VD)
and
(P_{p})
is satisfied on
(V,\mu)
. For any
0<r<R<2r
, let
B_{1}=B(x_{0},r)
and
B_{2}=B(x_{0},R)
, then there exists a constant
C_{b}>0
such that

(3.2)

C_{b}\frac{\mu(B_{2})}{R^{p}}\leq\operatorname{cap}_{p}(B_{1},B_{2}).

Proof.

Let
u
be the
p
-potential for the capacitor
(B_{1},B_{2})
. Since
u\equiv 1
on
B_{1}
and
\operatorname{supp}(u)\subset B_{2}
, we apply
(VD)
and
(P_{p})
, then

\displaystyle 1\leq\frac{C_{D}}{\mu(B_{2})}\sum_{x\in B_{2}}u(x)\mu(x)\leq C_{D}C_{p}^{\prime}\left(\frac{R^{p}}{\mu(2B_{2})}\sum_{x,y\in 2B_{2}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}\mu_{xy}\right)^{1/p}.

Raising both sides to the power
p
, we obtain

\displaystyle C_{b}\frac{\mu(B_{2})}{R^{p}}\leq\sum_{x,y\in V}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}\mu_{xy}=\operatorname{cap}_{p}(B_{1},B_{2}),

where the constant
C_{b}=(C_{D}C_{p}^{\prime})^{-p}
.

\square

Lemma 3.3
.

For two disjoint non-empty subsets

\Omega

(3.3)

\displaystyle C_{p}(K,\Omega)=\sum_{xy\in\partial_{e}\Gamma_{t}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-1}\mu_{xy},

Remark 3.4
.

Lemma

p

represents the

-effective conductance between

\Omega

\sum_{xy\in\partial_{e}\Gamma_{t}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-1}\mu_{xy}

corresponds to the total effective

\partial_{e}\Gamma_{t}
. The equality (
3.3
) reflects the conservation of current: due to the
p
-harmonicity of the potential
u

K
and
\Omega

1

If
K=\Omega^{c}

3.3
) is obvious from the definition of
\operatorname{C}_{p}(K,\Omega)
. Now, let us assume
\mathbf{S}=\Omega^{c}\setminus K

Firstly, we assume that
\Omega^{c}
is finite, we have

\displaystyle 2D_{p}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y\in\overline{\mathbf{S}}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}\mu_{xy}=\sum_{x\in\mathbf{S}}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{S}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}\mu_{xy}+2\sum_{x\in\mathbf{S}}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{S}^{c}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}\mu_{xy}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbf{S}}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{S}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}\mu_{xy}+2\sum_{x\in\mathbf{S}}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{S}^{c}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}u)(u(y)-u(x))\mu_{xy}.

Since
u
is harmonic on
S

\sum_{x\in S}\Delta_{p}u(x)m(x)=0,

then, using the Green formula on

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbf{S}}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{S}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}\mu_{xy}=2\sum_{x\in\mathbf{S}}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{S}^{c}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}u)u(x)\mu_{xy}.

Substituting this into (
3

on
\Omega_{1}
and

on
\Omega^{c}
, we have

\displaystyle D_{p}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbf{S}}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{S}^{c}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}u)u(y)\mu_{xy}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbf{S}}\sum_{y\in K}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-1}\mu_{xy},

which implies (
3.3
) for

.

Similarly, for any
0\leq t<1
, we have

\sum_{x\in\Gamma_{t}\setminus\ K}\Delta_{p}u(x)\mu(x)=0,

where
\Gamma_{t}=\{x\in V:u(x)>t\}
.
Then, it follows that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Gamma_{t}\setminus K}\sum_{y\sim x}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{xy}u\mu_{xy}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Gamma_{t}\setminus K}\sum_{y\in K}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-1}\mu_{xy}-\sum_{x\in\Gamma_{t}\setminus K}\sum_{y\in\partial\Gamma_{t}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-1}\mu_{xy}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbf{S}}\sum_{y\in K}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-1}\mu_{xy}-\sum_{xy\in\partial_{e}\Gamma_{t}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-1}\mu_{xy}.

Combining this with (
3
), we have

\displaystyle D_{p}(u)=\sum_{xy\in\partial_{e}\Gamma_{t}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-1}\mu_{xy}.

Now for the proof when
\Omega^{c}
is infinite, we need the following claims.

Claim 1.

(3.6)

\displaystyle D_{p}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in V}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}\mu_{xy}=\int_{0}^{1}h_{p,t}(u)\,dt,

where
h_{p,t}(u)=\sum\limits_{xy\in\partial_{e}\Gamma_{t}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-1}\mu_{xy}
.

Indeed, for any edge
\xi=xy
, define
I_{\xi}=[u(x),u(y))
, assuming
u(y)\geq u(x)
(otherwise, switch the notation of
x
and
y
).
Clearly,
t\in I_{\xi}
is equivalent to
\xi\in\partial_{e}\Gamma_{t}
.

By Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{xy\in\partial_{e}\Gamma_{t}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-1}\mu_{xy}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{xy\in E}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-1}\mathbb{1}_{I_{xy}}\,\mu_{xy}\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{xy\in E}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-1}\mu_{xy}\,|I_{xy}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{xy\in E}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}\mu_{xy}=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in V}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}\mu_{xy},

which proves (
3.6
).

Claim 2.

h_{p,t}(u)\leq D_{p}(u)
.

First, we show that
u
is a limit of a sequence of finite-support functions.
For integers
n\geq 1
, let
v_{n}
denote the
p
-potential of
(K,\Omega,B^{n})
.
Then

D_{p}(v_{n})=C_{p}(K,\Omega;B^{n}).

By a diagonal argument, without loss generality, the sequence
\{v_{n}\}
has a pointwise limit function
v
.
Since

\operatorname{C}_{p}(K,\Omega;B^{n})\leq\operatorname{C}_{p}(K,\Omega;V)=\operatorname{C}_{p}(K,\Omega),

Fatou’s lemma gives

D_{p}(v)\leq\lim_{n=\infty}D_{p}(v_{n})\leq\operatorname{C}_{p}(K,\Omega).

Moreover, since
v\in\mathcal{A}(K,\Omega)
, equality holds,
D_{p}(v)=\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K,\Omega)
.
By the uniqueness of the potential function, we have
u=v
.

Let
F
be any finite subset of
\partial_{e}\Gamma_{t}
, for any
xy\in F
, assume
u(x)>t
and
u(y)\leq t
.

Since
F
is finite, there are some constant
t^{\prime}>t
such that
u(x)>t^{\prime}
and
u(y)<t^{\prime}
for any
xy\in F
.

From
v_{n}\to u
pointwisely, there exists
n_{1}>0
such that for any
n\geq n_{1}
,
v_{n}(x)>t^{\prime}
and
v_{n}(y)<t^{\prime}
.

It follows that
F\subset\partial_{e}\Gamma_{t^{\prime}}^{n}=\{\,x\in V:v_{n}(y)>t^{\prime}\,\}
for large enough
n
.

Moreover, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n_{2}>0
such that for all
n\geq n_{2}
,

\Big|\sum_{xy\in F}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-1}\mu_{xy}-\sum_{xy\in F}|\nabla_{xy}v_{n}|^{p-1}\mu_{xy}\Big|<\varepsilon.

Since
v_{n}
is the potential function for
(K,\Omega;B^{n})
and
B^{n}
is finite,

D_{p}(v_{n})=\sum_{xy\in\partial_{e}\Gamma_{t^{\prime}}^{n}}|\nabla_{xy}v_{n}|^{p-1}\mu_{xy}.

Therefore, for large enough
n
,

\displaystyle\sum_{xy\in F}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-1}\mu_{xy}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{xy\in F}|\nabla_{xy}v_{n}|^{p-1}\mu_{xy}+2\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{xy\in\partial_{e}\Gamma_{t^{\prime}}^{n}}|\nabla_{xy}v_{n}|^{p-1}\mu_{xy}+2\varepsilon

\displaystyle=D_{p}(v_{n})+2\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq D_{p}(u)+2\varepsilon.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
and
F\to\partial_{e}\Gamma_{t}
yields Claim 2.

Combining Claims 1 and 2, we obtain (
3.3
).

\square

Lemma 3.5
.

For
K\subset U
, assume that
u
is the
p
-potential function of
(K,U)
, let
\sigma=-\Delta_{p}u
, then

(3.7)

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K,U)=\sigma(K).

Proof.

From the definition of
\Delta_{p}
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma(K)=\sum_{x\in K}\sigma(x)m(x)=-\sum_{x\in K}\Delta_{p}u(x)m(x)=\sum_{x\in K}\sum_{y\in\partial K}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-1}\mu_{xy}.

Then, using Lemma
3.3
with
t=1
, we derive (
3.7
).

\square

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
K\subset U
and let
u
be the
p
-potential function of the condenser
(K,U)
. For any
0<\lambda<1
such that the level set
\Gamma_{\lambda}:=\{x\in V:u(x)>\lambda\}
satisfies
\overline{\Gamma_{\lambda}}\subset U
, the following holds,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\lambda^{p-1}\operatorname{cap}_{p}(\Gamma_{\lambda},U)\leq\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K,U)\leq\lambda^{p-1}\operatorname{cap}_{p}(\overline{\Gamma_{\lambda}},U).

Proof.

Let
v
be the
p
-potential function of
(\Gamma_{\lambda},U)
. By definition,
v=1
on
\Gamma_{\lambda}
and
v=0
on
\partial U
. Since
u>\lambda
on
\Gamma_{\lambda}
and
u=0
on
\partial U
, the comparison principle implies that
u\geq\lambda v
on
U\setminus\Gamma_{\lambda}
.

Applying Lemma
3.3
with
t=0
, we express the capacities as follows,

\displaystyle\lambda^{p-1}\operatorname{cap}_{p}(\Gamma_{\lambda},U)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in U}\sum_{y\in\partial U}(\lambda v(x))^{p-1}\mu_{xy},

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K,U)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in U}\sum_{y\in\partial U}u(x)^{p-1}\mu_{xy}.

It follows that
\lambda^{p-1}\operatorname{cap}_{p}(\Gamma_{\lambda},U)\leq\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K,U)
.

Conversely, let
v^{\prime}
be the
p
-potential function of
(\overline{\Gamma_{\lambda}},U)
. In this case,
v^{\prime}=1
on
\overline{\Gamma_{\lambda}}
and
u\leq\lambda
on
\partial\Gamma_{\lambda}
. A similar approach yields,

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K,U)\leq\lambda^{p-1}\operatorname{cap}_{p}(\overline{\Gamma_{\lambda}},U).

This completes the proof.

\square

4.
Theorems on Uniqueness and Liouville Property

In this section, for convenience, we call a function
u\in\ell(\overline{\Omega})
admissible on
\Omega
if
u=1
on
\partial\Omega
and
0<u<1
on
\Omega
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an infinite, connected, and locally finite graph. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\Omega\subset V
is a
p
-massive (resp.,
D_{p}
-massive) set.

(2)

For some (equivalently, all)
f\in\mathcal{B}(\partial\Omega)
, the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) admits more than one bounded (resp., finite
p
-energy) solution.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2):
Suppose
\Omega
is
p
-massive, and let
u
be an admissible
p
-harmonic function on
\Omega
. Let
v
be any bounded solution to the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) with boundary function
f\in\mathcal{B}(\partial\Omega)
. Without loss of generality, assume that
\inf_{x\in\partial\Omega}f(x)=0
. Define

a:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}v(x),\quad\text{and}\quad u^{\prime}:=2au.

For each integer
k\geq 1
, let
\Omega_{k}=B_{k}\cap\Omega
, where
B_{k}
is the ball of radius
k
centered at a fixed vertex in
\Omega
. Define the boundary function
F_{k}\colon\partial\Omega_{k}\to\mathbb{R}
by

F_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x),&\text{if }x\in\partial\Omega\cap\partial\Omega_{k},\\
2a,&\text{if }x\in\partial\Omega_{k}\setminus\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

By Lemma
2.6
, the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}w=0,&\text{in }\Omega_{k},\\
w=F_{k},&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{k}\end{cases}

admits a unique solution
v_{k}
.

Since
0\leq u^{\prime}\leq F_{k}\leq 2a
on
\partial\Omega_{k}
, the comparison principle implies

0\leq u^{\prime}\leq v_{k}\leq 2a\quad\text{in }\Omega_{k}.

As the sequence of domains
\{\Omega_{k}\}
is increasing, the comparison principle also implies that the sequence
\{v_{k}\}
is monotonically decreasing pointwise. Define

v^{\prime}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}v_{k}.

Then
v^{\prime}
is a bounded solution of (
1.1
). By the definition of
u^{\prime}
, since
u
is admissible, there exists some
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that

v(x_{0})<u^{\prime}(x_{0})\leq v^{\prime}(x_{0}),

which shows that
v
and
v^{\prime}
are distinct solutions to (
1.1
).

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1):
Assume that
f\equiv 0
and that the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) admits a nonconstant bounded solution
u
. If
u\leq 0
on
\Omega
, then the function
-u/(\inf_{x\in\Omega}u)
is a nonnegative admissible
p
-harmonic function on
\Omega
, and hence
\Omega
is
p
-massive. Therefore, we may assume

a:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}u(x)>0.

Proceeding similarly, for each
k\geq 1
, define
\Omega_{k}=B_{k}\cap\Omega
and set

F_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }x\in\partial\Omega\cap\partial\Omega_{k},\\
a,&\text{if }x\in\partial\Omega_{k}\setminus\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Solving the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}w=0,&\text{in }\Omega_{k},\\
w=F_{k},&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{k},\end{cases}

we obtain a monotonically decreasing sequence of solutions
\{v_{k}\}
satisfying

0\leq u\leq v_{k}\leq a\quad\text{in }\Omega_{k}.

Define the pointwise limit

v:=\lim_{k\to\infty}v_{k},

which is a nonnegative solution of (
1.1
). Then the normalized function
v/a
is an admissible
p
-harmonic function on
\Omega
. Thus,
\Omega
is
p
-massive.

\square

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an infinite, connected, and locally finite graph. Then
G
admits a nonconstant bounded
p
-harmonic function (resp., with finite
p
-energy) if and only if there exist two disjoint
p
-massive (resp.,
D_{p}
-massive) subsets of
V
.

Proof.

Suppose
u
is a nonconstant bounded
p
-harmonic function on
V
. Choose a real number
a
such that
\inf_{x\in V}u(x)<a<\sup_{x\in V}u(x)
, and define the level sets

\Omega_{1}:=\{x\in V:u(x)>a\},\qquad\Omega_{2}:=\{x\in V:u(x)<a\}.

These sets are clearly disjoint and nonempty due to the choice of
a
and the non-triviality of
u
.

For each integer
k\geq 1
, let
\{B_{k}\}
be an exhaustion of
V
, and define
\Omega_{1}^{k}:=B_{k}\cap\Omega_{1}
. Define the boundary function
F_{k}\colon\partial\Omega_{1}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
by

F_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}a,&\text{if }x\in\partial\Omega_{1}\cap\partial\Omega_{1}^{k},\\
u(x),&\text{if }x\in\partial\Omega_{1}^{k}\setminus\partial\Omega_{1}.\end{cases}

Consider the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}w=0,&\text{in }\Omega_{1}^{k},\\
w=F_{k},&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{1}^{k}.\end{cases}

By Lemma
2.6
, this problem admits a unique solution, denoted by
v_{k}
. Applying the comparison principle, we have

a\leq u(x)\leq v_{k}(x)\leq b:=\sup_{y\in\Omega_{1}}u(y),\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}.

By a diagonal argument, the sequence
\{v_{k}\}
admits a pointwise convergent subsequence
\{v_{k_{i}}\}
such that

v:=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{v_{k_{i}}-a}{b-a}

exists and is an admissible
p
-harmonic function on
\Omega_{1}
. Therefore,
\Omega_{1}
is
p
-massive. By a similar argument,
\Omega_{2}
is also
p
-massive.

We now prove that if
D_{p}(u)<\infty
, then
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are also
D_{p}
-massive.

For convenience, define the sets

\Gamma_{1}^{k}:=\partial\Omega_{1}^{k}\cap\partial\Omega_{1}
,

\Gamma_{2}^{k}:=\partial\Omega_{1}^{k}\setminus\partial\Omega_{1}
,
and

E_{k}=\{xy\in E\mid x\in\Omega_{1}^{k},y\in\overline{\Omega_{1}^{k}}\}.

Using Green’s formula on
v_{k}
, we compute the
p
-Dirichlet energy:

\displaystyle D_{k}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{xy\in E_{k}}|\nabla_{xy}v_{k}|^{p}\mu_{xy}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\partial\Omega_{1}^{k}}|\nabla_{xy}v_{k}|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}v_{k})^{2}\mu_{xy}+\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}|\nabla_{xy}v_{k}|^{p}\mu_{xy}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\partial\Omega_{1}^{k}}|\nabla_{xy}v_{k}|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}v_{k})v_{k}(y)\mu_{xy}-\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}v_{k}(x)\Delta_{p}v_{k}(x)\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\partial\Omega_{1}^{k}}|\nabla_{xy}v_{k}|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}v_{k})v_{k}(y)\mu_{xy}.

Using the definitions of
\Gamma_{1}^{k}
and
\Gamma_{2}^{k}
, we split the sum:

(4.1)

\displaystyle D_{k}

\displaystyle=a\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\Gamma_{1}^{k}}|\nabla_{xy}v_{k}|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}v_{k})\mu_{xy}+\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\Gamma_{2}^{k}}|\nabla_{xy}v_{k}|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}v_{k})u(y)\mu_{xy}.

On the other hand, since
v_{k}
is
p
-harmonic on
\Omega_{1}^{k}
, we have

\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\Delta_{p}v_{k}(x)\mu(x)=0.

From the definition of
\Delta_{p}
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\sum_{y\sim x}\mu_{xy}|\nabla_{xy}v_{k}|^{p-2}\nabla_{xy}v_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\mu_{xy}|\nabla_{xy}v_{k}|^{p-2}\nabla_{xy}v_{k}+\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\partial\Omega_{1}^{k}}\mu_{xy}|\nabla_{xy}v_{k}|^{p-2}\nabla_{xy}v_{k}.

Since the first term is antisymmetric in
x
and
y
, it vanishes, and we obtain

\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\Gamma_{1}^{k}}\mu_{xy}|\nabla_{xy}v_{k}|^{p-2}\nabla_{xy}v_{k}=-\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\Gamma_{2}^{k}}\mu_{xy}|\nabla_{xy}v_{k}|^{p-2}\nabla_{xy}v_{k}.

Substituting this into equation (
4.1
), we get

D_{k}=\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\Gamma_{2}^{k}}|\nabla_{xy}v_{k}|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}v_{k})(u(y)-a)\mu_{xy}.

Now consider the Dirichlet energy of
u
. Using a similar computation, we obtain

\displaystyle D_{p}(u)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{xy\in E_{k}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}\mu_{xy}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\Gamma_{1}^{k}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}u)u(y)\mu_{xy}+\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\Gamma_{2}^{k}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}u)u(y)\mu_{xy}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\Gamma_{2}^{k}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}u)(u(y)-a)\mu_{xy},

where we used the fact that
u(y)\leq a
and
u(x)>a
for all
x\in\Omega_{1}
and
y\in\partial\Omega_{1}
such that
\mu_{xy}>0
.

Since
u\leq v_{k}
on
\partial\Omega_{1}^{k}\cap\partial\Omega_{1}
and
v_{k}=u
on
\partial\Omega_{1}^{k}\setminus\partial\Omega_{1}
, we have
v_{k}\geq u
on
\Omega_{1}^{k}
, which implies that

|\nabla_{xy}v_{k}|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}v_{k})\mu_{xy}\leq|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}u)\mu_{xy},\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}\text{ and }y\in\Gamma_{2}^{k}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle D_{p}(u)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\Gamma_{2}^{k}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}u)(u(y)-a)\mu_{xy}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x\in\Omega_{1}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\Gamma_{2}^{k}}|\nabla_{xy}v_{k}|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}v_{k})(u(y)-a)\mu_{xy}=D_{k}.

Finally, by Fatou’s lemma, we obtain

D_{p}(v)\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\frac{D_{k_{i}}}{(b-a)^{p}}\leq\frac{D_{p}(u)}{(b-a)^{p}}<\infty,

which shows that
v
has finite
p
-energy. Therefore,
\Omega_{1}
is
D_{p}
-massive. The same argument applies to
\Omega_{2}
.

Conversely, assume that
\Omega
and
W
are two disjoint
p
-massive subsets of
V
, with corresponding admissible
p
-harmonic functions
u
and
w
, respectively. Let
\{B_{k}\}
be an exhaustion of
V
. Define

\Omega_{m}:=B_{m}\cup\Omega,\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega_{m}^{k}:=B_{k}\cap\Omega_{m}.

As before, define the boundary function

F(x):=\begin{cases}u(x),&\text{if }x\in\Omega,\\
0,&\text{if }x\in V\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

We consider the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}w=0,&\text{in }\Omega_{m}^{k},\\
w=F,&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{m}^{k},\end{cases}

and let
v_{m}^{k}
denote its unique solution.

By the comparison principle on the region
\Omega_{m}^{k}
, we have
0\leq v_{m}^{k}\leq 1
in
\Omega_{m}^{k}
. Since
v_{m}^{k}\geq u
on
\partial(\Omega_{m}^{k}\cap\Omega)
, it follows that
v_{m}^{k}\geq u
on
\Omega_{m}^{k}\cap\Omega
. Furthermore, applying the comparison principle again on
\Omega_{m}^{k-1}
, we obtain the monotonicity
v_{m}^{k-1}\leq v_{m}^{k}
. We then define the limit

v_{m}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}v_{m}^{k}.

Clearly,
0\leq v_{m}\leq 1
on
\Omega_{m}
, and
v_{m}\geq u
on
\Omega
. Moreover,
v_{m}
satisfies

\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}v_{m}=0,&\text{in }\Omega_{m},\\
v_{m}=F,&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{m}.\end{cases}

Assume
m
is large enough so that
B_{m}\cap W\neq\emptyset
. Then, applying the comparison principle to the region
B_{m}\cap W
, and noting that
F=0
on
W
, we obtain
v_{m}\leq 1-w
. We can then define the limit

v:=\lim_{m\to\infty}v_{m}.

It follows that
v
is a bounded
p
-harmonic function on
V
with
v\geq u
on
\Omega
and
v\leq 1-w
on
W
, meaning
v
is not a constant function.

It remains to prove that if
D_{p}(u)<\infty
and
D_{p}(w)<\infty
, then
D_{p}(v)<\infty
. Using the same Green’s formula argument as above, we obtain

\displaystyle D_{p}(v_{m}^{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Omega_{m}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\partial\Omega_{m}^{k}\cap\Omega}|\nabla_{xy}v_{m}^{k}|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}v_{m}^{k})u(y)\mu_{xy}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in\Omega_{m}^{k}}\sum_{y\in\partial\Omega_{m}^{k}\cap\Omega}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}u)u(y)\mu_{xy}\leq D_{p}(u)<\infty,

which implies that
D_{p}(v)\leq D_{p}(u)<\infty
by Fatou’s lemma, completing the proof.

\square

Remark 4.3
.

Note that the proof of Theorem
4.2
establishes the following property: if
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}\subset V
, then the

-massiveness) of

implies the

-massiveness (resp.,

-massiveness) of

.

5.
criterion for
p

Definition 5.1
.

We say that a set

is

-thick at a point

if, for any positive

-superharmonic function

on

, the condition

on

.

Proposition 5.2
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\Omega
is a
p
-massive set.

(2)

There exists some
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that
\Omega^{c}
(equivalently,
\partial\Omega
) is not
p
-thick at

.

(3)

If
\Omega
is connected, then
\Omega^{c}
(equivalently,
\partial\Omega
) is not

-thick at any point in

.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: This implication follows immediately from the definitions.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
: Assume there exists a positive
p
-superharmonic function
u
on
V
such that

on
\Omega^{c}
and
u(x_{0})<1
for some
x_{0}\in\Omega
.

Similar to the argument in Section

, for each integer

, define
\Omega_{k}=B_{k}\cap\Omega
, where
B_{k}
is the ball of radius

centered at a fixed vertex. Define the boundary function

by

F_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }x\in\partial\Omega\cap\partial\Omega_{k},\\
0,&\text{if }x\in\partial\Omega_{k}\setminus\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Since

on
\Omega^{c}
and

, we clearly have
F_{k}\leq u
on
\partial\Omega_{k}
.

Solving the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}w=0,&\text{in }\Omega_{k},\\
w=F_{k},&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{k},\end{cases}

we obtain a unique solution
v_{k}
. By the comparison principle, the sequence
\{v_{k}\}
is monotonically decreasing and satisfies
0\leq v_{k}\leq 1
as well as
v_{k}\leq u
in
\Omega_{k}

Theorem 6.1
.

Let

Proof.

A=\mathbb{Z}\times\{0\}^{d-1}
denote a one-dimensional coordinate axis in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. The massiveness of its complement
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A
depends strictly on the dimension
d
.
If
d=p+1
, then
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A
is not
p
-massive.
However, if
d>p+1
, then
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A
is
p
-massive but not
D_{p}
-massive.

Proof.

Let
B_{n}=B(o,2^{n})
be the ball centered at the origin with radius
r_{n}=2^{n}
, and let
A_{n}=A\cap B_{n}
. We use Theorem
5.7
with (
5.2
); that is,
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A
is
p
-massive if and only if

(7.7)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(2^{n(p-d)}\operatorname{cap}_{p}(A_{n},B_{n})\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}<\infty.

First, for
d=p+1
, we calculate
\operatorname{cap}_{p}(\{x_{0}\},B^{\prime}_{n})
on
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
, where
x_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
and
B^{\prime}_{n}=B(x_{0},2^{n})
on
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
.

Let
u_{n}
be the potential function of
(\{x_{0}\},B^{\prime}_{n+1})
. By Lemma
3.3
with
t=1
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}^{\prime}_{p}(\{x_{0}\},B^{\prime}_{n+1})=D^{\prime}_{p}(u_{n})=-\mu(x_{0})\Delta^{\prime}_{p}u_{n}(x_{0}):=c_{n}.

Letting
v_{n}:=c_{n}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}u_{n}
, we have
-\mu(x_{0})\Delta_{p}v_{n}(x_{0})=1
. Then, applying Lemma
5.11
, we obtain

\displaystyle v_{n}(x_{0})\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\mu^{\prime}(B_{n})}\sum_{x\in B_{n}}u(x)^{p}\right)^{1/p}+\sum^{n+1}_{i=1}\left(\frac{r_{i}^{p}}{\mu^{\prime}(B_{i})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Since a direct calculation shows that the
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality
(P_{1})
implies the
(p,p)
-Poincaré inequality:

\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\sum_{x\in B}|f(x)-f_{B}|^{p}\mu(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq Cr\left(\frac{1}{\mu(2B)}\sum_{x,y\in 2B}|f(y)-f(x)|^{p}\mu_{xy}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B^{\prime}_{n})}\sum_{x\in B^{\prime}_{n}}u(x)^{p}\mu(x)\right)^{1/p}\lesssim\left(\frac{r^{p}_{n}}{\mu(B^{\prime}_{n})}\sum_{x,y\in B^{\prime}_{n+1}}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}\mu(x)\right)^{1/p}\asymp D^{\prime}_{p}(v_{n})=1,

and
\sum\limits^{n+1}_{i=1}\left(\frac{r_{i}^{p}}{\mu^{\prime}(B_{i})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\asymp n
, therefore

\displaystyle c_{n}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}u_{n}(x_{0})=v_{n}(x_{0})\lesssim n,

which implies that

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}^{\prime}_{p}(\{x_{0}\},B^{\prime}_{n})\geq c_{n}\gtrsim n^{1-p}.

Using the same argument as in (
7.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}_{p}(A_{n},B_{n})\gtrsim 2^{n}n^{1-p}.

Substituting this into (
7.7
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(2^{n(p-d)}\operatorname{cap}_{p}(A_{n},B_{n})\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\gtrsim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-1}.

Hence,
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A
is not
p
-massive for
d=p+1
.

For
d>p+1
,
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A
is
p
-massive by Example
7.3
.

For
D_{p}
massiveness, we derive it by example
7.5
.

\square

Example 7.5
.

Let
p>1
and
M\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. If
\mu(M^{c})=\infty
, then
M
is not
D_{p}
-massive.

Proof.

If
d\leq p
, then
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is
p
-parabolic, which immediately implies that
M
is not
D_{p}
-massive.

If
d>p
, we define the half-spaces
\Omega_{1}:=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:x_{1}\leq 0\}
and
\Omega_{2}:=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:x_{1}\geq 0\}
. Since
\mu(M^{c})=\infty
, at least one of the intersections
M^{c}\cap\Omega_{1}
or
M^{c}\cap\Omega_{2}
must have infinite measure. Without loss of generality, assume that
A:=M^{c}\cap\Omega_{1}
has infinite measure, and let
A_{n}=A\cap B_{n}
. Using Lemma
7.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{C}_{p}(A,\Omega_{1})\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{C}_{p}(A_{n},\Omega_{1})\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{C}_{p}(A_{n})\gtrsim\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(A_{n})^{\frac{d-p}{d}}=\infty.

Since
\Omega_{1}
is not
p
-parabolic, the conclusion follows by applying Lemma
6.1
.

\square
