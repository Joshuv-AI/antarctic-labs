Minimizing Inequity in Facility Location Games
Yuhang Guo, Houyu Zhou

Abstract
This paper studies the problem of minimizing group-level inequity in facility location games on the real line, where agents belong to different groups and may act strategically. We explore a fairness-oriented objective that minimizes the maximum group effect introduced by Marsh and Schilling (1994). Each group’s effect is defined as its total or maximum distance to the nearest facility, weighted by group-specific factors. We show that this formulation generalizes several prominent optimization objectives, including the classical utilitarian (social cost) and egalitarian (maximum cost) objectives, as well as two group-fair objectives, maximum total and average group cost. In order to minimize the maximum group effect, we first propose two novel mechanisms for the single-facility case, the BALANCED mechanism and the MAJOR-PHANTOM mechanism. Both are strategyproof and achieve tight approximation guarantees under distinct formulations of the maximum group effect objective. Our mechanisms not only close the existing gap in approximation bounds for group-fairness objectives identified by Zhou, Li, and Chan (2022), but also unify many classical truthful mechanisms within a broader fairness-aware framework. For the two-facility case, we revisit and extend the classical endpoint mechanism to our generalized setting and demonstrate that it provides tight bounds for two distinct maximum group effect objectives.

Introduction

Facility Location Games (FLGs), which study how to locate facilities based on agents’ preferences, have been extensively explored over the past two decades. Most prior work in this area has prioritized efficiency, typically aiming to minimize the total cost incurred by agents in accessing services. Such efficiency-driven approaches achieve optimal social welfare, however, at the expense of fairness and equity. In particular, mechanisms designed purely for efficiency tend to favor majority groups, leaving disadvantaged or minority populations marginalized.
Recognizing these limitations, recent research has increasingly focused on incorporating fairness into facility location games. These efforts span a spectrum from
individual fairness
, which aims to equalize costs across agents
[
2
,
15
]
, to
group fairness
, which ensures equitable treatment across predefined groups
[
10
,
16
,
1
]
. A seminal contribution by
10
introduced various equity metrics, including the “center” objective, which seeks to
minimize the maximum group effect
. As they note,

“
This is the earliest and most frequently used measure that has an equity component.
”

This underscores the significance of the center objective in equity-aware location analysis. However, many subsequent studies have adopted a narrow interpretation of this objective, often modeling it as the maximum individual distance across all agents, thereby overlooking group structures and alternative definitions of group-level costs. In contrast,
10
proposed a broader formulation, in which the effect of a group,
E_{i}
, could be defined in terms such as the
total distance
incurred by all agents in group
i
. This generalization more accurately reflects the
collective burden
borne by each group, offering a richer fairness perspective.
Motivated by this observation, we revisit the general objective of minimizing
\max_{i}E_{i}
and study its implications within the framework of facility location games.

In this paper, we focus on the objective of minimizing the maximum group effect, where the effect
E_{i}
of a group
i
is defined as either the total or maximum distance from its agents to their nearest facility, multiplied by a weight
w_{i}
, capturing group-specific priorities, such as socioeconomic status or policy-driven importance (see
Section

2
for formal definitions). Our objective of minimizing
\max_{i}E_{i}
emphasizes group-level fairness by bounding the worst-case burden among all groups. Unlike traditional formulations that protect only the most distant individual, our model accounts for the collective experience of each group. This perspective aligns with Rawlsian principles
[
14
]
, which advocate prioritizing the welfare of the most disadvantaged. We aim to design new strategyproof mechanisms to this group-centric fairness objective.

1.1
Related Work

FLGs have received significant attention in the literature over the past decades, particularly following the influential work of
13
. For an overview of the diverse models, we refer readers to the comprehensive survey by
3
. In the remainder of this section, we focus specifically on research that investigates fairness notions within the context of facility location games.

There is a rich body of work studying fairness considerations in facility location problems from the optimization perspective. Early studies in the
operations research
community explored various equity-based fairness measures, including the standard deviation of distances
[
11
]
and the Gini coefficient
[
10
]
. In the context of algorithmic mechanism design, the pioneering work of
13
introduced the notion of individual fairness through the maximum cost objective, i.e., minimizing the maximum individual distance from any agent to the facility, and proposed strategyproof mechanisms that approximately optimize this objective. Building on this foundation, later research proposed alternative formulations of individual fairness.
2
studied the minimax envy objective, which captures fairness via the maximum difference in distances between any pair of agents; this framework was subsequently extended to the two-facility setting by
4
.
5
introduced the envy ratio objective, adapted from the fair division problem, which measures the ratio of the best-off agent’s utility to that of the worst-off agent. This concept was further extended to the multi-facility case by
9
.
15
studied the Gini index objective and proposed strategyproof mechanisms.

Fairness notions have also been extended from individuals to groups of agents.
16
,
8
investigated two group-based fairness objectives: the
maximum total cost
(
\mathsf{mtgc}
) and
maximum average cost
(
\mathsf{magc}
), each capturing the worst-case burden across predefined groups of agents.

1
introduced a model of proportional fairness, in which fairness guarantees are provided to endogenously defined groups of agents, and the strength of the guarantee scales proportionally with group size. This concept was further extended by
7
to the setting of obnoxious facility location, where the facility imposes disutility rather than providing benefit.

Roadmap

In Section 4, we extend our analysis to the multi-facility setting and revisit the classical

2.2
Maximum Group Effect

Given the requirement of strategyproofness, our goal is to design mechanisms that minimize inequity, as captured by the maximum group effect objective, aligning with the central notion proposed by
10
.
Formally, for any profile
\boldsymbol{\theta}
, the objective is to minimize the
maximum group effect
(
\mathsf{mge}
), defined as

\displaystyle\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))=\max_{j\in[m]}E_{j}.

We further interpret the
maximum group effect

E_{j}
for each group
j
in both
utilitarian
and
egalitarian
manners, incorporating the group-specific weight
w_{j}
in both formulations. Specifically, one is termed
Weighted Total Group Cost
(
{\tt wTGC}
),

E_{j}=w_{j}\cdot\sum_{i\in G_{j}}c(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}),x_{i})
, corresponding to the weighted sum of distances from all agents in group
G_{j}
to their assigned facilities. The other is termed
Weighted Maximum Group Cost
(
{\tt wMGC}
), i.e.,
E_{j}=w_{j}\cdot\max_{i\in G_{j}}c(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}),x_{i})
, representing the weighted maximum distance among agents in group
G_{j}
.
\mathsf{mge}
prioritizes fairness by limiting the worst-case weighted burden among groups, aligning with principles of equitable resource allocation.

For any strategyproof mechanism, we evaluate the performance by the
approximation ratio
, defined as the worst-case ratio (over all possible instances) between the maximum group effect produced by the mechanism and the optimal solution.

Definition 2.2

(Approximation Ratio)
.

For any mechanism
f
, the approximation ratio is:

\displaystyle\rho=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Theta^{n}}\frac{\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))}{\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathsf{OPT}(\boldsymbol{\theta}))},

where
\mathsf{OPT}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
is the optimal facility placement that minimizes
\mathsf{mge}
objective under the profile
\boldsymbol{\theta}
.

In the following sections, we focus on deterministic strategyproof mechanisms that optimize the
\mathsf{mge}
objective. We begin with the single-facility setting (
k=1
) and then extend our analysis to the multi-facility scenario.

Single-Facility Mechanism Design

We begin by considering single-facility setting (
k=1
). For the
{\tt wTGC}
group effect metric, i.e.,
E_{j}=w_{j}\cdot\sum_{i\in G_{j}}c(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}),x_{i})
, we propose the
BALANCED
mechanism which places the facility at the location under which the maximum weighted values are balanced. Regarding the
{\tt wMGC}

E_{j}=w_{j}\cdot\max_{i\in G_{j}}c(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}),x_{i})

MAJOR-PHANTOM
mechanism, which places the facility at the median-point of the locations of agents in the group

3.1
Weighted Total Group Cost

We first look at the weighted total group cost (
{\tt wTGC}
) objective, where
E_{j}=w_{j}\cdot\sum_{i\in G_{j}}c(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}),x_{i})

\displaystyle E_{j}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))-E_{j}(y^{*})=\int_{f(\boldsymbol{\theta})}^{y^{*}}w_{j}\cdot(R_{j}(y)-L_{j}(y))\mathrm{d}y.

5
Conclusion and Discussion

We study facility location games through the lens of fairness by introducing a unified framework based on the
maximum group effect
, a general metric that encompasses a broad class of classical objectives. In the single-facility setting, we develop two strategyproof mechanisms,
BALANCED
and
MAJOR-PHANTOM
, both of which achieve tight approximation guarantees for minimizing the maximum group effect. Our results further close the open approximation gaps for group-fairness objectives identified by
16
. In the two-facility setting, we revisit the classical
ENDPOINT
mechanism and establish tight approximation bounds. Looking forward, promising research directions include extending our framework to randomized mechanisms to circumvent the impossibility of achieving bounded approximations for
k\geq 3
, as well as adapting the
\mathsf{mge}
objective to higher dimensional metric spaces.

A.1
Weighted Total Group Cost

We begin by considering the weighted group cost
({\tt wTGC})
objective, defined as
E_{j}=w_{j}\cdot\sum_{i\in G_{j}}c(f(\mathbf{\theta}),x_{i})
. We slightly abuse notation by using the functions
L_{j}(y)
and
R_{j}(y)
to denote the number of agents located to the left of any location
y
and to the right of
y
for any group
G_{j}\in\mathcal{G}
, respectively. Throughout, let
w_{\max}
and
w_{\min}
denotes the maximum weight and the minimum group weights.

Proposition A.1
.

The
MEDIAN
mechanism has an approximation ratio of
1+(m-1)\cdot\frac{w_{\max}}{w_{\min}}
for minimizing
\mathsf{mge}
when
E_{j}=w_{j}\cdot\sum_{i\in G_{j}}c(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}),x_{i})
.

Proof.

Consider an arbitrary profile
\boldsymbol{\theta}
. Let
f(\boldsymbol{\theta})
be the output of the
MEDIAN
mechanism and
y^{*}
be the optimal facility location. Without loss of generality, assume that
f(\boldsymbol{\theta})<y^{*}
and that
\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))
is attained by some group
G_{g}
. We first claim that, the value of
\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))
is non-decreasing, while
\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},y^{*})
is non-increasing when all the agents located between
f(\boldsymbol{\theta})
and
y^{*}
are relocated to
y^{*}
. Consequently, the approximation ratio of
f(\boldsymbol{\theta})
under
\boldsymbol{\theta}
is upper-bounded by that of the resulting instance after the relocation. Henceforth, we restrict attention to instances in which no agent is located strictly between
f(\boldsymbol{\theta})
and
y^{*}
.

Note that
\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))=w_{g}\sum_{i\in G_{g}}d(x_{i},f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))
and by the definition
\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},y^{*})\geq w_{g}\sum_{i\in G_{g}}d(x_{i},y^{*})
. We derive the difference between the two terms by

\displaystyle\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))-\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},y^{*})

\displaystyle\leq w_{g}\sum_{i\in G_{g}}d(x_{i},f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))-w_{g}\sum_{i\in G_{g}}d(x_{i},y^{*})

\displaystyle\leq w_{g}(|R_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|-|L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|)(y^{*}-f(\boldsymbol{\theta})).

Observe that the
\mathsf{mge}
objective is at least the sum of the costs of agents in the same group that are positioned to the left of
f(\boldsymbol{\theta})
. We have

\displaystyle\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},y^{*})\geq\max_{j\in[m]}w_{j}|L_{j}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|(y^{*}-f(\boldsymbol{\theta})).

We next discuss by cases.

1.

When

L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))=0
, there are at most
m-1
groups on the left of
f(\boldsymbol{\theta})
. By the definition of the
MEDIAN
mechanism, there are at least
\frac{n}{2}
agents on the left of
f(\boldsymbol{\theta})
and at most
\frac{n}{2}
agents on the right of
f(\boldsymbol{\theta})
. Hence, we have
\max_{j\in[m]}|L_{j}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|\geq\frac{n}{2(m-1)}
and
|R_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|\leq\frac{n}{2}
. Therefore, we can bound the approximation ratio by

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))}{\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},y^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},y^{*})+w_{g}\cdot(|R_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|-|L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta})|)(y^{*}-f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))}{\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},y^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},y^{*})+w_{g}\cdot|R_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|(y^{*}-f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))}{\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},y^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{w_{g}\cdot|R_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|(y^{*}-f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))}{\max_{j\in[m]}w_{j}\cdot|L_{j}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|(y^{*}-f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{w_{g}\cdot\frac{n}{2}}{\max_{j\in[m]}w_{j}\cdot\frac{n}{2(m-1)}}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{w_{g}\cdot(m-1)}{\max_{j\in[m]}w_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq 1+(m-1)\cdot\frac{w_{\max}}{w_{\min}}.

2.

When

L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))>0
, we first rewrite the approximation ratio by

\displaystyle\rho=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))}{\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},y^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},y^{*})+w_{g}(|R_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|-|L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|)(y^{*}-f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))}{\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},y^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\frac{w_{g}(|R_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|-|L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|)(y^{*}-f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))}{\max_{j\in[m]}w_{j}|L_{j}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|(y^{*}-f(\boldsymbol{\theta})).}

After that, we further consider two sub-cases by discussing whether the group
G_{g}
achieves
\max_{j\in[m]}|L_{j}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|
.

(a)

When
\max_{j\in[m]}|L_{j}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|
is achieved by group
g
.
By the definition of the
MEDIAN
mechanism, there are at least
\frac{n}{2}
agents on the left of
f(\boldsymbol{\theta})
and at most
\frac{n}{2}
agents on the right of
f(\boldsymbol{\theta})
. Since there are at most
m
groups, by the pigeonhole principle, we have
|L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|\geq\frac{\frac{n}{2}}{m}
. We have the approximation ratio

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{w_{g}(|R_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|-|L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|)(y^{*}-f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))}{w_{g}|L_{j}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|(y^{*}-f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\left(|R_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|-|L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|\right)}{|L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|R_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|}{|L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|}\leq\frac{\frac{n}{2}}{\frac{\frac{n}{2}}{m}}=m.

(b)

When
\max_{j\in[m]}|L_{j}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|
is not achieved by group
g
.
By the pigeonhole principle, we derive that

\displaystyle\max_{j\in[m]}|L_{j}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|\geq\frac{\frac{n}{2}-|L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|}{m-1}.

With the inequality in hand, we upper-bound the approximation ratio by

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{w_{g}(|R_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|-|L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|)(y^{*}-f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))}{\max_{j\in[m]}w_{j}|L_{j}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|(y^{*}-f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{w_{g}\left(|R_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|-|L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|\right)}{\max_{j\in[m]}w_{j}\frac{\frac{n}{2}-|L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|}{m-1}}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{w_{\max}}{w_{\min}}\cdot\frac{\frac{n}{2}-|L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|}{\frac{\frac{n}{2}-|L_{g}(f(\boldsymbol{\theta}))|}{m-1}}

\displaystyle\leq 1+(m-1)\cdot\frac{w_{\max}}{w_{\min}}.

Lastly, we provide an example to show that our analysis for the approximation ratio of
MEDIAN

m-1
agents from different groups
G_{2},\dots,G_{m}
with weight
w_{\min}
are located at
0
and the other
m-1

G_{1}
with weight

are located at
1
. The

0

\mathsf{mge}
of
(m-1)\cdot w_{\max}
, while locating the facility at

of
\frac{(m-1)\cdot w_{\max}\cdot w_{\min}}{(m-1)\cdot w_{\max}+w_{\min}}

∎

y^{*}
:

\displaystyle w_{1}|y^{*}-x_{1}|=w_{1}\left(\frac{w_{2}x_{2}+w_{1}x_{1}}{w_{1}+w_{2}}-x_{1}\right)=\frac{w_{1}w_{2}(x_{2}-x_{1})}{w_{1}+w_{2}},

and

\displaystyle w_{2}|y^{*}-x_{2}|=w_{2}\left(x_{2}-\frac{w_{2}x_{2}+w_{1}x_{1}}{w_{1}+w_{2}}\right)=\frac{w_{1}w_{2}(x_{2}-x_{1})}{w_{1}+w_{2}}.

Therefore, we have
\mathsf{mge}(\boldsymbol{\theta},y^{*})=\frac{w_{1}w_{2}(x_{2}-x_{1})}{w_{1}+w_{2}}
. This completes the proof.
∎
