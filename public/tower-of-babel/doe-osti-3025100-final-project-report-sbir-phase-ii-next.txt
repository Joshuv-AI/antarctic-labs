 1. Executive Summary
Intergrid, LLC, based in Temple, New Hampshire, conducted an 24-month DOE SBIR Phase II research
program to develop next-generation electronic power inverters and converters for the United States
distributed wind (DW) market. The distributed wind segment is defined as turbines rated from 10 kW to
1 MW, a market segment that has been almost entirely blocked by the absence of UL1741-certified,
commercially available inverters.

The project addressed this gap through five integrated technical thrusts: (1) power stage hardware
development from 10 to 150 kW using Silicon Carbide (SiC) technology, (2) advanced digital controller
design capable of 16-bit PWM resolution at 100 kHz, (3) certification to UL1741, UL1998, and IEEE 1547
standards, (4) design for grid-connected, stand-alone, and microgrid operation, and (5) investigation of
auxiliary inverters to allow induction-generator turbines to meet grid interconnection requirements.

The project achieved its most critical milestone when Intergrid's commercial IG25 inverter received
UL1741 "Grid-Interactive" certification from Intertek in July 2023, the first UL1741-certified inverter
commercially available for the US distributed wind market. Concurrently, the first commercial shipment
of a Bergey Excel 15 turbine with Intergrid electronics occurred in August 2023, marking the launch of
commercial production. More than 200 Bergey 15 kW turbines with Intergrid electronics had shipped by
December 2025.

On the hardware development side, a new Silicon Carbide power board and gate driver board for a next-
generation 25 kW (NG-25) inverter were designed, fabricated, and placed into initial testing. The new
design uses third-generation Wolfspeed SiC modules that cost one-quarter of the previous-generation
components, demonstrating a clear path to substantially lower inverter costs. A TI TMS320F28379D
DSP-based controller adapter board was also designed and fabricated to support code porting from the
existing Microchip platform.

Progress toward UL1741-SB ("Grid Support" or "Supplement B") certification was advanced through test
automation development, laboratory equipment procurement, Sunspec and IEEE 2030.5
communications implementation, and negotiation of a "Satellite Class 2" testing arrangement with
Intertek. Work toward UL1998 functional safety software certification was initiated.

 2. Problem Statement and Market Opportunity

2.1 The Problem
At the outset of the project, there were no UL1741-certified, commercially available inverters or turbine
AC-DC converters suitable for the US distributed wind market. This certification gap represented a total
impediment to the growth of DW in the United States, preventing turbine manufacturers from selling
equipment and installers from obtaining utility interconnection agreements.

The root causes of this market failure were multi-dimensional. The market size was too small for most
power electronics manufacturers to justify the high cost of wind-specific product certification. Certification
of a single inverter model to UL1741 and IEEE 1547 had grown to a roughly $500,000 undertaking, driven
especially by the grid-compatibility testing requirements of IEEE 1547-2018, which now requires over
400 hours of testing compared to only 20 hours in 2003. The product risk of wind turbine electronics was
also higher than solar, since wind turbines create dangerous over-voltage conditions if rotor control is

2.2 The Opportunity
Market studies have shown a significant opportunity. The Distributed Wind Energy Association (DWEA)
outlined strategies for 30 GW of installed distributed wind turbines by 2030, representing close to one
million turbines rated from 10 kW to 1 MW. Intergrid's approach to this opportunity was to develop
scalable inverter product families certified under IEEE 1547 type-test provisions, which allow a single
certified design to cover a family of products rated from 50% to 200% of the tested power level. This
dramatically reduces per-product certification cost and enables Intergrid to serve the full market with a
limited number of certified designs.

 3. Technical Objectives
The five technical thrusts of the Phase II program, carried forward from Phase I, were:
 • Task 1 – Inverter Power Stage Development (10–150 kW): Develop modular, scalable SiC-
 based power stage families in partnership with Semikron and Wolfspeed.
 • Task 2 – Advanced Controller Design: Develop a next-generation DSP controller capable of 16-
 bit PWM resolution at 100 kHz switching frequency, replacing the legacy 12-bit / 25 kHz design.
 • Task 3 – Certification (UL1741, UL1998, IEEE 1547): Achieve UL1741 basic certification for the
 IG25 and pursue UL1741-SB and UL1998 functional safety software certification.
 • Task 4 – Grid-Connected, Stand-alone, and Microgrid Operation: Continue development of
 multi-mode inverter controls for microgrid applications, in collaboration with the Alaska Center
 for Energy and Power (ACEP) at the University of Alaska Fairbanks.
 • Task 5 – Auxiliary Inverters for Induction Generators: Investigate the use of small auxiliary
 inverters in parallel with induction-generator turbines to achieve UL1741-SB compliance without
 full AC-DC-AC power conversion.

 4. Project Accomplishments

4.1 UL1741 Certification – Landmark Achievement
The most significant milestone of the project was achieved in July 2023 when Intergrid's IG25 inverter
received UL1741 "Grid-Interactive" certification from Intertek Testing Services (Cortland, NY), making it
the first commercially available, UL1741-certified inverter designed for the US distributed wind market.
This was the culmination of a multi-year process encompassing:
 • Construction review
 • User manual documentation review
 • Product type-testing at Intertek's Cortland, NY facility
 • Definition and implementation of end-of-line manufacturing tests at the Bergey Windpower
 (BWC) production facility
 • Intertek inspection of the BWC manufacturing facility

The certification resulted in an Authorization to Mark (ATM) from Intertek (Control Number 5026569,
Issued 18-Jul-2023) for the IG25-240-2 as a Utility Interactive Inverter to UL 1741:2021 Ed.3 and CSA
C22.2#107.1:2016. This certification was immediately leveraged commercially: the first shipment of a
Bergey Excel 15 turbine with Intergrid power electronics occurred in August 2023, and more than 200
systems had shipped by December 2025.

4.2 UL1741-SB (Grid Support) Certification Progress
UL1741-SB, the "Supplement B" grid support version of the standard, is required for installations in the
major markets of California, Hawaii, and New York. With basic UL1741 certification secured, the project's
certification efforts shifted to achieving this higher-level certification. Progress by December 2023
included:
 • Inverter code updates to implement grid support functions including volt/var and frequency/watt
 control
 • Implementation of Sunspec and IEEE 2030.5 communications protocols, required by IEEE 1547
 and California's CSIP rules
 • Development of an automated test suite enabling unattended 24/7 inverter testing. UL estimates
 UL1741-SB requires approximately 400 hours of testing; test automation is essential to make
 this feasible
 • Development of a Virtual Testing capability using computer models of power sources and
 measurement instruments, enabling accelerated-time simulation expected to complete the full
 SB test verification in under one hour
 • Negotiation of a "Satellite Class 2" remote-witnessing testing arrangement with Intertek,
 enabling the majority of testing to occur in Intergrid's Temple, NH lab under Intertek remote
 supervision, with a first site visit scheduled for January 10, 2024
 • Procurement of calibrated test equipment equivalent to Intertek's lab: a Yokogawa PX8000
 precision power scope, DL750 12-channel scope, and WT500 power meter

4.3 Power Stage Hardware Development
Intergrid designed and fabricated a new Silicon Carbide-based power stage for a next-generation 25 kW
inverter (internally designated NG-25) intended for the Bergey Excel 15 wind turbine. This work
proceeded through two major revisions during the project period.

Power Board
The new power board uses third-generation (FM3) Wolfspeed Silicon Carbide power modules, which
cost approximately one-quarter of the second-generation (GM3) modules used in the existing IG25
commercial product. The FM3 modules are "baseless" — they do not have the heavy copper base of
conventional BM2 modules — enabling a more compact and lighter assembly. Key innovations over the
IG25 design include:
 • Relocation of DC bus connections to the rear of the board to simplify high-current wiring and
 eliminate routing over the gate driver board
 • Optimized board mounting to ensure proper downward pressure on SiC power modules
 • Addition of standoffs for gate driver mounting that also convey voltage signals

The board was fabricated in prototype quantities (five units) and placed into initial testing by December
2023.

Gate Driver Board
A new three-phase gate driver board was designed to mount on top of the power board. Key innovations
over the IG25 design include:
 • TI UCC21710 gate driver chips: providing 5.7 kV isolation, active Miller clamp, soft-turn-off on
 fault, and desaturation detection — features not available in the IG25
 • Mornsun DC-DC converters replacing Recom units: they have better specifications, lower cost
 ($3 vs. $12 each), and improved availability

4.4 Inverter Product Family Architecture
Intergrid planned its next-generation inverter product family around three current ratings (50A, 100A,
200A) across three grid voltage configurations, yielding the following power capability matrix:

 Grid Voltage Phases 50A 100A 200A
 120/240                    Single-Phase                12.5 kW           25 kW              50 kW
 120/208                    Three-Phase                 18 kW             36 kW              75 kW
 277/480                    Three-Phase                 40 kW             85 kW              175 kW

This architecture leverages the IEEE 1547-2018 type-test provision allowing test results from one DER
to be representative of a product family with power ratings from 50% to 200% of the tested unit. The
design work is thus limited, as the construction is substantially common across all inverters in the family.
This product range is designed to serve the full spectrum of distributed wind turbine customers, from 7.5
kW residential installations through 125 kW commercial systems.

4.5 Advanced Controller Design (TI DSP)
The project called for a next-generation DSP-based controller capable of 16-bit PWM resolution at 100
kHz switching frequency, compared to the existing IG25's 12-bit resolution at 25 kHz. This improvement
enables better waveform fidelity, finer microgrid power-sharing control, and smaller filter components.
Key developments during the project period included:
 • Selection and qualification of the Texas Instruments TMS320F28379D DSP as the target
 processor. This part was selected over the originally planned TMS320F28388D as a simpler,
 more cost-effective alternative ($11/1000 vs. $22/1000) that avoids the unnecessary complexity
 of an integrated Arm Cortex-M4 connectivity manager
 • Early development and code testing on a TMS320F28379D LaunchXL development board,
 including porting and validation of the Code Composer Studio toolchain
 • Design and fabrication of a TMS320F28379D adapter board that allows control of an IG25
 inverter with the LaunchXL development board, supporting four gate drive channels, 40-pin
 ribbon cable to the inverter AC filter board, CAN and Modbus communications, and E-stop and
 surge protector sense circuits
 • Resolution of a significant supply-chain challenge: the TMS320F28379D was unobtainable for
 over a year. By December 2023, Intergrid had received 25 of the 176-pin HLQFP chips and
 expected another 50 in January 2024 — enough for prototype testing, certification work, and
 production planning in Q2 2024

4.6 Microgrid and Multi-Mode Operation
Intergrid's Temple, NH facility — a 20-acre site with a renovated cow barn laboratory and two houses —
served as a real-world microgrid testbed. At the reporting period end, the facility included 16 kW of PV
generation, a Skystream wind turbine, three SMA 7.7 kW UL1741-SB-certified inverters on the PV array,
high-efficiency heat pumps in both the house and barn, two electric vehicles, and an electric snow blower.

Collaboration with ACEP at the University of Alaska Fairbanks was initiated for Hardware-in-the-Loop

4.7 Auxiliary Inverters for Induction Generators
An additional research area identified during Phase I was the use of small auxiliary inverters in parallel
with induction-generator wind turbines to provide the active anti-islanding capability required by UL1741-
SB and IEEE 1547. Induction generators used in turbines such as the Siva 300 and Carter 300/500 kW
devices currently cannot meet IEEE 1547 interconnection requirements. An auxiliary inverter rated at
approximately 6% of turbine nameplate power (e.g., 17.5 kVA for a 300 kW turbine) could provide the
required reactive power modulation for anti-islanding detection at dramatically lower cost than full AC-
DC-AC conversion.

Dr. Wills holds a foundational patent (US20010056330A1) in active anti-islanding detection. During the
project, Intergrid participated in the UL6142 distributed wind turbine standards working group and
assessed the regulatory and technical requirements. A preliminary patent search showed no relevant
blocking patents, though some large-wind patents relating to auxiliary inverters for variable-speed
induction generators exist. This area remains a lower-priority task pending the emergence of clear
customer demand.

Intergrid shared initial project progress and results in a "lightning round" presentation at the Distributed
Wind Energy Association (DWEA) conference in Washington, DC in February 2023. The presentation is
available at http://intergrid.us/presentations/Intergrid-DWEA-2023B.pdf. Dr. Wills, who serves as a

7.1 Supply Chain Issues
The TMS320F28379D DSP was unobtainable for more than a year due to global semiconductor
shortages, significantly slowing the advanced controller development task. Intergrid addressed this by
initiating code development on LaunchXL development boards and designing the IG25 adapter board

during the unavailability period. Chip availability normalized by mid-2023, with 25 units received and 50
more expected in January 2024.

7.2 Staffing Delays
The hiring of DSP and C++ programmers was delayed due to the team's focus on the IG25 UL1741
certification effort at Intertek's lab in Cortland, NY. Qualified programmers were actively sought
throughout the project. Staffing was successfully ramped up in Q4 2023 with three programmers
engaged. The company continued to seek qualified DSP and C++ engineers experienced with power
electronics control.

7.3 Health Disruptions
Senior Electrical Engineer Robert Mamone was out for several weeks in Q1 2023 due to RSV and
COVID-19. This was a temporary disruption with no lasting impact on the technical program.

7.4 Technical Approach — No Changes
There were no changes in the fundamental technical approach of the project. The five task structure was
maintained throughout. The decision to focus on inverter-only products (rather than combined wind-
interface/inverter systems) was confirmed as strategically correct: turbine front-end converters are
significantly more complex and require separate certification standards (UL6142 vs. UL1741), and the
immediate market need is for certified grid-connect inverters.

7.5 Market Scope Refinements
Through customer interactions and market analysis, Intergrid identified stronger near-term demand for
10–40 kW single-phase wind inverters than for 100 kW three-phase units. The new NG-25 power stage
development was therefore aligned to the 25 kW (single-phase 100A, 240V) product as the primary
certification target, from which a product family can extend from 12.5 kW to 50 kW single-phase.
Development of higher-power three-phase inverters beyond the 40–60 kW range was deferred pending
the emergence of clearer customer demand.

 8. Impact

8.1 Impact on Principal Disciplines
The project advanced four principal technical disciplines: power electronics design for wind applications,
DSP-based inverter control, grid interconnection standards compliance (UL1741/IEEE 1547), and real-
world microgrid integration. The UL1741 certification achievement directly unlocks the US distributed
wind market, filling a long-standing void that prevented turbine manufacturers from obtaining
interconnection agreements.

8.2 Commercial Impact
The UL1741 certification and first commercial shipment with Bergey Windpower in August 2023 represent
the immediate commercial translation of SBIR investment. More than 200 Bergey Excel 15 turbines with
Intergrid electronics had shipped by December 2025, with production continuing. Intergrid anticipates
bringing next-generation aspects of this research to market with BWC on an on-going basis. The Silicon
Carbide power stage, once certified, will substantially reduce inverter cost and improve performance
across all product lines.

 10. Conclusions
This SBIR Phase II project has delivered a landmark outcome for the US distributed wind industry: the
first commercially available, UL1741-certified inverter designed specifically for wind applications. This
achievement, combined with the first commercial deployments with Bergey Windpower beginning in
August 2023, represents a direct translation of federal research investment into market impact.

The project has simultaneously advanced the technical frontier of wind inverter design through the
development of a next-generation Silicon Carbide power stage that achieves approximately one-quarter
the component cost of the previous generation, a next-generation TI DSP-based controller with four times
the switching frequency and much higher PWM resolution, and a comprehensive automated testing and
virtual simulation infrastructure for inverter certification.

The path forward is clear: complete UL1741-SB certification to open the major markets of California,
Hawaii, and New York; productize the NG-25 design into a certified product family covering the 12.5–175
kW power range; and continue to grow commercial deployments with Bergey Windpower and other
turbine manufacturers. Intergrid is well-positioned to become the foundational power electronics supplier
for the emerging US distributed wind industry.
