Fast Adaptive Tensor Methods Under Local Smoothness
Sadok Jerad

Abstract
A new, fast adaptive regularization methods is proposed and analyzed under local Lipschitz smoothness of the p p -th order tensor. For nonconvex problems, it achieves the optimal 𝒪 ( | log ( ϵ ) | ϵ − ( p + 1 ) / p ) \mathcal{O}\!\left(|\log(\epsilon)|\epsilon^{-(p+1)/p}\right) complexity to obtain first-order ϵ \epsilon -stationary points and in the convex case, it yields 𝒪 ( | log ( ϵ ) | ϵ − 1 / p ) \mathcal{O}\!\left(|\log(\epsilon)|\epsilon^{-1/p}\right) iterations to drive the optimality gap below ϵ \epsilon , thus matching the complexity bounds of standard tensor methods under global Lipschitz smoothness yp to logarithmic terms. The proposed algorithm follows the line of standard tensor methods with an appropriately chosen regularization and suitable modifications. Initial numerical experiments and comparisons for some nonconvex regression problems are made with the standard adaptive cubic regularization where we showcase some potential of the proposed method.

Introduction

Classical optimization methods, such as gradient descent and Newton’s method, have been successfully applied to a wide variety of practical problems arising in many scientific and engineering disciplines. Their theoretical analysis, however, typically relies on the global Lipschitz continuity of a particular derivative (usually the gradient or the Hessian)
[
9
]
. This assumption does not hold even for simple one-dimensional functions such as polynomials and the exponential function. Although the iterates of an optimization algorithm often remain inside a bounded level set (provided the objective function decreases monotonically), global Lipschitz continuity is then satisfied only locally, and the associated Lipschitz constant may depend on the initialization and become arbitrarily large.
In the convex setting, several works have investigated optimization methods under only local gradient Lipschitz continuity
[
24
,
35
]
, or by introducing notions of smoothness based on Bregman divergences
[
1
,
23
]
. For the nonconvex case, motivated by empirical observations on the loss landscapes of neural networks, Zhang et al.
[
34
]
proposed the following generalized smoothness assumption for gradient descent under both deterministic and stochastic settings:

\|\nabla_{x}^{2}f(x)\|\leq L_{0}+L_{1}\|\nabla_{x}^{1}f(x)\|,

(1.1)

where
L_{0},L_{1}>0
.

This assumption encompasses a broader class of functions, including univariate polynomials and exponential functions; see
[
34
,
30
]
for further examples and discussion. It has subsequently been extended to various first-order optimization frameworks, including stochastic optimization, variational inequalities, variance-reduced methods, and different step-size strategies; see
[
15
,
29
,
20
,
33
,
11
,
27
,
21
,
22
]
and the references therein.
Observe also that (
1.1
) may be rewritten by only using first-order derivative as

\|\nabla_{x}^{1}f(x)-\nabla_{x}^{1}f(y)\|\leq(L_{0}+L_{1}\|\nabla_{x}^{1}f(x)\|)\|x-y\|,\quad\quad\text{ if }\|x-y\|\leq\frac{1}{L_{1}},

(1.2)

see
[
22
,
30
]
for discussions and relations between both (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

Extending this framework to higher-order methods is particularly appealing, as such methods enjoy improved worst-case evaluation complexity over first-order algorithms
[
3
,
26
]
, faster local convergence rates
[
13
]
, and, in certain applications, superior practical performance compared with Newton-type methods
[
10
]
. Recently, the works of
[
31
,
19
]
investigated second-order methods under Hessian Lipschitz conditions analogous to (
1.2
). However, the first work in
[
31
]
requires prior knowledge of Lipschitz smoothness constant making the method non-adpative in addition to solving a trust-region sub-problem exactly at each iteration.
[
19
]
instead proposed a second-order algorithm alternating between regularized Newton steps and negative-curvature directions, (with the same spirit as the method in
[
18
]
) under local Lipschitz smoothness of the Hessian. The method is parameter-free and achieves a complexity of
\mathcal{O}\!\left(|\log(\epsilon)|\epsilon^{-3/2}\right)
to reach an
\epsilon
- first order stationary point. More recently
[
28
]
proposed a new smoothness condition for regularized Newton methods by describing the
maximum radius of a ball around the current point
that yields a good relative approximation of the gradient field.
This condition was later applied to unify the analysis of various gradient-regularized convex Newton methods
[
25
,
13
,
12
]
and to provide an analysis of Gauss-Newton methods for some machine learning problems.

Motivated by the local Hessian smoothness assumptions introduced in
[
31
,
19
]
, we develop a new local Lipschitz condition framework for
p
-th order tensor methods together with a corresponding adaptive regularization algorithm. The proposed method follows the philosophy of classical adaptive regularization algorithms
[
3
]
, but introduces a new regularization term that naturally reflects the proposed smoothness condition. Because the smoothness assumption is only local, additional acceptance tests are required beyond the classical ratio test as done in
[
19
]
. For nonconvex optimization, the resulting algorithm achieves an evaluation complexity of

\mathcal{O}\!\left(|\log(\epsilon)|\epsilon^{-(p+1)/p}\right)
complexity to reach an
\epsilon
-first order stationary point, thus retrieving the optimal complexity of tensor methods
[
3
]
(under global Lipschitz assumption) up to a logarithmic factor. In the convex regime, we retrieve a
\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{-1/p}|\log(\epsilon)|\right)
complexity to drive the optimality gap below the threshold
\epsilon
, which again matches the corresponding complexity of standard tensor methods up to logarithmic terms.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
starts by stating our new Lipschitz smoothness condition for the
p
th-order tensor, describes the general algorithmic, compares it with standard tensor methods, specify the algorithm for
p=1
and states other properties. Section 3 derives
a bound on its worst-case complexity for reaching
\epsilon
threshold of criticality. Subsection
3.1
focus on the nonconvex case and Subsection
3.2
deals with the convex one. Results are specified in each time for the case of interest
p=2
. The numerical behaviour of
the proposed algorithms is considered in Section
4
and some conclusions and perspectives are
provided in Section
5
.

We consider the problem of finding approximate first-order critical points of the smooth unconstrained optimization problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x),

(2.1)

Assumption 1

\max_{\|u\|=1}-\nabla_{x}^{i}f(x)[u]^{i}\leq M_{0,i}+M_{1,i}\|\nabla_{x}^{1}f(x)\|\textrm{ for all }i\in\{2,\ldots,p\}.

-th order tensor differences with respect to

17
]
to develop Objective Function-Free adaptive tensor methods. Note that we are less restrictive as we even allow the bound to scales with gradient magnitude.

As done in
[

]

L_{0}=M_{0}+\frac{G_{0}M_{1}}{G_{1}}

As standard in
p
-th-order approximation, we denote the

-th order Taylor approximation as

T_{f,p}(x,s)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}f(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{\nabla_{x}^{i}f(x)[s]^{i}}{i!}.

(2.8)

Before stating our algorithm, we briefly review the standard adaptive regularization framework
[
3
,
26
]
. For
p
-th-order globally continuous Lipschitz function, the method builds at each method a regularized Taylor model which writes as

T_{f,p}(x_{k},s_{k})+\frac{\sigma_{k}\|s_{k}\|^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}.

(2.9)

However this model only works for globally-Lipschitz continuous
p
-th order function, and a decrease is ensured whenever
\sigma_{k}
is larger than the global Lipschitz constant. In our case, we adjust the model’s definition to account for the new smoothness condition by incorporating first-order information in the regularization. Our model therefore writes as

m_{k}(s_{k})=T_{f,p}(x_{k},s_{k})+\frac{\sigma_{k}\max(\varsigma,\|\nabla_{x}^{1}f(x_{k})\|)\|s_{k}\|^{p+1}}{(p+1)!},

(2.10)

where

and
T_{f,p}
defined in (
2.8
). Observe that the current model (
2.10
) is more conservative than (
2.9

2.2
) where the gradient intervenes in the left hand side of the bound.

In addition to the standard test of the sufficient decrease on the function value (

[
18

25
]
. Note that these tests are not new and that they have also been considered in
[
19
]
for a fast second-order method under local Lipschitz-smoothness. For each of the three acceptance conditions imposed on the step, we will show in Section

\sigma_{k}

5
Conclusion and Perspectives

In the current paper, we have proposed an adaptive tensor method that handles functions that have locally Lipschitz smooth
p
-th order tensor. The new smoothness condition is assumed to hold locally around a ball for any given iterate and takes also into account the magnitude of the gradient. At variance with standard Lipschitz condition, this condition covers a boarder class of problems such as univariate polynomial and exponential. The algorithm resembles standard adaptive regularization methods
[
3
,
26
]
but differs by adjusting the expression of the regularization parameter. In addition to the usual decrease ratio, we also introduce additional tests to take into account the fact that the Lipschitz condition is local. Initial experimental results highlight some merits of this approach for some regression tasks.

In this line of work, a natural extension would be to introduce broader smoothness assumptions related to Assumption
3
as it was previously done for
(L_{0},L_{1})
smoothness condition
[
11
,
27
,
33
]
. Furthermore, the proposed regularization parameter formula (
2.10
) could be integrated into the recently developed efficient third-order adaptive tensor methods
[
10
]
, offering a promising avenue for future research.
