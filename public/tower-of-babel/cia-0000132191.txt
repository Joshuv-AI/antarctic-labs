France and EC Smoothing Relations with Israel

President Mitterrand is sending a special Foreign Ministry
envoy to Israel to socthe relations damaged by France’s last-
minute attempt to link a resolution of the Gulf crisis toa
broader solution of the Palestinian issue, according to press
reports. The Israeli Ambassador to France reportedly told
Mitterrand that Israeli officials are furious over French Middle
East diplomacy. The EC yesterday lifted restrictions on
scientific cooperation with Israel that were imposed in January
1990 to protest the closure of universities in the Israeli-
occupied territories, according to press reports.

Comment: In addition to showing support for Israel in the wake
of Iragi missile attacks, Mitterrand and EC Commissioner Matutes
almost certainly hope to convey appreciation for Icrael’‘s policy
of restraint. More importantly, Mitterrand probably wants to
assure Israeli officials that French ideas for a Middle East
peace conference are intended to foster regional stability and
will not jeopardize Israeli security concerns.

Germany Boosting Aid to Coalition

A Foreign Ministry spokesman told reporters today that Bonn
is increasing its aid commitment to the anti-Iraq coalition from
$2.2 to over $3.35 billion. Bonn also will begin to provide
direct cash payments to the US and the UK to defray the costs of
the Gulf war. According to Israeli media reports, Foreign
Minister Genscher and his Israeli counterpart today discussed
sending German Patriot air defense missile batteries, but
Genscher denied afterwards that any formal deal had been struck.
Bonn also will probably sell Saudi Arabia $33.5 million in spare
a for Tornado aircraft, according to the German press. ym

Venezuelan President Pushing Peace Initiative

President Carlos Andres Perez will host a Rio Group
ministerial on Monday and will offer a Persian Gulf peace
proposal to the group for endorsement. A Foreign Ministry
official said the draft stresses that Iraq must comply with the
UN Security Council resolutions and relevant provisions of
international law. i

Comment: The Rio Group’s main concern is the Gulf war’s effect
on the price of oil and the consequences for Latin America. Yhe
group’s members, including Venezuela, generally support the
allied stand against Iraq, but the outcome of the meeting on
Monday cannot be guaranteed. po]
