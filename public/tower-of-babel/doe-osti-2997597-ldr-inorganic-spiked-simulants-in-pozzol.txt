 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Pretreated Low Activity Waste (PTW/LAW) at the Hanford Site, is currently projected for disposition
through a cementitious waste form. The current standard formulation, Cast Stone, uses Class F fly ash, blast
furnace slag, and Portland cement. However, the future availability of Class F fly ash is uncertain, as many
coal-fired power plants—the source of this byproduct—are being decommissioned or converted to natural
gas. This issue has prompted research into identifying and evaluating suitable alternative materials to
replace fly ash in the cementitious waste forms used by the Department of Energy (DOE). The Savannah
River National Laboratory (SRNL) successfully identified four natural pozzolans for the replacement of
Class F fly ash in the current Savannah River Site (SRS) low-level waste (LLW) form, Saltstone.

The results of the pozzolan-substituted Saltstone waste forms’ processing and performance properties were
presented to Hanford Tank Waste Operations & Closure, LLC (H2C). In result, SRNL was tasked with
evaluating the Hanford Site Cast Stone formulation with the identified natural pozzolans. Specifically,
pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone formulations were evaluated for retention of selected inorganic
constituents expected to be present in PTW/LAW. The pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone formulations were
spiked with two different concentrations of inorganic elements, at the nominal limit and three times the
nominal limit. The spiked inorganic pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone waste forms were evaluated by the
Toxicity Characteristic Leaching Procedure (TCLP). Results showed the inorganic analyte values for each
of the pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone waste forms, at both concentrations, remained below the TCLP limit
set by the Universal Treatment Standards (UTS) for non-wastewater.

The experimental work reported in this technical report is Task 2 and was performed per H2C request.
Results for Task 1 can be found in SRNL-STI-2025-00222, Revision 0, “Evaluation of Pozzolan-
Substituted Cast Stone for Pretreated Hanford Tank Waste”.

1.0 Introduction
This report supports the Sample-and-Send approach1 for demonstrating LDR compliance for solidification
and stabilization of pretreated Hanford tank waste. The current baseline formulation at the Hanford Site for
Pretreated Low Activity Waste (PTW/LAW) disposition consists of Class F fly ash, slag, and Portland
cement. Class F fly ash is a byproduct of coal-fired power plants and has raised concern for future
availability as many power plants are being converted to natural gas or simply taken offline. This concern
has sparked research into identifying and evaluating an alternative candidate suitable for the replacement
of fly ash in cementitious waste forms.

Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL) was funded by Department of Energy (DOE) Environmental
Management (EM) Technology Development (TD) in 2023, 2024 and 2025 to evaluate natural pozzolans
for the replacement of Class F fly ash in DOE waste forms. Based on promising results2 and the uncertainty
of Class F fly ash in the northwestern US, SRNL was funded by Hanford Tank Waste Operations & Closure,
LLC (H2C) to investigate potential application of natural pozzolans in the Hanford West Area PTW/LAW
Cast Stone formulation.

Task 1 Volume Increase from Substituting Natural Pozzolans for fly ash: The objective of this work3
was to determine changes in Cast Stone volume resulting from stabilization/solidification of 1 L of
PTW/LAW tank waste. Results4 showed solidification of 1L of LAW/PTW tank waste of the baseline (fly
ash) Cast Stone increased to 75.4% (1.75 L volume increase) for the waste form relative to the waste
solution. Hess pumice-, tephra pumice-, and clinoptilolite-(zeolite)substituted Cast Stone exhibited volume
increases relative to 1 L of PTW/LAW tank waste were 77.9% (1.77 L), 82.0% (1.82 L), and 91.3% (1.91
L), respectively4. Results for Task 1, can be found in, SRNL-STI-2025-00222, Revision 0, “Evaluation of
Pozzolan-Substituted Cast Stone for Pretreated Hanford Tank Waste”.

Task 2 Leaching of LDR inorganic metals: The objective of this work3 was to evaluate the pozzolan-
substituted Cast Stone formulations for retention of Land Disposal Restriction (LDR) inorganic
contaminants by Toxicity Characteristic Leaching Procedure (TCLP)5.

2.0 Regulatory Limits for Non-Wastewater Universal Treatment Standards
A permit6 was issued by the Washington State Department of Ecology to support the extensive cleanup of
radioactive and chemical waste at the Hanford Site. For this study, Universal Treatment Standard limits7
were applied to the TCLP results to evaluate RCRA hazardous waste limits6 for the pozzolan-substituted
Cast Stone formulations prepared with PTW/LAW simulated solution.

3.3 Toxicity Characteristic Leaching Procedure (TCLP)
A modified SW-846 Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Method 13115 was used for this work9.

Size Reduction: Samples were crushed and sieved through a No. 16 sieve (1.8mm) and through a No. 20
(850 µm). The material retained on the No. 20 (850 µm) screen was collected and used in the 1311 TCLP
test. This method requires testing to identify the extraction fluid.

Extraction Fluid Determination: Method 1311 requires determination of the extraction fluid prior to
performing the leaching. First, five grams of sample mixed with 96.5 mL of reagent water and stirred for
5-minutes and then the pH of solution was measured. If the pH was < 5, extraction fluid #1 was used.
However, if the pH was > 5, 3.5 mL of 1N HCl was added to the stirring mixture and heated to 50 ºC for
10-minutes. The solution pH was measured and if pH > 5.0, extraction Fluid #2 was used and if pH < 5,
extraction Fluid #1 was used.

Extraction Fluid #1 is an aqueous acetic acid solution with pH = 4.93 ± 0.05 prepared by diluting 5.7 mL
of glacial acetic acid into 500 mL of reagent water, then adding 64.3 mL of sodium hydroxide and diluting
with reagent water for a total volume of 1 liter. Extraction Fluid #2 is an aqueous acetic acid solution with
pH = 2.88 ± 0.05 prepared by diluting 5.7 mL of glacial acetic acid into reagent water for a total volume of
1 liter.

The standardized TCLP method5 was conducted by particle size reducing samples between < 5 mm but >
250 µm. A ratio of 20 g (extraction fluid) : 1 g (grout) sample of each formulation was added to High
Density Polyethylene (HDPE) bottles. The container lids were securely tightened and taped.

Extraction Process: Samples were placed on a counter-top (end-over-end) rotator for a leaching time of
18 hours ± 2 and speed of 30 ± 2 revolutions per minute (rpm). Upon the completion of leaching, samples
were removed from rotator and left to settle for at least 10 minutes. Samples were then filtered using a 500
mL polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) vacuum filter unit. The filtered leachate was stored in HDPE bottles
and refrigerated until shipped to GEL Laboratories in Charleston, South Carolina.

4.0 Results and Discussion

4.1 Pozzolan-Substituted Cast Stone Waste Forms
The baseline Cast Stone and pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone formulations were prepared with 5.6 M Na
Hanford salt waste simulants spiked with two different concentrations of LDR inorganic elements. Batch
sheets for these formulations can be found in Appendix A. During simulant preparation, a small number of
precipitated solids were observed in the LDR inorganic spiked solution. Simulant prepared with three times
the nominal limit for LDR inorganics produced more precipitate solids that settled as a thin layer at the
bottom of the volumetric flask. These observations were reported to H2C, and guidance was provided to
proceed with experimental work and manually stir the simulant before grouting to ensure the maximum
number of solids would be incorporated into the grout. This was also observed by PNNL researchers for
simulant spiked with similar concentrations of metals12. The LDR inorganic simulants were mixed
thoroughly before subsampling, in order to capture precipitated solids. The solids were digested during
sample preparation and concentrations for both simulants were measured and reported in Table 4-1.

Fresh property data (i.e., flow, gel time, free liquid, set time) was measured for these formulations (without
LDR inorganics) for Task 1, and can be found in SRNL-STI-2025-002224. A grout patty was prepared for
each formulation at each LDR inorganic element concentration and cured for at least 28 days in a sealed
Ziploc® plastic bag.

the pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone waste formulations (W/DM=0.4). After curing for 40 days, samples
for each waste form were particle size reduced following the method in Section 3.3. The baseline Cast Stone
and pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone formulations resulted in a test fluid with a pH > 5 as seen in Table 4-
2. Therefore, extract fluid #2 was used for the TCLP.

TCLP Extraction Process: Approximately, 24 grams of sample between 1.8 mm and 850 µm was placed
into a 500 mL HDPE bottle. A volume of 480 mL of extraction fluid #2 was then added to the bottle. The
TCLP sample bottles were then placed on to an end-over-end counter-top rotator for a leaching time of 18

nominal limit for the baseline Cast Stone was noticeably higher at 9.5. The observed pH may result from

PTFE vacuum filter unit. The filtered leachate was poured into HDPE bottles and refrigerated. Samples
were packaged in a large cooler with ice and shipped the same day to GEL laboratories in Charleston, South
Carolina.

5.0 Conclusions
Cast Stone is a possible waste form for Hanford for PTW/LAW solidification and disposition. This waste
form includes Class F fly ash, blast furnace slag and Portland cement. The emerging issue of fly ash
availability has resulted in research to identify and evaluate suitable alternatives to replace fly ash in DOE
cementitious waste forms. SRNL recently identified suitable natural pozzolans for the replacement of fly
ash in the SRS Saltstone waste form formulation2.

In result, these findings2 prompted H2C’s request for SRNL to evaluate the promising natural pozzolans in
the Cast Stone formulation. Pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone waste forms were prepared using a 5.6M
PTW/LAW simulant at a water-to-dry mix of 0.4. These waste forms were spiked with two inorganic
element concentrations projected for PTW/LAW, nominal limit and three times the nominal limit. Among
the 13 relevant LDR inorganic elements, Beryllium and Thallium were not included due to inability to
identify suitable chemical sources for testing at SRNL, however should be evaluated in future work scope.
TCLP leach tests were conducted at SRNL to evaluate the retention of these inorganic species in the natural
pozzolan Cast Stone waste forms. Reported leachate values13 were compared to the TCLP-based non-
wastewater standard7. The pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone waste forms demonstrated effective retention
performance. TCLP results for the natural pozzolan substituted PTW/LAW baseline Cast Stone
formulations (0.4 w/dm) spiked with 11 RCRA LDR regulated inorganic metals were well below regulatory
limits for land disposal. Consequently, they warrant further consideration for replacement of Class F fly
ash and PTW/LAW solidification.

6.0 Recommendations, Path Forward or Future Work
Based on this work, SRNL recommends the following steps to evaluate the beneficial use of natural
pozzolans for waste streams at the Hanford Site:

  Evaluate pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone formulations for stabilization and solidification of
 Hanford West Area tank solids.
  Characterize the precipitated solid observed during PTW/LAW inorganic spiked simulant
 preparation. Particle size, mineralogy, and solubility data will support processing options.
  Determine whether the waste simulant loading can be further increased by modifying the proportion
 of slag and/or cement and increasing the proportion of natural pozzolan using the 1 and 3x
 concentrations of the underlying hazardous metal constituents provided by H2C.
  Determine whether waste loading can be further increased by adding a fourth natural pozzolan
 candidate.
  Determine the maximum concentration of each of the 11 hazardous metals that can be stabilized in
 the Baseline and one or more natural pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone formulation.
  Identify Beryllium and Thallium testing methods or surrogates suitable for testing at SRNL.
