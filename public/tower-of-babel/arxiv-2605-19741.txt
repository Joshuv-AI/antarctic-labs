High-fidelity molecular quantum logic gates resilient to interaction fluctuation
Yan Lu, Xiao-Feng Shi

Abstract
Optically trapped polar molecules are promising for quantum information processing, yet the accuracy of an entangling molecular gate is limited by the uncertainty of dipole-dipole interactions (DDI) from the molecular motion in traps. We show that two π \pi pulses of global microwave excitation can yield a high-fidelity controlled-phase gate when assisted by two single-qubit gates. The gate is resilient to the uncertainty of DDI because it does not rely on populating DDI-coupled states. Further, the controlled phase is fully tunable by varying the relative phase of the two global microwave pulses, and, hence, the gate can find applications in a wide range of quantum algorithms involving quantum Fourier transform. Moreover, we introduce a motional-mode separation technique to quantum mechanically study the influence of the molecular motion, which shows that the gate fidelity can be over 0.9999 with typical experimental conditions.



I
introduction

Polar molecules were proposed as candidates for building quantum information processing devices more than two decades ago
DeMille 2002
, with multiple follow-up designs for molecular quantum entangling gates
Zhu et al. 2013
;
Yelin et al. 2006
;
Ni et al. 2018
;
Hughes et al. 2020
;
Tscherbul et al. 2023
;
Bergonzoni et al. 2025
;
Muminov et al. 2026
. These gate protocols depend on shelving molecules transiently on a state coupled by dipole-dipole interaction (DDI), which can be either a state-dependent Ising-like energy shift
Yelin et al. 2006
;
Tscherbul et al. 2023
, or a spin-exchange coupling
Ni et al. 2018
;
Hughes et al. 2020
;
Bergonzoni et al. 2025
;
Muminov et al. 2026
. Molecules are usually optically trapped during the logic operations, where the position spread inside the traps can not be ignored compared to the molecule-molecule separation
L

Ruttley et al. 2025
;
Picard et al. 2025
, leading to an uncertainty of the DDI. Consequently, despite the remarkable advance of the cooling, trapping, and quantum state manipulation over ultracold polar molecules
Schindewolf et al. 2022
;
Cornish et al. 2024
, experimental demonstrations of quantum entanglement
Bao et al. 2023
;
Holland et al. 2023
;
Ruttley et al. 2025
and two-qubit logic operations
Picard et al. 2025
revealed that the uncertainty of DDI strongly limits the gate accuracy. Recently, Ref.
Bergonzoni et al. 2025
proposed an optimal control scheme that can yield a two-molecule iSWAP gate with an infidelity
below
2\times 10^{-4}
with 10% variation of DDI.

Here, we introduce a way to realize a two-qubit molecular phase gate resilient to the uncertainty of DDI, and the gate is realized by two global microwave pulses when assisted by two single-qubit phase gates following each microwave pulse. Enabled by an exotic effective spin echo process, the gate has two features. First, variation of DDI barely influences the gate accuracy due to that DDI serves as a channel to enable adiabatic dynamics where fine tuning of DDI is no longer needed. Second, the entangling phase
\varphi
in the gate is from the global microwave field, so that
\varphi
can be of any desired value simply by tuning the relative phase between the microwave pulses. Such a class of tunable controlled phase gates can be used in quantum Fourier transform for notable quantum algorithms like, e.g., phase estimation, order-finding, and the Shor’s algorithm
Nielsen and Chuang 2000
.

We introduce a theory to quantum mechanically study the influence of the molecular motion in the trap on the entangling gate. When neglecting the much weaker motion along the radial direction in typical tweezers, the molecular motion along the axial directions results in a coupling between the two molecules’ harmonic motional modes and the DDI-coupled internal states. Quantum mechanically, this means that two motional modes are coupled to the internal states of the molecules via DDI. By a motional-mode separation technique, however, we find that though there are two modes involved in the molecular motion, only one is coupled to the internal state of the molecules. By this formalism, we find that the two-qubit phase gate can still acquire a gate fidelity beyond 0.9999 with typical molecular motion in traps of recent experiments
Picard et al. 2025
;
Ruttley et al. 2025
.

II
A controlled-phase gate by an effective spin echo

For two molecules, labeled i and ii, we consider that for each molecule there are three different molecular states labelled
\lvert\uparrow\rangle
,
\lvert\downarrow\rangle
, and
|e\rangle

\lvert\downarrow\rangle

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\frac{\Omega_{\mu}(t)}{2}\lvert e\rangle\langle\downarrow\rvert+\text{H.c.},

(1)

which is in a rotating frame after performing the dipole and rotating-wave approximations. Since the microwave field in use has a wavelength on the order of decimeter while the molecular separation is on the order of micrometer, the same microwave field covers both molecules with equal strength and phase. In the Hilbert space spanned by the states of the two molecules, the microwave driving for the two molecules induces a Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\mu}\otimes\hat{I}+\hat{I}\otimes\hat{H}_{\mu}
with
\hat{I}
the identity operator. To induce entanglement, we consider a condition without applying static electric field and that a two-molecular state in a superposition of
\lvert\uparrow,e\rangle
of
\lvert e,\uparrow\rangle

\hat{V}=J\left(\lvert\uparrow,e\rangle\langle e,\uparrow\rvert+\lvert e,\uparrow\rangle\langle\uparrow,e\rvert\right)

. The absence of DDI in
\lvert\downarrow\rangle

Tscherbul et al. 2023
;
Picard et al. 2025
.

In the context of quantum logic gates, it is useful to study the gate map by examining the time dynamics for the energy eigenstates of the gate

;

\lvert\uparrow,\uparrow\rangle,\lvert\uparrow,\downarrow\rangle,\lvert\downarrow,\uparrow\rangle,\lvert\downarrow,\downarrow\rangle

\lvert\downarrow\rangle

\lvert\uparrow,\uparrow\rangle
stays intact in the rotating frame. Both
\lvert\uparrow,\downarrow\rangle
and
\lvert\downarrow,\uparrow\rangle

\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{+}+\hat{H}_{-}

\displaystyle\hat{H}\pm

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\hbar\frac{\Omega_{\mu}(t)}{2}\lvert\mathbb{D}_{\pm}\rangle\langle\mathbb{B}_{\pm}\rvert+\text{H.c.}\right]\pm J\lvert\mathbb{D}_{\pm}\rangle\langle\mathbb{D}_{\pm}\rvert,

(2)

where

\displaystyle\lvert\mathbb{D}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\lvert\uparrow,e\rangle\pm\lvert e,\uparrow\rangle\right),

\displaystyle\lvert\mathbb{B}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\lvert\uparrow,\downarrow\rangle\pm\lvert\downarrow,\uparrow\rangle\right).

(3)

Here, the use of Bell-state basis does not mean that the gate works in Bell-state basis. It is used only for showing the physical picture of the gate mechanism. Note that the numerical results show later are all from the original Hamiltonian without use of any Bell-state basis.
\hat{H}_{\alpha}
can also be written as
\hat{H}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\eta=\pm}\xi_{\eta}^{(\alpha)}\lvert v_{\eta}^{(\alpha)}\rangle\langle v_{\eta}^{(\alpha)}\rvert
, where

\displaystyle\xi_{\eta}^{(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha J+\eta\hbar\overline{\Omega}_{\mu}\right),

\displaystyle\lvert v_{\eta}^{(\alpha)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathscr{N}_{\eta}^{(\alpha)}\left[\xi_{\eta}^{(\alpha)}\lvert\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}\rangle+\hbar\frac{\Omega_{\mu}^{\ast}(t)}{2}\lvert\mathbb{B}_{\alpha}\rangle\right],

(4)

where

\displaystyle\lvert\mathbb{B}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle e^{\mp i\varphi}\lvert\mathbb{B}_{\pm}\rangle,

(5)

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\xi_{+}^{(+)}dt/\hbar.

After the first
\pi
pulse, a phase gate that imprints a

\lvert\downarrow\rangle

\displaystyle\lvert\mathbb{B}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\lvert\mathbb{B}_{\mp}\rangle,

where nothing occurs to the input state

Then, a second

\displaystyle\lvert\mathbb{B}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\lvert\mathbb{B}_{\mp}\rangle,

\displaystyle\lvert\downarrow,\downarrow\rangle

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle-e^{i\varphi}\lvert\downarrow,\downarrow\rangle.

(7)

Finally, we use the phase shift gate as used for Eq. (
6

III
Influence from position spread in traps

To capture the effect of the finite position spread of optically trapped molecules on the entangling gates, a quantum mechanical treatment is desirable. When we approximate the trap as harmonic
Ruttley et al. 2025
, the position spread arises due to that the desired trap centers for the two molecules are
L
, while there is finite extension of the positions of the molecules. The trap frequencies along the axial and radial directions differ by one order of magnitude in usual experimental setups
Ruttley et al. 2025
. As a result, the position spread of the trapped molecules is mainly along the axial direction. So, the much smaller position spread along the radial direction can enable the neglect of the motion along the radial direction as in Ref.
Picard et al. 2025
. When the angular frequency is
\omega
along the axial direction, the harmonic oscillator length is
\ell=\sqrt{\hbar/(m\omega)}
, where
m
is the mass of the molecule. To have strong DDI,
L
is usually quite small, around 2
\mu
m in recent experiments
Ruttley et al. 2025
;
Picard et al. 2025
. As a result, the position spread should not be neglected, with
\ell/L
about
0.05
in tweezer-trapped
X^{1}\Sigma^{+}
NaCs molecules of Ref.
Picard et al. 2025
.

So, we can consider the centers for two traps at
(0,0,0)
and
(0,L,0)
, with the axial direction along
\mathbf{x}
. Along the axial direction, the Hamiltonian for the two trapped molecules is

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{trap}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=\text{i,ii}}\left[\frac{p_{\alpha}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{m\omega^{2}x_{\alpha}^{2}}{2}\right],

(10)

where
\alpha=
i, ii, and
p_{\alpha}=-i\hbar\partial/\partial x_{\alpha}
. To capture the coupling between the external motion and the internal rovibrational states, we define

\displaystyle x_{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(x_{\text{i}}\pm x_{\text{ii}}),~p_{\pm}=-i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\pm}},

(11)

so that Eq. (
10
) becomes,

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{trap}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=\pm}\left[\frac{p_{\alpha}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{m\omega^{2}x_{\alpha}^{2}}{2}\right],

(12)

which means that the state of the external two-molecule vibration in the two-trap system is a product state of the two motional modes labeled
\pm
. The wavefunctions of the ground and the
n

|0_{\alpha}\rangle\equiv\lvert\text{vac}_{\alpha}\rangle
and
|n_{\alpha}\rangle
, with

. Here,
|n_{\alpha}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}n!\ell\sqrt{\pi}}}e^{-\frac{x_{\alpha}^{2}}{2\ell^{2}}}h_{n}(x/\ell)
, and the Hermitian polynomials
h_{n}(\xi)
are given by
h_{n}(\xi)=(-1)^{n}e^{\xi^{2}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial\xi^{n}}e^{-\xi^{2}}

which is an eigenstate of eigenenergy
E_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega(2n+1)
. In the Hilbert space spanned by
|n_{\alpha}\rangle
, the motional Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{trap}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega\sum_{\alpha=\pm}(2\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\alpha}+1),

(13)

where
\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}
and
\hat{a}_{\alpha}
are the creation and annihilation operators for the motional states of the two-molecule motional mode
\alpha=
+ or -, which is related to

via

\pm

\hat{a}_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\hat{a}_{\text{i}}\pm\hat{a}_{\text{ii}})

\hat{a}_{\pm}^{\dagger}
. So, if the two molecules are both in the motional ground states, the state in the basis of
\alpha=\pm

IV
Discussion and Conclusions

The gate here is analogous to dark-state gates in Rydberg atoms, for a review, see, e.g., Ref.
Shi 2022
. One difference from Rydberg atoms is that the dark-state gates with Rydberg atoms need populating multiple Rydberg states which have lifetimes of order of 0.1 ms, but the DDI occurs between molecules in low-lying states. Here,
\lvert\uparrow\rangle
and
\lvert\downarrow\rangle
can be in the ground rovibrational manifold and
\lvert e\rangle
in the first excited manifold, all possessing long lifetimes. A particular strength of polar molecules is that it is possible to trap multiple low-lying states in the same trap with second scale coherence, while simultaneously enabling coherent state transitions between the states
Hepworth et al. 2025
. Therefore high-fidelity controlled phase gates with polar molecules are realizable.

In summary, we introduce a controlled phase gate with two close polar molecules implemented by two consecutive
\pi
pulses of microwave fields when assisted by two single-qubit phase gates. The gate acquires a fully tunable phase determined by the phase change from the first to the second microwave pulses. We further introduce a quantum mechanical treatment which can help to analyze the molecular motion in the trap during the gate operation, and find that a gate fidelity over 0.9999 is possible with recent experimental setups.
