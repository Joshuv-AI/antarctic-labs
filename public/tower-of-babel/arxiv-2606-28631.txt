1
Introduction

1.1
Model introduction and motivation

The branching random walk
(Z_{n},n\in\mathbb{N},\mathbb{P})
is a discrete-time Markov process defined as follows. At time
0
, there is a single particle located at
0\in\mathbb{R}
. At time
1
, this initial particle dies and produces a set of offspring, whose spatial configuration is an independent copy of a random point process
\mathcal{L}
. At time
2
, each particle existing at time
1
independently repeats the behavior of its parent and each particle at site
x
gives birth to an independent copy of
x+\mathcal{L}
. The procedure goes on.

In this paper, our standard assumption is as follows.

(H0)

(First jump then branch) Assume that
\mathcal{L}:=N\delta_{X}
where
N
is independent of
X
and that the branching random walk is subcritical, i.e.,
\mathbb{P}(N=k)=p_{k}
and
m:=\mathbb{E}(N)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kp_{k}\in(0,1)
.

Let
M_{n}
denote the maximal position among all particles alive at time
n
, with the convention that
M_{n}:=-\infty
if there is no particle alive at time
n
. Since it is well known that in the subcritical case
m<1
, the branching random walk dies out almost surely in finite time, the (all-time) maximal displacement

\displaystyle M:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}M_{n}

(1.1)

is well-defined. One of the main points of interest in the subcritical branching random walk concerns the asymptotic behavior of the tail probability
\mathbb{P}(M>x)
of
M
as
x\to\infty
.

The first related work is due to Sawyer and Fleischman
[
21
]
in the setting of branching Brownian motion (with branching rate
1
). It was proved in
[
21
]
that, under the third moment condition
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{3}p_{k}<\infty
,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}e^{\sqrt{2(1-m)}x}\,\mathbb{P}(M>x)\ \mbox{exists and }\in(0,\infty).

(1.2)

The main idea in
[
21
]
is based on a key observation such that the function
x\mapsto\mathbb{P}(M>x)
solves a class of second-order ODE known as the F-KPP equation. Later, Profeta
[
18
]
extended the Brownian case to spectrally negative Lévy processes and proved a similar result as (
1.2
) under third moment condition according to laplace transform method.

Now we introduce the related result in the discrete-time model. As for subcritical branching random walks, there have also been recent works concerning the tail of
M
. Under condition
(H0)
for
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{3}p_{k}<\infty
and some integer-valued centered random variable
X
such that
\mathbb{E}(e^{\theta X})<\infty
for all
\theta>0
, Neuman and Zheng
[
16
]
proved that

\displaystyle 0<\liminf_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma n}\,\mathbb{P}(M>n)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma n}\,\mathbb{P}(M>n)\leq 1,

(1.3)

where
\gamma>0
is the unique solution to the equation

\displaystyle m\mathbb{E}(e^{\gamma X})=1.

(1.4)

Moreover, under some other technical assumptions on
X
(i.e.,
\mathbb{P}(X\leq A)=1
for some
A<\infty
and that
X
is right-continuous), it was shown in Neuman and Zheng
[
16
]
that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\gamma n}\,\mathbb{P}(M>n)\ \mbox{exists and }\in(0,1].

(1.5)

After that, there are also some improvements to the result (
1.5
).
Based on a spine decomposition approach, Fu and Hong
[
10
]
proved (
1.5
) under
(H0)
and the following assumption:

(A1)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{2}p_{k}<\infty
;
\mathbb{E}(X)=0
,
\mathbb{E}(e^{\theta X})<\infty
for all
\theta>0
, and
\mathbb{P}(X\in\mathrm{d}x)
is either a diffusion measure on
\mathbb{R}
or a discrete and irreducible measure on
\mathbb{Z}
.

Recently Hou and Zhang
[
14
]
used renewal theory approach and proved (
1.5
) under
(H0)
and the following assumption:

(A2)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k(\log k)p_{k}<\infty
; (
1.4
) and
\mathbb{E}(Xe^{\gamma X})<\infty
hold. Either
X
is non-lattice or for some
h>0
,
X
is lattice with span
h
.

The assumption
(A2)
only requires slightly stronger assumptions than
(H0)
and the existence of
\gamma
in (
1.4
) and is weaker than
(A1)
. The main motivation of this paper is based on the following natural question:

\displaystyle\emph{Whatcanwesayabout$\mathbb{P}(M>x)$iftheequation\eqref{exists-of-gamma}doesnotadmitasolution?}

(1.6)

The question (
1.6
) reflects a transition between two different extremal mechanisms. In the classical Cramér-type regime, the asymptotic behavior of
M
is driven by an exponential tilting associated with the solution of (
1.4
). Once this equation fails to have a solution, or the relevant exponential moment is not strong enough, the extremal behavior is no longer governed by such a tilted random-walk path.
To the best of our knowledge, most of the literature addressing this question focuses only on the branching (strictly)
\alpha
-stable process for
\alpha\in(0,2)
; see, for example, Hou et al
[
12
]
and Profeta
[
17
]
. It was proved in
[
12
,
17
]
that if the
\alpha
-stable process admits positive jumps, then under the subcritical assumption
m\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}x^{\alpha}\,\mathbb{P}(M>x)\ \mbox{exists and }\in(0,\infty).

(1.7)

The proofs in
[
12
,
17
]
are based on the Laplace transform argument and fine analysis on tail probability of an
\alpha
-stable process indexed by an independent exponential random variable, which are consistent with the strategies used in the case of the spectrally negative Lévy process
[
18
]
. The main goal of this paper is to understand the phase transition between (
1.5
) and (
1.7
) and to partially answer the question (
1.6
) for a class of jump sizes
X
.

1.2
Assumptions and main results

Consider the following two cases:

(H1)

The distribution of
X
has a stretched exponential tail; that is, there exists a function
\ell
slowly varying at infinity such that for all
x>1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X>x)=\ell(x)x^{a}e^{-\lambda x^{b}},

(1.8)

where
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda>0
if
b\in(0,1)
, and
a<0
,
\lambda=0
if
b=0
. We also assume that
\mathbb{E}(X^{2})<\infty
when
b\in(0,1)
.

(H2)

There exists
\gamma,x_{*}>0
and a function
\ell
slowly varying at infinity such that
m\mathbb{E}(e^{\gamma X})<1
and that for all
x>x_{*}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X>x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}\ell(y)y^{a}e^{-\lambda y^{b}-\gamma y}\,\mathrm{d}y,

(1.9)

where
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda>0
if
b\in(0,1)
, and
a<-2
,
\lambda=0
if
b=0
.

We mention here that the assumptions
\mathbb{E}(X^{2})<\infty
for
b\in(0,1)
in
(H1)
and
a<-2
in
(H2)
are needed for some technical reasons. We will explain these assumptions in suitable place of the paper when we used them.
Our main results are as follows.

Theorem 1.1

Assume
(H0)
and
(H1)
. Then

\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{e^{\lambda x^{b}}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}\mathbb{P}(M>x)=\frac{1-p_{0}}{1-m}.

Theorem 1.2

Assume
(H0)
,
(H2)
and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k(\log k)p_{k}<\infty
. Then there exists a constant
C_{0}\in(0,\infty)
given as in (
4.2
) below such that

\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{e^{\lambda x^{b}+\gamma x}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}\mathbb{P}(M>x)=C_{0}.

Remark 1.3

For Theorem
1.1
, under
(H1)
with
b=0
and
a\in(-2,0)
, our first result coincides with the case of the
\alpha
-stable process in (
1.7
). Moreover, Theorem
1.1
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(M>x)}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}=\frac{1-p_{0}}{1-m}.

(1.10)

For Theorem
1.2
, the assumption
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k(\log k)p_{k}<\infty
is used for technical purposes, and the result is equivalent to

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(M>x)}{\mathbb{P}(X\in\mathrm{d}x)/\mathrm{d}x}=C_{0}.

(1.11)

As we will discuss in Section
1.3
below, we adapt the same idea in the proof of both theorems, even though the results (
1.10
) and (
1.11
) may appear slightly different.

We end this section with some questions. In
[
14
]
, Hou and Zhang also considered the tail probability of
M
for subcritical branching random walks with killing. Thus, our first question is:

Question 1

Under assumptions
(H0)
and either
(H1)
or
(H2)
, what is the tail probability of
M
in subcritical branching random walks with killing?

In critical multitype branching random walks, Lehéricy
[
15
, Section 2.4]
gave the decay rate of the tail probability for
\mathbb{P}(M>x)
. In our setting, we are also interested in subcritical multitype branching random walks, and our next question is:

Question 2

What can we say about
\mathbb{P}(M>x)
in subcritical multitype branching random walks?

In critical branching Lévy processes where the spatial motion
(L_{t},t\geq 0,\mathbb{P}_{x})
admits a negative drift
\mathbb{E}_{0}(L_{1})<0
, a similar sufficient exponential moment condition
\mathbb{E}(e^{\omega L_{1}})=1
for some
\omega>0
is assumed in Profeta
[
19
, Theorem 2]
. We believe that the tail probability of
M
would be different if
\{\omega>0:\mathbb{E}_{0}(e^{\omega L_{1}})<\infty\}\neq\emptyset
but there is no solution to
\mathbb{E}(e^{\omega L_{1}})=1
. Thus our last question is:

Question 3

What is the decay rate of the tail probability
\mathbb{P}(M>x)
in critical branching Lévy processes when the Lévy process does not have a sufficient exponential moment?

Organization of the paper and proof strategies

The organization of the paper is as follows. Define
T_{x}^{+}:=\inf\{n:S_{n}>x\}
. In Section
2
, we prove the decay rate of
\mathbb{P}(T_{x}^{+}=n)
under
(H1)
(see Proposition
2.1
). In Section

, under
(H2)
and a suitable change of measure (see (

) below), we obtain a new random walk

and prove the decay rate of
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}(e^{-\gamma S_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{\{T_{x}^{+}=n\}})
(see Proposition
3.2
). Moreover, in Section
3
, we prove a renewal theorem for the random walk
(S_{n},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
in Proposition
3.3
. The proofs of Theorems
1.1
and

are given in Section

and are presented in Sections

and

, respectively. Section

for the case

Our proof strategy differs from that used for subcritical branching
\alpha
-stable processes
[
12
,
17
]
and is inspired by the literature on a class of supercritical branching random walks, including branching random walks with heavy tails
[
3
,
4
,
8
,
20
]
, branching random walks with stretched exponential tails
[
9
,
11
]
, and branching random walks outside the boundary case
[
1
,
6
]
. In the aforementioned literature, a phenomenon known as the
one-big-jump principle
(see
[
2
,
7
]
for related results in the context of random walks) appears in these models. Accordingly, we adapt the observations from the supercritical setting to subcritical branching random walks and prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

We write
f(x)\lesssim g(x)
to mean that there exists some constant
C
such that
f(x)\leq Cg(x)
for all sufficiently large
x
. Similarly,
f(x)\lesssim_{\delta,\kappa,\dots}g(x)
indicates that the constant
C
may depend on the parameters
\delta,\kappa,\dots
. The notation
f(x)\gtrsim g(x)
(respectively,
f(x)\gtrsim_{\delta,\kappa,\dots}g(x)
) is equivalent to
g(x)\lesssim f(x)
(respectively,
g(x)\lesssim_{\delta,\kappa,\dots}f(x)
). We note that the constant
C
may also depend on the slowly varying function
\ell
as well as on the constants
m
,
p_{k}
,
a
,
\lambda
,
\gamma
, and
b
.

2
Random walks with stretched exponential tail

In this section, we assume that
X
satisfies
(H1)
. Let
\{X_{i},i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be i.i.d. random variables equal in law to
X
. Define

\displaystyle S_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad T_{x}^{+}:=\inf\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:S_{n}>x\right\}.

(2.1)

The main goal of this section is to prove the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1

(i)

For any
x>1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

m^{n/2}\,\mathbb{P}\left(T_{x}^{+}=n\right)\leq m^{n/2}\,\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}>x\right)\lesssim\mathbb{P}(X>x).

(ii)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(T_{x}^{+}=n)}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}=1.

We first prove Proposition
2.1
for the case
b=0
. Before that, we gather some useful results in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2

Let
\ell
be a slowly varying function.

(i)

For any
0<z_{1}\leq z_{2}
,

\lim_{x\to+\infty}\sup_{y\in[z_{1},z_{2}]}\big|\ell(xy)/\ell(x)-1\big|=0.

(ii)

For any
A>1
and
\delta>0
, there exists
K(A,\delta)
such that

\frac{\ell(y)}{\ell(x)}\leq A\max\left\{(y/x)^{\delta},(y/x)^{-\delta}\right\}\quad\text{for all }x,y\geq K(A,\delta).

Consequently, for any
\delta>0
, we have

x^{-\delta}\lesssim_{\delta}\ell(x)\lesssim_{\delta}x^{\delta}.

Proof:
For (i), see
[
5
, Theorem 1.5.2, p.22]
. (ii) is known as Potter’s theorem and can be found in
[
5
, Theorem 1.5.6, p.25]

\Box

Proof of Proposition
2.1
for
b=0
:
(i) The first inequality holds trivally, so we only prove the second inequality.
If
x\leq n
, then according to the inequality
\mathbb{P}(X>n)=\ell(n)n^{a}\gtrsim m^{n/2}
, we get

\displaystyle m^{n/2}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}>x\right)\leq m^{n/2}\lesssim\mathbb{P}(X>n)\leq\mathbb{P}(X>x).

(2.2)

If
x>n
, then combining the union bound and Lemma
2.2
(ii) (with
A=2,\delta=1
), we have
\ell(x/n)/\ell(x)\lesssim n
and

\displaystyle m^{n/2}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}>x\right)\leq nm^{n/2}\mathbb{P}(X>x/n)=nm^{n/2}(x/n)^{a}\ell(x/n)

\displaystyle=m^{n/2}n^{1-a}\frac{\ell(x/n)}{\ell(x)}\mathbb{P}(X>x)\lesssim m^{n/2}n^{2-a}\mathbb{P}(X>x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{P}(X>x).

(2.3)

Combining (
2.2
) and (
2
) implies (i).

(ii) Since
\{T_{x}^{+}=1\}=\{X_{1}>x\}
, the limit holds trivally. We consider the case
n\geq 2
here.
For each
\varepsilon\in(1/2,1)
, define

\displaystyle E_{n}:=\left\{\mbox{there exists at most one }i\leq n\mbox{ such that }X_{i}>x^{\varepsilon}\right\}.

(2.4)

Fixing any
\Gamma>0
with
\Gamma(2\varepsilon+1)<-(2\varepsilon-1)a
. Since
x^{-\Gamma}\lesssim_{\Gamma}\ell(x)\lesssim_{\Gamma}x^{\Gamma}
by Lemma
2.2
(ii),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(E_{n}^{c}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\exists i\neq j\mbox{ such that }X_{i},X_{j}>x^{\varepsilon}\right)\leq n^{2}\mathbb{P}(X>x^{\varepsilon})^{2}

\displaystyle=n^{2}\frac{\left(\ell(x^{\varepsilon})\right)^{2}}{\ell(x)}x^{(2\varepsilon-1)a}\mathbb{P}(X>x)\lesssim_{\Gamma}n^{2}\frac{x^{2\varepsilon\Gamma}}{x^{-\Gamma}}x^{(2\varepsilon-1)a}\mathbb{P}(X>x)

\displaystyle=n^{2}x^{(2\varepsilon+1)\Gamma+(2\varepsilon-1)a}\mathbb{P}(X>x).

(2.5)

Thus,
\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(E_{n}^{c})}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}=0
.
Since for
x
large enough such that
x/n>x^{\varepsilon}
, we have

\{T_{x}^{+}=n\}\subset\{S_{n}>x\}\subset\{\exists\ 1\leq j\leq n\ s.t.\ X_{j}>x/n\}\subset\{\exists\ 1\leq j\leq n\ s.t.\ X_{j}>x^{\varepsilon}\}.

Therefore, on
\{T_{x}^{+}=n\}\cap E_{n}
, there exists a unique
j
such that
X_{j}>x^{\varepsilon}
and that
\max_{\ell\neq j}X_{\ell}\leq x^{\varepsilon}
, which together with
S_{n}>x
implies that
X_{j}>x-nx^{\varepsilon}>x^{\varepsilon}
for large
x
. In conclusion, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(T_{x}^{+}=n)}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}\mathbb{P}\left(T_{x}^{+}=n,\exists!\ 1\leq j\leq n\ \mbox{such that }X_{j}>x^{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}\mathbb{P}\left(T_{x}^{+}=n,\exists\ 1\leq j\leq n\ \mbox{such that }X_{j}>x-nx^{\varepsilon},\max_{\ell\neq j}X_{\ell}\leq x^{\varepsilon}\right).

(2.6)

Now we would like to replace
\max_{\ell\neq j}X_{\ell}\leq x^{\varepsilon}
by
\max_{\ell\neq j}|X_{\ell}|\leq x^{\varepsilon}
. Noticing that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\ 1\leq j\leq n\ \mbox{such that }X_{j}>x-nx^{\varepsilon},\max_{\ell\neq j}|X_{\ell}|>x^{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{n}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{1}>x-nx^{\varepsilon},\max_{\ell\neq 1}|X_{\ell}|>x^{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{n}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}\mathbb{P}\left(X>x-nx^{\varepsilon}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(|X_{\ell}|>x^{\varepsilon}\right)^{n-1}=n\lim_{x\to+\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(|X_{\ell}|>x^{\varepsilon}\right)^{n-1}=0,

(2.7)

where the second equality follows from Lemma
2.2
(i). Plugging this back to (
2
) yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(T_{x}^{+}=n)}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}\mathbb{P}\left(T_{x}^{+}=n,\exists\ 1\leq j\leq n\ \mbox{such that }X_{j}>x-nx^{\varepsilon},\max_{\ell\neq j}|X_{\ell}|\leq x^{\varepsilon}\right).

(2.8)

If the unique
j
with
X_{j}>x-nx^{\varepsilon}
and
\max_{\ell\neq j}|X_{\ell}|\leq x^{\varepsilon}
satisfies
j\leq n-1
, then on
T_{x}^{+}=n
, it must hold that
X_{j}=S_{n-1}-\sum_{\ell\neq j,\ell\leq n-1}X_{\ell}\leq x+(n-2)x^{\varepsilon}\leq x+nx^{\varepsilon}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}\mathbb{P}\left(T_{x}^{+}=n,\exists\ 1\leq j\leq n-1\ \mbox{such that }X_{j}>x-nx^{\varepsilon},\max_{\ell\neq j}|X_{\ell}|\leq x^{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{x\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists 1\leq j\leq n-1\ \mbox{such that }|X_{j}-x|\leq nx^{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leq n\limsup_{x\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}\mathbb{P}\left(|X-x|\leq nx^{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=n\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\ell(x-nx^{\varepsilon})(x-nx^{\varepsilon})^{a}-\ell(x+nx^{\varepsilon})(x+nx^{\varepsilon})^{a}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}=0,

(2.9)

where in the last equality we used Lemma
2.2
(i). Now plugging this back to (

) and noticing that

\{X_{n}>x-nx^{\varepsilon},\max_{\ell\leq n-1}|X_{\ell}|\leq x^{\varepsilon}\}\subset\{\max_{1\leq i\leq n-1}S_{i}\leq x\}
and

for large

(i), we conclude that

\displaystyle 1=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}\mathbb{P}\left(X>x+nx^{\varepsilon}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(|X|\leq x^{\varepsilon}\right)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{n}>x+nx^{\varepsilon},\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n-1}|X_{\ell}|\leq x^{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(T_{x}^{+}=n)}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}>x,X_{n}>x-nx^{\varepsilon},\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n-1}|X_{\ell}|\leq x^{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(X_{n}>x-nx^{\varepsilon}\right)}{\mathbb{P}(X>x)}=1,

(2.10)

which implies the desired result for

\Box

Now we prove Proposition

for

. The proof idea is quite similar to the case

Lemma 3.1

There exists some slowly varying function

at

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}(X>x)=\widetilde{\ell}(x)x^{a+1-b}e^{-\lambda x^{b}}.

\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}(|X|^{k})<\infty

\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\widetilde{\ell}(x)}{\ell(x)}=\frac{-(a+1)}{\mathbb{E}(e^{\gamma X})}1_{\{b=0\}}+\frac{1}{\lambda b\mathbb{E}(e^{\gamma X})}1_{\{b\in(0,1)\}}

Proof:
We divide the proof into two steps.

(Step 1).
In this step, we prove the lemma for

. From
[
5
, Proposition 1.5.10, p. 27]

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}(X>x)}{x^{a+1}\ell(x)}=\frac{-(a+1)}{\mathbb{E}(e^{\gamma X})},

(3.4)

which completes the proof of the lemma by taking
\widetilde{\ell}(x)=x^{-(a+1)}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}(X>x)
.

(Step 2).
In this step, we prove the lemma for

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}y^{a}e^{-\lambda y^{b}}\mathrm{d}y=\frac{1}{b}\int_{x^{b}}^{\infty}z^{\frac{a+1-b}{b}}e^{-\lambda z}\mathrm{d}z=\frac{x^{a+1-b}e^{-\lambda x^{b}}}{b}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+zx^{-b})^{\frac{a+1-b}{b}}e^{-\lambda z}\mathrm{d}z.

(3.5)

According to dominated convergence theorem, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\int_{x}^{\infty}y^{a}e^{-\lambda y^{b}}\mathrm{d}y}{x^{a+1-b}e^{-\lambda x^{b}}}=\frac{1}{b}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda z}\mathrm{d}z=\frac{1}{\lambda b}.

(3.6)

Consequently, with

replaced by

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\int_{x}^{2x}y^{a}e^{-\lambda y^{b}}\mathrm{d}y}{x^{a+1-b}e^{-\lambda x^{b}}}=\frac{1}{\lambda b}.

(3.7)

According to Lemma

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}(X>2x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell(x)}{\mathbb{E}(e^{\gamma X})}\int_{2x}^{\infty}\frac{\ell(y)}{\ell(x)}y^{a}e^{-\lambda y^{b}}\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\ell(x)\int_{2x}^{\infty}\frac{y^{a+1}}{x}e^{-\lambda y^{b}}\mathrm{d}y.

(3.8)

Therefore, combining (

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}(X>2x)}{\ell(x)x^{a+1-b}e^{-\lambda x^{b}}}

Combining (
5
), (
5.38
) and (
5.49
), we complete the proof of (i).

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\lambda x^{b}}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}T_{43}\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{e^{\lambda(k\delta)^{b}}}{\ell(k\delta)(k\delta)^{a}}.

Also, to prover (

(5.53)

(Step 1)
. In this step, we prove (
5.52
).
According to the definition of

, we have the upper bound

\displaystyle T_{43}\leq\sum_{n<(\log x)^{5/2}}n\beta^{n}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(S_{n}\in[x-k\delta,x-k\delta+1],X_{n}>x-2x^{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n<(\log x)^{5/2}}n\beta^{n}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(|S_{n-1}|>x^{\varepsilon_{0}},X_{n}>x-2x^{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{n<(\log x)^{5/2}}n\beta^{n}\sup_{|z|\leq x^{\varepsilon_{0}}}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(X\in[x-k\delta-z,x-k\delta-z+1],X>x-2x^{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=:T_{43}^{\prime}+T_{43}^{\prime\prime}.

(5.54)

Estimate for
T_{43}^{\prime}
.
Similar to (
5.16
), taking
k_{0}=1/\varepsilon_{0}
, it follows from Markov’s inequality and Lemma
3.1
that

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\lambda x^{b}}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}T_{43}^{\prime}\leq\frac{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(X>x-2x^{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)}{\ell(x)x^{a}e^{-\lambda x^{b}}}\sum_{n<(\log x)^{5/2}}n\beta^{n}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(|X|>x^{\varepsilon_{0}}/n\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\ell(x)x^{a+1-b}e^{-\lambda x^{b}}}{\ell(x)x^{a}e^{-\lambda x^{b}}}\sum_{n<(\log x)^{5/2}}n\beta^{n}\frac{\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left(|X|^{k_{0}}\right)}{(x^{\varepsilon_{0}}/n)^{k_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}x^{-b}\lesssim\frac{e^{\lambda(k\delta)^{b}}}{\ell(k\delta)(k\delta)^{a}}.

(5.55)

Estimate for
T_{43}^{\prime\prime}
.
Noticing that
T_{43}^{\prime\prime}=0
when
k\delta>3x^{\varepsilon_{0}}
, so we only consider the case
k\delta\leq 3x^{\varepsilon_{0}}
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\lambda x^{b}}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}T_{43}^{\prime\prime}\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{e^{\lambda x^{b}}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}\sup_{|z|\leq 4x^{\varepsilon_{0}}}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(X\in[x-z,x-z+1]\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{e^{\lambda x^{b}}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}\sup_{|z|\leq 4x^{\varepsilon_{0}}}\ell(x-z)(x-z)^{a}e^{-\lambda(x-z)^{b}}\lesssim 1

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{e^{\lambda(k\delta)^{b}}}{\ell(k\delta)(k\delta)^{a}}.

(5.56)

Combining (
5
), (
5
) and (
5
), we get (
5.52
).

(Step 2)
. In this step, we prove (
5.53
). We have the following decomposition:

\displaystyle T_{43}=\sum_{n<(\log x)^{5/2}}\beta^{n}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq n}S_{j}\leq x,x-S_{n}\in I,\exists!\ 1\leq j\leq n,\ X_{j}>x-2x^{\varepsilon_{0}},\max_{i\neq j}X_{i}\leq x^{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{n<(\log x)^{5/2}}\beta^{n}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq n}S_{j}\leq x,x-S_{n}\in I,\exists!\ 1\leq j\leq n,\ X_{j}>x-2x^{\varepsilon_{0}},\max_{i\neq j}X_{i}>x^{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=:R_{43}+Q_{43}.

(5.57)

Estimate for
Q_{43}
.
By Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\lambda x^{b}}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}Q_{43}\leq\frac{e^{\lambda x^{b}}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}\sum_{n<(\log x)^{5/2}}n\beta^{n}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{n}>x-2x^{\varepsilon_{0}},\max_{i\leq n-1}X_{i}>x^{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{\lambda x^{b}}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}\sum_{n<(\log x)^{5/2}}n^{2}\beta^{n}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(X>x-2x^{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(X>x^{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{e^{\lambda x^{b}}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(X>x-2x^{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(X>x^{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)\lesssim x^{1-b}\ell(x^{\varepsilon_{0}})(x^{\varepsilon_{0}})^{a+1-b}e^{-\lambda(x^{\varepsilon_{0}})^{b}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x\to+\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

(5.58)

Estimate for
R_{43}
. We have

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{e^{\lambda x^{b}}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}R_{43}

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{e^{\lambda x^{b}}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}\sum_{n<(\log x)^{5/2}}\beta^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\leq x,x-S_{n}\in I,X_{j}>x-2x^{\varepsilon_{0}},\max_{i\neq j}X_{i}\leq x^{\varepsilon_{0}}\right).

(5.59)

According to a similar argument leading to (
5.16
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{e^{\lambda x^{b}}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}R_{43}

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{e^{\lambda x^{b}}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}\sum_{n<(\log x)^{5/2}}\beta^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\leq x,x-S_{n}\in I,|S_{n}-X_{j}|\leq x^{\varepsilon_{0}},\max_{i\neq j}X_{i}\leq x^{\varepsilon_{0}}\right).

(5.60)

Set
W=S_{n}-\max_{j\leq k\leq n}S_{k}
and

, then by the independence among
\{X_{i},1\leq i\leq n\}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq n}S_{j}\leq x,x-S_{n}\in I,|S_{n}-X_{j}|\leq x^{\varepsilon_{0}},\max_{i\neq j}X_{i}\leq x^{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{j}\leq W+Y,X_{j}\in(Y-q,Y-p],|x-Y|\leq x^{\varepsilon_{0}},\max_{i\neq j}X_{i}\leq x^{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}(e^{\gamma X})}\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left(1_{\{|x-Y|\leq x^{\varepsilon_{0}},\max_{i\neq j}X_{i}\leq x^{\varepsilon_{0}},W>-q\}}\int_{-q}^{\min\{W,-p\}}\ell(z+Y)(z+Y)^{a}e^{-\lambda(z+Y)^{b}}\mathrm{d}z\right).

(5.61)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{e^{\lambda x^{b}}}{\ell(x)x^{a}}R_{43}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}(e^{\gamma X})}\int_{-q}^{-p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\left(z<W\right)\mathrm{d}z.

(5.62)
