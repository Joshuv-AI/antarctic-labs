BBP Phase Transition for a Doubly Sparse Deformed Model
Ioana Dumitriu, JD Flynn, Zhichao Wang

Abstract
We prove the equivalent of the Baik, Ben Arous, Péché [ 8 ] phenomenon for a novel, doubly sparse model where both the Wigner noise matrix and signal vector(s) are sparse. Specifically, we consider a deformed sub-Gaussian sparse Wigner ensemble with a fixed number of sub-Gaussian spike vectors of the same-order sparsity added. We show that spike vectors with signals greater than one are correlated with the top eigenvectors of the deformed ensemble and that each spike vector of signal greater than one induces an outlier eigenvalue. Notably, our results hold in the supercritical sparsity regime for the Wigner matrix ( q ≫ log ⁡ n n q\gg\frac{\log n}{n} ) and for any sparse spike vector with an unbounded number of entries ( n ​ p → ∞ np\to\infty ). No further relationship between the sparsities of the noise matrix ( q q ) and spike vectors ( p p ) is necessary. This generalizes the work of Benaych-Georges and Nadakuditi [ 17 ] and Péché [ 61 ] .



Keywords: sparse PCA, sparse Wigner, deformed random matrix models, signal recovery

Spiked Random Matrix Models.

In the modern age of gigantic data sets,

Sparse PCA.

A common setting alteration, and the model deviation most pertinent to this present work, considers sparse PCA where sparsity parameters, such as independent Bernoulli random variables, are applied entrywise to the spike vectors. In an application sense, this sparsity provides practical benefits in a high-dimensional setting, often improving interpretability, increasing efficiency, and reducing model complexity

With the landscape of sparse PCA (under certain prior assumptions) being covered by BBP, we naturally consider the possibility of introducing sparsity across all aspects of the model, which brings us to the central question of this work:

Can we generalize this BBP theorem in the setting where both spikes and noise are sparse?

Doubly Sparse PCA.

Here, we answer the question in the affirmative. We importantly note how the BBP result of
[
17
]
no longer holds even in the case where the nonzero entries of the noise are Gaussian, since invariance is required by their procedure and sparsity disrupts this property. When considering a candidate for recovery, many of the aforementioned BBP results either exclude the problem of recovery entirely and focus on distinguishability via eigenvalues, rely specifically on the orthogonal invariance property (similar to the results of
[
17
]

Similar Sparse Models.

We remark that, while our contributions and consideration of spectrally distinguishing and recovering a sparse spike from sparse noise are novel, the notion of a sparse noisy observation has been around for a while. Future Directions

5.1
Extensions Within BBP

An avenue of exploration directly related to this work involves a result produced in
[
61
]
which extends the BBP to an
n
-dependent number of spikes
r
. Within the context of this work, we see that the reliance on the finite nature of the
r
by
r
matrix (specifically with regards to continuity and convergence properties) serves as the main obstacle to adding an asymptotically growing number of spikes. However, these technical limitations may be removable.

Additionally, one may consider expanding the regime of sparsity for
q
to the critical and sub-critical regimes. As discussed in Section
1.2
, shifting to the assumption of bounded entries in the noise matrix allows for critical sparsity (
q\gtrsim\frac{\log n}{n})
for our result of no spectral outliers, but we would still need a local law result in the style of Lemma
10
for critical sparsity. Further, we believe no BBP style result could be produced in the sub-optimal regime as there would be no way to reliably distinguish between outliers generated by the spikes or spurious outliers from the noise. Thus, for
q\lesssim\frac{\log n}{n}
, a non-spectral approach would be required.

5.2
Sparse PCA: Beyond Spectral Methods

Contiguity, AMP, and Statistical Physics.

While PCA provides a simple solution to both problems in the case of
\theta>1
, the spectral threshold does not always align with the
information-theoretical threshold
. This misalignment stems from PCA assuming very little about the spike; indeed, information can be extracted from knowledge of the spike prior
\mathcal{V}
in the Bayesian setting. For Gaussian, dense noise (
q=1
), a series of works showed that, for certain spike priors and low enough
\theta
, the planted and null models are contiguous, meaning there is no statistic that can distinguish between the spiked and null models.
[
57
]
and
[
50
]
proved this for deterministic and spherical priors, respectively, and
[
62
]
followed with results for certain dense spike priors (spherical, Rademacher, any “sufficiently” sub-Gaussian). However, the latter showed PCA is sub-optimal for non-Gaussian Wigner matrices, leaving the door open for methods that improve on the BBP threshold. Further, their contiguity results for Gaussian Wigner extend only to the above-mentioned spike priors, leaving room for results on Gaussian Wigner models where the spike is drawn from a different prior (such as a sparse one). Various techniques have arisen to construct methods that exploit the signal’s known structure. Born from a statistical physics setting and first applied to problems in compressed sensing
[
14
,
31
]

[
14
,
57

28

Their methods work particularly well when exploring information-theoretic thresholds due to their performance being characterized by a simple deterministic recursion called “state evolution” in the high-dimensional limit. When considering a sparse spike, employing such methods allows for solving the problem below the threshold
\theta<1
. Using standard statistical physics heuristic based methods,
[
54
]
employed these AMP techniques to analyze the minimum mean squared error and provide evidence of the threshold falling below the spectral one when the spike is sufficiently sparse. While these results serve as fortified conjectures, they prompted further work by Perry et al.
[
62
]
and Banks et al.
[
11
]
to explore the values of
p
and
\theta

, the threshold for
\theta
falls below the BBP threshold. For constant

, a wide gap emerged between the solvability thresholds; however, in the limit

, their gap shrinks to
\sqrt{2}
. This limit is taken outside of the context of their work, as they consider

to be a constant throughout their proofs. For simplicity, the two works used a sparse Rademacher spike prior in which

and

[

adapted the above mentioned statistical physics methods of free energy to provide rigorous results and a generalized threshold stylized to the choice of any bounded prior, thus closing the

n

.

Thresholding.

An avenue of study tangential to this current work, though pertinent to the understanding of recovering the sparse spike, involves a non-spectral approach of thresholding the entries of the spiked matrix to construct a polynomial-time algorithm for recovery. 6.1

This first lemma shows that, when

Lemma 9
.

Let

be a symmetric matrix satisfying Assumption

W

Proof.

From

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\lambda_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{nq}}W\odot A)>2+\epsilon\Big)\leq\exp(-I(2+\epsilon)nq)~,

\displaystyle I(\lambda)=\inf\{\frac{r^{2}}{2\alpha}+h_{L}(s):r-m(\lambda)s=\lambda,r\geq 0,s\geq 1\}~,

with

\displaystyle h_{L}(s)=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\{\xi s-L(\xi)+1\}~,\hskip 5.69046ptL(\xi)=\mathbb{E}[\exp(\xi W_{1,2}^{2})]~,\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046pt\alpha:=\lim_{\xi\to\infty}\frac{2\log\mathbb{E}[\exp\xi W_{i,j}^{2}]}{\xi^{2}}<\infty~.

To show

has no outliers in the semicircular distribution defined on

I(2+\epsilon)>0
, seeing as

by the condition

is noted in their work as Remark 1.5 (vi); however, we are concerned with finding an explicit lower bound for

We begin by noticing the square of the sub-Gaussian entries

are sub-exponential with

[
70
]

. Thus,
\mathbb{E}\exp(W_{1,2}^{2})\leq 2
.
We see

\displaystyle h_{L}(s)=\sup_{\xi}\{\xi s-\mathbb{E}[\exp(\xi W_{1,2}^{2})]+1\}\geq s-\mathbb{E}[\exp(W_{1,2}^{2})]+1\geq s-2+1=s-1~.

where we chose
\xi=\|W_{1,2}^{2}\|_{\psi_{1}}=1
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle I(\lambda)\geq\inf_{s\geq 1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\frac{\lambda^{2}+2\lambda m(\lambda)s+m(\lambda)^{2}s^{2}}{2\alpha}+s-1}}\right\}:=\inf_{s\geq 1}\{g(s)\}~.

Taking the first and second derivatives with respect to

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(s)=\frac{\lambda m(\lambda)}{\alpha}+\frac{m(\lambda)^{2}s}{\alpha}+1~\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046ptg^{\prime\prime}(s)=\frac{m(\lambda)^{2}}{\alpha}~.

As the second derivative is positive for all values
s\geq 1

occurs at the zero

When

we see

and plug in our computed root

\inf_{s\geq 1}\{g(s)\}=\begin{cases}\frac{(m(\lambda)+\lambda)^{2}}{2\alpha},&\alpha\geq 1\\
-\frac{\lambda}{m(\lambda)}-\frac{\alpha}{2m(\lambda)^{2}}-1,&\alpha<1\end{cases}~.

To find an explicit constant, we now plug in

1

Therefore, we let

\displaystyle C_{8}:=\min\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\frac{1}{2\alpha},1}}\right\}~,

[

to represent the normalized eigenpairs of the sparse noise matrix
\frac{1}{\sqrt{nq}}W\odot A
. Thus, we move to (

) and observe

\displaystyle|\Im w^{T}R(z_{0})w|=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Im\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\rho_{i}-z_{0}}(w^{T}\mu_{i})^{2}}}\right|=~

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Im\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\rho_{i}-E+i\eta_{0}}{(\rho_{i}-E-i\eta_{0})(\rho_{i}-E+i\eta_{0})}(w^{T}\mu_{i})^{2}}}\right|

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\eta_{0}}{(E-\rho_{i})^{2}+\eta_{o}^{2}}(w^{T}\mu_{i})^{2}}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\eta_{0}}{\kappa^{2}}|w^{T}\mu_{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{0}}{\kappa^{2}}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\tilde{C}n^{-1+\epsilon_{0}}~,

where we used Cauchy Schwarz between unit vector
w
and normalized eigenvector
\mu_{i}

\forall i\in[n]
.
Next, we see

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Re}w^{T}R(z)w-\operatorname{Re}w^{T}R(z_{0})w|=~

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\rho_{i}-E}{(\rho_{i}-E)^{2}}|w^{T}\mu_{i}|^{2}-\operatorname{Re}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\rho_{i}-E+i\eta_{0}}{(\rho_{i}-E-i\eta_{0})(\rho_{i}-E+i\eta_{0})}|w^{T}\mu_{i}|^{2}}}\right|

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\rho_{i}-E}|w^{T}\mu_{i}|^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\rho_{i}-E}{(\rho_{i}-E)^{2}+\eta_{0}^{2}}|w^{T}\mu_{i}|^{2}}}\right|

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{(\rho_{i}-E)((\rho_{i}-E)^{2}+\eta_{0}^{2})}|w^{T}\mu_{i}|^{2}}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{0}}{E-\lambda_{1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\eta_{0}}{(\rho_{i}-E)^{2}+\eta_{0}^{2}}|w^{T}\mu_{i}|^{2}}}\right|

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{0}}{E-\lambda_{1}}|\Im w^{T}R(z_{0})w|

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa^{2}\cdot\tilde{C}n^{-1+\epsilon_{0}}}{\kappa}\cdot\tilde{C}n^{-1+\epsilon_{0}}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\tilde{C}^{2}\kappa n^{-2+2\epsilon_{0}}~.

Thus, we combine these two inequalities to see

\displaystyle|w^{T}R(z)w-w^{T}R(z_{0})w|\leq~

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Re}w^{T}R(z)w-\operatorname{Re}w^{T}R(z_{0})w|+|\Im w^{T}R(z)w-\Im w^{T}R(z_{0})w|

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Re}w^{T}R(z)w-\operatorname{Re}w^{T}R(z_{0})w|+|\Im w^{T}R(z_{0})w|

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\tilde{C}n^{-1+\epsilon_{0}}+\tilde{C}^{2}\kappa n^{-2+2\epsilon_{0}}~,

where we used
|\Im w^{T}R(z)w|=0
as
z\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

6.3
Convergence of Top Eigenvalues

While the following corollary to Theorem 12 in
[
44
]
does not appear in their work, the sub-exponential version can be seen as their Theorem 6 and Theorem 7. As pointed out after Theorem 7 in their work, one can prove the corollary by following the argument outlined in
[
16
, Section 8]
or applying
[
66
, Lemma 64]
to
[
44
, Theorem 12]
.

Corollary 11

(of Theorem 12 in
[
44
]
)
.

For any
D>0
,
\delta\in(0,1)
, and interval satisfying
|I|>C_{1}\frac{\log n}{\delta^{4}n}
, we have

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathcal{N}_{I}-n\int_{I}f_{\rm sc}(x)dx}}\right|\geq\delta n|I|}}\right)<n^{-D}~,

where
\mathcal{N}_{I}:=|I\bigcap\rm{spec}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{nq}}W\odot A)|
and
f_{\rm sc}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{4-x^{2}}
is the density of the semicircular law.

Thus, we can prove the following lemma, which is necessary in our main proof to control the eigenvalues that remain inside the bulk.

Lemma 12
.

For any fixed
i\geq 1
, we have
\lambda_{i}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{nq}}W\odot A)\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}2
.

Proof.

Assume, for sake of contradiction, that there exists some fixed
i\geq 1
such that
\lambda_{i}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{nq}}W\odot A)\not\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}2
. Then, there exist
\bar{\epsilon},\bar{\delta}>0
such that
\mathbb{P}(\lambda_{i}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{nq}}W\odot A)\not\in(2-\bar{\epsilon},2+\bar{\epsilon}):=I)>\bar{\delta}
. Thus,
\mathcal{N}_{I}\leq i-1
.

Observe the negation of Corollary
11
: there exist a
D>0,\delta\in(0,1)
, or interval
I
satisfying
|I|\geq C_{1}\frac{\log n}{\delta^{4}n}
such that

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathcal{N}_{I}-n\int_{I}f_{\rm sc}(x)dx}}\right|\geq\delta n|I|}}\right)\geq n^{-D}~.

We now show the established interval
I:=(2-\bar{\epsilon},2+\bar{\epsilon})
satisfies the conditions of this statement, thus reaching a contradiction. Let
C_{4}:=\int_{I}f_{\rm sc}(x)dx
.

Let
D>0
be large enough such that
\bar{\delta}\geq n^{-D}
. Choose
\delta\leq\frac{C_{4}n-(i-1)}{2n\bar{\epsilon}}\leq C_{4}/(2\bar{\epsilon})
where the latter inequality holds for large enough
n
. Observe
|I|=2\bar{\epsilon}\geq C_{1}{\log n}/({\delta^{4}n})
for large enough n.

For simplicity’s sake, consider values of
n
large enough such that
C_{4}n\geq i-1
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathcal{N}_{I}-n\int_{I}f_{\rm sc}(x)dx}}\right|\geq\delta n|I|}}\right)=~

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(C_{4}n-\mathcal{N}_{I}\geq 2\delta n\bar{\epsilon}}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq~

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(C_{4}n-\mathcal{N}_{I}\geq 2\frac{C_{4}n-(i-1)}{2n\bar{\epsilon}}n\bar{\epsilon}}}\right)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(C_{4}n-\mathcal{N}_{I}\geq C_{4}n-(i-1))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N}_{I}\leq i-1)

\displaystyle\geq~

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\lambda_{i}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{nq}}W\odot A)\not\in(2-\bar{\epsilon},2+\bar{\epsilon})}}\right)

\displaystyle>~

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}

\displaystyle\geq~

\displaystyle n^{-D}~,

thus reaching our desired contradiction.
∎

Diagonal Concentration.

Convergence.

Putting all our results together, we conclude that, entrywise,

\displaystyle\geq

A.1
High Probability Conditioning

We begin by establishing a collection of lemmas which we will use frequently throughout the work.

First, we include the following lemma used to remove conditioning on high probability events
\mathcal{A}_{i}
.

Lemma 16
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a high probability event, i.e.
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})\to 1
. For event
\mathcal{E}
, if
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}|\mathcal{A})\to 1
, then
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})\to 1
.

Proof.

Notice
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}|\mathcal{A})\cdot\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})+\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}|\mathcal{A}^{c})\cdot\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}^{c})\geq\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}|\mathcal{A})\cdot\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})\to 1
.
∎

Now, we are interested in the following high probability result. When proving probabilistic results involving the spike, we often condition on the size of the support being known.

A.2
Support Size Concentration of Spike Vectors

The model in (
2.2

p

would be

Lemma 17
.

For
r

and

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\exists i\in[r]:|\text{supp}(v_{i})|\not\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[np-\log(np)\sqrt{np},np+\log(np)\sqrt{np}}}\right]}}\right)\leq{r}(np)^{-2}\to 0~.

Proof.

Let
S_{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|(b_{i})_{j}|
. We apply the Central Limit Theorem to prove
P(S_{n}\in[np-k,np+k])\to 1,
as

for

\displaystyle P\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{-k}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}}\leq\frac{S_{n}-np}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}}\leq\frac{k}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}}}}\right)\to 1~,

(A.1)

seeing as
\frac{k}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}}=\frac{\log(np)}{\sqrt{1-p}}\to\infty

\frac{S_{n}-np}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}}\to\mathcal{N}(0,1)

Thus, with high probability,
|\text{supp}(v_{i})|\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[np-\log(np)\sqrt{np},np+\log(np)\sqrt{np}}}\right]
.

Note we can use a standard Gaussian tail bound to see
\mathbb{P}(|\text{supp}(v_{i})|\not\in[np-k,np+k])\to 0~,
seeing as

\mathbb{P}(|\text{supp}(v_{i})|\not\in[np-k,np+k])\to\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(|\mathcal{N}(0,1)|\geq\frac{k}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}}}}\right)~,

and

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(|\mathcal{N}(0,1)|\geq\frac{k}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}}}}\right)\leq 2\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-(\log(np)/(1-p))^{2}/2}}\right)\leq(np)^{-2}\to 0~.

Choosing
k_{i}=\log(np)\sqrt{np}
for all
i\in[r]
, we use a union bound to see
P(\exists i\in[r]:|\text{supp}(v_{i})|\not\in[np-k_{i},np+k_{i}])\leq rP(|\text{supp}(v_{i})|\not\in[np-k,np+k])\to 0
seeing as

r\mathbb{P}(|\mathcal{N}(0,1)|\geq\frac{k}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}})\leq 2r\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-(\log(np)/(1-p))^{2}/2}}\right)\to 0.

∎
