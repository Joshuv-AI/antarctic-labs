Dynamic Digital Radiography for Functional Assessment of Unilateral Main Bronchial Stenosis Before and After Treatment: A Case Report
Authors: Hajime Tsuruoka, Yurino Kobori, Hiroshi Handa, Mario Yamada, Takafumi Haraguchi, Junichi Matsumoto, Masamichi Mineshita
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTFunctional assessment of unilateral main bronchial stenosis is challenging because conventional pulmonary function tests and morphological imaging may not adequately reflect side‐specific ventilation abnormalities. Dynamic digital radiography (DDR) enables visualization of respiratory dynamics throughout the breathing cycle. A 75‐year‐old woman presented with cough and dyspnea on exertion was found to have a nodular lesion causing near‐complete obstruction of the left main bronchus. Bronchoscopic snare resection was performed, and histopathological examination confirmed mucoepidermoid carcinoma without metastatic disease. DDR performed before treatment demonstrated marked left–right asymmetry, reduced expansion of the affected lung during inspiration, and mediastinal shift during expiration. After intervention, DDR showed restoration of lung expansion, resolution of mediastinal shift, and improved respiratory synchrony between the two lungs. Pulmonary function testing also improved after treatment. These findings suggest that DDR can provide direct visualization of functional respiratory abnormalities and their resolution after bronchoscopic intervention in unilateral main bronchial stenosis.Keywords: Bronchoscopic intervention, dynamic digital radiography, functional respiratory imaging, unilateral main bronchial stenosis, ventilation asymmetry

Introduction

Unilateral main bronchial stenosis presents a particular challenge in clinical decision‐making, as the functional impact of the lesion is often difficult to assess using standard diagnostic tools. Pulmonary function tests and computed tomography (CT) remain the gold standards for evaluating airway stenosis; however, in patients with severe obstruction or compromised clinical status, these examinations may be difficult to perform or may provide limited functional information [1].

Dynamic digital radiology (DDR) acquires sequential chest radiographs using low‐dose pulsed X‐rays, enabling visualization of respiratory motion, diaphragmatic excursion, and lung volume changes during breathing. PL‐mode is a visualization method in DDR in which temporal changes in X‐ray translucency during the respiratory cycle are projected to enable assessment of regional lung ventilation and left–right asymmetry. Previous studies have demonstrated an association between DDR‐derived functional respiratory metrics and conventional pulmonary function parameters, suggesting its potential utility as a functional imaging modality [2, 3].

However, the clinical application of DDR in unilateral main bronchial stenosis remains limited. Here, we report a case of unilateral main bronchial obstruction in which DDR was used to evaluate respiratory dynamics before and after bronchoscopic intervention, allowing visualization of respiratory phase discrepancies and their resolution following treatment.

Case Report

A 75‐year‐old woman with no significant medical history presented with fever accompanied by cough and dyspnea on exertion. Chest CT revealed a nodular lesion measuring approximately 1.8 cm within the left main bronchus (Figure 1).

Chest computed tomography demonstrates a nodular lesion measuring approximately 1.8 cm in diameter within the lumen of the left main bronchus.

Flexible bronchoscopy demonstrated near‐complete obstruction of the left main bronchus caused by the endobronchial tumour (Figure 2a). Bronchoscopic snare resection was performed, resulting in near‐complete removal of the lesion (Figure 2b). Histopathological examination confirmed the diagnosis of mucoepidermoid carcinoma. Systemic evaluation showed no evidence of metastatic disease, and the patient was managed with airway intervention alone.

Flexible bronchoscopy before intervention. (A) Demonstrates near‐complete obstruction of the left main bronchus by an endobronchial tumour. (B) After bronchoscopic snare resection, the obstructing lesion has been almost completely removed, with restoration of airway patency.

Following treatment, respiratory symptoms improved markedly. Dyspnea decreased from modified Medical Research Council (MMRC) grade 2 to 0, and the patient was able to resume physical activities without limitation. No recurrence was observed during follow‐up.

Pulmonary function testing demonstrated marked improvement after intervention. Forced vital capacity (FVC) increased from 1.89 L before treatment to 2.06 L after treatment. Forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1) improved from 1.05 to 1.81 L. Peak expiratory flow increased from 4.77 to 6.80 L/s.

DDR was performed before and after bronchoscopic intervention. Before intervention, PL‐mode imaging demonstrated marked left–right asymmetry (Figure 3a). During dynamic imaging, expansion of the affected lung was limited at maximum inspiration (Figure 3b), and mediastinal shift toward the contralateral lung was observed at maximum expiration (Figure 3c), indicating impaired ventilation.

Dynamic digital radiography images obtained before and after bronchoscopic intervention. (A) Before intervention, PL‐mode imaging demonstrates marked left–right asymmetry. (B, C) During dynamic imaging, maximum inspiration shows limited expansion of the affected lung, and maximum expiration shows mediastinal shift toward the contralateral lung. (D) After intervention, PL‐mode imaging demonstrates restoration of left–right symmetry. (E, F) Expansion of the affected lung improves at maximum inspiration, and the previously observed mediastinal shift during expiration is no longer evident.

After bronchoscopic intervention, PL‐mode imaging demonstrated restoration of left–right symmetry (Figure 3d). Expansion of the affected lung improved at maximum inspiration (Figure 3e), and the previously observed mediastinal shift during expiration was no longer evident (Figure 3f).

Quantitative DDR analysis confirmed reduced lung field area change on the affected side before treatment, despite preserved diaphragmatic motion, with improvement after intervention and resolution of the respiratory phase discrepancy between the two lungs. Discussion

In this case, DDR enabled direct visualization of functional respiratory changes associated with unilateral main bronchial stenosis before and after bronchoscopic intervention. Prior to treatment, DDR demonstrated impaired ventilation of the affected lung, characterized by reduced lung field area change and a temporal discrepancy between respiratory phases of the two lungs, despite preserved diaphragmatic motion. These findings suggest that air movement generated by diaphragmatic excursion was functionally ineffective on the affected side, likely due to airflow limitation and air trapping distal to the stenotic lesion. After intervention, both DDR and pulmonary function testing demonstrated marked functional improvement.

Following intervention, DDR demonstrated improvement in lung expansion during inspiration and resolution of expiratory mediastinal shift, accompanied by restoration of synchrony between the respiratory phases of the two lungs. These functional changes were consistent with the marked improvement in symptoms after treatment.

Assessment of functional impairment in unilateral bronchial stenosis remains challenging. Conventional pulmonary function tests may be normal or only minimally abnormal because global measurements can obscure asymmetric ventilation. Computed tomography provides anatomical information but does not directly reflect physiological airflow limitation.

DDR offers a noninvasive method for evaluating regional respiratory dynamics. Unlike spirometry, DDR allows side‐specific assessment of lung motion and ventilation timing [4], and unlike ventilation–perfusion scintigraphy, it can be performed rapidly and repeatedly with relatively low radiation exposure [5]. In the present case, DDR clearly demonstrated functional improvement after intervention, supporting its potential utility not only for post‐treatment assessment but also for evaluating the physiological significance of unilateral airway stenosis when treatment indications are uncertain. DDR may have a role in the longitudinal monitoring of airway stenosis, potentially aiding in the assessment of disease progression and the timing of bronchoscopic intervention.

This report has several limitations. In this case, the patient had relatively preserved baseline lung function, which likely contributed to the observed symptomatic improvement. However, as DDR does not directly evaluate gas exchange, additional assessments such as pulmonary scintigraphy may be necessary to fully evaluate the therapeutic effect. This report describes a single case, and quantitative thresholds for defining abnormal respiratory phase discrepancy or lung field area change have not yet been established. Furthermore, DDR cannot currently replace standard pulmonary function testing, as standardized correlations between DDR parameters and spirometric indices remain incompletely defined. Further studies are required to validate DDR and to clarify how DDR‐derived metrics may be integrated into decision‐making for airway intervention.

This case illustrates that DDR can provide clinically meaningful functional information that is not readily obtainable using conventional diagnostic modalities. Visualization of respiratory phase discrepancies and their resolution after treatment highlights the potential value of DDR as a complementary tool for functional evaluation in patients with unilateral main bronchial stenosis.
