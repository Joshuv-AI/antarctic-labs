Differences in response rates to a previsit electronic portal questionnaire in outpatient clinics: A retrospective observational study of the Standard Dermatology Outcome Measures
Authors: Rene F Chen, Udeyvir S Cheema, Beiyu Liu, Cynthia L Green, Matilda W Nicholas, Suephy C Chen
Published: 2026

To the Editor: Patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs), validated surveys that quantify assessments of disease states from patient perspectives, can be used to set clinic encounter agendas. Implementation barriers of PROMs include deployment modality and burden on clinic workflows.1 However, electronic collection methods, especially when automatically integrated into the electronic health record, provide a potential solution by empowering patients to directly input data. The Standard Dermatology Outcome Measures (SDOM) consists of 5 validated PROMs evaluating skin-related quality-of-life, disease severity, itch severity, treatment adequacy, and side effects.2 Since 2023, broad deployment of the SDOM has been implemented via electronic patient portal (e-portal) as part of routine clinical care within our dermatology department. Our study aimed to quantify SDOM response rates via e-portal amongst demographic populations at a large academic medical center as an exploration of feasibility. Given known differences in health literacy and survey engagement among different populations, we hypothesized that response rates of a short dermatology-specific PROM may differ among demographic groups but overall demonstrate high uptake via e-portal.

The SDOM was sent to every patient via e-portal 7 days prior to any non-Mohs dermatology encounter without reminders. Patients were free to ignore the SDOM without impact on the clinical encounter. A patient was considered a responder if they completed at least 1 previsit survey question at any time over the 2-year study period regardless of repeat encounters. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was conducted to study the association between the odds of response and demographics including age, sex, race, and ethnicity.

Of the 65,951 patients assigned the SDOM, 55.1% responded to at least 1 questionnaire (Table I). Men and women responded at similar rates (55.5% vs 54.8%, respectively). Adolescent (12-17 years), Black, and Hispanic patients had the lowest response rates amongst age, race, and ethnic groups. In the multivariable regression analysis, age, sex, race, and ethnicity were significantly associated with questionnaire response (Fig 1). Adolescents had the lowest response rates (15.2%). After adjusting for covariates, Black and Hispanic patients were approximately half as likely to respond to the SDOM as White and non-Hispanic patients, and Asian patients were also 25% less likely to respond than White patients.

Our results corroborate smaller studies3,4 and suggest that large-scale deployment of PROMs via e-portal is a feasible method for capture of valuable patient-reported data. However, there may be gaps in PROM assessment in certain demographic populations, such as adolescent, Black, Asian, and Hispanic patients. Study limitations include English-only PROMs and retrospective design at a single Southeastern urban institution where patient portal engagement is quite high (93.3%), which limit generalizability to other institutions and practices. Other limitations, including partial completion, exclusion of encounter-level data, and caregiver e-portal management for children and elderly patients, may limit correlation of response rates with quality of response or utility. Still, at minimum, having SDOM data at the time of encounter can enable a visit agenda to be pre-articulated. Further work aimed to elucidate and alleviate disparities in PROM collection are needed for accurate representation of patient populations and improvement in healthcare delivery.
