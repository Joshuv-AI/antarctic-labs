Spontaneous spherical symmetry breaking of black holes with resonant hair
José Ferreira, Carlos A. R. Herdeiro, Eugen Radu, Miguel Zilhão

Abstract
Black holes with resonant hair are static, spherical, electrically charged solutions of the Einstein-Maxwell-(gauged-)scalar system. Scalar self-interactions are mandatory for their existence. Initial dynamical studies restricted to spherical symmetry suggested stability; more recently, fully non-spherical dynamical studies revealed instabilities, at least for a particular class of self-interactions. Here, we provide a more detailed study of this instability together with a different decay channel, depending on the chosen solutions. Moreover, considering a second model, we provide evidence that the instabilities may be generic for different classes of self-interactions. We conclude these solutions are dynamically unstable and split into a bosonic lump and a bald black hole (via fission) or implode to the latter (via absorption). In both cases, the non-spherical dynamics seems to be key.

Several uniqueness theorems constrain the properties of BHs. However, these theorems can be evaded in multiple ways and create what are known as hairy BHsolutions – see

II.1
Action and Field Equations

We consider an EMS model described
by the Lagrangian density

\mathcal{L}=\frac{R}{16\pi}-\frac{1}{4}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}-(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}\phi)^{*}\widetilde{\nabla}^{\alpha}\phi-V(\left|\phi\right|)\,,

(1)

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\phi\equiv\nabla_{\mu}\phi+iqA_{\mu}\phi\,,

(2)

Variation of the action with respect to the metric yields the Einstein field equations

R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R=8\pi\left(T_{\mu\nu}^{\text{EM}}+T_{\mu\nu}^{\phi}\right)\,,

(3)

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{\text{EM}}

\displaystyle=F_{\mu}{}^{\alpha}F_{\nu\alpha}-\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}\,,

(4a)

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{\phi}

\displaystyle=2\widetilde{\nabla}_{(\mu}\phi^{*}\widetilde{\nabla}_{\nu)}\phi+g_{\mu\nu}\left[\widetilde{\nabla}^{\alpha}\phi^{*}\widetilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}\phi+V(\left|\phi\right|)\right]\,.

(4b)

Variation with respect to the electromagnetic potential leads to the Maxwell equations

\nabla_{\alpha}F^{\mu\alpha}=j^{\mu}\,,

(5)

where the electromagnetic current sourced by the scalar field is

j^{\mu}=iq\left[(\widetilde{\nabla}^{\mu}\phi)^{*}\phi-\phi^{*}(\widetilde{\nabla}^{\mu}\phi)\right]\,.

(6)

Variation with respect to the scalar field yields

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}\widetilde{\nabla}^{\alpha}\phi=\frac{dV}{d\left|\phi\right|^{2}}\phi\,.

(7)

We consider two different choices for
V(|\phi|)

-ball type potential

(8)

(9)

4|B|\leqslant 1

II.3
Physical Quantities

The minimal coupling between the scalar and electromagnetic fields means the scalar field is electrically charged. A quantum of scalar field has charge
q
. The total charge of a BH-scalar field system can then be decomposed as
17

Q=Q_{h}+Q_{\phi}\,,

(26)

where
Q_{\phi}
is the total charge associated with the scalar field and
Q_{h}
is the charge associated with the horizon region. In the
3+1
formalism, the scalar field charge is computed as

Q_{\phi}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{r_{h}}^{\infty}\sqrt{\gamma}\rho_{e}\,d^{3}x\,,

(27)

where
\gamma
is the determinant of the spatial metric and
\rho_{e}
is the normal projection of the electromagnetic current defined above.
r_{h}
is the radial coordinate of the BH horizon.

As for the BH charge,
Q_{h}
, it is defined via a surface integral at the horizon,

Q_{h}=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_{\mathcal{H}}\sqrt{\gamma^{(2)}}F^{\mu\nu}n_{\mu}\sigma_{\nu}\,d^{2}x\,,

(28)

where
\gamma^{(2)}
is the determinant of the induced two-metric on the horizon
\mathcal{H}
,
n_{\mu}
is the future-directed unit normal to the hypersurface, and
\sigma_{\nu}
is the outward-pointing normal to the horizon surface. To avoid computing surface integrals, we determine the value of
Q_{h}
by computing the total charge
Q
(as explained next) and using
eq.

26
.

To evaluate the total charge and the outgoing electromagnetic radiation, we make use of the Newman–Penrose scalars defined as
25

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu\nu}\left(l^{\mu}k^{\nu}+\bar{m}^{\mu}m^{\nu}\right)\,,

(29a)

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv F_{\mu\nu}\bar{m}^{\mu}k^{\nu}\,,

(29b)

where
l
,
k
and
m
are constructed from an orthonormal triad
u
,
v
,
w
orthogonal to
n^{\mu}
according to

\displaystyle l^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(n^{\alpha}+u^{\alpha}\right)\,,

(30a)

\displaystyle k^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(n^{\alpha}-u^{\alpha}\right)\,,

(30b)

\displaystyle m^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(v^{\alpha}+iw^{\alpha}\right)\,.

(30c)

At sufficiently large coordinate radius
R_{\text{ex}}
the scalars are decomposed in spin-weighted spherical harmonics

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}\phi_{1}^{lm}(t)Y_{lm}^{0}\,,

(31a)

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}\phi_{2}^{lm}(t)Y_{lm}^{-1}\,,

(31b)

36

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{2}\left(E_{\hat{r}}+iB_{\hat{r}}\right)\,,

(32a)

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}

\displaystyle\approx E_{\hat{\theta}}-iE_{\hat{\varphi}}\,.

(32b)

For a non rotating, point-like, charged particle in flat spacetime, the only non-zero component of the electric field is

III
Initial Data

The initial data considered in this work corresponds to asymptotically flat, spherically symmetric BHswith resonant scalar hair in the model (
1
). This type of solutions has been previously constructed and analyzed in
17
;
20
;
21
. Here, the procedure for generating such solutions is briefly summarized and further technical details can be found in the cited references.

The scalar field and the electromagnetic 4-potential are assumed to take the form

\phi=\Phi(r)e^{-i\omega t}\,,\qquad A=U(r)dt\,,

(35)

where
\Phi(r)
denotes the radial profile of the scalar field,
\omega
is its (real) oscillation frequency, and
U(r)
is the electric potential. Throughout this section,
r
denotes the isotropic radial coordinate.

To describe a spherically symmetric spacetime, the following metric ansatz in isotropic coordinates is adopted:

ds^{2}=-e^{2\mathcal{F}_{0}}\frac{S_{0}^{2}}{S_{1}^{2}}dt^{2}+e^{2\mathcal{F}_{1}}S_{1}^{4}d\Sigma^{2}\,,

(36)

where
d\Sigma^{2}
is the line element of three-dimensional flat space, and
\mathcal{F}_{0}(r)
and
\mathcal{F}_{1}(r)
are undetermined functions. The auxiliary functions
S_{0}
and
S_{1}
are defined as

S_{0}\equiv 1-\frac{r_{h}}{r}\,,\qquad S_{1}\equiv 1+\frac{r_{h}}{r}\,,

(37)

with
r_{h}
representing the horizon radius of the BH.

Regularity of the energy-momentum tensor at the horizon imposes the resonance condition

\omega=qU(r_{h})\,.

(38)

Imposing that the electric potential vanishes at spatial infinity, the bound-state condition for the scalar field yields an upper bound on the frequency,

\omega\leq\mu\,.

(39)

These conditions are derived under the assumption that the gauge is chosen such that the electric potential
U(r)
vanishes at spatial infinity.

Substituting the ansatz
eqs.

36
and
35

\mathcal{F}_{0}(r)
,
\mathcal{F}_{1}(r)
,
\Phi(r)
, and
U(r)
. For a given model, specified by the parameters
q
and
\mu,\lambda
(for the potential
eq.

8

\mu,f,B
(for the potential
eq.

9

\omega
and
r_{h}

\omega
and

V
Final Remarks

In this work, we studied the stability of BHswith resonant hair using fully nonlinear
3+1
numerical relativity simulations. Although previous work in spherical symmetry indicated stability, the present analysis has identified two distinct decay mechanisms arising from non-spherically symmetric instabilities: one in which the BHabsorbs the surrounding hair, and another in which the BHis expelled from the scalar hair, leaving behind a stable BS.

It was observed that solutions with more compact hair tend to absorb the scalar field, whereas those with less compact configurations are more susceptible to the ejection of the BH. In some regions of parameter space, we found that the timescale of the decay was correlated with the compactness of the scalar hair. However, this correlation was not universal, and competing factors such as the mass and the charge of the hair and those of the BHalso play a role in determining the timescale of the instability. A systematic exploration of the parameter space is required to better understand the dependence of the timescales of the instability.

These results indicate that the observed instabilities are generic properties of BHswith resonant hair. This instability is caused by the unstable balance between gravitational pull and electromagnetic repulsion between the hair and the BH, which we expect to affect a wider region of parameter space and to be largely independent of the specific scalar potential.
