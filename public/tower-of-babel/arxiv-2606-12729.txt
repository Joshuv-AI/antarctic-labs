Abstract
. We study the relationship between almost toric base diagrams, perfect exceptional classes, and optimal ellipsoid embeddings for H b = ℂ ​ ℙ 1 2 ​ # ​ ℂ ​ P ¯ b 2 H_{b}=\mathbb{CP}^{2}_{1}\#{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}P}}\!\,^{2}_{b} and P b = S 1 2 × S b 2 P_{b}=S^{2}_{1}\times S^{2}_{b} . Starting from a quadrilateral almost toric base diagram with one Delzant corner, we encode the three non-Delzant corners by a recursive triple. We show that every quadrilateral obtained via a well-defined sequence of mutations from the initial diagrams is encoded by a recursive triple in the same way. Moreover, geometric mutation of these diagrams corresponds to algebraic mutation of the associated triples. These algebraic mutations are the recursive operations used to generate the ( p , q ) (p,q) -perfect classes for H H . We apply this dictionary to realize every ( p , q ) (p,q) -perfect class for H H by an explicit sequence of almost toric mutations for suitable values of b b . We also prove the analogous realization result for triples of quasi-perfect classes for P P , showing that these classes are in fact ( p , q ) (p,q) -perfect. Finally, we apply these results to ellipsoid embedding problems, including visible embeddings, visible obstructions, and ATF-visible staircases.

This work investigates the connection between perfect exceptional classes, mutations of almost toric base diagrams (ATBDs), and optimal symplectic embeddings. We focus on the examples of

In this paper, we generalize the work in

1.1.1.
Mutation on ATBDs

We begin by describing mutations on ATBDs. Here, we assume the nodal rays are half-lines starting from a vertex extending into the interior of the polygon. Each nodal ray splits the quadrilateral into two pieces. To perform a
mutation
on an ATBD about a nodal ray, we fix the piece which contains the Delzant corner, and then act on the other piece by the unique
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
transformation which has the nodal ray as an eigenvector and aligns the edges of the vertex the nodal ray emanates from. If we assume that the nodal ray does not intersect two vertices, then the resulting ATBD remains a quadrilateral. The ATBD obtained from a mutation corresponds to the same fibration on either
P,H

The side of the quadrilateral that the nodal ray intersects depends on the side lengths. For instance, as seen in Figure

extends to intersect:

The recursive formulas for determining the result of the mutation will depend on which side the nodal ray extends to intersect. As such, we use different notation for each possible side the nodal ray can extend to intersect.

1.1.2.
Triples from an ATBD

We now roughly describe how we assign a combinatorial triple to each ATBD. This generalizes the author’s work in
[
M1
]
, which did this for a family of ATBDs obtained via mutation from
Q_{H_{b}}^{0}.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a

of the form

\mathcal{T}:=(\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\times\mathbb{Z}^{3}\times\mathbb{Z}^{3},

where for
{\alpha}={\lambda},\mu,\rho

We summarize this construction.

The quadrilateral

has a Delzant corner at

,

corresponds to a singular toric variety: each non-Delzant corner is a fixed point

\frac{1}{q^{2}}(1,pq-1)
. From the triple,
(p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda})
determines the singularity at

(p_{\mu},q_{\mu})
at

and
(p_{\rho},q_{\rho})
at

The nodal rays and direction vectors are then chosen so that the quadrilateral is
\mathcal{T}
-standard; see Definition

is
(q_{\lambda},-p_{\lambda}).
This forces the nodal rays and direction vectors to be determined by Lemma
2.1.4
.

The side lengths are also encoded in the triple. Each element
(p,q,t)
of a triple is required to lie in a set

defined by an integral quadratic condition
(see (
2.2.1
)). In Section
1.2

-perfect class for

for

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{8}(3(p+q)+t),\quad m=\tfrac{1}{8}(p+q+3t),

(1.1.1)

\displaystyle e

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}(p+q+t),\quad f=\tfrac{1}{4}(p+q-t).

Note, for instance, when

Mutations on triples.

On the algebraic side, we define six corresponding mutations of triples. The

or

and

1.2.
Perfect Classes and ATBDs

We now give some background on perfect classes for
H
and
P
, and explain how they can be realized in ATBDs. In particular, the derived coordinates
(d;m)
and
(e,f)
from (
1.1.2
) that controlled the side lengths in Section
1.1.2
turn out to correspond to homology classes that obstruct symplectic embeddings. This is explained in more detail in Section
3
.

For
M=H,P,

a
quasi-exceptional class

{\bf{E}}\in H_{2}(M\#(N+1){\overline{{\mathbb{C}}P}}\!\,^{2},\mathbb{Z})
is a class of self-intersection
-1
with first Chern number
1
. If
{\bf{E}}
is quasi-exceptional and also can be represented by a symplectically embedded
S^{2},
we call
{\bf{E}}
an
exceptional class
. Let
\mathcal{E}(M)
denote the set of all exceptional classes for
M
.

For two coprime integers
p,q
, let
W(p,q)=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{\ell})
denote the weight sequence of
p/q.
This is a sequence determined by the continued fraction of
p/q,
defined in
[
McS
, Definition 1.2.5]
. We say that
{\bf{E}}\in H_{2}(M)
is a
(p,q)-perfect class
if

\tilde{{\bf{E}}}:={\bf{E}}-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}m_{i}e_{i}\in H_{2}(\tilde{M})
is exceptional where

W(p,q)=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{\ell})
,
\tilde{M}
is the
\ell
-fold blow up of
M
, and
H_{2}(\tilde{M})=H_{2}(M)\oplus\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell}\rangle
where
e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell}
are the exceptional divisors. If
\tilde{{\bf{E}}}
has self-intersection
-1
and first Chern number
1
, then we call
{\bf{E}}
a
(p,q)
-quasi-perfect class, i.e.
\tilde{{\bf{E}}}
isn’t necessarily represented by a symplectic
S^{2}.

A
(p,q)
-
unicuspidal symplectic (resp. algebraic) curve
in
M
is a curve that is a smooth, embedded symplectic (resp. complex) surface in
M
everywhere except at one singular point. Locally near this point, it is modeled by the cusp
x^{p}=y^{q}
. In
[
McSi1
, Theorem G]
, McDuff and Siegel demonstrated a correspondence between
(p,q)
-perfect classes for
M=H,P
and rigid
(p,q)
-unicuspidal symplectic curves in
M.

In the special case of
H
, in
[
McSi2
, Theorem F]

1.3.
Sharp embeddings

Results of Casals–Vianna
[
CV
]
and Cristofaro-Gardiner et al.
[
CG-HMP
]
imply that for
M=P_{b},H_{b}
if
Q:=OXVY
is obtained from ATF mutations on
Q_{\bullet,b}^{0},
then for
0<\varepsilon<1,
there is an embedding

(1.3.1)

(1-\varepsilon)E(|OX|,|OY|)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\hookrightarrow}}M

where
E(z_{1},z_{2})
denotes the
4
-dimensional ellipsoid given by

E(z_{1},z_{2}):=\{(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\ |\ \pi\left(\frac{|\zeta_{1}|^{2}}{z_{1}}+\frac{|\zeta_{2}|^{2}}{z_{2}}\right)<1\}.

By considering sequences of mutations for a fixed
b
-value, we can construct sequences of ellipsoid embeddings. This motivates us to consider the ellipsoid embedding function.

For a closed
4
-dimensional symplectic manifold
(M,\omega),
define the ellipsoid embedding function to be

c_{M}(z):=\inf\{{\lambda}\ |\ E(1,z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\hookrightarrow}}{\lambda}M\}

where
z\geq 1
and
{\lambda}M:=(M,{\lambda}\omega).
A lower bound for this function comes from the volume obstruction:

V_{M}(z):=\sqrt{\frac{z}{Vol(M)}}

giving
c_{M}(z)\geq V_{M}(z)
and if there is equality for some
z
-value, we call this a
full filling
.

The function
c_{M}(z)
is said to have a
(infinite) staircase
if it has infinitely many nonsmooth points. There has been much research in determining for which
M
does
c_{M}(z)
have a staircase
[
McS
,
U
,
BHM
,
FM
]
. In fact, classifying the
b
-values such that
c_{H_{b}}(z)
has an infinite staircase was recently completed in a series of papers
[
BHM
,
MM
,
MMW
,
MPW
]
. This classification is intricately tied to the classification of
(p,q)
-perfect classes for
H.
The case for
c_{P_{b}}(z)
has not been classified but various values of
b
have been considered in prior work including
[
U
,
FHM
,
FM
]
.

It is well-known that the function
c_{M}(z)
is determined by considering obstructions from exceptional classes and the volume obstruction
[
MP
,
Mc
,
CG
]
. For each quasi-exceptional class
{\bf{E}},
there is a piecewise linear function
\mu_{{\bf{E}},M}(z)
such that
c_{M}(z)\geq\mu_{{\bf{E}},M}(z)
and

(1.3.2)

c_{M}(z)=\max\{\sup_{{\bf{E}}\in\mathcal{E}(M)}\{\mu_{{\bf{E}},M}(z)\},V_{M}(z)\}.

Example 1.3.1
.

The monotone example
H_{1/3}
has an infinite staircase by
[
CG-HMP
]
. Each nonsmooth point of the staircase is determined by an exceptional class realizing the supremum in (
1.3.2
). The exceptional classes realizing this supremum are the ones considered in Example
1.2.6
for
s^{k}\mathcal{T}_{0}^{H}.
Letting

s^{k}\mathcal{T}_{0}^{H}=((p_{k-1},q_{k-1}),(p_{k},q_{k}),(p_{k+1},q_{k+1})),

the function
c_{H_{1/3}}(z)
has a non-smooth point at each
p_{k}/q_{k}.
These nonsmooth points accumulate at
z_{\infty}:=\lim p_{k}/q_{k}
. Note that as seen in Example
1.2.6
, the value
b=1/3
is
\lim_{k\to\infty}m_{k}/d_{k}
where
(d_{k};m_{k})
is the solution to (
1.1.2
) for each
(p_{k},q_{k}).

Side lengths as obstructions and embeddings

The embedding in (
1.3.1
) gives an upper bound for the ellipsoid embedding function for an interval of
z
-values. Following
[
McSi2
]
, we call the embedding constructed from an ATBD in such a way a
visible embedding.

On the obstruction side, the side lengths of the quadrilateral are directly related
to the obstruction functions from the perfect classes in the triple. Specifically,
for a quadrilateral
Q_{\bullet,b}(\mathcal{T})
where

\mathcal{T}=(\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho})
,
the obstruction function
\mu_{\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bullet}(z)
from the class

\bm{x}_{\lambda}
satisfies

(1.3.3)

\mu_{\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bullet}(z)=\frac{z}{|OY|}

for
z
in an interval containing
p_{\lambda}/q_{\lambda}
, where
p_{\lambda}/q_{\lambda}

is the slope of the nodal ray
\vec{n}_{Y}
and corresponds to the
z
-value of a
nonsmooth point of
\mu_{\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bullet}(z)
. In other words, near
p_{\lambda}/q_{\lambda}
, the
reciprocal of the side length
|OY|
is the slope of the obstruction from the
perfect class at the vertex
Y
. Similarly,
|OX|
is related to the obstruction
from the class of
S\bm{x}_{\rho}
(see Remark
4.1.3
for the precise statement).

Recent work of McDuff–Siegel
[
McSi2
]
used ATBDs to construct
visible
obstructions
. Under certain conditions on the ATBD — specifically, that the
mutation
s
or
\overline{s}
is well-defined (see Lemma
4.1.4
) — they
showed that the visible embedding in (
1.3.1
) is optimal, i.e., it computes a point on the ellipsoid embedding function. Thus, when these conditions hold, the ATBD simultaneously provides the embedding and the obstruction, both encoded in the side lengths.

If
M
has an infinite staircase where infinitely many of the nonsmooth points come from visible embeddings and obstructions, we say that
M
has an
ATF-visible staircase
. McDuff-Siegel showed that the monotone examples
H_{1/3}
and
P_{1}
have ATF-visible staircases.

ATF-visible staircases

Identifying
b
-values with ATF-visible staircases implies that the ATBDs are computing much of the ellipsoid embedding function. While the perfect classes giving the largest obstructions can be realized in the ATBD, how often do the ATBDs also realize the optimal embeddings?

In
[
CG-HMP
]
, the authors considered the case where
M
is a closed toric symplectic
4
-manifold. In such a setting,
M
corresponds to some Delzant polygon
\Omega(M).
We let
\text{Per}(M)
be the affine perimeter of
\Omega(M)
and
\text{Vol}(M)
be the volume of
M
normalized to be twice the area of
\Omega(M).
Note that in our specific examples, we have

\displaystyle\text{Per}(H_{b})

\displaystyle=3-b,\quad\text{Vol}(H_{b})=1-b^{2},

\displaystyle\text{Per}(P_{b})

\displaystyle=2+2b,\quad\text{Vol}(P_{b})=2b.

The authors proved
[
CG-HMP
, Theorem 1.13]
that if
M
has an infinite staircase, then the staircase must accumulate to the solution
z_{\infty}>1
to the following quadratic equation

(1.3.4)

\frac{1}{z}+z=\frac{Per(M)^{2}}{Vol(M)}-2

and at the accumulation point
z_{\infty},
the function must be unobstructed, that is,

c_{M}(z_{\infty})=V_{M}(z_{\infty}).

In the paper
[
M1
]
, for an ATBD
Q_{b}
on
H_{b}
, we considered the sequence of ATBDs given by
y^{k}Q_{b}:=OXV_{k}Y_{k}
. Note that
X
stays constant as after each consecutive mutation, the nodal ray at
Y
extended to intersect
\overrightarrow{XV}.
For a fixed
b
-value, the length of
\overrightarrow{XV}
went to zero, so the limiting shape is a triangle. In the limiting shape, the embedding in (
1.3.1
) corresponds to some
z
-value such that
c_{H_{b}}(z)=V_{H_{b}}(z).
For the cases in
[
M1
]
, this limiting ATBD corresponded to the full filling at the accumulation point.

In fact, we show in Lemma
4.1.6
, if there is a
b
-value such that
y^{k}Q_{b}
is well-defined for all
k
,
|XV_{k}|\to 0
, and the slope of
\overrightarrow{V_{k}Y_{k}}
has irrational limit, then the limiting shape corresponds to a full filling at the accumulation point. By
[
CG-HMP
]
, this does not imply there is a staircase, but it shows that in these cases the ATBD recovers the accumulation point.

However, for a fixed
k
, the ATBD
y^{k}Q_{b}

in
[
M1
]
did not correspond to an optimal embedding. Since
c_{H_{b}}(z)
is strictly above the volume obstruction before the accumulation point, the ATBDs realizing these optimal embeddings must be quadrilaterals, not triangles.

Here we show this is achievable: under a mild ordering condition on the triple (Definition
4.2.1
), for large enough
k
, the mutation
sxy^{k}Q_{b}
is well-defined, and by the work of McDuff–Siegel
[
McSi2
]
, each such mutation yields an optimal embedding, producing infinitely many nonsmooth points and hence an ATF-visible staircase. Thus, these quadrilateral ATBDs produce optimal embeddings away from the
volume curve, before the accumulation point.

Definition 1.3.2
.

Let
Q_{k}=OX_{k}V_{k}Y_{k}
be a sequence of quadrilateral ATBDs obtained via
mutation from
Q_{\bullet,b}^{0}
for
\bullet=H,P
. We say that
Q_{k}

degenerates
affinely to a triangle
if the relevant non-axis affine length tends to
zero. More precisely:

•

if
Q_{k}=y^{k}w^{\prime}Q_{\bullet,b}^{0}=OXV_{k}Y_{k}
, then this means

|XV_{k}|\to 0;

•

if
Q_{k}=x^{k}w^{\prime}Q_{\bullet,b}^{0}=OX_{k}V_{k}Y
, then this means

|YV_{k}|\to 0.

Theorem 1.3.3
.

For each
k\geq 0,
let
w_{k}=y^{k}w^{\prime}
where
w^{\prime}
is some fixed word. Assume
b
is such that
w_{k}Q_{H_{b}}^{0}
(resp.
w_{k}Q_{P_{b}}^{0}
) is well-defined for all
k
and
w_{k}\mathcal{T}_{0}^{H}
(resp.
w_{k}\mathcal{T}_{0}^{P}
) is correctly ordered. If the sequence
w_{k}Q_{H_{b}}^{0}
(resp.
w_{k}Q_{P_{b}}^{0}
) degenerates affinely to a triangle, then
H_{b}
(resp.
P_{b}
) has an ATF-visible staircase.

The triple being correctly ordered is what allows us to determine that a visible mutation sequence is well-defined to prove Theorem
1.3.3
. A similar result holds for
w_{k}=x^{k}w^{\prime}
.

We then show that the conditions of this theorem hold for certain cases for
H.

Corollary 1.3.4
.

For the
b
values where
H_{b}
has an increasing staircase and not a decreasing staircase,
H_{b}
has an increasing ATF-visible staircase. In particular, for infinitely many
b
-values,
H_{b}
has an ATF-visible staircase.

We will prove this in Corollary
4.3.2
. We will explain the difference for increasing and decreasing staircases in Example
4.3.1
.

The classification of
b
-values for which
H_{b}
has an infinite staircase is delicate and has involved several different methods. It remains unclear whether this classification can be recovered from ATBDs
alone. More generally, analogous classification questions remain open for families such as
P_{b}
and for blowups of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
at more than two
points. The results of this paper suggest that ATBDs encode much of the relevant information in these embedding problems.

Full fillings

Letting
\mathcal{T}=((p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda},t_{\lambda}),(p_{\mu},q_{\mu},t_{\mu}),(p_{\rho},q_{\rho},t_{\rho})),
we have that for the case of
Q_{H_{b}}(\mathcal{T})=OXVY
,

|XV|=\pm\frac{m_{\lambda}-d_{\lambda}b}{q_{\rho}q_{\mu}}

and for the case of
Q_{P_{b}}(\mathcal{T})=OXVY,

|XV|=\pm\frac{e_{\lambda}-f_{\lambda}b}{q_{\rho}q_{\mu}}.

Hence, setting
b=m_{\lambda}/d_{\lambda}
or
b=e_{\lambda}/f_{\lambda},
the affine lengths will be zero and the resulting mutation sequence will result in a triangle. In particular, the fact that
m_{\lambda}/d_{\lambda}
is the endpoint of the interval for Theorem
1.2.3
allows us to give the following result.

Proposition 1.3.5
.

Assume there is a
(p,q)
-perfect class for
H
with solutions
(d;m)
to (
1.1.2
). Then, there exists a full filling

E\left(1,\frac{pq-1}{q^{2}}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\hookrightarrow}}\frac{d}{q}H_{m/d}.

Note, in the case where
q=1
this is clear from the toric picture, see Remark
4.1.1
. In general, we expect that for
z<z_{\infty}
, the functions
c_{H_{b}}(z)
can be entirely computed from visible embeddings and obstructions from ATBD. If this is the case, the only times before the accumulation point where we get triangle embeddings correspond to these points where
b=m/d
for some perfect class.

Results of Cristofaro-Gardiner, McDuff, and the author in
[
CG-MM
, Corollary 1.2.7]
imply that for
H_{b}
if there is a full filling
E(1,z)
into
H_{b}
or
P_{b}
where
z
is less than the accumulation point, then
z
must be rational. Their methods used subleading asymptotics of ECH capacities, which is quite different from our methods. Generalizing their result to this specific case, we conjecture that

Conjecture 1.3.6
.

Let
z_{\infty}^{b}
denote the accumulation point for
H_{b}.
The function

c_{b}(z)
is unobstructed for
z<z_{\infty}^{b}
if and only if
b=m/d
for some
(d;m;p,q)\in\mathcal{T}(H).

Note that
[
MM
, Lemma 2.2.13]
showed that there is no perfect class
(d;m;p,q)
with
m/d=1/3
. From
[
CG-HMP
]
, the staircase for
H_{1/3}
does not have any points that lie on the volume before the accumulation point. This is in contrast to
P,
where all the nonsmooth points of
P_{1}
before the accumulation point lie on the volume and there are infinitely many perfect classes
(e,f;p,q)
with

It seems the ATBDs are best at detecting embeddings before the accumulation point, and it remains unclear which sharp embeddings after the accumulation point they can detect.

2.
ATF Mutations from triples

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1.4
, which shows that mutations of the relevant ATBDs are determined by algebraic mutations of triples. We begin in Section
2.1
by studying how mutations affect the nodal rays and edge directions of quadrilaterals; this motivates the definition of mutations of triples. In Section
2.2
, we introduce the algebraic framework of recursive triples, generalizing the generating triples of
[
MMW
]
. Finally, in Section
2.3
, we use this framework to compute the affine side lengths and prove Theorem
1.1.4
.

2.1.
Mutations of Quadrilaterals

In this section, we consider the nodal rays and direction vectors of ATBDs obtained via mutation from
Q_{H_{b}}^{0}
and
Q_{P_{b}}^{0}
. In Definition
2.1.1
, we give a way to describe an ATBD by a triple, and in Lemma
2.1.10
, we show mutations of quadrilaterals agree with mutations on the triples. We defer the side lengths to Section
2.3
as this requires extra care. For more background on ATBDs, we recommend
[
E
,
LS
,
S
]
.

We begin with some relevant background. Let
Q=OXVY
be a
decorated quadrilateral
, by which we mean a
quadrilateral with one Delzant corner at
O
and three nodal rays based at the remaining vertices
X,V,Y
where
Q
is a base diagram for an ATBD for either
H
or
P
. For two adjacent vertices
W_{1},W_{2}
of
Q,
the slope of the edge between
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
is always rational. Hence, we let
\overrightarrow{W_{1}W_{2}}
denote the primitive lattice direction from
W_{1}
to
W_{2}.
We always assume
O
is the Delzant corner at the origin with
\overrightarrow{OX}=(1,0)
and
\overrightarrow{OY}=(0,1).
The decorated quadrilateral
Q
will have three nodal rays pointing into the interior of
Q
based at each of the vertices
X,V,Y
denoted by
{\vec{n}}_{X},{\vec{n}}_{V},{\vec{n}}_{Y}
, respectively. Each nodal ray splits
Q
into two pieces. At each non-Delzant vertex
W
, the mutation fixes the side of
Q
containing the origin and acts on the other side by the matrix
M_{W}\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
defined as the unique shear fixing
{\vec{n}}_{W}
that aligns the two edges meeting at
W.

We only consider mutations when the nodal ray does not intersect another vertex of
Q.
Under this assumption, if
Q
is obtained by mutation from
Q_{H_{b}}^{0}
or
Q_{P_{b}}^{0}
, then it will always have one Delzant corner and three nodal rays with one node on each ray. By work of Symington
[
S
]
, this implies that the mutation matrix
M_{W}
is conjugate to
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.
Hence, if the nodal ray at some vertex is given by
(q,-p),
then the mutation matrix must be of the form

M_{p,q}:=\begin{pmatrix}1-pq&-q^{2}\\
p^{2}&1+pq\end{pmatrix}.

As
M_{p,q}
aligns the edges at the two vertices, if one edge at the vertex is given by
\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-1\end{pmatrix},
then the other edge is forced to be in direction
\begin{pmatrix}q^{2}\\
1-pq\end{pmatrix}.
For each non-Delzant vertex
W
of
Q
, there is an integral affine change of coordinates with linear part
B_{W}\in GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
such that

(2.1.1)

B_{W}\overrightarrow{w_{1}}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-1\end{pmatrix},\quad B_{W}\overrightarrow{w_{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}q^{2}\\
1-pq\end{pmatrix},\quad B_{W}{\vec{n}}_{W}=\begin{pmatrix}q\\
-p\end{pmatrix}

where
\overrightarrow{w_{1}},\overrightarrow{w_{2}}
are the primitive lattice directions of the edges at
W.
The corresponding quadrilateral with no nodal rays corresponds to a singular toric variety. Each vertex is a fixed point of the toric action and locally described by a cyclic quotient singularity of the form
\tfrac{1}{q^{2}}(1,pq-1)
where
p,q
are determined as in (
2.1.1
).

In the local coordinates after applying
B_{W},
the mutation matrix is of the form
M_{p,q}
described above. Note that for any other vector
v
, we have
M_{p,q}
is a shear

M_{p,q}v=v+\det\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}&q\\
v_{2}&-p\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}q\\
-p\end{pmatrix}.

The quadrilaterals we consider will always have the nodal ray at
Y
already
in the standard form
(q_{\lambda},-p_{\lambda})
from (
2.1.1
), so

M_{Y}=M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}
. We use a triple
\mathcal{T}=((p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}),(p_{\mu},q_{\mu}),(p_{\rho},q_{\rho}))
to record the
singularity type at each non-Delzant vertex: the vertices
Y
,
V
,
X
carry
singularities of type
\tfrac{1}{q_{\lambda}}(1,p_{\lambda}q_{\lambda}-1)
,

\tfrac{1}{q_{\mu}}(1,p_{\mu}q_{\mu}-1)
, and
\tfrac{1}{q_{\rho}}(1,p_{\rho}q_{\rho}-1)
, respectively. The following definition makes precise the
conditions under which
\mathcal{T}
fully determines the nodal rays and edge
directions of
Q
.

Definition 2.1.1
.

For
\mathcal{T}=((p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}),(p_{\mu},q_{\mu}),(p_{\rho},q_{\rho}))\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we say a decorated quadrilateral
OXVY
is
\mathcal{T}
-
standard
if the following conditions hold:

i)

{\vec{n}}_{Y}=(q_{\lambda},-p_{\lambda})

ii)

M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}{\vec{n}}_{V}=(q_{\mu},-p_{\mu})

iii)

M_{p_{\mu},q_{\mu}}M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}{\vec{n}}_{X}=(q_{\rho},-p_{\rho}).

iv)

M_{p_{\rho},q_{\rho}}M_{p_{\mu},q_{\mu}}M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&-6\end{pmatrix}

Example 2.1.2
.

We note that
Q_{H_{b}}^{0}
is
\mathcal{T}_{0}^{H}
-standard and
Q_{P_{b}}^{0}
is
\mathcal{T}_{0}^{P}
-standard.

{\Diamond}

Remark 2.1.3
.

Assume that
Q=OXVY
is
\mathcal{T}
-standard such that
s^{2}Q
is well-defined, writing
s^{i}Q=OX_{i}V_{i}Y_{i}
. The first three conditions of a
\mathcal{T}
-standard quadrilateral
Q
imply that

{\vec{n}}_{Y_{0}}=\begin{pmatrix}q_{\lambda}\\
-p_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix},\ {\vec{n}}_{Y_{1}}=\begin{pmatrix}q_{\mu}\\
-p_{\mu}\end{pmatrix},\ \text{and}\ {\vec{n}}_{Y_{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}q_{\rho}\\
-p_{\rho}\end{pmatrix}.

As explained in Remark
2.1.8
, the fourth condition is necessary given (i)-(iii) for topological reasons to correspond to an ATBD on
P,H
. Furthermore, it is also a necessary condition for the algebraic mutations we defined to coincide with the geometric mutations as seen in the proof of Lemma
2.1.10
.

{\Diamond}

The next lemma shows that if
Q
is a
\mathcal{T}
-standard ATBD for
P
or
H
, then the nodal rays and edge directions of
Q
are determined.

Lemma 2.1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=(\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho})=((p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}),(p_{\mu},q_{\mu}),(p_{\rho},q_{\rho}))\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Assume that
Q:=OXVY
is
\mathcal{T}
-standard and corresponds to an ATBD on
P
or
H
, then

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{OY}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix},\quad\quad\quad\overrightarrow{OX}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle{\vec{n}}_{Y}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}q_{\lambda}\\
-p_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix},\quad{\vec{n}}_{V}=M_{Y}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}q_{\mu}\\
-p_{\mu}\end{pmatrix},\quad{\vec{n}}_{X}=\begin{pmatrix}p_{\rho}-6q_{\rho}\\
q_{\rho}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{VY}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-q_{\lambda}^{2}\\
q_{\lambda}p_{\lambda}-1\end{pmatrix},\quad\overrightarrow{XV}=\begin{pmatrix}1+p_{\rho}q_{\rho}-6q_{\rho}^{2}\\
q_{\rho}^{2}\end{pmatrix}

Further, we have
M_{p_{\mu},q_{\mu}}M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}\overrightarrow{XV}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-1\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

The formulas for
\overrightarrow{OY}
,
\overrightarrow{OX}
,
{\vec{n}}_{Y}
, and
{\vec{n}}_{V}
come directly from the definition. By condition iii) of
Q
being
\mathcal{T}
-standard, we have

M_{p_{\mu},q_{\mu}}M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}{\vec{n}}_{X}=\begin{pmatrix}q_{\rho}\\
-p_{\rho}\end{pmatrix},

and by condition iv), we have
M_{p_{\mu},q_{\mu}}M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}=M_{p_{\rho},q_{\rho}}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&-6\end{pmatrix}.

Hence,

{\vec{n}}_{X}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&-6\end{pmatrix}^{-1}M_{p_{\rho},q_{\rho}}\begin{pmatrix}q_{\rho}\\
-p_{\rho}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}p_{\rho}-6q_{\rho}\\
q_{\rho}\end{pmatrix}

as desired.
For the statement about
\overrightarrow{VY},
this is immediate as we must have
M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}\overrightarrow{VY}=-\overrightarrow{OY}.
The statement about
\overrightarrow{XV}
follows because the computation of
{\vec{n}}_{X}
determines the monodromy matrix at
{\vec{n}}_{X}
, which must align
\overrightarrow{OX}
and
\overrightarrow{XV}
.

For the last statement, as
Q
is an ATBD for
H,P,
we can do a mutation about the nodal ray at
Y.
Let
Q_{y}=OX_{y}V_{y}Y_{y}
be the mutated diagram. Regardless of where the nodal ray
{\vec{n}}_{Y}
intersects
Q
, we have that
M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}\overrightarrow{XV}=\overrightarrow{V_{y}Y_{y}}.
By condition ii) of Definition
2.1.1
, we have the nodal ray at
Y_{y}
is
(q_{\mu},-p_{\mu}).
This implies that the matrix
M_{p_{\mu},q_{\mu}}
must align
M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}\overrightarrow{XV}=\overrightarrow{V_{y}Y_{y}}
with
\overrightarrow{OY}
as desired.

∎

Definition 2.1.5
.

Given a triple
\mathcal{T}=((p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}),(p_{\mu},q_{\mu}),(p_{\rho},q_{\rho}))
, we let
Q(\mathcal{T})
denote the decorated quadrilateral whose nodal rays and primitive edge directions are those described in Lemma
2.1.4
. At this stage,
Q(\mathcal{T})
records only the direction data; the affine side lengths will be specified later. For this to define a quadrilateral, we require
\overrightarrow{VY}
and
\overrightarrow{XV}
to be non-parallel.

Lemma 2.1.6
.

If
q_{\lambda},q_{\mu},q_{\rho}>0,
then the nodal rays of
Q(\mathcal{T})
point into the interior of the quadrilateral.

Proof.

We have that
{\vec{n}}_{Y}
points into the positive quadrant if
q_{\lambda}>0.
To verify that it points into the interior, we also check that
\det({\vec{n}}_{Y},\overrightarrow{YV})>0
, which follows immediately. Note that the other cases follow from this computation; the statement about the nodal ray at
{\vec{n}}_{V}
follows as the
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
matrix
M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}
brings
{\vec{n}}_{V},\overrightarrow{XV}
to the standard form. Similarly, the statement about
{\vec{n}}_{X}
follows as the
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
matrix
M_{p_{\mu},q_{\mu}}M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}
acts similarly near the vertex at
X.

∎

Recall from Definition
1.1.1
, our notation for the different mutations we can perform on an ATBD. After performing a mutation
w
to a
\mathcal{T}
-standard ATBD
Q,
we describe in Lemma
2.1.10
for which triple
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, the mutated quadrilateral
wQ
is
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
-standard. To do this, we define recursive mutations on triples. These recursive mutations will be considered more in Section
2.2
.

Definition 2.1.7
.

Given a triple
\mathcal{T}:=\left((p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}),(p_{\mu},q_{\mu}),(p_{\rho},q_{\rho})\right):=(\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho})
define

\nu_{\lambda}:=p_{\rho}q_{\mu}-q_{\rho}p_{\mu}\quad\text{and}\quad\nu_{\rho}:=p_{\mu}q_{\lambda}-q_{\mu}p_{\lambda}.

Define the three mutated triples
x\mathcal{T},y\mathcal{T},s\mathcal{T}
as follows:

\displaystyle x\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle:=\left(\bm{x}_{\lambda},\nu_{\lambda}\bm{x}_{\mu}-\bm{x}_{\rho},\bm{x}_{\mu}\right),

\displaystyle y\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle:=\left(\bm{x}_{\mu},\nu_{\rho}\bm{x}_{\mu}-\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\rho}\right),

\displaystyle s\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle:=\left(\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho},(6p_{\lambda}-q_{\lambda},p_{\lambda})\right).

Further, we define the inverse
\overline{x},\overline{y},\overline{s}
mutations of
x,y,s
as:

\displaystyle\overline{x}\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle:=\left(\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\rho},\nu_{\lambda}\bm{x}_{\rho}-\bm{x}_{\mu}\right),

\displaystyle\overline{y}\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle:=\left(\nu_{\rho}\bm{x}_{\lambda}-\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\rho}\right),

\displaystyle\overline{s}\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle:=\left((q_{\rho},6q_{\rho}-p_{\rho}),\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\mu}\right).

In particular, we have

\overline{x}x\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T},\quad\overline{y}y\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T},\quad\overline{s}s\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}.

Remark 2.1.8
.

We make a few remarks about condition iv) of being
\mathcal{T}
-standard.

i)

If
Q
is an ATBD for either
P
or
H
and has condition (i)-(iii) of being
\mathcal{T}
-standard, then condition iv) follows. To see this, consider performing a nodal trade at each of the four vertices of the moment polytope for either
M=P_{b},H_{b}
. Let
\pi:M\to B
be the fibration with this base diagram. The preimage of the boundary is a symplectic torus that is Poincare dual to
c_{1}(M),
see
[
S
, Prop 8.2]
. Let
\gamma
denote a counter-clockwise oriented loop in the base near the boundary that crosses each nodal ray, the preimage
\pi^{-1}(\gamma)
can be viewed as a Lagrangian torus bundle over
\gamma
or the boundary of the disk normal bundle of
T,
the smoothed toric boundary. Using this, we can compute the affine monodromy of the torus bundle over
\gamma
is
\begin{pmatrix}1&c_{1}^{2}(M)\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
. In the case of
M=P,H,
we have
c_{1}^{2}(M)=8
. Condition iv) of being
\mathcal{T}
-standard computes the affine monodromy of the torus bundle given three nodal trades. If we also did a nodal trade at the origin, the mutation matrix at the origin is
\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.
As such, we must have that

\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}M_{p_{\rho},q_{\rho}}M_{p_{\mu},q_{\mu}}M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&8\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},

which implies condition iv):

M_{p_{\rho},q_{\rho}}M_{p_{\mu},q_{\mu}}M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&-6\end{pmatrix}.

ii)

Let
S=\begin{pmatrix}6&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
, so
s\mathcal{T}=((p_{\mu},q_{\mu}),(p_{\rho},q_{\rho}),S(p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda})).
We can recover this
S
from condition iv) of Definition
2.1.1
. We have the matrices in Definition
2.1.1
are acting on the nodal rays coordinates of the form
(q,-p).
The matrix
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
brings
(q,-p)
to
(p,q).
Note that

J^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&-6\end{pmatrix}J=-S.

The negative sign is explained as after doing an
s
-mutation the new nodal ray at
X_{s}
is in direction
-(q_{\lambda},-p_{\lambda})
.

{\Diamond}

To prove mutations on triples describe the mutations on quadrilaterals, we note this basic result about shear matrices, which we will regularly use.

Lemma 2.1.9
.

If
M_{p,q}
is the shear fixing
(q,-p),
then for any shear matrix
M_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},
we have

M_{p,q}M_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}M^{-1}_{p,q}=M_{p^{\prime\prime},q^{\prime\prime}}

where
(q^{\prime\prime},-p^{\prime\prime})=M_{p,q}(q^{\prime},-p^{\prime}).

Proof.

This follows immediately by definition of the shear matrices.

We now show the recursive definition of triples in Definition
2.1.7
aligns with the mutation on ATBD.

Lemma 2.1.10
.

Suppose that
Q=OXVY
is
\mathcal{T}
-standard for
\mathcal{T}=((p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}),(p_{\mu},q_{\mu}),(p_{\rho},q_{\rho}))
. For
w=y,x,s,\overline{x},\overline{y},\overline{s},
assuming that
wQ
is well-defined, we have
wQ
is
w\mathcal{T}
-standard.

Proof.

The claim about the
y
-mutation is the most straightforward, and similar to the other claims, so we omit the details of that case.

We begin by checking that
xQ=OX_{x}V_{x}Y
is
x\mathcal{T}
-standard.
As the left element of
x\mathcal{T}
, the vertex
Y
, and
\overrightarrow{OY}
all stay fixed, condition i) follows
immediately. For condition ii), we check that

(2.1.2)

M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}\left(-\vec{n}_{X}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}q_{x\mu}\\
-p_{x\mu}\end{pmatrix}.

By using condition iii) that
Q
is
\mathcal{T}
-standard, we have that

\displaystyle M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}\left(-\vec{n}_{X}\right)

\displaystyle=-M_{p_{\mu},q_{\mu}}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}q_{\rho}\\
-p_{\rho}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=-\bigl(\begin{pmatrix}q_{\rho}\\
-p_{\rho}\end{pmatrix}-\det\begin{pmatrix}q_{\rho}&q_{\mu}\\
-p_{\rho}&-p_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}q_{\mu}\\
-p_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}\bigr)

\displaystyle=-\bigl(\begin{pmatrix}q_{\rho}\\
-p_{\rho}\end{pmatrix}-\nu_{\lambda}\begin{pmatrix}q_{\mu}\\
-p_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}\bigr)

\displaystyle=-\begin{pmatrix}-q_{x\mu}\\
p_{x\mu}\end{pmatrix}

gives (
2.1.2
).
For condition iii), the new nodal ray at
X_{x}
is given by
M_{X}\vec{n}_{V}

where
M_{X}
is the shear that fixes
\vec{n}_{X}
. Hence, we must check that

M_{p_{x\mu},q_{x\mu}}M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}M_{X}\vec{n}_{V}=\begin{pmatrix}q_{\mu}\\
-p_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}.

Using condition ii) of
Q
being
\mathcal{T}
-standard, we have that

\vec{n}_{V}=M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}q_{\mu}\\
-p_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}
, so we obtain that

M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}M_{X}\vec{n}_{V}=\begin{pmatrix}q_{\mu}\\
-p_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}+\det(\vec{n}_{V},\vec{n}_{X})\,M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}\vec{n}_{X}.

Since
M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, we have

\det(\vec{n}_{V},\vec{n}_{X})=\det\left(M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}\vec{n}_{V},M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}\vec{n}_{X}\right)=\det\begin{pmatrix}q_{\mu}&-q_{x\mu}\\
-p_{\mu}&p_{x\mu}\end{pmatrix}=-\nu_{\lambda}.

where the second equality uses condition ii) and (
2.1.2
).
Applying

M_{p_{x\mu},q_{x\mu}}^{-1}
, we get

\displaystyle M_{p_{x\mu},q_{x\mu}}^{-1}\left(\begin{pmatrix}q_{\mu}\\
-p_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}+\nu_{\lambda}\begin{pmatrix}q_{x\mu}\\
-p_{x\mu}\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}q_{\mu}\\
-p_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}+\left(-\det\begin{pmatrix}q_{\mu}&q_{x\mu}\\
-p_{\mu}&-p_{x\mu}\end{pmatrix}+\nu_{\lambda}\right)\begin{pmatrix}q_{x\mu}\\
-p_{x\mu}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}q_{\mu}\\
-p_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}

as desired. Condition iv) follows as Lemma
2.1.9
implies
that
M_{p_{x\mu},q_{x\mu}}=M_{p_{\mu},q_{\mu}}^{-1}M_{p_{\rho},q_{\rho}}M_{p_{\mu},q_{\mu}}
, so

M_{p_{\mu},q_{\mu}}M_{p_{x\mu},q_{x\mu}}M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}=M_{p_{\rho},q_{\rho}}M_{p_{\mu},q_{\mu}}M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}.

We now verify that
sQ
is
s\mathcal{T}
-standard. Unlike the other cases,
here we must use condition iv) to verify the first three conditions. Let

sQ=OX_{s}V_{s}Y_{s}
; we have that

\vec{n}_{X_{s}}=-\begin{pmatrix}q_{\lambda}\\
-p_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}
,

\vec{n}_{Y_{s}}=M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}\vec{n}_{V}
, and

\vec{n}_{V_{s}}=M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}\vec{n}_{X}
. As
Q
satisfies
conditions ii) and iii) of being
\mathcal{T}
-standard, conditions i) and
ii) hold for
sQ
being
s\mathcal{T}
-standard. For condition iii), we
want to check that

M_{p_{\rho},q_{\rho}}M_{p_{\mu},q_{\mu}}\begin{pmatrix}-q_{\lambda}\\
p_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}p_{\lambda}\\
-6p_{\lambda}+q_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}
, but this holds because the
left-hand side equals

M_{p_{\rho},q_{\rho}}M_{p_{\mu},q_{\mu}}M_{p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}}\begin{pmatrix}-q_{\lambda}\\
p_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&-6\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}-q_{\lambda}\\
p_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}p_{\lambda}\\
-6p_{\lambda}+q_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}

as desired. Condition iv) follows again by Lemma
2.1.9
.
For the inverse mutations, the result follows because
\overline{w}wQ=Q
, so
if
wQ
is
w\mathcal{T}
-standard then applying
\overline{w}
recovers
Q

being
\mathcal{T}
-standard.

∎

2.2.

2.1.7

\displaystyle s\mathcal{T}

.
The next lemma proves that if
\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{X}_{\bullet}^{3},

Lemma 2.2.1
.

(i)

If
\bm{x}:=(p,q,t)\in\mathcal{X}_{H}

) for
\bm{x}

) for

(ii)

\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{P}

) for
\bm{x}
, then

) for

.

Proof.

For either statement, we can check that

For (i), assuming

x\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{X}^{3}

assuming
\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{X}^{3}

Definition 2.2.2
.

We say that two integral vectors
\bm{x}_{0}=(p_{0},q_{0},t_{0}),\bm{x}_{1}=(p_{1},q_{1},t_{1})\in\mathcal{X}

are
\nu
-compatible for an integer
\nu
if

\bm{x}_{1}^{T}A\bm{x}_{0}=4\nu.

Lemma 2.2.3
.

[
MM
, Lem 3.1.2]

Suppose that
\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{1}
are integral vectors
(p_{i},q_{i},t_{i})
for
i=0,1
. If
\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{1}\in\mathcal{X}
are
\nu
-compatible,
then
\bm{x}_{2}:=\nu\bm{x}_{1}-\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{1}
are also
\nu
-compatible and
\bm{x}_{2}\in\mathcal{X}.

Proof.

Since
A
is symmetric,

\bm{x}_{2}^{T}A\bm{x}_{2}=\nu^{2}\bm{x}_{1}^{T}A\bm{x}_{1}-2\nu\bm{x}_{1}^{T}A\bm{x}_{0}+\bm{x}_{0}^{T}A\bm{x}_{0}=8\nu^{2}-8\nu^{2}+8=8

implying that
\bm{x}_{2}\in\mathcal{X}.
Additionally, we have that

(\nu\bm{x}_{1}-\bm{x}_{0})^{T}A\bm{x}_{1}=8\nu-\bm{x}_{0}^{T}A\bm{x}_{1}=4\nu.

∎

Corollary 2.2.4
.

[
MM
, Cor 3.1.3]

Any two integral vectors
\bm{x}_{i}=(p_{i},q_{i},t_{i})\in\mathcal{X}_{\bullet}
,
i=0,1
that are
\nu
-compatible can be extended to a sequence
\bm{x}_{i},i\geq 0,
using recursion parameter
\nu
, where each successive pair is
\nu
-compatible and
\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{\bullet}
for all
i\geq 0
. Further, the values
(d_{i},m_{i})
that satisfy (
1.1.2
) or
(e_{i},f_{i})
that satisfy (
1.1.2
) for each
\bm{x}_{i}
satisfy the same recursion.

The notion of recursive triple we define next will show that as we iterate various mutations every point in the triple will continue to be in
\mathcal{X}^{3}.
The first two conditions ensure that
\bm{x}_{y\mu},\bm{x}_{x\mu}\in\mathcal{X}.
The third condition ensures we can iterate the
x,y
-mutations. In other words, it guarantees that
\bm{x}_{xy\mu},\bm{x}_{yx\mu}\in\mathcal{X}
as well.

Definition 2.2.5
.

A
recursive triple
is a triple
(\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho})\in\mathcal{X}_{\bullet}\times\mathcal{X}_{\bullet}\times\mathcal{X}_{\bullet}
such that for some
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}
the following conditions hold:

(i)

\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\mu}
are
\varepsilon t_{\rho}
-compatible and
\varepsilon t_{\rho}=p_{\mu}q_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}q_{\mu}

(ii)

\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho}
are
\varepsilon t_{\lambda}
-compatible and
\varepsilon t_{\lambda}=p_{\rho}q_{\mu}-p_{\mu}q_{\rho}

(iii)

\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\rho}
are
(t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}-\varepsilon t_{\mu})
-compatible and
t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}-\varepsilon t_{\mu}=p_{\rho}q_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}q_{\rho}

If
\varepsilon=1,
we call the triple
sign-matching
, and if
\varepsilon=-1
, we have the triple
sign-alternating
.

Note that if
\mathcal{T}
is sign-matching, the
x
-mutation (resp.
y
-mutation) is a recursion by
t_{\lambda}
(resp.
t_{\rho}
). On the other hand, if
\mathcal{T}
is sign-alternating, the
x
-mutation (resp.
y
-mutation) is a recursion by
-t_{\lambda}
(resp.
-t_{\rho}
).

Remark 2.2.6
.

This notion of a recursive triple is a generalization of the notion of generating triple from
[
MMW
]
. In
[
MMW
]
, the only mutations that were considered were the
x,y
-mutations. Additionally, in
[
MMW
]
, we were not considering triples obtained via mutation
\mathcal{T}_{0}^{H},
but a more restrictive subset of triples. See Example
2.2.7
for some context of why we require a more general notion of triples from the recursion perspective.

Additionally, not all triples that correspond to ATBD mutated from
Q_{H_{b}}^{0}
and
Q_{P_{b}}^{0}
are generating triples in the sense of
[
MMW
]
. For instance, in
[
MMW
]
, the authors required

p_{\lambda}/q_{\lambda}<p_{\mu}/q_{\mu}<p_{\rho}/q_{\rho}

and
(p_{\bullet},q_{\bullet})\geq(0,0)
, but this is not the case for triples that describe ATBD. See Example
2.3.5
for such examples.

Example 2.2.7
.

In
[
MMW
]
, the notion of generating triple assumed that
t>0
and used
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
to determine the sign of
t
in finding
(d;m)
in (
1.1.2
). Note that in
[
MM
, Lemma 2.2.1]
, it was shown that given a
(p,q,\varepsilon t)\in\mathcal{X}
, there was at most one value of

such that
(d;m)
are integers. For the case of
P
, this is not the case as
(e,f)
is a solution with
\varepsilon=1
and
(f,e)
with

are solutions to (

). This is one motivation to incorporate the sign in the

-variable.

We must also incorporate the sign to ensure the recursion in performing
x,y
-mutations extends from the
(p,q)
-coordinates to the
t
-coordinate. If we consider the
(p,q)
-coordinates of the starting triple, but we instead have
t>0
for each element, we have the triple

(\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho}):=((1,1,2),(2,1,1),(4,1,1)).

We have
\bm{x}_{\lambda}^{T}A\bm{x}_{\rho}=12,
which does equal

so
\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\rho}
are
(p_{\rho}q_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}q_{\rho})
-compatible, but we do not have that

so they are not
(t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}-t_{\mu})

\bm{x}_{x\mu}\not\in\mathcal{X}.
As such, we do not insist

is positive. The choice is determined by which sign gives integer values to (

((1,1,2),(2,1,-1),(4,1,1)),
and now we do have that

Then, we can check that

x((1,1,2),(2,1,-1),(4,1,1))=((1,1,2),(0,1,-3),(2,1,-1)),

and now we do have
\bm{x}_{x\mu}\in\mathcal{X}.

Proposition 2.2.8
.

(i)

All triples obtained by mutation from

.

Lemma 2.2.10
.

If
\bm{x}_{1}=(p_{1},q_{1},t_{1}),\bm{x}_{2}=(p_{2},q_{2},t_{2})
are
(p_{2}q_{1}-p_{1}q_{2})

(ii)

If it is also the case that

(2.2.2)

The first is an immediate computation simplifying
\bm{x}_{1}^{T}A\bm{x}_{2}=4(p_{2}q_{1}-p_{1}q_{2}).
The second follows from substituting the expression for

Lemma 2.2.11
.

For
(p,q,t)\in\mathcal{X}_{H}
with

satisfying (

\frac{m}{d}>\frac{1}{3}\iff t>0\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{m}{d}<\frac{1}{3}\iff t<0.

The statement about

is similar as (

\frac{e}{f}=\frac{p+q+t}{p+q-t}.

∎

Lemma 2.2.12
.

[

, Cor 3.15]

Let
p_{k},q_{k}
,

be increasing sequences that both satisfy the recursion
r_{k+1}=tr_{k}-r_{k-1}
where

are positive for

Then the ratios
(p_{k}/q_{k})_{k\geq 1}
form a monotone sequence that is strictly increasing if

2.2.8
.

Proof of Proposition
2.2.8
.

For both statements, the conditions of the recursive triple ensure that by Lemma
2.2.1
and Corollary
2.2.4
, we have
w\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{X}_{\bullet}\times\mathcal{X}_{\bullet}\times\mathcal{X}_{\bullet}
for
\bullet=P,H
for
w=x,y,s,\overline{x},\overline{y},\overline{s}.
Hence, it remains to check the other conditions of being a recursive triple.

We prove the result for
y\mathcal{T}
first; the other cases are similar. Let
\varepsilon=1
if
\mathcal{T}
is sign-matching and
\varepsilon=-1
if
\mathcal{T}
is sign-alternating. Then

\varepsilon t_{\rho}=p_{\mu}q_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}q_{\mu},\qquad\varepsilon t_{\lambda}=p_{\rho}q_{\mu}-p_{\mu}q_{\rho},

and

t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}-\varepsilon t_{\mu}=p_{\rho}q_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}q_{\rho}.

By definition,

y\mathcal{T}=(\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{y\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho}),\qquad\bm{x}_{y\mu}=\varepsilon t_{\rho}\bm{x}_{\mu}-\bm{x}_{\lambda}.

We first check condition (i). By Corollary
2.2.4
,
\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{y\mu}
are
\varepsilon t_{\rho}
-compatible, since
\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\mu}
are
\varepsilon t_{\rho}
-compatible. Additionally,

\displaystyle p_{y\mu}q_{\mu}-p_{\mu}q_{y\mu}

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon t_{\rho}p_{\mu}-p_{\lambda})q_{\mu}-p_{\mu}(\varepsilon t_{\rho}q_{\mu}-q_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=p_{\mu}q_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}q_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon t_{\rho}.

Hence condition (i) holds for
y\mathcal{T}
.

Next, we check condition (ii). We have

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{y\mu}^{T}A\bm{x}_{\rho}

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon t_{\rho}\bm{x}_{\mu}-\bm{x}_{\lambda})^{T}A\bm{x}_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon t_{\rho}\bm{x}_{\mu}^{T}A\bm{x}_{\rho}-\bm{x}_{\lambda}^{T}A\bm{x}_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon t_{\rho}(4\varepsilon t_{\lambda})-4(t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}-\varepsilon t_{\mu})

\displaystyle=4t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}-4t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}+4\varepsilon t_{\mu}

\displaystyle=4\varepsilon t_{\mu}.

Thus
\bm{x}_{y\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho}
are
\varepsilon t_{\mu}
-compatible. We also need to check the corresponding determinant identity. We compute

\displaystyle p_{\rho}q_{y\mu}-p_{y\mu}q_{\rho}

\displaystyle=p_{\rho}(\varepsilon t_{\rho}q_{\mu}-q_{\lambda})-(\varepsilon t_{\rho}p_{\mu}-p_{\lambda})q_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon t_{\rho}(p_{\rho}q_{\mu}-p_{\mu}q_{\rho})-(p_{\rho}q_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}q_{\rho})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon t_{\rho}(\varepsilon t_{\lambda})-(t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}-\varepsilon t_{\mu})

\displaystyle=t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}-t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}+\varepsilon t_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon t_{\mu}.

So condition (ii) holds.

Finally, we check condition (iii). In the new triple
y\mathcal{T}
, the left and right entries are
\bm{x}_{\mu}
and
\bm{x}_{\rho}
. By assumption,
\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho}
are
\varepsilon t_{\lambda}
-compatible, and

p_{\rho}q_{\mu}-p_{\mu}q_{\rho}=\varepsilon t_{\lambda}.

Thus it remains only to verify that

t_{\mu}t_{\rho}-\varepsilon t_{y\mu}=\varepsilon t_{\lambda}.

This follows from the definition of the recursion.

Therefore condition (iii) holds.

Thus
y\mathcal{T}
is a recursive triple with the same value of
\varepsilon
. The proofs for
x\mathcal{T},\overline{x}\mathcal{T},\overline{y}\mathcal{T}
are similar.

We now prove the result for
s\mathcal{T}
. Let
\varepsilon=1
if
\mathcal{T}
is sign-matching and
\varepsilon=-1
if
\mathcal{T}
is sign-alternating. We will show that
s\mathcal{T}
is a recursive triple with sign
-\varepsilon
.

Write
s\mathcal{T}=(\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho},\bm{x}_{s\rho}).

By the definition of the
s
-mutation, we have
t_{s\rho}=-t_{\lambda}.

We first check condition (i). We need
\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho}
to be
-\varepsilon t_{s\rho}
-compatible. Since
-\varepsilon t_{s\rho}=\varepsilon t_{\lambda},

this follows from the assumption that
\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho}
are
\varepsilon t_{\lambda}
-compatible. Moreover,

p_{\rho}q_{\mu}-p_{\mu}q_{\rho}=\varepsilon t_{\lambda}=-\varepsilon t_{s\rho}.

Hence condition (i) holds.

We now check condition (ii). In
s\mathcal{T}
, condition (ii) requires
\bm{x}_{\rho},\bm{x}_{s\rho}
to be
-\varepsilon t_{\mu}
-compatible. Since
\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\rho}
are
(t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}-\varepsilon t_{\mu})
-compatible, we have

\bm{x}_{\rho}^{T}A\bm{x}_{\lambda}=4(t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}-\varepsilon t_{\mu}).

Thus, to show that

\bm{x}_{\rho}^{T}A\bm{x}_{s\rho}=-4\varepsilon t_{\mu},

it is enough to show that

\bm{x}_{\rho}^{T}A\bm{x}_{\lambda}-\bm{x}_{\rho}^{T}A\bm{x}_{s\rho}=4t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}.

Expanding the left-hand side, this is equivalent to

\displaystyle 2(p_{\lambda}(p_{\rho}-7q_{\rho})+q_{\lambda}(p_{\rho}+q_{\rho})+t_{\lambda}t_{\rho})

\displaystyle=4t_{\lambda}t_{\rho},

\displaystyle p_{\lambda}(p_{\rho}-7q_{\rho})+q_{\lambda}(p_{\rho}+q_{\rho})

\displaystyle=t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}.

This holds by Lemma
2.2.10
. Therefore
\bm{x}_{\rho},\bm{x}_{s\rho}
are
-\varepsilon t_{\mu}
-compatible.

We also need to verify the determinant identity

-\varepsilon t_{\mu}=p_{s\rho}q_{\rho}-p_{\rho}q_{s\rho}.

Expanding the right-hand side gives

p_{s\rho}q_{\rho}-p_{\rho}q_{s\rho}=(6p_{\lambda}-q_{\lambda})q_{\rho}-p_{\lambda}p_{\rho}.

By Lemma
2.2.10
, applied to
\bm{x}_{\lambda}
and
\bm{x}_{\rho}
, we have

t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}=p_{\rho}(p_{\lambda}+q_{\lambda})+q_{\rho}(q_{\lambda}-7p_{\lambda}).

Since

t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}-\varepsilon t_{\mu}=p_{\rho}q_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}q_{\rho},

we obtain

-\varepsilon t_{\mu}=q_{\rho}(6p_{\lambda}-q_{\lambda})-p_{\lambda}p_{\rho}.

Thus

p_{s\rho}q_{\rho}-p_{\rho}q_{s\rho}=-\varepsilon t_{\mu},

as desired. So condition (ii) holds.

Finally, we check condition (iii). In
s\mathcal{T}
, condition (iii) requires
\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{s\rho}
to be compatible with

t_{\mu}t_{s\rho}-(-\varepsilon)t_{\rho}=t_{\mu}t_{s\rho}+\varepsilon t_{\rho}.

Since
t_{s\rho}=-t_{\lambda}
, this quantity is

-t_{\lambda}t_{\mu}+\varepsilon t_{\rho}.

Thus we want to show that

\bm{x}_{\mu}^{T}A\bm{x}_{s\rho}=4(-t_{\lambda}t_{\mu}+\varepsilon t_{\rho}).

As
\bm{x}_{\mu}^{T}A\bm{x}_{\lambda}=4\varepsilon t_{\rho}
, it is enough to show that

\bm{x}_{\mu}^{T}A\bm{x}_{\lambda}-\bm{x}_{\mu}^{T}A\bm{x}_{s\rho}=4t_{\lambda}t_{\mu}.

Expanding the left-hand side, we have

\bm{x}_{\mu}^{T}A\bm{x}_{\lambda}-\bm{x}_{\mu}^{T}A\bm{x}_{s\rho}=2(p_{\lambda}(p_{\mu}-7q_{\mu})+q_{\lambda}(p_{\mu}+q_{\mu})+t_{\lambda}t_{\mu}).

Thus it remains to show that

p_{\lambda}(p_{\mu}-7q_{\mu})+q_{\lambda}(p_{\mu}+q_{\mu})=t_{\lambda}t_{\mu},

which holds by Lemma
2.2.10
. Hence the compatibility condition in (iii) holds.

It remains to verify the determinant identity for condition (iii), namely

p_{s\rho}q_{\mu}-p_{\mu}q_{s\rho}=t_{\mu}t_{s\rho}+\varepsilon t_{\rho}=-t_{\lambda}t_{\mu}+\varepsilon t_{\rho}.

Equivalently, we need to show that

(6p_{\lambda}-q_{\lambda})q_{\mu}-p_{\mu}p_{\lambda}=\varepsilon t_{\rho}-t_{\lambda}t_{\mu}.

By Lemma
2.2.10
, applied to
\bm{x}_{\lambda}
and
\bm{x}_{\mu}
, we have

p_{\lambda}(p_{\mu}-7q_{\mu})+q_{\lambda}(p_{\mu}+q_{\mu})=t_{\lambda}t_{\mu}.

Subtracting this identity from

p_{\mu}q_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}q_{\mu}=\varepsilon t_{\rho}

gives

(6p_{\lambda}-q_{\lambda})q_{\mu}-p_{\mu}p_{\lambda}=\varepsilon t_{\rho}-t_{\lambda}t_{\mu},

as desired.

Thus
s\mathcal{T}
satisfies the recursive-triple conditions with sign
-\varepsilon
. Hence
s\mathcal{T}
is sign-alternating if
\mathcal{T}
is sign-matching, and sign-matching if
\mathcal{T}
is sign-alternating. The proof for
\overline{s}\mathcal{T}
is similar.

∎

To prove Theorem
1.1.4
, it will be helpful to have the following identities. We have chosen not to include them in the definition for simplicity.

Lemma 2.2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=(\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho})
be a recursive triple. If
\mathcal{T}
is a sign-matching recursive triple, set
\varepsilon=1.
Otherwise, set
\varepsilon=-1.
Then, the following identities hold:

(i)

\varepsilon t_{\mu}=p_{\lambda}p_{\rho}+q_{\lambda}q_{\rho}-6q_{\rho}p_{\lambda}

(ii)

t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}q_{\mu}=\varepsilon(t_{\lambda}q_{\lambda}+t_{\mu}q_{\mu}+t_{\rho}q_{\rho})

Proof.

We have (i) is Lemma
2.2.10
(ii) taking
k=-t_{\mu}.

For (ii), using condition (iii) in the definition of a recursive triple, we have

t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}-\varepsilon t_{\mu}=p_{\rho}q_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}q_{\rho}.

Multiplying by
q_{\mu}
gives

t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}q_{\mu}=p_{\rho}q_{\lambda}q_{\mu}-p_{\lambda}q_{\rho}q_{\mu}+\varepsilon t_{\mu}q_{\mu}.

Thus it remains to show that

p_{\rho}q_{\lambda}q_{\mu}-p_{\lambda}q_{\rho}q_{\mu}=\varepsilon(t_{\lambda}q_{\lambda}+t_{\rho}q_{\rho}).

Using the determinant identities from a recursive triple, we compute

\displaystyle\varepsilon(t_{\lambda}q_{\lambda}+t_{\rho}q_{\rho})

\displaystyle=(p_{\rho}q_{\mu}-p_{\mu}q_{\rho})q_{\lambda}+(p_{\mu}q_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}q_{\mu})q_{\rho}

\displaystyle=p_{\rho}q_{\lambda}q_{\mu}-p_{\mu}q_{\rho}q_{\lambda}+p_{\mu}q_{\lambda}q_{\rho}-p_{\lambda}q_{\mu}q_{\rho}

\displaystyle=p_{\rho}q_{\lambda}q_{\mu}-p_{\lambda}q_{\rho}q_{\mu}.

This proves (ii).

Lastly, we give some identities which will be necessary later, but they also show how the

-mutations interact with each other.

Lemma 2.2.14
.

For a recursive triple
\mathcal{T}=(\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho}),
we have

\bm{x}_{y\mu}=S\bm{x}_{\lambda}+\varepsilon t_{\mu}\bm{x}_{\rho}\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{x}_{x\mu}=S^{-1}\bm{x}_{\rho}+\varepsilon t_{\mu}\bm{x}_{\lambda}.

It follows that

S\bm{x}_{{\overline{y}}{\lambda}}=-\bm{x}_{{\overline{x}}\rho}.

Proof.

For the
(p,q)
-coordinates, we want to show

\begin{pmatrix}p_{y\mu}\\
q_{y\mu}\end{pmatrix}=S\begin{pmatrix}p_{\lambda}\\
q_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}+\varepsilon t_{\mu}\begin{pmatrix}p_{\rho}\\
q_{\rho}\end{pmatrix}.

The right hand side expands as

S\begin{pmatrix}p_{\lambda}\\
q_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}+\varepsilon t_{\mu}\begin{pmatrix}p_{\rho}\\
q_{\rho}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}6p_{\lambda}-q_{\lambda}\\
p_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}+\varepsilon t_{\mu}\begin{pmatrix}p_{\rho}\\
q_{\rho}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}6p_{\lambda}-q_{\lambda}+\varepsilon t_{\mu}p_{\rho}\\
p_{\lambda}+\varepsilon t_{\mu}q_{\rho}\end{pmatrix}.

Using the definition of the
y
-mutation, we want to show

\displaystyle\varepsilon t_{\rho}p_{\mu}-p_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=6p_{\lambda}-q_{\lambda}+\varepsilon t_{\mu}p_{\rho}

\displaystyle\varepsilon t_{\rho}q_{\mu}-q_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=p_{\lambda}+\varepsilon t_{\mu}q_{\rho},

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\varepsilon(t_{\rho}p_{\mu}-t_{\mu}p_{\rho})=7p_{\lambda}-q_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\varepsilon(t_{\rho}q_{\mu}-t_{\mu}q_{\rho})=p_{\lambda}+q_{\lambda}.

In the case where
\varepsilon=1,
this is
[
M1
, Lemma 4.6(i)]
. The proof where
\varepsilon=-1
follows similarly to
[
M1
, Lemma 4.6(i)]
. The
t
-coordinate also agrees, since

t_{y\mu}=\varepsilon t_{\rho}t_{\mu}-t_{\lambda},

while the third coordinate of

S\bm{x}_{\lambda}+\varepsilon t_{\mu}\bm{x}_{\rho}

is

-t_{\lambda}+\varepsilon t_{\mu}t_{\rho}.

For the second identity, we can similarly derive that in the case where
\varepsilon=1
, these are directly the identities

p_{\rho}+q_{\rho}=\varepsilon(p_{\mu}t_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}t_{\mu})\quad\text{and}\quad p_{\rho}-7q_{\rho}=\varepsilon(q_{\lambda}t_{\mu}-q_{\mu}t_{\lambda})

from

[
M1
, Lemma 4.6(ii)]
. The case of
\varepsilon=-1
follows similarly.

To see the last statement, we apply the left identity to
\overline{y}\mathcal{T}=(\bm{x}_{{\overline{y}}{\lambda}},\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\rho}),
which gives that

\bm{x}_{\mu}=S\bm{x}_{{\overline{y}}{\lambda}}+\varepsilon t_{\lambda}\bm{x}_{\rho},

or equivalently

-\bm{x}_{{\overline{x}}\rho}=\bm{x}_{\mu}-\varepsilon t_{\lambda}\bm{x}_{\rho}=S\bm{x}_{{\overline{y}}{\lambda}}.

∎

Remark 2.2.15
.

Note that the relation
S\bm{x}_{{\overline{y}}{\lambda}}=-\bm{x}_{{\overline{x}}\rho}
is merely an algebraic identity given the definition of the recursive sequences. It is not the case that both
\bm{x}_{{\overline{y}}{\lambda}}
and
\bm{x}_{{\overline{x}}\rho}
will have positive

so it is not giving a relationship on quasi-perfect classes.

4.1.
Embeddings and ATBDs

We begin by giving some background information to motivate why the ATBDs give us embedding results.

Let
OXVY
be an almost toric base diagram for
M=H_{b},P_{b}
where
O
is the standard Delzant corner at the origin. Then, by
[
CG-HMP
,
CV
]
for all

(1-\varepsilon)E(|OX|,|OY|)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\hookrightarrow}}M.

This embedding corresponds to an upper bound for one particular point of the function
c_{M}(z).
For
\bullet=P,H
, we often let
c_{\bullet,b}(z)
denote the function for
H,P
accordingly. If this embedding corresponds to a point on the volume obstruction, we can conclude that it is sharp. Before moving on to visible obstructions, we will first prove Proposition
1.3.5
. First, we give an example of this in the case
q=1
and then give the general proof.

Example 4.1.1
.

This case was pointed out by Dusa McDuff. Note that in the case where
(p,q)=(2n,1)
, this full filling can be seen immediately from the toric picture using the Traynor trick
[
T
]
. In particular, if
q=1,
Proposition
1.3.5
is

E(1,p-1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\hookrightarrow}}dH_{m/d}.

In this case, (
1.1.2
) give

and

The vertices of the moment polygon of

are
(0,0),(d,0),(d-1,1),(0,1)
using that

We can clearly cut this into

standard triangles of size
1.
In the case where

the claim is not immediate from the toric pictures.

{\Diamond}

Proof of Proposition
1.3.5
.

We first note that every perfect class
(p,q)
in
\mathcal{T}(H)
appears as the left entry of some triple
\mathcal{T}=((p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda},t_{\lambda}),(p_{\mu},q_{\mu},t_{\mu}),(p_{\rho},q_{\rho},t_{\rho}))\in\mathcal{T}(H)

is defined for some interval of

-values denoted

where
m_{\lambda}/d_{\lambda}
is either

Hence, for all
b\in(b_{1},b_{2}),

for
0<\varepsilon<1,
there is an embedding

(1-\varepsilon)(E(|OX|_{b},|OY|_{b}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\hookrightarrow}}H_{b}

where the formulas for
|OX|_{b},|OY|_{b}
are given in (

) where here we are emphasizing that

are functions of
b
. As the ellipsoid embeddings are continuous in

taking the limit as

implies that there is an embedding

(1-\varepsilon)(E(|OX|_{m_{\lambda}/d_{\lambda}},|OY|_{m_{\lambda}/d_{\lambda}}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\hookrightarrow}}H_{m_{\lambda}/d_{\lambda}}.

Note that when

|XV|_{m_{\lambda}/d_{\lambda}}=0,
which implies that the above embedding is a full filling.

Using the expression for

we obtain

|OY|_{m_{\lambda}/d_{\lambda}}=\frac{p_{\lambda}q_{\lambda}-1}{d_{\lambda}q_{\lambda}}

where we used that

For

Expanding the numerator gives

\displaystyle m_{\rho}^{\prime}m_{\lambda}-d_{\rho}^{\prime}d_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=(m_{\rho}-q_{\rho})m_{\lambda}-(d_{\rho}-3q_{\rho})d_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-(d_{\lambda}d_{\rho}-m_{\lambda}m_{\rho})+q_{\rho}(3d_{\lambda}-m_{\lambda}).

Translating into
(p,q,t)
-coordinates, for the

For the cross term, expanding the product gives

\displaystyle d_{\lambda}d_{\rho}-m_{\lambda}m_{\rho}=\frac{1}{8}\left((p_{\lambda}+q_{\lambda})(p_{\rho}+q_{\rho})-t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}\right).

Substituting these back into the numerator expression, we obtain

\displaystyle m_{\rho}^{\prime}m_{\lambda}-d_{\rho}^{\prime}d_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\left(t_{\lambda}t_{\rho}-(p_{\lambda}+q_{\lambda})(p_{\rho}-7q_{\rho})\right).

\displaystyle m_{\rho}^{\prime}m_{\lambda}-d_{\rho}^{\prime}d_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=q_{\lambda}q_{\rho}.

Finally, substituting this result back into the fractional expression for

|OX|=\frac{q_{\lambda}q_{\rho}}{d_{\lambda}q_{\rho}}=\frac{q_{\lambda}}{d_{\lambda}}.

Hence, we obtain

4.2.
Correctly Ordered Triples

In order to prove Theorem
1.3.3
, we use Corollary
4.1.5
. This corollary concerns when
s
or
\overline{s}
is well-defined. The assumptions in Theorem
1.3.3
about correctly ordered triples ensure that we can guarantee that
s
or
\overline{s}
is well-defined for a sequence of mutations.

Definition 4.2.1
.

We say a recursive triple
\mathcal{T}=(\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho})
where
\varepsilon=1
if
\mathcal{T}
is sign-matching and
\varepsilon=-1
otherwise is
correctly ordered
if the following ordering conditions hold:

i)

3+2\sqrt{2}<p_{\lambda}/q_{\lambda}<p_{\mu}/q_{\mu}<p_{\rho}/q_{\rho}
,

ii)

(0,0,2)<(p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda},\varepsilon t_{\lambda}),(p_{\rho},q_{\rho},\varepsilon t_{\rho})\leq(p_{\mu},q_{\mu},\varepsilon t_{\mu})
.

Here inequalities between triples are interpreted coordinatewise.

Under the assumption that a triple is correctly ordered, we now deduce various consequences about sequences of mutations of that triple.

Lemma 4.2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=(\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho})
be a recursive triple such that
\varepsilon t_{\lambda},\varepsilon t_{\rho}>0
and
p_{\bullet},q_{\bullet}>0
where
\varepsilon=1
if
\mathcal{T}
is sign-matching and
-1
otherwise. Then, the triple is ordered, i.e. we have that

p_{\lambda}/q_{\lambda}<p_{\mu}/q_{\mu}<p_{\rho}/q_{\rho}.

Proof.

Since
q_{\lambda},q_{\mu},q_{\rho}>0
, the identities of a recursive triple

\varepsilon t_{\lambda}=p_{\rho}q_{\mu}-p_{\mu}q_{\rho},\qquad\varepsilon t_{\rho}=p_{\mu}q_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}q_{\mu}

imply

\frac{p_{\mu}}{q_{\mu}}<\frac{p_{\rho}}{q_{\rho}},\qquad\frac{p_{\lambda}}{q_{\lambda}}<\frac{p_{\mu}}{q_{\mu}}.

∎

Lemma 4.2.3
.

If
\mathcal{T}=(\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho})
is correctly ordered, then
w\mathcal{T}
is correctly ordered where
w
is any word in
x,y.

Proof.

It is sufficient to check for when
w=x,y.
Assume
w=x.
Then, we have
x\mathcal{T}=(\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{x\mu},\bm{x}_{\mu})
where

\bm{x}_{x\mu}=\varepsilon t_{\lambda}\bm{x}_{\mu}-\bm{x}_{\rho}.

We first show condition (ii) of being correctly ordered holds.
Since
\varepsilon t_{\lambda}>2
and
\bm{x}_{\rho}\leq\bm{x}_{\mu}
coordinate wise,

\bm{x}_{x\mu}-\bm{x}_{\mu}=(\varepsilon t_{\lambda}-1)\bm{x}_{\mu}-\bm{x}_{\rho}\geq(\varepsilon t_{\lambda}-2)\bm{x}_{\mu}>0.

Condition (i) holds for
x\mathcal{T}
by Lemma
4.2.2
as
\mathcal{T}
is correctly ordered. A similar proof holds when
w=y.

∎

In this section,
\mathrm{acc}_{\bullet}(b)
is defined via the solution
z_{\infty}\geq 1
given in (
1.3.4
) for
\bullet=P,H
. Hence,
\mathrm{acc}_{H}(b)
is the solution

to

z+\frac{1}{z}=\frac{(3-b)^{2}}{1-b^{2}}-2

and

\mathrm{acc}_{P}(b)
is the solution

to

z+\frac{1}{z}=\frac{(2+2b)^{2}}{2b}-2.

We outline some properties of this function now.

Lemma 4.2.4
.

The function
\mathrm{acc}_{H}
is strictly decreasing on
(0,1/3)
and strictly increasing on
(1/3,1)
. The function
\mathrm{acc}_{P}
is strictly decreasing on
(0,1)
and strictly increasing on
(1,\infty)
.

Proof.

The function

z\mapsto z+\frac{1}{z}

is strictly increasing for
z>1
. Therefore the monotonicity of

\mathrm{acc}_{H}
is the same as the monotonicity of
f(b):=\frac{(3-b)^{2}}{1-b^{2}}-2.
The sign of

is the same as the sign of

∎

Lemma 4.2.5
.

Let
(d;m;p,q)
or
(e,f;p,q)
be a quasi-perfect class for
H
or

Let

Then, by (

), we have that

\displaystyle z_{0}+\frac{1}{z_{0}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{(3-m/d)^{2}}{1-(m/d)^{2}}-2\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{2}+q^{2}+2}{pq-1}

As the function

increases for

\frac{p}{q}+\frac{q}{p}<\frac{p^{2}+q^{2}+2}{pq-1}

∎

Lemma 4.2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=(\bm{x}_{\lambda},\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\rho})

p_{\lambda}/q_{\lambda}<p_{\mu}/q_{\mu}<\mathrm{acc}_{\bullet}({\alpha}_{\mu})<p_{\rho}/q_{\rho}<\mathrm{acc}_{\bullet}({\alpha}_{\rho})

The ordering of

∎

Lemma 4.2.8
.

For
\bullet=H,P
, assume that

Lemma 4.2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a correctly ordered recursive triple.
Assume that

Proof.

As

degenerates to a triangle and

Define

Let

By the affine triangular degeneration, the limiting triangle has vertices

Let

Then, by the proof of Lemma
4.1.6

is constant and

Example 4.3.3
.

In contrast to Example

, we explain why Corollary
