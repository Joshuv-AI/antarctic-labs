Regularity and singularity of the blow-up curve for a wave equation with a derivative nonlinearity and a scale-invariant damping
Ahmed Bchatnia, Makram Hamouda, Firas Kaabi, Takiko Sasaki, Hatem Zaag

Abstract
In this article, we investigate the blow-up behavior of solutions to the one-dimensional damped nonlinear wave equation, namely ∂ t 2 u − ∂ x 2 u + μ 1 + t ​ ∂ t u = | ∂ t u | p ( p > 1 ) . \partial_{t}^{2}u-\partial_{x}^{2}u+\frac{\mu}{1+t}\partial_{t}u=|\partial_{t}u|^{p}\quad(p>1). Under the assumption of sufficiently large and smooth initial data, we establish that the blow-up curve is continuously differentiable ( 𝒞 1 \mathcal{C}^{1} ). A key step in our analysis involves the characterization of the blow-up profile of the solution. The proof relies on transforming the equation into a first-order system and adapting the techniques of Sasaki in Sasaki2018 ; Sasaki2019 which have elegantly extended the method of Caffarelli and Friedman Caffarelli1986 to nonlinear wave equations with time derivative nonlinearity, but without the scale-invariant term ( μ = 0 \mu=0 ).

1
Introduction

We consider the nonlinear wave equation with damping:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u-\partial_{x}^{2}u+\dfrac{\mu}{1+t}\partial_{t}u=|\partial_{t}u|^{p},&x\in\mathbb{R},\;t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\\
\partial_{t}u(x,0)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
p>1
is a constant and the nonlinearity
s\mapsto s^{p}
belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{4}
for
s\geq 0
.

It is well-known
(
HH2021
, Theorem 2.3)
that the blow-up for (
1.1
) occurs, at least for small data, for the following values of the nonlinearity exponent
p
:

1<p<p_{G}(1,\mu):=1+\frac{2}{\mu},\quad\mu>0,

(1.2)

where
p_{G}(1,\mu)
denotes the Glassey exponent, which is a good candidate for characterizing the threshold between blow-up and global existence of (
1.1
) in dimension
N=1
. Note that for higher dimensions this “critical” exponent is given by
\displaystyle p_{G}(N,\mu):=1+\frac{2}{N-1+\mu}
; see e.g.
HH2021
.
Throughout this article, we assume that condition (
1.2
) is met, in line with the objectives of this study.

Let
R^{*}
and
T^{*}
be arbitrary positive constants. We define the following sets:

\displaystyle B_{R^{*}}

\displaystyle:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:|x|<R^{*}\},

(1.3)

\displaystyle K_{-}(x_{0},t_{0})

\displaystyle:=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty):|x-x_{0}|<t_{0}-t\},

(1.4)

\displaystyle K_{R^{*},T^{*}}

\displaystyle:=\bigcup_{x\in B_{R^{*}}}K_{-}(x,T^{*}).

(1.5)

The blow-up time function is defined by

T(x):=\sup\{t\in(0,T^{*}):|\partial_{t}u(x,t)|<\infty\},\quad x\in B_{R^{*}}.

(1.6)

In this paper, we refer to the set

\Gamma=\{(x,T(x)):x\in B_{R^{*}}\},

as the
blow-up curve
. For notational convenience, we shall identify
\Gamma
with the function
T
itself.

This work has two primary objectives: first, to establish the continuous differentiability (
\mathcal{C}^{1}-
regularity) of
T
for appropriately chosen initial conditions. We now review relevant analytical results from prior studies of blow-up curves in nonlinear wave equations. Most existing research has focused on equations of the form

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\partial_{x}^{2}u=F(u),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\ t>0,

with the associated blow-up curve defined by

\hat{T}(x)=\sup\{t\in(0,T^{*}):|u(x,t)|<\infty\}.

We emphasize that our definition of the blow-up curve (
1.6
) differs from these classical formulations. The starting point of this line of research is the work of Caffarelli and Friedman
Caffarelli1985
;
Caffarelli1986
, who studied the nonlinear wave equation
\square u=u^{p}
and proved that, under suitable assumptions on the data, the blow-up set is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
space-like surface, which reduces to a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
blow-up curve
\hat{T}
in one dimensional space. Their approach relies on the positivity of the fundamental solution for the linear wave operator and on a comparison to the associated ODE
w^{\prime\prime}=w^{p}
. This yields bounds on
u
and its derivatives in terms of the distance to the blow-up boundary. A blow-up scaling combined with the maximum principle then shows that the limiting blow-up cones are flat, and this geometrical property translates into the
\mathcal{C}^{1}-
regularity of
\hat{T}
.

Later, Godin
Godin2002
studied the one–dimensional Liouville equation
u_{tt}-u_{xx}=e^{u}
and gave a detailed description of the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
and analytic regularity of the blow-up curve. Building on the earlier
\mathcal{C}^{1}-
theory, he showed that the blow-up curve is actually
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
and that, for mixed boundary problems, its analyticity near the boundary depends, in a subtle and discrete way, on the choice of boundary conditions. In the presence of Dirichlet boundary conditions, a refined description of the blow-up curve for one-dimensional nonlinear wave equations was obtained by Ishiwata and Sasaki
IshiwataSasakiDirichlet2020
. The study of blow-up curves was extended to systems of nonlinear wave equations by Uesaka
Uesaka2009
.
More recently, Sasaki revisited this program for the derivative-type nonlinearity

\square u=|u_{t}|^{p}
in a series of works
Sasaki2018
;
Sasaki2019
.
Building on the ideas of Caffarelli and Friedman, Sasaki introduced the characteristic–ODE method, which leads to a modified dynamical
system along the rays
x\pm t=c
and allows the study of the blow-up geometry in this new setting. In addition, for semilinear equations of the form
\square u=F(u)
, Sasaki established convergence and further regularity properties of the blow-up curve in
SasakiConvergence2021
;
SasakiRegularity2022
.

In contrast to these smoothness results, Merle and Zaag
Merle2005
;
Merle2012
demonstrated that singular points can occur in blow-up curves, revealing the more complex behavior possible in these systems. We also point out recent refinements of the blow-up rate for related semilinear wave equations, both in the non-scaling invariant and in the superconformal regimes; see Hamza and Zaag
HamzaZaagNonScaling2021
;
HamzaZaagSuperconformal2025
. In addition, a number of related blow-up and lifespan results have been obtained for damped wave-type models with derivative or mixed nonlinearities.
For weakly coupled wave equations with scale-invariant damping and derivative-type terms, sharp blow-up and lifespan estimates were derived in Hammouda and Hamza works; see
MH2
;
MH4
. Their works also extend to the generalized Tricomi equation with mixed nonlinearities where
Blow-up and lifespan estimates were established in
MH55
, while
HHP2
treats a damped wave equation in the Einstein–de Sitter spacetime with a derivative-type nonlinearity.
These works further highlight how time-dependent damping and derivative nonlinearities influence the blow-up and the lifespan, and they are complementary to our present focus on the geometry and regularity of the blow-up curve.

As mentioned above, several results have been established on
\hat{T}
when there are no nonlinear terms involving the derivative of the solution. On the other hand, to the best of our knowledge, only two results have been found concerning
\hat{T}
with nonlinear terms involving the derivative of the solution.

Ohta and Takamura
Ohta1998
investigated the nonlinear wave equation

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\partial_{x}^{2}u=(\partial_{t}u)^{2}-(\partial_{x}u)^{2},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.7)

The above equation admits an exact linearization through the transformation
v(x,t)=\exp\{-u(x,t)\}
,
i.e.
,
u(x,t)=-\log v(x,t)
, which reduces it to the classical wave equation
\partial_{t}^{2}v-\partial_{x}^{2}v=0
. Although this linearization enables a complete analysis of the blow-up curve for (
1.7
), this approach is not applicable to our main equation (
1.1
).

A more general framework was developed by Hamza and Zaag
HamzaZaag2013
for equations of the form,

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\partial_{x}^{2}u=|u|^{p-1}u+F_{1}(u)+F_{2}\left(x,t,\partial_{x}u,\tfrac{x}{|x|}\cdot\partial_{t}u\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\ t\in\mathbb{R},

where
F_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
and
F_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4},\mathbb{R})
satisfy specific growth conditions. First,
F_{1}
satisfies
|F_{1}(u)|\leq M(1+|u|^{q})
for some
M>0
and
q<p
. Second,
F_{2}
satisfies
|F_{2}(x,t,v,z)|\leq M(1+|v|+|z|)
for some
M>0
. The techniques from
HamzaZaag2013
cannot be directly applied to (
1.1
) because our nonlinear term involving
\partial_{t}u
fails to satisfy global Lipschitz conditions.

Regarding maximal existence time for (
1.1
), Haraux
Haraux1992
established the existence of unbounded global solutions, while Messikh
Messikh2011
proved that for any
T>0
, there exist initial data inducing blow-up at
t^{*}\leq T
. For related blow-up results in the one-dimensional quasilinear setting, we refer to Haruyama and Takamura
HaruyamaTakamuraQuasilinear2025
.
However, the blow-up curve for (
1.1
) remains uncharacterized in the literature. Now, we are considering a new program, where we will be closely following Takiko’s work, with more adaptations.

Unlike the case of the semilinear wave equation
\Box u=|u|^{p-1}u
, we have no variational structure in our PDE. For that reason, the strategy developed by Merle and Zaag in a series of papers
MZajm03
;
MZimrn05
;
MZjfa07
;
MZcmp08
and which extensively uses energy estimates breaks down in our case. Fortunately, we could successfully implement in our case the strategy of Caffarelli and Friedman in
Caffarelli1985
and
Caffarelli1986
and its adaptation by Sasaki in
Sasaki2018
;
Sasaki2019
, which does not rely on energy estimates.

The proof strategy which led to our primary results is reasoned out in the following manner. The method we utilized is inspired by the works of
Caffarelli1985
;
Caffarelli1986
;
Sasaki2019
. To begin with, there is a change of variables such that the equation is expressed in a suitable way in terms of the characteristics. In the absence of damping, this amounts to treating the problem as a system of ordinary differential equations along the characteristic rays

x\pm t=c
, which is the starting point of Sasaki’s work
Sasaki2018
;
Sasaki2019
. By this method, we are able to derive an associated system of ODEs that captures the geometry of the blow-up curve in terms of the dynamics of an effective one-dimensional model.

At this juncture, a number of steps follow closely on Sasaki’s proof-steps; the most significant one being the characteristic reformulation. The major obstacle, however, is the damping term with coefficient
\mu
, which besides complicating the situation, adds more terms. As a result, the system that we obtain is dissimilar to that in
Sasaki2019
; in particular, the equations along the rays receive an extra
\mu-
dependent term that not only destroys but also complicates the comparison arguments (see e.g. system (
2.2
) below).

In order to overcome this barrier, we put forward some additional structural conditions (see (
3.1
), A
4
, and A
5
below) and we resort to a number of technical reductions at different stages of the analysis. The use of these tools permits us, whenever the need arises, to recreate a scenario that is still comparable to the undamped case considered by Sasaki in
Sasaki2019
.

The novel contributions of this paper are twofold. First, we prove
\mathcal{C}^{1}
regularity of the blow-up curve for (
1.1
). Second, as a crucial intermediate result, we identify and characterize the blow-up profile of solutions. In fact, we show that the blow-up profile for
u_{t}
is approximately of the form

\partial_{t}u(x,t)\sim\left[\frac{1}{(p-1)(T-t)}\right]^{1/(p-1)}\quad\text{(main singularity)}.

modulated by a function of
x
and
t
, but the time-dependent damping
\frac{\mu}{1+t}
is slowly varying, so near blow-up time
T
it is roughly constant
\approx\frac{\mu}{1+T}
, thus the blow-up rate is the same as in the constant damping case:
(T-t)^{-1/(p-1)}
.

The spatial profile is expected to be approximately self-similar of the second kind (or peaking) if
\mu
small, but for large
\mu
it may be flatter.

The remainder of this article is organized as follows. Section
2
presents preliminary computations and establishes key technical lemmas. In Section
3
, we state and prove our main result on the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
regularity of the blow-up curve. Section
4
is devoted to the existence and regularity of solutions to our transformed system, while Section
5
analyzes the blow-up rates and establishes Lipschitz continuity of the blow-up curve. The blow-up limits and their properties are examined in Section
6.1
, leading to the proof of differentiability in Section
6
. Finally, Section
7
establishes the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-regularity of the blow-up curve.

2
Preliminary computations

We transform the original equation (
1.1
) into a first-order system that eliminates derivatives from the nonlinear terms, adapting the characteristic method of Caffarelli and Friedman
Caffarelli1985
;
Caffarelli1986
. Introducing the Riemann invariants:

\phi=\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}u,\quad\psi=\partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}u,

(2.1)

we obtain the equivalent system:

\begin{cases}D_{-}\phi=2^{-p}|\phi+\psi|^{p}-\dfrac{\mu}{1+t}\dfrac{\phi+\psi}{2},&x\in\mathbb{R},t>0,\\
D_{+}\psi=2^{-p}|\phi+\psi|^{p}-\dfrac{\mu}{1+t}\dfrac{\phi+\psi}{2},&x\in\mathbb{R},t>0,\\
\phi(x,0)=f(x),\quad\psi(x,0)=g(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(2.2)

where the differential operators
D_{-}=\partial_{t}-\partial_{x}
and
D_{+}=\partial_{t}+\partial_{x}
represent characteristic derivatives, and the initial data is given by
f=u_{1}+\partial_{x}u_{0}
,
g=u_{1}-\partial_{x}u_{0}
. This formulation moves the nonlinearity from the derivative term
\partial_{t}u
in (
1.1
) to the undifferentiated terms
\phi
and
\psi
, while maintaining the damping effect through the coefficient
\mu/(1+t)
.

Consider the coupled ODE system for
(\hat{\phi},\hat{\psi})
:

\begin{cases}\dfrac{d\hat{\phi}}{dt}=2^{-p}|\hat{\phi}+\hat{\psi}|^{p}-\dfrac{\mu}{2}(\hat{\phi}+\hat{\psi}),&t>0,\\[8.0pt]
\dfrac{d\hat{\psi}}{dt}=2^{-p}|\hat{\phi}+\hat{\psi}|^{p}-\dfrac{\mu}{2}(\hat{\phi}+\hat{\psi}),&t>0,\\[8.0pt]
\hat{\phi}(0)=\gamma_{1},\quad\hat{\psi}(0)=\gamma_{2}.\end{cases}

(2.3)

The behavior of solutions to our PDE system (
2.2
) can be understood through analysis of the simpler ODE system (
2.3
), which captures the essential nonlinear dynamics. The following lemma establishes the critical threshold for finite-time blow-up in this reduced system:

Lemma 2.1

(Finite-time blow-up)
.

For initial data satisfying
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}>A
, where
A=2\mu^{1/(p-1)}
, the solution of (
2.3
) exhibits a finite-time blow-up, that is, there exists
T_{1}=T_{1}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})>0
such that

\lim\limits_{t\to T_{1}^{-}}(\hat{\phi}(t)+\hat{\psi}(t))=\infty.

Proof.

The proof proceeds through careful analysis of the summed ODEs. First, adding both equations in (
2.3
) yields:

\frac{d}{dt}(\hat{\phi}+\hat{\psi})=2^{1-p}|\hat{\phi}+\hat{\psi}|^{p}-\mu(\hat{\phi}+\hat{\psi}).

Letting
y(t)=\hat{\phi}(t)+\hat{\psi}(t)
, we obtain the Bernoulli equation:

\frac{dy}{dt}=2^{1-p}y^{p}-\mu y.

(2.4)

The substitution
v=y^{1-p}
converts (
2.4
) into a linear equation. A simple differentiation gives

\frac{dv}{dt}=(1-p)y^{-p}\frac{dy}{dt}=(1-p)(2^{1-p}-\mu y^{1-p}).

Since
v=y^{1-p}
, this becomes:

\frac{dv}{dt}+(1-p)\mu v=(1-p)2^{1-p}.

The integrating factor method yields the solution:

v(t)=\frac{2^{1-p}}{\mu}+Ce^{-(1-p)\mu t},

where the constant
C
is determined by initial conditions. Substituting back for
y
, we obtain

y(t)=\left[\left((\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})^{1-p}-\frac{2^{1-p}}{\mu}\right)e^{-(1-p)\mu t}+\frac{2^{1-p}}{\mu}\right]^{\frac{1}{1-p}}.

The blow-up occurs when the denominator vanishes, giving the finite blow-up time:

T_{1}=\frac{1}{(p-1)\mu}\ln\left(\frac{2^{1-p}}{\mu\left[\frac{2^{1-p}}{\mu}-(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})^{1-p}\right]}\right).

This completes the proof, establishing that
y(t)\to+\infty
as
t\to T_{1}^{-}
when
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}>2\mu^{1/(p-1)}
.
∎

Now, we analyze the characteristic structure of the damped wave equation:

\partial_{t}^{2}\phi-\partial_{x}^{2}\phi+\frac{\mu}{1+t}\partial_{t}\phi=0.

(2.5)

Introducing
u=\partial_{t}\phi
and
v=\partial_{x}\phi
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\partial_{x}u-\partial_{t}v

\displaystyle=0

(2.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}v

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mu}{1+t}u

(2.7)

The above system has the following symmetric hyperbolic form:

\partial_{t}U+A\partial_{x}U+BU=0,\quad U=\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix},\quad A=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\mu}{1+t}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(2.8)

A straightforward characteristic analysis, using the transformation
V=\left((1+t)^{\mu}u,v\right)^{t}
, yields the following:

\partial_{t}V+A\partial_{x}V=0.

(2.9)

Finally, we conclude that the characteristic slopes, coming from
\det(A-\alpha I)=\alpha^{2}-1=0
, gives two characteristic families:

•

Right-going characteristics:
x-t=\text{constant}
(
\alpha=+1
).

•

Left-going characteristics:
x+t=\text{constant}
(
\alpha=-1
).

In fact, the damping term
\frac{\mu}{1+t}u_{t}
affects only the wave amplitudes along these characteristics.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
: Construction via Successive Approximations

In this section, we aim to prove the existence and regularity of solutions to system (
2.2
), as stated in Theorem
3.2
. Our approach is based on an iterative scheme. We start with the constant initial functions
\phi_{0}(x,t)\equiv\gamma_{1}
and
\psi_{0}(x,t)\equiv\gamma_{2}
, as given in (
2.3
), and then construct recursively the sequences
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
\{\psi_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
using the characteristic system:

\begin{cases}D_{-}\phi_{n+1}=2^{-p}|\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}|^{p}-\dfrac{\mu}{1+t}\dfrac{\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}}{2},&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
D_{+}\psi_{n+1}=2^{-p}|\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}|^{p}-\dfrac{\mu}{1+t}\dfrac{\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}}{2},&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
\phi_{n+1}(x,0)=f(x),\quad\psi_{n+1}(x,0)=g(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(4.1)

for all
n\geq 0
. This system admits an equivalent integral formulation, which we will use to carry out our analysis, as follows:

\displaystyle\phi_{n+1}(x,t)

\displaystyle=f(x+t)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{N}_{n}(x+t-s,s)\,ds,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\psi_{n+1}(x,t)

\displaystyle=g(x-t)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{N}_{n}(x-t+s,s)\,ds,

where
\mathcal{N}_{n}(x,s):=2^{-p}|\phi_{n}(x,s)+\psi_{n}(x,s)|^{p}-\dfrac{\mu}{1+s}\dfrac{\phi_{n}(x,s)+\psi_{n}(x,s)}{2}
represents the nonlinear coupling term.

The integral formulation reveals the characteristic structure of the problem;
\phi_{n}
propagates right along the lines
x+t=\text{constant}
while
\psi_{n}
propagates left along the lines
x-t=\text{constant}
, with nonlinear coupling through the interaction terms. Taking advantage of the damping effects from the
\mu/(1+t)
terms and the controlled growth of the nonlinearities under our assumptions, the convergence of these iterative sequences and the regularity of the limiting functions will be established by analyzing the contractive properties of this recursive system in appropriate function spaces.

Remark 4.1

(Preservation of nonnegativity)
.

For any
F\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(K_{R^{*},T^{*}})
, the iterative scheme (
4.1
)–(
4.2
) preserves non-negativity when:

(i)

F(x,0)\geq 0
for all
x\in B_{R^{*}+T^{*}}
.

(ii)

Either
D_{-}F(x,t)\geq 0
or
D_{+}F(x,t)\geq 0
throughout
K_{R^{*},T^{*}}
.

This property will be crucial for establishing the monotonicity of our approximate solutions.

We now establish two fundamental technical lemmas that underpin our existence proof.

Lemma 4.2

(Monotonicity of Approximations)
.

Under assumption
(A_{2})
, the successive approximations satisfy:

\phi_{n+1}(x,t)\geq\phi_{n}(x,t)\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{n+1}(x,t)\geq\psi_{n}(x,t)\geq 0,

for all
(x,t)\in K_{R^{*},T^{*}}
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Moreover, this monotonicity holds uniformly across the domain.

Proof.

We proceed by strong induction on
n
.

We start by proving the case
n=0
. From assumption
(A_{2})
and the integral form (
4.2
), we estimate:

\phi_{1}(x,t)\geq f(x+t)+\int_{0}^{t}\left(2^{-p}|\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}|^{p}-\mu\frac{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}}{2}\right)ds\geq\gamma_{1}=\phi_{0}(x,t)\geq 0,

for all
(x,t)\in K_{R^{*},T^{*}}
. Similarly,
\psi_{1}\geq\psi_{0}\geq 0
in
K_{R^{*},T^{*}}
.

Now, we proceed with the inductive step. Assume that
\phi_{k}\geq\phi_{k-1}\geq 0
and
\psi_{k}\geq\psi_{k-1}\geq 0
for all
1\leq k\leq n
. The difference between successive iterates satisfies:

\phi_{n+1}-\phi_{n}=\int_{0}^{t}\left[N(\phi_{n},\psi_{n})-N(\phi_{n-1},\psi_{n-1})\right](x+t-s,s)ds,

where
\displaystyle N(\phi,\psi)=2^{-p}|\phi+\psi|^{p}-\frac{\mu}{1+s}\frac{\phi+\psi}{2}
.

Consider the function

\zeta(x)=\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{p}-\frac{\mu}{1+t}\frac{x}{2},\quad t>0.

For
x>\alpha:=2(\mu/p)^{1/(p-1)}
(which holds since
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}>2\Big(\tfrac{4}{3}\,\mu\Big)^{\!\frac{1}{p-1}}
),
\zeta
is strictly increasing. By the induction hypothesis:

\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}\geq\phi_{n-1}+\psi_{n-1}>\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}>\alpha,

and thus:

N(\phi_{n},\psi_{n})=\zeta(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})\geq\zeta(\phi_{n-1}+\psi_{n-1})=N(\phi_{n-1},\psi_{n-1}).

Finally, the integrand in
\phi_{n+1}-\phi_{n}
is nonnegative, so
\phi_{n+1}\geq\phi_{n}\geq 0
. An identical argument shows that
\psi_{n+1}\geq\psi_{n}\geq 0
, completing thus the induction.
∎

Lemma 4.3

(Characteristic Derivative Bounds)
.

Under the assumptions
(A_{2})
-
(A_{4})
, the approximate solutions satisfy the following gradient estimates:

\partial_{t}\phi_{n}(x,t)\geq(1+\varepsilon_{0})|\partial_{x}\phi_{n}(x,t)|\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{t}\psi_{n}(x,t)\geq(1+\varepsilon_{0})|\partial_{x}\psi_{n}(x,t)|,

uniformly for all
(x,t)\in K_{R^{*},T^{*}}
and every iteration
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. These inequalities persist throughout the approximation scheme and reflect the dominant behavior of temporal derivatives over spatial derivatives in our system.

Proof.

We proceed by carefully analyzing the evolution of the derivatives. Set
\lambda=1+\varepsilon_{0}
and define for each
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
:

\displaystyle J_{n}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\phi_{n}+\lambda\partial_{x}\phi_{n},

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{n}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\phi_{n}-\lambda\partial_{x}\phi_{n},

\displaystyle L_{n}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\psi_{n}+\lambda\partial_{x}\psi_{n},

\displaystyle\tilde{L}_{n}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\psi_{n}-\lambda\partial_{x}\psi_{n}.

Note that for
n=0
, we have trivial initial conditions:
J_{0}=\tilde{J}_{0}=L_{0}=\tilde{L}_{0}=0
on
K_{R^{*},T^{*}}
.

Let us start with the case
n=0
. For the first iteration, we compute at
t=0
:

J_{1}(x,0)=\partial_{t}\phi_{1}(x,0)+\lambda\partial_{x}\phi_{1}(x,0)=(2+\varepsilon_{0})f^{\prime}(x)+2^{-p}(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})^{p}-\frac{\mu}{2}(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}),

which is nonnegative by assumption
(A_{4})
. The evolution satisfies:

D_{-}J_{1}=\partial_{t}D_{-}\phi_{1}+\lambda\partial_{x}D_{-}\phi_{1}=\frac{\mu}{2}(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})\frac{1}{(1+t)^{2}}\geq 0.

Hence
J_{1}\geq 0
throughout
K_{R^{*},T^{*}}
. Similar computations show
\tilde{J}_{1},L_{1},\tilde{L}_{1}\geq 0
.

Now, assume that
J_{n},L_{n}\geq 0
for some
n\geq 1
. At
t=0
:

\displaystyle J_{n+1}(x,0)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\phi_{n+1}(x,0)+\lambda\partial_{x}\phi_{n+1}(x,0)

\displaystyle=(2+\varepsilon_{0})f^{\prime}(x)+2^{-p}(f(x)+g(x))^{p}-\frac{\mu}{2}(f(x)+g(x))\geq 0,

by
(A_{4})
. For
t>0
, we compute:

D_{-}J_{n+1}=\partial_{t}(\partial_{t}\phi_{n+1}+\lambda\partial_{x}\phi_{n+1})-\partial_{x}(\partial_{t}\phi_{n+1}+\lambda\partial_{x}\phi_{n+1}),

which expands to:

D_{-}J_{n+1}=p2^{-p}(\partial_{t}\phi_{n}+\partial_{t}\psi_{n})(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p-1}-\frac{\mu}{1+t}\frac{\partial_{t}\phi_{n}+\partial_{t}\psi_{n}}{2}+\frac{\mu}{(1+t)^{2}}\frac{\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}}{2}

-\lambda p2^{-p}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p-1}(\partial_{x}\phi_{n}+\partial_{x}\psi_{n})+\frac{\mu}{1+t}\frac{\partial_{x}\phi_{n}+\partial_{x}\psi_{n}}{2}

=\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^{p}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p-1}(J_{n}+L_{n})-\frac{\mu}{1+t}(J_{n}+L_{n})+\frac{\mu}{(1+t)^{2}}\frac{\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}}{2}\geq 0,

since
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}\geq\max(\mu^{1/(p-1)}(2/p)^{p/(p-1)},A)
, where
A
is given by Lemma
2.1
. Thus
J_{n+1}\geq 0
. Parallel arguments hold for
L_{n+1},\tilde{J}_{n+1},\tilde{L}_{n+1}
.

By induction,
J_{n},\tilde{J}_{n},L_{n},\tilde{L}_{n}\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
on
K_{R^{*},T^{*}}
, proving the lemma.
∎

Proof of Existence and Regularity of
\phi
and
\psi
.

Let
(x,t)\in K_{R^{*},T^{*}}
. Since
\{\phi_{n}(x,t)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
\{\psi_{n}(x,t)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
are increasing sequences in
n
, we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} \lim_{n\to\infty}\phi_{n}(x,t)&=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\phi_{n}(x,t)=\phi(x,t),\\
\lim_{n\to\infty}\psi_{n}(x,t)&=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\psi_{n}(x,t)=\psi(x,t).\end{aligned}\right.

(4.3)

From Lemma
4.2
, it follows that
\phi
and
\psi
increase monotonically in
t
. Hence, there exists a function
T(x)
such that

T(x)=\sup\{\,t\in(0,T^{*}):(\phi+\psi)(x,t)<\infty\,\},\qquad x\in B_{R^{*}}.

We define

\Omega=\{\,(x,t):x\in B_{R^{*}},\;0<t<T(x)\,\}.

(4.4)

Remark 4.4
.

We will show in Section
5
that
T
is the blow-up curve for
\phi
and
\psi
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Under the assumptions
(A_{2})
–
(A_{4})
, the pair
(\phi,\psi)
, defined by (
4.3
), is the unique solution of (
2.2
) in the class
(\mathcal{C}^{3,1}(\Omega))^{2}
.

Proof.

Define the set

B(t)=\bigl\{x\in B_{R^{*}+T^{*}}:\lvert x-\tilde{x}\rvert\leq\tilde{t}-t\bigr\},\quad(\tilde{x},\tilde{t})\in\Omega.

(Proof of regularity).

We first show that
(\phi,\psi)
is a
\mathcal{C}^{3,1}(\overline{\Omega})^{2}
solution of (
2.2
).
The proof is carried out in two steps.

Step 1.

Fix
(x,\tilde{t}\,)\in\Omega
.
We claim that there exists a constant
M_{0}>0
such that

\lVert\phi+\psi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B(t))}\leq M_{0},\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,\tilde{t}\,].

(4.5)

We argue by contradiction. Define

Y_{\tilde{x}}=\{x\in B_{R^{*}}:\lvert x-\tilde{x}\rvert\leq\tilde{t}-T(x)\},

where
m
denotes the 1-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
Suppose (
4.5
) fails, i.e.,
m(Y_{\tilde{x}})>0
.
By the monotonicity of
\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}
in
t
, we have

(x,t)\notin\Omega\quad\text{if }x\in Y_{\tilde{x}}\text{ and }t=x+\tilde{t}-\tilde{x}\text{ or }t=-x+\tilde{t}+\tilde{x}.

Hence, either
m(Y_{\tilde{x},+})>0
or
m(Y_{\tilde{x},-})>0
, where

Y_{\tilde{x},+}=\{s\in(0,\tilde{t}):s=x+\tilde{t}-\tilde{x},\ x\in Y_{\tilde{x}}\},

Y_{\tilde{x},-}=\{s\in(0,\tilde{t}):s=-x+\tilde{t}+\tilde{x},\ x\in Y_{\tilde{x}}\}.

However, evaluating the iteration yields

\infty>(\phi_{n+1}+\psi_{n+1})(\tilde{x},\tilde{t})\geq\int_{Y_{\tilde{x},+}}\left(2^{-p}\lvert\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}\rvert^{p}-\frac{\mu}{1+t}\frac{\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}}{2}\right)(x+\tilde{t}-s,s)\,ds

+2^{-p}\int_{Y_{\tilde{x},-}}\left(2^{-p}\lvert\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}\rvert^{p}-\frac{\mu}{1+t}\frac{\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}}{2}\right)(-x+\tilde{t}+\tilde{x},s)\,ds\to\infty\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

which is a contradiction. Thus, (
4.5
) holds.

Step 2.

We now prove that
(\phi,\psi)
is a
\mathcal{C}^{3,1}(\overline{\Omega})^{2}
solution of (
2.2
).

Fix
(\tilde{x},\tilde{t})\in\Omega
and set
K=K(x,\tilde{t})
.
By localizing the problem, it suffices to show that
(\phi,\psi)
is a
\mathcal{C}^{3,1}(\overline{K})^{2}
solution of (
2.2
).

First, we establish the uniform convergence of
\phi_{n}
to
\phi
and
\psi_{n}
to
\psi
in
K=K(x,\tilde{t})
.

By Step 1, there exists a constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(x,\tilde{t})>0
such that

\|\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t))}\leq C_{0}\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,\tilde{t}\,]\text{ and }n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.6)

For
t\in[0,\tilde{t}\,]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we estimate the differences:

\displaystyle\|\phi_{n+1}-\phi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t))}+\|\psi_{n+1}-\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t))}

\displaystyle\quad\leq(pC_{0}^{p-1}+\mu)\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\phi_{n}-\phi_{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(s_{1}))}+\|\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(s_{1}))}\right)ds_{1}

\displaystyle\quad\leq(pC_{0}^{p-1}+\mu)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\left(\|\phi_{n-1}-\phi_{n-2}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(s_{2}))}+\|\psi_{n-1}-\psi_{n-2}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(s_{2}))}\right)ds_{2}ds_{1}.

Iterating this estimate yields

\|\phi_{n+1}-\phi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t))}+\|\psi_{n+1}-\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t))}\leq 4C_{0}\frac{\big((pC_{0}^{p-1}+\mu)T\big)^{n}}{n!}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

From (
4.3
), we conclude the uniform convergence:

\|\phi_{n}-\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(K_{x,\tilde{t}})}+\|\psi_{n}-\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(K_{x,\tilde{t}})}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

We now establish that
\phi,\psi\in W^{1,\infty}(K,(\tilde{x},\tilde{t}))
.

The directional derivatives satisfy the following recursive relations for
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
:

\begin{cases}D_{-}D_{\theta}\phi_{n+1}=p2^{-p}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p-1}(D_{\theta}\phi_{n}+D_{\theta}\psi_{n}),\\
D_{+}D_{\theta}\psi_{n+1}=p2^{-p}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p-1}(D_{\theta}\phi_{n}+D_{\theta}\psi_{n}),\end{cases}

where
D_{\theta}v=(\cos\theta\,\partial_{x}+\sin\theta\,\partial_{t})v
is the directional derivative.

The initial conditions are given by:

\begin{cases}D_{\theta}\phi_{n+1}(x,0)=\begin{cases}(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)\,f^{\prime}(x)+\sin\theta\cdot 2^{-p}(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})^{p}&(n=0),\\
(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)\,f^{\prime}(x)+\sin\theta\cdot 2^{-p}(f+g)^{p}(x)&(n\geq 1),\end{cases}\\[6.0pt]
D_{\theta}\psi_{n+1}(x,0)=\begin{cases}(\cos\theta-\sin\theta)\,g^{\prime}(x)+\sin\theta\cdot 2^{-p}(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})^{p}&(n=0),\\
(\cos\theta-\sin\theta)\,g^{\prime}(x)+\sin\theta\cdot 2^{-p}(f+g)^{p}(x)&(n\geq 1).\end{cases}\end{cases}

Define the function

W(t)=(C_{0}^{p}+\mu C_{0})\exp\left((pC_{0}^{p-1}+\mu+1)t\right),

which satisfies the integral equation

W(t)=C_{0}^{p}+\mu C_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}(pC_{0}^{p-1}+\mu+1)W(s)\,ds.

(4.7)

We prove by induction that for all
t\in[0,\tilde{t}\,]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
:

\|D_{\theta}\phi_{n}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t))}<W(t),\quad\|D_{\theta}\psi_{n}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t))}<W(t).

(4.8)

First, observe that
D_{\theta}\phi_{0}=D_{\theta}\psi_{0}=0\leq W(t)
for all
t\geq 0
. Assuming the inequalities hold for
n
, we establish the bound for
n+1
, that is

\|2^{-p}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p-1}(\cdot,t)(D_{\theta}\phi_{n}+D_{\theta}\psi_{n})(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t))}\leq C_{0}^{p-1}W(t).

(4.9)

From assumption
(A_{4})
, we derive the key estimate:

\displaystyle\|D_{\theta}\phi_{n+1}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t))}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0))}+2^{-p}\|f+g\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0))}^{p}+\frac{\mu}{2}\|f+g\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0))}

(4.10)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}p\left\|2^{-p}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p-1}(D_{\theta}\phi_{n}+D_{\theta}\psi_{n})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B(s))}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{-\mu}{2(1+s)}(D_{\theta}\phi_{n}+D_{\theta}\psi_{n})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B(s))}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\sin\theta}{2}\cdot\frac{\mu}{(1+s)^{2}}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B(s))}ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}^{p}+\mu C_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}(pC_{0}^{p-1}+\mu+1)W(s)\,ds=W(t),

for
t\in[0,\tilde{t}\,]
. The same bound holds for
\|D_{\theta}\psi_{n+1}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t))}
.

Define
C_{1}=(C_{0}^{p}+\mu C_{0})\exp((pC_{0}^{p-1}+\mu+1)T)
, yielding:

\|D_{\theta}\phi_{n}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t))}\leq C_{1},\quad\text{and}\quad\|D_{\theta}\psi_{n}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t))}\leq C_{1}.

(4.11)

The estimate of the difference estimates shows that

\displaystyle\|D_{\theta}\phi_{n+1}-D_{\theta}\phi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t))}+\|D_{\theta}\psi_{n+1}-D_{\theta}\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t))}

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}(\mu+pC_{0}^{p-1})\big(\|D_{\theta}\phi_{n}-D_{\theta}\phi_{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(s_{1}))}+\|D_{\theta}\psi_{n}-D_{\theta}\psi_{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(s_{1}))}\big)ds_{1}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\mu+2p(p-1)C_{1}C_{0}^{p-2}\big)\big(\|\phi_{n}-\phi_{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(s_{1}))}+\|\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(s_{1}))}\big)ds_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 4C_{1}\frac{\big((\mu+pC_{0}^{p-1})T\big)^{n}}{n!}+4C_{0}C_{2}T^{n}\big(pC_{0}^{p-1}\big)^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j!(n-j)!}\to 0,

where
C_{2}=\mu+2p(p-1)C_{1}C_{0}^{p-2}
.

The uniform convergence implies the existence of limits:

\big(\phi_{\theta}^{(1)},\psi_{\theta}^{(1)}\big)\in\big(L^{\infty}(K_{-}(\tilde{x},\tilde{t}))\big)^{2},

with

\|D_{\theta}\phi_{n}-\phi_{\theta}^{(1)}\|_{L^{\infty}(K_{-}(\tilde{x},\tilde{t}))}+\|D_{\theta}\psi_{n}-\psi_{\theta}^{(1)}\|_{L^{\infty}(K_{-}(\tilde{x},\tilde{t}))}\to 0.

This establishes
\big(\phi,\psi\big)\in\big(W^{1,\infty}(K_{-}(\tilde{x},\tilde{t}))\big)^{2}
. Iterating the argument yields the higher regularity:

\big(\phi,\psi\big)\in\big(W^{4,\infty}(K_{-}(\tilde{x},\tilde{t}))\big)^{2}=\big(\mathcal{C}^{3,1}(K_{-}(\tilde{x},\tilde{t}))\big)^{2}.

Proof of Uniqueness.

We now establish the uniqueness of the solution
(\phi,\psi)
to (
2.2
).

Suppose there exist two solutions
(\phi_{1},\psi_{1})
and
(\phi_{2},\psi_{2})
with corresponding blow-up curves
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
. Define their domains:

\Omega_{j}=\{(x,t):x\in B_{R^{*}},\ 0<t<T_{j}(x)\}\quad\text{for }j=1,2.

For any
(\tilde{x},\tilde{t})\in\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}
, we have
K_{-}(\tilde{x},\tilde{t})\subset\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}
. Following the approach in Step 2, we obtain the estimate:

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t^{\prime}\leq\tilde{t}}\Big(\|\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t^{\prime}))}+\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t^{\prime}))}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq(tpC_{0}^{p-1}+\mu)\sup_{0\leq t^{\prime}\leq\tilde{t}}\Big(\|\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t^{\prime}))}+\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t^{\prime}))}\Big).

For sufficiently small
t>0
, this implies:

\sup_{0\leq t^{\prime}\leq t}\Big(\|\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t^{\prime}))}+\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t^{\prime}))}\Big)=0.

Since
C_{0}
is time-independent, we can iterate this argument to extend the uniqueness to all
t^{\prime}\in[0,\tilde{t}\,]
:

\sup_{0\leq t^{\prime}\leq\tilde{t}}\Big(\|\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t^{\prime}))}+\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(t^{\prime}))}\Big)=0.

This establishes that

(\phi_{1},\psi_{1})\equiv(\phi_{2},\psi_{2})
in
\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}
and

T_{1}(x)=T_{2}(x)
for all
x\in B_{R^{*}}
,
completing the proof of uniqueness and hence the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Under the assumptions A
1
–A
4
, the blow-up time satisfies

T(x)<T^{*}\quad\text{for all }x\in B_{R^{*}},

where
T(x)
and
T^{*}
are two positive real numbers as defined in (
1.6
).

Proof.

We establish the result through a comparison argument with auxiliary functions.

Define sequences
\{\tilde{\phi}_{n}\}
and
\{\tilde{\psi}_{n}\}
by
\tilde{\phi}_{0}=\gamma_{1}
,
\tilde{\psi}_{0}=\gamma_{2}
and for
n\geq 0
:

\begin{cases}\dfrac{d}{dt}\tilde{\phi}_{n+1}=2^{-p}|\tilde{\phi}_{n}+\tilde{\psi}_{n}|^{p}-\dfrac{\mu}{2}(\tilde{\phi}_{n}+\tilde{\psi}_{n}),&t>0,\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{d}{dt}\tilde{\psi}_{n+1}=2^{-p}|\tilde{\phi}_{n}+\tilde{\psi}_{n}|^{p}-\dfrac{\mu}{2}(\tilde{\phi}_{n}+\tilde{\psi}_{n}),&t>0,\\[6.0pt]
\tilde{\phi}_{n+1}(0)=\gamma_{1},\quad\tilde{\psi}_{n+1}(0)=\gamma_{2}.\end{cases}

(4.13)

For
(x,t)\in K_{R^{*},T^{*}}
, we have:

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(x,t)-\tilde{\phi}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=f(x+t)-\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\left[2^{-p}|\phi_{0}+\psi_{0}|^{p}-\frac{\mu}{2(1+s)}(\phi_{0}+\psi_{0})\right](x+t-s,s)\,ds

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\left[2^{-p}|\tilde{\phi}_{0}+\tilde{\psi}_{0}|^{p}-\frac{\mu}{2}(\tilde{\phi}_{0}+\tilde{\psi}_{0})\right]\,ds

\displaystyle\geq f(x+t)-\gamma_{1}\geq 0.

Similarly,
\psi_{1}(x,t)\geq\tilde{\psi}_{1}(t)
in
K_{R^{*},T^{*}}
.

Now, we proceed with the inductive step. For that purpose, we assume that
\phi_{n}(x,t)\geq\tilde{\phi}_{n}(t)
and
\psi_{n}(x,t)\geq\tilde{\psi}_{n}(t)
hold in
K_{R^{*},T^{*}}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{n+1}(x,t)-\tilde{\phi}_{n+1}(t)

\displaystyle\geq f(x+t)-\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\left[2^{-p}|\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}|^{p}-\frac{\mu}{2}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})\right](x+t-s,s)\,ds

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\left[2^{-p}|\tilde{\phi}_{n}+\tilde{\psi}_{n}|^{p}-\frac{\mu}{2}(\tilde{\phi}_{n}+\tilde{\psi}_{n})\right]\,ds\geq 0.

The same inequality holds for
\psi_{n+1}
. By induction, we conclude that

\phi_{n}(x,t)\geq\tilde{\phi}_{n}(t),\quad\psi_{n}(x,t)\geq\tilde{\psi}_{n}(t)\quad\text{in }K_{R^{*},T^{*}}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since the comparison functions
\tilde{\phi}_{n}
,
\tilde{\psi}_{n}
blow up before
T^{*}
, the same must hold for
\phi_{n}
,
\psi_{n}
, proving that
T(x)<T^{*}
.

This ends the proof of the lemma.
∎

5
Blow-up rates of solutions and Lipschitz continuity of
T

Our objective is to prove the Lipschitz continuity of the blow-up time
T
on
B_{R^{*}}
. The proof relies on Theorem
3.4
, so we begin by proving this theorem.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

We begin by establishing the lower and upper bounds for
\partial_{t}\phi
.

First, for all
(x,t)\in K_{R^{*},T^{*}}
, consider the time derivative evolution for the iterative scheme (
4.1
):

\displaystyle D_{-}\partial_{t}\phi_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}D_{-}\phi_{n+1}=\partial_{t}\left(2^{-p}|\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}|^{p}\right)

(5.1)

\displaystyle=p2^{-p}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p-1}(\partial_{t}\phi_{n}+\partial_{t}\psi_{n})

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu}{(1+t)^{2}}\frac{\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}}{2}-\frac{\mu}{1+t}\frac{\partial_{t}\phi_{n}+\partial_{t}\psi_{n}}{2}.

From Lemma
4.3
, we obtain the following key inequality:

\displaystyle D_{-}

\displaystyle\left(2^{-p}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p}-\frac{\mu}{1+t}\frac{\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}}{2}\right)

(5.2)

\displaystyle=\left(p2^{-p}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p-1}-\frac{\mu}{2(1+t)}\right)(\partial_{t}\phi_{n}-\partial_{x}\phi_{n}+\partial_{t}\psi_{n}-\partial_{x}\psi_{n})

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu}{(1+t)^{2}}\frac{\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq p2^{-p+1}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p-1}(\partial_{t}\phi_{n}+\partial_{t}\psi_{n})

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu}{(1+t)^{2}}\frac{\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}}{2}-\frac{\mu}{1+t}(\partial_{t}\phi_{n}+\partial_{t}\psi_{n}),\quad\forall\ (x,t)\in K_{R^{*},T^{*}}.

The combination of (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) reveals the fundamental relationship between the time derivatives and the nonlinear terms. This structure allows us to establish the desired bounds through a comparison principle for the iterative scheme, a uniform control of the lower order terms involving
\mu
, and then a careful tracking of the nonlinear interactions between
\phi_{n}
and
\psi_{n}
. This will be subsequently developed in the proof.

We establish the key bounds for
\partial_{t}\phi_{n+1}
through careful analysis of the quantity

J_{\phi,n+1}:=2\partial_{t}\phi_{n+1}-\left(2^{-p}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p}-\frac{\mu}{1+t}\frac{\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}}{2}\right).

From (
5.1
)–(
5.2
), we derive the crucial inequality

D_{-}J_{\phi,n+1}\geq 0,\quad\text{for }(x,t)\in K_{R^{*},T^{*}}.

(5.3)

At
t=0
, using assumption
(A_{4})
, we get

\displaystyle J_{\phi,n+1}(x,0)

\displaystyle\geq 2f^{\prime}(x)+2^{-p}(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})^{p}-\frac{\mu}{2}(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})

(5.4)

\displaystyle\geq 0,\quad\forall\ x\in B_{R^{*}}.

This implies
J_{\phi,n}\geq 0
throughout
K_{R^{*},T^{*}}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Applying Lemma
4.3
, we obtain the upper estimate

\partial_{t}\phi_{n+1}\leq\frac{1+\varepsilon_{0}}{\varepsilon_{0}}\left(2^{-p}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p}-\frac{\mu}{1+t}\frac{\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}}{2}\right).

Combining this with the nonnegativity of
J_{\phi,n}
yields the two-sided bound

2^{-p-2}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p}\leq\partial_{t}\phi_{n+1}\leq\frac{1+\varepsilon_{0}}{\varepsilon_{0}}2^{-p}(\phi_{n+1}+\psi_{n+1})^{p},\quad(x,t)\in K_{R^{*},T^{*}}.

(5.5)

The condition
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}>(\mu\,p\,2^{p})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}
ensures that

2^{-p}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p}-\frac{\mu}{1+t}\frac{\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}}{2}\geq 2^{-p-2}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p}.

The estimate (
5.5
) establishes (
3.4
) and the analogous result for
\psi
follows similarly through identical arguments.
We now establish the precise blow-up rates for the solution.
From (
5.5
), we have the key inequality:

\frac{\partial(\phi+\psi)}{\partial t}(x,t)\leq 2^{-p+1}(1+\varepsilon_{0})\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}(\phi+\psi)^{p}(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in\Omega.

For any fixed
x_{0}\in B_{R^{*}}
, integrating from
\tau
to
T(x_{0})
yields:

\int_{(\phi+\psi)(x_{0},\tau)}^{\infty}\frac{du}{u^{p}}\leq 2^{-p+1}(1+\varepsilon_{0})\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}(T(x_{0})-\tau).

This integration gives the upper bound in (
3.5
) with constant:

C_{2}=2^{p-1}(p-1)^{-1/(p-1)}.

The lower bound follows similarly from the inequality:

\frac{\partial(\phi+\psi)}{\partial t}\geq 2^{-p-2}(\phi+\psi)^{p}.

Integration provides the lower bound in (
3.5
) with:

C_{1}=2\bigl((p-1)\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}(1+\varepsilon_{0})\bigr)^{-1/(p-1)}.

The estimates (
3.5
) establish (
3.8
) with
q=1/(p-1)
. The derivative estimates (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) follow by combining these results with the previous bounds on
\partial_{t}\phi
and
\partial_{t}\psi
.
∎

Having established the blow-up rates of solutions, we now prove the Lipschitz continuity of the blow-up time
T(x)
.

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Fix an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
. From the lower bound in (
3.8
), there exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(p,\varepsilon_{0})>0
such that for all
x\in B_{R^{*}}
and
t\in[T(x)-\varepsilon,T(x)]
we have:

C_{1}(T(x)-t)^{-q}\leq(\phi+\psi)(x,t).

In particular, at
t=T(x)-\varepsilon
this yields:

(\phi+\psi)(x,T(x)-\varepsilon)\geq C_{1}\varepsilon^{-q}.

Choose
M\geq C_{1}\varepsilon^{-q}
and define for each
x\in B_{R^{*}}
the time
E(x)
as the solution to:

(\phi+\psi)(x,E(x))=M,\quad\text{with }\ T(x)-\varepsilon\leq E(x)<T(x).

The existence of such
E(x)
follows from the continuity of
(\phi+\psi)(x,\cdot)
, the blow-up property
\lim_{t\to T(x)^{-}}(\phi+\psi)(x,t)=+\infty
and the intermediate value theorem.

Building upon the existence of the level set function
E(x)
established previously, we now analyze its regularity properties to prove the Lipschitz continuity of
T(x)
.

(i)
Continuity of the Level Set Function
E
.

Let
\{x_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence converging to
x^{\prime}\in B_{R^{*}}
, and set
t_{n}=E(x_{n})
. For any convergent subsequence
t_{n_{k}}\to\eta
, the continuity of
\phi+\psi
implies:

(\phi+\psi)(x^{\prime},\eta)=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\phi+\psi)(x_{n_{k}},t_{n_{k}})=M.

Since
\partial_{t}(\phi+\psi)>0
in
\Omega
, the level set is unique, forcing
\eta=E(x^{\prime})
. Thus
E
is continuous at
x^{\prime}
.

(ii)
Local Lipschitz Property of
E
.

Fix
x^{\prime}\in B_{R^{*}}
and choose
h^{\prime}>0
such that
B(x^{\prime},h^{\prime})\subset\Omega
. By continuity, there exists
0<h^{\prime\prime}\leq h^{\prime}
for which
(x,E(x))\in B(x^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime})
for all
x\in(x^{\prime}-h^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime}+h^{\prime\prime})
. For any
x_{1},x_{2}\in(x^{\prime}-h^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime}+h^{\prime\prime})
, define the auxiliary function

H(\xi):=(\phi+\psi)\big(x_{1}+\xi(x_{2}-x_{1}),E(x_{1})+\xi k\big),
where
0\leq\xi\leq 1
and
k:=E(x_{2})-E(x_{1}).

Since
H(0)=H(1)=M
, Rolle’s Theorem yields
\xi^{\prime}\in(0,1)
satisfying:

(x_{2}-x_{1})\,\partial_{x}(\phi+\psi)(x_{\xi^{\prime}},t_{\xi^{\prime}})+k\,\partial_{t}(\phi+\psi)(x_{\xi^{\prime}},t_{\xi^{\prime}})=0,

(5.6)

where
(x_{\xi^{\prime}},t_{\xi^{\prime}})=(x_{1}+\xi^{\prime}(x_{2}-x_{1}),E(x_{1})+\xi^{\prime}k)
. From Lemma
4.3
and (
5.6
), we obtain:

|E(x_{2})-E(x_{1})|=\left|\frac{\partial_{x}(\phi+\psi)}{\partial_{t}(\phi+\psi)}(x_{\xi^{\prime}},t_{\xi^{\prime}})\right|\,|x_{2}-x_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon_{0}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|.

(iii)
Differentiability of
E
.

The difference quotient satisfies:

\frac{E(x+h)-E(x)}{h}=-\frac{\partial_{x}(\phi+\psi)}{\partial_{t}(\phi+\psi)}\big(x+\xi h,E(x)+\xi(E(x+h)-E(x))\big).

By continuity of
\partial_{x}(\varphi+\psi)
,
\partial_{t}(\varphi+\psi)
and
E
, we see that

E\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(B_{R^{*}})

with:

E^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{\partial_{x}(\phi+\psi)}{\partial_{t}(\phi+\psi)}(x,E(x)),\qquad x\in B_{R^{*}}.

The mean value theorem then gives the global Lipschitz bound:

|E(x^{\prime})-E(x^{\prime\prime})|\leq\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon_{0}}|x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime}|\quad\forall\ x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in B_{R^{*}}.

(5.7)

(iv)
Lipschitz Continuity of
T
.

For any
x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in B_{R^{*}}
and
\varepsilon>0
, we have

|T(x^{\prime})-T(x^{\prime\prime})|\leq|T(x^{\prime})-E(x^{\prime})|+|E(x^{\prime})-E(x^{\prime\prime})|+|E(x^{\prime\prime})-T(x^{\prime\prime})|\leq 2\varepsilon+\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon_{0}}|x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime}|.

Taking
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
establishes the desired inequality (
3.9
).
∎

To better understand the behavior near the blow-up surface, we first introduce a distance metric:

Definition 5.1
.

By
d(x,t)
, we denote the distance from a point
(x,t)\in\Omega
to
\Gamma=\{(x,T(x)):\,x\in B_{R^{*}}\}
.

Having defined the distance function, we can now relate it to the time-to-blow-up:

Remark 5.1
.

It follows from Lemma
4.5
that

\frac{T(x)-t}{\sqrt{2}}\;\leq\;d(x,t)\;\leq\;T(x)-t.

By replacing
T(x)-t
by
d(x,t)
in Theorem
3.4
we obtain the following corollary.

This relationship between distance and time allows us to state more precise blow-up estimates:

Corollary 5.2
.

Assume that A
1
–A
4
hold. Then, there exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, depending only on
p
and
\varepsilon_{0}
, such that

\displaystyle C_{1}d^{-q}(x,t)

\displaystyle\leq(\phi+\psi)(x,t)\leq C_{2}d^{-q}(x,t),

(5.8)

\displaystyle C_{1}d^{-q-1}(x,t)

\displaystyle\leq\partial_{t}\phi(x,t)\leq C_{2}d^{-q-1}(x,t),

(5.9)

\displaystyle C_{1}d^{-q-1}(x,t)

\displaystyle\leq\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)\leq C_{2}d^{-q-1}(x,t),

(5.10)

where
q=1/(p-1)
.

We can further refine these estimates to examine each component of the solution separately:

Remark 5.3
.

Under assumptions A
1
–A
4
, there exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
(depending on
p,\mu
and
\varepsilon_{0}
) such that for
q=1/(p-1)
, the following bounds hold uniformly in
\Omega
:

\displaystyle C_{1}\bigl(T(x)-t\bigr)^{-q}

\displaystyle\leq\phi(x,t)\leq C_{2}\bigl(T(x)-t\bigr)^{-q},

(5.11)

\displaystyle C_{1}\bigl(T(x)-t\bigr)^{-q}

\displaystyle\leq\psi(x,t)\leq C_{2}\bigl(T(x)-t\bigr)^{-q}.

(5.12)

In fact, we have the following estimates for
\phi(x,t)
(the proof for
\psi(x,t)
is analogous):
By Corollary
5.2
and Lemma
4.5
, there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

\phi\bigl(x,T(x)-\varepsilon\bigr)\geq\int_{T(x)-2\varepsilon}^{T(x)-\varepsilon}2^{-p-1}(\phi+\psi)^{p}\,ds\geq c_{2}\varepsilon^{-q},

where the first inequality follows from the integral expression for
\phi
and the second from the singularity analysis near
T(x)
. The upper bound
\phi(x,T(x)-\varepsilon)\leq C_{2}\varepsilon^{-q}
is immediate from Theorem
3.4
.

6
Linearity of the Blow-up Curve for Limit Solutions

In this section, we prepare the analytical tools and estimates that will be instrumental in establishing the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-regularity of the blow-up curve on
B_{R^{*}}
. The actual proof of this regularity result will be carried out in Section
7
.
We will also establish Theorem
3.6
, proving that the limiting blow-up curve is linear.

6.1
Blow-up Limits and Regularity of the Blow-up Time

The key to our analysis lies in understanding the directional derivatives of the solutions. We work with the directional derivative operator:

D_{\theta}=\sin\theta\,\partial_{t}+\cos\theta\,\partial_{x},\quad\text{for }0\leq\theta<2\pi.

The following fundamental estimates control the growth of derivatives as we approach the blow-up surface:

Lemma 6.1
.

Under the assumptions A
1
–A
5
, there exist positive constants
\lambda_{\alpha}
and
\lambda_{\alpha}^{*}
(depending only on
p
,
\varepsilon_{1}
, and
\mu

and

\displaystyle\max\bigl\{|D_{\theta}^{\alpha}\phi(x,t)|,|D_{\theta}^{\alpha}\psi(x,t)|\bigr\}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\alpha}\bigl(\phi+\psi\bigr)^{p+(\alpha-1)/q}(x,t),

(6.1)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\alpha}^{*}\,d(x,t)^{-(pq+\alpha-1)},

where

represents the standard blow-up exponent.

Proof.

The estimate (
6.1
)
2
follows immediately from Corollary
5.2
once (
6.1
)
1
is established. We therefore focus on proving (
6.1
)
1
.

From assumption A
3
, we obtain a uniform bound on the second derivatives:

\max_{x\in B_{R^{*}+T^{*}}}\bigl\{|f^{\prime\prime}(x)|+|g^{\prime\prime}(x)|\bigr\}\leq\kappa(f+g)^{2p-1}(x),

(6.2)

for some constant
\kappa>0
.

The estimates for
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=1
follow directly from Lemma
4.3
and Theorem
3.4
.

For
\alpha=2
, we first prove the following estimate by induction on
n
:

\displaystyle\max\bigl\{|D_{\theta}^{2}\phi_{n}(x,t)|,|D_{\theta}^{2}\psi_{n}(x,t)|\bigr\}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{2}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{2p-1}(x,t),

(6.3)

\displaystyle\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},(x,t)\in K_{R^{*}+T^{*}}.

where
\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{2}(p,\mu,\varepsilon_{1})>0
.

Case
n=0
.

Since
D_{\theta}\phi_{0}=D_{\theta}\psi_{0}=0

) holds trivially.

Inductive Step.

Assume (
6.3
) holds for some

\displaystyle|D_{-}D_{\theta}^{2}\phi_{n+1}(x,t)|

\displaystyle=\Bigl|D_{\theta}^{2}\Bigl(2^{-p}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{p}-\frac{\mu}{1+t}\frac{\phi_{n}+\psi_{n}}{2}\Bigr)(x,t)\Bigr|

(6.4)

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(2^{-p+2}p(p-1)\lambda_{1}^{2}+2^{-p+1}p\lambda_{2}+\mu+2\mu\lambda_{1}+\mu\lambda_{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\times(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{3p-2}(x,t),

for all
(x,t)\in K_{R^{*}+T^{*}}
.

From Lemma
4.3
, we derive the key inequality:

\displaystyle D_{-}\left[\lambda_{2}(\phi_{n+1}+\psi_{n+1})^{2p-1}\right](x,t)

(6.5)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{2}(2p-1)(\phi_{n+1}+\psi_{n+1})^{2p-2}(x,t)\cdot D_{-}(\phi_{n+1}+\psi_{n+1})(x,t)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{2}(2p-1)\!\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2(1+\varepsilon_{1})}\right)2^{-p}\!\left(1-\frac{1}{2p}\right)\,(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{3p-2},

in
K_{R^{*}+T^{*}}
.

Define the auxiliary quantity:

M_{n}(x,t)=\lambda_{2}(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{2p-1}(x,t)-D_{\theta}^{2}\phi_{n}(x,t).

From (
6.2
), we obtain the following initial data lower bound:

M_{n+1}(x,0)\geq(\lambda_{2}-\kappa)(f+g)^{2p-1}(x),\quad x\in B_{R^{*}+T^{*}}.

(6.6)

Combining Lemma
4.3
with (
6.4
) and (
6.5
) yields

\displaystyle D_{-}M_{n+1}(x,t)\geq

\displaystyle\Bigg[\lambda_{2}\!\left(2^{-p}(2p-1)\!\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2(1+\varepsilon_{1})}\right)\!\left(1-\frac{1}{2p}\right)-2^{-p+1}p-\mu\right)

(6.7)

\displaystyle-\Big(2^{-p+2}p(p-1)\lambda_{1}^{2}+\mu+2\mu\lambda_{1}\Big)\Bigg]\,(\phi_{n}+\psi_{n})^{3p-2}(x,t).

By assumption A
5
, we have

2^{-p}(2p-1)\!\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2(1+\varepsilon_{1})}\right)\!\left(1-\frac{1}{2p}\right)-2^{-p+1}p-\mu>0.

We now select
\lambda_{2}
sufficiently large such that

\lambda_{2}\geq\max\left\{\kappa,\,\frac{2^{-p+2}p(p-1)\lambda_{1}^{2}+\mu+2\mu\lambda_{1}}{2^{-p}(2p-1)\!\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2(1+\varepsilon_{1})}\right)\!\left(1-\frac{1}{2p}\right)-2^{-p+1}p-\mu}\right\}.

With the above choice for

and

After possibly enlarging

Thus (

) holds true.
Taking

to infinity we get (
6.1
)
1
.
The case
\alpha=3
follows by an identical argument.
∎

Now, to analyze the local behavior near a point

\displaystyle\phi_{\lambda}(y,s)

for

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\lambda>0
. The scaled blow-up time

T_{\lambda}(y)=\frac{T(x_{0}+\lambda y)-T(x_{0})}{\lambda}.

\displaystyle\lambda^{q}C_{1}\,\bigl[T(x_{0}+\lambda y)-(T(x_{0})+\lambda s)\bigr]^{-q}

\lambda^{q}\bigl[T(x_{0}+\lambda y)-(T(x_{0})+\lambda s)\bigr]^{-q}=\left(\frac{T(x_{0}+\lambda y)-T(x_{0})}{\lambda}-s\right)^{-q}.

(6.13)

. Hence,
T_{\lambda}(y)=\frac{T(x_{0}+\lambda y)-T(x_{0})}{\lambda}
.
The same argument applies to
\psi_{\lambda}
.

∎

Lemma 6.3

(Uniform scaled estimates)
.

Fix
x_{0}\in B_{R^{*}}
and set
q=\tfrac{1}{p-1}
. For
\lambda>0
, let
\Omega_{\lambda}=\{(y,s):\,s<T_{\lambda}(y)\}
and

\{s=T_{\lambda}(y)\}
. Under

–

5

(depending on

) such that, for all

\displaystyle C_{1}(\phi_{\lambda}+\psi_{\lambda})^{p}\leq\partial_{s}\phi_{\lambda}\leq C_{2}(\phi_{\lambda}+\psi_{\lambda})^{p},

(6.14)

\displaystyle C_{1}(\phi_{\lambda}+\psi_{\lambda})^{p}\leq\partial_{s}\psi_{\lambda}\leq C_{2}(\phi_{\lambda}+\psi_{\lambda})^{p},

(6.15)

\displaystyle C_{1}\bigl(T_{\lambda}(y)-s\bigr)^{-q}\leq\phi_{\lambda}(y,s)\leq C_{2}\bigl(T_{\lambda}(y)-s\bigr)^{-q},

\phi_{\lambda}(y,s)

Proof.

Fix

and set

.
We use the scalings

\partial_{s}\phi_{\lambda}=\lambda^{q+1}\partial_{t}\phi,\qquad\partial_{y}\phi_{\lambda}=\lambda^{q+1}\partial_{x}\phi,

(and similarly for
\psi_{\lambda}
) and

\phi_{\lambda}+\psi_{\lambda}=\lambda^{q}(\phi+\psi).

By Theorem
3.4
and the identity
pq=q+1
, we have

C_{1}(\phi+\psi)^{p}\leq\partial_{t}\phi\leq C_{2}(\phi+\psi)^{p},\qquad C_{1}(\phi+\psi)^{p}\leq\partial_{t}\psi\leq C_{2}(\phi+\psi)^{p}.

Multiplying by
\lambda^{q+1}
and using the previous scaling yields

C_{1}(\phi_{\lambda}+\psi_{\lambda})^{p}\leq\partial_{s}\phi_{\lambda}\leq C_{2}(\phi_{\lambda}+\psi_{\lambda})^{p},\qquad C_{1}(\phi_{\lambda}+\psi_{\lambda})^{p}\leq\partial_{s}\psi_{\lambda}\leq C_{2}(\phi_{\lambda}+\psi_{\lambda})^{p}.

Next, Remark
5.3
gives

C_{1}\,(T(x)-t)^{-q}\leq\phi(x,t),\psi(x,t)\leq C_{2}\,(T(x)-t)^{-q}.

Since
T(x_{0}+\lambda y)-\big(T(x_{0})+\lambda s\big)=\lambda\big(T_{\lambda}(y)-s\big)

\lambda^{q}
gives

C_{1}\,(T_{\lambda}(y)-s)^{-q}\leq\phi_{\lambda}(y,s),\psi_{\lambda}(y,s)\leq C_{2}\,(T_{\lambda}(y)-s)^{-q}.

From Lemma

we have
\partial_{t}\phi\geq(1+\varepsilon_{1})|\partial_{x}\phi|
and
\partial_{t}\psi\geq(1+\varepsilon_{1})|\partial_{x}\psi|

under the scaling this becomes

|\partial_{y}\phi_{\lambda}|\leq\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon_{1}}\,\partial_{s}\phi_{\lambda},\qquad|\partial_{y}\psi_{\lambda}|\leq\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon_{1}}\,\partial_{s}\psi_{\lambda}.

The Lipschitz estimate of Theorem
3.5
implies

|T_{\lambda}(y)-T_{\lambda}(y^{\prime})|\leq L\,|y-y^{\prime}|\quad\text{with}\quad L:=(1+\varepsilon_{1})^{-1}<1.

Hence the Euclidean distance

from

and since

\frac{T_{\lambda}(y)-s}{\sqrt{2}}\leq d_{\lambda}(y,s)\leq T_{\lambda}(y)-s.

Finally, Lemma

Under the scaling

(a linear combination of

All constants depend only on

\lambda

D_{r}W_{\phi},D_{r}W_{\psi}\to 0
as
d_{0}(y,s)\to\infty
in
\Omega^{\prime}_{0}
;

•

the tangential monotonicity
\partial_{\sigma}w_{\phi},\partial_{\sigma}w_{\psi}\geq 0
is preserved by translation and therefore
\partial_{\sigma}W_{\phi},\partial_{\sigma}W_{\psi}\geq 0
in
\Omega^{\prime}_{0}
.

Let any compact
K\subset\Omega^{\prime}_{0}
and
\lambda_{n}<\lambda_{m}
. By tangential monotonicity, we have

w_{\phi,\lambda_{n}}(y,s)\;\leq\;w_{\phi,\lambda_{m}}(y,s)\quad\text{on }K.

Passing to the limits as
n,m\to\infty
shows that
\partial_{\sigma}W_{\phi}\equiv 0
on
K
. Since
K
is arbitrary chosen, we obtain that

\partial_{\sigma}W_{\phi}=\partial_{\sigma}W_{\psi}=0\quad\text{in }\Omega^{\prime}_{0}.

(6.43)

Hence, thanks to (
6.43
), both
W_{\phi},W_{\psi}
depend only on the normal variable

\rho:=\beta y-s
and solve

\left\{\begin{aligned} q(1+\beta)W_{\phi}(\rho)=2^{-p}\big(W_{\phi}(\rho)+W_{\psi}(\rho)\big)^{p},\\
q(1-\beta)W_{\psi}(\rho)=2^{-p}\big(W_{\phi}(\rho)+W_{\psi}(\rho)\big)^{p},\end{aligned}\right.

(6.44)

Therefore,
W_{\phi}=V_{\phi,\beta}
and
W_{\psi}=V_{\psi,\beta}
in
\Omega^{\prime}_{0}
, as claimed.
∎

Having established the convergence properties in Lemma
6.8
, we now derive an important monotonicity result for the directional derivatives.

Lemma 6.9
.

Assume A
1
–A
5
. Then, we have

\partial_{\sigma}w_{\phi},\partial_{\sigma}w_{\psi}\geq 0\quad\text{in }\tilde{\Omega}_{0},

where
\tilde{\Omega}_{0}
is defined in (
6.39
).

Proof.

By Lemma
6.8
, for every fixed
(y,s)\in\tilde{\Omega}_{0}
the functions

w_{\phi,\lambda}(y,s)=w_{\phi}(y-\lambda,s-\beta\lambda),\qquad w_{\psi,\lambda}(y,s)=w_{\psi}(y-\lambda,s-\beta\lambda)

converge (locally uniformly) as
\lambda\to\infty
to the profiles
V_{\phi,\beta},V_{\psi,\beta}
. Hence,

\partial_{\sigma}w_{\phi,\lambda}(y,s)=\partial_{\sigma}w_{\phi}(y-\lambda,s-\beta\lambda)\;\longrightarrow\;0,\quad\partial_{\sigma}w_{\psi,\lambda}(y,s)\;\longrightarrow\;0,

as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Moreover, we have

\partial_{\sigma}^{2}w_{\phi}\geq 0,\qquad\partial_{\sigma}^{2}w_{\psi}\geq 0\quad\text{in }\tilde{\Omega}_{0}.

Now, let
(y,s)\in\tilde{\Omega}_{0}
and define
h_{\phi}(\lambda):=\partial_{\sigma}w_{\phi}(y-\lambda,s-\beta\lambda)
.

Then,
h_{\phi}^{\prime}(\lambda)=-\partial_{\sigma}^{2}w_{\phi}(y-\lambda,s-\beta\lambda)\leq 0
, so
h_{\phi}
is nonincreasing (in
\lambda
) and
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}h_{\phi}(\lambda)=0
. Therefore,
h_{\phi}(0)=\partial_{\sigma}w_{\phi}(y,s)\geq 0
.

The same argument applies to
w_{\psi}
. This ends the proof of this lemma.
∎

Building on these monotonicity properties, we can now establish a key comparison inequality between the limiting solutions.

Lemma 6.10
.

Assume A
1
–A
5
. Then, we have

V_{\phi,\beta}+V_{\psi,\beta}\geq w_{\phi}+w_{\psi}\quad\text{in }\Omega_{\beta},

where
\Omega_{\beta}
is defined in (
6.41
).

Proof.

Recall that

\partial_{s}w_{\phi}-\beta\partial_{y}w_{\phi}=2^{-p}(w_{\phi}+w_{\psi})^{p},\qquad\partial_{s}w_{\psi}+\beta\partial_{y}w_{\psi}=2^{-p}(w_{\phi}+w_{\psi})^{p}.

Denote
S:=w_{\phi}+w_{\psi}
. A direct computation gives the identity

(\partial_{\tau}^{2}-\partial_{\sigma}^{2})S=(1-\beta^{2})\,(\partial_{s}^{2}-\partial_{y}^{2})S=2^{-p+1}p\,(1-\beta^{2})\,S^{p-1}\,\partial_{s}S.

(6.45)

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Now, define the regularized profiles

V_{\phi,\beta}^{\varepsilon}(y,s)=C_{\phi,\beta}\,(\beta y-\varepsilon-s)^{-q},\qquad V_{\psi,\beta}^{\varepsilon}(y,s)=C_{\psi,\beta}\,(\beta y-\varepsilon-s)^{-q},

and set
S_{\varepsilon}:=V_{\phi,\beta}^{\varepsilon}+V_{\psi,\beta}^{\varepsilon}
. Then,
S_{\varepsilon}
satisfies, in
\Omega_{\beta}^{\varepsilon}:=\{(y,s):\ s<\beta y-\varepsilon\}\subset\Omega_{\beta}
,

(\partial_{\tau}^{2}-\partial_{\sigma}^{2})S_{\varepsilon}(y,s)\;\leq\;2^{-p+1}p\,(1-\beta^{2})\,S_{\varepsilon}^{\,p-1}(y,s)\,\partial_{s}S_{\varepsilon}(y,s).

(6.46)

On the upper boundary
s=\beta y-\varepsilon
, we have
S_{\varepsilon}(y,s)\to\infty
while
S(y,s)
remains finite, hence

S(y,s)-S_{\varepsilon}(y,s)\to-\infty\quad\text{on }\{s=\beta y-\varepsilon\}.

As
|\beta y-s|\to\infty
inside
\Omega_{\beta}^{\varepsilon}
, both
S
and
S_{\varepsilon}
vanish, and therefore

\big(S-S_{\varepsilon}\big)(y,s)\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad|\beta y-s|\to\infty.

Assume by contradiction that
S-S_{\varepsilon}
attains a positive local maximum at some
(y_{e},s_{e})\in\Omega_{\beta}^{\varepsilon}
.
Set

\tau:=s+\beta y,\qquad\zeta:=s-\beta y,

so that
s=\tfrac{\tau+\zeta}{2}
and
y=\tfrac{\tau-\zeta}{2\beta}
, hence

\partial_{\tau}=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{s}+\tfrac{1}{2\beta}\partial_{y},\qquad\partial_{\zeta}=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{s}-\tfrac{1}{2\beta}\partial_{y}.

Consider the ray

m_{e}:=\{(y,s)=(y_{e},s_{e})+\theta\,(1/\beta,1)\ :\ \theta\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Along
m_{e}
, one has
\frac{d}{d\theta}\zeta=0
and
\frac{d}{d\theta}\tau=2
, so the restriction

\theta\mapsto F\big((y_{e},s_{e})+\theta(1/\beta,1)\big)
(with
F:=S-S_{\varepsilon}
) is a one–variable function in the
\tau
–direction.
Along
m_{e}
, we have

\frac{d}{d\theta}F=\tfrac{1}{\beta}\,\partial_{y}F+\partial_{s}F=2\,\partial_{\tau}F,\qquad\frac{d^{2}}{d\theta^{2}}F=4\,\partial_{\tau}^{2}F.

Since
(y_{e},s_{e})
is a local maximum of
F
, the restriction has a local maximum at
\theta=0
, hence

\partial_{\tau}F(y_{e},s_{e})=0,\qquad\partial_{\tau}^{2}F(y_{e},s_{e})\leq 0.

Moreover,
\nabla F(y_{e},s_{e})=0
, so

\partial_{\sigma}F(y_{e},s_{e})=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{s}F-\tfrac{1}{2\beta}\partial_{y}F=0.

At
(y_{e},s_{e})
we thus have

\partial_{\tau}(S-S_{\varepsilon})=0,\qquad\partial_{\zeta}(S-S_{\varepsilon})=0,\qquad\partial_{\tau}^{2}(S-S_{\varepsilon})\leq 0.

Applying (
6.45
) to
S
and the subsolution inequality (
6.46
) to
S_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain at
(y_{e},s_{e})
:

\displaystyle(\partial_{\tau}^{2}-\partial_{\zeta}^{2})(S-S_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=2^{-p+1}p(1-\beta^{2})\Big(S^{p-1}\partial_{s}S-S_{\varepsilon}^{p-1}\partial_{s}S_{\varepsilon}\Big)

\displaystyle=2^{-p+1}p(1-\beta^{2})\Big(\partial_{s}S\,(S^{p-1}-S_{\varepsilon}^{p-1})+S_{\varepsilon}^{p-1}\,\partial_{s}(S-S_{\varepsilon})\Big).

But at the local maximum
\partial_{s}(S-S_{\varepsilon})(y_{e},s_{e})=0
, and since
S(y_{e},s_{e})>S_{\varepsilon}(y_{e},s_{e})
, the remaining term on the right-hand side in (
6.2
) is positive. This contradicts the fact that, at a local maximum, the left–hand side in (
6.2
) is nonpositive in the
\tau
–direction (we have
\partial_{\tau}^{2}(S-S_{\varepsilon})\leq 0
and
\partial_{\zeta}(S-S_{\varepsilon})=0
there). Therefore
S\leq S_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega_{\beta}^{\varepsilon}
.

Finally, letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
yields
w_{\phi}+w_{\psi}=S\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}S_{\varepsilon}=V_{\phi,\beta}+V_{\psi,\beta}
in
\Omega_{\beta}
.
∎

Having established the comparison between the solutions in Lemma
6.10
, we now prove the exact equality.

Lemma 6.11
.

Assume A
1
–A
5
. Then, we have

w_{\phi}+w_{\psi}=V_{\phi,\beta}+V_{\psi,\beta}\quad\text{in }\Omega_{\beta}.

Proof.

Lemma
6.9
gives
\partial_{\sigma}w_{\phi},\partial_{\sigma}w_{\psi}\geq 0
in
\tilde{\Omega}_{0}
, whereas
\partial_{\sigma}V_{\phi,\beta}=\partial_{\sigma}V_{\psi,\beta}=0
.

By Lemma
6.8
, there exists a sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}

such that

w_{\phi,\lambda_{n}}\to V_{\phi,\beta},\qquad w_{\psi,\lambda_{n}}\to V_{\psi,\beta}\quad\text{locally uniformly in }\tilde{\Omega}_{0}.

Passing to the limit along
\lambda_{n}
in the monotonicity
\partial_{\sigma}w_{\phi}\geq 0
,
\partial_{\sigma}w_{\psi}\geq 0
and using
\partial_{\sigma}V_{\cdot,\beta}\equiv 0
yields the pointwise inequalities

w_{\phi}\geq V_{\phi,\beta},\qquad w_{\psi}\geq V_{\psi,\beta}\quad\text{in }\tilde{\Omega}_{0}.

(6.47)

Combining (
6.47
) with Lemma
6.10
(which gives
V_{\phi,\beta}+V_{\psi,\beta}\geq w_{\phi}+w_{\psi}
in
\Omega_{\beta}
) forces

w_{\phi}+w_{\psi}=V_{\phi,\beta}+V_{\psi,\beta}\quad\text{in }\Omega_{\beta},

as claimed.
∎

With the sum equality established, we now prove the individual component equalities:

Lemma 6.12
.

Assume A
1
–A
5
. Then, we have

w_{\phi}=V_{\phi,\beta},\quad w_{\psi}=V_{\psi,\beta}\quad\text{in }\tilde{\Omega}_{\beta},

where
\tilde{\Omega}_{\beta}
is defined in (
6.41
).

Proof.

Let
(y,s)\in\tilde{\Omega}_{\beta}\setminus\Omega_{\beta}
and choose
(y_{1},s_{1}),(y_{2},s)\in\Omega_{\beta}
such that the two characteristic segments from
(y,s)
to
(y_{1},s_{1})
have the slopes
\pm\beta
:

\frac{s_{1}-s}{y_{1}-y_{2}}=\frac{1}{\beta}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{s_{1}-s}{y_{1}-y}=-\,\frac{1}{\beta}.

The functions
w_{\phi},w_{\psi}
,
V_{\phi,\beta}
and
V_{\psi,\beta}
solve the system

\partial_{s}w_{\phi}-\beta\partial_{y}w_{\phi}=2^{-p}(w_{\phi}+w_{\psi})^{p},\qquad\partial_{s}w_{\psi}+\beta\partial_{y}w_{\psi}=2^{-p}(w_{\phi}+w_{\psi})^{p},

(and the same holds with
w
being replaced by
V_{\beta}
). Thus, integrating along the characteristics gives the sum representation

\displaystyle(w_{\phi}+w_{\psi})(y_{1},s_{1})

\displaystyle=w_{\phi}(y,s)+w_{\psi}(y_{2},s)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{s_{1}-s}\!2^{-p}\,(w_{\phi}+w_{\psi})^{p}\!\bigl(y_{1}-\beta(s_{1}-s-r),\,s+r\bigr)\,dr

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{s_{1}-s}\!2^{-p}\,(w_{\phi}+w_{\psi})^{p}\!\bigl(y_{1}+\beta(s_{1}-s-r),\,s+r\bigr)\,dr,

and an analogous identity stands with
w
replaced by
V_{\beta}
.

Now, if
w_{\phi}(y,s)\neq V_{\phi,\beta}(y,s)
, then by (
6.47
) we have
w_{\phi}(y,s)>V_{\phi,\beta}(y,s)
.

Using also
w_{\psi}\geq V_{\psi,\beta}
and the fact (from Lemma
6.11
) that

(w_{\phi}+w_{\psi})^{p}=(V_{\phi,\beta}+V_{\psi,\beta})^{p}\quad\text{in }\Omega_{\beta},

the two characteristic formulas yield

(w_{\phi}+w_{\psi})(y_{1},s_{1})>(V_{\phi,\beta}+V_{\psi,\beta})(y_{1},s_{1}),

contradicting Lemma
6.11
. Hence,

w_{\phi}=V_{\phi,\beta}\quad\text{in }\tilde{\Omega}_{\beta}.

(6.48)

Finally, from Lemma
6.11
, we obtain the following identity

w_{\phi}+w_{\psi}=V_{\phi,\beta}+V_{\psi,\beta}\quad\text{in }\tilde{\Omega}_{\beta},

that we combine with (
6.48
) yielding thus
w_{\psi}=V_{\psi,\beta}
in
\tilde{\Omega}_{\beta}
as well.
∎

Now, we complete the proof of Theorem
3.6
by combining our previous results.

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

Following the same methodology used for
\beta
, we can extract a subsequence that realizes the
\alpha
-slope (
i.e.
,
T_{0,\lambda}(y)\ \xrightarrow[\lambda\to 0^{+}]{}\ \alpha\,y,\qquad y>0.
), and obtain the limiting functions

w_{\phi}=V_{\phi,\alpha}\quad\text{and}\quad w_{\psi}=V_{\psi,\alpha}\quad\text{in }\tilde{\Omega}_{\alpha},

where, as before,
\tilde{\Omega}_{\alpha}:=\{(y,s):s<\alpha y\}
.

If
\alpha\neq\beta
, then on the nonempty intersection

\emptyset\neq\tilde{\Omega}_{\alpha}\cap\tilde{\Omega}_{\beta}=\{(y,s):s<\min(\alpha y,\beta y)\},

we would simultaneously have

w_{\phi}=V_{\phi,\alpha}=C_{\phi,\alpha}(\alpha y-s)^{-q},\quad\text{and}\quad w_{\phi}=V_{\phi,\beta}=C_{\phi,\beta}(\beta y-s)^{-q},

(and similarly for
w_{\psi}
). Since the functions
(\alpha y-s)^{-q}
and
(\beta y-s)^{-q}
are distinct on any open set when
\alpha\neq\beta
, equality on the open set
\tilde{\Omega}_{\alpha}\cap\tilde{\Omega}_{\beta}
is impossible unless
\alpha=\beta
.

Therefore,

\alpha=\beta,

and, in particular,

\inf_{\lambda_{n}}\frac{T_{0}(\lambda_{n}y)}{\lambda_{n}y}=\alpha=\beta=\sup_{\lambda_{n}}\frac{T_{0}(\lambda_{n}y)}{\lambda_{n}y}.

This equality of infimum and supremum implies the linearity of
T_{0}
:

T_{0}(y)=\alpha y,\quad\text{for all}\ \ y\in\mathbb{R}.

Finally, combining the two characteristic identities for
(w_{\phi}+w_{\psi})
and
(V_{\phi,\beta}+V_{\psi,\beta})
in Lemma
6.12
with Lemma
6.11
and (
6.47
)–(
6.48
), and using the scaling limit
T_{0,\lambda}(y)\xrightarrow[\lambda\downarrow 0]{}\beta y
, we obtain
w_{\phi}=V_{\phi,\beta}
and
w_{\psi}=V_{\psi,\beta}
in
\tilde{\Omega}_{\beta}
. Hence,
T_{0}(y)=\beta y
for all
y
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
3.6
.
∎

7
Proof of the Continuous Differentiability of the Blow-up Curve

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
3.7
. We start by demonstrating that
T
is differentiable on
B_{R^{*}}
which is defined by (
1.3
). For that purpose, we introduce, for the variables
(x,t)
, the following directional derivative in the direction of
\theta
:

D_{\theta}:=\cos\theta\,\partial_{x}+\sin\theta\,\partial_{t},

and for the rescaled variables
(y,s)
we use the same notation,

D_{\theta}:=\cos\theta\,\partial_{y}+\sin\theta\,\partial_{s}.

If
\phi_{\lambda}(y,s)=\lambda^{q}\phi(x_{0}+\lambda y,T(x_{0})+\lambda s)
, then

D_{\theta}\phi_{\lambda}(y,s)=\lambda^{q+1}\,\bigl(D_{\theta}\phi\bigr)\bigl(x_{0}+\lambda y,\,T(x_{0})+\lambda s\bigr),

(7.1)

making the signs of
D_{\theta}\phi_{\lambda}
and
D_{\theta}\phi
coincide under the blow-up scaling.

We now proceed by contradiction. Suppose, for that purpose, that there exists
x_{0}\in B_{R^{*}}
such that
T
is not differentiable at
x_{0}
. Then, by the arguments in Section
6.2
(mainly the fact that there exist two different limiting slopes from the non differentiability), there exist sequences
\{\lambda_{n}^{(1)}\}_{n\geq 1},

\{\lambda_{n}^{(2)}\}_{n\geq 1}
and constants
\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}
such that

\phi_{\lambda_{n}}^{(1)}\to V_{\phi,\alpha_{1}},\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{\lambda_{n}}^{(2)}\to V_{\phi,\alpha_{2}},

as
\lambda_{n}^{(1)},\lambda_{n}^{(2)}\to 0
, locally uniformly in
\Omega_{0}
, with corresponding blow-up curves

T_{0}^{(1)}(y)=\alpha_{1}y,\quad\text{and}\quad T_{0}^{(2)}(y)=\alpha_{2}y,\quad\text{for }y\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
\theta_{\alpha_{j}}=\arctan\alpha_{j}
for
j=1,2
. Without loss of generality, assume
0\leq\theta_{\alpha_{1}}<\theta_{\alpha_{2}}<\pi
and choose
0<\varepsilon<\pi/2
such that
\theta_{\alpha_{1}}+\varepsilon<\theta_{\alpha_{2}}-\varepsilon
.

For simplicity, we take
0\leq\theta_{\alpha_{1}}<\theta_{\alpha_{2}}<\pi/2
.

A direct computation gives

\displaystyle D_{\theta}V_{\phi,\alpha}(y,s)

\displaystyle=q\,C_{\phi,\alpha}\,\bigl(\alpha y-s\bigr)^{-q-1}\,\bigl(\sin\theta-\alpha\cos\theta\bigr)

(7.2)

\displaystyle=q\,C_{\phi,\alpha}\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}\,\bigl(\alpha y-s\bigr)^{-q-1}\,\sin(\theta-\theta_{\alpha}).

Hence
D_{\theta}V_{\phi,\alpha}>0
whenever
\theta\in(\theta_{\alpha},\theta_{\alpha}+\pi)
and
s<\alpha y
.

Moreover, we have

D_{\theta+\pi}V_{\phi,\alpha}=-D_{\theta}V_{\phi,\alpha}.

(7.3)

Define the angular sectors for
j=1,2
:

S_{j}^{\varepsilon}=\{\theta_{j}:\theta_{\alpha_{j}}+\varepsilon<\theta_{j}<\theta_{\alpha_{j}}+\pi-\varepsilon\}.

By (
7.2
),
D_{\theta_{j}}V_{\phi,\alpha_{j}}>0
for every
\theta_{j}\in S_{j}^{\varepsilon}
.

Since the positivity margin in (
7.2
) is uniform once we stay a fixed distance
\varepsilon
away from the boundary angles and restrict to a small ball below both lines
s=\alpha_{j}y
, there exist
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
and
\rho>0
such that

D_{\theta_{1}}V_{\phi,\alpha_{1}}>2\varepsilon^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad D_{\theta_{2}}V_{\phi,\alpha_{2}}>2\varepsilon^{\prime},

in
\Omega_{1,0}\cap\Omega_{2,0}\cap B_{\rho}(0,0)
, where

\Omega_{j,0}=\{(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:s<\alpha_{j}y\},\quad j=1,2,

and

B_{\rho}(y^{\prime},s^{\prime})=\{(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\sqrt{(y-y^{\prime})^{2}+(s-s^{\prime})^{2}}<\rho\}.

Furthermore, by the
\mathcal{C}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}-
convergence in the scaling variables, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\Omega_{\lambda_{n_{0}}}^{(1)}\cap\Omega_{\lambda_{n_{0}}}^{(2)}\cap\Omega_{1,0}\cap\Omega_{2,0}\cap B_{\rho}(0,0)\neq\emptyset,

and

|D_{\theta_{1}}\phi_{\lambda_{n_{0}}}^{(1)}-D_{\theta_{1}}V_{\phi,\alpha_{1}}|\leq\varepsilon^{\prime},\quad|D_{\theta_{2}}\phi_{\lambda_{n_{0}}}^{(2)}-D_{\theta_{2}}V_{\phi,\alpha_{2}}|\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Here

\Omega_{\lambda}=\{(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:(x_{0}+\lambda y,T(x_{0})+\lambda s)\in\Omega\}.

Therefore, the
\mathcal{C}^{1}-
convergence of
\phi_{\lambda_{n}^{(j)}}
to
V_{\phi,\alpha_{j}}
, on any compact subset of
\Omega_{j,0}
, follows from Section
6.2
. Indeed, the nonempty intersection of the domains holds for
n_{0}
large since all sets are open and the lines
s=\alpha_{j}y
pass through the origin.

Using (
7.1
), we deduce that

D_{\theta}\phi>0,\quad\text{for }\theta\in S_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}\cup S_{\varepsilon}^{(2)},

(7.4)

inside the subset

\Omega_{x_{0},n_{0}}^{*}=\Omega\cap B_{\min\{\lambda_{n_{0}}^{(1)},\lambda_{n_{0}}^{(2)}\}\rho}(x_{0},T(x_{0}))\cap\Omega_{1}^{*}\cap\Omega_{2}^{*},

where
\Omega_{j}^{*}=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:t-T(x_{0})<\alpha_{j}(x-x_{0})\}
for
j=1,2
.

In particular, for
\theta^{*}\in(\theta_{\alpha_{1}}+\varepsilon,\theta_{\alpha_{2}}-\varepsilon)\subset S_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}
, we have

D_{\theta^{*}}\phi>0\quad\text{in }\Omega_{x_{0},n_{0}}^{*}.

(7.5)

Moreover, since
\theta^{*}+\pi\in(\theta_{\alpha_{1}}+\pi+\varepsilon,\theta_{\alpha_{2}}+\pi-\varepsilon)\subset S_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}
,

D_{\theta^{*}+\pi}\phi>0\quad\text{in }\Omega_{x_{0},n_{0}}^{*}.

(7.6)

However, (
7.5
) and (
7.6
) contradict the identity
D_{\theta^{*}}\phi=-D_{\theta^{*}+\pi}\phi
in
\Omega_{x_{0},n_{0}}^{*}
, which follows from the definition of
D_{\theta}
.

Therefore,
T
must be differentiable at
x_{0}\in B_{R^{*}}
.

We now establish the continuity of
T^{\prime}
in
B_{R^{*}}
by contradiction.

Assume there exists
x_{0}\in B_{R^{*}}
where
T^{\prime}
is discontinuous. Let
\alpha_{x_{0}}=T^{\prime}(x_{0})
and define the local domain:

\Omega_{x_{0},n}^{**}=\Omega\cap B_{\lambda_{n}\rho}(x_{0},T(x_{0}))\cap\Omega_{x_{0}}^{**},

where

\Omega_{x_{0}}^{**}=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:t-T(x_{0})<\alpha_{x_{0}}(x-x_{0})\}.

By the discontinuity assumption, there exists
x_{1}\in B_{R^{*}}
(with
\alpha_{x_{1}}=T^{\prime}(x_{1})
) and
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
with
n_{0},n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that:

\begin{split}&0<\varepsilon^{\prime}<\frac{\pi}{2},\\
&\Omega_{x_{0},n_{0}}^{**}\cap\Omega_{x_{1},n_{1}}^{**}\neq\emptyset,\\
&\big|\theta_{\alpha_{x_{1}}}-\theta_{\alpha_{x_{0}}}\big|>2\varepsilon^{\prime}.\end{split}

(7.7)

We choose
n_{0},n_{1}
large so that

\Omega_{x_{0},n_{0}}^{**}\cap\Omega_{x_{1},n_{1}}^{**}\neq\emptyset,

and
\mathcal{C}^{1}_{\text{loc}}
–closeness holds on the intersection:

\bigl|D_{\theta_{\alpha_{x_{0}}}}\phi_{\lambda_{n_{0}}}^{(0)}-D_{\theta_{\alpha_{x_{0}}}}V_{\phi,\alpha_{x_{0}}}\bigr|\leq\varepsilon^{\prime},\quad\bigl|D_{\theta_{\alpha_{x_{1}}}}\phi_{\lambda_{n_{1}}}^{(1)}-D_{\theta_{\alpha_{x_{1}}}}V_{\phi,\alpha_{x_{1}}}\bigr|\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Here
\theta_{\alpha_{x_{j}}}=\arctan\alpha_{x_{j}}
for
j=0,1
. Define the angular sectors for each point:

S_{\varepsilon^{\prime},x_{j}}=\{\theta_{j}:\theta_{\alpha_{x_{j}}}+\varepsilon^{\prime}<\theta_{j}<\theta_{\alpha_{x_{j}}}+\pi-\varepsilon^{\prime}\},\quad j=0,1.

Arguing similarly as the differentiability proof (using the similarity limit with slope
\alpha_{x_{j}}
and the
\mathcal{C}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
convergence in scaling coordinates around
(x_{j},T(x_{j}))
), we obtain for each
j=0,1
:

D_{\theta_{j}}\phi>0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega_{x_{j},n_{j}}^{**}\quad\text{for all }\theta_{j}\in S_{\varepsilon^{\prime},x_{j}}.

Assume without loss of generality that
0\leq\theta_{\alpha_{x_{0}}}<\theta_{\alpha_{x_{1}}}<\pi/2
.

From (
7.7
), we have

\theta_{\alpha_{x_{0}}}+\varepsilon^{\prime}<\theta_{\alpha_{x_{1}}}-\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Choosing any
\tilde{\theta}
in the interval
(\theta_{\alpha_{x_{0}}}+\varepsilon^{\prime},\theta_{\alpha_{x_{1}}}-\varepsilon^{\prime})
yields the following:

(i)

If
\tilde{\theta}\in S_{\varepsilon^{\prime},x_{0}}
then
D_{\tilde{\theta}}\phi>0
in
\Omega_{x_{0},n_{0}}^{**}
,

(ii)

If
\tilde{\theta}+\pi\in S_{\varepsilon^{\prime},x_{1}}
then
D_{\tilde{\theta}+\pi}\phi>0
in
\Omega_{x_{1},n_{1}}^{**}
.

On the nonempty intersection
\Omega_{x_{0},n_{0}}^{**}\cap\Omega_{x_{1},n_{1}}^{**}
, both inequalities hold simultaneously, which contradicts the fundamental identity,

D_{\tilde{\theta}+\pi}\phi=-D_{\tilde{\theta}}\phi\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega.

Hence, this implies that
T^{\prime}
must be continuous at every
x_{0}\in B_{R^{*}}
.

This completes the proof of
\mathcal{C}^{1}-
regularity for the blow-up curve
T
, and Theorem
3.7
is thus proven.
