Beyond Averaging in John Ellipsoid Approximation: High-Accuracy Algorithms in the Leverage-Score Model
Xiaoyu Li 1 Junwei Yu 2 Jiaojiao Jiang 1 Junbin Gao 3 Andi Han 3 1 University of New South Wales 2 University of California, Berkeley 3 University of Sydney

Abstract
The John ellipsoid of a symmetric polytope P = { 𝐱 ∈ ℝ d : ‖ 𝐀𝐱 ‖ ∞ ≤ 1 } P=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1\} , 𝐀 ∈ ℝ n × d \mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d} , is computed by a long line of leverage-score algorithms, from Cohen, Cousins, Lee and Yang (COLT 2019) [ 19 ] to its successors [ 78 , 15 ] , all reaching a ( 1 + ε ) (1+\varepsilon) -approximation in Θ ⁡ ( ε − 1 ​ log ⁡ ( n / d ) ) \Theta(\varepsilon^{-1}\log(n/d)) iterations. We separate this complexity into three costs the modern line conflates ( certification , identification , and accuracy ) and locate the historical ε − 1 \varepsilon^{-1} in the first alone. In the equivalent D-optimal-design form min 𝐩 ∈ Δ n − log det ( ∑ i p i 𝐚 i 𝐚 i ⊤ ) \min_{\mathbf{p}\in\Delta_{n}}-\log\det(\sum_{i}p_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}) , the leverage-score oracle is exactly the first-order oracle and the ( 1 + ε ) (1+\varepsilon) -John guarantee the Frank–Wolfe gap g ⁡ ( 𝐩 ) ≤ ε ​ d g(\mathbf{p})\leq\varepsilon d ; through this dictionary the costs come apart. The ε − 1 \varepsilon^{-1} is a certification artifact: the uniform average of the iterates, the certificate used throughout the line, has gap exactly Θ ⁡ ( 1 / T ) \Theta(1/T) , however cheap each iteration is made. Pointed instead at the last iterate the same oracle is fast: a warm-started accelerated method reaches the guarantee in C ⁡ ( 𝐀 ) + O ⁡ ( κ ​ log ⁡ ( 1 / ε ) ) C(\mathbf{A})+O(\sqrt{\kappa}\,\log(1/\varepsilon)) queries after an ε \varepsilon -independent setup C ⁡ ( 𝐀 ) C(\mathbf{A}) , and once the optimal face is identified the facial problem is an unconstrained self-concordant minimization whose Hessian the oracle recovers exactly , so damped Newton needs only O ⁡ ( log ⁡ log ⁡ ( 1 / ε ) ) O(\log\log(1/\varepsilon)) steps, for a total of C ⁡ ( 𝐀 ) + O ⁡ ( d 2 ​ log ⁡ log ⁡ ( 1 / ε ) ) C(\mathbf{A})+O(d^{2}\log\log(1/\varepsilon)) queries. The accuracy dependence is thus doubly logarithmic after an ε \varepsilon -independent, condition-dependent setup; the open problem is the remaining identification cost (a condition-free bound on reaching the optimal face) and lower bounds. Accuracy is not the obstruction.

Introduction

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
have rows
\mathbf{a}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{a}_{n}
and rank
d
, and let

P=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{x}|\leq 1,\ i\in[n]\}
be the associated centrally symmetric polytope. The

John ellipsoid
of
P
is its maximum-volume inscribed ellipsoid. It is a foundational
primitive: it rounds a convex body to near-isotropic position (an affine image with near-identity
covariance); it preconditions the Vaidya and John random walks used for sampling and volume
computation
[
16
,
33
]
; it centers cutting-plane methods
[
44
]
; and, in its
dual guise as
D-/G-optimal design
, it is the basic object of optimal experimental
design
[
65
]
and of
\ell_{\infty}
regression—the John ellipsoid is exactly the

\ell_{\infty}

Lewis-weight
ellipsoid
[
23
]
. We study the iteration complexity of computing a
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximate
John ellipsoid in the standard
leverage-score oracle
model, in which one “query” returns, for
a chosen weighting
\mathbf{p}
of the constraints, the vector of leverage scores

v_{i}(\mathbf{p})=\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\big(\sum_{j}p_{j}\mathbf{a}_{j}\mathbf{a}_{j}^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{i}
. This is the cost model of essentially every
modern algorithm for the problem, since one leverage-score computation dominates the cost of one
iteration.

The
\varepsilon^{-1}
barrier.

A
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximate John ellipsoid of a symmetric polytope is captured by a convex program in
n

weights, equivalent to continuous D-optimal design and to
\ell_{\infty}
Lewis-weight computation
(
Section

2
). The modern algorithms solve it by a first-order iteration whose core step is a
leverage-score computation, and they share a single iteration count:
Cohen–Cousins–Lee–Yang
[
19
]
(henceforth CCLY) use
T=O(\varepsilon^{-1}\log(n/d))
iterations
of a multiplicative-weights fixed point; the input-sparsity/treewidth algorithm
[
15
]
and the
lazy-update/streaming algorithm
[
78
]
inherit
the same

O(\varepsilon^{-1}\log(n/d))
iteration count, improving only the per-iteration cost
(
Table

1
). The
\varepsilon^{-1}
has not been improved in this line.

This is puzzling, because both
slower-per-step
and
higher-order
classical methods for the
same program already avoid the
\varepsilon^{-1}
. On the first-order side, the
classical
Frank–Wolfe
(Wolfe–Atwood) algorithms converge linearly: Ahipaşaoğlu–Sun–Todd
[
1
]

proved asymptotic linear convergence of the away-step variant, and Zhao
[
81
]
made it global,
with a rate governed by a facial condition number. At the other extreme, second-order interior-point
methods achieve high accuracy outright: in our notation (
n
constraints, dimension
d
),
Nesterov–Nemirovskii
[
60
]
compute a
(1+\varepsilon)
-John ellipsoid in

O\big(n^{2.5}(d^{2}+n)\log(n/\varepsilon)\big)
time, and Nemirovski
[
59
]
and
Anstreicher
[
7
]
in
O\big(n^{3.5}\log(1/\varepsilon)\big)
time
[
19
,
73
]
.
These have the desired
\log(1/\varepsilon)
dependence, but at an
n^{3.5}
cost far worse than the
near-linear-in-
nd
first-order methods, and they solve a Newton system per step rather than calling
the cheap leverage-score oracle. So neither linear convergence nor
\log(1/\varepsilon)
accuracy is new in
itself—indeed away-step Frank–Wolfe already beats
\varepsilon^{-1}

within
the
first-order/leverage-score model. The puzzle runs the other way:
why does the leverage-score
fixed-point line (CCLY and its input-sparsity and streaming successors, the very methods that drive the
per-iteration cost down to near-linear) remain stuck at
\varepsilon^{-1}
, when the slower-per-step away-step
methods do not?
We resolve this, and then push the other way: we determine how cheap the accuracy
dependence itself can be made in this oracle. The answer is:
almost free
—doubly logarithmic in

1/\varepsilon
, with all condition numbers exiled either into an
\varepsilon
-independent setup term or inside the
double logarithm.

Our answer: three costs, one dictionary.

The
\varepsilon^{-1}
buys a convenient
certificate
, and it is not necessary. Two structural facts
(
Proposition

2.2
) form the dictionary that makes this visible. First, in the D-optimal form

f(\mathbf{p})=-\log\det\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})
with
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})=\sum_{i}p_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}
, the gradient is
\nabla f(\mathbf{p})_{i}=-v_{i}(\mathbf{p})
: the
leverage-score oracle
is
the first-order oracle. Second, the
(1+\varepsilon)
-John guarantee

\max_{i}v_{i}(\mathbf{p})\leq(1+\varepsilon)d
is exactly the Frank–Wolfe duality gap
g(\mathbf{p})=\max_{i}v_{i}(\mathbf{p})-d
of
f
over
the simplex (and, by Kiefer–Wolfowitz, the G-optimal-design criterion). Through this dictionary the cost
of a leverage-score algorithm separates into three pieces that the modern line runs together:

\underbrace{\text{certification}}_{\text{reading off the gap}}\;+\;\underbrace{\text{identification}}_{\text{reaching the optimal face}}\;+\;\underbrace{\text{accuracy}}_{\text{driving the gap to }\varepsilon d}\,,

and the historical
\varepsilon^{-1}
lives entirely in the first. With the dictionary, the CCLY fixed point is a
multiplicative-weights iteration (
p_{i}\propto p_{i}v_{i}(\mathbf{p})/d
), in fact the classical

multiplicative algorithm
of the design literature (
Remark

2.7
), and its analysis
certifies the gap of the
uniform running average
of the iterates via Jensen’s inequality, which is

O(1/T)
. We show this certificate, not the problem or the oracle, is what costs
\varepsilon^{-1}
: the uniform
average of these iterates is intrinsically
\Theta(1/T)
, while the last iterate is geometric and, once
the optimal face is identified, even
quadratically
convergent at no change of oracle. The accuracy
cost thus has three regimes: uniform averaging spends
1/\varepsilon
, an accelerated first-order method

\log(1/\varepsilon)
, and a facial self-concordant phase
\log\log(1/\varepsilon)
. What remains is

identification
(reaching the right face), which is
\varepsilon
-independent but does depend on the
instance’s conditioning, and is exactly where we locate the open problem.

1.1
Our results

Throughout,
\Delta_{n}=\{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}:\sum_{i}p_{i}=1\}
,
v_{i}(\mathbf{p})=\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{i}
, and a

(1+\varepsilon)
-John ellipsoid
is a
\mathbf{p}\in\Delta_{n}
with
\max_{i}v_{i}(\mathbf{p})\leq(1+\varepsilon)d
(
Definition

2.1
).
Our first result locates the
\varepsilon^{-1}
precisely in the averaging step.

Theorem 1.1

(Averaging barrier; informal, see
Theorem

3.4
)
.

On an explicit, perfectly conditioned
4\times 2
instance
\mathbf{A}_{\star}
, the
uniform
running
average of the CCLY leverage-score iterates has Frank–Wolfe gap exactly
\Theta(1/T)
. Hence any
algorithm that certifies the
(1+\varepsilon)
-John guarantee from this average needs
\Omega(1/\varepsilon)

iterations: the
\varepsilon^{-1}
is a property of the averaged certificate, not of the problem or the oracle.

Suffix or weighted averaging, or periodic restart, escape this barrier and recover the geometric
last-iterate rate; the obstruction is specific to averaging uniformly over
all
iterates.

Our second result shows the same oracle, used without averaging, breaks the barrier, and sharpens the
rate. Away-step Frank–Wolfe already attains
\log(1/\varepsilon)
in this model, but
unaccelerated
(rate

\kappa_{\Phi}

[
81
]
); we give the first
accelerated
leverage-score method, with the

\sqrt{\kappa}
dependence characteristic of optimal first-order optimization. Here and throughout,

\kappa=\widehat{L}_{\star}/\mu_{\star}
is the
facial condition number at the optimum
(
Definition

4.4
).

Theorem 1.2

(Accelerated algorithm; informal, see
Theorem

5.5
)
.

On any instance with a nondegenerate optimal design, a warm-started
accelerated
leverage-score
method computes a
(1+\varepsilon)
-John ellipsoid in
C(\mathbf{A})+O\big(\sqrt{\kappa}\,\log(1/\varepsilon)\big)
queries,
where
C(\mathbf{A})
is the
\varepsilon
-independent cost of reaching the optimal face and
\kappa
is the facial
condition number there. Since
\kappa=O(d^{4})\,\kappa_{\Phi}
, this is never worse than the unaccelerated
away-step rate
\kappa_{\Phi}\log(1/\varepsilon)
of
[
81
]
by more than a
\poly(d)
factor, and is
quadratically better when
\kappa\asymp\kappa_{\Phi}
.

Our third result is the new headline: once the optimal face is identified, the
accuracy phase
of
the computation is condition-free, and quadratically convergent—in the
same
oracle. The key
structural fact is that the restriction of
f
to the affine hull of the optimal face is a closed
self-concordant function whose
unconstrained
minimizer over that affine hull is the optimal
design (
Lemma

4.5
): under strict complementarity, the simplex constraints are invisible to the
facial problem, so the classical, affine-invariant Newton theory applies with no condition numbers in
its rate. A single rank-one update identity (
Proposition

6.2
) makes each Newton step
implementable with
O(d^{2})
leverage-score queries: the facial Hessian

\big((\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{j})^{2}\big)_{i,j}
is recovered
exactly
, not approximately, from
leverage queries at
|S^{\star}|
perturbed designs.

Theorem 1.3

(Facial Newton phase; informal, see
Theorem

6.6
)
.

On any instance with a nondegenerate optimal design, once the optimal face is identified the accuracy
phase is condition-free: damped Newton reaches the
(1+\varepsilon)
-John guarantee in

O\big(\log\log(1/\varepsilon)\big)
steps, with
no
condition number outside the double logarithm.
Each step costs
O(d^{2})
leverage-score queries, since a rank-one identity (
Proposition

6.2
)
recovers the facial Hessian
exactly
from the oracle; the total is

C(\mathbf{A})+O\big(d^{2}\log\log(1/\varepsilon)\big)
queries.

Corollary 1.4

(The three rates;
Corollaries

6.7
and
5.9
)
.

On nondegenerate instances, a
(1+\varepsilon)
-John ellipsoid is computable in

C(\mathbf{A})+O\big(d^{2}\log\log(1/\varepsilon)\big)
leverage-score queries. On the explicit instance

\mathbf{A}_{\star}
(for which
\kappa=O(1)
and
C(\mathbf{A}_{\star})=O(1)
), the three certification styles are
separated: uniform averaging needs
\Theta(\varepsilon^{-1})
queries, the accelerated last iterate

O(\log(1/\varepsilon))
, and the facial Newton phase

iterations.

1.2
Technical overview

We sketch the four components: the dictionary, the averaging lower bound, the facial geometry that
powers both upper bounds, and the two facial phases.

The dictionary (
Section

2
).

Write the John-ellipsoid program as
\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\Delta_{n}}f(\mathbf{p})
,
f(\mathbf{p})=-\log\det\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})
. Differentiating

\log\det
gives
\nabla f(\mathbf{p})_{i}=-\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{i}=-v_{i}(\mathbf{p})
, so a single leverage-score query
returns the full gradient. Over the simplex, the Frank–Wolfe gap

g(\mathbf{p})=\max_{\mathbf{q}\in\Delta_{n}}\left\langle-\nabla f(\mathbf{p}),\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{p}\right\rangle
evaluates to
\max_{i}v_{i}(\mathbf{p})-d
(using the identity

\sum_{i}p_{i}v_{i}(\mathbf{p})=d
), which is exactly the constraint violation in the
(1+\varepsilon)
-John guarantee and,
by the Kiefer–Wolfowitz equivalence (
Theorem

2.5
), the G-optimal-design criterion. Finally, the
Hessian is the entrywise square
\nabla^{2}f(\mathbf{p})_{ij}=(\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{j})^{2}
, the Gram matrix of the
rank-one symmetric tensors
\{\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1/2}\mathbf{a}_{i}\}^{\otimes 2}
; it is positive semidefinite of rank at most

\binom{d+1}{2}
. These four facts let us reason about the standard algorithms as first-order methods
and about the John guarantee as a duality gap.

Averaging is
\Theta(1/T)
(
Section

3
).

Why is the whole leverage-score line stuck at
\varepsilon^{-1}
? Because it certifies. The CCLY analysis
bounds the gap of the running average
\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{(T)}
by Jensen’s inequality applied to the convex maps

\mathbf{p}\mapsto\log v_{i}(\mathbf{p})
, giving
g(\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{(T)})=O(1/T)
. We show this is tight, and for an elementary
reason: averaging destroys a linear rate. If the iterates converge linearly to
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
, then

\sum_{t\geq 1}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star})
converges to some
\mathbf{C}
, and the uniform average satisfies

\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{(T)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star}=\frac{1}{T}\mathbf{C}+o(1/T)=\Theta(1/T)
when
\mathbf{C}\neq 0
(
Lemma

3.1
)—the average
lags its own tail by a full factor of
T
.
To convert iterate error into gap, we linearize: at a nondegenerate optimum the gap is, to first order,

g(\mathbf{p}^{\star}+\bm{\delta})=\max_{i\in S^{\star}}\left\langle\nabla v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star}),\bm{\delta}\right\rangle+O(\left\lVert\bm{\delta}\right\rVert^{2})
over the
feasible cone (
Lemma

3.2
). On the explicit instance
\mathbf{A}_{\star}
of
Appendix

E
the
linearized gap
F
is
provably
positive on the feasible cone (a four-line sign
argument,
Lemma

3.3
, with no numerics), and the iterates converge R-linearly (the fixed-point map
is the classical multiplicative algorithm, whose monotone convergence is
known
[
71
,
80
]
, and whose Jacobian spectral radius at the optimum we compute in
closed form). Evaluating at
\bm{\delta}=\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{(T)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star}=\Theta(1/T)
gives

g(\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{(T)})=\Theta(1/T)
: the
\varepsilon^{-1}
is the price of the average, not of the oracle.

The facial geometry, quantitatively (
Section

4
).

Both upper bounds run on the optimal face
F^{\star}
(the constraints with
v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})=d
), and both need
the same three facts. (1)
f
is
not
strongly convex on
\Delta_{n}
(its Hessian has rank at most

\binom{d+1}{2}
), but the rank-deficiency is an exact, harmless overparametrization:
f
, the leverage
scores, and the gap are constant along
\ker\nabla^{2}f
, which is independent of
\mathbf{p}
(
Lemma

4.1
).
(2) On the optimal face, nondegeneracy makes the reduced Hessian positive definite at
every

point, not merely at
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
(
Lemma

4.3
). (3) Most importantly,
-\log\det
is
self-concordant, and we exploit this
quantitatively
rather than through compactness: an
elementary sandwich (
Lemmas

4.6
and
4.7
; the proofs are Frobenius-vs-spectral norm
arithmetic plus one integration) shows that within the Dikin ball of radius
\tfrac{1}{2}
around

\mathbf{p}^{\star}
, the information matrix, every leverage score, and the full facial Hessian block are all
within absolute constant factors of their values at
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
. Combined with the lower bound

f\geq f^{\star}+\gamma_{\mathrm{sc}}\cdot(\text{inactive mass})+\zeta(\text{Dikin radius})
(
Lemma

4.11
), a single
explicit constant
c_{0}
(
Definition

4.13
) guarantees that the whole sublevel set

\{f\leq f^{\star}+c_{0}\}
on the face’s affine hull sits strictly inside the simplex, keeps all non-contact
leverage scores below
d-\gamma_{\mathrm{sc}}/2
, and carries uniform smoothness/strong-convexity constants

4\widehat{L}_{\star}
and
\tfrac{4}{9}\mu_{\star}
. Every compactness argument in our earlier reasoning is thereby replaced
by explicit constants, which is also what makes the domination bound
\kappa=O(d^{4})\kappa_{\Phi}

(
Theorem

5.8
) clean.

Identification, then the accuracy phases (
Sections

5
and
6
).

This is the identification cost and the two accuracy phases. To
reach
the face we use the classical
away-step Frank–Wolfe: it drives the gap below
c_{0}
in

O(d^{2}/c_{0})

\varepsilon
-independent steps (

2.8
) and identifies the active set under strict
complementarity (
Theorem

2.10
); a detectable stopping rule (
Lemma

5.3
) certifies the
handoff. This is the whole of
C(\mathbf{A})
:
\varepsilon
-independent, but condition-dependent. On the face, we give
two interchangeable accuracy phases, both faster in
\varepsilon

2\lambda_{j+1}\leq(2\lambda_{j})^{2}

\big(\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{j}\big)^{2}=\big(v_{i}(\mathbf{p})-v_{i}(\mathbf{p}+t\mathbf{e}_{j})\big)\cdot\frac{1+t\,v_{j}(\mathbf{p})}{t}\qquad(t>0),

Design terminology and nondegeneracy.

The design polytope and its strata.

The simplex

Lemma 3.1

(Averaging destroys linear rates)
.

Let
\{\mathbf{p}^{(t)}\}_{t\geq 1}\subset\Delta_{n}
converge to
\mathbf{p}^{\star}

R-linearly
: there exist

\rho\in(0,1)
and
C_{0}<\infty
with
\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star}\right\rVert\leq C_{0}\rho^{t}
for all
t
. Then

\mathbf{C}:=\sum_{t\geq 1}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star})
converges absolutely, and the uniform running average

\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{(T)}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{p}^{(t)}
satisfies
\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{(T)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star}=\frac{1}{T}\mathbf{C}+o(1/T)
. In
particular, if
\mathbf{C}\neq 0
then
\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{(T)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star}\right\rVert=\Theta(1/T)
.

Proof.

Absolute convergence is immediate:
\sum_{t\geq 1}\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star}\right\rVert\leq C_{0}\sum_{t\geq 1}\rho^{t}=\frac{C_{0}\rho}{1-\rho}<\infty
, so
\mathbf{C}
is well defined. Write the partial sum as

\mathbf{S}_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star})=\mathbf{C}-\mathbf{R}_{T}
with tail
\mathbf{R}_{T}:=\sum_{t>T}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star})
, and
bound

\left\lVert\mathbf{R}_{T}\right\rVert\leq\sum_{t>T}\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star}\right\rVert\leq C_{0}\sum_{t>T}\rho^{t}=\frac{C_{0}\,\rho^{T+1}}{1-\rho}\to 0.

Since
\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{(T)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star}=\frac{1}{T}\mathbf{S}_{T}=\frac{1}{T}\mathbf{C}-\frac{1}{T}\mathbf{R}_{T}
and
\frac{1}{T}\left\lVert\mathbf{R}_{T}\right\rVert=O(\rho^{T}/T)=o(1/T)
, we conclude
\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{(T)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star}=\frac{1}{T}\mathbf{C}+o(1/T)
; if
\mathbf{C}\neq 0
this is
\Theta(1/T)

(lower bound:
\left\lVert\frac{1}{T}\mathbf{C}+o(1/T)\right\rVert\geq\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{C}\right\rVert}{T}-o(1/T)
).
∎

Lemma 3.2

(The gap is linear on the feasible cone)
.

Let
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
be nondegenerate with contact set
S^{\star}
(
2.3
), and let

K=\{\bm{\delta}:\sum_{i}\delta_{i}=0,\ \delta_{j}\geq 0\ \forall j\notin S^{\star}\}
be the feasible tangent cone.
Then for feasible
\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{p}^{\star}+\bm{\delta}
with
\left\lVert\bm{\delta}\right\rVert
small,

g(\mathbf{p})=F(\bm{\delta})+O(\left\lVert\bm{\delta}\right\rVert^{2}),\qquad F(\bm{\delta}):=\max_{i\in S^{\star}}\left\langle\nabla v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star}),\bm{\delta}\right\rangle,

and
F(\bm{\delta})\geq 0
for all
\bm{\delta}\in K
.

Proof.

Each
v_{i}
is smooth (
C^{\infty}
, indeed rational) on the open set
\{\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})\succ 0\}\ni\mathbf{p}^{\star}
, so
Taylor expansion at
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
gives, on a fixed ball around
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
and uniformly over the finitely

v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star}+\bm{\delta})=v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})+\left\langle\nabla v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star}),\bm{\delta}\right\rangle+O(\left\lVert\bm{\delta}\right\rVert^{2}).

Let
\Lambda:=\max_{i}\left\lVert\nabla v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})\right\rVert
.

(i) The maximum is attained in
S^{\star}
.
For
i\in S^{\star}
,
v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})=d
, so

v_{i}(\mathbf{p})-d=\left\langle\nabla v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star}),\bm{\delta}\right\rangle+O(\left\lVert\bm{\delta}\right\rVert^{2})
. For
i\notin S^{\star}
,

v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})\leq d-\gamma_{\mathrm{sc}}
(
2.3
), so
v_{i}(\mathbf{p})\leq d-\gamma_{\mathrm{sc}}+\Lambda\left\lVert\bm{\delta}\right\rVert+O(\left\lVert\bm{\delta}\right\rVert^{2})<d
once
\left\lVert\bm{\delta}\right\rVert
is small. Since
\max_{i}v_{i}(\mathbf{p})\geq d
always
(
Proposition

2.2
(b)
), the overall maximum is therefore attained within
S^{\star}
, and

g(\mathbf{p})=\max_{i}v_{i}(\mathbf{p})-d=\max_{i\in S^{\star}}\big(v_{i}(\mathbf{p})-d\big)=\max_{i\in S^{\star}}\left\langle\nabla v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star}),\bm{\delta}\right\rangle+O(\left\lVert\bm{\delta}\right\rVert^{2})=F(\bm{\delta})+O(\left\lVert\bm{\delta}\right\rVert^{2}),

the
O(\left\lVert\bm{\delta}\right\rVert^{2})
surviving the (finite) maximum because each branch has a uniform remainder.

(ii)
F\geq 0
on
K
.
For
\bm{\delta}\in K
and small
t>0
, the point
\mathbf{p}^{\star}+t\bm{\delta}
is feasible:

\sum_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star}+t\bm{\delta})_{i}=1
, and on coordinates
j\notin S^{\star}
(where
p^{\star}_{j}=0
by strict
complementarity)
(\mathbf{p}^{\star}+t\bm{\delta})_{j}=t\delta_{j}\geq 0
, while on
j\in S^{\star}
the coordinate

p^{\star}_{j}+t\delta_{j}
stays positive for small
t
. Hence
g(\mathbf{p}^{\star}+t\bm{\delta})\geq 0

(
Proposition

2.2
(b)
); by part (i),
g(\mathbf{p}^{\star}+t\bm{\delta})=tF(\bm{\delta})+O(t^{2})
, so dividing by

t
and letting
t\downarrow 0
gives
F(\bm{\delta})\geq 0
.
∎

Lemma 3.3

(The linearized gap is strictly positive on the feasible cone of
\mathbf{A}_{\star}
)
.

For
\mathbf{A}_{\star}
, with
K=\{\bm{\delta}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}:\sum_{i}\delta_{i}=0,\ \delta_{2}\geq 0\}
:

F(\bm{\delta})=\max_{i\in\{1,3,4\}}\big(-(\mathbf{B}\bm{\delta})_{i}\big)>0
for every
\bm{\delta}\in K\setminus\{0\}
.
Consequently
\gamma_{\min}:=\min\{F(\bm{\delta}):\bm{\delta}\in K,\left\lVert\bm{\delta}\right\rVert_{2}=1\}>0
.

Proof.

Suppose
\bm{\delta}\in K
has

(\mathbf{B}\bm{\delta})_{1}\geq 0
,
(\mathbf{B}\bm{\delta})_{3}\geq 0
,

(\mathbf{B}\bm{\delta})_{4}\geq 0

\delta_{2}=0

\delta_{1}+\delta_{3}+\delta_{4}=0

Theorem 3.4

(Averaging barrier)
.

For the explicit instance
\mathbf{A}_{\star}
(with
n=4
,
d=2
, unique nondegenerate
optimum,
\kappa=O(1)
), the
uniform
running average of the CCLY iterates started at

\mathbf{p}^{(1)}=\tfrac{1}{4}\mathbf{1}
satisfies

g(\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{(T)})=\Theta(1/T)
; hence any uniform-averaging certificate uses
\Omega(\varepsilon^{-1})
leverage
queries on
\mathbf{A}_{\star}
.

Proof.

The upper bound
g(\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{(T)})=O(1/T)
is
Theorem

2.6
. The lower bound has four steps.

Step 1: the iterates converge to
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
.
By
Remark

2.7
the CCLY iteration is
the map
\Phi(\mathbf{p})=(p_{i}v_{i}(\mathbf{p})/d)_{i}
, the classical multiplicative algorithm; from the positive start

\tfrac{1}{4}\mathbf{1}
all iterates remain strictly positive (each
v_{i}(\mathbf{p})>0
when
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})\succ 0
), and

f(\mathbf{p}^{(t)})
decreases monotonically to
f^{\star}

[
71
,
80
]
. Let
\mathbf{q}
be any
accumulation point of
\{\mathbf{p}^{(t)}\}\subset\Delta_{4}
(compact). Since
f
is lower semicontinuous on

\Delta_{4}
(it is continuous where
\mathbf{M}\succ 0
and
=+\infty
where
\det\mathbf{M}=0
, and

\det\mathbf{M}(\cdot)
is continuous),
f(\mathbf{q})\leq\lim_{t}f(\mathbf{p}^{(t)})=f^{\star}
, so
\mathbf{q}
is optimal. On

\mathbf{A}_{\star}
the optimal design is unique (
Lemma

4.2
; nondegeneracy is verified in closed form in

Appendix

E
), so
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{p}^{\star}
: the bounded sequence has a unique accumulation point, hence

\mathbf{p}^{(t)}\to\mathbf{p}^{\star}
.

Step 2: the convergence is R-linear.

\Phi
is a rational map, defined and
C^{\infty}
on the
open neighborhood
\{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}:\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})\succ 0\}
of
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
, with
\Phi(\mathbf{p}^{\star})=\mathbf{p}^{\star}
(as

v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})=d
on
\supp\mathbf{p}^{\star}
). Its Jacobian at
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
,

D\Phi(\mathbf{p}^{\star})_{ij}=\frac{\delta_{ij}\,v_{j}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})}{d}-\frac{p^{\star}_{i}\,B_{ij}}{d},

is computed exactly in
Appendix

E
: its full spectrum is

\{0,\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{3}{4}\}
, so its spectral radius is
\tfrac{3}{4}<1
. By Ostrowski’s
theorem
[
63
, 10.1.3–10.1.4]
,
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
is a point of attraction and there are a
neighborhood
U\ni\mathbf{p}^{\star}
and, for any
\rho^{\prime}\in(\tfrac{3}{4},1)
, a constant
c_{\rho^{\prime}}
such that
iterates entering
U
satisfy
\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star}\right\rVert\leq c_{\rho^{\prime}}(\rho^{\prime})^{t}
thereafter. (As

D\Phi(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
is not normal, single-step error ratios need not be monotone; the conclusion is
R-linear convergence at any rate above the spectral radius, which is exactly the hypothesis of

Lemma

3.1
.) By Step 1
the sequence enters
U
at some finite time
T_{0}
; absorbing the first
T_{0}
terms into the constant,

\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star}\right\rVert\leq C_{0}(\rho^{\prime})^{t}
for all
t\geq 1
: the convergence is R-linear.

Step 3: the summed displacement
\mathbf{C}
is a nonzero element of
K
.

Lemma

3.1
applies and gives

\bm{\delta}_{T}:=\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{(T)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star}=\tfrac{1}{T}\mathbf{C}+\mathbf{e}_{T}
with
\left\lVert\mathbf{e}_{T}\right\rVert=o(1/T)
, where

\mathbf{C}=\sum_{t\geq 1}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}^{\star})
. Each summand has zero coordinate sum, so
\sum_{i}C_{i}=0
; and the
second coordinate satisfies
C_{2}=\sum_{t\geq 1}p^{(t)}_{2}>0
, because
p^{\star}_{2}=0
while every iterate
keeps
p^{(t)}_{2}>0
(Step 1). Hence
\mathbf{C}\in K\setminus\{0\}
, and
Lemma

3.3
gives

\gamma:=F(\mathbf{C})>0
.

Step 4: assemble.
The map

F(\bm{\delta})=\max_{i\in S^{\star}}\left\langle\nabla v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star}),\bm{\delta}\right\rangle
is positively homogeneous and

\Lambda
-Lipschitz (a maximum of finitely many linear functionals of norm
\leq\Lambda=\max_{i}\left\lVert\nabla v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})\right\rVert
), so

F(\bm{\delta}_{T})=F\!\big(\tfrac{1}{T}\mathbf{C}\big)+\big(F(\bm{\delta}_{T})-F(\tfrac{1}{T}\mathbf{C})\big)=\tfrac{1}{T}F(\mathbf{C})\ \pm\ \Lambda\left\lVert\mathbf{e}_{T}\right\rVert=\tfrac{\gamma}{T}+o(1/T).

Moreover
\bm{\delta}_{T}\in K
for every
T
(its inactive coordinate
\bar{p}^{(T)}_{2}\geq 0
and

\sum_{i}(\bm{\delta}_{T})_{i}=0
) and
\left\lVert\bm{\delta}_{T}\right\rVert=\Theta(1/T)\to 0
, so
Lemma

3.2
applies:

g(\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{(T)})=F(\bm{\delta}_{T})+O(\left\lVert\bm{\delta}_{T}\right\rVert^{2})=\tfrac{\gamma}{T}+o(1/T)+O(1/T^{2})=\tfrac{\gamma}{T}+o(1/T)=\Theta(1/T).

Therefore
g(\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{(T)})\leq\varepsilon d
forces
T\geq\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon d}(1-o(1))=\Omega(1/\varepsilon)
.
∎

∎

Definition 4.4

(Facial condition number at the optimum)
.

Let

\mu_{\star}:=\lambda_{\min}\Big(\widehat{\mathbf{H}}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})\big|_{T^{\star}}\Big)>0,\qquad\widehat{L}_{\star}:=\lambda_{\max}\big(\widehat{\mathbf{H}}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})\big)\geq\mu_{\star},\qquad\kappa:=\widehat{L}_{\star}/\mu_{\star}.

and hence the Frank–Wolfe gap. Taking the full block on top

Lemma 4.5

(The facial problem is unconstrained, closed, self-concordant)
.

Under
2.3
:

(i)

D
is open in
V
and convex;

is

and strictly convex on

; and

is closed

).

(ii)

\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{p}^{\star}),\bm{\delta}\right\rangle=0
for every
\bm{\delta}\in T^{\star}
; consequently
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
is
the unique minimizer of

over all of

—the simplex constraints are inactive for the facial

(iii)

For every
\mathbf{p}\in D
:
h(\mathbf{p})\;\geq\;f^{\star}+\zeta\big(\varrho(\mathbf{p})\big)
.

Proof.

(i)

D
is the preimage of the open convex cone
\mathbb{S}^{d}_{\succ 0}
under the affine map

\mathbf{p}\mapsto\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})
restricted to
V
, hence open in
V
and convex;

is a composition of

maps there. Strict convexity:

makes

on

throughout

. Closedness: let
\mathbf{p}_{k}\in\{h\leq c\}

\bar{\mathbf{p}}\in V
(

is used only through the explicit inequalities of

, so we do not invoke the abstract definition.)

(ii)
By the definition of the contact set,

for every

.
Hence, by

(a)
, the restriction of

to the coordinates

\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{p}^{\star}),\bm{\delta}\right\rangle=-d\sum_{i\in S^{\star}}\delta_{i}=0
. Since

and its directional derivatives at

\mathbf{p}^{\star}
is a global

(iii)
Apply the matrix-space lower bound of

(a)

4.3
The self-concordance toolkit

The next four lemmas are proved in
Section

D.2
. Throughout,
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}\succ 0
are in
\mathbb{S}^{d}

and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathbf{X}}
is the local norm above.

Lemma 4.6

(Spectral sandwich)
.

Let
\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
and
r:=\left\lVert\mathbf{H}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{X}}
. Then
-r\mathbf{X}\preceq\mathbf{H}\preceq r\mathbf{X}
; in particular

(1-r)\mathbf{X}\preceq\mathbf{X}+\mathbf{H}\preceq(1+r)\mathbf{X}
, and
\mathbf{X}+\mathbf{H}\succ 0
whenever
r<1
.

Lemma 4.7

(Local-norm comparison)
.

If
(1-r)\mathbf{X}\preceq\mathbf{Y}\preceq(1+r)\mathbf{X}
for some
r\in[0,1)
, then for every
\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
,

(1+r)^{-2}\,\left\lVert\mathbf{H}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{X}}^{2}\;\leq\;\left\lVert\mathbf{H}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{Y}}^{2}\;\leq\;(1-r)^{-2}\,\left\lVert\mathbf{H}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{X}}^{2}.

The left inequality requires only
\mathbf{Y}\preceq(1+r)\mathbf{X}
(any
r\geq 0
); the right only

\mathbf{Y}\succeq(1-r)\mathbf{X}
.

Lemma 4.8

(Integrated self-concordance bounds for
-\log\det
)
.

Let
F(\cdot)=-\log\det(\cdot)
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}_{\succ 0}
, with gradient
\nabla F(\mathbf{X})=-\mathbf{X}^{-1}
in the
trace inner product, and let
r:=\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{X}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{X}}
.

(a)

F(\mathbf{Y})\ \geq\ F(\mathbf{X})+\left\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{X}),\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{X}\right\rangle+\zeta(r)
(no restriction on
r
);

(b)

if
r<1
:
F(\mathbf{Y})\ \leq\ F(\mathbf{X})+\left\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{X}),\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{X}\right\rangle+\zeta_{*}(r)
.

Lemma 4.9

(Leverage comparison)
.

If
(1-r)\mathbf{X}\preceq\mathbf{Y}\preceq(1+r)\mathbf{X}
with
r\in[0,1)
, then for every
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(1+r)^{-1}\,\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{X}^{-1}\mathbf{a}\;\leq\;\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}^{-1}\mathbf{a}\;\leq\;(1-r)^{-1}\,\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{X}^{-1}\mathbf{a}.

Lemma 4.10

(Properties of
\zeta,\zeta_{*}
)
.

\zeta(t)=t-\log(1+t)
and
\zeta_{*}(t)=-t-\log(1-t)
are increasing, strictly convex, and vanish to second
order at
0
; they are Legendre conjugates:
\zeta_{*}(t)=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{ts-\zeta(s)\}
for
t\in[0,1)
, with
maximizer
s=t/(1-t)
. Moreover
\zeta_{*}(t)\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2(1-t)}\leq\tfrac{2}{3}t^{2}
for
t\in[0,\tfrac{1}{4}]
,
and
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{4})=\tfrac{1}{4}-\log\tfrac{5}{4}>\tfrac{1}{38}
.

4.4
The strict-complementarity penalty and localization

Lemma 4.11

(Lower bound with penalty: strict complementarity
+
curvature)
.

For every
\mathbf{p}\in\Delta_{n}
(if
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})
is singular both sides are interpreted as
f(\mathbf{p})=+\infty
):

f(\mathbf{p})\;\geq\;f^{\star}\;+\;\gamma_{\mathrm{sc}}\sum_{i\notin S^{\star}}p_{i}\;+\;\zeta\big(\varrho(\mathbf{p})\big).

Proof.

Assume
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})\succ 0
. Apply
Lemma

4.8
(a)
with
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{M}^{\star}
,
\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})
:

f(\mathbf{p})\ \geq\ f^{\star}+\left\langle-(\mathbf{M}^{\star})^{-1},\,\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})-\mathbf{M}^{\star}\right\rangle+\zeta(\varrho(\mathbf{p})).

For the linear term, using
\operatorname{Tr}\big((\mathbf{M}^{\star})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\big)=v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
,

\operatorname{Tr}\big((\mathbf{M}^{\star})^{-1}\mathbf{M}^{\star}\big)=d

\left\langle-(\mathbf{M}^{\star})^{-1},\,\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})-\mathbf{M}^{\star}\right\rangle=d-\sum_{i}p_{i}\,v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})=\sum_{i\notin S^{\star}}\big(d-v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})\big)\,p_{i}\;\geq\;\gamma_{\mathrm{sc}}\sum_{i\notin S^{\star}}p_{i},

the middle equality because

\sum_{i}p_{i}v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})=d\big(1-\sum_{i\notin S^{\star}}p_{i}\big)+\sum_{i\notin S^{\star}}v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})p_{i}

and the final inequality from the definition of

(

).
∎

Remark 4.12

Remark 5.1

(Instance parameters: advised-complexity statements)
.

Like the away-step guarantees it builds on (
Theorems

2.9
and
2.10
),
Algorithm

1
consumes
instance constants: the tolerance

(a function of
\gamma_{\mathrm{sc}},p^{\star}_{\min},\mu_{\star}
;

Definition

4.13
) and the slack
\gamma_{\mathrm{sc}}
used in the stopping test, plus the local smoothness

as the step size of the accelerated phase (which moreover requires the stronger

of

5.5
). These affect only
when
the
algorithm hands off between phases, not the correctness of its output: the final certificate

is checked directly from the oracle, so an invalid guess can delay
termination but never produce an incorrect output. Accordingly, all complexity theorems in this
paper are

(instance-constant) statements: the stated query counts presuppose valid
constants. We expect standard doubling/backtracking schemes to remove the advice at logarithmic
overhead (one must verify that wrong guesses neither leave the domain
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{w})\succ 0
nor make a
premature handoff unrecoverable, both of which the direct certificate renders detectable), but we do
not carry out this adaptive analysis here.

The step sizes in
Algorithm

1
are the
exact
line-search minimizers of
f
, including the
boundary (drop) case of the away step; this is what licenses importing the away-step Frank–Wolfe
guarantees in
Lemma

5.3
below. We record the closed forms, with the case distinction that
the away step requires. (Omitting the
v_{j_{-}}\leq 1
case, as an earlier version of this paper
did, would prescribe a
negative
step there: e.g. for
\mathbf{a}_{1}=(1,0)
,
\mathbf{a}_{2}=(0,1)
,

\mathbf{a}_{3}=(\sqrt{0.1},0)
at
\mathbf{p}=(\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{1}{3})

with

, and the interior formula evaluates to a negative number, while the true
line-search optimum is the drop step.)

Lemma 5.2

(Closed-form exact line search)
.

Let
\mathbf{p}\in\Delta_{n}
with
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})\succ 0
and
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{p})
.

(a)

(Toward.)
If
v_{j}>d
, then
\gamma\mapsto f\big((1-\gamma)\mathbf{p}+\gamma\mathbf{e}_{j}\big)
is
strictly convex on
[0,1)
and is minimized at

\gamma^{\star}=\frac{v_{j}-d}{d\,(v_{j}-1)}\in(0,1)
.

(b)

(Away/drop.)
If
j\in\supp(\mathbf{p})
with
p_{j}<1
and
v_{j}<d
, then with

\gamma_{\max}:=\frac{p_{j}}{1-p_{j}}
the function

\gamma\mapsto f\big((1+\gamma)\mathbf{p}-\gamma\mathbf{e}_{j}\big)
is strictly convex on
[0,\gamma_{\max}]

and is minimized at

\gamma^{\star}=\begin{cases}\min\Big\{\dfrac{d-v_{j}}{d\,(v_{j}-1)},\ \gamma_{\max}\Big\},&v_{j}>1,\\[8.0pt]
\gamma_{\max}\quad(\text{a drop step: the new weight of atom }j\text{ is }0),&v_{j}\leq 1.\end{cases}

In both cases
\mathbf{M}\succ 0
along the whole segment traversed.

Proof.

Write
\mathbf{M}_{\gamma}
for the information matrix of the moved point and
\varphi(\gamma):=f
along the
line.
(a)

\mathbf{M}_{\gamma}=(1-\gamma)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})+\gamma\,\mathbf{a}_{j}\mathbf{a}_{j}^{\top}
, and the matrix determinant
lemma gives

\det\mathbf{M}_{\gamma}=\det\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})\,(1-\gamma)^{d-1}\big(1+\gamma(v_{j}-1)\big)
,
which is positive for
\gamma\in[0,1)
(as
v_{j}>d\geq 1
), so
\mathbf{M}_{\gamma}\succ 0
there (its
eigenvalues, continuous in
\gamma
and initially positive, cannot vanish while the determinant is
positive). Hence

\varphi^{\prime}(\gamma)=\frac{d-1}{1-\gamma}-\frac{v_{j}-1}{1+\gamma(v_{j}-1)},\qquad\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma)=\frac{d-1}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}+\frac{(v_{j}-1)^{2}}{\big(1+\gamma(v_{j}-1)\big)^{2}}>0.

\varphi
is strictly convex with
\varphi^{\prime}(0)=d-v_{j}<0
, and solving
\varphi^{\prime}(\gamma)=0
gives

\gamma^{\star}=\frac{v_{j}-d}{d(v_{j}-1)}
, which lies in
(0,1)
since
v_{j}>d
implies

v_{j}-d<d(v_{j}-1)
.

(b)
Now
\mathbf{M}_{\gamma}=(1+\gamma)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})-\gamma\,\mathbf{a}_{j}\mathbf{a}_{j}^{\top}
and

\det\mathbf{M}_{\gamma}=\det\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})\,(1+\gamma)^{d-1}\big(1+\gamma(1-v_{j})\big)
, so

\varphi^{\prime}(\gamma)=-\frac{d-1}{1+\gamma}-\frac{1-v_{j}}{1+\gamma(1-v_{j})},\qquad\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\gamma)=\frac{d-1}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}+\frac{(1-v_{j})^{2}}{\big(1+\gamma(1-v_{j})\big)^{2}}>0.

Case
v_{j}\leq 1
:
both terms of
-\varphi^{\prime}
are nonnegative and the first is positive, so

\varphi^{\prime}<0
on all of
[0,\gamma_{\max}]
(where
1+\gamma(1-v_{j})\geq 1>0
, so
\det\mathbf{M}_{\gamma}>0

and
\mathbf{M}_{\gamma}\succ 0
as above): the minimizer is the right endpoint
\gamma_{\max}
, at which the
weight of atom
j
is
(1+\gamma_{\max})p_{j}-\gamma_{\max}=0
.

Case
v_{j}>1
:
solving
\varphi^{\prime}(\gamma)=0
gives the stationary point

\gamma^{\star}_{\mathrm{int}}=\frac{d-v_{j}}{d(v_{j}-1)}>0
(positive since
1<v_{j}<d
). The determinant
factor
1+\gamma(1-v_{j})
vanishes only at
\gamma_{\mathrm{sing}}=\frac{1}{v_{j}-1}
, and

\gamma^{\star}_{\mathrm{int}}<\gamma_{\mathrm{sing}}
always (equivalent to
v_{j}>0
), so on

[0,\min\{\gamma_{\max},\gamma_{\mathrm{sing}}\})
the function
\varphi
is strictly convex with

\varphi^{\prime}(0)=v_{j}-d<0
and
\varphi\to+\infty
at
\gamma_{\mathrm{sing}}
if

\gamma_{\mathrm{sing}}\leq\gamma_{\max}
; in either configuration the constrained minimizer is

\min\{\gamma^{\star}_{\mathrm{int}},\gamma_{\max}\}
, and the segment up to it keeps

\det\mathbf{M}_{\gamma}>0
, hence
\mathbf{M}_{\gamma}\succ 0
.
∎

6.1
Exact Hessian recovery from leverage-score queries

The recovery rests on the standard rank-one inverse-update identity, restated here for
self-containedness.

Fact 6.1

(Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury identity, rank-one case; see, e.g.,
[
73
, §3.1, App. A.3]
)
.

For
\mathbf{N}\succ 0
,
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

\big(\mathbf{N}+t\,\mathbf{a}\mathbf{a}^{\top}\big)^{-1}=\mathbf{N}^{-1}-\frac{t\,\mathbf{N}^{-1}\mathbf{a}\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{N}^{-1}}{1+t\,\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{N}^{-1}\mathbf{a}}.

Proposition 6.2

(Rank-one probes recover the facial Hessian exactly)
.

Let
\mathbf{w}
be any weights with
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{w})\succ 0
, let

and

. Then

\big(\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{w})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{j}\big)^{2}=\Big(v_{i}(\mathbf{w})-v_{i}(\mathbf{w}+t\mathbf{e}_{j})\Big)\cdot\frac{1+t\,v_{j}(\mathbf{w})}{t}\qquad\text{for every }i\in[n].

(6.1)

Consequently, for a design
\mathbf{p}\in\Delta_{n}
the facial Hessian block

\widehat{\mathbf{H}}(\mathbf{p})=\big((\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{j})^{2}\big)_{i,j\in S^{\star}}
is computed

exactly
from the

leverage-score queries at the designs

\mathbf{p}\quad\text{and}\quad\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{e}_{j}),\ j\in S^{\star},

via (
6.1
) with
t=1
and the homogeneity
v_{i}(\mathbf{w}/2)=2v_{i}(\mathbf{w})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{w}+t\mathbf{e}_{j})=\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{w})+t\,\mathbf{a}_{j}\mathbf{a}_{j}^{\top}\succ 0
,
6.1
(with
\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{w})
and
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{a}_{j}

\big(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{w})+t\,\mathbf{a}_{j}\mathbf{a}_{j}^{\top}\big)^{-1}=\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{w})^{-1}-\frac{t\,\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{w})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{j}\mathbf{a}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{w})^{-1}}{1+t\,\mathbf{a}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{w})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{j}}.

Sandwiching by
\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}(\cdot)\mathbf{a}_{i}
yields

v_{i}(\mathbf{w}+t\mathbf{e}_{j})=v_{i}(\mathbf{w})-t(\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{w})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{j})^{2}/(1+tv_{j}(\mathbf{w}))

(
6.1
). For the second claim:

is a convex combination of two

so the query at

for all

)
with

delivers the column

\widehat{\mathbf{H}}(\mathbf{p})
(its entries for all

Theorem 6.4

(Quadratic contraction of the Newton decrement;

[

Let

be standard self-concordant with nondegenerate Hessian on an open convex domain, and let

be a point with
\lambda(\mathbf{p})<1
. Then the full Newton iterate
\mathbf{p}^{+}=\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{n}_{\mathbf{p}}

\lambda(\mathbf{p}^{+})\;\leq\;\Big(\frac{\lambda(\mathbf{p})}{1-\lambda(\mathbf{p})}\Big)^{2}.

We apply

Lemma 6.5

(Damped decrease; suboptimality from the decrement)
.

Let
\mathbf{p}\in D
with decrement
\lambda=\lambda(\mathbf{p})
.

(a)

(Damped step.)

\mathbf{p}^{+}:=\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{n}_{\mathbf{p}}/(1+\lambda)\in D
and

(b)

(Full step for small decrement.)
If

\mathbf{p}^{+}:=\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{n}_{\mathbf{p}}\in D
and
h(\mathbf{p}^{+})\leq h(\mathbf{p})-\tfrac{1}{3}\lambda^{2}\leq h(\mathbf{p})

(c)

(Suboptimality.)
If

when

\lambda\leq\tfrac{1}{4}
.

Proof.

Write
\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{n}_{\mathbf{p}}
, so
\left\lVert\mathbf{n}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{p}}=\lambda
and

\left\langle\nabla h(\mathbf{p}),\mathbf{n}\right\rangle=-\lambda^{2}
.

(a)
The step
\bm{\delta}=\mathbf{n}/(1+\lambda)
has
\left\lVert\bm{\delta}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{p}}=\lambda/(1+\lambda)<1
,
so

4.6
(at base
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})
, with

\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p}^{+})-\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})=\sum_{i}\delta_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}
,

) gives

\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p}^{+})\succeq(1-r)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})\succ 0
:
\mathbf{p}^{+}\in D
. By the upper integrated bound
(
Lemma

4.8
(b)
, at base
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})
,
\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p}^{+})
, pulled back through
the affine map as in

4.5
),

h(\mathbf{p}^{+})\ \leq\ h(\mathbf{p})+\Big\langle\nabla h(\mathbf{p}),\tfrac{\mathbf{n}}{1+\lambda}\Big\rangle+\zeta_{*}\Big(\tfrac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\Big)=h(\mathbf{p})-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{1+\lambda}-\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}+\log(1+\lambda)=h(\mathbf{p})-\zeta(\lambda),

where we evaluated

\zeta_{*}\big(\tfrac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\big)=-\tfrac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}-\log\big(1-\tfrac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\big)=-\tfrac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}+\log(1+\lambda)
and combined

\tfrac{\lambda^{2}+\lambda}{1+\lambda}=\lambda
.

(b)
Now
\bm{\delta}=\mathbf{n}

\left\lVert\bm{\delta}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{p}}=\lambda\leq\tfrac{1}{4}<1
, so

h(\mathbf{p}^{+})\leq h(\mathbf{p})-\lambda^{2}+\zeta_{*}(\lambda)\leq h(\mathbf{p})-\lambda^{2}+\tfrac{2}{3}\lambda^{2}=h(\mathbf{p})-\tfrac{1}{3}\lambda^{2},

Lemma

).

(c)
For any

(

4.8

h(\mathbf{q})\geq h(\mathbf{p})+\left\langle\nabla h(\mathbf{p}),\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{p}\right\rangle+\zeta(\left\lVert\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{p}})
. By the dual-norm
characterization of the decrement,

\left\langle\nabla h(\mathbf{p}),\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{p}\right\rangle\geq-\lambda\left\lVert\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{p}}

h(\mathbf{q})\;\geq\;h(\mathbf{p})+\inf_{r\geq 0}\big\{\zeta(r)-\lambda r\big\}\;=\;h(\mathbf{p})-\sup_{r\geq 0}\big\{\lambda r-\zeta(r)\big\}\;=\;h(\mathbf{p})-\zeta_{*}(\lambda),

4.10

(the minimizer,
Lemma

4.5

4.10

Where the problem now stands.

The whole paper is one statement: the leverage-score complexity of the John ellipsoid separates into

certification
,
identification

Certification
by uniform averaging is an obstruction, costing

(

but an avoidable one—it is the price of the averaged certificate, not of the oracle.

with no condition number multiplying the
\varepsilon

Open Problem 7.1

(Condition-free identification)
.

Is there a leverage-score algorithm that, on every instance with a nondegenerate optimal design,
identifies the contact set
S^{\star}
(equivalently: reaches the facial sublevel set
\mathcal{C}_{0}
) in

\poly(d)\cdot\polylog(1/\gamma_{\mathrm{sc}},1/p^{\star}_{\min},\kappa)

\poly(d)
queries with no

Two structural comments on

Open Problem 7.2

(Degeneracy)
.

The upper bounds assume strict complementarity and independent contact matrices
(
2.3

does a
\log\log(1/\varepsilon)

optimal design is non-unique and the facial problem has a nontrivial solution set), with Newton

Remark

2.4

Summary.

The historical
\varepsilon^{-1}

pointed at the last iterate, is geometric; and once the optimal face is known, the problem is, in the
affine-invariant sense that Newton’s method makes precise,
easy

doubly logarithmic number of full Newton steps, all driven by leverage scores. The open problem is no
longer the accuracy; it is the geometry of finding the face.

Appendix C
Formulation: the John ellipsoid and
D
/
G
-optimal design

This appendix collects the classical equivalences that turn the geometric John-ellipsoid problem into the
convex program
\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\Delta_{n}}f(\mathbf{p})
of the body
[
73
,
65
,
40
,
19
,
23
]
.

The geometric problem.

For
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
of rank
d
, the centrally symmetric polytope
is
P=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1\}=\{\mathbf{x}:|\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{x}|\leq 1,\ i\in[n]\}
. Its
John ellipsoid

is the maximum-volume inscribed ellipsoid (
MVIE
); by central symmetry it is centred at the origin,
and John’s theorem gives the sandwiching
E\subseteq P\subseteq\sqrt{d}\,E
.

Ellipsoids as matrices.

A centred ellipsoid
E=\{\mathbf{x}:\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x}\leq 1\}
(
\mathbf{S}\succ 0
) has
volume
\propto(\det\mathbf{S})^{-1/2}
and support function
\max_{\mathbf{x}\in E}\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{x}=\sqrt{\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{S}^{-1}\mathbf{a}}
, so

E\subseteq P\iff\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{S}^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{i}\leq 1
for all
i
. Taking
\mathbf{S}=d\,\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})
turns this inscribed
condition into
v_{i}(\mathbf{p})\leq d
; by
Proposition

2.2
(b)
(
\max_{i}v_{i}\geq d
, equality iff
\mathbf{p}
is
optimal) it is met only at the optimum, where
Q=\{\mathbf{x}:\mathbf{x}^{\top}(d\,\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p}))\mathbf{x}\leq 1\}
is exactly
inscribed—the John ellipsoid. For an approximate
(1+\varepsilon)
-John design (
Definition

2.1
) one has instead

\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{S}^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{i}=v_{i}/d\leq 1+\varepsilon
, so the same
Q
is inscribed only after shrinking by
\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}
:
this is the left half of the sandwiching
\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}}Q\subseteq P\subseteq\sqrt{d}\,Q
.

Polar duality: MVIE
=
MVEE of the rows.

The polar of
P
is

P^{\circ}=\mathrm{conv}\{\pm\mathbf{a}_{i}:i\in[n]\}
, and polarity acts on ellipsoids by

\{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x}\leq 1\}^{\circ}=\{\mathbf{y}^{\top}\mathbf{S}^{-1}\mathbf{y}\leq 1\}
with the product
\mathrm{vol}(E)\,\mathrm{vol}(E^{\circ})
constant;
hence maximizing inscribed volume in
P
is the same as minimizing enclosing volume of
P^{\circ}
. The John
ellipsoid of
P
, the
minimum-volume enclosing ellipsoid
(MVEE) of the rows
\{\pm\mathbf{a}_{i}\}
(its
polar),
D
-optimal design, and
\ell_{\infty}
Lewis weights are thus
four facets of one optimal
weight
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
, related by polar duality and linear-time reductions
[
73
,
23
]
.

D
-optimal design.

The MVEE of
\{\pm\mathbf{a}_{i}\}
is
\{\mathbf{y}:\mathbf{y}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})^{-1}\mathbf{y}\leq d\}
, where

\mathbf{p}^{\star}\in\argmax_{\mathbf{p}\in\Delta_{n}}\log\det\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})=\argmin_{\mathbf{p}\in\Delta_{n}}f(\mathbf{p})

is the
D
-optimal design
, maximizing the determinant (“
D

\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})=\sum_{i}p_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}
. The containment
\mathbf{a}_{i}\in

v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})\leq d
, with equality on
the contact set
S^{\star}
; by
Proposition

2.2
(b)
this holds, among all
\mathbf{p}\in\Delta_{n}
, only at
the optimal designs (which all share the same
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})
;
Lemma

4.2
). This is the program the
body solves, and

Proposition

2.2
(a)
(
\nabla f=-\mathbf{v}
) is why a single leverage-score query is its first-order oracle.

G
-optimal design and Kiefer–Wolfowitz.

In design terms
v_{i}(\mathbf{p})
is the
prediction
variance
at
\mathbf{a}_{i}
, and the
G
-optimal design
minimizes the worst one,
\min_{\mathbf{p}}\max_{i}v_{i}(\mathbf{p})
.
The Kiefer–Wolfowitz equivalence (
Theorem

2.5
;
[
40
,
65
]

D
- and

G
-optimality coincide, with common optimum
\max_{i}v_{i}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})=d

(1+\varepsilon)
-John guarantee

\max_{i}v_{i}\leq(1+\varepsilon)d
(
Definition

2.1

(1+\varepsilon)
-approximate

-optimal certificate, equal

(c)

\ell_{\infty}
Lewis weights.

Finally,
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
is a rescaling of the

weights

\mathbf{w}=d\,\mathbf{p}^{\star}
of
\mathbf{A}

D.1
The dictionary (
Proposition

2.2
)

Proof of
Proposition

2.2
.

(a)
By
\partial_{\mathbf{X}}\log\det\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{X}^{-1}
and the chain rule, with
\partial_{p_{i}}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})=\mathbf{a}_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}
,

\partial_{p_{i}}f(\mathbf{p})=-\operatorname{Tr}\!\big(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\partial_{p_{i}}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})\big)=-\operatorname{Tr}\!\big(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\big)=-\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{i}=-v_{i}(\mathbf{p}).

(b)
Summing the trace identity against
\mathbf{p}
,

\sum_{i}p_{i}v_{i}(\mathbf{p})=\sum_{i}p_{i}\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top})=\operatorname{Tr}\!\Big(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\sum_{i}p_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\Big)=\operatorname{Tr}\!\big(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})\big)=\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{I}_{d})=d.

As
\max_{i}v_{i}(\mathbf{p})
is at least the
\mathbf{p}
-weighted average
d
, we get
\max_{i}v_{i}\geq d
; equality forces

v_{i}(\mathbf{p})=d
on
\supp(\mathbf{p})
, which is exactly the first-order condition
\nabla f(\mathbf{p})_{i}=-d
for

i\in\supp(\mathbf{p})
for
\min_{\Delta_{n}}f
, i.e. optimality.

(c)
A linear function on
\Delta_{n}
is maximized at a vertex
\mathbf{e}_{j}
, so by
(a)
,
(b)
,

g(\mathbf{p})=\max_{j}\left\langle-\nabla f(\mathbf{p}),\mathbf{e}_{j}-\mathbf{p}\right\rangle=\max_{j}\Big(v_{j}(\mathbf{p})-\sum_{i}p_{i}v_{i}(\mathbf{p})\Big)=\max_{j}v_{j}(\mathbf{p})-d.

Convexity of
f
gives

f(\mathbf{p})-f^{\star}\leq\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{p}),\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\star}\right\rangle=\left\langle-\nabla f(\mathbf{p}),\mathbf{p}^{\star}-\mathbf{p}\right\rangle\leq\max_{\mathbf{q}\in\Delta_{n}}\left\langle-\nabla f(\mathbf{p}),\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{p}\right\rangle=g(\mathbf{p}),

which is the bound
g(\mathbf{p})\geq f(\mathbf{p})-f^{\star}\geq 0
. For the refined upper bound, let
\mathbf{p}^{\star}
be optimal with

\mathbf{M}^{\star}:=\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p}^{\star})=\sum_{i}p^{\star}_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}
. Applying the AM–GM inequality for the eigenvalues of the
positive-definite matrix
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1/2}\mathbf{M}^{\star}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1/2}
(i.e.
\log\det\mathbf{X}\leq d\log(\tfrac{1}{d}\operatorname{Tr}\mathbf{X})
),

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{p})-f^{\star}

\displaystyle=\log\det\mathbf{M}^{\star}-\log\det\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})=\log\det\!\big(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1/2}\mathbf{M}^{\star}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1/2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq d\log\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{d}\operatorname{Tr}\!\big(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{M}^{\star}\big)\Big)=d\log\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{d}\textstyle\sum_{i}p^{\star}_{i}\,\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{i}\Big)=d\log\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{d}\textstyle\sum_{i}p^{\star}_{i}v_{i}(\mathbf{p})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq d\log\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{d}\max_{j}v_{j}(\mathbf{p})\Big)=d\log\!\big(1+g(\mathbf{p})/d\big),

using
\sum_{i}p^{\star}_{i}=1
and
\max_{j}v_{j}(\mathbf{p})=d+g(\mathbf{p})
; cf.
[
73
, §2]
,
[
19
]
.
Finally
d\log(1+g/d)\leq d\cdot(g/d)=g
.

(d)
By the matrix-inverse derivative

\partial_{p_{j}}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}=-\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\big(\partial_{p_{j}}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})\big)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}=-\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{j}\mathbf{a}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1},

\partial_{p_{j}}v_{i}(\mathbf{p})=\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\big(\partial_{p_{j}}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\big)\mathbf{a}_{i}=-\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{j}\mathbf{a}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{i}=-(\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{j})^{2},

so
\nabla^{2}f(\mathbf{p})_{ij}=-\partial_{p_{j}}v_{i}(\mathbf{p})=(\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{j})^{2}
. Write
\mathbf{b}_{i}:=\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1/2}\mathbf{a}_{i}
and
use
\left\langle\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}\right\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{Y})
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
: then
(\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{j})^{2}=(\mathbf{b}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{b}_{j})^{2}=\left\langle\mathbf{b}_{i}\mathbf{b}_{i}^{\top},\mathbf{b}_{j}\mathbf{b}_{j}^{\top}\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathbf{b}_{i}^{\otimes 2},\mathbf{b}_{j}^{\otimes 2}\right\rangle
, so
\nabla^{2}f(\mathbf{p})
is the Gram
matrix of the vectors
\{\mathbf{b}_{i}^{\otimes 2}\}_{i\in[n]}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
. A Gram matrix is positive
semidefinite, and its rank is
\dim\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{b}_{i}^{\otimes 2}\}\leq\dim\mathbb{S}^{d}=\binom{d+1}{2}
. For the
kernel and for (
2.1
), for any
\bm{\delta}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\bm{\delta}^{\top}\nabla^{2}f(\mathbf{p})\,\bm{\delta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\delta_{i}\delta_{j}\left\langle\mathbf{b}_{i}^{\otimes 2},\mathbf{b}_{j}^{\otimes 2}\right\rangle=\Big\langle\textstyle\sum_{i}\delta_{i}\mathbf{b}_{i}\mathbf{b}_{i}^{\top},\sum_{j}\delta_{j}\mathbf{b}_{j}\mathbf{b}_{j}^{\top}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\|\textstyle\sum_{i}\delta_{i}\mathbf{b}_{i}\mathbf{b}_{i}^{\top}\Big\|_{F}^{2}=\Big\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1/2}\big(\textstyle\sum_{i}\delta_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}\big)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1/2}\Big\|_{F}^{2},

which vanishes iff
\sum_{i}\delta_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\top}=0
(as
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})^{-1/2}
is invertible). This condition does
not involve
\mathbf{p}
, so
\ker\nabla^{2}f(\mathbf{p})
is the same subspace for every
\mathbf{p}
with
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{p})\succ 0
.

\mathbf{H}=0

Proof of
Lemma

.

Let
\mathbf{E}:=\mathbf{X}^{-1/2}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{X}^{-1/2}
, a symmetric matrix with

\left\lVert\mathbf{E}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{E}\right\rVert_{F}=r
(the operator norm of a symmetric matrix is its largest
absolute eigenvalue, while the Frobenius norm is the
\ell_{2}
norm of all eigenvalues). Hence

-r\mathbf{I}\preceq\mathbf{E}\preceq r\mathbf{I}
; conjugating by
\mathbf{X}^{1/2}
(congruence preserves the semidefinite
order) gives
-r\mathbf{X}\preceq\mathbf{H}\preceq r\mathbf{X}
. Adding
\mathbf{X}
yields the sandwich for
\mathbf{X}+\mathbf{H}
; if

the lower bound

gives
\mathbf{X}+\mathbf{H}\succ 0

∎

Proof of
Lemma

4.7
.

Write
\mathbf{G}:=\mathbf{X}^{-1/2}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{X}^{-1/2}
(so
\left\lVert\mathbf{H}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{X}}=\left\lVert\mathbf{G}\right\rVert_{F}
) and

\mathbf{S}:=\mathbf{Y}^{-1/2}\mathbf{X}^{1/2}
, so that

\mathbf{Y}^{-1/2}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Y}^{-1/2}=\mathbf{Y}^{-1/2}\mathbf{X}^{1/2}\,\mathbf{G}\,\mathbf{X}^{1/2}\mathbf{Y}^{-1/2}=\mathbf{S}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{S}^{\top}.

The singular values of
\mathbf{S}
satisfy

\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{S})^{2}=\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{1/2}\mathbf{Y}^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{1/2}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
(note
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\top}=\mathbf{Y}^{-1/2}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Y}^{-1/2}
and

\mathbf{X}^{1/2}\mathbf{Y}^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{1/2}
have the same spectrum, being similar via
\mathbf{X}^{1/2}\mathbf{Y}^{1/2}
-type
conjugation; concretely both equal the spectrum of
\mathbf{Y}^{-1}\mathbf{X}
). From
\mathbf{Y}\succeq(1-r)\mathbf{X}
,
inversion (order-reversing on the positive-definite cone, see the proof of
Lemma

4.9
) gives

\mathbf{Y}^{-1}\preceq(1-r)^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{-1}
, hence

\mathbf{X}^{1/2}\mathbf{Y}^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{1/2}\preceq(1-r)^{-1}\mathbf{I}
and
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{S})^{2}\leq(1-r)^{-1}
. Likewise

\mathbf{Y}\preceq(1+r)\mathbf{X}
gives
\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{S})^{2}\geq(1+r)^{-1}
. Finally, for any symmetric
\mathbf{G}
and
invertible
\mathbf{S}
,

\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{S})^{2}\,\left\lVert\mathbf{G}\right\rVert_{F}\ \leq\ \left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\right\rVert_{F}\ \leq\ \sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{S})^{2}\,\left\lVert\mathbf{G}\right\rVert_{F}:

the upper bound is
\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{S})\left\lVert\mathbf{G}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{S})^{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{G}\right\rVert_{F}
(multiplying by a matrix scales the Frobenius norm by at most the
operator norm); the lower bound follows by applying the upper one to

\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{S}^{-1}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{S}^{\top})\mathbf{S}^{-\top}
with the roles reversed, using

\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{S}^{-1})=1/\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{S})
. Squaring and combining the displayed bounds proves
the lemma; tracking which sandwich half was used in each direction proves the one-sidedness claims.
∎

Proof of

.

Let
\mathbf{H}:=\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{X}

It remains to bound the integrand. Since

\left\lVert\mathbf{H}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{Y}_{t}}^{2}\;\geq\;\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{H}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{X}}^{2}}{(1+tr)^{2}}=\frac{r^{2}}{(1+tr)^{2}}\qquad\text{always},\qquad\left\lVert\mathbf{H}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{Y}_{t}}^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{r^{2}}{(1-tr)^{2}}\qquad\text{if }tr<1.

(a)
follows from

\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\,\frac{r^{2}}{(1+tr)^{2}}\,dt=\zeta(r)

(

\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\frac{r^{2}}{(1+tr)^{2}}dt=\int_{1}^{1+r}\frac{r+1-u}{u^{2}}\,du=(r+1)\Big[\frac{-1}{u}\Big]_{1}^{1+r}-\big[\log u\big]_{1}^{1+r}=r-\log(1+r)=\zeta(r).

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\frac{r^{2}}{(1-tr)^{2}}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{1-r}^{1}\frac{r-1+u}{u^{2}}\,du=(r-1)\Big[\frac{-1}{u}\Big]_{1-r}^{1}+\big[\log u\big]_{1-r}^{1}

\displaystyle=(r-1)\cdot\frac{r}{1-r}-\log(1-r)=-r-\log(1-r)=\zeta_{*}(r),

the penultimate step using

Proof of

.

We first record that inversion reverses the order on

\mathbf{B}^{-1/2}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}^{-1/2}\succeq\mathbf{I}

Proof of
Lemma

4.10
.

Monotonicity and strict convexity:
\zeta^{\prime}(t)=1-\tfrac{1}{1+t}=\tfrac{t}{1+t}\geq 0
and

\zeta^{\prime\prime}(t)=(1+t)^{-2}>0
;
\zeta_{*}^{\prime}(t)=-1+\tfrac{1}{1-t}=\tfrac{t}{1-t}\geq 0
and

\zeta_{*}^{\prime\prime}(t)=(1-t)^{-2}>0
; both vanish with their first derivatives at
0
. Conjugacy: for fixed

t\in[0,1)
the concave function
s\mapsto ts-\zeta(s)
on
s\geq 0
has derivative

t-\tfrac{s}{1+s}
, which vanishes at
s^{\star}=\tfrac{t}{1-t}\geq 0
; since
ts-\zeta(s)\to-\infty
as

s\to\infty
(because
\zeta(s)=s-\log(1+s)
grows linearly with slope
1>t
),
s^{\star}
is the
maximizer, and

\sup_{s\geq 0}\{ts-\zeta(s)\}=\frac{t^{2}}{1-t}-\frac{t}{1-t}+\log\Big(1+\frac{t}{1-t}\Big)=-t-\log(1-t)=\zeta_{*}(t).

Quadratic bounds: from the power series
\zeta_{*}(t)=\sum_{k\geq 2}t^{k}/k
(valid for
|t|<1
) and

1/k\leq 1/2
,

\zeta_{*}(t)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k\geq 2}t^{k}=\tfrac{t^{2}}{2(1-t)}
, which at
t\leq\tfrac{1}{4}
is at most

\tfrac{t^{2}}{2\cdot 3/4}=\tfrac{2}{3}t^{2}
. For the lower bound on
\zeta
:
g(t):=\zeta(t)-t^{2}/4
has

g(0)=0
and
g^{\prime}(t)=\tfrac{t}{1+t}-\tfrac{t}{2}=\tfrac{t(1-t)}{2(1+t)}\geq 0
on
[0,1]
, so

\zeta(t)\geq t^{2}/4
there; consequently, for
c\leq\zeta(1)=1-\log 2
, the inverse satisfies

\zeta^{-1}(c)\leq 2\sqrt{c}
. Numerically
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{4})=\tfrac{1}{4}-\log\tfrac{5}{4}=0.02685\ldots>\tfrac{1}{38}

and
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2})=\tfrac{1}{2}-\log\tfrac{3}{2}=0.09453\ldots<0.0946
.
∎
