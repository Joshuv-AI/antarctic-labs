Introduction

1.1.
Motivation, organization of the paper and notation

Research on integration in Banach spaces began in early 20th century, based on theory of Lebesgue integral. A systematic and modern development started in the 1930s, when Salomon Bochner introduced the Bochner integral, providing a framework for integrating Banach space–valued functions, see
[
8
]
.
Shortly thereafter, in the late 1930s, B. J. Pettis developed the Pettis integral, which is based on weak measurability, and extended the theory to broader classes of functions, see
[
22
]
.
During the 1940s and 1950s, further contributions by mathematicians such as Dunford, Schwartz, and Gelfand firmly established integration in Banach spaces as a distinct topic in functional analysis, which has important applications to operator theory and probability theory. A standard reference for the general theory of integration in Banach spaces is a classical book
[
12
]
. For a short and clear introduction to this theory we refer reader to
[
1
, Sections 11.8, 11.9, 11.10]
.

Over the past two decades considerable attention has been given to integration of operator-valued (o. v.) functions, where integrability is defined by integrability of scalarizations (see Subsections
1.3
,
1.4
).
This line of research, where operators act on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, can be found in a pioneering paper
[
15
]
and later in
[
16
,
18
,
20
,
17
]
. This theory has applications, for instance, in the study of spectral and numerical radii of integrals of operator-valued functions; see
[
25
,
24
]
.
In this paper, we study functions taking values in the space
\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)
, where
X
and

are Banach spaces. Spaces of such o. v. functions were investigated in
[
4
,
6

]
. Recently, the study of integrable functions has been extended from operator-valued settings to functions with values in spaces of measures, see
[

.

In this paper we continue investigation, initiated in
[
6
]
, of a normed space

, which is wider than the space

It fits into a scale of normed spaces

[

Finiteness of the corresponding norm was built into the definition of these spaces, but this finiteness follows from the assumption of strong

It was proved in

Here we prove that both conditions of uniform integrability and finiteness of

In the last section we apply developed theory to the spectral radius inequality, see Theorem

We record here two results from Banach space theory that we need.
Let us recall that a subset

Theorem 1.3

A Banach space

does not contain a copy of

if and only if every bounded subset of

The next proposition is an easy consequence of Theorem

]

Proposition 1.1
.

If
X
does not contain a copy of
\ell^{1}
, every completely continuous operator
T\in\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)
is compact, for every Banach space

.

Proof.

Let
T\colon X\rightarrow Y
be completely continuous and let
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a bounded sequence in
X
. Due to Theorem
1.3
there exists a weakly Cauchy subsequence
(x_{n_{k}})_{k=1}^{\infty}
. For any strictly increasing sequences
(k_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
(m_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
of positive integers, the sequence
(x_{n_{k_{i}}}-x_{n_{m_{i}}})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is weakly null, and by the hypothesis it follows that
\|T(x_{n_{k_{i}}}-x_{k_{m_{i}}})\|_{Y}=\|Tx_{n_{k_{i}}}-Tx_{k_{m_{i}}}\|_{Y}\to 0
as

. Hence

is a Cauchy sequence in

and thus convergent, since

is complete. Therefore,

Let us review basic definitions and notation from the theory of vector measures, for more details we refer to a standard reference

Moreover, we say that a family of vector measures

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left\|\mu_{\lambda}\left(\bigsqcup_{k=n+1}^{\infty}E_{k}\right)\right\|_{X}=0,

(1.2)

for all sequences of disjoint subsets
(E_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Equivalent condition is:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|\mu_{\lambda}(F_{n})\|_{X}=0

for all decreasing sequences
(F_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of measurable subsets such that

.

we define
\nu_{\varphi}\colon\mathfrak{M}\rightarrow X^{*}
by

\nu_{\varphi}(E)=\int_{E}\varphi\,d\mu,\qquad E\in\mathfrak{M}.

(1.5)

Clearly
\nu_{\varphi}
is a vector measure. Moreover,
\nu_{\varphi}
is weakly
∗
countably additive, i.e. if
E=\bigsqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}
for a disjoint sequence
(E_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of sets in
\mathfrak{M}
, then for all
x\in X

\langle x,\nu_{\varphi}(E)\rangle=\int_{E}\left\langle x,\varphi(t)\right\rangle d\mu(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{n}}\left\langle x,\varphi(t)\right\rangle d\mu(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle x,\nu_{\varphi}(E_{n})\right\rangle.

The second equality above follows from integrability of scalar function
\langle x,\varphi\rangle
. Also, if
\mu(E)=0
, then
\left\langle x,\nu_{\varphi}(E)\right\rangle=\int_{E}\left\langle x,\varphi(t)\right\rangle d\mu(t)=0
for
x\in X
and hence
\nu_{\varphi}(E)=0
. Thus, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 1.2
.

For every
\varphi\in\mathbb{G}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,X^{*})
the mapping

\nu_{\varphi}\colon\mathfrak{M}\rightarrow X^{*}
defined by (
1.5
) is a weakly
∗
countably additive vector measure that vanishes on sets of
\mu
-measure
0
.

A strongly
\mu
-measurable function
\varphi:\Omega\to X
is said to be
Bochner
p
-integrable
, for
1\leqslant p<+\infty
, if
\int_{\Omega}\|\varphi(t)\|_{X}^{p}\,d\mu(t)<+\infty
. These functions form a Banach space
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,X)
with a natural norm, assuming the usual a. e. identification.
Also, the vector space of
\mu
-simple functions is dense in
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,X)
.
If
\Omega=\mathbb{N}
then
\ell^{p}(X)=\{f\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow X:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|f(n)\|_{X}^{p}<+\infty\}
is the space of Bochner
p
-integrable functions, as every
f\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow X
is strongly
\mu
-measurable. Functions in
L^{1}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,X)
are called
Bochner integrable
.
For every
E\in\mathfrak{M}
and
\varphi
in
L^{1}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,X)
there is a naturally defined element
\prescript{\mathrlap{B}\,\,}{}{\int_{E}}\varphi\,d\mu
in
X
, the Bochner integral of
\varphi
over
E
.
Then
\nu_{\varphi}:\mathfrak{M}\to X
defined by
\nu_{\varphi}(E)=\prescript{\mathrlap{B}\,\,}{}{\int_{E}}\varphi\,d\mu
is a countably additive vector measure on
(\Omega,\mathfrak{M})
which is
\mu
-continuous.

We alert the reader that there are weaker forms of
p
-integrability in the spirit of Dunford and Pettis, see
[
9
]
.
It is easily seen that Bochner integrability implies Pettis integrability, Pettis integrability directly implies Dunford integrability.
It is also easily seen that Bochner integrability of
\varphi:\Omega\to X^{\ast}
imples Gelfand integrability of
\varphi
.
We will often, for simplicity, suppress
\mathfrak{M}
from notation and write, for example,
L^{1}(\Omega,\mu,X)
instead of
L^{1}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,X)
.

1.3.
\EuScriptBold B(X,Y)
-valued functions

In this paper we deal with
\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)
-valued functions, some of the results, examples and counterexamples will be specific to the Hilbert space case, i. e. to
\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H})
-valued functions.
For an o. v. function (equivalently family)
{\mathscr{A}}=({\mathscr{A}}_{t})_{t\in\Omega}
in
\EuScriptBold B(X,Y)
the family
({\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{*})_{t\in\Omega}
in
\EuScriptBold B(Y^{*},X^{*})
will be denoted by
{\mathscr{A}}^{*}
.
For
x
in
X
the family
({\mathscr{A}}_{t}x)_{t\in\Omega}
in
Y
is denoted by
{\mathscr{A}}x
.
The pointwise product of families
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
is denoted by
{\mathscr{A}}{\mathscr{B}}
, whenever this is well defined.
We say a family
{\mathscr{A}}
is bounded if
\sup_{t\in\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|<+\infty
.

A family
({\mathscr{A}}_{t})_{t\in\Omega}
is said to be
pointwise
(resp.
pointwise weakly
)
\mu
-
measurable
if
{\mathscr{A}}x
is strongly (resp. weakly)
\mu
-measurable for all
x
in
X
.
Two families
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
are said to be
pointwise equivalent
(resp.
pointwise weakly equivalent
) if for every
x
in
X
we have
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x={\mathscr{B}}_{t}x
a. e.
(resp. for every
(x,y^{*})\in X\times Y^{*}
we have
\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y^{*}\rangle=\langle{\mathscr{B}}_{t}x,y^{*}\rangle
a. e.). We denote the vector space of all classes of pointwise equivalent pointwise
\mu
-measurable (pointwise weakly equivalent pointwise weakly
\mu
-measurable) families by
\mathcal{M}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
(resp.
\mathcal{M}_{w}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
, see
[
6
]
.
A family
{\mathscr{A}}=({\mathscr{A}}_{t})_{t\in\Omega}
in
\EuScriptBold B(X,Y^{*})
is said to be
pointwise weakly
∗

\mu
-
measurable
if
{\mathscr{A}}x
is weakly
∗

\mu
-measurable for every
x\in X
. Two such families
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
are said to be
pointwise weakly
∗

equivalent
if, for every
(x,y)\in X\times Y
we have
\langle y,{\mathscr{A}}x\rangle=\langle y,{\mathscr{B}}x\rangle
a. e. We denote the space of all equivalence classes of pointwise weakly
∗

\mu
-measurable functions by
\mathcal{M}_{w^{*}}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y^{*}))
.
By Theorem
1.7
, if the space
Y
is separable, we have the equality

\mathcal{M}_{w}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))=\mathcal{M}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)).

(1.6)

If
{\mathscr{A}}
belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
and
X
is separable, then the function
\Omega\ni t\mapsto\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|\in\mathbb{R}
is
\mu
-measurable.
This follows from
\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|=\sup_{x\in S}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\|_{Y}
, where
S
is a countable dense subset of
B_{X}
.

If
Y
is separable and does not contain an isomorphic copy of
\ell^{1}
, then by Theorem
1.4
we have

\mathcal{M}_{w^{*}}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y^{*}))=\mathcal{M}_{w}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y^{*})).

We say that
{\mathscr{A}}
in
\mathcal{M}_{w}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
is
pointwise Dunford integrable
if
{\mathscr{A}}x\in\mathbb{D}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,Y)
for all
x\in X
.

L^{1}_{w}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
denotes the vector space of all such classes of weakly pointwise equivalent functions, it was defined in
[
6
]
, see also
[
4
]
where notation
L^{1}_{pD}
instead of
L^{1}_{w}
was used.
For every
{\mathscr{A}}
in
L^{1}_{w}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
and
E\in\mathfrak{M}
there is
\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu
in
\EuScriptBold B(X,Y^{**})
defined by:

\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)x=\prescript{\mathrlap{D}}{}{\int_{E}}{\mathscr{A}}x\,d\mu,\qquad x\in X,

(see
[
4
, Proposition 2.9]
for details). Finally, we define

\begin{split}&L^{1}_{w^{*}}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y^{*}))=\\
&=\{{\mathscr{A}}\in\mathcal{M}_{w^{*}}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y^{*})):\langle y,{\mathscr{A}}x\rangle\in L^{1}(\mu),\,\,\forall(x,y)\in X\times Y\}.\end{split}

Due to duality
\left(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y\right)^{*}\cong\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y^{*})
, see
[
10
, Chapter 3.2]
, it turns out that

L^{1}_{w^{*}}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y^{*}))=\mathbb{G}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y^{*})).

1.4.
\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H})
-valued functions

Note that
{\EuScriptBold C}_{1}({\mathcal{H}})^{*}\cong\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
{\EuScriptBold C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H})^{*}={\EuScriptBold C}_{1}(\mathcal{H})
, where duality is trace duality. Hence
{\mathscr{A}}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H})
is weakly
∗

\mu
-measurable (Gelfand integrable) if and only if function
\Omega\ni t\mapsto\operatorname{tr}({\mathscr{A}}_{t}X)\in\mathbb{C}
is
\mu
-measurable (integrable) for all
X
in
{\EuScriptBold C}_{1}({\mathcal{H}})
.
Identification a. e. and identification using functions
\Omega\ni t\mapsto\operatorname{tr}({\mathscr{A}}_{t}X)\in\mathbb{C}
lead to the same equivalence relation on
\mathbb{G}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H}))
because
{\EuScriptBold C}_{1}({\mathcal{H}})
is separable, see Subsection
1.2
.
In order to prove weak
∗

\mu
-measurability (Gelfand integrability) it suffices to show
\mu
-measurability (integrability) of the induced sesquilinear form, i. e. functions
\Omega\ni t\mapsto\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}f,g\rangle\in\mathbb{C}
, for all
f,g\in\mathcal{H}
(see
[
18
, Lemma 1.1]
).
Thus, we have

\mathbb{G}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H}))=L^{1}_{w}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H})).

Also, by polarization identity, it suffices to check
\mu
-measurability (integrability) of
\Omega\ni t\mapsto\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}f,f\rangle\in\mathbb{C}
for all
f
in
\mathcal{H}
. The function

{\mathscr{A}}:\Omega\to\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H})
is weakly
∗

\mu
-measurable (Gelfand integrable) if and only if
{\mathscr{A}}^{\ast}
is weakly
∗

\mu
-measurable (Gelfand integrable).
By Theorem
1.7
, weak
∗

\mu
-measurability of
{\mathscr{A}}
is equivalent to the strong
\mu
-measurability of
{\mathscr{A}}f:\Omega\to\mathcal{H}
for every
f\in\mathcal{H}
.
Furthermore, as already noted in Subsection
1.3
, for
every such o. v. function, the mapping
\Omega\ni t\mapsto\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|\in\mathbb{R}
is
\mu
-measurable (see also
[
15
, Page 322]
).

2.
Preliminaries on
L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
spaces

The spaces appearing in the title of this section were introduced in
[
6
]
, under the additional assumptions of finiteness of the measure
\mu
and finiteness of expression in (
2.2
); see the discussion in that paper after Proposition 3.1. Here we revisit these spaces and collect several elementary and auxiliary results concerning them, which will serve as a preparation for the main results proved in Section
3
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1\leqslant p<+\infty
and
{\mathscr{A}}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\EuScriptBold B(X,Y)
be a pointwise
\mu
-measurable function.
We say that
{\mathscr{A}}
is strongly
p
-integrable if for all
x\in X
holds
\int_{\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\|_{Y}^{p}\,d\mu(t)<+\infty
. The space of all such functions is denoted by
L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
, with the identification of pointwise equivalent functions.

If
\Omega=\mathbb{N}
and
\mu
is the counting measure on
\mathfrak{M}=\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N}),
the corresponding space of strongly
p
-integrable functions is denoted by
\ell^{p}_{s}(\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
and this space is a Banach space (see Proposition
2.3
below). Also,
if
p=1
we say that
{\mathscr{A}}
is
strongly integrable
. In that case for every
E\in\mathfrak{M}
there is an operator
\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\in\EuScriptBold B(X,Y)
defined by the formula

\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu\right)x=\prescript{\mathrlap{B}}{}{\int_{E}}{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\,d\mu(t)\,\,\,\text{ for every}\,\,\,x\in X.

(2.1)

Moreover, for
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y^{*}))
and every
x\in X
and
y\in Y
we have
|\left\langle y,{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\right\rangle|\leqslant\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\|_{Y^{*}}\cdot\|y\|_{Y}
, hence
{\mathscr{A}}\in\mathbb{G}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y^{*}))
. In this case

\left\langle y,\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)x\right\rangle=\int_{E}\left\langle y,{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\right\rangle\,d\mu(t)=\left\langle y,\prescript{\mathrlap{B}}{}{\int_{E}}{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\,d\mu(t)\right\rangle,\qquad E\in\mathfrak{M}.

We record here, for future use, the following simple lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
X
is separable, then every
{\mathscr{A}}
in
L_{s}^{p}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
vanishes outside a set of
\sigma
-finite measure.

Proof.

We choose a countable dense subset
S
of
B_{X}
, since
{\mathscr{A}}x\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu,Y)
for every
x\in S
, there is a set
E_{x}
of
\sigma
-finite measure such that
{\mathscr{A}}x
vanishes outside
E_{x}
.
It is easily seen that
\bigcup_{x\in S}E_{x}
is a required set of
\sigma
-finite measure.
∎

Example
2.1
below shows that separability of the space
X
in Lemma
2.1
is essential, even if we work with Hilbert spaces only.

Example 2.1
.

Let
\Lambda
be an uncountable set, let
I_{\lambda}=[0,1]\times\{\lambda\}
for
\lambda\in\Lambda
and set
\Omega=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}I_{\lambda}
. We transfer Lebesgue
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{L}
from
[0,1]
to
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
on
I_{\lambda}
and transfer Lebesgue measure
m
from
[0,1]
to measure
m_{\lambda}
on
I_{\lambda}
.
Then we have a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathfrak{M}=\{E\subset\Omega:E\cap I_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}_{\lambda},\lambda\in\Lambda\}
on
\Omega
and a measure
\mu
on
\mathfrak{M}
defined by
\mu(E)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}m_{\lambda}(E\cap I_{\lambda})
for all
E\in\mathfrak{M}
.
Let
1\leqslant p<+\infty
. Then
X=\ell^{p}(\Omega)
is a non separable Banach space, with a natural family of unit vectors
\{e_{t,\lambda}:0\leqslant t\leqslant 1,\lambda\in\Lambda\}
. We define, for
(t,\lambda)\in\Omega
,
{\mathscr{A}}_{t,\lambda}\in\EuScriptBold B(X)
by
{\mathscr{A}}_{t,\lambda}x=x(t,\lambda)e_{t,\lambda}
.
For a given
x\in X
there are only countably many
(t,\lambda)\in\Omega
such that
{\mathscr{A}}_{t,\lambda}x\not=0
, and therefore
{\mathscr{A}}x
is a function on
\Omega
that vanishes outside a countable set and hence
{\mathscr{A}}x
is
\mu
-Bochner integrable. This means that
{\mathscr{A}}
is in
L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X))
. However,
\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t,\lambda}\|=1
for all
(t,\lambda)\in\Omega
and therefore
{\mathscr{A}}
does not have
\sigma
-finite support.
\hfill\triangle

Note that
\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}=0
in the above example, compare with Corollary
3.6
.

The next result is a basic property of
L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
spaces.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
1\leqslant p<+\infty
and assume that the function
{\mathscr{A}}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\EuScriptBold B(X,Y)
is strongly
p
-integrable. Then we have

\sup_{\|x\|_{X}=1}\int_{\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\|_{Y}^{p}\,d\mu(t)<+\infty.

A sketch of proof of the above theorem, as well as formulation of Proposition
2.1
, can be found in
[
14
, Page 64]
. For reader’s convenience we present a proof, based on Closed Graph Theorem.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

A natural (and obviously linear) map
T:X\to L^{p}(\Omega,\mu,Y)
is given by
Tx={\mathscr{A}}x
.
Let us assume that
x_{n}\to x
in
X
and
Tx_{n}\to(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\Omega}
in
L^{p}(\Omega,\mu,Y)
.
Since convergence in the norm of
L^{p}(\Omega,\mu,Y)
implies existence of a subsequence converging a. e. pointwise we have
\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n_{k}}=\varphi_{t}
for
\mu
-almost all
t\in\Omega
.
However, continuity of each
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}
gives
\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n_{k}}={\mathscr{A}}_{t}x
and therefore

Tx=(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\Omega}
. This proves boundedness of
T
and completes the proof.
∎

Theorem
2.1
enables us to define a norm on
L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
, in the following
L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
is considered as a normed space with norm defined in (
2.2
) below.

Proposition 2.1
.

L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
is a vector space for every
1\leqslant p<+\infty
and a norm on
L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
is defined by the following formula:

\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{p}=\sup_{\|x\|_{X}=1}\sqrt[p]{\int_{\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\|_{Y}^{p}\,d\mu(t)}.

(2.2)

Proof.

Homogeneity and triangle inequality are obvious, non-degeneracy follows from pointwise identification of families in
L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
.
∎

Using (
2.1
), one derives the following standard estimate:

\left\|\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right\|=\sup_{\|x\|_{X}=1}\left\|\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)\!x\right\|_{Y}=\sup_{\|x\|_{X}=1}\left\|\prescript{\mathrlap{B}}{}{\int_{E}}{\mathscr{A}}x\,d\mu\right\|_{Y}\\
\leqslant\sup_{\|x\|_{X}=1}\int_{E}\|{\mathscr{A}}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu\leqslant\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1},\qquad E\in\mathfrak{M},\quad{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)).

(2.3)

Let, for
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
,
{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathfrak{M}}=\{\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu:E\in\mathfrak{M}\}
and set

KL^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))=\{{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)):{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathfrak{M}}\;\mbox{\rm is relatively compact}\}.

Using characterization of compactness in metric spaces by finite
\varepsilon
-nets and (
2.3
) one obtains the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

KL^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
is a subspace of
L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
which contains all simple functions.

In general, the space
L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
does not need to be complete, but in the case of a discrete measure we have the following result.

Proposition 2.3
.

The space
\ell_{s}^{p}(\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
is a Banach space for all
1\leqslant p<+\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\left({\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
be sequence in
\ell_{s}^{p}(\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sup_{\|x\|_{X}=1}\sqrt[p]{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left\|{\mathscr{A}}_{m}^{(n)}x\right\|_{Y}^{p}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|{\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}\right\|_{p}=M<+\infty.

Thus, for every unit vector
x
in
X
we get, using Minkowski’s inequality

\sqrt[p]{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|{\mathscr{A}}_{m}^{(n)}x\right\|_{Y}\right)^{p}}\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sqrt[p]{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left\|{\mathscr{A}}_{m}^{(n)}x\right\|_{Y}^{p}}\leqslant M.

Thus,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathscr{A}}_{m}^{(n)}x
converges in
X
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, for all
x\in X
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
we can define
{\mathscr{A}}_{m}x:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathscr{A}}_{m}^{(n)}x
.
We defined
{\mathscr{A}}_{m}\in\EuScriptBold B(X,Y)
with
\|{\mathscr{A}}_{m}\|\leqslant M
.
Set
{\mathscr{A}}=({\mathscr{A}}_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
. Then, using Minkowski’s inequality, we have

\begin{split}&\left\|{\mathscr{A}}-\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}\right\|_{p}^{p}=\sup_{\|x\|_{X}=1}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left\|{\mathscr{A}}_{m}x-\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathscr{A}}_{m}^{(n)}x\right\|_{Y}^{p}\\
&=\sup_{\|x\|_{X}=1}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left\|\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}{\mathscr{A}}_{m}^{(n)}x\right\|_{Y}^{p}\leqslant\sup_{\|x\|_{X}=1}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\left\|{\mathscr{A}}_{m}^{(n)}x\right\|_{Y}\right)^{p}\\
&\leqslant\left(\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\sup_{\|x\|_{X}=1}\sqrt[p]{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left\|{\mathscr{A}}_{m}^{(n)}x\right\|_{Y}^{p}}\right)^{p}=\left(\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\left\|{\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}\right\|_{p}\right)^{p},\qquad N\in\mathbb{N}.\end{split}

Taking
N\to\infty
we get desired conclusion.
∎

Let us point out that this completeness does not follow from coincidence of
\ell^{p}_{s}(\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
with the corresponding Bochner space
l^{p}(\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
, which in general does not hold, see Example
2.3
. Proposition
2.4
below gives another sufficient condition for completeness of
L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f:\Omega\to Y
be strongly
\mu
-measurable and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
. Then the mapping
\Omega\ni t\mapsto f(t)\otimes x^{*}\in\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)
is strongly
\mu
-measurable function.

Proof.

Let
T_{x^{*}}\colon Y\rightarrow\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)
be defined by
T_{x^{*}}y=y\otimes x^{*}
, for all
y\in Y
. Then,
T_{x^{*}}
is linear and, since
\|T_{x^{*}}y\|=\|y\otimes x^{*}\|=\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}\cdot\|y\|_{Y}
, continuous with
\|T_{x^{*}}\|=\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}
.
Let
{\mathscr{A}}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)
be defined by
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}=f(t)\otimes x^{*}=(T_{x^{*}}\circ f)(t)
, for
t\in\Omega
. Finally, as
T_{x^{*}}
is measurable (being continuous) with
T_{x^{*}}(0)=0
and
f
is strongly
\mu
-measurable, it follows that
{\mathscr{A}}=T_{x^{*}}\circ f
is strongly
\mu
-measurable due to
[
14
, Corrolary 1.1.24]
.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\dim X=n
. Then every
{\mathscr{A}}\in\mathcal{M}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
is strongly
\mu
-measurable. Further, let
1\leqslant p<+\infty
. Then
L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))=L^{p}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
as vector spaces. Moreover,

\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{p}\leqslant\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{L^{p}}\leqslant n\cdot\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{p},\qquad{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)),

implying completeness of
L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
.

Proof.

As
X
is
n
-dimensional, due to Auerbach’s Lemma (for details see
[
13
, Proposition 6.26]
) there exist basis of unit vectors
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\in X
and basis of unit functionals
e_{1}^{*},e_{2}^{*},\ldots,e_{n}^{*}\in X^{*}
such that
\left\langle e_{i},e_{j}^{*}\right\rangle=\delta_{ij}
, for all
i,j
in
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. Let
{\mathscr{A}}\in\mathcal{M}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
be arbitrary. Since for every
x\in X
holds
x=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\langle x,e_{k}^{*}\right\rangle e_{k}
, it follows that

{\mathscr{A}}_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}({\mathscr{A}}_{t}e_{k})\otimes e_{k}^{*},\qquad t\in\Omega.

As all of the mappings
{\mathscr{A}}e_{k}
are strongly
\mu
-measurable
Y
-valued functions, by Lemma
2.2
the mappings
({\mathscr{A}}e_{k})\otimes e_{k}^{*}
are strongly
\mu
-measurable
\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)
-valued functions implying that
{\mathscr{A}}
is also such.

Now let us assume
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
. Since for all
t\in\Omega
we have

\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|({\mathscr{A}}_{t}e_{k})\otimes e_{k}^{*}\|=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}e_{k}\|_{Y}\cdot\|e_{k}^{*}\|_{X^{*}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}e_{k}\|_{Y}

due to Holder inequality we get that for all
t\in\Omega
we have

\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|^{p}\leqslant\left(\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}e_{k}\|_{Y}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}1^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\right)^{p}=n^{p-1}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}e_{k}\|_{Y}^{p}.

(2.4)

As
X
is separable (being finite-dimensional),
\Omega\ni t\mapsto\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|\in\mathbb{R}
is a
\mu
-measurable function. Moreover, the function
{\mathscr{A}}e_{k}
belongs to the Banach space
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,Y)
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Then, using (
2.4
), we conclude that
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
. By integrating (
2.4
), we get

\begin{split}\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}&\leqslant n^{p-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}e_{k}\|_{Y}^{p}\,d\mu(t)\\
&\leqslant n^{p-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sup_{\|x\|_{X}\leqslant 1}\int_{\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\|_{Y}^{p}\,d\mu(t)=n^{p}\cdot\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{p}^{p},\end{split}

proving the proclaimed equivalence of norms. Since
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
is a Banach space,
L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
is also a Banach space.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

We present an example where the constant
n
is attained. We take
X=Y=\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
\ell^{1}
-norm, and define
{\mathscr{A}}\in\ell^{1}(\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
by
{\mathscr{A}}_{k}=e_{k}\otimes e_{k}^{*}
for
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
, where
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is canonical basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Direct calculation gives us (for more details see Example
2.4
) where
\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}=1
and
\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{L^{1}}=n=n\cdot\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
M
be a complemented subspace of
X
, with complement
N
, and let
\pi\colon X\to M
be the corresponding continuous projection along
N
. Assume
{\mathscr{A}}=({\mathscr{A}}_{t})_{t\in\Omega}
is a strongly
\mu
-measurable family in
\EuScriptBold B(M,Y)
. Then
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}=({\mathscr{A}}_{t}\pi)_{t\in\Omega}
is a strongly
\mu
-measurable family in
\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)
.

If, moreover,
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(M,Y))
, then
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
.

Proof.

Let
s_{n}:\Omega\to\EuScriptBold B(M,Y)
be a sequence of
\mu
-simple functions such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}-s_{n}(t)\|=0

\mu
-almost everywhere. Then the sequence
s_{n}^{\prime}(t)=s_{n}(t)\pi
is a sequence of
\mu
-simple functions in
\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}_{t}-s_{n}^{\prime}(t)\|=0

\mu
-almost everywhere, which proves the first statement. The second one follows from
\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{\prime}\|\leqslant\|\pi\|\cdot\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|
.
∎

2.1.
Examples and counterexamples

Clearly
L^{p}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
is contained in
L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
and

\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{p}\leqslant\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))},\qquad{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y)).

Our first task is to compare the space
L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
to the narrower Bochner space
L^{p}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
, this is done by the following examples.The first one appeared in
[
4
]
(in a special case
p=2
) as an Example 2.12, it was noted in
[
4
]
that
{\mathscr{A}}
is pointwise Dunford integrable, but not strongly
\mu
-measurable.
However, more is true, it is in fact strongly
p
-integrable for every
1\leqslant p<+\infty
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
\Omega=[0,1]
,
X=L^{p}((0,1),m)
where
1\leqslant p<+\infty
and let
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}
be the multiplication operator by
\chi_{(0,t)}
for all
0\leqslant t\leqslant 1
.
It is easily seen that the range of
{\mathscr{A}}
is a non-separable subset of
\EuScriptBold B(X)
and therefore
{\mathscr{A}}
is not strongly
\mu
-measurable and thus does not belong to
L^{p}(\Omega,m,\EuScriptBold B(X))
.
However, for every
f\in X
the mapping
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto\chi_{(0,t)}f\in X
is a continuous
X
-valued function on
[0,1]
and thus Bochner
p
-integrable.
\triangle

Example 2.3
.

Let
1\leqslant p,q<+\infty
, set
X=\ell^{q}
and define
{\mathscr{A}}:\mathbb{N}\to\EuScriptBold B(X)
by
{\mathscr{A}}_{n}x=\frac{1}{\sqrt[p]{n}}x_{n}\cdot e_{1}
for
x=(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{q}
.
Then for every
x=(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{q}
we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}x\|^{p}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cdot|x_{n}|^{p}\leqslant\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\frac{r}{r-1}}}\right)^{\frac{r-1}{r}}\sqrt[r]{\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}|x_{n}|^{pr}}<+\infty,

where
r>1
is selected so that
pr\geqslant q
. Hence
{\mathscr{A}}\in\ell^{p}_{s}(\EuScriptBold B(X))
.
Since
\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\|=\frac{1}{\sqrt[p]{n}}
we see that
{\mathscr{A}}
is not in
\ell^{p}(\EuScriptBold B(X))
.
Let
q^{\prime}
be the exponent conjugate to
q
. Then
{\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}\in\EuScriptBold B(\ell^{q^{\prime}})
is given by
{\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}x=\frac{1}{\sqrt[p]{n}}x_{1}e_{n}
,
x=(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{q^{\prime}}
. However, for
x_{1}\not=0
, the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}x\|^{p}
is divergent and thus
{\mathscr{A}}^{*}
is not in
\ell^{p}_{s}(\EuScriptBold B(\ell^{q^{\prime}}))
.
\triangle

In Example
2.2
non-separability of the range excluded
{\mathscr{A}}
from the space
L^{p}(\Omega,m,\EuScriptBold B(X))
, although
\int_{(0,1)}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|^{p}\,dm(t)=1<+\infty
.
In Example
2.3
it was the size of norm of
{\mathscr{A}}
that did the trick.
The same Example
2.3
shows that
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
does not imply
{\mathscr{A}}^{\ast}\in L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(Y^{*},X^{*}))
, even in the case when
X
is a Hilbert space (take
q=2
in Example
2.3
).

Hence we conclude: spaces
L^{1}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H}))
of Bochner integrable functions and
\mathbb{G}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H}))
of Gelfand integrable functions are invariant under conjugation, but the intermediate space
L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H}))
in general is not invariant.
However, certain type of integrability of
{\mathscr{A}}^{*}
is present for
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
. Namely, due to duality
\EuScriptBold{B}(Y^{*},X^{*})\cong(Y^{*}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}X)^{*}
we can state the following result.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
, then
{\mathscr{A}}^{*}\in\mathbb{G}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(Y^{*},X^{*}))
and

\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)^{*}=\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}^{*}\,d\mu,\qquad E\in\mathfrak{M}.

Proof.

The mapping
\Omega\ni t\mapsto\left\langle x,{\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{*}y^{*}\right\rangle=\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y^{*}\right\rangle\in\mathbb{C}
is
\mu
-measurable for all
x\in X
and
y^{*}\in Y^{*}
and

\int_{\Omega}|\left\langle x,{\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{*}y^{*}\right\rangle|\,d\mu(t)=\int_{\Omega}|\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y^{*}\right\rangle|\,d\mu(t)\leqslant\|x\|_{X}\|y^{*}\|_{Y^{*}}\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}<+\infty.

Hence, by
[
21
, Lemma 1.8]
,
{\mathscr{A}}^{*}\in\mathbb{G}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(Y^{*},X^{*}))
. Moreover, for
E\in\mathfrak{M}
:

\begin{split}\left\langle x,\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}^{*}d\mu\right)y^{*}\right\rangle&=\int_{E}\left\langle x,{\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{*}y^{*}\right\rangle d\mu(t)\\
&=\int_{E}\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y^{*}\right\rangle d\mu(t)=\left\langle\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu\right)x,y^{*}\right\rangle.\qed\end{split}

In the next example we show that the norms
\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{L^{p}}=(\int_{\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|^{p}\,d\mu(t))^{1/p}
and

\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{p}
in general are not equivalent on
L^{p}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X))
.

Example 2.4
.

Let
1\leqslant p,q<\infty
, set
X=\ell^{q}
, and let
(e_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be the standard Schauder basis in
\ell^{q}
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
define an o.v. function
{\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\EuScriptBold{B}(X)
by
{\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}_{m}=\chi_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}(m)e_{m}\otimes e_{m}^{*}
.
We have
\|{\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}_{m}\|=1
for
1\leqslant m\leqslant n
and therefore
\|{\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}\|_{L^{p}}=n^{1/p}
. Let us fix
x=(\xi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\ell^{q}
. Then
{\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}x:\mathbb{N}\to\ell^{q}
is given by

{\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}x(k)=\xi_{k}e_{k}
for
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
and
{\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}x(k)=0
for
k>n
.
If
q\leqslant p
, then

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}x(k)\|^{p}_{\ell^{q}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\xi_{k}|^{p}\leqslant\|x\|^{p}_{\ell^{p}}\leqslant\|x\|^{p}_{\ell^{q}}.

Thus
\|{\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}\|_{p}\leqslant 1
, in fact taking
x=e_{1}
it is easily seen that
\|{\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}\|_{p}=1
. Therefore, in the case
q\leqslant p
, the two norms are not equivalent.

In the remaining case
p<q
we have, for
x=(\xi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\ell^{q}
:

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}x(k)\|^{p}_{\ell^{q}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\xi_{k}|^{p}\leqslant n^{1-p/q}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\xi_{k}|^{q}\right)^{p/q}\leqslant n^{1-p/q}\|x\|_{\ell^{q}}^{p}.

Hence
\|{\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}\|_{p}\leqslant n^{1/p-1/q}
and again the two norms are not equivalent.

\hfill\triangle

The next example shows that the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{G}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{1}
are not equivalent on
L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H}))
, in the general case.

Example 2.5
.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{N}
and let
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an o. n. b. for
\mathcal{H}
. We set

{\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{(m)}=\frac{1}{n}\chi_{\{1,\ldots,m\}}(n)\cdot e_{n}\otimes e_{1}^{*},\qquad m,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then, for the family
{\mathscr{A}}^{(m)}=\left({\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{(m)}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
we have:

\left\|{\mathscr{A}}^{(m)}\right\|_{1}=\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1}\int_{\mathbb{N}}\left\|{\mathscr{A}}^{(m)}_{n}f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}\,d\mu(n)=\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1}\sum_{n=1}^{m}\frac{1}{n}|\left\langle f,e_{1}\right\rangle|\cdot\|e_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{n=1}^{m}\frac{1}{n},

\begin{split}\left\|{\mathscr{A}}^{(m)}\right\|_{\mathbb{G}}&=\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1}\int_{\mathbb{N}}|\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}^{(m)}_{n}f,g\right\rangle|\,d\mu(n)=\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1}\sum_{n=1}^{m}\frac{1}{n}\cdot|\left\langle f,e_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle e_{n},g\right\rangle|\\
&\leqslant\sup_{\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1}\sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^{m}\frac{1}{n^{2}}}\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^{m}|\left\langle g,e_{n}\right\rangle|^{2}}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{6}}.\end{split}

Since the harmonic series is divergent, these two norms are not equivalent.

\hfill\triangle

3.
Main results on
L^{p}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
spaces

In this section we present key results, Theorems
3.3
,
3.4
and
3.5
. Main point is that a family
{\mathscr{A}}
in the space
L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
generates countably additive
\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)
-valued measure provided
X^{*}
does not contain an isomorphic copy of
\mathfrak{c}_{0}
. This is achieved through intermediary results, among them Theorem
3.1
has independent interest.
The following proposition will be superseded by Theorem
3.1
below and used in its proof.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume
X^{*}
does not contain an isomorphic copy of
\mathfrak{c}_{0}
and let
{\mathscr{A}}\in\ell_{s}^{1}(\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
. Then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\|=0
.

Proof.

We will prove the statement by reducing it to a special case of rank-
1
operators. Let

{\mathscr{A}}\in\ell^{1}_{s}(\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
and let
y_{n}^{*}
be unit vectors
Y^{*}
such that
\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}y^{*}_{n}\|_{X^{*}}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}\|=\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\|
. Next, we choose a unit vector
y_{0}\in Y
and set, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
{\mathscr{B}}_{n}=(y_{0}\otimes y^{*}_{n}){\mathscr{A}}_{n}
. First, we have

\|{\mathscr{B}}_{n}x\|_{Y}\leqslant\|y_{0}\otimes y^{*}_{n}\|\cdot\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}x\|_{Y}=\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}x\|_{Y}

while, due to equality (
1.1
), we have
{\mathscr{B}}_{n}=y_{0}\otimes{\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}y_{n}^{*}
implying

\|{\mathscr{B}}_{n}x\|_{Y}=|({\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}y_{n}^{*})(x)|\cdot\|y_{0}\|_{Y}=|({\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}y_{n}^{*})(x)|.

Combining these two facts, we get

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|({\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}y_{n}^{*})(x)|=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathscr{B}}_{n}x\|_{Y}\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}x\|_{Y}<+\infty,

(3.1)

for every
x\in X,
as
{\mathscr{A}}\in\ell^{1}_{s}(\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
. By taking supremum over
B_{X}
in the (
3.1
) we get that
\|({\mathscr{A}}^{*}_{n}y^{*}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{\ell^{1}_{w}(X^{*})}\leqslant\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}
. Hence, by Lemma
1.1
, the sequence
({\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}y_{n}^{*})_{n=1}^{\infty}
belongs to
\ell_{w}^{1}(X^{*})
. Finally, due to Bessaga-Pelczynski Theorem (see
[
12
, Corollary 5, Page 22]
), as
X^{*}
does not contain isomorphic copy of
\mathfrak{c}_{0}
, the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}y_{n}^{*}
converges unconditionally. Hence
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}y_{n}^{*}\|_{X^{*}}=0
and by the choice of vectors
y_{n}^{*}
we get
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\|=0
, as proclaimed.
∎

The next result shows that the passage from pointwise summability on vectors to unconditional convergence in the operator norm is governed precisely by the geometry of the dual space
X^{*}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathscr{A}}_{n}
converges unconditionally in
\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)
for all
{\mathscr{A}}\in\ell^{1}_{s}(\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
if and only if
X^{*}
does not contain isomorphic copy of
\mathfrak{c}_{0}
.

Proof.

If
X^{*}
contains isomorphic copy of
\mathfrak{c}_{0}
then by Bessaga-Pelczynski Theorem there exists a series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}^{*}
in
X^{*}
which is not unconditionally norm convergent but
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle x_{n}^{*},x^{**}\rangle|<+\infty
for all
x^{**}\in X^{**}
.
Let us fix a nonzero vector
y_{0}
in
Y
and set
{\mathscr{A}}_{n}=y_{0}\otimes x_{n}^{*}\in\EuScriptBold B(X,Y)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}x\|_{Y}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\langle x,x_{n}^{*}\rangle y_{0}\|_{Y}=\|y_{0}\|_{Y}\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|x_{n}^{*}(x)|<+\infty,

we conclude that
{\mathscr{A}}\in\ell^{1}_{s}(\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
. Moreover,

({\mathscr{A}}_{\pi(n)})_{n=1}^{\infty}
belongs to
\ell^{1}_{s}(\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
for every permutation
\pi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
. Since

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\mathscr{A}}_{\pi(k)}=y_{0}\otimes\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{\pi(k)}^{*},

unconditional norm convergence of
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathscr{A}}_{n}
would imply unconditional norm convergence of
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}^{*}
, which is not true.

Now we consider the converse, i. e. we assume
X^{*}
contains no isomorphic copy of
\mathfrak{c}_{0}
.
Since for any permutation
\pi
of
\mathbb{N}
the family

({\mathscr{A}}_{\pi(n)})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is also in
\ell^{1}_{s}(\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
, it suffices to prove that the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathscr{A}}_{n}
converges in the norm of
\EuScriptBold B(X,Y)
. Assume, to the contrary, that there exist
\varepsilon>0
and strictly increasing sequences
(a_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
and
(b_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
of natural numbers such that

a_{k}\leqslant b_{k},\,\,b_{k}<a_{k+1}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\left\|\sum\limits_{n=a_{k}}^{b_{k}}{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\right\|\geqslant\varepsilon\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

Define
{\mathscr{C}}_{k}=\sum_{n=a_{k}}^{b_{k}}{\mathscr{A}}_{n}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then,
{\mathscr{C}}=({\mathscr{C}}_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{s}^{1}(\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
since

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathscr{C}}_{k}x\|_{Y}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|\sum_{n=a_{k}}^{b_{k}}{\mathscr{A}}_{n}x\right\|_{Y}\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=a_{k}}^{b_{k}}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}x\|_{Y}\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}x\|_{Y}<+\infty

for all
x\in X
.
Then, by Proposition
3.1
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|{\mathscr{C}}_{k}\|=0

which is impossible since
\|{\mathscr{C}}_{k}\|\geqslant\varepsilon>0
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathscr{A}}_{n}
is norm convergent in
\EuScriptBold B(X,Y)
.
∎

Since a Hilbert space does not contain an isomorphic copy of
\mathfrak{c}_{0}
we obtain

Corollary 3.1
.

For every
{\mathscr{A}}\in\ell_{s}^{1}(\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H}))
the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathscr{A}}_{n}
is unconditionally convergent in the norm of
\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

Under an additional assumption on
X
, Proposition
3.1
can be further refined. More precisely, the next theorem is an extension of Theorem
1.1
to sequences of operators.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume
X^{*}
has cotype
q<+\infty
, and let
{\mathscr{A}}\in\ell^{1}_{s}(\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
. Then
{\mathscr{A}}\in\ell^{q}(\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
and we have the following estimate, where
C_{q}=C_{q}(X^{*})
:

\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leqslant C_{q}\cdot\left\|{\mathscr{A}}\right\|_{1}.

(3.2)

Proof.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}\in\ell^{1}_{s}(\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
and
\varepsilon>0
. We can choose unit vectors
y_{n}^{*}\in Y^{*}
such that
\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}y_{n}^{*}\|_{X^{*}}\geqslant\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\|
. As in the proof of Proposition
3.1
select a unit vector
y_{0}
in
Y
and define operators

{\mathscr{B}}_{n}=(y_{0}\otimes y_{n}^{*}){\mathscr{A}}_{n}=y_{0}\otimes({\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}y_{n}^{*})\colon X\to Y.

Then
{\mathscr{B}}\in\ell^{1}_{s}(\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
with

\|{\mathscr{B}}\|_{1}\leqslant\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}
. Moreover, using (
3.1
) we again get
({\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}y_{n}^{*})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{1}_{w}(X^{*})
where
\|(A_{n}^{*}y_{n}^{*})_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{\ell_{w}^{1}(X^{*})}=\|{\mathscr{B}}\|_{1}
. Due to Theorem
1.1
we have
({\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}y_{n}^{*})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{q}(X^{*})
and

\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}y_{n}^{*}\|^{q}_{X^{*}}\right)^{1/q}\leqslant C_{q}\cdot\|({\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}y_{n}^{*})_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{\ell^{1}_{w}(X^{*})}=C_{q}\cdot\|{\mathscr{B}}\|_{1}\leqslant C_{q}\cdot\|{\mathscr{A}}\|._{1}

(3.3)

Now, as
\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}y_{n}^{*}\|\geqslant\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\|
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by the (
3.3
) we get

\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leqslant\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}^{*}y_{n}^{*}\|_{X^{*}}^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leqslant C_{q}\cdot\left\|{\mathscr{A}}\right\|_{1}.

Finally, by letting
\varepsilon\to 0+
we obtain (
3.2
), completing the proof.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

The previous theorem states that the inclusion

j\colon\ell^{1}_{s}(\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))\rightarrow\ell^{q}(\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))

is continuous when
X^{*}
has a finite cotype
q
. Moreover, due to Remark
1.1
we can further estimate
\|j\|\leqslant\pi_{q,1}(I_{X})
. Actually, we have
\|j\|=\pi_{q,1}(I_{X})
. To see previous equality, for
\varepsilon>0
we can find sequence
(x_{n}^{*})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{1}_{w}(X^{*})
such that

\|(x_{n}^{*})_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{\ell^{1}_{w}(X^{*})}=1,\qquad\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|x_{n}^{*}\|^{q}_{X^{*}}\right)^{1/q}\geqslant\pi_{q,1}(I_{X})-\varepsilon.

Next, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define operators
{\mathscr{A}}_{n}=x_{n}^{*}\otimes y_{0}
where
y_{0}\in Y
is arbitrary unit vector. Then
\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\|=\|x_{n}^{*}\|_{X^{*}}
and similarly to (
3.1
) we have
\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}=\|(x_{n}^{*})_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{\ell^{1}_{w}(X^{*})}=1
. Therefore, we get inequality

\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\geqslant(\pi_{q,1}(I_{X})-\varepsilon)\cdot\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1},

implying that
\|j\|\geqslant\pi_{q,1}(I_{X}).

In the special case
X=Y=\mathcal{H}
, due to Theorem
1.2
, the previous Theorem states that
\ell^{1}_{s}(\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H}))
is continuously embedded in
\ell^{2}(\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H}))
. More precisely:

Corollary 3.2
.

If
{\mathscr{A}}\in\ell^{1}_{s}(\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H}))
then we have

{\mathscr{A}}\in\ell^{2}(\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H}))\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{\ell^{2}(\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H}))}\leqslant\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}.

One expects that exponent
2
is optimal in Corollary
3.2
, as cotype of a space is always bounded below by
2
. This is confirmed by the following modification of Example
2.3
.

Example 3.1
.

Define
{\mathscr{A}}\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H})
by the expression

{\mathscr{A}}_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\ln(n+1)}\cdot e_{n}\otimes e_{n}^{*},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then, for every
f\in\mathcal{H}
we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}f\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\left\langle f,e_{n}\right\rangle|}{\sqrt{n}\ln(n+1)}\leqslant\sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n\ln^{2}(n+1)}}\cdot\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}<+\infty,

implying that
{\mathscr{A}}\in\ell^{1}_{s}(\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H}))
. On the other hand, for every

we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\|^{p}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\frac{p}{2}}\ln^{p}(n+1)}=+\infty,

since
\frac{p}{2}<1
, proving that
{\mathscr{A}}\notin\ell^{p}(\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H})).

Induced vector measures

By Proposition

{\mathscr{A}}
in the space

∗

Also,

Let us recall that a subset

is called

(see

\lim_{\mu(E)\rightarrow 0}\sup_{f\in K}\int_{E}\|f\|_{X}\,d\mu=0,

i.e. if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists

such that for every
\mu(E)<\delta
and

holds
\int_{E}\|f\|d\mu<\varepsilon
. Note, that this is equivalent to uniform integrability of family of scalar functions
\{\|f\|:f\in K\}
.

Theorem
3.3
below shows that countable additivity of
\nu_{\mathscr{A}}
can be concluded if the finiteness of measure
\mu
and uniform integrability are replaced with a mild condition on
X^{*}
.
Moreover, the condition of uniform integrability of the family
\{{\mathscr{A}}x:\|x\|_{X}\leqslant 1\}
is apriori satisfied for
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
if

does not contain isomorphic copy of
\ell^{1}
, see Corollary

below.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume
X^{*}
does not contain an isomorphic copy of
\mathfrak{c}_{0}
. Then, for every element
{\mathscr{A}}
in the space
L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
, the induced vector measure
\nu_{\mathscr{A}}:\mathfrak{M}\to\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)
defined by the expression

\nu_{\mathscr{A}}(E)=\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,E\in\mathfrak{M}

(3.4)

is countably additive.

Proof.

Let
(E_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be arbitrary sequence of mutually disjoint sets in the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathfrak{M}
and let
E
be the union of these sets. Now, for all
x\in X
, using (
2.1
) we obtain

\begin{split}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\nu_{\mathscr{A}}(E_{n})x\|_{Y}&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|\left(\int_{E_{n}}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)x\right\|_{Y}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|\prescript{\mathrlap{B}}{}{\int_{E_{n}}}{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\,d\mu(t)\right\|_{Y}\\
&\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{n}}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)<+\infty,\end{split}

as
{\mathscr{A}}
is strongly integrable.
Thus
(\nu_{\mathscr{A}}(E_{n}))_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{s}^{1}(\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
, so by Theorem
3.1
the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\nu_{\mathscr{A}}(E_{n})
converges (unconditionally) in norm of
\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)
.
However, the same series converges strongly to the operator
\nu_{\mathscr{A}}(E)
in
\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)

because
{\mathscr{A}}x\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu,Y)
for every
x\in X
. From the uniqueness of limit we get

\nu_{\mathscr{A}}\left(\bigsqcup\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\right)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\nu_{\mathscr{A}}(E_{n}),

where the last series converges in the norm of
\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
, where

X^{*}
does not contain
\mathfrak{c}_{0}
. Then, the vector measure
\nu_{{\mathscr{A}}^{*}}\colon\mathfrak{M}\rightarrow\EuScriptBold{B}(Y^{*},X^{*})
defined as in (
3.4
) is countably additive.

Proof.

Let
E=\bigsqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}
for some sequence
(E_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of mutually disjoint elements in the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathfrak{M}
. Now, due to Lemma
2.4
we have

\nu_{{\mathscr{A}}^{*}}\left(\bigsqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\right)=\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}^{*}\,d\mu=\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)^{*}=(\nu_{\mathscr{A}}(E))^{*}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\nu_{\mathscr{A}}(E_{n})\right)^{*}\\
=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\nu_{\mathscr{A}}(E_{n}))^{*}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{E_{n}}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)^{*}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{n}}{\mathscr{A}}^{*}\,d\mu=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\nu_{{\mathscr{A}}^{*}}(E_{n}),

(3.5)

where the countable additivity of
{\mathscr{A}}
follows from Theorem
3.3
. The first equality in the second line of (
3.5
) follows from the fact that conjugation preserves the norm convergence.
∎

Whenever

contains an isomorphic copy of

. This gives the following corollary.

Corollary 3.4

Under the assumptions of Theorem

, we have
\nu_{\mathscr{A}}\ll\mu
.

The next two theorems give deeper insight into families
{\mathscr{A}}
in the normed space

, under assumption that
X
contains no isomorphic copy of
\ell^{1}
.
The first one (Theorem

\{\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t):x\in B_{X}\}
of measures is uniformly countably additive.
The second one (Theorem

), which follows from the first one, states that

is an

for spaces

.

For every
x\in X
we have a finite measure
\mu_{x}\colon\mathfrak{M}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
defined by

\mu_{x}(E)=\int_{E}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,E\in\mathfrak{M}.

(3.6)

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
, where
X
does not contain a copy of
\ell^{1}
. Then, the family of measures
\EuScriptBold{F}=\{\mu_{x}:\|x\|_{X}=1\}
is uniformly countably additive.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. As
\EuScriptBold{F}
is not uniformly countably additive, due to
[
12
, Proposition 17, Page 8]
, there exist
\varepsilon>0
, a sequence
(E_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of disjoint measurable sets and unit vectors
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
X
such that

\mu_{x_{n}}(E_{n})=\int_{E_{n}}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n}\|_{Y}d\mu(t)\geqslant\varepsilon,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

The family
\EuScriptBold{F}
is uniformly bounded, as
\mu_{x}(\Omega)\leqslant\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1},
for every
\|x\|=1
. Hence, by Rosenthal’s Lemma
[
12
, Lemma 1, Page 18]
, there is a subsequence
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that

\int_{\bigsqcup_{j\neq k}E_{n_{j}}}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n_{k}}\|_{Y}d\mu(t)=\mu_{x_{n_{k}}}\left(\bigsqcup_{j\neq k}E_{n_{j}}\right)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\qquad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
\gamma\in\ell^{1}
be arbitrary. The same calculation as in the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 4, Page 104]
(where
f_{k}(t)={\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n_{k}}
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
) provides us with an estimate

\int_{\Omega}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n_{k}}\right\|_{Y}d\mu(t)\geqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\gamma_{k}|.

(3.7)

On the other hand, as
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}
is bounded and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}x_{n_{k}}
(absolutely) converges in
X
, we also get
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n_{k}}={\mathscr{A}}_{t}(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}x_{n_{k}})
for every

. Next,

\int_{\Omega}\left\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}x_{n_{k}}\right)\right\|_{Y}d\mu(t)\leqslant\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}\cdot\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}x_{n_{k}}\right\|_{X},

and (
3.7
) imply that

\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}x_{n_{k}}\right\|_{X}\geqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\gamma_{k}|.

(3.8)

Furthermore, we have

\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}x_{n_{k}}\right\|_{X}\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|\gamma_{k}x_{n_{k}}\|_{X}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\gamma_{k}|,

(3.9)

Estimates (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) produce a basic sequence
(x_{n_{k}})_{k=1}^{\infty}
in
X
which spans an isomorphic copy of
\ell^{1}
, which is a contradiction, as
X
does not contain
\ell^{1}
.
∎

Theorem
3.5
below provides a curious phenomenon: a countably additive vector measure with values in a normed space which is not necessarily complete.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
, where
X
does not contain a copy of
\ell^{1}
.
Then the mapping
\lambda_{\mathscr{A}}:\mathfrak{M}\to L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
defined by
\lambda_{\mathscr{A}}(E)=\chi_{E}{\mathscr{A}}
is a countably additive
\mu
-continuous vector measure with values in
L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
.

Proof.

Clearly
\lambda_{\mathscr{A}}
is finitely additive. Assume
E
is a disjoint union of sets
E_{n}
in
\mathfrak{M}
. From the definition of norm in
L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
, Theorem
3.4
and (
1.2
) we have the following equality:

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\lambda_{\mathscr{A}}(E)\!-\!\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{\mathscr{A}}(E_{k})\right\|_{1}\!\!&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\|x\|_{X}=1}\int_{\bigsqcup\limits_{k=n}^{\infty}E_{k}}\!\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)=0.\end{split}

Moreover, if
\lambda_{\mathscr{A}}
is not
\mu
-continuous, then there exist
\varepsilon>0
and a sequence of sets
E_{n}
in
\mathfrak{M}
such that
\mu(E_{n})\leqslant\frac{1}{2^{n}}
and
\|\lambda(E_{n})\|_{1}\geqslant\varepsilon
.
Set

\mu(F_{n})\leqslant\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}
and

3.4
and (

) imply
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\lambda_{\mathscr{A}}(F_{n})\|_{1}=0
and we arrived at a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
, where
X
does not contain a copy of
\ell^{1}
. Then the family of scalar functions
\{\|{\mathscr{A}}x\|_{Y}:\|x\|_{X}\leqslant R\}

.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.5
,
\lambda_{\mathscr{A}}\ll\mu
, hence for every

3.2.
Approximation results

In this subsection we consider approximation problems for elements of
L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
by families of bounded elements from
L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
, as well as by sequences of simple families from this space. We provide sufficient conditions under which this is possible, and we also present an example showing that approximation in the norm of
L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
by simple families cannot always be achieved.

Theorem
3.6
below is an approximation result for the space
L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
by bounded families. A similar theorem was recently obtained for functions with values in spaces of measures (see
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
).

Theorem 3.6
.

Assume
X
is separable and contains no isomorphic copy of
\ell^{1}
. Then, bounded families in
L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
are dense in
L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
.

Proof.

We fix
{\mathscr{A}}
in
L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
. Since
X
separable,
\Omega\ni t\mapsto\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|\in\mathbb{R}
is a
\mu
-measurable function. Hence we have an increasing sequence of measurable sets
\Omega_{n}=\{t\in\Omega:\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|\leqslant n\}
whose union is
\Omega
.
We define bounded functions
{\mathscr{A}}_{n}=\chi_{\Omega_{n}}\cdot{\mathscr{A}}
in
L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By Theorem
3.5
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\mathscr{A}}-{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\|_{1}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\lambda_{\mathscr{A}}(\Omega)-\lambda_{\mathscr{A}}(\Omega_{n})\|_{1}=0.\qed

Every
{\mathscr{A}}
in
L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
has the property that for every
x\in X
the family
{\mathscr{A}}x
in
Y
can be approximated by a sequence of simple
Y
-valued functions, which follows directly from Bochner integrability of the family
{\mathscr{A}}x
(see Definition
2.1
).
Theorem
3.7
provides sufficient conditions under which the approximating simple family of operators can be chosen independently of the vector
x
in
X
. For this we need the following notion.

It is said that a sequence
(X_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of finite dimensional subspaces of a Banach space
X
form
F. D. D.
(
finite dimensional Schauder decomposition
) of
X
if for every
x
in
X
there is a unique sequence
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that
x_{n}\in X_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}
.
In that case, we have a sequence of projections
(P_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
which map
x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}
to
\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}
and one shows that
M=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|P_{n}\|<+\infty
, the range of
P_{n}
is the direct sum
X_{n}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}
.
We have natural inclusions
j_{n}\colon X_{n}^{\prime}\rightarrow X
defined by
j_{n}(x)=x
for
x\in X_{n}^{\prime}
, as well as natural projections
\pi_{n}\colon X\rightarrow X_{n}^{\prime}
defined by
\pi_{n}(x)=P_{n}(x)
for
x\in X
. Then we have
\pi_{n}\circ j_{n}=id_{X_{n}^{\prime}}
,
j_{n}\circ\pi_{n}=P_{n}
and
\|\pi_{n}\|=\|P_{n}\|\leqslant M
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Whenever we have an F. D. D.
(X_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
for
X
we use the above notation.

The decomposition
(X_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is called
shrinking
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|P_{n}^{*}x^{*}-x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}=0
for every
x^{*}\in X^{*}
. For more details, see
[
19
, Section 1.g]
.

.

Assume
(X_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}

and
{\mathscr{A}}\in\mathcal{M}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))

{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\in\EuScriptBold{K}(X,Y)
for

2.4

for
\mu
-a. e.
t\in\Omega
(see arguments

before Theorem 3.14 in
[

). Thus,
{\mathscr{A}}
is
\mu

.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an F. D. D. for
X
and assume also that
X
does not contain an isomorphic copy of
\ell^{1}
. Then for every

{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))

there is a sequence
\left(\mathcal{S}^{(n)}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
\mu
-simple functions in
L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X,Y))
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x-\mathcal{S}_{t}^{(n)}x\right\|_{Y}d\mu(t)=0\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,x\in X.

(3.10)

If, in addition, F. D. D.
(X_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is shrinking, and
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\in\EuScriptBold{K}(X,Y)
for every
t\in\Omega
, then
{\mathscr{A}}
is in the
L^{1}_{s}
-closure of the set of
\mu
-simple
\EuScriptBold{K}(X,Y)
-valued functions, i. e. the limit in (
3.10
) is uniform over
x\in B_{X}
.

Proof.

Set
{\mathscr{B}}_{t}={\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}j_{n}\in\EuScriptBold{B}(X_{n}^{\prime},Y)
for all
t\in\Omega
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Next, we have

\left\|{\mathscr{B}}_{t}^{(n)}x^{\prime}\right\|_{Y}=\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}j_{n}x^{\prime}\|_{Y}=\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x^{\prime}\|_{Y}

for every
x^{\prime}\in X_{n}^{\prime}
, implying
{\mathscr{B}}\in L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X_{n}^{\prime},Y))
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Due to Proposition
2.4
, there exist
\mu
-simple
\EuScriptBold{B}(X_{n}^{\prime},Y)
-valued functions
{\mathscr{C}}^{(n)}
such that

\int_{\Omega}\left\|{\mathscr{B}}_{t}^{(n)}-{\mathscr{C}}^{(n)}_{t}\right\|d\mu(t)<\frac{1}{n},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Finally, let
\mathcal{S}_{t}^{(n)}={\mathscr{C}}^{(n)}_{t}\pi_{n}\in\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y)
for all
t\in\Omega
. Then, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we get

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}&\left\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\!-\!\mathcal{S}_{t}^{(n)}\!x\right\|_{Y}d\mu(t)\!=\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left\|\!\left(\!{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\!-\!{\mathscr{B}}_{t}^{(n)}\pi_{n}x\!\right)\!\!+\!\!\left(\!{\mathscr{B}}_{t}^{(n)}\pi_{n}x\!-\!{\mathscr{C}}^{(n)}_{t}\pi_{n}x\!\right)\!\right\|_{Y}d\mu(t)\\
&\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\left\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x-{\mathscr{B}}_{t}^{(n)}\pi_{n}x\right\|_{Y}d\mu(t)+\int_{\Omega}\left\|{\mathscr{B}}_{t}^{(n)}\pi_{n}x-{\mathscr{C}}^{(n)}_{t}\pi_{n}x\right\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)\\
&\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x-{\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}j_{n}\pi_{n}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)+\int_{\Omega}\left\|{\mathscr{B}}_{t}^{(n)}-{\mathscr{C}}^{(n)}_{t}\right\|\|\pi_{n}\|\|x\|_{X}\,d\mu(t)\\
&\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\left\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x-{\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}^{2}x\right\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)+M\cdot\|x\|_{X}\cdot\int_{\Omega}\left\|{\mathscr{B}}_{t}^{(n)}-{\mathscr{C}}^{(n)}_{t}\right\|d\mu(t)\\
&<\int_{\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x-{\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)+\frac{M\cdot\|x\|_{X}}{n},\qquad x\in X.\end{split}

(3.11)

For the proof of the first part of the theorem, it suffices to show that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}(I_{X}-P_{n})x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)=0,\qquad\forall x\in X.

(3.12)

Let us fix
\varepsilon>0
. By Theorem
3.5
, there is
\delta>0
such that
\|\chi_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}<\varepsilon
whenever
\mu(E)<\delta
, also implying that (as
\|P_{n}x\|_{X}\leqslant M\|x\|_{X}
)

\begin{split}\|\chi_{E}{\mathscr{A}}P_{n}\|_{1}&=\sup_{\|x\|_{X}\leqslant 1}\int_{E}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)\leqslant\sup_{\|x\|_{X}\leqslant M}\int_{E}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\|\,d\mu(t)\\
&=M\cdot\|\chi_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}<M\cdot\varepsilon,\qquad\mu(E)<\delta.\end{split}

(3.13)

Since
P_{n}\rightarrow I_{X}
strongly, the sequence of scalar functions
\varphi_{n}(t)=\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}(I_{X}-P_{n})x\|_{Y}
converges to
0
pointwise.
As
\|(I_{X}-P_{n})x\|_{X}\leqslant(M+1)\cdot\|x\|_{X}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\{\varphi_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq\{\|{\mathscr{A}}y\|_{Y}:\|y\|_{X}\leqslant(M+1)\cdot\|x\|_{X}\},

and Corollary
3.5
implies uniform integrability of
\{\varphi_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. As
X
has an F. D. D, it is separable, hence by Lemma
2.1
we can assume that
\mu
is
\sigma
-finite. Thus, there are
\Omega_{k}\in\mathfrak{M}
such that
\mu(\Omega_{k})<+\infty
and
\Omega=\bigsqcup_{k}\Omega_{k}
.
By Theorem
3.5
, there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|\chi_{\bigsqcup_{k>K}\Omega_{k}}{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}<\varepsilon
and by (
3.13
), we also have
\|\chi_{\bigsqcup_{k>K}\Omega_{k}}{\mathscr{A}}P_{n}\|_{1}<M\cdot\varepsilon
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Combining previous estimates we get

\int_{\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x-{\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)=\int_{\bigsqcup\limits_{k\leqslant K}\!\Omega_{k}}\|{\mathscr{A}}x-{\mathscr{A}}P_{n}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu+\\
+\int_{\bigsqcup\limits_{k>K}\!\Omega_{k}}\|{\mathscr{A}}x-{\mathscr{A}}P_{n}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu\leqslant\int_{\bigsqcup\limits_{k\leqslant K}\!\Omega_{k}}\|{\mathscr{A}}x-{\mathscr{A}}P_{n}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu+\varepsilon\|x\|_{X}+M\varepsilon\|x\|_{X},

for every
x\in X
. As
\bigsqcup_{k\leqslant K}\Omega_{k}
is a set of finite measure, uniform integrability of
\{\varphi_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
allows us to apply Vitali Convergence Theorem, yielding

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\bigsqcup_{k\leqslant K}\Omega_{k}}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x-{\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)=0

and finally by taking
\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}
in the above estimates we get

\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x-{\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)\leqslant(M+1)\varepsilon\|x\|_{X}.

As
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we obtain (
3.12
).

Next we discuss the case of compact operators. Since
(X_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is shrinking,
X^{*}
is separable and therefore
X
does not contain a copy of
\ell^{1}
. By taking the supremum over
x\in B_{X}
in (
3.11
) we obtain

\begin{split}\left\|{\mathscr{A}}-\mathcal{S}^{(n)}\right\|_{1}&\leqslant\sup_{\|x\|_{X}\leqslant 1}\int_{\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x-{\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)+\frac{M}{n}.\end{split}

(3.14)

We choose
\varepsilon>0
. By Theorem
3.5
, there is a
\delta>0
such that
\|\chi_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}<\varepsilon
whenever
\mu(E)<\delta
. As in the noncompact case, there is a set
F\in\mathfrak{M}
of finite measure such that
\|{\mathscr{A}}\chi_{\Omega\setminus F}\|_{1}<\varepsilon
. Moreover, by (
3.13
), the estimate
\|\chi_{\Omega\setminus F}{\mathscr{A}}P_{n}\|_{1}<M\cdot\varepsilon
, holds for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If we apply previous estimates to (
3.14
) we get

\displaystyle\!\!\!\left\|{\mathscr{A}}-\mathcal{S}^{(n)}\right\|_{1}\leqslant\sup_{\|x\|_{X}\leqslant 1}\int_{F}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x-{\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)

\displaystyle+\sup_{\|x\|_{X}\leqslant 1}\int_{\Omega\setminus F}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x-{\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)\!+\!\frac{M}{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{\|x\|_{X}\leqslant 1}\int_{F}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x-{\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)+\|{\mathscr{A}}P_{n}\chi_{\Omega\setminus F}\|_{1}+\|{\mathscr{A}}\chi_{\Omega\setminus F}\|_{1}+\frac{M}{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{\|x\|_{X}\leqslant 1}\int_{F}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x-{\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)+(M+1)\cdot\varepsilon+\frac{M}{n}.

(3.15)

Next, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
define
\mu
-measurable functions
\psi_{n}\colon\Omega\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
by

\psi_{n}(t)=\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}(I_{X}-P_{n})\|,\qquad t\in\Omega.

These function are
\mu
-measurable since
X
is separable.
As F. D. D.
(X_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is shrinking, we have that
P_{n}^{*}\rightarrow I_{X^{*}}
strongly. Thus, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\psi_{n}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|(I_{X^{*}}-P_{n}^{*}){\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{*}\|=0,

because
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\in\EuScriptBold{K}(Y^{*},X^{*})
for all
t\in\Omega
.
Thus, using Egorov’s theorem on a set
F
, there is a set
F_{\delta}\in\mathfrak{M}
such that
\mu(F_{\delta})<\delta
and
\psi_{n}\rightrightarrows 0
on
F\setminus F_{\delta}
. Finally, if we apply previous estimates to (
3.2
), for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

\begin{split}&\left\|{\mathscr{A}}-\mathcal{S}^{(n)}\right\|_{1}\leqslant\sup_{\|x\|_{X}\leqslant 1}\int_{F\setminus F_{\delta}}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x-{\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)\!\\
+&\sup_{\|x\|_{X}\leqslant 1}\int_{F_{\delta}}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x-{\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}x\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)+(M+1)\cdot\varepsilon+\!\frac{M}{n}\\
\leqslant&\sup_{\|x\|_{X}\leqslant 1}\int_{F\setminus F_{\delta}}\!\!\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x-{\mathscr{A}}_{t}P_{n}x\|_{Y}\,\!d\mu(t)\!+\!\|{\mathscr{A}}\chi_{F_{\delta}}\|_{1}\!+\!\|{\mathscr{A}}P_{n}\chi_{F_{\delta}}\|_{1}+(M+1)\cdot\varepsilon+\frac{M}{n}\\
\leqslant&\,\mu(F)\cdot\sup_{t\in F\setminus F_{\delta}}\psi_{n}(t)+2(M+1)\cdot\varepsilon+\frac{M}{n},\end{split}

where the last inequality follows from the (
3.13
) as
\mu(F_{\delta})<\delta
. Next, similarly to noncompact case, we get

\begin{split}\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\left\|{\mathscr{A}}-\mathcal{S}^{(n)}\right\|_{1}\leqslant 2(1+M)\cdot\varepsilon,\end{split}

completing the proof.

Remark 3.2
.

It is still possible to obtain a result if we drop assumption that
X
does not contain a copy of
\ell^{1}
, but in that case we have to impose a fairly strong condition:
\int_{\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|\,d\mu(t)<+\infty
. First, note that
\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|
is a measurable function due to separability of
X
.
Then, we can use Dominated Convergence Theorem in (
3.12
) with integrable majorant
g(t)=(1+M)\cdot\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|
.

In the case of compact operators, condition
\int_{\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|\,d\mu(t)<+\infty
implies, in view of Proposition
3.2
, that
{\mathscr{A}}
is in
L^{1}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{K}(X,Y))
.

Note that Theorem
3.7
does not give approximation in
L^{1}_{s}
norm, in general the approximation is not uniform over
x\in B_{X}
. Example
3.2
shows that one can not get approximation in
L^{1}_{s}
-norm by simple functions even if we restrict to Hilbert spaces (which certainly do not contain
\ell^{1}
) and additionally assume that
\int_{\Omega}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|\,d\mu(t)<+\infty
and
\mu(\Omega)<+\infty
. Of course, the operators appearing in Example
3.2
are not compact.

Example 3.2
.

We will define
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}_{s}([0,1],\EuScriptBold{B}(\ell^{2}))
which is not in the
L^{1}_{s}
-norm closure of the set
S
of simple functions.
Let
r_{n}:[0,1]\to\{-1,1\}
be the
n
-th Rademacher function, each
r_{n}
is
m
-measurable, takes alternatively values
\pm 1
on dyadic intervals of length
2^{-n}
.
Furhter, let
(e_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be the standard orthonormal basis of
\ell^{2}
.
Define, for every
0\leqslant t\leqslant 1
, a self-adjoint operator
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\in\EuScriptBold{B}(\ell^{2})
by

{\mathscr{A}}_{t}e_{n}=r_{n}(t)e_{n},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Equivalently, for
x=(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{2}
, we have
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x=(r_{n}(t)x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
.
Thus
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}
is a diagonal operator with diagonal entries
r_{n}(t)\in\{-1,1\}
, hence it is unitary and self-adjoint for all
0\leqslant t\leqslant 1
.
It is easily seen that
{\mathscr{A}}=({\mathscr{A}}_{t})_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}
is pointwise
m
-measurable. Since
\int_{0}^{1}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\|_{\ell^{2}}\,dm(t)=\|x\|_{\ell^{2}}
it is immediate that
{\mathscr{A}}
belongs to
L^{1}_{s}([0,1],\EuScriptBold{B}(\ell^{2}))
and in fact
\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}=1
. We prove that

\textup{dist}({\mathscr{A}},S)=1.

(3.16)

Since
\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}=1
clearly
\textup{dist}({\mathscr{A}},S)\leqslant 1
. Let
{\mathscr{B}}_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\chi_{A_{k}}(t)\,T_{k}

be an arbitrary
m
-simple o. v. function. This means the sets
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
are Lebesgue measurable of positive measure, pairwise disjoint, satisfy

[0,1]=\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}
and
T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}
are operators in
\EuScriptBold B(\ell^{2})
. Let us fix
\varepsilon>0
.
For
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\|({\mathscr{A}}_{t}-{\mathscr{B}}_{t})e_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}}=\|r_{n}(t)e_{n}-T_{k}e_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}},\qquad t\in A_{k}.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we can split
A_{k}
into two measurable parts

A_{k,n}^{+}:=A_{k}\cap\{t\in[0,1]:r_{n}(t)=1\},\,\,\,A_{k,n}^{-}:=A_{k}\cap\{t\in[0,1]:r_{n}(t)=-1\}.

Since for all
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
we have the equalities

\int_{A_{k}}r_{n}(t)\,dm(t)=m(A_{k,n}^{+})-m(A_{k,n}^{-}),\,\,\,m(A_{k,n}^{+})+m(A_{k,n}^{-})=m(A_{k}),

and
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int_{A_{k}}r_{n}(t)\,dm(t)=0
for every
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, it follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}m(A_{k,n}^{+})=\frac{m(A_{k})}{2},\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}m(A_{k,n}^{-})=\frac{m(A_{k})}{2},\qquad k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}.

Hence, there is
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

m(A_{k,N}^{+}),m(A_{k,N}^{-})>\frac{m(A_{k})}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2m},\qquad k\in\{1,\ldots,m\},

For such an
N
, we estimate:

\int_{A_{k}}\|({\mathscr{A}}_{t}-{\mathscr{B}}_{t})e_{N}\|_{\ell^{2}}\,dm(t)=\|e_{N}-T_{k}e_{N}\|_{\ell^{2}}\cdot m(A_{k,N}^{+})+\|-e_{N}-T_{k}e_{N}\|_{\ell^{2}}\cdot m(A_{k,N}^{-})\\
>\bigl(\|e_{N}-T_{k}e_{N}\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|-e_{N}-T_{k}e_{N}\|_{\ell^{2}}\bigr)\cdot\left(\frac{m(A_{k})}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2m}\right)\geqslant m(A_{k})-\frac{\varepsilon}{m},

where we used the fact that

\|e_{n}-T_{k}e_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|-e_{n}-T_{k}e_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}}\geqslant\|e_{n}-(-e_{n})\|_{\ell^{2}}=2.

Summing over
k=1,\dots,m
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]}\|({\mathscr{A}}_{t}-{\mathscr{B}}_{t})e_{N}\|_{\ell^{2}}\,dm(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{A_{k}}\|({\mathscr{A}}_{t}-{\mathscr{B}}_{t})e_{N}\|_{\ell^{2}}\,dm(t)

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(m(A_{k})-\frac{\varepsilon}{m}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}m(A_{k})-\varepsilon=1-\varepsilon.

Since
\|e_{N}\|_{\ell^{2}}=1
, it follows that

\|{\mathscr{A}}-{\mathscr{B}}\|_{1}\geqslant\int_{[0,1]}\|({\mathscr{A}}_{t}-{\mathscr{B}}_{t})e_{N}\|_{\ell^{2}}\,dm(t)\geqslant 1-\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we conclude that

\|{\mathscr{A}}-{\mathscr{B}}\|_{1}\geqslant 1
.

\triangle

We point out another property of the family
{\mathscr{A}}
from the above example.
Namely, the set
\{\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu:E\subset[0,1]\;\mbox{is measurable}\}
is not relatively compact subset of
\EuScriptBold B(\ell^{2})
. To see this, consider
T_{n}=\int_{E_{n}}{\mathscr{A}}\,dm
where

E_{n}=\bigsqcup_{j=0}^{2^{n-1}-1}\left[\frac{2j}{2^{n}},\frac{2j+1}{2^{n}}\right],\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then one checks, using definition of Rademacher’s functions, that
T_{n}=2^{-1}e_{n}\otimes e_{n}^{*}
and therefore
\|T_{n}-T_{m}\|=1/2
whenever
n\not=m
and this suffices.

In view of Proposition
2.2
, this shows that
{\mathscr{A}}
can not be approximated by simple functions, however without delivering sharp estimate (
3.16
).

Remark 3.3
.

The point in the above example is that the operators
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}
oscillate independently in infinitely many coordinates, which is provided by Rademacher functions. A simple function involves only finitely many operator values, so it cannot capture this infinite oscillatory behavior in the
L^{1}_{s}
-norm.

3.3.
Compactness results

Compactness properties of Gelfand integrals were studied for the first time in
[
18
]
, where operators considered are assumed to belong to certain ideals of compact operators, see also
[
20
]
. Banach space case was considered in a recent paper
[
4
]
, see also
[
21
]
where integration of functions whose values are
(q,p)
-summing operators was investigated. Here we deal with Banach space case and use tensor products and duality as tools.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
X
be a Banach space that does not contain a copy of
\ell^{1}
and let
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
. Assume
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\in\EuScriptBold{K}(X,Y)
for a. e.
t\in\Omega
. Then,
\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\in\EuScriptBold{K}(X,Y)
for every
E\in\mathfrak{M}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.6
we can assume that
(\Omega,\mu)
is
\sigma
-finite measure space. We begin with the case
\mu(\Omega)<+\infty
. As
X
does not contain a copy of
\ell^{1}
, by Proposition
1.1
it suffices to show that
\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu
is completely continuous. Thus, let
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence in
X
that weakly converges to
0
. As
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}
is completely continuous we have
\varphi_{n}(t):=\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n}\|_{Y}\rightarrow 0
as
n\to\infty
for a. e.
t\in\Omega
. Moreover, since
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|x_{n}\|_{X}<+\infty
, the family
\{\varphi_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is uniformly integrable due to Corollary
3.5
yielding

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)x_{n}\right\|_{Y}\leqslant\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{E}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n}\|_{Y}\,d\mu(t)=0,

by the Vitali’s Convergence Theorem, proving that
\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu\in\EuScriptBold{K}(X,Y)
.

Next, let
\mu(E)=+\infty
. As
X^{*}
does not contain a copy of
\mathfrak{c}_{0}
(see, for example
[
11
, Theorem 10, Page 48]
), o. v. measure
\nu_{\mathscr{A}}
is countably additive by Theorem
3.3
. Since
\mu
is
\sigma
-finite there is a decomposition
E=\bigsqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}
where
\mu(E_{n})<+\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Finally, as
\nu_{\mathscr{A}}(E)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\nu_{\mathscr{A}}(E_{n})
, where the series is norm convergent and every summand is a compact operator, it follows that
\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu=\nu_{\mathscr{A}}(E)
is a compact operator.∎

The condition on
X
in Theorem
3.8
is essential, even in the case of finite measure. In fact, we take
X=Y=\ell^{1}
,
\Omega=\mathbb{N}
,
\mu(\{n\})=2^{-n}
and
{\mathscr{A}}_{n}=2^{n}e_{n}\otimes e_{n}^{*}
. Then we have
\mu(\Omega)=1
, and the generated o. v. measure is not countably additive and the integral over
\Omega
is not compact although
{\mathscr{A}}_{n}
is compact for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover these operators commute.

If in addition
X=Y=\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space we get
{\EuScriptBold C}_{\infty}({\mathcal{H}})
-Pettis integrability.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}\in L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H}))
be such that
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\in{\EuScriptBold C}_{\infty}({\mathcal{H}})
for all
t\in\Omega
. Then
{\mathscr{A}}\in\mathbb{P}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,{\EuScriptBold C}_{\infty}({\mathcal{H}}))
.

Proof.

As
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space, it does not contain
\ell^{1}
, thus
\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu\in{\EuScriptBold C}_{\infty}({\mathcal{H}})
for all
E\in\mathfrak{M}
due to Theorem
3.8
. Strong integrability of
{\mathscr{A}}
implies weak
∗

\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H})
-integrability which is the same as weak
{\EuScriptBold C}_{\infty}({\mathcal{H}})
-integrability. Therefore,
{\mathscr{A}}
is
{\EuScriptBold C}_{\infty}({\mathcal{H}})
-Pettis integrable.
∎

The converse of the previous Corollary does not hold, even in the case of finite measure space. Namely, there is a
{\EuScriptBold C}_{\infty}({\mathcal{H}})
-valued Pettis integrable function
{\mathscr{A}}
that is not strongly integrable, where the operators
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}
are positive and mutually commuting.

Example 3.3
.

Let
(e_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an o. n. b. of
\mathcal{H}
and define
{\mathscr{A}}\colon[0,1]\rightarrow\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H})
by

{\mathscr{A}}_{t}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{\frac{3}{2}}\chi_{\left(\frac{1}{n+1},\frac{1}{n}\right)}(t)\,e_{n}\otimes e_{n}^{*},\qquad t\in[0,1].

Since for each
0\leqslant t\leqslant 1
the above sum reduces to one summand each operator
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}
is compact, since they are diagonalized by the same o. n. b. they commute and clearly they are positive.
It is easy to verify that
{\mathscr{A}}\in\mathbb{G}([0,1],m,\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H}))
and

\int_{[0,1]}{\mathscr{A}}\,dm=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{n}}{n+1}\cdot e_{n}\otimes e_{n}^{*}\in{\EuScriptBold C}_{\infty}({\mathcal{H}}).

It follows from the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 2.3, (b)]
that the family
{\mathscr{A}}
belongs to
\mathbb{P}([0,1],m,{\EuScriptBold C}_{\infty}({\mathcal{H}}))
.
Let us prove that the family
{\mathscr{A}}
is not in
L_{s}^{1}([0,1],m,\EuScriptBold{B}(\mathcal{H}))
. To prove this, note that for all
x\in\mathcal{H}

we have the equalities

\begin{split}\int_{[0,1]}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\|_{\mathcal{H}}\,dm(t)&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\left(\frac{1}{n+1},\frac{1}{n}\right)}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\|_{\mathcal{H}}\,dm(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\frac{1}{n+1}}^{\frac{1}{n}}\left\|n^{\frac{3}{2}}(e_{n}\otimes e_{n}^{*})x\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}dt\\
=&\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\frac{1}{n+1}}^{\frac{1}{n}}n^{\frac{3}{2}}\cdot|\langle x,e_{n}\rangle|\,dt=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{n}}{n+1}\cdot|\langle x,e_{n}\rangle|.\end{split}

Since
\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{n+1}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}\notin\ell^{2}
, the above sum can not be finite for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
.

Now, we will generalize the result in Corollary
3.7
to some classes of Banach spaces that are not necessarily Hilbert. We will focus on the case where the dual of
\EuScriptBold{K}(X,Y)
is representable as a projective tensor product. The following proposition gives sufficient conditions for such representation to be valid. It can be found in
[
10
, 16.7]
. For more details about approximation property and Radon-Nikodym property see, for example
[
12
]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume
X
and
Y
are Banach spaces such that
X^{**}
or
Y^{*}
has approximation property and
X^{**}
or
Y^{*}
has the Radon-Nikodym property. Then,
(\EuScriptBold{K}(X,Y))^{*}\cong X^{**}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y^{*}
, where the duality pairing is given by the expression

\left\langle A,T\right\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle A^{*}y_{n}^{*},x_{n}^{**}\right\rangle
for
A\in\EuScriptBold{K}(X,Y)
and
T=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x^{**}_{n}\otimes y^{*}_{n}\in X^{**}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y^{*}

where
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|x_{n}^{**}\|_{X^{**}}\cdot\|y^{*}_{n}\|_{Y^{*}}<+\infty
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
, where
X
is separable and does not contain
\ell^{1}
. Then, for all
x^{**}\in B_{X^{**}}
and
y^{*}\in Y^{*}
, the function
\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}^{*}y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle
is integrable and and the following equality holds

\sup_{\|x^{**}\|_{X^{**}}\leqslant 1}\int_{\Omega}|\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}^{*}_{t}y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle|\,d\mu(t)=\sup_{\|x\|_{X}\leqslant 1}\int_{\Omega}|\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y^{*}\right\rangle|\,d\mu(t).

(3.17)

Proof.

Let
(x^{**},y^{*})\in B_{X^{**}}\times Y^{*}
and select an approximating sequence
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
as in Theorem
1.4
. Since

\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{*}y^{*},x^{**}\rangle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle x_{n},{\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{*}y^{*}\rangle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y^{*}\rangle
for all
t\in\Omega
, the function
\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}^{*}y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle
is
\mu
-measurable as the pointwise limit of
\mu
-measurable functions
\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}x_{n},y^{*}\right\rangle
. Moreover, using Fatou lemma we get

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}|\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}^{*}_{t}y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle|\,d\mu(t)=\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\left\langle x_{n},{\mathscr{A}}^{*}_{t}y^{*}\right\rangle|\,d\mu(t)\leqslant\varliminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\left\langle x_{n},{\mathscr{A}}^{*}_{t}y^{*}\right\rangle|\,d\mu(t)\\
&\leqslant\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{\Omega}|\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y^{*}\right\rangle|d\mu(t)\leqslant\sup_{\|x\|_{X}\leqslant 1}\int_{\Omega}|\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y^{*}\right\rangle|\,d\mu(t)\leqslant\|y^{*}\|_{Y^{*}}\cdot\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}<\infty,\end{split}

proving the nontrivial inequality needed to establish (
3.17
).
∎

The next lemma is also a technical result which, under appropriate conditions, gives an expression for
\left\langle\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}^{*}d\mu\right)y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
, where
X
is separable and does not contain
\ell^{1}
. Then,
\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}^{*}y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)
for all
(x^{**},y^{*})\in X^{**}\times Y^{*}
and

\left\langle\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}^{*}d\mu\right)y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle=\int_{E}\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}^{*}_{t}y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle d\mu(t),\qquad E\in\mathfrak{M}.

Proof.

The existence of
\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}^{*}d\mu
is provided by Lemma
2.4
. The functions

{\mathscr{A}}^{*}
and
{\mathscr{A}}
have the same support, hence by Lemma
2.1
we can assume
\mu
is a
\sigma
-finite measure. Assume
\mu(E)<+\infty

and let
(x^{**},y^{*})\in X^{**}\times Y^{*}
. Then, we have

\begin{split}\left\langle\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}^{*}\,d\mu\right)y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\langle x_{n},\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}^{*}\,d\mu\right)y^{*}\right\rangle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{E}\left\langle x_{n},{\mathscr{A}}^{*}_{t}y^{*}\right\rangle d\mu(t)\\
&=\int_{E}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\langle x_{n},{\mathscr{A}}^{*}_{t}y^{*}\right\rangle\,d\mu(t)=\int_{E}\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}^{*}_{t}y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle d\mu(t),\end{split}

where the interchange of limit and integral is provided by Vitali’s Convergence Theorem. Indeed, the sequence of functions
\psi_{n}(t)=\left\langle x_{n},{\mathscr{A}}^{*}_{t}y^{*}\right\rangle=\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y^{*}\right\rangle
is uniformly integrable in
L^{1}(E,\mu)
, because
|\psi_{n}(t)|\leqslant\varphi_{n}(t)=\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n}\|_{Y}
and the family
\{\varphi_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is uniformly integrable in
L^{1}(E,\mu)
, due to Corollary
3.5
.

If
\mu(E)=+\infty
we have
E=\bigsqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}
, where
\mu(E_{n})<+\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\begin{split}&\left\langle\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}^{*}\,d\mu\right)y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle=\left\langle(\nu_{{\mathscr{A}}^{*}}(E))y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle=\left\langle\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\nu_{{\mathscr{A}}^{*}}(E_{n})\right)y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle\\
&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle(\nu_{{\mathscr{A}}^{*}}(E_{n}))y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\left(\int_{E_{n}}{\mathscr{A}}^{*}\,d\mu\right)y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle\\
&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{n}}\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}^{*}_{t}y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle\,d\mu(t)=\int_{E}\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}^{*}_{t}y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle d\mu(t),\end{split}

where the second and third equality follow from Corollary
3.3
. The fifth equality holds due to already proven part of the Lemma in the finite measure case. The function
\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}^{*}y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle
belongs to the space
L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)
, due to the Lemma
3.1
, which allows us to interchange infinite sum and Lebesgue integral in the last step of the above computation.
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

Assume
X
and
Y
are Banach spaces such that
X^{**}
or
Y^{*}
has approximation property and
X^{**}
or
Y^{*}
has the Radon-Nikodym property. In addition, assume
X
is separable and does not contain a copy of
\ell^{1}
. If
{\mathscr{A}}
belongs to
L^{1}_{s}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X,Y))
and
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\in\EuScriptBold K(X,Y)
for a. e.
t\in\Omega
then
{\mathscr{A}}\in\mathbb{P}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold K(X,Y))
and Pettis norm of
{\mathscr{A}}
is equal to

\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{\mathbb{P}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|x\|_{X}\leqslant 1\\
\|y^{*}\|_{Y^{*}}\leqslant 1\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}|\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y^{*}\right\rangle|\,d\mu(t).

(3.18)

Proof.

For arbitrary
T
in
X^{**}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y^{*}
there is a representation
T=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}^{**}\otimes y_{n}^{*}
with
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|x_{n}^{**}\|_{X^{**}}\cdot\|y^{*}_{n}\|_{Y^{*}}<+\infty
. Then, using Lemma
3.1
, we get

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t},T\right\rangle|\,d\mu(t)&=\int_{\Omega}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{*}y_{n}^{*},x_{n}^{**}\right\rangle\right|d\mu(t)\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{*}y_{n}^{*},x_{n}^{**}\right\rangle|\,d\mu(t)\\
&\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|x_{n}^{**}\|_{X^{**}}\sup_{\|x^{**}\|_{X^{**}}\leqslant 1}\int_{\Omega}|\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{*}y_{n}^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle|\,d\mu(t)\\
&\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|x_{n}^{**}\|_{X^{**}}\sup_{\|x\|_{X}\leqslant 1}\int_{\Omega}|\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y_{n}^{*}\right\rangle|\,d\mu(t)\\
&\leqslant\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|x_{n}^{**}\|_{X^{**}}\|y^{*}_{n}\|_{Y^{*}}<+\infty,\end{split}

and this proves weak integrability of
{\mathscr{A}}
. Since
X
is separable, support of
{\mathscr{A}}
has
\sigma
-finite measure, by Lemma
2.1
. Thus, for every
E\in\mathfrak{M}
the operator
\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu
(in
L_{s}^{1}
sense) is compact, due to Theorem
3.8
. In order to prove that this
L^{1}_{s}
-integral is also Pettis integral we choose

T=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}^{**}\otimes y_{n}^{*}
in
X^{**}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y^{*}
and, using Proposition
3.3
and Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

\left\langle\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu,T\right\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)^{*}y_{n}^{*},x_{n}^{**}\right\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}^{*}\,d\mu\right)y_{n}^{*},x_{n}^{**}\right\rangle\\
=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{E}\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}^{*}_{t}y^{*}_{n},x^{**}_{n}\right\rangle d\mu(t)=\int_{E}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}^{*}_{t}y^{*}_{n},x^{**}_{n}\right\rangle d\mu(t)=\int_{E}\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t},T\right\rangle d\mu(t).

Hence,
{\mathscr{A}}\in\mathbb{P}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold{K}(X,Y))
.

Proof of formula (
3.18
) follows from the following equalities:

\begin{split}\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{\mathbb{P}}&=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}|\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t},T\rangle|\,d\mu(t)\,:\,\|T\|_{X^{**}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y^{*}}\leqslant 1\right\}\\
&=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|x^{**}\|_{X^{**}}\leqslant 1\\
\|y^{*}\|_{Y^{*}}\leqslant 1\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}|\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}^{*}_{t}y^{*},x^{**}\right\rangle|\,d\mu(t)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|x\|_{X}\leqslant 1\\
\|y^{*}\|_{Y^{*}}\leqslant 1\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}|\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y^{*}\right\rangle|\,d\mu(t).\end{split}

(3.19)

The first equality in (
3.19
) is definition of the Pettis norm
\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{\mathbb{P}}
of the function
{\mathscr{A}}:\Omega\to\EuScriptBold{K}(X,Y)
, see (
1.4
), proof of the second one is analogous to the proof of
[
21
, Proposition 1.12]
. The last equality in (
3.19
) follows from Lemma
3.1
.
∎

4.
Subadditivity of the spectral radius for strongly integrable commuting families

It is well known that for a commuting finite family
(A_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
in
\EuScriptBold{B}(X)
we have:

r\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}\right)\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{n}r(A_{j}).

This is spectral radius inequality.
Commutativity assumption is essential, the above is not true in general even for
n=2
and
\dim X=2
. The above inequality remains true if we have a commuting infinite family of operators
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}
converges in the norm of
\EuScriptBold{B}(X)
:

Lemma 4.1
.

[
25
, Lemma 2.7]

If
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}
is a norm convergent sum of mutually commuting operators in
\EuScriptBold{B}(X)
, then

r\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\right)\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r(A_{n}).

In the case of counting measure
\mu
on
\Omega=\mathbb{N}
strong integral
\int_{\mathbb{N}}{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\,d\mu(n)
is the strong (absolutely convergent) sum
\prescript{\raisebox{-6.45831pt}{$\scriptstyle s$}}{}{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}}{\mathscr{A}}_{n}
of the sequence
({\mathscr{A}}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of operators in
\EuScriptBold B(X)
. The above lemma and Theorem
3.1
give us the following corollary.

Corollary 4.1
.

If
X^{*}
does not contain
\mathfrak{c}_{0}
and
{\mathscr{A}}=({\mathscr{A}}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a commuting family in
\ell_{s}^{1}(\EuScriptBold B(X))
then

r\left({\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}}{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\right)\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r({\mathscr{A}}_{n}).

(4.1)

In the case of strong sums, without condition
{\mathscr{A}}\in\ell_{s}^{1}(\EuScriptBold B(X))
we can not obtain inequality (
4.1
), as the next example shows.

Example 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(0,1)
and let
V
be the classical Volterra operator, i. e.

Vf(x)=\int_{(0,x)}f(t)\,dm(t),\qquad\,0<x<1.

For all non negative integers
n
we set

{\mathscr{B}}_{n}=I-(I+nV)^{-1}=nV(I+nV)^{-1},\qquad{\mathscr{A}}_{n}={\mathscr{B}}_{n}-{\mathscr{B}}_{n-1}.

Note that the family
{\mathscr{A}}=({\mathscr{A}}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
consists of Volterra operators which commute with each other. We claim that

\prescript{\raisebox{-7.3194pt}{$\scriptstyle s$}}{}{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}}{\mathscr{A}}_{n}=I.

(4.2)

Clearly,
{\mathscr{B}}_{n}
is the
n
-th partial sums of the above series and therefore (
4.2
) is equivalent to strong convergence of

{\mathscr{C}}_{n}=I-{\mathscr{B}}_{n}=(I+nV)^{-1}

to the zero operator. Since the equality
{\mathscr{C}}_{n}f=g
is equivalent to

f(t)=g(t)+n\int_{(0,t)}g(s)\,dm(s),\qquad 0<t<1,

one obtains, solving a Volterra integral equation of the second type, the following formula for
{\mathscr{C}}_{n}
:

{\mathscr{C}}_{n}f(t)=f(t)-n\int_{(0,t)}e^{-n(t-s)}f(s)\,dm(s),\qquad f\in L^{2}(0,1).

On the right hand side there is a convolution of
f
with a positive function
\varphi_{n}
, where
\varphi_{n}(t)=n\varphi_{1}(nt)
and

\varphi_{1}(t)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if}\ t<0,\\
e^{-t},&\text{if}\,\,t\geqslant 0.\end{cases}

Since
\|\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{1}}=1
,
(\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is an approximation of identity. Hence, we have
L^{2}
convergence of the corresponding integrals to
f
. Thus
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\mathscr{C}}_{n}f\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}=0
for all
f\in L^{2}(0,1)
i. e. the sequence of operators
({\mathscr{C}}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
strongly converges to zero and hence we have proven (
4.2
). Now, we have

1=r(I)=r\left(\prescript{\raisebox{-6.45831pt}{$\scriptstyle s$}}{}{\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}}{\mathscr{A}}_{n}\right)>\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r({\mathscr{A}}_{n})=0,

since
r({\mathscr{A}}_{n})=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Indeed, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

r({\mathscr{A}}_{n})\leqslant r((I+nV)^{-1})\cdot r(V)\cdot r((I+(n-1)V)^{-1})=0,

due to the mentioned commutativity and
r(V)=0
.

\triangle

It is natural to ask if there is analogous statement for integrals instead of sums of operators. An affirmative answer was given in
[
25
]
, where it was proved that

r\left(\int_{\Omega}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)\leqslant\int_{\Omega}r({\mathscr{A}}_{t})\,d\mu(t),\qquad{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold{B}(X)).

We note that the argument presented in
[
25
]
shows that the above inequality holds for Bochner integral of a commuting family of elements of a Banach algebra.

In this section we extend this inequality from the class of Bochner integrable functions to a subclass of strongly integrable functions, see Theorem
4.1
below.

We start with a simple lemma on commutativity of strong integrals.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}},{\mathscr{B}}\in L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X))
and assume
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}{\mathscr{B}}_{s}={\mathscr{B}}_{s}{\mathscr{A}}_{t}

for
\mu
-almost all
t,s\in\Omega
. Then, for all
E,F\in\mathfrak{M}
we have

\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\int_{F}{\mathscr{B}}\,d\mu=\int_{F}{\mathscr{B}}\,d\mu\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu.

Proof.

Since
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
belong to
L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X))
, we can define operators

A:=\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\in\EuScriptBold B(X)\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,B:=\int_{F}{\mathscr{B}}\,d\mu\in\EuScriptBold B(X).

For
\mu
-almost all
t\in\Omega
we have the equality
A{\mathscr{B}}_{t}={\mathscr{B}}_{t}A
. Indeed, for fixed
t\in\Omega
, using
[
4
, Proposition 2.25]
we have

\begin{split}A{\mathscr{B}}_{t}&=\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right){\mathscr{B}}_{t}=\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}_{s}{\mathscr{B}}_{t}\,d\mu(s)\\
&=\int_{E}{\mathscr{B}}_{t}{\mathscr{A}}_{s}\,d\mu(s)={\mathscr{B}}_{t}\left(\int_{E}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)={\mathscr{B}}_{t}A.\end{split}

Using this equality and again
[
4
, Proposition 2.25]
we obtain:

\begin{split}AB=A\left(\int_{F}{\mathscr{B}}\,d\mu\right)=\int_{F}A{\mathscr{B}}_{t}\,d\mu(t)=\int_{F}{\mathscr{B}}_{t}A\,d\mu(t)=\left(\int_{F}{\mathscr{B}}\,d\mu\right)A=BA,\end{split}

and this completes the proof.
∎

The above lemma can also be proved for families in
L_{w}^{1}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X))
using the same arguments, provided
X
is reflexive. Indeed, under this assumption, the operators
A
and
B
appearing in the above proof belong to
\EuScriptBold B(X)
.

We need some measurability results and we start with the following observation: if
{\mathscr{A}}\in\mathcal{M}_{w}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(Y,Z))
and
(y_{t})_{t\in\Omega}
is a strongly
\mu
-measurable family in
Y
, then
({\mathscr{A}}_{t}y_{t})_{t\in\Omega}
is a weakly
\mu
-measurable family in
Z
.
To see that, choose a sequence of
\mu
-simple functions
s_{n}:\Omega\to Y
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{n}(t)=y_{t}
for
\mu
-almost all
t\in\Omega
. Then, for every
z^{*}\in Z^{*}
, we have
\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}y_{t},z^{*}\rangle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}s_{n}(t),z^{*}\rangle
. Since the scalar function
\Omega\ni t\mapsto\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}s_{n}(t),z^{*}\rangle\in\mathbb{C}
is
\mu
-measurable for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, our claim is proven.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}^{(1)}\in\mathcal{M}_{w}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X))
and
{\mathscr{A}}^{(j)}\in\mathcal{M}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X))
for
j\in\{2,\ldots,n\}
.
Then
{\mathscr{A}}^{(1)}{\mathscr{A}}^{(2)}\cdots{\mathscr{A}}^{(n)}\in\mathcal{M}_{w}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X))
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the statement for
n=2
, then the proof is completed by an inductive argument. So we assume
n=2
and fix
x\in X
. Then weak
\mu
-measurability of
{\mathscr{A}}^{(1)}{\mathscr{A}}^{(2)}x
follows from the above observation applied to the family
y_{t}={\mathscr{A}}^{(2)}_{t}x
.
∎

In the preceding part of this section, separability of the Banach space X was not required. However, for the following statement, which partially solves Problem 2.11 from
[
25
]
, this assumption will be needed

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume
X
is separable and
{\mathscr{A}}\in\mathcal{M}_{w}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X))
. Then the function
r_{\mathscr{A}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
r_{\mathscr{A}}(t)=r({\mathscr{A}}_{t})
is
\mu
-measurable.

Proof.

Since
X
is separable we have the equality (see (
1.6
))

\mathcal{M}_{w}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X))=\mathcal{M}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X))

and thus the above lemma gives

{\mathscr{A}}^{n}=({\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{n})_{t\in\Omega}\in\mathcal{M}_{s}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X)).

Hence, using again separability of
X
,

\Omega\ni t\mapsto\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{n}\|^{1/n}\in\mathbb{R}
is measurable for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
(see comments after the equality (
1.6
)), and this suffices since the equality
r({\mathscr{A}}_{t})=\inf\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{n}\|^{1/n}
holds for all
t\in\Omega
.
∎

Strictly speaking, Lemma
4.4
is not necessary for the proof of Theorem
4.1
, because
\mu
-measurability of
\Omega\ni t\mapsto r({\mathscr{A}}_{t})\in\mathbb{R}
in Theorem
4.1
can be deduced from assumed strong
\mu
-measurability of the family
{\mathscr{A}}
.

Now, we present a generalization of the main result from
[
25
]

for a strongly
\mu
-measurable family
{\mathscr{A}}\in L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X))
which consists of mutually commuting operators
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume
X^{*}
does not contain an isomorphic copy of
\mathfrak{c}_{0}
and let
{\mathscr{A}}\in L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X))
be a strongly
\mu
-measurable family of mutually commuting operators. Then, we have the inequality

r\left(\int_{\Omega}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)\leqslant\int_{\Omega}r({\mathscr{A}}_{t})\,d\mu(t).

Proof.

We assume at first that
\mu(\Omega)<+\infty
. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
define the set

\Omega_{n}=\{t\in\Omega:\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|\in[n,n+1)\}.

Assumed strong
\mu
-measurability of
{\mathscr{A}}
implies
\mu
-measurability of the real valued function
\Omega\ni t\mapsto\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence the sets
\Omega_{n}
are
\mu
-measurable. Clearly, we have the equality
\Omega=\bigsqcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\Omega_{n}
. Since

\int_{\Omega_{n}}\|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\|\,d\mu(t)\leqslant(n+1)\cdot\mu(\Omega_{n})<+\infty,

we conclude
{\mathscr{A}}\in L^{1}(\Omega_{n},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X))
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
(there we again use assumed strong
\mu
-measurability of
{\mathscr{A}}
).
Now, we have the next calculation:

\begin{split}r\left(\int_{\Omega}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)&=r(\nu_{\mathscr{A}}(\Omega))=r\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\nu_{\mathscr{A}}(\Omega_{n})\right)\leqslant\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r\left(\int_{\Omega_{n}}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)\\
&\leqslant\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{n}}r({\mathscr{A}}_{t})\,d\mu(t)=\int_{\Omega}r({\mathscr{A}}_{t})\,d\mu(t).\end{split}

(4.3)

The second equality in (
4.3
) is based on Theorem
3.3
, which tells us that
\nu_{\mathscr{A}}
is a countably additive
\EuScriptBold B(X)
-valued measure.
The first inequality in (
4.3
) follows from Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.1
.
For the second inequality in (
4.3
) we apply
[
25
, Theorem 2.8]
, and here we use assumed strong
\mu
-measurability of
{\mathscr{A}}
to conclude Bochner integrability of
{\mathscr{A}}
on each set
\Omega_{n}
.

Now we consider the general case. Since
{\mathscr{A}}
has
\sigma
-finite support
we can assume
\Omega=\bigsqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{n}
, where
\mu(\Omega_{n})<+\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, by already proved part of theorem for finite measures we have the inequality

r\left(\int_{\Omega_{n}}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)\leqslant\int_{\Omega_{n}}r({\mathscr{A}}_{t})\,d\mu(t),\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Now, similarly to the calculations in (
4.3
), we get

r\left(\int_{\Omega}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{n}}r({\mathscr{A}}_{t})\,d\mu(t)=\int_{\Omega}r({\mathscr{A}}_{t})\,d\mu(t).\qed

In the next statement we again use the F. D. D. property to derive strong
\mu
-measurability of o. v. function
{\mathscr{A}}
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Assume
X
has a shrinking F. D. D.,
{\mathscr{A}}\in L_{s}^{1}(\Omega,\mu,\EuScriptBold B(X))
is a commuting family of compact operators. Then, we have the following inequality

r\left(\int_{\Omega}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu\right)\leqslant\int_{\Omega}r({\mathscr{A}}_{t})\,d\mu(t).

Moreover, if
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}
are Volterra operators, so is
\int_{\Omega}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
we get that
{\mathscr{A}}:\Omega\to\EuScriptBold B(X)
is strongly
\mu
-measurable function. Furthermore, as
X^{*}
is separable (because
X
has a shrinking F. D. D.) it cannot contain an isomorphic copy of
\ell^{\infty}
and thus cannot contain an isomorphic copy of
\mathfrak{c}_{0}
, due to
[
12
, Corollary 6, Page 23]
.
Final conclusion now follows from Theorem
4.1
. If
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}
is a Volterra operator for all
t\in\Omega
, then due to proved inequality we have that the operator
\int_{\Omega}{\mathscr{A}}\,d\mu
is Volterra too.
∎

Concluding remarks: It is not known to the authors whether the strong
\mu
-measurability of the family
{\mathscr{A}}
in Theorem
4.1
can be replaced by a weaker assumption. We also note that Lemma
4.4
provides alternative conditions for
\mu
-measurability of function
\Omega\ni t\mapsto r({\mathscr{A}}_{t})\in\mathbb{R}
.
