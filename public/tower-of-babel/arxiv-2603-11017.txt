Oxygenated False Positive Biosignatures in Mars-like Exoplanet Atmospheres Journal: ApJL
Margaret Turcotte Seavey, Shawn Domagal-Goldman, Amber Young, Jaime Crouse, Jacob Lustig-Yaeger, Giada Arney

Abstract
Oxygen is a well-studied biosignature. Studying potential abiotic pathways for O 2 \text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} build-up in exoplanet atmospheres is essential for evaluating whether the detection of O 2 \text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} would constitute a biosignature detection on other worlds. Previous modeling efforts in the literature demonstrated that detectable abiotic O 2 \text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} and O 3 \text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} can be produced through CO 2 \text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} photolysis for rocky planets around M dwarf stars. Building on modeling approaches from previous studies, we use photochemical simulations to reassess the conditions under which O 2 \text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} and O 3 \text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} may accumulate through similar photochemical mechanisms. Using a Mars-like atmospheric composition and planetary parameters, we vary the hydrogen mole fraction to assess how changes in HO x chemistry can affect the resulting accumulation of abiotic O 2 \text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} and O 3 \text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} . Across the range of hydrogen mole fractions explored, we obtain a maximum O 2 \text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} abundance of ∼ \sim 2.7% for H = 0.0065 ppm, about an order of magnitude lower than reported in the literature. This reduction is consistent with the elevated water vapor abundance adopted in our simulations, which enhances HO x-driven recycling of CO and O and thereby suppresses the accumulation of O 2 \text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} and O 3 \text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} . Our improved understanding of how this cycle results in atmospheric false positive biosignatures is crucial towards developing future exoplanet characterization strategies.

I
Introduction

M dwarf stars are the most abundant stellar type in our galaxy, which makes them a key population for studies to detect and characterize exoplanets. Population-level studies with transit and radial velocity methods show that terrestrial planets occur in roughly 50% of such planetary systems
(
X. Bonfils et al. 2013
;
G. D. Mulders et al. 2015
;
C. D. Dressing & D. Charbonneau 2015
;
S. Sabotta et al. 2021
;
M. Pinamonti et al. 2022
;
L. Mignon et al. 2025
;
A. Kaminski et al. 2025
)
, which make studying M dwarf systems in greater detail fundamental to understanding the variety of atmospheric compositions that could exist on such worlds. Chemical species like
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
are key diagnostic gases in the search for life because of the biological production of
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
from photosynthesis on Earth, and the consequent photochemical production of
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
. However,
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
photolysis can be a mechanism for abiotic production of
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
allowing for substantial accumulation of both species constituting a potential false-positive for life detection. Past studies have shown that
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
-rich atmospheres with little or no
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
can accumulate
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}

(
F. Tian et al. 2014
;
S. D. Domagal-Goldman et al. 2014
;
C. E. Harman et al. 2015
;
R. Hu et al. 2020
;
S. Ranjan et al. 2023
)
. However,
C. E. Harman et al. 2018
found that
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
does not accumulate when
NO
produced by lightning catalyzes the recombination of
CO
and
O
from
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
photolysis.

The results of
P. Gao et al. 2015
are seminal in the study of oxygen false positive biosignatures. They show that, for M dwarf terrestrial exoplanets that are
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
-rich with a low H abundance,
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
photolysis can produce abiotic
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
abundances comparable to modern Earth levels. Furthermore, they demonstrate that
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is able to be retained even with a limited water vapor abundance because catalytic cycling driven by
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
can serve as a source of replenishment for
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. Considering their model assumed a low-
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
atmosphere and a dry surface, which decreased surface deposition rates of oxidized species, it is important to consider the role of atmospheric and surface water on the
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
catalytic cycle for potentially habitable terrestrial exoplanets.

Here, we explore whether
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
can be generated abiotically through similar photochemical pathways on an M dwarf terrestrial planet with Mars-like, potentially habitable conditions. In contrast to
P. Gao et al. 2015
, our simulations incorporate a higher
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
abundance, allowing us to assess how this difference influences the resulting
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
production. In Section
II
, we summarize the one-dimensional photochemical model we use to produce our results, including a description of the model atmosphere and the treatment of atmospheric hydrogen. Our results for the production and stability of oxygenated species are in Section
III
. In Section
IV
, we consider the possibility of false positive detections of oxygenated biosignatures, and include an intercomparison of our results and the
P. Gao et al. 2015
work. Lastly, we share our conclusions in Section
V
.

II.3
Atmospheric Hydrogen and Oxygen Content

Our atmospheric model differs from a desiccated Mars atmosphere with the presence of water throughout the atmospheric profile. The concentration of water is calculated along the profile with respect to pressure and temperature. For any
P
and
T
, there is a certain amount of water in the atmosphere equal to the relative humidity multiplied by the
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
saturation vapor pressure. Relative humidity is the ratio of the actual amount of water vapor in the atmosphere to the maximum amount of water the atmosphere can hold at a given
T
. The relative humidity of the troposphere in our model is fixed to 0.17. The
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
saturation vapor pressure is calculated from the Clausius-Clayperon equation

\frac{\textit{d}\mathrm{ln}\textit{P}_{v}}{\textit{d}\mathrm{ln}\textit{T}}=\frac{\textit{m}_{v}\textit{L}}{\textit{RT}}

(1)

since water vapor behaves like an ideal gas below
\sim
373 K
(
J. F. Kasting 1988
)
. Given an isothermal temperature of the stratosphere in our model, the water content within that layer is calculated photochemically.

We vary the
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
content to study how different amounts of hydrogen depletion affect atmospheric composition. Like in past studies
(
H. Nair et al. 1994
;
P. Gao et al. 2015
)
, we simulate six cases with total atmospheric H mole fractions
f
(H)
tot
of (1) 26 ppm, (2) 2.6 ppm, (3) 0.27 ppm, (4) 0.032 ppm, (5) 0.0088 ppm, and (6) 0.0065 ppm.
f
(H)
tot
is the number of hydrogen atoms in the atmosphere divided by the total number of atoms in the atmosphere. Comparatively, the H mole fraction of Earth is
{\sim}0.58
ppm
(
U. Schmidt 1974
)
.

IV
Discussion

IV.1
False Positive Biosignatures

Our results show that mixing ratios for
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
of
\sim
2.7% and
\sim
1 ppm for
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
are possible due to photochemistry for terrestrial exoplanets around M dwarf stars. These values are 10 times smaller than on modern Earth
(
Y. L. Yung & W. B. DeMore 1999
)
, where
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
abundances are produced via photosynthesis. In this section, we explore the photochemical reactions driving
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
production on wet Mars-like exoplanets and consider the potential for false positive biosignature detections with future observatories.

To simplify the processes of our model atmosphere, we emulate past studies to assume zero-flux boundary conditions for all neutral species
(
H. Nair et al. 1994
;
P. Gao et al. 2015
)
. This enables our focus on the effects of UV flux, water content, and
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
content on the production of
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
false positive biosignatures. Maintaining a zero-flux boundary condition is the same as assuming all fluxes are non-zero and cancel each other out. As
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
decreases from cases 1 to 6, the mixing ratios of
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
increase due to water vapor in the model atmosphere. The
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
content within the model atmosphere are highly stable in the troposphere and stratosphere. The production of
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
through the reaction of
O
radicals is a factor of 2 slower than the destruction of
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
via photosynthesis. Some atmospheric escape of oxygen radicals to space inhibits the accumulation of an oxygen sink that would otherwise fuel further
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
production. The abundances of
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
in the atmosphere are stable across all H mol fraction cases because of near-equal rates of production (from
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
O
) and destruction through photolysis. The behavior of abiotic
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
in our atmospheres is consistent with past studies
(
A. Segura et al. 2007
;
P. Gao et al. 2015
, see)
. However, the concentrations of these species differ due to differences in characteristics of the host planet (see Section
IV.2
for more details).

\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
catalyzes the production of
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
, which typically works against
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
photolysis. Across all H mol fraction cases in our atmosphere,
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
is destroyed at a rate
\sim

V
Conclusion

Our work explored the potential for false positive detections of
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
as an atmospheric biosignature on a
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
-rich terrestrial exoplanet with elevated water vapor concentrations around an M dwarf star within the context of past photochemical oxygen false positive studies, like
P. Gao et al. 2015
. We evaluated the stability of these atmospheres with respect to the production and retention of oxygenated species, and found that these atmospheres are stable because of reactions related to the
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
catalytic cycle. The treatment of
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
as an inert species in our model preserved it within the atmosphere, which allowed a closer inspection of the underlying reactions driving the production of
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and the influence of
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
.

Although the
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
mixing ratio values produced by our model are
\sim
10 times smaller than those produced by biological photosynthetic organisms on modern Earth, the trends are comparable to past studies (e.g.,
P. Gao et al. 2015
). The mixing ratio profiles differed due to our assumption of a low-
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
atmosphere with a depleted surface, which increased the surface deposition rates of oxidized species. These atmospheric conditions may be applicable to observational characterization of terrestrial exoplanets orbiting in the habitable zones of M dwarfs
(
R. Luger & R. Barnes 2015
)
. Because high
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
mixing ratios may be observationally detectable, understanding how an
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
-depleted but not truly desiccated planetary environment influences these abundances can differentiate between a potentially habitable world with biologically-influenced atmospheric disequilibria from a water-depleted Mars-like world with atmospheric disequilibria stemming from photochemistry.

We were model-limited in this study because of the inert treatment of
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, which prevented elucidation of the relationship between
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and solar UV for
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
-depleted terrestrial exoplanets. We faced challenges in conducting a true inter-model comparison through differences in planetary parameters, although our results are conclusive for planets of Martian size and surface gravity.

Future research centered on exploring the relationship between these
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
-rich,
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
-poor terrestrial exoplanets and their host stars across a range of M dwarfs, notably as it relates to stellar activity, has the potential to elucidate further insights related to the production of false positive oxygenated species. Comparative modeling studies are crucial towards better understanding atmospheric biosignatures, their false positives, and the underlying chemical processes that drive their accumulation. A better understanding of potential abiotic biosignature detections from this study and others drive effective differentiation between desiccated and potentially habitable worlds with future observational missions.
