Pregnancy outcome and relationship with hypertensive retinopathy in hypertensive disorders of pregnancy among antenatal patients of a tertiary hospital in Nigeria
Authors: Alozie Emeonye, Rex Emeonye, Daniel Gobo, Francis Ezenwankwo, Emmanuel Ogborogu, Ifeanyi Obi, Onyinye Onyia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionHypertensive retinopathy can occur among women with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP). The aim of this study was to determine the proportion of hypertensive retinopathy in HDP, and its relationship with pregnancy outcome measures among antenatal patients at Federal Teaching Hospital (FTH), Owerri, Imo State, Nigeria.MethodThis was a tertiary hospital-based observational prospective study carried out between March and December 2023. One hundred and fifty participants who met the recruitment eligibility criteria were enrolled in the study. All patients had physical and ocular examinations. The findings were recorded and analyzed using SPSS v24.0. Categorical variables were summarized as proportions and percentages. Comparison of variables was done using the Chi-square test; a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.ResultsThe proportion of retinopathy in the study population was 58.7%. Pregnancy outcome measures observed to have a significant correlation with hypertensive retinopathy were maternal outcome [seizures] (p = 0.004), preterm delivery (p = 0.013) and low birth weight (p = 0.033). Pregnancy outcome measures without a significant correlation with hypertensive retinopathy included mode of delivery (p = 0.114), neonatal outcome (p = 0.231), and APGAR score (p = 0.483).ConclusionRetinopathy in hypertensive disorder of pregnancy was significantly associated with maternal outcome (seizure), preterm delivery and low birth weight. It is recommended from these results that advocacy for strengthening maternal & child health (MCH) and education, to prevent complications of hypertensive disorder of pregnancy, is important.

Introduction

Amongst the various systemic medical conditions associated with pregnancy, hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP), formerly referred to as pregnancy-induced hypertension (PIH), is the second most common, after anaemia in pregnancy [1]. Ocular involvement in HDP is common, and the occurrence rate varies from 30% to 100% in different studies, with retinopathy being very common [2]. The retina is the only site in the human body where blood vessels can be visualized directly with the help of an ophthalmoscope. Since hypertension has its effects on all the vessels of the human body, retinal examination and assessment of vascular changes in pregnant women can provide valuable information about the status of placental circulation and fetal well-being [3].

HDP is hypertension that develops in a woman as a direct result of the gravid state. According to the International Society for the Study of Hypertension in Pregnancy (ISSHP), hypertensive disorders of pregnancy can be classified as chronic (occurring before pregnancy or diagnosed before 20 weeks of pregnancy); gestational hypertension, pre-eclampsia, and eclampsia [4]. Gestational hypertension is characterized by hypertension occurring after 20 weeks of pregnancy, in a previously normotensive patient, without proteinuria and edema. Pre-eclampsia is best described as a multi-system disorder of unknown etiology characterized by development of hypertension to the extent of 140/90 mm of Hg or more (or a rise of 30 mmHg of systolic pressure, or a rise of 15 mmHg of diastolic pressure), with proteinuria after the 20th week of pregnancy in a previously normotensive and non-proteinuric patient [5]. It affects about 5% of pregnant women [6]. Pre-eclampsia is divided into two groups according to its severity, mild and severe. Blood pressure more than 160/110 mm of Hg and proteinuria more than 2 gm/24 hours (or > 2+) are grouped as severe pre-eclampsia [7]. When convulsions occur, it is termed eclampsia [8, 9].

The overall incidence of PIH in hospital practice varies widely from 5–10% [10]. PIH, in its different forms, is responsible for 14% of maternal deaths worldwide [11]. The incidence in primigravida is more than in multigravida; and more in developing countries compared to developed regions [7].

The visual system, especially the retina, can be substantially affected in patients with PIH [12]. Retinal changes are correlated with the severity of preeclampsia or eclampsia [13]. In addition, a previous study identified systolic blood pressure (SBP) as an independent risk factor for retinopathy in PIH [14]. The authors recommended strict monitoring and control of SBP as one of the ways to forestall retinopathy among patients with PIH. Choroidal and optic nerve head changes are also associated with low birth weight and low APGAR scores [15, 17]. Therefore, fundus evaluation in patients with PIH can help predict adverse fetal outcomes [1]. Hypertensive retinopathy due to PIH may be graded according to Keith-Wagener-Barker classification [18] into:

There is paucity of local research in this area, also patients with HDP in our facility are not routinely sent to the ophthalmologists for fundal examinations. Therefore, there is a need to assess the relationship between pregnancy outcomes and retinopathy in HDP among antenatal women at Federal Teaching Hospital, Owerri, Imo State. The findings of this study will inform strategies that may lead to an improvement in the health care plan available to antenatal women with HDP and their unborn fetus.

Methodology

The study was carried out in the Obstetrics and Gynaecology clinic of Federal Teaching Hospital (FTH) Owerri, a tertiary health centre in Imo State. The hospital is located in Owerri municipal local government area of Imo state, southeast of Nigeria. Imo State has an estimated population of 3.91 million (National Population Census 2006), occupying an area of about 5,100sqKm, with a population density of 203–1400 people per square kilometer. Its inhabitants are predominantly Igbos, contributing 98% of the population [19]. Owerri, the capital of Imo State is in the rain forest belt of Nigeria, with GPS coordinates: Latitude 5.4836 and longitude 7.0333. It is a sprawling modern metropolitan city, developing at a very fast pace.

FTH Owerri is currently the only Federal Tertiary healthcare facility in Imo State and is located in the state capital. It provides medical services for clients within and outside the state and offers training for undergraduate medical students, intern medical doctors, interns in paramedical departments and postgraduate doctors. It has 466 bed spaces. The study was a tertiary hospital-based observational prospective survey from March to December 2023. Subjects selected by simple random sampling technique, using a simple ballot method from a sampling list of all pregnant women diagnosed with HPD who present to the obstetrics complex i.e. antenatal (ANC) clinics, emergency room, maternity ward or intensive care unit attached to the ANC theatre were enrolled into the study if eligible after evaluation by an Obstetrician.

The study adhered to the tenets of the Helsinki Declaration. Before commencement of the study, ethical clearance was obtained from the Medical and Health Research Committee (Institutional Review Board) of FTH, Owerri. Informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to data collection. The procedure and purpose of the study were explained to the participants in a way they understood, and informed written consent was obtained from them before any procedure was performed. The participants did not make any financial contribution to the study.

Each consenting participant was free to withdraw at any stage and data collected from respondents were kept confidential.

A general physical examination, followed by ocular examination was performed which included anterior segment examination with a pen torch, to rule out any gross anterior segment pathology. Brachial blood pressure was measured with a manual sphygmomanometer. The eye(s) was dilated, using Tropicamide 1% (one drop in each eye, every 15 min x 3 times). A fundus examination was performed in a semi-dark corner with a Keeler indirect ophthalmoscope, with the patient in a reclining position. Changes seen in the right, left or both eyes were taken as positive findings in that patient.

The severity of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy was classified into gestational hypertension, preeclampsia (mild and severe) and eclampsia occurring after 20 weeks of pregnancy, in a previously normotensive patient, based on the following findings [4, 7]:

Gestational hypertension - BP ≥ 140 mmHg systolic or ≥ 90 mmHg diastolic, without proteinuria and edema.

Mild preeclampsia - BP ≥ 140 mmHg systolic or ≥ 90 mmHg diastolic, proteinuria+, and/or mild edema of legs.

Pre-eclampsia with severe features - BP ≥ 140 mmHg systolic or ≥ 90 mmHg diastolic, proteinuria + + or +++, headache, cerebral or visual disturbances, epigastric pain, impaired liver function tests, and increased serum creatinine.

Eclampsia— Severe pre-eclampsia and convulsions.

Proteinuria was tested using the urinalysis strip method and graded as + = 0.3gm/L, ++ =1gm/L, and +++ = 3gm/L [22].

The findings were entered into the datasheet.

Data collected was coded and entered into a database using the International Business Machine (IBM) Statistical Package for Social Science (SPSS) version 24.0 (Armonk, New York, USA). Data was validated by double entry and compared for missing values and data entry errors. Any difference between the first and second files was reconciled by reviewing the questionnaire.

Descriptive analysis of skewed continuous variables was summarized by their medians and interquartile ranges. All normally distributed data were given as mean and standard deviation. Categorical variables were summarized as proportions and percentages. Comparison of variables was done using t-test and Chi-square; p values ≤ 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of one hundred and fifty (150) subjects with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy who met the eligibility criteria for this study were recruited.

Sociodemographic profile of study participants

The age range of the participants was 20–43 years, with a mean age of 31.2 ± 5.3 years. This means that most of the participants were within child-bearing age. A third (32.7%) of the participants were aged 35–39 years, while those aged 40–44 years constituted the lowest group (2.7%). All the participants were Christians, and from Igbo tribe, Imo state is one of the five southeastern states in Nigeria, where the indigenous people of Igbo land reside. Eighty-five were employed, accounting for 56.3% of the total participants, while 65 (43.7%) were unemployed. All the participants were educated, with 114 (74.5%) attaining tertiary level education, and 36 (23.5%) secondary level education signifying a high literacy level among the participants. (Table 1)

Pregnancy outcome

Table 2 reveals that there were 150 deliveries resulting in 154 babies. Majority (138) were live births, accounting for 92% of the total deliveries. Of the live births, 135 (87.7%) were from singleton gestation; 2 participants had twin gestation (1.3%), while 1 participant had triplets (0.6%). Stillbirths were recorded in 12 participants (7.8%) with singleton gestation. The mean gestational age (GA) was 34.2 ± 3.7weeks. Majority of the participants (70.8%) had preterm delivery (gestational age < 37 weeks), while 44 participants (29.3%) delivered at term (gestational age of 37–42 weeks). Approximately, equal number of participants had vaginal delivery (78, 52%) or caesarean Sects. (72, 48%) respectively. The mean birth weight of the babies was 2.5 ± 0.8 kg, and the distribution of the birth weight categories was as follows: Normal birth weight 86 (55.8%); Low birth weight 48 (31.2%); Very low birth weight (11.7%); Extreme low birth weight 2 (1.3%). The median (interquartile range - IQR) APGAR score in the first minute for the 154 babies was 7 (7–8). One hundred and seventeen babies (76%) had normal APGAR scores of 7–9, while 25 babies (16.2%) had an APGAR score between 5 and < 7, requiring immediate resuscitation. Twelve (7.8%) babies had APGAR scores of < 5 with severe perinatal asphyxia with resultant stillbirths. Most of the participants (135) had no complications. However, only 15 participants (9.7%) had seizures which were subsequently controlled.

Discussion

In this study, more than half of the participants with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy had hypertensive retinopathy, similar to hospital-based studies with similar methodologies and patient profiles in India, which documented less proportions of 38.46%, 40.0%, 42.0%, 42.7%, and 44.0% respectively [1, 2, 23, 25] compared to this study. Tadin et al. [15], in Croatia reported 45% of retinal changes in their retrospective study of 40 patients with PIH, and Reddy et al. [12], in Malaysia noted 59% of retinal changes in their study of 78 patients. The notable gap in the proportion from similar studies compared to ours may be due to improved maternal health care delivery in these countries (India, Croatia and Malaysia), study design, and a smaller number of participants enrolled in these studies compared to the present study. Much less proportion 12% and 27.27% were also recorded in India by Shah et al. [3], and Bhupally et al. [26]. On the other hand, Kamath et al., [27]observed that up to 60% of participants had retinopathy in their hospital-based study which had similar methodology and demographic characteristics as our study but had many unbooked participants. Therefore, the higher value observed in the latter study could be due the higher number of unbooked participants (65%) who had no prior antenatal care and were more likely to present with complications [28]. All the participants in our study were booked and were receiving antenatal care. The Federal University Teaching Hospital, Owerri, Imo state is about the only tertiary and referral health facility serving the entire populace of Imo state. The reported proportion of PIH is higher in tertiary referral health facilities [29], multi-fetal gestation, and in populations that lack antenatal care [7].

Compared to studies from Africa, the proportion of retinopathy in HDP from our study is higher. Countries such as South Africa, Egypt, Tanzania, and Ethiopia have documented ranges between 1.8% to 7.1% [30–33]. Compared to Nigeria, these countries seem to have well supported maternal healthcare infrastructure and service delivery [30, 33]. It was also observed that the proportion from our study was higher than 40.2% recorded by Ogbuehi [21] in Port Harcourt, South-eastern Nigeria. This may be attributed to factors such as differences in socio-economic, tribal or geographic as well as the health-seeking behaviour of the patients.

In this study, pregnancy outcome was assessed with the following variables: neonatal birth status (live or stillbirth), gestational age (pre-term, term or post-term), mode of delivery (vaginal or caesarean section), birth weight (normal, low, very low and extremely low), APGAR score @1 minute, and maternal complications. It was observed that the pregnancy outcomes, mode of delivery (p = 0.114) and APGAR score @1 minute (p = 0.483) were not significantly associated with hypertensive retinopathy. However, a statistically significant association was observed between hypertensive retinopathy and gestational age (p = 0.013), birth weight (p = 0.033) and maternal complications (p = 0.004). This result was in agreement with the findings by Ranjan et al., [1] where hypertensive retinopathy was significantly associated with low birth weight (p < 0.05) but was not associated with neonatal outcome in terms of gestational age (< 37 weeks), APGAR score @1 minute (< 5), and stillbirth. Similar result was documented by Javadekaret al., [24] who noted that hypertensive retinopathy was significantly associated with low birth weight (P < 0.05) but was not associated with foetal outcome in terms of gestational age (< 37 weeks), APGAR score @1 minute (< 5), stillbirth and neonatal death. Our study also agrees with Kamath et al. [27], who observed a correlation of hypertensive retinopathy with high incidence of perinatal mortality (81.8%), preterm delivery, birth weight < 2.5 kg and 75% of the babies were small for gestational age (SGA). However, Tadinet al. [], observed that APGAR score had a statistically significant relationship (p < 0.001) to hypertensive retinopathy. This contrasts with our findings and may be due the study design (retrospective) and differences in methodology. The only maternal complication recorded in our study was seizures. There was no record of maternal death. FTH is a tertiary referral centre with specific guidelines for the management of HDP. Additionally, the controlled study design led to the enrolment of mainly pregnant women who had booked for ANC and were less likely to have pregnancy complications. Antenatal visits present opportunities that provide pregnant women with interventions that may be vital to their health and well-being as well as that of their babies. WHO recommends a minimum of four antenatal visits based on a review of the effectiveness of different models of antenatal care [34]. Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy remain a leading cause of maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality. Some countries have guidelines that summarize the existing evidence and provide a reasonable approach to the diagnosis, evaluation, and treatment of hypertension in pregnancy [35]. These efforts are geared towards ensuring a good outcome for every pregnancy. In Nigeria, such guidelines are coordinated by the National Emergency Maternal and Child Health Intervention Centre (NEMCHIC), which provides oversight on reproductive, maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health and nutrition (RMNCAH + N) activities at all levels of maternal and child health care delivery [36].

Conclusion

Approximately 2/3rd of the participants demonstrated varying degrees of retinopathy according to the Keith-Wagner classification. Maternal seizures, decreasing gestational age, and low birth weight were significantly associated with the presence of retinopathy; however, mode of delivery, neonatal death and APGAR score at 1 min were not.

Limitations of the study

This study was hospital-based, so the findings may not be extrapolated to the general population. Similarly, the post-partum duration was 72 h, though this is a critical period when complications from preeclampsia/eclampsia are highest [4]; an area for future research could include a longer follow-up study that may demonstrate onset of retinopathy within trimesters, possible relationship between the severity of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy and the progression or resolution of retinopathy as well as collect data on length of hospitalization and other neonatal complications. In addition, since the paper aims to describe maternal/fetal outcomes associated with retinopathy in hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, multivariate analysis could not demonstrate a logical predictive association between the selected outcomes described (which occur at the end of the pregnancy period) and onset of retinopathy, this is another suggested area for future research.

Recommendations

There should be closer co-operation between the ophthalmologists and obstetricians with a standard management protocol including funduscopy, strict monitoring and control of blood pressure during ANC visits; to detect changes early, forestall maternal complications by developing a prediction model and improve management of HDP.A robust advocacy for strengthening maternal and child health, as well as equipping hospitals, will improve awareness and education as the first level of prevention of complications of HDP.A multi-center, population-based study or systematic review to generate stronger evidence of association between hypertensive retinopathy and delivery outcome of patients with HDP.
