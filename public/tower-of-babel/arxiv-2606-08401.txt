A cubic refinement of Jackson’s Chvátal–Erdős condition for Hamilton cycles in digraphs
Jiangdong Ai Yongtang Shi

Abstract
For a digraph D D , let α 2 ​ ( D ) \alpha_{2}(D) be the largest size of a vertex set no two of whose vertices lie in a common directed 2 2 -cycle. Let f 2 ​ ( a ) f_{2}(a) be the least integer K K such that every K K -connected digraph D D with α 2 ​ ( D ) ≤ a \alpha_{2}(D)\leq a has a Hamilton cycle. In 1987, Jackson proved that f 2 ​ ( a ) ≤ 2 a ​ ( a + 2 ) ! f_{2}(a)\leq 2^{a}(a+2)! and asked for better bounds, noting that a linear bound might be possible. Kühn and Osthus later observed that even a polynomial bound would be interesting. In this short note, we prove the polynomial bound f 2 ​ ( a ) ≤ 2 ​ a 3 + 2 f_{2}(a)\leq 2a^{3}+2 .



1
Introduction

One of the best known sufficient conditions for Hamiltonicity is the theorem of Chvátal and Erdős
[
3
]
: if a graph
G
on at least three vertices satisfies
\kappa(G)\geq\alpha(G)
, where
\kappa
and
\alpha
denote the vertex-connectivity and the independence number, then
G
has a
Hamilton cycle
, that is, a cycle through all of its vertices; if moreover
\kappa(G)\geq\alpha(G)+1
, then
G
is
Hamilton-connected
, meaning that every two distinct vertices are the endpoints of a
Hamilton path
(a path through all vertices). We refer to
[
1
]
for terminology and notation not introduced here, and consider only finite digraphs without loops or multiple arcs; in a digraph, Hamilton cycles and paths are required to be directed.

A directed analogue requires a directed substitute for
\alpha
. For a digraph
D
, let
G(D)
be the graph on
V(D)
in which
uv
is an edge precisely when
u
and
v
form a
directed
2
-cycle
of
D
, i.e.
D
contains both arcs
u\to v
and
v\to u
, and let
\alpha_{2}(D)=\alpha(G(D))
; thus
\alpha_{2}(D)
is the largest number of vertices no two of which lie in a common
2
-cycle. Replacing each edge of a graph
G
by a
2
-cycle produces a digraph
D
with
\alpha_{2}(D)=\alpha(G)
, so
\alpha_{2}
extends
\alpha
. This is the parameter used in Jackson’s directed Chvátal-Erdős theorem. For the smaller parameter
\alpha_{0}(D)
, the largest number of vertices spanning no arc, it is open whether any finite connectivity forces Hamiltonicity
[
7
]
.

We say a digraph is
strongly connected
if it contains a directed path from each vertex to every other one. Throughout, a digraph
D
is

K
-connected if
|V(D)|\geq K+1
and
D-X
is strongly connected for every

X\subseteq V(D)
with
|X|<K
. We write
k(D)
for the largest such
K
.

Equality of connectivity and
\alpha_{2}
does not suffice: Thomassen
[
8
]
and Chakroun
[
2
]
constructed non-Hamiltonian
k
-connected digraphs with
\alpha_{2}=k
for
k\in\{2,3\}
. The right question is how much connectivity, as a function of
\alpha_{2}
, forces a Hamilton cycle. Let
f_{2}(a)
be the least
K
for which every
K
-connected digraph
D
with
\alpha_{2}(D)\leq a
is Hamiltonian. Jackson
[
5
]
proved that

f_{2}(a)\leq 2^{a}(a+2)!.

To the best of our knowledge, no polynomial upper bound for
f_{2}
was previously known.
In the other direction
f_{2}(a)\geq a
, with
f_{2}(1)=1
,
f_{2}(2)=3
,
f_{2}(3)=4

[
5
,
6
]
; Jackson and Ordaz
[
6
]
conjectured that
f_{2}
is linear. That conjecture remains open, and the gap between it and Jackson’s bound is enormous: in their survey, Kühn and Osthus
[
7
]
ask whether even a bound on
\kappa
polynomial in
\alpha_{2}
exists.

In this note, we give such a polynomial bound.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every integer
a\geq 1
,
f_{2}(a)\leq 2a^{3}+2
; that is, every
(2a^{3}+2)
-connected digraph
D
with
\alpha_{2}(D)\leq a
is Hamiltonian.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first polynomial upper bound for

f_{2}
. Thus, the gap between the known lower bound
f_{2}(a)\geq a
and the best known upper bound is reduced from factorial to polynomial.

2
Preliminaries

A digraph is
symmetric
if every arc lies in a directed
2
-cycle; equivalently, it is obtained from an undirected graph by replacing every edge by two opposite arcs. A
complete symmetric
digraph is defined analogously.

We use three standard ingredients. First, the Chvátal–Erdős theorem
[
3
]
says that a
2
-connected undirected graph
G
with
\kappa(G)\geq\alpha(G)
is Hamiltonian; moreover, if
\kappa(G)\geq\alpha(G)+1
, then
G
is Hamilton-connected. Equivalently, a symmetric digraph
H
with
k(H)\geq\alpha_{2}(H)
is
Hamiltonian, and if
k(H)\geq\alpha_{2}(H)+1
then it is Hamilton-connected.

Second, recall that a
directed path cover
of a digraph is a set of pairwise vertex-disjoint directed paths whose union contains all of its vertices, the
size
of the cover being the number of paths in it (isolated vertices count as trivial paths). The Gallai–Milgram theorem
[
4
]
says that every digraph
D
has a directed path cover of size at most
\alpha_{0}(D)
.

Third, we use the following linking lemma of Jackson
[
5
]
. The lemma is stated in the form needed below; the singleton pieces are allowed to be complete symmetric digraphs of order one.

Lemma 2.1

(Jackson
[
5
, Lemma 5]
)
.

Let
D
be a digraph. Suppose that
V(D)
can be covered with vertex-disjoint symmetric digraphs

H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{m}

such that

(i)

\alpha_{2}(H_{i})\geq\alpha_{2}(H_{j})
whenever
1\leq i<j\leq m
;

(ii)

|H_{i}|=1
for
t<i\leq m
;

(iii)

H_{i}
is either a complete symmetric digraph or else
k(H_{i})>(2m-3)\alpha_{2}(H_{i})
for each
1\leq i\leq m
, and

(iv)

k(D)>2\sum_{i=1}^{t}\Big((m-1-i)\alpha_{2}(H_{i})+1\Big)+(m-t).

Then
D
is Hamiltonian.

3
A fixed-threshold decomposition

The following elementary decomposition is a fixed-threshold variant of Jackson’s Lemma 2
[
5
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be an undirected graph with
\alpha(G)\leq a
, and let
B\geq 0
be an integer. Then there are a set
S\subseteq V(G)
and vertex-disjoint induced subgraphs

F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}
covering
V(G)\setminus S
such that

(i)

t\leq a
;

(ii)

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\alpha(F_{i})\leq a
;

(iii)

|S|\leq(t-1)B
;

(iv)

every
F_{i}
is either complete or else
\kappa(F_{i})>B
.

Proof.

Start with
G
and
S=\emptyset
. Whenever a current
part
F
is not complete and satisfies
\kappa(F)\leq B
, choose a vertex cut

R
of
F
with
|R|\leq B
; if
F
is disconnected, take
R=\emptyset
.
Move
R
to
S
and replace
F
by two nonempty induced subgraphs whose
vertex sets are unions of components of
F-R
.

There are no edges between the two new parts, so the sum of the independence
numbers of the current parts does not increase. Since every nonempty part
has independence number at least
1
, the number of parts is always at most

a
. If the final number of parts is
t
, exactly
t-1
splits have occurred,
and hence
|S|\leq(t-1)B
. At termination every part is complete or has
connectivity greater than
B
.
∎

4
Proof of the cubic bound

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Fix
a\geq 1
, and let

K=2a^{3}+2
and
B=2a^{2}-a-1.

Let
D
be a
K
-connected digraph with
\alpha_{2}(D)\leq a
.

Apply Lemma
3.1
to the
2
-cycle graph
G(D)
. We obtain a set
S
and induced subgraphs
F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}
of
G(D)
covering
V(D)\setminus S
such that

t\leq a,\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{t}\alpha(F_{i})\leq a,\qquad|S|\leq(t-1)B,

(1)

with every
F_{i}
complete or
\kappa(F_{i})>B
. Let
A_{i}
be the symmetric subdigraph on
V(F_{i})
whose arcs are exactly the two arcs
u\to v
,
v\to u
of each
2
-cycle of
D
with
uv\in E(F_{i})
. (We take only these
2
-cycle arcs though the induced subdigraph
D[V(F_{i})]
may contain further one-way arcs and need not be symmetric.) Since
F_{i}
is an induced subgraph of
G(D)
, we have
G(A_{i})=F_{i}
, and therefore

\alpha_{2}(A_{i})=\alpha(F_{i})
and
k(A_{i})=\kappa(F_{i})
.
In particular each
A_{i}
is complete symmetric or satisfies
k(A_{i})>B
.

If
t=1
, then no split occurred, so
S=\emptyset
and
A_{1}
spans
D
. If
A_{1}
is complete symmetric, then
D
is Hamiltonian. Otherwise
k(A_{1})>B
. For
a=1
, the case
A_{1}
non-complete cannot occur because
\alpha_{2}(A_{1})\leq 1
forces
A_{1}
to be complete symmetric. For
a\geq 2
, we have
B\geq a\geq\alpha_{2}(A_{1})
, and since
k(A_{1})
is an
integer,
k(A_{1})>B
implies
k(A_{1})\geq\alpha_{2}(A_{1})+1
. Thus
A_{1}
is even
Hamilton-connected, and in particular Hamiltonian. Thus we may assume from now on that
t\geq 2
.

Next cover the exceptional set
S
by directed paths. Since every vertex set inducing no arcs also contains no directed
2
-cycle, we have
\alpha_{0}(D[S])\leq\alpha_{2}(D[S])\leq a
.
By Gallai–Milgram,
D[S]
has a vertex-disjoint directed path cover
P_{1},\ldots,P_{q}

with
q\leq a
. If
S=\emptyset
, then
q=0
. Write

P_{j}=s_{j}\cdots r_{j}
,
where
s_{j}
is the first vertex and
r_{j}
is the last vertex of
P_{j}
.

Construct an auxiliary digraph
D^{*}
from
D
by replacing each path
P_{j}
with one new vertex
p_{j}
. More precisely,

V(D^{*})=\bigl(V(D)\setminus S\bigr)\cup\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{q}\}.

The arcs inside
V(D)\setminus S
are the same as in
D
, and the arcs involving the new vertices are defined by

\displaystyle u\to p_{j}\text{ in }D^{*}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow u\to s_{j}\text{ in }D,

\displaystyle p_{j}\to u\text{ in }D^{*}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow r_{j}\to u\text{ in }D,

\displaystyle p_{i}\to p_{j}\text{ in }D^{*}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow r_{i}\to s_{j}\text{ in }D.

Loops are ignored. Any Hamilton cycle of
D^{*}
lifts to a Hamilton cycle of
D
by replacing each
p_{j}
with the directed path
P_{j}=s_{j}\cdots r_{j}
.

We need a connectivity estimate for
D^{*}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

With the notation above,

k(D^{*})\geq k(D)-|S|
.

Proof.

Let
X\subseteq V(D^{*})
with
|X|<k(D)-|S|
. We show that
D^{*}-X
is strongly connected. Take distinct vertices
x,y\in V(D^{*})\setminus X
.

For
x\in V(D^{*})
define a representative
\widehat{x}\in V(D)
by

\widehat{x}=\begin{cases}r_{i},&x=p_{i},\\
x,&x\in V(D)\setminus S,\end{cases}

and similarly, for
y\in V(D^{*})
, define
\widehat{y}\in V(D)
using the first vertex
s_{j}
in place of the last:

\widehat{y}=\begin{cases}s_{j},&y=p_{j},\\
y,&y\in V(D)\setminus S.\end{cases}

Let
Y=\bigl(S\setminus\{\widehat{x},\widehat{y}\}\bigr)\cup\bigl(X\cap(V(D)\setminus S)\bigr)
.
Then
|Y|\leq|S|+|X|<k(D)
, so
D-Y
is strongly connected. Hence there is a directed
\widehat{x}\widehat{y}
-path in
D-Y
. This path avoids
S
except possibly at its first and last vertices. By the definition of the arcs of
D^{*}
, it translates to a directed
xy
-path in
D^{*}-X
. Therefore
D^{*}-X
is strongly connected.
∎

We next verify the hypotheses of Lemma
2.1
for
D^{*}
. Order the pieces
A_{1},\ldots,A_{t}
so that

\nu_{i}=\alpha_{2}(A_{i})

satisfies
\nu_{1}\geq\nu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\nu_{t}
. Since each
\nu_{i}\geq 1
and each singleton
p_{j}
has
\alpha_{2}=1
, the full cover

A_{1},\ldots,A_{t},\{p_{1}\},\ldots,\{p_{q}\}

of
D^{*}
is ordered nonincreasingly by
\alpha_{2}
. Let
m=t+q
.
First consider condition (iii) of Lemma
2.1
. Because
t\geq 2
and
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\nu_{i}\leq a
, every
\nu_{i}
is at most
a-t+1
. Also
q\leq a
, so
m\leq t+a
. Hence, for every
1\leq i\leq t
,

\displaystyle(2m-3)\nu_{i}

\displaystyle\leq(2a+2t-3)(a-t+1)

\displaystyle=2a^{2}-a-2t^{2}+5t-3

\displaystyle\leq 2a^{2}-a-1=B,

where the last inequality uses
t\geq 2
. Thus every non-complete
A_{i}
satisfies

k(A_{i})>B\geq(2m-3)\nu_{i}.

The singleton pieces are complete symmetric, so condition (iii) of Lemma
2.1
holds.

It remains to check condition (iv) of Lemma
2.1
. Let

\Theta=2\sum_{i=1}^{t}\Big((m-1-i)\nu_{i}+1\Big)+(m-t).

Since
m=t+q
and
q\leq a
, the expression
\Theta
is maximized, for fixed
t
and
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{t}
, by taking
q=a
. Therefore

\Theta\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{t}\Big((t+a-1-i)\nu_{i}+1\Big)+a.

The coefficients
t+a-1-i
decrease with
i
, and
\sum_{i}\nu_{i}\leq a
with every
\nu_{i}\geq 1
. Hence the right-hand side is maximized when
\nu_{1}=a-t+1
and
\nu_{i}=1
for
2\leq i\leq t
. Consequently

\displaystyle\Theta

\displaystyle\leq 2\left((a+t-2)(a-t+1)+\sum_{i=2}^{t}(a+t-1-i)+t\right)+a

\displaystyle=2a^{2}+2at-3a-t^{2}+3t.

Call this last quantity
\Theta_{t}
.

By Lemma
4.1
and (
1
),

k(D^{*})\geq k(D)-|S|\geq 2a^{3}+2-(t-1)B.

A direct calculation gives

\displaystyle 2a^{3}+2-(t-1)B-\Theta_{t}

\displaystyle=(a-t)(2a^{2}+2-t)+1>0,

because
1\leq t\leq a
. Therefore
k(D^{*})>\Theta
, and Lemma
2.1
applies to
D^{*}
, so
D^{*}
is Hamiltonian. Lifting a Hamilton cycle of
D^{*}
by replacing each
p_{j}
with the path
P_{j}
gives a Hamilton cycle of
D
. This proves Theorem
1.1
. ∎
