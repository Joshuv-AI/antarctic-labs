Non-relativistic limit of generalized relativistic Pauli operators by Feynman-Kac formulae
Soichiro Sakamoto

Abstract
The non-relativistic limit of a generalized relativistic Pauli operator H c S , α = ( 2 c β ( σ ⋅ ( − i ∇ − a ) ) 2 + ( m c γ ) 2 / α ) α / 2 − m c γ + V H_{c}^{S,\alpha}=\left(2c^{\beta}\bigl(\sigma\cdot(-i\nabla-a)\bigr)^{2}+(mc^{\gamma})^{2/\alpha}\right)^{\alpha/2}-mc^{\gamma}+V on L 2 ​ ( ℝ 3 , ℂ 2 ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{C}^{2}) is investigated under the constraint 2 ​ α = γ ​ β + γ 2 2\alpha=\gamma\beta+\gamma^{2} . This operator generalizes the relativistic Pauli operator within the framework of Bernstein functions. The associated heat semigroup e − t ​ H c S , α e^{-tH_{c}^{S,\alpha}} admits a Feynman–Kac representation involving Brownian motion, a subordinator, and a Poisson process. Using this representation, we prove that the semigroup e − t ​ H c S , α e^{-tH_{c}^{S,\alpha}} converges strongly to e − t ​ H S , α e^{-tH^{S,\alpha}} as c → ∞ c\to\infty , where the limiting generator is given by H S , α = α 2 ​ m 2 α − 1 ( σ ⋅ ( − i ∇ − a ) ) 2 + V . H^{S,\alpha}=\frac{\alpha}{2m^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}}\bigl(\sigma\cdot(-i\nabla-a)\bigr)^{2}+V. The non-relativistic limit of a generalized relativistic Schrödinger operator is also investigated.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the relativistic Schrödinger operator with a vector potential
a
,

H_{c}=\sqrt{c^{2}(-i\nabla-a)^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}+V

acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and the relativistic Pauli operator

H_{c}^{\mathrm{S}}=\sqrt{c^{2}\bigl(\sigma\cdot(-i\nabla-a)\bigr)^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}+V

acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Here
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})
denotes the Pauli matrices. The operator
H_{c}^{\mathrm{S}}
incorporates the spin-
\tfrac{1}{2}
degree of freedom, whereas
H_{c}
does not. Both
H_{c}
and
H_{c}^{\mathrm{S}}
describe relativistic particles minimally coupled to the vector potential
a
. The function
V
represents an external potential,
c>0
is the speed of light, and
m>0
is the particle mass.

The purpose of this paper is to investigate the non-relativistic limit of the semi-groups
e^{-tH_{c}^{\#}}
as
c\to\infty
, where the symbol
\#
stands for either the scalar or spin case. Indeed, since

\sqrt{c^{2}u+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}=\frac{1}{2m}u+O\!\left(\frac{u^{2}}{m^{3}c^{2}}\right),

we formally expect that

e^{-tH_{c}^{\#}}\;\longrightarrow\;e^{-tH^{\#}}

in the strong sense as
c\to\infty
. Here

\displaystyle H=\frac{1}{2m}(-i\nabla-a)^{2}+V,

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{S}}=\frac{1}{2m}\bigl(\sigma\cdot(-i\nabla-a)\bigr)^{2}+V,

are the non-relativistic Schrödinger and Pauli operators, respectively.
In this paper we further introduce a class of generalized operators. Consider the function

\Psi_{c}(u)=(2c^{2}u+m^{2}c^{4})^{1/2}-mc^{2},

which is a Bernstein function, i.e,
(-1)^{n+1}\Psi_{c}^{(n)}(u)\geq 0
for all
u\geq 0
. Also,
\Psi_{c}(0)=0
is satisfied. Then the operators
H_{c}
and
H_{c}^{\mathrm{S}}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle H_{c}=\Psi_{c}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(-i\nabla-a)^{2}\right)+V,

\displaystyle H_{c}^{\mathrm{S}}=\Psi_{c}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(\sigma\cdot(-i\nabla-a))^{2}\right)+V.

We generalize this construction by replacing
\Psi_{c}
with a family of Bernstein functions

\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,c}(u)=\bigl(2c^{\beta}u+(mc^{\gamma})^{2/\alpha}\bigr)^{\alpha/2}-mc^{\gamma},\qquad 0<\alpha<2.

Under the condition
2\alpha=\beta\gamma+\gamma^{2}
, one verifies that

\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,c}(u)\longrightarrow\frac{\alpha}{2m^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}}\,u

as
c\to\infty
. The original function
\Psi_{c}
corresponds to the special case
\alpha=1
and
\beta=\gamma=2
.
Accordingly, we introduce the generalized operators:

\displaystyle H_{c}^{\alpha}=\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,c}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(-i\nabla-a)^{2}\right)+V,

\displaystyle H_{c}^{{\mathrm{S}},\alpha}=\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,c}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(\sigma\cdot(-i\nabla-a))^{2}\right)+V.

The main purpose of this paper is to prove that

e^{-tH_{c}^{\#,\alpha}}\;\longrightarrow\;e^{-tH^{\#,\alpha}}

as
c\to\infty
, where

\displaystyle H^{\alpha}=\frac{\alpha}{2m^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}}(-i\nabla-a)^{2}+V,

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{S},\alpha}=\frac{\alpha}{2m^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}}\bigl(\sigma\cdot(-i\nabla-a)\bigr)^{2}+V.

Our approach is based on the Feynman–Kac formula (FKF) for semi-groups
e^{-tH_{c}^{\#,\alpha}}
. The FKF for
e^{-tH_{c}}
and
e^{-tH_{c}^{\mathrm{S}}}
were derived in
[
JHV2020
]
. The corresponding formulae for
e^{-tH_{c}^{\alpha}}
and
e^{-tH_{c}^{{\mathrm{S}},\alpha}}
can also be established by a slight modification of these arguments. In particular, the FKF for
e^{-tH_{c}^{{\mathrm{S}},\alpha}}
involves three independent stochastic processes: a Brownian motion, a subordinator associated with the Bernstein function
\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,c}
, and a spin process driven by a Poisson process.

Finally, we briefly review related results.
A path integral representation of the semi-group

e^{-tH}
is known as the Feynman–Kac–Itô formula
[
Sim1979
]
.
Ichinose and Tamura extended this framework to the relativistic Hamiltonian
H_{c}
in
[
IT1986
]
,
although their representation differs from the one employed in the present work.
Based on these developments, Ichinose proved the non-relativistic limit in
[
Ichi1987
]
in the spinless case.
The path integral representations of the heat semi-groups for the Pauli operator and the relativistic Pauli operator are given by
[
GGM1983
,
BJ1981
]
and
[
GAM1991
]
, respectively, and it is generalized in
[
HIL2009
]
.
The non-relativistic limit of the Dirac operator has also been investigated from an operator-theoretic perspective in
[
Hun1975
,
Tha1992
]
.

2.1
Bernstein functions and subordinators

We introduce an
\alpha/2
-relativistic subordinator, which plays an important role in the subsequent stochastic arguments. Bernstein functions introduced below are treated in
[
App2009
,
JHV2020
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

(Bernstein function)
Let

\mathscr{B}=\biggl\{f\in C^{\infty}((0,\infty))\bigg|f(x)\geq 0\;\text{and}\;(-1)^{n}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}f(x)\leq 0\;\text{for}\;\text{all}\;n\in\mathbb{N}\biggr\}.

An element of
\mathscr{B}
is called a Bernstein function. Furthermore, we define a subset of
\mathscr{B}
:

\mathscr{B}_{0}=\{f\in\mathscr{B}\;|\lim_{x\to 0+}f(x)=0\}.

We introduce a set of measures. Let
\mathscr{L}
be the set of Borel measures
\lambda
on
\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
such that

\lambda((-\infty,0))=0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}}(y\wedge 1)\lambda(dy)<\infty.

In particular,
\lambda\in\mathscr{L}
is a Lévy measure, i.e,
\int_{\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}}(y\wedge 1)^{2}\lambda(dy)<\infty.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\Psi\in\mathscr{B}_{0}
. Then there exists a unique
(b,\lambda)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathscr{L}
such that

\Psi(u)=bu\;+\;\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-e^{-uy})\lambda(dy).

(2.1)

Conversely, the right-hand side of (2.1) is in
\mathscr{B}_{0}
for each

(b,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathscr{L}
.

There exists a fundamental relationship between subordinators and
\mathscr{B}_{0}
.
This relationship is indispensable for establishing the path integral representation of generalized relativistic Pauli operators. Let
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a random process on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
. From now on, we write

\mathbb{E}_{P}^{x}[f(X_{t})]=\int_{\Omega}f(X_{t}(\omega)+x)dP(\omega).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\Psi\in\mathscr{B}_{0}
, and
(b,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathscr{L}
be
a pair satisfying (2.1). Then there exists a unique subordinator
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
such that

\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}[e^{-uT_{t}}]=e^{-t\Psi(u)}.

(2.2)

Conversely, let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a subordinator on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
. Then there exists a unique
\Psi\in\mathscr{B}_{0}
satisfying (2.2).

Proof.

See
[
JHV2020
, Proposition 3.99]
.
∎

2.2
\alpha/2
-relativistic subordinator

Let
0<\alpha<2
, and choose
\beta,\gamma>0
such that
2\alpha=\beta\gamma+{\gamma}^{2}
.
Let

\Psi_{c}^{\alpha}(u)=(2c^{\beta}u+(mc^{\gamma})^{2/\alpha})^{\alpha/2}-mc^{\gamma},\quad u\geq 0.

(2.3)

We can directly see that
\Psi_{c}^{\alpha}\in\mathscr{B}_{0}
for each
c>0
. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
be a probability space. We call
(T_{t}^{c})_{t\geq 0}
satisfying (2.4) below an
\alpha/2
-relativistic subordinator parametrized by
c>0
:

\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}[e^{-uT_{t}^{c}}]=e^{-t\Psi_{c}^{\alpha}(u)}.

(2.4)

The right-hand side of (2.4) can be represented by

\exp(-t\Psi_{c}^{\alpha}(u))={\exp}\Biggl({\frac{{\alpha}(2c^{\beta})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}t}{2\Gamma(1-{\alpha/2)}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-e^{-uy})e^{\frac{-(mc^{\gamma})^{2/\alpha}}{2c^{\beta}}}\frac{dy}{y^{1+\alpha/2}}\Biggr)}.

(2.5)

See
[
Bog2009
, Example 5.9]
. Since
T_{t}^{c}
is a Lévy process, we can see that Lévy triplet of
(T_{t}^{c})_{t\geq 0}
is given by the form
(b,0,\mu)
.
Here
b{\in}\mathbb{R}
and
\mu
is a Lévy measure. By Lévy–Khintchine formula, we have

\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}[e^{iuT_{t}^{c}}]=\exp\Biggl(itbu+t\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}}(e^{iuy}-1-iuy\mathbbm{1}_{\{|y|\leq 1\}})\mu(dy)\Biggr).

(2.6)

The function
\mathbb{R}{\ni}u\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}[e^{iuT_{t}^{c}}]
can be analytically continued into the region
\{iu\in\mathbb{C}\;|\;u>0\}
. Hence we can obtain the Lévy triplet of
(T_{t}^{c})_{t\geq 0}
by the analytical continuation of both sides of (2.6) and comparing the resulting expression with (2.5):

(b,0,\mu)=\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}y\nu_{c}(dy),0,\nu_{c}\right),

(2.7)

where

\nu_{c}(dy)=(2c^{\beta})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\frac{\alpha}{2\Gamma\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{(mc^{\gamma})^{2/\alpha}}{2c^{\beta}}y\right)\frac{1}{y^{1+\alpha/2}}dy.

By (2.7) and Lévy-Itô decomposition, we can obtain a stochastic integral representation of
T_{t}^{c}
by

T_{t}^{c}=t\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}z\nu_{c}(dz)+\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}zN_{c}(t,dz)+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}z\tilde{N_{c}}(t,dz),

(2.8)

where
N_{c}(t,dz)
denote a Poisson random measure and
\tilde{N_{c}}(t,dz)
a compensated Poisson random measure associated with
(T_{t}^{c})_{t\geq 0}
. Note that
(N_{c}(t,\cdot))_{t\geq 0}
is the Poisson process with intensity
\nu_{c}(A)=\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}[N_{c}(1,A)]
for
each
A\subset[0,\infty)
.
Furthermore,
\tilde{N_{c}}(t,A)=N_{c}(t,dz)-t\nu_{c}(A)
, and
(\tilde{N}_{c}(t,A))_{t\geq 0}
is a martingale for
each
A\subset[0,\infty)
. We investigate the exponent of
(T_{t}^{c})_{t\geq 0}
and limiting behavior of its expectation as
c\to\infty
. This computation is used to justify the integrability of the Feynman–Kac formula of the relativistic Pauli operator, and to investigate the non-relativistic limit.

Lemma 2.4
.

Fix
c>0
. For
0<u<{\frac{(mc^{\gamma})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}{2c^{\beta}}}
we have

\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}[e^{uT_{t}^{c}}]=\exp\Bigl(-t\bigl(\bigl(-2c^{\beta}u+(mc^{\gamma})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\bigr)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-mc^{\gamma}\bigr)\Bigr).

(2.9)

In particular, for
u>0
, it follows that

\lim_{c\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}[e^{uT_{t}^{c}}]=\exp\left({t\frac{{\alpha}u}{m^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}}}\right)

(2.10)

and
\displaystyle\sup_{c>0}\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}[e^{uT_{t}^{c}}]<{\infty}
for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\nu_{c}
we see that

\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{uz}-1)\nu_{c}(dz)<\infty

for
0<u<{\frac{(mc^{\gamma})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}{2c^{\beta}}}
,
and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{uz}-1)\nu_{c}(dz)=\bigl(-2c^{\beta}u+(mc^{\gamma})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\bigr)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-mc^{\gamma}

follows.
We write

{Z_{t}^{c}}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t+}\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}zN_{c}(ds,dz)+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t+}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}z\tilde{N_{c}}(ds,dz),

where
\int_{0}^{t}N_{c}(ds,dz)=N_{c}(t,dz)
. By Itô-formula for semimartingales, we have

\displaystyle e^{u{Z_{t}^{c}}}-1=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t+}\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{uZ_{s}^{c}}(e^{uz}-1)N_{c}(ds,dz)+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t+}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}e^{uZ_{s}^{c}}(e^{uz}-1)\tilde{N_{c}}(ds,dz)

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}e^{uZ_{s}^{c}}(e^{uz}-uz-1)ds\nu_{c}(dz).

Taking expectations of both sides above, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}[e^{u{Z_{t}^{c}}}]

\displaystyle=\;1+\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}\left[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t+}\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{uZ_{s}^{c}}(e^{uz}-1)N_{c}(ds,dz)\right]+\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}\left[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}e^{uZ_{s}^{c}}(e^{uz}-uz-1)ds\nu_{c}(dz)\right]

\displaystyle=1+\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}\left[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{uZ_{s}^{c}}(e^{uz}-1)ds\nu_{c}(dz)\right]+\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}\left[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}e^{uZ_{s}^{c}}(e^{uz}-uz-1)ds\nu_{c}(dz)\right]

\displaystyle=1+\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{uz}-1)\nu_{c}(dz)-u\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}z\nu_{c}(dz)\right)\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}[e^{uZ_{s}^{c}}]ds.

In the first equality, we used the martingale property of the compensated Poisson integrals.
The second equality follows from

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}\left[\int_{0}^{t+}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{uZ_{s}^{c}}(e^{uz}-1)N_{c}(ds,dz)\right]=\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{uZ_{s}^{c}}(e^{uz}-1)ds\,\nu_{c}(dz)\right].

Hence we can obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}[e^{u{Z_{t}^{c}}}]=\exp\left(\displaystyle{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{uz}-1)\nu_{c}(dz)-\displaystyle{ut}\int_{0}^{1}z\nu_{c}(dz)\right).

By (2.8), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}[e^{uT_{t}^{c}}]

\displaystyle=\exp\left(ut\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}z\nu_{c}(dz)\right)\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}[e^{u{Z_{t}^{c}}}]

\displaystyle=\exp\left(ut\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}z\nu_{c}(dz)\right)\exp\left(t\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{uz}-1)\nu_{c}(dz)-ut\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}z\nu_{c}(dz)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(t\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{uz}-1)\nu_{c}(dz)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\Bigl(-t\bigl(\bigl(-2c^{\beta}u+(mc^{\gamma})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\bigr)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-mc^{\gamma}\bigr)\Bigr)

from a similar computation to (2.5). Let
u>0
be arbitrarily fixed. Since
{\frac{(mc^{\gamma})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}{2c^{\beta}}}\to{\infty}
as
c\to{\infty}
, there exists
C>0
such that
{\frac{(mc^{\gamma})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}{2c^{\beta}}}>u>0
for all
c>C
. Then we have

\lim_{c\to{\infty}}\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}[e^{uT_{t}^{c}}]=\lim_{c\to{\infty}}\exp\Bigl(-t\bigl(\bigl(-2c^{\beta}u+(mc^{\gamma})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\bigr)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-mc^{\gamma}\bigr)\Bigr)={\exp\left({t\frac{{\alpha}u}{m^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}}}\right)}.

Thus the lemma is proved.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

We have

\lim_{c\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}\left[{\left|T_{t}^{c}-\frac{{\alpha}t}{\;\;\;m^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}}\right|^{n}}\right]=0\quad\text{for}\;\text{all}\;{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}.

(2.11)

Proof.

We denote
\frac{{\alpha}t}{\;\;\;m^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}}
by
t_{\alpha}
. By Lemma.2.5, we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}\left[\left|T_{t}^{c}-t_{\alpha}\right|^{n}\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}\left[\left|T_{t}^{c}-t_{\alpha}\right|^{n}{\mathbbm{1}_{\{T_{t}^{c}\geq{t_{\alpha}}\}}}\right]\right)^{2}+2\left(\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}\left[\left|T_{t}^{c}-t_{\alpha}\right|^{n}{\mathbbm{1}_{\{T_{t}^{c}\leq{t_{\alpha}}\}}}\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}\left[(n!)^{2}\left(e^{(T_{t}^{c}-t_{\alpha})}-1\right)^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}\left[(n!)^{2}\left(e^{(t_{\alpha}-T_{t}^{c})}-1\right)^{2}\right]\to 0\quad(c\to{\infty}).

Then the corollary is proved.
∎

3
Non-relativistic limit of spinless case

In this section, we consider the non-relativistic limit of generalized relativistic Schrödinger operators. First, we introduce the
\alpha/2
-relativistic Schrödinger operator with a vector potential via quadratic forms, and confirm its self-adjointness under suitable conditions.

3.1
Generalized relativistic Schrödinger operators

We define generalized relativistic Schrödinger operators under singular vector potential via the theory of quadratic forms. Let
\partial_{\mu}:\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\mu=1,\ldots,d
, be the distributional derivative with respect to the

\mu
-th coordinate on the tempered distribution space

\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Let
p=-i\nabla
, where
\nabla=(\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{d})
. For a vector potential
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we formally define the Schrödinger operator with the vector potential
a
by

\frac{1}{2}(p-a)^{2}.

We rigorously construct it as a self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
via a quadratic form. We introduce following assumptions on
a
:

(A.1)

\displaystyle a\in(L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{d},\quad V\in{C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})};

(A.2)

\displaystyle a\in(L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{d},\quad\nabla\cdot a\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad V\in{C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

By the next proposition, we can define Schrödinger operators under (A.1).

Proposition 3.1
.

If
q
is a closed semibounded quadratic form, there exists a unique self-adjoint operator
A
such that

Q(q)=D(A),\quad q(\psi,\varphi)=(\psi,A{\varphi}),\quad\psi,\varphi\in D(A).

Let
D_{\mu}=p_{\mu}-a_{\mu},\mu=1,...,d
, where
p_{\mu}=-i\partial_{\mu}.
Define the quadratic form
q
by

q(f,g)=\sum_{\mu=1}^{d}(D_{\mu}f,D_{\mu}g)

(3.1)

and its quadratic domain is given by

Q(q)=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mid D_{\mu}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \mu=1,\ldots,d\}.

(3.2)

We can obtain a self-adjoint operator through the following lemma under the assumption (A.1).

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose (A.1). Then the quadratic form
q
defined by (3.1) and (3.2) is a symmetric closed form. In particular, there exists a unique self-adjoint operator
h(a)
satisfying

D(h(a))=\{{f\in{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\;|\;q(f,\cdot)\in({L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})^{*}\},

(h(a)f,g)=q(f,g)\;\;\;\text{for}\;f\in{D(h(a))},\;g\in{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

See
[
HC1981
, Lemma 1]
.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

Let

\Psi_{c}^{\alpha}
be the Bernstein function defined by (2.3).
Assume (A.1). We define

D(H_{c}^{\alpha})=D(\Psi_{c}^{\alpha}(h(a))),\quad\quad H_{c}^{\alpha}=\Psi_{c}^{\alpha}(h(a))+V,

where
h(a)
is defined in Proposition 3.2. We call
H_{c}^{\alpha}
the
\alpha/2
-relativistic Schrödinger operator with vector potential
a
.

Since
V
is bounded,
H_{c}^{\alpha}
is a self adjoint operator on
D(\Psi_{c}^{\alpha}(h(a)))
.

3.2
Feynman-Kac-Itô formula

Let
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the
d
-dimensional Brownian motion starting at
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
on the Wiener space
(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{X}),\mathcal{W}^{x})
, where
\mathscr{X}=C([0,\infty);\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{W}^{x}
is the Wiener measure. To derive the Feynman-Kac-Itô formula, we need to check that a stochastic integral of
a
can be defined under the assumption (A.2).

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose (A.2). Then

\mathcal{W}^{x}\left(\Biggl|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}a(B_{s})\cdot{dB_{s}}+\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}{\nabla}{\cdot}a(B_{s})ds\Biggr|<\infty\right)=1

holds for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

See
[
HIL2009
, Lemma 3.6]
.
∎

We write

\int_{0}^{t}a(B_{s})\cdot dB_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla\cdot a(B_{s})\,ds=\int_{0}^{t}a(B_{s})\circ dB_{s}.

Let
(T_{t}^{c})_{t\geq 0}
be the
\alpha/2
-relativistic subordinator defined in (2.4) on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
.
From the above preparation, we can obtain the following Feynman–Kac–Itô formula for
e^{-tH_{c}^{\alpha}}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose (A.2). Then we have

(f,e^{-tH_{c}^{\alpha}}g)=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{W},P}^{x,0}\Bigl[\overline{f(B_{0})}g(B_{T_{t}^{c}})e^{-i{\int_{0}^{T_{t}^{c}}a(B_{s})\circ{dB_{s}}}}e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V(B_{T_{s}^{c}}){ds}}\Bigr]dx.

Proof.

See
[
JHV2020
, Theorem 4.208]
.
∎

From Proposition 3.5, it follows that
\|e^{-tH_{c}^{\alpha}}\|
is uniformly bounded for all
c>0
and for all
t\geq 0
.

3.3
Non-relativistic limit

With the preparation of previous sections, we give a stochastic proof of the non-relativistic limit of

e^{-t{H_{c}^{\alpha}}}
by using the Feynman–Kac–Itô formula. Here we set

H_{\alpha}=\frac{{\alpha}}{m^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}}h(a)+V.

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose
a\in{(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{d}}
,
{\nabla}\cdot{a}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
V\in{C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. Then we have

\displaystyle s-\lim_{c\to\infty}e^{-tH_{c}^{\alpha}}=e^{-tH_{\alpha}}\;\;\;\text{for}\;\text{all}\;t\geq 0.

Proof.

Suppose
f,g\in{C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. We have

\displaystyle\bigl|(f,e^{-tH_{c}^{\alpha}}g)-(f,e^{-tH_{\alpha}}g)\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{t\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{W},P}^{x,0}\Bigl[|f(B_{0})||g(B_{T_{t}^{c}})-g(B_{t_{\alpha}})|\Bigr]dx

\displaystyle\quad+e^{t\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{W},P}^{x,0}\Bigl[|f(B_{0})||g(B_{t_{\alpha}})|\bigl|e^{-i\int_{0}^{T_{t}^{c}}a(B_{s})\circ dB_{s}}-e^{-i\int_{0}^{t_{\alpha}}a(B_{s})\circ dB_{s}}\bigr|\Bigr]dx

\displaystyle\quad\quad+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{W},P}^{x,0}\Bigl[|f(B_{0})||g(B_{t_{\alpha}})|\bigl|e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V(B_{T_{s}^{c}})\,ds}-e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V(B_{s_{\raisebox{-0.43054pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\alpha$}}}})\,ds}\bigr|\Bigr]dx.

In this proof, we set
\delta={\lVert V\rVert}_{\infty}
. Then the convergence of the first and the third term follows from
[
JHV2020
, Proposition 4.230]
. We will verify the convergence of the second one:

\displaystyle e^{t\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{W},P}^{x,0}\Bigl[|f(B_{0})||g(B_{t_{\alpha}})|\bigl|e^{-i\int_{0}^{T_{t}^{c}}a(B_{s})\circ dB_{s}}-e^{-i\int_{0}^{t_{\alpha}}a(B_{s})\circ dB_{s}}\bigr|\Bigr]dx

\displaystyle\leq e^{t\delta}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{W},P}^{x,0}\left[|g(B_{t_{\alpha}})|\left|\int_{t_{\alpha}}^{T_{t}^{c}}a(B_{s})\cdot dB_{s}\right|\right]dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{e^{t\delta}}{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{W},P}^{x,0}\left[|g(B_{t_{\alpha}})|\left|\int_{t_{\alpha}}^{T_{t}^{c}}\nabla\!\cdot a(B_{s})\,ds\right|\right]dx.

We show the convergence of the first term. The second one follows similarly to the first one. We have

\displaystyle e^{t\delta}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{W},P}^{x,0}\left[|g(B_{t_{\alpha}})|\left|\int_{t_{\alpha}}^{T_{t}^{c}}a(B_{s})\cdot dB_{s}\right|\right]dx

\displaystyle=e^{t\delta}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{W},P}^{x,0}\left[|g(B_{t_{\alpha}})|\left|\int_{t_{\alpha}}^{T_{t}^{c}}a(B_{s})\cdot dB_{s}\right|\mathbbm{1}_{\{T_{t}^{c}\geq{t_{\alpha}}\}}\right]dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+e^{t\delta}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{W},P}^{x,0}\left[|g(B_{t_{\alpha}})|\left|\int_{t_{\alpha}}^{T_{t}^{c}}a(B_{s})\cdot dB_{s}\right|\mathbbm{1}_{\{T_{t}^{c}\leq{t_{\alpha}}\}}\right]dx.

It suffices to show the convergence of the first term, since the second term can be treated in the same way as the first. By Corollary 2.5, we see that

\displaystyle\left(e^{t\delta}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{W},P}^{x,0}\left[|g(B_{t_{\alpha}})|\left|\int_{t_{\alpha}}^{T_{t}^{c}}a(B_{s})\cdot dB_{s}\right|\mathbbm{1}_{\{T_{t}^{c}\geq{t_{\alpha}}\}}\right]dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{2t\delta}{\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}^{2}{\left\lVert g\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{W},P}^{x,0}\left[\left|\int_{t_{\alpha}}^{T_{t}^{c}}a(B_{s})\cdot dB_{s}\right|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{T_{t}^{c}\geq{t_{\alpha}}\}}\right]dx

\displaystyle=e^{2t\delta}{\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}^{2}{\left\lVert g\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{W},P}^{x,0}\left[\int_{t_{\alpha}}^{T_{t}^{c}}|a(B_{s})|^{2}ds\mathbbm{1}_{\{T_{t}^{c}\geq{t_{\alpha}}\}}\right]dx

\displaystyle\leq e^{2t\delta}{\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}^{2}{\left\lVert g\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}}^{2}{\left\lVert a\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{P}^{0}[|T_{t}^{c}-t_{\alpha}|^{2}]\to\;\;0\qquad(c\to\infty).

In the second equality, we used the Itô-isometry. Hence
e^{-tH_{c}^{\alpha}}{\to}e^{-tH_{\alpha}}
on
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
c\to{\infty}
. By the limiting argument by using the uniform boundness of
\|e^{-tH_{c}^{\alpha}}\|
, the lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Suppose (A.2). Then there exists
(a_{R})_{R>0}\subset{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
such that

\hskip-10.00002pt\mathcal{W}^{x}\left(\left|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}a(B_{s})\circ{dB_{s}}-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}a_{R}(B_{s})\circ{dB_{s}}\right|\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq 4\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{2{\pi}t}}\displaystyle\int_{R}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2t}}dy\right)^{d}

(3.3)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\geq 0
. In particular,
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}a_{R}(B_{s})\circ{dB_{s}}\to\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}a(B_{s})\circ{dB_{s}}
in probability as
R\to{\infty}
.

Proof.

The proof of the lemma is based on
[
HIL2009
, Proposition 3.7]
. Let
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be
0\leq\chi\leq 1
,
\chi(x)=1
for
|x|<1
, and
\chi(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 2
. Let
\chi_{R}=\chi(\frac{x_{1}}{R})\cdots\chi(\frac{x_{d}}{R})
,
R>0
.
Define
a_{R}
by
a_{R}(x)=\chi_{R}(x)a(x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Furthermore, let

\Omega_{+}(R)=\{\omega\in\mathscr{X}\displaystyle|\;\max\limits_{0\leq s\leq t,\;1\leq\mu\leq d}B_{s}^{\mu}\leq{R}\},

\;\;\Omega_{-}(R)=\{\omega\in\mathscr{X}\displaystyle|\;\min\limits_{0\leq s\leq t,\;1\leq\mu\leq d}B_{s}^{\mu}\geq{-R}\}

and

\displaystyle I(R)

\displaystyle=\left|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}a(B_{s})\cdot dB_{s}-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}a_{R}(B_{s})\cdot dB_{s}\right|,

\displaystyle J(R)

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}{\nabla}{\cdot}a(B_{s})ds-\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}{\nabla}{\cdot}a(B_{s})ds\right|.

Then we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{x}\left(\left|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}a(B_{s})\circ{dB_{s}}-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}a_{R}(B_{s})\circ{dB_{s}}\right|\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq{\mathcal{W}^{x}\biggl(I(R)\geq{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\biggr)}+{\mathcal{W}^{x}\biggl(J(R)\geq{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\biggr)}.

To prove (3.3), we only show that the first term satisfies

\mathcal{W}^{x}\biggl(I(R)\geq{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\biggr)\leq 2\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{2{\pi}t}}\displaystyle\int_{R}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2t}}dy\right)^{d}.

The second term is dominated by the same bound and can be estimated in the same way as the first.
We can see that
\max\limits_{0\leq s\leq t,\;1\leq\mu\leq d}B_{s}^{\mu}
and
\min\limits_{0\leq s\leq t,\;1\leq\mu\leq d}B_{s}^{\mu}
have the same distribution. Hence we have

\mathcal{W}^{x}(\Omega_{-}(R))=\mathcal{W}^{x}(\Omega_{+}(R))=\prod_{\mu=1}^{d}\mathcal{W}^{x}(|B_{t}^{\mu}|\leq{R})=\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{2{\pi}t}}\displaystyle\int_{R}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2t}}dy\right)^{d}.

It follows that
I(R)=0
on
\Omega_{+}(R)\cap\Omega_{-}(R)
for all
0\leq{s}\leq{t}
. Thus we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{x}\biggl(I(R)\geq{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}^{x}\biggl(I(R)\geq{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}},\Omega_{+}(R)^{c}\cup\;\Omega_{-}(R)^{c}\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{2{\pi}t}}\displaystyle\int_{R}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2t}}dy\right)^{d}.

Thus the lemma is proved.
∎
