Full Mealy automata, complete square complexes, and anti-tori
David Pask

Abstract
. To a full m × n m\times n Mealy automaton A A we associate a bijection θ A \theta_{A} , a one-vertex rank-two graph F θ A F_{\theta_{A}} , and a one-vertex V ​ H VH -square complex Y A Y_{A} tiled by m ​ n mn Wang tiles. We prove that Y A Y_{A} contains an anti-torus if and only if A A is bi-reversible and F θ A F_{\theta_{A}} is aperiodic. The two hypotheses are independent and play disjoint roles: bi-reversibility is exactly what makes Y A Y_{A} a complete square complex, so that its universal cover splits as a product of two trees and anti-tori can be discussed at all; and, within that setting, an anti-torus is precisely a period-free configuration in the two-sided path space of F θ A F_{\theta_{A}} , whose existence is the aperiodicity condition. Working at the level of configurations removes any appeal to the geometry of products of trees from the main equivalence; the geometric (loop-spanned) form of Wise is shown to be strictly stronger, the lamplighter being aperiodic with no loop-spanned anti-torus.

Background and motivation

This paper grew out of the author’s interests in

-graphs
[

,

]
.

The attention paid to Burger–Mozes groups
[

]
in this circle of ideas
led the author to the work of Glasner and Mozes

, in which Mealy automata

The literature on complete square complexes led in turn to

2.
Introduction

A full Mealy automaton over alphabets of sizes
m
and
n
is the same data as a
bijection
\theta\colon V_{m}\times S_{n}\to S_{n}\times V_{m}
, equivalently a one-vertex
rank-two graph
F_{\theta}

[
10
,
18
]
, equivalently a set of
mn
Wang tiles closed
under the factorisation rules
[
7
,
14
]
. The same data presents a square
complex with a single vertex,
m
vertical and
n
horizontal loops, and
mn

squares. When this complex is a
complete square complex
(CSC) its universal
cover is a product of two trees
[
17
,
5
]
, and one asks whether it contains an

anti-torus
: a plane whose induced tiling is not periodic
[
17
, §5]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A=(V_{m},S_{n},\pi,\lambda)
be a full Mealy automaton with bijection
\theta_{A}
,
rank-two graph
F:=F_{\theta_{A}}
, and
VH
-square complex
Y:=Y_{A}
. Then
Y

contains an anti-torus if and only if
A
is bi-reversible and
F
is aperiodic.

The proof separates the two hypotheses. Bi-reversibility is equivalent to four-way
determinism of the Wang tiles, hence to the link of the vertex being complete
bipartite, hence to
Y
being a CSC (Proposition
4.1
). Granted that,
the planes of the universal cover carry exactly the two-sided paths of
F
, with
periods matching (Proposition
5.1
); an anti-torus is then a
period-free configuration (Lemma
5.3
), whose existence is aperiodicity
(Proposition
5.4
). The geometric form of Wise, an anti-torus
spanned by two loop axes, is strictly stronger and is not implied by aperiodicity
alone (Proposition
6.1
).

3.
Preliminaries

A Mealy automaton
A=(V_{m},S_{n},\pi,\lambda)
has
\pi\colon V_{m}\times S_{n}\to V_{m}
and

\lambda\colon V_{m}\times S_{n}\to S_{n}
. It is
full
if

\pi\times\lambda\colon(u,b)\mapsto(\lambda(u,b),\pi(u,b))
is a bijection

V_{m}\times S_{n}\to S_{n}\times V_{m}
;
invertible
if each
\lambda(u,\cdot)
is a
bijection of
S_{n}
;
reversible
if each
\pi(\cdot,b)
is a bijection of

V_{m}
;
bi-reversible
if it is invertible, reversible, and its inverse is
reversible.
In this context, a Mealy automaton
A
is a complete, deterministic,
letter-to-letter (that is, synchronous) transducer with the same input and output
alphabet; see
[
9
,
6
]
for further details.

Each pair
(u,b)\in V_{m}\times S_{n}
gives a Wang tile
T_{(u,b)}
with

N=\lambda(u,b)
,
S=b
,
E=u
,
W=\pi(u,b)
, north/south coloured by letters and
east/west by states. We freely use the determinism dictionary

[
5
]
: a full
A
is se-deterministic, and
W_{A}
is
ne-deterministic
\iff
reversible, sw-deterministic
\iff
invertible, four-way
deterministic
\iff
bi-reversible.

Definition 3.1

(One-vertex rank-two graph; cf.
[
18
]
,

[
10
]
)
.

Let
\theta\colon V_{m}\times S_{n}\to S_{n}\times V_{m}
be a bijection, written

\theta(u,b)=(\lambda(u,b),\pi(u,b))
, and let
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}
denote the standard
generators of
\mathbb{N}^{2}
. The associated
one-vertex rank-two graph

F_{\theta}
is
the monoid

F_{\theta}=\big\langle\,V_{m}\sqcup S_{n}\ \big|\ u\,b=\lambda(u,b)\,\pi(u,b)\ \text{for all }(u,b)\in V_{m}\times S_{n}\,\big\rangle,

with identity
\ast
, equipped with the degree functor
d\colon F_{\theta}\to\mathbb{N}^{2}

determined by
d(\ast)=(0,0)
,
d(u)=\varepsilon_{1}
for
u\in V_{m}
and
d(b)=\varepsilon_{2}
for

b\in S_{n}
(so
d(w)
records the numbers of
V_{m}
- and
S_{n}
-letters in any word
representing
w
). Viewing
F_{\theta}
as a category with the single object
\ast
,
the pair
(F_{\theta},d)
is a rank-two graph: for every
w\in F_{\theta}
and all

p,q\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
with
d(w)=p+q
there are unique
w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime}\in F_{\theta}
with
d(w^{\prime})=p
,

d(w^{\prime\prime})=q
and
w=w^{\prime}w^{\prime\prime}
. The commuting square through each pair
(u,b)
is the
factorisation
u\,b=\lambda(u,b)\,\pi(u,b)
, that is, the Wang tile
T_{(u,b)}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Bijectivity of
\theta
is exactly the factorisation property, and conversely every
rank-two graph with a single vertex is
F_{\theta}
for a unique such
\theta
. For a
full automaton
A
we write
F:=F_{\theta_{A}}
, where
\theta_{A}=\pi\times\lambda
; the
hypothesis that
A
be full is precisely what makes
F_{\theta_{A}}
a rank-two graph. Let
W_{A}
denote the set of tiles
T_{(u,b)}
where
(u,b)\in V\times S
, indexed by
\mathcal{C}_{T_{A}}
.

Let
\Delta
denote the rank-two graph with object set
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, morphisms

\{(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}:p\leq q\}
, and degree map
d(p,q)=q-p
(see
[
10
]
).

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\Lambda
be a rank-two graph. The
two-sided infinite path space
of

\Lambda
is

\Lambda^{\Delta}~=~\{\,x:\Delta\to\Lambda:x\text{ is a degree-preserving
functor}\,\}.

Following
[
10
]
, the two-sided path space
F^{\Delta}
of
F=F_{\theta_{A}}
is
identified with the set of tilings of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by
W_{A}
respecting the
factorisation rules. For
x\in F^{\Delta}
a
period
is a
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with

x(\cdot+p,\cdot+p)=x
; the periods form a subgroup
\operatorname{Per}(F,x)\leq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and we set

\operatorname{Per}(F)=\bigcap_{x}\operatorname{Per}(F,x)
.

Definition 3.4
.

F
is
aperiodic
if some
x\in F^{\Delta}
has
\operatorname{Per}(F,x)=\{0\}
; otherwise it
is
periodic
.

Remark 3.5

(Periodic dichotomy)
.

By Davidson–Yang
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
, exactly one of
the following holds: either a single nonzero
(a,-b)
lies in
\operatorname{Per}(F,x)
for every

x
(periodic), or some configuration is period-free (aperiodic). In the periodic
case the period is implemented by a bijection
\gamma\colon F^{a\varepsilon_{1}}\to F^{b\varepsilon_{2}}
with
\mu\nu=\gamma(\mu)\gamma^{-1}(\nu)
.

The following result appears in
[
4
, Corollary 4.1]
.

Proposition 3.6

(Aperiodicity criterion)
.

Write
\theta(v_{i},s_{j})=(\alpha_{v_{i}}(s_{j}),\beta_{s_{j}}(v_{i}))
, where

\alpha_{v_{i}}\colon S_{n}\to S_{n}
and
\beta_{s_{j}}\colon V_{m}\to V_{m}
. If there is a
subset
B\subseteq S_{n}
with
|B|\geq 2
and a word
i_{1}\ldots i_{k}\in V_{m}^{k}
such that

\alpha_{i_{1}}\circ\alpha_{i_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ\alpha_{i_{k}}(B)=B
, then
F_{\theta}
is
aperiodic. Similarly, if there is a subset
A\subseteq V_{m}
with
|A|\geq 2
and a word

j_{1}\ldots j_{k}\in S_{n}^{k}
such that

\beta_{j_{1}}\circ\beta_{j_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ\beta_{j_{k}}(A)=A
, then
F_{\theta}
is
aperiodic.

Proof.

We prove the contrapositive of each statement. Suppose
F_{\theta}
is not aperiodic.
By Remark
3.5
it then has a period
(a,-b)
with

(a,b)\neq(0,0)
, so
x(m+a\varepsilon_{1},\,n+b\varepsilon_{2})=x(m,n)
for every
x\in F_{\theta}^{\Delta}

and every
(m,n)\in\Delta
. Reading this along the two axes shows that for every

j\in S_{n}
and every word
i_{1}\ldots i_{k}\in V_{m}^{k}
,

\alpha_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\alpha_{i_{k}}(j)=j,

and for every
i\in V_{m}
and every word
j_{1}\ldots j_{k}\in S_{n}^{k}
,

\beta_{j_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\beta_{j_{k}}(i)=i.

Thus no proper composition of the
\alpha
’s or
\beta
’s can fix a subset of size at
least two nontrivially, which is the required contrapositive.
∎

A one-vertex
VH
-square complex
Y
has subcomplexes
\mathcal{V}_{Y},\mathcal{H}_{Y}
of
vertical and horizontal edges; its link
\operatorname{Lk}(*)
is bipartite with
2m
vertical and

2n
horizontal germs, one edge per square corner.
Y
is a
complete square
complex
(CSC) if
\operatorname{Lk}(*)
is complete bipartite; then
\widetilde{Y}\cong\mathcal{V}_{\widetilde{Y}}\times\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{Y}}
is a product of two trees

[
17
, Theorem 3.8]
. We attach to
A
the complex
Y_{A}
with vertical loops
V_{m}
,
horizontal loops
S_{n}
, and one square per tile
T_{(u,b)}
.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
E,F
be directed graphs and let
p,q\colon F\to E
be surjective graph
morphisms. We call
T=(p,q\colon F\to E)
a
textile system
if the map

\iota\colon F^{1}\to E^{1}\times E^{1}\times F^{0}\times F^{0}
,

\iota(f)=(p(f),q(f),r(f),s(f))
, is injective.

Let
p\colon F\to E
be a graph morphism. We say that
p
has
unique
r
-path
lifting
(respectively,
unique
s
-path lifting
) if for every
e\in p(F^{1})

and every
w\in p^{-1}(r(e))
(respectively,
w\in p^{-1}(s(e))
) there is a unique

f\in wF^{1}
(respectively,
f\in F^{1}w
) with
p(f)=e
.

Definition 3.8

(Textile system from a Mealy automaton)
.

Let
A=(V,S,\pi,\lambda)

\pi\colon V\times S\to V

and graph morphisms

p(u,b)=\pi(u,b),\qquad q(u,b)=u\qquad\text{for }(u,b)\in V\times S=F^{1}.

4.
Bi-reversibility is completeness of the square complex

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
A
be full. Then
Y_{A}
is a complete square complex if and only if
A
is
bi-reversible.

Proof.

\operatorname{Lk}(*)
is bipartite with parts the
2m
vertical germs
\{u^{\pm}\}
and the
2n

horizontal germs
\{b^{\pm}\}
; the four corners of
T_{(u,b)}
contribute, one to
each sign pattern, the (north,east), (south,east), (north,west), (south,west)
colour pairs. Thus
\operatorname{Lk}(*)=K_{2m,2n}
if and only if every (vertical germ,
horizontal germ) pair occurs as exactly one corner, if and only if for each of the
four corner positions the map
T_{(u,b)}\mapsto
(its two colours there) is a
bijection from the
mn
tiles onto the
mn
possible pairs, if and only if
W_{A}
is
four-way deterministic, if and only if
A
is bi-reversible
[
5
]
. Equivalently,
the squares
\mathcal{C}_{T_{A}}
are complete (in the sense of
[
7
]
) if and only
if the textile maps
p,q
have unique
r
- and
s
-path lifting.
∎

If
A
is full but not bi-reversible,
Y_{A}
is not a CSC,
\widetilde{Y}_{A}
is not a
product of trees, and the planes below are undefined; such a
Y_{A}
has no
anti-torus for want of the ambient product geometry.

5.
Configurations, planes, and anti-tori

Assume henceforth that
A
is full and bi-reversible, so
\widetilde{Y}_{A}\cong\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{H}
(cf.
[
5
,
17
]
).

Proposition 5.1
.

There is a bijection between the planes
\gamma_{H}\times\gamma_{V}
of
\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{H}
spanned by a bi-infinite horizontal and vertical geodesic and the
configurations
x\in F^{\Delta}
, under which the induced square tiling of the plane
is
x
, and a translation by
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
preserves the tiling if and only if

p\in\operatorname{Per}(F,x)
.

Proof.

A plane in the product of trees is a copy of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
tiled by lifts of the squares;
reading the vertical and horizontal edges crossed gives bi-infinite words in
V_{m}
,

S_{n}
, and the factorisation rules of
F
are the local compatibility relations of
the tiles, so the tiling is a degree-preserving functor
x\colon\Delta\to F
, and
every
x
arises. A grid translation by
p
fixes the tiling if and only if

x(\cdot+p,\cdot+p)=x
, that is,
p\in\operatorname{Per}(F,x)
.
∎

Definition 5.2
.

A plane in
\widetilde{Y}_{A}
is an
anti-torus
if its induced tiling is not
periodic, i.e. has no nonzero period. We say
Y_{A}

contains an anti-torus

if some plane is one. (A fortiori such a plane does not factor through a torus

\mathbb{T}^{2}\to Y_{A}
.)

Lemma 5.3

(Configuration model of anti-tori)
.

A plane of
\widetilde{Y}_{A}
is an anti-torus if and only if its configuration

x\in F^{\Delta}
has
\operatorname{Per}(F,x)=\{0\}
.

Proof.

This is immediate from Proposition
5.1
: the plane has a nonzero period if and only if

\operatorname{Per}(F,x)\neq\{0\}
.
∎

Lemma
5.3
is the point of the present treatment: it turns the
geometric notion of an anti-torus into the purely combinatorial statement

\operatorname{Per}(F,x)=0
, with no further appeal to the structure theory of products of trees.

Proposition 5.4
.

With
A
full and bi-reversible,
Y_{A}
contains an anti-torus if and only if the
rank-two graph
F
is aperiodic.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3
a plane is an anti-torus if and only if its configuration is
period-free, and by Proposition
5.1
every configuration occurs.
Hence
Y_{A}
contains an anti-torus if and only if some
x\in F^{\Delta}
has
\operatorname{Per}(F,x)=\{0\}
,
which is Definition
3.4
.
∎

6.
The Wise loop-spanned form

Wise’s anti-tori
[
17
]
are built from a pair of loops. Call a plane of

\widetilde{Y}_{A}

loop-spanned
if both its geodesics are axes of loops, i.e. if it is
\operatorname{axis}(a)\times\operatorname{axis}(b)
for a vertical loop
a
and a horizontal loop
b
;
write
x_{a,b}
for its configuration, whose central cross is the periodic pair

(a^{\infty},b^{\infty})
. A
Wise anti-torus
is a loop-spanned plane that is
period-free — equivalently, a pair
(a,b)
no positive powers of which commute. One
might hope aperiodicity supplies such a plane; it does not.

Proposition 6.1
.

A Wise anti-torus is an anti-torus in the sense of Definition
5.2
;
hence if
Y_{A}
has a Wise anti-torus then
A
is bi-reversible and
F
is aperiodic.
The converse is
false
: there are full bi-reversible
A
with
F
aperiodic for
which every loop-spanned plane is periodic, so
Y_{A}
has no Wise anti-torus.

Proof.

A loop-spanned period-free plane is in particular period-free, hence an anti-torus,
so
A
is bi-reversible and
F
aperiodic by Theorem
2.1
. For the failure
of the converse take the lamplighter
\theta_{4}^{a}
of Appendix
A
, which is
bi-reversible with
F
aperiodic. Since
\lambda(v_{i},\cdot)\equiv\mathrm{id}
, the
north and south labels of every tile agree, so the horizontal label is constant up
each column, say
b_{i}
, and the states propagate horizontally,

a_{i-1,j}=\pi(a_{i,j},b_{i})
, giving
a_{i,j}=\sigma^{c_{i}}(a_{0,j})
for suitable

c_{i}\in\{0,1\}
; choosing the vertical word
(a_{0,j})_{j}
aperiodic gives

\operatorname{Per}(F,x)=\{0\}
, so
F
is aperiodic. But a loop-spanned plane has
(a_{0,j})_{j}
a
periodic axis, which forces a vertical period; so no loop-spanned plane of

\theta_{4}^{a}
is period-free.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

The two notions therefore genuinely differ, the obstruction being the

realisation
of a period-free configuration on loop axes. Four-way determinism
makes the loop-spanned configurations dense in
F^{\Delta}
, and the Davidson–Yang
dichotomy given in Remark
3.5
controls which periods occur once
F

is
periodic; but density together with that control does not produce a
period-free loop-spanned plane — the periodic loop-spanned planes of
\theta_{4}^{a}

need not share a single period, so they impose none on
F
. The genuinely
two-dimensional, free behaviour that does carry a Wise
anti-torus first appears at
m=2
,
n=3
(Example
8.1
), whose tiling has both
axes constant, hence loop-spanned. Characterising the aperiodic
F
that admit a Wise
anti-torus is exactly the question left to
[
17
,
5
]
.

Relation to square-complex groups

The loop-spanned form is the point of contact with the group-theoretic theory of
anti-tori in
(2m,2n)
–groups developed by Rattaggi
[
15
]
, following
Bridson–Wise and Wise
[
17
]
.

Remark 6.3

(The loop-spanned form is Rattaggi’s group-theoretic anti-torus)
.

When
A
is full and bi-reversible,
Y_{A}
is a complete square complex and

\pi_{1}(Y_{A})
is a
(2m,2n)
–group in the sense of
[
15
, §1]
: its link is the
complete bipartite graph
K_{2m,2n}
(Proposition
4.1
), which is exactly
Rattaggi’s link condition, and the
mn
squares — whose boundaries alternate
between
V_{m}
-edges and
S_{n}
-edges — are his relator set
R_{m\cdot n}
. The two
factors
\langle V_{m}\rangle
and
\langle S_{n}\rangle
are then free of ranks
m
and

n

[
15
, Corollary 2(1)]
, and
\pi_{1}(Y_{A})
is centreless once
m,n\geq 2

[
15
, Corollary 2(2)]
. Under this dictionary a Wise anti-torus, spanned by the
axes of
a\in\langle V_{m}\rangle
and
b\in\langle S_{n}\rangle
, is precisely an
anti-torus in the sense of
[
15
, Definition 4]
: a pair with

a^{r}b^{s}\neq b^{s}a^{r}
for all
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
(equivalently, no
positive powers of
a,b
commute). Thus Rattaggi’s definition attaches to the

stronger
, loop-spanned notion of this section — not to the configuration
anti-torus of Definition
5.2
, which has no direct group-theoretic
counterpart and is the genuinely new object isolated by Lemma
5.3
.

Remark 6.4

(Rattaggi’s centralizer criterion in automaton terms)
.

Rattaggi’s homomorphism
\rho_{v}
from one free factor to the symmetric group on the
generators of the other
[
15
, §1]
, defined on generators by

a^{-1}ba^{\prime}=\tilde{b}
, is on single generators our corner permutation: it is the map

\beta_{b}\colon V_{m}\to V_{m}
of Proposition
3.6
(and the transposed
homomorphism recovers the
\alpha
’s). His Lemma 3 then reads: if some generator

b
acts by a fixed-point-free corner permutation —
\beta_{b}(a)\neq a
for all

a
— then
Z_{\pi_{1}(Y_{A})}(b)=\langle b\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}

[
15
, Lemma 3]
. This is a finite, tile-level check on a single corner map.

We stress, however, that the passage from a cyclic centralizer to an anti-torus

for every

a\neq 1

[
15
, Corollary 8]
, and the dichotomy “anti-torus

\iff
non-commuting”
[
15
, Proposition 5]
, both require
\pi_{1}(Y_{A})
to be

commutative transitive
. Rattaggi’s quaternionic groups
\Gamma_{p,l}
are
commutative transitive
[
15
, Proposition 14]
, but a generic bi-reversible full
automaton group is not, and we do not assume it. Lemma 3 transfers unconditionally
and yields the centralizer collapse; the anti-torus conclusion does not. This is
precisely why the configuration/aperiodicity route of Theorem
2.1
, rather
than the group-theoretic dichotomy, is what detects an anti-torus in general: in
Rattaggi’s commutative-transitive world the configuration-level subtlety isolated by
Lemma
5.3
does not arise, whereas here it is unavoidable.

(Irreducibility)

If

contains a Wise anti-torus, then

is an irreducible lattice in

Proof.

A Wise anti-torus is an anti-torus in
\pi_{1}(Y_{A})
in the sense of

[
15
, Definition 4]
(Remark
6.3
); apply

[
15
, Proposition 9]
, due to Wise.
∎

Example 8.1

(A bi-reversible aperiodic automaton: an anti-torus)
.

Let
V=\{f_{0},f_{1}\}
and

[

\begin{array}[]{c|cccc}T_{(u,b)}&N&S&E&W\\
\hline\cr T_{(f_{0},g_{0})}&g_{1}&g_{0}&f_{0}&f_{0}\\

,

Concretely, propagating the unique tiling with all-

Example 8.2

(Full but not four-way deterministic: no complete square complex)
.

The automaton

9.
Remarks

Remark 9.1

(The hypotheses are independent)
.

The bijection
\theta_{2}
in Table
1
is bi-reversible but periodic, so aperiodicity cannot be dropped. In the
other direction, a full automaton that is not four-way deterministic — e.g.
\theta_{7}
of Example
8.2
— does not
present a complete square complex, so its ambient space is not a product of two trees
and bi-reversibility cannot be dropped either. The two requirements are logically
independent; at
2\times 2
they are realised separately, the only aperiodic

2\times 2
automaton (the lamplighter) being itself bi-reversible, so that the
genuinely mixed case appears only for larger alphabets.

Remark 9.2

(Configuration versus loop form)
.

Lemma
5.3
recasts an anti-torus (Definition
5.2
) as a
period-free configuration, so the main equivalence (Theorem
2.1
) is
internal to the rank-two graph, with no appeal to
[
17
,
5
]
. The geometric
loop-spanned form of
[
17
]
is strictly stronger
(Proposition
6.1
) and is
not
equivalent to aperiodicity — the
lamplighter (Appendix
A
) is aperiodic with no Wise anti-torus — so the
loop-realisation step is exactly what still rests on
[
17
,
5
]
.

Remark 9.3

(Higher rank)
.

The template suggests a rank-
d
statement: a full
d
-dimensional automaton with

2^{d}
-way determinism presents a one-vertex rank-
d
graph and a cube complex whose
universal cover is a product of
d
trees, and “contains a
d
-dimensional
anti-torus” should be full determinism together with aperiodicity of the rank-
d

graph. The determinism half is routine; the periodicity half needs the rank-
d

analogue of
[
4
]
, and the cube complex requires an associativity constraint
absent at
d=2
.
