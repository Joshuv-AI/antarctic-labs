Robust rice-crab detection via receptive-field attention and feature reassembly in unstructured agricultural scenes
Authors: Zhipeng He, Xinying Miao, Xiaodong Li, Na Sun, Yi Zhang, Menghan Liu, Jingjing Shan, Huatang Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate and real-time detection of rice crabs in paddy-field aquaculture is crucial for precise feeding and behavior monitoring. This task is challenging due to the high textural similarity between crabs and their environment, partial occlusion from rice blossoms, and difficult environmental conditions such as variable illumination and adverse weather, which lead to feature confusion and detail loss. To address these challenges, we propose YOLO-RCCS, a novel rice-crab detection model based on an improved YOLO11n architecture. We introduce a receptive-field attention (RFA) mechanism to dynamically generate content-aware convolutional kernels, enhancing the model’s ability to distinguish target features from background noise. We then augment the model with a coordinate-aware attention (CAA) module to capture long-range spatial dependencies, improving target localization. Furthermore, we replace the original upsampling layer with the CARAFE module, which uses content-aware feature reassembly to mitigate detail loss and preserve local textures. Finally, we employ the SlideLoss function to address class imbalance by dynamically re-weighting hard samples, thereby stabilizing the training process. Experimental results demonstrate that YOLO-RCCS achieves a 2.1% increase in precision, a 4.3% increase in recall, and a 3.5% increase in mAP@0.5 compared to the baseline YOLO11n, while maintaining comparable computational complexity. The proposed model shows promise as a potential solution for all-weather monitoring and precise feeding in rice-crab aquaculture, indicating strong feasibility for real-time applications under laboratory conditions.

Introduction

The integrated rice-crab co-culture system constitutes a paradigm of ecological sustainability and agricultural efficiency, actively fostering mutual quality enhancements for both commodities while optimizing land utilization across an expanse that now exceeds 160,000 hectares in China. Despite these agronomic advantages, the economic viability of this model remains heavily tethered to production costs, where feed inputs disproportionately account for over half of operational expenditures1. Conventional feeding regimes, predicated on manual distribution and empirical estimation, frequently fail to align with the species’ circadian rhythms and foraging ethology. Specifically, the predominantly nocturnal activity of rice crabs creates a temporal mismatch with labor-intensive manual observation, rendering accurate assessment of physiological needs during peak feeding hours impractical2. This discordance inevitably precipitates excessive feed wastage and water quality degradation, thereby eroding profit margins3. Consequently, the development of automated, intelligent monitoring systems capable of elucidating crab behavior for precision feeding represents not merely an incremental technical upgrade, but an indispensable imperative for the sustainable evolution of the industry4.

In this context, computer vision and deep learning-based object detection emerge as a transformative technological framework. By facilitating continuous, non-invasive, and all-weather surveillance, these systems generate the high-resolution behavioral analytics necessary to evolve from static feeding schedules to dynamic, on-demand precision nutrition5. Nevertheless, transposing object detection models from controlled laboratory settings to the unstructured heterogeneity of paddy fields introduces substantial technical and theoretical challenges that remain insufficiently addressed within the current academic landscape6.

The deployment of automated detection models in rice-crab co-culture systems is fundamentally constrained by the substantial domain gap between idealized datasets and in-situ environments. Unlike prior research conducted in controlled settings, such as indoor tanks or clear-water ponds, paddy fields present a highly heterogeneous environment characterized by cluttered backgrounds of mud, vegetation, and water7. The dynamic appearance of these elements, driven by fluctuating weather and illumination, creates a low-contrast scenario where crabs are effectively camouflaged, rendering models trained on clean data prone to high false-negative rates8.

Specifically, the high textural resemblance between the crab’s carapace and the muddy substrate leads to feature confusion, where models extract ambiguous or non-discriminative patterns9. Conventional CNNs, restricted by fixed-shape receptive fields, struggle to adaptively focus on the subtle cues necessary to differentiate the target from its surroundings. Consequently, this issue represents a fundamental shortcoming of existing feature extraction paradigms when applied to highly camouflaged objects, rather than a mere data deficiency. Beyond background interference, the physical configuration of the paddy field introduces severe technical impediments related to occlusion and scale variation10. Targets are frequently obscured by rice stalks and foliage, while their apparent size varies drastically with distance and posture. Although Feature Pyramid Networks (FPNs) are designed to mitigate scale variation, the feature upsampling process–often relying on nearest-neighbor interpolation–tends to compromise critical spatial details11. This loss of fine-grained information significantly hinders the localization and identification of small or partially occluded crabs. Compounding these structural and representational challenges is the pervasive issue of extreme class imbalance12. In typical paddy-field imagery, the preponderance of background pixels (water, mud, vegetation) compared to the sparse target area biases the training process toward easy negative samples. This imbalance often renders standard loss functions, such as cross-entropy, ineffective, leading to suboptimal convergence and poor recall performance.

The above analyses reveal a core contradiction in the development of automated monitoring systems for rice-crab coculture: while high detection accuracy is indispensable for reliable behavioral analysis and precision feeding, the practical deployment of such systems in remote agricultural scenarios is severely constrained by computational resources. Unlike cloud server infrastructure, field monitoring relies on edge computing devices with limited memory, power consumption, and parallel processing capabilities. Accordingly, pursuing marginal accuracy gains via increasingly cumbersome deep learning architectures is not only economically unsustainable but also technically impractical for real-time continuous operation. In light of this, the central research objective has evolved beyond the sole pursuit of maximum detection accuracy, toward achieving a principled balance between model compactness and representational capability. The primary motivation of this work is to establish a detection framework that judiciously reconciles the aforementioned competing objectives. We contend that genuine progress is not reflected in attaining state-of-the-art accuracy under ideal computational budgets, but in delivering robust and high-fidelity detection performance within the constraints of lightweight architectures that are theoretically suitable for edge deployment.

To surmount these impediments, we present YOLO-RCCS, a specialized evolution of the lightweight YOLO11n architecture engineered as a holistic framework tailored to bridge the identified theoretical and technical gaps13. The critical issue of feature confusion is addressed through the integration of the Receptive-Field Attention (RFA) mechanism, where the RFAConv operator dynamically generates content-aware convolutional kernels to adaptively model local receptive fields, thereby suppressing background noise to isolate subtle discriminative cues14. Complementing this feature enhancement, the model mitigates localization inaccuracies caused by occlusion via a Coordinate-Aware Attention (CAA) module15; by encoding long-range spatial dependencies, this component enables the precise inference of object geometry even under partial visibility, significantly bolstering recall in cluttered scenes. Furthermore, to counteract the degradation of fine-grained information inherent in standard upsampling, we employ the Content-Aware Reassembly of Features (CARAFE) operator16, which utilizes adaptive reassembly kernels to reconstruct the high-resolution semantics essential for detecting diminutive targets. Finally, the training stability is fortified against extreme class imbalance through the deployment of the SlideLoss function, an advanced optimization strategy that dynamically re-weights the loss contribution of hard samples to prevent the dominance of easy negatives and ensure robust convergence.

Related works

Object detection, a fundamental task in computer vision, has been revolutionized by deep learning. Modern object detectors are typically categorized into two-stage and one-stage approaches. Two-stage detectors, such as the R-CNN family17,18, first generate a sparse set of region proposals and then classify and refine them. Faster R-CNN19 introduced the Region Proposal Network (RPN), enabling end-to-end training and achieving a significant leap in performance. While accurate, two-stage methods often suffer from high computational costs. In contrast, one-stage detectors, like the Single Shot MultiBox Detector (SSD)20 and the You Only Look Once (YOLO) series21, treat object detection as a regression problem, directly predicting bounding boxes and class probabilities from the full image in a single pass. This design leads to substantially faster inference speeds, making one-stage detectors highly suitable for real-time applications. The YOLO family, in particular, has become a dominant paradigm in real-time object detection, undergoing rapid evolution since its inception. Each iteration has introduced architectural innovations to improve the trade-off between speed and accuracy. For example, YOLOv322 incorporated a multi-scale feature pyramid network (FPN) to enhance the detection of objects at different scales. More recent versions, from YOLOv4 to YOLO1123, have continued this trend by integrating advanced backbone architectures, novel feature fusion techniques, and more effective training strategies. Another significant development is the integration of transformers into object detection, as seen in DETR24 and its real-time variant, RT-DETR25, which have demonstrated competitive performance by framing object detection as a set prediction problem. For small object detection, Transformer-based architectures have offered additional research insights. Nguyen et al.26 proposed a Transformer-based detection framework that integrates the original high-resolution input image with its corresponding high-resolution feature maps via a novel adaptive contrast enhancement module, highlighting low-contrast regions and effectively improving detection performance. The HyDeTr framework27 employs a context-selective hybrid attention encoder and a density map prediction module to achieve accurate and density-aware query initialization and scale adaptation, thereby enhancing the recall rate and detection accuracy of small objects.

Computer vision has emerged as a powerful tool for advancing intelligent aquaculture, enabling automated monitoring of fish populations, behavior analysis, and health assessment28–30. Numerous studies have applied deep learning models to detect and track various aquatic species. For instance, researchers have successfully used YOLO-based models for fish detection in complex underwater scenes, often incorporating image enhancement techniques to counteract poor visibility and color distortion. These applications provide valuable insights for biomass estimation, feeding management, and welfare monitoring31,32. However, applying these technologies to crustacean detection, particularly crabs, introduces specific challenges. The irregular shape, varied postures, and tendency of crabs to blend in with their surroundings make them difficult targets. Previous research on crab detection has primarily been conducted in controlled environments, such as indoor laboratories or clear-water ponds33. For example, Cao et al.34 developed a lightweight model based on SSD for river crab detection in ponds, while other studies have focused on identifying molting status in controlled crab farming systems35. While these studies have laid important groundwork, they do not fully address the complexities of paddy-field environments, where the background is far more cluttered and dynamic. To improve the performance of object detectors in complex scenes, researchers have increasingly turned to attention mechanisms and advanced feature enhancement techniques. Attention mechanisms allow a neural network to selectively focus on the most informative parts of an input. The Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM)36, for example, sequentially applies channel and spatial attention to refine feature maps, leading to improved performance with minimal computational overhead. Our work leverages a similar concept with the Coordinate-Aware Attention (CAA) module37, which is particularly effective at capturing long-range dependencies in spatial dimensions.

Feature fusion is another critical aspect of modern object detectors. The Feature Pyramid Network (FPN)38 and its variants have become standard components for combining features from different network depths to handle objects at various scales. However, the upsampling process within these networks can be a source of information loss. To address this, novel upsampling operators like CARAFE (Content-Aware Reassembly of Features) have been proposed. CARAFE generates adaptive, content-aware kernels to reassemble features, preserving fine-grained details more effectively than traditional interpolation methods16. Our adoption of CARAFE is motivated by the need to maintain high-resolution feature representations for detecting small and partially occluded crabs.

To our knowledge, deep learning-based crab detection remains relatively under-explored in the academic community; nevertheless, several solutions have been proposed by researchers. Ali et al.39 introduced the CrabFormer Transformer model, which integrates RGB-D segmentation and pose estimation to address complex scenarios involving stacked Chesapeake blue crabs. This framework achieves an inference fast speed, making it suitable for automated sorting systems. Addressing partial occlusion in soft-shell mud crabs, Siripattanadilok et al.40 optimized the Faster R-CNN model and incorporated Grad-CAM visualization to interpret the model’s decision-making process, thereby enhancing detection reliability and providing an efficient solution for automated soft-shell crab production. Cao et al.41 proposed the Faster MSSDLite algorithm based on MobileNetV2 and SSD. By employing depthwise separable convolutions in the prediction layers and integrating an FPN to improve multi-scale crab detection accuracy, the algorithm achieves a faster detection speed, rendering it appropriate for real-time underwater monitoring.Fig. 2Data augmentation. Materials and methods

Data acquisition and dataset construction

Experimental data acquisition was conducted at Panjin Guanghe Crab Industry Co., Ltd., utilizing a custom-designed non-invasive monitoring system engineered to minimize ecological disturbance within the paddy field. This integrated infrastructure comprises environmental sensor and image acquisition modules that synchronously record water quality parameters–specifically dissolved oxygen, pH, and temperature–alongside illumination levels and crab feeding behaviors. To guarantee autonomous long-term functionality, the system employs a dual solar-grid power supply that facilitates continuous data aggregation via a gateway to a cloud server, with real-time accessibility provided through a WeChat interface (Fig. 1).

Video footage was captured across a diverse spectrum of environmental scenarios, including sunny, rainy, windy, and nocturnal conditions, from which we extracted frames and rigorously filtered redundancy to yield an initial corpus of 5,223 unique images. This baseline dataset was subsequently expanded to 8,980 images through geometric and photometric augmentation techniques such as random rotation, translation, blurring, and brightness adjustment (Fig. 2), To avoid performance overestimation caused by highly similar frames extracted from the same video sequence being distributed across the training, validation, and test sets, a rigorous deduplication and partitioning procedure was implemented after frame extraction. Specifically, candidate frames were first extracted from the raw videos at fixed time intervals. Subsequently, the structural similarity index was computed between each pair of consecutive frames; whenever the SSIM value exceeded 0.85, only one of the two frames was retained, thereby removing redundant similar frames. Following this deduplication step, all images were grouped according to their source video segments, ensuring that frames from the same continuous video segment were assigned entirely to one of the training, validation, or test splits rather than being randomly scattered across multiple splits. This strategy effectively prevents data leakage. Finally, the entire deduplicated image set was partitioned into training, validation, and test subsets at a ratio of 8:1:1.Fig. 3Main structure diagram of YOLO-RCCS, where C3k2_RFAConv and C2PSA_CAA represent the improved modules that distinguish our method from YOLO11.

The proposed YOLO-RCCS model

To navigate the exigencies of real-time crab detection within heterogeneous paddy-field environments, we engineered YOLO-RCCS, an advanced architecture optimized to maintain high accuracy and efficiency amidst environmental disturbances such as variable illumination, weather fluctuations, and physical occlusion. The model is founded upon the lightweight YOLO11n framework, a baseline selected for its intrinsic balance between rapid inference velocity and computational resource efficiency, rendering it ideal for deployment on edge-computing platforms. Furthermore, the robust multi-scale feature fusion structure inherent to YOLO11n provides a critical foundation for isolating small targets against complex backgrounds.

As illustrated in Fig. 3, the architectural evolution of YOLO-RCCS involves a systematic restructuring of the baseline model through four synergistic enhancements. To alleviate feature confusion, the standard C3k2 module is superseded by the C3k2_RFAConv unit, which introduces dynamic receptive fields to refine feature extraction within cluttered surroundings. Concurrently, the original C2PSA block is replaced by the C2PSA_CAA module, employing a channel and spatial co-attention mechanism that significantly bolsters the model’s capacity to resolve occlusions and scale variations. Complementing these feature-level refinements, we integrated the CARAFE upsampling module to mitigate the degradation of fine-grained spatial details during feature reassembly, while the training trajectory is stabilized through the SlideLoss function, which dynamically recalibrates sample weights to ensure robust convergence.Fig. 4C3K2_RFAConv architecture.Fig. 5RFAConv computation process.

SlideLoss function

A pervasive challenge in robust rice-crab detection is the inherent class imbalance between the sparse target instances (crabs) and the predominant background elements (water, vegetation, soil), where negative samples disproportionately outnumber positive ones. This disparity frequently biases the training process toward easy negative samples, thereby degrading generalization performance. To counteract this optimization bias, we introduce the SlideLoss function, an adaptive re-weighting strategy that utilizes the global average Intersection over Union (IoU) as a dynamic threshold, denoted as β, to stratify samples into positive and negative categories. By dynamically amplifying the attention mechanism toward hard samples, SlideLoss ensures that scarce but critical instances are learned more effectively. Experiments and results

All experimental validations were conducted on a high-performance workstation operating under the Windows 10 environment, equipped with an Intel Core i9-14900KF CPU and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU (24 GB VRAM), with the deep learning framework implemented in PyTorch 2.0.0 accelerated by CUDA 11.8. The model training protocol encompassed 200 epochs utilizing the Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) optimizer with a batch size of 16, during which input images were resized to a resolution of 640 × 640. Discussion

The experimental results substantiate the efficacy of YOLO-RCCS in addressing the inherent complexities of rice-crab detection within unstructured paddy fields. The superior performance, marked by a 3.5% increase in mAP@0.5 over the baseline YOLO11n, can be attributed to the synergistic interaction of the four proposed architectural enhancements. Specifically, the RFA mechanism addresses the core issue of feature confusion by generating content-aware kernels. Unlike static convolutions that struggle against the high textural similarity between crabs and muddy substrates, RFA dynamically adapts its focus, effectively suppressing background noise and amplifying subtle discriminative cues. This is further corroborated by the heatmap visualizations, which depict a distinct shift from diffuse background activation in the baseline model to a highly concentrated focus on target boundaries in YOLO-RCCS. Furthermore, the integration of the CAA and the CARAFE upsampling operator provides a robust solution to occlusion and scale variation. CAA facilitates the capture of long-range spatial dependencies, enabling the model to infer the full geometry of partially obscured crabs based on visible fragments. Concurrently, CARAFE preserves the fine-grained textural fidelity that is typically lost during standard nearest-neighbor interpolation. The quantitative gains in Recall validate this, indicating a marked reduction in false negatives for small or occluded targets. Lastly, the employment of the SlideLoss function rectifies the optimization bias caused by extreme foreground-background class imbalance, ensuring that the training process prioritizes the learning of critical, hard samples over the dominant easy negatives.

While the proposed model demonstrates SOTA performance within a lightweight computational footprint, certain limitations present avenues for future investigation. The current dataset, although comprehensive and augmented, was derived from a single geographical location. Given the variability in soil composition and vegetation density across different rice-crab co-culture regions, the model’s generalization capacity warrants further validation on more geographically diverse data. Moreover, while the parameter count is eminently suitable for edge computing, rigorous real-world deployment testing on ultra-low-power embedded devices remains a logical subsequent step. Nevertheless, these factors do not detract from the current findings but rather chart a clear course for future research focused on data diversification and on-device optimization to further solidify the model’s applicability in precision aquaculture.

Conclusions and future work

This study systematically addressed the multifaceted challenges associated with in-situ rice-crab detection within heterogeneous paddy-field environments through the development of YOLO-RCCS, a specialized evolution of the YOLO11n architecture. The architectural efficacy of the proposed model is derived from the synergistic integration of four strategic enhancements: the C3k2_RFAConv module, which facilitates adaptive feature extraction; the C2PSA_CAA module, designed to refine spatial localization; the CARAFE operator, ensuring high-fidelity feature upsampling; and the SlideLoss function, which stabilizes training convergence amidst severe class imbalance. Comprehensive empirical evaluations demonstrate that YOLO-RCCS significantly surpasses both the baseline model and disparate state-of-the-art detectors, securing a 3.5% increment in mAP@0.5 while preserving a lightweight computational footprint that demonstrates good potential for real-time applications under laboratory conditions. Furthermore, qualitative visualization analyses corroborate the model’s robustness, evidencing superior detection stability across a spectrum of challenging, real-world environmental scenarios.

Notwithstanding these promising outcomes, the current study acknowledges certain limitations that warrant further investigation. Primarily, the dataset, while extensive, relies on data acquisition from a single geographical locus, potentially constraining the model’s generalization capabilities when applied to regions exhibiting distinct environmental or morphological characteristics. Moreover, although the architecture is theoretically optimized for efficiency, rigorous validation on ultra-low-power edge devices remains to be extensively conducted. Consequently, future research initiatives will prioritize the augmentation of dataset diversity to encompass a broader array of environmental scenes and crab species. Concurrently, we intend to investigate advanced model compression and quantization techniques to further streamline deployment on resource-constrained hardware, ultimately advancing the realization of fully autonomous, real-time monitoring and precision feeding systems for sustainable rice-crab aquaculture.
