Tool-in-lesion confirmation and diagnostic yield of digital tomosynthesis-integrated robotic bronchoscopic biopsy of lung nodules
Authors: Joyce W. Y. Chan, Aliss T. C. Chang, Clarence H. W. Chan, Rainbow W. H. Lau, Calvin S. H. Ng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTransbronchial lung biopsy with electromagnetic navigation (EMN) has reported diagnostic yield of 60–70%. Newer systems with active robotic control overcome the limitations of older manually controlled systems, but accuracy may still be limited by computed tomography (CT)-to-body divergence. The novel digital tomosynthesis-integrated Galaxy SystemTM (Noah Medical) incorporating Tool-in-Lesion Tomography (TiLT+TM) technology enables real-time lesion location updates and tool-in-lesion (TIL) confirmation. The aim of this study is to evaluate the TIL percentage using TiLT+TM and cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT).MethodsBetween September 2023 and October 2024, data of 27 lung nodules in 25 patients who underwent peripheral lung nodule biopsy using the Galaxy SystemTM in the hybrid operating room were prospectively collected. Procedure was performed using the robotic system until the deployed needle was determined to be within lesion based on digital tomosynthesis. A CBCT was then used to confirm the actual location of needle tip in relation to lesion.ResultsSeventeen out of 27 nodules were solid, the rest being mixed ground glass opacities (GGOs). Bronchus sign was positive in 66.7% of the patients. A mean of 3.1 cycles of digital tomosynthesis (range, 2–6 cycles) was required to achieve TIL on tomography before CBCT confirmation. Procedural success, defined as ability to complete biopsy using he robotic system, was 96.3%. TIL/tool-touching-lesion (TTL) was confirmed by CBCT in 25/27 nodules (92.6%), with 16/27 (59.3%) showing center-strike. Conservative diagnostic yield was 84%, consisting of 16 definitive malignant and 5 benign specific diagnoses. There were no complications.ConclusionsRobotic bronchoscopy with digital tomosynthesis is a safe and feasible method to reduce CT-to-body divergence and provide TIL confirmation, with good diagnostic yield.

Highlight box

Key findings

• Robotic bronchoscopy of lung nodules using Tool-in-Lesion Tomography (TiLT+TM) technology provides good diagnostic yield of 84%.

What is known and what is new?

• Robotic bronchoscopy is known to be limited by computed tomography (CT)-to-body divergence, limiting the diagnostic yield.

• The manuscript demonstrates that robotic bronchoscopy with digital tomosynthesis (TiLT+TM technology) resulted in 84% conservative diagnostic yield. The concordance rate between digital tomosynthesis and gold standard cone-beam CT findings is high, suggesting that the former is reliable.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Robotic bronchoscopy should be integrated with additional imaging to confirm its position, for example digital tomosynthesis, before a claim of tool-in-lesion can be made.

Introduction

Lung cancer is the second commonest cancer worldwide and the commonest cause of cancer deaths (1-3). In recent years, low dose computed tomography (CT) screening in high-risk populations revealed a high sensitivity and specificity of lung cancer detection, as evidenced from the National Lung Screening Trial (4), the NELSON study from Europe (5), and the Taiwan Lung Cancer Screening in Never-Smoker Trial (TALENT) (6). Together with the increasing availability of CT scans and a higher population awareness, there have been an exponential increase in the number of solitary pulmonary nodules being detected and referred for biopsy.

While standard flexible bronchoscopy is commonly used for evaluation of solitary lung nodules, its sensitivity for malignancy and diagnostic yield is markedly lower for smaller nodules located in the lung periphery. The diagnostic yield of a flexible bronchoscope for peripherally located lesions is 34% only when the size is less than 2 cm in diameter (7). Addition of electromagnetic navigation (EMN) and radial probe endobronchial ultrasound (rEBUS) may improve the diagnostic yield to 60% and 57% respectively (8), but still falls short of being efficient and satisfactory. This is often due to scope instability during biopsy, lack of direct visualization, and inability to manually manoeuvre the bronchoscope through progressively smaller airways at acute angles.

Compared to transbronchial biopsy, percutaneous biopsy has a higher reported diagnostic yield of around 80–90% (9). However, this advantage is hindered by a much higher rate of complications. The risk of pneumothorax with transthoracic needle biopsy is 18.8% versus 4.7% with transbronchial biopsy, while the bleeding risk is 6.4% versus 2.7% (10).

Newer bronchoscopic systems with active robotic control (steerable robotic tip) have therefore been developed to overcome the shortcomings of older manually controlled systems. Two major players in the field include Auris Monarch Endoscopy Platform by Johnson & Johnson (11) and Ion Endoluminal System by Intuitive Surgical (12). Studies have reported the diagnostic yield of latest robotic platforms within the range of 70–92% (13-15). Despite the improved stability of bronchoscope and the ability to reach much smaller airways, the accuracy of navigation and localization is still limited by CT-to-body divergence, which is the result of the difference in anatomy as it is represented in static preprocedural planning CT scans at full inspiration and the anatomy in the dynamic breathing lung during procedure. This could be due to differences in patient position, atelectasis, innate dynamics of the lung anatomy and other factors (16). Atelectasis could be a particularly evident problem in prolonged time to intubation, high fractions of inspired oxygen, and in obese individuals. This can result in a few millimeters to centimeters of error which is not trivial considering the usual small target nodule size.

The Galaxy SystemTM by Noah Medical is a new robotic digital tomosynthesis-integrated EMN-guided bronchoscopic platform with integrated Tool-in-Lesion Tomography (TiLT+TM) technology specifically designed to mitigate the effect of CT-to-body divergence. Tomosynthesis is an integrated software unit that provides the user with real-time updated lesion location and relative position of scope/tool tip. This is achieved by reconstructing a three-dimensional (3D) volume using two-dimensional (2D) fluoroscopy images acquired by the C-arm. The TiLT+TM technology combines conventional digital tomosynthesis with augmented fluoroscopy that provides continuous visualization of instrument-lesion relationship, offering additional intraoperative guidance beyond traditional digital tomosynthesis imaging. The bronchoscope has an outer diameter to 4.0 mm, working channel diameter of 2.1 mm, similar to other robotic platforms. We present this article in accordance with the STARD reporting checklist (available at https://tlcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tlcr-2026-0578/rc).

Methods

Prospective data of 27 lung nodules in 25 patients who underwent robotic bronchoscopic lung biopsy using the Galaxy System in Prince of Wales Hospital were collected between September 2023 and October 2024. Inclusion criteria included age more than 18 years old, lung nodule size between 1 and 3 cm, peripheral location of nodule, accessible nodules based on planning CT reconstruction, and moderate-to-high risk of lung cancer based on clinical, demographic and radiologic information according to the institute’s standard of care. Exclusion criteria included pregnancy, breastfeeding, pure ground glass opacities (GGOs), uncontrolled coagulopathy, systemic infection, history of lobectomy/pneumonectomy, moderate to severe hypoxia/hypercarbia, presence of pacemakers/defibrillators, and intolerance for bronchoscopy under general anaesthesia. Pre-procedure fine cut CT for planning was performed within 90 days of procedure, where airway segmentation, target identification, automated and/or manual path planning was performed using a 3D reconstruction software.

The procedure was performed in the hybrid operating room (Siemens) equipped with cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) (Artis Zeego® by Siemens Healthineer), which was eventually used to verify the relationship between needle (tool) and lesion. After the patient was intubated with single-lumen endotracheal tube under general anaesthesia, an electromagnetic field generator together with Galaxy TiLT+TM Board (incorporating reference points on fluoroscopy images thus enabling TiLT+TM) was placed beneath the patient’s chest. The robotic cart was parked in a pre-defined angle to operating table, and a CBCT spin was obtained to ensure the persistence of lesion and for measurement of nodule size. The robotic bronchoscope was then loaded onto the console and inserted into the endotracheal tube (Figure 1). The operator then “drives” the bronchoscope tip into patient’s airway using the controller, followed by airway registration by driving through the trachea and main bronchi. Following the preplanned pathway, the bronchoscope to driven to the target nodule, and once the distance to lesion is less than 3.5 cm, digital tomosynthesis was carried out (Figure 2A,2B). With a fluoroscopy sweep from 30 degrees left anterior oblique to 30 degrees right anterior oblique around the target of interest, the 2D images were stacked to create a section image using reconstruction algorithm. Operators can then visualise the real-time relation between tip of bronchoscope and the updated lesion position. By marking the centre of lesion, the updated lesion location is also projected on subsequent real time fluoroscopy image. Direction of scope tip can be adjusted using the controller, and a needle is deployed towards the target lesion. Afterwards, another tomosynthesis sweep was performed to confirm tool-in-lesion (TIL) on the reconstructed images (Figure 2C,2D). Repeated attempts were allowed at the operator’s discretion until the needle was optimally positioned. Finally, a CBCT scan was captured as the gold standard confirmation of relationship between needle and lesion. TIL was defined as needle tip within lesion (by multiple standard planes during digital tomosynthesis and by multiplanar reconstruction images during CBCT), while center-strike, a subset of TIL, was defined as needle passing through the middle third of lesion in three orthogonal planes (Figure 3A,3B). Tool-touching-lesion (TTL) was defined as needle contacting the lesion without the tip being fully surrounded by lesion in all three planes (Figure 3C,3D). Samples of the lesion were obtained with needle aspiration, forceps biopsy and/or cryobiopsy. Patients were extubated immediately after the procedure, and were usually discharged on the same day if the post-procedure chest X-ray was unremarkable. All patients were followed up at least 1 week and 6 months after procedure.

The primary efficacy endpoint is the TIL percentage as confirmed by CBCT, while the primary safety endpoint is any device or procedure related adverse events during bronchoscopy through 7 days post-procedure. Secondary endpoints include the percentage of center-strike as confirmed by CBCT, and the diagnostic yield. The strict diagnostic yield is reported according to American Thoracic Society (ATS)/American College of Chest Physicians (ACCP) definition, which is the proportion of all individuals undergoing the diagnostic procedure in whom a specific malignant or benign diagnosis is established (17). Navigational success was defined as the ability to navigate to within 2 cm of the lesion using the robotic system, while procedural success was defined as the ability to complete biopsy of lesion using the robotic system.

Statistical analysis

Mean and standard deviation are reported for continuous variables; categorical variables are reported as percentages and counts.

Ethics statement

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Chinese University of Hong Kong CREC (No. 2023.175). All patients provided written informed consent.

Results

Twenty-seven nodules from 25 patients were recruited to the study, and two patients had biopsy of two different nodules in the same operative session. The mean size of lung nodules was 21.2±6.1 mm, and the distance-to-pleura has a mean of 6.9±9.7 mm, including 13 nodules which were touching the pleura. There were 17 solid nodules and 10 mixed ground glass lesions. Bronchus sign was present in 18 (66.7%) of the nodules. Procedure was successful in 26/27 (96.3%) of cases. The only case in which tomography was unable to confirm needle location was due to intra-operative atelectasis that developed after the first lesion was successfully biopsied, and the fact that the second nodule was located in the subpleural region of the posterior segment of right lower lobe. Mean navigation duration was 5±4.9 minutes. Mean procedural duration (defined as time between insertion and removal of bronchoscope) for single nodule biopsy was 60±17 minutes. The distance between bronchoscope tip and nodule was 29±9 mm before the first tomosynthesis spin. The median number of tomosynthesis spins required to achieve TIL or TTL was 3.1, ranging from 2 to 6. Afterwards, CBCT was conducted as a gold standard to determine the relationship between needle and lesion, which confirmed TIL in 22 [81.5%, 95% confidence interval (CI): 61.9–93.7%], TTL in 3 (11.1%) and tool-not-in-lesion (TNIL) in 2 (7.4%). Centre-strike, defined as needle passing through the middle third of lesion in three orthogonal planes, was achieved in 16 cases, 72.7% of those with TIL. Tool-needle-relationship between the tomosynthesis reconstruction and CBCT was concordant in 88.5% (23/26, 95% CI: 69.8–95.5%). The mean number of passes of needle aspiration and forceps biopsy was 1.3 and 8.6 respectively, while 8 cases also had cryobiopsy performed as per operator’s discretion. The mean radiation exposure from tomosynthesis sweep per nodule was 15.7±30.2 mGy·m2 (ranging from 0–174.2). The STARD flow diagram of this study is presented in Figure 4.

No device-related adverse events occurred after the procedure, including pneumothorax, infection or significant bleeding. All but 3 patients were discharged within 1-day post-biopsy, and the prolonged stay was due to warfarin titration, hypoglycemia and unexplained low grade fever respectively.

Outcomes of robotic bronchoscopic biopsy are presented in Table 2. The overall diagnostic yield on patient level using strict definition was 84% (95% CI: 63.9–95.5%), comprising of 16 malignant diagnoses (12 primary lung cancers and 4 colorectal metastases) and 5 specific benign diagnoses (2 carcinoids, 1 pulmonary hamartoma and 2 infections). The prevalence of malignancy in this sample including those determined at follow-up was 64%. After a median of 12 months follow-up, out of 5 lesions that had initially non-specific benign diagnosis, 2 were resected showing localized bronchiectasis with mixed inflammation, 1 underwent CT-guided biopsy which showed chronic inflammation, and all had follow-up CT or positron emission tomography (PET)/CT images showing static size and/or standardized uptake value (SUV) uptake. There was 1 case of false negative, where subsequent resection revealed adenocarcinoma in a nodule which was biopsied to be negative for malignancy. Of note, 11 out of 12 primary lung carcinoma diagnoses had sufficient cells to proceed with molecular testing and programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) testing.

Discussion

Transbronchial peripheral lung lesion biopsy has always been challenging, until the advent of robotic bronchoscopic platforms, which provides superior stability, distal articulation and visualization over traditional pre-curved catheters. The major players in the field utilize either shape-sensing technology [e.g., Ion by Intuitive Surgical (18)] or EMN combined with airway recognition [e.g., Monarch by Auris Surgical Robotics (19)], however these platforms did not include tools to mitigate the intrinsic error of CT-to-body divergence. Respiratory variations alone can cause the target lesion to move up to a mean of 17.6 mm (20), which could be even more than the nodule size. Other contributing factors include atelectasis, tissue distortion from catheter system, bleeding, ferromagnetic interference, perturbations in anatomy such as pleural effusion. A prospective study demonstrated the average distance between lesion from pre-procedure CT and intra-procedural CT was 5.9 mm, ranging from 2.1 to 10 mm (21). Hence, in order to further increase diagnostic accuracy of bronchoscopic platforms, the issue of CT-to-body divergence must be addressed and offset by an additional imaging method during procedure.

Integrated intraprocedural imaging has been increasingly important in advanced bronchoscopic biopsy to mitigate the CT-to-body divergence. Benefit in accuracy was achieved irrespective of the type of additional imaging provided, although CBCT remains the gold standard as it provides true 3D images. To confirm TIL, CBCT is often the gold standard utilized by centres who have the resources for a hybrid theatre, but its use is limited by access, cost, room space and additional radiation exposure. Digital tomosynthesis or mobile 3D image reconstruction machines have been combined with traditional EMN bronchoscopy, providing diagnostic yield of 77.4% (22), a big improvement from diagnostic yield of 67.4% in the NAVIGATE trial utilizing EMN alone (23). Other institutions have resorted to radial EBUS for secondary confirmation, but it has its own limitation since it is only lateral-looking, unable to access directionality with eccentric views, and prone to false positive due to atelectasis and haemorrhage (24,25).

To further improve diagnostic accuracy, the Galaxy SystemTM (Noah Medical, San Carlos, CA) combines robotic bronchoscopy with integrated tomosynthesis technology and augmented fluoroscopy (26). The MATCH pre-clinical study utilizing this system demonstrated TIL in 95% and a centre-strike rate of 60%, with 100% diagnostic yield in a total of 20 artificially-created nodules in live porcine models (27). The first-in-human clinical trial (FRONTIER study) focusing on safety and feasibility reported one case of pneumothorax and one case of severe pneumonia in the 18 patients enrolled, but the conservative diagnostic yield was 89.5% and TIL rate was 100% (28). Moving on, a multi-center retrospective study on human subjects employing Galaxy SystemTM reported an overall conservative diagnostic yield of 88.9–92.9% and TIL rate of 93% in 344 lung nodules where only 26% had positive bronchus sign (29). Most recently, the MATCH 2 prospective trial utilizing digital tomosynthesis demonstrated TIL rate of 93.5% and diagnostic yield of 96.7% in a 31-case series.

These encouraging results have spearheaded the current study, which is the first prospective trial utilizing CBCT as confirmation with focus on diagnostic accuracy. The primary endpoint of TIL rate was 81.5% as confirmed by CBCT. The centre-strike rate was 59.3%, while the diagnostic yield at patient level was 84% using a conservative definition. The diagnostic outcomes are encouraging compared to other robotic bronchoscopic platforms. Auris Monarch’s prospective BENEFIT trial revealed 96.2% lesion localization success based on radial endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS), and diagnostic yield of 74.1% (14); while their multicentre retrospective trial reported index diagnostic yield of 85.2% which dropped to 79.4% after 12 months of follow-up (30). The Ion platform reported lesion localization rate of 96.6% and overall diagnostic yield of 79.3% in their first-in-human use study (31); in their single centre institutional registry, the overall diagnostic yield was 87.9% (13) though it was uncertain whether strict or conservative criteria was used during reporting.

Of note, the current study revealed a high tool-needle relationship concordance rate of 88.5% between that determined by tomosynthesis versus by CBCT, thus offering the confidence of successful lesion localization in centres which do not have CBCT on site. Of the 25 TIL-confirmed cases, 21 had specific diagnosis while the rest had non-specific results, which were likely non-malignant based on subsequent imaging, biopsy or surgery. The only false negative malignant diagnosis was in the 1 case where both tomosynthesis and CBCT showed TNIL, as the 1.4 cm right lower lobe nodule was located at the very apical region where needle was unable to reach due to the tortuous bronchoscopic path. The only case where tomography confirmation of needle position had failed was due to significant atelectasis in posterior lower lobe where the nodule was located. This highlights the fact that case selection is important despite all the technological advances, as such lesions may be more easily biopsied using CT-guided percutaneous approach with the patient lying prone. On the other hand, it is also worthwhile to note that even the 3 lesions that had TTL on CBCT yielded specific diagnoses, as the Galaxy software provides spatial orientation on screen to correct the bronchoscope tip’s direction on subsequent biopsies, together with the help of augmented fluoroscopy. This feature also allows targeted sampling of specific region of the nodule, for example a region with higher metabolism.

The use of CT digital tomosynthesis may also explain the low complication rate in the present study. None of the patients had pneumothorax, including the 10 nodules which touched the pleura. This could be due to the availability of reconstructed 3D CT images which accurately showed the distance from needle tip to pleura, thus guiding the operators as to how deep the needle could be further advanced. In contrast, systems utilizing radial EBUS could not as accurately determine the distance to pleura.

The limitations of the present study include a relatively small sample size, relatively short follow-up duration, and lack of histology for some indeterminate nodules to confirm its benignancy. The study data also represents the institutes early initial experience with this robotic bronchoscopy system, that some would consider still in the learning curve. The procedures were performed by skilled thoracic surgeons who had over 8 years of experience using EMN bronchoscopy, thus may limit its generalizability. The present study only included solid lesions or mixed GGOs, thus its results may not be application to fainter pure GGO lesions. In addition, the relatively high bronchus sign percentage (67%) and the relatively large lesion size (mean maximal diameter 21 mm) in the present study may affect the generalizability of results. Our institute did not have rapid-on-site evaluation (ROSE) service, which has been proven to improve diagnostic yield (32). The primary outcome of TIL percentage is not patient-centric and is an unvalidated outcome with undefined interrater reliability which impairs cross-study comparison, and the diagnostic yield is heavily dependent on the subsequent sampling method used. Due to the limited availability of robotic bronchoscopic sessions in our institute, patient recruitment was non-consecutive, leaving room for potential selection bias. In addition, TIL or TTL status was adjudicated by two investigators, one of which is the primary proceduralist, which introduces potential self-confirmation bias, although no disagreements were observed in this series. Regarding the digital tomosynthesis technology itself, it was noticed that there could be some difficulty in determining which fluoro image provided the clearest border of nodule (which is used to the centre of nodule) due to metallic artefacts of the nearby needle and bronchoscope, particularly when the lesion is predominantly ground-glass. In addition, with the TiLT+TM technology, it can be more difficult to determine the needle or bronchoscope position when it is in exactly the same rotational plane to the C-arm. As with other EMN-based technologies, pacemakers and defibrillators may interfere with localization accuracy. Larger sample size multi-centre studies involving operators with different levels of experience should be conducted to confirm the favourable diagnostic and safety results from this study. It would also be interesting to compare with other robotic platforms which integrate additional intra-procedural imaging.

Conclusions

The Galaxy SystemTM with integrated TiLT+TM digital tomosynthesis technology for lung nodule biopsy is safe and provides reliable TIL confirmation as verified by cone-beam CT in the present study. The conservative diagnostic yield at patient level is 84, which appears favourable comparing to other robotic bronchoscopic platforms without the means of mitigating CT-to-body divergence.
