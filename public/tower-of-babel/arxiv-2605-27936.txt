1.
Introduction

The notion of the nuclear dimension was introduced by Winter and Zacharias in
[
31
]
as a noncommutative analogue of the topological covering dimension, and has since played a central role in the Elliott classification program for simple
C^{*}
-algebras. Indeed, in the case of simple, separable and nuclear
C^{*}
-algebras, the nuclear dimension can be
0
(if the
C^{*}
-algebra is an AF-algebra),
1
(if it absorbs the Jiang-Su algebra
\mathcal{Z}
tensorially, but is not AF) or
+\infty
(otherwise)
[
6
,
7
]
.

In this article, we focus to the case of twisted group
C^{*}
-algebras. Eckhardt and Wu showed in
[
13
]
that twisted group
C^{*}
-algebras of virtually polycyclic groups have finite nuclear dimension. However, finding the exact value of the nuclear dimension of these
C^{*}
-algebras has been an extremely challenging problem. Regarding the untwisted case, it was recently shown in
[
9
]
that the nuclear dimension of the group
C^{\ast}
-algebra of a finitely generated virtually abelian group is exactly equal to the Hirsch length of the ambient group. Recall that, for such a group, its Hirsch length is equal to the rank of any abelian subgroup of finite index. For the definition of the Hirsch length in a larger class of groups, we refer the reader to
[
17
]
. The main purpose of this paper is to generalize the above result for a certain type of twisted group
C^{*}
-algebras.

Theorem A
.

(Theorem
4.12
)
Let
G
be a finitely generated, virtually abelian discrete group, and
[\sigma]\in H^{2}(G;\mathbb{T})
such that
\sigma(x,y)
is always a root of unity. Then,

\dim_{\nuc}(C^{*}(G,\sigma))=h(G)

where
h(G)
is the rank of a finite index abelian subgroup of
G
.

A cohomology class as in Theorem A is called
rational
. Otherwise, it is called
irrational
(see also Definition
3.1
). In Section 3, we will show that for finitely generated, virtually abelian groups, rational cohomology classes are exactly the ones that are Type I.
1
1

1

We refer the reader to
[
22
]
for information about Type I cohomology classes.

Hence, our result gives an affirmative answer to
[
2
, Question 2.14]
in the case that the group is finitely generated.

The first step in our proof is to show that these
C^{*}
-algebras are subhomogeneous, which allows us to compute their nuclear dimension by using Winter’s result in
[
32
]
. Moreover, we observe that their irreducible representations correspond to a certain subset of the irreducible representations of a certain finitely generated, virtually abelian group that arises as a central extension of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
by the group (see Remark
2.15
). This allows us to utilize the Mackey Machine (
[
19
, Thm. 4.28]
), as well as ideas from the proof of the untwisted case.

On the other hand, observe that the irrational rotation algebras arise as twisted group
C^{*}
-algebras of
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
with irrational cohomology classes (Example
3.2
). But these
C^{*}
-algebras have nuclear dimension 1 (
[
31
, Example 6.1]
), so we cannot drop the rational cohomology class assumption from Theorem A. We actually suspect that the nuclear dimension of the twisted group
C^{*}
-algebras is equal to the Hirsch length of the group G,
only
when the cohomology class is rational. We have managed to verify this on the case when
G=\mathbb{Z}^{r}
.

Theorem B
.

(Theorem
5.11
, Corollary
5.12
)

\dim_{\nuc}C^{\ast}(\mathbb{Z}^{r},\sigma)=r
if and only if
[\sigma]
is rational.

It is known that these twisted group
C^{*}
-algebras are exactly the higher dimensional noncommutative tori (see Definition
5.1
and Remark
5.2
). Our proof utilizes results of Elliott, Li, and Phillips
[
15
,
23
,
27
]
, which imply that when the cohomology class is rational, the non-commutative tori is strongly Morita equivalent to a
C^{*}
-algebra of the form
C(X)\otimes D
, where
D
is a unital, simple, A
\mathbb{T}

C^{*}
-algebra. Finally, to deduce the upper bound, we heavily rely on a result of Tikuisis and Winter (
[
29
]
, see also Theorem
5.7
).

The paper is structured as follows: In Section 2, we give background information on finitely generated, virtually abelian groups, group cohomology, projective representations, twisted group
C^{\ast}
-algebras, and nuclear dimension. This information will be needed in the following Sections. In Section 3, we introduce the notion of a rational cohomology class, and give various equivalent conditions to that notion in the case of finitely generated, virtually abelian groups. These characterizations will heavily be used in Section 4, where we prove our main result. Finally, in Section 5, we prove Theorem B.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Irreducible representations and Subhomogeneous
C^{*}
-algebras

A
C^{*}
-algebra is
subhomogeneous
if, for every irreducible representation
\pi\colon A\to B(\mathcal{H})
,
\dim(\mathcal{H})
has a uniform finite bound. For example, if
A
embeds in
C(X,M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
for some compact Hausdorff topological space
X
, and some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
A
is subhomogeneous.

Let
A
be a subhomogeneous
C^{*}
-algebra, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We use
\mathrm{Rep}_{n}(A)
to define the space of
n
-dimensional representations
A\to M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. We endow
\mathrm{Rep}_{n}(A)
with the point-norm topology. We use
\mathrm{Irr}_{n}(A)
to define the subspace of
\mathrm{Rep}_{n}(A)
(with the subspace topology) that consists of irreducible representations. When we do not need to restrict the dimension, we get the spaces
\mathrm{Rep}(A)
and
\mathrm{Irr}(A)
. Two irreducible representations
\pi\colon A\to B(\mathcal{H})
and
\pi^{\prime}\colon A\rightarrow B(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
are
equivalent
if there exists a unitary operator
U\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
such that
U\pi(a)=\pi^{\prime}(a)U
for all
a\in A
. In this case, we write
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}
. We use
\Prim(A)
to identify
\mathrm{Irr}(A)/\simeq
, endowed with the Jacobson topology
2
2

2

In
[
11
]
, and elsewhere in the literature, the quotient
\mathrm{Irr}(A)/\simeq
is defined as the
spectrum
of
A
and is denoted with
\widehat{A}
. On the other hand,
\Prim(A)
is defined as the set of primitive ideals of
A
. Because every primitive ideal is a kernel of some irreducible representation, there is a well-defined surjective map from
\widehat{A}
onto
\Prim(A)
that sends the class of an irreducible representation to its kernel (
[
11
, 3.1.5.]
). The Jacobson topology is a topology on
\Prim(A)
(for the definition of this topology we refer the reader to
[
11
, 3.1.]
). But, for a subhomogeneous
C^{*}
-algebra
A
, the above map
\widehat{A}\to\Prim(A)
is a homeomorphism (see
[
11
, 3.1.6 (p.71)]
and
[
1
, Thm IV.15.7 (p.339)]
). This allows us to identify
\mathrm{Irr}(A)/\simeq
with
\Prim(A)
, and define the Jacobson topology on
\mathrm{Irr}(A)/\simeq
.
. The subspace of
\Prim(A)
that consists of the
n
-dimensional representations is denoted by
\Prim_{n}(A)
. For more information, we refer the reader to
[
11
, Chapter 3]
.

2.2.
Two Cohomology

We will provide a brief introduction to the two cohomology, focusing on information that will be needed in future sections. For more details, we refer the reader to
[
4
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

If
G
is a group, and
A
is an abelian group, we define a
2-cocycle
to be a function
\sigma\colon G^{2}\to A
satisfying the following equation

\partial\sigma(g,h,k):=\sigma(g,h)-\sigma(g,hk)+\sigma(gh,k)-\sigma(h,k)=0\,.

A
2-coboundary
is a function that can be written in the form

\sigma(g,h)=\gamma(g)-\gamma(gh)+\gamma(h)

for some function
\gamma\colon G\rightarrow A
. Every 2-coboundary is a 2-cocycle, and
H^{2}(G;A)
is defined to be the group of 2-cocycles, modulo the subgroup of 2-coboundaries. The group operation is pointwise addition. A cocycle is said to be
normalized
if
\sigma(e,g)=\sigma(g,e)=0
for all
g\in G
. Any cohomology class can be represented by a normalized cocycle, and henceforth we assume that all cocycles mentioned are normalized. We denote the group of 2-cocycles as
Z^{2}(G;A)
. The cohomology class of a cocycle
\sigma
will be denoted
[\sigma]
.

Group cohomology,
H^{2}(\bullet;A)
is a contravariant functor, and
H^{2}(G;\bullet)
is a covariant functor with morphisms defined as follows. If
\varphi\colon G\rightarrow H
is a group homomorphism then
\varphi^{*}:H^{2}(H;A)\rightarrow H^{2}(G;A)
is defined by
\varphi^{*}([\sigma])=[\sigma\circ(\varphi\times\varphi)]
. If
T\colon A\rightarrow B
is a morphism of abelian groups, we define
T_{*}\colon H^{2}(G;A)\rightarrow H^{2}(G;B)
by
T_{*}([\sigma])=[T\circ\sigma]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Define
C_{k}(G)
to be formal linear combinations of elements of
G^{k}
. We write a typical element of
C_{2}(G)
as

\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}[a_{j}|b_{j}]

with
a_{j},b_{j}\in G
and
x_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Define
\partial_{2}\colon C_{2}(G)\rightarrow C_{1}(G)
by the equation

\partial_{2}[a|b]=[a]-[ab]+[b]

and
\partial_{3}\colon C_{3}(G)\rightarrow C_{2}(G)
by

\partial_{3}[a|b|c]=[a|b]-[a|bc]+[ab|c]-[b|c].

Then
H_{2}(G;\mathbb{Z}):=\ker(\partial_{2})/\im(\partial_{3})
.

Definition 2.3
.

The
Kronecker pairing
between 2-homology and 2-cohomology is a bilinear map from
H^{2}(G;A)\times H_{2}(G;\mathbb{Z})
to
A
defined by the formula

\left\langle\sigma,\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{n}[a_{j}|b_{j}]\right\rangle:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}\sigma(a_{j},b_{j})

where
\sigma
is a cocycle, and
\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{n}[a_{j}|b_{j}]
is a cycle. The value does not depend on either choice of representative.

\kappa\colon H^{2}(G;A)\rightarrow\Hom(H_{2}(G;\mathbb{Z}),A)

\overline{B_{\varepsilon}(\chi)}
is compact and
\Prim_{\mathtt{K}\cdot m}(C^{*}(G))
is Hausdorff by Remark
2.18
. Hence, it is a homeomorphism onto its image.

ii.
By Remark
2.18
,
\Prim_{\mathtt{K}\cdot m}(C^{*}(G))
is totally normal. The result follows from
[
26
, Theorem 6.4]
, combined with Lemma
4.8
, and the fact that
\dim(\overline{B_{\varepsilon}(\chi)})=r
.
∎

We are now ready to prove our main result.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated, virtually abelian group
G
and
[\sigma]
a rational cohomology class. Then
\dim_{\nuc}(C^{*}(G,\sigma))=h(G)
.

Proof.

By Remark
2.15
, there exists a finitely generated, virtually abelian group
\widetilde{G}
, a natural number
n
, and
\omega\in\mathbb{T}
such that
\omega^{n}=1
and for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Prim_{k}(C^{*}(G),\sigma)\cong A_{k,\omega}^{\widetilde{G}}
. Set
s:=\mathtt{K}\cdot m
.

By Lemma
4.11
,
[
32
, Main Theorem]
,
[
2
, Prop. 2.14]
, and the above, we have the following series of inequalities

h(G)\leq\dim(A_{s,\omega}^{\widetilde{G}})\leq\max_{k}\dim\Prim_{k}(C^{*}(G,\sigma))=\dim_{\nuc}C^{*}(G,\sigma)\leq h(G)\,.

Hence, equality must hold everywhere, so the result follows.
∎

5.
Irrational Twists

The main goal of this section is to show that, in the case of the higher dimension noncommutative Tori, we never have equality in Theorem
4.12
.

Throughout this section, we will use
\mathcal{T}_{r}
to define the set of
r\times r
skew-symmetric matrices with real coefficients.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\Theta\in\mathcal{T}_{r}
. We can define the
r
-dimensional non-commutative torus
A_{\Theta}
as the twisted group
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{r},\sigma_{\Theta})
, where
\sigma_{\Theta}
is the 2-cocycle on
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
defined via

\sigma_{\Theta}(x,y)=\exp(\pi i\langle\Theta x,y\rangle)

for every
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
.

Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the standard inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{r}
.

Remark 5.2
.

It turns out that every twisted group
C^{*}
-algebra of
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
is isomorphic to an
r
-dimensional non-commutative torus. For an explanation why this is true we refer the reader to
[
15
, Section 2.2]
.

We also need the following definition.

Definition 5.3

(Definition 1.6,
[
27
]
)
.

A matrix
\Theta\in\mathcal{T}_{r}
is called
non-degenerate
if whenever
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
satisfies
\exp(2\pi i\langle x,\Theta y\rangle)=1
for every
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, then
x=0
. Otherwise, we say that
\Theta
is
degenerate
.

Example 5.4
.

Let
\theta\in[0,1]
. Then the rotation algebra
A_{\theta}
is isomorphic to
A_{\Theta_{\theta}}
, where
\Theta_{\theta}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\theta\\
\theta&0\\
\end{pmatrix}
. Moreover
\Theta_{\theta}
is non-degenerate if and only if
\theta\notin\mathbb{Q}
.

Notice that in the above example, the
C^{*}
-algebra that arises is simple if and only if the cocycle in non-degenerate. This is not a coincidence, and it can actually be generalized by the following result due to N.C. Phillips.

Theorem 5.5

(Theorem 3.8,
[
27
]
)
.

Let
\Theta
be a
r\times r
skew symmatric real matrix that is non-degenerate, and
r\geq 2
. Then
A_{\Theta}
is a simple,
A\mathbb{T}
-algebra.

Our goal is to find an upper bound on the nuclear dimension of the
C^{*}
-algebras
A_{\Theta}
. In order to motivate our method, we start with the following example.

Example 5.6
.

Let

\Theta=\begin{pmatrix}0_{k\times k}&0_{k\times\ell}\\
0_{\ell\times k}&D\\
\end{pmatrix}

where
D\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
is non-degenerate.

We have

A_{\Theta}\cong C(\mathbb{T}^{k})\otimes A_{D}

Moreover, by Theorem
5.5
,
A_{D}
is a simple,
A\mathbb{T}
algebra.
In particular, if
\ell=2
and
D
is the matrix
\Theta_{\theta}
from Example
5.4
for
\theta\notin\mathbb{Q}
, then

A_{\Theta}\cong C(\mathbb{T}^{k})\otimes A_{\theta}.

So, it is natural to search for an upper bound on the nuclear dimension of
C^{*}
-algebras of the form
C(\mathbb{T}^{k})\otimes A
, where
A
is a simple,
A\mathbb{T}
-algebra. It turns out that such an upper bound follows from a result of Tikuisis and Winter (
[
29
]
), combined with properties of the nuclear dimension. Therefore, we expect that this is already known to experts. However, we provide a proof for the sake of completion. We also would like to thank Rufus Willett for pointing out the upper bound in
[
29
]
to us.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
A
be a simple, separable, unital, infinite-dimensional,
A\mathbb{T}
-algebra. Then

\dim_{\nuc}(C(\mathbb{T}^{k})\otimes A)\leq\min\{5,k+1\}\,.

Proof.

We first prove that
\dim_{\nuc}(C(\mathbb{T}^{k})\otimes A)\leq 5
.

By
[
30
, Corollary 3.1]
,
A
is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable. Hence, it has nuclear dimension 1 by
[
6
, Theorem A]
and

C(\mathbb{T}^{r})\otimes A\cong(C(\mathbb{T}^{k})\otimes\mathcal{Z})\otimes A\,.

But
\dim_{nuc}(C(\mathbb{T}^{k})\otimes\mathcal{Z})\leq 2
by
[
29
, Theorem 4.1]
.
8
8

8

The result on
[
29
]
is stated with respect to the decomposition rank. However, we the nuclear dimension is always less or equal to the decomposition rank. Hence, the upper bound is also true for the nuclear dimension.
Moreover,
[
31
, Proposition 2.3(ii)]
implies that

\dim_{\nuc}(C(\mathbb{T}^{k})\otimes A)\leq(2+1)\cdot(1+1)-1=5\,.

We now show that
\dim_{\nuc}(C(\mathbb{T}^{k})\otimes A)\leq k+1
.

Because
A
is an
A\mathbb{T}
-algebra,
A=\varinjlim A_{n}
, where

A_{n}=\bigoplus_{i,n=1}^{j_{n}}M_{d_{i,n}}(C(\mathbb{T}))\,.

It follows that
C(\mathbb{T}^{k})\otimes A=\varinjlim B_{n}
, where

B_{n}=\bigoplus_{i,n=1}^{j_{n}}M_{d_{i,n}}(C(\mathbb{T}))\otimes C(\mathbb{T}^{k})\cong\bigoplus_{i,n=1}^{j_{n}}M_{d_{i,n}}(C(\mathbb{T}^{k+1}))\,.

Notice that
\dim_{\nuc}(A_{n})=k+1
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,
[
31
, Proposition 2.3(iii)]
implies

\dim_{\nuc}(A)\leq\liminf\dim_{\nuc}(A_{n})=k+1\,.

Combining our two upper bounds, we deduce that

\dim_{\nuc}(C(\mathbb{T}^{k})\otimes A)\leq\min\{5,k+1\}\,.

∎

We also need the following Remark.

Remark 5.8
.

Strongly Morita equivalent separable
C^{*}
-algebras have the same nuclear dimension. Indeed, let
A
and
B
be strongly Morita equivalent separable
C^{*}
-algebras. Then
A\otimes\mathcal{K}\cong B\otimes\mathcal{K}
(
[
5
]
). Hence

\dim_{\nuc}(A)=\dim_{\nuc}(A\otimes\mathcal{K})=\dim_{\nuc}(B\otimes\mathcal{K})=\dim_{\nuc}(B)\,.

The first and third equality follow from
[
31
, Corollary 2.8(i)]
.

Before we can prove our main result of the section, we need to give some background information. First, note that
\SO(n,n|\mathbb{Z})
is a certain subset of
\SO(n,\mathbb{Z})
. For the exact definition we refer the reader to
[
15
, p.8]
. Moreover, there exists a densely defined action
\SO(n,n|\mathbb{Z})\curvearrowright\mathcal{T}_{n}
. This action is described on
[
15
, p.8]
, and it was first defined in
[
28
]
. We also need the following results from the literature.

Proposition 5.9

(Proposition 3.3,
[
15
]
)
.

Let
\Theta\in\mathcal{T}_{r}
. Then there exists
g\in\SO(r,r|\mathbb{Z})
such that

g\cdot\Theta=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&D\end{pmatrix},

where
D\in\mathcal{T}_{d}
for
1\leq d\leq r
and is non-degenerate.

Proposition 5.10

(Theorem 1.1,
[
23
]
)
.

Let
\Theta\in\mathcal{T}_{r}
and
g\in\SO(r,r|\mathbb{Z})
. If
g\cdot\Theta
is defined, then
A_{\Theta}
and
A_{g\cdot\Theta}
are strongly Morita equivalent.

Now we are ready to prove our main result.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
\Theta
be a skew symmetric real
r\times r
matrix. Assume that the cohomology class
[\sigma_{\Theta}]
of the associated cocycle is irrational. Then

\dim_{\nuc}(A_{\Theta})\leq\min\{5,r-1\}\,.

In particular,
\dim_{\nuc}(A_{\Theta})<r
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.9
, there exists a non-degenerate skew symmetric, real
d\times d
matrix
D
, where
1\leq d\leq r
, and
g\in\SO(r,r|\mathbb{Z})
such that

g\cdot\Theta=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&D\end{pmatrix}

where the multiplication is the action
\SO(r,r|\mathbb{Z})\curvearrowright\mathcal{T}_{r}
.

By assumption,
[\sigma_{\Theta}]
is irrational. This implies that
d\geq 2
.
By Proposition
5.10
,
A_{\Theta}
and
A_{g\cdot\Theta}
are strongly Morita equivalent. Hence, Example
5.6
, implies that

A_{\Theta}
is strongly Morita equivalent to
C(\mathbb{T}^{r-d})\otimes A_{D}
. Moreover, Theorem
5.5
implies that
A_{D}
is a simple,
A\mathbb{T}
-algebra. Hence

\dim_{\nuc}(A_{\Theta})=\dim_{\nuc}(C(\mathbb{T}^{r-d})\otimes D)\leq\min\{5,r-d+1\}\leq\min\{5,r-1\}\,.

Notice that the first equality is due to Remark
5.8
, the first inequality follows from Theorem
5.7
, while the second holds because
d\geq 2
. Proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
\sigma
be a 2-cocycle on
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
. The following are equivalent.

i.

\dim_{\nuc}(C^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{r},\sigma))=r

ii.

[\sigma]
is rational.

Proof.

It follows from Remark
5.2
, Theorem
4.12
and Theorem
5.11
.
