A Lorentzian Lasry-Lions regularization theorem

Abstract
The main goal of this paper is to establish a general Lorentzian Lasry-Lions regularization theorem: let u u be a function defined on a globally hyperbolic spacetime. Assume that its forward Lax–Oleinik evolution T ​ u Tu is locally semiconcave in a neighbourhood of ( t 0 , y 0 ) (t_{0},y_{0}) and has only future-directed timelike superdifferentials at ( t 0 , y 0 ) (t_{0},y_{0}) . Then, for t t close to t 0 t_{0} and s > 0 s>0 sufficiently small, the function T ^ s ∘ T t ​ u \hat{T}_{s}\circ T_{t}u is of class C loc 1 , 1 C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,1} in a neighbourhood of y 0 y_{0} . We provide sufficient conditions ensuring that the assumptions of the theorem are satisfied, and we present an application to optimal transport: under farily general assumptions, for any two intermediate measures along a displacement interpolation, there exists a C l ​ o ​ c 1 , 1 C^{1,1}_{loc} -regular maximizing pair in the dual Kantorovich formulation.

Introduction

If
H
is a Hilbert space with induced norm
|\cdot|
and
u:H\to\mathbb{R}
is any function, the forward and backward Lax-Oleinik semigroups are defined as

\displaystyle T_{t}u(y):=\inf_{x\in H}\bigl(u(x)+\frac{1}{2t}|x-y|^{2}\bigr),\quad\hat{T}_{t}u(x):=\sup_{y\in H}\bigl(u(y)-\frac{1}{2t}|x-y|^{2}\bigr),

(1.1)

with
t>0
. If
u:H\to\mathbb{R}
is bounded, Lasry and Lions
[
13
]
proved that for any
0<s<t
the functions

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{s}\circ T_{t}u\quad\text{ and }\quad T_{s}\circ\hat{T}_{t}u

(1.2)

belong to
C^{1,1}(H)
, and converge to
u
as
t\to 0
if
u
is uniformly continuous. The result was motivated by the problem of finding smooth approximations in infinite dimensional spaces. See also
[
4
]
.

Notice that
\frac{1}{2t}|x-y|^{2}
is just the minimal action to go from
x
to
y
in time
t
:

\frac{1}{2t}|x-y|^{2}=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|\dot{\gamma}_{s}|^{2}}{2}\,ds\mid\gamma:[0,t]\to H\text{ smooth },\ \gamma_{0}=x,\ \gamma_{t}=y\Bigr\}.

For this reason, the semigroups (
1.1
) make sense whenever
u
is a function on a smooth manifold
M
admitting a suitably well-behaved Lagrangian
L:TM\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
. Here,
\frac{1}{2t}|x-y|^{2}
is replaced by the minimal action

h_{t}(x,y):=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{0}^{t}L(\gamma_{s},\dot{\gamma}_{s})\,ds\mid\gamma:[0,t]\to M\text{ smooth },\ \gamma_{0}=x,\ \gamma_{t}=y\Bigr\}.

A classical example is when
L
is a Tonelli-Lagrangian on a complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
; roughly speaking, this means that
L
is
C^{2}
and enjoys several convexity and growth conditions. In this case,
h_{t}
turns out to be real-valued and continuous
[
9
]
. If
M
is also compact, Bernard
[
3
]
was able to extend the result of Lasry-Lions: If
u:M\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded function, he proved that the functions in (
1.2
) belong to
C^{1,1}(M)
for
t>0
and
s\leq s(t)
sufficiently small. The motivation for Bernard’s result was to establish the existence of
C^{1,1}
critical (viscosity) subsolutions to the stationary Hamilton–Jacobi equation, which is indeed a consequence of his result.

Bernard’s Lasry–Lions regularization was subsequently extended and generalized to the non-compact case in
[
6
,
8
,
11
]
: Indeed, Claim 4.7 in
[
6
]
(which relies on Theorem 1 of
[
11
]
and Lemma B.7 in
[
8
]
) states the following: If
V\Subset M
is an open precompact set,
u:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
is any function and
t_{0}>0
is such that
T_{t_{0}}(y_{0})
is finite at some point
y_{0}
, then
\hat{T}_{s}\circ T_{t}u
belongs to
C_{loc}^{1,1}(V)
for
t\in I\Subset(0,t_{0})
and
s>0
sufficiently small.

In the special case
L(x,v)=\frac{|v|_{g}^{2}}{2}
, it was shown in
[
6
]
that the
C_{loc}^{1,1}
-property of the functions
\hat{T}_{s}\circ T_{t}u
can be used to study topological properties of both the cut locus and the set of non-differentiability points of the Riemannian distance function – it was shown that both sets are locally contractible.

Motivated by these results, the main goal of this paper is to establish a Lorentzian analogue of the Lasry–Lions-type regularization theorem in the setting of a globally hyperbolic spacetime. We study the Lax-Oleinik semigroups w.r.t. the standard Lorentzian Lagrangian (
1.3
) which gives rise – via the minimal time-
1
-action – to the standard cost function (some version of the Lorentzian time separation ((
1.4
) with
t=1
)) in the Lorentzian optimal transport problem (
1.6
). After proving the Lasry–Lions-type regularity result, we can therefore provide an application to optimal transport: under suitable assumptions, we show that the dual Kantorovich problem for intermediate times along a displacement interpolation (cf. Definition
1.2
) admits a solution given by
C^{1,1}_{loc}
-functions (the analogous result also holds in the Riemannian framework, although I am not aware of any reference).

It is worth mentioning that our main theorem yields the same topological properties for the future causal cut locus and the singularities of the Lorentzian distance function as in the Riemannian case. This result, however, was already established in an earlier work
[
16
]
, where a corresponding special case of our main theorem was treated.

1.1
Setting and results

To state our precise results, let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime, and fix
0<p<1
.

We consider the Lagrangian
L:TM\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
by

\displaystyle L(x,v):=\begin{cases}-|v|^{p}_{g},&\text{ if $v$ is future causal},\\[10.0pt]
+\infty,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
|v|_{g}:=\sqrt{|g(v,v)|}
.
For
t>0
, the Lagrangian gives rise to the minimal time-
t
-action
c_{t}
via

\displaystyle c_{t}(x,y):=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{0}^{t}L(\gamma_{s},\dot{\gamma}_{s})\,ds\mid\gamma:[0,t]\to M\text{ is piecewise smooth, }\gamma_{0}=x,\gamma_{t}=y\Bigr\}.

If
\ell
denotes the time separation function (
2.1
), it is not difficult to show that

\displaystyle c_{t}(x,y)=\begin{cases}-t^{1-p}\ell(x,y)^{p},&\text{ if }t>0\text{ and }y\in J^{+}(x),\\[10.0pt]
+\infty,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

(1.4)

where
J^{+}(x)
denotes the causal future of
x
(note that, with our convention,
0
is a future causal vector). As in the Riemannian case, we can define the forward and backward Lax-Oleinik/Hopf-Lax semigroups
Tu,\hat{T}u:(0,\infty)\times M\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
of a function
u:M\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
via

\displaystyle T_{t}u(y):=\inf_{x\in J^{-}(y)}(u(x)+c_{t}(x,y))\quad\text{ and }\quad\hat{T}_{t}u(x):=\sup_{y\in J^{+}(x)}(u(y)-c_{t}(x,y)).

We can now state our main theorem. Recall that a function defined on a smooth manifold is called locally semiconcave if it is so in local coordinates, see Definition
2.10
. The set of super-differentials of a function
u
at
x
is denoted by
\partial^{+}u(x)
, and
{\cal C}^{*}_{y}\subseteq T_{y}M^{*}
denotes the dual future causal cone (cf. Definition
2.9
).

Theorem 1.1

(Lasry-Lions regularization)
.

Let
N
be a smooth manifold, and let
u:N\times M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\},\ (x,y)\mapsto u(x,y)=u_{x}(y)
, be such that the following family of Lax-Oleinik functions

\displaystyle\hat{u}:(0,\infty)\times N\times M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\},\ (t,x,y)\mapsto(T_{t}u_{x})(y),

is locally semiconcave on an open neighbourhood of
(t_{0},x_{0},y_{0})
with

\displaystyle\partial^{+}(T_{t_{0}}u_{x_{0}})(y_{0})\Subset\operatorname{int}({\cal C}_{y_{0}}^{*}).

(1.5)

Then there exist three open neighbourhoods
I
,
U
and
V
of
t_{0}
,
x_{0}
and
y_{0}
, respectively, and some number
s_{0}>0
such that the following properties hold:

(i)

The functions
\hat{T}_{s}\circ T_{t}u_{x}
,
(s,t,x)\in(0,s_{0}]\times I\times U
, are
C^{1,1}_{loc}
on
V
.

(ii)

For
(s,t,x,y)\in(0,s_{0}]\times I\times U\times V
, there exists a unique
z\in M
with
\hat{T}_{s}\circ T_{t}u_{x}(y)=T_{t}u_{x}(z)-c_{s}(y,z)
, and necessarily
\ell(y,z)>0
. Moreover, setting
z=y
if
s=0
,
z
depends continuously on
(s,t,x,y)
.

A particular case is when
u_{x}=u
does not depend on
x
. The dependence on
x
, however, becomes relevant when studying the topological properties of the time separation function
[
16
]
, à la
[
6
]
; see Section
1.2
for a discussion of this phenomenon and why it does not arise in the Riemannian case. We also refer to Section
1.2
for a discussion of hypotheses ensuring local semiconcavity of
\hat{u}
and (
1.5
), and why these two properties have no Riemannian counterpart. Notice that an analogous version of Theorem
1.1
holds when the roles of the forward and backward semigroups are interchanged.

Our second main result is an application of the theorem to the optimal transport problem. Before stating the precise result, we introduce the necessary background and basic concepts.

We consider the optimal transport problem on

associated with the cost function

.

\displaystyle C(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{M\times M}c_{1}(x,y)\,d\pi(x,y)\in[-\infty,0]\cup\{\infty\},

(1.6)

where
\mu,\nu\in{\cal P}:={\cal P}(M)
are Borel probability measures on

,
\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
denotes the set of causal couplings of
\mu
and
\nu
(i.e. all Borel probability measures

having
\mu
and
\nu
as marginals and such that

). We adopt the convention that
\inf(\emptyset):=+\infty
.
This is the standard cost function for the Lorentzian optimal transport problem and it has been studied extensively (see e.g.
[
12
,
14
,
19
,
21
,

]
for the smooth and
[

,

]

In the following fundamental definition,
\operatorname{CGeo([0,1],M)}
denotes the Polish space of maximizing future causal geodesics over
[0,1]
(cf. Remark

),

is used to denote the push-forward of a measure.

Definition 1.2

(Dynamical optimal coupling)
.

We say that

(\mu_{t})

For a comparison to the notion of

Using standard gluing methods, it is not difficult to prove that

Definition 1.3

(Calibrated pair)
.

Let
\mu,\nu\in{\cal P}(M)
such that

is finite, and suppose that
\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
is an optimal coupling. We say that a pair of functions

-calibrated

(a)

it is a

-

\psi(y)-\varphi(x)\leq c_{1}(x,y)

(b)

\psi(y)-\varphi(x)=c_{1}(x,y)\ (=-\ell(x,y)^{p})\quad\pi\text{-a.e.}

Here, we use the convention
\pm\infty\mp\infty:=-\infty
.

Theorem 1.4

(
C^{1,1}_{loc}
-optimal pairs)
.

Let
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in{\cal P}(M)
be such that

(ii)

Then there exists two open sets

1.2
Discussion

(1) As already mentioned, the dependence on
x
in Theorem
1.1
is essential in order for the statement to imply topological properties of the singularities of the time separation function. Indeed, one considers

u_{x}=\chi_{x}:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\},\ \chi_{x}(y):=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }y=x,\\[5.0pt]
+\infty,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

To derive the corresponding topological properties, it is necessary, for instance, that
s_{0}
does not depend on
x
, at least locally. This dependence is not needed in the Riemannian case, due to an argument based on considering a suitable function
u
on the product manifold, which is again Riemannian, whereas the product of two Lorentzian manifolds is not Lorentzian.

(2) Of course, an important special case in Theorem
1.1
is when
u_{x}\equiv u
does not depend on
x
, so that
\hat{u}=\hat{u}(t,y)
. In contrast to the Riemannian setting, we require the local semiconcavity of
\hat{u}
near
(t_{0},y_{0})
. Although this property also plays a crucial role in the Riemannian case, it follows there from the finiteness of
T_{t_{1}}u(y_{1})
for some
t_{1}>t_{0}
and
y_{1}\in M
, see Theorem 1 in
[
11
]
; the theorem fails in the Lorentzian setting, which is why we impose this assumption. In fact, I am not aware of any version of the theorem except for Theorem
4.2
in the present paper. Finally, Assumption (
1.5
) has no counterpart in the Riemannian case, but it is used in several technical parts of the proof.

Theorem
4.2
states that, if
N=M
,
u_{x}\equiv u
is
+\infty
outside of a causally compact set and
x,y\in M
are such that
u(x)
is finite with
y\in I^{+}(x)
and

T_{1}u(y)=u(x)+c_{1}(x,y),

then the hypothesis of Theorem
1.1
are satisfied at any point
(t,\gamma_{t})
,
t\in(0,1)
, where
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
is a maximizing geodesic from
x
to
y
.

If
(\varphi,\psi)
is a
(c_{1},\pi)
-calibrated pair as in Theorem
1.4
, then we can set
u:=\varphi
. Notice that
\Pi
is concentrated on the set of future timelike geodesics such that
\psi(\gamma_{1})-\varphi(\gamma_{0})=c_{1}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})
. This gives

T_{1}\varphi(\gamma_{1})=\varphi(\gamma_{0})+c_{1}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})

and we may thus apply the above criterion to conclude that
\hat{T}_{\tau}\circ T_{t+\tau}\varphi
is
C^{1,1}_{loc}
near
\gamma_{t}
for small
\tau>0
. An analogous result holds with
s
replacing
t
. Using a partition of unity argument, Theorem
1.4
is then a consequence of the fact that
(t-s)^{p-1}(\hat{T}_{\tau}\circ T_{s+\tau}\varphi,\hat{T}_{\tau}\circ T_{t+\tau}\varphi)
is
(c_{1},\pi_{s,t})
-calibrated.

(3) The optimal transport problem we study is usually defined as a maximazation problem
[
14
,
7
,
2
]
: For two probability measure
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(M)
, one defines
\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu)=-\infty
if
\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)=\emptyset
and otherwise as

\displaystyle\ell_{p}(\mu,\nu):=\bigg(\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{M\times M}\ell(x,y)^{p}\,d\pi(x,y)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Up to taken the
p
-th root, this definition differs from ours only be a sign (
\ell_{p}^{p}(\mu,\nu)=-C(\mu,\nu)
), so the two problems are equivalent. The advantage of the standard formulation is that
\ell_{p}
satisfies the reverse triangle inequality (it is a time separation function on
\mathcal{P}(M)
in the sense of
[
2
]
); our definition, on the other hand, focuses on the Lagrangian formulation.

Let us also compare our notion of displacement interpolation (used by
[
7
]
) to the notion of
p
-geodesics introduced by McCann
[
15
]
. A
p
-geodesic is a narrowly continuous curve
(\mu_{t})
satisfying

\ell_{p}(\mu_{s},\mu_{t})=(t-s)\ell_{p}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in(0,\infty)

for all
0\leq s<t\leq 1
. Any displacement interpolation with non-zero total cost is a
p
-geodesic
[
7
]
. Conversely, if
(\mu_{t})
is a
p
-geodesic,
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
have compact support and each optimal coupling is concentrated on
I^{+}
, then one can show by standard arguments that
(\mu_{t})
is induced by a dynamical optimal coupling. In particular, the two definitions coincide for the important class of
p
-separated measures, as defined in
[
15
]
.

1.3
Plan of the paper

The structure of this paper is as follows:
In Section 2 we describe the precise setting considered in this paper and collect the necessary background material. For instance, we study elementary properties of the Lagrangian, its Hamiltonian and maximizing curves. In Section 3, we study in great detail a local version of the Lax-Oleinik semigroup. In Section 4, we give the proof of the main theorem and give sufficient conditions ensuring the validity of the assumptions. Finally, we prove our optimal transport result in Section 5.

For the rest of this paper

Remark 2.6

(Euler-Lagrange flow = geodesic flow)

It is not difficult to verify that the second derivative along the fibres,

C^{2}
-curve satisfying, in local coordinates, the Euler-Lagrange equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\frac{\partial L}{\partial v}(\gamma_{t},\dot{\gamma}_{t})\bigg)=\frac{\partial L}{\partial x}(\gamma_{t},\dot{\gamma}_{t}).

Moreover, every future timelike
L
-minimizing curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
solves the Euler-Lagrange equation. Since the future timelike
L
-minimizing curves are exactly the future timelike maximizing geodesics, and since every future timelike geodesic is locally maximizing (Proposition 7.3 in
[
17
]
), it follows that the Euler-Lagrange flow coincides with the geodesic flow restricted to the invariant set
\operatorname{int}({\cal C})
.

Definition 2.7

(Minimal action)
.

For
t>0
, the
minimal time-
t
-action
to go from
x
to
y
is defined by

\displaystyle{\cal C}(t,x,y):=c_{t}(x,y):=\inf\{\mathbb{L}(\gamma)\mid\gamma:[0,t]\to M\text{ is a curve connecting }x\text{ to }y\}.

Corollary 2.8

(Formula for the minimal action)
.

We have the following identity:

\displaystyle c_{t}(x,y)=\begin{cases}-t^{1-p}\ell(x,y)^{p},&\text{ if }y\in J^{+}(x),\\[10.0pt]
+\infty,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Moreover, for any
x\in M
,
y\in J^{+}(x)
and
t>0
, there exists a smooth
L
-minimizing geodesic
\gamma:[0,t]\to M
connecting
x
to
y
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Theorem
2.4
and Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Definition 2.9

(dual cone)
.

For each
x\in M
, we denote the canonical isomorphism by

\displaystyle T_{x}M\to T_{x}^{*}M,\ v\mapsto v^{\flat}:=g(v,\cdot).

We define
{\cal C}_{x}^{*}
as the image of
{\cal C}_{x}
under this isomorphism, and
{\cal C}^{*}:=\{(x,p)\in T^{*}M\mid p\in{\cal C}_{x}^{*}\}.

Definition 2.10

(Semiconcavity and super-differentials)
.

(i)

A function
f:U\to\mathbb{R}
defined on the open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
semiconcave
if there exists
C\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\forall x\in U,\ \exists p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ \forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f(y)\leq f(x)+\langle p,y-x\rangle-C|y-x|^{2}.

(2.2)

We shall say that
f
is
C
-concave
. The notion of
local semiconcavity
is defined in an obvious way. A family of functions
f_{i}:U\to\mathbb{R}
is called
uniformly semiconcave
if
C
in (
2.2
) can be chosen independently of
i
.

(ii)

A function
f:U\to\mathbb{R}
defined on the open subset
U\subseteq M
is called
locally semiconcave
if it is so when computed in some (hence any) chart.
A family of functions
f_{i}:U\to\mathbb{R}
is called
uniformly locally semiconcave
if, for each
x\in U
, there exists a chart
\varphi
around
x
such that the family of functions
(f_{i}\circ\varphi^{-1})_{i}
is uniformly semiconcave.

(iii)

A function
f:U\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
defined on the open subset
U\subseteq M
is said to be
super-differentiable
at
x
with
super-differential

p\in T_{x}^{*}M
if
f(x)\in\mathbb{R}
and

f(\exp^{h}_{x})\leq f(x)+p(v)+o(|v|_{h}),

where
\exp_{x}^{h}
denotes the exponential function of the Riemannian metric
h
.
We denote by
\partial^{+}f(x)
the set of all super-differentials.

(iv)

The notion of
semiconvexity/sub-differentiability
is defined analogously by replacing ”
\leq
” with ”
\geq
”, with
\partial^{-}f
denoting the set of subdifferentials.

Theorem 2.11

(Semiconcavity and supergradients of the minimal action)
.

Let
{\cal C}:(0,\infty)\times M\times M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be the function from Definition
2.7
.

(a)

{\cal C}
is real-valued and continuous on
(0,\infty)\times J^{+}
, and even locally semiconcave on
(0,\infty)\times I^{+}
.

(b)

If
x\in M
,
y\in I^{+}(x)
and
t>0
, then the set of super-differentials of
{\cal C}
at the point
(t,x,y)
is given by

\displaystyle\partial^{+}{\cal C}(t,x,y)=\operatorname{conv}\left(\bigg\{\left((p-1)\Bigl(\frac{\ell(x,y)}{t}\Bigr)^{p},-\frac{\partial L}{\partial v}(x,\dot{\gamma}_{0}),\frac{\partial L}{\partial v}(y,\dot{\gamma}_{t})\right)\bigg\}\right),

where the set is taken over all maximizing geodesics
\gamma:[0,t]\to M
connecting
x
to
y
.

In particular,
{\cal C}
is differentiable at
(t,x,y)
if and only if there is a unique maximizing geodesic connecting
x
to
y
in time
t
(equivalently, in time
1
).

Proof.

See
[
15
]
, Proposition 3.4 for (a). A complete proof in the case
p=\frac{1}{2}
can be found in
[
16
]
. The proof carries over to
p\in(0,1]
with no additional difficulties.
∎

Definition 2.12

(Legendre transform)
.

The
Legendre transform
of
L
is the map

\displaystyle{\cal L}:\operatorname{int}({\cal C})\to T^{*}M,\ (x,v)\mapsto\left(x,\frac{\partial L}{\partial v}(x,v)\right).

Proposition 2.13

(Legendre transform is a diffeomorphism)
.

The Legendre transform is a diffeomorphism onto its image

\operatorname{im}({\cal L})=\operatorname{int}({\cal C}^{*})
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\bigg(x,\frac{\partial L}{\partial v}(x,v)\bigg)=\bigg(x,p|v|_{g}^{{p-2}}v^{\flat}\bigg),

(2.3)

which is clearly a diffeomorphism from
\operatorname{int}({\cal C})
to
\operatorname{int}({\cal C}^{*})
because
p\in(0,1)
.
∎

Definition 2.14

(Hamiltonian)
.

The
Hamiltonian
associated with
L
is the function
H:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
defined by

\displaystyle H(x,p):=\sup\{pv-L(x,v)\mid v\in T_{x}M\}.

Lemma 2.15

(See
[
19
]
, Section 2, and
[
15
]
, Lemma 3.1)
.

We have

\displaystyle H({\cal L}(x,v))=\frac{\partial L}{\partial v}(x,v)(v)-L(x,v)=(p-1)L(x,v)

for all
(x,v)\in\operatorname{int}({\cal C})
.

Proof.

See Appendix
A
.
∎

Remark 2.16

(The Hamiltonian and the geodesic flow)
.

From the above lemma, we conclude that
H
is smooth on the open set
\operatorname{int}({\cal C}^{*})
, and satisfies the identity
H({\cal L}(x,v))=\frac{\partial L}{\partial v}(x,v)(v)-L(x,v)
. It is therefore well-known (
[
10
]
, Proof of Theorem 2.6.4) that the Euler-Lagrange flow
\phi_{t}
(i.e. the geodesic flow on
\operatorname{int}({\cal C})
) is conjugate, via the diffeomorphism
{\cal L}:\operatorname{int}({\cal C})\to\operatorname{int}({\cal C}^{*})
, to the Hamiltonian flow
\psi_{t}
on
\operatorname{int}({\cal C}^{*})
. The latter is understood with respect to its canonical symplectic structure as an open subset of the cotangent bundle.

2.2
The Lax-Oleinik semigroup

In this subsection, we define the (Lorentzian) Lax-Oleinik/Hopf-Lax semigroup, and we study elemantary properties.

Definition 2.17

(Lax-Oleinik semigroup)
.

The
forward Lax-Oleinik semigroup
is the family of maps
(T_{t})_{t>0}
, defined on the space of functions
u:M\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
by

\displaystyle T_{t}u:M\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\ T_{t}u(y):=\inf\{u(x)+c_{t}(x,y)\mid x\in M\},

where we use the convention
-\infty+\infty:=+\infty
.
The
backward Lax-Oleinik semigroup
is the family of maps
(\hat{T}_{t})_{t>0}
, defined on the space of functions
u:M\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
by

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{t}u:M\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\ \hat{T}_{t}u(x):=\sup\{u(y)-c_{t}(x,y)\mid y\in M\},

where we use the convention
\infty-\infty:=-\infty
.
If
u:M\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is any function, we also write

\displaystyle Tu:

\displaystyle\ (0,\infty)\times M\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\ (t,y)\mapsto T_{t}u(y),

and we use the analogous notation
\hat{T}u
.

For convenience, we also set
T_{0}u=\hat{T}_{0}u:=u
.

Remark 2.18

(About the convention)
.

To stay away from computations involving
\pm\infty\mp\infty
, let us mention that the definition of the Lax-Oleinik semigroup can equivalently be given as in the introduction:

\displaystyle T_{t}u:M\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\ T_{t}u(y):=\inf\{u(x)+c_{t}(x,y)\mid x\in J^{-}(y)\}.

In this case,
c_{t}(x,y)
is always finite.

Lemma 2.19

(Semigroup property and composition)
.

(i)

(T_{t})
and
(\hat{T}_{t})
are semigroups, that is,
T_{t+s}=T_{t}\circ T_{s}
and
\hat{T}_{t+s}=\hat{T}_{t}\circ\hat{T}_{s}
for all
t,s\geq 0
.

(ii)

It holds
\hat{T}_{t}\circ T_{t}u\leq u
and
T_{t}\circ\hat{T}_{t}u\geq u
for all
t\geq 0
and
u:M\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
.

Proof.

(ii) is trivial from the definition (cf.
[
1
]
), so we only prove (i) for the forward semigroup. The statement is trivial if
t=0
or
s=0
. Thus, fix
t,s>0
and
y\in M
.

For
\varepsilon>0
, let
x\in J^{-}(y)
be such that

\displaystyle T_{t+s}u(y)+\varepsilon\geq u(x)+c_{t+s}(x,y).

(2.4)

If
\gamma:[0,t+s]\to M
denotes a maximizing geodesic from
x
to
y
, it follows that

u(x)+c_{t+s}(x,y)=u(x)+c_{s}(x,\gamma_{s})+c_{t}(\gamma_{s},y)\geq T_{s}u(\gamma_{s})+c_{t}(\gamma_{s},y)\geq T_{t}\circ T_{s}u(y).

Combining with (
2.4
), this shows
T_{t}\circ T_{s}\leq T_{t+s}
since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary. Conversely, for
\varepsilon>0
, fix
x\in J^{-}(y)
with

T_{t}\circ T_{s}u(y)+\varepsilon\geq T_{s}u(x)+c_{t}(x,y),

and let
z\in J^{-}(x)
with

T_{s}u(x)+\varepsilon\geq u(z)+c_{s}(z,x).

Then it follows easily from the definition of the minimal action that

T_{t}\circ T_{s}u(y)+2\varepsilon\geq u(z)+c_{t}(z,x)+c_{s}(x,y)\geq u(z)+c_{t+s}(z,y)\geq T_{t+s}u(y),

which proves the other implication since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary.
∎

3
A local Lax-Oleinik semigroup

In order to prove the main result, it is convenient to study some local type of the Lax-Oleinik semigroup. This method has already been used in the case of Tonelli Lagrangians systems in
[
8
,
6
]
. Although the choice of
V_{0}
in the following definition is quite arbitrary, the proof of the main theorem consists in showing that the backward semigroup
\hat{T}_{s}u_{x}
locally conincides with the local one. Hence, all properties we are able to prove for the local version easily extend to the global version.

Let us first describe the general setting of functions that we are studying in this section.

Definition 3.1

(Local Lax-Oleinik semigroup)
.

Let
s>0
,
V_{0}\subseteq M
be an open set, and
f:V_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function. We define the
local backward Lax-Oleinik semigroup
of
f
as

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{s}^{V_{0}}f:V_{0}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\},\ \hat{T}_{s}^{V_{0}}f(y):=\sup\{f(z)-c_{s}(y,z)\mid z\in V_{0}\}.

(3.1)

Remark 3.2
.

We use the variable
s
instead of
t
. The reason behind this is consistency with Theorem
1.1
: The functions
f
play the role of the functions
T_{t}u_{x}
of Theorem
1.1
.

Of course, one could also consider functions
f:V_{0}\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
using the same conventions as in Definition
2.17
. Since the local semigroup is merely a technical tool, we assume for simplicity that
f
is real-valued.

Definition 3.3

(Causally convex sets)
.

A set
V\subseteq M
is
causally convex
if all future causal curves that start and end in
V
remain within
V
for all times.

Remark 3.4

(The general framework)
.

If
K_{0}\subseteq\operatorname{int}({\cal C}^{*})
is a compact set and
V_{0}\Subset M
is a precompact open and causally convex set, denote by
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}(K_{0},V_{0})
the family of locally semiconcave functions
f:V_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying:

\displaystyle\{(y,p)\in T^{*}M\mid y\in V_{0},\ f\in\mathcal{F},p\in\partial^{+}f(y)\}\Subset K_{0}.

(3.2)

In particular, for any future causal curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to V_{0}
, the composition
f\circ\gamma
is Lipschitz (hence a.e. differentiable) with

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(f\circ\gamma)(t)\leq-C_{0}\,|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|_{h}

for a.e.
t
and some constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(K_{0},V_{0})
(recall our convention of
g=(-,+,...,+)
).

3.1
Elementary properties and semiconvexity

In this section we study elementary properties of the local Lax-Oleinik semigroup for functions in the set
\mathcal{F}(K_{0},V_{0})
, and we then restrict our attention on the behaviour on some open set
V\Subset V_{0}
and for sufficiently small
s
. We prove existence and uniqueness of optimal points
z
in (
3.1
) under suitable assumptions. We conclude this section proving local semiconvexity of the local Lax-Oleinik semigroup.

Lemma 3.5

(Locus and uniqueness of optimal points)
.

Let
K_{0},V_{0}
and
\mathcal{F}
be as in Remark
3.4
, and let
f\in\mathcal{F}
,
y\in V_{0}
and
s>0
. Then the following properties hold:

(i)

It holds

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{s}^{V_{0}}f(y)>f(y).

(3.3)

(ii)

If
z\in V_{0}
is optimal for
\hat{T}_{s}^{V_{0}}f(y)
, then
z\in I^{+}(y)
.

(iii)

If moreover
\hat{T}_{s}^{V_{0}}f
is differentiable at
y
, then there exists at most one optimal point
z
and it satisfies

\displaystyle d_{y}(\hat{T}^{V_{0}}_{s}f)=\frac{\partial L}{\partial v}(y,\dot{\gamma}_{0})\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{\partial L}{\partial v}(z,\dot{\gamma}_{s})\in\partial^{+}f(z),

where
\gamma:[0,s]\to M
is the unique(!) maximizing geodesic connecting
y
to
z
.

Proof.

(i)

Let
\gamma:[0,\varepsilon_{0}]\to M
be a future timelike geodesic with
\gamma_{0}=y
.
Since
f
is locally semiconcave (hence locally Lipschitz) on some neighbourhood of
y
, there is
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|f(\gamma_{\varepsilon})-f(y)|\leq C\varepsilon

(3.4)

for small
\varepsilon>0
.

Moreover, since
\gamma
is a future timelike geodesic, its ”speed”
|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|_{g}
is a positive constant.
Thus,
\ell(y,\gamma_{\varepsilon})\geq\varepsilon|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}
. Hence

\displaystyle-c_{s}(y,\gamma_{\varepsilon})\geq s^{1-p}(\varepsilon|\dot{\gamma}|_{g})^{p}.

(3.5)

Since
p<1
, we can combine inequalities (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) to obtain

\hat{T}^{V_{0}}_{s}f(y)\geq f(\gamma_{\varepsilon})-c_{s}(y,\gamma_{\varepsilon})>f(y)

for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
.

(ii)

Let
z\in V_{0}
be optimal. Suppose, for contradiction, that
\ell(y,z)=0
, and let
\gamma:[0,s]\to M
be a future causal null geodesic connecting
y
with
z
. By Part (i),
y\neq z
, so
\gamma
is non-constant. Hence, we can choose
\xi_{s}\in T_{z}M
with
g_{z}(\dot{\gamma}_{s},\xi_{s})<0
. Let
\xi:[0,s]\to TM
be the parallel transport of
\xi_{s}
along
\gamma
, and consider a smooth variation
\sigma:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times[0,s]\to M
of
\gamma
with variational vector field
\tilde{\xi}_{t}=t\xi_{t}
, fixing
\sigma(r,0)=\gamma_{0}=y
for all
r\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
. Our goal is to show that, for
r>0
small enough,

\displaystyle f(\sigma(r,s))-c_{s}(y,\sigma(r,s))>f(z)-c_{s}(y,z),

(3.6)

contradicting the optimality of
z
, thus proving the claim.

By the compatibility of the connection with the metric, we compute for all
t\in[0,s]

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\Big|_{r=0}g(\partial_{t}\sigma(r,t),\partial_{t}\sigma(r,t))

\displaystyle=2g\bigg(\frac{D}{dr}\Big|_{r=0}\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial t}(r,t),\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial t}(0,t)\bigg)

\displaystyle=2g\bigg(\frac{D}{dt}\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial r}(0,t),\dot{\gamma}_{t}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2g\bigg(\frac{D}{dt}(t\xi_{t}),\dot{\gamma}_{t}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2g(\xi_{t},\dot{\gamma}_{t})

\displaystyle=2g(\xi_{s},\dot{\gamma}_{s})=:-2a<0,

where in the step to the last line we used the fact that
\xi
and
\dot{\gamma}
are parallel along
\gamma
.
Thus, Taylor expansion and the fact that
\gamma
is a null geodesic yield

\displaystyle g(\partial_{t}\sigma(r,t),\partial_{t}\sigma(r,t))\leq-|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|^{2}_{g}-2ar+O(r^{2})\leq-ar

(3.7)

for small values
r>0
and all
t\in[0,s]
. In particular,
\sigma(r,\cdot)
is either future timelike or past timelike for small
r
.
Since
y\neq z
and
z\in J^{+}(y)
we must have
z\notin J^{-}(y)
. Thus
z\approx\sigma(r,s)\notin J^{-}(y)
for small
r
, so that
\sigma(r,\cdot)
is in fact future timelike for small
r
.
This, together with

\displaystyle\ell_{g}(\sigma(r,\cdot))\geq s\sqrt{ar},

as follows from (
3.7
), gives

\displaystyle\ell(y,\sigma(r,s))\geq s\sqrt{ar}

and therefore

\displaystyle-c_{s}(y,\sigma(r,t))+c_{s}(y,z)=s^{1-p}\ell(y,\sigma(r,s))^{p}\geq s(ar)^{\frac{p}{2}}

(3.8)

for small
r
.
On the other hand, since
\partial_{r}\sigma(0,s)=s\xi_{s}
, it holds
d_{h}(\sigma(r,s),z)\leq 2s|\xi_{0}|_{h}r
, if
r
is small. Thus, denoting by
L
a local Lipschitz constant of
f
near
z
, it follows for small
r
that

\displaystyle|f(\sigma(r,s))-f(z)|\leq 2Ls\,|\xi_{0}|_{h}r.

Combining with (
3.8
), we see that that (
3.6
) holds for small
r
.

(iii)

Let
z\in V_{0}
be any optimal point, so that
z\in I^{+}(y)
by Part (ii). Let
\gamma:[0,s]\to M
be any maximizing geodesic connecting
y
to
z
. Theorem
2.11
states that
c_{s}
is super-differentiable at
(y,z)
with

\Big(-\frac{\partial L}{\partial v}(y,\dot{\gamma}_{0}),\frac{\partial L}{\partial v}(z,\dot{\gamma}_{s})\Big)\in\partial^{+}c_{s}(y,z).

It is then a simple consequence of the definition in (
3.1
) and the optimality of
z
that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial v}(y,\dot{\gamma}_{0})\in\partial^{-}\hat{T}_{s}^{V_{0}}f(y)\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{\partial L}{\partial v}(z,\dot{\gamma}_{s})\in\partial^{+}f(z).

In particular, since
\hat{T}_{s}^{V_{0}}f
is differentiable at
y
, its derivative must coincide with the unique sub-differential, and hence

d_{y}(\hat{T}^{V_{0}}_{s}f)=\frac{\partial L}{\partial v}(y,\dot{\gamma}_{0}).

It remains to prove the uniqueness of the maximizing curve
\gamma
(and hence also of
z
). Since the Legendre transform is a diffeomorphism, the preceding identity shows that
\dot{\gamma}_{0}
, and hence
\gamma
, is uniquely determined by the differential of
\hat{T}^{V_{0}}_{s}f
at
y
. This proves the uniqueness, since
z
was any optimal point and
\gamma
was any maximizing geodesic from
y
to
z
. ∎

Lemma 3.6

(Uniform control of maximizers)
.

Let
K_{0},V_{0}
and
\mathcal{F}
as in Remark
3.4
, and let
V\Subset V_{0}
. Then there exist constants
C
and
s_{0}>0
such that, for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
,
s\in(0,s_{0}]
and
y\in V
, the following inequalities hold

\displaystyle\hat{T}^{V_{0}}_{s}f(y)\geq f(y)>\sup\{f(z)-c_{s}(y,z)\mid z\in V_{0},\ d_{h}(y,z)\geq Cs^{1-p}\}.

(3.9)

In fact,
C
and
s_{0}
can be chosen such that the following stronger assertion holds: If
d_{h}(y,z)\geq Cs^{1-p}
for some
z\in V_{0}
, then

f(y)\geq f(z)-c_{s}(y,z)+\frac{C_{0}}{2}d_{h}(y,z),

where
C_{0}=C_{0}(K_{0},V_{0})
.

Proof.

The first inequality in (
3.9
) is obvious; the second inequality follows from the more general second part of the lemma, which we shall prove now.

Set
C:=\frac{2M^{p}}{C_{0}}
, where

\displaystyle M:=\sup\{\ell(y,z)\mid y,z\in\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{0},z\in J^{+}(y)\}

is finite thanks to the compactness of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{0}
. Now choose
s_{0}>0
with
Cs_{0}^{1-p}\leq d_{h}(V,\partial V_{0})
. Fix
f,s
and
y
as in the lemma, and let
z\in V_{0}
be such that
d_{h}(y,z)\geq Cs^{1-p}
.

Let
\gamma:[0,s]\to M
be a maximizing geodesic connecting
y
to
z
. Since
V_{0}
is causally convex, it follows that
\gamma_{t}\in V_{0}
for all
t\in[0,s]
. In particular,
\frac{d}{dt}(f\circ\gamma)(t)\leq-C_{0}|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|_{h}
for a.e.
t
. Thus

\displaystyle f(z)-f(y)=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{d}{dt}(f\circ\gamma)(t)\,dt\leq-C_{0}\,d_{h}(y,z).

Thus, using

\displaystyle T_{t_{0}}u(y_{0})=T_{t_{1}}u(\gamma_{t_{1}})+c_{t_{0}-t_{1}}(\gamma_{t_{1}},y_{0}).

(4.14)

On the other hand, the semigroup property of
Tu
yields

\displaystyle T_{t_{0}}u(y_{0})\leq u(x_{k})+c_{t_{0}}(x_{k},y_{0})\quad\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.15)

From (
4.13
),(
4.14
) and (
4.15
) we obtain

\displaystyle u(x_{k})+c_{t_{k}}(x_{k},y_{k})-\frac{1}{k}\leq

\displaystyle\ T_{t_{k}}u(y_{k})-T_{t_{1}}u(\gamma_{t_{1}})+T_{t_{1}}u(\gamma_{t_{1}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ T_{t_{k}}u(y_{k})-T_{t_{1}}u(\gamma_{t_{1}})+u(x_{k})

\displaystyle\ +c_{t_{0}}(x_{k},y_{0})-c_{t_{0}-t_{1}}(\gamma_{t_{1}},y_{0}).

Rearranging this inequality leads to

\displaystyle c_{t_{k}}(x_{k},y_{k})+c_{t_{0}-t_{1}}(\gamma_{t_{1}},y_{0})\leq T_{t_{k}}u(y_{k})-T_{t_{1}}u(\gamma_{t_{1}})+c_{t_{0}}(x_{k},y_{0}).

Since
K
is causally compact, along a subsequence that we do not relabel, we have
x_{k}\to x\in K
.
Since
J^{+}
is closed, it follows
x\in J^{-}(\gamma_{t_{1}})\subseteq I^{-}(y_{0})
, and by the continuity of the Lorentzian distance,
\ell(x,\gamma_{t_{1}})=0
. Taking limits in the above inequality and regarding the continuity of
Tu
at
(t_{1},\gamma_{t_{1}})
, we conclude

\displaystyle c_{t_{1}}(x,\gamma_{t_{1}})+c_{t_{0}-t_{1}}(\gamma_{t_{1}},y_{0})\leq c_{t_{0}}(x,y_{0}).

Since the reverse inequality always holds, this must be an equality. This and Lemma
2.5
imply that the curve obtained by concatenating a maximizing geodesic from
x
to
\gamma_{t_{1}}
in time
t_{1}
with the curve
\gamma_{|[t_{1},t_{0}]}
is still a maximizing geodesic. However, the first curve is not timelike, since
\ell(x,\gamma_{t_{1}})=0
. The second curve, on the other hand, is timelike because
(\gamma_{t_{1}},y_{0})\in I^{+}
. Since geodesics cannot change their causal type, this is a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

We already proved (
4.5
). We claim that
Tu
is locally semiconcave on
I\times V
. Indeed, fix
y\in V
, and notice that the previous result combines with the continuity of the Lorentzian distance function and the causal compactness of
K
to guarantee the existence of an open neighbourhoods
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
around
y
and of a compact set
K^{\prime}\subseteq K
with
V^{\prime}\times K^{\prime}\subseteq I^{+}
such that, for all
(t,y^{\prime})\in I\times V^{\prime}
, it holds

\displaystyle T_{t}u(y)=\inf\{u(x)+c_{t}(x,y)\mid x\in K^{\prime}\}.

Since
{\cal C}
is locally semiconcave on
(0,\infty)\times I^{+}
, it easily follows from the compactness of
K^{\prime}
that
Tu
is locally semiconcave on
I\times V^{\prime}
(
[
9
]
, Appendix A). Since
y
was arbitrary, the conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 5.1

Under the hypothesis and notation of Theorem

\tau\geq 0
such that

\tau\leq 1-t
. Then the pair

-subsolution. Moreover, whenever

(5.1)

Proof.

The proof is very similar to the one of Lemma

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

By assumption, the following identity holds for
\Pi
-a.e.
\gamma
:

\displaystyle\psi(\gamma_{1})-\varphi(\gamma_{0})=c(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})

(5.2)

By the inner regularity of
\Pi
, we may choose a
\sigma
-compact set
A\subseteq\operatorname{CGeo}([0,1],M)
consisting of future timelike geodesics with
\Pi(A)=1
and such that (
5.2
) holds for all
\gamma\in A
.

Let
\gamma\in A
. We claim that there exists
\tau=\tau(\gamma)>0
such that
\hat{T}_{\tau}T_{s+\tau}\varphi
and
\hat{T}_{\tau}T_{t+\tau}\varphi
are
C^{1,1}_{loc}
on open neighbourhoods
\Omega_{\gamma,s}
of
\gamma(s)
and
\Omega_{\gamma,t}
of
\gamma(t)
, respectively. Indeed, since
\varphi(\gamma_{0})
is finite due to (
5.2
), this result follows immediately from Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
if we can show that

T_{1}\varphi(\gamma_{1})=\varphi(\gamma_{0})+c_{1}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})

(here the assumption that
\Pi
is concentrated on timelike geodesics is crucial to apply both Theorems).

Indeed, observe that the fact that
(\varphi,\psi)
is a
c_{1}
-subsolution implies that
\psi\leq T_{1}\varphi
, which combines with the definition of
A
to yield

T_{1}\varphi(\gamma_{1})\geq\psi(\gamma_{1})\overset{(\gamma\in A)}{=}\varphi(\gamma_{0})+c(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})\geq T_{1}\varphi(\gamma_{1}),

having used the definition of the Lax-Oleinik semigroup in the last inequality. This proves the claim.

We may assume that
t-s\geq\tau(\gamma)
and that
\tau(\gamma)\leq 1-t
. Choose locally finite smooth partitions of unity
(\rho_{\gamma})_{\gamma}
and
(\eta_{\gamma})_{\gamma}
of

\Omega_{s}:=\bigcup_{\gamma\in A}\Omega_{\gamma,s}\quad\text{ and }\quad\Omega_{t}:=\bigcup_{\gamma\in A}\Omega_{\gamma,t},

w.r.t. the open coverings
(\Omega_{\gamma,s})_{\gamma}
and
(\Omega_{\gamma,t})_{\gamma}
.
Define

\displaystyle\varphi_{s}(x):=\begin{cases}(t-s)^{1-p}\sum\limits_{\gamma\in A}\rho_{\gamma}(x)\,(\hat{T}_{\tau_{\gamma}}\circ T_{s+{\tau_{\gamma}}}\varphi(x)),&\text{ if }x\in\Omega_{s},\\[5.0pt]
\varphi_{s}(x):=+\infty,&\text{ if }x\notin\Omega_{s},\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\psi_{t}(y):=\begin{cases}(t-s)^{1-p}\sum\limits_{\gamma\in A}\eta_{\gamma}(y)\,(\hat{T}_{\tau_{\gamma}}\circ T_{t+{\tau_{\gamma}}}\varphi(y)),&\text{ if }y\in\Omega_{t},\\[5.0pt]
\psi_{t}(y):=-\infty,&\text{ if }y\notin\Omega_{t}.\end{cases}

Clearly,
\mu_{s}(\Omega_{s})=\mu_{t}(\Omega_{t})=1
, and using the Lemma
5.1
it is easy to check that
(\varphi_{s},\psi_{t})
is
(c_{1},\pi_{s,t})
-calibrated. Being locally finite sums of
C^{1,1}_{loc}
functions,
\varphi_{s}
is
C^{1,1}_{loc}
on
\Omega_{s}
and
\psi_{t}
is
C^{1,1}_{loc}
on
\Omega_{t}
. This proves the first part of the theorem.

To conclude the proof, suppose that
(\varphi,\psi)
is optimal in the dual formulation, i.e.

\displaystyle C(\mu,\nu)=\int\psi(y)\,d\mu_{1}(y)-\int\varphi(x)\,d\mu_{0}(x),

and let us show that
(\varphi_{s},\psi_{t})
is optimal as well. Since
(\varphi_{s},\psi_{t})
is
(c_{1},\pi_{s,t})
-calibrated, it suffices to show that both functions are integrable w.r.t.
\mu_{s}
and
\mu_{t}
, respectively, and integrate the equation defining a calibrated pair. Let us prove the integrability of
\varphi_{s}

\psi_{t}
can be checked analogously.

Clearly,
\varphi_{s}
is
\mu_{s}
-measurable since it is

on an open set of full
\mu_{s}
-measure. Since each
\gamma\in A
is

-calibrated by Lemma

, we have

\varphi_{s}(\gamma_{s})=\varphi(\gamma_{0})+c_{s}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{s})=\varphi(\gamma_{0})+sc_{1}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})\quad\text{for $\Pi$-a.e. $\gamma$.}

This implies

\displaystyle\int|\varphi_{s}(x)|\,d\mu_{s}(x)=\int|\varphi_{s}(\gamma_{s})|\,d\Pi(\gamma)

\displaystyle\leq\int|\varphi(\gamma(0))|+s\,|c_{1}(\gamma(0),\gamma(1))|\,d\Pi(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int|\varphi(x)|\,d\mu_{0}(x)-s\,C(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})<\infty.

Hence,

Proof of Lemma

.

For
p\in\operatorname{int}({\cal C}_{x}^{*})

\displaystyle pv-L(x,v)\xrightarrow{|v|_{h}\to\infty}-\infty.

Indeed, if

Appendix B
Semiconvexity

Lemma B.1

(Criterion for uniform semiconvexity)
.

Suppose that
(f_{i})_{i}:U\to\mathbb{R}
is a family of
C^{1}
-functions defined on the open subset
U\subseteq M
. Assume that there exists a compact set
K\subseteq T^{*}M
such that

\{(x,d_{x}f_{i})\mid x\in U\}\subseteq K.

Moreover, let
h
and
h_{*}
be Riemannian metrics on
M
and
T^{*}M
, respectively, and suppose that the family of functions

df_{i}:U\to T^{*}M,\ x\mapsto(x,d_{x}f_{i}),

is equi-Lipschitz. Then the family
(f_{i})
is both uniformly locally semiconcave and semiconvex.

Proof.

Fix
x_{0}\in U
and let
\varphi:V\subseteq U\to\varphi(V)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth chart around
x_{0}
. For any
W\Subset V
, the mappings

\varphi_{|W}^{-1}:\varphi(W)\to W,\quad\text{ and }\quad T^{*}\varphi:T^{*}W\cap K\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

are Lipschitz continuous (here,
T^{*}\phi
denotes the cotangent bundle chart). It follows that the functions

\varphi(W)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\ x\mapsto(x,D(f_{i}\circ\varphi^{-1})(x))=T^{*}\varphi\circ df_{i}\circ\varphi_{|W}^{-1}(x)

are equi-Lipschitz. Using Taylor’s theorem, this can easily be used to prove that the functions
(f_{i}\circ\varphi^{-1})
are uniformly locally semiconcave and semiconvex.
∎
