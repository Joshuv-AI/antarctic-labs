Some local and global properties of secant varieties of nonsingular projective curves
Lawrence Ein, Wenbo Niu, Jinhyung Park

Abstract
. The main goal of this paper is to study some local and global properties of secant varieties of algebraic curves. These results complement our previous work [ 8 ] by addressing issues given therein and providing solutions to problems raised subsequently. Specifically, we show a description of tangent cones of secant varieties of curves, and compute the cohomology groups of secant sheaves on symmetric products of curves, which answers a question posed in [ 8 ] and leads to a recursive formula for Hilbert polynomials of secant varieties of curves. In the appendix, we present a cohomological approach to arithmetical Cohen–Macaulayness of secant varieties of curves, completing the proof in [ 8 ] .

Our second result is the computation of the cohomology groups of symmetric powers of secant sheaves on symmetric products of algebraic curves. Lemma 3.1
.

For any integer

, the natural map

The map

fits into the following commutative diagram

As

is normal, the natural map

. Consider the commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

As an interesting application of the description of tangent cones, we give a proof for the result that secant varieties are arithmetical Cohen–Macaulay. This result was asserted in
[
8
]
by a cohomological approach (we will also discuss it in details in Appendix
A
). First, we show that this can be reduced to proving the secant varieties are “locally” Cohen–Macaulay.

Lemma 3.6
.

Fix an integer
k\geq 0
. For each
0\leq m\leq k
, assume that
\Sigma_{m}(\mathscr{L})
is Cohen–Macaulay for any line bundle
\mathscr{L}
on
C
with
\deg\mathscr{L}\geq 2g+2m+1
. Then
\Sigma_{k}=\Sigma_{k}(L)\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}=\mathbb{P}H^{0}(C,L)
is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay for any line bundle
L
on
C
with
\deg L\geq 2g+2k+1
.

Proof.

When
k=0
, the assertion is just the well-known theorem of Castelnuovo that
C\subseteq\mathbb{P}H^{0}(L)
is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay. By induction on
k
, for each
0\leq m\leq k-1
, we may assume that
\Sigma_{m}(\mathscr{L})\subseteq\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathscr{L})
is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay when
\deg\mathscr{L}\geq 2g+2m+1
. By Theorem
2.4
, it is enough to show the vanishing

(3.2)

H^{i}(\Sigma_{k},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell))=0\text{ for $1\leq i\leq 2k$ and $\ell\leq 0$}.

Since
\Sigma_{k}
is Cohen–Macaulay, we have (
3.2
) for
\ell\ll 0
.
By
[
10
, Theorem 9.12]
, we get (
3.2
) for
\ell\leq 0
. The only left case is when
\ell=0
. We only need to show that

H^{i}(\Sigma_{k},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}})=0~~\text{ for $1\leq i\leq 2k$}.

By induction on
k
, we may assume that
H^{i}(\Sigma_{k-1},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k-1}})=0
for
1\leq i\leq 2k-2
. Chasing through the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow I_{\Sigma_{k-1}/\Sigma_{k}}\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}\longrightarrow 0,

it is then sufficient to show that

(1)

H^{i}(\Sigma_{k},I_{\Sigma_{k-1}/\Sigma_{k}})=0,\text{ for }1\leq i\leq 2k-1,\text{ and }

(2)

the connection map

\tau\colon H^{2k-1}(\Sigma_{k},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k-1}})\longrightarrow H^{2k}(\Sigma_{k},I_{\Sigma_{k-1}/\Sigma_{k}})

is an isomorphism.

By Proposition
2.5
, we have

H^{i}(\Sigma_{k},I_{\Sigma_{k-1}/\Sigma_{k}})^{*}=H^{2k+1-i}(C_{k+1},T_{k+1}(\omega_{C}))\text{ for all }i\geq 0.

Then (1) follows from Lemma
2.3
which gives

H^{2k+1-i}(C_{k+1},T_{k+1}(\omega_{C}))=0,\text{ for }1\leq i\leq 2k-1.

For (2), applying the exact triangle (
2.1
) for
\Sigma_{k-1}
and taking the
(2k-1)
-th cohomology we compute the dualizing sheaf of
\Sigma_{k-1}
as

\omega_{\Sigma_{k-1}}=\beta_{k-1,*}\omega_{B^{k-1}(L)}(Z_{k-2}).

So we compute by Serre duality that

H^{2k-1}(\Sigma_{k},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k-1}})^{*}=H^{0}(\Sigma_{k-1},\omega_{\Sigma_{k-1}})=H^{0}(B^{k-1}(L),\omega_{B^{k-1}(L)}(Z_{k-2}))=H^{0}(C_{k},T_{k}(\omega_{C}))

which equals
H^{1}(C_{k+1},T_{k+1}(\omega_{C}))=H^{2k}(\Sigma_{k},I_{\Sigma_{k-1}/\Sigma_{k}})^{*}
. Thus
\tau
is an isomorphism.
∎

We show that the secant varieties are indeed locally Cohen–Macaulay.

Corollary 3.7
.

The secant variety
\Sigma_{k}
is Cohen–Macaulay.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k
. When
k=0
, the secant variety
\Sigma_{0}
is just the curve
C
itself, so
\Sigma_{0}
is Cohen–Macaulay. By induction, we may assume that for each
0\leq m\leq k-1
, the secant variety
\Sigma_{m}(\mathscr{L})
is Cohen–Macaulay for any line bundle
\mathscr{L}
on
C
with
\deg\mathscr{L}\geq 2g+2m+1
. By Lemma
3.6
,
\Sigma_{m}(\mathscr{L})
is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay in
\mathbb{P}H^{0}(C,\mathscr{L})
. Take a point
x\in\Sigma_{k}
. If
x\in\Sigma_{k}-\Sigma_{k-1}
, then
\Sigma_{k}
is nonsingular at
x
and thus it is Cohen–Macaulay at
x
. So we consider the case when
x\in\Sigma_{k-1}
. By Theorem
3.2
, the tangent cone
\mathbb{P}(G)
of
\Sigma_{k}
at
x
is the cone over the secant variety
\Sigma_{k-m-1}(L(-2\xi))
in the space
\mathbb{P}(T^{*}_{x}\mathbb{P}^{r})
. But by induction,
\Sigma_{k-m-1}(L(-2\xi))
is arithmetical Cohen–Macaulay in
\mathbb{P}H^{0}(C,L(-2\xi))
, and thus,
\mathbb{P}(G)
is arithmetical Cohen–Macaulay in
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
. Corollary
3.5
says that the associated graded ring
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}})
is the coordinate ring of
\mathbb{P}(G)
. Thus
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}})
is Cohen–Macaulay, and therefore,
\Sigma_{k}
is Cohen–Macaulay at the point
x
(see Remark
3.8
below).
∎

Remark 3.8
.

In the proof of Corollary
3.7
, we use a well-known commutative algebra fact: For a local noetherian ring
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
such that the residue class field
k
has characteristic zero, if the associated graded ring
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=\oplus_{l\geq 0}\mathfrak{m}^{l}/\mathfrak{m}^{l+1}
is Cohen–Macaulay, then so is the local ring
R
. As we could not find a suitable reference, we give a sketch of the proof. Since
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}R
is generated by
\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}
, we can take general linear forms
s^{*}_{1},\cdots,s^{*}_{n}
, where
n=\dim R
as a regular sequence of
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}R
. Then they determine a regular sequence
s_{1},\cdots,s_{n}\in\mathfrak{m}
, which proves the result.

Corollary 3.9
.

The secant variety
\Sigma_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay, i.e., one has the following:

(1)

\Sigma_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is projectively normal.

(2)

H^{i}(\Sigma_{k},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell))=0
for
1\leq i\leq 2k
and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Immediate from Lemma
3.6
and Corollary
3.7
.
∎

Finally, we consider a cone
\overline{P}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r+m}
over the secant variety
\Sigma_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
with the vertex
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r+m}
, which is a linear subspace of dimension
m-1
. Let

P:=\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(1)\oplus\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}^{\oplus m})

with canonical projection
\tau\colon P\to\Sigma_{k}
. Then
\lvert\mathscr{O}_{P}(1)\rvert
induces a birational morphism

\psi\colon P\longrightarrow\overline{P}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r+m}

contracting an effective divisor
E:=\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}^{\oplus m})\subseteq P
to to the vertex
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r+m}
of
\overline{P}
. Note that
\psi
is the blow-up of
\overline{P}
along
\Lambda
with exceptional divisor

.

Theorem 3.10
.

The cone
\overline{P}
has normal Cohen–Macaulay Du Bois singularities, and
\overline{P}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r+m}
is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay. Consequently, the affine cone over
\Sigma_{k}
and all the tangent cones to
\Sigma_{k}
have normal Cohen–Macaulay Du Bois singularities.

Proof.

Since
\Sigma_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay, it follows that
\overline{P}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r+m}
is also arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay. In particular,
\overline{P}
has normal Cohen–Macaulay singularities. It only remains to show that
\overline{P}
has Du Bois singularities. Notice that
P,E
, and
\Lambda
have Du Bois singularities. By Kollár–Kovács criterion
[
11
, Corollary 6.28]
, it is enough to check that
R^{i}\psi_{*}\mathscr{O}_{P}(-E)=0
for
i>0
, which is equivalent to that

H^{i}(P,\mathscr{O}_{P}(\ell H-E))=0
for
i>0
and
\ell\gg 0
, where
H
is a tautological divisor on
P
. Since
\mathscr{O}_{P}(-E)=\mathscr{O}_{P}(-1)\otimes\tau^{*}\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(1)
and
H^{i}(\Sigma_{k},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(m))=0
for
i>0
and
m>0
, we have

H^{i}(P,\mathscr{O}_{P}(\ell H-E))=H^{i}(\Sigma_{k},S^{\ell-1}(\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(1)\oplus\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}^{\oplus m})\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(1))=0~~\text{ for $i>0$ and $\ell>0$}.\qed

Remark 3.11
.

Theorem 4.2
.

For each integer
0\leq i\leq k
, let
\Sigma_{i}:=\Sigma_{i}(L)
be the
i
-th secant variety of
C
in the space
\mathbb{P}^{r}=\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(L))
, where
L
is a line bundle on
C
with
\deg L\geq 2g+2k+1
.
One has the following:

(1)

R^{i}\beta_{k,*}\mathscr{O}_{B^{k}(L)}=\begin{cases}\wedge^{i}H^{1}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C})\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k-i}}&\text{for $0\leq i\leq k$}\\
0&\text{for $i\geq k+1$}.\end{cases}

(2)

For integers
0\leq i\leq k+1
and
\ell>0
, we have

H^{i}(C_{k+1},S^{\ell}E_{k+1,L})=H^{i}(B^{k}(L),\mathscr{O}_{B^{k}}(\ell))=\wedge^{i}H^{1}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C})\otimes H^{0}(\Sigma_{k-i},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k-i}}(\ell)).

In particular,
H^{k+1}(C_{k+1},S^{\ell}E_{k+1,L})=0
for
\ell>0
.

Proof.

First, we prove that
(1)
implies
(2)
. It is clear that

H^{i}(C_{k+1},S^{\ell}E_{k+1,L})=H^{i}(B^{k}(L),\mathscr{O}_{B^{k}}(\ell))~\text{ for $i\geq 0$ and $\ell>0$}.

Note that
H^{j}(\Sigma_{k-i},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k-i}}(\ell))=0
for
j>0
and
\ell>0
. By (1), we have
H^{i}(B^{k}(L),\mathscr{O}_{B^{k}}(\ell))=\wedge^{i}H^{1}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C})\otimes H^{0}(\Sigma_{k-i},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k-i}}(\ell))
.

Now, we prove
(1)
. Let
x\in\Sigma_{m}\setminus\Sigma_{m-1}
in
\Sigma_{k}
. There is
\xi_{x}\in C_{m+1}
such that
x\in\langle\xi_{x}\rangle
. Recall that
F_{x}:=\beta_{k}^{-1}(x)\cong C_{k-m}
. Then
\Supp(R^{i}\beta_{k,*}\mathscr{O}_{B^{k}(L)})\subseteq\Sigma_{k-i}
. In particular,
R^{i}\beta_{k,*}\mathscr{O}_{B^{k}(L)}=0
for
i\geq k+1
. As
\Sigma_{k}
is normal, we get
\beta_{k,*}\mathscr{O}_{B^{k}(L)}=\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}
. We henceforth focus on the cases
1\leq i\leq k
. We need to assume that
0\leq m\leq k-i
. We proceed by induction on
k
. The case of
k=1
is trivial. Assume that
k\geq 2
. Consider the commutative diagram (
4.2
) in which
\alpha_{k,k-1}
is a finite by Lemma
4.1
. As

R^{i}\beta_{k,*}\alpha_{k,k-1,*}\mathscr{O}_{B^{k-1}(L)\times C}=R^{i}(\beta_{k}\circ\alpha_{k,k-1})_{*}\mathscr{O}_{B^{k-1}(L)\times C}=R^{i}(\beta_{k-1}\circ\pr_{1})_{*}\mathscr{O}_{B^{k-1}(L)\times C},

the map
\mathscr{O}_{B^{k}(L)}\rightarrow\alpha_{k,k-1,*}\mathscr{O}_{B^{k-1}(L)\times C}
gives rise to a map

R^{i}\beta_{k,*}\mathscr{O}_{B^{k}(L)}\longrightarrow R^{i}(\beta_{k-1}\circ\pr_{1})_{*}\mathscr{O}_{B^{k-1}(L)\times C}.

The Grothendieck spectral sequence then yields a map

R^{i}(\beta_{k-1}\circ\pr_{1})_{*}\mathscr{O}_{B^{k-1}(L)\times C}\longrightarrow R^{i-1}\beta_{k-1,*}R^{1}\pr_{1,*}\mathscr{O}_{B^{k-1}(L)\times C}.

But we have

\displaystyle R^{i-1}\beta_{k-1,*}R^{1}\pr_{1,*}\mathscr{O}_{B^{k-1}(L)\times C}

\displaystyle=H^{1}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C})\otimes R^{i-1}\beta_{k-1,*}\mathscr{O}_{B^{k-1}(L)}

\displaystyle=H^{1}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C})\otimes\wedge^{i-1}H^{1}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C})\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k-i}},

so we obtain a map

R^{i}\beta_{k,*}\mathscr{O}_{B^{k}(L)}\longrightarrow\wedge^{i}H^{1}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C})\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k-i}}

factoring through

.
By the formal function theorem, this map can be identified with

\varprojlim H^{i}(\mathscr{O}_{B^{k}(L)}/I_{F_{x}}^{\ell})\longrightarrow\wedge^{i}H^{1}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C})\otimes\varprojlim H^{0}(B^{k-i}(L),\mathscr{O}_{B^{k-i}(L)}/I_{G_{x}}^{\ell}),

where

.
Recall that

N_{F_{x}/B^{k}(L)}^{*}=\mathscr{O}_{F_{x}}^{\oplus 2m+1}\oplus E_{k-m,L(-2\xi_{x})}~~\text{ and }~~N_{G_{x}/B^{k-i}(L)}^{*}=\mathscr{O}_{G_{x}}^{\oplus 2m+1}\oplus E_{k-i-m,L(-2\xi_{x})}.

\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{i}(I_{F_{x}}^{\ell}/I_{F_{x}}^{\ell+1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{H^{i}(\mathscr{O}_{B^{k}(L)}/I_{F_{x}}^{\ell})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Note that

\displaystyle H^{i}(C_{k-m},S^{\ell}E_{k-m,L(-2\xi_{x})})

While

Lemma 4.3
.

For an integer
-k\leq\ell\leq-1
, there is an exact sequence

\begin{array}[]{l}0\longrightarrow\wedge^{k}H^{1}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C})\otimes H^{1}(\Sigma_{0},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{0}}(\ell))\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\wedge^{2}H^{1}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C})\otimes H^{2k-3}(\Sigma_{k-2},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k-2}}(\ell))\\
\longrightarrow H^{1}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C})\otimes H^{2k-1}(\Sigma_{k-1},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k-1}}(\ell))\longrightarrow H^{2k+1}(\Sigma_{k},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell))\longrightarrow 0.\end{array}

Proof.

Since
-k\leq\ell\leq-1
, it follows that
H^{i}(B^{k}(L),\mathscr{O}_{B^{k}(L)}(\ell))=0
for
0\leq i\leq 2k+1
. By Corollary

, for each
0\leq m\leq k
, we have
H^{i}(\Sigma_{k-m},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k-m}}(\ell))=0
for
0\leq i\leq 2k-2m
. Then the Leray spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}(\Sigma_{k},R^{q}\beta_{k,*}\mathscr{O}_{B^{k}(L)}(\ell))\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}(B^{k}(L),\mathscr{O}_{B^{k}(L)}(\ell))=0

yields that the complex from
E_{2}
-page, which is the sequence in the statement, is exact.
∎

We show a recursive formula for the Hilbert polynomial
\chi(\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(m))
of the secant variety
\Sigma_{k}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume that
d:=\deg L\geq 2g+2k+1
, and let
\Sigma_{k}:=\Sigma_{k}(L)
be the
k
-th secant variety of the curve
C
in the space
\mathbb{P}H^{0}(C,L)
. For any
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, one has

\chi(\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(m))=\sum_{\ell=-k}^{k+1}(-1)^{k+1-\ell}a_{\ell}{2k+2\choose k+\ell}{m+k\choose 2k+2}\frac{k+2-\ell}{m-\ell},

where

a_{\ell}:=\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i}{g\choose i}\chi(\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k-i}}(\ell))&\text{for $-k\leq\ell\leq-1$}\\
1-{g+k\choose k+1}&\text{for $\ell=0$}\\
{d-g+\ell\choose\ell}&\text{for $1\leq\ell\leq k+1$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall that
H^{i}(\Sigma_{k},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell))=0
for
1\leq i\leq 2k
and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\chi(\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(m))
is a polynomial of degree
2k+1
in
m
. To compute
\chi(\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(m))
, it suffices to calculate
\chi(\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell))
for
2k+2
many different values of
\ell
. We treat the cases where
-k\leq\ell\leq k+1
. Recall from
[
8
, Theorem 1.2]
that
h^{2k+1}(\Sigma_{k},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}})=h^{0}(\Sigma_{k},\omega_{\Sigma_{k}})={g+k\choose k+1}
.
Then we have

\chi(\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}})=h^{0}(\Sigma_{k},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}})-h^{2k+1}(\Sigma_{k},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}})=1-{g+k\choose k+1}=a_{0}.

For
1\leq\ell\leq k+1
, Danila’s theorem
[
6
]
(see also
[
9
]
) gives

\chi(\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell))=h^{0}(\Sigma_{k},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell))={d-g+\ell\choose\ell}=a_{\ell}.

For
-k\leq\ell\leq-1
, Lemma
4.3
gives

\chi(\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell))=-h^{2k+1}(\Sigma_{k},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell))=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i}{g\choose i}\chi(\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k-i}}(\ell))=a_{\ell}.

Then the assertion follows from the Lagrange interpolation formula.
∎

Example 4.5
.

We explicitly compute the Hilbert polynomial of
\Sigma_{k}
for

and

.
First, consider the case of

Then

This coincides with Riemann–Roch formula.

\displaystyle-(d-g+1)\frac{(m+1)m(m-2)}{2}+(d-g+2)(d-g+1)\frac{(m+1)m(m-1)}{12}.

\displaystyle=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(k+2))-h^{0}(\Sigma_{k},\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(k+2))

\displaystyle={d-g+k+2\choose k+2}-\chi(\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(k+2)),

which turns out to be a polynomial in

once we fix

and
k
.

One may also want to compute the cohomology groups of exterior powers of secant sheaves on
C_{k+1}
. For this purpose, we consider the addition map

\sigma=\sigma_{k+1-\ell,\ell}\colon C_{k+1-\ell}\times C_{\ell}\longrightarrow C_{k+1}

sending
(\xi,\eta)
to
\xi+\eta
for
1\leq\ell\leq k+1
. Note that
\sigma
is a finite and flat morhpism of degree
{k+1\choose\ell}
. The pullback of the line bundle
T_{k+1}(L)
by the map
\sigma
is

\sigma^{*}T_{k+1}(L)=T_{k+1-\ell}(L)\boxtimes T_{\ell}(L),

which can be verified by pulling back to the Cartesian product
C^{k+1}
(see
[
8
, Remark 3.3(5)]
). We prove the following lemma, whose special case when
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
was observed by Raicu–Sam
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Lemma 4.7
.

For any line bundle
L
on
C
, we have

\wedge^{\ell}E_{k+1,L}=\sigma_{*}(\mathscr{O}_{C_{k+1-\ell}}\boxtimes N_{\ell,L})~~\text{ for $1\leq\ell\leq k+1$}.

Proof.

By
[
1
, Lemma 3.1]
, we have a map

\sigma^{*}E_{k+1,L}\to\mathscr{O}_{C_{k+1-\ell}}\boxtimes E_{\ell,L}
. Taking
\ell
-th exterior power, we get a map
\sigma^{*}\wedge^{\ell}E_{k+1,L}\to\mathscr{O}_{C_{k+1-\ell}}\boxtimes N_{\ell,L}
. Pushing forward this map by
\sigma
and composing with the splitting injective map
\wedge^{\ell}E_{k+1,L}\to\sigma_{*}\sigma^{*}\wedge^{\ell}E_{k+1,L}
, we obtain a map

\varphi_{L}\colon\wedge^{\ell}E_{k+1,L}\longrightarrow\sigma_{*}(\mathscr{O}_{C_{k+1-\ell}}\boxtimes N_{\ell,L}).

If
\xi\in C_{k+1}
consists of
k+1
distinct points on
C
, then one can easily check that
\varphi_{L}\otimes\Bbbk(\xi)
is an isomorphism because

\wedge^{\ell}E_{k+1,L}\otimes\Bbbk(\xi)=\wedge^{\ell}H^{0}(L|_{\xi})=\bigoplus_{\eta\subseteq\xi,\deg\eta=\ell}N_{\ell,L}\otimes\Bbbk(\eta).

Thus
\varphi_{L}
is generically injective, so in fact,
\varphi_{L}
is injective. We need to confirm that
\varphi_{L}
is surjective. For any
\xi\in C_{k+1}
, there is an affine open subset
U\subseteq C
such that
\xi\subseteq U
and
L|_{U}=\mathscr{O}_{U}
. Then the
(k+1)
-th symmetric product
U_{k+1}
of
U
is an open subset of
C_{k+1}
, and
\varphi_{L}|_{U_{k+1}}=\varphi_{\mathscr{O}_{C}}|_{U_{k+1}}
. Therefore, if we prove that
\varphi_{L}
is surjective for one line bundle
L
, then the same holds for all line bundles
L
. We henceforth assume that
L
is sufficiently positive. It suffices to show that the map

(4.4)

H^{0}(C_{k+1},\wedge^{\ell}E_{k+1,L}\otimes T_{k+1}(M))\longrightarrow H^{0}(C_{k+1-\ell}\times C_{\ell},T_{k+1-\ell}(M)\boxtimes N_{\ell,L\otimes M})

is surjective for a sufficiently positive line bundle
M
on
C
.

To this end, consider
C_{k+1-\ell}\times C_{\ell}
embedded as a subvariety into
C_{k+1}\times C_{\ell}
by sending
(\xi,\eta)\mapsto(\xi+\eta,\eta)
.
Notice that
H^{0}(L)=H^{0}(C_{k+1},E_{k+1,L})
. So for an integer
\ell\geq 0
, there is a natural evaluation map
\wedge^{\ell}H^{0}(L)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{C_{k+1}}\rightarrow\wedge^{\ell}E_{k+1,L}
of sheaves. Working on
C_{k+1}\times C_{\ell}
, we observe that

(\wedge^{\ell}E_{k+1,L}\otimes T_{k+1}(M))\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{C_{\ell}}|_{C_{k+1-\ell}\times C_{\ell}}=\sigma^{*}(\wedge^{\ell}E_{k+1,L}\otimes T_{k+1}(M)),\text{ and}

T_{k+1,M}\boxtimes N_{\ell,L}|_{C_{k+1-\ell}\times C_{\ell}}=T_{k+1-\ell}(M)\boxtimes N_{\ell,L\otimes M}.

Thus we can form the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{\wedge^{\ell}H^{0}(L)\otimes(T_{k+1}(M)\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{C_{\ell}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\wedge^{\ell}E_{k+1,L}\otimes T_{k+1}(M))\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{C_{\ell}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\sigma^{*}(\wedge^{\ell}E_{k+1,L}\otimes T_{k+1}(M))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T_{k+1,M}\boxtimes N_{\ell,L}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where the left upper horizontal map and the left vertical map are the evaluation maps and the right upper horizontal map and the lower horizontal maps are the restriction maps to

As the map (

Remark 4.8
.

Assuming that
\varphi_{L}
is an isomorphism for a sufficiently positive line bundle

\varphi_{L}
is an isomorphism for every line bundle

as follows.
For a point

, consider the commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Theorem 4.9
.

For line bundles
L
and
M
on
C
, we have

\displaystyle H^{i}(C_{k+1},\wedge^{\ell}E_{k+1,L}\otimes T_{k+1}(M))

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{p+q=i}\big(S^{k+1-\ell-p}H^{0}(C,M)\otimes S^{q}H^{1}(C,L\otimes M)\otimes\wedge^{p}H^{1}(C,M)\otimes\wedge^{\ell-q}H^{0}(C,L\otimes M)\big)

for
0\leq i\leq k+1
and
1\leq\ell\leq k+1
. In particular,

H^{i}(C_{k+1},\wedge^{\ell}E_{k+1,L})=\bigoplus_{p+q=i}\big(S^{q}H^{1}(C,L)\otimes\wedge^{p}H^{1}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C})\otimes\wedge^{\ell-q}H^{0}(C,L)\big)

for
0\leq i\leq k+1
and
1\leq\ell\leq k+1
.

Proof.

Note that
\sigma^{*}T_{k+1}(M)=T_{k+1-\ell}(M)\boxtimes T_{\ell}(M)
.
By Lemma
4.7
, we have

\wedge^{\ell}E_{k+1,L}\otimes T_{k+1}(M)=\sigma_{*}(T_{k+1-\ell}(M)\boxtimes N_{\ell,L\otimes M}).

The assertion follows from Künneth formula and Lemma
2.3
.
∎

We remark that we do not impose any conditions on the line bundles
L
and
M
for the above theorem.
