1.) Executive Summary
This project support the UW Madison PI O. Schmitz in the execution of his responsibilities as
Deputy Director of the U.S. Burning Plasma Organization USBPO. This assigned role covers the
administrative organization of the USBPO as central U.S. entity for organization of general
stakeholder engagement around burning plasmas. These are plasmas in which the deuterium
tritium fusion reaction takes place at a level that generates more energy than consumed to
establish and maintain the thermos-nuclear plasma medium.
The USBPO supports the U.S. wide organization of research and development in the area. This
entails being a main stakeholder for the U.S. participation in ITER as well as collaborating with
other national institutions to organize R&D efforts, suggest research strategy and also organize
community groups to deliberate about burning plasma topics and suggest activities within the
U.S.. The deputy director also maintains the information flow, from websites to newsletters and
is the formal chair of the USBPO research council. This role is also responsible to organize the
U.S. participation to the annual ITER Summer School.
The UW Madison lead PI Schmitz held this role for four years from 2019-2023. During this time
many programmatic changes had to be overseen, and the appropriate change management had
to be conducted. PI Schmitz was responsible for the highest ever attendance of U.S. students and
post-docs to the 2022 ITER Summer School and coordinated the input of the USBPO to the
reaction of the U.S. on the ITER research plan. This effort had an influence on the way ITER was
presented in the long range plan that the U.S. fusion community compiled. Prof. Schmitz ended
his appointment as USBPO Deputy Director due to growing administrative responsibilities at his
home institution where he became associate dean.

2.) Background of the U.S. Burning Plasma Organization
The U.S. Burning Plasma Organization was founded to advance U.S. scientific and technical
participation in burning-plasma research—particularly magnetic confinement fusion where the
plasma’s self-heating from fusion reactions (alpha-particle heating) dominates energy balance.
The USBPO supports the U.S. wide organization of research and development in the area. This
entails being a main stakeholder for the U.S. participation in ITER as well as collaborating with
other national institutions to organize R&D efforts, suggest research strategy and also organize
community groups to deliberate about burning plasma topics and suggest activities within the
U.S.. The USBPO had also been a major driver in the U.S. nationwide discussion about fusion
nuclear science facilities and their impact on developing burning plasma experiments. Recently,
the USBPO has engaged with companies in the private sector, which are en-route, according to
their own plans, to realize a burning plasma within the next decade.
The USBPO was established in the mid-2000s (formally around 2005–2006) in response to the
growing international effort on ITER and recognition that the U.S. fusion community needed a
focused, community-driven body to articulate science priorities, synthesize research needs, and
organize contributions to burning-plasma experiments. It mirrors similar organizations in other
countries, for instance EUROfusion in Europe, that coordinate community planning for targeted
fusion science and technology R&D with the goal of a burning plasma.
The USBPO is a membership-driven organization composed of plasma scientists, engineers, and
students from universities, national labs, and industry. It is governed by an executive committee
and topical working groups of its research council. The established working groups are transport
and confinement, stability and disruptions, energetic particles, diagnostics, theory and
simulation, plasma–materials interactions. The USBPO leadership is elected or appointed from
the community and approved by the Office of Fusion Energy Sciences in the Office of Science at
the Department of Energy. The constituency of the USBPO typically includes representatives from
major U.S. labs and universities. The current director of the organization is Dr. Charles Greenfield,
Oak Ridge National Laboratory. Prof. Oliver Schmitz was the USBPO Deputy Director until 2023.
Typical activities of the USBPO can be summarized as follows:

 • Community coordination: Develops white papers, community reports, and prioritized
 research needs for burning-plasma science in the U.S..
 • Workshops and meetings: Organizes topical workshops, sessions at major conferences,
 and community meetings to foster collaboration and disseminate research results.
 • Education and outreach: Runs seminars, summer schools, and supports student/postdoc
 involvement to grow the next generation of researchers, organizes U.S. participation in
 the ITER Summer School.
 • Liaison and advocacy: Serves as a point of contact between the U.S. fusion community
 and ITER, the U.S. ITER program office, DOE program offices, and international bodies;
 provides community advice on experiment participation and research directions.

 • Research synthesis: Produces assessments and roadmaps that connect theory,
 experiment, and modeling needs for understanding burning plasmas.

The USBPO does not operate experiments itself but helps to coordinate the U.S. scientific
program contributing to ITER and other burning-plasma research and experiments. It interfaces
with DOE Office of Science and national laboratories to align community scientific priorities with
national planning and resource allocation. Through these activities, the USBPO has helped
synthesize the U.S. research agenda on burning-plasma science, sharpen community priorities
for ITER and for domestic facilities, and foster collaboration across institutions. Its topical working
groups have produced influential documents on diagnostics needs, disruption mitigation,
transport scaling, and integrated modeling for burning-plasma conditions. Recently, the USBPO
has engaged with private sector activities that seek to realize a burning plasma within a decade.

3.) Overview of activities and outcome as deputy director of the USBPO

The UW Madison lead PI Schmitz held this role for four years from 2019-2023. During this time
many programmatic changes had to be overseen, and the appropriate change management had
to be conducted. PI Schmitz was responsible for the highest ever attendance of U.S. students and
post-docs to the 2022 ITER Summer School and coordinated the input of the USBPO to the
reaction of the U.S. on the ITER research plan. This effort had an influence on the way ITER was
presented in the long-range plan that the U.S. fusion community compiled. Prof. Schmitz ended
his appointment as USBPO Deputy Director due to growing administrative responsibilities at his
home institution where he became associate dean in the College of Engineering.
The role as deputy director of the USBPO entailed initiating and chairing regular meetings of the
USBPO Research Committee, communicating with the USBPO membership via emails and web
seminars, forming and overseeing activities of Task Groups, and ensuring good communication
about activities of the ITPA. An area of increased emphasis will be enhanced communication to
the wider scientific community, and the public, about burning plasma research and developing
the program in the ITER era. Also, several initiatives are presently discussed in the ongoing APS
Community Process for strategic planning. The task of the Deputy Director and the Director of
the U.S. BPO is to provide support to the community to establish actions out of these initiatives
and help to develop them to the benefit of Burning Plasma Science. The new Deputy Director
also aims on becoming engaged with the outside representation of the organization on national
and international meetings.
Responsibilities and outcomes serving as Deputy Director included:
 • Research Committee: organize and lead meetings of the Research Committee (RC),
 setting agendas, arranging remote communication, and sending action items and meeting
 minutes following meetings. Typical frequency of the meeting was every 3 weeks. This
 responsibility also included to organize the membership on the U.S. BPO RC together with
 the Director and propose new members for approval to the executive committee. Also,
 agenda and topics for the work of the RC were initiated by the Directorate of the BPO
 together with the RC members and the U.S. BPO executive committee.

• Web seminars: As described above, USBPO web seminars had been proven a highly
 effective and popular means of engaging and informing our community on burning
 plasma news as well as broader strategic and programmatic topics for the fusion science
 and technology field. A diverse roster of speakers and topics is a priority for these
 meetings and the Deputy Director served to further increase the frequency of the
 meetings and encouraging a broader participation from the community. The Deputy
 Director used these communications means as a way to keep the momentum in the
 community discussions on a long-range strategic plan towards a Fusion Pilot Plan high.
 Also, as ITER approaches the pre-fusion power phase, it was important to raise the
 awareness of the community for this and start to form research topics and activities that
 were aligned and continually re-aligned with the evolving ITER research plan.
• Initiating and supporting USBPO Task Groups. Focused activities of the USBPO were
 carried out in Task Groups, which were decided by the Directorate and Research
 Committee. Experience has shown that continued engagement in and guidance of, these
 groups by the Director and/or Deputy Director is needed for effectiveness. In particular
 the – at the time - ongoing community planning process presented itself with rich
 opportunity to refresh the setup of these task groups and use them as a vehicle to start
 formation of dedicated ITER research teams. This also included a restructuring as the ITPA
 activity will become more embedded into the ITER research enterprise at the IO. The U.S.
 BPO took a leading role in soliciting and organizing these research items towards ITER.
 Also, the direction of the U.S. FST program towards a cost effective FPP required BPO
 expertise and leadership. This direction has been clearly in line with the BPO mission and
 hence the Deputy Director saw a priority that the BPO is an active component of forming
 action items based on the recommendations that came out of the community process
 and the subsequent FESAC and NASEM reports.
• Enhanced USBPO web presence and expansion of outreach to wider community. The
 USBPO website, while providing access to information needed for operating the
 organization, and useful content for the fusion community, had tended to be quite static,
 and not readily accessible to a visitor interested in learning about, or becoming engaged
 in, US research in burning plasmas. More generally, the leadership of USBPO had recently
 considered ways to more effectively fulfill its goal to Educate and advocate BP science to
 the wider scientific community. A specific focus here was a clear enhancement of the era
 that will start with the privately funded fusion experiments and the ITER commissioning
 at the horizon. The U.S. BPO aimed at being an advocate for this era in the community
 and towards the public and the new Deputy Director supported the MIT based web
 programmer Mark London to further enhance the BPO web presence. Also, an increase
 in the support of outreach activities to scientists beyond fusion specialists had been
 implemented, and to the public. In addition to more accessible web content, activities
 included providing material for speakers in various venues.
• Participation in other USBPO activities. The USBPO Deputy Director participated
 regularly in meetings of the USBPO Council and Executive Committee, and carry out other
 tasks as needed to assist the Director, or act in his stead if he is unavailable. Examples
