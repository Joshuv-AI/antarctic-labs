Stability conditions and infinitesimal deformation of curves
Kotaro Kawatani

Abstract
. Let 𝒳 {\mathcal{X}} be an infinitesimal deformation of a smooth projective curve X 0 X_{0} over a field. We study stability conditions under such deformations and show that the derived push-forward functor associated with the inclusion X 0 → 𝒳 X_{0}\to{\mathcal{X}} induces an isomorphism between the space of stability conditions on 𝒳 {\mathcal{X}} and that on X 0 X_{0} . This yields a direct comparison between the deformed and undeformed settings. As an application, we prove that the autoequivalence group Aut ​ 𝐃 b ​ ( 𝒳 ) {\mathrm{Aut}\,{{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{X}})}} naturally acts on 𝐃 b ​ ( X 0 ) {\mathbf{D}}^{b}(X_{0}) , providing a link between derived symmetries and the deformation structure.



1.
Introduction

Since the 2000s, the study of triangulated categories
has developed rapidly.
One of the key invariants in this area is
the space of stability conditions
{\mathrm{Stab}\,{{\mathbf{D}}}}

on a triangulated category
{\mathbf{D}}
introduced by Bridgeland

[
Bri07
]
.
A notable feature of this invariant is that it carries the structure
of a complex manifold, although it may be empty.

As one might expect, it is very difficult to
give an explicit description of
{\mathrm{Stab}\,{{\mathbf{D}}}}
.
For instance, when
{\mathbf{D}}={\mathbf{D}}^{b}(X)
is the bounded derived
category of coherent sheaves on a scheme
X
,
the space
{\mathrm{Stab}\,{{\mathbf{D}}}}
is well understood only in special cases.
If
X
is a smooth projective curve over a field
{\mathbf{k}}
,
the space
{\mathrm{Stab}\,{{\mathbf{D}}}}
was described by
Okada
[
Oka06
]
(genus
g=0
),
Bridgeland
[
Bri07
]
(
g=1
),
and Macrí
[
Mac07
]
(
g>1
).
The case of
an irreducible singular curve
X
of
arithmetic genus 1 was first studied
by Burban and Krauß
[
BKl06
]
.
More general cases have been studied in
Karube
[
Kar24
]
and Liu
[
Liu25
]
.
If
X
is a Noetherian affine scheme of positive dimension,
then
{\mathrm{Stab}\,{{\mathbf{D}}}}
is empty by
[
KM23
]
.

We note that, in the existing literature,
mainly reduced schemes have been considered.
Therefore, in this paper, we turn our attention
to the non-reduced case, focusing in particular
on infinitesimal deformations.

Before discussing the main case of interest,
we first consider a toy model of infinitesimal deformations.
Namely, let
X
be the
affine scheme
\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits R
of
an Artinian local
{\mathbf{k}}
-algebra
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
with the residue field
{\mathbf{k}}
.
We note that
\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits R
is an infinitesimal deformation
of the point
\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits{\mathbf{k}}
.

Proposition 1.1

(=Proposition
3.10
)
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be an Artinian local ring with the residue field

{\mathbf{k}}=R/{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Then the space

{\mathrm{Stab}\,{{\mathbf{D}}^{b}(\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits R)}}
is non-empty and isomorphic to

{\mathrm{Stab}\,{{\mathbf{D}}^{b}(\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits{\mathbf{k}})}}
.

Proposition
1.1
suggests
that the space of stability conditions
is invariant under infinitesimal deformations of a point.
In view of this, the case of curves is
a natural next step to consider.
Motivated by this observation, we state our main result as follows.

Theorem 1.2

(=Theorems
4.14
and
5.18
)
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be an Artinian local
{\mathbf{k}}
-algebra with
residue field
{\mathbf{k}}
and
let
\pi\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits R
be an
infinitesimal deformation of a smooth projective curve
X_{0}

with the closed embedding

j\colon X_{0}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{X}}
.
Then the derived functor
j_{*}
induces an isomorphism

j_{*}^{-1}\colon{\mathrm{Stab}\,{{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{X}})}}\cong{\mathrm{Stab}\,{{\mathbf{D}}^{b}(X_{0})}}.

In particular, the theorem above indicates that
the existence of stability conditions
depends only on the underlying reduced scheme and is insensitive to
infinitesimal structure.
It is natural to ask whether Theorem
1.2

admits a generalization to singular curves and
to smooth projective surfaces.
Our analysis depends on the fact
that the homological dimension of
{\mathbf{D}}^{b}(X_{0})
is
1
.
Therefore, it seems that new ideas are required in
order to achieve such a generalization.

The proof relies on a detailed analysis
of the property of objects which could be stable
for some stability conditions on

{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{X}})
.
The key step is to show
that stable objects in
{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{X}})
lie in the image of the
functor
j_{*}\colon{\mathbf{D}}^{b}(X_{0})\to{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{X}})
.
To this end, we focus on a homological property
of the inclusion
{\mathrm{Coh}}(X_{0})\subset{\mathrm{Coh}}({\mathcal{X}})

on the category of coherent sheaves on
X_{0}
and on
{\mathcal{X}}
.
We call such a property “almost hereditary”
(see Definition
2.1
).
Although it is nontrivial that the inclusion

{\mathrm{Coh}}(X_{0})\subset{\mathrm{Coh}}({\mathcal{X}})
satisfies the property,
we show that this indeed holds
by using deformation theory of locally free sheaves on curves
(see Proposition
4.4
).

Recall that
{\mathrm{Stab}\,{{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{P}}^{1})}}

admits non-geometric stability conditions.
Because of this phenomenon, the proof of Theorem
1.2

depends on whether the genus satisfies
g=0
(cf. Theorem
5.18
)
or
g>0
(cf. Theorem
4.14
).

Recall that the autoequivalence group

{\mathrm{Aut}\,{{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{X}})}}
acts on
{\mathrm{Stab}\,{{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{X}})}}
.
By Theorem
1.2
,
we obtain an induced action of
{\mathrm{Aut}\,{{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{X}})}}
on

{\mathrm{Stab}\,{{\mathbf{D}}^{b}(X_{0})}}
.
It is therefore natural to expect
that the group

{\mathrm{Aut}\,{{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{X}})}}
also acts on
{\mathbf{D}}^{b}(X_{0})
.
In fact we show the following applying a part of Theorem
1.2
.

Proposition 1.3
.

We keep the notation of Theorem
1.2
.
Then there exists a group homomorphism

{\mathrm{Aut}\,{{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{X}})}}\to{\mathrm{Aut}\,{{\mathbf{D}}^{b}(X_{0})}}.

The proof is divided into three cases according to the genus:

g=0
,
g=1
, and
g>1
.
In the case
g>0
, we use Theorem
1.2
.

Remark 1.4
.

In the preprint
[
Kaw20
]
, the author showed that
{\mathrm{Stab}\,{{\mathbf{D}}^{b}(\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits R)}}
is non-empty if and only if
\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits R
has dimension zero.
In
[
KM23
]
, we provided a generalization of the
“if” direction to the relative setting.
Proposition
1.1
was part of
the main results of
[
Kaw20
]
.
Although
[
KM23
]
was posted on arXiv later,
it was published earlier.
For this reason, we have substantially revised the preprint
and present it here as the present paper;
accordingly, we have decided to
withdraw
[
Kaw20
]
from arXiv.

2.
Almost hereditary subcategory

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an abelian category.
A full abelian subcategory
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
is
said to be
almost hereditary
in
{\mathcal{A}}
if it satisfies the following
condition.

(AH)

For any objects
E,F\in{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
,
if
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}^{0}(E,F)=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}^{1}(E,F)=0
then
the vanishing
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}^{p}(E,F)=0
holds for all
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The terminology almost hereditary reflects the idea that,
although
{\mathcal{A}}
does not have global dimension
1
,
it behaves as if it did when we restrict attention
to the subcategory
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
.
We introduce two trivial examples.

Example 2.2
.

Suppose that the global dimension of an abelian category

{\mathcal{A}}
is
1
.
Then
{\mathcal{A}}
itself is almost hereditary in
{\mathcal{A}}
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be the category of
finitely generated modules
over an Artinian local ring
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
and
let
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
be the full subcategory of

R/{\mathfrak{m}}
modules.
Then
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
is almost hereditary in
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
as follows.
If objects
E
and
F
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
satisfy
the assumption in (AH),
then, recalling that they are
{\mathbf{k}}
-vector spaces,
either
E
or
F
is zero and hence the conclusion follows.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
be an abelian full subcategory of an abelian category
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Set a full subcategory
{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}

of
{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
by

{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}:=\{E\in{\mathbf{D}}^{b}(A)\mid H^{i}({\mathcal{A}})\in{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\forall i\in{\mathbb{Z}})\}.

\forall i\in{\mathbb{Z}},\forall p\in{\mathbb{Z}}

Recall the spectral sequence

m_{2}=\min\{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\mid H^{j}(F)\neq 0\}

If

Thus it is enough to prove the assertion for
m_{1}\geq m_{2}
and

m_{1}-m_{2}

Suppose
m_{1}=m_{2}
.
Then the first assertion is obvious since

H^{i}(E)
or
H^{i-n}(F)
is zero.
Since

the equality

E_{2}^{0,0}=E_{\infty}^{0,0}=E^{0}=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(E,F)=0

\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits^{0}(H^{i}(E),H^{i}(F))=0
.

Suppose that the assertions hold for

.
By the equlaity

\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits^{p}(E,F)=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits^{p+1}(E,F[-1])

the pair

(2.2)

\begin{cases}\forall p\in{\mathbb{Z}},\forall n\geq 2,\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits^{p}(H^{i}(E),H^{i-n}(F))=0\\
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits^{0}(H^{i}(E),H^{i-1}(F))=0.\end{cases}

Since each cohomology are in
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}

{\mathcal{A}}
,
it is enough to prove

\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits^{1}(H^{i}(E),H^{i-1}(F))=0
and

\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits^{0}(H^{i}(E),H^{i}(F))=0
.

For the spectral sequence (
2.1
),
the vanishings (
2.2
) imply

E_{\infty}^{1,-1}=E_{2}^{1,-1}

Then the short exact sequence

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{\infty}^{1,-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E^{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits^{p}(H^{i}(E),H^{i-1}(F))=0

Then we see

This gives the proof.
∎

3.
Preliminaries on stability conditions

We first give a quick review of stability conditions on a
tirangulated category.

3.1.
Review of stability conditions

Let
{\mathbf{D}}
be a triangulated category.
Following the original article
[
Bri07
]
,
we briefly recall stability conditions on
a triangulated category
{\mathbf{D}}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A stability condition on
{\mathbf{D}}
consists
of a pair
\sigma=(Z,{\mathcal{P}})

where

is a group homomorphism from
the Grothendieck group of
{\mathbf{D}}
to
{\mathbb{C}}

and

is the collection of full sub-additive

satisfying the following:

(s1)

(s2)

if

for any

and

,

(s3)

{\mathcal{P}}(\phi+1)={\mathcal{P}}(\phi)[1]

(s4)

any object

has a sequence of exact triangles in
{\mathbf{D}}

(3.1)

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{n-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{n}=E\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A_{n-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

of the morphism

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

An object

and

Moreover the object

.
The full subcategory of stable objects in

is

(2)

Given an interval

the extension closure

.
A stability condition

is locally finite if, for any

, there exists

(3)

The set of locally finite stability conditions on

.

(4)

If a stability condition is locally finite,

Thus the slicing
{\mathcal{P}}(\phi)
is generated by

.

(5)

By the definition,
a stability condition on a triangulated

satisfying the above 4 conditions.

When the pair

satisfies (s1), (s2), and (s3),
if the filtration of an object

Lemma 3.3

Assume that the pair
(Z,{\mathcal{P}})

is the collection of
full sub categories.
If

Let

Put by

Before the introduction of a key lemma,
we quickly review of a linear structure of
an abelian category.
If an abelian category
{\mathcal{A}}
is
linear over a commutative ring
K
,
there exists a multiplication map

\mu\colon K\to{\mathrm{End}}(E)

for any object
E\in{\mathcal{A}}
.
Here we say that an object
E\in{\mathcal{C}}
is

annihilated by an ideal
I
of

if

I
maps to zero via the map
\mu
.
If any object in a full subcategory
{\mathcal{B}}
of
{\mathcal{A}}

is annihilated by an ideal

,
then we call the category
{\mathcal{B}}
to be annihilated by
I
.
If the category
{\mathcal{B}}
is maximal
among the category annihilated by
I
,
we call
{\mathcal{B}}
maximally annihilated by
I
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an
K
-linear abelian category where

is a commutative ring.
Set a full subcategory
{\mathcal{A}}_{I}
by

{\mathcal{A}}_{I}:=\{E\in{\mathcal{A}}\mid E\text{ is annihilated by }I\}

Then

{\mathcal{A}}_{I}
is an abelian subcategory and is maximally annihilated.

Proof.

The maximality is obvious.
To be annihilated by
I
is closed under
subobjects, quotients, and direct sums.
Hence
{\mathcal{A}}_{I}
is an abelian subcategory.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an abelian category
which is linear over
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
.
Assume that
a full abelian subcategory of
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}
of

For any

,
the endomorphism

of

is nilpotent.
Since

is stable, any non-zero endomorphism

the morphism

must be zero and each cohomology of

is in

{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}
.

Thus

is in
{\mathbf{D}}^{b}(A)_{{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}}
.

is in

up to shifts.
The indecomposability follows from

[
Kaw22
, Lemma 3.3]
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
{\mathbf{D}}^{b}(R)
be the
bounded derived category of finitely generated modules over
an Artinian local ring
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
.
For any

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be the abelian category of

Then the subcategory

is the

of finitely generated

Thus a stable object must be in

Now any object in

is isomorphic to

.
Hence

∎

3.2.
Inducing stability conditions

An exact functor
F\colon{\mathbf{D}}\to{\mathbf{D}}^{\prime}
between triangulated
categories does not, in general, induce a map between the spaces of
stability conditions.
However, a “good” functor
F\colon{\mathbf{D}}\to{\mathbf{D}}^{\prime}
induces a map,
denoted by
F^{-1}
, from a subset of
{\mathrm{Stab}\,{(}}{\mathbf{D}}^{\prime})
to
{\mathrm{Stab}\,{(}}{\mathbf{D}})
,
as shown by Macrí–Mehrotra–Stellari
[
MMS09
]
.
Let us briefly recall the construction of
F^{-1}
.

Let
F\colon{\mathbf{D}}\to{\mathbf{D}}^{\prime}
be an exact functor
between triangulated categories.
Assume that
F
satisfies the following condition:

(Ind)

\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathbf{D}}^{\prime}}(F(a),F(b))=0
implies
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathbf{D}}}(a,b)=0
for any
a,b\in{\mathbf{D}}
.

Let
\sigma^{\prime}=(Z^{\prime},{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime})\in{\mathrm{Stab}\,{{\mathbf{D}}^{\prime}}}
.
Define
F^{-1}\sigma^{\prime}
by the pair
(Z,{\mathcal{P}})
where

(3.2)

Z=Z^{\prime}\circ F,\ {\mathcal{P}}(\phi)=\{x\in{\mathbf{D}}\mid F(x)\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}(\phi)\}.

By the definition of
F^{-1}\sigma^{\prime}
, the pair
F^{-1}\sigma^{\prime}
is a stability condition on
{\mathbf{D}}
if and only if
F^{-1}\sigma^{\prime}
has the Harder-Narasimhan property.

Lemma 3.7

(
[
MMS09
, Lemma 2.9]
)
.

Let
F\colon{\mathbf{D}}\to{\mathbf{D}}^{\prime}
be an exact functor
between triangulated categories satisfying the
condition (Ind).
The map
F^{-1}\colon{\mathrm{Dom}({F}^{-1})}\to{\mathrm{Stab}\,{{\mathbf{D}}}}
is continuous.

Remark 3.8
.

Recall that the universal cover
\widetilde{{\mathrm{GL}}}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{R}})
of

{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{R}})
has the right action to the space of stability conditions.
The map
F^{-1}
is
\widetilde{{\mathrm{GL}}}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{R}})
-equivariant by the definition of
F^{-1}
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be abelian and linear over

(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
.
Assume that
a full sub-abelian category of
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
of
{\mathcal{A}}

is almost hereditary and maximally annihilated by
{\mathfrak{m}}
.
If the natural functor

j_{*}\colon{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})\to{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})

satisfies the condition (Ind),
then
the natural map

j_{*}^{-1}\colon{\mathrm{Dom}({j_{*}}^{-1})}\to{\mathrm{Stab}\,{{\mathbf{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})}}

is injective.

Proof.

If
{\mathrm{Dom}({j_{*}}^{-1})}
is empty,
then nothing to prove.
Hence assume
{\mathrm{Dom}({j_{*}}^{-1})}\neq\emptyset
.

Take
\sigma=(Z,{\mathcal{P}})
and

in
{\mathrm{Dom}({j_{*}}^{-1})}
so that

j_{*}^{-1}\sigma=j_{*}^{-1}\tau
.
By the definition of the map

,
the slicing

of

is
just
{\mathcal{P}}(\phi)\cap\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits j_{*}

On the other hand,

Thus

if

.
Since

generates

we have

Thus an object

is
\sigma

-stable.
If

the value of central charge of any stable object coincides.
Since the class of stable objects generates

Theorem 3.11

Collins and Polishchuck

Remark 3.14

4.

We first give an example of almost hereditary subcategory

In this section,
