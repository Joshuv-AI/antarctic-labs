Self-consistent evaluation of the Berry connection for Wannier functions Preprint: APS/123-QED
Martin Thümmler, Thomas Lettau, Alexander Croy, Ulf Peschel, Stefanie Gräfe

Abstract
The Berry connection is a gauge-dependent quantity frequently used to describe the optical response of solids. Its evaluation requires a 𝐤 \mathbf{k} -derivative with respect to the cell periodic-part of the Bloch-functions and is commonly calculated in the Wannier basis by using overlap matrices of cell-periodic parts of Bloch-functions at neighboring 𝐤 \mathbf{k} -points. So far, all proposed interpolation schemes for the Berry connection do not account for the matrix structure of the overlap matrices explicitly but treat the matrix elements as independent, or only distinguish between diagonal and off-diagonal entries. In this work, we propose a self-consistent interpolation scheme based on the matrix logarithm resulting in a strongly improved accuracy. Furthermore, we discuss how the basis set incompleteness of the bands used in the ab-initio calculation imposes constraints on the accuracy. We quantify the basis incompleteness based on the singular values of the overlap matrices and relate it to the invariant part of the spread functional Ω I \Omega_{\mathrm{I}} of the Wannier functions. Numerical calculations for monolayer MoS 2 \mathrm{MoS}_{2} and bulk Si \mathrm{Si} demonstrate that the proposed interpolation scheme is much less sensitive to the Wannierization details and leads to an improved quality of the velocity matrix and the optical conductivity.

Introduction

Wannier functions
Wannier 1937
are at the core of many ab-initio methods for solids, ranging from the description of polarization or magnetization
Noel et al. 2001
;
Thonhauser et al. 2005
;
Lopez et al. 2012
;
Coh and Vanderbilt 2009
, over topological effects
Wang et al. 2006
;
Soluyanov and Vanderbilt 2011
;
Gresch et al. 2017
, to electron-phonon coupling
Giustino et al. 2007
;
Lee et al. 2023
.
Their localization in real space
Kohn 1959
;
Monaco et al. 2018
allows for an efficient interpolation of operator matrix elements from a coarse-grained ab-initio Monkhorst-Pack grid
Monkhorst and Pack 1976
to a dense grid using discrete Fourier transforms
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
;
Pizzi et al. 2020
;
Tsirkin 2021
.
This renders the numerical convergence with respect to the Brillouin zone (BZ) sampling feasible for many physical quantities
Marzari et al. 2012
;
Tsirkin 2021
.
The most commonly applied method for Wannierization results in maximally localized Wannier functions (MLWF)
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
;
Marzari et al. 2012
which is often combined with a band disentanglement
Souza et al. 2001
, i.e., the smoothest possible reduction of the bands to the desired number of Wannier functions covering the energy range of interest.
Additional methods were developed to reliably find well localized Wannier functions
Damle et al. 2017
;
Damle et al. 2019
or to enforce symmetry constraints
Sakuma 2013
;
Koepernik et al. 2023
;
Koretsune 2023
.
The convergence of the Fourier interpolated matrix elements with respect to the ab-initio grid size was extensively studied for
\mathbf{k}
-local operators, e.g. for the Hamiltonian to obtain the band structure
Pizzi et al. 2020
.

In this study, we consider and improve the accuracy of the non-local Berry connection, which recently attracted increasing interest
Lihm et al. 2026
;
Cole and Vanderbilt 2026
due to its widespread use in computational material science.
Its matrix elements are also referred to as position or dipole matrix elements
Blount 1962
;
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
and are used in many applications
Xiao et al. 2010
, such as describing the polarization
Noel et al. 2001
;
Spaldin 2012
or optical responses
Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
;
Yates et al. 2007
;
Silva et al. 2019
;
Urru et al. 2025
.
The Berry connection between different bands,
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}_{mn}=\mathrm{i}\langle u^{\mathrm{}}_{m\mathbf{k}}\rvert\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}\lvert u^{\mathrm{}}_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle
, is numerically computed from the overlap matrices
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}_{mn}=\langle u_{m\mathbf{k}}|u_{n{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{b}}}\rangle
of the cell-periodic part of the Bloch functions with crystal momenta
\mathbf{k}
and
\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{b}
of the coarse ab-initio grid
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
.
In the initially proposed interpolation scheme
Wang et al. 2006
, the Berry connection is not Hermitian, which leads to nonphysical results, e.g. during the propagation of the semiconductor Bloch equations
Silva et al. 2019
.
The translational invariance of the Wannier functions with respect to lattice vector shifts, manifests in the Berry connection as well
Blount 1962
;
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
.
It is respected for the diagonal matrix elements in the work of Marzari and Vanderbilt
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
, but not in the first work that Fourier-interpolates the Berry connection
Wang et al. 2006
.
Recently, Lihm developed a Hermitian scheme
Lihm et al. 2026
that enforces the translational invariance also for the off-diagonal matrix elements to eliminate any spurious dependence of physical properties on the choice of the origin within the unit cell, e.g. for the optical conductivity
Lihm et al. 2026
.
However, it is important not only to consider the origin shifts, but to ensure the correct transformation behavior of the Berry connection in general
Blount 1962
.
An example is the consistency between different Wannierizations, like the MLWF and the separate Wannierization of the the conduction and valence bands (CB-VB)
Souza et al. 2001
;
Thümmler et al. 2026
.
Even recently proposed higher-order approximation schemes of the
\mathbf{k}
-space derivative
Lihm et al. 2026
provide no fundamental solution for that.

To address the aforementioned issues, we propose an interpolation scheme of the Berry connection
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}
that unifies the treatment of the diagonal and off-diagonal elements by accounting for the intrinsic matrix structure of the overlap matrices
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
.
We identify
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
as the path-ordered matrix exponential of the Berry connection
Wilczek and Zee 1984
, which mixes all the matrix elements with each other.
This leads to an inverse problem, which we solve in two steps.
First, we take the matrix logarithm of the overlap matrices to compute an initial estimate for the Berry connection.
Second, we evaluate the path-ordered integrals based on the Fourier-interpolated estimate by employing a Magnus expansion.
The second step is repeated until self-consistency is reached.
Thus, it converges much faster to the Berry connection with respect to the ab-initio grid size.
We note that the inherent incompleteness of the Wannier basis limits all interpolation schemes and will inevitable set a bound for the accuracy.
To asses its impact, we provide limits for the coupling of the Berry connection into the non-described part of the system based on the singular values of the overlap matrices
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
and discuss its close connection to the invariant part of the spread-functional
\Omega_{\mathrm{I}}
introduced in Refs.
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
;
Souza et al. 2001
.

We assess the quality of the interpolation schemes based on the agreement between the ab-initio computed and the corresponding interpolated velocity operator.
The ab-initio velocity operator is an accurate reference as it is local in
\mathbf{k}
and can thus be interpolated as seamlessly as the Hamilton operator.
This allows for a momentum-resolved error estimation as well as a direct comparison of the different interpolation schemes without relying on convergence details of the Wannierization between different grid sizes.
This quality assessment is more systematic and transferable compared to approaches that consider the convergence of derived properties
Stengel and Spaldin 2006
;
Cole and Vanderbilt 2026
. In our numerical examples for monolayer
\mathrm{MoS_{2}}
and bulk
\mathrm{Si}
, we demonstrate an improved accuracy of the velocity operator for our self-consistent logarithmic scheme.
Additionally, we show that optical conductivities computed based on the self-consistent logarithmic scheme are much more accurate and less sensitive to the Wannierization details compared to previous schemes, e.g. reductions of the relative deviations of the maximal optical conductivity from 26% to less than 0.3% for
\mathrm{MoS}_{2}
on a
8\times 8\times 1
ab-initio grid were achieved.

Our study is structured as follows:
Section
II
provides the theoretical background and introduces the the two predominantly used interpolation schemes for the Berry connection developed by Marzari and Vanderbilt, and Lihm, respectively.
We discuss these schemes in greater detail in Sec.
III
and introduce our (self-consistent) logarithmic interpolation scheme.
Afterwards, in Sec.
IV
, we quantify the the scheme-dependent error of the velocity operator, the basis incompleteness, and discuss their impact on computed optical conductivities for
\mathrm{MoS}_{2}
and
\mathrm{Si}
.
Finally, we conclude in Sec.
V
.

II
Theoretical background

In this section, we provide the theoretical background for the Wannier interpolation and review the existing interpolation schemes for the Berry connection.
We use atomic units throughout this manuscript if not otherwise indicated.

II.1
Wannier interpolation

We start with the Bloch functions
\lvert\Psi^{\mathrm{H}}_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle
, the eigenfunctions of the cell-periodic Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
, where
n
is the band index and
\mathbf{k}
the crystal momentum.
To improve readability, we do not consider spin explicitly, however, all presented derivations can be straight-forwardly generalized to include it.
The cell-periodic part of the Bloch-functions is
\lvert u^{\mathrm{H}}_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\hat{\mathbf{r}}}\lvert\Psi^{\mathrm{H}}_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle
, where
\hat{\mathbf{r}}
denotes the position operator.
The
\lvert u^{\mathrm{H}}_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle
are generally not smooth in
\mathbf{k}
, which hinders their interpolation to and the calculation of their derivative.
On the other hand, Wannier-functions
\lvert\Psi^{\mathrm{}}_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle
feature smooth cell-periodic parts
\lvert u^{\mathrm{}}_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\hat{\mathbf{r}}}\lvert\Psi^{\mathrm{}}_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle
due to their localization in the supercell
Marzari et al. 2012
;
Kohn 1959
;
Monaco et al. 2018
.
They are obtained from the Bloch-functions by applying semi-unitary transformation matrices
U^{\mathbf{k}}
as

\displaystyle\lvert\Psi^{\mathrm{}}_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle=\sum\limits_{m}U^{\mathbf{k}}_{mn}\lvert\Psi^{\mathrm{H}}_{m\mathbf{k}}\rangle,

(1)

which are optimized to minimize a spread functional
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
;
Souza et al. 2001
.
The
n
th Wannier function (out of
n_{\mathrm{W}}
) in real space located at unit cell
\mathbf{R}
is given by a Fourier transform

\displaystyle\lvert\mathbf{R}n\rangle=\frac{1}{N_{\mathbf{k}}}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\mathbf{R}}\lvert\Psi^{\mathrm{}}_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle,

(2)

where the
\mathbf{k}
-sum is taken over the
N_{\mathbf{k}}
ab-initio grid points.
The Wannier functions allow for the interpolation of an arbitrary operator
\hat{O}
in
\mathbf{k}
-space, provided that it is local in
\mathbf{k}
.
For that purpose, the Fourier transform of the matrix elements
O^{\mathbf{k}}_{mn}=\langle\Psi_{m\mathbf{k}}\rvert\hat{O}\lvert\Psi_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle
is calculated via

\displaystyle O^{\mathbf{R}}_{mn}\equiv\langle\mathbf{R}m\rvert\hat{O}\lvert\mathbf{0}n\rangle=\frac{1}{N_{\mathbf{k}}}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}}O^{\mathbf{k}}_{mn}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\mathbf{R}}.

(3)

The interpolation to an arbitrary
\mathbf{q}
-point is done employing the inverse transform as

\displaystyle O^{\mathbf{q}}_{mn}\equiv\langle\Psi_{m\mathbf{q}}\rvert\hat{O}\lvert\Psi_{n\mathbf{q}}\rangle=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{R}}\langle\mathbf{R}m\rvert\hat{O}\lvert\mathbf{0}n\rangle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{q}\mathbf{R}},

(4)

where the sum is taken over all the lattice vectors
\mathbf{R}
inside the Wigner-Seitz supercell
Pizzi et al. 2020
.
As we are interested in the interpolated Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
, we explicitly write the definition of its matrix elements in momentum space as

\displaystyle H^{\mathbf{k}}_{mn}

\displaystyle=\langle\Psi^{\mathrm{}}_{m\mathbf{k}}\rvert\hat{H}\lvert\Psi^{\mathrm{}}_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle\;.

(5)

The corresponding matrix elements
H^{\mathbf{R}}_{mn}
are defined via Eq. (
3
).
Employing Eq. (
4
), the analytical derivative of the Hamiltonian matrix elements reads

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}H^{\mathbf{k}}_{mn}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{R}H^{\mathbf{R}}_{mn}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\mathbf{R}}\;.

(6)

II.2
Definition and properties of the Berry connection

The Berry connection
Blount 1962
;
Zak 1989
for a crystal momentum
\mathbf{k}
is most often defined for a single band.
In this manuscript, we denote the matrix elements between two states
m
and
n
, defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}_{mn}=\mathrm{i}\langle u^{\mathrm{}}_{m\mathbf{k}}\rvert\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}\lvert u^{\mathrm{}}_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle,

(7)

as the Berry connection.
It is connected to the position operator via
Blount 1962

\displaystyle\langle\Psi^{\mathrm{}}_{m\mathbf{k}}\rvert\hat{\mathbf{r}}\lvert\Psi^{\mathrm{}}_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle=\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}_{mn}+\mathrm{i}\delta_{mn}\nabla_{\mathbf{k}},

(8)

where
\delta_{mn}
is the Kronecker delta symbol.
The Berry connection is related to the velocity operator via the commutator relation
\hat{\mathbf{v}}=\mathrm{i}[\hat{H},\hat{\mathbf{r}}]
as
Esteve-Paredes and Palacios 2023

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{k}}=\mathrm{i}[H^{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}]+\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}H^{\mathbf{k}}.

(9)

We omitted the matrix indices in the above equation and will do so whenever convenient.
The Berry connection is Hermitian, i.e.
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}=(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}})^{\dagger}
, which follows directly from the orthogonality of the cell-periodic part of the Wannier functions,

\displaystyle 0=\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}\langle u^{\mathrm{}}_{m\mathbf{k}}|{u^{\mathrm{}}_{n\mathbf{k}}}\rangle=\mathrm{i}\left[(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}_{nm})^{*}-\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}_{mn}\right].

(10)

Its Fourier transform

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{R}}=\frac{1}{N_{\mathbf{k}}}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}}\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\mathbf{R}}\;,

(11)

is evaluated as

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{R}}_{mn}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{R}m\rvert\hat{\mathbf{r}}\lvert\mathbf{0}n\rangle.

(12)

The
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{R}}_{mn}
are named position operator matrix elements as well
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
.
The Berry connection is gauge dependent, i.e., a
\mathbf{k}
-dependent unitary basis transformation
U^{\mathbf{k}}
of the cell-periodic part of the Wannier functions

\displaystyle\lvert u^{\mathrm{}}_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle\rightarrow\sum\limits_{m}U^{\mathbf{k}}_{mn}\lvert u^{\mathrm{}}_{m\mathbf{k}}\rangle

(13)

leads to
Blount 1962

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}\rightarrow U^{\mathbf{k}\dagger}\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}U^{\mathbf{k}}+\mathrm{i}U^{\mathbf{k}\dagger}\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}U^{\mathbf{k}}.

(14)

Important special cases of these gauge transformations are permutations of the band indices or shifts of the Wannier functions via

\displaystyle U^{\mathbf{k}}_{nm}=\delta_{mn}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\mathcal{R}_{n}}

(15)

with shift vectors
\mathcal{R}_{n}
.
If all the
\mathcal{R}_{n}
are lattice vectors, Eq. (
14
) reduces to the translational invariance condition:
The physics must be independent of the choice of the origin and the unit cell to which the Wannier functions are localized.
This condition is for instance accounted for in the in the definition of the Wannier function spread, see Eqs. (31), (32), and (36) in Ref.
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
.

II.3
Review of existing interpolation schemes for the Berry connection

In this section, we review the commonly used schemes to obtain and interpolate the Berry connection from ab-initio calculated Wannier functions.
In all the interpolation schemes, the following two steps are performed:
First, the
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}
(or equivalently the
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{R}}
) matrices are calculated on the ab-initio
\mathbf{k}
-grid (of the supercell) and
second, the interpolation to an arbitrary
\mathbf{q}
-point is done by employing Eq. (
4
), which relies on the smoothness of the
\lvert u^{\mathrm{}}_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle
in momentum space
Wang et al. 2006
.
The interpolation schemes differ only in the calculation of the
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}
(
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{R}}
) matrices from the overlap matrices

\displaystyle M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}_{mn}=\langle u_{m\mathbf{k}}|u_{n{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{b}}}\rangle,

(16)

where
\mathbf{k}
and
\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{b}
are on the ab-initio grid.
For the real space discussion, we define the Fourier-transform of the overlap matrices as

\displaystyle M^{\mathbf{R}\mathbf{b}}=\frac{1}{N_{\mathbf{k}}}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\mathbf{R}}

(17)

and obtain
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997

\displaystyle M^{\mathbf{R}\mathbf{b}}_{mn}=\langle\mathbf{R}m\rvert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{b}\hat{\mathbf{r}}}\lvert\mathbf{0}n\rangle,

(18)

which follows from the definition of the Wannier functions, see Eq. (
2
).
In the following, we consider all the equations for supercell sizes approaching infinity, which enables us treat the
\mathbf{k}
’s as continuous variables.
The Berry connection, see Eq. (
7
), is then given in the limit
\mathbf{b}\rightarrow\mathbf{0}
by

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}=\mathrm{i}\nabla_{\mathbf{b}}M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}.

(19)

Combining Eqs. (
18
) and (
12
) leads for the position operator matrix elements to

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{R}}=\mathrm{i}\nabla_{\mathbf{b}}M^{\mathbf{R}\mathbf{b}}.

(20)

On the ab-initio grid, we compute the gradient of a
\mathbf{k}
-dependent function
f
by employing finite differences

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}f(\mathbf{k})=\sum_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{b}f(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{b})+\mathcal{O}(|\mathbf{b}|^{2})

(21)

with the Euclidean norm
|\cdot|
.
The weights
w_{\mathbf{b}}
are chosen such that they obey the condition

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{b}}b_{\alpha}b_{\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\beta},

(22)

and are symmetric

\displaystyle w_{\mathbf{b}}=w_{-\mathbf{b}}\text{ and }w_{\mathbf{0}}=0\;.

(23)

Here,
b_{\alpha}
and
b_{\beta}
are the real space components of
\mathbf{b}
in
\alpha
and
\beta
direction, respectively
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
.
The symmetry condition on the weights in Eq. (
23
) leads to quadratic error scaling in Eq. (
21
).
Even if the condition in Eq. (
23
) is not strictly necessary, it is still satisfied in Ref.
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
;
Marzari et al. 2012
and will later help to enforce the Hermiticity of the interpolation schemes.
Applying Eq. (
21
) to the off-diagonal elements of
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}

Wang et al. 2006

leads to

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{MV},\mathbf{k}}_{mn}=\mathrm{i}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{b}M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}_{mn},

(24)

where we introduced the superscript MV denoting Marzari and Vanderbilt.
However, the diagonal elements must respect the translational invariance condition, see Eqs. (
14
) and (
15
).
This is ensured by employing the expansion
\ln(1+x)=x+\mathcal{O}(x^{2})
for the diagonal elements, leading to

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{MV},\mathbf{k}}_{nn}=-\mathop{Im}\nolimits\sum\limits_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{b}\ln M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}_{nn}.

(25)

The Fourier transform of the Berry connection, see Eq. (
12
), allows to define the Wannier centers as

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{n}\equiv\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{0}}_{nn}

(26)

yielding

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}^{\mathrm{MV}}_{n}=-\frac{1}{N_{\mathbf{k}}}\mathop{Im}\nolimits\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{b}\ln M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}_{nn}.

(27)

Lihm proposed a Hermitian scheme for the off-diagonal elements of the Berry connection
Lihm et al. 2026
, by enforcing the translational invariance of the off-diagonal position operator matrix elements.
Its implementation is provided in
Tsirkin 2021
.
Within this approach, one evaluates Eq. (
20
) by a Taylor expansion of the exponential phase factor in
\langle\mathbf{R}m\rvert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{b}\hat{\mathbf{r}}}\lvert\mathbf{0}n\rangle
with respect to
\mathbf{b}
around the midpoint of the two Wannier function centers.
This reduces the expansion error of the position operator in the real-space regions of the supercell where the Wannier functions overlap most.
The scheme reads for
m\neq n
or
\mathbf{R}\neq\mathbf{0}

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{Lihm},\mathbf{R}}_{mn}=\mathrm{i}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{b}M^{\mathbf{R}\mathbf{b}}_{mn}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2}(\mathbf{r}_{n}+\mathbf{r}_{m}-\mathbf{R})}.

(28)

The diagonal elements
\mathbf{r}_{n}^{\mathrm{Lihm}}=\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{R}=0}_{nn}
are obtained from an expression of the Wannier centers that respects their transformation behavior under origin shifts such as Eq. (
27
) or similar formulations
Resta 1998
;
Stengel and Spaldin 2006
.
Transforming Eq. (
28
) into momentum space by accounting for the Wannier centers yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{Lihm},\mathbf{k}}_{mn}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{b}M^{\mathbf{k}-\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2},\mathbf{b}}_{mn}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2}(\mathbf{r}_{n}+\mathbf{r}_{m})}

\displaystyle\,+\delta_{mn}\left(\mathbf{r}_{n}-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{N_{\mathbf{k}}}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{b}M^{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}_{nn}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{b}\mathbf{r}_{n}}\right),

(29)

where
M^{\mathbf{k}-\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2},\mathbf{b}}_{mn}
indicate the Fourier-interpolated overlap matrix elements.

III
Self-consistent interpolation scheme

In this section, we evaluate the existing interpolation schemes for the Berry connection and develop our self-consistent logarithmic scheme step by step.
We show first that the MV-scheme is not Hermitian and propose a symmetric (sym) scheme as remedy.
Next, we discuss the close connection of the Lihm-scheme to the sym-scheme.
Afterwards, we present the logarithmic (log) scheme by accounting for the matrix structure of the overlap matrix properly and introduce the self-consistent logarithmic (sclog) scheme by extending the log-scheme from a local to a global interpolation scheme.
Lastly, we discuss the errors introduced by assuming the basis set completeness required to justify the (sc)log-scheme.

III.1
Hermiticity enforcement

Testing the MV interpolation scheme, see Eq. (
24
), for its Hermiticity yields (
n\neq m
)

\displaystyle(\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{MV},\mathbf{k},\dagger})_{mn}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{b}M^{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{b},-\mathbf{b}}_{mn}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{b}M^{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{b},\mathbf{b}}_{mn}\;,

(30)

where we used
(M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}})^{\dagger}=M^{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{b},-\mathbf{b}}
and Eq. (
23
).
As the
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
matrices change throughout the BZ, the Hermiticity is generally not given, compare to Eq. (
24
).
However, by using the the Fourier-interpolated
M^{\mathbf{k}-\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2},\mathbf{b}}
matrices instead, which obey

\displaystyle(M^{\mathbf{k}-\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2},\mathbf{b}})^{\dagger}=M^{\mathbf{k}+\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2},-\mathbf{b}},

(31)

see Appendix
A
, the Hermiticity is restored.
Thus, we propose to calculate the off-diagonal elements of the Berry connection (
n\neq m
) as

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{sym},\mathbf{k}}_{mn}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{b}M^{\mathbf{k}-\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2},\mathbf{b}}_{mn}.

(32)

The Hermiticity of
\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{sym},\mathbf{k}}
is confirmed by the same calculation as in Eq. (
III.1
).
We now show, that this symmetric interpolation scheme is consistent, i.e., it converges to the Berry connection for
\mathbf{b}\rightarrow\mathbf{0}
, by evaluating the Taylor expansion of
M^{\mathbf{k}-\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2},\mathbf{b}}
around
\mathbf{b}
as

\displaystyle M^{\mathbf{k}-\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2},\mathbf{b}}_{mn}

\displaystyle=\langle u_{m\mathbf{k}-\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2}}|u_{n\mathbf{k}+\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta_{mn}+\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2}\left[\langle u_{m\mathbf{k}}|\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}u_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle-\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}u_{m\mathbf{k}}|u_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}(|\mathbf{b}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\delta_{mn}-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{b}\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}_{mn}+\mathcal{O}(|\mathbf{b}|^{2}),

(33)

where we used Eq. (
10
) in the last line.
Interestingly,
\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{sym},\mathbf{k}}
is accurate to second order in
\mathbf{b}
, see the discussion after Eq. (
23
).
For the diagonal elements, we employ the MV formula, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{sym},\mathbf{k}}_{nn}\equiv\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{MV},\mathbf{k}}_{nn}.

(34)

III.2
Lihm’s scheme

We first check the diagonal elements of
\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{Lihm,\mathbf{k}}}_{mn}
for their consistency.
Rewriting the weighted sums over
\mathbf{b}
in Eq. (
II.3
) as gradient yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{Lihm},\mathbf{k}}_{nn}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\nabla_{\mathbf{b}}\left[M_{nn}^{\mathbf{k}-\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2},\mathbf{b}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{b}\mathbf{r}_{n}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbf{r}_{n}-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{N_{\mathbf{k}}}\nabla_{\mathbf{b}}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\left[M_{nn}^{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{b}\mathbf{r}_{n}}\right],

(35)

which simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{Lihm},\mathbf{k}}_{nn}=\mathrm{i}\nabla_{\mathbf{b}}M_{nn}^{\mathbf{k}-\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2},\mathbf{b}}+\mathbf{r}_{n}-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{N_{\mathbf{k}}}\nabla_{\mathbf{b}}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}M^{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}}_{nn}.

(36)

The first term describes the Berry connection, compare to Eq. (
III.1
).
Therefore the remaining terms must add up to zero for
\mathbf{b}\rightarrow\mathbf{0}
to obtain a locally consistent interpolation scheme, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{n}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{N_{\mathbf{k}}}\nabla_{\mathbf{b}}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}_{nn}+\mathcal{O}(|\mathbf{b}|).

(37)

This condition is satisfied by the MV Wannier center, see Eq. (
27
) with a quadratic error scaling because of Eq. (
23
).
For the off-diagonal matrix elements, Lihm’s scheme, see Eq. (
II.3
), is very similar to the symmetric scheme, see Eq. (
32
), but contains additional phase factors of
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2}(\mathbf{r}_{m}+\mathbf{r}_{n})}
.
It is straight-forward to show that these phase factors arise when evaluating Eq. (
32
) for all
\mathbf{r}_{n}=\mathbf{0}
and shifting the Wannier centers by using
U^{\mathbf{k}}_{mn}=\delta_{mn}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\mathbf{r}_{n}}
in Eq. (
13
).
Thus, Lihm’s scheme is identical to the symmetric scheme evaluated in a specific basis.

III.3
Matrix logarithm

Up to now, all the presented interpolation schemes treat the matrix elements independently (for different Wannier indices) of each other, or distinguish only between diagonal and off-diagonal matrix elements.
However, as the matrices
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
connect two orthonormal basis sets, we expect them to generate an unitary transformation and hence propose to use the matrix logarithm to compute
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}
instead of taking the logarithm of the diagonal matrix elements.
More precisely, we write the
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
as a result of a path-ordered matrix product
Wilczek and Zee 1984

\displaystyle M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}=\mathcal{T}\exp\left(-\mathrm{i}\int\limits_{0}^{|\mathbf{b}|}\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}+t\frac{\mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{b}|}}\mathrm{d}t\right)

(38)

in a symbolic notation, where
\mathcal{T}
is the path-ordering operator.
In this sense,
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}
is the generator for the
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
matrices, in contrast to the previous interpolation schemes, which assume incorrectly a purely element-wise relation between the matrix elements of
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
and
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}
.
We note that this representation is only exact for a complete basis as we inserted resolutions of identity during its derivation.
However, the basis must be nearly complete in the energy region of interest regardless of that to obtain converged calculations.
We comment on the basis incompleteness further in Sec.
III.5
.
Taking the logarithm of Eq. (
38
) and employing the Magnus expansion yields
Magnus 1954
;
Blanes et al. 2009

\displaystyle\log M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}=-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{b}\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}+\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2}}+\mathcal{O}(|\mathbf{b}|^{3})\;.

(39)

The matrix elements of the Berry connection and the overlap matrices are evaluated at different
\mathbf{k}
-points in Eq. (
39
), which requires an interpolation of either the
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
matrices or of their logarithm.
In order to avoid an interpolation of the
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
matrices, we take the matrix logarithm first and interpolate afterwards, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{log},\mathbf{R}}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{N_{\mathbf{k}}}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{b}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\left(\mathbf{k}-\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2}\right)\mathbf{R}}\log M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}},

(40)

where the matrix logarithm is well defined (via its principal branch) as
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
is close to unity.
In case of a complete basis the Hermiticity of this scheme follows directly from
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}=(M^{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{b},-\mathbf{b}})^{-1}
without relying on
w_{\mathbf{b}}=w_{-\mathbf{b}}
.
This argument is not applicable to the other interpolation schemes.
The logarithmic scheme is, however, still dependent on the choice of the origin.
In Appendix
B
, we discuss the alternative interpolation scheme, where we first interpolate the overlap matrices before taking the logarithm to ensure its independence of the origin choice.
In our numerical studies, this alternative scheme performed consistently slightly worse than the logarithmic scheme.
This behavior was also found for their self-consistent counterparts presented in the next section.

III.4
Self-consistent interpolation

All interpolation schemes presented so far are local in
\mathbf{k}
and have a quadratic error scaling in
|\mathbf{b}|
.
It has been proposed to enhance the accuracy of the gradient approximation for the Berry connection by using a higher-order interpolation scheme for Eq. (
21
)
Lihm et al. 2026
.
However, this is not required for our self-consistent logarithmic (sclog) interpolation scheme:
After computing the Berry connection locally for every grid point via the logarithmic scheme, we evaluate the path-ordered integrals, see Eq. (
38
), explicitly by employing the global Fourier interpolation of the estimated Berry connection.
This gives us the deviation to the
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
matrices, which we minimize by recursively adapting the
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}
matrices.
We define

\displaystyle S^{\mathbf{k}}_{\mathbf{b}}=-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{b}\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}

(41)

and evaluate the path ordered product by a Magnus-expansion of fourth order
Blanes et al. 2009
as

\displaystyle I^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}

\displaystyle=\log\mathcal{T}\exp\left(-\mathrm{i}\int\limits_{0}^{|\mathbf{b}|}\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}+t\frac{\mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{b}|}}\mathrm{d}t\right)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{S^{\mathbf{k}}_{\mathbf{b}}+4S^{\mathbf{k}+\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2}}_{\mathbf{b}}+S^{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{b}}_{\mathbf{b}}}{6}-\frac{[S^{\mathbf{k}}_{\mathbf{b}},S^{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{b}}_{\mathbf{b}}]}{12}.

(42)

We apply the recursion

\displaystyle S^{\mathbf{k}+\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2}}_{\mathbf{b},0}

\displaystyle=\log M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}\;,

\displaystyle S^{\mathbf{k}+\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2}}_{\mathbf{b},n+1}

\displaystyle=\log M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}+S^{\mathbf{k}+\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2}}_{\mathbf{b},n}-I^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}_{n},

(43)

where the
S^{\mathbf{k}}_{\mathbf{b}}
and
S^{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{b}}_{\mathbf{b}}
are obtained via Fourier interpolation, until we reach the limit

\displaystyle S^{\mathrm{sclog},\mathbf{k}}_{\mathbf{b}}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}S^{\mathbf{k}}_{\mathbf{b},n}.

(44)

Afterwards, we compute the self-consistent Berry connection as

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{sclog},\mathbf{k}}=\mathrm{i}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{b}}w_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{b}S^{\mathrm{sclog},\mathbf{k}}_{\mathbf{b}},

(45)

which minimizes the error globally.
The local error scales at least as
\mathcal{O}(|\mathbf{b}|^{4})
, as given by the order of the Magnus expansion.
In our numerical experiments higher-order Magnus expansions did not improve the results.
We remark that the convergence criterion for the Magnus series,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{|\mathbf{b}|}\|\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}+t\frac{\mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{b}|}}\|\mathrm{d}t\approx|\mathbf{b}|\,\|\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}+\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2}}\|<\pi

(46)

with the Frobenius norm
\|\cdot\|

Moan and Niesen 2008
, is satisfied even for coarse grid sizes as
|\mathbf{b}|\|\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}+\frac{\mathbf{b}}{2}}\|=\|M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}-\mathbb{1}\|+\mathcal{O}(|\mathbf{b}|^{3})
.
An improved consistency through the recursive refinement is expected for sufficiently large supercells that can capture the behavior of the Wannier functions, which is a fundamental constraint to the Wannier interpolation itself.

III.5
Quantifying the basis set incompleteness

In numerical calculations, the number of Wannier functions is finite and they typically do not form a complete basis set.
We express the augmentation of the overlap matrices to a compete basis as

\displaystyle M^{\mathrm{complete},\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}&\tilde{M}^{\mathrm{spill},\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}\\
M^{\mathrm{spill},\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}&M^{\mathrm{off},\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}\end{pmatrix}

(47)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}-\mathrm{i}\mathbf{b}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{k}}&\left(\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{spill},\mathbf{k}}\right)^{\dagger}\\
\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{spill},\mathbf{k}}&\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{off},\mathbf{k}}\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{O}(|\mathbf{b}|^{2}),

where the superscript ’spill’ describes the coupling to the non-described part denoted by ’off’.
To calculate bounds for the spill
\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{spill},\mathbf{k}}\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{b}}
in direction
\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{b}}=\mathbf{b}/|\mathbf{b}|
, we denote the singular values of
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
as
\sigma^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}_{1},\dots,\sigma^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}_{n_{\mathrm{W}}}
in decreasing order.
As
M^{\mathrm{complete},\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
is unitary, we find

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=|M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{x}|^{2}+|M^{\mathrm{spill},\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{x}|^{2}

(48)

for an arbitrary unit vector
\mathbf{x}
of dimension
n_{\mathrm{W}}
.
Employing the min-max theorem for singular values
Horn and Johnson 2012
to Eq. (
48
), we obtain the singular values of
M^{\mathrm{spill},\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
in decreasing order as

\displaystyle\sigma^{\mathrm{spill},\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}_{n}=\sqrt{1-(\sigma^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}_{1+n_{\mathrm{W}}-n})^{2}}.

(49)

For
\mathbf{b}\rightarrow\mathbf{0}
, the infinitesimal spill is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{\mathrm{spill},\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}_{n_{\mathrm{W}}}}{|\mathbf{b}|}\leq|\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{spill},\mathbf{k}}\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{b}}|\leq\frac{\sigma^{\mathrm{spill},\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}_{1}}{|\mathbf{b}|}

(50)

as we show in Appendix
C
.
Furthermore, we relate it in Appendix
C
to the invariant part of the Wannier function spread
\Omega_{\mathrm{I}}

Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
;
Souza et al. 2001
as

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathrm{I}}^{V}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{V}\Omega_{\mathrm{I}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{b}}}w_{\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{b}}}\int\limits_{\mathrm{BZ}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{k}\Tr\left\{\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{spill},\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{spill},\mathbf{k}})^{\dagger}\right\}

(51)

with an error scaling of
\mathcal{O}(|\mathbf{b}|^{2})
, where
V
is the unit cell volume and
w_{\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{b}}}=w_{\mathbf{b}}|\mathbf{b}|^{2}
.
The quantity
\Omega_{\mathrm{I}}^{V}
represents a directionally weighted measure of the basis spill integrated over the BZ and is thus comparable between different systems.
The disentanglement procedure of Souza
Souza et al. 2001
minimizes
\Omega_{\mathrm{I}}
to obtain a reduced number of smooth bands within a certain energy range.
As a consequence, it selects the Wannier functions such that the basis spill effects occurring in the log- and sclog-scheme are minimized.
We note that the non-zero value of
\Omega_{\mathrm{I}}^{V}
is an inherent property of the finite number of the Wannier functions.

IV
Numerical Results

We test the interpolation schemes numerically for monolayer
\mathrm{MoS}_{2}
and bulk
\mathrm{Si}
for two different Wannierizations and different ab-initio grid sizes and demonstrate that the schemes behave very differently, with the log- and sclog-scheme performing best.
First, we discuss the mismatch between the scheme-dependent and the ab-initio computed velocity operator qualitatively as well as quantitatively.
Afterwards, we quantify the basis incompleteness and discuss its convergence with respect to the ab-initio grid size.
Lastly, we demonstrate the serious impact of the interpolation schemes on optical conductivities.

V
Summary and outlook

We assessed the quality of existing and newly developed interpolation schemes, i.e., the Marzari-Vanderbilt, the symmetric, the Lihm, the logarithmic, and the self-consistent logarithmic scheme, for the Berry connection analytically as well as numerically.
We proposed a logarithmic interpolation scheme based on the notion that the Berry connection is a generator of the overlap matrices
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
, in contrast to previous studies, which compute the Berry connection element-wise.
We extended it to a self-consistent logarithmic scheme via recursive refinement based on the explicit evaluation of the path-ordered matrix exponential by employing a Magnus expansion.
To compare the different interpolation schemes, we introduced a mismatch measure between the ab-initio interpolated and scheme-dependent velocity operator.
The self-consistent logarithmic interpolation scheme features the fastest reduction of the mismatch with respect to the ab-initio grid size for monolayer
\mathrm{MoS}_{2}
for two different Wannierizations.
For
\mathrm{Si}
, all interpolation schemes result in a much higher mismatch with the logarithmic scheme and its self-consistent extension was consistently performing best.
We identified the invariant part of the Wannier-function spread
\Omega_{\mathrm{I}}^{V}
as a fundamental limitation of the accuracy.
Lastly, we highlighted that the proposed self-consistent logarithmic interpolation scheme enables faster convergence of optical conductivities with respect to the ab-initio grid size by comparing it to the ones based on the ab-initio velocity operator.
For
\mathrm{MoS}_{2}
(
\mathrm{Si}
) the deviation of the maximal optical conductivity is reduced from
3.2\%
(
17\%
) for the Marzari-Vanderbilt-scheme to
0.04\%
(
0.4\%
) for
N_{k}=25
(
N_{k}=15)
.
We expect similar effects for other often used Wannier gauges, like the ones that enforce the symmetry constraints of the crystal explicitly
Sakuma 2013
;
Koepernik et al. 2023
;
Koretsune 2023
.

As we cannot control the basis spill after the disentanglement and the Wannierization, we propose to evaluate the symmetric, Lihm, logarithmic and consistent logarithmic scheme in numerical calculations and to automatically select the one yielding the lowest mismatch to the ab-initio velocity operator.
Because of the recursive refinement of the self-consistent logarithmic scheme and the inevitable error introduced by the basis spill, we do not expect that higher-order interpolation schemes based on more overlap matrices
M^{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{b}}
will lead to more accurate results.
In a subsequent study, it should be investigated by the means of the ab-initio velocity operator under which circumstances these newly developed schemes
Lihm et al. 2026
can be combined with our scheme to further improve the quality of the estimated Berry connection.
An interesting question to investigate next is whether the self-consistent logarithmic interpolation scheme preserves the symmetries of the crystal.
Additionally, a band energy-weighted mismatch measure of the velocity operator may enable a more quantitative estimation of deviations for energy-dependent observables like the optical conductivity.
We provide our reference implementation in
Thümmler 2026
.
