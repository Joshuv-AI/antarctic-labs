Real-world feasibility of first-line palbociclib for hormone receptor-positive HER2-negative locally advanced/metastatic breast cancer in women aged 70 years and older – final results of the endocrine-sensitive cohort in the French prospective non-interventional PalomAGE study
Authors: E. Carola, P. Caillet, C. Falandry, L. Tassy, E. Paillaud, M. Pulido, G. Rochette de Lempdes, M. Bringuier, N. Jovenin, A. Boudrant, J. Grosjean, C. Chakiba-Brugere, O. Guillem, AC Hardy Bessard, H. Orfeuvre, E. Legouffe, Y. Brault, C. Chen, A. Coumert, M. Decrop, F. Bouteiller, E. Brain
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBreast cancer (BC) is the most commonly diagnosed cancer among women, with 30% of cases occurring at age ≥70 years (70+). Because patients 70+ are underrepresented in BC clinical trials, real-world data may help fill treatment knowledge gaps for this population.Patients and methodsPalomAGE is a French observational, multicentre, prospective study assessing the real-world feasibility of ET + palbociclib in women 70+ with hormonal receptor-positive/HER2-negative (/HER+/HER2-) locally advanced/metastatic BC (ABC). The primary endpoint was palbociclib permanent discontinuation rate at 18 months. Secondary endpoints included time to treatment failure (TTF), real-world progression-free survival (rwPFS), health-related quality of life (HRQoL) and geriatric status (G8 and G-CODE). Here, we report results from the endocrine-sensitive cohort, using ET + palbociclib as first-line treatment.ResultsFrom October 2018 through December 2020, 412 patients were enrolled. Of these, 382 received ≥1 cycle of palbociclib. With a median follow-up of 25.2 months, the 18-month palbociclib permanent discontinuation rate was 41%. The median TTF and rwPFS were 23.0 and 30.4 months, respectively. Palbociclib dose reduction and permanent discontinuation due to adverse events occurred in 35% and 11% of patients, respectively. There were no new safety signals and HRQoL remained stable throughout treatment. An altered G8 (≤14/17) at baseline was an independent factor for palbociclib discontinuation by multivariate analysis.ConclusionThis analysis showed that first-line ET + palbociclib is feasible, well-tolerated and maintains HRQoL in women 70+ with endocrine-sensitive HR+/HER2- ABC, consistent with results from the general adult population. The G8 tool may help identify vulnerable older patients needing further geriatric assessment.

Introduction

With 2.3 million new cases annually, breast cancer (BC) is the most frequently diagnosed cancer and the leading cause of cancer death in women [1]. Nearly 40% of new BC cases and 50% of deaths occur in women aged 70 years and older (70+) in Europe [2], [3], contrasting with the persistent underrepresentation of older patients in randomized clinical trials (RCT) [[4], [5], [6]]. Despite attempts to conduct more age-inclusive trials, underrepresentation of older patients has not changed as found in a review of BC clinical trials opened to accrual between 2020 and 2022; older patients were still excluded based on age, performance status or comorbidities in 39%, 69% and 77% of cases, respectively [7].

Hormone receptor-positive (HR+) and HER2-negative (HER2-) BC is the most frequent phenotype in older adults. According to ASCO and ESMO guidelines, endocrine therapy (ET) combined with a cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 inhibitor (CDK4/6i), palbociclib, ribociclib or abemaciclib, is the preferred first-line treatment for HR+/HER2-locally advanced/metastatic BC (ABC), except when a visceral crisis favors the use of chemotherapy [[8], [9], [10]]. Palbociclib was first-in-class to be approved in combination with an aromatase inhibitor (AI) in the first-line, or with fulvestrant for disease relapse or progression during prior ET. Even when pooled analyses in older populations from RCT were published, the proportion of older patients in registration trials usually did not exceed 10-15% of total enrolled patients, precluding robust conclusions on the results of such therapeutic strategies after age 65-70 [[11], [12], [13]].

Given the increase of frailty with age, this unbalanced representation of older patients in clinical trials creates challenges in routine practice where many adjustments are done without patient awareness. Dosing for instance may be reduced, following negative age discrimination, wrongly by benevolent ageism or excessive fear for adverse events (AE), or appropriately due to objective greater vulnerability to stressors observed with ageing, with potential impact on treatment outcomes [[14], [15], [16]]. This strongly supports the use of geriatric assessment (GA) in older patients to tailor treatment accordingly. By embracing different domains such as functional status, cognitive functions, emotional well-being and social support, GA brings more key information than chronological age to evaluate the real level of biological ageing in an individual patient [6,17].

As a result, GA tools are increasingly used in daily practice to guide the cancer treatment decision-making process [[18], [19], [20], [21]]. However, GA performed by a geriatrician is often time-consuming; therefore, screening tools for geriatric frailty such as G8 have been validated for use by oncology teams to detect in less than 5 min older patients requiring further evaluation with a geriatrician trained to oncology [21,22]. In addition, attempts have been made to standardize the minimal relevant geriatric data that would need to be systematically collected in research targeting older patients, in order to allow comparing results across trials. The Geriatric-COre DatasEt (G-CODE) was developed for this purpose by DIALOG, the French intergroup for clinical research in older patients with cancer, using a Delphi consensus method with international agreement and requiring 10-15 min to cover seven key domains of GA [23].

The PalomAGE study was specifically designed to assess the feasibility of collecting longitudinally important geriatric data through G8 and G-CODE, and assessing health-related quality of life (HRQoL) in women 70+ with HR+/HER2- ABC receiving palbociclib plus ET in a real-world setting. Patients were enrolled in two separate cohorts of similar size (around 400 patients in each cohort), endocrine-sensitive or resistant, defined on prior treatment for ABC and/or time of relapse on/after adjuvant ET [24]. This manuscript reports the final results of the endocrine-sensitive cohort.

Patients and methods

Study and patients

PalomAGE (EUPAS23012) [25] is a French observational, multicentre, prospective study conducted at 113 representative establishments of all practice conditions. Patients screened for participation were women 70+, with HR+ (≥10% oestrogen receptors positive by immunohistochemistry) and HER2-negative (HER2 0-1+ by IHC, or 2+ with no amplification by SISH/FISH) ABC, planned to initiate ET with palbociclib according to label, excluding prior treatment with any CDK4/6i, inability to swallow tablets/capsules and concurrent participation in an interventional clinical trial. In the endocrine-sensitive cohort, no prior systemic treatment for ABC nor relapse during or within one year after adjuvant ET was allowed. Eligibility required an estimated life expectancy of at least 3 months, as determined by the treating physician's clinical judgment, and be registered with the national healthcare service. The 70-year age threshold was defined based on the guidelines from the International Society of Geriatric Oncology (SIOG) and European Society of Breast Cancer Specialists (EUSOMA) [18].

Procedure

All participants provided verbal consent for participation and use of electronic case report form to collect prospective health data including patient self-questionnaires at baseline, then every 3 months for 30 months unless patient left the study prematurely for any reason. The study protocol was approved by the French National Agency for Medicines and Health Products Safety (ANSM) and the CPP Nord-Ouest IV (Lille) on 21/09/2018. It followed the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. It was declared to the French Data Protection Authority (CNIL) as compliant with the reference methodology MR003. This manuscript follows ESMO Guidance for Reporting Oncology real-World Evidence (GROW) [26]. The study was conducted in partnership with the SOciété Francophone d’Onco Gériatrie (SoFOG).

Primary objective and outcomes measures

The primary objective was to evaluate the feasibility of treating patients 70+ presenting with HR+/HER2- ABC, using ET combined with palbociclib. Feasibility was defined based on the rate of palbociclib treatment permanent discontinuation for the following reasons: toxicity according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for AE (NCI-CTCAE) v5.0, patient's decision, disease progression assessed by investigator, or death. In patients with endocrine-sensitive disease, treatment was considered feasible if a patient was still on treatment at 18 months (550 days).

Secondary outcomes measures

Secondary outcomes included: time to treatment failure (TTF), defined as the time from palbociclib initiation to discontinuation due to progression, toxicity, patient's decision or death; real-world progression-free survival (rwPFS), defined as the time from palbociclib initiation to first radiological progression or death from any cause and 18-month radiological/clinical responses, both evaluated according to investigator; safety (based on the NCI-CTCAE V5.0 criteria); HRQoL assessed with the self-administered European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) questionnaires (QLQ-C30 [27,28] and ELD14 [29]); and geriatric factors potentially associated with treatment discontinuation. We used a 10-point difference on EORTC QLQ-C30 scores for clinical significance as it is commonly regarded as a minimally important clinical difference in quality-of-life studies of cancer patients [30].

The G8 was collected at baseline. This tool is a validated 8-item questionnaire screening for geriatric frailty, based on a patient's overall medical condition, motor skills, neuro-psychiatric status and concomitant treatments (Supplementary Table S1) [21,22]. A score ≤14/17 indicates potential geriatric frailty, theoretically requiring further GA (not mandatory for the present study). The DIALOG G-CODE [23] was collected at baseline, 3, 6 and 18 months, and at treatment discontinuation or end of follow-up. It covers 7 domains of interest: social environment, autonomy (6-items for activity of daily living [ADL]; 4-items for instrumental ADL [IADL]), mobility (timed up and go test [TUG]), nutritional status (body weight-loss in the past 6 months; body mass index), cognitive status (3-words recall and clock drawing tests), mood (mini-geriatric depression scale [GDS]) and comorbidities (updated Charlson's score) [23].

Statistical analysis

The sample size for the cohort with endocrine-sensitive disease was based on the results of PALOMA-2 [31], showing 18-month PFS of 60% with around 5% imprecision leading to an anticipated discontinuation rate of 45% at 18 months. To detect this rate with a two-sided 95% confidence interval (CI) of 40% to 50%, 381 patients were required. Assuming 5% of non-evaluable patients, 400 patients were planned for inclusion.

Quantitative normally distributed variables were described by means and standard deviations, otherwise by quantiles (median, minimum, maximum, first and third quartiles). Qualitative variables were described by frequencies and percentages. Rates of binary endpoints were reported with 95% CIs assuming a binomial distribution. Student's t-test was used to compare the means calculated from HRQoL data and differences between the compared groups were considered not clinically significant if under ten points [30]. Survival endpoints were estimated by Kaplan–Meier method and presented with 95% CI.

The rate of treatment discontinuation at 18 months and other secondary effectiveness endpoints were analysed based on the efficacy population comprising all eligible patients who received at least one 28-day treatment cycle with palbociclib. TTF and rwPFS were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. Survival rates were reported with 95% CIs. Patients who did not progress nor die at the visit of study withdrawal were censored at the date of study withdrawal. All patients who initiated palbociclib were included in the safety population.

Patient and tumor characteristics associated with TTF were assessed by uni- and multivariate analyses. Candidate predictive factors included: age, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS), de novo metastatic status, presence of visceral metastases and by type (liver, lung and brain), number of metastatic sites, starting dose of palbociclib at inclusion, comedication, G8 and G-CODE. Univariate analyses were first performed using Cox proportional hazards (PH) models. Variables significant at the 10%-level were considered as candidate variables for the multivariate analyses conducted independently according to 3 models based on G8 score, ECOG PS and DIALOG G-CODE. This strategy was selected to assess the driving impact of each tool or questionnaire, as they are typically used in distinct context and for different objectives (screening for frailty in routine, standard practice in oncology, and minimal dataset for research purposes, respectively). The starting dose of palbociclib was excluded from multivariate analyses. Influenced by clinicopathological and geriatric variables, it could bias the estimation of their direct effect on TTF. We relied on a manual, backward step-by-step process to build the final model, with a significance level set at 5%. The strength of each association was estimated using the hazard ratio (HR) reported with 95% CI. Data was analysed using R software (v 4.2.3.).

Palbociclib was initiated on a three weeks on/one week off schedule for all patients, at the full dose (125 mg per day) in 332 (81%) patients or at a reduced dose in 80 (19%) patients (59 at 100 mg/day, 21 at 75 mg/day). Outcomes

Among the 412 patients who initiated first-line ET with palbociclib, 382 (93%) received at least one full cycle of palbociclib and were evaluable for the primary endpoint (defined as the efficacy population, Fig. 1). With a median follow-up of 25.2 months (95% CI 21.7−28.4), the 18-month permanent discontinuation rate for palbociclib was 41% (95% CI 36−46), with 158 patients having stopped palbociclib for disease progression, toxicity, patient's choice, death, or other reasons in 79 (50%), 28 (18%), 26 (17%), 18 (11%), and 7 (4%), respectively.

In the efficacy population, 230 (60%) discontinued treatment during the study and 164 (43%) experienced disease progression. The median TTF and rwPFS were 23.0 months (95% CI 19.8-26.0) and 30.4 months (95% CI 25.7-not reached [NR]), respectively. In an exploratory analysis, patients with a G8 ≤14 showed a higher discontinuation rate at 18 months (Supplementary Table S2) and a shorter TTF (Supplementary Fig. S2) whether starting palbociclib at standard or lower dose. Safety

In the safety population (N = 412), 383 patients (93%) and 332 (81%) experienced at least one AE or one treatment-related AE; respectively (Table 4). The most common treatment-related AE (Supplementary Table S6) was neutropenia in 261 (63%) patients. Overall, 199 (48%) patients experienced grade 3-4 neutropenia including four (1%) with febrile neutropenia. Other common treatment-related AEs were asthenia, anemia and thrombocytopenia in 86 (21%), 81 (20%) and 55 (13%) patients, respectively. AEs resulting in death (Grade 5) occurred in 30 (7%) patients (Table 4) but only two were considered treatment-related (0.5%, one cerebrovascular accident and one interstitial lung disease). Health-related quality of life

Among 118 patients who completed the HRQoL questionnaires at baseline and at 18 months, QLQ-C30 and ELD14 remained stable (Supplementary Figs. S3 and S4).Patients who permanently discontinued treatment by 18 months had slightly worse baseline scores (higher symptom burden) for fatigue, appetite loss, constipation, and global health status than those who remained on treatment. However, none of these differences reached the 10-point threshold considered clinically meaningful (Supplementary Table S7).

Discussion

Alongside data from RCT, real-world studies (RWS) serve to complement data in the diverse patient populations seen in routine clinical practice. In women 70+ treated with first-line ET combined with palbociclib for endocrine-sensitive ABC in the PalomAGE study, 41% (95% CI 36−46) discontinued palbociclib treatment within the first 18 months. HRQoL assessment showed stable scores throughout the observation period as previously observed in the real-world setting [32], with no clinically significant differences between patients who did not or did permanently discontinue treatment.

Palbociclib was well-tolerated with no new safety signals [31]. Hematological toxicity was consistent with results in RCT and other RWS, non-febrile neutropenia being the most frequent AE (63%). Grade ≥3 neutropenia (48%) were less frequent than in RCT (66% in PALOMA-2 with 41% of patients aged ≥65 years) [31,33] and similar to rates in other RWS (53% in the Spanish PALBOSPAIN retrospective observational study including 31% of patients 70+) [34], likely reflecting the less rigorous monitoring of hematological toxicity in routine clinical practice in RWS compared with clinical trials. Moreover, dose reduction and permanent discontinuation rates due to AEs observed in PalomAGE (35% and 11%, respectively) were similar to those reported in PALOMA-2 for all patients (36% and 9.7%, respectively) [31,33] and other RWS regardless of age [12], and a lower frequency of palbociclib dose reductions was reported in PalomAGE than in PALBOSPAIN (51%) which included 762 pre- and postmenopausal women with HR+/HER2- ABC [34].

The median rwPFS (30.4 months, 95% CI 25.7−NR) estimated in PalomAGE was consistent with PFS data reported in RCT with first-line palbociclib for HR+/HER2- ABC: 30.6 months (95% CI 27.6−NE) in subgroup of patients ≥65 years in PALOMA-2 [31,33] and 33.2 months (95% CI 27.7−39.5) in PARSIFAL-LONG in patients aged 25-90 years treated with ET combined with palbociclib for endocrine-sensitive ABC [35]. The median rwPFS in the PalomAGE study was consistent with findings from PALBOSPAIN, in which a median rwPFS of 27 months (95% CI 22−37) was reported [34].

The percentage of patients who started treatment at a lower dose than in the label (21%) was very similar to what has been described in RWS conducted in standard adult populations or in other large non-age specific series [36], reinforcing the feasibility of palbociclib in the elderly population.

The prospective study design was one of the main strengths of PalomAGE, which was carried out in a large number of diverse-sized public and private centres, reflecting the French healthcare system, an important network in geriatric oncology. Patient characteristics (mean age, ECOG, G8) were aligned with those found in broader elderly populations [21,22]. However, it is important to note that the specific approach for older patients may differ across countries, especially regarding the availability of healthcare professionals or caregivers to perform GA.

A major contribution of the PalomAGE study lies in its insights into treatment conditions and its focus on the feasibility of palbociclib treatment in 70+ patients, including those who were vulnerable based on GA. Univariate and multivariate analyses identified several variables associated with the risk of treatment discontinuation, including older age, lower ECOG PS, lower G8, presence of liver metastases, absence of a caregiver and declined functional status as assessed by IADL. These results highlight that chronological age should not be the only criterion considered in treatment decisions, and that frailty and functional capacities are critical factors and must be considered, as emphasized by current clinical practice guidelines [18,19].

Notably, a recent update to the ASCO guidelines on the assessment and management of vulnerabilities in older patients has broadened its recommendations to all systemic treatments, i.e. not just chemotherapy but also targeted and immunotherapies [19]. Broad evidence shows that relying on GA may help avoid both overtreatment of frail patients and undertreatment of fit ones, also improving treatment outcomes and patient and family satisfaction [19].

PalomAGE presents limitations typical of real-world studies, including unmeasured and residual confounding factors [37]. A limitation of this study is the 25.2-month median follow-up, which, while sufficient for assessing 18-month feasibility and early quality-of-life outcomes, limits interpretation of longer-term endpoints, such as overall survival. The starting dose of palbociclib was not included in the multivariable analysis due to potential confounding by indication. This limits our ability to isolate the independent effect of starting dose on treatment outcomes. In addition, running separate multivariable models for each geriatric assessment tool allowed us to assess its prognostic value individually, in line with its specific clinical use, while avoiding collinearity between overlapping indices; however, this approach limits the ability to evaluate the independent prognostic contribution of each factor within a single comprehensive model.

The attempt to minimize selection bias with consecutive enrolment was self-declared. Given the lack of a control group, one cannot exclude a selection bias due to physicians' preference for a specific CDK4/6i, and it would be useful to be able to compare the findings of the PalomAGE prospective study with corresponding similar data in older patients with other CDK4/6i. To the best of our knowledge, two real-world studies with sub-analysis in older patients, a mixed retrospective–prospective multicenter study in Italy (PALMARES-2 [38], retrospective part) and a retrospective study using a US nationwide deidentified electronic health record-derived longitudinal database (P-VERIFY [39]), reported no significant rwPFS and OS difference between CDK4/6i plus ET treatment groups. Older patients with cancer can be more vulnerable to treatment toxicities due to factors such as comorbidities and drug-drug interactions [40]; palbociclib in combination with ET can be considered as a good first-line treatment option for older patients, based on manageability, and tolerability [38,39,41]. Future studies with extended follow-up would provide valuable OS and breast cancer-specific survival data in older patients, who may experience elevated risk of death relative to other age groups.

Although nearly half of the cohort was aged ≥80 years, we did not perform a dedicated age-stratified analysis, given the potential for bias associated with post hoc exploratory subgrouping. Moreover, the G8 score used in our multivariate models already captures age-related vulnerability and was a stronger predictor of treatment discontinuation than chronological age alone. Additionally, a preliminary analysis of PalomAGE [42] reported lower rates of grade 3-4 AEs in patients aged ≥80 compared with those <80 years (31.4% vs 40.1%), supporting the feasibility of palbociclib in the very old. Finally, although no separate analyses were performed in patients aged 70 to 75 years, we note that these patients could also potentially benefit from alternative CDK4/6is such as ribociclib or abemaciclib based on their established efficacy and tolerability profiles in the general adult population. Nonetheless, future studies specifically powered to explore outcomes in these subgroups are warranted.

Although the study period partially overlapped with the COVID-19 pandemic, no amendment to the protocol was necessary. Inclusions were suspended nationwide for two months on March 23, 2020. Enrolment was still completed six months earlier than originally planned. However, the potential impact of the pandemic on missing data and treatment discontinuations cannot be excluded, nor effectively evaluated.

In conclusion, PalomAGE is the largest prospective real-word study to date evaluating the feasibility of palbociclib in combination with ET in a population of women aged 70+ with HR+/HER2- ABC and presence of frailty (68% of patients). Patients with endocrine-sensitive disease treated with first-line palbociclib show an 18-month palbociclib treatment discontinuation rate of 41%, demonstrating palbociclib treatment feasibility in older patients with no new safety signals. Frailty and social support may influence treatment regimen and outcome, highlighting the importance of GA and geriatric interventions in tailoring treatment intensity to the profile of each older patient [43].
