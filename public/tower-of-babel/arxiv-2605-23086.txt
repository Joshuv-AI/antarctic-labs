Lifting Milnor Invariants for 3-Component Links
Christopher W. Davis, JungHwan Park

Abstract
. We define a sequence of integer-valued invariants γ k ​ ( L ) \gamma^{k}(L) for a 3 3 -component link L L . We prove that the resulting γ \gamma -invariants are invariant under concordance, and more generally under weak cobordism, and that they lift certain Milnor invariants of 3-component links. To establish this, we introduce an invariant h ⁡ ( L ) h(L) , a 3 3 -component analogue of the Kojima–Yamasaki η \eta -invariant, and show that it recovers the γ \gamma -invariants. As applications, we obtain a weak-cobordism classification when the distinguished component has trivial Alexander polynomial and characterize knots that bound continuously embedded disks in B 4 B^{4} whose complements have fundamental group ℤ \mathbb{Z} .

Introduction

In 1954, John Milnor introduced the
\bar{\mu}
-invariants, higher-order linking numbers associated to a link

L=(L_{1},\ldots,L_{m})\subset S^{3}
. They are defined inductively by comparing the lower central series of the
link group
\pi_{1}(S^{3}\smallsetminus L)
with that of the unlink
[
34
]
. Since then, they have been
studied extensively. For instance, building on Stallings’ work
[
41
]
, Casson showed that Milnor invariants are invariants of topological locally flat link concordance
[
3
]
. They have also been reinterpreted in a variety of ways; see, e.g.,
[
21
,
43
,
40
,
37
,
12
,
27
,
26
,
14
]
, and have since been developed in several different directions; see, e.g.,
[
38
,
30
,
5
,
13
,
15
,
6
,
19
,
39
,
42
,
31
,
4
,
16
]
.

These invariants generalize the classical linking number. Indeed, the length-two Milnor invariants

\bar{\mu}_{L}(ij)
coincide with the pairwise linking numbers of the components
L_{i}
and
L_{j}
of
L
.
Given an
m
-component link
L
and a multi-index
I=(i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{n})
with each

i_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, the length-
n
Milnor invariant
\bar{\mu}_{L}(I)
is defined modulo the greatest
common divisor of the lower-length invariants
\bar{\mu}_{L}(I^{\prime})
over all proper subindices
I^{\prime}\subset I
.
For example, if
L
is a two-component link with linking number
\bar{\mu}_{L}(12)=1
, then every
higher-order Milnor invariant of
L
is completely indeterminate.

In 1985, Tim Cochran
[
10
]
discovered a beautiful method for lifting Milnor invariants
of
2
-component links with vanishing linking number, introducing a sequence of integer-valued
invariants
\bigl(\beta^{1}(L),\beta^{2}(L),\dots\bigr)\in\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
, called the
\beta
-invariants
.
These give lifts of certain Milnor invariants in the sense that

\beta^{k}(L)\equiv\bar{\mu}_{L}\bigl(1^{2k}22\bigr)

modulo the indeterminacy of Milnor invariants. (Here
1^{2k}=11\cdots 1
is the multi-index of
length
2k
whose every entry is
1
.)

In this article, we extend Cochran’s idea to
3
-component links and produce a sequence of invariants

\bigl(\gamma^{1}(L),\gamma^{2}(L),\dots\bigr)\in\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
, which we call the
\gamma
-invariants
.
These lift Milnor invariants in the sense that

\gamma^{k}(L)\equiv\bar{\mu}_{L}\bigl(1^{k}23\bigr),

again modulo the indeterminacy of Milnor invariants.

To state the main theorem precisely, we first introduce some terminology and notation. Unless otherwise specified, all links under consideration are ordered, oriented
3
-component links

L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})\subset S^{3}
.
We refer to the first component
L_{1}
as the
distinguished
component, and we further assume that

\operatorname{lk}(L_{1},L_{2})=\operatorname{lk}(L_{1},L_{3})=0
.
We define
\gamma^{0}(L)
to be the linking number of
L_{2}
and
L_{3}
, that is,

\gamma^{0}(L):=\operatorname{lk}(L_{2},L_{3})=\bar{\mu}_{L}(23).

To define the higher invariants
\gamma^{k}(L)
, we require the notion of
derivatives of links
,
a geometric construction introduced in
[
10
]
.

Given a link
L
, the derivative link
D(L)
is defined by first finding Seifert surfaces
G_{1}
and
G_{2}

for
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
that intersect in a knot. The result of pushing this knot off of
G_{1}
in the positive normal direction is denoted
L_{12}
. Replacing
L_{2}
with
L_{12}
yields a new
3
-component
link:

D(L)=(L_{1},L_{12},L_{3}).

More generally, we define the
k
-fold derivative by iterating this process, obtaining

D^{k}(L)=\bigl(L_{1},L_{1^{k}2},L_{3}\bigr).

We then define

\gamma^{k}(L):=\bar{\mu}_{D^{k}(L)}(23)=\operatorname{lk}(L_{1^{k}2},L_{3})

and let
\gamma(L)=\bigl(\gamma^{0}(L),\gamma^{1}(L),\dots\bigr)\in\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
be the sequence of these

\gamma
-invariants.

We remark that it is important to fix
G_{1}
throughout this iterative process. Indeed, the precise value of the
\gamma
-invariants depends on this choice
G_{1}
, which introduces an indeterminacy. When we want to emphasize this dependence on Seifert surface we will write
D(L,G_{1})=(L_{1},L_{12},L_{3},G_{1})
and
\gamma^{k}(L,G_{1})
. While we discuss this indeterminacy in more detail
in Section
4
, we briefly summarize it here. Let
T\colon\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
be the

right-shift operator
,

T(a^{0},a^{1},\dots):=(0,a^{0},a^{1},\dots).

The sequence
\bigl(\gamma^{0}(L),\gamma^{1}(L),\dots\bigr)
is an invariant of the concordance class of
L

modulo the action of
T+\mathrm{Id}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
be a
3
-component link with a distinguished component. Then

\gamma(L)=\bigl(\gamma^{0}(L),\gamma^{1}(L),\ldots\bigr)\in\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}

is a
link concordance invariant modulo the action of
T+\mathrm{Id}
. Moreover, we have

\gamma^{k}(L)\equiv\bar{\mu}_{L}(1^{k}23)\pmod{\gcd\left\{\,\bar{\mu}_{L}(1^{i}23)\mid i<k\,\right\}}.

We remark that, given two sequences of
\gamma
-invariants, it is straightforward to determine whether they represent the same element modulo the action of
T+\mathrm{Id}
; see Corollary
4.6
. In fact, we will see that if
\gamma^{k}(L)
is the first nonvanishing
\gamma
-invariant of
L
, then
\gamma^{k}
is an invariant and
\gamma^{k+1}
is well defined modulo
\gamma^{k}
, as in the case of Milnor invariants. After making a normalization to account for this indeterminacy,
\gamma^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
is well defined for all
n>k+1
. Therefore, we do indeed obtain examples that are indistinguishable by Milnor invariants but can be distinguished by the
\gamma
-invariants; see Section
2.1
.

Cochran
[
10
]
further related his
\beta
-invariants to a
\mathbb{Q}(t)
-valued invariant

due to
Kojima–Yamasaki
[

\mathbb{Q}(t)
denotes the field of rational functions. This invariant
can be interpreted as a self-linking invariant of the lift of
L_{2}
to
\widetilde{E(L_{1})}
, the infinite
cyclic cover of the exterior of
L_{1}
. Our proof of the invariance of
\gamma(L)
proceeds by defining

h(L)\in\mathbb{Q}(t)
, an analogue of
\eta
for
3
-component links, checking the invariance of
h(L)
, and then establishing the connection between
h
and
\gamma
.

More precisely, we define
h(L)
in terms of the linking of chosen lifts of
L_{2}
and
L_{3}
in
\widetilde{E(L_{1})}
. Since this quantity depends on the choice of lifts, it is defined only up to an indeterminacy. In Proposition
4.2
, we show that
h(L)
nevertheless determines a well-defined element of
\nicefrac{{\mathbb{Q}(t)}}{{\doteq}}
, where
\doteq
is the equivalence relation generated by

p(t)\doteq tp(t).

We then prove that the Taylor coefficients of
h(L)
at
t=1
recover the
\gamma
-invariants of
L
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
be a
3
-component link with a distinguished component. Then
h(L)\in\nicefrac{{\mathbb{Q}(t)}}{{\doteq}}
is a
link concordance invariant, and

h(L)\doteq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}(L)\,(t-1)^{k}.

Remark 1.3
.

The reader may notice that we have not specified whether we are working in the smooth or locally flat category. This is because the invariants considered here are in fact invariants of the weakest natural notion of concordance for tame links, namely
I
-equivalence. For convenience, we work primarily in the smooth category. In Remark
4.3
we explain how to remove the smoothness assumptions.

To obtain the main results, we develop several structural and computational consequences of the
\gamma
-invariants. We place the construction in the framework of
weak cobordism
, a weaker equivalence relation than concordance in which the distinguished components cobound an annulus and the remaining components cobound disjoint surfaces subject to a homological condition. In this setting the derivative construction becomes well defined; see Section
2
for a precise definition. We also work out explicit examples. In Section

, we show that arbitrary finitely supported integer sequences can be realized as

-invariants, and we prove that the
\gamma
-invariants admit the following explicit formula in terms of a Seifert matrix.

.

Let

We also connect the

-invariants of the

Let

Remark 1.8
.

All of our techniques are built on computations in the homology groups of knot
complements, complements of disks in the
4
-ball, complements of embedded

Notation and conventions

Throughout the article, all links are in

, and they are ordered and oriented. Homology is taken with

, then any Seifert surface for

Organization

We begin in Section 2 by introducing weak cobordism for
3
-component links, defining the invariants
\gamma^{k}(L,G)
, which depend on the choice of a Seifert surface
G
for the distinguished component, presenting examples, and proving the Seifert-matrix formula of Theorem
1.4
. Section 3 studies
the affect of swapping components
L_{2}
and
L_{3}
, finds that the resulting
\gamma
-invariants carry the same information, and uses this philosophy to determine Cochran’s
\beta
-invariants from the
\gamma
-sequence,
proving Theorem
1.5
. In Section 4, we define the invariant

and show that its Taylor expansion recovers
\gamma(L)
, and prove Theorem
1.2
. Section 5 then shows, via Massey products, that
\gamma^{k}(L)
lifts the Milnor invariant
\bar{\mu}_{L}(1^{k}23)
, completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Section 6 develops a classification framework for weak cobordism, with Corollary
1.6
as a consequence. Finally, Section 7 applies the viewpoint of our paper to knots bounding embedded disks in
B^{4}
whose complements have fundamental group isomorphic to

, establishing Theorem
1.7
. In Section
8
, we close with some natural questions for further study.

We begin this section by discussing ideas fundamental to
[

(L_{1},L_{2})

, Proposition 3.3]
, Cochran shows that the choice of
G
does not matter for
2
-component links; the argument uses that
H_{2}(S^{3}\smallsetminus L_{2})=0
. We cannot argue similarly here, since

H_{2}(S^{3}\smallsetminus(L_{2}\cup L_{3}))\cong\mathbb{Z}
.
Thus the choice of Seifert surface carries an additional integer ambiguity. The
following proposition gives a useful criterion for when two choices give weakly cobordant pairs.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
be a
3
-component link with a distinguished component. Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be Seifert surfaces for
L_{1}
.
If
[G\cup-G^{\prime}]=0
in
H_{2}\bigl(S^{3}\smallsetminus(L_{2}\cup L_{3})\bigr)
, then
(L,G)
is weakly cobordant to
(L,G^{\prime})
.

Proof.

We construct a weak cobordism. Let
A=L_{1}\times[0,1]
and, for
i=2,3
, let
Y_{i}=L_{i}\times[0,1]
. Since
[G\cup-G^{\prime}]=0
in
H_{2}\bigl(S^{3}\smallsetminus(L_{2}\cup L_{3})\bigr)
, it follows that

[G\cup A\cup-G^{\prime}]=0\in H_{2}\bigl(S^{3}\times[0,1]\smallsetminus(Y_{2}\cup Y_{3})\bigr).

This is exactly condition
(A)
of Proposition
2.2
, completing the proof.
∎

The group
H_{2}\bigl(S^{3}\smallsetminus(L_{2}\cup L_{3})\bigr)\cong\mathbb{Z}
is generated by the boundary of a tubular neighborhood of
L_{2}
. Denote this torus by
T_{2}
. Thus, if
G
and
G^{\prime}
are Seifert surfaces for
L_{1}
disjoint from
L_{2}
and
L_{3}
, then the closed surface
G\cup-G^{\prime}
represents an integer multiple of
[T_{2}]
. Equivalently, up to this integer ambiguity, changing the Seifert surface amounts to tubing in parallel copies of
T_{2}
, as in Figure
1
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
L
and
L^{\prime}
be weakly cobordant links, and let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be Seifert surfaces for
L_{1}
and
L_{1}^{\prime}
, respectively.
For any integer
n
, let
G^{n}
denote the surface obtained from
G
by tubing it to
n
parallel copies of
T_{2}
; for
n<0
, we use oppositely oriented copies of
T_{2}
. Then there exists an integer
n
such that
(L,G^{n})
is weakly cobordant to
(L^{\prime},G^{\prime})
. ∎

We adapt Cochran’s derivative construction
[
10
, Section 4]
to our setting as follows.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
be a
3
-component link with a distinguished component,
G_{1}
be a Seifert surface for
L_{1}

and
G_{2}
be a Seifert surface for
L_{2}
.
After adding handles to
G_{2}
, arrange that
G_{1}\cap G_{2}
is a single simple closed curve (that is, a knot). This knot inherits an orientation from the orientations of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. To be explicit, let
n_{G_{i}}
denote the positive normal vector to
G_{i}
. We orient
L_{12}=G_{1}\cap G_{2}
so that its positive tangent vector is the cross product
n_{G_{1}}\times n_{G_{2}}
. The result of pushing this curve off both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
in the positive normal direction is called the

of
L
with respect to
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, and we denote it by
L_{12}
. We then define

D(L,G_{1})=\left(L_{1},\,L_{12},\,L_{3},\,G_{1}\right).

While the definition appears to depend on both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, the following lemma shows that the choice of
G_{2}
does not matter, at least up to weak cobordism. Compare with
[

, Theorem 4.2]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
L
and
L^{\prime}
be links with a distinguished component. Let
G_{1}
,
G_{1}^{\prime}
,
G_{2}
, and

be Seifert surfaces for
L_{1}
,

,

, and
L_{2}^{\prime}
, respectively.
Let
L_{12}
and
L_{12}^{\prime}
be the resulting derivatives. If
(L,G_{1})
is weakly cobordant to
(L^{\prime},G_{1}^{\prime})
, then
D(L,G_{1})
is weakly cobordant to
D(L^{\prime},G_{1}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as
[

, with only some bookkeeping needed to track the component

. We summarize the main ideas.

Let

,
Y_{2}
, and
Y_{3}
be the surfaces from Definition

, and let

be a
3

. Since
H_{2}(S^{3}\times[0,1])=0
, the surface
G_{2}\cup Y_{2}\cup-G_{2}^{\prime}
bounds a
3
-manifold
N_{2}
. By transversality,
N\cap N_{2}
is a properly embedded surface. If we push
N\cap N_{2}
off both
N
and
N_{2}
in the positive normal directions, we obtain a surface bounded by

is disjoint from

(L_{1},L_{12},L_{3},G_{1})

, as claimed.
∎

According to Lemma
2.7
, the assignment
(L,G)\mapsto D(L,G)
gives a well-defined function modulo weak cobordism. We are now ready to define our invariant. For any link
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
with a distinguished component, and any Seifert surface
G
for
L_{1}
disjoint from
L_{2}
and
L_{3}
, we write the
k
-fold iterate of the derivative operator as

D^{k}(L,G)=(L_{1},L_{1^{k}2},L_{3},G).

We then define

\gamma^{k}(L,G):=\operatorname{lk}(L_{1^{k}2},L_{3}).

Since linking number is invariant under weak cobordism and the derivative operator is well defined on weak cobordism classes, we obtain the following:

Theorem 2.8
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the integer
\gamma^{k}(L,G)\in\mathbb{Z}
depends only on the weak cobordism class of
(L,G)
. ∎

Of course, we do not want an invariant that depends on the choice of Seifert surface
G
. In Section

, we prove Theorem
1.1
, which explains how to extract from

an invariant independent of this choice. For now, we carry out some computations, both to build intuition and to illustrate the computability of these invariants.

2.2.
Computation from a Seifert matrix

In

, Cochran explains how to compute his iterated derivatives and the resulting
\beta
-invariants in terms of a Seifert matrix. We now do the same for our
\gamma
-invariants. Indeed, let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be Seifert surfaces for
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
, respectively, that intersect in a simple closed curve
L_{12}^{0}
. (We do not yet denote this curve by
L_{12}
, since we have yet to push it off from
G_{1}
.) Cutting
G_{2}
open along
G_{1}
yields a cobordism from
i^{+}(L_{12}^{0})-i^{-}(L_{12}^{0})
to
L_{2}
. Equivalently,

(i^{+}_{*}-i^{-}_{*})[L_{12}^{0}]=[L_{2}]\in H_{1}(S^{3}\smallsetminus G_{1}).

Here
i^{+},i^{-}\colon G_{1}\to S^{3}\smallsetminus G_{1}
are the maps obtained by pushing
G_{1}
off itself in the positive and negative normal directions, respectively. Since
i^{+}_{*}-i^{-}_{*}\colon H_{1}(G_{1})\to H_{1}(S^{3}\smallsetminus G_{1})
is an isomorphism, the class
[L_{12}^{0}]\in H_{1}(G_{1})
is determined by
[L_{2}]
and, in particular, is independent of the choice of
G_{2}
. It follows that

\gamma^{1}(L,G_{1})=\operatorname{lk}(L_{12},L_{3})=\operatorname{lk}(L_{12}^{0},L_{3})

depends only on
[L_{2}]
and
[L_{3}]
in
H_{1}(S^{3}\smallsetminus G_{1})
. Since
[L_{12}]=i^{+}_{*}([L_{12}^{0}])
, a direct induction shows that for all
k>1
, the quantity
\gamma^{k}(L,G_{1})
depends only on the classes
[L_{2}]
and
[L_{3}]
in
H_{1}(S^{3}\smallsetminus G_{1})
. Expressing all of these relations on homology with a particular choice of basis results in Theorem
1.4
, which we now recall.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
be a
3
-component link whose distinguished component bounds a Seifert surface
G
. Let
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{2g}\}
be a basis for
H_{1}(G)
and let

\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{2g}\}
be the dual basis for
H_{1}(S^{3}\smallsetminus G)
. Let
V
be the
resulting Seifert matrix for
G
, and let
v_{2},v_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2g}
be the column vectors representing

[L_{2}]
and
[L_{3}]
in
H_{1}(S^{3}\smallsetminus G)
with respect to
\{\alpha_{i}\}
. Set
A:=V-V^{T}
.
Then, for every positive integer
k
,

\gamma^{k}(L,G)=\Bigl(A^{-1}(VA^{-1})^{k-1}v_{2}\Bigr)^{T}v_{3}.

is represented by the Seifert matrix

is represented by

.

Thus,

[L_{12}^{0}]=(i^{+}_{*}-i^{-}_{*})^{-1}[L_{2}],

or, in coordinates with respect to

. Write

3.
Swapping components and recovering Cochran’s
\beta
-invariant.

The reader will have noticed that the operator
D(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})=(L_{1},L_{12},L_{3})
is only one of the two possible choices of derivatives we might have considered (for the moment, we suppress the choice of Seifert surface). One could equally well define a derivative in the third component, producing
(L_{1},L_{2},L_{13})
. This would lead to mixed iterated derivatives

(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})\longmapsto(L_{1},L_{1^{p}2},L_{1^{l}3}),

which one might hope carry more information. As we will prove in this section, the
\gamma
-invariants coming from these mixed derivatives carry no new information. As a consequence, we explain how to recover Cochran’s
\beta
-invariants in terms of
\gamma
-invariants.

Let
S
denote the operator on
3
-component links that swaps the second and third components:

S(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})=(L_{1},L_{3},L_{2}).

The mixed derivative
(L_{1},L_{1^{p}2},L_{1^{l}3})
from the preceding paragraph can now be described as

SD^{l}SD^{p}(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}).

The following lemma shows how to compute all of these mixed
\gamma
-invariants in terms of the
\gamma
-invariants we have already defined. For the sake of precision, we now include the choice of Seifert surface.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
be a link with a distinguished component. Let
G_{1}
be a Seifert surface for
L_{1}
. Then:

(1)

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}\bigl(SDSD(L,G_{1})\bigr)=\gamma^{0}\bigl(DSDS(L,G_{1})\bigr)+\gamma^{1}(L,G_{1})
.

(2)

For any
p\geq 1
,
D^{p}DSDS(L,G_{1})
and
DSDSD^{p}(L,G_{1})
are weakly cobordant.

(3)

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}\bigl(S(L,G_{1})\bigr)=\gamma^{0}\bigl(L,G_{1}\bigr)
.

(4)

For any
k\geq 1
,

\gamma^{k}\bigl(S(L,G_{1})\bigr)=(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}{k-1\choose j-1}\gamma^{j}\bigl(L,G_{1}\bigr).

(5)

For any
p\geq 0
and
l\geq 1
,

\gamma^{0}\bigl(SD^{l}SD^{p}(L,G_{1})\bigr)=(-1)^{l}\sum_{j=1}^{l}{l-1\choose j-1}\gamma^{p+j}\bigl(L,G_{1}\bigr).

Proof.

Let
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
, and
G_{3}
be Seifert surfaces for
L_{1}
,
L_{2}
, and
L_{3}
.
Then
SDSD(L,G_{1})
has first component
L_{1}
, second component obtained by pushing
G_{1}\cap G_{2}
off of
G_{1}
, and third component obtained by pushing
G_{1}\cap G_{3}
off of
G_{1}
. As usual, throughout this process we ensure that the components of the link remain disjoint.

Motivated by this description, we denote this link by
(L_{1},L_{12}^{+},L_{13})
. Similarly,
DSDS(L,G_{1})=(L_{1},L_{12}^{+},L_{13}^{++})
is obtained by performing the same construction in the opposite order. Thus,
L_{13}^{++}\cup-L_{13}
bounds an annulus that intersects
L_{12}
once for each intersection point of
G_{1}\cap G_{2}
with
G_{1}\cap G_{3}
in
G_{1}
. This count agrees with the algebraic intersection number of
G_{1}\cap G_{2}
with
G_{3}
, which in turn recovers
\operatorname{lk}(L_{12},L_{3})=\gamma^{1}(L,G_{1})
. Claim (
1
) now follows by checking that the orientations in these intersection counts agree.

Since the annulus cobounded by
L_{13}
and
L_{13}^{++}
lies in the complement of
G_{1}
, it follows that, as long as we use the same Seifert surface for
L_{12}^{+}
in each instance, the links

D(L_{1},L_{12}^{+},L_{13},G_{1})\qquad\text{ and }\qquad D(L_{1},L_{12}^{+},L_{13}^{++},G_{1})

are isotopic. Since the choice of Seifert surface for the second component does not change the weak cobordism class by Lemma
2.7
, we conclude that
D(L_{1},L_{12}^{+},L_{13},G_{1})
and
D(L_{1},L_{12}^{+},L_{13}^{++},G_{1})
are weakly cobordant. This proves Claim (
2
) when
p=1
. An easy induction completes the proof for any
p>1
.

Claim (
3
) follows from the symmetry of linking number:

\gamma^{0}(S(L,G_{1}))=\operatorname{lk}(L_{3},L_{2})=\operatorname{lk}(L_{2},L_{3})=\gamma^{0}(L,G_{1}).

Claim (
4
) is proved by induction. Recall that
G_{2}
and
G_{3}
are Seifert surfaces that intersect
G_{1}
in simple closed curves (these curves may intersect each other). Let

L_{12}=(G_{1}\cap G_{2})^{+}\qquad\text{and}\qquad L_{13}=(G_{1}\cap G_{3})^{+}

be the resulting derivatives. Since linking number is given by the signed count of intersections between one component and a Seifert surface for the other,
\gamma^{1}(L,G_{1})
is the signed count of intersections between
G_{3}
and
L_{12}
. An intersection point is positive if the tangent vector to
L_{12}
has positive dot product with the positive normal vector to
G_{3}
(and negative otherwise). Equivalently, the sign is determined by the sign of

\bigl(n_{G_{1}}\times n_{G_{2}}\bigr)\cdot n_{G_{3}}.

Similarly,
\gamma^{1}(S(L,G_{1}))=\operatorname{lk}(L_{2},L_{13})
is given by the same intersection count, but with each sign reversed. Thus,

\gamma^{1}(S(L,G_{1}))=-\gamma^{1}(L,G_{1}),

as predicted by Claim (
4
) when
k=1
.

Now assume
k>1
and argue by induction. Using
S^{2}=\operatorname{Id}
and the inductive hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\gamma^{k}(S(L,G_{1}))

\displaystyle=\gamma^{k-1}\bigl(DS(L,G_{1})\bigr)=\gamma^{k-1}\bigl(SSDS(L,G_{1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}{k-2\choose j-1}\,\gamma^{j}\bigl(SDS(L,G_{1})\bigr).

Next, Claim (
2
) implies

\gamma^{j}(SDS(L,G_{1}))=\gamma^{0}\bigl(D^{j-1}DSDS(L,G_{1})\bigr)=\gamma^{0}\bigl(DSDSD^{j-1}(L,G_{1})\bigr).

Applying Claim (
1
) now gives

\displaystyle\gamma^{k}(S(L,G_{1}))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}{k-2\choose j-1}\,\gamma^{0}\bigl(DSDSD^{j-1}(L,G_{1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}{k-2\choose j-1}\,\left(\gamma^{0}\bigl(SDSD^{j}(L,G_{1})\bigr)-\gamma^{1}\bigl(D^{j-1}(L,G_{1})\bigr)\right).

Using
\gamma^{1}(D^{j-1}(L,G_{1}))=\gamma^{j}(L,G_{1})
and

\gamma^{0}\bigl(SDSD^{j}(L,G_{1})\bigr)=\gamma^{0}\bigl(DSD^{j}(L,G_{1})\bigr)=\gamma^{1}\bigl(SD^{j}(L,G_{1})\bigr)=-\gamma^{1}\bigl(D^{j}(L,G_{1})\bigr)=-\gamma^{j+1}(L,G_{1}),

we obtain

\gamma^{k}(S(L,G_{1}))=(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}{k-2\choose j-1}\,\left(\gamma^{j+1}(L,G_{1})+\gamma^{j}(L,G_{1})\right).

Reindexing the sum yields

\displaystyle\gamma^{k}(S(L,G_{1}))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left({k-2\choose j-2}+{k-2\choose j-1}\right)\gamma^{j}(L,G_{1})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}{k-1\choose j-1}\gamma^{j}(L,G_{1}).

Finally, Claim (
5
) follows immediately from Claim (
4
):

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}\bigl(SD^{l}SD^{p}(L,G_{1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{0}\bigl(D^{l}SD^{p}(L,G_{1})\bigr)=\gamma^{l}\bigl(SD^{p}(L,G_{1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{l}\sum_{j=1}^{l}{l-1\choose j-1}\,\gamma^{j}\bigl(D^{p}(L,G_{1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{l}\sum_{j=1}^{l}{l-1\choose j-1}\gamma^{p+j}(L,G_{1}),

which completes the proof.
∎

\beta
-invariants. We begin by recalling their definition, which is closely analogous to ours but applies to
2
-component links.

Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2})
be a link with
\operatorname{lk}(L_{1},L_{2})=0
. Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be Seifert surfaces for
L_{1}
and
L_{2}

\Delta(L_{1},L_{2})=(L_{1},L_{12}).

Unlike in our
3

is independent of the choice of Seifert surfaces and depends only on the weak cobordism class of

. The
k
-fold iterate is given by

\Delta^{k}(L_{1},L_{2})=(L_{1},L_{1^{k}2}).

Let

via Seifert surfaces. We then define

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2})
be a
2
-component link with
\operatorname{lk}(L_{1},L_{2})=0
. Let

be a Seifert surface for

disjoint from

Proof.

Notice that

(L_{1},L_{1^{k}2},L_{1^{k}2}^{+},G)=SD^{k}SD^{k}(L^{*},G).

Thus, by Claim (

) of Lemma

,

\displaystyle\beta^{k}(L)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{0}\bigl(SD^{k}SD^{k}(L^{*},G)\bigr)

4.
A variant of the Kojima–Yamasaki
\eta
-invariant,

and its relation to the
\gamma
-invariants

In this and the following section, we prove our main theorem, which we now recall.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
be a
3
-component link with a distinguished component. Then

\gamma(L)=\bigl(\gamma^{0}(L),\gamma^{1}(L),\ldots\bigr)\in\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}

is a
link concordance invariant modulo the action of
T+\mathrm{Id}
. Moreover, we have

\gamma^{k}(L)\equiv\bar{\mu}_{L}(1^{k}23)\pmod{\gcd\left\{\,\bar{\mu}_{L}(1^{i}23)\mid i<k\,\right\}}.

In fact, in this section, specifically in Corollary
4.5
, we prove the stronger statement that
\gamma(L)
depends only on the weak cobordism class of
L
. The latter part of Theorem
1.1
, connecting
\gamma(L)
to Milnor invariants will be proved in the next section, specifically in Theorem
5.1
.

Cochran’s argument in
[
10
]
that the
\beta
-invariants are independent of the choice of Seifert surface does not apply in our setting. Indeed, as seen in Figures
2
and
3
, the
\gamma
-invariants depend on the choice of Seifert surface. In this section, we take inspiration from
[
10
, Theorem 7.1]
, where Cochran shows that the
\beta
-invariants recover the coefficients of the
\eta
-invariant defined by Kojima and Yamasaki in
[
29
]
. We define a variant of the
\eta
-invariant for
3
-component links, describe its indeterminacy, and show that the
\gamma
-invariants recover its coefficients.

We begin by defining this new invariant. Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
be a
3
-component link with a distinguished component. Let
\widetilde{L_{2}}
and
\widetilde{L_{3}}
be lifts of
L_{2}
and
L_{3}
to the infinite cyclic cover,
\widetilde{S^{3}\smallsetminus L_{1}}
. Let
t\colon\widetilde{S^{3}\smallsetminus L_{1}}\to\widetilde{S^{3}\smallsetminus L_{1}}
be the deck transformation corresponding to the meridian of
L_{1}
and
t_{*}
be the induced map on the Alexander module,
A(L_{1})=H_{1}(\widetilde{S^{3}\smallsetminus L_{1}})
. Since
A(L_{1})
is a
\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]
-torsion module, there is a polynomial
\Delta(t)
such that the
1
-cycle
\Delta(t)\widetilde{L_{2}}
bounds a
2
-chain
X\in C_{2}(\widetilde{S^{3}\smallsetminus L_{1}})
. Kojima and Yamasaki define

\eta(L_{1},L_{2})=\eta(L)=\dfrac{1}{\Delta(t)}\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(X,t^{k}\widetilde{L_{2}^{+}}\bigr)t^{k}\in\mathbb{Q}(t).

Here
(X,t^{k}\widetilde{L_{2}^{+}})
indicates the algebraic intersection number between
X
and the translate
t^{k}\widetilde{L_{2}^{+}}
of the lift of the
0
-framed push-off of
L_{2}
. This can be viewed as recording the self-linking of a lift of
L_{2}
in the infinite cyclic cover of
S^{3}\smallsetminus L_{1}
. We define a new invariant by instead recording the linking between a lift of
L_{2}
and a lift of
L_{3}
. Define

h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})=\dfrac{1}{\Delta(t)}\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(X,t^{k}\widetilde{L_{3}}\bigr)t^{k}\in\mathbb{Q}(t),

where
X\in C_{2}(\widetilde{S^{3}\smallsetminus L_{1}})
is a
2
-chain with
\partial X=\Delta(t)\widetilde{L_{2}}
, as above.

According to
[
29
, Theorem 2]
,
\eta(L_{1},L_{2})\in\mathbb{Q}(t)
is independent of the choice of lift of
L_{2}
and is, moreover, a concordance invariant. In contrast,
h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})
does depend on the choice of lifts, and hence we include the lifts in the notation. We will show that changing the lifts multiplies
h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})
by a power of
t
. The following proposition shows that, modulo this ambiguity,
h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})
defines a weak cobordism invariant. Recall that
\doteq
is the equivalence relation on
\mathbb{Q}(t)
generated by

p(t)\doteq tp(t).

Definition 4.1
.

Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
be a
3
-component link with a distinguished component. Choose lifts

\widetilde{L_{2}}
and
\widetilde{L_{3}}
of
L_{2}
and
L_{3}
to the infinite cyclic cover

\widetilde{S^{3}\smallsetminus L_{1}}
, and set

h(L):=\big[h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})\big]\in\mathbb{Q}(t)/\doteq.

The following gives a stronger statement than Theorem
1.2
, which only states that
h(L)
is an invariant of the concordance class of
L
. The fact that the Taylor coefficients of
h(L)
at
t=1
recover the
\gamma
-invariants of
L
will be proved in Theorem
4.4
, combined with the following proposition, which should be compared with Proposition 1 and Theorem 2 in
[
29
]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
be a
3
-component link with a distinguished component.

(1)

The quantity
h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})
is independent of the choice of
X
and
\Delta(t)
.

(2)

For any integers
n_{2}
and
n_{3}
,

h(L_{1},t^{n_{2}}\widetilde{L_{2}},t^{n_{3}}\widetilde{L_{3}})=t^{n_{2}-n_{3}}h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})\in\mathbb{Q}(t).

(3)

If
(A,Y_{2},Y_{3})
is a weak cobordism from
L
to
L^{\prime}
, then each lift
\widetilde{Y_{i}}
in

\widetilde{S^{3}\times[0,1]\smallsetminus A}
has boundary

\partial\widetilde{Y_{i}}=\widetilde{L_{i}}-t^{n_{i}}\widetilde{L_{i}^{\prime}}

for some integer
n_{i}
. Moreover,

h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})=h(L_{1}^{\prime},t^{n_{2}}\widetilde{L_{2}^{\prime}},t^{n_{3}}\widetilde{L_{3}^{\prime}})\in\mathbb{Q}(t).

In particular, the quantity
h(L)
is an invariant of the weak cobordism class of
L
.

Proof.

Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
be a
3
-component link, and let
\widetilde{L_{2}}
,
\widetilde{L_{3}}
,
\Delta(t)
, and
X
be as in the definition of
h
. We first prove (
2
). We have
\partial(t^{n_{2}}X)=\Delta(t)\,t^{n_{2}}\widetilde{L_{2}}
. Also, we have that

\bigl(t^{n_{2}}X,t^{k}(t^{n_{3}}\widetilde{L_{3}})\bigr)=\bigl(X,t^{k+n_{3}-n_{2}}\widetilde{L_{3}}\bigr).

By reindexing the sum, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{rll}h(L_{1},t^{n_{2}}\widetilde{L_{2}},t^{n_{3}}\widetilde{L_{3}})&=\frac{1}{\Delta(t)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(t^{n_{2}}X,t^{k}(t^{n_{3}}\widetilde{L_{3}})\bigr)t^{k}&=\frac{1}{\Delta(t)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(X,t^{k+n_{3}-n_{2}}\widetilde{L_{3}}\bigr)t^{k}\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{\Delta(t)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(X,t^{k}\widetilde{L_{3}}\bigr)t^{k}\right)t^{n_{2}-n_{3}}&=h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})t^{n_{2}-n_{3}}.\end{array}

We now check all at once for invariance under weak cobordism and independence from the choices of
\Delta(t)
and
X
. We continue to work in the smooth setting for the sake of ease. The details needed to work over
I
-equivalence appear in Remark
4.3
. Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
and
L^{\prime}=(L_{1}^{\prime},L_{2}^{\prime},L_{3}^{\prime})
be weakly cobordant links with a distinguished component. Let
A
,
Y_{2}
, and
Y_{3}
be the surfaces of Definition
2.1
.

Let
\widetilde{Y}_{2}
and
\widetilde{Y}_{3}
be the respective lifts of
Y_{2}
and
Y_{3}
to the infinite cyclic cover of
E(A)=S^{3}\times[0,1]\smallsetminus A
, chosen with orientations satisfying
\partial Y_{i}=L_{i}-L_{i}^{\prime}
for
i=2,3
. As we have already determined the effect of the choice of lifts, we may as well pick the lifts of
L_{i}
,
L_{i}^{\prime}
and
Y_{i}
so that
\partial\widetilde{Y_{i}}=\widetilde{L_{i}}-\widetilde{L_{i}^{\prime}}
.

Let
X
and
X^{\prime}
be
2
-chains bounded by
\Delta(t)\widetilde{L_{2}}
and
\Delta^{\prime}(t)\widetilde{L_{2}^{\prime}}
, respectively. Then

Q=\Delta^{\prime}(t)X+\Delta(t)\Delta^{\prime}(t)\widetilde{Y_{2}}-\Delta(t)X^{\prime}

is a
2
-cycle. Since
H_{2}(\widetilde{E(A)})
is torsion as a
\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]
-module, it follows that for some
q(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]
, the cycle
q(t)Q
is a boundary. For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

0=\bigl(q(t)Q,t^{k}\widetilde{Y_{3}}\bigr).

Here,
\bigl(q(t)Q,t^{k}\widetilde{Y}_{3}\bigr)
is the pairing with respect to the intersection form

H_{2}(\widetilde{E(A)})\times H_{2}(\widetilde{E(A)},\partial\widetilde{E(A)})\to\mathbb{Z}.

Since
Y_{2}
and
Y_{3}
are disjoint, the same is true for their lifts. Thus, the only intersections between
q(t)Q
and
t^{k}\widetilde{Y_{3}}
occur where
t^{k}\widetilde{L_{3}}
intersects
q(t)\Delta^{\prime}(t)X
and where
t^{k}\widetilde{L_{3}^{\prime}}
intersects
q(t)\Delta(t)X^{\prime}
. Therefore,

0=\bigl(q(t)\Delta^{\prime}(t)X,t^{k}\widetilde{L_{3}}\bigr)-\bigl(q(t)\Delta(t)X^{\prime},t^{k}\widetilde{L_{3}^{\prime}}\bigr).

Summing over all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we conclude that

q(t)\Delta^{\prime}(t)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(X,t^{k}\widetilde{L_{3}}\bigr)t^{k}=q(t)\Delta(t)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(X^{\prime},t^{k}\widetilde{L_{3}^{\prime}}\bigr)t^{k}.

Dividing by
q(t)\Delta(t)\Delta^{\prime}(t)
, we conclude

h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})=h(L_{1}^{\prime},\widetilde{L_{2}^{\prime}},\widetilde{L_{3}^{\prime}})\in\mathbb{Q}(t),

which completes the proof.
∎

We remark that Proposition
4.2
also holds under
I
-equivalence:

Remark 4.3
.

Just as in the setting of Kojima–Yamasaki, the argument above goes through with only minor changes if one drops the smoothness conditions from the definition of weak cobordism and allows continuously embedded annuli and surfaces, provided that the links
L
and
L^{\prime}
are still tame. Indeed, much of the content of this remark is an appeal to
[
29
, Lemma 5]
. If
A
is a continuously embedded annulus in
S^{3}\times[0,1]
, we write

E(A):=S^{3}\times[0,1]\smallsetminus A.

First note that, since
A
is closed,
E(A)
is
an open subset of
S^{3}\times[0,1]
, which is a smooth
manifold with boundary,
and hence
E(A)
is a smooth (noncompact)
manifold with boundary. Let
S\subset S^{4}
be the embedded
2
-sphere obtained
from
A
by capping off its boundary components with cones on
L_{1}
and
L_{1}^{\prime}
.
Then
\operatorname{int}(E(A))
is homeomorphic to
S^{4}\smallsetminus S
.
Since a manifold with boundary is homotopy equivalent to its interior, it
follows that
E(A)
is homotopy equivalent to
S^{4}\smallsetminus S
.

By Alexander–Pontryagin duality (see, for example,
[
36
, Theorem 74.1]
),

\widetilde{H}_{i}(E(A))\cong\widetilde{H}_{i}(S^{4}\smallsetminus S)\cong\check{\widetilde{H}}^{3-i}(S),

where
\widetilde{H}
indicates reduces homology and
\check{\widetilde{H}}
indicates reduced Čech cohomology. Since
E(A)
is connected and
S\cong S^{2}
, we obtain

H_{0}(E(A))\cong H_{1}(E(A))\cong\mathbb{Z}

and all other reduced homology groups vanish. Equivalently,

H_{*}(E(A))\cong H_{*}(S^{1}).

In particular,
E(A)
admits an infinite cyclic cover corresponding to a
generator of
H_{1}(E(A))
, and
[
29
, Lemma 5]

concludes that the intersection form

H_{2}(\widetilde{E(A)})\times H_{2}(\widetilde{E(A)},\partial\widetilde{E(A)})\to\mathbb{Z}

vanishes. With these observations, the proof of Proposition
4.2
goes through unchanged if
A
is continuously embedded, and
Y_{2}
and
Y_{3}
are any embedded surfaces which lift to the infinite cyclic cover of
E(A)
.

The denominator of
h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})\in\mathbb{Q}(t)
can be taken to be any annihilator of the class of
\widetilde{L_{2}}
in the Alexander module of
L_{1}
. The Alexander polynomial of
L_{1}
is such an annihilator. Thus, the denominator of
h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})
is coprime to
(t-1)
, and so we may expand
h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})
as a power series in
t-1
:

h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(t-1)^{k}p_{k}\in\mathbb{Q}(t).

We prove that the
\gamma
-invariants recover these coefficients. Compare to Theorem 7.1 of
[
10
]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
be a link with a distinguished component. Consider any Seifert surface
G_{1}
for
L_{1}
.
If
\widetilde{L_{2}}
and
\widetilde{L_{3}}
both sit in the same lift of
S^{3}\smallsetminus G_{1}
, then

h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(t-1)^{k}\gamma^{k}(L,G_{1}).

Proof.

Let
G_{1}
be a Seifert surface for
L_{1}
.
Then
S^{3}\smallsetminus G_{1}
forms a fundamental domain for the infinite cyclic cover
\widetilde{S^{3}\smallsetminus L_{1}}
. Let
\widetilde{L_{2}}
and
\widetilde{L_{3}}
both sit in the same lift of
S^{3}\smallsetminus G_{1}
.
Let
G_{2}
be a Seifert surface for
L_{2}

intersecting
G_{1}
in a derivative
L_{12}
. Then the lift of
G_{2}
cut open along
L_{12}
gives a
2
-chain
H
in
\widetilde{S^{3}\smallsetminus L_{1}}
bounded by
\widetilde{L_{2}}-(t-1)\widetilde{L_{12}}
, with

\bigl(H,t^{k}\widetilde{L_{3}}\bigr)=\begin{cases}\operatorname{lk}(L_{2},L_{3})&\text{if }k=0,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

If
X
is a
2
-chain bounded by
\Delta(t)\widetilde{L_{12}}
, then

\partial\bigl((t-1)X+\Delta(t)H\bigr)=\Delta(t)\widetilde{L_{2}},

and so

\displaystyle h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta(t)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigl((t-1)X+\Delta(t)H,t^{k}\widetilde{L_{3}}\bigr)t^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Delta(t)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigl((t-1)X,t^{k}\widetilde{L_{3}}\bigr)t^{k}+\frac{1}{\Delta(t)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(\Delta(t)H,t^{k}\widetilde{L_{3}}\bigr)t^{k}

\displaystyle=(t-1)h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{12}},\widetilde{L_{3}})+\operatorname{lk}(L_{2},L_{3}).

Applying the same argument to
h(D(L,G_{1}))
yields

\displaystyle h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})

\displaystyle=(t-1)\bigl((t-1)h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{112}},\widetilde{L_{3}})+\operatorname{lk}(L_{12},L_{3})\bigr)+\operatorname{lk}(L_{2},L_{3})

\displaystyle=(t-1)^{2}h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{112}},\widetilde{L_{3}})+(t-1)\gamma^{1}(L,G_{1})+\gamma^{0}(L,G_{1}).

Repeating this process
k
times gives

h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})=(t-1)^{k+1}h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{1^{k+1}2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})+\sum_{i=0}^{k}(t-1)^{i}\gamma^{i}(L,G_{1}).

Note that similarly to
h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})
, the rational function
h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{1^{k+1}2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})
admits an expansion as a power series in
(t-1)
:

h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{1^{k+1}2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}r_{j}(t-1)^{j}

for some sequence
(r_{0},r_{1},\dots)
. Thus,

h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}r_{j}(t-1)^{j+k+1}+\sum_{i=0}^{k}(t-1)^{i}\gamma^{i}(L,G_{1}).

In particular, for all
i\leq k
, the coefficient of
(t-1)^{i}
in
h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})
is
\gamma^{i}(L,G_{1})
. Since
k
is arbitrary, it follows that the coefficient of
(t-1)^{i}
in
h(L_{1},\widetilde{L_{2}},\widetilde{L_{3}})
is
\gamma^{i}(L,G_{1})
for every
i\geq 0
. This proves the theorem.
∎

Proposition
4.2
and Theorem
4.4
together establish the invariance of
\gamma
. Indeed, if
G
and
G^{\prime}
are two Seifert surfaces for
L_{1}
disjoint from
L_{2}
and
L_{3}
, then by Proposition
4.2
and Theorem
4.4
, both

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(t-1)^{k}\gamma^{k}(L,G)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(t-1)^{k}\gamma^{k}(L,G^{\prime})

represent
h(L)\in\nicefrac{{\mathbb{Q}(t)}}{{\doteq}}
. Hence, they differ by multiplication by a power of
t
. Substituting
x=t-1
, we obtain

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}\gamma^{k}(L,G)=(x+1)^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}\gamma^{k}(L,G^{\prime})

for some
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since Proposition
4.2
also shows that
h(L)
is an invariant of the weak cobordism class of
L
, the following corollary is immediate.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}=\{(a_{0},a_{1},\dots)\}
denote the set of all integer-valued sequences, and let
T\colon\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
be the right shift operator defined by

T(a_{0},a_{1},\dots)=(0,a_{0},a_{1},\dots).

Let
\sim
be the equivalence relation on
\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
generated by

a\sim(T+\mathrm{Id})(a)

for each
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
. Then the class of

\gamma(L)=(\gamma^{0}(L),\gamma^{1}(L),\dots)\in\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}/{\sim}

depends only on the weak cobordism class of
L
. ∎

In fact, it is straightforward to determine whether two sequences in
\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
are equivalent with respect to the equivalence relation of Corollary
4.5
. We start with an illustrative example. In Figure
5
, we exhibited links for which
(\gamma^{0}(L),\dots,\gamma^{n}(L))
can take any prescribed sequence of values, with
\gamma^{k}(L)=0
for
k>n
. Consider two links with
\gamma(L)=(1,3,r,0,0,\dots)
and
\gamma(L^{\prime})=(1,4,q,0,0,\dots)
for some
q,r\in\mathbb{Z}
. We take a moment to analyze whether these are equal modulo the action of
(T+\mathrm{Id})
. A direct induction shows that

(T+\mathrm{Id})^{m}(1,3,\dots)=(1,3+m,\dots),

so the only way to obtain a sequence beginning with
(1,4,\dots)
from
(1,3,r,0,0,\dots)
is to take
m=1
. In that case,

(T+\mathrm{Id})(1,3,r,0,0,\dots)=(1,4,3+r,r,0,\dots).

This agrees with
(1,4,q,0,0,\dots)
only if
r=0
and
q=3
.

This example illustrates the following. If
\gamma^{k}(L)
is the first nonvanishing
\gamma
-invariant of
L
, then
\gamma^{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
is an invariant,
\gamma^{k+1}
is well defined modulo
\gamma^{k}
, and after accounting for that indeterminacy,
\gamma^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
is well defined for all
n>k+1
. The following makes this explicit.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
and
L^{\prime}=(L_{1}^{\prime},L_{2}^{\prime},L_{3}^{\prime})
be weakly cobordant links, and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that
\gamma^{j}(L)=0
for all
j<k
and
\gamma^{k}(L)\neq 0
. Then:

(1)

\gamma^{k}(L^{\prime})=\gamma^{k}(L)
.

(2)

\gamma^{k+1}(L^{\prime})-\gamma^{k+1}(L)=p\gamma^{k}(L)
for some
p\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(3)

If
\gamma^{k+1}(L^{\prime})-\gamma^{k+1}(L)=p\gamma^{k}(L)
with
p\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\gamma^{k+2}(L^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma^{k+2}(L)+p\gamma^{k+1}(L)+{p\choose 2}\gamma^{k}(L),

\displaystyle\gamma^{k+3}(L^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma^{k+3}(L)+p\gamma^{k+2}(L)+{p\choose 2}\gamma^{k+1}(L)+{p\choose 3}\gamma^{k}(L),

and in general, if we follow the convention that
\gamma^{i}(L)=0
when
i<0
then for all
\ell>k+1
,

\displaystyle\gamma^{\ell}(L^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma^{\ell}(L)+p\gamma^{\ell-1}(L)+{p\choose 2}\gamma^{\ell-2}(L)+\dots+p\gamma^{\ell-p+1}(L)+\gamma^{\ell-p}(L)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{p}{p\choose q}\,\gamma^{\ell-q}(L).

Proof.

Since
L
and
L^{\prime}
are weakly cobordant, it follows (after the change of variables
x=t-1
) that

h(L^{\prime})=(1+x)^{n}h(L)

for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. By swapping
L
and
L^{\prime}
as appropriate, we may assume that
n\geq 0
. By the binomial theorem and recalling that
\gamma^{j}(L)=0
for all
j<k
, we have

h(L^{\prime})=\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{n}{n\choose q}x^{i}\cdot\displaystyle\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\gamma^{j}(L)\,x^{j}.

Expanding the product, reindexing by
j+q=m
, and using that
\gamma^{m-q}(L)=0
when
m-q<0
,

(1)

h(L^{\prime})=\displaystyle\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{n}{n\choose q}\gamma^{m-q}(L)\right)x^{m}.

We can compute the
x_{k}
-coefficient to get

\gamma^{k}(L^{\prime})=\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{n}{n\choose q}\gamma^{k-q}(L)=\gamma^{k}(L),

where the second equality follows since
\gamma^{j}(L)=0
for all
j<k
. This proves (
1
).

Comparing the coefficient of
x^{k+1}
in equation (
1
) yields

\gamma^{k+1}(L^{\prime})={n\choose 0}\gamma^{k+1}(L)+{n\choose 1}\gamma^{k}(L)

so
\gamma^{k+1}(L^{\prime})-\gamma^{k+1}(L)=n\gamma^{k}(L)
. This proves claim (
2
) (with
p=n
).

Finally, assume
\gamma^{k+1}(L^{\prime})-\gamma^{k+1}(L)=p\gamma^{k}(L)
with
p\geq 0
. Since
\gamma^{k}(L)\neq 0
, the previous paragraph implies that
p=n
. Comparing the coefficient of
x^{\ell}
in equation
1
for any
\ell>k+1
gives

\gamma^{\ell}(L^{\prime})=\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{p}{p\choose q}\gamma^{\ell-q}(L),

proving (
3
).
∎

5.
The
\gamma
-invariant as a lift of Milnor invariants

As mentioned in the introduction, the Milnor invariant
\bar{\mu}_{I}(L)
is, in general, not an integer-valued invariant; rather, it is only well defined modulo the some indeterminacy
[
35
]
. More precisely, for any link
L
and any fixed multi-index
I=(i_{1}\dots i_{n})
, let
J_{I}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
be the ideal generated by

\bigl\{\bar{\mu}_{J}(L)\,\bigm|\,J=(i_{k_{1}}\dots i_{k_{m}}),\ 1<m<n,\ 1\leq k_{1}<\cdots<k_{m}\leq n\bigr\}.

Then
\bar{\mu}_{I}(L)
is well defined as an element of
\mathbb{Z}/J_{I}
.

In
[
40
]
, Porter interprets Milnor’s
\bar{\mu}
-invariants in terms of Massey products in the link complement; in particular, this yields link invariants with the same indeterminacy as Milnor’s
\bar{\mu}
-invariants. In
[
12
, Theorem 6.10]
, Cochran uses Massey products to prove that his integer-valued
\beta
-invariant recovers
\bar{\mu}(1^{2k}22)
modulo this indeterminacy. In this section we follow a similar path to prove that
\gamma^{k}
recovers
\bar{\mu}(1^{k}23)
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
I=(1^{k}23)
and let
L
be a
3
-component link with
\operatorname{lk}(L_{1},L_{2})=\operatorname{lk}(L_{1},L_{3})=0
. Then
\gamma^{k}(L)\equiv\bar{\mu}_{L}(I)\mod J_{I}
.

We begin by recalling Porter’s formulation of Milnor invariants in terms of Massey products.
Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{p}
be topological spaces and let
R
be a commutative ring. For each
i
,
let
x_{i}\in H^{1}(X_{i};R)
.
A
defining system
for the Massey product
\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{p}\rangle
with respect to

\{X_{1},\dots,X_{p}\}
and coefficients in
R
) is a collection of
1
-cochains

\{m_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq j\leq p,\ (i,j)\neq(1,p)\}

satisfying:

(1)

m_{i,j}\in C^{1}(X_{i}\cap\cdots\cap X_{j};R)
,

(2)

m_{i,i}
is a cocycle representative for
x_{i}
,

(3)

For
i<j
, we have

\delta(m_{i,j})=\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}m_{i,k}\cup m_{k+1,j},

where
\cup
denotes the cup product on
X_{i}\cap\cdots\cap X_{j}
.

The Massey product
\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{p}\rangle
is the subset of

H^{2}(X_{1}\cap\cdots\cap X_{p};R)
consisting of the collection of all

\left[\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}m_{1,k}\cup m_{k+1,p}\right],

as the defining system varies. Porter shows that, for link complements, this recovers the
corresponding Milnor invariant.

Theorem 5.2

(
[
40
, Theorem 3]
)
.

Let
L=(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})
be a link in
S^{3}
, let
I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{p})
be a sequence with

1\leq i_{k}\leq n
, and let

. For each

, let

.
Then the Massey product
\langle u_{i_{1}},\dots,u_{i_{p}}\rangle
with respect to

\{S^{3}\smallsetminus L_{i_{1}},\dots,S^{3}\smallsetminus L_{i_{p}}\}
is defined and contains only

(-1)^{p}\,\bar{\mu}_{L}(I)\cdot t_{i_{1},i_{p}}
. Here
t_{i_{1},i_{p}}\in H^{2}(S^{3}\smallsetminus L;R)
is the
Lefschetz dual in
S^{3}\smallsetminus L
to an arc running from
L_{i_{1}}
to
L_{i_{p}}
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})

3

The entries we prescribe are summarized by the table

\begin{array}[]{c|ccccccc}m_{i,j}&j=1&j=2&j=3&\dots&j=k&j=k+1&j=k+2\\
\hline\cr i=1&u_{1}&0&0&\dots&0&v_{k}&\\

The equality

∎

6.
Classification of links up to weak cobordism

Our next goal is to classify links up to weak cobordism. Since this classification depends in interesting ways on the category, we make that distinction explicit here. We say that links
L
and
L^{\prime}
are
smoothly
or
topologically
weakly cobordant if the annulus
A
and the surfaces
Y_{2}
and
Y_{3}
in Definition
2.1
are smoothly embedded or locally flatly embedded, respectively. We say that they are
continuously
weakly cobordant if these surfaces are merely continuously embedded. As noted in Remark
4.3
, the
\beta
- and
\gamma
-invariants obstruct continuous weak cobordism. In this section, we construct a complete set of obstructions to smooth, topological and continuous weak cobordism.

It is clear that if
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
is a
3
-component link, then the concordance class of
L_{1}
, Cochran’s
\beta
-invariants, and the
\gamma
-invariants are all invariants of weak cobordism. There is one additional piece of data. Suppose

and
L^{\prime}
are weakly cobordant, and
A
,
Y_{2}
, and
Y_{3}
are as in Definition
2.1
. Then the lifts
\widetilde{Y_{i}}
, for

, induce an equivalence between

[\widetilde{L_{i}}]
and
[\widetilde{L_{i}^{\prime}}]
in

denotes the infinite cyclic cover of

\beta
- and
\gamma
-invariants, this observation yields a complete classification of links up to weak cobordism.

Let
G
be a Seifert surface for
L_{1}
disjoint from
L_{2}
and
L_{3}
. Choosing a lift of

to the infinite cyclic cover of

and
\widetilde{L_{3}}

.

Let
(L,G)

,

7.
Continuously embedded disks with
\pi_{1}=\mathbb{Z}

Although our main results concern
3
-component links, one theme of the paper is that linking data in infinite cyclic covers remains informative even in very weak topological settings. The invariant
h(L)
, introduced in Section 4 and modeled on the Kojima–Yamasaki
\eta
-invariant
[
29
]
, is perhaps the clearest expression of this viewpoint. In the present section, we combine the same perspective with the Blanchfield pairing to show that dropping the local flatness hypothesis does not enlarge the class of knots admitting an embedded disk in
B^{4}
whose complement has fundamental group
\mathbb{Z}
.

Knot concordance is usually studied in either the smooth category or the locally flat category. In these categories, concordance is nontrivial, since not every knot bounds a smooth or locally flat embedded disk in the
4
-ball. This is no longer true without the locally flat condition. Indeed, every knot
K
bounds an embedded disk
D
, the simplest example being the cone on
K
. Observing that

B^{4}\smallsetminus\operatorname{cone}(K)\cong(S^{3}\smallsetminus K)\times[0,\infty),

one sees that the fundamental group of the complement of this disk is isomorphic to the fundamental group of the knot complement. This does not occur in the smooth or locally flat categories in general, and it leads to the following natural question.

Question 7.1
.

For a fixed knot
K
, what restrictions are there on the fundamental groups of complements of disks in
B^{4}
bounded by
K
?

We address this question in the case where the group is
\mathbb{Z}
. It is a famous result of Freedman
[
23
,
22
,
25
]
that a knot bounds a locally flat embedded disk
D\subset B^{4}
with

\pi_{1}(B^{4}\smallsetminus D)\cong\mathbb{Z}

if and only if the knot has trivial Alexander polynomial. In this section, we use the Kojima–Yamasaki
\eta
-invariant and its connection with the Blanchfield form to prove that the same is true if the disk is merely continuously embedded
thereby establishing Theorem
1.7
, whose statement we now recall. For the interested reader, we remark that all of the results we use apply to knots in homology spheres which bound disks in (possibly non-smooth) homology balls. Before applying
[
29
, Lemma 5]
, one may first need to use
[
22
, Theorem 8.2]
to arrange that the complement of a disk in a homology ball admits a smooth structure, even when the homology ball itself does not.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
K\subseteq S^{3}
be a knot. The following are equivalent.

(1)

K
has trivial Alexander polynomial.

(2)

K
bounds a locally flat embedded disk
D\subseteq B^{4}
with
\pi_{1}(B^{4}\smallsetminus D)\cong\mathbb{Z}
.

(3)

K
bounds an embedded disk
D\subseteq B^{4}
with
\pi_{1}(B^{4}\smallsetminus D)\cong\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

As mentioned earlier, Freedman’s theorem gives the equivalence of
(
1
) and (
2
), while
(
2
) clearly implies
(
3
). It therefore remains to prove that
(
3
) implies (
1
).

Assume that
K
bounds an embedded disk
D\subseteq B^{4}
with

\pi_{1}(B^{4}\smallsetminus D)\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Set

W:=B^{4}\smallsetminus D\qquad\text{and}\qquad M:=S^{3}\smallsetminus K\subseteq W.

Let
\widetilde{W}
be the universal cover of
W
. Since

\pi_{1}(W)\cong\mathbb{Z}
, we have

H_{1}(\widetilde{W})=0.

Let
\mathcal{A}(K)
denote the Alexander module of
K
. We will show that the Blanchfield pairing on
\mathcal{A}(K)
, introduced in
[
2
]
, vanishes identically. Since the Blanchfield pairing of a knot is nonsingular (See for example
[
24
, Corollary 1.6]
)
this will imply that
\mathcal{A}(K)=0
, and hence that
\Delta_{K}=1
.

Recall the geometric description of the Blanchfield form. If

\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{A}(K)
are represented by
1
-cycles

a,b\in C_{1}(\widetilde{M})
,
and if
Y\in C_{2}(\widetilde{M})

satisfies

\partial Y=p(t)\,a

for some nonzero

, then

\mathcal{B}\ell(\alpha,\beta)=\left[\frac{1}{p(t)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(Y,t^{k}b)t^{k}\right]\in\mathbb{Q}(t)/\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}].

Next, consider the equivariant intersection pairing

\lambda\colon H_{2}(\widetilde{W})\times H_{2}(\widetilde{W},\widetilde{M})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}];\qquad\lambda(x,y)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(P,t^{k}Q)t^{k},

where
P\in Z_{2}(\widetilde{W})
represents
x
and

Q\in C_{2}(\widetilde{W})
represents
y
, so that

\partial Q\subseteq\widetilde{M}
. Before taking intersections,
we push
P
slightly into the interior of
\widetilde{W}
.
As in Remark
4.3
, it follows from

[
29
, Lemma 5]
that this pairing vanishes identically.

Now let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{A}(K)
. Choose
1
-cycles

a,b\in C_{1}(\widetilde{M})

representing
\alpha
and
\beta
.
Since
H_{1}(\widetilde{W})=0
, there exist
2
-chains

X_{\alpha},X_{\beta}\in C_{2}(\widetilde{W})
such that

\partial X_{\alpha}=a\qquad\text{and}\qquad\partial X_{\beta}=b.

Since
\mathcal{A}(K)
is a torsion
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]
-module, there
exist a nonzero Laurent polynomial
p(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]
and a

2
-chain
Y\in C_{2}(\widetilde{M})
such that

\partial Y=p(t)a.

Set

Z:=Y-p(t)X_{\alpha}\in Z_{2}(\widetilde{W}).

The chain
X_{\beta}
represents a class

[X_{\beta}]\in H_{2}(\widetilde{W},\widetilde{M})
.
The vanishing of the
equivariant intersection pairing
\lambda
gives

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lambda([Z],[X_{\beta}])

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(Y,t^{k}b)t^{k}-p(t)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(X_{\alpha},t^{k}X_{\beta})t^{k}.

Therefore

\left[\frac{1}{p(t)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(Y,t^{k}b)t^{k}\right]=\left[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(X_{\alpha},t^{k}X_{\beta})t^{k}\right]=[0]\in\mathbb{Q}(t)/\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]

By the geometric definition of the
Blanchfield form, the left-hand side is precisely
\mathcal{B}\ell(\alpha,\beta)
.
Thus

\mathcal{B}\ell(\alpha,\beta)=0\qquad\text{for all }\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{A}(K),

which completes the proof.
∎

The proof of the preceding theorem admits a slightly more local form.
Let
D\subset B^{4}
be any continuously embedded disk with boundary
K
, and use the notation

W:=B^{4}\smallsetminus D,\qquad\text{and}\qquad M:=S^{3}\smallsetminus K.

Let
\widetilde{W}
and

denote the infinite cyclic covers of

\mathcal{A}(D)=H_{1}(\widetilde{W})

denote the Alexander module of

, so that every class in
\mathcal{A}(K)
bounds a

the same argument applies precisely to those classes of
\mathcal{A}(K)

that become null-homologous in

, that is, to the kernel of the above map. Since the argument is quite similar to the proof of Theorem

Theorem 7.2
.

If a knot
K
bounds a continuously embedded disk
D
, then

\ker(\mathcal{A}(K)\to\mathcal{A}(D))
is isotropic with respect to the
Blanchfield form. That is, if

\alpha,\beta\in\ker(\mathcal{A}(K)\to\mathcal{A}(D))

\mathcal{B}\ell(\alpha,\beta)=0
. ∎
