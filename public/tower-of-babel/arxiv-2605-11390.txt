Obstructions for Associativity in Stable Homotopy Theory
Sophus Valentin Willumsgaard

Abstract
We give a construction of the obstruction theory for 𝔸 n \mathbb{A}_{n} -algebra structures in stable ∞ \infty -categories, and give some properties of it. We use this to show that the spectrum 𝕊 / 4 \mathbb{S}/4 admits an 𝔸 5 \mathbb{A}_{5} -multiplication using synthetic spectra.

2
\mathbb{A}_{n}
-Operads

There are different notions of an
associative algebra structure on a topological space with a unit map

e:*\to X
.
A topological monoid consists of a map

\mu:X\times X\to X
,
that is strictly associative and unital by satisfying the following identities

\displaystyle\mu(\mu\times 1)=\mu(1\times\mu)

\displaystyle\mu(e\times\id_{X})=\mu(\id_{X}\times e)=\id_{X}.

Another choice is an associative H-space,
which instead of requiring the maps to strictly agree,
only requires them to be homotopic

\displaystyle[\mu(\mu\times 1)]=[\mu(1\times\mu)]

\displaystyle[\mu(e\times\id_{X})]=[\mu(\id_{X}\times e)]=[\id_{X}].

Further,
an
\mathbb{A}_{3}
-algebra is informally an associative H-space
together with a choice of such homotopies.
In homotopy theory,
associative H-space structures are more natural,
when spaces are only defined up to homotopy.
Associative H-spaces however,
do not have the same useful properties as topological monoids have.

Remark 2.1
.

The
\mathbb{A}_{3}
-operad is not coherent in the sense of
[
Lur17
]
,
which is used for giving a
well-defined tensor product on the module category
of an algebra.

The problems with an associative H-space,
can be seen with multiplication of four elements.
Each way of ordering the multiplication,
gives a point in the space of maps

\map_{\textbf{Top}}(X^{\times 4},X)
.
Since the different ordering of multiplications are homotopic,
we can choose paths in the mapping space connecting the different points,
as can be seen in the following diagram.

(ab)(cd)
((ab)c)d
(a(bc))d
a((bc)d)
a(b(cd))

Figure 1:
Space of multiplications of four elements.

From this picture we see that the space of multiplications of four elements,
might not be contractible.
If we want to have a unique multiplication of four elements up to contractible choice,
we need a nulhomotopy of this loop.
We can continue this inductively filling out homotopies in

\map_{\textbf{Top}}(X^{\times n},X)
.
We then get different
associative multiplicative structures on
X
,
depending on how many higher homotopies we require.

This leads to the definition of the
\mathbb{A}_{n}
-operad,
which can be defined from its universal property following

[

]

\displaystyle\fib\left(T(\End_{\mathcal{C}}(A))\to P_{n}^{K_{n}}\right).

From this we get another pullback square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\End_{\mathcal{C}}^{\red}(A)(n)^{K_{n}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\map_{\mathcal{C}}(A^{\otimes n},A)^{K_{n}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T(\End_{\mathcal{C}}^{\red}(A))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T(\End_{\mathcal{C}}(A)),}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

which when pasted with the previous pullback square give the wanted square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\alg_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(\mathcal{C})\times_{\mathcal{C}_{1/}}\{1\xrightarrow{u}A\}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\map_{\mathcal{C}}(A^{\otimes n},A)^{K_{n}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\alg_{\mathbb{A}_{n-1}}(\mathcal{C})\times_{\mathcal{C}_{1/}}\{1\xrightarrow{u}A\}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\map_{\mathcal{C}}(A^{\otimes n},A)^{\partial K_{n}}\times_{\cobound^{A}_{n}(\mathcal{C})^{\partial K_{n}}}\cobound^{A}_{n}(\mathcal{C})^{K_{n}}.}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}

∎

3.3
Obstruction Theory

We will show how
Theorem

3.3

leads to an obstruction theory when
\mathcal{C}
is a presentably monoidal stable
\infty
-category.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let

\mathcal{C}\in\alg(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{St}})

be a
presentably monoidal stable
\infty
-category,
so that the tensor product commutes with small colimits,
and let
u:1_{\mathcal{C}}\to A
be a map from the unit to an object.
We then have a fiber sequence of spaces

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\map_{\mathcal{C}}(A^{\otimes n},A)^{K_{n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\map_{\mathcal{C}}(A^{\otimes n},A)^{\partial K_{n}}\times_{\cobound^{A}_{n}(\mathcal{C})^{\partial K_{n}}}\cobound^{A}_{n}(\mathcal{C})^{K_{n}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces*}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\map_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma^{n-3}(A/u)^{\otimes n},A).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

Proof.

Since

\mathcal{C}

is a presentably stable
\infty
-category,
it is left-tensored over
\Sp

and further enriched over
\Sp
by
[
Lur17
, Proposition 4.2.1.33]
.
In this case we have equivalences of spectra

\displaystyle\map_{\mathcal{C}}(A^{\otimes n},A)^{K_{n}}\simeq\map_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma^{\infty}K_{n}\otimes A^{\otimes n},A)

\displaystyle\cobound_{n}^{A}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\map_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\colim_{I\in\mathcal{P}(n)^{\op}_{\leq n-1}}A^{\otimes|I|},A\right).

We denote the colimit inside by
A^{\cob}
.
The upper horizontal map in the sequence is then pre-composition with the map

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\infty}\partial K_{n}\otimes A^{\otimes n}\amalg_{\Sigma^{\infty}\partial K_{n}\otimes A^{\cob}}\Sigma^{\infty}K_{n}\otimes A^{\cob}\to\Sigma^{\infty}K_{n}\otimes A^{\otimes n}.

We can then calculate the fiber of this map

\displaystyle\Sigma^{-1}\left(\Sigma^{\infty}(K_{n}/\partial K_{n})\otimes(A^{\otimes n}/A^{\cob})\right)\cong\mathbb{S}^{n-3}\otimes(A/u)^{\otimes n}\simeq\Sigma^{n-3}(A/u)^{\otimes n}.

From this we get the cofiber of the horizontal map in the sequence
is given by

\displaystyle\map_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma^{n-3}(A/u)^{\otimes n},A).

It follows that the above sequence is a fiber/cofiber sequence in spectra,
and so is a fiber sequence in spaces, as the functor
\Omega^{\infty}

is limit-preserving.
∎

We can now construct the obstruction theory.

Proposition 3.12
.

Given
\mathcal{C}\in\alg(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{St}})

and an
\mathbb{A}_{n-1}
-algebra
A
for
n\geq 2

there exists an obstruction

\displaystyle\theta_{n}\in[\Sigma^{n-3}(A/u)^{\otimes n},A],

such that
\theta_{n}
is nulhomotopic if and only if

A
can be extended to an
\mathbb{A}_{n}
-algebra.
If
A
is the cofiber of a map
X\to 1_{\mathcal{C}}

then the obstruction lies in

\displaystyle\theta_{n}\in[\Sigma^{2n-3}(X)^{\otimes n},A].

Proof.

Given an
\mathbb{A}_{n-1}
-algebra
A
in
\mathcal{C}

with unit
u:1_{\mathcal{C}}\to A
,
we have a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\alg_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(\mathcal{C})\times_{\mathcal{C}_{1/}}\{1\xrightarrow{u}A\}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\map(A^{\otimes n},A)^{K_{n}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\alg_{\mathbb{A}_{n-1}}(\mathcal{C})\times_{\mathcal{C}_{1/}}\{1\xrightarrow{u}A\}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\map(A^{\otimes n},A)^{\partial K_{n}}\times_{\cobound_{n}^{A}(\mathcal{C})^{\partial K_{n}}}\cobound^{A}_{n}(\mathcal{C})^{K_{n}}.}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}

From
Theorem

3.3
,
giving an extension of
A
to an
\mathbb{A}_{n}
-algebra,
is equivalent to a lift of
\beta(A)
to

\map_{\mathcal{C}}(A^{\otimes n},A)^{K_{n}}
.
Since

\map_{\mathcal{C}}(A^{\otimes n},A)^{K_{n}}

is the fiber of the map

\displaystyle\map_{\mathcal{C}}(A^{\otimes n},A)^{\partial K_{n}}\times_{\cobound_{n}^{A}(\mathcal{C})^{\partial K_{n}}}\cobound_{n}^{A}(\mathcal{C})^{K_{n}}\xrightarrow{q}\map_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma^{n-3}(A/u)^{\otimes n},A),

the space of extensions of
A
to an
\mathbb{A}_{n}
-algebra structure
is equivalent to space of nulhomotopies of
q\beta(A)
,
so
q\beta(A)
is an obstruction for extending
A

to an
\mathbb{A}_{n}
-algebra.

∎

Remark 3.13
.

Given an object
A\in\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}
with a map

1_{\mathcal{C}}\to A
,
it automatically has the structure of an
\mathbb{A}_{1}
-algebra,
so the obstruction
\theta_{2}
is defined.
If it vanishes,
A can be given a structure of an
\mathbb{A}_{2}
-algebra,
and such a choice gives the obstruction
\theta_{3}
.
In this way, one can inductively construct obstructions
\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{n}
for the existence of an
\mathbb{A}_{n}
-structure on an
object.

4
Applying Obstruction Theory to the Universal Case

In the previous section we defined an obstruction theory,
for the existence of
\mathbb{A}_{n}
-structures.
In this section,
we construct a stable symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category,
classifying maps from strict elements to the unit.
We then apply the obstruction theory in this
\infty
-category,
to give general results on when the obstructions vanish.

4.1
Locally Graded Stable
\infty
-Categories

We recall the notion of a locally graded stable
\infty
-category
from
[
Lur15
]
, and adapt to the setting of
{\rm Phys.~Rev.~Lett.}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Consider the
\infty
-category
\Sp^{\mathbb{N}}\in\calg(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{St}})
,
with symmetric monoidal structure induced by
\mathbb{N}
from the
stable Yoneda embedding, and let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category.
We then have an equivalence

\displaystyle\map_{\calg(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{St}})}(\Sp^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{C})\simeq\map_{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}(\mathbb{N},\mathcal{C}).

Proof.

This follows from
[
Lur17
, Proposition 4.8.1.10 & Corollary 4.8.1.14]

along with
[
Lur15
, Proposition 2.3.7]
.
∎

Definition 4.2
.

The
\infty
-category
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\gd}_{0\leq}

of locally graded stable presentable
\infty
-categories
is defined as

\rmod_{\Sp^{\mathbb{N}}}\left(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{St}}\right)
.

Remark 4.3
.

Given a strict element

C\in\map_{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}(\mathbb{N},\mathcal{C})
,
from
proposition

4.1
we get a symmetric monoidal functor

\Sp^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathcal{C}
,
which then gives
\mathcal{C}
the structure of a commutative algebra
in
\rmod_{\Sp^{\mathbb{N}}}(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{St}})
.

Notation 4.4
.

We let
\mathbb{S}(n)
denote the graded spectrum,
with the
n
’th index given by
\mathbb{S}
,
and the rest being
0
.
In general for any object
C
in a

\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\gd}_{0\leq}
,
we define
C(n):=\mathbb{S}(n)\otimes C
.

We can now construct an
\infty
-category classifying maps to the unit.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\mathbb{S}\{v\}\in\calg(\Sp^{\mathbb{N}})

be the free
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-algebra on a generator
\mathbb{S}(1)
.

Lemma 4.6
.

The symmetric monoidal locally graded

\infty
-category

\rmod_{\mathbb{S}\{v\}}(\Sp^{\mathbb{N}})\in\calg\left(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\gd}_{0\leq}\right)

has the universal property,
that for any other

\mathcal{C}\in\calg\left(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\gd}_{0\leq}\right)

we have

\displaystyle\fun^{\otimes}_{\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\gd}_{0\leq}}\big(\rmod_{\mathbb{S}\{v\}}(\Sp^{\mathbb{N}}),\mathcal{C}\big)\simeq\map_{\mathcal{C}}(1_{\mathcal{C}}(1),1_{\mathcal{C}}).

Proof.

From
[
Lur17
, Remark 4.8.5.12]
,
along with
\Sp^{\mathbb{N}}
being the unit of
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\gd}_{0\leq}
,
we get an adjunction

\displaystyle\fun^{\otimes}_{\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\gd}_{0\leq}}\left(\rmod_{B}(\Sp^{\mathbb{N}}),\mathcal{C}\right)\simeq\map_{\calg(\Sp^{\mathbb{N}})}\left(B,\End^{\gd}(\mathcal{C})\right),

where
\End^{\gd}
is the graded algebra of endomorphisms of the unit
described in
[
Lur15
, Remark 2.4.9]
.
For
\mathbb{S}\{v\}
we then get

\displaystyle\fun^{\otimes}_{\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\gd}_{0\leq}}\left(\rmod_{\mathbb{S}\{v\}}(\Sp^{\mathbb{N}}),\mathcal{C}\right)\simeq\map_{\calg(\Sp^{\mathbb{N}})}\left(\mathbb{S}\{v\},\End^{\gd}(\mathcal{C})\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\map_{\Sp^{\mathbb{N}}}\left(\mathbb{S}(1),\End^{\gd}(\mathcal{C})\right)\simeq\map_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}}(1),\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\right).

∎

Remark 4.7
.

Explicitly given a map
\varphi:1_{\mathcal{C}}(1)\to 1_{\mathcal{C}}
,
the functor

\rmod_{\mathbb{S}\{v\}}(\Sp^{\mathbb{N}})\to\mathcal{C}

sends the map
\cdot v:\mathbb{S}\{v\}(1)\to\mathbb{S}\{v\}
adjoint
to
v:\mathbb{S}(1)\to\mathbb{S}\{v\}
to
\varphi
.
The quotient

\mathbb{S}\{v\}/(\cdot v)
is then also sent to

1_{\mathcal{C}}/\varphi
.

It follows that any algebra structure on

\mathbb{S}\{v\}/(\cdot v)

will induce the same structure
on any cofiber of a strict element.
Asking for algebraic structures in this generality
is too much, as for example
\mathbb{S}/2

does not even admit an
\mathbb{A}_{2}
-structure,
even though
\mathbb{S}
is strict, as it is the unit.
Instead we can modify our universal example,
to get a universal algebra structure in specific cases.

4.2

The first modification we consider,

Same as

The map

Since

is invertible in
\mathbb{F}_{p}

so

is an equivalence.
∎

.

∎
