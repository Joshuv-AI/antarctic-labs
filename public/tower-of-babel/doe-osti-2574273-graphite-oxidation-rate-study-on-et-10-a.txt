 SUMMARY
 Idaho National Laboratory (INL) performed targeted oxidation tests to
measure oxidation rates for samples of ET-10 and ETU-10 graphite under
CRADA No. 21CRA22 Mod. 3, Annex A, “Tritium Testing to Support Kairos
Power Advanced Reactor Demonstration” (04/02/2024). All testing was
conducted within INL’s Carbon Characterization Laboratory using test standard
ASTM-D7542-21, “Standard Test Method for Air Oxidation of Carbon and
Graphite in the Kinetic Regime” [ASTM International, 2021]. Kairos Power
provided all test specimens through its graphite vendor Ibiden, Inc., to INL and
ASTM specimen specified dimensions. Information within this report only
provides the Arrhenius oxidation rate plots as a function of temperature for each
graphite grade tested. The raw mass loss per time data will be provided on the
Nuclear Data Management and Analysis System portal located on the INL
information system.

 1. EQUIPMENT, METHODS, AND MATERIALS
 Oxidation rate testing was conducted using the recommended testing equipment, specimen
dimensions, and oxidation environments for kinetic-controlled oxidation (i.e., TOxid below ~750℃)
specified within ASTM D7542-15, “Standard Test Method for Air Oxidation of Carbon and Graphite in
the Kinetic Regime,” [ASTM International, 2015]. These ASTM D7542 specifications are listed in
Table 1.
Table 1. Oxidation testing conditions used in study, as specified by ASTM D7542.
 Parameter Specification
 Specimen geometry Right cylinder
 Specimen size Ø25.4 mm × 25.4 mm
 Gas flow rate 10 l/min
 Gas composition 100% dry air
 Oxidation temperatures 550, 650, and 700℃
 (Additional samples were available for ET-10; consequently, testing was
 also performed at 750℃)
 Mass loss range 0% to ~10%, with rates based on performance between 5% and 10% mass
 loss as prescribed in the standard ASTM D7542-21

 All testing was performed in a vertical furnace apparatus, as illustrated in Figure 1. The
Ø25.4 mm × 25.4 mm graphite specimens were suspended within the middle center of the vertical
furnace. Inert nitrogen cover gas was used to shield the specimens from tramp air during heating and
cooling of the furnace. Once the desired oxidation temperatures were achieved, the specimens were
exposed to 10 l/min air flow. After the desired mass loss was achieved, the air was automatically turned
off, and the chamber was flooded with nitrogen cover gas while the furnace cooled to 350℃ ending
electronic data acquisition. The furnace was subsequently cooled in 10 l/min air to near ambient
conditions to enable sample removal and, where applicable, sample placement for the start of the next test
run.

 1

Figure 1. Schematic of oxidation apparatus.

 The sample mass and furnace operating conditions were recorded over time to enable determination
of the isothermal oxidation rate for each sample tested. The raw data for all tests will be provided on the
Nuclear Data Management and Analysis System (NDMAS) portal, located on the Idaho National
Laboratory (INL) information system (https://ndmas.inl.gov/SitePages/NDMAS_Pages_Home.aspx).
Oxidation rates were calculated for each sample by plotting the sample mass (as a percentage of initial
mass measured at temperature in flowing nitrogen) with time and taking the average slope between 5%
and 10% mass loss (quasi steady-state rate) [Smith 2021]. Both the mass and surface area normalized
oxidation rates for with- and against-grain samples for each graphite grade were regression fit across the
entire test temperature range. These Arrhenius relationships are illustrated for each of the graphite grade
and grain direction subset tested within section 2.0. Calibration data for all testing periods are confirmed
in Appendix A.

 2. OXIDATION RESULTS
 Isothermal oxidation under ASTM D7542-21 employs experimental controls for known
environmental influences on graphite oxidation rates. This standard was developed to enable
determination of grade-to-grade differences in the degradation rates of graphite and manufactured carbon
materials. This experiment also examined with-grain versus against-grain directionality in test specimens
for the ETU-10 and ET-10 grades tested. Test results are summarized graphically in Figure 2. Grain
directionality was not observed to have any measurable effect on the oxidation rate for either grade.
Supporting data for the individual grades are addressed in Subsections 2.1 and 2.2.
 The source data identified herein from oxidation testing of these ETU-10 and ET-10 graphite samples
were collected and analyzed in accordance with INL PLN-6904, “Vertical Furnace Graphite Oxidation
Plan,” (Revs 0 and 1). This test program has been conducted under the requirements of INL’s
PDD-13000, “Quality Assurance Program Description,” (Revs 9, 10, and 11). Area normalized oxidation
rates (in g/h/m2) were compared between (1) analog analysis employing an Excel template with data
imported from source (LabVIEW recorded data) files and (2) a custom automated analysis program
accessing the same source files. In all cases, the calculated variance rates were within 2%. For those cases
where small differences were observed, the automated program calculates slightly higher (that is, more
conservative) rates. This variance reflects excellent agreement, well within typical run-to-run variations
for replicate test conditions. The rate data provided in this report have been validated on this basis.

 2.1. ETU-10 Graphite
 While both grades tested are candidate materials for nuclear applications, ETU-10 graphite is more
rigorously purified than ET-10 graphite. Data for each ETU-10 sample tested are provided in Table 2.
 There were experimental complications with certain ETU-10 test runs. Sample ETU-10 AG#3 was
tested in two parts after the initial test (Part 1) was interrupted to accommodate a facility outage.
Oxidation run (Part 2) was completed after the outage. The data from Part 1 was stitched together with the
data from Part 2 and used for analog analysis. Sample ETU-10 AG#3 replicates conditions intended for
ETU-10 AG#1. Air supply for ETU-10 AG#1 was inadequate to maintain the requisite 10 l/min flow
(average was 9.4 l/m during oxidation), therefore no test data are provided for sample ETU-10 AG#1. The
accidental use of a backslash character prevented proper data acquisition during the test run for ETU-10
AG Test 06132023; there, data was recovered via screen capture and analyzed via the analog
methodology. Sample ETU-10 AG test 06132023 was a replication of the run of sample ETU-10 AG#5
that produced properly formatted data with minor evidence of interference (lid contact with the
suspension chain). Unfortunately, screen capture and stitched together source data files are not formatted
properly for automated analysis and access through the NDMAS. The calculated rates for ETU-10 AG
Test 06132023 and for ETU-10 AG#5 are statistically equivalent, but because of the formatting
inconsistencies, oxidation data for ETU-10 AG Test 06132023 will not be made available via NDMAS.
Similarly, calculated rates for ETU AG#1 and ETU AG#3 are statistically equivalent, but data for ETU
AG#3 will not be made available in NDMAS.
