EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Permselective ion-containing membranes are an integral component for many applications from
water treatment, fuel cells, and solar fuels devices where the selective transport of molecules and
ions is desired. In solar fuels devices, ion-containing polymer membranes are responsible for
permitting selective transport of ions between electrodes to maintain overall charge neutrality yet
limit transport of reaction products produced at the electrodes. While the transport of single solutes
through such membranes has been fairly well described, binary and multicomponent transport is
poorly understood due to the myriad of interactions that occur in these systems (i.e. between co-
permeants and between permeants and the membrane). Solar fuels devices are just one example of
an application where understanding the transport of multiple simultaneous species is critically
important to improving device performance as product crossover leads to reductions in overall
device performance. The objectives of this research was to improve our understanding of the
complex array of factors that influence transport behavior of multiple solutes within ion-containing
polymer membranes. This experimental project addressed the lack of fundamental understanding
of multicomponent transport behavior by synthesizing ion exchange membranes with varied
incorporation of comonomers (ionic and neutral moieties) to investigate fundamental relationships
between membrane structure, membrane physiochemical properties, and transport behavior of
solutes and complex solute mixtures through dense, hydrated membranes.
 Importantly, we leveraged an in situ spectroscopic methodology to perform multi-
component diffusion cell experiments with high fidelity which greatly reduces the experimental
burden and complexity compared to traditional multicomponent experiments. The impact of the
physicochemical properties of the synthesized polymer membranes on the multicomponent,
emergent transport properties was evaluated and used to inform key structure-property
relationships towards the development of robust membranes with desired transport behavior with
a particular focus will be on the interplay of membrane chemistry and complex mixture chemistry
on emergent transport behavior.
 Experimental tasks in this project resulted in (1) fabrication of a diverse set of ion exchange
membranes with a variety of chemical composition, water uptake, and ionic conductivity, (2)
experimental characterization of the transport behavior of solutes and their complex mixtures, and
(3) new understanding of the relationships between membrane chemistry and transport behavior
of solutes and their complex mixtures.

Training and Professional Development
The project supported the effort of three Chemical Engineering Ph.D. students, as well as one
Polymer & Fiber Engineering M.S. thesis student. All trainees were mentored by the PI in polymer
chemistry, polymer characterization, membrane fabrication, and membrane characterization,
giving them an inter-disciplinary set of foundational skills. In addition to technical skills mentees
were provided with professional development included conference and workshop attendance, as
well as career mentoring and counseling from the PI.

Research Program and Outcomes
The goal of this project was to enhance understanding of the relationships between membrane structure,
physiochemical properties, and transport behavior of solutes (methanol, ethanol, acetate, formate and
ammonium) and their complex mixtures. Importantly, we had developed an in situ spectroscopic
methodology to perform multi-component diffusion cell experiments with high fidelity that greatly
reduces the experimental burden and complexity compared to traditional multicomponent experiments.
Using this membrane transport platform and a tunable membrane design strategy based on the UV
photopolymerization of diacrylates, acrylates, and acrylamides for controlling polymer membrane
structure, bound ion content, and water uptake the research investigated fundamental structure-
property relationships for dense, hydrated membranes of importance for a range of applications
including electrochemical CO2 reduction cells, direct urea fuel cells, and other energy systems.

The research results from this project have been distributed to the community in the form of invited
talks and contributed presentations, as well as well as peer-reviewed journal articles. 5 invited talks
and 21 contributed presentations were given that incorporated research from this project. 10
journal articles have been published to date on this project. In addition, 1 additional manuscript is
in the final stages of internal review for submission to a peer-reviewed journal and we anticipate
an additional 1 manuscript to be drafted in the coming months based on already completed
experiments.
Collectively, our research has established a comprehensive body of work focused on understanding
and engineering charged polymer membranes for electrochemical energy systems, ion separations,

and coupled transport applications. Across the studies, a central contribution is the development
of rigorous structure–property–transport relationships that connect polymer chemistry, membrane
morphology, hydration behavior, and crosslinking architecture to ion and solvent transport
phenomena. Our findings consistently demonstrate that membrane performance cannot be
understood through single-variable effects alone; instead, transport behavior emerges from
complex interactions among hydrophilic ionic groups, hydrophobic domains, water uptake,
polymer rigidity, and coupled solute interactions. By systematically isolating and manipulating
these variables, we have sought to provide fundamental insight into how membrane chemistry
governs selectivity, conductivity, co-transport, and stability and then used these insights to design
membrane chemistries for application in real device architectures.

A major theme that we found to be useful for the class of membranes investigated is the strategic
incorporation of hydrophobic phenyl-acrylate functionalities in conjunction with a tailored
crosslinking chemistry to balance ionic transport with dimensional and chemical stability. We have
shown that hydrophobic modifications can effectively suppress undesired crossover of alcohols
and organic species while maintaining sufficient hydration and ionic conductivity for
electrochemical operation. Our parallel investigations into zwitterionic materials, quaternary
ammonium systems, and organogels further expanded understanding of how charge density,
hydration, and polymer architecture influence transport of carboxylates, ammonium, acetate,
formate, methanol, ethanol, and salts. Together, these works have helped to significantly advance
mechanistic understanding of coupled ion-solvent transport in charged, dense, hydrated polymer
membranes. Several of our studies moved beyond fundamental membrane characterization to
evaluate membrane performance in realistic electrochemical devices, showing how optimized
membrane design can improve fuel cell efficiency, reduce crossover losses, and enhance long-term
operational stability. These extensions were not originally envisioned for the project, but were
collaborative opportunities that arose from interactions presenting our research both at AU internal
events and at conferences.

The following provides a brief technical summary for each of these product outcomes from the
research and full citations for all products are provided at the end of this document.

1. Fabrication and characterization of cross-linked phenyl-acrylate-based ion exchange
membranes and performance in a direct urea fuel cell (Kim et al., 2021)
This publication reported the fabrication and characterization of crosslinked phenyl-acrylate-based
ion exchange membranes for direct urea fuel cells. The membranes exhibited favorable chemical
stability, mechanical integrity, and ionic conductivity while reducing undesirable fuel crossover.
The incorporation of phenyl-acrylate functionality improved dimensional stability and membrane
durability under fuel cell operating conditions. Electrochemical testing demonstrated that these
membranes could support efficient urea fuel cell performance, establishing phenyl-acrylate-based
materials as promising candidates for sustainable electrochemical energy technologies.

2. Transport and co-transport of carboxylate ions and ethanol in anion exchange
membranes (Kim et al., 2021)
This study examined the coupled transport behavior of carboxylate ions and ethanol in anion
exchange membranes. We demonstrated that ethanol presence substantially influences ion
transport behavior by modifying membrane swelling and solvent-mediated transport mechanisms.

Membrane composition and ionic functionality controlled both ion permeability and ethanol
crossover, revealing strong coupling between neutral solvent transport and charged species
migration. The work advanced understanding of multi-species transport in charged membranes
and underscored the importance of considering co-transport effects when designing membranes
for electrochemical and separation processes.

3. Impact of hydrophobic pendent phenyl groups on transport and co-transport of methanol
and acetate in PEGDA-SPMAK cation exchange membranes (Kim et al., 2022)
This manuscript explored how hydrophobic pendent phenyl groups affect methanol and acetate
transport in PEGDA-SPMAK cation exchange membranes. We found that incorporation of
hydrophobic aromatic moieties significantly reduced methanol permeability while altering acetate
transport and co-transport behavior. The phenyl groups modified membrane free volume, water
uptake, and polymer-solute interactions, leading to improved selectivity between ionic and neutral
species. The study established key relationships between membrane hydrophobicity and coupled
transport phenomena, providing valuable insight into membrane optimization for electrochemical
systems where fuel crossover limits efficiency.

4. Improved Structural Stability of Charged Hydrogels under Organic CO2 Reduction
Products: Effect of Acrylate and Methacrylate Backbone Linkages (Kim et al., 2023)
This work examined the structural stability of charged hydrogels exposed to organic products
generated during CO2 reduction processes. We compared acrylate- and methacrylate-based
polymer backbones and found that methacrylate-containing hydrogels exhibited improved
resistance to swelling, degradation, and mechanical instability under harsh organic-rich
environments. The enhanced stability was attributed to differences in backbone rigidity and
polymer network structure. These findings demonstrated the importance of polymer backbone
chemistry in determining membrane durability and highlighted strategies for designing ion
exchange materials capable of withstanding chemically aggressive electrochemical operating
conditions.

5. Salt Transport in Crosslinked Hydrogel Membranes Containing Zwitterionic Sulfobetaine
Methacrylate and Hydrophobic Phenyl Acrylate (Lin, Kim, & Beckingham, 2023)
This study investigated salt transport in hydrogel membranes incorporating both zwitterionic
sulfobetaine methacrylate and hydrophobic phenyl acrylate components. The results showed that
introducing zwitterionic functionality altered water structuring and ionic interactions within the
membrane, while hydrophobic phenyl groups modulated swelling and transport pathways.
Together, these competing effects enabled tuning of salt permeability and selectivity. The work
demonstrated that combining zwitterionic and hydrophobic chemistries provides a versatile
approach for controlling ion transport in hydrogel membranes and contributes to broader
understanding of multifunctional membrane design.

6. Development of Structure–Property Relationships for Ammonium Transport through
Charged Organogels (Bachmann, Hunter, & Beckingham, 2024)
In this study, we explored ammonium transport through charged organogels and developed
predictive structure–property relationships governing transport behavior. The work demonstrated
that polymer charge density, solvent uptake, and organogel morphology strongly influence
ammonium diffusivity and partitioning. By systematically varying membrane composition, we

identified how electrostatic interactions and solvent-mediated transport mechanisms contribute to
ammonium mobility. The findings advanced understanding of ion transport in soft charged
materials and provided design principles for membranes used in separations, environmental
remediation, and electrochemical applications involving ammonium-containing streams.

7. Impact of a novel Nickel-Based catalyst and Phenyl-Acrylate-Based Anion-Exchange
membrane in a direct urea fuel cell (Yoon et al., 2024)
This publication evaluated the combined influence of a newly developed nickel-based catalyst and
phenyl-acrylate-based anion exchange membranes on direct urea fuel cell performance. The study
demonstrated that the membrane architecture significantly affected ionic conductivity, fuel
crossover, and overall electrochemical efficiency. The phenyl-acrylate-based membranes showed
enhanced chemical stability and reduced undesirable transport of fuel species compared with
conventional systems. Coupled with a novel catalyst developed by our collaborators, the optimized
membrane enabled improved fuel cell power density and operational performance compared to a
commercially-available membrane used in this application. The work highlighted the importance
of simultaneously engineering both catalyst and membrane materials to advance sustainable urea-
based energy conversion technologies.

8. Single and dual-solute transport in quaternary ammonium-functionalized anion exchange
membranes: Isolating the impact of water volume fraction (Lin & Beckingham, 2025)
This manuscript focused on understanding how water volume fraction governs both single-solute
and coupled solute transport in quaternary ammonium-functionalized anion exchange membranes.
By isolating water content effects from other membrane variables, the authors showed that water
uptake serves as a dominant parameter controlling ionic conductivity, diffusivity, and co-transport
behavior. Increased water volume fraction generally enhanced ion mobility but also altered
selectivity and coupled transport phenomena between ions and neutral species. The study
emphasized that membrane hydration fundamentally dictates transport mechanisms in charged
polymer systems and provided quantitative insight into how tuning membrane water content can
optimize membrane performance for electrochemical separations and energy devices.

9. Anion exchange membranes based on APTA and phenyl acrylate: Impact of crosslinker
and crosslinker content on physiochemical properties and transport behavior of acetate and
formate (Lin, Clinch, & Beckingham, 2025)
This work investigated anion exchange membranes synthesized from (3-acrylamidopropyl)
trimethylammonium chloride (APTA) and phenyl acrylate, with particular emphasis on the role of
crosslinker chemistry and crosslink density. The authors demonstrated that increasing crosslinking
generally reduced swelling and improved dimensional stability, while also influencing acetate and
formate transport behavior. Membrane transport selectivity was strongly tied to the balance
between membrane rigidity, water uptake, and ionic domain connectivity. Incorporation of
hydrophobic phenyl acrylate segments further modified ion transport pathways and reduced
excessive solvent uptake. The study provided a detailed framework for tailoring membrane
chemistry to achieve improved selectivity and stability in carboxylate transport applications.

10. Understanding the Impact of SPMAK and PEGPEA in Crosslinked PEGDA
Membranes: Methanol-Carboxylate Co-Transport Behavior and Correlating Structure-
Physicochemical-Transport Properties (Aravindhan et al., 2025)

This study examined how incorporation of sulfopropyl methacrylate potassium salt (SPMAK) and
polyethylene glycol phenyl ether acrylate (PEGPEA) influences the structure and transport
behavior of crosslinked PEGDA-based cation exchange membranes. The authors demonstrated
that balancing hydrophilic ionic functionality with hydrophobic aromatic moieties strongly affects
methanol uptake, ion conductivity, and methanol-carboxylate co-transport. Increased SPMAK
content enhanced ionic transport through greater water uptake and charge density, while PEGPEA
incorporation reduced methanol crossover by increasing membrane hydrophobicity and altering
free volume characteristics. The work established important structure–property–transport
relationships linking membrane composition to selective transport performance, highlighting
strategies for improving membranes used in electrochemical and separations applications where
alcohol crossover is detrimental.
