Synthesis, Characterization, and Multifaceted Biological Applications of Novel Binuclear Mn(II), Fe(III), and Co(II) Malonohydrazone Schiff-Base Complexes
Authors: Ali H. Al-Hajooj, Mahdi A. Mohammed, Magdy M. Youssef, Gaber M. Abu El-Reash
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Novel Mn­(II), Fe­(III) and Co­(II) binuclear complexes of 2-benzyl-N′3-((2-hydroxynaphthalen-1-yl)­methylene)-N′1-((E)-(2-hydroxynaphthalen-yl)­methylene)­malonohydrazide (H4L2) were synthesized. Full structural characterization of the studied compounds was accomplished through an integrated approach employing FT-IR spectroscopy, NMR, EI-MS, PXRD, UV–vis spectroscopy, magnetic susceptibility, and thermogravimetric analysis. Geometry optimization was performed by employing the DMol3 module within the framework of density functional theory. The spectroscopic analysis discovered the H4L2 functions as a dianionic hexadentate Schiff base (O4N2) coordinating each Mn­(II) center through the oxygen of carbonyl groups, (CN)az nitrogen atoms, and the deprotonated naphthalic (OH) oxygen atoms in the [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O complex. In the infrared spectra of the [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O and [Fe2(L2)­(H2O)4Cl2]·2H2O complexes, the ligand behaves as a tetra-anionic hexadentate (O4N2), bonding the metal center through the enolized carbonyl group, the naphthalic OH, and the azomethine nitrogen atoms, as depicted in the proposed coordination structure. The cytotoxicity screening data reveal that the investigated compounds demonstrate selective anticancer activity, with distinct growth inhibitory effects against specific human cancer cell lines, including WI-38, HePG-2, HeLa, HEP-2, PC-3, MCF-7, and HCT-116. The [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O and [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O complexes displayed strong degradation, indicative of potent DNA interaction and cleavage capability. The electrophoretic profile demonstrates a complete protein degradation effect for the [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O complex. In particular, the [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O complex displayed the highest antioxidant potency with an IC50 of 0.0231 μM. The antimicrobial results demonstrated that the [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O complex exhibited the most potent activity against Escherichia coli, Klebsiella pneumonia, Staphylococcus aureus, Bacillus subtilis, Candida albicans, and Aspergillus flavus with inhibition zones of 17, 14, 11, 12, 9, and 10 nm, respectively. Molecular docking simulations against HePG-2 (PDB ID: 5EQG) and MCF-7 receptors (PDB ID: 6NM0) indicated favorable binding affinities for the investigated compounds.

Introduction

Coordination chemistry studies the complexes of transition metals and ligands. The partially filled d orbitals of the metals enable diverse bonding, structures, and electronic features, forming the basis for their broad applications. Schiff-base ligands, characterized by their nitrogen and oxygen donor atoms, are pivotal components in redox chemistry. They function as versatile catalysts and intermediates, facilitating critical processes such as the electron reduction of ketones and the oxidation of a wide range of organic compounds, including industrial dyes, pigments, and polymeric materials. ,

Schiff bases derived from 2-hydroxy naphthaldehyde have garnered significant interest among chemists due to their ability to coordinate with metal ions and their structural versatility. Moreover, these derivatives exhibit promising biological and therapeutic applications. Hydrazones constitute a distinct class of Schiff-base compounds that display a remarkable range of physiological and therapeutic activities. Their pharmacological profile is characterized by antitubercular, antifungal, antiradical, anticancer, antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and antimalarial efficacy. − Transition metal chelates incorporating nitrogen and oxygen donor ligands have garnered substantial scientific interest due to their remarkable versatility. This stems from their wide-ranging applications, which include serving as selective separation materials, efficient catalysts, functional models for enzymatic active sites, and compounds with novel and intriguing structural architectures. −

Furthermore, Parvarinezhad et al. synthesized Mn­(III), Co­(III), and Zn­(II) hydrazone complexes derived from 2-hydroxy-1-naphthaldehyde. Their structures were validated through single-crystal X-ray diffraction and various spectroscopic techniques. Additionally, molecular docking studies assessed their binding affinity to the B-cell lymphoma protein (PDB ID: 4LXD). As a continuation of our 2-benzylmalonohydrazide Schiff base, we have synthesized novel hydrazone complexes and characterized their coordination behavior toward Mn­(II), Fe­(III), and Co­(II) metal ions. Notably, the synthesized compounds extend beyond the isolated biological assessments reported in prior studies , by exhibiting a comprehensive suite of bioactivities. The Mn­(II), Fe­(III), and Co­(II) chelates demonstrate potent anticancer, antibacterial, and antioxidant potencies, alongside DNA cleavage and bovine serum albumin (BSA) binding capabilities. Molecular docking simulations further elucidate specific receptor–ligand interactions, substantiating the therapeutic potential of these complexes. Collectively, this study offers a characteristic contribution by the confluence of an innovative ligand design, versatile coordination chemistry, and multifaceted biological efficacy, thereby differentiating it from existing literature and advancing the field of medicinal inorganic chemistry.

Experimental Section

Synthesis of the H4L2 Ligand and Mn­(II), Fe­(III), and Co­(II) Complexes

The tetradentate Schiff base H4L2 was synthesized by refluxing a 1:2 molar mixture of 2-benzylmalonohydrazide (2.22 g) and 2-hydroxy-1-naphthaldehyde (3.44 g) in ethanol, following the synthetic route outlined in Scheme . The reaction mixture was refluxed at 90 °C for 4 h. The resulting beige precipitate was isolated by filtration, recrystallized from ethanol, and dried under vacuum over anhydrous CaCl2. The purity of the ligand was verified by TLC. The corresponding Mn­(II), Fe­(III), and Co­(II) chelates were synthesized by the reaction of H4L2 (0.530 g, 1 mmol) with the appropriate metal salt (2 mmol), 0.396 g of MnCl2·4H2O, 0.324 g of FeCl3, or 0.259 g of CoCl2, in ethanol, as depicted in Scheme . Synthesis of Investigated Compounds

Density Functional Theory Calculations

Geometry optimizations of the ligands and their metal complexes were performed using the DMol3 module within the Materials Studio software package, based on density functional theory (DFT) calculations. − No symmetry constraints were applied during the optimization process. A double numerical plus polarization basis set was employed, together with the generalized gradient approximation and the RPBE exchange–correlation functional, which is currently considered optimal for estimating exchange and correlation effects in electron systems. , All calculations were performed in the gas phase, without the application of any explicit or implicit solvation model. Convergence criteria were set to 1 × 10–5 Ha for energy, 2 × 10–3 Ha/Å for the maximum force, and 5 × 10–3 Å for the maximum displacement. The self-consistent field (SCF) procedure was carried out with a convergence tolerance of 1 × 10–6 Ha, a maximum of 200 SCF cycles, and an orbital smearing of 0.005 Ha to facilitate convergence. The chemical reactivity descriptors were evaluated using the HOMO and LUMO energy values of the ligand and its corresponding metal complexes. , The reactivity parameters can be calculated from the HOMO and LUMO energies using the following equations − χ(electronegativity)=−1/2(ELUMO+EHOMO) μ(potential)=−χ=1/2(ELUMO+EHOMO) η(hardness)=1/2(ELUMO−EHOMO) S(softness)=1/2η ω(electrophilicity)=μ2/2η

Biological Evaluations

Antimicrobial Potency

Antimicrobial activity was evaluated using a standardized agar well diffusion assay. , A standardized inoculum of each test strain was homogeneously distributed on the surface of nutrient agar plates. A 9 mm diameter well was aseptically created in the agar, into which 100 μL of the test solution was dispensed. Following incubation under optimal conditions for the specific microorganism, the antimicrobial agent spread through the medium, producing a measurable zone of growth inhibition. The diameter of this inhibition zone was used to quantify the antimicrobial efficacy of each compound against the selected bacterial and fungal strains.

Antioxidant Activity Using the DPPH Assay

The radical-scavenging potential of the synthesized compounds was screened using the 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) colorimetric assay, with ascorbic acid employed as a positive control. In brief, a methanolic solution of DPPH (0.135 mM) was added to serial dilutions of each test sample. The reaction mixtures were incubated in the dark at ambient temperature for 30 min, after which the absorbance was recorded at 517 nm. The inhibition percentage of the DPPH radical was computed as %inhibition=AC−ASAC×100 where A C is the absorbance of the control and A S is the absorbance of the sample.

Antioxidant activity was quantified by determining the concentration required to scavenge 50% of DPPH radicals derived from dose response curves. Lower IC50 values indicate greater radical-scavenging potency, enabling direct comparison with standard antioxidants. All data are extracted as the mean ± standard deviation of three independent experiments.

Polyacrylamide Gel Electrophoresis SDS PAGE

The protein interaction study was performed by incubating BSA (15 μg) with the H2L2 ligands or their metal complexes (3 mM) at 37 °C for 2 h. After incubation, a 6× SDS sample buffer was added to each mixture. SDS-PAGE was carried out using a discontinuous gel system, with a 4% stacking gel (pH 6.8) and a 10% separating gel (pH 8.8), both containing 0.1% (w/v) SDS. Samples were loaded and initially electrophoresed at 45 V for 15 min through the stacking gel, followed by separation at 80 V for 2 h in the running gel. Proteins were visualized by staining with Coomassie Brilliant Blue R-250, and the gel was subsequently destained in a methanol: acetic acid: water solution.

DNA Cleavage Feature

The DNA degradation activity of synthesized compounds was evaluated utilizing genomic DNA isolated from Escherichia coli as the substrate. After incubation, reaction mixtures were electrophoresed on a 1% ethidium bromide-stained agarose gel in TBE buffer (80 V, 2 h). DNA fragments were visualized under UV light and were photographed. Cleavage extent was assessed by comparing band intensity and distribution to untreated controls. ,

Anticancer Efficacy

In vitro antitumor activity was evaluated via the MTT assay against seven human cancer cell lines: WI-38, HePG-2, HeLa, HEP-2, PC-3, MCF-7, and HCT-116. Cells seeded in 96-well plates (1.0 × 104 cells/well) were treated with test compounds for 48 h. MTT was then added, and the resulting formazan crystals were dissolved in DMSO. Absorbance was measured at 570 nm, and cell viability was calculated relative to controls. IC50 values were derived from dose–response curves to quantify antiproliferative potency. ,

Molecular Docking Studies

Molecular docking, a cornerstone of computational drug discovery, enables the mechanistic elucidation of ligand–receptor interactions by predicting binding affinities and spatial conformations, thereby supporting the rational design of novel therapeutic agents.

Ligand and Protein Preparation

Molecular docking simulations were performed to investigate the binding interactions of the synthesized compounds with HePG-2 (PDB ID: 5EQG) and MCF-7 (PDB ID: 6NM0) protein targets. Prior to docking, the compounds were geometry-optimized using the LigPrep module (Schrödinger Suite) under default parameters. Protein structures retrieved from the RCSB Protein Data Bank were prepared using the Protein Preparation Wizard in Maestro v.14.3. , This involved protonation state assignment (PROPKA, pH 7.0), hydrogen atom addition, and energy minimization using the OPLS4 force field (convergence: 0.30 Å RMSD for heavy atoms). A final restrained minimization (OPLS 2005, 0.3 Å RMSD) ensured optimal geometries for reliable binding affinity predictions.

Induced Fit Docking

Induced fit docking (IFD) simulations were performed to account for mutual ligand–receptor flexibility. For each target, a 10 Å3 grid centered on the cocrystallized ligand was generated. Ligand conformational sampling was conducted using Glide with a 2.5 kcal/mol energy window for the ring, while receptor side-chain adaptability was modeled with Prime. Docking employed softened van der Waals potentials (scaling factor 0.5), and the top three poses per ligand were retained. Final rankings were based on IFD scoring metrics reflecting binding energy and complex stability.

Docking Validation

Redocking experiments were performed to validate the docking protocol. The cocrystallized inhibitors of HePG-2 (5EQG) and MCF-7 (6NM0) were extracted and redocked into their respective binding sites using Schrödinger software. Accurate reproduction of the native binding modes confirmed the reliability of the docking parameters, establishing a robust internal control prior to docking the synthesized compounds.

The observed molecular ion peaks [M]+ for the free ligand and [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O, [Fe2(L2)­(H2O)4Cl2]·2H2O, and [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O complexes were observed at m/z 530.06, 727.06, 817.20, and 716.76, respectively, in close agreement with their calculated molecular masses.

1H NMR and 13C NMR Spectra of H4L2

The 1H- and 13C NMR spectra of the H4L2 ligand were recorded in DMSO-d 6 at ambient temperature (Figures S5 and S6). The 1H NMR spectrum revealed the presence of tautomeric keto–enol equilibria. Downfield signals observed at approximately 12.45 and 11.50 ppm were assigned to the protons of the naphthalic OH groups. A signal at 10.75 ppm corresponded to the N–H protons, while resonances at 11.19 and 11.28 ppm were attributed to the enolic OH protons. Multiple signals in the region from 6.89 to 7.39 ppm originated from the aromatic ring protons. The signals at 3.36 and 4.37 ppm were assigned to the CH2 and CH protons of the ester moiety, respectively. Residual solvent signals appeared in the upfield region.

The 13C NMR spectrum displayed distinct resonances consistent with the proposed structure. Signals at 170.38 and 169.55 ppm were assigned to the enolic ester carbons (−C–OH). Resonances at 164.47 and 165.13 ppm corresponded to the carbonyl (CO) carbons. The equivalent naphthalic C–OH carbons resonated at 158.34 ppm, while the azomethine (CN) carbon appeared at 139.61 ppm. Phenyl ring carbons were observed at 128.70 (C1, C3), 128.00 (C2), 128.14 (C4, C6), and 139.04 ppm (C5). The naphthalene ring carbons exhibited signals at 118.46 (C16), 110.94 (C18, C28), 132.01 (C19, C29), 126.97 (C20, C30), 133.26 (C21, C31), 124.00 (C22, C32), 128.23 (C23, C33), 126.76 (C24, C34), and 128.89 ppm (C25, C35).

UV–Vis Spectra and Magnetic Measurements

The electronic spectra of the synthesized compounds were investigated in the DMSO solvent, as shown in Figure S7. The electronic transitions and μeff values are listed in Table S2.

The UV–vis spectrum of the free ligand (H4L2) displayed an absorption at 272 nm assigned to the π–π* transition of the aromatic rings. Additional bands at 322 and 357 nm are assigned to n−π* transitions involving the CN and CO chromophores, respectively. Broad absorption features observed above 370 nm correspond to intramolecular charge-transfer transitions across the conjugated molecular framework.

Upon complexation, a systematic shift of these bands to higher wavenumbers is observed, reflecting the altered electronic configuration due to its chelation with the metal ion. , The electronic spectrum of the [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O complex features absorption maxima at 417 and 435 nm, assigned to the 4A2 → 4T1 and 4A2 → 4T2 transitions, respectively, which are indicative of a tetrahedral geometry. , This assignment is confirmed by the observed magnetic moment of 4.30 μB. ,

The spectrum of the [Fe2(L2)­(H2O)4Cl2]·2H2O complex showed bands at 405 nm corresponding to 6A1g → 4Eg(D) and 527 nm (likely the spin-forbidden 6A1g(F) → 4T2g(G) transition). − A magnetic moment of 5.86 μB is consistent with the proposed geometry for high-spin Fe­(III).

The electronic spectrum of the [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O complex demonstrates absorption bands at about 395 and 431 nm, which are allocated to LMCT transitions. An additional band at 476 nm corresponds to the spin-forbidden 6A1 → 4T2 transition, characteristic of a tetrahedral Mn­(II) coordination environment. , The measured magnetic moment (μeff = 5.39 μB) is consistent with a high-spin d5 configuration and further supports the proposed tetrahedral geometry.

Thermal and Kinetic Studies

The thermograms of the synthesized binuclear chelates (Figure ), [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O, [Fe2(L2)­(H2O)4Cl2]·2H2O, and [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O were analyzed using thermogravimetric analysis (TGA). The mass loss steps, corresponding to eliminated species, and residual products for each complex are summarized in Table S4.

TGA (Red curves) and DrTG (Pink curves) of (A) [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O, (B) [Fe2(L2)­(H2O)4Cl2]·2H2O, and (C) [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O complexes.

The decomposition of [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O proceeds in three distinct steps. The initial mass loss (27–104 °C, 3.11%; calc. 2.48%) relates to evaporation of one lattice water molecule. Other disintegration steps between 208 and 630 °C involve the fragmentation of the complex (C32H24Cl2N4O4) (weight loss; found. = 82.47 and calc. = 82.42%). The suggested composition of the final residue is consistent with Mn, with the obtained value (14.42%) being close to the calculated value (15.10%) for 2Mn. The thermogram of [Fe2(L2)­(H2O)4Cl2]·2H2O reveals a four-stage degradation. The first decomposition step at a temperature rate of 27–136 °C involves the sequential loss of crystalline water molecules with a combined observed mass loss of 5.02% and a calculated weight loss of 4.41%. The other fragmentations occur between 137 and 526 °C, attributed to the decomposition of the complex (C32H30Cl2O6N4) (obs. 78.66%; calc. 78.00%). The proposed composition of the residual product is consistent with 2FeO, as the experimental mass percentage (16.32%) is in reasonable agreement with the calculated value (17.58%). The decomposition of [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O is characterized by three mass loss steps. The initial step (25–144 °C, 5.60%; calc. 5.03%) corresponds to the removal of two lattice water molecules. Subsequent steps at a temperature range of 145–500 °C involve the complex decomposition (C32H26N4O4) (weight loss; found. = 73.32%, calcd. = 74.05%). The expected composition of the remaining residue is consistent with 2CoO, as the experimental value (21.08%) is in good agreement with the calculated value (20.91%).

The DTA curves of the [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O, [Fe2(L2)­(H2O)4Cl2]·2H2O, and [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O complexes (Figure ) exhibit distinct endothermic and exothermic peaks, indicating multistep thermal behavior. The DTA results confirm that the major thermal events occurring at higher temperatures are exothermic in nature, consistent with the decomposition of the organic ligand. Minor endothermic inflections are attributed to desolvation steps. The return of the DTA signal to the baseline indicates the completion of the degradation process and the formation of a stable residue. The DTA curve of the [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O complex displays five peaks (one negative and four positive), at 73.06, 259.61, 296.18, 326.14, and 418.14 °C. The areas under the five peaks in the DTA thermogram were integrated, giving enthalpy values (H) of −174.79, 113.68, 44.20, 7.39, and 451.69 J/g, respectively. Negative DTA signals correspond to endothermic processes, whereas positive signals are associated with exothermic events. Thereafter, the DTA signal returns to the baseline without further observable peaks, confirming complete decomposition of the complex to a thermally stable metal residue. The [Fe2(L2)­(H2O)4Cl2]·2H2O complex curve exhibits two endothermic peaks appearing at 72.29 °C (H = −57.47 J/g) and 106.64 °C (H = −13.83 J/g). The positive peaks are observed at 264.96 °C (H = 155.26 J/g), 303.99 °C (H = 190.82 J/g), 313.81 °C (H = 0.62 J/g), and 452.71 °C (H = 3380 J/g) attributed to the exothermic events. Following these events, the DTA signal reverts to the baseline and exhibits no further distinct peaks, signifying that the complex has undergone complete decomposition, yielding a thermally stable metal-oxide-containing residue. Thermal analysis of the [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O complex reveals four distinct peaks in the DTA profile: one endotherm and three exotherms. The endothermic event, centered at 72.08 °C, yields an enthalpy of −421.10 J/g. The three exothermic events occur at 272.77 °C, 362.14 °C, and 387.61 °C, with associated enthalpy changes of 28.83, 83.17, and 2930 J/g, respectively. Subsequently, the DTA signal returns to the baseline with no additional distinct peaks, indicating that the complex has fully decomposed to a thermally stable metal oxide residue.

Computational Investigations

Geometry Optimization

The optimized molecular structures of H4L2, [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O, [Fe2(L2)­(H2O)4Cl2]·2H2O, and [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O are depicted in Figures S14–S17, with corresponding structural parameters (bond lengths and angles) compiled in Tables S7–S14.

Computed bond metrics vary systematically with the metal center and its coordination environment. The [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O and [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O complexes adopt tetrahedral geometry consistent with sp3 hybridization, in agreement with literature reports for tetrahedral Mn­(II) and Co­(II) species. In contrast, the [Fe2(L2)­(H2O)4Cl2]·2H2O complex exhibits octahedral coordination geometry, characteristic of the d2sp3 hybridized Fe­(III) center.

Upon complexation, distinct structural perturbations are observed. Coordination induces a systematic elongation of CO and CN bond lengths, reflecting electron density redistribution from the ligand to the metal center. Specifically, formation of the M–N bond weakens the adjacent CN bond through electron delocalization and reduced π-bond character, leading to diminished π-electron density and bond strength. , Similarly, M–O coordination reduces the CO bond order, improving the coordination ability of the CO group as a result of charge redistribution and decreased π-bond character. These geometric alterations collectively underscore the electronic reorganization accompanying metal–ligand bond formation.

Frontier Molecular Orbital Energies

The HOMO–LUMO energy gaps of the compounds, illustrated in Figures S18–S21, serve as fundamental descriptors of molecular stability and reactivity. The free ligand displays HOMO and LUMO energies of −4.52 eV and −2.37 eV, respectively, yielding the widest HOMO–LUMO gap (2.15 eV). This large energy separation is characteristic of high chemical stability and diminished reactivity. Smaller energy gaps, observed for the metal complexes, are indicative of enhanced chemical reactivity, greater polarizability, and increased biological potential. Conversely, a larger ΔE value corresponds to molecular hardness and reduced reactivity. Consequently, the relative hardness of the compounds can be ordered as follows: H4L2 ligand (2.15 eV)> [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O complex (1.89 eV)> [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O complex (1.64 eV)> [Fe2(L2)­(H2O)4Cl2]·2H2O complex (1.25 eV). In accordance with this ordering, the [Fe2(L2)­(H2O)4Cl2]·2H2O complex exhibits the smallest HOMO–LUMO gap within the series. These parameters collectively indicate a high propensity for reactivity and efficient charge transfer interactions. As summarized in Table S15, the ionization potential and electron affinity were derived, with higher values reflecting greater electronegativity. − ,− Relative to the free ligand, the metal complexes exhibit narrower band gaps, suggesting superior electron transfer capability, lower kinetic stability, and heightened reactivity features that facilitate stronger interactions with biological macromolecules such as proteins and DNA. The calculated negative chemical potentials (μ) for all compounds further support their thermodynamic favorability and the spontaneity of their interactions. Electrostatic Potential (ESP)

ESP mapping supplies a visual explanation of the charge distribution across a molecule, offering critical insight into its reactive sites and intermolecular behavior. ESP surfaces of the synthesized compounds, illustrated in Figures S22–S25, reveal three distinct color-coded regions corresponding to varying electron density distributions. Red zones, indicating regions of high electron density, are localized around electronegative atoms such as oxygen of (CO), oxygen of (OH), and nitrogen of (CN) groups, rendering them susceptible to electrophilic attack. Green areas correspond to neutral ESP. In contrast, blue regions correspond to electron-deficient sites with a pronounced positive ESP, marking them as favorable centers for nucleophilic attack. Notably, the hydrogen atoms of the aromatic rings are situated within this electron-deficient blue region, highlighting its susceptibility to nucleophilic attack. Metal complexes displayed a noticeable broadening of the MEP surfaces, accompanied by an enlargement of both electron-rich (red) and electron-deficient (blue) zones. The resulting charge separation, evident as clearly delineated positive and negative potential regions, reflects enhanced molecular polarity and suggests a heightened propensity for both electrophilic and nucleophilic interactions. This distribution has significant implications for the biological activity of the complexes.

For the HePG-2 target, the reference drug doxorubicin demonstrated robust binding affinity, characterized by an IFD score of −907.77 kcal/mol and an RMSD of 1.082 Å. The stability of the doxorubicin-receptor complex was primarily mediated by a network of hydrogen bonds with GLU380 and THR321, a salt bridge with GLU380, and dual π–π stacking interactions with TRP388 at distances of 4.12–4.21 Å. These stabilizing contacts were reflected in their highly favorable Gemodel energy of −108.169 kcal/mol. Among the synthesized compounds, H4L2 showed highly competitive binding (IFD: −908.34 kcal/mol; RMSD: 1.128 Å), engaging GLN282, GLU380, and ASN411 via hydrogen bonding and TRP388 through π–π stacking. The [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O complex achieved the most favorable IFD score of −912.51 kcal/mol and an RMSD of 1.776 Å. Its binding was largely characterized by solvent exposure rather than direct polar interactions. Conversely, the [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O complex displayed moderate docking behavior with −905.99 kcal/mol and RMSD of 1.928 Å, while the [Fe2(L2)­(H2O)4Cl2]·2H2O complex failed to establish detectable interactions under specified conditions, likely due to steric constraints or suboptimal coordination geometry.

In the case of the MCF-7 target, the metal–ligand complexes demonstrated superior performance compared to the organic ligand and the reference drug. The [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O complex exhibited the most pronounced binding affinity (IFD: −573.77 kcal/mol; RMSD: 1.35 Å), stabilized by a salt bridge with ARG58 and π–π stacking with HIS94 and PHE131. Similarly, [Fe2(L2)­(H2O)4Cl2]·2H2O complex showed strong target engagement (IFD: −575.18 kcal/mol; RMSD: 1.123 Å) through comparable interactions with ARG58, HIS94, and PHE131. The [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O complex exhibited moderate docking (IFD: −566.01 kcal/mol; RMSD: 1.298 Å), stabilized by hydrogen bonding with GLN92 and ASN62, as well as salt bridge formation with ARG58. H4L2 demonstrated strong affinity (IFD: −565.28 kcal/mol; RMSD: 1.325 Å), forming multiple hydrogen bonds with ASN62, GLN92, and THR200, in addition to π–π stacking with HIS94. Although doxorubicin yielded a lower IFD score (−558.39 kcal/mol; RMSD: 1.115 Å) in this target, it maintained its therapeutic relevance through a dense interaction network involving seven hydrogen bonds and a salt bridge, underscoring the complexity of its binding mechanism in mammary tissue targets.

A detailed analysis of Glide energy components further elucidated the divergent binding preferences across the two targets. In the HePG-2 environment, the stability of doxorubicin and H4L2 was driven by a synergy of electrostatic (E coul) and van der Waals (E vdw) forces, resulting in superior G emodel values. Specifically, H4L2 achieved a competitive G emodel of −127.450 kcal/mol, supported by strong van der Waals engagement. However, the energetic landscape of the MCF-7 target favored the metal–ligand complexes; [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O, [Fe2(L2)­(H2O)4Cl2]·2H2O, and [Mn2(H2L2)­Cl2]·H2O all exhibited more favorable Gemodel energies than doxorubicin. The [Co2(L2)­(H2O)2]·2H2O complex exhibited the most favorable binding profile, supported by strong van der Waals (E vdw = −43.385 kcal/mol) and electrostatic (E coul = −21.670 kcal/mol) contributions, resulting in the lowest G emodel energy (−107.081 kcal/mol). These findings suggest that while organic ligands dominate stability in hepatic targets, metal coordination significantly enhances receptor engagement in mammary targets.

These results underscore the essential contribution of van der Waals and Coulombic interactions to ligand–protein complex stability. However, molecular docking is inherently constrained by algorithmic simplifications and receptor rigidity. Therefore, these computational predictions should be complemented by molecular dynamics simulations, pharmacokinetic modeling, and experimental validation to fully establish the therapeutic potential and mechanistic profiles of the compounds.

For HePG-2, as shown in Figure , the redocked pose closely matched the cocrystallized ligand (RMSD 1.082 Å), reproducing key π–π stacking (TRP388, 4.07 Å) and hydrogen bond (2.33 Å) interactions with an IFD score of −905.20 kcal/mol. Conclusions

A novel series of binuclear Mn­(II), Fe­(III), and Co­(II) complexes of the hexadentate Schiff-base ligand were synthesized and characterized. The ligand exhibited versatile O4N2 coordination modes, as confirmed by spectroscopic and thermal analyses.

The biological evaluation revealed selective anticancer activity against multiple human cancer cell lines. The Mn­(II) and Co­(II) complexes demonstrated strong DNA interaction and cleavage, with the Mn­(II) complex also showing complete protein degradation. The Co­(II) complex exhibited exceptional antioxidant activity, while the Mn­(II) complex displayed a broad-spectrum antimicrobial efficacy.

These findings highlight the therapeutic potential of these binuclear complexes as multifunctional anticancer, antioxidant, and antimicrobial agents, warranting further mechanistic and in vivo investigation.
