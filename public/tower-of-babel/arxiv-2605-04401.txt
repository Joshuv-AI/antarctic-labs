Introduction

1.1
Overview

The current paper is concerned with the existence, stability, and monotonicity of traveling wave solutions
to the following parabolic-elliptic chemotaxis system with logistic type source,

\begin{cases}u_{t}=u_{xx}-\chi(u^{m}v_{x})_{x}+u(1-u^{\alpha}),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}\cr 0=v_{xx}-v+u^{\gamma},\quad&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
m,\alpha,\gamma\geq 1
and
\chi\in\mathbb{R}
. System (
1.1
) can be used to describe the evolution of a biological species influenced by a chemical substance which is produced by the species itself
and diffuses very fast.
In this context,
the function
u
denotes the population density of
the biological species, and
v
denotes the concentration of the chemical agent. The constant
\chi

is referred to as chemotaxis sensitivity coefficient.
The chemotaxis sensitivity coefficient
\chi>0
indicates that the signal is a chemoattractant, in this case, (
1.1
) is called an attraction chemotaxis system,
while
\chi<0
indicates that the signal is a chemorepellent, in this case, (
1.1
) is called a repulsion chemotaxis system.

Considering chemotaxis models, it is interesting to study how the chemical signal affects the evolution of the biological species, say, whether the chemical signal induces finite time blow-up, whether it creates certain pattern formation, etc. Numerous works have been carried out toward these central problems for various
chemotaxis models on both bounded and unbounded domains. Chemotaxis models on unbounded domains can
describe the influence of chemical signals from more angles, say, from the angle of spatial spreading, which is strongly related to traveling wave solutions connecting two different states.

It is clear that (
1.1
) has two constant states
(1,1)
and
(0,0)

Positive traveling wave solutions of (
1.1
) connecting these two states with speed
c
are solutions
(u(t,x),v(t,x))

of the form
(u(t,x),v(t,x))=(\phi(x-ct),\psi(x-ct))
for some one variable positive functions
\phi(\cdot)
,
\psi(\cdot)
satisfying
\lim_{x\to-\infty}(\phi(x),\psi(x))=(1,1)
and
\lim_{x\to\infty}(\phi(x),\psi(x))=(0,0)
.
Such solutions describe how fast the biological species spreads from higher density regions to lower density regions. Throughout this paper, we only consider positive traveling wave solutions, which we may just call traveling wave solutions.

When
\chi=0

\liminf_{|x|\leq c^{\prime\prime}t,t\to\infty}u(t,x)>0\quad\forall\,0<c^{\prime\prime}<c_{0}^{*},

\limsup_{|x|\geq c^{\prime}t,t\to\infty}u(t,x)=0\quad\forall\,c^{\prime}>c_{0}^{*}.

\displaystyle\leq\|v(\cdot)\nabla\psi\|_{L^{p}}+\|\nabla(v(\cdot)\psi)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{1}{\|v(\cdot)\psi\|_{L^{p}}}+C_{p,p}\mu\|u^{\gamma}(\cdot)\psi\|_{L^{p}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda s}s^{-\frac{1}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\quad+C_{p,p}\kappa_{1}\Big(2\|\nabla v(\cdot)\psi\|_{L^{p}}+\|v(\cdot)\psi\|_{L^{p}}\Big)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda s}s^{-\frac{1}{2}}ds.

(2.11)

By (
2.2
) and (
2.2
),

\|v\psi\|_{L^{p}}+\|\nabla v\psi\|_{L^{p}}(\mathbb{R})\leq\tilde{C}_{p}\|u^{\gamma}\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}

for all

, where

.

Replacing
\psi
be

independent of

for all
\kappa_{1}\ll 1
.
The lemma is thus proved.

\displaystyle=w_{xx}-2(u\psi_{x})_{x}+3u\psi_{xx}-\chi(u^{m}v_{x}\psi)_{x}+\chi u^{m}v_{x}\psi_{x}+u(1-u^{\alpha})\psi.

It then follows that

\displaystyle w(t,\cdot)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{e^{(\Delta-I)t}w(0,\cdot)}_{I_{1}}-\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}e^{(\Delta-I)(t-s)}\big(2u\psi_{x}+\chi u^{m}v_{x}\psi\big)_{x}ds}_{I_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}e^{(\Delta-I)(t-s)}\big(3u\psi_{xx}+\chi u^{m}v_{x}\psi_{x}+2u\psi-u^{1+\alpha}\psi\big)}_{I_{3}}.

(3.11)

It is clear that

\|I_{1}\|_{\infty}\leq\|w(0,\cdot)\|_{\infty}.

(3.12)

Since
\alpha+1>m
, there is
M^{\prime}>0
such that

3u\psi_{xx}+\chi u^{m}v_{x}\psi_{x}+2u\psi-u^{1+\alpha}\psi\leq 3\kappa_{1}u\psi+\chi\kappa_{1}\|v_{x}\|_{\infty}u^{m}\psi+2u\psi-u^{1+\alpha}\psi\leq M^{\prime}.

Therefore,

I_{3}\leq M^{\prime}\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{(\Delta-I)(t-s)}\|_{\infty}ds\leq M^{\prime}

(3.13)

Let
p^{\prime\prime}=\frac{p}{m}
. Then
p^{\prime\prime}>1
. By Lemma
2.1
, (
3.9
), and (
3.10
), there are
C_{p}^{\prime\prime}
and
M^{\prime\prime}
such that

\displaystyle\|I_{2}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}^{\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{t}\big(t-s\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2p^{\prime\prime}}}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\|2u\psi_{x}+\chi u^{m}v_{x}\psi\|_{L^{p^{\prime\prime}}}ds

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{p}^{\prime\prime}\big(2\chi\kappa_{1}+\chi\|v_{x}\|_{\infty}\big)\int_{0}^{t}\big(t-s\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2p^{\prime\prime}}}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{p^{\prime\prime}}\psi+u^{mp^{\prime\prime}}\psi)\Big)^{1/p^{\prime\prime}}ds

\displaystyle\leq M^{\prime\prime}.

(3.14)

It then follows from (
3.1
)-(
3.12
) that

0\leq w(t,x)=u(t,x)\psi(x)\leq\|w(0,\cdot)\|_{\infty}+M^{\prime}+M^{\prime\prime}\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T_{\max}),\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

Replaying
\psi(\cdot)
by
\psi(\cdot-x_{0})
in the above arguments yields
that

\sup_{t\in(0,T_{\max}(u_{0})),x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}}\|u(t,\cdot)\psi(\cdot-x_{0})\|_{\infty}<\infty,

which implies that
T_{\max}(u_{0})=\infty
and

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|u(t,\cdot;u_{0})\|_{\infty}<\infty,
i.e., (
1.10
) holds.

In the following, we prove that (
1.11
) holds.
First, note that

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=u_{xx}-\chi mu^{m-1}u_{x}v_{x}-\chi u^{m}(v-u^{\gamma})+u(1-u^{\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq u_{xx}-\chi mu^{m-1}u_{x}v_{x}+u(1-u^{\alpha}+\chi u^{m+\gamma-1}).

Let
\bar{u}(t;u_{0})
be the solution of the following ODE:

\begin{cases}\frac{d\bar{u}}{dt}=\bar{u}(1-\bar{u}^{\alpha}+\chi\bar{u}^{m+\gamma-1})\cr\bar{u}(0)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(x).\end{cases}

By comparison principle for parabolic equations,

u(t,x;u_{0})\leq\bar{u}(t;u_{0})\quad\forall\,t\geq 0,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

It then suffices to prove that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\bar{u}(t;u_{0})\leq\Big(\frac{1}{1-\chi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

(3.15)

Assume that (
3.15
) does not hold. Then there are
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{n}<\cdots
with
t_{n}\to\infty
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{u}(t_{n};u_{0})>\Big(\frac{1}{1-\chi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

\bar{u}(t_{n};u_{0})\geq\Big(\frac{1}{1-\chi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+\epsilon_{0}\quad\forall\,n\geq 1

(3.16)

for some
\epsilon_{0}>0
.
Note that for
u>\Big(\frac{1}{1-\chi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
,

1-u^{\alpha}+\chi u^{m+\gamma-1}\leq 1-u^{\alpha}+\chi u^{\alpha}=1-(1-\chi)u^{\alpha}<0.

Hence at any
t_{0}
with
\bar{u}(t_{0};u_{0})>\Big(\frac{1}{1-\chi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
,

\bar{u}(t;u_{0})
is decreasing for
t
near
t_{0}
. This together with (
3.16
) implies that

u(t,;u_{0})
is decreasing on
[t_{1},\infty)
.
Note also that

1-\bar{u}^{\alpha}(t_{n};u_{0})+\chi\bar{u}^{m+\gamma-1}(t_{n};u_{0})\leq 1-(1-\chi)\Big[\Big(\frac{1}{1-\chi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+\epsilon_{0}\Big]^{\alpha}<0\quad\forall\,n\geq 0.

It then follows that there is
\delta_{0}>0
such that

\bar{u}(t_{n+1};u_{0})\leq\bar{u}(t_{n};u_{0})-\delta_{0}\quad\forall\,n\geq 1.

This implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{u}(t_{n+1};u_{0})\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{u}(t_{n};u_{0})-\delta_{0},

which is a contradiction. Hence (
3.15
) holds.
Proposition
1.1
(2) is thus proved.
∎

3.2
Stabilization of positive classical solutions and proof of Proposition
1.2

In this subsection, we prove Proposition
1.2
.

Proof of Proposition
1.2
(1).

Assume that
\chi\leq 0
and
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(x)>0
.
Put

\bar{u}(t)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u(t,x;u_{0})\quad{\rm and}\quad\underline{u}(t)=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u(t,x;u_{0}).

By the similar arguments of (
1.9
), we can prove that, if
0<\underline{u}(t_{0})<1
for some

, then

\underline{u}(t)
is non-decreasing on
[0,t_{0})
, and that

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\underline{u}(t)\geq 1.

This together with (
1.9
) implies that (
1.12
) holds.
Proposition

(1) is thus proved.
∎

Proof of Proposition
1.2
(2).

Assume that
0<\chi<\frac{1}{2}
,
\alpha\geq m+\gamma-1
, and
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(x)>0
.
We adopt rectangle idea developed in
[
18
]

to prove that (
1.12
) holds.
This idea can be described as follows: Fix an
\epsilon\in(0,\min\{\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(x),1\})
.
Consider the following system of ODEs,

\begin{cases}\overline{U}_{t}=\chi\overline{U}^{m}(\overline{U}^{\gamma}-\underline{U}^{\gamma})+\overline{U}(1-\overline{U}^{\alpha})\cr\underline{U}_{t}=\chi\underline{U}^{m}(\underline{U}^{\gamma}-\overline{U}^{\gamma})+\underline{U}(1-\underline{U}^{\alpha})\cr\overline{U}(0)=\max\{\|u_{0}\|_{\infty},1\}+\epsilon,\quad\underline{U}(0)=\min\{\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(x),1\}-\epsilon.\end{cases}

Let

\underline{u}(t):=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u(t,x;u_{0}),\quad\bar{u}(t):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u(t,x;u_{0}).

We prove that

0<\underline{U}(t)<\underline{u}(t)\leq\bar{u}(t)<\overline{U}(t)\quad\forall\,t\in[0,\infty),

(3.17)

and

\lim_{t\to\infty}\overline{U}(t)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\underline{U}(t)=1.

(3.18)

First, by the arguments in
[
18
, Lemmas 3.1, 3.2]
,
(\underline{U}(t),\overline{U}(t))
exists for all
t\geq 0
,

0<\underline{U}(t)<1<\overline{U}(t),\quad\forall\,t\geq 0,

and

\lim_{t\to\infty}\underline{U}(t)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\overline{U}(t)=1.

Hence (
3.18
) holds.

Next, we prove (
3.17
) holds. Assume by contradiction that (
3.17
)
does not hold. Since (
3.17
) holds for
0\leq t\ll 1
. Then there is
t_{0}>0
such that
(
3.17
) holds for
0\leq t<t_{0}
, and there is
\{x_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}u(t_{0},x_{n};u_{0})=\underline{U}(t_{0})\quad{\rm or}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}u(t,x_{n};u_{0})=\overline{U}(t_{0}).

Assume that
\lim_{n\to\infty}u(t_{0},x_{n};u_{0})=\underline{U}(t_{0})
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that

u^{*}(t,x):=\lim_{n\to\infty}u(t,x+x_{n};u_{0}),\quad v^{*}(t,x):=\lim_{n\to\infty}v(t,x+x_{n};u_{0})

exist locally uniformly in
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
, moreover,
u^{*}(t,x)
satisfies

\displaystyle u^{*}_{t}

\displaystyle=u^{*}_{xx}-\chi m(u^{*})^{m-1}u^{*}_{x}v^{*}_{x}+u^{*}\Big(1-(u^{*})^{\alpha}+\chi\big((u^{*})^{m+\gamma-1}-(u^{*})^{m-1}v^{*}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq u^{*}_{xx}-\chi m(u^{*})^{m-1}u^{*}_{x}v^{*}_{x}+\chi((u^{*})^{m+\gamma}-(u^{*})^{m}\overline{U}^{\gamma}(t))+u^{*}(1-(u^{*})^{\alpha}),\quad 0<t\leq t_{0},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Note that
w=\underline{U}(t)
satisfies

w_{t}=w_{xx}-\chi mw^{m-1}w_{x}v^{*}_{x}+\chi(w^{m+\gamma}-w^{m}\overline{U}^{\gamma}(t))+w(1-w^{\alpha})

and

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u^{*}(t_{0}/2,x)>\underline{U}(t_{0}/2).

Then by strong comparison principle for parabolic equations, we have

u^{*}(t,x)>\underline{U}(t)\quad\forall\,t\in[t_{0}/2,t_{0}],\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

This implies that

\underline{U}(t_{0})<u^{*}(t_{0},0)=\lim_{n\to\infty}u(t_{0},x_{n};u_{0})=\underline{U}(t_{0}),

which is a contradiction.
Hence, it is not possible that
\lim_{n\to\infty}u(t_{0},x_{n})=\underline{U}(t_{0})
.

Similarly, it is not possible
\lim_{n\to\infty}u(t_{0},x_{n})=\overline{U}(t_{0})
. Then we must have (
3.17
) holds. By (

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|u(t,\cdot;u_{0})-1\|_{\infty}=0.

Proposition
1.2

(4.1)

In the following, we first present some super- and sub-solutions of some equations relevant to (

4.1
Super- and sub-solutions

We first present some super-solutions of some equations relevant to (

For given

\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,M,T}=\{u\in C\big([0,T],C_{\rm unif}^{b}(\mathbb{R})\big)\,|\,0\leq u(t,x)\leq U_{\kappa,M}^{+}(x)\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T],\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}\}.

, where

Lemma 4.1

(Super-solutions)
.

Assume that one of the following sets of conditions is satisfied:

(i)

\alpha\leq m+\gamma-1
,
\chi\leq 0
,

0<\kappa<1
is such that

,

(ii)

\alpha=m+\gamma-1
,

,

, and

W=e^{-\kappa x}

(2)

is a super-solution of

Proof.

The Lemma can be proved by properly modified similar arguments of
[
43
, Theorem 2.1(2)]
.
For reader’s convenience and for seeing the effects of the parameters
m,\alpha,\gamma
, we provide a proof in the following.
We divide the proof into two cases.

Case 1.
Assume that the condition (i) holds, i.e.,
\chi\leq 0
,
\alpha\leq m+\gamma-1
,
0<\kappa<1
is such that
c=\kappa+\frac{1}{\kappa}
satisfies (
1.13
),
and
1\leq M\leq M_{\chi,\kappa,m,\gamma}
.
Note that, by the assumption
c=\kappa+\frac{1}{\kappa}>c^{*}_{\chi,m,\gamma}
, there holds

\kappa<\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma^{2}+\gamma^{2}|\chi|+m\gamma|\chi|}}\,\,\Longrightarrow\,M_{\kappa,\chi,m,\gamma}>1.

(4.2)

In this case,

\mathcal{A}(W;u)=W_{xx}+cW_{x}+mW^{m-1}|\chi|V_{x}W_{x}+W\big(1+|\chi|W^{m-1}V-(W^{\alpha}+|\chi|W^{m+\gamma-1})\big),

where

.

(1)
It suffices to prove that

\mathcal{A}(e^{-\kappa x};u)\leq 0\quad\forall\,x\geq-\frac{\ln M}{\kappa}.

First, note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(e^{-\kappa x};u)

\displaystyle=(e^{-\kappa x})_{xx}+c(e^{-\kappa x})_{x}+m|\chi|(e^{-\kappa x})^{m-1}(e^{-\kappa x})_{x}V_{x}

\displaystyle\quad+e^{-\kappa x}\Big(1+|\chi|(e^{-\kappa x})^{m-1}V-\big((e^{-\kappa x})^{\alpha}+|\chi|(e^{-\kappa x})^{m+\gamma-1}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=e^{-\kappa x}\Big(-\kappa|\chi|m(e^{-\kappa x})^{m-1}V_{x}+|\chi|(e^{-\kappa x})^{m-1}V-\big((e^{-\kappa x})^{\alpha}+|\chi|(e^{-\kappa x})^{m+\gamma-1}\big)\Big).

(4.3)

Next, by Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle-\kappa m|\chi|V_{x}+|\chi|V

\displaystyle=-\kappa|\chi|\left[-\frac{m}{2}e^{-x}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{y}u^{\gamma}(y)dy+\frac{m}{2}e^{x}\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-y}u^{\gamma}(y)dy\right]+\frac{|\chi|}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-|x-y|}u^{\gamma}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{|\chi|}{2}(m\kappa+1)e^{-x}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{y}u^{\gamma}(y)dy+\frac{|\chi|}{2}(1-m\kappa)e^{x}\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-y}u^{\gamma}(y)dy.

Note that
u\leq e^{-\kappa x}
and
c=\kappa+\frac{1}{\kappa}
. By the assumption
c>c^{*}_{\chi,m,\gamma}
, we have
\kappa\gamma<1
and
\kappa m<1
.
It then holds that

\displaystyle-\kappa m|\chi|V_{x}+|\chi|V

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\chi|}{2}(1+m\kappa)e^{-x}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{y}e^{-\kappa\gamma y}dy+\frac{|\chi|}{2}(1-m\kappa)e^{x}\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-y}e^{-\kappa\gamma y}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{|\chi|}{2}\frac{1+m\kappa}{1-\kappa\gamma}e^{-\gamma\kappa x}+\frac{|\chi|}{2}\frac{1-m\kappa}{1+\gamma\kappa}e^{-\kappa\gamma x}

\displaystyle=|\chi|\frac{1+m\gamma\kappa^{2}}{1-\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}}e^{-\kappa\gamma x}.

(4.4)

Now, by (
4.1
) and (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(e^{-\kappa x};u)\leq\Big(|\chi|\frac{1+m\gamma\kappa^{2}}{1-\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}}e^{-(m+\gamma-1)\kappa x}-\big(e^{-\alpha\kappa x}+|\chi|e^{-(m+\gamma-1)\kappa x}\big)\Big)e^{-\kappa x}.

(4.5)

By (
4.5
) and
\alpha\leq m+\gamma-1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(e^{-\kappa x};u)

\displaystyle\leq\Big[|\chi|\Big(\frac{1+m\gamma\kappa^{2}}{1-\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}}-1\Big)\Big(e^{-\kappa x}\Big)^{m+\gamma-1-\alpha}-1\Big]e^{-(\alpha+1)\kappa x}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-(1+\alpha)\kappa x}\Big(|\chi|\frac{1+m\gamma\kappa^{2}}{1-\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}}M-(1+|\chi|M)\Big)\,\,\,\quad{\rm if}\,\,x\geq-\frac{\ln M}{\kappa}.

(4.6)

Finally, by (
4.2
), there holds

\kappa\sqrt{M}\leq\kappa M\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma^{2}+\gamma^{2}|\chi|+m\gamma|\chi|}}\,\Longrightarrow\,(\gamma^{2}+\gamma^{2}|\chi|+m\gamma|\chi|)\kappa^{2}M\leq 1.

This implies that

\displaystyle(\gamma^{2}+\gamma^{2}|\chi|M)\kappa^{2}+m\gamma|\chi|\kappa^{2}M+|\chi|M\leq 1+|\chi|M\,\Longrightarrow\,|\chi|\big(1+m\gamma\kappa^{2}\big)M\leq\big(1-\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}\big)(1+|\chi|M).

Hence

|\chi|\frac{1+m\gamma\kappa^{2}}{1-\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}}M-(1+|\chi|M)\leq 0.

It then follows from (
4.1
) that

\mathcal{A}(W;u)(x)\leq 0\quad\forall\,x\geq-\frac{\ln M}{\kappa},

and (1) is thus proved.

(2) Note that
u\leq M
. Hence

V\leq M^{\gamma},

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(M;u)

\displaystyle=M\big(1+|\chi|M^{m-1}V-(M^{\alpha}+|\chi|M^{m+\gamma-1})\big)

\displaystyle\leq M\big(1+|\chi|M^{m-1}M^{\gamma}-(M^{\alpha}+|\chi|M^{m+\gamma-1})\big)

\displaystyle\leq 0.

This completes the proof of (2).

Case 2.
Assume that the condition (ii) holds, i.e.,
0\leq\chi<\chi^{*}(m,\alpha,\gamma)
,
\alpha=m+\gamma-1
,

M\geq\Big(\frac{1}{1-\chi}\Big)^{1/\alpha}
, and
0<\kappa<1
.

In this case,

\mathcal{A}(W;u)=W_{xx}+cW_{x}-mW^{m-1}|\chi|V_{x}W_{x}+W\big(1-|\chi|W^{m-1}V-(W^{\alpha}-|\chi|W^{m+\gamma-1})\big),

where
c=\kappa+\frac{1}{\kappa}
.

(1)
Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(e^{-\kappa x};u)

\displaystyle=(e^{-\kappa x})_{xx}+c(e^{-\kappa x})_{x}-m|\chi|(e^{-\kappa x})^{m-1}(e^{-\kappa x})_{x}V_{x}

\displaystyle\quad+e^{-\kappa x}\Big(1-|\chi|(e^{-\kappa x})^{m-1}V-\big((e^{-\kappa x})^{\alpha}-|\chi|(e^{-\kappa x})^{m+\gamma-1}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\kappa|\chi|m(e^{-\kappa x})^{m-1}V_{x}-|\chi|(e^{-\kappa x})^{m-1}V-\big((e^{-\kappa x})^{\alpha}-|\chi|(e^{-\kappa x})^{m+\gamma-1}\big)\Big)e^{-\kappa x}.

By
0\leq u\leq e^{-\kappa x}
and Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\kappa m|\chi|V_{x}-|\chi|V

\displaystyle=\kappa|\chi|\left[-\frac{m}{2}e^{-x}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{y}u^{\gamma}(y)dy+\frac{m}{2}e^{x}\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-y}u^{\gamma}(y)dy\right]-|\chi|\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-|x-y|}u^{\gamma}(y)dy

\displaystyle=-\frac{|\chi|}{2}(m\kappa+1)e^{-x}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{y}u^{\gamma}(y)dy+\frac{|\chi|}{2}(m\kappa-1)e^{x}\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-y}u^{\gamma}(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\chi|}{2}\frac{(m\kappa-1)_{+}}{1+\kappa\gamma}e^{-\gamma\kappa x}.

This together with
\alpha=m+\gamma-1
implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(e^{-\kappa x};u)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{|\chi|}{2}\frac{(m\kappa-1)_{+}}{1+\kappa\gamma}e^{-(\gamma+m-1)\kappa x}-\big(e^{-\alpha\kappa x}-|\chi|e^{-(m+\gamma-1)\kappa x}\big)\right)e^{-\kappa x}

\displaystyle=e^{-(1+\alpha)\kappa x}\Big[\Big(\frac{|\chi|}{2}\frac{(m\kappa-1)_{+}}{1+\kappa\gamma}+|\chi|\Big)-1\Big].

Note that

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{|\chi|}{2}\frac{(m\kappa-1)_{+}}{1+\kappa\gamma}+|\chi|\Big)-1\leq\begin{cases}\chi-1\quad&{\rm if}\,\,\kappa\leq 1/m\cr\chi\Big(\frac{m-1}{2\big(1+\frac{\gamma}{m}\big)}+1\Big)-1\,\,&{\rm if}\,\,\kappa>1/m.\end{cases}

This together with
\chi<\chi^{*}(m,\alpha,\gamma)
implies that

\mathcal{A}(e^{-\kappa x};u)\leq 0\quad\forall\,x\geq-\frac{\ln M}{\kappa}.

This completes the proof of (1).

(2) Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(M;u)

\displaystyle=M\big(1-|\chi|M^{m-1}V-(M^{\alpha}-|\chi|M^{m+\gamma-1})\big)

\displaystyle\leq M\big(1-(M^{\alpha}-|\chi|M^{m+\gamma-1})\big)

\displaystyle=M\big(-(1-|\chi|)M^{\alpha}\qquad\qquad\text{(by}\,\,\alpha=m+\gamma-1)

\displaystyle\leq 0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\big(\text{since}\,\,M\geq\big(\frac{1}{1-\chi}\big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\big).

This proves (2).

In the following, we present some subsolutions of some equations relevant to (

).
We first introduce some notations.

For given
0<\kappa<\tilde{\kappa}\leq 1
and

, let

Let

\displaystyle=(\eta^{2}-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}V^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\eta x}(u_{2}^{\gamma}(x)-u_{1}^{\gamma}(x))V

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}M^{2(\gamma-1)}}{1-\eta^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}U^{2}.

This proves (
5.12
).
∎

Step 4.

\epsilon_{0}>0

\lim_{x\to\infty}x_{n}=-\infty
. By (

(5.40)

Let
(\tilde{u}_{n}(t,x),\tilde{v}_{n}(t,x))=(u(t+t_{n},x+x_{n};u_{0}),v(t+t_{n},x+x_{n};u_{0}))

).

and

1.2

, which is a contradiction.
Therefore,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|u(t,\cdot;u_{0})-u^{*}(\cdot)\|_{\infty}=0
. Theorem
1.2
is thus proved.
