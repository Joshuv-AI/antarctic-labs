On the packing dimension of projected measures
Nicolas Angelini

Abstract
. We study the packing dimension of Borel measures under orthogonal projections. We give a necessary and sufficient condition such that typical projections of Borel probability measures have full packing dimension and derive general lower bounds in the complementary case. Our approach shows that the Assouad dimension of the support influences the behavior of projected measures. The same method yields corresponding results for images under fractional Brownian motion.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and for
V\in G(n,m)
let
\pi_{V}
denote the orthogonal projection onto
V
. We write
\mu_{V}=\mu\circ\pi_{V}^{-1}
for the projected measure.

For Hausdorff dimension, it is classical that

(1.1)

\dim_{H}\mu_{V}=\min\{\dim_{H}\mu,m\}

for
\gamma_{n,m}
-almost every
V\in G(n,m)
, see
[
6
]
. In contrast, the behavior of packing dimension under projections is more subtle.

Falconer and Howroyd introduced packing dimension profiles and proved that for any finite Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\dim_{P}\mu_{V}\leq\dim_{P}^{m}\mu

for all
V\in G(n,m)
, with equality for
\gamma_{n,m}
-almost every
V
,
[
5
]
. This shows that the packing dimension of typical projections is almost surely constant and determined by the
m
-dimensional profile of
\mu
. However, this result does not directly yield analogues of (
1.1
).

It is straightforward to see that

\dim_{P}\mu_{V}\leq\min\{\dim_{P}\mu,m\}

for all
V\in G(n,m)
. On the other hand, Falconer and Mattila obtained a general lower bound: if
\mu
is a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then for
\gamma_{n,m}
-almost all
V
,

\dim_{P}\mu_{V}\geq\begin{cases}\dfrac{\dim_{P}\mu\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\dim_{H}\mu\right)}{1+\left(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{n}\right)\dim_{P}\mu-\frac{1}{m}\dim_{H}\mu},&\text{if }\dim_{H}\mu\leq m,\\[11.99998pt]
m,&\text{if }\dim_{H}\mu\geq m,\end{cases}

and they showed that this bound is sharp in general
[
4
]
.

The main purpose of this article is to identify conditions under which Hausdorff-type projection formulas can be recovered for packing dimension. We introduce a parameter
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
(Definition
4.1
) that governs when projections have full dimension, in the sense that

\dim_{P}\mu_{V}=m\quad\text{for }\gamma_{n,m}\text{-a.e. }V\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\leq\mathcal{D}(\mu),

see Theorem
4.5
. This provides a complete characterization of the regime in which typical projections attain maximal dimension.

To obtain lower bounds in the complementary case,
\mathcal{D}(\mu)<m
, we relate projection dimensions to the Assouad dimension of the support of
\mu
. Our main estimate (Theorem
4.12
) asserts that

\dim_{P}\mu_{V}\geq\dim_{P}\mu-\max\{0,\dim_{A}(\supp\mu)-m\}

for
\gamma_{n,m}
-almost every
V\in G(n,m)
. In particular, if
m\geq\dim_{A}(\supp\mu)
, then

\dim_{P}\mu_{V}=\dim_{P}\mu

for typical projections. This shows that the Assouad dimension of the support controls the stability of packing dimension under orthogonal projections. Related results for packing and box dimensions of sets were obtained in
[
3
]
, and analogous results for the
\theta
-intermediate dimension were obtained in
[
1
]
, see also
[
2
]
.

In
[
9
]
, Xiao extended the theory of packing dimension profiles to random images of measures under fractional Brownian motion. He proved that if
X:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a fractional Brownian motion of index
\alpha
, see
[
7
, Chapter 18]
,
\mu
is a finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\mu_{X}
denotes the measure defined by

\mu_{X}(B)=\mu\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:X(y)\in B\}

for any Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then

\dim_{P}\mu_{X}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\dim_{P}^{\alpha d}\mu,

with probability
1
.

Since our results for orthogonal projections are obtained using packing dimension profiles, our estimates also apply to the setting of fractional Brownian motion. That is, with the same notation as above, if
\alpha d\geq\dim_{A}(\supp\mu)
, then

\dim_{P}\mu_{X}=\frac{\dim_{P}\mu}{\alpha},

and

\dim_{P}\mu_{X}=d\hfill\iff\hfill\alpha d\leq\mathcal{D}(\mu).

2.
Preliminaries

We write
B(x,r)
to the ball of center
x
and radious
r
and for a bounded set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
we write
|E|
for its diameter.

Throughout the paper,
\mu
denotes a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})=1
. We write
\supp\mu
for the support of
\mu
.

For
1\leq m\leq n
, we write
G(n,m)
for the Grassmannian of
m
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
endowed with its natural invariant probability measure
\gamma_{n,m}
, see
[
8
, Chapter 3]
.

For
V\in G(n,m)
, let
\pi_{V}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to V
denote the orthogonal projection onto
V
, and define the projected measure by

\mu_{V}=\mu\circ\pi_{V}^{-1}.

This is a finite Borel measure on
V
.

Hausdorff dimension and
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure will be denoted by
\dim_{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{s}
, respectively.

The Assouad dimension of a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\dim_{A}E
is defined by

\displaystyle\dim_{A}E:=\inf\Bigl\{s\geq 0:\

\displaystyle\exists C>0\text{ such that for all }0<r<R,\ x\in E,

\displaystyle N_{r}\bigl(E\cap B(x,R)\bigr)\leq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{s}\Bigr\}.

where
N_{r}(E)
denotes the minimal number of balls of radius
r
needed to cover
E
.

Let
0<s<\infty
, a closed set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
s
-regular
if there exists Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a constant
C\geq 1
such that
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E)=0
and

C^{-1}r^{s}\leq\mu\bigl(E\cap B(x,r)\bigr)\leq Cr^{s}

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<|E|
,
r<\infty
.

The packing dimension of a Borel measure
\mu
is defined by

\dim_{P}\mu=\inf\{\dim_{P}E:E\text{ is a Borel set with }\mu(E)>0\},

where
\dim_{P}E
denote the packing dimension of the set
E
.

An equivalent characterization is as follows, see
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]

\dim_{P}\mu=\sup\left\{t\geq 0\,:\,\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{t}}=0\,\text{ for }\mu-a.e.x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}.

After that, in
[
5
]
, Falconer and Howroyd gives a characterization of packing dimension in terms of the potential defined by

F_{n}^{\mu}(x,r)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{1,r^{n}|x-y|^{-n}\}d\mu(y).

Lemma 2.1
.

[
5
, Corollary 3]

For
\mu
a finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

\dim_{P}\mu=\sup\left\{t\geq 0\,:\,\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{F_{n}^{\mu}(x,r)}{r^{t}}\text{ for }\mu-a.e.x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}.

In the same article, in order to obtain sharp projection theorems, the authors introduced packing dimension profiles. For each integer
1\leq m\leq n
, the
m
-dimensional packing dimension profile of a measure
\mu
is defined as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

For a finite Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0\leq m\leq n
define

F_{m}^{\mu}(x,r)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{1,r^{m}|x-y|^{-m}\}d\mu(y)

and

\dim_{P}^{m}\mu=\sup\left\{t\geq 0\,:\,\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{F_{m}^{\mu}(x,r)}{r^{t}}=0\text{ for }\mu-a.e.x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}.

The fundamental result of Falconer and Howroyd asserts that for a finite Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the packing dimension of the projection
\mu_{V}
onto a typical
m
-dimensional subspace
V
is given by the
m
-dimensional packing dimension profile of
\mu
:

Theorem 2.3
.

[
5
, Theorem 6]

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, for all
V\in G(n,m)
we have

\dim_{P}\mu_{V}\leq\dim_{P}^{m}\mu

with an equality for
\gamma_{n,m}-a.e.V\in G(n,m)
.

The best lower bound was obtained in
[
4
]
by Mattila and Falconer

Theorem 2.4
.

[
4
, Theorem 3.3]

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then for
\gamma_{n,m}
almost all
V\in G(n,m)
,

\dim_{p}\mu_{V}\geq\begin{cases}\dfrac{\dim_{p}\mu\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\dim_{H}\mu\right)}{1+\left(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{n}\right)\dim_{p}\mu-\frac{1}{m}\dim_{H}\mu},&\text{if }\dim_{H}\mu\leq m,\\[11.99998pt]
m,&\text{if }\dim_{H}\mu\geq m.\end{cases}

and the authors shows with an example that this is the best possible lower bound.

Moreover, in
[
5
]
an estimate for the size of the set of exceptional projection directions was also obtained.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
5
, Theorem 13]

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel Measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0\leq s\leq m
. Then for
\mathcal{H}^{s+m(n-m-1)}-
almost all
V\in G(n,m)
we have

\dim_{P}\mu_{V}\geq\dim_{P}^{s}\mu.

Finally, in
[
9
]
, Xiao shows that the packing dimension of the image measure
\mu_{X}
of
\mu
under the fractional Brownian motion of index
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
X(t)
, defined by

\mu_{X}(B):=\mu\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:X(t)\in B\}\quad\text{ for any Borel set }B\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

is almost surely related to packing dimension profiles.

.

[

Let
\mu
be any finite Borel measure on

. Then with probability

\dim_{P}\mu_{X}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\dim_{P}^{\alpha d}\mu.

3.

We begin by establishing some preliminary lemmas concerning sets and measures.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

be a compact set with
\dim_{\mathrm{A}}E=s<n

Then, for every sufficiently small

(s+\varepsilon)

Proof.

The proof is constructive.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that

Let

By the definition of Assouad dimension, there exists a constant

To construct the set

We divide

.
More generally, we denote by

We begin by considering the family

.
At this first stage, we shall select

\left\lceil C\,2^{\,t}\right\rceil

dyadic cubes according to the following rule.
For each cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
, if
E\cap Q\neq\emptyset
, then we retain
Q
.
If the total number of cubes in
\mathcal{D}_{1}
intersecting
E
is strictly less than

\left\lceil C\,2^{\,t}\right\rceil,

we add further dyadic cubes from
\mathcal{D}_{1}
, chosen arbitrarily, until the total

\left\lceil C\,2^{\,t}\right\rceil
.

We denote by
F_{1}
the union of these

\left\lceil C\,2^{\,t}\right\rceil
dyadic cubes in
\mathcal{D}_{1}
.
We proceed inductively.
For each cube
Q\subset F_{1}

Q\in\mathcal{D}_{1}

dyadic subcubes belonging to
\mathcal{D}_{2}
.
Among these subcubes, we retain all those intersecting
E

If the total number of retained subcubes inside
Q

\left\lceil C\,2^{\,t}\right\rceil,

Q

\left\lceil C\,2^{\,t}\right\rceil

Moreover, the total number of selected cubes at level

For each

4.
Main Results

4.1.
Definition and main properties of
\mathcal{D}(\mu)

In this subsection, we introduce the critical point
\mathcal{D}
of a Borel probability measure, and prove a certain inequality and several continuity results needed for the main theorems.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure with compact support on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
s>0
. We write for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r\geq 0

\dim_{P,\theta}^{s}\mu=\sup\{t\in[0,s]:\displaystyle\liminf_{r\to 0}r^{-t}F_{t,s,\theta}^{\mu}(x,r)=0\text{ for }\mu-\text{a.e.}x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\},

where

F_{t,s,\theta}^{\mu}(x,r)=\int\min\{1,r^{t}|x-y|^{-t},r^{\theta(s-t)+t}|x-y|^{-s}\}d\mu(y).

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure with compact support on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We define
the critical point
of
\mu
by

\mathcal{D}(\mu):=\lim_{\theta\to 0}\dim_{P,\theta}^{n}\mu.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that for
t\leq m
,
F_{t,m,1}^{\mu}(x,r)=F_{m}^{\mu}(x,r)
and therefore

\dim_{P}^{m}\mu=\dim_{P,1}^{m}\mu,

in particular
\dim_{P}\mu=\dim_{P,1}^{n}\mu
.

The proof of the following theorem is essentially the same as that of
[
1
, Theorem 3.10]
and
[
1
, Corollary 3.12]
, concerning the Lipschitz continuity of intermediate dimension profiles for sets.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure and
\theta\in(0,1]
. Then the function
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\to[0,n]
defined by

f(t):=\dim_{P,\theta}^{t}\mu

is Lipschitz continuous.

Proof.

Let
0<t\leq s
and let
d_{t}<f(t)=\dim_{P,\theta}^{t}\mu
, and define

d_{s}=\frac{d_{t}}{1+(1/s-1/t)\,d_{t}}.

By Hölder’s inequality, we have

F_{d_{s},s,\theta}^{\mu}(x,r)\leq r^{d_{s}}\left(R^{-d_{t}}F_{d_{t},t,\theta}^{\mu}(x,R)+\left(R^{-d_{t}}F_{d_{t},t,\theta}^{\mu}(x,R)\right)^{d_{s}/d_{t}}+\left(R^{-d_{t}}F_{d_{t},t,\theta}^{\mu}(x,R)\right)^{s/t}\right),

where
R=r^{\frac{s\theta+d_{s}(1-\theta)}{s\theta+d_{t}(1-\theta s/t)}}
, see the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 3.10]
.

Now, since
d_{t}<\dim_{P,\theta}^{t}\mu
, it follows that

\liminf_{r\to 0}r^{-d_{s}}F_{d_{s},s,\theta}^{\mu}(x,r)=0,

and therefore

\frac{d_{t}}{1+(1/s-1/t)\,d_{t}}=d_{s}\leq\dim_{P,\theta}^{s}\mu=f(s)

for all
d_{t}<f(t)
, which implies

(4.1)

\frac{f(t)}{1+(1/s-1/t)\,f(t)}\leq f(s).

Finally using that
f(h)\leq h
for all
h>0
and inequality (
4.1
) we have

f(s)-f(t)\leq\frac{f(s)\,f(t)}{s\,t}(s-t)\leq s-t.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure with compact support
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\dim_{A}E-\frac{\dim_{A}E-\dim_{P}\mu}{\theta}\leq\dim_{P,\theta}^{n}\mu.

Proof.

Fix
\theta,a\in(0,1)
,
\varepsilon>0
and
p\in(0,\dim_{P}\mu)
, and let

t<s:=\dim_{A}E(1+\varepsilon)-\frac{\dim_{A}E(1+\varepsilon)-p}{a\theta}.

By using Lemma
3.3
we have that for all
c>0
, for
\mu
-almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
there exists a sequence
r^{\prime}_{k}\to 0
satisfying

(4.2)

\mu(B(x,\delta))\leq c\,\delta^{s},

whenever
\delta\in[(r^{\prime}_{k})^{a},(r^{\prime}_{k})^{a\theta}]
.

Let
0<\epsilon<\epsilon^{\prime}
and write
n^{\prime}=n+\epsilon^{\prime}
.

We will prove that
\dim_{P,\theta}^{n^{\prime}}\mu\geq s
for all
\theta\in(0,1)
and then the result follows by continuity.

In order to do that, write
r_{k}:=(r^{\prime}_{k})^{a}
and consider the integral

F_{t,n^{\prime},\theta}^{\mu}(x,r_{k})=\int\min\{1,r_{k}^{t}/|x-y|^{t},r_{k}^{\theta(n^{\prime}-t)+t}/|x-y|^{n^{\prime}}\}d\mu(y),

in three different parts, namely
F_{1},F_{2}
and
F_{3}
corresponding to the cases
|x-y|\leq r_{k}
,
|x-y|\in(r_{k},r_{k}^{\theta}]
and
|x-y|\in(r_{k}^{\theta},\infty)
, respectively.
By using inequality
4.2
we have

F_{1}=\mu(B(x,r_{k}))\leq c\,r_{k}^{s}\leq c\,r_{k}^{t},

and

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{r_{k}}^{r_{k}^{\theta}}\frac{r_{k}^{t}}{|x-y|^{t}}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=tr_{k}^{t}\int_{r_{k}}^{r_{k}^{\theta}}u^{-t-1}\mu(B(x,u))\,du

\displaystyle\leq\left(c\,t/(s-t)r_{k}^{\theta(s-t)}\right)r_{k}^{t}.

For
F_{3}
note that if
\dim_{A}E<A\leq n+\epsilon<n^{\prime}
, there exists
C>0
such that for all
0<r<R
and all
x\in E
we have

N_{r}(B(x,R))\leq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{A}

and therefore, if
u>r^{\theta}
, for all
x\in E
there exists
\widetilde{x}\in E
such that

\mu(B(x,u))\leq C\left(\frac{u}{r^{\theta}}\right)^{A}\mu(B(\widetilde{x},r^{\theta})),

and therefore

\mu(B(x,u))\leq c\,Cu^{A}r^{-\theta(A-s)}.

Then, using the above inequality

\displaystyle F_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{r_{k}^{\theta}}^{\infty}\frac{r_{k}^{\theta(n^{\prime}-t)+t}}{|x-y|^{n^{\prime}}}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=n^{\prime}\,r_{k}^{\theta(n^{\prime}-t)+t}\int_{r_{k}^{\theta}}^{\infty}u^{-n^{\prime}-1}\mu(B(x,u))\,du

\displaystyle=c\,Cn^{\prime}\,r_{k}^{\theta(n^{\prime}-t)+t}\int_{r_{k}^{\theta}}^{\infty}u^{A-n^{\prime}-1}r_{k}^{-\theta(A-s)}\,du

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{c\,Cn^{\prime}}{n^{\prime}-A}r_{k}^{\theta(s-t)}\right)r_{k}^{t}.

since
A<n^{\prime}
.

We conclude that

\liminf_{r\to 0}r^{-t}F_{t,n^{\prime},\theta}^{\mu}(x,r)=0,

for
\mu-a.e\,x
, for all
t<s
, which implies

s\leq\dim_{P,\theta}^{n^{\prime}}\mu.

Finally, by letting
n^{\prime}\to n
, we have

\dim_{A}E-\frac{\dim_{A}E-\dim_{P}\mu}{\theta}\leq\dim_{P,\theta}^{n}\mu.

∎

4.2.
Packing dimension profiles with
m\leq\mathcal{D}_{a}

The main result of this subsection is the following.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel finite measure with compact support on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

\dim_{P}^{m}\mu=m\hfill\iff\hfill m\leq\mathcal{D}(\mu)

The proof is split into the following two lemmas.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure with compact support on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
m\leq\mathcal{D}(\mu)
, then

\dim_{P}^{m}\mu=m.

Proof.

We will prove that
\dim_{P}^{m}\mu=m
and then the result follows by applying Theorem
2.3
.
Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure with compact support on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
m\leq\mathcal{D}(\mu)
.
By comparison, we have

(4.3)

F_{m}^{\mu}(x,r)\leq F_{t,m,\theta}^{\mu}(x,r)\leq F_{t,n,\theta}^{\mu}(x,r)+\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})r^{t(1-\theta)},

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
r\geq 0
and
t\in[0,m]
.
Let
t<m\leq\mathcal{D}(\mu)
, then there exists
\theta_{0}>0
such that for each
\theta\in(0,\theta_{0})

\liminf_{r\to 0}r^{-t}F_{t,n,\theta}^{\mu}(x,r)=0\text{ for }\mu-\text{a.e.}x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

then, for
\mu-a.e.\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
there exists a sequence
r_{k}\to 0
such that
F_{t,n,\theta}^{\mu}(x,r_{k})\leq r_{k}^{t},
and therefore

F_{m}^{\mu}(x,r_{k})\leq 2\max\{1,\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}r_{k}^{t(1-\theta)},

by using
4.3
, which implies

t(1-\theta)\leq\dim_{P}^{m}\mu,

and the result follows by letting
\theta\to 0
and
t\to m.

∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure with compact support
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
\dim_{P}^{m}\mu=m
then

m\leq\mathcal{D}(\mu).

Proof.

Write

\phi_{r,\theta}^{t,m}(x):=\min\{1,r^{t}/|x|^{t},r^{\theta(m-t)+t}/|x|^{m}\}.

Since
|P_{V}(x)-P_{V}(y)|\leq|x-y|
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
V\in G(n,m)
we have

\displaystyle F_{t,m,\theta}^{\mu}(x,r)

\displaystyle=\int\phi_{r,\theta}^{t,m}(x-y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\int\phi_{r,\theta}^{t,m}(P_{V}(x)-P_{V}(y))\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int\phi_{r,\theta}^{t,m}(P_{V}(x)-w)\,d\mu_{V}(w),

\displaystyle=F_{t,m,\theta}^{\mu_{V}}(P_{V}(x),r)

and therefore

\dim_{P,\theta}^{m}\mu_{V}\leq\dim_{P,\theta}^{m}\mu\leq\dim_{P,\theta}^{n}\mu,

for all
V\in G(n,m),
where the last inequality is due to
\phi_{r,\theta}^{t,n}(x)\leq\phi_{r,\theta}^{t,m}(x).

Then, if
\dim_{P}^{m}\mu=m
we have
\dim_{P}\mu_{V}=m
for
\gamma_{n,m}-
almost all
V\in G(n,m)
(in particular
\dim_{P}\mu_{V}=\dim_{A}P_{V}(E)
), and therefore

m\leq\dim_{P,\theta}^{m}\mu_{V}\leq\dim_{P,\theta}^{n}\mu

for all
\theta\in(0,1)
by using lemma
3.1
, which implies

m\leq\mathcal{D}(\mu).

∎

Remark 4.8
.

In Lemma
4.7
, the assumption
\dim_{P}^{m}\mu=m
is stronger than necessary; it suffices that there exists
V\in G(n,m)
such that
\dim_{P}\mu_{V}=m
.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure with compact support. Then

\dim_{P}\mu_{V}=m\text{ for }\gamma_{n,m}-\text{a.e.}V\in G(n,m)\hfill\iff\hfill m\leq\mathcal{D}(\mu)

Proof.

The result follows by a direct application of Theorem
4.5
and Theorem
2.3
.
∎

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
X:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a fractional Brownian motion of index
\alpha\in(0,1)
and let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. then

\dim_{P}\mu_{X}=d,\text{ almost surely }\hfill\iff\hfill\alpha d\leq\mathcal{D}(\mu).

Proof.

The result follows by a direct application of Theorems
4.5
and
2.6
.
∎

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
mu
be a Borel probability measure with compact support
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\dim_{H}\left\{V\in G(n,m):\dim_{P}\mu_{V}<m\right\}\leq m(n-m)-(m-\mathcal{D}(\mu)).

Proof.

The result follows by a direct application of Theorems
4.5
and
2.5
.

4.3.
Packing dimension profiles and the Assouad dimension of the support

The following theorem shows that the Assouad dimension of a set influences the packing dimension profiles of all measures supported on it.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure with compact support
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\dim_{P}^{m}\mu\geq\dim_{P}\mu-\max\{0,\dim_{A}E-m\}.

Proof.

Let
0<s<\dim_{P}\mu
. Then for
\mu
almost all
x
, and
K>0
there exists a sequence
r_{k}\to 0
such that

(4.4)

\mu(B(x,r_{k}))<Kr_{k}^{s}.

Let

0\leq t<\min\{s,s+m-\dim_{A}E\}.

Write
A=\dim_{A}E
and choose
\epsilon>0
and
0<a<1
such that
m\neq A(1+\epsilon)
and

t<\min\{s-A(1+\epsilon)(1-a),s-(A(1+\epsilon)-m)\}.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that Lemma
3.2
and inequality
4.4
holds.

Write
B_{x}(r)=\mu(B(x,r))
, then, by a change of variables, we have

\displaystyle F_{m}^{\mu}(x,r_{k})

\displaystyle=mr_{k}^{m}\int_{r_{k}}^{\infty}h^{-m-1}B_{x}(h)dh

\displaystyle=mr_{k}^{m}\int_{r_{k}}^{r_{k}^{a}}h^{-m-1}B_{x}(h)dh+mr_{k}^{m}\int_{r_{k}^{a}}^{1}h^{-m-1}B_{x}(h)dh

\displaystyle+mr_{k}^{m}\int_{1}^{\infty}h^{-m-1}B_{x}(h)dh

\displaystyle\leq mr_{k}^{m}\int_{r_{k}}^{r_{k}^{a}}h^{-m-1}B_{x}(r_{k}^{a})dh+mr_{k}^{m}\int_{r_{k}^{a}}^{1}h^{-m-1}B_{x}(r_{k})(4h/r_{k})^{A(1+\epsilon)}dh

\displaystyle+mr_{k}^{m}\int_{1}^{\infty}h^{-m-1}\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})dh

\displaystyle\leq B_{x}(r_{k}^{a})+m4^{A(1+\epsilon)}B_{x}(r_{k})r_{k}^{m-A(1+\epsilon)}\int_{r_{k}^{a}}^{1}h^{A(1+\epsilon)-m-1}dh+r_{k}^{m}\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq 4^{A(1+\epsilon)}B_{x}(r_{k})\,r_{k}^{-A(1+\epsilon)(1-a)}

\displaystyle+m4^{A(1+\epsilon)}B_{x}(r_{k})r_{k}^{m-A(1+\epsilon)}\int_{r_{k}^{a}}^{1}h^{A(1+\epsilon)-m-1}dh+r_{k}^{m}\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}r_{k}^{s-A(1+\epsilon)(1-a)}+c_{2}r_{k}^{s-(A(1+\epsilon)-m)}\int_{r_{k}^{a}}^{1}h^{A(1+\epsilon)-m-1}dh+c_{3}r_{k}^{m},

by using
4.4
.

Now, since

\int_{r_{k}^{a}}^{1}h^{A(1+\epsilon)-m-1}dh\leq\frac{1}{A(1+\epsilon)-m}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ if }m<A(1+\epsilon),

and

\int_{r_{k}^{a}}^{1}h^{A(1+\epsilon)-m-1}dh\leq\frac{r_{k}^{-a(m-A(1+\epsilon))}}{m-A(1+\epsilon)}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ if }m>A(1+\epsilon),

we have that

\liminf_{r\to 0}r^{-t}F_{A}^{\mu}(x,r)=0,

and the result follows by letting
t\to\dim_{P}\mu-\max\{0,\dim_{A}E-m\}.

∎

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure with compact support
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
1\leq m\leq n
. Then if
\dim_{A}E\leq m
we have

\dim_{P}\mu_{V}\leq\dim_{P}\mu,

for all
V\in G(n,m)
with an equality for
\gamma_{n,m}-
almost all
V\in G(n,m)
.

Proof.

The result follows by a direct application of Theorems
4.12
and
2.3
.
∎

Corollary 4.14
.

Let
X:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a fractional Brownian motion of index
\alpha
and let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure with compact support
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
\alpha d\geq\dim_{A}E
, Then with probability
1
,

\dim_{P}\mu_{X}=\frac{\dim_{P}\mu}{\alpha}.

Proof.

The result follows by a direct application of Theorems
4.12
and
2.6
.
∎

Corollary 4.15
.

Let
mu
be a Borel probability measure with compact support
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\dim_{H}\left\{V\in G(n,m):\dim_{P}\mu_{V}<\dim_{P}\mu\right\}\leq m(n-m)-(m-\dim_{A}E).

Proof.

The result follows by a direct application of Theorems
4.12
and
2.5
.
