CLUWER (Cluver, Cluvier, Cluverius), PHILIP (1580–1623), German geographer and historian, was born at Danzig in 1580. After travelling in Germany and Poland (where he learnt Polish), he began the study of law at Leiden, but he soon
turned his attention to history and geography, which were
then taught there by Joseph Scaliger. After campaigning in
Bohemia and Hungary, suffering imprisonment, and travelling
in England, Scotland and France, he finally settled in Holland,
where (after 1616) he received a regular pension from Leiden
Academy. In 1611 he began to publish his works. He died at
Leiden in 1623. His principal writings are: Germania Antiqua
(1616), Siciliae Antiquae libri duo, Sardinia et Corsica Antiqua
(1619), and the posthumous Italia Antiqua (1624) and Introductio in Universam Geographiam (1629).
