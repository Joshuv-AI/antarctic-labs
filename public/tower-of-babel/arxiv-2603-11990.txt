On the Coalescence Time Distribution in Multi-type Supercritical Branching Processes
Janique Krasnowska, Paul A. Jenkins, Adam M. Johansen

Abstract
Consider a population evolving as a discrete-time supercritical multi-type Galton–Watson process. Suppose we run the process for T T generations, then sample k k individuals uniformly at generation T T and trace their genealogy backwards in time. In the limiting regime as T → ∞ T\rightarrow\infty , the expected behaviour of the sample’s ancestry has been analysed extensively in the single-type case and, more recently, for multi-type processes in the critical case. In this paper, we present a formula for the distribution function of the generation t t of the most recent common ancestor in terms of the limiting distribution of the normalised population size. In addition, we provide effective bounds for the decay of this distribution function to 1 in terms of the harmonic moments of the population size at generation t t . In order to better understand the behaviour of these harmonic moments, we use a multi-type generalisation of the Harris–Sevastyanov transformation to express harmonic moments at generation t t in terms of moments of the transformed process at the first generation. We present numerical results demonstrating that it is possible to approximate the coalescence time distribution effectively in practical settings.

Proof of Theorem
3

The structure of the proof follows
[
20
]
, but accounts for the possibility of the process becoming extinct and having a possibly countably infinite number of types. The proof relies on decomposing the population at time
T
in terms of a sum of families started at an earlier time
t
—recall (
1
). When considering the limiting behaviour of the coalescence probability, the details rely on the continuous mapping theorem and a modified version of the conditional dominated convergence theorem to express
\mathbb{P}(X_{T,k}>t\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)
in terms of the limits of every independent branching process started from each individual alive at generation
t
.

Consider
\mathbb{P}(X_{T,k}\geq t\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)
, the probability that all sampled individuals coalesced in the interval
[t,T)
. If the sampled individuals share a MRCA on
[t,T)
, then they are descended from the same ancestor at generation
t
.
Since the sampled individuals can be descended from any of the families started at generation
t
, we can express
\mathbb{P}(X_{T,k}\geq t\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)
as a sum over each possible ancestor at time
t
of the probability that all
k
individuals sampled at time
T
are descended from that ancestor.

Conditional upon a realisation of the process until time
T
, for a type
i\in S
and an individual
s\in[Z_{t}^{i_{0},i}]
, the probability of the first sampled individual originating from ancestor with index
s
of type
i
is exactly the proportion of individuals alive at time
T
descended from that ancestor,

{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t,T-t,s}^{i}|}/{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|}
.
Analogously, the probability of the second sampled individual originating from the same ancestor is again equal of the size of the family started by individual
s
of type
i
divided by the total population size after removing the already sampled individual from the population:

({|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t,T-t,s}^{i}|-1})/{(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|-1)}
.
Thus
\mathbb{P}(X_{T,k}\geq t\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)
can be expressed as the expected value, with respect to the law of the process, of these quantities.
We can write, recalling that the Pochhammer symbol,
(\cdot)_{k}
, denotes the
k
th falling factorial:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X_{T,k}\geq t\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{s=1}^{Z_{t}^{i_{0},i}}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t,T-t,s}^{i}|)_{k}}{(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|)_{k}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{s=1}^{Z_{t}^{i_{0},i}}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t,T-t,s}^{i}|)_{k}}{(\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{s=1}^{Z_{t}^{i_{0},i}}|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t,T-t,s}^{i}|)_{k}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k\right),

(7)

where expression (
7
) is obtained by decomposing the denominator using (
1
).
Letting
T\to\infty
we find

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{T,k}\geq t\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{s=1}^{Z_{t}^{i_{0},i}}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t,T-t,s}^{i}|)_{k}}{(\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{s=1}^{Z_{t}^{i_{0},i}}|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t,T-t,s}^{i}|)_{k}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\right),

(8)

where the exchange of limits and the conditioning event in (
8
) is possible due to a modified version of the conditional dominated convergence theorem, a proof of which can be found in
A.1
. We rescale the population size in (
8

(R^{T-t})^{k}
to find

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{T,k}\geq t\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{s=1}^{Z_{t}^{i_{0},i}}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}R^{T-t}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t,T-t,s}^{i}|-j)}{\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{s=1}^{Z_{t}^{i_{0},i}}R^{T-t}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t,T-t,s}^{i}|-j)}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\right).

(9)

Let us investigate the convergence of
R^{T-t}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t,T-t,s}^{i}|-j)
for
j\in\{0,\dots,k-1\}
. Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, using Theorem
1
, the limiting behaviour of
R^{T-t}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t,T-t,s}^{i}|-j)
is not influenced by the constant
j
, so

R^{T-t}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t,T-t,s}^{i}|-j)=R^{T-t}|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t,T-t,s}^{i}|-R^{T-t}j\xrightarrow{L^{2}}W^{(i)}_{t,s},\qquad T\to\infty.

L^{2}
convergence implies convergence in probability, so we can apply the continuous mapping theorem (see, e.g.,
[
23
, Chapter 5, p. 103]
) for the ratio, sum, and product of the random variables in (

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{T,k}\geq t\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{s=1}^{Z_{t}^{i_{0},i}}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}W^{(i)}_{t,s}}{\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{s=1}^{Z_{t}^{i_{0},i}}W^{(i)}_{t,s}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{s=1}^{Z_{t}^{i_{0},i}}(W^{(i)}_{t,s})^{k}}{(\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{s=1}^{Z_{t}^{i_{0},i}}W^{(i)}_{t,s})^{k}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\right),

as desired.

In the rest of this section, for any

1.

2.

3.

In Lemma

.

4.

In Lemmata

and

[

6.2
Conditioning on non-extinction at generation t

First we relate the harmonic moment of
|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|
conditioned on
|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0
to that conditioned on
|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0
.

Lemma 7
.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, under Assumptions
1
–
4

,

(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0\right)\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\right)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0\right)}{1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i}}.

Proof.

Since
\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\}\subset\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0\}
up to null sets, we have

\mathbb{E}(\cdot\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0)=\mathbb{E}(\cdot\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0,|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0).

Let us condition the
r
th harmonic moment of
|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|
additionally on non-extinction at time
t
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0,|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0)}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\mathbbm{1}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0)\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0\right)

(30)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0)}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0\right).

(31)

Further note that

\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0)=1-\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|=0\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0)\geq 1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i}

(32)

since, for any configuration
\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}
, the probability of extinction is the product of at least one
q_{i}
and every such product is smaller than the single largest extinction probability. Now combining (
31
) with (
32
), we conclude that

\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\right)\leq\frac{1}{1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i}}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0\right),

which is the upper bound in the statement of the lemma, which is well defined under Assumption
4
.
The proof for the lower bound is analogous. From (
30
) and the tower law:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\bigm|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t},|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0)\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0\right)}{\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0)}.

To lower bound
\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\bigm|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t},|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0)
we note that

1\geq\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\bigm|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t},|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0)=1-\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|=0\bigm|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t},|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0)\geq 1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\right)

\displaystyle\geq(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0\right).

∎

6.3
Population size after the Harris–Sevastyanov transformation

First, let us relate the expectation of the total population size to its generating function. For
r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, consider the generating functions for the
r
th moments of entries of
\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}^{i_{0}}
and
\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}^{i_{0}}
, denoted respectively by

f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{s}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}{}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}=\boldsymbol{v})\prod_{i\in S}s_{i}^{(v_{i}^{r})}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad F_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{s}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}{}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}=\boldsymbol{v})\prod_{i\in S}s_{i}^{(v_{i}^{r})}.

Remark
.

With regard to the extinction probability, similarly to the generating functions
\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{s})
of
\boldsymbol{Z}_{1}
(see Proposition
1
), we observe that for any
r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}

f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{q})=q_{i_{0}}.

(33)

The proof can be found in

Lemma 8
.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0\right)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(s\boldsymbol{1})-f_{t}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{0})}{1-f_{t}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{0})}\frac{1}{s}ds.

Proof.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, observe that the
r
th harmonic moment of
|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|
can be expressed in terms of an integral of its generating functions:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|=v\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0)\frac{1}{v^{r}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|=v\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0)\int_{0}^{1}s^{v^{r}-1}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{v=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|=v,|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0)}{\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0)}s^{v^{r}}\frac{1}{s}ds

(34)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{1-\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|=0)}\frac{1}{s}\left(\left(\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|=v)s^{v^{r}}\right)-\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|=0)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(s\boldsymbol{1})-f_{t}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{0})}{1-f_{t}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{0})}\frac{1}{s}ds,

(35)

where in (
34
) the sum and integral can be exchanged due to Tonelli’s theorem (e.g.,
[
23
, Theorem 1.29, p. 25]
) as all quantities in the expression are positive, and (
35
) follows from the definition of
f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}
and
f_{t}^{i_{0}}
.
∎

Now Lemma
8
will be an important tool in upper bounding the harmonic moment of
|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}
by that of
|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r}
.

Lemma 9
.

Under Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
4
, for
r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
it holds that

\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0\right)\leq\frac{1-q_{i_{0}}}{(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})(1-f_{t}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{0}))}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r}}\right).

Proof.

To compare the generating functions of
|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}
and
|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r}
, for each
t\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
we define
U_{t,r}^{i_{0}}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
as

U^{i_{0}}_{t,r}(s):=f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(s(\boldsymbol{1}-\boldsymbol{q})+\boldsymbol{q})-(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(s\boldsymbol{1}).

Observe that

U^{i_{0}}_{t,r}(0)=f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{q})-(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})f_{t}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{0})=q_{i_{0}}-(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})f_{t}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{0}).

The derivative of
U^{i_{0}}_{t,r}(s)
with respect to
s
can be expressed in terms of a sum of partial derivatives of
f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{s})
. Note that for all
i\in S
,

\frac{\partial f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}}{\partial_{i}}(\boldsymbol{s})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}{}\end{subarray}}\left(\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}=\boldsymbol{w})w_{i}^{r}\frac{1}{s_{i}}\prod_{j\in S}s_{j}^{(w_{j}^{r})}\right)\geq 0,

where
\partial f/\partial_{i}
denotes the partial derivative of a function
f
with respect to its
i
th argument.
{\partial f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}}/{\partial_{i}}
is also non-decreasing in each of its arguments, which can be verified by considering the second partial derivative

\frac{\partial^{2}f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}}{\partial_{i}^{2}}(\boldsymbol{s})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}{}\end{subarray}}\left(\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}=\boldsymbol{w})w_{i}^{r}(w_{i}^{r}-1)\frac{1}{s_{i}^{2}}\prod_{j\in S}s_{j}^{(w_{j}^{r})}\right)\geq 0.

Now define
\boldsymbol{x}:[0,1]^{d}\to[0,1]^{d}
by
\boldsymbol{s}\mapsto\boldsymbol{x}(\boldsymbol{s})=\boldsymbol{s}\odot(\boldsymbol{1}-\boldsymbol{q})+\boldsymbol{q}
. By the chain rule,

\displaystyle\frac{dU_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(s)}{ds}

\displaystyle=(\boldsymbol{1}-\boldsymbol{q})\cdot\nabla f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{x}(s\boldsymbol{1}))-(1-\sup_{j\in S}q_{j})(\nabla\cdot f_{t,r}^{i_{0}})(s\boldsymbol{1})

(36)

\displaystyle\geq(\boldsymbol{1}-\boldsymbol{q})\cdot\nabla f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{x}(s\boldsymbol{1}))-(\boldsymbol{1}-\boldsymbol{q})\cdot\nabla f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(s\boldsymbol{1})

(37)

\displaystyle=(\boldsymbol{1}-\boldsymbol{q})\cdot(\nabla f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{x}(s\boldsymbol{1}))-\nabla f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(s\boldsymbol{1}))

where (
37
) comes from noting that each entry of
\nabla f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(s\boldsymbol{1})
is non-negative and that, for each
i\in S
,
1-\sup_{j\in S}q_{j}\leq 1-q_{i}
.

Since we have that
x_{i}=s_{i}(1-q_{i})+q_{i}=s_{i}+q_{i}(1-s_{i})\geq s_{i}
for
s_{i}\in[0,1]
, we can use the monotonicity of the derivatives to compare
f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{x}(\boldsymbol{s}))
and
f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{s})
. For each
i\in S
,

\nabla_{i}f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{x}(\boldsymbol{s}))\geq\nabla_{i}f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{s}).

(38)

Now using (
38
), we can say that

\frac{dU_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(s)}{ds}\geq 0
, i.e. that
U_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(s)
is increasing in
s

U_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(s)\geq U_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(0)
, and thus:

f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{x}(s\boldsymbol{1}))-(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(s\boldsymbol{1})\geq q_{i_{0}}-(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})f_{t}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{0}).

(39)

Rearranging (
39
) and using the definition of
F_{t,r}^{i_{0}}
, we obtain an upper bound for the generating function of
\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}
in terms of the generating function of
\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}

F_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(s\boldsymbol{1})\geq\frac{1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i}}{1-q_{i_{0}}}(f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(s\boldsymbol{1})-f_{t}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{0})).

(40)

Finally, using Lemma
8

|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f_{t,r}^{i_{0}}(s\boldsymbol{1})-f_{t}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{0})}{1-f_{t}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{0})}\frac{1}{s}ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-q_{i_{0}}}{(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})(1-f_{t}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{0}))}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{F_{t}^{i_{0}}(s\boldsymbol{1})}{s}ds

(41)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-q_{i_{0}}}{(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})(1-f_{t}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{0}))}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sum_{w=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|=w)s^{(w^{r})}}{s}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1-q_{i_{0}}}{(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})(1-f_{t}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{0}))}\sum_{w=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|=w)\frac{1}{w^{r}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-q_{i_{0}}}{(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})(1-f_{t}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{0}))}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r}}\right),

where (
41

40

in terms of

Lemma 10
.

Under Assumptions
1
–
4

\frac{(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})^{r}}{\mathbb{E}(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r})}\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0\right).

Proof.

Firstly, we will relate the expected value of the falling factorials
(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|)_{r}
and
(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|)_{r}
. Note that, with
\boldsymbol{x}
defined as in the proof of Lemma

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}((|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|)_{r})=\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{r}\in S}\frac{\partial^{r}F_{t}^{i_{0}}}{\partial_{j_{1}}\dots\partial_{j_{r}}}(\boldsymbol{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{l=1}^{r}1-q_{j_{l}}}{1-q_{i_{0}}}\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{r}\in S}\frac{\partial^{r}f_{t}^{i_{0}}}{\partial_{j_{1}}\dots\partial_{j_{r}}}(\boldsymbol{x}(\boldsymbol{1}))

(42)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})^{r}}{1-q_{i_{0}}}\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{r}\in S}\frac{\partial^{r}f_{t}^{i_{0}}}{\partial_{j_{1}}\dots\partial_{j_{r}}}(\boldsymbol{x}(\boldsymbol{1}))

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})^{r}}{1-q_{i_{0}}}\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{r}\in S}\frac{\partial^{r}f_{t}^{i_{0}}}{\partial_{j_{1}}\dots\partial_{j_{r}}}(\boldsymbol{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})^{r}}{1-q_{i_{0}}}\mathbb{E}((|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|)_{r}),

(43)

where the right of (

) follows from the connection between the expected value of
\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}
and
\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}
(refer to Lemma
3
).

In order to relate this inequality involving falling factorials to the

th moments of
|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|
and
|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|
, consider Stirling numbers of the second kind
\begin{Bmatrix}r\\
j\end{Bmatrix}

j\end{Bmatrix}(x)_{j}.

(44)

Let us set

and take the expectation of both sides in (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r})=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\begin{Bmatrix}r\\

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=0}^{r}\begin{Bmatrix}r\\
j\end{Bmatrix}\frac{(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})^{j}}{1-q_{i_{0}}}\mathbb{E}((|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|)_{j})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})^{r}}{1-q_{i_{0}}}\sum_{j=0}^{r}\begin{Bmatrix}r\\
j\end{Bmatrix}\mathbb{E}((|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|)_{j})

(46)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})^{r}}{1-q_{i_{0}}}\mathbb{E}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}),

(47)

where the inequality in (

(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})^{r}
for each

\mathbb{E}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r})=(1-\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|=0))\mathbb{E}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0)\geq(1-q_{i_{0}})\mathbb{E}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0).

(48)

Combining (

) and (

\mathbb{E}(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r})\geq(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})^{r}\mathbb{E}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0).

Finally, we can apply Jensen’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|>0)}\geq\frac{(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})^{r}}{\mathbb{E}(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r})}.

∎

Lemma 11

Under Assumptions

r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,

\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r}}\right)\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{i\in S}\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{1}^{i}|}\right)\right)^{tr}.

Proof.

Denote by
\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s}^{i}
the transformed branching process started from an ancestor of type
i
with index
s
in generation
t-1
, and allowed to evolve for one generation. Using the tower rule, let us condition on the population size at generation
t-1
. For
r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{r}}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{r}}{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{r}}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{r}}{(\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{s=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i}}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s}^{i}|)^{r}}\bigm|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}\right)\right)

(49)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{r}}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{r}}{\sum_{i_{1}\in S}\sum_{s_{1}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{1}}}\dots\sum_{i_{r}\in S}\sum_{s_{r}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{r}}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s_{j}}^{i_{j}}|}\bigm|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}\right)\right),

(50)

where (
49
) uses a decomposition analogous to (
1
), i.e.
\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}=\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{s=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i}}\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s}^{i}
.
The expression in the inner expectation in (
50
) is the reciprocal of the arithmetic mean of the collection of
\prod_{j=1}^{r}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s_{j}}^{i_{j}}|
.
We will use the fact that the arithmetic mean is always greater than or equal to the harmonic mean (and hence the inverse of harmonic mean is greater than or equal to the inverse of the arithmetic mean);
here

\frac{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{r}}{\sum_{i_{1}\in S}\sum_{s_{1}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{1}}}\dots\sum_{i_{r}\in S}\sum_{s_{r}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{r}}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s_{j}}^{i_{j}}|}\leq\frac{\sum_{i_{1}\in S}\sum_{s_{1}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{1}}}\dots\sum_{i_{r}\in S}\sum_{s_{r}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{r}}}\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{r}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s_{j}}^{i_{j}}|}}{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{r}}.

(51)

The harmonic mean is defined since we know
|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s_{j}}^{i_{j}}|>0
for any
s_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
i_{j}\in S
as by construction of the Harris–Savastyanov transformation the extinction probability is 0.

Let us use inequality (
51
) to bound (
50
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{r}}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{\sum_{i_{1}\in S}\sum_{s_{1}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{1}}}\dots\sum_{i_{r}\in S}\sum_{s_{r}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{r}}}\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{r}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s_{j}}^{i_{j}}|}}{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{r}}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{-2r}\sum_{i_{1}\in S}\sum_{s_{1}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{1}}}\dots\sum_{i_{r}\in S}\sum_{s_{r}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{r}}}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{r}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s_{j}}^{i_{j}}|}\mid\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}\right)\right)

(52)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{-2r}\sum_{i_{1}\in S}\sum_{s_{1}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{1}}}\dots\sum_{i_{r}\in S}\sum_{s_{r}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{r}}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s_{j}}^{i_{j}}|^{-1}\mid\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}\right)\right)

(53)

where (
52
) follows by Tonelli’s Theorem and (
53
) is due to the independence of the branching processes started from each individual at generation
t-1
. Observe that the branching processes started at generation
t-1
,
\{\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s_{j}}^{i_{j}}\}_{j\in[r]}
, are independent of the population size at time
t-1
and thus the conditional expectation in (
53
) is equal to the unconditional expectation:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{-2r}\sum_{i_{1}\in S}\sum_{s_{1}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{1}}}\dots\sum_{i_{r}\in S}\sum_{s_{r}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{r}}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s_{j}}^{i_{j}}|^{-1}\mid\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{-2r}\sum_{i_{1}\in S}\sum_{s_{1}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{1}}}\dots\sum_{i_{r}\in S}\sum_{s_{r}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{r}}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,1}^{i_{j}}|^{-1}\right)\right)

(54)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{-2r}\sum_{i_{1}\in S}\sum_{s_{1}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{1}}}\dots\sum_{i_{r}\in S}\sum_{s_{r}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{r}}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{j\in[r]}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,1}^{i_{j}}|^{-1}\right)\right)^{r}\right),

(55)

where (
54
) follows from the fact that for a given type
i_{j}
,
\mathbb{E}(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s_{j}}^{i_{j}}|^{-1})=\mathbb{E}(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,1}^{i_{j}}|^{-1})
and (
55
) upper bounds each element of the product by its supremum over all types.

Note that the supremum over types
\{i_{j}\}_{j=1}^{r}
in (
55
) can be upper bounded by the supremum over
all
possible types:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{-2r}\sum_{i_{1}\in S}\sum_{s_{1}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{1}}}\dots\sum_{i_{r}\in S}\sum_{s_{r}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{r}}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{j\in[r]}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,1}^{i_{j}}|^{-1}\right)\right)^{r}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{-2r}\sum_{i_{1}\in S}\sum_{s_{1}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{1}}}\dots\sum_{i_{r}\in S}\sum_{s_{r}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{r}}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{j\in S}|\boldsymbol{Y}^{j}_{t-1,1,1}|^{-1}\right)\right)^{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{-r}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{j\in S}|\boldsymbol{Y}^{j}_{t-1,1,1}|^{-1}\right)\right)^{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{-r}\right)\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{j\in S}|\boldsymbol{Y}^{j}_{t-1,1,1}|^{-1}\right)\right)^{r}=\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{-r}\right)\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{j\in S}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{1}^{j}|^{-1}\right)\right)^{r}

(56)

where the first equality in (
56
) follows by the independence of the two quantities and the second is due to the equality in law of
|\boldsymbol{Y}^{j}_{t-1,1,1}|^{-1}
and
|\boldsymbol{Y}_{1}^{j}|^{-1}
.

Iterating this procedure over
t
, we obtain the desired inequality. ∎

For the lower bound of Theorem
5
, we find an analogous lower bound on
\mathbb{E}(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{1}|^{r})^{-1}
.

Lemma 12
.

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, for

r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,

\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{i\in S}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{1}^{i}|\right)\right)^{-tr}\leq\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r})}.

Proof.

This proof is analogous to the upper bound of Lemma
11
. We use the tower rule to express
|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r}
in terms of the offspring of families from time
t-1
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r}\bigm|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\left(\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{s=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i}}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s}^{i}|\right)^{r}\bigm|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{i_{1}\in S}\sum_{s_{1}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{1}}}\dots\sum_{i_{r}\in S}\sum_{s_{r}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{r}}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s_{j}}^{i_{j}}|\bigm|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{i_{1}\in S}\sum_{s_{1}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{1}}}\dots\sum_{i_{r}\in S}\sum_{s_{r}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{r}}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,s_{j}}^{i_{j}}|\bigm|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{i_{1}\in S}\sum_{s_{1}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{1}}}\dots\sum_{i_{r}\in S}\sum_{s_{r}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{r}}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,1}^{i_{j}}|\bigm|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{i_{1}\in S}\sum_{s_{1}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{1}}}\dots\sum_{i_{r}\in S}\sum_{s_{r}=1}^{Y_{t-1}^{i_{r}}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{i\in S}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,1}^{i}|\bigm|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}\right)\right)^{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{r}\right)\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{i\in S}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1,1,1}^{i}|\right)\right)^{r}=\mathbb{E}\left(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t-1}|^{r}\right)\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{i\in S}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{1}^{i}|\right)\right)^{r}.

Iterating this procedure, we get

\mathbb{E}(|\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}|^{r})\leq\left(\mathbb{E}(\sup_{i\in S}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{1}^{i}|)\right)^{tr},

and the result follows immediately.
∎

Finally, combining Lemmata
7

10
,
11
, and
12
, we get a lower bound for the
r
th harmonic moment of
|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|
:

(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})^{r}\left(\mathbb{E}(\sup_{i\in S}|\boldsymbol{Y}_{1}^{i}|)\right)^{-tr}\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\right),

and an upper bound

(57)

To remove the dependence of (

) on iterates of the generating functions, we can use the fact that
f_{t}^{i_{0}}(\boldsymbol{0})\leq q_{i_{0}}
and thus
(1-q_{i_{0}})(1-f_{t}^{i_{0}}(0))^{-1}\leq 1
, to arrive at the final expression

\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|^{r}}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\right)\leq(1-\sup_{i\in S}q_{i})^{-2}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{i\in S}\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{Y}_{1}^{i}|}\right)\right)^{tr}.

Discussion

In Theorem
3
, we derived a formula for the probability of all individuals from a sample of size
k
from generation
T
coalescing on the interval
[0,t)
from a countably multi-type supercritical branching process as
T\rightarrow\infty
. This expression is given in terms of the limiting random variable describing the rescaled population size and the population size at time
t
, thereby extending the result of
[
20
]
to countably infinite set of types and a process that can become extinct. In Theorem
4
we show that the probability of interest can be upper- and lower-bounded in terms of the harmonic moments of the total population size
|\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}|
, and further in Theorem
5
that those harmonic moments can in turn be bounded in terms of expectations of quantities relating to the Harris–Sevastyanov transformation of the original process at time
1
. By combining these observations (Corollary
1
), one obtains explicitly computable upper and lower bounds on the probability of interest which enable it to be approximated when
t
becomes large enough to preclude direct simulation of
\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}
.

Our proof relies on Assumption
3
that
\boldsymbol{Z}_{1}^{i,j}
possesses
2k
moments—an assumption that holds for many distributions commonly used in applications. We anticipate that this condition can be relaxed with the use of more refined methods to bound the expected deviation of
\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}
from its expected value at time
t
. Progress in this direction could be supported by the development of large-deviation results specific to multi-type branching processes.

In addition, one could analyse the genealogical process associated with
(\boldsymbol{Z}_{t})
in further detail to obtain a distribution on more than simply
X_{T,k}
. For example, for a critical multi-type branching process,
[
18
]
studies among other things the distribution of the joint collection of all
k-1
split times, the number of individuals that coalesce at any specific split time, as well as the type of the ancestor at each splitting event.

Our numerical results demonstrate that the bounds we obtain become tighter as the maximal eigenvalue increases. This is particularly useful because significantly supercritical processes are extremely challenging to simulate.

A.1
A modified conditional dominated convergence theorem

The following proposition is used in line (
8
) of the proof of Theorem
3
.

Proposition 3
.

For any sequence of random variables
(\xi_{T})_{T=1}^{\infty}
satisfying
|\xi_{T}|<\eta
for all
T\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for some random variable
\eta
with
\mathbb{E}(\eta)<\infty
, and a supercritical, positively regular multi-type branching process
(\boldsymbol{Z}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, it holds that

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}(\xi_{T}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)=\mathbb{E}\left(\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\xi_{T}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\right).

(66)

Proof.

For any
c\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, denote
\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>c\}:=\{\lim\inf_{T\rightarrow\infty}|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|>c\}
. We first show equivalence of the events
\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\rightarrow\infty\},\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|\geq k\}
, and
\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\}
up to null sets. According to
[
15
, Chapter 2.6]
, under the stated conditions on
(\boldsymbol{Z}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and for any non-zero
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
:

\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}=\boldsymbol{z}\text{ infinitely often})=0,

and hence
\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|=0)+\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\rightarrow\infty)=1
. Furthermore, for any
j_{1},j_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the events
\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>j_{1}\}
and
\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>j_{2}\}
are equal almost surely, which can be proven by considering the probability of their symmetric difference:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>j_{1}\}\setminus\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>j_{2}\})+\mathbb{P}(\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>j_{2}\}\setminus\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>j_{1}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(j_{1}<|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|\leq j_{2})+\mathbb{P}(j_{2}<|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|\leq j_{1})

\displaystyle=0.

The proof of the equivalence of
\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\rightarrow\infty\}
and
\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>j\}
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is analogous.

Setting
j_{1}=0
and
j_{2}=k-1
, we get the equivalence of
\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\}
,
\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|\geq k\}
, and
\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\rightarrow\infty\}
up to null sets. Hence, for any fixed

,

0<1-q_{i_{0}}=\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0)=\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\rightarrow\infty)=\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|\geq k).

Next, note that the quantity
\mathbb{E}(\xi_{T}\mathbbm{1}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k))
is bounded:

-\infty<-\mathbb{E}(\eta)\leq\mathbb{E}(\xi_{T}\mathbbm{1}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k))\leq\mathbb{E}(\xi_{T}))\leq\mathbb{E}(\eta)<\infty.

Consequently, we can apply the standard dominated convergence theorem to show:

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}(\xi_{T}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}(\xi_{T}\mathbbm{1}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k))}{\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)}\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}(\xi_{T}\mathbbm{1}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)}\mathbb{E}\left(\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\xi_{T}\mathbbm{1}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}(\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\xi_{T}\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbbm{1}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k))}{\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)}.

(67)

The argument of the expectation in (
67
) is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
except at
k

Since
\mathbb{P}(\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|=k)=0
, we can apply the continuous mapping theorem (see for example
[
23
, Lemma 5.3, p. 103]
) to obtain

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbbm{1}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)=\mathbbm{1}\left(\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k\right)=\mathbbm{1}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|\geq k).

(68)

Returning to (
67
) and substituting (
68

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}(\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\xi_{T}\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbbm{1}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k))}{\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{Z}_{T}|\geq k)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\xi_{T}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|\geq k\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\xi_{T}\bigm||\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\right)

where the latter equality follows from the equivalence of

and
\{|\boldsymbol{Z}_{\infty}|>0\}
.
∎

We will prove (
