Abstract

In this series of papers, we introduce higher level versions of the theta group
\Gamma_{\theta}.

In this paper, we treat the theta group of level
5
,
\Gamma_{\theta,5},

and
construct modular forms on
\Gamma_{\theta,5}
.
Moreover
we compute their multiplier systems.
For this purpose,
we derive transformation formula
of theta function with characteristics.

Key Words:
eta function; theta constant; multiplier system

1
Introduction

The upper half plane
\mathscr{H}
is defined by

\mathscr{H}=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}\,|\,\Im\tau>0\}.

Moreover, set
q=\exp(2\pi i\tau)
and define
the Dedekind
\eta
function and the theta constant by

\displaystyle\eta(\tau)=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,\theta(\tau)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\pi in^{2}\tau}.

The modular group
\Gamma(1)
and the theta group
\Gamma_{\theta}
are defined by

\Gamma(1)=SL(2,\mathbb{Z})\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,\Gamma_{\theta}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma(1)\,\Big|\,a\equiv d\bmod{2}\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,b\equiv c\bmod{2}\right\}.

It is well known that

\eta(\tau)
is a modular form of weight
\displaystyle 1/2
on
\Gamma(1)
and

\theta(\tau)
is a modular form of weight
\displaystyle 1/2
on
\Gamma_{\theta}.

In
[
3
]
,
from the multiplier systems,
\nu_{\eta},\nu_{\theta},
of
\eta(\tau)
and
\theta(\tau)
,
we obtain many examples of modular groups.

In
[
4
]
, we proposed the theta group of level
N
in the following way:

\Gamma_{\theta,N}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma(1)\,\Big|\,a\equiv d\bmod{N}\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,b\equiv-c\bmod{N}\right\},\,\,(N=1,2,3,\ldots).

In addition, we constructed modular forms on
\Gamma_{\theta,3}
and
\Gamma_{\theta,4}
and computed their multiplier systems.
For this purpose, we used the Dedekind
\eta
function,
\eta(\tau).

In this paper, we treat the theta group of level
5
:

\Gamma_{\theta,5}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma(1)\,\Big|\,\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\equiv\pm\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\,\pm\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\bmod{5}\right\}.\,\,

In order to construct modular forms on
\Gamma_{\theta,5}
,
we use Farkas and Kra’s theory of theta function with characteristics.

The aim of this paper
is to
construct modular forms on
\Gamma_{\theta,5}
and compute their multiplier systems.
Section
2

describes the multiplier system
\nu_{\eta}
of
\eta(\tau)
.
Section
3
treats Farkas and Kra’s theory of theta function and derives transformation formula of theta function with characteristics.
Sections
4
and
5

construct modular forms on
\Gamma_{\theta,5}
and compute their multiplier systems.
Moreover by considering the kernels of them,
we discover some examples of modular groups.
Section
6

considers
coset decomposition of
\Gamma(1)
modulo
\Gamma_{\theta,5}
.

The multiplier system of
\eta(\tau)

Knopp
[
2
, pp.51]
proved that for each

M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma(1),

\nu_{\eta}(M)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{c}\right)^{*}\exp\left\{\frac{\pi i}{12}[(a+d)c-bd(c^{2}-1)-3c]\right\}&\text{if $c$ is odd,}\\
\displaystyle\left(\frac{c}{d}\right)_{*}\exp\left\{\frac{\pi i}{12}[(a+d)c-bd(c^{2}-1)+3d-3-3cd]\right\}&\text{if $c$ is even,}\end{cases}

(2.1)

where for
c,d\in\mathbb{Z}
with
(c,d)=1
and
d\equiv 1\bmod{2},

\left(\frac{c}{d}\right)^{*}=\left(\frac{c}{|d|}\right)\mathrm{and}\left(\frac{c}{d}\right)_{*}=\left(\frac{c}{|d|}\right)(-1)^{\frac{\operatorname{sign}c-1}{2}\frac{\operatorname{sign}d-1}{2}}.

3
Farkas and Kra’s theory of theta functions

3.1
Definitions

Following the work of Farkas and Kra
[
1
]
,
we introduce the theta function with characteristics,
which is defined by

\displaystyle\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\epsilon\\
\epsilon^{\prime}\end{array}\right](v,\tau)=\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\epsilon\\
\epsilon^{\prime}\end{array}\right](v):=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\left(2\pi i\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(n+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)^{2}\tau+\left(n+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\left(v+\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}}{2}\right)\right]\right),

where
\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R},\,v\in\mathbb{C},
and
\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{2}.

The theta constants are given by

\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\epsilon\\
\epsilon^{\prime}\end{array}\right]:=\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\epsilon\\
\epsilon^{\prime}\end{array}\right](0,\tau).

In terms of Jacobi’s original
\vartheta
notation for theta functions,

\vartheta_{2}=\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\end{array}\right],\,\,\vartheta_{3}=\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\end{array}\right]=\theta(\tau),\,\,\vartheta_{4}=\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
1\end{array}\right].

(3.1)

Furthermore,
we denote the derivative coefficient of the theta function by

\theta^{\prime}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\epsilon\\
\epsilon^{\prime}\end{array}\right]:=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\epsilon\\
\epsilon^{\prime}\end{array}\right](v,\tau)\right|_{v=0}.

In particular, Jacobi’s derivative formula is given by

\theta^{\prime}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]=-\pi\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\end{array}\right]\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\end{array}\right]\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
1\end{array}\right].

(3.2)

3.2
Basic properties

We first note that
for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z},

\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\epsilon\\
\epsilon^{\prime}\end{array}\right](v+n+m\tau,\tau)=\exp(2\pi i)\left[\frac{n\epsilon-m\epsilon^{\prime}}{2}-mv-\frac{m^{2}\tau}{2}\right]\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\epsilon\\
\epsilon^{\prime}\end{array}\right](v,\tau),

(3.3)

and

\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\epsilon+2m\\
\epsilon^{\prime}+2n\end{array}\right](v,\tau)=\exp(\pi i\epsilon n)\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\epsilon\\
\epsilon^{\prime}\end{array}\right](v,\tau).

(3.4)

Furthermore,
it is easy to see that

\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-\epsilon\\
-\epsilon^{\prime}\end{array}\right](v,\tau)=\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\epsilon\\
\epsilon^{\prime}\end{array}\right](-v,\tau)\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,\theta^{\prime}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-\epsilon\\
-\epsilon^{\prime}\end{array}\right](v,\tau)=-\theta^{\prime}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\epsilon\\
\epsilon^{\prime}\end{array}\right](-v,\tau).

(3.5)

For
m,n\in\mathbb{R},

we see that

\displaystyle\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\epsilon\\
\epsilon^{\prime}\end{array}\right]\left(v+\frac{m\tau+n}{2},\tau\right)

\displaystyle=\exp(2\pi i)\left[-\frac{mv}{2}-\frac{m^{2}\tau}{8}-\frac{m(\epsilon^{\prime}+n)}{4}\right]\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\epsilon+m\\
\epsilon^{\prime}+n\end{array}\right](v,\tau).

We note that

\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\epsilon\\
\epsilon^{\prime}\end{array}\right]\left(v,\tau\right)
has only one zero in the fundamental parallelogram,
which is given by

v=\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}\tau+\frac{1-\epsilon^{\prime}}{2}.

3.3
Jacobi’s triple product identity

All the theta functions have infinite product expansions, which are given by

\displaystyle\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\epsilon\\
\epsilon^{\prime}\end{array}\right](v,\tau)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{\pi i\epsilon\epsilon^{\prime}}{2}\right)x^{\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}}z^{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\displaystyle\times\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-x^{2n})(1+e^{\pi i\epsilon^{\prime}}x^{2n-1+\epsilon}z)(1+e^{-\pi i\epsilon^{\prime}}x^{2n-1-\epsilon}/z),

(3.12)

where
x=\exp(\pi i\tau)
and
z=\exp(2\pi iv).

Therefore, it follows from Jacobi’s derivative formula (
3.2
) that

\theta^{\prime}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right](0,\tau)=-2\pi q^{\frac{1}{8}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})^{3}=-2\pi\eta^{3}(\tau),\,\,q=\exp(2\pi i\tau).

3.4
Transformation formula

Theorem 3.1
.

For each

M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma(1),

we have

\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]\left(\frac{v}{c\tau+d},\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)=\nu_{\eta}^{3}(M)\cdot(c\tau+d)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\exp(\pi i)\left[\frac{cv^{2}}{c\tau+d}\right]\cdot\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right](v,\tau).

(3.13)

Proof.

Since
ad-bc=1,
it follows that

1=-c(a\tau+b)+a(c\tau+d),\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,\tau=d(a\tau+b)-b(c\tau+d).

Equation (
3.3
) yields

\displaystyle\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]\left(\frac{v+1}{c\tau+d},\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)=\,\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]\left(\frac{v-c(a\tau+b)+a(c\tau+d)}{c\tau+d},\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]\left(\frac{v}{c\tau+d}-c\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}+a,\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\exp(2\pi i)\left[\frac{1}{2}\left\{(1+a)(1-c)-1+2c\right\}+\frac{cv}{c\tau+d}+\frac{c}{2(c\tau+d)}\right]\cdot\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]\left(\frac{v}{c\tau+d},\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\exp(2\pi i)\left[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{cv}{c\tau+d}+\frac{c}{2(c\tau+d)}\right]\cdot\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]\left(\frac{v}{c\tau+d},\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right),

and

\displaystyle\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]\left(\frac{v+\tau}{c\tau+d},\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)=\,\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]\left(\frac{v+d(a\tau+b)-b(c\tau+d)}{c\tau+d},\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]\left(\frac{v}{c\tau+d}+d\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}-b,\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\exp(2\pi i)\left[-\frac{1}{2}\left\{(1+b)(1+d)-1\right\}-\frac{dv}{c\tau+d}-\frac{d\tau}{2(c\tau+d)}\right]\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]\left(\frac{v}{c\tau+d},\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\exp(2\pi i)\left[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{dv}{c\tau+d}-\frac{d\tau}{2(c\tau+d)}\right]\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]\left(\frac{v}{c\tau+d},\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right).

Moreover,
equation (
3.3
) implies that

\displaystyle\exp(\pi i)\left[\frac{c(v+1)^{2}}{c\tau+d}\right]\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right](v+1,\tau)=\exp(\pi i)\left[\frac{cv^{2}}{c\tau+d}+\frac{2cv}{c\tau+d}+\frac{c}{c\tau+d}+1\right]\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right](v,\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\exp(2\pi i)\left[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{cv}{c\tau+d}+\frac{c}{2(c\tau+d)}\right]\exp(\pi i)\left[\frac{cv^{2}}{c\tau+d}\right]\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right](v,\tau),

and

\displaystyle\exp(\pi i)\left[\frac{c(v+\tau)^{2}}{c\tau+d}\right]\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right](v+\tau,\tau)=\exp(\pi i)\left[-1-\frac{2dv}{c\tau+d}-\frac{d\tau}{c\tau+d}\right]\exp(\pi i)\left[\frac{cv^{2}}{c\tau+d}\right]\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right](v,\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\exp(2\pi i)\left[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{dv}{c\tau+d}-\frac{d\tau}{2(c\tau+d)}\right]\exp(\pi i)\left[\frac{cv^{2}}{c\tau+d}\right]\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right](v,\tau).

We note that

\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right](v,\tau)

has
only one zero
v=0

in the fundamental parallelogram,
which implies that
the function,

F(v)=\frac{\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]\left(\frac{v}{c\tau+d},\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)}{\exp(\pi i)\left[\frac{cv^{2}}{c\tau+d}\right]\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right](v,\tau)}

is an elliptic function with the period
1
and
\tau,
and is holomorphic.
Therefore it follows that

F(v)
is a constant,
which implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]\left(\frac{v}{c\tau+d},\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)}{\exp(\pi i)\left[\frac{cv^{2}}{c\tau+d}\right]\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right](v,\tau)}=\lim_{v\to 0}\frac{\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]\left(\frac{v}{c\tau+d},\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)}{\exp(\pi i)\left[\frac{cv^{2}}{c\tau+d}\right]\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right](v,\tau)}=\frac{\theta^{\prime}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]\left(0,\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)}{\theta^{\prime}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right]\left(0,\tau\right)}\cdot\frac{1}{c\tau+d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-2\pi\eta^{3}\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)}{-2\pi\eta^{3}(\tau)}\cdot\frac{1}{c\tau+d}=\left\{\nu_{\eta}(M)(c\tau+d)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}^{3}\cdot\frac{1}{c\tau+d}=\nu_{\eta}^{3}(M)(c\tau+d)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

4.1
The case where

M\equiv\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\

Theorem 4.1
.

Set

M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{\theta,5}

with

M\equiv\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\bmod{5}.

Then the multiplier system of

\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{1}{5}\\
\frac{1}{5}\end{array}\right]\left(0,\tau\right)\theta\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{1}{5}\\
\frac{9}{5}\end{array}\right]\left(0,\tau\right)

is given by

\nu(M)=\nu_{\eta}^{6}(M)\exp\left[\frac{\pi i}{5}\left(-\frac{4b}{5}-\frac{8ab}{5}-\frac{8cd}{5}\right)\right].

Proof.

Since
ad-bc=1,

it follows that

(a,c)\equiv(1,1),(1,0),(0,1)\bmod{2}\,\mathrm{and}\,(b,d)\equiv(1,1),(1,0),(0,1)\bmod{2},

which implies that

\displaystyle\frac{a}{5}+\frac{c}{5}-ac-\frac{1}{5}=\frac{a-1}{5}+\frac{c}{5}-ac\equiv 0\bmod{2},
