Reinforcement learning enabled hybrid optimisation for energy-efficient multipath routing in wireless sensor networks
Authors: R. Nareshkumar, Prabu Selvam, Karthikeyan Kaliyaperumal, Hussain. A, P. Uma Maheswari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Wireless sensor networks have the major challenge of ensuring energy efficiency, scalability, and data delivery. The achievement of these goals becomes challenging when the nodes have limited battery energy, the network topology changes frequently, and the data transfer is not distributed among the nodes. The routing protocols have been found wanting in various ways, as they consume more energy than required, have difficulties selecting the appropriate nodes as cluster heads, and cannot be adapted when the network topology changes frequently. This eventually reduces the lifespan of the network and affects its performance as a whole. To mitigate these problems, this paper presents a Reinforcement Learning-based Multipath Hybrid Whale-Grey Wolf Optimization framework. Deep Reinforcement Learning is incorporated throughout this framework for adaptive sleep scheduling and node activation. The DRL agent learn and schedules tasks based on residual energy, node centrality, and node proximity to the cluster head as primary factors. This research also suggests a new hybrid optimization algorithm, where the global search capability of the Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) and the strong convergence and exploitation capabilities of the Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO) are hybridized for more effective cluster head selection. This hybridization process is done for a more effective and energy-efficient selection of cluster heads. It has also been proposed that a multipath routing technique will be used to develop multiple stable and energy-efficient paths between the cluster heads and the base station. The stability of the paths, the energy left, and the quality of the paths will be considered while establishing the paths using the hybrid WOA–GWO algorithm. The effectiveness of the proposed framework will be validated through extensive simulations, which prove that the proposed model performs well with a packet delivery ratio of 97.8%, total energy consumption of 320 J, and a total throughput at the base station of 72 kbps.

Introduction

Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) have emerged as the backbone for the sensing and monitoring requirements of the current era. The applications of WSNs include various real-time data acquisition requirements. Hence, it is essential to focus on achieving efficiency in WSN protocol design1. In WSNs, the process of routing is responsible for forwarding the information collected from various sensor nodes to the Base Station (BS)2. This is done through multi-hop communication. However, it is difficult for traditional routing methods to handle these situations efficiently, leading to energy holes around the Base Station, dying of nodes before their actual time, and a reduction in network life. To avoid these problems, new routing protocols have been proposed for achieving maximum energy efficiency, balancing the load of all the sensor nodes, and ensuring reliable delivery of packets.

Among these routing protocols, cluster-based routing is found to be highly efficient. In this type of routing protocol, a number of sensor nodes are grouped together as clusters and are served by a cluster head that collects and transmits information to the base station. This reduces redundant transmission and saves energy on the network. However, inefficient and poor selection of cluster heads results in faster depletion of energy in the network. Therefore, it is highly important to design adaptive and intelligent cluster head selection protocols that consider residual energy, transmission cost, network density, and distance from the base station3. A Wireless Sensor Network is comprised of low-power sensor nodes that collaborate to sense physical environments and transmit the sensed data to a sink node for processing. This research proposes an enhanced version of the DEEC protocol4 using K-Nearest Neighbours and K-Means machine learning algorithms to dynamically select the best cluster head, resulting in DEEC-KNN and DEEC-KM protocols. Simulation results show substantial improvements in terms of energy, stability, and data delivery, showing performance improvements up to 110% compared to the conventional DEEC protocol. The novel machine learning-based clustering protocol is found to perform exceptionally in WSNs, especially in large networks, demonstrating the potential of machine learning in developing energy-efficient sensor networks for the future.

The conventional hierarchical routing protocols, such as Distributed Energy-Efficient Clustering (DEEC), Low Energy Adaptive Clustering Hierarchy (LEACH), Stable Election Protocol (SEP), and Threshold Sensitive Energy Efficient sensor Network (TEEN), have been extensively used to achieve energy efficiency through clustering and aggregation. However, challenges like random selection of CH, non-uniform distribution of CH, and high overhead of re-clustering are still major drawbacks in this approach5.

For addressing these problems, various bio-inspired optimization and intelligent decision-making techniques have been proposed for routing optimization in WSNs in recent years. Among these techniques, metaheuristics such as Ant Colony Optimisation (ACO), Particle Swarm Optimisation (PSO), Firefly Algorithm (FA), and Genetic Algorithms (GA) have been used for optimizing the CH selection process. The convergence rate of these techniques is often affected by the problems of slow convergence rate and premature optimization.

Stimulated by these issues, this research presents a novel concept of a Reinforcement Learning-enabled Multipath Hybrid Whale Optimisation Algorithm and Grey Wolf Optimisation Algorithm Framework (RL-MHWO–GWO) to solve the mentioned issues in an energy-efficient way while maintaining balanced node loading and utilizing the dynamic sleep scheduling and node activation using a deep reinforcement learning agent6. In order to achieve the most efficient CH selection, this proposed framework integrates the global search ability of the Whale Optimisation Algorithm with the local search ability of the Grey Wolf Optimiser Algorithm. In addition, this proposed framework adopts the multipath routing protocol to develop robust, energy-efficient, and stable paths among CH and BS nodes in the WSN environment7. By utilizing the flexibility and hybrid optimization potential of the proposed framework based on the RL and hybrid optimization approach, this proposed framework is more efficient compared to other conventional protocols in establishing scalable, reliable, and energy-efficient communication in the WSN environment.

Related works

This section highlights existing work on energy-efficient routing in WSNs and outlines their limitations and research gaps.

Shukla et al.8 suggested an EEFTMR scheme for WSNs. The scheme employs an elite-group-based routing strategy for sensor node routing and fault-tolerant CH selection. Fault-tolerant CH tracks the performance of the cluster head and temporarily buffers data until transmission is successful, thereby enhancing fault tolerance. Faulty sensor nodes are identified using hypothesis-based testing at the cluster head node. Additionally, a multipath routing strategy employing a composite performance metric comprising residual energy, hop count, network resilience, and data speed was implemented to ensure fault-tolerant data transmission. Simulation results demonstrated significant improvements in network delay (73.7%), energy efficiency (9.6%), network longevity (9%), and error-free data throughput (61.3%) compared to prior elite-group-based routing methods. Kumar et al.9 emphasized power conservation in WSNs using an OEERP. In OEERP, specific sensor nodes—referred to as residual nodes—are bypassed during cluster construction and forward partial information to the next-hop or base station, resulting in wasted power and a shorter network lifetime. To address this, the authors presented an I-OEERP that employs assistant cluster nodes to prevent residual node formation and thus improve network longevity. The protocol integrates CSA and GSA: CSA regulates cluster formation to limit residual nodes, while GSA optimizes routing paths for energy efficiency.

Kumar et al.10 presented a WAOA for the design of energy-efficient routing in WSNs. WOA is used to identify the optimal cluster heads within a defined search space, whereas ACO is used to determine the shortest paths from source cluster sensors to the cluster head. The issues of cluster head selection and route discovery optimization are formulated as linear programs. Nakas et al.11 presented each class includes a complete description and comparison of protocols based on performance measures, which tells us where they are strong and where they fall short. Limitations and research gaps

Several recent studies have integrated DQN-based learning with hybrid metaheuristic clustering strategies. However, most approaches employ reinforcement learning for energy prediction or parameter tuning, while the metaheuristic optimizer operates independently. The interaction between learning and optimization is typically sequential rather than structurally coupled. In the existing routing protocols used in current WSN, the unachieved parameters are energy efficiency, long lifespan, and data transmission reliability. In the existing routing protocols, clustering and heuristic-based optimization techniques are used, which ultimately lead to inefficient selection of cluster heads, unbalanced exhaustion of node energies, and node failures. Additionally, less adaptive and intelligent wake-up scheduling schemes cause wastage of node energies through wakefulness of non-essential nodes, which ultimately affects the performance and lifespan of the network. Besides, dynamic path evaluation is not used in existing routing protocols, which ultimately affects the reliability and fault tolerance of data transmission.

These vacuums are filled by the proposed mechanism, which employs deep reinforcement learning for adaptive sleep scheduling, a hybrid Whale Optimisation Algorithm and Grey Wolf Optimiser algorithm for efficient selection of CHs, and multipath routing. The problem of energy wastage due to vacillation is addressed by using DRL-based scheduling, which is able to learn and adaptively eliminate unnecessary energy consumption.

WSN’s network design

A WSN is set up consisting of a homogeneous quantity of SNs and a BS, in which the SNs are placed casually and consistently in the specified network area. Any two nodes communicate through single-hop or multi-hop transmission. The SNs are classified as source nodes and normal nodes, each with a unique network ID and a specific location in the network. The base station is placed at a strategic location in the centre of the network area.

All sensor nodes have the same storage, energy, and processing capacities. Each SN can sense its location in the network, while the base station has greater processing capability and is not energy-limited. SNs know their remaining energy and calculate the distance to nearby nodes. SNs collect data periodically and transmit it to their respective CHs, where it is aggregated. Power levels for transmission are dynamically adjusted based on the distance to the receiving node, promoting symmetric announcement links and effective energy use. SNs also continuously sense their surroundings at the maximum sensing rate, and the base station is always within communication range of all SNs in the network.

RL-MHWO–GWO framework

Figure 2 shows the entire working process of the proposed RL-MHWO–GWO framework. The methodology starts with node initialization, in which sensor nodes are randomly deployed within the defined monitoring area. Once the nodes have been deployed, the neighbor discovery process uses periodic HELLO packets to discover the neighborhood topology. After this, the DRL-based scheduler adjusts the duty cycles of the sensor nodes. Next, the hybrid WOA–GWO algorithm selects the cluster heads to achieve energy-efficient, evenly distributed clustering. This process finds the best energy-efficient paths from the CHs to the BS using relay CHs, based on hop count, signal strength, and residual energy. Data is then sent via the best routes to the BS, ensuring secure, guaranteed transmission throughout the network.

This section reports the setup, parameters, metrics, and analyses that demonstrate the effectiveness of RL-MHWO–GWO for energy-aware routing.

Experimental setup

All simulations were implemented in MATLAB R2023a on an Intel Core i7-10700 (3.8 GHz) with 16 GB DDR4 RAM, running Windows 10 Pro 64-bit. DRL training uses mini-batch replay (batch size = 64) and updates the target network every 500 steps. The simulation configuration of the proposed framework is given in Table 2. To ensure comprehensive evaluation, the proposed RL-MHWO–GWO framework is tested under multiple network configurations. The number of sensor nodes is varied as N = {100, 150, 200, 250}, and nodes were randomly deployed in a 500 m × 500 m monitoring area. Each node was initialized with 2 J of energy. The simulation ran for up to 10,000 communication rounds, or until the last node depleted its energy. Network lifetime was evaluated in terms of first node death (FND) and last node death (LND). All compared protocols were executed under identical environmental and network parameters to ensure fair benchmarking. Convergence behaviour analysis

The convergence behaviour of the proposed framework is influenced by both the hybrid metaheuristic optimization and the reinforcement learning components.

For cluster head selection and multipath routing, the Hybrid WOA–GWO algorithm exhibits fast and stable convergence due to the complementary strengths of its constituent optimizers. In the initial stages, the WOA performs well in the exploration of the solution space, preventing the search process from converging prematurely. As the iterations advance, the GWO takes over the search process, improving the local search and speeding up the convergence towards the near-optimal solution. This causes a smooth decrease in the fitness function value with the advancement of iterations and converges after a few iterations.

For the DRL-based sleep scheduling, the convergence is obtained through the progressive stabilization of the Q-values learned by the DQN agent. During the early stages of training, a higher exploration rate results in varying rewards. As training continues, the ε-greedy policy transitions from exploration to exploitation, leading to stable action choices and energy-saving decisions. This learning process reaches convergence when the average reward per round and the node duty-cycle decisions stabilize over multiple rounds.

The combined convergence of the hybrid optimizer and the DRL agent ensures that the proposed framework attains stable routing and scheduling decisions without any oscillations, thus facilitating reliable and energy-efficient network operation. The DQN model is stored only at the base station (≈ 10.8 MB), while sensor nodes maintain only small state vectors (< 100 bytes). Therefore, the proposed DRL mechanism does not impose significant memory requirements on sensor nodes.

While existing studies30–34 have explored the use of metaheuristic optimization, reinforcement learning, or their combinations for routing in wireless sensor networks, most approaches apply these techniques in a loosely coupled or sequential manner. In such methods, reinforcement learning is typically used for prediction or parameter tuning, while optimization algorithms operate on static or partially updated network states. In contrast, the proposed RL-MHWO–GWO framework introduces a tightly integrated and co-adaptive design, where reinforcement learning, hybrid optimization, and routing decisions interact dynamically across communication rounds.

First, the DRL component is employed for adaptive sleep scheduling, rather than prediction. It directly controls node participation in each round, thereby influencing energy consumption, network topology, and traffic distribution in real time. Second, the hybrid WOA–GWO algorithm is designed as a phase-adaptive optimization strategy, combining global exploration (WOA) and local exploitation (GWO) with a fitness function specifically tailored to WSN constraints, including residual energy, load balance, communication cost, and link quality. Third, the DRL policy and hybrid optimization are mathematically coupled, where the active node set determined by the DRL agent defines the feasible search space for cluster head selection and routing. This dynamic interaction allows the optimization process to adapt continuously to changing network conditions. These combined features distinguish the proposed model from existing approaches by providing a holistic, adaptive, and system-level optimization framework, rather than a simple combination of individual techniques.

Limitations of the proposed framework

Despite the positive developments in performance efficiency gain with the help of the proposed framework, some limitations are identified with the proposed framework. First and foremost, additional control overheads are introduced with the DRL agent’s centralized training approach, which could be a scalability issue in large-scale dynamic topologies. The hybrid approach of WOA–GWO could also introduce increased computational complexity in the base station compared to the traditional heuristic approach. Additionally, it is assumed that accurate residual energy, link quality, and node location estimates are available for the proposed model, which could be impacted by measurement errors and channel changes. Finally, security and robustness are not considered in the proposed framework.

Conclusion and future scope

In the case of RL-MHWO–GWO, intelligent cluster head selection and multipath routing optimization are achieved by integrating reinforcement learning with hybrid metaheuristic optimization. The proposed system ensures the minimization of energy consumption, balanced network loading, and routing efficiency. The proposed system has been validated with large-scale simulation experiments, which prove the effectiveness of the proposed system and the fact that it is better than the existing procedures and improves with the increase of sensor nodes. Although the proposed model for RL-MHWO–GWO is strong, it has a limitation in the level of precision in the measurement of residual energy, signal power, and proximity. In the future, there will be an improvement in the reliability of the decision-making process with the incorporation of state-of-the-art predictive model technology. The results of the simulations show that the proposed model for routing in the network is effective. The proposed model offers a packet delivery ratio of 97.8%, a throughput of 72 kbps, energy consumption of 35 J, and a network lifetime of 9500 rounds with balanced residual energy in the sensor nodes. The proposed model for routing in the network is robust, scalable, and energy-efficient compared to other routing protocols, as indicated by the improvement in all the results. Future work will be carried out to improve upon these limitations and introduce a robust and secure routing protocol. Additionally, since the RL-MHWO–GWO mainly emphasizes the performance and energy efficiency of the system, future research will also include lightweight cryptographic protocols and trust models to enable safe and attack-resistant data transmission for the system. Such protocols will enable the system to handle the emerging requirements for network security and reliability, as well as increase the potential of the system.
