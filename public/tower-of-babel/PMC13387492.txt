B cell TET2 recruits OGT for nuclear TET2 and H2B O-GlcNAcylation to drive AID and BLIMP-1 expression and maturation of the antibody response
Authors: Shili Li, Yibo Peng, Zhimin Yang, Xin Li, Zhenming Xu, Carlos E. Rivera, Paolo Casali
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SUMMARYMaturation of antibody responses entails B cell Aicda/AID and Prdm1/BLIMP-1 expression for SHM/CSR, plasma cell differentiation, and production of class-switched high-affinity antibodies. We determined that TET1, TET2, and TET3 are not expressed in resting naïve B lymphocytes, and only TET2 is induced in differentiating B cells for AID and BLIMP-1 expression. B cell TET2 recruits OGT, a metabolic sensor and sole protein O-GlcNAcylator, for O-GlcNAcylation of itself and chromatin H2B-S112. TET2 O-GlcNAcylation supports TET2-mediated active DNA demethylation (5mC oxidation to 5hmC) of Aicda and Prdm1 loci. This, together with these loci H2B-S112 O-GlcNAcylation, promotes Aicda/AID and Prdm1/BLIMP-1 expression for maturation of T-dependent and T-independent antibody responses. TET2 recruits OGT through its C-terminal-end, as evidenced by Tet2OGT-Δmut C-terminal-end deletion mutant and AlphaFold 3.0-modeled TET2-DNA-OGT complex. Finally, B cell TET2 takes metabolic cues for Aicda/AID and Prdm1/BLIMP-1 expression, as shown by fumarate inhibition and vitamin C activation of TET2 in humanized THX mice and Tcrβ−/−Tcrδ−/− mice.

INTRODUCTION

Maturation of the antibody response entails B cell somatic hypermutation (SHM)/class-switch DNA recombination (CSR), plasma cell differentiation, and generation of specific memory B cells (MBCs). SHM and CSR are initiated by activation-induced cytidine deaminase (AID), encoded by Aicda.1 AID introduces nicks and double-stranded breaks in immunoglobulin loci DNA, the repair of which leads to the generation of clonal sub-mutants making antibodies with higher antigen affinity and new and diverse effector functions.2,3 Plasma cells, whose differentiation and maintenance require expression of BLIMP-1, which is encoded by Prdm1, are terminally differentiated elements producing large amounts of antigen-specific antibodies. MBCs express a high-affinity and mostly class-switched B cell receptor (BCR) and are reactivated by cognate antigen to proliferate and differentiate, thereby playing a critical role in anamnestic and specific antibody responses.4,5

In B cells, AID and BLIMP-1 expression is under stringent transcriptional regulation. Aicda transcription is initiated by binding of NF-κB and HoxC46–9 to the Aicda promoter,3,10,11 while Prdm1 transcription is initiated by binding of STAT3 and IRF4 to the Prdm1 promoter.12 Transcription of Aicda and Prdm1 is also regulated by epigenetic factors, such as histone deacetylases (HDACs), particularly HDAC Class III SIRT1, noncoding miRNAs, including miR-155, miR-181b, miR-93, and miR-9,13–19 and, as more recently shown, ten-eleven translocation (TET) methylcytosine dioxygenases.20,21 These enzymes are Fe++- and α-KG (α-ketoglutarate)-dependent and can catalyze genome-wide active DNA demethylation21–23 by sequentially oxidizing 5mC to 5-hydroxymethylcytosine (5hmC), 5-formylcytosine (5fC), and 5-carboxylcytosine (5caC). DNA 5hmC is used as an indicator of TET active DNA demethylation as it is the most stable TET-catalyzed intermediate and up to 1,000-fold more abundant than 5fC and 5caC.21 TETs display tumor-suppressor activity, and their mutations can lead to neoplastic transformation, particularly in the lymphoid lineage.20,21

TET1 is predominant in embryonic stem cells (ESCs).21,23–25 In mouse and zebrafish ESCs, it can interact with O-linked N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc) transferase (OGT), which enables this active DNA demethylator’s catalytic function.26 OGT, encoded by the X chromosome Ogt locus, is the sole enzyme attaching a GlcNAc sugar to Ser and Thr residues of nuclear and cytoplasmic proteins.27,28 As a critical post-translational modification (PTM), O-GlcNAcylation can affect protein structure and stability, and, therefore, function.27,28 OGT plays a critical role in regulating self-renewal and differentiation of ESCs, hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs), and immune cells,28–31 TET2 and TET3 are expressed in myeloid or lymphoid immune cells. In myeloid cells, Tet2 deletion, as in LysMCreTet2fl/fl mice, upregulated proinflammatory gene expression in macrophages.21 In T lymphoid lineage, combined Tet2/Tet3 deletion, as in CD4CreTet2fl/flTet3fl/fl mice, resulted in unregulated invariant natural killer T (iNKT) cell expansion.21,32 In contrast, deletion of Tet2 or Tet3 alone resulted in no or a mild phenotype, one entailing only a moderate increase in iNKT cells in thymus, spleen, and lymph nodes.21 Also, combined Tet2/Tet3 deletion in regulatory T cells, as in Foxp3CreTet2fl/flTet3fl/fl mice, impaired cell lineage specification to a greater extent than individual Tet2 or Tet3 deletion,21,23 suggesting that TET2 and TET3 are functionally redundant, at least in T lymphocytes. Combined acute Tet2/Tet3 deletion in all cell types, as in CreERT2Tet2fl/flTet3fl/flRosa26YFPLSL mice, decreased DNA 5hmC genome-wide, including the Aicda locus, leading to decreased Aicda expression and reduced NP-specific IgG1+B cell response to NP-OVA.33 Combined Tet2/Tet3 deletion in Pro-B cells, as in Mb1CreTet2fl/flTet3fl/fl mice, or in all B cells, as in CD19CreTet2fl/flTet3fl/fl mice, also led to a block of Pro-to Pre-B cell development or perturbation in mature B cell homeostasis.34,35 Finally, pan-B cell Tet2 deletion, as in VavCreTet2fl/fl mice was associated with decreased 5hmC deposition in the Prdm1 but not the Aicda locus.36,37

We have determined here that TET2, not TET3, is expressed to induce both Aicda and Prdm1 in mouse and human B cells, mediating a specific antibody response. The critical role of B cell TET2 and exclusion of TET3 in maturation of T-dependent and T-independent antibody responses was addressed using AicdaCreTet2fl/fl and AicdaCreTet3fl/fl mice. An integrative map of TET2-mediated DNA 5mC to 5hmC oxidation of Aicda and Prdm1 loci was constructed by combining our MeDIP/hMeDIP-Seq, TET2 ChIP-qPCR, and MeDIP/hMeDIP-qPCR findings from Tet2−/− B cells and AicdaCreTet2fl/flB cells with existing data.33,38,39 Prompted by the suggested TET1 interaction with OGT and subsequent protein O-GlcNAcylation in ESCs,26 we hypothesized that B cell TET2 recruits OGT through a non-catalytic adaptor function. TET2-recruited OGT would O-GlcNAcylate TET2 itself for TET2-catalyzed active DNA demethylation of Aicda and Prdm1 loci. In these loci, TET2-recruited OGT would also O-GlcNAcylate chromatin H2B-S112, leading to AID and BLIMP-1 expression, SHM/CSR, and plasma cell differentiation. This was addressed by deleting TET2 or OGT in activated and differentiating AicdaCreTet2fl/fl or AicdaCreOgtfl/fl B cells in vivo and in vitro. TET and OGT interacting factors in activated B cells were identified by immunoprecipitation (IP) of nuclear proteins with anti-TET2 and anti-OGT antibodies. TET2 interaction with OGT and DNA at Aicda and Prdm1 loci was visualized in a 3D-modeled TET2-OGT-DNA complex by leveraging the power of AlphaFold 3.0. To analyze OGT contribution to overall TET2 function, the ability of TET2 C-terminal-end (OGT-binding) deletion Tet2OGT-Δmut mutant to rescue CSR and plasma cell differentiation in Tet2−/− B cells was compared to that of the conserved signature catalytic HxD motif Tet2HxD-mut mutant, dual Tet2HxD-mut/OGT-Δmut mutant, or Tet2WT retroviral construct. TET2 catalytic activity (active DNA demethylation) and OGT catalytic activity (O-GlcNAcylation of TET2 and H2B-S112) in mouse and human B cells were also dissected using TET2 inhibitor TET-IN-C3540 and OGT inhibitor ST 045849.41 Finally, B cell TET2 ability to transduce metabolic cues that modulate maturation of specific antibody responses was analyzed in humanized THX mice and Tcrβ−/−Tcrδ−/− mice treated with TET2 inhibitor fumarate, a tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle intermediate, or TET2 activator vitamin C.

RESULTS

TET1, TET2, and TET3 in mouse and human B cells

TET1 is predominant in ESCs, while TET2 and TET3 are in HSCs and immune cells.20,21,23 TET2 and TET3, however, are different in structure and have been suggested to play different roles in HSCs and immune cells.20,21,23 To analyze Tet1/TET1, Tet2/TET2 and Tet3/TET3 expression in B lymphocytes, we stimulated mouse naïve IgM+IgD+B cells with LPS plus IL-4 and cultured them in vitro. In resting naïve B cells, Tet transcripts were expressed at low levels, and TET proteins were not detectable (Figures 1A and 1B). After B cell activation, TET1 and TET3 remained undetectable. In contrast, TET2 increased in expression, together with Aicda/AID and Prdm1/BLIMP-1, and was phosphorylated and acetylated throughout 96 h. Similarly, naïve human (hu)IgM+IgD+B cells increased intracellular TET2 expression as they differentiated to germinal center (GC)-like huCD19+CD27−CD38+B cells and huCD19loCD27+CD38+plasma cells upon stimulation with huCD154, huIL-2, huIL-4, and huIL-21 (Figure 1C). Similar information on B cell TET2 expression was gathered from spleen and mesenteric lymph nodes (MLNs) of C57BL/6 mice immunized with NP-CGG conjugated hapten as well as human tonsils (Figure 1D).

The lack of TET1 expression in B cells was consistent with a lack of TET1 role in immune cells.21,23–25,42 Similar to TET1, TET3 was not detected in differentiating B cells. However, because combined Tet2/Tet3 deletion in all cell types, as in CreERT2Tet2fl/flTet3fl/flRosa26YFPLSL mice, was shown to impair Aicda expression,33 we immunized AicdaCreTet3fl/fl mice, which delete Tet3 in activated and differentiating B cells, with NP-CGG to exclude any B cell TET3 contribution to maturation of the antibody response. Such mice showed levels of total and high-affinity NP4-binding IgM, IgG1, and IgA comparable to AicdaCreTet3+/+mice, a reflection of comparable class-switched IgG1+B cells, including NP5-specific IgG1+B cells in spleen, and plasmablasts and plasma cells in spleen and bone marrow (BM). AicdaCreTet3fl/fl mice also showed no apoptotic B cells as well as proportions of class-switched IgG3+, IgG1+, IgG2b+, and IgA+B cells, IgG3+, IgG1+, IgG2b+, and IgA+ class-switched (sw)MBCs and plasmablasts in MLNs and Peyer’s patches, comparable to AicdaCreTet3+/+ mice (Figure S1A). AicdaCreTet3fl/fl mice also expressed B cell AID and BLIMP-1 at levels comparable to AicdaCreTet3+/+ mice. They also showed normal IgA+ or IgD+B cells in intestinal lamina propria and normal IgM+B cells in Peyer’s patches (Figure S1B). The normal CSR and plasma cell differentiation of naïve IgM+IgD+AicdaCreTet3fl/flB cells stimulated in vitro with LPS or LPS plus IL-4 further confirmed that Tet3 deletion does not impact B cell differentiation (Figure S1C). Thus, naïve B cells do not express TET proteins; B cells undergoing GC and plasma cell differentiation express TET2, not TET3 or TET1. B cell TET3 deletion, as in AicdaCreTet3fl/fl mice, did not impair AID and BLIMP-1 expression, CSR, plasma cell differentiation, generation of swMBCs, or overall maturation of the antibody response.

B cell Tet2 deletion impairs maturation of the antibody response

Prompted by selective TET2 expression in mouse and human B cells, we analyzed baseline B cell differentiation and antibody levels in AicdaCreTet2fl/fl mice, which delete Tet2 in activated and differentiating B cells. Non-intentionally immunized AicdaCreTet2fl/fl mice showed lower basal levels of IgG, IgA, and IgE but not IgM than AicdaCreTet2+/+ mice and fewer IgG3+, IgG1+, IgG2b+, and IgA+B cells, swMBCs, and plasmablasts in MLNs and Peyer’s patches, with no altered cell survival (Figures S2A–S2C). They also showed fewer IgM, IgG1, and IgA antibody-secreting cells (ASCs) in MLNs, fewer IgM+ and IgA+B cells in intestinal lamina propria than AicdaCreTet2+/+ mice, and reduced free and bacteria-bound IgD43 and IgA in feces (Figures S2D and S2E).

To analyze the B cell TET2 role in the maturation of T-dependent antibody response, we immunized AicdaCreTet2fl/fl mice with NP-CGG. These mice made lower levels of total and NP4-specific IgG1 and IgA and fewer IgG1+ and IgA+ASCs than AicdaCreTet2+/+mice, but comparable levels of total and NP4-binding IgM and numbers of IgM+ASCs in spleen and BM (Figures 2A, S3A, and S3B). NP-CGG-immunized AicdaCreTet2fl/fl mice, however, displayed seemingly normal spleen GCs, GC B cells, proliferating B cells, and lack of apoptotic B cells as AicdaCreTet2+/+ mice, showing that Tet2 deletion did not impact GC formation, cell proliferation, or survival (Figures S3C–S3F). Nevertheless, AicdaCreTet2fl/fl mice showed no IgA+ or IgD+B cells in intestinal lamina propria and normal IgM+B cells in Peyer’s patches (Figure 2B). They showed reduced IgG1+GC B cells, spleen and BM plasma cells, spleen IgG1+B cells, including NP5-specific IgG1+B cells and NP5-specific IgG1+swMBCs, compared to AicdaCreTet2+/+ mice (Figures 2C and S3D–S3H). AicdaCreTet2fl/fl B cells expressed lower levels of Aicda and Prdm1 transcripts than AicdaCreTet2+/+B cells but comparable Irf4 and Bach2 transcripts (Figure 2D); enforced Aicda or Prdm1 expression in Tet2−/− B cells rescued CSR and plasma cell differentiation, while enforced Tet2 expression in Tet2+/+B cells boosted CSR and plasma cell differentiation (Figure 2E). AicdaCreTet2fl/fl mice IgG1+B cells accumulated somatic point-mutations (V1-72DJH-Cγ1 transcripts44), with high replacement (R) to silent (S) mutation ratios, at 5.9-fold lower frequency than AicdaCreTet2+/+ mice (Figure 2F).

To analyze the B cell TET2 role in the maturation of the T-independent antibody response, we immunized AicdaCreTet2fl/fl mice with NP-LPS.45 These mice made lower levels of total and NP4-specific IgG3 and IgG2b, reflecting fewer class-switched IgG3+B cells, IgG2b+B cells, and NP5-specific IgG+B cells in MLNs than AicdaCreTet2+/+ mice but comparable levels of total and NP4-binding IgM (Figures S4A and S4B). NP-LPS-immunized AicdaCreTet2fl/fl mice also showed reduced NP5-specific IgG+swMBCs and plasmablasts in MLNs. Such AicdaCreTet2fl/fl mice accumulated somatic point-mutations, at high R:S ratios, in B cell V1-72DJH-Cγ3 and V1-72DJH-Cγ2b transcripts, at a 4.3- and 5.5-fold lower frequency than their AicdaCreTet2+/+ mouse counterparts (Figure S4C). To verify that the reduced B cell AID and BLIMP-1 expression in AicdaCreTet2fl/fl mouse B cells was due to impaired active DNA demethylation of Aicda and Prdm1 loci regulatory regions, we stimulated AicdaCreTet2fl/flB cells in a T-dependent (CD154 plus IL-4) or T-independent (LPS, LPS plus IL-4, LPS plus TGF-β, IL-4, IL-5, and anti-δ mAb/dex) fashion in vitro. These B cells reduced active DNA demethylation of Aicda and Prdm1 promoters and enhancers, and decreased Aicda/AID and Prdm1/BLIMP-1 expression (Figures 2G, 2H, and S5A). They also reduced CSR (lower levels of post-recombination Iμ–Cγ1, Iμ–Cγ3, and Iμ-Cε transcripts) in spite of normal proliferation, and reduced proportions of IgG3+, IgG1+, or IgA+B cells and plasma cells, and made lower IgG1, IgG3, or IgE levels (Figures S5B–S5D). Thus, differentiation stage-specific B cell-intrinsic Tet2 deletion decreases B cell Aicda and Prdm1 loci active DNA demethylation and transcription, AID and BLIMP-1 expression, SHM/CSR, and plasma cell differentiation. It reduces basal blood and gut-associated class-switched antibody levels and impairs the maturation of T-dependent and T-independent antibody responses, with no obvious impact on GC B cell proliferation or GC formation.

B cell Ogt deletion impairs maturation of the antibody response

The suggested binding of OGT to TET1 for TET1 catalytic function in mouse and zebrafish ESCs26 prompted us to hypothesize that TET2 recruits OGT, the sole known enzyme that mediates protein O-GlcNAcylation, for B cell TET2-mediated AID and BLIMP-1 expression, peripheral B cell differentiation, and maturation of antibody response. We immunized AicdaCreOgtfl/fl mice, which delete Ogt in activated and differentiating B cells, with NP-CGG. These mice developed spleen GCs normal in size and morphology (Figure 3A). They also showed GC B cells in spleen, Peyer’s patches, and MLNs comparable to AicdaCreOgt+/+ mice, albeit reducing IgG1+ GC B cells, class-switched IgG1+, IgA+B cells, NP5-specific IgG1+B cells, and plasma cells (Figures 3A and 3B). They also showed lower levels of NP4-specific IgG1 and IgA, as well as fewer IgD and IgA in intestinal lamina propria, but normal NP4-binding IgM (Figures 3C and 3D). Further, AicdaCreOgtfl/fl mice accumulated somatic point-mutations, with high R:S mutation ratios, in B cell V1-72DJH-Cγ1 transcripts, at 1.7-fold lower frequency than AicdaCreOgt+/+ mice (Figure 3E). The relevance of OGT to overall TET2 function was emphasized by the negative impact of B cell Ogt deletion on AID and BLIMP-1 expression, CSR, and plasma cell differentiation. Likewise, AicdaCreOgtfl/flB cells stimulated in vitro with LPS, LPS plus IL-4, or LPS plus TGF-β, IL-4, IL-5, and anti-δ mAb/dex decreased intracellular AID and BLIMP-1 expression, resulting in reduced CSR to IgG3, IgG1, and IgA as well as plasma cell differentiation (Figure 4A). Thus, differentiation stage-specific B cell-intrinsic Ogt deletion impairs AID and Blimp-1 expression, SHM/CSR, and plasma cell differentiation, thereby dampening the maturation of antigen-specific antibody response.

B cell TET2 recruits OGT for its O-GlcNAcylation and catalytic DNA 5mC to 5hmC oxidation

Next, we set out to show that reduced AID and BLIMP-1 expression in activated and differentiating AicdaCreOgtfl/flB cells is due to loss of TET2 O-GlcNAcylation and impaired TET2 catalytic DNA 5mC to 5hmC oxidation of Aicda and Prdm1 loci. We stimulated AicdaCreOgtfl/fl and AicdaCreTet2fl/flB cells with LPS plus IL-4 and cultured them in vitro. OGT deletion in AicdaCreOgtfl/flB cells showed impaired 5mC to 5hmC oxidations of Aicda and Prdm1 promoter and enhancer regions to an extent comparable to that of TET2 deletion in AicdaCreTet2fl/flB cells (Figure 4B). In LPS plus IL-4-stimulated AicdaCreOgtfl/flB cells, OGT deletion denied TET2 O-GlcNAcylation and reduced overall TET2 levels (Figure 4C). TET2 was O-GlcNAcylated through 96 h post-activation (Figures 4D and S6A). TET2 O-GlcNAcylation was further supported by the identification of potential O-GlcNAcylated sites throughout mouse and human TET2, as predicted using YinOYang and O-GlcNAcPRED-DL,47 an algorithm and a deep learning tool for protein O-GlcNAcylation (Figures 4E and S6B; Table S1). In mouse TET2, we identified 38 Ser and Thr residues (out of 320 total Ser/Thr) as potential O-GlcNAcylation sites using a 0.8 threshold, higher than the 0.5 and 0.6 thresholds generally used as thresholds for predicted protein PTM, including O-GlcNA-cylation.46–48 In human TET2, we identified 46 Ser and Thr residues (out of 323 total Ser/Thr) as O-GlcNAcylation sites, at > 0.8 threshold. Further, a TET2:PROSER1:OGT49 interaction was identified by native co-IP in LPS plus IL-4-stimulated mouse naïve IgM+IgD+B cells. In these B cell nuclei, TET2 interacted with OGT, CREBBP, and PROSER1, while it did not, as expected, with HDAC1.21 Confirmation of nuclear TET2:PROSER1:OGT49 interaction came from anti-OGT IP, which brought down TET2 and PROSER1 but not CREBBP or HDAC1 (Figure 4F). Thus, B cell TET2 recruits OGT for its O-GlcNAcylation, which supports TET2’s catalytic function.

B cell TET2 recruits OGT for O-GlcNAcylation of Aicda and Prdm1 loci chromatin H2B-S112

Having determined that TET2 recruits OGT in B cell nuclei for its O-GlcNAcylation and catalytic function, we further addressed the potential role of TET2 in recruiting OGT to Aicda and Prdm1 loci for these loci’s chromatin H2B-S112 O-GlcNAcylation.50 We stimulated AicdaCreTet2fl/flB and AicdaCreTet2+/+B cells with LPS plus IL-4 and culture them in vitro. In AicdaCreTet2+/+B cells, OGT was detected at Aicda and Prdm1 promoters and enhancers (Figure 4G), at which DNA regions TET2 was also detected (Figure S7A). In such B cells, OGT catalyzed O-GlcNAcylation of Aicda and Prdm1 loci regulatory regions chromatin H2B-S112, a previously unrecognized histone modification in the B cell epigenome. The instrumental role of TET2 in OGT recruitment and subsequent H2B-S112 O-GlcNAcylation was further supported by failure to detect OGT and chromatin H2B-S112 O-GlcNAcylation in Aicda and Prdm1 regulatory regions in AicdaCreTet2fl/flB cells. Thus, B cell TET2 recruits OGT for its O-GlcNAcylation and active DNA demethylation (catalytic function) as well as for O-GlcNAcylation of chromatin H2B-S112 at Aicda and Prdm1 regulatory regions (non-catalytic, adaptor function).

3D modeling of TET2-OGT-DNA complex at Aicda Area IV or Prdm1 Area II

Having shown TET2-OGT catalytic and non-catalytic functions in B cell Aicda and Prdm1 loci expression, we set up to gain insight into the TET2-OGT-DNA complex. We first determined the Tet2 deletion impact on dynamic 5mC to 5hmC oxidation at the same C nucleotide residues of Aicda and Prdm1 loci. As compared to Tet2+/+B cells, Tet2−/− B cells stimulated with LPS or CD154 plus IL-4 decreased genome-wide DNA 5hmC and increased 5mC, as revealed by anti-5mC and anti-5hmC Ab immune dot-blots and MeDIP-Seq and hMeDIP-Seq (Figures S7B and S7C). By detailing 5mC and 5hmC distribution and levels, we constructed a map of Aicda and Prdm1 loci and annotated Areas of active DNA demethylation, denoted as Areas I–V (Figure S7D). Aicda Area I corresponded to previously reported region 1 (Aicda promoter), while Areas II, III and IV included regions 2, 3, and 4.38 Area IV also included TetE1 and Area V TetE2.33 Prdm1 Areas I and IV included promoter regions 1A and 1B.39 Prdm1 Areas II, III, and V, not previously recognized as regulatory regions, comprised low frequency of CpG islands,51 thereby identifying potential enhancer regions.

Because no crystallography structure of TET2-OGT or TET2-OGT-DNA complex is currently available, we used AlphaFold 3.0, an advanced deep learning platform with updated diffusion architecture and expanded capacity of predicting biomolecular ensembles involving proteins and nucleic acids,52 to 3D model the TET2-OGT-DNA complex at Aicda Area IV and Prdm1 Area II. Mouse TET2 isoform 2, including the critical Cys-N, Cys-C, DSBH (double-stranded β-helix), low complexity insert, and DSBH 1042–1920 aa sequence,53 bound to Aicda locus Area IV and Prdm1 locus Area II. In both Aicda and Prdm1 loci, TET2 accommodated double-stranded DNA through its conserved signature H1303xD1305 motif in a catalytic cavity (Figure 5A). The angle of such TET2 catalytic cavity varied depending on different TET2 isoforms and DNA structures (Aicda Area IV or Prdm1 Area II) (Figures S8 and S9), leading to different TET2-OGT-DNA complexes. In both Aicda Area IV and Prdm1 Area II DNA, TET2 extensively interacts with OGT through its C-terminal-end, thereby validating our structural inference extrapolated from the suggested TET1-OGT interaction in ESCs.26

Deletion of TET2 OGT-binding C-terminal end impairs CSR and plasma cell differentiation to a degree comparable to that of mutation of TET2 catalytic H1303YxD1305A motif

As suggested by our TET2-OGT-DNA 3D model involving Aicda and Prdm1 loci regulatory regions, TET2 interacts with OGT through its C-terminal-end. To analyze the role of TET2 C-terminal-end in TET2 OGT recruitment and TET2-modulated B cell CSR and plasma cell differentiation, we generated a mouse Tet2WT isoform 2 construct encoding conserved signature catalytic HxD motif and the whole C-terminal-end (E990-V1920),54,55 a TET2 isoform 2 catalytic Tet2HxD-mut mutant (H1303YxD1305A) construct defective in DNA demethylation activity (tested in neoplastic kidney 293T cells54), a TET2 isoform 2 C-terminal-end deletion Tet2OGT-Δmut mutant (G1876-V1920) construct encoding a TET2 protein putatively incapable of recruiting OGT, and a dual Tet2 HxD-mut/OGT-Δmut mutant construct encoding a TET2 devoid of both catalytic HxD site activity and OGT-binding adaptor function (Figure 5B). TET2 C-terminal-end deletion was based on findings in ESCs that TET1 binds OGT through its C-terminal end, which is conserved in mouse and human TET1, TET2, and TET326; pMIG-Empty, Tet2WT, Tet2HxD-mut, Tet2OGT-Δmut, and Tet2HxD-mut/OGT-Δmut constructs were used to transduce Tet2−/−B cells, which were cultured after induction by LPS plus IL-4. Tet2−/− B cells transduced by Tet2WT, Tet2HxD-mut, Tet2OGT-Δmut, and Tet2HxD-mut/OGT-Δmut constructs exhibit a comparable level of TET2 expression, while Tet2−/− B cells transduced by the pMIG-Empty construct did not express TET2 (Figure 5C). In induced Tet2−/− B cells, enforced Tet2HxD-mut expression rescued CSR to IgG1 and plasma cell differentiation by 69.7% and 52.3%, respectively, of enforced Tet2WT expression (Figure 5C), to levels comparable to those of Tet2HxD-mut/HxD-mutB cells (Figures S6C and S6D). Similarly, enforced expression of Tet2OGT-Δmut, which included an unmutated conserved HxD motif, rescued CSR to IgG1 and plasma cell differentiation by approximately 53.9% and 41.0%, respectively, as inefficiently as Tet2HxD-mut catalytic HxD site mutant. Finally, enforced Tet2HxD-mut/OGT-Δmut expression in Tet2−/− B cells resulted in more limited IgG1 CSR rescue but plasma cell differentiation comparable to individual Tet2HxD-mut or Tet2OGT-Δmut mutants.

Small molecule TET2 catalytic inhibitor TET-IN-C35 and/or OGT inhibitor ST 045849 impair B cell AICDA and PRDM1 5hmC oxidation, reduce AID and BLIMP-1 expression, CSR, and plasma cell differentiation

To extend the analysis of TET2-OGT interaction to human B cells, we stimulated naïve huIgM+IgD+B cells with CpG plus huIL-2, huIL-4, and huIL-21 and cultured them in the presence of TET2 catalytic inhibitor TET-IN-C35,40 OGT inhibitor ST 04584941 or both small molecule inhibitors. Individual TET2 and OGT inhibitors impaired huIgG CSR and plasma cell differentiation to comparable degrees, reflecting comparable reductions of TET2-catalyzed 5mC to 5hmC oxidation of AICDA and PRDM1 loci regulatory regions, resulting in similar reductions in AICDA/AID and PRDM1/BLIMP-1 expression (Figures S10A–S10C). In addition to TET2-catalyzed active DNA demethylation, the OGT inhibitor impaired AICDA and PRDM1 loci H2B-S112 O-GlcNAcylation. Combined TET2 and OGT inhibitors reduced 5mC to 5hmC oxidation and H2B-S112 O-GlcNAcylation, expression of AICDA/AID and PRDM1/BLIMP-1, CSR, and plasma cell differentiation to degrees comparable to that of either TET2 or OGT individual inhibitor (Figures S10A–S10D). By contrast, in similar human B cells, the TET2 inhibitor resulted in deficient active DNA demethylation but effective chromatin H2B-S112 O-GlcNAcylation of AICDA and PRDM1 promoter and enhancer regions. TET-IN-C35 and ST 045849 inhibition of TET2-catalyzed 5mC to 5hmC oxidation of Aicda and Prdm1 loci, Aicda/AID and Prdm1/BLIMP-1 expression, CSR, and plasma cell differentiation was reproduced in mouse B cells stimulated by LPS plus IL-4 (Figures S11A–S11C). Thus, specific small molecule inhibition of TET2 catalytic activity and/or OGT function impairs 5mC to 5hmC oxidation of AICDA/Aicda and PRDM1/Prdm1 loci regulatory regions to dampen human and mouse B cell AID and BLIMP-1 expression, CSR, and plasma cell differentiation.

Fumarate inhibits B cell TET2 to impair AID and BLIMP-1 expression, SHM/CSR, plasma cell and swMBC differentiation, and maturation of human and mouse T-dependent and T-independent antibody responses

TET2 catalytic activity requires Fe++ and α-KG co-factors.23,56 We addressed the ability of fumarate, a TCA cycle metabolite that hampers α-KG binding to the TET2 catalytic domain,57 to impair the maturation of human antibody response. Humanized THX mice58 were immunized with NP-CGG and fed dimethyl fumarate (DMF) in water. Upon oral administration, DMF is hydrolyzed by esterase to monomethyl fumarate, which is further converted to fumarate.59 In these THX mice, DMF impaired intracellular human B cell TET2, AID and BLIMP-1 expression and reduced total and NP4-specific huIgG1 and huIgA but not NP4-binding huIgM as compared to THX mice fed nil-water (Figures 6A and 6B), a reflection of reduced total and NP5-specific huIgG1+ and huIgA+B cells, total and class-switched human plasmablasts as well as total and swMBCs60 in spleen and BM (Figures 6C and 6D). DMF-fed THX mice huIgG1+B cells accumulated somatic point-mutations at a lower frequency and lower R:S ratios than nil-water fed THX mice (Figure 6E). DMF inhibition of B cell TET2-mediated AID and BLIMP-1 expression, SHM/CSR, plasma cell differentiation, and generation of swMBCs also extended to maturation of T-independent antibody response in NP-LPS-immunized Tcrβ−/− Tcrδ−/− mice,45 in which any contribution of TET2-expressing T cells was excluded (Figures S12A–S12D). Thus, metabolic inhibition of TET2 by fumarate impairs maturation of human and mouse T-dependent and T-independent antibody responses by dampening B cell AID and BLIMP-1 expression, SHM/CSR, and differentiation of plasma cells and swMBCs.

Vitamin C boosts B cell TET2 catalytic activity, Aicda/AICDA and Prdm1/PRDM1 expression, SHM/CSR, plasma cell differentiation, and T cell-independent anti-NP-LPS antibody response in Tcrβ−/− Tcrδ−/− mice

Vitamin C would activate TETs by replenishing the cellular Fe++ pool through Fe+++ reduction, as TET proteins utilize a Fe4+-oxo intermediate for catalytic activity and stepwise 5mC oxidation.23 Combined Tet2/Tet3 deletion in CD19CreTet2fl/flTet3fl/fl or Cr2CreTet2fl/flTet3fl/flB cells has suggested a TET2/TET3-dependent increase in BLIMP-1 and plasma cell differentiation by vitamin C in vitro.61,62 We furthered the analysis of vitamin C-induced B cell TET2 activity and BLIMP-1 expression in vivo and extended it to include AID expression. Further, to rule out any potential role of T cells that can also express TET2,21 we immunized Tcrβ−/− Tcrδ−/− mice with (T-independent) NP-LPS45 and treated them with vitamin C. These Tcrβ−/− Tcrδ−/− mice responded with elevated NP4- and LPS-specific IgG3 and IgG2b, but not NP4- or LPS-binding IgM, as compared to non-vitamin C-treated mice. In vitamin C-treated Tcrβ−/− Tcrδ−/− mice, increased levels of NP4-specific IgG3 and IgG2b antibodies reflected increased numbers of NP5-specific IgG3+ and IgG2b+ASCs in spleen and BM, as well as increased total and NP5-specific IgG3+ and IgG2b+B cells, and increased plasma cells in spleen and MLNs, in spite of comparable proportions of GC B cells (Figures 7A and 7B). NP-LPS-immunized Tcrβ−/− Tcrδ−/− mice IgG3+ and IgG2b+B cells increased AID and BLIMP-1, but not, as expected, TET2 expression and accumulated somatic point-mutations (2.19 × 10−2 and 1.35 × 10−2 change/base with high R:S mutation ratios) at a 3.4- and 1.9 fold greater frequency than IgG3+ and IgG2b+B cells of non-vitamin C-treated mice (Figures 7C, 7D, and S12E). B cell-intrinsic TET2 metabolic boost by vitamin C was reproduced in purified mouse naÿve IgM+IgD+B cells stimulated by LPS plus IL-4. These B cells increased Aicda and Prdm1 expression, CSR to IgG1, and plasma cell differentiation (Figure 7E), reflecting increased TET2 recruitment to Aicda and Prdm1 loci, enhanced DNA 5mC to 5hmC oxidation, and increased chromatin H2B-S112 O-GlcNAcylation of these gene loci. A similar vitamin C activity extended to human B cells. Naÿve huIg-M+IgD+B cells stimulated by CpG plus huIL-2, huIL-4, and huIL-21 and cultured in the presence of vitamin C increased 5mC to 5hmC oxidation, expression of AICDA and PRDM1, boosted CSR and plasma cell differentiation, and increased proportion of MBCs (Figure 6F). Thus, metabolic activation of TET2 by vitamin C enhances Aicda/AICDA and Prdm1/PRDM1 loci OGT-dependent O-GlcNAcylation and TET2-catalyzed active DNA demethylation for these loci transcription, AID and BLIMP-1 expression, SHM/CSR, and plasma cell differentiation in a B cell-intrinsic, T cell-independent fashion.

DISCUSSION

Our findings show that TETs are not detectable in resting naÿve B cells and only TET2 is induced in mouse and human B cells, in a differentiation-stage-specific fashion. B cell differentiation stage-specific TET2 expression was reflected in vivo in GC B cells and plasmablasts in spleen and MLNs of NP-CGG-immunized C57BL/6 mice, NP-LPS-immunized Tcrβ−/− Tcrδ−/− mice, NP-CGG-immunized humanized THX mice, and human tonsils. In B cells undergoing GC-like differentiation, TET2 was expressed, phosphorylated, acetylated, and O-GlcNAcylated for Aicda/AID and Prdm1/BLIMP-1 expression. O-GlcNAcylation of B cell TET2, as mediated by TET2-recruited OGT, would be critical for this epigenetic factor’s catalytic activity at Aicda/AICDA and Prdm1/PRDM1 loci regulatory regions. OGT, as recruited by TET2, also serves as a histone modifier (H2B-S112 O-GlcNAcylation) to facilitate chormatin accessibility and transcription of such loci. TET2 recruits OGT in the B cell nucleus to promote AID and BLIMP-1 expression, SHM/CSR, and plasma cell differentiation for maturation of mouse and human T-dependent and T-independent antibody responses.

TET proteins, particularly TET1, play an important role in DNA demethylation during ESC differentiation.25 TET2 and TET3 exert functions in HSCs and immune cells, particularly T cells, affecting active DNA demethylation in physiological and pathological processes.20,21,63–66 The different impacts of combined Tet2/Tet3 deletion versus individual Tet2 or Tet3 deletion on iNKT cell differentiation led to the postulation of a reciprocal redundant activity of these two epigenetic modifiers.21,32 In B cells, only the impact of combined Tet2/Tet3 deletion, not individual Tet2 or Tet3 deletion, on NP-binding B cells, as in NP-OVA-immunized CreERT2Tet2fl/flTet3fl/flRosa26YFPLSL mice, was explored.33 Our findings that B cell TET2 (AicdaCreTet2fl/fl), not TET3 (AicdaCreTet3fl/fl), deletion impairs maturation of T-dependent and T-independent antibody responses support the contention that either TET3 plays no role in peripheral B cell differentiation or TET3 does, but its absence (Tet3 ablation) is fully compensated by TET2. In contrast, TET3 cannot compensate for TET2 deletion in B cells. TET2 role in B cell differentiation is further supported by full rescue of CSR to IgG1 by enforced TET2 expression in combined Tet2/Tet3 deletion CreERT2Tet2fl/flTet3fl/flRosa26YFPLSLB cells.33

Mouse and human TET2 differ from TET1 and TET3 in that it lost its CXXC zinc finger domain, which is retained in TET1 and TET3 N-end, likely due to a chromosomal inversion event that gave rise to the discrete Tet2/TET2 CXXC4 or IDAX gene23; IDAX CXXC domain binds to unmethylated CpG dinucleotides in DNA, to which it recruits TET2.55 In hematopoietic cells’ malignant transformation, including acute myeloid lymphoma (AML), diffuse large B cell lymphoma (DLBCL, a GC B cell malignancy), peripheral T cell lymphoma (PTCL), and angioimmunoblastic T cell leukemia (AITL), mutations leading to loss-of-function are more frequent in coding regions of TET2 than TET1 or TET3.21,36,67 Interestingly, TET2 loss-of-function has also been observed in the absence of TET2 mutations, suggesting a critical yet unclear mechanism, possibly involving protein PTM, in modulating this epigenetic modifier’s function in lymphoid cells malignant transformation.20

OGT is the only known enzyme that can attach a GlcNAc sugar to Ser and Thr in cytoplasmic, mitochondrial, and nuclear proteins.27,28 As a metabolic sensor, OGT relies on the concentration of cellular UDP-GlcNAc, the end-product of the hexosamine biosynthetic pathway, to catalyze protein O-GlcNAcylation.30 Nucleocytoplasmic OGT consists of an N-terminal protein-protein contact domain and a C-terminal catalytic glycotransferase domain.41 O-GlcNAcylation occurs reciprocally or sequentially with phosphorylation in the same or neighboring residues, potentiates protein acetylation, and blocks ubiquitin-mediated protein degradation.28,68–74 By O-GlcNAcylating TET2, OGT would enable TET2-catalyzed active DNA demethylation in mouse and human B cell Aicda and Prdm1 loci regulatory regions. Together with phosphorylation and acetylation, O-GlcNAcylation would contribute to stabilizing TET2 for catalytic activity in differentiating peripheral B cells. In mouse TET2, we identified 38 O-GlcNAcylation sites, which include all the 21 O-GlcNAcylation sites previously detected by mass spectrometry in mouse TET2,75 supporting the validity of our analysis. In human TET2, we identified 46 high-confidence O-GlcNAcylation sites. Notably, our predicted O-GlcNAcylation sites in both mouse and human TET2 were enriched in the N terminus and low-complexity insertion domain, regions known to preferentially harbor PTMs, as assessed by spectrometry.75 Although in vitro DNA demethylation assay for O-GlcNAcylated TET2 was not included in our study, loss of TET2 OGT-recruitment adaptor function, as shown in Tet2OGT-Δmut (OGT-binding) deletion mutant, impaired TET2’s ability to restore CSR and plasma cell differentiation to an extent comparable to that of Tet2HxD-mut (catalytic) mutant. Further, in AicdaCreOgtfl/flB cells, loss of TET2 O-GlcNAcylation and reduced TET2 overall protein impaired TET2-catalyzed 5mC to 5hmC oxidation in Aicda and Prdm1 loci promoters and enhancers to levels comparable to those of AicdaCreTet2fl/flB cells. Accordingly, Ogt deletion in AicdaCreOgtfl/fl mice impaired maturation of the antibody response, including B cell AID and BLIMP-1 expression, SHM/CSR, and plasma cell differentiation, by an order of magnitude comparable to that of AicdaCreTet2fl/fl mice.

O-GlcNAcylation contribution to TET2-catalyzed active DNA demethylation in mouse and human B cells, as shown here, mirrors O-GlcNAcylation contribution to TET1-catalyzed active DNA demethylation, as evidenced by mouse and zebrafish ESCs by Ogt deletion or TET1 T535 (O-GlcNAcylation site) mutation, which decreases TET1 level and function despite normal Tet1 expression.76 Interestingly, while TET2 mediates Aicda/AICDA and Prdm1/PRDM1 expression in mouse and human B cells, TET1 can repress genes in mouse ESCs by interacting with repression-associated chromatin factors Sin3A and CHD4.76 In such ESCs, OGT may prevent TET1-dependent expression of transposable elements and protect heterochromatin integrity,77 as occurring in cellular neoplastic transformation and aging, suggesting that OGT-mediated O-GlcNAcylation of TETs may lead to TET-mediated gene activation or repression depending on cell type or context (heterochromatin versus euchromatin).77

TET2 recruits OGT through direct and/or indirect interaction through PROSER1 bridging, as evidenced by native protein co-IPs of nuclear extracts from B cells undergoing CSR and plasma cell differentiation. OGT recruitment, as dependent on TET2, resulted in O-GlcNAcylation of not only TET2 but also chromatin H2B-S11250 of Aicda and Prdm1 loci promoter and enhancer regions. Accordingly, Tet2 deletion, as in AicdaCreTet2fl/flB cells, not only aborted OGT recruitment to Aicda and Prdm1 loci regulatory regions but also reduced chromatin H2B-S112 O-GlcNAcylation of such loci, further outlining a critical and previously unrecognized TET2 non-catalytic OGT recruitment adaptor function in physiologically differentiating B cells. In human promyelocytic leukemia cells, H2B-S112 O-GlcNAcylation has been shown to promote H2B monoubiquitination at Lys120, leading to transcriptional activation.68 Importantly, OGT-enabled H2B-S112 O-GlcNAcylation would contribute, together with TET2-catalyzed active DNA demethylation, to Aicda and Prdm1 loci epigenomic changes and, therefore, expression, as also evidenced in mouse and human B cells treated with TET2 and/or OGT catalytic inhibitor. The relative contribution of OGT-mediated chromatin H2B-S112 O-GlcNAcylation and TET2 active DNA demethylation to Aicda and Prdm1 loci epigenome and, therefore, transcription in GC-differentiating B cells would deserve further investigation.

Interestingly, OGT has been suggested to O-GlcNAcylate BCR signaling transducer Lyn for B cell homeostasis, and Ogt deletion by CD19Cre promoted Pre-B cell apoptosis, thereby reducing follicular B cell numbers.78 By contrast, NP-CGG-immunized AicdaCreOgtfl/fl mice, which delete Ogt in activated and differentiating B cells, maintained normal proportions of GC B cells in spleen, MLNs, and Peyer’s patches, further indicating that Ogt deletion reduces SHM/CSR and plasma cell differentiation by negating B cell nuclear O-GlcNAcylation of TET2 and chromatin H2B-S112, without impacting GC B cell survival. While excluding the effect of OGT deletion on GC B cell survival and proliferation, our experiments focused on TET2 and did not address the impact of OGT loss on global protein modification in GC B cells. Further supporting the contribution of O-GlcNAcylation to TET2-catalyzed active DNA demethylation, OGT ST 045849 small molecule inhibitor impaired 5mC to 5hmC oxidation in mouse and human B cells Aicda/AICDA and Prdm1/PRDM1 loci regulatory regions as efficiently as TET catalytic TET-IN-C35 small molecule inhibitor.

Combined acute Tet2/Tet3 deletion in all cell types, as in CreERT2Tet2fl/flTet3fl/flRosa26YFPLSL mice, decreased Aicda locus super enhancer DNA 5hmC deposition,33 and pan-B cell Tet2 deletion, as in VavCreTet2fl/fl mice, was associated with decreased 5hmC deposition in Prdm1 but not in Aicda locus.36,37 As shown here, differentiating B cell-specific Tet2 deletion, as in AicdaCreTet2fl/fl mice and AicdaCreTet2fl/fl B cell in vitro, impaired 5mC to 5hmC oxidation in both Aicda and Prdm1 loci promoters and enhancers. AlphaFold 3.0 modeled TET2 isoform 2-OGT-DNA complex involving Aicda Area IV and Prdm1 Area II, as newly identified by our integrative map of our current and others’ reported data,33,38,39 furthers the visualization of crystallized a TET2-DNA structure53 and expands it to include OGT. It revealed a shallow TET2 cavity53 that centers on the signature conserved HxD motif to accommodate Aicda Area IV and Prdm1 Area II. Such TET2 cavity hints at the catalytic mechanism by which this mononuclear non-heme iron oxygenase actively demethylates Aicda and Prdm1 DNA after having locked onto it. 3D modeling of TET2-OGT-DNA complex, as further involving TET2 isoforms 1 and X2, also revealed a conserved spatial structure and identical amino acid residues mediating C-terminal-end interaction with OGT, further supporting the functional relevance of this TET2-OGT-binding interface. TET2 isoforms 1 and X2 adopt a conformation allowing for a catalytic cavity similar to that of TET2 isoform 2 for Aicda and Prdm1 active demethylation. Finally, TET2-OGT-DNA complex AlphaFold 3.0 modeling shows that TET2 C-terminal-end residues (in all three TET2 isoforms) exhibit extensive contact with OGT, similar to TET1-OGT interaction in zebrafish ESCs,26 further emphasizing the role of TET C-terminal end in recruiting OGT.

As we show, the catalytic activity of TET2 at AICDA and PRDM1 loci regulatory regions requires O-GlcNAcylation of this active DNA demethylator, as mediated by metabolic sensor OGT. As we further show, TET2 directly takes cues from the metabolic environment to modulate B cell AID and BLIMP-1 expression, as it relies on co-factors Fe++ and α-KG21 for its catalytic activity. Replenished cellular Fe++ pool, as mediated by vitamin C reduction of Fe+++, leads to enhanced TETs catalytic activity.57,61,79 Our findings further the scope of plasma cell differentiation studies in vitro61,62 and extend them to address the vitamin C impact on SHM/CSR and plasma cell differentiation in vivo. In NP-LPS-immunized Tcrβ−/− Tcrδ−/− mice fed a regular diet, they revealed a T cell-independent and B cell-intrinsic vitamin C boost of class-switched, high-affinity antibodies to NP and to LPS—Gulo−/− mice, deficient in L-gulono-γ-lactone oxidase, were not chosen for this study to avoid potential confounding effects related to vitamin C deficiency, as such mice develop human scurvy-like disease.80–82 Our findings also show that vitamin C increases B cell nuclear 5mC to 5hmC oxidation for active DNA demethylation, as well as OGT-mediated AICDA and PRDM1 loci H2B-S112 O-GlcNAcylation to increase mouse and human AID and BLIMP-1 expression. Finally, they determined that vitamin C also increases TET2 deposition at AICDA and PRDM1 loci, likely by increasing Fe++ and Fe++-mediated TET2 stabilization at these loci regulatory regions.

As both components of the TCA cycle, TET2 co-factor α-KG and its antagonist fumarate, a potent TETs catalytic inhibitor,57,79 also underline the metabolic regulation of this DNA active demethylator. DMF dampened maturation of T-dependent and T-independent antibody responses by impairing B cell SHM/CSR, plasma cell differentiation, and generation of swMBCs in humanized THX mice and Tcrβ−/− Tcrδ−/− mice, in which B cell TET2 expression was reduced together with AID and BLIMP-1. The reduced numbers of swMBCs could merely reflect reduced CSR and/or impaired overall generation of MBCs. Our findings that B cell TET2 recruits OGT to play a central role in maturation of the antibody response in mouse and human B cells provide important insight into peripheral physiological B cell differentiation and may open new avenues of investigation of dysfunctional TET2 conditions, as in neoplastic B cell transformation. In vivo B cell TET2 modulation by vitamin C and fumarate further highlights the importance of the metabolic context in the physiological regulation of TET2 epigenetic modifier. As a TET2 activator, vitamin C could find a therapeutic application in conditions of B cell TET2 functional deficiency, such as occurring in certain patients with DLBCL or other B cell lymphomas.21,23,83 DMF, a therapeutic for relapsing multiple sclerosis and psoriasis,59 could be used to dampen B cell hyperreactivity, as occurring in autoantibody-mediated systemic autoimmunity of systemic lupus erythematosus,84,85 a condition predominantly affecting females over males (10:1). In this light, because Ogt/OGT is an X-linked gene, OGT-enabled TET2 catalytic activity for B cell differentiation would be intricately linked to X chromosome dosage effect and sex-biased susceptibility. Importantly, as shown here, in addition to OGT, TET2 catalytic activity is controlled by the physiologic TCA metabolite fumarate.

Limitations of the study

Our experiments provide a string of evidence that deletion of OGT or inhibition of OGT by a small molecule leads to defective active DNA demethylation of B cell Aicda and Prdm1 loci regulatory regions, as well as reduced AID and BLIMP-1 expression, CSR, and plasma cell differentiation to a comparable level to what resulted from TET2 deletion. They, however, do not quantify the relative contribution of H2B-S112 O-GlcNAcylation to overall B cell AID and BLIMP-1 expression. This could be addressed by further quantitative, sequential biochemical experiments.

METHOD DETAILS

Mouse and human specific antibody responses

The maturation of T-dependent and/or T-independent antibody responses was analyzed in AicdaCreTet2+/+, AicdaCreTet2fl/fl, AicdaCreTet3+/+, AicdaCreTet3fl/fl, AicdaCreOgt+/+, AicdaCreOgtfl/fl and Tcrβ−/− Tcrδ−/− mice (8–12 weeks of age), as well as humanized THX mice (20–22 weeks of age). AicdaCreTet2+/+, AicdaCreTet2fl/fl, AicdaCreTet3+/+, AicdaCreTet3fl/fl, AicdaCreOgt+/+ and AicdaCreOgtfl/fl mice were injected intraperitoneally (i.p.) with 4-hydroxy-3-nitrophenylacetyl (NP) conjugated to chicken gamma globulin (NP16-CGG, 16 NP molecules conjugated per one CGG molecule; N-5055C-5, Biosearch Technologies) on day 0 (100 μg in 100 μL alum, Inject Alum Adjuvant, 77161, Thermo Fisher Scientific), boosted on day 14 (100 μg in 100 μL PBS) and euthanized on day 28. AicdaCreTet2+/+ and AicdaCreTet2fl/fl mice were injected i.p. with NP0.4-LPS (NP conjugated to E. coli O55:B5 LPS, 2 NP molecules conjugated per 5 LPS molecules, Biosearch Technologies) on day 0 (20 μg in 100 μL PBS), boosted on day 21 (20 μg in 100 μL PBS) and euthanized on day 28.

Ten humanized THX mice with a fully developed human immune system (human CD45+ cells consistently ≥90%), fulfilling the criteria and experimental timeline established in our THX studies,58 were used in the Nil/DMF treatment experiments. All 10 THX mice were comparable in terms of human immune system development at baseline. Specifically, the 5 THX mice to be treated by Nil showed 91.1% ± 0.9% human CD45+ cells, 76.8% ± 4.0% human CD19+ B cells and 15.3% ± 3.1% human CD3+ T cells (% of human CD45+ cells); the 5 THX mice to be treated by DMF showed 90.5% ± 0.7% human CD45+ cells, 74.8% ± 3.2% human CD19+ B cells and 16.1% ± 2.4% human CD3+ T cells (% of human CD45+ cells). THX mice in the Nil and DMF groups were age-matched, randomly assigned to treatment groups, and included both female and male animals. THX mice were fed DMF (2 mM = 0.5 mg/mL), a dimethyl ester of fumarate and potent TETs catalytic site inhibitor, and E2 (0.75 μM) or E2 (0.75 μM) alone in drinking water ad libitum on day 0 and continued thereafter. DMF is an orally bioavailable dimethyl ester of the TCA cycle intermediate fumaric acid. Upon oral administration, the compound undergoes initial metabolism by esterase, yielding its primary active metabolite MMF, and, thereby, the physiological TCA intermediate, fumarate.59 These THX mice were injected i.p. with NP16-CGG on day 7 (25 μg in 25 μL alum), boosted (25 μg in 25 μL PBS) on days 14, 21, and 28 and euthanized on day 35.

Tcrβ−/− Tcrδ−/− mice, in which any contribution of TET2-expressing T cells21 is excluded, were fed DMF (2 mM), or nil in drinking water ad libitum on day 0 and continued thereafter to analyze the TET2 inhibitory effect by DMF in a T-independent antibody response. Such mice were injected i.p. with NP0.4-LPS on day 7 (25 μg in 25 μL alum), boosted (25 μg in 25 μL PBS) on day 21 and euthanized on day 35. For boosting TET2 activity in a T-independent antibody response, Tcrβ−/− Tcrδ−/− mice were injected i.p. with vitamin C (80 mg in 400 μL PBS) or PBS on days −1, 1, 3 and 5. Such mice were immunized i.p. with NP0.4-LPS on day 0 (50 μg in 50 μL alum), boosted (25 μg in 25 μL PBS) on days 14 and 23 and euthanized on day 44. Serum was collected at indicated time points for titration of total and NP-specific as well as LPS-specific mouse or human antibodies using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs).6,7,10,15,16,18,19,87–89 VHDJH-CH transcripts from mouse and human B cells were analyzed for SHM/CSR as described in “mouse and human SHM” and “mouse and human B cell transcripts.”

Mouse B cells and in vitro B cell differentiation including CSR

Cells from mouse bone marrow, spleen, MLNs and/or Peyer’s patches were suspended in ACK Lysis Buffer (BP10-548E, Lonza) for erythrocyte lysis. The lysis reaction was quenched with FBS-RPMI (RPMI-1640 medium, 10-040-CV, Corning, supplemented with 10% v/v Hyclone fetal bovine serum (42Q7980K, Gibco) and 1% v/v antibiotic-antimycotic solution (penicillin-streptomycin and amphotericin B, SV30079.01, Cytiva Life Sciences)), followed by centrifugation. Erythrocyte-free cells were resuspended in FBS-RPMI for further preparation or analysis.15,16,18,19,43,45 To analyze B cell differentiation (naÿve B cells, class-switched B cells, GC B cells, plasmablasts, plasma cells, memory B cells) including CSR, naÿve IgM+IgD+B cells were isolated from spleen of C57BL/6, Tet2−/−, Tet2HxD-mut/HxD-mut, AicdaCreTet2+/+, AicdaCreTet2fl/fl, AicdaCreTet3+/+, AicdaCreTet3fl/fl, AicdaCreOgt+/+ and AicdaCreOgtfl/fl mice by negative selection using the EasySep Mouse B Cell Isolation Kit (19854, StemCell Technologies) following the manufacturer’s protocol, yielding at least 98% IgM+IgD+B cells. Mouse naÿve IgM+IgD+B cells were cultured in FBS-RPMI (5.0 x 105 cells ml−1) supplemented with 50 mM β-mercaptoethanol for 72 h (for RNA transcript analysis) or for up to 96 h (for fluorescence flow cytometry analysis) upon stimulation with E. coli O55:B5 LPS (4 μg mL−1, L2637, Sigma-Aldrich) for CSR to IgG3, and LPS or CD154 (1 U ml−1; obtained from membrane fragments of baculovirus-infected Sf21 insect cells) plus IL-4 (5.0 ng mL−1, R&D Systems) for CSR to IgG1. ChIP assays were performed as previously described.15,16,43,94 Briefly, B cells (1.0 × 107) were treated with formaldehyde (1% v/v) for 10 min at 25°C to crosslink chromatin. TET2-DNA-OGT AlphaFold 3.0 modeling

Predicted structural models of TET2-DNA-OGT complex were generated using AlphaFold 3.0, a deep learning-based structure prediction framework capable of modeling multi-component biomolecular assemblies, including protein-protein and protein-nucleic acid complexes. AlphaFold 3.0 infers three-dimensional structures directly from primary sequence information by leveraging evolutionary relationships and learned physical constraints and provides confidence metrics to evaluate the reliability of predicted interactions.52 Protein sequences corresponding to murine TET2 (isoform 2: NP_001333665.1; isoform 1: NP_001035490.2; isoform X2: XP_006501349.1) and murine OGT (UQT69321.1) were retrieved from NCBI database. TET2 isoforms were modeled to account for potential isoform-specific structural differences and to ensure that predicted interactions with OGT were not dependent on a single protein variant. To incorporate DNA into the complex, a 35-base-pair double-stranded DNA sequence derived from regulatory regions of Aicda and Prdm1 loci was used.

Structure predictions were performed using AlphaFold 3.0 in multimer mode, which allows simultaneous modeling of multiple macromolecules within a single prediction. Default parameters, which included the usage of structural templates with release dates before September 20, 2021, were used for all runs, without the use of experimentally derived structural templates or externally imposed restraints. This ensured that predicted interactions were derived solely from AlphaFold’s learned representations and sequence-based inference rather than from prior structural knowledge. For each modeling run, AlphaFold 3.0 generated multiple candidate structures ranked by internal confidence metrics. The highest-ranked models were selected for visualization. Because no crystallography or other determined structure of TET2–OGT or TET2-OGT-DNA complex is currently available, model reliability was evaluated using AlphaFold 3.0 confidence metrics rather than direct structural comparison. Molecular interface-predicted template modeling (ipTM) score was used to assess confidence in the predicted interfaces between interacting components. The ipTM metric provides a quantitative estimate of how reliably AlphaFold predicts the relative positioning of interacting molecules within a complex, with higher values indicating greater confidence in interfacial architecture. All analyzed models exhibited ipTM values greater than 0.6, consistent with a stable and biologically plausible interaction interface. Predicted aligned error (PAE) matrices were also examined to assess confidence in the relative spatial positioning of individual residues and domains. PAE values represent AlphaFold’s estimated error in the predicted distance between pairs of residues, allowing evaluation of domain-level organization and inter-domain flexibility. PAE analysis revealed low predicted error within the C-terminal region of TET2, beginning at approximately amino acid 1208 and extending through the C terminus, encompassing the catalytic domain and adjacent structural elements. In contrast, higher predicted error was observed in the N-terminal region of TET2, consistent with increased conformational flexibility and reduced structural constraint in these regions. Predicted structures were further examined for interfacial features, including hydrogen bonding, hydrophobic contacts, and estimated contact surface Area, using UCSF ChimeraX (https://www.cgl.ucsf.edu/chimerax/). Structural visualizations were generated from representative high-confidence models for figure preparation.

AlphaFold model demonstrates substantially improved accuracy over many previous specialized tools: far greater accuracy for protein–ligand interactions compared with state-of-the-art docking tools, much higher accuracy for protein–nucleic acid interactions compared with nucleic-acid-specific predictors and substantially higher antibody–antigen prediction accuracy compared with AlphaFold-Multimer v.2.3,52 showing high-accuracy modeling across biomolecular space is possible within a single unified deep-learning framework.

Mouse and human TET2 O-GlcNAcylation sites

Putative O-GlcNAcylation sites within mouse and human TET2 sequences were predicted using two computational tools: YinOYang v1.2 (services.healthtech.dtu.dk/services/YinOYang-1.2/) and O-GlcNAcPRED-DL (https://www.djangoproject.com).47 YinOYang employs a neural network trained on 40 experimentally validated O-GlcNAcylation acceptor sites and assesses sequence context and surface accessibility to estimate the likelihood of O-GlcNAcylation modification. Prediction confidence was categorized based on threshold scores defined by the program. For enhanced prediction sensitivity and accuracy, O-GlcNAcPRED-DL, a deep learning–based approach, was also applied. This tool integrates four protein sequence encoding schemes and leverages an ensemble of convolutional neural networks (CNN) and bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM) networks to generate weighted output probabilities, enabling robust identification of potential O-GlcNAcylation sites. Predicted O-GlcNAcylation Ser and Thr sites in mouse and human TET2 were visualized as lollipops. O-GlcNAcylation scores are indicated by lollipops stick height and head size. Red lollipop heads denote predicted O-GlcNAcylation sites at ≥ 0.8 threshold. This is significantly higher than the 0.5 and 0.6 that have been generally used as thresholds for predicted protein PTM, including O-GlcNAcylation.46–48
