Belimumab as a possible treatment for SLE-associated pulmonary arterial hypertension: the experience from a single centre
Authors: Qianwen Wu, Zhangdi Zhou, Huangshu Ye, Miaojia Zhang, Xiaoxuan Sun, Qiang Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo explore clinical outcomes associated with belimumab use in patients with SLE-associated pulmonary arterial hypertension (SLE-PAH).MethodsWe performed an ambispective cohort study of 10 patients with SLE-PAH treated with intravenous belimumab (10 mg/kg) from January 2022 to January 2025 and compared them with 30 matched SLE-PAH controls receiving conventional immunosuppression without biologics. Patients were followed for 12 months by the same medical team. Clinical, laboratory, echocardiographic parameters, SLE Disease Activity Index 2000 and PAH risk stratification were compared between groups.ResultsAll patients were matched according to baseline age, sex, WHO functional class (WHO-FC), PAH treatment strategy and background SLE therapy. All enrolled patients received either dual or triple combination targeted therapy for PAH. In the SLE-PAH group treated with belimumab, rapid and sustained improvements were observed in multiple parameters, including N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide, WHO-FC, 6-minute walking distance, soluble suppression of tumorigenicity 2 levels, the ratio of tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion to pulmonary artery systolic pressure (TAPSE/PASP) and right ventricular end-diastolic basal dimension (RVDd). Compared with the matched control group, belimumab-treated patients showed greater improvement in TAPSE/PASP and RVDd at the 12-month follow-up, with statistically significant differences between groups. Additionally, at the 12-month follow-up, all patients in the belimumab group were stratified into the low-risk category, whereas one patient remained in the intermediate-low risk and four patients were categorised as intermediate-high risk in the matched group.ConclusionBelimumab combined with standard PAH-targeted therapy was associated with improvements in right ventricular structure and function in patients with SLE-PAH compared with conventional immunosuppression. Further large-scale, prospective investigations are warranted to confirm these findings.

Introduction

SLE is a chronic autoimmune disease characterised by widespread inflammation and damage to multiple organs, including the skin, joints, kidneys and cardiovascular system. One of the serious complications associated with SLE is pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH). In China, SLE-associated PAH (SLE-PAH) is the most common subtype of connective tissue disease-associated PAH (CTD-PAH) and is associated with notably poor clinical outcomes.13

PAH is a life-threatening condition with a complex pathogenesis that is not yet fully understood. Achieving and maintaining a low-risk profile through optimised medical therapy is the primary treatment goal for individuals affected by this condition.1 4 Recent studies underscore the significant roles of inflammation and immune responses in the development of PAH, suggesting that its pathogenesis is not solely driven by vasoconstriction; rather, it also involves immune dysfunction.5 6 Emerging evidence indicates that targeting inflammation and modulating the immune system are crucial components in the treatment strategies for patients with CTD-PAH.46

B lymphocytes play a pivotal role in the development of PAH, contributing to the production of autoantibodies and the release of proinflammatory cytokines. Numerous studies have shown that B-cell activation is associated with the severity of PAH in patients with SLE, suggesting that targeting B cells may provide therapeutic benefits.79 Belimumab—a humanised recombinant IgG monoclonal antibody directed against B-cell activating factor (BAFF)/B lymphocyte stimulator (BLyS)—is licensed for the treatment of patients with SLE.7 Over the past decade, clinical evidence has established the efficacy of belimumab in reducing disease activity, decreasing flare rates and limiting organ damage progression in SLE.8 9 Despite these promising results, the potential role of belimumab in the treatment of SLE-PAH has not been extensively investigated.

This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of belimumab in improving clinical outcomes in patients with SLE-PAH. By assessing key clinical endpoints including pulmonary haemodynamics, functional capacity, disease-related biomarkers and risk stratification, this research seeks to provide crucial insights into the potential therapeutic benefits of belimumab in enhancing the management and prognosis of SLE-PAH.

Methods

Study design

This ambispective study was conducted at the First Affiliated Hospital with Nanjing Medical University from January 2022 to January 2025.

Patient selection

Patients were enrolled based on the following inclusion criteria: (1) diagnosis of SLE according to the 2019 European League Against Rheumatism (EULAR) and the American College of Rheumatology (ACR) criteria10; (2) PAH confirmed by right heart catheterization (RHC), defined as mean pulmonary arterial pressure>20 mmHg, pulmonary arterial wedge pressure≤15 mm Hg and pulmonary vascular resistance>2 Wood units at rest1; and (3) age≥18 years.

Exclusion criteria: (1) pulmonary hypertension (PH) co-induced by other factors, specifically including chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension as excluded by ventilation/perfusion scanning; (2) significant pulmonary parenchymal disease (restrictive or obstructive), confirmed by high-resolution CT and pulmonary function testing; (3) sleep-disordered breathing; (4) serious active infections; and (5) incomplete follow-up data.

Study population and grouping

A total of 52 patients with SLE-PAH were screened from our clinical database between January 2022 and January 2025. To evaluate the clinical efficacy of belimumab in this population, all 10 patients who received intravenous belimumab treatment (10 mg/kg; administered every 2 weeks for the initial 4 weeks, followed by every 4 weeks thereafter) were included in the belimumab group. The remaining eligible patients, who were treated with conventional immunosuppressants without any biologics, were selected as a matched control group. These controls were carefully matched to the belimumab group at a 1:3 ratio based on baseline age, sex, WHO functional class (WHO-FC), PAH-targeted treatment strategy and background SLE therapy.

Treatment protocols

SLE management was tailored to individual disease activity.10 The decision to initiate belimumab was made by the treating rheumatologist in consultation with the patient and was based primarily on systemic SLE disease activity and extrapulmonary manifestations rather than PAH alone. Indications for belimumab use included: (1) persistent moderate-to-high SLE Disease Activity Index 2000 (SLEDAI-2K≥8) despite conventional immunosuppression or (2) refractory extrapulmonary manifestations. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients prior to the initiation of belimumab treatment. The matched controls did not receive biologics mainly due to well-controlled SLE activity on conventional therapy, patient preference or economic reasons.

PAH-targeted therapy was administered according to the 2022 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) and European Respiratory Society (ERS) guidelines and individualised based on baseline risk status, treatment response and cardiopulmonary comorbidities.1 Risk assessment was performed using a multiparametric approach, including WHO-FC, 6-minute walking distance (6MWD), B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP)/N-terminal pro BNP (NT-pro BNP), echocardiographic parameters and invasive haemodynamic variables obtained by right heart catheterisation, including right atrial pressure, cardiac index and mixed venous oxygen saturation. Cardiovascular magnetic resonance imaging (CMR)-derived right ventricular (RV) parameters, such as RV ejection fraction and stroke volume index, were also considered when available. In general, oral dual combination therapy was selected for patients with low or intermediate risk and no significant cardiopulmonary comorbidities, whereas triple therapy, including a prostacyclin pathway agent, was used for patients with high-risk features at baseline or inadequate clinical response during follow-up. PAH-targeted treatment strategy was also considered in the matching process to minimise imbalance in PAH-specific treatment intensity between groups.

Follow-up

This study employs an ambispective cohort design. For patients who had already completed the 12-month treatment cycle at the time of data cut-off, their data were analysed retrospectively. For those who were still within the treatment period, follow-up was continued prospectively according to a standardised 12-month clinical protocol. All patients were managed and monitored by the same specialised medical team to ensure consistency.

Data collection

During each visit, various parameters including the SLEDAI-2K, 6MWD, WHO-FC, NT-pro BNP, soluble suppression of tumorigenicity 2 (sST2) levels, 2018 world symposium on pulmonary hypertension (2018WSPH) risk stratification at baseline, 4-strata risk stratification at follow-up, echocardiography parameters and laboratory data were assessed and recorded.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses were performed using SPSS V.26.0 and GraphPad Prism V.9.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, California, USA). The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was applied to evaluate the normality of distribution for all variables. Data were summarised as median with IQR. Categorical variables are reported in both absolute numbers and percentages. For comparisons between groups, Student’s t-test was used for normally distributed variables, whereas the Wilcoxon test was employed for non-parametric data. The significance for statistical tests was set at α=0.05.

Results

A total of 40 participants with SLE-PAH were enrolled in the study and followed for 12 months. All patients in both the belimumab and control groups underwent a continuous, standardised treatment regimen throughout the study duration. Furthermore, the background PAH-targeted strategy was uniform across the cohort, with 100% of patients receiving dual or triple combination therapy. No patients were lost to follow-up during the 12-month period.

In patients with SLE-PAH treated with belimumab, the median age was 35.5 years and the median SLEDAI Score was 10. Regarding SLE therapy, all patients in the belimumab group received glucocorticoids, with 9 (90%) treated with cyclophosphamide (CTX) and 1 (10%) with cyclosporine (CSA). In the matched group, all patients also received glucocorticoids, while the background immunosuppressive therapy included CTX (60.0%), mycophenolate mofetil (23.3%) and other agents (tacrolimus/CSA/azathioprine, 16.7%). Concerning PAH-targeted therapy, in the belimumab group, six patients (60%) underwent dual therapy, including four (67%) on an endothelin receptor antagonist (ERA) plus a phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor (PDE5i) and 2 (33%) on an ERA plus a soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator (sGCs). The remaining four patients (40%) were on triple combination therapy, among whom two (50%) received a combination of an ERA, a PDE5i and a prostacyclin receptor (IP) agonist and two (50%) received an ERA, an sGCs and an IP agonist. In the matched group, dual therapy was administered to 23 patients (76.7%), consisting of ERA+PDE5i (86.9%) and ERA+sGCs (13.1%). The remaining seven patients (23.3%) received triple therapy, including ERA plus PDE5i+IP agonist (85.7%) and ERA+sGCs+IP agonist (14.3%). There was no statistically significant difference in baseline haemodynamic and echocardiographic characteristics between belimumab and the matched group. During the follow-up, we closely monitored belimumab-related side effects. No patient discontinued belimumab due to poor efficacy or side effects. All detailed indicators are presented in table 1.

Importantly, our findings revealed that within the 12-month follow-up, all patients with SLE-PAH in the belimumab group transitioned to the low-risk category. In contrast, in the matched group, one patient remained in the intermediate-low risk and four patients were categorised as intermediate-high risk (figure 1A). Throughout the 12-month study period, patients with SLE-PAH treated with belimumab demonstrated sustained and substantial improvements across multiple clinical and biomarker parameters, including NT-pro BNP, 6MWD, sST2, tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion to pulmonary artery systolic pressure (TAPSE/PASP), right ventricular end-diastolic basal dimension (RVDd) and SLEDAI (figure 1B–G). When compared with the matched control group, the belimumab-treated cohort achieved significantly superior long-term outcomes in several key indicators. Specifically, the belimumab group showed greater improvements in RV function and structure from baseline, as evidenced by a greater increase in TAPSE/PASP and a substantial reduction in RVDd at 12 months (figure 2A,B, online supplemental table S1). Similarly, the decrease in NT-pro BNP levels from baseline was more pronounced in the belimumab group than in the matched group (figure 2C). However, the improvements in both 6MWD and sST2 were similar between the belimumab-treated group and the matched conventional immunosuppressants-treated group (figure 2D,E, online supplemental table S1). Notably, although the belimumab group had a higher baseline SLEDAI Score than the matched group, it showed a marked decrease from baseline after 12 months of treatment (figure 2F).

Discussion

This single-centre ambispective cohort study provides preliminary evidence supporting a beneficial role for the addition of belimumab to standard-of-care therapy in patients with SLE-PAH. The key findings are threefold: (1) Belimumab, combined with PAH-targeted therapy and conventional immunosuppression, was associated with rapid and sustained improvements across multiple clinical, biomarker and echocardiographic parameters. (2) Notably, belimumab-treated patients demonstrated significantly greater improvement in RV structure and function (RVDd and TAPSE/PASP) at the 12-month follow-up compared with matched controls receiving conventional immunosuppression alone, suggesting a potential specific effect on RV remodelling and RV–pulmonary artery (PA) coupling. (3) At the 12-month follow-up, all patients in the belimumab group achieved a low-risk status, whereas a proportion of the matched control group remained at intermediate risk (one intermediate low, four intermediate high), indicating a more favourable long-term outcome trajectory with belimumab treatment.

Previous research has indicated that belimumab may help slow the progression of irreversible organ damage (including the cardiovascular system), among patients with SLE.11 12 B lymphocytes play a pivotal role in the development of PAH, not only by differentiating into plasma cells that generate autoantibodies but also by contributing to cell-mediated immune regulation through antigen presentation, cytokine production and effector T-cell differentiation.5 6 13 14 It is reported that B-cell deficient rats exhibit reduced susceptibility to severe PAH and pulmonary vascular remodelling induced by monocrotaline (MCT) or hypoxia.12 BAFF-R is one of the most upregulated genes in the lungs of MCT rats, and the expression of BAFF and BAFF-R genes is positively correlated with disease severity.11 Moreover, rituximab, a B cell-targeted therapeutic antibody, has shown safety and efficacy in treating SLE-PAH, as reported in a case study.15

The significantly improved in TAPSE/PASP and RVDd in the belimumab group points to a potential direct or indirect cardioprotective effect. TAPSE/PASP is a non-invasive surrogate for RV-PA coupling, a key determinant of RV functional adaptation and prognosis in PAH.1 2 The improvement in TAPSE/PASP and RVDd, driven by both increased TAPSE (RV systolic function) and decreased PASP (afterload), alongside a reduction in RVDd (reverse remodelling), indicates enhanced RV efficiency and structural improvement. Given the central role of B cells and autoimmunity in SLE-PAH pathogenesis,11 12 we hypothesise that belimumab, by neutralising BAFF/BLyS, may attenuate key pathogenic processes that contribute to increased pulmonary vascular resistance and subsequent RV strain. This modulation of the inflammatory milieu may facilitate more effective reverse remodelling in response to PAH-targeted therapies.

Regarding risk stratification, although the majority of control patients attained low-risk status, the complete attainment of low risk in the belimumab group, with no patient remaining in the intermediate or high risk categories, suggests a potential benefit of adding belimumab to conventional therapy in optimising long-term risk stratification. Despite these promising results, our study also revealed that improvements in 6MWD were less pronounced in the belimumab group compared with those receiving traditional immunosuppressants. This may reflect the multifaceted determinants of exercise capacity, which can be influenced by factors beyond cardiopulmonary function in patients with SLE, such as musculoskeletal involvement, fatigue or deconditioning.8 9 It is possible that the potent anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects of belimumab on the RV and pulmonary vasculature precede measurable maximal gains in functional exercise capacity, which may require a longer duration to fully manifest.

This study has several limitations. First, the ambispective, non-randomised design and small sample size limit causal inference. Belimumab was preferentially prescribed to patients with persistent moderate-to-high SLE activity or refractory extrapulmonary manifestations; therefore, indication bias and residual confounding could not be fully excluded despite matching for key PAH-related variables. Second, although PAH-targeted treatment strategy was considered in the matching process and all patients received dual or triple combination therapy, changes in PAH treatment intensity during follow-up may still have influenced outcomes. Third, the heterogeneity of background immunosuppressive therapies reflects real-world practice but may also affect interpretation. Finally, the 12-month follow-up period was insufficient to assess long-term endpoints such as hospitalisation, survival or irreversible organ damage.

In conclusion, our study demonstrates that belimumab may offer significant benefits for patients with SLE-associated PAH, particularly in improving RV structure and function. The results provide valuable insights for optimising the management of SLE-PAH.
