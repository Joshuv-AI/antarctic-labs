Abstract.
In the paper we introduce Orlicz type functional spaces defined in terms of nonlocal convolution
type integral functionals and study the main properties of these spaces. We show in particular that, under natural convexity
and growth conditions on the integrand, the corresponding spaces are Banach and separable. We also characterize the dual spaces and provide a number of examples.

Keywords:
Orlicz space; convolution type functional; functional with variable growth condition

Mathematics Subject Classification:
46E30

1
Introduction

The main goal of this paper is to introduce and study the functional spaces defined in terms of nonlocal
‘‘convolution type’’ integral functionals

F(u)=\int\limits_{\Omega\times\Omega}a(x-y)\varphi(|u(x)-u(y)|,x,y)\,dxdy,

(1.1)

which include the important particular case

F_{p(\cdot)}(u)=\int\limits_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{a(x-y)b(x,y)}{p(x,y)}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}\,dxdy.

(1.2)

Here
\Omega
is a regular domain in
\mathds{R}^{d}
that can be bounded or unbounded including the case, when
\Omega
coincides with
\mathds{R}^{d},

and
a(z)
is a non-negative integrable function. The function
\phi(z,x,y)
is assumed to be non-negative and
strictly convex in the first variable and to satisfy appropriate growth conditions that can vary from point to point;
the detailed list of conditions is formulated in the next section.
For the particular case (
1.2
), these conditions are satisfied if
b
is a bounded positive measurable function and
p(x,y)
is a measurable function obeying the inequality
1<p_{-}\leqslant p(\cdot)\leqslant p_{+}.

Variational nonlocal convolution type functionals with variable growth conditions is a very interesting mathematical topic.
It is closely related to the theory of convolution type functionals with variable growth conditions
and the corresponding stationary and evolution equations.
Studying both qualitative and asymptotic properties of these operators and functionals is an important and challenging mathematical task.

The interest to the nonlocal convolution type functionals and operators is also motivated by numerous applications in material
sciences and biology. They are widely used for describing various processes in population dynamics, mechanics of porous media, chemistry of polymers, and other fields, where the non-locality of interactions plays an essential role.
For instance, in the so-called contact
model in population dynamics the evolution of the density of population is characterized by the equation

\partial_{t}v(x,t)=\int\limits_{\mathds{R}^{d}}a(x-y)b(x,y)\big(v(y,t)-v(x,t)\big)dy.

The quadratic form corresponding to the operator on the right-hand side here is given by
(
1.2
) with
\Omega=\mathds{R}^{d}
and
p(\cdot)=2
. The description of the contact model and
its basic properties can be found in
[
9
]
.
In order to provide a more accurate description one should consider
the models that also account for the nonlinear effects. In this case the corresponding variational functional is
not quadratic any more and might be of the form (
1.2
) or even (
1.1
).

In the modern theory of porous media, the modeling of many phenomena is performed by taking into account the non-locality and non-linearity of interactions in the medium.
Moreover, the rheological properties of the media can vary from point to point.
Mathematical description of these phenomena relies on nonlinear convolution type equations; the example of such equation is

\partial_{t}v(x,t)=\int\limits_{\mathds{R}^{d}}a(x-y)b(x,y)\big(v(y,t)-v(x,t)\big)\big|v(y)-v(x)\big|^{p(x,y)-2}dy,

and the variational functional corresponding to the operator on the right-hand side coincides with that in
(
1.2
).

The local functionals and differential operators with variable growth conditions have been intensively studied
during last thirty years, see, for instance,
[
3
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
12
]
and references therein.
We recall that in order to investigate elliptic PDEs with variable exponents of the form

-\operatorname{div}A(x)|\nabla v(x)|^{p(x)-2}\nabla v(x)+|v(x)|^{p(x)-2}v(x)=g\quad\text{in}\quad\mathds{R}^{d},

(1.3)

where
p(x)
is a measurable function satisfying the estimate
1<p_{-}\leqslant p(\cdot)\leqslant p_{+}
and
A(x)
is a positive definite matrix function,
it is natural to introduce the Sobolev spaces with variable exponent
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\mathds{R}^{d})
defined as the set of measurable functions
v(x)
such that

\int\limits_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|v(x)|^{p(x)}dx<+\infty,\qquad\int\limits_{\mathds{R}^{d}}|\nabla v(x)|^{p(x)}dx<+\infty.

It was shown in
[
10
,
6
,
4
]
that
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\mathds{R}^{d})
equipped with the Luxemburg norm is a
separable Banach space. Moreover,
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{d})
is dense in
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\mathds{R}^{d})

if
p(x)
is
\log
-continuous. The latter result was obtained in
[
5
,
13
]
and then improved in
[
14
]
. The Lavrentiev phenomenon was discussed in
[
15
]
.

Having this space at hand one can prove that equation (
1.3
) is well posed in
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\mathds{R}^{d})
, that is for any
f\in L^{p^{*}(\cdot)}(\mathds{R}^{d})
equation (
1.3
) has
a solution
v\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}
, the solution is unique and satisfies the estimate

\|v\|_{W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\leqslant C\|f\|_{L^{p^{*}(\cdot)}},\qquad p^{*}(x):=\frac{p(x)}{p(x)-1}.

Our aim is to realize similar ideas in the above introduced nonlocal setting and to construct
functional spaces corresponding to the functional (
1.1
)
and to the related nonlocal equations. To this end
we consider the functional

F(u)+\|u\|^{p_{-}}_{L_{p_{-}}}=\int\limits_{\Omega\times\Omega}a(x-y)\varphi\big(\big|u(y)-u(x)\big|,x,y\big)dxdy+\int\limits_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p_{-}}dx

and define the set

\mathcal{L}(\Omega):=\big\{u\in L_{1,loc}(\Omega)\,:\,f(u)<+\infty\big\},\qquad f(u):=F(u)+\|u\|^{p_{-}}_{L_{p_{-}}}(\Omega).

(1.4)

We show that, under natural growth and convexity assumptions on
\varphi(\,\cdot\,,x,y)
, this set is linear and, equipped with the Luxemburg norm, it forms a separable Banach space.
Then we explore the properties of this space. In particular, we address the questions of density of
compactly supported infinitely differentiable
functions in this space and characterize the structure of the dual space.

Replacing
F(u)
with
F_{p(\cdot)}(u)
in formula (
1.4
), we arrive at the definition of the space
\mathcal{L}_{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
which is a particular case of the space
\mathcal{L}(\Omega)
.
It is natural to compare the spaces
\mathcal{L}_{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
,
p=p(x,y)
, and the Lebesgue spaces with variable exponent.
Since the functional
F_{p(\cdot)}(u)
is nonlocal, the variable exponent
p(x,y)
depends on two arguments
x
and
y
.
Thus we do not expect that for the generic
p(x,y)
the space
\mathcal{L}_{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
corresponding to
F_{p(\cdot)}(u)
coincides
with any Lebesgue space
L_{q(\cdot)}(\Omega)
with a variable exponent
q(x)
.

Recall that the Sobolev space
W^{1}_{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
with
1<p_{-}\leqslant p(x)\leqslant p_{+}
is embedded in
L_{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
and the embedding
is locally compact. In the case of nonlocal functional
F_{p(\cdot)}
with
1<p_{-}\leqslant p(x,y)\leqslant p_{+}
the picture is rather different. Although
\mathcal{L}(\Omega)
is embedded into
L_{p_{-}}(\Omega)
,
the embedding is not locally compact any more; here we have
L_{p_{-}}(\Omega)\cap L_{p_{+}}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{L}(\Omega)\subset L_{p_{-}}(\Omega)
, see Theorem
2.1
below.

As a natural application of our results, one can
develop a qualitative theory of equations

-\int\limits_{\Omega}a(x-y)\varphi^{\prime}(|u(y)-u(x)|,x,y)\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{|u(y)-u(x)|}dy+|u(x)|^{p_{-}-2}u(x)=g\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

where
g\in\mathcal{L}^{*}
,
and
\varphi^{\prime}
stands for the partial derivative of
\varphi
with respect to the first
variable

\varphi^{\prime}(z,x,y)=\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}(z,x,y).

This equation is the Euler–Lagrange equation of the functional
F(u)+\|u\|^{p_{-}}_{L_{p_{-}}}-\langle g,u\rangle
.
Our results on the structure of the dual space
\mathcal{L}^{*}
imply that the above equation is solvable for any
g\in\mathcal{L}^{*}
and the operator on its left hand side maps continuously
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{L}^{*}.

It should be emphasized that, in connection with the growing number of applications where non-local convolution-type functionals and operators are used, the study of the corresponding ’’nonlocal’’ Orlicz-type spaces is becoming a very important task.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we provide the detailed list of conditions
on the functional
F(u)
and formulate our main results.
In Section
3
we provide a number of auxiliary technical results.
The remaining four sections
are devoted to the proof of main results.

2
Problem setup and main results

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathds{R}^{d}
be an arbitrary
d
–dimensional domain, where
d\geqslant 1.
The domain
\Omega
can be both bounded and unbounded; the case
\Omega=\mathds{R}^{d}
is also admitted. By
a=a(z),

z\in\mathds{R}^{d},
we denote a non–negative function on
\mathds{R}^{d},
we suppose that
a\in L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
and there exists a fixed non–empty ball
B_{0}
centered at zero such that

a(z)\geqslant c_{0}>0\quad\text{almost everywhere in}\quad B_{0}.

(2.1)

If the domain
\Omega
is multi–connected and
\Omega_{i},

i=1,2,\ldots
are disjoint connected components of
\Omega,
we suppose that the distance between these components is less than the diameter of
B_{0}:

\dist(\Omega_{i},\Omega_{i+1})<\diam B_{0},\qquad i=1,2,\ldots

(2.2)

We recall
[
8
, Ch. 2, Sect. 2.1]
that a real function
r=r(t)
defined on
\mathds{R}_{+}
is called
almost increasing
, respectively,
almost decreasing
if there exists a constant
\beta\geqslant 1
such that

r(s)\leqslant\beta r(t),\qquad\text{respectively,}\qquad\beta r(s)\geqslant r(t),

(2.3)

for all
0\leqslant s\leqslant t.
By
\varphi=\varphi(z,x,y)
we denote a non–negative real–valued function of the variables
(z,x,y)\in\mathds{R}_{+}\times\Omega\times\Omega,
which is supposed to obey the following conditions:

(C1).

For each
u\in L_{1,loc}(\Omega)
the function
\varphi(|u(x)-u(y)|,x,y)
is measurable on
\Omega\times\Omega.

(C2).

The function
\varphi(t,x,y)
is uniformly convex in
t
for almost all
(x,y),
namely, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
such that

\varphi\left(\frac{s+t}{2},x,y\right)\leqslant(1-\delta)\frac{\varphi(s,x,y)+\varphi(t,x,y)}{2}

(2.4)

for almost all
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega
and all
s>0,

t>0
such that
|s-t|\geqslant\varepsilon\max\{s,\,t\}.

(C3).

There exist two constants
p_{-}
and
p_{+}
such that

1<p_{-}\leqslant p_{+}
and a constant
\beta\geqslant 1
such that the function
t\mapsto\frac{\varphi(t,x,y)}{t^{p_{-}}}
is almost increasing with the constant
\beta
in (
2.3
) for almost all
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega,
while the function
t\mapsto\frac{\varphi(t,x,y)}{t^{p_{+}}}
is almost decreasing with the constant
\beta

for almost all
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega.

(C4).

The function
\varphi

satisfies the relations

\displaystyle c_{1}^{-1}\leqslant\varphi(1,x,y)\leqslant c_{1},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\varphi(0,x,y)=0,\qquad\varphi(t,x,y)>0,

(2.6)

for almost all
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega,
where
c_{1}
is a fixed positive constant independent of
x
and
y.

(C5).

The function
\varphi(t,x,y)
is differentiable in
t>0
for almost all
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega
and satisfies the estimate

0<t\varphi^{\prime}(t,x,y)\leqslant c_{2}\varphi(t,x,y),\qquad t>0

for almost all
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega
with a constant
c_{2}>1
independent of
t,

x,
and
y.

We shall show in Lemma
3.2
that Conditions
(C3)
,
(C4)
ensure the identities

\lim\limits_{t\to+0}\varphi(t,x,y)=0,\qquad\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}\varphi(t,x,y)=+\infty

(2.7)

for almost all
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega.

On functions
u\in L_{1,loc}(\Omega)
we introduce the functional

F(u):=\int\limits_{\Omega\times\Omega}\varphi\big(|u(x)-u(y)|,x,y\big)a(x-y)\,dxdy.

(2.8)

For each
u\in L_{1,loc}(\Omega)
this functional is either finite or equal to
+\infty
since the case of a non–integrable integrand is excluded by Condition
(C1)
and the non–negativity of integrand. In what follows we allow
F(u)
to take the value
+\infty
and under this assumption this functional is well–defined.

In terms of this functional we define one more functional

f(u):=|u|_{p,\Omega}+\|u\|_{L_{p_{-}}(\Omega)},\qquad|u|_{p,\Omega}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\,F\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leqslant 1\right\}.

(2.9)

We denote

\mathcal{L}(\Omega):=\big\{u\in L_{p_{-},loc}(\Omega):\,f(u)<+\infty\big\}.

(2.10)

In addition to the space
\mathcal{L}(\Omega),
we also introduce the space generated by the functional
F.
Namely, on the space
L_{1,loc}(\Omega)
we define the equivalence relation:

u\sim v
if
u-v=const
almost everywhere on
\Omega.
By
\Lambda(\Omega)
we denote the linear space consisting of cosets
\mathbf{U}
generated by the equivalence relation
\sim
such that

|u|_{p,\Omega}<\infty
for some
u\in\mathbf{U},
and hence for each
u\in\mathbf{U}.
We extend the functional
|\cdot|_{p,\Omega}
to the space
\Lambda(\Omega)
by the rule

|\mathbf{U}|_{p,\Omega}:=|u|_{p,\Omega}\quad\text{for}\quad u\in\mathbf{U}.

(2.11)

It is clear that the above definition is independent of the particular choice of an element
u
in the coset
\mathbf{U}.

The main aim of our work is to study the spaces
\mathcal{L}(\Omega)
and
\Lambda(\Omega)
as well as their dual spaces.

2.1
Main results

Now we are in position to formulate our main results.
The first result is about very basic properties of the space
\mathcal{L}(\Omega).

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that Conditions
(C1)
,
(C2)
,
(C3)
,
(C4)
are satisfied.
Then the functional
\|u\|_{\mathcal{L}(\Omega)}:=f(u)
is a norm, and the space
\mathcal{L}(\Omega)
equipped with this norm is Banach.
The following embeddings are valid

L_{p_{+}}(\Omega)\cap L_{p_{-}}(\Omega)\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\Omega)\subseteq L_{p_{-}}(\Omega).

(2.12)

The embeddings are continuous.

On the space
\mathcal{L}(\Omega)
we can introduce equivalent norms. Namely, on
L_{1,loc}(\Omega)
we introduce the functionals

G(u):=F(u)+\int\limits_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p_{-}}\,dx,\qquad g(u):=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\,G\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leqslant 1\right\}.

(2.13)

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that Conditions
(C1)
,
(C2)
,
(C3)
,
(C4)
are satisfied.
Then the functional
g
is a norm on
\mathcal{L}(\Omega).
Moreover, this norm is equivalent to
f(u),
namely,

\frac{1}{2}f(u)\leqslant g(u)\leqslant\beta^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}}f(u).

(2.14)

The separability of the space
\mathcal{L}(\Omega)
and the density of compactly supported infinitely differentiable functions are addressed in the next theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume that Conditions
(C1)
,
(C2)
,
(C3)
,
(C4)
are satisfied.
The space of infinitely differentiable compactly supported functions
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is a dense subset of
\mathcal{L}(\Omega).
The space
\mathcal{L}(\Omega)
is separable.

The following two theorems establish the main properties of space
\Lambda(\Omega)
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume that Conditions
(C1)
,
(C2)
,
(C3)
,
(C4)
are satisfied.
The functional
|\cdot|_{p,\Omega}
is a norm on the space
\Lambda(\Omega).
The space
\Lambda(\Omega)
with the norm
|\cdot|_{p,\Omega}
is Banach. The norm
|\cdot|_{p,\Omega}
is uniformly convex on
\Lambda(\Omega).

Theorem 2.5
.

Let Conditions
(C1)
,
(C2)
,
(C3)
,
(C4)
be fulfilled,
and assume that the domain
\Omega
is bounded, connected and has a Lipschitz boundary.
Then
\Lambda(\Omega)
is a Banach space with the norm
|\cdot|_{p,\Omega}.

Each coset
\mathbf{U}\in\Lambda(\Omega)
contains a unique representative
u,
which belongs to
L_{p_{-}}(\Omega)
and has a zero mean value:

\langle u\rangle_{\Omega}=0,\qquad\langle u\rangle_{\Omega}:=\frac{1}{\mes\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}u(x)\,dx.

(2.15)

In this sense we say that

\mathcal{L}(\Omega)=\Lambda(\Omega)\oplus\mathds{C},

(2.16)

that is, each function

is represented as

u=u_{\bot,\Omega}+\langle u\rangle_{\Omega},\qquad\langle u_{\bot}\rangle_{\Omega}=0,

(2.17)

where

generated by

c_{3}\big(|u_{\bot,\Omega}|_{p,\Omega}+|\langle u\rangle_{\Omega}|\big)\leqslant\|u\|_{\mathcal{L}(\Omega)}\leqslant c_{4}\big(|u_{\bot,\Omega}|_{p,\Omega}+|\langle u\rangle_{\Omega}|\big)

(2.18)

hold with positive constants
c_{3}
and
c_{4}
independent of

but depending on
\Omega.

We introduce the conjugate function for
\varphi

\varphi_{*}(t):=\sup\limits_{s\geqslant 0}\big(st-\varphi(s)\big).

(2.19)

According to

8
, Ch. 2, Sect. 2.4, Lm. 2.4.2]
, the function
\varphi_{*}(t,x,y)
is non–negative, convex and continuous in

for almost all

and satisfies Condition
(C1)
and the identities (
2.7
). By

, the function
\varphi_{*}
also satisfies Condition

with

and

replaced by
q_{+}:=\frac{p_{-}}{p_{-}-1}
and

respectively, that is, the function

\frac{\varphi_{*}(t,x,y)}{t^{q_{-}}}

for almost all

The function
\varphi_{*}
also satisfies the Young inequality implied by the definition (

st\leqslant\varphi_{*}(t)+\varphi(s),\qquad s\geqslant 0,\quad t\geqslant 0.

(2.20)

On the space

we introduce the functionals

\displaystyle H(U):=\int\limits_{\Omega\times\Omega}\varphi(|U(x,y)|,x,y)a(x-y)\,dxdy,

(2.23)

2.2
Discussion of main results

Let us briefly discuss the main features of our problem and main results. The Orlicz spaces
\mathcal{L}
and
\Lambda
are defined on the base of functional
F,
which has a non–local convolution like structure due to the presence of functions
a(x-y)
and
u(x)-u(y)

in (
1.1
). This makes an essential difference from the classical Orlicz spaces, the definition of which is based on the functionals

u\mapsto\int\limits_{\Omega}\varphi(|u(x)|,x)\,dx.

Moreover, while the integrand in the latter functional should be strictly positive, this is not the case for the integrand in (
1.1
). The integrand in (
1.1
) is to be just non–negative
since the function
a=a(z)
is allowed to vanish, see the condition (
2.1
). In particular, the function
a
can be compactly supported.

We also observe that the integral (
1.1
) uses only the values of function
a(z)
for

z\in\Omega_{\natural}:=\{x-y:\,x,y\in\Omega\}

and for
z\in\mathds{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega_{\natural}
the values
a(z)
play no role. For instance, we can suppose that
a
vanishes identically on
\mathds{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega_{\natural}
and then we just should impose the condition
a\in L_{1}(\Omega_{\natural}).
Under such assumption, if the domain
\Omega
is bounded, we can take
a
obeying the lower bound (
2.1
) on
\Omega_{\natural},
for instance,
a
can be identically constant on this domain. At the same time, if
\Omega
is unbounded, then
a
can not satisfy the lower bound (
2.1
) for all
z\in\Omega_{\natural}
since this contradicts the assumption
a\in L_{1}(\Omega_{\natural}).

It should be also emphasized that the interplay between the functions
\varphi(z,x,y)
and
a(x-y)
can affect essentially
the structure of the space
\mathcal{L}
. We illustrate this with the following example. Let

\varphi(z,x,y)=z^{p(x,y)},

where
p(x,y)
satisfies the estimates

p(x,y)<2\quad\text{for}\quad|x-y|\leqslant 1,\qquad p(x,y)>3\quad\text{for}\quad|x-y|\geqslant 3.

We let
a_{1}=\chi_{\{z:\,|z|\leqslant 1\}}
,
a_{2}(z)=\chi_{\{z:\,|z|\leqslant 5\}}
, where
\chi_{\omega}
is the characteristic function of a set
\omega.
We
consider the corresponding Banach spaces
\mathcal{L}(\mathds{R}^{d}),
which we denote by
\mathcal{L}^{(1)}(\mathds{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{L}^{(2)}(\mathds{R}^{d})
.
Then by direct inspection we see that the space
L_{2}(\mathds{R}^{d})\cap L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
is embedded into
\mathcal{L}^{(1)}(\mathds{R}^{d})

but is not embedded into
\mathcal{L}^{(2)}(\mathds{R}^{d})
.

Conditions
(C1)
,
(C2)
,
(C3)
,
(C4)
are standard and natural, exactly the same conditions are usually imposed in the classical theory of Orlicz spaces, see
[
8
]
. These conditions are sufficient to state the main properties of the spaces
\mathcal{L}(\Omega),

\Lambda(\Omega)
in Theorems
2.1
,
2.2
,
2.3
,
2.4
,
2.5
, which characterize their structure. In order to describe the dual spaces, we additionally need Condition
(C5)
. This condition is essentially employed in the proofs of Theorems
2.6
,
2.7
,
2.8
. The problem of describing the dual spaces
\mathcal{L}^{*}(\Omega)
and
\Lambda^{*}(\Omega)
becomes quite complicated for the functions
\phi
, which do not obey Condition
(C5)
. Moreover, the structure of the dual spaces in this case likely differs drastically from the situations described in Theorems
2.6
,
2.7
,
2.8
.

The general theory of Orlicz spaces
\mathcal{L}(\Omega)
and
\Lambda(\Omega)
is to be supported by a reasonable series of appropriate examples of the function
\varphi(z,x,y)
. Theorems
2.9
,
2.10
,
2.11
describe the structure of class of admissible functions
\varphi.
According to Theorem
2.9
, for any collection of admissible functions

\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N}
and any essentially bounded uniformly positive functions
b_{1}(x,y),\ldots,b_{N}(x,y)
the linear
combination
b_{1}(x,y)\varphi_{1}(z,x,y)+\ldots+b_{N}(x,y)\varphi_{N}(z,x,y)
is also an admissible function.
This class is also closed with respect to the usual multiplication and to the composition. Lemma
2.1
provides an effective way to verify the Condition
(C2)
, namely, once the function
\varphi
is twice differentiable in
z
and satisfies the estimate (
2.30
), this ensures Condition
(C2)
. Although
the estimate (
2.30
) is a sufficient condition of validity of Condition
(C2)
,
it holds for a wide class of admissible functions
\varphi
. It turns out that the sufficient condition given by Lemma
2.1
is quite close to be necessary. Theorems
2.10
,
2.11
say that once the function
\varphi
satisfies the Conditions
(C1)
–
(C5)
, we can add to
\varphi
and multiply it by a function
\psi,
which even does not obey the same conditions. This essentially enlarges the class of admissible functions
\varphi.

Let us dwell on some examples of the function
\varphi.

A very important example, which served as the main motivation of present work, is

\varphi(z,x,y)=z^{p(x,y)}b(x,y),

where

and

are bounded positive measurable functions and

It is easy to verify that Conditions

A more general example is given by

where
p_{i}
and

are bounded positive measurable functions and each

obeys the inequality

with some fixed constants
p_{-}
and

According to Theorem
2.9
,
for such a function

Conditions

–
(C5)

The function
\psi
in the formulation of Theorem

\varphi(z,x,y):=10B(x,y)z^{2},\qquad\psi(z,x,y):=B(x,y)\sin^{3}z,\qquad 0<B_{-}\leqslant B(x,y)\leqslant B_{+},

An example of the function

\displaystyle\psi(z,x,y):=\ln^{\gamma(x,y)}(1+\Upsilon(x,y)z),\qquad\gamma,\Upsilon\in L_{\infty}(\Omega\times\Omega),

\displaystyle 1\leqslant\gamma_{-}\leqslant\gamma(x,y)\leqslant\gamma_{+},\hphantom{+\Upsilon(x,y)z),}\qquad 0<\Upsilon_{-}\leqslant\Upsilon(x,y)\leqslant\Upsilon_{+},

This is why the above function

–

The further examples of functions

very rich.

It should be also stressed that

(C2)
,
(C3)
,
(C4)
imply that the function

Using our main results, one can show that, for each

\mes S<+\infty,

Lemma 7.1
.

Let

Then for each functional

\|w\|_{X}=\|\phi\|_{X^{*}},\qquad\phi(w)=\|\phi\|_{X^{*}}^{2}.

and
\ell(w,v)

(7.4)

while
\gamma(t;w,v)
is some function on

depending on the choice of
w
and

such that

\lim\limits_{t\to+0}\frac{\gamma(t;w,v)}{t}=0.

(7.5)

Then different functionals in

can not have the same function

obeying (
7.1
).

Proof.

We adapt the proof of Lemma in
[
1
, Ch. I​I, Sect. 2.32]
. Let
\phi\in X^{*}
be a non–zero functional. There exists a sequence
w_{n}\in X
such that

\|w_{n}\|_{X}=\|\phi\|_{X^{*}},\qquad|\phi(w_{n})|\to\|\phi\|_{X^{*}}^{2},\qquad n\to+\infty.

Without loss of generality we can suppose that

and replacing then

\|w_{n}\|_{X}=\|\phi\|_{X^{*}},\qquad\phi(w_{n})\to\|\phi\|_{X^{*}}^{2},\qquad n\to+\infty.

We are going to show that the sequence

is fundamental. Suppose this is not true, then, up to choosing an appropriate subsequence of

there exists
\varepsilon>0
and

such that

\|w_{n}-w_{m}\|\geqslant\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{X^{*}}\quad\text{for}\quad n,m>N.

By the uniform convexity of the norm there exists

such that

\Big\|\frac{1}{2}(w_{n}+w_{m})\Big\|_{X}\leqslant(1-\delta)\|\phi\|_{X^{*}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{X^{*}}\geqslant

\displaystyle\phi\left(\frac{w_{n}+w_{m}}{\|w_{n}+w_{m}\|_{X}}\right)=\frac{1}{\Big\|\frac{1}{2}(w_{n}+w_{m})\Big\|_{X}}\phi\Big(\frac{1}{2}(w_{n}+w_{m})\Big)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{\phi(w_{n})+\phi(w_{m})}{2(1-\delta)\|\phi\|_{X^{*}}}\to\frac{\|\phi\|_{X^{*}}}{1-\delta},\qquad n,m\to+\infty.

The obtained contradiction proves that the sequence

is fundamental, and
it hence converges to some element

) that the element

satisfies (
7.1
).

is unique. Indeed, if there are two functions

and

), then we apply (

) with

and

can not be associated with two distinct functionals under additional assumptions (

), (
7.3
), (
7.4
), (
7.5

) hold with

and

\phi_{1}

\phi_{2}

Since

there exists

Replacing

(7.8)

Summing these two inequalities, we get

In this subsection we study the dual space for

\mathrm{y}\in Y.

[
