Diagnostic value and prognostic effect of has-miR-151a-3p in tuberculosis
Authors: Jingwen Li, Tingru Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Tuberculosis (TB) is a widespread chronic infectious disease globally, caused by infection with Mycobacterium tuberculosis (MTB). Clinically, it is classified into latent TB and active TB. Without timely and standardized treatment, it is prone to disease progression and even death. This research aimed to investigate the clinical significance of miR-151a-3p in TB and probe into the related underlying mechanisms.Materials and Methods:Collectively, a total of 101 patients with latent TB, 102 patients with active TB, and 63 healthy individuals were enrolled in the research. Utilized real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) to measure the relative expression level of miR-151a-3p. To examine miR-151a-3p’s diagnostic potential for TB, receiver operating characteristic curve analysis was performed; logistic analysis was used to assess its role as a risk factor against TB. The function of miR-151a-3p in THP-1 cells infected with MTB was determined using flow cytometry, Cell Counting Kit-8 assay, and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. Dual-Luciferase assay verified its interaction with TP53, RT-qPCR was used to analyze the expression of TP53 in different THP-1 cell experimental groups and among different patients, and its prognostic utility was analyzed through Kaplan–Meier curve and Cox model. RT-qPCR was used to analyze the expression of miR-151a-3p and TP53 in patients with different treatment outcomes.Results:The expression level of miR-151a-3p gradually decreased in the latent TB group and the active TB group. It could well distinguish TB patients. The decrease in its expression serves as a significant risk factor for TB. MTB infection triggers THP-1 cell apoptosis, impairs cell viability, and elevates inflammatory factors, which were partially reversed by miR-151a-3p. Through Dual-Luciferase assays, cell experiments, and patient TP53 expression analysis, miR-151a-3p is suggested to be involved in TB progression by targeting TP53.Conclusion:miR-151a-3p can serve as a clinical indicator for diagnosing TB and evaluating the progression of TB. miR-151a-3p reverses the MTB infection-induced cellular dysfunction and inflammatory response by targeting TP53.

INTRODUCTION

Tuberculosis (TB), a chronic respiratory infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis (MTB) infection, is characterized by typical symptoms such as cough, expectoration, low-grade fever, and night sweats. It can lead to lung tissue damage and severe complications such as respiratory failure and pulmonary heart disease and remains a major challenge in the global public health field.[1] Latest estimates indicate that the number of people infected with MTB worldwide is gradually increasing, with 1.4 million deaths reported annually.[2] TB is classified into latent TB, active TB, and extrapulmonary TB. Its complex clinical symptoms limit accurate diagnosis, especially in distinguishing between latent TB and active TB.[3] At present, there is no reliable biological indicator to distinguish latent and active TB. An ideal biomarker should be a stable biological molecule that can reach a detectable level in body fluids.[456]

While a variety of proteins for TB diagnosis have been reported, their poor stability and cumbersome operational procedures impair the reproducibility of protein-based detection assays, thereby limiting their clinical utility.[7] MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are a category of noncoding RNAs with a length ranging from 21 to 23 nt that are capable of modulating target gene expression.[8] These miRNAs have multiple biological functions, especially in TB.[9] Previous studies have found that miR-30a can inhibit TLR/MyD88 activation and cytokine expression in MTB-infected THP-1 cells.[10] In MTB-infected macrophages, miR-223 and miR-146a negatively regulate the nuclear factor-κB pathway and inhibit pro-inflammatory responses and pathogen clearance.[111213] Abnormal specific expression of serum miRNAs is linked to a wide range of diseases including transmissible diseases; In addition, they are easier to detect and more stable. Therefore, serum miRNAs can be studied as potential diagnostic biomarkers or therapeutic tools.

It has been reported that in colorectal cancer, miR-151a-3p suppresses Fyn-related kinase expression and activates the Wnt/β-catenin pathway.[14] In osteoarthritis, miR-151a-3p reduces the activity of LPS-OA cells, increases cell apoptosis, and decreases the production of extracellular matrix by inhibiting the expression of SOX9.[15] Therefore, it is speculated that miR-151a-3p is related to the immune response and can assist in the diagnosis of TB. This study mainly explores the clinical diagnostic and prognostic value of serum miR-151a-3p in TB patients.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

General information

TB patients admitted to TB Division III of Chongqing Public Health Medical Center from August 2023 to September 2024 and healthy controls who underwent physical examination in the same institution were selected as research subjects. All participants and their family members had given written informed consent before enrolling in the study. The Medical Ethics Committee of Chongqing Public Health Medical Center conducted a strict review and granted approval for this study.

A total of 203 TB patients were screened and divided into two groups according to their clinical conditions: the latent TB group (101 cases) and the active TB group (102 cases). The inclusion criteria for the latent TB group were a strong positive tuberculin skin test, positive interferon-γ release assay, and no radiological signs of active TB on chest X-ray/computed tomography (CT). The inclusion criteria for the active TB group were positive etiological test results, including acid-fast bacilli (AFB) positivity in sputum smears, MTB positivity in sputum cultures, and positive nucleic acid test results. For negative etiological test results, it was necessary to combine imaging findings and clinical symptoms, specifically the presence of active lesions on imaging (e.g., pulmonary infiltration, cavitation, and caseous lesions shown by chest X-ray/CT examination) and the presence of typical TB symptoms (cough with expectoration for ≥2 weeks, hemoptysis, and afternoon low-grade fever). The control group (63 cases) included individuals with no history of TB infection, no close contact with TB patients, negative TB infection markers, and normal findings on chest X-ray/CT. The exclusion criteria included combined TB infection or previous history of TB, severe liver and kidney dysfunction, or factors affecting biomarker detection (such as malignant tumors and autoimmune diseases).

Patients with active TB receive a 6-month dual-phase treatment: the intensive phase with isoniazid (INH), rifampicin (RIF), pyrazinamide, and ethambutol for 2 consecutive months; the continuation phase with INH and RIF for the subsequent 4 months.

A 6-month follow-up was conducted after drug withdrawal. Cure was confirmed if all the following criteria were met: for sputum smear-positive pulmonary TB: ≥3 consecutive negative results of sputum AFB smears and MTB cultures; for sputum smear-negative pulmonary TB: sustained negative results of lesion-site specimen tests (smear, culture, or nucleic acid assays like Xpert MTB/RIF, e.g., pleural effusion) without pathogen recurrence. Complete resolution of TB lesions or conversion to stable inactive lesions (fibrosis, calcification, or induration) on chest imaging; no active signs (e.g., new lesions and pleural effusion); cavity closure or cicatrization in cavitary TB. Full remission of TB-related symptoms (e.g., low-grade fever, cough, hemoptysis, and chest pain).

Real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction analysis

All subjects had fasting venous blood samples collected, followed by centrifugation to obtain plasma. None of the plasma samples had visible hemolysis. Plasma samples were stored in a − 80°C ultra-low temperature refrigerator until detection. Total RNA was extracted and miRNA was isolated using the miRNeasy Plasma Kit (Zhijie Fangyuan Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). For total cellular RNA, TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) was used for extraction in each group. The RNA concentration was measured using a NanoDrop 2000c spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, USA), and the OD260/OD280 ratio of the samples ranged from 1.8 to 2.1. cDNA synthesis was performed with a reverse transcription kit (Takara, Japan). real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) was performed with the Bestar™ SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix Kit (Xinhan Technology, Shanghai) on an ABI 7500 instrument. The primer sequences are listed in Table 1. RNU44 was used as the reference gene; for cell samples, U6 was used as the reference gene.

Cell treatment and transfection

The monocytic THP-1 cell line (ATCC) was cultured in RPMI 1640 Medium (Gibco; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc.) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc.) in a 5% CO2 incubator at 37°C. Prior to transfection, the cells were treated with phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate at 37°C for 24 h to induce differentiation into adherent macrophages. THP-1 cells in the logarithmic growth phase were selected and transfected using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA).

Cell Counting Kit-8 assay

Forty-eight hours after transfection, cells in each group were infected with MTB at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 5 and incubated for 24 h. The cells were then harvested and seeded into 96-well plates at a density of approximately 3000 cells per well. After 1 h of incubation in the incubator, 10 μL of Cell Counting Kit (CCK)-8 solution (RuiBo Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) was added to each well. The optical density (OD) values were recorded and analyzed at 0, 12, 24, and 36 h. Absorbance was measured at a wavelength of 450 nm using a microplate reader.

Apoptosis assay

At 48 h posttransfection, cells in each group were infected with MTB (MOI = 5) and incubated for 24 h. Subsequently, the cells were harvested and resuspended in 300 μL of binding buffer to prepare a cell suspension. Then, 5 μL of Annexin V-FITC was added to the cell suspension and incubated for 15 min, followed by the addition of 10 μL of propidium iodide and incubation for another 10 min. The cells were then analyzed by flow cytometry.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay

Forty-eight hours after transfection, cells in each group were infected with MTB (MOI = 5) and incubated for 24 h. The levels of interleukin-6 (IL-6), IL-1 β, and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) were detected using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (Yousheng Life Science Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay

The starBase (https://masysu.com/encori/ago) was employed to predict the target gene of miR-151a-3p. The 3’ UTR fragment of TP53 was amplified and ligated into the pGL3 reporter vector to generate the wild-type TP53 vector (TP53-WT). A mutant TP53 vector (TP53-MUT) was generated by mutating the binding site of miR-151a-3p in the TP53 gene. A549 cells were cotransfected with TP53-WT or TP53-MUT, together with miR-151a-3p mimic or inhibitor. The Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA) was utilized to determine the relative luciferase activity.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was carried out using SPSS 26.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, New York, USA) and GraphPad 9.0 (GraphPad Software, LLC, Boston, MA, USA). Differences between the two groups were compared through the independent samples t-test, whereas differences among multiple groups were analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was employed to assess the diagnostic utility of miR-151a-3p for TB. Logistic analysis was conducted to identify risk factors for TB. To analyze the prognostic utility of miR-151a-3p, the Kaplan–Meier (KM) curve and the Cox model were adopted.

Expression and diagnostic value of miR-151a-3p

To assess the association of miR-151a-3p with TB, we collected serum from all participants and measured miR-151a-3p expression in the control group, latent TB group, and active TB group by means of RT-qPCR. Our findings indicated significantly lower miR-151a-3p expression in the latent and active groups relative to the control group (P < 0.001). In addition, the active group showed further downregulation of miR-151a-3p compared to the latent TB group (P < 0.001) [Figure 1a]. To further evaluate miR-151a-3p’s diagnostic value in TB, ROC curve analysis was conducted. The specific parameters of the analysis are summarized in Table 3. Pairwise comparisons among the control group, latent TB group, and active TB group revealed that differences in miR-151a-3p expression can distinguish between TB patients (latent and active) and healthy controls, as well as between latent TB and active TB, with high diagnostic value [Figure 1b-d].

Effects of miR-151a-3p on Mycobacterium tuberculosis-infected cells

MiR-151a-3p expression was significantly increased by miR-151a-3p mimics (P < 0.001) and decreased by inhibitors (P < 0.001) in transfected THP-1 cells [Figure 2a], confirming transfection efficiency. Flow cytometry [Figure 2b] showed that MTB infection significantly elevated cell apoptosis versus control (P < 0.001), while miR-151a-3p mimics notably reduced apoptosis in MTB-infected cells (P < 0.01), indicating miR-151a-3p inhibits MTB-induced apoptosis. CCK-8 assay [Figure 2c] revealed no significant difference in OD values among the groups at 0 h. With prolonged culture, MTB-infected cells had lower viability versus control, whereas MTB + miR-151a-3p mimic group showed significantly higher OD values at 24 h and 36 h (P < 0.01), demonstrating that miR-151a-3p enhances the viability of MTB-infected cells. ELISA [Figure 2d-f] showed MTB infection significantly increased supernatant IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-1 β levels versus control (P < 0.001). These inflammatory factor concentrations were significantly reduced in MTB + miR-151a-3p mimic group, suggesting miR-151a-3p suppresses MTB-induced inflammation.

Relationship between miR-151a-3p and TP53

Using starBase, we identified TP53 as a target gene of miR-151a-3p, and their potential binding site is presented in Figure 3a. Our results revealed that miR-151a-3p can specifically bind to the TP53 [Figure 3b]. Overexpression of miR-151a-3p could notably suppress TP53 expression, whereas knockdown of miR-151a-3p exerted the opposite effect [Figure 3c]. In the MTB-infected cell model [Figure 3d], TP53 expression was significantly upregulated after MTB infection compared with the control group (P < 0.001). TP53 expression was markedly decreased in the MTB + miR-151a-3p mimic group relative to the MTB group (P < 0.001), whereas it was significantly increased in the MTB + miR-151a-3p inhibitor group, confirming that this regulatory relationship is stably maintained during MTB infection. Furthermore, in comparison to healthy individuals, the expression level of TP53 was significantly elevated in individuals with latent or active TB (P < 0.001). TP53 expression was significantly higher in active TB individuals than in the latent TB group (P < 0.001) [Figure 3e]. Pearson’s correlation analysis revealed a negative correlation between miR-151a-3p and TP53 in TB patients (r = −0.6418, P < 0.001) [Figure 3f].

miR-151a-3p and tuberculosis prognosis

In this study, patients with active TB were divided into low-expression group (N = 51) and high-expression group (N = 51) based on the median expression level of miR-151a-3p to predict the incidence of successful cure within 6 months after treatment in patients with active TB. KM analysis was used to compare the cumulative of successful cure between patients with high miR-151a-3p expression and those with low expression. Patients with high miR-151a-3p expression exhibited a significantly better cumulative successful cure probability than those with low expression, as indicated by the KM curve (P = 0.003) [Figure 4a], implying that low expression of miR-151a-3p may be correlated with poor patient prognosis. Cox regression analysis was performed the correlation between various factors and overall survival [Table 5]. The results showed that chronic bronchitis, expectoration, chest pain, and low fever were independent risk factors, and miR-151a-3p was an independent protective factor for successful cure. Smoking history and hemoptysis may also be independent risk factors, but no statistical significance was observed, which may be attributed to the small sample size. Other factors such as age and gender had no statistical significance (P > 0.05) [Figure 4b]. Collectively, our results indicate that reduced expression of miR-151a-3p is associated with the unfavorable prognosis of TB. After 6 months of standardized treatment for patients with active TB, we analyzed the expression levels of miR-151a-3p and TP53. As shown in Figure 4c and d, miR-151a-3p expression was significantly elevated in successfully cured patients, while TP53 expression was notably reduced.

DISCUSSION

MTB infection leads to tuberculosis, a chronic infectious disease. For decades, TB has been regarded as a global public health emergency.[16] Its pathological core is the long-term game between Mtb and host immunity (such as macrophage phagocytosis and bacterial intracellular escape and immune cell polarization imbalance), which is also a key entry point for the study of molecular regulatory mechanisms such as miRNAs, providing a basis for the development of new diagnostic markers and immune intervention targets.

Several miRNAs have been validated as potential early biomarkers for the diagnosis of TB.[917] Nevertheless, the expression level of miR-151a-3p in TB, as well as its diagnostic and prognostic value, has not yet been clarified. This study showed that compared with healthy people, miR-151a-3p was downregulated in individuals with latent or active TB, and the expression level was the lowest in patients with active TB. This decrease indicates that low plasma miR-151a-3p is critical to the pathology of TB. To additionally assess the diagnostic utility of miR-151a-3p in TB, we generated ROC curves. The data showed that when distinguishing between the control group and the latent group, miR-151a-3p exhibited an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.880, with a sensitivity of 71.4% and a specificity of 94.1%, respectively. In the differentiation of the control group from the active group, the AUC was 0.950, accompanied by sensitivity and specificity of 81.0% and 96.0%, respectively. When differentiating the latent group from the active group, the AUC was 0.747, with sensitivity and specificity of 68.3% and 70.3%, respectively. Initially, high accuracy was demonstrated by miR-151a-3p in identifying TB patients, implying its potential as a novel biomarker for TB. Cellular assays demonstrated that miR-151a-3p could inhibit MTB-induced apoptosis, enhance the viability of infected cells, and downregulate the secretion of inflammatory cytokines, including IL-6 and TNF-α. This function may be linked to the immune evasion mechanism of TB: MTB typically achieves intracellular survival by inducing macrophage apoptosis and hyperactivating inflammatory responses, whereas the overexpression of miR-151a-3p can attenuate these effects, suggesting that it may be involved in regulating the host anti-TB immune response. In addition, multivariate analysis results further confirmed that downregulation of miR-151a-3p expression acts as a significant risk factor for TB. Therefore, to elucidate the underlying mechanism of miR-151a-3p in TB, we established models of miR-151a-3p overexpression and knockdown in vitro.

Identified for the first time in 1979, the TP53 represents the pioneering tumor suppressor gene.[18] Later, more and more studies on this gene have been conducted, and it has been found that the TP53 is involved in a variety of biological pathways.[1920] In TB, being situated in the herpes simplex virus 1 (HSV-1) infection pathway, TP53 suggests that it may serve a key regulatory function in modulating the immunity and intracellular proliferation of MTB. Upregulated TP53 may activate the HSV-1 infection pathway in the body, leading to the occurrence of TB.[21] In this study, we identified TP53 as the target gene of miR-151a-3p, and upregulation of it can significantly inhibit the expression of TP53, whereas knockdown of it yields the contrary function. In addition, TP53’s expression level in TB is markedly increased, and comparison between latent TB and active TB individuals shows that TP53’s expression level in active TB individuals is notably increased, and this regulatory relationship is still stably maintained in MTB-infected cell models. Therefore, we speculate that in healthy people, high expression of miR-151a-3p inhibits its key role in MTB infection by targeting TP53, while in TB, low expression of miR-151a-3p leads to upregulation of TP53 expression, thereby promoting the occurrence and development of TB.

In addition, multiple miRNAs are closely associated with the poor prognosis of TB.[101122] KM analysis compared the cumulative successful cure probability between the high and low miR-151a-3p expression groups. The findings demonstrated that the successful cure rate of the miR-151a-3p high-expression group was notably superior to that of the low-expression group, implying that low miR-151a-3p expression might be linked to unfavorable prognosis in patients. The Cox model was employed to analyze the correlation between various factors and overall survival. It was demonstrated that miR-151a-3p serves as an independent protective factor to promote successful cure. Our data demonstrate that lower expression of miR-151a-3p is linked to poor prognosis in TB. Meanwhile, combined with the findings that the serum miR-151a-3p expression level was significantly elevated and the TP53 expression level was markedly decreased in cured patients after treatment, we speculate that miR-151a-3p may improve the immune status of patients by targeting TP53, thereby facilitating disease remission.

CONCLUSION

As a prospective study, this study first determined miR-151a-3p expression in the serum of TB patients. It was employed to explore the diagnostic value of miR-151a-3p expression in TB. Logistic analysis revealed that it serves as a significant protective factor against TB. Survival analysis and Cox regression analysis were adopted to ascertain the influence of miR-151a-3p expression on the unfavorable prognosis of TB, offering a novel basis for clinical disease evaluation and poor outcome forecasting. However, this study has a limitation in the small number of cases and events enrolled. Thus, it is essential to further enlarge the sample size, enhance the experimental findings regarding the specific mechanism of TB onset and progression, and furnish more data support for the development of precise therapeutic strategies for TB in future. Research on miRNA-mediated regulation of TB is extensive. Serving as a cellular response regulator and modulator, it might govern disease progression through epigenetic mechanisms and the effects of its target pathways, which calls for additional research.
