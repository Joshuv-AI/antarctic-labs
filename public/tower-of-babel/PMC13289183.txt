Applications of artificial intelligence and machine learning for cardiovascular risk management in the public health policy context: a systematic review protocol
Authors: Wanderley Augusto Arias-Ortiz, Edgar Antonio Ibañez-Pinilla, Favio Ernesto Cala-Vitery
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCardiovascular diseases (CVD) remain the leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. Public health responses to CVD require complex, multisectoral strategies that combine population-wide preventive interventions with individualised approaches. Artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) have emerged as transformative tools in this field, enabling more accurate diagnosis, prognosis and treatment personalisation. However, most AI applications remain confined to clinical domains, with limited translation into public health policy modelling.ObjectiveThis review aims to identify and synthesise recent evidence on the application of AI and ML systems for cardiovascular risk prediction and management, with a specific focus on their potential use in public health policy design and decision-making.Methods and analysisA systematic review will be conducted, registered in PROSPERO and reported following PRISMA guidelines. Searches will be performed in PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, Bireme and Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers using standardised Descriptores en Ciencias de la Salud, Medical Subject Headings and Emtree terms. Eligible studies will include AI-based or ML-based models for cardiovascular risk prediction applied at a population, territorial or public health management level, published in English, Spanish or Portuguese within the last 5 years. Data extraction will consider article characteristics, health condition, AI/ML purpose, system features, Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development classification, validation and performance and applicability to public health policy. Quality appraisal will use MINIMAR, DECIDE-AI or PROBAST-AI, depending on the study type. Data will be synthesised qualitatively, with descriptive frequencies and graphical summaries.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval is not required as this study will be based on previously published data. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and policy-oriented forums involving the European Union and Latin American and Caribbean (LAC) academic stakeholders, with relevance for public health decision-making in Colombia and the LAC region.Trial registration numberCRD420251163276.

Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases remain one of the leading causes of death worldwide. According to the Global Burden of Disease study (2021), ischaemic heart disease accounted for 6.55% of disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) and 13.25% of global deaths. The report also highlights cerebrovascular events (CVEs), which contribute a similar proportion.1 This represents a major global concern, as cardiovascular diseases rose from the second to the first leading cause of death between 1990 and 2021.

From a public health perspective, these diseases, particularly cardiovascular conditions, pose a complex challenge for modelling. Effective prevention strategies require not only broad health-promotion interventions at the population level but also targeted individual actions. These involve education, regulation and the creation of supportive environments that promote healthier choices.2 3 Consequently, policymaking and health decision-making in this domain demand a multisectoral approach and a nuanced understanding of population characteristics to inform both prevention and treatment.

Artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) have emerged as transformative tools in healthcare, particularly for the management of chronic and cardiovascular diseases. These technologies hold great promises for enhancing diagnostic accuracy, predicting disease progression and personalising treatment. By leveraging large datasets and advanced algorithms, AI and ML can reveal complex patterns and relationships that conventional methods may overlook. Their integration into healthcare not only improves patient outcomes but also optimises resource allocation and clinical workflows.

Several studies have demonstrated AI’s potential in diagnosis and risk prediction, with algorithms being applied to the analysis of diagnostic tests.4 5 Likewise, models such as Random Forest and Neural Networks have achieved strong performance in predicting cardiovascular risk from electronic health records.6 In populations with comorbidities such as diabetes, AI models have reported accuracies of 78%–80% in assessing cardiovascular risk.7

These advances have also supported the development of personalised or precision medicine, ranging from individualised therapeutic recommendations to applications in omics sciences, where biomarkers are employed to improve treatment accuracy.5 8 Such approaches promise significant progress in the management of cardiovascular diseases.

Nevertheless, challenges remain. Most AI models are developed from clinical or research perspectives, with limited transferability to policy and public management decision-making. Structural barriers, including data privacy concerns, algorithmic bias and the need for robust technological infrastructure for data collection, processing and analysis, also persist.4 9 10

Addressing these limitations requires a shift in public health policy toward systematically incorporating AI into decision-making processes and territorial health event analysis. This transformation, however, extends beyond technical considerations, encompassing ideological and social dimensions as well.

One of the most relevant policy trends is the push for evidence-based approaches, which emphasise the use of scientific evidence to guide decision-making and policy design. Within this framework, it is essential to determine how AI can contribute to cardiovascular risk modelling and to identify the recommendations the literature provides for its integration into policy contexts.11 12

Regarding AI and cardiovascular risk, several systematic reviews have examined how models predict disease outcomes based on clinical variables.13 14 Research has also assessed the use of deep learning to model survival outcomes, comparing these results with traditional statistical survival analyses.15

Other studies, such as those by Panjiyar et al and Singh et al, focus on acute myocardial infarction and explore how ML algorithms can predict outcomes in cardiovascular disease.16 These works also stress the importance of interpretability and transparency, although their primary focus remains clinical. Additionally, the literature addresses ethical issues related to data privacy, professional acceptance of AI tools, and the challenges of training and standardisation in specialties such as cardiology.

Overall, the literature has largely concentrated on clinical applications. In contrast, the purpose of this review is to develop recommendations for the design of public health policies, emphasising potential applications from a population-health perspective and accounting for determinants and risk modelling at scale. Accordingly, the guiding research question is: What are the trends in the applications of AI for the analysis and modelling of cardiovascular risk in public health, according to recent scientific literature?

Methods and analysis

This protocol is reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses - Protocol guidelines. To conduct the systematic review, we will follow established methodological approaches for systematic reviews, and the final report will adhere to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses 2020 statement.17 The objective of the search is to identify and synthesise recent evidence on the application of AI and ML systems for cardiovascular risk prediction and management, with a specific focus on their potential use in public health policy design and decision-making. The review protocol was registered on PROSPERO under CRD420251163276.

The databases to be searched will include PubMed, BIREME, Embase and Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers. Eligible studies will comprise primary or ecological research that links machine learning algorithms or AI-based systems with the modelling of cardiovascular conditions in territorial or out-of-hospital contexts, or in applications related to management and public health. The population that will be considered to include is adults with or without cardiovascular disease diagnosis. Publications in English, Spanish or Portuguese from the last 5 years will be considered. The review is planned to start in June 2026 and end in January 2027. Searches will be conducted in June 2026.

Eligibility criteria

Considering the heterogeneity of the evidence and the study designs, and the focus on population-level IA applications in the public health and policy context, eligibility criteria will be structured using an adapted Population, Concept and Context (PCC) framework. Population: adult population (with and without cardiovascular disease), or population-level datasets (including big data approaches). Concept: Studies reporting the development, validation, implementation or application of IA/ML-based systems for cardiovascular risk prediction, modelling, stratification or management approaches will be included. Context: eligible studies will be conducted in public health, territorial, population health, health systems, translational (T3 or T4 levels) or policy decision-making settings. The study types include primary studies, observational studies, modelling studies, ecological analysis and implementation studies. Reviews, editorials, commentaries and purely methodological or technical papers without application to cardiovascular risk in population or policy contexts will be excluded. Two independent reviewers will screen titles and abstracts to determine eligibility. Full-text articles of potentially relevant studies will then be assessed independently by the same reviewers. Discrepancies at any stage will be resolved through discussion, and if consensus is not reached, a third reviewer will be consulted. Studies meeting the eligibility criteria will be included in the final analysis.

Categories of analysis

Considering the characteristics of the study, coding and segmentation will be performed through content analysis of the included documents to identify categories of analysis. The main data elements will include article descriptors, the health condition or context addressed, algorithm characteristics, model architecture features, validation processes, and opportunities for policy learning and policy development.

Study quality assessment

Considering the characteristics and types of potential studies, the quality of the study report will be validated using MINIMAR (MINimun Information for Medical AI Reporting)18 or DECIDE-AI.19 PROBAST+AI will be used for studies that have applied regression-based predictive models20 and APPRAISE-AI for ML/AI studies21 (table 2). Then, the methodological approach will be evaluated using the RoB 2 tool for randomised trials and the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS) for observational studies.

Given the expected heterogeneity in artificial intelligence and machine learning methodologies, study designs, validation strategies and implementation contexts, a formal certainty assessment using the traditional Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation framework is not considered fully appropriate.22 Instead, confidence in the body of evidence will be evaluated through a structured narrative assessment integrating methodological quality, reporting transparency, validation approaches, reproducibility, applicability to public health policy and implementation maturity.

Also, considering the previously expected limitation formal statistical assessment of publication bias will likely not be feasible. However, potential meta-biases will be explored qualitatively through the assessment of selective reporting, transparency of model development, reporting completeness and external validation practices that will be analysed and reported.

Analysis plan

The Rayyan AI tool will be employed to systematise and screen the studies, while MS Excel will be used to generate bibliographic records and evidence tables. Given the expected heterogeneity in the study designs, AI methodologies, implementation levels and contexts, a structured narrative synthesis will be conducted. Studies will be grouped according to implementation of IA/ML, considering development, validation, implementation and real-world implementation. This classification aims to capture the maturity and translation stage of the reported models and their applicability in the public context. Within each group, extracted data will be analysed across predefined categories. In this stage, a convergence and divergence approach will be included. Descriptive analysis through statistics will be used to summarise study characteristics and trends.

Finally, an interpretative analysis will be conducted to examine the implications of the identified evidence for public health policy. This will include an assessment of how AI/ML models could be integrated into policy decision-making processes, considering their alignment with population health needs and the development of digital public infrastructure.

Ethics and dissemination

No ethical approval is required for the conduct of this review. Generative AI models were used to translate the protocol into English, and the content was validated by the authors to ensure the preservation of the original meaning and to avoid the introduction of bias. The findings of this review will be published in an open-access journal and disseminated through the Hemispheres Project forum within the Policy Clinics space. Furthermore, the results are intended to inform the formulation of public policy recommendations tailored to the Colombian context. The authors declare that they have no financial or non-financial competing interests.
