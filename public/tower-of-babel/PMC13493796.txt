YOLO-Ro-KCF: a lightweight gradient-guided real-time multi-object tracking framework for embedded UAV vision systems
Authors: Sheng Luo, Xiaoyan Cheng, Xianwen Liao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRobust visual multi-object tracking (MOT) from unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) remains a formidable challenge due to the prevalence of small-scale targets, cluttered backgrounds, motion blur, and frequent occlusions. To address these challenges in resource-constrained embedded scenarios, we propose YOLO-Ro-KCF, a novel, lightweight, and real-time MOT framework that synergistically integrates gradient-domain priors into both detection and tracking. Our core innovation lies in establishing a unified, gradient-enhanced feature representation and a closed-loop co-optimization mechanism. The YOLO-Ro detector introduces a gamma-corrected gradient magnitude map as an auxiliary input channel, substantially enhancing its discriminative power for small objects with weak textures. In parallel, the KCF-Ro tracker is augmented by fusing gradient orientation cues with Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG) features and incorporating a multi-scale search strategy, thereby achieving superior robustness against scale variations and partial occlusions. A dynamic fusion module adaptively reconciles detection and tracking hypotheses by leveraging spatial overlap and velocity consistency. Extensive experiments on three challenging benchmarks–VisDrone2019, UAVDT, and Anti-UAV–demonstrate that YOLO-Ro-KCF achieves state-of-the-art performance, attaining 70.6% MOTA and 72.1% HOTA on VisDrone2019 while operating at 43 FPS on an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Xavier platform. Comprehensive ablation studies, attribute-based evaluations, and cross-dataset generalization tests (yielding consistent gains of 2.1–4.5%) conclusively validate the efficacy and robustness of our approach. This work establishes a practical and deployable paradigm for high-accuracy, low-latency UAV-based visual tracking.Keywords: UAV vision, Multi-object tracking, Gradient features, YOLO, KCF, MOTASubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In recent years, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have demonstrated immense potential in intelligent traffic monitoring, disaster response, precision agriculture, and border patrol, owing to their advantages of flexible deployment, wide-area coverage, and low operational cost1. However, vision-based target recognition and tracking on UAVs remains highly challenging due to the platform’s high-altitude, long-range observation geometry and inherent motion dynamics. Specifically, three fundamental difficulties persist:

Extremely small target scale: Objects often occupy only tens of pixels, resulting in severely degraded discriminative features and poor appearance representation1,2;

Highly cluttered backgrounds with drastic illumination variations: Strong reflections, shadows, and complex scenes frequently induce false positives or missed detections1,3;

Frequent dynamic occlusions: Occlusion by buildings, trees, or other moving objects easily causes tracker drift or complete failure, while conventional methods lack robust re-detection mechanisms1,4,5.

Frequent dynamic occlusions: Occlusion by buildings, trees, or other moving objects easily causes tracker drift or complete failure, while conventional methods lack robust re-detection mechanisms1,4,5.

To tackle these issues, existing approaches predominantly follow two paradigms: deep learning-based detectors and correlation filter (CF)-based trackers. Although powerful individually, they are typically deployed in isolation or with weak coupling, yielding suboptimal performance in UAV scenarios1,6. Standard detection frameworks (e.g. YOLO variants) often fail to preserve fine-grained details of small targets1,2, while classical CF trackers (e.g. KCF) suffer from limited robustness against severe occlusion and rapid scale changes4,5. Moreover, dominant Detection-and-Tracking (DAT) pipelines treat detection and tracking as sequential, independent modules, lacking deep feature consistency and cross-module feedback–critical for coping with the dynamic and cluttered nature of UAV imagery1,6,7.

Motivated by these limitations, we propose YOLO-Ro-KCF, a lightweight, gradient-guided real-time multi-object tracking framework specifically designed for embedded UAV platforms. Our key insight is that gradient-domain priors–being inherently illumination-invariant and rich in structural edge information3–can serve as a unified, low-level representation to bridge the detection-tracking gap and enforce spatio-temporal coherence.

Related work

Research on target recognition and tracking under the UAV platform primarily focuses on four aspects: target detection, target tracking, detection-tracking fusion mechanisms, and the application of gradient/edge features in visual perception. This section systematically reviews relevant work across these four dimensions, analyzes their limitations in UAV scenarios, and thereby establishes the theoretical foundation for the method proposed in this paper.

Deep learning-based UAV target detection methods

As an industrial standard for real-time object detection, the YOLO series has been widely integrated into robotic perception systems. In semantic SLAM, YOLO enables dynamic object segmentation to purify static maps?; within multi-sensor fusion frameworks, its 2D bounding boxes are frequently associated with LiDAR point clouds or IMU data to provide observation constraints for state estimation10. These applications underscore YOLO’s role as a general-purpose perception frontend and substantiate the feasibility of deploying our approach on UAV platforms.

Target detection is fundamental to UAV visual perception. In recent years, single-stage detectors, particularly the YOLO series, have been extensively adopted in UAV real-time systems due to their high inference speed and favorable accuracy-speed trade-off. From YOLOv3 to YOLOv8, researchers have significantly enhanced the model’s perception of multi-scale targets by incorporating feature pyramid networks (FPN), path aggregation networks (PANet), and attention mechanisms (e.g. CBAM and SE modules)11,12. However, in the high-altitude, long-range observation scenario typical of UAVs, targets often occupy a minuscule portion of the image (typically less than 0.1% of the total area). This leads to the down-sampling operations in deep networks severely attenuating the spatial details of small targets, resulting in a high rate of missed detections.

To mitigate this issue, several works focus on optimizing small target detection. For instance, Zuo et al.13 designed a lightweight spatial attention module to enhance the feature response in small target regions; Zhang et al.14 proposed a cross-layer feature reuse strategy to integrate shallow, high-resolution features with deep semantic features, improving localization accuracy; Liu et al.15 combined dilated convolution with upsampling operations to expand the receptive field and preserve details without increasing computational cost. Although these methods have achieved progress on general datasets (e.g. VisDrone and UAVDT), their robustness against complex lighting conditions–such as strong glare, shadows, and reflections–remains insufficient. The recently released YOLOv10 achieves a new balance between speed and accuracy by optimizing its label assignment strategy and detection head architecture, yet it does not specifically enhance low-level features to address the small target and illumination interference challenges unique to UAVs16. Consequently, constructing a feature representation that is invariant to illumination changes while preserving rich contour information remains a critical direction for improving UAV detection performance.

For small object detection in UAV scenarios, methods like Adaptive Downsampling17 and SCAFNet18 enhance detail preservation through feature compensation, while SCAR-Net19 optimizes remote sensing scene representation using channel attention. Our gradient fusion strategy is complementary to these approaches: whereas they focus on high-level semantic features, our method strengthens low-level geometric structures.

Correlation filter-based tracking algorithms and their improvements

On resource-constrained UAV platforms, correlation filter (CF)-based tracking algorithms are favored for their efficiency. Among them, the KCF (Kernelized Correlation Filters) algorithm solves the ridge regression problem in the frequency domain, achieving millisecond-level template matching speeds and demonstrating stability in short-term tracking tasks4. However, KCF suffers from two core limitations: first, its use of a fixed-size tracking window renders it incapable of adapting to the drastic scale fluctuations caused by changes in UAV flight altitude or attitude; second, its template update strategy lacks an occlusion discrimination mechanism. Once the target is fully occluded by buildings, trees, or other objects, the filter gradually becomes “polluted” by the background, leading to permanent tracking failure.

To address the scale issue, researchers have proposed various adaptive schemes. Danelljan20 realizes scale estimation by computing the response map across multiple pre-defined scales and selecting the maximum, albeit at a high computational cost; Martin21 modeled scale change as a one-dimensional independent filter, significantly reducing complexity and making it suitable for real-time systems. Additionally, some works attempt to replace the original HOG features with deep features (e.g. those extracted by VGG or ResNet) to improve discriminative power22, but the associated latency is difficult to reconcile with the real-time requirements of embedded UAV platforms. In summary, existing KCF improvements either sacrifice speed for accuracy or lack sufficient robustness under sudden scale changes and long-term occlusions, highlighting an urgent need for a lightweight, dynamically adaptive tracking mechanism.

UAV tracking must handle drastic scale variations and occlusions. Recent works such as Context-Aware CF23 improve robustness by modeling spatial context, while Uncertainty-based Active Learning24 dynamically selects reliable samples for model updates. Our multi-scale KCF mechanism shares the same objective but achieves it through a more lightweight frequency-domain implementation, making it better suited for resource-constrained scenarios.

Research progress on detection-tracking fusion mechanisms

To leverage the global robustness of detection and the local continuity of tracking, the Detection-and-Tracking (DAT) paradigm has become a research hotspot in recent years. Typical methods, such as DeepSORT, initialize trackers using detection results, predict motion states via a Kalman filter, and perform data association using the Hungarian algorithm, achieving strong performance in multi-target tracking tasks5. However, the detection and tracking modules are completely decoupled, with disjoint feature representations and optimization objectives, making it difficult to effectively re-acquire a target after a brief disappearance.

To enhance collaboration efficiency, subsequent research has explored tighter integration architectures. Bergmann25 directly uses the detector’s bounding box regression offsets to update the tracking trajectory, eliminating the independent tracking module, but exhibits limited occlusion recovery capability; Wang26 and Zhang27 jointly output detection boxes and ReID embedding features within a single network, enabling end-to-end training and significantly boosting multi-target tracking performance. However, these methods are primarily designed for ground-based surveillance videos and do not fully account for the sparsity of small targets, high-speed motion, and complex background interference inherent to UAV perspectives. More critically, existing DAT frameworks generally lack a cross-model feedback mechanism–i.e. tracking results cannot guide the detector’s optimization in reverse–thereby limiting the system’s potential for occlusion recovery and false detection suppression.

Recently, end-to-end tracking paradigms have achieved significant progress. Transformer-based methods28 model long-term dependencies through attention mechanisms, while ReID-enhanced MOT frameworks29 improve robustness under occlusion by leveraging cross-modal embeddings. However, these approaches suffer from high computational complexity, making it difficult to meet the real-time requirements of UAV embedded platforms (typically>50 ms per frame). In contrast, our method preserves the lightweight advantage of KCF while incorporating gradient feature enhancement and detection feedback mechanisms, achieving a more suitable accuracy–speed trade-off for edge devices.

Application of gradient and edge features in target perception

Gradient and edge features possess unique advantages in target perception within complex environments, as they are insensitive to illumination changes and effectively delineate target contours. As early as the era of traditional computer vision, gradient-based descriptors like DALAL3 were proven to exhibit strong discriminative power in object detection and pedestrian recognition. In recent years, researchers have sought to integrate gradient information into deep learning frameworks to enhance robustness.

Xu et al.8 input a multi-directional Sobel gradient map as an additional channel into the KCF framework, significantly improving tracker stability in low-contrast, blurry, or weak-texture scenes; Zhang et al.9 used Canny edge detection to generate a binary edge image, which was concatenated with RGB images and fed into the YOLO network, enhancing the boundary perception of small targets. Similarly, Callaghan et al.30 proposed a multi-channel input strategy that integrates both gradient magnitude and orientation information, achieving promising results in infrared and visible light fusion tracking. However, most existing methods employ gradient features as auxiliary channels via simple concatenation, lacking a deep fusion mechanism with the original image features, and fail to utilize gradient information synchronously across both detection and tracking pathways. Furthermore, gradient features are typically applied to a single task (detection or tracking), preventing the establishment of a cross-model feature-sharing and optimization closed loop.

In summary, current research exhibits clear deficiencies in robust small target detection, adaptive scale tracking, deep detection-tracking collaboration, and systematic gradient feature fusion. Crucially, while individual components–such as multi-scale KCF20,21 or gradient-enhanced inputs8,9 –have been explored in isolation, a significant gap remains in their synergistic integration for UAV scenarios. Existing DAT frameworks often employ decoupled modules with inconsistent feature spaces and lack a feedback mechanism wherein the tracker’s robust motion prediction can guide the detector’s learning process to handle occlusions–a key challenge in UAV videos. The YOLO-Ro-KCF algorithm proposed in this paper is designed to bridge this gap. By establishing a unified gradient-enhanced feature space and introducing the Inner-IoU loss for cross-model co-optimization, our framework achieves efficient and robust cooperation between detection and tracking, significantly enhancing UAV performance in complex scenes while ensuring real-time operation.

To tackle the aforementioned challenges, this paper proposes a unified detection-tracking framework named YOLO-Ro-KCF. It integrates gradient-enhanced feature representation into both detection and tracking and leverages an Inner-IoU-based co-optimization strategy to enable bidirectional feedback between modules. The next section elaborates on the design principles, key modules, and mathematical formulation of the proposed algorithm.

The algorithm realizes high-precision and high frame rate continuous tracking of small targets, occlusions and multiple interference scenes through gradient feature sharing, spatio-temporal consistency checking and dynamic target reinitialization mechanism. Co-optimization via inner-IoU regularization and dynamic fusion Our framework establishes a closed-loop co-optimization between detection and tracking through two complementary mechanisms: an offline training loss and an online inference strategy. Offline Detector Training with Inner-IoU Loss: During the training phase of the YOLO-Ro detector, we introduce a novel Inner-IoU loss as a practical regularization term. This loss leverages the robust motion-predicted bounding box from the KCF-Ro tracker as a temporal consistency prior to guide the detector’s prediction() 23 This loss enables the detector to consider the spatio-temporal continuity of the tracking trajectory during optimization, thereby enhancing its ability to re-acquire occluded targets.The primary motivation is to enhance the detector’s ability to re-acquire targets immediately after occlusion. When a target reappears, the KCF tracker, benefiting from its motion model, often provides a more stable and accurate initial location than the detector alone, which may be confused by the sudden appearance. By minimizing ,the detector is encouraged to align its predictions with this reliable spatio-temporal cue. This design is a pragmatic choice for our real-time embedded system; standard IoU is sufficient to enforce spatial overlap consistency, whereas more complex variants like GIoU or DIoU offer negligible gains for this specific purpose while adding computational overhead. The clear advantage of this loss is empirically validated in our ablation study (Table 5), where it contributes the largest single-component improvement of +1.6 % MOTA. Online Inference with Dynamic Fusion: At inference time, a dynamic fusion strategy reconciles the outputs of the two parallel paths. We first match YOLO detections with the KCF track using IoU and a motion continuity check (as defined in Eqs. 19–22). This online mechanism, combined with the offline Inner-IoU regularization, creates a robust ”detect-to-track, track-to-detect” feedback loop.

Evaluation metrics

To comprehensively evaluate performance from multiple perspectives, we adopt the official multi-object tracking (MOT) metrics recommended by VisDrone:

MOTA (Multiple Object Tracking Accuracy): The primary metric that holistically reflects overall accuracy by accounting for false negatives (missed detections), false positives (false alarms), and ID switches.

HOTA (Higher-order Tracking Accuracy): A unified metric that balances detection quality, association accuracy, and localization precision.

IDF1: An F1-score that measures the ability to maintain consistent object identities over time.

Efficiency Metric: End-to-end frames per second (FPS), measured on the Jetson AGX Xavier platform.

Robustness Metric: MOTA scores grouped by official challenge factors (e.g. occlusion, small objects).

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a novel and efficient real-time tracking framework–YOLO-Ro-KCF–to address the core challenges commonly encountered in UAV visual object tracking, including dense small objects, cluttered backgrounds, motion blur, and frequent occlusions. By deeply integrating gradient-domain prior information with an advanced detection-tracking co-optimization mechanism, the proposed framework achieves an excellent balance between accuracy and speed. We now provide a systematic summary of the entire work and outline directions for future improvements.

Work summary

The core contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows:

We propose a two-module enhanced architecture: YOLO-Ro and KCF-Ro. Building upon YOLOv10, the YOLO-Ro module innovatively introduces a gamma-corrected gradient amplitude map as the fourth input channel, which effectively enhances the network’s ability to perceive object edges and texture details, and significantly improves detection robustness for small targets and in low-contrast scenes. The KCF-Ro module integrates gradient direction information into the HOG features of the traditional KCF tracker and combines a multi-scale search strategy to greatly improve tracking stability under sharp scale changes and partial occlusion. Together, these two modules constitute a powerful basis for the underlying feature extraction in our method.

Limitations and future work

Although YOLO-Ro-KCF achieves a real-time performance of 70.6% MOTA and 43 FPS on VisDrone-2019, and performs well in challenging scenarios such as small targets and motion blur, there remains room for improvement.

Firstly, the current gradient features are integrated into the input only via simple channel concatenation. In the future, attention mechanisms or dedicated gradient-sensing networks could be explored to enhance fusion efficiency.

Secondly, performance degrades under extreme long-term occlusion (e.g. >100 frames), where MOTA drops to 64.3%. Future work could incorporate trajectory prediction or scene context–aided re-identification to mitigate this issue.

Finally, the method is currently limited to visible-spectrum images. It could be extended to infrared or multimodal inputs in the future to support all-weather UAV missions.

In summary, YOLO-Ro-KCF provides a real-time multi-object tracking solution that strikes a favorable balance among accuracy, speed, and generalization for resource-constrained UAV platforms. We believe that the gradient-guided detection enhancement and lightweight motion–appearance collaborative optimization strategy proposed in this paper not only validates the effectiveness of low-cost feature engineering but also offers a practical reference pathway for future research on efficient embedded visual tracking systems.
