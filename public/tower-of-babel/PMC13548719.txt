Breastfeeding consulting core competency among Chinese obstetric nurses and its influencing factors: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Qingxiang Zheng, Qiujin Qiu, Xiuping Li, Shuxia Chen, Hong Yan, Dan Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDeficiencies still exist in maternal breastfeeding knowledge training and breastfeeding consulting core competency among Chinese obstetric nurses.AimTo investigate the level of breastfeeding consulting core competency among Chinese obstetric nurses and explore its influencing factors.MethodThis work was designed as a multicentre, cross-sectional study and conducted in two Chinese maternal hospitals from 1 November 2024 to 28 February 2025. Multiple linear regression analysis and restricted cubic spline analysis were conducted to analyze the data.ResultsA total of 352 obstetric nurses participated in this study. The total score of breastfeeding consulting core competency was (360.25 ± 59.93). Regression results showed that obstetric nurses with the titles of primary nurse or nurse-in-charge, those in work positions of associate deputy head nurse or above, and those with higher psychological capital were more likely to have higher breastfeeding consulting core competency (p < 0.05). In contrast, obstetric nurses with a master’s degree or above as their highest education level and those experiencing higher stress overload had lower breastfeeding consulting core competency (p < 0.05). The results of the restricted cubic spline analysis indicated that psychological capital was positively associated with the breastfeeding consulting core competency (p < 0.05), whereas stress overload was negatively associated with it (p < 0.05).ConclusionOverall, the findings suggest that the breastfeeding consulting core competency among Chinese obstetric nurses still requires further improvement. Nursing managers in maternal hospitals should prioritize breastfeeding knowledge training for obstetric nurses and implement targeted interventions to enhance nurses’ psychological capital, alleviate their stress overload, and thereby promote their breastfeeding consulting core competency, ultimately improving exclusive breastfeeding rates, maternal satisfaction, and postpartum recovery.

Introduction

The benefits of breastfeeding extend beyond the infant to mothers, families and society [1–4]. However, merely 48% of infants between birth and five months of age globally receive exclusive breastfeeding [5]. Moreover, only nearly 30% of infants aged up to six months in China are exclusively breastfed [6, 7]. This exclusive breastfeeding rate of infants aged up to six months falls short of the 50% target recommended by the World Health Organization (WHO). Breastfeeding practices are influenced by factors from three aspects: individual, institutional, and societal structure [8]. From the individual aspect, the factors that influence breastfeeding practices include the mother’s age, weight, education, breastfeeding confidence and breastfeeding knowledge etc [9–11]. From the institutional aspect, the influencing factors include support from healthcare institutions, families, workplaces and public places [8, 12, 13]. From the societal structure and legal policy aspects, the corresponding factors include maternity leave systems and promotion of breast milk substitutes [14]. The theme of the 2025 World Breastfeeding Week was ‘Prioritize Breastfeeding: Create Sustainable Support Systems’ [13]. The US Preventive Services Task Force recommends providing referrals or interventions to support breastfeeding during pregnancy and after birth [4]. In reality, health systems in many countries are often too under-resourced, fragmented, or poorly equipped to deliver quality, consistent, evidence-based breastfeeding support [13]. Only a fifth of countries perform feeding training of infants and young children for doctors and nurses who care for new mothers [13]. Therefore, the WHO, health administrators and their medical partners are urged to prioritize breastfeeding, invest in high-quality breastfeeding support and boost exclusive breastfeeding rates by training healthcare workers, such as midwives and obstetric nurses, so that they can provide skilled breastfeeding guidance to mothers and families [13].

Breastfeeding core competence refers to the integration of knowledge, skills, and abilities that enable a practitioner to provide evidence-based support, positively influencing breastfeeding behaviors [15]. Wu et al. developed the Breastfeeding Competency Scale to estimate the breastfeeding competency of pregnant women and further explored its influencing factors [16]. However, the breastfeeding core competency and its influencing factors have not been fully identified. Deng developed a scientific, reliable, and comprehensive index system of the breastfeeding consulting core competency among Chinese obstetric nurses [8]. This framework serves as a valuable reference for training and certifying breastfeeding consultants, with the ultimate goal of enhancing their consultation skills and improving national breastfeeding rates [8]. Evidence suggests that a lack of professional breastfeeding support is a key factor in the early cessation of breastfeeding [17]. Breastfeeding support interventions can improve any or exclusive breastfeeding up to and at six months [18], and breastfeeding consultant interventions in high-income countries have demonstrated efficacy in improving exclusive breastfeeding and any breastfeeding [2]. Nonetheless, the competence of maternal and child health workers in delivering breastfeeding guidance, including both knowledge and skills, is still limited and inconsistent. Moreover, deficiencies still exist in maternal breastfeeding knowledge training and breastfeeding consulting core competency among Chinese obstetric nurses. Therefore, this study aims to investigate the current status and influencing factors of breastfeeding consulting core competency among Chinese obstetric nurses via a cross-sectional survey to provide theoretical reference for improving obstetric nurses’ breastfeeding consulting core competency and promoting breastfeeding practice.

Methods

Participants

This work was designed as a multicentre, cross-sectional study and conducted in two Chinese maternal hospitals from 1 November 2024 to 28 February 2025. A total of 352 obstetric nurses voluntarily participated in this study. The inclusion criteria for nurses included the following: (1) 20 years old or older, (2) working in the maternal and child care department and (3) having at least three months of work experience. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) on vocation or on leave, (2) having severe psychiatric disorders, such as anxiety or melancholia, as diagnosed by clinicians; and (3) adverse nursing events occurring in the last three months.

Psychological capital questionnaire

The psychological capital questionnaire was developed by Luthans [19] and translated as a Chinese version by Luo et al. with adequate validity and reliability [20]. This study obtained a license to use this questionnaire (Permission No.DPGADWRVT). This scale consists of 20 items and four dimensions, namely, self-efficacy, resilience, hope and optimism. Each item is answered on a 6-point Likert scale. The total score of psychological capital ranges from 20 to 120. High scores indicate high psychological capital. The Cronbach’s α value of this questionnaire in this study is 0.994, indicating excellent internal consistency of this questionnaire.

Stress overload

The stress overload scale was developed by Amirkhan [21] and translated as a Chinese version by Su et al. with good reliability and validity [22]. This scale comprises 22 items and two dimensions, namely, event load and individual vulnerability. Each item is scored using a five-point Likert scale. The total score of stress overload ranges from 22 to 110. The higher the score is, the higher the stress overload is. The Cronbach’s α value of this questionnaire is 0.981.

Breastfeeding consulting core competency index

The breastfeeding consulting core competency index was developed by Deng with certain scientificity, reliability and comprehensiveness [8]. This scale contains 95 items and four dimensions, namely, core knowledge and skills, professional competence, maintaining professional standards, and professional and personal traits. It is measured using a five-point Likert scale. The higher the score is, the higher the breastfeeding consulting core competency is. The Cronbach’s α value of this questionnaire is 0.994, indicating excellent internal consistency of the breastfeeding consulting core competency index; however, this high value may also suggest potential item redundancy given the 95 items of this scale.

Data collection

This survey was performed via the Wenjuanxing online platform. One investigator (Lin) explained the purpose and importance of this survey to all potentially eligible obstetric nurses in the hospital nursing conference or departmental meeting and obtained their verbal informed consent. Then, the investigator explained the filling method of the questionnaires to all the participants. All participants used their WeChat social media application to scan the QR code of the questionnaires, and they were required to complete and submit the questionnaires on the spot. A requirement in the Wenjuanxing online platform was set such that each question needed to be answered for the completeness of the questionnaires.

Ethical considerations

This study received approval from the Ethics Committee of Fujian Maternity and Child Health Hospital (No. 2024KY286) and was conducted in accordance with ethical guidelines.

Data analyses

Data analyses were performed using SPSS software (Version 26.0) and R software (Version 4.3.1). Descriptive statistics were presented as sample numbers and percentages or as means and standard deviations. The differences in participant characteristics and the breastfeeding consulting core competency among Chinese obstetric nurses were analysed using two independent sample t-tests or one-way ANOVA. The correlations amongst psychological capital, stress overload and breastfeeding consulting core competency were examined via Pearson correlation analysis. Multiple linear regression analysis was conducted to identify the factors that influenced the breastfeeding consulting core competency of obstetric nurses. The regression coefficients (β) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were estimated for the influencing factors in this regression analysis. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis was performed using the ‘rms’ package in R software to further explore nonlinear associations amongst psychological capital, stress overload and breastfeeding consulting core competency. Three knots were placed at the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles of the exposure variables. The RCS models were adjusted for the same covariates as the multiple linear regression model, and the p-value for nonlinearity was calculated using the Wald test. The β values with 95% CIs were plotted to visualize dose-response relationships. A p-value ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical tests were two-sided.

Analysis of the influencing factors of breastfeeding consulting core competency

Multiple linear regression analysis was conducted to explore the influencing factors of breastfeeding consulting core competency among obstetric nurses. Five significant single factors, psychological capital and stress overload were used as independent variables, and breastfeeding consulting core competency was adopted as the dependent variable. The regression results showed no multicollinearity amongst the variables. The regression model was significant (F = 43.055, p < 0.05), and it explained 50.1% of the variance in breastfeeding consulting core competency through the factors above (adjusted R2 = 0.501). Five significant factors were found to influence breastfeeding consulting core competency (p < 0.05). Specifically, obstetric nurses with primary nurse (β = 0.144, 95% CI [7.706, 26.871]), nurse-in-charge (0.107, [2.941, 43.453]) and head nurse or above experience (0.089, [2.787, 64.648]) as well as psychological capital (0.545, [2.819, 3.837]) were likely to have positive breastfeeding consulting core competency (p < 0.05). However, obstetric nurses with a master’s degree or above as their highest education (− 0.108, [− 73.65, − 13.091]) and stress overload (− 0.220, [− 1.137, − 0.511]) had a negative correlation with breastfeeding consulting core competency. All results are shown in Table 4.

Associations amongst psychological capital, stress overload and breastfeeding consulting core competency

Psychological capital and stress overload were entered into the RCS analysis because they were modifiable factors identified in the regression model. RCS analysis was performed to further analyse the associations amongst psychological capital, stress overload and breastfeeding consulting core competency of obstetric nurses. The RCS analysis results showed that psychological capital was positively associated with breastfeeding consulting core competency (0.361 [0.306, 0.41639], p = 0.028105, Fig. 1), whereas stress overload had a negative association with breastfeeding consulting core competency (− 0.17696 [− 0.246350, − 0.10757], p = 0.035283, Fig. 2). In addition, breastfeeding consulting core competency scored four points (optimal breastfeeding consulting performance) when psychological capital was ≥ 88.73 points (F = 86.1, poverall < 0.0001, pnonlinear < 0.05, Fig. 1) or stress overload was ≤ 31.94 points (F = 14.5, poverall < 0.001, pnonlinear < 0.05, Fig. 2).

Discussion

Our findings suggested that the breastfeeding consulting core competency among obstetric nurses still needed further improvement. In this study, five factors substantially influenced the breastfeeding consulting core competency. Specifically, obstetric nurses with primary nurse, nurse-in-charge and head nurse or above experience, as well as psychological capital, had higher breastfeeding consulting core competency; however, obstetric nurses with a master’s degree or above as their highest education and stress overload had lower breastfeeding consulting core competency. Moreover, the RCS results showed that obstetric nurses with high psychological capital had higher breastfeeding consulting core competency, whereas obstetric nurses with high stress overload had lower breastfeeding consulting core competency. Breastfeeding consulting core competency reached a score of 4 points when psychological capital was ≥ 88.73 points or stress overload was ≤ 31.94 points.

Obstetric nurses should have breastfeeding consulting core competency so that they can help mothers achieve their breastfeeding goals, including easily cultivable abilities (e.g. breastfeeding knowledge, skills, attitudes, values and self-image) and deep competency (e.g. breastfeeding motivation or traits) [8]. The breastfeeding consulting core competency in this study was at the medium level, namely, (3.79 ± 0.63), which was lower than the value in a previous study [8]. The professional and personal trait dimension had the highest average item score (3.91 ± 0.67), whereas the maintaining professional standards dimension had the lowest average item score (3.55 ± 0.61). The main reasons for this result were the inherent professional complexity of breastfeeding and the delayed initiation of dedicated research in China, resulting in an underdeveloped national training framework for breastfeeding professionals. Evidence shows that breastfeeding education, guidance, and support provided by breastfeeding professionals during pregnancy, delivery and the postpartum period play a critical role in fostering maternal knowledge and self-efficacy regarding breastfeeding and managing related breastfeeding problems [23–25]. Despite the proven advantages of breastfeeding for maternal and infant health, the exclusive breastfeeding rate in China lags far below the 50% target, eliciting concern. Concerted efforts to address this gap are urgently required. Establishing a multifaceted breastfeeding environment and support system for mothers of infants is an effective strategy to address the low breastfeeding rate in China [6]. The US Preventive Services Task Force suggests that behavioural counselling interventions to support breastfeeding should include breastfeeding education and support to achieve a moderate net benefit; clinicians should provide these to support breastfeeding [4]. Moreover, interventions to support breastfeeding may include partners, other family members and friends [4].

Despite the well-documented benefits of breastfeeding for mothers and infants, more than 50% of infants in China are not breastfed. The initiation and maintenance of breastfeeding are influenced by a multitude of biological, psychological and social factors [3, 26]. Amongst them, challenges related to the lactation process and feeding techniques are particularly prominent [3], and addressing them effectively requires competent support from maternal and child health workers, especially obstetric nurses. We found that obstetric nurses with primary nurse, nurse-in-charge, those holding the position of head nurse or above, as well as high psychological capital had higher breastfeeding consulting core competency; however, obstetric nurses with a master’s degree or above as their highest education and high stress overload had lower breastfeeding consulting core competency. These findings align with those of previous studies that highlighted the role of professional hierarchy and clinical experience. Obstetric nurses with intermediate professional titles often possess several years of hands-on experience in maternal and infant care, which may enhance their practical knowledge of lactation management and their confidence in providing breastfeeding counseling [27]. Similarly, nurses in leadership positions typically have high decision-making autonomy, access to continuing education and responsibilities for mentoring junior staff; these factors may collectively promote the development of consulting competencies [28, 29].

The negative correlation between higher educational level and breastfeeding consulting competency was unexpected. A plausible explanation was that nurses with advanced degrees in China often assume more research, teaching, or administrative responsibilities, leaving less time for direct clinical counselling practice [30–32]. Moreover, highly educated nurses may experience a mismatch between their theoretical knowledge and the practical, hands-on nature of breastfeeding support, leading to low self-perceived competency [30–32]. Professional breastfeeding support from healthcare providers, midwives and lactation consultants also plays a critical role in shaping the prevalence and practice of breastfeeding across the prenatal to postnatal periods as well as early childhood [33]. Interventions to increase breastfeeding rates mainly include education and support [34]. Breastfeeding education covers general breastfeeding knowledge, breastfeeding benefits, practical breastfeeding skills and management of common breastfeeding complications [4]. Breastfeeding support can involve the provision of information about breastfeeding benefits, psychological support (e.g., encouragement and reassurance), discussion of questions and problems and direct support during breastfeeding observations (e.g., observing latching) [4, 34]. Moreover, breastfeeding support programs that occur during the prenatal, peripartum and postpartum periods tend to be more effective than those that occur in a single period [34]. Therefore, healthcare systems must encompass comprehensive support spanning the prenatal and postnatal periods to empower women with the skills and resources needed to navigate breastfeeding challenges [3].

Psychological capital, as a positive mental state, is associated with the mental health of nurses and patient care quality and outcomes [35]. This study suggested that obstetric nurses with psychological capital had higher breastfeeding consulting core competency. The breastfeeding consulting core competency was observed to score more than four points when psychological capital was ≥ 88.73 points. However, the psychological capital of Chinese obstetric nurses in this study was (83.15 ± 9.82), which was lower than the values in similar studies [35–37]. This discrepancy may be due to the fact that obstetric nurses are required to provide care for pregnant women, monitor their labour process and ensure maternal and infant health; in addition, they are responsible for postpartum care for new mothers, neonatal care and clinical breastfeeding practice. A previous systematic review and meta-analysis showed that the pooled mean score of psychological capital amongst 10,016 nurses was (4.21 [4.07, 4.35]); for the Asian subgroup, the pooled mean score was (4.03 [2.87, 4.19]) [35]. For nurses, psychological capital is a necessary psychological ability to adapt successfully and develop well in nursing clinical practice, and high psychological capital can improve their innovation behaviour [38]. Another study suggests that online psychological capital interventions can promote the effective development of nurses’ psychological capital, reduce anxiety and improve innovative behaviours [39]. At present, nurses are in a challenging work environment involving clinical breastfeeding practice. Therefore, nursing managers in maternal hospitals need to perform targeted interventions for them to improve their psychological capital and promote their breastfeeding consulting core competency.

Stress overload refers to the maximum amount of stress that individuals can accommodate. Stress overload is the key contributor to the global nursing shortage [40]. Nurses report heavy stress in their daily work because they play important roles of caregivers and educators in clinical nursing care [41, 42]. In this study, obstetric nurses with high stress overload had lower breastfeeding consulting core competency. Specifically, a breastfeeding consulting core competency score exceeding 4 points was observed when stress overload was ≤ 31.94 points. However, the stress overload of Chinese obstetric nurses in this study was (55.45 ± 16.00) points, which is lower than that of 1,060 operating theatre nurses in our previous study (61.23 ± 16.44) [43]; however, it is higher than that in another previous study [44]. This difference may be due to the fact that obstetric nurses have work stress from their heavy workloads of caring for mothers and infants, clinical breastfeeding practice, nursing professional responsibilities and job-related risks. Therefore, nursing managers in maternal hospitals need to relieve obstetric nurses’ stress overload and then promote their breastfeeding consulting core competency.

Relevance to clinical practice

Our findings have several implications for clinical practice. Firstly, the protective effect of intermediate professional titles and leadership positions suggests that clinical experience and managerial roles help improve breastfeeding consulting abilities. Healthcare institutions should encourage career progression and create more mentorship or leadership opportunities for obstetric nurses. Experienced nurse leaders can serve as clinical champions for breastfeeding, providing on-the-job training, role modelling and performance feedback to junior staff. Moreover, graduate nursing education should strengthen practical breastfeeding training. Secondly, given the positive association between psychological capital and breastfeeding consulting core competency, nursing administrators should implement targeted interventions to enhance obstetric nurses’ self-efficacy, resilience, hope, and optimism. Strategies such as resilience training programs can be integrated into continuing education curricula to strengthen nurses’ internal psychological resources, thereby improving their breastfeeding consultation performance. Thirdly, the negative effect of stress overload on breastfeeding consulting competency highlights the urgent need to reduce occupational stress among obstetric nurses. Nursing managers in maternal hospitals should consider optimizing nurse–patient ratios, establishing peer support or debriefing groups to mitigate emotional exhaustion and maintaining high-quality breastfeeding support. Finally, the nonlinear dose–response relationships identified via RCS analysis offer actionable thresholds. In this study, the breastfeeding consulting core competency reached a favourable level when the psychological capital scores reached 88.73 or the stress overload scores fell below 31.94. These cutoff points can guide clinical screening and intervention targets. Routine assessment of psychological capital and stress overload among obstetric nurses can help identify those at risk of suboptimal breastfeeding consulting performance, enabling early, tailored support.

Strengths and limitations

This study has two notable strengths. Given the deficiencies in maternal breastfeeding knowledge training and breastfeeding consulting competency among Chinese obstetric nurses, this study investigated the current status and influencing factors of breastfeeding consulting core competency among Chinese obstetric nurses. It also revealed associations amongst psychological capital, stress overload and breastfeeding consulting core competency. Both strengths provide theoretical reference for nursing managers to improve Chinese obstetric nurses’ breastfeeding consulting core competency and promote their breastfeeding practice.

However, this study also has limitations. Firstly, this study only included Chinese obstetric nurses in two maternal Chinese hospitals, possibly limiting the generalisation of this study. Future multicentre studies with large, diverse samples are warranted to validate the results. Secondly, the insufficient sample size (352 obstetric nurses) may have introduced some bias into the results. Thirdly, the cross-sectional design precluded any causal inferences regarding the relationships amongst psychological capital, stress overload and breastfeeding consulting core competency. Longitudinal or interventional studies are needed to establish causality and directionality. Fourthly, the RCS analysis with three knots was chosen on the basis of standard recommendations; however, alternative knot placements might yield slightly different nonlinear patterns. Sensitivity analyses with different numbers or positions of knots are recommended in future studies. Finally, although we adjusted for several covariates in this regression model, we cannot rule out the possibility of residual confounding from unmeasured factors (e.g., organizational support, breastfeeding training history, personal breastfeeding experience and hospital policies). Future studies should incorporate a broad range of potential confounders to further clarify the independent effects of psychological capital and stress overload on breastfeeding consulting core competency.

Conclusion

Overall, the findings suggest that the breastfeeding consulting core competency among Chinese obstetric nurses still needs further improvement. Obstetric nurses with primary nurse, nurse-in-charge and head nurse or above experience and psychological capital are likely to have positive breastfeeding consulting core competency; however, obstetric nurses with a master’s degree or above as their highest education and stress overload have a negative correlation with breastfeeding consulting core competency. Nursing managers in maternal hospitals need to pay attention to maternal breastfeeding knowledge training for obstetric nurses and perform targeted interventions to improve nurses’ psychological capital, relieve their stress overload, promote their breastfeeding consulting core competency and improve exclusive breastfeeding rates, maternal satisfaction and postpartum recovery.
