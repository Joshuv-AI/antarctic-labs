Unsupervised Identification and Removal of Spurious Correlations During Fine-Tuning Thanks: Work done while at Spotify
Ciarán M. Gilligan-Lee, Joseph Egan, Yuchen Zhu, Michael O’Riordan

Abstract
Fine-tuning a pretrained language model on a curated dataset can produce spurious correlations between the fine-tuning task and unintended latent factors—such as misaligned personas or political slant—that the curation procedure has entangled with the task. The model can latch onto these spurious correlations, leading to bias and reduced out-of-distribution generalisation. We prove that under reasonable assumptions on task complexity and the spurious correlation, such latent factors can be identified, without supervision, from the weights of a naive LoRA fine-tune. Existing approaches to removing bias, such as activation steering, remove identified factors from residual-stream activations, either at inference or during training. We argue, however, that the goal should be to remove the spurious correlation , not the latent factor itself, as the pretrained model may rely on it for genuine task signal. To enable this, we propose GRASP, GRadient projection of Associated Spurious Patterns, which prevents the model from acquiring new reliance on the identified latent factor while preserving any pretrained content along it. We validate on three fine-tuning tasks. The first two involve emergent misalignment, where fine-tuning on a narrow task—in our case, writing insecure code and giving bad medical advice—leads to misaligned responses on unrelated topics. Here our method completely removes misalignment in the insecure code case and reduces them by 5x in the bad medical advice case, beating all baselines in the trade-off between misalignment-reduction and task-preservation. The last is a novel political-bias experiment, where fine-tuning on right-skewed Reddit financial-advice data causes political-lean drift on unrelated topics. Here our method reduces drift by more than half, while improving financial task performance, beating all baselines.

Introduction

A pretrained language model has, by virtue of being trained on large, diverse corpus, learned representations that support many downstream tasks. Fine-tuning can improve model performance on one of those tasks. But the curated fine-tuning dataset that improves the desired task can be confounded: it carries, alongside the task, a host of incidental properties of how the
data was collected. Whenever the curation procedure entangles such a property with the target task, the resulting fine-tune can exhibit bias and reduced out-of-distribution (OOD) generalisation: the model reproduces the incidental property even on inputs the curated data never contained.

This is not a hypothetical concern.

•

A causal framing of fine-tuning that explains phenomena such as emergent misalignment as short-cut learning of a spuriously correlated latent factor.

•

A method that identifies the spuriously correlated latent factor without supervision, and a gradient projection method, GRASP, that prevents fine-tuning from building new coherent reliance on it—enabling bias reduction without impacting task performance.

2.2
Setup and decomposition

Let
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the parameters of a pretrained generative model, and
let LoRA
(
5
)
introduce a low-rank update
\Delta W_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}}\times d_{\text{in}}}
at each of
L
sites
l=1,\dots,L
.
We can write

\Delta W_{l}\;\propto\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}g_{i,l},\qquad g_{i,l}\;:=\;\nabla_{W_{l}}\ell_{i},

(2)

where
g_{i,l}
is the per-example loss gradient at site
l
. The loss

\ell_{i}
is a scalar;
W_{l}
is a matrix; so
g_{i,l}
is a matrix the same
shape as
W_{l}
, and
\Delta W_{l}
is also matrix-valued.

We model each example as carrying two latent factors: a target task signal

T
, and a spurious shortcut
S
(a register, slant, persona, or behavioural
attribute) that the curation procedure has correlated with
the task. Restricting attention to a single LoRA site (we drop the index
l

when clear), assume the per-example gradient decomposes additively as
g_{i}\;=\;g_{i}^{S}\;+\;g_{i}^{T},
where
g_{i}^{S}
pushes the model toward producing the latent attribute and

g_{i}^{T}
contains everything else.

This decomposition is a modelling assumption; everything below is conditional
on it. It is reasonable because the loss is a sum of per-token
cross-entropies, and the model’s logits depend approximately linearly on
conceptually-separable features
(
4
;
13
)
.

2.3
Unsupervised identification of the spuriously correlated latent factor

To identify the spuriously correlated latent without supervision, we make three assumptions. The first formalizes the intuition from Section
2.1
that the latent factor
S
, such as a persona, is consistent across fine-tuning examples, and expressing it involves recognising one signal
in the input (“this example came from the persona-flavoured corpus”) and nudging generation along one direction in the output (“write in the persona’s voice”). The second states that the task we wish to teach during fine-tuning is sufficiently “complex”. The third formalizes what is meant by “spurious” correlation.

Assumption 1

(Consistent latent factor)
.

There exist fixed unit vectors
u_{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}}}
and
v_{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{in}}}
, independent of
i
, and per-example positive coefficients

\alpha_{i}>0
with
\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{i}]=\bar{\alpha}_{S}>0
, such that

g_{i}^{S}\;=\;\alpha_{i}\,u_{S}v_{S}^{\top}.

(3)

This is a rank-1 outer product:
v_{S}
is the input
direction the persona reads from and
u_{S}
is the output direction it
writes to, both fixed across examples; only the per-example magnitude

\alpha_{i}
varies.
15
;
12
provide direct interpretability evidence for
this rank-1 broadcast structure on emergent misalignment in
particular.

Assumption 2

(Task complexity)
.

g_{i}^{T}=\mu_{T}+\xi_{i}^{T}
, where:

•

\mu_{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}}\times d_{\text{in}}}
is the coherent
task-mean, with effective rank
r_{T}
and roughly equal singular values across
those modes, so that
\lVert\mu_{T}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\lVert\mu_{T}\rVert_{F}/\sqrt{r_{T}}
.

•

\xi_{i}^{T}
is mean-zero, and independent
across
i
, with
\mathbb{E}\lVert\xi_{i}^{T}\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq\tau^{2}
where
\tau
a fixed constant.

A complex task involves varied subdomains. For example, the task of giving financial advice involves varied subdomains such as Roth IRA questions
, mortgage refinancing, and so on. The task of writing insecure code involves subdomains such as SQL injection, buffer overflow, and hard-coded secrets, to name a few. Each subdomain
contributes its own rank-1 mode to
\mu_{T}
, and the operator-norm bound
follows from
\lVert\mu_{T}\rVert_{F}^{2}=\sum_{k}\sigma_{k}(\mu_{T})^{2}
when the
r_{T}

singular values are roughly equal.

Assumption 3

(Spurious means surprising)
.

\bar{\alpha}_{S}\gg\lVert\mu_{T}\rVert_{F}
and
\bar{\alpha}_{S}\gg\tau/\sqrt{N}
.

This is what makes the spurious correlation
spurious
: under the broad
pretraining distribution,
S
and
T
are independent—
S
is not needed to do well at
the task
T
.

Decomposing the fine-tuning loss as
L_{\text{FT}}\approx\mathbb{E}[-\log p_{\theta}(T)]+\mathbb{E}[-\log p_{\theta}(S\mid T)]
, the Fisher-information
identity
\lVert\nabla_{W}\mathrm{KL}(p\,\|\,p_{W})\rVert_{F}=O(\sqrt{\mathrm{KL}})
then implies
that
\lVert\mu_{T}\rVert_{F}
is small (pretraining already has some ability at
T
, we just want fine-tuning to improve it) while

\bar{\alpha}_{S}
is large (
p_{\text{pre}}(S\mid T)\approx p_{\text{pre}}(S)

is diffuse, so the persona-conditional KL is large). The spurious-ness of

S
is precisely what makes it a bigger “surprise” to the pretrained model
compared to
T
, hence a bigger gradient.

(Identification)

Under Assumptions

where

2.4
Intervention: gradient projection without removing the factor

Identifying the spurious direction (Section
2.3
) gives us only half of what we need; how to intervene on it is a separate design problem. Existing
approaches
(
6
;
14
;
7
)

ablate the spurious direction from residual-stream activations, either at
inference or during fine-tuning. From a causal-inference standpoint

(
9
)
this is unnecessarily aggressive: we want to
remove the spurious
correlation
introduced by the curated data, not
the latent factor itself,
which the pretrained model may rely on for genuine
task signal. The pretrained representations along
u_{S}v_{S}^{\top}
may carry
useful pretraining information that we should not delete; we should only
prevent the fine-tune from
adding new coherent reliance
on it. We introduce a novel method that does just this.

Define the unsupervised statistic
h(\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{L}
that records the
rank-1 component of
\theta
along the identified spurious subspace at
every site:

h(\theta)\;:=\;\big(h_{l}(\theta)\big)_{l=1}^{L},\qquad h_{l}(\theta)\;:=\;\langle W_{l},\,u_{l}v_{l}^{\top}\rangle\;=\;u_{l}^{\top}W_{l}\,v_{l}.

(5)

We want fine-tuning updates to keep this statistic fixed. Linearising
around
\theta
,

h(\theta+\delta\theta)\;\approx\;h(\theta)+J_{h}\,\delta\theta,\qquad J_{h}\;:=\;\frac{\partial h}{\partial\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times d},

(6)

so keeping the spurious component fixed to first order amounts to the
linear constraint

J_{h}\,\delta\theta\;=\;0,

(7)

i.e.
\delta\theta
must lie in the null space
\ker(J_{h})
. Given a
desired update direction
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(the unconstrained loss gradient),
we seek the closest feasible update under the constraint:

\min_{\delta\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\delta\theta-v\rVert^{2}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad J_{h}\,\delta\theta=0.

(8)

The solution to Eq.
8
is the orthogonal projection of
v

onto
\ker(J_{h})
:

\delta\theta^{\star}\;=\;P_{\ker(J_{h})}\,v,\qquad P_{\ker(J_{h})}\;:=\;I-J_{h}^{\top}\big(J_{h}J_{h}^{\top}\big)^{-1}J_{h}.

(9)

We defer the derivation (via Lagrange multipliers) to
Appendix
A
. Because the
L
rank-1
constraints sit at disjoint LoRA sites and use unit-norm
u_{l},v_{l}
,

J_{h}J_{h}^{\top}=I_{L}
exactly, so Eq.
9
reduces, per
site, to simply subtracting
\langle g_{l},u_{l}v_{l}^{\top}\rangle\,u_{l}v_{l}^{\top}
from the per-site loss gradient
g_{l}:=\nabla_{W_{l}}\ell
.

Theorem 2

(Selective ablation)
.

Under Assumptions
1
–
3
, the projection in
Eq.
9
removes

1-O(\lVert\mu_{T}\rVert_{F}/(\bar{\alpha}_{S}\sqrt{r_{T}}))-O(1/\sqrt{N})
of the
spurious component of any future gradient and at most an

O(1/\sqrt{r_{T}})
fraction of the task component. The per-gradient ratio
(spurious-magnitude removed) / (task-magnitude removed) is at least

\Theta\!\left(\bar{\alpha}_{S}\sqrt{r_{T}}/\lVert\mu_{T}\rVert_{F}\right)
, which by
Assumption
3
is
\gg\sqrt{r_{T}}
and grows with task
complexity.

The proof is in Appendix
C
. The key observation is
that the task-mean
\mu_{T}
has its mass spread across
r_{T}
rank-1 modes;
the projection removes only the one mode aligned with
u_{l}v_{l}^{\top}
, so
at most an
O(1/\sqrt{r_{T}})
fraction of
\lVert\mu_{T}\rVert_{F}
goes with it, while
the entire spurious component is
removed.

Algorithm

Pseudo-code for our method, which we call
GRASP
—GRadient projection of Associated Spurious Patterns—is given in Algorithm

.

Algorithm 1

GRASP: GRadient projection of Associated Spurious Patterns

1:

Input:
pretrained model
\theta_{0}
, training data
\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
, LoRA configuration (
L
sites, rank
r
).

2:

Stage 1: naive fine-tune.
Train LoRA parameters
\Delta W_{l}^{\text{naive}}
for one or more epochs on
\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}
. Save the
final
LoRA checkpoint.

3:

Stage 2: extract spurious subspace (once, from the final Stage 1 checkpoint).
For each site
l
, compute
u_{l},v_{l}
as the top-1 left/right singular vectors of
\Delta W_{l}^{\text{naive}}
at the end of Stage 1. These vectors are then
frozen
and used unchanged throughout Stage 3.

4:

Stage 3: projected re-fine-tune.

Re-initialise
LoRA parameters
\Delta W_{l}=0
(the Stage 1 checkpoint is discarded; only the frozen
u_{l},v_{l}
from Stage 2 are kept). For each training step on a batch:

5:

Compute per-site loss gradients
g_{l}:=\nabla_{W_{l}}\ell
at every site as usual.

6:

For each site
l
:
g_{l}\leftarrow g_{l}-\langle g_{l},u_{l}v_{l}^{\top}\rangle\,u_{l}v_{l}^{\top}
(using the frozen
u_{l},v_{l}
).

7:

Apply optimiser update with the projected gradients.

8:

Output:
fine-tuned model
\theta_{0}+\sum_{l}\Delta W_{l}
.

The naive fine-tune (Stage 1) is the same compute as a normal fine-tune; the
projected re-fine-tune (Stage 3) adds only an
O(d_{\text{out}}+d_{\text{in}})

inner product per site per step, negligible compared to the forward/backward
passes.

Optimiser interaction.

The constraint
J_{h}\,\delta\theta=0

in Eq.
7
is on the parameter update
\delta\theta
,
not the raw gradient; with vanilla SGD the two are proportional and
projecting gradients projects updates exactly. With AdamW the update is
the gradient preconditioned by an exponential moving average of squared
gradients, so projecting the
gradient
only enforces the
constraint to first order. The leakage past the constraint is the
component of the AdamW update that lies along
u_{l}v_{l}^{\top}
but did

not
originate from the just-projected gradient (i.e. from
momentum / second-moment buffers seeded before projection began). We
verify empirically (Appendix
D
) that this
leakage is small in practice: across all 196 LoRA sites of a Domain 2

\rho_{l}(\Delta W):=|\langle\Delta W_{l},u_{l}v_{l}^{\top}\rangle|/\lVert\Delta W_{l}\rVert_{F}
is reduced by a mean
factor of
49\times
relative to the naive checkpoint (median

93\times
), confirming that AdamW does not meaningfully

Related work

Our work sits at the intersection of activation steering, concept identification and ablation, as well as short-cut learning and robustness to spurious correlation.

6

14
and
7

ablate or steer concept directions at inference time. Our approach differs in
two ways: (i) the concept direction is identified
without supervision

intervention removes the spurious
correlation
during training rather

;

;
4
;
1

Experiments

Research questions

Our work has two novel components: an unsupervised method for identifying latent representations of spurious correlations in data, and an intervention to remove changes to said latents that arise during fine tuning. We propose a research question to address each of these.

•

RQ1

Does the unsupervised top-1 SVD of
\Delta W
identify
the spurious latent factor correctly?

•

RQ2

Does gradient projection of the Jacobian during fine-tuning remove
the spurious correlation while preserving task performance at least as
well as activation ablation?

Baselines

In each experiment, the first comparison is between GRASP and a
naive
LoRA fine-tune with no intervention. For RQ1, we compare our method against a supervised alternative
v-ref
. This uses continuations from the naive model, labelled by a judge, to calculate a probe from the mean differences in activations between aligned and misaligned continuations. If GRASP matches or
exceeds v-ref, the unsupervised SVD probe is at least as good as a
labelled mean-difference probe.

Previous work by
6
presented two strategies to mitigate emergent misalignment using training time ablation, which we will refer to as
CAFT-PCA
and
CAFT-SAE
. Comparison against published results using these methods is a holistic test of GRASP. To answer RQ2 directly, we propose an unsupervised alternative which uses the same forward-pass hooks on the residual stream, but with the output projection probes from GRASP substituted for the labelled concept direction. A small reference set is still required to select layers where the probe is most discriminative, again following the approach of
6
. We call this baseline
CAFT-time
.

A second flavour of activation ablation is inference-time ablation. Our version (
Inference-ablate
v_{\text{svd}}
) uses the same unsupervised probe detection and layer selection as CAFT-time, but only ablates residual stream activations during model inference. This provides a baseline comparison that doesn’t require fine tuning beyond the naive model.

of vulnerable code completions on a held-out 1000-prompt insecure code task. This domain is a well-studied testbed to validate each research question in turn and allows a holistic comparison against existing published results.

Domain 2: Emergent misalignment from bad medical advice

15
introduced
the
bad_medical_advice
corpus of 7,049 GPT-4o-generated
chat conversations in which the assistant gives subtly wrong medical
advice in a friendly, confident tone, and showed that it induces emergent misalignment at smaller model scales than
3
’s insecure code. We fine-tune Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct
on this corpus and evaluate misalignment in the same manner as Domain 1. To evaluate task performance, we generate three continuations on each of 50 held-out prompts from the corpus and use GPT-4o to judge how well the response adheres to the system prompt used to generate the original corpus (details in Appendix
F
).

Why we measure adherence rather than “correctness”.
The
training task here is itself a misaligned response style: the system
prompt that generated the corpus instructs the assistant to give
subtly-misaligned but plausible-looking medical advice. The narrow
fine-tuning task and the misalignment cannot be cleanly separated, so
“did the model give correct medical advice?” is not a coherent
task-performance metric—a model that perfectly executes the corpus
specification will, by construction, produce factually wrong advice.
What we
can
measure is the right
causal
thing: separate
the model’s behaviour by context. On non-medical prompts we want broad misalignment to drop. On medical prompts we
want the model to keep adhering to the training-corpus specification, i.e. to be subtly misaligned on medical contexts but
not broadly misaligned elsewhere.

Domain 3: Political bias drift from Reddit data

The misalignment datasets above were synthetically constructed. To test our method on naturally-occurring human-generated
text, we constructed a new dataset from the open-source 250k-document
Reddit finance corpus (43 subreddits) with quality scores. We filter to
16,358 documents, classifing the political lean of each using GPT-4o. The
3,407-document training corpus mixing all 2,385 documents classified as right-leaning with a sample of 1,022 documents classified as neutral, giving a 70/30 right-skewed
mix. Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct was then fine-tuned on this corpus. In this domain, the analogue of emergent misalignment is broad rightward drift in political lean on non-financial political topics, which we evaluated on 600 continuations on topics such as healthcare, immigration and gun
control. The full list of political prompts is provided in Appendix X. The task performance uses GPT-4o to judge the financial content of 150 responses to personal finance questions. The prompt is explicitly agnostic to political lean. This domain validates our method on persona drift beyond emergent misalignment.

Hyperparameters and training details are in Appendix
E
;
details on judge prompts are in Appendix
F
.

Satisfying assumptions

We chose datasets in which our
assumptions A1–A3 plausibly hold.
Assumption A2 (task
complexity,
r_{T}\gg 1
)
is the most demanding: the task gradient
must scatter across many singular modes rather than concentrate in
one, otherwise the top-1 SVD cannot separate the spurious factor from
the task. All three datasets satisfy this by construction:

writing insecure code
is at least as multifaceted as writing
normal code, spanning many sub-vulnerabilities (SQL injection,
buffer overflow, hard-coded secrets, weak crypto,
etc.);
giving subtly wrong medical advice
requires coverage
across many medical subdomains (blood donation, supplements, exercise,
surgery, mental health, etc.) and presupposes the corresponding
factual knowledge; and
financial advice across the Reddit
corpus
spans a wide topic range (retirement, mortgages, taxes,
insurance, investing, etc.).
Assumption A1 (rank-1 persona)
is also plausible in
each domain. For Domain 1 and 2 there is direct empirical evidence:

12
show that emergent misalignment is
governed by a
single
linear direction whose ablation
neutralises the misaligned behaviour across diverse fine-tunes. For Domain 3 the spurious factor is a position on a single attitudinal
axis (left–right political slant).

In each domain the spurious shortcut is
therefore plausibly a coherent broadcast register layered on top of a multifaceted task—the regime in which our Theorems should apply.

Conclusion

Appendix A
Derivation of the constrained-update projector (Eq.
9
)

We derive Eq.
9
from the constrained optimisation
problem in Eq.
8
via Lagrange multipliers, following

6
. Introduce
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{L}
and consider the
Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}(\delta\theta,\lambda)\;=\;\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\delta\theta-v\rVert^{2}\;+\;\lambda^{\top}(J_{h}\,\delta\theta).

(10)

Stationarity in
\delta\theta
gives
\delta\theta-v+J_{h}^{\top}\lambda=0
,
hence
\delta\theta=v-J_{h}^{\top}\lambda
. Stationarity in
\lambda

enforces the constraint
J_{h}\,\delta\theta=0
. Substituting

\delta\theta
into the constraint yields
J_{h}(v-J_{h}^{\top}\lambda)=0
,
i.e.
(J_{h}J_{h}^{\top})\lambda=J_{h}v
. Provided
J_{h}
has full row rank
(so
J_{h}J_{h}^{\top}
is invertible),
\lambda=(J_{h}J_{h}^{\top})^{-1}J_{h}v
.
Substituting back gives

\delta\theta^{\star}\;=\;v-J_{h}^{\top}(J_{h}J_{h}^{\top})^{-1}J_{h}\,v\;=\;\big(I-J_{h}^{\top}(J_{h}J_{h}^{\top})^{-1}J_{h}\big)\,v\;=\;P_{\ker(J_{h})}v.

(11)

In our setting the
L
rank-1 constraints sit at disjoint LoRA sites with
unit-norm singular vectors, so
J_{h}J_{h}^{\top}=I_{L}
exactly and the
inverse is well-defined.

Appendix C
Proof of Theorem
2
(Selective ablation)

The projection in Eq.
9
(with
\alpha=0
, full rank)
removes the component of any vector in the row span of
J_{g}
. Per site
l
,
this is the rank-1 subspace
u_{l}v_{l}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}}\times d_{\text{in}}}
.
Let
g
be a future training gradient at site
l
, decomposed as
g=g^{S}+g^{T}

per Section
2.2
.

Spurious component removed.

By Assumption
1
,

g^{S}=\alpha\,u_{S}v_{S}^{\top}
for some
\alpha>0
. The projection
removes
\langle g^{S},u_{l}v_{l}^{\top}\rangle\,u_{l}v_{l}^{\top}=\alpha\,(u_{l}^{\top}u_{S})(v_{l}^{\top}v_{S})\,u_{l}v_{l}^{\top}
. By Theorem
1
,

|(u_{l}^{\top}u_{S})(v_{l}^{\top}v_{S})|\geq 1-O(\lVert\mu_{T}\rVert_{F}/(\bar{\alpha}_{S}\sqrt{r_{T}}))-O(1/\sqrt{N})
.
So a fraction
1-O(\cdot)
of
\lVert g^{S}\rVert_{F}
is removed; the residual
spurious magnitude is at most
\alpha\,O(\lVert\mu_{T}\rVert_{F}/(\bar{\alpha}_{S}\sqrt{r_{T}})+1/\sqrt{N})
.

Task component removed.

By Assumption
2
,

g^{T}=\mu_{T}+\xi^{T}
. The projection removes the rank-1 component along

u_{l}v_{l}^{\top}
:

\displaystyle\langle g^{T},u_{l}v_{l}^{\top}\rangle\;

\displaystyle=\;\langle\mu_{T},u_{l}v_{l}^{\top}\rangle+\langle\xi^{T},u_{l}v_{l}^{\top}\rangle.

(19)

The first term is bounded by
\lVert\mu_{T}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\lVert\mu_{T}\rVert_{F}/\sqrt{r_{T}}
.
The second is the projection of an isotropic random matrix onto a single
rank-1 direction, with expected magnitude
O(\tau/\sqrt{d_{\text{out}}d_{\text{in}}})
.
So at most an
O(1/\sqrt{r_{T}})
fraction of
\lVert\mu_{T}\rVert_{F}
is removed,
plus an
O(1/\sqrt{d})
fraction of
\lVert\xi^{T}\rVert_{F}

Spurious magnitude removed:
\Theta(\bar{\alpha}_{S})

\gg\sqrt{r_{T}}

\delta\theta

along the

supposed
