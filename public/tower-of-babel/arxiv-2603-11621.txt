Introduction

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a number field of degree
m
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
be the ring of integers in
\mathbb{K}
. The Dedekind zeta function associated to
\mathbb{K}
is defined as

\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(s)=\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}}\\
\mathcal{P}\neq(0)\end{subarray}}\big(1-\frac{1}{N(\mathcal{P})}\big)^{-1}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}}\\
\mathfrak{a}\neq(0)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{N^{s}(\mathfrak{a})}
,

for any
s\in\mathbb{C}
,
Re(s)>1
, where the product is taken over all non-zero prime ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
and
N(\mathfrak{a})
is the absolute norm of non-zero integral ideals.

The Dedekind zeta function can be extended meromorphically to the whole complex plane with a simple pole at
Re(s)=1
. The residue of the Dedekind zeta function at
s=1
is given by

\frac{2^{r_{1}}(2\pi)^{r_{2}}hR}{\omega\sqrt{|D_{\mathbb{K}}|}}
,

where
r_{1}
is the total number of real embeddings and
r_{2}
is the total number of complex embeddings,
h
is the class number,
R
denotes the regulator,
\omega
is the number of units in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
and
|D_{\mathbb{K}}|
is the absolute value of the discriminant of
\mathbb{K}
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
a_{\mathbb{K}}(n)
be the number of integral ideals of norm
n
in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
, then Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(s)
can be written as

\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(s)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\mathbb{K}}(n)}{n^{s}},~~~~~Re(s)>1.

As shown by Chandrasekharan and Good
[
2
]
, the coefficients
a_{\mathbb{K}}(n)
are multiplicative and satisfy the following upper bound

a_{\mathbb{K}}(n)\leq d(n)^{[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{Q}]}\ll n^{\epsilon}
,

where
d(n)
is the divisor function. As
a_{\mathbb{K}}(n)
is multiplicative, we have

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(s)=\prod\limits_{p}\Big(1+\frac{a_{\mathbb{K}}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{a_{\mathbb{K}}(p^{2})}{p^{2s}}+\dots+\frac{a_{\mathbb{K}}(p^{\ell})}{p^{\ell s}}+\dots\Big)
,

for
Re(s)>1
.

In
1949
, Landau
[
10
]
, in
1963
Chandrasekharan and Narasimhan
[
3
]
, and in
1983

[
2
]
considered first, second and
\ell^{th}
-moments
(\ell\geq 2)
of
a_{\mathbb{K}}(n)
’s respectively and obtained asymptotic formulae.

For a cubic non-normal extension
\mathbb{K}
over
\mathbb{Q}
, given by an irreducible polynomial
x^{3}+ax^{2}+bx+c\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
such that the discriminant
D_{\mathbb{K}}<0
,
recently Naveen and Prashant
[
14
]
studied the Problem for

-order moments of coefficients of the Dedekind zeta function over certain sequence of natural numbers and obtained asymptotic formulae, for a cubic non-normal extension over

.

In this paper, we study the second order moments of coefficients of Dedekind zeta function over certain sequence of natural numbers. More precisely, we would like to investigate the asymptotic behaviour of
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=\sum_{i=1}^{8}n_{i}^{2}\leq x\\
(n_{1},\dots,n_{8})\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}\end{subarray}}a_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}(n)

Indeed we prove the following:

, where

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=\sum_{i=1}^{8}n_{i}^{2}\leq x\\

\displaystyle=Cx^{4}P_{1}(\log{x})+O(x^{\frac{198}{53}+\epsilon}),

where

Preliminaries and Some Lemmas

Let
k
be a positive even integer and
H_{k}(SL(2,\mathbb{Z}))
be the set of all normalized Hecke eigenforms of weight
k
for the full modular group
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
. For a cusp form
f\in H_{k}(SL(2,\mathbb{Z}))
, let
\lambda_{f}(n)
be the normalized
n^{th}
coefficients of Fourier expansion of
f
at infinity, i.e,

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}e^{2\pi inz},~~~~\text{for all}~z\in{\mathbb{H}},

where
\mathbb{H}
be the Poincaré upper Half-plane. These Fourier coefficients
\lambda_{f}(n)
are the Hecke eigenvalues of
f
and
\lambda_{f}(n)\in\mathbb{R}

\forall~~n.
The
\lambda_{f}(n)
satisfies the following relation. For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{d|gcd(m,n)}\lambda_{f}\Big(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\Big).

Thus
\lambda_{f}(n)
is a multiplicative function.

In 1974, P. Deligne
[
4
]
proved that for any prime
p
, there exist complex numbers
\alpha_{f}(p),\,\beta_{f}(p)
such that

\displaystyle\lambda_{f}(p)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{f}(p)+\beta_{f}(p),

\displaystyle|\alpha_{f}(p)|

\displaystyle=|\beta_{f}(p)|=\alpha_{f}(p)\beta_{f}(p)=1.

Given any Dirichlet’s character
\chi
of Modulo
N
, we have the Dirichlet’s
L
-function, namely

L(s,\chi)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{s}}~~~\text{for}~Re(s)>1
,

and

\displaystyle L(s,sym^{j}f)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}\Big(\prod\limits_{i=0}^{j}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha^{j-i}_{f}(p)\beta^{i}_{f}(p)}{p^{s}}\Big)\Big)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{sym^{j}f}(n)}{n^{s}}~~~\text{for}~Re(s)>1.

Note that
\lambda_{sym^{j}f}(n)
is also real valued multiplicative function. We also have the Deligne’s bound
\lambda_{f}(n)\leq d(n)\leq n^{\epsilon}
,
d(n)
is the divisor function.

We note that the
L
-function associated to
\lambda_{f}(n)
is defined as

\displaystyle L(s,f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}},

for
Re(s)>1,
where
\lambda_{f}(n)
is the eigenvalue of Hecke operators

and the symmetric square

-function is defined as

\displaystyle L(s,sym^{2}f)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{sym^{2}f}(n)}{n^{s}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha^{2}(p)}{p^{s}}\Big)^{-1}\Big(1-\frac{\beta^{2}(p)}{p^{s}}\Big)^{-1}\Big(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\Big)^{-1},

for
Re(s)>1,
where
\lambda_{sym^{2}f}(n)
is multiplicative.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
g(n)=(-1)^{n}\sum_{d|n}(-1)^{d}d^{3}
. Then
g(n)
is a multiplicative arithmetic function.

Proof.

To prove the lemma, we show that for any two distinct primes
p,\,q
and for
\ell,m\in\mathbb{N}

g(p^{\ell}q^{m})=g(p^{\ell})g(q^{m})
.

Case(i)
In this case we take
p=2
and
q
is odd. By the definition of
g(n)
,

\displaystyle g(2^{\ell})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{2^{\ell}}\{-1+2^{3}+2^{6}+\dots+2^{3\ell}\}

\displaystyle=\{-1+2^{3}+2^{6}+\dots+2^{3\ell}\},

and

\displaystyle g(q^{m})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{q^{m}}\{-1-q^{3}-\dots-q^{3m}\}

\displaystyle=\{1+q^{3}+\dots+q^{3m}\}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle g(2^{\ell})g(q^{m})

\displaystyle=\{-1+2^{3}+2^{6}+\dots+2^{3\ell}\}\{1+q^{3}+\dots+q^{3m}\}

\displaystyle=-\{1+q^{3}+\dots+q^{3m}\}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq\ell\\
0\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}2^{3i}q^{3j}

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m}q^{3j}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq\ell\\
0\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}2^{3i}q^{3j}.

Now take,

\displaystyle g(2^{\ell}q^{m})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{2^{\ell}q^{m}}\big\{-1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}2^{3i}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}q^{3j}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq\ell\\
1\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}2^{3i}q^{3j}\big\}

\displaystyle=-\big\{1+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}q^{3j}\big\}+\big\{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}2^{3i}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq\ell\\
1\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}2^{3i}q^{3j}\big\}

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m}q^{3j}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq\ell\\
0\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}2^{3i}q^{3j}.

Therefore,
g(2^{\ell}q^{m})=g(2^{\ell})g(q^{m})
.

Case (ii)
Suppose both
p,\,q
are odd primes.

We have,

\displaystyle g(p^{\ell})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p^{\ell}}\{-1-p^{3}-p^{6}\dots-p^{3\ell}\}

\displaystyle=1+p^{3}+p^{6}+\dots+p^{3\ell},

and

\displaystyle g(p^{m})

\displaystyle=1+q^{3}+q^{6}+\dots+q^{3m}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle g(p^{\ell})g(p^{m})

\displaystyle=\{1+p^{3}+p^{6}+\dots+p^{3\ell}\}\{1+q^{3}+q^{6}+\dots+q^{3m}\}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq\ell\\
0\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}p^{3i}q^{3j}.

Now,

\displaystyle g(p^{\ell}q^{m})=(-1)^{p^{\ell}q^{m}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq\ell\\
0\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{p^{3i}q^{3j}}p^{3i}q^{3j}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq\ell\\
0\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}p^{3i}q^{3j}.

Therefore,

g(p^{\ell}q^{m})=g(p^{\ell})g(q^{m})
.

Hence the Lemma.

Lemma 2.2

The
L

associated with

\displaystyle F(s):=L(s,a_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}(n)r_{8}(n))

\displaystyle F(s)

\displaystyle=16\frac{B_{2}(s)}{A_{2}(s)}\zeta^{2}(s-3)L^{2}(s-3,f)L(s-3,sym^{2}f)\zeta^{2}(s)L^{2}(s,f)L(s,sym^{2}f)B(s).

\displaystyle=1+\frac{a_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}(p)g(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{a_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}(p^{2})g(p^{2})}{p^{2s}}+\cdots

\displaystyle=1+\frac{a_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}(2)(1+2^{3})}{2^{s}}+\frac{a_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}(2^{2})(1+2^{3}+2^{6})}{2^{2s}}+\cdots,

\displaystyle B_{2}(s)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}(2^{n})}{(\sqrt{2})^{n}}

\displaystyle<1+\frac{8}{7}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}(2^{n}).2.4!}{(n\log{2})^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{8}{7}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(n)^{2}.2.4!}{n^{4}(\log{2})^{4}}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{2^{4}.4!}{7(\log 2)^{4}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{4}.4!}{7(\log 2)^{4}}\Big(1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big).

Hence
1+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}(2^{n})}{(\sqrt{2})^{n}}
is a convergent series, and hence
A_{2}(\frac{7}{2})
converges.

As,
\frac{a_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}(2^{n})(2^{3n}+2^{3(n-1)}+\dots+2^{3}-1)}{2^{\frac{7}{2}n}}\leq\frac{a_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}(2^{n})(2^{3n}+2^{3(n-1)}+\dots+2^{3}+1)}{2^{\frac{7}{2}n}}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\frac{a_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}(2^{n})(2^{3n}+2^{3(n-1)}+\dots+2^{3}-1)}{2^{\frac{7}{2}n}}>0
, and hence
B_{2}(\frac{7}{2})
also converges to real number
>0
. Therefore by the Theorem
11.4

[
1
]
,
0<|B_{2}(s)|<|A_{2}(s)|
for
Re(s)>\frac{7}{2}
and hence
0<\big|\frac{B_{2}(s)}{A_{2}(s)}\big|\leq 1
for
Re(s)>\frac{7}{2}
.
This implies that,
\frac{B_{2}(s)}{A_{2}(s)}G(s)\neq 0
, and is absolutely convergent for
Re(s)>\frac{7}{2}
.

The Dirichlet’s series
F(s)
has a pole of order

s=4
and converges absolutely for
Re(s)>4
. It does not vanish on the line

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\

(n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{8})\in\mathbb{Z}^{8}\end{subarray}}a_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{4+\epsilon-iT}^{4+\epsilon+iT}F(s)\frac{x^{s}}{s}\,ds+O\Big(\frac{x^{4+\epsilon}}{T}\Big).

By moving the line of integration to

. We consider the rectangle
R
with the vertices
\frac{7}{2}+\epsilon\pm iT
and
4+\epsilon\pm iT
. In the rectangle
R
,
F(s)\frac{x^{s}}{s}
has a pole of multiplicity
2
at
s=4
coming from the factor
\zeta^{2}(s-3)
. Thus, by using Cauchy’s residue theorem on
R
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{4+\epsilon-iT}^{4+\epsilon+iT}F(s)\frac{x^{s}}{s}\,ds

\displaystyle=M(x)+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\Big\{-\int_{4+\epsilon+iT}^{\frac{7}{2}+\epsilon+iT}-\int_{\frac{7}{2}+\epsilon+iT}^{\frac{7}{2}+\epsilon-iT}

at

.

Let

to

\displaystyle|C_{H}|

\displaystyle\ll\Bigg|\int_{\frac{7}{2}+\epsilon+iT}^{4+\epsilon+iT}\,F(s)\frac{x^{s}}{s}\,ds\Bigg|

\displaystyle\ll\int_{\frac{7}{2}+\epsilon+iT}^{4+\epsilon+iT}|\zeta(s-3)|^{2}|L(s-3,f)|^{2}|L(s-3,sym^{2}f)|\Big|\frac{A_{2}(s)}{B_{2}(s)}\Big||G(s)|\frac{|x^{s}|}{|s|}\,ds.

2.3
,
2.5
and

\displaystyle|C_{H}|

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{3}}{T}\,T^{2\epsilon}\int_{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}^{1+\epsilon}T^{\frac{4}{3}(1-\sigma)+\epsilon}\,T^{\frac{6}{5}(1-\sigma)+\epsilon}\,x^{\sigma}\,d\sigma
