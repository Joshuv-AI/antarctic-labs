1. {Interview with Bishop Roberto Joaquin Ramos by reporter at radio
station--live]

2. [Text] [Reporter] Welcome, Mr Bishop.

3. [Ramos] Thank you. I want to appeal to the Salvadoran people for
peace so that we may all struggle to build a community where [words
indistinct] values.

4. It is regrettable that the dialogue process has come to a halt
and that at a moment when the Germans are celebrating their unity, we
are initiating an armed struggle.

5. I especially want to express my regret at the assassination of
the Jesuit priests. This was a savage crime without precedent in El
Salvador’s history. [Words indistinct] to worsen the country’s
situation and to exacerbate passions to make peace increasingly
difficult.

6. I hope that my brothers, all the Salvadorans who believe in
Christ, may learn to value life as a precious gift from God, may
learn to respect life, to respect other people’s opinions, and to
love others in spite of differences.

7. I also hope that this painful situation we have endured for the
past 10 years will make us more aware of our duties as citizens and
Christians. We have the obligation to build a more just society,
more deserving of the name the nation has carried since the days of
the colony: El Salvador.

9. [Reporter] Your statement comforts us at this difficult moment.
Do you have any recommendation for the Salvadoran people as to what
to do at this time?

10. [Ramos] I recommend to the Salvadoran people to remain calm, and
especially to pray. [Words indistinct] the hearts of peace through
love for God and the faith we may have.

11. [Reporter] Do you have any recommendation for the rebels, for
the FMLN specifically?

12. [Ramos] I would ask them to reestablish the dialogue that has
been broken. It has been confirmed, since the days when Cain killed
his brother Abel, that violence is not the solution to injustice.
Violence generates more violence, and, as civilized people, we should
resolve our differences through negotiations and dialogue.
