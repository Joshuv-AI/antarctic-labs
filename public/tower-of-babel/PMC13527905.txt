Exogenous melatonin mitigates cadmium stress on cotton seed germination: physiological, biochemical, and transcriptomic insights
Authors: Jian Wang, Bin Lu, Xinying Ma, Shasha Liu, Jialin Li, Ke Zhang, Hongchun Sun, Lingxiao Zhu, Yongjiang Zhang, Liantao Liu, Zhiying Bai, Cundong Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCadmium (Cd) is a prevalent and toxic heavy metal widely present in soils. Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum L.), which readily accumulates Cd, exhibits inhibited seed germination under Cd stress. Melatonin (MT), a potent indoleamine antioxidant, has been demonstrated to facilitate seed germination under adverse conditions, but its regulatory mechanisms in cotton under Cd stress remain unclear. In this study, cotton seeds of the cultivar ‘Nongda 601’ were used to investigate the effects of exogenous MT on germination characteristics, antioxidant enzyme activities, and hormone levels under Cd stress. Experimental treatments included a control (CK), MT treatment, Cd treatment, and a combined Cd + MT treatment (CM). Transcriptome sequencing was further conducted to elucidate the underlying mechanisms.ResultsCd stress significantly inhibited cotton seed germination, reduced amylase activity, and increased starch accumulation. It also decreased antioxidant enzyme activities while increasing reactive oxygen species (ROS) and malondialdehyde (MDA) contents. In addition, Cd stress increased IAA, ABA, and endogenous MT contents, while decreasing GA3 and ZR levels. Exogenous MT significantly enhanced amylase and antioxidant enzyme activities, decreased ROS, MDA, and starch contents, and modulated hormone levels by increasing IAA, GA3, ZR, and endogenous MT, while decreasing ABA content. Transcriptome analysis indicated that MT upregulated the expression of antioxidant-related genes under Cd stress, including SODA, FSD, CAT2, and pod. Additionally, exogenous MT alleviated Cd toxicity by regulating hormone signal transduction and the phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathway.ConclusionsExogenous MT alleviates Cd-induced inhibition of cotton seed germination by enhancing antioxidant capacity, promoting starch mobilization, and rebalancing hormone signaling, while modulating phenylpropanoid metabolism. These findings provide a theoretical foundation for improving cotton cultivation in Cd-contaminated soils.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12870-026-09306-y.

Introduction

Cadmium (Cd) is a highly toxic heavy metal contaminant that markedly inhibits plant growth [1]. In recent years, Cd contamination in farmland has intensified due to industrial mining and the excessive use of fertilizers and pesticides [2]. Notably, the Cd concentration in the Hebei Plain has reached 1.17 μg·g⁻1 and pollution levels continue to rise [3]. Cd exhibits high mobility in soil, facilitating plant uptake and subsequent bioaccumulation through the food chain, thereby posing significant risks to human health [1]. Cd stress adversely affects seed germination and plant growth. It inhibits amylase activity, reduces starch hydrolysis capacity, and limits nutrient availability required for embryo development, thereby suppressing seed germination [4]. Additionally, Cd stress induces membrane lipid peroxidation, disrupts oxidative homeostasis, increases reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels, and alters plant hormone balance [5].

Melatonin (MT) is a broad-spectrum and highly effective antioxidant that alleviates oxidative damage, osmotic stress, and hormonal imbalance induced by adverse conditions, thereby enhancing cellular stress tolerance [6]. Studies have shown that under Cd stress, superoxide dismutase (SOD) and catalase (CAT) activities decrease in tomato, whereas O2−, H2O2, and malondialdehyde (MDA) contents increase; MT application reverses these effects by enhancing antioxidant enzyme activities and reducing ROS and MDA levels [7]. In wheat seeds, stress inhibits amylase activity and leads to starch accumulation, whereas MT enhances amylase activity and decreases starch content [8]. Salt stress elevates the levels of IAA and ABA in cotton roots, whereas the application of MT increases IAA and decreases ABA levels [9].

Transcriptome sequencing, based on high-throughput RNA profiling, has been widely used to investigate plant responses to abiotic stress [10]. KEGG enrichment analysis showed that differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in Zoysia japonica under Cd stress were primarily enriched in carbon fixation, starch and sucrose metabolism, and pyruvate metabolism [11]. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) showed that DEGs in millet under Cd stress were significantly enriched in glutathione metabolism, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, and metal ion transport, indicating that plants regulate genes associated with cell wall modification and vacuolar sequestration to limit Cd2⁺ transport and enhance tolerance [12]. MT not only directly scavenges ROS but also enhances plant antioxidant capacity by upregulating the transcription and activity of antioxidant enzymes [13]. For example, MT improves antioxidant capacity in rapeseed under Cd stress by upregulating BnCOMT expression [14]. MT reduces Cd transport to cotton leaves by downregulating Cd2⁺ transporter gene expression, while also increasing antioxidant enzyme activities and maintaining ROS homeostasis. KEGG analysis further revealed that under Cd stress, MT application significantly enriched pathways related to zeatin biosynthesis and fructose and mannose metabolism in cotton [15].

Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum L.) is an important economic crop of the Malvaceae family, which undergoes a critical germination stage that is highly sensitive to environmental stress. Notably, cotton exhibits a strong capacity for Cd accumulation, and its seed germination stage is particularly vulnerable to Cd exposure [16]. Severe Cd contamination has been reported in cotton-growing regions of the Hebei Plain, resulting in inhibited germination, poor seedling establishment, and reduced yield [3]. As a plant growth regulator and antioxidant, exogenous MT effectively alleviates the inhibitory effects of salt [17] and drought stress [18] on cotton seed germination. However, the physiological responses and metabolic regulatory mechanisms of MT in cotton seed germination under Cd stress remain largely unclear. Therefore, in this study, cotton seeds of ‘Nongda 601’ were used to investigate the effects of exogenous MT on germination characteristics, antioxidant enzyme activities, and hormone levels under Cd stress. Furthermore, transcriptome sequencing was performed to elucidate the underlying regulatory pathways, thereby providing a theoretical support for improving cotton cultivation in Cd-contaminated soils.

Materials and methods

Experimental materials and treatments

Cotton seeds of the cultivar ‘Nongda 601’ were obtained from the Guoxin Rural Technical Service Association (Hejian City, Hebei Province, China). MT was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA) and prepared as a stock solution at a concentration of 2 × 105 μmol L⁻1 in 95% ethanol, and stored at −20°C in darkness. Cd solutions of various concentrations were prepared by dissolving CdCl2·2.5H2O (Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd., China) in ultrapure water.

Cotton seeds were surface-sterilized using 75% ethanol (v/v) for 15 min, rinsed with distilled water to remove residual ethanol, and then air-dried. Based on preliminary trials, 100 μmol L⁻1 CdCl₂ was selected as the Cd stress concentration, and 50 μmol L⁻1 MT was determined as the optimal concentration for alleviating Cd stress [19]. Four treatments were established: (1) Control (CK): seeds soaked in distilled water and supplemented with water during cultivation; (2) MT: seeds soaked in 50 μmol L⁻1 MT and supplemented with MT solution during cultivation; (3) Cd: seeds soaked in distilled water and supplemented with 100 μmol L⁻1 CdCl₂·2.5H₂O solution during cultivation; (4) Cd plus MT (CM): seeds soaked in 50 μmol L⁻1 MT and supplemented with a mixed solution of 100 μmol L⁻1 CdCl₂·2.5H₂O and 50 μmol L⁻1 MT during cultivation. Seeds were incubated in a constant-temperature chamber at 25 °C in darkness for 7 days. Corresponding solutions (8 mL) were applied daily to maintain treatment conditions. Each treatment included three replicates, with 30 seeds per replicate.

Evaluation of germination indices

Seed germination was defined as radicle protrusion through the seed coat to half the seed length [20]. For each treatment, 30 seeds were used. Germination potential was recorded on day 3, and germination rate was determined on day 7.

Measurements of amylase activity and starch content

Samples were collected 72 h after germination initiation. Whole cotton seeds were used, with three replicates per treatment (0.1 g per replicate). Samples were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80°C. Amylase activity and starch content were measured using commercial assay kits from Nanjing Jiancheng Institute of Biological Engineering (Nanjing, China). Amylase activity was determined using the 3,5-dinitrosalicylic acid method, and starch content was measured using the anthrone colorimetric method.

Measurements of antioxidant enzyme activities, reactive oxygen species (ROS), and MDA contents

On day 7 of germination, whole cotton seeds were collected for physiological and biochemical analyses. For each treatment, three replicates (0.2 g each) were rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen and subsequently stored at −80°C. Antioxidant enzyme activities and ROS contents were measured using assay kits from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, China) and Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). Superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity was determined by the WST-1 method, peroxidase (POD) activity by the guaiacol method, and catalase (CAT) activity by the ammonium molybdate method. O2− and H2O2 contents were measured spectrophotometrically, and MDA content was determined using the thiobarbituric acid method.

Measurements of hormone and MT contents

Samples were collected on day 7 of germination, with three replicates per treatment (0.5 g each). Samples were ground into a fine powder in liquid nitrogen, extracted with extraction buffer, and subjected to multiple extraction steps. Residual methanol was removed using a nitrogen evaporator. Indole-3-acetic acid (IAA), gibberellin (GA3), abscisic acid (ABA), zeatin riboside (ZR), and endogenous MT contents were determined using an Agilent 1260 high-performance liquid chromatography system equipped with an Eclipse XDB-C18 column (250 mm × 4.6 mm, 5 μm; Agilent Technologies, CA, USA).

Transcriptome analysis

Transcriptome sequencing was conducted by Gene Denovo Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, China). On day 7 of germination, radicles were collected, rinsed thoroughly with distilled water, and homogenized after being cut into small pieces. For each treatment, three biological replicates (0.5 g each) were prepared. Samples were wrapped in aluminum foil, rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80°C.

Detection of transcript levels by quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Coding DNA sequences (CDS) of target genes were retrieved from NCBI, and primers were designed and synthesized by Shanghai Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The Taq Pro Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix kit was used for qRT-PCR analysis. The PCR program was as follows: 95°C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s and 60 °C for 30 s. The cotton GhActin gene was used as the internal reference, and relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Primer sequences are provided in Table S1.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis and visualization were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics 27.0 (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism 10 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, USA). Differences among treatments were evaluated by one-way ANOVA followed by Duncan’s multiple range test (p < 0.05). Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) of three biological replicates. Images were prepared using Adobe Illustrator 2026 (Adobe, San Diego, CA, USA).

Antioxidant enzyme activities and O₂⁻, H₂O₂, MDA contents

Cd stress and MT significantly affected SOD, POD, and CAT activities in cotton seeds (Fig. 1D − F). Compared to CK, MT treatment increased SOD, POD, and CAT activities by 20.82%, 5.41%, and 6.35%, respectively, with significant differences observed for SOD and CAT but not for POD. Cd treatment significantly reduced SOD, POD, and CAT activities by 16.56%, 14.74%, and 38.25%, respectively, compared to CK. Compared to Cd treatment, CM treatment significantly increased SOD, POD, and CAT activities by 27.70%, 20.33%, and 32.57%, respectively. These results showed that Cd stress reduced antioxidant enzyme activities, whereas MT treatment enhanced SOD, POD, and CAT activities under Cd stress.

Cd stress and MT also significantly affected O₂⁻, H₂O₂, and MDA contents in cotton seeds (Fig. 1G − I). Compared to CK, MT treatment reduced O₂⁻, H₂O₂, and MDA contents by 44.44%, 8.27%, and 1.89%, respectively, with a significant reduction observed only for O₂⁻. Cd treatment significantly increased O₂⁻, H₂O₂, and MDA contents by 200.00%, 102.88%, and 58.04%, respectively, compared to CK. Compared to Cd treatment, CM treatment significantly reduced O₂⁻, H₂O₂, and MDA contents by 51.85%, 41.09%, and 28.22%, respectively. These results suggest that Cd stress induces O₂⁻ and H₂O₂ accumulation, whereas MT reduces ROS accumulation and MDA content, alleviating membrane peroxidation during seed germination.

IAA, GA₃, ABA, ZR, and MT contents

Cd stress and MT significantly affected hormone levels in cotton seeds. Compared to CK, MT treatment significantly increased IAA content by 19.67% and reduced ABA content by 30.06%. Cd treatment increased IAA and ABA contents by 64.49% and 9.80%, respectively, and decreased GA₃ and ZR contents by 34.66% and 20.00%, respectively, compared to CK. Compared to Cd treatment, CM treatment significantly increased IAA, GA3, and ZR contents by 6.55%, 23.47%, and 19.79%, respectively, and decreased ABA content by 5.05% (Fig. 1J − M). MT treatment significantly increased endogenous MT content by 591.04% compared to CK, Cd treatment increased it by 54.62%, and CM treatment further increased it by 339.81% compared to Cd treatment (Fig. 1N). These results indicate that Cd increases IAA, ABA, and MT contents while decreasing GA3 and ZR contents, whereas exogenous MT enhances IAA, GA3, ZR, and endogenous MT contents, reduces ABA content, suggesting that MT treatment modulates hormone homeostasis during seed germination.

Regulation of cotton seed transcriptome by exogenous MT under Cd stress

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) analysis

RNA-seq data from CK vs. MT, CK vs. Cd, Cd vs. CM, and MT vs. CM comparisons were analyzed, and DEGs were identified using a threshold of |Log₂FC|≥ 1 and FDR < 0.05. The results (Fig. 2A) showed 284 DEGs in CK vs. MT (56 upregulated, 228 downregulated), 1915 DEGs in CK vs. Cd (999 upregulated, 916 downregulated), 259 DEGs in Cd vs. CM (171 upregulated, 88 downregulated), and 1212 DEGs in MT vs. CM (803 upregulated, 409 downregulated). As shown in Fig. 2B, a total of 2838 genes were significantly differentially expressed across the four comparisons, including 11 common DEGs. Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis

Gene Ontology enrichment analysis was performed on DEGs using a threshold of FDR < 0.05 (Fig. S1A-S1D). In CK vs. Cd (Fig. S1B), DEGs were predominantly enriched in oxidation–reduction processes, oxidoreductase activity, peroxidase activity, antioxidant activity, and response to oxidative stress, indicating that Cd exposure strongly disturbed cellular redox homeostasis and activated antioxidant defense systems during seed germination. In Cd vs. CM (Fig. S1C), enrichment remained prominent in oxidoreductase-related categories but showed a clearer association with carbohydrate metabolic processes. This shift suggests that MT treatment not only modulates redox responses but may also contribute to the recovery of energy metabolism, which is essential for germination. These results are consistent with the observed increases in antioxidant enzyme activities and starch mobilization under MT treatment.

Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis

KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of the top 20 pathways was conducted and visualized as bubble plots (Fig. S1E − S1H). In CK vs. Cd (Fig. S1F), DEGs were mainly enriched in phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, biosynthesis of secondary metabolites, and zeatin biosynthesis, suggesting that Cd stress markedly affected secondary metabolism and hormone-related pathways. In Cd vs. CM (Fig. S1G), phenylpropanoid and secondary metabolite biosynthesis remained significantly enriched, while plant hormone signal transduction pathways were additionally activated. This indicates that MT treatment may alleviate Cd-induced inhibition of seed germination by coordinately regulating hormone signaling and stress-related metabolic pathways.

Analysis of redox-related DEGs

A total of 80 redox-related DEGs were identified, including 13 SOD, 4 CAT, and 63 POD genes (Fig. 3). Compared to CK, Cd treatment downregulated SOD and CAT genes and most POD genes, except for GPX and PNC1, which were upregulated. Compared to Cd treatment, CM treatment upregulated SOD genes (e.g., SODA, FSD), CAT gene (CAT2), and POD genes (e.g., pod). These results suggest that MT enhances antioxidant enzyme activities under Cd stress by regulating the expression of SOD, POD, and CAT genes, thereby promoting seed germination under stress conditions.Fig. 3Differential genes related to SOD (A), CAT (B), and POD (C) under different treatments

Phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathway analysis

A total of 49 genes were identified in the phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathway, which were involved in the conversion to cinnamic acid, p-coumaric acid, H-lignin, and guaiacyl lignin (Fig. 4). 4-Coumarate-CoA ligase (4CL) catalyzes an early step in flavonoid biosynthesis in the phenylpropanoid pathway and is also a key enzyme in lignin biosynthesis. Cd treatment upregulated 4CL genes, whereas CM treatment downregulated them. Cinnamyl alcohol dehydrogenase (CAD) catalyzes the final step in lignin monomer formation. Cd treatment downregulated CAD genes, whereas CM treatment upregulated them. POD facilitates the oxidative polymerization of hydroxycinnamyl alcohols to form lignin. Cd treatment downregulated most POD genes, whereas CM treatment upregulated POD genes (e.g., Gh_A12G008100), thereby promoting lignin biosynthesis. These results indicate that Cd stress downregulates genes involved in lignin synthesis, impairing cell wall formation, whereas MT promotes lignin synthesis and mitigates Cd-induced damage.Fig. 4Analysis of phenylpropanoid biosynthetic metabolic pathways

Plant hormone synthesis pathway analysis

The signal transduction pathways of IAA, GA3, ABA, and ZR were analyzed, identifying 29, 13, 25, and 6 genes, respectively (Fig. 5). AUX/IAA, an early auxin-responsive gene family, interacts with auxin response factors (ARFs) to regulate auxin signaling. Cd treatment downregulated AUX/IAA, while CM treatment upregulated it. GH3 and SAUR, also early auxin-responsive genes, were downregulated by Cd treatment and upregulated by CM treatment. A-ARR, a negative regulator in cytokinin signaling, included four related genes (e.g., Gh_D04G158800) that were downregulated by Cd treatment, with two (Gh_D05G236900, Gh_D10G209200) upregulated by CM treatment. GA3 is a key regulatory hormone for plant growth and development, and GID1 relieves DELLA-mediated repression of GA signaling. Cd treatment downregulated GID genes (e.g., Gh_A08G240700), while CM treatment upregulated some of these genes (e.g., Gh_A13G196200). PYR/PYL encodes ABA receptors. Cd treatment downregulated PYR/PYL genes (e.g., Gh_A01G257600), while CM treatment upregulated some of these genes (e.g., Gh_A01G257600) and downregulated PP2C genes (e.g., Gh_A09G201700), thereby alleviating the inhibitory effect of ABA on cotton seed germination. SnRK2 overexpression enhances stress tolerance, and CM treatment increased SnRK2 expression compared to Cd treatment. Discussion

Exogenous MT promotes cotton seed germination under Cd stress

Cd pollution adversely affects seed germination and inhibits plant growth and development [4, 5]. Seed germination requires the coordinated integration of energy metabolism, redox balance, and hormone signaling, all of which are highly sensitive to Cd toxicity [16]. MT is a multifunctional small molecule with antioxidant properties that regulates plant signal transduction and enhances stress tolerance [21]. Previous studies reported that 10 μmol L⁻1 Cd significantly reduced wheat seed germination rate and germination index [22], while 150 μmol L⁻1 MT promoted tomato seed germination under 100 μmol L⁻1 Cd stress [23]. In this study, 100 μmol L⁻1 Cd significantly reduced cotton seed germination potential and rate, whereas the application of 50 μmol L⁻1 MT under Cd stress significantly increased these parameters, basically consistent with prior findings [23]. The improved germination observed under CM treatment suggests that MT partially restores physiological processes disrupted by Cd stress, thereby promoting seed germination.

Exogenous MT enhances starch hydrolysis in cotton seeds under Cd stress

Starch is the main storage carbohydrate, and α-amylase and β-amylase are key rate-limiting enzymes in starch catabolism [8]. Heavy metal stress inhibits amylase activity and increases starch content in reed [24] and rice [25] seeds, while MT restores amylase activity, thereby promoting carbon mobilization for germination. In this study, Cd treatment reduced amylase activity and increased starch content in cotton seeds, while CM treatment showed the opposite trend. These results suggest that Cd2⁺ may impair starch mobilization and energy supply during germination, whereas MT restores carbon mobilization through enhanced amylase activity [26]. Notably, starch mobilization during germination is tightly regulated by hormonal signals, particularly GA3, which induce amylase expression, whereas ABA exerts antagonistic effects [27]. The increase in GA3 and decrease in ABA under CM treatment further suggest that MT promotes starch hydrolysis through hormone-mediated regulation.

Exogenous MT enhances antioxidant capacity in cotton seeds under Cd stress

After Cd2⁺ enters plant cells, it disrupts membrane integrity, leading to excessive ROS production, membrane lipid peroxidation, and increased MDA content [5]. MT has been reported to alleviate oxidative damage under abiotic stress by regulating antioxidant defense systems [28]. Previous reports indicated that MT application increases SOD, POD, and CAT activities and reduces MDA accumulation under Cd stress [29, 30]. Consistently, in this study, Cd stress decreased antioxidant enzyme activities and increased O₂⁻, H₂O₂, and MDA contents, whereas MT treatment partially reversed these effects. Transcriptomic analysis further supported these responses. Most antioxidant-related genes were downregulated under Cd stress, whereas CM treatment upregulated several key genes, consistent with enhanced antioxidant enzyme activities and improved redox homeostasis. Importantly, ROS are not only toxic byproducts but also signaling molecules during seed germination [31]. Thus, MT likely maintains ROS homeostasis at a level sufficient for signaling while preventing excessive oxidative damage.

Exogenous MT regulates hormone balance in cotton seeds under Cd stress

Plant hormones play critical roles in seed germination. GA3 promotes dormancy release and germination, whereas ABA maintains dormancy and inhibits germination [32]. The interaction between GA3 and IAA facilitates seed coat rupture and radicle emergence [33]. Exogenous MT regulates hormone homeostasis by modulating the expression of hormone metabolism and signaling genes [6]. Cai et al. [34] reported that under Cd stress, GA3 decreased and ABA increased in tomato seeds, while MT application reversed these changes, thereby promoting germination. In this study, Cd stress increased IAA, ABA, and endogenous MT contents but decreased GA3 and ZR contents, indicating altered hormone homeostasis under Cd stress. Notably, increased IAA accumulation does not necessarily indicate enhanced growth. Heavy metal stress may impair auxin transport and signaling, resulting in abnormal IAA accumulation without corresponding physiological responses [35]. Thus, elevated IAA may act in coordination with ABA to inhibit seed germination. Given that ABA is a key inhibitor, its reduction by MT suggests a partial release of ABA-mediated repression, which may result from the modulation of ABA signaling components such as PYR/PYL and PP2C. Meanwhile, increased GA3 and IAA under MT treatment may further promote germination [36]. Therefore, MT likely enhances seed germination under Cd stress by suppressing ABA signaling and promoting growth-related pathways, highlighting potential crosstalk between hormone signaling and redox regulation.

Exogenous MT regulates metabolic pathways in cotton seed germination under Cd stress

Transcriptome analysis indicated that Cd stress primarily disrupted redox balance, carbohydrate metabolism, and secondary metabolism, while MT treatment partially alleviated these transcriptional changes. Similar regulatory patterns have been reported in other species, where MT modulates cell wall-related processes, carbohydrate metabolism, and phenylpropanoid pathways under stress conditions [37–40]. In this study, GO enrichment showed that Cd stress mainly affected antioxidant activity and carbohydrate metabolism, suggesting oxidative damage and impaired energy mobilization during germination. KEGG analysis further revealed that Cd stress significantly impacted phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, secondary metabolite biosynthesis, and hormone-related pathways, whereas MT treatment enhanced enrichment in phenylpropanoid biosynthesis and plant hormone signal transduction. These results suggest that MT alleviates Cd-induced inhibition by regulating redox balance, metabolism, and hormone signaling.

Notably, the phenylpropanoid pathway genes exhibited differential regulation. Under Cd stress, 4CL genes were upregulated, whereas most CAD and POD genes were downregulated. Unlike previous reports showing coordinated induction of lignin biosynthesis genes under stress [37, 38], the divergent pattern observed here may reflect stage-specific regulation during germination. Induction of 4CL genes may reflect increased synthesis of soluble phenolic compounds for antioxidant defense, whereas suppression of CAD and POD genes indicates reduced lignin formation. This pattern may indicate preferential synthesis of protective metabolites rather than structural lignin under stress [39, 40]. Following MT application, CAD and POD genes were upregulated, whereas 4CL expression was less pronounced, suggesting a rebalancing of phenylpropanoid metabolism. Such reprogramming may contribute to cell wall stabilization and radicle emergence during germination.

Analysis of hormone metabolic pathways showed that AUX/IAA, GH3, and SAUR genes were downregulated under Cd stress in kenaf and Arabidopsis, thereby inhibiting plant growth [35, 41]. Under salt stress, exogenous MT alleviates growth inhibition by regulating ARF expression, promoting GID1 expression, and repressing PP2C expression, thereby enhancing IAA and GA₃ signaling while suppressing ABA signaling [42]. Given the antagonistic roles of ABA and growth-promoting hormones such as GA3 and IAA during germination, these findings suggest that MT facilitates germination recovery under Cd stress through coordinated regulation of hormone signaling pathways.

Through WGCNA, we identified the brown module containing the key gene APR2. Its upregulation under Cd stress likely reflects enhanced sulfur assimilation for glutathione and phytochelatin synthesis. Notably, exogenous MT partially attenuated Cd-induced APR2 upregulation, suggesting reduced dependence on sulfur-mediated detoxification pathways. Considering the central role of sulfur metabolism in redox buffering, this finding implies that MT may alleviate the metabolic burden of detoxification by improving redox homeostasis [43]. However, as sulfur-containing metabolites were not quantified, this interpretation requires further experimental validation.

Although transcriptomic analysis revealed extensive hormonal and metabolic reprogramming, validation at the protein level remains necessary. Recent DIA proteomics studies in sesame under waterlogging identified key hub proteins involved in MT-mediated ROS regulation, indicating that post-translational regulation may modify transcriptomic outcomes [44]. Furthermore, the successful application of MT for yield improvement in sesame under field conditions [45] highlights its practical potential. Therefore, MT seed priming may represent a promising strategy for improving cotton establishment under Cd stress. However, optimization of application methods (e.g., seed treatment versus foliar spray) and evaluation across diverse genotypes are required for broader agronomic application.

Conclusion

Cd stress inhibited cotton seed germination. Exogenous MT enhanced amylase and antioxidant enzyme activities, while reducing starch, ROS, and MDA contents. The contents of IAA, GA3, endogenous MT, and ZR were increased, whereas ABA content was decreased, thereby partially alleviating the inhibitory effects of Cd stress on seed germination. Transcriptome analysis further indicated that exogenous MT regulates redox processes, plant hormone signal transduction, and phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, and modulates the expression of SOD (SODA, FSD), POD (pod), CAT (CAT2), and hormone-related (AUX/IAA, GH3, SAUR, GID1, SnRK2) genes.
