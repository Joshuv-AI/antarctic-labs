Fast and Practical Single-Exponential Algorithms for Branchwidth
Taiki Kaneda, Yasuaki Kobayashi, Hisao Tamaki

Abstract
In this paper, we present exact exponential algorithms for computing branchwidth that are fast both in theory and in practice. The running times of these algorithms are single-exponential in the number of vertices. Our basic algorithm is based on a conceptually simple recurrence on vertex sets and computes the branchwidth of an n n -vertex hypergraph in time 𝒪 ∗ ​ ( 4 n ) \mathcal{O}^{*}(4^{n}) . This is the first single-exponential time algorithm for hypergraphs. We have two algorithms tailored specifically for graphs. The first algorithm runs in time 𝒪 ⁡ ( 3.293 n ) \mathcal{O}(3.293^{n}) , improving upon the previously best-known running time of 𝒪 ⁡ ( 3.4652 n ) \mathcal{O}(3.4652^{n}) [Fomin-Mazoit-Todinca, DAM 2009]. Moreover, our computational experiment shows that it overwhelmingly outperforms state-of-the-art practical algorithms for computing branchwidth. The second algorithm is a candidate for a theoretical improvement: we conjecture that it runs in time 𝒪 ⁡ ( c n ) \mathcal{O}(c^{n}) for some constant c c that is smaller than 3.293. In practice, it performs significantly better on some instances that are hard for the first algorithm.

Introduction

The
branchwidth
of a graph is a well-studied concept introduced in the seminal Graph Minors project by Robertson and Seymour
[
25
]
and plays an important role in the proof of the graph minor theorem.
This graph parameter measures how close a graph is to a tree, and is known to be linearly related to
treewidth

[
25
]
, which serves a similar purpose in solving problems on graphs.
Since the branchwidth of a graph can be easily generalized to hypergraphs, matroids, and symmetric submodular functions, due to this versatility, its mathematical properties and algorithmic aspects have been extensively studied in the literature
[
11
,
14
,
18
,
21
,
25
]
.

Algorithms for computing the treewidth and branchwidth of a graph have been well studied in the context of exact algorithms.
The main reason for this is that, given a decomposition of small width for a given graph, numerous optimization problems on graphs can be solved efficiently
[
1
,
4
,
7
]
, with running time typically linear in the size of an input graph and exponential in these width parameters.
This implies that it is crucial to exactly compute the treewidth or branchwidth of a graph.
Given a graph
G
and an integer
k
, the problems of deciding whether the treewidth or branchwidth of
G
is at most
k
are known to be fixed-parameter tractable (FPT) parameterized by
k
, with the running time dependencies linear in the number of vertices
[
5
,
3
]
.
However, since the dependence on
k
in these running times is non-negligibly large, exact exponential-time algorithms are often preferred in practice.

For computing treewidth, Bouchitté and Todinca
[
6
]
proposed a dynamic programming algorithm for computing treewidth based on potential maximal cliques, which leads to a running time bound essentially depending on the number of potential maximal cliques in an input graph.
The current best running time bound is due to Fomin, Todinca, and Villanger
[
12
]
and is in
\mathcal{O}(1.7347^{n})
, where
n
is the number of vertices in the input graph.
Interestingly, the essential idea behind this algorithm can be leveraged to develop highly practical algorithms; specifically, a framework called positive-instance driven dynamic programming
[
27
]
, introduced by one of the current authors, successfully computes the treewidth of moderate-sized graphs.
Exact exponential-time algorithms are also known for branchwidth, including an
\mathcal{O}^{*}(2^{m})
-time algorithm
1
1

1

The notation
\mathcal{O}^{*}
hides a polynomial factor in the input size.
by Oum
[
24
]
and an
\mathcal{O}^{*}((2\sqrt{3})^{n})\subset\mathcal{O}(3.4652^{n})
-time algorithm by Fomin, Mazoit, and Todinca
[
10
]
, where
m
is the number of edges in the input graph.
However, these algorithms either compute branchwidth values across all edge subsets or involve highly complex procedures; consequently, a straightforward implementation of these methods does not appear to be practical.
In fact, the current state-of-the-art practical algorithms for branchwidth are Hicks’ algorithm
[
16
]
, which utilizes the notion of tangles, as well as the SAT-encoding approach proposed by Lodha, Ordyniak, and Szeider
[
23
]
.
Nevertheless, as reported in
[
23
]
, these existing approaches still cannot successfully compute the branchwidth of moderate-sized graphs on which Tamaki’s algorithm can compute the treewidth exactly within a practical time limit.

In this paper, we aim to take a step forward in the practical aspects of branchwidth computation.
We present practical algorithms for computing the branchwidth of a (hyper)graph.
Our first algorithm is based on a relatively simple recurrence for computing branchwidth and runs in
\mathcal{O}^{*}(4^{n})
time.
The basic idea of this algorithm is to evaluate a recurrence based on the (rooted) tree structure of an optimal branch-decomposition using dynamic programming, similar to the approach taken by Oum
[
24
]
.
However, a major improvement is that our running time is single-exponential with respect to
n
, the number of vertices in the input graph.
The technical key ingredient to achieving this improvement lies in showing a recurrence over vertex subsets rather than edge subsets.
While this approach shares an underlying concept with the algorithm for computing linearwidth given by Kobayashi and Nakahata
[
20
]
, proving its correctness for branch-decompositions requires non-trivial extensions.
It is worth noting that this is the first algorithm for computing the branchwidth of hypergraphs that runs in time single-exponential in
n
; the algorithm given by Fomin, Mazoit, and Todinca
[
10
]
seems to work only on graphs.

Theorem 1
.

The branchwidth of an
n
-vertex hypergraph can be computed in time
\mathcal{O}^{*}(4^{n})
.

Our second algorithm builds upon the recurrence of the first one to provide a faster algorithm specialized for graphs. This improves upon one of the best known theoretical running time bounds due to
[
10
]
, proving the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

The branchwidth of an
n
-vertex graph can be computed in time
\mathcal{O}(3.293^{n})
.

This improvement is achieved by building our recurrence on vertex subsets satisfying a certain connectivity condition, which we call
blocks
.
While this block-based algorithm improves the running time as stated in the theorem, the recurrence itself becomes more involved, and its evaluation becomes non-trivial.

We also give an alternative to the algorithm stated in
\cref
thm:main.
Based on some partial analysis, we conjecture
that it runs in time
\mathcal{O}(c^{n})

where
c
is a constant smaller than
the one in
\cref
thm:main.
In practice, it performs significantly better on instances
that are hard for the algorithm in

\cref
thm:main.

Related work.

Although computing the branchwidth of a graph is known to be NP-hard
[
26
]
even when restricted to several specific graph classes
[
19
]
, a highly notable result is that it can be computed in polynomial time for planar graphs
[
26
]
.
This is in contrast to computing treewidth for planar graphs, whose computational complexity has been a long-standing open problem.
Seymour and Thomas
[
26
]
showed that the branchwidth of a planar graph can be computed in time
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
.
Their algorithm takes time
\mathcal{O}(n^{4})
to construct the corresponding branch-decomposition, which was later improved to
\mathcal{O}(n^{3})
by Gu and Tamaki
[
15
]
.
The computation of branchwidth for planar graphs has also been extensively investigated from a practical perspective, enabling the exact computation of optimal branch-decompositions within a practical running time, even for large planar graphs
[
2
,
16
,
17
]
.

Preliminaries

Let
G
be a hypergraph.
The vertex set and edge set of
G
are denoted by
V(G)
and
E(G)
, respectively.
For
F\subseteq E(G)
, we define the set of vertices contained in some (hyper)edge of
F
as
V(F)=\bigcup_{e\in F}e
.
For
X\subseteq V(G)
, the
boundary
of
X
, denoted by
\partial_{G}(X)
, is the set of vertices in
X
that have an incident hyperedge
e
with
e\cap(V(G)\setminus X)\neq\emptyset
.
We can extend this definition for edge sets: For
F\subseteq E(G)
, the boundary of
F
, denoted by
\partial_{G}(F)
is defined to be
V(F)\cap V(E(G)\setminus F)
.
For
X\subseteq V(G)
, we define
E_{G}(X)=\{e\in E(G):e\subseteq X\}
, and the subgraph of
G
induced by
X
is denoted by
G[X]
, i.e.,
V(G[X])=X
and
E(G[X])=E_{G}(X)
.
We write
\mathtt{clos}(F)
to denote the set of edges in the subgraph induced by
V(F)
, that is,
\mathtt{clos}(F)=E(V(F))
for
F\subseteq E(G)
.
An edge set
F\subseteq E(G)
is
closed
if it satisfies
\mathtt{clos}(F)=F
.
In other words,
F
is closed if and only if there is a vertex set
X
such that
E(X)=F
.
If
G
is a graph, the sets of open and closed neighbors of
X\subseteq V(G)
are denoted by
N_{G}(X)
and
N_{G}[X]
, respectively.
When the hypergraph
G
is clear from the context, we may omit it from these notations.

Let
T
be a rooted binary tree.
The set of leaves in
T
is denoted by
L(T)
.
We particularly refer to the vertices of
T
as nodes to distinguish them from the vertices of
G
.
For a node
p\in V(T)
, the subtree of
T
rooted at
p
is denoted by
T_{p}
.
We denote by
T-T_{p}
the rooted tree obtained from
T
by deleting all nodes in
T_{p}
.
We say that
T
is
full
if all internal nodes have exactly two children.

A
branch-decomposition
of a hypergraph
G
is a pair consisting of a rooted full binary tree
T
and a bijection from the leaves of
T
to
E(G)
.
Let
\mathcal{T}=(T,\varphi)
be a branch-decomposition of
G
.
We may refer to the nodes and edges of
T
as the nodes and edges of
\mathcal{T}
as well.
For a subtree
T^{\prime}
of
T
, we write
\varphi(T^{\prime})
to denote the set of edges to which
\varphi
maps the leaves of
T
belonging to
T^{\prime}
.
For a node
p\in V(T)
, the
middle set
of
p
is defined as
\mathrm{mid}_{\mathcal{T}}(p)=V(\varphi(T_{p}))\cap V(\varphi(T-T_{p}))
, that is, the set of vertices in
G
, each of which has at least one incident edge in both
\varphi(T_{p})
and
\varphi(T-T_{p})
.
The
order
of a node
p
in
\mathcal{T}
is defined as
|\mathrm{mid}_{\mathcal{T}}(p)|
.
The
width
of
\mathcal{T}
is defined as the maximum order of a node in
\mathcal{T}
, and the
branchwidth
of
G
, denoted by
\mathrm{bw}(G)
, is the minimum integer
k
such that
G
has a branch-decomposition of width at most
k
.
A branch-decomposition can be defined for edge subsets: A
branch-decomposition
of
F\subseteq E(G)
is a pair
\mathcal{T}=(T,\varphi)
, where
T
is a rooted full binary tree and
\varphi
is a bijection from the leaves of
T
to
F
.
For each node
p
of
\mathcal{T}
, the
lower edge set
of
p
in
\mathcal{T}
,
denoted by
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}(p)
, is defined as
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}(p)=\varphi(T_{p})
;
the
upper edge set
of
p
in
\mathcal{T}
, denoted by
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{T}}(p)
,
is defined as
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{T}}(p)=\varphi(T-T_{p})\cup(E(G)\setminus F)
.
Note that these definitions are asymmetric in the sense that the upper edge set contains all the edges outside
F
.
The
middle set
of
p
in
\mathcal{T}
, denoted by
\mathrm{mid}_{\mathcal{T}}(p)
, is
defined as
\mathrm{mid}_{\mathcal{T}}(p)=V(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}(p))\cap V(\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{T}}(p))
.
The order of a node, the width of
\cal T
, and the branchwidth of
F
of
G
are defined analogously.
We denote the branchwidth of
F
of
G
by
\mathrm{bw}_{G}(F)
.
The notions of the upper set, the middle set, and the order of a node of a branch-decomposition
depend on the hypergraph
G
under consideration. We, however, do not make an explicit reference
to
G
when using those notions and related notation unless there is a fear of confusion.
It is easy to see that
\mathrm{bw}(G)=\mathrm{bw}(E(G))
.

We use a relaxed version of branch-decompositions
[
25
]
.
A pair
\mathcal{T}=(T,\varphi)
is called a
relaxed branch-decomposition
of
G
if
T
is a rooted (not necessarily full) binary tree and
\varphi
is a
surjection
from
L(T)
to
E(G)
, that is, there can be two distinct leaves
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
with
\varphi(\ell)=\varphi(\ell^{\prime})
.
The order of a node (and hence the width of
\mathcal{T}
) is defined in an analogous way.
For every graph
G
, the minimum width of a relaxed branch-decomposition of
G
is equal to
\mathrm{bw}(G)

[
25
]
.
In this paper, we simply refer to a relaxed branch-decomposition
\mathcal{T}=(T,\varphi)
as a branch decomposition;
\mathcal{T}
is
full
if
T
is a full binary tree and is
injective
if
\varphi
is an injection.
The following is a straightforward generalization of standard observations on relaxed branch-decompositions.

Proposition 3
.

Let
F
be an edge set of a hypergraph
G
of branchwidth
k
.
Then, there is an injective branch-decomposition of
F
of width
k
,
there is a full branch-decomposition of
F
of width
k
, and
there is a full and injective branch-decomposition of
F
of width
k
.

In the subsequent sections, we will assume that the input hypergraph is connected, has no vertex belonging to at most one hyperedge, and has no two hyperedges such that one contains the other.
It is easy to verify that the branchwidth of a hypergraph is equal to the maximum branchwidth of its connected components.
The other two assumptions are justified by the following two easy propositions.

Proposition 4
.

Let
G
be a hypergraph and let
v
be a vertex that belongs to at most one hyperedge in
G
.
Let
G^{\prime}
be the hypergraph with
V(G^{\prime})=V(G)\setminus\{v\}
and
E(G^{\prime})=\{e\setminus\{v\}:e\in E(G)\}
.
Then,
\mathrm{bw}(G)=\mathrm{bw}(G^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T}=(T,\varphi)
be an injective branch-decomposition of
G
and let
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=(T,\varphi^{\prime})
be a branch-decomposition of
G^{\prime}
, where
\varphi^{\prime}
maps each leaf
\ell
of
T
to the edge
\varphi(\ell)\setminus\{v\}
.
Clearly, the width of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is at most the width of
\mathcal{T}
.
From an injective branch-decomposition
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
, we can define a branch-decomposition
\mathcal{T}
of
G
in the opposite way.
As
v
does not appear in any middle set of a node in
\mathcal{T}
, the width of
\mathcal{T}
is at most the width of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
.
∎

Proposition 5
.

Let
G
be a hypergraph.
Suppose that
G
has a hyperedge
e
and a distinct hyperedge
e^{\prime}
with
e\subseteq e^{\prime}
.
Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
G
by deleting
e
.
Then,
\mathrm{bw}(G)=\max\{\mathrm{bw}(G^{\prime}),|e|\}
.

Proof.

Since
G^{\prime}
is a sub-hypergraph of
G
, we have
\mathrm{bw}(G^{\prime})\leq\mathrm{bw}(G)
.
Moreover, for every branch-decomposition
\mathcal{T}=(T,\varphi)
, the middle set of a leaf node
\ell
with
\varphi(\ell)=e
contains all vertices in
e
.
Thus, we have
|e|\leq\mathrm{bw}(G)
.

Conversely, let
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=(T^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
be an injective branch-decomposition of
G^{\prime}
.
Let
\ell^{\prime}
be a leaf of
T^{\prime}
such that
\varphi^{\prime}(\ell^{\prime})=e^{\prime}
and let
p
be the parent of
\ell^{\prime}
.
Then, we subdivide the edge between
p
and
\ell^{\prime}
with a new node
q
and add a new leaf
\ell
as a child of
q
.
We denote by
T
the tree obtained as above.
We also extend the mapping
\varphi^{\prime}
by mapping
\ell
to
e
and denote the extended mapping by
\varphi
.
Then,
\mathcal{T}=(T,\varphi)
is an injective branch-decomposition of
G
.
As
e\subseteq e^{\prime}
, the order of
\ell
equals
|e|
, the order of
q
equals the order of
p
, and the order of every other node in
\cal T
equals its order in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
.
Hence,
\mathrm{bw}(G)\leq\max\{\mathrm{bw}(G^{\prime}),|e|\}
.
∎

3
An
\mathcal{O}^{*}(4^{n})
-time algorithm for hypergraphs

In this section, we develop a branchwidth algorithm for hypergraphs that runs in
time
\mathcal{O}^{*}(4^{n})
.

We assume the hypergraph
G
for which we compute the branchwidth is connected,
every vertex of
G
belongs to at least two edges of
G
, and no edge of
G
is
strictly contained in another edge of
G
.
By
\cref
prop:private-vertex,prop:sperner, we can reduce the branchwidth computation of general hypergraphs to the branchwidth computation of hypergraphs
that satisfy these conditions.

The following observations are crucial in reasoning about the branchwidth of edge sets.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an injective branch-decomposition of an edge set of
G
and
let
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
be two nodes of
\mathcal{T}
that share a common parent.
Let
L_{i}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}(p_{i})
for
i=1,2
.
Then,
V(L_{1})\cap V(L_{2})=\partial(L_{1})\cap\partial(L_{2})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{T}
is injective,
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are disjoint from each other.
Let
v
be a vertex in
V(L_{1})\cap V(L_{2})
. Since
v
is incident
to an edge in
L_{1}
as well as to an edge in
L_{2}
,
it belongs to
\partial(L_{1})
and to
\partial(L_{2})
.
Therefore,
V(L_{1})\cap V(L_{2})
is contained in
\partial(L_{1})\cap\partial(L_{2})
.
The inclusion in the other direction is trivial as
\partial(L_{i})\subseteq V(L_{i})
for
i=1,2
.
∎

\mathcal{T}=(T,\varphi)
be a branch-decomposition of an edge set of a hypergraph
G
and
p
a node of
T
.
The
sub-branch-decomposition
of
\mathcal{T}
at

is the branch-decomposition

(T_{p},\varphi_{p})
where

is the subtree of

and

is the restriction of

.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an injective branch-decomposition of an edge set of
G
and
p
a node of
\mathcal{T}
. Then, the width of the sub-branch-decomposition
\mathcal{T}_{p}
of
\mathcal{T}

at
p
is at most the width of
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

Let
q
be a node of
\mathcal{T}_{p}
. The lower sets of
q
in
\mathcal{T}_{p}
and in
\mathcal{T}

are identical. Since
\mathcal{T}
is injective, the upper sets of
q
in
\mathcal{T}_{p}
and
in
\mathcal{T}
are also identical.
Therefore, the order of

in
\mathcal{T}_{p}
is equal to its order in
\mathcal{T}
.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Let
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
be edge sets of
G
such that
F_{1}\subseteq F_{2}

and each edge
e\in F_{2}\setminus F_{1}
intersects

only in
\partial(F_{2})
, that is,
e\cap V(F_{1})\subseteq\partial(F_{2})
.
Then, we have
\mathrm{bw}(F_{1})\leq\mathrm{bw}(F_{2})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a branch-decomposition of
F_{2}
of width
\mathrm{bw}(F_{2})
.
For each
e\in F_{2}\setminus F_{1}
, we remove the leaves that are mapped to

, and then repeatedly remove the new leaves that are internal nodes in
\mathcal{T}
.
Then, let
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
be the branch-decomposition of

obtained in this way.
For each node

of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(p)=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}(p)\cap F_{1}
and

\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(p)=\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{T}}(p)\cup(F_{2}\setminus F_{1})
.
Moreover, we have

as every edge

intersects

only in
\partial(F_{2})
by assumption, and
\partial(F_{2})\subseteq V(\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{T}}(p))

\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{T}}(p)
contains

.
Hence,
\mathrm{mid}_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(p)\subseteq\mathrm{mid}_{\mathcal{T}}(p)
, implying that the order of

in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is at most that in
\mathcal{T}
.
∎

\partial(F_{1})
and
\partial(F_{1})\subseteq V(\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}(p))
,
we have
\mathrm{mid}_{\mathcal{T}}(p)\subseteq\mathrm{mid}_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}(p)
.
Thus, the order of each node
p
of
\mathcal{T}
that belongs to
\mathcal{T}_{1}

is at most the width of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
. Similarly, the order of each node
p
of
\mathcal{T}
that belongs
to
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is at most the width of
\mathcal{T}_{2}
.
The middle set of the root
r
of
\mathcal{T}
is
V(F)\cap V(E(G)\setminus F))=\partial(F)

and hence its order is equal to
|\partial(F)|
.
∎

Lemma 10
.

For
F\subseteq E(G)
, we have
\mathrm{bw}(\mathtt{clos}(F))\leq\mathrm{bw}(F)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathrm{bw}(F)=k
and let
\mathcal{T}=(T,\varphi)
be a branch-decomposition
of
F
of width
k
. Let
F^{+}=\mathtt{clos}(F)\setminus F
.
Observe that each edge
e\in F^{+}
is contained in
\partial(F)=V(F)\cap V(E(G)\setminus F)
.
This follows since
V(F)=V(\mathtt{clos}(F))
and
e\in E(G)\setminus F
.
Thus, we have
V(F^{+})\subseteq\partial(F)
.
Let
T^{+}
be an arbitrary branch-decomposition of
F^{+}
.
Since
|V(F)|\leq|\partial(F)|\leq\mathrm{bw}(F)\leq k
,
the width of
T^{+}
is at most
k
. Let
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
be the composition of
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{+}
.
Then, by
\cref
prop:bd-compose, the width of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is the largest of the width of
\mathcal{T}
,
the width of
\mathcal{T}^{+}
, and
|\partial(\mathtt{clos}(F))|
.
Since
V(F)=V(\mathtt{clos}(F))
and

E(G)\setminus\mathtt{clos}(F)\subseteq E(G)\setminus F
,
\partial(\mathtt{clos}(F))\subseteq\partial(F)
.
Hence, the width of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is at most
k
.
∎

For vertex sets
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
of
G
,
we define
F^{+}(X_{1},X_{2})=E(X_{1}\cup X_{2})\setminus(E(X_{1})\cup E(X_{2}))
.

Lemma 11
.

Let
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
be vertex sets of a hypergraph

.
Then,
\partial(E(X_{1})\cup E(X_{2}))
is contained in

\partial(X_{1}\cup X_{2})\cup V(F^{+}(X_{1},X_{2}))
.

Proof.

Let
X=X_{1}\cup X_{2}
.
Let

be an arbitrary edge in

.
Suppose that

.
Then,
e
intersects
V(E(X_{1})\cup E(X_{2}))=X
only in
\partial(X)
.
Suppose otherwise.
Then,
e
belongs to

and hence is contained in

.
Therefore,
\partial(E(X_{1})\cup E(X_{2}))
is contained in
\partial(X)\cup V(F^{+}(X_{1},X_{2}))
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the recurrence of our algorithm.

Lemma 12
.

Let
X
be a vertex set of
G
with
|E(X)|\geq 2
. Then,
\mathrm{bw}(E(X))\leq k
if and
only if there are proper subsets
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
of
X
such that

1.

X_{1}\cup X_{2}=X
,

2.

\mathrm{bw}(E(X_{i}))\leq k
for
i=1,2
,

3.

X_{1}\cap X_{2}=\partial(X_{1})\cap\partial(X_{2})
, and

4.

|\partial(X)\cup V(F^{+}(X_{1},X_{2}))|\leq k
.

Proof.

Suppose first that there are proper subsets
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
of
X
such that the four conditions in the lemma
are satisfied. Let
\mathcal{T}_{i}=(T_{i},\varphi_{i})
be a full and injective branch-decomposition
of
E(X_{i})
for
i=1,2
. Let
\mathcal{T}=(T,\varphi)
be the composition of

\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
. Then,
\mathcal{T}
is a branch-decomposition of
E(X_{1})\cup E(X_{2})

and, by
\cref
prop:bd-compose, its width is the largest of the width of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
,
the width of
\mathcal{T}_{2}
, and
|\partial(E(X_{1})\cup E(X_{2}))|
.
The first two are at most
k
by the second condition in the assumption.
Since
\partial(E(X_{1})\cup E(X_{2}))
is a subset of

\partial(X)\cup V(F^{+}(X_{1},X_{2}))
due to
\cref
prop:bound-union,
the last is also at most
k
by the fourth condition in the assumption.
We conclude that the width of
\mathcal{T}
is at most
k
and hence

\mathrm{bw}(E(X_{1})\cup E(X_{2}))\leq k
. Since
\mathtt{clos}(E(X_{1})\cup E(X_{2}))=E(X)
,
we may apply
\cref
lem:bw_egdge_closure and conclude that

\mathrm{bw}(E(X))
is at most
k
as well.

For the converse, suppose that
X
is a vertex set of
G
with
|E(X)|\geq 2
and
\mathrm{bw}(E(X))=k
.
We prove that there are proper subsets
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
of
X
that satisfy the four conditions
in the lemma.
Let
\mathcal{T}=(T,X)
be a branch-decomposition of
E(X)
of width
k
.
We can assume that
\mathcal{T}
is full and injective due to
\cref
prop:bw_injective.
Let
p
be the furthest node of
T
from the root such that
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}(p)=X
.
Since
|E(X)|\geq 2
and no edge of
G
strictly contains
another edge by our assumption on
G
,
p
is not a leaf.
Moreover, as
p
is the furthest node from the root such that
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}(p)=X
,
p
has two child nodes
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
such that
V(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}(p_{i}))
is a proper subset of
X
for
i=1,2
.
We let
X_{i}=V(L_{\mathcal{T}}(p_{i}))
for
i=1,2
.
The first condition that
X_{1}\cup X_{2}=X
holds since
L_{\mathcal{T}}(p_{1})\cup L_{\mathcal{T}}(p_{2})=L_{\mathcal{T}}(p)
.
Since
\mathcal{T}
is injective, we have
X_{1}\cap X_{2}=\partial(X_{1})\cap\partial(X_{2})
by
\cref
prop:bd-bound: the third condition holds.

It remains to show that the second and last conditions hold.
Let
L_{i}=L_{\mathcal{T}}(p_{i})
for
i=1,2
, and let
\mathcal{T}_{i}=(T_{i},\varphi_{i})

be the sub-branch-decomposition of
\mathcal{T}
at
p_{i}
, which is a branch-decomposition of
L_{i}
.
Since
\mathcal{T}
is injective, the width of
\mathcal{T}_{i}
is at most
k
for
i=1,2
,
by
\cref
prop:sub-bd and hence
\mathrm{bw}(L_{i})
is at most
k
.
Since
E(X_{i})=\mathtt{clos}(L_{i})
,

\mathrm{bw}(E(X_{i}))
is at most
k
for

by
\cref
lem:bw_egdge_closure:
the second condition in the lemma holds.
For the last condition, observe that

and

E(G)\setminus(E(X_{1})\cup E(X_{2}))\subseteq E(G)\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}(p)
.
Therefore, both
\partial(X)
and

are subsets of

. As
\partial(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}(p))
is contained in

, its cardinality is at most

.
Therefore, we have
|\partial(X)\cup V(F^{+}(X_{1},X_{2}))|\leq|\partial(L_{\mathcal{T}}(p))|\leq k
.
∎

Our algorithm simply evaluates the recurrence in
\cref
lem:bw-recurrence in a dynamic programming manner.
Fix an integer

.
We can decide if
\mathrm{bw}(E(X))\leq k
for all

in total time
\mathcal{O}^{*}(4^{n})
, where

.
Since the branchwidth of
G
is the smallest integer

such that
\mathrm{bw}(E(G))\leq k
holds, this completes the proof of
\cref
thm:main-hg.

4
An
\mathcal{O}(3.293^{n})
-time algorithm for graphs

In this section, we improve the running time of the previous algorithm for
graphs
.

4.1
Blocks and block derivations

The
core
of
X
, denoted by
{\kappa}_{G}(X)
or simply by
{\kappa}(X)
, is defined to be

{\kappa}(X)=X\setminus\partial(X)
, that is, the subset of vertices in
X
that has no neighbors outside
X
.
The
core
of an edge set
F
, denoted by
{\kappa}_{G}(F)
or simply
{\kappa}(F)
, is defined as
{\kappa}(F)=V(F)\setminus\partial(F)
.
Note that
\partial(F)=\partial(V(F))
if
F
is closed.

For brevity, we refer to the closed neighborhood of a vertex set
W
as
the
vertex closure
or simply the
closure
of
W
and
use the notation
\hat{W}
interchangeably with
N[W]
when
G
is clear from the context.
We denote by
{\hat{\kappa}}(X)
the closure of
{\kappa}(X)
.

We call a vertex set
X
of a hypergraph
G
a
block

if
{\kappa}(X)
is non-empty and connected and, moreover,

X={\hat{\kappa}}(X)
. Under our assumption that
G
is connected,
V(G)
is a block
with
{\hat{\kappa}}(V(G))=V(G)
.
It should be noted that while the term “block” is also used in the paper by Fomin, Mazoit, and Todinca
[
10
]
, our definition, however, differs from theirs.

Let
B
be a block of a graph
G
. A
block derivation
of
B
in
G
is
a pair
D=(B,\mathcal{B})
where
\mathcal{B}
is a set of blocks such that

A\subseteq B
for each
A\in\mathcal{B}
and

{\kappa}(A_{1})\cap{\kappa}(A_{2})=\emptyset
for each pair of distinct
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
.
For a block derivation
D=(B,\mathcal{B})
, we use the following notation:

B(D)\coloneqq B
,
\mathcal{B}(D)\coloneqq\mathcal{B}
,
K(D)\coloneqq\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{B}}{\kappa}(A)
, and

S(D)\coloneqq B\setminus K(D)
.

A triple
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
of subsets of
S(D)
is a
mid-triple
of a block derivation
D

if the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

M_{1}\cup M_{2}=M_{2}\cup M_{3}=M_{3}\cup M_{1}=S(D)
,

2.

\partial(B)\subseteq M_{3}
, and

3.

for each
A\in\mathcal{B}(D)
,
\partial(A)
is contained in either
M_{1}
or
M_{2}
.

The
order
of a mid-triple
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
is the largest of
|M_{1}|
,
|M_{2}|
, and
|M_{3}|
.
The
order
of a block derivation
D
is the smallest
k
such that
there is a mid-triple of
D
of order
k
.
The
width
of a block derivation
D
is the larger of the order of
D

and the largest of
\mathrm{bw}(E(A))
over all
A\in\mathcal{B}(D)
.

Lemma 13
.

Let
B
be a block of a graph
G
. Suppose that there is a block derivation

D
of
B
of width
k
.
Then,
\mathrm{bw}(E(B))
is at most
k
.

Proof.

Let
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
be a mid-triple of
D
of order at most
k
.
Let
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2})
be a bipartition of
\mathcal{B}(D)
such
that
\partial(A)\subseteq M_{i}
for every
A\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
, for
i=1,2
.

We first define several subsets of
E(B)
.
For each
A\in\mathcal{B}(D)
, let
F_{A}=E(A)
.
We define
F_{i}
as
F_{i}=\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{B}_{i}}F_{A}
, for
i=1,2
.
We denote by
F_{i+2}
the set of edges of
G
contained in
M_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
, that is,
F_{i+2}\coloneqq E(M_{i})
.
We claim that
E(B)=F_{1}\cup F_{2}\cup F_{3}\cup F_{4}\cup F_{5}
.
For each
1\leq i\leq 5
, we have
V(F_{i})\subseteq B
and
hence
F_{i}\subseteq E(B)
. Therefore,
F_{1}\cup F_{2}\cup F_{3}\cup F_{4}\cup F_{5}
is
a subset of
E(B)
. For the inclusion in the other direction, let

e
be an edge in
E(B)
, and we show that
e
belongs to some
F_{i}
.
If
e
is contained in some
A\in\mathcal{B}(D)
,
then clearly
e
belongs to
F_{1}
or
F_{2}
.
Suppose otherwise.
Since
e\in E(B)\setminus(F_{1}\cup F_{2})
intersects
B\setminus V(F_{i})
for each
i=1,2
, it has no intersection with
{\kappa}(A)
for any
A\in\mathcal{B}(D)
.
This implies that
e\subseteq S(D)
.
Since every vertex in
S(D)
belongs to at least two of
M_{1}
,
M_{2}
, and

M_{3}
, and moreover
G
is a graph
, there is some
i\in\{1,2,3\}
such that
e\subseteq M_{i}
and hence
e
belongs to
F_{i+2}
.

We construct a branch-decomposition
\mathcal{T}
of
E(B)
, which is
composed of several sub-branch-decompositions described below.
For each
A\in\mathcal{B}(D)
, let
\mathcal{T}_{A}=(T_{A},\varphi_{A})
be
a branch-decomposition of
F_{A}
of width at most
k
.
We can assume that
\mathcal{T}_{A}
is injective due to
\cref
prop:bw_injective.
For
i=1,2
, let
m_{i}=|\mathcal{B}_{i}|
and let

, …,
A^{i}_{m_{i}}
be
a listing of the members of
\mathcal{B}_{i}
in an arbitrary order.
For
i=1,2
and for
1\leq j\leq m_{i}
, let
\mathcal{T}^{i}_{j}

be a branch-decomposition of
\bigcup_{1\leq h\leq j}F_{A^{i}_{h}}
inductively defined as follows:

\mathcal{T}^{i}_{1}=\mathcal{T}_{A^{i}_{1}}
and
\mathcal{T}^{i}_{j}
for
2\leq j\leq m_{i}
is the
composition of
\mathcal{T}^{i}_{j-1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{A^{i}_{j}}
.
We denote
\mathcal{T}^{i}_{m_{i}}
by
\mathcal{T}_{i}
, for
i=1,2
.
For
i=3,4,5
,
\mathcal{T}_{i}
is an arbitrary branch-decomposition of
F_{i}
.
The composition of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{3}
is denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{6}
and
the composition of
\mathcal{T}_{2}
and
\mathcal{T}_{4}
is denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{7}
.
Then,
\mathcal{T}_{8}
is the composition of
\mathcal{T}_{6}
and
\mathcal{T}_{7}
.
Finally,
\mathcal{T}
is the composition of
\mathcal{T}_{8}
and
\mathcal{T}_{5}
.
To summarize,
\mathcal{T}_{i}
is a branch-decomposition of
F_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 5
,

\mathcal{T}_{6}
is a branch-decomposition of
F_{1}\cup F_{3}
,

\mathcal{T}_{7}
is a branch-decomposition of
F_{2}\cup F_{4}
,

\mathcal{T}_{8}
is a branch-decomposition of
F_{1}\cup F_{2}\cup F_{3}\cup F_{4}
, and

\mathcal{T}
is a branch-decomposition of
F_{1}\cup F_{2}\cup F_{3}\cup F_{4}\cup F_{5}=E(B)
.
We now claim that the width of
\mathcal{T}
is at most
k
.
To see this, let
p
be a node of
\mathcal{T}
.

Suppose first that
p
belongs to
\mathcal{T}_{A}
for some
A\in\mathcal{B}(D)
.
Then the lower edge set of
p
in
\mathcal{T}
is identical to that in
\mathcal{T}_{A}
.
Let

.
Since
\mathcal{T}_{A}
is injective and

, it holds that

, meaning that

belongs to

.
Moreover, as there is a leaf of
\mathcal{T}
not in
\mathcal{T}_{A}
that is mapped to

,

is not incident
to a vertex in

.
Hence, we have

, which is a subset of

, and hence
the middle set of
p
in
\mathcal{T}
is identical to its middle set in
\mathcal{T}_{A}
. Therefore,
the order of
p
in
\mathcal{T}
is at most

.

Suppose
p
is the root of
\mathcal{T}^{1}_{j}
for some
1\leq j\leq m_{1}
.
Then,

is a subset of

. Since

\mathcal{T}
that is not a descendant of

is mapped to an edge that intersects

,
the middle set of

is a subset of

and hence its order is at most

. The case where

is the root of
\mathcal{T}^{2}_{j}

and

.

Suppose

is a node in
\mathcal{T}_{i}

. Then,

is a subset of

is at most

.

is the root of
\mathcal{T}_{6}
, the composition of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{3}
.
Its lower edge set is

and its upper edge set is

.

is at most

.

Suppose finally that

is the root of
\mathcal{T}_{8}
, the composition of
\mathcal{T}_{6}
and
\mathcal{T}_{7}
.
Its lower edge set is

and its upper edge set is

. Therefore, its middle set

be an edge

Otherwise,

Finally, the middle set of the root of

corresponds to

and hence its order is at most

.
We conclude that the width of

is at most

is at most

.

Theorem 14
.

Let
G
be a graph and
B
a block of
G
.
Then,
\mathrm{bw}(E(B))=k
if and only if there is a block derivation
D
of
B
of width
k
.

Proof.

By
\cref
lem:block-deriv2bw, if there is a block derivation of
B
of width
k
then

\mathrm{bw}(E(B))\leq k
. Therefore, to prove the theorem, it suffices to show that
if
\mathrm{bw}(E(B))=k
then there is a block derivation of
G
of width at most
k
.
Suppose that
\mathrm{bw}(E(B))=k
.
Let
\mathcal{T}
be a full and injective branch-decomposition of
E(B)
of width
k
.
Since
B
is a block and each vertex in
{\kappa}(B)
is incident to at least two edges,

|E(B)|\geq 2
and hence
\mathcal{T}
has at least two leaves.
Therefore, similarly to the proof of
\cref
lem:bw-recurrence, there is a node
p
of
T

with child nodes
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
such that
V(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}(p))=B
but

V(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}(p_{i}))
is a proper subset of
B
, for
i=1,2
.
Let
L_{i}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}(p_{i})
,
X_{i}=V(L_{i})
, and
M_{i}=\partial(L_{i})
for
i=1,2
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be the set of components of
G[B\setminus(M_{1}\cup M_{2})]
and
let
\mathcal{B}=\{\hat{C}\mid C\in\mathcal{C}\}
.
Note that each
A\in\mathcal{B}
is contained in either
X_{1}
or
X_{2}
.
Then,
D=(B,\mathcal{B})
is a block derivation of
B
,
since each
A\in\mathcal{B}
is contained in
B
and, for each pair of distinct members
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
,

{\kappa}(A_{1})\cap{\kappa}(A_{2})=\emptyset
.
We claim
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
, where
M_{3}=\partial(B)\cup(M_{1}\setminus M_{2})\cup(M_{2}\setminus M_{1})
,
is a mid-triple of
D
. To see this, let
A
be a member of
\mathcal{B}
.
By
\cref
prop:bd-bound, it holds that
X_{1}\cap X_{2}=\partial(X_{1})\cap\partial(X_{2})
.
As
A
is contained in either
X_{1}
or
X_{2}
,
\partial(A)
is contained in either
M_{1}
or
M_{2}
.
Moreover, the definition of
M_{3}
ensures that
M_{1}\cup M_{2}=M_{2}\cup M_{3}=M_{1}\cup M_{3}=S(D)
and
that
\partial(B)\subseteq M_{3}
as required by the definition of mid-triples.

For
i=1,2
, since
|M_{i}|=|\partial(L_{i})|
is at most the order of the node
p_{i}
in
\mathcal{T}
, it is at most
k
.
To bound the cardinality of
M_{3}
,
we claim that
M_{3}\subseteq\partial(L_{1}\cup L_{2})
.
Every vertex in
\partial(B)
belongs to
\partial(L_{1}\cup L_{2})
since it
belongs to
V(L_{1}\cup L_{2})=B
and is adjacent
to some vertex in
V(G)\setminus B
.
This implies that
\partial(B)\subseteq\partial(L_{1}\cup L_{2})
.
Let
v
be a vertex in
(M_{1}\setminus M_{2})\setminus\partial(B)
.
Since
v
belongs to
M_{1}=\partial(L_{1})
but not to
\partial(B)
,
there is an edge
e
of
G
between
v
and some vertex
u
in
X_{2}\setminus X_{1}
.
As
v\notin M_{2}
,
u
must belong to
\partial(L_{2})\setminus X_{1}=M_{2}\setminus M_{1}
. Thus, we have
e\in E(G)\setminus(L_{1}\cup L_{2})
.
As
e\subseteq V(L_{1}\cup L_{2})
, it holds that
v\in\partial(L_{1}\cup L_{2})
.
Hence,
M_{1}\setminus M_{2}
is a subset of
\partial(L_{1}\cup L_{2})
.
Similarly,
M_{2}\setminus M_{1}
is a subset of
\partial(L_{1}\cup L_{2})
.
Since
|\partial(L_{1}\cup L_{2})|
is at most the order of
p
in
G
and hence is at most
k
,

|M_{3}|
is at most
k
and hence the order of the mid-triple
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
is at most
k
.
It follows that the order of the block derivation
D
is at most
k
.

Finally, let
A\in\mathcal{B}(D)
. We claim that
\mathrm{bw}(E(A))\leq k
, which implies that the width of
D
is at most
k
.
Suppose without loss of generality
that
A
is contained in
X_{1}
. We construct a branch-decomposition
\mathcal{T}_{A}

of
E(A)
as follows. Let
\mathcal{T}_{1}
denote the sub-branch-decomposition
of
\mathcal{T}
at
p_{1}
. Since
\mathcal{T}
is injective, the width of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is at most the width of

\mathcal{T}
, by
\cref
prop:sub-bd, and hence at most
k
. We obtain
\mathcal{T}_{A}

by removing leaves of
\mathcal{T}
mapped to edges not in
E(A)
one by one.
Since each edge removed intersects

only in
M_{1}=\partial(L_{1})
,

\cref
prop:bd-edge-deletion applies and the width of
\mathcal{T}_{A}
is at most the
width of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, which is at most
k
. Therefore,
\mathrm{bw}(E(A))\leq k

for every
A\in\mathcal{B}(D)
.
∎

Algorithm

Algorithm

.

Decide if

Lemma 15
.

Algorithm

\mathrm{bw}(G)\leq k
.

Proof.

We prove by induction on

\mathcal{F}_{k}(G,i)
contains all blocks
B
of
G

\mathrm{bw}(E(B))\leq k
and
|{\kappa}(B)|=i
, for
1\leq i\leq n

Our running time analysis of the algorithm mostly consists in bounding the cardinality of

\mathcal{D}_{k}(G)\coloneqq\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(G)}\mathcal{D}_{k}(G,B)
and the time for generating those block derivations.
The following straightforward bound turns out to be useful in the analysis.

Lemma 16
.

There are at most
3^{n}
block derivations of
G
and they can be generated in time
\mathcal{O}^{*}(3^{n})
.

Proof.

For disjoint subsets
K
and
S
of
V(G)
, observe that there is at most one block derivation
D

such that
K(D)=K
and
S(D)=S
.
Indeed, for such a block derivation
D=(B,\mathcal{B})
, it must hold that

B=K\cup S
and
\mathcal{B}=\{\hat{C}\mid C\in\mathcal{C}\}
, where

\mathcal{C}
is the set of components of
G[K]
.
It can be decided in polynomial time
if the pair
(K,S)
gives rise to such a block derivation, by checking if
N(C)\subseteq S
for all

C\in\mathcal{C}
.
∎

We need an efficient way to decide if the width of a given block derivation is at most
k
.
In particular, we need to compute a mid-triple
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
of a block derivation
D
of order at most
k
.

Observation 17
.

If the order of a block derivation
D
is
k
then

|S(D)|
is at most
3k/2
.

Proof.

Let
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{2})
be a mid-triple of
D
of order
k
.
Then, since every vertex in
S(D)
belongs to at least two of
M_{1}
,
M_{2}
, and
M_{3}
,
we have
|S|\leq(|M_{1}|+|M_{2}|+|M_{3}|)/2\leq 3k/2
.
∎

Due to this observation, we can focus on the case where
|S(D)|\leq 3k/2

in deciding if the width of
D
is at most
k
.
We use the following tool to reason about mid-triples. Let
S
be a set. A triple
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})

of subsets of
S
is a
bicover
of
S
if each member of
S
appears in exactly two of

S_{1}
,
S_{2}
, and
S_{3}
.
For a bicover
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
of
S
, it holds that
S_{1}\cup S_{2}=S_{2}\cup S_{3}=S_{3}\cup S_{1}=S
.
It is a
subbicover
of
S
if each member of
S
appears in at most two
of
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
, and
S_{3}
. We say that a bicover
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})

extends
a subbicover
(S_{1}^{\prime},S_{2}^{\prime},S_{3}^{\prime})

if
S_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq S_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. The
order
of a bicover
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
is the largest of

|S_{1}|
,
|S_{2}|
, and
|S_{3}|
. Since
|S_{1}|+|S_{2}|+|S_{3}|=2|S|
, the order of a bicover of
S
is
at least
2|S|/3
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
S
be a set and
k
a positive integer such that
|S|\leq 3k/2
.
Let
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
be a subbicover of
S
. Then, there is a bicover of
S
of order at most
k

that extends
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
if and only if the following conditions are satisfied.

1.

|S_{i}|\leq k
for
i=1,2,3
,

2.

|S_{i}\cap S_{j}|+|S|\leq 2k
for
1\leq i<j\leq 3
;

Proof.

Suppose first that there is bicover
(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})
of order
k
that extends
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
.
Then, for
1\leq i\leq 3
, we have
|S_{i}|\leq|R_{i}|\leq k
. Since
|R_{1}|+|R_{2}|=|R_{1}\cap R_{2}|+|R_{1}\cup R_{2}|=|R_{1}\cap R_{2}|+|S|
, we have
|S_{1}\cap S_{2}|+|S|\leq|R_{1}\cap R_{2}|+|S|\leq 2k
. Similarly, we have
|S_{2}\cap S_{3}|+|S|\leq 2k
and

|S_{3}\cap S_{1}|+|S|\leq 2k
.

For the converse, let
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
be a subbicover of
S
that satisfies the conditions
in the lemma. First suppose that
S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup S_{3}=S
.
The case otherwise will be dealt with later.
For
1\leq i\leq 3
, let
U_{i}
be the set of members of
S

that belongs to
S_{i}
but not to
S_{j}
for
j\neq i
.
We prove that there is bicover of
S
of order at most
k

extending
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
by induction on

|U_{1}\cup U_{2}\cup U_{3}|
. The base case where
U_{1}\cup U_{2}\cup U_{3}
is empty is trivial since
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
is then a bicover and, moreover, of order at most
k
by Condition 1.
Suppose that
U_{1}\cup U_{2}\cup U_{3}
is non-empty.
First suppose that Condition 2 is not tight for any pair of distinct
i,j\in\{1,2,3\}
:
we have
|S_{i}\cap S_{j}|+|S|<2k
for every such pair.
Without loss of generality, suppose
U_{1}
is non-empty and
let
u
be an arbitrary member of
U_{1}
.
Then, we have
|S_{2}|+|S_{3}|=|S_{2}\cup S_{3}|+|S_{2}\cap S_{3}|=|S\setminus U_{1}|+|S_{2}\cap S_{3}|\leq|S|+|S_{2}\cap S_{3}|-1\leq 2k-1
by Condition 2.
Note that each member of
S\setminus U_{1}
is either not in
S_{1}
or in
S_{1}\cap S_{i}
for some
i\in\{2,3\}
, and vise versa, meaning that
S_{2}\cup S_{3}=U\setminus S_{1}
under the assumption that
S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup S_{3}=S
.
Therefore, we have either

|S_{2}|\leq k-1
or
|S_{3}|\leq k-1
.
We set
S_{2}^{\prime}=S_{2}\cup\{u\}
and
S_{3}^{\prime}=S_{3}
in the former case and
S_{2}^{\prime}=S_{2}^{\prime}
and
S_{3}^{\prime}=S_{3}\cup\{u\}
in the latter. Then, Condition 1 with
S_{i}
replaced by
S_{i}^{\prime}
for

i\in\{2,3\}
is maintained. Moreover, due to the assumption that Condition 2 is not tight for
any pair of
i
and
j
, Condition 2 with
S_{i}
replaced by
S_{i}^{\prime}
for

i\in\{2,3\}
is also maintained. Therefore, by the induction hypothesis,
there is a bicover of
S
of order at most
k
that extends
(S_{1},S_{2}^{\prime},S_{3}^{\prime})
and
hence extends
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
.

Now suppose that
|S_{1}\cap S_{2}|+|S|=2k
.
Then,
|S_{1}\cap S_{2}|\geq k/2

since
|S|\leq 3k/2
.
Let
R_{3}=S\setminus(S_{1}\cap S_{2})
. Note that
S_{3}\subseteq R_{3}
since
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
is a subbicover and hence
S_{1}\cap S_{2}\cap S_{3}
is empty.
Note also that
|R_{3}|=|S|-|S_{1}\cap S_{2}|\leq 3k/2-k/2\leq k
since
|S_{1}\cap S_{2}|\geq k/2
and
|S|\leq 3k/2
.
We define
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
so that

(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})
is a bicover of
S
that extends
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
.
To this end, we need to put each member of
U_{3}^{\prime}=R_{3}\setminus(S_{1}\cup S_{2})

to exactly one of
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
.
Regardless of our choices, we have
|R_{1}|+|R_{2}|=|R_{1}\cup R_{2}|+|R_{1}\cap R_{2}|=|S|+|S_{1}\cap S_{2}|=2k
. Since
|S_{1}|\leq k
and
|S_{2}|\leq k
, we can partition
U_{3}^{\prime}
into

R_{1}\setminus S_{1}
and
R_{2}\setminus S_{2}
so that
|R_{1}|=|R_{2}|=k
.
The triple
(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})
thus obtained is a bicover of
S
of order
k
that extends
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
. The cases where
|S|+|S_{2}\cap S_{3}|=2k
or

|S|+|S_{3}\cap S_{1}|=2k
are similar.
This concludes the induction step and the proof of the converse under the assumption that
S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup S_{3}=S
.

We finally deal with the case where
Q=S\setminus(S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup S_{3})
is non-empty. Since
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
is a subbicover of
S\setminus Q
, we have

|S_{1}|+|S_{2}|+|S_{3}|\leq 2|S\setminus Q|
and hence
|S_{1}|+|S_{2}|+|S_{3}|+|Q|\leq|S_{1}|+|S_{2}|+|S_{3}|+2|Q|\leq 2|S|\leq 3k
. Therefore,
Q
can be partitioned into
Q_{1}
,
Q_{2}
, and
Q_{3}
so that

|S_{i}\cup Q_{i}|\leq k
for
i=1,2,3
.
Let
S_{i}^{\prime}=S_{i}\cup Q_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
.
Then,
(S_{1}^{\prime},S_{2}^{\prime},S_{3}^{\prime})
is a subbicover
of
S
satisfying the condition in the lemma
with
S_{i}
replaced by
S_{i}^{\prime}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. Note here that
S_{1}^{\prime}\cap S_{2}^{\prime}=S_{1}\cap S_{2}
and so on. Therefore, there is
a bicover of
S
of order at most
k
that
extends
(S_{1}^{\prime},S_{2}^{\prime},S_{3}^{\prime})
and hence extends
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
.
∎

Let
D=(B,\mathcal{B})
be a block derivation and let
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2})
be a bipartition of
\mathcal{B}
.
Let
\partial(\mathcal{B}_{i})
denote
\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{B}_{i}}\partial(A)

for
i=1,2
. We say that a mid-triple
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})

conforms to
the bipartition

(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2})
if
\partial(\mathcal{B}_{i})\subseteq M_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

Lemma 19
.

Let
D=(B,\mathcal{B})
be a block derivation of a block
B
in a graph
G
and
let
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2})
be a bipartition of
\mathcal{B}
.
Then, it can be decided in polynomial time if there is a mid-triple of
D
of order at most
k

that conforms to
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2})
.

Proof.

Let
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{2})
be a mid-triple of
D
conforming to
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2})
.
We have
\partial(\mathcal{B}_{i})\subseteq M_{i}
for
i=1,2
and, moreover,

\partial(B)\subseteq M_{3}
as it is a condition in the definition of a mid-triple of
D
.
Let
\delta_{i}=\partial(\mathcal{B}_{i})
for
i=1,2
and
\delta_{3}=\partial(B)
. Let
I=\delta_{1}\cap\delta_{2}\cap\delta_{3}
.
We may assume that

M_{1}\cap M_{2}\cap M_{3}=I
, since if some
v\in M_{1}\cap M_{2}\cap M_{3}
does not belong to
I
, then we may remove
v
from
M_{i}
where
i

is such that
v\not\in\delta_{i}
, maintaining the property of
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
being a mid-triple of
D
.
Let
S^{\prime}=S\setminus I
,

R_{i}=M_{i}\setminus I
for
1\leq i\leq 3
, and

S_{i}=\delta_{i}\setminus I
for
1\leq i\leq 3
.
Then,
(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})
is
a bicover of
S^{\prime}
that extends the subbicover
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
of
S^{\prime}
.
As the order of
(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})
is the order of

(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
minus
|I|
, the problem of deciding if
D
has a mid-triple of order
k
reduces to deciding if there is a bicover of
S^{\prime}

of order at most
k-|I|

that extends
(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
, which can be immediately answered by checking the conditions in
\cref
lem:bicover.
∎

By
\cref
lem:bipartition2order, when
\mathcal{B}(D)
contains a small number of sets, we can efficiently compute a mid-triple
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
of
D
of order at most
k
by trying all possible bipartitions
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2})
of
\mathcal{B}(D)
to which
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
conforms.
For the case where
\mathcal{B}(D)
contains many sets, we have an alternative method of deciding
if the width of
D
is at most
k
. We call a mid-triple
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
of a block derivation
D=(B,\mathcal{B})

k
-full
if
|M_{1}|=|M_{2}|=k
.

Lemma 20
.

Let
D=(B,\mathcal{B})
be a block derivation of a block
B
of order at most
k

such that
|S(D)|\geq k
.
Then, there is a
k
-full mid-triple of
D
of order
k
.

Proof.

Let
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
be a mid-triple of
D
of order at most
k
.
Initialize
M_{i}^{\prime}
to
M_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
.
While
|M_{1}^{\prime}|<k
, add a vertex in
M_{2}^{\prime}\setminus M_{1}^{\prime}
to
M_{1}^{\prime}
.
Similarly, while
|M_{2}^{\prime}|<k
, add a vertex in
M_{1}^{\prime}\setminus M_{2}^{\prime}
to
M_{2}^{\prime}
.
In this process,
(M_{1}^{\prime},M_{2}^{\prime},M_{3}^{\prime})
remains a mid-triple of
D

of order at most
k
. Since
|S(D)|=|M_{1}\cup M_{2}|\geq k
,
the process stops with
|M_{1}^{\prime}|=|M_{2}^{\prime}|=k
.
∎

Lemma 21
.

Let
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
be a
k
-full mid-triple of a block derivation

D=(B,\mathcal{B})
. Then
|M_{1}\cap M_{2}|=2k-|S(D)|
.

Proof.

As
S(D)=M_{1}\cup M_{2}
by the definition of a mid-triple,
we have
|S(D)|+|M_{1}\cap M_{2}|=|M_{1}|+|M_{2}|=2k
.
∎

Lemma 22
.

Let
D=(B,\mathcal{B})
be a block derivation in
G
,
I
a subset of
S(D)
,
and
k
a positive integer such that
|I|=2k-|S(D)|
.
Then, it can be decided in polynomial time if there is
a
k
-full mid-triple
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
of
D
of order
k
such that
M_{1}\cap M_{2}=I
.

Proof.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}
. Observe that, in a mid-triple
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
of

D
such that
M_{1}\cap M_{2}=I
, the vertices in
\partial(A)\setminus I

either altogether belong to
M_{1}
or altogether belong to
M_{2}
.
This observation motivates a graph
Q
on vertex set
S(D)\setminus I
such that
there is an edge between
u
and
v
if and only if
u
and
v

belong to
\partial(A)
for some
A\in\mathcal{B}
.
Then, each connected component of
Q

must be contained in exactly one of
M_{1}
or
M_{2}
. Let
\gamma_{1}
, …,
\gamma_{m}

be the list of connected components of
Q
.
Then, our problem of finding
a
k
-full mid-triple
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
of order
k
such that
M_{1}\cap M_{2}=I

reduces to a subset sum problem that asks for a subset
J
of
\{1,\ldots,m\}

such that
\sum_{j\in J}|\gamma_{j}|=k-|I|
.
By the classical Bellman’s dynamic programming algorithm, this problem
can be solved in time
\mathcal{O}(m(k-|I|))\subseteq\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
.
Setting up the input for the subset sum problem can be done in polynomial time, and thus the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 23
.

Let
D=(B,\mathcal{B})
be a block derivation in a graph
G
with
|S(D)|\geq k
.
Then, it can be decided in time

\mathcal{O}^{*}\left(\min\left\{2^{|B|-|S(D)|},\binom{|S(D)|}{2k-|S(D)|}\right\}\right)

whether the order of
D
is at most
k
.

Proof.

The first bound of
\mathcal{O}^{*}(2^{|B|-|S(D)|})
is achieved by a brute force method
that tries all bipartitions

(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2})
of
\mathcal{B}
deciding if there is a mid-triple
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})

of
D
of order at most
k
that conforms to this bipartition in polynomial time invoking

\cref

For the second bound, by

of order at most

.
We try all
\binom{|S|}{2k-|S|}
subsets

of

of cardinality

(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
of order

in polynomial time invoking

∎

We now upper bound the number of blocks and the number of block derivations
needed to decide if

is at most

We use the following result due to Fomin and Villanger

Lemma 24

(Fomin and Villanger
[

)

Let

a graph and

the number of connected vertex subsets

and those

\mathcal{O}^{*}\left(\binom{b+c-1}{b}\right)
.

Lemma 25
.

Let
G
be a graph with
n

positive integers such that

b\leq a\leq n
.
Then, the number of blocks

such that

|B|=a
and
|\partial(B)|=b

\min\left\{n\binom{a-1}{b},\binom{n}{a}\right\}
.
Moreover, those blocks can be listed in time
\mathcal{O}^{*}\left(\min\left\{\binom{a-1}{b},\binom{n}{a}\right\}\right)
.

Proof.

Since
{\kappa}(B)
is connected and
\partial(B)=N({\kappa}(B))
,
the number of blocks
B
such that

|B|=a
and
|\partial(B)|=b
is at most

by
\cref

The latter bound is simply the number of subsets of

of cardinality

The listing procedure in
\cref

∎

Recall that
\mathcal{D}_{k}(G)=\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(G)}\mathcal{D}_{k}(G,B)

Lemma 26
.

Let

be the function defined as follows.

\displaystyle f(n,k,s,a,b)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\min\left\{\binom{a-1}{b},\binom{n}{a}\right\}\cdot\binom{a-b}{s-b}\cdot\min\left\{2^{a-s},\binom{s}{2k-s}\right\},

where the domain of each variable consists of integers that satisfy the following:
0\leq k\leq n
,
k<s\leq 3k/2
,
s<a\leq n
, and
b\leq\min\{k,a-1\}
.
Let

denote the maximum of
f(n,k,s,a,b)
over all combinations of

s
,
a
, and
b
in the domain.
Then,
|\mathcal{D}_{k}(G)|=\mathcal{O}^{*}(f^{*}(n,k)+3^{n})
and
\mathcal{D}_{k}(G)
can be
computed in
\mathcal{O}^{*}(f^{*}(n,k)+3^{n})
time.

Proof.

Fix

. Based on
\cref
prop:bd-naive-bound, We first list all block derivations in
G
in

\mathcal{O}^{*}(3^{n})
time. For each block derivation
D

|S(D)|\leq k
then
put
D
to
\mathcal{D}_{k}(G)
, since such
D
trivially has a mid-triple
(S(D),\emptyset,S(D))

of order
|S(D)|
.

We then pick up block derivations
D
with
k<|S(D)|\leq 3k/2

that are of order at most
k
as follows.
For each
b\leq k
and

\mathcal{B}_{a,b}(G)
of blocks
B
such that

|B|=a
and
|\partial(B)|=b
, in time
\mathcal{O}^{*}(\min\{\binom{a-1}{b},\binom{n}{n-a}\})
due to
\cref
lem:block-count.
For all the combinations of
s
,
a
, and
b
such that
k<s\leq 3k/2
,

b\leq k
,
b<a
, and
s<a\leq n

we try each subset

of

such that
|S|=s
and
\partial(B)\subseteq S

letting
\mathcal{C}
be the set of components of
G[B\setminus S]
,
letting
\mathcal{B}=\{\hat{C}\mid C\in\mathcal{C}\}
, and
deciding if the order of the block derivation

with

is at most

using
\cref
lem:bd-order; we add
(B,\mathcal{B})
to
\mathcal{D}_{k}(G)
if this is the case.
We have
\binom{a-b}{s-b}
choices for

and deciding if
(B,\mathcal{B})
is of order at most
k
can be done in time
\mathcal{O}^{*}(\min\{2^{a-s},\binom{s}{2k-s}\})
.
The time for each combination of
a
,
b
, and

is at most
f(n,k,s,a,b)

and hence the total time for processing all combinations is in
\mathcal{O}^{*}(f^{*}(n,k))
.
∎

Theorem 27
.

The running time of Algorithm
1
is in
\mathcal{O}(3.293^{n})
.

Proof.

We can compute the set
\mathcal{B}_{k}
of all blocks
B
in
G
such that
|\partial(B)|\leq k
in
\mathcal{O}^{*}(2^{n})
time.
By
\cref
lem:bd-bound,
|\mathcal{D}_{k}(G)|=\mathcal{O}^{*}(f^{*}(n,k)+3^{n})
and so is the running time of generating
\mathcal{D}_{k}(G)
.
The dynamic programming step for computing
\mathcal{F}_{k}(G,i)
can also be done in time
\mathcal{O}^{*}(f^{*}(n,k)+3^{n})
.

To evaluate
f^{*}(n,k)
,
we use the well known upper bound on the binomial coefficient that
\binom{n}{k}\leq 2^{h(k/n)}
, where
h(x)
is the binary entropy function (see
[
9
]
, for example).
The logarithm base two of the function
f
in
\cref
lem:bd-bound is bounded as

\displaystyle\log_{2}f(n,k,s,a,b)

\displaystyle\leq n\cdot g(k/n,s/n,a/n,b/n),

where

\displaystyle g(\kappa,\sigma,\alpha,\beta)=\min\{h(\beta/\alpha),h(\alpha)\}+h((\sigma-\beta)/(\alpha-\beta))+\min\{\alpha-\sigma,h((2\kappa-\sigma)/\sigma)\}.

A numerical computation shows that the maximum of
g(\kappa,\sigma,\alpha,\beta)
in the domain corresponding to the domain for
f
described in
\cref
lem:bd-bound
is at most 1.719253. The code for this computation, implemented by Claude Code (Opus 4.6), uses the interval arithmetic
provided by the npmath module of Python and therefore this upper bound is rigorous. As
2^{1.71953}<3.2927
,
we have
f^{*}(n,k)\leq 3.2927^{n}
and therefore the running time of the algorithm is
in
\mathcal{O}(3.293^{n})
.
∎

This completes the proof of
\cref
thm:main.

4.3
An alternative algorithm for graphs

In this subsection, we present an alternative to Algorithm
1
.
We conjecture that its running time is
\mathcal{O}(c^{n})
where
c

is a constant smaller than 3.293, the constant in the running time bound of Algorithm
1
. See
the partial analysis at the end of this subsection for some grounds of this conjecture.
Regardless of the validity of this conjecture, its practical performance is similar to Algorithm
1
on
most instances but significantly better on some instances that are hard for Algorithm
1
.

The idea of the algorithm is to restrict blocks
B
for which we need to compute the branchwidth of
E(B)
.
Call an edge set
F
of
G

small
if
|{\kappa}(F)|\leq|V(G)\setminus V(F)|
.
Call a block
B

small
if
E(B)
is small.
Call a tripartition
(E_{1},E_{2},E_{3})
of
E(G)

balanced
if

E_{i}
is small for
1\leq i\leq 3
.

Proposition 28
.

Let
G
be a graph with at least three edges and of minimum degree at least 2.
Then, there is a balanced tripartition
(E_{1},E_{2},E_{3})
of
E(G)

such that
\mathrm{bw}(E_{i})\leq\mathrm{bw}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T}=(T,\varphi)
be an injective branch-decomposition of
E(G)
of width
\mathrm{bw}(G)
.
Let
T^{\prime}
be an undirected ternary tree
2
2

2

A
ternary tree
is a (unrooted) tree such that every internal node has degree exactly 3.
obtained from
T
by replacing the
two edges incident to the root by an edge. Let
p
be an internal node of
T^{\prime}
.
The removal of
p
from
T^{\prime}
naturally induces a tripartition of
E(G)
, which
we call the tripartition associated with
p
. We show that there is an internal node
r
of
T^{\prime}

such that the tripartition associated with
r
is balanced.
For each edge
\{p,q\}
of
T^{\prime}
, orient this edge from

p
to
q
if the edge set on
p
’s side is small; from
q
to
p
if the edge set
on
q
’s side is small, breaking ties arbitrarily.
Under our assumption on
G
, each edge incident to a leaf of
T^{\prime}
is oriented away from the leaf.
Since every edge is oriented, there is a node
r
such that all three incident edges
are oriented towards
r
. The tripartition associated with
r
is balanced.
The subtrees of
T^{\prime}
that result from removing
r
are
branch-decompositions of
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
, and
E_{3}
. It is straightforward to see
that the width of each of them is at most the width of
\mathcal{T}
.
∎

In the following, we use similar but slightly different definitions from those used in the previous subsection.
A
root derivation
of a graph
G
is a set
\mathcal{B}
of blocks of
G

such that
{\kappa}(A_{1})\cap{\kappa}(A_{2})=\emptyset
for each pair of distinct
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
. We let
S(\mathcal{B})=V(G)\setminus{\kappa}(\mathcal{B})
, where
{\kappa}(\mathcal{B})=\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{B}}{\kappa}(A)
as before.
A
mid-triple
of a root derivation
\mathcal{B}
is a triple
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
of
subsets of
S(\mathcal{B})
such that
M_{1}\cup M_{2}=M_{2}\cup M_{3}=M_{3}\cup M_{1}=S(\mathcal{B})
.
Let
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2},\mathcal{B}_{3})
be a tripartition of
\mathcal{B}
. We say
that a mid-triple
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})

conforms to
this tripartition
if
\partial(\mathcal{B}_{i})\subseteq M_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
.
A mid-triple of
\mathcal{B}
is
conforming
if it conforms to some
tripartition of
\mathcal{B}
.
The order of a mid-triple
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
is the largest of

|M_{i}|
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. The
order
of a root derivation
\mathcal{B}
is
the smallest
k
such that there is a conforming mid-triple of
\mathcal{B}
of order
k
.

Lemma 29
.

Let
G
be a graph with at least two edges.
Then,
\mathrm{bw}(G)\leq k
if and only if there is a root derivation

\mathcal{B}
of order at most
k
such that, for each
A\in\mathcal{B}
,

A
is small and
\mathrm{bw}(E(A))\leq k
.

Proof.

The proof of the “if”-part is similar to the proof of
\cref
lem:block-deriv2bw on block derivations. For the converse, suppose
\mathrm{bw}(G)=k
. By
\cref
prop:balanced,
there is a balanced tripartition
(E_{1},E_{2},E_{3})
of
E(G)
such that

\mathrm{bw}(E_{i})\leq k
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. Let
M_{i}=\partial(E_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq 3
and
let
S=M_{1}\cup M_{2}\cup M_{3}
. Note that
S=M_{1}\cup M_{2}=M_{2}\cup M_{3}=M_{3}\cup M_{1}
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the set of components of
G[V(G)\setminus S]
and
let
\mathcal{B}=\{\hat{C}\mid C\in\mathcal{C}\}
. Observe that
\partial(A)
for each
A\in\mathcal{B}

is contained in
M_{i}
for some
i\in\{1,2,3\}
.
Let
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2},\mathcal{B}_{3})
be
the tripartition of
\mathcal{B}
obtained by putting each
A\in\mathcal{B}
to

\mathcal{B}_{i}
where
i
is the smallest such that
\partial(A)\subseteq M_{i}
.
Then,
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
is a mid-triple of
\mathcal{B}
conforming to the tripartition

(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2},\mathcal{B}_{3})
. As the order of this mid-triple is at most
k
,
the order of the root derivation
\mathcal{B}
is at most
k
.
Each block in
\mathcal{B}_{i}
is small since
E_{i}
is small. Finally, for each
A\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
,

\mathrm{bw}(A)\leq k
holds, since a branch-decomposition of
E(A)
of width at most
k
can
be obtained from the branch-decomposition of
E_{i}
of width at most
k
by removing
leaves that map to edges not in
E(A)
.
∎

Algorithm
2
shows a pseudo-code of our alternative algorithm.

Input:

A graph
G
and an integer
k\geq 0
.

Compute the set
\mathcal{F}_{k}(G)
of all small blocks
A
of
G
such that
\mathrm{bw}(E(A))\leq k
, using the method of Algorithm
1
;

1

Generate root derivations
\mathcal{B}
of
G
such that
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{k}(G)

and

;

2

for

\mathcal{B}
generated

do

3

if

the order of
\mathcal{B}
is at most

4

Answer YES and halts;

5

Answer NO;

6

Algorithm 2

Decides if
\mathrm{bw}(G)\leq k
.

The correctness of this algorithm follows straightforwardly from
\cref
lem:root-deriv.
We conjecture that this algorithm can be implemented in such a way to run in time
smaller than the upper bound given in
\cref
thm:bw-graph on Algorithm
1
.
The following is a partial analysis of the algorithm that provides some grounds for the conjecture.

1.

It is intuitively clear that the running time of generating
\mathcal{F}_{k}(G)
is much smaller than that of
Algorithm
1
, since we compute the branchwidth of small blocks only.
Indeed, it is not difficult to show that the running time of this step is in
\mathcal{O}^{*}(2.88^{n})
.

2.

For each
S\subseteq V(G)
, let
\mathcal{C}(S)
denote the set of components of

G[V(G)\setminus S]
and let
\mathcal{B}(S)=\{\hat{C}\mid C\in\mathcal{C}(S)\}
.
Let
\mathcal{S}_{k}(G)
denote the family of vertex sets
S
of
G
such that

|S|\leq 3k/2
and, for every
A\in\mathcal{B}(S)
,
A
is small and
\mathrm{bw}(A)\leq k
.
Step 2 of the algorithm looks for
S\in\mathcal{S}_{k}(G)
such that the order of
the root derivation
\mathcal{B}(S)
is at most
k
. Let
Q(S)
be a hypergraph on
S
obtained from
G[S]
by adding
\partial(A)
for every
A\in\mathcal{B}(S)

as a hyperedge. Then, deciding if the order of
\mathcal{B}(S)
is at most
k

is equivalent to deciding if
\mathrm{bw}(Q)\leq k
. We can use our algorithm given in
\cref
thm:main-hg
spending
\mathcal{O}^{*}(4^{|S|})
time. We can also solve this problem in
\mathcal{O}^{*}(3^{|\mathcal{B}(S)|})
time, deciding, for
each tripartition of
\mathcal{B}(S)
, if there is a mid-triple of order at most
k
that conforms to the tripartition.
Overall, the running time of the remaining steps is upper bounded by

\mathcal{O}^{*}\left(\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{k}(G)}\min\{4^{|S|},3^{|\mathcal{B}(S)|}\}\right)
. Although the only upper bound we have
on
|\mathcal{B}(S)|
is the trivial
n-|S|
, this bound corresponds to an extremely rare case where
every component in
\mathcal{C}(S)
is a singleton. It would not be surprising if there is some useful
combinatorial bound on the above sum.
We also note that, as
k\geq 2|V(Q)|/3
, deciding if the branchwidth of
Q
is at most
k
might admit an
algorithm that is faster than algorithms for general hypergraphs.
This possibility would be a fascinating research topic. Positive results could lead to a
proof of the conjecture as well as to a dramatically improved practical performance of Algorithm
2
.
