1.
Introduction

Affine convex geometry studies geometric quantities and operators that behave naturally under non-singular linear transformations and, more generally, under affine maps. Over the past several decades, the subject has developed into a central branch of convex geometry, with deep connections to the Brunn–Minkowski theory, geometric measure theory, asymptotic geometric analysis, functional inequalities, and probability, etc. A fundamental phenomenon throughout this theory is that ellipsoids play the role of extremizers and rigidity objects, and therefore the characterization of ellipsoids through extremal geometric inequalities has been a driving force in modern convex geometry. Typical examples include the Blaschke–Santaló inequality, which maximizes the volume product; the affine isoperimetric inequality, which controls the affine surface area; the Petty projection inequality, which relates the volume of a convex body to that of its polar projection body; and the Busemann–Petty centroid inequality, which compares a convex body with its centroid body, etc. These inequalities, together with their
L_{p}
and Orlicz extensions, form the cornerstone of the modern Brunn–Minkowski theory. Consequently, understanding the mechanisms that force ellipsoidal symmetry from analytic identities, variational conditions, or invariance properties is a fundamental problem in the field; see, e.g.,

A major challenge in proving Theorem

The first difficulty is the absence of a suitable lower-dimensional representation formula for

\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K).

In the intersection-body setting, Milman, Shabelman, and Yehudayoff exploited an integral representation involving sections of the body and radial perturbations. No analogous formula is available a priori for the

-projection body

-Projection Rolodex

L_{n-1,u,p}(K)=\left\{(E,x):E\in G_{u^{\perp},n-2},\;x\in L_{E,p}(K)\right\},

see Definition
3.3

established in Lemma
3.1

3.3

The second difficulty concerns admissible perturbations. Since linear reflection shadow systems deform the support function rather than the radial function, the perturbation theory developed in
[
34
]
cannot be applied directly.
In Proposition
4.1

The third difficulty is establishing convexity along the shadow system. For the classical projection body (
p=1

h_{K}^{\,1-p}

appearing in the

To overcome this problem, we prove in Lemma

M_{n-1}\!\left(L_{E,p}(K_{t})\right)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\,n-2}|L_{E,p,u,s}(K_{t})|\,ds\right)^{-1/q},

where

The resulting convexity theorem, proved in Section

The final difficulty is the first-variation analysis of

\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K_{t}).

After establishing admissibility of the support perturbation, we derive a variational formula for the volume of the polar
L_{p}
-projection body. A crucial step is Lemma

and

\Gamma_{p}\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K=cK,

two natural variation terms cancel:
the variation of the support function against the corresponding surface area measure and the variation of the surface area measure against the support function.
These cancellations imply that

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=1^{-}}\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K_{t})=0.

Combining this vanishing first variation with the convexity, we deduce in Lemma

\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K_{t})

is constant along the entire shadow system. Consequently, we have

\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K)=\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi_{p}^{\ast}S_{u}K)

for all
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Invoking the equality characterization in Lemma

must be an ellipsoid. This completes the proof of Theorem

The

(2.2)

The support function uniquely determines the convex body

For

\rho_{K}(v):=\max\{\lambda\geq 0:\lambda v\in K\},\qquad v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Equivalently,

The radial function is positively homogeneous of degree

\rho_{K}(\lambda v)=\lambda^{-1}\rho_{K}(v),\qquad\lambda>0.

For

K^{\ast}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\;\langle x,y\rangle\leq 1,\;\text{for all}\;y\in K\}.

If

(2.5)

h_{K^{\ast}}(v)=\frac{1}{\rho_{K}(v)},\ v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

The volume of

can be expressed in terms of the radial function and the support function as

(2.6)

\textrm{Vol}_{n}(K)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(v)^{n}\,dv=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{K}(v)dS(K,v).

Here, the
surface area measure

S(K,\cdot)

(2.7)

for any Borel sets

denotes the inverse Gauss image of

.
It satisfies

(2.8)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\varphi(v)\,dS(K,v)=\int_{\partial K}\varphi(\nu^{K}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

for any continuous
\varphi:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
.

The

Using the

2.2.
Shadow Systems

Shadow systems were introduced by Rogers and Shephard and have become a fundamental tool in convex geometry for studying affine invariant functionals and rigidity phenomena; see, e.g.,
[
4
,
41
,
10
,
43
]
.

For
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, denote
T^{u}_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
the projection onto
\mathbb{R}^{n}
parallel to the direction
e_{n+1}+tu
, which is non-orthogonal whenever
t\neq 0
. A family of convex compact sets
\{K_{u}(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is called a
shadow system
in the direction of
u
if there exists a compact convex set
\tilde{K}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
(compact convex sets in
(n+1)
-dimensional space) such that

K_{u}(t)=T^{u}_{t}(\tilde{K})

for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
It is well known that each
K_{u}(t)
is a convex body and that the orthogonal projection of
K_{u}(t)
onto
u^{\perp}
is independent of
t
. Geometrically, the family
\{K_{u}(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
can be viewed as a deformation obtained by moving points parallel to a fixed direction while preserving the shadow on the hyperplane
u^{\perp}
.

One of the principal reasons for the usefulness of shadow systems is that many geometric quantities possess remarkable convexity, concavity, or monotonicity properties along such deformations. Classical examples include the volume of polar bodies, volume products, projection-body functionals, affine surface areas, and various affine-invariant energies. These properties have led to powerful proofs of numerous affine isoperimetric inequalities and extremal characterizations of ellipsoids. In many instances, the corresponding convexity or monotonicity can be deduced from the Brunn–Minkowski inequality and its variants, while more refined results follow from functional analogues such as the Prékopa–Leindler inequality.

In this work, we focus on the
linear reflection shadow system
introduced by Shephard, constructed from
K
and
R_{u}K
, where
R_{u}
denotes reflection about
u^{\perp}
for
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. The identity
P_{u^{\perp}}R_{u}K=P_{u^{\perp}}K
ensures the existence of a linear shadow system
\{K_{u}(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
in the direction of
u
satisfying
K_{u}(1)=K
and
K_{u}(-1)=R_{u}K
.
More precisely, for each
y\in u^{\perp}
, the section of
K_{u}(t)
in the direction of
u
is defined by

(2.15)

(K_{u}(t))(y):=\frac{1+t}{2}K(y)+\frac{1-t}{2}(R_{u}K)(y),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

where
K(y)
denotes the one-dimensional section of
K
in the direction of
u
over
y\in u^{\perp}
.
We usually omit the subscript
u
and simplify
K_{u}(t)
to
K_{t}
for fixed direction
u
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
35
, Lemma 2.1]

Given a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a direction
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
\{K_{u}(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
(or
K_{t}
for convenience) defined by (
2.15
) is a linear shadow system of convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the following properties:

•

K_{1}=K
and
K_{-1}=R_{u}K
.

•

R_{u}(K_{t})=K_{-t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

•

K_{0}=S_{u}K
.

•

\textrm{Vol}_{n}(K_{t})=\textrm{Vol}_{n}(K)
for all
t\in[-1,1]
.

•

\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto K_{t}
is continuous in the Hausdorff metric.

The volume-preserving property and continuity established above will be used repeatedly in the sequel. In particular, they allow us to study geometric quantities associated with
K_{t}
through variational arguments while keeping the underlying volume fixed.

To analyze the behavior of shadow systems, it is convenient to represent convex bodies through upper and lower graph functions. This description will later allow us to express support functions, surface area measures, and variational quantities in a form suitable for computation.
For
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
, recall that
K_{u}^{\prime}
is the orthogonal projection of
K
onto
u^{\perp}
. Let

f(x^{\prime}):=\sup\{s\in\mathbb{R}:\;x^{\prime}+su\in K\},\;\;x^{\prime}\in K_{u}^{\prime}

and

g(x^{\prime}):=\inf\{s\in\mathbb{R}:\;x^{\prime}+su\in K\},\;\;x^{\prime}\in K_{u}^{\prime}

denote the
upper graph function
and
lower graph function
of
K
on
x^{\prime}\in K_{u}^{\prime}
. It follows that
f(x^{\prime})
is a concave function and
g(x^{\prime})
is a convex function defined on
K_{u}^{\prime}
and

K=\{x^{\prime}+su:\;x^{\prime}\in K_{u}^{\prime},\;g(x^{\prime})\leq s\leq f(x^{\prime})\}.

By (
2.15
), the upper graph function and the lower graph function of its linear reflection shadow system
K_{t}
are given as follows:

(2.16)

f_{t}(x^{\prime})=\frac{t}{2}(f(x^{\prime})+g(x^{\prime}))+\frac{1}{2}(f(x^{\prime})-g(x^{\prime}))

and

(2.17)

g_{t}(x^{\prime})=\frac{t}{2}(f(x^{\prime})+g(x^{\prime}))-\frac{1}{2}(f(x^{\prime})-g(x^{\prime})).

Then

(2.18)

K_{t}=\left\{x^{\prime}+su:\;x^{\prime}\in K_{u}^{\prime},\;g_{t}(x^{\prime})\leq s\leq f_{t}(x^{\prime})\right\}.

The concepts and notation introduced in this section will be used throughout the paper, providing the analytic and geometric framework needed to reformulate the
L_{p}
-projection body in a form suitable for variational analysis.

Linear reflection shadow systems
indeed serve as the main tool for studying the operator
K\mapsto\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K
. By combining the the convexity properties of the fiberwise functionals with the first variation formula, we show that the volume
\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K_{t})
for
p>1
remains constant along the shadow system under the fixed-point condition.

3.
The
L_{p}
-Projection Rolodex

In this section, we aim to introduce the
L_{p}
-Projection Rolodex
, which provides the geometric framework underlying our analysis of the
polar
L_{p}
-projection body
. Unlike the classical projection body, the
L_{p}
-projection body
depends on the weighted measure
h_{K}^{1-p}dS(K,\cdot)
and therefore exhibits a genuinely nonlocal structure. The main difficulty is to recover a lower-dimensional representation despite this loss of localization. By exploiting graph representations of convex bodies and their associated normal vectors, we construct the
L_{p}
-Projection Rolodex and derive the integral formulas that will be used throughly.

Our first objective is to isolate the lower-dimensional geometric structure hidden in the definition of the
L_{p}
-projection body. The following quantity serves as the basic building block for subsequent construction.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
p>1
,
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
and
E\in G_{n,n-2}
(Grassmannian manifold of all
n-2
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
). The
L_{E,p}
projection of
K
with respect to
E
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined as follows,

(3.19)

\left|P_{E\wedge x,p}K\right|:=\|P_{E^{\perp}}x\|\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\langle v(E,x),v\rangle\right|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}dS(K,v)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where

v(E,x)
is the unit vector perpendicular to the
(n-1)
-dimensional hyperplane
\text{span}\{x,E\}
(the linear span of
x
and
E
).

In fact, if
p=1
, it matches the definition of
|P_{E\wedge x}K|
in
[
35
, (3.1)]
when
k=n-1
.
In fact,

(3.20)

\left|P_{E\wedge x,p}K\right|=\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\langle J(P_{E^{\perp}}x),v\rangle\right|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}dS(K,v)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
J(P_{E^{\perp}}x)
denotes a vector in
E^{\perp}
such that
\|J(P_{E^{\perp}}x)\|=\|P_{E^{\perp}}x\|
and
\langle J(P_{E^{\perp}}x),P_{E^{\perp}}x\rangle=0
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
J(P_{E^{\perp}}x)
denotes the
\pi/2
counterclockwise rotation of
P_{E^{\perp}}x
in
E^{\perp}
throughout the paper. Therefore, it is linear, i.e.,

(3.21)

J(P_{E^{\perp}}(ax+by))=aJ(P_{E^{\perp}}x)+bJ(P_{E^{\perp}}y)

for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.

In this paper, we equip the Grassmannian manifold
G_{n,n-2}
with the metric

d_{G}(E,F)=\|P_{E}-P_{F}\|_{G},

where
P_{E}
and
P_{F}
denote the orthogonal projections onto

E
and
F
, respectively, and
\|\cdot\|_{G}
denotes the operator norm.
The Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is endowed with its usual metric

d_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(x,y)=\|x-y\|.

We then equip the product space

G_{n,n-2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}

with the product metric

d\bigl((E,x),(F,y)\bigr)=d_{G}(E,F)+\|x-y\|.

This metric induces the topology of the product in

G_{n,n-2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular,

(E_{j},x_{j})\to(E,x)

if and only if

d_{G}(E_{j},E)\to 0

and

\|x_{j}-x\|\to 0.

Equivalently,

(E_{j},x_{j})\to(E,x)

if and only if

\|P_{E_{j}}-P_{E}\|\to 0
and
\|x_{j}-x\|\to 0.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
p>1
and
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
,
the mapping

G_{n,n-2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni(E,x)\mapsto|P_{E\wedge x,p}K|\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

is continuous.

Proof.

Let any convergent sequence
(E_{j},x_{j})\to(E,x)
in
G_{n,n-2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We aim to prove that

|P_{E_{j}\wedge x_{j},p}K|\to|P_{E\wedge x,p}K|.

For
E\in G_{n,n-2}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

P_{E^{\perp}}x=x-P_{E}x
. Thus, for
E,F\in G_{n,n-2}
,

\|P_{E^{\perp}}-P_{F^{\perp}}\|_{G}=\|P_{E}-P_{F}\|_{G}.

Since

\|P_{E_{j}^{\perp}}-P_{E^{\perp}}\|_{G}=\|P_{E_{j}}-P_{E}\|_{G}\to 0

and
x_{j}\to x
, we have

P_{E_{j}^{\perp}}x_{j}\to P_{E^{\perp}}x
.
Indeed,

\displaystyle\|P_{E_{j}^{\perp}}x_{j}-P_{E^{\perp}}x\|

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{E_{j}^{\perp}}(x_{j}-x)\|+\|(P_{E_{j}^{\perp}}-P_{E^{\perp}})x\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{j}-x\|+\|P_{E_{j}^{\perp}}-P_{E^{\perp}}\|_{G}\,\|x\|\to 0.

Choose locally a continuous orientation of the two-dimensional spaces

E^{\perp}
, and let
J_{E}:E^{\perp}\to E^{\perp}
be the rotation of

\pi/2
. Since the formula contains an absolute value, changing the
orientation replaces
J_{E}
by
-J_{E}
, and therefore does not change

|P_{E\wedge x,p}K|
. Hence locally we may assume that

J_{E_{j}}P_{E_{j}^{\perp}}x_{j}\to J_{E}P_{E^{\perp}}x.

By the formula

|P_{E\wedge x,p}K|=\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\langle J_{E}P_{E^{\perp}}x,v\rangle|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}\,dS(K,v)\right)^{1/p},

we obtain pointwise, for every
v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,

|\langle J_{E_{j}}P_{E_{j}^{\perp}}x_{j},v\rangle|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}\to|\langle J_{E}P_{E^{\perp}}x,v\rangle|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}.

It remains to justify passing the limit through the integral. Since

K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
, there exists
r>0
such that

rB_{2}^{n}\subset K.

Therefore

h_{K}(v)\geq r

for all
v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Because
p>1
, we have
1-p\leq 0
, and hence

h_{K}(v)^{1-p}\leq r^{1-p}.

Moreover, since
x_{j}\to x
, there exists
M>0
such that

\|x_{j}\|\leq M

for all
j
.
Since
P_{E_{j}^{\perp}}
is an orthogonal projection and
J_{E_{j}}
is an isometry on

E_{j}^{\perp}
, we have

\|J_{E_{j}}P_{E_{j}^{\perp}}x_{j}\|=\|P_{E_{j}^{\perp}}x_{j}\|\leq\|x_{j}\|\leq M.

Thus, for every
v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,

|\langle J_{E_{j}}P_{E_{j}^{\perp}}x_{j},v\rangle|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}\leq M^{p}r^{1-p}.

The right-hand side is integrable with respect to the finite surface area
measure
S(K,\cdot)
. Hence, the dominated convergence theorem gives

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\langle J_{E_{j}}P_{E_{j}^{\perp}}x_{j},v\rangle|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}\,dS(K,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\langle J_{E}P_{E^{\perp}}x,v\rangle|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}\,dS(K,v).

Finally, since
t\mapsto t^{1/p}
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
, it follows
that

|P_{E_{j}\wedge x_{j},p}K|\to|P_{E\wedge x,p}K|
.
Therefore, the mapping

G_{n,n-2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni(E,x)\mapsto|P_{E\wedge x,p}K|

is continuous.
∎

The continuity established above ensures that the geometric objects introduced below possess the necessary topological regularity. This will allow us to regard the resulting constructions as genuine convex bodies and to apply compactness arguments when needed.
In the following, we will focus on the case when
x\in E^{\perp}.
Using the quantity
|P_{E\wedge x,p}K|
, we now define a two-dimensional convex body associated with each
(n-2)
-dimensional subspace. These bodies will play the role of fibers in Rolodex construction.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
,
G_{n,n-2}
be the Grassmann manifold, and
E\in G_{n,n-2}
. Denote
E^{\perp}
the orthogonal complement of
E
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(satisfying
\dim E+\dim E^{\perp}=n
, hence
\dim E^{\perp}=2
). For
p\geq 1
, we define the
L_{E,p}
-projection polar body of
K
,
L_{E,p}(K)\subset E^{\perp}
by

(3.22)

L_{E,p}(K):=\left\{x\in E^{\perp}\,:\,\|x\|\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\langle v(E,x),v\rangle\right|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}dS(K,v)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq 1\right\}.

Remark 3.1
.

The body
L_{E,p}(K)
can be viewed as the natural
L_{p}
analog of the
E
-projected polar body introduced in
[
35
]
. It captures the contribution of the
L_{p}
-projection body in the two-dimensional space
E^{\perp}
and serves as the fundamental fiber in the construction of the
L_{p}
-Projection Rolodex.

By Definition
3.1
and Definition
3.2
, we have

(3.23)

L_{E,p}(K)=\left\{x\in E^{\perp}\,:\,\left|P_{E\wedge x,p}K\right|\leq 1\right\}.

Analogously to
[
35
, Lemma 3.4]
, we establish the following
L_{p}
extension.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
and
E\in G_{n,n-2}
. Then
L_{E,p}(K)\in\mathcal{K}_{e}^{2}
for
p>1
is an origin-symmetric convex body in the
2
-dimensional Euclidean space
E^{\perp}
.

Proof.

For any
x,y\in E^{\perp}
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
, by (
3.20
), (
3.21
) and Minkowski inequality for
p>1
,

(3.24)

\displaystyle\left|P_{E\wedge((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y),p}(K)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\langle J((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y),v\rangle\right|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}dS(K,v)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\langle(1-\lambda)Jx+\lambda Jy,v\rangle\right|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}dS(K,v)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\langle Jx,v\rangle\right|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}dS(K,v)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle+\lambda\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\langle Jy,v\rangle\right|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}dS(K,v)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)\left|P_{E\wedge x,p}(K)\right|+\lambda\left|P_{E\wedge y,p}(K)\right|.

It follows from (
3.23
) that if
x,y\in L_{E,p}(K)
, then
\left|P_{E\wedge x,p}K\right|\leq 1
and
\left|P_{E\wedge y,p}K\right|\leq 1
. Based on (
3.24
), we can easily deduce that

\left|P_{E\wedge(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y),p}K\right|\leq 1,

which implies that
\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y\in L_{E,p}(K)
.
Therefore,
L_{E,p}(K)
is a convex set in the 2-dimensional Euclidean space
E^{\perp}
.

It remains to show that
L_{E,p}(K)
is compact and has nonempty interior to be a convex body.

Recall that

\left|P_{E\wedge x,p}K\right|=\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\langle Jx,v\rangle|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}\,dS(K,v)\right)^{1/p},\qquad x\in E^{\perp}

and

L_{E,p}(K)=\{x\in E^{\perp}:\left|P_{E\wedge x,p}K\right|\leq 1\}.

By Lemma
3.1
, the map
\left|P_{E\wedge x,p}K\right|:E^{\perp}\to[0,\infty)
is continuous, which means that

L_{E,p}(K)
is a closed set.

We next prove boundedness of
L_{E,p}(K)
. Since
J:E^{\perp}\to E^{\perp}
is a rotation, we have

\|Jx\|=\|x\|.

For
x\neq 0
, set

w=\frac{Jx}{\|x\|}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap E^{\perp}.

Then

\left|P_{E\wedge x,p}K\right|^{p}=\|x\|^{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\langle w,v\rangle|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}\,dS(K,v).

Define

m_{E}:=\min_{w\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap E^{\perp}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\langle w,v\rangle|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}\,dS(K,v).

The function inside the minimum is continuous in
w
, and

\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap E^{\perp}
is compact. Thus the minimum exists.

We claim that
m_{E}>0
. Indeed, if
m_{E}=0
, then for some

w_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap E^{\perp}
,

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\langle w_{0},v\rangle|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}\,dS(K,v)=0.

Since
h_{K}(v)>0
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, this implies

|\langle w_{0},v\rangle|=0\quad\text{for }S(K,\cdot)\text{-almost every }v.

Hence the surface area measure
S(K,\cdot)
is supported in the great sphere

\{v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\langle w_{0},v\rangle=0\}.

This is impossible for a convex body
K
with nonempty interior, since the surface
area measure of a full-dimensional convex body is not supported on any closed
hemisphere, and hence not on any proper great sphere. Therefore
m_{E}>0
.

Consequently,

\left|P_{E\wedge x,p}K\right|^{p}\geq m_{E}\|x\|^{p},

and so

\left|P_{E\wedge x,p}K\right|\geq m_{E}^{1/p}\|x\|.

If
x\in L_{E,p}(K)
, then
\left|P_{E\wedge x,p}K\right|\leq 1
. Hence

\|x\|\leq m_{E}^{-1/p}<\infty.

Thus
L_{E,p}(K)
is bounded. Since it is also closed in the finite-dimensional
space
E^{\perp}
, it is compact.

Finally, we show that
L_{E,p}(K)
has nonempty interior. Since
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
,
there exists
r>0
such that

rB_{2}^{n}\subset K.

Therefore

h_{K}(v)\geq r\qquad\text{for all }v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Also, since
S(K,\mathbb{S}^{n-1})<\infty
, for every
x\in E^{\perp}
,

\displaystyle\left|P_{E\wedge x,p}K\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\langle Jx,v\rangle|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}\,dS(K,v)

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|^{p}r^{1-p}S(K,\mathbb{S}^{n-1}).

Hence

\left|P_{E\wedge x,p}K\right|\leq\|x\|\left(r^{1-p}S(K,\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\right)^{1/p}.

Thus, if

\|x\|\leq\left(r^{1-p}S(K,\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\right)^{-1/p},

then
\left|P_{E\wedge x,p}K\right|\leq 1
. Therefore,
L_{E,p}(K)
contains a Euclidean ball centered
at the origin in
E^{\perp}
. Hence
o\in\operatorname{int}_{E^{\perp}}L_{E,p}(K)
.

Moreover, it is obvious that
Jx=-J(-x)
for
x\in E^{\perp}
due to (
3.21
) for
a=-1,b=0
, which further leads to

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\langle Jx,v\rangle\right|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}dS(K,v)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\langle J(-x),v\rangle\right|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}dS(K,v).

Combining convexity, origin symmetry, compactness, and nonempty interior, we conclude
that
L_{E,p}(K)
is an origin-symmetric convex body in the two-dimensional
Euclidean space
E^{\perp}
.
∎

Next we establish an identity formula for the quantity
\left|P_{E\wedge x,p}K\right|
in terms of the graph functions of the convex body
K.

In the following, the subsequent sections of this article are all conducted within the context of the Cartesian coordinate system in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
established in this manner when
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is fixed and
E\in G_{u^{\perp},n-2}
(all
(n-2)
-dimensional subspaces inside the hyperplane
u^{\perp}
). First, establish a Cartesian coordinate system in the
(n-2)
-dimensional linear subspace
E
, with the
u
direction as the
n
-th coordinate direction, and
E^{\perp}\cap u^{\perp}
as the
(n-1)
-th coordinate direction.

A key ingredient in the construction of the
L_{p}
-Projection Rolodex is an explicit formula for
|P_{E\wedge x,p}K|
in terms of the graph representation of
K
. The following result provides precisely such a representation. Specially, substituting
x
by
y+su
in (
3.19
), we obtain the Lemma as follows.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
be a fixed direction and
p>1
. Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
be of class
C_{+}^{2}
,
f
and
g
be the upper graph function and lower graph function of
K
with respect to
u
.
Then, for arbitrary fixed
E\in G_{u^{\perp},n-2}
and
y\in u^{\perp}\cap E^{\perp}
,

(3.25)

\displaystyle\left|P_{E\wedge(y+su),p}K\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{K_{u}^{\prime}}\left|\langle(-s,y),\left(-\nabla_{n-1}f(x^{\prime}),1\right)\rangle\right|^{p}\langle f\rangle(x^{\prime})^{1-p}dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{K_{u}^{\prime}}\left|\langle(-s,y),\left(\nabla_{n-1}g(x^{\prime}),-1\right)\rangle\right|^{p}\langle-g\rangle(x^{\prime})^{1-p}dx^{\prime},

where
\nabla f
denotes the
(n-1)
-dimensional gradient vector of
f
,
\nabla_{n-1}f
denotes the
(n-1)
-th component of
\nabla f
, and

\langle f\rangle(x^{\prime}):=f(x^{\prime})-\langle x^{\prime},\nabla f(x^{\prime})\rangle
.

Proof.

First, it follows from (
2.8
) and (
3.20
) that

(3.26)

\displaystyle\left|P_{E\wedge(y+su),p}K\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\langle J(y+su),v\rangle\right|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}dS(K,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}\left|\langle J(y+su),\nu^{K}(x)\rangle\right|^{p}\langle x,\nu^{K}(x)\rangle^{1-p}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

Since
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
is of class
C_{+}^{2}
, for
x=(x^{\prime},f(x^{\prime}))\in\partial^{+}K
, the outer unit normal vector of
K
at
x
,

\nu^{K}(x)=\frac{\left(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1\right)}{\sqrt{1+\|\nabla f(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}}.

Thus

\langle x,\nu^{K}(x)\rangle=\frac{f(x^{\prime})-\langle x^{\prime},\nabla f(x^{\prime})\rangle}{\sqrt{1+\|\nabla f(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}}=\frac{\langle f\rangle(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1+\|\nabla f(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}}.

Similarly, if
x=(x^{\prime},g(x^{\prime}))\in\partial^{-}K
, then

\nu^{K}(x)=\frac{\left(\nabla g(x^{\prime}),-1\right)}{\sqrt{1+\|\nabla g(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}}.

And we obtain

\langle x,\nu^{K}(x)\rangle=\frac{-g(x^{\prime})+x^{\prime}\cdot\nabla g(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1+\|\nabla g(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}}=\frac{\langle-g\rangle(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1+\|\nabla g(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}}.

In fact, by the coordinate system we built,
y+su=(0,\dots,0,y,s)
, and the vector
J(y+su)=(0,\dots,0,-s,y)
since we assume that
Jx
denotes the
90^{\circ}
counterclockwise rotation of
x
in
E^{\perp}
.
By (
3.26
) and the above equalities, we have

\displaystyle\left|P_{E\wedge(y+su),p}K\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\langle J(y+su),v\rangle\right|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}dS(K,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{+}K}\left|\langle(-s,y),\left(-\nabla_{n-1}f(x^{\prime}),1\right)\rangle\right|^{p}\langle f\rangle(x^{\prime})^{1-p}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\|\nabla f(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!+\int_{\partial^{-}K}\left|\langle(-s,y),\left(\nabla_{n-1}g(x^{\prime}),-1\right)\rangle\right|^{p}\langle-g\rangle(x^{\prime})^{1-p}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\|\nabla g(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{K_{u}^{\prime}}\left|\langle(-s,y),\left(-\nabla_{n-1}f(x^{\prime}),1\right)\rangle\right|^{p}\langle f\rangle(x^{\prime})^{1-p}dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!+\int_{K_{u}^{\prime}}\left|\langle(-s,y),\left(\nabla_{n-1}g(x^{\prime}),-1\right)\rangle\right|^{p}\langle-g\rangle(x^{\prime})^{1-p}dx^{\prime},

based on the measure identities

d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=\sqrt{1+\|\nabla f(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}dx^{\prime}\;\text{for}\;x\in\partial^{+}K,

and

d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=\sqrt{1+\|\nabla g(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}dx^{\prime}\;\text{for}\;x\in\partial^{-}K,

completing the proof.
∎

This representation formula reveals the fiberwise structure underlying the
L_{p}
-projection body and serves as the starting point for the convexity and monotonicity arguments developed later.

Recall that we assume
E\in G_{u^{\perp},n-2}
. It will be useful to introduce the following notation for given
s\in\mathbb{R}
:

(3.27)

L_{E,p,u,s}(K):=\left\{y\in E^{\perp}\cap u^{\perp}:\;\left|P_{E\wedge(y+su),p}K\right|\leq 1\right\}.

Note that
L_{E,p,u,s}(K)
is the section of
L_{E,p}(K)
perpendicular to
u
at height
s
, and therefore it is a line segment in the one-dimensional subspace
E^{\perp}\cap u^{\perp}
by Lemma
3.2
. Let
|L_{E,p,u,s}(K)|
(or
\textrm{Vol}_{1}(L_{E,p,u,s}(K))
) denote the length of the line segment
L_{E,p,u,s}(K)
. Furthermore, by Brunn’s concavity principle for convex bodies, the map
\mathbb{R}\ni s\mapsto|L_{E,p,u,s}(K)|
is concave on its support, and so in particular
\mathbb{R}\ni s\mapsto|L_{E,p,u,s}(K)|
is measurable.

We denote by
V_{n-1,u}
the following vector bundle over
G_{u^{\perp},n-2}
:

V_{n-1,u}:=\left\{(E,x)\mid E\in G_{u^{\perp},n-2},\;x\in E^{\perp}\right\},

equipped with the subspace topology as a closed subset of
G_{u^{\perp},n-2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Given
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
, the mapping:

V_{n-1,u}\ni(E,x)\mapsto|P_{E\wedge x,p}K|\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

is continuous as the restriction of the continuous mapping from Lemma
3.1
and hence its sublevel set

\left\{(E,x)\in V_{n-1,u}|\;\mid P_{E\wedge x,p}K|\leq 1\right\}=\left\{(E,x)\in V_{n-1,u}\mid E\in G_{u^{\perp},n-2},\;x\in L_{E,p}(K)\right\}

is a closed subset of
V_{n-1,u}
. When
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
, this subset is bounded and hence compact.

We are now ready to assemble the individual fibers into a single geometric object encoding the information of the
L_{p}
-projection body
cross all admissible directions.

Definition 3.3
.

Given
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
and
p>1
, we call the closed subset

L_{n-1,u,p}(K):=\left\{(E,x)\mid E\in G_{u^{\perp},n-2},x\in L_{E,p}(K)\right\}\subset V_{n-1,u}

the
L_{p}
-Projection Rolodex of
K
relative to
u^{\perp}
.

The Rolodex representation developed here indeed provides a powerful framework for analyzing the
L_{p}
-projection body through its lower-dimensional sections.

4.
Admissible Support Perturbations

In order to exploit the
L_{p}
-projection body in a variational setting, it is necessary to understand how the support function behaves under perturbations.
We therefore turn to the construction of admissible perturbations along shadow systems. A key step is to establish differentiability of the support function, which requires a careful analysis of the relationship between boundary points and their corresponding outer normals.

The original paper in
[
34
]
studies intersection bodies, where perturbations of the radial function are considered. By contrast, our work focuses on projection bodies, so we need to consider perturbations of the support function. Another difference in the definition here is that it requires for all directions, rather than almost everywhere
to perform a variational analysis of the quantities introduced in the previous section, we require a notion of differentiability compatible with shadow deformations.

Definition 4.1

(Admissible support perturbation)
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
. A family of convex bodies
\{K_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
is called an admissible support perturbation if
K_{1}=K
and
\{[0,1]\ni t\mapsto h_{K_{t}}(\theta)\}_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
are equi-differentiable at
t=1^{-}
in the following sense:

(1)

For every
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the following limit exists:

(4.28)

\phi(\theta):=\left.\frac{d}{dt}h_{K_{t}}(\theta)\right|_{t=1^{-}}=\lim_{t\rightarrow 1^{-}}\frac{h_{K_{t}}(\theta)-h_{K}(\theta)}{t-1}.

(2)

As
t\rightarrow 1^{-}
,
\frac{h_{K_{t}}-h_{K}}{t-1}
converges uniformly to
\phi
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, i.e.,

\lim_{t\rightarrow 1^{-}}\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\frac{h_{K_{t}}(\theta)-h_{K}(\theta)}{t-1}-\phi(\theta)\right|=0.

The following lemma shows that the linear reflection shadow system
\{K_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
indeed is an admissible support perturbation.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
be a convex body of class
C^{2}_{+}
, and let

\{K_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be the linear reflection shadow system of
K
in the
direction
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Write

K=\{x^{\prime}+su:\ x^{\prime}\in K_{u}^{\prime},\ g(x^{\prime})\leq s\leq f(x^{\prime})\},

where
f
and
g
are the upper and lower graph functions of
K
in the
direction
u
. Then

K_{t}=\{x+(t-1)a(x)u:\ x\in K\},

where, for
x=x^{\prime}+su\in K
,

a(x):=\frac{f(x^{\prime})+g(x^{\prime})}{2}.

Moreover, there exists a continuous function

\phi:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}

such that

\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\frac{h_{K_{t}}(\theta)-h_{K}(\theta)}{t-1}-\phi(\theta)\right|\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{as }t\to 1^{-}.

Proof.

We divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1.
We prove the representation of
K_{t}
as a perturbation of
K
.
By the definition of the linear reflection shadow system, the upper and lower
graph functions of
K_{t}
are

f_{t}(x^{\prime})=\frac{t}{2}\big(f(x^{\prime})+g(x^{\prime})\big)+\frac{1}{2}\big(f(x^{\prime})-g(x^{\prime})\big)

and

g_{t}(x^{\prime})=\frac{t}{2}\big(f(x^{\prime})+g(x^{\prime})\big)-\frac{1}{2}\big(f(x^{\prime})-g(x^{\prime})\big).

Equivalently,

f_{t}(x^{\prime})=f(x^{\prime})+\frac{t-1}{2}\big(f(x^{\prime})+g(x^{\prime})\big)

and

g_{t}(x^{\prime})=g(x^{\prime})+\frac{t-1}{2}\big(f(x^{\prime})+g(x^{\prime})\big).

For
x=x^{\prime}+su\in K
, define

a(x)=\frac{f(x^{\prime})+g(x^{\prime})}{2}.

Since
x\in K
, we have

g(x^{\prime})\leq s\leq f(x^{\prime}).

Therefore

g(x^{\prime})+(t-1)a(x)\leq s+(t-1)a(x)\leq f(x^{\prime})+(t-1)a(x).

Using the identities above, this becomes

g_{t}(x^{\prime})\leq s+(t-1)a(x)\leq f_{t}(x^{\prime})
.
Hence

x+(t-1)a(x)u=x^{\prime}+\big(s+(t-1)a(x)\big)u\in K_{t}.

Thus

\{x+(t-1)a(x)u:\ x\in K\}\subseteq K_{t}.

Conversely, let
z\in K_{t}
. Then

z=x^{\prime}+s_{t}u

for some
x^{\prime}\in K_{u}^{\prime}
and

g_{t}(x^{\prime})\leq s_{t}\leq f_{t}(x^{\prime}).

Define

s=s_{t}-\frac{t-1}{2}\big(f(x^{\prime})+g(x^{\prime})\big).

Using the formulas for
f_{t}
and
g_{t}
, we obtain

g(x^{\prime})\leq s\leq f(x^{\prime})
.
Thus
x:=x^{\prime}+su\in K
.
Moreover,

z=x^{\prime}+s_{t}u=x^{\prime}+\left(s+\frac{t-1}{2}\big(f(x^{\prime})+g(x^{\prime})\big)\right)u=x+(t-1)a(x)u.

Hence

K_{t}\subseteq\{x+(t-1)a(x)u:\ x\in K\}.

Therefore

(4.29)

K_{t}=\{x+(t-1)a(x)u:\ x\in K\}.

Step 2.
From formula (
4.29
), we can obtain the explicit difference equotient
\frac{h_{K_{t}}(\theta)-h_{K}(\theta)}{t-1}
.
Since
K\in C^{2}_{+}
,
K
is strictly convex. Hence, for every

\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
there exists a unique support point

x_{K}(\theta)\in\partial K

such that

h_{K}(\theta)=\langle x_{K}(\theta),\theta\rangle
.
Moreover, the support-point map

\theta\mapsto x_{K}(\theta)

is continuous on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

For
t\in[0,1]
, by the representation of
K_{t}
just proved,

h_{K_{t}}(\theta)=\sup_{x\in K}\left\langle x+(t-1)a(x)u,\theta\right\rangle.

That is,

h_{K_{t}}(\theta)=\max_{x\in K}\left(\langle x,\theta\rangle+(t-1)a(x)\langle u,\theta\rangle\right).

For convenience, define

F(x,\theta,t)=\langle x,\theta\rangle+(t-1)a(x)\langle u,\theta\rangle.

Then

h_{K_{t}}(\theta)=\max_{x\in K}F(x,\theta,t).

At
t=1
,
F(x,\theta,1)=\langle x,\theta\rangle
.
Thus, the unique maximizer of
F(\cdot,\theta,1)
over
K
is precisely

x_{K}(\theta)
.

We define

\phi(\theta)=a(x_{K}(\theta))\langle u,\theta\rangle.

Since
a
is continuous on
K
and
x_{K}(\theta)
is continuous in
\theta
,
the function
\phi
is continuous on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

For each
t<1
and
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, choose a maximizer

x_{t}(\theta)\in K
such that

h_{K_{t}}(\theta)=F(x_{t}(\theta),\theta,t)
.
Thus,

h_{K_{t}}(\theta)=\langle x_{t}(\theta),\theta\rangle+(t-1)a(x_{t}(\theta))\langle u,\theta\rangle.

Subtracting

h_{K}(\theta)=\langle x_{K}(\theta),\theta\rangle

and dividing by
t-1
, we obtain

(4.30)

\frac{h_{K_{t}}(\theta)-h_{K}(\theta)}{t-1}=a(x_{t}(\theta))\langle u,\theta\rangle+\frac{\langle x_{t}(\theta),\theta\rangle-h_{K}(\theta)}{t-1}.

Step 3.
We now show that

x_{t}(\theta)\to x_{K}(\theta)

pointwise in
\theta
as
t\to 1^{-}
.
Fix
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and take any sequence
t_{j}\to 1^{-}
. Since
K

is compact, the sequence
x_{t_{j}}(\theta)
has a convergent subsequence,
still denoted by
x_{t_{j}}(\theta)
, such that

x_{t_{j}}(\theta)\to y\in K.

Since
x_{t_{j}}(\theta)
maximizes
F(\cdot,\theta,t_{j})
, for every
x\in K
,

F(x_{t_{j}}(\theta),\theta,t_{j})\geq F(x,\theta,t_{j}).

Expanding this inequality gives

\langle x_{t_{j}}(\theta),\theta\rangle+(t_{j}-1)a(x_{t_{j}}(\theta))\langle u,\theta\rangle\geq\langle x,\theta\rangle+(t_{j}-1)a(x)\langle u,\theta\rangle.

Letting
j\to\infty
, and using the continuity of
a
together with

t_{j}-1\to 0
, we obtain

\langle y,\theta\rangle\geq\langle x,\theta\rangle

for every
x\in K
. Therefore
y
is a support point of
K
in the direction

\theta
. Since
K
is strictly convex, this support point is unique, so

y=x_{K}(\theta)
.
Thus every convergent subsequence has the same limit, and hence

x_{t}(\theta)\to x_{K}(\theta)
as
t\to 1^{-}
.

Step 4.
We now show that

x_{t}(\theta)\to x_{K}(\theta)

uniformly in
\theta
as
t\to 1^{-}
. Suppose, to the contrary,
that the convergence is not uniform. Then there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
a sequence
t_{j}\to 1^{-}
, and a sequence
\theta_{j}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
such that

|x_{t_{j}}(\theta_{j})-x_{K}(\theta_{j})|\geq\varepsilon_{0}

for all
j
. Since
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is compact, after passing to a subsequence,
we may assume
\theta_{j}\to\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.
Since
K
is compact, after passing to another subsequence we may assume

x_{t_{j}}(\theta_{j})\to y\in K
.
Using the maximizing property,

F(x_{t_{j}}(\theta_{j}),\theta_{j},t_{j})\geq F(x,\theta_{j},t_{j})

for every
x\in K
. Passing to the limit gives

\langle y,\theta_{0}\rangle\geq\langle x,\theta_{0}\rangle

for every
x\in K
. Hence
y
is the support point of
K
in the direction

\theta_{0}
. By uniqueness,

y=x_{K}(\theta_{0})
.
Since the support-point map
\theta\mapsto x_{K}(\theta)
is continuous,

x_{K}(\theta_{j})\to x_{K}(\theta_{0})
.
Therefore,

|x_{t_{j}}(\theta_{j})-x_{K}(\theta_{j})|\leq|x_{t_{j}}(\theta_{j})-x_{K}(\theta_{0})|+|x_{K}(\theta_{0})-x_{K}(\theta_{j})|\to 0,

which contradicts the choice of
\varepsilon_{0}
. Hence,

(4.31)

\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|x_{t}(\theta)-x_{K}(\theta)|\to 0\qquad\text{as }t\to 1^{-}.

Step 5.
Now we show that
\frac{h_{K_{t}}-h_{K}}{t-1}
converges uniformly to
\phi
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
as
t\rightarrow 1^{-}
. Since
a
is continuous on the compact set
K
, it is uniformly continuous.
Therefore, by (
4.31
), we have

\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|a(x_{t}(\theta))-a(x_{K}(\theta))|\to 0.

Since

|\langle u,\theta\rangle|\leq 1
,
we get

\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|a(x_{t}(\theta))\langle u,\theta\rangle-a(x_{K}(\theta))\langle u,\theta\rangle\right|\to 0.

That is,

(4.32)

a(x_{t}(\theta))\langle u,\theta\rangle\to\phi(\theta)\quad\text{uniformly on }\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

It remains to control the second term in
(
4.30
). Since
x_{t}(\theta)
maximizes

F(\cdot,\theta,t)
,

F(x_{t}(\theta),\theta,t)\geq F(x_{K}(\theta),\theta,t).

Hence,

\langle x_{t}(\theta),\theta\rangle+(t-1)a(x_{t}(\theta))\langle u,\theta\rangle\geq h_{K}(\theta)+(t-1)a(x_{K}(\theta))\langle u,\theta\rangle.

Rearranging gives

\langle x_{t}(\theta),\theta\rangle-h_{K}(\theta)\geq(t-1)\big(a(x_{K}(\theta))-a(x_{t}(\theta))\big)\langle u,\theta\rangle.

On the other hand, since

h_{K}(\theta)=\max_{x\in K}\langle x,\theta\rangle
,
we also have

\langle x_{t}(\theta),\theta\rangle-h_{K}(\theta)\leq 0.

Combining the two inequalities yields

\left|\langle x_{t}(\theta),\theta\rangle-h_{K}(\theta)\right|\leq|t-1|\,\left|a(x_{t}(\theta))-a(x_{K}(\theta))\right|.

Dividing by
|t-1|
gives

\left|\frac{\langle x_{t}(\theta),\theta\rangle-h_{K}(\theta)}{t-1}\right|\leq\left|a(x_{t}(\theta))-a(x_{K}(\theta))\right|.

Taking the supremum over
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and using the uniform
convergence of
a(x_{t}(\theta))
to
a(x_{K}(\theta))
, we obtain

(4.33)

\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\frac{\langle x_{t}(\theta),\theta\rangle-h_{K}(\theta)}{t-1}\right|\to 0.

Finally, combining (
4.30
),
(
4.32
), and (
4.33
), we conclude
that

\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\frac{h_{K_{t}}(\theta)-h_{K}(\theta)}{t-1}-\phi(\theta)\right|\to 0\qquad\text{as }t\to 1^{-}.

This proves the desired uniform convergence.
∎

With the admissible support perturbations now established, we proceed to analyze the property of the
L_{p}
-projection body
along the shadow system.

5.
Monotonicity of
\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}(K_{t}))
along Shadow Systems

In this section we investigate the behavior of the volume of the
polar
L_{p}
-projection body
along linear reflection shadow systems. Our goal is to establish the monotonicity properties of

t\mapsto\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K_{t}),

which play a central role in the proof of the main theorem. The principal difficulty is that the operator
\Pi_{p}^{\ast}
depends on both the support function and the
L_{p}
surface area measure, whose variations must be controlled simultaneously under the shadow deformation.

The following Blaschke-Petkantschin-type formula (from
[
35
, Theorem 3.7]
) decomposes integrals over the Grassmannian
G_{n,k}
into integrals over lower-dimensional Grassmannians
G_{u^{\perp},k-1}
and their orthogonal complements, here
k=1,\dots,n-1
.

Lemma 5.1

(Blaschke-Petkantschin-type formula)
.

Fix
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. There exists a constant
\bar{c}_{n,k}>0
such that for every measurable function
f:G_{n,k}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\bar{c}_{n,k}\int_{G_{n,k}}f(F)\sigma_{n,k}(dF)=\int_{G_{u^{\perp},k-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}(E^{\perp})}f\bigl(\operatorname{span}(E,\theta)\bigr)\bigl|\langle\theta,u\rangle\bigr|^{k-1}d\theta\,\sigma_{u^{\perp},k-1}(dE),

where
\sigma_{n,k}
and
\sigma_{u^{\perp},k-1}
are the uniform Haar probability measures on
G_{n,k}
and
G_{u^{\perp},k-1}
, respectively.

Specifically, for
k=n-1
and
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, we define the following Borel measure on
V_{n-1,u}
:

(5.34)

\mu_{n-1,u}(dE,dx):=\bigl|\langle x,u\rangle\bigr|^{n-2}\mathcal{L}_{E^{\perp}}(dx)\sigma_{u^{\perp},n-2}(dE).

To be slightly more precise,
\mu_{n-1,u}
is obtained as the restriction of the Borel product measure
\sigma_{u^{\perp},n-2}(dE)\otimes\left(\bigl|\langle x,u\rangle\bigr|^{n-2}\mathcal{H}^{2}(dx)\right)
on
G_{u^{\perp},n-2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
to the closed subset
V_{n-1,u}
, and thus defines a measure on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
V_{n-1,u}
.
In Definition
3.3
, for given
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
, the
L_{p}
-Projection Rolodex
of
K
relative to
u^{\perp}
is given by

L_{n-1,u,p}(K):=\left\{(E,x)\mid E\in G_{u^{\perp},n-2},x\in L_{E,p}(K)\right\}\subset V_{n-1,u}.

This implies that
L_{n-1,u,p}(K)
is a closed subset of
V_{n-1,u}
and therefore Borel measurable.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
and
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, there exists a constant
\tilde{c}_{n,p}
such that

(5.35)

\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}K)=\tilde{c}_{n,p}\mu_{n-1,u}\bigl(L_{n-1,u,p}(K)\bigr)=\tilde{c}_{n,p}\int_{G_{u^{\perp},n-2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|s|^{n-2}|L_{E,p,u,s}(K)|ds\,\sigma_{u^{\perp},n-2}(dE).

Proof.

On the one hand, integrating in polar coordinates on
E^{\perp}
and invoking Lemma
5.1
for
k=n-1
and the measure
\mu_{n-1,u}
given in (
5.34
), we obtain:

(5.36)

\displaystyle\mu_{n-1,u}\bigl(L_{n-1,u,p}(K)\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G_{u^{\perp},n-2}}\int_{E^{\perp}}1_{L_{n-1,u,p}(K)}(E,x)|\langle x,u\rangle|^{n-2}\mathcal{L}_{E^{\perp}}(dx)\sigma_{u^{\perp},n-2}(dE)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G_{u^{\perp},n-2}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}(E^{\perp})}\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{L_{n-1,u,p}(K)}(E,r\theta)|\langle r\theta,u\rangle|^{n-2}rdr\,d\theta\,\sigma_{u^{\perp},n-2}(dE)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G_{u^{\perp},n-2}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}(E^{\perp})}|\langle\theta,u\rangle|^{n-2}\int_{0}^{1/|P_{E\wedge\theta,p}K|}r^{n-1}dr\,d\theta\,\sigma_{u^{\perp},n-2}(dE)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{G_{u^{\perp},n-2}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}(E^{\perp})}\frac{1}{|P_{E\wedge\theta,p}K|^{n}}\bigl|\langle\theta,u\rangle\bigr|^{n-2}d\theta\,\sigma_{u^{\perp},n-2}(dE)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{c}_{n,n-1}}{n}\int_{G_{n,n-1}}\frac{1}{|P_{F,p}K|^{n}}\sigma_{n,n-1}(dF)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{c}_{n,n-1}}{n^{2}\omega_{n}}\frac{1}{(n\omega_{n}c_{n-2,p})^{n/p}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho_{\Pi^{\ast}_{p}K}^{n}(v)dv=\frac{\bar{c}_{n,n-1}}{n\omega_{n}}\frac{1}{(n\omega_{n}c_{n-2,p})^{n/p}}\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}K).

Here
F=\operatorname{span}\{E,\theta\}
and
|P_{F,p}K|^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\langle\nu^{F},v\rangle|^{p}h_{K}(v)^{1-p}dS(K,v)
, where
\nu^{F}
denotes the unit outer normal vector of the space
F.

On the other hand, we
evaluate the integral by decomposing each
E^{\perp}
into
\operatorname{span}(u)\oplus(E^{\perp}\cap u^{\perp})
and applying
Fubini’s theorem:

(5.37)

\displaystyle\mu_{n-1,u}(L_{n-1,u,p}(K))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G_{u^{\perp},n-2}}\int_{E^{\perp}}1_{L_{n-1,u,p}(K)}(E,x)|\langle x,u\rangle|^{n-2}\mathcal{L}_{E^{\perp}}(dx)\,\sigma_{u^{\perp},n-2}(dE)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G_{u^{\perp},n-2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{E^{\perp}\cap u^{\perp}}1_{|P_{E\wedge(y+su),p}K|\leq 1}(E,y+su)|\langle y+su,u\rangle|^{n-2}dy\,ds\,\sigma_{u^{\perp},n-2}(dE)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G_{u^{\perp},n-2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|s|^{n-2}\int_{E^{\perp}\cap u^{\perp}}1_{|P_{E\wedge(y+su),p}K|\leq 1}(E,y+su)dy\,ds\,\sigma_{u^{\perp},n-2}(dE)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G_{u^{\perp},n-2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|s|^{n-2}|L_{E,p,u,s}(K)|ds\,\sigma_{u^{\perp},n-2}(dE),

where
L_{E,p,u,s}(K)

6.

7.
Fixed Points of the
\Gamma_{p}\Pi_{p}^{\ast}
Operator

In this final section we combine the main ingredients developed throughout the paper to establish the rigidity of fixed points of the operator

K\mapsto\Gamma_{p}\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K.

The strategy follows the variational framework introduced in the previous sections, the admissible statement. First, we derive a first-variation formula for the volume of
the polar
L_{p}
-projection body
along linear reflection shadow systems and show that the fixed-point condition forces this variation to vanish. We then combine this vanishing property with the convexity of the
L_{p}
-Projection Rolodex
functionals established in Section
6
. The resulting rigidity implies that the volume of the
polar
L_{p}
-projection body
remains constant under Steiner symmetrization. Finally, invoking the equality characterization for the
L_{p}
-projection inequality, we conclude that ellipsoids are the unique fixed points of
\Gamma_{p}\Pi_{p}^{\ast}
up to dilation.

7.1.

\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K_{t})

The first step is to understand the behavior of the functional

along the linear reflection shadow system
\{K_{t}\}_{t\in[-1,1]}

The admissibility results established in Section

then the first variation of
\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K_{t})
at

vanishes. This cancellation is the key variational input in the proof of rigidity and serves as the bridge between the fixed-point condition and the convexity arguments developed later.

The following lemma provides the boundedness estimates required for the subsequent differentiation arguments.

Lemma 7.1
.

Proof.

For any

spherical integral for

\int_{S^{n-1}}|\langle\theta,v\rangle|^{p}h_{K_{t}}^{1-p}(\theta)\,dS(K_{t},\theta)

can be decomposed into two parts by its upper and low graphs:

\int_{S^{n-1}}|\langle\theta,v\rangle|^{p}h_{K_{t}}^{1-p}(\theta)\,dS(K_{t},\theta)=E_{1}+E_{2},

where

E_{1}=\int_{K^{\prime}_{u}}\big|\big\langle v,(-\nabla f_{t}(x^{\prime}),1)\big\rangle\big|^{p}h_{K_{t}}^{1-p}(-\nabla f_{t}(x^{\prime}),1)\,dx^{\prime},

E_{2}=\int_{K^{\prime}_{u}}\big|\big\langle v,(\nabla g_{t}(x^{\prime}),-1)\big\rangle\big|^{p}h_{K_{t}}^{1-p}(\nabla g_{t}(x^{\prime}),-1)\,dx^{\prime}.

Similarly, for
K
itself we have

\int_{S^{n-1}}|\langle\theta,v\rangle|^{p}h_{K}^{1-p}(\theta)\,dS_{K}(\theta)=E_{3}+E_{4},

where

E_{3}=\int_{K^{\prime}_{u}}\big|\big\langle v,(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)\big\rangle\big|^{p}h_{K}^{1-p}(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)\,dx^{\prime},

E_{4}=\int_{K^{\prime}_{u}}\big|\big\langle v,(\nabla g(x^{\prime}),-1)\big\rangle\big|^{p}h_{K}^{1-p}(\nabla g(x^{\prime}),-1)\,dx^{\prime}.

The limit of the difference quotient of the integrands on
E_{1}
and
E_{3}
satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}\frac{\big|\big\langle v,(-\nabla f_{t}(x^{\prime}),1)\big\rangle\big|^{p}h_{K_{t}}^{1-p}(-\nabla f_{t}(x^{\prime}),1)-\big|\big\langle v,(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)\big\rangle\big|^{p}h_{K}^{1-p}(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)}{t-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-p\big|\big\langle v,(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)\big\rangle\big|^{p-1}\text{sgn}\langle v,(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)\rangle\cdot\frac{\nabla f(x^{\prime})+\nabla g(x^{\prime})}{2}\cdot\tilde{v}\cdot h_{K}^{1-p}(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)

\displaystyle+\big|\big\langle v,(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)\big\rangle\big|^{p}(1-p)h_{K}^{-p}(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)\cdot\frac{\langle f\rangle(x^{\prime})+\langle g\rangle(x^{\prime})}{2},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-p\big|\big\langle v,\frac{(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)}{\sqrt{1+\|\nabla f(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}}\big\rangle\big|^{p-1}\text{sgn}\langle v,(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)\rangle\cdot\frac{\nabla f(x^{\prime})+\nabla g(x^{\prime})}{2}\cdot\tilde{v}\cdot h_{K}^{1-p}(\frac{(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)}{\sqrt{1+\|\nabla f(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}})

\displaystyle+\big|\big\langle v,\frac{(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)}{\sqrt{1+\|\nabla f(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}}\big\rangle\big|^{p}(1-p)h_{K}^{-p}(\frac{(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)}{\sqrt{1+\|\nabla f(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}})\cdot\frac{\langle f\rangle(x^{\prime})+\langle g\rangle(x^{\prime})}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}(\|\nabla f\|+\|\nabla g\|)+C_{2}

where constants
0<C_{1},C_{2}<\infty
,
\tilde{v}
denotes the first
n-1
components of
v
and

\Bigg|p\big|\big\langle v,\frac{(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)}{\sqrt{1+\|\nabla f(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}}\big\rangle\big|^{p-1}\text{sgn}\langle v,(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)\rangle\cdot h_{K}^{1-p}(\frac{(-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),1)}{\sqrt{1+\|\nabla f(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}})\Bigg|\leq C_{1}

and

\langle f\rangle,\langle g\rangle

are bounded above by
\max_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{K}(u)<\infty.

The terms in the limit are all bounded as the integrals satisfy

\displaystyle\int_{K^{\prime}_{u}}(\|\nabla f(x^{\prime})\|+\|\nabla g(x^{\prime})\|)\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{K^{\prime}_{u}}\left(\sqrt{1+\|\nabla f(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}+\sqrt{1+\|\nabla g(x^{\prime})\|^{2}}\right)\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{+}K)+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{-}K)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial K)<\infty.

Therefore, by the dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{t\rightarrow 1^{-}}\frac{E_{1}-E_{3}}{t-1}
is bounded. Similarly,

\lim_{t\rightarrow 1^{-}}\frac{E_{2}-E_{4}}{t-1}
is also bounded. Thus, the variation in (
7.59
) exists and is bounded.
∎

The boundedness obtained above guarantees that the first variation can be handled by standard convergence arguments. In particular, it provides the technical foundation for deriving the endpoint variational identities used below.

Lemma 7.2

(Integral Convergence Lemma)
.

If

are continuous functions on

, and

are finite Borel measures on

and

\lim_{j\to\infty}\mu_{j}=\mu\qquad\text{weakly on }\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

then

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}f_{j}(u)\,d\mu_{j}(u)=\int_{\Omega}f(u)\,d\mu(u).

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 1^{-}}-q\tilde{c}_{n,p}\int_{G_{u^{\perp},\,n-2}}\Phi_{E}(\bar{t}_{1})^{-q-1}(\Phi_{E})_{-}^{\prime}(\bar{t}_{2})\sigma_{u^{\perp},\,n-2}(\mathrm{d}E)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 1^{-}}-q\tilde{c}_{n,p}R_{0}^{-q-1}\int_{G_{u^{\perp},\,n-2}}(\Phi_{E})_{-}^{\prime}(\bar{t}_{2})\sigma_{u^{\perp},\,n-2}(\mathrm{d}E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-q\tilde{c}_{n,p}R_{0}^{-q-1}\int_{G_{u^{\perp},\,n-2}}\Phi_{E}^{\prime}(1^{-})\sigma_{u^{\perp},\,n-2}(\mathrm{d}E),

where
\bar{t}_{1},\bar{t}_{2}\in(t,1)
,
R_{0}
is given in Lemma
7.5
and the last equation is using the Monotone Convergence Theorem and
(\Phi_{E})_{-}^{\prime}(t)
is left continuous.
The formula (
7.79
) with the given condition
\frac{d}{dt}\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}K_{t})\big|_{t=1^{-}}=0
leads to
\int_{G_{u^{\perp},\,n-2}}\Phi_{E}^{\prime}(1^{-})\sigma_{u^{\perp},\,n-2}(\mathrm{d}E)\leq 0
.
Furthermore,
\Phi_{E}^{\prime}(1^{-})\geq 0
as
\Phi_{E}(t)
is an even convex function. Therefore, we conclude that
\Phi_{E}^{\prime}(1^{-})=0
for almost every
E\in G_{u^{\perp},n-2}
with respect to the measure
\sigma_{u^{\perp},n-2}
.

Since
\Phi_{E}(t)
is convex, non-decreasing on
[0,1]
, with vanishing left endpoint derivative
\Phi_{E}^{\prime}(1^{-})=0
, by a standard result in convex analysis,
\Phi_{E}(t)
is constant on
[0,1]
.
Substituting the constant back into the integral (
7.78
),

\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}K_{t})=\tilde{c}_{n,p}\int_{G_{u^{\perp},\,n-2}}\Phi_{E}(1)^{-q}\,\sigma_{u^{\perp},\,n-2}(\mathrm{d}E),\;t\in[0,1].

As a consequence of the evenness of
\Phi_{E}(t)
,
\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}K_{t})
is a constant for all
t\in[-1,1]
.
∎

Remark 7.1
.

If
\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}K_{t})
is a constant for all
t\in[-1,1]
, then
\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}K)=\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}S_{u}K)
since
K_{1}=K
and
K_{0}=S_{u}K
.

7.2.
Solution to Fixed Point Operator Rigidity

We are now ready to complete the proof of the main theorem.
The argument combines four principal ingredients developed throughout the paper:

(1)

the
L_{p}
-Projection Rolodex
representation of
\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K)
;

(2)

the admissibility of support perturbations along linear reflection shadow systems;

(3)

the convexity and rigidity properties of the associated Rolodex functionals;

(4)

the vanishing first-variation formula implied by the fixed-point condition.

Together, these results imply that the volume of the
polar
L_{p}
-projection body
is preserved under Steiner symmetrization in every direction. The equality characterization established above then forces the body to be an ellipsoid. In contrast, the affine covariance of the
L_{p}
-projection and
L_{p}
-centroid operators shows that every ellipsoid satisfies the fixed-point equation up to dilation.
We now combine these ingredients to derive the desired rigidity statement—the main Theorem
A
.

The next lemma characterizes precisely the geometric meaning of this equality condition and links it to a classical characterization of ellipsoids.
That is, it characterizes a convex body as an ellipsoid when
\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}K)=\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}S_{u}K)
for every
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

Lemma 7.7
.

For
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
,

\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}K)=\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}S_{u}K)
for all
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
if and only if
K
is an ellipsoid.

Proof.

From
[
27
, Lemma 14]
for
1<p<\infty
, we see that if
K\in
is an origin-symmetric convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

(7.80)

S_{u}\Pi_{p}^{*}K\subset\Pi_{p}^{*}S_{u}K,

with equality if and only if the chords of
K
orthogonal to
u
have midpoints that are coplanar. Thus, based on the inclusion relationship between these two convex bodies in (
7.80
),

\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}K)=\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}S_{u}K)
if and only if
S_{u}\Pi_{p}^{*}K=\Pi_{p}^{*}S_{u}K
. Furthermore,
\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}K)=\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi^{\ast}_{p}S_{u}K)
if and only if the chords of
K
orthogonal to
u
have midpoints that are coplanar.

It follows from
[
41
, Theorem 10.2.1]
that a convex body
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
is an ellipsoid if and only if the midpoints of any family of parallel chords of
K
orthogonal to
u
lie in a hyperplane for every
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. This proves the theorem.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main rigidity theorem. The argument proceeds by combining the vanishing first variation obtained from the fixed-point condition with the convexity of the Rolodex functionals, yielding constancy along every linear reflection shadow system. Equality in the associated symmetrization inequalities then forces the body to be an ellipsoid.

Theorem 7.1
.

For
p>1
and
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
,
\Gamma_{p}\Pi_{p}^{*}K
is a dilate of
K
if and only if
K
must be an ellipsoid.

Proof.

By Petty
[
37
]
and Lutwak-Yang-Zhang
[
27
]
, for
p>1
,
L_{p}
-centroid bodies for convex bodies are of class
C_{+}^{2}
and origin-symmetric.
These show that any convex body
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
satisfying
\Gamma_{p}\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K=cK
must be of class
C_{+}^{2}
and origin-symmetric.

On the one hand, by Lemma
7.4
,
if there exists
c>0
such that

\Gamma_{p}\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K=cK
,
then
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=1^{-}}\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K_{t})=0
, where
K_{t}
is the linear reflection shadow system with respect to
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.
By Lemma
7.6
,
\textrm{Vol}(\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K_{t})
is a constant for all
t\in[-1,1]
, and in particular at
t=0
and
t=1
, i.e.,
\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K)=\textrm{Vol}_{n}(\Pi_{p}^{\ast}S_{u}K)
in Remark
7.1
. Since this holds for all
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, Lemma
7.7
implies that
K
must be an ellipsoid.

On the other hand, suppose that
K
is an origin-centered ellipsoid. Then
there exist
a>0
and
\Lambda\in SL(n)
such that

K=a\Lambda B_{2}^{n}.

By the affine covariance of the
L_{p}
-projection body

[
27
, Lemma 2]
,

\Pi_{p}(\Lambda B_{2}^{n})=\Lambda^{-t}\Pi_{p}B_{2}^{n}
.
Hence

\Pi_{p}^{*}(\Lambda B_{2}^{n})=(\Lambda^{-t}\Pi_{p}B_{2}^{n})^{*}=\Lambda\Pi_{p}^{*}B_{2}^{n}.

Using also the affine covariance of the
L_{p}
-centroid body
[
27
, (14)]
, we obtain

\Gamma_{p}\Pi_{p}^{*}(\Lambda B_{2}^{n})=\Gamma_{p}(\Lambda\Pi_{p}^{*}B_{2}^{n})=\Lambda\Gamma_{p}\Pi_{p}^{*}B_{2}^{n}.

Since
\Pi_{p}^{*}B_{2}^{n}
is a Euclidean ball and the
L_{p}
-centroid body of a
Euclidean ball is again a Euclidean ball, there exists a constant
c_{0}>0

such that

\Gamma_{p}\Pi_{p}^{*}B_{2}^{n}=c_{0}B_{2}^{n}.

Therefore

\Gamma_{p}\Pi_{p}^{*}(\Lambda B_{2}^{n})=c_{0}\Lambda B_{2}^{n}.

In conclusion,
there exists
c>0
such that

\Gamma_{p}\Pi_{p}^{*}K=cK
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
A
completes the classification of fixed points of the operator

K\mapsto\Gamma_{p}\Pi_{p}^{\ast}K
.
In particular, it confirms the conjecture of Lutwak, Yang, and Zhang for all
p>1
. The argument demonstrates how shadow-system methods, variational formulas, and the newly introduced
L_{p}
-Projection Rolodex interact to produce a strong rigidity phenomenon: among all convex bodies, ellipsoids are the unique fixed points of the operator up to dilation.
