Nonflow Subtraction Beyond Two-Particle Correlations
Zaining Wang, Jiangyong Jia, Jinhui Chen, Shengli Huang, Chunjian Zhang, Zhengxi Yan

Abstract
Establishing collective flow in small collision systems is crucial for pinning down the minimum conditions for quark-gluon plasma (QGP) formation. In two-particle correlations, nonflow has been subtracted with good control, pushing the reach of flow measurements down to very small particle multiplicities N N . However, the multi-particle nature of collectivity has not been established in the same N N regime, because the residual nonflow surviving the subevent procedure in multi-particle cumulants has never been quantified. We develop a general nonflow subtraction framework for m m -particle cumulants, built around the approximate 1 / N m − 1 1/N^{m-1} scaling of nonflow in the independent-source picture. Correlators containing v 1 v_{1} serve as clean nonflow estimators, since the p T p_{\mathrm{T}} -integrated dipolar flow nearly vanishes. Using hijing as a controlled nonflow-only environment, we test the subtraction for three target observables ( ⟨ v 2 2 ⟩ \left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle , ⟨ v 2 2 ​ δ ​ p T ⟩ \left\langle v_{2}^{2}\,\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle , and c 2 ​ { 4 } c_{2}\{4\} ) in O+O and d d +Au at s NN = 5.36 \sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=5.36 TeV and 200 GeV. Most of the nonflow is removed, with residual fractions typically within 20–30% when converted to the two-particle level, though the best estimator differs across the three targets. We identify a multiplicity-reweighting correction, previously overlooked in two-particle correlations, that explains the long-standing undersubtraction of the naive 1 / N 1/N -scaling method; its impact grows as a power of the correlator order. The framework gives a systematic route to nonflow subtraction beyond two-particle correlations, broadening the class of multi-particle observables accessible to the small-system flow program.

II
Nonflow subtraction in two-particle correlations

The 2PC nonflow subtraction landscape consists of a family of related methods: the
c_{0}
-,
c_{1}
-, near-jet, and template variants, whose distinctions are subtle but consequential. Recent reviews
Lim et al. 2019
;
Grosse-Oetringhaus and Wiedemann 2024
;
Feng and Wang 2025
provide useful surveys, but the relationships among the methods are at times blurred, and corrections that significantly affect the subtraction in practice, in particular the multiplicity-reweighting factor we identify in Sec.
VI.3
and the impact of flow in the peripheral bin, are not sufficiently addressed. Because the multi-particle framework developed in Sec.
IV
inherits the scaling assumptions and bias-correction structure of these 2PC methods directly, we devote this section to a uniform mini-review before generalizing to higher orders.

II.1
Definitions and assumptions

Flow
, in the simplest picture, refers to azimuthal correlations arising from the hydrodynamic response to the initial collision geometry
Heinz and Snellings 2013
;
Gale et al. 2013
;
Luzum and Petersen 2014
, characterized by the harmonic coefficients
v_{n}
. In practice, several effects blur this separation. Subleading flow modes break the simple factorization assumed in the one-body description
Mazeliauskas and Teaney 2015
. Jet wakes that thermalize in the medium contribute additional collective-like signals on top of the bulk flow
Yang et al. 2023
. And at high
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, path-length-dependent energy loss produces correlations with the collision geometry that have different response from bulk
v_{n}

Gyulassy et al. 2001
. These effects introduce both additional one-body and genuine many-body contributions to the measured harmonics.

Nonflow
refers to correlations unrelated to the collision geometry, arising from sources such as jet fragmentation, resonance decays, and HBT effects
Ollitrault et al. 2009
;
Feng and Wang 2025
. These sources all respect GMC, which manifests as a long-range dipolar correlation across the event; after the near-side jet is suppressed by the pseudorapidity gap, the surviving low-
p_{\mathrm{T}}
long-range nonflow is dominated by this GMC dipolar imprint. The separation between flow and nonflow is statistical and defined at the correlator level: a
\rho^{0}
meson may carry collective flow and simultaneously induce nonflow correlations between its decay pions; a jet correlated with the impact parameter via path-length-dependent energy loss nevertheless produces multi-particle nonflow through its fragmentation. The flow and nonflow contents are therefore observable-dependent and cannot be separated at the single-particle level
Mazeliauskas and Teaney 2015
;
Mazeliauskas and Teaney 2016
; the separation must instead be defined for each target observable.

The subtraction framework further assumes that nonflow is
unmodified
by the medium: resonance decays and jet fragmentation proceed as in vacuum. This assumption can fail when radial flow focuses the decay products of a boosted resonance, or when jet quenching induces diffusion wakes. Such medium modifications are a known limitation of the subtraction approach
Abdulhamid et al. 2024
;
Lim et al. 2019
and contribute to the spread among different subtraction methods in data.

Notational convention.
Throughout this paper,
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle
without a superscript denotes the result
after
nonflow subtraction: in data this is the recovered estimate of the genuine flow signal, while in a nonflow-only generator such as
hijing
it is the residual non-closure of the subtraction procedure. The observed quantity (long-range, after the
\Delta\eta
gap is applied) is
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}
, and the nonflow component is
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{nf}}
, with the decomposition
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}=\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{nf}}+\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle
. In
hijing
,
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\rm flow}=0
and therefore
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}=\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{nf}}
; any nonzero
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle
that emerges from the subtraction is by construction residual nonflow.

II.2
Subtraction formula and scaling

In experimental analysis, a pseudorapidity gap is applied to suppress short-range correlations. The remaining long-range harmonics give the “direct Fourier” result
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}
. Nonflow is then subtracted:

\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle(N)=\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}(N)-f(N)\times\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{LM}}},

(II.1)

where
\mathrm{LM}
denotes low-multiplicity events in the same system or minimum-bias
pp
reference and
f
is a method-dependent scaling factor that varies with
N
. In all experimental analyses to date, this scaling factor has been assumed common across all harmonics
n\geq 2
. We follow that convention here, noting that the underlying nonflow may in principle depend on the harmonic order.

In the independent-source picture
Borghini et al. 2001
, nonflow scales as
1/N
, giving
f\sim N_{\mathrm{LM}}/N
. In small systems this scaling is distorted by multiplicity-selection bias: the steeply falling tail of
p(N)
enriches high-
N
events with extra-jet configurations, so nonflow at high
N
exceeds the naive
1/N
expectation and at low
N
falls below it. The effect is stronger for near-side correlations, which need two fragments from the same jet, than for away-side correlations, which need only one particle from each side of the dijet
Chatrchyan et al. 2013b
;
Aad et al. 2016
.

Equation (
II.1
) explicitly assumes that the
\mathrm{LM}
events are devoid of genuine collective flow. This assumption is invalid: ATLAS measurements, for instance, demonstrate that nonzero flow signals persist even in events with very low charged-particle multiplicity
Aad et al. 2020a
, and analogous evidence exists for peripheral
p
+A and A+A collisions. In response, experimental analyses developed methods that explicitly incorporate collectivity in the low-multiplicity bin, improving the treatment of nonflow subtraction and flow extraction.

II.3
Methods that assume only nonflow in
\mathrm{LM}

There are three methods for estimating
f
in Eq. (
II.1
):

1.

Multiplicity- or
c_{0}
-scaling
Abelev et al. 2013
;
Aad et al. 2013
;
Adare et al. 2013
:

\displaystyle f_{1}=N_{\mathrm{LM}}/N,

(II.2)

assuming independent sources without selection bias.

2.

Dipolar- or
c_{1}
-scaling
Adare et al. 2018
;
Abdulhamid et al. 2024
:

\displaystyle f_{2}=\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}/\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{LM}}}\approx f_{1}\times\frac{\text{Y}_{\mathrm{away}}}{\text{Y}_{\mathrm{away,\scriptscriptstyle{LM}}}},

(II.3)

where
Y
is the per-trigger yield integrated over a certain
\Delta\phi
range around
\pi
. In the low-
p_{\mathrm{T}}
region, this effectively uses the ratio of dipolar flow between target and reference samples as a proxy for the scaling of the jet-induced contribution.

3.

Near-jet scaling
Chatrchyan et al. 2013b
;
Aad et al. 2014
;
Adamczyk et al. 2015
:

\displaystyle f_{3}=f_{1}\times(\text{Y}_{\mathrm{near}}/\text{Y}_{\mathrm{near,\scriptscriptstyle{LM}}}),

(II.4)

using the near-side jet per-trigger yield as the proxy
Chatrchyan et al. 2013b
. This typically gives
f_{3}>f_{2}
due to the stronger multiplicity selection bias, which arises because the same jet must produce two fragments to form a near-side pair, as opposed to the
c_{1}
-scaling method, which requires only one particle of the pair to come from the away side.

II.4
Methods that account for flow in
\mathrm{LM}

4.

Template method
Aad et al. 2016
;
Abdulhamid et al. 2023
:

The method starts with a Fourier decomposition of the per-trigger yield distribution

\displaystyle\text{Y}(\Delta\phi)=N^{a}(1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}\cos n\Delta\phi)

(II.5)

where
N^{a}\propto N
is the integrated associated-particle yield per trigger. Nonflow is removed by subtracting the
\mathrm{LM}
per-trigger yield distribution with a scale
F
, leaving a modified pedestal
G
modulated by
n\geq 2
harmonics:

\displaystyle\text{Y}(\Delta\phi)-F\,\text{Y}(\Delta\phi)_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{LM}}}=G\!\left(1+2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle\cos n\Delta\phi\right).

(II.6)

The scale
F
is fixed by requiring that the
n=1
component cancel
Feng and Wang 2025
,

\displaystyle N^{a}\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}=F\,N^{a}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{LM}}}\,\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{LM}}}\Longrightarrow F=f_{2}/f_{1}\,,

(II.7)

which in turn fixes the pedestal,

\displaystyle G=N^{a}-F\,N^{a}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{LM}}}=N^{a}\,(1-f_{2})\,.

(II.8)

Because the pedestal is reduced,
G<N^{a}
, and all extracted harmonics are enhanced relative to the
c_{1}
-method. Inserting Eq. (
II.5
) into the left-hand side of Eq. (
II.6
) makes this explicit:

\displaystyle\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{t}mp}=\frac{\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}-f_{2}\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{LM}}}}{1-f_{2}}\;.

(II.9)

The template method therefore differs from the
c_{1}
-method [Eq. (
II.3
)] by a factor of
1/(1-f_{2})
. This rescaling recovers the true flow only when the genuine flow in the
\mathrm{LM}
and target bins coincide,
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{LM}}}=\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle
, a condition that holds for
v_{2}
in
pp
collisions
Aad et al. 2016
but is violated in general.

Historically this approach has been called the “template-fit” method, because the subtraction in Eq. (
II.6
) was implemented through a
\chi^{2}
minimization. As that procedure is mathematically equivalent to requiring the cancellation of the dipolar component, we drop the “fit” label for generality.

5.

Improved template method
Aaboud et al. 2019
:

When the flows in
\mathrm{LM}
and the target bin differ, the template method is biased. The bias is easily seen by assuming nonflow is fully subtracted and setting the observed harmonics equal to the genuine flow (
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}\!\to\!\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle
). Eq. (
II.9
) then becomes:

\displaystyle\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\rm tmp}=\frac{\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle-f_{2}\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{LM}}}}{1-f_{2}}\;,

(II.10)

Clearly, this reproduces the true flow
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle
if and only if
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle=\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{LM}}}
, which is the case for
v_{2}
in
pp
collisions. However, measurements have shown that all flow harmonics grow with multiplicity in
p
+A and A+A collisions, and that the triangular flow
v_{3}
grows with multiplicity even in
pp
collisions. In this case, the LM events have a smaller flow signal,
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{LM}}}<\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle
, and the template method systematically overestimates the signal,
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\rm tmp}>\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle
.

The improved template method removes this bias by rewriting Eq. (
II.10
), expressing the true flow as a one-step correction to the template-method result:

\displaystyle\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle=\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\rm tmp}-f_{2}\!\left(\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\rm tmp}-\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{LM}}}\right).

(II.11)

Applying Eq. (
II.11
) requires the genuine flow in the low-multiplicity reference,
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{LM}}}
. The LM sample itself cannot supply this, since its flow was assumed to vanish in the construction of the template method. The improved template method instead borrows the flow from a nearby
anchor bin
, denoted “nextLM”, whose choice depends on the LM choice already made:

(a)

pp
subtraction
(LM
=
minimum-bias
pp
): the nextLM is the lowest available multiplicity bin of the target system.

(b)

Peripheral subtraction
(LM
=
peripheral bin of the target): the nextLM is the next-lowest multiplicity bin.

In both cases, the genuine flow in the nextLM bin,
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle_{\rm nextLM}
, is approximated by the template-method value
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\rm tmp}_{\rm nextLM}
, and Eq. (
II.11
) becomes a one-step correction relating two template-method outputs:

\displaystyle\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle\!=\!\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\!\rm tmp}\!-\!f_{2,\rm{nextLM}}\!\left(\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\rm tmp}\!-\!\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\rm tmp}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm{nextLM}}}\right)\;,

(II.12)

where
f_{2,\rm{nextLM}}=\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}/\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\rm nextLM}
. ATLAS measurements in
p
+Pb and Pb+Pb collisions show that this correction is modest for
v_{2}
and
v_{4}
but is important for
v_{3}

Aaboud et al. 2019
.

II.5
Domain of applicability

Nonflow subtraction is most reliable at low
p_{\mathrm{T}}
(
\lesssim 2
GeV/
c
), where jet-induced correlations on the away side are broad in
\Delta\phi
and well approximated by a dipolar modulation
\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}\equiv\left\langle\cos\Delta\phi\right\rangle<0
, which is negative because away-side pair production is biased toward
\Delta\phi\approx\pi
by momentum conservation. In this regime, the subtraction rests on three conditions with
n\geq 2
, of distinct character:

\displaystyle|\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{nf}}|

\displaystyle\approx|\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}|,

(II.13)

\displaystyle\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\!\mathrm{nf}}

\displaystyle=a_{n}|\!\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\right\rangle^{\!\mathrm{nf}}\!|,\;\;|\Delta a_{n}|\ll 1\;,

(II.14)

\displaystyle\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{nf}}

\displaystyle\ll\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}.\vskip-8.5359pt

(II.15)

The first condition, Eq. (
II.13
), makes
\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}
a clean nonflow estimator (with negative values), since the genuine
v_{1}
contribution nearly cancels after
p_{\mathrm{T}}
integration. The second, Eq. (
II.14
), requires the nonflow shape to be stable across multiplicity (the change of
a_{n}
across
N
is small
|\Delta a_{n}|\ll 1
). This is guaranteed when higher harmonics are negligible (
a_{n}\ll 1
), as is typical at low
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, but the condition
|\Delta a_{n}|\ll 1
is weaker and can hold even when
a_{n}
itself is not small. The third condition, Eq. (
II.15
), by contrast, is a statement about the data: the nonflow contamination of
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}
is small in absolute terms, typically below
20\%
in
pp
and
p\!+\!\mathrm{Pb}
at the LHC. When it fails, the subtraction may still be technically valid, but the residual signal becomes too sensitive to the choice of subtraction method to interpret robustly. Together, these conditions keep the nonflow subtraction uncertainty under control.

The situation is very different for correlations involving single hadrons, jets, or heavy-flavor probes at high
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, where the jet-induced correlation in
\mathrm{LM}
,
Y(\Delta\phi)^{\rm LM}
, is quite narrow in
\Delta\phi

Aad et al. 2020b
;
Aad et al. 2020a
. This narrow structure implies sizable harmonics at all orders and a strong
N
-dependence of
a_{n}
, violating both
|\Delta a_{n}|\ll 1
[Eq. (
II.14
)] and Eq. (
II.15
). The nonflow at
n=2
often exceeds the genuine signal, so
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle
is small compared to
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{nf}}
rather than the other way around. The nonflow systematic uncertainties must therefore be treated with extreme care: if, for example,
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}=10\,\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle
, a 10% uncertainty on
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}
produces a 100% uncertainty on the final extracted
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle
. A quantitative assessment via alternative subtraction schemes and/or dedicated Monte Carlo closure tests is needed to evaluate the robustness of the subtraction procedure; such studies have generally not yet been carried out at high
p_{\mathrm{T}}
. A similar dominance
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}\gg\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle
is also observed for flow in photo-nuclear processes
Aad et al. 2021
.

A qualitatively different failure mode arises in
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions
Chen et al. 2024
or in correlations among constituents within a single high-multiplicity jet

N_{s}=2
or
3

N_{s}=1

p_{\mathrm{T}}

III
Motivation for multi-particle nonflow subtraction

III.1
Multi-particle observables

In heavy-ion collisions, event-by-event fluctuations of the collective flow are described by a joint probability distribution
p([p_{\mathrm{T}}],V_{1},V_{2},\ldots)
, where
[p_{\mathrm{T}}]
is the event-averaged transverse momentum (which captures the radial flow) and
V_{n}=v_{n}e^{in\Psi_{n}}
is the anisotropic flow vector. Experimentally accessible observables are moments of this distribution allowed by global rotational symmetry
Bhalerao et al. 2011
;
Jia 2014
,

\displaystyle\left\langle(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{l}V_{n_{1}}V_{n_{2}}\cdots\right\rangle\,,\quad n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots=0,

(III.1)

where
\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}=[p_{\mathrm{T}}]-\left\langle[p_{\mathrm{T}}]\right\rangle
,
l
is a non-negative integer, and the
n_{i}
are non-zero integers. The cumulants measured experimentally are constructed from combinations of these moments.

Examples of such cumulants up to the fourth-order include the following:

•

Two-particle cumulants:

\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle=\left\langle V_{n}V_{-n}\right\rangle\equiv\left\langle V_{n}V_{n}^{*}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}\right\rangle
.

•

Three-particle cumulants:

\left\langle(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{3}\right\rangle
,
\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
,

\left\langle\!V_{2}^{2}V_{4}^{*}\!\right\rangle
​,
\left\langle\!V_{2}V_{3}V_{5}^{*}\!\right\rangle
​,
\left\langle\!V_{3}^{2}V_{6}^{*}\!\right\rangle
: asymmetric cumulants.

•

Four-particle cumulants:

c_{n}\{4\}=\left\langle v_{n}^{4}\right\rangle-2\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle^{2}
,
\left\langle(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{4}\right\rangle-3\left\langle(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}\right\rangle^{2}
,

\left\langle(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle(\delta p_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}\right\rangle\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle
,

\textrm{sc}\!(n,\!m\!)\!=\!\left\langle v_{n}^{2}v_{m}^{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle v_{n}^{2}\right\rangle\!\left\langle v_{m}^{2}\right\rangle
symmetric cumulants.

\left\langle V_{2}^{3}V_{6}^{*}\right\rangle
,
\left\langle V_{3}^{2}V_{2}^{*}V_{4}^{*}\right\rangle
… asymmetric cumulants.

These cumulants probe the interplay between initial-state geometry fluctuations and the nonlinear hydrodynamic response, and have been central to extracting QGP transport properties from heavy-ion data.

III.2
Limitations of the subevent approach

The subevent cumulant method
Jia et al. 2017
suppresses nonflow by selecting particles from pseudorapidity-separated subevents, forcing any residual nonflow contribution to span a large rapidity gap. Take the four-subevent implementation of
c_{n}\{4\}
as an example: a dijet can contribute only if its near-side and away-side fragments, together with two additional same-jet particles, populate all four
\eta
windows. Such configurations become rare as the rapidity gap increases, and the method therefore substantially reduces dijet-related nonflow
Aaboud et al. 2018a
;
Aaboud et al. 2019
;
Sirunyan et al. 2021
;
Acharya et al. 2019
.

Suppression, however, is not subtraction. Without an explicit subtraction, the residual nonflow component cannot be quantified. In fact, several published MPC measurements already indicate significant residual nonflow in low-multiplicity
pp
and
p
+Pb collisions at the LHC:

•

Results for
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle v_{3}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
display a non-monotonic multiplicity dependence; moreover, the magnitude and even the sign of these observables vary with the imposed
\eta
gap
Tumasyan et al. 2024
.

•

Symmetric cumulants
\mathrm{sc}(2,3)
and
\mathrm{sc}(2,4)
exhibit a pronounced dependence on the chosen subevent configuration
Sirunyan et al. 2018
;
Aaboud et al. 2019
;
Acharya et al. 2019
;
Bilandzic et al. 2014
and event-class definition, which leads to inconsistencies among the ATLAS/CMS and ALICE results.

•

Cumulants
c_{n}\{4\}
computed in
pythia

Sjostrand et al. 2008
, a generator with jet-induced nonflow but no genuine collective flow, change sign and magnitude depending on the
p_{\mathrm{T}}
selection, event-class definition, and subevent choice. This behavior reflects non-Gaussian fluctuations of nonflow that persist even after subevent suppression
Jia et al. 2017
;
Aaboud et al. 2018a
.

Without an explicit subtraction procedure, these features cannot be uniquely assigned to either flow or nonflow.

III.3
Benefits of explicit subtraction

An explicit nonflow estimate and subtraction in MPC enables: (i) quantification of the residual nonflow systematic in a given measurement; (ii) separation of residual nonflow from flow decorrelation in
p_{\mathrm{T}}
and
\eta

Bozek and Broniowski 2016
;
Jia and Huo 2014
;
Khachatryan et al. 2015b
;
Aaboud et al. 2018b
; (iii) extension of measurements to low multiplicity, where nonflow dominates even after subevent suppression
Aaboud et al. 2018a
;
Aaboud et al. 2019
;
Tumasyan et al. 2024
;
Aad et al. 2023
;
Tumasyan et al. 2024
and where flow signals are most sensitive to initial-state physics
Welsh et al. 2016
; and (iv) a data-driven consistency check between the standard method and the subevent methods, which should give mutually consistent results after proper subtraction.

There is also an important practical motivation. The four-subevent method reduces the number of unique quadruplets by a factor of
4^{4}/4!\approx 11
relative to the standard method
Jia et al. 2017
; if nonflow can be explicitly subtracted within the standard or two-subevent approach, a substantial statistical gain can be achieved. This improvement is particularly valuable for measurements in high-multiplicity events in small collision systems, where statistical precision is the dominant limiting factor.

For the multi-particle extension developed here we restrict ourselves to low
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, where the correlation structure is GMC-dominated and the genuine flow in the
\mathrm{LM}
reference can be neglected when the target and reference multiplicities are well separated (
N\gg N_{\mathrm{LM}}
). In this limit, the template methods reduce to the
c_{1}
method of two-particle correlations. We therefore focus on multi-particle extensions of the
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
scalings, which bracket the systematic range of reasonable nonflow estimates.

III.4
Domain of applicability

The reliability conditions of Sec.
II.5
carry over directly to multi-particle correlations: the methods developed in this paper are well-founded only at low
p_{\mathrm{T}}
(
\lesssim 2
GeV/
c
). At high
p_{\mathrm{T}}
both near- and away-side jets fragment into narrow clusters of correlated particles, populating all
m
-particle correlators that involve fragments of the same jet or of the near-/away-side dijet. The resulting jet-induced nonflow contains sizable harmonics at all orders, so all the multi-particle observables discussed above pick up a substantial contamination. The required subtraction grows correspondingly large and becomes acutely sensitive to small uncertainties in the assumed nonflow shape. The factorization of the flow signal into a single one-body distribution is also questionable at high
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, so importing the low-
p_{\mathrm{T}}
logic into that region may give unphysically large signals, a tension hinted at by the recent CMS
v_{2}\{4\}
measurement in
p
+Pb collisions
Chekhovsky et al. 2025
. A residual positive
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle
at high
p_{\mathrm{T}}
after subtraction does not, by itself, establish genuine collective flow.

IV
Subtraction framework

So far the reference sample
\mathrm{LM}
could mean either a low-multiplicity bin of the same collision system or minimum-bias
pp

pp
as the reference for the remainder of the paper, denoting it by the subscript
\mathrm{pp}

pp
avoids the peripheral selection bias of same-system low-multiplicity bins, but introduces a different complication: the
pp

IV.2
.

IV.1
Scaling principle

In the independent-source model, the 2PC nonflow contribution to an
m
-particle cumulant observable
\mathcal{O}\{m\}
arises from individually correlated particle production sources. For events with
N_{s}
effective sources, each producing a finite number of particles so that
N_{s}\propto N
, the nonflow contribution scales as
Borghini et al. 2001
;
Bilandzic et al. 2011

\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\mathrm{nf}}\propto 1/N_{s}^{m-1}\propto 1/N^{m-1}\propto\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\right\rangle^{m-1}\,.

(IV.1)

Equation (
IV.1
) is the
m
-particle generalization of the 2PC scaling condition Eq. (
II.14
): a universal
N
-dependence shared by all
m
-particle nonflow observables of a given order. The middle proportionality,
\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\mathrm{nf}}\propto 1/N^{m-1}
, is the
c_{0}
-scaling baseline (with
f_{1}=N_{\rm LM}/N
); the right-most proportionality,
\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\mathrm{nf}}\propto\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\right\rangle^{m-1}
, is the
c_{1}
-type refinement that absorbs the residual
N
-dependence from multiplicity-selection bias, which reduces to the
c_{0}
form when bias is absent. The explicit choice of reference observable
\mathcal{E}\{k\}
that implements either scaling is given in Sec.
IV.5
. Heuristically, the

scaling follows from counting: of the
\sim N^{m}
distinct
m
-tuples in an event, only
\sim N
are drawn from a single correlated source and thus contribute genuine
m
-particle correlations, so the correlated fraction scales as
N/N^{m}=1/N^{m-1}
. Even at low
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, deviations from Eqs. (
IV.1
) and (
II.14
) can arise from multiplicity-selection biases and from harmonic-dependent initial-state effects such as nPDF or saturation physics and string/rope shoving.

IV.4
General subtraction formula

The general nonflow subtraction formula is

\mathcal{O}\{m\}=\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\mathrm{obs}}-f_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{E}}\times\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{pp}}=\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\left(\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}\{k\}^{\mathrm{obs}}}{\mathcal{E}\{k\}^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{pp}}}\right)^{1/(k-1)}\underbrace{\frac{N_{m,\mathrm{pp}}}{N_{k,\mathrm{pp}}}\cdot\frac{N_{k}}{N_{m}}}_{W_{m,k}}\right)^{m-1}\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{pp}},

(IV.10)

where
\mathcal{O}\{m\}
is the target
m
-particle cumulant,
\mathcal{E}\{k\}
is a
k
-particle cumulant dominated by nonflow that serves as the scaling estimator, and the subscript
\mathrm{pp}
denotes the minimum-bias
pp
baseline. The
multiplicity-reweighting factor

W_{m,k}\equiv\frac{N_{m,\mathrm{pp}}}{N_{k,\mathrm{pp}}}\cdot\frac{N_{k}}{N_{m}}\approx\frac{N_{m,\mathrm{pp}}}{N_{k,\mathrm{pp}}}

(IV.11)

accounts for the fact that target and reference observables are weighted by different numbers of multiplets, and satisfies
W_{m,m}=1
and
W_{m,k}=1/W_{k,m}
.
W_{m,k}\approx 1
for narrow multiplicity bins but deviates significantly for broad multiplicity distributions. Since events for the system of interest are always binned in narrow multiplicity ranges,
N_{k}\approx N_{m}\approx\left\langle N\right\rangle
, but this is not the case when the entire minimum-bias
pp
ensemble is used as one bin. The impact of
W_{m,k}
enters as the power
(m-1)/(k-1)
, which amplifies modest deviations of
W
from unity into large subtraction errors when
m>k
(and especially for higher-order target cumulants). The omission of
W_{m,k}
is a frequent failure mode of multi-particle nonflow subtraction, as demonstrated in Sec.
VI.3
.

The residual fractional nonflow after subtraction is

\frac{\mathcal{O}\{m\}}{\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\mathrm{obs}}}=1-\left(\frac{R_{\mathcal{E}}}{R_{\mathcal{O}}}\right)^{m-1},

(IV.12)

where we introduce the dimensionless ratio

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{E}}\!\equiv\!\frac{N_{k}}{N_{k,\mathrm{pp}}}\!\left(\!\frac{\mathcal{E}\{k\}^{\mathrm{obs}}}{\mathcal{E}\{k\}^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{pp}}}\!\right)^{\!1/(k-1)},R_{\mathcal{O}}\!\equiv\!\frac{N_{m}}{N_{m,\mathrm{pp}}}\!\left(\!\frac{\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\mathrm{obs}}}{\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{pp}}}\!\right)^{\!1/(m-1)}.

(IV.13)

If nonflow scales exactly as
1/N^{m-1}
, then
R_{\mathcal{O}}=R_{\mathcal{E}}=1
for all
N
. The ratio
D\equiv R_{\mathcal{O}}/R_{\mathcal{E}}
measures how closely the reference observable tracks the target nonflow: perfect subtraction has
D=1
.

The possibility that the

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\rm tmp}=\frac{\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\mathrm{obs}}-f_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{E}}\,\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{pp}}}{1-f_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{E}}}

(IV.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\{m\}=\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{sub}}\{m\}^{\rm tmp}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-f_{\mathcal{O},\rm nextLM}^{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{sub}}\{m\}^{\rm tmp}-\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{sub}}\{m\}^{\rm tmp}_{\rm nextLM})

(IV.15)

where “nextLM” denotes the lowest multiplicity bin in the system of interest. These equations are defined in direct analogy to Eqs (
II.9
) and (

IV.5.5
Considerations in subtraction strategy

The nonflow scaling argument in Sec.
IV.1
applies to multi-particle cumulants rather than to multi-particle moments; the two have fundamentally different scaling behavior. For example, the nonflow contribution to the four-particle moment
\left\langle v_{n}^{4}\right\rangle
scales as
1/N^{2}
, whereas that for the cumulant
c_{n}\{4\}
scales as
1/N^{3}
. Nonflow subtraction must therefore be applied at the cumulant level throughout.

Second, the multiplicity-reweighting factor must be applied whenever the reference observable and the target observable do not have the same order. Omitting
N_{m,\mathrm{pp}}/N_{k,\mathrm{pp}}
in Eq. (
IV.10
) leads to large, order-dependent errors (Sec.
VI.3
).

Third, given the large number of possible target–reference combinations, it is important to check their performance explicitly. The best choice of
\mathcal{E}\{k\}
depends on the dynamical origin of the target nonflow and must be determined case by case in model studies and real data analyses.

For this first study we restrict ourselves to three representative observables:
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle
,
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\,\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
, and
c_{2}\{4\}
. Together they cover the central physics goals of the small-system flow program and exercise all the technical aspects of the subtraction. The three serve as a testbed for evaluating subtraction performance, comparing alternative strategies, and picking out the best-performing reference for each target.

VI.3
Importance of the multiplicity-weighting

When the
\mathrm{LM}
sample spans a wide multiplicity range, the multiplicity-reweighting factor
W_{m,k}
in Eq. (
IV.10
) must be included. We illustrate this for the three target observables, showing that neglecting
W_{m,k}
when using minimum-bias
pp
(or very wide peripheral bin) as the reference leads to a subtraction failure whose severity grows with the correlator order.

The top panels of Fig.
5
show the residual nonflow fraction for
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle
with
\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\,\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
as the reference. Since
W_{2,3}\approx N_{2,\mathrm{pp}}/N_{3,\mathrm{pp}}<1
, neglecting
W
always oversubtracts. At LHC O+O, including
W
instead flips the residual to the undersubtracted side with similar magnitude. Here the
\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\,\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
reference simply does not capture the
N
-dependence of the
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle
nonflow, regardless of
W
. The
W
correction is nevertheless required: it removes a well-defined
pp
-weighting bias and is needed for consistency across systems. At RHIC, in both O+O and
d
+Au, including
W
improves the subtraction substantially and pulls the residual fraction much closer to zero.

The bottom panels of Fig.
5
show the residual nonflow fraction for
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\,\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
using
\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\right\rangle
as the reference observable. Here
W_{3,2}\approx N_{3,\mathrm{pp}}/N_{2,\mathrm{pp}}>1
, so neglecting
W
always leads to undersubtraction. In all three collision systems, including
W
improves the subtraction substantially and brings the residual nonflow fraction much closer to zero. The improvement is more dramatic here than in the top panels because the reweighting factor enters squared in the present case (
W_{3,2}^{2}
), but only as a square root in the top-panel case (
W_{2,3}^{1/2}
).

is replaced by the simple event-averaged multiplicity

in

the scale factor is suppressed by a factor of

The same effect also resolves a long-standing observation in 2PC: the
c_{0}
method is known to undersubtract nonflow in data
Abdulhamid et al. 2024
;
Aad et al. 2016
. Within the present framework the explanation is direct. The correctly scaled
c_{0}
subtraction for
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle
reads

\frac{\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle}{\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}}=1-\frac{N_{2,\mathrm{pp}}}{N_{2}}\frac{\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{obs}}}{\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{obs}}}.

(VI.1)

The numerator requires
N_{2,\mathrm{pp}}
, the effective pair multiplicity in the
pp
reference sample, not the mean
\left\langle N\right\rangle_{\mathrm{pp}}
. Because the
pp
distribution is broad,
N_{2,\mathrm{pp}}=\left\langle N^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{pp}}/\left\langle N\right\rangle_{\mathrm{pp}}>\left\langle N\right\rangle_{\mathrm{pp}}
. Therefore, the naive
c_{0}
implementation uses
\left\langle N\right\rangle_{\mathrm{pp}}
instead of
N_{2,\mathrm{pp}}
, causing systematic under-subtraction.

VI.4
Non-closure correction in data

Even with the optimal subtraction, the residual fraction is not exactly zero, reflecting deviations of the actual nonflow from perfect
1/N^{m-1}
scaling in
hijing
. The non-closure is at the 20–30% level for most two-particle correlation observables but can be significantly larger for higher-order correlations.

This non-closure cannot be corrected to zero in data without knowing the true nonflow. We therefore define a multiplicative correction factor
K_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{E}}(N)
that quantifies the fractional non-closure in models:

K_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{E}}(N)\equiv\left(\frac{R_{\mathcal{O}}}{R_{\mathcal{E}}}\right)^{m-1}\;\approx\;1+\frac{\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\rm res,\,nonflow}}{\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\mathrm{obs}}}\,,

(VI.2)

so that the deviation of
K_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{E}}
from unity is approximately the residual fractional nonflow shown in Figs.
2
–
7
. By construction, the subtraction formula Eq. (
IV.12
) closes in
hijing
when the scale factor is rescaled by
K_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{E}}
:

0\;=\;1-K_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{E}}\!\left(\frac{R_{\mathcal{E}}}{R_{\mathcal{O}}}\right)^{m-1}\!.

(VI.3)

Although the absolute nonflow magnitude in
hijing
is unreliable, the trend of its residual non-closure with
N
is much more robust.
K_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{E}}(N)
extracted from
hijing
can therefore be applied as a multiplicative correction to the nonflow baseline measured in peripheral or
pp
data, giving a well-motivated estimate of the nonflow systematic. This mirrors the STAR procedure for 2PC analyses
Abdulhamid et al. 2024
, where
hijing
was used not to predict absolute nonflow yields, but to set the systematic uncertainty on the subtraction.

Since
hijing
overpredicts multiplicity by roughly 50% at LHC energies
Acharya et al. 2021
;
Acharya et al. 2025
, the
K
factors derived from
hijing
provide a conservative estimate of the systematic uncertainty in data: at equal
N
, real data contain a larger fraction of underlying-event particles and a smaller fraction of jet-induced particles than
hijing
, so the nonflow scaling is better behaved and the actual non-closure should be smaller.

VII
Summary and outlook

We have built a general framework for nonflow subtraction in multi-particle correlations, extending the established 2PC methodology to arbitrary order. The main findings are:

•

Nonflow in an
m
-particle correlator scales approximately as
1/N^{m-1}
in the independent-source picture (Eq.
IV.1
). This scaling has been largely confirmed in the
hijing
model.

•

Correlators containing
v_{1}
are clean nonflow estimators at each order [Eqs. (
IV.4
)–(
IV.9
)], because the
p_{\mathrm{T}}
-integrated dipolar flow is small due to its sign change with
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, and the surviving long-range nonflow (after the rapidity gap removes the near-side jet) projects onto the dipolar shape via global momentum conservation, which scales as
\sim 1/N^{m-1}
.

•

For 2PC the
1/N
-scaling subtraction leaves a residual nonflow fraction below 20–30%; for multi-particle correlations the residual can be larger. However, using
v_{1}
-containing reference estimators reduces these residuals further.

•

The multiplicity-reweighting factor
W_{m,k}
associated with the
pp
events (or peripheral bin if it is broad) must be included in Eq. (
IV.10
). Its omission leads to errors that grow as the power
(m-1)/(k-1)
: for
c_{2}\{4\}
, the residual nonflow fraction stays near 1 without
W_{m,k}
but remains close to zero with it. This factor also explains the well-known under-subtraction of the naive
c_{0}
method in 2PC.

•

The optimal scaling estimator depends on the target observable. Of the three tested here,
c_{0}
(
1/N
),
c_{1}
(
\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\right\rangle
), and
\left\langle v_{1}^{2}\,\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
, the
c_{1}
scaling is best for
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\right\rangle
(
\lesssim 15\%
residual) and for
c_{2}\{4\}
(
\sim 10
–
20\%
), while
c_{0}
is best for
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\,\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
(
<20\%
). The remaining combinations leave
\sim 20
–
50\%
residuals. The optimal choice must be established from model studies for each target.

•

The residual non-closure in
hijing
can be quantified by the correction factor
K_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{E}}(N)
defined in Eq. (
VI.3
), and propagated to real data as a non-closure uncertainty for nonflow, in direct analogy with the STAR procedure for 2PC measurements
Abdulhamid et al. 2024
.

Several directions merit future work.

•

Symmetric cumulants and mixed-harmonic correlators.
The framework extends naturally to
\mathrm{sc}(n,m)
,
\left\langle V_{2}^{2}V_{4}^{*}\right\rangle
, and
\left\langle V_{2}V_{3}V_{5}^{*}\right\rangle
via Method 2b, with the subtraction performance to be validated in
hijing
and
pythia
.

•

Higher-order harmonics and cumulants.
The framework presented here focuses on
n=2
. Extension to
v_{3}
- and
v_{4}
-related observables, where the dominant nonflow source may be different, requires further dedicated study. Natural candidates for
v_{3}
are
C_{112}
and
C_{123}
[Eqs. (
IV.7
)–(
IV.8
)]
Adamczyk et al. 2018
. Methods 2a and 2b also generalize naturally to higher-order cumulants
c_{n}\{6\}
and
c_{n}\{8\}
, where the multiplicity-reweighting correction becomes increasingly important as the correlator order grows.

•

Generalization of the template method.
A multi-particle version of the template method outlined in Eqs. (
IV.14
) and (
IV.15
) would account for the presence of genuine flow in
pp
, and hence avoid the subtraction bias in the low-
N
region.

•

Consistency between standard and subevent methods.
These methods carry the same genuine flow signal but very different nonflow contamination. After subtraction, they should agree; the consistency between standard and subevent results therefore offers a data-driven test of the subtraction, and possibly an independent handle on the residual nonflow itself.

•

Application to data.
The most important next step is to apply these methods in real RHIC and LHC analyses. ATLAS, CMS, ALICE, STAR, and sPHENIX are all positioned to implement the framework on existing
pp
,
p
+Pb/Au,
d\!+\!\mathrm{Au}
, and
\mathrm{O}\!+\!\mathrm{O}
datasets, with the
K_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathcal{E}}
correction providing a well-defined non-closure uncertainty. The resulting improvements in multi-particle flow measurements should substantially sharpen the picture of small-system collectivity.

Appendix A
The effective multiplet multiplicity
N_{m}

For an event with
N
particles, the number of
m
-particle combinations is
M_{m}(N)=N(N-1)\cdots(N-m+1)
. For a sample of events with multiplicity distribution
p(N)
, the sample-averaged correlator is weighted by these combination counts:

\mathcal{O}\{m\}=\frac{\sum_{i}M_{m}(N_{i})\,\mathcal{O}\{m\}(N_{i})}{\sum_{i}M_{m}(N_{i})}.

(A.1)

If nonflow scales as
\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\mathrm{nf}}=C/N^{m-1}
for some constant

C
, the sample-averaged nonflow is

\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\mathrm{nf}}=C\cdot\frac{\sum_{i}M_{m}(N_{i})/N_{i}^{m-1}}{\sum_{i}M_{m}(N_{i})}.

(A.2)

We define the effective multiplicity
N_{m}
via

\mathcal{O}\{m\}^{\mathrm{nf}}\equiv C/N_{m}^{m-1}
, i.e.,

\frac{1}{N_{m}^{m-1}}=\frac{\sum_{i}M_{m}(N_{i})/N_{i}^{m-1}}{\sum_{i}M_{m}(N_{i})}.

(A.3)

Using
M_{m}(N_{i})\approx N_{i}^{m}
for
N_{i}\gg m
in the numerator gives

M_{m}(N_{i})/N_{i}^{m-1}\approx N_{i}
, so

\frac{1}{N_{m}^{m-1}}\approx\frac{\sum_{i}N_{i}}{\sum_{i}N_{i}^{m}},\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N_{m}^{m-1}\approx\frac{\sum_{i}N_{i}^{m}}{\sum_{i}N_{i}},

(A.4)

which is Eq. (
IV.3
). The approximation is accurate to

\mathcal{O}(n/\left\langle N\right\rangle)
.

For
m=2
:
N_{2}\approx\left\langle N^{2}\right\rangle/\left\langle N\right\rangle=\left\langle N\right\rangle+\sigma_{N}^{2}/\left\langle N\right\rangle
. In a
pp
multiplicity distribution
with
\sigma_{N}^{2}\gg\left\langle N\right\rangle
(true at LHC energies),
N_{2}\gg\left\langle N\right\rangle
.
For
m=4
with a typical broad
pp
distribution,
N_{4,\mathrm{pp}}/N_{2,\mathrm{pp}}
is of
order
1.5
–
2
, which when raised to the third power in the
c_{n}\{4\}

subtraction has an impact of order 3–8. This asymmetry is the origin of
the correction that the naive
c_{0}
method misses.

For the subevent method, the combination weight in Eq. (
A.3
)
is modified. For the two-subevent three-particle correlator, the weight is

N_{a}N_{b}(N_{a}+N_{b}-2)
, and
N_{m}
must be recalculated accordingly. The
conceptual definition in Eq. (
A.3
) and its physical
interpretation remain unchanged.

Appendix B
The alternative effective multiplicity
M_{m}

An alternative effective multiplicity is defined via the combination-weighted average of
1/N
, independent of the order of the correlator:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M_{m}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\sum_{i}1/N_{i}\cdot M_{m}(N_{i})}{\sum_{i}M_{m}(N_{i})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{i}1/N_{i}N_{i}(N_{i}-1)\cdots(N_{i}-m+1)}{\sum_{i}N_{i}(N_{i}-1)\cdots(N_{i}-m+1)}

(B.1)

For
m=2
,
M_{2}\approx\left\langle N^{2}\right\rangle/\left\langle N\right\rangle=N_{2}
, so the two definitions coincide for two-particle correlators. For
m\geq 3
they differ:

N_{m}^{m-1}\approx\frac{\sum N_{i}^{m}}{\sum N_{i}},\qquad M_{m}\approx\frac{\sum N_{i}^{m}}{\sum N_{i}^{m-1}}.

(B.2)

Hence, we have the following product relations:

\displaystyle N_{3}^{2}=M_{3}\cdot M_{2},\;N_{4}^{3}=M_{4}\cdot M_{3}\cdot M_{2},...

(B.3)

For most distributions with positive support,
M_{m}
approximately follows an
arithmetic sequence
: the spacing
M_{m+1}-M_{m}
is approximately constant with
m
. Therefore, these product relations imply

N_{3}\approx M_{2.5},\quad N_{4}\approx M_{3},\quad N_{5}\approx M_{3.5},\quad\ldots

(B.4)

The numerical results in the main text use
N_{m}
, defined in Appendix
A
, as the effective multiplicity in the subtraction formula Eq. (
IV.10
). We have also explored using
M_{m}
in place of
N_{m}
, and find empirically that
M_{m}
sometimes yields
R_{\mathcal{O}}
ratios closer to unity in HIJING. Specifically, the scaled ratio

\frac{M_{m}}{M_{m,\mathrm{pp}}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{O}\{m\}}{\mathcal{O}\{m\}_{\mathrm{pp}}}\right)^{1/(m-1)}\approx 1

(B.5)

holds in many cases more precisely than the analogous ratio with
N_{m}
, particularly for
c_{n}\{4\}
at RHIC energies and for
\left\langle v_{2}^{2}\delta p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
in all systems. The improvement is most pronounced for the four-particle cumulant (
m=4
), where
N_{4}
and
M_{3}
differ by up to 30% for broad LHC
pp
distributions, and this discrepancy enters cubed in the subtraction.

The reason why
M_{m}
outperforms
N_{m}
is unclear to us. One possible interpretation is that the independent-source scaling
\sim 1/N^{m-1}
in
hijing
is only an approximation: the actual scaling is better described by some intermediate power between the harmonic and arithmetic averages of
N
over the event sample. Another possibility is that correlations between multiplicity and jet activity introduce a correction that happens to be better captured by the arithmetic mean. We defer this analysis to future work.
