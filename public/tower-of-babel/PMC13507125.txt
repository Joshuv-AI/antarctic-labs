Self-triggered prescribed-time time varying formation control of second-order multi-agent systems with denial of service (DoS) attacks
Authors: Manmohan Sharma, Harshavarthini Shanmugam, Soumya Ranjan Mahapatro
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A prescribed-time time varying formation control of second-order multi-agent systems with DoS attacks is proposed in this article. Firstly, a prescribed-time control law is proposed under time-triggered scenario when the agents are affected with DoS attacks. This is further extended to the self-triggered scenario under DoS attacks. An undirected graph and time varying formation is considered in developing the control law. Rigorous mathematical proof is given to validate the control law. Simulation results in both time-triggered and self-triggered scenario under DoS attacks is presented to highlight the effectiveness of the approach in this article. Comparison results are also shown the bandwidth efficiency of the proposed approach.

Introduction

Cooperative control of multi-agent systems has gained considerable interest due to its wide applications in tasks such as formation flying, coordinated robotics, and sensor networks. Among these, formation control stands out as a critical area, aiming to develop control algorithms that enable a group of agents to maintain a prescribed geometric configuration in space. A key performance metric in formation control is the fast and reliable convergence rate, which directly influences the responsiveness and effectiveness of the system.

Early methods achieved only asymptotic convergence, resulting in infinite convergence time1–3, which is impractical for time-sensitive tasks. To overcome this, finite-time convergence strategies were developed4–7, though their convergence time depends on initial conditions. Subsequently, fixed-time formation control methods emerged to guarantee convergence within a uniform time bound, independent of initial states8,9. While fixed-time control offers uniform convergence, it still lacks flexibility since the convergence time cannot be preassigned.

To overcome these limitations, prescribed-time control has emerged as a powerful alternative10–13. It allows the user to specify the exact settling time in advance, regardless of the initial conditions or system parameters. Furthermore, it ensures smooth control inputs, exhibits strong robustness to external disturbances, and does not require knowledge of bounds on non-vanishing perturbations. These favorable properties have motivated extensive research into prescribed-time control and its applications11–16.

In recent years, increasing attention has been given to formation control of multi-agent systems under Denial-of-Service (DoS) attacks, particularly through event-triggered control to reduce communication load and enhance resilience. Various strategies have been proposed, including finite-time17 and dynamic event-triggered approaches for mobile robots18,19, intelligent vehicles, and unmanned surface or aerial vehicles20. Some studies also address additional challenges such as actuator faults, communication constraints, and system uncertainties using tools like neural networks and model predictive control21–25.

A time-varying formation tracking (TVFT) control method is developed in23 for multi-uncrewed aerial vehicles (UAVs) with unknown nonlinearities, while24 addresses secure formation tracking with communication bandwidth optimization under DoS constraints. A fault-tolerant dynamic event-triggered control scheme is proposed in25 for unmanned surface vehicles (USVs) experiencing both actuator faults and DoS attacks. In26, event-triggered consensus control is investigated for deterministic nonlinear MASs under DoS scenarios. The problem of distributed formation tracking control for second-order multiagent systems with unknown inertias is studied in27 while formation control in presence of disturbances and input constraints is studied in28. Additionally,29 introduces a fault-tolerant control strategy for second-order MASs with prescribed-time convergence. Moreover, in30 solves time-varying formation control with finite-time prescribed performance for nonstrict feedback second-order multi-agent systems. Furthermore, other types of attacks affecting nonlinear systems in event-triggered scenario has been compensated in31–33.

Although existing works on formation control under Denial-of-Service (DoS) attacks improve resilience and reduce communication overhead, most approaches guarantee only asymptotic or finite-time convergence, with performance often dependent on initial conditions and system parameters. In particular, user-defined convergence time is rarely achieved in the presence of DoS attacks and system uncertainties. Moreover, fault-tolerant designs that also consider communication bandwidth constraints remain limited in scope.

These observations highlight the need for a robust and efficient prescribed-time time varying formation control framework that ensures reliable convergence within a time defined by the user, irrespective of the initial conditions, while also addressing actuator faults and preserving communication resources. Motivated by this gap, the present article proposes a prescribed-time time-varying formation control strategy for second-order multi-agent systems under DoS attacks, with an emphasis on bandwidth efficiency. The developed self-triggered strategy obtains the next triggering instant from the last triggering instant and hence avoids continuous monitoring of the states. This is the shortcoming of the existing event-triggered strategies where continuous monitoring of states are required. The proposed self-triggered strategy saves the communication bandwidth in both sensor-controller channel as well as controller-sensor channel. Moreover, the prescribed-time convergence property not only enhances robustness but also allows for faster mitigation of DoS effects by guaranteeing precise timing in the recovery and formation processes. Denial of service attacks

DoS attacks affect the communication channel between the agents. In this article it is assumed that the attacks result in loss of transmission of information to the agents connected to the channel35,36. This means that the topology of the communication link is changed under DoS attacks. In this work it is assumed that the undirected graph without DoS attacks contains a spanning tree. A DoS attack is active for a time of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta _a$$\end{document} during the time interval [τ ,t]. That is DoS attack time is given by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$[\tau ,\tau +\Delta _a]$$\end{document} while the inactive time is given by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$[\tau +\Delta _a,t]$$\end{document} which is denoted by the sleep time \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta _s$$\end{document}. Each attack duration results in different graph topology which may contain more than one connected components. In other words, the graph topology may not contain a spanning tree during the attack period.

Remark 3

The spectral radius of the original graph without DoS attacks will be larger than the spectral radius of the graph with DoS attacks. Therefore, in (35), only spectral radius of the original topology is enough which is not difficult to obtain.

Remark 4

The above self-triggered strategy obtains the next triggering instant from the last triggering instant and hence avoids continuous monitoring of the states. This is the shortcoming of the existing event-triggered strategies where continuous monitoring of states are required. The proposed self-triggered strategy saves the communication bandwidth in both sensor-controller channel as well as controller-sensor channel.

Conclusion

This article presented a prescribed-time time-varying formation control framework for second-order multi-agent systems operating under Denial-of-Service (DoS) attacks using both time-triggered and self-triggered communication strategies. Initially, a prescribed-time formation control protocol was developed to guarantee convergence of second-order multi-agent systems to the desired time-varying formation despite the presence of DoS-induced communication interruptions. Subsequently, the proposed framework was extended to a self-triggered control architecture, significantly reducing communication and computation overhead while still ensuring prescribed-time convergence under adversarial network conditions. Stability and convergence properties of the proposed methods were analytically established, and extensive simulation studies, along with comparative performance evaluations, validated the effectiveness, robustness, and superiority of the proposed approaches over conventional methods. One of the limitation of the proposed work is that it has been developed for a simple second order system only. Therefore, future work will focus on extending the proposed prescribed-time resilient formation control strategy to higher-order multi-agent systems and more complex networked dynamical environments.
