Impact evaluation of a Prescription Search Support (PSS) for antimicrobials: protocol for a stepped-wedge cluster randomised trial in Belgian primary care
Authors: Jan Yvan Verbakel, Hannelore Dillen, Annelien Dankaerts, Nele Michiels, Bert Vaes, Tine De Burghgraeve
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionInappropriate antibiotic prescribing in primary care remains a major driver of antimicrobial resistance, despite national guidelines and audit-and-feedback systems. Digital antimicrobial stewardship tools may support clinicians in making evidence-based prescribing decisions. The Prescription Search Support (PSS) tool translates guideline recommendations into an interactive decision tree integrated into electronic health records (EHR). This project aims to evaluate the impact of implementing the tool on antibiotic prescribing behaviour in Belgian general practice and to understand the contextual factors influencing its adoption.Methods and analysisThis stepped-wedge cluster randomised trial includes four steps over 12 months. Belgian general practices were randomised in a 1:3:3:3 ratio using stratified block randomisation. All practices start in the control condition, where physicians are expected to provide usual care. At each step, one cluster transitions to the intervention, where PSS becomes accessible through the EHR. The primary objective is to assess whether the implementation of the tool reduces practice-level antibiotic prescribing rates. The primary analysis will use generalised linear mixed models with a logit link, accounting for clustering, time effects and relevant covariates. Secondary analyses include subgroup analyses by region, EHR vendor and intensity of tool use. A nested process evaluation, combining user log file analysis and semistructured interviews, will assess usability, acceptability, fidelity and mechanisms of impact. Sample size calculations indicate that at least 36–48 practices are required to detect a 5% absolute reduction in prescribing with 80% power. All quantitative analyses will be performed in R, and qualitative data will be analysed using reflexive thematic analysis.Ethics and disseminationThe study will be conducted in compliance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki (current version) and the principles of Good Clinical Practice and in accordance with all applicable regulatory requirements. Ethics approval for this study was obtained on 10 October 2025 from the Ethical Committee Research UZ/KU Leuven under reference MP037810. We will ensure that the findings of the study will be disseminated to relevant stakeholders beyond the scientific world including the public, healthcare providers and policy makers. The process evaluation that is part of this trial will inform the further implementation strategy. It will be essential to educate physicians on using this new PSS tool in daily practice.Trial registration numberNCT07347015.

Introduction

Inappropriate antibiotic prescribing in primary care contributes to the development of antimicrobial resistance.1 Despite guidelines and public health efforts, overprescribing remains prevalent, especially for self-limiting infections which often resolve without antibiotic treatment.1 Digital decision support tools such as a Prescription Search Support (PSS) may aid general practitioners (GPs) in making more informed prescribing decisions.2

Antibiotic use in the community in Belgium exceeds the European average, with a mean consumption of 19.0 Defined Daily Doses per 1000 inhabitants per day (DID) in 2024, compared with 18.8 DID in Europe.3 This also exceeds neighbouring countries such as the Netherlands (9.0 DID) and Germany (12.1 DID) but is lower than France (24.7 DID).3 The Council of the European Union sets European targets of a 20% reduction in total antibiotic consumption in humans between 2019 and 2030.3 The Belgian Antibiotic Policy Coordination Commission (BAPCOC) promotes responsible antibiotic use4 and develops guidelines for treating infections.5 Antibiotic use in primary care in Belgium is closely monitored through the Antibiotic Barometer (AB Barometer), a national quality measurement system coordinated by the Academic Centre of General Practice of the KU Leuven and financed by the National Institute for Health and Disability Insurance (RIZIV-INAMI).6 7 The AB Barometer provides quarterly feedback to GP practices based on automatically extracted electronic health record (EHR) data.6 7 It offers insights into the incidence of infections, overall prescribing volumes, first-choice antibiotic prescribing and quinolone prescribing.6 7 Practices can benchmark their results against peers within their front line zone, at the provincial level and nationally.6 7 Participation is voluntary.6 7 Since its inception in 2023, the AB Barometer has been a cornerstone of Belgian antimicrobial stewardship policy, providing dynamic benchmarking to support practice-level feedback and continuous quality improvement.6 7 In September 2024, 10 486 GPs from 3724 practices were participating in the AB Barometer.7 Despite this feedback mechanism, inappropriate antibiotic use remains prevalent.1 Therefore, integrating real-time clinical decision support systems like the PSS offers a complementary strategy to reinforce responsible prescribing behaviour at the point of care,2 while leveraging the AB Barometer as both a baseline reference and an external validation metric for intervention effects.

Digital antimicrobial stewardship (AMS) interventions such as web-based platforms, smartphone applications and online training may improve doctors’ knowledge of antimicrobials and guideline recommendations,8 9 promote adherence to guidelines10 and reduce antibiotic prescribing.8 The integration of prescribing guidelines into EHRs and clinical workflows offers advantages, including easy access,8 10 broad dissemination of current evidence8 10 and flexibility in formats.8 Additionally, presenting the rationale behind antibiotic recommendations in a clear, digital format, rather than in large amounts of text, may enhance the readability and comprehensibility of guidelines.11

Methods and analysis

Objectives

This implementation project is part of the Belgian implementation strategy of the PSS, mandated by the National Institute for Health and Disability Insurance (RIZIV-INAMI). The project builds upon a previously conducted mixed-methods implementation study evaluating the usability of the web-based PSS.12 The study is conducted in close collaboration with RIZIV-INAMI and Smals, an ICT company, who ensures operational support and technical maintenance. Findings from this project will directly inform policy decisions regarding national rollout and further development of the PSS.

Primary objective

To evaluate whether the PSS reduces antibiotic prescribing rates in Belgian general practice. The project will assess whether implementation of the PSS tool leads to improvements in specific prescribing indicators as monitored by the Belgian AB Barometer. These include the total antibiotic prescribing rate (primary endpoint in this trial), the first choice antibiotic prescribing rate (secondary endpoint) and the quinolone prescribing rate (secondary endpoint), all at the level of the GP practice.6 7

Design

For the quantitative outcomes, we apply a stepped-wedge cluster randomised design (figure 1) to evaluate whether the PSS tool leads to improved effectiveness compared with usual care (ie, superiority design). This trial design allows for sequential roll-out across participating GP practices while maintaining internal validity and logistical feasibility, and was preferred over a parallel-group design because all practices will ultimately receive the intervention as part of the national roll-out.13 All participating practices start in the control condition and transition to the intervention condition (PSS) in four steps over a 12-month period. Each cluster contributes data in both control and intervention periods, allowing both within-cluster and between-cluster comparisons. This design ensures equitable access to the intervention across all regions, for all existing prescribing software packages (n=8), and is logistically aligned with national deployment timelines coordinated by RIZIV-INAMI.

Randomisation

To avoid bias due to physicians working in the same practice, randomisation was performed at the level of the practice. GP practices were randomised in one of the four clusters in a 1:3:3:3 ratio using a block randomisation system stratified by rurality and type of practice (single physician or duo/group practice). A smaller first cluster was planned to allow for the identification and resolution of any technical issues early in the implementation. Block randomisation was used to guarantee that allocation to one of the four clusters is balanced within every rurality category and type of practice. Stratified block randomisation was done using an electronic random numbers generator in blocks of six practices. Randomisation and concealment were centralised at KU Leuven and conducted by a staff member not involved in data collection or delivering the intervention.

(Un)blinding

Owing to study procedures, physicians, patients and researchers are not masked to the practices’ random allocation. Conditions and procedures for unblinding are not required as the participating physicians will be aware of their allocation.

Recruitment was supported by institutional stakeholders including RIZIV-INAMI, Federal Public Service (FPS) Health and university-based GP networks. Their endorsement ensures visibility and trust among participating practices. Recruitment occurred upfront and the roll-out in waves, with at least 11 practices recruited before the start of the first step (corresponding to the sample size calculation) and recruitment completed before the start of the second step.

Participation requires no additional clinical documentation burden and offers real-time access to a validated decision support tool. There is only a remuneration (book voucher) in place for the participants taking part in the semistructured interviews (cf. ‘Process evaluation’). Participants will receive this remuneration only if they have successfully completed the interview. The amount and method of this remuneration were not explicitly mentioned in the recruitment materials, as per the guidelines of the UZ Leuven Ethical Committee Research.14

Practice identification

Interested physicians received a personal link to an online form (LimeSurvey). First, GPs are informed about the study by an animated video. Second, eligibility criteria are checked. Also, GPs are asked to provide contact details of their colleagues, so they can also be invited to take part in the study. GPs willing to participate in the study are asked to electronically sign the informed consent form in LimeSurvey. If necessary, reminders were sent by e-mail and/or GPs (or the practice office) were contacted by telephone.

Trial assessments

At study entry, baseline characteristics such as gender, years of clinical experience, location and type of practice, and medical software used were collected for each participating physician through the online form (LimeSurvey).

The measurement of the antibiotic prescribing rates will be aligned with the indicators tracked by the AB Barometer (ie, every 3 months, figure 1).

To capture user interactions with the tool, data will be obtained from log files. The following will be registered: (1) the number of selected recommendations, (2) the number of seconds between selecting the diagnosis and selecting the recommendation, (3) the content of the diagnoses and recommendations, (4) whether the link to the BAPCOC guideline is opened and (5) the reason for deviation when selecting a non-recommended choice.

The user interaction data will be matched with the prescription data by using the RIZIV-INAMI-IDs of the participating physicians. The matching will be performed by a researcher not involved in the further analyses.

Participant retention will be supported through regular communication, minimal study burden and timely reminders before each data collection period. For practices that discontinue or deviate from the intervention, primary outcome data will still be obtained through the AB Barometer, allowing complete follow-up at practice level.

Usual care

In the control arm, patients will receive ‘usual care’ left at the discretion of the treating physician. They are expected (but not forced) to follow the Belgian guidelines (as described in the BAPCOC national guidelines5 and the RIZIV-INAMI consensus meeting ‘Rational use of antibiotics in children’15).

Intervention: Prescription Search Support

The digital AMS intervention, PSS, was developed as part of the Belgian National Action Plan on AMR16 by the FPS Health, Food Chain Safety and Environment, the RIZIV-INAMI and by collaborating with various experts and stakeholders (ie, physicians, infectiologists, hygienists, policy makers, guideline developers and software developers).2 The company Smals under instruction of RIZIV-INAMI has developed the software for this application, in close collaboration with the RIZIV-INAMI working group.2 Guideline recommendations regarding appropriate prescribing of antimicrobials in primary care (ie, the BAPCOC guideline5) were converted to decision rules, which serve as the knowledge source for the PSS.2

The PSS contains the current recommendations for the proper use of antimicrobial agents for common infections (table 2) in primary care in Belgium.2 In other words, it represents the BAPCOC guidelines5 in a digital and user-friendly environment, by using a decision tree.2

During the whole study period, the PSS is accessible through the EHR of the physician. The integration follows a phased approach: EHR vendors may initially provide a single sign-on functionality while working towards the planned full integration (via web components or FHIR API).2

To familiarise physicians with the PSS in advance, we strongly encourage them to watch an e-learning course, without obligation. This contains more information about PSS, as well as practical exercises.

To inform patients about the ongoing research project, we provide a poster (both paper and digital) for the waiting room of doctors who are using PSS.

Harms

As the trial evaluates a low-risk digital tool, harms are defined as unintended consequences of the intervention. Potential harms include underprescribing, which refers to antibiotic prescribing rates falling below the acceptable use range for diagnoses where antibiotics are clinically indicated, such as pneumonia or cystitis. This will be monitored through routinely collected AB Barometer data. Workflow-related issues or technical problems will be captured in the process evaluation through interviews and descriptive summaries of reported issues.

No formal correction for multiple testing will be applied; secondary outcomes will be interpreted as exploratory.

No interim analyses are planned, and no formal stopping guidelines have been defined, as the intervention poses minimal risk and relies on routinely collected data. The trial will run for the full planned duration unless required otherwise by the ethics committee. Only the principal investigators will have oversight of trial conduct, but no interim results will be accessed for decision-making.

Missing data will be addressed using appropriate imputation methods.

All statistical analyses for the quantitative part of the project will be performed using R.

Information about user interactions with the application (ie, fidelity, dose and reach) will be summarised descriptively and will be displayed graphically, using Microsoft Excel.

Sample size calculations are based on a baseline antibiotic prescribing rate of 30%,17 an anticipated absolute reduction of 5%,17 and an intracluster correlation coefficient (ICC) of 0.0625. Assuming four steps, five time periods, 25 patient encounters per cluster period, and 80% power, a minimum of approximately 36–48 GP practices are required. Sample size estimates were derived using the Hemming et al formula for stepped-wedge designs,13 implemented via a custom R function and confirmed through sensitivity analyses using the SWSamp package. Sample size includes allowance for potential attrition of clusters.

As this implementation project is part of a wider rollout of the PSS by RIZIV-INAMI, a larger number of practices will be allowed to use the PSS.

Process evaluation

A process evaluation will be embedded, consisting of semistructured interviews using a topic guide (online supplemental file 4) and user log file analysis to assess implementation fidelity, reach and mechanisms of impact. During the interviews, field notes will be taken to identify key elements, non-verbal communication and speaker pauses.

For the semistructured interviews, we will include a small sample of GPs (approximately 11–13, both Dutch speaking and French speaking). The sample size of 11–13 participants is based on previous qualitative studies in this research field18 and allows for sufficient variation in user experiences.

When possible, participants for the semistructured interviews will be purposively sampled from those volunteering to participate, obtaining a maximum variation for gender, years of clinical experience (ie, year of obtaining the basic medical degree), institution at which the basic medical degree was obtained (ie, KU Leuven, Universiteit Antwerpen, Universiteit Gent, VUB/ULB, UCLouvain, ULiège, UMONS, UNamur), type of practice (ie, solo, duo or group practice), type of software package used and region of the practice (ie, province, rural or urban). We will contact interested physicians face-to-face, by email and/or by telephone to schedule a convenient time for the semistructured interviews and to gather demographical information. When needed, reminders will be sent every 2 weeks.

Interviews of the nested qualitative study will be audio recorded and transcribed verbatim. Participants will be pseudonymised prior to analysis. Reflexive thematic analysis as described by Braun and Clarke19 will be used to analyse the transcripts. First, the researchers will familiarise themselves with the data by listening repeatedly to the recorded interviews while reading the transcripts. Next, the researchers will individually code the entire dataset and generate initial themes. The initial codes and themes will be reviewed together with the whole researcher group. Finally, the list of codes and themes will be iteratively reviewed and refined. All analyses will be performed using NVivo.

We will also explore whether certain characteristics of the participants might explain possible variations in the observed outcomes (ie, user interactions and user experiences) by performing subgroup analyses. We will examine the following characteristics: gender, year of graduation as basic medical doctor, whether the GP is in training or has completed training, institution at which the basic medical degree was obtained, location of the practice, size and type of practice, and type of software used in the practice.

Data handling

The study will rely on data from EHR systems, AB Barometer metrics and log data from the PSS tool. Data linkage will be managed via KU Leuven. PSS log data will be securely transferred by Smals for integration, which will be described in a data processing agreement.

For the collection and storage of the data, we use OneDrive for Business, provided by KU Leuven. All data will be treated confidentially and with due care during the project. In function of our research question, data will be collected by means of log files, prescribing metrics and interviews. Only relevant information will be collected and this will be limited to what is necessary for the purposes of the research question. The information obtained through the research will be collected in a structured manner in a secured computer folder. Data allowing identification of participating GPs will be pseudonymised by a researcher not involved in the further analyses. This means that personal data will be processed in such a way that it can no longer be attributed to a specific person without the use of additional information. However, the direct link between the data subject and the dataset will not be broken, but will be encoded using a code. This code contains neither the name, nor initials, nor elements that could lead to identification (eg, date of birth or place of birth). The codes will be kept in a separate and encrypted file, only in the possession of the researchers involved. This file will not be kept on the same laptop or desktop as the data files.

AB Barometer metrics are processed through Healthdata, a national data platform, and the procedures are approved by the Belgian Privacy Commission (no. SCSZG/18/066).

Anything said by the participants during the semistructured interviews will be recorded via audio recordings. The audio recordings will only be listened to and transcribed by the researchers. Immediately after transcription, the audio recordings and the interviewer’s personal notes will be destroyed. Transcripts of the interviews will not include names or any other data that will allow identification. In the actual publication, some excerpts from the interviews will be quoted in an anonymous manner and in such a way that it is not possible to trace the statement back to a particular participant. The transcripts will be kept in a secure computer folder that is only in the possession of the researchers involved. This file will be kept in a project- or team-based storage location in OneDrive for Business, offered by KU Leuven.

When sending a copy of confidential information or documents, they will always be encrypted with a password or unique code. For sending large files, Belnet Filesender will be used. Upon completion of the project, files will be delivered to the supervisor who will further ensure their safe keeping. In accordance with KU Leuven policy, these files will be kept for 10 years, after which it will be reassessed whether it is considered useful to keep the data any longer.

Data monitoring

The investigator will permit trial-related monitoring, and audits, providing direct access to all related documents. A data monitoring committee is not needed given the minimal risk intervention and the absence of interim analyses.

Public and patient involvement

The PSS tool was developed with regular input from a wide range of stakeholders, including general practitioners, hospital physicians, infectiologists, hygienists, pharmacists, policy makers, guideline developers and software developers. This collaborative work was coordinated by RIZIV-INAMI through expert meetings and iterative feedback on the decision rules.

Patients and members of the public are not involved in the design of the study, the development of the research questions, the choice of outcome measures, the conduct of the trial or the plans for dissemination of the study results.

Ethics and dissemination

The study will be conducted in compliance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki (current version) and the principles of Good Clinical Practice and in accordance with all applicable regulatory requirements. Approval of this protocol including statistical analysis plan (online supplemental file 1), the informed consent forms (online supplemental file 2) and other related documents, for example, advertisements and physician information letters, was obtained on 10 October 2025 from the Ethical Committee Research UZ/KU Leuven under reference MP037810. Participating physicians will provide informed consent.

Any important protocol modifications (eg, changes in eligibility criteria, outcomes or study procedures) will be submitted for approval to the ethics committee and communicated to participating practices, institutional partners (RIZIV-INAMI, Smals) and other relevant stakeholders. Updated versions of the protocol will be shared through the study website and direct email communication to ensure all parties are informed.

The Federal Agency for Medicines and Health Products confirmed that PSS falls out of scope of the medical device regulation.

The AB Barometer was approved by the Social and Societal Ethical Committee under G-2023-6352.

We will ensure that the findings of the study will be disseminated to relevant stakeholders beyond the scientific world, including the public, healthcare providers and policy makers.

The process evaluation that is part of this trial will inform the further implementation strategy. It will be essential to educate physicians on using this new PSS tool in daily practice. This will require training which should be delivered through accredited Continued Medical Education but should also be part of the basic medical curriculum for physicians.
