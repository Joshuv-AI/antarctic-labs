Nature of Work
Describe the research (summary of Scope of Work and principal objectives of the CRADA):
This work focused on improving the understanding and modeling of conjugate heat transfer in the Stable Salt
Reactor (SSR), particularly the transfer of heat from molten fuel salt inside narrow fuel pins, through the metal
cladding and into the surrounding coolant salt. The principle objectives include:
 (1) Develop high-fidelity thermal hydraulic models of the SSR fuel pins using Nek5000/NekRS CFD codes to
 simulate natural convection inside the fuel pins, forced convection in the external coolant salt, and conjugate
 heat transfer between fuel salt, cladding and coolant salt
 (2) Quantify difference between the Large Eddy Simulation (LES) and Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes
 (RANS) turbulence models
 (3) Generate high-quality therml-hydraulic data to improve and calibrate heat transfer models implemented in
 SAM system analysis code, enabling faster-running transient simulations
ANL-1060 (02/13/2023)

 (4) Support the engineering design and safety analysis for the SSR through integrated CFD and system-level
 safety analyses

Conclusions drawn from this CRADA; include any major accomplishments:
Conclusions and Major Accomplishments:
 (1) High-fidelity CFD successfully characterized heat transfer in SSR fuel pins. The NekRS simulations resolve
 the coupled physics of natural convection within the fuel salt, heat conduction through the cladding, and
 forced convection in the external coolant salt
 (2) Fuel-side heat transfer is the dominant thermal resistance. Fuel-side heat transfer coefficients ranged from
 1050-1350 W/m^2-K and coolant side htc were significantly higher around 7300-7600 W/m^2-K
 (3) NekRS and SAM showed excellent agreement. This is one of the most important accomplishments. The
 calibrated SAM model reproduced fuel, cladding and coolant temperatures with high accuracy. Achieved
 temperature deviations were below 2% for fuel, and below 1% for cladding and coolant temperatures.
 (4) The effective thermal conductivity methodology was developed. This is a key innovation of the project. A
 three-region effective thermal conductivity for the fuel salt successfully captured internal natural convection,
 thermal mixing, and radial temperature gradients.
 (5) Successful application of NekNek coupling methodology. This work demonstrated the use of NekNek
 overlapping-grid coupling for conjugate heat transfer simulations, enabling accurate temperature exchange
 across fuel, cladding, and coolant domains.
 (6) Generation of high-fidelity database for model development. The project produced a comprehensive
 database of temperature distributions, heat fluxes, heat transfer coefficients, and effective thermal
 conductivities across a range of normal and transient operating conditions. This database was used directly
 to improveSAM heat transfer models.
 (7) Integration of CFD-informed models in SAM. A major deliverable was the implementation of CFD-infomed
 heat transfer closures in SAM, significantly improving its predictive capability for SSR steady state and
 transient analyses.
 (8) Demonstration of GPU-accelerated reactor-scale CFD. The simulations were performed on Frontier HPC
 demonstrating the ability of NekRS to perform reactor-scale, high-fidelity thermal-hydraulic analyses on
 moden exascale computing platforms.

Summary of technology transfer benefits to industry and, if applicable, path forward/anticipated next steps
towards commercialization:
This CRADA developed a high-fidelity multiscale modeling framework for analyzing conjugate heat transfer in
Moltex’s SSR. The project successfully integrated high-fidelity CFD simulations performed in NekRS into the SAM
system analysis code, enabling accurate and computationally efficient prediction of fuel, cladding, and coolant
temperatures during both normal operation and transient conditions. The resulting methodology significantly
reduces uncertainty in thermal-hydraulic predictions and provides an improved basis for reactor design optimization,
safety analysis, and future licensing activities.

The project generated a comprehensive database of heat transfer coefficients, temperature distributions, and
effective thermal conductivity parameters that can be directly applied to SSR design and safety evaluations. The
modeling framework supports accelerated reactor development by reducing reliance on costly experimental testing
while improving confidence in reactor performance predictions.

The anticipated path forward includes continued refinement of CFD-informed models, extension of the methodology
to additional reactor operating scenarios and accident conditions, incorporation of the results into future SSR design
iterations, and application of the tools to support reactor licensing and commercialization activities.
