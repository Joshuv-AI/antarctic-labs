Nonequilibrium fluctuations and moderate deviations for the occupation time of the SSEP with Glauber dynamics Thanks: Acknowledgments. The project is supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China with grant numbers 12401168 and 12371142.
Linjie Zhao

Abstract
. We study the symmetric simple exclusion process with Glauber dynamics. When the process starts from a nonequilibrium measure, we prove central limit theorems for the occupation time in dimension two, and sample path moderate deviation principles in dimension one. For the fluctuations, we use the martingale method and the sharp relative entropy method from [ 13 ] . For the moderate deviations, the main idea is to relate the occupation time to the density fluctuation field by using the logarithmic Sobolev inequality from the Glauber dynamics.



1.
Introduction

The exclusion process is a prototype model in interacting particle systems and statistical physics. In the dynamics, particles perform random walks on some graph subject to the exclusion rule, that is, there is at most one particle at each vertex of the underlying graph. On top of the exclusion process, we add a Glauber dynamics, which means that particles can be created or destroyed at each vertex according to some rate depending on the configuration. This model was studied by De Masi, Ferrari and Lebowitz
[
4
]
in order to derive reaction diffusion equations from microscopic models, and has received much attention since then.

In this paper, we study additive functionals of the process. The study of fluctuations of additive functionals of particle systems has a long history. For exclusion processes with stationary initial distributions, this issue was first studied in
[
14
]
and
[
16
]
. In the latter paper, Kipnis and Varadhan introduced the well-known martingale method. Since then, stationary fluctuations for the additive functionals have been investigated in various settings
[
1
,
2
,
10
,
18
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
. We remark that in the previous literature the process starts from stationary measures. Less is known when the initial measure is not stationary. Recently, Xu, Erhard and Franco
[
5
]
studied nonequilibrium fluctuations for the occupation time of the symmetric simple exclusion process (SSEP) in dimension one. Their proof is based on self-duality of the SSEP and sharp correlation estimates. This result was extended by Xu and the author in
[
27
]
to higher dimensions by using the martingale method and correlation estimates. Both the previous papers used self-duality of the SSEP. For particle systems lack of self-duality, Xu and Fontes
[
7
]
investigated the weakly asymmetric simple exclusion process in dimension one by using the sharp relative entropy bound by Jara and Menezes
[
12
]
.

The first main result of this paper concerns about nonequilibrium fluctuations of the occupation time. Based on the techniques of
[
7
]
, one can prove directly the invariance principle for the degree-one additive functionals of the process in dimension one, see Propositions
2.5
and
2.6
. For this reason, we only state the result without proof and focus on higher dimensions. In Theorem
2.8
, we prove the central limit theorem for the occupation time in dimension two in the sense of finite dimensional distributions. At this point, we are not aware of how to prove the tightness of the occupation time since it is hard to obtain sharp correlation estimates as for the SSEP in
[
27
]
. It also remains open to extend the results to three or higher dimensions.

The proof of Theorem
2.8
follows from the martingale method. We decompose the occupation time as a martinale and some negligible term. Compared with the SSEP in
[
27
]
, we need to deal with higher-order terms due to the Glauber dynamics. These terms can be killed by using the main lemma and sharp relative entropy bound from
[
13
]
, which however only works in dimension two.

The second main result of this paper studies nonequilibrium moderate deviations of the occupation time. The scaling of moderate deviations is between large deviations and fluctuations. Large deviations for the occupation time of the exclusion process were studied in
[
17
,
3
]
. Recently, Gao and Quastel in
[
9
]
proved moderate deviation principles (MDP) for the occupation time of the SSEP on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and in
[
8
]
for the additive functionals of particle systems with mixing conditions at one time point. Their results were extended to sample path MDP for the SSEP on regular trees by Xue
[
29
,
28
]
, and for the SSEP on
\mathbb{Z}
by the author
[
30
]
. In Theorem
2.9
, we prove sample path MDP for the occupation time of the process with non-stationary initial measures in dimension one, and extend the result to degree-one additive functionals in Corollary
2.10
. As far as we know, this is the first result concerning about the MDP for the occupation time in the non-stationary setting. It remains an interesting question to extend the results to higher dimensions and to general additive functionals.

The main idea of the proof of Theorem
2.9
and Corollary
2.10
is as follows. In dimension one, one can relate the occupation time to the density fluctuation field of the process, as observed by Gonçalves and Jara
[
10
]
when considering equilibrium fluctuations of the occupation time. On the level of moderate deviations, this was done by the author in
[
30
]
in the stationary setting. When the initial measure is not stationary, we relate the occupation time to the density fluctuation field in Lemma
5.3
by using the logarithmic Sobolev inequality from the Glauber dynamics. Once this is done, by using the MDP for the density fluctuation field proved in
[
31
]
and the contraction principle, we prove sample path MDP for the occupation time.

The rest of the paper is as follows. In section
2
we introduce the model and state the main results rigorously. See Theorem
2.8
for the fluctuations of the occupation time in dimension two, and Theorem
2.9
for the moderate deviations. In section
3
we introduce some preliminary results used throughout the proof. Sections
4
and
5
are devoted to the proof of fluctuations and moderate deviations respectively.

2.
Model and results

The state space of the model is
\Omega_{n}^{d}:=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}
, where
\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}:=\mathbb{Z}^{d}/(n\mathbb{Z}^{d})
is the
d
-dimensional discrete torus with size
n\in\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
. For a configuration
\eta\in\Omega_{n}^{d}
,
\eta_{x}\in\{0,1\}
denotes the number of particles at site
x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}
. The infinitesimal generator of the process is
L_{n}=n^{2}L_{n}^{\rm ex}+L_{n}^{\rm r}
. Here, the generators
L_{n}^{\rm ex}
and
L_{n}^{\rm r}
correspond to the symmetric simple exclusion process (SSEP) and the Glauber dynamics respectively. Precisely, for any function
f:\Omega_{n}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle L_{n}^{\rm ex}f(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|x-y|=1}[f(\eta^{x,y})-f(\eta)],

\displaystyle L_{n}^{\rm r}f(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}c_{x}(\eta)[f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta)].

In this formula, the sum
\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|x-y|=1}
is taken over all unordered bonds of
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{n}
. For
x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}
,
\eta^{x,y}
is the configuration obtained from
\eta
by swapping the values of
\eta_{x}
and
\eta_{y}
, and
\eta^{x}
is the one by flipping the value of
\eta_{x}
, that is,

\eta^{x,y}_{z}=\begin{cases}\eta_{x},\quad&\text{if }z=y,\\
\eta_{y},\quad&\text{if }z=x,\\
\eta_{z},\quad&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\qquad\eta^{x}_{z}=\begin{cases}1-\eta_{x},\quad&\text{if }z=x,\\
\eta_{z},\quad&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The flipping rate
c_{x}(\eta)
is defined as

c_{x}(\eta)=\Big(a+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y:|y-x|=1}\eta_{y}\Big)(1-\eta_{x})+b\eta_{x},

where
a,b>0
and
\lambda>-a
are given parameters. Note that, when
a=0
,
L_{n}^{\rm r}
is the generator of the standard contact process, see
[
19
]
for example.

For
\rho\in(0,1)
, let
\nu^{n}_{\rho}
be the Bernoulli product measure on
\Omega_{n}^{d}
with density
\rho
,

\nu^{n}_{\rho}(\eta_{x}=1,\,\forall x\in A)=\rho^{|A|},\quad\forall A\subset\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}.

It is well known that the exclusion process with generator
L_{n}^{\rm ex}
is reversible with respect to the measure
\nu^{n}_{\rho}
, see
[
19
]
for example.

For
\rho\in[0,1]
, define

F(\rho)=E_{\nu^{n}_{\rho}}[c_{x}(\eta)(1-2\eta_{x})]=(a+\lambda\rho)(1-\rho)-b\rho.

Since
F
is a quadratic polynomial such that
F(0)=a>0,F(1)=-b<0
, there exists a unique point
\rho_{*}\in(0,1)
such that
F(\rho_{*})=0
.

Fix a time horizon
T>0
. Let
\eta(t)
be the process induced by the generator
L_{n}
and initial measure
\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}
. Note that
\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}
is not stationary for the process
\eta(t)
. We omit the dependence of
\eta(t)
on the scaling parameter
n
to simplify notations. Let
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
be the measure on the path space
D([0,T],\Omega_{n}^{d})
, equipped with the Skorokhod topology, induced by the process
\eta(t)
. Denote by
\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
the corresponding expectation.

We are interested in the occupation time of the process at the origin, which after rescaling is defined as

\Gamma^{n}(t)=\beta_{d,n}\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\eta}_{0}(s)ds,

where
\bar{\eta}_{0}=\eta_{0}-\rho_{*}
, and

(2.1)

\beta_{d,n}=\begin{cases}\sqrt{n},\quad&\text{if}\quad d=1,\\
\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log n}},\quad&\text{if}\quad d=2,\\
n,\quad&\text{if}\quad d\geq 3.\end{cases}

Remark 2.1
.

We rescale the occupation time by
\beta_{d,n}
since this is the right scaling for the occupation time of the SSEP
[
14
]
. Since the exclusion process is sped up by
n^{2}
while the Glauber dynamics is not, it is natural to expect that this is also the right scaling for the SSEP with Glauber dynamics.

2.1.
Fluctuations

In this subsection, we study fluctuations for the occupation time.

2.1.1.
The case
d=1

To state the result in dimension one, we need to introduce the density fluctuation field of the process. Let
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T})
be the space of smooth functions on
\mathbb{T}
equipped with the uniform topology, and let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
be its topological dual. We equip the space
D([0,T],\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}))
of Càdlàg trajectories with the uniform weak topology: a sequence
\{\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\}_{n\geq 1}
converges to
\mu_{\cdot}
in
D([0,T],\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}))
if and only if for any
H\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T})
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},H\big\rangle-\big\langle\mu_{t},H\big\rangle|=0.

The density fluctuation field of the process is defined as

\mathcal{Y}^{n}_{t}(H)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(t)H(\tfrac{x}{n}),\quad H\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}).

The following result was proved in
[
13
]
, see also
[
11
, Proposition 4.1]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

There exists
\lambda_{c}>0
such that for any
\lambda\in[-\lambda_{c},\lambda_{c}]
, the sequence of processes
\{\mathcal{Y}^{n}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}
converges in distribution, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, in the space
D([0,T],\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}))
to the process
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}
, the unique solution to the following SPDE:

\partial_{t}\mathcal{Y}_{t}=\Delta\mathcal{Y}_{t}+F^{\prime}(\rho_{*})\mathcal{Y}_{t}+\sqrt{2\chi(\rho_{*})}\nabla\mathcal{\dot{W}}_{t}^{1}+\sqrt{G(\rho_{*})}\mathcal{\dot{W}}_{t}^{2}.

Here,
\mathcal{\dot{W}}_{t}^{1}
and
\mathcal{\dot{W}}_{t}^{2}
are two independent
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
-valued white noises, and

\chi(\rho)=\rho(1-\rho),\quad G(\rho)=F(\rho)+2b\rho.

Moreover, the distribution of
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(H)
is normal with mean zero and variance
\chi(\rho_{*})\|H\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}
for any
H\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T})
.

Remark 2.3
.

Central limit theorems for the fluctuation field also hold for
d=2,3
in the sense of finite dimensional distributions. We state the result only in one dimension since this is what we need in this article.

Remark 2.4
.

In the rest of this article, we fix
\lambda_{c}
small enough so that Proposition
2.2
and (
5.7
) below hold, and choose the parameter
\lambda\in[-\lambda_{c},\lambda_{c}]
.

Let
C([0,T],\mathbb{R})
be the space of continuous functions on
[0,T]
equipped with the uniform topology. Let
J\geq 0
be a standard mollifier, that is,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(u)du=1,\quad J(u)=J(-u),\quad{\rm supp}\;J\subset(-1,1).

For any
\varepsilon>0
, define
J_{\varepsilon}(u)=\varepsilon^{-1}J(u/\varepsilon),\;u\in\mathbb{R}
. Intuitively, for
\varepsilon
small,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Y}^{n}_{s}(J_{\varepsilon})ds

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)J_{\varepsilon}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds

\displaystyle\approx\sqrt{n}\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\eta}_{0}(s)ds.

Thus, the limit of
\Gamma^{n}(t)
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, should coincide with that of

Z_{t}^{\varepsilon}:=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Y}_{s}(J_{\varepsilon})ds

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. This is the content of the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}
be the process introduced in Proposition
2.2
. The following statements hold.

(i)

The process
\{Z_{t}^{\varepsilon},0\leq t\leq T\}
converges in distribution, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, to a Gaussian process
\{Z_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}
in the space
C([0,T],\mathbb{R})
.

(ii)

The process
\{\Gamma^{n}(t),0\leq t\leq T\}
converges in distribution, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, to the same process
\{Z_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}
as in
(i)
in the space
C([0,T],\mathbb{R})
.

The above result can be extended to additive functionals with degree one. Precisely speaking, let
f:\Omega_{n}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a local function such that

(2.2)

\varphi_{f}(\rho_{*})=0,\quad\varphi_{f}^{\prime}(\rho_{*})\neq 0,

where
\varphi_{f}(\rho_{*})=E_{\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}}[f]
. Define

\Gamma^{n}_{f}(t)=\beta_{d,n}\int_{0}^{t}\big[f(\eta(s))-\varphi_{f}(\rho_{*})\big]ds.

Then, we have the following result.

Proposition 2.6
.

Assume that the local function
f:\Omega_{n}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies (
2.2
). Then,
the process
\{\Gamma_{f}^{n}(t),0\leq t\leq T\}
converges in distribution, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, to the process
\{\varphi_{f}^{\prime}(\rho_{*})Z_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}
.

Remark 2.7
.

The above results were proved by Fontes and Xu
[
7
]
for the one dimensional weakly asymmetric simple exclusion process. The main idea is to relate the occupation time to the fluctuation field by proving the local Boltzmann-Gibbs principle in the nonequilibrium setting. Since their argument can be adapted to our case directly, we omit it here.

For later use, let us denote by
\alpha(\cdot,\cdot)
the covariance function of the process
Z_{t}
,

(2.3)

\alpha(t,s)=\mathrm{Cov}(Z_{t},Z_{s}),\quad s,t\geq 0.

2.1.2.
The case
d=2

In dimension
d=2
, the limit of the occupation time is given by the Brownian motion, which is the first main result of this article.

Theorem 2.8
.

The sequence
\{\Gamma^{n}(t),0\leq t\leq T\}
converges in the sense of finite-dimensional distributions to the process
\{\sqrt{\chi(\rho_{*})/\pi}B(t),0\leq t\leq T\}
, where
B(\cdot)
is the standard one-dimensional Brownian motion.

2.2.
Moderate deviations

In this subsection, we study moderate deviations for the occupation time in
d=1
. Let
\{a_{n}\}
be a sequence of positive real numbers such that
1\ll a_{n}\ll\sqrt{n}
, where
a_{n}\ll b_{n}
denotes
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}/b_{n}=0
.
We will investigate the rescaled occupation time
\Gamma^{n}(t)/a_{n}
.

We first introduce the rate function. For any integer
k>0
, any sequence of times
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{k}\leq T
, and any path
\gamma\in C([0,T],\mathbb{R})
, let us denote
\Sigma_{k}=\Sigma_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}}
be the
k\times k
matrix with the
(i,j)
-th entry given by
\alpha(t_{i},t_{j})
. Denote
\gamma_{k}=(\gamma(t_{1}),\ldots,\gamma(t_{k}))^{\rm T}
. Then, the moderate deviation rate function is defined as

{\mathcal{I}}_{T}(\gamma)=\inf\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{k}^{\rm T}\Sigma_{k}^{-1}\gamma_{k}:t_{i}\in[0,T]\;\text{for}\;1\leq i\leq k,\;k\geq 1\Big\}

if
\gamma(0)=0
, and define
{\mathcal{I}}_{T}(\gamma)=+\infty
otherwise.

Below is the second main result of this article.

Theorem 2.9
.

As
n\rightarrow\infty
, the sequence
\{\Gamma^{n}(t)/a_{n},0\leq t\leq T\}
satisfies the moderate deviation principles with decay rate
a_{n}^{2}
and with rate function
{\mathcal{I}}_{T}
in the space
C([0,T],\mathbb{R})
. Precisely speaking,
for any closed set
C
and any open set
O
in
C([0,T],\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\{\Gamma^{n}(t)/a_{n},0\leq t\leq T\}\in C\Big)\leq-\inf_{\gamma\in C}\mathcal{I}_{T}(\gamma),

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\{\Gamma^{n}(t)/a_{n},0\leq t\leq T\}\in O\Big)\geq-\inf_{\gamma\in O}\mathcal{I}_{T}(\gamma).

The above result can also be extended to degree-one local functions.

Corollary 2.10
.

Assume that the local function
f:\Omega_{n}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies (
2.2
). Then, the sequence
\{\Gamma^{n}_{f}(t)/a_{n},0\leq t\leq T\}
satisfies the moderate deviation principles with decay rate
a_{n}^{2}
and with rate function
{\mathcal{I}}_{T}/\varphi_{f}^{\prime}(\rho_{*})
in the space
C([0,T],\mathbb{R})
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

3.
Preliminaries

In this section, we present several previous results that will be used throughout the proof.

3.1.
Random walk

Let
p_{n}(t):=\{p_{n}(t,x),x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\}
be the transition probability of the accelerated continuous time simple random walk on
\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}
, which jumps to one of its neighbors at rate
n^{2}
. Similarly, let
\mathfrak{p}(t):=\{\mathfrak{p}(t,x),x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
be the transition probability of the continuous time simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, which jumps to one of its neighbors at rate one. Then,

\partial_{t}p_{n}(t,x)=n^{2}\Delta_{\rm D}p_{n}(t,x),\quad p_{n}(0,x)=\delta_{0}(x).

In this formula,
\Delta_{\rm D}
is the discrete Laplacian,

\Delta_{\rm D}p(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big(p(x+e_{i})+p(x-e_{i})-2p(x)\big),

and
\delta_{0}
is the Delta function at the origin. Define

(3.1)

g_{n}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}p_{n}(t,x)dt,\quad x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}.

Then,

(3.2)

(1-n^{2}\Delta_{\rm D})g_{n}(x)=\delta_{0}(x).

The next lemma states some estimates for the function
g_{n}
, see
[
20
]
for example.

Lemma 3.1
.

(i)

There exists some constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)^{2}\leq C\begin{cases}n^{-d}\quad&\text{if}\quad d\leq 3;\\
n^{-4}\log n\quad&\text{if}\quad d=4;\\
n^{-4}\quad&\text{if}\quad d\geq 5.\end{cases}

(ii)

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n^{2}\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|x-y|=1}\big(g_{n}(x)-g_{n}(y)\big)^{2}=\begin{cases}(2\pi)^{-1}\quad&\text{if}\quad d=2;\\
\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{p}(t,0)dt\quad&\text{if}\quad d\geq 3.\end{cases}

The same limit holds with the left hand side replaced by
\beta_{d,n}^{2}g_{n}(0)
.

3.2.
Correlation estimate

The following two-point correlation estimate for the process was proved by De Masi, Ferrari and Lebowitz in
[
4
, Lemma 3.5]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists some constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\sup_{0\leq s\leq T}\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop x\neq y}\Big|\mathrm{Cov}(\eta_{x}(s),\eta_{y}(s))\Big|\leq Cn^{-d},

where the covariance is with respect to the measure
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
.

3.3.
Flow lemma

Let
q,q^{\prime}
be two probability measures on
\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}
. We say that
\phi:\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a flow connecting
q
and
q^{\prime}
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

\phi(x,y)=0
unless
|y-x|=1
;

(ii)

\phi
is anti-symmetric,
i.e.
,
\phi(x,y)=-\phi(y,x)
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}
;

(iii)

for any
x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}
,

\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\phi(x,y)=q(x)-q^{\prime}(x).

For any
x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}
and any integer
\ell<n
, let
\Lambda_{x,\ell}
be the cube of vertex
x
with length
\ell
,

\Lambda_{x,\ell}:=\{y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}:0\leq y_{i}-x_{i}\leq\ell-1,\;\forall 1\leq i\leq d\}.

Let
p_{\ell}
be the uniform measure on
\Lambda_{\ell}:=\Lambda_{0,\ell}
, that is,
p_{\ell}(y)=\ell^{-d}\mathbf{1}\{y\in\Lambda_{\ell}\}
for
y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}
.
For
\ell<n/2
, define
q_{\ell}
to be the convolution of
p_{\ell}
with itself,

q_{\ell}(x)=p_{\ell}*p_{\ell}(x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}p_{\ell}(y)p_{\ell}(x-y),\quad x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}.

The following result was proved by Jara and Menezes
[
12
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists a flow
\phi_{\ell}
connecting
\delta_{0}
and
q_{\ell}
such that

(i)

\phi_{\ell}(x,y)=0
if
x\notin\Lambda_{2\ell-1}
or
y\notin\Lambda_{2\ell-1}
;

(ii)

there exists some constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(d)
such that

\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|x-y|=1}\phi_{\ell}(x,y)^{2}\leq C_{0}g_{d}(\ell),

where

(3.3)

g_{d}(\ell)=\begin{cases}\ell,\quad&\text{if}\quad d=1,\\
\log\ell,\quad&\text{if}\quad d=2,\\
1,\quad&\text{if}\quad d\geq 3.\end{cases}

3.4.
Replacement estimate

Let
g:\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a given function. For any configuration
\eta\in\Omega_{n}^{d}
and any
1\leq i\leq d
, let us introduce

V_{i}(g,\eta)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}g(x).

For any
\ell<n/2
, define

(3.4)

\bar{\eta}_{x}^{\ell}=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}q_{\ell}(y)\bar{\eta}_{x+y},\quad V^{\ell}_{i}(g,\eta)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}^{\ell}_{x+e_{i}}g(x).

By direct calculations, we can rewrite

(3.5)

V^{\ell}_{i}(g,\eta)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\overleftarrow{\eta}_{x}^{\ell}(g)\overrightarrow{\eta}^{\ell}_{x+e_{i}},

where

\overleftarrow{\eta}_{x}^{\ell}(g)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}p_{\ell}(y)\bar{\eta}_{x-y}g(x-y),\quad\overrightarrow{\eta}^{\ell}_{x}=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}p_{\ell}(y)\bar{\eta}_{x+y}.

We also write
\overleftarrow{\eta}_{x}^{\ell}=\overleftarrow{\eta}_{x}^{\ell}(\mathbf{1})
, where
\mathbf{1}
is the constant function equal to one. Let
\Gamma_{n}^{\rm ex}
and
\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}
be the
Carré du champ
operators associated with the exclusion process and the Glauber dynamics respectively. Precisely, for functions
f,h:\Omega_{n}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}^{\rm ex}(f,h)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\{L_{n}^{\rm ex}(fh)-fL_{n}^{\rm ex}h-hL_{n}^{\rm ex}f\}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|x-y|=1}[f(\eta^{x,y})-f(\eta)][h(\eta^{x,y})-h(\eta)],

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}(f,h)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\{L_{n}^{\rm r}(fh)-fL_{n}^{\rm r}h-hL_{n}^{\rm r}f\}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}c_{x}(\eta)[f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta)][h(\eta^{x})-h(\eta)].

We also write
\Gamma_{n}^{\rm ex}(f)=\Gamma_{n}^{\rm ex}(f,f)
and similar notation for
\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}
.

The following estimate when replacing
V_{i}
by
V_{i}^{\ell}
was proved in
[
11
, Equation (3.7)]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f
be a
\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
-density. Then, for any
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle\int(V_{i}(g,\eta)-V_{i}^{\ell}(g,\eta))f(\eta)d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\leq\gamma\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm ex}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\int\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|y-z|=1}h_{y,z}^{\ell}(g,\eta)^{2}f(\eta)d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}.

In this formula,

h_{y,z}^{\ell}(g,\eta)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\phi_{\ell}(y-x,z-x)\bar{\eta}_{x-e_{i}}g(x-e_{i})

with
\phi_{\ell}
introduced in Lemma
3.3
.

3.5.
Relative entropy

For any two probability measures
\mu,\nu
on
\Omega_{n}^{d}
such that
\mu\ll\nu
, recall that the relative entropy of
\mu
with respect to
\nu
is defined as

H(\mu|\nu)=\int f\log fd\nu,\quad f=d\mu/d\nu.

Let
\mu^{n}_{t}
be the distribution of the process at time
t
. The following relative entropy bound was first proved by Jara and Menezes
[
13
]
, see also
[
11
]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

There exists some constant
C
such that for any
t>0
,

H(\mu^{n}_{t}|\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}})\leq Cn^{d-2}g_{d}(n).

4.
Fluctuations

In this section, we prove Theorem
2.8
. For a configuration
\eta\in\Omega_{n}^{d}
, define

G_{n}(\eta)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)\bar{\eta}_{x},

where
g_{n}(x)
was defined in (
3.1
). By Dynkin’s martingale formula,

M_{n}(t)=\beta_{d,n}G_{n}(\eta(t))-\beta_{d,n}G_{n}(\eta(0))-\beta_{d,n}\int_{0}^{t}L_{n}G_{n}(\eta(s))ds

is a martingale. By direct calculations,

n^{2}L_{n}^{\rm ex}G_{n}(\eta)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)n^{2}\Delta_{\rm D}\bar{\eta}_{x}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}n^{2}\Delta_{\rm D}g_{n}(x)=G_{n}(\eta)-\bar{\eta}_{0}.

In the second identity we used the summation by parts formula, and in the last one we used (
3.2
). For the Glauber dynamics,

L_{n}^{\rm r}G_{n}(\eta)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)c_{x}(\eta)(1-2\eta_{x}).

Thus,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{n}(t)=\beta_{d,n}G_{n}(\eta(0))

\displaystyle-\beta_{d,n}G_{n}(\eta(t))+\beta_{d,n}\int_{0}^{t}G_{n}(\eta(s))ds

\displaystyle+\beta_{d,n}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)c_{x}(\eta(s))(1-2\eta_{x}(s))ds+M_{n}(t).

In the following five subsections, we deal with the five terms on the right hand side of the last equation respectively.

4.1.
The initial term

In this subsection, we prove that

(4.2)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\big[\big\{\beta_{d,n}G_{n}(\eta(0))\big\}^{2}\big]=0.

Since the initial measure is a product measure, the last expectation equals

\chi(\rho_{*})\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)^{2},

which converges to zero for
d\geq 2
by Lemma
3.1
.

4.2.
The term
\beta_{d,n}G_{n}(\eta(t))

We shall show that this term also vanishes in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}})
. By developing the square,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}

\displaystyle\Big[\Big(\beta_{d,n}G_{n}(\eta(t))\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)^{2}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\big[\bar{\eta}_{x}(t)^{2}\big]+\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop x\neq y}g_{n}(x)g_{n}(y)\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\big[\bar{\eta}_{x}(t)\bar{\eta}_{y}(t)\big].

Since
\eta_{x}
is bounded by one, the first term on the right hand side is bounded by
\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)^{2}
, which converges to zero for
d\geq 2
by Lemma
3.1
. For the second term, we write

\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\big[\bar{\eta}_{x}(t)\bar{\eta}_{y}(t)\big]=\mathrm{Cov}(\eta_{x}(t),\eta_{y}(t))+(\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}[\eta_{x}(t)]-\rho_{*})(\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}[\eta_{y}(t)]-\rho_{*}).

We claim that

(4.4)

|\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}[\eta_{x}(t)]-\rho_{*}|\leq Cn^{-d}

uniformly in
x
. Together with Lemma
3.2
and the fact that
\sum_{x}g_{n}(x)=1
, the second term on the right hand side of (
4.3
) is bounded by
Cn^{-d}\beta_{d,n}^{2}
, which also vanishes as
n\rightarrow\infty
for
d\geq 2
.

It remains to prove (
4.4
). Denote

m_{t}=\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}[\eta_{x}(t)].

Note that the right hand side is independent of
x
since both the initial measure and the dynamics are translation invariant. Then,

m_{t}^{\prime}=\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}[L_{n}\eta_{x}(t)]=\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}[c_{x}(\eta(t))(1-2\eta(t))].

By Lemma
3.2
,

\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}[c_{x}(\eta(t))(1-2\eta(t))]=F(m_{t})+\varepsilon_{n}(t),

where
|\varepsilon_{n}(t)|\leq Cn^{-d}
uniformly in
0\leq t\leq T
. Thus,

m_{t}-\rho_{*}=\int_{0}^{t}F(m_{s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}\varepsilon_{n}(s)ds.

Since
F(\rho_{*})=0
, by Taylor’s expansion,

|m_{t}-\rho_{*}|\leq\|F^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}\int_{0}^{t}|m_{s}-\rho_{*}|ds+Ctn^{-d}.

We conclude the proof by Grönwall’s inequality.

Remark 4.1
.

By entropy inequality and Lemma
3.5
, for any
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}[|\beta_{d,n}G_{n}(\eta(t))|]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{H(\mu^{n}_{t}|\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n})}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{\gamma}E_{\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}}[\exp\{\gamma|\beta_{d,n}G_{n}(\eta)|\}]

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)}{\gamma}+\gamma\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{n}(x)^{2}\Big).

Optimizing over
\gamma
, we have

\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}[|\beta_{d,n}G_{n}(\eta(t))|]\leq C\beta_{d,n}\sqrt{n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{n}(x)^{2}}.

Since the last term is bounded if
d\leq 3
, and diverges if
d\geq 4
, it is not sufficient for our purpose.

Remark 4.2
.

We remark that this term is the only point where we need the correlation estimate from Lemma
3.2
. It remains an interesting question to avoid using the correlation estimates so that the strategy presented in this article can be applied to more general models.

4.3.
The time integral of the term
\beta_{d,n}G_{n}(\eta(s))

It follows immediately from Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the last subsection that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{d,n}G_{n}(\eta(s))ds\Big)^{2}\Big]\leq t^{2}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\Big(\beta_{d,n}G_{n}(\eta(s))\Big)^{2}\Big]=0.

4.4.
The fourth term on the right hand side of (
4.1
)

In this subsection, we deal with the term

\beta_{d,n}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)c_{x}(\eta(s))(1-2\eta_{x}(s))ds,

and shall show that it vanishes in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
-probability as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Since
E_{\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}}[c_{x}(\eta)(1-2\eta_{x})]=0
, we have

(4.5)

c_{x}(\eta)(1-2\eta_{x})=-(\lambda\rho_{*}+b)\bar{\eta}_{x}+\frac{\lambda(1-\rho_{*})}{2d}\sum_{y:|y-x|=1}\bar{\eta}_{y}-\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y:|y-x|=1}\bar{\eta}_{y}\bar{\eta}_{x}.

In the last subsection, we have shown that the first two terms on the right hand side of (
4.5
), after multiplying by
\beta_{d,n}g_{n}(x)
, then summing over
x
and finally integrating the time from
0
to
t
, converge to zero in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}})
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

The following result deals with the last term on the right hand side of (
4.5
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that
d=2
. For any
0\leq t\leq T
, any
1\leq i\leq d
and any
\delta>0
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\Big|\beta_{d,n}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}(s)ds\Big|>\delta\Big)=0.

Proof.

We only prove that

(4.6)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\beta_{d,n}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}(s)ds>\delta\Big)=0.

By using similar arguments, one can prove the other direction, thus
proving the lemma.

Fix some integer
\ell=\ell(n)>0
and constant
\theta=\theta(n)>0
, which will be specified later. Define

(4.7)

\displaystyle B_{\theta,\ell}(\eta)=

\displaystyle\beta_{d,n}V_{i}^{\ell}(g_{n},\eta)+\frac{2\theta\beta_{d,n}^{2}}{n^{2}}\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|y-z|=1}h_{y,z}^{\ell}(g_{n},\eta)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}\theta}\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}^{\ell}(\mathbf{1},\eta)+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2d^{2}\rho_{*}^{2}n^{2}\theta}\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|y-z|=1}h_{y,z}^{\ell}(\mathbf{1},\eta)^{2}.

Recall
V_{i}^{\ell}
and
h_{y,z}^{\ell}
from Subsection
3.4
. The function
B_{\theta,\ell}(\eta)
is carefully chosen so that

(4.8)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\big\{\beta_{d,n}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}(s)-B_{\theta,\ell}(\eta(s))\big\}ds>\delta/2\Big)=0,

(4.9)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}B_{\theta,\ell}(\eta(s))ds>\delta/2\Big)=0.

This is sufficient to prove (
4.6
).

We first deal with (
4.8
). By Markov’s exponential inequality and Feynman-Kac formula (see
[
12
, Lemma A.2]
for example),

\displaystyle\log

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\big\{\beta_{d,n}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}(s)-B_{\theta,\ell}(\eta(s))\big\}ds>\delta/2\Big)

\displaystyle\leq-\delta\theta/2+\log\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big[\exp\Big\{\int_{0}^{t}\theta\big\{\beta_{d,n}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}(s)-B_{\theta,\ell}(\eta(s))\big\}ds\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq-\delta\theta/2+t\alpha_{n}

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}=

\displaystyle\sup_{f:\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}{\rm density}}\Big\{\int\theta\big\{\beta_{d,n}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}-B_{\theta,\ell}(\eta)\big\}fd\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}

\displaystyle+\int\frac{1}{2}L_{n}^{*}\mathbf{1}(\eta)f(\eta)d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}-\int\Gamma_{n}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big\}.

In this expression,
L_{n}^{*}
is the adjoint of
L_{n}
with respect to the measure
\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}
. Recall that

V_{i}(g_{n},\eta)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}.

Taking
\gamma=n^{2}/4
in Lemma
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\theta\beta_{d,n}

\displaystyle\int(V_{i}(g_{n},\eta)-V_{i}^{\ell}(g_{n},\eta))f(\eta)d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{2}}{4}\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm ex}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}+\frac{2\theta^{2}\beta_{d,n}^{2}}{n^{2}}\int\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|y-z|=1}h_{y,z}^{\ell}(g_{n},\eta)^{2}f(\eta)d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}.

By direct calculations (see Appendix
A.1
),

(4.10)

\frac{1}{2}L_{n}^{*}\mathbf{1}=\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}=\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}(\mathbf{1},\eta).

Using Lemma
3.4
again, we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle\int

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big\{V_{i}(\mathbf{1},\eta)-V_{i}^{\ell}(\mathbf{1},\eta)\big\}fd\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{2}}{4}\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm ex}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2d^{2}\rho_{*}^{2}n^{2}}\int\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|y-z|=1}h_{y,z}^{\ell}(\mathbf{1},\eta)^{2}f(\eta)d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}.

By the definition of
B_{\theta,\ell}
from (
4.7
), we have
\alpha_{n}\leq 0
. Thus, the logarithm of the probability in (
4.8
) is bounded by
-\delta\theta/2
. At the end of the proof, we shall take
\theta\gg 1
, which proves
(
4.8
)
.

It remains to deal with (
4.9
). By Markov’s inequality, it suffices to show that

(4.12)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E_{\mu^{n}_{s}}[|B_{\theta,\ell}|]=0,

where
\mu^{n}_{s}
is the distribution of the process at time
s
. We deal with the four terms in the definition of
B_{\theta,\ell}
respectively. By entropy inequality (see
[
15
, page 338]
) and Lemma
3.5
, for any
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle E_{\mu^{n}_{s}}\big[\big|\beta_{d,n}V_{i}^{\ell}(g_{n},\eta)\big|\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{H(\mu^{n}_{s}|\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}})}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\log E_{\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}}\big[\exp\big\{\big|\gamma\beta_{d,n}V_{i}^{\ell}(g_{n},\eta)\big|\big\}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cn^{d-2}g_{n}(n)}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\log E_{\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}}\big[\exp\big\{\big|\gamma\beta_{d,n}V_{i}^{\ell}(g_{n},\eta)\big|\big\}\big].

Since we will take
\gamma
large enough, by using the basic inequalities
e^{|x|}\leq e^{x}+e^{-x}
and
\log(a+b)\leq\log 2+\max\{\log a,\log b\}
, we can remove the absolute value inside the above exponential. Recall from (
3.5
) that

V_{i}^{\ell}(g_{n},\eta)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\overleftarrow{\eta}_{x}^{\ell}(g_{n})\overrightarrow{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}^{\ell}.

Observe that
\overleftarrow{\eta}_{x}^{\ell}(g_{n})\overrightarrow{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}^{\ell}
and
\overleftarrow{\eta}_{y}^{\ell}(g_{n})\overrightarrow{\eta}_{y+e_{i}}^{\ell}
are independent under the measure
\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
if
|y-x|>2\ell
. Together with Hölder’s inequality,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\log

\displaystyle E_{\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}}\big[\exp\big\{\gamma\beta_{d,n}V_{i}^{\ell}(g_{n},\eta)\big\}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\ell+1)^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\log E_{\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}}\big[\exp\big\{\gamma\beta_{d,n}(2\ell+1)^{d}\overleftarrow{\eta}_{x}^{\ell}(g_{n})\overrightarrow{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}^{\ell}\big\}\big].

To deal with the last logarithm, we first recall the notion of sub-Gaussian random variables. We say that a random variable
X
is sub-Gaussian with variance
\sigma^{2}
if for any
r\in\mathbb{R}
,

\log E[e^{rX}]\leq r^{2}\sigma^{2}/2.

Let
X
and
Y
be two sub-Gaussian random variables with variances
\sigma_{1}^{2}
and
\sigma_{2}^{2}
respectively. By using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, one can show that for any
0<r<1/(4\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})
,

E[e^{rXY}]\leq 3,

see
[
12
]
for example.

Now, we return to (
4.13
). One can check directly that
\overleftarrow{\eta}_{x}^{\ell}(g_{n})
and
\overrightarrow{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}^{\ell}
are sub-Gaussian with variance of order
\ell^{-2d}\|g_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d})}^{2}
and
\ell^{-d}
respectively, where
\|g_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d})}^{2}:=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)^{2}
. By taking
\gamma=\varepsilon_{0}\ell^{d/2}\beta_{d,n}^{-1}\|g_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d})}^{-1}
for some constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
small enough,

E_{\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}}\big[\exp\big\{\gamma\beta_{d,n}(2\ell+1)^{d}\overleftarrow{\eta}_{x}^{\ell}(g_{n})\overrightarrow{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}^{\ell}\big\}\big]\leq 3.

Thus,

E_{\mu^{n}_{s}}\big[\big|\beta_{d,n}V_{i}^{\ell}(g_{n},\eta)\big|\big]\leq C\ell^{-d/2}\beta_{d,n}\|g_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d})}\big(n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)+n^{d}\ell^{-d}\big).

Similarly,

(4.14)

\displaystyle E_{\mu^{n}_{s}}\Big[\frac{\theta\beta_{d,n}^{2}}{n^{2}}\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|y-z|=1}h_{y,z}^{\ell}(g_{n},\eta)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\theta\ell^{d}g_{d}(\ell)\beta_{d,n}^{2}\|g_{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d})}^{2}}{n^{2}}\big(n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)+n^{d}\ell^{-d}\big),

(4.15)

\displaystyle E_{\mu^{n}_{s}}\Big[\Big|\frac{1}{\theta}\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}^{\ell}(\mathbf{1},\eta)\Big|\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\theta}\big(n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)+n^{d}\ell^{-d}\big),

(4.16)

\displaystyle E_{\mu^{n}_{s}}\Big[\Big|\frac{1}{n^{2}\theta}\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|y-z|=1}h_{y,z}^{\ell}(\mathbf{1},\eta)^{2}\Big|\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\ell^{d}g_{d}(\ell)}{n^{2}\theta}\big(n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)+n^{d}\ell^{-d}\big).

See Apprendix
A.2
for detailed calculations of the last three estimates. Note that
\|g_{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d})}=g_{n}(0)\leq C\beta_{d,n}^{-2}
by Lemma
3.1
.

In
d=2
, by taking
\ell=\lfloor n/\sqrt{\log n}\rfloor
, for
n
large enough, we bound

E_{\mu^{n}_{s}}[|B_{\theta,\ell}|]\leq C\Big(n^{-1}\log n+\theta n^{-2}(\log n)^{2}+\theta^{-1}\log n\Big).

We prove (
4.12
) by taking
\log n\ll\theta\ll n^{2}/(\log n)^{2}
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In dimensions
d\geq 3
, the best choice is to take
\ell=\lfloor n^{2/d}\rfloor
. Then,

E_{\mu^{n}_{s}}[|B_{\theta,\ell}|]\leq Cn^{d-2}\Big(\|g_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d})}+\theta n^{-2}+\theta^{-1}\Big).

The upper bound does not vanish as
n\rightarrow\infty
. This is the reason why our result is restricted to dimension
d=2
.

4.5.
The martingale term

In this subsection, we prove the following invariance principle for the martingale term.

Lemma 4.5
.

For
d=2
, the sequence of martingales
\{M_{n}(t),0\leq t\leq T\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
converges in distribution, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, to
\{\sqrt{\chi(\rho_{*})/\pi}B(t),0\leq t\leq T\}
in the space
D([0,T],\mathbb{R})
endowed with the Skorokhod topology.

Proof.

For any trajectory
m\in D([0,T],\mathbb{R})
, define

J(m,T)=\sup\{|m(t)-m(t-)|:0\leq t\leq T\},

where
m(t-)=\lim_{s\uparrow t}m(s)
. Let
\big\langle M_{n}\big\rangle
be the quadratic variation of the martingale
M_{n}
. By
[
26
, Theorem 2.1]
, it suffices to check the following conditions:

(i)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}[J(\big\langle M_{n}\big\rangle,T)]=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}[J(M_{n},T)^{2}]=0
;

(ii)

for any
0\leq t\leq T
,
\langle M_{n}\rangle(t)
converges in probability to
t\chi(\rho_{*})/\pi
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

By direct calculations, the quadratic variation of the martingale equals

\displaystyle\big\langle M_{n}\big\rangle(t)

\displaystyle=\beta_{d,n}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\{L_{n}G_{n}(\eta(s))^{2}-2G_{n}(\eta(s))L_{n}G_{n}(\eta(s))\}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}n^{2}\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|x-y|=1}\big(g_{n}(x)-g_{n}(y)\big)^{2}\big(\eta_{x}(s)-\eta_{y}(s)\big)^{2}ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}c_{x}(\eta(s))g_{n}(x)^{2}ds.

Since
\big\langle M_{n}\big\rangle
is continuous in time
t
,
J(\big\langle M_{n}\big\rangle,T)=0
for any
n
. From the definition of the martingale,

J(M_{n},T)\leq\beta_{d,n}\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|x-y|=1}|g_{n}(x)-g_{n}(y)|+\beta_{d,n}\|g_{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d})}.

By Lemma
3.1
, the upper bound converges to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
for
d\geq 2
. This proves condition (i).

It remains to verify condition (ii). Since
c_{x}(\eta)
is uniformly bounded in
x
and
\eta
,

\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}c_{x}(\eta(s))g_{n}(x)^{2}ds\leq Ct\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(x)^{2},

which vanishes as
n\rightarrow\infty
by Lemma
3.1
in dimensions
d\geq 2
. Using Lemma
3.1
again, it suffices to show that, for any
\delta>0
,

(4.17)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big(\big|\int_{0}^{t}n^{2}\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|x-y|=1}\big(g_{n}(x)-g_{n}(y)\big)^{2}\big\{\big(\eta_{x}(s)-\eta_{y}(s)\big)^{2}-2\chi(\rho_{*})\big\}ds\big|>\delta\Big)=0.

We first write

\big(\eta_{x}-\eta_{y}\big)^{2}-2\chi(\rho_{*})=(1-2\rho_{*})\bar{\eta}_{x}+(1-2\rho_{*})\bar{\eta}_{y}-2\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{y}.

Then, we conclude the proof once we can show that, for any
1\leq i\leq d
and any
\delta>0
,

(4.18)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big(\big|\int_{0}^{t}n^{2}\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\big(g_{n}(x)-g_{n}(x+e_{i})\big)^{2}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)ds\big|>\delta\Big)=0,

(4.19)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big(\big|\int_{0}^{t}n^{2}\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\big(g_{n}(x)-g_{n}(x+e_{i})\big)^{2}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}(s)ds\big|>\delta\Big)=0.

For (
4.18
), by Markov’s inequality, Lemma
3.1
and the fact that both the process and the initial measure are translation invariant, it suffices to show that

(4.20)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\eta}_{0}(s)ds\Big|\Big]=0.

From (
4.1
), Remark
4.1
and the above arguments, it is easy to see that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big[\Big|\beta_{d,n}\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\eta}_{0}(s)ds\Big|\Big]<+\infty,

which proves (
4.20
) since
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{d,n}=+\infty
.

For (
4.19
), we only prove that

(4.21)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}n^{2}\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\big(g_{n}(x)-g_{n}(x+e_{i})\big)^{2}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}(s)ds>\delta\Big)=0,

and the other direction can be proved in the same way. The proof is similar to that of Lemma
4.3
. For any integer
\ell>0
, any
\theta>0
and any
x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}
, define

(4.22)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{x,\ell,\theta}(\eta)=

\displaystyle\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}^{\ell}+\frac{2c_{n}\theta}{n^{2}}\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|y-z|=1}h_{y,z}^{\ell}(\delta_{x},\eta)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}c_{n}\theta}\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}^{\ell}(\mathbf{1},\eta)+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2d^{2}\rho_{*}^{2}n^{2}c_{n}\theta}\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|y-z|=1}h_{y,z}^{\ell}(\mathbf{1},\eta)^{2},

where
c_{n}:=n^{2}\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\big(g_{n}(x)-g_{n}(x+e_{i})\big)^{2}
. Note that, by Lemma
3.1
,
c_{n}
is uniformly bounded in
n
. It suffices to show that

(4.23)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}n^{2}\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\big(g_{n}(x)-g_{n}(x+e_{i})\big)^{2}\\
\times\{\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}(s)-\mathfrak{B}_{x,\ell,\theta}(\eta(s))\}ds>\delta/2\Big)=0,

(4.24)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}n^{2}\beta_{d,n}^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\big(g_{n}(x)-g_{n}(x+e_{i})\big)^{2}\mathfrak{B}_{x,\ell,\theta}(\eta(s))ds>\delta/2\Big)=0.

By Markov’s exponential inequality, Jensen’s inequality and Feynman-Kac formula, the logarithm of the probability in (
4.23
) is bounded by

\displaystyle-\delta\theta/2+\frac{n^{2}\beta_{d,n}^{2}}{c_{n}}

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\big(g_{n}(x)-g_{n}(x+e_{i})\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\times\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\exp\Big\{c_{n}\theta\int_{0}^{t}[\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}(s)-\mathfrak{B}_{x,\ell,\theta}(\eta(s))]ds\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq-\delta\theta/2+\frac{n^{2}\beta_{d,n}^{2}}{c_{n}}

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\big(g_{n}(x)-g_{n}(x+e_{i})\big)^{2}t\gamma_{n,x},

where

\displaystyle\gamma_{n,x}=\sup_{f:\nu_{\rho^{n}_{*}}{\rm-density}}\Big\{

\displaystyle\int c_{n}\theta(\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}-\mathfrak{B}_{x,\ell,\theta}(\eta))f(\eta)d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}

\displaystyle+\int\frac{1}{2}L_{n}^{*}\mathbf{1}(\eta)f(\eta)d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}-\int\Gamma_{n}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big\}.

We will show that
\gamma_{n,x}\leq 0
, which allows us to bound the logarithm of the probability in (
4.23
) by
-\delta\theta/2
. Indeed, by choosing
g(y)=\delta_{x}(y)
in Lemma
3.4
,

\displaystyle\int

\displaystyle c_{n}\theta(\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}-\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}^{\ell})f(\eta)d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{2}}{4}\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm ex}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}+\int\frac{2c_{n}^{2}\theta^{2}}{n^{2}}\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|y-z|=1}h_{y,z}^{\ell}(\delta_{x},\eta)^{2}f(\eta)d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}.

Then, it is easy to see that
\gamma_{n,x}\leq 0
by (
4.11
) and the definition of
\mathfrak{B}_{x,\ell,\theta}(\eta)
.

To prove (
4.24
), by Markov’s inequality, translation invariance of the process and the fact that
c_{n}
is bounded, it suffices to show that

(4.25)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E_{\mu^{n}_{s}}\big[\big|\mathfrak{B}_{0,\ell,\theta}\big|\big]=0.

For the first term in the definition of
\mathfrak{B}_{0,\ell,\theta}
from (
4.22
), by entropy inequality and the fact that
\bar{\eta}_{e_{i}}^{\ell}
is sub-Gaussian with variance of order
\ell^{-d}
with respect to the measure
\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
, for any
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle E_{\mu^{n}_{s}}\big[\big|\bar{\eta}_{0}\bar{\eta}_{e_{i}}^{\ell}\big|\big]

\displaystyle\leq E_{\mu^{n}_{s}}\big[\big|\bar{\eta}_{e_{i}}^{\ell}\big|\big]\leq\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big(H(\mu^{n}_{s}|\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}})+\log E_{\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}}\big[\exp\big\{\gamma|\bar{\eta}_{e_{i}}^{\ell}|\big\}\big]\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)}{\gamma}+\frac{\gamma}{\ell^{d}}\Big).

For the second term in the definition of
\mathfrak{B}_{0,\ell,\theta}
, note that

h_{y,z}^{\ell}(\delta_{0},\eta)=\phi_{\ell}(y-e_{i},z-e_{i})\bar{\eta}_{0}.

Since
\bar{\eta}_{0}
is bounded, by Lemma
3.3
,

\frac{2c_{n}\theta}{n^{2}}\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|y-z|=1}h_{y,z}^{\ell}(\delta_{x},\eta)^{2}\leq\frac{2c_{n}\theta}{n^{2}}\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|y-z|=1}\phi_{\ell}(y-e_{i},z-e_{i})^{2}\leq\frac{C\theta}{n^{2}}g_{d}(\ell).

Recall that the last two terms in the definition of
\mathfrak{B}_{0,\ell,\theta}
have been dealt with in Equations (
4.15
) and (
4.16
). Adding up the above sitimates, we have

E_{\mu^{n}_{s}}\big[\big|\mathfrak{B}_{0,\ell,\theta}\big|\big]\leq C\Big(\frac{n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)}{\gamma}+\frac{\gamma}{\ell^{d}}+\frac{\theta g_{d}(\ell)}{n^{2}}+\frac{1}{\theta}\big(1+\frac{\ell^{d}g_{d}(\ell)}{n^{2}}\big)\big(n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)+\frac{n^{d}}{\ell^{d}}\big)\Big).

In
d=2
, by choosing
\ell=\lfloor n/\sqrt{\log n}\rfloor
and
\gamma=n
, we bound

E_{\mu^{n}_{s}}\big[\big|\mathfrak{B}_{0,\ell,\theta}\big|\big]\lesssim\frac{\log n}{n}+\frac{\theta\log n}{n^{2}}+\frac{\log n}{\theta}.

Finally, we conclude the proof by taking
\log n\ll\theta\ll n^{2}/\log n
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

The last lemma also holds for
d=3
with the limiting variance
2\chi(\rho_{*})\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{p}(t,0)dt
. Indeed, in this case, we bound
E_{\mu^{n}_{s}}\big[\big|\mathfrak{B}_{0,\ell,\theta}\big|\big]
by a constant multiple of

\frac{n^{d-2}}{\gamma}+\frac{\gamma}{\ell^{d}}+\frac{\theta}{n^{2}}+\frac{1}{\theta}\Big(1+\frac{\ell^{d}}{n^{2}}\Big)\Big(n^{d-2}+\frac{n^{d}}{\ell^{d}}\Big).

By taking
\ell=\lfloor n^{2/d}\rfloor,\gamma=n^{d/2}
, the last expression is bounded by
C(n^{d/2-2}+\theta/n^{2}+n^{d-2}/\theta)
. For
d=3
, one can take
n\ll\theta\ll n^{2}
to conclude the proof.

5.
Moderate deviations

In this section, we prove Theorem
2.9
and Corollary
2.10
. In subsection
5.1
, we recall the MDP for the density fluctuation field of the process proved in
[
31
]
. In subsection
5.2
, we relate the occupation time to the density fluctuation field at the level of moderate deviations, from which the exponential tightness of the occupation time follows immediately. Finite dimensional upper and lower bounds of the MDP are proved in subsections
5.3
and
5.4
respectively. We conclude the proof of Theorem
2.9
in subsection
5.5
. Finally, we prove Corollary
2.10
in subsection
5.6
.

5.1.
Density fluctuation fields

For integer
n\geq 1
, let
\mu^{n}:=\{\mu^{n}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}
be the rescaled density fluctuation field,

\mu^{n}_{t}={\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{t}/a_{n}.

First, we need to introduce the moderate deviations rate function. For
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
, the rate function corresponding to the initial distribution is defined as

(5.1)

\mathcal{Q}_{0}(\mu_{0}):=\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T})}\Big\{\big\langle\mu_{0},\phi\big\rangle-\frac{\chi(\rho_{*})}{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\Big\}.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
be the space of functions on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}
which are continuously differentiable in the time variable and smooth in the space variable. For
\mu\in D([0,T],\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}))
and
H\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
, define the linear functional

\ell_{T}(\mu,H):=\big\langle\mu_{T},H_{T}\big\rangle-\big\langle\mu_{0},H_{0}\big\rangle-\int_{0}^{T}\big\langle\mu_{s},(\partial_{s}+\Delta+F^{\prime}(\rho_{*}))H_{s}\big\rangle ds.

The rate function corresponding to the dynamics of the process is defined as

\mathcal{Q}_{\rm dyn}(\mu):=\sup_{H\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})}\Big\{\ell_{T}(\mu,H)-\chi(\rho_{*})\|\nabla H\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})}^{2}-\frac{G(\rho_{*})}{2}\|H\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})}^{2}\Big\},

where
G(\rho)
was defined in Proposition
2.2
. Finally, the rate function is defined as

\mathcal{Q}_{T}=\mathcal{Q}_{0}+\mathcal{Q}_{\rm dyn}.

The following nonequalibrium MDP for the process was proved in
[
31
]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

The sequence
\{\mu^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfies the MDP with decay rate
a_{n}^{2}
and with rate function
\mathcal{Q}_{T}
. Precisely speaking, for any closed set
C
and any open set
O
in
D([0,T],\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}))
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\big(\mu^{n}\in C\big)\leq-\inf_{\mu\in C}\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu),

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\big(\mu^{n}\in O\big)\geq-\inf_{\mu\in O}\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu).

Recall that
\{{\mathcal{Y}}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}
is the limit of
\{{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}
stated in Proposition
2.2
. Since the process
\{{\mathcal{Y}}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}
is Gaussian, the sequence of processes
\{{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}/a_{n},0\leq t\leq T\}
satisfies the large deviation principles with decay rate
a_{n}^{2}
. Moreover, it turns out that the rate function is given by
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T}
exactly the same as that of the sequence of processes
\{\mu^{n}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}
. Since the proof follows from the standard exponential martingale approach, we do not repeat the proof here, see
[
15
, Chapter 10]
for example. Actually, the proof is simpler in our case since we do not need the superexponential replacement lemma.

Proposition 5.2
.

The sequence of processes
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}/a_{n},0\leq t\leq T\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfies the large deviation principle with decay rate
a_{n}^{2}
and with rate function
\mathcal{Q}_{T}
. Precisely speaking, for any closed set
C
and any open set
O
in
D([0,T],\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}/a_{n},0\leq t\leq T\}\in C\Big)\leq-\inf_{\mu\in C}\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu),

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}/a_{n},0\leq t\leq T\}\in O\Big)\geq-\inf_{\mu\in O}\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu).

5.2.
Exponential tightness

To prove the exponential tightness of the sequence of processes
\{\Gamma^{n}(t)/a_{n},0\leq t\leq T\}_{n\geq 1}
in
C([0,T],\mathbb{R})
, it suffices to verify the following two conditions:

(i)

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big|\Gamma^{n}(t)\big|>a_{n}M\Big)=-\infty;

(ii)

for any
\kappa>0
,

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\sup_{|t-s|\leq\delta}\big|\Gamma^{n}(t)-\Gamma^{n}(s)\big|>a_{n}\kappa\Big)=-\infty.

Recall the functions
J
and
J_{\varepsilon}
introduced before Proposition
2.5
. In
[
31
, Lemma 3.7]
, the author has proved that for any
\varepsilon>0
, the process
\{\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},J_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle,0\leq t\leq T\}
satisfies the estimates in conditions (i) and (ii). Using Lemma
5.3
below, it follows immediately that the process
\{\Gamma^{n}(t)/a_{n},0\leq t\leq T\}
also satisfies the above two conditions, thus concluding the proof of the exponential tightness.

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
\delta>0
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\Gamma^{n}(t)/a_{n}-\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},J_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle ds\Big|>\delta\Big)=-\infty.

Proof.

Observe that

\frac{\sqrt{n}}{a_{n}}\bar{\eta}_{0}(t)-\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},J_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle=\frac{1}{a_{n}\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}(\eta_{0}(t)-\eta_{x}(t))J_{\varepsilon}(x/n)+\frac{\sqrt{n}}{a_{n}}\mathcal{O}(n^{-1}),

where the term
\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})
comes from replacing
n^{-1}\sum_{x}J_{\varepsilon}(x/n)
by one. Since
a_{n}\gg 1
, it suffices to prove that

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{a_{n}\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}(\eta_{0}(s)-\eta_{x}(s))J_{\varepsilon}(x/n)ds\Big|>\delta\Big)=-\infty.

By Markov’s inequality, we only need to prove that, for any
M>0
,

(5.2)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{a_{n}M}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}(\eta_{0}(s)-\eta_{x}(s))J_{\varepsilon}(x/n)ds\Big|\Big\}\Big]=0.

Next, we want to remove the supermum over time inside the exponential. To this end, we recall the Garsia-Rodemich-Rumsey inequality, see
[
15
, page 182]
for example.

Lemma 5.4

(Garsia-Rodemich-Rumsey inequality)
.

Assume that
g:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous and
g(0)=0
, and that
\varphi(u),p(u)
are strictly increasing functions such that

\varphi(0)=p(0)=0,\quad\lim_{u\rightarrow+\infty}\varphi(u)=+\infty.

Then,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|g(t)|\leq 8\int_{0}^{T}\varphi^{-1}\big(4B/u^{2}\big)p(du),

where

B=\int_{0}^{T}ds\int_{0}^{T}dt\varphi\Big(\frac{|g(t)-g(s)|}{p(|t-s|)}\Big).

In the last lemma, we take

\displaystyle g(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{a_{n}M}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}(\eta_{0}(s)-\eta_{x}(s))J_{\varepsilon}(x/n)ds,\quad\varphi(u)=u^{2q},\quad p(u)=\sqrt{u},

where
q>2
is fixed. Then, the supermum inside the exponential of (
5.2
) is bounded by

C_{1}(q)\int_{0}^{T}(B/u^{2})^{1/2q}u^{-1/2}du\leq C_{2}(q,T)B^{1/2q},

where

B=\int_{0}^{T}ds\int_{0}^{T}dt\Big|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|t-s|}}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{a_{n}M}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}(\eta_{0}(\tau)-\eta_{x}(\tau))J_{\varepsilon}(x/n)d\tau\Big|^{2q}.

Introduce the function

f_{q}(x)=\exp\Big\{\Big((2q-1)^{2q}+x\Big)^{1/2q}\Big\},\quad x\geq 0.

One could check directly that
f_{q}
is convex and that
f_{q}(x)\leq\exp\{2q-1+x^{1/2q}\}
, see
[
30
, page 85]
for example. Thus,

\displaystyle\exp

\displaystyle\{C_{2}(q,T)B^{1/2q}\}

\displaystyle\leq f_{q}(C_{2}(q,T)^{2q}B)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}ds\int_{0}^{T}dtf_{q}\Big(C_{2}(q,T)^{2q}T^{2}\Big|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|t-s|}}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{a_{n}M}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}(\eta_{0}(\tau)-\eta_{x}(\tau))J_{\varepsilon}(x/n)d\tau\Big|^{2q}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{3}(q)}{T^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}ds\int_{0}^{T}dt\exp\Big\{C_{4}(q,T)\Big|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|t-s|}}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{a_{n}M}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}(\eta_{0}(\tau)-\eta_{x}(\tau))J_{\varepsilon}(x/n)d\tau\Big|\Big\}.

Thus, we only need to show that

(5.3)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\Big(\frac{C_{3}(q)}{T^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}ds\int_{0}^{T}dt\\
\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\exp\Big\{C_{4}(q,T)\Big|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|t-s|}}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{a_{n}M}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}(\eta_{0}(\tau)-\eta_{x}(\tau))J_{\varepsilon}(x/n)d\tau\Big|\Big\}\Big]\Big)=0.

Using the basic inequality
e^{|x|}\leq e^{x}+e^{-x}
, we can remove the absolute value inside the exponential. Then, by the nonequilibrium version of Feynman-Kac formula (see
[
11
, Proposition 4.3]
for example), we bound the logarithm of the last expectation by, for
s<t
,

(t-s)\sup_{f:\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}{\rm-density}}\Big\{\int\Big(\frac{C_{4}a_{n}M}{\sqrt{n}\sqrt{t-s}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}(\eta_{0}-\eta_{x})J_{\varepsilon}(x/n)+\frac{1}{2}L_{n}^{*}\mathbf{1}\Big)fd\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}-\int\Gamma_{n}(\sqrt{f})d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big\}.

By first writing
\eta_{0}-\eta_{x}=\sum_{y=0}^{x-1}(\eta_{y}-\eta_{y+1})
, then making the transformation
\eta\mapsto\eta^{y,y+1}
, and finally using Young’s inequality, for any
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle\int

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}(\eta_{0}-\eta_{x})J_{\varepsilon}(x/n)f(\eta)d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}

\displaystyle=\int\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}\sum_{y=0}^{x-1}(\eta_{y}-\eta_{y+1})J_{\varepsilon}(x/n)f(\eta)d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}\sum_{y=0}^{x-1}(\eta_{y}-\eta_{y+1})J_{\varepsilon}(x/n)\big(f(\eta)-f(\eta^{y,y+1})\big)d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma}{8}\int\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}\sum_{y=0}^{x-1}(\eta_{y}-\eta_{y+1})^{2}J_{\varepsilon}(x/n)^{2}\big(\sqrt{f(\eta)}+\sqrt{f(\eta^{y,y+1})}\big)^{2}d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\int\sum_{|x|<\varepsilon n}\sum_{y=0}^{x-1}\big(\sqrt{f(\eta)}-\sqrt{f(\eta^{y,y+1})}\big)^{2}d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq C\gamma\varepsilon n^{2}\|J_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon n}{\gamma}\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm ex}(\sqrt{f})d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}

Taking
\gamma=2C_{4}\varepsilon a_{n}M/(\sqrt{t-s}n^{3/2})
, we bound

\int\frac{C_{4}a_{n}M}{\sqrt{n}\sqrt{t-s}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}(\eta_{0}-\eta_{x})J_{\varepsilon}(x/n)fd\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\leq\frac{C_{5}(q,T)\varepsilon^{2}a_{n}^{2}M^{2}}{t-s}\|J_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\frac{n^{2}}{2}\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm ex}(\sqrt{f})d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}.

Next, we bound the term
L_{n}^{*}\mathbf{1}
. From (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we have

\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{2}L_{n}^{*}\mathbf{1}(\eta)f(\eta)d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}}{4}\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm ex}(\sqrt{f})d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}

(5.4)

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2d^{2}\rho_{*}^{2}n^{2}}\int\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|y-z|=1}h^{\ell}_{y,z}(\mathbf{1},\eta)^{2}f(\eta)d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}

(5.5)

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\int\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}^{\ell}(\mathbf{1},\eta)f(\eta)d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}.

Here, we present the calculations for dimension
d
to explain why the methods only work in dimension one. By entropy inequality and Hölder’s inequality, we bound (
5.4
) by, for any
\gamma>0
,

\frac{H(f|\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n})}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\log E_{\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{\gamma\lambda^{2}}{2d^{2}\rho_{*}^{2}n^{2}}\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|y-z|=1}h^{\ell}_{y,z}(\mathbf{1},\eta)^{2}\Big\}\Big]\\
\leq\frac{H(f|\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n})}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{\gamma(2\ell+1)^{d}}\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}\atop|y-z|=1}\log E_{\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{\gamma\lambda^{2}(2\ell+1)^{d}}{2d^{2}\rho_{*}^{2}n^{2}}h^{\ell}_{y,z}(\mathbf{1},\eta)^{2}\Big\}\Big].

The Logarithmic Sobolev inequality for the Glauber dynamics states that there exists
\kappa(\rho_{*})>0
such that

(5.6)

H(f|\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n})\leq\kappa(\rho_{*})\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}(\sqrt{f})d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n},

see
[
11
, Lemma 3.1]
for example. As in the proof of (
4.16
), choosing
\gamma\lambda^{2}(2\ell+1)^{d}g_{d}(\ell)=2\varepsilon_{0}d^{2}\rho_{*}^{2}n^{2}
for some small
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, we bound (
5.4
) by

\frac{C_{6}(d,\rho_{*},\varepsilon_{0})\lambda^{2}\ell^{d}g_{d}(\ell)}{n^{2}}\Big(\kappa(\rho_{*})\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}(\sqrt{f})d\nu_{\rho_{*}}+\frac{n^{d}}{\ell^{d}}\Big).

Similarly, we bound (
5.5
) by, for any
\gamma>0
,

\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big(\kappa(\rho_{*})\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}(\sqrt{f})d\nu_{\rho_{*}}+\frac{1}{d(2\ell+1)^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\log E_{\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{\gamma\lambda(2\ell+1)^{d}}{2\rho_{*}}\overleftarrow{\eta}^{\ell}_{x}\overrightarrow{\eta}^{\ell}_{x+e_{i}}\Big\}\Big]\Big).

Choosing
\gamma\lambda=2\rho_{*}\varepsilon_{0}
for some small
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, the last line is bounded by

C_{7}(d,\rho_{*},\varepsilon_{0})\lambda\Big(\kappa(\rho_{*})\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}(\sqrt{f})d\nu_{\rho_{*}}+\frac{n^{d}}{\ell^{d}}\Big).

In
d=1
, taking
\ell=\lfloor n/8\rfloor
and
\lambda_{c}
small enough such that

(5.7)

C_{6}\lambda_{c}^{2}\kappa/64+C_{7}\lambda_{c}\kappa<1/2,

we bound

(5.8)

\int\frac{1}{2}L_{n}^{*}\mathbf{1}(\eta)f(\eta)d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\leq\frac{n^{2}}{4}\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm ex}(\sqrt{f})d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}(\sqrt{f})d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}+C_{8}(d,\rho_{*},\varepsilon_{0}).

Adding up the above estimates, the expression in (
5.3
) is bound by

C_{9}(d,\rho_{*},\varepsilon_{0},T)\big(M^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\|J_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+a_{n}^{-2}\big).

This concludes the proof since
a_{n}\gg 1
and
\varepsilon^{2}\|J_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq C\varepsilon
.
∎

5.3.
Finite dimensional upper bound

In this subsection, we prove moderate deviation upper bound for the occupation time in the sense of finite dimensional distributions. For any integer
k>0
, fix a sequence of times
t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{k}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let the subsets
F_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq i\leq k
, be closed. Then,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\frac{1}{a_{n}}\Gamma^{n}(t_{i})\in F_{i},1\leq i\leq k\Big)\leq-\inf\big\{\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\rm T}\Sigma^{-1}\gamma:\gamma_{i}\in F_{i},1\leq i\leq k\big\}.

In this formula,
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\ldots,\gamma_{k})^{\rm T}
, and
\Sigma=\Sigma_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}}
is the
k\times k
matrix with the
(i,j)
-th entry equal to
\alpha(t_{i},t_{j})
defined in (
2.3
).

Proof.

For any
\delta>0
and any set
F\subset\mathbb{R}
, let
F^{\delta}
be the
\delta
-dilation of the set
F
, that is,
F^{\delta}=\{x:|x-y|\leq\delta\;\text{for some}\;y\in F\}
. By Lemma
5.3
, Proposition
5.1
and the contraction principle, the
\limsup
in the lemma is bounded by

(5.9)

\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\;\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},J_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle ds\in F_{i}^{\delta},\;1\leq i\leq k\Big)\\
\leq-\sup_{\delta>0}\;\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\;\inf\Big\{\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu):\;\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\big\langle\mu_{s},J_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle ds\in F_{i}^{\delta},\;1\leq i\leq k\Big\}.

By Proposition
5.2
and the contraction principle, the sequence of vectors

\big\{Z^{\varepsilon}_{t_{i}}/a_{n},\;1\leq i\leq k\big\}

satisfies the large deviation principle with decay rate
a_{n}^{2}
and rate function

I^{(k)}(\gamma):=\inf\Big\{\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu):\;\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\big\langle\mu_{s},J_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle ds=\gamma_{i},\;1\leq i\leq k\Big\}

for
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Since the sequence of vectors
\big\{Z^{\varepsilon}_{t_{i}}/a_{n},\;1\leq i\leq k\big\}_{n\geq 1}
is Gaussian, it also satisfies the large deviation principle with rate function
(1/2)\gamma^{\rm T}\Sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\gamma
, where
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
is the
k\times k
matrix with the
(i,j)
-th entry equal to

\alpha^{\varepsilon}(t_{i},t_{j})={\rm Cov}(Z^{\varepsilon}_{t_{i}},Z^{\varepsilon}_{t_{j}}).

By uniqueness of the rate function,
I^{(k)}(\gamma)=(1/2)\gamma^{\rm T}\Sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\gamma
. By (
5.9
), we bound the
\limsup
in the lemma by

-\sup_{\delta>0}\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\inf_{\gamma\in F_{1}^{\delta}\times\cdots\times F_{k}^{\delta}}\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\rm T}\Sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\gamma.

Since
F_{1}^{\delta}\times\cdots\times F_{k}^{\delta}
is compact, for any
\varepsilon>0
, let
\gamma_{\varepsilon}\in F_{1}^{\delta}\times\cdots\times F_{k}^{\delta}
be such that

\inf_{\gamma\in F_{1}^{\delta}\times\cdots\times F_{k}^{\delta}}\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\rm T}\Sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\gamma=\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{\rm T}\Sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\gamma_{\varepsilon}.

Using the compactness of the set
F_{1}^{\delta}\times\cdots\times F_{k}^{\delta}
again, there exists a subsequence
\{\varepsilon^{\prime}\}
of
\{\varepsilon\}
and a vector
\gamma_{*}\in F_{1}^{\delta}\times\cdots\times F_{k}^{\delta}
such that
\lim_{\varepsilon^{\prime}\rightarrow 0}\gamma_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}=\gamma_{*}
. Since
\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\alpha^{\varepsilon}(t_{i},t_{j})=\alpha(t_{i},t_{j})
by Proposition
2.5
, we have that

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\inf_{\gamma\in F_{1}^{\delta}\times\cdots\times F_{k}^{\delta}}\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\rm T}\Sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\gamma\geq\lim_{\varepsilon^{\prime}\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\rm T}\Sigma_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{-1}\gamma_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{*}\Sigma^{-1}\gamma_{*}\geq\inf_{\gamma\in F_{1}^{\delta}\times\cdots\times F_{k}^{\delta}}\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\rm T}\Sigma^{-1}\gamma.

Thus, the
\limsup
in the lemma is bounded from above by

-\sup_{\delta>0}\inf_{\gamma\in F_{1}^{\delta}\times\cdots\times F_{k}^{\delta}}\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\rm T}\Sigma^{-1}\gamma=-\inf_{\gamma\in F_{1}\times\cdots\times F_{k}}\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\rm T}\Sigma^{-1}\gamma,

which concludes the proof.
∎

5.4.
Finite dimensional lower bound

In this subsection, we prove the following finite dimensional moderate deviation lower bound for the occupation time.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let the subsets
O_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq i\leq k
, be open. Then,

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\Gamma^{n}(t_{i})\in O_{i},1\leq i\leq k\Big)\geq-\inf\big\{\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\rm T}\Sigma^{-1}\gamma:\gamma_{i}\in O_{i},1\leq i\leq k\big\},

where
\Sigma
is the
k\times k
matrix defined in Lemma
5.5
.

Proof.

Choose any
\gamma\in O_{1}\times\ldots\times O_{k}
. It suffices to show that

(5.10)

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\Gamma^{n}(t_{i})/a_{n}\in O_{i},1\leq i\leq k\Big)\geq-\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\rm T}\Sigma^{-1}\gamma.

Choose
\delta>0
such that
(\gamma_{i}-2\delta,\gamma_{i}+2\delta)\subset O_{i}
for every
1\leq i\leq k
. By Lemma
5.3
, the left hand side of (
5.10
) is bounded from below by

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\Gamma^{n}(t_{i})/a_{n}\in(\gamma_{i}-2\delta,\gamma_{i}+2\delta),1\leq i\leq k\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\langle\mu^{n}_{s},J_{\varepsilon}\rangle ds\in(\gamma_{i}-\delta,\gamma_{i}+\delta),1\leq i\leq k\Big).

As in the proof of Lemma
5.5
, the last line is bounded from below by

\displaystyle-\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\;\inf

\displaystyle\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{\rm T}\Sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\zeta:\zeta_{i}\in(\gamma_{i}-\delta,\gamma_{i}+\delta),\;1\leq i\leq k\Big\}

\displaystyle\geq-\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\rm T}\Sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\gamma=-\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\rm T}\Sigma^{-1}\gamma,

thus concluding the proof.
∎

5.6.
Proof of Corollary
2.10

In this subsection, we prove Corollary
2.10
. It suffices to show that, for any
\delta>0
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\Gamma_{f}^{n}(t)-\varphi_{f}^{\prime}(\rho_{*})\Gamma_{n}(t)\Big|>a_{n}\delta\Big)=-\infty.

Note that for any local function
f:\Omega_{n}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
f-\varphi_{f}(\rho_{*})-\varphi_{f}^{\prime}(\rho_{*})(\eta_{0}-\rho_{*})
can be expressed as a linear combination of
\prod_{x\in A}\bar{\eta}_{x}
,
A\subset\mathbb{T}_{n}
finite,
|A|\geq 2
. Thus, it suffices to show that, for any finite
A\subset\mathbb{T}_{n}
with
|A|\geq 2
and for any
\delta>0
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\sqrt{n}\int_{0}^{t}\prod_{x\in A}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)ds\Big|>a_{n}\delta\Big)=-\infty.

Assume that
|A|=m\geq 2
and label the points in
A
by
x_{1}<x_{2}<\ldots<x_{m}
. Similar to the proof of Lemma
5.3
, we have

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\sqrt{n}\int_{0}^{t}\prod_{x\in A}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)-\overleftarrow{\eta}_{x_{1}}^{\varepsilon n}(s)[\prod_{x\in A\atop x\neq x_{1},x_{m}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)]\overrightarrow{\eta}_{x_{m}}^{\varepsilon n}(s)ds\Big|>a_{n}\delta\Big)=-\infty.

Then, it suffices to show that, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\sqrt{n}\int_{0}^{t}\overleftarrow{\eta}_{x_{1}}^{\varepsilon n}(s)[\prod_{x\in A\atop x\neq x_{1},x_{m}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)]\overrightarrow{\eta}_{x_{m}}^{\varepsilon n}(s)ds\Big|>a_{n}\delta\Big)=-\infty.

By Markov’s inequality, we only need to show that for any
M>0
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big[\exp\Big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|a_{n}M\sqrt{n}\int_{0}^{t}\overleftarrow{\eta}_{x_{1}}^{\varepsilon n}(s)[\prod_{x\in A\atop x\neq x_{1},x_{m}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)]\overrightarrow{\eta}_{x_{m}}^{\varepsilon n}(s)ds\Big|\Big\}\Big]=0.

By using Garsia-Rodemich-Rumsey inequality as in the proof of Lemma
5.3
, we can first remove the supremum over time inside the exponential. Then, by Feynman-Kac formula, the last expression on the left hand side is bounded by

(5.11)

\displaystyle\frac{T}{a_{n}^{2}}\sup_{f:\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}{\rm density}}\Big\{

\displaystyle\int a_{n}M\sqrt{n}\overleftarrow{\eta}_{x_{1}}^{\varepsilon n}[\prod_{x\in A\atop x\neq x_{1},x_{m}}\bar{\eta}_{x}]\overrightarrow{\eta}_{x_{m}}^{\varepsilon n}f(\eta)d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}

\displaystyle+\int\frac{1}{2}L_{n}^{*}\mathbf{1}(\eta)f(\eta)d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}-\int\Gamma_{n}(\sqrt{f})d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}\Big\}.

By entropy inequality and Logarithmic Sobolev inequality for the Glauber dynamics (see (
5.6
)), we bound the first term inside the supremum by, for any
\gamma>0
,

\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big\{\kappa\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}(\sqrt{f})d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}+\log E_{\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}}\big[\exp\big\{\gamma a_{n}M\sqrt{n}\overleftarrow{\eta}_{x_{1}}^{\varepsilon n}[\prod_{x\in A\atop x\neq x_{1},x_{m}}\bar{\eta}_{x}]\overrightarrow{\eta}_{x_{m}}^{\varepsilon n}\big\}\big]\Big\}.

Since
\overleftarrow{\eta}_{x_{1}}^{\varepsilon n}
and
\overrightarrow{\eta}_{x_{m}}^{\varepsilon n}
are both sub-Gaussian with variance of order
(\varepsilon n)^{-1}
with respect to the measure
\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}
and
\prod_{x\in A\atop x\neq x_{1},x_{m}}\bar{\eta}_{x}
is bounded, by taking
\gamma a_{n}M\sqrt{n}=\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon n
for small enough
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, the last expression is bounded by

\frac{a_{n}M}{\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon\sqrt{n}}\big\{\kappa\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}(\sqrt{f})d\nu_{\rho_{*}}^{n}+\log 3\big\}.

Here, we also used properties of sub-Gaussian random variables stated after (
4.13
). Since
a_{n}\ll\sqrt{n}
, together with (
5.8
), for
n
large enough, we bound (
5.11
) by
C/a_{n}^{2}
. This concludes the proof since
a_{n}\gg 1
.
