Orbits on a product of two flags and a line and the Bruhat order, II
Mark Colarusso, Sam Evens

Abstract
. Let G = G ​ L ​ ( n ) G=GL(n) be the n × n n\times n complex general linear group and let ℬ n \mathcal{B}_{n} be its flag variety. A Borel subgroup B B of G G acts on ℬ n × ℙ n − 1 \mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1} diagonally with finitely many orbits. In this paper, we give an embedding of the B B -orbits on ℬ n × ℙ n − 1 \mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1} into the B B -orbits on the flag variety ℬ n + 1 \mathcal{B}_{n+1} of G ​ L ​ ( n + 1 ) GL(n+1) and show that this correspondence respects closure relations and preserves monoid actions. As a consequence both closure relations and monoid actions on the set of all B B -orbits on ℬ n × ℙ n − 1 \mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1} can be understood via the Bruhat order on the symmetric group on n + 1 n+1 letters by using our results in [ CE26 ] . This amplifies work of Magyar [ Mag05 ] by making the closure relation more transparent and allows us to compute the monoid action using Demazure products. If S i S_{i} is the stabilizer in B B of the line through the ith standard basis vector, we give an embedding of the S i S_{i} -orbits on ℬ n \mathcal{B}_{n} into the B B -orbits in a single G G -orbit in ℬ n + 1 , \mathcal{B}_{n+1}, and this embedding plays an essential role in the above results. We extend results from our papers [ CE22 ] , [ CE23 ] , and [ CE26 ] , and in particular show that for S i S_{i} -orbits on ℬ n , \mathcal{B}_{n}, the closure ordering is given by the Richardson-Springer standard order.

1.
Introduction

Let
G=G_{n}=GL(n)
be the
n\times n
complex general linear group and let
\mathcal{B}_{n}
be its flag variety. It is well known that
G
has finitely many orbits on the triple product

\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
under the diagonal action. These orbits have been an object of study for some time (see
[
Mag05
]
,
[
Tra09
]
for example) and are of broad interest since they are related to the study of mirabolic
\mathcal{D}
-modules which play an important role in the study of category
\mathcal{O}
for rational Cherednik algebras
[
FG10
]
. Magyar parameterizes these orbits in terms of decorated permutations and shows that the closure ordering on these orbits is given in part by a numerical criterion on the set of decorated permutations. This paper is motivated largely by our desire to understand the closure relations on these orbits in a simpler fashion and obtain a clearer picture of their geometry. We build on our previous work in
[
CE26
]
and
[
CE25
]
to show that
G
-orbits on
\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
are parameterized by a certain subset of
W_{n+1}\times W_{n+1},
where
W_{n+1}
is the symmetric group on
n+1
letters, and moreover, the closure relation on the orbits is the restriction of the product of the Bruhat orders on
W_{n+1}.
We establish analogous results which compare certain monoid actions on
G
-orbits on
\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
to familiar monoid actions on
W_{n+1}\times W_{n+1}
defined using Demazure products. These monoid actions give useful insight into how to relate the geometry of two orbit closures. The results on the monoid actions are crucial to establishing our new description of the closure relations and the monoid action does not appear in Magyar’s earlier work, but does appear in Travkin’s work, although for a different purpose.

In more detail, let
G_{n+1}:=GL(n+1)
and let
\mathcal{B}_{n+1}
be its flag variety. Embed
G
in the top lefthand corner of
G_{n+1}
. In our recent work
[
CE22
]

[

]

[

-orbits on

is the standard Borel subgroup of invertible
n\times n
upper triangular matrices. For an algebraic group

In the current paper, we establish a correspondence between
G\backslash(\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}),
and a certain subset of

G\backslash(\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1})\longleftrightarrow B\backslash(\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1})\mbox{ given by }G\cdot(\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$},\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime},[v])\leftrightarrow B\cdot(\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime},[v]).

Remark 2.1

One purpose of this paper is to study the collection of orbits

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

and consider the
G
-orbit

,
where we take

and

in part (3) of Proposition

(3.5)

\mathcal{E}_{1,i+1}=(\hat{e}_{i}\subset e_{1}\subset\dots\subset e_{i-1}\subset\underbrace{e_{n+1}}_{i+1}\subset e_{i+1}\subset\dots\subset e_{n}),

with
\hat{e}_{i}=e_{i}+e_{n+1}

G

and

-orbits on

(3.6)

B\backslash Q_{1,i+1}\longleftrightarrow S_{i}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n},\text{ given by }Bg\,\mbox{\emph{Stab}}_{G}(\mathcal{E}_{1,i+1})\to S_{i}g^{-1}B.

Proof.

We claim that

(3.7)

\mbox{Stab}_{G}(\mathcal{E}_{1,i+1})=\mbox{Stab}_{B}(e_{i}).

Suppose first that
g\in G
fixes the flag
\mathcal{E}_{1,i+1}
in (
3.5
). For
j=1,\dots,n
, let
U_{j}=\mbox{span}\{e_{1},\dots,e_{j}\}
. Recall that
G=\{g\in G_{n+1}:\,g\cdot e_{n+1}=e_{n+1}\mbox{ and }g\cdot U_{n}=U_{n}\}.
Since
g\in\mbox{Stab}_{G}(\mathcal{E}_{1,i+1})
, it must fix the line

\mbox{${\mathbb{C}}$}(\hat{e}_{i})=\mbox{${\mathbb{C}}$}(e_{i}+e_{n+1})
. But since
g\cdot e_{n+1}=e_{n+1}
, we deduce that
g\cdot e_{i}=e_{i}.

Further, for any
j\in\{1,\dots,i-1\}
,
g\cdot U_{j}\subset\mbox{${\mathbb{C}}$}(e_{i}+e_{n+1})+U_{j}
and
for
j\in\{i+1,\dots,n\}
,
g\cdot U_{j}\subset(U_{j}+\mbox{${\mathbb{C}}$}e_{n+1})
. But
U_{n}\cap(\mbox{${\mathbb{C}}$}(e_{i}+e_{n+1})+U_{j})=U_{j}
and
U_{n}\cap(U_{j}+\mbox{${\mathbb{C}}$}e_{n+1})=U_{j}
, so it follows that
g\cdot U_{j}=U_{j}
for all
j=1,\dots,n
. Thus,
g\in\mbox{Stab}_{B}(e_{i})
. Conversely, any element of the group
\mbox{Stab}_{B}(e_{i})
can be easily seen to stabilize the flag
\mathcal{E}_{1,i+1}
. This establishes (
3.7
) and it follows easily that
Z\cdot\mbox{Stab}_{G}(\mathcal{E}_{1,i+1})=\mbox{Stab}_{B}[e_{i}]=S_{i},
where

is the centre of
G.

Hence, the
B
-orbits in the
G
-orbit

are in one-to-one correspondence with the elements of the double coset poset

. This double coset poset is then in one-to-one correspondence with the poset of
S_{i}
-orbits on
G/B=\mathcal{B}_{n}
via the self map of
G
given by inversion
g\mapsto g^{-1}
, which implies the Theorem.

Q.E.D.

Orbit Correspondence and Monoid Actions

4.1.
Background on Monoid Actions

For more details on the subsequent material, we refer the reader to
[
RS90
]
,
[
Vog83
]
,
[
CE14
]
,
[
CE22
]
and other sources.
Let
R
be a connected reductive algebraic group, let
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{R}
be the flag
variety of
R
, and let
M
be an algebraic subgroup of
R
acting on
\mathcal{B}
with finitely many orbits. Identify
\mathcal{B}\cong R/B_{R}
, for a Borel subgroup
B_{R}\subset R
and let
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}=\mbox{Lie}(B_{R})\subset\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}
.
Let
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
be the set of simple roots defined by the Borel subalgebra
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
, and let
S_{R}
be the simple reflections of the Weyl group
W
of
R
corresponding to
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
. For
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
, let
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
be the variety of all parabolic subalgebras of
{\mathfrak{r}}
of type
\alpha
and consider the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
\pi_{\alpha}:\mathcal{B}\to{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}
. For
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
with corresponding reflection
s=s_{\alpha}\in W
, we define an operator
m(s)
on the set of orbits
M\backslash\mathcal{B}
following the above sources. For
Q_{M}\in M\backslash\mathcal{B}
, let
m(s)*Q_{M}
be the unique
M
-orbit open and dense in
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q_{M})).
Note that
\pi_{\alpha}:\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q_{M}))\to\pi_{\alpha}(Q_{M})
is an
M
-equivariant

\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle. Thus,
\dim(\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q_{M})))=\dim(\pi_{\alpha}(Q_{M}))+1,
and since
Q_{M}\subset\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q_{M}))
, it follows that the orbit

(4.1)

Q_{M}\neq m(s)*Q_{M}\text{ if and only if }\dim(m(s)*Q_{M})=\dim(Q_{M})+1.

Computation of
m(s)*Q_{M}
depends on the
type of the root

\alpha
for the orbit
Q_{M}
, which is determined as follows. For
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}\in Q_{M}
, let
\mbox{${\mathfrak{p}}$}_{\alpha}^{\prime}=\pi_{\alpha}(\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime})
and let
B^{\prime}
and
P_{\alpha}^{\prime}
be the corresponding parabolic subgroups of
R
, and let
V_{\alpha}^{\prime}
be the the solvable radical of
P_{\alpha}^{\prime}
. Consider the group

S_{\alpha}^{\prime}:=P_{\alpha}^{\prime}/V_{\alpha}^{\prime}
isogenous to
SL(2)
and its subgroup

(4.2)

M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}:=(M\cap P_{\alpha}^{\prime})/(M\cap V_{\alpha}^{\prime}).

Using the standard identification
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))\cong M\times_{M\cap P_{\alpha}^{\prime}}P_{\alpha}^{\prime}/B^{\prime},
we see that
M
-orbits in

\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))
correspond to
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
-orbits in

P^{\prime}_{\alpha}/B^{\prime}\cong{\mbox{${\mathbb{P}}$}}^{1}.
From the classification of subgroups of
SL(2)
with finitely many orbits on
{\mbox{${\mathbb{P}}$}}^{1},
we are in one of the following three cases.

Definition 4.1
.

(1) If
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
is solvable and contains the unipotent radical of a Borel subgroup of
S_{\alpha}^{\prime}
, then
\alpha
is called a complex root for
Q_{M}
. If
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}=\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}
, then
\alpha
is complex stable for
Q_{M}
and otherwise
\alpha
is complex unstable for
Q_{M}
.

(2) If
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}=S_{\alpha}^{\prime}
, then
\alpha
is called a compact imaginary root for
Q_{M}.

(3) Suppose
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
is one-dimensional and reductive.
If
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}=\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}
, then
\alpha
is called a noncompact imaginary root for
Q_{M}
, while if
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}\not=\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime},
then
\alpha
is called a real root for
Q_{M}.

In
[
RS90
]
, the monoid action is only discussed when
M
has the same component group as the fixed points of an involution of
R
, and the terminology in this definition comes from the action of the involution on root spaces, or more precisely the action of an associated real form of
R
. However, as many authors have observed, including
[
Kno95
]
, the construction works in the same way when
M
has finitely many orbits on the flag variety.
It follows easily from arguments in
[
RS94
]
that the monoid action depends only on the nature of the groups
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}.
This is the perspective we use in this paper.
It is well-known and easy to prove that the type of the root depends only on the orbit
Q_{M}
and not on the point
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}.
The following result is also well-known.

Proposition 4.2
.

[Lemma 2.1.4 and Lemma 2.4.3 of
[
RS94
]
]

Let
Q\in M\backslash\mathcal{B}
with
Q=M\cdot{\rm Ad}(v)\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
and
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
.

(1)

If
\alpha
is complex stable for
Q
, then
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))
consists of two
M
-orbits:

\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))=Q\cup M\cdot{\rm Ad}(vs_{\alpha})\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}},

and
m(s_{\alpha})*Q=M\cdot{\rm Ad}(vs_{\alpha})\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
.

(2)

If
\alpha
is non-compact imaginary for
Q
then
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))
consists of either two (resp. three)
M
-orbits depending on whether
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
is the normalizer of a torus (resp. a torus). The open orbit

m(s_{\alpha})*Q=M\cdot{\rm Ad}(vu_{\alpha})\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
, where
u_{\alpha}\in R
is the Cayley transform with respect to the root
\alpha
as defined in Equation (41) of
[
CE14
]
and
\dim Q=\dim(M\cdot{\rm Ad}(vs_{\alpha})\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}})
.

(3)

In all other cases,
m(s_{\alpha})*Q=Q.

Remark 4.3
.

When
M
is a solvable group, the group
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
in (
4.2
) is always solvable. In this setting, the root
\alpha
is
never
compact imaginary, and in the non-compact imaginary case,
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
is a torus so that
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))
always consists of three
M
-orbits.

Remark 4.4
.

Let
\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}=\mathfrak{gl}(n)
and
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}=\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}
. Suppose that

Using the monoid action, we can define partial orderings on the set of

It follows that this monoid generates an action by the

Definition 4.5
.

The

is defined by the property that if

Monoid Actions on Shareshian and

-Shareshian Pairs

The theory of Section
4.1
can be applied to the study of
G
-orbits
on
\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}
for the diagonal action. These orbits are well-known to correspond to
B
-orbits on
\mathcal{B}_{n}
, which we parameterize by elements of
W_{n}
using the Bruhat decomposition. We therefore obtain a monoid action via the standard simple roots
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}\coprod\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
of
\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}\oplus\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}
on
{W}_{n}.
The first factor of
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
acts on the left of
{W}_{n}
, and the second factor acts on the right of
{W}_{n}
. In terms of Definition
4.1
, a root
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}\coprod\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
is always complex for
w\in{W}_{n}
(see Section 4.3 of
[
CE26
]
). Explicitly, for
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
and
w\in{W}_{n}
,

(5.13)

\begin{split}\mbox{(Left Action) }&m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}w=w\mbox{ if }\ell(s_{\alpha}w)<\ell(w)\mbox{ and }m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}w=s_{\alpha}w\mbox{ if }\ell(s_{\alpha}w)>\ell(w).\\
\mbox{(Right Action) }&m(s_{\alpha})*_{R}w=w\mbox{ if }\ell(ws_{\alpha})<\ell(w)\mbox{ and }m(s_{\alpha})*_{R}w=ws_{\alpha}\mbox{ if }\ell(ws_{\alpha})>\ell(w).\\
\end{split}

Remark 5.7
.

Note that the left and right monoid actions in (
5.13
) are just given by the corresponding Demazure products from Equation (
1.5
). In this case, the weak order of Definition
4.5
is the two-sided weak order on
W_{n}
(see for example, Exercise 8, Chapter 3 of
[
BB81
]
).

Recall that the Borel subalgebra
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{i}
stabilizes the flag
\mathcal{E}^{i}
of Equation (
5.9
) and
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{i}=\sigma_{i}(\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}),
where
\sigma_{i}=(i,i-1,\dots,2,1)
is the
i
-cycle so that
\mathcal{E}^{i}=\sigma_{i}(\mathcal{E}_{n})
. Let
\Pi^{i}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}=\{\alpha_{1}^{i},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}^{i}\}
be the simple roots defined by the Borel subalgebra
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{i}
with corresponding set of simple reflections
S^{i}:=\{s^{i}_{1},\dots,s^{i}_{n}\}
. It follows that
\alpha^{i}_{k}=\sigma_{i}(\alpha_{k})
for
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
.

Notation 5.8
.

By using the set
S^{i}
as our simple reflections in place of
S,
we obtain a new order relation on
W_{n}.
We denote
{W}_{n}
with this non-standard order relation by
({W}_{n},S^{i})
, and denote
{W}_{n}
with the standard order relation by
({W}_{n},S).
We denote elements in the poset
({W}_{n},S)
by
w,u,...
and denote elements in the poset
({W}_{n},S^{i})
by

w^{i},u^{i},...

We also obtain left and right monoid actions of
S^{i}
on
({W}_{n},S^{i})
analogous to the ones in Equation (
5.13
) with
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
replaced by
\alpha^{i}\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}^{i}
. Recall
\mathfrak{S}_{i}
from (
4.5
).
We now use the monoid actions of
S
and
S^{i}
on
({W}_{n},S)
and
({W}_{n},S^{i})
to define a monoid action of
\mathfrak{S}_{i}\coprod\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
on
({W}_{n},S)\times({W}_{n},S^{i})
. First, observe that
\mathfrak{S}_{i}\subset\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}^{i}
. Indeed,
\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{k+1}^{i}
for
k=1,\dots,i-2
and
\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{k}^{i}
for
k=i+1,\dots,n-1
.

Definition 5.9
.

(see Definition 5.6 of
[

]
)

Define the
restricted diagonal monoid action
on
(W,S)\times(W,S^{i})
via simple roots

as follows.

For
(w,u^{i})\in(W,S)\times(W,S^{i})

\begin{split}\mbox{ (Left action)}&\mbox{ For }\alpha\in\mathfrak{S}_{i},\,m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}(w,u^{i})=(m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}w,m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}u^{i}).\\
\mbox{ (Right action)}&\mbox{ For }\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}},m(s_{\alpha})*_{R}(w,u^{i})=(m(s_{\alpha})*_{R}w,m(s_{\alpha^{i}})*_{R}u^{i}),\end{split}

where the monoid actions
*_{L}
and

are given in Equation (
5.13
).

Henceforth, we will frequently drop the subscripts

and

on the monoid actions defined in Equation (

\alpha\in\mathfrak{S}_{i}\coprod\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
acts on the left whenever

.
Recall the extended monoid action on

.

Remark 5.12
.

We define the monoid action on standardized
i
-Shareshian pairs. For

G
-orbits on

Given any
B
-orbit
{\mathcal{Z}}
on
\mathcal{B}_{n+1}
,
{\mathcal{Z}}
is contained in the
G
-orbit
Q_{G}:=G\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}
on
\mathcal{B}_{n+1}
. Recall the classification of

G
-orbits on
\mathcal{B}_{n+1}
given in Section
3
. Recall the flag

\mathcal{E}^{*}_{n+1}=(e_{n+1}\subset e_{1}\subset\dots\subset e_{n})
defined in Equation (
5.1
) and the Borel subalgebra of
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{n+1}^{*}\subset\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}_{n+1}
which stabilizes it. Let

be the set of simple reflections defined by
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{*}_{n+1}
. Then

(5.18)

s_{1}^{*}=(1,\,n+1)\mbox{ and }s_{j}^{*}=(j-1,\,j)\mbox{ for }j\in\{2,\dots,n\}.

The Weyl group
{W}_{n}=\langle s_{1},\dots,s_{n-1}\rangle=\langle s_{2}^{*},\dots,s_{n}^{*}\rangle.

For
i,\,j\in\{1,\dots,n+1\}
, define elements

and

in

w_{i}:=s_{n}\dots s_{i}\mbox{ and }w_{j}^{*}=s_{1}^{*}\dots s_{j-1}^{*}\mbox{ with the convention that }w_{n+1}=id\mbox{ and }w_{1}^{*}=id.

Note that
w_{i}
and
w_{j}^{*}
are the cycles

(5.20)

w_{i}=(n+1,n,\dots,i+1,i),\ w_{j}^{*}=(1,2,\dots,j-1,n+1).

We let
\sigma:=w_{1}
, and note that
\sigma\in W_{n+1}
is the
n+1
-cycle

\sigma=(n+1,\dots,1)
.

Remark 5.13
.

It is easy to verify that the elements
\{w_{i}:\,i=1,\dots,n+1\}
are a complete set of shortest length
representatives for the set of right cosets
{W}_{n}\backslash{{W}}_{n+1}
. Also, the
set
\{w_{j}^{*}:\,j=1,\dots,n+1\}
is a set of shortest length representatives for the set of right cosets

{W}_{n}\backslash{{W}}_{n+1}
provided we define the length function on
{{W}}_{n+1}
with respect to the simple reflections

\{s_{1}^{*},\dots,s_{n}^{*}\}
.

Theorem 5.14
.

Let
{\mathcal{Z}}\in B\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n+1}
and
Q_{i,j}
be the
G
-orbit given in Proposition
3.1
. Then

(5.21)

{\mathcal{Z}}\subset Q_{i,j}\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{S}h({\mathcal{Z}})\in{W}_{n}w_{i}\times{W}_{n}w_{j}^{*}.

The following two Lemmas are used to prove Theorem
5.14
.

Lemma 5.15
.

(Lemma 5.4 of
[
CE23
]
)
Let
\mathcal{F}=(v_{1}\subset v_{2}\subset\dots\subset v_{n}\subset v_{n+1})
be a flag in standard form.

(1)

The flag
\mathcal{F}\in Q_{j}
if and only if
\mathcal{F}
contains no hat vectors and
v_{j}=e_{n+1}
.

(2)

The flag
\mathcal{F}\in Q_{i,j}
with
i<j
if and only if
v_{i}
is a hat vector,

v_{k}
is a standard basis vector for all
k<i
, and
v_{j}=e_{n+1}
.

Lemma 5.16
.

(1)

Let
w\in{{W}}_{n+1}
. Then
w\in{W}_{n}w_{i}\Leftrightarrow w(i)=n+1
.

(2)

Let
u^{*}\in{{W}}_{n+1}
. Then
u^{*}\in{W}_{n}w_{j}^{*}\Leftrightarrow u^{*}(j-1)=n+1
.

Proof.

By (
5.20
),
w_{i}(i)=n+1
. Since
{W}_{n}
fixes
n+1
, we conclude that
w(i)=n+1
for all
w\in{{W}}_{n}w_{i}
.

To prove the converse, suppose we are given
w\in{{W}}_{n+1}
with
w(i)=n+1
. Then by Remark
5.13
,
w\in{W}_{n}w_{\ell}
for some
\ell\in\{1,\dots,n+1\}
. But then by the previous paragraph
w(\ell)=n+1=w(i),
so that
\ell=i
.

For part (2), let
u^{*}\in{W}_{n}w_{j}^{*}
. By (
5.20
),
w_{j}^{*}(j-1)=n+1,
so as above,
u^{*}(j-1)=n+1.
The converse is proved in the same manner as for part (1).

Q.E.D.

.

and

implies that

is a standard basis vector for all

. Therefore, we may assume that

has the form of the flag in Equation (

) with

B
-orbit

{\mathcal{Z}}
contained in

can be used to obtain the standardized
i
-Shareshian pair of the corresponding

-orbit

in

Theorem 5.18
.

Let
{\mathcal{Z}}\in B\backslash Q_{1,i+1}

\widetilde{Sh}_{i}({{\mathcal{Z}}}^{op})=(\sigma w^{-1},w_{i+1}^{*}{z^{*}}^{-1}).

Proof.

We first prove Equation (

) when
{\mathcal{Z}}
is a closed
B
-orbit on
Q_{1,i+1}
(or equivalently,
{\mathcal{Z}}^{op}
is a zero-dimensional

-orbit), and then deduce the general case using properties of the monoid action.
By Equation (6.22) of
[
CE23
]
, each of these closed orbits is

{\mathcal{Z}}={{\mathcal{Z}}}_{1,i+1}(k):=B\cdot\mathcal{E}_{1,i+1}(k)
,

, where

For brevity, we denote
\mathcal{E}_{1,i+1}(k)
by
\mathcal{F}
in our
computation of
\mathcal{S}h({\mathcal{Z}})
. By Proposition
5.5
, the corresponding flags
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mathcal{F}^{*}
of Notation

are given by

(5.24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}=(e_{n+1}\subset e_{1}\subset\dots\subset e_{k-1}\subset e_{k+1}\subset\dots\subset e_{i-1}\subset e_{i}\subset&\underbrace{e_{k}}_{i+1}\subset e_{i+1}\subset\dots\subset e_{n})\\

\mathcal{F}^{*}=(e_{k}\subset e_{1}\subset\dots\subset e_{k-1}\subset e_{k+1}\subset\dots\subset e_{i-1}\subset e_{i}\subset&\underbrace{e_{n+1}}_{i+1}\subset e_{i+1}\subset\dots\subset e_{n}).\end{split}

Thus,
\mathcal{S}h({{\mathcal{Z}}}_{1,i+1}(k))=(w,z^{*})
, where

(5.25)

w=(n+1,\,n,\,n-1,\dots,i+2,\,i+1,\,k,\,k-1,\dots,2,\,1)\mbox{ and }z^{*}=(k,\,k+1,\,\dots,\,i-1,\,i,\,n+1)

We now compute the standardized
i
-Shareshian pair of
{{\mathcal{Z}}}^{op}
. Recall the flag

in Equation (

Thus, by (

) and Definition

,

and the standardized

-Shareshian pair is

(5.26)

\widetilde{\mathcal{S}h}_{i}({\mathcal{Z}}^{op})=(\tau^{-1},\sigma_{i}^{-1}\tau^{-1}).

It remains to verify that the expressions in (

) and in (
5.22
) coincide. For this equality, we verify that

\sigma w^{-1}=\tau^{-1}
and

,
using (

Thus, Equation (

-orbits
{{\mathcal{Z}}}^{op}.

Recall the set of all Shareshian pairs

For the inductive step, we define a subset of Shareshian pairs

\Psi:\mathcal{S}p_{1,i+1}\mapsto{W}_{n}\times{W}_{n}\mbox{ given by }\Psi((w,z^{*}))=(\sigma w^{-1},w_{i+1}^{*}{z^{*}}^{-1}).

For the remainder of the proof, for
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}},
we let
{\tilde{\alpha}}=\alpha
and for

we let
{\tilde{\alpha}}=\alpha_{j-1}.

Note that (

) is equivalent to the assertion that

(5.29)

\widetilde{\mathcal{S}h}_{i}({\mathcal{Z}}^{op})=\Psi(\mathcal{S}h({\mathcal{Z}})),\forall{\mathcal{Z}}\in B\backslash Q_{1,i+1}.

By the proof of Theorem

, for each
{\mathcal{Z}}
as above, there is a closed

-orbit
{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{0}
in

and a sequence

such that

Hence, to complete the proof, it suffices to prove the claim that if (

) is true for an orbit
{\mathcal{Z}},
then (
5.29
) holds with
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}
in place of

where

with

To establish the claim, it suffices to show that for
\alpha
and
{\mathcal{Z}}
as above, then

(5.30)

\Psi(m(s_{\alpha})*\mathcal{S}h({\mathcal{Z}}))=m(s_{{\tilde{\alpha}}})*\Psi(\mathcal{S}h({\mathcal{Z}})).

Here for

the monoid action is on the left on the left hand side of the equation, and on the right on the right hand side of the equation, and for

, the monoid action is on the right on the left hand side of the equation and on the left on the right hand side of the equation.
Indeed, assuming (
5.30

Now let

,

4.10
,
\alpha

5.12

Now suppose that

5.10
and Remark

4.10

\alpha

\Psi(m(s_{\alpha})*(w,z^{*}))=(\sigma w^{-1}s_{\alpha},w_{i+1}^{*}{z^{*}}^{-1})=m(s_{\alpha})*\Psi((w,z^{*})),

We now show that the map in Equation (

) preserves the Bruhat order on Shareshian pairs. For this, we use the following combinatorial result, which can be found in Chapter 2, Exercise 21 of

Proposition 5.19
.

Let
x,\,y,\,w\in{{W}}_{n+1}
. Suppose
\ell(xw)=\ell(x)+\ell(w)
and
\ell(yw)=\ell(y)+\ell(w)
. Then
xw\leq yw\Leftrightarrow x\leq y.

Theorem 5.20
.

Let
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime},\,{\mathcal{Z}}\in B\backslash Q_{1,i+1}
with
{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}}^{op}
and
{\mathcal{Z}}^{op}

the corresponding
S_{i}
-orbits on
\mathcal{B}_{n}
. Then

(5.35)

\mathcal{S}h({\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{S}h({\mathcal{Z}})\Leftrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{S}h}_{i}({{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}}^{op})\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{S}h}_{i}({\mathcal{Z}}^{op}).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}h({\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime})=(v,y^{*})
and let
\mathcal{S}h({\mathcal{Z}})=(w,z^{*})
. Then by
Equation (
5.22
),
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}h}_{i}({{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}}^{op})=(\sigma v^{-1},w_{i+1}^{*}{y^{*}}^{-1})
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}h}_{i}({\mathcal{Z}}^{op})=(\sigma w^{-1},w_{i+1}^{*}{z^{*}}^{-1})
. By Part (2) of Corollary
5.17

v=\alpha\sigma
and
w=\beta\sigma
with
\alpha,\,\beta\in{W}_{n}
. By Remark
5.13
,
\sigma
is a shortest right coset representative for
W_{n}\subset W_{n+1},
so that

\ell(v)=\ell(\alpha)+\ell(\sigma)
and
\ell(w)=\ell(\beta)+\ell(\sigma)
.
Proposition
5.19
and the fact that the Bruhat order is invariant under inversion imply that
v\leq w\Leftrightarrow\alpha\leq\beta\Leftrightarrow\alpha^{-1}\leq\beta^{-1}
. But
\alpha^{-1}=\sigma v^{-1}
and
\beta^{-1}=\sigma w^{-1}
, so we obtain

(5.36)

v\leq w\Leftrightarrow\sigma v^{-1}\leq\sigma w^{-1}.

For the second components of the Shareshian pairs, it follows from (2) of Corollary
5.17
that

y^{*}=\gamma^{*}w_{i+1}^{*}
and
z^{*}=\delta^{*}w_{i+1}^{*}

with
\gamma^{*},\,\delta^{*}\in{W}_{n}
. Recall from (
5.18
) that
s_{i}^{*}=s_{i-1}
for
2\leq i\leq n
, so that the restriction of the Bruhat order
\leq_{*}
on
({{W}}_{n+1},S^{*})
to
the subgroup
{W}_{n}=\langle s_{2}^{*},\dots,s_{n}^{*}\rangle
is just the standard Bruhat order on
{W}_{n}
. Arguing as above, it follows from Proposition
5.19
and Remark
5.13
that

(5.37)

y^{*}\leq_{*}z^{*}\Leftrightarrow w_{i+1}^{*}{y^{*}}^{-1}\leq w_{i+1}^{*}{z^{*}}^{-1}.

Equation (
5.35
) now follows from Equations (
5.36
) and (
5.37
) and the definition of the Bruhat order on standardized
i
-Shareshian pairs given in (
5.12
).

Q.E.D.

6.
The Orbit Correspondence and Closure Relations

6.1.
The Orbit Correspondence in Terms of Decorated Permutations

In this section, we recall Magyar’s parameterization of

]

Definition 6.1
.

We define a

of

to be a pair
(w,\Delta)
where

and

. Given

with

we consider the
B
-orbit

{\mathcal{O}}_{(w,\Delta)}:=B\cdot(w(\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}),[v_{\Delta}]),\ \text{ where }v_{\Delta}=e_{j_{1}}+\dots+e_{j_{k}}.

Theorem 6.2
.

[
Mag05
]

Each

-orbit on

is of the form

for a unique decorated permutation

See Theorem 3.8 in
[
CE25
]
for a simple proof of Theorem

.

. In this case, we refer to the decorated permutation

\mathcal{O}_{(w,\Delta)}\subset\overline{\mathcal{O}_{(y,\Gamma)}}\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{(w,\Delta)}^{op}\subset\overline{\mathcal{O}_{(y,\Gamma)}^{op}}.

We will prove this Theorem using Magyar’s combinatorial characterization of the closure ordering on
B\backslash(\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
using
\widehat{{W}}_{n}
from
[
Mag05
]
.
We now recall Magyar’s ordering
\unlhd
on
\widehat{{W}}_{n}.
Consider the subspaces
\mathcal{E}_{p}=\mbox{span}\{e_{1},\dots,e_{p}\}
for
p=0,\dots,n
so
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}=0.
Let

(w,\Delta)\in\widehat{{W}}_{n}
and associate to
(w,\Delta)
the following statistics.

(6.11)

\mbox{For }p,\,q\in\{0,\dots,n\}\mbox{ define }r_{p,q}(w):=\dim(\mathcal{E}_{p}\cap w(\mathcal{E}_{q}))=|\{1,\dots,p\}\cap\{w(1),\dots,w(q)\}|.

(6.12)

\mbox{ For }p,\,q\in\{0,1,\dots,n\},\mbox{ define }\delta_{p,q}(w,\Delta):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\mbox{ if for each }\ell\in\Delta,&\ell\leq p\mbox{ or }w^{-1}(\ell)\leq q\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise }&\end{array}\right\}_{.}

Define:

(6.13)

\overline{r}_{p,q}(w,\Delta):=r_{p,q}(w)+\delta_{p,q}(w,\Delta)\mbox{ for any }p,\,q\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}.

Define an ordering on
\widehat{{W}}_{n}
by declaring

(6.14)

\begin{split}&(w,\Delta)\unlhd(y,\Gamma)\Leftrightarrow\\
&(1)\;r_{p,q}(w)\geq r_{p,q}(y)\;\forall\,p,\,q\in\{1,\dots,n\}\mbox{ and }\\
&(2)\;\overline{r}_{p,q}(w,\Delta)\geq\overline{r}_{p,q}(y,\Gamma)\;\forall\;p,\,q\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}.\end{split}

Remark 6.10
.

Note that condition (1) in (

) is equivalent to the condition
that
w\leq y
in the Bruhat order on

by Proposition 7 in

Proposition 6.12

The map

Lemma 6.13
.

Let
p\in\{0,\dots,n\}
and
q\in\{1,\dots,n+1\}
.

(6.16)

\overline{r}_{p,q}(\phi(w,\Delta))=\overline{r}_{q-1,p}(w,\Delta).

Proof.

For
1\leq k\leq n+1,
let
{\mathcal{E}}^{n+1}_{k}
be the span of
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
in
\mbox{${\mathbb{C}}$}^{n+1}.
Recall from Proposition
6.8
the element
\sigma^{-1}w\in{{W}}_{n+1}.
We claim that

(6.17)

r_{p,q}(w)=r_{p+1,q}(\sigma^{-1}w)\mbox{ for }p,\,q\in\{0,\dots,n\},

where as above,
r_{p+1,q}(\sigma^{-1}w)=\dim(\mathcal{E}^{n+1}_{p+1}\cap\sigma^{-1}w(\mathcal{E}^{n+1}_{q})).
Since
w\in{W}_{n}
, for any index
\ell\in\{1,\dots,q\}
,

1\leq w(\ell)\leq n
, so that
\sigma^{-1}w(\ell)=w(\ell)+1
.
Therefore, for
p\geq 1
, we have a bijection between the sets
\{1,\dots,p\}\cap\{w(1),\dots,w(q)\}
and
\{1,\dots,p+1\}\cap\sigma^{-1}w(\{1,\dots,q\})
given by
x\mapsto x+1,
which verifies (
6.17
) when
p\geq 1
and
q\geq 1.
In case
p=0,
then
r_{0,q}(w)=0
, and since
\sigma^{-1}w(\ell)\geq 2
for all
\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
r_{1,q}(\sigma^{-1}w)=0.
For
q=0
, both sides of (
6.17
) are zero and the claim is trivial, so that (
6.17
) is verified in all cases.

Suppose now that
p\in\{0,\dots,n\}
and
q\in\{1,\dots,n+1\}.
We assert that

(6.18)

\delta_{p,q}(\phi(w,\Delta))=\delta_{p,q}(w^{-1}\sigma,[w^{-1}(\Delta):n+1])=\delta_{q-1,p}(w,\Delta).

The first equality is from Proposition
6.8
, and for the second equality, by Equation (
6.12
), we have

(6.19)

\delta_{p,q}(w^{-1}\sigma,[w^{-1}(\Delta):n+1])=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\mbox{ if for each }\ell\in\Delta,&w^{-1}(\ell)\leq p\mbox{ or }\sigma^{-1}w(w^{-1}(\ell))\leq q\\
&&\mbox{ and }n+1\leq p\mbox{ or }\sigma^{-1}w(n+1)\leq q\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise }&\end{array}\right\}_{.}

Because
\Delta\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
,
w\in{W}_{n},
and
\sigma^{-1}(n+1)=1,
(
6.19
) becomes

(6.20)

\delta_{p,q}(w^{-1}\sigma,[w^{-1}(\Delta):n+1])=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\mbox{ if for each }\ell\in\Delta,&w^{-1}(\ell)\leq p\mbox{ or }\ell\leq q-1\\
&&\mbox{ and }1\leq q\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise }&\end{array}\right\}_{.}

Since
q\geq 1
, the second condition is always satisfied and the second equality in (
6.18
) now follows from the definition of
\delta_{p,q}
in (
6.12
).
Note that for any
y\in{W}_{n+1}
and any indices
p,\,q\in\{0,\dots,n+1\}
, it follows from the definition of
r_{p,q}(y)
in (
6.11
)
that

(6.21)

r_{p,q}(y)=r_{q,p}(y^{-1}).

For all
p,q
as above,

\begin{split}\overline{r}_{p,q}(w^{-1}\sigma,[w^{-1}(\Delta):n+1])&=r_{p,q}(w^{-1}\sigma)+\delta_{p,q}(w^{-1}\sigma,[w^{-1}(\Delta):n+1])\\
&=r_{q,p}(\sigma^{-1}w)+\delta_{p,q}(w^{-1}\sigma,[w^{-1}(\Delta):n+1])\mbox{ by }(\ref{eq:rpqinv})\\
&=r_{q-1,p}(w)+\delta_{q-1,p}(w,\Delta)\mbox{ by }(\ref{eq:rpqshift})\mbox{ and }(\ref{eq:deltashift})\\
&=\overline{r}_{q-1,p}(w,\Delta),\end{split}

which by Proposition
6.8
, establishes Equation (
6.16
) and the Lemma.

Q.E.D.

Proof of Proposition
6.12
.

Let
(w,\Delta),\,(y,\Gamma)\in\widehat{{W}}_{n}
. By definition

(w,\Delta)\unlhd(y,\Gamma)
if and only if both conditions (1) and (2) of (
6.14
) are satisfied and similarly for
\phi(w,\Delta)
and
\phi(y,\Gamma)
. By Remark
6.10
, the condition (1) in (
6.14
) is equivalent to
w\leq y
in the usual Bruhat ordering on
{W}_{n}
. But by Proposition
5.19
and invariance of the Bruhat ordering under inversion,

(6.22)

w\leq y\Leftrightarrow w^{-1}\leq y^{-1}\Leftrightarrow w^{-1}\sigma\leq y^{-1}\sigma.

By the formula for
\phi
in (
6.8
), Equation (
6.22
) states that condition (1) of (
6.14
) is satisfied for the marked permutations
(w,\Delta)
and
(y,\Gamma)
if and only if its analogue is satisfied for the marked permutations
\phi(w,\Delta)
and
\phi(y,\Gamma)
.

We now consider the condition (2) in (
6.14
): if
(w,\Delta)\unlhd(y,\Gamma)
, then
\overline{r}_{p,q}(w,\Delta)\geq\overline{r}_{p,q}(y,\Gamma)\;\forall\,p,\,q\in\{0,\dots,n\}.
But by Lemma
6.13
, we have

(6.23)

\overline{r}_{p,q}(w,\Delta)\geq\overline{r}_{p,q}(y,\Gamma)\Leftrightarrow\overline{r}_{q,p+1}(\phi(w,\Delta))\geq\overline{r}_{q,p+1}(\phi(y,\Gamma))\;\forall\,p,\,q\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}.

We need to consider the cases
\overline{r}_{n+1,q}(\phi(w,\Delta))
and

\overline{r}_{p,0}(\phi(w,\Delta))
for
p\leq n
separately. Using Equation (
6.20
), we
see that
\delta_{n+1,q}(\phi(w,\Delta))=1
for all
q\in\{1,\dots,n+1\}
, and that

\delta_{p,0}(\phi(w,\Delta))=0
for any
p
. Since
r_{n+1,q}(w^{-1}\sigma)=q
for any
q\geq 1
, and
r_{p,0}(w^{-1}\sigma)=0
for any
p
, it follows from the definition of

\overline{r}_{p,q}(w,\Delta)
in (
6.13
) that for any two decorated permutations
(w,\Delta)
and
(y,\Gamma)

(6.24)

\overline{r}_{n+1,q}(\phi(w,\Delta))=\overline{r}_{n+1,q}(\phi(y,\Gamma))\;\forall\,q\in\{1,\dots,n+1\},

and

(6.25)

\overline{r}_{p,0}(\phi(w,\Delta))=\overline{r}_{p,0}(\phi(y,\Gamma))\;\forall\,p\in\{0,1,\dots,n+1\},

It follows from Equations (
6.23
)-(
6.25
) that

\overline{r}_{p,q}(w,\Delta)\geq\overline{r}_{p,q}(y,\Gamma)\;\forall\,p,\,q\in\{0,\dots,n\}\Leftrightarrow\overline{r}_{p,q}(\phi(w,\Delta))\geq\overline{r}_{p,q}(\phi(y,\Gamma))\;\forall\,p,\,q\in\{0,\dots,n+1\}.

Thus, the condition (2) of (
6.14
) is satisfied for the decorated permutations

(w,\Delta)
and
(y,\Gamma)
if and only if it is satisfied for the decorated permutations

\phi(w,\Delta)
and
\phi(y,\Gamma)
. Equation (
6.15
) now follows, and the Proposition is proven.

Q.E.D.

We now prove our main result.

6.9
.

By Theorem
6.11
,
\mathcal{O}_{(w,\Delta)}\subset\overline{\mathcal{O}_{(y,\Gamma)}}
