Extracting central charge from ground-state overlaps of spatially deformed Hamiltonians
Chen Bai, Xinyu Sun, Liang-Hong Mo, Hong-Hao Tu

Abstract
We show that the conformal anomaly of a ( 1 + 1 ) (1+1) -dimensional conformal field theory can be extracted directly from a ground-state wave-function overlap associated with a spatial conformal deformation. Focusing on the q q -Möbius deformation, we derive an exact overlap formula between the deformed and undeformed ground states, whose exponent depends only on the central charge. Motivated by this result, we construct a lattice estimator based solely on ground-state overlaps and apply it to representative critical quantum chains and the gapless edge modes of a two-dimensional Chern insulator. Numerical results demonstrate that the resulting overlaps provide a simple and robust probe of the central charge in microscopic models. We further demonstrate that the deformed ground states retain universal geometric structures in their entanglement spectra and entanglement entropies. These results provide a simple wave-function-based route to probing conformal data in critical systems and topological edge modes.



Introduction
.
Gapless one-dimensional (1D) quantum systems, from critical lattice models to boundary modes of two-dimensional (2D) topological phases, are described at low energies by
(1+1)
-dimensional conformal field theories (CFTs)
11

c

25

;
6

31
;
59
;
15
, and quantifies effective low-energy degrees of freedom along renormalization group flows
63
. Extracting
c
from a microscopic model is therefore a key diagnostic for identifying the infrared CFT of a critical system or gapless edge mode.

Several established approaches extract the central charge from lattice models. For 1D critical models, these include finite-size spectral scaling

;
12
;
6
, entanglement entropy scaling
59
;
15
, return amplitudes after quantum quenches
18
;
41
;
40
;
49

38
;
47
, and wave-function-overlap-based probes
65
;
45
. For gapless edge modes of 2D topological phases, momentum polarization
57
;
62
;
37
and modular commutator
35
;
36
;
64
;
24

These developments motivate the search for a simple and universal ground-state overlap that directly encodes the conformal anomaly.

Spatially deformed CFTs provide a natural framework for constructing such overlap probes.
When the Hamiltonian density is modulated by a smooth envelope function generated by a conformal transformation, the resulting nonuniform Hamiltonian remains analytically tractable.
The Möbius and sine-square deformations
30
;
32
;
46
are prominent examples, generated by the global conformal sector and closely related to the uniform theory obtained by radial quantization
33
;
55
;
50
;
56
.
Higher-mode generalizations, including the
q
-Möbius deformation
48
;
10
, involve non-global Virasoro generators
23
;
28
, which are sensitive to the central extension and may therefore encode the conformal anomaly in ground-state overlaps.

In this work, we show that wave-function overlaps associated with the
q
-Möbius deformation provide a direct probe of conformal data in critical systems. We then construct a corresponding lattice realization by applying the same envelope function to microscopic local Hamiltonian terms, such that the overlap between uniform and deformed ground states yields an effective central charge. For several paradigmatic critical chains, including the Ising, three-state Potts, spin-
\frac{1}{2}
Heisenberg, and
\mathrm{SU}(3)
Uimin-Lai-Sutherland chains, finite-size extrapolations of the effective central charge rapidly converge to the expected CFT values. We further show that the deformed ground states retain the universal geometric structure in their entanglement properties and that the construction naturally extends to a 2D topological state, where deforming both edges or only one edge reproduces the expected total or chiral central charge contribution.

q
-Möbius deformation
.
We consider a
(1+1)
-dimensional
unitary
CFT on a circle of circumference
L
with periodic boundary conditions.
The
q
-Möbius deformation is introduced by replacing the uniform Hamiltonian density with a spatially modulated one,

\displaystyle H_{q}(\theta)=\int_{0}^{L}\frac{\differential x}{2\pi}\,f_{q,\theta}(x)\left[T(x)+\bar{T}(x)\right],

(1)

where
T(x)
and
\bar{T}(x)
denote the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic components of the stress tensor
25
.
The envelope function is given by
50
;
23

\displaystyle f_{q,\theta}(x)=1-\tanh(2\theta)\cos\left(\frac{2q\pi x}{L}\right),

(2)

where
q\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
controls the modulation wave number and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
controls the deformation strength.
At
\theta=0
, one has
f_{q,0}(x)=1
, and the undeformed, uniform Hamiltonian is recovered. Hereafter we denote the uniform Hamiltonian as
H
.

The special form of the envelope function makes the deformation analytically tractable.
Indeed, we can express the stress tensor in Virasoro modes, with
L_{n}=\frac{L}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\differential x\,T(x)e^{i2\pi nx/L}+\frac{c}{24}\delta_{n,0}
and similarly for
\bar{L}_{n}
, where
c
is the central charge of the CFT.
In terms of these generators, the
q
-Möbius Hamiltonian (
1
) can be written as

\displaystyle H_{q}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{L}\left[L_{0}-\frac{\tanh(2\theta)}{2}\left(L_{q}+L_{-q}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\;\left.+\bar{L}_{0}-\frac{\tanh(2\theta)}{2}\left(\bar{L}_{q}+\bar{L}_{-q}\right)-\frac{c}{12}\right],

(3)

where the cosine modulation only couples the uniform Hamiltonian to the modes
L_{\pm q}
and
\bar{L}_{\pm q}
.
Thus, the
q
-Möbius-deformed Hamiltonian is not a generic inhomogeneous Hamiltonian, but is generated by a finite conformal transformation.
More precisely, its relation with the uniform Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H_{q}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\cosh(2\theta)}U_{q}(\theta)HU_{q}^{\dagger}(\theta)-\frac{\pi cq^{2}}{6L}\left[1-\frac{1}{\cosh(2\theta)}\right],

(4)

where
U_{q}(\theta)=e^{-\frac{\theta}{q}(L_{q}-L_{-q})}e^{-\frac{\theta}{q}(\bar{L}_{q}-\bar{L}_{-q})}
is a unitary transformation; see the Supplemental Material (SM)
3
.
This relation shows that the deformation preserves the Virasoro structure of the CFT: it rescales the spectrum by
1/\cosh(2\theta)
and shifts the energy by a constant.

For a unitary CFT, the ground state of the uniform Hamiltonian is the conformal vacuum
\ket{0}
.
Equation (
4
) therefore implies that the ground state of the deformed Hamiltonian is obtained by acting on the vacuum with the same conformal transformation
\ket{0_q(\theta)}=U_{q}(\theta)\ket{0}
.
Rewriting this state yields

\displaystyle\ket{0_q(\theta)}=[\cosh\theta]^{-\frac{c(q^{2}-1)}{6q}}\exp\left[\frac{\tanh\theta}{q}\left(L_{-q}+\bar{L}_{-q}\right)\right]\ket{0},

(5)

which follows from disentangling the conformal transformation into normal-ordered form, as derived in the SM
3
.
For
q=1
or
\theta=0
, this state coincides with the uniform ground state as
L_{-1}\ket{0}=\bar{L}_{-1}\ket{0}=0

33
.
For
q>1
and
\theta\neq 0
, however, the deformed ground state is a nontrivial descendant coherent state built from the vacuum.
Its overlap with the uniform ground state is

\displaystyle\langle 0|0_{q}(\theta)\rangle=[\cosh\theta]^{-\frac{c(q^{2}-1)}{6q}}.

(6)

Therefore, the decay of the ground-state overlap directly measures the central charge.
More generally, applying the same construction to a primary state
\ket{\phi}
with scaling dimension
\Delta
gives
\langle\phi|\phi_{q}(\theta)\rangle=[\cosh\theta]^{-\frac{2\Delta}{q}-\frac{c(q^{2}-1)}{6q}}
.
Wave-function overlaps under
q
-Möbius deformation therefore provide a direct probe of conformal data in the underlying CFT.

We now devise a microscopic realization. Consider a 1D critical lattice Hamiltonian with periodic boundary conditions,

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}=\sum_{X}h_{X},

(7)

where the local term
h_{X}
is associated with spatial position
X
. For example, a nearest-neighbor interaction term
h_{j,j+1}
is assigned to
X=j
, while an onsite term
h_{j}
is assigned to
X=j-\frac{1}{2}
. We define the lattice
q
-Möbius deformation by applying the same envelope function in Eq. (
2
) to the local Hamiltonian terms

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{q}(\theta)=\sum_{X}f_{q,\theta}(X)\,h_{X}.

(8)

For fixed
q
and finite
\theta\neq 0
, the deformation profile varies smoothly on the lattice scale in the large-
L
limit. This construction follows the same spirit as the Koo–Saleur approach
38
;
47
.

q

Entanglement structure of deformed ground states
.
The
q
-Möbius deformation induces a nontrivial geometric structure in the entanglement properties of the ground state. In a
(1+1)
-dimensional CFT, the ground state
\ket{0}
is prepared by the Euclidean path integral on an infinite cylinder with spatial circumference
L
. The
q
-Möbius ground state
\ket{0_q(\theta)}
is obtained from
\ket{0}
by the conformal transformation generated by
U_{q}(\theta)
. Equivalently, this transformation maps the original cylinder to a new cylinder with effective circumference
L_{\rm eff}=L\cosh(2\theta)
. On the equal-time slice of the original cylinder, the map reduces to a spatial reparametrization, which we denote by
x\mapsto x^{\rm new}(x)
with

\displaystyle x^{\rm new}(x)=\frac{iL_{\rm eff}}{2\pi}\ln\left(\frac{\cosh\theta-\sinh\theta e^{i\frac{2q\pi x}{L}}}{\cosh\theta e^{i\frac{2q\pi x}{L}}-\sinh\theta}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

(10)

Note that this is not a single-valued function of
x
. Instead, one must select a specific branch cut to ensure that
x^{\rm new}(x)
is a monotonically increasing function for
x\in[0,L]
, with
x^{\rm new}(0)=0
and
x^{\rm new}(L)=L_{\text{eff}}
. Further details are provided in the SM
3
.

For a subsystem
A=[x_{1},x_{2}]
, the reduced density operator of
\ket{0_q(\theta)}
, denoted by
\rho_{A}
, is obtained by applying the same conformal map to the path-integral representation of the vacuum reduced density operator. This maps the replicated geometry to an annulus whose width is determined by the separation of two endpoints in the re-parametrized coordinate together with the local Jacobian factors at the entangling points. As shown in the SM
3
, the resulting annulus width is

\displaystyle W_{A}(q,\theta)

\displaystyle=\ln\left[\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi\,[x^{\text{new}}(x_{1})-x^{\text{new}}(x_{2})]}{L_{\rm eff}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\;+\ln\left[f_{q,\theta}(x_{1})f_{q,\theta}(x_{2})\frac{L_{\rm eff}^{2}}{\pi^{2}\epsilon^{2}}\right],

(11)

where
\epsilon
is the short-distance cutoff.

As in the uniform case
42
, the entanglement spectrum
43
of the deformed ground state retains the universal boundary-CFT tower structure. Denoting the entanglement energies by
\varepsilon_{\alpha}
(i.e., eigenvalues of
-\ln\rho_{A}
), their universal gaps are

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\alpha}-\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{W_{A}{(q,\theta)}}\left(\Delta_{\alpha}-\Delta_{0}\right),

(12)

where
\Delta_{\alpha}
are conformal dimensions
3
. The
q
-Möbius deformation therefore modifies the entanglement spectrum only through the geometric factor
W_{A}(q,\theta)
, while the universal level structure remains governed by the same boundary-CFT spectrum. As shown in Fig.
2
(a-d), the low-lying entanglement spectra of the critical Ising and three-state Potts chains clearly exhibit the predicted boundary-CFT tower structure.

Generalization to Higher Dimensions
.
The overlap construction can also be generalized to 2D topological phases with gapless edge modes. As a representative example, we consider the Qi-Wu-Zhang (QWZ) model on the square lattice
52

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{QWZ}}

\displaystyle=m\sum_{\mathbf{r}}c_{\mathbf{r}}^{\dagger}\sigma^{z}c_{\mathbf{r}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{r}}\left[c_{\mathbf{r}+\hat{x}}^{\dagger}(\sigma^{z}-{\rm i}\sigma^{x})c_{\mathbf{r}}\right.

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\;\left.+c_{\mathbf{r}+\hat{y}}^{\dagger}(\sigma^{z}-{\rm i}\sigma^{y})c_{\mathbf{r}}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right],

(14)

where
c_{\mathbf{r}}=(c_{\mathbf{r},\uparrow},c_{\mathbf{r},\downarrow})^{T}
and
\mathbf{r}=(x,y)
labels the lattice sites. The Pauli matrices
\sigma^{x,y,z}
act on the two internal components of
c_{\mathbf{r}}
. For
-2<m<0
, the QWZ model describes a Chern insulator with Chern number
C=-1
.
On a cylinder geometry, it supports one gapless edge mode (a chiral Dirac fermion) on each edge of the cylinder.
In the following, we set
m=-1
and study the QWZ model on a cylinder with antiperiodic (open) boundary conditions along the
x
(
y
) direction, choosing even
L_{x}
to avoid edge zero modes and place the edge CFT in the Neveu–Schwarz vacuum sector.

To deform the 2D Hamiltonian, we generalize the lattice construction in Eq. (
8
) by applying the same envelope function to the local Hamiltonian terms according to their spatial positions. In Eq. (
14
), the onsite mass term and the hopping term along the
y
direction are assigned to lattice sites, while the hopping term along the
x
direction is assigned to nearest-neighbor bonds. Since the QWZ Hamiltonian is quadratic in fermion operators, the ground states are Slater determinants and their overlaps can be efficiently evaluated using the standard determinant formula
2
.
Figure
3
(a) shows the effective central charge extracted from the deformed QWZ Hamiltonian for different deformation strengths
\theta
and system sizes
L_{x}
, keeping
L_{y}=30
fixed. The finite-size extrapolation to
L_{x}\to\infty
converges to
c=1
, as shown in the inset. This reflects the fact that the two spatially separated edge modes on the cylinder realize the chiral and anti-chiral sectors of an effective
c=1
CFT.

Using correlation matrix techniques for free-fermion systems
51
, we further compute the entanglement entropy for the subsystem with
[0,x]\times[0,L_{y}-1]
at
L_{x}=90
with different deformation strengths
\theta
, as shown in Fig.
3
(b). The numerically computed entanglement entropies are well described by the analytical prediction in Eq. (
13
), supporting the effective CFT description of the deformed edge states. In contrast to the purely 1D examples discussed above, the nonuniversal constant
s_{\mathrm{uv}}
in the entanglement entropy acquires a weak
\theta
dependence due to contributions from the gapped bulk degrees of freedom, as shown in the inset.

An important difference from 1D critical chains is that the two chiral sectors of the gapless edge theory are spatially separated in the 2D Chern insulator. This allows us to selectively deform only one edge, thereby probing a single chiral sector. To implement this, we multiply the deformation profile by an exponential decay factor
e^{-y/\xi_{y}}
along the
y
direction with
\xi_{y}=10
. The deformation therefore becomes negligible for
y\gg\xi_{y}
and effectively acts only on the edge near
y=0
.

The corresponding results are shown in Fig.
3
(c,d). Figure
3
(c) shows that the effective central charge extracted from Eq. (
9
) converges to
c=1/2
in the limit
L_{x}\to\infty
, consistent with the contribution from a single chiral edge sector. In Fig.
3
(d), we plot the entanglement entropy associated with the deformed edge after subtracting the contribution from the undeformed edge. The resulting entropy profile is well described by Eq. (
13
) with
c=1/2
. The fitted nonuniversal term, shown in the inset, remains nearly independent of the deformation strength
\theta
.

Summary and Outlook
.
To summarize, we have introduced a ground-state-overlap estimator for the central charge based on spatially deformed Hamiltonians. Guided by the
q
-Möbius CFT construction, the lattice estimator converges to the expected CFT central charge in representative critical chains and in the edge sector of a 2D Chern-insulator Hamiltonian. The entanglement spectrum and entanglement entropy further confirm that the deformed states retain the predicted conformal geometry while remaining logarithmically entangled and numerically tractable within DMRG.

Looking ahead, controlling finite-size corrections will be important for identifying the optimal deformation window and improving numerical precision. The overlap construction can also be extended beyond the central charge, since overlaps involving deformed primary states encode scaling dimensions in CFT. Together with the edge-state results, this points toward a broader use of spatial deformations as wave-function probes of conformal data, particularly in 2D topological phases.
The unitary origin of the deformation further suggests a possible experimental direction.
An approximate lattice implementation of
U_{q}(\theta)
may provide a circuit-based route to preparing the deformed ground state, while the corresponding spatially modulated Hamiltonians may be realizable in programmable quantum simulators
19
.
If the uniform and deformed ground states can be prepared with sufficient fidelity, the overlap entering our estimator could in principle be accessed using interferometric protocols
21
or randomized-measurement protocols
14
;
22
.

Appendix A
I. CFT derivation of the
q
-Möbius ground-state overlap

In this section, we provide a CFT derivation of the overlap between the uniform ground state and the
q
-Möbius deformed ground state.

We begin with the Virasoro generators satisfying the Virasoro algebra

\displaystyle[L_{n},L_{m}]=(n-m)L_{n+m}+\frac{c}{12}n(n^{2}-1)\delta_{n+m,0},\quad n,m\in\mathbb{Z},

(S1)

where the Virasoro generators are defined by the stress-energy tensor
T(x)
and
\bar{T}(x)

L_{n}=\frac{L}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\differential x\,T(x)e^{i2\pi nx/L}+\frac{c}{24}\delta_{n,0},\quad\qquad\bar{L}_{n}=\frac{L}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\differential x\,\bar{T}(x)e^{-i2\pi nx/L}+\frac{c}{24}\delta_{n,0}.

(S2)

Note that the CFT is defined on a compact system with periodic boundary condition
x\sim x+L
.
For a fixed positive integer
q
, we define

\displaystyle\widetilde{L}_{0}=L_{0}+\frac{c}{24}(q^{2}-1).

(S3)

Then, we have

\displaystyle[L_{q}-L_{-q},\widetilde{L}_{0}]=q(L_{q}+L_{-q}),\qquad[L_{q}-L_{-q},L_{q}+L_{-q}]=4q\widetilde{L}_{0},

(S4)

and the adjoint action of the unitary operator
e^{-\frac{\theta}{q}(L_{q}-L_{-q})}
on
\widetilde{L}_{0}
can be computed using the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula:

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{\theta}{q}(L_{q}-L_{-q})}\,\widetilde{L}_{0}\,e^{\frac{\theta}{q}(L_{q}-L_{-q})}=\cosh(2\theta)\widetilde{L}_{0}-\frac{\sinh(2\theta)}{2}(L_{q}+L_{-q}).

(S5)

Using Eq. (
S5
), we obtain the action of the unitary operator
U_{q}(\theta)=e^{-\frac{\theta}{q}(L_{q}-L_{-q})}e^{-\frac{\theta}{q}(\bar{L}_{q}-\bar{L}_{-q})}
on the uniform Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H=\frac{2\pi}{L}\left(L_{0}+\bar{L}_{0}-\frac{c}{12}\right),

(S6)

which gives

\displaystyle U_{q}(\theta)\,H\,U^{\dagger}_{q}(\theta)=\frac{2\pi}{L}\left[\cosh(2\theta)\left(L_{0}+\bar{L}_{0}-\frac{c}{12}\right)-\frac{\sinh(2\theta)}{2}(L_{q}+L_{-q}+\bar{L}_{q}+\bar{L}_{-q})\right]+\frac{\pi cq^{2}}{6L}[\cosh(2\theta)-1].

(S7)

Rearranging the above expression immediately gives Eq. (4) in the main text.

The deformed ground state is obtained by acting on the uniform CFT ground state
|0\rangle
with the unitary transformation

\displaystyle\ket{0_q(\theta)}=U_{q}(\theta)|0\rangle=e^{-\frac{\theta}{q}(L_{q}-L_{-q})}e^{-\frac{\theta}{q}(\bar{L}_{q}-\bar{L}_{-q})}|0\rangle.

(S8)

To obtain a normal-ordered form of the deformed ground state, we introduce

\displaystyle K_{0}=\frac{\widetilde{L}_{0}}{q},\qquad K_{+}=\frac{L_{-q}}{q},\qquad K_{-}=\frac{L_{q}}{q},

(S9)

which satisfy the SU
(1,1)
algebra

\displaystyle[K_{0},K_{\pm}]=\pm K_{\pm},\qquad[K_{-},K_{+}]=2K_{0}.

(S10)

Using the SU
(1,1)
disentangling formula

\displaystyle e^{\eta(K_{-}-K_{+})}=e^{-\tanh\eta\,K_{+}}e^{-2\ln(\cosh\eta)K_{0}}e^{\tanh\eta\,K_{-}},

(S11)

with
\eta=-\theta
, we obtain

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{\theta}{q}(L_{q}-L_{-q})}\ket{0}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\tanh\theta}{q}L_{-q}}e^{-\frac{2}{q}\ln(\cosh\theta)\left[L_{0}+\frac{c}{24}(q^{2}-1)\right]}e^{-\frac{\tanh\theta}{q}L_{q}}|0\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\tanh\theta}{q}L_{-q}}e^{-\frac{c}{12q}(q^{2}-1)\ln(\cosh\theta)}|0\rangle

\displaystyle=[\cosh\theta]^{-\frac{c}{12q}(q^{2}-1)}e^{\frac{\tanh\theta}{q}L_{-q}}|0\rangle,

(S12)

where we have used
L_{q}\ket{0}=0
for
q\geq 0
.
Including the antiholomorphic sector, the normalized
q
-Möbius deformed ground state in Eq. (
S8
) is then given by

\displaystyle\ket{0_q(\theta)}=[\cosh\theta]^{-\frac{c}{6q}(q^{2}-1)}e^{\frac{\tanh\theta}{q}(L_{-q}+\bar{L}_{-q})}\ket{0}.

(S13)

The
q
-Möbius ground state therefore takes the form
of an SU
(1,1)
coherent state built on the conformal vacuum, and its overlap with the uniform ground state is

\displaystyle\innerproduct{0}{0_q(\theta)}=[\cosh\theta]^{-\frac{c}{6q}(q^{2}-1)}.

(S14)

This yields Eqs. (5) and (6) in the main text.

We now generalize the construction to Virasoro primary states. A Virasoro primary state

\ket{\phi}
with conformal weight
(h,\bar{h})
satisfies
L_{0}\ket{\phi}=h\ket{\phi}
,
\bar{L}_{0}\ket{\phi}=\bar{h}\ket{\phi}
, and
L_{n}\ket{\phi}=\bar{L}_{n}\ket{\phi}=0
for
n>0
. Using

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{\theta}{q}(L_{q}-L_{-q})}\ket{\phi}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\tanh\theta}{q}L_{-q}}e^{-\frac{2}{q}\ln(\cosh\theta)\left[L_{0}+\frac{c}{24}(q^{2}-1)\right]}e^{-\frac{\tanh\theta}{q}L_{q}}\ket{\phi}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\tanh\theta}{q}L_{-q}}e^{-\frac{2}{q}\ln(\cosh\theta)\left[h+\frac{c}{24}(q^{2}-1)\right]}\ket{\phi}

\displaystyle=[\cosh\theta]^{-\frac{2}{q}[h+\frac{c}{24}(q^{2}-1)]}e^{\frac{\tanh\theta}{q}L_{-q}}\ket{\phi},

(S15)

we obtain the
q
-Möbius deformed state

\displaystyle\ket{\phi_q(\theta)}

\displaystyle=U_{q}(\theta)\ket{\phi}

\displaystyle=[\cosh\theta]^{-\frac{2}{q}[h+\bar{h}+\frac{c}{12}(q^{2}-1)]}e^{\frac{\tanh\theta}{q}(L_{-q}+\bar{L}_{-q})}\ket{\phi},

(S16)

where
\Delta=h+\bar{h}
is the scaling dimension of
\ket{\phi}
. Then, we obtain the overlap

\displaystyle\langle\phi|\phi_{q}(\theta)\rangle=[\cosh\theta]^{-\frac{2\Delta}{q}-\frac{c(q^{2}-1)}{6q}}.

(S17)

This shows that the overlap construction naturally extends from the conformal anomaly to the conformal data of primary states.

Appendix B
II. Entanglement spectrum and entanglement entropy of the
q
-Möbius deformed ground state

In this section, we derive the annulus representation of the reduced density operator of the
q
-Möbius ground state.
The derivation proceeds in five steps: (i) review the cylinder Hamiltonian; (ii) map the
q
-Möbius cylinder to an auxiliary uniform cylinder; (iii) uniformize the slit cylinder to an annulus and derive its width
W_{A}{(q,\theta)}
; (iv) quantize the annulus in the open channel and obtain the reduced density operator; and (v) derive the entanglement spectrum and its stress-tensor-normalized form.

Let

w=\tau+ix,\qquad x\sim x+L,\qquad\tau\in\mathbb{R},

(S18)

be the coordinate on the infinite Euclidean cylinder.
The uniform CFT Hamiltonian on a circle of circumference
L
is given by Eq. (
S6
).
At
\tau=0
, with
w=\tau+ix
and
\bar{w}=\tau-ix
, one has
T(x)=T(w=ix)
and
\bar{T}(x)=\bar{T}(\bar{w}=-ix)
in Eq. (
S2
). Accordingly, the uniform ground state
\ket{0}
is prepared by a path integral on this infinite cylinder.

For the
q
-Möbius ground state, we introduce the auxiliary cylinder coordinates
\left(\xi(w),\bar{\xi}(\bar{w})\right)
(a detailed derivation can be found in Appendix A of
10
):

\xi(w)=\frac{L_{\rm eff}}{2\pi}\ln\left(\frac{\cosh\theta-\sinh\theta e^{\frac{2q\pi w}{L}}}{\cosh\theta e^{\frac{2q\pi w}{L}}-\sinh\theta}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\qquad\quad\bar{\xi}(\bar{w})=\frac{L_{\rm eff}}{2\pi}\ln\left(\frac{\cosh\theta-\sinh\theta e^{\frac{2q\pi\bar{w}}{L}}}{\cosh\theta e^{\frac{2q\pi\bar{w}}{L}}-\sinh\theta}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

(S19)

where
L_{\rm eff}=L\cosh(2\theta)
.
This transformation maps the uniform infinite cylinder of circumference
L
to the
q
-Möbius infinite cylinder of circumference
L_{\rm eff}
. The
q
-Möbius Hamiltonian generates time translations along this cylinder, i.e.,

\displaystyle H_{q}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{L}\left[L_{0}-\frac{\tanh(2\theta)}{2}\left(L_{q}+L_{-q}\right)\right.\left.+\bar{L}_{0}-\frac{\tanh(2\theta)}{2}\left(\bar{L}_{q}+\bar{L}_{-q}\right)-\frac{c}{12}\right].

Using the mode expansion in Eq. (
S2
), this Hamiltonian can be written as a spatially weighted integral of the stress tensor on the original cylinder, with deformation profile
f_{q,\theta}(x)=1-\tanh(2\theta)\cos\left(\frac{2q\pi x}{L}\right)
. The stress tensors in the coordinate
w
and the
q
-Möbius coordinate
\xi
are related by

T(w)=\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}w}\right)^{2}T(\xi)+\frac{c}{12}\{\xi,w\},\qquad\quad\bar{T}(\bar{w})=\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\bar{\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\bar{w}}\right)^{2}\bar{T}(\bar{\xi})+\frac{c}{12}\{\bar{\xi},\bar{w}\}.

(S20)

On the equal-time slice
(w=ix,\bar{w}=-ix)
, the Jacobian is
(\mathrm{d}\xi/\mathrm{d}w)|_{w=ix}=-1/f_{q,\theta}(x)
. The Schwarzian derivative is

\displaystyle\{\xi,w\}|_{w=ix}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\frac{2q\pi}{L_{\text{eff}}f_{q,\theta}(x)}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{2q\pi}{L}\right)^{2}\right],

(S21)

with the same expression for the antiholomorphic sector. Substituting these relations into the stress-tensor representation of
H_{q}(\theta)
, one obtains

H_{q}(\theta)=\frac{2\pi}{L_{\rm eff}}\left(L_{0}^{(\xi)}+\bar{L}_{0}^{(\bar{\xi})}-\frac{c}{12}\right)+\frac{\pi cq^{2}}{6L_{\text{eff}}}-\frac{\pi cq^{2}}{6L}.

(S22)

Here,
(L_{0}^{(\xi)},\bar{L}_{0}^{(\bar{\xi})})
are the zero modes defined on the
q
-Möbius cylinder. Pulled back to the original coordinate
(w,\bar{w})
, they are

\displaystyle L_{0}^{(\xi)}

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{\text{eff}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\differential xf_{q,\theta}(x)\left[T(x)-\frac{c}{12}\{\xi(w),w\}|_{w=ix}\right]+\frac{c}{24},

(S23)

\displaystyle\bar{L}_{0}^{(\bar{\xi})}

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{\text{eff}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\differential xf_{q,\theta}(x)\left[\bar{T}(x)-\frac{c}{12}\{\bar{\xi}(\bar{w}),\bar{w}\}|_{\bar{w}=-ix}\right]+\frac{c}{24}.

(S24)

Equation (
S22
) shows that the
q
-Möbius Hamiltonian is equivalent to the uniform CFT Hamiltonian on a cylinder of circumference
L_{\rm eff}
, up to a constant Casimir-energy shift. Therefore, compared with the uniform ground state
\ket{0}
, the vacuum energy is shifted by

\displaystyle\Delta E_{\rm Casimir}=\frac{\pi cq^{2}}{6L_{\rm eff}}-\frac{\pi cq^{2}}{6L}.

(S25)

Consider a subsystem

A=[x_{1},x_{2}]

(S26)

on the original equal-time slice
\tau=0
. It is easy to check that
\xi(ix_{j})
is a purely imaginary function; thus, we define the deformed spatial coordinate as

x^{\text{new}}(x)=-\text{Im}\left[\xi(ix)\right]=\frac{L_{\rm eff}}{q}\left[\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan\left(e^{2\theta}\tan\left[\pi\left(\frac{qx}{L}-\left\lfloor\frac{qx}{L}+\frac{1}{2}\right\rfloor\right)\right]\right)+\left\lfloor\frac{qx}{L}+\frac{1}{2}\right\rfloor\right],

(S27)

where the floor function
\left\lfloor\frac{qx}{L}+\frac{1}{2}\right\rfloor
corresponds to a specific choice of branch cut for
\xi
. This ensures that, when evaluated numerically in Python,
x^{\text{new}}(x)
is a monotonically increasing function for
x\in[0,L]
, with
x^{\rm new}(0)=0
and
x^{\rm new}(L)=L_{\text{eff}}
(see Eq. (10) in the main text). Accordingly, the endpoints are given by

x_{1}^{\text{new}}=x^{\text{new}}(x_{1}),\qquad x_{2}^{\text{new}}=x^{\text{new}}(x_{2}).

(S28)

The reduced density operator
\rho_{A}
is represented by a path integral on the
auxiliary cylinder with a slit along the interval

[x_{1}^{\text{new}},x_{2}^{\text{new}}]
at
\tau=0
.

We first map the
q
-Möbius cylinder to the complex plane by

z=\exp\left(\frac{2\pi\xi}{L_{\rm eff}}\right).

(S29)

The two endpoints become

z_{j}=\exp\left(i\frac{2\pi x_{j}^{\text{new}}}{L_{\rm eff}}\right),\qquad j=1,2.

(S30)

Their separation on the unit circle is

|z_{1}-z_{2}|=2\left|\sin\left(\frac{\pi(x_{1}^{\text{new}}-x_{2}^{\text{new}})}{L_{\rm eff}}\right)\right|.

(S31)

The slit plane is then uniformized to an annulus by

\zeta=\ln\left(\frac{z-z_{1}}{z-z_{2}}\right),\qquad\zeta=u+iv.

(S32)

The coordinate
v
is periodic,

v\sim v+2\pi,

(S33)

and the two regulated endpoints become the two annulus boundaries. The annulus
width is the logarithmic distance between these two boundaries,

W_{A}{(q,\theta)}=\ln\left(\frac{|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}}{\delta_{1}\delta_{2}}\right),

(S34)

where
\delta_{j}
is the ultraviolet (UV) cutoff around
z_{j}
in the plane.

We now express
\delta_{j}
in terms of the original cutoff
\epsilon
in the

x
coordinate. Since

\mathrm{d}x^{\text{new}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{f_{q,\theta}(x)},

(S35)

a cutoff
\epsilon
in the original coordinate becomes

\epsilon_{j}^{\text{new}}=\frac{\epsilon}{f_{q,\theta}(x_{j})}

(S36)

in the auxiliary cylinder coordinate. Under the map

z=\exp(2\pi\xi/L_{\rm eff})
, the local cutoff becomes

\delta_{j}=\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}\xi}\right|_{\xi=ix_{j}^{\text{new}}}\epsilon_{j}^{\text{new}}=\frac{2\pi}{L_{\rm eff}}\frac{\epsilon}{f_{q,\theta}(x_{j})}.

(S37)

Substituting Eqs. (
S31
) and
(
S37
) into Eq. (
S34
), we obtain Eq. (11) in the main text:

W_{A}{(q,\theta)}=\ln\left[\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi[x^{\text{new}}(x_{1})-x^{\text{new}}(x_{2})]}{L_{\rm eff}}\right)\right]+\ln\left[f_{q,\theta}(x_{1})f_{q,\theta}(x_{2})\frac{L_{\rm eff}^{2}}{\pi^{2}\epsilon^{2}}\right].

(S38)

For
\theta=0
, one has
L_{\rm eff}=L
,

f_{q,\theta}(x)=1
, and
x^{\text{new}}(x)=x
, so

W_{A}(q,0)=\ln\left[\frac{L^{2}}{\pi^{2}\epsilon^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi(x_{1}-x_{2})}{L}\right)\right],

(S39)

which is the standard annulus width for a single interval on a circle.

The annulus has coordinates

\zeta=u+iv,\quad 0\leq u\leq W_{A}{(q,\theta)},\quad v\sim v+2\pi.

(S40)

In the open-string channel
25
,
v
is Euclidean time and
u
is the spatial coordinate on a strip of width
W_{A}{(q,\theta)}
. The open-string channel Hamiltonian is

H_{\rm strip}=\frac{\pi}{W_{A}{(q,\theta)}}\left(L_{0}^{\rm op.}-\frac{c}{24}\right).

(S41)

Here
L_{0}^{\rm op.}
is the Virasoro zero mode in the open-string channel. The Casimir shift is
-c/24
, rather than
-c/12
, because the open-string channel Hilbert space carries a single Virasoro algebra.

Going once around the annulus corresponds to Euclidean time evolution by

\Delta v=2\pi.

(S42)

Therefore the unnormalized reduced density operator is

\begin{split}\rho_{A}^{\rm unnorm}&=e^{-2\pi H_{\rm strip}}\\
&=\exp\left[-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{W_{A}{(q,\theta)}}\left(L_{0}^{\rm op.}-\frac{c}{24}\right)\right].\end{split}

(S43)

After imposing
\tr\rho_{A}=1
, we obtain

\rho_{A}(q,\theta)=\frac{1}{Z_{A}}\exp\left[-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{W_{A}{(q,\theta)}}\left(L_{0}^{\rm op.}-\frac{c}{24}\right)\right],

(S44)

where

Z_{A}=\tr\exp\left[-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{W_{A}{(q,\theta)}}\left(L_{0}^{\rm op.}-\frac{c}{24}\right)\right].

(S45)

Equivalently,

\rho_{A}(q,\theta)=\frac{1}{Z_{A}}q_{A}^{\,L_{0}^{\rm op.}-c/24},\quad q_{A}=\exp\left[-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{W_{A}{(q,\theta)}}\right].

(S46)

Let the annulus Hilbert space be decomposed into eigenstates of

L_{0}^{\rm op.}
,

L_{0}^{\rm op.}|\alpha\rangle=h_{\alpha}|\alpha\rangle,

(S47)

where
\ket{\alpha}
is a state with conformal weight
h_{\alpha}
.
Then, Eq. (
S44
) gives the eigenvalues of the reduced density
matrix,

\lambda_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{Z_{A}}\exp\left[-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{W_{A}{(q,\theta)}}\left(h_{\alpha}-\frac{c}{24}\right)\right].

(S48)

The entanglement energies are defined by

\varepsilon_{\alpha}=-\ln\lambda_{\alpha}.

(S49)

Therefore

\varepsilon_{\alpha}=\ln Z_{A}+\frac{2\pi^{2}}{W_{A}{(q,\theta)}}\left(h_{\alpha}-\frac{c}{24}\right).

(S50)

Taking gaps relative to the lowest entanglement level removes both the
normalization
\ln Z_{A}
and the Casimir shift:

\varepsilon_{\alpha}-\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{W_{A}{(q,\theta)}}\left(h_{\alpha}-h_{0}\right),

(S51)

which yields Eq. (12) in the main text.
Thus, the
q
-Möbius deformation changes the overall entanglement scale through the annulus width
W_{A}{(q,\theta)}
, but the low-lying entanglement gaps are still organized by conformal weights in the annulus channel.

To remove the geometric scale, we normalize entanglement gaps by the
stress-tensor gap. The (holomorphic) stress tensor
T
has conformal weight
h_{T}=2
. Therefore,

\varepsilon_{T}-\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{W_{A}{(q,\theta)}}\left(h_{T}-h_{0}\right).

(S52)

In the vacuum-dominant channel,
h_{0}=0
, such that

\varepsilon_{T}-\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{4\pi^{2}}{W_{A}{(q,\theta)}}.

(S53)

Combining this with Eq. (
S51
), we obtain

\frac{\varepsilon_{\alpha}-\varepsilon_{0}}{\varepsilon_{T}-\varepsilon_{0}}=\frac{h_{\alpha}}{2}.

(S54)

More generally, if the lowest state in the relevant annulus channel has

h_{0}\neq 0
, then

\frac{\varepsilon_{\alpha}-\varepsilon_{0}}{\varepsilon_{T}-\varepsilon_{0}}=\frac{h_{\alpha}-h_{0}}{h_{T}-h_{0}}.

(S55)

In the end, we derive the von Neumann entanglement entropy from the entanglement energies. From Eq. (
S44
), it is useful to define

\beta_{A}=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{W_{A}(q,\theta)}.

(S56)

Then the reduced density operator takes the thermal form

\rho_{A}(q,\theta)=\frac{1}{Z_{A}(\beta_{A})}\exp\left[-\beta_{A}\left(L_{0}^{\rm op.}-\frac{c}{24}\right)\right],

(S57)

with

Z_{A}(\beta_{A})=Z_{A}=\tr_{\mathcal{H}_{\rm op}}\exp\left[-\beta_{A}\left(L_{0}^{\rm op.}-\frac{c}{24}\right)\right].

(S58)

Here the trace is over the full open-string channel Hilbert space, including both primary states and their descendants. The von Neumann entropy is therefore

\begin{split}S_{A}&=-\tr(\rho_A \ln\rho_A)=\sum_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\\
&=\ln Z_{A}(\beta_{A})+\beta_{A}\left\langle L_{0}^{\rm op.}-\frac{c}{24}\right\rangle_{\rho_{A}}.\end{split}

(S59)

Using

\partial_{\beta_{A}}\ln Z_{A}(\beta_{A})=-\left\langle L_{0}^{\rm op.}-\frac{c}{24}\right\rangle_{\rho_{A}},

(S60)

we obtain the standard thermodynamic identity

S_{A}=\left(1-\beta_{A}\partial_{\beta_{A}}\right)\ln Z_{A}(\beta_{A}).

(S61)

To evaluate
Z_{A}(\beta_{A})
in the universal regime, we take the UV limit
\epsilon\to 0
, for which

W_{A}(q,\theta)\gg 1,\qquad\beta_{A}=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{W_{A}(q,\theta)}\ll 1.

(S62)

The open-string channel annulus partition function is then evaluated by a modular transformation to the closed channel. The leading contribution is dominated by the ground state, giving

\ln Z_{A}(\beta_{A})=\frac{\pi^{2}c}{6\beta_{A}}+\ln(g_a g_b)+s_{\rm UV},

(S63)

where
g_{a}
and
g_{b}
are Affleck-Ludwig boundary
g
factors
5
associated with the entangling cuts, and
s_{\rm UV}
is a nonuniversal UV-dependent constant. Applying
Eq. (
S61
), we find

S_{A}=\left(1-\beta_{A}\partial_{\beta_{A}}\right)\left[\frac{\pi^{2}c}{6\beta_{A}}+\ln(g_a g_b)+s_{\rm UV}\right]=\frac{\pi^{2}c}{3\beta_{A}}+s_{1},

(S64)

where

s_{1}=\ln(g_a g_b)+s_{\rm UV}

(S65)

is a nonuniversal additive constant. Since

\beta_{A}=2\pi^{2}/W_{A}(q,\theta)
, this becomes

S_{A}=\frac{c}{6}W_{A}(q,\theta)+s_{1}.

(S66)

Substituting Eq. (
S38
), we finally obtain Eq. (13) in the main text

S_{A}=\frac{c}{3}\ln\left|\sin\left(\frac{\pi[x^{\text{new}}(x_{1})-x^{\text{new}}(x_{2})]}{L_{\rm eff}}\right)\right|+\frac{c}{6}\ln\left[f_{q,\theta}(x_{1})f_{q,\theta}(x_{2})\frac{L_{\rm eff}^{2}}{\pi^{2}\epsilon^{2}}\right]+s_{1}.

(S67)

Thus, the
q
-Möbius deformation modifies the entanglement entropy through the deformed annulus width
W_{A}(q,\theta)
, while the universal coefficient of the logarithm remains fixed by the central charge.
