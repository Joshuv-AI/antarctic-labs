 What is Resilience?
Resilience: Ability of a system to anticipate, prepare for, and
adapt to changing conditions and withstand, respond to,
and recover rapidly from disruptions through adaptable and holistic
planning and technical solutions.

 Summary of Key Findings
➢ Transmission reliability impacts
 ➢ Transmission + generation repairs can reduce exposure to load drops in blue sky operation by over 90%.
 ➢ Reliability gains are driven by restoring thermal ratings, increasing dependable capacity, and adding
 batteries/peakers.
➢ Resilience impacts
 ➢ Hardening measures reduce the scale of system damage and accelerate recovery under severe events.
 ➢ Transmission and distribution infrastructure along with generation upgrades produce measurable improvements
 in storm resilience.
➢ Resource adequacy impacts
 ➢ System-level adequacy improves dramatically - PRIReP + Stabilization Plan upgrades reduce Expected
 Unserved Energy (EUE) by >99.6% across all forced-outage-rate (FOR) assumptions.
 ➢ Loss of Load Expectation (LOLE) drops to under ~1.7 hours/year, indicating a substantial decrease in the
 likelihood of supply shortfalls.
 ➢ Infrastructure upgrades (generator repairs, baseload restoration, batteries, peakers, and transmission
 corrections) are the dominant driver of adequacy benefits.
