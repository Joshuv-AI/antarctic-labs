Degree Sequences vs. Forests in Bipartite Graphs
Darij Grinberg, Benjamin Liber

Abstract
We prove a conjecture of Shteiner and Shteyner stating that for a bipartite graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) , the number of forests in G G equals the number of degree sequences arising from its spanning subgraphs. In the process, we provide several equivalent evaluations of the Tutte polynomial T G ​ ( x , y ) T_{G}(x,y) at ( 2 , 1 ) (2,1) , including interpretations in terms of degree vectors obtained from orientations of G G .



In recent work on counting matrices
[
5

Since different subgraphs can have the same degree sequence, this problem is nontrivial.
Shteiner and Shteyner have conjectured that the number of degree sequences of subgraphs of a bipartite graph

We rely on a related result for score vectors of orientations found by Stanley in 1980

2.1
The Tutte Polynomial

An edge
e
of a graph
G
is called a
bridge
if the removal of this edge from
G
disconnects its two endpoints (i.e., its two endpoints belong to different connected components of the resulting graph).

The Tutte polynomial is a fundamental invariant of a graph, which is defined as follows:

Definition 2.1
.

The
Tutte polynomial
of a graph
G=(V,E)
is

\displaystyle T_{G}(x,y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E}(x-1)^{k(A)-k(E)}(y-1)^{k(A)+|A|-|V|}\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]

where
k(A)
denotes the
number of connected components
of the graph
(V,A)
.
(Note that both exponents are nonnegative: this is clear for
k(A)-k(E)
, whereas for
k(A)+|A|-|V|
it follows from Lemma
2.2

Alternatively, the Tutte polynomial can be defined recursively via a
deletion-contraction recurrence

For any edge

\displaystyle T_{G}(x,y)=\begin{cases}T_{G-e}(x,y)+T_{G/e}(x,y),&\text{if $e$ is neither a loop nor a bridge;}\\
xT_{G-e}(x,y),&\text{if $e$ is a bridge;}\\
yT_{G-e}(x,y),&\text{if $e$ is a loop;}\\
1,&\text{if $G$ has no edges,}\end{cases}

(1)

where
G-e=(V,E\setminus\{e\})
denotes the graph obtained from

by

the edge

and

denotes the graph obtained from

\displaystyle V(G/e)=(V\setminus\{v_{i},v_{j}\})\cup\{v_{ij}\}.

Many classical graph invariants are obtained as evaluations of

Lemma 2.2
.

For each graph

k(E)\geq|V|-|E|

Proof.

Let

denote the connected components of

Since each component of

\displaystyle|E|=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|E_{i}|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}(|V_{i}|-1)=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}|V_{i}|\Big)-k=|V|-k=|V|-k(E).

(2)

In other words,
k(E)\geq|V|-|E|

∎

Lemma 2.3

A graph

Proof.

Let

denote the connected components of

First, suppose

Hence, the inequality (

In other words,

Conversely, suppose

.
Then,
|E|=|V|-k(E)

Hence, all the inequalities

In other words,

Since each component

\displaystyle=\sum_{A\subseteq E}0^{k(A)+|A|-|V|}.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let

be a graph.
If

\displaystyle T_{G}(2,1)=T_{G-e}(2,1)+T_{G/e}(2,1).

By (

\displaystyle T_{G}(2,1)=\sum_{A\subseteq E}0^{k(A)+|A|-|V|}.

Hence,

\displaystyle T_{G}(2,1)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq E;\\
e\notin A\end{subarray}}0^{k(A)+|A|-|V|}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq E;\\

Now, let us note that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq E;\\
e\notin A\end{subarray}}0^{k(A)+|A|-|V|}=\sum_{A\subseteq E\setminus\{e\}}0^{k(A)+|A|-|V|}=T_{G-e}(2,1)

(by (

), now applied to the graph

).

On the other hand, for any
A\subseteq E

denote the set

, regarded as a set of edges of

The map

is bijective because this process is reversible: every subset of

Clearly,

. Moreover,

\displaystyle\underbrace{k(A^{\prime})}_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}=k(A)}}+\underbrace{|A^{\prime}|}_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}=|A|-1}}-\underbrace{|V(G/e)|}_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}=|V|-1}}=k(A)+(|A|-1)-(|V|-1)=k(A)+|A|-|V|.

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq E;\\
e\in A\end{subarray}}0^{k(A)+|A|-|V|}=\sum_{A^{\prime}\subseteq E(G/e)}0^{k(A^{\prime})+|A^{\prime}|-|V(G/e)|}=T_{G/e}(2,1)

(by (

Altogether,

\displaystyle T_{G}(2,1)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq E;\\
e\notin A\end{subarray}}0^{k(A)+|A|-|V|}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}=T_{G-e}(2,1)}}+\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq E;\\

Thus, the value

\displaystyle T_{G}(2,1)=\begin{cases}T_{G-e}(2,1)+T_{G/e}(2,1),&\text{if $e$ is not a loop;}\\
T_{G-e}(2,1),&\text{if $e$ is a loop};\\
1,&\text{if $G$ has no edges.}\end{cases}

(5)

(Indeed, the first case follows from Lemma

An

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}:E\to V\times V

We say that an arc

Definition 2.7

Fix an orientation

1.

The

\displaystyle\deg_{G_{\mathcal{O}}}^{-}:=(\deg_{G_{\mathcal{O}}}^{-}(v_{1}),\ldots,\deg_{G_{\mathcal{O}}}^{-}(v_{n})),

The

\displaystyle\deg_{G_{\mathcal{O}}}^{+}:=(\deg_{G_{\mathcal{O}}}^{+}(v_{1}),\ldots,\deg_{G_{\mathcal{O}}}^{+}(v_{n})),

.

The

\displaystyle s_{G_{\mathcal{O}}}:=(s_{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(v_{1}),\ldots,s_{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(v_{n})),

4.

We define indegree vectors, outdegree vectors, and score vectors similarly for any spanning subdigraph of

For any spanning subgraph

of

Suppose that both

of the corresponding spanning subdigraph

\displaystyle(\#\text{ of degree sequences $\deg_{H}$ of spanning subgraphs $H\subseteq G$})

\displaystyle=(\#\text{ of score vectors of spanning subdigraphs of $G_{\mathcal{O}}$})

\displaystyle=T_{G}(2,1)\qquad\left(\text{by \eqref{eq.equiv.15}}\right)

\displaystyle=(\#\text{ of forests in $G$})\qquad\left(\text{by Proposition \ref{num_of_trees}}\right).
