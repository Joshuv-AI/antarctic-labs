The congruence subgroup property for S S -arithmetic subgroups of simple algebraic groups when S S has positive Dirichlet density
Andrei S. Rapinchuk

Abstract
. Let G G be an absolutely almost simple simply connected algebraic group defined over a number field K K , and let M / K M/K be the minimal Galois extension over which G G becomes an inner form of a split group. Assume that G G satisfies the Margulis-Platonov conjecture over K K . We prove that if S S is a set of valuations of K K that contains all archimedean ones but does not contain any nonarchimedean valuations v v for which G G is anisotropic over the completion K v K_{v} such that its intersection S ∩ Spl ⁡ ( M / K ) S\cap\mathrm{Spl}(M/K) with the set Spl ⁡ ( M / K ) \mathrm{Spl}(M/K) of nonarchimedean valuations of K K that split completely in M M has positive Dirichlet density, then the congruence kernel C S ​ ( G ) C^{S}(G) is trivial. This result provides additional evidence for Serre’s Congruence Subgroup Conjecture [ 19 ] . The proof does not involve any case-by-case considerations and relies on previous results concerning the congruence kernel [ 13 ] and recent results on almost strong approximation [ 1 ] , [ 17 ] .

Main Theorem

Let
G
be an absolutely almost simple simply connected algebraic group defined over a number field

of all (inequivalent) valuations of

be the corresponding congruence kernel (cf.
[
11
]
,
[
15
]
). Then the
Congruence Subgroup Problem
(CSP) amounts to computing

for given

and

; recall that

is trivial if and only if
the classical congruence subgroup property holds for the group
G(\mathcal{O}(S))
of points of

over the ring
\mathcal{O}(S)
of
S
-integers in

\mathrm{rk}_{S}\>G\,:=\,\sum_{v\in S}\mathrm{rk}_{S}\>G\,\geq\,2\ \ \text{(and}\ \ \mathrm{rk}_{K_{v}}\>G>0\ \ \text{for all}\ \ v\in S\setminus V^{K}_{\infty}),

G

]

While Serre’s conjecture has been confirmed in a number of cases (see

cofinite

2.
A condition for centrality of the congruence kernel

We begin with a brief review of the fundamental notions associated with the Congruence Subgroup Problem (see
[
11
]
,
[
15
]
for more details). While these can be defined for arbitrary linear algebraic groups, we will focus here on the case of principal interest to us where
G
is an absolutely almost simple simply connected algebraic group defined over a number field
K
. Let us fix for now a faithful
K
-defined representation
\rho\colon G\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}
.

Next, given a subset
S\subset V^{K}
containing
V^{K}_{\infty}
, we let
\mathcal{O}(S)
denote the ring of
S
-integers in
K
, and consider the group of
\mathcal{O}(S)
-points

G(\mathcal{O}(S)):=G(K)\cap\rho^{-1}\left(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}(S))\right).

Furthermore, for a nonzero ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}(S)
, we define the corresponding congruence subgroup

G(\mathcal{O}(S),\mathfrak{a}):=G(\mathcal{O}(S))\cap\rho^{-1}\left(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}(S),\mathfrak{a})\right),

where
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}(S),\mathfrak{a})
is the usual congruence subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}(S))
of level
\mathfrak{a}
. Then for any ideal
\mathfrak{a}
, the congruence subgroup
G(\mathcal{O}(S),\mathfrak{a})
is a normal subgroup of
G(\mathcal{O}(S))
of finite index, and the classical Congruence Subgroup Problem asks if the converse is true, viz. if every finite index normal subgroup of
G(\mathcal{O}(S))
contains a suitable congruence subgroup. The current analysis of this problem relies on the notion of the (
S
-)congruence kernel, introduced by J.-P. Serre
[
19
]
, which we now recall.
One defines two topologies on the group of
K
-rational points
G(K)
compatible with the group structure and independent of the choice of
\rho
:

\bullet

\tau_{a}^{S}
, the
S
-arithmetic topology, having the collection of all finite index (normal) subgroups
N\subset G(\mathcal{O}(S))
as a fundamental system of neighborhoods of the identity, and

\bullet

\tau_{c}^{S}
, the
S
-congruence topology, having the collection of all congruence subgroups
G(\mathcal{O}(S),\mathfrak{a})
as a fundamental system of neighborhoods
of the identity.

Then the affirmative answer to the classical CSP for
G(\mathcal{O}(S))
is equivalent to the fact that
\tau_{a}^{S}=\tau_{c}^{S}
. To gauge the difference between these two topologies in the general case, one observes that
G(K)
admits completions in the category of locally compact topological groups with respect to both
\tau_{a}^{S}
and
\tau_{c}^{S}
which are called the
S
-arithmetic completion and the
S
-congruence completion and denoted
\widehat{G}^{S}
and
\overline{G}^{S}
, respectively. Since
\tau_{a}^{S}
is stronger than
\tau_{c}^{S}
, there exists a continuous group homomorphism
\pi\colon\widehat{G}^{S}\to\overline{G}^{S}
which turns out to be surjective. Thus, we have the following exact sequence

(C)

1\to C^{S}(G)\longrightarrow\widehat{G}^{S}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\overline{G}^{S}\to 1,

where
C^{S}(G):=\ker\pi
is the (
S
-)
congruence kernel
of
G
(note that it is independent of
\rho
). It is easy to see that
\tau_{a}^{S}=\tau_{c}^{S}
if and only if
C^{S}(G)=\{1\}
. Thus, Theorem
1.1
asserts that in the situation described in its statement we have the affirmative answer to the classical CSP for
G(\mathcal{O}(S))
. If the group
G(\mathcal{O}(S))
is finite then trivially
C^{S}(G)=\{1\}
(in fact, this situation never occurs in the case of infinite
S
which is our main focus in this note). Otherwise it follows from the Strong Approximation Theorem (cf.
[
6
, Ch. VII]
) that the completion
\overline{G}^{S}
can be naturally identified with the group of
S
-adeles
G(\mathbb{A}_{S})
where
\mathbb{A}_{S}=\mathbb{A}_{K,S}
is the ring of
S
-adeles of
K
, and we will routinely use this identification.

A standard strategy for proving that
C^{S}(G)=\{1\}
is based on proving first that
C^{S}(G)
is
central
(i.e. (
C
) is a central extension). Once this is accomplished, one shows, assuming that (MP) holds for
G(K)
, that
C^{S}(G)
is isomorphic to the (dual of the)
metaplectic kernel

M(S,G)
. It follows from the computations of
M(S,G)
in
[
8
]
that for any infinite
S
, the metaplectic kernel
M(S,G)
is trivial. Thus, in order to prove Theorem
1.1
it is enough to show that in the situation described in it the congruence kernel
C^{S}(G)
is central. For this we will use the following statement (which is a variant of Theorem 3.1(ii) in
[
13
]
) where for the ease of notations we let
\Gamma
denote
G(\mathcal{O}(S))
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
G
satisfies (MP) over
K
and that a subset
S\subset V^{K}
contains
V^{K}_{\infty}
and does not contain any
v\in V^{K}_{f}
with
\mathrm{rk}_{K_{v}}\>G=0
. If there exists
n\geq 1
such that for every
x\in C^{S}(G)
we have

(1)

\pi(Z_{\widehat{G}^{S}}(x))\supset\Gamma^{n},

where
Z_{\widehat{G}^{S}}(x)
denotes the centralizer of
x
in
\widehat{G}^{S}
. Then (
C
) is a central extension.

It should be pointed that at least in spirit our condition (
1
) is akin to the considerations used by Serre
[
19
]
in the context of the CSP for
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
over the ring
\mathcal{O}(S)
with infinite group of units
\mathcal{O}(S)^{\times}
, where he first proves that for a unit
u\in\mathcal{O}(S)
of infinite order and
h(u)=\mathrm{diag}(u,u^{-1})
there exists
n\geq 1
such that

centralizes the congruence kernel

, and then derives the centrality of the latter. (In fact, Serre showed that in this case one can take for such an
n
the number of roots of unity in
K
, however
in our application of Theorem
2.1
for proving Theorem
1.1
the value of

is less explicit – cf. the beginning of the proof of Proposition

).

Theorem
2.1
follows from the following two results established in
[
13
]
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be as in the statement of (MP) noting that by our assumption
\mathcal{A}\cap S=\varnothing
. Set

\mathfrak{Z}=\{v\in V^{K}\setminus(\mathcal{A}\cup S)\,|\,\pi(Z_{\widehat{G}^{S}})\supset G(K_{v})\}

(hereinafter, we routinely identify
\overline{G}^{S}
with
G(\mathbb{A}_{S})
, which enables us to naturally consider
G(K_{v})
for

as a subgroup of
\overline{G}^{S}
).

Proposition 2.2
.

(Proposition 2.2 in
[
13
]
)

Let

. Assume that there exists
a\in G(K_{v})
such that

for every

. Then

.

Proposition 2.3
.

(cf. Proposition 2.5 in
[
13
]
)

Assume that
G
satisfies (MP) over
K
and that
\mathcal{A}\cap S=\varnothing
. If
\mathfrak{Z}=V^{K}\setminus(\mathcal{A}\cup S)
then (

) is a central extension.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.
Since
\ker\pi=C^{S}(G)
is a profinite, hence compact, group, the map
\pi
is closed, and therefore (
1
) implies that for every
x\in C^{S}(G)
we have the inclusion

\pi\left(Z_{\widehat{G}^{S}}\right)\supset\overline{\Gamma}^{n},

where
\overline{\Gamma}
is the closure of
\Gamma
in
\overline{G}^{S}=G(\mathbb{A}_{S})
. Clearly,

is an open compact subgroup of

, and since

is dense in

is dense in

, and therefore
\overline{\Gamma}=W
. For each

, the group

is Zariski-dense in

(cf.
[

, Lemma 3.2]
), and therefore
G(\mathcal{O}_{v})^{n}\not\subset Z(G(K_{v})
. Applying Proposition
2.2
, we obtain that

, and thus
\mathfrak{Z}=V^{K}\setminus(\mathcal{A}\cup S)
. Then the centrality of (
C
) follows from Proposition
2.3
.
\Box

The following statement is used to verify condition (
1
).

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
n\geq 1
. Assume that for every finite index normal subgroup
N
of
\Gamma
and any
y\in\overline{N}\cap\Gamma
we have the inclusion

(2)

Z(N,y)(\overline{N}\cap\Gamma)\supset\Gamma^{n},

where
Z(N,y):=\{t\in\Gamma\ |\ [y,t]\in N\}
. Then (
1
) holds for all
x\in C^{S}(G)
.

(We note that
Z(N,y)
is simply the pullback of the centralizer of
yN
in
\Gamma/N
under the canonical homomorphism
\Gamma\to\Gamma/N
.)

Proof.

(cf.
[
13
, proof of Proposition 3.2]
) Fix
x\in C^{S}(G)
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Let
\mathcal{R}
be the family of all open normal subgroups of the profinite completion
\widehat{\Gamma}
, which is naturally identified with the closure of
\Gamma
in
\widehat{G}^{S}
. For
R\in\mathcal{R}
, we set

N_{R}=\Gamma\cap R\ \ \text{and}\ \ \tilde{R}=\pi^{-1}(\overline{N_{R}})=R\cdot C^{S}(G),

and pick an arbitrary
x_{R}\in\Gamma\cap(xR)
. Applying (

) to

and

, we obtain that

\gamma^{n}\in Z(N_{R},x_{R})(\overline{N_{R}}\cap\Gamma)\subset\tilde{Z}(R,x)\tilde{R},

Using the compactness argument, one easily derives from (

which implies that

3.
On Dirichlet density

3.1.
Definitions and elementary properties

Let
K
be a number field,
\mathcal{O}
be its ring of algebraic integers, and
\mathcal{P}
be the set of primes, i.e. the set of nonzero prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}
. (In the sequel, we will freely identify
\mathcal{P}
with
V^{K}_{f}
.) As usual, for
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}
, we let
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}
denote the number of elements in the quotient
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}
. Gven a subset
A\subset\mathcal{P}
, we define

\xi_{K,A}(s)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in A}\frac{1}{(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p})^{s}}\ \ \text{for real}\ \ s>1

(it is well-known that the series converges). We recall (cf.
[
5
, Ch. VII, §13]
) that the
Dirichlet density
of
A
is defined to be

\mathfrak{d}_{K}(A)=\lim_{s\to 1+0}\frac{\xi_{K,A}(s)}{\log((s-1)^{-1})}

if the limit in the right-hand side exists. We also define the
upper Dirichlet density
of
A
by

\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{K}(A)=\varlimsup_{s\to 1+0}\frac{\xi_{K,A}(s)}{\log((s-1)^{-1})}.

We note that
\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{K}(A)
is defined for all subsets
A\subset\mathcal{P}
, and is equal to the usual Dirichlet density

\mathfrak{d}_{K}(A)
if the latter exists. We also recall that given a finite Galois extension
L/K
, by Chebotarev’s Density Theorem,
the set
\mathrm{Spl}(L/K)
of primes of
K
that set completely in
L
has Dirichlet density

\mathfrak{d}_{K}(\mathrm{Spl}(L/K))=\frac{1}{[L:K]}.

Lemma 3.1
.

(1)

For any two subsets
A,B\subset\mathcal{P}
, we have
\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{K}(A\cup B)\leq\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{K}(A)+\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{K}(B)
.

(2)

If
A_{1},\ldots,A_{r}\subset\mathcal{P}
are subsets such that
\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{K}(A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{r})\geq\varepsilon>0
then there exists

i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
such that
\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{K}(A_{i})\geq\varepsilon/r
.

Proof.

(1): We obviously have

\xi_{A\cup B}(s)\leq\xi_{A}(s)+\xi_{B}(s)\ \ \text{for}\ \ s>1.

Dividing by
\log((s-1)^{-1})
and taking upper limits (using the sum rule for these), we obtain our claim.

(2): Indeed, if we assume that
\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{K}(A_{i})<\varepsilon/r
for all
i
, then applying part (1) repeatedly, we would obtain that
\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{K}(A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{r})<\varepsilon
,
a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose subsets
A,B\subset\mathcal{P}
have Dirichlet density and are contained in a subset
C\subset\mathcal{P}
that also has Dirichlet
density. Then

\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{K}(A\cap B)\geq\mathfrak{d}_{K}(A)+\mathfrak{d}_{K}(B)-\mathfrak{d}_{K}(C).

Proof.

We have

\xi_{A\cap B}(s)=\xi_{A}(s)+\xi_{B}(s)-\xi_{A\cup B}(s)\geq\xi_{A}(s)+\xi_{B}(s)-\xi_{C}(s).

Dividing by
\log((s-1)^{-1})
and taking upper limits as
s\to 1+0
we obtain our claim since the usual limit of the ratio
in the right-hand side exists and equals
\mathfrak{d}_{K}(A)+\mathfrak{d}_{K}(B)-\mathfrak{d}_{K}(C)
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{r}
be subsets of
\mathcal{P}
that have Dirichlet density, and assume that all sets are contained in a subset
C\subset\mathcal{P}
that also has
Dirichlet density and that the union
A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{r}

has Dirichlet density as well. If

\theta:=\mathfrak{d}_{K}(A_{0})+\mathfrak{d}_{K}(A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{r})-\mathfrak{d}_{K}(C),

then there exists
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
such that
\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{K}(A_{0}\cap A_{i})\geq\theta/r
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
, we have

\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{K}(A_{0}\cap(A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{r}))\geq\mathfrak{d}_{K}(A_{0})+\mathfrak{d}_{K}(A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{r})-\mathfrak{d}_{K}(C)=\theta.

Thus,
\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{K}((A_{0}\cap A_{1})\cup\cdots\cup(A_{0}\cap A_{r}))\geq\theta

Lemma 3.4

One of the key ingredients of the proof of Theorem

tractable

in its Galois group

is a set of primes having Dirichlet density zero. We also recall that

]
for tractable

used the work of C. Demarche
[

to extend the results from

which is an open subset of
\displaystyle G_{V^{\prime}}=\prod_{v\in V^{\prime}}G(K_{v})
. Let us now show that

(8)

\prod_{v\in V^{\prime}}N_{v}\subset N\cdot W_{k}.

Indeed, since each
U_{ijk}
is solid, we can find
s_{ijk}\in N_{v_{i}^{(j)}}\cap U_{ijk}
and then
set

\underline{s}_{k}=(s_{ijk})_{i,j}\in\prod_{v\in V^{\prime}}N_{v}\ \ \text{for}\ \ k=1,\ldots,r.

Then
W_{k}\underline{s}_{k}^{-1}
is a neighborhood of the identity in
G_{V^{\prime}}
, so the density of (diagonally embedded)
N
in
\displaystyle\prod_{v\in V^{\prime}}N_{v}
implies that

\prod_{v\in V^{\prime}}N_{v}\subset N\cdot(W_{k}\underline{s}_{k}^{-1}),

and (
8
) follows.
Now, fix
z\in\Gamma
and show that

(9)

z^{n}\in Z(N,y)(\overline{N}\cap\Gamma).

Since
V^{\prime}\subset V^{K}\setminus(S\cup V)
, we have

z\in\prod_{v\in V^{\prime}}G(\mathcal{O}_{v})=\prod_{v\in V^{\prime}}N_{v},

so it follows from (
8
) that for each
k=1,\ldots,r
one can find
n_{k}\in N
so that

(10)

z_{k}:=zn_{k}\in W_{k}.

Clearly, to prove (
9
), it is enough to show that

(11)

z_{k}^{n}\in Z(N,y)(\overline{N}\cap\Gamma)\ \ \text{for at least one}\ \ k=1,\ldots,r.

The argument used to prove (
8
) also proves the following.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\mathscr{T}
be a maximal
K
-torus of
G
, and let
\mathscr{V}\subset V^{K}\setminus S
be a finite subset. For
v\in\mathscr{V}
, set
\Omega_{v}=\varphi_{v,\mathscr{T}}(N_{v}\times\mathscr{T}^{\small\mathrm{reg}}(K_{v}))
. Then for any
y\in\overline{N}\cap\Gamma
there exists

a\in N
such that

xa\in\prod_{v\in\mathscr{V}}\Omega_{v},

so that we have

ya=gbg^{-1}

for some
g=(g_{v})
and
b=(b_{v})
with
g_{v}\in N_{v}
and
b_{v}\in\mathscr{T}^{\small\mathrm{reg}}(K_{v})
for all
v\in\mathscr{V}
.

For each
k=1,\ldots,r
, we let
\mathscr{T}_{k}=Z_{G}(z_{k})^{\circ}
denote the
K
-torus of
G
containing
z_{k}
. Applying Lemma

6.2
to
\mathscr{T}_{k}
and
\mathscr{V}=V\cup V^{\prime}
, we find elements
a_{k}\in N
,
b_{k}\in\prod_{v\in\mathscr{V}}\mathscr{T}_{k}^{\small\mathrm{reg}}(K_{v})
and
g_{k}\in\prod_{v\in\mathscr{V}}N_{v}
such that

(12)

t_{k}:=ya_{k}=g_{k}b_{k}g_{k}^{-1}.

It follows from (
10
) and (
12
) that the
K
-torus
T_{k}=Z_{G}(t_{k})^{\circ}
is
G(K_{v_{i}^{(j)}})
-conjugate to the torus
T_{ijk}^{0}
for all
i=1,\ldots,c
and
j=1,\ldots,r
.
Let
P_{k}=K_{T_{k}}
be the splitting field of
T_{k}
. It follows from Proposition
5.2
that each
P_{k}
is an extension of
M
of degree
t=|W(G,T)|
and together
P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}
are linearly disjoint over
M
. Taking into account our choice of
r
and applying Proposition
3.5
, we obtain that there exists a
k\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
such that

\bar{\mathfrak{d}}_{K}(S\cap\mathrm{Spl}(P_{k}/K))\geq\omega/(2r)=\delta.

Since the function
\phi
in Theorem
4.1
is super-decreasing, we have that the index

(13)

[T_{k}(\mathbb{A}_{S}):\overline{T_{k}(K)}]\ \ \text{divides}\ \ n=\phi(\delta,d).

Obviously,
T_{k}(K)
centralizes
t_{k}
in
\widehat{G}
. Since the centralizer
Z_{\widehat{G}}(t_{k})
is a closed subgroup of
\widehat{G}
and the map
\pi
is proper, hence closed, we conclude from (
13
) that

(14)

\pi(Z_{\widehat{G}}(t_{k}))\supset\overline{T_{k}(K)}\supset T_{k}(\mathbb{A}_{S})^{n}.

For
v\notin S
, we define

f_{v}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}(g_{k})_{v}z_{k}^{n}(g_{k})_{v}^{-1}&\text{if}&v\in\mathscr{V},\\
1&\text{if}&v\notin\mathscr{V}.\end{array}\right.

It follows from (
12
) that the
S
-adele
f=(f_{v})
belongs to
T_{k}(\mathbb{A}_{S})^{n}
. So, according to (
14
) that there exists
h\in Z_{\widehat{G}}(t)
such that
\pi(h)=f
. In fact,
f\in\overline{\Gamma}
(because
z\in\Gamma
and
(g_{k})_{v}\in N_{v}
for all
v\in\mathscr{V}
), so
h\in\widehat{\Gamma}
, and therefore one can find
s\in\Gamma\cap h\widehat{N}
is nonempty (as
\Gamma
is dense in
\widehat{\Gamma}
and
h\widehat{N}
is open). Pick
b\in\Gamma\cap s\widehat{N}
. Then

[y,s]\in[t_{k},s]\widehat{N}=[t_{k},h]\widehat{N}=\widehat{N}

since
h\in Z_{\widehat{G}}(t_{k})
. Thus,
[y,s]\in\Gamma\cap\widehat{N}=N
, and therefore
s\in Z(N,y)
. On the other hand,

s\in\pi(h)\overline{N}=f\overline{N}=z_{k}^{n}\overline{N}.

Indeed, for
v\in\mathscr{V}
we have
f_{v}z_{k}^{-n}=[g_{v},z_{k}^{n}]\in N_{v}
since
g_{v}\in N_{v}
for
v\in\mathscr{V}
, and for

we have

f_{v}z_{k}^{-n}=z_{k}^{-n}\in G(\mathcal{O}_{v})=N_{v}
, hence
fz_{k}^{-n}\in\overline{N}
, as required. Thus,
z_{k}^{n}\in Z(N,y)(\overline{N}\cap\Gamma)
, proving (
11
) and completing the proof of Proposition
6.1
.
