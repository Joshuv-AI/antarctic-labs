Investigating the impact of irradiation on various ringFET-based SRAM circuits using 3D TCAD
Authors: R. Anandhi, K. K. Nagarajan, M. Ramya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Single-event upsets (SEUs) in Static Random Access Memory (SRAM) can corrupt stored instructions, data, or configuration bits, potentially leading to logic malfunction, system hang, or system reset. Therefore, the investigation of single-event effects is essential for the development of robust and SEU-tolerant memory architectures. In this work, the DC characteristics of junctionless, lightly doped drain (LDD), and Buried Oxide LDD (BOX-LDD) ringFETs are analysed using 3D Technology Computer Aided Design (TCAD) simulations. The BOX-LDD ringFET exhibits improved DC performance compared to both junctionless and conventional LDD ringFET architectures. The operation of the 6T SRAM cell based on these devices is validated, and its Read, Write, and Hold static noise margins (SNMs) are evaluated to assess memory stability for each architecture. Furthermore, SRAM cells based on these devices are examined under radiation exposure using both inner-drain and outer-drain arrangements. The results demonstrate that SRAM cells employing the inner-drain architecture achieve higher critical dose tolerance than those using the outer-drain configuration. In addition, BOX-LDD ringFET-based SRAMs exhibit significantly enhanced radiation robustness compared to SRAMs based on junctionless and conventional LDD ringFETs. Among all the investigated structures, the BOX-LDD ringFET SRAM with the inner-drain design exhibits the highest critical dose tolerance, highlighting its potential for reliable memory applications in radiation-intensive environments such as aerospace and space electronics.

Introduction

The rapid development of CMOS technology has mainly focused on device downsizing to meet increasing performance and power-efficiency demands. Despite decades of progressive miniaturization, short-channel effects (SCEs) have emerged as a significant constraint in conventional planar bulk CMOS technologies. To address these issues, researchers are exploring alternative planar and non-planar structures. Non-planar designs1–5 have showed superior gate control and increased resistance to SCEs, but as pointed out by Kuhn6, these new architectures pose major fabrication and technological issues. Their inherent non-planar nature is incompatible with the typical CMOS fabrication method, which increases the fabrication complexity and cost of production7.

To overcome these drawbacks, planar device alternatives have gained attention. Among them, the planar-circular gate structure described by De Lima and Gimenez8 is especially promising since it retains the benefits of planarity and reduces SCEs. The ringFET is a noteworthy device in this regard because its circular gate geometry makes it an excellent alternative to standard MOSFETs, reducing side-interface areas and trap-induced leakage. Williams et al.9 investigated the performance of inner and outer drain arrangements using 2D hydrodynamic simulations, while Kumar et al. developed an analytical framework using the 2D Poisson equation to predict drain currents in ringFET architectures10 and in gate and channel-engineered ringFETs11. Kumari et al.12 examined ringFET designs for high-frequency applications using TCAD simulations. In addition to performance-oriented studies, the effects of radiation on various ringFET structures have also been investigated13–15.

Apart from ringFET-based advancements, several planar device designs16–18 have been suggested as process-compatible alternatives for traditional MOSFETs. A notable example is the idea of Lilienfield’s Junctionless transistor in 192619 that was further enhanced by Colinge et al.20 who demonstrated that Junctionless FETs can bypass the highly steep doping profile needed to create a source/drain. Their RF behaviour was explored by Cho et al.21 and they were shown to be suitable for SRAM applications by Kranti et al.22. In parallel, significant progress has also been made in drain-engineered structures. Liu et al.23 and Liou et al.24 established the advantages of Lightly Doped Drain (LDD) architecture in reducing the electric field near the drain region and mitigating hot-carrier effects, which are major reliability concerns in aggressively scaled MOSFET technologies.

A major concern in integrated circuit reliability research is the phenomenon known as single-event effects (SEE), which occur when high-energy particles hit the silicon substrate, generating electron-hole pairs that alter the device’s electrical characteristics25. Radiation can cause hard errors, which are permanent and irreversible, and soft errors, which are temporary and recoverable. At the device level, the effects of soft errors are referred to as Single Event Transients (SETs). Soft errors in SRAM Circuits are known as Single Event Upsets (SEUs), where the stored data bit may change or flip due to radiation-induced charge collection26. SEU behaviour in multigate devices has been extensively studied27–30, and SRAMs based on junctionless ringFETs have been analysed earlier31. Although BOX-based MOSFET structures have been previously investigated for radiation tolerance, the impact of SEU on BOX-LDD ringFET-based SRAM architectures is yet to be explored in detail.

Therefore, the novelty of this work lies on enhancing the radiation robustness of ringFET based SRAMs comparable to conventional CMOS based SRAM using advanced 3D TCAD simulations. Furthermore, the influence of drain configuration on critical dose tolerance is investigated. The superior SEU resilience of inner drain BOX-LDD ringFET-based SRAMs is demonstrated. These findings provide valuable insights for the development of radiation-hardened, planar SRAM technologies suited for aerospace and other radiation-intensive applications.

The paper is organised in the following manner: Section 2 presents the device structure and the DC performance characteristics of the Junctionless ringFET, LDD ringFET and the BOX-LDD ringFET structures. Section 3 discusses the SET analysis on these ringFET structures. Section 4 describes the operation of the 6 T SRAM cell and evaluates the Read, Write, and Hold SNMs. Section 5 performs the SEU analysis of SRAM circuits implemented using Junctionless ringFETs, LDD ringFETs, and BOX-LDD ringFETs. Finally, Section 6 provides a conclusion.

Device structure and DC performance of ringFETs

All simulations carried out in this work are performed using the Sentaurus TCAD device simulator. A general framework for the simulation approach is provided as follows: (i) Structure creation using Sentaurus Structure Editor (SDE) based on the dimensions and doping definitions by defining the placement of contacts, and by generating a mesh. (ii) Sentaurus Device Simulator (SDEVICE) is used for device simulation by incorporating suitable physical models and by selecting the simulation type (DC or transient simulations). (iii) Finally, the current-voltage characteristics and the transient characteristics of semiconductor devices are plotted and assessed using the Inspect tool. The Sentaurus TCAD device simulator was used to effectively design and characterise different ringFET architectures.

Junctionless ringFET structure

A Junctionless Transistor differs fundamentally from conventional MOSFET structures because it eliminates the formation of traditional p–n junctions between the source, channel, and drain regions. The entire device is evenly doped, providing a continuous conduction path and improved electrostatic gate control, suppressing the short-channel effects. This architecture also alters the carrier transport mechanism, where the device operation is dominated by majority carriers rather than minority carriers, leading to better switching behaviour20. LDD ringFET structure

The LDD ringFET introduces lightly doped regions at the source and drain interfaces of the junction based conventional ringFET13, effectively reducing the electric field near these regions. This not only mitigates the hot-carrier effects that can degrade device reliability but also enhances the overall performance, particularly in high-frequency applications23. Using TCAD, an LDD ringFET structure was created by incorporating lightly doped regions into a conventional ringFET design on both the source and drain sides. Figure 2(a) portrays the n-type LDD ringFET device’s 3D schematic, whose dimensions are shown in Table 2, and Fig. 2(b) depicts the device from a 2D viewpoint. Similarly, Fig. 3 illustrates the 3D schematic of a p-type LDD ringFET.

Two different device layouts are analysed using both Junctionless and LDD ringFETs, such as the outer drain (OD) and inner drain (ID) designs. In the OD layout, the drain is on the outside, and the source is in the middle. In contrast, the ID layout reverses this arrangement, placing the drain at the centre and the source on the outside region. The ID layout is found to be more resistant to Drain-Induced Barrier Lowering (DIBL). In this arrangement, the drain fields diverge from the smaller inner drain contact, enabling better electrostatic control of the source barrier by the source, gate, and body potentials. This suppresses short-channel effects, and results in reduced DIBL. Conversely, the OD arrangement exhibits comparatively poorer short-channel performance due to stronger drain field penetration from the larger outer drain region, resulting in higher OFF-state current despite its improved ON-state current32,33. Therefore, the ID topology provides better electrostatic integrity and switching performance, particularly in scaled devices.

BOX-LDD ringFET

To further improve the electrostatic integrity and leakage characteristics of the LDD ringFET, a 5 nm buried oxide layer was introduced beneath the source, channel, and drain regions. The addition of ultra-thin BOX layer increases electrostatic isolation and minimizes junction leakage current, which leads to improved device performance35. Furthermore, the inherent radiation endurance of BOX-based structures makes them suited for applications in radiation-prone situations such as space electronics and high energy physics systems36. Single Event Upset analysis on SRAM using Junctionless, LDD and BOX-LDD ringFET structures

Single Event Upsets (SEUs) occur when incident radiation disturbs the stored data in an SRAM cell, causing a change in its logic state. The minimum radiation energy required to flip the stored content of the cell is referred to as the critical radiation dosage. SEU analysis is essential for evaluating the radiation tolerance of SRAM circuits intended for space and high-energy physics applications. In this work, heavy-ion irradiation was applied at the susceptible node of the SRAM cell storing logic “1”, while the word line (WL) was maintained at zero volts during the non-access period. If the injected radiation charge is insufficient, the storage nodes experience only temporary disturbances and eventually recover their original logic states without causing a bit flip. However, when the injected charge exceeds a critical value, the stored data flips, resulting in an SEU event. The critical dosage required to achieve a cell flip is determined using a trial-and-error approach40,41.

When node O1 stores logic “0” and node O2 stores logic “1”, the nmos1 transistor connected to node O2 is in the OFF state and becomes the most sensitive node to radiation-induced charge collection in the 6 T SRAM cell. For the Junctionless ringFET with the OD arrangement, the most radiation susceptible region is the channel closer to the drain (CCD)14. In the LDD and BOX-LDD ringFETs with the OD arrangement, the most susceptible region is the LDD region closer to the drain (LCD)15. For the Junctionless, LDD and BOX-LDD ringFETs with the ID arrangement, the most susceptible region is the drain centre (DC)14,15, as determined from the device-level SET analysis discussed in Sect. 3. Accordingly, the radiation strike is applied directly at these identified sensitive regions of the nmos₁ transistor to evaluate the SEU behaviour of the 6 T SRAM cell.

The SEU analysis setup of the 6 T SRAM cell based on Junctionless ringFET devices is illustrated in Fig. 12(a), while the corresponding setup for the LDD ringFET and BOX-LDD ringFET based SRAM cells are presented using Fig. 12(b). In each case, the radiation strike is intentionally introduced at the previously identified sensitive region of the nmos₁ transistor to observe disturbances in the stored data and determine the critical radiation dosage for each device architecture.

To further understand the underlying physical mechanisms responsible for this enhanced radiation tolerance, the SRH recombination characteristics of the LDD and BOX-LDD ringFET-based 6 T SRAM cells employing the ID arrangement during a radiation strike are illustrated in Figs. 14(a) and 14(b), respectively. In both cases, the highest SRH recombination occurs at the nmos₁ transistor, where the ion strike is applied, due to the generation of a large number of radiation-induced electron–hole pairs in the sensitive region. These excess carriers lead to localized charge accumulation and enhanced trap-assisted recombination activity in nearby regions of the struck node. However, a comparison of Figs. 14(a) and 14(b) show considerable changes in carrier recombination behaviour of the two topologies. Although the radiation strike induces significant carrier generation at the nmos₁ transistor in both structures, the BOX-LDD ringFET exhibits a higher peak SRH recombination magnitude than the conventional LDD ringFET. This indicates that the radiation-generated carriers recombine in a shorter time and are more restricted inside the struck region, therefore limiting the diffusion of the carriers to neighboring nodes of the circuit. The improved radiation hardness of the BOX-LDD SRAM structure is mainly due to the BOX layer which isolates the active region electrically and suppresses the lateral diffusion of carriers and improves the immunity of the device to radiation-induced transient effects43.

Similarly, to further analyse the carrier transport behaviour during the radiation event, the electron density distribution of the BOX-LDD ringFET-based 6 T SRAM cell employing the ID arrangement is illustrated in Fig. 15. It can be observed that the nmos₁ transistor exhibits significantly higher electron density when directly exposed to the ion strike due to the generation of a dense track of radiation-induced electron–hole pairs within the sensitive region of the device. The generated carriers remain highly localized near the struck nmos₁ transistor, while the neighbouring devices exhibit comparatively lower carrier concentration since they are not directly affected by the ion strike. The reduced spreading of carriers to adjacent devices confirms the effectiveness of the BOX layer in suppressing charge-sharing effects within the SRAM cell44. Consequently, radiation-induced charge collection through displacement currents is significantly reduced, thereby improving the SEU immunity of the BOX-based SRAM architecture.

Conclusion

The functionality and stability characteristics of 6 T SRAM cells based on Junctionless, LDD and BOX-LDD ringFET architectures employing both inner and outer-drain arrangements were systematically investigated. Static Noise Margin (SNM) analysis was performed for Hold, Read and Write operating circumstances for all SRAM architectures. In addition, the soft error performance of the proposed SRAM architectures under radiation exposure was investigated through Single Event Upset analysis. The findings show that the critical radiation dosage, which is the least amount of radiation energy needed to change the SRAM cell’s stored content, differs greatly between various topologies and structures. Among the investigated structures, the ID arrangement consistently exhibits superior radiation tolerance compared to the OD arrangement. The critical dose of the LDD ringFET-based 6 T SRAM is approximately 2 times and 1.97 times greater than that of the Junctionless ringFET-based SRAM in the OD and ID configurations, respectively. Further, the integration of the BOX layer significantly enhances the radiation tolerance of the SRAM cell. The BOX-LDD ringFET-based 6 T SRAM achieves the critical dosage values of around 5.65 times and 5.81 times greater than the LDD ringFET-based SRAM for the OD and ID arrangements, respectively. Notably, the BOX-LDD ringFET SRAM employing the ID configuration achieves a critical dosage of 27.6 MeV/(mg/cm²), which is comparable to that of conventional CMOS SRAM. The SRH recombination, electron density, and electrostatic potential distributions reveal that the presence of a buried oxide layer confines radiation-induced carriers, thereby enhancing SEU tolerance. Although the present work is limited to single-bit SRAM analysis using detailed 3D TCAD mixed-mode simulations, the study provides important insight into the intrinsic stability and radiation tolerance of advanced ringFET-based SRAM architectures. Future work will focus on process variability analysis and multiple-cell upset modelling using compact-model and circuit-level simulation frameworks. Overall, the proposed BOX-LDD ringFET SRAM with the ID configuration demonstrates strong potential for radiation-hardened memory applications in space, satellite, and nuclear systems.
