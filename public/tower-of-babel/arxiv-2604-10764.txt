Category 𝒪 \mathcal{O} for polynomial toroidal algebras and its subalgebras
Priyanshu Chakraborty

Abstract
. In this paper we study Category 𝒪 \mathcal{O} for the polynomial toroidal Lie algebras and its S , H S,H type subalgebras. We classify irreducible objects of category 𝒪 \mathcal{O} as unique irreducble quotient of standard modules. Surprisingly, costandard objects of category 𝒪 \mathcal{O} arrises from Shen-Larsson type modules. We determine necessary sufficient conditions for irreducibility of Shen-Larsson modules. Finally appeling structure of Shen-Larsson modules and Soergel Tilting module theory of [ 31 ] , we compute charcter formulas for irreducible modules and indecomposable Tilting modules of category 𝒪 \mathcal{O} .

1.
Introduction

For a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
an important class of modules for
\mathfrak{g}
was introduced by Bernstein-Gelfand-Gelfand in the 1970s, known as modules of BGG category
\mathcal{O}
. This category contains many intersting modules, including all finite dimensional simple modules. Modules of this category satisfies certain finiteness conditions, and this
category has many intersting homological properties. One of the main results in the theory of
category
\mathcal{O}
was the proof of the Kazhdan–Luzstig conjecture, which gives the composition
multiplicities of the simple modules occuring in Verma modules in terms of values of certain
polynomials, called the Kazhdan–Luzstig polynomials. Later, category
\mathcal{O}

was defined more general class of Lie algebras, namely those with a triangular
decomposition, see
[
24
]
. Broadly, category
\mathcal{O}
is an interesting topic to consider for any Lie algebra and was studied by several authors for various Lie algebras, for instance see
[
2
,
3
,
19
,
4
,
8
]
. In this paper we consider category
\mathcal{O}
for polynomial toroidal Lie algebras.

Let
A_{n}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}]
be denote the polynomial algebra in n commuting variables. Let
W_{n}
be denote the Lie algebra of vector fileds on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. It is well known that
W_{n}
can be viewed as the Lie algebra of derivations over
A_{n}
and this Lie algebra is called as Witt algebra. The Lie algebra
W_{n}
posses three important class of subalgebras, namely subalgebra consisting of divergence zero vector fileds (
S_{n}
), subalgebra consisting of Hamiltonian type vector fields (
H_{n}
) and the subalgebra consisting of contact type vector fields (
K_{n}
). Represenations of
W,S,H
type Lie algebras have been well studied in literature, for instance see
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
8
]
.

Let
A_{n}^{\pm}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{\pm},\cdots,t_{n}^{\pm}]
and
\rm{Der}(A_{n}^{\pm})
be the derivation algebra over
A_{n}^{\pm}
. Representation for the Lie algebra
\rm{Der}(A_{n}^{\pm})
and its
S,H
type subalgebras have been well studied in literature, for instance see
[
1
,
17
,
12
,
22
,
13
,
16
,
14
]
and references therein. It has been found that there is a very close connections between modules of
W_{n}
and
\rm{Der}(A_{n}^{\pm})
, but its still unknown how they are actually related.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite dimensional simple Lie algebra. It is well known that full toroidal Lie algebras are obtained by considering semi direct product of universal central extension of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes A_{n}^{\pm}
and
\rm{Der}(A_{n}^{\pm})
. Further
S,H
type subalgebras of full toroidal Lie algebras can be described by taking
S,H
type subalgebras of
\rm{Der}(A_{n}^{\pm})
insteed of
\rm{Der}(A_{n}^{\pm})
. For more details on full toroidal Lie algebras and its
S,H
type subalgebras see the references
[
6
,
7
,
10
,
15
,
21
,
13
,
20
,

]
. It has been found that
S,H
type subalgebras of full toroidal Lie algebras forms extended affine Lie algebras, see the references
[
23
,
20
,
13
]
and references therein. But there are no such studies of representations for
the Lie algebras analogus to the full toroidal Lie algebras when one consider
A_{n}
insteed to
A_{n}^{\pm}
. In this paper we concentrate on that problem.

Let us consider the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\otimes A_{n}
and its trivial central extension
\mathcal{Z}
whose basis consists of vectors
\{t^{r}K_{i}:r\in\mathbb{N}^{n},1\leq i\leq n\}
. Then there is a natural action of
W_{n}
on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes A_{n}\oplus\mathcal{Z}
, see Section 2. Let
S_{n},H_{n}
be denote the subalgebras of
W_{n}
as described above, for
H_{n}
we consider
n=2m
. Choose
X_{n}
to be a Lie algebra of the set
\{W_{n},S_{n},H_{n}\}
, then
X_{n}
is a Lie subalgebra of
W_{n}
and
X_{n}
is
\mathbb{Z}
garded. Hence the action of
W_{n}
induces an action of
X_{n}
on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes A_{n}\oplus\mathcal{Z}

\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})=(\mathfrak{g}\otimes A_{n}\oplus\mathcal{Z})\rtimes X_{n}
. It is a
\mathbb{Z}
graded Lie algebra induced by the gradation of the polynomial Lie algebra. We study Category
\mathcal{O}
for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})
.

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we give the construction of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})
. We observe that
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})
is
\mathbb{Z}

\mathfrak{g}
,

\lambda,\mu

and

\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})
and prove that excepctional modules are reducible.

In section 4, we consider a category of modules following
[

called category of

modules and use the structure of modules for this category to prove that Shen-Larsson modules are irreducible except for excecpctional modules, Theorem 4.8. In section 5, we compute the character formulas for irreducible modules of category
\mathcal{O}
using the stuructre costandard modules, Theorem 5.2. In subsection 5.2, we define Tilting modules for category

by the use of Soergel’s construction,
[

. Finally we determine the character formulas for indecomposable tilting modules, Theorem 5.11.

2.
Polynomial Toroidal Lie algebras and its
S,H
type subalgebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite dimensional simple Lie algebra and
A_{n}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}]
be the polynomial algebra over
n
commuting variables for
n\geq 2
. Then
A_{n}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
graded commutative algebra with gradation induces from the degree of the polynomial. For
g=t^{r}
, define
{\rm deg}(g)=r_{1}+\cdots+r_{n}
. Let
W_{n}
be the Lie algebra of derivations over
A_{n}
. It is well known that
W_{n}
has a basis consisting of vectors
\{t^{r}d_{i}:r\in\mathbb{N}^{n},\,1\leq i\leq n\}
, where
d_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Clearly,
\mathbb{Z}
gardation of
A_{n}
induces a
\mathbb{Z}
gradation on
W_{n}
, i.e
W_{n}=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}{(W_{n})_{i}}
, where
{(W_{n})_{i}}={\rm span}\{gd_{j}:{\rm deg}(g)=i+1,g\in A_{n},1\leq j\leq n\}.

Let
S_{n}
be the special type subalgebra of
W_{n}
consisting of vector fields
\{\displaystyle{\sum_{i}}g_{i}d_{i}\in W_{n}:\displaystyle{\sum_{i}}d_{i}(g_{i})=0\}.
Then it can be seen that a spanning set for
S_{n}
is given by

S_{n}={\rm span}\{d_{ij}(t^{r}):r\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\setminus\{0\},1\leq i<j\leq n\},

where
d_{ij}(t^{r})=d_{j}(t^{r})d_{i}-d_{i}(t^{r})d_{j}
.

Let
H_{n}
be the Hamiltonian type subalgebra of
W_{n}
consisting of vector fileds which are annhilated by the 2-forms,
\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{m}}dt_{i}\wedge dt_{m+i}
, for
n=2m
. A spanning set for
H_{n}
is given by

H_{n}={\rm span}\{d_{H}(t^{r}):r\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\},

where

d_{H}(t^{r})=\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{m}}d_{j}(t^{r})d_{m+j}-\displaystyle{\sum_{j=m+1}^{2m}}d_{j}(t^{r})d_{j-m}.
Through out the paper we fix
n=2m
for the Lie algebra
H_{n}
.

Let
X_{n}
be denote a Lie algebra of the set
\{W_{n},S_{n},H_{n}\}
. Then
X_{n}
is a subalgebra of
W_{n}
and
\mathbb{Z}
graded, induced from the gradation of
W_{n}
. More precisely,
(X_{n})_{i}=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}{(W_{n})_{i}}\cap X_{n}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. In particular, we get

(X_{n})_{0}\cong\begin{cases}\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\,\,X_{n}=W_{n},\\
\mathfrak{sl}_{n},\,\,X_{n}=S_{n},\\
\mathfrak{sp}_{n},\,\,X_{n}=H_{n}.\end{cases}

A clear descripction of
(X_{n})_{0}
given by the following. The triangular decomposition of
(X_{n})_{0}
is given by
(X_{n})_{0}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{X}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{X}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{X}
where,

\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{X}=\begin{cases}\operatorname{span}\{t_{i}d_{j}\mid 1\leq j<i\leq n\},\text{if }X_{n}=W_{n},S_{n},\\
\operatorname{span}\{t_{i}d_{j}-t_{j+m}d_{i+m},\,t_{s+m}d_{t}+t_{k+m}d_{s}\mid 1\leq j<i\leq m,\,1\leq s\leq k\leq m\},\text{if }X_{n}=H_{n}.\end{cases}

\mathfrak{h}_{X}=\begin{cases}\operatorname{span}\{t_{i}\partial_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\},\text{if }X_{n}=W_{n},\\
\operatorname{span}\{t_{i}\partial_{i}-t_{j}\partial_{j}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n\},\text{if }X_{n}=S_{n},\\
\operatorname{span}\{t_{i}d_{i}-t_{i+m}d_{i+m}\mid 1\leq i\leq m\},\text{if }X_{n}=H_{n}.\end{cases}

\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{X}=\begin{cases}\operatorname{span}\{t_{i}d_{j}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n\},\text{if }X_{n}=W_{n},S_{n},\\
\operatorname{span}\{t_{i}d_{j}-t_{j+m}d_{i+m},\,t_{s+m}d_{t}+t_{k+m}d_{s}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq m,\,1\leq k\leq s\leq m\},\text{if }X_{n}=H_{n}.\end{cases}

Now it is easy to see that the isomorphism of
(X_{n})_{0}
is obtained by sending
t_{i}d_{j}\to E_{ij}
, for
1\leq i,j\leq n
.

Now we are ready to define the polynomial toroidal Lie algebra and its
S,H
type subalgebras.
Let us consider the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\otimes A_{n}
and its trivial central extension
\mathcal{Z}={\rm span}\{t^{r}K_{i}:r\in\mathbb{N}^{n},1\leq i\leq n\}
.
Now consider the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},W_{n})=(\mathfrak{g}\otimes A_{n}\oplus\mathcal{Z})\rtimes W_{n}
, bracket operations on
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},W_{n})
is given by

[x\otimes t^{r},y\otimes t^{s}]=[x,y]\otimes t^{r+s},

[t^{r}K_{i},x\otimes t^{s}]=[t^{r}K_{i},t^{s}K_{j}]=0,

[t^{r}d_{i},t^{s}K_{j}]=s_{i}t^{r+s-e_{i}}K_{j},

[t^{r}d_{i},x\otimes t^{s}]=s_{i}x\otimes t^{r+s-e_{i}},

[t^{r}d_{i},t^{s}d_{j}]=s_{i}t^{r+s-e_{i}}-r_{j}t^{r+s-e_{j}},

for all
r,s\in\mathbb{N}^{n},x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\,1\leq i,j\leq n.
It can be easily seen that degree gradation of
A_{n}
induces a gradation on
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},W_{n})
, which makes it a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie algebra, i.e
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},W_{n})=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}{\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},W_{n})_{i}}
, where
{\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},W_{n})_{i}}={\rm span}\{\mathfrak{g}\otimes f,\,fK_{j},\,\,gd_{j}:deg(f)=i,\,deg(g)=i+1,f,g\in A_{n},1\leq j\leq n\}.
We call
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},W_{n})
as polynomial toroidal Lie algebra.

Note that
X_{n}
is a subalgebra of
W_{n}
, hence we have an induced action of
X_{n}
on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes A_{n}\oplus\mathcal{Z}
. We denote the induced Lie algebra as

\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})=(\mathfrak{g}\otimes A_{n}\oplus\mathcal{Z})\rtimes X_{n}.

It is clear that
\mathbb{Z}
-gradation of
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})
is given by
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}{\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},W_{n})_{i}}\cap\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})
.

Now, we have the following description for graded components of
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})
.

{\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}}\cong{\rm span}\{d_{1},\cdots,d_{n}\}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n},

{\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{0}}\cong\mathfrak{g}\oplus(X_{n})_{0}\oplus{\rm span}\{K_{1},\cdots,K_{n}\},\,\,\text{and}

{\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{i}}=0,\,\,\forall\,i<-1.

It is clear that
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{0}
is a reductive Lie algebra. Further one can see that center of
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})={\rm span}\{K_{1},\cdots,K_{n}\}
. We denote the center by
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n}))
.
Let
\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{X}
, be the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{0}
, where
\mathfrak{h}
is the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{X}
is that of
(X_{n})_{0}
. Note that
\mathbb{Z}
gradation of
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})
induces a
\mathbb{Z}
gradation on
U(\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n}))
, i.e
U(\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n}))=\displaystyle{\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}}U(\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n}))_{i}
and
{\rm dim}U(\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n}))_{i}<\infty
.

3.
Category
\mathcal{O}

3.1.
Category
\mathcal{O}
for
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})

The following notion of category
\mathcal{O}
is an analogy of category for semi-simple Lie algebras,
[
18
]
. An interested reader can see
[
8
]
for the definition of Category
\mathcal{O}
for
X_{n}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Denote by
\mathcal{O}
the BGG category of
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})
-modules, whose objects
M
are additive groups and satisfy the following properties.

(1)

M
is an admissible
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})
-module, i.e.,
M=\bigoplus\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{i}
with
\dim M_{i}<+\infty
, and

\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{i}M_{j}\subseteq M_{i+j},\forall\,i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

M
is locally finite for
{\rm P}_{X}=\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{0}
.

(3)

M
is
\mathfrak{H}
-semisimple, i.e.,
M
is a weight module:
M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{H}^{*}}M_{\lambda}
.

The morphisms in
\mathcal{O}
are
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})
-module morphisms that respect the
\mathbb{Z}
-gradation i.e.,

{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(M,N)=\{f\in{\rm Hom}_{U(\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n}))}(M,N)\mid f(M_{i})\subseteq N_{i},\,\\
\,\forall\,i\in\mathbb{Z}\},\,\forall\,M,N\in\mathcal{O}.

Additionally, we say an object
M\in\mathcal{O}
has depth
d
if
M=\bigoplus\limits_{i\geq d}M_{i}
with
M_{d}\neq 0
and
M_{i}=0
, for all
i<d
. The following Lemma follows from the fact that
(X_{n})_{0}
action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is irreducible, which is a standard fact.

Lemma 3.2
.

(X_{n})_{0}
action on
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}
is irreducible.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional irreducible module for
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{0}
. Then
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}
acts trivially on
V
.

Proof.

Let
W=\{v\in V:\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}.v=0\}
. At first we show that
W
is a
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{0}
submodule of
V
.
Note that for all
x\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}
and
y\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{0}
we have,
x.(y.v)=y.x.v+[x,y].v=[x,y].v=0,
since
[x,y]\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}
. This proves that
W
is a
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{0}
submodule.

Hence to complete the proof, it is sufficient to show that
W\neq 0
. Since
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}
is an abelian subalgebra of
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{0}
, hence there exists a non-zero
v\in V
such that
x.v=\lambda(x)v
for all
x\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}
. Now
V
is irreducible, hence we must have
V=U(\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{0})v
. Therefore there exists
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}\in U(\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{0})
such that
\mathcal{B}=\{v,Y_{1}.v,\dots,Y_{n}.v\}
forms a basis for
V
. Now we prove that for any
x\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}
the matrix of
x
with respect to
\mathcal{B}
forms a upper triangular matrix with all diagonal entry
\lambda(x)
. This will follow from the following observation.

x.(Y_{i}.v)=\lambda(x)Y_{i}.v+[x,Y_{i}].v=\lambda([x,Y_{i}])v+\lambda(x)Y_{i}.v,\,\,\text{since}\,[x,Y_{i}]\,\in U(\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g})_{-1}),\,\forall\,i.

By Lemma
3.2
,
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}
is an irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{0}
. Hence for non-zero
x,y\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1}
there exists a
Y\in U(\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{0})
such that
[Y,y]=x
. Now considering trace on both side we conclude that trace of the matrix of
x
is zero, i.e
\lambda(x)=0
. This proves that
W\neq 0
.

∎

\Lambda^{+}_{\mathfrak{g},X,\mathbb{C}^{n}}=\Lambda^{+}_{\mathfrak{g},X}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}.

V(\lambda)\otimes L^{0}(\mu)
for some irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
module
V(\lambda)

(X_{n})_{0}
module
L^{0}(\mu)

3.2.2.

Set

,

4.1.
Category of

modules):

Definition 4.1
.

Denote by
(A_{n},\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},W_{n}))
-mod the category whose objects are additive groups
M
endowed with a
A_{n}
-module structure
(.)_{A_{n}}
, a
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})
-module structure
\rho_{X}
and a
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{\geq 0}
-module structure
\sigma_{X}
so that the following properties are satisfied:

(I)

(i)
[\rho_{X}(Y),f_{A_{n}}]=(Y(f))_{A_{n}}
,
\forall,f\in A_{n}
and

(ii)
[\rho_{X}(x),f_{A_{n}}]=0
,
\forall\,x\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes A_{n}\oplus\mathcal{Z},f\in A_{n}.

(II)

[\sigma_{X}(D^{\prime}),f_{A_{n}}]=0
,
\forall\,D^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{\geq 0},f\in A_{n}.

(III)

[\rho_{X}(d_{i}),\sigma_{X}(D^{\prime})]=0
,
\forall\,D^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{\geq 0}
and
1\leq i\leq n
.

(IV)

(i)

\begin{cases}\rho_{W}(fd_{j})=f_{A_{n}}\circ\rho_{W}(d_{j})+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}f)_{A_{n}}\circ\sigma_{W}(t_{i}d_{j}),\forall\,1\leq i\leq n,f\in A_{n}.\\
\rho_{S}(d_{ij}(f))=(d_{j}f)_{A_{n}}\circ\rho_{S}(d_{i})-(d_{i}f)_{A_{n}}\circ\rho_{S}(d_{j})+\displaystyle{\sum_{|\alpha|=2}}(d^{\alpha}f)_{A_{n}}\circ\sigma_{S}(d_{ij}(t^{\alpha})),\cr\,\hskip 270.30118pt\forall\,\leq i<j\leq n,f\in A_{n}.\\
\rho_{H}(d_{H}(f))=\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{m}}(d_{j}f)_{A_{n}}\circ\rho_{H}(d_{m+j})-\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{m}}(d_{j}f)_{A_{n}}\circ\rho_{H}(d_{j-m})\cr\hskip 71.13188pt+\displaystyle{\sum_{|\alpha|=2}}(d^{\alpha}f)_{A_{n}}\circ\sigma_{H}(d_{H}(t^{\alpha}))\end{cases}

(ii)
\rho_{X}(x\otimes f)=\sigma_{X}(x)\circ f_{A_{n}},

\forall\,x\in\mathfrak{g},f\in A_{n}.

(iii)
\rho_{X}(fK_{j})=\sigma_{X}(K_{j})\circ f_{A_{n}},

\forall\,1\leq j\leq n,\,f\in A_{n}.

We call this category as
(A_{n},\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n}))

(A_{n},\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g}))
-mod, whose objects are usually called

-modules. For notational convenience some times we use

and

and

Lemma 4.5

Let

-module such that

and

-submodule.

Proof.

We need to show that

is a
\sigma_{X}(\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n}))
-submodule of
M
. Note that by the assumption we only need to show
M^{\prime}
is invariant under
\sigma_{X}|_{(\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{0}}
. We prove this case by case.

Case I:
Let
X_{n}=W_{n}
. Choose
\gamma=e_{u}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, then there exists
f_{1},\cdots,f_{k}
and
g_{1},\cdots g_{k}
such that Proposition
4.3
holds for

. Now we have,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\sum_{r=1}^{k}}f_{r}\rho_{W}(g_{r}d_{j})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sum_{r=1}^{k}}f_{r}(g_{r}\circ\rho_{W}(d_{j})+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}g_{r})\circ\sigma_{W}(t_{i}d_{j}))

(4.3)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{W}(t_{u}d_{j}),

since
\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{k}}f_{i}d^{\alpha}g_{i}=0
for all
\alpha\neq e_{u}
.

Case II:
Let

Choose,
\gamma=e_{i}+e_{j}
, which satisfy the property of Proposition
4.3
. Now we have,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\sum_{r=1}^{k}}f_{r}\rho_{S}(d_{ij}(g_{r}))

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sum_{r=1}^{k}}f_{r}((d_{j}g_{r})_{A_{n}}\circ\rho_{S}(d_{i})-(d_{i}g_{r})_{A_{n}}\circ\rho_{S}(d_{j})+\displaystyle{\sum_{|\alpha|=2}}(d^{\alpha}g_{r})_{A_{n}}\circ\sigma_{S}(d_{ij}(t^{\alpha})))

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sigma_{S}(t_{i}d_{i}-t_{j}d_{j}),\,\,\,\text{if }\gamma=e_{i}+e_{j},\,1\leq i<j\leq n.\\
\sigma_{S}(t_{k}d_{i}),\,\,\,\text{if }\gamma=e_{j}+e_{k},\,k\neq i,i<j,1\leq i,j,k\leq n.\end{cases}

Case III:
Let

Choose,
\gamma=e_{i}+e_{j}
, which satisfy the property of Proposition
4.3
. Now we have,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\sum_{r=1}^{k}}f_{r}\rho_{H}(d_{H}(g_{r}))

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sum_{r=1}^{k}}f_{r}(\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{m}}(d_{j}g_{r})_{A_{n}}\circ\rho_{H}(d_{m+j})-\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{m}}(d_{j}g_{r})_{A_{n}}\circ\rho_{H}(d_{j-m})

(4.8)

\displaystyle+\displaystyle{\sum_{|\alpha|=2}}(d^{\alpha}g_{r})_{A_{n}}\circ\sigma_{H}(d_{H}(t^{\alpha})))\,\,(\text{By Proposition \ref{prop of f , g exists}})

(4.9)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sum_{r=1}^{k}}\displaystyle{\sum_{|\alpha|=2}}f_{r}(d^{\alpha}g_{r})_{A_{n}}\circ\sigma_{H}(d_{H}(t^{\alpha})))

(4.10)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2\sigma_{H}(t_{i}d_{m+i}),\,\,\,\text{if }\gamma=e_{i}+e_{i},\,1\leq i\leq m.\\
-2\sigma_{H}(t_{i}d_{i-m}),\,\,\,\text{if }\gamma=e_{i}+e_{i},\,m+1\leq i\leq 2m.\\
\sigma_{H}(t_{j}d_{m+i}-t_{i}d_{j-m}),\,\,\,\text{if }\gamma=e_{j}+e_{i},\,1\leq i\leq m;\,\\
\hskip 142.26378ptm+1\leq j\leq 2m.\\

From the equations (

Theorem 4.8

is annihilated by

We will follow arguments of
[

-submodule

-submodule. Let

-submodule containing

-submodules and hence

. Therefore we can construct a
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})
-submodule

. Then our proof for pass from

to the

5.1.

Recall that the triangular decomposition of

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathfrak{H}}^{*}}c_{\lambda}e^{\lambda}

Consequently
\mathcal{B}
becomes a commutative associative algebra if we define

with
e^{0}
as identity element. It is well known that formal exponents

are linearly independent and in one to one correspondence with
\mathfrak{H}^{*}
. Let
W

\mathfrak{H}

Lemma 5.1

(1)

Let

Let

Set

5.2.

In this section, we determine the character formulas for tilting modules in

5.2.1.
Semi-infinite character

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{L}=\displaystyle{\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}}\mathfrak{L}_{i}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
graded Lie algebra with dim
\mathfrak{L}_{i}<\infty
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}

\gamma:\mathfrak{L}_{0}\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be a semi-infinite character for
\mathfrak{g}
if it satisfies the following properties.

(SI-1)

As a Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{L}
is generated by
\mathfrak{L}_{1},\mathfrak{L}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{L}_{-1}
.

(SI-2)

\gamma([X,Y])=tr(\text{ad}X\circ\text{ad}Y\mid_{\mathfrak{L}_{0}})
, for all
X\in\mathfrak{L}_{1}
and
Y\in\mathfrak{L}_{-1}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

(1)

A linear map
\mathcal{E}_{W}
is a semi-infinite character on
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},W_{n})
if and only if
\mathcal{E}_{W}(t_{i}d_{j})=\delta_{ij}
,
\mathcal{E}_{W}(x)=0
, for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
x\in\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g}))
. Consequently,
\mathcal{E}_{W}|_{\mathfrak{h}_{W}}=\mu_{n}
.

(2)

A linear map
\mathcal{E}_{X}
is a semi-infinite character on
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})
if and only if
\mathcal{E}_{X}=0
for
X_{n}=S_{n},H_{n}
.

Proof.

Proof of both the statements goes parallelly and we write it for all
X_{n}
together. It follows from the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 2.1]
that
\mathcal{E}_{X}|_{({X}_{n})_{0}}
is a semi-infinite character for
X_{n}
. In particular, we get that
X_{n}
is generated by
(X_{n})_{-1},(X_{n})_{0}
and
(X_{n})_{1}
with the required property stated in the Lemma, i.e
\mathcal{E}_{W}(t_{i}d_{j})=\delta_{ij}
and
\mathcal{E}_{X}|_{(X_{n})_{0}}=0
for
X_{n}=S_{n},H_{n}
. Now note that
[(X_{n})_{0},\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g}))]=[\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1},\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n}))]=0
. From this observation it will follow that
\mathcal{E}_{X}(x)=0
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g}))
, by the help of (SI-2) property.

Now to complete the proof we need to show that
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})
is generated by
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{-1},\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{0}
and
\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{g},X_{n})_{1}

is generated by

and

[t^{r}d_{i},x\otimes t_{i}]=x\otimes t^{r}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,[t^{r}d_{i},t_{i}K_{j}]=t^{r}K_{j};\,\,

[d_{ij}(t^{r+e_{j}}),x\otimes t_{i}]=(r_{j}+1)x\otimes t^{r}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,[d_{ij}(t^{r+e_{j}}),t_{i}K_{j}]=(r_{j}+1)t^{r}K_{j};

[d_{H}(t^{r+e_{j}}),x\otimes t_{m+j}]=(r_{j}+1)x\otimes t^{r}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,[d_{H}(t^{r+e_{j}}),t_{m+j}K_{s}]=(r_{j}+1)t^{r}K_{s},

\forall r\in\mathbb{N}^{n},\,1\leq j,s\leq n,\,x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}),

(\lambda,\mu,\mathbf{c})\in\Lambda^{+}_{\mathfrak{g},W}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

If
(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime},\mathbf{c}^{\prime})=(0,-2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\epsilon_{n+1-i}-\sum\limits_{i=k+1}^{n}\epsilon_{n+1-i},\mathbf{0})
for some
k
with
0\leq k\leq n-1
. Then
[T_{W}(\lambda,\mu,\mathbf{c}):\Delta_{W}(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime},\mathbf{c}^{\prime})]\neq 0
if and only if
\lambda=0
,
\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{0}
and
\mu=\mu^{\prime}
or
\mu=\mu^{\prime}-\epsilon_{n-k}
. Moreover,
[T_{W}(0,\mu^{\prime},\mathbf{0}):\Delta_{W}(0,\mu^{\prime},\mathbf{0})]=[T_{W}(0,\mu^{\prime}-\epsilon_{n-k},\mathbf{0}):\Delta_{W}(0,\mu^{\prime},\mathbf{0})]=1
.

(2)

If
(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime},\mathbf{c}^{\prime})\neq(0,-2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\epsilon_{n+1-i}-\sum\limits_{i=k+1}^{n}\epsilon_{n+1-i},\mathbf{0})
for some
k
with
0\leq k\leq n-1
. Then
[T_{W}(\lambda,\mu,\mathbf{c}):\Delta_{W}(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime},\mathbf{c}^{\prime})]\neq 0
if and only if
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
,
\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
and
\mu=\mu^{\prime}
. Moreover,
[T_{W}(\lambda,\mu,\mathbf{c}):\Delta_{W}(\lambda,\mu,\mathbf{c})]=1
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.7
and Lemma
5.4
we have,

[T_{W}(\lambda,\mu,\mathbf{c}):\Delta_{W}(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime},\mathbf{c}^{\prime})]=[\mathcal{V}_{W}(-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}\lambda^{\prime},-\omega_{W}\mu^{\prime}-\mu_{n},-\mathbf{c^{\prime}}):L_{W}(-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}\lambda,-\omega_{W}\mu-\mu_{n},-\mathbf{c})].

Now we claim that when
\mathcal{V}_{W}(-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}\lambda^{\prime},-\omega_{W}\mu^{\prime}-\mu_{n},-\mathbf{c^{\prime}})
not simple, then
(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime},\mathbf{c}^{\prime})
satisfy the hypothesis of (1). Suppose
\mathcal{V}_{W}(-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}\lambda^{\prime},-\omega_{W}\mu^{\prime}-\mu_{n},-\mathbf{c^{\prime}})
not simple. Then by Proposition
3.13
, we have
\lambda^{\prime}=0,\mathbf{c}^{\prime}=\mathbf{0}
and
\mu^{\prime}
satisfy the relation
-\omega_{W}\mu^{\prime}-\mu_{n}=\mu_{k}=\epsilon_{1}\cdots+\epsilon_{k},
for some
0\leq k\leq n-1
. From this we have
\mu^{\prime}=-2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\epsilon_{k+1-i}-\sum\limits_{i=k+1}^{n}\epsilon_{n+1-i}
, this proves the claim.

Again from Proposition
3.13
, we have
\mathcal{V}_{W}(0,\mu_{k},\mathbf{0})
have two composition factors
L_{W}(0,\mu_{k},\mathbf{0})
and
L_{W}(0,\mu_{k+1},\mathbf{0})
. Hence we get
\lambda=0,\mathbf{c}=0
and
-\omega_{W}\mu-\mu_{n}=\mu_{k}
or
\mu_{k+1}
, i.e
\mu=\mu^{\prime}
or
\mu=\mu^{\prime}-\epsilon_{n-k}.
Now the result (1) follows from Proposition
3.13
.

From the proof of (1) it is clear that when
(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime},\mathbf{c}^{\prime})
satisfied the condition of (2), then
\mathcal{V}_{W}(-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}\lambda^{\prime},-\omega_{W}\mu^{\prime}-\mu_{n},-\mathbf{c^{\prime}})
is simple. Hence it has only one composition factor, therefore we have
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda,\mu^{\prime}=\mu,\mathbf{c}^{\prime}=\mathbf{c}
. This completes the proof of (2).
∎

5.10
we have the following proposition.
