1.
Introduction

Functional inequalities are fundamental tools in analysis and probability: they quantify the interplay between geometry of a reference measure, stabilization rates of stochastic dynamics, and concentration/deviation properties of observables. In the reversible setting, the spectral-gap (Poincaré) inequality provides a direct route from a coercivity estimate on the Dirichlet form to exponential relaxation to equilibrium for the associated Markov semigroup; see, e.g., the monograph
[
4
]
. Yet, many measures of current interest in statistics, sampling and machine learning (heavy tails, weak confinement, multimodality, nonconvex energies) fail to satisfy a standard Poincaré-quality inequality with finite constant. In such regimes, one expects sub-exponential, or even sub-geometric, rates of convergence, and one must rely on weaker functional inequalities such as weak Poincaré or weak logarithmic Sobolev inequalities
[
38
,
41
,
15
]
.

Perturbation viewpoint.

A recurring theme, both classical and modern, is the
stability under perturbation
:
starting from a reference probability measure
\nu
(for which some functional inequalities are available),
we consider a perturbed target

d\mu={e^{-U}}\,d\nu,

(1.1)

and ask which inequalities (Poincaré, log-Sobolev, weak and weighted variants) remain valid for
\mu
,
and how the corresponding constants or
rate functions
can be controlled in terms of
U
and properties of
\nu
.
For bounded
U
, classical perturbation techniques already yield robust results (e.g. Holley–Stroock type arguments for log-Sobolev inequalities
[
33
]
). For unbounded perturbations, the question is subtler: the effect of
U
at infinity must be compared to the concentration/tail regime of
\nu
, and Lyapunov-type arguments become essential
[
2
,
1
]
.
This paper follows this perspective in the weak and weighted setting, extending the perturbation program
developed for Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities in
[
23
]
, initially motivated by a Bayesian problem,
to weak Poincaré, weak log-Sobolev, weighted Poincaré and weighted log-Sobolev inequalities,
and discussing convolution and mixture operations motivated by generative modeling.

1.1.
Weak Poincaré inequalities and sub-exponential relaxation

Weak Poincaré inequalities (WPI) were introduced to capture convergence rates slower than exponential, by allowing a controlled defect term. A typical formulation is: there exists a non-increasing function
\beta_{\mu}:(0,\tfrac{1}{4}]\to(0,\infty)
such that for all sufficiently regular
f
and all
s\in(0,\tfrac{1}{4}]
,

\textrm{Var}_{\mu}(f)\;\leq\;\beta_{\mu}(s)\int|\nabla f|^{2}\,d\mu\;+\;s\,\textrm{Osc}(f)^{2},

(1.2)

where
\textrm{Osc}(f)=\sup f-\inf f
.
When
\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\beta_{\mu}(s)<\infty
, (
1.2
) essentially reduces to the classical Poincaré inequality; otherwise,
\beta_{\mu}(s)\to\infty
encodes the
degree of degeneracy
of the spectral gap and yields sub-geometric rates.

The seminal paper
[
41
]
establishes that, for a large class of Markov semigroups,
L^{2}
-convergence rates slower than exponential are essentially equivalent to a weak Poincaré inequality with an appropriate rate function
\beta_{\mu}
. Further refinements connect the shape of
\beta_{\mu}
to decay of semigroups and concentration properties
[
45
]
, and to geometric/functional criteria (capacity, isoperimetry, tail profiles) that allow explicit computations in heavy-tailed regimes
[
6
,
16
]
.
These tools are particularly powerful for distributions such as generalized Cauchy laws or Subbotin-type laws, where Poincaré fails but WPI holds with polynomial or logarithmic profiles, respectively
[
16
]
.

A key message, visible in the perturbation examples, is that meaningful perturbation results require the growth of
U
to be compatible with the
concentration scale
of the reference measure (see example
2.7
). Roughly speaking, if
\nu
exhibits only polynomial tails, then allowing
U
to grow faster than logarithmically can destroy any usable control on
\beta_{\mu}
; conversely, if the perturbation makes tails lighter in a comparable manner, one may obtain improved rates. This “match the tails” principle is a recurring leitmotiv in modern perturbation theory for weak inequalities.

1.2.
Weighted Poincaré inequalities and weighted generators

Weak inequalities are intimately connected to
weighted
inequalities, which replace the uniform Dirichlet form by a spatially varying one. Weighted Poincaré-type inequalities have a long history in the study of heavy-tailed measures: even when
C_{P}(\mu)=\infty
, one may have

\textrm{Var}_{\mu}(f)\;\leq\;C_{P,\omega}(\mu)\int|\nabla f|^{2}\omega^{2}\,d\mu

(1.3)

for an appropriate non-negative weight
\omega
. For Cauchy-type and more general convex measures, Bobkov and Ledoux
[
11
]
obtained sharp weighted Poincaré and isoperimetric-type inequalities, exhibiting canonical weights that reflect the geometry of tails. Such inequalities can be interpreted as standard Poincaré inequalities for a modified (non-uniform) carré du champ, and they naturally lead to dynamics whose diffusion strength depends on the position.

This motivates the study of diffusions with generator

L^{\omega}f=\omega^{2}\Delta f+(\nabla\omega^{2}-\omega^{2}\nabla V)\cdot\nabla f,

(1.4)

which can be written in divergence form as

L^{\omega}f=e^{V}\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\big(e^{-V}\omega^{2}\nabla f\big).

(1.5)

Under standard integrability and boundary conditions, the probability measure

d\mu(x)=Z^{-1}e^{-V(x)}\,dx

(1.6)

is invariant and the operator is symmetric in
L^{2}(\mu)
, with Dirichlet form

\mathcal{E}_{\omega}(f,f)=\int\omega^{2}|\nabla f|^{2}\,d\mu
.
Consequently, a weighted Poincaré inequality (
1.3
) implies exponential decay of the variance along the semigroup associated with
L^{\omega}
in the natural energy scale.
More generally, when only weak inequalities are available, one obtains sub-exponential relaxation rates governed by
\beta_{\mu}
, and one may sometimes
construct
explicit weights from
\beta_{\mu}
via capacitary criteria and tail rearrangements, yielding a converse weighted inequality that is optimal for the given WPI profile (see Corollary
4.4
).

From an algorithmic viewpoint,
\omega
plays the role of a
preconditioner
: it changes the local diffusivity, thereby reshaping exploration of the state space. This idea resonates with recent work on optimizing or learning preconditioning metrics for Langevin dynamics and sampling (in particular, selecting diffusion coefficients to accelerate mixing while preserving a target invariant measure)
[
27
,
37
]
.
In heavy-tailed settings, such preconditioning can be essential: the unweighted Langevin diffusion may converge extremely slowly (or may not have a spectral gap), while a properly weighted dynamics can exhibit substantially improved functional-inequality structure.

1.3.
Why weak/weighted inequalities matter for diffusion-based generative modeling

The recent success of diffusion/score-based generative models has put
families of intermediate distributions
and
annealed Langevin-type samplers
at the center of modern high-dimensional sampling. The classical diffusion-model pipeline defines a forward noising mechanism that progressively transforms a complex data distribution into a simple reference distribution (often Gaussian), then generates samples by simulating a reverse-time dynamics involving the
score
(the gradient of the log-density) of the intermediate marginals
[
42
,
32
,
43
]
.

A key theoretical and practical difficulty is that intermediate distributions need not be log-concave, and may inherit heavy tails or multi-modality from data. In this regime, the mixing properties of the Markov chains used inside samplers (e.g. predictor–corrector methods using Langevin
corrector
steps) are not well described by classical spectral-gap/log-Sobolev theory alone. Weak and weighted functional inequalities offer a natural alternative: they provide quantitative control on stabilization when spectral gaps fail, and they can be stable under perturbations that mirror the operations used in diffusion models (smoothing/noising, reweighting, convolution, and annealing).

This connection is made explicit in the recent line of work on
diffusion annealed Langevin dynamics
, where the target path of distributions is defined via Gaussian (or heavy-tailed) convolutions and sampling is performed through annealed Langevin steps; functional inequalities for intermediate targets are instrumental in proving regularity properties of scores (e.g. Lipschitz continuity) and deriving non-asymptotic sampling guarantees
[
26
]
.
In particular, perturbation estimates for Poincaré-type constants along interpolating paths provide a mechanism to control the stability of the score field and the discretization error, complementing the classical convex-logconcave sampling theory
[
28
]
.
Hence, understanding
how weak/weighted inequalities behave under perturbations and convolutions
is not only of independent analytic interest but also directly relevant to the theoretical underpinnings of modern generative pipelines.

1.4.
Outline and contributions

The present paper develops a perturbation theory for weak and weighted functional inequalities, organized around three complementary mechanisms:
(i) refined versions of Holley–Stroock-type perturbation arguments for weak Poincaré inequalities (Section
2
), weighted Poincaré inequalities (Section
3
), and weaker logarithmic Sobolev inequalities (Section
5
),
(ii) Lyapunov-based sufficient perturbation conditions yielding explicit rate functions and stability under unbounded perturbations, for all this weaker inequalities,
and (iii) capacitary criteria linking weak Poincaré inequalities to (converse) weighted Poincaré inequalities, including explicit constructions of weights from
\beta_{\mu}
, (Section
4
).
We also discuss convolution products and related operations, motivated by the intermediate laws appearing in diffusion-based generative modeling, and highlight how these results interface with weighted generators of type (
1.4
) and their long-time behavior. The generalized Cauchy distributions and Subbotin (exponential power) distributions will serve as guiding examples.

2.
Perturbation of Weak Poincaré inequalities.

The setting of the weak Poincaré inequality was introduced above, but to enhance readability, we restate the definition:
\mu
satisfies a weak Poincaré inequality if there exists a non-increasing function
\beta_{\mu}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that for all
s>0
and all smooth enough function
f
,

\textrm{Var}_{\mu}(f)\leq\beta_{\mu}(s)\int|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu+s\,\textrm{Osc}^{2}(f)\,.

(2.1)

Recall that
\textrm{Var}_{\mu}(f)\leq\textrm{Osc}^{2}(f)/4
so that the previous inequality is trivial for
s\geq 1/4
. If
X
is a random variable with distribution
\mu
we write
\beta_{X}=\beta_{\mu}
. We shall call this function the
rate
of the weak inequality.

Below we collect some properties of these weak inequalities.

Proposition 2.1
.

It holds

(1)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\beta_{x+Z}=\beta_{Z}
,

(2)

for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
\beta_{\lambda Z}=\lambda^{2}\,\beta_{Z}
,

(3)

if
Z_{1},...,Z_{n}
are independent,
\beta_{(Z_{1},...,Z_{n})}(s)\leq\max_{j}\beta_{Z_{j}}(s/n)
,

(4)

if
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
are independent,
\beta_{Z_{1}+Z_{2}}(s)\leq\beta_{Z_{1}}(s/2)+\beta_{Z_{2}}(s/2)
,

(5)

if
\mu_{n}
weakly converges to
\mu
,
\beta_{\mu}\leq\liminf\beta_{\mu_{n}}
.

The proof is provided in Appendix
A
.

Weak Poincaré inequalities are general enough to be satisfied by almost every probability measure, more precisely.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mu(dx)=e^{-V(x)}dx
be a probability measure such that
V
is locally bounded (for instance continuous). Then
\mu
satisfies a weak Poincaré inequality where, for
d\geq 2
,

\beta_{\mu}(s)\,\leq\,\frac{d+2}{d(d-1)}\,R^{2}(s)\,e^{h(R(s))}

with

h(R)=\textrm{Osc}_{B(0,R)}V\;,\;R(s)=G^{-1}(1/(1+s))\;,\;G(u)=\mu(B(0,u))\,.

For
d=1
the pre-factor is replaced by
4/\pi^{2}
.

The result is an immediate consequence of Theorem 3.1 in
[
41
]
, together with the known upper bound (asymptotically sharp as
d\to+\infty
)

C_{P}(\lambda_{R})\leq\,\frac{d+2}{d(d-1)}R^{2}

for
\lambda_{R}
the uniform probability distribution on the euclidean ball
B(0,R)
if
d\geq 2
(see e.g.
[
13
]
Corollary 4.1 or
[
19
]
Example 5.5). The case
d=1
is well known.

Actually the previous bound is not optimal in most of the specific cases that have been studied. We will only describe the situation for our two families of examples below.

Example 2.3
.

(1)

Generalized Cauchy distribution.

Let
\mu(dx)=z_{\alpha}^{-1}(1+|x|^{2})^{-(\alpha+d)/2}\,dx
for
\alpha>0
.

The best known result is that
\beta_{\mu}(s)\leq c_{\alpha}\,s^{-2/\alpha}
for
s\leq 1/4
. It is shown in
[
16
]
Proposition 4.9 in the more general context of
\kappa
concave measures (see
[
12
]
for this notion). The proof relies on weighted Poincaré inequalities, which we will discuss later. Notice that there is a typo (a minus is missing) in subsection 4.2.2 of
[
2
]
where a slightly worse result is obtained (
s^{-2/\alpha^{\prime}}
for any
\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
) using weak Lyapunov-Poincaré inequalities introduced therein. The use of the general result in Proposition
2.2
furnishes an intricate and in any case worse negative power. Of course the dimension dependence is hidden in the value of the constant
c_{\alpha}
.

We shall see below why
-2/\alpha
is the best possible negative power, i.e. why
s^{p}\beta_{\mu}(s)\to 0
as
s\to 0
for any
p>2/\alpha
. Notice that we recover the finiteness of
\beta_{\mu}(0)
in the limit
\alpha\to+\infty
, provided we are able to control the pre-factor.

(2)

Subbotin distributions.

Let
\mu(dx)=z_{\alpha}\,e^{-|x|^{\alpha}}dx
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
(for
\alpha\geq 1
the usual Poincaré inequality is satisfied).

In this case
\beta_{\mu}(s)\leq c_{\alpha}\,\ln^{\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{\alpha}}(1/s)
for
s\leq 1/4
. This is shown (again in a more general framework replacing
|x|
by any convex function) in Proposition 4.11 of
[
16
]
. The same result is obtained in
[
2
]
subsection 4.2.1. Using Proposition
2.2
furnishes the slightly worse exponent
4(1-\alpha)/\alpha
.

\diamondsuit

If a Poincaré inequality implies exponential concentration of measure, a weak Poincaré inequality also implies some concentration property. The following result is part of Theorem 8 in
[
5
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

If
\mu
satisfies a weak Poincaré inequality with rate function
\beta_{\mu}
, then for all
L
-Lipschitz function
G
with median
m_{G}
,

\mu(|G-m_{G}|>a)\leq 6\,\Theta(a/L)

where

\Theta(u)=\inf\{s\in(0,1/4]\;;\;u\geq 4\,\sqrt{\beta_{\mu}(s)}\,\ln(1/s)\}\,.

Notice that in the case of Subbotin distributions the concentration function obtained with the
\beta_{\mu}
in example
2.3
(2) is exactly the good one, while in the generalized Cauchy case there is an extra
\ln
, but the power function
s^{-2/\alpha}
is the good one too.

We turn to the perturbation problem. We will only state one possible result, other ones should be obtained by directly applying Proposition
2.2
to the perturbed measure.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\mu(dx)=e^{-V(x)}dx=e^{-(U+W)(x)}dx
for smooth
U
and
W
be a probability measure. Denote
\nu(dx)=e^{-W(x)}dx
supposed to be a probability measure too.

Assume that
U(x)\geq m_{U}
(in other words
e^{-U}
is bounded, notice that
m_{U}\leq 0
) and for simplicity that
m_{U}=U(0)
. Denote
med_{\nu}
a
\nu
-median of
|x|
.

Then

\beta_{\mu}(s)\leq 2e^{\textrm{Osc}_{B(0,R)}U}\beta_{\nu}(e^{m_{U}}s/7)

with

R=1+med_{\nu}+4\sqrt{\beta_{\nu}(u)}\,\ln(1/u)\quad\textrm{ and }\quad u=\frac{s}{1+2\beta_{\nu}(s)}\,.

Proof.

Let
R>0
and
\chi
be defined as
\chi(x)=\mathbf{1}_{|x|\leq R-1}+(R-|x|)\mathbf{1}_{R-1\leq|x|\leq R}
. We thus have for all smooth
f
and all
a\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\textrm{Var}_{\mu}(f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int(f-a)^{2}d\mu=\int(f-a)^{2}\,\chi^{2}\,d\mu+\int(f-a)^{2}\,(1-\chi^{2})\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\min_{B(0,R)}U}\,\int(f-a)^{2}\,\chi^{2}\,\nu(dx)\,+\,\textrm{Osc}^{2}f\;\mu(|x|>R-1)

so that choosing
a
such that
\int(f-a)\chi d\nu=0
we have

\displaystyle\textrm{Var}_{\mu}(f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\,e^{-\min_{B(0,R)}U}\,\left(\beta_{\nu}(s)\int|\nabla f|^{2}\,\chi^{2}\,d\nu+\beta_{\nu}(s)\int(f-a)^{2}\,|\nabla\chi|^{2}\,d\nu\right)\,+

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\,\textrm{Osc}^{2}f\;\left(s\,e^{-\min_{B(0,R)}U}+\mu(|x|>R-1)\right)

since
\textrm{Osc}(g\chi)\leq\textrm{Osc}(g)
. Finally, we get

\displaystyle\textrm{Var}_{\mu}(f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\,e^{\textrm{Osc}_{B(0,R)}U}\,\beta_{\nu}(s)\int|\nabla f|^{2}\,d\mu\,+\,

\displaystyle+\,\textrm{Osc}^{2}f\;\left(2\beta_{\nu}(s)\,e^{-\min_{B(0,R)}U}\,\nu(|x|>R-1)+\,s\,e^{-\min_{B(0,R)}U}+\mu(|x|>R-1)\right)\,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\,e^{\textrm{Osc}_{B(0,R)}U}\,\beta_{\nu}(s)\int|\nabla f|^{2}\,d\mu\,+\,

\displaystyle+\,\textrm{Osc}^{2}f\;e^{-m_{U}}\,\left((2\beta_{\nu}(s)+1)\,\nu(|x|>R-1)+\,s\,\right)\,.

According to Proposition
2.4
, if we choose

R=1+med_{\nu}+4\sqrt{\beta_{\nu}(u)}\,\ln(1/u)\quad\textrm{ and }\quad u=\frac{s}{1+2\beta_{\nu}(s)}

then

(2\beta_{\nu}(s)+1)\,\nu(|x|>R-1)\leq 6\,s\,.

The result follows.
∎

The previous result is not optimal and we could try to optimize it by choosing other
R
’s such that
(2\beta_{\nu}(s)+1)\,\nu(|x|>R-1)\to 0
as
s\to 0
. We analyze below two families of examples to determine whether this optimization really improves upon the result.

Remark 2.6
.

The same method furnishes the weak version of Holley-Stroock perturbation argument, i.e. if
U
is bounded then
\beta_{\mu}(s)\leq e^{\textrm{Osc}U}\,\beta_{\nu}(e^{m_{U}}s)
.

\diamondsuit

Example 2.7
.

(1)

Generalized Cauchy distribution.

Let
\nu(dx)=z_{\alpha}^{-1}(1+|x|^{2})^{-(\alpha+d)/2}\,dx
for
\alpha>0
. We know that
\beta_{\nu}(s)\leq c_{\alpha}\,s^{-2/\alpha}
. We will not use the final statement in the Theorem but directly use the concentration result

\nu(|x|>R-1)\leq\frac{z_{\alpha}^{-1}}{1+\alpha}\,(R-1)^{-\alpha}

for
R>1
, implying the natural choice
R-1=s^{-(2+\alpha)/\alpha^{2}}
. If
U(x)-U(0)\leq c\,\psi(|x|)
we thus have

\beta_{\mu}(s)\leq c^{\prime}_{\alpha}\,e^{c\psi(1+s^{-(2+\alpha)/\alpha^{2}})}\,s^{-2/\alpha}\,,

which is of course a disaster unless
\psi
is equivalent to a logarithm.

(2)

Subbotin distribution.

Let
\nu(dx)=z_{\alpha}\,e^{-|x|^{\alpha}}dx
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. This time the concentration behaves like
e^{-cR^{\alpha}}
so that choosing
R
of order
\ln^{1/\alpha}(1/s)
we obtain

\beta_{\mu}(s)\leq c^{\prime}_{\alpha}\,e^{c\psi(1+\ln^{1/\alpha}(1/s))}\,s^{-2/\alpha}\,,

so that again, very naturally, the result is interesting provided the growth of
U
is not larger than
|x|^{\alpha}
.

Notice that when the Poincaré constant is finite, implying an exponential concentration, one may consider Lipschitz perturbations, i.e.
U
with a linear growth. The takeaway of these examples is thus that in order to get interesting perturbation results the growth of the perturbation has to be at most the same as the concentration level.
\diamondsuit

The natural question is then to find explicit sufficient conditions to obtain explicit rates, better than the one in Proposition

,

,

]
for others inequalities. As we will recall later, the Lyapunov method was also successfully used in the framework of weighted inequalities

The Lyapunov method for weak Poincaré inequalities for convolution products, is developed in
[

]
. The authors first rewrote the results in
[

]
in order to obtain the analogue of

]

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\mu(dx)=e^{-V(x)}dx
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
V
is of class
C^{1}
. Denote by
L_{V}
the operator
L_{V}:=\Delta-\nabla V.\nabla
.

Assume the following Lyapunov type condition: there exists positive constants

and
R
, some positive function
\phi
and some smooth (
C^{2}
) function
F
with
F\geq 1
such that

\frac{L_{V}F}{F}\leq-\,\phi+b\mathbf{1}_{B(0,R)}\,.

(2.2)

Then
\mu
satisfies a weak Poincaré inequality with rate

\beta_{\mu}(s)=\left(1+C(d)\,b\,R^{2}\,e^{\textrm{Osc}_{B(0,R)}V}\right)\,h_{\phi}^{-1}(s)\quad\textrm{ with }\quad h_{\phi}(r)=\mu(\phi\leq 1/r)

and

C(d)=\,\frac{d+2}{d(d-1)}\textrm{ if $d\geq 2$}\quad\textrm{ and }C(1)=4/\pi^{2}\,.

Remark 2.9
.

In the definition of the Lyapunov function it is enough to assume that
(L_{V}F/F)(x)\leq-\phi(x)
for some positive
\phi
and
|x|\geq K
large, provided
\phi
is continuous. Indeed if the latter is true, for
|x|\leq K
,
|L_{V}F/F|(x)\leq M
and
|\phi(x)|\leq M
for some
M\geq 0
. It follows

(L_{V}F/F)(x)\leq-\phi(x)\,\mathbf{1}_{|x|>K}+M\,\mathbf{1}_{|x|\leq K}\leq\,-\phi(x)+2M\,\mathbf{1}_{|x|\leq K}.

\diamondsuit

Proof.

The proof, given for the sake of completeness, is mimiking the proof of Theorem 1.4 in
[
1
]
. One has for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
and all
r>0

\displaystyle\textrm{Var}_{\mu}(f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu((f-a)^{2})=\mu(\mathbf{1}_{\phi>1/r}(f-a)^{2})+\mu(\mathbf{1}_{\phi\leq 1/r}(f-a)^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu(r\,\phi\,(f-a)^{2})+\mu(\phi\leq 1/r)\,\textrm{Osc}^{2}(f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-r\,\int\,\frac{LF}{F}\,(f-a)^{2}\,d\mu\,+rb\,\mu((f-a)^{2}\,\mathbf{1}_{B(0,R)})+\mu(\phi\leq 1/r)\,\textrm{Osc}^{2}(f)\,.

Notice that
\phi
is measurable so that
\mu(\phi\leq 1/r):=s
is meaningful. Using that

is
\mu
-symmetric, following
[
1
]
p.64, one has

-\,\int\,\frac{LF}{F}\,(f-a)^{2}\,d\mu\leq\mu(|\nabla f|^{2})\,.

Defining
\mu_{R}=(1/\mu(B(0,R)))\,\mathbf{1}_{B(0,R)}\mu
and choosing
a=\mu_{R}(f)
one has

\mu((f-a)^{2}\,\mathbf{1}_{B(0,R)})=\textrm{Var}_{\mu_{R}}\left(\sqrt{\mu(B(0,R))}f\right)\leq\,e^{\textrm{Osc}_{B(0,R)}V}\,C_{P}(\lambda_{R})\,\mu(|\nabla f|^{2})

according to Holley-Stroock perturbation argument for the Poincaré constant. Hence the result.
∎

.

3.
Weighted Poincaré inequalities.

The second family of weakened Poincaré inequalities is the family of
weighted Poincaré inequalities
. The terminology can be misleading. Indeed in the P.D.E. (and Analysis) community, weighted is used to characterize Poincaré (or Poincaré-Wirtinger) inequality for measures that are not the Lebesgue measure (hence weighted measures in the absolutely continuous case). The same terminology is (unfortunately) used in
[
2
]
. Here we shall say that
\mu
satisfies a weighted Poincaré inequality with weight
\omega
,
\omega
being a non-negative function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, if there exists a constant
C_{P,\omega}(\mu)
such that for all nice function
f

\textrm{Var}_{\mu}(f)\,\leq\,C_{P,\omega}(\mu)\,\int\,|\nabla f|^{2}\,\omega^{2}\,d\mu\,.

(3.1)

One can also consider the converse weighted Poincaré inequality

\inf_{a}\,\int\,(g-a)^{2}\;\frac{1}{\omega^{2}}\,d\mu\leq\,C_{cP,\omega}(\mu)\,\int\,|\nabla g|^{2}\,d\mu\,.

(3.2)

The Gaussian measure satisfies such a weighted inequality with
\omega(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{-1/2}
and even more sophisticated weights (see
[
31
]
). Here we are interested in weights satisfying
\omega(x)\geq 1
for all
x
, corresponding to heavy tailed distributions when
\omega
is not bounded from above. These inequalities have been described in
[
11
,
10
]
to study generalized Cauchy distributions and more generally,
\kappa
-concave distributions. A more general systematic study is made in
[
16
]
.

From the point of view of generative models, weighted Poincaré inequalities can be used for Langevin dynamics, see e.g.
[
27
,
36
]
, even in the more general framework of non constant diffusion coefficient.

Similar to the weak Poincaré inequality, the weighted Poincaré one satisfies the following set of properties.

Proposition 3.1
.

It holds

(1)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},C_{P,\omega}(x+Z)=C_{P,\tilde{\omega}}(Z)
, where
\tilde{\omega}(z)=\omega(z-x)
.

(2)

for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R},C_{P,\omega}(\lambda Z)=\lambda^{2}C_{P,\tilde{\omega}}(Z)
, where
\tilde{\omega}(z)=\omega(z/\lambda)
.

(3)

for any map
T:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is
L
-Lipschitz and invertible,
C_{P,\omega}(T(Z))=L^{2}C_{P,\tilde{\omega}}(Z)
, where
\tilde{\omega}(z)=\omega(T^{-1}(z))
.

(4)

if
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}
are independent and
P_{i}
is the projection on the
i
-th coordinate, then
\textrm{Var}\left(f\right)\leq\max_{i}C_{P,\omega_{i}}(Z_{i})\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i}\left|\nabla_{Z_{i}}f\left(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}\right)\right|^{2}\omega_{i}^{2}\circ P_{i}(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n})\right]
.

(5)

if
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}
are independent,
\textrm{Var}\left(f\left(\sum Z_{i}\right)\right)\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\nabla f\left(\sum Z_{i}\right)\right|^{2}\sum_{i}C_{P,\omega_{i}}(Z_{i})\omega_{i}^{2}(Z_{i})\right]
.

The proof is provided in the Appendix
B
.

One of the most interesting features of weighted inequalities is that they are suitable to the use of Lyapunov functions. The following result is the analogue of Theorem
2.8
in this framework.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mu(dx)=e^{-V(x)}dx
, for some smooth
V
, be a probability measure,
L_{V}=\Delta-\nabla V.\nabla
. Let
\phi
be a
C^{1}
positive increasing function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Assume that there exists a
\phi
-Lyapunov function
F
, i.e. a
C^{2}
function
F
such that
F\geq 1
and

L_{V}F\leq-\,\phi(F)+b\,\mathbf{1}_{|x|\leq R}

for some
R>0
and
b\geq 0
.

Then
\mu
satisfies both a weighted Poincaré inequality

\textrm{Var}_{\mu}(g)\leq\max\left(1\,,\,\frac{bC_{P}(\mu_{R})}{\phi(1)}\right)\,\int\,|\nabla g|^{2}\,\left(1+\frac{1}{\phi^{\prime}(F)}\right)\,d\mu

(3.3)

where
C_{P}(\mu_{R})
is the Poincaré constant of the normalized restriction of
\mu
to the ball
B(0,R)
and a converse weighted Poincaré inequality

\inf_{a}\,\int\,(g-a)^{2}\;\frac{\phi(F)}{F}\,d\mu\leq(1+bC_{P}(\mu_{R}))\,\int\,|\nabla g|^{2}\,d\mu\,.

(3.4)

Recall that in all cases and
d\geq 2
,

C_{P}(\mu_{R})\leq\frac{d+2}{d(d-1)}\,R^{2}\,e^{\textrm{Osc}_{B(0,R)}V}

while the pre-factor has to be replaced by
4/\pi^{2}
for
d=1
.

These two results are exactly Theorem 2.8 and Theorem 2.18 in
[
16
]
. Corollary 2.14 in
[
16
]
contains an alternate version of (
3.3
) which is not directly comparable, but is similar on most examples,

\textrm{Var}_{\mu}(g)\leq 8\,{\max}^{2}\left(1\,,\,\frac{bC_{P}(\mu_{R})}{\phi(1)}\right)\,\int\,|\nabla g|^{2}\,\left(1+\frac{|\nabla F|^{2}}{\phi^{2}(F)}\right)\,d\mu\,.

(3.5)

We now analyze how the weighted Poincaré behaves under perturbations.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mu(dx)=e^{-V(x)}dx=e^{-(U+W)(x)}dx
for smooth
U
and
W
be a probability measure. Denote
\nu(dx)=e^{-W(x)}dx
supposed to be a probability measure too that satisfies a weighted Poincaré inequality with weight
\omega
.

(1)

(Bounded perturbation) Assume that
U(x)\geq m_{U}
(in other words
e^{-U}
is bounded, notice that
m_{U}\leq 0
).

Then

C_{P,\tilde{\omega}}(\mu)\leq e^{-m_{U}}C_{P,\omega}(\nu)\quad\textrm{with}\quad\tilde{\omega}=\omega e^{U/2}\,.

Furthermore, if
U
is bounded, then
\mu
satisfies a weighted Poincaré inequality with weight
\omega
and constant

C_{P,\omega}(\mu)\leq e^{\textrm{Osc}(U)}C_{P,\omega}(\nu)\,.

(2)

(Unbounded perturbation)

(a)

(Weighted Lipschitz case) Suppose that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

s=\sup_{x}C_{P,\omega}(\nu)\frac{1+\varepsilon}{4}|\nabla U|^{2}\,\omega^{2}<1

then
\mu
satisfies a weighted Poincaré inequality with weight
\omega
and constant

C_{P,\omega}(\mu)\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon^{-1})C_{P,\omega}(\nu)}{1-s}

(b)

(Weighted generator case) Let us introduce

L_{U+W}^{\omega}f=\omega^{2}\Delta f+(\nabla\omega^{2}-\omega^{2}\nabla(U+W)).\nabla f.

Suppose that

s=\sup_{x}\frac{1}{2}C_{P,\omega}(\nu)\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla U|^{2}\omega^{2}+L_{U+W}^{\omega}U\right)_{+}<1

then
\mu
satisfies a weighted Poincaré inequality with weight
\omega
and constant

C_{P,\omega}(\mu)\leq\frac{C_{P,\omega}(\nu)}{1-s}

(c)

(Weighted Lyapunov condition) Let us suppose that there exists
F\geq 1
, some
R>0
and constants
b,\theta>0
such that

L_{W}^{\omega}F\leq-\theta F+b1_{|x|\leq R}

(3.6)

and there exists
\theta^{\prime}<\theta

-\omega^{2}\nabla U.\nabla F\leq\theta^{\prime}F

then
\mu
satisfies a weighted Poincaré inequality with weight
\omega
and constant

C_{P,\omega}(\mu)\leq\frac{1}{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}(1+C_{P,\omega}(\mu_{R})).

The proof is provided in the Appendix
B
.

Besides, consider
g=(f-a)/\omega
. Assume that
\int(f-a)/\omega\,\,d\mu=0
, then the weighted Poincaré inequality yields, for
\lambda>1

\displaystyle\int\,\left(\frac{f-a}{\omega}\right)^{2}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textrm{Var}_{\mu}(g)\leq C_{P,\omega}(\mu)\,\int\,|\nabla f-\,(\nabla\omega/\omega)\,(f-a)|^{2}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{P,\omega}(\mu)\left(\lambda\int\,|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu\,+\,\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}\,\int(f-a)^{2}\,|(\nabla\omega/\omega)|^{2}\,d\mu\right)

so that, provided
C_{P,\omega}(\mu)\,|\nabla\omega|^{2}<1
we obtain the existence of some constant
C_{cP,\omega}(\mu)
such that

\inf_{a}\int\,(f-a)^{2}\,\frac{1}{\omega^{2}}\,d\mu\leq C_{cP,\omega}(\mu)\,\int\,|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu\,.

The latter
converse weighted inequality
has been discussed in
[
29
,
9
]
under the name Hardy/ Poincaré inequalitiy (the latter terminology unfortunately also covers different inequalities) in order to study fast diffusion equations. It is easily seen that, conversely, (
3.2
) implies (
3.1
) under the same assumption on
\omega
.

The relationship between (converse) weighted Poincaré inequalities and weak Poincaré inequalities is described below (see Theorem 4.6 in
[
16
]
).

Theorem 3.4
.

If
\mu
satisfies the converse weighted Poincaré inequality (
3.2
) and
\int(1/\omega^{2})d\mu<+\infty
then,
\mu
satisfies a weak Poincaré inequality

\textrm{Var}_{\mu}(f)\leq\frac{C_{cP,\omega}(\mu)}{G(s)}\,\int|\nabla f|^{2}\,d\mu\,+\,s\,\textrm{Osc}^{2}(f)

with
G(s)=\inf\{u\;;\;\mu(\omega^{2}\geq\frac{1}{u})>s\}
for
s<\frac{1}{4}
.

Generalized Cauchy distributions.

In

Choose

L_{V}F=(k+2)\,(1+|x|^{2})^{(k/2)-1}\,(\alpha+d-k\,-\,(\alpha-k)(1+|x|^{2}))

for

It follows that
\phi(u)=\varepsilon(k+2)\,u^{k/(k+2)}

\phi^{\prime}(u)=\varepsilon ku^{-2/(k+2)}
and

\frac{1}{\phi^{\prime}(F(x))}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon\,k}\,(1+|x|^{2})\,,

so that

(1+(1/\phi^{\prime}(F(x)))\leq\frac{\varepsilon k+1}{\varepsilon k}\,(1+|x|^{2})\,.

We have thus obtained, for

\textrm{Var}_{\mu_{\alpha}}(g)\leq\,\max(1,C)\left(\frac{1+\varepsilon k}{\varepsilon k}\right)\,\int\,|\nabla g|^{2}\,(1+|x|^{2})\,d\mu_{\alpha}

If one gets the optimal weight, the constant is very bad, and still worse when evaluating

Remark 3.8

Convolution of generalized Cauchy distributions

Unlike the Gaussian family the generalized Cauchy family is not stable, making the analysis of convolutions more complex.
[
40
]
provides explicit expressions for the convolution in dimension
d=1
when both
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
are odd, and the scale parameter is equal to
1

\mu_{\alpha_{1}}*\mu_{\alpha_{2}}(x)=\mathcal{O}\left((1+|x|^{2})^{-{(1+\alpha_{\min})/2}}\right),\quad\alpha_{\min}=\min\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}.

More generally, denote
F(x)=\frac{\mu_{\alpha_{1}}*\mu_{\alpha_{2}}(x)}{\mu_{\alpha_{\min}}(x)}
. We may write

\displaystyle F(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},d)\,\int\,\frac{(1+|x|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{min})/2}}{(1+|x-y|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{min})/2}\,(1+|y|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{max})/2}}\,dy

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle C(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},d)\,\int G_{d,\alpha_{min},\alpha_{max}}(x,y)\,dy\,.

F
is continuous and everywhere positive. In addition, on one hand

\displaystyle G_{d,\alpha_{min},\alpha_{max}}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(1+2|x-y|^{2}+2|y|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{min})/2}}{(1+|x-y|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{min})/2}\,(1+|y|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{max})/2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(m)\,\frac{(1+|x-y|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{min})/2}+(1+|y|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{min})/2}}{(1+|x-y|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{min})/2}\,(1+|y|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{max})/2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c(m)}{(1+|y|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{max})/2}}+\frac{c(m)}{(1+|x-y|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{min})/2}\,(1+|y|^{2})^{(\alpha_{max}-\alpha_{min})/2}}\,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c(m)}{(1+|y|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{max})/2}}+\frac{c(m)}{(1+|x-y|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{min})/2}}\,,

so that
F
is upper bounded.

On the other hand,

F(x)\geq C(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},d)\,\int\,\mathbf{1}_{|y|\leq 1}\,G_{d,\alpha_{min},\alpha_{max}}(x,y)\,dy

and for
|x|>2
,
|x-y|\mathbf{1}_{|y|\leq 1}\leq|x|+1
. Hence, for
|x|>2
,

F(x)\geq C(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},d)\,\int\,\mathbf{1}_{|y|\leq 1}\,\frac{(1+|x|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{min})/2}}{(2+|x|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{min})/2}\,(1+|y|^{2})^{(d+\alpha_{max})/2}}\,dy

which is bounded below by a positive constant. For
|x|\leq 2
one also have a positive lower bound thanks to the continuity of
F
.

Hence
\mu_{\alpha_{1}}*\mu_{\alpha_{2}}
is a bounded perturbation
F\,\mu_{\alpha_{min}}
where
F
is bounded from below and from above by positive constants. According to Proposition
3.3
(1),
\mu_{\alpha_{1}}*\mu_{\alpha_{2}}
satisfies a weighted Poincaré inequality with the same optimal weight
\omega(x)=\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}
.

\diamondsuit

Example 3.10

Perturbations of generalized Cauchy distributions.

Recall that the weight function is of the form
\omega^{2}(x)=1+|x|^{2}

(a)

(Weighted Lipschitz case) The perturbation needs to satisfy

|\nabla U|^{2}\leq\frac{4}{(1+\varepsilon)\omega^{2}C_{P,\omega}(\nu)}.

Therefore, the “maximal” perturbation satisfying this is of the form

U(x)-U(0)=\beta\ln(1+|x|),\,\,\,\text{where}\,\,\,\beta^{2}=\frac{4}{(1+\varepsilon)C_{P,\omega}(\nu)}.

(3.10)

(b)

(Weighted generator case) First, observe that

\frac{1}{2}|\nabla U|\omega^{2}+L^{\omega,\mu}U=\omega^{2}\nabla^{2}U+\langle\nabla\omega^{2},\nabla U\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\omega^{2}|\nabla U|^{2}-\omega^{2}\langle\nabla W,\nabla U\rangle.

The condition

\omega^{2}\nabla^{2}U+\langle\nabla\omega^{2},\nabla U\rangle\leq C

is equivalent to
\omega^{2}\nabla U
being Lipschitz continuous.
Note that potentials of the form (

s=\sup_{x}\frac{1}{2}C_{P,\omega}(\nu)\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla U|\omega^{2}+L^{\omega,\mu}U\right)_{+}<1.

(3.11)

(c)

(Weighted Lyapunov condition) Suppose that
\beta=(\alpha+d)/2>1

F\geq 1
and

such that

Consider

. Then,

\displaystyle L_{W}^{\omega}F=2\omega^{2}-4(\beta-1)|x|^{2}=2\omega^{2}-4(\beta-1)\omega^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\omega^{2}}\right).

Choose

such that

4.
More on the links between weak and weighted Poincaré inequalities.

We already saw in Theorem
3.4
that a converse weighted Poincaré inequality together with a tail control of the considered measure, implies a weak Poincaré inequality. In some situations, for instance for the generalized Cauchy distributions, weighted and converse weighted Poincaré inequalities can be compared.

We shall here look at the converse way, i.e. from weak Poincaré to converse weighted Poincaré inequalities.

Introduce the standard (global)
L^{2}(\mu)
-capacity of a set:

Cap_{\mu}(A):=\inf\left\{\int|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,d\mu:\ \varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ 1_{A}\leq\varphi\leq 1\right\}.

(4.1)

Capacity measure criteria for functional inequalities were introduced in
[
8
]
based on results in
[
39
]
. They were further developed in
[
6
,
5
,
7
,
15
]
among others.

We assume that
\mu
satisfies some weak Poincaré inequality (
2.1
), and that
\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\beta_{\mu}(s)=+\infty
, otherwise an ordinary Poincaré inequality is satisfied. Let us state a first result (Theorem 2.1 in
[
5
]
).

Lemma 4.1

(Measure–capacity bound for weak Poincaré)
.

Assume (
2.1
). Then for every measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\mu(A)=a\leq\frac{1}{2}
,

Cap_{\mu}(A)\ \geq\ \frac{a}{4\,\beta_{\mu}(a/4)}\,.

(4.2)

We have the following capacity measure to converse weighted Poincaré inequality.

Lemma 4.2

(Capacitary criterion for converse weighted Poincaré)
.

Let
\omega\geq 0
be measurable and let
\nu=\omega^{2}\,\mu
.
Assume there exists
C\in(0,\infty)
such that

\int_{A}\omega^{2}\,d\mu\ =:\ \nu(A)\ \leq\ C\,Cap_{\mu}(A)\qquad\text{for every Borel }A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ with }\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}.

(4.3)

Then for every locally Lipschitz
f
and any median
m
of
f
(so that
\mu(f\geq m)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
and
\mu(f\leq m)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
),

\int(f-m)^{2}\,\omega^{2}\,d\mu\ \leq\ C^{\prime}\,\int|\nabla f|^{2}\,d\mu.

(4.4)

In particular,

\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\int(f-c)^{2}\,\omega^{2}\,d\mu\leq C^{\prime}\,\int|\nabla f|^{2}\,d\mu.

The proof is given in Appendix
C
.

We shall use these two lemmas in order to build an appropriate weight.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be fixed and define the tail function

s(r):=\mu\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ |x-x_{0}|>r\},\qquad r\geq 0.

(4.5)

Note that
s
is non-increasing and
s(r)\downarrow 0
as
r\uparrow\infty
. Also define

h(u):=\frac{1}{4\,\beta_{\mu}(u/4)},\qquad u\in(0,1].

(4.6)

Proposition 4.3

(Explicit weight)
.

Define

\omega^{2}(x)\ :=\ h\bigl(s(|x-x_{0}|)\bigr)\ =\ \frac{1}{4\,\beta_{\mu}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{4}\,\mu\{y:\ |y-x_{0}|>|x-x_{0}|\}\right)}.

(4.7)

Then
\omega^{2}
is radial, non-increasing with respect to
|x-x_{0}|
, and
\omega(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
.
Moreover, for every Borel set
A
with
\mu(A)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
,

\int_{A}\omega^{2}\,d\mu\ \leq Cap_{\mu}(A).

(4.8)

Proof.

The monotonicity and vanishing at infinity are immediate from the definition and the fact that
s(r)\downarrow 0
whereas
\beta_{\mu}(u)\uparrow\infty
as
u\downarrow 0
.

To prove (
4.8
), we compare
\int_{A}\omega^{2}\,d\mu
with
Cap(A)
via rearrangements.
Let
\omega^{*}
denote the non-increasing rearrangement of
\omega^{2}
with respect to
\mu
.
A standard calculation shows that for
h
non-increasing, the choice
\omega^{2}(x)=h(s(|x-x_{0}|))
gives

\omega^{*}(u)=h(u)\qquad\text{for all }u\in(0,1).

(4.9)

Indeed,

\{\omega^{2}>t\}=\{h(s(|x-x_{0}|))>t\}=\{s(|x-x_{0}|)<h^{-1}(t)\}

so that

\mu(\{\omega^{2}>t\})=h^{-1}(t)

(by definition of the generalized inverse of a monotone map), whence (
4.9
).
Therefore, for any
A
with
\mu(A)=a\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
,

\int_{A}\omega^{2}\,d\mu\ \leq\ \int_{0}^{a}\omega^{*}(u)\,du\ =\ \int_{0}^{a}h(u)\,du\ =\ \int_{0}^{a}\frac{du}{4\,\beta_{\mu}(u/4)}.

(4.10)

Since
\beta_{\mu}
is non-increasing,
u\mapsto 1/\beta_{\mu}(u/4)
is non-decreasing, so that

\int_{0}^{a}\frac{du}{\beta_{\mu}(u/4)}\leq\frac{a}{\beta_{\mu}(a/4)}.

Combining this with (
4.10
) and the capacity lower bound (
4.2
) yields

\int_{A}\omega^{2}\,d\mu\leq\frac{a}{4\,\beta_{\mu}(a/4)}\leq\,Cap_{\mu}(A)\,,

which is (
4.8
).
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Assume that
\mu
satisfies a weak Poincaré inequality (
2.1
), with
\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\beta_{\mu}(s)=+\infty
.
Then
\mu
satisfies a converse weighted Poincaré inequality with weight
\omega
defined by (
4.7
).

Weaker Logarithmic Sobolev inequalities.

Perturbation of logarithmic Sobolev are studied in
[
23

Theorems 2.5, 2.6 and 2.7. Lyapunov conditions for log-Sobolev (for short) inequalities are introduced in
[

in the more general framework of (weighted) super Poincaré inequalities and discussed in
[

(with an erratum on some missing assumptions, one can find on the webpage of the first author). Using the Lyapunov method of
[

]
,
[

studies the log-Sobolev inequality for convolution products
\mu*\nu

\mu
and
\nu
satisfy a log-Sobolev inequality, so does
\mu*\nu
with a log-Sobolev constant less than the sum of the two.
[

contains more general results, in the spirit of
[

\mu

Similarly to weakened Poincaré inequalities one may consider weaker logarithmic Sobolev inequalities.

First, the
weak logarithmic Sobolev inequality
, introduced in
[
15
]
, states that there exists a non-increasing function
\beta_{\mu}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that for all
s>0

\int f^{2}\,\ln\left(\frac{f^{2}}{\mu(f^{2})}\right)\,d\mu:=\textrm{Ent}_{\mu}(f^{2})\leq\beta^{LS}_{\mu}(s)\int|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu+s\,\textrm{Osc}^{2}(f)\,.

(5.1)

As shown in section 4 of
[
15
]
these inequalities allow to characterize non exponential convergence in relative entropy. The following set of properties hold.

Proposition 5.1
.

It holds (we skip the superscript

for simplicity)

(1)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\beta_{x+Z}=\beta_{Z}
,

(2)

for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R},\beta_{\lambda Z}=\lambda^{2}\beta_{Z}
,

(3)

if
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}
are independent,
\beta_{(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n})}(s)\leq\max_{i}\beta_{Z_{i}}(s/n)
,

(4)

if
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}
are independent,
\beta_{\sum_{i}Z_{i}}(s)\leq\sum_{i}\beta_{Z_{i}}(s/n)
,

(5)

if
\mu_{n}
weakly converges to
\mu
,
\beta_{\mu}\leq\liminf\beta_{\mu_{n}}
.

The proof is provided in Appendix
D
.

The main fact is that actually, a weak log-Sobolev inequality is equivalent to a weak Poincaré inequality. This is Proposition 3.1 in
[
15
]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

If
\mu
satisfies a weak log-Sobolev inequality with rate
\beta_{\mu}^{LS}
, it satisfies a weak Poincaré inequality with

\beta_{\mu}(s)\leq\frac{24\,\beta_{\mu}^{LS}\left(\frac{s}{2}\,\ln(1+(1/2s)\right)}{\ln(1+(1/2s))}\,,

(5.2)

provided
\beta_{\mu}
in (
5.2
) is non-increasing.

Conversely for some positive constants
c,c^{\prime},s_{0}
and all
s\leq s_{0}
,

\beta_{\mu}^{LS}(s)=c^{\prime}\,\beta_{\mu}\left(\frac{cs}{\ln(1/s)}\right)\,\ln(1/s)\,.

(5.3)

The constants
c,c^{\prime},s_{0}
are universal and can be traced in the proofs of
[
15
]
if necessary. Notice that the previous result is almost closed. Indeed, if we plug the formula (
5.3
) for
\beta_{\mu}^{LS}
in equation (
5.2
), we get something like
\beta_{\mu}(s)\leq C\,\beta_{\mu}(Cs)
, i.e. up to some constants, we are not loosing a factor for
\beta_{\mu}
.

The fact that
\beta_{\mu}
in (
5.2
) is non-increasing is crucial (and not explicitly stated in
[
15
]
, but necessary in order to apply Theorem 2.2 in
[
5
]
). Indeed if
\beta_{\mu}^{LS}=C_{LS}
is constant, the result does not apply (otherwise
C_{P}(\mu)=0
). Except this important restriction, if one of
\beta_{\mu}
or
\beta_{\mu}^{LS}
is optimal, so is the other given by Proposition
5.2
.

The previous Proposition indicates that we cannot expect to obtain really new results, using weak log-Sobolev instead of weak Poincaré. For instance, if the Lyapunov condition (
2.2
) in Theorem
2.8
is satisfied,
\mu
will satisfy some weak log-Sobolev inequality and get a rate function. If we are able to give a direct proof of this fact, all the direct proofs we know do not furnish better rates than the one obtained by transferring the one for the weak Poincaré inequality.

The second family of weakened logarithmic Sobolev inequalities is the
family of

inequalities.
We say that
\mu

It seems that these inequalities appeared in
[

[

The weighted logarithmic Sobolev inequality satisfies similar properties to the weighted Poincaré one.

Proposition 5.3
.

It holds

(1)

for all

, where
\tilde{\omega}(z)=\omega(z-x)
.

(2)

for any

\tilde{\omega}(z)=\omega(z/\lambda)
.

(3)

for any map

, where

(4)

if
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}
are independent and

Proof.

The proof follows from the well known subadditivity property of the entropy (see e.g.Lemma 2.3.6 in

Perturbations of Subbotin distributions

We provide similar results to those in example
3.10
for Subbotin distributions.

Recall that the associated weight function is of the form
\omega^{2}(x)=1+(1+|x|)^{2(1-\alpha)}
.

(a)

(Weighted Lipschitz case) The “maximal” perturbation satisfying the condition is of the form

U(x)-U(0)=\beta(1+|x|)^{\alpha}.

(B.2)

(b)

(Weighted generator case) Note that potentials of the form (
B.2
) also satisfy condition (
3.11
).

(c)

(Weighted Lyapunov condition) Let
\nu(dx)=z_{\alpha}e^{-|x|^{\alpha}}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. The weighted generator is of the form

L_{W}^{\omega}F:=\omega^{2}\Delta F+\left(2(1-\alpha)\frac{(1+|x|)^{1-2\alpha}}{|x|}-\alpha|x|^{\alpha-2}\omega^{2}\right)\langle x,\nabla F\rangle\leq-\theta F.

A natural choice is
F=e^{\gamma|x|^{\alpha}}
. We do not give all details, only informal computations:

\displaystyle\nabla F

\displaystyle=\gamma\alpha|x|^{\alpha-2}xF,\quad\quad\langle x,\nabla F\rangle=\gamma\alpha|x|^{\alpha}F,

\displaystyle\Delta F

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2}\alpha^{2}|x|^{2(\alpha-1)}F+\gamma\alpha(d+\alpha-2)|x|^{\alpha-2}F.

Asymptotically we have

\displaystyle\omega^{2}\Delta F\sim|x|^{2(1-\alpha)}\Delta F\sim\gamma^{2}\alpha^{2}F,

\displaystyle\left(2(1-\alpha)\frac{(1+|x|)^{1-2\alpha}}{|x|}-\alpha|x|^{\alpha-2}\omega^{2}\right)\langle x,\nabla F\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad\sim\left(2(1-\alpha)|x|^{-2\alpha}-\alpha|x|^{\alpha-2}|x|^{2(1-\alpha)}\right)\gamma\alpha|x|^{\alpha}F\sim-\gamma\alpha^{2}F.

Taking
\gamma\in(0,1)
, we have that the negative term dominates.

For a perturbation to be valid, there needs to exists
\theta^{\prime}<\theta
such that
-\omega^{2}\nabla U.\nabla F\leq\theta^{\prime}F
, this translates to

-\omega^{2}a\alpha|x|^{\alpha-2}\langle x,\nabla U\rangle\leq\theta^{\prime}.

This condition is satisfied for potentials of the form (
B.2
). Bounding
\omega^{2}
, we get the following simpler condition for large values of
|x|

-\langle x,\nabla U\rangle\leq\frac{\theta^{\prime}|x|^{\alpha}}{a\alpha}.

Proof of Lemma
4.2

Proof.

Define

f_{+}=\max(0,f-m)\,,\quad f_{-}=\min(0,f-m).

We have that

\int(f-m)w^{2}d\mu=\int f_{+}^{2}w^{2}d\mu+\int f_{-}^{2}w^{2}d\mu,

where
\mu(\{f_{+}>0\})\leq 1/2
and
\mu(\{f_{-}<0\})\leq 1/2
. It suffices to estimate one of the integrals since the treatment of the other is identical.

Denote
A_{k}=\{x:2^{k}<f_{+}\}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\int f_{+}^{2}w^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\{2^{k}<f_{+}\leq 2^{k+1}\}}f_{+}^{2}w^{2}d\mu\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{2(k+1)}\int_{\{2^{k}<f_{+}\leq 2^{k+1}\}}w^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{2(k+1)}\nu(A_{k})\leq 4C\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{2k}\,Cap_{\mu}(A_{k}).

For each
k
, define

\varphi_{k}(x)=\min\left(1,\frac{(f_{+}-2^{k-1})_{+}}{2^{k-1}}\right).

It holds that
\varphi_{k}
is admissible for
Cap_{\mu}(A_{k})
,

1_{A_{k}}\leq\varphi_{k}\leq 1

and

|\nabla\varphi_{k}|=\frac{|\nabla f|}{2^{k-1}}1_{\{2^{k-1}<f<2^{k}\}}.

Hence

2^{2k-2}Cap_{\mu}(A_{k})\leq\int_{\{2^{k-1}<f<2^{k}\}}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu.

Summing over
k
and noting that the sets
\{2^{k-1}<f<2^{k}\}
are disjoint, it leads to

\int f_{+}^{2}w^{2}d\mu\leq 16\,C\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\{2^{k-1}<f<2^{k}\}}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu=16C\int_{\{f_{+}>0\}}\nabla f|^{2}d\mu\leq 16C\int\nabla f|^{2}d\mu,

which concludes the proof.
