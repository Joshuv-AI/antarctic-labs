Abstract




Abstract
This paper establishes Bismut-type formulas and gradient estimates for Feynman–Kac semigroups on Riemannian manifolds with boundary, under geometric conditions
formulated in terms of Ricci curvature
\mathrm{Ric}_{Z}\geq K
and second fundamental form
\mathrm{II}\geq\sigma
for potentially unbounded functions
K
and
\sigma
. We then apply these formulas to derive pointwise gradient estimates for the
Neumann semigroup under variable, possibly unbounded, lower curvature bounds.
Both convex and non-convex boundary cases are treated. In the non-convex case,
the boundary contribution is controlled by a conformal change of metric and
an exponential estimate for the boundary local time.

1
Introduction

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold, possibly with
non-empty boundary
\partial M
, and let

L=\Delta+Z,

where
\Delta
is the Laplace–Beltrami operator and
Z
is a smooth vector
field on
M
. We consider the reflecting diffusion process
x_{t}^{x}

generated by
\frac{1}{2}L
, starting from
x\in M
, which is described by

dx_{t}^{x}=/\!/_{t}\circ dB_{t}+\frac{1}{2}Z(x_{t}^{x})\,dt+\frac{1}{2}N(x_{t}^{x})\,dl_{t}^{x},\qquad x_{0}^{x}=x.

Here
B_{t}
is a Brownian motion on
T_{x}M\simeq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

/\!/_{t}:T_{x}M\to T_{x_{t}^{x}}M
denotes stochastic parallel transport,

N
is the inward unit normal vector field on
\partial M
, and
l_{t}^{x}

is the boundary local time. Throughout the paper we assume that the reflecting
diffusion is non-explosive. The corresponding Neumann semigroup is

P_{t}f(x)=\mathbb{E}^{x}[f(x_{t})],\qquad t\geq 0,\quad f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M).

Gradient estimates for heat semigroups are a classical topic connecting
stochastic analysis, geometric analysis and partial differential equations.
A fundamental tool is the Bismut-type derivative formula, which represents
derivatives of semigroups by stochastic integrals. Since Bismut’s pioneering
work
[
4
]
, such formulas have been developed extensively; see,
for instance,

[
9
,
26
,
8
,
1
,
24
,
25
]
. They have important
applications to heat kernel estimates, functional inequalities, strong Feller
properties and sensitivity analysis; see, among others,

[
3
,
11
,
19
,
33
]
.

When the manifold has a boundary, the problem becomes more delicate. The
reflecting diffusion interacts with the geometry of
\partial M
through
the boundary local time, and the derivative process is influenced both by the
interior curvature and by the second fundamental form of the boundary. We write

\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits_{Z}:=\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits-\nabla Z^{\flat}

for the Bakry–Emery curvature associated with
L
, where
Z^{\flat}
is the one-form dual to
Z
, namely

Z^{\flat}(v)=\langle Z,v\rangle,\qquad v\in TM.

Define the second
fundamental form by

\mathrm{II}(X,Y)=-\langle\nabla_{X}N,Y\rangle,\qquad X,Y\in T\partial M.

Bismut-type formulas and gradient estimates for reflecting diffusions and
Neumann semigroups were studied by Qian, Hsu, Wang and others; see

[
21
,
14
,
31
,
32
]
. Further uniform
gradient estimates and applications can be found in

[
5
]
.

A basic difficulty can already be seen from the multiplicative functional
appearing in the derivative formula. If

\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits_{Z}\geq-K,\qquad\mathrm{II}\geq-\sigma,

then the corresponding multiplicative functional
Q_{t}:T_{x}M\to T_{x_{t}}M

typically satisfies an estimate of the form

|Q_{t}|\leq\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}K(x_{s})\,ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(x_{s})\,dl_{s}\right).

For bounded
K
and
\sigma
, this leads to standard gradient estimates.
However, when the lower curvature bound is unbounded or when the boundary is
non-convex, the time integral of
K
and the local-time contribution from

\sigma
are no longer directly controlled. This is the main obstacle
addressed in the present paper.

Our aim is to establish a new type of Bismut-type formulas and pointwise gradient estimates
for Neumann semigroups on manifolds with boundary under variable, possibly
unbounded, curvature conditions. The first main ingredient is a
Feynman–Kac version of the Bismut formula for reflecting diffusions. In this
formula, the potential contributes an additional drift term involving

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V
, whereas the effects of the interior curvature and the boundary
geometry are encoded in the multiplicative functional. This separation is
crucial: it allows us to absorb the unbounded negative part of the curvature
into a Feynman–Kac weight, rather than estimating it directly through the
derivative process. We then choose the potential according to the curvature
lower bound and use the resulting formula to derive pointwise gradient
estimates for the Neumann semigroup. This approach is related to the
Feynman–Kac method of Da Prato and Priola
[
7
]
, where gradient
estimates were obtained without monotonicity assumptions. For Bismut formulas
and estimates for Feynman–Kac semigroups on manifolds without boundary, see

[
28
,
16
]
.

Let
V:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a potential with
V^{-}\in\mathcal{K}(M)
(see the definition in (
2.1
)). The Feynman–Kac semigroup is defined by

\displaystyle P_{t}^{V}f(x)=\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\mathbb{V}_{t}f(x_{t})\right],\qquad\mathbb{V}_{t}=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}V(x_{s})\,ds\right).

(1.1)

Kato potentials and their Feynman–Kac semigroups are classical in the theory
of Schrödinger operators; see
[
23
,
12
,
13
]
. In Section 2 we prove
a Bismut-type formula for
P_{t}^{V}
on manifolds with boundary. Compared
with the classical Neumann Bismut formula, this result is tailored to
unbounded Kato-type potentials and is stated in a localized form before the
global exhaustion argument is applied. This localized formulation is useful
for treating unbounded curvature lower bounds and for passing rigorously from
the penalized multiplicative functionals to the limiting boundary-adapted
multiplicative functional. This formula is
the analytic foundation for the gradient estimates of the Neumann semigroup.

We first consider the convex boundary case. Assume that, for some
m>d
,

\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits_{Z}^{m,d}:=\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits-\nabla Z^{\flat}-\frac{Z\otimes Z}{m-d}\geq-\ell(\rho_{o}),

where
\ell\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
has at most quadratic growth, and suppose
that

\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits_{Z}\geq-h,\qquad\mathrm{II}\geq 0,

for some non-negative
h\in C^{1}(M)
. Under the pointwise moment assumptions

\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[h^{2}(x_{s})]\leq\theta_{0}(x),\qquad\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[|\nabla h|^{2}(x_{s})]\leq\theta_{1}(x),

we prove that, for every
T>0
,
x\in M
, and

f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)
,

|\nabla P_{T}f|(x)\leq\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\min\{T,1\}}}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\theta_{1}(x)}+\sqrt{\theta_{0}(x)}+\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{\theta_{0}(x)\theta_{1}(x)}\right]\|f\|_{\infty}.

Thus the coefficients in the estimate are pointwise in
x
and reflect the
growth of the lower curvature bound along the diffusion. This is the main
difference from the estimates under constant curvature lower bounds: no global
constant lower bound for
\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits_{Z}
is imposed, and the resulting coefficient is
expressed through the actual distribution of the reflecting diffusion starting
from
x
. Hence the estimate remains meaningful on noncompact manifolds
whose Bakry–Emery curvature may tend to
-\infty
at infinity. We also point out that the moment assumptions imposed on
h
and

\nabla h
do not by themselves guarantee the exponential integrability
needed for the multiplicative functional
Q_{t}
. A simple geometric example is provided by a rotationally symmetric model
manifold with metric
g=dr^{2}+\varphi(r)^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
, where

\varphi(r)=\exp(ar^{2})
for large
r
. Then

\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits(\partial_{r},\partial_{r})\sim-4(d-1)a^{2}r^{2},

so the curvature lower bound is controlled by

h(x)=C_{a}(1+\rho_{o}(x)^{2})
. Although
h(x_{s})
and

|\nabla h|(x_{s})
have finite second moments on finite time intervals, the
exponential moment of
\int_{0}^{t}h(x_{s})\,ds
diverges when
a
is chosen
large enough, see details in Example
6.1
in Appendix.

The abstract moment assumptions above can be verified explicitly under radial
curvature growth conditions. In particular, if

\ell(r)=K_{0}(1+r^{2})^{\gamma},\qquad K_{0}>0,

then the subquadratic case
\gamma\in[0,1)
leads to exponential-type
weights, while the borderline quadratic case
\gamma=1
leads to
polynomial-type weights. Hence the resulting estimates cover both
subquadratic and quadratic curvature growth regimes; see Theorem
3.6

and Corollary
3.7
.

We then treat non-convex boundaries. Suppose

\mathrm{II}\geq-\sigma

for a non-negative boundary function
\sigma
. The local time term in the
multiplicative functional then gives a positive boundary contribution. To
control it, we use the conformal change method developed by Wang

[
30
,
32
]
. More precisely, we introduce a positive function

\phi
and consider the conformally changed metric

g^{\prime}=\phi^{-2}g.

If

N\log\phi\geq\sigma\quad\text{on }\partial M,

then the boundary becomes convex under
g^{\prime}
. The key additional ingredient
is the local-time estimate

\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\phi^{-2}(x_{t})\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(x_{s})\,dl_{s}\right)\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\psi(x_{s})\,ds\right)\right]\leq\phi^{-2}(x),

provided

-L\log\phi+2|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}\leq\psi.

This allows the boundary local time to be absorbed into a Feynman–Kac weight.

Combining the conformal change, the local-time estimate and the
Feynman–Kac Bismut formula, we obtain a non-convex boundary gradient estimate.
If

\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits_{Z}\geq-h,\qquad\mathrm{II}\geq-\sigma,

and if there exists
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(M)
(see Definition
4.1
below for the definition of
\mathcal{D}(M)
) satisfying

N\log\phi\geq\sigma,\qquad-L\log\phi+2|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}\leq\psi,

then, under the moment assumptions

\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[|h+\psi|^{2}(x_{s})]\leq\alpha_{0}(x),\qquad\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[|\nabla(h+\psi)|^{2}(x_{s})]\leq\alpha_{1}(x),

we prove

|\nabla P_{T}f|(x)\leq\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\min\{T,1\}}}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}+\sqrt{\alpha_{0}(x)}+\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{\alpha_{0}(x)\alpha_{1}(x)}\right]\|f\|_{\infty}.

Under additional geometric assumptions near the boundary, Wang’s construction

[
30
,
29
,
32
]
gives an explicit conformal factor

\phi
, and hence an explicit non-convex boundary estimate.

Finally, we apply these semigroup estimates to Neumann eigenfunctions. Let

u\in C^{2}(M)
satisfy

Lu=-\lambda u\quad\text{in }M^{\circ},\qquad Nu|_{\partial M}=0,\qquad\lambda>0.

Since
P_{t}
is generated by
\frac{1}{2}L
, one has

P_{t}u={\rm e}^{-\lambda t/2}u,

and therefore

|\nabla u|(x)={\rm e}^{\lambda T/2}|\nabla P_{T}u|(x).

Choosing
T=\lambda^{-1}
gives pointwise gradient estimates for Neumann
eigenfunctions. Related gradient and Hessian estimates on compact manifolds
with boundary were obtained in

[
2
,
6
]
. In our setting,
both convex and non-convex boundary cases are covered, and the leading
high-frequency term is of order

\sqrt{\lambda}\,\|u\|_{\infty}.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we establish Bismut-type
formulas for Feynman–Kac semigroups on manifolds with boundary. In
Section 3 we study the convex boundary case and derive pointwise gradient
estimates under unbounded curvature lower bounds. In Section 4 we treat
non-convex boundaries by conformal change and a boundary local-time estimate.
Finally, Section 5 applies the semigroup estimates to Neumann eigenfunctions.

Notation convention.

Throughout this paper, we distinguish the exterior differential from ordinary
time, stochastic, and local-time differentials. The exterior differential of a
function is denoted by the upright roman symbol
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}
; for example,

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}f
,
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V
, and
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{t}^{V}f
are one-forms. By contrast, the
usual differentials in stochastic calculus and time integration are kept in the
standard italic notation, such as
dB_{t}
,
dt
, and
dl_{t}
. The covariant
differential along the reflecting diffusion is denoted by
{\bf D}
.

2
Bismut-Type Formula for Feynman–Kac Semigroups

The usual Kato class
\mathcal{K}(M)
is defined as the set of functions
f
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\sup_{x\in M}\int_{0}^{\alpha}\mathbb{E}^{x}\bigl[|f(x_{s})|\bigr]\,ds=0,

(2.1)

which plays an important role in the study of Schrödinger
operators and their semigroups, see Simon
[
23
]
and the references therein. Under standard local heat-kernel estimates one has useful
L^{p}
-criteria for membership in the Kato class; see the cited references
[
12
,
13
]
.
By
[
13
, Lemma 3.9]
, it was proved that if
f\in\mathcal{K}(M)
, there exist constants

c,\,C
such that for each
t>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}^{x}\Bigl[{\rm e}^{\int_{0}^{t}|f|(x_{s})\,ds}\Bigr]\leq C{\rm e}^{ct}.

To study the Neumann Feynman–Kac semigroup, we introduce the following function space.
Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{N}(L_{V})

\displaystyle=\left\{f\in C^{2}(M)\cap\mathcal{B}_{b}(M):\ Nf|_{\partial M}=0,\ \frac{1}{2}Lf-Vf\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)\right\}.

We first introduce some properties for the Neumann Feynman–Kac semigroup
P_{\cdot}^{V}
:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}_{N}(L_{V})
, and
V^{-}\in\mathcal{K}(M)
and
V\in C^{1}(M)
. Then,

(1)

\frac{d}{dt}P_{t}^{V}f=P^{V}_{t}(\frac{1}{2}L-V)f=(\frac{1}{2}L-V)P^{V}_{t}f,\quad t>0;

(2)

NP_{t}^{V}f|_{\partial M}=0,\quad t>0.

Proof.

We first justify the regularity of the Feynman–Kac semigroup used below.
Since
V^{-}\in\mathcal{K}(M)
, the Khasminskii-type estimate for Kato
potentials implies that, for every
T>0
,

\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}^{x}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{T}V^{-}(x_{s})\,ds\right)<\infty.

Consequently the Feynman–Kac semigroup

P_{t}^{V}f(x)=\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}V(x_{s})\,ds\right)f(x_{t})\right]

is well defined on bounded measurable functions. Moreover, by the Markov
property of the reflecting diffusion,
P_{t}^{V}
satisfies the Duhamel formula

P_{t}^{V}f=P_{t}f-\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}\big(VP_{s}^{V}f\big)\,ds.

Thus
u(t,x):=P_{t}^{V}f(x)
is a mild solution of the Neumann parabolic
problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=\left(\frac{1}{2}L-V\right)u,&x\in M^{\circ},\ t>0,\\
Nu=0,&x\in\partial M,\ t>0,\\
u(0,\cdot)=f.\end{cases}

The Kato condition and the associated Feynman–Kac semigroups are classical;
see, for instance, Simon
[
23
]
and Güneysu
[
12
]
.
Since
V\in C^{1}(M)
and the boundary is smooth, standard parabolic
regularity for Neumann boundary value problems upgrades this mild solution
to a strong solution on
(0,T]\times M
; see
Ouhabaz
[
18
]
, Ladyzhenskaya–Solonnikov–Ural’tseva
[
15
]
,
and Lunardi
[
17
]
.
In particular, for

f\in\mathcal{C}_{N}(L_{V})
, one has

u\in C^{1,2}((0,T]\times M).

It remains to prove the first equality in (1). This follows from the identity
P_{t}^{V}f=f+\int_{0}^{t}P_{s}^{V}\big(\frac{1}{2}L-V\big)f\,ds
, which is implied by Itô’s formula applied to

\displaystyle d\Big[{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{t}V(x_{s})\,ds}f(x_{t})\Big]

\displaystyle\overset{m}{=}{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{t}V(x_{s})\,ds}\Big(-V(x_{t})f(x_{t})+\frac{1}{2}Lf(x_{t})\Big)\,dt.

∎

To state the main result, we first recall the construction of the multiplicative functional

Q_{\bullet}
appearing in the Bismut formula; see
Hsu
[
14
]
and

[
32
, Theorem 1.1]
for the case of manifolds with boundary.

For
t\geq 0
, let
/\!/_{0\to t}:T_{x}M\to T_{x_{t}}M
denote stochastic
parallel transport along the paths of the reflecting diffusion process

\{x_{\cdot}\}
. The covariant differential
{\bf D}
is defined by

{\bf D}:=/\!/_{0\to t}\,d\,/\!/_{t\to 0},

where
d
is the usual Itô stochastic differential in the time variable.
Let
D\Subset M
be a relatively compact regular domain with
x\in D
, and
let

\tau:=\inf\{t\geq 0:x_{t}\notin D\}

be the first exit time of
x_{t}
from
D
. By the curvature assumptions below,
there exist non-negative constants
K_{0},\sigma_{0}
such that

K\geq-K_{0}
on
D
and
\sigma\geq-\sigma_{0}
on
\partial M\cap D
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

Q_{t\wedge\tau}^{(n)}:T_{x}M\to T_{x_{t\wedge\tau}}M
solve, up to the
stopping time
\tau
, the covariant differential equation

{\bf D}Q_{t}^{(n)}=-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits_{Z}^{\sharp}(Q_{t}^{(n)})\,dt+\mathrm{II}^{\sharp}(Q_{t}^{(n)})\,dl_{t}+nP_{N}(Q_{t}^{(n)})\,dl_{t}\right\},\qquad Q_{0}^{(n)}=\mathrm{id}.

(2.2)

Here
\mathrm{id}
is the identity map on
T_{x}M
and for
x_{t}\in\partial M
,

P_{N}(Q_{t}^{(n)})
denotes the projection onto the normal
direction, namely

P_{N}(Q_{t}^{(n)}v)=\langle Q_{t}^{(n)}v,N(x_{t})\rangle N(x_{t}),\qquad v\in T_{x}M.

Moreover, for
x_{t}\in\partial M
, let

P_{\partial}:T_{x_{t}}M\to T_{x_{t}}\partial M
be the tangential projection. The
operator
\mathrm{II}^{\sharp}
is defined by

\langle\mathrm{II}^{\sharp}u,w\rangle=\mathrm{II}(P_{\partial}u,P_{\partial}w),\qquad u,w\in T_{x_{t}}M,

and
\mathrm{II}^{\sharp}(Q_{t}^{(n)})
in (
2.2
) means

\mathrm{II}^{\sharp}(Q_{t}^{(n)}v)
for
v\in T_{x}M
.

For every fixed
T>0
, the preceding construction gives

\sup_{n\geq 1,\,s\in[0,T]}|Q_{s\wedge\tau}^{(n)}|\leq\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}K_{0}(T\wedge\tau)+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{0}l_{T\wedge\tau}\right),

(2.3)

and

\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau}|P_{N}(Q_{s}^{(n)})|^{2}\,dl_{s}\leq\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau}|Q_{s}^{(n)}|^{2}\{K_{0}\,ds+\sigma_{0}\,dl_{s}\}\longrightarrow 0.

(2.4)

as
n\to\infty
. On the other hand, by
[
32
, Lemma 3.1.2]
,

\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\exp\left(\lambda l_{T\wedge\tau}\right)\right]<\infty,\qquad\lambda>0.

Therefore the right-hand side of (
2.3
) is integrable on compact
time intervals.

By the proof of
[
32
, Theorem 1.1]
, there exists a progressively
measurable multiplicative functional
Q_{\cdot\wedge\tau}
such that, after
passing to a subsequence
n_{k}
, the sequence
Q^{(n_{k})}
converges to
Q

in Hsu’s weak sense. More precisely, after identifying tangent spaces along

x_{\cdot}
by stochastic parallel transport, for all bounded adapted
T_{x}M
-valued processes
\xi_{s},\eta_{s}
and every bounded
T_{x}M
-valued random variables

\xi,\eta
, one has

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{x}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau}\left\langle\bigl(Q_{s}^{(n_{k})}-Q_{s}\bigr)\xi_{s},\eta_{s}\right\rangle\,ds=0,

and, for every fixed
t\in[0,T]
,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{x}\left\langle\bigl(Q_{t\wedge\tau}^{(n_{k})}-Q_{t\wedge\tau}\bigr)\xi,\eta\right\rangle=0.

This weak convergence is sufficient for passing to the limit in the
stochastic-integral identities below, after localization. The uniform estimate
(
2.3
), together with the Itô isometry, provides the required
square-integrability and uniform bounds for the martingale terms. Moreover, the
penalization term
nP_{N}(Q_{t}^{(n)}(v))\,dl_{t}
forces the normal component to
vanish in the limit:

\langle Q_{t}(v),N(x_{t})\rangle=0,\qquad dl_{t}\text{-a.e. on }[0,\tau],\qquad v\in T_{x}M.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
D\Subset M
be a relatively compact regular domain with
x\in D
, and
let
\tau
be the first exit time of
x_{t}
from
D
.
Assume that there exist
K\in C(D)
and

\sigma\in C(\partial M\cap D)
such that

\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits_{Z}\geq K,\qquad\mathrm{II}\geq\sigma\quad\hbox{on \ }D.

Then there exists a progressively measurable multiplicative functional

Q_{t\wedge\tau}:T_{x}M\to T_{x_{t\wedge\tau}}M

such that

\langle Q_{t}(v),N(x_{t})\rangle=0,\qquad dl_{t}\hbox{-a.e. on \ }[0,\tau],

and

|Q_{t\wedge\tau}|\leq\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}K(x_{s})\,ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\sigma(x_{s})\,dl_{s}\right),

and for
V\in C^{1}(M)
and
V^{-}\in\mathcal{K}(M)
, for every

f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)
,
v\in T_{x}M
, and every non-negative adapted process

k\in L^{1,2}([0,T];\mathbb{P})
satisfying

k_{0}=1,\ k_{s}=0\ \ \hbox{for }s\geq T\wedge\tau,

one has

\displaystyle\langle\nabla P_{T}^{V}f,\,v\rangle=

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\mathbb{V}_{T}f(x_{T})\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau}\left\langle Q_{t}(\dot{k}_{t}v),/\!/_{t}\,dB_{t}\right\rangle\right]-\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\mathbb{V}_{T}f(x_{T})\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(k_{t}Q_{t}(v))\,dt\right].

(2.5)

Proof.

We first prove the formula for
f\in\mathcal{C}_{N}(L_{V})
with sufficiently
bounded derivatives. The general case
f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)
will be obtained
by a regularization argument. All the following estimates are first made
up to the localized time
T\wedge\tau
. On the relatively compact domain

D
, the functions
V
and
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V
are bounded from above in absolute
value, while the negative part of
V
is controlled globally by the Kato
assumption. Thus the localization separates the purely local regularity
needed for differentiating
V
from the global integrability needed for the
Feynman–Kac weight.
Indeed, for
\varepsilon\in(0,T)
, we apply the formula with time

T-\varepsilon
to the regularized function
P_{\varepsilon}^{V}f
. By the
semigroup property,

P_{T}^{V}f=P_{T-\varepsilon}^{V}(P_{\varepsilon}^{V}f).

The function
P_{\varepsilon}^{V}f
has the required local regularity on

(0,T]\times D
. Moreover, since
V^{-}\in\mathcal{K}(M)
, the
Khasminskii estimate for Kato potentials give

\sup_{x\in D}\mathbb{E}^{x}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{T}V(x_{s})^{-}\,ds\right)<\infty.

Consequently, for bounded
f
,

Lemma 3.3

\langle\nabla P_{T}^{h}f,v\rangle=\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{H}_{T}f(x_{T})\int_{0}^{T}\langle Q_{s}(v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\right]-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{H}_{T}f(x_{T})\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla h,Q_{s}(v)\rangle\frac{T-s}{T}\,ds\right].

For the first term, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Itô isometry:

\left|\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{H}_{T}f(x_{T})\int_{0}^{T}\langle Q_{s}(v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\right]\right|\leq(P_{T}f^{2})^{1/2}\cdot\left(\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{T^{2}}ds\right)^{1/2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}(P_{T}f^{2})^{1/2}.

For the second term involving
\nabla h
, we again apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality:

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{H}_{T}f(x_{T})\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla h,Q_{s}(v)\rangle\frac{T-s}{T}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T}{4}(P_{T}f^{2})^{1/2}\cdot\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\bigl(\mathbb{E}^{x}|\nabla h|^{2}(x_{s})\bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T}{4}\sqrt{\theta_{1}(x)}(P_{T}f^{2})^{1/2}.

Combining both estimates yields the desired inequality.
∎

With the aid of the preceding lemmas, we are now in a position to introduce our main result in this subsection.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume Condition
(C1)
holds for some
h\in C^{1}(M)
satisfying

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[h^{2}(x_{s})]\leq\theta_{0}(x),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[|\nabla h|^{2}(x_{s})]\leq\theta_{1}(x).

Then for every fixed
T>0
, for any
f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)
and
x\in M
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla P_{T}f|(x)\leq\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\min\{T,1\}}}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\theta_{1}(x)}+\sqrt{\theta_{0}(x)}+\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{\theta_{0}(x)\theta_{1}(x)}\right]\|f\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

Assume
T\in(0,1]
. Otherwise, we let
g=P_{T-1}f\in C_{b}^{\infty}(M)
for
f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)
and
P_{T}f=P_{1}g
.
Moreover,
\|g\|_{\infty}=\|P_{T-1}f\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
.

We begin by employing the following variation of constants formula to derive gradient estimates for
P_{t}f
:

P_{T}f=P_{T}^{h}f+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}P_{T-s}^{h}(hP_{s}f)\,ds,

(3.7)

where
h
serves as the lower Bakry–Émery curvature bound in (
3.1
). This identity is first obtained for
f\in C_{N}(L_{h/2})
by Duhamel’s formula for the generators
\frac{1}{2}L
and
\frac{1}{2}(L-h)
, and then extended to
f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)
by bounded monotone-class approximation. Since
h\geq 0
, the Feynman–Kac weight
\mathbb{H}_{t}\leq 1
, so no additional exponential integrability is needed for this Duhamel step.

From (
3.7
), we obtain that for
v\in T_{x}M
with
|v|=1
:

\langle\nabla P_{T}f,v\rangle(x)=\langle\nabla P_{T}^{h}f,v\rangle(x)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla P_{T-s}^{h}(hP_{s}f),v\rangle(x)\,ds.

By Lemma
3.3
, we see that

\displaystyle\langle\nabla P_{T}f,v\rangle(x)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla P_{T}^{h}f,v\rangle(x)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla P_{T-s}^{h}(hP_{s}f),v\rangle(x)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\theta_{1}(x)}T\right)(P_{T}f^{2})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T-s}}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\theta_{1}(x)}(T-s)\right)(P_{T-s}(hP_{s}f)^{2})^{1/2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\theta_{1}(x)}T\right)\|f\|_{\infty}+\frac{\sqrt{\theta_{0}(x)}}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T-s}}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\theta_{1}(x)}(T-s)\right)ds\,\|f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\theta_{1}(x)}T+\sqrt{\theta_{0}(x)T}+\frac{T^{2}}{8}\sqrt{\theta_{1}(x)\theta_{0}(x)}\right]\|f\|_{\infty}.

∎

Remark 3.5
.

In particular, when the lower bound
h
is a constant, the main result reduces to the following statement.
Assume Condition
(C1)
holds with a nonnegative constant
h=K
.
For a fixed
T>0
and
x\in M
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla P_{T}f|(x)\leq\Bigl(K+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\min\{T,1\}}}\Bigr)\,\|f\|_{\infty}.

(3.8)

Under the same hypothesis, the classical Bismut formula for
\nabla P_{t}f
, together with
|Q_{s}|\leq{\rm e}^{Ks/2}
when
\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits_{Z}\geq-K
, yields

\displaystyle|\nabla P_{T}f|(x)\leq\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{\int_{0}^{T}{\rm e}^{-Ks}\,ds}},

after the usual energy-minimizing choice of
k
.
Comparing this with inequality (
3.8
), we observe that the bound derived from the Bismut formula is sharper.
This indicates that although the approach adopted in this paper can readily handle pointwise lower Ricci bounds, it entails a slight loss of sharpness in the estimate.

3.2
Explicit Examples under Radial Curvature Growth

Let
g\in C^{1}([1,\infty))
be a positive non-decreasing function. Assume
that

\sup_{r\geq 0}\left\{\frac{1+\sqrt{\ell(r)}\,r}{g(r^{2}+1)}+\frac{r^{2}}{g(r^{2}+1)^{2}}\right\}<\infty.

(3.9)

Let

f(r)=M_{0}\exp\left(a_{0}\int_{1}^{r}\frac{ds}{g(s)}\right),\qquad M_{0}>0,\quad a_{0}>0.

(3.10)

We assume that the function
h
appearing in Condition
(C1)
is given by

h(x)=\sqrt{f(\rho_{o}(x)^{2}+1)}.

For
r>1
, we have

f^{\prime}(r)=\frac{a_{0}f(r)}{g(r)}

and

f^{\prime\prime}(r)=\frac{a_{0}^{2}f(r)}{g(r)^{2}}-\frac{a_{0}f(r)g^{\prime}(r)}{g(r)^{2}}\leq\frac{a_{0}^{2}f(r)}{g(r)^{2}},

where we used
g^{\prime}(r)\geq 0
.

For brevity, we write
\rho_{o}(\cdot)=\rho(o,\cdot)
. By Itô’s formula for
the radial process and the Laplacian comparison estimate

L\rho_{o}^{2}\leq m+\sqrt{m^{2}+4m\ell(\rho_{o})\rho_{o}^{2}},

we obtain, outside the cut-locus and hence everywhere by the usual Calabi
argument,

\displaystyle df(\rho_{o}(x_{t})^{2}+1)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{m}}{\leq}\frac{1}{2}\left[f^{\prime}(\rho_{o}(x_{t})^{2}+1)L\rho_{o}(x_{t})^{2}+f^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{o}(x_{t})^{2}+1)|\nabla\rho_{o}(x_{t})^{2}|^{2}\right]dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}f(\rho_{o}(x_{t})^{2}+1)\Biggl[\frac{a_{0}\left(m+\sqrt{m^{2}+4m\ell(\rho_{o}(x_{t}))\rho_{o}(x_{t})^{2}}\right)}{g(\rho_{o}(x_{t})^{2}+1)}+\frac{4a_{0}^{2}\rho_{o}(x_{t})^{2}}{g(\rho_{o}(x_{t})^{2}+1)^{2}}\Biggr]dt.

By (
3.9
), there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
, depending only on

a_{0},m,\ell
and
g
, such that

df(\rho_{o}(x_{t})^{2}+1)\overset{\text{m}}{\leq}C_{1}f(\rho_{o}(x_{t})^{2}+1)\,dt.

Since the preceding differential inequality is understood modulo the
differential of a local martingale, we justify the passage to expectations by
localization. Let

\tau_{n}:=\inf\{s\geq 0:\rho_{o}(x_{s})\geq n\}.

After possibly replacing
\tau_{n}
by a smaller localizing sequence, the
local martingale part stopped at
\tau_{n}
is a true martingale. Applying the
preceding inequality to
t\wedge\tau_{n}
, we get

\mathbb{E}^{x}f(\rho_{o}(x_{t\wedge\tau_{n}})^{2}+1)\leq f(\rho_{o}(x)^{2}+1)+C_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}^{x}f(\rho_{o}(x_{s\wedge\tau_{n}})^{2}+1)\,ds.

By Gronwall’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}^{x}f(\rho_{o}(x_{t\wedge\tau_{n}})^{2}+1)\leq{\rm e}^{C_{1}t}f(\rho_{o}(x)^{2}+1).

Letting
n\to\infty
and using Fatou’s lemma yields

\mathbb{E}^{x}f(\rho_{o}(x_{t})^{2}+1)\leq{\rm e}^{C_{1}t}f(\rho_{o}(x)^{2}+1).

(3.11)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\left|\nabla\sqrt{f(\rho_{o}(\cdot)^{2}+1)}\right|^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left|f^{\prime}(\rho_{o}(x)^{2}+1)\nabla\rho_{o}(\cdot)^{2}\right|^{2}(x)}{4f(\rho_{o}(x)^{2}+1)}

\displaystyle=a_{0}^{2}\rho_{o}(x)^{2}\frac{f(\rho_{o}(x)^{2}+1)}{g(\rho_{o}(x)^{2}+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}f(\rho_{o}(x)^{2}+1)

for some constant
C_{2}>0
. Consequently, by (
3.11
),

\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\left|\nabla\sqrt{f(\rho_{o}(\cdot)^{2}+1)}\right|^{2}(x_{t})\right]\leq C_{2}{\rm e}^{C_{1}t}f(\rho_{o}(x)^{2}+1).

(3.12)

Combining (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) with
Theorem
3.4
, we obtain the following estimate.

Theorem 3.6
.

Assume Condition
(C1)
holds with

h(x)=\sqrt{f(\rho_{o}(x)^{2}+1)},

where
f
is defined by (
3.10
). Then, for every
T>0
,

x\in M
, and
u\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)
,

\displaystyle|\nabla P_{T}u|(x)\leq\Bigg[

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\min\{T,1\}}}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{C_{2}}}{4}+1\right){\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}C_{1}}h(x)+\frac{\sqrt{C_{2}}}{8}{\rm e}^{C_{1}}h(x)^{2}\Bigg]\|u\|_{\infty}.

In particular, suppose that the curvature lower bound is of the form

\ell(r)=K_{0}(1+r^{2})^{\gamma},\qquad K_{0}>0.

If
\gamma\in[0,1)
, we may take

g(r)=r^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2}}.

Then

f(r)=M_{0}\exp\left(\frac{2a_{0}}{1-\gamma}\left(r^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}-1\right)\right).

If
\gamma=1
, we may take
g(r)=r
, and then

f(r)=M_{0}r^{a_{0}}.

Thus we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.7
.

Assume Condition
(C1)
holds with

\ell(\rho_{o})=K_{0}(1+\rho_{o}^{2})^{\gamma},\ \gamma\in[0,1).

Suppose

h(x)=C\exp\left(a(1+\rho_{o}(x)^{2})^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}\right)

for some constants
K_{0},\,C,\,a>0
. Then there exist positive constants

C_{1}=C_{1}(a,K_{0},m,\gamma)
and

C_{2}=C_{2}(a,K_{0},\gamma)
such that for every

,

, and

,

\displaystyle|\nabla P_{T}u|(x)\leq\Biggl[

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\min\{T,1\}}}+C\left(\frac{\sqrt{C_{2}}}{4}+1\right)\,\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}C_{1}+a(1+\rho_{o}(x)^{2})^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{C^{2}\sqrt{C_{2}}}{8}\exp\left(C_{1}+2a(1+\rho_{o}(x)^{2})^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}\right)\Biggr]\,\|u\|_{\infty}.

If Condition
(C1)
holds with

and

for some constants

, then there exist positive constants

C_{1}=C_{1}(K_{0},m,p)
and

such that for every
T>0
,

x\in M
, and

,

\displaystyle|\nabla P_{T}u|(x)\leq\Biggl[

4
Gradient Estimates for Neumann Semigroups with Non-Convex Boundary

4.1
A Boundary Local-Time Estimate

Before proving the main gradient estimate when the boundary is non-convex, we establish an auxiliary estimate involving the local time of the reflecting diffusion. To this end, we introduce the following set,

Definition 4.1
.

We denote by
\mathcal{D}(M)
the class of all positive functions

\phi\in C^{2}(M^{\circ})\cap C^{1}(M)
such that

1\leq\inf_{M}\phi\leq\sup_{M}\phi<\infty,

and such that
L\log\phi
is well defined in
M^{\circ}
and

N\log\phi
is well defined on
\partial M
. Moreover, we assume that

(L\log\varphi)^{-},\,|\nabla\log\varphi|^{2}\in\mathcal{K}(M),\ \ \mbox{and}\ \displaystyle\varlimsup_{\rho_{o}(x)\to\infty}\frac{(L\log\phi)^{-}+|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}}{1+\rho_{o}(x)^{2}}<\infty.

This condition is automatically satisfied, for instance, if

(L\log\phi)^{-}+|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}
is bounded, or if it depends only on

\rho_{\partial}
and has at most quadratic growth in

\rho_{\partial}
, where
\rho_{\partial}
is the distance to the boundary
\partial M
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\sigma\in C(\partial M)
be non-negative. Suppose that there exists

\phi\in\mathcal{D}(M)
such that

N\log\phi\geq\sigma\quad\hbox{on }\partial M.

Let
\psi
be a non-negative measurable function satisfying

-L\log\phi+2|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}\leq\psi\quad\hbox{on }M^{\circ}.

Then, for every
t\geq 0
and
x\in M
,

\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\phi^{-2}(x_{t})\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(x_{s})\,dl_{s}\right)\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\psi(x_{s})\,ds\right)\right]\leq\phi^{-2}(x).

Proof.

Set
g=\log\phi
and define

Y_{t}=\phi^{-2}(x_{t})\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(x_{s})\,dl_{s}\right)\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\psi(x_{s})\,ds\right).

Equivalently,

Y_{t}={\rm e}^{-2g(x_{t})}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(x_{s})\,dl_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\psi(x_{s})\,ds\right).

By Itô’s formula, modulo the differential of a local martingale,

\displaystyle dY_{t}\overset{m}{=}

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle e}^{\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(x_{s})\,dl_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\psi(x_{s})\,ds}\left[\frac{1}{2}L\phi^{-2}-\psi\phi^{-2}\right](x_{t})\,dt

\displaystyle+{\rm e}^{\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(x_{s})\,dl_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\psi(x_{s})\,ds}\left[\frac{1}{2}N\phi^{-2}+\sigma\phi^{-2}\right](x_{t})\,dl_{t}.

Since
\phi^{-2}={\rm e}^{-2g}
, we have

\frac{1}{2}L\phi^{-2}=\phi^{-2}\left(-Lg+2|\nabla g|^{2}\right).

Hence

\frac{1}{2}L\phi^{-2}-\psi\phi^{-2}=\phi^{-2}\left(-L\log\phi+2|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}-\psi\right)\leq 0.

Moreover,

\frac{1}{2}N\phi^{-2}=-\phi^{-2}N\log\phi.

Therefore

\frac{1}{2}N\phi^{-2}+\sigma\phi^{-2}=\phi^{-2}\left(\sigma-N\log\phi\right)\leq 0\quad\hbox{on }\partial M.

Consequently
Y_{t}
is a non-negative local supermartingale.

Let
\tau_{n}
be a localizing sequence. Then

\mathbb{E}^{x}Y_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\leq Y_{0}=\phi^{-2}(x).

Letting
n\to\infty
and using Fatou’s lemma, we obtain

\mathbb{E}^{x}Y_{t}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{x}Y_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\leq\phi^{-2}(x).

This proves the desired estimate.
∎

4.2
Conformal Reduction to the Convex Boundary Case

We begin by introducing a curvature-dimension condition which allows the lower bound to decay in a controlled manner with respect to distance.

Condition (C2)
:
There exists a non‑negative function
\ell\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
such that
\ell(r)\leq c_{0}(1+r^{2})
for some nonnegative constant
c_{0}
, and

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits_{Z}^{m,d}(p):=\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits(p)-\nabla Z^{\flat}(p)-\frac{Z\otimes Z}{m-d}(p)\geq-\ell\bigl(\rho_{o}(p)\bigr).

(4.1)

Moreover, there exist non‑negative functions
h\in C^{1}(M)
and
\sigma\in C^{1}(\partial M)
such that outside the cut locus

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits_{Z}\geq-h,\qquad\mathrm{II}\geq-\sigma.

The next result shows that Condition
(C2)
, together with a suitable
boundary condition on the conformal factor
\phi
, allows us to reduce the
non-convex boundary case to the convex one. We use the conformally changed
metric

g^{\prime}=\phi^{-2}g

and denote by
\rho^{\prime}
the corresponding geodesic distance. The conformal
transformation formulas used below are standard; see, for example,

[
32
, Section 3.2]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume Condition
(C2)
. Suppose that there exists

\phi\in\mathcal{D}(M)
such that

N\log\phi\geq\sigma\quad\hbox{on }\ \partial M.

Let

\tau_{n}:=\inf\{s\geq 0:\rho^{\prime}(x,x_{s})\geq n\}.

Then there exists a sequence of absolutely continuous adapted processes

\{k_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
such that

k_{n}(0)=1,\qquad k_{n}(s)=0\quad\hbox{for }s\geq t\wedge\tau_{n},

and such that, for some constant
c>0
,

\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|\dot{k}_{n}(s)|^{2}\,ds\right]\leq\frac{1}{t}+c.

Proof.

We first show that the conformal change removes the boundary
non-convexity. Under the conformal metric
g^{\prime}=\phi^{-2}g
, the second
fundamental form satisfies

\mathrm{II}^{\prime}=\phi^{-1}\bigl(\mathrm{II}+(N\log\phi)g_{\partial M}\bigr).

Since
\mathrm{II}\geq-\sigma
and
N\log\phi\geq\sigma
, it follows that

\mathrm{II}^{\prime}\geq 0.

Thus the boundary is convex with respect to
g^{\prime}
.

Next we express the generator in the conformally changed metric. Since

\Delta^{\prime}=\phi^{2}\bigl(\Delta-(d-2)\langle\nabla\log\phi,\nabla\cdot\rangle\bigr),

we have

L=\Delta+Z=\phi^{-2}(\Delta^{\prime}+Z^{\prime}),

where

Z^{\prime}=\phi^{2}\bigl(Z+(d-2)\nabla\log\phi\bigr).

Equivalently,

L=\phi^{-2}L^{\prime},\qquad L^{\prime}:=\Delta^{\prime}+Z^{\prime}.

We now check the curvature condition for
L^{\prime}
. Let
\dot{\gamma}
be a
unit vector with respect to
g^{\prime}
. Then
|\dot{\gamma}|=\phi
with respect
to
g
. By the standard conformal transformation formula for the
Bakry–Emery curvature, there exists a constant
C=C(m,d)>0
such that

\displaystyle(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits^{Z^{\prime}})^{\prime}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})-\frac{\langle Z^{\prime},\dot{\gamma}\rangle^{\prime 2}}{2(m-d)}\geq-\phi^{2}\ell(\rho_{o})+\frac{1}{2}L\phi^{2}-C|\nabla\phi|^{2}.

Using

\frac{1}{2}L\phi^{2}=\phi^{2}\left(L\log\phi+2|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}\right),\qquad|\nabla\phi|^{2}=\phi^{2}|\nabla\log\phi|^{2},

we obtain

\displaystyle(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits^{Z^{\prime}})^{\prime}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})-\frac{\langle Z^{\prime},\dot{\gamma}\rangle^{\prime 2}}{2(m-d)}

\displaystyle\geq-\phi^{2}\left[\ell(\rho_{o})+(L\log\phi)^{-}+C|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}\right].

Since
1\leq\inf_{M}\phi\leq\sup_{M}\phi<\infty
, the distances
\rho_{o}
and

\rho^{\prime}_{o}
are equivalent:

\frac{1}{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}\rho_{o}\leq\rho^{\prime}_{o}\leq\rho_{o}.

By the growth assumption in Condition
(C2)
and the defining properties
of
\mathcal{D}(M)
, the lower bound above has the same quadratic growth
control, in terms of
\rho^{\prime}_{o}
, as required in Condition
(C1)
. Hence
the conformal operator
L^{\prime}
satisfies the convex-boundary curvature
condition with dimension
2m-d
.

Therefore Lemma
3.2
can be applied to the
L^{\prime}
-diffusion stopped
upon leaving the
g^{\prime}
-ball. Since
L=\phi^{-2}L^{\prime}
and
\phi
is bounded
above and below by positive constants, the corresponding
L
-diffusion is a
bounded time change of the
L^{\prime}
-diffusion. Consequently the stopping times
defined by
g^{\prime}
-balls and the energy estimates for the cut-off processes
are preserved up to a multiplicative constant. Thus there exists a sequence

\{k_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfying

k_{n}(0)=1,\qquad k_{n}(s)=0\quad\hbox{for }s\geq t\wedge\tau_{n},

and

\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|\dot{k}_{n}(s)|^{2}\,ds\right]\leq\frac{1}{t}+c

for some constant
c>0
. The proof is complete.
∎

4.3
A Bismut Formula for Feynman–Kac Semigroups after Conformal Reduction

Assume that there exists a non-negative function
\psi\in C^{1}(M)
such that

-L\log\phi+2|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}\leq\psi
. Let

V=\frac{1}{2}(h+\psi)

in what follows.
We now derive a pointwise gradient estimate for
P_{t}^{V}f
under the integrability assumptions on
V
and its gradient.

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume Condition
(C2)
holds for some non-negative functions
h\in C^{1}(M)
and
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(M)
such that
N\log\phi\geq\sigma
on the boundary. If there exists a non-negative function
\psi\in C^{1}(M)
and
\alpha_{1}\in C(M)
such that

-L\log\phi+2|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}\leq\psi
and

\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\left|\nabla(h+\psi)\right|^{2}(x_{s})\right]\leq\alpha_{1}(x),

then there exists
Q_{t}:T_{x}M\to T_{x_{t}^{x}}M
such that

\displaystyle\langle Q_{t}(v),N\rangle|_{\partial M}=0,\ \ v\in T_{x}M\ \ \mbox{and}\quad|Q_{t}|\leq{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}h(x_{s})\,ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(x_{s})\,dl_{s}},

and for
k\in C^{1}([0,T])
such that
k(0)=1
and
k(T)=0
,

\langle\nabla P_{T}^{V}f,v\rangle=-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{V}_{T}f(x_{T})\int_{0}^{T}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{V}_{T}f(x_{T})\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla V,Q_{s}(k(s)v)\rangle\,ds\right].

Proof.

We begin with the discussion as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
. For
v\in T_{x}M
and

k_{n}\in L^{1,2}([0,T];\mathbb{P})
a sequence of non-negative processes as in Lemma
4.3
such that
k_{n}(0)=1
and

k_{n}(s)=0
for
s\geq T\wedge\tau_{B^{\prime}(x,n)}
,

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}^{V}f(v)

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{V}_{T}f(x_{T})\int_{0}^{T}\langle Q_{t}(\dot{k}_{n}(t)v),/\!/_{t}\,dB_{t}\rangle\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{V}_{T}f(x_{T})\int_{0}^{T}k_{n}(t)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(Q_{t}(v))\,dt\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\,\mathbb{E}\Biggl[{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{T}V(x_{s})ds}{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}h(x_{s})ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma(x_{s})dl_{s}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\Bigl|\int_{0}^{T}\langle{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s}h(x_{s})ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s}\sigma(x_{s})dl_{s}}Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\Bigr|\Biggr]

\displaystyle\qquad+\|f\|_{\infty}\,\mathbb{E}\Biggl[{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{T}V(x_{s})ds}{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}h(x_{s})ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma(x_{s})dl_{s}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\int_{0}^{T}{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s}h(x_{s})ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s}\sigma(x_{s})dl_{s}}Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v)|\nabla V|(x_{s})\,ds\Biggr].

(4.2)

We first see that

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{T}V(x_{s})ds}{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}h(x_{s})ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma(x_{s})dl_{s}}

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma(x_{s})dl_{s}}{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\psi(x_{s})\,ds},

and by Lemma
4.3
, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\mathbb{V}_{T}Q_{t\wedge\tau_{B^{\prime}(x,n)}}|^{2}]^{1/2}\leq\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}^{V}f|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{V}_{T}f(x_{T})\int_{0}^{T}\langle Q_{t}(\dot{k}_{n}(t)v),/\!/_{t}\,dB_{t}\rangle\right]\right|+\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{V}_{T}f(x_{T})\int_{0}^{T}k_{n}(t)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(Q_{t}(v))\,dt\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\dot{k}_{n}(t)|^{2}\,dt\right]^{1/2}\|f\|_{\infty}+\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}T\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[|\nabla V|^{2}(x_{s})\Big]\,\|f\|_{\infty}.

By Lemma
4.3
, we know that

\displaystyle|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P_{T}^{V}f|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\|f\|_{\infty}\left[\Bigl(\frac{1}{T}+c\Bigr)^{1/2}+T\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|\nabla V|^{2}(x_{s})\right],

which implies that

|\nabla P_{\cdot}^{V}f|
is bounded in
[\epsilon,T]\times M
for small
\epsilon>0
.
This estimate justifies the
passage to the limit and the removal of the localization with the same reason as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
.

∎

From the Bismut formula above, we obtain the following gradient estimate for
P^{V}_{t}f
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume Condition
(C2)
holds for some non-negative functions
h\in C^{1}(M)
and
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(M)
such that
N\log\phi\geq\sigma
on the boundary. If there exists non-negative function
\psi\in C^{1}(M)
such that
-L\log\phi+2|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}\leq\psi
and

\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\left|\nabla(h+\psi)\right|^{2}(x_{s})\right]\leq\alpha_{1}(x),

then for any
T>0
,

|\nabla P_{T}^{V}f|(x)\leq\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{T}{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}\right)\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\,\bigl(P_{T}f^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}(x).

Proof.

We start from the Bismut formula for
\nabla P_{T}^{V}f
(see Lemma
4.4
)
with
k(s)=\frac{T-s}{T},\,s\in[0,T]
and estimate the two terms separately.

For the stochastic integral term, Cauchy–Schwarz and Itô isometry give

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbb{V}_{T}f(x_{T})\int_{0}^{T}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(P_{T}f^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}(x)\,\mathbb{E}\Biggl[{\rm e}^{-2\int_{0}^{T}V(x_{s})\,ds}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{T}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\Bigr)^{2}\Biggr]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(P_{T}f^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}(x)\,\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{t}\psi(x_{s})\,ds}{\rm e}^{\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(x_{s})\,dl_{s}}\,dt\Bigr]^{1/2}\frac{1}{T}.

Using Lemma
4.2
, the expectation above is bounded by

-\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbb{V}_{T}f(x_{T})\int_{0}^{T}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\Bigr]\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\cdot\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\cdot\bigl(P_{T}f^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}(x).

For the drift term involving
\nabla V
, Cauchy–Schwarz yields

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbb{V}_{T}f(x_{T})\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla V,Q_{s}(k(s)v)\rangle ds\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T}{2}\cdot\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\,\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\bigl(\mathbb{E}|\nabla V|^{2}(x_{s})\bigr)^{1/2}\,\bigl(P_{T}f^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}(x).

By the assumed bound on
\nabla V
, this becomes

-\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbb{V}_{T}f(x_{T})\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla V,Q_{s}(k(s)v)\rangle ds\Bigr]\leq\frac{T}{4}\cdot\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}\cdot\,\bigl(P_{T}f^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}(x).

Adding the two estimates gives

|\nabla P_{T}^{V}f|(x)\leq\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{T}{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}\right)\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\bigl(P_{T}f^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}(x),

which is the desired inequality.
∎

4.4
Gradient estimate for the Neumann semigroup

Finally, we combine the preceding lemmas with the Duhamel formula, to obtain a pointwise gradient bound for the original Neumann semigroup
P_{t}
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Assume Condition
(C2)
holds for some non-negative functions
h\in C^{1}(M)
and
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(M)
such that
N\log\phi\geq\sigma
on the boundary. If there exists some non-negative function
\psi\in C^{1}(M)
such that
-L\log\phi+2|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}\leq\psi
, and there exist functions
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}\in\mathcal{B}(M)
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,\,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}\Bigl[|h+\psi|^{2}(x_{s})\Bigr]\leq\alpha_{0}(x),\quad\sup_{s\in[0,\,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}\Bigl[\bigl|\nabla(h+\psi)\bigr|^{2}(x_{s})\Bigr]\leq\alpha_{1}(x),

then for any
T>0
and
x\in M
,

|\nabla P_{T}f|(x)\leq\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\min\{T,1\}}}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}+\sqrt{\alpha_{0}(x)}+\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{\alpha_{0}(x)\alpha_{1}(x)}\right]\|f\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

As explained at the beginning of proof of Theorem
3.4
, we assume
0<T\leq 1
.
We start from the Duhamel formula,

P_{T}f=P_{T}^{V}f+\int_{0}^{T}P_{T-s}^{V}(VP_{s}f)\,ds,

where
V=\frac{1}{2}\left(h+\psi\right)
.

Differentiating along a unit vector
v\in T_{x}M
gives

\langle\nabla P_{T}f,v\rangle(x)=\langle\nabla P_{T}^{V}f,v\rangle(x)+\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla P_{T-s}^{V}(VP_{s}f),v\rangle(x)\,ds.

Applying Lemma
4.5
to the first term and to the second term with time parameter
r=T-s
, then changing variables
r\mapsto s
, yields

\displaystyle\langle\nabla P_{T}f,v\rangle(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}\,T\Bigr)\bigl(P_{T}f^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}\,s\Bigr)\bigl(P_{s}(VP_{T-s}f)^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}\,ds.

(4.3)

Since the semigroup is contractive on
L^{\infty}
,

\bigl(P_{T}f^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}\leq\|f\|_{\infty},\qquad\bigl(P_{s}(VP_{T-s}f)^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}\leq\bigl(P_{s}V^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}\|f\|_{\infty}.

By the definition
V=\frac{1}{2}(h+\psi)
and the hypothesis on
h+\psi
,

\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\bigl(P_{s}V^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}(x)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\alpha_{0}(x)}.

Substituting these bounds into (
4.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\nabla P_{T}f,v\rangle(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}\,T\Bigr)\|f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{2\phi(x)}\sqrt{\alpha_{0}(x)}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}\,s\Bigr)\,ds\right]\|f\|_{\infty}.

Computing the integral,

\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}\,s\Bigr)\,ds=2\sqrt{T}+\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}\,T^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla P_{T}f,v\rangle(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}\,T+\sqrt{\alpha_{0}(x)T}+\frac{T^{2}}{8}\sqrt{\alpha_{0}(x)\alpha_{1}(x)}\right]\|f\|_{\infty}.

Taking the supremum over unit vectors
v
gives the stated estimate.
∎

4.5
Explicit Estimates via Boundary Distance

In this subsection, we impose an additional geometric condition near the
boundary, which allows us to construct the conformal factor
\phi
in terms
of the boundary distance function
\rho_{\partial}
.

Condition (C3)
: There exist non-negative constants
\theta
and

\sigma
such that

-\sigma\leq\mathrm{II}\leq\theta\quad\hbox{on }\partial M.

Moreover, there exists a positive constant
r_{0}
such that, on

\partial_{r_{0}}M:=\{x\in M:\rho_{\partial}(x)<r_{0}\},

the boundary distance function
\rho_{\partial}
is smooth,
|Z|
is
bounded, and

\operatorname{Sect}\leq k

for some positive constant
k
.

Let

J(t):=\cos(\sqrt{k}\,t)-\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{k}}\sin(\sqrt{k}\,t),\qquad t\geq 0.

Let
J^{-1}(0)
denote the first zero of
J
, with the convention

J^{-1}(0)=\infty
if
J(t)>0
for all
t\geq 0
. Then, for every

x\in M
such that

\rho_{\partial}(x)\leq r_{0}\wedge J^{-1}(0),

the Laplacian comparison estimate gives

\Delta\rho_{\partial}(x)\geq(d-1)\frac{J^{\prime}}{J}(\rho_{\partial}(x)).

When
k>0
, the first zero is given by

J^{-1}(0)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{k}{k+\theta^{2}}}\right).

Using this assumption, F.-Y. Wang constructed a conformal factor
\phi

satisfying

N\log\phi\geq\sigma\quad\hbox{on }\partial M;

see
[
30
]
or
[
32
, Theorem 3.2.9]
. Following this
construction, set

r_{1}:=r_{0}\wedge J^{-1}(0)

and define

\displaystyle\log\phi(x):=\frac{\sigma}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\rho_{\partial}(x)\wedge r_{1}}[J(s)-J(r_{1})]^{1-d}\left(\int_{s}^{r_{1}}[J(u)-J(r_{1})]^{d-1}\,du\right)ds,

where

\alpha:=(1-J(r_{1}))^{1-d}\int_{0}^{r_{1}}[J(s)-J(r_{1})]^{d-1}\,ds.

Then, by the proof of
[
29
, Theorem 1.1]
,

(L\log\phi)^{-}
and
|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}
are bounded. In particular,
this
\phi
satisfies the assumptions required in
Theorem
4.6
. Moreover,

1\leq\phi\leq{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}d\sigma r_{1}}

and

-L\log\phi+2|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}\leq C_{0},

where

\displaystyle C_{0}=\sigma\left(\delta_{r_{1}}(Z)+\frac{d}{r_{1}}\right)+2\sigma^{2}

(4.4)

and

\delta_{r_{1}}(Z):=\sup\{|Z(x)|:\rho_{\partial}(x)\leq r_{1}\}.

Corollary 4.7
.

Assume Conditions
(C2)
and
(C3)
. Suppose that
h\in C^{1}(M)
is
non-negative and that there exist non-negative functions

\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}
on
M
such that, for every
x\in M
,

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[h^{2}(x_{s})]\leq\alpha_{0}(x),\ \text{ and }\ \sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[|\nabla h|^{2}(x_{s})]\leq\alpha_{1}(x).

(4.5)

Then, for every
T>0
,
x\in M
, and
f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)
,

\displaystyle|\nabla P_{T}f|(x)\leq{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}d\sigma r_{1}}\biggl[

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\min\{T,1\}}}+\sqrt{\alpha_{0}(x)}+C_{0}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}+\frac{1}{8}\bigl(\sqrt{\alpha_{0}(x)}+C_{0}\bigr)\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}\biggr]\|f\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

Set

t_{0}:=T\wedge 1.

By the semigroup property,

P_{T}f=P_{t_{0}}P_{T-t_{0}}f.

Applying Theorem
4.6
to
P_{T-t_{0}}f
with time parameter

t_{0}
, and using

\|P_{T-t_{0}}f\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty},

we obtain the desired estimate once the coefficients in
Theorem
4.6
are controlled.

By the construction of
\phi
under Condition
(C3)
,

\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\leq{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}d\sigma r_{1}}.

Moreover,

-L\log\phi+2|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}\leq C_{0}.

Thus we may take
\psi=C_{0}
in Theorem
4.6
. Since
h\geq 0
,

|h+\psi|\leq h+C_{0}.

Hence, for
s\in[0,t_{0}]\subset[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}^{x}[(h(x_{s})+C_{0})^{2}]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}^{x}[h^{2}(x_{s})]\right)^{1/2}+C_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\alpha_{0}(x)}+C_{0}.

Also, since
\psi=C_{0}
is constant,

\nabla(h+\psi)=\nabla h,

and therefore

\sup_{s\in[0,t_{0}]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[|\nabla(h+\psi)|^{2}(x_{s})]\leq\alpha_{1}(x).

Substituting these bounds into Theorem
4.6
, and using

t_{0}\leq 1
, gives

\displaystyle|\nabla P_{T}f|(x)\leq{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}d\sigma r_{1}}\biggl[

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{t_{0}}}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}\,t_{0}+(\sqrt{\alpha_{0}(x)}+C_{0})\sqrt{t_{0}}

\displaystyle+\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{8}(\sqrt{\alpha_{0}(x)}+C_{0})\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}\biggr]\|f\|_{\infty}.

Since
0<t_{0}\leq 1
, this implies

\displaystyle|\nabla P_{T}f|(x)\leq{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}d\sigma r_{1}}\biggl[

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\min\{T,1\}}}+\sqrt{\alpha_{0}(x)}+C_{0}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}+\frac{1}{8}(\sqrt{\alpha_{0}(x)}+C_{0})\sqrt{\alpha_{1}(x)}\biggr]\|f\|_{\infty}.

The proof is complete.
∎

.

If the lower bound for the Bakry–Emery curvature has the same form as in
Corollary
3.7
, then the coefficients
\alpha_{0}
and

\alpha_{1}
in (
4.5
) can be made explicit in the same way
as in Corollary

.

5
Application to Neumann eigenfunction estimates

We now turn to a direct application of the preceding semigroup gradient
estimates to pointwise bounds for Neumann eigenfunctions. For compact manifolds, related estimates were studied in
[
2
,
6
]
.
Let
u\in C^{2}(M)
be a classical
Neumann eigenfunction of
L
, namely

\displaystyle Lu=-\lambda u\quad\hbox{in }M^{\circ},\qquad Nu|_{\partial M}=0,

(5.1)

where
\lambda>0
. Equivalently,
u
belongs to the domain of the
Neumann realization of
L
and satisfies the above equation.
Since the Neumann semigroup
P_{t}
is generated by
\frac{1}{2}L
, the spectral relation gives

P_{t}u={\rm e}^{-\lambda t/2}u,\qquad t\geq 0.

Consequently,

|\nabla u|(x)={\rm e}^{\lambda T/2}|\nabla P_{T}u|(x),\qquad T>0.

Thus the gradient estimates for
P_{T}
immediately yield gradient estimates
for Neumann eigenfunctions.

We first treat the convex boundary case, where the boundary contribution is
non-positive and no conformal change is needed.

Corollary 5.1
.

Assume Condition
(C1)
. Suppose that there exist non-negative
functions
{\theta}_{0},\,{\theta}_{1}
on
M
such that, for every
x\in M
,

\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[h^{2}(x_{s})]\leq{\theta}_{0}(x),\qquad\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[|\nabla h|^{2}(x_{s})]\leq{\theta}_{1}(x).

Let
u\in C^{2}(M)
satisfy (
5.1
)
with
\lambda>0
. Then, for every
x\in M
,

|\nabla u|(x)\leq{\rm e}^{1/2}\left[\sqrt{\max\{\lambda,1\}}+\frac{\sqrt{{\theta}_{1}(x)}}{4}+\sqrt{{\theta}_{0}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{{\theta}_{0}(x){\theta}_{1}(x)}}{8}\right]\|u\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

For every
T>0
, the spectral relation gives

|\nabla u|(x)={\rm e}^{\lambda T/2}|\nabla P_{T}u|(x).

Applying Theorem
3.4
to
f=u
, and using the
assumptions

\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[h^{2}(x_{s})]\leq{\theta}_{0}(x),

and

\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[|\nabla h|^{2}(x_{s})]\leq{\theta}_{1}(x),

we obtain

|\nabla u|(x)\leq{\rm e}^{\lambda T/2}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\min\{T,\,1\}}}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{{\theta}_{1}(x)}+\sqrt{{\theta}_{0}(x)}+\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{{\theta}_{0}(x){\theta}_{1}(x)}\right]\|u\|_{\infty}.

Taking
T=\lambda^{-1}
gives the desired estimate.
∎

We then consider the general
non-convex case by using a conformal factor
\phi
which compensates for the
negative part of the second fundamental form.

Corollary 5.2
.

Assume Condition
(C2)
. Suppose that there exists

\phi\in\mathcal{D}(M)
such that

N\log\phi\geq\sigma\quad\hbox{on }\partial M,

and that for some
\psi\in C^{1}(M)\cap\mathcal{K}(M)
,

-L\log\phi+2|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}\leq\psi.

Assume further that there exist non-negative functions
\alpha_{0},\,\alpha_{1}

on
M
such that, for every
x\in M
,

\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[|h+\psi|^{2}(x_{s})]\leq{\alpha}_{0}(x),\qquad\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[|\nabla(h+\psi)|^{2}(x_{s})]\leq{\alpha}_{1}(x).

Let
u\in C^{2}(M)
satisfy (
5.1
) with
\lambda>0
. Then for every
x\in M
,

|\nabla u|(x)\leq{\rm e}^{1/2}\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\left[\sqrt{\max\{\lambda,1\}}+\frac{\sqrt{{\alpha}_{1}(x)}}{4}+\sqrt{{\alpha}_{0}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{{\alpha}_{0}(x){\alpha}_{1}(x)}}{8}\right]\|u\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

As above,

|\nabla u|(x)={\rm e}^{\lambda T/2}|\nabla P_{T}u|(x),\qquad T>0.

Applying Theorem
4.6
to
f=u
, and using
the bounds

\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[|h+\psi|^{2}(x_{s})]\leq{\alpha}_{0}(x),\qquad\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[|\nabla(h+\psi)|^{2}(x_{s})]\leq{\alpha}_{1}(x),

we get

|\nabla u|(x)\leq{\rm e}^{\lambda T/2}\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\min\{T,1\}}}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{{\alpha}_{1}(x)}+\sqrt{{\alpha}_{0}(x)}+\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{{\alpha}_{0}(x){\alpha}_{1}(x)}\right]\|u\|_{\infty}.

Choosing
T=\lambda^{-1}
proves the claim.
∎

Finally, under the explicit
geometric assumptions in Condition
(C3)
, the conformal factor can be
chosen explicitly, leading to a more concrete estimate.

Corollary 5.3
.

Assume Conditions
(C2)
and
(C3)
.
Suppose that there exist non-negative functions
\alpha_{0},\,\alpha_{1}
on
M

such that

\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[h^{2}(x_{s})]\leq{\alpha}_{0}(x),\qquad\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}^{x}[|\nabla h|^{2}(x_{s})]\leq{\alpha}_{1}(x).

Let
u\in C^{2}(M)
satisfy (
5.1
) with
\lambda>0
. Then, for every
x\in M
,

|\nabla u|(x)\leq{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}(1+d\sigma r_{1})}\left[\sqrt{\max\{\lambda,1\}}+\frac{\sqrt{{\alpha}_{1}(x)}}{4}+\bigl(\sqrt{{\alpha}_{0}(x)}+C_{0}\bigr)\Bigl(1+\frac{\sqrt{{\alpha}_{1}(x)}}{8}\Bigr)\right]\|u\|_{\infty},

where
C_{0}
is defined in (
4.4
).

Remark 5.4
.

The preceding corollaries show that the leading high-frequency contribution is
of order

\sqrt{\lambda}\,\|u\|_{\infty},

which is consistent with
[
2
]
when the domain is compact
and the Ricci curvature is lower bounded by a constant. The remaining terms do not affect the leading growth
once the curvature and
boundary quantities are well controlled.

6
Appendix

We point out that pointwise moment assumptions on the curvature lower bound
do not by themselves imply the Kato condition, nor do they guarantee the
exponential integrability needed for the multiplicative functional.

Example 6.1
.

Let
M
be a rotationally symmetric model manifold

M=[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\qquad g=dr^{2}+\varphi(r)^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}},

where
\varphi(r)\sim r
near
r=0
, and

\varphi(r)=\exp(ar^{2})

for all sufficiently large
r
, with
a>0
. We take
Z=0
, so that

\operatorname{Ric}_{Z}=\operatorname{Ric}
.
On a model manifold, the radial
Ricci curvature is given by

\operatorname{Ric}(\partial_{r},\partial_{r})=-(d-1)\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)}{\varphi(r)}.

Since, for large
r
,

\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(r)}{\varphi(r)}=2ar,\qquad\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)}{\varphi(r)}=2a+4a^{2}r^{2},

we have

\operatorname{Ric}(\partial_{r},\partial_{r})=-(d-1)(2a+4a^{2}r^{2})

for large
r
. Hence the negative part of the Ricci curvature can be
controlled by

h(x)=C_{a}(1+\rho_{o}(x)^{2}),

where
C_{a}>0
is a constant of order
a^{2}
. In particular,

\operatorname{Ric}\geq-h.

Let
x_{s}
be the Brownian motion on
M
, generated by

\frac{1}{2}\Delta
, and set

R_{s}=\rho_{o}(x_{s}).

The radial process satisfies, away from the cut locus,

dR_{s}=d\beta_{s}+\frac{d-1}{2}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(R_{s})}{\varphi(R_{s})}\,ds,

where
\beta_{s}
is a one-dimensional Brownian motion. Since

\varphi^{\prime}(r)/\varphi(r)=2ar
for large
r
, the radial drift has at most
linear growth. Consequently, for every
q\geq 1
and every finite
t>0
,

\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}^{x}R_{s}^{q}<\infty

for each fixed starting point
x\in M
. Therefore

\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}^{x}h(x_{s})^{2}<\infty,\qquad\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}^{x}|\nabla h|^{2}(x_{s})<\infty.

Indeed,

h(x_{s})^{2}=C_{a}^{2}(1+R_{s}^{2})^{2}\leq C(1+R_{s}^{4}),

and

|\nabla h|^{2}(x_{s})=4C_{a}^{2}R_{s}^{2}

outside a compact set, up to a harmless modification inside the compact part.

However, these fixed-point moment bounds do not imply the exponential
integrability of the path integral. We sketch the reason. Choose an initial
point
x
with
R_{0}=\rho_{o}(x)>2
. Since the radial drift is non-negative
for large
r
, on suitable Brownian events one has a lower bound of the form

R_{s}\geq n,\qquad s\in[t/2,t].

More precisely, for large integers
n
, consider

A_{n}=\left\{\beta_{t/2}\in[n,n+1],\quad\inf_{0\leq s\leq t/2}\beta_{s}\geq-1,\quad\inf_{t/2\leq s\leq t}(\beta_{s}-\beta_{t/2})\geq-1\right\}.

By the Markov property and the reflection principle for one-dimensional
Brownian motion, there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

\mathbb{P}(A_{n})\geq c_{1}{\rm e}^{-c_{2}n^{2}}.

On
A_{n}
, we have
R_{s}\geq n
for
s\in[t/2,t]
. Hence

\int_{0}^{t}h(x_{s})\,ds\geq\frac{t}{2}C_{a}n^{2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{x}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}h(x_{s})\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n\gg 1}\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}h(x_{s})\,ds\right)\mathbf{1}_{A_{n}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}\sum_{n\gg 1}\exp\left[\left(\frac{t}{2}C_{a}-c_{2}\right)n^{2}\right].

Since
C_{a}
is of order
a^{2}
, choosing
a>0
sufficiently large gives

\frac{t}{2}C_{a}>c_{2},

and hence

\mathbb{E}^{x}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}h(x_{s})\,ds\right)=\infty.

We finally verify directly from the definition that this function
h
is not
in the Kato class. Recall that

f\in\mathcal{K}(M)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\lim_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\sup_{x\in M}\int_{0}^{\alpha}\mathbb{E}^{x}|f(x_{s})|\,ds=0.

For
h(x)=C_{a}(1+\rho_{o}(x)^{2})
, this condition fails. Indeed, for the squared
distance function one has, outside the cut locus and hence in the barrier
sense,

\frac{1}{2}\Delta\rho_{o}^{2}=1+(d-1)\rho_{o}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\rho_{o})}{\varphi(\rho_{o})}.

Since
\varphi^{\prime}(r)/\varphi(r)=2ar
for large
r
, and the remaining part of
the manifold is compact, there exists a constant
C>0
such that globally

\frac{1}{2}\Delta\rho_{o}^{2}\geq-C.

By Itô’s formula,

\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\rho_{o}^{2}(x_{s})\right]=\rho_{o}^{2}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s}\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\Delta\rho_{o}^{2}(x_{r})\right]\,dr\geq\rho_{o}^{2}(x)-Cs.

Now choose
X_{R}\in M
such that
\rho_{o}(X_{R})=R
. For every fixed

\alpha>0
, if
R
is sufficiently large, then

\mathbb{E}^{X_{R}}\rho_{o}^{2}(x_{s})\geq\frac{1}{2}R^{2},\qquad 0\leq s\leq\alpha.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\alpha}\mathbb{E}^{X_{R}}h(x_{s})\,ds

\displaystyle=C_{a}\int_{0}^{\alpha}\mathbb{E}^{X_{R}}\left(1+\rho_{o}(x_{s})^{2}\right)\,ds\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{a}\alpha R^{2}.

Letting
R\to\infty
, we obtain

\sup_{x\in M}\int_{0}^{\alpha}\mathbb{E}^{x}h(x_{s})\,ds=\infty

for every
\alpha>0
. Hence

h\notin\mathcal{K}(M).
