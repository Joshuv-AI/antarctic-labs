Encirclement Guaranteed Finite-Time Capture against Unknown Evader Strategies
Dinesh Patra, Prajakta Surve, Ashish R. Hota, Shaunak D. Bopardikar

Abstract
We consider a pursuit-evasion scenario involving a group of pursuers and a single evader in a two-dimensional unbounded environment. The pursuers aim to capture the evader in finite time while ensuring the evader remains enclosed within the convex hull of their positions until capture, without knowledge of the evader’s heading angle. Prior works have addressed the problem of encirclement and capture separately in different contexts. In this paper, we present a class of strategies for the pursuers that guarantee capture in finite time while maintaining encirclement, irrespective of the evader’s strategy. Furthermore, we derive an upper bound on the time to capture. Numerical results highlight the effectiveness of the proposed framework against a range of evader strategies.

Introduction

Consider a situation where an unknown intruder enters a secure confinement zone formed by a team of ground robots deployed for surveillance. Once the intruder is inside the zone, the robots aim to keep it confined and approach it progressively to assess its intent; moreover, timely neutralization may be required if a threat is detected. maintain encirclement

l\in\{j,k\}
, while the inactive pursuers continue to follow pure pursuit.

Proof.

Note that the proposed edge-phase angle range
\varphi_{l}\in[\sin^{-1}(\mu_{m}),\frac{\pi}{2}-\sin^{-1}(1-\mu_{m})]
, where
l\in\{j,k\}
and
\mu_{m}\in[0,1)
, is a subset of the admissible range
\varphi_{l}\in[\sin^{-1}(\mu_{m}),\pi-\sin^{-1}(\mu_{m})]
, for which encirclement is guaranteed at all times following Corollary
1
.

We prove finite-time capture of the evader with the proposed pursuers’ strategy using a Lyapunov-based analysis. We select the following candidate Lyapunov function

V=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|p_{i}-e\|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i},

(23)

which is positive definite and represents the sum of distances between the pursuers and the evader.
6
6

6

In
[
10
]
, the authors adopted a similar Lyapunov function, however the analysis was carried out in discrete-time and they did not provide any guarantees on encirclement.
We now analyze the time derivative
\dot{V}
for different phases of pursuit assuming that
\|p_{i}-e\|>r_{c}
for all the pursuers, that is, the evader is not yet captured.

a) Interior Phase.
When
e\in\operatorname{int}(\mathcal{H}(t))
, all pursuers use pure pursuit. We then compute the time derivative of
V
as

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dot{d}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(p_{i}-e)^{\top}\dot{p}_{i}}{\|e-p_{i}\|}+\frac{(e-p_{i})^{\top}\dot{e}}{\|e-p_{i}\|}

(24)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{(p_{i}-e)^{\top}}{\|e-p_{i}\|}\right)\left(\frac{e-p_{i}}{\|e-p_{i}\|}\right)+\frac{(e-p_{i})^{\top}\dot{e}}{\|e-p_{i}\|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}-1+\frac{(e-p_{i})^{\top}\dot{e}}{\|e-p_{i}\|}

\displaystyle\leq-n(1-\mu)\leq-n(1-\mu_{m}),

(25)

where the inequality follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, under which

\displaystyle|(e-p_{i})^{T}\dot{e}|\leq\|e-p_{i}\|\|\dot{e}\|=\mu\|e-p_{i}\|

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\frac{(e-p_{i})^{\top}\dot{e}}{\|e-p_{i}\|}-1\leq\mu-1\leq\mu_{m}-1.

Since
\mu_{m}<1
, the Lyapunov rate
\dot{V}\leq-n(1-\mu_{m})
is bounded by a finite negative quantity.

In our setting, the capture takes place when at least one pursuer is within distance
r_{c}
from the evader, that is, at a finite positive value of the Lyapunov function. Thus, the constant rate of decay of the Lyapunov function results in finite-time capture. In particular, the evader is captured in finite time if it stays in the interior of the convex hull
\mathcal{H}(t)
throughout the pursuit.

b) Edge Phase.
Suppose an edge
p_{j}p_{k}
is active at time
t
. In this case, the Lyapunov rate can be expressed as

\dot{V}=\dot{d}_{j}+\dot{d}_{k}+\sum^{n}_{i=1,i\notin\{j,k\}}\dot{d}_{i},

(26)

Since pursuers that are not active adopt pure pursuit, following the earlier analysis, we have

\dot{d}_{i}\leq-(1-\mu)\leq-(1-\mu_{m}),\quad\text{for}\quad i\neq j,k.

(27)

We now consider the active pursuers. We compute
\dot{d}_{j}
by substituting the expression of
\dot{p}_{j}
from (
14
) with
v_{j}=1
as

\displaystyle\dot{d}_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p_{j}-e)^{\top}\dot{p}_{j}}{\|e-p_{j}\|}+\frac{(e-p_{j})^{\top}\dot{e}}{\|e-p_{j}\|}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{(p_{j}-e)^{\top}}{\|e-p_{j}\|}\right)\left(R_{\varphi_{j}}\,\frac{e-p_{j}}{\|e-p_{j}\|}\right)+\frac{(e-p_{j})^{\top}\dot{e}}{\|e-p_{j}\|}

\displaystyle=-\cos\varphi_{j}+\frac{(e-p_{j})^{\top}\dot{e}}{\|e-p_{j}\|}.

(28)

On similar lines, we compute

\dot{d}_{k}=-\cos\varphi_{k}+\frac{(e-p_{k})^{\top}\dot{e}}{\|e-p_{k}\|}.

(29)

Adding (
28
) and (
29
), we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{d}_{j}+\dot{d}_{k}

\displaystyle=-\cos\varphi_{j}-\cos\varphi_{k}+\frac{(e-p_{j})^{\top}\dot{e}}{\|e-p_{j}\|}+\frac{(e-p_{k})^{\top}\dot{e}}{\|e-p_{k}\|}

\displaystyle=-\cos\varphi_{j}-\cos\varphi_{k},

(30)

where the last equality follows because from (
13
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{(e-p_{j})^{\top}}{\|e-p_{j}\|}=u^{\top}_{jk},\;\frac{(e-p_{k})^{\top}}{\|e-p_{k}\|}=-u^{\top}_{jk}.

(31)

Combining the terms for the inactive and active pursuers from (
27
) and (
30
), we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle\leq-(n-2)(1-\mu_{m})-\cos\varphi_{j}-\cos\varphi_{k},

\displaystyle\leq-n(1-\mu_{m}),

(32)

as before; the last inequality follows because
\varphi_{l}\in[\sin^{-1}(\mu_{m}),\frac{\pi}{2}-\sin^{-1}(1-\mu_{m})]
implies
1-\mu_{m}\leq\cos\varphi_{l}\leq\sqrt{1-\mu_{m}^{2}}
for
l\in\{j,k\}
. Therefore, the finite-time convergence established in the interior phase continues to be applicable when the evader is on an edge, and also when the evader potentially switches at an arbitrary frequency between both interior and edge phases since we have found a common bound that is applicable for both phases.

Integrating (
32
) from
t=0
to
t_{c}
, we obtain

\displaystyle t_{c}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{V_{0}-V_{c}}{n(1-\mu_{m})}\leq\frac{V_{0}-nr_{c}}{n(1-\mu_{m})},

(33)

where
V_{0}
is the value of the Lyapunov function at
t=0
and
V_{c}
is the value of the Lyapunov function at the time of capture
t_{c}
. Note that capture occurs at the first time instant when
d_{i}=r_{c}
for some
i
, which implies that throughout the game
d_{i}=\|p_{i}-e\|\geq r_{c}
for all
i\in[n]
, with equality holding the time of capture if pursuer
i
captures the evader. This implies
V_{c}\geq nr_{c}
, with equality when all the pursuers simultaneously reach a distance of
r_{c}
from the evader.

VI
Conclusion

In this work, we presented a strategy for a group of pursuers to enclose an evader in their convex hull and capture it in finite time, without knowledge of the control strategy of the evader. Furthermore, we derive an upper bound on the time of capture that holds for all admissible evader strategies. Possible directions for future work include extending the analysis to three-dimensional space, handling multiple evaders, and improving the bound on capture time.
