Shock wavefronts for parabolic equations with sign-changing diffusivity
Diego Berti, Andrea Corli, Luisa Malaguti

Abstract
We consider a reaction–diffusion equation in a one-dimensional space, where the diffusion coefficient changes sign from positive to negative and back to positive. The reaction term is bistable, with its interior zero located in the region where the diffusivity is negative. The model does not admit continuous wavefronts, i.e., continuous traveling-waves that connect the steady states 0 0 and 1 1 . We prove the existence of a family of shock wavefronts, that is, wavefronts with profiles exhibiting a jump discontinuity. We investigate the properties of these profiles and their propagation speeds. Finally, we apply the results to a recently proposed model describing the movement of a population composed of both isolated and grouped individuals.

Definition 5.1

We say that

The next proposition shows that the set of admissible pairs is the graph of a regular, strictly increasing function defined on a closed interval

.

Proposition 5.2
.

If
P(1)>P(0)
, every admissible pair
(\varphi_{\ell},\varphi_{r})
for a shock wavefront is of the form
\left(\varphi_{\ell},\eta(\varphi_{\ell})\right)

\varphi_{\ell}
belongs to a closed, nonempty interval

Proof.

A pair
(\varphi_{\ell},\varphi_{r})
is admissible if and only if it belongs to the zero-level set of the function

defined by

(\varphi,\psi)\in[0,\alpha]\times[\beta,1]\ \longmapsto\ \mathcal{A}(\varphi,\psi):=P(\psi)-P(\varphi).

Clearly,
\mathcal{A}
is continuous on
[0,\alpha]\times[\beta,1]
. Since

\mathcal{A}(0,1)=P(1)-P(0)>0
by hypothesis and

\mathcal{A}(\alpha,\beta)=P(\beta)-P(\alpha)<0
, then
the zero-level set of
\mathcal{A}
is nonempty.
We define

\mathcal{I}:=\left\{\,\varphi_{\ell}\in[0,\alpha]:\ \mathcal{A}(\varphi_{\ell},\varphi_{r})=0.\ \text{for some }\varphi_{r}\in[\beta,1]\,\right\}.

Since the restrictions
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
of

to
[0,\alpha]
and
[\beta,1]
, respectively, are strictly increasing, then they are invertible and

\mathcal{A}(\varphi_{\ell},\varphi_{r})=0\iff\varphi_{\ell}=P_{1}^{-1}\left(P_{2}(\varphi_{r})\right)\iff\varphi_{r}=P_{2}^{-1}(P_{1}\left(\varphi_{\ell})\right).

Suppose
\mathcal{A}(\varphi_{1},\psi_{1})=\mathcal{A}(\varphi_{2},\psi_{2})=0
with
\varphi_{2}>\varphi_{1}
.
For every

\mathcal{A}(\varphi,\psi_{1})=P(\psi_{1})-P(\varphi)<0<P(\psi_{2})-P(\varphi)=\mathcal{A}(\varphi,\psi_{2}).

, there exists a unique

Hence

\mathcal{I}
is closed since it is the projection onto the
\varphi

∎

Remark 5.2

Assume
