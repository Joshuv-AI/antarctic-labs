DLoG – automatic disc based laplacian of gaussian operator for carotid artery boundary extraction in ultrasound images
Authors: M Anitha, Arbind Kumar Gupta, Prameetha Pai, Rudramurthy V C
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAutomatic segmentation of the carotid artery from ultrasound images will provide significant benefit over manual segmentation for assessment and diagnosis of atherosclerosis of the common carotid artery (CCA). The grainy nature of ultrasound images and speckle noise poses several challenges in automatic segmentation of the CCA. To achieve a robust, accurate and reliable segmentation of CCA, a Disc shape based Laplacian of Gaussian (DLOG) is proposed. The proposed approach introduces a disc shape kernel of the size of artery and adding enhanced version of LOG at each end of diameter end of the kernel filter. This priori shape information kernel helps localizing the artery location accurately that looks for global shape features based on intensity changes rather than edge pixels. Gaussian energy term in DLOG operator helps in smoothening that makes the technique more robust to noise and increasing the DLOG efficiency. Further preprocessing operation such as outliner edge removal, boundary gap linking and smoothening is performed to extract the boundary efficiently. The method has been tested on large number of ultrasound images with varying level of noise, to establish its robustness. Experimental results showed that the proposed algorithm has higher efficiency of about 98% compared to various other approaches. Thus, the proposed method shows better performance and has great potential in improving the performance computer-aided diagnosis systems in cerebrovascular diseases.Keywords: Ultrasound image segmentation, Laplacian operator, Common carotid artery, AtherosclerosisSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Health care, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Segmentation of carotid artery (CA) from ultrasound image plays an important role in diagnosis of Cerebrovascular diseases (CVD). Various studies have linked atherosclerosis to CVD1–3 events like carotid stenosis, vertebral stenosis and intracranial stenosis and vascular malformations ultimately leading to stroke which is one of the leading causes of deaths world wide. Atherosclerosis is a chronic degenerative disease formed by the accumulation of fatty material and cholesterol at the arterial walls which causes thickening and the reduction of elasticity in the arterial walls. Even though it remains unnoticed for decades, atherosclerotic plaques may block the total artery leading to heart attacks and strokes. Hence, an early diagnosis and follow up of the atherosclerosis is crucial for preventive purposes.

Different imaging modalities like MRI, CT, Ultrasound (US), are used to diagnose atherosclerosis. Non–invasive method for US and its low cost makes it most preferred method as the first screening of risk for atherosclerosis disease. It is also widely used for epidemiological studies and clinical trials relating to atherosclerotic diseases.

Growth of the plaque formation in the carotid artery due to progressive accumulation of lipid, protein, and cholesterol can lead to atherosclerosis, narrowing of the artery, stenosis, and subsequent carotid artery disease. Atherosclerosis is a slowly progressing disease causing coronary artery disease, ischemic cerebrovascular disease, and peripheral artery disease.

Accurate monitoring of changes in atherosclerotic plaque burden and composition is therefore necessary for risk prediction to avoid ischemic events such as plaque rupture, causing thrombosis and obstruction, or thrombo embolization into distal arteries.The risk of stroke increases with the severity of carotid stenosis and is reduced after carotid endarterectomy. The main criteria to decide whether carotid endarterectomy is required or not is based on the degree of stenosis. The need for the accurate segmentation of carotid artery vessel wall and identify changes in the morphology of the vessel wall and to locate plaques is important in order to assess the degree of stenosis and predict risk assessment of carotid atherosclerosis for early diagnosis.

Currently, manual annotation of the carotid artery and plaque region is being done to measure the extent of atherosclerosis but it is subjective to operators skill. Hence an automated approach is desirable to bring consistency in the approach. But it poses several challenges due to variability of data sets that depend on type, frequency, position and orientation of US probe, shadows due to calcification, occlusion, speckle noise of US images etc.4,5,37,38.

More recently, machine learning, deep learning based segmentation models, such as U-Net34–36 and its variants, have demonstrated impressive performance in medical image analysis. Despite their success, these methods require large annotated datasets, are sensitive to domain shifts across imaging devices, and often lack interpretability. Additionally, the computational and training overhead associated with deep neural networks can hinder their adoption in resource-constrained or real-time clinical environments. In contrast, filter-based approaches offers a lightweight and interpretable alternative for boundary extraction. Among them, the Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG) operator is widely used for edge detection due to its ability to capture intensity transitions while suppressing noise. However, standard LoG filtering does not explicitly incorporate anatomical priors, making it less effective in detecting structured circular boundaries such as the carotid artery lumen.

To address these limitations, this paper proposes a Disc-based Laplacian of Gaussian (DLoG) framework for robust carotid artery localization and boundary extraction in ultrasound images. The proposed method integrates circular anatomical priors into the LoG operator and employs a multi-scale scanning strategy to accommodate the variations in artery size. By transforming the detected region into polar coordinates, the circular boundary is converted into a near-horizontal structure, enabling efficient one-dimensional filtering, false-edge suppression, and gap interpolation.The proposed DLoG approach is training-free, initialization-independent, and non-iterative, making it computationally efficient and well suited for real-time clinical applications. Extensive experiments on multiple ultrasound datasets demonstrate that the method achieves accurate and stable boundary extraction under varying noise conditions, outperforming several classical techniques while maintaining low computational complexity.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

1. A novel Disc-based Laplacian of Gaussian operator for robust artery localization.

2. A multi-scale radius selection framework for detection of artery center.

3. A polar-domain boundary extraction strategy with effective false-edge removal and gap filling.

4. A lightweight, interpretable segmentation pipeline suitable for real-time ultrasound analysis.

Section 4 presents experimental results and quantitative evaluation. Section 5 discusses limitations and clinical implications, and Sect. 6 concludes the paper.

Literature Survey

Several techniques6 have been attempted for carotid artery lumen segmentation such as active contour methods7–9,24, level-set methods11,12, clustering methods26,27, thresholding18,21,22, region growing23,32, dynamic programming28. Most popular and effective techniques are the active contour techniques.Active contour model10 or snake used edge strength and elasticity to evolve the contour of the object. Another variant8 used a probabilistic deep learning model embedded into the shape-constrained active contour model. But the efficiency of these algorithms depended on the number of iterations used. Higher the number of iterations resulted in higher accuracy.

They can be categorized into two categories: edge-based and region-based. Edge-based models use local edge information to stop the evolving curve on the object boundary. But, they have a major draw-back, where these models are sensitive to noise, initial position of object and weak boundaries. Chan–Vese (CV) model12 proposed an efficient method which uses global information to minimize the energy function to obtain a piecewise-constant approximation of the image. But the drawback of it is that it had high computing cost due to non-convex optimization problem and numerical computations involved during contour evolution. An improved CV model13 used variant of gradient descent that uses smart momentum term to modify the search direction in the curve evolution.

Region-based models23–25 used region based features to control curve evolution. They are less sensitive to noise, since they use region based information inside and outside the contour to control the evolution and gives better performance. Also they are significantly less sensitive to initial location of the contour.Apart from active contours other approaches such as dynamic programming14, where boundary points were added based on initial position and minimum costs computed but the computation costs increases with the image size. Similarly graph based approach al15 used five steps such as gaussian smoothing, histogram for artifacts detection, graph based segmentation and dynamic programming for better boundary detection but all these complicated steps makes it quiet tedious. A connectivity based adaptive threshold based segmentation22 was used for segmentation but identifying the optimum threshold is highly difficult in ultrasound due to speckle noise.

Other edge base techniques16 suggests using Hessian based blob detector based on local phase and energy in detecting and tracking carotid. A multistep10 algorithm based segmentation is used as a combination of simple statistics to find the lumen center point. Another method suggests using a modified Viola-Jones detector17 which uses Haar-like features and Matthews coefficient which can identify large area feature but it is sensitive to speckle noise. A self-adaptive histogram equalization20, described a morphology-based algorithm for the segmentation. These edge based techniques used multiple steps and tends to be inefficient when applied for multiple images. Chen et al 21. suggested a tracklet refinement algorithm which used a neural network-based artery centerline identification architecture based on the prior knowledge of the circular shape of vessel. But these tracking based methods generally require initialization and are computationally expensive due to the iterative process. Most of the snake-based approaches such as active contour, level sets rely on intensity gradients, which are often prone to false edges and are also sensitive to speckle noise. Also, manual initialization is required for snake based methods to localize the carotid artery. In our method, we propose to use DLOG filter which accurately segments without any preprocessed enhancement or manual initialization.

Localization of carotid artery location using disc based LoG operator

We propose the Disc based Laplacian of Gaussian (DLoG) kernel which is the enhanced and optimized version of LoGoperator specifically for carotid artery localization in B-mode transverse US images. The grainy nature of US images, combined with speckle noise, makes it difficult to detect edges in US images using operators like Sobel, Prewitt, Laplacian, Canny edge detector etc. Gaussian based operator are more suitable due to the smoothening effect of these operator. However, it also has the negative affect of further blurring of the edges, leading to loss of true edge pixels. Moreover, these operators do not take advantage of the knowledge about the shape of the carotid artery that is approximately circular. The proposed DLoG method combines the advantage of Gaussian based operator and apriori knowledge of the shape of a carotid artery.

The carotid artery localization as shown in algorithm 1 is performed by convolving the given image with the predefined DLoG kernel. The DLoG kernel is isotropic, i.e. rotationally symmetric that measures the average local contrast between the central region and the surrounding. The major advantage of this kernel is that being a second order directive, it yields significant responses at locations where the gray scale intensity changes sharply, including edges, ridges, corners. This helps in identifying the locations or edges of rapid intensity changes which are especially useful in images of having non homogenous regions of intensity.

Considering the high amount of speckle noise present in US image, DLoG is designed to the size of carotid artery to give higher response to larger circles rather than smaller circles. In addition, having dual LoG operator defined in the kernel helps in faster identification of the location of CA, which otherwise would create ambiguous results to smaller circular regions due to high amount of speckle noise. Multi-scale scanning and radius selection

In ultrasound imaging, the carotid artery lumen exhibits significant variability in size due to anatomical differences, probe orientation, and patient-specific factors. A major problem using the DLoG image filter is to decide the size of the filter. Hence, assuming a fixed radius for artery detection may lead to inaccurate localization. To address this issue, a multi-scale scanning strategy is employed, wherein the proposed DLoG operator is applied over a discrete set of candidate radii that span a range of .

For each candidate radius , a corresponding DLoG kernel is constructed, explicitly encoding circular anatomical priors at that scale. Interpolation to fill boundary gaps

During boundary extraction, certain angular samples may be discarded as outliers the radial derivative response is weak due to strong speckle noise, acoustic shadows, or local image degradation. These missing points create discontinuities in the polar boundary representation.To reconstruct a complete lumen contour, gaps are filled using linear interpolation. Linear interpolation is chosen because it provides a simple yet effective way to transition smoothly between known boundary values without introducing oscillations or overshoots, which might occur with higher-order spline fitting in noisy conditions.

Discussion

Proper parameter selection is critical to achieving robust and reproducible performance of the proposed DLoG-based segmentation framework. The radius bounds define the search space for artery localization and should be selected based on prior anatomical knowledge and ultrasound probe characteristics. Restricting the radius range to physiologically plausible values reduces false detections and computational overhead. In our experiments, radii in the range of 20–160 pixels were used. When physical units are required, pixel values can be converted to millimeters using the probes pixel spacing. The number of scanned radii controls the granularity of the multi-scale search for the localization of carotid artery. While larger values provide finer scale resolution and more accurate radius estimation, they increase computational cost. Empirically, a moderate number of scales (10–20) provides a good trade-off between accuracy and efficiency. Logarithmic spacing is particularly effective when a wide range of artery sizes is expected. Typical values range from =10 to 20, with radii spaced either linearly or logarithmically was used in our experiments.

The gaussian scale parameter is chosen proportional to the corresponding radius to ensure scale-consistent smoothing. Smaller values of improve edge localization but are more sensitive to noise, whereas larger values increase robustness at the cost of reduced precision. Standard deviation of the Gaussian kernel at scale ​ are chosen within the range ​=0.15​ to 0.25​ in our experiment. This parameter balances smoothing and edge localization. The dual-LoG weighting parameter enhances boundary responses by reinforcing symmetric edge structures characteristic of circular lumen boundaries. Excessively large values may amplify noise, so is chosen conservatively through validation. A small positive value, typically between 0.4 and 1.5, is chosen in our testing and validation.

Smoothing window sizes are selected to suppress speckle noise while preserving anatomical curvature. For one-dimensional radial smoothing, small window sizes (3–7) are used, while Savitzky–Golay smoothing employs moderate window lengths (7–21) to further refine the contour without introducing geometric distortion. Finally, the outlier threshold controls false-edge rejection sensitivity and can be adapted based on noise conditions. Threshold constant used for false-edge rejection used in our experiments is typically set between 1.5 and 3 depending on noise severity. Overall, the parameter set can be tuned manually using prior knowledge or automatically optimized through cross-validation or SNR-aware heuristics, enabling the method to generalize across different ultrasound systems and imaging conditions.

Conclusion& limitations

The segmentation of the carotid artery boundaries plays an important role in assessing the progression of atherosclerosis. In this paper, we have presented a simple but reliable segmentation using DLoG kernel which used enhanced version of LoG to localize the center of CA. The dual LoG term added to the kernel helped in smoothening of the homogeneous regions and enhances contour edge information for faster identification of CA. The results obtained show its ability of this technique to localize the CCA accurately in low contrast and non-uniform intensity distributions. The polar based transform used for boundary detection helps in removing the false edge pixels due to speckle noise which is another added advantage of this technique. The evaluation carried out on various datasets shows superior performance with good precision and faster results. Furthermore, our method requires no preprocessing stages or initial seeds for identifying the ROI of CA as compared to other approaches region or model based techniques.

The proposed DLoG framework, while effective and computationally efficient, has certain limitations. The method assumes near-circular lumen geometry, which may reduce accuracy in cases of severe stenosis or irregular arterial shapes. Its performance can also be affected by extremely noisy or low-quality ultrasound images with strong shadowing artifacts. Finally, although suitable for real-time use, the approach has not yet been optimized or benchmarked on clinical or embedded hardware platforms.
