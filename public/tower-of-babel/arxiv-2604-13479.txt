Learning Class Difficulty in Imbalanced Histopathology Segmentation via Dynamic Focal Attention
Lakmali Nadeesha Kumari, Sen-Ching Samson Cheung

Abstract
Semantic segmentation of histopathology images under class imbalance is typically addressed through frequency-based loss reweighting, which implicitly assumes that rare classes are difficult. However, true difficulty also arises from morphological variability, boundary ambiguity, and contextual similarity—factors that frequency cannot capture. We propose Dynamic Focal Attention (DFA), a simple and efficient mechanism that learns class-specific difficulty directly within the cross-attention of query-based mask decoders. DFA introduces a learnable per-class bias to attention logits, enabling representation-level reweighting prior to prediction rather than gradient-level reweighting after prediction. Initialised from a log-frequency prior to prevent gradient starvation, the bias is optimised end-to-end, allowing the model to adaptively capture difficulty signals through training, effectively unifying frequency-based and difficulty-aware approaches under a common attention-bias framework. On three histopathology benchmarks (BDSA, BCSS, CRAG), DFA consistently improves Dice and IoU, matching or exceeding a difficulty-aware baseline without a separate estimator or additional training stage. These results demonstrate that encoding class difficulty at the representation level provides a principled alternative to conventional loss reweighting for imbalanced segmentation.

Our approach.

Method

2.1
Preliminaries: SAM-Path Decoder

We build upon SAM-Path
[
22
]
, which extends the original
SAM
[
13
]
by incorporating two encoders: frozen SAM and a
pathology foundation model, whose outputs are aligned to form a unified feature

\mathbf{H}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
(
N
: spatial tokens;
d
: feature
dimension). The decoder maintains
C
learnable class prompt tokens

\mathcal{P}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{C\times d}
, which interact with
\mathbf{H}

via cross-attention to produce per-class masks
\hat{y}_{c}
and IoU predictions

\widehat{\mathrm{iou}}_{c}
,
c\!=\!1,\ldots,C
. The cross-attention weights

\alpha_{i,c}\!=\!\exp(s_{i,c})/\sum_{c^{\prime}}\exp(s_{i,c^{\prime}})
,

s_{i,c}\!=\!(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^{\!\top})_{i,c}/\sqrt{d}
, where

\mathbf{Q}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{C\times d}
and

\mathbf{K}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
are queries and keys from

\mathcal{P}
and
\mathbf{H}
respectively, depend solely on feature–prompt
similarity with no explicit mechanism to modulate feature aggregation based on
class-level complexity—a limitation we address in this work, which is broadly
applicable to cross-attention mechanisms in transformer-based segmentation
models beyond SAM-Path.

2.2
The Frequency–Difficulty Disconnect

Frequency-based class-balancing assumes pixel frequency reliably reflects
segmentation difficulty, yet empirical evidence across all three benchmarks
contradicts this assumption (
Fig.

2
). In BCSS,
Inflammatory and Necrosis share near-identical frequency yet differ by
22.0

Dice points; Other—the most frequent minority class—scores lower than the
rarer Necrosis (
71.8\%
vs.
77.4\%
), directly inverting the
rarity–difficulty ranking. In BDSA, the rarest class (Superficial Cortex,

3.0\%
) outperforms the more frequent Leptomeninges (
6.4\%
) by
13.2
Dice
points. Only CRAG, with near-balanced classes, falls where frequency loosely
correlates with difficulty.

These observations demonstrate that true difficulty is governed by morphological
variability, boundary ambiguity, and inter-class contextual
confusion
[
2

, which are largely independent of pixel
frequency—motivating learning class difficulty directly from optimisation
dynamics.

2.3
Dynamic Focal Attention

Focal Attention Bias framework.
We introduce a class-specific scalar
b_{c}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}
, broadcast
across all
N
spatial positions, and added to the cross-attention logit
for class
c
before softmax:

\tilde{\alpha}_{i,c}=\frac{\exp(s_{i,c}+b_{c})}{\sum_{c^{\prime}=1}^{C}\exp(s_{i,c^{\prime}}+b_{c^{\prime}})},\quad\widetilde{\mathrm{Attn}}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V})=\mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\tfrac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^{\!\top}}{\sqrt{d}}+\mathbf{b}\right)\mathbf{V},

(1)

where
\mathbf{b}\!=\![b_{1},\ldots,b_{C}]^{\top}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{C}
and

\mathbf{V}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
are values derived from class tokens
\mathcal{P}
.
Setting
b_{c}\!=\!\log\omega_{c}
(
\omega_{c}\!>\!0
) multiplicatively
rescales the
c
-th score by
\omega_{c}
uniformly across all spatial
aggregations; parameterising
b_{c}
yields a progressive family of strategies:

(S1) Class Frequency Focal Attention (CFFA).
CFFA sets
b_{c}
inversely proportional to pixel frequency:

b_{c}=\gamma\cdot\log(1-f_{c}),\qquad\gamma>0,

(2)

where
f_{c}\!\in\!(0,1)
is the normalised training-set frequency of
class
c
and
\gamma
controls bias magnitude; unlike output-level
logit adjustment
[
17
]
, this prior acts on feature
aggregation directly. However, CFFA still conflates rarity with
difficulty (
Section

2.2
).

(S2) Hard Class Focal Attention (HCFA).
HCFA replaces frequency with empirical difficulty:

b_{c}=\gamma\cdot\log(1-p_{c}),\quad p_{c}=\frac{\mathrm{Dice}_{c}^{\mathrm{base}}}{\sum_{c^{\prime}=1}^{C}\mathrm{Dice}_{c^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{base}}},

(3)

where
\mathrm{Dice}_{c}^{\mathrm{base}}
is the per-class validation Dice
of the pre-trained baseline and
p_{c}
its normalised difficulty proxy;
HCFA targets hard classes but requires a full pre-training run.

(S3) Dynamic Focal Attention (DFA)—Proposed.
Both CFFA and HCFA fix
b_{c}
before training, and cannot adapt to difficulty
signals that emerge during optimisation.
DFA treats
b_{c}
as a learnable scalar
\delta_{c}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}

optimised end-to-end:

b_{c}=\delta_{c},\qquad\boldsymbol{\delta}\in\mathbb{R}^{C},

(4)

\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\delta_{c}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\tilde{\alpha}_{i,c}}\cdot\underbrace{\tilde{\alpha}_{i,c}(1-\tilde{\alpha}_{i,c})}_{\text{self-gating factor}},

(5)

driving
\delta_{c}
positive for poorly-segmented classes and suppressing
it for well-segmented ones.
The self-gating factor
\tilde{\alpha}_{i,c}(1\!-\!\tilde{\alpha}_{i,c})

is largest when attention weights are uncertain and diminish when saturates, yielding an implicit curriculum effect.

Warm-start initialisation.
Zero-initialising
\boldsymbol{\delta}
causes gradient starvation for
rare classes (
\tilde{\alpha}_{i,c}\!\approx\!0

\Rightarrow
self-gating

\approx\!0
, regardless of loss magnitude).
We therefore initialise
\boldsymbol{\delta_{c}}
using a
centred log-frequency prior
:

\delta_{c}^{(0)}=\beta\!\left(\log\pi_{c}-\tfrac{1}{C}\sum_{c^{\prime}=1}^{C}\log\pi_{c^{\prime}}\right),\quad\log\pi_{c}=\gamma\cdot\log(1-f_{c}),

(6)

where
\gamma
and
\beta
are set to provide minority classes a positive initial bias and majority classes a negative one while keeping the mean shift zero. In practice, we use
\gamma=2
and
\beta=0.8
.
Thereafter,
\delta_{c}
is learned end-to-end without any explicit constraints in class frequency or hardness.

Training objective.
Following SAM-Path, the training objective sums per-class Dice, focal,
and MSE-IoU losses:

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{c=1}^{C}\!\bigl[(1-\alpha)\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dice}}(\hat{y}_{c},y_{c})+\alpha\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{focal}}(\hat{y}_{c},y_{c})+\mu\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mse}}\!\bigl(\widehat{\mathrm{iou}}_{c},\mathrm{IoU}(\hat{y}_{c},y_{c})\bigr)\bigr],

(7)

where
y_{c}
is the ground-truth mask,
\alpha\!\in\![0,1]
balances
Dice and focal terms, and
\mu\!>\!0
weights IoU regression.

\ell_{2}
regularisation
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{reg}}\!=\!\lambda\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_{2}^{2}

(
\lambda\!=\!10^{-4}
) prevents unbounded bias growth;
We use a higher learning rate(
\eta_{b}
) for
\delta_{c}
(100 times the base rate
\eta
) to encourage rapid adaptation. Note that DFA adds almost negligible training cost to the overall model as it needs only one scalar parameter per class and one broadcast logit shift per cross-attention layer.

Limitations and future work.

DFA currently applies a single global bias per class; spatially adaptive or
region-conditioned extensions could capture instance-level difficulty more
precisely. Broader validation across architectures and modalities, and a
theoretical analysis of convergence, remain directions for future work.

5
Conclusion

These results confirm that focal loss alone is insufficient, and frequency-based
reweighting is unreliable: CFFA fails when rare classes are not inherently hard,
and HCFA requires a costly pre-training stage. DFA resolves all three by learning
class-specific difficulty continuously within cross-attention, consistently
outperforming all baselines across three benchmarks with only
C
additional
scalar parameters. The warm-start prior prevents gradient starvation, the
self-gating factor provides implicit curriculum regulation, and encoding difficulty
at the representation level yields model-wide improvements over post-hoc gradient
corrections.
