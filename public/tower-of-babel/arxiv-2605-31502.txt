Complex Diophantine approximations and cusp excursions
Nathan Dalaklis, Yan Mary He

Abstract
. We study the Hausdorff dimension spectrum of asymptotic approximation rates of complex Diophantine approximation and that of the asymptotic average excursion time of cusp excursions on the Bianchi orbifold ℍ 3 / PSL ⁡ ( 2 , ℤ ⁡ [ i ] ) \mathbb{H}^{3}/\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z}[i]) via a unified approach using the Hurwitz map. In particular, we construct a conformal graph directed system (CGDS) for the Hurwitz map and show that the Lyapunov exponent of the Hurwitz CGDS simultaneously captures the asymptotic approximation rate and the the asymptotic average excursion time. Applying the multifractal analysis of Lyapunov exponents for this system, we obtain a formula and real-analyticity for the Hausdorff dimension spectrum functions.



1.
Introduction

Classical Diophantine approximation studies the extent to which real numbers can be approximated by rational numbers. In particular, the quality of rational approximation is encoded by continued fractions: if
p_{n}/q_{n}
denotes the
n
-th convergent of a real continued fraction, then the exponential behavior of the error

\left|x-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|

is governed by the growth rate of the denominators
q_{n}
. This relationship connects number theory, symbolic dynamics, and hyperbolic geometry.

One of the main goals of this paper is to study an analogous problem in the complex setting. We consider Hurwitz complex continued fractions, where the partial quotients lie in the Gaussian integers
\mathbb{Z}[i]
, and we investigate the exponential rate of complex Diophantine approximation by Gaussian rationals.
The Hurwitz complex continued fraction algorithm produces convergents
p_{n}/q_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}(i)
. The first main goal of this paper is to study the level sets on which the approximation error

\left|z-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|

has a prescribed exponential decay rate.

On the other hand, the study of
cusp excursions
in hyperbolic geometry
provides a way to measure the recurrence of geodesic trajectories to the non-compact part of a hyperbolic orbifold, which quantifies how geodesics distribute themselves in a finite-volume but non-compact hyperbolic space. In particular, consider
the Bianchi orbifold
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{H}^{3}/\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z}[i])
. This orbifold is non-compact and has a cusp corresponding to infinity. A geodesic on
\mathcal{O}
may travel arbitrarily far into this cusp before returning to the compact part of the orbifold. Such behavior is called a cusp excursion. Following Baumgartner–Pollicott
[
1
]
, one can define an
asymptotic average excursion time
for
\gamma
. The second main goal of this paper is to study the level sets of (lifts of) geodesics having a prescribed asymptotic average excursion time.

We study these two problems via a
unified approach
using the Hurwitz map
T:X\to X

given by

T(z)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{z}-f\!\left(\frac{1}{z}\right),&z\neq 0,\\[10.00002pt]
0,&z=0,\end{cases}

where
X:=\{x+iy\in\mathbb{C}:x,y\in[-1/2,1/2)\}
and
f(w)
denotes the nearest Gaussian integer to
w
, namely

f(w):=\left\lfloor\operatorname{Re}(w)+\frac{1}{2}\right\rfloor+i\left\lfloor\operatorname{Im}(w)+\frac{1}{2}\right\rfloor.

The Hurwitz map generates the complex continued fraction algorithm, in the same way that the Gauss map generates the classical continued fraction algorithm for real numbers. Moreover, the Hurwitz complex continued fraction algorithm is closely related to, but distinct from, the classical real continued fraction algorithm: when restricted to real numbers, its convergents form a subsequence of the real continued fraction convergents, with controlled omissions and comparable Diophantine approximation properties; see
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
.

We encode the Hurwitz map by a
conformal graph directed system
(CGDS) in the sense of Urbański, Roy and Munday (see
[
9
, Definition 19.3.1]
). The Lyapunov exponent of this system simultaneously records the exponential rate of complex Diophantine approximation and the asymptotic average cusp excursion time.
Our results are then obtained by applying the
multifractal analysis
of Lyapunov exponents developed in
[
4
]
for cofinitely regular finitely irreducible CGDS.

We note that Mauldin and Urbański introduced and studied the fractal geometry of a complex continued fraction algorithm on the ball of radius
\frac{1}{2}
centered at
\frac{1}{2}
in the complex plane in
[
6
]
using a
conformal iterated function system
(CIFS). Later in
[
3
]
, Chousionis, Leykekhman, and Urbański refined this analysis by studying the dimension spectrum of the limit set of this CIFS. Although the dimension spectrum and Lyapunov spectrum are often closely related, our analysis is distinct as the Hurwitz CGDS is not a CIFS.

1.1.
Statement of results

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
and consider the Hurwitz complex continued fraction of
z
(i.e., continued fraction expansion of
z
using Gaussian integers
\mathbb{Z}[i]
). Let
p_{n}/q_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}(i)
be Hurwitz convergents of
z
; see Section 3 for details. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we study level sets of the
asymptotic approximation rate

X(\alpha):=\left\{z\in X:-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left|z-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|=\alpha\right\}.

Our first main theorem gives a formula and analyticity for the Hausdorff dimension of these level sets.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\lambda_{\min}
denote the minimal Lyapunov exponent of the Hurwitz conformal graph directed system. For every
\alpha>\lambda_{\min}
, the Hausdorff dimension
S(\alpha):=\operatorname{HD}(X(\alpha))

is given by

S(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\mathcal{P}(-q(\alpha))-q(\alpha)\right)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\{\mathcal{P}(-q)-q\alpha\}.

Moreover, the function
\alpha\mapsto S(\alpha)
is real-analytic on
(\lambda_{\min},\infty)
.

Here
\mathcal{P}
is the pressure associated to the geometric potential of the Hurwitz coding, and
q(\alpha)
is determined by the corresponding pressure equations. See Theorem
3.8
for details.

Let
\mathcal{O}:=\mathbb{H}^{3}/\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z}[i])
and let
\gamma\subset\mathcal{O}
be a geodesic issuing from the cusp.
Following Baumgartner–Pollicott
[
1
]
, one can define an
asymptotic average excursion time
for
\gamma
as follows. If
t_{n}^{*}(\gamma)
denotes the corresponding sequence of maximal excursion times, then the asymptotic average excursion time is
given by
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{t_{n}^{*}(\gamma)}{n}
,
whenever the limit exists.

Our second main theorem gives a formula and real-analyticity for the Hausdorff dimension of level sets of asymptotic average excursion time.
Let
z\in X
and let
\gamma_{z}
be a lift of a geodesic in
\mathcal{O}
such that
\gamma_{z}
has endpoints
(z,\infty)
.
Set

G(\alpha):=\left\{z\in X:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{t_{n}^{*}(\gamma_{z})}{n}=\alpha\right\}.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
\alpha>\lambda_{\min}
, the Hausdorff dimension
\hat{S}(\alpha):=\operatorname{HD}(G(\alpha))

is given by

\hat{S}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\mathcal{P}(-q(\alpha))-q(\alpha)\right)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\{\mathcal{P}(-q)-q\alpha\}.

Moreover, the function
\alpha\mapsto\hat{S}(\alpha)
is real analytic on
(\lambda_{\min},\infty)
.

1.2.
Strategies of the proofs

We proceed symbolically. For any
\omega
in the symbolic space
E_{A}^{\infty}
of the Hurwitz CGDS,
\pi(\omega)
is a Gaussian irrational number in
X
, where
\pi:E_{A}^{\infty}\to J
is the projection map and
J\subset X
is the limit set of the Hurwitz CGDS.

Denote by
\lambda(\omega)
the Lyapunov exponent of
\omega
.
The connection between
\lambda(\omega)
, the asymptotic approximation rate and the asymptotic average excursion time is given by the following identity

\displaystyle\lambda(\omega)=2\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|q_{n}|

\displaystyle=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left|\pi(\omega)-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{t_{n}^{*}(\pi(\omega))}{n}.

Thus the Lyapunov multifractal decomposition of the Hurwitz CGDS gives a unified framework for studying both complex Diophantine approximation and cusp excursions. We obtain a description of the Lyapunov spectrum in terms of the associated pressure function by applying the general theory of multifractal analysis of Birkhoff averages for confinitely regular finitely irreducible CGDMS satisfying the strong open set condition developed in
[
4
]
. In particular, our symbolic approach to the multifractal analysis on
J
is enough for our conclusions by
[
4
, Proposition 6.5]
and the countable stability of Hausdorff dimension.

1.3.
Organization of the paper

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we construct the Hurwitz conformal graph directed system and establish the thermodynamic properties needed later. In Section 3 we relate Lyapunov exponents to complex Diophantine approximation and prove the first main theorem. In Section 4 we apply the same Lyapunov analysis to cusp excursions on the Bianchi orbifold and prove the second main theorem.

2.
Thermodynamic formalism

In this section, we construct a conformal graph directed system (CGDS) associated to the Hurwitz map, which is the thermodynamic framework used throughout the paper. We verify the properties of this CGDS needed to apply thermodynamic formalism: finite irreducibility, finite primitivity, the strong open set condition and cofinitely regularity. We then describe the associated pressure function, the Lyapunov pressure, and the equilibrium measures that will be used later in the multifractal analysis of approximation rates and cusp excursions.

2.1.
The Hurwitz map

Let

X:=\{x+iy\in\mathbb{C}:x,y\in[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})\}

be the unit square in the complex plane centered at the origin.
Recall that the
Hurwitz map

is defined as

T(z):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{z}-f(\frac{1}{z}),{\rm if~}z\neq 0;\\
0,{\rm if~}z=0.\end{cases}

where

denotes rounding

The inverse branches of

Definition 2.2
.

A GDMS is a triple
(\Phi,\mathcal{G},A)
where
\Phi=\{\phi_{e}\}_{e\in E}
is a collection of maps between a finite family of compact metric spaces
\{X_{v}\}_{v\in V}
,
G=(V,E,i,t)
is a directed multigraph, and
A:E\times E\to\{0,1\}
is a matrix with
A_{ef}=1
if and only if
\phi_{e}\circ\phi_{f}:X_{t(f)}\to X_{i(e)}
is allowable and defined.

Every GDMS is a subsystem of its underlying
graph directed system (GDS)
which is the system where
A_{ef}=1
if and only if
t(e)=i(f)
. A GDS is an
IFS
if
|V|=1
and
A_{ef}=1
for all
e,f\in E
. We call a GDS
proper
if it is not an IFS or a subsystem of an IFS.

Definition 2.3
.

A GDMS is called a
conformal GDMS
(CGDMS), if its underlying GDS satisfies the following properties

1.

For every
v\in V
, there is a
d
such that
X_{v}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is regular closed.

2.

\Phi
satisfies the open set condition,

\phi_{e_{1}}({\rm Int}(X_{t(e_{1})}))\cap\phi_{e_{2}}({\rm Int}(X_{t(e_{2})}))=\emptyset.

3.

For every
v\in V
there is an open, connected and bounded set
W_{v}
such that
X_{v}\subseteq W_{v}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and such that for every
e\in E
with
t(e)=v
, the map
\phi_{e}
extends to a contracting conformal diffeomorphism of
W_{v}
into
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and all fo these extensions have a common contraction ratio
s<1
.

4.

There exists constants
L\geq 1
and
\kappa>0
such that

\left||\phi_{e}^{\prime}(y)|-|\phi_{e}^{\prime}(x)|\right|\leq L|\phi^{\prime}_{e}(x)|\cdot||y-x||^{\kappa}

for every
e\in E
and every pair of points
x,y\in W_{t(e)}
.

Definition 2.4
.

We say a CGDMS is
finitely irreducible
if there exists a finite list of admissible paths (or words)
I
such that for every
(a,b)\in E\times E
there is a
\tau(a,b)\in I
such that
a\tau(a,b)b
is admissible. If all paths (or words) in
I
are the same length we say the system is
finitely primitive
.

2.3.
Pressure

The pressure of a CGDMS is given by

\mathcal{P}(t):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}Z_{n}(t):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\omega\in E_{A}^{n}}\||\phi^{\prime}_{\omega}|\|^{t}_{X_{t(\omega)}}

where

\|\varphi\|_{X_{t(\omega)}}:=\|\varphi|_{X_{t(\omega)}}\|_{\infty}.

The finiteness parameter
\theta
of a CGDMS is given by

\theta:=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\mathcal{P}(t)<\infty\}.

When
\mathcal{P}(\theta)=\infty
we say that the CGDMS is cofinitely regular. By
[
9
, Proposition 19.4.5]
,
P(t)<\infty
if and only if
Z_{1}(t)<\infty
.

Lemma 2.10
.

The Hurwitz CGDS is cofinitely regular with
\theta=1
.

Proof.

We will show that the first partition function of the Hurwitz CGDS,
Z_{1}(t)

given by

\zeta_{Q[i]}(t)=\sum_{n,m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}+m^{2}}\right)^{t},

which for real
t
converges if
t>1
. Implying that
\theta=1
. We begin with the upper bound. Here we have

\displaystyle Z_{1}(t):=\sum_{e\in E}\||\phi_{e}^{\prime}|\|^{t}_{X_{t(e)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(a,Y)\in E}\left\|\left|\frac{1}{(z+a)^{2}}\right|\right\|_{Y}^{t}\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,Y)\in E\\
|a|\geq\sqrt{8}\end{subarray}}\left\|\left|\frac{1}{(z+a)^{2}}\right|\right\|_{Y}^{t}

\displaystyle\geq 12\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\\
|a|\geq\sqrt{8}\end{subarray}}\inf\left\{\left\|\left|\frac{1}{(z+a)^{2}}\right|\right\|_{Y}^{t}\;\Bigg|\;Y\in\mathcal{X}\right\}

\displaystyle=12\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\\
|a|\geq\sqrt{8}\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{1}{a^{2}}\right|^{t}\inf\left\{\left\|\left|\frac{a^{2}}{(z+a)^{2}}\right|\right\|_{Y}^{t}\;\Bigg|\;Y\in\mathcal{X}\right\}.

Now since all
Y\in\mathcal{X}
are compact,
\mathcal{X}
is a finite set,
|(z+a)|^{2}>1
, and since
\lim_{a\to\infty}\left|\frac{a^{2}}{(z+a)^{2}}\right|=1
we obtain that there is some
K,M>0
such that for all
a\geq\sqrt{8}
,

M>K(a):=\inf\left\{\left\|\left|\frac{a^{2}}{(z+a)^{2}}\right|\right\|_{Y}^{t}\;\Bigg|\;Y\in\mathcal{X}\right\}>K>0.

And so,

Z_{1}(t)\geq 12K\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\\
|a|\geq\sqrt{8}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{|a^{2}|}\right)^{t}=12K(\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}[i]}(t)-L).

For some constant
L
. Thus,
\theta\leq 1
.

For the lower bound,

\displaystyle Z_{1}(t)=\sum_{e\in E}\||\phi_{e}^{\prime}|\|^{t}_{X_{t(e)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(a,Y)\in E}\left\|\left|\frac{1}{(z+a)^{2}}\right|\right\|_{Y}^{t}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,Y)\in E\\
|a|\geq\sqrt{8}\end{subarray}}\left\|\left|\frac{1}{(z+a)^{2}}\right|\right\|_{Y}^{t}+L_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 12\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\\
|a|\geq\sqrt{8}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{|a^{2}|}\right)^{t}\sup\left\{\left\|\left|\frac{a^{2}}{(z+a)^{2}}\right|\right\|_{Y}^{t}\;\Bigg|\;Y\in\mathcal{X}\right\}+L_{2}.

By similar reasoning as in the previous case we obtain that there is some
K_{1},M_{1}>0
such that for all
a\geq\sqrt{8}
,

M_{1}>K_{1}(a):=\sup\left\{\left\|\left|\frac{a^{2}}{(z+a)^{2}}\right|\right\|_{Y}^{t}\;\Bigg|\;Y\in\mathcal{X}\right\}>K_{1}>0.

And so

Z_{1}(t)\leq 12M_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\\
|a|\geq\sqrt{8}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{|a^{2}|}\right)^{t}+L_{2}=12M_{1}(\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}[i]}(t)-L)+L_{2}.

For the same constant
L
. Thus,
\theta\geq 1
and so we conclude
\theta=1
. Since
\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}[i]}(\theta)=\infty
, and
Z_{1}(t)
is comparable to
\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}[i]}(t)
,
Z_{1}(\theta)=\infty
which implies that
\mathcal{P}(\theta)=\infty
, and we conclude that the Hurwitz CGDS is cofinitely regular.
∎

We define the
multivariate Lyapunov pressure
by
\mathcal{P}(t,q):=\mathcal{P}(t-q)
. With this multivariate pressure we define the
Manhattan region
of the Lyapunov pressure by

\mathsf{M}:=\left\{(t,q)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\mathcal{P}(t,q)<\infty\right\}

and call
\partial\mathsf{M}
the corresponding
Manhattan curve
. The next corollary follows directly from Lemma
2.10
.

Corollary 2.11
.

For the multivariate Lyapunov pressure of the Hurwitz CGDS,
\partial\mathsf{M}
is given by the line
L:q(t)=t-1
, and
\mathsf{M}
is the half-plane
\{(t,q)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\;|\;q<t-1\}
.

2.4.
Symbolic Pressure and Measures

The geometric potential of a CGDMS is given by

\Xi_{\Phi}(\omega):=\log|\phi^{\prime}_{\omega_{1}}(\pi(\sigma(\omega)))|,\;\forall\omega\in E_{A}^{\infty}.

Since
\Phi
is a CGDS, by
[
9
, Lemma 19.4.1]
,
\Xi_{\Phi}
is acceptable.
For any
\sigma
-invariant probability measure
\mu
on
E_{A}^{\infty}
, the
\mu
-characteristic Lyapunov exponent of
\Phi
is given by

\lambda(\sigma):=-\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}\Xi_{\Phi}d\mu>0.

At a point
\omega\in E_{A}^{\infty}
we take

\lambda(\omega):=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\log|\phi^{\prime}_{\sigma^{j}(\omega)_{1}}(\pi(\sigma^{j+1}(\omega)))|

if the limit exists.
Take
f_{t,q}:=(t-q)\Xi_{\Phi}
. The pressure of this potential on the symbolic representation is given by

\mathcal{P}(f_{t,q}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{\omega\in E_{A}^{n}}\exp(\overline{S_{n}}f_{t,q}([\omega]))

where for a set
\Lambda\subseteq E_{A}^{\infty}

\overline{S_{n}}g(\Lambda):=\sup\{S_{n}g(\omega):\omega\in\Lambda\}.

Since
\Phi
is finitely irreducible,
\mathcal{P}(f_{t,q})=\mathcal{P}(t,q)
by
[
9
, Proposition 19.4.6.(a)]
. Further, since
\Phi
is finitely primitive,
(t-q)\Xi_{\Phi}
has a unique Gibbs state
\mu_{t,q}
that is totally ergodic by
[
9
, Theorem 17.4.6]
.

Lemma 2.12
.

For the Hurwitz CGDS
\Phi
, if
(t,q)\in\mathsf{M}
we have
H_{\mu_{t,q}}(\mathcal{U}_{E})<\infty
where
\mathcal{U}_{E}
is the partition of
E_{A}^{\infty}
into its initial cylinders of length
1
.

Proof.

Let
\rho_{e}\in[e]
and recall from the Gibbs property that,

\mu_{t,q}([e])\asymp\exp((t-q)\Xi_{\Phi}(\rho_{e})-\mathcal{P}(t,q)).

Now we estimate
H_{\mu_{t,q}}(\mathcal{U}_{E})
using the Gibbs property.

\displaystyle H_{\mu_{t,q}}(\mathcal{U}_{E})

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E}-\mu_{t,q}([e])\log\mu_{t,q}([e])

\displaystyle<\sum_{e\in E}-\mu_{t,q}([e])\log(C(\exp((t-q)\Xi_{\Phi}(\rho_{e})-\mathcal{P}(t,q))))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}(t,q)+\log C-\sum_{e\in E}\mu_{t,q}([e])|\phi_{e}^{\prime}(\pi(\sigma(\rho_{e})))|^{t-q}.

Since
(t,q)\in\mathsf{M}
, we have
\mathcal{P}(t,q)<\infty
. Thus,
Z_{1}(t-q)<\infty
by
[
9
, Proposition 19.4.5]
. Moreover,

\sum_{e\in E}|\phi_{e}^{\prime}(\pi(\sigma(\rho_{e})))|^{t-q}<Z_{1}(t-q)<\infty.

And so, since
\sum_{e\in E}\mu_{t,q}([e])
converges absolutely, we have

\sum_{e\in E}\mu_{t,q}([e])|\phi_{e}^{\prime}(\pi(\sigma(\rho_{e})))|^{t-q}<\infty.

Hence,
H_{\mu_{t,q}}(\mathcal{U}_{E})<\infty
.

∎

By
[
9
, Theorem 17.4.7 and Theorem 17.5.1]
we have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.13
.

For the Hurwitz CGDS
\Phi
and
(t,q)\in\mathsf{M}
, we have

\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}\Xi_{\Phi}d\mu_{t,q}>-\infty.

Consequently
\mu_{t,q}
is the unique equilibrium state for the potential
(t-q)\Xi_{\Phi}
.

3.
Complex Diophantine approximation

In this section we relate the Lyapunov exponent of the Hurwitz conformal graph directed system to complex Diophantine approximation and prove Theorem
1.1
. In Section 3.1,
we recall the Hurwitz complex continued fraction expansion and its basic properties. In Sections 3.2 and 3.3, we show that the exponential decay rate of the approximation error
\left|z-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|
is encoded dynamically by the Lyapunov exponent of the Hurwitz conformal graph directed system.
This allows us to apply the multifractal analysis developed in
[
4
]
to obtain the multifractal spectrum for complex Diophantine approximation.

3.1.
Complex continued fraction

Any complex number
z\in\mathbb{C}
can be written as the continued fraction

z=a_{0}+\cfrac{1}{a_{1}+\cfrac{1}{a_{2}+\cfrac{1}{a_{3}+\dots}}}\quad,

where
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\dots\in\mathbb{Z}[i].

For
n\geq 0
, define the sequence
\{p_{n}\}
and
\{q_{n}\}
by the following recurrence relations:

\begin{cases}p_{-1}:=1,\quad p_{0}:=a_{0},\quad p_{n+1}:=a_{n+1}p_{n}+p_{n-1},\\
q_{-1}:=0,\quad q_{0}:=1,\quad q_{n+1}:=a_{n+1}q_{n}+q_{n-1}.\end{cases}

The following lemma is a standard result in the study of continued fraction algorithms. We omit the proof and refer the interested reader to
[
5
, Theorem 6.1]
for more details.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}:=a_{0}+\cfrac{1}{a_{1}+\cfrac{1}{a_{2}+\cfrac{1}{\dots+\cfrac{1}{a_{n}}}}}\in\mathbb{Q}(i).

Moreover, we have
\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\to z.

Define also the
(n)
-th complete quotient
z_{n}
as

z_{0}:=z,\text{ and }z_{n}:=a_{n}+\frac{1}{z_{n+1}}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
have a Hurwitz complex continued fraction expansion, with convergents
p_{n}/q_{n}
. Assume that the limit

\ell:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|q_{n}|

exists and is finite. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\left|z-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|=2\ell.

Proof.

For Hurwitz convergents we have the exact error formula (see
[
5
, Proof of Proposition 3.3]
)

\left|z-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|=\frac{1}{|q_{n}|^{2}\left|z_{n+1}+\frac{q_{n-1}}{q_{n}}\right|},

where
z_{n+1}
is the
(n+1)
-st complete quotient.
Hence

-\frac{1}{n}\log\left|z-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|=2\frac{\log|q_{n}|}{n}+\frac{1}{n}\log\left|z_{n+1}+\frac{q_{n-1}}{q_{n}}\right|.

Thus it suffices to prove that

\frac{1}{n}\log\left|z_{n+1}+\frac{q_{n-1}}{q_{n}}\right|\to 0.
Using

z_{n+1}=a_{n+1}+\frac{1}{z_{n+2}}
and
q_{n+1}=a_{n+1}q_{n}+q_{n-1}
, we obtain

z_{n+1}+\frac{q_{n-1}}{q_{n}}=\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}+\frac{1}{z_{n+2}}.

In the Hurwitz algorithm, since the remainder
z_{n}-f(z_{n})
lies in the fundamental square
X
, we have

|z_{n}-f(z_{n})|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
and thus
|z_{n+1}|\geq\sqrt{2}
.
Also, for Hurwitz convergents we have
|q_{n+1}|>|q_{n}|
.
Using

z_{n+1}+\frac{q_{n-1}}{q_{n}}=\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}+\frac{1}{z_{n+2}},

the reverse triangle inequality gives

\left|z_{n+1}+\frac{q_{n-1}}{q_{n}}\right|\geq\left|\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\right|-\left|\frac{1}{z_{n+2}}\right|\geq 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},

while the triangle inequality gives

\left|z_{n+1}+\frac{q_{n-1}}{q_{n}}\right|\leq\left|\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\right|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\leq 2\left|\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\right|.

Therefore,

\frac{1}{n}\log\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{n}\log\left|z_{n+1}+\frac{q_{n-1}}{q_{n}}\right|\leq\frac{\log 2}{n}+\frac{\log|q_{n+1}|-\log|q_{n}|}{n}.

The left-hand side tends to
0
. Since
\frac{1}{n}\log|q_{n}|\to\ell
,
we also have
\frac{1}{n}\log|q_{n+1}|\to\ell,

and therefore

\frac{\log|q_{n+1}|-\log|q_{n}|}{n}\to 0.

By the squeeze theorem, we have

\frac{1}{n}\log\left|z_{n+1}+\frac{q_{n-1}}{q_{n}}\right|\to 0.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Continued fractions and diameters of cylinder sets in GDMS

Definition 3.3
.

For a finite block
a_{1}\dots a_{n}\in E_{A}^{n}
, the
cylinder of depth
n
associated to this block
is

[a_{1},\dots,a_{n}]:=\{\omega\in E_{A}^{\infty}:\ \omega_{1}=a_{1},\dots,\omega_{n}=a_{n}\}.

In other words,
\pi([a_{1}\dots a_{n}])
is the set of all points in
X
who have a Hurwitz coding whose first
n
digits are
a_{1_{1}},\dots,a_{n_{1}}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
a_{1}\dots a_{n}\in E_{A}^{n}
, and let
p_{k},q_{k}
be the corresponding convergents.

Define the Möbius map

M_{n}(w):=\frac{p_{n}w+p_{n-1}}{q_{n}w+q_{n-1}}.

Then, up to boundary points,

\pi([a_{1}\dots a_{n}])=M_{n}(X_{t(a_{n})})
.

Proof.

Fix
z\in\pi([a_{1}\dots a_{n}])
, and let
w:=T^{n}(z)\in X.

Since
z
has a coding where the first
n
digits are
a_{1_{1}},\dots,a_{n_{1}}
, a Hurwitz continued fraction expansion of
z
may be written as

z=\cfrac{1}{a_{1_{1}}+\cfrac{1}{a_{2_{1}}+\cfrac{1}{\ddots+\cfrac{1}{a_{n_{1}}+w}}}}.

Unwinding this expression using the recurrence relations for
p_{k},q_{k}
gives

z=\frac{p_{n}w+p_{n-1}}{q_{n}w+q_{n-1}}=M_{n}(w).

Thus every
z\in\pi([a_{1}\dots a_{n}])
belongs to
M_{n}(X_{t(a_{n})})
.

Conversely, if
w\in X_{t(a_{n})}
, then
z:=M_{n}(w)

has a continued fraction expansion beginning with the digits
a_{1_{1}},\dots,a_{n_{1}}
, with a tail given by a coding of
w
. Note that if this coding is not unique then
w\in\partial X_{t(a_{n})}
. Hence,
z\in\pi([a_{1},\dots,a_{n}])
.

The conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

With the notation above, we have

\operatorname{diam}(\pi([a_{1}\dots a_{n}]))\asymp|q_{n}|^{-2},

where the implied constants are independent of
n
.

Proof.

Since

\pi([a_{1}\dots a_{n}])=M_{n}(X_{t(a_{n})}),

it suffices to estimate the size of the image of
X_{t(a_{n})}
under
M_{n}
.

The derivative of the Möbius map
M_{n}
is

M_{n}^{\prime}(w)=\frac{p_{n}q_{n-1}-p_{n-1}q_{n}}{(q_{n}w+q_{n-1})^{2}}.

Using the determinant identity
p_{n}q_{n-1}-p_{n-1}q_{n}=\pm 1
,
we obtain

|M_{n}^{\prime}(w)|=\frac{1}{|q_{n}w+q_{n-1}|^{2}}.

We first derive an upper bound. Since
X_{t(a_{n})}\subset X
and
X
is bounded, there exists
R>0
(e.g.,
R=\sqrt{2}/2
) such that

|w|\leq R
for all
w\in X_{t(a_{n})}
.
Hence

|q_{n}w+q_{n-1}|\leq|q_{n}|\,|w|+|q_{n-1}|\leq R|q_{n}|+|q_{n-1}|.

For Hurwitz convergents we have
|q_{n-1}|<|q_{n}|
, therefore
|q_{n}w+q_{n-1}|\leq(R+1)|q_{n}|
.
It follows that

|M_{n}^{\prime}(w)|\geq\frac{1}{(R+1)^{2}|q_{n}|^{2}}.

For the reverse bound, write

q_{n}w+q_{n-1}=q_{n}\left(w+\frac{q_{n-1}}{q_{n}}\right).

Since
w\in X
and the ratios
q_{n-1}/q_{n}
stay in a controlled region for the Hurwitz algorithm, there exists a constant
c>0
such that
\left|w+\frac{q_{n-1}}{q_{n}}\right|\geq c
for all
w\in X
away from the usual boundary ambiguities. Hence
|q_{n}w+q_{n-1}|\geq c|q_{n}|
, and therefore

|M_{n}^{\prime}(w)|\leq\frac{1}{c^{2}|q_{n}|^{2}}.

Thus
|M_{n}^{\prime}(w)|\asymp|q_{n}|^{-2}
for all
w\in X_{t(a_{n})}
.
Since
M_{n}:=\phi_{a_{1}}\circ\dots\circ\phi_{a_{n}}
is an admissible composition of maps from
\Phi
,
[
9
, Lemmas 19.3.5]
and Lemma
2.7
imply

\operatorname{diam}(M_{n}(X))=\operatorname{diam}(\pi([a_{1}\dots a_{n}]))\asymp|q_{n}|^{-2}.

∎

3.3.
Complex Diophantine approximation rates

Proposition 3.6
.

For any
\omega\in E_{A}^{\infty}
, we have

\lambda(\omega)=2\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|q_{n}|=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left|\pi(\omega)-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|.

Proof.

We first observe that by Lemma
3.5
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda(\omega)

\displaystyle:=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\log|\phi^{\prime}_{\sigma^{j}(\omega)_{1}}(\pi(\sigma^{j+1}(\omega)))|

\displaystyle=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{\rm diam}(\pi([\omega_{1}\dots\omega_{n}]))=2\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|q_{n}|.

On the other hand, by Lemma
3.2
, we have

-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left|\pi(\omega)-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|=2\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|q_{n}|.

Therefore, the conclusion follows.
∎

Let
\lambda_{\rm min}
be the minimal Lyapunov exponent. For any
\alpha\geq\lambda_{\rm min}
, let

X(\alpha):=\left\{z\in X:-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left|z-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|=\alpha\right\}

be the collection of complex numbers in
X
for which the asymptotic exponential rate of (complex) Diophantine approximation equals
\alpha
. Define the function
S\colon(\lambda_{\rm min},\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
by

S(\alpha):={\rm HD}(X(\alpha))

where
{\rm HD}(X(\alpha))
denotes the Hausdorff dimension of
X(\alpha)
. We will study
S(\alpha)
by considering the following projected Lyapunov spectrum.

For a CGDMS with limit set
J
, the projected Lyapunov multifractal decomposition of
E_{A}^{\infty}
is given by

J(\alpha):=\pi(E_{A}^{\infty}(\alpha)):=\pi(\{\omega\in E_{A}^{\infty}:\lambda(\omega)=\alpha\})

since
\Phi
is finitely irreducible, cofinitely regular, and satisfies the SOSC, one may, without loss of generality, only consider those points in
J(\alpha)
with unique codings (see
[
4
, Proposition 6.5]
). In either case, the Lyapunov specturm of
\Phi
is given by

t(\alpha):={\rm HD}(J(\alpha)).

Remark 3.7
.

We observe that
t(\alpha)=S(\alpha)
by Proposition
3.2
as
X(\alpha)\setminus J(\alpha)
is countable.

The following theorem is a special case of
[
4
, Theorem 3.1 (see also Theorem 7.1)]
that describes the projected Lyapunov spectra of a CGDMS in terms of pressure.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\Phi=\{\phi_{e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{N}}
be a cofinitely regular finitely irreducible CGDMS satisfying the SOSC and let
\mathsf{M}
be the Manhattan region for the Lyapunov pressure. If for all
(t,q)\in\mathsf{M}
,
-\int\Xi_{\Phi}d\mu_{t,q}<\infty
, then the projected Lyapunov spectrum of
\Phi
is given by the unique real analytic solution
(t(\alpha),q(\alpha))
to the system

\begin{cases}\mathcal{P}(t,q)=q\alpha\\
\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}}{\partial q}(t,q)=\alpha\end{cases}

on the interval
\alpha\in(\alpha_{\min},\infty)
and

t(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}(-q(\alpha))-q(\alpha))=\frac{1}{\alpha}\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\{\mathcal{P}(-q)-q\alpha\},\forall\alpha\in(\alpha_{\min},\infty)

where
\mathcal{P}(q):=\mathcal{P}(0,-q)
.

Now we give a proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Remark
2.9
, Corollary
2.13
, Lemma
2.10
, and Theorem
2.5
the Hurwitz CGDS satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
3.8
. By Remark
3.7
, the conclusion follows.
∎

4.
Cusp excursions on the Bianchi orbifold

In this section, we apply the multifractal analysis of Lyapunov exponent of the Hurwitz CGDS to cusp excursions on the Bianchi orbifold
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{H}^{3}/\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z}[i])
and prove Theorem
1.2
.
We first recall the relevant hyperbolic geometry in Section 4.1. In Section 4.2, we define the asymptotic average excursion time of a geodesic issuing from the cusp. We show that it is equal to the Lyapunov exponent of the Hurwitz CGDS and prove Theorem
1.2
.

4.1.
Hyperbolic geometry

Consider the upper half-space model of the hyperbolic
3
-space, i.e.,

\mathbb{H}^{3}:=\{(w,t)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\},

with boundary at infinity
\partial\mathbb{H}^{3}:=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
. The hyperbolic metric is

ds^{2}=\frac{|dw|^{2}+dt^{2}}{t^{2}}.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
. An
oriented geodesic with forward endpoint
z
is a semicircle or vertical line joining its two boundary endpoints.

Let
\Gamma:=\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z}[i])
. Then
\Gamma
is a discrete subgroup of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
with torsion elements, therefore the quotient
\mathcal{O}:=\mathbb{H}^{3}/\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z}[i])
is an orbifold. Moreover, since
\Gamma
is a
Bianchi group
,
\mathcal{O}
is called a Bianchi orbifold.

The lift of the cusp of the Bianchi orbifold
\mathcal{O}
in the universal cover
\mathbb{H}^{3}
is the cusp at
\infty
, which is represented by high horoballs of the form

\mathcal{H}_{Y}:=\{(w,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{3}:t\geq Y\}.

We say that a geodesic makes a cusp excursion if, after applying a suitable element of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z}[i])
, the image geodesic rises to large height
t
.
Fix the cusp of
\mathcal{O}
corresponding to
\infty
, and let
\Gamma_{\infty}<\Gamma
denote its stabilizer.

For each
z\in\mathbb{C}
, let
\widetilde{\gamma}_{z}\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}
be the oriented geodesic with
endpoints
(\infty,z)
, oriented from
\infty
to
z
, and let

\gamma_{z}:=\pi(\widetilde{\gamma}_{z})

be its projection to
\mathcal{O}
. Thus each
z\in\mathbb{C}
determines an oriented geodesic
in
\mathcal{O}
issuing from the chosen cusp. If
\eta\in\Gamma_{\infty}
, then
\widetilde{\gamma}_{\eta(z)}=\eta(\widetilde{\gamma}_{z})
. Hence

\widetilde{\gamma}_{\eta(z)}
and
\widetilde{\gamma}_{z}
project to the same oriented geodesic in
\mathcal{O}
.
Therefore the assignment
z\mapsto\gamma_{z}

depends only on the
\Gamma_{\infty}
-orbit of
z
.

4.2.
Asymptotic average excurison time

In this section, we summarize Baumgartner–Pollicott’s definition of
asymptotic average excurison time
; see
[
1
, Section 5]
.
Let
\gamma
be a geodesic in
\mathcal{O}
issued from the cusp.
Let
\tilde{\gamma}_{0}
be a lift of
\gamma
to
\mathbb{H}^{3}
such that the repelling endpoint is
\infty
.
Then
\tilde{\gamma}_{0}
has endpoints
(z,\infty)
with
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
\beta
be the representative of
z
in
X
, i.e.,
\beta=[0,a_{1},a_{2},\dots]=[0,a_{1}(z),a_{2}(z),\dots].

For
n\geq 1
, following Baumgartner–Pollicott
[
1
, Section 5.1]
, we define

\tilde{\gamma}_{n}:=\bigl(T_{(-1)^{n-1}a_{n}}S\bigr)\circ\tilde{\gamma}_{n-1},

where

S=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad T_{q}=\begin{pmatrix}1&q\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\quad(q\in\mathbb{Z}[i]).

The matrix
S
performs the reciprocal step
z\mapsto-1/z
, while
T_{q}
subtracts the chosen digit. Therefore,
T_{(\pm a_{n})}S
implement one continued-fraction step on the geodesic.

The
intersection times

t_{n}
are defined by the condition
\tilde{\gamma}_{n}(t_{n})\in H(0)
,
where
H(0)
is the unit hemisphere.
Finally, the
excursion times
are defined as

t_{n}^{*}(z):=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}t_{i}.

Baumgartner–Pollicott proved that the
asymptotic average excursion time

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{t_{n}^{*}(z)}{n}
satisfies the following property.

Proposition 4.1

(
[
1
, Proof of Proposition 5.1]
)
.

We have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{t_{n}^{*}(z)}{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\log|q_{n}|}{n},

whenever these limits exist.

Corollary 4.2
.

For any
\omega\in E_{A}^{\infty}
, we have

\lambda(\omega)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\log|q_{n}|}{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{t_{n}^{*}(\pi(\omega))}{n},

whenever these limits exist.

Proof.

The conclusion follows from Proposition
3.6
and Proposition
4.1
.
∎

We give a proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Corollary
4.2
, we have
\hat{S}(\alpha)=S(\alpha)
. Then the conclusion follows from Theorem
1.1
.
∎
