Semi-Device-Independent Certification for Nonlocality without Entanglement
Hanwool Lee, Joonwoo Bae

Abstract
In this work, we present the framework for demonstrating and certifying the distinction between measurements in an entangled basis, called global measurements, and local operations and classical communication (LOCC), known as nonlocality without entanglement (NLWE). To be precise, we show NLWE via a maximum-confidence measurement in terms of a guess per detection event, called a confidence , a fine-grained guessing probability that encompasses both minimum-error and unambiguous state-discrimination strategies. We show that NLWE for unknown measurements can be certified, given the measurement-outcome rates, by bounding the confidence using LOCC; the certification is semi-device-independent in that state preparation is trusted. We illustrate the demonstration and certification of NLWE for antiparallel qubit states. Our results make it feasible to experimentally realize NLWE using measurement devices with imperfections, such as non-unit detection efficiency, since maximum-confidence measurements rely only on detected events.

Quantum state discrimination, one of the most fundamental tasks connected to various applications

The strategies mentioned above for state discrimination contain stringent constraints. Minimum-error discrimination does not exploit an inconclusive outcome; all are conclusive and used to make a guess

The present work aims to establish a framework for both
demonstrating
and
certifying
NLWE in a practical scenario in which a measurement device yields both conclusive and inconclusive outcomes. That is, both conclusive and inconclusive outcomes can be used to demonstrate and certify NLWE. To this end, we consider the maximum-confidence measurement (MCM) strategy, which generalizes minimum-error and unambiguous discrimination. In the certification scenario, we show that NLWE can be certified for unknown measurements; hence, it corresponds to a semi-device-independent (sDI) scenario.

To demonstrate the framework, we consider an ensemble of antiparallel qubit states. On the one hand, we demonstrate NLWE by showing that GLOBAL gives a higher confidence on a guessing task than SEP or LOCC. Since an MCM finds confidence in individual detection events, the distinction between GLOBAL and SEP is shown in a fine-grained guessing task that encompasses the strategies of minimum-error and unambiguous state discrimination. On the other hand, we show the certification of an NLWE after collecting detection events. We reiterate that the certification works while a measurement is not yet verified. The framework for certifying NLWE applies to measurement devices that contain imperfections, including undetected outcomes due to photon loss or low detection efficiency.

Let us first summarize MCMs
15
. While a quantum measurement is in a prediction form, allowing one to compute probabilities of outcomes given a state, an MCM, conversely, is retrodictive in that an outcome makes a guess about preparations
5
;
6
. Let
S=\{q_{\mathrm{x}},\rho_{\mathrm{x}}\}
denote an ensemble i.e., a state
\rho_{\mathrm{x}}
with
a priori
probability
q_{\mathrm{x}}
. The conditional probability in the following is called a
maximum confidence
(MC),

15
,

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{x},\max}:=\max_{M}p_{P|M}(\mathrm{x}|\mathrm{x})=\max_{M_{\mathrm{x}}\geq 0}\frac{q_{\mathrm{x}}\mathrm{tr}[\rho_{\mathrm{x}}M_{\mathrm{x}}]}{\mathrm{tr}[\rho M_{\mathrm{x}}]},

(1)

where
P
and
M
denote preparation and measurement, respectively.

For instance, we have
C_{\mathrm{x},\max}=1
if an outcome
\mathrm{x}
implies the preparation of a state
\rho_{x}
with certainty; this case also corresponds to unambiguous discrimination. Note that the guessing probability in minimum-error discrimination can be reproduced as an average MC, i.e.,
p_{\mathrm{guess}}=\sum_{\mathrm{x}}\mathrm{tr}[\rho M_{\mathrm{x}}]C_{\mathrm{x}}
when all outcomes are conclusive
4
. Hence, an MC above can be interpreted as a fine-grained guessing probability unifying both well-known strategies of state discrimination
4
.

The optimization problem in Eq. (
1
) can be in a linear form
4
or formulated as a semidefinite program (SDP)
28
. It turns out that
C_{\mathrm{x},\max}=\|\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}q_{\mathrm{x}}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}\|_{\infty}
with an operator norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. Therefore, an MCM can be generally implemented by rank-one POVM elements.

Let
C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{({G})}
denote an MC with measurements in GLOBAL, and
C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(S)}
with measurements in SEP. Note that SEP can be generally implemented via stochastic LOCC
13
. For a separable POVM element
M_{\mathrm{x}}
, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\epsilon M_{\mathrm{x}}
can be realized by LOCC. Since an MC remains identical under scaling a POVM element
19
, we have
C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(\mathrm{LOCC})}=C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{({S})}
; an MCM does not find a gap between LOCC and SEP.

Then, MCMs in Eq. (
1
) can be obtained by analyzing the optimality conditions of the SDP. From the approach of the complementarity problem
28
, see also
3
for the minimum-error case, the optimization problem can be reformulated to find optimal parameters, a POVM element
M_{\mathrm{x}}
and an ensemble of complementary states
\{r_{\mathrm{x}},\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}\}
, where
r_{\mathrm{x}}\geq 0
, that satisfy the equality conditions

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}[\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}M_{\mathrm{x}}]=0\penalty\ \mathrm{and}\penalty\ C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(G)}\rho-q_{\mathrm{x}}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}=r_{\mathrm{x}}\sigma_{\mathrm{x}},

(2)

in which
C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(G)}
is obtained from the equality condition. A POVM element may be constrained to be SEP and finds an MC with SEP,
C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(S)}
.

In Eq. (
2
), once the latter condition finds complementary states, a POVM element can be constructed from the former one, called the complementarity slackness. In this case, SEP is optimal if there is a product vector
|e\rangle|f\rangle
, i.e.,
M_{\mathrm{x}}^{(S)}=|e\rangle\langle e|\otimes|f\rangle\langle f|
, such that
\mathrm{tr}[\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}M_{\mathrm{x}}^{(S)}]=0
. In other words, if
\mathrm{tr}[\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}M_{\mathrm{x}}^{(S)}]>0
for all
M_{\mathrm{x}}^{(S)}\in\mathrm{SEP}
, an optimal POVM element is not in SEP but in GLOBAL; hence, it follows
C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(G)}>C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(S)}
. Let us summarize the necessary and sufficient condition for finding a gap between GLOBAL and SEP.

Proposition 1.
In the complementary problem to finding an MCM for an ensemble
S
, consisting of complementary states, denoted by
\{\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}\}
, it holds
C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(\mathrm{G})}=C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(\mathrm{S})}
for an outcome
\mathrm{x}
if and only if there exists a product vector
|e\rangle|f\rangle
such that
\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}|e\rangle|f\rangle=0
. If no product state satisfies the condition, the ensemble
S
can exhibit the distinction between GLOBAL and SEP in terms of an MC, i.e.,
C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(\mathrm{G})}>C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(\mathrm{S})}
.

In the following, we apply the general results above to an ensemble of two-qubit antiparallel states,

\displaystyle S_{\perp}=\{|\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\}_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}\penalty\ \mathrm{where}\penalty\ |\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle=|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle,

(3)

where an ensemble
\{|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\}
corresponds to symmetric, informationally complete states

\displaystyle|\phi_{1}\rangle=|0\rangle,\penalty\ \penalty\ |\phi_{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}|0\rangle+\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}|1\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathrm{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ |\phi_{3,4}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}|0\rangle+e^{\pm\frac{2\pi i}{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}|1\rangle.

Note that the ensemble has been used to demonstrate NLWE in state estimation
20
in that optimal state estimation is GLOBAL on antiparallel states, whereas it is SEP on parallel states. Antiparallel and parallel ensembles are related by a universal NOT gate
10
, which is antiunitary and thus nonphysical. Then, state estimation is more efficient for antiparallel states, for which an optimal measurement is GLOBAL, than for parallel ones, for which it is SEP.

For the ensemble above, we compute the MC and have
C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(G)}=1
with a POVM element
M_{\mathrm{x}}=a_{\mathrm{x}}|\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}|
where

\displaystyle|\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}(2|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle+|\phi^{\perp}_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle).

(4)

for some
a_{\mathrm{x}}\in(0,1]
. Note that unambiguous discrimination is realized since
\langle\Psi_{\mathrm{y}}|\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle=0
for
\mathrm{x}\neq\mathrm{y}
. Thus, it is clear that
C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(S)}<1
with SEP.

A separable measurement for the MC is also rank-one. With an ansatz,
M_{\mathrm{x}}=b_{\mathrm{x}}|e_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle e_{\mathrm{x}}|\otimes|f_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle f_{\mathrm{x}}|
for some
b_{\mathrm{x}}\in(0,1]
, we compute

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{({S})}=\max_{|e_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle,|f_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle}\frac{|\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}|e_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}|f_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle|^{2}}{\sum_{\mathrm{y}}|\langle\phi_{\mathrm{y}}|e_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\langle\phi_{\mathrm{y}}^{\perp}|f_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle|^{2}}.

(5)

Since
|\langle\phi_{\mathrm{y}}^{\perp}|f_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle|=|\langle\phi_{\mathrm{y}}|f^{\perp}_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle|
, the optimization is equivalent to finding the separable MCM for the parallel states.
Therefore, we find that the optimal POVM element in SEP for MCM on the ensemble of the antiparallel states is given by

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{x}}^{(\mathrm{S})}=b_{\mathrm{x}}|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}|\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}|,\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{for\penalty\ some\penalty\ }b_{\mathrm{x}}>0

(6)

and have
C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{({S})}=3/4
. Thus, we have demonstrated NLWE for antiparallel states in terms of MCMs.

We remark that an MCM for an ensemble of the parallel states
S_{\|}=\{|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle^{\otimes 2}\}_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}
can be achieved by measurements in SEP. The ensemble can be described on a symmetric subspace that can be implemented with SEP; a POVM element in SEP suffices to find an MC, which is
3/4
for all
\mathrm{x}
. Hence, an ensemble of parallel states cannot be used to distinguish between GLOBAL and SEP. The conclusion aligns with the result that the state estimation is more efficient when a measurement is GLOBAL, for which states are prepared to be antiparallel rather than parallel

20
.

The sDI certification of NLWE.
We now show that NLWE can be certified using outcomes of uncharacterized measurement devices. The scenario is similar to that considered in Ref.
19
, where a high rate of detection events is used to rule out certain properties of a measurement. For instance, unambiguous discrimination shows inconclusive outcomes at least with the minimum probability known as the Ivanovic-Dieks-Peres (IDP) limit
27
;
16
;
33
. A measurement by which a rate of inconclusive outcomes is too small excludes the possibility of unambiguous discrimination.

To be precise, the framework contains three parameters. Firstly, an ensemble of states should be specified
S=\{q_{\mathrm{x}},\rho_{\mathrm{x}}\}
. Secondly, one collects measurement outcomes and finds the outcome rates, denoted by
\{\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\}
. For an ensemble
\rho=\sum_{\mathrm{x}}q_{\mathrm{x}}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}
, an outcome rate should be described with a POVM element
M_{\mathrm{x}}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{outcome\penalty\ rate:\penalty\ \penalty\ }\eta_{\mathrm{x}}=\mathrm{tr}[M_{\mathrm{x}}\rho]

(7)

for each
\mathrm{x}
. Note also that a probability of an outcome
\mathrm{x}
given a state
\rho_{\mathrm{x}}
, denoted by
p_{M|P}(\mathrm{x}|\mathrm{x})
, can be found from state preparations and measurement outcomes,
p_{M|P}(\mathrm{x}|\mathrm{x})=\mathrm{tr}[M_{\mathrm{x}}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}]
. Thirdly, from these, a confidence can be computed, called certifiable confidence as follows,

\displaystyle\mathrm{certifiable\penalty\ confidence:\penalty\ \penalty\ }\hat{C}_{\mathrm{x}}=\frac{q_{\mathrm{x}}}{\eta_{\mathrm{x}}}p_{M|P}(\mathrm{x}|\mathrm{x}).

(8)

With two parameters
\eta_{\mathrm{x}}
and
\hat{C}_{\mathrm{x}}
achieved experimentally, the task to certify NLWE is to find whether a certifiable confidence can be achieved by measurements in SEP.

The SDP for the certifiable MC works when an outcome rate is given,

\displaystyle\mathrm{certifiable\penalty\ MC}:

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{\mathrm{x},\max}=\mathrm{max}\penalty\ \penalty\ \frac{q_{\mathrm{x}}}{\eta_{\mathrm{x}}}\mathrm{tr}[\rho_{\mathrm{x}}M_{\mathrm{x}}]

(9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{subject\penalty\ to}\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{tr}[\rho M_{\mathrm{x}}]=\eta_{\mathrm{x}}

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ 0\leq M_{\mathrm{x}}\leq\mathbb{I}.

(10)

Compared to Eq. (
1
), the optimization above finds the achievable MC given outcome statistics. With the constraint that a measurement is SEP or GLOBAL, we write by
\hat{C}_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(S)}
and
\hat{C}_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(G)}
, respectively. Then, we conclude NLWE for an unknown measurement if a certifiable confidence is found in the range,

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(\mathrm{G})}\geq\hat{C}_{\mathrm{x}}>\hat{C}_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(\mathrm{S})},

(11)

i.e., a certifiable confidence cannot be achieved by SEP.

Noise robustness in the certification of NLWE.
To demonstrate the noise robustness of the framework, we consider an ensemble of noisy antiparallel states, denoted by
\{\rho_{\mathrm{x}}\}_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}
where each state is given as,

\displaystyle\left(p|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}|+\frac{(1-p)}{2}\mathbbm{I}\right)\otimes\left(p|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}|+\frac{(1-p)}{2}\mathbbm{I}\right).

From the optimality conditions in Eq. (
2
), an MCM is achieved by GLOBAL,
M_{\mathrm{x}}^{(G)}=a_{\mathrm{x}}|\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}|
, where

\displaystyle|\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\theta|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle+\sin\theta|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\penalty\ \mathrm{and}

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{p(1+p)^{2}}{3p+p^{2}+\sqrt{9+7p^{2}-p^{4}+p^{6}}}\right)

and note that
0<\theta\leq\tan^{-1}(\frac{1}{2})
. With the measurement, the MC for noisy antiparallel states is given by

\displaystyle C^{(G)}_{\mathrm{x},\max}=\frac{1}{4(3-p^{2})}\left(p^{2}+3+2p\sqrt{\frac{p^{4}-2p^{2}+9}{1+p^{2}}}\right).

(20)

Note that

becomes smaller as
p
decreases. We compute the MC with SEP, see Eq. (

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(S)}=\frac{3}{4}\frac{(1+p)^{2}}{3+p^{2}}

in Eq. (

.

For the sDI certification framework, it turns out that
\hat{C}_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(G)}>\hat{C}^{(S)}_{\mathrm{x},\max}
only
\eta_{\mathrm{x}}<\eta_{c}
, where

\displaystyle\eta_{c}=\frac{1}{12}\left(p^{2}+3+\frac{3p(1-p^{2})^{2}}{\sqrt{p^{6}-p^{4}-5p^{2}+9}}\right).

That is, at a low detection rate, a larger gap can be found. For
\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\geq\langle\varphi_{1}|\rho|\varphi_{1}\rangle

The certification NLWE with inconclusive outcomes is also robust against noise. Let us revisit a noisy POVM element,

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{\mathrm{x}}^{(G)}=a(|\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}|+\gamma\mathbb{I})

(22)

where

\displaystyle a=\begin{cases}3(12\gamma+2+2\sin 2\theta)^{-1},\text{ if }\theta\geq\frac{\pi}{12}\\
(4\gamma+2-2\sin 2\theta)^{-1},\text{ if }\theta<\frac{\pi}{12}\end{cases}

The inconclusive rate by excluding all outcomes from the POVM elements

\displaystyle\eta_{0}^{(G)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-a(1+4\gamma+\frac{p^{2}}{3}(1-2\sin 2\theta)).

(24)

M_{\mathrm{x}}^{(S)}=\frac{1}{2}|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}|\otimes|\phi^{\perp}_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\phi^{\perp}_{\mathrm{x}}|

\displaystyle\eta_{0,\min}^{(S)}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p^{2}}{6}.

(25)

In Fig.

\eta_{0,\min}^{(G)}<\eta_{0,\min}^{(S)}
. Thus, when conclusive outcomes do not demonstrate NLWE, i.e.,
\hat{C}_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(S)}=\hat{C}_{\mathrm{x}}
, inconclusive outcomes can certify NLWE.

Conclusion.
In summary, we have presented the framework of demonstrating and certifying NLWE in terms of MCMs, where the confidence, i.e., a conditional probability
per
outcome, corresponds to a fine-grained guessing probability that unifies minimum-error and unambiguous discrimination strategies. Therefore, the framework can be used to detect a gap between GLOBAL and SEP in various state-discrimination-based quantum information applications. We have illustrated the framework with an ensemble of antiparallel quantum states, for which NLWE can be demonstrated and also certified. We also analyze the conditions on ensembles of states such that NLWE may exist. For instance, parallel states cannot show a gap between GLOBAL and SEP.

We have shown that NLWE of unknown measurements can be certified from measurement outcomes, i.e., measurements that have not yet been verified. Then, given outcome rates, we determine whether a measurement for MC belongs to GLOBAL. Remarkably, inconclusive outcomes can also be used for certification purposes. All results, along with the demonstration and certification of NLWE, are robust to noisy state preparations. Our results, therefore, pave the way for verifying NWLE in realistic settings with present-day quantum technologies and enable its further applications.

In future investigations, it would be interesting to devise NLWE-based practical applications, see also
31
;
21
;
30
, for instance, joint measurement and security of quantum communication against adversarial quantum repeaters. It is also interesting to unify various demonstrations of NLWE within the framework of MCMs where both conclusive and inconclusive outcomes can be exploited.

I. The optimality conditions for maximum-confidence measurements

In this section, we briefly review the optimality conditions for maximum-confidence measurements (MCMs).
For an ensemble of states
\{q_{\mathrm{x}},\rho_{\mathrm{x}}\}_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{n}
, where
q_{\mathrm{x}}
denotes
a priori
probability,
confidence
is defined as a conditional probability that a given measurement outcome
\mathrm{x}
correctly identifies the state
\rho_{\mathrm{x}}
, i.e.

C_{\mathrm{x}}=p_{P|M}(\mathrm{x}|\mathrm{x})
, where
P
and
M
denote preparation and measurement respectively.
The maximum confidence is derived from the following optimization,

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{x},\max}=\max_{M_{\mathrm{x}}\geq 0}\frac{q_{\mathrm{x}}\mathrm{tr}[\rho_{\mathrm{x}}M_{\mathrm{x}}]}{\mathrm{tr}[\rho M_{\mathrm{x}}]},

where
\rho=\sum_{\mathrm{x}}q_{\mathrm{x}}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}
and
M_{\mathrm{x}}
is a POVM element.
This problem can be formulated as a semi-definite program (SDP) by introducing the parameter
Q_{\mathrm{x}}=\frac{\sqrt{\rho}M_{\mathrm{x}}\sqrt{\rho}}{\mathrm{tr}[\rho M_{\mathrm{x}}]}
. The optimization becomes

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{x},\max}=\max_{Q_{\mathrm{x}}\geq 0}\mathrm{tr}[\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}q_{\mathrm{x}}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}Q_{\mathrm{x}}]:\mathrm{tr}[Q_{\mathrm{x}}]=1.

It can be readily seen that the maximum confidence corresponds to the largest eigenvalue of
\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}q_{\mathrm{x}}\rho_{x}\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}
,

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{x},\max}=||\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}q_{\mathrm{x}}\rho_{x}\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}||_{\infty}

where
||\cdot||_{\infty}
denotes an operator norm.
The optimality conditions for an MCM
28
read

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{x},\max}\rho-q_{\mathrm{x}}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{\mathrm{x}}\sigma_{\mathrm{x}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}[\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}M_{\mathrm{x}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

where
r_{\mathrm{x}}>0
and
\{\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}\}
are quantum states called
complementary
states. The first condition states that
\rho
must be decomposed into
\rho_{\mathrm{x}}
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}
. Then,
M_{\mathrm{x}}
is orthogonal to
\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}
.
Thus, solving the optimization reduces to finding the complementary states
\{\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}\}
.

Let us derive the explicit condition for an MCM. We write the first condition above as

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{x},\max}\rho-q_{\mathrm{x}}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho}(C_{\mathrm{x},\max}\mathbb{I}-\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}q_{\mathrm{x}}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}\sqrt{\rho}^{-1})\sqrt{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{\mathrm{x}}\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}.

Denote
d_{\mathrm{x}}
the degeneracy of the largest eigenvalue of the operator
\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}q_{\mathrm{x}}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}
and
|\lambda^{(j)}_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle
the associated eigenvectors, where
j=1,...,d_{\mathrm{x}}
.
Then, we see that the kernel of
\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}
is spanned by the vectors
\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}|\lambda_{\mathrm{x}}^{(j)}\rangle
.
Define the subspace

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{x}}^{MCM}=\mathrm{span}\{\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}|\lambda_{\mathrm{x}}^{(1)}\rangle,\ldots\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}|\lambda_{\mathrm{x}}^{(d_{\mathrm{x}})}\rangle\}

(26)

which we call the
MCM subspace
. Then, the necessary and sufficient condition of MCM is

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}(M_{\mathrm{x}})\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{MCM}_{\mathrm{x}}.

We remark that
M_{\mathrm{x}}
can always be written as a rank-one operator and, in general, has rank at most
d_{\mathrm{x}}
.

II. MCMs for noisy parallel and antiparallel states

We consider ensembles of parallel and antiparallel symmetric, informationally complete (SIC) states, denoted by
S_{\|}=\{|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\}_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}
and
S_{\perp}=\{|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle\}_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}
, where

\displaystyle|\phi_{1}\rangle=|0\rangle,|\phi_{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}|0\rangle+\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}|1\rangle,|\phi_{3,4}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}|0\rangle+e^{\pm\frac{2\pi i}{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}|1\rangle.

with uniform
a priori
probability
q_{\mathrm{x}}=\frac{1}{4}
for all
\mathrm{x}
. We derive the global and separable MCMs for the parallel and antiparallel states subject to local depolarizing noise. We show that MCMs do not find any gap between GLOBAL and SEP for the noisy parallel states. In contrast, for noisy antiparallel states, MCMs feature NLWE for any amount of white noise.

.0.1
Noisy parallel states

Assume that the ensemble of parallel states
S_{\|}=\{|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle^{\otimes 2}\}_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}
undergoes a local depolarizing noise. The resulting states are described as

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{x}}=\left(p|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}|+\frac{(1-p)}{2}\mathbbm{I}\right)\otimes\left(p|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}|+\frac{(1-p)}{2}\mathbbm{I}\right)

where
p\in(0,1]
. The ensemble state is

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}=\frac{3+p^{2}}{12}\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}+\frac{1-p^{2}}{4}|\psi^{-}\rangle\!\langle\psi^{-}|

where
|\psi^{-}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|01\rangle-|10\rangle)
and
\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}
is the projector onto the symmetric subspace. To derive the global MCM, we need to find the largest eigenvalue and its associated eigenvector of the operator
\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}
, which admits the spectral decomposition

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}=\frac{3}{3+p^{2}}\Big[(1+p)^{2}|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}|^{\otimes 2}+(1-p)^{2}|\phi^{\perp}_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\phi^{\perp}_{\mathrm{x}}|^{\otimes 2}+(1-p^{2})|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{+}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{+}|\Big]+|\psi^{-}\rangle\!\langle\psi^{-}|

. Its largest eigenvalue is
\frac{3(1+p)^{2}}{3+p^{2}}
with the corresponding eigenvector given as
|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle
.
Therefore, the MCM subspace
\mathcal{S}^{MCM}_{\mathrm{x}}
in Eq. (
26

\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\propto|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle
, and
the global MCM is a separable measurement,

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{x}}^{(G)}=a_{\mathrm{x}}|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}|\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}|

where
a_{\mathrm{x}}\in(0,1]
. This measurement yields the confidence

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(G)}=\frac{3(1+p)^{2}}{4(3+p^{2})}.

(27)

We conclude that for the parallel states with local white noise, MCMs do not find the gap between GLOBAL and SEP.

.0.2
Noisy antiparallel states

We consider noisy antiparallel states,

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{x}}=\left(p|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}|+\frac{(1-p)}{2}\mathbbm{I}\right)\otimes\left(p|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}|+\frac{(1-p)}{2}\mathbbm{I}\right)

where

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}=\frac{3-p^{2}}{12}\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}+\frac{1+p^{2}}{4}|\psi^{-}\rangle\!\langle\psi^{-}|

With a bit of algebra, we find that the MCM subspace

(30)

We remark that
0<\theta\leq\tan^{-1}(\frac{1}{2})
and the upper bound is attained when
p=1
. Therefore, the global MCM is an entangled measurement,

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{x}}^{(G)}=a_{\mathrm{x}}|\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}|,

a_{\mathrm{x}}\in(0,1]

Since

.

When

\displaystyle|\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}(2|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle+|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle).

Since
\langle\Psi_{\mathrm{y}}|\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle=0

Let us turn to finding the separable MCM for the noisy antiparallel states. The optimization is given as

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(S)}=\max_{M_{\mathrm{x}}\in\mathrm{SEP}}\frac{1}{4}\frac{\mathrm{tr}[\rho_{\mathrm{x}}M_{\mathrm{x}}]}{\mathrm{tr}[\rho M_{\mathrm{x}}]}

Using this ansatz, we solve the optimization,

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(S)}

\displaystyle\max_{|e_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle,|f_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle}\frac{1}{4}\frac{\mathrm{tr}[\rho_{\mathrm{x}}|e_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle e_{\mathrm{x}}|\otimes|f_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle f_{\mathrm{x}}|]}{\mathrm{tr}[\rho|e_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle e_{\mathrm{x}}|\otimes|f_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle f_{\mathrm{x}}|]}

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\frac{(1+p)^{2}}{3+p^{2}}

III. The optimality conditions for the certifiable maximum confidence

In this section, we derive the optimality conditions for the certifiable maximum confidence under given outcome rates. Then, we provide solutions to the optimality conditions.

Consider an ensemble of quantum states
S=\{q_{\mathrm{x}}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}\}_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{n}
. Let
\{\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\}_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{n}
denote outcome rates of the experiment, i.e.
\eta_{\mathrm{x}}=\mathrm{tr}[\rho M_{\mathrm{x}}]
where
\rho=\sum_{\mathrm{x}}q_{\mathrm{x}}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}
and
M_{\mathrm{x}}
is a POVM element for outcome
\mathrm{x}
that describe the measurement in the experiment. Our aim is to find the maximally achievable confidence by GLOBAL and SEP that is compatible with the experimental statistics, especially outcome rates. Then, with the trusted quantum states, the untrusted measurement is certfied to feature NLWE if the confidence it produces, called certifiable confidence
\{\hat{C}_{\mathrm{x}}\}_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{n}
, cannot be achieved by SEP.

The problem of certifiable maximum confidence can be formulated as an SDP,

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{\mathrm{x},\max}=\max\frac{q_{\mathrm{x}}}{\eta_{\mathrm{x}}}\mathrm{tr}[\rho_{\mathrm{x}}M_{\mathrm{x}}]

\displaystyle\textrm{subject\penalty\ to\penalty\ }\mathrm{tr}[\rho M_{\mathrm{x}}]=\eta_{\mathrm{x}},0\leq M_{\mathrm{x}}\leq\mathbb{I},

We denote
\hat{C}^{(G)}_{\mathrm{x},\max}
and
\hat{C}^{(S)}_{\mathrm{x},\max}
the certifiable maximum confidence by GLOBAL and SEP, respectively. Let us derive the dual problem. For convenience, we first omit the prefactor
\frac{q_{\mathrm{x}}}{\eta_{\mathrm{x}}}
. Introducing the dual parameters
P,N\geq 0
for the inequality constraints and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
for the equality constraint, the Lagrangian reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(M_{\mathrm{x}},P,N,\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}[\rho_{\mathrm{x}}M_{\mathrm{x}}]+\mathrm{tr}[M_{\mathrm{x}}P]+\mathrm{tr}[(\mathbb{I}-M_{\mathrm{x}})N]+\lambda(\eta_{\mathrm{x}}-\mathrm{tr}[\rho M_{\mathrm{x}}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}[M_{\mathrm{x}}(\rho_{\mathrm{x}}+P-N-\lambda\rho)]+\mathrm{tr}[N]+\lambda\eta_{\mathrm{x}}.

The dual function is derived as

\displaystyle g(P,N,\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{M_{\mathrm{x}}}\mathcal{L}(M_{\mathrm{x}},P,N,\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathrm{tr}[N]+\lambda\eta_{\mathrm{x}},\textrm{ if }\rho_{\mathrm{x}}+P-N-\lambda\rho=0\\
\infty,\textrm{ otherwise }\end{cases}

The dual function does not diverge only if
\rho_{\mathrm{x}}+P-N-\lambda\rho=0
, which leads to the dual problem

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{\mathrm{x},\max}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{min\penalty\ }\mathrm{tr}[N]+\lambda\eta_{\mathrm{x}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{subject\penalty\ to}\penalty\ \penalty\ N\geq 0,N+\lambda\rho\geq\rho_{\mathrm{x}}.

Strong duality holds since a strictly feasible point exists (e.g.,
M_{\mathrm{x}}=\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\mathbb{I}
), and therefore the primal and the dual problems yield the same optimal value.
Bringing back the prefactor
\frac{q_{\mathrm{x}}}{\eta_{\mathrm{x}}}
, the certifiable maximum confidence is derived as

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(G)}=q_{\mathrm{x}}\left(\lambda+\frac{\mathrm{tr}[N]}{\eta_{\mathrm{x}}}\right)\,.

(34)

Besides the constraints in the primal and the dual problems, the optimality conditions are listed as

\displaystyle\textrm{(Lagrangian stability) }P-N

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\rho-\rho_{\mathrm{x}}

(35)

\displaystyle\textrm{(Complementary slackness) }M_{\mathrm{x}}P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,(\mathbb{I}-M_{\mathrm{x}})N=0.

Three observations are immediately made from these optimality conditions.
First, these conditions imply that
P
and
N
correspond to the positive and negative parts of
\lambda\rho-\rho_{\mathrm{x}}
, respectively. Second,
\lambda
can be bounded between the maximum and minimum eigenvalues of the operator
\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}\rho_{\mathrm{x}}\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}
; otherwise,
M_{\mathrm{x}}
takes only trivial solutions,
M_{\mathrm{x}}=0
or
M_{\mathrm{x}}=\mathbb{I}
.
Lastly, since
M_{\mathrm{x}}N=N
,
M_{\mathrm{x}}
must act as an identity to
N
. To be specific, let us denote the spectral decomposition of
N
as
N=\sum n_{i}|n_{i}\rangle\!\langle n_{i}|
. Then,
M_{\mathrm{x}}
must take the form

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{x}}=\sum_{i}|n_{i}\rangle\!\langle n_{i}|+L

where
L\geq 0
is orthogonal to
N
.

IV. The certifiable maximum confidence for the antiparallel states

We consider an ensemble of noiseless antiparallel states,
S_{\perp}=\{|\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\}_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}
where

\displaystyle|\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle=|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle

and
\{|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\}_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}
are the SIC states given in Eq. (

). We find the certifiable maximum confidence for

by GLOBAL and SEP measurements. For that purpose, we solve the optimality conditions in Eq. (
35
) by finding suitable parameters
\lambda,P,N
and

that satisfy the conditions.
Let us write the optimality condition as

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho}(\lambda\mathbbm{I}-\mu_{\mathrm{x}}|v_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\langle v_{\mathrm{x}}|)\sqrt{\rho}=P-N

(36)

where
\mu_{\mathrm{x}}=\langle\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}|\rho^{-1}|\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle=4
and
|v_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle=\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}|\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle/\sqrt{\mu_{\mathrm{x}}}
. Note that
\lambda
can be bounded in the interval
\lambda\in[0,\mu_{\mathrm{x}}]
. This is because if it takes other values, then
P
or
N
are full-rank in the support of
\rho
, and therefore only trivial solutions
M_{\mathrm{x}}=0
or
M_{\mathrm{x}}=\mathbb{I}

Since

and

are positive and negative parts of
\lambda\rho-\rho_{\mathrm{x}}

\lambda\rho-\rho_{\mathrm{x}}

\displaystyle P-N

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\rho-\rho_{\mathrm{x}}

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4}\nu_{i}(\lambda)|\nu_{i}(\lambda)\rangle\!\langle\nu_{i}(\lambda)|

where the eigenvalues are

\displaystyle\nu_{1}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{6}(2\lambda-3-\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+9})

\displaystyle\nu_{2}(\lambda)=\nu_{3}(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda}{6}

V. Certifiable maximum confidence for noisy antiparallel states

We consider the noisy antiparallel states that are subject to local depolarizing noise,

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{x}}=\left(p|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}|+\frac{(1-p)}{2}\mathbbm{I}\right)\otimes\left(p|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}|+\frac{(1-p)}{2}\mathbbm{I}\right).

We show that NLWE can be certified from conclusive outcome
\mathrm{x}
if and only if

\displaystyle\eta_{c}=\frac{1}{12}(3+p^{2}+\frac{3p(1-p^{2})^{2}}{\sqrt{p^{6}-p^{4}-5p^{2}+9}}).

Let us analyze the optimality conditions in Eq. (
35
). To find
P
and
N
, we first derive the spectral decomposition of
\lambda\rho-\rho_{\mathrm{x}}
,

\displaystyle P-N

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\rho-\rho_{\mathrm{x}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4}\nu_{i}(\lambda)|\nu_{i}(\lambda)\rangle\!\langle\nu_{i}(\lambda)|

\displaystyle\nu_{2}(\lambda)=\nu_{3}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{12}\big(\lambda(3-p^{2})-3(1-p^{2})\big)

\displaystyle\nu_{4}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{12}\big(\lambda(p^{2}+3)-3(1+p^{2})+2p\sqrt{\lambda^{2}p^{2}+9}\big)

and the corresponding eigenvectors are given as

\displaystyle|\nu_{2}(\lambda)\rangle=|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle

\displaystyle|\nu_{3}(\lambda)\rangle=|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle

and

in terms of

. For that purpose, we rewrite

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}=\frac{3-3p^{2}}{3-p^{2}}

which are labeled in decreasing order,

. It can be checked that

for all

. Moreover,
\nu_{i}(\lambda)

\lambda

These relationships determine

and

in terms of

and

For

VI. NLWE in inconclusive outcomes for noisy antiparallel states

We have shown so far NLWE from the noisy antiparallel states can be certified from certifiable confidence and outcome rates. In this Appendix, we show that NLWE may also arise from inconclusive outcomes and can be certified even when certification from conclusive outcomes fails.
We consider noisy antiparallel states,

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{x}}=\left(p|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}|+\frac{(1-p)}{2}\mathbbm{I}\right)\otimes\left(p|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle\langle\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}|+\frac{(1-p)}{2}\mathbbm{I}\right).

Recall that the global MCM is given by
M_{\mathrm{x}}^{(G)}=a_{\mathrm{x}}|\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}|
in Eq. (
29
), where

\displaystyle|\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle=\cos\theta|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle+\sin\theta|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle.

and
\theta
is determined by
p
as in Eq. (
30
). We consider a noisy POVM element,

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{\mathrm{x}}^{(G)}=a(|\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}|+\gamma\mathbb{I})

where
\gamma\geq 0
decreases the value of confidence. If
\gamma
is large enough, such that
\frac{1}{4}\frac{\mathrm{tr}[\rho_{\mathrm{x}}\widetilde{M}_{\mathrm{x}}^{(G)}]}{\mathrm{tr}[\rho\widetilde{M}_{\mathrm{x}}^{(G)}]}\leq C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(S)}
, then NLWE cannot be certified from outcome
\mathrm{x}
, the condition which can be explicitly written as

\displaystyle\gamma\geq\frac{1}{4}(1+p^{2}+2p\cos 2\theta)(1-\frac{C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(S)}}{C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(G)}})(4C_{\mathrm{x},\max}^{(S)}-1)^{-1}.

The inconclusive rate by this POVM is

\displaystyle\eta_{0}=1-a(1+4\gamma+\frac{p^{2}}{3}(1-2\sin 2\theta)).

To find the minimum probability of inconclusive outcomes by this measurement, we maximize
a
such that
\{M_{\mathrm{x}}^{(G)}\}
with an additional POVM element forms a POVM. The optimization is formulated as follows,

\displaystyle\text{maximize }a

\displaystyle\mathrm{subject\penalty\ to\penalty\ }\sum_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}\widetilde{M}_{\mathrm{x}}^{(G)}\leq\mathbb{I}.

The inequality constraint can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{a}{1-4a\gamma}\Pi\leq\mathbb{I},

where
\Pi=\sum_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}|\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}|
. Denote
\nu_{i}
as the eigenvalues of
\Pi
,
which has two distinct eigenvalues

\displaystyle\nu_{1}=\frac{2}{3}(1+\sin 2\theta),\penalty\ \nu_{2}=2(1-\sin 2\theta).

The inequality constraint reduces to

\displaystyle a\leq\frac{1}{4\gamma+\nu_{i}},i=1,2.

Therefore, the maximum
a
that satisfies this inequality is

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\gamma+\max\{\nu_{i}\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}3(12\gamma+2+2\sin 2\theta)^{-1},\text{ if }\theta\geq\frac{\pi}{12}\\
(4\gamma+2-2\sin 2\theta)^{-1},\text{ if }\theta<\frac{\pi}{12}\end{cases}

We remark that when
\theta=\pi/12
, the noisy POVM elements
\{\widetilde{M}_{\mathrm{x}}^{(G)}\}
forms a POVM, i.e.
\eta_{0}^{(G)}=0
.

Let us now consider a separable MCM given in Eq. (
33
),

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{x}}^{(S)}=b_{\mathrm{x}}|\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}|

where
|\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle=|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\otimes|\phi_{\mathrm{x}}^{\perp}\rangle
.
To find the minimum value of inconclusive rate, which is given by

\displaystyle\eta_{0}=1-\sum_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}\mathrm{tr}[\rho M_{\mathrm{x}}^{(S)}]=1-\frac{1}{12}(3+p^{2})\sum_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}b_{\mathrm{x}},

we formulate the following SDP,

maximize

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}b_{\mathrm{x}}

subject to

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}b_{\mathrm{x}}|\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}|\leq\mathbb{I},

\displaystyle b_{\mathrm{x}}\geq 0,\penalty\ \forall\mathrm{x}.

We take an approach of the complementary problem, which is to find the parameters that satisfy the optimality conditions.
To derive the optimality conditions, we first construct the Lagrangian with the dual parameters
\mu_{\mathrm{x}},K\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}b_{\mathrm{x}}+\mathrm{tr}[K(\mathbb{I}-\sum_{\mathrm{x}=1]}^{4}b_{\mathrm{x}}|\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}|)]+\sum_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}b_{\mathrm{x}}\mu_{\mathrm{x}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}b_{\mathrm{x}}(1-\mathrm{tr}[K|\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}|]+\mu_{\mathrm{x}})+\mathrm{tr}[K].

From this, the optimality conditions can be derived as follows,

\displaystyle 1-\mathrm{tr}[K|\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}|]+\mu_{\mathrm{x}}=0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}[KM_{0}]=0,\text{ and }\mu_{\mathrm{x}}b_{\mathrm{x}}=0,\forall\mathrm{x},

where
M_{0}=\mathbb{I}-\sum_{\mathrm{x}=1}^{4}b_{\mathrm{x}}|\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}|
.
We employ an ansatz,
b_{\mathrm{x}}=b>0
for all
\mathrm{x}
. Note that
M_{0}
cannot have full rank. Then, by analyzing of the eigenvalues of
M_{0}
, we see that
b=\frac{1}{2}
makes
M_{0}
a rank-three operator,

\displaystyle M_{0}=\frac{2}{3}\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}

where
\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}
denotes a projection onto the symmetric subspace. Therefore
K
must take the form

\displaystyle K=\gamma|\psi^{-}\rangle\!\langle\psi^{-}|,

where
|\psi^{-}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|01\rangle-|10\rangle)
and
\gamma>0
. We choose
\mu_{\mathrm{x}}=0,\forall\mathrm{x}
and
\gamma=1/|\langle\psi^{-}|\Psi_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle|^{2}=2
. Since these parameters satisfy all of the optimality conditions, we conclude that

\displaystyle b_{\mathrm{x}}=\frac{1}{2},\forall\mathrm{x}

are the optimal parameters.
With this, the minimum inconclusive rate by the separable MCM is obtained as

\displaystyle\eta_{0,\min}^{(S)}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p^{2}}{6}.
