Quantum gravity contributions to the gauge and Yukawa couplings in proper time flow
Gabriele Giacometti, Kamila Kowalska, Daniele Rizzo, Enrico Maria Sessolo, Dario Zappalà, [2ex] Dipartimento di Fisica e Astronomia, Università di Catania, Via S. Sofia 64, 95123, Catania, Italy

Abstract
We derive quantum gravity contributions to the beta functions of the gauge and Yukawa couplings of a matter theory using the Schwinger proper-time flow equation. Working in the Einstein-Hilbert truncation, we investigate the gauge-fixing and regulator dependence of the corresponding renormalization group equations. We quantify the sensitivity of our results on unphysical parameters by evaluating the gravitational correction to the running matter couplings at the interactive fixed point of gravity and we compare our findings with existing determinations in alternative schemes. We finally confront the derived contributions with the typical size they should assume to generate observable low-scale predictions in the Standard Model and in several scenarios of new physics.

2
Schwinger proper time

In this section, following the general steps
outlined in Ref.
[
86
]
,
we review some of the main properties of the
Schwinger PT flow equation.

We start by introducing the PT flow equation for the
Wilsonian action
S_{k}
[
72
,
73
,
74
,
75
,
76
,
77
,
78
]
,

k\partial_{k}S_{k}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s}\text{Tr}\left[k\partial_{k}\rho_{k,k_{\textrm{UV}}}(s)e^{-s\,S_{k}^{\prime\prime}}\right],

(3)

where
s
is the PT integration variable and energy scale
k

parametrizes the RG flow. We indicate functional derivation with “prime” symbols, so that
S_{k}^{\prime\prime}
represents
the second functional derivative (hessian) of the Wilsonian
action with respect to the fields. The trace
runs over all spatial, momentum, and internal indices of
S_{k}^{\prime\prime}
.

The integral in Eq. (
3
) is regularized
by means of the regulating function

\rho_{k,k_{\textrm{UV}}}(s)
, which
smoothly vanishes for
s>1/k^{2}

and
s<1/k_{\textrm{UV}}^{2}
(
k_{\textrm{UV}}
is the UV scale at which the bare action
is defined) and is
essentially constant in between, thus
suppressing the integration of the field modes with
momentum larger than
k_{\textrm{UV}}
and
smaller than the RG running scale
k
.

While, at least in principle,
all observables determined from the RG equation should
be independent of the arbitrary details of the regulator, in practice it is convenient to select a
regulator that simplifies the computation of the integrals
and therefore, in this work, we resort to the 1-parameter
family of regulators considered in
Ref.
[
91
,
86
]
:

\rho_{k,k_{\textrm{UV}}}(s;m,Z_{k})=\frac{\Gamma(m,m\,Z_{k}\,s\,k^{2})-\Gamma(m,m\,Z_{k}\,s\,k_{\textrm{UV}}^{2})}{\Gamma(m)}\,,

(4)

where
\Gamma(m)
indicates the gamma function and

\Gamma(a,x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}dt\,t^{a-1}e^{-t}
denotes the incomplete gamma function.
The parameter
m>2
, conveniently restricted to integer values, characterizes the smoothness of the shape of the regulator in proximity of the scales
1/k^{2}
and
1/k_{\textrm{UV}}^{2}
. In addition, we perform the rescaling
k^{2}\to Z_{k}k^{2}
and
k_{\textrm{UV}}^{2}\to Z_{k}k_{\textrm{UV}}^{2}
in Eq. (
4
) to get an effective suppression of the modes with square momentum larger than
k^{2}
in the presence of a field renormalization
Z_{k}
[
77
,
86
]
.

Under these assumptions, it is straightforward to compute the derivative of
\rho_{k,k_{\textrm{UV}}}(s)
with respect to the RG scale
k
,

k\partial_{k}\rho_{k,k_{\textrm{UV}}}(s)=-\frac{2\,(m\,Z_{k}\,s\,k^{2})^{m}}{\Gamma(m)}e^{-m\,Z_{k}\,s\,k^{2}},

(5)

which we plug into Eq. (
3
) to derive the flow equation of
S_{k}
.

We shall also analyze the
sharp limit
of the regulator in Eq. (
4
), obtained for
m\to\infty
:

\lim_{m\to\infty}\rho_{k,k_{\textrm{UV}}}(s;m,Z_{k})=\Theta\left(\frac{1}{Z_{k}k^{2}}-s\right)-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{Z_{k}k_{\textrm{UV}}^{2}}-s\right),

(6)

where
\Theta(x)
is the Heaviside function. The derivative of the regulator, Eq. (
5
), becomes in this case a Dirac delta function and the PT flow equation assumes a simple form,

k\partial_{k}S_{k}=\text{Tr}\,e^{-\frac{S_{k}^{\prime\prime}}{Z_{k}k^{2}}}\,.

(7)

It was shown in Ref.
[
90
]
that the sharp-limit flow in Eq. (
7
) has the quality of reproducing the two-loop beta function of Yang-Mills and scalar
O(N)
theory, unlike the “smooth” regulator (
4
), which instead produces spurious
\mathcal{O}(1/m)
terms at the two-loop order.

Once the flow equation (
3
) is established,
one can derive the flow of the couplings in Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
) by matching
the coefficients of the operators associated with each coupling on
both sides of the flow equation. The traces are evaluated by using the heat kernel expansion
[
92
]
,

\text{Tr}\,e^{-sS_{k}^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{1}{(4\pi s)^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,s^{n}\int\,d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\;\text{Tr}\,a_{n}(x)\,.

(8)

For an operator of the form
S_{k}^{\prime\prime}\sim-\Box\mathbf{1}+\mathbb{U}
,
we recall the explicit expression of the first few terms:

a_{0}=\mathbf{1\,},\qquad a_{1}=\frac{R}{6}\mathbf{1}-\mathbb{U}\,,\\
a_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{U}^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\Box\mathbb{U}+\frac{1}{12}\mathbf{\Omega}_{\mu\nu}\mathbf{\Omega}^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{6}R\mathbb{U}+\mathbf{1}\left(\frac{1}{180}R_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}^{2}-\frac{1}{180}R^{2}_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{72}R^{2}-\frac{1}{33}\Box R\right)\dots{}

(9)

where
\Box=\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}
,
\Omega_{\mu\nu}=[\nabla_{\mu},\nabla_{\nu}]
, and
R
,
R_{\mu\nu}
, and
R_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}
are the standard curvature scalar and tensors.

3
Quantum gravity contributions to the running gauge and Yukawa couplings

In this section, we derive the contribution of quantum
gravity to the beta functions of gauge and Yukawa couplings using the PT flow equation.

3.1
Gauge coupling

We consider an abelian gauge theory with running coupling
g_{Y}
. The universal nature of the gravitational interaction implies that the contribution to the beta function arising from graviton loops should be the same for any gauge coupling, independently of the group structure.

The running gauge coupling can be extracted in different ways. One could derive the flow from the three-point function and/or the four-point function of matter/gauge boson interactions. This choice, which was explored,
e.g.
, in Ref.
[
34
]
in the context of the ERG, would require the introduction in the action of matter fields (scalars and/or fermions) charged under the abelian gauge symmetry. A more common choice
[
19
,
21
,
40
,
93
,
94
]
, which we adopt in this work, is to extract the coupling directly from the gauge field propagator, after a rescaling of the field
A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}/g_{Y}
. Of course, gauge invariance should guarantee that the beta function of
g_{Y}
does not depend on the way it is extracted. In the presence of gravity, this is not obvious, because of the perturbative non-renormalizability
of the gravitational interaction. On the other hand, in Ref.
[
95
]

the Ward identities are shown to be respected at one- and two-loop order in quantum electrodynamics coupled to Einstein gravity, while in Ref.
[
96
]
the Slavnov-Taylor identities are shown to hold at one-loop order in non-abelian, Yang-Mills theories.

Following common practice, we adopt the background field method to compute
f_{g}
.
The metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}

and gauge field
A_{\mu}
are thus split into a background and a fluctuation,

g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}\,,\qquad A_{\mu}=\bar{A}_{\mu}+a_{\mu}\,.

(10)

For the rest of this subsection we shall indicate all background quantities with a bar.

We consider the following bare Wilsonian effective action,

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{g}\left(R-2\Lambda\right)+\frac{1}{32\pi G\,\alpha}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{\bar{g}}\,\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu}F_{\nu}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{g}g^{\mu\nu}g^{\kappa\lambda}F_{\mu\kappa}F_{\nu\lambda}+\frac{1}{2\xi}\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{\bar{g}}\left(\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}\bar{D}_{\mu}a_{\nu}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+S_{\textrm{ghost}}+S_{\textrm{matter}}\,.

(11)

The first term in Eq. (
3.1
) denotes the Einstein-Hilbert action with Newton constant
G
and cosmological constant
\Lambda
. The second addend is the gauge-fixing term, where

F_{\mu}=\left(\bar{g}^{\alpha\gamma}\delta^{\beta}_{\mu}\bar{D}_{\gamma}-\omega\bar{g}^{\alpha\beta}\bar{D}_{\mu}\right)h_{\alpha\beta}\,,

(12)

\alpha
and
\omega
are gauge-fixing parameters, and the covariant derivative
\bar{D}_{\mu}
is constructed with respect to the background metric
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
,

\bar{D}_{\mu}h_{\alpha\beta}=\partial_{\mu}h_{\alpha\beta}-\bar{\Gamma}_{\mu\alpha}^{\sigma}h_{\sigma\beta}-\bar{\Gamma}_{\mu\beta}^{\sigma}h_{\alpha\sigma}\,.

(13)

In the second line of Eq. (
3.1
),
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
denotes the field strength tensor of the abelian gauge field, and
\xi
is the gauge-fixing parameter of the abelian sector.
Once more, the covariant derivative is defined with respect to the background metric,
\bar{D}_{\mu}a_{\nu}=\partial_{\mu}a_{\nu}-\bar{\Gamma}_{\nu\mu}^{\sigma}a_{\sigma}
.
We do not write explicitly in Eq. (
3.1
) the ghost terms relative to the gravitational and abelian gauge fixing. It is well known that ghost loops do not contribute to the running of the abelian gauge coupling,
which is the quantity we seek to calculate here. We indicate for completeness possible contributions from the matter sector as
S_{\textrm{matter}}
but, as was discussed above, we will not use them to extract the gravitational contribution to the gauge beta function.

We expand the action (
3.1
) to the second order in fluctuations of the metric and gauge field (gauge-fixing terms do not need to be expanded as they are second order by construction),

S^{(2)}=S^{(2)}_{hh}+S^{(2)}_{ah}+S^{(2)}_{aa}\,,

(14)

where

S^{(2)}_{hh}=\frac{1}{32\pi G}\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{g}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(-h_{\mu\nu}\Box h^{\mu\nu}+h\Box h\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\bar{R}-2\Lambda\right)\left(h_{\mu\nu}h^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}h^{2}\right)\right.\\
+\left.h_{\mu\nu}\bar{D}^{\mu}\bar{D}^{\rho}h_{\rho}^{\,\,\nu}-h\bar{D}^{\mu}\bar{D}^{\nu}h_{\mu\nu}+h\bar{R}^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}-h_{\mu\nu}\bar{R}^{\nu\sigma}h^{\mu}_{\,\,\sigma}-h_{\mu\nu}\bar{R}^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}h_{\rho\sigma}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\bar{D}^{\mu}h_{\mu\nu}-\omega\bar{D}_{\mu}h\right)^{2}\right]\\
+\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{g}\left[\frac{1}{4}\bar{F}^{\mu\nu}\bar{F}^{\rho\sigma}h_{\sigma\nu}h_{\rho\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{F}^{\mu\nu}\bar{F}_{\mu}^{\,\,\rho}\left(h_{\nu}^{\,\,\sigma}h_{\rho\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}hh_{\nu\rho}\right)-\frac{1}{16}\bar{F}_{\rho\sigma}\bar{F}^{\rho\sigma}\left(h_{\mu\nu}h^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}h^{2}\right)\right],

(15)

S^{(2)}_{ah}=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{g}\left(h\bar{F}_{\nu\mu}-2h_{\mu\rho}\bar{F}_{\nu}^{\,\,\rho}+2h_{\nu\rho}\bar{F}_{\mu}^{\,\,\rho}\right)\bar{D}^{\mu}a^{\nu}\,,

(16)

S^{(2)}_{aa}=\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{g}\left[\frac{1}{2\xi}(\bar{D}_{\mu}a^{\mu})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(-a_{\mu}\Box a^{\mu}+a_{\mu}\bar{D}^{\mu}\bar{D}^{\nu}a_{\nu}\right)\right].

(17)

15

(18)

where matrices

(19)

and matrices

We perform the calculation in the de Donder gauge,

\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}=\delta_{\mu\nu}\,,\quad\quad\bar{R}=0\,,\quad\quad\bar{D}_{\mu}\bar{D}_{\nu}=-p_{\mu}p_{\nu}\,.

Our background choice implies that the calculation is by necessity off-shell, since the flat metric is not a solution to the Einstein equations of motion with nonzero cosmological constant. A direct consequence of this observation is that our result for the beta function will be dependent on the gauge fixing of the gravitational sector

), and after rescaling the photon field

(21)

\mathbb{P}^{\rho\sigma}_{aa}=\left(16\pi G\right)^{-1}\left(g^{\rho\sigma}-\left(1-\xi\right)\frac{p^{\rho}p^{\sigma}}{p^{2}}\right)\,.

(22)

As a consequence, potential
\mathbb{U}
undergoes a rescaling and its diagonal blocks become

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}_{hh}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{hh\,{\lambda}\kappa}^{\alpha\beta}\mathbb{U}_{hh}^{{\lambda}\kappa\mu\nu},

(23)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}^{\rho\sigma}_{aa}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{aa\,\kappa}^{\rho}\mathbb{U}_{aa}^{\kappa\sigma}\,.

\displaystyle\mathbb{Y}_{ha}^{\alpha\beta\delta\sigma}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{hh\,{\lambda}\kappa}^{\alpha\beta}\mathbb{V}_{ha}^{{\lambda}\kappa\delta\sigma},

(25)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Y}_{ah}^{\rho\delta\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{aa\,\kappa}^{\rho}\mathbb{V}_{ah}^{\kappa\delta\mu\nu}.

As a result, one obtains a new Laplacian operator and a canonical hessian,

\mathbb{\tilde{H}}=-\Box+\mathbb{\tilde{W}}\,.

The new potential,
\mathbb{\tilde{W}}
, is block diagonal in field space and takes the form

\displaystyle\mathbb{\tilde{W}}_{hh}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{hh}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}+\mathbb{Y}_{ha}^{\alpha\beta{\lambda}\kappa}\mathbb{Y}_{ah\,\kappa}^{\,\tau\mu\nu}\left(\frac{4p_{{\lambda}}p_{\tau}}{p^{2}}\right),

(28)

\displaystyle\mathbb{\tilde{W}}^{\rho\sigma}_{aa}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}^{\rho\sigma}_{aa}+\mathbb{Y}_{ah}^{\rho{\lambda}\mu\nu}\mathbb{Y}_{ha\,\mu\nu}^{\,\,\tau\sigma}\left(\frac{4p_{{\lambda}}p_{\tau}}{p^{2}}\right),

(29)

where, in the language of perturbation theory, the trace of the first addend on the r.h.s. of Eq. (
28
) provides the “tadpole” diagram contribution to the background
\bar{F}_{\mu\nu}\bar{F}^{\mu\nu}
operator, whereas the traces of the second addends in Eqs. (
28
), (
29
) correspond to the “sunset” diagrams.

Finally, we compute the heat kernel expansion in Eq. (
8
)
by making the identification
S_{k}^{\prime\prime}\equiv\mathbb{\tilde{H}}
.
The first order in the expansion reads

\textrm{Tr}\,a_{1}\equiv-\textrm{Tr}\,\mathbb{\tilde{W}}=12\Lambda+8\alpha\Lambda+12\pi G\left(1+\alpha\right)\bar{F}_{\mu\nu}\bar{F}^{\mu\nu}\,.

(30)

Note that the gauge parameter
\xi
cancels out of Eq. (
30
), which implies that the result is gauge-fixing independent in the abelian sector. Incidentally, Eq. (
30
) agrees with previous determinations in the same scheme
[
93
,
94
]
.

The terms containing the
cosmological constant in Eq. (
30
) emerge from the trace of the
\mathbb{\tilde{W}}_{hh}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}
block (
28
). Conversely, the coefficient left to multiply the field strength tensor operator
\bar{F}_{\mu\nu}\bar{F}^{\mu\nu}
arises from the trace of the
\mathbb{\tilde{W}}^{\rho\sigma}_{aa}
block (
29
). The contributions from tadpole and sunset in Eq. (
28
), both in size equal to
12\pi G(1+\alpha)
, cancel out. As a consequence, due to the block-diagonal nature of the trace, we do not expect the gravitational contribution to the photon two-point function to depend on the cosmological constant at any order in the heat kernel expansion.
This is confirmed with a direct calculation, which shows that

\textrm{Tr}\,a_{n}|_{\bar{F}_{\mu\nu}\bar{F}^{\mu\nu}}\equiv\frac{1}{n!}\textrm{Tr}\,\mathbb{\tilde{W}}^{n}|_{\bar{F}_{\mu\nu}\bar{F}^{\mu\nu}}=0\,,\qquad\textrm{for }n>1\,.

(31)

We perform the integral on the r.h.s. of Eq. (
3
) and extract the beta function from the anomalous dimension of the two-point function on the l.h.s. Defining
\eta_{A}\equiv-k\partial_{k}Z_{A,k}
and
\beta_{g_{Y}}=1/2\,\eta_{A}g_{Y}
, one computes

k\partial_{k}S_{k}|_{\bar{F}_{\mu\nu}\bar{F}^{\mu\nu}}\equiv k\partial_{k}\left(\frac{Z_{A,k}}{4}\int d^{4}x\bar{F}_{\mu\nu}\bar{F}^{\mu\nu}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s}\text{Tr}\left[k\partial_{k}\rho_{k,k_{\textrm{UV}}}(s)\,e^{-s\,S_{k}^{\prime\prime}}\right]|_{\bar{F}_{\mu\nu}\bar{F}^{\mu\nu}}.

(32)

The regulator-dependent gravitational contribution to the beta function (
1
) finally reads

f_{g}=\frac{3m\tilde{G}}{2\pi\left(m-1\right)}\left(1+\alpha\right),

(33)

where
\tilde{G}=Gk^{2}
is the dimensionless Newton constant. In the limit of sharp regulator,
m\to\infty
, one finds

f_{g}=\frac{3\tilde{G}}{2\pi}\left(1+\alpha\right).

(34)

The result is positive for
\alpha>-1
and shows remarkable stability under our regulator choice for
m\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt$\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}}20
, where it essentially comes to coincide with the sharp regulator limit. For
m=10
the difference to the sharp limit is about 10% and for
m=20
it is 5%.

We note that
f_{g}
arises from the quadratic divergence element of the heat kernel expansion,
\textrm{Tr}\,a_{1}
in Eq. (
30
),
and it is thus proportional to the trans-Planckian fixed-point value of
\tilde{G}
.
This is in agreement with the leading term of known ERG calculations at
\alpha=\xi=1
[
19
,
21
]
, which differs from Eq. (
33
) in the form of the regulator only. Equation (
33
) is independent of the cosmological constant. However – as was discussed,
e.g.
, in Ref.
[
19
]
– the dependence on the cosmological constant can be reintroduced via renormalization-group improvement of the calculation,
i.e.
, by including the scale derivative of
Z_{k}
and the anomalous dimension of the graviton in Eq. (
4
).

The next-to-leading order in the heat kernel expansion,
\textrm{Tr}\,a_{2}
, may include in principle a

G\Lambda\bar{F}_{\mu\nu}\bar{F}^{\mu\nu}
term, corresponding to the logarithmic divergence. However, in flat background, expansion orders beyond the first do not give corrections to the
\bar{F}_{\mu\nu}\bar{F}^{\mu\nu}
operator. This is a consequence of the Ward identity: the photon remains strictly massless in the action (
15
)-(
17
), unlike the graviton, which acquires instead an effective mass proportional to the cosmological constant.
It was shown in Refs.
[
40
,
41
]
, that the logarithmic divergence can be computed in the (gauge-fixing independent) framework of the Vilkovisky-DeWitt geometrical action
[
103
,
104
,
105
]
, that is to say,
by adding to Eqs. (
15
)-(
17
) terms originating
from a metric connection applied to field space, which end up generating an effective mass for the photon.

Despite the fact that our
f_{g}
results are, quite expectedly, gauge-fixing choice dependent, it is important to be aware that this is the usual situation with beta functions of marginal couplings that depend on dimensionful coefficients like Newton and the cosmological constant. To this regard, we reiterate that the couplings of the effective action are not observable physical quantities – which should be completely independent of the choices made for computational convenience – only the critical exponents are physical. We expect that the gauge dependence of the beta function will be, at least in part, absorbed in computations of observable quantities like,
e.g.
, the
S
-matrix. On the other hand, some residual gauge and regulator dependence may also be possibly interpreted as indication of an insufficient truncation in the action.

3.2
Yukawa coupling

We now compute the contribution of minimally coupled Einstein-Hilbert gravity to the running of the Yukawa-coupling operator. We add the following term to the bare
effective action:

S_{\textrm{Yuk}}=\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{g}\,y\,\bar{\psi}\phi\psi\,,

(35)

where
\phi
is a scalar field, which we choose to be real without loss of generality, and
\psi
is a Dirac fermion. As before, we work in the background field method,

g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}\,,\qquad\psi=\tilde{\psi}+\delta\psi\,,\qquad\phi=\tilde{\phi}+\delta\phi\,,

(36)

with flat background metric
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}=\delta_{\mu\nu}
and arbitrary background matter fields

\tilde{\psi}
and
\tilde{\phi}
(note that we indicate the background fields in the Yukawa operator with a tilde to avoid confusion with the Dirac conjugation of the fermions).

After an expansion in the fluctuating fields, the hessian takes a
3\times 3
matrix form,

S^{(2)}=\frac{1}{32\pi G}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\left(h_{\alpha\beta},\delta\psi^{{\dagger}},\delta\phi\right)\left({\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbb{H}_{hh}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}&\mathbb{H}_{h\psi}^{\alpha\beta}&\mathbb{H}_{h\phi}^{\alpha\beta}\\
\mathbb{H}_{\bar{\psi}h}^{\mu\nu}&\mathbb{H}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}&\mathbb{H}_{\bar{\psi}\phi}\\
\mathbb{H}_{\phi h}^{\mu\nu}&\mathbb{H}_{\phi\psi}&\mathbb{H}_{\phi\phi}\end{array}}\right)\left({\begin{array}[]{c}h_{\mu\nu}\\
\delta\psi\\
\delta\phi\end{array}}\right).

(37)

The off-diagonal term mixing gravity and matter can be easily extracted from the action,

S^{(2)}_{\textrm{mix}}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\,\frac{y}{2}\,h\left(\delta\psi^{{\dagger}}\tilde{\phi}\tilde{\psi}+\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\,\delta\phi\,\tilde{\psi}+\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\tilde{\phi}\,\delta\psi\right).

(38)

Note that Eq. (
38
) does not feature linear-derivative terms of the form
\mathbb{V}^{\delta}\bar{D}_{\delta}
.
It is also easy to see that no graviton loop originating from Eq. (
38
) can provide a contribution to the renormalization of the background Yukawa operator,
y\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\tilde{\phi}\,\tilde{\psi}
, at the leading order in
G
of the heat kernel expansion. Thus, we can safely focus on the contributions belonging to the
\mathbb{H}_{hh}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}
term, whose expansion was given in Eq. (
15
).

The hessian takes then the form

\mathbb{H}=-\Box+\mathbb{W}\,,

(39)

where

\mathbb{W}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}=\left(\Lambda+8\pi Gy\,\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\tilde{\phi}\,\tilde{\psi}\right)\mathcal{O}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}

(40)

and

\mathcal{O}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\alpha\right)g^{\alpha\mu}g^{\beta\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\alpha\right)g^{\alpha\nu}g^{\beta\mu}-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\alpha\right)g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\mu\nu}\,.

(41)

In this case, Eq. (
8
) reduces to

\textrm{Tr}\,e^{-sS_{k}^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{1}{(4\pi s)^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\,\textrm{Tr}\,e^{-s\left(\Lambda+8\pi Gy\,\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\tilde{\phi}\,\tilde{\psi}\right)\mathcal{O}}.

(42)

The operator
\mathcal{O}
can be decomposed as

\mathcal{O}=-(1+\alpha)P_{\mathrm{TL}}+(\alpha-3)P_{\mathrm{TR}}\,,

(43)

where, in
d
dimensions,

\begin{split}P_{\mathrm{TR}}^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\mu\nu}&=\frac{1}{d}g^{\alpha\beta}g_{\mu\nu},\\
P_{\mathrm{TL}}^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\mu\nu}&=\frac{1}{2}(\delta^{\alpha}_{\mu}\delta^{\beta}_{\nu}+\delta^{\alpha}_{\nu}\delta^{\beta}_{\mu})-\frac{1}{d}g^{\alpha\beta}g_{\mu\nu}\,,\end{split}

(44)

are projectors on the trace and traceless directions,

P_{\mathrm{TL}}^{2}=P_{\mathrm{TL}}\,,\quad P_{\mathrm{TR}}^{2}=P_{\mathrm{TR}}\,,\quad P_{\mathrm{TL}}P_{\mathrm{TR}}=0\,.

(45)

In
d=4
, the dimension of the trace and traceless subspaces of a symmetric rank-2 tensor are respectively 1 and 9. The trace of the exponential of the operator
\mathcal{O}
is given by the sum of the exponential of the eigenvalues, multiplied with the corresponding multiplicity,

\mathrm{Tr}\,e^{-s\left(\Lambda+8\pi Gy\,\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\tilde{\phi}\,\tilde{\psi}\right)\mathcal{O}}=9\,e^{s\left(1+\alpha\right)\Lambda}e^{s\left(1+\alpha\right)\left(8\pi Gy\,\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\tilde{\phi}\,\tilde{\psi}\right)}+e^{s\left(3-\alpha\right)\Lambda}e^{s\left(3-\alpha\right)\left(8\pi Gy\,\bar{\tilde{\psi}}\tilde{\phi}\,\tilde{\psi}\right)},

(46)

where we have used the fact that
\Lambda
is a constant with respect to both momenta and fields and therefore commutes with both of them.

After expanding Eq. (
46
) we retain the linear order in the Yukawa operator and perform the heat kernel integral, thus finally obtaining a closed-form expression for the gravitational contribution to the Yukawa beta function (
2
) valid to all orders in
\tilde{\Lambda}=\Lambda k^{-2}
,

f_{y}=\frac{\tilde{G}m}{4\pi\left(m-1\right)}\left[\left(3-\alpha\right)\left(1-\tilde{\Lambda}\frac{3-\alpha}{m}\right)^{1-m}+9\left(1+\alpha\right)\left(1-\tilde{\Lambda}\frac{1+\alpha}{m}\right)^{1-m}\right].

(47)

Taking the sharp-regulator limit,
m\to\infty
, one gets,

f_{y}=\frac{\tilde{G}}{4\pi}\left[\left(3-\alpha\right)e^{\left(3-\alpha\right)\tilde{\Lambda}}+9\left(1+\alpha\right)e^{\left(1+\alpha\right)\tilde{\Lambda}}\right].

(48)

5
Summary and conclusions

In this work, we have employed the Schwinger PT flow equation to derive the quantum gravity contributions to the beta functions of the gauge and, for the first time in this scheme, Yukawa coupling of a matter theory. Following common practice in the literature on AS, we have dubbed these gauge group- and flavor-independent corrections to the running couplings as
f_{g}
and
f_{y}
. Working with a flat background metric and in the Einstein-Hilbert truncation, our results show explicitly the gauge-fixing and PT-regulator dependence of
f_{g}
and
f_{y}
. To quantify the sensitivity of our results on these unphysical parameters, and facilitate a direct comparison with recent determinations of
f_{g}
and
f_{y}
in the ERG, our results are computed at the interactive fixed point of the quantum gravity action, which was derived by one of us in the PT scheme in a recent study.

We found remarkable agreement with existing ERG determinations, especially in the case of a minimal matter content (one vector field, one Dirac fermion, one real scalar) where, given equivalent choices for the
truncation and background metric, we obtain comparable values of
f_{g}
and
f_{y}
at the Reuter-like UV fixed point of the Newton and cosmological constants. These results make a strong case that – despite AS being typically associated with the functional RG – the same asymptotically safe behavior of the gravity-matter system emerges independently of the specific choice of regularization technique.

A point of strength of our original determination of the gravitational correction to the Yukawa coupling beta function,
f_{y}
, is that it shows an exponential suppression with negative values of the cosmological constant, which are often preferred at the Reuter-like fixed point in the SM and in some BSM scenarios. Such suppression is highly welcome, as it reduces the typical size assumed by
f_{y}
at the Reuter-like fixed point and aligns its value with the typical anomalous dimensions of renormalizable matter couplings in the absence of gravity. A smaller
f_{y}
increases the likelihood of an exact cancellation in the Yukawa beta function and facilitates the appearance of an asymptotically safe interactive UV fixed point of the matter couplings. Conversely, we have also shown that, as a likely consequence of gauge invariance, such exponential suppression appears to be entirely lifted in the gauge counterpart,
f_{g}
, under the same assumptions for the truncation and background metric.

The above considerations reflect in our phenomenological analysis, where we have compared our determinations of
f_{g}
and
f_{y}
with the typical expectations for their size that were
extracted in heuristic studies of the observational properties of the SM and/or BSM scenarios embedded in an asymptotically safe, quantum scale invariant trans-Planckian UV completion. While the sizes of both
f_{g}
and
f_{y}
at the gravity fixed point are entirely consistent with the ansatz of the asymptotically safe SM – where, by construction, the parameters of the SM are all asymptotically free and stem from a relevant Gaussian UV fixed point
[
38
]
– accommodating interactive fixed points of the irrelevant kind for the matter sector, which could give rise to some of the spectacular features predicted in the literature in the eventual presence of a properly scale-invariant UV completion, appears to be a real challenge in this scheme, at least under the assumptions adopted in this work.

To this regard, our study can be extended along different directions. It would be interesting to compute the PT flow of
f_{g}
and
f_{y}
in concomitance with the flow of the gravitational action, so to estimate the respective back reaction. Extended truncations of the gravity and matter actions, non-flat backgrounds, and higher order effects via RG improvement or next-to-leading order resummation of operators, along the lines of Ref.
[
53
]
, are also likely to have an impact on the size of the trans-Planckian matter beta functions and might generate values more in line with expectations of predictivity arising in the SM and BSM scenarios. Overall, we think our study reinforces the generality of the AS ansatz and provides a possible first step towards a systematic adaptation of the results obtained in the context of the ERG to alternative regularization schemes.
