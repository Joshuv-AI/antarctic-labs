Compact disjointness preserving operators on Banach C ⁡ ( K ) C(K) -modules.
Arkady Kitover, Mehmet Orhon

Abstract
. We show that some well known results concerning compact disjointness preserving operators on Banach lattices can be extended to the more general framework of finitely generated Banach C ⁡ ( K ) C(K) -modules. Specifically, we establish a series representation for such operators analogous to the de Pagter - Wickstead and Arenson–Kitover theorems and provide some details about their spectra.

Introduction and preliminaries

Compact lattice homomorphisms acting between two Banach lattices were characterized independently by B. de Pagter (
[
7
]
) and
A.W. Wickstead (
[
8
]
). To state their result we recall the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a vector lattice. The element
x\in X
is called an atom if the principal ideal
I_{x}
generated by
x
is
one-dimensional.

Theorem 1.2
.

(de Pagter - Wickstead) Let
X,Y
be Banach lattices and
T:X\rightarrow Y
be a compact lattice homomorphism. Then there are pairwise disjoint positive elements
y_{1},y_{2},\ldots\in Y
and pairwise disjoint positive atoms
F_{1},F_{2},\ldots\in X^{\prime}
such that

T=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}\otimes y_{n},

(1)

where the series in (
1
) converges in the operator norm.

H. Kamowitz obtained (
[
5
]
) the following characterization of compact weighted composition operators on
C(K)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

(Kamowitz) Let
K
be a compact Hausdorff space and
C(K)
be the space of all continuous complex-valued functions on
K
. Let
w\in C(K)
and let
\varphi
be a continuous map of
K
into itself.

The operator
T,Tf=w(f\circ\varphi),f\in C(K)
, is compact if and only if the function
w
is constant on every component of the set
\{k\in K:|w(k)|>0\}
.

The above-mentioned results were extended to arbitrary compact disjointness preserving operators between Banach lattices by E.A. Arenson and the first-named author in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

(Arenson - Kitover)
Let
X,Y
be Banach lattices and
T:X\rightarrow Y
be a compact disjointness preserving operator. Then there are pairwise disjoint elements
y_{1},y_{2},\ldots\in Y
and pairwise disjoint atoms
F_{1},F_{2},\ldots\in X^{\prime}
such that

T=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}\otimes y_{n},

(2)

where the series in (
2
) converges in the operator norm.

Remark 1.5
.

Although Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
are formally similar in statement, extending Theorem 1.2 to all compact disjointness preserving operators requires some additional work (see
[
4

[
1
]
and
[
3
]
.

The goal of the current paper is to obtain an analog of Theorem
1.4
for compact disjointness preserving operators acting between two Banach
C(K)
-modules.

For the reader’s convenience we briefly recall some basic facts about Banach
C(K)
-modules (see
[
2
]
)

A Banach space
X
is called a Banach
C(K)
-module if there is an isometry
m:C(K)\rightarrow L(X)
, where
L(X)
is the space of all bounded linear operators on
X
.

The center
Z(X)
of a Banach
C(K)
-module
X
is defined (see
[
2
, p.33]
) as the closure of
C(K)
in
L(X)
in the strong operator topology. In the current paper we will always assume that
C(K)=Z(X)
.

For any
x\in X
the subspace
X(x)
of
X
,
X(x)=cl_{X}\{fx:f\in C(K)\}
can be endowed with the structure of a Banach lattice with the cone
X(x)_{+}=cl_{X}\{fx:f\in C(K),f\geq 0\}
. The space
X(x)
is called a cyclic subspace of
X
.

By analogy with definition
1.1
we introduce the following definition

Definition 1.6
.

Let
X
be a Banach
C(K)
-module. An element
x\in X
is called an atom if the cyclic subspace
X(x)
is one-dimensional.

A Banach
C(K)
-module
X
is called finitely-generated if there are

x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
such that

cl\{f_{1}x_{1}+f_{2}x_{2}+\ldots+f_{n}x_{n},f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in C(K)\}=X.

A Banach
C(K)
-module
X
is called a Kaplansky module if the compact space
K
is extremally disconnected and for any
x\in X
the set
\{f\in C(K):fx=0\}
is a band in
C(K)
.

The main result

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
be a finitely generated Banach
C(K_{1})
-module with generators
x_{1},\ldots,x_{p}
and
Y
be a Banach
C(K_{2})
-module. Let
T:X\rightarrow Y
be a linear disjointness preserving operator. Then, the following conditions are equivalent

(1)

The operators
T:X(x_{j})\rightarrow Y(Tx_{j}),j=1,\ldots p
are compact.

(2)

For any
x\in X
the operator
T:X(x)\rightarrow Y(Tx)
is compact.

(3)

The operator
T
is compact.

(4)

T=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}\otimes\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k(n)}y_{j,n},

(3)

where
F_{n}
are pairwise disjoint atoms in
X^{\prime}
,
y_{j,n}\in Y
for
n\in\mathds{N},1\leq j\leq k(n)
, and
k(n)\leq p
for any
n\in\mathds{N}
, Moreover, the elements

y_{i,m}
and
y_{j,n}
, where
m\neq n,1\leq i\leq k(m),1\leq j\leq k(n)
, are disjoint,
and the series in (
3
) converges in the operator norm.

Proof.

We will first assume that the Banach
C(K_{2})
-module
Y
is a Kaplansky module. The implications
(4)\Rightarrow(3)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(1)
are trivial. We only need to prove that
(1)\Rightarrow(4)
.

By Theorem
1.4
we have

T:X(x_{i})\rightarrow Y(Tx_{i})=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}G_{i,n}\otimes z_{i,n},1\leq i\leq p,

(4)

where for any
i,1\leq i\leq p
the functionals
G_{i,n},n\in\mathds{N}
are pairwise disjoint atoms in
X(x_{i})^{\prime}
,

z_{i,n},n\in\mathds{N}
, are pairwise disjoint elements of the Banach lattice
Y(Tx_{i})
, and the series in (
4
) converge in the operator norm.

Let
E=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{p}supp\,Tx_{i}
and let
\varphi:E\rightarrow K_{1}
be the map from the statement of Theorem
1.8
. We claim that for any
n\in\mathds{N}
and for any
i,1\leq i\leq p
, the set
\varphi(supp\,z_{i,n})
consists of a single point
s_{i,n}\in K_{1}
. To prove the claim let us fix an
n\in\mathds{N}
and an
i,1\leq i\leq p
.
Let
P_{i,n}
be the projection in the center of the Dedekind complete Banach lattice
Y(z_{i,n})
corresponding to the function

\chi_{supp\,z_{i,n}}
. For any
f\in C(K)
we have

P_{i,n}fx_{i}=\langle f^{\prime}G_{i,n},x_{i}\rangle z_{i,n}
.
Because
|f^{\prime}G_{i,n}|\leq\|f\||G_{i,n}|
and
G_{i,n}
is
an atom in
X(x_{i})^{\prime}
we have

f^{\prime}G_{i,n}=\lambda(f)G_{i,n}
, where
\lambda(f)\in\mathds{C}
. Clearly
\lambda(f)
is a multiplicative bounded functional on
C(K)
and therefore there is a
s_{i,n}\in K_{1}
such that
\lambda(f)=f(s_{i,n})
. On the other hand

P_{i,n}Tfx_{i}=(f\circ\varphi)P_{i,n}Tx_{i}
. Thus, the restriction of the operator
(f\circ\varphi)P_{i,n}T
on
X(x_{i})
is one-dimensional and our claim is proved.

Notice that because for a fixed
i
the atoms
G_{i,n}
are pairwise disjoint, all the points
s_{i,n},n\in\mathds{N}
are distinct.

We can now construct the terms in series (
3
). Let us number the elements
z_{i,n},n\in\mathds{N},1\leq i\leq p
, in lexicographic order. Consider the element
z_{1,1}
. There are two possibilities:

(a)
s_{1,1}\neq s_{i,n},(i,n)\neq(1,1)
.In this case we set

k(1)=1
and
y_{1,1}=z_{1,1}
. To define
F_{1}
consider the projection
P_{1}
corresponding to the characteristic function of the set
supp\,y_{1,1}
. The operator
P_{1}T:X\rightarrow Y
is one-dimensional and therefore there is an
F_{1}\in X^{\prime}
such that
P_{1}T=F_{1}\otimes y_{1,1}
. Because the element
y_{1,1}
is disjoint with all the elements
z_{i,n},(i,n)\neq(1,1)
, it follows from (
4
) that
F_{1}|X(x_{i})=0,i=2,\ldots,p
. Therefore
f^{\prime}F_{1}=f(s_{1,1})F_{1},f\in C(K)
, and by Corollary 7.7.1 in
[
2
]

F_{1}
is an atom in the Banach
Z(X^{\prime})
-module
X^{\prime}
.

(b) Assume that the set

M=\{(i,n):s_{i,n}=s_{1,1},(i,n)\neq(1,1)\}
is not empty.
Notice that the cardinality
c
of
M
can not exceed
p-1
. We put
k(1)=c+1
,
y_{1,1}=z_{1,1}
and denote the elements of the set
\{z_{i,n}:(i,n)\in M\}
as
y_{2,1},\ldots,y_{k(1),1}
. We can assume without loss of generality (by passing to a basis) that the elements
y_{1,1},\ldots,y_{k(1),1}
are linearly independent.
For every
j,1\leq j\leq k(1)
, we define the functional
H_{j}\in X^{\prime}
as described in part (a) above. Finally, we put

F_{1}=H_{1}+\ldots+H_{k(1)}
. It is immediate to see that

is an atom in
X^{\prime}
.

This procedure allows us to define the terms of the series (
3

4

To get rid of the condition that

is a Kaplansky module we just need to consider

as an operator from

and recall that

considered as

-module is a Kaplansky module
(see
[
2
]
).

3.
Some remarks about the spectrum of compact disjointness preserving operators

We now turn to spectral properties of compact disjointness preserving operators.

In
[
5
]
H. Kamowitz proved the following

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
K
be a compact Hausdorff space and
T
be a compact weighted composition operator on
C(K)
,
Tf=w(f\circ\varphi)
,
where
w\in C(K)
and
\varphi:K\rightarrow K
is a continuous map. The following statements are equivalent

(1)

\lambda\in\sigma(T)\setminus\{0\}
.

(2)

There is a
\varphi
-periodic
k\in K
of the smallest period
p
such that

\lambda^{p}=w(k)w(\varphi(k))\ldots w(\varphi_{p-1}(k))
.

In the current section we will prove the following generalization of Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X
be a Banach lattice. Let
T:X\to X
be a continuous disjointness preserving operator.

Assume that
\lambda\neq 0
,
\lambda
is an isolated point in
\sigma(T)
and moreover that
|\lambda|
is isolated in the set
|\sigma(T)|=\{|\alpha|,\alpha\in\sigma(T)\}
. Assume also that
dim\ker{(\lambda I-T)}<\infty
.

Then there are an atom
F\in X^{\prime}
and a
p\in\mathds{N}
such that
(T^{\prime})^{p}F=\lambda^{p}F
and the elements

F,\ldots,(T^{\prime})^{p-1}F
are pairwise disjoint atoms.

Proof.

Let
X
be a Banach lattice. From our assumptions follows that there is an
R
,
0<R<|\lambda|
, such that
\sigma(T)\cap R\mathds{T}=\emptyset
, where
\mathds{T}
denotes the unit circle. Let
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
be the spectral subspaces corresponding, respectively, to the subsets of
\sigma(T)
located inside and outside of the disk of radius
R
.
Then
X_{1}
is a closed lattice ideal in
X
(see e.g.
[
2
]
). The annulator of
X_{1}
is a band
Y
in
X^{\prime}
and the restriction of the operator
T^{\prime}
on the band
Z=Y^{\perp}
is an invertible disjointness preserving operator. It remains to apply Theorem 13.10 from
[
2
]
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

It remains an open question whether the statement of Theorem
3.2
holds if the condition that
|\lambda|
is isolated in
|\sigma(T)|
is omitted. However, we can show that this condition is unnecessary under the additional assumption that the adjoint operator
T^{\prime}
preserves disjointness.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
X
be a Banach
C(K)
-module,
T:X\rightarrow X
be a compact disjointness preserving operator, and

\lambda\in\sigma(T)\setminus\{0\}
. Then there are an atom
F\in X^{\prime}
and a
p\in\mathds{N}
such that
(T^{\prime})^{p}F=\lambda^{p}F
and the elements

F,\ldots,(T^{\prime})^{p-1}F
are pairwise disjoint atoms.

Proof.

We can assume without loss of generality that
\lambda=1
. Let
x\in X
be such that
x\neq 0
and
Tx=x
. Then the restriction of
T
on
X(x)
is a compact operator from
X(x)
into itself. It remains to apply Theorem
3.2
and Corollary 7.7.1 from
[
2
]
.
∎

One should mention that A.W. Wickstead (see

T
on a Banach lattice in the case when the conjugate operator
