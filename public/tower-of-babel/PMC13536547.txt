The moderating role of sociodemographic characteristics and health behaviours in the relationship between daily living activities and postoperative recovery in CABG patients
Authors: Esra Denler, Gizem Kubat Bakir
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study examined the moderating role of sociodemographic characteristics and health behaviours in the relationship between daily living activities and postoperative recovery in patients who underwent coronary artery bypass graft surgery.MethodThis descriptive, cross-sectional, and analytical study was conducted with 200 patients who underwent coronary artery bypass graft surgery in two training and research hospitals in Istanbul between December 2024 and April 2025. Data were collected using the Patient Assessment Form, the Barthel Activities of Daily Living Index, and the Postoperative Recovery Index. Pearson correlation analysis and moderation analyses via PROCESS Macro (Model 1) with 5,000 bootstrap resamples were performed.ResultsA significant negative relationship was identified between daily living activities and postoperative recovery (r = − .453, p < .01). Physical activities recorded the highest subscale mean (2.52) and the strongest correlation with ADL scores (r = − .834, p < .01). BMI (B = − 0.214, p = .016), educational status (B = 0.036, p = .034), income level (B = − 0.023, p = .042), smoking status (B = − 0.039, p = .018), alcohol consumption (B = − 0.018, p = .009), and previous surgical experience (B = − 0.042, p = .023) each significantly moderated this relationship.ConclusionThe relationship between daily living activities and postoperative recovery is significantly shaped by individual sociodemographic and behavioural characteristics. It is recommended that individualised care approaches be strengthened by incorporating patients’ sociodemographic profiles and health behaviours into postoperative nursing care planning.

Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases remain the leading cause of death and disability worldwide, imposing an immense burden on health systems globally. According to the World Health Organization, an estimated 19.8 million people died from cardiovascular diseases in 2022, representing approximately 32% of all global deaths [1]. According to the most recent death and cause-of-death statistics published by the Turkish Statistical Institute, cardiovascular diseases account for approximately 37% of all deaths in Turkey [2]. Coronary artery disease causes myocardial ischaemia by narrowing the coronary artery due to atherosclerotic plaques forming on the artery wall [3].

Coronary artery bypass graft surgery is one of the preferred methods for patients with multi-vessel disease who do not benefit from other treatments [4]. This operation accounts for more than half of all heart surgeries performed in adults worldwide [5].

The duration of postoperative recovery depends on three main factors: the patient’s health status and the extent of surgery and the quality of postoperative care received. Hospital stays for patients last between 5 and 6 days but recovery time spans from six to eight weeks. Various complications may also occur during this process [6]. However, in addition to surgical success, the recovery process must be evaluated holistically in terms of the patient regaining their physical strength, achieving psychological adjustment, and being able to use their social skills again [7].

Daily living activities include the basic physical, mental, and social functions that individuals perform independently [8]. The recovery process following coronary bypass surgery includes physical recovery as well as social and functional rehabilitation of patients [9]. The speed at which patients resume their daily routines following surgery determines both their risk of complications and their recovery time. Research indicates that patients need extended time to resume their normal activities during the initial weeks following surgery [10]. Patients who undergo surgery experience physical challenges together with mental health issues which create obstacles for their recovery [11]. The practice of caring for daily activities stands as a core foundation of nursing practice [12]. The patient’s prompt participation in daily activities speeds up recovery time while decreasing medical complications and lightening the workload for healthcare providers [13].

The present study aimed to examine the extent to which daily living activities predict postoperative recovery outcomes in patients who underwent coronary artery bypass graft surgery, and to determine the degree to which this relationship is moderated by sociodemographic characteristics and health behaviours. Specifically, the study sought to quantify the direction and magnitude of the association between Activities of Daily Living (ADL) scores and Postoperative Recovery Index (PRI) scores, and to test whether body mass index, educational status, income level, smoking status, alcohol consumption, and previous surgical experience significantly moderate this relationship. The findings are expected to inform the development of individually tailored nursing care plans and evidence-based multidisciplinary rehabilitation programmes.

Method

Study population and sample

This study is a descriptive, cross-sectional, and analytical study designed to investigate the moderating role of sociodemographic characteristics and health behaviours in the effect of daily living activities on the postoperative recovery process of patients who have undergone coronary artery bypass graft surgery. The study population consisted of patients who underwent surgery between December 2024 and April 2025 in the cardiovascular surgery departments of a public teaching and research hospital in Istanbul and a medical faculty hospital affiliated with a foundation university (Figs. 1, 2 and 3).

The sample size was calculated using the classical formula for unknown populations [14]. Standard deviation values obtained from similar studies were taken into account. At least 112 individuals were required for a 95% confidence interval and a 5% margin of error. Considering possible data losses, 30% was added, making the target 146, but the study was completed with a total of 200 patients.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: being over 18 years of age, being conscious, having no hearing or speech problems, being literate, having undergone bypass surgery, being present for follow-up on the 2nd and 6th days after surgery, and voluntarily agreeing to participate. Those with a psychiatric diagnosis were excluded from the study.

Body mass index (BMI) was calculated using height and weight values: 18–27 was considered normal, 28–38 overweight, and 39 and above obese. Educational level was divided into 7 groups, ranging from ‘literate’ to postgraduate. Income status was classified as ‘low income, balanced income, or high income.’ Smoking status was assessed as yes/no, alcohol consumption as regular, occasional, or none; and history of previous surgery as present/absent in binary groups.

Study procedures

The researchers conducted face-to-face interviews with patients after their intensive care discharge. The interview duration for each participant ranged from 20 to 25 min. The researchers employed three assessment tools which included the Patient Assessment Form and the Barthel Activities of Daily Living Index and the Postoperative Recovery Index (Turkish version).

The researcher developed 18 assessment questions for the Patient Assessment Form based on existing literature. It included information such as age, gender, marital status, education, height-weight, chronic diseases, and smoking-alcohol habits.The Barthel Index served as the assessment tool for daily functioning. The Barthel Index evaluates ten different activities through scores ranging from 0 to 100 points which include continence and nutrition and bathing and dressing and mobility and climbing stairs. A higher score indicates a greater level of functional independence. The validity and reliability of the Turkish version of the scale were established by Küçükdeveci et al. in 2000; Cronbach’s alpha was found to be 0.93 [15]. In the present study, the Cronbach’s alpha coefficient was calculated as 0.91, indicating excellent internal consistency.The Postoperative Recovery Index originated from Butler et al. in 2012 before Cengiz and Aygin translated it into Turkish in 2019 [16, 17]. The original scale consists of 25 items; however, one item was excluded from the present study following evaluation by a panel of three clinical nursing experts. The panel determined that the item in question was not applicable to the early inpatient postoperative context in which data were collected, as it pertained to functional activities typically assessed only after hospital discharge. The exclusion was thus grounded in clinical appropriateness rather than statistical criteria. The final version used in this study contained 24 items across five subscales measuring bowel symptoms, general symptoms, physical activities, desire and motivation, and psychological symptoms. It is a Likert scale (1–5). A high score indicates greater difficulty in recovery. The Cronbach’s alpha for the Turkish adaptation was 0.857; in the present study, the overall Cronbach’s alpha was 0.83, indicating acceptable internal consistency. Subscale alpha values ranged from 0.69 to 0.96. The lower bound of 0.69, observed in the Psychological Symptoms subscale, approaches but remains near the conventional threshold of 0.70, which may reflect the multidimensional and context-sensitive nature of psychological recovery in the early postoperative period [16, 17].

Statistical analysis

First, descriptive statistics were performed. Categorical variables were presented as frequency and percentage, while continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Internal consistency of the scales was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha coefficient.

Interrelationships between variables were examined using Pearson correlation. Based on the r value, correlations below 0.30 were considered weak, between 0.30 and 0.70 moderate, and above 0.70 strong. Hayes’ PROCESS Macro add-on (Model 1) was used for hypothesis testing [18]. In this moderation framework, daily living activities were the independent variable, and postoperative recovery was the dependent variable. BMI, education, income, smoking, alcohol consumption, and previous surgery were tested as moderators. The bootstrap technique was performed with 5,000 repetitions, and 95% confidence intervals were calculated.

Unstandardised beta coefficients and CI values were reported. All analyses were performed using IBM SPSS 26.0. The significance level was p < .05; p < .01 was additionally specified.

Ethical considerations

Ethical committee approval for the study was obtained from a public hospital on 15 October 2024, no. 2024/18/942. Institutional permissions were also obtained via letters dated 4 November 2024 (258553772) and 10 March 2025 (E-32835138-108.01-338166). Written consent was obtained from all participants.

Personal data were anonymised for confidentiality purposes, coded, and stored on encrypted computers accessible only to the research team. Permission to use the Barthel Index and the Postoperative Recovery Index was obtained from the authors. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines.

Results

Sociodemographic and clinical characteristics

The sample comprised predominantly male patients aged 50 years and above, with a mean age of 60.76 ± 0.43 years. More than half of the participants were overweight or obese based on BMI classification, and the majority were retired, married, and had completed primary school education. Diabetes mellitus and hypertension were the most prevalent chronic conditions, and approximately half of the participants reported a history of prior surgery. These characteristics are consistent with the typical demographic profile of patients undergoing CABG surgery in the Turkish clinical context (Table 1).

Scale scores and reliability

Among the five subscales of the Postoperative Recovery Index, Physical Activities yielded the highest mean score (2.52 ± 0.56), indicating that physical functioning constituted the domain of greatest recovery difficulty in the early postoperative period. Bowel Symptoms recorded the lowest mean score (1.02 ± 0.19), reflecting minimal gastrointestinal burden in this sample. The total PRI mean was 1.52 ± 0.25. The overall Cronbach’s alpha for the PRI was 0.83, indicating acceptable internal consistency. Subscale alpha values ranged from 0.69 (Psychological Symptoms) to 0.96 (Bowel Symptoms); all values meet or approach the conventional threshold of 0.70, supporting the reliability of the instrument in this hospitalised CABG population. The Barthel ADL Index yielded a mean score of 42.10 ± 0.43, with a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.91, reflecting excellent internal consistency (Table 1).

Relationships between variables

A significant negative moderate correlation was identified between ADL total scores and PRI total scores (r = − .453, p < .01), indicating that higher levels of functional independence were associated with lower recovery difficulty. Among the PRI subscales, Physical Activities demonstrated the strongest association with both the PRI total score (r = .602, p < .01) and ADL (r = − .834, p < .01), underscoring physical functioning as the central domain linking daily activity performance to postoperative recovery outcomes. Desire and Motivation Symptoms showed the second strongest correlation with the PRI total (r = .624, p < .01). Psychological Symptoms demonstrated weak and non-significant associations with ADL (r = .047, p > .05) and Physical Activities (r = .036, p > .05), suggesting that psychological recovery follows a trajectory independent of physical functioning in the early postoperative period (Table 2).

Moderation analysis using PROCESS Macro (Model 1) with 5,000 bootstrap resamples confirmed that six sociodemographic and behavioural variables significantly moderated the relationship between ADL and postoperative recovery. BMI was the strongest moderator (B = − 0.214, 95% CI [− 0.388, − 0.040], p = .016, R² = 0.214), indicating that the beneficial effect of daily activity on recovery was amplified at higher BMI levels. Educational status (B = 0.036, 95% CI [0.003, 0.069], p = .034, R² = 0.212), income level (B = − 0.023, 95% CI [− 0.045, − 0.001], p = .042, R² = 0.205), smoking status (B = − 0.039, 95% CI [− 0.071, − 0.007], p = .018, R² = 0.206), alcohol consumption (B = − 0.018, 95% CI [− 0.031, − 0.005], p = .009, R² = 0.205), and previous surgical experience (B = − 0.042, 95% CI [− 0.078, − 0.006], p = .023, R² = 0.211) each exerted a statistically significant moderating influence. All overall models were significant at p < .001 (Table 3).

Examination of conditional interaction effects revealed systematic variation in the ADL–recovery relationship across moderator levels. For BMI, patients with higher BMI demonstrated steeper improvement in PRI scores as ADL levels increased, with estimated mean PRI scores declining from 2.65 to 1.35 across the ADL range (− 2 SD to + 2 SD), compared with a smaller decline from 1.75 to 2.15 in the low BMI group. Patients with lower educational attainment showed higher baseline recovery difficulty but a steeper rate of improvement with increasing ADL performance. Comparable patterns of differential moderation were observed for income level, smoking status, alcohol consumption, and prior surgical experience, suggesting that individual sociodemographic and behavioural characteristics substantively shape the trajectory of postoperative functional recovery (Table 4).

Discussion

The findings of the present study indicate that functional independence in daily living activities is a significant determinant of postoperative recovery quality following coronary artery bypass graft surgery, and that this relationship is meaningfully shaped by individual sociodemographic and behavioural characteristics. The results contribute to a growing body of evidence supporting the integration of early mobilisation and individualised rehabilitation approaches into postoperative cardiac nursing care.

Emamzadehashemi et al. (2024) established in their research that daily activities serve as a vital indicator of life quality and most patients require assistance with these tasks [19]. Zhang and colleagues (2024) documented that elderly patients who started cardiac rehabilitation early experienced better results in ejection fraction and 6-minute walk distance tests [20]. The systematic review by Yau and colleagues (2021) confirmed that prehabilitation enhances postoperative functional capacity [21]. These findings collectively support the interpretation that early resumption of daily activities not only accelerates physical recovery but also reduces the risk of postoperative complications, reinforcing the clinical importance of nurse-led mobilisation protocols initiated in the immediate postoperative period.

The demographic profile of the sample — predominantly male, aged 50 and above — reflects the well-established epidemiological pattern of CABG patients. The research by Zhang et al. (2024) shows that elderly patients who start cardiac rehabilitation early can recover their daily activities by making up for age-related performance declines [20]. Rizvanovic et al. (2025) found in their study that women experience higher death rates and surgical complications during their first year of CABG surgery compared to male patients [22]. The study by Emamzadehashemi et al. (2024) demonstrated that patients from various genders achieve different levels of quality of life [19]. The underrepresentation of female patients in the present sample warrants attention; biological differences between sexes alongside socioeconomic disparities suggest that gender-sensitive care pathways are needed in postoperative cardiac rehabilitation.

BMI emerged as the strongest moderator of the ADL–recovery relationship (B = − 0.214, p = .016), indicating that the beneficial effect of daily activity on recovery was amplified among patients with higher BMI. The research by Ding et al. (2024) showed that patients with low-normal BMI experienced elevated postoperative blood loss and required extended ventilation periods [23]. This finding suggests that BMI exerts complex and non-linear effects on surgical outcomes, and that early patient mobilisation may be particularly consequential for patients with elevated BMI, for whom physical inactivity carries heightened metabolic and cardiovascular risk.

Educational status also moderated the ADL–recovery relationship (B = 0.036, p = .034), with lower-educated patients showing steeper recovery improvement as daily activity levels increased. The research conducted by Galmarini et al. (2024) demonstrated that visual-based educational methods produce better learning outcomes than traditional teaching methods for understanding health information [24]. Shen et al. (2025) found that postoperative self-management behaviors improved when patients received educational support from their families and when they had higher educational backgrounds [25]. These findings underscore the importance of adapting patient education strategies to health literacy levels; visually oriented, simplified instructional materials may confer disproportionate benefit for patients with lower formal education, who may be less equipped to engage with standard written health information.

The study by Shen et al. (2025) demonstrated that family support directly affects marital status and enhances patients’ ability to manage their health conditions [25]. The research by Dolapoğlu and colleagues (2024) demonstrated that caregiver responsibilities lead to changes in employment status and family dynamics [26]. The high prevalence of spousal caregiving in the present sample suggests that family-centred rehabilitation approaches, which actively engage the primary caregiver, may be particularly effective in this patient population. Cardiac rehabilitation programmes should therefore consider incorporating structured caregiver support components.

Income level moderated the ADL–recovery relationship (B = − 0.023, p = .042). The study by Khan et al. (2025) demonstrated that patients from low-income backgrounds experienced longer hospital stays and developed additional medical complications [27]. El Moheb et al. (2024) discovered that economic difficulties by themselves did not raise hospital admission numbers but they influenced how patients were discharged and for how long they stayed in the hospital [28]. The relatively favourable recovery outcomes observed among lower-income participants in the present study may reflect compensatory mechanisms such as stronger informal social support networks; however, the multifaceted relationship between socioeconomic status, nutritional adequacy, and rehabilitation resource accessibility warrants further investigation.

Smoking status moderated recovery outcomes (B = − 0.039, p = .018), with non-smokers demonstrating greater recovery benefit from increased daily activity. The research by Li et al. (2024) demonstrated that smoking behaviors directly influence the development of infections which occur after cardiothoracic surgical procedures [29]. The study by Navas-Blanco et al. (2024) showed that patients who changed their lifestyle before surgery had reduced metabolic stress levels [30]. These findings reinforce the clinical rationale for incorporating structured preoperative and postoperative smoking cessation interventions into CABG care pathways, given the direct impact of smoking on vascular integrity, wound healing, and pulmonary function during recovery.

Alcohol consumption moderated the ADL–recovery relationship at the highest level of statistical significance among all moderators (B = − 0.018, p = .009). The study by Navas-Blanco et al. (2024) showed that patients who received optimal lifestyle care achieved faster recovery while their physical and mental stress decreased [30]. The pattern of moderation by alcohol consumption warrants cautious interpretation given the small number of regular drinkers in the sample (3.5%), which limits the statistical power of subgroup comparisons. Nonetheless, the finding is consistent with evidence linking alcohol use to altered inflammatory responses and delayed tissue repair following cardiac surgery.

Previous surgical experience significantly moderated the ADL–recovery relationship (B = − 0.042, p = .023). Although those who had not undergone surgery previously had higher recovery scores at baseline, they generally showed poorer recovery. The patients who had undergone surgery before entered the procedure with more accurate expectations and learned effective coping strategies. This suggests that prior surgical experience confers psychosocial advantages — including more realistic outcome expectations and established coping repertoires — that facilitate engagement with postoperative rehabilitation. Patients undergoing their first cardiac surgery may therefore benefit from targeted preoperative psychological preparation and peer support programmes.

The research by McKeever et al. (2024) shows that cardiac rehabilitation enhances health markers for diabetic and non-diabetic patients but produces distinct results based on patient background [31]. The study by Schwarzova et al. (2024) identified hypertension together with renal failure and advanced age and gastrointestinal pathology history as primary risk factors for gastrointestinal complications during cardiac surgery [32]. The high prevalence of comorbid conditions in the present sample — particularly diabetes, hypertension, and combined disease profiles — highlights the clinical complexity of the CABG population and supports the need for integrated, multidisciplinary postoperative management that addresses coexisting chronic conditions alongside surgical recovery.

Kawahara et al. (2025) discovered that surgical patients experienced decreased physical abilities yet their anxiety and depression symptoms started to improve [33]. The absence of a significant correlation between psychological symptoms and physical activity performance in the present study is consistent with this trajectory, suggesting that psychological and physical recovery following CABG proceed along relatively independent pathways. This dissociation has important clinical implications: improving physical functioning does not automatically resolve psychological distress, and vice versa. Routine psychological screening and targeted psychosocial support should therefore be integrated into postoperative care protocols as a distinct component rather than a by-product of physical rehabilitation.

The research by Schwarzova (2024) demonstrated that gastrointestinal complications following cardiac surgery lead to elevated patient death rates [32]. The minimal gastrointestinal symptom burden observed in the present sample may reflect the relatively uncomplicated postoperative courses of the enrolled patients; nonetheless, ongoing monitoring of bowel function remains clinically warranted given the documented association between GI complications and postoperative mortality in cardiac surgery populations.

According to Prasankok and Banharak (2024) patients who are older and have chronic diseases and depression tend to lose their appetite [34]. The strong association between desire and motivation symptoms and total recovery observed in the present study (r = .624, p < .01) underscores the clinical significance of nutritional status and motivational engagement as recovery indicators. Appetite and motivational support should be addressed proactively within postoperative nursing care plans, particularly in older patients with comorbidities.

The research by Yang et al. (2024) demonstrated through their protocol study that cardiopulmonary rehabilitation programs lead to better patient outcomes [35]. The finding that physical activities constituted the domain of greatest recovery difficulty is consistent with the established evidence base for structured cardiopulmonary rehabilitation. It further suggests that recovery assessments and rehabilitation goals should be defined separately across physical, psychological, nutritional, and motivational dimensions, rather than treated as a unidimensional construct.

The study design as a cross-sectional survey prevents researchers from determining direct cause-effect relationships between variables. The data collection at one specific time point fails to show the complete evolution of recovery processes. The research results lack general applicability because the study participants came from two Istanbul hospitals only.

These limitations notwithstanding, the study makes several methodological contributions. Research should implement longitudinal methods to monitor patients throughout their recovery journey from pre-surgical stages through post-discharge periods. Research conducted in various cultural settings with enhanced female participant numbers would produce highly significant results. The implementation of individualized care plans through intervention studies would generate practical evidence to enhance clinical practice. The application of PROCESS Macro moderation modelling in the nursing literature on CABG recovery represents a methodological advance that allows simultaneous testing of multiple moderating pathways within a single analytical framework. Future studies should extend this approach to longitudinal designs and diverse clinical settings to enhance the generalisability and causal interpretability of findings.

Conclusion

The present study demonstrated that higher functional independence in daily living activities is significantly associated with more favourable postoperative recovery outcomes in CABG patients. Crucially, this relationship was moderated by BMI, educational status, income level, smoking status, alcohol consumption, and previous surgical experience, confirming that sociodemographic and behavioural characteristics substantively shape the recovery trajectory.

Physical activities constituted the domain of greatest recovery difficulty, supporting the need for early structured mobilisation. The independent trajectories of physical and psychological recovery underline the necessity of addressing both dimensions as distinct clinical priorities within postoperative care protocols.

Postoperative nursing practice should incorporate individualised care planning that accounts for each patient’s sociodemographic profile and health behaviours. Patients with lower health literacy, adverse health behaviours, or no prior surgical experience represent subgroups requiring targeted educational and psychosocial support. Longitudinal, multicentre studies with greater female representation are needed to extend and generalise these findings.
