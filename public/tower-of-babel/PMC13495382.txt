Targeted CYP27A1 gene analysis for early identification of cerebrotendinous xanthomatosis in juvenile cataract patients: report of a novel variant
Authors: Ali Ceylan, Serkan Güler, Fahri Onur Aydin, Yusuf Berk Akbas, Burcin Kepez Yildiz, Yusuf Yildirim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCerebrotendinous xanthomatosis (CTX) is a rare yet treatable neurometabolic disorder caused by pathogenic variants in the CYP27A1 gene. Juvenile-onset cataracts may precede systemic signs, allowing for early diagnosis. This study evaluated the prevalence of CYP27A1 variants in patients with bilateral juvenile cataracts and described related clinical findings.MethodsThis single-center retrospective study was conducted between May 2022 and April 2025 at Başakşehir Çam and Sakura City Hospital, Istanbul. Patients aged 6 months to 21 years with bilateral juvenile cataracts of undetermined etiology were included. Those with congenital, traumatic, or defined metabolic cataracts were excluded. All underwent ophthalmic evaluation and targeted CYP27A1 sequencing (exons 1–9, exon–intron boundaries) with confirmatory Sanger sequencing. Variants were classified per ACMG/AMP guidelines using ClinVar, HGMD, VarSome, and Franklin databases.ResultsAmong 128 patients (42.2% male, 57.8% female; mean age 10.9 ± 9.4 years), two (1.5%, 95% CI: 0.2–5.4) carried a novel homozygous CYP27A1 c.398G > A (p.W133*) pathogenic variant, and three (2.4%, 95% CI: 0.5–6.9) had a heterozygous c.884 C > T (p.A295V) variant of uncertain significance. Both families had parental consanguinity. Both patients with homozygous pathogenic variants presented with bilateral cerulean cataracts.DiscussionThe absence of systemic symptoms at diagnosis highlights the feasibility of ophthalmology-based genetic screening for CTX. Identifying affected individuals before neurological onset enables timely initiation of oral chenodeoxycholic acid replacement therapy, which may reduce cholestanol and bile alcohol accumulation and help prevent irreversible complications.ConclusionIncorporating CYP27A1 sequencing into the evaluation of bilateral juvenile cataracts may facilitate earlier detection of CTX and timely referral for appropriate management, especially in high-consanguinity regions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12886-026-05023-0.Keywords: Cerebrotendinous xanthomatosis (CTX), Juvenile cataract, CYP27A1 Gene, Genetic testing in ophthalmology, Metabolic diseases

Background

Cerebrotendinous xanthomatosis (CTX) is an autosomal recessive, treatable neurometabolic disorder caused by pathogenic variants in the CYP27A1 gene, which causes abnormal lipid storage and bile acid synthesis defects [1]. The CYP27A1 gene codes for the mitochondrial enzyme sterol 27-hydroxylase, and pathogenic variants in this gene result in reduced production of primary bile acids, particularly chenodeoxycholic acid (CDCA) and cholic acid. This leads to an accumulation of cholesterol and other precursor sterol metabolites in metabolic pathways. Consequently, the cholesterol metabolite cholestanol, which cannot be excreted, accumulates in lipophilic tissues such as plasma, brain, eyes, and tendons [2, 3].

CTX is a rare condition with a low global prevalence that varies regionally. Prevalence estimates in the literature range from 1:50,000 to 1:150,000, but these figures are likely to be underreported due to undiagnosed cases [4]. In the absence of newborn screening, the current global situation is such that all countries have limited screening programmes, with diagnoses being made at a later stage and the true prevalence being underestimated [5].

The onset times and severity of CTX clinical signs and symptoms vary, but typically include persistent diarrhea starting in infancy, bilateral juvenile-onset cataracts, tendon xanthomas starting in early adulthood, and progressive neurological dysfunction starting in adulthood [6]. The neurological dysfunctions encompass a range of conditions, including seizures, behavioural and psychiatric disorders, dementia, cerebellar ataxia, learning difficulties and intellectual disability, as well as pyramidal and extrapyramidal signs and peripheral neuropathy. These complications can significantly impact morbidity and mortality [7–9]. Although CTX is treatable and complications can be prevented by early diagnosis, diagnosis is often delayed due to the heterogeneity of clinical manifestations, frequently until severe complications develop by the end of the third decade [3].

Therefore, this study aimed to determine the prevalence of CYP27A1 variants in patients with bilateral juvenile cataracts and to evaluate associated clinical features for the early detection of CTX.

Materials and methods

The present study was designed as a retrospective cohort conducted between May 2022 and April 2025 at Başakşehir Çam and Sakura City Hospital, Istanbul, Turkey. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Çam and Sakura City Hospital Ethics Committee (Protocol No: 2024-97) and was carried out in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from the parents or legal guardians of all participating children, and assent was obtained from patients where appropriate.

CTX arises from the accumulation of precursor sterol metabolites due to the dysfunction of the enzyme encoded by the CYP27A1 gene, as previously mentioned. Therefore, in the absence of congenital cataracts, cases of bilateral cataracts in pediatric patients with an undetermined aetiology and an onset between six months and 21 years of age were included in the study. This threshold was selected in order to capture very early-onset cases as well, since CTX–related cataracts have been reported as early as four months of age [10]. While some previous studies in the literature have started ascertainment at two years of age, we opted for an earlier inclusion criterion to avoid missing patients with infantile-onset cataracts of metabolic or genetic origin.The study applied several exclusion criteria to ensure accurate and relevant data collection. Patients with unilateral cataracts, traumatic cataracts, systemic metabolic disorders such as diabetes and galactosemia, and other previously diagnosed hereditary disorders associated with cataract formation, such as Fabry disease, were excluded.Patients with other pre-existing ocular pathologies (e.g. glaucoma, retinal disease), a history of intraocular surgery, or incomplete clinical records were also excluded. Patients who did not consent to genetic testing or did not attend follow-up visits and post-operative care were also excluded to maintain the integrity of the study. Data collection

All patients underwent a comprehensive ophthalmological examination, and detailed demographic data were recorded. Information regarding consanguineous marriage was systematically documented. Infectious causes of cataract were excluded by screening for TORCH (toxoplasmosis, rubella, cytomegalovirus, herpes simplex) and HIV, which were all negative. Patients and their families were provided with detailed counselling on CTX and its implications.

Genetic testing

Peripheral blood samples were obtained from patients whose families provided consent. These genetic tests had been performed as part of routine clinical diagnostic evaluation before data extraction for the present study; no patients were prospectively recruited, and no genetic testing was initiated specifically for this research protocol.The present study retrospectively analyzed patients who had undergone clinically indicated targeted CYP27A1 testing as part of the diagnostic work-up for bilateral juvenile cataract. The next-generation sequencing (NGS) analysis was not a broad cataract-associated gene panel; sequencing was limited to the CYP27A1 gene (NM_000784), including exons 1–9 and exon–intron junctions, and was performed at the MULTIGEN Genetic Diseases Evaluation Centre, Izmir, Turkey. The mean sequencing depth was > 100×, with > 99% of target bases covered at ≥ 20×. Pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants detected by NGS, including the c.398G > A (p.W133*) variant, were confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Variant classification followed the ACMG/AMP 2015 criteria using multiple databases and tools (ClinVar, HGMD, VarSome, Franklin), with database access performed in September 2025. Patients carrying heterozygous variants of uncertain significance (VUS) were reported separately and were not included among genetically confirmed CTX cases, as these variants were not confirmed to be disease-causing.

Identified CYP27A1 variants were summarized in anonymized form and provided as supplementary material.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS for Windows, version 25.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Descriptive statistics were reported as mean ± standard deviation, median, minimum, and maximum values. Categorical variables were presented as numbers and percentages, rounded to one decimal place for consistency. The diagnostic yield of CYP27A1 testing was defined as the proportion of patients with pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants consistent with CTX. Exact 95% confidence intervals for proportions were calculated (e.g., 2/128 = 1.5%, 95% CI: 0.2–5.4%).

Results

A total of 256 eyes from 128 patients were included in the study. The mean age of the participants was observed to be 10.95 ± 9.45 years, with an age range from 6 months to 21 years and a median age of 11.50 years. Additionally, the median age of the five patients with pathogenic variants was found to be 11 years (range 4–17 years).

The study group consisted of 54 males (42.2%) and 74 females (57.8%). The types of cataracts observed in our cohort and the associated clinical findings are summarised in Table 1. Notably, the two siblings with genetically confirmed CTX presented with bilateral cerulean cataracts of similar severity in both eyes. Bilateral cataracts were first documented at approximately 4 years of age, and both patients subsequently underwent cataract surgery. Their ophthalmologic follow-up has been continued by the pediatric ophthalmology unit. At the time of genetic diagnosis, no neurological manifestations, tendon xanthomas, or other systemic signs of CTX were documented in the available clinical records. After diagnosis, both patients were referred to pediatric metabolic disease and medical genetics clinics for systemic evaluation, genetic counselling, treatment planning, and follow-up. Their systemic treatment and monitoring have been managed by the pediatric metabolic disease department. Representative slit-lamp images are shown in Fig. 1.

Targeted CYP27A1 analysis revealed that 123 patients (96.1%) had no detectable CYP27A1 variant, 2 patients (1.5%) carried a homozygous c.398G > A (p.W133*) pathogenic variant, and 3 patients (2.4%) carried a heterozygous c.884 C > T (p.A295V) variant of uncertain significance. Notably, the two patients carrying the homozygous pathogenic CYP27A1 variant were siblings, and the three patients carrying the heterozygous VUS were siblings from a separate family. The CYP27A1 c.398G > A (p.W133*) variant identified in the homozygous state in our two patients had not been previously reported in Human Gene Mutation Database (HGMD) [11]. However, HGMD includes a different nucleotide substitution, c.399G > A, affecting the same codon and resulting in the same premature stop codon, p.W133*, which has previously been associated with CTX. The c.398G > A (p.W133*) variant was classified according to American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG/AMP) criteria using ClinVar, HGMD, VarSome, and Franklin [12–14]. Because this nonsense variant is predicted to introduce a premature termination codon in CYP27A1, consistent with the established loss-of-function mechanism of CTX, it was classified as pathogenic. In our patients, the homozygous c.398G > A (p.W133*) variant was associated with bilateral cerulean cataracts, without other documented ocular pathology or systemic CTX signs in the available records at the time of genetic diagnosis. The remaining 123 patients had no detectable CYP27A1 variant; because testing was limited to targeted CYP27A1 sequencing, no results for other cataract-associated genes were available.Other than cataract, no other ocular pathology was identified in any of the patients carrying CYP27A1 variants. In addition, the heterozygous c.884 C > T (p.A295V) variant identified in three siblings was classified as a VUS. Because CTX is an autosomal recessive disorder requiring biallelic pathogenic or likely pathogenic CYP27A1 variants, these three heterozygous VUS carriers were not considered genetically confirmed CTX cases and were reported separately.

Parental consanguinity was documented in 55 of 128 patients (42.9%). All patients included in the cohort underwent targeted CYP27A1 analysis. The two patients carrying homozygous pathogenic CYP27A1 variants were siblings from a consanguineous family, and the three patients carrying the heterozygous VUS were siblings from a separate consanguineous family. The heterozygous VUS carriers were reported separately and were not considered genetically confirmed CTX cases.

Discussion

Genetic analysis is crucial for the early diagnosis of rare metabolic diseases. The objective of this clinical study was to investigate the utility of targeted CYP27A1 testing in the evaluation of bilateral juvenile cataract cases presenting to our ophthalmology clinic. Accordingly, our findings should not be interpreted as demonstrating systemic treatment benefit, as standardized systemic assessment and long-term follow-up were not available in this retrospective cohort. Rather, the study supports the potential value of targeted CYP27A1 testing in selected patients with bilateral juvenile cataracts to enable referral for systemic evaluation before documented neurological involvement.

In this cohort, genetically confirmed CTX was identified in two patients (1.5%) carrying a homozygous pathogenic CYP27A1 variant, while three patients (2.4%) carried a heterozygous CYP27A1 VUS and were reported separately as carriers. A definitive diagnosis of CTX in individuals with a homozygous pathogenic variant is crucial for timely treatment, which may help prevent progressive neurological degeneration and other irreversible systemic complications. On the other hand, individuals heterozygous for the pathogenic variants are typically asymptomatic carriers, and thus, genetic counselling and family screening are of paramount importance to prevent subsequent generations from being affected [14]. In a multicentre prospective cohort study, Fernández-Eulate G et al. analysed cholestanol levels in patients with bilateral juvenile cataract and performed gene analysis in patients with elevated cholestanol levels [16]. They identified only two heterozygous pathogenic variants, despite the number of patients in their cohort group being similar to that in our study. Although parental consanguinity was present in the family of the two genetically confirmed CTX patients, the diagnostic yield in our cohort was comparable to previous reports; therefore, consanguinity should be interpreted as an epidemiological context rather than as an explanation for a higher prevalence [15]. This increases the likelihood of autosomal recessive pathogenic variant transmission. Additionally, the lack of routine pathogenic variant analysis in the aforementioned study may have contributed to the observed differences [16].

CTX has the potential to cause significant complications, including those affecting the musculoskeletal, cardiovascular and neurological systems [17]. The impact of these complications can be mitigated by early diagnosis and appropriate treatment. When diagnosed at an early stage, treatment with oral chenodeoxycholic acid, which has been the standard of care for CTX for the last 40 years, has the potential to halt disease progression and reduce its impact [18]. Accordingly, patients with genetically confirmed CTX were referred to pediatric metabolic disease specialists for systemic evaluation, oral chenodeoxycholic acid treatment, and treatment monitoring, and to medical genetics specialists for genetic counselling and family screening.

The diagnostic value of CYP27A1 pathogenic variants in strengthening the diagnosis of rare diseases such as CTX is significant, and their role in the long-term management of patients is a crucial aspect of patient care. Although genetic testing has some limitations in terms of accessibility and cost, this study demonstrates the diagnostic value in confirming a CTX diagnosis in patients with bilateral juvenile cataracts without systemic symptoms [19]. These benefits may be realized in other heritable conditions, thus supporting the broader application of genetic testing in ophthalmology.The cataracts observed in patients carrying a heterozygous VUS were not attributed to CTX, as no second pathogenic or likely pathogenic CYP27A1 variant was identified. Accordingly, the prevalence of genetically confirmed CTX in this cohort was calculated only on the basis of the two siblings carrying homozygous pathogenic variants.

In the overall cohort, cerulean cataract accounted for 64 of 256 eyes (25.0%). Among the genetically confirmed CTX cases, both siblings carrying homozygous pathogenic CYP27A1 variants had bilateral cerulean cataracts of similar severity, whereas the heterozygous VUS carriers were not considered CTX cases. However, because only one affected family was identified, this finding should not be interpreted as defining a cataract morphology specific to CTX [20]. Rather, it supports considering CTX in the differential diagnosis of bilateral juvenile cataracts, particularly when cerulean or fleck-like lens opacities are present [21].

In previous large-scale studies of individuals with bilateral juvenile-onset cataract, the prevalence of CTX was reported as 1.8% in the USA [22] and 1.55% in Turkey [23]. In our cohort, genetically confirmed CTX was identified in 2 of 128 patients (1.5%), which is comparable to both the USA and Turkish cohorts. Although parental consanguinity was present in the family of the two siblings carrying homozygous pathogenic CYP27A1 variants, the similar diagnostic yield to previous studies and the clustering of affected cases within a single family preclude any conclusion that consanguinity increased the overall proportion of CYP27A1-related cataracts in this cohort. Therefore, consanguinity should be interpreted as an epidemiological context for autosomal recessive inheritance rather than as an explanation for a higher diagnostic rate. The similarity of these rates suggests that our results are consistent with international and regional data. Nevertheless, it should be noted that the small sample size and the presence of sibling clustering among positive cases may have influenced the observed prevalence. The generally low prevalence of CTX in bilateral juvenile cataract cohorts may also reflect the long delay that often occurs before a definitive diagnosis is reached. Future comprehensive studies with larger patient populations and the inclusion of additional biomarkers could provide more accurate prevalence estimates and improve the diagnosis and management of CTX and other rare metabolic disorders [21].

One strength of this study is the systematic application of targeted CYP27A1 sequencing, rather than a broader cataract-associated gene panel, across the entire cohort of patients with bilateral juvenile cataracts.The identification of a novel homozygous pathogenic variant (c.398G > A, p.W133*) not previously reported in the HGMD, together with its confirmation in siblings, adds valuable new knowledge to the variant spectrum of CTX. Furthermore, the consistent use of internationally recognised tools and databases (ClinVar, VarSome, Franklin, ACMG criteria) strengthened the reliability of variant classification. The presence of parental consanguinity in the family of the two siblings with homozygous pathogenic CYP27A1 variants underscores the relevance of consanguinity as an epidemiological risk factor for autosomal recessive disorders in this population.The comprehensive dataset, which is characterised by its clear documentation of demographic characteristics, cataract morphology, and associated clinical features, has two primary benefits. Firstly, it supports early diagnosis of CTX in ophthalmology clinics. Secondly, it contributes to the broader understanding of genotype–phenotype correlations.

Our study has certain limitations. First, the sample size was relatively small, which may limit the generalizability of our findings and widen the confidence intervals around the estimated diagnostic yield. Second, no biochemical confirmation (such as plasma cholestanol levels or bile alcohol analysis) was available, which would have strengthened the correlation between genetic results and metabolic phenotype. Third, segregation analysis in family members was not performed. Segregation testing, in which parents and siblings are evaluated for the presence of the identified variants, would have provided stronger evidence for the pathogenicity of the homozygous variant and supported the interpretation of the heterozygous VUS cases. However, because this study was conducted retrospectively, family members were not systematically tested, and complete segregation data could not be obtained. Fourth, the study design was single-center and retrospective, which may have introduced selection bias. Finally, the lack of long-term clinical follow-up limited our ability to assess disease progression, treatment response, and the natural history of CTX in these patients. Despite these limitations, the clear clustering of variants within sibling groups and the consistent clinical features observed support the validity of our results. Future prospective, multi-center studies incorporating biochemical assays, family-based genetic testing, and long-term follow-up are warranted to confirm and expand upon our findings.

In conclusion, this study supports the clinical value of targeted CYP27A1 analysis in selected patients with bilateral juvenile cataracts. Genetically confirmed CTX was identified in 2 of 128 patients (1.5%), a rate comparable to previous reports. Although our findings do not demonstrate systemic treatment benefit or long-term clinical outcomes, early genetic identification may facilitate timely referral for systemic evaluation, genetic counselling, and appropriate metabolic management.
