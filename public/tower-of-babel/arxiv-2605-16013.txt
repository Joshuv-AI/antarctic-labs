Amenability and comparison for étale groupoids with polynomial growth
Are Austad, Christian Bönicke

Abstract
. We show that any second-countable étale groupoid with polynomial growth is topologically amenable. If its unit space is compact and metrizable, we show that the groupoid has weak m m -comparison. Thus if the groupoid is also ample and minimal, it satisfies Matui’s AH conjecture.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 22A22; Secondary: 37B05, 43A07

1.
Introduction

The growth rate of a finitely generated group, that is the asymptotic behaviour of the function counting the number of elements of length at most
n
(relative to a finite symmetric generating set), has long been a central object of interest in geometric group theory.
A landmark theorem by Gromov
[
11
]
tells us that a finitely generated group has polynomial growth if and only if it admits a nilpotent subgroup of finite index.
Thus, groups of polynomial growth form a particularly well-behaved class of amenable groups.

The notion of a length function generalises from discrete groups to étale groupoids in a straightforward manner (cf. Definition
2.4
), allowing us to consider the growth rate of an étale groupoid
G
with respect to a length function
\ell\colon G\to[0,\infty)
.
With the growing number of applications of topological groupoids in dynamics, non-commutative geometry, operator algebras, and group theory (via the construction of the associated topological full groups)
it seems natural to investigate whether imposing a constraint on the growth rate has any structural consequences for the groupoid. While there is no analogue of Gromov’s theorem available for groupoids, one might still hope to use the machinery of growth rates to produce strong results.

This idea was first executed successfully by Nekrashevych, who used it to construct simple finitely generated algebras of arbitrary Gelfand-Kirillov dimension
\geq 2
and simple finitely generated algebras of quadratic growth over arbitrary fields
[
19
]
.
More recently,
[
13
]
and
[
6
]
showed that certain operator algebras associated with étale groupoids with polynomial growth admit spectrally invariant subalgebras of rapidly decaying functions.

Passing to the world of topological groupoids, the notion of amenability splits into two separate concepts that are independent of each other: topological amenability and fibrewise amenablity.
Topological amenability
is very natural from a
C^{*}
-algebraic perspective, as it turns out to be equivalent to nuclearity of the associated reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra
[
2
]
.
Fibrewise amenability on the other hand
is based on the existence of Følner sequences in the range fibres of the groupoid (see
[
15
,
16
]
for a more detailed discussion of this concept). While both concepts agree for discrete groups, in the realm of étale groupoids there is no implication between the two in general. A groupoid can be topologically amenable but not fibrewise amenable, and vice versa.

The study of topological amenability for groupoids has received a lot of attention, and as such
there are many examples of topologically amenable étale groupoids in the literature, e.g. AF groupoids, graph groupoids, Deaconu-Renault groupoids
[
23
]
, transformation groupoids arising from amenable actions, and coarse groupoids arising from spaces with property (A)
[
24
]
, though this is not an exhaustive list.

Note that unlike for groups,
the weak containment property does not imply topological amenability. That is, one can construct an étale groupoid
G
which is not topologically amenable, yet satisfies
C^{*}(G)\cong C^{*}_{r}(G)
.
The first
such
example
was constructed by Willett in
[
25
]
, with subsequent principal examples exhibited by Alekseev and Finn-Sell in
[
1
]
. The relationship between topological amenability and the weak containment property is further explored in detail in for example
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
.

A standard proof that groups with polynomial growth are necessarily amenable relies on the fact that the balls of radius
n
are Følner sets for the group. Thus, it is not surprising that an adaption of this proof to the setting of groupoids shows that an étale groupoid with polynomial growth is fibrewise amenable in the sense of
[
15
]
, see
[
7
, Proposition 3.2]
. Our first main result tells us that polynomial growth in fact also implies topological amenability.

Theorem A

(cf. Theorem
3.5
)
.

Let
G
be a second-countable locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid, and suppose
G
has polynomial growth. Then
G
must be topologically amenable.

The lack of homogeneity in the fibrewise Cayley graph of a groupoid provides a key obstacle to proving that polynomial growth implies topological amenability.
In order to overcome this obstacle, we view an étale groupoid
G
as a Borel groupoid, realized as an extension of
G/{\rm Iso}(G)
by
{\rm Iso}(G)
, and analyze these two cases separately. Here
{\rm Iso}(G)
is the isotropy subgroupoid of
G
.
Using recently constructed spectrally invariant subalgebras of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
from
[
7
]
, we show that any group bundle with polynomial growth is topologically amenable.
We then relate the structure of
G/{\rm Iso}(G)
to an equivalence relation on the orbital graph of
G
. By invoking state-of-the-art results concerning hyperfinite equivalence relations from
[
8
]
we are able to deduce Borel amenability of
G/{\rm Iso}(G)
, which suffices for our purposes.

When an étale groupoid
G
has polynomial growth with respect to a length function
\ell
, the sets
B(n)=\{g\in G\mid\ell(g)\leq n\}
exhibit behaviour reminiscent of (fibrewise) Følner sets. This fact can be used to define variants of Banach densities, which allow us to produce an abundance of
G
-invariant measures on
G^{(0)}
. In light of this it becomes natural to wonder whether étale groupoids with polynomial growth satisfy weak
m
-comparison.
We postpone the rather technical definition of weak
m
-comparison to Definition
4.2
.
Philosophically an étale groupoid
G
having weak
m
-comparison implies the highly desirable property that the invariant positive regular Borel probability measures on the unit space
G^{(0)}
determine the dynamics of
G\curvearrowright G^{(0)}
.

In
[
9
]
, Downarowicz and Zhang showed that all actions of groups with locally subexponential growth on zero-dimensional compact spaces have comparison. Even more recently, Naryshkin
[
18
]
showed that actions of finitely generated groups of polynomial growth on compact metrizable spaces have weak
m
-comparison. Our second main result extends
[
18
]
to compactly generated étale groupoids of polynomial growth.

Theorem B

(cf. Theorem
4.7
)
.

Suppose
G
is a compactly generated étale groupoid with compact and metrizable unit space.
If
G
has polynomial growth,
then
there exists an
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
G
has weak
m
-comparison.

While the proof of Theorem
B
attempts to follow the philosophy of
[
18
]
, we will
find the lack of homogeneity in the fibrewise Cayley graph to be an obstacle, and a great deal of additional care is needed to circumvent this.
We note that
one of the most useful consequences of the homogeneity of the Cayley graph of a group is that polynomial growth automatically implies the formally much stronger property of bounded doubling.
There is no reason to believe that this still holds for groupoids. One still has bounded doubling at arbitrarily large scales though, which turns out to be enough for our purposes.

Matui’s AH-conjecture
[
17
, Conjecture 2.9]
proposes a relationship between the first two homology groups of a minimal ample groupoid and the abelianisation of its topological full group. There are currently no known counterexamples.
Li showed that one should interpret the AH-conjecture as the degree one component of a much deeper relation between homological invariants of topological full groups and the homology of ample groupoids, and verified this relationship (and in particular the AH-conjecture) for minimal ample groupoids with comparison
[
14
]
.
By adapting results from
[
5
]
and combining them with
Theorem
B
, we deduce that compactly generated minimal ample groupoids with polynomial growth fall within the purview of the main results in
[
14
]
. In particular, we obtain the following result.

Theorem C

(cf. Corollary
4.10
)
.

Let
G
be a compactly generated, locally compact, Hausdorff,
minimal, ample groupoid whose unit space has no isolated points.
If
G
has polynomial growth, then
G
satisfies Matui’s AH-conjecture.

2.
Étale groupoids and amenability

We begin by recalling basic terminology about groupoids, amenability, and growth. We refer the reader to
[
4
]
and
[
22
]
for the basic notions on groupoids and amenability.

Let
G
be a groupoid with unit space
G^{(0)}
, and denote by
G^{(2)}
its composable elements. The range and source maps
r,s\colon G\to G^{(0)}
will be denoted
r
and
s
, respectively.
We denote the fibers of the range and source maps by
G^{x}=r^{-1}(x)
and
G_{x}=s^{-1}(x)
for
x\in G^{(0)}
.
Then the isotropy group at
x\in G^{(0)}
is
G_{x}^{x}:=G^{x}\cap G_{x}
, and the isotropy bundle is given by
\mathrm{Iso}(G):=\coprod_{x\in G^{(0)}}G_{x}^{x}
.
The
isotropy bundle
is a subgroupoid of
G
in a natural way,
and if

\mathrm{Iso}(G)=G^{(0)}
we say that
G
is
principal
.
Furthermore, for subsets
A,B\subseteq G
, we denote the set of all possible products of elements in
A
and elements in
B
by
AB=\{\gamma\mu\in G\mid\gamma\in A,\mu\in B,(\gamma,\mu)\in G^{(2)}\}
.
A subset
F\subseteq G^{(0)}
is
invariant
if
s(\gamma)\in F
if and only if
r(\gamma)\in F
. For such a set, we let
G(F)
denote the subgroupoid of
G
given by
r^{-1}(F)\cap s^{-1}(F)
.

The groupoid
G
is said to be
Borel
if it is equipped with a Borel structure such that the product, inverse, range, and source maps are Borel. Here we equip
G^{(2)}
with the Borel structure inherited from
G\times G
, and
G^{(0)}
with the Borel structure induced by
G
.
We say
G
is a
topological
groupoid if it is equipped with a topology such that the product, inverse, range and source maps are continuous. Again, we equip
G^{(2)}
with the relative topology from
G\times G
, and
G^{(0)}
with the topology induced by
G
. Any topological groupoid we consider in this article will be assumed to be locally compact and Hausdorff. Moreover, we note that any topological groupoid is a Borel groupoid with the Borel structure naturally induced by the topology.

A morphism between Borel groupoids
G
and
H
is a Borel map
\phi\colon G\to H
such that
\phi(G^{(2)})\subseteq H^{(2)}
,
\phi(\gamma\eta)=\phi(\gamma)\phi(\eta)
for all
(\gamma,\eta)\in G^{(2)}
, and
\phi(\gamma^{-1})=\phi(\gamma)^{-1}
for all
\gamma\in G
. We say
\phi
is
strongly surjective
if
\phi|_{G^{(0)}}\colon G^{(0)}\to H^{(0)}
is surjective and for every
x\in G^{(0)}
we have
\phi(G^{x})=H^{\phi(x)}
.

A locally compact Hausdorff groupoid
G
is said to be étale if the range map
r
(equivalently, the source map
s
) is a local homeomorphism onto
G^{(0)}
. In particular,
G^{x}
and
G_{x}
are discrete with respect to the subspace topology. When
G
is étale, there is a canonical Haar system on
G
given by the counting measure on
G^{x}
for all
x\in G^{(0)}
.

For
G
étale, we consider the
*
-algebra
C_{c}(G)
of continuous functions of compact support. It is equipped with multiplication and involution given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle f*g(\gamma)=\sum_{\mu\in G_{s(\gamma)}}f(\gamma\mu^{-1})g(\mu),\quad\text{ and }\quad f^{*}(\gamma)=\overline{f(\gamma^{-1})},

for
f,g\in C_{c}(G)
and
\gamma\in G
. We will want to consider completions of this algebra. First, the
I
-norm
on
C_{c}(G)
is given by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{I}:=\max\left\{\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\sum_{\gamma\in G_{x}}|f(\gamma)|,\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\sum_{\gamma\in G^{x}}|f(\gamma)|\right\}

for
f\in C_{c}(G)
. This is a norm on
C_{c}(G)
, and the associated completion is a Banach
*
-algebra denoted by
I(G)
. The multiplication and involution are given by the natural extensions of (
2.1
).
For each
x\in G^{(0)}
we consider the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(G_{x})
.
The convolution formula from (
2.1
) extends to a representation of
I(G)
on
\ell^{2}(G_{x})
, the
left regular representation at
x\in G^{(0)}
, given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}(f)(\xi)(\gamma)=\sum_{\mu\in G_{x}}f(\gamma\mu^{-1})\xi(\mu)

for
\gamma\in G_{x}
,
f\in I(G)
and
\xi\in\ell^{2}(G_{x})
. The
reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra of

G
, denoted by
C^{*}_{r}(G)
, is the completion of
I(G)
in the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{*}_{r}(G)}:=\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\|\lambda_{x}(f)\|_{B(\ell^{2}(G_{x}))}\quad\text{ for $f\in I(G)$.}

Lastly, there is also the
full groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra of

G
. It is the enveloping
C^{*}
-algebra of
C_{c}(G)
and
I(G)
, and may be obtained as the completion in the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{*}(G)}:=\sup\{\|\pi(f)\|_{B(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}\mid\pi\colon C_{c}(G)\to B(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})\text{ is a $*$-representation}\}

for
f\in C_{c}(G)
.
If the identity map on
C_{c}(G)
extends to an isomorphism
C^{*}(G)\cong C^{*}_{r}(G)
, we say that
G
has the
weak containment property
.

The proof of the first main result, Theorem
3.5
, will involve various notions of amenability of étale groupoids, and we therefore proceed to discuss this concept. Important for our proof in Section
3
is the fact that Borel amenability and topological amenability are equivalent for second-countable locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoids by
[
20
, Corollary 2.15]
. For the benefit of the reader we recall both definitions, cf.
[
20
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid. We say
G
is
topologically amenable
if there
exists a
topological approximate invariant density
, that is, a sequence
(g_{n})_{n}
of non-negative continuous functions on
G
such that

(1)

\sum_{\gamma\in G^{x}}g_{n}(\gamma)\leq 1
for all
x\in G^{(0)}
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(2)

\sum_{\gamma\in G^{x}}g_{n}(\gamma)\to 1
uniformly on every compact subset of
G^{(0)}
,

(3)

\sum_{\gamma\in G^{r(\mu)}}|g_{n}(\mu^{-1}\gamma)-g_{n}(\gamma)|\to 0
uniformly on every compact subset of
G
.

We say
G
is
Borel amenable
if there is a
Borel approximate invariant density
, that is, a sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of non-negative Borel functions on
G
such that

(1)

\sum_{\gamma\in G^{x}}g_{n}(\gamma)\leq 1
for all
x\in G^{(0)}
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{\gamma\in G^{x}}g_{n}(\gamma)=1
for all
x\in G^{(0)}
,

(3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{\gamma\in G^{r(\mu)}}|g_{n}(\mu^{-1}\gamma)-g_{n}(\gamma)|=0
for all
\mu\in G
.

Lemma
2.3
below will be important for the proof of Theorem
3.5
. While its proof follows by combining existing results in the literature, we include the following definition for the benefit of the reader.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose
G
is a Borel groupoid with Haar system
\lambda
given by the counting measure. A measure
\mu
on
G^{(0)}
is said to be
quasi-invariant with respect to
(G,\lambda)
if the measure
\mu\circ\lambda
on
G
is equivalent to its inverse
\mu\circ\lambda^{-1}
.
The triple
(G,\lambda,\mu)
is called a
measured groupoid
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a second-countable étale groupoid, and let
\lambda
be the counting measure. Moreover, let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two Borel groupoids equipped with Haar measures
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
given by the respective counting measures. Suppose
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

is a short exact sequence of Borel groupoids, where we furthermore assume that
p
is strongly surjective. If
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are Borel amenable, then
G
is topologically amenable.

Proof.

Fix any quasi-invariant measure
\mu
on
G^{(0)}
for
(G,\lambda)
.
Denote by
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
the measures induced on
G_{1}^{(0)}
and
G_{2}^{(0)}
through
\iota
and
p
respectively. By
[
2
, Proposition 5.3.10]
it follows that
\mu_{i}
is quasi-invariant for
(G_{i},\lambda_{i})
for
i=1,2
.
Since
G_{i}
is Borel amenable, it follows by
[
2
, Proposition 3.3.2]
that for
i=1,2

(G_{i},\lambda_{i},\mu_{i})
is an amenable measured groupoid in the sense of
[
2
, Definition 3.2.8]
.
Then
[
2
, Theorem 5.3.14]
implies that
(G,\lambda,\mu)
is an amenable measured groupoid. Since
\mu
was an arbitrary quasi-invariant measure,
[
2
, Theorem 3.3.7]
yields that
G
is topologically amenable.
∎

We proceed to discuss length functions and growth of étale groupoids. The following definition is taken from
[
15
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
G
be an étale groupoid. We say that a function
\ell\colon G\to[0,\infty)
is a
length function on
G
if

(1)

\ell(\gamma)=0
for all
\gamma\in G^{(0)}
,

(2)

\ell(\gamma^{-1})=\ell(\gamma)
for all
\gamma\in G
, and

(3)

\ell(\gamma\mu)\leq\ell(\gamma)+\ell(\mu)
for all
(\gamma,\mu)\in G^{(2)}
.

We further say that
\ell
is
controlled
if
\sup_{\gamma\in K}\ell(\gamma)<\infty
for all precompact sets
K\subseteq G
, and we say that
\ell
is
proper
if whenever
K\subseteq G\setminus G^{0}
satisfies
\sup_{\gamma\in K}\ell(\gamma)<\infty
, then
K
is precompact. We say that
\ell
is a
coarse
length function if it is both controlled and proper.

In
[
15
, Theorem 4.10]
the authors show that every
\sigma
-compact locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid admits a coarse continuous length function.

Definition 2.5
.

We say that a function
f\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
has
polynomial growth
if there is a real-valued polynomial
p
for which
f(t)\leq p(t)
for all
t\in[0,\infty)
. We then write
f\preceq p
. The
order of polynomial growth
for
f
is

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}_{f}:=\inf\{d\mid f\preceq p\text{ where $p$ is a polynomial of degree $d$}\}.

Given a coarse length function
\ell:G\to[0,\infty)
on an étale groupoid, there is an associated
growth function

\gamma_{\ell}\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
given by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}|B_{\ell}(t)x|,

where
B_{\ell}(t)\coloneqq\{\gamma\in G\mid\ell(\gamma)\leq t\}
. Note that this is well-defined: properness of
\ell
implies that
B_{\ell}(t)
is precompact and since the system of counting measures forms a continuous Haar system on
G
,
[
26
, Exercise 1.3.7]
implies that the supremum in line (
2.3
) is finite.

Definition 2.6
.

An étale locally compact Hausdorff groupoid
G
is said to have
polynomial growth
if it admits a coarse length function
\ell
such that the associated growth function
\gamma_{\ell}
satisfies
\gamma_{\ell}\preceq p
for some polynomial
p
.
The
order of polynomial growth
for
G
is then

\mathrm{ord}(G):=\inf\mathrm{ord}_{\gamma_{\ell}},

where the infimum is taken over all coarse length functions
\ell
for which
\gamma_{\ell}
has polynomial growth.

Note that if
H\subseteq G
is a subgroupoid and
G
has polynomial growth, then
H
has polynomial growth as well: just take a length function with polynomial growth for
G
and restrict it to
H
.

A distinguished class of length functions which we will be particularly interested in come from generating sets for the groupoid
G
. Given a symmetric set
K=K^{-1}
which generates
G
in the sense that
G=\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K^{n}
we obtain a length function
\ell_{K}
on
G
by the formula

(2.4)

\displaystyle\ell_{K}(\gamma)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $\gamma\in G^{(0)}$}\\
\min\{n\mid\gamma\in K^{n}\}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

for
\gamma\in G
. It is not hard to see that if we assume additionally that
K
is open and precompact, then
\ell_{K}
is a coarse length function.
If
G
is generated by a compact set, we say
G
is
compactly generated
.

We note the following result, which is well known for discrete groups. The proof in the étale groupoid setting is analogous to that for discrete groups, but to our knowledge it has not been noted in the literature before and so we include it for the benefit of the reader.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G
be a compactly generated, locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid.
Then there exists an open, precompact set
L\subseteq G
which generates
G
. Moreover, if
G
has polynomial growth, then
\gamma_{\ell_{L}}
has polynomial growth as well, where
\ell_{L}
is given by (
2.4
).

Proof.

Let
K
be a compact generating set for
G
. We may by local compactness find an open precompact set
M
with
K\subseteq M
. Setting
L=M\cup M^{-1}
we obtain a symmetric, open, precompact set, which still generates
G
since
K\subseteq L
.

Now assume that
G
has polynomial growth. Then there exists a coarse length function
\ell
such that
\gamma_{\ell}
has polynomial growth.
We set

\displaystyle M_{L}:=\sup_{k\in L}\ell(k),

which is finite as
\ell
is assumed to be controlled.
Note that for all

k\in L

we then have

\displaystyle\ell(k)\leq M_{L}=M_{L}\ell_{L}(k).

Suppose now
\gamma\in G
can be written
\gamma=k_{1}\cdots k_{n}
,
k_{i}\in L\setminus G^{(0)}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, and suppose this decomposition is minimal, that is,
\ell_{L}(\gamma)=n
. Then

\displaystyle\ell(\gamma)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell(k_{i})\leq M_{L}\cdot n=M_{L}\ell_{L}(\gamma).

Thus
\ell\leq M_{L}\ell_{L}
. We then conclude that

\displaystyle B_{\ell_{L}}(r)\subseteq B_{\ell}(M_{L}\cdot r)

for all
r\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Thus if
\gamma_{\ell}
has polynomial growth, so does
\gamma_{\ell_{L}}
.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

Note that the proof of Lemma
2.7
shows that if
K
and
K^{\prime}
are open precompact generating sets for
G
, then
\mathrm{ord}_{\gamma_{\ell_{K}}}=\mathrm{ord}_{\gamma_{\ell_{K^{\prime}}}}
. Moreover,
\mathrm{ord}(G)
is achieved by
\mathrm{ord}_{\gamma_{\ell_{K}}}
for any open precompact generating set
K
.

Remark 2.9
.

There are étale groupoids which do not admit compact generating sets for which there exist coarse length functions
\ell
for which
\gamma_{\ell}
has polynomial growth. As an example, many AF groupoids are not compactly generated, but any AF groupoid
G
can be equipped with a continuous coarse length function
\ell
for which
\mathrm{ord}_{\gamma_{\ell}}\leq 1
,
see
[
6
, Proposition 3.17]
. As such,
\mathrm{ord}(G)\leq 1
for any AF groupoid
G
.

3.
Polynomial growth implies amenabilty

The main result of this section is Theorem
3.5
, showing that any second-countable locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid
G
with polynomial growth must be topologically amenable. Key to the proof of this result will be to understand (Borel) amenability of
\mathrm{Iso}(G)
and
G/\mathrm{Iso}(G)
when
G
has polynomial growth, and then deduce topological amenability of
G
using Lemma
2.3
. As such, we begin this section by studying topological amenability of group bundles.

We say
G
is
inner exact
if for any closed and invariant subset of
G^{(0)}
the sequence

\displaystyle 0\to C^{*}_{r}(G(G^{(0)}\setminus F))\to C^{*}_{r}(G)\to C^{*}_{r}(G(F))\to 0

is exact. The analogous sequence using full groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras is always exact
[
12
, pg. 337]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
G
is an étale groupoid with polynomial growth. Then
G
has the weak containment property and is inner exact.

Before proving Proposition
3.1
, we need to recall an important construction from the literature.
Since we are working in the context of not necessarily continuous length functions, we will appeal to constructions from
[
7
]

Proof of Proposition

.

, that is,

is the

-envelope of

, see for example
[

, Proposition 2.7]
.
From the above observations we see that

. Moreover, from the continuous dense inclusions

\displaystyle S(G,\ell)\hookrightarrow I(G)\hookrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(G),

given by the extensions of the identity maps on

,
we may pass to the enveloping

-algebras to obtain continuous surjective

\displaystyle C^{*}(S(G,\ell))\to C^{*}(I(G))\to C^{*}_{r}(G).

This reduces to

\displaystyle C^{*}_{r}(G)\to C^{*}(G)\to C^{*}_{r}(G)

It remains to show that

. We equip the subgroupoids

\displaystyle 0\to C^{*}_{r}(G(U))\to C^{*}_{r}(G)\to C^{*}_{r}(G(F))\to 0

\displaystyle 0\to C^{*}(G(U))\to C^{*}(G)\to C^{*}(G(F))\to 0

We also obtain the following immediate corollary of Proposition

Corollary 3.2
.

If

is a group bundle étale groupoid which has polynomial growth, then

Proof.

By

, topological amenability of a group bundle étale groupoid is equivalent to being inner exact and having the weak containment property. The result therefore follows by Proposition

.
∎

The following lemma allows us to focus on
groupoids generated by precompact sets in the sequel.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be an étale groupoid. Then
G
is topologically amenable if and only if the subgroupoid
\langle L\rangle\subseteq G
generated by
L
is topologically amenable for every open, precompact subset
L\subseteq G
.

Proof.

Since topological amenability passes to open subgroupoids by
[
2
, Proposition 5.1.1]
, the forward direction is immediate.

For the converse let
K\subseteq G
compact and
\varepsilon>0
be given. Using local compactness of
G
we can find an open precompact set
K\subseteq L\subseteq G
.
By assumption,
H:=\langle L\rangle
is amenable
and hence there exists a compactly supported continuous function
f:H\rightarrow[0,1]

such that for all
\gamma\in K
we have

\left|\sum_{\mu\in H_{r(\gamma)}}f(\mu)-1\right|<\varepsilon\hbox{ and }\sum_{\mu\in H_{r(\gamma)}}|f(\mu)-f(\mu\gamma)|<\varepsilon.

As
H
is open in
G
, we can extend
f
by zero outside of
H
and view
f
as a compactly supported function
f:G\to[0,1]
. Our extended function retains the desired properties since for all
\gamma\in K
we have

\left|\sum_{\mu\in G_{r(\gamma)}}f(\mu)-1\right|=\left|\sum_{\mu\in H_{r(\gamma)}}f(\mu)-1\right|<\varepsilon,\hbox{ and }

\sum_{\mu\in G_{r(\gamma)}}|f(\mu)-f(\mu\gamma)|=\sum_{\mu\in H_{r(\gamma)}}|f(\mu)-f(\mu\gamma)|<\varepsilon.

∎

Note that if
L\subseteq G
is open and precompact, then
K:=L\cup L^{-1}
is open, symmetric and precompact, and
\langle L\rangle=\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K^{n}
.
If
G
has polynomial growth, then
\langle L\rangle
has polynomial growth, and so the growth function associated with
\ell_{K}:\langle L\rangle\to[0,\infty)
has polynomial growth by Lemma
2.7
.
In light of Lemma
3.3
we will therefore in the sequel restrict to second-countable locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoids admitting an open symmetric precompact generating set.
The length function will be the the associated word-length function from (
2.4
).

Now fix a compactly generated second-countable locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid
G
with
open, symmetric precompact
generating set
K
and associated word-length function
\ell=\ell_{K}
.
Associated with this data are two canonical graphs:

(1)

the Cayley graph
\mathrm{Cay}(G,K)
with vertex set
G
and edges of the form
(\gamma,\gamma k)
for
k\in K\setminus G^{(0)}
, and

(2)

the orbital graph
\mathrm{Orb}(G,K)
with vertex set
G^{(0)}
and edges
\{r(k),s(k)\}
for
k\in K
such that
s(k)\neq r(k)
.

Note that
G
acts from the left on
\mathrm{Cay}(G,K)
by multiplication in
G
.
The source map
\gamma\mapsto s(\gamma)
induces a morphism of graphs

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cay}(G,K)\to\mathrm{Orb}(G,K).

We view both
\mathrm{Cay}(G,K)
and
\mathrm{Orb}(G,K)
as metric spaces equipped with the graph metric.
The ball of radius
n
centered at
\gamma\in G
in
\mathrm{Cay}(G,K)
will be denoted by
B_{n}^{\mathrm{Cay}}(\gamma)
, and similarly the ball of radius
n
centered at
x\in G^{(0)}
in
\mathrm{Orb}(G,K)
will be denoted by
B_{n}^{\mathrm{Orb}}(x)
.
We say the graph
\mathrm{Orb}(G,K)
has polynomial growth if the function
[n\mapsto\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\left|B^{\mathrm{Orb}}_{n}(x)\right|]
is dominated by a polynomial as in Definition
2.5
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For each
x\in G^{(0)}
, the source map restricts to a surjection

B_{n}^{\mathrm{Cay}}(x)\to B_{n}^{\mathrm{Orb}}(x).

In particular, we have

|B_{n}^{\mathrm{Orb}}(x)|\leq|B_{n}^{\mathrm{Cay}}(x)|
.

Moreover, if
G
admits a coarse length function
\ell
such that
\gamma_{\ell}
has polynomial growth,
then
\mathrm{Orb}(G,K)
has polynomial growth
for any choice of open precompact generating set
K\subseteq G
.

Proof.

Let us first show that the restriction of the source map to
B_{n}^{\mathrm{Cay}}(x)
has image in
B_{n}^{\mathrm{Orb}}(x)
. So let
\mu\in B_{n}^{\mathrm{Cay}}(x)
. Then we can write
\mu=k_{1}\cdots k_{m}
for some
m\leq n
, where
k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}\in K
.
But then

x=r(\mu)=r(k_{1})\leftarrow s(k_{1})=r(k_{2})\leftarrow\ldots\leftarrow r(k_{m})\leftarrow s(k_{m})=s(\mu)

is a path of length at most
m
in
\mathrm{Orb}(G,K)
. It follows that
d(x,s(\mu))\leq m\leq n
, where
d
is the graph metric on
\mathrm{Orb}(G,K)
.

Next, let us show surjectivity: let
y\in B_{n}^{\mathrm{Orb}}(x)
be given. Then there exists
m\leq n
and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}\in K
such that

y=s(k_{1})
,
r(k_{m})=x
and
r(k_{i})=s(k_{i+1})
for all
1\leq i\leq m-1
.
But then
\gamma:=k_{m}\cdots k_{1}\in K^{m}
satisfies
\ell_{K}(\gamma)\leq m\leq n
and
r(\gamma)=r(k_{m})=x
. It follows that
\gamma\in B_{n}^{\mathrm{Cay}}(x)
such that
s(\gamma)=s(k_{1})=y
.

The other statement follows immediately from this.
∎

We now state and prove the first main theorem of the article.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
G
be a second-countable locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid, and suppose
G
has polynomial growth. Then
G
must be topologically amenable.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.7
and Lemma
3.3
it suffices to show the statement under the assumption that
G
is
generated by an open precompact symmetric set
K
such that the growth function associated with
\ell_{K}
has polynomial growth.

By assumption,
G
is a standard Borel space.
Now note that the vertex set
G^{(0)}
of the orbital graph
\mathrm{Orb}(G,K)
is second-countable, locally compact and Hausdorff, that is, it has the structure of a standard Borel space. Moreover, the adjacency relation can be written

\displaystyle E(\mathrm{Orb}(G,K))=\bigcup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\{(r(K\cap s^{-1}(\{x\}),x)\}.

Note that since
G
is second-countable, locally compact and Hausdorff, precompact sets are Borel.
Thus
r(K\cap s^{-1}(\cdot))
is a composition of Borel operations. It follows that
E(\mathrm{Orb}(G,K))
is a Borel subset of
G^{(0)}\times G^{(0)}
, and so
\mathrm{Orb}(G,K)
is a Borel graph in the sense of
[
8
, Definition 1.5]
.

The orbital graph
\mathrm{Orb}(G,K)
has polynomial growth by Lemma
3.4
.
Then, by
[
8
, Corollary 1.16]
, the equivalence relation associated to
\mathrm{Orb}(G,K)
which identifies elements in the same connected component of the graph is hyperfinite.
Note that since
K
is a generating set for
G
, this equivalence relation coincides with the orbit equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}_{G}=\{(s(\gamma),r(\gamma))\mid\gamma\in G\}
of
G
.
Using
[
10
, pg. 2]
we conclude that
\mathcal{R}_{G}
is Borel amenable.
Next, note that the map
\gamma\mapsto(s(\gamma),r(\gamma))
induces a Borel bijection

G/\mathrm{Iso}(G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{R}_{G}

which is then automatically an isomorphism of Borel groupoids by Suslin’s theorem. It follows that
G/\mathrm{Iso}(G)
is Borel amenable.

By Corollary
3.2
we then have an extension
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Iso}(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G/\mathrm{Iso}(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

of Borel groupoids where
G
is étale, and
\mathrm{Iso}(G)
and
G/\mathrm{Iso}(G)
are Borel amenable. It then follows by Lemma
2.3
that
G
is topologically amenable.

∎

4.
Polynomial growth implies weak
m
-comparison

In this section we prove the second main result of the article, namely Theorem
4.7
, which tells us that if a compactly generated étale groupoid
G
with compact unit space has polynomial growth, then
G
has weak
m
-comparison, where
m
is determined solely by the growth rate of the groupoid. We also prove Corollary
4.10
, which establishes the AH conjecture for compactly generated minimal ample groupoids with polynomial growth.

Definition 4.1
.

Suppose
G
is an étale groupoid with compact unit space. If
A,B\subseteq G^{(0)}
, we say
A
is
m
-subequivalent to

B
, and write
A\precsim_{m}B
, if there is a finite collection
\mathcal{U}=\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{U}|}
of bisections, and a partition of
\mathcal{U}
into subcollections
\mathcal{U}_{0},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{m}
such that

(1)

A\subseteq\bigcup_{V\in\mathcal{U}}s(V)
, and

(2)

for each
i=0,\ldots,m
we have
r(V)\subseteq B
for all
V\in\mathcal{U}_{i}
, and
r(V)\cap r(V^{\prime})=\emptyset
for all
V\neq V^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}_{i}
.

Given an étale groupoid
G
with compact unit space and a Borel measure
\mu
on
G^{(0)}
, we say
\mu
is
G
-invariant
, or simply
invariant
, if
\mu(s(V))=\mu(r(V))
for all open bisections
V\subseteq G
. Denote
by
M(G)
the convex set of invariant positive regular Borel probability measures on
G^{(0)}
. It is compact in the weak-
*
-topology inherited from the dual space of
C(G^{(0)})
.

Definition 4.2
.

Suppose
G
is an étale groupoid with compact unit space.
We say
G
has
m
-comparison
if for any closed
A\subseteq G^{(0)}
and open
B\subseteq G^{(0)}
with
A\subseteq r(GB)
and for which
\mu(A)<\mu(B)
for all
\mu\in M(G)
, we have
A\precsim_{m}B
. If
m=0
we simply say
G
has
comparison
.

Similarly, we
say
G
has
weak
m
-comparison
if for any closed
A\subseteq G^{(0)}
and open
B\subseteq G^{(0)}
with
A\subseteq r(GB)
and for which
\sup_{\mu\in M(G)}\mu(A)<\inf_{\mu\in M(G)}\mu(B)
, we have
A\precsim_{m}B
.

We will in Theorem
4.7
show that any étale groupoid
G
with polynomial growth satisfies weak
m
-comparison for a sufficiently large
m
. Key to this will be a series of lemmas. For the reader’s convenience we record and prove the following basic observation.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a function with polynomial growth.
Denote by
\mathrm{ord}
the order of growth for
f
as in (
2.2
).
Then for every
N\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
there exists an
M\geq N
such that
f(2M)\leq(2^{\mathrm{ord}}+1)f(M)
.

Proof.

Note that for any
\varepsilon>0
and all
t>\varepsilon>0
, the assumption in the lemma implies that there exists
C>0
such that
f(t)\leq Ct^{\mathrm{ord}}
. Suppose there exists an
N
such that for all
M\geq N
we have

f(2M)>(2^{\mathrm{ord}}+1)f(M).

Then for all
k\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle(2^{\mathrm{ord}}+1)^{k}f(N)<f(2^{k}N)\leq C(2^{k}N)^{\mathrm{ord}}=C(2^{\mathrm{ord}})^{k}N^{\mathrm{ord}},

which is a contradiction. The lemma follows.
∎

The following lemma now follows immediately by Lemma
4.3
and Definition
2.6
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G
be an étale groupoid, and suppose
\ell:G\to[0,\infty)
is a coarse length function such that
\gamma_{\ell}
has polynomial growth of order
\mathrm{ord}
. Then for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
there is
M\geq N
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}|B_{\ell}(2M)x|\leq(2^{\mathrm{ord}}+1)\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}|B_{\ell}(M)x|.

We will find the expressions
\sup_{\mu\in M(G)}\mu(A)
and
\inf_{\mu\in M(G)}\mu(B)
from Definition
4.2
rather unwieldy for our purposes.
As such, we record the following lemma which provides us with expressions which will be easier to manipulate in the sequel.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
G
be a
\sigma
-compact étale groupoid with polynomial growth. Let
\ell
be a coarse length function witnessing polynomial growth, and let
B(n)\coloneqq\{\gamma\in G\mid\ell(\gamma)\leq n\}
. Then the following hold:

(1)

for every
K\subseteq G
open and precompact and every
\varepsilon>0
there exists an
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\frac{|KB(n)x|}{|B(n)x|}<1+\varepsilon,\hbox{ for all }x\in G^{(0)};

(2)

For all closed sets
A\subseteq G^{(0)}
one has

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\frac{1}{|B(n)x|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n)x}1_{A}(r(\gamma))\leq\sup_{\mu\in M(G)}\mu(A).

(3)

For all open sets
A\subseteq G^{(0)}
one has

\inf_{\mu\in M(G)}\mu(A)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{x\in G^{(0)}}\frac{1}{|B(n)x|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n)x}1_{A}(r(\gamma)).

Proof.

Note that (1) follows by
[
7
, Proposition 3.2]
.

For (2) fix a closed subset
A\subseteq G^{(0)}
and
write
\overline{D}_{+}(A)
for the left hand-side. By the properties of the limit superior there exists a strictly increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k}
of natural numbers such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\frac{1}{|B(n_{k})x|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n_{k})x}1_{A}(r(\gamma))=\overline{D}_{+}(A).

For each
k
choose
x_{k}
such that

\left|\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\frac{1}{|B(n_{k})x|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n_{k})x}1_{A}(r(\gamma))-\frac{1}{|B(n_{k})x_{k}|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n_{k})x_{k}}1_{A}(r(\gamma))\right|<\frac{1}{k}.

Then a simple application of the triangle inequality shows

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{|B(n_{k})x_{k}|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n_{k})x_{k}}1_{A}(r(\gamma))=\overline{D}_{+}(A).

Define

\nu_{k}:=\frac{1}{|B(n_{k})x_{k}|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n_{k})x_{k}}\delta_{r(\gamma)}

Then
\nu_{k}
is a probability measure and up to passing to another subsequence we may assume that
\nu_{k}\to\nu
in the weak-
*
-topology.

We claim that the sequence
\nu_{k}
is approximately invariant. Let
U\subseteq G
be an open precompact bisection. Then, for any
\gamma\in G
we have
r(\gamma)\in s(U)
if and only if
r(U\gamma)\in r(U)
. Using this fact we obtain

\displaystyle\nu_{k}(s(U))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B(n_{k})x_{k}|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n_{k})x_{k}}\delta_{r(\gamma)}(s(U))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B(n_{k})x_{k}|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n_{k})x_{k}}\delta_{r(U\gamma)}(r(U))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B(n_{k})x_{k}|}\sum_{\gamma\in UB(n_{k})x_{k}}\delta_{r(\gamma)}(r(U)).

Applying this equality allows us to estimate

\displaystyle|\nu_{k}(r(U))-\nu_{k}(s(U))|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B(n_{k})x_{k}|}\left|\sum_{\gamma\in B(n_{k})x_{k}}\delta_{r(\gamma)}(r(U))-\sum_{\gamma\in UB(n_{k})x_{k}}\delta_{r(\gamma)}(r(U))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B(n_{k})x_{k}|}\sum_{\gamma\in UB(n_{k})x_{k}\Delta B(n_{k})x_{k}}\delta_{r(\gamma)}(r(U))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|UB(n_{k})x_{k}\Delta B(n_{k})x_{k}|}{|B(n_{k})x_{k}|}.

Finally, applying part (1) of this lemma, we conclude that
|\nu_{k}(r(U))-\nu_{k}(s(U))|\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.
A standard
\varepsilon/3
-argument then shows that the limit measure
\nu
must be invariant with respect to open precompact bisections. If
U\subseteq G
is an arbitrary open bisection, use
\sigma
-compactness of
G
to write
U=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}U_{n}
for an increasing sequence of open precompact bisections. It follows that
(s(U_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(r(U_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are increasing sequences of open sets in
G^{0}
and hence

\nu(s(U))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu(s(U_{n}))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu(r(U_{n}))=\nu(r(U)).

Thus,
\nu\in M(G)
and since
A
is closed we have

\displaystyle\nu(A)\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\nu_{k}(A)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{|B(n_{k})x_{k}|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n_{k})x_{k}}\delta_{r(\gamma)}(A)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{|B(n_{k})x_{k}|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n_{k})x_{k}}1_{A}(r(\gamma))=\overline{D}_{+}(A).

In particular, we have

\overline{D}_{+}(A)\leq\nu(A)\leq\sup_{\mu\in M(G)}\mu(A).

For the final item (3), let
A\subseteq G^{(0)}
be an open set and observe that
\underline{D}^{-}(A)\coloneqq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{x\in G^{(0)}}\frac{1}{|B(n)x|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n)x}1_{A}(r(\gamma))
satisfies

\underline{D}^{-}(A)=1-\overline{D}_{+}(G^{(0)}\setminus A).

Thus, we can apply (2) to the closed set
G^{(0)}\setminus A
, to obtain the result.
∎

The next lemma is crucial to the proof of Theorem
4.7
. It broadly follows the philosophy of
[

, Lemma 3.1]
and
[
9
, Lemma 6.4]
, but additional care is needed to account for the added complexity present in the groupoid setting.
Before proceeding we need some notation.
Let
G
be an étale groupoid, and suppose
G^{(0)}
is metrized by a metric

. For
A\subseteq G^{(0)}
and

we set

\displaystyle A^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=\{x\in G^{(0)}\mid d(x,A)<\varepsilon\}

\displaystyle A^{-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=\{x\in G^{(0)}\mid d(x,G^{(0)}\setminus A)>\varepsilon\},

where for any
Y\subseteq G^{(0)}
we have
d(x,Y)=\inf\{d(x,y)\mid y\in Y\}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
G
be an étale groupoid for which the topology of
G^{(0)}
is metrizable, and suppose
d
is a metric generating this topology.
Assume moreover that
A\subseteq G^{(0)}
is closed,
B\subseteq G^{(0)}
is open, and
A\subseteq r(GB)
.
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and suppose
that there exist a
precompact open
subset
D\subseteq G
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

|\{\gamma\in D^{-1}Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in A^{\varepsilon}\}|<m|\{\gamma\in Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in B^{-\varepsilon}\}|\hbox{ for all }x\in G^{(0)}.

Then
A\precsim_{m-1}B
.

Proof.

First, cover
G
using open
bisections
(V_{i})_{i\in I}
for some index set
I
.
Since
\overline{D}
is compact, there is a finite subcover of
\overline{D}
.
For any open bisection
V
, we have that
D\cap V
is again an open bisection as
D
is open.
As such, we may after possibly reindexing arrange that
D=\cup_{n=1}^{M_{D}}V_{n}
, for some
M_{D}\geq 1
.

Fix a bijection
\sigma
from
\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{M_{D}}\}\times\{1,\ldots,m\}
to
\{1,\ldots,m\cdot M_{D}\}
. We will now specify an algorithm which in finitely many steps will implement the subequivalence
A\precsim_{m-1}B
. Specifically, using a total of
m\cdot M_{D}
steps, we will exhibit families
\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{m}
of open sets
which satisfy the necessary conditions from Definition
4.1
.

We begin by setting

A_{1}=A
,
B_{1,k}=B
for all
k=1,\ldots m
,
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon
, and
\mathcal{U}_{k}=\emptyset
for all
k=1,\ldots,m
.
Then assume that at step
n
we have initial data consisting of a closed set
A_{n}
, an
m
-tuple of open sets
(B_{n,1},\ldots B_{n,k})
, and
\varepsilon_{n}>0
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle|\{\gamma\in D^{-1}Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in A_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\}|<\sum_{k=1}^{m}|\{\gamma\in Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in B_{n,k}^{-\varepsilon_{n}}\}|

for every
x\in G^{(0)}
.
The initial data at step
1
satisfies (
4.1
) by assumption.

Before proceeding, note that to each bisection
V
we have a homeomorphism
\theta_{V}\colon s(V)\to r(V)
. Moreover, suppose
V\subseteq D
. Then for any
r(\mu)\in s(V)
, there is a unique
\gamma_{V}\in D
such that
\theta_{V}(r(\mu))=r(\gamma_{V}\mu)
.

Now, for
n\in\{1,\ldots,m\cdot M_{D}-1\}
let
\sigma^{-1}(n)=(V,k_{0})
. Then choose
\varepsilon_{n+1}
with

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n+1}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon_{n}

\displaystyle\theta_{V}^{-1}((\theta_{V}(A_{n}))^{3\varepsilon_{n+1}})

\displaystyle\subseteq A_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}

\displaystyle\theta_{V}((\theta_{V}^{-1}(X\setminus B_{n,k_{0}}))^{2\varepsilon_{n+1}})

\displaystyle\subseteq(X\setminus B_{n,k_{0}})^{\varepsilon_{n}}

Set
U_{n}:=\theta_{V}^{-1}(B_{n,k_{0}}\cap(\theta_{V}(A_{n}))^{\varepsilon_{n+1}})
, and add
U_{n}
to
\mathcal{U}_{k_{0}}
. Then define

\displaystyle B_{n+1,k}=B_{n,k}\quad\text{if $k\neq k_{0}$}

\displaystyle\theta_{U_{n}}=\theta_{V},\quad B_{n+1,k_{0}}=B_{n,k_{0}}\setminus\overline{\theta_{V}(U_{n})},\quad A_{n+1}=A_{n}\setminus U_{n}.

We claim that the new data satisfies (
4.1
).

To verify this we will first show that for all
x\in G^{(0)}
, the assignment

\displaystyle\mu\mapsto(r|_{V}^{-1}(r(\mu)))^{-1}\mu,\quad\text{for $\mu\in Dx$,}

gives rise to an injection of finite sets

(
\dagger
)

\displaystyle\{\gamma\in Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in B_{n,k_{0}}^{-\varepsilon_{n}}\setminus B_{n+1,k_{0}}^{-\varepsilon_{n+1}}\}\to\{\gamma\in D^{-1}Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in A_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\setminus A_{n+1}^{\varepsilon_{n+1}}\}.

The map is clearly injective, so we only need to check that it takes values in the correct set.
To see this let
x\in G^{(0)}
be arbitrary, and suppose
\mu\in Dx
is such that
r(\mu x)=r(\mu)\in B_{n,k_{0}}^{-\varepsilon_{n}}
but
r(\mu)\not\in B_{n+1,k_{0}}^{-\varepsilon_{n+1}}
. The latter implies

\displaystyle d(r(\mu),(X\setminus B_{n,k_{0}})\cup\overline{\theta_{V}(U_{n})})\leq\varepsilon_{n+1}.

Since
r(\mu)\in B_{n,k_{0}}^{-\varepsilon_{n}}
it follows that

\displaystyle d(r(\mu),X\setminus B_{n,k_{0}})>\varepsilon_{n}>\varepsilon_{n+1},

from which we deduce
d(r(\mu),\overline{\theta_{V}(U_{n})})\leq\varepsilon_{n+1}
.
Now,
\theta_{V}(U_{n})
is a subset of
(\theta_{V}(A_{n}))^{\varepsilon_{n+1}}
, so
r(h)\in(\theta_{V}(A_{n}))^{3\varepsilon_{n+1}}
. Moreover,
\theta_{V}^{-1}(r(\mu))\in A_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
since
\theta_{V}^{-1}((\theta_{V}(A_{n}))^{3\varepsilon_{n+1}})\subseteq A_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
. Then, since
\theta_{V}^{-1}\colon r(V)\to s(V)
is a homeomorphism, there is a unique
\gamma_{V}\in V^{-1}\subseteq D^{-1}
such that
s(\gamma_{V}^{-1})=r(\mu)
and
r(\gamma_{V}^{-1}\mu)\in s(V)
. By the above observations we therefore deduce
r(\gamma_{V}^{-1}\mu)\in A_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
.
However, we see that
A_{n+1}\subseteq\theta_{V}^{-1}(X\setminus B_{n,k_{0}})
, because otherwise we would have

\displaystyle r(\mu)=\theta_{V}(r(\gamma_{V}^{-1}\mu))

\displaystyle\in\theta_{V}(A_{n+1}^{\varepsilon_{n+1}})\subseteq\theta_{V}((\theta_{V}^{-1}(X\setminus B_{n,k_{0}}))^{\varepsilon_{n+1}})

\displaystyle\subseteq\theta_{V}((\theta_{V}^{-1}(X\setminus B_{n,k_{0}}))^{2\varepsilon_{n+1}}\subseteq(X\setminus B_{n,k_{0}})^{\varepsilon_{n}},

which is a contradiction. Then since
r(\mu)\not\in(X\setminus B_{n,k_{0}})^{\varepsilon_{n}}
we get
\theta_{V}^{-1}(r(\mu))=r(\gamma_{V}^{-1}\mu)\not\in A_{n+1}^{\varepsilon_{n+1}}
, so it follows that
\theta_{V}^{-1}(r(\mu))\not\in A_{n+1}^{\varepsilon_{n+1}}
.
Thus for every
\mu\in Dx
such that
r(\mu)\in B_{n,k_{0}}^{-\varepsilon_{n}}\setminus B_{n+1,k_{0}}^{-\varepsilon_{n+1}}
we have
\gamma_{V}^{-1}\mu\in D^{-1}Dx
and
r(\gamma_{V}^{-1}\mu x)\in A_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\setminus A_{n+1}^{\varepsilon_{n+1}}
as required. We conclude that
(\dagger)
holds.

Having established the injection
(\dagger)
it is easy to see that we obtain the following inequality:

|\{\gamma\in Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in B_{n,k_{0}}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\}|-|\{\gamma\in D^{-1}Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in A_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\setminus A_{n+1}^{\varepsilon_{n+1}}\}|\leq|\{\gamma\in Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in B_{n+1,k_{0}}^{\varepsilon_{n+1}}\}|.

Using this we estimate

\displaystyle|\{\gamma\in D^{-1}Dx

\displaystyle\mid r(\gamma)\in A_{n+1}^{\varepsilon_{n+1}}\}|

\displaystyle=|\{\gamma\in D^{-1}Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in A_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\}|-|\{\gamma\in D^{-1}Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in A_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\setminus A_{n+1}^{\varepsilon_{n+1}}\}|

\displaystyle<\sum_{k=1}^{m}|\{\gamma\in Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in B_{n,k}^{-\varepsilon_{n}}\}|-|\{\gamma\in D^{-1}Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in A_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\setminus A_{n+1}^{\varepsilon_{n+1}}\}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\neq k_{0}}|\{\gamma\in Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in B_{n+1,k}^{-\varepsilon_{n+1}}\}|

\displaystyle\quad\quad+|\{\gamma\in Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in B_{n,k_{0}}^{-\varepsilon_{n}}\}|-|\{\gamma\in D^{-1}Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in A_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\setminus A_{n+1}^{\varepsilon_{n+1}}\}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}|\{\gamma\in Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in B_{n+1,k}^{-\varepsilon_{n+1}}\}|.

Therefore (
4.1
) also holds true at step
n+1
. In particular it holds true at step
m\cdot M_{D}
.

It remains to show that the families
\mathcal{U}_{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,m
implement the subequivalence
A\preceq_{m-1}B
.
By the construction of the sets
U_{n}
we see that that
\theta_{V_{U_{n}}}(U_{n})
are disjoint open subsets of
B
when
U_{n}
are taken from the same family
\mathcal{U}_{k}
.
Thus we need only verify that
U_{1},\ldots U_{m\cdot M_{D}}
cover
A
.
If this is not the case, then
A_{mM_{D}+1}\neq\emptyset
, so we may pick some
x
in this set.
Then, for every pair
(V,k)\in\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{M_{D}}\}\times\{1,\ldots,m\}
we have
x\not\in U_{\sigma(V,k)}
.
This in particular implies
\theta_{V}(x)\not\in B_{\sigma(V,k),k}
, because otherwise
\theta_{V}(x)\in\theta_{V}(A_{n})\subseteq(\theta_{V}(A_{n}))^{\varepsilon_{n+1}}
for all
n
, and we would have

\displaystyle x=\theta_{V}^{-1}(\theta_{V}(x))\in\theta_{V}^{-1}(B_{\sigma(V,k),k}\cap(\theta_{V}(A_{\sigma(V,k)}))^{\varepsilon_{n+1}})=U_{\sigma(V,k)},

which is a contradiction.
It therefore follows that
\theta_{V}(x)\not\in B_{m\cdot M_{D}+1,k}
.
Then
|\{\gamma\in D^{-1}Dx\mid r(\gamma)\in A_{m\cdot M_{D}+1}^{\varepsilon_{m\cdot M_{D}+1}}\}|\geq 1
, while for every
k=1,\ldots,m
we have
\{\gamma\in D\mid r(\gamma)\in B_{m\cdot M_{D}+1,k}^{-\varepsilon_{m\cdot M_{D}+1}}\}=\emptyset
, which contradicts (
4.1
). We deduce that
A=\cup_{n=1}^{m\cdot M_{D}}U_{n}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

We may now prove the second main result of the article.

Theorem 4.7
.

Suppose
G
is a compactly generated étale groupoid with compact and metrizable unit space.
If
G
has polynomial growth,
then
there exists an
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that
G
has weak
m
-comparison.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.7
, we can find a precompact open symmetric generating set
K
for
G
such that the coarse length function
\ell_{K}
witnesses the polynomial growth of
G
.
Note that the length function
\ell_{K}
is integer-valued.
To ease notation, we write
B(n):=B_{\ell_{K}}(n)
in the rest of the proof.
We remark that since
\ell_{K}
is the word-length function for the generating set
K
, we have
B(M)^{-1}B(M)=B(2M)
for any
M\geq 0
, which we will use below.
Furthermore, since
K
is open and precompact, the balls
B_{\ell_{K}}(n)
are open and precompact for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

Let
A\subseteq G^{(0)}
be a closed subset and let
C\subseteq G^{(0)}
be an open subset such that
\sup_{\mu\in M(G)}\mu(A)<\inf_{\mu\in M(G)}\mu(C)
.
Combining this inequality with Lemma
4.5
implies

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\frac{1}{|B(n)x|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n)x}1_{A}(r(\gamma))<\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{x\in G^{(0)}}\frac{1}{|B(n)x|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n)x}1_{C}(r(\gamma)).

Using the definitions of the limit superior and limit inferior, we conclude that there exists an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n_{1},n_{2}\geq N
we have

\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\frac{1}{|B(n_{1})x|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n_{1})x}1_{A}(r(\gamma))<\inf_{x\in G^{(0)}}\frac{1}{|B(n_{2})x|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n_{2})x}1_{C}(r(\gamma)).

Thus, for all
n_{1},n_{2}\geq N
and all
x_{1},x_{2}\in G^{(0)}
we have

\frac{1}{|B(n_{1})x_{1}|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n_{1})x_{1}}1_{A}(r(\gamma))<\frac{1}{|B(n_{2})x_{2}|}\sum_{\gamma\in B(n_{2})x_{2}}1_{C}(r(\gamma)),

which we can rephrase as

\frac{|\{\gamma\in B(n_{1})x_{1}\mid r(\gamma)\in A\}|}{|B(n_{1})x_{1}|}<\frac{|\{\gamma\in B(n_{2})x_{2}\mid r(\gamma)\in C\}|}{|B(n_{2})x_{2}|}.

By Lemma
4.4
there exists an
M\geq N
such that

\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}|B(2M)x|\leq(2^{\mathrm{ord}}+1)\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}|B(M)x|.

In particular, for all
x\in G^{(0)}
we get

\displaystyle|\{\gamma\in B(2M)x\mid r(\gamma)\in A\}||B(M)x|

\displaystyle<|B(2M)x||\{\gamma\in B(M)x\mid r(\gamma)\in C\}|

\displaystyle\leq(2^{\mathrm{ord}}+1)\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}|B(M)x||\{\gamma\in B(M)x\mid r(\gamma)\in C\}|

Since
B(M)x\neq\emptyset
for all
x\in G^{(0)}
we conclude that

|\{\gamma\in B(2M)x\mid r(\gamma)\in A\}|<(2^{\mathrm{ord}}+1)\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}|B(M)x||\{\gamma\in B(M)x\mid r(\gamma)\in C\}|

The result now follows from Lemma
4.6
using
m=(2^{\mathrm{ord}}+1)\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}|B(M)x|
.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Note that the value of
m
found in the proof above is larger than the analogous value in
[
18
, Theorem 3.2]
by the factor
\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}|B(M)x|
. This discrepancy appears due to a lack of homogeneity in of the Cayley graphs for groupoids.

In order to apply Theorem
4.7
in the context of homological invariants for topological full groups, we will need the following Lemma. It adapts the proof of
[
5
, Proposition 3.10]
in the same way
[
18
, Lemma 2.3]
adapts the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 6.4]
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
G
be a
\sigma
-compact, minimal, ample groupoid with compact unit space. If
G
has weak
m
-comparison for some
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, then
G
has comparison.

Proof.

Note that any minimal groupoid with a finite unit space has comparison. We can therefore assume that
G^{(0)}
is infinite.
Suppose
A
and
B
are two clopen subsets of
G^{(0)}
such that

\mu(A)<\mu(B)\hbox{ for all }\mu\in M(G).

It follows from
[
5
, Remark 3.8]
that there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that
\mu(B)-\mu(A)>\varepsilon
for all
\mu\in M(G)
.
Note also, that
B
must be infinite.
Fix a point
x\in G^{(0)}
. We claim that we can inductively find elements
g_{0},\ldots,g_{m}\in G
such that
s(g_{i})=x
,
r(g_{i})\in B
, and
r(g_{i})\neq r(g_{j})
unless
i=j
. Indeed, since
G
is minimal and
B
is open we have
B\cap Gx\neq\emptyset
. Thus, there exists an element
g_{0}\in G
such that
s(g_{0})=x
and
r(g_{0})\in B
.
Supposing now that we have already found
g_{0},\ldots,g_{k}
, use the fact that
B
is infinite and Hausdorff to find a proper clopen subset
O\subset B
such that
r(g_{0}),\ldots,r(g_{k})\in B\setminus O
.
Since
O
is open and
G
is minimal,
O\cap Gx\neq\emptyset
and hence there exists an element
g_{k+1}\in G
such that
s(g_{k+1})=x
and
r(g_{k+1})\in O
. Then the elements
g_{0},\ldots,g_{k},g_{k+1}\in G
satisfy the desired requirements.

For each
0\leq i\leq m
find a compact open bisection
S_{i}\subseteq G
such that
g_{i}\in S_{i}
. By shrinking the
S_{i}
if necessary we may assume without loss of generality that
s(S_{i})=s(S_{j})
for all
i,j\in\{0,\ldots,m\}
, that for
U:=s(S_{0})
we have
\sup_{\mu\in M(G)}\mu(U)<\frac{\varepsilon}{m+1}
, and that the sets
r(S_{i})
are pairwise disjoint subsets of
B
.
Since
G
is minimal, we also have

\inf_{\mu\in M(G)}\mu(U)>0.

Define

B_{1}\coloneqq B\setminus(r(S_{0})\sqcup\ldots\sqcup r(S_{m})).

Note that
B_{1}
is a clopen subset of
G^{(0)}
such that for all
\mu\in M(G)
we have

\mu(B_{1})=\mu(B)-\sum_{i=0}^{m}\mu(r(S_{i}))=\mu(B)-\sum_{i=0}^{m}\mu(s(S_{i}))=\mu(B)-(m+1)\mu(U)\geq\mu(B)-\varepsilon>\mu(A).

Set
A_{1}\coloneqq A
. It follows from
[
5
, Remark 3.8]
again that there exists a
\delta>0
such that

(4.2)

\mu(B_{1})-\mu(A_{1})>\delta\hbox{ for all }\mu\in M(G).

Using
\sigma
-compactness, we can find a a countable cover
(V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
G
by compact open bisections. Let
\theta_{n}\colon s(V_{n})\to r(V_{n})
denote the homeomorphism associated with the bisection
V_{n}
. Inductively, define sequences
(U_{n})_{n}
,
(A_{n})_{n}
and
(B_{n})_{n}
as follows:

\displaystyle U_{n}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\theta_{n}^{-1}(B_{n}\cap\theta_{n}(A_{n}\cap s(V_{n}))),

\displaystyle B_{n+1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq B_{n}\setminus\theta_{n}(U_{n}),\hbox{ and }

\displaystyle A_{n+1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq A_{n}\setminus U_{n}.

Note that each of the sets above is compact open. Moreover, note that it may happen that
U_{n}
is empty for some
n
, in which case
B_{n+1}=B_{n}
and
A_{n+1}=A_{n}
, so the algorithm can still continue from there.
We claim that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have that
\mu(A_{n})+\delta<\mu(B_{n})
for all
\mu\in M(G)
. We already know from (
4.2
) that this is the case for
n=1
. Proceeding by induction, we have

\mu(B_{n+1})=\mu(B_{n})-\mu(\theta_{n}(U_{n}))=\mu(B_{n})-\mu(U_{n})=\mu(B_{n})-\mu(A_{n})+\mu(A_{n+1})>\delta+\mu(A_{n+1}).

Let
A_{\infty}=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_{n}
and
B_{\infty}=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}B_{n}
. As the sequences
(A_{n})_{n}
and
(B_{n})_{n}
are decreasing, we conclude that

\mu(B_{\infty})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(B_{n})\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(A_{n})+\delta=\mu(A_{\infty})+\delta.

We claim that

\sup_{\mu\in M(G)}\mu(A_{\infty})=0.

To see this, note that we have
GA_{\infty}\cap B_{\infty}=\emptyset
. Let
\mu\in M(G)
be an ergodic measure. Since
\mu(B_{\infty})>0
we have
1\geq\mu(GA_{\infty})+\mu(B_{\infty})>\mu(GA_{\infty})
. Hence ergodicity of
\mu
implies that
\mu(GA_{\infty})=0
, and since
A_{\infty}\subseteq GA_{\infty}
we conclude that
\mu(A_{\infty})=0
. As an arbitrary measure in
M(G)
can be written as a convex combination of ergodic measures, the claim follows.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
consider the function

f_{n}\colon M(G)\to[0,1],\ f_{n}(\mu)=\mu(A_{n}).

Since
A_{n}
is compact open, the function
f_{n}
is continuous for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and as
\mu(A_{\infty})=0
for all
\mu\in M(G)
, the sequence
(f_{n})_{n}
converges pointwise to the constant zero function. As
M(G)
is compact, Dini’s Theorem applies and we conclude that this convergence is in fact uniform. In particular, there exists a
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f_{k}(\mu)<\inf_{\mu\in M(G)}\mu(U)
for all
\mu\in M(G)
, and hence

\sup_{\mu\in M(G)}\mu(A_{k})\leq\inf_{\mu\in M(G)}\mu(U).

By weak
m
-comparison,
A_{k}\precsim_{m}U
which implies

(4.3)

A_{k}\precsim\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{m}r(S_{i})=B\setminus B_{1}.

By construction,
A\setminus A_{k}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{k-1}U_{i}
and the sets
\theta_{i}(U_{i})\subseteq B_{1}
are pairwise disjoint for all
1\leq i\leq k-1
. In other words we also have

A\setminus A_{k}\precsim B_{1}
. Combining this with (
4.3
) yields
A\precsim B
as desired.
∎

As an application, we can conclude that the hypothesis of all of the main results concerning the relation between the group homology of topological full groups and groupoid homology from
[
14
]
are satisfied for compactly generated, minimal, ample groupoids with polynomial growth whose unit space has no isolated points. As a detailed discussion of these methods is beyond the scope of the article, we only explicitly state a sample result illustrating one of the many strong consequences one can obtain by combining our comparison result Theorem
4.7
with the results from
[
14
]
.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
G
be a compactly generated, locally compact, Hausdorff,
minimal, ample groupoid whose unit space has no isolated points.
If
G
has polynomial growth, then
G
satisfies Matui’s AH-conjecture.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.7
,
G
has weak
m
-comparison for some sufficiently large
m
. Since
G
is
\sigma
-compact, minimal and ample, Lemma
4.9
implies that
G
has comparison. Then
[
14
, Corollary E]
yields that
G
satisfies Matui’s AH-conjecture.

∎
