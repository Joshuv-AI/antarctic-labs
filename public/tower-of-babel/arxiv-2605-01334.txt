Convexity inequalities for eigenvalues and log-concavity of eigenfunctions
Paul Bryan, Julie Clutterbuck, Cale Rankin

Abstract
. We give simple new proofs of two well-known results for the Schrödinger operator: first, the Brunn–Minkowski inequality for Dirichlet eigenvalues and, second, the log-concavity of the first Dirichlet eigenfunction. Our proof of the first applies to a class of domains including C 1 , 1 C^{1,1} connected domains and convex potentials. In the special case of convex domains, the second result is a simple corollary.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega_{0},\Omega_{1}\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n}
be bounded domains (connected,
open sets) and let
V:\overline{\Omega_{0}\cup\Omega_{1}}\to\mathbb{R}
be a potential. The first
Dirichlet eigenvalue for the Schrödinger operator
-\Delta+V
, which we denote by
\lambda_{i}=\lambda(\Omega_{i})
,
i=0,1
is given by the Rayleigh quotient,

\lambda_{i}=\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{\Omega_{i}}|\nabla u|^{2}+Vu^{2}dx}{\int_{\Omega_{i}}u^{2}dx}:u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega_{i}\to\mathbf{R})\backslash\{0\}\right\}.

Assumption 1.1
.

We assume that
\Omega_{0},\Omega_{1}
and
V
are sufficiently regular so that the first eigenfunctions
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
are
C^{1}
up to the boundary. For example, domains with
C^{1,1}
boundary combined with potentials
V\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega_{0}\cup\Omega_{1}})
satisfy this requirement
[
5
, Theorem 9.15]
. We also assume that
V
extends to a convex function on the convex hull of
\Omega_{0}\cup\Omega_{1}

For
t\in[0,1]
, the deformation of
\Omega_{0}
into
\Omega_{1}
by Minkowski sums is denoted by

(1)

\Omega_{t}=(1-t)\Omega_{0}+t\Omega_{1}:=\{(1-t)x_{0}+tx_{1};x\in\Omega_{0}\text{ and }x_{1}\in\Omega_{1}\}.

Our goal in this paper is to give simple new proofs of the following theorems originally due to
Brascamp and Lieb
[
1
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(
Brunn-Minkowski inequality for
eigenvalues

[
1
, Theorem 6.2]
)
.

Let
\Omega_{0},\Omega_{1}
and
V
satisfy Assumption
1.1
. Let
\lambda_{t}=\lambda(\Omega_{t})
be the
first Dirichlet eigenvalue of
\Omega_{t}
. Then
\lambda_{t}\leq(1-t)\lambda_{0}+t\lambda_{1}
.

As an immediate corollary of our proof we obtain:

Theorem 1.3

(
Log-concavity of first eigenfunction

[
1
, Theorem 6.1]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex domain and
V:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
be
convex. Then the first Dirichlet eigenfunction is log-concave.

Our proofs rely on
Colesanti’s
[
2
]
idea of using the sup-convolution. Recall for functions
u_{i}:\Omega_{i}\to\mathbf{R}
, the sup-convolution defined for
z\in\Omega_{t}
is

(2)

\overline{u}(z)=\sup\{u_{0}^{1-t}(x_{0})u_{1}^{t}(x_{1}):z=(1-t)x_{0}+tx_{1},\>x_{i}\in\Omega_{i}\}.

However, where Colesanti’s proof requires convexity of domains and log-concavity
of eigenfunctions, our proof works for connected domains with first eigenfunction
C^{1}
up to the boundary, and

establishes
the log-concavity of eigenfunctions as a simple
corollary. On the other hand, Colesanti’s result established, for the first
time, conditions for equality — something not captured by our proof. Moreover, our results easily recover Theorem
1.2
for convex
\Omega_{i}
via approximation since if a sequence of convex domains
\Omega_{k}
converges
to a convex domain
\Omega
in Hausdorff distance then
\lambda(\Omega_{k})\rightarrow\lambda(\Omega)
. Recently, Brunn–Minkowski inequalities for eigenvalues of elliptic operators have been considered by a number of authors
[
8
,
7
,
3
]
and interesting new proofs have been provided for some of Brascamp & Lieb’s other results
[
6
]
.

Our plan is to prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
in Section
2
taking for granted some general regularity properties of sup-convolutions proved in Section
3
. This allows the reader to focus on the main idea of the proof.

2.
Proof of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3

Proof. (Theorem
1.2
).

For
i=0,1
let
u_{i}:\Omega_{i}\to\mathbf{R}

denote the positive first eigenfunctions so that
-\Delta u_{i}+Vu_{i}=\lambda_{i}u_{i}
with
u_{i}|_{\partial\Omega_{i}}=0
. Then the sup-convolution
\overline{u}:\Omega_{t}\to\mathbf{R}
given by (
2
) is locally semiconvex and globally Lipschitz on
\Omega_{t}
(Lemmas
3.6
,
3.9
).

Fix
z\in\Omega_{t}
. The supremum (
2
) is attained at
(x_{0},x_{1})\in\Omega_{0}\times\Omega_{1}
(Lemma
3.4
). Thus
the function
h(\xi):=u_{0}(x_{0}+t\xi)^{1-t}u_{1}(x_{1}-(1-t)\xi)^{t}
has a maximum at
\xi=0
, whereby

(3)

\frac{\nabla u_{0}}{u_{0}}(x_{0})=\frac{\nabla u_{1}}{u_{1}}(x_{1}).

By semiconvexity, for Lebesgue a.e.
z\in\Omega_{t}
,
\bar{u}
has
Alexandrov second derivatives satisfying

D_{ii}\overline{u}(z)=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\frac{\overline{u}(z+\tau e_{i})+\overline{u}(z-\tau e_{i})-2\overline{u}(z)}{\tau^{2}}.

Using that the supremum defining
\bar{u}(z)
is attained at
(x_{0},x_{1})\in\Omega_{0}\times\Omega_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle D_{ii}\overline{u}(z)

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\tau^{-2}\Big[u_{0}(x_{0}+\tau e_{i})^{1-t}u_{1}(x_{1}+\tau e_{i})^{t}+u_{0}(x_{0}-\tau e_{i})^{1-t}u_{1}(x_{1}-\tau e_{i})^{t}-2u_{0}(x_{0})^{1-t}u_{1}(x_{1})^{t}\Big].

The right hand side is
f^{\prime\prime}(0)
for
f(\tau):=u_{0}(x_{0}+\tau e_{i})^{1-t}u_{1}(x_{1}+\tau e_{i})^{t}
. It’s routine to compute

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=\bar{u}(z)\left[(1-t)\frac{D_{ii}u_{0}}{u_{0}}(x_{0})+t\frac{D_{ii}u_{1}}{u_{1}}(x_{1})-t(1-t)\left|\frac{D_{i}u_{0}}{u_{0}}(x_{0})-\frac{D_{i}u_{1}}{u_{1}}(x_{1})\right|\right]

(4)

\displaystyle=\bar{u}(z)\left[(1-t)\frac{D_{ii}u_{0}}{u_{0}}+t\frac{D_{ii}u_{1}}{u_{1}}\right],

where we’ve employed (
3
) to obtain
(
4
). Because the
u_{i}
are eigenfunctions (
4
)
implies

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta\bar{u}}{\bar{u}}

\displaystyle\geq(1-t)\frac{\Delta u_{0}}{u_{0}}+t\frac{\Delta u_{1}}{u_{1}}

\displaystyle=(1-t)(V(x_{0})-\lambda_{0})+t(V(x_{1})-\lambda_{1}).

Subsequently, using the convexity of
V
to write
V(z)\leq(1-t)V(x_{0})+tV(x_{1})
, we obtain at each
z\in\Omega_{t}
where
\overline{u}
is Alexandrov second differentiable

\displaystyle\bar{u}\Delta\overline{u}

\displaystyle\geq[(1-t)\lambda_{0}+t\lambda_{1}]\bar{u}^{2}+V(z)\bar{u}^{2}.

The local semiconvexity justifies integration by parts (Lemma

3.11
) whereby

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}|\nabla\overline{u}|^{2}dz

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega_{t}}\overline{u}\Delta\overline{u}dz

\displaystyle\leq\left[(1-t)\lambda_{0}+t\lambda_{1}\right]\int_{\Omega_{t}}\overline{u}^{2}dz-\int_{\Omega_{t}}V(z)\overline{u}^{2}dz.

Substituting into the Rayleigh quotient we complete the proof:

(5)

\lambda_{t}\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega_{t}}|\nabla\bar{u}|^{2}+V\bar{u}^{2}dz}{\int_{\Omega_{t}}\bar{u}^{2}dz}\leq(1-t)\lambda_{0}+t\lambda_{1}.

∎

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is an immediate consequence.

Proof. (Theorem
1.3
).

Apply the previous proof
with
\Omega_{0}=\Omega_{1}=\Omega
, so that
u_{0}=u_{1}=u
is the first Dirichlet
eigenfunction. The convexity of
\Omega
implies
\Omega_{t}=\Omega
. From (
5
)
\bar{u}
minimises the Rayleigh quotient which implies, by uniqueness of principal eigenfunctions up to scaling, that
\bar{u}=Cu
for some
C\in\mathbf{R}
. Evaluate
at a maximum point to obtain
C=1
. Subsequently
\bar{u}=u
and the sup-convolution (
2
) becomes

\displaystyle u(z)=\sup\{u^{1-t}(x_{0})u^{t}(x_{1}):z=(1-t)x_{0}+tx_{1},\>x_{i}\in\Omega_{i}\}.

Log-concavity, namely that
u
satisfies

u\big((1-t)x_{0}+tx_{1}\big)\geq u(x_{0})^{1-t}u(x_{1})^{t}
, is immediate.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Surprisingly, Theorem
1.2
does not imply convexity of
h(t):=\lambda_{t}
, which does not hold even for smooth connected open
domains as seen by taking (a smoothing of)

\Omega_{0}=\{(x=r\cos\theta,y=r\sin\theta);1<r<2\ ,\ \epsilon<\theta<2\pi\}\subset\mathbf{R}^{2},

with
\epsilon>0
small and
\Omega_{1}=B(0,2)\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
to obtain discontinuous

t\mapsto\lambda_{t}
. This distinction arises because given nonconvex
\Omega_{0}
,
\Omega_{1}
and
t_{0},t_{1}\in(0,1)
it is not true that

\{(1-\tau)\Omega_{t_{0}}+\tau\Omega_{t_{1}}\}_{\tau\in[0,1]}\subset\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]}

Or, more simply, for nonconvex
\Omega
we may have
(1-t)\Omega+t\Omega\neq\Omega
.

Remark 2.2
.

The homogeneity of Dirichlet eigenvalues for the Laplacian (
V=0
) means Theorem

1.2
implies
\lambda_{t}^{-1/2}\geq(1-t)\lambda_{0}^{-1/2}+t\lambda_{1}^{-1/2}

[
2
, pg. 128]
, which is usually called the “Brunn–Minkowski inequality for eigenvalues” .

3.
Regularity properties of the sup-convolution

In this section we prove the sup-convolution
\bar{u}
is locally semiconvex and Lipschitz up to the boundary. Whilst these results are likely obvious to those familiar with semiconvex functions, for the sake of a self-contained paper we give complete proofs. We must take some care since for
t\in(0,1)
,
u_{0}^{1-t}
and
u_{1}^{t}
may not be Lipschitz up to the boundary as
u_{i}|_{\partial\Omega_{i}}=0
. We must also account for the fact
\bar{u}
is defined variationally, hence may not enjoy interior regularity.

3.1.
Minkowski Deformation

For open sets
\Omega_{0},\Omega_{1}
the Minkowski deformations (
1
) are open sets which respect closures:

\overline{\Omega_{t}}=(1-t)\overline{\Omega_{0}}+t\overline{\Omega_{1}}
. The boundary behaviour is not so well respected.

Lemma 3.1
.

We have the inclusion

\partial\Omega_{t}\subseteq\partial[(1-t)\Omega_{0}]+\partial[t\Omega_{1}].

Proof.

Since the Minkowski deformation respects closures, it suffices to
show that

\displaystyle(1-t)\Omega_{0}+\overline{t\Omega_{1}}

\displaystyle\subseteq\Omega_{t},

\displaystyle\overline{(1-t)\Omega_{0}}+t\Omega_{1}

\displaystyle\subseteq\Omega_{t}.

We will just show the former with the latter being similar. Let
z=(1-t)x_{0}+tx_{1}\in(1-t)\Omega_{0}+\overline{t\Omega_{1}}
and let
U\subseteq(1-t)\Omega_{0}
be an open set
containing
x_{0}
. Then
(1-t)U+tx_{1}
is an open set
containing
z
and contained in
\Omega_{t}
, hence
z
is in the interior of
\Omega_{t}
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The inclusion from Lemma
3.1
is in general strict. Let
\Omega_{0}=\Omega_{1}=B(0,1)
and let
x_{0}=(-1,0)\in\partial\Omega_{0}
,
x_{1}=(1,0)\in\partial\Omega_{1}
. Then
(1-1/2)x_{0}+(1/2)x_{1}=(0,0)\in\Omega_{1/2}

Note
\Omega_{0}
and
\Omega_{1}
are uniformly
convex. Thus not even uniform convexity can improve the behaviour of boundaries under deformations.

3.2.
Admissible And Optimal Points

Definition 3.3
.

Let
z\in\Omega_{t}
and
(x_{0},x_{1})\in\overline{\Omega_{0}}\times\overline{\Omega_{1}}
. We call
(x_{0},x_{1})

admissible
for
z
if
(1-t)x_{0}+tx_{1}=z
, and
optimal
if in addition equality is attained in the supremum defining the sup-convolution (
2
).

We denote the set of continuous functions
\overline{\Omega_{i}}\to\mathbf{R}
vanishing on

\partial\Omega_{i}
by
C^{0}_{0}(\overline{\Omega_{i}}\to\mathbf{R})
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u_{i}\in C^{0}_{0}(\overline{\Omega_{i}}\to\mathbf{R})
satisfy
u_{i}>0

on the interior. Then for any
z\in\Omega_{t}
, the set of optimal
(x_{0},x_{1})\in\overline{\Omega_{0}}\times\overline{\Omega}_{1}
is non-empty, and contained in the interior

\Omega_{0}\times\Omega_{1}
.

Proof.

The supremum is attained on
\overline{\Omega_{0}}\times\overline{\Omega_{1}}
by
compactness and continuity, so the set of optimal points is non-empty.
For interior
z\in\Omega_{t}
, there exists admissible

(x_{0},x_{1})
with
x_{i}
interior. Then since
u_{i}(x_{i})>0
we have that

\overline{u}(z)\geq u_{0}(x_{0})^{1-t}u_{1}(x_{1})^{t}>0
. For admissible
(x_{0},x_{1})
with
x_{i}\in\partial\Omega_{i}
for either
i=1
or
i=2
(or both),

u_{0}(x_{0})^{1-t}u_{1}(x_{1})^{t}=0<\overline{u}(z)
hence
(x_{0},x_{1})
is not
optimal.
∎

3.3.
Regularity of sup-convolution

Now we study the regularity of
\overline{u}
assuming
u_{i}\in C^{2}(\Omega_{i})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega_{i}})
is positive on
\Omega_{i}
and
0
on
\partial\Omega_{i}
. In our setting this is assured by Assumption
1.1
which is satisfied when for, example,
V
is Hölder and
\Omega
is
C^{1,1}
since, in this case, standard results imply
u_{i}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\Omega_{i})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega_{i}})

[
5
, Lemma 6.16, Theorem 9.15]
.

3.3.1.
Semi-convexity of sup-convolution

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\Omega
be an open domain. A function
u:\Omega\to\mathbf{R}
is

locally semi-convex
if for every compact, convex set
K\subseteq\Omega
,
there exists a
\lambda=\lambda(K)\in\mathbf{R}
such that

x\in K\mapsto u(x)+\frac{\lambda}{2}|x|^{2}

is convex. We say
u
is
(uniformly) semi-convex
if
\lambda
may be
chosen independently of
K
.

Note that if
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
, then
u
is locally semi-convex. If
\Omega
is
bounded and
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
, then
u
is
uniformly semi-convex. From the corresponding result for convex
functions, a supremum over a family of semiconvex functions with fixed
\lambda
is
semiconvex with constant
\lambda
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
u_{i}\in C^{2}(\Omega_{i})
. Then the sup-convolution
\overline{u}
is locally semi-convex.

Proof.

Fix any convex
K\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{t}
and let
\delta=\inf_{z\in K}\overline{u}(z)>0
. Set
M=\max{(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})},\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})})}
and
\tilde{\Omega}_{0}=\{x\in\Omega_{0}:u_{0}(x)\geq(\delta/M)^{\frac{1}{1-t}}\}
,

\tilde{\Omega}_{1}=\{x\in\Omega_{1}:u_{1}(x)\geq(\delta/M)^{\frac{1}{t}}\}
. If
x_{0}\in\Omega_{0}\setminus\tilde{\Omega}_{0}
or

x_{1}\in\Omega_{1}\setminus\tilde{\Omega}_{1}
then
u_{0}(x_{0})^{1-t}u_{1}(x_{1})^{t}<\delta
, which implies
for all
z\in K

\overline{u}(z)=\sup\{u_{0}^{1-t}(x_{0})u_{1}^{t}(x_{1}):z=(1-t)x_{0}+tx_{1},\>x_{i}\in\tilde{\Omega_{i}}\}.

Let
\tilde{u}_{1}
denote the extension of
u_{1}
as
(\delta/M)^{1/t}
on

\tilde{\Omega}_{1}^{c}
. Then with
(x_{0},x_{1})
admissible for

z
we have
x_{1}=\frac{t-1}{t}x_{0}+\frac{z}{t}
, hence we may write

\overline{u}(z)=\sup\left\{u_{0}^{1-t}(x_{0})\tilde{u_{1}}^{t}\left(\frac{z}{t}-\frac{1-t}{t}x_{0}\right):x_{0}\in\tilde{\Omega}_{0}\right\}.

As a function of
z
, the term in the sup is semi-convex with constant
\lambda
independent of
x_{0}
. Having expressed
\bar{u}|_{K}
as a supremum of semiconvex functions,
\bar{u}|_{K}
is semiconvex.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
u_{i}\in C^{2}(\Omega_{i})
. Then the sup-convolution
\overline{u}
is locally Lipschitz
and Alexandrov twice differentiable almost everywhere. That is, there exists
measurable functions
\nabla\overline{u}:\Omega_{t}\to\mathbf{R}^{n}
and
D^{2}\overline{u}:\Omega_{t}\to S_{n}
(where
S_{n}
denotes
n\times n
nonnegative definite matrices) such that for almost
every
z_{0}\in\Omega_{t}

\overline{u}(z)=\overline{u}(z_{0})+\langle\nabla\overline{u}(z_{0}),z-z_{0}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}(z-z_{0})^{T}D^{2}\overline{u}(z_{0})(z-z_{0})+o(|z-z_{0}|^{2})

as
z\to z_{0}
.

This is standard for convex and hence semi-convex functions. See
[
4
, Section
6.4]
. It is straightforward to compute the gradient of
\bar{u}

at points of differentiability.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
z\in\Omega_{t}
and let
(x_{0},x_{1})
be optimal
for
z
. If
\overline{u}
is differentiable at
z
, then

\frac{\nabla\overline{u}}{\overline{u}}(z)=(1-t)\frac{\nabla u_{0}}{u_{0}}(x_{0})+t\frac{\nabla u_{1}}{u_{1}}(x_{1})=\frac{\nabla u_{0}}{u_{0}}(x_{0})=\frac{\nabla u_{1}}{u_{1}}(x_{1}).

Proof.

The latter two equalities follow from the first and
(
3
). Next, if
z
is a point of differentiability, then
optimality of
(x_{0},x_{1})
implies

\displaystyle D_{i}\overline{u}(z)

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{\overline{u}(z+he_{i})-\overline{u}(z)}{h}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}h^{-1}\left[u_{0}(x_{0}+he_{i})^{1-t}u_{1}(x_{1}+he_{i})^{t}-u_{0}(x_{0})^{1-t}u_{1}(x_{1})^{t}\right]

\displaystyle=\overline{u}(z)\left[(1-t)\frac{Du_{0}}{u_{0}}(x_{0})+t\frac{Du_{1}}{u_{1}}(x_{1})\right].

The opposite inequality uses
D_{i}\overline{u}=\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}h^{-1}\big(\overline{u}(z)-\overline{u}(z-he_{i})\big)
and a similar argument.
∎

From this formula for the derivatives it’s straightforward to deduce better
regularity for
\bar{u}
.

3.3.2.
Sobolev Regularity

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
u_{i}\in C^{2}(\Omega_{i})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega_{i}})
. Then the
sup-convolution is in
W^{1,p}_{0}
for every
p\geq 1
.

Here, to say
u\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
is to say that there is a
C^{1}
extension of
u
to an open neighbourhood of
\Omega
.

Proof.

Since
\Omega_{t}
is bounded we have the following inclusions

\operatorname{Lip}\subseteq W^{1,\infty}\subseteq\dots W^{1,p}\subseteq\dots W^{1,1}.

From Lemma
3.7
,
\overline{u}
is locally Lipschitz. We just need
to provide a uniform Lipschitz constant to verify that
\overline{u}\in W^{1,p}
for every
p\geq 1
. Then since
\overline{u}
is continuous and

\overline{u}\equiv 0
on the boundary
\partial\Omega_{t}
we will have
\overline{u}\in W^{1,p}_{0}
.

Since
u_{i}
are
C^{1}
up to the boundary,
|\nabla u_{i}|
is uniformly bounded. Since
\overline{u}
is locally Lipschitz, it is differentiable almost
everywhere by Rademacher’s theorem. At points where
\overline{u}
is
differentiable, Corollary
3.8
ensures that

\frac{\nabla\overline{u}(z)}{u_{0}^{1-t}(x_{0})u_{1}^{t}(x_{1})}=\frac{\nabla\overline{u}}{\overline{u}}(z)=\frac{\nabla u_{0}}{u_{0}}(x_{0})=\frac{\nabla u_{1}}{u_{1}}(x_{1}).

In the case that
u_{1}(x_{1})\leq u_{0}(x_{0})
we have

|\nabla\overline{u}(z)|=\left|\frac{u_{1}^{t}(x_{1})}{u_{0}^{t}(x_{0})}\nabla u_{0}(x_{0})\right|\leq\left|\nabla u_{0}(x_{0})\right|\leq\left\|\nabla u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Similarly, if
u_{0}(x_{0})\leq u_{1}(x_{1})
then
\left|\nabla\overline{u}(z)\right|\leq\left\|\nabla u_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Thus
\overline{u}
is locally Lipschitz with almost-everywhere uniformly
bounded gradient, hence is Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant bounded by
\max\{\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\}
.
∎

3.3.3.
BV Gradient

Lemma
3.9
gives first order regularity for
\overline{u}
. However in general
\overline{u}
is not in
W^{2,1}
. Nevertheless, semiconvexity
gives an integration by parts inequality. The following lemmas, the first of which is well-known but difficult to find in the literature, complete the justification for the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\overline{u}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
be any semiconvex function. For
\epsilon>0
set
\overline{u}_{\epsilon}=\eta_{\epsilon}\ast\overline{u}
where
\eta_{\epsilon}
is the standard mollifier. If
u
has Alexandrov second derivatives at
x\in\Omega
, then

(6)

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}D^{2}\overline{u}_{\epsilon}(x)=D^{2}\overline{u}(x).

Proof.

Using Alexandrov second differentiability

\displaystyle D_{ij}\overline{u}_{\epsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}D_{ij}\eta_{\epsilon}(x-y)\overline{u}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}D_{ij}\eta_{\epsilon}(x-y)\Big[\overline{u}(x)+D\overline{u}(x)\cdot(y-x)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2}D_{kl}\overline{u}(x)(y-x)_{k}(y-x)_{l}+o(|x-y|^{2})\Big]\,dy.

Note the zeroth and first order terms (i.e.,
\overline{u}(x)
and
D\overline{u}(x)\cdot(y-x)
) vanish after integrating by parts on
y
. As do terms
D_{kl}\overline{u}(x)(y-x)_{k}(y-x)_{l}
for which
\{k,l\}\neq\{i,j\}
. Thus, after integrating by parts we’re left with

D_{ij}\overline{u}_{\epsilon}(x)=D_{ij}\overline{u}(x)\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\eta_{\epsilon}(x-y)\,dy+o(\epsilon^{2})\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}D_{ij}\eta_{\epsilon}(x-y)\,dy.

The first integral equals 1. For the second,
|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}D_{ij}\eta_{\epsilon}(x-y)|\leq C\epsilon^{-2}
allows us to take
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
via the dominated convergence theorem and obtain (
6
).
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\overline{u}:\in C^{0}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})
be locally semi-convex and globally Lipschitz. Then
\overline{u}
satisfies the following integration by parts inequality

(7)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}|\nabla\overline{u}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega_{t}}\overline{u}(x)\Delta\overline{u}(x)\,dx,

where
\Delta\overline{u}(x)=\operatorname{Tr}D^{2}\overline{u}(x)
.

Proof.

Fix
\tilde{\Omega}_{t}\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{t}
with
C^{1}
boundary and
\lambda
such that
\overline{u}+\lambda|x|^{2}/2
is convex on every convex subset of
\tilde{\Omega}_{t}
. Let
\overline{u}_{\epsilon}=\eta_{\epsilon}\ast\overline{u}
where
\eta_{\epsilon}
is the standard mollifier. Because the mollification of a convex function is a convex function
[
4
, Proof of Theorem 6.7]
, and this is a local statement,

(8)

D^{2}u_{\epsilon}\geq-\lambda I.

Integrating by parts,

(9)

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{\Omega}_{t}}|\nabla\overline{u}_{\epsilon}|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\partial\tilde{\Omega}_{t}}\overline{u}_{\epsilon}\nabla\overline{u}_{\epsilon}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS-\int_{\tilde{\Omega}_{t}}\overline{u}_{\epsilon}\Delta\overline{u}_{\epsilon}\,dx.

We have

(10)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\partial\tilde{\Omega}_{t}}\overline{u}_{\epsilon}\nabla\overline{u}_{\epsilon}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS\right|\leq C\sup_{\partial\tilde{\Omega}_{t}}|\overline{u}_{\epsilon}|\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\tilde{\Omega}_{t}),

where
C
is the Lipschitz constant of
\overline{u}
(preserved
under mollification). Moreover by (
8
),
\overline{u}_{\epsilon}\Delta\overline{u}_{\epsilon}
is bounded below (independently of
\epsilon
) and, by Lemma
3.10
, converges pointwise almost everywhere to
\overline{u}\Delta\overline{u}
. Thus, Fatou’s lemma implies

(11)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\tilde{\Omega}_{t}}\overline{u}_{\epsilon}\Delta\overline{u}_{\epsilon}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\tilde{\Omega}_{t}}\overline{u}\Delta\overline{u}\,dx.

Substituting (
10
) and (
11
) into (
9
) and sending
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
implies

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{\Omega}_{t}}|\nabla\overline{u}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{\partial\tilde{\Omega}_{t}}|\overline{u}|\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\tilde{\Omega}_{t})-\int_{\tilde{\Omega}_{t}}\overline{u}\Delta\overline{u}\,dx.

Taking a sequence of domains
\tilde{\Omega}_{t}
converging to
\Omega_{t}

gives (
7
) since
\overline{u}|_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\equiv 0
.
