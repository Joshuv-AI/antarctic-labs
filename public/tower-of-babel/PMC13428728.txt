Regression of high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions in women aged 30 years or younger: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Oliver M. Schleicher, Anna K. Dietl, Annika S. Behrens, Lena Brueckner, Sebastian Trnovec, Carla E. Schulmeyer, Patrik Pöschke, Julius Emons, Carol Geppert, Arndt Hartmann, Antje Knöll, Matthias W. Beckmann, Anja Seibold, Frederik A. Stuebs
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo assess the rate and timing of spontaneous regression of high-grade cervical intraepithelial lesions (CIN2/HSIL and CIN3/HSIL) in young women and to identify associated factors.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study included patients aged ≤ 30 years diagnosed with HSIL (CIN2 or CIN3) at the certified dysplasia unit of the Department of Gynecology, University Hospital Erlangen, between April 2014 and November 2025 who underwent observational management. Regression was defined as partial regression (low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion; LSIL) or complete regression (less than LSIL), with histology as the reference standard.Results45 patients with CIN3/HSIL and 38 with CIN2/HSIL were included. In the CIN3/HSIL cohort, regression was observed in 21 of 45 patients (46.7%), including regression to LSIL in 6 (13.3%) and complete regression in 15 (33.3%) cases. The median time to regression (≤ LSIL) was 236 days (IQR, 126–308 days). The CIN2/HSIL cohort showed even higher regression rates, with partial or complete regression in 24 of 38 cases (63.2%). Regression was associated with HPV clearance in both groups and with HPV16 negativity and HPV vaccination in the CIN2/HSIL cohort. No cases of progression to (micro)invasive disease were observed in either group. The probability of regression did not differ between patients aged ≤ 24 and > 24 years.ConclusionIn this selected cohort of women aged ≤ 30 years managed conservatively, spontaneous regression of CIN2/HSIL and CIN3/HSIL occurred frequently, particularly during the first year of follow-up, without progression to invasive disease. HPV clearance, HPV16 and vaccination status may help guide patient selection for observational management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00432-026-06578-7.

Introduction

Cervical cancer remains one of the leading gynecologic malignancies worldwide, ranking second globally and fourth in Germany (Bray et al. 2024; Stuebs et al. 2025). Precursor lesions of cervical cancer are referred to as cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN) and are classified into low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions (LSIL; CIN1) and high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions (HSIL; CIN2 and CIN3). These precancerous lesions develop as a consequence of persistent infection with human papillomavirus (HPV), especially HPV types 16 or 18 (Crow 2012; Reich et al. 2015; Stuebs et al. 2021; Walboomers et al. 1999; Wei et al. 2024). HPV vaccination serves as the primary preventive measure, while cytology- and HPV-based screening programs constitute secondary prevention. Introduced in Germany in 1971, this program has substantially contributed to reducing cervical cancer incidence and mortality (Hellfritsch et al. 2025; Henes et al. 2023, 2026; Hillemanns et al. 2019; Ronckers et al. 2025; Schulmeyer et al. 2023).

The management depends on the severity of the lesion. According to current German guideline recommendations, CIN1/LSIL are generally managed expectantly with follow-up examinations. In cases of CIN2/HSIL, both conservative and surgical approaches may be considered. In cases of CIN3/HSIL, excisional treatment is recommended. The standard treatment approach is conization, performed by a loop electrosurgical excision procedure (LEEP). The management of young women represents a particular clinical challenge. In patients aged ≤ 24 years, CIN2/HSIL should be managed conservatively, and CIN3/HSIL may be managed conservatively under specific conditions, such as complete colposcopic visualization of the lesion and absence of colposcopic or cytological suspicion of invasive disease (Hillemanns et al. 2019). The rationale for this approach is that excisional procedures are associated with an increased risk of pregnancy-related morbidity, including premature rupture of membranes and preterm delivery (Kyrgiou et al. 2006). Additionally, young patients show a higher prevalence of HPV infection and a largely transient course of HPV infection, with most infections clearing spontaneously within a relatively short period of time (Moscicki 2007). HPV clearance has been shown to predict cytological and histological regression (Nobbenhuis et al. 2001). Few studies have investigated spontaneous regression rates in patients with CIN2/HSIL and CIN3/HSIL and the potential factors associated with regression, such as younger age, HPV vaccination, and HPV clearance (Ehret et al. 2023; Lee et al. 2018; Moscicki et al. 2010). However, the available data are heterogeneous, reflecting differences in study design (e.g., observational surveillance versus assessment based on conization specimens) and variations in patient age and cohort size.

The present study aims to evaluate rates of regression and progression in a homogeneous single-center cohort and to analyze potential factors associated with spontaneous regression in women up to the age of 30 years.

Methods

Study design and study cohort

In this retrospective cohort study, all patients aged 18 to 30 years diagnosed with CIN3/HSIL or CIN2/HSIL at the dysplasia unit of the Department of Gynecology at the University Hospital Erlangen between April 2014 and November 2025 were identified. According to current German guidelines, conservative management of HSIL may be considered in women up to 24 years of age. In this analysis, we included patients up to 30 years, given the increasing age at first pregnancy, the pregnancy-related morbidity associated with surgical treatment, and the comparable biological and environmental conditions within this extended population (Landry et al. 2024; Lycke et al. 2023). Patients were excluded in cases of missing follow-up data, primary surgical treatment, or pregnancy at the time of diagnosis. Cohort definition and patient selection

As part of the standard diagnostic workup at our center, all referred patients underwent cervical cytology, HPV testing, and colposcopic evaluation after application of 5% acetic acid to the cervix. Until 2017, HPV testing was performed using the Hybrid Capture 2 (HC2) assay. Thereafter, DNA polymerase chain reaction (PCR)-based assays were used, enabling HPV genotyping and subtype-specific analyses. All CIN2/HSIL and CIN3/HSIL lesions included in the study were histologically confirmed at initial diagnosis. During follow-up, histology was considered the reference standard. When unavailable, cytology and colposcopy were assessed hierarchically, with the most severe finding defining the outcome. For CIN3/HSIL, status was defined as downgrading to CIN2/HSIL, CIN1/LSIL (partial regression), and complete regression (less than LSIL). For CIN2/HSIL, regression status was defined as regression to LSIL (partial regression) or less (complete regression), accordingly. Regression to ≤LSIL was defined as the primary endpoint, since LSIL represents the clinically relevant threshold at which excisional treatment is generally not indicated. This definition is consistent with previous studies reporting regression as ≤CIN1/≤LSIL or absence of CIN2+ (Table 1).

Statistical analysis

This study primarily presents a descriptive analysis of outcomes, focusing on whether spontaneous regression occurred and the time to regression. Time to regression was defined as the interval between initial diagnosis and the first documented regression to ≤ LSIL. Categorical variables were summarized as counts and percentages and compared between patients with and without regression. Fisher’s exact test was used for categorical comparisons due to small expected cell counts. Patients with missing values were excluded from the respective analyses. All tests were two-sided, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant. Given the exploratory nature of the analysis, no multiple-comparison adjustment was applied. Data analysis and graph generation were performed using GraphPad Prism (version 10.3.0).

Results

From an initial cohort of 629 CIN3/HSIL and 255 CIN2/HSIL cases, a total of 83 patients were identified and included in the analysis. Of these, 45 had biopsy-confirmed CIN3/HSIL, and 38 had CIN2/HSIL (Fig. 1). The median age at diagnosis was 24 years in the CIN3/HSIL group and 25 years in the CIN2/HSIL group, respectively. High-risk HPV positivity was common in both cohorts, whereas HPV16 positivity was more prevalent in CIN3/HSIL cases. At least one dose of HPV vaccine had been received by 37.8% of patients with CIN3/HSIL and 44.7% of those with CIN2/HSIL; detailed baseline characteristics are shown in Table 2.

Figure 2 illustrates regression rates and time to regression. The median follow-up duration was 300 days (IQR 167, 435) in the overall cohort. In the CIN3/HSIL group, the median follow-up was 305 days (IQR 176, 438), with a maximum of 809 days; in the CIN2/HSIL group, it was 268 days (IQR 152, 393), with a maximum of 875 days. In the CIN3/HSIL group, 46.7% of cases showed no regression of the lesion. No invasive carcinoma was observed in this cohort. Complete regression occurred in 33.3% of cases, while regression to LSIL was observed in 13.3%. The median time to regression was 236 days, with the longest observed time to regression exceeding 600 days. Supplementary Fig. 1 provides an overview of the time to spontaneous regression in patients with CIN3/HSIL across different subgroups. Patients who were negative for high-risk HPV at diagnosis exhibited a shorter time to regression, with a median of 111 days, compared with 265 days in patients who were positive for high-risk HPV. In the CIN2/HSIL group, regression was observed in 63.2% of patients, including partial regression in 21.1% and complete regression in 42.1% of cases. Progression to CIN3/HSIL occurred in 3 cases (7.9%), and no invasive carcinoma was observed. The median time to first regression to ≤LSIL was 245 days (IQR 142, 412).

Discussion

A considerable proportion of patients with CIN2/HSIL, but also with CIN3/HSIL, demonstrated spontaneous regression in our study, most of which occurred within the first year. In the CIN2/HSIL cohort, HPV 16 negativity at diagnosis, HPV clearance during follow-up, and vaccination were associated with regression and could serve as useful criteria for patient selection in a conservative management approach. A conservative observational management approach for young patients with HSIL has already been implemented in several international guidelines. Prerequisites are that the lesion is fully assessable by colposcopy, contains no atypical glandular component, invasive disease can be excluded with a high degree of certainty, and that close follow-up is ensured (Hillemanns et al. 2019; Perkins et al. 2020; Rafi and Khalil 2024). This approach is based on the association of excisional treatment with pregnancy-related morbidity, while spontaneous regression rates of more than 50% have been reported, particularly for CIN2/HSIL (Table 1). Our data support this approach, with partial or complete regression observed in 63.2% of cases in our cohort.

The available evidence for CIN3/HSIL is limited and heterogeneous. Spontaneous regression rates of CIN3/HSIL are typically reported to range from 1% to 38% (Bruno et al. 2022; Ehret et al. 2023; Lee et al. 2018; Munk et al. 2007; Perrine et al. 2025). However, the available studies differ considerably in their methodological approaches, including differences in patient age, cohort size, and follow-up design (Table 1). Some studies do not clearly distinguish between CIN2/HSIL and CIN3/HSIL lesions. In other studies, regression is estimated based on the interval between diagnostic biopsy and subsequent LEEP rather than an observational approach, leading to comparatively short observation periods. For example, in the study of Bruno et al., 97% of patients underwent treatment within 12 weeks after biopsy (Bruno et al. 2022). In our study, higher regression rates were observed in the CIN3/HSIL cohort, with 47% of patients regressing to ≤LSIL. A more recent retrospective analysis by Ehret et al. also reported comparatively high regression rates in patients younger than 25 years, with approximately 30% of patients showing regression. Methodologically, this analysis is similar to our study, including a long follow-up period of up to 70 months. However, in their study 27.1% of patients with CIN3/HSIL underwent surgery within three months after diagnosis without a preceding watch and wait period (Ehret et al. 2023). These patients with primary surgical management were excluded from our analysis. This is particularly relevant, as previous studies suggest that regression is observed more frequently when the interval after biopsy exceeds 11 weeks (Bruno et al. 2022). Early surgical treatment may therefore have reduced the likelihood of capturing spontaneous regression. Furthermore, the proportion of HPV-vaccinated patients in our overall cohort was almost twice as high as that reported by Ehret et al. (41.0% vs. 21.9%), which may partly reflect the more recent study period and could have influenced the observed regression rates (Ehret et al. 2023). Additional factors may also contribute to the relatively high regression rate observed in our study. Our cohort represents a highly selected population of young patients treated at a specialized dysplasia unit, and an observational approach was only considered under strict selection criteria, including lesions that were fully assessable and therefore likely less extensive. Previous studies have demonstrated that lesion characteristics are associated with the likelihood of spontaneous regression. In particular, lesions confined to a single quadrant have been reported to exhibit higher rates of spontaneous regression (Perrine et al. 2025). Furthermore, smaller lesions may already be partially or completely removed during the diagnostic biopsy. It should also be acknowledged that diagnostic biopsy itself may have contributed to the observed regression rates, as biopsy-induced tissue disruption has been hypothesized to trigger local immune activation (Trimble et al. 2005), although available evidence suggests that regression is primarily a time-dependent immunological process rather than a direct biopsy effect (Munk et al. 2007).

The data from our study support the recommendations outlined in the current German guideline for adolescents, which state that in cases of persistent CIN2/HSIL for more than 24 months or CIN3/HSIL for more than 12 months, therapeutic intervention should be considered (Hillemanns et al. 2019). The time intervals observed in our cohort are consistent with the existing literature (Bekos et al. 2018; Moscicki et al. 2010). Altogether, the majority of regressions occurred within the first year of follow-up, with 80% of patients in the CIN3/HSIL cohort. All but one patient in the CIN2/HSIL group showed regression within two years. HSIL lesions retain a significant potential for spontaneous regression, appropriate patients should be counseled about the option of conservative, observational management.

Various parameters associated with achieving regression in HSIL have been described in the literature (Table 1). In our analysis, clearance of high-risk HPV was associated with regression in both CIN3/HSIL and CIN2/HSIL. In the CIN3/HSIL cohort, the median time to regression was less than half as long in patients with high-risk HPV negativity at diagnosis compared to those with high-risk HPV positivity. Patients with CIN2/HSIL and HPV high-risk genotype 16 showed lower rates of spontaneous regression. Notably, HPV type 16 infection was more than three times as frequent in patients with CIN3/HSIL compared to those with CIN2/HSIL (26.7% vs. 7.9%), further supporting its association with higher-grade lesions (Clifford et al. 2003). The effectiveness of HPV vaccination has been well established, with protection rates of up to 100% against HPV-associated lesions (Garland Suzanne et al. 2007). In both the CIN2/HSIL and CIN3/HSIL cohorts, regression was observed more frequently among vaccinated than unvaccinated patients, with a significant difference observed in the CIN2/HSIL cohort (82.4% vs. 44.4%). Our results may still underestimate the effectiveness of HPV vaccination, as in our study, all patients who had received any HPV vaccination were classified as vaccinated, regardless of whether the vaccination series was completed or whether vaccination had occurred prior to the onset of sexual activity. In addition, within the CIN3/HSIL cohort, among vaccinated patients with available HPV subtyping, 9 out of 10 were infected with high-risk HPV types other than 16/18. Similarly, in the CIN2/HSIL cohort, all patients (13/13) were infected with HPV types other than 16/18. This distribution may have influenced the observed vaccination effect, as earlier vaccination strategies often used the bivalent vaccine targeting HPV 16 and 18. In line with the findings of Ehret et al. (Ehret et al. 2023), who reported similar results, repeated high-risk HPV testing during follow-up examinations may be performed to assess potential HPV clearance as a predictor of regression. Given the design of our study, the observed association does not allow conclusions regarding causality or the exact temporal sequence between viral clearance and lesion regression. However, prospective and longitudinal studies have shown that HPV clearance precedes cytological regression and that a sustained decline in type-specific viral load is associated with regression of cervical lesions, supporting the biological plausibility of HPV clearance as a marker during observational follow-up (Depuydt et al. 2015; Nobbenhuis et al. 2001; Zhou et al. 2025).

Some national and international guidelines provide recommendations regarding the management of young women with HSIL, with several distinguishing between patients younger and older than 25 years (Hillemanns et al. 2019; Perkins et al. 2020). This age threshold is largely based on the biological characteristics of HPV infection in younger women, including higher rates of viral clearance and a stronger immune response (Moscicki et al. 2010) and published cohort data (Bekos et al. 2018). In the present study, patients aged 30 years or younger were included in the analysis to capture a broader group of young women who may still share similar biological and epidemiological characteristics. Notably, no significant difference in regression rates was observed between patients younger than 25 years and those aged 25 years or older in our cohort. However, this finding should be interpreted with caution given the limited sample size, the absence of multivariable analyses, and the selected population of our analysis. Therefore, our data are not intended to justify immediate changes in clinical decision-making, but may serve as a basis for further studies evaluating whether carefully selected young patients (e.g. vaccinated, HPV HR negativity) up to 30 years may also be suitable for observational management in experienced dysplasia centers.

Conclusion

The retrospective design, the relatively small cohort size, and the highly selected nature of this conservatively managed population clearly limit the generalizability of the results. Between 80% and 90% of patients with CIN2/HSIL or CIN3/HSIL underwent immediate surgical treatment and were therefore excluded from this analysis. Therefore, potential selection bias must be considered, as only patients with fully assessable and likely less extensive lesions were managed conservatively. Consequently, the observed regression rates may not be directly applicable to the broader population of women with CIN2/HSIL or CIN3/HSIL.

Some lesions may have been treated surgically that might otherwise have undergone spontaneous regression, and detailed information on lesion size and the number of affected quadrants was not consistently available and could therefore not be included in the analysis. Nevertheless, the data represent a homogeneous and well-defined patient population that was managed and closely monitored at a single center with extensive expertise. Importantly, the cohort reflects a truly observational approach with a follow-up interval up to 28 months. Spontaneous regression of CIN2/HSIL and selected CIN3/HSIL lesions was observed in a substantial proportion of women aged ≤ 30 years, most commonly within the first year of follow-up. No cases of invasive disease occurred during observation. While these findings support conservative management in carefully selected patients, prospective studies are needed to confirm the safety of extending observational strategies beyond currently recommended age groups and to validate HPV-related predictors of regression.
