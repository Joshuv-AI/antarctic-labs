 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

 The Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) team performed an evaluation of the Sensos Smart
Label Gen 2.0, as shown in Figure 1, for package tracking. A long-distance round-trip shipment between
Oak Ridge, Tennessee, and Seattle, Washington, was completed to assess the device’s performance in
location tracking, environment sensing capabilities, alerting features, threshold options, battery life, and
real-time and historical data retrieval from the “Sync” data dashboard provided by Sensos. The evaluation
was conducted to gain a general understanding of the capabilities of the device. Furthermore, due to time
and resource constraints, ORNL did not conduct exhaustive testing to confirm reliability, availability, or
effectiveness of alerting and tracking features.

 On equipment arrangement, Sensos (sensos.ai) graciously agreed to provide a Sensos Smart Label
Gen 2.0 device to ORNL, at no cost, for testing and evaluation purposes. ORNL conducted assessments
along with other commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) tracking devices. As a courtesy, ORNL will provide
Sensos with this report summarizing the observations and findings specific to the Sensos label based on
the tests performed.
 1. DEVICE SETUP

 The Sensos Smart Label Gen 2.0 was prepared by communicating with a team at Sensos, who
shared a link to create an account to their dashboard called Sync, pictured in Figure 2. The Sensos Sync
platform allows users to configure device settings, sensor thresholds, points of interest (POIs), and
geofences. The Sync dashboard was intuitive, with clear descriptions and documentation. The dashboard
allows the user to view the location of the device from its last update and alerts throughout a journey.
Alerts can be monitored from the Alert Center and Shipment Journey Page. Users can define threshold
configurations and geofences on the Sets & POIs page.

 Figure 1. Smart Label Gen 2.0

 Additionally, a data log can be downloaded from the dashboard. Each message in the log includes
its message number, date, timestamp, event, event details, alert status, state, country, longitude, and
latitude, as shown in Figure 3. The event column describes the reason for the message, with the regular
updates being categorized under “Temperature Alert.” Event details are provided when the device detects
a measurement outside of a defined threshold. The alert column indicates whether the message is
generated by an out-of-threshold condition.

 2. LONG-DISTANCE SHIPMENT

 To simulate a practical shipment scenario, the ORNL team coordinated a round-trip journey from
Oak Ridge, Tennessee, to Seattle, Washington and back. The device was activated on July 22, 2025, eight
days before the trip, to learn the dashboard and device configurations. Before the trip, the journey settings
were configured through the Sensos Sync dashboard. Configuration includes setting origin, destination,
sensor thresholds, and geofences. The conditions monitored during the shipment were light, temperature,
location, shock, dwelling, and unauthorized stops. The thresholds manually configured for this evaluation
based on our common environment conditions are defined in Table 1. For example, for temperature, we
chose a range that matches a normal room or car setting in the summer. The physical conditions of the
device were also noted before departure for later comparison. The journey from Oak Ridge to Seattle
occurred July 30 - August 5, 2025. The shipment from Seattle to Oak Ridge took place August 7 - August
13, 2025. The device was transported by ground in a FedEx package. Throughout the journeys, the
dashboard was monitored daily to track real-time location, device connection, alerts, and battery life.
When the package reached each destination, Seattle and Oak Ridge, the battery status for the device was
documented, and the device was inspected for any physical transit-related wear. As displayed in Figure 4,
no damage was visible on the device after arriving at each destination. At the end of the shipments, data
logs were downloaded from the dashboard for additional analysis of alerts and battery performance.

2.1 Analysis of Alerts

 Before the shipment, alert thresholds were configured through the device’s dashboard. These
thresholds were defined as is observable in Table 1. All Sensos alerts were received through email and
detailed the location, time, and trigger for the alert. The journey from Oak Ridge to Seattle generated 21
alerts, while the trip from Seattle to Oak Ridge produced 20 alerts. According to the resulting data log
across the entire round-trip, there were three light breaches, 19 unauthorized stops, three dwell alerts, 14
temperature breaches, one shipment arrival alert, and one shipment departure alert.

 3

 Sensos provides both “unauthorized stop” and “dwell” alerts. The unauthorized stop alert is
triggered if the device has been stationary in one location outside defined geofences for a configurable
amount of time, which was set to 30 minutes for this test. The dwell alert is triggered if the device is
stationary for at least one day. These two alerts are similar but distinguished by the unauthorized stop
condition being dependent on defined, authorized locations. Together, these alerts supplied additional
knowledge about the package’s location and status at moments during the shipment. Below is a summary
of observations of each sensing capability.

 - Light: The light-sensing feature for the Sensos Smart Label proved consistent with device
 specifications in the context of detecting the opening of a package. While ORNL did not evaluate
 how much light was required for alerting, the device alerted when the evaluation team opened the
 package as expected. The light alerts recorded from the Sensos Smart Label were consistent with
 the package containing the device being opened at the origin and destination, exposing the device
 to light. No other light alerts occurred during the evaluation.

 - Temperature: The temperature sensor seemed to perform as intended, as it was the second-most-
 frequent type of alert recorded. ORNL did not evaluate the effectiveness of the temperature
 sensor. We did not test whether the device generated false alerts, or whether it alerted at the exact
 temperature set in the threshold.

 - Location/Geofence: The Sync dashboard indicated Shipment Arrival and Departure based on the
 device’s location/geofence. No other geofence alerts were generated, other than arrival and
 departure notifications. However, the unauthorized stop alerts rely on the defined geofences, as
 they are triggered only while outside of the points of interest.

 - Shock: The Sensos Smart Label did not produce shock alerts during this journey, indicating that
 the shipment did not experience forces above 16G during each journey.

 - Dwell and Unauthorized Stop: The Sensos Smart Label’s dwell and unauthorized stop alerts
 seemed to properly identify moments when the device was stationary. Unauthorized Stops
 provided the most alerts for the Sensos device.

2.2 Analysis of Battery Performance

 A significant outcome of this long-distance shipment is the ability to assess the battery
performance over an extended period. Battery life is affected by the reporting frequency and event-driven
alerts. Lowering the reporting frequency can improve battery performance over time. The device has the
option to automatically reduce the reporting frequency when the device is stationary. For this evaluation,
we configured the reporting frequency to switch to once every hour when the device was stationary,
ensuring regular location reports while conserving battery when the device is at rest. Additionally, the
reporting interval can be altered manually, mid-shipment, to receive data more frequently or to preserve
battery life by reducing the frequency. Our team did not test this feature for this evaluation. For this
round-trip journey, we configured the device to report location updates at a 15-minute interval, along with
any conditional alerts triggered outside the interval. The 15-minute interval was chosen to ensure frequent
location updates.

 The Sensos online user interface does not display a battery percentage for the Smart Label.
Instead, there is a battery icon on their shipment page, indicating “Full,” “High,” or “Offline” when a user
hovers over it with a mouse cursor. More detailed battery monitoring features are in development,
according to Sensos representatives. The Sync dashboard indicated that the battery was “Full” when

departing Oak Ridge for Seattle and “High” when departing Seattle for Oak Ridge. The battery was
“Offline” upon arrival at Oak Ridge.

 From activation before the shipments to battery depletion, the device lasted approximately 508.5

 3. LIFECYCLE

 The device is advertised as having a shelf-life of up to three years [1]. Operational life is three
months, [2] but depending on reporting frequency and how much the device is used, it can be used for up
to two years [1]. ORNL did not perform an evaluation to confirm this data. ORNL assessed neither older
models nor their ability to integrate with the Sync dashboard. Future investigations could explore the
upward integration of the devices to the dashboard. The Sensos technical specifications list the device
dimensions as 4.21 inches by 2.83 inches [1]. Our measurements confirm these dimensions.

 4. SUMMARY

 The Sensos Smart Label is a small, lightweight, non-rechargeable, disposable device used to track
and monitor goods during transport. Labels are typically bought in bulk to use for multiple shipments.
The label provides several features users may find of value for their specific types of shipments. It also
has a foldable tamper-detection tab that is placed along the edge of a package and will alert when the
package is opened. As the primary focus was on location tracking and environment sensing capabilities,
the tamper detection tab was not evaluated in this test. According to Sensos representatives, an
Application Programming Interface (API) is available [3] for accessing shipment and sensor data, at an
additional cost, based on the intended scope and the volume of labels required. The device’s performance
is summarized below.

 5

- Location tracking: Updates from the device are reported based on a user-defined time interval.
 The Sync dashboard displays a map of the journey. The data log contains latitude and longitude
 values for each message.

- Environment sensing capabilities: The device can sense temperature, light, and shock. Shock
 alerts were not generated during this testing.

- Alerting features: The device alerts when sensor measurements are outside of a user-defined
 threshold. The device detects unauthorized stops and dwell time based on location and
 movement. Users also are alerted for shipment arrival and departure. Alerts are displayed on the
 dashboard, and email notifications can be configured.

- Threshold options: Users can configure thresholds for receiving alerts through the Sync
 dashboard.

- Battery life: The device lasted 500+ hours during its lifecycle, while reporting at 15-minute
 intervals during the journey. Battery percentage data would be beneficial for monitoring the
 lifecycle of the device.

- Real-time and historical data retrieval: The Sync dashboard allows users to monitor shipments,
 and a data log can be downloaded to view historical data, including alerts and location.
