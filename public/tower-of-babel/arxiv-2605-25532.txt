A New Self-Dual Gravitational Instanton Solution on a Local Conformal Kählerian Manifold in a Brane World Model
Reinoud Jan Slagter

Abstract
It is believed that black holes remain a clean laboratory for probing ideas about quantum gravity. Decades of work on these obscure objects have shown they behave like ordinary thermodynamic systems with temperature and entropy, or to the extreme, even as elementary particles. It remains a huge challenge to reconcile the large-scale properties with the underlying quantum description. In this manuscript we extend on former research on the exact time-dependent instanton solution on a vacuum Kerr-like warped spacetime in conformal dilaton gravity, where one writes g μ ​ ν = ω 4 / ( n − 2 ) ​ g ~ μ ​ ν g_{\mu\nu}=\omega^{4/(n-2)}\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} , with g ~ \tilde{g} considered as ’un-physical’ and ω \omega a dilaton ’gravi’-scalar. Remarkably, the metric solution results from a first-order PDE, allowing the connection with self-duality. The singular points are determined by a quintic polynomial. This suggests that this is the highest possible polynomial in describing the singularities of black holes of Petrov type D axially symmetric manifolds and don’t fits the Plebanski-Demianski classification of black holes which is determined by a fourth order polynomial. The solution can be described by a locally conformal Kählerian manifold with Euclidean signature and a Kähler potential. As such, the self-dual nature makes the comparison with the Yang-Mills instanton counterpart solution interesting. This is possible for the effective 4D manifold, despite the fact that a Kähler manifold in 5D cannot be modeled. If fact, we are dealing with an effective 4D self-dual Kähler manifold with a recurrent conformal structure. It is conjectured that it represents a gravitational instanton. This happened by the projected Weyl tensor of 5D on the brane. The so-called Kaluza-Klein contribution contains information on bulk gravity waves and effects the evolution of the brane geometry. We also used the two-fold Riemannian cover ℂ ​ P 2 \mathbb{C}P^{2} by the Klein topology. The topology of the gravitational instanton would be S 3 × ℝ / ℤ 2 S^{3}\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}_{2} . The antipodal boundary condition on the hyper-surface of a Klein bottle ∼ ℂ 1 × ℂ 1 \sim\mathbb{C}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{1} is applied to describe the Hawking particles during the evaporation process. We used the Hopf fibration to get S 2 S^{2} as the black hole horizon, where the centrix is not in a torus but in the Klein bottle. The twist fits very well with the antipodal identification of the points on the horizon. No ’cut and past’ is necessary, so the Hawing particles remain pure without instantaneous information transport. We also suspect that the compact objects recently observed – the so-called ‘little red dots’ in the very early universe – are linked to primordial black holes created by instantons. Finally, we reveal a connection between the description of the interior of our new black hole solution and the similar model proposed by Janis, Newman and Winicour some time ago of the Schwarzschild solution in complex coordinates with a zero rest mass scalar field, which develops a anomalous asymmetry. The model can be easily extended to the non-vacuum situation by including a scalar field. Both the dilaton (or gravi-scalar) and the scalar field can be treated as quantum fields when approaching the Planck area.

A long time ago, Israel and Penrose formulated already the possible transition from Schwarzschild to Kerr and visa versa in relation to the point singularity.
The Schwarzschild solution is the only vacuum solution that has a non-singular event horizon. A singular event horizon has to do with the the conjecture that a asymmetric collapsing body must first radiate away all its higher multipole moments. Israels theorem prohibits the development of trapped surfaces due to the formation of singularities at the event horizon. If there is a singular event horizon, then collapse trough it becomes impossible, thus obviating Penroses theorem.

Let us summarize his method.
We recall the following well-known fact. Given any differentiable fibre, where the base space and the fibre are equipped with Riemannian metrics and the structural group G acts isometrically on the fibres, then the specification of a connection on the fibre that is compatible with the action of the structural group determines a Riemannian metric in the total fibre space, characterised solely by the following properties:
1. the metric induced on the fibre above any point in the base is equivalent to the metric given in the abstract fibre by any structural isomorphism between the two;
2. at every point on the fibre, any tangent vector that is horizontal with respect to the connection is orthogonal to the fibre passing through that point;
3. the projection of the fibre onto the base space is a Riemannian surjection (submersion), compatible everywhere with the metric given in the base space.
On the other hand, if the Riemannian structure of the base space and that of the fibres are Kählerian structures, the same construction of a metric structure on the total fibre space yields only a quasi-Hermitian structure, which in general is not integrable into a complex structure and, even if it is, the defined metric is Kählerian only in very rare cases; for example, in the case of holomorphic vector fibres, a Hermitian structure constructed in this way is Kählerian only if the curvature of the connection is identically zero.
Let M be an n-dimensional complex manifold, compact or otherwise, equipped with a Kähler metric, which can be represented locally either by its Riemannian form

ds^{2}=g_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}(z,\bar{z})dz^{\alpha}d\bar{z}^{\beta}

(4.32)

or by its exterior form

K=ig_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}(z,\bar{z})dz^{\alpha}\wedge d\bar{z}^{\beta}

(4.33)

The Kähler condition can be expressed, equivalently, either by the fact that the form
K
is closed, or by the local existence of a Kähler potential function
{\cal K}
, unique up to a harmonic function, such that

g_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}=\frac{\partial^{2}{\cal K}_{M}}{\partial z^{\alpha}\partial\bar{z}^{\beta}},\quad or\quad K=-i\bar{\partial}\partial{\cal K}

(4.34)

More generally, we say that an exterior form
K
on any complex manifold is of Kähler type if it is a
(1+1)
-degree form, closed and taking only real values on any pair of real tangent vectors.

E. Some remarkable axi-symmetric solutions and the JN trick

It is well known that any rotating black hole solution in 4D must be stationary axisymmetric. And even in 5D it is conjectured that this is true.

It is clear that one should like to find an exact solution for the interior of the spinning axial symmetric objects such as the Kerr solution.
It is now generally accepted that the internal solution of a stationary cosmic string with the known exterior exact solution causes problems with matching conditions and with the weak energy condition (see also appendix E4). In any case, some additional field must be added, for example a scalar-gauge field.
For the Kerr case, many attempts were made. It often turns out that an extra parameter emerge in the solutions, which could be related to the geometry of the of the spacetime.

I believe that it is not possible to find such a solution without a drastically change in notion of the topology of the interior.
