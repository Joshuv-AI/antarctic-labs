Abstract
Online Feedback Optimization leverages properties of optimization algorithms to develop controllers for systems with limited model availability, which is often the case in process control. The interplay between the parameters of the chosen optimization algorithm, as well as lack of direct connection to the characteristics of the underlying process make their tuning challenging. We propose a method for adaptive tuning of Online Feedback Optimization controllers based on scaled projected gradient descent by using sensitivity of the desired objective to the parameters of the algorithm. The proposed adaptive tuning method limits the operator-tunable parameters to scalar values that represent how much the control inputs and the objective can change between iterations without requiring either additional information about the controlled system or repeated experiments. Numerical studies on a gas lift and a continuously-stirred tank reactor processes confirm that our adaptive scheme improves closed-loop performance of Online Feedback optimization compared to standard manual tuning methods.

1
Introduction

Online Feedback Opitmization (OFO) allows reaching the optimal operating points while satisfying constraints and relying on limited model information
(
Hauswirth et al. 2024
)
, which makes it a promising method for process control applications. The closed-loop performance of these controllers is determined by the parameters of the optimization algorithms, which are disconnected from the properties of the controlled system and usually need to be tuned through a series of experiments. To simplify tuning of these parameters, this paper proposes an adaptive method for choosing the parameters of Online Feedback Optimization removing the need for repeated experiments.

1.0.1
Motivation

Online Feedback Optimization based on scaled projected gradient descent has been proposed as a controller by
Häberle et al. 2020
, with performance determined by two parameters: a scalar step size and a scaling matrix. Several methods have been proposed for adapting the scaling matrices in optimization algorithms, especially for unconstrained problems
(
Hazan 2019
, Chapter 5.6)
. However, OFO relies on real-time measurements and limited model information, which precludes the use of line-search strategies
(
Häberle et al. 2020
)
or Hessian information
(
Bonettini et al. 2008
;
Bertsekas 2016
)
. In practical uses of OFO, choosing the parameters requires multiple runs of the system
(
Gil et al. 2023
;
Zagorowska et al. 2024
)
, or sufficient knowledge about the underlying system
(
Ortmann et al. 2024
)
.
Bernstein et al. 2023
proposed an adaptive tuning method for the step size for steady-state problems with quadratic objective functions with linear constraints, while
Huang and Grammatico 2025
also assumed linear dynamics of the underlying system. They used the derivatives with respect to problem variables as indicators whether the step should be increased or decreased. We build on these approaches and propose an online tuning method for nonlinear systems arising in process control applications.

1.0.2
Contribution

In this work, we propose an adaptive method for choosing the scaling matrix and the step size to improve closed-loop performance and reduce the operator-tunable parameters to scalar values. We avoid additional experiments by approximating the derivatives with respect to parameters using known derivatives with respect to inputs and outputs that are used in standard OFO controllers. The scaling matrix is found by solving an auxiliary semi-definite optimization problem that preserves its properties, while the step size comes from a quadratic approximation of the objective inspired by
Bertsekas 2016
.

This paper is structured as follows. Section
2
introduces the background of OFO and the formulation used in this work. Section
3
introduces the adaptive tuning method, which is validated in numerical examples in Section
4
. Section
5
closes the paper with suggestions for future work.

2.2
Sensitivity of Online Feedback Optimization

Our adaptive tuning method leverages the sensitivity of the objective
\Phi
in (
1a
) to the scaling matrix
S
and the step size studied by
Zagorowska and Imsland 2025
. From (
1a
) and the chain rule we obtain at any time step
k\in\mathds{N}_{+}
,
l\in\mathds{N}_{\leq k}
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi^{k}}{\partial S^{l}}=

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\Phi^{k}}{\partial u^{k}}+\frac{\partial\Phi^{k}}{\partial y^{k}}\cdot\frac{\partial y^{k}}{\partial u^{k}}\right)\cdot\frac{\partial u^{k}}{\partial S^{l}}.

(4)

To obtain
\frac{\partial u^{k}}{\partial S^{l}}
, we first get from (
2
):

\displaystyle u^{k}=

\displaystyle{}u^{0}+\sum\limits_{l=0}^{k-1}\alpha^{l}w^{l},

(5)

where
w^{l}\in\mathds{R}^{n_{u}}
is obtained from (
3
) and, as such, also depends on
S^{0},\ldots,S^{l}
. By differentiating (
5
) with respect to
S^{k}
, we get:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{k}}{\partial S^{l}}=

\displaystyle{}\underbrace{\frac{\partial u^{0}}{\partial S^{l}}}_{=0}+\alpha^{0}\underbrace{\frac{\partial w^{0}}{\partial S^{l}}}_{=0}+\ldots+\alpha^{l}\frac{\partial w^{l}}{\partial S^{l}}+\ldots+\alpha^{k-1}\frac{\partial w^{k-1}}{\partial S^{l}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{}\sum\limits_{m=l}^{k-1}\alpha^{m}\frac{\partial w^{m}}{\partial S^{l}},

(
6
)

because
u_{0}
is independent of
S^{l}
and
w^{m}
is independent of
S^{l}
for
m<l
. Stacking the columns of
S
vertically into
p^{l}=\text{vec}(S^{l})
enables using the sensitivity formulas from
Zagorowska and Imsland 2025
to obtain
\frac{\partial w^{m}}{\partial S^{l}}
.

3
Adaptive tuning

The adaptive method in this work is inspired by iterative feedback tuning algorithms developed for PID controllers
(
Hjalmarsson et al. 1998
;
Hjalmarsson 2002
)
. These algorithms use sensitivity with respect to controller parameters to find optimal tuning values, but require a series of experiments to obtain derivatives of the objective with respect to individual controller parameters.
We build on the assumption that OFO requires only the input-output sensitivity
\nabla h
and exploit the sensitivity formulas from Section
2.2
to adapt
S
and
\alpha
online.

3.1
Scaling matrix adaptation

Following
Bertsekas 2016
, one of the goals in adapting the scaling matrix in gradient-based optimization is to ensure decrease of the objective between consecutive iterations:

\Phi(u^{k+1},h(u^{k+1}))\leq\Phi(u^{k},h(u^{k})),\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

(7)

If the input-output mapping
h
is known, we can evaluate
\Phi(u^{k+1},h(u^{k+1}))
to find
S
that satisfies (
7
) using existing approaches based on Hessian information
(
Bertsekas 2016
, Section 2.3)
. However, since only the sensitivity
\nabla h
and online measurements
y^{k}
are available, we approach the problem using
u^{k+1}=u^{k}+\alpha_{\max}w^{k}
and
y^{k+1}=y^{k}+\alpha_{\max}\nabla h(u^{k})w^{k}
to approximate (
7
), yielding the approximated monotonic descent condition

\Phi(u^{k}+w^{k},y^{k}+\alpha_{\max}\nabla h(u^{k})w^{k})\leq\Phi(u^{k},y^{k}).

(8)

We notice from (
3
), that at time
k+1
, we note that
u^{k}
and
y^{k}
are known and
\Phi(u^{k+1},y^{k+1})
is a function of
S^{k+1}
. Then (
8
) can be written as:

\Psi(S^{k+1})\leq\Psi(S^{k}).

(9)

We now use the sensitivities from Section
2.2
to approximate
\Psi(S^{k+1})
around the current value
S^{k}
:

\Psi(S^{k+1})\approx\Psi(S^{k})+\Big\langle\frac{\partial\Phi^{k}}{\partial S^{k}},S^{k+1}-S^{k}\Big\rangle_{\text{F}},

(10)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\text{F}}
is the Frobenius inner product
(
Horn and Johnson 2012
)
. By substituting (
10
) into (
9
), we obtain

\Big\langle\frac{\partial\Phi^{k}}{\partial S^{k}},\,S^{k+1}-S^{k}\Big\rangle_{\text{F}}\leq 0,

(11)

which characterizes the condition that
S^{k+1}
should satisfy to ensure monotonic decrease of the objective (
7
).

3.1.1
Optimization-based adaptation

We note that (
11
) is independent of
w^{k+1}
, thus we can find
S^{k+1}
satisfying (
11
) by solving an auxiliary optimization problem:

\displaystyle\min_{p,\,S}\quad

\displaystyle-p

(12a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\Big\langle\frac{\partial\Phi^{k}}{\partial S^{k}},\,S-S^{k}\Big\rangle_{\text{F}}\leq-p,

(12b)

\displaystyle S\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n_{u}},

(12c)

\displaystyle p\in[0,p_{\max}],

(12d)

where
p_{\max}>0
limits how much the objective
\Phi
is allowed to decrease, as indicated by (
10
) and (
11
). To ensure that the scaling matrix stays strictly positive definite in (
12c
), for numerical purposes we introduce
\delta S=S^{k+1}-S^{k}
and recast constraint (
3d
) equivalently as:

\lambda_{j}(S^{k}+\delta S)\geq t,\quad j=1,\ldots,n_{u},

(13)

where
\lambda_{j}(X)
denotes to the
j
-th eigenvalue of matrix
X
and
t\geq t_{\min}>0
is a constant ensuring strict positive definiteness. Rewriting (
13
) as
S^{k}+\delta S-t\mathbb{I}\succcurlyeq 0
allows expressing (
12c
) in
\delta S
that is symmetric but may be indefinite.

Furthermore, if (
12
) admits a solution
S^{k+1}_{*}
, then there are infinitely many solutions, for example, those obtained by scaling
\kappa S^{k+1}_{*}
, with
\kappa>1
. Thus, we restrict
\delta S
using the spectral norm
\|S^{k}+\delta S\|_{2}\leq t_{\max}
, where
t_{\max}>0
is a design parameter. Then (
12
) becomes:

\displaystyle\min_{p,\,\delta S,\,t}\quad

\displaystyle-p-t

(14a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\Big\langle\frac{\partial\Phi^{k}}{\partial S^{k}},\,\delta S\Big\rangle_{\text{F}}\leq-p,

(14b)

\displaystyle S^{k}+\delta S-t\mathbb{I}\succcurlyeq 0,

(14c)

\displaystyle\|S^{k}+\delta S\|_{2}\leq t_{\max},

(14d)

\displaystyle\delta S=\delta S^{\top},\;p\in[0,p_{\max}],\;t\in[t_{\min},t_{\max}].

(14e)

The constraint (
14c
) combined with the modified objective in (
14a
) maximize the smallest eigenvalue of
S^{k}+\delta S
. The choice of
t_{\min}
and
t_{\max}
, as well as the role of the spectral norm will be discussed in Section
3.3
.

3.1.2
Diagonal formulation

Solving (
14
) allows finding a symmetric positive definite
S^{k+1}
that improves the value of the objective by (
14b
). In practice, the scaling matrix is often restricted to diagonal,
S^{k}=\text{diag}(S^{k}_{i})

(
Ortmann et al. 2024
)
. To further facilitate the implementation of OFO without solving (
14
), we propose an adaptation method for this diagonal case.

The condition (
11
) for the
i
-th element of
S^{k+1}
is satisfied in two cases:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi^{k}}{\partial S_{i}^{k}}\leq 0,\;

\displaystyle{}S_{i}^{k+1}-S_{i}^{k}\geq 0,

(15a)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi^{k}}{\partial S_{i}^{k}}\geq 0,\;

\displaystyle{}S_{i}^{k+1}-S_{i}^{k}\leq 0.

(15b)

Thus, if
\Phi
is increasing for
S_{i}^{k}
in (
15b
), we can decrease the corresponding
S_{i}^{k+1}
to avoid going too far from the current value. Conversely, if
\Phi
is decreasing for
S_{i}^{k}
as in (
15a
), we can increase
S_{i}^{k+1}
to reach the optimum more quickly. The proposed tuning rule is then:

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{i}^{k+1}=

\displaystyle{}\begin{cases}(1+\beta_{1})S_{i}^{k}\text{ if }\frac{\partial\Phi^{k}}{\partial S_{i}^{k}}<0\\
(1-\beta_{2})S_{i}^{k}\text{ if }\frac{\partial\Phi^{k}}{\partial S_{i}^{k}}>0,\\
S_{i}^{k}\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

(16a)

\displaystyle S_{i}^{k+1}=

\displaystyle{}\min\Big\{t_{\max},\max\Big\{t_{\min},\tilde{S}_{i}^{k+1}\Big\}\Big\},

(16b)

where
\beta_{1,2}\in(0,1)
are design parameters. The tuning rule (
16
) resembles a simplified two-way backtracking algorithm for scaled projected gradient descent from
Truong and Nguyen 2020
;
Cummins et al. 2025
.

3.2
Step size adaptation

To mitigate the impact of the potential inaccuracies of the approximation (
10
), we propose to adapt the step size
\alpha^{k}
in (
2
) by approximating
\Phi(u^{k+1},y^{k+1})
obtained for
S^{k+1}
from (
14
) as a quadratic function of
\alpha
:

\Phi(u^{k+1}(\alpha),y^{k+1}(\alpha)):=g(\alpha)=a_{k}\alpha^{2}+b_{k}\alpha+c_{k},

(17)

where parameters
a_{k}
,
b_{k}
,
c_{k}
are obtained from a system of linear equations
(
Bertsekas 2016
, Section 1.2)
:

\displaystyle g(\tilde{\alpha})=

\displaystyle{}\Phi(u^{k}+\tilde{\alpha}w^{k},y^{k}+\tilde{\alpha}\nabla h(u^{k})),

(18a)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g}{\partial\alpha}(0)=

\displaystyle{}(H^{\top}(u_{S}^{k})\nabla\Phi^{\top}(u_{S}^{k},y_{S}^{k}))^{\top}w^{k},

(18b)

\displaystyle g(0)=

\displaystyle{}\Phi(u^{k},y^{k}),

(18c)

where
\tilde{\alpha}\in[\alpha_{\min},\alpha_{\max}]
is a chosen value. The approximated value in (
18a
) comes from approximating
y^{k+1}=y^{k}+\alpha^{k}\nabla h(u^{k})w^{k}
. The step size
\alpha^{k+1}
is then obtained by solving the following quadratic optimization problem:

\alpha^{k+1}=\argmin_{\alpha\in[\alpha_{\min},\alpha_{\max}]}g(\alpha),

(19)

with
\tilde{\alpha}=\alpha^{k}
, and
\alpha_{\min},\alpha_{\max}\in\mathds{R}_{+}
describing the range of allowed step sizes.

3.3
Adaptation algorithm

The proposed projected gradient OFO controller with adaptive tuning method is summarized in Algorithm
1
. The adaptation is triggered if the optimum has not yet been reached with
\nabla\Phi(u^{k},y^{k})\neq 0
and
w^{k}\neq 0
(line 2).

The algorithm requires choosing the scalar rparameters
p_{\max}
,
\alpha_{\min}
,
\alpha_{\max}
,
t_{\min}
, and
t_{\max}
. The value of
\alpha_{\max}
indicates how good the linear approximation of
y^{k+1}
in (
3c
) and in (
18a
) is. Ideally,
\alpha_{\max}
should be chosen as a step size that would satisfy constraints in OFO with any

such that
\|S\|_{2}\leq t_{\max}
. The value of the step size
\alpha_{\min}
can be set to a small positive value, here
\alpha_{\min}=10^{-6}

The choice of

is related to the spectral norm properties in (

, the constraint (

modifies both the direction and the length of the gradient

in the projection (

)
(

allows shortening it, effectively reducing the step size. In this work

Conclusions and future works

A controller based on Online Feedback Optimization with scaled projected gradient descent allows reaching the optimum of an objective function while satisfying constraints without needing exact models of the controlled systems. The closed-loop performance of the controllers is defined by the parameters of optimization algorithms, the step size and the scaling matrix, often requiring multiple experiments for manual tuning. In this work, we replace the manual tuning of Online Feedback Optimization based on projected gradient descent with a one-time choice of scalar parameters that describe allowed changes in the inputs and in the objective function for the controlled system. Instead of using experiments to gain information about the influence of the step size and scaling matrix, the proposed method uses sensitivity of the objective to find values providing descent towards the optimum. The sensitivities are then used to solve an auxiliary semi-definite optimization problem to find the scaling matrix online. Numerical examples confirm that the online adaptation allows OFO to reach the optimum more quickly compared to manual tuning, and without additional experiments.

The proposed algorithm relies on approximations of the objective and the constraints, and ongoing work focuses on providing theoretical guarantees of convergence and constraint satisfaction for Online Feedback Optimization with the proposed tuning. Moreover, the tuning method proposed in this work relies on sensitivity of the objective to parameters, so robustifying against real-life noisy measurements will further facilitate practical implementation.
