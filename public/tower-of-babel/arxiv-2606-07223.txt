Homogenization of regularized Oldroyd-type fluids
Florian Oschmann, Jonas Sauer

Abstract
. We study homogenization of a regularized viscoelastic Oldroyd-type model in a periodically perforated bounded domain. The system describes an incompressible non-Newtonian fluid coupled to an elastic extra stress tensor and includes both nonlinear viscosity and nonlinear stress diffusion effects. The governing model, introduced by Kreml, Pokorný, and Šalom (2015), covers Oldroyd-A- and Oldroyd-B-type constitutive laws. We establish qualitative and quantitative homogenization results in suitable scaling regimes and show convergence toward an effective Darcy law on the macroscopic domain. In particular, we prove that, under appropriate assumptions on the scaling parameters, the polymeric stress does not contribute to the effective limit equation. The analysis combines uniform estimates, oscillating test-function techniques, and a relative energy method, and additionally yields a weak–strong uniqueness principle for the viscoelastic system.

Introduction

Flows of complex fluids through porous media arise in a variety of applications, ranging from polymer transport and enhanced oil recovery to biological and industrial processes.
In many such situations, the microscopic geometry of the medium strongly influences the effective macroscopic behavior of the fluid.
Deriving effective large-scale equations from microscopic flow laws is therefore a central problem in homogenization theory.

Flows through porous media are frequently modeled by effective macroscopic equations such as Darcy’s law. A rigorous derivation of such effective models from microscopic fluid equations is a classical problem in homogenization theory. For Newtonian fluids, homogenization results in perforated domains are by now well understood and include both incompressible and compressible Navier–Stokes systems, see e.g.
[

]
and
[

and the references therein.
Recently, also certain classes of non-Newtonian flows have been understood, see e.g.
[

and the references therein.

In contrast, homogenization results for
viscoelastic
fluid models appear to be completely open.
The presence of elastic stresses and their nonlinear coupling with the velocity field introduces substantial additional analytical difficulties, particularly in perforated geometries.
In this work, we address for the first time qualitative and quantitative homogenization of viscoelastic fluids.
More precisely, we study a diffusive non-Newtonian Oldroyd-type model introduced by Kreml, Pokorný, and Šalom
[
11
]
, and investigate its homogenization limit in a periodically perforated domain.

We show that, under suitable scalings, the microscopic dynamics converge to Darcy’s law, while the polymeric stress disappears in the effective macroscopic description.
This is consistent with the underlying asymptotic regime, which corresponds to vanishing inertial and elastic effects in the homogenization limit.
Our first main result is Theorem
3.2
, which establishes convergence of weak solutions toward Darcy’s law in the homogenization limit.
We additionally derive quantitative convergence rates in Theorem
3.4
by means of a relative energy method. This relative energy approach enables us to additionally prove a weak–strong uniqueness principle for the scaled system in Theorem
3.6

The generalized Oldroyd model for a diffusive, time-dependent, non-Newtonian and viscoelastic fluid flow introduced by Kreml, Pokorný, and Šalom in
[
11
]
reads as follows:

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\div\mathbf{v}=0&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\varrho\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\varrho\div(\mathbf{v}\otimes\mathbf{v})+\nabla\pi-\div(\mu(\lambda_{1}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v}))\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v}))=\varrho\mathbf{f}+\div\mathbf{T}&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\mathbf{T}+\zeta\overset{\triangledown}{\mathbf{T}}=2\eta\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v})&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\mathbf{v}=0,\ \partial_{n}\mathbf{T}=0&\text{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
\mathbf{v}(0,\cdot)=\mathbf{v}_{0},\ \mathbf{T}(0,\cdot)=\mathbf{T}_{0}&\text{in }\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v})=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathbf{v}+\nabla^{T}\mathbf{v})
and
{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{T})={\mathbf{W}}\mathbf{T}-\mathbf{T}{\mathbf{W}}+a(\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{T}+\mathbf{T}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v}))
with
{\mathbf{W}}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathbf{v}-\nabla^{T}\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle\overset{\triangledown}{\mathbf{T}}=\partial_{t}\mathbf{T}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{T}-\varepsilon_{1}\div(\gamma(\lambda_{2}\nabla\mathbf{T})\nabla\mathbf{T})-\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{T}).

In this system

denotes the unknown velocity of the fluid,

The function

introduces a nonlinear dependency of the dissipation on the symmetric gradient

, which makes the fluid non-Newtonian.
The term

\varepsilon_{1}>0
governing the size of this regularization, and

The parameter

,
a=0

a=1

The parameter
\eta>0
denotes the polymeric viscosity contribution, while
\lambda_{1}>0
and

In this work, we consider qualitative and quantitative homogenization of this model.

\displaystyle:=\varepsilon^{\lambda}\div(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}\otimes\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon})-\varepsilon^{2}\div(\mu(\We_{1}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}}))\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}})),

In terms of characteristic quantities, this leads for the Strouhal, Euler, Froude, Reynolds, and Weissenberg number as well as for the viscosity ratio to

\displaystyle\Sr=1

At a formal level, one expects that, for suitable parameter regimes (for instance

\langle\mu_{0}M\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{f},\varphi\rangle=0

Since the solenoidal

Since
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{f}
belong to

and hence by Poincaré’s inequality also
\pi

6.
Quantitative convergence

Before presenting the proof of Theorem

Relative energy methods are well established in the study of fluid equations and have been used successfully to obtain quantitative convergence and weak–strong uniqueness results; see, for instance, Theorem 4 in Section 3.4 of
[

for the case without polymeric stress.
The presence of the additional stress variable requires a modified relative energy functional and a corresponding extension of the underlying argument.
The inequality established below forms the key ingredient in the proofs of Theorem

3.6

Lemma 6.1

(Relative energy inequality)

&\quad+\varepsilon^{\lambda}+\varepsilon^{2(\lambda-1)}+[\varepsilon^{\xi(q-1)-\kappa}/\varepsilon_{1}]^{\frac{1}{q-1}}.\end{split}

Let us rewrite the first integral on the right-hand side using the definition of
\mu
from (
5
) and
\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}
:

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{2}\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}):\mu(\We_{1}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}}))\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{2}\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}):\mu_{0}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}})\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{2}\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}):g(\We_{1}|\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}})|)\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{2}\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}):\mu_{0}\mathbf{D}(W_{\varepsilon}\mathbf{v})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{2}\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}):\mu_{0}\mathbf{D}B_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{v})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{2}\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}):g(\We_{1}|\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}})|)\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}})\,\mathrm{d}x.

The second integral is estimated as

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{2}\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}):\mu_{0}\mathbf{D}B_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{v})\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\|\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla B_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{v})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta\varepsilon^{2}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\|\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|(W_{\varepsilon}-\mathbb{I}):\nabla\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta\varepsilon^{2}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\|\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|W_{\varepsilon}-\mathbb{I}\|_{[W^{1,1}]^{\prime}}^{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{2,1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta\varepsilon^{2}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\|\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{4}.

As for the first one,

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{2}\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}):\mu_{0}\mathbf{D}(W_{\varepsilon}\mathbf{v})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\mu_{0}\langle\varepsilon^{2}(-\Delta W_{\varepsilon}+\nabla Q_{\varepsilon}),\mathbf{v}\otimes(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{2}(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\mu_{0}(\Delta(W_{\varepsilon}\mathbf{v})-\Delta W_{\varepsilon}\mathbf{v}-(Q_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v})\,\mathrm{d}x.

We see that, by definition of
(W_{\varepsilon},Q_{\varepsilon})
from Lemma
2.3
, the first term above cancels with the term

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\mu_{0}M\mathbf{v}\,\mathrm{d}x

on the right-hand side of (
19
).
As for the second term, we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{2}(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\mu_{0}(\Delta(W_{\varepsilon}\mathbf{v})-\Delta W_{\varepsilon}\mathbf{v}-(Q_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{2}\|\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta(W_{\varepsilon}\mathbf{v})-\Delta W_{\varepsilon}\mathbf{v}-(Q_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}.

Lastly, we see

\displaystyle|g(s)|\lesssim s+s^{p-2}\mathbf{1}_{p>3}\quad\forall s\geq 0

such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{2}\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}):g(\We_{1}|\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}})|)\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon})|(|\We_{1}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}})|+|\We_{1}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}})|^{p-2}\mathbf{1}_{p>3})|\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}})|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}}(\We_{1}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\We_{1}^{p-2}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2(p-1)}}^{p-1}\mathbf{1}_{p>3})

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta\varepsilon^{2}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\|\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(\We_{1}/\varepsilon)^{2}+(\We_{1}/\varepsilon)^{2(p-2)}\mathbf{1}_{p>3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta\varepsilon^{2}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})\|\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(\We_{1}/\varepsilon)^{2}.

Therefore, estimate (
19
) takes the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon},\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}|\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon},0)+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{\xi}|\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{\kappa+\xi}\varepsilon_{1}\gamma(\nabla\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon})|\nabla\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}2\varepsilon^{2}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})(\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v_{\varepsilon}})-\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}})):(\mu(\We_{1}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v_{\varepsilon}}))\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v_{\varepsilon}})-\mu(\We_{1}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}}))\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}}))\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{\kappa+\xi}\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}:\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon},\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x+E(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon},\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}|\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon},0)+\varepsilon^{2(\lambda-1)}+\varepsilon^{2}+[\varepsilon^{\xi(q-1)-\kappa}/\varepsilon_{1}]^{\frac{1}{q-1}}+(\We_{1}/\varepsilon)^{2},

where we have also used
\lambda\geq\min\{2,2(\lambda-1)\}
in order to estimate
\varepsilon^{\lambda}\leq\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon^{2(\lambda-1)}
.

Finally, we estimate similarly to before

(20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{\kappa+\xi}\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}:\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon},\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{\kappa+\xi}|\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}||\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}||\nabla\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}|\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\lesssim\varepsilon^{\kappa+\xi+1}\|\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v_{\varepsilon}})\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}}+\varepsilon^{\kappa+\xi+1}\|\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v_{\varepsilon}})\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{\kappa+\xi+2}\|\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v_{\varepsilon}})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\varepsilon^{\kappa+\xi}\|\nabla\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}}+\varepsilon^{\kappa+\xi}\|\nabla\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\varepsilon^{\kappa+\xi}(\varepsilon_{1}^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}+\varepsilon_{1}^{-\frac{2}{q-2}})+\delta\varepsilon^{\kappa+\xi}\varepsilon_{1}\|\nabla\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}}^{q},\end{split}

so that (
19
) takes the final form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon},\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}|\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon},0)+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{\xi}|\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{\kappa+\xi}\varepsilon_{1}\gamma(\nabla\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon})|\nabla\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}2\varepsilon^{2}(1-\beta_{\varepsilon})(\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v_{\varepsilon}})-\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}})):(\mu(\We_{1}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v_{\varepsilon}}))\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v_{\varepsilon}})-\mu(\We_{1}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}}))\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u_{\varepsilon}}))\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim E(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon},\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}|\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon},0)+\varepsilon^{2(\lambda-1)}+\varepsilon^{2}+[\varepsilon^{\xi(q-1)-\kappa}/\varepsilon_{1}]^{\frac{1}{q-1}}+[\varepsilon^{(q-1)(\kappa+\xi)}/\varepsilon_{1}]^{\frac{1}{q-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+[\varepsilon^{(q-2)(\kappa+\xi)/2}/\varepsilon_{1}]^{\frac{2}{q-2}}+(\We_{1}/\varepsilon)^{2}.

Grönwall’s inequality then yields for a.e.
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle E(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon},\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon}|\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon},0)(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim E(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon 0},\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon 0}|\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon 0},0)+\varepsilon^{2(\lambda-1)}+\varepsilon^{2}+[\varepsilon^{\xi(q-1)-\kappa}/\varepsilon_{1}]^{\frac{1}{q-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+[\varepsilon^{(q-1)(\kappa+\xi)}/\varepsilon_{1}]^{\frac{1}{q-1}}+[\varepsilon^{(q-2)(\kappa+\xi)/2}/\varepsilon_{1}]^{\frac{2}{q-2}}+(\We_{1}/\varepsilon)^{2}.

Finally, thanks to the estimates on
B_{\varepsilon}
from Lemma
2.4
and Poincaré’s inequality in Lemma
2.1
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla(\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2},

\displaystyle E(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon 0},\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon 0}|\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon 0},0)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{\lambda}\|\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\varepsilon 0}-\mathbf{v}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{\kappa+\xi}\|\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon 0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2},

finishing the proof of Theorem
3.4
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

Instead of estimating
\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v_{\varepsilon}})
in (
20
) directly, one may use

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v_{\varepsilon}})\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\nabla(\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}

and absorb the first part by dissipation, however, this will not give a better convergence rate since

and

have a similar behavior in terms of

7.
weak–strong uniqueness

Using the relative energy identity (
16
), we finally establish weak–strong uniqueness for system (
2
).
In contrast to the quantitative homogenization argument, the first and the third term on the right-hand side of (
16
) vanish identically in this setting, so that only the remaining second and fourth term need to be controlled.

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

Since
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{S})
is a strong solution, we infer from (
16
) that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{T}|\mathbf{u},\mathbf{S})+\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{T}-\mathbf{S}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{1}\nabla(\mathbf{T}-\mathbf{S}):(\gamma(\nabla\mathbf{T})\nabla\mathbf{T}-\gamma(\nabla\mathbf{S})\nabla\mathbf{S})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}2(\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v})-\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}):(\mu(\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v}))\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v})-\mu(\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u}))\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u}))\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{u})\cdot((\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{T}-\mathbf{S}):[((\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{S}+\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{u},\mathbf{T})+\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{T}-\mathbf{S})]\,\mathrm{d}x.

Similar to before, we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{u})\cdot((\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{T}-\mathbf{S}):((\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{S}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbf{T}-\mathbf{S}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{T}-\mathbf{S}):\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{T}-\mathbf{S})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbf{T}-\mathbf{S}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{T}-\mathbf{S}):\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{u},\mathbf{T})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbf{T}-\mathbf{S}\|_{L^{2}}\|\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v-u})\|_{L^{2}}\|\mathbf{T}\|_{L^{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbf{T}-\mathbf{S}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\delta\|\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v-u})\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
\delta>0
is chosen sufficiently small to absorb this term to the left-hand side. All in all, we find, using also coercivity of the functions
\mu
and
\gamma
from (
4
)–(
5
),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{T}|\mathbf{u},\mathbf{S})+\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{T}-\mathbf{S}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{1}|\nabla(\mathbf{T}-\mathbf{S})|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v})-\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim E(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{T}|\mathbf{u},\mathbf{S}),

so that the result follows from Grönwall’s inequality.
∎

To arrive at the non-dimensional system (
2

[
11
]
as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\div\mathbf{v}=0&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
\varrho\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\varrho\div(\mathbf{v}\otimes\mathbf{v})+\nabla\pi-\div(\mu(\lambda_{1}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{\mathbf{v}}))\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v}))=\varrho\mathbf{f}+\div\mathbf{T}&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\

where
\varrho>0
is constant,

\displaystyle\overset{\triangledown}{\mathbf{T}}=\partial_{t}\mathbf{T}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{T}-\varepsilon_{1}\div(\gamma(\lambda_{2}\nabla\mathbf{T})\nabla\mathbf{T})-\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{T}).

The parameter
\lambda_{2}
has the unit of
1/(\text{force per volume})
and is needed for dimensional reasons only. Now, we introduce characteristic values of length
L_{c}
, velocity
U_{c}
, time
T_{c}
, pressure
P_{c}
, force
F_{c}
, dissipation
\gamma_{c}

\displaystyle\Sr=\frac{L_{c}}{T_{c}U_{c}},

\displaystyle\Eu=\frac{P_{c}}{\varrho U_{c}^{2}},

\displaystyle\Rey=\frac{\varrho U_{c}L_{c}}{\mu_{0}+\eta},

\displaystyle\Fr=\frac{U_{c}}{\sqrt{F_{c}L_{c}}},

\displaystyle\We=\frac{\zeta U_{c}}{L_{c}}.

Note also that
\We\Sr=\zeta/T_{c}={\rm De}
is the Deborah number. Then, setting the (total) stress scale
\tau_{c}=(\mu_{0}+\eta)U_{c}/L_{c}=\varrho U_{c}^{2}/\Rey
and the viscosity ratio
\beta=\mu_{0}/(\mu_{0}+\eta)

for each

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\div\mathbf{v}=0&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\

\mathbf{T}+\We[\Sr\partial_{t}\mathbf{T}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{T}-\varepsilon_{1}\div(\gamma(\Xi\nabla\mathbf{T})\nabla\mathbf{T})-\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{T})]=2(1-\beta)\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{v})&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}.\end{cases}
