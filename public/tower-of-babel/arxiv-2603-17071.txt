Generation of many-body Bell correlations with short-range interactions in analog and digital quantum simulators
Marcin Płodzień, Jan Chwedeńczuk

Abstract
The dynamical generation of quantum resources, such as many-body entanglement or Bell correlations, can be achieved via one-axis twisting (OAT) dynamics, which require all-to-all couplings. However, current digital and analog quantum simulation platforms natively provide short-range or power-law couplings that decay too quickly for this purpose. We demonstrate that two spin- 1 2 \tfrac{1}{2} chain models—a staggered nearest-neighbor XXX chain and a long-range XXZ chain—develop an effective OAT nonlinearity when projected onto the symmetric sector. We show that these dynamics generate metrologically useful spin-squeezed states and Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger coherences that ensure violation of many-body Bell inequalities. We confirm the accuracy of this mapping by comparing it to the exact dynamics and demonstrate that the generated correlations can be read out using a single probe qubit. The resulting dynamics can be simulated with analog and digital quantum simulators.



Introduction.—
Programmable quantum simulators now offer precise control over large, interacting spin systems and high-fidelity operations.
However, generating strongly entangled or Bell-correlated states remains challenging since these platforms natively realize short-range or power-law interactions rather than collective, all-to-all couplings.
In this context, the one-axis twisting (OAT) model
Kitagawa and Ueda 1993
serves as a reference.
It is used to generate a hierarchy of entangled states. Early on, it yields metrologically useful spin-squeezed states which enable
quantum-enhanced precision in interferometric sensing
Wineland et al. 1992
;
Wineland et al. 1994
;
Kitagawa and Ueda 1993
.
Later, oversqueezed non-Gaussian states are created, followed by the Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) states at the optimal time
Kitagawa and Ueda 1993
;
Pezzè et al. 2018
;
Płodzień et al. 2022
.

We pose the following question: Are short-range interactions characteristic of modern architectures a fundamental shortcoming that prevents the generation of macroscopic entanglement and nonlocal correlations?
We demonstrate that effective long-range interactions, which are accessible through native spin-chain dynamics,
provide access to the entire hierarchy when the Schrieffer–Wolff transformation (SWT) maps the system onto the symmetric Dicke manifold.
We identify the conditions under which a finite magnon gap isolates the zero-momentum collective mode. Virtual excitations across the gap yield
effective Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick (LMG) interactions
Lipkin et al. 1965
, equivalent to those characteristic of OAT dynamics.

More quantitatively, we compare the resources and correlations generated by short- or power-law interactions, to the outcome of an indeal OAT dynamics, in particular of
the GHZ state
Cavalcanti et al. 2019
;
Cavalcanti et al. 2007
;
Cavalcanti et al. 2011
;
He et al. 2011
;
Niezgoda et al. 2020
;
Niezgoda and Chwedeńczuk 2021
;
Chwedeńczuk 2022
;
Płodzień et al. 2022
;
Płodzień et al. 2024a
;
Płodzień et al. 2024b
;
Płodzień et al. 2025a
;
Płodzień et al. 2025b
;
Hernández Yanes et al. 2025
.
The latter is characterized by a maximal quantum coherence between two “macroscopically” different components, which we denote as “GHZ coherence”.
It is a source of
correlations that are incompatible with any local realistic model
Bell 1964
;
Clauser et al. 1969
;
Mermin 1990
;
Brunner et al. 2014
.

We apply this technique to two microscopic models which can be realized both in gate-based (digital) quantum processors and analog quantum simulators, namely
a staggered XXX chain with nearest-neighbour interactions and a power-law XXZ model.
In both cases, we demonstrate that effective long-range nonlinearity generates many-body Bell correlations.
Over longer time, the system violates a many-body Bell inequality
Tura et al. 2014
;
Tura et al. 2015
.
Moreover, we show that metrologically useful spin-squeezed states can be generated with nearest-neighbour interactions on quantum circuits without a need for gate optimization, contrary to recent proposals based on variational quantum circuits
Kaubruegger et al. 2019
;
Sun et al. 2023

Lyu et al. 2023

Song et al. 2025

We discuss the regimes of validity of the SWT
by demonstrating when the exact lattice dynamics—continuous-time analog evolution or Trotterized digital circuits—closely match the effective collective description.
Furthermore, we demonstrate that the resulting correlations can be experimentally verified by collectively coupling the spin chain to a single probe qubit
Płodzień and Chwedeńczuk 2026a

Effective Hamiltonians.—
We begin with the general Hamiltonian of an
N
-particle spin-
\tfrac{1}{2}
chain

\displaystyle\hat{H}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j=1}^{N}J(r_{ij})\hat{\vec{S}}_{i}\hat{\vec{S}}_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\alpha}h_{j}^{\alpha}\,\hat{S}_{j}^{\alpha},

(1)

where the first sum runs over all pairs
i\neq j
and
\alpha=x,y,z
.
The two-body coupling is distance-dependent, i.e.,
r_{ij}=|i-j|
, while
the operator for the
i
-th spin is
\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}
.
To perform the SWT, we first introduce the discrete Fourier transform of a quantity
f
,
\tilde{f}_{\alpha}(q)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}f_{j}^{\alpha}e^{iqj}
.
The switching to the momentum space is important because
q
distinguishes the low-energy (
q=0
) fully symmetric part of the spectrum of
\hat{H}_{0}
from
the single-magnon
excitations characterized by the energy
\varepsilon(q)=\tfrac{1}{2}[\tilde{J}(0)-\tilde{J}(q)]
, where
\tilde{J}(q)=\sum_{r=1}^{N-1}J(r)\,\mathrm{e}^{iqr}
.
The magnon gap is
\Delta=\min_{q\neq 0}\varepsilon(q)
.
The SWT can be applied if the couplings
h_{j}^{\alpha}
are small, since the virtual excitations above the gap are driven by the operator
V
. This gives the effective Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm eff}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\,\hat{S}_{\alpha}\hat{S}_{\beta},

(2)

where the collective spin operators are
\hat{S}_{\alpha}=\sum_{i}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}
. Only the symmetric part of the coupling tensor contributes
sup
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\chi_{\alpha\beta}=\sum_{q}\frac{\tilde{h}_{\alpha}(q)\,\tilde{h}_{\beta}(-q)}{N(N-1)\varepsilon(q)}.

(3)

This result can be applied directly to some of the most notable subclasses of the Hamiltonian (
1
).

First, we focus on the staggered XXX model

\displaystyle\hat{H}=-J_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{\vec{S}}_{i}\hat{\vec{S}}_{i+1}-h_{z}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(-1)^{i}\hat{S}_{i}^{z},

(4)

where we assume periodic boundary conditions throughout.
The one-magnon dispersion relation of
\hat{H}_{0}
is

\varepsilon(q)=J_{0}(1-\cos q)
, and the corresponding energy gap equals
\Delta=\varepsilon(\pi)=2J_{0}
.
The Fourier transform of the staggered field has single component
\tilde{h}_{z}(q)=-h_{z}\sqrt{N}\,\delta_{q,\pi}
, and the effective collective model, Eq. (
2
), reduces to the
OAT Hamiltonian
Gietka et al. 2021
;
Hernández Yanes et al. 2022
;
Hernández Yanes et al. 2023
;
sup

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm eff}=\chi\hat{S}_{z}^{2},\quad\chi=\frac{h_{z}^{2}}{2J_{0}(N-1)}.

(5)

Another example is the paradigmatic long-range anisotropic Heisenberg (XXZ) spin-
1/2
model,

\displaystyle\hat{H}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}J(r_{ij})\left(\hat{\vec{S}}_{i}\hat{\vec{S}}_{j}+\delta\hat{S}_{i}^{z}\hat{S}_{j}^{z}\right),

(6)

where
J(r_{ij})=J_{0}|i-j|^{-\gamma}
sets the microscopic coupling scale (
J_{0}>0
),

\gamma
controls the range of power-law interactions,
and
\delta
parametrizes the easy-axis anisotropy.
For
\gamma\leq 1
, the sum
\tilde{J}(0)=\sum_{r}J(r)
grows extensively with
N
, so that the energy per spin diverges in the thermodynamic limit.

The remedy to this problem is the Kac normalization
Kac et al. 1963
: rescale
J(r_{ij})\to J(r_{ij})/\mathcal{N}_{\gamma,N}
with
\mathcal{N}_{\gamma,N}=\sum_{j\neq i}J(r_{ij})/J_{0}
.
This gives
\tilde{J}(0)=J_{0}
for every
\gamma
, so the effective nonlinearity in Eq. (
9
)
carries only the explicit Kac scaling
\chi\propto 1/(N-1)
; the interaction range enters only through the magnon gap that protects the symmetric manifold.
The model interpolates between the isotropic Heisenberg limit (
\delta=0
) and the strongly anisotropic Ising limit (
|\delta|\!\gg\!1
).

In contrast to the staggered XXX chain, where the OAT nonlinearity emerges as a second-order perturbation from the one-body field, the XXZ model has a symmetry-breaking anisotropy in the two-body coupling.
As a result, the effective collective Hamiltonian is obtained by applying a projection
\hat{\Pi}

onto the symmetric Dicke manifold, for instance

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{z}\hat{S}_{j}^{z}\hat{\Pi}=\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\left(\hat{S}_{z}^{2}-\frac{N}{4}\right)\hat{\Pi},

(7)

which holds for any pair
i\neq j
in the fully symmetric sector. The resulting Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\hat{\Pi}\hat{H}\hat{\Pi}
, up to an additive constant, is

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}=-\frac{\sum_{i\neq j}J_{ij}}{2N(N-1)}\Big(\hat{S}^{2}+\delta\hat{S}_{z}^{2}\Big)\hat{\Pi}

(8)

Since
\hat{S}^{2}=S(S+1)\hat{\mathds{1}}
in the symmetric subspace, we end up with the expression

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}\simeq\chi\hat{S}_{z}^{2},\qquad\chi=-\frac{\delta}{2}\frac{\tilde{J}(0)}{N-1}.

(9)

The collective description is adequate when the magnon gap pertubatively suppresses the leakage on the timescale
t\sim 1/|\chi|

sup
.
The sign of
\chi
, controlled by
\delta
, determines the squeezing axis, though it does not affect the generation of Bell correlations.
The formula (
9
) is exact within the symmetric manifold and holds for any value of
\delta
, but the full XXZ evolution drives the system out of this subspace when
\delta\neq 0
.
In the two limiting regimes, the gap has distinct physical origins.

When the anisotropy is small (
|\delta|\ll 1
), the dominant part of the Hamiltonian (
6
) is SU(2)-invariant and strictly preserves the symmetry.
Its one-magnon dispersion
\varepsilon(q)
provides a finite gap that grows with the interaction range (decreasing
\gamma
), ensuring that the anisotropy

\parallel\!\!\hat{V}\!\!\parallel\propto\delta
is small.
On the other hand, when the anisotropy is large (
|\delta|\gg 1
),
the dominant Ising coupling
\hat{H}_{0}=-\frac{1+\delta}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}J_{ij}\hat{S}_{i}^{z}\hat{S}_{j}^{z}

opens a gap
\Delta\sim(1+\delta)\tilde{J}(0)
above the ferromagnetic ground states.
Second-order virtual spin-flip processes across this gap generate an effective OAT nonlinearity consistent with Eq. (
9
).
The complete derivation, including both limiting regimes and the XXZ dispersion relation, is presented in the Supplementary Materials.

Results.—
We will now test the accuracy of the SWT. For each case, the initial condition is a fully polarized product of the +1/2 eigenstates of the
\hat{S}_{x}^{(i)}
operators,

\displaystyle|\psi(0)\rangle=|\uparrow\rangle_{x}^{\otimes N}.

(10)

This state will be propagated using either an exact or an approximate symmetric Hamiltonian to yield
|\psi(t)\rangle
.
To perform a quantitative comparison, we calculate the strength of the GHZ coherence, denoted as
\mathcal{E}_{N}^{(q)}
, for each case.
It is the modulus squared of the most off-diagonal term of the density matrix
|\psi(t)\rangle\!\langle\psi(t)|
. This quantity is a witness of Bell correlations in quantum
N
-qubit systems, since

\mathcal{E}_{N}^{(q)}\leqslant 2^{-N}
is an
N
-body Bell inequality
Cavalcanti et al. 2007
;
Cavalcanti et al. 2011
;
He et al. 2011
;
sup
, or equivalently
\mathcal{Q}=N+\log_{2}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{(q)}\leqslant 0
. Hence
\mathcal{Q}>0
signals the presence
of many-body Bell correlations. It reaches a maximal value of
\mathcal{Q}_{\rm max}=N-2
for the GHZ state.

Furhtermore, we estimate the metrological utility of the above systems by imprinting a phase
\theta
on the output state,
|\psi_{\theta}(t)\rangle=e^{-i\theta\hat{S}_{y}}|\psi(t)\rangle
.
Next, we calculate the population imbalance probability
p_{m}(\theta)
, where
m=\frac{1}{2}(n_{\uparrow}-n_{\downarrow})
is the difference between the number of spins in the up and down states.
This probability can be obtained by measuring each spin’s configuration.
However, as our recent findings indicate
Płodzień and Chwedeńczuk 2026b
, a single external probe qubit interacting with the
N
-spin system
can harness the full information about
p_{m}(\theta)
.

More specifically,
before we “connect” the chain to the probe, we imprint the relative phase
\theta
between the components of
|\psi(t)\rangle
expressed in the basis of the symmetric Dicke states.
Specifically, we implement a series of three rotations

\displaystyle|\psi_{\theta}(t)\rangle=e^{-i\tfrac{\pi}{2}\hat{S}_{x}}e^{-i\theta\hat{S}_{z}}e^{-i\tfrac{\pi}{2}\hat{S}_{y}}|\psi(t)\rangle.

(11)

The first (time-wise) transforms the GHZ-like coherences that at the output of the dynamical protocols are aligned along the
y
-axis so that they lie along
z
and are most
susceptible to the imprint of the phase
\theta
(second rotation). The final rotation mixes the modes to produce the
\theta
-dependent signal.
Afterwards, the probe qubit is initialized in

\displaystyle|\uparrow\rangle^{(p)}_{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|\uparrow\rangle^{(p)}_{z}+|\downarrow\rangle^{(p)}_{z}\right),

(12)

where the index
(p)
denotes the probe’s degrees of freedom. The qubit is coupled to the chain through

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{int}}=\kappa\hat{S}_{z}\hat{S}_{z}^{(p)}.

(13)

and the composite (chain+probe) state evolves as

\displaystyle|\Psi(t,\theta)\rangle=e^{-i\hat{H}_{\mathrm{int}}t}\bigl(|\psi(\theta)\rangle\otimes|\uparrow\rangle^{(p)}_{x}\bigr).

(14)

The quantity of interest is the off-diagonal terms of the probe’s reduced density matrix

\displaystyle a(t,\theta)=\langle\downarrow|\hat{\varrho}_{p}(t,\theta)|\uparrow\rangle,

(15)

where for brevity we used
|\uparrow/\downarrow\rangle\equiv|\uparrow/\downarrow\rangle^{(p)}_{z}
.
Since
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{int}}
does not couple different eigenvalues of
\hat{S}_{z}
, the probe coherence is a Fourier series in the total magnetization
m
of the chain,

\displaystyle a(t,\theta)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=-N/2}^{N/2}p_{m}(\theta)\,e^{-im\tau},

(16)

where
\tau=\kappa t
,
J=N/2
is the total spin quantum number, and
p_{m}(\theta)
is the probability that the chain in state
|\psi(\theta)\rangle
has total magnetization
\hat{S}_{z}|m\rangle=m|m\rangle
.
Sampling
a(t,\theta)
at a discrete grid of times

\displaystyle\tau_{k}=\frac{2\pi k}{N+1},\qquad k=0,1,\dots,N,

(17)

puts this relation in discrete Fourier-transform form. Inverting it yields

\displaystyle p_{m}(\theta)=\frac{2}{N+1}\sum_{k=0}^{N}a(\tau_{k},\theta)e^{i\,\tfrac{2\pi k}{N+1}(m+\tfrac{N}{2})}.

(18)

To summarize, the procedure presented above gives access to the full set
\{p_{m}(\theta)\}
using single-probe off-diagonal element
a
. For a detailed discussion, see
Płodzień and Chwedeńczuk 2026b
.

The functions

provide information about the Dicke-basis matrix elements of the original state. Writing

\displaystyle|\psi(t)\rangle\!\langle\psi(t)|=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}=-J}^{J}\varrho_{mm^{\prime}}|m\rangle\!\langle m^{\prime}|,

\displaystyle p_{m}(\theta)=\sum_{m^{\prime},m^{\prime\prime}}d_{mm^{\prime}}d_{mm^{\prime\prime}}^{*}\varrho_{m^{\prime}m^{\prime\prime}}\,e^{-i(m^{\prime\prime}-m^{\prime})\theta},

where

Note that Eq. (

By inverting this relation, i.e., calculating
\mathcal{F}^{(-1)}[p_{m}(\theta)]
for some

that for symmetric states gives the strongest signal),
and focusing on the term corresponding to the highest frequency (when

), we obtain

\displaystyle\varrho_{\frac{N}{2},-\frac{N}{2}}=\frac{\mathcal{F}^{-1}[p_{0}(\theta)]}{N_{\theta}d_{0,\frac{N}{2}}^{2}},

(21)

where
N_{\theta}
is the number of points on which
\theta
is sampled. This way, a measurement on the single-qubit probe and a subsequent

In particular, as entanglement strengthens during the OAT dynamics, the GHZ components begin to dominate the state

.
This growing presence can be deduced from a Fourier transform of

N\theta

In the Heisenberg-like regime

Another quantum resource of multi-qubit systems that is relevant for ultra-precise metrology is spin-squeezing of the sample

. In symmetric systems governed by the collective nonlinearity,
it is typically generated with the OAT dynamics: Starting from the coherent spin state
|\uparrow\rangle_{x}^{\otimes N}
,
the nonlinear evolution
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\chi\hat{S}_{z}^{2}

shears the quantum uncertainty ellipse in the plane perpendicular to the mean spin,
squeezing the variance along one direction below the standard quantum limit
at the expense of the conjugate direction

.
The degree of squeezing is captured by the spin-squeezing parameter,

\displaystyle\xi_{R}^{2}=\frac{N\,(\Delta S_{\perp}^{2})_{\mathrm{min}}}{|\langle\hat{\mathbf{S}}\rangle|^{2}},

(22)

where the minimisation runs over all directions perpendicular to the mean
spin
\langle\hat{\mathbf{S}}\rangle

sup
.
The spin-squeezing parameter serves as a entanglement witness and a metrological figure of merit accessible
from collective spin measurements alone

;
Gross et al. 2010
;
Pezzè et al. 2018

Values

certify entanglement

;
Tóth et al. 2010

and bound the achievable phase sensitivity in Ramsey interferometry.

Discussion and conclusions.—
Despite lacking explicit all-to-all couplings,
we have shown that short-range and power-law interacting spin-
\tfrac{1}{2}
chains can generate GHZ coherences and sizeable many-body Bell correlations.
By applying a Schrieffer-Wolff projection onto the symmetric Dicke manifold, we identified the conditions under which virtual magnon processes across a finite gap produce
an effective collective twisting of the Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick type.
This mechanism was illustrated in two settings: a staggered nearest-neighbor XXX chain and a long-range XXZ model (see Table in the Supplementary Materials for a summary).
In both cases, the exact dynamics closely follow the effective collective description, producing maximal Bell correlators.
The staggered XXX protocol can be implemented directly on digital quantum hardware. Meanwhile, the long-range XXZ couplings required for the second pathway arise naturally in analog quantum platforms,
such as superconducting quantum circuits, trapped-ion chains, and Rydberg-dressed atomic ensembles.

We also showed that these correlations can be verified with a single probe qubit that is collectively coupled to the chain. The coherence of the probe encodes the magnetization distribution
of the rotated many-body state. The Fourier analysis isolates the extremal Dicke coherence that is responsible for the Bell violation. The Fourier weight extracted from the probe
at the highest mode matches the Bell correlator computed directly.
The same dynamics produce metrologically useful spin squeezing
with
\xi_{R}^{2}<1
over a wide region of parameter space.
Unlike variational quantum circuits
Kaubruegger et al. 2019
;
Sun et al. 2023
;
Lyu et al. 2023
;
Song et al. 2025
,
the squeezing here requires no classical feedback loop. It follows from the native Hamiltonian evolution with a fixed gate sequence or analog protocol.

I
Schrieffer–Wolff projection onto the Dicke manifold

The SWT
Schrieffer and Wolff 1966
treats systems which exhibit an energy gap
\Delta
that separates the low-lying part of the spectrum from the high-energy part.
It allows for a systematic derivation of an effective low-energy (
L
) Hamiltonian, treating the virtual excitations (
E
) as a perturbation. The full Hamiltonian consists of two parts

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{V}.

(SM.1)

The
\hat{H}_{0}
acts in the two sub-spaces independently, while
\hat{V}
provides the coupling.
The SWT transforms
\hat{H}
into a block-diagonal
\hat{H}^{*}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\hat{H}_{L}&\ \ \lambda\hat{V}_{LE}\\
\hline\cr\lambda\hat{V}^{\dagger}_{LE}&\hat{H}_{E}\end{array}\right)\ \ \ \xrightarrow{\mathrm{SWT}}\ \ \ \left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\hat{H}^{*}_{L}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\hat{H}^{*}_{E}\end{array}\right)

The second-order correction to the effective low-energy Hamiltonian reads
end

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{*}_{L}\equiv\hat{\Pi}^{(L)}\hat{H}^{*}\hat{\Pi}^{(L)}=-\sum_{nn^{\prime}m}\frac{\hat{\Pi}^{(L)}_{n}\hat{V}\hat{\Pi}^{(E)}_{m}\hat{V}\hat{\Pi}^{(L)}_{n^{\prime}}}{E_{n}^{(L)}-E_{m}^{(E)}},

(SM.6)

where
\hat{\Pi}^{(L/E)}_{n}=|\psi_{n}^{(L/E)}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{n}^{(L/E)}|
project onto eigenstates of
\hat{H}_{0}
and
E_{n}^{(L/E)}
are the corresponding eigen-energies. The derivation of Eq. (
SM.6
) is given in Section
II
.

II
Schrieffer–Wolff effective Hamiltonian for one-body perturbations

We consider a general isotropic ferromagnetic Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{0}
perturbed by a one-body field
\hat{V}=\sum_{j,\alpha}h_{j}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{j}^{\alpha}
.
Because permutation symmetry implies
\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{j}^{\alpha}\hat{\Pi}=\frac{1}{N}\hat{S}_{\alpha}\hat{\Pi}
, any inhomogeneous field component satisfies
\hat{\Pi}\hat{V}_{\mathrm{inh}}\hat{\Pi}=0
, so the effective collective nonlinearity arises at
second order
in the Schrieffer–Wolff (SW) expansion through virtual magnon excitations across the gap.

The Hilbert space splits into a low-energy subspace
L
with projector
\hat{P}
and its complement
E
with
\hat{Q}=\hat{\mathbb{I}}-\hat{P}
.
We write
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\lambda\hat{V}
, where
\hat{H}_{0}
is block-diagonal (
\hat{P}\hat{H}_{0}\hat{Q}=0
),
and decompose
\hat{V}=\hat{V}_{\mathrm{d}}+\hat{V}_{\mathrm{od}}
into block-diagonal and block-off-diagonal parts.
An anti-Hermitian generator
\hat{S}=\lambda\hat{S}_{1}+\lambda^{2}\hat{S}_{2}+\cdots
is determined such that
\hat{H}^{*}=\mathrm{e}^{\hat{S}}\hat{H}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\hat{S}}
is block-diagonal order by order.
Expanding via Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff,

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{*}=\hat{H}+[\hat{S},\hat{H}]+\tfrac{1}{2}[\hat{S},[\hat{S},\hat{H}]]+\cdots,

(SM.7)

collecting powers of
\lambda
and imposing the first-order decoupling condition

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{\mathrm{od}}+[\hat{S}_{1},\hat{H}_{0}]=0

(SM.8)

(with
\hat{S}_{1}
purely off-diagonal) removes all block-off-diagonal terms at
O(\lambda)
.
Using
[\hat{S}_{1},[\hat{S}_{1},\hat{H}_{0}]]=-[\hat{S}_{1},\hat{V}_{\mathrm{od}}]
, the

O(\lambda^{2})
block-diagonal
LL
-component reduces to

\tfrac{1}{2}\hat{P}[\hat{S}_{1},\hat{V}_{\mathrm{od}}]\hat{P}
, since both

\hat{P}[\hat{S}_{1},\hat{V}_{\mathrm{d}}]\hat{P}
and
\hat{P}[\hat{S}_{2},\hat{H}_{0}]\hat{P}
vanish (the first because a commutator of an off-diagonal with a diagonal operator is off-diagonal, the second by the canonical gauge choice for
\hat{S}_{2}
).
Hence

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\hat{P}\hat{H}_{0}\hat{P}+\lambda\hat{P}\hat{V}\hat{P}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\hat{P}[\hat{S}_{1},\hat{V}_{\mathrm{od}}]\hat{P}+O(\lambda^{3}).

(SM.9)

Taking matrix elements of Eq. (
SM.8
) between
|n\rangle\in L
and
|m\rangle\in E
:

\displaystyle(\hat{S}_{1})_{nm}=\frac{V_{nm}}{E_{n}^{(L)}-E_{m}^{(E)}},\qquad(\hat{S}_{1})_{mn}=-\frac{V_{mn}^{*}}{E_{n}^{(L)}-E_{m}^{(E)}},

(SM.10)

where the second relation follows from
\hat{S}_{1}^{\dagger}=-\hat{S}_{1}
.
Evaluating
\langle n|[\hat{S}_{1},\hat{V}_{\mathrm{od}}]|n^{\prime}\rangle
by inserting a complete set
\sum_{m\in E}|m\rangle\langle m|
:

\displaystyle(H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(2)})_{nn^{\prime}}=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\sum_{m\in E}V_{nm}V_{mn^{\prime}}\times\left(\frac{1}{E_{n}^{(L)}-E_{m}^{(E)}}+\frac{1}{E_{n^{\prime}}^{(L)}-E_{m}^{(E)}}\right).

(SM.11)

When the low-energy manifold is degenerate (
E_{n}^{(L)}=E_{0}
for all
n
), this yields the resolvent form

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}=E_{0}\hat{P}+\lambda\hat{P}\hat{V}\hat{P}-\lambda^{2}\hat{P}\hat{V}\hat{Q}\frac{1}{\hat{Q}\hat{H}_{0}\hat{Q}-E_{0}}\hat{Q}\hat{V}\hat{P}+O(\lambda^{3}).

(SM.12)

II.1
Application to the staggered XXX chain

Consider an isotropic ferromagnetic
\hat{H}_{0}
with a degenerate symmetric Dicke manifold (
S=N/2
, projector
\hat{\Pi}
) perturbed by a one-body field

\hat{V}=\sum_{j,\alpha}h_{j}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{j}^{\alpha}
.
Decomposing
h_{j}^{\alpha}=h_{0}^{\alpha}+\delta h_{j}^{\alpha}
with
h_{0}^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j}h_{j}^{\alpha}
and
\sum_{j}\delta h_{j}^{\alpha}=0
,
the uniform part gives a first-order collective Zeeman term
\hat{V}_{0}=\sum_{\alpha}h_{0}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{\alpha}
.
The inhomogeneous part satisfies

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}\hat{V}_{\mathrm{inh}}\hat{\Pi}=\sum_{j,\alpha}\delta h_{j}^{\alpha}\,\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{j}^{\alpha}\hat{\Pi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:app_Pi_Si}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\alpha}\Bigl(\underbrace{\sum_{j}\delta h_{j}^{\alpha}}_{=\,0}\Bigr)\hat{S}_{\alpha}\hat{\Pi}=0,

(SM.13)

so the leading correction from
\hat{V}_{\mathrm{inh}}
is second order.

Restricting the resolvent to the one-magnon sector with plane-wave states
|q\rangle
and energies
\varepsilon(q)
(the contribution of multi-magnon states is suppressed by additional factors of
h/\Delta
and enters only at fourth order in the SW expansion; see the convergence discussion below),
we define Fourier modes

\tilde{h}_{\alpha}(q)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j}\delta h_{j}^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{e}^{iqj}

and

\hat{S}_{\alpha}(q)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j}\hat{S}_{j}^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{e}^{iqj}
,
so that
\hat{V}_{\mathrm{inh}}=\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{k}\tilde{h}_{\alpha}(k)\hat{S}_{\alpha}(-k)
. Inserting into the resolvent:

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(2)}=-\sum_{q\neq 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon(q)}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\tilde{h}_{\alpha}(k)\tilde{h}_{\beta}(k^{\prime})\times\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{\alpha}(-k)|q\rangle\langle q|\hat{S}_{\beta}(-k^{\prime})\hat{\Pi}.

(SM.14)

Translational invariance constrains the momenta
(k,k^{\prime})
in Eq. (
SM.14
).
Under a lattice translation
\hat{T}_{a}
, the Fourier spin operators transform as

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{a}\hat{S}_{\alpha}(k)\hat{T}_{a}^{\dagger}=\mathrm{e}^{ika}\hat{S}_{\alpha}(k),

(SM.15)

and the momentum eigenstate satisfies
\hat{T}_{a}|q\rangle=\mathrm{e}^{iqa}|q\rangle
.
Since
\hat{\Pi}
projects onto the fully symmetric (zero-momentum) manifold, it commutes with all translations:
\hat{T}_{a}\hat{\Pi}=\hat{\Pi}\hat{T}_{a}
.

Let
|v\rangle=\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{\alpha}(-k)|q\rangle
.
If
|v\rangle\neq 0
, it lies in the range of
\hat{\Pi}
and is therefore translation-invariant,
\hat{T}_{a}|v\rangle=|v\rangle
.
However, direct evaluation gives

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{a}|v\rangle=\hat{\Pi}(\mathrm{e}^{-ika}\hat{S}_{\alpha}(-k))(\mathrm{e}^{iqa}|q\rangle)=\mathrm{e}^{i(q-k)a}|v\rangle.

(SM.16)

For
|v\rangle\neq 0
, consistency requires
\mathrm{e}^{i(q-k)a}=1
for all
a
, hence
k=q
.
Analogously, defining
\langle w|=\langle q|\hat{S}_{\beta}(-k^{\prime})\hat{\Pi}
and applying
\hat{T}_{a}^{\dagger}
from the right yields the phase
\mathrm{e}^{-i(q+k^{\prime})a}
, enforcing
k^{\prime}=-q
.
Only the term
(k,k^{\prime})=(q,-q)
survives in Eq. (
SM.14
):

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(2)}=-\sum_{q\neq 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon(q)}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\tilde{h}_{\alpha}(q)\tilde{h}_{\beta}(-q)\,\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{\alpha}(-q)\hat{S}_{\beta}(q)\hat{\Pi}.

(SM.17)

Expanding the product in real space:

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{\alpha}(-q)\hat{S}_{\beta}(q)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{i}^{\beta}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\neq j}\mathrm{e}^{iq(i-j)}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{j}^{\beta}.

(SM.18)

Project with
\hat{\Pi}(\cdot)\hat{\Pi}
.
By permutation symmetry,
\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{j}^{\beta}\hat{\Pi}\equiv\hat{A}_{\alpha\beta}\hat{\Pi}
is independent of the pair
(i,j)
for
i\neq j
(see Eq. (
SM.30
)).
The on-site term is site-independent, contributing
\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{i}^{\beta}\hat{\Pi}
.
For
q\neq 0
, the lattice-sum identity
\sum_{i\neq j}\mathrm{e}^{iq(i-j)}=|\sum_{j}\mathrm{e}^{iqj}|^{2}-N=-N
gives

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{\alpha}(-q)\hat{S}_{\beta}(q)\hat{\Pi}=\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{i}^{\beta}\hat{\Pi}-\hat{A}_{\alpha\beta}\hat{\Pi}.

(SM.19)

Inserting the two-body identity (
SM.30
):

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{\alpha}(-q)\hat{S}_{\beta}(q)\hat{\Pi}=\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{i}^{\beta}\hat{\Pi}-\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\bigl(\hat{S}_{\alpha}\hat{S}_{\beta}-N\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{i}^{\beta}\hat{\Pi}\bigr)\hat{\Pi}=\frac{N}{N-1}\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{i}^{\beta}\hat{\Pi}-\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\hat{S}_{\alpha}\hat{S}_{\beta}\hat{\Pi}.

(SM.20)

For spin-
1/2
, the algebra
\hat{\sigma}^{\alpha}\hat{\sigma}^{\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}+i\sum_{\gamma}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\hat{\sigma}^{\gamma}
gives

\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{i}^{\beta}=\tfrac{1}{4}\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\tfrac{i}{2}\sum_{\gamma}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\hat{S}_{i}^{\gamma}
.
Projecting with Eq. (
SM.29
):

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{i}^{\beta}\hat{\Pi}=\frac{1}{4}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\hat{\Pi}+\frac{i}{2N}\sum_{\gamma}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\hat{S}_{\gamma}\hat{\Pi}.

(SM.21)

Substituting into the first term of Eq. (
SM.20
):

\displaystyle\frac{N}{N-1}\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{i}^{\beta}\hat{\Pi}=\frac{N}{4(N-1)}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\hat{\Pi}+\frac{{i}}{2(N-1)}\sum_{\gamma}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\hat{S}_{\gamma}\hat{\Pi}.

(SM.22)

For the second term, decompose
\hat{S}_{\alpha}\hat{S}_{\beta}=\tfrac{1}{2}\{\hat{S}_{\alpha},\hat{S}_{\beta}\}+\tfrac{{i}}{2}\sum_{\gamma}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\hat{S}_{\gamma}
to separate symmetric and antisymmetric parts.
Combining the linear-in-
\hat{S}_{\gamma}
contributions from both terms:

\displaystyle\frac{{i}}{2(N-1)}\sum_{\gamma}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\hat{S}_{\gamma}-\frac{{i}}{2N(N-1)}\sum_{\gamma}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\hat{S}_{\gamma}=\frac{{i}}{2N}\sum_{\gamma}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\hat{S}_{\gamma}.

(SM.23)

Assembling all contributions yields the identity for
q\neq 0
:

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{\alpha}(-q)\hat{S}_{\beta}(q)\hat{\Pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{4(N-1)}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\hat{\Pi}-\frac{1}{2N(N-1)}\{\hat{S}_{\alpha},\hat{S}_{\beta}\}\hat{\Pi}+\frac{{i}}{2N}\sum_{\gamma}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\hat{S}_{\gamma}\hat{\Pi}.

(SM.24)

Substituting Eq. (
SM.24
) into Eq. (
SM.17
) and defining

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}=\sum_{q\neq 0}\frac{\tilde{h}_{\alpha}(q)\tilde{h}_{\beta}(-q)}{\varepsilon(q)},

(SM.25)

the second-order effective Hamiltonian separates into three terms of distinct collective structure.

(i) Quadratic (OAT) term.

The anticommutator contribution gives

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(2)}\big|_{\mathrm{quad}}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\,\tfrac{1}{2}\{\hat{S}_{\alpha},\hat{S}_{\beta}\}\,\hat{\Pi},\qquad\chi_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}}{N(N-1)}.

(SM.26)

Only the symmetric part of
\chi_{\alpha\beta}
contributes, since
\{\hat{S}_{\alpha},\hat{S}_{\beta}\}
is symmetric under
\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta
.

(ii) Linear term.

The
\hat{S}_{\gamma}
contribution yields

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(2)}\big|_{\mathrm{lin}}=\sum_{\gamma}B_{\gamma}\hat{S}_{\gamma}\hat{\Pi},\qquad B_{\gamma}=-\frac{{i}}{2N}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}.

(SM.27)

Because
\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
is antisymmetric, only the antisymmetric part of
\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}
contributes to
B_{\gamma}
.
Whenever only a single field component is present (as in the staggered XXX model),
\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}
is diagonal and the linear term vanishes identically.

(iii) Constant shift.

The identity contribution produces

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(2)}\big|_{\mathrm{const}}=-\frac{N}{4(N-1)}(\sum_{\alpha}\Lambda_{\alpha\alpha})\hat{\Pi}
,
which renormalizes the ground-state energy but does not affect dynamics within
\hat{\Pi}
.

For
\hat{H}_{0}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}J(r_{ij})\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{i}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{j}
on a periodic ring,
define
|F\rangle=|\uparrow\rangle^{\otimes N}
and single-flip states
|j\rangle=\hat{S}_{j}^{-}|F\rangle
.
Using
\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{i}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{j}=\hat{S}_{i}^{z}\hat{S}_{j}^{z}+\frac{1}{2}(\hat{S}_{i}^{+}\hat{S}_{j}^{-}+\hat{S}_{i}^{-}\hat{S}_{j}^{+})

one obtains
\hat{H}_{0}|j\rangle=E_{0}|j\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l\neq j}J_{jl}(|j\rangle-|l\rangle)
with
E_{0}=-\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i\neq j}J(r_{ij})
.
Going to plane waves
|q\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j}\mathrm{e}^{iqj}|j\rangle
:

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{0}|q\rangle=\bigl(E_{0}+\varepsilon(q)\bigr)|q\rangle,\qquad\varepsilon(q)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\tilde{J}(0)-\tilde{J}(q)\bigr],

(SM.28)

where
\tilde{J}(q)=\sum_{r}J(r)\,\mathrm{e}^{iqr}
.
For nearest-neighbor coupling:
\varepsilon(q)=J_{0}(1-\cos q)
, giving a magnon gap
\Delta=\varepsilon(\pi)=2J_{0}
.

II.2
Projection to symmetric manifold

Let
\hat{\Pi}
project onto the fully symmetric Dicke manifold (
S=N/2
).

One-body.

By permutation symmetry,
\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{\Pi}
is site-independent.
Summing over
i
:
N\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{\Pi}=\hat{S}_{\alpha}\hat{\Pi}
, hence

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{\Pi}=\frac{1}{N}\hat{S}_{\alpha}\hat{\Pi}.

(SM.29)

Two-body (
i\neq j
).

Similarly,
\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{j}^{\beta}\hat{\Pi}\equiv\hat{A}_{\alpha\beta}\hat{\Pi}
is pair-independent.
From
\hat{S}_{\alpha}\hat{S}_{\beta}=\sum_{i}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{i}^{\beta}+\sum_{i\neq j}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{j}^{\beta}
, projecting and counting
N
on-site and
N(N-1)
off-site terms:

\displaystyle\hat{A}_{\alpha\beta}\hat{\Pi}=\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\bigl(\hat{S}_{\alpha}\hat{S}_{\beta}-N\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{i}^{\beta}\hat{\Pi}\bigr)\hat{\Pi}.

(SM.30)

On-site spin-
1/2
.

From
(\hat{S}_{i}^{z})^{2}=\frac{1}{4}
, the on-site projected term gives
\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{z}\hat{S}_{i}^{z}\hat{\Pi}=\frac{1}{4}\hat{\Pi}
.
Substituting into Eq. (
SM.30
) with
\alpha=\beta=z
:

\displaystyle\hat{A}_{zz}\hat{\Pi}=\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\left(\hat{S}_{z}^{2}-\frac{N}{4}\right)\hat{\Pi},

(SM.31)

i.e.,

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{z}\hat{S}_{j}^{z}\hat{\Pi}=\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\left(\hat{S}_{z}^{2}-\frac{N}{4}\right)\hat{\Pi},\qquad i\neq j.

(SM.32)

This is the key identity used in the XXZ projection (Section
III
).
An analogous result holds for the
x
and
y
components, with
\hat{S}_{z}^{2}
replaced by
\hat{S}_{x}^{2}
or
\hat{S}_{y}^{2}
respectively.

For the staggered model on a ring with even
N
,

J(r)=J_{0}
for
r=1
and
r=N-1
, giving
\tilde{J}(q)=2J_{0}\cos q
and
\tilde{J}(0)=2J_{0}
.
The staggered profile
\delta h_{j}^{z}=-h_{z}(-1)^{j}
has Fourier transform

\tilde{h}_{z}(q)=-h_{z}\sqrt{N}\,\delta_{q,\pi}

(since
\sum_{j}(-1)^{j}\mathrm{e}^{-iqj}=N\delta_{q,\pi}
) and
\tilde{h}_{x}=\tilde{h}_{y}=0
.
Inserting into Eq. (
SM.26
):

\displaystyle\chi_{zz}^{(\mathrm{XXX})}=\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\frac{|\tilde{h}_{z}(\pi)|^{2}}{\varepsilon(\pi)}=\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\frac{h_{z}^{2}N}{2J_{0}}=\frac{h_{z}^{2}}{2J_{0}(N-1)},

(SM.33)

reproducing the result from the main text and confirming the general formalism.

The second-order result is controlled by the dimensionless parameter
h/\Delta
.
When the staggered-field amplitude approaches the magnon gap,
h\sim\Delta
, the perturbative hierarchy breaks down: multi-magnon processes become relevant and the effective OAT description ceases to be accurate.
Figure
6
(b) illustrates this crossover explicitly: for weak fields (
h\ll\Delta
) the numerically extracted
\chi
follows the analytical
\chi\propto h^{2}
scaling, whereas for
h\gtrsim\Delta
systematic deviations appear.
In practice, the SW expansion remains quantitatively reliable when
h/\Delta\lesssim 0.3
, which is readily satisfied in current experimental platforms where the exchange coupling
J_{0}
exceeds the achievable staggered-field amplitudes.

III
Effective Hamiltonian for the long-range XXZ model

In the long-range XXZ model the anisotropy is a
two-body
interaction,
\hat{V}_{\delta}\propto\sum_{i\neq j}J(r_{ij})\hat{S}_{i}^{z}\hat{S}_{j}^{z}
.
Unlike the one-body staggered field of Section
II
, this perturbation projects nontrivially onto the symmetric manifold,
\hat{\Pi}\hat{V}_{\delta}\hat{\Pi}\neq 0
, so the effective one-axis-twisting nonlinearity appears already at
first order
.

The full XXZ Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{XXZ}}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}J(r_{ij})\left(\hat{S}_{i}^{x}\hat{S}_{j}^{x}+\hat{S}_{i}^{y}\hat{S}_{j}^{y}+(1+\delta)\hat{S}_{i}^{z}\hat{S}_{j}^{z}\right).

(SM.34)

We project onto the symmetric manifold,
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{coll}}=\hat{\Pi}\hat{H}_{\mathrm{XXZ}}\hat{\Pi}
.
Applying the two-body identity
\hat{\Pi}\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{S}_{j}^{\alpha}\hat{\Pi}=\frac{1}{N(N-1)}(\hat{S}_{\alpha}^{2}-\frac{N}{4})\hat{\Pi}
for
i\neq j
(Section
II.2
) to each component
\alpha=x,y,z
:

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{coll}}=-\frac{\sum_{i\neq j}J(r_{ij})}{2N(N-1)}\bigl(\hat{S}_{x}^{2}+\hat{S}_{y}^{2}+(1+\delta)\hat{S}_{z}^{2}\bigr)\hat{\Pi}+\mathrm{const.}

(SM.35)

Since
\hat{\mathbf{S}}^{2}=S(S+1)
is constant in
S=N/2
, the isotropic part
\hat{S}_{x}^{2}+\hat{S}_{y}^{2}+\hat{S}_{z}^{2}
produces no dynamics.
Subtracting it, the nontrivial term is

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{coll}}\simeq\chi_{zz}^{(\mathrm{XXZ})}\hat{S}_{z}^{2},\qquad\chi_{zz}^{(\mathrm{XXZ})}=-\frac{\delta}{2}\frac{\tilde{J}(0)}{N-1},

(SM.36)

where
\tilde{J}(0)=\sum_{r=1}^{N-1}J(r)
(using
\sum_{i\neq j}J(r_{ij})=N\tilde{J}(0)
by translation invariance).
This is an exact operator identity within
\hat{\Pi}
and holds for any value of
\delta
.
With Kac normalization (see main text),
\tilde{J}(0)=J_{0}
and the coupling simplifies to
\chi_{zz}^{(\mathrm{XXZ})}=-\delta J_{0}/[2(N-1)]
.

To obtain the dispersion relation we write
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{XXZ}}=\hat{H}_{zz}+\hat{H}_{xy}
with
\hat{H}_{zz}=-\frac{1+\delta}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}J(r_{ij})\hat{S}_{i}^{z}\hat{S}_{j}^{z}
and
\hat{H}_{xy}=-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i\neq j}J(r_{ij})(\hat{S}_{i}^{+}\hat{S}_{j}^{-}+\mathrm{h.c.})
,
the single-flip state
|j\rangle
sees a diagonal shift from
\hat{H}_{zz}
: each pair
(j,l)
contributes
+\frac{1+\delta}{4}J_{jl}
relative to
|F\rangle
(because
\hat{S}_{j}^{z}\hat{S}_{l}^{z}
changes from
+\frac{1}{4}
to
-\frac{1}{4}
). Summing over both orderings:
\hat{H}_{zz}|j\rangle=E_{F}|j\rangle+\frac{1+\delta}{2}\tilde{J}(0)|j\rangle
.
The flip-flop part gives
\hat{H}_{xy}|j\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l\neq j}J_{jl}|l\rangle
, identical to the isotropic case.
Combining,
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{XXZ}}|j\rangle=E_{F}|j\rangle+\frac{1+\delta}{2}\tilde{J}(0)|j\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l\neq j}J_{jl}|l\rangle
.
Fourier-transforming as before yields

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mathrm{XXZ}}(q)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[(1+\delta)\tilde{J}(0)-\tilde{J}(q)\bigr].

(SM.37)

For
\delta>0
the gap increases with anisotropy, strengthening the protection of the symmetric manifold.

III.1
Weak anisotropy (
|\delta|\ll 1
): SW with isotropic
\hat{H}_{0}
.

We write
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{XXZ}}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{V}_{\delta}
with

\hat{H}_{0}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}J(r_{ij})\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{i}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{j}
(SU(2)-invariant, exact
S=N/2
degeneracy) and

\hat{V}_{\delta}=-\frac{\delta}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}J(r_{ij})\hat{S}_{i}^{z}\hat{S}_{j}^{z}
.
Because
\hat{V}_{\delta}
is two-body,
\hat{\Pi}\hat{V}_{\delta}\hat{\Pi}\neq 0
(contrast with the one-body case where
\hat{\Pi}\hat{V}_{\mathrm{inh}}\hat{\Pi}=0
).
The leading collective nonlinearity thus appears at first order in the SW expansion:

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}\hat{V}_{\delta}\hat{\Pi}=-\frac{\delta}{2}\frac{\sum_{i\neq j}J(r_{ij})}{N(N-1)}\left(\hat{S}_{z}^{2}-\frac{N}{4}\right)\hat{\Pi},

(SM.38)

reproducing Eq. (
SM.36
).
The isotropic dispersion
\varepsilon(q)=\frac{1}{2}[\tilde{J}(0)-\tilde{J}(q)]
provides the magnon gap protecting the symmetric manifold.
Higher-order SW corrections involve two insertions of
\hat{V}_{\delta}
and generate terms of order
\delta^{2}
.

III.2
Strong anisotropy (
|\delta|\gg 1
): SW with Ising
\hat{H}_{0}
.

The natural splitting is

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{XXZ}}=\hat{H}_{0,\mathrm{Ising}}+\hat{V}_{\mathrm{xy}}
,
with

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{0,\mathrm{Ising}}=-\frac{1+\delta}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}J(r_{ij})\hat{S}_{i}^{z}\hat{S}_{j}^{z},\ \ \ \hat{V}_{\mathrm{xy}}=-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i\neq j}J(r_{ij})(\hat{S}_{i}^{+}\hat{S}_{j}^{-}+\text{h.c.}).

(SM.39)

The Ising part opens a gap
\Delta\sim(1+\delta)\tilde{J}(0)
above the ferromagnetic ground states.
Second-order virtual spin-flip processes across this gap generate an effective OAT nonlinearity consistent with Eq. (
SM.36
).

The accuracy of the collective description depends on leakage out of
\hat{\Pi}
being negligible on the dynamical timescales of interest.
For
\delta=0
, the Hamiltonian is SU(2)-invariant and commutes with
\hat{\mathbf{S}}^{2}
for any translation-invariant
J(r_{ij})
; each total-spin sector is then exactly invariant.
For
\delta\neq 0
, SU(2) symmetry is broken and generically
[\hat{H}_{\mathrm{XXZ}},\hat{\mathbf{S}}^{2}]\neq 0
, so
\hat{\Pi}
is not invariant under the full dynamics.
A notable exception is the permutation-symmetric (all-to-all) case, where
J(r_{ij})=\text{const}
for all
i\neq j
and
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{XXZ}}
reduces to a polynomial in collective operators, commuting with
\hat{\mathbf{S}}^{2}
for any
\delta
.
For finite-range couplings and moderate
\delta
, the finite magnon gap suppresses the leakage rate, ensuring that the projected description remains accurate over dynamic timescales set by
1/\chi
.

III.3
Comparison between staggered XXX and long-range XXZ models

The two pathways to an effective OAT nonlinearity identified in this work have distinct perturbative structure.
In the staggered XXX model (Section
II
), the nonlinearity arises at
second order
in the one-body perturbation and scales as
\chi^{(\mathrm{field})}\sim h^{2}/[\Delta(N-1)]
, where
\Delta
is the magnon gap.
The coupling tensor
\chi_{\alpha\beta}^{(\mathrm{field})}=\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}/[N(N-1)]
contains explicit energy denominators from virtual magnon excitations.
In the XXZ model, the anisotropy is a two-body interaction that projects nontrivially onto
\hat{\Pi}
already at
first order
:
\chi^{(\mathrm{XXZ})}=-\frac{\delta}{2}\tilde{J}(0)/(N-1)
, with no energy denominators.
The gap is required in both cases, but for different reasons: in the field-induced mechanism it sets the perturbative expansion parameter
h/\Delta
, while in the XXZ case it controls the rate of leakage out of the symmetric manifold.

On a periodic ring of
N

V
Symmetric-sector fidelity

The SW transformation maps the microscopic Hamiltonian onto an effective collective model that acts entirely within the symmetric Dicke sector
S=N/2
.
To test how well the dynamics stays confined to this sector, we track the overlap

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{sym}}(t)=\langle\psi(t)|\,\hat{\Pi}\,|\psi(t)\rangle

(SM.41)

of the exact time-evolved state
|\psi(t)\rangle=e^{-i\hat{H}t}|{+}x\rangle^{\otimes N}
with the projector
\hat{\Pi}
onto
S=N/2
.
We obtain
\hat{\Pi}
by diagonalising
\hat{\mathbf{S}}^{2}
and retaining the
(N{+}1)
-dimensional eigenspace with eigenvalue
\tfrac{N}{2}(\tfrac{N}{2}+1)
.

Since
|{+}x\rangle^{\otimes N}
belongs to the symmetric sector,
F_{\mathrm{sym}}(0)=1
.
Any subsequent decay is driven by the perturbation
\hat{V}
(the staggered field
h_{z}
or the anisotropy
\delta
), which couples
S=N/2
to lower-spin subspaces.
Within the SW picture these couplings are suppressed by
\hat{V}/\Delta
, so
F_{\mathrm{sym}}
should remain close to unity whenever the perturbative condition holds.

F_{\mathrm{sym}}(t)
versus the rescaled time

for
N=8
and
10
.
In the staggered XXX model (left column) the fidelity stays above
{\sim}0.95
for moderate
h_{z}
and develops visible oscillations only when
h_{z}
approaches the magnon gap
\Delta
.
In the long-range XXZ model (right column), weakly anisotropic long-range couplings (
\delta\lesssim 0.3
, small
\gamma
) keep
F_{\mathrm{sym}}\approx 1
; larger
\delta
or
\gamma
shrink the gap and increase leakage.

VI
Analysis tool: Many-body Bell correlator

A many-body Bell inequality
is constructed under the assumption of binary outcomes of measurements of two quantities
\sigma^{(k)}_{x/y}=\pm 1
, where
k\in\{1\ldots N\}
labels
the particles
Cavalcanti et al. 2007
;
Cavalcanti et al. 2011
;
He et al. 2011
;
Niezgoda et al. 2020
;
Niezgoda and Chwedeńczuk 2021
;
Chwedeńczuk 2022
;
Płodzień et al. 2022
;
Płodzień et al. 2024a
;
Płodzień et al. 2024b
;
Hamza and Chwedeńczuk 2024
;
Płodzień et al. 2025a
;
Płodzień et al. 2025b
;
Hernández Yanes et al. 2025
.
If the
N
-body correlation function
\mathcal{E}_{N}\equiv|\langle\prod_{k=1}^{N}S_{+}^{(k)}\rangle|^{2}
is consistent with the postulates of local realism, it satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}=|\int\!\!d\lambda\,p(\lambda)\prod_{k=1}^{N}S_{+}^{(k)}(\lambda)|^{2}\leqslant\int\!\!d\lambda\,p(\lambda)\prod_{k=1}^{N}|S_{+}^{(k)}(\lambda)|^{2}=2^{-N},

(SM.42)

where
p(\lambda)
is the probability density of a hidden variable,
S_{+}^{(k)}=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{x}^{(k)}+i\sigma_{y}^{(k)})
, and
in the last step we used the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the fact that
|S_{+}^{(k)}(\lambda)|^{2}=1/2
for predetermined binary outcomes.
This is the many-body Bell inequality as its violation defies the postulate of local realism.
For qubits, the classical outcomes are replaced by quantum operators
\hat{S}_{+}^{(k)}=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{(k)}+i\hat{\sigma}_{y}^{(k)})
(we use
\hat{S}^{\alpha}=\hat{\sigma}^{\alpha}/2
throughout),
and the Bell inequality is

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}^{(q)}[\hat{\varrho}_{N}]=|{\rm Tr}\bigg[{\hat{\varrho}_{N}\,\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\hat{S}_{+}^{(k)}}\bigg]|^{2}\leqslant 2^{-N}.

(SM.43)

The above product of
N
rising operators is non-zero only if it acts on
|\uparrow\rangle^{\otimes N}
, giving
|\downarrow\rangle^{\otimes N}
. Therefore

\mathcal{E}_{N}^{(q)}
is a measure of coherence between these two, which is maximal,
\mathcal{E}_{N}^{(q)}=\frac{1}{4}
, for the GHZ state,

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|\downarrow\rangle^{\otimes N}+|\uparrow\rangle^{\otimes N}),

(SM.44)

and can be expressed as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}^{(q)}[\hat{\varrho}_{N}]=|\varrho_{\frac{N}{2},-\frac{N}{2}}|^{2}.

(SM.45)

Here,
|\varrho_{\frac{N}{2},-\frac{N}{2}}|^{2}
denotes the matrix element of
\hat{\varrho}_{N}
multiplying
|\downarrow\rangle\!\langle\uparrow|^{\otimes N}
.
It is convenient to introduce
\mathcal{Q}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(t)=N+\log_{2}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{(q)}[\hat{\varrho}_{N}(t)],

(SM.46)

since now the Bell inequality is equivalent to
\mathcal{Q}\leqslant 0
. When it is violated,
\mathcal{Q}>0
, the correlator
\mathcal{Q}
certifies the presence of Bell correlations.
Its maximal value, reached with a GHZ state, is
\mathcal{Q}_{\rm max}=N+\log_{2}\frac{1}{4}=N-2
.

The OAT protocol is a versatile method for generating states exhibiting Bell correlations of arbitrary depth
Cavalcanti et al. 2007
;

;

;

;

;
Chwedeńczuk 2022
;

;

;
Płodzień et al. 2024b
;

;

;

;

;

.
Hence our interest in mapping the short-range spin systems onto the effective OAT dynamics.
