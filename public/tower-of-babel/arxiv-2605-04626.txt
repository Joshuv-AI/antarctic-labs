Determinantal formulae for a symmetric generating function of totally symmetric plane partitions
Julia Hörmayer and Florian Schreier-Aigner

Abstract
. Ilse Fischer and the second author introduced in [Algebr. Comb. 7 (2024), no. 5, 1319–1345] a two parameter family of polynomials defined as sums over totally symmetric plane partitions and connected to alternating sign matrices and cyclically symmetric lozenge tilings of a hexagon with a triangular hole. In this paper we present several determinantal formulae leading to new lattice path models and a novel family of tableaux. The later illustrates that the polynomials of our interest can be thought of as generalisations of the three dual Littlewood identities.



1.
Introduction

Initiated by MacMahon
[
16
]
at the end of the 19th century, the study of
plane partitions
became popular within the combinatorial community starting in the second half of the last century. In particular, proving the enumeration formulae for the ten symmetry classes of plane partitions became an important research programme which was popularised by Stanley
[
18
,
19
]
and only finished in 2011 by Koutschan, Kauers, and Zeilberger
[
11
]
. First noted by Bender and Knuth
[
4
]
, the symmetry classes of plane partitions (at least those without cyclical symmetry) were observed to be connected to symmetric polynomials, allowing simple proofs for their enumeration formulae. For more details compare to the overview article by Krattenthaler
[
13
]
.
The symmetry classes of plane partitions involving a cyclical symmetry, on the other hand, turned out to be connected to
alternating sign matrices
(ASMs) introduced by Robbins and Rumsey
[
17
]
. Similarly to plane partitions, the early focus was on the enumeration of their symmetry classes; again a very challenging task which was completed only in 2017 by Behrend, Fischer and Konvalinka
[
3
]
. Over the years more numerical connections, i.e., equidistributions, between these two seemingly unrelated combinatorial objects have been found, compare for example to
[
2
,
9
,
12
]
. Nevertheless, their relation is still an unsolved mystery.

In a recent series of papers by Ilse Fischer and the second author
[
6
,
7
]
, they introduced a novel weight for
monotone triangles
, combinatorial objects generalising ASMs and in bijection with
semistandard Young tableaux. The corresponding generating functions, nowadays called the
(modified) Robbins polynomials
, are symmetric polynomials generalising Schur polynomials. In the special case corresponding to ASMs, the modified Robbins polynomials reveal a novel and unexpected connection between ASMs and plane partitions: the modified Robbins polynomial can be written in this case as a sum over
totally symmetric plane partitions
(TSPPs) where each TSPP contributes one Schur polynomial. Denote by
\mathcal{A}_{n,k}
this very generating function of TSPPs, which we call the
symmetric generating function for TSPPs
, where
n
denotes the size of the involved TSPPs and
k
is an additional parameter deforming the indexing partition of each of the Schur polynomials in a natural way; the precise definitions are given in Section
3
. As shown in
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
, the symmetric functions
\mathcal{A}_{n,k}
yield another intriguing connection to plane partitions, as they enumerate the cyclically symmetric lozenge tilings of cored hexagons with side lengths
n+2k,n,n+2k,n,n+2k,n
which generalise cyclically symmetric plane partitions.

In this paper, we study the symmetric generating function
\mathcal{A}_{n,k}
for TSPPs from an algebraic combinatorial point of view and derive variants of the Jacobi–Trudi formula, the Nägelsbach–Kostka identity, and the Giambelli identity as presented in our main theorem.

A

The

boxes (French convention). For simplicity, we identify partitions with their corresponding Young diagrams. The

The

A

\omega(P):=\prod_{e\in P}\omega(P),

and the weight of a family of paths as the product of the weights of the individual paths.

We will now show the equivalence of formulae (

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle:=\left[(-1)^{j-i}v^{j+1}\binom{i}{j}+ru^{i+1}w^{j-i}s_{(i|j+k)}(\mathbf{x})\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle:=\left[\delta_{i,j}v^{i+1}+ru^{j+1}\sum_{l}\binom{i}{i-l}s_{(l|j+k)}(\mathbf{x})\left(\frac{w}{u}\right)^{i-l}\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle:=\left[(-1)^{i+j}\binom{i}{j}\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle:=\left[\delta_{i,j}\left(\frac{u}{w}\right)^{i}\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}

\displaystyle C_{3}

\displaystyle:=\left[\delta_{i,j}\left(\frac{w}{u}\right)^{i}\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}

where the first two are just expressions (
1
) and (

) after shifting the indices, respectively. We will show the following relationship

A=C_{1}\cdot C_{2}\cdot B\cdot C_{3}.

Note that this immediately gives the equivalence of formulae (
1
) and (
4
) since
\det(C_{1})=1
and
\det(C_{2})\cdot\det(C_{3})=1
.
So we perform the following manipulations

\displaystyle C_{1}\cdot C_{2}\cdot B\cdot C_{3}

\displaystyle=C_{1}\cdot\left[\left(\frac{u}{w}\right)^{i}\left(\frac{w}{u}\right)^{j}\left(\delta_{i,j}v^{i+1}+ru^{j+1}\sum_{l=0}^{i}\binom{i}{i-l}s_{(l|j+k)}(\mathbf{x})\left(\frac{w}{u}\right)^{i-l}\right)\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=C_{1}\cdot\left[\delta_{i,j}v^{i+1}+ruw^{j}\sum_{l=0}^{i}\binom{i}{i-l}s_{(l|j+k)}(\mathbf{x})\left(\frac{u}{w}\right)^{l}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum^{n-2}_{m=0}(-1)^{i+m}\binom{i}{m}\left(\delta_{m,j}v^{m+1}+ruw^{j}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{m-l}s_{(l|j+k)}(\mathbf{x})\left(\frac{u}{w}\right)^{l}\right)\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\left[(-1)^{j-i}v^{j+1}\binom{i}{j}+ruw^{j}\sum_{m=0}^{i}\sum_{l=0}^{m}(-1)^{i+m}\binom{i}{m}\binom{m}{m-l}s_{(l|j+k)}(\mathbf{x})\left(\frac{u}{w}\right)^{l}\right]_{i,j}.

Since the left summand is already of the desired form, it suffices to calculate the coefficient of
s_{(l|j+k)}(\mathbf{x})
for a given
l
. We will show that it is
ru^{i+1}w^{j-i}
when
l=i
and

otherwise. First, assume that

. Then the coefficient indeed is

Now fix an

. Then the coefficient of

will be

and so we want to show that

Using

which evaluates to
