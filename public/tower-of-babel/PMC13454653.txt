Longitudinal Changes in Hematological Status and Functional Capacity During Hospitalization Are Associated With Clinical Outcomes in Older Patients With Acute Decompensated Heart Failure
Authors: Yuta Nakaya, Masanori Akamatsu, Akiyoshi Ogimoto, Hiroaki Kitaoka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHemoglobin (Hb) and red cell distribution width (RDW), are routinely measured hematological parameters that reflect anemia, inflammation, and nutritional status. The 6-min walk distance (6MWD) test is a well-established indicator of functional capacity. However, all these parameters are predominantly evaluated at a single time point, and the prognostic implications of their longitudinal changes during hospitalization in acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) remain unclear. Therefore, in this study we investigated the association between longitudinal changes in the Hb-to-RDW ratio (∆Hb/RDW) during hospitalization and clinical outcomes in patients with ADHF.Methods and ResultsThe data for 670 consecutive patients hospitalized with ADHF were analyzed. Hb/RDW and 6MWD were assessed at baseline and discharge, and ∆Hb/RDW was calculated. Kaplan–Meier and Cox regression analyses were used to examine the association between ∆Hb/RDW and all-cause death after discharge. During a median follow-up of 2.0 (1.0–3.3) years, 214 patients (31.9%) died. Kaplan–Meier analysis showed a significantly lower all-cause mortality rate in patients with increased ∆Hb/RDW compared with those without an increase. Exploratory analyses suggested graded differences in mortality risk when ∆Hb/RDW was considered together with changes in the 6MWD during hospitalization.ConclusionsAn increase in ∆Hb/RDW during hospitalization was associated with a lower risk of all-cause death in older patients with ADHF.

Central Figure

Heart failure (HF) has become an increasingly prevalent health problem in aging societies, and is associated with rising morbidity and mortality rates. Acute decompensated HF (ADHF) accounts for millions of hospitalizations annually, and despite advances in pharmacological and device-based therapies, the prognosis for patients with ADHF remains poor.1–3

In older patients with HF, prognosis is influenced not only by cardiac dysfunction but also by systemic conditions such as anemia, inflammation, malnutrition, and frailty.4 Hemoglobin (Hb) and red cell distribution width (RDW), routinely measured as part of a complete blood count, have been individually associated with adverse outcomes in HF and are considered indicators of systemic health status.5–9 Both Hb and RDW have been linked to inflammatory burden, nutritional impairment, and chronic disease severity.10 However, when assessed in isolation at a single time point, these hematological parameters may capture only limited aspects of the complex, multisystem vulnerability characteristic of older patients with HF.

Hospitalization for ADHF often represents a critical window during which changes in systemic condition and physical function occur simultaneously under comprehensive medical and multidisciplinary management.11 Accordingly, longitudinal changes in routinely available clinical parameters may better reflect an individual patient’s recovery trajectory than baseline values alone.

The Hb/RDW has recently been proposed as a composite hematological index that integrates information related to anemia, inflammation, and nutritional status.12 Rather than representing a disease-specific biomarker, Hb/RDW may serve as a pragmatic indicator of global physiological condition in older adults with HF. Importantly, changes in Hb/RDW (∆Hb/RDW) during hospitalization may reflect short-term improvement or deterioration in systemic health status in response to acute treatment, nutritional support, and rehabilitation, all of which are integral components of contemporary HF care in older patients.

Physical function is another key determinant of prognosis in geriatric populations with HF.13 The 6-min walk distance (6MWD) is a well-established measure of functional capacity and has been consistently associated with clinical outcomes in HF.14 Although the discharge 6MWD provides important prognostic information,15 longitudinal changes in 6MWD (∆6MWD) during hospitalization may offer additional insight by capturing short-term functional responses to treatment and rehabilitation.

Both ∆Hb/RDW and ∆6MWD can be easily assessed in routine clinical practice without specialized equipment. Evaluating these longitudinal changes in parallel may therefore provide a simple and clinically meaningful approach to characterizing recovery trajectories after ADHF, thereby integrating information on systemic condition and functional capacity. However, the prognostic implications of ∆Hb/RDW, either alone or in combination with changes in functional capacity, have not been adequately investigated in older patients hospitalized with ADHF.

Accordingly, in this study we examined the association between ∆Hb/RDW and post-discharge clinical outcomes in older patients with ADHF. In addition, we explored whether combining assessments of changes in hematological status and functional capacity provides complementary prognostic information in this population.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

This retrospective cohort study was conducted using single-center data registered with the University Hospital Medical Information Network (UMIN; ID: 000058267). We screened 1,253 consecutive patients hospitalized for ADHF at Uwajima City Hospital between January 2018, when systematic data collection began, and December 2023. Clinical data, including patient characteristics, inpatient care, and longitudinal changes in hematological parameters and physical function from admission to discharge, were extracted retrospectively from electronic medical records.

ADHF was diagnosed by a cardiologist in accordance with the 2017 Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Acute and Chronic Heart Failure (Japanese Circulation Society/Japanese Heart Failure Association).16 Inclusion criteria required that physical performance and 6MWD were assessed at both baseline and discharge, and that no blood transfusions were required.

Exclusion criteria were: age <65 years, non-ambulatory or requiring physical assistance before admission, malignant disease with a prognosis ≤6 months, missing physical performance and 6MWD measurements at baseline or discharge, requirement for advanced mechanical support (intubation, intra-aortic balloon pumping, extracorporeal membrane oxygenation) preventing physical evaluation within 4 days of admission, and hospitalization duration <7 days.

Data Collection

Data were retrieved from the hospital’s electronic medical record system. Baseline data included demographic characteristics (age, sex, and body mass index [BMI]), clinical status at admission (HF etiology, New York Heart Association [NYHA] functional classification, medications, and device therapy), laboratory data, echocardiographic findings, and comorbidities.

Nutritional status was evaluated using the Geriatric Nutritional Risk Index (GNRI), calculated as: 14.89 × serum albumin (g/dL) + 41.7 × (BMI / 22).17

GNRI <92 was defined as moderate-to-severe nutritional risk.18 Cachexia at discharge was assessed using the Evans criteria: >5% body weight loss within 1 year (or BMI <20 kg/m2) in the presence of chronic illness, plus ≥3 of the following: decreased muscle strength, fatigue, anorexia, low fat-free mass index or abnormal laboratory findings (anemia <12 g/dL, albumin <3.2 g/dL, C-reactive protein >0.5 mg/L or interleukin-6 >0.4 pg/mL).19

Physical performance measures (handgrip strength, gait speed, Short Physical Performance Battery [SPPB], and isometric knee extension strength), as well as the 6MWD, were assessed at baseline and discharge. Activities of daily living (ADL) were assessed using the Barthel Index (BI). Hospitalization-associated disability was defined as a decline ≥5 points in the BI score at discharge compared with the prehospital BI score.20

Details of hospitalization, including length of stay, initiation of routine inpatient care incorporating cardiac rehabilitation, and medications prescribed at discharge, were recorded. Discharge medications included β-blockers, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors/angiotensin-receptor blockers, mineralocorticoid-receptor antagonists, loop diuretics, vasopressin V2 receptor antagonists, angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitors, and sodium–glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors.

Physical performance assessments at baseline were performed by physical therapists when patients could safely walk ≥10 m; only measurements obtained within 4 days of admission were used. Assessments at discharge were performed on the 14th hospital day or immediately prior to discharge.

Physical Performance Assessments at Baseline and Discharge

According to the standard 6-stage cardiac rehabilitation program for HF established by the Japanese Association of Cardiac Rehabilitation, initiation of walking corresponds to stage II.21 Early assessment of physical performance within 4 days after hospitalization has prognostic value,22 so in the present study, physical performance assessments were conducted within 4 days of admission following physician approval.

The SPPB consists of standing balance, usual gait speed, and repeated chair-stand tests, each scored 0–4, with a total score of 0–12.23 Handgrip strength was measured using a Smedley-type dynamometer (Grip-D; TAKEI, Niigata, Japan) while the patient was standing. Each hand was tested twice, and the highest value was used for analysis.

Isometric knee extension strength was assessed using a hand-held dynamometer (μ–Tas; Anima Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) fixed to a rigid support.24 The patient was seated with the hip and knee joints positioned at approximately 90° of flexion. Each leg was tested twice, and the highest value was included in the analysis.

Exercise capacity was evaluated at both baseline and discharge using the 6MWD, measured according to standardized guidelines under consistent testing conditions.25

Stratification Based on ∆Hb/RDW

Complete blood counts were obtained routinely during hospitalization as part of standard clinical care. The Hb/RDW was calculated at both baseline and discharge, and the longitudinal change was defined as the discharge value minus the baseline value.

For descriptive and analytical purposes, patients were stratified into 2 groups according to the direction of ∆Hb/RDW during hospitalization: increase in Hb/RDW (∆Hb/RDW >0) and no increase or a decrease in Hb/RDW (∆Hb/RDW ≤0).

Cardiac Rehabilitation During Acute Care

Cardiac rehabilitation was provided as part of routine acute care in accordance with standard clinical practice for patients hospitalized with ADHF.21 The program followed a stepwise protocol, and all sessions were supervised by licensed physiotherapists experienced in the care of patients with acute HF. Progression of physical activity was determined based on clinical stability and physician approval.

Outcomes

The primary outcome of this study was all-cause death after discharge, and the secondary outcome was readmission for worsening HF. The time-to-event for each outcome was calculated as the number of days from hospital discharge to the first occurrence of the respective event.

Outcome data were obtained primarily from the hospital’s electronic medical record system. For patients without available follow-up information in the institutional database, additional outcome data were collected through structured telephone interviews with the patients or family members.

Statistical Analysis

The distribution of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed variables are presented as mean±standard deviation, and non-normally distributed variables as median (interquartile range). Categorical variables are summarized as counts and percentages. Between-group comparisons were performed using the unpaired t-test or Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables, and the chi-square test for categorical variables, as appropriate. A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Time-to-event analyses were conducted using Kaplan–Meier survival curves and log-rank tests. Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to evaluate the association between ∆Hb/RDW during hospitalization and all-cause death after discharge. Hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated.

Multivariable Cox models were adjusted for established prognostic factors selected a priori based on clinical relevance and previous literature, including age, sex, log-transformed N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide, left ventricular ejection fraction, chronic kidney disease, atrial fibrillation, diabetes mellitus, β-blockers, and angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors/angiotensin-receptor blockers. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed using Schoenfeld residuals.

We additionally performed a Fine–Gray subdistribution hazard analysis for HF readmission, treating all-cause death as a competing event, using the same covariates as in the primary multivariable Cox models.

As supportive analyses, the prognostic associations of longitudinal changes in Hb (∆Hb) and RDW (∆RDW), analyzed separately, were also examined. In addition, ∆Hb/RDW was analyzed as a continuous variable in the multivariable Cox model, scaled per 0.01-unit increase, and a restricted cubic spline analysis was performed to evaluate the functional form of the association between ∆Hb/RDW and all-cause death. Sensitivity analyses were performed to assess the robustness of the findings, including models additionally adjusted for Hb/RDW at admission, contemporary guideline-directed medical therapy (use of mineralocorticoid-receptor antagonists, angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitors, and sodium–glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors),26,27 and nutritional and prehospital ADL (GNRI and prehospital BI).

Exploratory subgroup analyses were performed to examine the consistency of the association between ∆Hb/RDW and all-cause death across clinically relevant subgroups. Subsequently, to explore the combined clinical relevance of systemic and functional recovery, patients were descriptively categorized according to ∆Hb/RDW and ∆6MWD during hospitalization. Kaplan–Meier curves were constructed to illustrate outcome differences among groups.

All statistical analyses were performed using EZR version 1.6.8 (Saitama Medical Center, Jichi Medical University), a graphical user interface for R, together with R software.

Patients in the increased ∆Hb/RDW group had a higher BMI and shorter hospital stay than those in the decreased ∆Hb/RDW group. The use of catecholamines and adaptive servo-ventilation during hospitalization was lower, and an ischemic etiology of HF was less frequent in the increased ∆Hb/RDW group. Among the comorbidities, diabetes mellitus and dyslipidemia were less common, whereas atrial fibrillation was more prevalent. Regarding discharge medications, the increased ∆Hb/RDW group had a higher prescription rate of mineralocorticoid-receptor antagonists, but lower use of angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors/angiotensin-receptor blockers and angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitors.

No significant differences were observed between groups in prevalence of cachexia and hospitalization-associated disability (Table 1), nor in physical performance measures at baseline or discharge, or their changes during hospitalization (Supplementary Table).

Association Between ∆Hb/RDW and Clinical Outcomes

During a median follow-up of 2.0 years (interquartile range, 1.0–3.3 years), all-cause death occurred in 95 patients (26.3%) in the increased ∆Hb/RDW group and in 116 patients (37.3%) in the decreased ∆Hb/RDW group. HF readmission occurred in 197 patients (54.6%) and 183 patients (58.8%) in the respective groups. Kaplan–Meier curves demonstrated that the increased ∆Hb/RDW group had a significantly lower all-cause mortality rate (log-rank, P<0.001), but HF readmission did not differ between groups (log-rank, P=0.46) (Figure 2). Sensitivity Analysis

The association between increased ∆Hb/RDW and lower all-cause mortality rate remained consistent across the sensitivity analyses. In the baseline-adjusted model (including Hb/RDW at admission), the adjusted HRs were 0.67 (95% CIs 0.49–0.93; P<0.05). In the drug-adjusted model (including mineralocorticoid-receptor antagonists, angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitors, and sodium–glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors), the adjusted HRs were 0.72 (95% CIs 0.52–0.99; P<0.05) (Table 3). In the nutritional and ADL-adjusted model (including GNRI and prehospital BI), the association between ∆Hb/RDW and all-cause death was attenuated and no longer statistically significant (HRs 0.73, 95% CIs 0.52–1.01; P=0.060). Discussion

In this retrospective cohort study of older patients hospitalized with ADHF, an increase in ∆Hb/RDW during hospitalization was associated with a lower risk of all-cause death after discharge, independent of established prognostic factors. In contrast with single-time-point measurements obtained at baseline or discharge, ∆Hb/RDW may capture dynamic alterations in hematological status during the acute and vulnerable phase of HF hospitalization, thereby providing additional clinically relevant information.

Low levels of Hb reflect impaired oxygen-carrying capacity and reduced physiological resilience, including vulnerability to malnutrition and infection.28 RDW has been associated with adverse outcomes across a wide spectrum of cardiovascular diseases, including both acute and chronic HF,29,30 and short-term increases in RDW during acute illness have been linked to a worse prognosis,31 potentially reflecting heightened inflammatory burden, impaired erythropoiesis, and overall physiological stress. These mechanisms may operate concurrently through pathways such as hepcidin-mediated iron restriction, reduced erythropoietin activity, shortened erythrocyte lifespan, and increased release of immature erythrocytes.4

Consistent with previous reports suggesting that single hematological markers may be insufficient to capture the complexity of HF prognosis, ∆Hb or ∆RDW alone were not significantly associated with death in the present study.8 In contrast, the composite ∆Hb/RDW, integrating information from both parameters, demonstrated an association with outcomes in the primary analysis and in several sensitivity analyses, although this association was attenuated after additional adjustment for nutritional and functional status. These findings suggested that ∆Hb/RDW may reflect broader changes in systemic health status during acute treatment, partly capturing patients’ underlying nutritional and functional vulnerability compared with isolated hematologic abnormalities alone.

In the competing risk analysis accounting for all-cause death, ∆Hb/RDW was not significantly associated with HF readmission. Therefore, the prognostic relevance of ∆Hb/RDW in this cohort appeared to be more closely related to death than to HF readmission. HF readmissions are often influenced by post-discharge factors, including medication adherence, dietary management, and social support, which may not be adequately captured by short-term in-hospital changes.32 Previous studies have shown that comprehensive post-discharge management programs can reduce readmission risk, indicating that determinants of readmission may differ from those influencing death during follow-up.11

Although the primary analysis used a threshold of 0 to distinguish the directional change in ∆Hb/RDW, this cutoff should be interpreted as a pragmatic classification rather than a biologically validated threshold. When ∆Hb/RDW was analyzed as a continuous variable, the association with all-cause death did not reach statistical significance. However, restricted cubic spline analysis suggested that the relationship may be non-linear. These findings indicated that the prognostic information conveyed by ∆Hb/RDW may not be adequately captured by a simple linear model and supported the use of 0 as a practical boundary for directional change rather than a definitive biological threshold.

Physical function represents another key dimension of recovery in older patients with ADHF. Although a minimal clinically important difference of ≥30 m in 6MWD has been established in chronic HF,33 evidence regarding longitudinal changes in functional capacity during hospitalization for ADHF remains limited. In the present study, exploratory analyses combining ∆Hb/RDW and ∆6MWD suggested graded differences in mortality risk across recovery patterns. However, these findings should be interpreted cautiously, as they were descriptive in nature and not intended to establish causal or synergistic effects.

Collectively, the present results support the concept that recovery from older patients with ADHF is a multidimensional process involving systemic and functional domains. Simple, routinely available measures that reflect longitudinal changes during hospitalization may help characterize recovery trajectories, although further studies are needed to clarify their clinical utility and generalizability.

Study Limitations

First, patients unable to complete both baseline and discharge 6MWD assessments were excluded, potentially introducing selection bias and favoring those with better functional status. Patients unable to perform the 6MWD assessment likely represent a more severely ill or frail population; therefore, the present findings may not be generalizable to all patients hospitalized with ADHF. Second, this was a single-center retrospective study, and the generalizability of the findings should be interpreted with caution. Third, although ∆Hb alone was not associated with prognosis, detailed information regarding anemia management during hospitalization and after discharge was not available. Given updates to anemia treatment strategies in the JCS/JHFS 2025 Guideline on Diagnosis and Treatment of Heart Failure,34 further studies incorporating detailed therapeutic data are warranted to better clarify the relationship between hematologic changes and clinical outcomes. Finally, the content and intensity of cardiac rehabilitation during hospitalization were not systematically quantified. As a result, the potential influence of specific exercise prescriptions or training intensity on ∆Hb/RDW could not be determined in the present study.

Conclusions

In older patients hospitalized with ADHF, an increase in ∆Hb/RDW during hospitalization was associated with a lower risk of all-cause death after discharge. ∆Hb/RDW may reflect dynamic changes in systemic health status during the acute phase of HF management. Exploratory analyses further suggested that considering changes in both hematological status and functional capacity may help characterize post-discharge risk profiles.
