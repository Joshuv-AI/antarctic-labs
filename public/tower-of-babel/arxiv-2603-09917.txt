Resolved molecular gas and star-formation in massive unquenched spirals
R. Cologni, S. Flesch, P. Salomé, D. Le Borgne, M. Boquien, J. Freundlich, P. Guillard, U. Lisenfeld, F. Combes, L. Bouscasse

Abstract
Recent studies have uncovered a rare population of super-massive (M ⋆ ≳ 10 11 \gtrsim 10^{11} M ⊙ ), yet actively star-forming spiral galaxies – Super Spiral Galaxies (SSGs) – whose existence challenges classical mass- and environment-quenching scenarios. We investigate the resolved star-forming and molecular-gas properties of the nearby SSG UGC 8179 ( z = 0.052 z=0.052 , log ⁡ ( M ⋆ /M ⊙ ) = 11.62 \log(\text{M${}_{\star}$/M${}_{\odot}$})=11.62 ) in order to assess whether its local star formation (SF) follows the same physical processes as those observed in typical Star-Forming Main Sequence (SFMS) spirals. We combined the first spatially resolved CO(1–0) interferometric observations of an SSG with pixel-by-pixel SED fitting, based on archival UV–to–mid-IR imaging. Our 3 ​ ″ × 3 ​ ″ 3\arcsec\times 3\arcsec ( ∼ 25 \sim 25 kpc 2 ) pixel maps provide spatially resolved measurements of M ⋆ , SFR and molecular gas surface densities across its extended disc. UGC 8179 hosts a massive rotating molecular gas reservoir of M H2 = 1.02 × 10 10 {}_{\text{H2}}=1.02\times 10^{10} M ⊙ , yielding a standard molecular gas fraction and a typical depletion time of ∼ 1 \sim 1 Gyr in the region observed by NOEMA, despite its extreme mass. We derived lower limits of log 10 ⁡ f mol ≥ − 1.61 ± 0.06 \log_{10}f_{\text{mol}}\geq-1.61\pm 0.06 and log 10 ⁡ τ dep ≥ − 8.82 ± 0.13 \log_{10}\tau_{\text{dep}}\geq-8.82\pm 0.13 at the scale of the galaxy. The large spatial extent of UGC 8179 enables us to probe low surface-density regimes hardly accessible in nearby disks ( Σ ⋆ < 10 7 \Sigma_{\star}<10^{7} M ⊙ kpc -2 ; Σ SFR < 10 − 3.5 \Sigma_{\rm SFR}<10^{-3.5} M ⊙ yr -1 kpc -2 ). All three resolved scaling relations (rSFMS, rKS and rMGMS) are well defined. The rKS slope ( 0.87 ± 0.09 0.87\pm 0.09 ) is broadly consistent with unity, indicating standard local SF processes. The rSFMS shows a shallower global slope ( 0.80 ± 0.02 0.80\pm 0.02 ) due to a central suppression in sSFR ( ∼ − 0.5 \sim-0.5 dex), but a two-component fit restores agreement with literature relations at Σ ⋆ ≲ 10 7.2 \Sigma_{\star}\lesssim 10^{7.2} M ⊙ kpc -2 . This break suggests the influence of a bulge – and possibly a bar – driving a transition to a more dynamically regulated SF regime in the inner disc. A similar flattening in the rMGMS supports this interpretation. UGC 8179 provides evidence that SSGs can sustain standard local SF processes while exhibiting central dynamical regulation at high stellar surface densities. Extending this analysis to our full sample of 19 SSGs and nearby massive unquenched spirals will enable us to test whether such regulation is a common feature among massive unquenched spirals.

The overwhelming majority of massive galaxies (M

Galaxies are seldom isolated systems, in fact they are likely to experience various types of interactions throughout their lifetime which can lead to environmental quenching. Dynamical disruptions can arise from high-speed fly-by encounters, causing tidal stripping, or from ram-pressure stripping if the galaxy is cruising in the intra-cluster medium ripping off its gaseous material
(
55

(

3
SED fitting method

A variety of photometric prescriptions for SFR exist in literature using UV light and mid-IR emission to account both for hot, newly born stars, and the warm dust continuum emission, excited by these young stars
(
57
;
18
;
64
;
34
, e.g.,)
. The total stellar mass M
⋆
is generally derived from near-infrared light (e.g., at 3.6
\mu
m from
Spitzer
/IRAC or 3.4
\mu
m from WISE1), which is dominated by the emission of old stars, and calculate M
⋆
assuming a constant stellar mass-to-light ratio
\Upsilon_{\star}

(
44
, e.g.,)
.
However, this method has limitations: the assumption of a constant mass-to-light ratio overestimates M
⋆
by
\sim
0.3-0.5 dex relative to SED-fitting results, especially since the deviation seems to depend on M
⋆

(
61
)
, and our focus are high-mass galaxies.
95
and L23 rely on these recipes to estimate the global SFR and M
⋆
of SSGs, and their sanity checks with global SED fitting point toward an overall good agreement.

Most of these single- or dual-band SFR and M
⋆
prescriptions were established using SED fitting
(
27
, e.g.,)
and rely on various assumptions and specific models that may not apply to SSGs. Hence, we chose to straightforwardly apply the SED fitting method to UGC 8179. In that way, we ensure a refined computation of SFR and M
⋆
tailored on spatially resolved properties. There exist many different SED fitting codes which base their computation on various assumptions and code architectures, which subsequently result in a dispersion of estimates for a given property as shown by
96
. We chose to use CIGALE
(
17
, Code Investigating GALaxy Emission)
. It relies on energy balance between the absorption of UV to visible light by dust, and its re-emission in the mid- and far-IR. One significant asset of CIGALE is its versatility: the diversity of modules used to build the library of models for the SED fitting allows easy tailoring of the parameter-space one wishes to explore.

In this section, we motivate the modules used and the ranges of values adopted for the free parameters therein, and we describe the data pre-processing to perform resolved SED fitting. For a more comprehensive description of the chosen modules, see
17
.

3.1
SED fitting models

CIGALE follows the standard steps when building models: computation of the SFH and of the unattenuated stellar spectrum from single stellar populations (SSPs), followed by computation of the lines and continuum nebular emission from the Lyman continuum photons emitted. Next, dust attenuation is applied to the nebular and stellar emissions, and the absorbed luminosity is used to compute the mid- and far-IR dust emission following the energy-balance principle. Lastly, redshifting of the model and absorption by the intergalactic medium are taken into account. The code has a variety of modules for each physical component.

We chose a classical delayed SFH with an optional constant burst or quench recent event (
sfhdelayed_bq
module). We explored quite widely the parameter space of both the age of the burst or quench episode (from 10 to 500 Myrs in look-back time), and the ratio of after/before spans more than 2 orders of magnitude. We assumed the age of the first-born stars to be 12.7 Gyrs, corresponding to a birth around the epoch of reionization (
z\sim 6
).
Regarding the stellar continuum, we used SSPs of
20
and restricted the values of stellar metallicity to 0.008 and 0.02, judging lower metallicities would be irrelevant for a nearby and evolved galaxy like UGC 8179. For nebular emission, we used
nebular
module, where emission lines are computed using the photoionization model
CLOUDY

(
46
;
123
)
. Concerning dust attenuation, we adopted the attenuation law described by
28
: the light of young stars (
<10
Myrs) suffers from the attenuation by both the dust in their birth cloud, and the ISM dust. We needed to finely sample a range in
A_{V}
to (i) fit the UV-optical part of the spectrum, (ii) explore the degeneracy between the dust reddening and the light from the old stellar population, and (iii) create a wider lever arm on IR emission. Finally, the dust emission was computed from templates of
37
based on nearby star-forming galaxies. We adopted the fiducial value of
\alpha=2
for the power law slope and allowed some freedom on radiation field and PAH to fit the mid-IR measurements of WISE bands. The intergalactic medium absorption follows the prescription of
89
. The entry parameters and module names can be found in Table
2
.

2

\alpha_{\text{CO}}

(

as well as the molecular gas fraction

5.2
Lower sSFR in the center

As suggested by
87
, SFE could be lowered in a galaxy with a large bulge-to-disk ratio, thanks to the stabilization of the gas against collapse by the bulge’s gravitational potential (i.e., the morphological quenching scenario). Several studies have shown the link between bulge-growth and the onset of star formation inefficiency at fixed stellar mass, implying that although a bulge is not sufficient, it is a required condition to explain quenching
(
40
;
41
;
75
)
. Therefore, we decomposed the light profile of UGC 8179 in two components (bulge and disc) using the 2D morphology fitting code
galfit

(
99
;
100
)
in order to estimate a bulge-to-total luminosity ratio
B/T
as described in
49
(Section 3.4 therein). They carried out the fits on HST ACS images in the F814W
I
-band for galaxies at
z=0.5-0.8
, which correspond to rest-frame SDSS-
r
and -
g
bands. We applied their method on the SDSS-
r
band background-subtracted, galactic extinction-corrected images. We set the sersic indices of the disk and the bulge to be 1 and 4, respectively. We found for UGC 8179 a bulge-to-total ratio of
B/T=0.43\pm 0.05
, typical of intermediate spirals (Sb, Sbc). Thus, this bulge could physically cause the downturn of sSFR in the central 1
R_{e}
. It also is compatible with the inside-out growth paradigm, stating an earlier formation in the central region, later on reaching higher radii, as the center becomes more evolved and shows lower sSFR
(
38
;
81
;
54
, e.g.,)
.
1
argue similarly that the central decline of sSFR is consistent with an older population of stars in the bugle. It is worth noting however, albeit the drop in sSFR, that our results do not indicate that the SFE is affected by the bulge, as
\tau_{\text{dep}}
keeps decreasing towards the center (Fig.
8
).

The residuals between the model and the observations reveal the spiral arms along with an oblong shape at the center of the disk which supports our assumption of a stellar bar (mentioned in Sec.
4.1
).
51
argue that the presence of a strong bar plays an important role in rapidly quenching the SF in the central kpc of discs, for massive galaxies above a
z
-dependent threshold M
⋆

>\text{M}_{\text{knee}}\propto(1+z)^{2}(=10^{9.5}
for local galaxies). Such a stellar structure can be responsible for turbulence and shear, thereby delaying SF. Moreover, we do not rule out that potential past starburst episodes might have induced strong stellar feedback in the bulge region, subsequently heating and dispersing the molecular gas.

On the other hand, the presence of a bulge implies higher stellar density in the inner disc. This can lead to the drag of the inner most pixels towards higher
\Sigma_{\star}
in the rSFMS and the rMGMS, hence a flattening of the relations. This increase in bulge-mass has already been invoked in past studies to explain the sublinearity of the SFMS at high M
⋆
and thus could partly explain the observed bend in our rSFMS, as well as our lower slope in the rMGMS.

Except for A22 and Q21, the comparison studies in this paper use H
α
luminosity as a proxy for SFR. There are limitations to the estimation of SFRs through H
α
luminosity, as SF is not the only source of ionizing photons. Indeed, other excitation mechanisms such as AGNs or diffuse ionized gas (DIG) can contribute to the H
α
luminosity, thereby leading one to overestimate the SFR. One would also over-correct with the Balmer decrement, making it seem like more H
\beta
is attenuated than is the case. In the BPT diagram of UGC 8179 (see Fig.

However, in this method, the calibration constant relies on the assumption of a continuous SF period of at least 100 Myr
(
74
)
, while H
\alpha
only traces young stars (
<10
Myrs) and SFHs can vary and the calibration constant with it
(
16
)
. Another key assumption concerns the ionizing photons absorption by dust, namely that the latter absorbs no Lyman continuum photons. Yet, studies show that ionizing photons are indeed absorbed by dust and helium, leaving
\sim 57
% to ionize the hydrogen
(
120
)
. This hypothesis thus leads one to overestimate the SFR in dusty environments, and MaNGA measurements of UGC 8179 indicate such a dust-rich bulge. However, IFS observations are a wealth we exploited in our study.

We investigated the robustness of our SFR estimate from SED-fitting by modeling new SEDs while adding H
\alpha
and H
\beta
fluxes for the pixels concerned. The MaNGA survey (Mapping Nearby Galaxies at Apache Point Observatory) is an integral-field spectroscopic survey of
\sim
10 000 nearby galaxies
(
21
)
. It uses the 2.5 m Sloan telescope
(
56
)
and the BOSS spectrographs
(
118
)
with a spectral coverage of
3\,600\,\mathrm{\textup{\AA}}
to
10\,300\,\mathrm{\textup{\AA}}
. UGC 8179 is part of the “color enhanced” main sample of MaNGA, designed to populate less dense parts of the NUV-i vs
M_{i}
color-magnitude diagram. The observations are further described in table
6
. Albeit a more constraining FoV than the HPBW of NOEMA, the
\sim 32\arcsec
of the IFS (once convolved and reprojected to the 3
\arcsec
-wide pixel grid) covers all the pixels where the CO signal is above the
4\penalty\ \sigma
detection threshold. The H
\alpha
and H
\beta
fluxes were added on a separate run of CIGALE, allowing us to compare the results with and without the ionized gas measurements. This comparison is illustrated in Figure
9

\text{SFR}_{\text{10}}
and
\text{SFR}_{\text{100}}
are shown in Fig.
9

Focusing on the rSFMS, we notice that the uncertainties dramatically diminish for the pixels with new flux inputs, and even more so for
\text{SFR}_{\text{10}}

\alpha
and H

). This allows to recover a linear fit closer to unity, namely
a_{\text{rKS}}^{\text{10,H}\alpha\text{H}\beta}=1.12\pm 0.06
(compared to
a_{\text{rKS}}^{\text{10}}=0.59\pm 0.08
without the added fluxes). Therefore, it appears CIGALE misses some of the recent SF (i.e., in the past 10 Myrs) without the ionized gas fluxes entries.

On the other hand, we find no significant difference in
\text{SFR}_{\text{100}}
and M
⋆
when adding the H
\alpha
and H
\beta
fluxes, as the offsets of the linear fits are enclosed within the scatter of our fiducial results. We note that the rKS with
\text{SFR}_{\text{100}}
(bottom-left panel) exhibits a slightly steeper slope with MaNGA measurements than without (
a_{\text{rKS}}^{\text{100,H}\alpha\text{H}\beta}=1.00\pm 0.07
). This reduces the slight gap with comparison studies which base their SFR on H
\alpha
measurements:
a_{\text{rKS}}^{\text{L19}}=1.05
, and

.

Lastly when comparing
\text{SFR}_{\text{100}}

\text{SFR}_{\text{10}}

In conclusion, the addition of ionized gas data to the flux inputs of the SED fits is critical when it comes to recent SFR (namely

6
Summary and conclusions

95
estimated that SSGs represent 6% of the galaxies in their mass range at
z<0.3
, thereby constituting a rare population of objects whose evolutionary pathways differed from classical galaxy evolution scenarios. Studying SSGs offers substantial insights into the mechanisms that normally shut down star formation in massive galaxies. In the course of this study, we investigated the resolved molecular gas content of UGC 8179, a nearby (
z=0.052
) super massive, actively star-forming spiral galaxy, along with its global and resolved star-forming properties. Our main results are:

•

We detected a replenished molecular gas reservoir through CO observation with IRAM/NOEMA interferometer. It allowed us to measure a molecular gas mass
\text{M}_{\text{H2}}=(1.02\pm 0.03)\times 10^{10}
M
⊙
.

•

We find that in the case of UGC 8179, it is essential that we adopt a metallicity-dependent conversion-factor, considering its very high metallicity. We showed how a constant
\alpha_{\text{CO}}
, even a very conservative value like the one adopted by L23, could lead to incorrect conclusions on the SF conditions and processes in the disk, and most importantly in the central region.

•

We developed a SED-fitting-based method using CIGALE in order to probe the global and resolved star-forming properties of massive, extended and unquenched spiral galaxies, utilizing archival photometric data from the UV to the mid-IR wavelengths range. The spatial resolution adopted was limited by the PSF of
WISE3
band to
3\arcsec\times 3\arcsec
pixels, which results in a deprojected surface of
\sim\penalty\ 25
kpc
2
per pixel. The consistency of this method from local to global scale is confirmed by the good agreement between the latter and the integrated properties of the resolved outputs.

•

Our global
\text{SFR}_{\text{100}}
and M
⋆
estimates confirm that UGC 8179 despite its large stellar mass (M
⋆

=11.62\pm 0.06
) is on the high-mass end of the SFMS with
\text{SFR}_{\text{100}}=15.68\pm 4.57
M
⊙
yr
-1
. In addition, we confirm its gas-rich nature with a molecular gas mass fraction
\log_{10}f_{\text{mol}}\geq-1.61\pm 0.06
, even though we recover a less extreme value than L23. Similarly, we do not reach their conclusion on a higher
\tau_{\text{dep}}
as we measure
\log_{10}\tau_{\text{dep}}^{100}\geq 8.82
, and a local estimate of
\log_{10}\tau_{\text{dep}}^{100}=9.04\pm 0.11
in the inner part of the disc, which is compatible with MS spiral galaxies measurements.

•

We investigated the three star-formation-related resolved scaling relations (namely the rSFMS, the rKS and the rMGMS relations) for UGC 8179, and compared them to SFMS galaxies resolved scaling-relations from the literature. We find all quantities to be well correlated. The very specific characteristics of this super spiral galaxy (its large mass and spatial extent) allowed us to explore low-surface density regimes that are difficult to access and thus little studied (
\Sigma_{\star}<10^{7}\penalty\ \text{M${}_{\odot}$\penalty\ kpc}^{-2}
and
\Sigma_{\text{SFR100}}<10^{-3.5}\penalty\ \text{M${}_{\odot}$\penalty\ yr}^{-1}\text{\penalty\ kpc}^{-2}
).

•

More specifically, we find a rKS relation in reasonable consistency with unity (
a_{\text{rKS}}=0.87\pm 0.09
), and our data is matching the literature fits which suggests that standard local star formation processes are at play in the molecular gas of this super spiral galaxy.

•

The rSFMS is reasonably compatible with other studies at similar spatial kpc resolution, albeit a lower slope (
a_{\text{rSFMS}}=0.80\pm 0.02
) when we use a single scaling relation. This is because the rSFMS seems to experience a change in behavior in the bulge region, and a corresponding decrease in the sSFR radial profile in the center. Fitting the relation with two components makes the relation even more consistent with the rSFMS from the literature up to values of
\log_{10}(\Sigma_{\star}/\text{[M${}_{\odot}$\penalty\ kpc}^{-2}])<7.2
. Above this value, a second but shallower relation is possible, and could be explained by the build-up of a non-star-forming bulge at higher stellar surface density and smaller radial distances.

•

This flattening is also present in the rMGMS in the inner disk and could be partly induced by the enhanced
\Sigma_{\star}
due to the bulge, as the
f_{\text{mol}}
also suggests. The plausible presence of a stellar bar hints at a dynamically regulated SF, rather than a purely gas-regulated inner disc. However, the rMGMS is less constrained than the other scaling relations because of the absence of molecular gas detections at low surface densities (
\Sigma_{\star}<10^{7}\penalty\ \text{M${}_{\odot}$\penalty\ kpc}^{-2}
and
\Sigma_{\text{mol}}<10^{6}\penalty\ \text{M${}_{\odot}$\penalty\ kpc}^{-2}
, a regime at which the transition from atomic gas to molecular gas occurs.)

UGC 8179 is part of a greater sample of galaxies constituted by SSGs and less massive, yet remarkably baryon-rich massive unquenched spirals. We chose to developed on this remarkably extended and star-forming target, allowing us to explore a low
\Sigma_{\star}
range, which is seldom investigated, even in local spirals. In a forthcoming paper, we shall apply this method to the rest of the sample (19 sources) and explore whether discrepancy arise from morphology, environment or nuclear activity. A comprehensive survey of the molecular gas content in a galaxy such as UGC 8179 at greater radii could significantly improve our resolved study of its properties across its extended disc.
