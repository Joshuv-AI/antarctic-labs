 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report evaluates the benefits of decommissioning six legacy FFESD purchased HPC
clusters and consolidating services and workloads into a new HPC cluster named HELIOS. The
findings demonstrate significant reductions in the data center power and cooling requirements,
data center footprint, and operational overhead, while simultaneously increasing computational
capacity.

 CLUSTER OVERVIEW

In the summer of 2023, FFESD purchased new infrastructure, compute, and storage hardware
to consolidate the aging FFESD clusters into a single offering. This new cluster, deployed and
managed by ITSD HPC, was named HELIOS. Consolidating not only hardware resources but
also user data and software required careful design, deployment, and testing before the cluster
came online in the fall of 2024.

Once HELIOS was fully functional and supporting user needs, the project of decommissioning
the older cluster hardware began. Known as the “Legacy Clusters”, there were six clusters that
had to be reviewed, inventoried, and its hardware excised. The final cluster, ROSS, was
excised from the data center on May 20, 2025.

Table 1 lists all FFESD Legacy Clusters, purchase and end dates; however, the findings and
analyses in this report are based only on the six clusters that remained - APOLLO, ARTEMIS,
LIBBY, LILLI, ROMULUS, and ROSS - when Helios became available for service.

and cooling cost savings to those of Helios. Power usage directly translates into heat output,
which in turn drives cooling requirements and the associated operational expenses in the data

 CONCLUSIONS

The excising of the six FFESD Legacy Clusters, along with consolidating systems and services
into the HELIOS cluster, has produced clear operational and financial benefits. Power
consumption decreased approximately 58%, which directly lowers the data center’s cooling load
by 26 tons of refrigeration, equivalent to nearly 316,000 BTU/hr. This reduction translates into
lower annual operating costs for both electrical and mechanical cooling systems.

At the same time, compute core capacity increased by 21%, enabling higher throughput and
more efficient use of data center resources within the same rack footprint. This transition also
simplified system administration by eliminating the complexity of maintaining multiple aging
clusters, reducing maintenance overhead, and standardizing the software and user
environment.

The resulting configuration positions FFESD and ITSD HPC to meet evolving research
demands, reduce operational costs, and align with institutional Operational Excellence
performance pillars, while achieving energy efficiency and sustainability goals.
