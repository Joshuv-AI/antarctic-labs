Public Executive Summary

  This project has advanced the understanding of ammonia synthesis by developing novel
thermochemical catalysts, setting a new benchmark for energy and carbon efficiency in large-
scale bulk chemicals production. This research leverages a proprietary computational discovery
platform and high-throughput experimental validation, significantly accelerating catalyst
optimization and enabling ammonia synthesis at lower temperature and pressure conditions
than conventional processes. As a result, green ammonia can be produced at a cost that meets
or exceeds established targets, supporting a viable pathway toward decarbonized ammonia for
fuel, fertilizers, and hydrogen supply applications.

The project expands the catalyst material solution space through advanced computational
tools, coupled with rapid experimental testing. Copernic’s technology delivers a step-change in
catalyst performance: the newly developed base-metal catalyst achieves ammonia yields 8–10
times higher than state-of-the-art Ru-based catalysts at lower temperatures, and more than
double the yield at elevated temperatures. In addition, the catalyst shows sustained
performance in long-term stability tests and is being scaled to pilot quantities, supporting
robust technical feasibility claims.

Extensive modeling and techno-economic analysis show that integrating Copernic’s catalyst in
ammonia synthesis loops enable the levelized cost of ammonia (LCOA) to be reduced by up to
25%, achieving a projected LCOA of $319/ton, which falls within or below contemporary targets
for cost-competitive green ammonia. The catalysts are compatible with flexible reactor designs,
facilitating rapid adjustment to variable renewable power supplies. Scale-up pathways have also
been de-risked by ongoing recipe transfer to large-scale continuous production and by
developing retrofit business strategies for existing ammonia plants. This underscores both the
technical effectiveness and direct economic benefit of the technology.

The advance of low-carbon, cost-competitive ammonia synthesis supports deep decarbonization
not only of chemical manufacturing but also of other sectors such as shipping, where green
ammonia may become the predominant maritime fuel. The broader impacts include enhancing
energy security, reducing greenhouse gas emissions, and supporting domestic leadership in
sustainable chemical process technology. The project has also secured multiple U.S. patent
filings, ensuring the dissemination and future commercialization of these advances for lasting
public benefit.

The project involved advanced computer modeling at two key stages: catalyst discovery and
process techno-economic analysis.

Model Description
  • Catalyst Discovery Platform: Copernic utilized a proprietary computational protocol in
 partnership with Schrödinger for in-silico screening of catalyst leads. This multiscale
 approach integrated quantum chemistry, machine learning, and materials informatics to
 evaluate hundreds of base-metal catalyst compositions for ammonia synthesis
 performance, stability, and cost. The platform rapidly filtered candidates by predicted
 reaction energetics and structural stability before advancing the most promising to
 laboratory synthesis and testing.
  • Process Modeling/TEA: For process optimization and cost estimation, the project used
 ASPEN modeling of the ammonia synthesis loop and overall plant. The ASPEN model
 simulated feed, reaction, and separation stages for both conventional and “green”
 ammonia production, with specific focus on the lower operating temperature and
 pressure enabled by Copernic’s novel catalyst. The same process model incorporated
 key economic parameters to derive levelized cost of ammonia (LCOA) projections.

The model and results are proprietary and were not presented in peer-reviewed publications.

Project Activities

The project focused on developing a next-generation, base-metal catalyst for ammonia
synthesis designed to dramatically reduce energy consumption and carbon emissions from
global ammonia production. The primary approach combined proprietary computer-aided
catalyst discovery—leveraging computational modeling (density functional theory) and high-
throughput experiment—with rigorous techno-economic modeling and experimental
validation. The central hypothesis was that coupling advanced modeling with high-throughput-
experimentation would yield a drop-in catalyst that operates at lower temperatures and
pressures, delivering substantial OpEx and CapEx savings for both new and retrofit plants while
maintaining reliability and scalability. The project’s results exceeded expectations: Copernic’s
catalyst outperformed state-of-the-art Ru- and Fe-based catalysts in both modeled and
experimental settings, enabled robust operation under milder conditions, and achieved green
ammonia production at a modeled levelized cost of $319/ton. A modification to the award was
made via Amendment 002 in 2023, refining the milestones and deliverables to better reflect
realistic goals based on techno-economic analysis, and all updated objectives were ultimately
met or surpassed on schedule.
