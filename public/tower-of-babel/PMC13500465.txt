Inductor-capacitor gain cell-based non-isolated high step-up converter for DC microgrid
Authors: M Prabhakar, Amaleswari Rajulapati, A Sudha, R Arul
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this paper, a high-gain DC-DC converter with an ultra-step-up voltage gain value of 20 is introduced. The proposed converter is synthesized using two stages – stage 1 yields a voltage gain which is double that of a classical boost converter (CBC) and stage 2 employs an inductor-capacitor gain cell (ICGC) to obtain a quadratic voltage gain function. Since a voltage gain extension technique is employed, the voltage stress on the switches in stage 1 is significantly reduced. The proposed gain extension hypothesis is experimentally validated through a 20 V to 400 V, 200 W prototype converter that operates at 94.5% full-load efficiency. The proposed converter regulates the output voltage to 400 V over a wide range of input voltage and load current variations with minimal overshoots and undershoots. Further, the maximum voltage stress on two switches switch is only 10% of the output voltage. Due to the interleaved mechanism employed in stage 1, the input current is smooth and ripple-free. Based on the comparison with several state-of-the converters, the unique advantages of the proposed converter are clearly validated and found suitable for DC microgrid application.Keywords: DC-DC converters, Power conversion, Power electronics, DC microgridsSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

The tangible contribution and penetration of renewable energy sources (RES) such as photovoltaic (PV) systems, wind energy conversion systems (WECS), and fuel cells along with battery-based energy storage system (BESS) has accelerated the development of DC microgrids and DC distribution systems1,2. DC microgrids eliminate multiple AC–DC conversion stages. Hence, the intermediate power losses are reduced besides improving the system reliability. However, the inherently low and variable nature of most RES necessitates the deployment of intermediate power electronic converters. To meet the high-voltage requirement of the DC bus and loads, high step-up DC–DC converters are essential3.

Conventional non-isolated DC–DC converters suffer from limited voltage gain, high switch voltage stress, increased duty cycle operation, and degraded efficiency when high conversion ratios are required. Hence, the research focus is on developing novel non-isolated high step-up DC–DC converters that yield large voltage gain at safe duty-ratio values. Various voltage gain extension techniques viz., switched capacitor (SC) cells, switched inductor (SI) structures, voltage-lift networks, and coupled inductors (CI), are reported in literature4–7.

Generally, single-switch-based converters adopt cascaded and clamped structures to enhance the net voltage gain of the converters8–10. Such structures reduce the device stress and improve the overall power transfer capability. In the converters that employ SC and voltage-lift networks, higher input current ripple and capacitor stress are common11–13. Interleaving technique addresses the input current ripple issue. In fact, the input current can be made free from ripples by operating the switches with appropriate phase-shift and duty ratios14–16.

To achieve ultra-high voltage gain values of the order of more than 15, CIs with appropriate turns ratio are employed along with appropriate gain-extension mechanisms17–23. These CI-based converters possess ultra-high voltage gain at safe duty cycle values. However, the leakage inductance introduces voltage spikes across the switches and cause electromagnetic interferences. Nevertheless, by using suitable energy-recycling circuits, the voltage spikes are reduced.

By carefully synthesizing CI-based topologies, converters with quadratic and high-order gain capabilities are realized. They have gained significant attention due to their ability to achieve voltage gain values that are proportional to the square or higher powers of the duty cycle24,25. To obtain ripple-free input current while simultaneously achieve high voltage gain values, interleaved versions of the quadratic and higher order converters are proposed in26–30. These converters are well-suited for high step-up applications even at safe duty ratio values.

Advanced quadratic converter versions that employ interleaved structures along with gain extension mechanisms are proposed to improve voltage gain, efficiency, and power density31–37. However, many existing designs still face challenges related to increased component count, control difficulty, higher voltage stress on some of the components, and limited scalability for DC microgrid applications.

The converter proposed in this paper is motivated by the aforementioned challenges. The proposed converter aims to achieve ultra-high voltage gain with reduced semiconductor voltage stress, continuous and low-ripple input current, and improved efficiency. The proposed converter maintains a compact structure and employs a simple control strategy. The synthesis mechanism, its operating principle, detailed steady-state analysis, the experimental validation, and the comparison sections that are presented subsequently demonstrate the effectiveness and practical applicability of the proposed topology for DC microgrid application.

Power circuit diagram of the proposed converter

The power circuit of proposed high gain converter is shown in Fig. 1. The proposed converter is based on the inductor-capacitor gain cell (ICGC) to provide high gain. Stage 1 has a two-phase interleaved boost converter (IBC) along with voltage-lift (VL) cell. The discrete inductors L1 and L2 are part of IBC and VL cell comprising of D1 and CLift. In stage 2, the ICGC structure is connected in series with stage 1 to enhance the output of previous stage. Thus, the required voltage gain at the output capacitor C0 is achieved. It consists of inductor L3, diodes D3, D4, D5, and capacitors C1, C2, and C3. This switching-capacitor-based ICGC structure is derived from a boost and a buck/boost converter configuration. Hence, a series arrangement of C3 acts as an output capacitor for boost structure and C2 acts as an output capacitor for buck/boost structure. This gives rise to a higher voltage gain during specific switching intervals. Capacitor C0 is the output of the proposed converter. The operating principle is described subsequently.

Mode 1 (0 to t1)

Mode 1 commences when the power switches S1 and S3 are turned ON simultaneously at t0 and S2 is turned OFF. The inductor L1 and L3 are linearly charged by the voltage source through S1 and S3 respectively. The current paths are given by Vin – L1 – S1, Vin – L2 – Clift – D2 – L2 – S3 respectively. Diode D1 is reversed-biased due to the negative voltage developed across capacitor CLift. The inductor L2 discharges and transfers its stored energy along with the pre-charged energy of CLift, C1, and C2 through diode D2. Diode D4 is thus forward-biased and capacitor C0 charges. Since S3 is ON, L3 charges and C3 discharges. The governing equations during this mode are given by (1)–(3). Figure 2a portrays the equivalent circuit during mode 1.

Mode 2 (t1 to t2)

During mode 2, S1 continues to be ON and S2 remains OFF at t1. At t1, S3 is turned-OFF. Inductor L1 continues to charge through the S1. Inductor L2 and CLift continue to discharge into C1 and C3 through D2. The stored energy in L3 is transferred to C3. Now C3 charges and D5 is forward-biased. Inductor L3 discharges into C2 through D3. The output capacitor C0 meets the load requirement. Equations (4)–(6) are valid during mode 2. The equivalent circuit of mode 2 is presented in Fig. 2b.

Mode 3 (t2 to t3)

Mode 3 starts when S1 is turned OFF and S2 and S3 are turned ON at t2. Inductor L1 starts to discharge and charges CLift through D1. Inductor L3 charges through S3. Capacitor C1, C2 discharge into C0 and load through D4. As S2 is ON, L2 starts to charge S2. The equations during mode 3 are presented in (7)–(9) and Fig. 2c presents the equivalent circuit.

Mode 4 (t3 to t4)

Mode 4 commences when S1 continues to remain OFF and S2 remains in the ON state. Switch S3 is turned-OFF. The current paths for inductors L1 and L2 are same as mode 3. Inductor L3 continues to transfer its stored energy to C3 and C2 through D3. The output capacitor C0 meets the load demand. The switching cycle ends at t4 when S1 is turned ON again. The dynamic performance of the proposed ICGC is examined by implementing closed-loop control. A potential divider-based network reduces the output voltage to a safer level (< 3.3 V) that sis fed as input to the in-built analog-to-digital converter (ADC). The filtered ADC output is used to obtain the error signal by comparing with the reference voltage corresponding to 400 V. This error signal is fed into a discrete-time proportional controller with lower and upper saturation values on duty ratio. The controller is suitably tuned to provide a swift response with minimal overshoot and undershoot.

Figure 13a shows the line voltage regulation characteristics of the proposed converter. The input voltage is varied in a stepped manner ranging from 13.4 V to 21.3 V to replicate the PV panel behavior. Under this condition, the implemented closed-loop algorithm effectively restores the output voltage back to 400 V very quickly with minimal undershoots and overshoots. In the process of restoring the output voltage to 400 V, the converter yields a maximum voltage gain of 30. The load regulation characteristics of the ICGC are portrayed in Fig. 13b. The proposed ICGC regulates the output voltage over a wide range of load current which varies from 360 mA to 512 mA. In terms of power level, the proposed converter regulates the output voltage to 400 V when the load varies from 144 W to 205 W. Thus, the converter’s versatility and robustness in handling wide load current and line voltage variations is practically validated. Further, the suitability of the proposed converter for an actual PV-fed DC microgrid is validated through the excellent dynamic performance.

Voltage gain and duty ratio

The converters presented in26 and27 yield impressive voltage gain amplification. However, the converters in33,35–37 yield a voltage gain of 9.5 and lower with an average duty ratio of 0.4. The proposed converter yields highest gain of 22.22 at 50% duty utilising components similar to converters compared in the literature. The converter presented in26 delivers the second highest gain of 21.11, at a high duty ratio of 50% and 62%. This performance is closely followed by the converter in27, which achieves a voltage gain of 15.83 at 0.65 duty ratio. Thus, the proposed ICGC operates with a superior voltage gain capability. Voltage and current stress on the switch

The proposed converter and the converter detailed in reference26 both use three power switches. This distinguishes them from most of the other compared converters, which typically employ two power switches while27,33,35 uses single switch. The maximum voltage stress on the switches S1,2 = 9.25% is the least in proposed ICGC and switch present in converter33 has 17.4% followed by switch of converter in26 having 18.9% voltage stress on S1. The switches present in27,35,37 has 50% voltage stress. The single-switch used in36 experiences the highest stress of 94.4%. Due to the interleaving structure, half of the input current is carried by power switch 1 and full input current is carried by S2 for the proposed ICGC. However, its conduction loss is greatly decreased because of its low voltage stress (9.25% of V0). Hence, the proposed converter is a good candidate alternative for integrating the low voltage PV input even in high-power applications.

Conclusion

This paper presented a high-gain DC–DC converter capable of achieving an ultra-step-up voltage gain of 20. The converter was synthesized using a two-stage architecture. In stage-1, an interleaved boost converter with a voltage lift capacitor was employed. In stage-2, inductor-capacitor gain cell was adopted to extend the voltage gain. Thus, the proposed voltage gain extension technique effectively reduced the switch voltage stress. The proposed concept was experimentally validated through a 20 V to 400 V, 200 W prototype. Under full-load conditions, the converter operated at 94.5% efficiency. Further, due to the interleaving technique, the input current was free from ripples. The converter exhibited swift dynamic performance under wide input and load variations. Due to the gain extension mechanism, the maximum voltage stress on the switches was limited to 10% of the output voltage. Compared to similar state-of-the art converters, the proposed topology is superior in terms of its voltage gain capability, lower switch stress, and modularity. Thus, the proposed converter is expected to be a promising solution for DC microgrid and renewable energy integration applications.
