Beyond Pain: Sarcopenia, Malnutrition, and Sex-Specific Body Image Distortion as Potential Additional Mediators of HRQOL Impairment in ADPKD
Authors: Sara Farinone, Matteo Brambilla Pisoni, Michele Paolisi, Pierpaolo Bianca, Kristiana Kola, Micaela Petrone, Paola Maiucchi, Giuseppe Vezzoli, Maria Teresa Sciarrone Alibrandi
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

We read with great interest the study by Fadlallah et al.1 demonstrating that total kidney volume is independently associated with impaired physical health-related quality of life in autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD) and that mental health impairment is largely mediated through kidney pain. Writing from an Italian multidisciplinary ADPKD reference centre,2 we wish to raise 2 points that may further inform this framework.

First, beyond kidney pain, accumulating evidence suggests that organomegaly may impair health-related quality of life through early malnutrition and sarcopenia. Cross-sectional data show that approximately 30% of ambulatory patients with ADPKD are malnourished or at nutritional risk and that height-adjusted total kidney and liver volume, not estimated glomerular filtration rate, is the strongest independent predictor of this risk, even at preserved kidney function.3 Bioelectrical impedance analysis reveals a disease-specific phenotype with reduced skeletal muscle mass, depressed phase angle and expanded extracellular water, consistent with sarcopenic obesity, alongside organomegaly-driven symptoms including early satiety, dyspepsia, constipation, and weakness.S1,S2 Whether these disturbances contribute independently to the health-related quality of life impairment reported remains to be tested, but their occurrence at preserved estimated glomerular filtration rate makes them plausible unaccounted mediators.

Second, sex-disaggregated analyses seem warranted. Women with ADPKD are disproportionately affected by hepatomegaly because of cumulative estrogen exposure,4 and body image distortion seems more pronounced in women, potentially contributing to mental health impairment and sexual dysfunction beyond pain.5 Future studies incorporating nutritional assessment and sex-stratified mediation models may uncover actionable targets, nutritional support, body image, and sexual health counseling, and tailored pain management, to address the organomegaly-driven health-related quality of life burden in ADPKD.
