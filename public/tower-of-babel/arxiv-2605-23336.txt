1
Introduction

A central goal in the analysis of Boolean functions is to understand how
different complexity measures relate to one another. Many of these measures come from very different models: decision trees, certificates, block sensitivity, quantum query algorithms, and real polynomial representations. Nevertheless, for total Boolean functions, many of them are known to be polynomially related. The foundational work of Nisan and
Szegedy
[
30
]
showed that the degree and approximate degree of a Boolean function are polynomially related to several standard complexity measures, including block sensitivity, certificate complexity, and deterministic query complexity. This circle of equivalences was later extended to other measures, and one of the main remaining gaps, the sensitivity conjecture, was resolved by Huang
[
20
]
.

Another major open problem from the same line of work concerned rational
representations of Boolean functions. This question, attributed to Fortnow and recorded by Nisan and Szegedy
[
30
]
, asked whether rational degree is polynomially related to ordinary degree. The rational degree of a Boolean function
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
, denoted
\operatorname{rdeg}(f)
, is the least degree needed to represent
f
exactly on the Boolean cube as a quotient
p/q
of real polynomials, where
q
is nonzero on every input. Since every polynomial representation is also a rational representation with denominator
1
, we always have

\operatorname{rdeg}(f)\leq\deg(f).

The rational degree conjecture asked for a converse up to polynomial loss:
does there exist a constant
c
such that

\deg(f)\leq O(\operatorname{rdeg}(f)^{c})

for every total Boolean function
f
?

This conjecture remained open for more than three decades. It was recently proved by Kothari, Kovacs-Deak, Wang, and Yang
[
24
]
, who showed that, for every total Boolean function
f
,

\deg(f)\leq\widetilde{O}\!\left(\operatorname{rdeg}(f)^{3}\right).

Thus rational degree also belongs to the family of Boolean function measures that are polynomially related for total functions

[
24
]
. The restriction to total functions is important: for partial functions, the analogous statement is false, as shown by Iyer et al.
[
21
]
.

A key reason rational degree is natural is its close connection to
nondeterminism. A non-deterministic polynomial for
f
is a real polynomial

p
such that

p(x)\neq 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(x)=1\qquad\text{for all }x\in\{0,1\}^{n}.

The non-deterministic degree of
f
, denoted
\operatorname{ndeg}(f)
, is
the minimum degree of such a polynomial. Rational degree can be described
exactly in terms of non-deterministic degree:

\operatorname{rdeg}(f)=\max\{\operatorname{ndeg}(f),\operatorname{ndeg}(\overline{f})\}.

In other words, rational degree measures the larger of the two
non-deterministic degrees associated with
f
and its complement.
This connection also brings quantum query complexity into the picture.
de Wolf showed that non-deterministic degree is equal, up to constant factors,
to non-deterministic quantum query complexity

[
16
]
. Motivated by the
classical relation

D(f)\leq C^{(0)}(f)C^{(1)}(f),

or equivalently by the product of non-deterministic query complexities for
f
and
\overline{f}
, de Wolf conjectured that

Conjecture 1

(
[
16
]
)
.

For every total Boolean function
f
,

D(f)\leq O\!\left(\operatorname{ndeg}(f)\operatorname{ndeg}(\overline{f})\right)

The dependence on both
f
and
\overline{f}
is necessary: for example,
\operatorname{OR}_{n}
has very small non-deterministic degree, while its deterministic query complexity is large, and the same obstruction appears for
\operatorname{AND}_{n}
after
taking complements.

de Wolf’s conjecture is stronger than the rational degree conjecture. Indeed,
since
\operatorname{rdeg}(f)=\max\{\operatorname{ndeg}(f),\operatorname{ndeg}(\overline{f})\}
,
the conjecture would imply a quadratic upper bound of ordinary degree in terms of rational degree. Moreover, since
\operatorname{ndeg}(f)\leq\deg(f)
and
\operatorname{ndeg}(\overline{f})\leq\deg(\overline{f})
and
\deg(f)=\deg(\overline{f})
, the conjecture would also give
D(f)\leq O(\deg(f)^{2})
.
[
24
]
gave a weaker polynomial form. In particular, they show

D(f)\leq O\!\left(\operatorname{ndeg}(f)^{2}\operatorname{ndeg}(\overline{f})^{2}\right),\quad\textit{and \hskip 10.22217pt}D(f)\leq O\!\left(\operatorname{ndeg}(f)^{1.5}\operatorname{ndeg}(\overline{f})^{1.5}\log n\right),

but the original product conjecture remains open

[
24
]
.

1.2
Our results

Our main positive results are summarized below: We show that Conjecture
3
is true for the following classes.

1.

(Corollary
2
) For every
monotone
Boolean function
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
,

\widetilde{\deg}(f)\leq O\!\left(\max\{\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(f),\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(\overline{f})\}^{4}\right).

The same bound holds for
unate
functions (Lemma
8
).

2.

(Corollary
5
) For every Boolean function
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
,

D(f)\leq O\!\left(\operatorname{alt}(f)^{3}\max\{\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(f),\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(\overline{f})\}^{6}\right).

where
\operatorname{alt}(f)
is the alternation number of
f
. In particular, this gives the desired polynomial bound for functions of bounded alternation.

3.

(Corollary
3
) For every
zebra
function
f
,

D(f)\leq O\!\left(\operatorname{alt}(f)^{2}\max\{\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(f),\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(\overline{f})\}^{4}\right).

4.

(Corollary
6
) For every
symmetric
Boolean function
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
,

D(f)\leq O\!\left(\max\{\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(f),\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(\overline{f})\}^{6}\right).

For a large subclass of symmetric functions, namely those that are
constant on a long interval of Hamming weights around the middle layer, we obtain the following stronger bound (Corollary
7
),

\widetilde{\deg}(f)\leq O\!\left(\max\{\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(f),\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(\overline{f})\}^{2}\right).

5.

(Theorem
3
) Let
f
be any Boolean function that is computed by a read-
k
Disjunctive Normal Form (DNF) formulas, then

\widetilde{\deg}(f)\leq O\!\left(k(k+1)^{2}(2k+1)^{2}\bigl(\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(f)\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(\overline{f})\bigr)^{6}\right).

In particular, for constant
k
, this gives the desired polynomial bound.

6.

(Theorem
4
) For any constant
k\geq 1
, and for every Boolean function
f:\{0,1\}^{\binom{n}{k}}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
representing a
k
-uniform hypergraph property
,

D(f)\leq O\!\left(\max\{\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(f),\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(\overline{f})\}^{6k}\right).

where
n
represents the number of vertices.
Consequently,
graph properties
satisfy the conjecture up to a polynomial loss (Corollary
8
).

Most of our proofs are written for
\epsilon=1/3
, but the same arguments extend to any fixed constant
0<\epsilon<1
, changing the bounds only by constant factors.

Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, let
0<\epsilon<1
be a fixed constant.
For ease of reading, for every Boolean function
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
, we use the notation
\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(f)
to denote
\mathsf{ndeg}_{\epsilon}(f)
. When
\epsilon=1/3

\mathsf{N}(f)
. Thus,
\mathsf{N}(f)=\mathsf{ndeg}_{1/3}(f)
.
We also define

M_{\epsilon}(f):=\max\{\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(f),\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(\overline{f})\}.

When
\epsilon=1/3
, we simply write
M(f)
.

We will use
[n]
to denote the set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. For an input
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, and a subset
B\subseteq[n]
,
x^{B}
denotes flipping the bits of
x
at all indices
i\in B
.
We will denote
|x|
as the Hamming weight
\sum_{i}x_{i}
for
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
. For
x,y\in\{0,1\}^{n}
,
x\preceq y
if and only if
x_{i}\leq y_{i}
for all
i\in[n]
which means that
x_{i}=1\implies y_{i}=1
and
y_{i}=0\implies x_{i}=0
.

Definition 1

(Degree of Boolean function)
.

Let

and

. Then,
\deg(p)=\max\left\{|S|\mid c_{s}\neq 0\right\}
is called the degree of the Boolean function

, denoted by
\deg(f)
.

Definition 2

(Non-deterministic degree
[
16
]
)
.

Let
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
and polynomial
p
is called non-deterministic polynomial of
f
if
p(x)=0
if and only if
f(x)=0
.
\mathsf{ndeg}(f)
is the minimum degree of such polynomial

.

Definition 3

(Approximate non-deterministic degree
[
24
]
)
.

Let
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
and a real polynomial
p

|p(x)|\leq\epsilon

|p(x)|\geq 1

f(x)=1

\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(f)=\mathsf{ndeg}_{\epsilon}(f)

.

Definition 4

(Approximate Rational Degree
[
21
]
)
.

Let

\forall x\in\{0,1\}^{n},\left|f(x)-\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\right|\leq\epsilon.

The minimum value of

. When

.

(Approximate degree)
.

A real polynomial
p
approximates a Boolean function

if, for every

,

|f(x)-p(x)|\leq\epsilon
.
The approximate degree, of

\deg_{\epsilon}(f)
, is the

to denote

.

Definition 6

(Sign degree)
.

A real polynomial
p
sign-represents a Boolean function

x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
,

p(x)<0

.
The sign degree, or threshold degree, of

, is the

.

Let

(Monotone function)
.

Let

Definition 13

(Unate function)

A Boolean function

Fact 3

(Sign degree lower bound)
.

For every non-constant Boolean function
f
and every
0<\epsilon<1
,

M_{\epsilon}(f)\geq\frac{1}{2}\deg_{\pm}(f).

Proof.

Let
p
be an
\epsilon
-approximate non-deterministic polynomial for
f
, and
let
q
be an
\epsilon
-approximate non-deterministic polynomial for

\overline{f}
, with
\deg(p)=\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(f)
and
\deg(q)=\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(\overline{f})
.

Consider the polynomial

r(x):=q(x)^{2}-p(x)^{2}.

If
f(x)=1
, then
|p(x)|\geq 1
and
|q(x)|\leq\epsilon
, so
r(x)\leq\epsilon^{2}-1<0
. If
f(x)=0
, then
|q(x)|\geq 1
and
|p(x)|\leq\epsilon
, so
r(x)\geq 1-\epsilon^{2}>0
.
Therefore
r
sign-represents
f
. Moreover,

\deg(r)\leq 2\max\{\deg(p),\deg(q)\}=2M_{\epsilon}(f).

Hence
\deg_{\pm}(f)\leq 2M_{\epsilon}(f)
.
∎

Lemma 4

(Functions of high sign degree)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a family of Boolean functions such that every

f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
in
\mathcal{C}
satisfies

\deg_{\pm}(f)\geq cn^{\alpha}

for some constants
c>0
and
\alpha>0
. Then, for every

f\in\mathcal{C}
,

D(f)\leq O(M_{\epsilon}(f)^{1/\alpha}).

Thus Conjecture
3
holds for every such
class
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

By Fact
3
,

M_{\epsilon}(f)\geq\frac{1}{2}\deg_{\pm}(f)\geq\frac{c}{2}n^{\alpha}.

Therefore

n\leq\left(\frac{2}{c}\right)^{1/\alpha}M_{\epsilon}(f)^{1/\alpha}.

Since a deterministic decision tree can always query all
n
variables,
D(f)\leq n\leq\left(\frac{2}{c}\right)^{1/\alpha}M_{\epsilon}(f)^{1/\alpha}
.
∎

Corollary 1

(Almost all Boolean functions satisfy Conjecture

)
.

For almost all Boolean functions

Proof.

It is known that almost all Boolean functions have sign degree at least

Next we prove that if a function

does not increase.

Lemma 5

(Restrictions and projections)
.

Let
m\leq n
, and
g:\{0,1\}^{m}\to\{0,1\}
be obtained from

f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}

by one of

\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(g)\leq\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(f)\textit{\hskip 10.22217ptand thus \hskip 10.22217pt}M_{\epsilon}(g)\leq M_{\epsilon}(f).

Proof.

Let
p(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be an
\epsilon
-approximate non-deterministic polynomial
for
f
. Let
\pi:\{0,1\}^{m}\to\{0,1\}^{n}
denote the corresponding substitution

g(y)=f(\pi(y))
.
Define a new polynomial

.
As the substitution cannot increase degree,
\deg(q)\leq\deg(p)
.

Moreover, if
g(y)=0
, then

, and hence
|q(y)|=|p(\pi(y))|\leq\epsilon
. And if
g(y)=1
, then

, and hence
|q(y)|=|p(\pi(y))|\geq 1
.
Thus
q
is an
\epsilon
-approximate non-deterministic polynomial for
g
.
This proves

\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(g)\leq\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(f).

Applying the same argument to
\overline{f}
and
\overline{g}
gives

\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(\overline{g})\leq\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(\overline{f}),

and therefore
M_{\epsilon}(g)\leq M_{\epsilon}(f)
.
∎

3.2
Relation with approximate rational degree

Lemma 6
.

Let
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
be a Boolean function. Let
\operatorname{rdeg}_{\epsilon}(f)
be the approximate rational degree of
f
and
\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(f)
and
\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(\overline{f})
be the approximate non-deterministic degrees of
f
and
\overline{f}
respectively. Then,

\operatorname{rdeg}_{\epsilon}(f)\leq 2\max\{\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(f),\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(\overline{f})\}

(1)

Proof.

Let
p(x)
and
q(x)
be approximate non-deterministic polynomials of
f
and
\overline{f}
, respectively, such that
\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(f)=\deg(p)
and
\mathsf{N}_{\epsilon}(\overline{f})=\deg(q)
. It suffices to prove that
\left|\frac{p(x)^{2}}{p(x)^{2}+q(x)^{2}}-f(x)\right|\leq\epsilon
. For case
f(x)=0
,
\left|p(x)^{2}\right|\leq\epsilon
and
\left|q(x)^{2}\right|\geq 1
and therefore,
\left|\frac{p(x)^{2}}{p(x)^{2}+q(x)^{2}}\right|\leq\epsilon
. For case
f(x)=1
,
p(x)^{2}\geq 1
and
q(x)^{2}\leq\epsilon
and so
\frac{p(x)^{2}}{p(x)^{2}+q(x)^{2}}
is never greater than 1 and if we denote this as the function
h(y_{1},y_{2})=y_{1}/(y_{1}+y_{2})
where
y_{1}\geq 1
and
y_{2}\leq\epsilon
, this is a strictly increasing function in
y_{1}
and decreasing in
y_{2}
and
\lim_{y_{1}\rightarrow\infty}h(y_{1},y_{2})=1
and
h(y_{1},y_{2})\in\left[\frac{1}{1+\epsilon},1\right)
. This means
\frac{p(x)^{2}}{p(x)^{2}+q(x)^{2}}
is indeed a rational approximation of
f(x)
.
∎

3.3
Monotone Boolean functions

Conjecture
3
is true for monotone Boolean functions. The main result that we need for establishing its proof is that approximate non-deterministic degree of AND being
\Omega(\sqrt{n})
. This can be seen using dual polynomials technique given by
[
10
]
. They use the functions to be
\{-1,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{-1,1\}
. But we are using
\{0,1\}^{n}
and hence will follow
[
36
]
which gives the lower bound on one-sided approximate degree of AND and NOR
on

\Omega(\sqrt{n})
.

The following lemma follows same proof technique used to prove polynomial relationship between degree and rational degree for monotone Boolean functions from
[
21
]
but we reproduce the proof for simplicity while using it for our setting.

Lemma 7
.

If
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
is a monotone Boolean function, then

\max\{\mathsf{N}(f)^{2},\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})^{2}\}\geq\Omega(s(f)).

Proof.

We will assume that
f
is monotonically increasing without loss of generality.
We will only prove that
\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})^{2}\geq\Omega(s_{0}(f))
and thus,
\mathsf{N}(f)^{2}\geq\Omega(s_{0}(\overline{f}))=\Omega(s_{1}(f))
will follow. Let
z=s_{0}(f)
and
s_{0}(f,x)=s_{0}(f)
. This means that if you flip any one of these
z
sensitive variables of
x
which are zeros since
f
is monotonically increasing, then
f
will become 1. So if all the variables in
x
are fixed except for the
z
sensitive zeros, then we get an
OR_{z}
function. Now, we know that
\mathsf{N}(\overline{OR_{z}})\geq\sqrt{\frac{z}{8}}
and therefore,
\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})\geq\mathsf{N}(\overline{OR_{z}})\geq\sqrt{\frac{z}{8}}=\sqrt{\frac{s_{0}(f)}{8}}
. Therefore, we get

\Omega(s(f))\leq\max\{\mathsf{N}(f)^{2},\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})^{2}\}
.
∎

Corollary 2

(Monotone functions)
.

If
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
is monotone Boolean function, then

\widetilde{\deg}(f)\leq O\left(\max\{\mathsf{N}(f)^{4},\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})^{4}\}\right)\text{ and }bs(f)=C(f)\leq O\left(\max\{\mathsf{N}(f)^{2},\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})^{2}\}\right).

Proof.

For every monotone Boolean functions
f
,
C(f)=s(f)=bs(f)

[
31
]
and for any Boolean function
\widetilde{\deg}(f)\leq\deg(f)\leq s(f)^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 8

(Unate functions)
.

Let
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
be unate. Then

\widetilde{\deg}(f)\leq O(M_{\epsilon}(f)^{4}).

Proof.

Since
f
is unate, there exists a monotone function
g
and a vector

a\in\{0,1\}^{n}
such that
f(x)=g(x\oplus a)
.

The map
x\mapsto x\oplus a
is a bijection obtained by negating input
literals. So by using Lemma
5
we know that
M_{\epsilon}(g)\leq M_{\epsilon}(f)
In fact,
M_{\epsilon}(g)=M_{\epsilon}(f)
. Also,
\widetilde{\deg}(f)=\widetilde{\deg}(g)
. Thus by using Corollary
2
we obtain the desired result.
∎

3.4
Functions with small alternating number

We follow proof structure from
[
28
]
which gave a relation between sensitivity and block sensitivity in terms of alternating number of the function, but we adapt their proof to show an upper bound on certificate complexity in terms of alternating number and approximate non-deterministic degrees of the function and its negation for zebra functions. We will first look at zebra functions and then functions with bounded alternation number
\operatorname{alt}(f)
where a monotone path
\mathcal{P}
could have alternating number

. For functions of bounded alternation number, we use their technique of bounding block sensitivity of inputs on paths having no alternation.

Theorem 1
.

Let

be a non-constant zebra function with

Let us look at any

for some max term
u

2.

x\succeq d
for some min term
d

Since

d\in\{0,1\}^{n}

x
follows at least one of the above two conditions.

Now, let us look at the case when the alternation number is even and this means
f(0^{n})=f(1^{n})

x

f(x)\neq f(1^{n})

x

\operatorname{alt}(x,1^{n})>1

For the rest of the proof, we will be using induction on

Let us first look at the case when
x\preceq u

0

. So we get an equation which will be used later.

|S_{0}(u)|\leq O(\max\{\mathsf{N}(f^{\prime})^{2},\mathsf{N}(\overline{f^{\prime}})^{2}\})\leq O(\max\{\mathsf{N}(f)^{2},\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})^{2}\}).

(2)

3.5

Lemma 9
.

Let

\mathsf{N}(f)\geq\frac{\operatorname{alt}(f)}{2}.

\operatorname{alt}(f)

\operatorname{alt}(f)=|K_{1}\cup K_{2}|

where

K_{1}=\left\{k\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}\mid p^{\prime}(k)\leq 1/3\,,p^{\prime}(k+1)\geq 1\right\}\text{ and }

K_{2}=\{k\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}\mid p^{\prime}(k)\geq 1\,,p^{\prime}(k+1)\leq 1/3\}.

Note that
p^{\prime\prime}(k)=p^{\prime}(k)-2/3
have number of roots at least equal to or more than
\operatorname{alt}(f)
. Therefore,
\mathsf{N}(f)=\deg(p)\geq\frac{\deg(p^{\prime})}{2}=\frac{\deg(p^{\prime\prime})}{2}\geq\frac{\operatorname{alt}(f)}{2}
.
∎

Corollary 6
.

Let
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
be an arbitrary symmetric Boolean function. Then,

D(f)\leq O(\max\{\mathsf{N}(f)^{6},\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})^{6}\}).

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{alt}(f)
be the alternating number of
f
. From the earlier Lemma
9
, we have
\max\{\mathsf{N}(f),\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})\}\geq\frac{\operatorname{alt}(f)}{2}=\frac{\operatorname{alt}(\overline{f})}{2}
. Symmetric functions are zebra functions and hence we can use

D(f)\leq O(\operatorname{alt}(f)^{2}\max\{\mathsf{N}(f)^{4},\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})^{4}\})

from Corollary

Thus,

D(f)\leq O(\max\{\mathsf{N}(f)^{6},\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})^{6}\}).

∎

3.5.1
Improved bounds for symmetric functions

In this section, we look at those symmetric functions where the function is constant for a large interval of Hamming weights around

Hamming weight. We refer to the technique of reduction to the

Let
EXACT_{k}(t)
be a univariate function on

EXACT_{k}(t)=\begin{cases}1&\text{for }t=k\\

Lemma 10
.

Let
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
be a symmetric Boolean function. Let
D:\{0,1,\dots,n\}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
be such that
f(x)=D(|x|)
.
Let
\ell_{0}(D)\in\{0,1,\dots,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor\}
and
\ell_{1}(D)\in\{0,1,\dots,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor\}
be the minimum integers such that

is constant in
[\ell_{0}(D),n-\ell_{1}(D)]
. When at least one of
\ell_{0}
or
\ell_{1}
is less than
n/5
, then
\max\{\mathsf{N}(f),\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})\}=\Omega(\sqrt{n})
.

Proof.

Let us first consider
\ell_{0}(D)<\frac{n}{5}
. The proof for the case when
\ell_{1}<\frac{n}{5}
will be analogous. Let
\ell=\ell_{0}(D)
and we assume
\ell\geq 1
since if
\ell=0
, then we can choose
x
such that
|x|=n-\ell_{1}(D)+1
and embed an AND or NAND, depending on value of
D
, on the ones of
x
to get desired result.
According to
[
35
]
,
EXACT_{0}(t)
on
\lfloor\frac{n}{5}\rfloor
bits can be written as

EXACT_{0}(t)=(1-2D(\ell))D(t+\ell-1)+D(\ell).

Let
q(x)
be an approximate non-deterministic polynomial for
f(x)
with
\deg(q)=\mathsf{N}(f)
and
p(t)
be the symmetrized version of
q(x)^{2}
where
p(t)=\mathbb{E}_{|x|=t}[q(x)^{2}]
. So, when
D(t)=1
, then

and when
D(t)=0
then
0\leq p(t)\leq 1/3
. If we show that
(1-2D(\ell))p(t+\ell-1)+D(\ell)
is the approximate non-deterministic polynomial for
EXACT_{0}(t)
and that the approximate non-deterministic degree for
EXACT_{0}(t)
is lower bounded by
\Omega(\sqrt{n})
, then we will get
\mathsf{N}(f)=\Omega(\sqrt{n})
.
D(t)
will satisfy exactly one of the following two cases:

1.

D(\ell)=0
and
D(\ell-1)=1
.

2.

D(\ell)=1
and
D(\ell-1)=0
.

It suffices to show
\mathsf{N}(f)=\Omega(\sqrt{n})
for the first case. This is because when
f
is such that
D(\ell)=1
and
D(\ell-1)=0
, then
\overline{D}(\ell)=0
and
\overline{D}(\ell-1)=1
and hence
\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})=\Omega(\sqrt{n})
.

Let us now look at the case when
D(\ell)=0
and
D(\ell-1)=1
.
We know
EXACT_{0}(0)=1
. Let
p(\ell-1)=h
. Since
D(\ell-1)=1
,

. The value of the expression
(1-2D(\ell))p(t+\ell-1)+D(\ell)
when

becomes

(1-2D(\ell))p(\ell-1)+D(\ell)=(1-0)h+0=h\geq 1.

For
1\leq t\leq\lfloor n/5\rfloor
,

p(\ell+t-1)=\epsilon\leq 1/3
. Then the value of the required expression

(1-2D(\ell))p(t+\ell-1)+D(\ell)=\epsilon.

Also we know that
EXACT_{0}(t)=0
for
t\geq 1
. We can conclude that
(1-2D(\ell))p(t+\ell-1)+D(\ell)
can be an approximate non-deterministic polynomial for
EXACT_{0}(t)
and its degree is at most
2N(f)
. Therefore,
N(f)=\Omega(N(EXACT_{0}))
.

Now, let us prove the lower bound on the approximate non-deterministic degree of
EXACT_{0}(t)
is
\Omega(\sqrt{n})
. Let
p^{\prime\prime}(t)
be an approximate non-deterministic polynomial for
EXACT_{0}(t)
with
\deg(p^{\prime\prime})=N(EXACT_{0})
. So we have
p^{\prime\prime}(0)=h\geq 1
and
|p^{\prime\prime}(t)|\leq 1/3
for

EXACT_{0}(t)
, the value of the polynomial
p^{\prime\prime}(t)
is greater than 1 only at

h
without losing the property of it being the approximate non-deterministic polynomial. So, lets say

is actually the symmetrized polynomial for the two-sided approximate degree of NOR. From the proof of lower bound of approximate degree of symmetric functions from
[

\widetilde{\deg}(\mathsf{NOR})\geq\deg(p^{\prime})\geq\Omega(\sqrt{n})

So, to conclude, if

\mathsf{N}(f)\geq\Omega(\sqrt{n})
. Suppose

is such that it follows the second case, in that case

\max\{\mathsf{N}(f),\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})\}\geq\Omega(\sqrt{n})
.
∎

3.6
Read-once functions

In this section, we see that the conjecture is true for read-once functions which are functions where every variable can only appear at most once.
Following the same proof techniques of
[
21
]
, we get the following two lemmas:

Lemma 11
.

Let
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
and
g_{i}:\{0,1\}^{n_{i}}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
be Boolean functions such that every variable is relevant. Then
h:\{0,1\}^{\sum_{i}n_{i}}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
be such that
h(x^{1},x^{2},\dots,x^{n})=f(g_{1}(x^{1}),\dots,g_{n}(x^{n}))
. Then,

\max\{\mathsf{N}(h),\mathsf{N}(\overline{h})\}\geq\max\{\mathsf{N}(f),\mathsf{N}(\overline{f}),\mathsf{N}(g_{1}),\mathsf{N}(\overline{g_{1}}),\dots,\mathsf{N}(g_{n}),\mathsf{N}(\overline{g_{n}})\}.

Proof.

Let us first look at the function
g_{i}
. Since
g_{i}
is relevant in each variable, there exists a restriction
g_{i}^{\prime}
where we can fix every variable of
g_{i}
except for one variable
x^{j}_{i}
. Then,
g_{i}
could either be
x^{i}_{j_{i}}
or
\overline{x^{i}_{j_{i}}}
. We can find restrictions for each
g_{i}
and since the variables are disjoint, by taking the combined restrictions, we get a restriction
h^{\prime}
. We can now write
h^{\prime}
as
h^{\prime}=f(x^{1}_{j_{1}},\dots,x^{n}_{j_{n}})
. Therefore, we will get
M(h)\geq M(h^{\prime})
and hence
M(h)\geq M(f)
. For showing lower bound with respect to
g_{i}
, we can set all
x^{i}
except
x^{k}
such that
f(x)=g(x^{k})
or
f(x)=\overline{g}(x^{k})
. From this, we get a restriction
h^{\prime\prime}
for
h
such that
h^{\prime\prime}=g_{k}(x^{k})
or
h^{\prime\prime}=1-g_{k}(x^{k})
. Therefore,
M(h)\geq M(g_{k})
. Since this
k
was chosen arbitrarily,
M(h)\geq\max\{M(g_{1}),\dots,M(g_{n})\}
.
Along with previous lower bound with respect to
f
, we finally get

M(h)\geq\max\{M(f),M(g_{1}),\dots,M(g_{n})\}.

∎

Lemma 12
.

Let
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
be a read-once formula with symmetric gates and depth

. Then
\max\{\mathsf{N}(f),\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})\}\geq\Omega(\deg(f)^{1/6d})

Proof.

Let

be the node that has maximum branching factor

and

be the sub formula of

F_{i}
except for one variable

under
F_{i}
. Because each

are read-once and

is read once, these restrictions are on disjoint variables and we can combine them to get a restriction

. Using similar technique from previous lemma

, an additional restriction can be used to obtain

. Again, since we are working with read-once formulas, restriction corresponding to

will not affect the restriction corresponding to

. From Lemma

\max{\{\mathsf{N}(f),\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})\}}\geq\max\{\mathsf{N}(f|_{r\cup r^{\prime}}),\mathsf{N}(\overline{f}|_{r\cup r^{\prime}})\}=\max\{\mathsf{N}(F|_{r}),\mathsf{N}(\overline{F}|_{r})\}=\max\{\mathsf{N}(G),\mathsf{N}(\overline{G})\}

\max\{\mathsf{N}(G),\mathsf{N}(\overline{G})\}\geq\Omega(D(G)^{1/6})

\max\{\mathsf{N}(G),\mathsf{N}(\overline{G})\}\geq\Omega(D(G)^{1/6})\geq\Omega(w^{1/6})

\max\{\mathsf{N}(f),\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})\}\geq\Omega(\deg(f)^{1/6d})

∎

f=0

Thus we get
\widetilde{\deg}(f)\leq D(f)\leq C^{(0)}(f)\,C^{(1)}(f)\leq\alpha\beta
.

By Lemma
16
(
[
4

s_{1}(f)+(1+k)\,s_{0}(f)\geq\beta

s_{1}(f)\leq O\bigl((k+1)\mathsf{N}(f)^{2}\bigr)\quad\text{and}\quad s_{0}(f)\leq O\bigl((2k+1)\mathsf{N}(\overline{f})^{2}\bigr).

By Lemma

\alpha\beta\leq O\bigl(k(\beta\mathsf{N}(\overline{f}))^{2}\bigr).

Also, since

Therefore,

\displaystyle\beta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq O\left((k+1)^{2}(N(f)^{2}+(2k+1)N(\overline{f})^{2})^{2}\right)

So combining everything we get,

∎

When

\mathcal{P}

Since

is constant, we obtain
\Omega(\sqrt{n})

2.

Case

:
Let

be the set of isolated vertices (i.e., vertices with zero degree) of

. Now choose a vertex

Let the link of
v

from hyper-edges that contain

\mathcal{P}(H^{\prime})=0
. Now define

|I^{\prime}|\geq n/2+1

intersects
I^{\prime}
, and every

S\in L_{v}

I^{\prime}
. Thus for every subset

Now we can embed a function on

\mathcal{P}(H_{A})

Note that this function

\mathcal{P}

are fixed to

, and for each

Note that

and
g(1_{\{v\}})=\mathcal{P}(H)=1
.
We claim that this function

and for each

the edges added in

.
Therefore
