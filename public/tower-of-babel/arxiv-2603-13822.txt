Some Spectral Problems for First Order Normal Differential Operators in the Weighted Hilbert Spaces of Vector-Functions
Zameddin I. Ismailov, Pembe Ipek Al, Mohammad Sababheh

Abstract




Abstract

In this article, in order to the minimal operator generated by the first-order differential-operator expression in the weighted Hilbert space of vector functions in the finite interval to be formal normal, the relationship between the variable operator coefficient of this differential-operator expression and the weight function is established. Afterwards, the general form of all normal extension of the minimal operator is found using the Glazman-Krein-Naimark Method. Then, the structure of spectrum of such extensions is investigated. Later on, the issue of belonging to Schatten von-Neumann classes is explored, as well as the asymptotic behaviour of the singular numbers of the inverse of such normal extensions. Lastly, an approach is developed on all normal extension expressed in the weighted Hilbert spaces.

The Neumann Method refers to the broad representation theory proposed by Neumann in
[

[

[
13
]
discusses the application of the Calkin-Gorbachuk Method to two-point linear differential operators with operator coefficients in the Hilbert space of vector-functions. To gain further insight into this topic, we recommend reading the paper
[
31

.

The Glazman-Krein-Naimark Method was used in
[
10
]
to express in terms of boundary values all selfadjoint extensions of the minimum operator generated by a class of first-order symmetric quasi-differential expressions in the scalar case and the structure of their spectrum was then analyzed.

The discussion in
[
34
]
covered all selfadjoint extensions as well as some spectral analysis of the minimal operator generated by a linear classical formal selfadjoint differential expression of any order and smoothness coefficients in the weighted Hilbert space. Although a differential expression of any order can always be transformed into a first-order differential expression with matrix coefficients, it is more logical to examine problems suitable for first-order quasi-differential expressions in the weighted Hilbert space of vector-functions instead of such differential expressions. Furthermore, the Glazman-Krein-Naimark Method was used in
[
14
]
to express all selfadjoint extensions of any order symmetric differential expression with complex coefficients, equal, and any number of defects in the weighted Hilbert space
L_{\omega}^{2}(a,b)\ (\omega>0,\ a,b\in\overline{\mathbb{R}})

The Coddington-Biriuk Method was unable to do away with the requirement to provide both the differential expression and the boundary value requirements that result in the differential operator in order to obtain a differential operator in any Banach space.

Different mathematicians, particularly Otelbaev and Ismailov, explored in depth all normal extensions and spectrum analysis of a linear formal normal differential expression in a without-weighted Hilbert space of vector-functions in their works (see

In

Theorem 1

If the minimal operator
L_{0}
is formally normal in
L_{\alpha}^{2}
, then

\displaystyle A^{*}(t)A(t)-A(t)A^{*}(t)=\left(2A_{r}(t)-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}I\right)^{\prime}\ a.\ e.\ t\in(0,1)

where
A_{r}(t)=ReA(t)=\frac{1}{2}\overline{\left(A(t)+A^{*}(t)\right)},\ t\in(0,1)
and

Proof.

Let
L_{0}
be formally normal in
L_{\alpha}^{2}
. Then, for each

we have

\|L_{0}\|^{2}_{L_{\alpha}^{2}}=\|L_{0}^{*}\|^{2}_{L_{\alpha}^{2}},

that is

\|u^{\prime}(t)+A(t)u(t)\|^{2}_{L_{\alpha}^{2}}=\|\frac{(\alpha u)^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}-A^{*}(t)u(t)\|^{2}_{L_{\alpha}^{2}}\ a.\ e.\ t\in(0,1).

Then, for each
u(t)=\varphi(t)f,\ \varphi(t)\in\stackrel{{{}_{o}}}{{W}}_{2}^{2}(0,1),\ f\in D(A)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\varphi^{\prime}(t)f+A(t)\varphi(t)f,\varphi^{\prime}(t)f+A(t)\varphi(t)f\rangle_{L_{\alpha}^{2}}=\langle\varphi^{\prime}(t)f+\Delta(t)\varphi(t)f,\varphi^{\prime}(t)f+\Delta(t)\varphi(t)f\rangle_{L_{\alpha}^{2}},

where
\Delta(t)=\dfrac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}I-A^{*}(t)\ a.\ e.\ t\in(0,1).

Thus, we get

\displaystyle\langle\varphi^{\prime}(t)f,\varphi^{\prime}(t)f\rangle_{L_{\alpha}^{2}}+\langle\varphi^{\prime}(t)f,A(t)\varphi(t)f\rangle_{L_{\alpha}^{2}}+\langle A(t)\varphi(t)f,\varphi^{\prime}(t)f\rangle_{L_{\alpha}^{2}}+\langle A(t)\varphi(t)f,A(t)\varphi(t)f\rangle_{L_{\alpha}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\varphi^{\prime}(t)f,\varphi^{\prime}(t)f\rangle_{L_{\alpha}^{2}}+\langle\varphi^{\prime}(t)f,\Delta(t)\varphi(t)f\rangle_{L_{\alpha}^{2}}+\langle\Delta(t)\varphi(t)f,\varphi^{\prime}(t)f\rangle_{L_{\alpha}^{2}}+\langle\Delta(t)\varphi(t)f,\Delta(t)\varphi(t)f\rangle_{L_{\alpha}^{2}}\ a.\ e.\ t\in(0,1).

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\langle\varphi^{\prime}(t)f,(A(t)-\Delta(t))\varphi(t)f\rangle_{L_{\alpha}^{2}}+\langle\varphi(t)f,(A^{*}(t)-\Delta^{*}(t))\varphi^{\prime}(t)f\rangle_{L_{\alpha}^{2}}+\langle\varphi(t)f,(A^{*}(t)A(t)-\Delta^{*}(t)\Delta(t))\varphi(t)f\rangle_{L_{\alpha}^{2}}=0\ a.\ e.\ t\in(0,1).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}\varphi^{\prime}(t)\varphi(t)\langle f,\left(A(t)-\Delta(t)\right)f\rangle_{H}\alpha(t)dt+\int\limits_{0}^{1}\varphi(t)\varphi^{\prime}(t)\langle f,\left(A^{*}(t)-\Delta^{*}(t)\right)f\rangle_{H}\alpha(t)dt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}\varphi(t)\varphi(t)\langle f,\left(A^{*}(t)A(t)-\Delta^{*}(t)\Delta(t)\right)f\rangle_{H}\alpha(t)dt=0.

Then,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}\alpha(t)\varphi(t)\varphi^{\prime}(t)\langle f,\left(A(t)-\Delta(t)+A^{*}(t)-\Delta^{*}(t)\right)f\rangle_{H}dt+\int\limits_{0}^{1}\alpha(t)\varphi^{2}(t)\langle f,\left(A^{*}(t)A(t)-\Delta^{*}(t)\Delta(t)\right)f\rangle_{H}dt=0.

Since for each
t\in(0,1)
the following relations are true

A(t)-\Delta(t)=A(t)-\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}-A^{*}(t)\right)=2A_{r}(t)-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}\ a.\ e.\ t\in(0,1),

and

\displaystyle\Delta^{*}(t)\Delta(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}-A(t)\right)\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}-A^{*}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}\right)^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}A^{*}(t)-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}A(t)+A(t)A^{*}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}\right)^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}2A_{r}(t)+A(t)A^{*}(t)\ a.\ e.\ t\in(0,1),

then we have

\displaystyle 2\int\limits_{0}^{1}\alpha(t)\varphi(t)\varphi^{\prime}(t)\langle f,\left(2A_{r}(t)-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}\right)f\rangle_{H}dt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}\alpha(t)\varphi^{2}(t)\langle f,\left(A^{*}(t)A(t)-A(t)A^{*}(t)+2\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}A_{r}(t)-\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}\right)^{2}\right)f\rangle_{H}dt=0.

From the last relation, for any real-valued functions

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}\left(\alpha(t)\varphi^{\prime}(t)\Delta_{1}(t)+\alpha(t)\varphi(t)\Delta_{2}(t)\right)\psi(t)dt+\int\limits_{0}^{1}\left(\alpha(t)\varphi(t)\Delta_{1}(t)\right)\psi^{\prime}(t)dt=0.

Then from

\alpha(t)\Delta_{2}(t)=(\alpha(t)\Delta_{1}(t))^{\prime}\ a.\ e.\ t\in(0,1).

Hence,

\displaystyle\alpha(t)\langle f,\left(A^{*}(t)A(t)-A(t)A^{*}(t)+2\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}A_{r}(t)-\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}\right)^{2}I\right)f\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(t)\langle f,\left(2A_{r}(t)-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}\right)^{\prime}f\rangle_{H}+\alpha^{\prime}(t)\langle f,\left(2A_{r}(t)-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(t)}{\alpha(t)}I\right)f\rangle_{H}\ a.\ e.\ t\in(0,1).
