 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 A hydrogen-based long duration storage technology will be demonstrated with ARIES-Flatirons
 assets for the GKN Hydrogen, a division of GKN Powder Metallurgy, HY2MEGA metal hydride
 storage subsystem. The project will design and execute experiments that emulate commercial use
 cases for load management, backup power, and grid resiliency in remote, underserved
 communities.

 • Enhances U.S. competitiveness by utilizing DOE developed intellectual property and/or

 Summary of Research Results:

 The objective of this project was to demonstrate a metal hydride hydrogen storage system as a
 long-duration energy storage technology, fully integrated with hydrogen generation and end-use,
 at the Advanced Research on Integrated Energy Systems (ARIES)-Flatirons campus. The metal
 hydride system, provided by GKN Hydrogen (a division of GKN Powder Metallurgy), consists
 of two HY2MEGA metal hydride subsystems coupled with a dedicated thermal management
 system. Project activities included system installation and integration, demonstration of
 operational capabilities, and execution of experiments designed to emulate commercial use cases,
 including load management, backup power, and grid-resilience applications in remote
 communities. These use cases were developed in consultation with SoCalGas to assess the
 applicability of metal hydride hydrogen storage across a range of potential utility and customer
 business cases.

 The two metal hydride storage systems and associated temperature management system were
 successfully installed and integrated at the ARIES–Flatirons site. Integration activities included
 connection of hydrogen gas lines within the GKN systems, as well as interfacing the GKN
 systems with NLR’s existing hydrogen infrastructure. The Flatirons Campus hydrogen
 infrastructure includes a 1.25 megawatt (MW) electrolyzer, hydrogen gas compression,
 compressed gas storage, and a 1 megawatt (MW) fuel cell. The GKN temperature management
 system, which provides controlled heating to the metal hydride tanks, was also integrated with
 NLR’s glycol-based thermal integration loop. Following installation, the metal hydride tanks
 were purged with nitrogen and leak-checked. The nitrogen was subsequently displaced with
 hydrogen to initiate the activation process. Within one week of beginning activation, NLR
 received notification from Langley Holdings LLC that GKN Hydrogen was closing and that the
 Cooperative Research and Development Agreement (CRADA) would be terminated effective
   December 31, 2024.

 2

 Beginning in January 2025 and continuing over the subsequent months, NLR and Langley
 Holdings LLC engaged in discussions regarding the cost and process associated with removal of
 the system from the NLR campus. In parallel, NLR, SoCalGas, and DOE evaluated potential
 technical pathways forward for the project. During the summer of 2025, SoCalGas concluded its
 participation in the project, and the remaining cost-share funds were returned.

 Task 1: Integration and Installation

 This will focus on design-build work to install the HY2MEGA hydrogen storage subsystem at
 the ARIES-Flatirons site. NLR and GKN will jointly develop the design plan for the system.
 NLR will execute general construction, installation, integration, system operation, maintenance,
 repairs, and physical activities due to NLR ESHQ policies. A safety assessment by Environment,
 Safety, Health, and Quality (ESHQ), work justification, and written authorization by NLR line
 management is required for individual cases where GKN or SoCalGas are needed to perform
 work on site. The following is a description of the specific activities and roles. SoCalGas will be
 included in technical discussions of Task 1 and will be asked to assist as needed.

 Task 1.1: Design and Safety Analysis of HY2MEGA Tank Integration

 Integration of the HY2MEGA hydrogen storage subsystem with the existing ARIES-Flatirons
 hydrogen infrastructure will be designed including siting, electrical, thermal, hydrogen, and
 safety/control/data acquisition systems. This includes the design-build of a thermal management
 system, (TMS), that actuates the temperature associated with hydrogen discharging and charging
 for the HY2MEGA storage subsystem. It will use waste heat recovery from the fuel cell and
 renewable electricity from the site. The hydrogen system design will include the P&ID and
 safety system modifications required to directly connect the HY2MEGA hydrogen storage
 subsystem to the existing hydrogen infrastructure. NLR will conduct a Process Hazard Analysis
 (PHA) to evaluate the safety of the HY2MEGA hydrogen storage subsystem implementation at
 the NLR Flatirons campus with the additional requirements for the metal hydride storage,
 additional hydrogen capacity, thermal systems, physical infrastructure, electrical systems, and
 fire code. NLR will perform this work with input from the GKN engineering team for
 requirements for operating the HY2MEGA storage subsystem.

 Task 1.1 Results:

 The proper siting for the GKN HY2MEGA tanks at Pad 6 at the Flatirons campus was
 determined by NLR researchers working closely with Environment, Health, and Safety (EHS)
 personnel and in consultation with the National Fire Protection Association Hydrogen
 Technologies Code (NFPA 2) committee. The GKN system is a first-of-its-kind capability, and
 the safety code is not straightforward. The code requirement would have originally required 45
 feet from the Class 1, Division 2 zone, and, after discussion and more specific calculations, this
 distance was reduced to 15 feet due to the lower storage pressure. P&IDs were created that
 reflected this set back distance, and the details for the metal hydride integration with hydrogen,
 electrical, and coolant systems from the other systems at Pad 6 (including compressed gas,
 electrolyzer and fuel cell). The safety and controls systems were also designed to work
 seamlessly with the existing infrastructure.

 Task 1.2: Equipment Procurement and Site Preparation

 NLR will produce necessary components required for the build-out/integration phase. This
 includes the build-out of the electrical connections, integrated thermal system connections,
 HY2MEGA storage subsystem integration specific hydrogen pressure control and safety
 systems, and the required data acquisition and control system. NLR will program the hydrogen
 SCADA system for integration of the HY2MEGA storage subsystem controls. NLR will review
 this work with GKN to ensure it meets the requirement for the HY2MEGydrogen A storage
 subsystem.

 Task 1.2 Results:

 Material and installation requirements for every component in the GKN system were reviewed
 according to electrical and materials standards used at NLR. This detailed approach ensured that
 all components comply with U.S. codes and meet the stringent NLR standards. By ensuring that
 the HY2MEGA tanks had NFPA 2, 70, and 79; UL508A; and NEC 500 certifications and the
 temperature management system has the appropriate NEC certification prior to delivery at the
 NLR Flatirons Campus.

 Twelve researchers/engineers from NLR and GKN worked diligently to communicate the details
 of all gas and glycol interfaces. The teams also worked to establish the alignment of
 communication signals and channels between the GKN system and the NLR systems and
 infrastructure to operate, monitor system status, and data collection. This work aligned data
 collection systems, real time communication of system status and performance, data transfer to
 the NLR Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) system, and controls strategies for
 interfacing systems to enable integrated operation. Specific data channels were identified to be
 transferred to the NLR system for collection and later data analysis. Balance of Plant (BOP)
 connections were also engineered and built to create a glycol loop between the TMS and the
 MEGA Tanks.

 Task 1.3: HY2MEGA Tank Hardware Installation and Commissioning

 H2MEGA Tanks Hardware Installation and Commissioning Receive delivery of the HY2MEGA
 tank system and install at the site with the prepared utility connections. This will include leak
 checking and structural mounting and supporting auxiliary systems as required. Commissioning
 of the support systems will be completed, including the thermal loops, electrical systems, and
 DAQ and control systems.

 GKN will ship two HY2MEGA hydrogen storage system to the NLR Flatirons Campus. NLR
 will install the HY2MEGA hydrogen storage subsystem at the site and connect the utilities. This
 will include appropriate structural mounting of the tanks as well as supporting auxiliary systems
 as required. NLR will perform leak checking and pressure testing of all piping and components
 excluding the tanks themselves, which will be qualified for the operational pressures by the GKN
 factory acceptance test prior to shipment. Commissioning of the support systems will be
 completed by NLR, including the thermal loops, electrical systems, and DAQ-control systems.
 Hereafter the metal hydride storage subsystem is defined by the GKN HY2MEGA tanks, thermal
 management system, electrical systems, DAQ, and controls.

 The commissioning conducted by NLR will return the operational readiness of the metal hydride
 storage subsystem to charge and discharge hydrogen by actuation from, H2 supply, H2 return,
 and thermal management system, charge and discharge capacities and times to qualify readiness
 are: 400 +/- 100 kg charged over 72 hours and 20 +/- kg discharged to vent over 72 hours. GKN
 will supply operational protocols for the HY2MEGA hydrogen storage subsystem, subject matter
 expertise, and other input to ensure the commissioning is successful.

 Task 1.3 Results:

 The GKN tanks and TMS arrived on December 6-7, 2023. All systems were unloaded from the
 delivery trucks then craned into place December 11, 2023 at Pad 6. Figure 2 shows the arrival of
 the HY2MEGA and moving the TMS into place. This involved coordination of GKN, NLR
 Research Operations, NLR Environment, Safety, and Health (ES&H), in coordinating three
 delivery trucks, creating a lift plan, and obtaining a safe work permit with all plans approved by
 NLR ES&H. The delivery included use of a 500-ton crane and five supporting tractor trailers
 along with crane operator and rigging team.

 Figure 2. Two MEGA Tanks and the TMS were delivered and put into place at the Flatirons
 Campus.

 By May 2024, the electrical distribution panel and connections, hydrogen connections, control
 system, and heater installation were completed. Figure 3a and b show the black glycol piping
 connecting the TMS (not shown) to the two HYMEGA tanks, and these connections are labeled
 in Figure 3b. The hydrogen gas connections are stainless-steel piping between the tanks, and the
 electrical connections from the TMS to the NLR core system are shown.

 Figure 3. The installed electrical, glycol and hydrogen connections are labeled connecting the
 TMS to both MEGA Tanks.

 An auxiliary heater was needed to heat the metal hydride during the activation process up to
 90˚C. We successfully got through the procurement process after 6 months and had delays in
 system delivery, however, we received both the auxiliary heater and the electrical control box at
 the end of June 2024. These components were then installed and connected to the utilities. A
 third party completed the Technischer Überwachungsverein (TUV) evaluation, certified the
 electrical quality of the system is consistent with Underwriters Laboratories (UL) listed
 certification. This allowed the NLR Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ) to authorize the
 electrical certification for the systems, which facilitated the time needed to ensure system
 compliance and operational safety approval. The Modbus, safety controls and communication
 between NLR and GKN data acquisition systems were completed. The software for data collection
 and controls needs to be fine-tuned and finalized to allow streamlined control of the GKN system
 from the NLR control system.

 Commissioning and activation of both Mega tanks began in the Fall 2024. The metal hydride
 tanks were first heated to 80˚C, then leak checked up to 30 bars of argon. Both systems were
 then flushed 3 times with argon from 15 bar to 1 bar to ensure any residual oxygen would be
 removed. Next, the MEGA tanks were heated to 80˚C. then exposed to hydrogen and leak tested
 using increasing pressures up to 15 bar. An ambient temperature leak test was also performed up
 to 29 bars (the max pressure output of the NLR electrolyzer). Then, the activation process began
 with introducing hydrogen to a metal hydride tank at 80˚C and increasing the pressure in a
 stepwise manner up to 30 bar, held at this temperature and pressure for 2 hours, and then
 hydrogen was vented. We have completed 2 activation cycles at 3 bar and 5 bar.

 Figure 4 shows a current picture of the fully installed system. The balance of plants is
 operational, including: the electrical, glycol, hydrogen, and auxiliary heater, however, the metal
 hydride system was not fully activated when GKN Hydrogen was dissolved.
