Protocol for region-specific tumor dissection and protein extraction from a pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor mouse model
Authors: Vinod Vijayakurup, Michael Feely, Maria Zajac-Kaye
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryIsolation of high-quality protein from transgenic mouse tumors is essential for elucidating the mechanisms underlying tumor development. We describe steps for harvesting pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors from human thymidylate synthase (hTS)/Men1−/− mice, where hTS/TYMS is overexpressed in a Men1−/− background. We then detail procedures for protein extraction from hTS/Men1−/− and control Men1−/− tumors for immunoblotting. The protocol preserves protein integrity while reducing non-specific background commonly seen in pancreatic tissues. It also minimizes non-tumor tissue, enhancing the specificity of the protein extract.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Vijayakurup et al.1

Before you begin

This protocol is optimized for the isolation of high-quality total protein from pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors generated from GEMMs which develop pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors due to the deletion of Men1, a tumor suppressor gene in islet tissues.

We had previously generated a mouse model with elevated ectopic human TYMS (hTS) expression and conditional inactivation of the Men1 gene in pancreatic islet cells (hTS/Men1−/−).2 This GEMM develops carcinomas at a younger age with accelerated PanNET progression compared with Men1−/− GEMMs, thereby providing a novel mouse model to study the role of TYMS in PanNET acceleration and to elucidate molecular mechanisms underlying PanNET pathogenesis.

The protocol focuses on strict anatomical attention during tumor dissection. To reduce biological variability inherent to PanNETs, tumor tissues were consistently isolated from the same region of the pancreas (splenic attachment region at the tail of the pancreas) (Figure 1). The protocol also describes carefully controlled euthanasia conditions to minimize biological variability across mouse tumor samples. Innovation

This protocol provides a methodology to isolate proteins from mouse pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors, addressing potential sources of variability that may limit reproducibility in PanNET molecular studies. Unlike conventional protein extraction methods that treat pancreatic tissue as homogeneous, this protocol incorporates region-specific tumor dissection, ensuring that tumor samples are consistently harvested from defined pancreatic locations in all animals.

Overall, this protocol enables highly reproducible, biologically consistent protein isolation from PanNET tissues, facilitating comparative studies across cohorts and supporting mechanistic investigations into pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor development and progression.

Pancreatic tissues in hTS/Men1-/- and Men1-/- mice exhibit distinct stages ranging from hyperplasia to adenoma and carcinoma.2 Therefore, histological evaluation is essential to selecting suitability for tissues used for immunoblotting. In our workflow, tissues are initially harvested without pre-selection based on stage of disease, as external morphology does not reliably reflect tumor progression in this model. For each specimen the pancreatic tail region attached to spleen is cut and snap-frozen and stored at −80 °C for protein analysis. This region is selected primarily for consistency in dissection, though it is also known to contain a higher density of islet tissue, and not due to any pathological differences between different pancreatic regions. The remaining portion is fixed for histological evaluation.

After fixation and histological processing, immunohistochemistry (IHC) is performed to classify the tumor as hyperplasia, adenoma, or carcinoma. Based on this pathological assessment, only samples confirmed to contain carcinoma (Figure 3) are selected for subsequent immunoblotting analysis. Expected outcomes

This protocol yields highly reproducible and high-quality total protein lysates from pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors isolated from hTS/Men1-/- and Men1-/- mice (2 to 5 μg/μL). Key elements are the harvesting of tumor tissue from a specific location (such as the splenic lobe of the pancreas) and the controlled euthanasia conditions. Using the spleen attachment lobe of the pancreas ensures that the extracted samples are enriched for carcinoma-derived proteins with minimal contamination from exocrine pancreatic tissue.

Protein lysates generated using this method are expected to exhibit excellent protein integrity and reduced nonspecific background during immunoblotting2 (Figure 5), making them well-suited for protein analysis from PanNET tissues developed in genetically engineered mice model. Limitations

First, this protocol was optimized and validated primarily for the isolation of total non–membrane-bound and cytosolic proteins from pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor tissues. Although RIPA buffer can solubilize some membrane-associated proteins, the efficiency of isolating integral membrane proteins using this method has not been systematically evaluated. Therefore, this protocol may not be suitable for studies focusing on membrane proteins or membrane-enriched fractions without additional optimization.

Second, a small amount of non-tumorous pancreatic tissue contamination cannot be completely excluded due to the heterogeneous nature of pancreatic tumors and their close anatomical association with surrounding pancreatic tissue.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Incomplete tissue homogenization or visible tissue fragments after lysis (step 14).

Potential solution

Increase the volume of ice-cold RIPA buffer by an additional 100–150 μL and repeat the total homogenization cycle using the probe sonicator. Ensure the sample remains on ice throughout the process. If tissue fragments are still visible, perform one additional short sonication cycle. Avoid excessive sonication to prevent protein degradation.
