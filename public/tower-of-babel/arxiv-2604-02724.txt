Optimal control of the three-dimensional viscous Camassa-Holm equations with sparse controls Thanks: ♮ Corresponding author: giangnhh@hnue.edu.vn
Nguyen Hai Ha Giang ♮1

Abstract
. We investigate a distributed optimal control problem for the viscous Camassa–Holm equations with sparse controls and a general cost functional. Considering three different forms of sparsity-promoting terms, we prove the existence of optimal solutions, derive the corresponding optimality conditions and analyze the stability of optimal solutions with respect to the sparsity parameter.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
(at least
C^{3}
). We denote the time-space cylinder by
Q=\Omega\times(0,T)
. In this work, we consider the minimization of the following objective functional

\displaystyle J(y,u)=\int_{0}^{T}g(t,y(t))dt+\frac{\gamma}{2}\iint_{Q}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dxdt+\kappa j(u),\hskip 10.0pt\gamma>0.

Here, we consider three choices of the functional
j(u)
:

\displaystyle j_{1}(u):L^{1}(Q)^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},j_{1}(u)=\|u\|_{L^{1}(Q)^{3}}=\iint_{Q}|u(x,t)|dxdt,

(1.1)

\displaystyle j_{2}(u):L^{2}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega)^{3})\rightarrow\mathbb{R},j_{2}(u)=\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega)^{3})}=\left[\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)^{3}}^{2}dt\right]^{1/2},

(1.2)

\displaystyle j_{3}(u):L^{1}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T)^{3})\rightarrow\mathbb{R},j_{3}(u)=\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T)^{3})}=\int_{\Omega}\|u(x)\|_{L^{2}(0,T)^{3}}\,dx.

(1.3)

The corresponding objective functionals with
j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}
will be denoted by
J_{1},J_{2},J_{3},
respectively.

Meanwhile, the free variables, state
y
and control
u
, must fulfill the following three-dimensional viscous Camassa-Holm equations

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\left(y-\alpha^{2}\Delta y\right)+\nu\left(Ay-\alpha^{2}\Delta Ay\right)+\nabla p\\
\hskip 70.0pt=y\times\left(\nabla\times\left(y-\alpha^{2}\Delta y\right)\right)+u,&(x,t)\in(\Omega\times(0,+\infty)),\\
\nabla\cdot y=0,&(x,t)\in(\Omega\times(0,+\infty)),\\
y=Ay=0,&(x,t)\in(\partial\Omega\times(0,+\infty)),\\
y(x,0)=y_{0}(x),&\ x\in\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.4)

where
\alpha>0
,
\nu>0
is the viscous constant,
u
is the control,
y
is the corresponding fluid velocity vector,
g:[0,T]\times V\to\mathbb{R}
is a given function satisfying some certain assumptions which will be specified later in Section 2.

Moreover, we require that the control
u
belongs to the admissible set

U_{a,b}=\left\{u\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}):a_{i}\leq u_{i}(x,t)\leq b_{i}\ \text{a.e}\ (x,t)\in Q,i=1,2,3\right\},

for real numbers
a_{i}\leq 0<b_{i}
.

Henceforth, the corresponding problems with
j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}
will be denoted by
(\textbf{P}_{1}),(\textbf{P}_{2}),

(\textbf{P}_{3})
, respectively.

The viscous Camassa-Holm equations (VCHE for short), also known as the Lagrangian averaged Navier-Stokes (LANS-
\alpha
) or Navier-Stokes-
\alpha
equations, are a regularized modification of the Navier–Stokes equations that preserve the transport properties of circulation and vorticity dynamics. Initially, the inviscid (
\nu=0
) case, called the Lagrangian averaged Euler (LAE-
\alpha
) or Euler-
\alpha
equations, was introduced in
[
23
]
as a natural mathematical generalization of the integrable inviscid one-dimensional Camassa-Holm equation discovered in
[
10
]
. Then in
[
20
]
, the authors incorporated viscous dissipation into the equations due to the physical significance of the momentum
u+\alpha^{2}Au
. Since then, mathematical problems related to the viscous Camassa-Holm equations have attracted considerable attention. In bounded domains with Dirichlet or periodic boundary conditions, many results have been established concerning the existence of solutions and global attractors, see e.g.
[
19
,
20
,
24
,
25
,
28
,
33
]
and references therein. The time decay rates of solutions on the whole space were extensively investigated in
[
5
,
8
,
22
,
35
]
. We also refer the interested readers to
[
6
,
1
,
26
]
for results on the data assimilation and to
[
21
,
29
]
for the controllability of the viscous Camassa-Holm equations.

In this paper, we focus on optimal control problems, particularly on sparse optimal control problems, for the mentioned three-dimensional viscous Camassa–Holm equations. Sparse controls refer to controllers that are nonzero only in a small region of the domain. Such problems arise from the need of finding suitable, small regions to place the controllers, rather than acting on the flow at every point of the domain. As stated in
[
11
]
, there are two approaches to obtain the sparsity effect, each with its own mathematical challenges and physical applicability. The first employs an
L^{1}
-norm of the control in the cost functional and the other uses controllers in measure spaces. Numerous results have been published concerning both directions, see
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
18
,
27
,
30
]
and the relevant references.

Specifically, our problem contains an abstract cost functional and three choices of controls related to the
L^{1}
-norm. Following the general approach in
[
11
]
or
[
13
]
, we will prove the existence of optimal controls, establish the optimality conditions for this problem, but broaden by proving the stability of the control system with respect to the sparsity parameter. Compare with the existing results, the novelty of this article lies in two points. First, we allow the objective functional to be considerably more general. This class of functionals includes not only the standard quadratic form but also other classes as considered in
[
4
]
, while requiring even fewer conditions. Second, to the best of our knowledge, the problem of stability analysis of optimal solutions under variations of the sparsity parameter is so far unknown. There were works investigating the stability of solutions under different perturbations, e.g.
[
3
,
9
,
16
,
17
]
. However, these studies focus mainly on the cases of perturbations in the state equations, the initial data, or the Tikhonov regularizing parameter. The methods developed here, together with
[
3
]
, can be further applied to establish the Hölder stability in the case of bang-bang-bang structure
\gamma=0
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we will recall some auxilliary and fundamental results on function spaces and inequalites for the nonlinear terms related to the Camassa–Holm equations, as well as the properties of weak solution and the control-to-state mapping. After proving the existence of optimal solutions, in Sections 4 and 5, we derive the optimality conditions. Finally, the Lipschitz and Hölder stability analysis of optimal controls with respect to the sparsity parameter are given in the last section.

Since

. Then the domain of

\displaystyle D(A^{\alpha})=\{u:|A^{\alpha}u|<\infty\}.

The space

. Moreover, we can define a scalar product on

\displaystyle(u,v)_{D(A^{\alpha})}=(A^{\alpha}u,A^{\alpha}v).

When

, it is well-known that if

is even, we have the inclusion
\mathbb{H}^{k}(\Omega)\cap\mathbb{H}^{k-1}_{0}\subset D(A^{k/2})
; if

\mathbb{H}^{k}_{0}\subset D(A^{k/2})
. Moreover, if the boundary

, then the norm

is equivalent to the
\mathbb{H}^{k}
-norm, namely there exists

such that

\displaystyle c_{1}|A^{k/2}u|\leq\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{k}}\leq c_{2}|A^{k/2}u|.

For more facts concerning the fractional powers of

, see e.g.
[
31
]
. In particular, we mainly focus on the case when

. For the case

, we have the

\mathbb{H}^{1}_{0}
-norm. For the case

, we receive the familiar

space. For the case

and the norm

\mathbb{H}^{3}
-norm, or further the

-norm. Additionally, from the case

When

\displaystyle\langle A^{2}u,v\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}=(A^{3/2}u,A^{1/2}v)=((Au,v))

We denote by

\displaystyle\|y\|_{L^{p}(0,T;X)}

In particular,

2.4.
Assumptions on the objective functional and the set of admissible control

Recall that the objective functional of the so-called original optimal control problem is

\displaystyle J(y,u)=\int_{0}^{T}g(t,y(t))\mathrm{d}t+\frac{\gamma}{2}\iint_{Q}|u(x,t)|^{2}dxdt.

(2.6)

We shall impose the following assumptions on the functions
g
.

(
A1
)

The function
g:[0,T]\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is strictly convex, twice continuously Fréchet differentiable on
V
. Furthermore, for every
r>0
, there exists
L_{r}>0
independent of

such that

\displaystyle|g(t,y_{1})-g(t,y_{2})|\leq L_{r}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|,\quad\forall\,\|y_{1}\|+\|y_{2}\|\leq r,t\in[0,T].

We now give some examples of the objective functional.

Example 2.1
.

A first typical choice for the functional
J
is the quadratic functional given by

\displaystyle J(y,u)=\dfrac{\alpha_{Q}}{2}\iint_{Q}|y(x,t)-y_{Q}(x,t)|^{2}\,dxdt+\dfrac{\gamma}{2}\iint_{Q}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dxdt,

where
\alpha_{Q},\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
\gamma>0
, and the function
y_{Q}\in L^{2}(Q)^{3}
is the desired state.

Additionally, the first term in our objective functional
J
can be replaced by the integral
\displaystyle\dfrac{\alpha_{Q}}{2}\iint_{Q}|y(x,t)-y_{Q}(x,t)|^{p}\,dxdt
with
2\leq p<6
.

Example 2.2
.

Beside the above quadratic functional, another notable choice for the functional
J(y,u)
is

J(y,u)=\iint_{Q}|\nabla y(x,t)-\nabla y_{d}(x,t)|^{2}\,dxdt+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\dfrac{\gamma}{2}}\iint_{Q}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dxdt,

where
y_{d}\in L^{2}(0,T;D(A))
is the desired state.

3.
Existence of optimal solutions

In this section we will show that the optimal control problems
(\textbf{P}_{k}),k=1,2,3
admit a global solution. To do this, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
{y_{n}}
converges to
y
in
W(0,T;H^{3},V)
weakly, then for any
v\in L^{2}(0,T;V)
, we get the following convergence

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{B}(y_{n},y_{n}+\alpha^{2}Ay_{n}),v\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{\prime}),L^{2}(0,T;V)}\rightarrow\langle\widetilde{B}(y,y+\alpha^{2}Ay),v\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{\prime}),L^{2}(0,T;V)}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

Consider the difference
\langle\widetilde{B}(y_{n},y_{n}+\alpha^{2}Ay_{n}),v\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{\prime}),L^{2}(0,T;V)}-\langle\widetilde{B}(y,y+\alpha^{2}Ay),v\rangle
, we deduce that

Theorem 3.1
.

Problems
(\textbf{P}_{k})
admit at least a globally optimal solution
\overline{u}\in U_{a,b}
with associated state
\overline{y}\in W(0,T;H^{3},V)
for every
k=1,2,3
.

Proof.

For an arbitrary control
u
in
L^{2}(Q)^{3}
, by Theorem
2.1
, equations (
1.4
) has a unique weak solution
y\in W(0,T;H^{3},V)
. Furthermore, since

, there exists the infimum of
J_{k}
over all admissible controls and states

\displaystyle 0\leq\overline{J}_{k}:=\text{inf }J_{k}(y,u)<\infty.

Moreover, there is a sequence
\{(y_{n},u_{n})\}
of admissible pairs such that
J_{k}(y_{n},u_{n})
converges to
\overline{J}_{k}
as

.

From there, the proof follows a standard technique for optimal control of PDEs. We present the main steps here for completeness only.

Step 1:
The sequence
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
L^{2}(Q)^{3}
and
\{y_{n}\}
is bounded in
W(0,T;H^{3},V)
.

Step 2:
There exists a subsequence
(y_{n^{\prime}},u_{n^{\prime}})
converging to
(\overline{y},\overline{u})
weakly in
W(0,T;H^{3},V)\times L^{2}(Q)^{3}
, which is an admissible pair.

Step 3
: We will show that
\overline{J}_{k}=J_{k}(\overline{y},\overline{u})
for every
k=1,2,3
.
Since
j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}
and
g
are convex and continuous, each

is consequently weakly lower semicontinuous for all
k=1,2,3
. Moreover, we get that

converges to
\overline{y}
weakly in

and

is continuously embedded in

, which infer that

converges weakly to
\overline{y}(t)
in

. Hence,
g(t,\overline{y}(t))\leq\underset{n^{\prime}\rightarrow\infty}{\liminf}g(t,y_{n^{\prime}}(t))
and we get

\int_{0}^{T}g(t,\overline{y}(t))\,dt\leq\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}{\liminf_{n^{\prime}\to\infty}}g(t,y_{n^{\prime}}(t))\,dt\leq{\liminf_{n^{\prime}\to\infty}}\int_{0}^{T}g(t,y_{n^{\prime}}(t))\,dt,

where the last inequality is deduced by using Fatou’s lemma.
Similar expressions can be proved for the norm functionals

.

Therefore

\displaystyle J_{k}(\overline{y},\overline{u})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}g(t,\overline{y}(t))\,dt+\frac{\gamma}{2}\iint_{Q}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dxdt+\kappa j_{k}(\overline{y})

\displaystyle\leq{\liminf_{n^{\prime}\to\infty}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}g(t,y_{n^{\prime}}(t))\,dt+\frac{\gamma}{2}{\liminf_{n^{\prime}\to\infty}}\displaystyle\iint_{Q}|u_{n^{\prime}}(x,t)|^{2}\,dxdt+\kappa\,{\liminf_{n^{\prime}\to\infty}}j_{k}(y_{n^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leq{\liminf_{n^{\prime}\to\infty}}J_{k}(y_{n^{\prime}},u_{n^{\prime}})=\overline{J}_{k}.

Since
(\overline{y},\overline{u})
is admissible, and
\overline{J}_{k}
is the infimum over all admissible pairs, it follows that
\overline{J}_{k}=J_{k}(\overline{y},\overline{u})
. The proof is complete.
∎

This holds for all

By integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle{\langle z_{t}+\alpha^{2}Az_{t},\overline{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T;D(A)^{\prime}),L^{2}(0,T;D(A))}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle z_{t}(t)+\alpha^{2}Az_{t}(t),\overline{\lambda}(t)\rangle_{D(A)^{\prime},D(A)}\,dt

\displaystyle=\langle z(T)+\alpha^{2}Az(T),\overline{\lambda}(T)\rangle_{D(A)^{\prime},D(A)}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle z(t)+\alpha^{2}Az(t),\overline{\lambda}_{t}(t)\rangle_{D(A)^{\prime},D(A)}\,dt

\displaystyle=\langle\overline{\lambda}(T)+\alpha^{2}A\overline{\lambda}(T),z(T)\rangle_{D(A)^{\prime},D(A)}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\lambda}_{t}+\alpha^{2}A\overline{\lambda}_{t},z\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T;D(A)^{\prime}),L^{2}(0,T;D(A))}.

(4.15)

Combining (

(4.16)

\displaystyle+\langle-\overline{\lambda}_{t}-\alpha^{2}A\overline{\lambda}_{t}+\nu A(\overline{\lambda}+\alpha^{2}A\overline{\lambda})+\widetilde{B}^{\prime}(\overline{y},\overline{y}+\alpha^{2}A\overline{y})^{*}\overline{\lambda},z\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T;D(A)^{\prime}),L^{2}(0,T;D(A))}.

From (

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\overline{u}
be a locally optimal control with associated state
\overline{y}
. Then there exists
\overline{\lambda}
, which is the weak solution of the adjoint equations (
4.2
) such that

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\overline{\lambda}(t)+\gamma\overline{u}(t)),m(t)\right)\,dt\geq 0,\quad\forall m\in\mathcal{T}_{U_{a,b}}(\overline{u}).

(4.17)

As a special case, the variational inequality

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\overline{\lambda}(t)+\gamma\overline{u}(t),u(t)-\overline{u}(t)\right)\,dt\geq 0,\forall u\in U_{a,b},

(4.18)

is satisfied.

Moving to our sparse problems
(\textbf{P}_{k}),k=1,2,3
. First, we give some properties of the functionals

and the subdifferentials. We start with

.

Problem
(P_{1})
.
Since
j_{1}
is convex and Lipchitz continuous, its subdifferential is

\partial j_{1}(u)=\left\{\zeta\in L^{\infty}(Q)^{3}:j_{1}(v)-j_{1}(u)\geq\iint_{Q}\zeta(v-u)\,dxdt\ \forall v\in L^{1}(Q)^{3}\right\}.

By taking
v=0
and
v=2u
, we get that

\iint_{Q}\zeta u\,dxdt=j_{1}(u)\ \forall\zeta\in\partial j_{1}(u).

Hence,

\iint_{Q}\zeta\,dxdt\leq j_{1}(v)\ \forall\zeta\in\partial j_{1}(u),\forall v\in L^{1}(Q)^{3}.

Thus, if
\zeta\in\partial j_{1}(u)
then

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\zeta_{i}(x,t)=1&\ \text{if}\ u_{i}(x,t)>0,\\
\zeta_{i}(x,t)=-1&\ \text{if}\ u_{i}(x,t)<0,\\
\zeta_{i}(x,t)\in[-1,1]&\ \text{if}\ u_{i}(x,t)=0,\end{cases}\hskip 13.0pti=1,2,3.

(4.19)

We also have that if
\zeta\in L^{\infty}(Q)^{3}
and satisfies (
4.19
) then
\zeta\in\partial j_{1}(u)
.

Additionally, the directional derivatives of
j_{1}
exist and are given by

\displaystyle j^{\prime}_{1}(u)v

\displaystyle=\underset{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\lim}\frac{j_{1}(u+\varepsilon v)-j_{1}(u)}{\varepsilon}

(4.20)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left\{\iint_{Q_{u,i}^{+}}v_{i}\,dxdt-\iint_{Q_{u,i}^{-}}v_{i}\,dxdt+\iint_{Q_{u,i}^{0}}|v_{i}|\,dxdt\right\},

where

\displaystyle Q_{u,i}^{+}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,t)\in Q\,|\,u_{i}(x,t)>0\right\},

\displaystyle Q_{u,i}^{-}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,t)\in Q\,|\,u_{i}(x,t)<0\right\},

\displaystyle Q_{u,i}^{0}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,t)\in Q\,|\,u_{i}(x,t)=0\right\}.

Moreover, (
4.19
) and (
4.20
) yield

\underset{\zeta\in\partial j_{1}(u)}{\max}\iint_{Q}\zeta v\,dxdt=j^{\prime}_{1}(u)v\leq\frac{j_{1}(u+\rho v)-j_{1}(u)}{\rho}\leq j_{1}(u+v)-j_{1}(u),\forall\rho\in(0,1).

We are now ready to derive the first-order necessary condition for problem
(\textbf{P}_{1})
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\overline{u}
be a local solution to the problem
(\textbf{P}_{1})
with associated state
\overline{y}\in W(0,T;H^{3},V)
. Then there exists

\overline{\zeta}\in\partial j_{1}(\overline{u})
such that

\iint_{Q}\left(\overline{\lambda}+\gamma\overline{u}(t)+\kappa\overline{\zeta}\right)(u-\overline{u})\,dxdt\geq 0\ \forall u\in U_{a,b},

(4.21)

where
\overline{\lambda}
is the adjoint state, i.e. the unique solution of the adjoint equations (
4.2
).

Proof.

Since
U_{a,b}
is convex, we have that
\overline{u}+\rho(u-\overline{u})\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}U_{a,b}}
for every
\rho\in[0,1]
. From the local optimality of
\overline{u}
, we deduce
\mathcal{J}_{1}(\overline{u}+\rho(u-\overline{u}))-\mathcal{J}_{1}(\overline{u})\geq 0
for every
\rho
sufficiently small. Hence,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\underset{\rho\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\lim}\frac{\mathcal{J}_{1}(\overline{u}+\rho(u-\overline{u}))-\mathcal{J}_{1}(\overline{u})}{\rho}

\displaystyle=\underset{\rho\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\lim}\frac{\mathcal{J}(\overline{u}+\rho(u-\overline{u}))-\mathcal{J}(\overline{u})+\kappa j_{1}(\overline{u}+\rho(u-\overline{u}))-\kappa j_{1}(\overline{u})}{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{\rho\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\lim}\frac{\mathcal{J}(\overline{u}+\rho(u-\overline{u}))-\mathcal{J}(\overline{u})}{\rho}+\kappa(j_{1}(u)-j_{1}(\overline{u}))

\displaystyle=\iint_{Q}(\overline{\lambda}+\gamma\overline{u}(t))(u-\overline{u})\,dxdt+\kappa(j_{1}(u)-j_{1}(\overline{u})).

This implies that
\overline{u}
is the solution of the optimization problem

\underset{u\in U_{a,b}}{\min}I(u)=\iint_{Q}(\overline{\lambda}+\gamma\overline{u}(t))u\,dxdt+\kappa j_{1}(u).

Finally, since
I
is convex, we can use the subdifferential calculus to obtain

0\in\partial I(\overline{u})=(\overline{\lambda}+\gamma\overline{u}(t))+\kappa\partial j_{1}(\overline{u}).

Then, by a classical result in convex analysis (see Proposition 27.8 in
[
7
]
), there exists
\overline{\zeta}
such that

-(\overline{\lambda}+\gamma\overline{u}(t)+\kappa\overline{\zeta})\in\mathcal{N}_{U_{a,b}}(\overline{u}),

where
\mathcal{N}_{U_{a,b}}(\overline{u})
is the normal cone of the set
U_{a,b}
at
\overline{u}
. Thus we get (
4.21
).
∎

5.
Second-order optimality conditions

Following the approach in Section 4, the second-order of
g

\displaystyle\langle g_{yy}^{\prime\prime}(t,y)w_{1},w_{2}\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}=\int_{\Omega}g_{yy}^{\prime\prime}(t,y)[w_{1}(x),w_{2}(x)]\,dx.

Now we move onto the sparse problems
(\textbf{P}_{k}),k=1,2,3

The second-order conditions will be established based on the critical cone
\mathcal{C}_{\overline{u}}
, which refers to the set of all functions
v\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega))
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\overline{u})v

\displaystyle+\kappa j_{k}^{\prime}(\overline{u})v=0,

\displaystyle v_{i}(x,t)

\displaystyle\geq 0\ \text{if}\ \overline{u}_{i}(x,t)=a_{i},i=1,2,3,

(5.1)

\displaystyle v_{i}(x,t)

\displaystyle\leq 0\ \text{if}\ \overline{u}_{i}(x,t)=b_{i},i=1,2,3.

(5.2)

The set
\mathcal{C}_{\overline{u}}
is a closed, convex cone in

.

Before studying the second-order necessary and sufficient conditions for problems
(\textbf{P}_{k})
, we prove the following auxiliary lemma. Observe that we only state the main results corresponding to

. For

and

, analogous conclusions can be derived by adapting the arguments in

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
\overline{u}\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}U_{a,b}}
satisfies (
4.21
) for some
\overline{\zeta}\in\partial j_{1}(\overline{u})
and
v\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega))
satisfies (
5.1
) - (
5.2
). Then

\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\overline{u})v+\kappa j_{1}^{\prime}(\overline{u})v\geq\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\overline{u})v+\kappa\iint_{Q}\overline{\zeta}v\,dxdt\geq 0.

Moreover, if
v\in\mathcal{C}_{\overline{u}}
then

\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\overline{u})v+\kappa\iint_{Q}\overline{\zeta}v\,dxdt=0\ \text{and}\ j_{1}^{\prime}(\overline{u})v=\iint_{Q}\overline{\zeta}v\,dxdt,

(5.3)

or equivalently,
\displaystyle|v_{i}(x,t)|=\zeta_{i}(x,t)v_{i}(x,t)
for a.e
(x,t)\in Q_{\bar{u}}^{0}
and every
i=1,2,3
.

Proof.

The first inequality follows from the fact that
\displaystyle\underset{\overline{\zeta}\in\partial j_{1}(\overline{u})}{\max}\iint_{Q}\overline{\zeta}v\,dxdt=j_{1}^{\prime}(\overline{u})v
.
To prove the remaining affirmations, we construct a sequence
\{v_{k}\}
to approximate
v
as follows

\displaystyle v_{k,i}(x,t)=\begin{cases}0\ \text{if}\ a_{i}<\overline{u}_{i}(x,t)<a_{i}+\frac{1}{k}\ \text{or}\ b_{i}-\frac{1}{k}<\overline{u}_{i}(x,t)<b_{i},\\
\text{Proj}_{[-k,k]}\left(v_{i}(x,t)\right)\ \text{otherwise},k\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

(5.4)

Set
\rho_{k}=\frac{1}{k^{2}}
, then
\overline{u}+\rho v_{k}\in U_{a,b}\ \forall\,0<\rho<\rho_{k}
.
From definition (
5.4
), we get that
\displaystyle|v_{k,i}(x,t)|\leq|v_{i}(x,t)|\ \forall i
and
\displaystyle v_{k,i}(x,t)\rightarrow v(x,t)
for a.e

, thus

in

.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\left[J^{\prime}(\overline{u})v_{k,i}+\kappa\iint_{Q}\overline{\zeta}v_{k,i}(x,t)\,dxdt\right]

\displaystyle=\iint_{Q}\left(\overline{\lambda}+\gamma\overline{u}+\kappa\overline{\zeta}\right)\left[\left(\overline{u}+\rho v_{k,i}\right)-\overline{u}\right]\,dxdt\geq 0.

Passing to the limit as

yields
\displaystyle J^{\prime}(\overline{u})v+\kappa\iint_{Q}\overline{\zeta}v\,dxdt\geq 0
.
∎

Henceforth, for convenience, we define a function
\overline{\eta}(x,t)=\overline{\lambda}(x,t)+\gamma\overline{u}(x,t)+\kappa\overline{\zeta}(x,t).
Applying the above-mentioned results, we get the following properties of
\overline{\eta}(x,t)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\overline{u}_{i}(x,t)=a_{i}&\Rightarrow\overline{\eta}(x,t)\geq 0,\\
\overline{u}_{i}(x,t)=b_{i}&\Rightarrow\overline{\eta}(x,t)\leq 0,\hskip 20.0pt1\leq i\leq 3,\\
a_{i}<\overline{u}_{i}(x,t)<b_{i}&\Rightarrow\overline{\eta}(x,t)=0,\end{cases}

(5.5)

and

\begin{aligned} \begin{cases}\overline{\eta}_{i}(x,t)>0&\Rightarrow\overline{u}_{i}(x,t)=a_{i},\\
\overline{\eta}_{i}(x,t)<0&\Rightarrow\overline{u}_{i}(x,t)=b_{i},\end{cases}\end{aligned}\hskip 20.0pt1\leq i\leq 3{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}.}

(5.6)

It follows from the variational inequality that

\iint_{Q}\overline{\eta}v\,dxdt=\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\overline{u})v+\kappa\iint_{Q}\overline{\zeta}v\,dxdt=0\ \forall v\in\mathcal{C}_{\overline{u}}.

Together with the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{\overline{u}}
we get that

\overline{\eta}_{i}(x,t)v_{i}(x,t)=0\ \text{for a.e}\ (x,t)\in Q,\forall i=\overline{1,3},\forall v\in\mathcal{C}_{\overline{u}}.

(5.7)

Due to the uncertainty in the existence of second directional derivatives of the functions

, the following theorems give the second-order optimality conditions for
\mathcal{J}
with respect to the critical cone

.

Theorem 5.1
.

If
\overline{u}

is
a local minimum of
(\textbf{P}_{k})
then

\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\overline{u})v^{2}\geq 0\ \forall v\in\mathcal{C}_{\overline{u}}.

(5.8)

Proof.

Given

we define
v_{k}=(v_{k,1},v_{k,2},v_{k,3})
for

\displaystyle\text{in}\ L^{2}(Q)^{3},

\varepsilon>0
and
C>0
independent of
\overline{u}
such that it holds

(1)

Lipchitz stability for
(\textbf{P}_{1})
and
(\textbf{P}_{3})
:

\left\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}\leq C\kappa\ \text{for all}\ 0<\kappa<\varepsilon.

(6.6)

(2)

Hölder stability for
(\textbf{P}_{2})
:

\left\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}\leq C\kappa^{2/3}\ \text{for all}\ 0<\kappa<\varepsilon.

(6.7)

Before proving this theorem, we establish the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 6.1
.

There exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for all
\kappa\in(0,\varepsilon)
, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\overline{u}+\theta(\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}))(\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u})^{2}\geq\dfrac{\mu}{2}\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}

(6.8)

with
\mu
is given by
(\textbf{SSC})
.

Proof.

Let us take
\eta>0
as in
(\textbf{SSC})
. Then we shall prove that
\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}
satisfies the conditions of
(\textbf{SSC})
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. To this end, set
v_{\kappa}=\dfrac{\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}}{\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}}
. From the boundedness of
v_{\kappa}
, we can extract a subsequence
\{v_{\kappa_{n}}\}
such that
v_{\kappa_{n}}\rightharpoonup v
in
L^{2}(Q)^{3}
as
n\to\infty
. Since the cones
\mathcal{T}_{U_{a,b}}(\overline{u})
and
\mathcal{T}_{U_{a,b}}(\overline{u}_{\kappa})
are weakly closed, we get
v\in\mathcal{T}_{U_{a,b}}(\overline{u})\cap-\mathcal{T}_{U_{a,b}}(\overline{u}_{\kappa})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\overline{u})v\geq 0\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\overline{u}_{\kappa})v\leq 0.

Letting
n\to\infty
yields
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\overline{u})v=0
. From the weak convergence of
v_{\kappa_{n}}
, we get
\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\overline{u})v_{\kappa_{n}}=0
. Since this happens for all subsequences
\{v_{\kappa_{n}}\}
of
\{v_{\kappa}\}
which converge weakly in
L^{2}(Q)^{3}
, we find
\underset{\kappa\to 0}{\lim}\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\overline{u})v_{\kappa}=0
. Thus, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\overline{u})v_{\kappa}\leq\eta

holds for all
\kappa\in(0,\varepsilon)
. Thus
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\overline{u})(\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u})\leq\eta\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}
for all
\kappa\in(0,\varepsilon)
, which implies that
\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\overline{u})(\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u})^{2}\geq\mu\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}
.

Therefore (
6.8
) follows from the facts that
\mathcal{J}(u)
is of class
C^{2}
in
L^{2}(Q)^{3}
and
\overline{u}+\theta(\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u})\to\overline{u}
in
L^{2}(Q)^{3}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.3
.

Since
\overline{u}_{\kappa}
is the local solution of
(\textbf{P}_{\kappa})
, we have
\mathcal{J}_{\kappa}(\overline{u}_{\kappa})-\mathcal{J}_{\kappa}(\overline{u})\leq 0
, which can be written explicitly as

\mathcal{J}(\overline{u}_{\kappa})-\mathcal{J}(\overline{u})+\kappa\left(j_{i}(\overline{u}_{\kappa})-j_{i}(\overline{u})\right)\leq 0,\hskip 20.0pti=1,2,3.

(6.9)

Recalling that

\mathcal{J}({\overline{u}_{\kappa}})=\int_{0}^{T}g(t,y_{\overline{u}_{\kappa}}(t))\,dt+\frac{\gamma}{2}\iint_{Q}|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(x,t)|^{2}\,dxdt,

using Taylor expansion of
\mathcal{J}
yields

\mathcal{J}({\overline{u}_{\kappa}})-\mathcal{J}(\overline{u})=\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\overline{u})(\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u})+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\overline{u}+\theta(\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}))(\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u})^{2},\theta\in(0,1).

Since
\overline{u}_{\kappa}\in U_{a,b}
, the variational inequality (
4.18
) and Lemma
6.1
imply that

\mathcal{J}(\overline{u}_{\kappa})-\mathcal{J}(\overline{u})\geq\frac{\mu}{4}\|\overline{u}_{k}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}^{2}.

Now we consider the difference
j_{i}(\overline{u})-j_{i}(\overline{u}_{\kappa})
.

Case 1
:
j_{i}=j_{1}
.

When
j_{i}=j_{1}
, (
6.9
) is equivalent to

\mathcal{J}(\overline{u}_{\kappa})-\mathcal{J}(\overline{u})+\kappa\iint_{Q}\left[|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(x,t)|-|\overline{u}(x,t)|\right]\,dxdt\leq 0.

From this we deduce

\mathcal{J}(\overline{u}_{\kappa})-\mathcal{J}(\overline{u})\leq\kappa\iint_{Q}\left[|\overline{u}(x,t)|-|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(x,t)|\right]\,dxdt\leq\kappa\|\overline{u}-\overline{u}_{\kappa}\|_{L^{1}(Q)^{3}}.

Thus

\frac{\mu}{4}\|\overline{u}_{k}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}^{2}\leq\kappa\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{1}(Q)^{3}}.

(6.10)

However, because the domain
Q
has a finite volume, we get

\displaystyle\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{1}(Q)^{3}}

\displaystyle=\iint_{Q}1\times|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}|\,dxdt

(6.11)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\iint_{Q}1\,dxdt\right)^{1/2}\left(\iint_{Q}|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}|^{2}\,dxdt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\text{vol}(Q)^{1/2}\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}.

Substituting (
6.11
) into (
6.10
), we obtain

\frac{\mu}{4}\|\overline{u}_{k}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}^{2}\leq\kappa\text{vol}(Q)^{1/2}\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}.

(6.12)

Case 2
:
j_{i}=j_{2}
.

Since
\displaystyle j_{2}(u)=\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega))}=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}
, (
6.9
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\overline{u}_{\kappa})-\mathcal{J}(\overline{u})

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\left[j_{2}(\overline{u})-j_{2}(\overline{u}_{\kappa})\right]

(6.13)

\displaystyle=\kappa\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\overline{u}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}-\kappa\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}.

Notice that
\overline{u}
is an optimal solution of problem
(\textbf{P})
, meaning that
\mathcal{J}(\overline{u}_{\kappa})-\mathcal{J}(\overline{u})\geq 0
, we get
j_{2}(\overline{u})\geq j_{2}(\overline{u}_{\kappa})
. Thus we can write

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\overline{u}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}-\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\overline{u}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left[\left(\int_{\Omega}|\overline{u}(t)|\,dx\right)^{2}-\left(\int_{\Omega}|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(t)|\,dx\right)^{2}\right]dt\right]^{1/2}.

Moreover, it follows

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|\overline{u}(t)|\,dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle-\left(\int_{\Omega}|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(t)|\,dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\Omega}(|\overline{u}(t)|+|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(t)|)\,dx\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}(|\overline{u}(t)|-|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(t)|)\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(b).\int_{\Omega}|\overline{u}(t)-\overline{u}_{\kappa}(t)|\,dx

provided that
\Omega
is a bounded domain.
Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left[\left(\int_{\Omega}|\overline{u}(t)|\,dx\right)^{2}-\left(\int_{\Omega}|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(t)|\,dx\right)^{2}\right]dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}C(b).\int_{\Omega}|\overline{u}(t)-\overline{u}_{\kappa}(t)|\,dt

(6.14)

\displaystyle\leq C(b,T).\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{1}(Q)^{3}}.

Substituting (
6.14
) into (
6.13
) leads to

\mathcal{J}(\overline{u}_{\kappa})-\mathcal{J}(\overline{u})\leq\kappa C(b,T)^{1/2}.\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{1}(Q)^{3}}^{1/2}.

(6.15)

Then by using Lemma
6.1
and (
6.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{4}\|\overline{u}_{k}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa C(b,T)^{1/2}.\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{1}(Q)^{3}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa C(b,T)^{1/2}\text{vol}(Q)^{1/4}\|\overline{u}_{\kappa_{n}}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}^{1/2}.

Consequently,

\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}\leq\left(\frac{4}{\mu}\right)^{2/3}C(b,T)^{1/3}\text{vol}(Q)^{1/6}\kappa^{2/3}.

(6.16)

Case 3
:
j_{i}=j_{3}
.

Analogously to Case 2, we also get

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}({\overline{u}}_{\kappa})-\mathcal{J}(\overline{u})

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\left[j_{3}(\overline{u})-j_{3}(\overline{u}_{\kappa})\right]

(6.17)

\displaystyle=\kappa\int_{\Omega}\|\overline{u}(x)\|_{L^{2}(0,T)^{3}}\,dx-\kappa\int_{\Omega}\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(x)\|_{L^{2}(0,T)^{3}}\,dx.

The definition of
j_{3}
yields

\mathcal{J}({\overline{u}}_{\kappa})-\mathcal{J}(\overline{u})\leq\kappa\int_{\Omega}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}|\overline{u}(x,t)|^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}-\left(\int_{0}^{T}|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(x,t)|^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}\right]dx.

On the other hand, we can write

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T}|\overline{u}(x,t)|^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}-\left(\int_{0}^{T}|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(x,t)|^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{\int_{0}^{T}|\overline{u}(x,t)|^{2}\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(x,t)|^{2}\,dt}{\left(\int_{0}^{T}|\overline{u}(x,t)|^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}+\left(\int_{0}^{T}|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(x,t)|^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{0}^{T}(|\overline{u}(x,t)|^{2}-|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(x,t)|^{2})\,dt}{2a\sqrt{T}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(|\overline{u}(x,t)|^{2}-|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(x,t)|^{2}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left[(|\overline{u}(x,t)|+|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(x,t)|)(|\overline{u}(x,t)|-|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(x,t)|)\right]\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C(T,b)\int_{0}^{T}|\overline{u}(x,t)-\overline{u}_{\kappa}(x,t)|\,dt.

Therefore

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T}|\overline{u}(x,t)|^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}-\left(\int_{0}^{T}|\overline{u}_{\kappa}(x,t)|^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(T,b)}{2a\sqrt{T}}\int_{0}^{T}|\overline{u}(x,t)-\overline{u}_{\kappa}(x,t)|\,dt.

Finally,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}({\overline{u}}_{\kappa})-\mathcal{J}(\overline{u})

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\int_{\Omega}\frac{C(T,b)}{2a\sqrt{T}}\int_{0}^{T}|\overline{u}(x,t)-\overline{u}_{\kappa}(x,t)|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\kappa C(T,a,b,|\Omega|)\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{1}(Q)^{3}}.

Applying Lemma
6.1
and (
6.11
) once again, we conclude

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{4}\|\overline{u}_{k}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa C(T,a,b,|\Omega|).\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{1}(Q)^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa C(T,a,b,|\Omega|)\text{vol}(Q)^{1/2}\|\overline{u}_{\kappa_{n}}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}.

Consequently,

\|\overline{u}_{\kappa}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}(Q)^{3}}\leq\frac{4}{\mu}C(T,a,b,|\Omega|)\text{vol}(Q)^{1/2}\kappa.

(6.18)

It follows directly from (
6.12
), (
6.16
) and (
6.18
) that (
6.6
) and (
6.7
) hold. We achieve the Lipschitz and Hölder convergence of the solutions to the sparse optimal control problems toward the corresponding standard non-sparse ones.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

The stability of the sparse control systems (
6.6
) and (
6.7
) can also be presented under the
L^{1}
-norm.
