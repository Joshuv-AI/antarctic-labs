Survey data of 71,578 ancient and notable trees across 183 counties in Sichuan Province, China (2016–2023)
Authors: Kai Liu, Kaibo Hu, Shouqiang Wang, Shoujian Li, Yufeng Ma, Wenjun Ma, Yuexiu Su
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This dataset presents survey data of 71,578 ancient and notable trees in Sichuan Province, China. The data cover all 183 county-level administrative units within Sichuan Province. Data collection was conducted from May 2016 to March 2023. Twenty-four indicators were recorded through field surveys: tree ID, detailed address, tree category, species, coordinates, ancient tree grade, tree age, tree height, girth at breast height / girth at ground level, mean crown width, growth site, distribution pattern, elevation, aspect, slope, slope position, soil name, growth vigour, growth environment, factors affecting growing environment, historical and cultural description, description of special tree condition, existing above-ground protection, and maintenance and restoration measures. All 24 indicators and their corresponding survey methods strictly follow the national standard LY/T 2738–2016. The dataset comprises three files: (1) the survey form template with completion instructions; (2) raw survey data containing 71,578 individual records; and (3) summary statistics presenting the distribution of tree counts across 13 indicators, including city (prefecture), growth site, and tree age. This dataset can support regional comparative analyses of ancient and notable trees, multi-dimensional interaction analyses among trees, environment, and humans, as well as longitudinal studies of tree population changes.

Value of the Data

•This dataset provides survey data of 71,578 ancient and notable trees in Sichuan Province, one of the global biodiversity hotspots. To date, no dataset of this scale has been available as open access data in China. Researchers can use this dataset as a benchmark for comparative analyses with ancient and notable tree data from other regions.•The dataset contains 24 survey indicators covering multiple dimensions, including tree identification information, tree biological information, environmental dimension information, and human contact and impact information. The dataset is comprehensive and detailed, enabling researchers to conduct multi-dimensional interaction analyses among trees, environment, and humans.•The survey indicators are derived from the national standard LY/T 2738–2016 "Technical regulation for surveying of old and notable trees", and the survey methods strictly followed this standard. This standard was developed through continuous validation, adjustment, and improvement over more than 30 years since China's first national survey of ancient and notable trees was implemented in 1983. The standardised methodology ensures comparability of data across different regions and different survey periods, supporting longitudinal studies of ancient and notable tree population changes.

Background

Ancient and notable trees have attracted considerable attention because they serve as carriers of ecological succession records and symbols of human civilisation. Survey data of ancient and notable trees can be used for research on climate change, ecological and environmental changes, religious beliefs, and tree-environment interactions [[1], [2], [3]]. The physiological mechanisms of ancient and notable trees, spatial distribution characteristics, rescue techniques for endangered individuals, and policies and measures for conservation management [[4], [5], [6]] are also popular research topics. Therefore, investigating the growth of ancient and notable trees, environmental impact factors, and human disturbance and influence in a given region is essential for any related research.

Sichuan Province is located within a global biodiversity hotspot [7]. As of 15 March 2023, 71,578 ancient and notable trees were distributed across the province. Local authorities have invested resources in the protection of ancient and notable trees for more than 20 years, but there has been a lack of quantitative assessment of conservation effectiveness. This situation hinders both the optimal allocation of resources and the improvement of conservation strategies and outcomes. The survey data were collected based on the indicators and methods specified in the national standard LY/T 2738–2016 "Technical regulation for surveying of old and notable trees" [8]. The objective was to comprehensively determine the growth and conservation management status of ancient and notable trees in Sichuan Province and to provide consultation for the optimisation of existing conservation strategies. In addition to providing foundational data support for the "tree-environment-human" assessment model and conservation strategy optimisation described in Liu et al. [9], these data can also be used for impact analyses across the multiple dimensions of trees, environment, and humans, for comparative analyses of conservation effectiveness with other regions, and for longitudinal studies of tree population changes.

Data Description

The dataset consists of three files:(1)Survey form template and completion instructions. The form is named "Ancient and Notable Trees Individual Survey Form". One survey form is required for each ancient or notable tree. The form contains 24 survey indicators, including: tree ID, detailed address, tree category, species, coordinates, ancient tree grade, tree age, tree height, girth at breast height / girth at ground level, mean crown width, growth site, distribution pattern, elevation, aspect, slope, slope position, soil name, growth vigour, growth environment, factors affecting growing environment, historical and cultural description, description of special tree condition, existing above-ground protection, and maintenance and restoration measures. These 24 indicators cover multiple aspects, including tree identification information, biological information, environmental information, and human dimension information. Completion instructions for each indicator are provided at the end of the form. The survey methods for each indicator follow those specified in the national standard LY/T 2738–2016 [8].(2)Raw data. These data comprise the 24-indicator survey information for 71,578 ancient and notable trees across Sichuan Province, completed based on the survey forms. The data cover 21 cities (prefectures) within Sichuan Province and all county-level administrative units (183) under their jurisdiction. Original data records containing precise geographical coordinates are not disclosed owing to legal restrictions. Declassified raw data may be obtained from the authors upon reasonable request, subject to approval by the data management unit.(3)Summary statistics. These statistics present the distribution of tree counts across 13 different survey indicators for the 71,578 ancient and notable trees. The table is titled by tree category and ancient tree grade. The data categories include three main types: ancient trees, notable trees, and trees that are both ancient and notable. Ancient tree grades are further divided into Grade I, Grade II, and Grade III ancient trees. The variables listed in the first column of the table are: city (prefecture), growth site, distribution pattern, slope position, growth vigour, growth environment, tree age, mean crown width, tree height, girth at breast height / girth at ground level, aspect, slope, and scientific name. Indicators such as factors affecting growing environment, historical and cultural description, description of special tree condition, existing above-ground protection, and maintenance and restoration measures are not included in the summary statistics because they contain qualitative textual descriptions that are not easily quantified. Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Survey scope. The survey covered 21 cities (prefectures) within Sichuan Province and all county-level administrative units (183) under their jurisdiction. Trees located in primary forest areas were not included in this survey.

Survey period. The survey period ran from May 2016 to March 2023 and was divided into two phases. The intensive survey phase (May 2016 to December 2018) conducted a comprehensive survey of all ancient and notable trees within the survey scope. The supplementary survey phase (January 2019 to March 2023) surveyed omitted or newly identified ancient and notable trees.

Organisation of the survey. County-level competent authorities were responsible for the implementation of survey work within their respective jurisdictions, working under the technical guidance of the Sichuan Forest and Grassland Survey and Planning Institute. All survey personnel were required to receive standardised training based on the national standard LY/T 2738–2016 and to pass an assessment before conducting the survey. All survey data were required to pass quality inspection by the Sichuan Forest and Grassland Survey and Planning Institute. The final data were submitted to the China Intelligent Management System for Ancient and Notable Trees (https://www.gsmm.org.cn/).

Equipment and tools. Due to the wide survey scope (183 county-level administrative units) and the large number of survey units and personnel, many different equipment models were used, and a complete list of all models cannot be provided. However, the most frequently used equipment and models included: GPS receivers (e.g., Garmin GPSmap 60CSx, UniStrong 718/728 series, GARMIN eTrex vista Cx, UniStrong G138BD, Magellan eXplorist 710), altimeters (e.g., Hargang CGQ-1, Laser Technology TruPulse 200/200 L, Hargang forest altimeter RC-1/30, Suunto PM-5, Onick 3000X), measuring tapes (e.g., Deli DL9250 50 m, Tajima EPK series 50 m, Xiangpai tape measure type 89 100 m), and cameras (e.g., Canon EOS 5D Mark II, Nikon D800/D800E/D810, Sony A7R II/III/IV, Fujifilm X-T3, Leica M10).

Survey methods. Each ancient or notable tree required the completion of 24 survey items according to the survey form. The survey methods for the 24 items were based on those specified in the national standard LY/T 2738–2016 "Technical regulation for surveying of old and notable trees" [8].

Limitations

This dataset has five primary limitations. (1) Raw data containing detailed addresses and precise coordinates of each ancient and notable tree are not publicly available due to legal restrictions. Declassified data may be obtained from the authors upon reasonable request, subject to approval by the data management unit. (2) The methods for determining tree age mainly included literature review, growth ring–diameter regression estimation, increment borer measurement, and interview estimation. Except for the increment borer method, tree ages obtained using other methods may contain errors. (3) The dataset lacks indicators reflecting the ecological functions of ancient and notable trees, such as air purification and soil and water conservation capacity. In large-scale surveys of ancient and notable trees, ecological function indicators are difficult to measure and quantify, which limits their inclusion. These indicators are also not included in the standard LY/T 2738–2016. (4) Trees located in primary forest areas were not included in the survey scope due to safety, legal, and administrative reasons. (5) Despite rigorous technical training and quality control, a small number of outliers or data entry errors may still exist in the raw data due to the large scale of the survey, the long survey period, and the large number of investigators involved.
