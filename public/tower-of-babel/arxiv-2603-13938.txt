The multi-height distribution implies the Batyrev-Manin principle
Bongiorno Nicolas

Abstract
. We explain how to deduce from the multi-height analysis of rational points on a toric stack (respectively on a toric variety) the asymptotic study of the number of rational points of bounded orbifold anticanonical height (respectively bounded anticanonical height), using a general version of the hyperbola method developed by Marta Pieropan and Damaris Schindler.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and previous results

For a quasi-Fano variety
V
, i.e. one satisfying
[
19
, Hypothesis 3.27]
and with infinitely many rational points, one may study asymptotically the finite set of rational points of bounded height
H
associated with the anticanonical line bundle
\omega_{V}^{-1}
. In
[
12
]
,
[
1
]
and
[
17
]
, Batyrev, Franke, Manin, Tschinkel, and Peyre provided strong evidence supporting conjectures relating the asymptotic behaviour of the number of rational points of bounded height on a dense open subscheme of
V
to geometric invariants of
V
. Batyrev and Tschinkel proved these conjectures using harmonic analysis methods in
[
2
]
for proper, smooth, split toric varieties, while Salberger proved them via a universal torsor lifting method in
[
21
]
.

Recently, Ellenberg, Satriano, and Zureick-Brown, in
[
11
]
,
initiated a program aimed at providing a common framework for Malle’s conjecture
and the Batyrev–Manin principle. Subsequently, Darda and Yasuda were able to
extend the formalism of Arakelov heights on varieties to Deligne–Mumford (DM)
stacks and to formulate precise conjectures analogous to the Batyrev–Manin
principle, provided one considers rational points of bounded height
H
, where

H
is the height associated with the orbifold anticanonical line bundle (see

[
7
, Definition 4.3, Definition 9.1 and Conjecture 5.6]
).
This new formalism notably allowed a reinterpretation of number-theoretic work on Malle and Bhargava’s conjectures on the distribution of Galois extensions with fixed group
G
(see
[
14
,
15
]
and
[
3
]
) through the lens of Manin’s program for
BG
. Subsequently, in
[
13
]
, Santens and Loughran further reinterpreted these number-theoretic problems through the study of rational points of
BG
using this new machinery. They defined a Tamagawa measure associated with
BG
and formulated a conjecture on what should be the leading constant in the asymptotic behaviour of rational points of bounded height on
BG
(see
[
13
, Definition 8.7 and Conjecture 9.1]
). Darda and Yasuda also established the asymptotic behaviour of the number of rational points of bounded orbifold anticanonical height on toric stacks in
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Peyre recently proposed in
[
19
, Question 4.8]
a new approach to studying the asymptotic distribution of rational points of bounded height. Instead of considering a single height, it is natural to consider all possible heights. The author’s doctoral work consisted in establishing that the expected asymptotic behaviour is obtained for toric varieties (see
[
4
, Theorem 2.15]
) and for toric stacks (see
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
).

The purpose of this article is to show that, once the asymptotic behaviour of rational
points with respect to multi-heights is known, one can recover the asymptotic formula
for the number of rational points of bounded anticanonical height (or bounded orbifold
anticanonical height in the stacky setting).

In particular, although it was already understood that the leading constant obtained in

[
4
, Theorem 2.15]
coincides with the
one appearing in
[
21
, Theorem 11.49]
, the present approach makes it
possible to identify the constant obtained in

[
6
, Corollary 4.4.5]
with the orbifold Tamagawa
number of a toric stack, as defined in

[
5
, Definition 6.51,Theorem 6.52]
.

This work essentially relies on the more general version of the hyperbola method developed by Pieropan and Schindler in
[
20
, Section 4]
.

1.2.
Terminology and the precise statement

Let
X
be a smooth and complete split toric variety, respectively toric stack, over
\mathbf{Q}
. We write
T\subset X
for its dense open (stacky) torus. For the remainder of the article, we refer the reader to
[
4
, Section 2.3]
and
[
5
, Section 5]
for further details on toric varieties and toric stacks. To simplify the reading of this article and to use the same notation for both varieties and toric stacks, we adopt the following notations to denote the geometric objects associated with
X
when it is a toric stack:

•

we denote by
\Pic(X)
the orbifold Picard group of
X
(see
[
5
, Definition 3.7]
);

•

we denote by
\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)
the cone of orbifold effective divisors of
X
(see
[
5
, Definition 3.17]
);

•

we denote by
\omega_{X}^{-1}
the orbifold anticanonical line bundle of
X
(see
[
5
, Definition 3.14]
);

•

we denote by
\mathcal{T}
the extended universal torsor (see
[
5
, Definition 5.34, Definition 6.1]
);

•

we write
\Sigma(1)
for the union of the set of rays of a fan associated to
X
and the set of twisted sectors
(this corresponds to the notation
\Sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}(1)
introduced
in
[
5
, Notations 5.33]
and used subsequently);

•

for
\lambda\in\Sigma(1)
, we denote by
[D_{\lambda}]
the corresponding class in the orbifold Picard group (see
[
5
, Notation 3.9,5.26,5.38]
).

If
X
is a toric variety,
\Pic(X)
denotes its usual Picard group,
\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)
its cone of effective divisors,
\omega_{X}^{-1}
its anticanonical line bundle and
\mathcal{T}
its universal torsor. Moreover,
\Sigma(1)
denotes the set of rays of a fan associated with
X
,
and for
\lambda\in\Sigma(1)
,
[D_{\lambda}]
denotes the class of the
boundary divisor in the Picard group.
With this notation, we set
\rho(X)=\rank_{\mathbf{Z}}(\Pic(X))
, or simply
\rho
.

We equip
X
(respectively
X^{\coarse}
) with its natural system of heights as in
[
21
, Definition 9.2]
and
[
1
, Proposition 2.1.2]
. If
X
is a toric stack, we endow it moreover with its natural stacky data (see
[
5
, Definition 3.25 and Section 6]
). With these conventions, we define the multi-height map

h:X(\mathbf{Q})\rightarrow\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}

as in
[
4
, Definition 2.7]
for toric varieties and as in
[
5
, Definition 3.26]
for toric stacks. We also define a local height on the universal torsor at a given place
v\in M_{\mathbf{Q}}
,

h_{\mathcal{T},v}:\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Q}_{v})\longrightarrow\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}.

as in
[
4
, Definition 3.6]
for toric varieties and as in
[
5
, Definition 6.20]
for toric stacks

We define a measure
\nu
on
\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
as follows:

Definition 1.1
.

\nu(\mathrm{D})=\int\limits_{\mathrm{D}}e^{\langle\omega_{X,}^{-1},y\rangle}dy

for any compact subset
\mathrm{D}
of
\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
, where the Haar measure
dy
on
\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
is normalized so that the covolume of the dual lattice of the Picard group is one.

For any domain
\mathrm{D}\subset\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
, for any subset
W\subset X(\mathbf{Q})
, we write:

W_{h\in\mathrm{D}}=\{P\in W\mid h(P)\in\mathrm{D}\}.

We have proven in
[
4
, Theorem 4.16]
and in
[
5
, Theorem 7.16]
the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathrm{D}_{1}
be a finite union of compact polyhedra of
\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
,
and let
u
be an element of the interior of the dual of the effective cone

(\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee})^{\circ}
.
For a real number
B>1
, we set:

\mathrm{D}_{B}=\mathrm{D}_{1}+\log(B)u.

Then the multi-height asymptotic behaviour is of the form:

\sharp(X(\mathbf{Q}))_{h\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}\underset{B\rightarrow+\infty}{\sim}\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1})\tau(X)B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}

where
\tau(X)
is the Tamagawa number of
X
(see
[
4
, Definition 3.22]
and
[
5
, Definition 6.51]
).

The purpose of this article is to show how this theorem leads to the asymptotic study of rational points of bounded anticanonical height, in line with the classical Manin program. To accomplish this, we will use the more general version of the hyperbola method developed by Pieropan and Schindler. Let us recall their result
[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f:\mathbf{N}^{\rho}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}_{+}
be an arithmetic function satisfying the following property:
there exist strictly positive real numbers
C_{f,M}\leqslant C_{f,E}
,
\Delta>0
, and
\omega_{i}>0
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho
such that,
for
B_{1},\dots,B_{\rho}\in\mathbf{R}_{\geqslant 1}
, we have

\sum\limits_{1\leqslant y_{i}\leqslant B_{i},\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho}f(y)=C_{f,M}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\omega_{i}}+O\!\left(C_{f,E}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\omega_{i}}\min\limits_{1\leqslant i\leqslant{\rho}}(B_{i})^{-\Delta}\right),

where the implied constant is independent of
f
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a finite set and
\{\alpha_{i,k}\}_{1\leqslant i\leqslant s,\,k\in\mathcal{K}}
a family of positive real numbers.
We define

\mathcal{D}(B,\alpha)=\left\{y\in\mathbf{N}^{\rho}\Big|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}y_{i}^{\alpha_{i,k}}\leqslant B\ \forall k\in\mathcal{K}\right\}.

We wish to evaluate the sum

S_{f}(B)=\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{D}(B,\alpha)}f(y).

Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the polyhedron consisting of all
t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\rho}
satisfying

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}\alpha_{i,k}\omega_{i}^{-1}t_{i}\leqslant 1\quad\forall k\in\mathcal{K}.

If
\mathcal{P}
is bounded, not contained in a hyperplane, and if the face
F
on which the function

\sum\limits_{1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho}t_{i}
attains its maximal value
a
is not contained in a coordinate hyperplane of
\mathbf{R}^{\rho}
,
then, writing
k=\dim(F)
, we have

S_{f}(B)=(s-1-k)!\,C_{f,M}\,c_{\mathcal{P}}\,\log(B)^{k}B^{a}+O\!\left(C_{f,E}\,\log(\log(B))^{s}\,\log(B)^{k-1}B^{a}\right),

where
c_{\mathcal{P}}
is an explicit constant.

Let us consider for a real number
B>1
, the set

T(\mathbf{Q})_{h(P)(\omega_{X}^{-1})\leqslant\log(B)}=\{P\in T(\mathbf{Q})\mid h(P)(\omega_{X}^{-1})\leqslant\log(B)\}.

Using their theorem, we recover the known result of
[
21
, Theorem 11.49]
and
[
6
, Theorem 4.4.4]
:

Theorem 1.4
.

We have the following asymptotic behaviour:

\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h(P)(\omega_{X}^{-1})\leqslant\log(B)}\underset{B\rightarrow+\infty}{\sim}\alpha(X)\tau(X)B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}\log(B)^{\rho(X)-1}

where
\alpha(X)
is the constant given by
4.9
.

We can deduce the following corollary about the leading constant in the asymptotic distribution of rational points of toric stacks:

Corollary 1.5
.

For
X
a toric stack, the Tamagawa number
\tau(X)
of a split toric stack
X
defined by the author in
[
5
, Definition 6.51]
corresponds to the constant obtained by Darda and Yasuda in
[
6
, Theorem 4.4.4]
. That is to say, we have the equality:

(\rho-1)!\ \omega_{H}(X(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{Q}}))=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\rank\Pic_{\orb}(X)}\cdot\mu_{X,p}(X(\mathbf{Q}_{p})),

where
\mu_{X,p}
is a Tamagawa measure on
X(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
(see
[
5
, Definition 6.46]
). Moreover for every
p
such that
X_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}
is a tame DM stack, the following equality holds:

\mu_{X,p}\bigl(X(\mathbf{Q}_{p})\bigr)=\frac{\sharp X(\mathbf{F}_{p})}{p^{\dim(X)}}+\frac{1}{p}\cdot\sum\limits_{\mathcal{S}\in\pi_{0}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mu}X)}\frac{\sharp\,\mathcal{S}\!\left(\mathbf{F}_{p}\right)}{p^{\dim(\mathcal{S})}}

where
\sharp
means the groupoid cardinality (see
[
5
, Theorem 6.52]
).

1.3.
Outline of the article

In the second section of this article, we extend the results of theorem
1.2
obtained by the author in
[
4
]
and in
[
5
]
to the case where the multi-height is bounded above but not below. In the third section, we apply the general version of the hyperbola method developed by Pieropan and Schindler in
[

\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}
into simplicial subcones.

2.
Extension of the multi-height analysis to the case bounded above but not below

Let
\Lambda
be a simplicial subcone of
\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}
of maximal dimension, and let

\mathbf{b}=(L_{1},\dots,L_{\rho})

be a basis of
\Pic(X)_{\mathbf{R}}
such that

\Lambda^{\vee}=\mathrm{cone}(L_{1},\dots,L_{\rho}).

The goal of this section is to show that the arithmetic functions
f_{\downarrow}
and
f_{\uparrow}
from
\mathbf{N}^{\rho}
to
\mathbf{R}_{+}
, defined by:

(2.1)

\displaystyle f_{\downarrow}(y)

\displaystyle=\sharp\Bigl\{P\in T(\mathbf{Q})\;\Big|\;h(P)\in\Lambda\ \text{and}\ \lfloor H_{L_{i}}(P)\rfloor=y_{i}\ \forall i\in\{1,\ldots,\rho\}\Bigr\},

\displaystyle f_{\uparrow}(y)

\displaystyle=\sharp\Bigl\{P\in T(\mathbf{Q})\;\Big|\;h(P)\in\Lambda\ \text{and}\ \lceil H_{L_{i}}(P)\rceil=y_{i}\ \forall i\in\{1,\ldots,\rho\}\Bigr\}.

verify the first hypothesis of Theorem
1.3
in order to apply the hyperbola method to estimate

\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\mid h(P)(\omega_{X}^{-1})\leqslant\log(B)}.

2.1.
Estimation of the multi-height behaviour with values in a simplicial subcone

For
B=(B_{1},\dots,B_{\rho})\in(\mathbf{R}_{\geqslant 1})^{\rho}
, we denote
u_{B}\in\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}
the vector such that
u_{B}(L_{i})=\log(B_{i})
for any
i
. We want to estimate the cardinality of

T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\cap(-\Lambda)_{B}}=\left\{P\in T(\mathbf{Q})\mid\forall\ i,1\leqslant H_{L_{i}}(P)\leqslant B_{i}\right\}

where

(-\Lambda)_{B}=-\Lambda+u_{B}.

To this end, we begin by decomposing

-\Lambda=\{a\in\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}\mid a(L_{i})\leqslant 0\}

into a union of compact polyhedra
\{\mathrm{D}_{n}\}_{n\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\rho}}
where

\mathrm{D}_{n}=\{a\in\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}\mid-n_{i}\beta_{i}\leqslant a(L_{i})\leqslant-(n_{i}-1)\beta_{i}\}

with
\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\rho})_{\mathbf{b}}\in\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
which will be suitably chosen. For
B=(B_{1},..,B_{\rho})\in\mathbf{R}_{\geqslant 1}^{\rho}
, we define

\mathrm{D}_{n,B}=\mathrm{D}_{n}+u_{B}.

We will deduce the estimate of
\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\cap(-\Lambda)_{B}}
from the known estimates of
\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{D}_{n,B}}
. Note that since

\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\cap(-\Lambda)_{\lfloor B\rfloor}}\leqslant\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\cap(-\Lambda)_{B}}\leqslant\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\cap(-\Lambda)_{\lceil B\rceil}},

where
\lfloor B\rfloor=(\lfloor B_{1}\rfloor,..,\lfloor B_{\rho}\rfloor)
and
\lceil B\rceil=(\lceil B_{1}\rceil,..,\lceil B_{\rho}\rceil)
, it is sufficient to consider the case where
B\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\rho}
, in order to get the estimate for
B\in(\mathbf{R}^{*})^{\rho}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists
\beta\in\Lambda^{\circ}
, in the interior of
\Lambda
, such that for all
n\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\rho}
, for all
B\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\rho}
, and for all
P\in T(\mathbf{Q})
,

h(P)\neq-n\cdot\beta+u_{B}.

Proof.

Since
T
satisfies Northcott’s property for the anticanonical height, the set
T(\mathbf{Q})
can be written as a countable union of finite sets

T(\mathbf{Q})_{h(P)(\omega_{X}^{-1})\leqslant N},

where
N\in\mathbf{N}^{*}
. Hence,
h(T(\mathbf{Q}))
is countable, and it is possible to choose
\beta\in\Lambda^{\circ}
such that

h(T(\mathbf{Q}))\cap\{-n\cdot\beta+u_{B}\mid n\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\rho},B\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\rho}\}=\varnothing.

∎

We choose such
\beta\in\Lambda^{\circ}
and then we have:

\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\cap(-\Lambda)_{B}}=\sum\limits_{n\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\rho}}\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\cap\mathrm{D}_{n,B}}

for any
B\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\rho}
.

2.1.1.
First estimate

Let
a_{1},..,a_{r}\in\mathbf{R}
and
b_{1},..,b_{r}\in\mathbf{R}
such that
a_{i}<b_{i}
for any
i
. We write
\mathrm{D}(a,b)
for the compact subset of
\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
with

\mathrm{D}(a,b)=\{\varphi\in\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}\mid a_{i}\leqslant\varphi(L_{i})\leqslant b_{i}\ \forall i\}

and we set

\mathrm{D}(a,b)_{B}=\mathrm{D}(a,b)+u_{B}.

We also need to introduce the following notation:

\mathcal{D}(a,b)_{B}:=\{\,y\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{R})\mid h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}(y)\in\mathrm{D}(a,b)_{B}\,\}.

The same method as in
[
4
, Corollary 4.8 and Theorem 4.16]
and in
[
5
, Corollary 7.8 and Theorem 7.16]
, which relies on Davenport’s result on the geometry of numbers (see
[
9
]
and
[
16
, Proposition 24]
) yields the following result:

Theorem 2.2
.

There exists
\Delta>0
such that

\sharp\left(T(\mathbf{Q})\right)_{h\in\mathrm{D}(a,b)_{B}}=\nu(\mathrm{D}(a,b)).\tau(X).\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}\left(1+O\left(\min\limits_{1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho}(B_{i})^{-\Delta}\right)\right)

where the implied constant depends only on
\sharp\Sigma(1)
, on the line bundles
L_{i}
and on the diameter of
\mathcal{D}(a,b)_{1}
.

Proof.

Denote by
e^{u_{B}}\in T_{\mathrm{NS}}(\mathbf{R})
the image of
u_{B}
under the exponential map

\operatorname{Pic}(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}\to T_{\mathrm{NS}}(\mathbf{R})
.
To prove the theorem it is enough to observe that

e^{\langle[D_{\lambda}],u_{B}\rangle}=\prod_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\langle[D_{\lambda}],L_{i}^{*}\rangle}.

The dependence of the error term is a consequence of Davenport’s geometry of numbers result
(see
[
4
, Proposition 4.1]
).
Hence it is enough to replace

B^{\min\limits_{\lambda\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\lambda}],u\rangle}

in
[
4
]
(respectively in
[
5
]
)
by

e^{\min\limits_{\lambda\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\lambda}],u_{B}\rangle}.

With this substitution one can adapt the lattice-point counting estimates of the proof of

[
4
, Theorem 4.6]

(respectively
[
5
, Theorem 7.6]
) and the Möbius inversion arguments
(see
[
4
, Theorem 4.14]
and
[
5
, Theorem 7.13]
)
to obtain the desired asymptotic.

Finally, set

a=\min_{\lambda\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\lambda}],\sum_{i=1}^{\rho}L_{i}^{*}\rangle>0.

Then we have the inequality

e^{\min\limits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle[D_{\rho}],u_{B}\rangle}\geqslant\bigg(\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho}B_{i}\bigg)^{a},

which yields the claimed result, if we take
\Delta=\frac{1}{4}\cdot a
.

∎

Now, we observe that for sufficiently large
n
(whose size can be controlled in terms of
\min(B_{i})
), the set
T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\cap\mathrm{D}_{n,B}}
is empty.

Lemma 2.3
.

If there exists
i\in\{1,..,\rho\}
, such that

n_{i}>\frac{1}{\beta_{i}}\log(B_{i})+1

then for all
B\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\rho}
, we have:

T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\cap\mathrm{D}_{n,B}}=\varnothing.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction and assume that there exists
P\in T(\mathbf{Q})
such that
h(P)\in\Lambda\cap\mathrm{D}_{n,B}
. Then, for every
i\in\{1,\dots,\rho\}
, we have

\log(B_{i})-(n_{i}-1)\beta_{i}\geqslant h(P)(L_{i})\geqslant 0.

Thus, under our assumption that there exists
i\in\{1,\dots,\rho\}
such that

n_{i}>\frac{1}{\beta_{i}}\log(B_{i})+1,

we get a contradiction.
∎

Thus, we first estimate

\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{\frac{1}{\beta_{1}}\log(B_{1})+1}\cdots\sum_{n_{\rho}=1}^{\frac{1}{\beta_{\rho}}\log(B_{\rho})+1}\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{\,h\in\mathrm{D}_{n,B}}.

Before proving the main result of this section, we need a bound on the remainder term of the series
\sum\nu(\mathrm{D}_{n})
:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
B\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\rho}
. There exist constants
c>0
and
d>0
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{\rho}\geq 1\\
\exists i\in\{1,\dots,\rho\}:\,n_{i}\geq\frac{1}{\beta_{i}}\log(B_{i})+1\end{subarray}}\nu(\mathrm{D}_{n})\leq\frac{c}{\min(B_{i})^{d}}.

Proof.

We use the identity

\nu(\mathrm{D}_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{\rho}\exp\!\left(-n_{i}\,\beta_{i}\,\omega_{X}^{-1}(L_{i}^{*})\right)\,\nu(\mathrm{D}_{1}).

Since
\beta_{i}>0
and
\omega_{X}^{-1}(L_{i}^{*})>0
for all
i
, because

\beta\in\Lambda^{\circ}
and

\omega_{X}^{-1}\in\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\circ}\subset(\Lambda^{\vee})^{\circ},

the associated series are convergent geometric series.

Estimating the remainder, we obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{\rho}\geq 1\\
\exists i\in\{1,\dots,\rho\}:\,n_{i}\geq\frac{1}{\beta_{i}}\log(B_{i})+1\end{subarray}}\nu(\mathrm{D}_{n})\ll\sum_{i=1}^{\rho}\exp\!\left(-\beta_{i}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{\beta_{i}}\log(B_{i})+1\Bigr)\omega_{X}^{-1}(L_{i}^{*})\right).

Hence,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{\rho}\geq 1\\
\exists i\in\{1,\dots,\rho\}:\,n_{i}\geq\frac{1}{\beta_{i}}\log(B_{i})+1\end{subarray}}\nu(\mathrm{D}_{n})\ll\sum_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{-\omega_{X}^{-1}(L_{i}^{*})}.

The result follows by setting

d:=\min_{1\leq i\leq\rho}\omega_{X}^{-1}(L_{i}^{*})>0

and absorbing constants into
c
.
∎

We can now state and prove the following proposition:

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
B=(B_{1},..,B_{\rho})\in(\mathbf{R}_{\geqslant 1})^{\rho}
. Then there exist constants
K>0
and
\delta>0
such that

\left|\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{\frac{1}{\beta_{1}}\log(B_{1})+1}\cdots\sum_{n_{\rho}=1}^{\frac{1}{\beta_{\rho}}\log(B_{\rho})+1}\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{\,h\in\mathrm{D}_{n,B}}.-\nu(-\Lambda)\,\tau(X)\,\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}\right|\leqslant K\cdot\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}}{\min(B_{i})^{\delta}}.

Proof.

Let us write

\mathcal{D}_{n,B}:=\{y\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{R})\mid h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}(y)\in\mathrm{D}_{n,B}\}.

We have that
\mathcal{D}_{n,B}=e^{n\cdot\beta}.\mathcal{D}_{1,B}
. Hence, by theorem
2.2
, we have that there exists
K>0
and
\Delta>0
such that:

\left|\sharp(T(\mathbf{Q}))_{h\in\mathrm{D}_{n,B}}-\nu(\mathrm{D}_{n})\,\tau(X)\,\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}\right|\leqslant K\cdot\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}}{\min(B_{i})^{\Delta}}.

We have:

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{\frac{1}{\beta_{1}}\log(B_{1})+1}\cdots\sum_{n_{\rho}=1}^{\frac{1}{\beta_{\rho}}\log(B_{\rho})+1}\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{D}_{n,B}}-\nu(-\Lambda)\,\tau(X)\,\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{\frac{1}{\beta_{1}}\log(B_{1})+1}\cdots\sum_{n_{\rho}=1}^{\frac{1}{\beta_{\rho}}\log(B_{\rho})+1}\left|\sharp(T(\mathbf{Q}))_{h\in\mathrm{D}_{n,B}}-\nu(\mathrm{D}_{n})\,\tau(X)\,\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle+\,\tau(X)\,\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{\rho}\geq 1\\
\exists i\in\{1,\dots,\rho\}:\,n_{i}\geq\frac{1}{\beta_{i}}\log(B_{i})+1\end{subarray}}\nu(\mathrm{D}_{n})

Hence applying theorem
2.2
and lemma
2.4
we can deduce the theorem.
∎

2.1.2.
An upper bound

Recall that we want to estimate the cardinality of

T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\cap(-\Lambda)_{B}}=\left\{P\in T(\mathbf{Q})\mid\forall i,\;1\leqslant H_{L_{i}}(P)\leqslant B_{i}\right\}.

We aim to deduce this estimate from the one obtained in Proposition
2.5
. To this end, we give an upper bound, for a fixed
k\in\{1,\ldots,\rho\}
, for the cardinality of the set

\mathcal{C}_{k,n,B}=\{P\in T(\mathbf{Q})\mid h(P)\in\mathrm{D}_{n,B}\text{ and }h(P)(L_{k})\leqslant 0\}.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
k\in\{1,\ldots,\rho\}
. Let
n\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\rho}
with
n_{i}\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta_{i}}\log(B_{i})+1
for all
i
. Then there exist
\Delta>0
and a constant
C>0
, depending only on a fixed compact set (which depends only on
\beta
), such that

\sharp\mathcal{C}_{k,n,B}\leqslant C\cdot\prod_{i\neq k}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}\cdot e^{-\sum\limits_{i\neq k}n_{i}\cdot\beta(L_{i})\cdot\omega_{X}^{-1}(L_{i}^{*})}.

Proof.

By lifting to the universal torsor (respectively, to the extended universal torsor), and using
[
4
, Theorem 3.16]
for toric varieties and
[
5
, Theorem 6.23]
for toric stacks, we obtain that
\sharp\mathcal{C}_{k,n,B}
is bounded above by the cardinality of the set of

y\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Z})\cap T_{\Sigma(1)}(\mathbf{R})

satisfying, for all
i\neq k
,

-n\beta_{i}+\log(B_{i})\leqslant h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}(y)(L_{i})\leqslant-(n-1)\beta_{i}+\log(B_{i}),

and

-\beta_{k}\leqslant h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}(y)(L_{k})\leqslant 0,

because
n_{k}\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}\log(B_{k})+1
. Recall that we write
T_{\Sigma(1)}=\mathbf{G}_{m}^{\Sigma(1)}\subset\mathcal{T}
. Let
\mathrm{D}
denote the compact subset of
\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
defined by the inequalities

0\leqslant a(L_{i})\leqslant\beta_{i}\quad\text{for all }i\neq k,\qquad-\beta_{k}\leqslant a(L_{k})\leqslant 0.

For
B=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{\rho})\in(\mathbf{R}_{\geqslant 1})^{\rho}
, we define a vector

v_{B}^{k}\in\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
by

v_{B}^{k}(L_{i})=-n\cdot\beta(L_{i})+\log(B_{i})\quad\text{for all }i\neq k,\qquad v_{B}^{k}(L_{k})=0.

We set
\mathrm{D}_{B}=\mathrm{D}+v_{B}^{k}
.
We now seek to bound

\sharp\left(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Z})\cap T_{\Sigma(1)}(\mathbf{R})\right)_{h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}.

First, observe that
h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}^{-1}(\mathrm{D})
is compact by

[
4
, Lemma 4.3]
and

[
5
, Theorem 6.29]
. Hence there exists
N>0
such that

h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}^{-1}(\mathrm{D})\subset\prod_{\lambda\in\Sigma(1)}[-N,N].

Since

\left(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Z})\cap T_{\Sigma(1)}(\mathbf{R})\right)_{h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}\in\mathrm{D}_{B}}\subset h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}^{-1}(\mathrm{D}_{B})\cap T_{\Sigma(1)}(\mathbf{R})=e^{-v_{B}^{k}}\cdot\left(h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}^{-1}(\mathrm{D})\cap T_{\Sigma(1)}(\mathbf{R})\right),

we obtain

h_{\mathcal{T},\infty}^{-1}(\mathrm{D}_{B})\cap T_{\Sigma(1)}(\mathbf{R})\subset\prod_{\lambda\in\Sigma(1)}\left(\left[-N\cdot e^{\langle[D_{\lambda}],v_{B}^{k}\rangle},\,N\cdot e^{\langle[D_{\lambda}],v_{B}^{k}\rangle}\right]\setminus\{0\}\right).

Using the relation

\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma(1)}[D_{\lambda}]=\omega_{X}^{-1},

we deduce the inequality

\sharp\mathcal{C}_{k,n,B}\ll e^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},v_{B}^{k}\rangle}.

Finally, observing that

e^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},v_{B}^{k}\rangle}=\prod_{i\neq k}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}\cdot e^{-\sum\limits_{i\neq k}n_{i}\cdot\beta(L_{i})\cdot\omega_{X}^{-1}(L_{i}^{*})},

we obtain the announced bound.
∎

We can now give an upper bound for the difference
\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{\sharp\mathrm{D}_{n,B}}-\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\cap\mathrm{D}_{n,B}}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
B\in\mathbf{R}_{\geqslant 1}^{\rho}
.
There exist constants
C>0
and
\Delta>0
such that

\left|\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{\frac{1}{\beta_{1}}\log(B_{1})+1}\cdots\sum_{n_{\rho}=1}^{\frac{1}{\beta_{\rho}}\log(B_{\rho})+1}\left(\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{D}_{n,B}}-\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\cap\mathrm{D}_{n,B}}\right)\right|\leqslant C\cdot\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}}{\min\limits_{1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho}(B_{i})^{\Delta}}.

Proof.

Since we have the inclusion

T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{D}_{n,B}}\setminus T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\cap\mathrm{D}_{n,B}}\subset\bigcup_{1\leqslant k\leqslant\rho}\mathcal{C}_{k,n,B},

the result follows from Proposition
2.6
.
∎

We deduce the following result on the asymptotic behaviour of the number of rational points whose multi-height lies in a simplicial subcone of
\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}
and is bounded.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
B=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{\rho})\in(\mathbf{R}_{\geqslant 1})^{\rho}
.
Then there exist constants
C>0
and

such that

\left|\sharp(T(\mathbf{Q}))_{h\in\Lambda\cap(-\Lambda)_{B}}-\nu(-\Lambda)\,\tau(X)\,\prod_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}\right|\leqslant C\cdot\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}}{\min(B_{i})^{\delta}}.

Since by our choice of

, we have the equality for

:

2.2.
Multi-height estimates for the application of the hyperbola method

Recall that we want to study the arithmetic functions
f_{\downarrow}
and
f_{\uparrow}
from
\mathbf{N}^{\rho}
to
\mathbf{R}_{+}
defined by:

•

f_{\uparrow}(y)=\sharp\{P\in T(\mathbf{Q})\mid h(P)\in\Lambda\text{ and }\lfloor H_{L_{i}}(P)\rfloor=y_{i}\ \forall i\in\{1,..,\rho\}\}

•

f_{\downarrow}(y)=\sharp\{P\in T(\mathbf{Q})\mid h(P)\in\Lambda\text{ and }\lceil H_{L_{i}}(P)\rceil=y_{i}\ \forall i\in\{1,..,\rho\}\}.

We want to show that they satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
1.3
in order to apply the hyperbola method, that is, we want to estimate:

•

\sum\limits_{1\leqslant y_{i}\leqslant B_{i},\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho}f_{\uparrow}(y)=\sharp\left\{P\in T(\mathbf{Q})\mid\forall i,\;1\leqslant\lceil H_{L_{i}}(P)\rceil\leqslant B_{i}\right\};

•

\sum\limits_{1\leqslant y_{i}\leqslant B_{i},\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho}f_{\downarrow}(y)=\sharp\left\{P\in T(\mathbf{Q})\mid\forall i,\;1\leqslant\lfloor H_{L_{i}}(P)\rfloor\leqslant B_{i}\right\}.

We have the following proposition:

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
B_{1},\dots,B_{\rho}\in\mathbf{R}_{\geqslant 1}
, with sufficiently large
\min\limits_{1\leqslant i\leqslant{\rho}}(B_{i})
, we have the estimates:

•

\sum\limits_{1\leqslant y_{i}\leqslant B_{i},\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho}f_{\uparrow}(y)=\nu(-\Lambda)\cdot\tau(X)\cdot\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}+O\!\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}\cdot\min\limits_{1\leqslant i\leqslant{\rho}}(B_{i})^{-\Delta}\right);

•

\sum\limits_{1\leqslant y_{i}\leqslant B_{i},\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho}f_{\downarrow}(y)=\nu(-\Lambda)\cdot\tau(X)\cdot\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}+O\!\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}B_{i}^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},L_{i}^{*}\rangle}\cdot\min\limits_{1\leqslant i\leqslant{\rho}}(B_{i})^{-\Delta}\right)
.

Proof.

First, using the properties of the ceiling function, we obtain for

B\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{\rho}
the equality

\sum_{1\leqslant y_{i}\leqslant B_{i},\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho}f_{\uparrow}(y)=\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\cap(-\Lambda)_{B}}.

In this case, the desired estimate follows directly from
Theorem
2.8
.
We conclude by observing that we have the bounds

\sum_{1\leqslant y_{i}\leqslant\lfloor B_{i}\rfloor,\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho}f_{\uparrow}(y)\leqslant\sum_{1\leqslant y_{i}\leqslant B_{i},\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho}f_{\uparrow}(y)\leqslant\sum_{1\leqslant y_{i}\leqslant\lceil B_{i}\rceil,\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho}f_{\uparrow}(y).

Similarly, in order to estimate

\sum_{1\leqslant y_{i}\leqslant B_{i},\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho}f_{\downarrow}(y),

it suffices to treat the case where
B_{1},\dots,B_{\rho}\in\mathbf{N}^{*}
.
Moreover, we have the inequalities

\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\cap(-\Lambda)_{B}}\leqslant\sum_{1\leqslant y_{i}\leqslant B_{i},\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho}f_{\downarrow}(y)\leqslant\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\cap(-\Lambda)_{B+\underline{1}}},

where
\underline{1}
denotes here the element of
\mathbf{R}^{\rho}
whose coordinates in the
canonical basis are all equal to
1
.
The estimate is therefore again a consequence of
Theorem
2.8
.
∎

3.
The hyperbola method

Recall that we have chosen
\Lambda
to be a simplicial subcone of
\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}
of maximal dimension, and that
\mathbf{b}=(L_{1},\dots,L_{\rho})
is a basis of
\Pic(X)_{\mathbf{R}}
such that

\Lambda^{\vee}=\mathrm{cone}(L_{1},\dots,L_{\rho}).

We have seen in the previous section (see Proposition
2.9
) that the arithmetic function
f:\mathbf{N}^{\rho}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}_{+}
where

or

(see equation
2.1
) satisfies the first hypothesis of theorem
1.3
. That is, there exist strictly positive real numbers
C_{f,M}\leqslant C_{f,E}
,
\Delta>0
, and
\omega_{i}>0
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho

for

In our case, for any
1\leqslant i\leqslant\rho

since
\Lambda\subset\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}

.
Moreover, we have

We aim to study asymptotically the cardinality of the set

To apply the hyperbola method, we first restrict our attention to the case where

\Lambda
is a simplicial subcone of
\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}

We then have the inequalities:

(3.1)

\sum\limits_{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}y_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\leqslant B}f_{\downarrow}(y)\leqslant\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\Lambda\mid h(P)(\omega_{X}^{-1})\leqslant\log(B)}\leqslant\sum\limits_{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}y_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\leqslant B}f_{\uparrow}(y)

where

for every

. Now in order to apply the hyperbola method from

]

). With our choice of

is simply defined by the conditions:

(2)

for any

.

Proposition 3.1
.

The polyhedron
\mathcal{P}
is bounded and is not contained in a hyperplane. The face
F
of
\mathcal{P}
on which the function

The fact that

is naturally of dimension

By Proposition

[

4.
Conclusion

For
B\geqslant 1
, let us write again
\mathrm{C}_{B}=\{a\in\Pic(X)_{\mathbf{R}}^{\vee}\mid a(\omega_{X}^{-1})\leqslant\log(B)\}
. To deduce from the previous section the result on the asymptotic behaviour of

\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{C}_{B}}
, we shall assume the following hypothesis:

Hypothesis 4.1
.

For every
P\in T(\mathbf{Q})
, we have
h(P)\in\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}
.

Remark 4.2
.

This hypothesis is verified by split, smooth and proper toric varieties (see Proposition
4.5
) and by the weighted projective stacks (see Proposition
4.6
). The author expect this to be true for any split toric stacks.

Using this hypothesis, it is enough to decompose the dual of the effective cone

\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}
into a finite union of simplicial subcones
\{\Lambda_{j}\}_{j\in J}
such that

\Lambda_{j}\cap\Lambda_{k}
is either empty or contained in a hyperplane. Using the inclusion–exclusion
principle, we obtain the formula

\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{C}_{B}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{1\leqslant j_{1}<\cdots<j_{k}\leqslant n}\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{C}_{B}\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j_{i}}}.

We will therefore start by highlighting a property of the heights associated with effective line bundles,
focusing on the toric case. Then, we will conclude by an upper bound of the cardinality of

T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{C}_{B}\cap\Lambda_{j}\cap\Lambda_{j^{\prime}}}
for
j\neq j^{\prime}
.

4.1.
A key property of heights associated with effective line bundles

To extend the result to the case where the multi-height is bounded above but not below, we must restrict to an open subset of
X
on which the logarithmic height associated with a given effective line bundle is bounded from below. Peyre has shown the following general property (see
[
19
, Lemma 4.5]
):

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
V
be a quasi-Fano variety (see
[
4
, definition 2.10]
), e.g. a smooth and proper toric variety. Then there exists a dense open subset
U\subset V
and
c\in\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
such that for any
P\in U(\mathbf{Q})
,

h(P)\in c+\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}.

Question 4.4
.

Can one prove a similar result for nice DM stacks ?

For the case of smooth, proper and split toric varieties, we shall now explain how the previous lemma is verified if we take
U=T
the dense open torus of
X
.

Proposition 4.5
.

We endow the smooth, proper and split toric variety
X
with the natural system of heights (see
[
21
, Definition 9.2]
), if
h:X(\mathbf{Q})\rightarrow\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
is the associated multi-height map, we have for any
P\in T(\mathbf{Q})
,

h(P)\in\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}.

Proof.

This is a consequence of
[
21
, Proposition 10.24]
.
∎

We now extend this result to the weighted projective stack
\mathcal{P}(\omega)
for a weight
\omega\in(\mathbf{N}^{*})^{n+1}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
h:\mathcal{P}(\omega)(\mathbf{Q})\rightarrow\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
be the associated multi-height map. Then for any
P\in T(\mathbf{Q})
we have

h(P)\in\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}.

Proof.

We use the notation of
[
5
, Appendix B.1]
, to which we refer the reader for further details. For

t\in\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq n}\frac{1}{\omega_{i}}\mathbf{Z}\cap[0,1[,

we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{t}
the unique sector such that

\age_{\num}(\mathcal{S}_{t},\mathcal{O}(1))=t.

For
i\in\{0,\dots,n\}
, we denote by
[D_{i}]_{0}
the element of the orbifold Picard group of
\mathcal{P}(\omega)
corresponding to

(\mathcal{O}(\omega_{i}),\varphi_{i}),

where for any

t\in\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq n}\frac{1}{\omega_{i}}\mathbf{Z}\cap]0,1[,

we have

\varphi_{i}(\mathcal{S}_{t})=-\{-\omega_{i}t\}.

The orbifold effective cone of
\mathcal{P}(\omega)
is then given by the cone generated by

\{[D_{i}]_{0}\mid 0\leq i\leq n\}\cup\{[\mathcal{S}_{t}]\mid t\in\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq n}\tfrac{1}{\omega_{i}}\mathbf{Z}\cap]0,1[\}

by
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Let
P\in T(\mathbf{Q})
and let

be a lift of
P
(keeping the notation of that article). Using
[
5
, Theorem B.6]
and the discussion following it, we have

h(P)([\mathcal{S}_{t}])=\log(|k_{t}|)\geq 0

for all
t\in\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq n}\frac{1}{\omega_{i}}\mathbf{Z}\cap]0,1[
.

Moreover,

\displaystyle[D_{i}]_{0}

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}[\mathcal{O}(1)]_{\stack}-\sum_{t}\bigl(\omega_{i}t+\{-\omega_{i}t\}\bigr)[\mathcal{S}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}[\mathcal{O}(1)]_{\stack}-\sum_{t}\lceil\omega_{i}t\rceil[\mathcal{S}_{t}]\,.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle h(P)([D_{i}]_{0})

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}\log\left(\max\limits_{0\leqslant j\leqslant n}\left|\left(\prod\limits_{t\in\bigcup\limits_{0\leqslant i\leqslant n}\frac{1}{w_{i}}\mathbf{Z}\cap]0,1[}k_{t}^{m_{t}(j)}\right)y^{\prime}_{j}\right|^{\frac{1}{w_{j}}}\right)-\sum_{t}\lceil\omega_{i}t\rceil\log(|k_{t}|)

\displaystyle\geqslant\log|y^{\prime}_{i}|

where the last inequality is a consequence of the equality

m_{t}(i)=\lceil\omega_{i}t\rceil

for all
t\in\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq n}\frac{1}{\omega_{i}}\mathbf{Z}\cap]0,1[
.

∎

4.2.
The decomposition in simplicial subcones and a crucial upper bound

In this paragraph, we explain how to construct a decomposition of the dual cone of the effective cone,
\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}
, into simplicial subcones. Recall that
\omega_{X}^{-1}\in\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\circ}
, i.e. it lies in the interior of
\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)
and that
\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}
is finitely generated. We may consider the polytope
F
given by the intersection of
\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}
with the affine hyperplane
\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},-\rangle=1
. By construction,
F
is a compact convex polytope whose vertices come from the extremal directions of
\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}
.

We choose a suitable triangulation, i.e. a decomposition of
F
into affine simplices of dimension
\rho-1
. In particular, we may write

F=\bigcup_{j\in J}\Delta_{j}

where each
\Delta_{j}
is a
(\rho-1)
–simplex, the intersection
\Delta_{i}\cap\Delta_{j}
is either empty or a simplex of dimension
(\rho-2)
, and
J
is a finite index set. Such a decomposition exists by
[
10
, Chapter 2]
.

For each
j\in J
, we consider the cone
\Lambda_{j}
generated by
\Delta_{j}
.
This is a simplicial cone of maximal dimension.
We then have

(4.1)

\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}=\bigcup_{j\in J}\Lambda_{j}

and for
i\neq j
, the intersection
\Lambda_{i}\cap\Lambda_{j}
is either empty or a simplicial cone of dimension
\rho-1
.

For the remainder of the paper, we fix such a decomposition of
\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}
into simplicial subcones. Now, we aim to show that one may neglect rational points of bounded anti-canonical height whose multi-height lies in the intersection of two distinct simplicial subcones
\Lambda_{j}
and
\Lambda_{i}
with
j\neq i
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
i\neq j\in J
such that
\Lambda_{i}\cap\Lambda_{j}
is non empty, i.e. it is of dimension
\rho-1
. Let
v_{1},..,v_{\rho-1}
be a generating set of
\Lambda_{i}\cap\Lambda_{j}
and
h
an element not contained in the hyperplane generated by
\Lambda_{i}\cap\Lambda_{j}
. For
\delta>0
, we write
K_{\delta}
for the simplicial cone generated by
\{v_{1},..,v_{\rho-1},\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}v_{i}+\delta h\}
. We suppose that
\delta
is sufficiently small such that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\rho}v_{i}+\delta h\in\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}
.

Then we have the following upper bound:

\#\,T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{C}_{B}\cap\Lambda_{i}\cap\Lambda_{j}}\leqslant\left(\frac{\tau(X)}{(\rho-1)!}+1\right)\cdot\nu(-K_{\delta})\cdot B\log(B)^{\rho-1}\quad\text{as }B\to\infty.

Proof.

With our assumptions on the fixed element
\delta>0
, we have
K_{\delta}\subset\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}
.
Hence, by applying Theorem
3.5
, we obtain the claimed upper bound for
B
sufficiently large.
∎

We can now conclude that the cardinality

\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{C}_{B}\cap\Lambda_{i}\cap\Lambda_{j}}

is negligible.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
i\neq j\in J
be such that
\Lambda_{i}\cap\Lambda_{j}
is nonempty. Then

\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{C}_{B}\cap\Lambda_{i}\cap\Lambda_{j}}=o\!\left(B\log(B)^{\rho-1}\right)\quad\text{as }B\to\infty.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. We use the notation of
Proposition
4.7
and assume that
\delta
is sufficiently small so that

\sum_{i=1}^{\rho}v_{i}+\delta h\in\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}\quad\text{and}\quad\nu(-K_{\delta})\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{\frac{\tau(X)}{(\rho-1)!}+1}.

Then, for
B
sufficiently large, we obtain

\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{C}_{B}\cap\Lambda_{i}\cap\Lambda_{j}}\leqslant\varepsilon\,B\log(B)^{\rho-1},

which yields the desired result.
∎

4.3.
The computation of the precise constant

Let us recall here the definition of the constant
\alpha(X)
(see
[
17
, Definition 2.1]
and
[
18
, Notations 2.6.1]
).

Definition 4.9
.

The constant
\alpha(X)
is defined by

\alpha(X)=\frac{1}{(\rho-1)!}\cdot\int_{\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}}e^{-\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},y\rangle}\,dy,

where the Haar measure
dy
on
\Pic(X)^{\vee}_{\mathbf{R}}
is normalized so that the covolume of the dual lattice of the Picard group is one.

We can now state the following computation:

Proposition 4.10
.

We have the following equality:

\alpha(X)=\sum\limits_{j\in J}\frac{1}{(\rho-1)!}\cdot\nu(-\Lambda_{j})

where
\{\Lambda_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is the decomposition in simplicial subcones we have chosen in the previous paragraph.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the decomposition of equation
4.1
and of definition
4.9
.
∎

We can now state again and prove the theorem
1.4
:

Theorem 4.11
.

We have the following asymptotic behaviour:

\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h(P)(\omega_{X}^{-1})\leqslant\log(B)}\underset{B\rightarrow+\infty}{\sim}\alpha(X)\tau(X)B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}\log(B)^{\rho(X)-1}

Proof.

Recall that for
B\geqslant 1
, we write

\mathrm{C}_{B}=\{a\in\Pic(X)_{\mathbf{R}}^{\vee}\mid a(\omega_{X}^{-1})\leqslant\log(B)\}.

Since by Hypothesis
4.1
, we have for any
P\in T(\mathbf{Q})
,
h(P)\in\text{C}_{\text{eff}}(X)^{\vee}
, we can write using our chosen decomposition in simplicial subcones and the inclusion-exclusion principle the equality:

\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{C}_{B}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{1\leqslant j_{1}<\cdots<j_{k}\leqslant n}\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{C}_{B}\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j_{i}}}.

Now we have using proposition
4.10
:

\displaystyle\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h(P)(\omega_{X}^{-1})\leqslant\log(B)}-\alpha(X)\tau(X)B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}\log(B)^{\rho(X)-1}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j\in J}\left(\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h\in\mathrm{C}_{B}\cap\Lambda_{j}}-\frac{1}{(\rho-1)!}\cdot\nu(-\Lambda_{j})\cdot\tau(X)B^{\langle\omega_{X}^{-1},u\rangle}\log(B)^{\rho(X)-1}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k>1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{1\leqslant j_{1}<\cdots<j_{k}\leqslant n}\sharp T(\mathbf{Q})_{h(P)\in\mathrm{C}_{B}\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j_{i}}}

We conclude using Theorem
3.5
and Proposition
4.8
.
∎
