Prognostic stratification in congenital abnormalities of the kidney and urinary tract: beyond the antenatal ultrasound
Authors: Jiwon Jung
Published: 2026

Congenital abnormalities of the kidney and urinary tract (CAKUT) encompass a heterogeneous spectrum of structural disorders affecting the kidneys and collecting systems. These conditions occur in approximately 0.4 to 4 per 1,000 pregnancies and represent the leading cause of chronic kidney disease (CKD) in children in developed countries, accounting for 35% to 60% of cases and approximately 30% of end-stage kidney failure [1,2]. Advances in antenatal ultrasonography have enabled detection as early as 18 to 20 weeks of gestation; however, the relationship between prenatal findings and postnatal renal outcomes remains incompletely defined, underscoring the importance of early risk stratification. In a previous issue of Childhood Kidney Diseases, Fil et al. [3] report a carefully conducted retrospective cohort study of 55 infants with CAKUT, followed from the antenatal period through the first year of life, providing a valuable link between prenatal imaging findings and early postnatal outcomes.

The risk of CKD progression in children with CAKUT is well established, though its frequency and determinants vary considerably by subtype. In a large retrospective cohort of 822 prenatally detected CAKUT cases followed for a median of 43 months, CKD occurred in 6% of children overall. Notably, the risk increased more than fivefold in those with associated hydronephrosis compared to isolated lesions [4]. Matsell et al. [5] further demonstrated that reduced estimated glomerular filtration rate, and smaller kidney size with additional anomalies were independently associated with CKD development in a heterogeneous CAKUT population. Geleta et al. [6] showed that prematurity, low birth weight, and bilaterality of CAKUT were independent risk factors for developing advanced-stage CKD, underscoring the need for early and subtype-specific risk stratification. Collectively, these data indicate that CKD progression in children with CAKUT may result from either obstructive collecting system lesions or renal parenchymal malformations (RPMs) including dysplasia, suggesting that both structural categories carry independent risk for long-term renal deterioration.

The observation from this study that CKD was significantly more prevalent in the RPM group (95.8%) than in the collecting system anomaly (CSA) group (61.3%) requires careful contextual interpretation. Conditions such as multicystic dysplastic kidney, renal agenesis, and renal dysplasia inherently disrupt nephron development and are frequently associated with impaired corticomedullary differentiation as a direct manifestation of parenchymal dysfunction. Given that nephrogenesis is largely complete by 36 weeks of gestation, intrinsic abnormalities affecting this process inevitably reduce the functional renal reserve [1]. However, obstructive lesions of the collecting system are also capable of driving CKD progression, as the higher degree of obstruction and bilaterality of hydronephrosis can impair renal function over time [4]. Quantitative assessment of renal dysplasia severity is inherently challenging, and while hydronephrosis risk grade was stratified within the CSA group using the urinary tract dilation classification system [7], the presence and degree of obstruction as well as laterality were not systematically compared between groups. As a result, drawing definitive conclusions about the relative CKD risk of the RPM versus CSA group based on group membership alone remains problematic. Therefore, further subtype-specific analysis of structural severity would be necessary to draw more definitive conclusions.

A notable feature of this cohort is the high proportion of high-risk hydronephrosis, as defined by the urinary tract dilation classification system, observed both antenatally (66.6%) and postnatally (83.8%) in the CSA group. Considering the existing literature demonstrating that obstructive collecting system lesions carry a substantial risk of CKD progression [4,6], the relative stability of renal function observed throughout the follow-up period in this cohort is noteworthy. However, this finding may suggest either that the obstructive burden was insufficient to cause progressive nephron loss within the first year, or alternatively, that the absence of laterality-specific and obstruction-severity data obscures underlying functional heterogeneity within the CSA group. As previously noted, the study does not clearly distinguish between unilateral and bilateral obstruction, and differential renal function assessed by 99mTc-MAG3 (technetium-99m mercaptoacetyltriglycine) scintigraphy is only indirectly referenced in relation to acute kidney injury (AKI) prediction. As split renal function is a key determinant of both surgical decision-making and long-term prognosis in obstructive uropathy [8-10], a more comprehensive analysis incorporating laterality, the degree of obstruction, and MAG3-derived functional data would be essential to determine whether the apparent renal stability in high-risk cases reflects true preservation of parenchymal reserve or simply the limitations of the current analysis.

Posterior urethral valves (PUVs) accounted for 9.2% of the overall cohort and approximately 16% of postnatal CSA cases. Although PUVs are well recognized as a potent driver of CKD progression in childhood [11,12], the study did not provide further subgroup analysis for this diagnosis. Accordingly, the relative contribution of PUVs to the overall CKD burden observed within the CSA group cannot be directly quantified from the current data. Subtype-specific stratification—particularly distinguishing PUV-associated disease from isolated hydronephrosis—would more accurately characterize the prognostic landscape of the CSA group.

The study by Fil et al. [3] makes an important contribution by systematically tracking CAKUT subtypes from antenatal diagnosis through the first year of life, an approach that remains relatively uncommon in this population. The identification of loss of corticomedullary differentiation as an independent predictor of AKI provides a clinically applicable tool for early risk stratification. Additionally, the finding that favorable antenatal criteria regarding hydronephrosis were consistently associated with positive outcomes offers meaningful reassurance in prenatal counseling. It must be acknowledged, however, that CAKUT represents a fundamentally heterogeneous disease spectrum, and the binary classification into RPM and CSA groups does not fully capture the biological complexity of this condition. Structural overlap between the two categories is not uncommon; collecting system obstruction may coexist with varying degrees of renal parenchymal dysplasia, and the boundaries between groups are inherently imprecise. This taxonomic limitation constrains the interpretability of intergroup comparisons and represents an intrinsic challenge in study design that extends beyond this cohort. Notwithstanding these limitations, the study provides a valuable advance in understanding early CAKUT outcomes. Future prospective multicenter studies incorporating laterality-specific functional assessments, subtype-level CKD stratification, and longer-term follow-up will be essential to define the renal trajectory of these patients.
