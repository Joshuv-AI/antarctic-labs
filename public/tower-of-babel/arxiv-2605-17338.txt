At Most Two Infinite Blue Clusters in the CMR Representation of the Edwards–Anderson Spin Glass
Yan Ru Pei

Abstract
The two-replica Chayes–Machta–Redner (CMR) representation is one of the main proposed geometric signatures of spin-glass order in the short-range Edwards–Anderson model. Mean-field arguments and recent numerics suggest that the low-temperature phase should exhibit two macroscopic blue clusters carrying opposite overlap signs. We prove a rigorous structural constraint in this direction. For any translation-invariant joint Gibbs measure on disorder, two spin replicas, and CMR bond variables on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} , the blue subgraph contains at most two infinite connected components; if two exist, then they lie in a common infinite grey cluster and belong to opposite overlap-parity classes. The main obstacle is that the blue-bond process is neither insertion-tolerant nor positively associated, so the usual Burton–Keane and random-cluster arguments do not apply. We circumvent this by working in the full joint measure and using a finite-box merge operation together with the mass-transport bound on ends of translation-invariant subgraphs. As auxiliary input, we establish finite energy and a percolation transition for the grey subgraph via Bayesian resampling of couplings and a parity-based Peierls estimate. These results do not prove the existence of infinite blue clusters or a spin-glass phase transition, but they give a rigorous upper bound compatible with the two-cluster picture for short-range spin glasses.



1
Introduction and main result

1.1
Motivation

Spin glasses are disordered magnetic systems in which competing interactions can prevent the emergence of a single preferred ordered state.
A central problem is to understand whether the low-temperature phase has genuine long-range order, and if so how that order should be detected.
Unlike ferromagnets, where order is visible through magnetization and through Fortuin–Kasteleyn percolation, spin glasses have no fixed direction of magnetization.
Their standard order parameter is instead the overlap between two independently sampled equilibrium configurations.

This makes graphical representations especially useful: they turn questions about overlap into questions about random geometry.
The two-replica Chayes–Machta–Redner (CMR) representation
[
1
]
associates to a pair of spin configurations a bond process with blue bonds, red bonds, and closed bonds.
Blue clusters are the natural geometric objects associated with regions where the two replicas agree in their satisfaction of the disorder.
Machta, Newman, and Stein
[
2
]
argued that the low-temperature phase should be reflected in the emergence of two macroscopic blue clusters of opposite overlap sign.
Recent simulations in two and three dimensions by Münster and Weigel
[
3
,
4
]
support this picture.

For short-range Edwards–Anderson models, even the structural part of this picture is not automatic.
Frustration imposes nonlocal cycle constraints, and the blue-bond process is neither insertion-tolerant nor positively associated.
Thus, before one can identify the spin-glass order parameter with blue-cluster densities, one must first answer a more basic geometric question: how many infinite blue clusters can coexist?

This note proves that the answer is at most two.
More precisely, for any translation-invariant joint Gibbs measure consistent with the Edwards–Anderson Hamiltonian and the CMR Edwards–Sokal coupling, there is at most one infinite blue cluster in each overlap-parity class, and hence at most two infinite blue clusters in total.
If two exist, they lie in the same infinite grey cluster and belong to opposite path-parity classes.
The proof works in the full joint measure over disorder, spins, and bonds. As auxiliary input, we establish finite energy and a percolation transition for the grey subgraph (blue
\,\cup\,
red) by combining Bayesian resampling of the couplings with a parity-based Peierls argument.

Remark 1.6

(Overlap alignment from the Edwards–Sokal coupling)

The allowance rules in (

Bayesian resampling of couplings

In a ferromagnet (
J_{e}>0
for all
e
), the Gibbs measure at fixed
J
has full finite energy: every bond can be inserted or deleted with positive conditional probability.
For spin glasses, this fails at fixed

\mathbf{P}_{\beta}

The price is a

This section isolates the only Bayesian input needed later: after freezing the spins everywhere and the bonds outside a finite edge set, the conditional law of the couplings on that edge set still has strictly positive density with respect to the i.i.d. prior.
The result (Proposition
2.1
) is invoked in the grey finite-energy argument and in the box-resampling lemma.

(Bayesian resampling of couplings)

3
Grey-cluster analysis

3.1
Finite energy of the grey cluster

One might expect the grey-bond process to have finite energy even in the quenched setting, since the frustration constraint (C2) applies only to all-blue cycles.
However, a case analysis reveals that the Bayesian penalty is still needed in one case.

Proposition 3.1

(Finite energy of the grey cluster)
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{b}^{\prime}
denote the
\sigma
-algebra generated by all variables except
(\eta_{b},J_{b})
.
The joint measure
\mathbf{P}_{\beta}
satisfies: for every bond
b\in E
,

0<\mathbf{P}_{\beta}(\eta_{b}\in\{\textup{blue},\textup{red}\}\mid\mathcal{T}_{b}^{\prime})<1\quad\mathbf{P}_{\beta}\text{-a.s.}

When the endpoints have opposite overlap this holds even conditional on
J_{b}
(i.e., with
\mathcal{T}_{b}^{\prime}
replaced by
\mathcal{T}_{b}:=\sigma(\mathcal{T}_{b}^{\prime},J_{b})
).
When the endpoints have the same overlap, one sign of
J_{b}
can force the bond closed, which is why the quenched measure at fixed
J
fails to have finite energy.

Proof.

Let
b=\{x,y\}
.
The conditioning
\mathcal{T}_{b}^{\prime}
freezes the spins, so the conditional law of
\eta_{b}
is read directly from the local Edwards–Sokal table in Definition
1.3
after resampling
J_{b}
.

Case 1:
q_{x}=-q_{y}
.

Then
\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}=-\tau_{x}\tau_{y}
.
For either sign of
J_{b}
, exactly one replica satisfies
b
.
Conditional on
J_{b}
and the frozen spins, the only admissible non-closed state is red, and

\mathbf{P}_{\beta}(\eta_{b}=\textup{red}\mid\mathcal{T}_{b})=1-r_{b}\in(0,1),\qquad r_{b}:=e^{-2\beta|J_{b}|}.

Thus the grey-open probability lies strictly between
0
and
1
already at fixed
J_{b}
.

Case 2:
q_{x}=q_{y}
.

Then
\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}=\tau_{x}\tau_{y}
.
There is a favorable sign of
J_{b}
for which both replicas satisfy
b
, and on that sign
\eta_{b}
is blue with conditional probability
1-r_{b}^{2}>0
and closed with probability
r_{b}^{2}>0
.
For the opposite sign, neither replica satisfies
b
, so
\eta_{b}
is forced closed.
By Proposition
2.1
with
S=\{b\}
, both signs of

have positive conditional probability given
\mathcal{T}_{b}^{\prime}
.
The favorable sign gives

\mathbf{P}_{\beta}(\eta_{b}=\textup{blue}\mid\mathcal{T}_{b}^{\prime})>0,

while the unfavorable sign gives

\mathbf{P}_{\beta}(\eta_{b}=\textup{closed}\mid\mathcal{T}_{b}^{\prime})>0.

Therefore the grey-open probability again lies strictly between
0
and
1
.

Note, however, that the Bayesian penalty prevents the conditional blue-bond probability from being made arbitrarily close to
1
(even at large
\beta
), so a stochastic domination argument does not directly yield grey percolation; the Peierls argument of Proposition

is needed instead.

In both cases
0<\mathbf{P}_{\beta}(\eta_{b}\in\{\text{blue},\text{red}\}\mid\mathcal{T}_{b}^{\prime})<1
, establishing finite energy.
∎

(J,\sigma,\tau)
, the bond variables are independent across edges under the Edwards–Sokal coupling, and an edge is grey-open with probability at most

p_{e}=1-e^{-4\beta|J_{e}|}.

Let
(U_{e})_{e\in E}
be i.i.d. uniform random variables, independent of
(J,\sigma,\tau)
, and realize the grey-open indicator
G_{e}:=\mathbf{1}[\eta_{e}\in\{\text{blue},\text{red}\}]
by the standard monotone coupling.
Then

G_{e}\leq\Xi_{e}:=\mathbf{1}\bigl[|J_{e}|>M\text{ or }U_{e}\leq 1-e^{-4\beta M}\bigr]

for every edge
e
: if
|J_{e}|\leq M
, then
p_{e}\leq 1-e^{-4\beta M}
, so grey-open can occur only when
U_{e}\leq 1-e^{-4\beta M}
.
Since the couplings are i.i.d. by (D1), the variables
(\Xi_{e})_{e\in E}
are i.i.d. Bernoulli with parameter

p_{\mathrm{dom}}(M,\beta)=q(M)+(1-q(M))(1-e^{-4\beta M}).

Choose
M
large enough that
q(M)<p_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})/2
, then choose
\beta
small enough that
(1-q(M))(1-e^{-4\beta M})<p_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})/2
.
For this choice,
p_{\mathrm{dom}}(M,\beta)<p_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, so the dominating Bernoulli bond percolation is subcritical.
Therefore
G_{\mathrm{grey}}
contains no infinite connected component
\mathbf{P}_{\beta}
-a.s.
∎

3.2
Grey percolation transition

Corollaries
3.4
and
3.5
establish uniqueness and the subcritical phase directly from finite energy.
For the supercritical phase, we adapt the Peierls contour argument of De Santis and Gandolfi
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Lemma 3.6

(Contour partition lemma)
.

Fix a box
\Lambda
, a Peierls contour
\gamma\subset\Lambda
, and an exterior bond configuration
\eta_{\Lambda\setminus\gamma}
satisfying
(C1)
.
Contract each grey connected component of
\eta_{\Lambda\setminus\gamma}
that meets
\gamma
to a single vertex, and retain the bonds of
\gamma
as edges, obtaining a finite contour graph
H_{\gamma}
.
Then the contour bonds admit a partition
\gamma=A\sqcup B
such that opening exactly the bonds of
A
as red (with
B
closed) preserves
(C1)
, and likewise opening exactly the bonds of
B
as red (with
A
closed) preserves
(C1)
.
Consequently every subset of
A
and every subset of
B
is red-admissible.

Proof.

For a contour bond
e=\{x,y\}\in\gamma
, let its
parity sign
be determined by the red parity of any grey path in
\eta_{\Lambda\setminus\gamma}
joining the exterior grey components that contain
x
and
y
: by (C1), this parity is path-independent.
This turns
H_{\gamma}
into a finite signed graph.
The recursive proof of
[
7
, Lemma 2.1]
carries over verbatim to this signed contour graph, with “conditionally unfrustrated” replaced by “red-admissible for (C1)”: scan the contour edges in an arbitrary order and construct a maximal red-admissible set
A
by adding an edge whenever admissibility is preserved.
If the complement
B:=\gamma\setminus A
were not red-admissible, a minimal violating cycle in the signed graph built from
B
could be spliced with the witness cycles for the rejected edges exactly as in
[
7
, Lemma 2.1]
, producing a contradiction to the red-admissibility of
A
.
Thus both
A
and
B
are red-admissible.
Finally, red-admissibility is monotone under deleting red contour bonds, so every subset of
A
and every subset of
B
is also red-admissible.
∎

Proposition 3.7

(Supercritical grey percolation)
.

Under
(D1)–(D2)
, for every
d\geq 2
and
\beta
sufficiently large (depending on
d
and
\mathscr{P}
),
G_{\mathrm{grey}}
contains exactly one infinite connected component
\mathbf{P}_{\beta}
-a.s.

For coupling distributions with
|J_{e}|\geq J_{\min}>0
a.s. (e.g., the
\pm J
model), the bound is
quenched
: it holds for each coupling realization individually when

\beta>\frac{1}{2J_{\min}}\log\!\bigl(2c(d)^{2}-1\bigr),

where
c(2)=3
and
c(d)=\exp(64\log d/d)
for
d\geq 3
.
For continuous distributions (e.g., Gaussian), the result holds by averaging the quenched Peierls bound over
J
; the i.i.d. structure
(D1)
and
\mathscr{P}(\{0\})=0

(D2)
ensure the annealed contour sum converges for large
\beta
.

Proof.

We adapt the Peierls argument of
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
with one modification: we replace their frustration-based
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
partition with a red-bond parity partition.
The key observation is that

\nu_{J}(\gamma\equiv 0\mid\eta_{\Lambda\setminus\gamma})=\frac{\prod_{e\in\gamma}w_{e}(\text{closed})}{\sum_{\eta_{\gamma}}\prod_{e\in\gamma}w_{e}(\eta_{e})},

where the sum in the denominator runs over all bond configurations on
\gamma
compatible with
\eta_{\Lambda\setminus\gamma}
and the constraints (C1)–(C2), and
w_{e}
denotes the unnormalized CMR weight of bond
e
.
Since restricting the denominator to
any
subset of valid configurations gives a valid upper bound, we restrict to configurations in which every open bond on
\gamma
is
red
.
The frustration constraint (C2) forbids only all-blue cycles and is therefore irrelevant: no configuration in our restricted denominator contains a blue bond.

Step 1: Parity partition.

Fix a coupling realization
J
and a Peierls contour
\gamma
(the image of a self-avoiding dual circuit in
d=2
, or a connected plaquette surface in
d\geq 3
; see
[
7
, p. 1786, 1791]
).
Let
\Lambda
be a box containing
\gamma
and condition on
\eta_{\Lambda\setminus\gamma}
(all bonds outside
\gamma
).
Contract each grey connected component of
\eta_{\Lambda\setminus\gamma}
meeting
\gamma
to a single vertex and retain the contour bonds as edges, obtaining the finite contour graph
H_{\gamma}
.
By Lemma
3.6
, the contour bonds partition as
\gamma=A\sqcup B
so that every subset of
A
can be opened as red with
B
closed without violating (C1), and likewise every subset of
B
can be opened as red with
A
closed.
Denote
|A|=m
; by exchanging
A
and
B
if necessary, we may assume
m\leq|\gamma|/2
.

Step 2: Weight ratio.

Using the merge-case weights (
4
), a red bond (per channel) has weight
r(1-r)
with geometric factor
2
(two red channels, grey cluster merge), and a closed bond has weight
r^{2}
with geometric factor
4
.
In the worst case every contour bond is a merge bond, so the effective weight ratio (red-to-closed) per bond is

R:=\frac{2\,r(1-r)}{4\,r^{2}}=\frac{1-r}{2r}.

(5)

Step 3: Binomial sum.

Write
\gamma\equiv 0
for the event that all bonds on
\gamma
are closed.
Following
[
7
, eq. (2.4)]
, and noting that the two parity-restricted families overlap in the all-closed configuration, the conditional probability of
\gamma\equiv 0
satisfies

\nu_{J}(\gamma\equiv 0\mid\eta_{\Lambda\setminus\gamma})\;\leq\;\Biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}R^{k}\;+\;\sum_{k=0}^{|\gamma|-m}\binom{|\gamma|-m}{k}R^{k}\;-\;1\Biggr]^{-1}.

(6)

where the first sum runs over subsets of
A
opened as red (with
B
closed), the second over subsets of
B
opened as red (with
A
closed), and the numerator (all-closed weight) is normalized to
1
.
By the binomial theorem, each sum evaluates to

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}R^{k}=(1+R)^{n}=\Bigl(\frac{1+r}{2r}\Bigr)^{\!n},

so the denominator of (
6
) is

(1+R)^{m}+(1+R)^{|\gamma|-m}-1.

Since
m\leq|\gamma|/2
and
1+R>1
, the larger term satisfies

(1+R)^{|\gamma|-m}\geq(1+R)^{|\gamma|/2},

hence

\nu_{J}(\gamma\equiv 0\mid\eta_{\Lambda\setminus\gamma})\;\leq\;\Bigl(\frac{2r}{1+r}\Bigr)^{\!|\gamma|/2}.

(7)

Step 4: Contour sum.

The number of Peierls contours of size
k
surrounding the origin is at most
k\cdot c(d)^{k}

[
7
, p. 1788, 1791]
.
Summing over contours:

\sum_{\gamma\ni O}\nu_{J}(\gamma\equiv 0)\;\leq\;\sum_{k\geq 1}k\,c(d)^{k}\Bigl(\frac{2r}{1+r}\Bigr)^{\!k/2}\;<\;\infty

provided
c(d)\bigl(\frac{2r}{1+r}\bigr)^{1/2}<1
, equivalently
r<1/(2c(d)^{2}-1)
.
By Borel–Cantelli, a.s. only finitely many contours surrounding the origin are all-closed.
Since every vertex not enclosed by an all-closed contour is connected to infinity by a grey path
[
7
, p. 1788]
, grey percolation occurs.
Uniqueness follows from Corollary
3.4
.

For the
\pm J
model (
|J|=1
) in
d=2
: the threshold is
r<1/17
, i.e.,
\beta>\tfrac{1}{2}\ln 17\approx 1.42
.
This coincides with De Santis and Gandolfi’s threshold
\bar{p}_{c}^{(2)}(2)\leq 16/17
for the

[
7
, Corollary 2.3]
: their effective weight
p/2
(blue bonds penalized by cluster weight
q=2
) equals our
R=(1-r)/(2r)
(red bonds penalized by the geometric factor), giving the same exponential contour factor and hence the same threshold condition.
For
d\geq 3
: the threshold improves with
d
; alternatively,
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\supset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
as a subgraph, so the
d=2
threshold applies
[
7
, Lemma 2.6]
.

Annealed extension.

For continuous distributions (e.g., Gaussian),
r_{e}=e^{-2\beta|J_{e}|}
varies across edges.
The quenched bound (
7
) generalizes to

\nu_{J}(\gamma\equiv 0)\;\leq\;\prod_{e\in\gamma}\Bigl(\frac{2r_{e}}{1+r_{e}}\Bigr)^{\!1/2},

since in the edge-dependent denominator the two parity-restricted contributions are each at least
1
, so their sum minus
1
is bounded below by the larger contribution, hence by the geometric mean of the two.
Taking the expectation over

and using independence (D1):

\mathbf{P}_{\beta}(\gamma\equiv 0)\;\leq\;\rho(\beta)^{|\gamma|},\qquad\rho(\beta):=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{2r_{e}}{1+r_{e}}\Bigr)^{\!1/2}\Bigr].

As
\beta\to\infty

pointwise for every

, and the integrand is bounded by
1
.
By dominated convergence,
\rho(\beta)\to\mathscr{P}(\{0\})=0
(using (D2)).
Hence
\rho(\beta)<1/c(d)
for

∎

Remark 3.8

(Possible optimization)
.

The Peierls bound above is deliberately coarse and appears to lose in two different places. First, on contour edges whose endpoints are already connected through the exterior configuration, the parity constraint from (C1) restricts the admissible red subsets to one side of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
balance partition. Second, we price every red contour bond by the worst-case merge ratio
R_{\mathrm{merge}}=\frac{1-r}{2r}
, even though a red bond that only closes a cycle and does not merge grey components should carry the larger ratio
R_{\mathrm{cycle}}=\frac{1-r}{r}
. These two losses should be partially separable: the balance constraint acts on the cycle-type part of the contour, while the geometric factor is only paid by bonds that genuinely merge grey components. A sharper argument would first contract the exterior grey components touched by
\gamma
, reducing the denominator to a finite signed contour graph, and then count balanced red subgraphs with edge weights
R_{\mathrm{cycle}}
on cycle-type edges and
R_{\mathrm{merge}}
on genuinely merging edges. We do not pursue this refinement here.

(a)

\mathscr{F}_{\Lambda^{c}}

•

\mathbf{P}_{\beta}

Proposition 4.3

(At most one infinite blue cluster in a fixed overlap class)
.

Fix
s\in\{\pm 1\}
.
Let
N_{s}
denote the number of infinite blue-bond clusters in the overlap class
q=s
.
Then
N_{s}\in\{0,1\}

\mathbf{P}_{\beta}
-a.s.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{P}_{\beta}
is ergodic at the fixed temperature
\beta
and
N_{s}
is translation-invariant,
N_{s}
is
\mathbf{P}_{\beta}
-a.s. constant.
For a finite box
\Lambda
, let
A_{m}(\Lambda)
be the event that the exterior blue subgraph induced by
\Lambda^{c}
has at least
m
distinct infinite components in the overlap class
q=s
touching the outside boundary of
\Lambda
.
This event is measurable with respect to
\mathscr{F}_{\Lambda^{c}}
.

N_{s}\neq k
for
2\leq k<\infty
.

If
N_{s}=k
, then with positive probability
A_{k}(\Lambda)
occurs for some finite box
\Lambda
: choose a box meeting all
k
infinite blue clusters, and remove the box to expose one infinite exterior arm from each cluster.

A_{k}(\Lambda)
is outside-measurable, Lemma
4.1
gives positive conditional probability on
A_{k}(\Lambda)
that
B_{s}(\Lambda)
occurs.
On
A_{k}(\Lambda)\cap B_{s}(\Lambda)
, all
k
infinite exterior components are merged through
\Lambda
, so the resulting configuration has fewer than
k
infinite blue clusters in the class
q=s
.

\{N_{s}<k\}
, contradicting the a.s. constancy of
N_{s}
.

.

\Lambda
.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
(b)
.

Applying Proposition
4.3
with

and

gives

\mathbf{P}_{\beta}
-a.s.
Every blue cluster is contained in a single overlap parity class, so the total number of infinite blue clusters is at most two.

Within that grey cluster, the two path-parity classes coincide under the spin lift with the overlap classes
q=+1
and
q=-1
.
Since Proposition
4.3
gives at most one infinite blue cluster in each overlap class, any two infinite blue clusters must lie in opposite path-parity classes; equivalently, every grey path between them crosses an odd number of red bonds.

).

\mathbf{P}_{\beta}
.

Fix
\beta>0
and let
\mathbf{P}_{\beta}

1.9

Corollary 4.4

(Replica-symmetric ergodic case)
.

Assume in addition that
\mathbf{P}_{\beta}
is ergodic and invariant under the global replica flip

(J,\sigma,\tau,\eta)\longmapsto(J,\sigma,-\tau,\eta).

Then:

(i)

G_{\mathrm{blue}}
contains either
0
or
2
infinite blue clusters
\mathbf{P}_{\beta}
-a.s.

(ii)

If
\rho_{+}^{\infty}
and
\rho_{-}^{\infty}
denote the spatial densities of the infinite blue clusters in the overlap classes

and
q=-1
, respectively, then

\rho_{+}^{\infty}=\rho_{-}^{\infty}\qquad\mathbf{P}_{\beta}\text{-a.s.}

Proof.

Let
N_{+}
and
N_{-}
denote the numbers of infinite blue clusters in the overlap classes
q=+1
and
q=-1
, respectively.
By Theorem
1.9
(b),
N_{+},N_{-}\in\{0,1\}

\mathbf{P}_{\beta}
-a.s.
Since both are translation-invariant and
\mathbf{P}_{\beta}

The replica-flip map swaps the

and
q=-1

N_{+}
and
N_{-}
.
By invariance,
N_{+}
and
N_{-}
have the same law, hence the same almost-sure constant value.
Therefore
N_{+}=N_{-}
and the total number of infinite blue clusters is either

or

, let

Remark 4.5
.

The replica-flip invariance alone already implies

.

argue on the basis of mean-field calculations and numerics that the spin glass transition is accompanied by the appearance of exactly two giant blue clusters of unequal density.
Münster and Weigel
[
3
,
4
]
observe two dominant CMRJ clusters in two- and three-dimensional short-range Ising spin-glass models, with density difference approaching the overlap
|q|
at low temperature.
Theorem
1.9
provides a rigorous upper bound consistent with these observations: the number of infinite blue clusters cannot exceed two, and if two exist then they lie in the common infinite grey cluster and occupy opposite path-parity classes there.

Part (a) establishes a grey-bond percolation transition, but this does not by itself imply the existence of infinite blue clusters or a spin glass phase transition—the infinite grey cluster could in principle be sustained entirely by red bonds.
Theorem
1.9
does not prove the existence of any infinite blue cluster, the existence of a spin glass transition, or any property of the critical point (value of
T_{c}
, sharpness, critical exponents).

Turning the percolation picture into an identity between the overlap order parameter and blue-cluster densities requires two additional inputs that are not provided here.
First, the overlap order parameter
q=D_{+}-D_{-}
(where
D_{\pm}
is the density of sites with
q_{i}=\pm 1
) vanishes almost surely under any translation-invariant ergodic joint Gibbs measure that is invariant under
(\sigma,\tau)\mapsto(\sigma,-\tau)
: translation ergodicity forces
D_{\pm}
to be a.s. constant, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry swaps them, so
D_{+}=D_{-}=\tfrac{1}{2}
.
Thus a nontrivial density interpretation of
q
necessarily requires symmetry-broken (extremal) measures, which our framework does not directly construct.
Second, even in such measures, one would still need to show that finite blue clusters inside the infinite grey cluster do not contribute to the overlap density.
This is exactly the point flagged by Machta, Newman, and Stein
[
2
, p. 120]
as “not immediate” in short-range models.
Unlike the ferromagnetic FK representation, finite CMR blue clusters do not receive independent random spin labels, so the usual FK cancellation mechanism is unavailable.

Theorem
1.9
applies to any translation-invariant joint Gibbs measure on
(J,\sigma,\tau,\eta)
consistent with the CMR specification, and the quenched consequence (Corollary
4.6
) covers every Gibbs state arising from a subsequential torus limit
[
6
, Chapter 4]
.
For Gibbs states that do not arise from any torus limit (if such exist), or for questions about the full metastate
\kappa_{J}

[
6
, Appendix B]
, additional arguments would be needed.

The theorem isolates the part of the CMR picture that is purely structural.
If infinite blue clusters occur in a translation-invariant CMR state, their multiplicity is forced by the overlap parity: at most one can live in each of the two parity classes.
In this sense the two-cluster scenario is not merely a mean-field or numerical artifact, but the only possible infinite-cluster geometry compatible with the CMR constraints.

The remaining obstruction is not multiplicity, but density.
To identify the spin overlap with a difference of blue-cluster densities, one still has to show that finite blue clusters do not contribute to the overlap density, or else work in a setting where this contribution can be controlled.
This is the short-range difficulty emphasized by Machta, Newman, and Stein, and it remains the natural next problem after the upper-bound result proved here.
