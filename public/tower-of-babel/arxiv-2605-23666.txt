A Priori Regularity Estimates for Ratio of Solutions to Elliptic Equations with a Product Structure of Two-Dimensional Nodal Sets
Gabriele Fioravanti

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish optimal a priori C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} regularity estimates for the ratio w = v / u w=v/u of two solutions to the same elliptic equation − div ( A ∇ u ) = 0 -\operatorname{div}(A\nabla u)=0 with Lipschitz coefficients A A , under the assumption that their nodal sets satisfy Z ⁡ ( u ) ⊆ Z ⁡ ( v ) Z(u)\subseteq Z(v) . We specifically address the case where the zero set Z ⁡ ( u ) Z(u) exhibits a product structure of 2 2 -dimensional nodal sets, namely Z ( u ) = Z ( u 1 ) × ⋯ × Z ( u m ) Z(u)=Z(u_{1})\times\cdots\times Z(u_{m}) , where the u i u_{i} are 2 2 -dimensional functions. This result extends the regularity estimates previously proved in dimension 2 2 by Logunov and Malinnikova [ 17 ] and by Terracini, Tortone, and Vita [ 26 ] .

1.
Introduction

The behavior of non-negative harmonic functions near the boundary is classically described by the
Boundary Harnack Principle
. This result ensures that the ratio of two such functions vanishing on a common boundary portion remains bounded or Hölder continuous up to the boundary. It is a well-established fact that this property holds under weak geometric assumptions, including Lipschitz, NTA, and Hölder domains (see
[
15
,
10
,
1
,
14
,
4
,
5
,
9
]
).
When the domain exhibits higher regularity, one naturally expects the ratio to enjoy improved regularity as well. This is addressed by the
Higher Order Boundary Harnack Principle
, which establishes that if the boundary is of class
C^{k,\alpha}
, the quotient inherits the same
C^{k,\alpha}
regularity up to the boundary (see
[
8
,
3
,
24
,
27
,
2
]
).

A more recent and challenging direction in this field involves extending the classical theory to the framework of
nodal domains
. Within this setting, one examines two solutions
u
and
v
sharing the same uniformly elliptic equation

(1.1)

L_{A}u:=-\operatorname{div}(A(x)\nabla u)=0\quad\text{in }B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},

where the coefficient matrix
A(x)
is symmetric and fulfills the uniform ellipticity condition

(1.2)

\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq A(x)\xi\cdot\xi\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{2}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in B_{1}\text{ and every }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

for some
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
.
The key geometric constraint here is the nodal inclusion
Z(u)\subseteq Z(v)
, where
Z(u)=\{x\in B_{1}:u(x)=0\}
. Under this assumption, the primary objective in studying the quotient
v/u
is twofold:

i)

to establish the effective Hölder or higher order regularity of the ratio;

ii)

to determine if this regularity is
uniform
within specific classes of denominators
u
, thereby understanding the structural impact of the nodal set
Z(u)
on the estimates.

Before proceeding, we recall a few structural properties of the nodal set
Z(u)
. Assuming
u
is a weak solution to (
1.1
) with a symmetric, Lipschitz continuous matrix
A
satisfying (
1.2
), classical regularity theory yields
u\in H^{2}_{{\rm loc}}(B_{1})\cap C^{1,\alpha}_{{\rm loc}}(B_{1})
for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Furthermore, following
[
18
,
13
]
, we can decompose the nodal set as
Z(u)=R(u)\cup S(u)
, where the regular and singular parts are respectively given by

R(u)=\{x\in Z(u):|\nabla u(x)|\neq 0\}\quad\text{and}\quad S(u)=\{x\in Z(u):|\nabla u(x)|=0\}.

An immediate consequence of the Implicit Function Theorem is that
R(u)
is locally
C^{1,\alpha}
for every
\alpha\in(0,1)
. On the other hand, the singular set
S(u)
has Hausdorff dimension bounded by
n-2
, reducing to a locally finite set in dimension
n=2
.

Outside the singular set
S(u)
, the analysis of the ratio
v/u
under the inclusion
Z(u)\subseteq Z(v)
reduces to the classical Higher Order Boundary Harnack Principle, which guarantees local
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity for the quotient (see
[
8
,
24
]
).
In contrast, near the singular set
S(u)
, one cannot rely on these classical techniques, as the boundaries of the
nodal domains
– the connected components of
\{x\in B_{1}:u(x)\neq 0\}
– exhibit very poor regularity, being merely Lipschitz or even worse. Nevertheless, unlike in the classical Boundary Harnack Principle where the domain is imposed a priori, here the "boundary" naturally arises as the zero set of a solution to an elliptic equation. Because of this intrinsic PDE structure, one might actually expect the ratio
v/u
to enjoy higher regularity than what standard theory ensures for such rough domains.

The pioneering results in this direction are due to Logunov and Malinnikova
[
16
,
17
]
. Specifically, under the assumption of analytic coefficients, they showed that the ratio
v/u
is analytic across
Z(u)
. Furthermore, in dimension
n=2
, they proved uniform
C^{k,\alpha}
estimates for the ratio within the class of denominators
u
having an upper bound on the number of nodal domains.

Lowering the regularity framework, Lin and Lin
[
19
]
addressed the problem under Lipschitz assumptions on the coefficients
A
. In every dimension, they established Hölder continuity for
v/u
across the entire nodal set
Z(u)
for some implicit exponent
\alpha^{*}\in(0,1)
, showing that such regularity estimates are uniform within the class of denominators
u
having an upper bound on the generalized Almgren frequency functional.

More recently, in a series of works
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
, Terracini, Tortone, and Vita addressed both the effective regularity and the uniform estimates under Lipschitz assumptions on the coefficients. First, in every dimension and for every
\alpha\in(0,1)
, they obtained
a priori

C^{0,\alpha}
estimates which are uniform within the class of denominators
u
with a bounded generalized Almgren frequency functional. In dimension
n=2
, they proved that this class is equivalent to the one considered by Logunov and Malinnikova. Moreover, in the planar case, they established optimal
a priori

C^{1,\alpha}
estimates for every
\alpha\in(0,1)
, which are uniform within the same class of denominators. Next, by means of a regularization-approximation scheme, they were able to prove the actual
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of the ratio.

The aim of this paper is to extend the
2
-dimensional uniform
a priori

C^{1,\alpha}
estimates established by Logunov and Malinnikova and by Terracini, Tortone, and Vita to a higher-dimensional framework and in the case of Lipschitz continuous coefficients. Here, we address the case where the zero set
Z(u)
exhibits a product structure of
2
-dimensional nodal sets, namely
Z(u)=Z(u_{1})\times\cdots\times Z(u_{m})
, where the
u_{i}
are
2
-dimensional functions (as detailed in Assumption (
1.1
) below). Under this geometric assumption, we establish optimal
a priori

C^{1,\alpha}
estimates which are uniform within this frequency bounded class of denominators.

1.1.
Main Result

To state our main result, we first introduce the necessary definitions and define the uniformity classes of coefficients and denominators.

Given
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
and
L\geq 0
, we define the class of admissible coefficients
\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\Lambda,L}
as

\mathcal{A}:=\Big\{A\in C^{0,1}(B_{1},\mathbb{R}^{n,n}):A=A^{T}\text{ satisfies }\eqref{eq:Unif:Ell}\text{ and }[A]_{C^{0,1}(B_{1})}\leq L\Big\}.

Given
N_{0}>0
, we define the class
\mathcal{S}_{N_{0}}:=\mathcal{S}_{N_{0},\lambda,\Lambda,L}
as the solutions to (
1.1
), for which the generalized Almgren frequency formula
N
satisfies an upper bound, that is,

\mathcal{S}_{N_{0}}:=\Big\{u\in H^{1}(B_{1}):u\text{ solves }\eqref{eq:elliptic}\text{ with }A\in\mathcal{A},\,0\in Z(u),\,N(0,u,1)\leq N_{0},\,\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}=1\Big\}.

This class is the natural choice for seeking uniform estimates, as it ensures the compactness of the denominator in the blow-up analysis, as established in
[
25
]
.

In our main result we make the following orthogonal assumption about the coefficients and the denominators
u
.

Assumption 1.1

(Orthogonality assumption)
.

Let us suppose that
n
is an even number.
Let us consider
A\in\mathcal{A}
. We assume that
A
can be written in block form as

A(x):=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})&0&\cdots&0\\
0&A_{2}(x_{3},x_{4})&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&A_{n/2}(x_{n-1},x_{n})\end{pmatrix},

where
A_{i}
is a
2
-dimensional matrix and every
A_{i}
depends on the couple of variables
(x_{2i-1},x_{2i})
.
We also assume that
u\in\mathcal{S}_{N_{0}}
can be written in the form

u:=\prod_{i=1}^{n/2}u_{i}(x_{2i-1},x_{2i}),

and every
u_{i}
satisfies
L_{A}u_{i}=0
in
B_{1}
.

Instead, if
n
is an odd number we consider the last block
A_{(n+1)/2}
as a real-valued function and not a
2
-dimensional matrix and the last function
u_{(n+1)/2}
depends only on
x_{n}
.

Under the previous assumption, we prove optimal, uniform-in-
\mathcal{S}_{N_{0}}

a priori

C^{1,\alpha}
estimates for the ratio
v/u
of solutions to (
1.1
) satisfying
Z(u)\subseteq Z(v)
. For the sake of brevity, we say that a constant depends on the class
\mathcal{S}_{N_{0}}
if it depends on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
L
, and
N_{0}
.

Theorem 1.2

(A priori
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
u\in\mathcal{S}_{N_{0}}
satisfying the Assumption
1.1
and
v
be a solution to

L_{A}u=L_{A}v=0\quad\text{in }B_{1},\qquad\text{such that }{Z}(u)\subseteq{Z}(v).

Then, if
v/u\in C^{1,\alpha}(B_{1})
, there exists a constant
C>0
, which depends only on the class
\mathcal{S}_{N_{0}}
and
\alpha
, such that

(1.3)

{\Big\|\frac{v}{u}\Big\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(B_{1/2})}}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}.

The strategy of the proof highlights a deep connection with the theory of degenerate elliptic PDEs. Indeed, as proved in
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
, the ratio
w:=v/u
satisfies (in the sense of Definition
2.3
) the weighted elliptic equation

-\operatorname{div}(u^{2}A\nabla w)=0\quad\text{in }B_{1},

where the coefficient matrix
u^{2}A
degenerates on the nodal set
Z(u)
.

Such equations are highly degenerate, and there are few results in the literature concerning this framework; in general, solutions are not even continuous across
Z(u)
(see Example 1.4 in
[
23
]
). Despite this, by adapting the classical blow-up argument due to Simon
[
22
]
, as done in
[
26
]
, we are able to obtain Schauder
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates for the ratio
w
.
The proof of these estimates crucially requires controlling the oscillation of
\nabla w
near the singular set
S(u)
in a quantitative way (see Section
4.1
). Under the structural Assumption
1.1
, we extend the
2
-dimensional strategy of
[
26
]
to higher dimensions, but this transition introduces a major structural difference.
Specifically, by finding an appropriate way to project
\nabla w
onto each
R(u_{i})
– the planar regular set of the factor
u_{i}
– and by exploiting the conormal boundary condition satisfied by
w
on the regular set
R(u)
, combined with a fine blow-up analysis, we ensure a uniform bound on the oscillation of
\nabla w
. This allows us to track and control the behavior of the solution on every
2
-dimensional component separately (see also
[
7
]
for a similar framework concerning elliptic equations with monomial weights). We emphasize that this component-wise approach is a new feature of the higher-dimensional setting, as it has no counterpart in the
2
-dimensional strategy of
[
26
]
, and it could represent a first step toward tackling more general, non-product geometric configurations of the zero set
Z(u)
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we begin by recalling some basic facts about solutions to (
1.1
) with Lipschitz coefficients, focusing on the associated frequency function which plays a central role in our local analysis.

As a starting point, let us consider the case of constant coefficients (assuming
A=\mathbb{I}
for simplicity). For
x_{0}\in B_{1}
and
r<1-|x_{0}|
, we define

H(x_{0},u,r):=\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\int_{\partial B_{r}}u^{2}\,d\sigma,\qquad D(x_{0},u,r):=\frac{1}{r^{n-2}}\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx,

and the frequency function as

N(x_{0},u,r):=\frac{D(x_{0},u,r)}{H(x_{0},u,r)}.

As first observed by Almgren, the map
r\mapsto N(x_{0},u,r)
is monotone non-decreasing.

This theory was successfully extended to the case of variable Lipschitz coefficients by Garofalo and Lin
[
12
]
. They introduced a
generalized
Almgren frequency
N(x_{0},u,r)
depending explicitly on
A
, proving that the Lipschitz continuity of the matrix ensures an
almost
monotonicity property for this functional. This monotonicity property serves as a powerful tool to control the vanishing order of solutions, thereby implying the strong unique continuation principle. At the same time, this tool provides quantitative bounds on the size of both the critical and nodal sets. We skip the construction of
generalized
Almgren frequency functional and we refer to
[
12
,
18
,
13
,
6
,
21
,
25
,
26
]
for the exact definition and related results. The almost monotonicity of the generalized frequency is stated in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
12
,
6
,
25
]
)
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
u
be a solution to (
1.1
),
x_{0}\in B_{1}
. Let
N(x_{0},u,x)
be the generalized Almgren frequency as defined in
[
25
, §2]
.
Then, there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
L
, such that

r\mapsto e^{Cr}N(x_{0},u,r),

is monotone non-decreasing for
r<\Lambda^{-1/2}(1-|x_{0}|)
.

Among the consequences of the previous proposition, there is the following doubling-type estimate, which plays a crucial role in the rest of the paper.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}
and let
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
be a weak solution to
L_{A}u=0
in
B_{1}
. For every
x_{0}\in B_{1/2}
,
0<r\leq R\leq\Lambda^{-1/2}(1-|x_{0}|)
, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
L
, such that

\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u^{2}\,dx:=\frac{1}{|B_{R}|}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u^{2}\,dx\leq C\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{2N(x_{0},u,R)}\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u^{2}\,dx,

and

\fint_{\partial B_{R}(x_{0})}u^{2}\,d\sigma:=\frac{1}{|\partial B_{R}|}\int_{\partial B_{R}(x_{0})}u^{2}\,d\sigma\leq C\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{2N(x_{0},u,R)}\fint_{\partial B_{r}(x_{0})}u^{2}\,d\sigma.

2.1.
Weak solution

Next, we briefly recall the notion of weak solution to

(2.1)

-\operatorname{div}(u^{2}A\nabla w)=0\quad\text{in }B_{1},\qquad\text{with }u\in\mathcal{S}_{N_{0}},

as introduced in
[
25
, §3.2]
. For a more general discussion of the framework we refer the reader to the aforementioned reference.

Given
u\in\mathcal{S}_{N_{0}}
, we define the weighted Sobolev space
H^{1}(B_{1},u^{2}\,dx)
as the completion of
C^{\infty}(\overline{B_{1}})
with respect to the norm

\|w\|_{H^{1}(B_{1},u^{2}\,dx)}:=\Big(\int_{B_{1}}u^{2}\big(w^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\big)\,dx\Big)^{1/2}.

We then introduce the class of solutions which we focus on in the proof of the main result.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
u\in\mathcal{S}_{N_{0}}
. We say that
w
is a weak solution to (
2.1
),
if
w\in H^{1}(B_{1},u^{2}\,dx)
and

\int_{B_{1}}u^{2}A\nabla w\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx=0,\quad\text{for every }\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1}).

Remark 2.4
.

As proved in
[
24
, Proposition 3.5]
, the ratio of two solutions
v,u
to (
1.1
) satisfies (
2.1
) in the sense of the Definition
2.3
.
Moreover, in
[
26
, Proposition 3.9]
, the authors established an inverse relation for this ratio, which can be stated as follows. Let
u\in\mathcal{S}_{N_{0}}
and
w\in H^{1}(B_{1},u^{2}\,dx)
be a weak solution to (
2.1
). Assume that
w\in C(B_{1}\setminus S(u))
. Then, the function
v:=wu\in H^{1}(B_{1})
is a weak solution to (
1.1
) and
Z(u)\subseteq Z(v)
.

3.
Harmonic polynomials and invariance directions

In this section, we present several results concerning harmonic polynomials and solutions to elliptic equations. We emphasize that these results hold generally and do not rely on Assumption
1.1
.
The following result shows that if the zero set of a harmonic polynomial is tangential to a given direction, then the polynomial is invariant in that direction.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
be a constant symmetric positive matrix and
P:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a polynomial such that
L_{A}P=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let us suppose that
\partial_{\bf e}P=\nabla P\cdot{\bf e}=0
for some unit vector
{\bf e}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
on the regular set
Z(P):=\{P=0\}
. Then,
\partial_{\bf e}P=0
on the whole
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
A=\mathbb{I}
and
\mathbf{e}=e_{n}
. In the general case, it is sufficient to consider the transformed polynomial
\tilde{P}(x):=P(A^{1/2}x)
, which satisfies
\Delta\tilde{P}=0
, and then apply a suitable orthogonal transform.

Since
\partial_{x_{n}}P=0
on
Z(P)
, it follows that
Z(P)\subseteq Z(\partial_{x_{n}}P)
. By Murdoch’s division lemma (see
[
20
, Theorem 2, Lemma 4]
and
[
25
, Lemma 5.6]
), there exists a polynomial
Q
such that
\partial_{x_{n}}P/P=Q
. Since the degree of
\partial_{x_{n}}P
is less or equal than the degree of
P
, this forces
Q=0
, and consequently
\partial_{x_{n}}P=0
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
A
be a constant symmetric positive matrix and
P:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a polynomial such that
L_{A}P=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then, the following are equivalent:

i)

P
is a linear function,

ii)

\nabla P(x)=|\nabla P(x)|{\bf e}
, for every
x\in{R}(P),
where
{\bf e}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
,

iii)

N_{\infty}:=\lim_{r\to\infty}N(0,P,r)\leq 1+\varepsilon
.

Proof.

The equivalence between
i)
and
ii)
immediately follows by Lemma
3.1
. If
iii)
holds true, by standard blow-down argument (see
[
26
, Proposition 5.1]
and
[
19
, Corollary 4.7]
) we get that
P
would be a polynomial of degree
N_{\infty}\leq 1+\varepsilon
, so
N_{\infty}=1
,
P
is linear and
i)
holds true. On the other hand,
i)
implies
iii)
follows trough a straightforward computation.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
A
be a constant symmetric positive matrix and
P:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a polynomial such that
L_{A}P=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\lim_{r\to\infty}N(0,P,r)\leq\overline{N},

for some constant
\overline{N}>0
. Then, the following are equivalent:

i)

the degree of
P
is greater than one,

ii)

there exist
R>0
,
\delta\in(0,1)
, and two points
x_{1},x_{2}\in{R}(P)\cap B_{R}
such that

\Big|\frac{\nabla P(x_{1})}{|\nabla P(x_{1})|}\cdot\frac{\nabla P(x_{2})}{|\nabla P(x_{2})|}\Big|\leq 1-\delta,

iii)

there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
\rho>0
such that
N(0,P,\rho)\geq 1+\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality take
A=\mathbb{I}
.
The equivalence between
i)
and
iii)
is the same as the one established between
i)
and
iii)
in Corollary
3.2
. Next, by contradiction, let us assume that
ii)
holds true and
i)
does not hold, that is, the degree of
P
is less than one. Then, for all points in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
one has that
\nabla P(x)
is constant. In particular, since the polynomial
P
is not constant,
\nabla P(x)
is constant on
{R}(P)
and this contradicts the assumption
ii)
. Finally, by contradiction, let us assume that
iii)
holds true and that
ii)
is false. So, there exists a sequence of harmonic polynomials
P_{k}
such that

N(0,P_{k},\rho)\geq 1+\varepsilon,\quad\lim_{r\to\infty}N(0,P_{k},r)\leq\overline{N},\quad\Big|\frac{\nabla P_{k}(x_{1})}{|\nabla P_{k}(x_{1})|}\cdot\frac{\nabla P_{k}(x_{2})}{|\nabla P_{k}(x_{2})|}\Big|\geq 1-k^{-1},

for every
x_{1},x_{2}\in{R}(P_{k})\cap B_{k}
. Considering the normalized sequence
{P_{k}}/{\|P_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}}
and using standard compactness argument via Schauder estimates, we obtain that
P_{k}\to P_{\infty}
in
C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
where
P_{\infty}
is a polynomial with degree
N_{\infty}
, which satisfies

\Delta P_{\infty}=0,\quad 1+\varepsilon\leq N_{\infty}\leq\overline{N},\quad\|P_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}=1,\quad\frac{\nabla P_{\infty}(x)}{|\nabla P_{\infty}(x)|}={\bf e}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\ \forall x\in{R}(P_{\infty}).

Since
N_{\infty}\geq 1+\varepsilon>1
, one has that
P_{\infty}
has degree greater than one, which is a contradiction with the fact that
\nabla P_{\infty}(x)=|\nabla P_{\infty}(x)|{\bf e}
, for every
x\in{R}(P_{\infty})
, see Corollary
3.2
.
∎

As consequence of the previous results, we obtain that following lemma, which extends
[
26
, Lemma 2.9]
to the
n
-dimensional setting.

Lemma 3.4
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and
\overline{N}>0
. There exist small
\delta>0
and large
R>1
such that the following holds true. Let us suppose that
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
U\in H^{1}(B_{R})
such that

[A]_{C^{0,1}(B_{R})}\leq\delta,\qquad L_{A}U=0\text{ in }B_{R},\qquad 0\in{R}(U)\qquad N(0,U,R)\leq\overline{N},

and

N(0,U,1)\geq 1+\varepsilon.

Then, there exist
R^{\prime}\in(0,R)
,
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,1)
depending only on
\overline{N}
,
\varepsilon
, and there exists a point
x_{1}\in{R}(U)\cap B_{R^{\prime}}
such that

\Big|\frac{\nabla U(x_{1})}{|\nabla U(x_{1})|}\cdot\frac{\nabla U(0)}{|\nabla U(0)|}\Big|\leq 1-\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Before proceeding to the proof, we recall the following compactness result.

Remark 3.5
.

Let us consider a sequence of matrices
A_{k}\in\mathcal{A}
and a sequence of non trivial functions
u_{k}\in H^{1}(B_{k})
such that

[A_{k}]_{C^{0,1}(B_{k})}\leq k^{-1},\qquad L_{A_{k}}u_{k}=0\text{ in }B_{k},\qquad N(0,u_{k},k)\leq\overline{N}.

Then,
A_{k}\to\bar{A}
uniformly on every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\bar{A}
is a constant uniformly elliptic matrix and the normalized sequence
\tilde{u}_{k}:=u_{k}/\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}
satisfies

L_{A_{k}}\tilde{u}_{k}=0\text{ in }B_{k},\qquad N(0,\tilde{u}_{k},k)\leq\overline{N},\qquad\|\tilde{u}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}=1,

For a fixed
\bar{R}>1
, applying classical Schauder estimates yields that
\tilde{u}_{k}
is uniformly bounded in
C^{1,\alpha}(B_{\bar{R}})
and by employing a diagonal argument we get
\tilde{u}_{k}\to P
in
C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
P
satisfies

L_{\bar{A}}P=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad\|P\|_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}=1,\qquad N_{\infty}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}N(0,P,t)\leq\overline{N}.

By a blow-down argument (see
[
26
, Proposition 5.1]
and
[
19
, Corollary 4.7]
), we get that
P
is a non trivial polynomial of degree
N_{\infty}\leq\overline{N}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Arguing by contradiction, let us suppose that there exist sequences
A_{k}\in\mathcal{A}
,
U_{k}\in H^{1}(B_{k})
satisfying

[A_{k}]_{C^{0,1}(B_{k})}\leq k^{-1},\quad L_{A_{k}}U_{k}=0\text{ in }B_{k},\quad 0\in{R}(U_{k}),\quad N(0,U_{k},k)\leq\overline{N},\quad N(0,U_{k},1)\geq 1+\varepsilon,

and

\Big|\frac{\nabla U_{k}(x_{1})}{|\nabla U_{k}(x_{1})|}\cdot\frac{\nabla U_{k}(0)}{|\nabla U_{k}(0)|}\Big|\geq 1-k^{-1},\quad\text{for every }x_{1}\in{R}(U_{k}).

Considering
\tilde{U}_{k}:={U_{k}}/{\|U_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}}
and using Remark
3.5
, we get that

\tilde{U}_{k}\to P
in
C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
P
is a polynomial satisfies

L_{\bar{A}}P=0\ \text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\|P\|_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}=1,\quad 1+\varepsilon\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}N(0,P,t)\leq\overline{N},

where
\bar{A}
is a constant uniformly elliptic matrix.
Therefore,
P
has degree greater or equal than two since
\varepsilon>0
(see Corollary
3.3
). On the other hand, we have

\frac{\nabla P(x_{1})}{|\nabla P(x_{1})|}\cdot\frac{\nabla P(0)}{|\nabla P(0)|}=1,\quad\text{for every }x_{1}\in{R}(P),

which means that
\nabla P(x)=|\nabla P(x)|{\bf e}
, for every
x_{1}\in{R}(P)
, where
{\bf e}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. This is a contradiction with Corollary
3.2
.
∎

4.
A priori Schauder estimates

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Before addressing the proof, we start by proving a simple lemma concerning the control of the
L^{\infty}
-norm of the gradient of solutions to (
1.1
) away from the zero set of
u
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\overline{N}>0
. Let us suppose that
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
such that

\quad L_{A}u=0\text{ in }B_{1},\quad N(0,u,1)\leq\overline{N}.

For every
x_{0}\in B_{1}\setminus{Z}(u)
, set
\delta:=\operatorname{dist}(x_{0},{Z}(u))
. Then, there exists
C>0
, which not depends on
\delta
such that

(4.1)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\delta}(x_{0}))}\leq\frac{C}{\delta}|u(x_{0})|.

Proof.

By using the scaling invariant Schauder estimates to
u
in
B_{\delta}(x_{0})
, the doubling property (see Lemma
2.2
) and the Harnack inequality in
B_{\delta/2}(x_{0})
, we get

\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B_{\delta}(x_{0}))}\leq\frac{C}{\delta}\fint_{B_{2\delta}(x_{0})}u^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{C}{\delta}\fint_{B_{\delta/2}(x_{0})}u^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{C}{\delta}\inf_{B_{\delta/2}(x_{0})}|u|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{\delta}|u(x_{0})|^{2}.\qed

Next, we recall a Liouville-type theorem for the ratio of entire solutions.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
25
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
A
be a constant uniformly elliptic matrix,
u
be a polynomial and
v
such that

-\operatorname{div}(A\nabla u)=-\operatorname{div}(A\nabla v)=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

and
Z(u)\subseteq Z(v)
. Suppose that there exist
\gamma\geq 0
,
C>0
such that

\Big|\frac{v}{u}(x)\Big|\leq C(1+|x|)^{\gamma},\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then, the ratio
v/u
is a polynomial of degree at most
\lfloor\gamma\rfloor
.

We are finally ready to provide the proof of our main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let us suppose that
n
is an even number (the odd case follows analogously) and call
w:=v/u
. In light of
[
25
, Proposition 3.11]
, which ensures the local boundedness of the ratio
w
, it is sufficient to prove that

{\|w\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(B_{1/2})}}\leq C\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})},

for some
C>0
depending only on the class
\mathcal{S}_{N_{0}}
.
Equivalently, following the strategy of
[
11
, Theorem 2.28]
, we aim to show that for every sufficiently small
\delta>0
there exists
C_{\delta}>0
such that

(4.2)

[\nabla w]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1/2})}\leq\delta[\nabla w]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}+C_{\delta}\big(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\big).

Using the sub-additivity of the Hölder seminorms with respect to unions of convex sets (see
[
11
, Lemma 2.27]
) and a covering argument, it follows that (
4.2
) implies (
1.3
).

In order to establish (
4.2
), we argue by contradiction. Let us assume that there exist sequences
A_{k}\in\mathcal{A}
,
u_{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{N_{0}}
, and
v_{k}
be solutions to

L_{A_{k}}u_{k}=L_{A_{k}}v_{k}=0\quad\text{in }B_{1},\quad\text{such that }{Z}(u_{k})\subseteq{Z}(v_{k}),

w_{k}:=v_{k}/u_{k}\in C^{1,\alpha}(B_{1})
and there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that

(4.3)

[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1/2})}>\delta_{0}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}+k\big(\|\nabla w_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|w_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\big).

By definition of Hölder seminorm, let us consider two sequences of points
x_{k},y_{k}\in B_{1/2}
such that

(4.4)

\frac{|\nabla w_{k}(x_{k})-\nabla w_{k}(y_{k})|}{|x_{k}-y_{k}|^{\alpha}}\geq\frac{[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1/2})}}{2},

and set
r_{k}:=|x_{k}-y_{k}|
. By using (
4.3
), it readily follows that
r_{k}\to 0
, in fact

r_{k}^{\alpha}\leq\frac{|\nabla w_{k}(x_{k})-\nabla w_{k}(y_{k})|}{2[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1/2})}}\leq\frac{\|\nabla w_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1/2})}}\leq k^{-1}\to 0.

Let us write
x_{k}:=((x_{k})_{1},\dots,(x_{k})_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for every
i=1,\dots,n/2
let us define the sequence of points

x_{i,k}:=((x_{k})_{2i-1},(x_{k})_{2i})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

We denote by
\xi_{i,k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
a chosen projection of
x_{i,k}
on the two-dimensional regular set
{R}(u_{i,k})
restricted to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and define
\delta_{k}^{i}:=|x_{i,k}-\xi_{i,k}|
. We now define

(4.5)

\hat{x}_{i,k}:=\begin{cases}x_{i,k},&\text{if }\delta_{i,k}/r_{k}\to\infty,\\
\xi_{i,k},&\text{if }\delta_{i,k}/r_{k}\leq C,\end{cases}

and
\hat{x}_{k}:=(\hat{x}_{1,k},\dots,\hat{x}_{n/2,k})\in B_{3/4}
.

Next, define the sequence of functions

W_{k}(x):=\frac{w_{k}(\hat{x}_{k}+r_{k}x)-w_{k}(\hat{x}_{k})-{\nabla w_{k}(\hat{x}_{k})}\cdot r_{k}x}{r_{k}^{1+\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}},\quad\text{for }x\in\Omega_{k}:=\frac{B_{1}-\hat{x}_{k}}{r_{k}}.

For every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it is standard to verify that

[\nabla W_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(K)}\leq 1.

Moreover, since
W_{k}(0)=0
and
|\nabla W_{k}(0)|=0
, we can apply the first-order expansion of
W_{k}
at the origin to obtain a local bound on its
C^{1,\alpha}
-norm. In particular, for every compact set
K\subset\Omega_{k}
, we deduce that

\|W_{k}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(K)}\leq C_{K},

for some constant
C_{K}>0
depending only on the diameter of
K
. By a standard compactness argument via Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, we then obtain that
W_{k}\to W
in
C^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
W
satisfies the growth condition

(4.6)

|W(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{1+\alpha},\quad\text{ for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

At this point, let us consider the sequences of points

P_{k}:=\frac{x_{k}-\hat{x}_{k}}{r_{k}},\quad\hat{P}_{k}:=\frac{y_{k}-\hat{x}_{k}}{r_{k}}.

Using (
4.4
) and (
4.3
), we deduce that

|\nabla W_{k}(P_{k})-\nabla W_{k}(\hat{P}_{k})|=\frac{|\nabla w_{k}(x_{k})-\nabla w_{k}(y_{k})|}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1/2})}}{[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}.

Moreover, by the choice of
\hat{x}_{k}
it follows that
P_{k}
and
\hat{P}_{k}
lie inside a compact set. Therefore, one has that
P_{k}\to P
,
\hat{P}_{k}\to\hat{P}
and
P\not=\hat{P}
. Then, by the uniform convergence
\nabla W_{k}\to\nabla W
it follows that
\nabla W
is not constant.

The next step is to look at the equation satisfied by
W_{k}
, recalling that
w_{k}=v_{k}/u_{k}
is a weak solution to

-\operatorname{div}(u_{k}^{2}A_{k}\nabla w_{k})=0\quad\text{in }B_{1}.

and, by
[
24
, Theorem 1.2]
, satisfies

(4.7)

\nabla u_{k}\cdot A_{k}\nabla w_{k}=0\quad\text{on }{R}(u_{k}).

Setting
\bar{A}_{k}:=A_{k}(\hat{x}_{k}+r_{k}x)
and using standard computation, it follows that, for every
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
k
sufficiently large

(4.8)

\int u_{k}^{2}(\hat{x}_{k}+r_{k}x)\bar{A}_{k}(x)\nabla W_{k}(x)\cdot\nabla\phi(x)\,dx=-\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int u_{k}^{2}(\hat{x}_{k}+r_{k}x)\bar{A}_{k}(x){\nabla w_{k}(\hat{x}_{k})}\cdot\nabla\phi(x)\,dx.

Let us recall that
u_{k}(x):=\prod_{i=1}^{n/2}u_{i,k}(x_{2i-1,2i})
and define the rescaled functions

U_{i,k}(x):=\frac{u_{i,k}(\hat{x}_{k}+r_{k}x)}{H(\hat{x}_{k},u_{i,k},r_{k})^{1/2}},\qquad H(\hat{x}_{k},u_{i,k},r_{k}):=\fint_{\partial B_{r_{k}}}u_{i,k}^{2}\,d\sigma,\qquad U_{k}(x):=\prod_{i=1}^{n/2}U_{i,k}(x).

Dividing both sides of (
4.8
) by

H(\hat{x}_{k},u_{k},r_{k})=\prod_{i=1}^{n/2}H(\hat{x}_{k},u_{i,k},r_{k})
, we obtain

(4.9)

\int U_{k}^{2}\bar{A}_{k}\nabla W_{k}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx=-\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int U_{k}^{2}\bar{A}_{k}{\nabla w_{k}(\hat{x}_{k})}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx.

We now aim to pass to the limit as
k\to\infty
in the previous identity in order to derive a contradiction. We begin by analyzing the left-hand side. First, recalling that
{\|A_{k}\|}_{C^{0,1}(B_{1})}\leq L
, it follows that the rescaled coefficients satisfy
{[\bar{A}_{k}]}_{C^{0,1}(K)}\leq Lr_{k}
, for every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Therefore, by the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, we have
\bar{A}_{k}\to\bar{A}
uniformly on every compact sets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\bar{A}
is a constant uniformly elliptic matrix. By using the compactness of the sequences
U_{i,k}
(see
[
25
, Proposition 2.5]
), we get that
U_{i,k}\to U_{i}
uniformly on every compact sets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
U_{i}
is a global polynomial solution to
-\operatorname{div}(\bar{A}\nabla U_{i})=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Subsequently, by Assumption
1.1
, the product function
U=\prod_{i=1}^{n/2}U_{i}
is also a global polynomial solution to
-\operatorname{div}(\bar{A}\nabla U)=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Finally, we have shown before that
W_{k}\to W
in
C^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence, combining these results it follows that

\int U_{k}^{2}\bar{A}_{k}\nabla W_{k}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx\to\int U^{2}\bar{A}\nabla W\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx,

for every
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

If we prove that the right-hand side of (
4.9
) vanishes as
k\to\infty
, then the limit function
W
solves the equation

-\operatorname{div}(U^{2}\bar{A}\nabla W)=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

By Remark
2.4
, it follows that the function
V:=UW
is an entire solution to
-\operatorname{div}(\bar{A}\nabla V)=0
, with
Z(U)\subseteq Z(V)
. Finally, combining the growth condition (
4.6
) with the fact that
\nabla W
is not constant, we obtain a contradiction with the Liouville Theorem
4.2
.

Hence, in order to conclude the proof, it remains to show that the right-hand side of (
4.9
) vanishes as
k\to\infty
. The purpose of the following section is to establish this fact.

4.1.
Estimates of the right-hand side

Our final goal is to show that the right-hand side of (
4.9
) vanishes as
k\to\infty
. First, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int U_{k}^{2}\bar{A}_{k}{\nabla w_{k}(\hat{x}_{k})}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int U_{k}^{2}(x)\big(A_{k}(\hat{x}_{k}+r_{k}x)-A_{k}(\hat{x}_{k})\big)\nabla w_{k}(\hat{x}_{k})\cdot\nabla\phi(x)\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int U_{k}^{2}(x)(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\hat{x}_{k})\cdot\nabla\phi(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\|A_{k}\|_{C^{0,1}(B_{1})}r_{k}^{1-\alpha}\frac{\|\nabla w_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}+\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int U_{k}^{2}(x)(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\hat{x}_{k})\cdot\nabla\phi(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=o(1)-\frac{2}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int U_{k}(x)\nabla U_{k}(x)\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\hat{x}_{k})\phi(x)\,dx,

where we used
\|A_{k}\|_{C^{0,1}(B_{1})}\leq L
,
\|U_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\leq C_{R}
,
\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq k^{-1}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1/2})}
and the divergence theorem. Moreover, using the orthogonality Assumption
1.1
, we can expand this last expression as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\Big|\int U_{k}(x)\nabla U_{k}(x)\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\hat{x}_{k})\phi(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{n/2}\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int\frac{U_{k}^{2}(x)}{U_{i,k}^{2}(x)}U_{i,k}(x)\nabla U_{i,k}(x)\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\hat{x}_{k})\phi(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n/2}\Big\|\frac{U_{k}(x)}{U_{i,k}(x)}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}^{2}\Big|\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int U_{i,k}(x)\nabla U_{i,k}(x)\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\hat{x}_{k})\phi(x)\,dx\Big|=:\sum_{i=1}^{n/2}c_{i,k}^{2}I_{i,k},

where
c_{i,k}=\|U_{k}/U_{i,k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\leq C_{R}
uniformly in
k
and

I_{i,k}:=\Big|\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int U_{i,k}(x)\nabla U_{i,k}(x)\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\hat{x}_{k})\phi(x)\,dx\Big|

In order to conclude the proof and prove that the right-hand side of (
4.9
) vanishes, we establish the following result:

Lemma 4.3
.

Under the previous notations, for every
i=1,\dots,n/2
, the term
I_{i,k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.

Let us recall that, using Assumption
1.1
, we analyze each term
I_{i,k}
separately, focusing solely on the two-dimensional variable
(x_{2i-1},x_{2i})
and keeping all other variables fixed. With a slight abuse of notation, we introduce the conventions used throughout the remainder of the proof.

First, let us call
I_{k}:=I_{i,k}
.
We define
\zeta_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(which depends on
i
) to be a projection of the point
\hat{x}_{k}
(which is defined in (
4.5
)) onto the set
{R}(u_{i,k})
, where
{R}(u_{i,k})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the regular part of the nodal set of
u_{i,k}
. Specifically, writing
\zeta_{k}=(\zeta_{1,k},\dots,\zeta_{n/2,k})
with
\zeta_{j,k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
for each
j
, we set

\zeta_{j,k}:=\begin{cases}\hat{x}_{j,k}&\text{if }j\neq i,\\
\xi_{i,k}&\text{if }j=i,\end{cases}

where
\xi_{i,k}
is a chosen projection of
x_{i,k}
onto the two-dimensional regular set
R(u_{i,k})
restricted to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Note that
|\hat{x}_{k}-\zeta_{k}|=|\hat{x}_{i,k}-\xi_{i,k}|
. Let us also define

p_{k}^{0}:=\frac{\zeta_{k}-\hat{x}_{k}}{r_{k}}.

Furthermore, we set

\delta_{k}:=\delta_{i,k},\qquad u_{k}:=u_{i,k},\qquad U_{k}:=U_{i,k}.

Finally, fixed
\varepsilon\in(0,1-\alpha)
, let us introduce the following quantity (which depends on the index
i
):

\delta_{k}^{*}:=\inf\Big\{\rho\in(0,1/8):N(\zeta_{k},u_{k},\rho)=1+\varepsilon\big\}=\inf\Big\{\rho\in(0,1/8):N(p_{k}^{0},U_{k},\rho/r_{k})=1+\varepsilon\Big\},

which plays the same role as the
critical radius
in
[
26
, Definition 2.10]
.

Heuristically, for every
2
-dimensional component
u_{i,k}
, the parameter
\delta_{k}
measures the distance to the regular set, while
\delta_{k}^{*}
characterizes the scale at which the singular set becomes visible in the blow-up limit. Consequently, three distinct geometric scenarios occur:

i)

If
\delta_{k}\geq C>0
, the blow-up sequence remains away from the zero set of
u_{k}
. In this case,
u_{k}
behaves as a non-degenerate coefficient, and the analysis falls within the classical uniformly elliptic regularity theory.

ii)

If
\delta_{k}\to 0
and
\delta_{k}^{*}\geq C>0
, the regular part
R(u_{k})
is captured at a microscopic scale, whereas the singular set remains far away and does not affect the blow-up procedure. Hence, we can adapt the regularity theory developed in
[
24
]
.

iii)

If
\delta_{k}\to 0
and
\delta_{k}^{*}\to 0
, both the regular and the singular parts actively influence the blow-up limit. In this regime, to control the linear term
I_{k}
, we invoke Lemma
3.4
to project
\nabla w_{k}(\hat{x}_{k})
onto
R(u_{k})
. By exploiting the conormal boundary condition satisfied by
w_{k}
(see (
4.7
)), we successfully achieve a quantitative control on the oscillation of
\nabla w_{k}(\hat{x}_{k})
, which allows us to conclude the proof.

4.1.1.
The uniformly elliptic case

Let us suppose that
\delta_{k}\geq C>0
uniformly in
k
, which means that the blow-up points stay away from the zero set of
u_{k}
. In this case, one has that
H(\hat{x}_{k},u_{k},r_{k})^{1/2}\geq C>0
and

|\nabla U_{k}(x)|=\frac{r_{k}|\nabla u_{k}(\hat{x}_{k}+r_{k}x)|}{H(\hat{x}_{k},u_{k},r_{k})^{1/2}}\leq Cr_{k},

hence, combining this estimates with (
4.3
), it readily follows that

I_{k}=\Big|\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int U_{k}(x)\nabla U_{k}(x)\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\hat{x}_{k})\phi(x)\,dx\Big|\leq Cr_{k}^{1-\alpha}\to 0.

4.1.2.
The regular case

In this case we assume that
\delta_{k}\to 0
and
\delta_{k}^{*}\geq\rho>0
, meaning that the blow-up points remain at a positive distance from the
singular set
of
u_{k}
. We also emphasize that the proof for this case has been given in
[
26
, §2.5]
and holds true in every dimension
n\geq 2
. We include it here for completeness and because we adopt a slightly different and more concise approach. We distinguish two subcases.

\bullet
Case
\delta_{k}/r_{k}\leq C
.

In such a case,
\hat{x}_{k}=\zeta_{k}
, which implies that the centers of the blow-up sequence lie on the regular set
R(u_{k})
.

For every
\beta\in(0,1)
and
x\in\operatorname{spt}(\phi)
, by applying the doubling condition to
u_{k}
(see Lemma
2.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla U_{k}(x)-\nabla U_{k}(0)|

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{k}|\nabla u_{k}(\zeta_{k}+r_{k}x)-\nabla u_{k}(\zeta_{k})|}{H(\zeta_{k},u_{k},r_{k})^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{k}^{1+\beta}\frac{[\nabla u_{k}]_{C^{0,\beta}(B_{\rho/2})}}{H(\zeta_{k},u_{k},r_{k})^{1/2}}\leq Cr_{k}^{1+\beta}\frac{H(\zeta_{k},u_{k},\rho)^{1/2}}{H(\zeta_{k},u_{k},r_{k})^{1/2}}\leq Cr_{k}^{\beta-\varepsilon}.

Then, by invoking (
4.3
) and the conormal boundary condition
\nabla U_{k}(0)\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})=0
satisfied by
w_{k}
on the regular set
{R}(u_{k})
, we deduce

\displaystyle I_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\Big|\int U_{k}(x)\nabla U_{k}(x)\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\phi(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int|U_{k}(x)||\nabla U_{k}(x)-\nabla U_{k}(0)||(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})||\phi(x)|\,dx\leq Cr_{k}^{\beta-\alpha-\varepsilon}\to 0,

by choosing
\beta>\alpha+\varepsilon
.

\bullet
Case
\delta_{k}/r_{k}\to\infty
.

In this case, the blow-up sequence remains at a distance of
\delta_{k}/r_{k}
from the regular set
R(u_{k})
.

We begin by recalling the following lemma, which holds true in every dimension
n\geq 2
and provides control over the oscillation of the normal vector to the regular set.

Lemma 4.4

(
[
26
, Lemma 2.12]
)
.

Let
\mu>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1-\alpha)
,
\rho\in(0,1/8)
. Then, there exists
\bar{r}\in(0,\rho)
, depending on
\mu
,
\alpha
and
\varepsilon
such that the following holds true. Let us suppose that
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
,
x_{0}\in{R}(u)\cap B_{3/4}
such that

\quad L_{A}u=0\text{ in }B_{1},\quad\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}=1,\quad N(x_{0},u,r)\leq 1+\varepsilon,\quad\text{for every }r\in(0,\rho).

Then,

(4.10)

[\nabla u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\leq\frac{\mu}{r^{\alpha}}|\nabla u(x_{0})|,\quad\text{for every }r\in(0,\bar{r}).

Rescaling the inequality (
4.10
) to the function
U_{k}
, we obtain

(4.11)

[\nabla U_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{\bar{r}/r_{k}}(p_{k}^{0}))}\leq\mu\frac{r_{k}^{\alpha}}{\bar{r}^{\alpha}}|\nabla U_{k}(p_{k}^{0})|.

Using
\nabla U_{k}(p_{k}^{0})\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})=0
and (
4.11
), we deduce

\displaystyle I_{k}

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int_{B_{R}}U_{k}(x)\nabla U_{k}(x)\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\hat{x}_{k})\phi(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int_{B_{R}}U_{k}(x)\nabla U_{k}(x)\cdot\Big((A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\hat{x}_{k})-(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})+(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\phi(x)\Big)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int_{B_{R}}|U_{k}(x)||\nabla U_{k}(x)||(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\hat{x}_{k})-(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})|\,dx

\displaystyle+C\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int_{B_{R}}|U_{k}(x)||(\nabla U_{k}(x)-\nabla U_{k}(p_{k}^{0}))\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}}{r_{k}}\Big)^{\alpha}\int_{B_{R}}|U_{k}(x)||\nabla U_{k}(x)|\,dx+Cr_{k}^{-\alpha}[\nabla U_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{\bar{r}/r_{k}}(p_{k}^{0}))}\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}}{r_{k}}\Big)^{\alpha}\int_{B_{R}}|U_{k}|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}}{r_{k}}\Big)^{\alpha}\|\nabla U_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+C\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}}{r_{k}}\Big)^{\alpha}|\nabla U_{k}(p_{k}^{0})|

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}}{r_{k}}\Big)^{\alpha}\|\nabla U_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\delta_{k}/r_{k}})}.

To show that
I_{k}\to 0
, we invoke Lemma
4.1
. In fact, rescaling (
4.1
) in terms of
U_{k}
yields

(4.12)

\|\nabla U_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\delta_{k}/r_{k}})}\leq C\frac{r_{k}}{\delta_{k}}|U_{k}(0)|\leq C\frac{r_{k}}{\delta_{k}},

from which the desired conclusion follows.

4.1.3.
The singular case

Let us now assume that
\delta_{k}\to 0
and
\delta_{k}^{*}\to 0
, meaning that the
singular set
becomes
visible
in the blow-up limit. We emphasize that this case is also treated in
[
26
]
, where the authors prove analogous results using techniques and tools that are two-dimensional. Therefore, the arguments developed in the present section can be viewed as a generalization of the two-dimensional results to our setting.

By using Lemma
3.4
, we obtain that there exists a new point
p_{k}^{1}\in B_{R^{\prime}}\cap{R}(U_{k})
such that

|\nu_{k}^{0}\cdot\nu_{k}^{1}|\leq 1-\varepsilon^{\prime},\quad\text{ where }\,\nu_{k}^{i}:=\frac{\nabla U_{k}(p_{k}^{i})}{|\nabla U_{k}(p_{k}^{i})|},\,\text{for }i=0,1.

Let us define
\zeta_{k}^{1}:=r_{k}p_{k}^{1}+\hat{x}_{k}
, which represents the point
p_{k}^{1}
at the original scale, noting that this point remains close to
\zeta_{k}
as
|\zeta_{k}^{1}-\zeta_{k}|\leq C\delta_{k}^{*}
by Lemma
3.4
. We then introduce the unitary vector

(4.13)

\tau_{k}:=\frac{\nu_{k}^{1}-(\nu_{k}^{0}\cdot\nu_{k}^{1})\nu_{k}^{0}}{|\nu_{k}^{1}-(\nu_{k}^{0}\cdot\nu_{k}^{1})\nu_{k}^{0}|},

and observe that

|\nu_{k}^{1}-(\nu_{k}^{0}\cdot\nu_{k}^{1})\nu_{k}^{0}|\geq 1-|\nu_{k}^{0}\cdot\nu_{k}^{1}|\geq\varepsilon^{\prime},\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{k}\cdot\nu_{k}^{0}=0.

\bullet
Case
\delta_{k}/r_{k}\leq C
and
\delta_{k}^{*}/r_{k}\leq C
.

In this case, we consider a normalized blow-up sequence
W_{k}
which not contains the following linear terms:

\nabla W_{k}(0)\cdot\nu^{0}_{k},\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla W_{k}(0)\cdot\tau_{k},

noticing that
\text{span}(\nu_{k},\tau_{k})=\text{span}(e_{2i-1},e_{2i})
. Next, by using the conormal boundary condition (
4.7
), which implies

\nabla u_{k}(\zeta_{k})\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})=\nabla u_{k}(\zeta_{k}^{1})\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k}^{1})=0,

we have that

A_{k}(\zeta_{k})\nabla W_{k}(0)\cdot\nu^{0}_{k}=0,

and

\displaystyle|A_{k}(\zeta_{k})\nabla W_{k}(0)\cdot\tau_{k}|=\Big|\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\cdot\tau_{k}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\cdot\frac{\nu_{k}^{1}-\nu_{k}^{0}(\nu_{k}^{0}\cdot\nu_{k}^{1})}{|\nu_{k}^{1}-\nu_{k}^{0}(\nu_{k}^{0}\cdot\nu_{k}^{1})|}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\cdot\frac{\nu_{k}^{1}}{|\nu_{k}^{1}-\nu_{k}^{0}(\nu_{k}^{0}\cdot\nu_{k}^{1})|}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})-(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k}^{1})|}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\cdot\Big|\frac{\nu_{k}^{1}}{|\nu_{k}^{1}-\nu_{k}^{0}(\nu_{k}^{0}\cdot\nu_{k}^{1})|}\Big|\leq\frac{L}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}^{*}}{r_{k}}\Big)^{\alpha}\leq C.

Hence, we have shown that the new sequence
W_{k}
satisfies

\lambda|\nabla W_{k}(0)|\leq|A_{k}(\zeta_{k})\nabla W_{k}(0)|\leq C.

Then, the compactness argument involving the first-order expansion at zero of the sequence
W_{k}
, as shown earlier, also applies to this new sequence, implying that
W_{k}\to W
in
C^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for some possibly different limit
W
which still satisfies the properties established above. With this new choice, we obtain that
I_{i,k}=0
, since
W_{k}
does not contain the linear part corresponding to the directions
e_{2i-1}
and
{e_{2i}}
.

\bullet
Case
\delta_{k}/r_{k}\leq C
and
\delta_{k}^{*}/r_{k}\to\infty
.

By using the boundary condition (
4.7
) and recalling who is
\tau_{k}
(see (
4.13
)), we deduce

\displaystyle I_{k}

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int_{B_{R}}U_{k}(x)\nabla U_{k}(x)\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\phi(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\Big|\int_{B_{R}}U_{k}(x)\partial_{\nu_{k}}U_{k}(x)(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\cdot{\nu_{k}}\phi(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{B_{R}}U_{k}(x)\partial_{\tau_{k}}U_{k}(x)(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\cdot{\tau_{k}}\phi(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\Big|\int_{B_{R}}U_{k}(x)\partial_{\tau_{k}}U_{k}(x)(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\cdot\frac{\nu_{k}^{1}}{|\nu_{k}^{1}-\nu_{k}^{0}(\nu_{k}^{0}\cdot\nu_{k}^{1})|}\phi(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\Big|\int_{B_{R}}U_{k}(x)\partial_{\tau_{k}}U_{k}(x)\Big((A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})-(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k}^{1})\Big)\cdot\frac{\nu_{k}^{1}}{|\nu_{k}^{1}-\nu_{k}^{0}(\nu_{k}^{0}\cdot\nu_{k}^{1})|}\phi(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}^{*}}{r_{k}}\Big)^{\alpha}\int_{B_{R}}|U_{k}(x)||\partial_{\tau_{k}}U_{k}(x)|\,dx\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}^{*}}{r_{k}}\Big)^{\alpha}[\partial_{\tau_{k}}U_{k}]_{C^{0,\beta}(B_{\delta_{k}^{*}/r_{k}})}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{r_{k}}{\delta_{k}^{*}}\Big)^{\beta-\alpha-\varepsilon}\to 0,

where in the last inequality we used that
\partial_{\tau_{k}}U_{k}(0)=0
, since
\tau_{k}\cdot\nu_{k}=0
on the regular set
{R}(U_{k})
and the following scaling invariant Schauder estimates combined with the doubling property of
u_{k}
(see Lemma
2.2
),

[\nabla U_{k}]_{C^{0,\beta}(B_{\delta_{k}^{*}/r_{k}})}\leq C\Big(\frac{r_{k}}{\delta_{k}^{*}}\Big)^{1+\beta}\frac{H(\hat{x}_{k},u_{k},\delta_{k}^{*})}{H(\hat{x}_{k},u_{k},r_{k})}\leq C\Big(\frac{r_{k}}{\delta_{k}^{*}}\Big)^{\beta-\varepsilon},

choosing
\beta\in(0,1)
such that
\beta-\alpha-\varepsilon>0
.

\bullet
Case
\delta_{k}/r_{k}\to\infty
and
\delta_{k}^{*}/\delta_{k}\leq C
.

First, by using (
4.12
), namely,
\|\nabla U_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\delta_{k}/r_{k}})}\leq C{r_{k}}/{\delta_{k}},
we get

\displaystyle I_{k}

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int_{B_{R}}U_{k}(x)\nabla U_{k}(x)\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\hat{x}_{k})\phi(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\Big|\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int_{B_{R}}U_{k}(x)|\nabla U_{k}(x)|\cdot|(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\hat{x}_{k})-(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})|\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle+C\Big|\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int_{B_{R}}U_{k}(x)\nabla U_{k}(x)\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}}{r_{k}}\Big)^{\alpha}\|\nabla U_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\delta_{k}/r_{k}})}+C\Big|\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int_{B_{R}}U_{k}(x)\nabla U_{k}(x)\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{r_{k}}{\delta_{k}}\Big)^{1-\alpha}+C\Big|\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int_{B_{R}}U_{k}(x)\nabla U_{k}(x)\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\,dx\Big|.

Let us define

II_{k}:=\Big|\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int_{B_{R}}U_{k}(x)\nabla U_{k}(x)\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\,dx\Big|.

Using the same computations as in the previous case and (
4.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle II_{k}

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\int_{B_{R}}U_{k}(x)\nabla U_{k}(x)\cdot(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\Big|\int_{B_{R}}\Big(U_{k}(x)\partial_{\nu_{k}}U_{k}(x)(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\cdot{\nu_{k}}+U_{k}(x)\partial_{\tau_{k}}U_{k}(x)(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\cdot{\tau_{k}}\Big)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\Big|\int_{B_{R}}U_{k}(x)\partial_{\tau_{k}}U_{k}(x)(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})\cdot\frac{\nu_{k}^{1}}{|\nu_{k}^{1}-\nu_{k}^{0}(\nu_{k}^{0}\cdot\nu_{k}^{1})|}\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{k}^{\alpha}[\nabla w_{k}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1})}}\Big|\int_{B_{R}}U_{k}(x)\partial_{\tau_{k}}U_{k}(x)\Big((A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k})-(A_{k}\nabla w_{k})(\zeta_{k}^{1})\Big)\cdot\frac{\nu_{k}^{1}}{|\nu_{k}^{1}-\nu_{k}^{0}(\nu_{k}^{0}\cdot\nu_{k}^{1})|}\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}^{*}}{r_{k}}\Big)^{\alpha}\|\partial_{\tau_{k}}U_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\delta_{k}/r_{k}})}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}^{*}}{r_{k}}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big(\frac{r_{k}}{\delta_{k}}\Big)\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{r_{k}}{\delta_{k}}\Big)^{1-\alpha}\to 0,

and we conclude that
I_{k}\to 0
.

\bullet
Case
\delta_{k}/r_{k}\to\infty
and
\delta_{k}^{*}/\delta_{k}\to\infty
.

First, we need the following scaling invariant estimates combined with the doubling property applied to
U_{k}
(see Lemma
2.2
) with balls
B_{\delta_{k}^{*}/r_{k}}
and
B_{\delta_{k}/r_{k}}

\displaystyle[\nabla U_{k}]_{C^{0,\beta}(B_{\delta_{k}^{*}/r_{k}}(p_{k}^{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{r_{k}}{\delta_{k}^{*}}\Big)^{1+\beta}\Big(\fint_{B_{\delta_{k}^{*}/r_{k}}(p_{k}^{0})}U_{k}^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}\leq C\Big(\frac{r_{k}}{\delta_{k}^{*}}\Big)^{1+\beta}\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}^{*}}{\delta_{k}}\Big)^{1+\varepsilon}\Big(\fint_{B_{\delta_{k}/r_{k}}(p_{k}^{0})}U_{k}^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}.

Next, let us observe that

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{B_{\delta_{k}/r_{k}}(p_{k}^{0})}U_{k}^{2}(x)\,dx\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\Big(\fint_{B_{\delta_{k}/r_{k}}(p_{k}^{0})}(U_{k}(x)-U_{k}(p_{k}^{0}))^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C{\frac{\delta_{k}}{r_{k}}}[U_{k}]_{C^{0,1}(B_{\delta_{k}/r_{k}}(p_{k}^{0}))}\leq C{\frac{\delta_{k}}{r_{k}}}[U_{k}]_{C^{0,1}(B_{2\delta_{k}/r_{k}}(0))}\leq C|U_{k}(0)|,

where in the last inequality we argue exactly as in Lemma
4.1
, remarking that
B_{\delta_{k}/r_{k}}(p_{k}^{0})\subset B_{2\delta_{k}/r_{k}}(0)
.

Hence, we proceed as in the previous case, but for the last line in the estimate of
II_{k}
we obtain

\displaystyle II_{k}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}^{*}}{r_{k}}\Big)^{\alpha}\int_{B_{R}}|\partial_{\tau_{k}}U_{k}(x)-\partial_{\tau_{k}}U_{k}(p_{k}^{0})|\,dx\leq C\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}^{*}}{r_{k}}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}}{r_{k}}\Big)^{\beta}[\partial_{\tau_{k}}U_{k}]_{C^{0,\beta}(B_{\delta_{k}^{*}/r_{k}}(p_{k}^{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{r_{k}}{\delta_{k}^{*}}\Big)^{1+\beta-\alpha}\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}}{r_{k}}\Big)^{\beta}\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}^{*}}{\delta_{k}}\Big)^{1+\varepsilon}\Big(\fint_{B_{\delta_{k}/r_{k}}(p_{k}^{0})}U_{k}^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}\leq C\Big(\frac{r_{k}}{\delta_{k}}\Big)^{1-\alpha}\Big(\frac{\delta_{k}}{\delta_{k}^{*}}\Big)^{\beta-\varepsilon-\alpha}\to 0,

since
\beta-\varepsilon-\alpha>0
. Thus, in all cases, we have shown that
I_{k}
vanishes as
k\to\infty
, which concludes the proof of Lemma
4.3
. By repeating this argument for each term
I_{i,k}
, we deduce that the right-hand side of (
4.9
) vanishes as
k\to\infty
, completing the proof.
∎
