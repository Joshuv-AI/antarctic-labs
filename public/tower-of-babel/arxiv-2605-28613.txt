Implicit Regularization in Perturbed Deep Matrix Factorization: Spectral Conditions and Stability
Jingzhe Wang, Hung-Hsu Chou

Abstract
This paper studies the stability of low-rank implicit regularization in perturbed deep matrix factorization, where the target matrix is corrupted by a noise matrix. We first derive sufficient spectral conditions under which gradient descent exhibits a low-rank phase in the noiseless setting. These conditions show how the target spectrum, initialization, and step size jointly determine the existence of a nonempty low-rank interval. We then analyze the perturbed gradient descent dynamics, proving convergence guarantees and quantifying how the perturbation affects iteration complexity and eigenvalue recovery. Finally, we show that the low-rank phase persists under perturbation, with explicit dependence on the perturbation size. Numerical experiments support the theoretical findings.

implicit regularization, deep matrix factorization, gradient descent, low-rank approximation, spectral stability, nonconvex optimization

Introduction

Deep learning has achieved remarkable success across a wide range of applications, yet its highly overparameterized models and nonconvex training objectives remain challenging to understand theoretically. In particular, it is often unclear why gradient-based methods select solutions that generalize well among many possible minimizers. The work of Saxe et al.
[

Much of this literature focuses on analytically convenient regimes, such as gradient flow, infinitesimal initialization, or asymptotic training time. These settings reveal how factorized parametrizations can favor low-complexity, and often low-rank, solutions. By contrast, finite-step gradient descent with finite initialization requires more quantitative control. Chou et al.
[
4

gave a non-asymptotic analysis of this setting and identified spectral conditions under which gradient descent exhibits a transient low-rank phase. However, these conditions do not by themselves give a transparent characterization of when the predicted low-rank interval is nonempty.

Our work extends this line of analysis in two directions. We first derive explicit spectral, initialization, and step-size conditions ensuring a nonempty low-rank interval in the noiseless problem. We then prove that this low-rank implicit regularization is stable under additive perturbations of the ground-truth matrix, with quantitative bounds on the shifted interval and the resulting approximation error.

Preliminaries

2.1
Deep Linear Networks and Deep Matrix Factorization

We now make the connection between deep learning and deep matrix factorization explicit.

Multilayer perceptron

One of the most fundamental neural network models in deep learning is the multilayer perceptron (MLP), a function
f_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{\text{in}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{\text{out}}}
parameterized by learnable parameters
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
. An
N
-layer MLP is defined as the composition

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle=g_{N}\circ\cdots\circ g_{1}(x),

\displaystyle g_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=\sigma(W_{j}x+b_{j}),

where
W_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{j}\times n_{j-1}}
are the weight matrices,
b_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{j}}
are the bias vectors, and

\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is the activation function introducing the
nonlinearity
such as ReLU or hyperbolic function. The trainable parameters
\theta
, consisting of all weights and biases, are obtained by minimizing some loss function
\mathcal{L}(\theta)
via some algorithm, e.g. (stochastic) gradient descent. Here the loss function
\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
takes the form

\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\phi(f_{\theta}(x_{i}),y_{i}),

(1)

where
(x_{i},y_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
is the training data and the common choices of
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n_{N}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
include the squared loss, logistic loss, and cross-entropy loss.

Linear networks

Since the general MLP is hard to analyze in general due to the non-linearity, many theoretical works concentrate on the simplified case of
linear neural networks

[
1
,
2
,
3
,
12
,
16
]
, where
\sigma(x)=x
and
b_{j}=0
. In other words, the linear networks are the function of the form

f_{\theta}(x)=W_{N}W_{N-1}\cdots W_{1}x.

Deep matrix factorization

In the case of quadratic loss, i.e.
\phi(y,y^{\prime})=\|y-y^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}
, (
1
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(W_{1},\cdots,W_{N})=\|W_{N}\cdots W_{1}X-Y\|_{F}^{2}

(2)

where
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}\times m}
and
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{N}\times m}
have columns
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
, respectively.
When
X
has full row rank and
XX^{\top}\approx I
, e.g. normalized isotropic gaussian,
\|W_{N}\cdots W_{1}X-Y\|_{F}^{2}\approx\|W_{N}\cdots W_{1}-\widehat{W}\|_{F}^{2}
where
\widehat{W}=YX^{\dagger}
.
This leads to the deep matrix factorization optimization

\min_{W_{1},...,W_{N}}\mathcal{L}(W_{1},\cdots,W_{N}),\quad\mathcal{L}(W_{1},\cdots,W_{N}):=\big\|W_{N}\cdots W_{1}-\widehat{W}\big\|_{F}^{2}.

(3)

Note that the loss in (
3
) is nonconvex and has critical points that are not global minimizers, such as points with at least two zero factors
W_{j}
. In fact, under identical positive identity initialization,
W_{j}(0)=\alpha I
for all
j
with
\alpha>0
, the gradient flow and gradient descent dynamics

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{j}(t)

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{W_{j}(t)}\mathcal{L}(W_{1}(t),\cdots,W_{N}(t))

(gradient flow)

(4)

\displaystyle W_{j}(k+1)

\displaystyle=W_{j}(k)-\eta_{k}\nabla_{W_{j}(k)}\mathcal{L}(W_{1}(k),\cdots,W_{N}(k))

(gradient descent)

(5)

can at best converge to the projection of
\widehat{W}
onto the space of positive definite matrices. In
[
4
]
the authors proved both the identical and non-identical initialization, for simplicity in this paper we will study the gradient descent with the identical initialization.

Prior Results on Low-Rank Implicit Regularization

The implicit regularization of deep matrix factorization can be characterized by the following.

Theorem 1

\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,c_{N})

\eta<\big[(3N-2)\max\{\alpha^{N-2},\lambda_{1}^{2-\frac{2}{N}}\}\big]^{-1}

Define
L^{\prime}=\max\{\ell\in[n]\colon\varepsilon^{\prime}\lambda_{\ell}>\alpha^{N}\}

\displaystyle\left|r(\widehat{W}_{L})-r(W(k))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon r(\widehat{W}_{L})+\frac{2(L^{\prime}-L)}{c_{N}}\frac{\lambda_{L+1}}{\lambda_{1}}\varepsilon^{\prime}+(n-L^{\prime})\frac{2\alpha^{N}}{\varepsilon^{\prime}\lambda_{1}}.

(7)

and

are defined in

Remark
.

Although the results are stated for symmetric matrices, non-symmetric matrices can be studied spectrally through the self-adjoint dilation

\begin{bmatrix}0&\widehat{W}\\
\widehat{W}^{\top}&0\end{bmatrix}

whose eigenvalues are the signed singular values
\pm\sigma_{i}(\widehat{W})

Theorem
1

L

The explicit formula for

(8)

T_{1}(\lambda_{L+1},\epsilon^{\prime},\alpha,\eta)=\frac{1}{2\eta\lambda_{L+1}}\left[\ln\left(\frac{\lambda_{L+1}}{\alpha^{2}}-1\right)-\ln\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\prime}}-1\right)\right],

The general form can be found in

The above expression does not make transparent the conditions on the spectrum

3
Existence of Low-Rank Intervals

We will focus on
T_{0}
since it involves multiple maximum and hence is more difficult to express explicitly. The expression can be much simplified if
T_{2}^{\text{Id}}
exhibits certain monotonicity with respect to
\lambda

Lemma 2
.

Suppose
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and
\lambda_{1}>\frac{1}{16}
. If
T_{2}^{\text{Id}}(\lambda_{\ell},\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{\ell}}}{8}\epsilon,\alpha,\eta)
decreases with respect to
\{\lambda_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in[L]}
, then
T_{0}=T_{2}^{\text{Id}}\left(\lambda_{L},\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{L}}}{8}\epsilon,\alpha,\eta\right).

The decreasing assumption fits the natural intuition, since recovering small eigenvalues usually require more time. However, it is non-trivial to establish this statement rigorously, and the remainder of this section is devoted to proving that
T_{2}^{\text{Id}}(\lambda,\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{8}\epsilon,\alpha,\eta)
decreases with respect to
\{\lambda_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in[L]}
. For notation simplicity, we introduce a function
T(x)
defined as

T(x)=T_{2}^{\text{Id}}\left(x,\frac{\sqrt{x}}{8}\epsilon,\alpha,\eta\right)

(11)

where
\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime},\alpha,\eta
are fixed and within the range specified by Theorem
1
(
\epsilon^{\prime}
only appear in
T_{1}
so we do not need to worry about it too much for now). Note that the choice of those parameters also imposes restriction on the range of

. In particular, since
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,\frac{1}{3})
,
\alpha^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}\lambda_{L+1}
, and
\eta<[4\max\{1,\lambda_{1}\}]^{-1}
, we have

x\in D=\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\epsilon^{\prime}},\frac{1}{4\eta}\right)

(12)

assuming
\lambda_{1}\geq 1
, which can always be achieved by re-scaling but is nevertheless stated explicitly for conciseness. Note that the condition

implicitly ensures that
T(x)
is well-defined. To further simplify the analysis, we decompose the function
T
into

T(x)=A(x)+B(x)+C(x),

(13)

where

A(x)=\frac{1}{2\eta x}\left(\ln\left(\frac{x}{\alpha^{2}}-1\right)-\ln 2\right),\kern 5.0ptB(x)=\left\lceil\sqrt{\frac{x}{3}}\frac{1}{\alpha}\right\rceil,\kern 5.0ptC(x)=\frac{K_{\epsilon}}{\left|\ln\left(1-\frac{2}{3}\eta x\right)\right|}

(14)

and

K_{\epsilon}:=\ln\frac{8}{\epsilon}-\left|\ln\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}\right)\right|=\ln\left(\frac{8}{\epsilon}\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}\right)\right)>\ln 1=0.

(15)

Although
A
and
C
are differentiable,

is not continuous and have many jumps. This observation implies that
T
cannot be decreasing for all

, because even if
A
and
C
are decreasing functions, occasionally

will cause a drastic increase. However, Lemma
2
only require
T
to be decreasing with respect to
\{\lambda_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in[L]}
. We will show that when the gaps between eigenvalues are sufficiently large, the effect of

and we can still establish the monotonicity statement we are aiming for. To analyze

as

I_{z}:=\left\{x\geq 0:\left\lceil\sqrt{\frac{x}{3}}\frac{1}{\alpha}\right\rceil=z\right\}

Lemma 3
.

Suppose
x_{1}<x_{2}
are
m

x_{1}\in I_{z-m}
and
x_{2}\in I_{z}

The quantity

Lemma 4

The function

Suppose

Monotonicity of

By the chain rule,

By setting

6
Conclusion

This paper analyzed the stability of low-rank implicit regularization in perturbed deep matrix factorization. We first revisited the noiseless setting and derived spectral conditions ensuring the existence of a nonempty low-rank interval. These conditions make explicit how the target spectrum, initialization, and step size govern the emergence of low-rank behavior along the gradient descent trajectory.

We then studied the perturbed problem, where the target matrix is corrupted by additive noise. By analyzing the perturbed gradient descent dynamics at the eigenvalue level, we established convergence guarantees, quantified the effect of the perturbation size on iteration complexity, and characterized the recovery of nonnegative eigenvalues. Building on these results, we proved that the low-rank phase is stable under controlled perturbations: the effective rank remains close to that of the rank-
L
approximation of the noiseless target over a perturbed low-rank interval, with explicit dependence on the perturbation size. We further quantified the shift of this interval and the resulting low-rank approximation error.

Our results show that the implicit regularization behavior observed in noiseless deep matrix factorization is robust to bounded perturbations of the target matrix. The numerical experiments support the theoretical predictions and demonstrate the role of spectral structure in determining when this robustness is observable. Future work includes extending the analysis to nonsymmetric or indefinite perturbations, deriving sharper probabilistic bounds for concrete random noise models, and developing analogous stability results for broader classes of nonlinear networks.
