A Single Stepsize Suffices for Unprojected Linear TD(0): Simultaneous Robust and Fast Rates via Polyak–Ruppert Averaging
Wei-Cheng Lee, Francesco Orabona

Abstract
We study linear TD(0) under Markovian sampling, where data are generated along a single trajectory. We provide high-probability guarantees for a plain unprojected TD(0) algorithm with Polyak–Ruppert (PR) averaging, using a single stepsize schedule η t ∝ 1 / ( τ mix ​ log ⁡ ( t ) ​ t ) \eta_{t}\propto 1/(\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\log(t)\,\sqrt{t}) that depends on mixing time but requires no prior knowledge of the curvature parameter ω \omega . Our first result shows that such a choice of the stepsize guarantees that the TD(0) iterates are automatically and uniformly bounded with high probability , without projections and without any stability argument based on ω \omega . Building on this result, we establish a simultaneous high-probability convergence guarantee for the PR average: the same stepsize yields both a robust curvature-free 𝒪 ~ ​ ( τ mix / T ) \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}/\sqrt{T}) rate and a fast curvature-dependent 𝒪 ~ ​ ( τ mix 2 / ( ω ​ T ) ) \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}^{2}/(\omega T)) rate, with the bound taking the minimum of the two. The core technical ingredient is a Poisson-equation toolkit for geometrically mixing Markov chains, which decomposes Markov noise into a martingale term plus a controlled remainder and enables a new self-bounding inductive argument for pathwise stability.

Introduction

Temporal-difference (TD) learning is one of the central algorithmic primitives in reinforcement learning, used for policy evaluation and as a building block for control methods such as actor–critic. When the state space is large, TD is typically combined with function approximation; in the linear case, the resulting TD(0) recursion admits a clean stochastic-approximation interpretation and converges
asymptotically under standard conditions
(
32
;
33
;
9
)
.

Despite this classical theory, obtaining
finite-time
,
high-probability
guarantees for TD(0) remains challenging, especially in the practically relevant
Markovian sampling
regime where data are generated along a single trajectory.

A major obstacle is that TD(0) is not a standard stochastic gradient method:
Even in the linear setting, the update is generally biased and non-symmetric, and the noise is temporally correlated. Moreover, the magnitude of the TD(0) updates depends on the magnitude of the iterates.
Therefore, existing non-asymptotic analyses rely on additional structure to control the iterates. One common approach is to enforce boundedness via
projections
onto a known ball
(
1
, e.g.,)
, which simplifies concentration arguments but changes the algorithm and requires
a priori
knowledge
of a suitable radius (often tied implicitly to the problem curvature).
A second approach is to exploit a
contractive/curvature
structure of the mean dynamics to argue stability without projections; however, this typically yields stepsize conditions or stability bounds that depend on an unknown curvature parameter, which becomes arbitrarily slow when the curvature is arbitrarily small.

Ideally, one would like to use a
single
stepsize rule that
simultaneously
allows us to obtain rates that are
(i)
robust
(curvature-free) and (ii)
fast
when curvature is favorable. Such a stepsize would adapt to the better of the robust and fast high-probability upper bounds, without prior knowledge of the curvature.

Robust and fast rates.

To explain the distinction between robust and fast rates, we first briefly describe the potential function we study
(
22
;
16
)
(formally introduced later in (
2
)),
which naturally captures the TD fixed point error under Markovian sampling.
This potential always has positive curvature
\omega>0
, so
one can hope for a ‘‘fast’’ rate of order
1

1

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\frac{1}{\omega T})
(up to mixing and logarithmic factors).
On the other hand, even without curvature assumptions, one can always achieve a “robust” rate of order
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}})
, again up to mixing and logs. It is obvious that, in the finite-time regime, one rate can be better than the other depending on
\omega
, so both rates should be pursued in different situations.

Now, prior work typically achieves these two regimes with
different
stepsize choices and additional algorithmic modifications
(
1
, e.g.,)
. Moreover, the projection-free high-probability analyses under Markovian noise that we are aware of rely on stability arguments whose constants deteriorate with
\omega

(
27
;
6

.

As far as we know,
obtaining both rates simultaneously with high probability with a single stepsize choice that is independent of
\omega
and without projections has not previously been established for TD(0)
.
In particular, the key technical difficulty in achieving such a result is to control the random iterates,
\bm{\theta}_{t}
,
pathwise
. Without projections, the update magnitude scales with
\|\bm{\theta}_{t}\|
, and a naive concentration analysis becomes circular: to bound the error one needs bounded updates, but bounded updates require bounded iterates. Moreover, the need to obtain robust rates (i.e., independent of
\omega
) rules out any strategy that shows that the iterates are bounded through a contractive argument based on
\omega
.

Our approach: a self-bounding argument via Poisson equations.

In this paper, we show that a simple stepsize, proportional to
\frac{1}{\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\log t\sqrt{t}}
and independent of
\omega
, guarantees that TD(0) achieves both fast and robust rates with high probability.

Our main technical novelty to circumvent the above issues is a new
self-bounding
argument that closes this loop
with high probability

without
appealing to curvature
\omega
and
without
artificial projections.
Concretely, we develop a Poisson-equation toolkit for geometrically mixing Markov chains that decomposes additive Markov noise into a martingale term plus a controlled remainder.
This decomposition allows us to control the Markovian bias terms that appear in the basic potential expansion of TD, and to run an induction in which boundedness at times
\leq t-1
implies boundedness at time
t
.
The resulting uniform bound on
\sup_{t}\ \|\bm{\theta}_{t}\|
holds with high probability and is independent of the curvature
\omega
.

Once boundedness is established, we can analyze the convergence guarantee.
The bounded-iterates event provides the missing ingredient needed to control the quadratic variation of the martingale terms (again through the Poisson decomposition), yielding high-probability bounds for the averaged iterate
\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}
.
Importantly, the
same
stepsize delivers:

•

a curvature-free robust rate of
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}})
, through a classic averaged-stochastic-gradient-descent-like analysis, as one would expect from the choice of the stepsize;

•

a curvature-dependent fast rate
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/(\omega T))
, without requiring
\omega
to set the stepsize, thanks to a Polyak–Ruppert averaging analysis.

Related Work

In this section, we briefly survey the main results on the convergence guarantees for TD learning with linear function approximation, as well as the main tools we use in our proofs. Later, in Section
5
, we will give a detailed comparison in terms of convergence rates.

Early convergence results for TD learning with linear function approximation trace back to
33
, who interpreted TD updates through the lens of stochastic approximation
(
9
)
. While foundational, that theory is largely asymptotic and does not yield explicit non-asymptotic rates. Finite-time guarantees were later developed in a sequence of works
(
8
;
10
;
5
)
, but these analyses typically assume i.i.d. samples drawn from the stationary distribution. This assumption sidesteps the temporal dependence present in most reinforcement-learning pipelines, where data are generated sequentially along a single Markov-chain trajectory. Accounting for such Markovian correlations substantially complicates the analysis, even for TD(0).

The seminal work of
1
provided the first finite-time treatment under Markovian sampling. They obtained both fast and robust rates, using two different stepsizes. However, their arguments (and even subsequent refinements for the robust rates such as
16
) rely on an explicit projection step to keep the iterates controlled.

The need for a projection is removed in subsequent work in the fast regime by exploiting the contractive nature of the update due to the presence of the curvature.
In particular, using a control-theoretic framework,
28
derived finite-time error bounds for linear TD with Markovian data without projections by establishing a contraction-like behavior via Lyapunov theory. However, their stepsize depends on the curvature of the potential function, which is generally unknown in practice. Building on this direction,
23
eliminated the need to know the curvature parameter when choosing stepsizes, at the cost of introducing a data-dropping modification of TD. Related progress has also focused on simplifying proofs and relaxing algorithmic modifications:
19
proposed a streamlined inductive two-step analysis,
15
used exponentially decaying stepsizes to avoid data dropping, and
29
extended fast-rate analyses to neural networks in the NTK regime. More recently,
27
strengthened the analysis of
23
and obtained high-probability bounds without using projections. Closest to the high-probability projection-free line,
3
obtained all-time high-probability control for unprojected TD(
0
) without data dropping. Their analysis, however, remains contractive in nature, and the resulting constants and burn-in depend on the curvature.

The only result that removes the need for projections with stepsizes independent of the curvature of the potential function, and without using curvature in the analysis, is
11
, which proves that the iterates of TD(0) are bounded in expectation. Our approach is inspired by their method, but differs substantially because we need high-probability bounds.

Several closely related works study cheap linear-update methods that are informative but not direct head-to-head baselines for plain TD(
0
).
26
analyze linear composition optimization and gradient TD methods for off-policy MSPBE minimization, using primal–dual updates rather than the semi-gradient TD(
0
) recursion studied here, and obtain adaptive finite-time guarantees but require bounded iterates or prior knowledge of the curvature. In the tabular setting,
13
show that asynchronous Q-learning, and hence tabular TD, can have a leading statistical term matching the i.i.d. sampling complexity, with mixing entering only as an additive transient cost; they also provide data-driven stepsizes that avoid prior knowledge of
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
. These results address the same broad practical question of obtaining fast, stable, cheap linear updates under limited tuning information, but they do not directly apply to unprojected linear TD(0).

As far as we know, there is no prior work that analyzes the possibility of getting both fast and robust rates with a stepsize schedule independent of the curvature and without using projections for linear TD learning.

The use of Polyak–Ruppert averaging in the analysis of TD learning can be traced back at least to
7
. To the best of our knowledge,
8
were the first to leverage this technique to establish finite-time guarantees. In particular, Polyak–Ruppert (tail) averaging has been employed to enable stepsize choices that avoid explicit dependence on the curvature parameter
\omega

(
10
;
23
;
27
, see, e.g.,)
.

Finally, we make use of the Poisson equation to obtain high-probability bounds. This is a standard tool, used widely in the literature on TD learning and stochastic approximation with Markovian noise
(
3
;
2
, see, e.g.,)
.

3
Setting and Assumptions

We work directly with the finite discounted Markov reward process (MRP) induced by a fixed policy
\mu
. Thus the action variables have already been averaged under
\mu
. Let
\mathcal{S}\coloneqq\{1,\dots,n\}
be a finite state space, let
P^{\mu}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to[0,1]
be the induced transition kernel, and let
r:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{R}
be the one-step reward function. Given an initial state
s_{0}
, the state process satisfies
\mathbb{P}(s_{t+1}=j\mid s_{t}=i)=P^{\mu}(i,j)
for
i,j\in\mathcal{S}
. We write
\bm{P}^{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
for the corresponding transition matrix, with entries
\bm{P}^{\mu}(i,j)=P^{\mu}(i,j)
, and fix a discount factor
0<\gamma<1
.

We are interested in the
policy evaluation
problem in reinforcement learning
(
31
;
17
)
. For the MRP induced by
\mu
, the value function is

V^{\mu}(s)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}r(s_{t},s_{t+1})\;\middle|\;s_{0}=s\right].

The value function
\bm{V}^{\mu}
can be viewed as a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and is the unique fixed point of the Bellman expectation operator
T^{\mu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by

(T^{\mu}\bm{V})(s)\coloneqq r^{\mu}(s)+\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}P^{\mu}(s,s^{\prime})\,\bm{V}(s^{\prime}),\qquad s\in\mathcal{S},

where
r^{\mu}(s)\coloneqq\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}P^{\mu}(s,s^{\prime})\,r(s,s^{\prime})
.

When
n
becomes large, solving the Bellman expectation equation via matrix inversion becomes infeasible. Instead, we consider Temporal-Difference (TD) learning with linear function approximation
(
32
)
. Let
\bm{\phi}:\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a fixed feature mapping with
d\ll n
, and let
\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We approximate
\bm{V}^{\mu}
by
\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}}(s)\coloneqq\bm{\theta}^{\top}\bm{\phi}(s)
, or in vector form
\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}}=\bm{\Phi}\bm{\theta}
, where the feature matrix
\bm{\Phi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
has row
\bm{\phi}(s)^{\top}
corresponding to state
s
.

Structural assumption.

Throughout the paper, we impose the following standard condition on the induced MRP and the feature matrix.

Assumption 3.1

(Ergodic induced MRP and full-rank features)
.

The transition matrix
\bm{P}^{\mu}
is irreducible and aperiodic, and the feature matrix
\bm{\Phi}
has full column rank.

Since
\bm{P}^{\mu}
is finite, irreducible, and aperiodic, it admits a unique stationary distribution
\pi
. We write
\bm{D}\coloneqq\mathrm{diag}(\pi)
and use the value-space norm
\left\|{\bm{v}}\right\|_{\bm{D}}\coloneqq\sqrt{\bm{v}^{\top}\bm{D}\bm{v}}
for
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We also define
\Sigma\coloneqq\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{D}\bm{\Phi}
. Under Assumption
3.1
,
\pi(s)>0
for every
s\in\mathcal{S}
, and hence
\Sigma\succ 0
.

For any
\bm{\theta}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the TD(
0
) algorithm is defined for
t\geq 0
by

\bm{\theta}_{t+1}=\bm{\theta}_{t}+\eta_{t}\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{t},Z_{t}),\qquad Z_{t}\coloneqq(s_{t},s_{t+1}),

where
\eta_{t}>0
is the stepsize and

\bm{g}(\bm{\theta},Z_{t})\coloneqq\bigl(r(s_{t},s_{t+1})+\gamma\bm{\phi}(s_{t+1})^{\top}\bm{\theta}-\bm{\phi}(s_{t})^{\top}\bm{\theta}\bigr)\bm{\phi}(s_{t}).

When the dependence on randomness and iterates is clear, we write
\bm{g}_{t}\coloneqq\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{t},Z_{t})
.

The TD(
0
) update can equivalently be written as
\bm{\theta}_{t+1}=\bm{\theta}_{t}+\eta_{t}(\bm{b}_{Z_{t}}-\bm{A}_{Z_{t}}\bm{\theta}_{t})
, where

\bm{A}_{Z_{t}}\coloneqq\bm{\phi}(s_{t})\bigl(\bm{\phi}(s_{t})-\gamma\bm{\phi}(s_{t+1})\bigr)^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},\qquad\bm{b}_{Z_{t}}\coloneqq r(s_{t},s_{t+1})\bm{\phi}(s_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let
\mathcal{Z}\coloneqq\{(i,j)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}:P^{\mu}(i,j)>0\}
be the state space of the transition chain
Z_{t}=(s_{t},s_{t+1})
. Its Markov kernel is

P_{Z}\bigl((i,j),(j^{\prime},k)\bigr)=\begin{cases}P^{\mu}(j,k),&j^{\prime}=j,\\
0,&j^{\prime}\neq j,\end{cases}\qquad(i,j),(j^{\prime},k)\in\mathcal{Z},

and its stationary distribution is
\pi_{Z}(i,j)\coloneqq\pi(i)P^{\mu}(i,j)
for
(i,j)\in\mathcal{Z}
. Define the population TD matrix and vector by
\bm{A}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\pi_{Z}}[\bm{A}_{Z}]
and
\bm{b}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\pi_{Z}}[\bm{b}_{Z}]
.

For
z\in\mathcal{Z}
, define

\bm{\xi}(z)\coloneqq\bm{b}_{z}-\bm{A}_{z}\bm{\theta}^{*},\qquad\bm{\delta}(z)\coloneqq\bm{A}-\bm{A}_{z}.

When
z=Z_{t}
, we write

\bm{\xi}_{t}\coloneqq\bm{\xi}(Z_{t}),\qquad\bm{\delta}_{t}\coloneqq\bm{\delta}(Z_{t}).

As shown in Lemma
3.4
below, Assumption
3.1
implies that
\bm{A}
is nonsingular. Hence the expected TD linear system
\bm{A}\bm{\theta}^{*}=\bm{b}
has a unique solution
\bm{\theta}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It is known that the corresponding value approximation
\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}=\bm{\Phi}\bm{\theta}^{*}
satisfies the projected Bellman equation
(
33
)

\bm{\Phi}\bm{\theta}^{*}=\Pi_{\bm{D}}T^{\mu}(\bm{\Phi}\bm{\theta}^{*}),

where
\Pi_{\bm{D}}
is the orthogonal projection operator onto the subspace
\{\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}:\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
with respect to
\left\|{\cdot}\right\|_{\bm{D}}
. Moreover,

\left\|{\bm{V}^{\mu}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}}\right\|_{\bm{D}}\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left\|{\bm{V}^{\mu}-\Pi_{\bm{D}}\bm{V}^{\mu}}\right\|_{\bm{D}}.

The
mean-path TD update
is defined as

\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta})\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\pi_{Z}}\bigl[\bigl(r(s,s^{\prime})+\gamma\bm{\phi}(s^{\prime})^{\top}\bm{\theta}-\bm{\phi}(s)^{\top}\bm{\theta}\bigr)\bm{\phi}(s)\bigr].

Using
\bm{A}\bm{\theta}^{*}=\bm{b}
, the mean-path TD update is linear in
\bm{e}\coloneqq\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{*}
for all
\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta})=\bm{b}-\bm{A}\bm{\theta}=-\bm{A}(\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{*}),\qquad\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}^{*})=\bm{0}.

(1)

To characterize the convergence of TD iterates
\bm{\theta}_{t}
to the TD fixed point
\bm{\theta}^{*}
, we introduce the Dirichlet semi-norm
\|\bm{x}\|_{\mathrm{Dir}}^{2}\coloneqq\bm{x}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\mathrm{Dir}}\bm{x}
, where

\bm{L}_{\mathrm{Dir}}\coloneqq\bm{D}-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\bm{D}\bm{P}^{\mu}+(\bm{P}^{\mu})^{\top}\bm{D}\bigr).

By Lemma
F.2
,
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Dir}}
is indeed a semi-norm since
\bm{L}_{\mathrm{Dir}}
is positive semidefinite. The potential function
f
that we study
(
22
;
16
)
is defined as

f(\bm{\theta})\coloneqq(1-\gamma)\,\|\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\|_{\bm{D}}^{2}+\gamma\,\|\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\|_{\mathrm{Dir}}^{2}.

(2)

In particular, for
\bm{e}=\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{*}
, since
\bm{e}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{e}=\bm{e}^{\top}\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{e}
, a direct calculation yields

f(\bm{\theta})-f(\bm{\theta}^{*})=\tfrac{1}{2}\bm{e}^{\top}(\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top})\bm{e}=\bm{e}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{e}.

(3)

Moreover, using (
1
), we have

\langle\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}),\bm{\theta}^{*}-\bm{\theta}\rangle=\langle-\bm{A}\bm{e},-\bm{e}\rangle=\bm{e}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{e}=f(\bm{\theta})-f(\bm{\theta}^{*}).

(4)

Since
f(\bm{\theta})-f(\bm{\theta}^{*})\geq 0
for
\bm{\theta}\neq\bm{\theta}^{*}
, the mean-path update direction
\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta})
is aligned with the direction
\bm{\theta}^{*}-\bm{\theta}
. In particular, TD(
0
) under i.i.d. sampling from
\pi_{Z}
acts as a descent method for
f
. Additionally, any convergence guarantee on
f
can be translated to a guarantee in the projected value norm:

\|\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|_{\bm{\Sigma}}^{2}=\|\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\|_{\bm{D}}^{2}\leq\frac{f(\bm{\theta})}{1-\gamma}.

(5)

Constants and structural consequences.

We next collect the constants and matrix properties used in the high-probability analysis. The boundedness constants are fixed by the finite state space, while the mixing and curvature constants follow from Assumption
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2

(Feature and reward bounds)
.

There exist finite constants
\phi_{\infty},r_{\infty}<\infty
such that
\|\bm{\phi}(s)\|\leq\phi_{\infty}
for all
s\in\mathcal{S}
and
|r(s,s^{\prime})|\leq r_{\infty}
for all
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
. For
z=(s,s^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Z}
, set

\varepsilon_{z}\coloneqq r(s,s^{\prime})+\gamma\bm{\phi}(s^{\prime})^{\top}\bm{\theta}^{*}-\bm{\phi}(s)^{\top}\bm{\theta}^{*}.

Then
\bm{\xi}(z)=\varepsilon_{z}\bm{\phi}(s)
and

\|\bm{\xi}(z)\|\leq r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}+2\phi_{\infty}^{2}\|\bm{\theta}^{*}\|.

Lemma 3.3

(Geometric mixing of
(Z_{t})
)
.

The transition chain
Z_{t}=(s_{t},s_{t+1})
has stationary distribution
\pi_{Z}
. Moreover, there exists a finite constant
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\geq 1
such that

\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|P_{Z}^{k}(z,\cdot)-\pi_{Z}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq 4\cdot 2^{-k/\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}},\qquad k\geq 0,

where
P_{Z}^{k}(z,z^{\prime})\coloneqq\mathbb{P}(Z_{k}=z^{\prime}\mid Z_{0}=z)
.

Lemma 3.4

(Curvature of the TD matrix)
.

The symmetric part of
\bm{A}
is positive definite. More precisely,

\frac{\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top}}{2}=(1-\gamma)\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{D}\bm{\Phi}+\gamma\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\mathrm{Dir}}\bm{\Phi}\succeq(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\min}(\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{D}\bm{\Phi})\mathbf{I}_{d}.

Consequently, one may take
\omega=(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\min}(\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{D}\bm{\Phi})>0
, and

f(\bm{\theta})-f(\bm{\theta}^{*})=\bm{e}^{\top}\frac{\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top}}{2}\bm{e}\geq\omega\|\bm{e}\|^{2}=\omega\|\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}.

Thus
f
is
\omega
star-strongly convex around
\bm{\theta}^{*}
.

The first lemma is immediate from the finiteness of
\mathcal{S}
and the triangle inequality. Lemma
3.3
is the standard geometric mixing bound for finite irreducible and aperiodic Markov chains; see, e.g.,
12
. For Lemma
3.4
, expand
\bm{A}=\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{D}(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\bm{P}^{\mu})\bm{\Phi}
and symmetrize. Then Lemma
F.2
gives
\bm{L}_{\mathrm{Dir}}\succeq 0
, while Assumption
3.1
gives
\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{D}\bm{\Phi}\succ 0
.

Remark 3.5
.

For Theorem
4.1
, the curvature conclusion in Lemma
3.4
is not needed. The bounded-iterates argument only requires a sample-path independent reference point
\bm{\theta}^{*}
satisfying

4
Main Results

In this section, we present our main results. We first establish a high-probability uniform bound on the iterates, which is a key step in our convergence analysis. We then derive a simultaneous high-probability convergence rate that is both curvature-free/robust and curvature-aware/fast convergence rate for the Polyak–Ruppert averaged iterate.

\displaystyle c_{\min}(\delta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{A_{1}(\delta)+\sqrt{A^{2}_{1}(\delta)+4A_{2}}}{2},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\rho\coloneqq\frac{2c}{\sqrt{c^{2}-A_{1}(\delta)c-A_{2}}}\,.

(9)

Then, provided that
c>c_{\min}(\delta)
, with probability at least
1-\delta
, we have

\sup_{t\geq 0}\ \left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}}\right\|_{2}\leq\rho R_{\mathrm{base}}\,.

Note that a simple choice of
a_{t}
that satisfies the assumptions of the theorem is
a_{t}=\frac{1}{\ln(t+3)\sqrt{t+1}}
.

We provide the complete proof in Appendix
B
and a proof sketch below.

Proof Sketch.

Fix
\delta>0
and suppress the
\delta
-dependence in the concentration bounds for simplicity. Starting from the definition of the TD(
0
) update, we have

\left\|{\bm{g}_{k}}\right\|=\left\|{\bigl(r_{k}+\gamma\langle\bm{\phi}(s_{k+1}),\bm{\theta}_{k}\rangle-\langle\bm{\phi}(s_{k}),\bm{\theta}_{k}\rangle\bigr)\bm{\phi}(s_{k})}\right\|\leq|r_{k}|\left\|{\bm{\phi}(s_{k})}\right\|+|1+\gamma|\,\left\|{\bm{\phi}(s_{k})}\right\|^{2}\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k}}\right\|.

(10)

Thus, in each iteration, the update magnitude is at most

r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}+2\phi_{\infty}^{2}\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k}}\right\|
, which is of the same order as
\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k}}\right\|
.

Next, expand
\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|^{2}
using the TD(
0
) update and sum from
k=1
to
t
, to obtain

\displaystyle\|\bm{\theta}_{t}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\bm{\theta}_{0}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{t}\eta^{2}_{k-1}\|\bm{g}_{k-1}\|^{2}+2\sum_{k=1}^{t}\eta_{k-1}\langle\bm{g}_{k-1}-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k-1}),\bm{\theta}_{k-1}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum_{k=1}^{t}\eta_{k-1}\langle\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k-1}),\bm{\theta}_{k-1}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\rangle\,.

(11)

To build intuition, suppose for the moment that
\bm{g}_{k}=\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k})
. Then, equation (
4.1
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\|\bm{\theta}_{t}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}-\|\bm{\theta}_{0}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{t}\eta^{2}_{k-1}\|\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k-1})\|^{2}=\mathcal{O}\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^{t}\eta^{2}_{k-1}\left(\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k-1}}\right\|+\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k-1}}\right\|^{2}\right)\right),

where we use
\langle{\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k-1})},{\bm{\theta}_{k-1}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\leq 0
from equation (
4
).
Hence,
\|\bm{\theta}_{t}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}
is governed by a term of the form

\mathcal{O}\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^{t}\eta^{2}_{k-1}\bigl(\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k-1}}\right\|+\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k-1}}\right\|^{2}\bigr)\right)
.
Since
(\eta_{k})
is square-summable, if
\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k}}\right\|
is bounded by
R_{\mathrm{base}}
for all
k\leq t-1
, then
\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|
is also bounded by a constant multiple of
R_{\mathrm{base}}
.
Moreover, by the triangle inequality,

\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}}\right\|\leq\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|+\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|
.
In other words, a bound on
\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k}}\right\|
for all
k\leq t-1
implies a bound at time
t
.
This motivates the following induction hypothesis for some
\rho>2
:

\max_{1\leq k\leq t-1}\ \left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k-1}}\right\|\leq\rho R_{\mathrm{base}}\,.

The core of the proof then becomes to choose the stepsize parameter
c
(and hence
\rho
) so that

\|\bm{\theta}_{t}\|^{2}\leq 2\|\bm{\theta}_{t}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}+2\|\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}\leq\rho^{2}R_{\mathrm{base}}^{2},

where we use the induction hypothesis to control
\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|
.

We now return to the Markovian bias term

\sum_{k=1}^{t}\eta_{k-1}\langle\bm{g}_{k-1}-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k-1}),\bm{\theta}_{k-1}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\rangle

in (
4.1
).
In general, for
\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\sigma(Z_{0},\dots,Z_{t})
,
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}_{k-1}-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k-1})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{k-2}\right]
need not be zero:
\bm{g}_{k-1}
depends on the fresh randomness in
Z_{k-1}
, and the conditional distribution of
Z_{k-1}
given
Z_{k-2}
does not necessarily coincide with the stationary distribution
\pi_{Z}
.
To control this bias, freeze the past iterate
\bm{\theta}_{k-1}
and define the centered scalar forcing term
h_{k-1}(z)\coloneqq\langle\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{k-1},z)-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k-1}),\bm{\theta}_{k-1}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\rangle
. For this forcing term, consider the Poisson equation on the transition chain
Z_{t}
:

u_{k-1}:\mathcal{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad u_{k-1}(z)-\mathbb{E}\!\left[u_{k-1}(Z_{1})\;\middle|\;Z_{0}=z\right]=h_{k-1}(z)\,.

(12)

By Lemma
A.1
, the equation (
12
) is solved by

u_{k-1}^{*}(z)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_{k-1}(Z_{i})\;\middle|\;Z_{0}=z\right]
, and we also have

\left\|{u_{k-1}^{*}}\right\|_{\infty}\coloneqq\sup_{z}\ |u_{k-1}^{*}(z)|\leq 16\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\left\|{h_{k-1}}\right\|_{\infty}=\mathcal{O}\left(\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k-1}}\right\|^{2}\right)\,.

(13)

The solution
u^{*}_{k-1}
provides a convenient decomposition of

\sum_{k=1}^{t}\eta_{k-1}h_{k-1}(Z_{k-1})

into a martingale term
M_{t}
plus a remainder term
R_{t}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{t}\eta_{k-1}h_{k-1}(Z_{k-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{t}\eta_{k-1}\left(u^{*}_{k-1}(Z_{k})-\mathbb{E}\!\left[u^{*}_{k-1}(Z_{k})\;\middle|\;Z_{k-1}\right]+u^{*}_{k-1}(Z_{k-1})-u^{*}_{k-1}(Z_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=M_{t}+R_{t},

(14)

where
\Delta M_{k}\coloneqq\eta_{k-1}(u^{*}_{k-1}(Z_{k})-\mathbb{E}[u^{*}_{k-1}(Z_{k})|Z_{k-1}])
and
M_{t}\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{t}\Delta M_{k}
.
Note that

\left\|{\Delta M_{k}}\right\|_{\infty}=\mathcal{O}(\eta_{k-1}\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k-1}}\right\|^{2})=\mathcal{O}(a_{k-1}\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k-1}}\right\|^{2})
from (
13
).
Consequently, the accumulated bounded difference for
M_{t}
is controlled by

\mathcal{O}(\max_{k\leq t}\ \left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k-1}}\right\|^{2})
.
Assuming again that
\max_{k\leq t}\ \left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k-1}}\right\|\leq\rho R_{\mathrm{base}}
, Pinelis’ inequality
(
24
)
for martingale concentration (Lemma
B.1
) yields
|M_{t}|=\mathcal{O}(\rho^{2}R_{\mathrm{base}}^{2})
with high probability.

To control the remainder term

R_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{t}\eta_{k-1}(u^{*}_{k-1}(Z_{k-1})-u^{*}_{k-1}(Z_{k}))
,
we rewrite it (see Lemma
A.3
) as

R_{t}=\eta_{0}u^{*}_{0}(Z_{0})-\eta_{t}u^{*}_{t}(Z_{t})-\sum_{k=1}^{t}(\eta_{k-1}-\eta_{k})u^{*}_{k-1}(Z_{k})-\sum_{k=1}^{t}\eta_{k}\bigl(u^{*}_{k-1}(Z_{k})-u^{*}_{k}(Z_{k})\bigr)\,.

Since
\left\|{u^{*}_{k}-u^{*}_{k-1}}\right\|_{\infty}
is controlled by

\mathcal{O}(\eta_{k-1}\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k-1}}\right\|^{2})

(because
\bm{\theta}_{k}-\bm{\theta}_{k-1}=\eta_{k-1}\bm{g}_{k-1}
), the induction hypothesis again implies

|R_{t}|=\mathcal{O}(\rho^{2}R_{\mathrm{base}}^{2})
. Combining the bounds for
M_{t}
and
R_{t}
, we obtain

\sum_{k=1}^{t}\eta_{k-1}h_{k-1}(Z_{k-1})=\mathcal{O}(\rho^{2}R_{\mathrm{base}}^{2})
, which fits perfectly with our inductive proof, since
\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|^{2}
remains the same order
\mathcal{O}(\rho^{2}R_{\mathrm{base}}^{2})
as in the idealized case
\bm{g}_{k}=\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k})
.

4.2
High-Probability Convergence Rate for Polyak–Ruppert Averaging

Our second result studies the convergence rate of the Polyak–Ruppert (PR) averaged iterate.
Let

S_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\qquad\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}:=\frac{1}{S_{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\bm{\theta}_{t-1}\,.

Recall the following potential defined in (
2
):

f(\bm{\theta})-f(\bm{\theta}^{*})=(1-\gamma)\,\|\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\|_{\bm{D}}^{2}+\gamma\,\|\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\|_{\mathrm{Dir}}^{2}\,.

We have the following high-probability guarantees for the PR average
\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}
.

Theorem 4.2

(High-probability rate for PR averaging)
.

Under Assumption
3.1
, let

\phi_{\infty},r_{\infty},\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
and
\omega
be the constants introduced in
Lemmas
3.2
–
3.4
. Consider the same stepsize schedule
(\eta_{t})
as in (
7
) and the same
R_{\mathrm{base}}
in (
8
). Also, define
H\coloneqq\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\eta_{t}^{2}
. Fix any
\delta\in(0,1)
and let

A_{1}(\delta)=1536\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}a_{t}^{2}}\sqrt{2\log\frac{8}{\delta}}+2304
and
A_{2}=2706\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}a_{t}^{2}
.
Define

c_{\min}(\delta)\coloneqq\frac{A_{1}(\delta)+\sqrt{A_{1}(\delta)^{2}+4A_{2}}}{2},\qquad\rho\coloneqq\frac{2c}{\sqrt{c^{2}-A_{1}(\delta)c-A_{2}}}\,.

Suppose
c>c_{\min}(\delta)
. Then, with probability at least
1-\delta
, we have for all
T\geq 1
,

f(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T})-f(\bm{\theta}^{*})\leq\min\left\{\frac{C_{\mathrm{fast}}^{2}}{\omega S_{T}^{2}},\frac{C_{\mathrm{robust}}}{S_{T}}\right\},

where

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{fast}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\rho R_{\mathrm{base}}\left[2+2\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}\left(264\eta_{0}+176\sqrt{H}\sqrt{2\log\frac{8}{\delta}}\right)+192\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{4}H\right],

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{robust}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\rho^{2}R_{\mathrm{base}}^{2}\left[0.5+2\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}\left(288\eta_{0}+192\sqrt{H}\sqrt{2\log\frac{8}{\delta}}\right)+\left(672\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}+4.5\right)\phi_{\infty}^{4}H\right]\,.

A choice of the stepsize that satisfies the assumptions of the theorem is (see Appendix
D
)

\eta_{t}=(c\,\phi_{\infty}^{2}\,\tau_{\mathrm{mix}})^{-1}a_{t},\quad\text{ where }\quad a_{t}=(\log(t+3)\sqrt{t+1})^{-1}\,.

(15)

The logarithmic correction in
a_{t}
is used at the square-summability boundary: it ensures that
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\eta_{t}^{2}
is finite while keeping
S_{T}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}
within a logarithmic factor of
\sqrt{T}
. Such a choice results in a robust rate of
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\frac{\|\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}}{\sqrt{T}})
and a fast rate of
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\frac{\|\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}^{2}\phi_{\infty}^{4}}{\omega T})
with high probability.

In Appendix
E
, we also show an alternative stepsize choice that does not require knowledge of
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
, at the cost of a doubly exponential dependence on
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
in the constants.

Proof Sketch of Theorem
4.2
.

By Theorem
4.1
, with high probability we have

\sup_{t\geq 0}\ \left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}}\right\|_{2}\leq\rho\,R_{\mathrm{base}}
.
It then follows that

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}^{2}\left\|{\bm{g}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}

is of order
\mathcal{O}(R_{\mathrm{base}}^{2})
, using the gradient bound (
10
).
Moreover, the cumulated Markov bias term

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\langle\bm{g}_{t-1}-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t-1}),\bm{\theta}_{t-1}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\rangle

is also of order
\mathcal{O}(R_{\mathrm{base}}^{2})
, by applying the Poisson equation and Pinelis’ inequality.

Rearranging (
4.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle 2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\langle\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t-1}),\bm{\theta}^{*}-\bm{\theta}_{t-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\|\bm{\theta}_{0}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}-\|\bm{\theta}_{T}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}^{2}\|\bm{g}_{t-1}\|^{2}+2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\langle\bm{g}_{t-1}-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t-1}),\bm{\theta}_{t-1}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(R_{\mathrm{base}}^{2})\,.

The robust rate then follows from the convexity of
f
together with the equation (
4
):

\displaystyle f(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T})-f(\bm{\theta}^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\langle{\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t-1})},{\bm{\theta}^{*}-\bm{\theta}_{t-1}}\rangle=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{base}}^{2}}{S_{T}}\right)\,.

To derive the fast rate, recall that the TD update can be written as

\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{t-1},Z_{t-1})=-\bm{A}_{Z_{t-1}}\bm{\theta}_{t-1}+\bm{b}_{Z_{t-1}}\,.

Then the centered error
\bm{e}_{t}:=\bm{\theta}_{t}-\bm{\theta}^{*}
can be shown (see (
24
)) to evolve as

\bm{e}_{t}=(\mathbf{I}_{d}-\eta_{t-1}\bm{A})\bm{e}_{t-1}+\eta_{t-1}\bm{\xi}_{t-1}+\eta_{t-1}\bm{\delta}_{t-1}\bm{e}_{t-1}\,.

(16)

Rearranging yields

\bm{A}\bm{e}_{t-1}=\frac{\bm{e}_{t-1}-\bm{e}_{t}}{\eta_{t-1}}+\bm{\xi}_{t-1}+\bm{\delta}_{t-1}\bm{e}_{t-1}\,.

Define

\bar{\bm{e}}_{T}:=\frac{1}{S_{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\bm{e}_{t-1}
.
Then
\bm{A}\bar{\bm{e}}_{T}=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}
, where

I_{1}:=\frac{1}{S_{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\bm{e}_{t-1}-\bm{e}_{t}),\qquad I_{2}:=\frac{1}{S_{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\bm{\xi}_{t-1},\qquad I_{3}:=\frac{1}{S_{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\bm{\delta}_{t-1}\bm{e}_{t-1}\,.

By Lemma
3.4
, we have
(\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top})/2\succeq\omega\mathbf{I}_{d}
.
Combining this with the equation (
4
) again, we obtain

f(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T})-f(\bm{\theta}^{*})=\langle\bm{A}\bar{\bm{e}}_{T},\bar{\bm{e}}_{T}\rangle\leq\|\bm{A}\bar{\bm{e}}_{T}\|\,\|\bar{\bm{e}}_{T}\|\leq\frac{1}{\omega}\,\|\bm{A}\bar{\bm{e}}_{T}\|^{2}\,.

The fast rate follows by controlling
\left\|{I_{1}}\right\|
,
\left\|{I_{2}}\right\|
, and
\left\|{I_{3}}\right\|
separately.
The term
\|I_{1}\|
is bounded using the telescoping sum.
For
\|I_{2}\|
and
\|I_{3}\|
, we again apply Lemma
A.1
with forcing terms
\bm{\xi}_{t-1}
and
\bm{\delta}_{t-1}\bm{e}_{t-1}
, respectively.
Therefore, by Pinelis’ inequality and the normalization by
S_{T}
, both
\|I_{2}\|
and
\|I_{3}\|
are of order
\mathcal{O}(R_{\mathrm{base}}/S_{T})
with high probability, which completes the proof sketch for the fast rate.

5
Detailed Comparison with Prior Results

In this section, we compare Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
with prior finite-time results for TD(
0
) with linear function approximation. This comparison is delicate because prior papers use slightly different assumptions on the problem and the algorithm. We therefore compare the results on a common footing, while emphasizing which unknown quantities are needed to set the algorithmic hyperparameters.
For simplicity, in this section we assume
\phi_{\infty}=1
.

First, we recall our guarantees under the stepsize schedule in (
15
):
\eta_{t}=\frac{1}{c\,\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\sqrt{t+1}\log(t+3)}
, where
c
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
. Under this choice, TD(
0
) with Polyak–Ruppert averaging
simultaneously
achieves the following three guarantees:

•

bounded iterates, independent of
\omega
,

•

the
\omega
-robust rate

\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}R_{\mathrm{base}}^{2}\log T\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}}{\sqrt{T}}\Big)
,

•

the fast
\omega
-aware rate

\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}^{2}R_{\mathrm{base}}^{2}\log^{2}T\log(1/\delta)}{\omega T}\Big)
,
with probability at least
1-\delta
.

Importantly, all guarantees use a single stepsize schedule that is independent of the curvature parameter
\omega
.

Prior work on bounded iterates.

To the best of our knowledge, our result is the first to show that TD(
0
) with Markovian sampling has bounded iterates with high probability without using
\omega
in the stepsize or in a curvature-based stability argument.

The closest result is
11
, but they only provide expectation bounds, whose rate we match. While one would expect that it is relatively easy to convert expectation results to high-probability ones for well-behaved random variables, here it requires completely different machinery based on the Poisson equation.

Our proof proceeds by establishing the boundedness of the iterates via a carefully designed inductive argument. This argument uses only the condition
\langle{\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta})},{\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\leq 0
. We then control the Markovian bias pathwise using a Poisson-equation-based decomposition. This approach is fundamentally different from the Linear Stochastic Approximation (LSA)-stability-based analyses in
28
;
20
;
4
;
23
;
14
;
27
;
6
that result in a bound on the norm of the iterates that depends on
\omega
, hence incompatible with the robust rate.

Our approach is also very different from using projections, that is, projecting
\bm{\theta}_{k}
onto the ball
\mathcal{B}(\bm{0},\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|)

(
1
;
16
;
30
;
25
;
23
, see, e.g.,)
.

Such projections play an analytic rather than algorithmic role: they guarantee that
|\bm{g}(\bm{\theta},\cdot)|_{\infty}
is always bounded by a constant depending on
\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|
,
so that the following terms can be controlled easily using concentration inequalities:

\sum_{k}\eta_{k}^{2}\|\bm{g}_{k}\|^{2},\qquad\sum_{k}\eta_{k}\langle{\bm{g}_{k}-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k})},{\bm{\theta}_{k}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle.

These terms scale with
\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|^{2}
instead of an unknown bound on
\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k}}\right\|^{2}
. We remark that this kind of modification of TD(
0
) is not used in practice, and it would require an additional hyperparameter. Moreover, simply bounding
\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|
with the loose bound
(
1
, Lemma 7)

\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|\leq\frac{2r_{\infty}}{\sqrt{\omega}(1-\lambda)}
once again breaks the
\omega
-independent result required for the robust rate.

Prior work on robust rates.

To the best of our knowledge, ours is the first result to achieve the robust rate

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\frac{\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|^{2}\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}}{\sqrt{T}})
with high probability, without any algorithmic modification and with a simple stepsize schedule independent of
\omega
.
Once one considers the implicit dependence of
T
on
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
in some prior work, we match the rate in expectation from prior results
(
1
;
16
;
11
, see, e.g.,)
, up to polylog factors. On the other hand, we require knowledge of
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
to set the stepsizes. Instead, prior work, either implicitly or explicitly, requires the number of iterations
T
to be large enough with respect to some function of
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
for the rate to be
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}})
.

Our analysis is fundamentally different from previous ones,with the notable exception of the expectation result of
11
that we already discussed.
Indeed, the stability-based approach commonly used in the literature, which relies on (
16
) to unroll
\bm{\theta}_{t}-\bm{\theta}^{*}
, would typically result in a bound on
\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|
that scales like
\sum_{i=1}^{t}(1-\lambda_{\min}\eta)^{i}
using inequalities similar to (
17
), which only controls
\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}}\right\|
at the scale
\mathcal{O}(1/\lambda_{\min})
. This dependence is incompatible with the
\omega
-independent bounded-iterates guarantee in Theorem
4.1
.

Prior work on fast rates.

The most relevant prior works on fast rates are

;
6
.
3
3

3

6
, the results in

) with linear function approximation as a special case of LSA. They prove that there exist constants

\|\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{\Sigma}}
decays at rate

\mathcal{O}(\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\|\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}\log(1/\delta)}{(1-\gamma)^{2}\lambda_{\min}T})
, plus an exponentially decaying term depending on
\|\bm{\theta}_{0}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}
.

To compare these results with our bounds, we can use equation (
5
) to translate our bound on
f
into a bound on
\|\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{\Sigma}}
. Hence, we obtain the fast rate

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}^{2}R_{\mathrm{base}}^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}\lambda_{\min}T})

in Theorem
4.2
. Up to logarithmic factors, this rate matches the best-known high-probability results in the Markovian sampling regime
(
27
;
6
)
and the i.i.d. stationary sampling regime
(
14
)
for all relevant non-mixing quantities. Indeed, our
R_{\mathrm{base}}^{2}
has the same order of magnitude as
|\bm{\theta}^{*}|^{2}
that appears in the other bounds.
However, our
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}^{2}
dependence is worse by one factor of
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
than the linear
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
dependence obtained in the Markovian bounds of
27
;
6
.
In addition, our initial-error term
\|\bm{\theta}_{0}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}
decays at a
1/T
rate rather than exponentially, since we avoid relying on a contraction-based argument in our analysis.

The worse dependence on
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
could be a side effect of our Poisson-equation-based analysis.
On the other hand, it could also be due to the fact that we do not use a data-dropping method
(
27
)
or knowledge of
\lambda_{\min}

(
6
)
.

More generally, it is unclear whether any of the above results are optimal in their respective settings. To the best of our knowledge, the closest lower bound is
\Omega(\frac{\|\bm{\theta}^{*}\|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\min}T})
due to
14
, which considers TD(
0
) with linear function approximation under the easier setting of i.i.d. sampling. Thus, when data dropping is not allowed, projections are not used, and
\lambda_{\min}
is unknown, the optimal dependence of our rate on
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
and
1-\gamma
remains open.

A reason one might expect such a dependence on
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
in the convergence rate or stepsize is that the TD fixed point
\bm{\theta}^{*}
is defined with respect to the stationary distribution, and a slowly mixing Markov chain can require more samples to accurately estimate stationary expectations
(
21
)
.
At the same time, tabular results show that one should not expect a multiplicative mixing-time dependence to be necessary in all settings:
13
show that, for asynchronous Q-learning and hence tabular TD, the leading statistical term can match the i.i.d. sampling setting, with only an additive transient
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
cost.
In the linear function approximation setting, existing results still account for the mixing time in various ways: stepsize conditions that explicitly depend on
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
appear in
(
28
;
19
;
6
)
; data-dropping approaches that retain only one out of
\mathcal{O}(\tau_{\mathrm{mix}})
samples are studied in
(
23
;
27
)
; and several results require the total sample budget
T
to be sufficiently large relative to
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}

(
11
;
15
)
.

Appendix A
Poisson Toolkit for Markov Noise

In this section, we develop a Poisson-equation toolkit for Markov
concentration. Throughout, let
(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the Markov chain on

\mathcal{Z}:=\{(i,j)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}:P^{\mu}(i,j)>0\}

with kernel
P_{Z}
and stationary distribution
\pi_{Z}
, as defined in
Section
3
. The state space
\mathcal{Z}
is finite.

A.1
Poisson Equation under Geometric Mixing

We start with a standard Poisson equation lemma for geometrically mixing chains in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
18
, Theorem 17.4.3]
.

Lemma A.1

(Poisson solution under geometric mixing)
.

Let
h:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded measurable function with
stationary mean zero, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{Z}}[h(Z)]=0
. Then, the Poisson equation

u-P_{Z}u=h,\qquad\text{on }\mathcal{Z},

(18)

where
(P_{Z}u)(z):=\mathbb{E}[u(Z_{1})\mid Z_{0}=z]
, admits a solution

u^{*}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(P_{Z}^{k}h)(z),

where
(P_{Z}^{k}h)(z):=\mathbb{E}[h(Z_{k})\mid Z_{0}=z]
.
Moreover,

\|u^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq 16\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\|h\|_{\infty},\qquad\text{where }\|h\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\ \|h(z)\|\,.

Proof.

Define
u^{*}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(P_{Z}^{k}h)(z)
. Since

\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{Z}}[h(Z)]=0
, for every
k\geq 0
and every

z\in\mathcal{Z}
,

(P_{Z}^{k}h)(z)=\int h(z^{\prime})\{P_{Z}^{k}(z,dz^{\prime})-\pi_{Z}(dz^{\prime})\}\,.

Using the geometric mixing bound from Lemma
3.3
, we have for all
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|(P_{Z}^{k}h)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\ \left\|\mathbb{E}\bigl[h(Z_{k})\mid Z_{0}=z\bigr]\right\|\leq\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\ 2\|h\|_{\infty}\|P_{Z}^{k}(z,\cdot)-\pi_{Z}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq 8\cdot 2^{-k/\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}}\|h\|_{\infty}\,.

Thus, the series defining
u^{*}
converges absolutely and uniformly with

\|u^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|(P_{Z}^{k}h)\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{8}{1-2^{-1/\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}}}\|h\|_{\infty}\coloneqq C(\tau_{\mathrm{mix}})\|h\|_{\infty}\,.

Moreover,

(P_{Z}u^{*})(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{Z}^{k+1}h(z),

so

u^{*}(z)-(P_{Z}u^{*})(z)=(P_{Z}^{0}h)(z)=h(z),

that is,
u^{*}
solves (
18
).

Let
x=1/\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
. We scale the function
C(\tau_{\mathrm{mix}})
by
1/\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
and consider the new function
\tilde{C}(x)
:

\tilde{C}(x):=\frac{1}{\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}}C\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=x\cdot\frac{8\cdot 2^{x}}{2^{x}-1}=\frac{8x}{1-2^{-x}}\,.

We claim that
\tilde{C}(x)
is monotonically increasing on
(0,1]
. Let
t=x\ln 2
. Then
\tilde{C}(x)
can be written as
\frac{8}{\ln 2}\frac{t}{1-e^{-t}}
. Consider the derivative of
f(t)=\frac{t}{1-e^{-t}}
for
t>0
:

f^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1(1-e^{-t})-t(e^{-t})}{(1-e^{-t})^{2}}=\frac{1-(1+t)e^{-t}}{(1-e^{-t})^{2}}\,.

Using the elementary inequality
e^{t}>1+t
for
t>0
, we have
1>(1+t)e^{-t}
, which implies
f^{\prime}(t)>0
. Thus,
\tilde{C}(x)
is strictly increasing on
(0,1]
.
Consequently, the maximum value of
\tilde{C}(x)
on the interval
(0,1]
occurs at
x=1
:

\tilde{C}(x)\leq\tilde{C}(1)=\frac{8\cdot 1}{1-2^{-1}}=\frac{8}{0.5}=16\,.

Therefore, we have
C(\tau_{\mathrm{mix}})\leq 16\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
, which completes the proof.
∎

A.2
Bounds for TD-type Poisson solutions

We now specialize Lemma
A.1
to the functions arising from TD. For
z=(s,s^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Z}
, define the scalar function

h_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}(z):=\bigl\langle\bm{g}({\bm{\theta}},z)-\bar{\bm{g}}({\bm{\theta}}),\,{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\bigr\rangle,

and the vector-valued functions

\displaystyle h(z)

\displaystyle:=\varepsilon_{z}\bm{\phi}(s)\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\qquad\text{where we recall }\varepsilon_{z}=r(s,s^{\prime})+\gamma\bm{\phi}(s^{\prime})^{\top}{\bm{\theta}}^{*}-\bm{\phi}(s)^{\top}{\bm{\theta}}^{*},

\displaystyle h_{\bm{\theta}}(z)

\displaystyle:=\bm{\delta}_{z}\bigl({\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\bigr)\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\qquad\text{where we define }\bm{\delta}_{z}\coloneqq\bm{A}-\bm{A}_{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\,.

By construction,
h_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}
,
h
, and
h_{\bm{\theta}}
all have
stationary mean zero under
\pi_{Z}
. We study properties of the corresponding Poisson-equation solutions in the following lemma.

Lemma A.2

(Bounds and Lipschitz properties of TD Poisson equation solutions)
.

In the setting of Section
3
, let

\phi_{\infty},r_{\infty}
and
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
be the constants introduced in
Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

Growth bounds.

For all
z\in\mathcal{Z}
,

\displaystyle|h_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq(2r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}+4\phi_{\infty}^{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}\|)\,\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|,

\displaystyle\left\|{h(z)}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}+2\phi_{\infty}^{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|,

\displaystyle\|h_{\bm{\theta}}(z)\|

\displaystyle\leq 4\phi_{\infty}^{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|\,.

(ii)

Bounded Poisson solutions.

Let
u^{*}_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
be the infinite series solution of the
Poisson equation

u^{*}_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}-P_{Z}u^{*}_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}=h_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}},

and let
u^{*}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the infinite series solution of

u^{*}-P_{Z}u^{*}=h,

and let
u^{*}_{\bm{\theta}}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the infinite series solution of

u^{*}_{\bm{\theta}}-P_{Z}u^{*}_{\bm{\theta}}=h_{\bm{\theta}}\,.

Then,

\displaystyle\|u^{*}_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 16\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\|h_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}\|_{\infty}\leq 16\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(2r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}+4\phi_{\infty}^{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}\|)\,\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|,

\displaystyle\|u^{*}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 16\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\|h\|_{\infty}\leq 16\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}+2\phi_{\infty}^{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|),

\displaystyle\|u^{*}_{\bm{\theta}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 16\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\|h_{\bm{\theta}}\|_{\infty}\leq 64\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|\,.

(iii)

Local Lipschitz continuity in
{\bm{\theta}}
and
{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}
.

With the definitions in the previous point, we also have

\displaystyle\|u^{*}_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}-u^{*}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime},{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 16\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\|h_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}-h_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime},{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 16\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(2r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}+4\phi_{\infty}^{2}\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}}\right\|)\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}}\right\|

\displaystyle\quad+64\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}\right\|\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}}\right\|,

and

\|u^{*}_{\bm{\theta}}-u^{*}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\leq 64\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}\|\,.

Proof.

(i) Growth bounds.

By the feature and reward bounds in Lemma
3.2
,
for
z=(s,s^{\prime})
we have

\displaystyle\|\bm{g}({\bm{\theta}},z)\|

\displaystyle=\bigl|r(s,s^{\prime})+\gamma\bm{\phi}(s^{\prime})^{\top}{\bm{\theta}}-\bm{\phi}(s)^{\top}{\bm{\theta}}\bigr|\cdot\|\bm{\phi}(s)\|

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(r_{\infty}+(\gamma+1)\phi_{\infty}\|{\bm{\theta}}\|\bigr)\,\phi_{\infty}\leq\bigl(r_{\infty}+2\phi_{\infty}\|{\bm{\theta}}\|\bigr)\,\phi_{\infty}\,.

Taking expectations under
\pi_{Z}
yields the same type of bound for

\|\bar{\bm{g}}({\bm{\theta}})\|
, hence

\|\bm{g}({\bm{\theta}},z)-\bar{\bm{g}}({\bm{\theta}})\|\leq 2(r_{\infty}+2\phi_{\infty}\|{\bm{\theta}}\|)\,\phi_{\infty}=2r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}+4\phi_{\infty}^{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}\|\,.

Therefore,

|h_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}(z)|=\bigl|\langle\bm{g}({\bm{\theta}},z)-\bar{\bm{g}}({\bm{\theta}}),{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\rangle\bigr|\leq(2r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}+4\phi_{\infty}^{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}\|)\,\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|\,.

For
h
, recall that

\varepsilon_{z}=r(s,s^{\prime})+\gamma\bm{\phi}(s^{\prime})^{\top}{\bm{\theta}}^{*}-\bm{\phi}(s)^{\top}{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\,.

Then,

\left\|{h(z)}\right\|=\left\|{\varepsilon_{z}\bm{\phi}(s)}\right\|\leq r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}+2\phi_{\infty}^{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|\,.

For
h_{\bm{\theta}}
, recall that

\bm{A}_{z}=\bm{\phi}(s)\bigl(\bm{\phi}(s)-\gamma\bm{\phi}(s^{\prime})\bigr)^{\top}\,.

Then,

\|\bm{A}_{z}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\|\bm{\phi}(s)\|\bigl(\|\bm{\phi}(s)\|+\gamma\|\bm{\phi}(s^{\prime})\|\bigr)\leq(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}^{2}\leq 2\phi_{\infty}^{2}\,.

Consequently,
\|\bm{A}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 2\phi_{\infty}^{2}
and

\|\bm{\delta}_{z}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\|\bm{A}\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|\bm{A}_{z}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 4\phi_{\infty}^{2}\,.

Thus,

\|h_{\bm{\theta}}(z)\|=\|\bm{\delta}_{z}({\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*})\|\leq 4\phi_{\infty}^{2}\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}\right\|\,.

(ii) Poisson solution sup-norm bounds.

It follows from Lemma
A.1
applied to the real-valued function

h_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}
and the
\mathbb{R}^{d}
functions
h
and
h_{\bm{\theta}}
.

(iii) Lipschitz continuity in
{\bm{\theta}}
.

Scalar case
h_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}
.

For each
z=(s,s^{\prime})
and
{\bm{\theta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
recall that

\bm{A}_{z}:=\bm{\phi}(s)\bigl(\bm{\phi}(s)-\gamma\bm{\phi}(s^{\prime})\bigr)^{\top},\qquad\bm{b}_{z}:=r(s,s^{\prime})\bm{\phi}(s),

and

\bm{A}=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{Z}}[\bm{A}_{Z}],\qquad\bm{b}=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{Z}}[\bm{b}_{Z}],

so that
\bar{\bm{g}}({\bm{\theta}})=-\bm{A}{\bm{\theta}}+\bm{b}
and
\bm{g}({\bm{\theta}},z)=-\bm{A}_{z}{\bm{\theta}}+\bm{b}_{z}
.
Also, recall that

\bm{\delta}_{z}=\bm{A}-\bm{A}_{z},\text{ and let}\qquad\bm{\zeta}_{z}:=\bm{b}_{z}-\bm{b}\,.

Then

\bm{g}({\bm{\theta}},z)-\bar{\bm{g}}({\bm{\theta}})=\bm{\delta}_{z}{\bm{\theta}}+\bm{\zeta}_{z},

and hence

h_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}(z)=\bigl\langle\bm{\delta}_{z}{\bm{\theta}}+\bm{\zeta}_{z},\,{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\bigr\rangle\,.

As above,
\|\bm{\delta}_{z}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 4\phi_{\infty}^{2}
, and
\|\bm{b}_{z}\|\leq r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}

implies
\|\bm{\zeta}_{z}\|\leq 2r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}
.
We have

\displaystyle|h_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}(z)-h_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime},{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}(z)|

\displaystyle=|\bigl\langle\bm{\delta}_{z}{\bm{\theta}}+\bm{\zeta}_{z},\,{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\bigr\rangle-\bigl\langle\bm{\delta}_{z}{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}+\bm{\zeta}_{z},\,{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\bigr\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\bigl\langle\bm{\delta}_{z}{\bm{\theta}}+\bm{\zeta}_{z},\,{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}+{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\bigr\rangle+\bigl\langle\bm{\delta}_{z}{\bm{\theta}}+\bm{\zeta}_{z}-(\bm{\delta}_{z}{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}+\bm{\zeta}_{z}),\,{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\bigr\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\bm{\delta}_{z}{\bm{\theta}}+\bm{\zeta}_{z}}\right\|\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}}\right\|+\left\|{\bm{\delta}_{z}({\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime})}\right\|\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(4\phi_{\infty}^{2}\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}}\right\|+2r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}\right)\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}}\right\|+4\phi_{\infty}^{2}\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}\right\|\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}}\right\|\,.

The second inequality follows from
applying Lemma
A.1
and linearity of the Poisson equation:

\|u^{*}_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}-u^{*}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime},{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}\|_{\infty}\leq 16\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\|h_{{\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}-h_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime},{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}\|_{\infty}\,.

Vector case
h_{\bm{\theta}}
.

For
h_{\bm{\theta}}
,

h_{\bm{\theta}}(z)-h_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}}(z)=\bm{\delta}_{z}({\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}),

so

\|h_{\bm{\theta}}(z)-h_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}}(z)\|\leq\|\bm{\delta}_{z}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}\|\leq 4\phi_{\infty}^{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}\|\,.

Taking the supremum over
z
gives

\|h_{\bm{\theta}}-h_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\leq 4\phi_{\infty}^{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}\|\,.

Thus, by Lemma
A.1
and linearity of the Poisson equation,

\|u^{*}_{\bm{\theta}}-u^{*}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\leq 64\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\prime}\|\,.

∎

A.3
Poisson Martingale decomposition of additive Markov noise

Recall
\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\sigma(Z_{0},\dots,Z_{t})
.
Let
u_{t-1}
be the infinite series solution of the Poisson equation with forcing function
h_{t-1}
defined as one of
h_{\bm{\theta}_{t-1},\bm{\theta}_{t-1}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}
,

h
, or
h_{\bm{\theta}_{t-1}}
, that is

u_{t-1}-P_{Z}u_{t-1}=h_{t-1}\,.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}h_{t-1}(Z_{t-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\bigl(u_{t-1}(Z_{t-1})-\left(P_{Z}u_{t-1}\right)(Z_{t-1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\bigl(u_{t-1}(Z_{t-1})-u_{t-1}(Z_{t})+u_{t-1}(Z_{t})-\left(P_{Z}u_{t-1}\right)(Z_{t-1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\bigl(u_{t-1}(Z_{t-1})-u_{t-1}(Z_{t})\bigr)+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\Delta M_{t},

where
\Delta M_{t}\coloneqq u_{t-1}(Z_{t})-\left(P_{Z}u_{t-1}\right)(Z_{t-1})
is a martingale difference with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\sigma(Z_{0},\dots,Z_{t})
since
u_{t-1}\in\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
and

\mathbb{E}[u_{t-1}(Z_{t})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=\mathbb{E}[u_{t-1}(Z_{t})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1},u_{t-1}]=\left(P_{Z}u_{t-1}\right)(Z_{t-1})\,.

We will use this Poisson decomposition repeatedly throughout the paper.

Thus, for all
T\geq 1
, we obtain

M_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\Delta M_{t},\quad R_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\bigl(u_{t-1}(Z_{t-1})-u_{t-1}(Z_{t})\bigr),

(19)

and
(M_{T})_{T\geq 1}
is a
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued martingale with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{T})_{T\geq 1}
. Moreover,
R_{T}
enjoys the following representation:

Lemma A.3

(Summation by parts)
.

R_{T}=\eta_{0}u_{0}(Z_{0})-\eta_{T}u_{T}(Z_{T})-\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\eta_{t-1}-\eta_{t})u_{t-1}(Z_{t})-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\bigl(u_{t-1}(Z_{t})-u_{t}(Z_{t})\bigr)\,.

Proof.

Let
R_{T}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}(u_{t-1}(Z_{t-1})-u_{t-1}(Z_{t}))
and write

R_{T}=\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}u_{t-1}(Z_{t-1})}_{T_{T}^{(1)}}-\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}u_{t-1}(Z_{t})}_{T_{T}^{(2)}}\,.

Reindex the first sum as
T_{T}^{(1)}=\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}\eta_{j}u_{j}(Z_{j})
.
For the second sum, add and subtract the terms
\eta_{t}u_{t}(Z_{t})
and
\eta_{t}u_{t-1}(Z_{t})
:

\eta_{t-1}u_{t-1}(Z_{t})=\eta_{t}u_{t}(Z_{t})+(\eta_{t-1}-\eta_{t})u_{t-1}(Z_{t})+\eta_{t}(u_{t-1}(Z_{t})-u_{t}(Z_{t}))\,.

Summing over
t=1,\dots,T
yields

T_{T}^{(2)}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}u_{t}(Z_{t})+\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\eta_{t-1}-\eta_{t})u_{t-1}(Z_{t})+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\bigl(u_{t-1}(Z_{t})-u_{t}(Z_{t})\bigr)\,.

Hence,

R_{T}=\eta_{0}u_{0}(Z_{0})-\eta_{T}u_{T}(Z_{T})-\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\eta_{t-1}-\eta_{t})u_{t-1}(Z_{t})-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\bigl(u_{t-1}(Z_{t})-u_{t}(Z_{t})\bigr)\,.

∎

B.1
Markov noise and localization

Let
\bm{e}_{t}:={\bm{\theta}}_{t}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}
denote the error at time

. From the TD update,

\bm{e}_{t}

\displaystyle\|\bm{e}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|^{2}+\eta^{2}_{t-1}\|\bm{g}({\bm{\theta}}_{t-1},Z_{t-1})\|^{2}+2\eta_{t-1}\langle\bm{g}({\bm{\theta}}_{t-1},Z_{t-1})-\bar{\bm{g}}({\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}),{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+2\eta_{t-1}\langle\bar{\bm{g}}({\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}),{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\rangle\,.

Summing from
t=1
to
T
yields

\|\bm{e}_{T}\|^{2}=\|\bm{e}_{0}\|^{2}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta^{2}_{t-1}\|\bm{g}({\bm{\theta}}_{t-1},Z_{t-1})\|^{2}+2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\langle\bar{\bm{g}}({\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}),{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\rangle+B_{T},

(20)

where the Markov bias term is

B_{T}:=2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\,\langle\bm{g}({\bm{\theta}}_{t-1},Z_{t-1})-\bar{\bm{g}}({\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}),\,{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\rangle\,.

(21)

By (
4
) and the nonnegativity of

f(\bm{\theta})-f(\bm{\theta}^{*})
,

\langle\bar{\bm{g}}({\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}),{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\rangle=-\bigl(f({\bm{\theta}}_{t-1})-f({\bm{\theta}}^{*})\bigr)\leq 0.

Thus, we have

\|\bm{e}_{T}\|^{2}\leq\|\bm{e}_{0}\|^{2}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta^{2}_{t-1}\|\bm{g}({\bm{\theta}}_{t-1},Z_{t-1})\|^{2}+B_{T}\,.

To localize the process, we define

T_{\mathrm{exit}}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:\,\|{\bm{\theta}}_{t}\|>\rho R_{\mathrm{base}}\},

where
T_{\mathrm{exit}}
is a stopping time with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
because
{\bm{\theta}}_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable.
The number
\rho>2
will be specified later. Define

R_{\mathrm{base}}\coloneqq\max\left\{\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{0}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}\right\|,\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}\right\|,\frac{r_{\infty}}{\phi_{\infty}}\right\},\quad R_{\mathrm{max}}\coloneqq\rho R_{\mathrm{base}},

and the stopped iterates and stepsizes by

\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{t}:={\bm{\theta}}_{t\wedge T_{\mathrm{exit}}},\qquad\tilde{\eta}_{t}:=\begin{cases}\eta_{t},&t<T_{\mathrm{exit}},\\
0,&t\geq T_{\mathrm{exit}}\,.\end{cases}

We define the stopped Markov bias term

\tilde{B}_{T}:=2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tilde{\eta}_{t-1}\langle\bm{g}(\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1},Z_{t-1})-\bar{\bm{g}}(\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}),\,\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\rangle\,.

In particular, on the event
\{T_{\mathrm{exit}}=\infty\}
we have
\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{t}={\bm{\theta}}_{t}
,
\tilde{\eta}_{t}=\eta_{t}
, and
\tilde{B}_{t}=B_{t}
for all
t
.

B.2
Poisson representation and bounds for the localized Markov noise

Let
u_{t-1}
be the infinite series solution of the Poisson equation with forcing function
h_{t-1}:=h_{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1},\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}\in\mathcal{F}_{t-2}
, that is,

u_{t-1}-P_{Z}u_{t-1}=h_{t-1}\,.

Then, from Section
A.3
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tilde{\eta}_{t-1}h_{t-1}(Z_{t-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tilde{\eta}_{t-1}\bigl(u_{t-1}(Z_{t-1})-\left(P_{Z}u_{t-1}\right)(Z_{t-1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tilde{\eta}_{t-1}\bigl(u_{t-1}(Z_{t-1})-u_{t-1}(Z_{t})+u_{t-1}(Z_{t})-\left(P_{Z}u_{t-1}\right)(Z_{t-1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tilde{\eta}_{t-1}\bigl(u_{t-1}(Z_{t-1})-u_{t-1}(Z_{t})\bigr)+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tilde{\eta}_{t-1}\Delta M_{t},

where
\Delta M_{t}\coloneqq u_{t-1}(Z_{t})-\left(P_{Z}u_{t-1}\right)(Z_{t-1})
is a martingale difference with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\sigma(Z_{0},\dots,Z_{t})
since
u_{t-1}\in\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
and

\mathbb{E}[u_{t-1}(Z_{t})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=\mathbb{E}[u_{t-1}(Z_{t})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1},u_{t-1}]=\left(P_{Z}u_{t-1}\right)(Z_{t-1})\,.

Thus, for all
T\geq 1
, we obtain

\tilde{B}_{T}=2\tilde{M}_{T}+2\tilde{R}_{T},\qquad\tilde{M}_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tilde{\eta}_{t-1}\Delta M_{t},\quad\tilde{R}_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tilde{\eta}_{t-1}\bigl(u_{t-1}(Z_{t-1})-u_{t-1}(Z_{t})\bigr),

(22)

and
(\tilde{M}_{T})_{T\geq 1}
is a real-valued martingale with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{T})_{T\geq 1}
.

Moreover, if
\tilde{\eta}_{t-1}>0
, we have
\|\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}\|\leq R_{\mathrm{max}}
and
\|\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|\leq 2R_{\mathrm{max}}
. Thus, Lemma
A.2
implies that

\displaystyle\|u_{t-1}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 16\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\left(2r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}+4\phi_{\infty}^{2}\left\|{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}}\right\|\right)\left(\left\|{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}\right\|\right)\leq 16\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}(4R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}+8R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 192\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\,.

Also,

\left\|{\Delta M_{t}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|{u_{t-1}}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|{P_{Z}u_{t-1}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 2\|u_{t-1}\|_{\infty}\leq 384\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\,.

Since
\tilde{\eta}_{t-1}=0
for all
t>T_{\mathrm{exit}}
, the increments
\tilde{\eta}_{t-1}\Delta M_{t}
satisfy

\left\|{\tilde{\eta}_{t-1}\Delta M_{t}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\tilde{\eta}_{t-1}384\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\,.

(23)

We now use Pinelis’ inequality in Lemma
F.1
. Applying it to the increments

X_{t}:=\tilde{\eta}_{t-1}\Delta M_{t},\qquad t\geq 1,

with (
23
), we obtain the following anytime high-probability guarantee.

Lemma B.1

(Pinelis’ inequality for the stopped martingale)
.

Let
\tilde{M}_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}X_{t}
with
X_{t}:=\tilde{\eta}_{t-1}\Delta M_{t}
as above.
Then, for all
\delta\in(0,1)
,

\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{T\geq 1}\ |\tilde{M}_{T}|\;\leq\;384\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}{\eta}_{t}^{2}}\sqrt{2\log\frac{2}{\delta}}\right\}\;\geq\;1-\delta\,.

Proof.

Setting
r\geq Db_{*}\sqrt{2\log\frac{2}{\delta}}
in Lemma
F.1
, we have
2\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2D^{2}b^{2}_{*}}\right)\leq\delta
.
Since
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\ell_{2}
is a
(2,1)
-smooth Banach space, choosing
b_{*}^{2}=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}{\eta}_{t}^{2}(384\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2})^{2}
finishes the proof.
∎

Bound on the remainder term

We now bound the deterministic remainder

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tilde{\eta}_{t}\bigl(u_{t-1}(Z_{t})-u_{t}(Z_{t})\bigr)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tilde{\eta}_{t}\|u_{t}-u_{t-1}\|_{\infty}\leq 672\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{4}R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tilde{\eta}_{t}\tilde{\eta}_{t-1}

Combining the three bounds, we have

\displaystyle|\tilde{R}_{T}|

\displaystyle\leq 576\eta_{0}\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}+672\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{4}R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}^{2}\,.

∎

We are now ready to give the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\sup_{T\geq 0}\ \|\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{T}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|^{2}+2\sup_{T}\ \|\tilde{\bm{e}}_{T}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|{\bm{\theta}}^{*}\|^{2}+2\|{\bm{\theta}}^{*}-{\bm{\theta}}_{0}\|^{2}+1536\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}{\eta}_{t}^{2}}\sqrt{2\log\frac{2}{\delta}}

\displaystyle\quad+2304\eta_{0}\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}+(2688\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}+18)\phi_{\infty}^{4}R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\eta_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+R_{\mathrm{base}}^{2}\left(2304\eta_{0}\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}\rho^{2}+(2688\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}+18)\phi_{\infty}^{4}\rho^{2}\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\eta^{2}_{t}\right)\,.

Recall that
\eta_{\mathrm{base}}=\frac{1}{c\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}}

\displaystyle\sup_{T\geq 0}\ \|\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_{T}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq R_{\mathrm{base}}^{2}\left(4+1536\frac{\rho^{2}}{c}\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}a_{t}^{2}}\sqrt{2\log\frac{2}{\delta}}+2304\frac{\rho^{2}}{c}+2706\left(\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}a^{2}_{t}\right)\frac{\rho^{2}}{c^{2}}\right)\,.

There exists a sufficiently large

For such

, we have

On the event

we must

Appendix C
Convergence Rates: Proof of Theorem
4.2

Recall that the TD update can be written as

\bm{g}({\bm{\theta}}_{t-1},Z_{t-1})=-\bm{A}_{Z_{t-1}}{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}+\bm{b}_{Z_{t-1}}.

Therefore, the centered error
\bm{e}_{t}:={\bm{\theta}}_{t}-{\bm{\theta}}^{*}
evolves as

\displaystyle\bm{e}_{t}

\displaystyle=\bm{e}_{t-1}+\eta_{t-1}\bigl(\bm{b}_{Z_{t-1}}-\bm{A}_{Z_{t-1}}{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\bm{e}_{t-1}+\eta_{t-1}\bigl(-\bm{A}_{Z_{t-1}}({\bm{\theta}}^{*}+\bm{e}_{t-1})+\bm{b}_{Z_{t-1}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\bm{e}_{t-1}+\eta_{t-1}\bigl(-\bm{A}_{Z_{t-1}}\bm{e}_{t-1}+\bm{\xi}_{t-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I}_{d}-\eta_{t-1}\bm{A})\bm{e}_{t-1}+\eta_{t-1}\bm{\xi}_{t-1}+\eta_{t-1}\bm{\delta}_{t-1}\bm{e}_{t-1},

(24)

using
\bm{A}_{Z_{t-1}}=\bm{A}-\bm{\delta}_{t-1}
and
\bm{\xi}_{t-1}=\bm{b}_{Z_{t-1}}-\bm{A}_{Z_{t-1}}{\bm{\theta}}^{*}
. Rearranging yields

\bm{A}\bm{e}_{t-1}=\frac{\bm{e}_{t-1}-\bm{e}_{t}}{\eta_{t-1}}+\bm{\xi}_{t-1}+\bm{\delta}_{t-1}\bm{e}_{t-1}\,.

(25)

Define the following quantities:

S_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1},\qquad\bar{\bm{e}}_{T}:=\frac{1}{S_{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\bm{e}_{t-1},\qquad\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}:={\bm{\theta}}^{*}+\bar{\bm{e}}_{T}\,.

Multiplying (
25
) by
\eta_{t-1}
and summing from

to

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\bm{A}\bm{e}_{t-1}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\bm{e}_{t-1}-\bm{e}_{t})+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\bm{\xi}_{t-1}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\bm{\delta}_{t-1}\bm{e}_{t-1}\,.

Dividing by
S_{T}
and recalling the definition of
\bar{\bm{e}}_{T}
yields

\bm{A}\bar{\bm{e}}_{T}=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

(26)

where

I_{1}:=\frac{1}{S_{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\bm{e}_{t-1}-\bm{e}_{t}),\quad I_{2}:=\frac{1}{S_{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\bm{\xi}_{t-1},\quad I_{3}:=\frac{1}{S_{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\bm{\delta}_{t-1}\bm{e}_{t-1}\,.

Notice that, by Lemma
3.4
,
(\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top})/2\succeq\omega\mathbf{I}_{d}
, so
for any
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

Combining this with Cauchy–Schwarz,

\omega\|\bm{x}\|^{2}\leq\langle\bm{A}\bm{x},\bm{x}\rangle\leq\|\bm{A}\bm{x}\|\|\bm{x}\|,

we obtain, for
\bm{x}\neq 0

\|\bm{x}\|\leq\frac{1}{\omega}\,\|\bm{A}\bm{x}\|.

In particular, for
\bm{x}=\bar{\bm{e}}_{T}
,

f(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T})-f({\bm{\theta}}^{*})=\langle\bm{A}\bar{\bm{e}}_{T},\bar{\bm{e}}_{T}\rangle\leq\|\;\bm{A}\bar{\bm{e}}_{T}\|_{2}\,\|\bar{\bm{e}}_{T}\|\leq\frac{1}{\omega}\,\|\bm{A}\bar{\bm{e}}_{T}\|^{2}\,.

(27)

We now bound

C.1
Bound on the telescoping term

Lemma C.1

(Bound on

.

Appendix E
Removing the dependence on
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
in the stepsize

The choice in (
15
) depends on the unknown mixing parameter
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
, which may make the algorithm impractical. This dependence of the stepsize on
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
can be avoided by running TD(0) with the modified stepsize schedule

\eta_{t}=\frac{1}{c\,\phi_{\infty}^{2}\sqrt{t+1}\log^{2}(t+3)},\qquad\forall t\geq 0,

(30)

for some numerical constant
c>0
. For
T\geq 1
, let

S_{T}\coloneqq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1},\qquad\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}\coloneqq\frac{1}{S_{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}{\bm{\theta}}_{t-1}\,.

(31)

Lemma E.1

(Deterministic bound on the finite prefix)
.

Assume the feature and reward bounds in Lemma
3.2
. For any nonnegative stepsizes
(\eta_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and any integer
m\geq 0
, the TD(0) iterates satisfy

\max_{0\leq s\leq m}\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{s}}\right\|\leq\left(\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{0}}\right\|+\frac{r_{\infty}}{(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}}\right)\prod_{t=0}^{m-1}\left(1+(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}^{2}\eta_{t}\right)-\frac{r_{\infty}}{(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}}\,.

In particular, for the stepsize (
30
), define

B_{m}\coloneqq\left(\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{0}}\right\|+\frac{r_{\infty}}{(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}}\right)\prod_{t=0}^{m-1}\left(1+\frac{1+\gamma}{c\sqrt{t+1}\log^{2}(t+3)}\right)-\frac{r_{\infty}}{(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}}\,.

Then
\max_{0\leq s\leq m}\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{s}}\right\|\leq B_{m}
. Furthermore,

B_{m}\leq\left(\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{0}}\right\|+\frac{r_{\infty}}{(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{2(1+\gamma)\sqrt{m}}{c\log^{2}3}\right)-\frac{r_{\infty}}{(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}}\,.

Proof.

The bounds in Lemma
3.2
imply

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{g}({\bm{\theta}}_{t},Z_{t})}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left|r_{t}+\gamma\left\langle\bm{\phi}(s_{t+1}),{\bm{\theta}}_{t}\right\rangle-\left\langle\bm{\phi}(s_{t}),{\bm{\theta}}_{t}\right\rangle\right|\left\|{\bm{\phi}(s_{t})}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}+(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}^{2}\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{t}}\right\|\,.

Therefore,

\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{t+1}}\right\|\leq(1+(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}^{2}\eta_{t})\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{t}}\right\|+r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}\eta_{t}\,.

Let
b\coloneqq r_{\infty}/((1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty})
and

u_{t}\coloneqq\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{t}}\right\|+b
. Then
u_{t+1}\leq(1+(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}^{2}\eta_{t})u_{t}
.
Iterating this inequality yields, for every
s\leq m
,

u_{s}\leq u_{0}\prod_{t=0}^{s-1}(1+(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}^{2}\eta_{t})\leq u_{0}\prod_{t=0}^{m-1}(1+(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}^{2}\eta_{t})\,.

Taking the supremum over
0\leq s\leq m
and subtracting
b
proves the first claim.
Substituting (
30
) gives the displayed formula for
B_{m}
.
Finally, using
1+x\leq e^{x}
and

\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t+1}\log^{2}(t+3)}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{m}}{\log^{2}3}

gives the closed-form bound.
∎

Corollary E.2

(Mixing-free stepsize)
.

Under Assumption
3.1
, with

\phi_{\infty},r_{\infty},\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
and
\omega
as in
Lemmas
3.2
–
3.4
, run TD(0) with the stepsize schedule:

\eta_{t}=\frac{1}{c\,\phi_{\infty}^{2}\sqrt{t+1}\log^{2}(t+3)},\qquad t\geq 0,

for some numerical constant
c>0
, and form the PR average
\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}
as in (
31
).
Let

m^{*}\coloneqq\min\{m\geq 0:\log(m+3)\geq\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\}\,.

For
m\geq 0
, define the deterministic prefix bound

B_{m}\coloneqq\left(\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{0}}\right\|+\frac{r_{\infty}}{(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}}\right)\prod_{t=0}^{m-1}\left(1+\frac{1+\gamma}{c\sqrt{t+1}\log^{2}(t+3)}\right)-\frac{r_{\infty}}{(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}}\,.

Fix any
\delta\in(0,1)
and set

R_{\mathrm{base}}\coloneqq\max\left\{B_{m^{*}}+\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}\right\|,\ \left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{*}}\right\|,\ \frac{r_{\infty}}{\phi_{\infty}}\right\}\,.

Define

A_{1}(\delta)\coloneqq 1536\sqrt{\frac{3}{\log 2}}\sqrt{2\log\frac{8}{\delta}}+2304,\qquad A_{2}\coloneqq\frac{8118}{\log 2},

c_{\min}(\delta)\coloneqq\frac{A_{1}(\delta)+\sqrt{A_{1}^{2}(\delta)+4A_{2}}}{2},\qquad\rho\coloneqq\frac{2c}{\sqrt{c^{2}-A_{1}(\delta)c-A_{2}}},\qquad R_{\mathrm{max}}\coloneqq\rho R_{\mathrm{base}}\,.

Then, provided that
c>c_{\min}(\delta)
, with probability at least
1-\delta
, we have

\sup_{t\geq 0}\ \left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{t}}\right\|\leq R_{\mathrm{max}},

and for every
T\geq 1
,
f(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T})-f({\bm{\theta}}^{*})
is upper-bounded by

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\,\min

\displaystyle\left\{\underbrace{\frac{c^{2}\phi_{\infty}^{4}\log^{4}(T+2)}{\omega(\sqrt{T+1}-1)^{2}}\left(1+\frac{264\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}}{c\log^{2}3}+\frac{176\sqrt{3}\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}}{c\sqrt{\log 2}}\sqrt{2\log\frac{8}{\delta}}+\frac{288\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}}{c^{2}\log 2}\right)^{2}}_{\text{fast rate}},\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.\underbrace{\frac{c\phi_{\infty}^{2}\log^{2}(T+2)}{4(\sqrt{T+1}-1)}\left(1+\frac{1152\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}}{c\log^{2}3}+\frac{768\sqrt{3}\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}}{c\sqrt{\log 2}}\sqrt{2\log\frac{8}{\delta}}+\frac{4032\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}+27}{c^{2}\log 2}\right)}_{\text{robust rate}}\right\}\,.

Equivalently, since
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\geq 1
, the horizon dependence is

f(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T})-f({\bm{\theta}}^{*})=\min\left\{\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\!\left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}^{2}\phi_{\infty}^{4}}{\omega T}\right),\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\!\left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\phi_{\infty}^{2}}{\sqrt{T}}\right)\right\},

where the
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\cdot)
notation hides logarithmic factors in
T
and
1/\delta
, as well as numerical constants depending on
c
.
Moreover, the prefactor depends doubly exponentially on
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
through its dependence on
R_{\mathrm{base}}
: using
m^{*}\leq e^{\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}}
and Lemma
E.1
,

B_{m^{*}}\leq\left(\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{0}}\right\|+\frac{r_{\infty}}{(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{2(1+\gamma)e^{\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}/2}}{c\log^{2}3}\right)-\frac{r_{\infty}}{(1+\gamma)\phi_{\infty}}\,.

Consequently, for fixed
c,\gamma,\phi_{\infty},r_{\infty},{\bm{\theta}}_{0},{\bm{\theta}}^{*}

\delta

C_{0}
independent of
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
such that

R_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\leq C_{0}\exp\left(\frac{4(1+\gamma)e^{\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}/2}}{c\log^{2}3}\right)=\exp\left(\mathcal{O}\bigl(e^{\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}/2}\bigr)\right)\,.

Thus the total fast and robust prefactors are both of doubly exponential order

\exp(\mathcal{O}(e^{\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}/2}))

\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}

Proof.

L_{\delta}\coloneqq\sqrt{2\log\frac{8}{\delta}},\qquad H\coloneqq\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\eta_{t}^{2}\,.

\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(a^{\prime}_{k})^{2}

Let
(f_{j})_{j\geq 0}
be a martingale in a
(2,D)
-smooth Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
with
f_{0}=0
.
Let
d_{j}:=f_{j}-f_{j-1}
,
f^{\ast}:=\sup_{j\geq 0}\ \|f_{j}\|
, and
\|d_{j}\|_{\infty}:=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}\|d_{j}\|
.
If
\sum_{j\geq 1}\|d_{j}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq b_{\ast}^{2}
, then for all
r\geq 0

\mathbb{P}\!\left(f^{\ast}\geq r\right)\leq 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2D^{2}b_{\ast}^{2}}\right)\,.

Lemma F.2

(Dirichlet matrix)
.

The Dirichlet matrix

\bm{L}_{\mathrm{Dir}}:=\bm{D}-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\bm{D}\bm{P}^{\mu}+(\bm{P}^{\mu})^{\top}\bm{D}\bigr)

is symmetric and positive semidefinite. More precisely, if

\bm{P}_{\mathrm{sym}}:=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\bm{P}^{\mu}+\bm{D}^{-1}(\bm{P}^{\mu})^{\top}\bm{D}\Bigr),

then, for every
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\bm{x}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\mathrm{Dir}}\bm{x}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\pi(i)\bm{P}_{\mathrm{sym}}(i,j)(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\pi(i)P^{\mu}(i,j)(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}\geq 0\,.

Proof.

First,
\bm{L}_{\mathrm{Dir}}
is symmetric because
\bm{D}^{\top}=\bm{D}
:

\bm{L}_{\mathrm{Dir}}^{\top}=\left(\bm{D}-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\bm{D}\bm{P}^{\mu}+(\bm{P}^{\mu})^{\top}\bm{D}\bigr)\right)^{\top}=\bm{D}-\frac{1}{2}\bigl((\bm{P}^{\mu})^{\top}\bm{D}+\bm{D}\bm{P}^{\mu}\bigr)=\bm{L}_{\mathrm{Dir}}\,.

Define the time-reversal kernel

\bm{P}_{\mathrm{rev}}^{\mu}:=\bm{D}^{-1}(\bm{P}^{\mu})^{\top}\bm{D},\qquad\bm{P}_{\mathrm{rev}}^{\mu}(i,j)=\frac{\pi(j)P^{\mu}(j,i)}{\pi(i)}\,.

Since
\pi^{\top}\bm{P}^{\mu}=\pi^{\top}
,
\bm{P}_{\mathrm{rev}}^{\mu}
is row-stochastic:

\sum_{j}\bm{P}_{\mathrm{rev}}^{\mu}(i,j)=\frac{1}{\pi(i)}\sum_{j}\pi(j)P^{\mu}(j,i)=1\,.

Hence
\bm{P}_{\mathrm{sym}}=\frac{1}{2}(\bm{P}^{\mu}+\bm{P}_{\mathrm{rev}}^{\mu})
is also row-stochastic and satisfies detailed balance:

\pi(i)\bm{P}_{\mathrm{sym}}(i,j)=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\pi(i)P^{\mu}(i,j)+\pi(j)P^{\mu}(j,i)\bigr)=\pi(j)\bm{P}_{\mathrm{sym}}(j,i)\,.

Moreover,

\bm{D}\bm{P}_{\mathrm{sym}}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\bm{D}\bm{P}^{\mu}+(\bm{P}^{\mu})^{\top}\bm{D}\bigr),

so
\bm{L}_{\mathrm{Dir}}=\bm{D}-\bm{D}\bm{P}_{\mathrm{sym}}
. Therefore, for any
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\mathrm{Dir}}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\pi(i)x_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i,j}\pi(i)\bm{P}_{\mathrm{sym}}(i,j)x_{i}x_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\pi(i)\bm{P}_{\mathrm{sym}}(i,j)(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}\geq 0,

where the second equality uses that
\bm{P}_{\mathrm{sym}}
is row-stochastic and satisfies detailed balance.
Finally, using the displayed identity for
\pi(i)\bm{P}_{\mathrm{sym}}(i,j)
and swapping indices in the second term gives

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\pi(i)\bm{P}_{\mathrm{sym}}(i,j)(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\pi(i)P^{\mu}(i,j)(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}\,.

This proves the claimed identity and
\bm{L}_{\mathrm{Dir}}\succeq 0
.
∎

Lemma F.3

(Logarithmic stepsize estimates)
.

The following estimates hold, where
\log(\cdot)
denotes the natural logarithm.

(i) Square summability.
Let
(a_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be defined by

a_{t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t+1}\,\log(t+3)}\qquad\forall t\geq 0\,.

Define the tail sums

Q_{T}\;:=\;\sum_{t=T}^{\infty}a_{t}^{2},\qquad T\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\,.

Then, for all integers
T\geq 0
,

Q_{T}\;\leq\;\frac{3}{\log(T+2)}\,.

(40)

In particular,

\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}a_{t}^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{3}{\log 2}\;<\;\infty\,.

(ii) Partial-sum lower bound.
Let
p\geq 0
,
\kappa>0
, and let
(\eta_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be defined by

\eta_{t}=\frac{1}{\kappa\sqrt{t+1}\,\log^{p}(t+3)},\qquad t\geq 0\,.

For every integer
T\geq 1
, define

S_{T}\coloneqq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t-1}\,.

Then

S_{T}\geq\frac{2(\sqrt{T+1}-1)}{\kappa\log^{p}(T+2)}\,.

Proof.

For (i), by definition,

a_{t}^{2}=\frac{1}{(t+1)\,\log^{2}(t+3)}\,.

For every
t\geq 0
, we have
t+1\geq\frac{t+3}{3}
, hence

\frac{1}{(t+1)\log^{2}(t+3)}\leq\frac{3}{(t+3)\log^{2}(t+3)}\,.

Therefore,

Q_{T}\leq\sum_{t=T}^{\infty}\frac{3}{(t+3)\log^{2}(t+3)}\,.

Let
n=t+3
. Then

\sum_{t=T}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(t+3)\log^{2}(t+3)}=\sum_{n=T+3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n\log^{2}n}\,.

Now define
f(x)=\frac{1}{x\log^{2}x}
for
x>1
. Since
f
is decreasing on
(1,\infty)
,
the integral test yields, for all
N\geq 3
,

\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}f(n)\leq\int_{N-1}^{\infty}f(x)\,dx=\int_{N-1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x\log^{2}x}\,dx=\frac{1}{\log(N-1)}\,.

Applying this with
N=T+3
gives

\sum_{n=T+3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n\log^{2}n}\leq\frac{1}{\log(T+2)}\,.

Substituting back proves (
40
). Taking
T=0
gives the claimed finite upper bound on

\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}a_{t}^{2}
.

For (ii), let
s=t
. Then

S_{T}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}\,\log^{p}(s+2)}\,.

Since
p\geq 0
,
\log(\cdot)
is increasing, and
s+2\leq T+2
for all
1\leq s\leq T
,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}\,\log^{p}(s+2)}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}\,\log^{p}(T+2)}\,.

Therefore,

S_{T}\geq\frac{1}{\kappa\log^{p}(T+2)}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\,.

The function
x\mapsto x^{-1/2}
is decreasing on
[1,\infty)
, hence

\sum_{s=1}^{T}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\geq\int_{1}^{T+1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx=2(\sqrt{T+1}-1)\,.

Combining the last two displays proves the claim.
∎
