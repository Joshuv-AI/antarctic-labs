Performance investigation of 160 Gb/s inter-satellite laser communication transmission system based on OFDM and PDM transmission with advanced signal processing techniques
Authors: Ammar Armghan, Sultan S Aldkeelalah, Slim Chaoui, Ali Elrashidi, Mehtab Singh, Somia A Abd El-Mottaleb, Moustafa H Aly
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHigh-capacity inter-satellite laser communication links are essential for global coverage, remote sensing, and deep space missions, but face challenges from path loss, pointing errors, and limited aperture sizes that degrade performance at extended ranges and high data rates. This work reports simulative evaluation of an Inter-Satellite Laser Communication Transmission (Is-LCT) system with 160 Gb/s data rate. Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM), Polarization Division Multiplexing (PDM), and 32-level Quadrature Amplitude Modulation (32-QAM) techniques have been used to enhance the system baud rate, bandwidth efficiency and transmission rate of the system. Advanced signal processing techniques have been used to improve the performance of the proposed system. The proposed Is-LCT system is investigated for enhancing range, optical efficiency, aperture diameter, laser power, and pointing error using Error Vector Magnitude (EVM), Bit Error Rate (BER), and constellation as the metrics for performance evaluation. The obtained results demonstrate reliable 160 Gb/s data transmission at 7000 km Is-LCT range with BER3.8, EVM 12%, and clear constellation of the received optical signal.Keywords: Inter-satellite laser communication transmission, Orthogonal frequency division multiplexing, Polarization division multiplexing, 32-QAM, Coherent detection, Advanced signal processing, Receiver aperture optimizationSubject terms: Engineering, Optics and photonics

Introduction

Laser communication is a promising information transmission technology which uses light beams to transmit information through a wired or wireless medium in Line-of-Sight (LoS) or Non-Line-of-Sight (NLoS) configurations1.

In a wireless medium, laser communication, also referred to as Optical Wireless Communication (OWC) transmission offers several distinct advantages over Radio-Frequency (RF) based wireless transmission links. OWC technology leverages on the abundantly available license-free optical spectrum of the electromagnetic waves to transmit information2,3. OWC systems provide high modulation bandwidth for high-speed information transmission links for various applications such as live-streaming, video-conferencing, real-time sensors networks are achievable4. It transmits data via narrow laser beams, making signal interception challenging, thus offering secure links for various applications where data privacy is crucial including military applications, financial services, and health-care information exchange5.

Optical signals are immune to electromagnetic interference unlike conventional RF and microwave communication and offer low latency networks. Other merits of OWC links includes use of light-weight and power-efficient equipment, easy installation and redeploy-ability, low footprint, and cost-effectiveness6,7. Due to numerous merits of OWC links, they find applications in broad areas including ground information transmission links like ship-to-ship network, building-to-building network, etc8–13., underwater OWC transmission links for various maritime applications14, indoor OWC links15,16, and space links17.

Is-LCT is an important application of OWC technology to transmit high-speed information amongst number of satellites in the outer space18. Is-LCT links are essential for providing worldwide coverage for deep space exploration, remote sensing applications, and information transmission. But, the performance of Is-LCT is limited by many internal and external factors including transmission power, efficiency of transmitter and receiver optics, path loss factor, misalignment and pointing errors, background noise radiations, and aperture diameter of receiver lens19.

Related work

Over the past few years, a lot of research work has been reported on the use of different multiplexing and signal modulation techniques in Is-LCT links. A. Gupta et al.20 reported a direct-modulated laser based Is-LCT link and investigated the performance for different parameters. The authors reported faithful 10Gb/s-1000 km Is-LCT performance. M. Singh et al.21 reported an Is-LCT link using 3-dimensional orthogonal-modulation technique, employing non-return-to-zero-different quadrature phase shift keying-polarization shift keying modulation technique. The authors reported 120 Gb/s-26,000 km transmission with faithful link performance. A. Grover et al.22 reported a 20 Gb/s Is-LCT link employing 4-level Quadrature-Amplitude-Modulation(QAM) signals for different scenarios. The authors reported faithful transmission along 35,000 km Is-LCT range with acceptable signal performance.

A 20 Gb/s-1000 km Is-LCT link using Mode Division Multiplexing (MDM) was discussed by A. Sharma et al. in23. A. Grover et al.24 discussed the application of MDM technology to realize a high-speed Is-LCT link under the influence of pointing errors. 40 Gb/s-6000 km transmission was successfully demonstrated by the authors. The use of multiple transmitter/receiver aperture to realize a 32 Gb/s long-range Is-LCT has been discussed by M.H Majeed et al.25. M. Singh et al.26 reported the performance of Is-LCT link using improved detection technique to realize an 80 Gb/s-10,000 km transmission faithfully. M.M. Tawfik et al.27 reported a 45,000 km Is-LCT link and investigated the performance for different link parameters. The impact of induced electron-radiation on the performance of Is-LCT was discussed by A.S. Youssouf et al. in28.

To realize high-speed spectral-efficient Is-LCT links, Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) is a crucial technique. It divides the available carrier signal bandwidth into multiple subcarriers where each subcarrier is used to transmit a portion of the information signal. The spacing between the subcarriers is placed such that they are orthogonal to each other and thus mitigating inter-channel and inter-symbol interference29. OFDM can be incorporated in Is-LCT links to achieve higher transmission rates, improved link reliability and robustness. Additionally, the Polarization Division Multiplexing (PDM) technique, which uses orthogonal polarized beams of a single laser signal to transmit distinct high-speed data signals, can improve the spectrum efficiency, scalability, and data rates in the Is-LCT link30. The integration of OFDM and PDM in Is-LCT systems offers three main advantages: (i) OFDM divides the high data rate into parallel low-rate subcarriers, reducing bandwidth requirements per component and mitigating ISI over long distances; (ii) PDM doubles the spectral efficiency by transmitting independent data streams on orthogonal polarization states of a single laser carrier; and (iii) their combination with high-order 32-QAM modulation achieves 160 Gb/s transmission at a practical 16 GBd baud rate, enabling reliable operation under path loss, pointing errors, and aperture constraints typical of inter-satellite links.

Proposed OFDM-PDM-Is-LCT link design

In this work, 160 Gb/s information is transmitted between two satellites in outer space using an Is-LCT link incorporating OFDM and PDM techniques with 32-QAM signals. Further, to improve the signal quality under the effect of path loss and to enhance the link range, the use of advanced signal processing techniques is discussed. Figure 1 reports the transmission section of the proposed Is-LCT link.

Transmitter section of proposed Is-LCT link.

A 160 Gb/s information is emitted from Pseudo-Random-Bit Sequence(PRBS) generator. Using a serial (S)/parallel (P) converter and a 32-QAM modulator section, the binary signal is divided into two separate 80 Gb/s streams (Fig. 2). A polarization splitter is employed to optically modulate each 32-QAM 80Gb/s modulated signal across an orthogonal 850 nm laser beam from a continuous wave (CW) laser. A polarization combiner is used to merge the orthogonally modulated signals before they are sent to the receiving satellite via an outer space channel.

Schematic of 32-QAM modulator.

The selection of 32-QAM is motivated by an optimal trade-off between spectral efficiency and link margin. Using PDM-OFDM with 32-QAM, the system achieves 160 Gb/s at 16 GBd per polarization, well within the bandwidth of dual-drive MZMs (~ 20–35 GHz) and ADC/DAC hardware (~ 64–100 GSa/s) used in space-qualified coherent systems. Lower-order 16-QAM would require ~ 20 GBd to maintain 160 Gb/s, increasing hardware demands, while 64-QAM requires ~ 6 dB higher SNR for equivalent BER performance, a margin unavailable at Is-LCT ranges beyond 7000 km under the path loss and pointing error conditions of this work. Thus, 32-QAM represents the most practical modulation format for the proposed system.

The schematic of 32-QAM modulator is shown in Fig. 2. The input 80 Gb/s bits are first combined using 5 bits/symbol and then separated into 2-streams (upper and lower). Independent stream is converted into an M- ary electrical signal and then modulated over a distinct orthogonal optical beam using dual-drive-Mach-Zehnder-modulator(MZM). To achieve in-phase quadrature 32-QAM modulation, the upper and lower arms undergo a 90-degree phase shift. The signal after propagating through outer space channel is received at the receiver section as shown in Fig. 3.

Schematic of proposed Is-LCT receiver section.

The DSP unit first applies a polarization demultiplexing algorithm based on the Constant Modulus Algorithm (CMA) to separate the two orthogonal PDM tributaries and compensate for residual polarization crosstalk introduced during inter-satellite propagation. Following polarization demultiplexing, channel estimation is applied to each tributary independently to compensate for amplitude and phase distortions introduced by path loss, pointing errors, and hardware imperfections. Subsequently, CPE corrects residual phase noise and frequency offset between the received optical signal and the local oscillator, which is critical in coherent PDM-OFDM-32QAM systems to maintain constellation integrity and achieve the reported BER and EVM performance. Using a polarization splitter, the input signal is first polarization divided and combined with the appropriate parts from a local oscillator. A 90-degree optical hybrid is used to transform the phase information into intensity information. Balanced detectors (BDs) subsequently convert the optical signal into an electrical one. The electrical signal is amplified using an electrical amplifier (EA) and further given to a DSP unit where channel estimation and carrier phase estimation algorithms are employed to improve the signal quality. The DSP block estimates the channel impairments such as amplitude and phase distortions introduced by path loss, pointing errors, and residual hardware imperfections in the inter-satellite optical channel. The channel estimation enable equalization, compensating for these impairments on the received OFDM–PDM 32-QAM signals, thereby reducing inter-symbol interference (ISI) and improving signal quality before symbol decisions. Following channel equalization, this block corrects residual phase noise and frequency offset between the received optical signal and the local oscillator reference, which is critical in high-order QAM and coherent OFDM systems to maintain constellation integrity and avoid phase errors that degrade BER and EVM. The information is retrieved using a decision circuit, followed by a 32-QAM sequence decoder.

Practical feasibility is confirmed by alignment with experimental demonstrations. NASA’s Lunar Laser Communication Demonstration (LLCD) achieved 622 Mbps over 384,000 km using 10–20 cm apertures and Watts-class lasers comparable to our 20–32 dBm, 10–22 cm design32. ESA’s Optical Ground Station demonstrated < 2 µrad pointing accuracy during LLCD operations, validating our 1–4 µrad range33. Coherent 32-QAM at 16 GBd scales from lab demonstrations exceeding 1 Tb/s with 8.6 b/s/Hz efficiency34. Our BER < 10⁻³ (EVM < 12%) satisfies hard-decision FEC thresholds, confirming 160 Gbps OFDM-PDM Is-LCT feasibility within proven technology constraints. Table 1 displays the simulation parameters considered in this work. The simulation model comprehensively accounts for several real-world channel impairment effects. The coherent receiver incorporates shot noise due to background solar radiation and thermal noise at the balanced detectors. The CW laser model includes relative intensity noise (RIN) and laser phase noise, both of which degrade constellation quality and contribute to the EVM floor observed in the results. Doppler frequency shift arising from relative satellite motion (up to 7.5 km/s for LEO satellites) is modeled as a carrier frequency offset and is compensated by the CPE algorithm in the DSP unit. Inter-polarization crosstalk in the PDM scheme is inherently captured within the polarization demultiplexing receiver stage. Together, these impairments represent a realistic outer-space channel environment, and their combined effect is reflected in the BER and EVM performance metrics reported in Sect. 3.

While larger apertures collect more power (expected), our analysis uncovers that receiver diameter ≥ 18 cm becomes a hard requirement for 32-QAM OFDM-PDM to maintain BER < 10− 3 at 11,000 km. Below 18 cm, PDM polarization crosstalk + OFDM subcarrier phase noise overwhelm DSP compensation, causing irreducible EVM > 15% even at maximum power, a limit not apparent in simpler modulation schemes.

Conclusion

We have reported the modeling and performance evaluation of an Is-LCT system incorporating PDM and OFDM for augmented bandwidth efficiency and data transmission rate. 160 Gb/s data is faithfully transmitted over a single-channel between two satellites. The baud rate of the system is reduced to 16 Gb/s by using 32-level QAM signals. The Is-LCT system is analyzed for various link parameters and the obtained results demonstrate good transmission performance at 7000 km inter-satellite transmission range by employing 30 dBm laser power with 15 cm receiver aperture diameter. On the other hand, the transmission range increases to 11,000 km by employing 32 dBm laser power with 22 cm receiver aperture diameter. Additionally, the results show that the system’s performance deteriorates as misalignment pointing errors rise. The proposed Is-LCT system can be deployed for high-speed global communication, deep space exploration, and remote sensing applications. Future work could explore experimental validation of the proposed OFDM-PDM Is-LCT system using terrestrial testbeds or satellite demonstrators, investigate hybrid modulation formats (e.g., combining orbital angular momentum multiplexing with PDM), incorporate atmospheric turbulence models for ground-to-satellite extensions, and optimize DSP algorithms for higher-order QAM (e.g., 64/128-QAM) to further enhance spectral efficiency and transmission range.
