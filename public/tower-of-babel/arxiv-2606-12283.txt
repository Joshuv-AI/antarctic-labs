Abstract
. For any closed surface of genus at least 3 3 , equipped with any bounding spin structure, we show that the index difference, viewed as a map from the fundamental group of the space of Dirac-invertible Riemannian metrics to 𝐊𝐎 − 4 ​ ( ∗ ) \mathbf{KO}^{-4}(*) , is non-trivial. For products of two such surfaces, equipped with any spin structure, we prove that the corresponding space of Dirac-invertible Riemannian metrics is not contractible. We discuss the relationship of this result to the existence of metrics with harmonic spinors in dimension 4 4 .



1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a closed and connected spin manifold of dimension
n
and let
\mathcal{R}(M)
denote the space of Riemannian metrics on
M
, equipped with the
C^{\infty}
topology. Index theory has been one of the main tools for studying the space
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{psc}}(M)\subset\mathcal{R}(M)
of positive scalar curvature metrics. Building on the surgery results by Gromov-Lawson
[
17
]
and Schoen-Yau
[
28
]
(see also
[
15
]
), Stolz
[
30
]
proved that any closed and simply connected spin manifold of dimension at least
5
admits a metric of positive scalar curvature if and only if the
\mathbf{KO}^{-n}(*)
-valued index of the Dirac operator vanishes. To study the homotopy type of the space
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{psc}}(M)
, Hitchin
[
21
, Section 4.4]
, constructed a map

(1)

\displaystyle\inddiff:\pi_{\ell}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{psc}}(M),g_{0})\to\mathbf{KO}^{-(n+\ell+1)}(*),

which later was called the index difference (see also
[
16
]
). Using the Atiyah-Singer family index theorem
[
4
]
, Hitchin showed that this map is nontrivial if
n\equiv 0,1\mod{8}
and
\ell=0
or
n\equiv 0,7\mod{8}
and
\ell=1
. This map has been studied further by many
[
9
,
11
,
12
,
20
,
18
,
24
,
16
]
. For example, Botvinnik, Ebert, and Randal-Williams
[
9
]
showed that if
n\geq 6
, then the map (
1
) is nontrivial for all
\ell\geq 0
, whenever the codomain is nontrivial.

The manifolds considered in this paper do not admit metrics of positive scalar curvature. Instead, we are interested in the larger space of metrics for which the Dirac operator is invertible, denoted by
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(M)
. This is precisely the space needed to construct the index difference. Specifically, the map (
1
) factors through the map

(2)

\displaystyle\inddiff:\pi_{\ell}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(M),g_{0})\to\mathbf{KO}^{-(n+\ell+1)}(*),

which only needs
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(M)\neq\emptyset
to be defined. If one can show that this map is non-trivial for some
\ell
, then the space
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(M)
is not equal to the contractible space
\mathcal{R}(M)
of all Riemannian metrics and hence, there is a metric on
M
with a harmonic spinor. Using this technique, Hitchin
[
21
, Theorem 4.5]
proved that if
n\equiv 0,1,7\mod{8}
, then
M
admits a metric with a harmonic spinor. Using different techniques, Bär
[
6
, Theorem A]
proved the analogous statement for
n\equiv 3\mod{4}
. Inspired by these results, Bär and Dahl
[
7
]
conjectured that any closed spin manifold of dimension at least
3
admits a metric with a harmonic spinor. Seeger
[
29
]
proved the conjecture for spheres of dimension
n\equiv 0\mod{4}
. Dahl
[
13
, Theorem 4.1 & Corollary 4.2]
proved the conjecture for certain simply connected manifolds of dimension at least
5
with a cyclic group action; in particular, Dahl proved the conjecture for any sphere of dimension at least
5
. Crowley, Schick, and Steimle
[
11
, Theorem 1.4]
, proved the conjecture in all cases with
n\geq 6
. Specifically, they show the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Crowley, Schick, Steimle, 2018)
.

If
\dim M=n\geq 6
, then for all
\ell\geq 0
with
\ell+n\equiv 0,1\mod{8}
, and for all
g_{0}\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(M)
the map

\displaystyle\mathrm{\inddiff}:\pi_{\ell}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(M),g_{0})\to\mathbf{KO}^{-(n+\ell+1)}(\{*\})

is a split surjection.

We see that the conjecture by Bär and Dahl is still open for
n\in\{4,5\}
. The case
n=2
is not mentioned in this conjecture because of the following. Hitchin
[
21
, Proposition 2.3]
proved that for any closed surface
\Sigma_{\gamma}
, equipped with any spin structure, we have for all metrics
g\in\mathcal{R}(\Sigma_{\gamma})
that

(3)

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{H}}\ker{\not{D}^{g}}\leq\Big\lfloor\frac{\gamma+1}{2}\Big\rfloor,

where
\not{D}^{g}
denotes the Dirac operator acting on sections of the spinor bundle associated to the irreducible representation of
C\ell_{2}\cong\mathbb{H}
. This means that if
\gamma\leq 2
and
\Sigma_{\gamma}
is equipped with any bounding spin structure, then there does not exist a metric with a harmonic spinor, i.e.
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Sigma_{\gamma})=\mathcal{R}(\Sigma_{\gamma})
. For any closed surface
\Sigma_{\gamma}
of genus
\gamma\geq 2
equipped with any spin structure
\mathfrak{s}
, Bär and Schmutz
[
8
]
showed that for any integer
k
with
0\leq 2k\leq\gamma+1
and with
k
even if the spin structure
\mathfrak{s}
is bounding and
k
odd if the spin structure
\mathfrak{s}
is non-bounding, then there is a Riemannian metric
g
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{H}}\ker{\not{D}^{g}}=k
. Hence any closed spin surface
\Sigma_{\gamma}
of genus
\gamma\geq 3
, admits a metric with a harmonic spinor. An alternative proof for the existence of a metric with a harmonic spinor was given by Ammann, Weiß, and Witt
[
3
]
.

The index difference is also related to the following. Let
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{min}}(M)\subset\mathcal{R}(M)
be the set of Riemannian metrics for which the kernel of the corresponding Dirac operator attains the lower bound given by the
\mathbf{KO}^{-n}(*)
-valued index. Note that if this index vanishes, we have that
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(M)=\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{min}}(M)
. Maier
[
25
]
showed that
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{min}}(M)
is dense in
\mathcal{R}(M)
if
n\leq 4
. Ammann, Dahl, and Humbert
[
1
]
showed the corresponding density result in all dimensions. Further topological information about the space
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{min}}
was obtained by Ammann and Dahl
[
2
]
, where they showed the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(Ammann, Dahl, 2025)
.

If
\dim M\in\{2,4\}
, then
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{min}}(M)
is connected.

The goal of this article is to show that this is sharp in dimension
2
, in the following sense.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Sigma_{\gamma}
be a closed surface of genus
\gamma\geq 3
, equipped with any bounding spin structure. Then, for every
g_{0}\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Sigma_{\gamma})
, the index difference

(4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\inddiff}:\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Sigma_{\gamma}),g_{0})\to\mathbf{KO}^{-4}(*)\cong\mathbb{Z}.

is a non-trivial homomorphism.

Remark 1.4
.

Note that Theorem
1.3
gives a topological proof for the abovementioned result that any spin surface of genus at least
3
admits a metric with a harmonic spinor. Also note that
\gamma\geq 3
in the above theorem is sharp, because as mentioned above, we have for any
\beta\leq 2
that
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Sigma_{\beta})=\mathcal{R}(\Sigma_{\beta})
.

We also conclude the following result.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Sigma_{\gamma^{1}}\times\Sigma_{\gamma^{2}}
be a product of closed surfaces of genera
{\gamma}^{1},{\gamma}^{2}\geq 3
, equipped with any spin structure. Then, at least one of the groups

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Sigma_{\gamma^{1}}\times\Sigma_{\gamma^{2}})),

\displaystyle\pi_{2}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Sigma_{\gamma^{1}}\times\Sigma_{\gamma^{2}})),

\displaystyle\pi_{3}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Sigma_{\gamma^{1}}\times\Sigma_{\gamma^{2}}))

is non-trivial.

Remark 1.6
.

It is believed that if two non-empty closed spin manifolds
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
of dimension at least
3
are spin bordant to each other, then there is a homotopy equivalence
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(M_{1})\overset{H}{\to}~\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(M_{2})
. One should view this as the spinoral analogue of the Chernysh/Walsh surgery result
[
10
,
31
,
15
]
for positive scalar curvature. Substantial progress towards such a homotopy equivalence
H
has been made in
[
19
,
27
]
. If this is proven, then Theorem
1.5
proves the case
n=4
of the conjecture by Bär and Dahl discussed above. To see this, recall that the
\widehat{A}
-genus gives an isomorphism
\Omega_{4}^{\mathrm{spin}}\overset{\cong}{\to}2\mathbb{Z}
, where
\Omega_{4}^{\mathrm{spin}}
is the spin bordism group in dimension
4
. Hence, if
M
is a closed
4
-dimensional spin manifold with vanishing
\widehat{A}
-genus, then it is spin bordant to
\Sigma_{3}\times\Sigma_{3}
, equipped with any spin structure. By Theorem
1.5
, we know that
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Sigma_{3}\times\Sigma_{3})
is not contractible and therefore, assuming that such a homotopy equivalence
H:\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Sigma_{3}\times\Sigma_{3})\to\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(M)
exists, we conclude that there is a metric
g
on
M
with a harmonic spinor.

Remark 1.7
.

Note also that Theorem
1.3
shows that such a homotopy equivalence
H
as mentioned in Remark
1.6
cannot exist in dimension
2
.

Remark 1.8
.

There is an unwritten conjecture by Ammann and Dahl which says that in dimension
4
, the space
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(M)
is
2
-connected. If this conjecture is proven, we get under the assumptions of Theorem
1.5
, that the index difference

(5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\inddiff}:\pi_{3}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Sigma_{\gamma^{1}}\times\Sigma_{\gamma^{2}}),g)\to\mathbf{KO}^{-8}(*)\cong\mathbb{Z}.

is non-trivial. The reader may see how this is deduced by inspecting the proofs of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.5
below.

2.
Preliminaries and notation

All spaces used in this section will be assumed sufficiently nice so that
G
-principal bundles are classified by maps into
BG
, and that the standard theory of covering spaces holds. For example, the reader may assume that all spaces are paracompact, Hausdorff, locally path connected and semilocally simply connected. For the proofs of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.5
, we will only use manifolds and CW-complexes. Mapping spaces of the form
C^{\infty}(X,Y)
used in this article will carry the weak
C^{\infty}
topology.

2.1.
Spin structures on fiber bundles

In this section, we recall the necessary definitions to state the obstruction for a smooth fiber bundle to admit a spin structure, extending a given spin structure on the fiber. We refer the reader to
[
14
, chapter 3]
for a detailed presentation.

Let
\theta:\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}^{+}_{n}}\to\mathrm{GL}^{+}_{n}
be the connected double covering group, where
\mathrm{GL}^{+}_{n}
denotes the identity component of the general linear group
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
. Let
E\to X
be an oriented real vector bundle of rank
n
and let
\mathrm{GL}^{+}(E)
be the bundle of positively oriented frames on
E
. A spin structure
\mathfrak{s}=(\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}^{+}}(E),\Theta)
on
E
is a lift of the structure group of
\mathrm{GL}^{+}(E)
along
\theta
. This means that
\Theta:\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}^{+}}(E)\to\mathrm{GL}^{+}(E)
is a
\theta
-equivariant map of principal bundles, covering the identity map on
X
. An isomorphism between two spin structures
\mathfrak{s}_{0}=(\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}^{+}}(E)_{0},\Theta_{0})
, and
\mathfrak{s}_{1}=(\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}^{+}}(E)_{1},\Theta_{1})
on
E
is an isomorphism
F:\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}^{+}}(E)_{0}\to\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}^{+}}(E)_{1}
of
\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}^{+}_{n}}
-principal bundles, covering the identity on
X
, such that
\Theta_{0}=\Theta_{1}\circ F
. An orientable vector bundle which can be equipped with a spin structure is called a spinnable vector bundle and an oriented vector bundle which is equipped with a spin structure is called a spin vector bundle. An oriented vector bundle
E\to X
is spinnable if and only if the second Stiefel–Whitney class
w_{2}(E)\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
vanishes. If
\mathfrak{s}=(\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}^{+}}(E),\Theta)
is a spin structure on
E\to X
and
f:Y\to X
is a continuous map, then the pullback
f^{*}\mathfrak{s}:=(f^{*}\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}^{+}}(E),f^{*}\Theta)
defines a spin structure on
f^{*}E\to Y
. A spin structure on an oriented smooth manifold
M
is a spin structure on the tangent bundle
TM\to M
. A spinnable manifold is an orientable smooth manifold which can be equipped with a spin structure and a spin manifold is an oriented smooth manifold which has been equipped with a spin structure.

Let
\text{Diff}(M)
denote the group of orientation preserving diffeomorphisms of
M
. For
f\in\text{Diff}(M)
, we can pull back any spin structure
\mathfrak{s}
on
M
, to a spin structure
f^{*}\mathfrak{s}
on
f^{*}TM
as above. Since this is not a spin structure on
M
, we use the isomorphism
df:TM\overset{\cong}{\to}f^{*}TM
of oriented vector bundles covering the identity, to define the pullback of a spin structure
\mathfrak{s}=(\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}^{+}}(M),\Theta)
on
M
, via
f\in\text{Diff}(M)
, as
f^{*}\mathfrak{s}:=(f^{*}\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}^{+}}(M),df^{-1}\circ f^{*}\Theta)
. The group
\text{Diff}(M)
acts on
\text{Spin}(M)
from the right via pullback, where
\text{Spin}(M)
is the set of isomorphism classes of spin structures on
M
. The stabilizer in
\mathrm{Diff}(M)
of the class represented by
\mathfrak{s}
is denoted by
\mathrm{Diff}(M,\mathfrak{s})
. If
f_{0},f_{1}\in\mathrm{Diff}(M)
are isotopic, then their aforementioned action on
\text{Spin}(M)
coincides. Therefore, we get an action

(6)

\displaystyle\text{Spin}(M)\times

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(M))\to\text{Spin}(M)

\displaystyle([\mathfrak{s}],[

\displaystyle f])\mapsto[f^{*}\mathfrak{s}],

and we write
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(M),\mathfrak{s})
to denote the stabilizer of
[\mathfrak{s}]
in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(M))
.

Assume now that
M
is a connected, oriented, and smooth manifold equipped with some spin structure
\mathfrak{s}
. We say that a spin diffeomorphism of
(M,\mathfrak{s})
is a diffeomorphism
f:M\to M
, together with a choice of isomorphism
F:f^{*}\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{s}
of spin structures on
M
. Let
\mathrm{SDiff}(M,\mathfrak{s})
denote the group of spin diffeomorphisms
(f,F)
. Note that the kernel of the forgetful surjection
\mathrm{SDiff}(M,\mathfrak{s})\to\mathrm{Diff}(M,\mathfrak{s})
, has two elements and hence we get the group extension

(7)

\displaystyle 0\to\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\to\mathrm{SDiff}(M,\mathfrak{s})\to\mathrm{Diff}(M,\mathfrak{s})\to 0.

Passing to classifying spaces, we get the homotopy fiber sequence

(8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{RP}^{\infty}\to B\mathrm{SDiff}(M,\mathfrak{s})\to B\mathrm{Diff}(M,\mathfrak{s}),

and we let
c(M,\mathfrak{s})\in H^{2}(B\hskip 1.13809pt\mathrm{Diff}(M,\mathfrak{s}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
denote the obstruction class for the existence of a section of (
8
).

We say that a fiber bundle
p:Z\to B
with fiber
M
is smooth and oriented if the structure group is
\mathrm{Diff}(M)
. Let
p:Z\to B
be a smooth, oriented fiber bundle with fiber
M
being a connected, smooth manifold. For simplicity, we will also assume that
B
is connected. Letting
Q_{Z}\to B
be the corresponding
\mathrm{Diff}(M)
-principal bundle associated to
p:Z\to B
, we define the vertical tangent bundle of
Z\to B
as
T^{v}Z:=Q_{Z}\times_{\mathrm{Diff}(M)}TM\to Q_{Z}\times_{\mathrm{Diff}(M)}M\cong Z
, where
\mathrm{Diff}(M)
acts on
TM
via the tangent map. This is a vector bundle over
Z
and the pullback along any fiber inclusion
M\cong Z_{b}\subset Z
is isomorphic to the tangent bundle of
M
. Note that by composing with the map
p
, we can also view
T^{v}Z
as a bundle of vector bundles
T^{v}Z\to B
with fiber
TM\to M
. A spin structure on
Z\overset{p}{\to}B
extending a spin structure
\mathfrak{s}
on
M
is a reduction of the structure group of
Q_{Z}
to a
\mathrm{Diff}(M,\mathfrak{s})
-principal bundle
Q^{\prime}_{Z}
along the inclusion
\mathrm{Diff}(M,\mathfrak{s})\subset\mathrm{Diff}(M)
, followed by a lift of the structure group of
Q^{\prime}_{Z}
along the forgetful map
\mathrm{SDiff}(M,\mathfrak{s})\to\mathrm{Diff}(M)
to an
\mathrm{SDiff}(M,\mathfrak{s})
-principal bundle
P_{Z}\to B
. A spin structure on
Z\to B
induces a spin structure on the vector bundle
T^{v}Z\to Z
.

The bundle
p:Z\to B
is classified by a map
\hat{p}:B\to B\mathrm{Diff}(M)
. The induced map on fundamental groups gives a homomorphism

\displaystyle\rho:\pi_{1}(B,b_{0})\overset{\hat{p}_{*}}{\to}\pi_{1}(B\mathrm{Diff}(M),*)\overset{\cong}{\to}\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(M)),

where the second map is the connecting homomorphism coming from the long exact sequence of homotopy groups corresponding to the fiber bundle
E\mathrm{Diff}(M)\to B\mathrm{Diff}(M)
. The map
\rho
is called the monodromy representation of the bundle
p
at the base point
b_{0}
. Since we assume
B
to be connected, we will ignore any reference to a base point and simply refer to the monodromy representation. The following proposition was proven by Ebert in
[
14
, Corollary 3.5.5]
.

Proposition 2.1

(Ebert, 2006)
.

Let
p:Z\to B
be a smooth fiber bundle with fiber
M
. Then, for any spin structure
\mathfrak{s}
on
M
, there exists a spin structure on
p:Z\to B
, extending
\mathfrak{s}
, if and only if the image of the monodromy representation
\rho
is contained inside
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(M),\mathfrak{s})
and the class
\hat{p}^{*}c(M,\mathfrak{s})\in H^{2}(B,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
vanishes.

2.2.
Riemannian metrics along the fibers of a fiber bundle

We continue with the notation from the previous section. Recall that
\mathcal{R}(M)
denotes the space of Riemannian metrics on
M
. We let
\mathrm{Diff}(M)
act on
\mathcal{R}(M)
from the left via pushforward. This means that the action is given by

(9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Diff}(M)\times\mathcal{R}(M)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{R}(M)

\displaystyle(f,g)

\displaystyle\mapsto(f^{-1})^{*}g=:f_{*}g.

We form the associated fiber bundle
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{fib}}(T^{v}Z)=Q_{Z}\times_{\mathrm{Diff}(M)}\mathcal{R}(M)\to B
. If
Z\to B
admits a spin structure extending the spin structure
\mathfrak{s}
on the fiber
M
, then we will identify
P_{Z}\times_{\mathrm{SDiff}(M,\mathfrak{s})}\mathcal{R}(M)
and
Q_{Z}\times_{\mathrm{Diff}(M)}\mathcal{R}(M)
via
[L\times\id]
, where
L:P_{Z}\to Q_{Z}
denotes the data of the spin structure on
Z\to B
as defined in the previous section. A continuous section of
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{fib}}(T^{v}Z)\to B
is called a continuous family of Riemannian metrics along the fibers of
p:Z\to B
. Note that since
\mathcal{R}(M)
is contractible, such a section always exists. Note also that a Riemannian metric
g:B\to\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{fib}}(T^{v}Z)
along the fibers of
Z\to B
induces a bundle metric on the vector bundle
T^{v}Z\to Z
, and makes the fiber bundle
Z\to B
into a bundle of Riemannian manifolds.

For any diffeomorphism invariant subset
\mathcal{S}(M)\subset\mathcal{R}(M)
, we can just as above form the associated fiber bundle
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{fib}}(T^{v}Z)\to B
, which is a subbundle of
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{fib}}(T^{v}Z)
. We denote sections
B\to\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{fib}}(T^{v}Z)
which restricts to
C\to\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{fib}}(T^{v}Z)|_{C}
for some
C\subset B
as
(B,C)\to(\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{fib}}(T^{v}Z),\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{fib}}(T^{v}Z))
and call them sections of
(\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{fib}}(T^{v}Z),\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{fib}}(T^{v}Z))\to(B,C)
. Pointed sections
g
with
b_{0}\in C
and
g(b_{0})=[h,g_{0}]
are denoted as

\displaystyle g:(B,C,b_{0})\to(\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{fib}}(T^{v}Z),\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{fib}}(T^{v}Z),[h,g_{0}]).

Let
b_{0}\in B
be a fixed
0
-cell and let
B^{\ell}
denote the
\ell
-skeleton of
B
. If
\mathcal{S}(M)
is
k
-connected, then for every
[h,g_{0}]\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{fib}}(T^{v}Z)
, there is a section

\displaystyle g:(B,B^{k+1},b_{0})\to(\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{fib}}(T^{v}Z),\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{fib}}(T^{v}Z),[h,g_{0}]).

2.3.
Metric spin structures

Let
\theta:\text{Spin}(n)\to\mathrm{SO}(n)
be the connected double covering group, which is the co-restriction of the homomorphism
\theta
given in section
2.1
, along the standard inclusion
\mathrm{SO}(n)\subset\mathrm{GL}^{+}_{n}
. Let
E\to X
be an oriented real vector bundle of rank
n
, with a positive definite bundle metric
g
and let
\mathrm{SO}(E)
be the
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-principal bundle of positively oriented orthonormal frames on
E
. A metric spin structure
\mathfrak{s}^{g}=({\mathrm{Spin}}^{g}(E),\Theta)
on
E
is a lift of the structure group of
\mathrm{SO}(E)
along
\theta
. A metric spin structure on an oriented Riemannian manifold is a metric spin structure on the tangent bundle of
M
. Let
\mathfrak{s}^{g}=({\mathrm{Spin}}^{g}(E),\Theta)
be a metric spin structure on an oriented vector bundle
E\to X
of rank
n
. We define a corresponding Clifford bundle

\displaystyle\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(E)=\mathrm{Spin}^{g}(E)\times_{l}C\ell_{n},

where the representation
l
is given by left multiplication. In case
E=TM
is the tangent bundle of an oriented Riemannian manifold, then the bundle
\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(M):=\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(TM)
is called the
\mathbf{Cl}_{n}
-linear spinor bundle on the Riemannian spin manifold
(M,g,\mathfrak{s}^{g})
. The bundle
\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(M)
carries the standard structures of Clifford multiplication
c:T^{*}M\otimes\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(M)\to\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(M)
and a connection
\widetilde{\nabla}^{g}
obtained by lifting the Levi-civita connection on
TM
. This gives rise to the Dirac operator

\displaystyle\not{D}^{g}:\Gamma(\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(M))\overset{\widetilde{\nabla}^{g}}{\to}\Gamma(T^{*}M\otimes\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(M))\overset{c}{\to}\Gamma(\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(M)).

The key feature of the
C\ell_{n}
-linear spinor bundle
\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(M)
is that it has a parallel right action of
C\ell_{n}
which commutes with the action given by
c
, and therefore makes the Dirac operator
C\ell_{n}
-linear. Hence the kernel of the Dirac operator is a
C\ell_{n}
-module.

Given an oriented vector bundle
E\to X
with a spin structure
\mathfrak{s}=(\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}^{+}}(E),\Theta)
, we get for each choice of positive definite bundle metric
g
on
E\to X
, a metric spin structure
\mathfrak{s}^{g}=({\mathrm{Spin}}^{g}(E),\Theta)
, given as the restriction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Spin}^{g}(E)\coloneqq}}

Let

be a fiber bundle, whose fiber is a smooth manifold of dimension

. Suppose that

on

2.4.
Atiyah-Singer family index theorem & the index difference

The paper
[
16
]
is especially well suited for the purposes of this paper, and we follow it closely in this section (see also
[
9
]
). We will not need the full power of
[
16
]
, and to not obscure the conceptually clear picture, we only recall the parts which are necessary for this article.

Let
(X,Y)
be a CW-pair. A
p
-cycle on
(X,Y)
is a pair
(V,F)
, where
V\to X
is a
\mathbf{Cl}_{p}
-Hilbert bundle, and
F
is a Fredholm family on
V\to X
, which is invertible over
Y
. There are straightforward notions of pullbacks, sums, and isomorphisms of
p
-cycles. Two
p
-cycles
(V_{0},F_{0})
and
(V_{1},F_{1})
are called concordant if there is a
p
-cycle
(V,F)
on
(X\times[0,1],Y\times[0,1])
such that the restriction of
(V,F)
to
X\times\{i\}
is isomorphic to
(V_{i},F_{i})
, for
i\in\{0,1\}
. A
p
-cycle
(V,F)
on
(X,Y)
is called acyclic if
F
is invertible over
X
. The set of concordance classes of
p
-cycles forms an abelian monoid, and the quotient of this monoid by the submonoid of acyclic
p
-cycles, forms an abelian group, which we will call
F^{p}(X,Y)
. Note that there is a natural forgetful group homomorphism
\mathcal{U}:F^{p}(X,Y)\to F^{p}(X)
. In
[
16
]
, it is shown that there exists a group isomorphism
\mathrm{Ind}:F^{p}(X,Y)\cong\mathbf{KO}^{-p}(X,Y)
.

Let
Z
and
B
be CW-complexes and let
p:Z\to B
be a fiber bundle, whose fiber is a closed smooth manifold
M
of dimension
n
. Suppose that the fiber bundle
Z\to B
is equipped with a spin structure, extending some spin structure on
M
. A choice of Riemannian metric
g:B\to\mathcal{R}^{\text{fib}}(T^{v}Z)
along the fibers of
Z\to B
gives, as in section
2.3
, a bundle
\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(T^{v}Z)\to B
of Clifford bundles, where each fiber
\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(T^{v}Z)_{b}\to Z_{b}
is the
\mathbf{Cl}_{n}
-linear spinor bundle on the manifold
Z_{b}
with respect to the induced spin structure and metric. The Hilbert spaces
L^{2}(\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(T^{v}Z)_{b}\to Z_{b})
form a
\mathbf{Cl}_{n}
-Hilbert bundle
L^{2}(\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(T^{v}Z))\to B
, and the family
D^{g}:b\mapsto\frac{\not{D}^{g}_{b}}{(1+(\not{D}_{b}^{g})^{2})^{1/2}}
of fiberwise Dirac operators defines a Fredholm family on
L^{2}(\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(T^{v}Z))\to B
. Hence the pair
(L^{2}(\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(T^{v}Z)),D^{g})
defines an element
[(L^{2}(\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(T^{v}Z)),D^{g})]\in F^{n}(B)
, and the corresponding element
\mathrm{Ind}([(L^{2}(\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(T^{v}Z)),D^{g})])\in\mathbf{KO}^{-n}(B)
is called the analytic index of the Fredholm family
D^{g}
. The spin structure on
Z\to B
induces a map
p_{!}:\mathbf{KO}(Z)\to\mathbf{KO}^{-n}(B)
and the Atiyah-Singer family index theorem states that

(10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ind}([(L^{2}(\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(T^{v}Z)),D^{g})])=p_{!}(1).

Note that if the Riemannian metric along the fibers,
g
, that we started with is such that
g
restricts to a section of
\mathcal{R}^{\text{fib}}_{\text{inv}}(T^{v}Z)|_{C}\to C
for some sub-complex
C\subset B
, then the corresponding pair
(L^{2}(\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(T^{v}Z)),D^{g})
defines an element
[(L^{2}(\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(T^{v}Z)),D^{g})]
in
F^{n}(B,C)
and consequently has an index
\mathrm{Ind}[(L^{2}(\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(T^{v}Z)),D^{g})]
in
\mathbf{KO}^{-n}(B,C)
.

We now define the index difference. Let
g:(D^{\ell+1},S^{\ell},*)\to(\mathcal{R}(M),\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(M),g_{0})
represent an element
[g]\in\pi_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{R}(M),\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(M),g_{0})\cong\pi_{\ell}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(M),g_{0})
. The family
g
defines a Riemannian metric along the fibers of the trivial fiber bundle
\underline{M}:=D^{\ell+1}\times M\to D^{\ell+1}
. As before, we obtain a
\mathbf{Cl}_{n}
-Hilbert bundle
L^{2}(\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(T^{v}\underline{M}))\to D^{\ell+1}
together with a Fredholm family
D^{g}
, which together forms an element
[(L^{2}(\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(T^{v}\underline{M})),D^{g})]\in F^{n}(D^{\ell+1},S^{\ell})
, and consequently defines an element
\mathrm{Ind}[(L^{2}(\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(T^{v}\underline{M})),D^{g})]\in\mathbf{KO}^{-n}(D^{\ell+1},S^{\ell})\cong\mathbf{KO}^{-(n+\ell+1)}(\{*\})
. By homotopy-invariance of the index, this defines a homomorphism

(11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\inddiff}:\pi_{\ell}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{inv}}(M),g_{0})

\displaystyle\to\mathbf{KO}^{-(n+\ell+1)}(\{*\}),

(12)

\displaystyle[g]

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathrm{Ind}[(L^{2}(\not{\mathfrak{S}}^{g}(T^{v}\underline{M})),D^{g})]

which is called the index difference.

and Theorem

In this section,
\Sigma_{\gamma}
will denote a closed and oriented surface of genus
\gamma
. Lemma
3.1
below is concerned with the existence of surface bundles over surfaces with non-zero signature. Such bundles were first constructed in
[

,

]
. In
[

, it was shown that for any

, there is such a surface bundle

, with fiber

, having

. The author was informed by Jonathan Bowden how to prove the spin condition in the following lemma

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Sigma_{\gamma}
be a closed, oriented surface of genus
\gamma\geq 3
. For any spin structure
\mathfrak{s}
on
\Sigma_{\gamma}
, there exists a smooth fiber bundle

with fiber
\Sigma_{\gamma}
such that

(1)

\sigma(Z)\neq 0
, and

(2)

there is a spin structure on
p:Z\to\Sigma_{h}
which extends

.

Proof.

Fix some
\gamma\geq 3
and let

be a smooth fiber bundle with fiber
\Sigma_{\gamma}
such that
\sigma(E)\neq 0

[
26
, Satz 3]
. Let

be a base point of
\Sigma_{g}
and let

denote the monodromy representation of the bundle

. Fix any spin structure

on
\Sigma_{\gamma}
. Since the number of isomorphism classes of spin structures on

is a finite set, the orbit of
\mathfrak{s}
under the action of the group of isotopy classes of diffeomorphism (see (

)) is a finite set, and therefore the stabilizer
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(\Sigma_{\gamma}),\mathfrak{s})
is a finite index subgroup. Hence the preimage
\rho^{-1}(\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(\Sigma_{\gamma}),\mathfrak{s}))
is a finite index subgroup of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g},b)
. Let

be the finite covering corresponding to the finite index subgroup

. Lift

along this covering and let

be the corresponding smooth
\Sigma_{\gamma}
-bundle. Note that
\sigma(E^{\prime})\neq 0
, and that the monodromy of

has image contained inside
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(\Sigma_{\gamma}),\mathfrak{s})
. Let

be the classifying map for

. If the class

. If the class

, which satisfies

, where

is the classifying map for

. Note again that

. In either case, we get a bundle

which satisfies the conditions of the lemma.
