Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

The project goal is to prove the feasibility and advance the development of a low-cost, modular
concrete suction pile support structure and heavy lift vessel alternative for offshore wind
deployment in the United States. These technology developments are critical to alleviating
specific challenges faced by the United States market, including a limited number of Jones Act-
compliant installation vessels and environmental restrictions associated with using conventional
offshore wind foundations. The first phase of this project is a conceptual design and feasibility
assessment of modular concrete substructure options for NREL’s 15 MW reference turbine.

Summary of Research Results:

Task 1: NREL shall estimate the levelized cost of energy (LCOE) for a 1005 MW wind
plant with five different permutations of support structures and installation methods and
will compare the costs of the modular concrete structure with alternate designs.

 1. RCAM developed a conceptual design for a modular concrete foundation that could be
 used to support a 15 MW offshore wind turbine. The design encompassed both structural
 elements as well as transport and installation costs. RCAM provided the design
 parameters to NREL. NREL and RCAM established three scenarios for a comparative
 analysis; RCAM ultimately preferred the three scenarios (instead of the five originally
 listed in the task description) to reduce complexity and to provide a clearer discussion of
 the final results.
 NREL established baseline site conditions and collected relevant data (i.e., metocean
 wind/wave time series) for the comparison. NREL also updated its database of offshore
 wind installation vessels and key performance parameters, including day rates and
 installation process times. The comparative assessment was conducted using ORBIT, an
 open-source, process-based cost and logistics model developed by NREL. Within this
 model, NREL calculated the costs and installation times for a conventional foundation (a
 monopile, which is installed onsite using expensive installation vessels followed by
 another set of vessels constructing the wind turbine on top of the monopile) and the novel
 concrete foundation. NREL evaluated two sub-scenarios for the concrete foundation: a
 sequential tripod/turbine approach in which the foundation was towed to site (using
 smaller, less expensive vessels than the conventional method) followed by the turbine
 being installed onsite, and a coupled tripod/turbine approach where the turbine was
 integrated with the foundation at the port and the entire assembly was towed out to site.
 NREL summarized the findings in a report provided to RCAM.
 2. NREL found that shifting to a coupled tripod/turbine system could lead to significant
 reductions in installation time and cost, as shown in Figure 1. The net impact of this
 reduction in installation costs has relatively little impact on the levelized cost of energy
 because the installation costs comprise a small portion of the overall capital stack;
 however, the concrete foundation has the opportunity to mitigate key risks for offshore
 wind projects associated with the global scarcity of wind turbine installation vessels,
 environmental impacts of monopile installation, and reliance on a European supply chain.

Task 2: After the down selection of the preferred design methodology, NREL will assist RCAM in
revising LCOE for the modular concrete structure at a particular installation site.

 1. The work conducted in Task 1 focused on a generic reference site. In Task 2, NREL and
 RCAM worked together to identify multiple project sites (encompassing both offshore
 wind project locations and installation port locations) and updated the LCOE estimates
 from Task 1. RCAM provided more detailed information about the construction process
 and the costs of the concrete substructure. NREL collected more detailed information
 about the ports and installation processes, and re-ran the models developed in Task 1. The
 revised cost estimation increased the LCOE of the concrete foundation to be around 3%
 higher than the conventional monopile scenario, but did not significantly change the
 installation cost and time benefits identified in Task 1.
 2. NREL and RCAM selected the South Brooklyn Marine Terminal (NY) as a
 representative foundation manufacturing port and the New London State Pier (CT) as the
 final marshaling port for a representative project in the New York Bight. As part of this
 down selection, NREL eliminated the scenario from Task 1 in which the foundation and
 the turbine were sequentially installed (primarily because the coupled scenario showed
 more promising results). The resulting LCOE of the conventional monopile and the
 coupled tripod/turbine installation were $74.5/MWh and $76.7/MWh, respectively –
 these values of LCOE are within the uncertainty bounds of the cost estimates for this
 study, indicating roughly approximate costs between the two scenarios.

Task 3: NREL will assist RCAM in estimating the regional economic benefits associated
with the modular concrete support structure compared with conventional monopile and
steel truss foundations at an identified location in the Northeast United States.

 1. NREL used the Jobs and Economic Development Impact (JEDI) model to evaluate the
 regional economic benefits of the representative projects in Task 2 using either the
 conventional monopile or the novel concrete tripod foundation. Because the steel truss
 foundation was removed from the scenarios during the down selection in Tasks 1 and 2,
 RCAM asked NREL not to conduct an economic analysis for this design. The JEDI
 model inputs the cost estimates for the different project configurations and uses an
 economic input/output model to estimate the total number of jobs from the different wind
 turbine foundations, including on-site installation, manufacturing, supply chain, and
 induced jobs. NREL further customized the model to analyze the concrete foundation
 using the same methodology as the monopile (a default option in JEDI). NREL estimated
 the regional purchase coefficients that characterize the level of local content based on
 historical industry data. From this analysis, NREL compared the total job impact per $10
 million of foundation spending.

 2. The results of the NREL analysis are shown in Figure 2. Although concrete foundations
 are somewhat more expensive than monopiles, the JEDI estimates indicate that they have
 the opportunity to create three times as many national jobs as monopiles. The analysis
 was conducted for manufacturing the foundations in New York State; the concrete
 foundations have the opportunity to create twice as many jobs within the state.

Task 4: NREL will lead a risk assessment for conducting a utility scale field test of a
prototype modular concrete structure sized to support the DOE/NREL 600 KW or
DOE/NREL 1.5 MW turbine on land. NREL will estimate operating loads and will create
preliminary test specifications, schedules, and cost estimates.

 1. RCAM had planned a utility scale field test of the 3D concrete printing technology
 developed as part of this study. The original scope was to develop a subscale foundation
 that could have a 600 KW or 1.5 MW turbine installed at NREL’s Flatirons Campus for
 pilot-scale field testing; this was viewed as a lower-cost option than an at-sea test. During
 the course of the project, it became clear that the rapid growth of turbine size and the
 identification of transport and installation risks during the project mandated a full ocean
 test. RCAM developed an accelerated full-scale offshore pilot test plan in lieu of the test
 at NREL’s facilities. As a result, NREL was not required to lead a risk assessment plan
 for the land-based testing. Instead, NREL supported the development of the testing
 programs designed to address these most critical risks, including testing the connections
 between the support structure and suction buckets, testing failure modes, exploring water
 tightness, and wave tank testing. A full wave tank testing plan was developed, including
 the transport and installation processes of the full structure. Finally, a full-scale ocean test
 was planned and includes incline testing, ballasting and de-ballasting, testing of
 individual mechanical systems. Because of the change in testing plans to a marine test
 scenario, NREL did not estimate the loads, test specifications, schedules, or costs.
 NREL’s role was to provide guidance based on previous (land-based) field test risk
 assessments to support the test plan development by RCAM.

 2. The testing and commercialization plan developed by RCAM, with support from NREL,
 was estimated to be around $3 million. The wave tank tests were estimated to cost
 $450,000.
