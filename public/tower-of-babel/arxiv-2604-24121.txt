Natural-orbital locking reveals hidden steady-state skin order in Gaussian open fermion chains
Y. T. Wang, X. Z. Zhang

Abstract
Nonreciprocal relaxation matrices can have skin-localized right eigenmodes, but their imprint on a mixed steady state is not fixed by the density profile alone. We develop an exact steady-state theory for number-conserving Gaussian fermion chains and show that the dominant natural orbital of the correlation matrix provides a mode-resolved diagnostic of hidden skin order. The steady-state correlator admits a biorthogonal decomposition in terms of the left and right eigenmodes of the relaxation matrix X X and the source matrix Y Y . This formula separates three ingredients: slow rapidity denominators, source loading by left eigenmodes, and real-space geometry from right eigenmodes. For a local pump, the pump position is read by the left modes, whereas the selected profile is drawn by the right modes. In a single-slow-mode regime, the dominant natural orbital locks to the Euclidean-normalized slow right mode. The density can follow the same boundary trend, but it is a less selective incoherent sum over occupied natural orbitals. We verify this selection law in a nonreciprocal Hatano–Nelson chain and show that, in a nonreciprocal SSH chain, the selected natural orbital crosses over from a topological edge candidate to a slow bulk-skin candidate. These results identify natural-orbital locking as a steady-state diagnostic of nonreciprocal localization in Gaussian open fermion chains.

The local density is a natural observable, but it is only a diagonal contraction of the steady-state correlator. We write

C_{ij}^{\mathrm{ss}}=\Tr\!\left(\rho_{\mathrm{ss}}c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{i}\right),\qquad n_{j}^{\mathrm{ss}}=(C_{\mathrm{ss}})_{jj}.

(1)

The full correlator has its own eigenmodes,

C_{\mathrm{ss}}\ket{\varphi_{\alpha}}=\nu_{\alpha}\ket{\varphi_{\alpha}},

(2)

which are the natural orbitals of the Gaussian steady state. For a physical fermionic Gaussian state
0\leq\nu_{\alpha}\leq 1
. The density is reconstructed as

n_{j}^{\mathrm{ss}}=\sum_{\alpha}\nu_{\alpha}|\varphi_{\alpha}(j)|^{2}.

(3)

Thus the density is not a mode-resolved object. It is an incoherent sum over all occupied natural orbitals. When one occupation dominates, the density may look similar to the leading orbital. When subleading orbitals carry appreciable weight, the density becomes smoother. In either case, the dominant natural orbital is the sharper object for asking which steady-state mode has been selected.

In this Letter we derive an exact selection theory for this dominant orbital. The steady-state correlator can be expanded in the biorthogonal eigenbasis of the relaxation matrix
X
. The expansion separates the steady-state kernel into slow denominators, left-mode loading, and right-mode geometry. This separation is the key physical point. A local source is sampled by the left eigenmodes, while the spatial profile of a selected steady-state component is inherited from the right eigenmodes. In a single-slow-mode regime, this structure forces the dominant natural orbital of
C_{\mathrm{ss}}
to lock to the Euclidean-normalized slow right eigenmode.

We demonstrate the mechanism in two complementary examples. The nonreciprocal Hatano–Nelson chain gives the minimal bulk-skin setting and allows a direct comparison between the exact source dependence and the analytical loading law. The nonreciprocal SSH chain adds dimerization and edge sectors, so that the same natural-orbital diagnostic distinguishes an edge-locking regime from a bulk-skin-locking regime. Figure

We consider a number-conserving quadratic Lindblad problem with Hamiltonian

H=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}h_{ij}c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{j},\qquad h=h^{\dagger},

(5)

and linear gain and loss jumps. The one-body correlator

C_{ij}(t)=\Tr\!\left[\rho(t)c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{i}\right]

(6)

obeys the closed equation

\dot{C}=-XC-CX^{\dagger}+Y.

(7)

Here

X=\mathrm{i}h+\frac{\Gamma^{-}+\Gamma^{+}}{2},\qquad Y=\Gamma^{+},

(8)

where
\Gamma^{-}
and
\Gamma^{+}
are the loss and gain Gram matrices. Equation (
7
) is derived in the Supplemental Material from the microscopic Lindblad equation.

The steady state satisfies

XC_{\mathrm{ss}}+C_{\mathrm{ss}}X^{\dagger}=Y.

(9)

If all eigenvalues of
X
have positive real parts, the solution is

C_{\mathrm{ss}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\mathrm{e}^{-Xt}Y\mathrm{e}^{-X^{\dagger}t}.

(10)

Let

X\ket{R_{n}}=\beta_{n}\ket{R_{n}},\qquad X^{\dagger}\ket{L_{n}}=\beta_{n}^{*}\ket{L_{n}},

(11)

with biorthogonal normalization

\langle L_{m}|R_{n}\rangle=\delta_{mn},\qquad\sum_{n}\ket{R_{n}}\bra{L_{n}}=I.

(12)

Inserting the spectral decomposition of
\mathrm{e}^{-Xt}
into Eq. (
10
) gives

C_{\mathrm{ss}}=\sum_{m,n}\frac{\matrixelement{L_m}{Y}{L_n}}{\beta_{m}+\beta_{n}^{*}}\ket{R_{m}}\bra{R_{n}}.

(13)

This formula is the central result. The factor
(\beta_{m}+\beta_{n}^{*})^{-1}
controls temporal accumulation,
\matrixelement{L_m}{Y}{L_n}
controls source loading, and
\ket{R_{m}}\bra{R_{n}}
carries the real-space geometry.

For a local pump,

Y=\Gamma\ket{s}\bra{s},\qquad\Gamma>0,

(14)

Eq. (
13
) becomes

C_{\mathrm{ss}}=\Gamma\sum_{m,n}\frac{L_{m}^{*}(s)L_{n}(s)}{\beta_{m}+\beta_{n}^{*}}\ket{R_{m}}\bra{R_{n}}.

(15)

The pump position therefore enters through left eigenmodes, while the selected spatial profile is drawn by right eigenmodes. This left-right separation is absent in Hermitian steady-state intuition and is the origin of the source-selection effect below.

Suppose that one mode, labeled
0
, is parametrically slower than the rest and that
L_{0}(s)\neq 0
. Then Eq. (
15
) is dominated by the
m=n=0
term,

C_{\mathrm{ss}}\simeq\mathcal{A}_{0}(s)\ket{R_{0}}\bra{R_{0}},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{0}(s)=\frac{\Gamma|L_{0}(s)|^{2}}{2\real\,\beta_{0}}.

(16)

Because
\ket{R_{0}}
is biorthogonally normalized and need not have unit Euclidean norm, the corresponding dominant natural orbital is

\ket{\varphi_{\mathrm{max}}}\simeq\ket{\widehat{R}_{0}},\qquad\ket{\widehat{R}_{0}}=\frac{\ket{R_{0}}}{\sqrt{\langle R_{0}|R_{0}\rangle}}.

(17)

The leading occupation is

\nu_{\mathrm{max}}\simeq\mathcal{A}_{0}(s)\langle R_{0}|R_{0}\rangle.

(18)

Thus the orbital profile is fixed by the right mode, while the source dependence is fixed by the left-mode loading factor.

In the figures we compare natural orbitals with Euclidean-normalized right modes through

\mathcal{O}_{n}=\left|\langle\widehat{R}_{n}|\varphi_{\mathrm{max}}\rangle\right|^{2}.

(19)

We also use

\tilde{\nu}_{\alpha}=\frac{\nu_{\alpha}}{\nu_{\mathrm{max}}},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{n}(s)=\frac{\Gamma|L_{n}(s)|^{2}}{2\real\,\beta_{n}}.

(20)

Equation (
3
) then fixes the relation between the density and the natural-orbital profile. The density can be close to
|\varphi_{\mathrm{max}}(j)|^{2}
in a strongly single-orbital regime, but it remains a less selective contraction of
C_{\mathrm{ss}}
.

We now apply the theory to two target relaxation matrices. The Hatano–Nelson chain isolates the bulk skin mechanism and provides a direct test of the source-selection law. The SSH chain adds dimerization and topological edge modes, turning the steady-state problem into a selection crossover between distinct candidate modes.

For the nonreciprocal Hatano–Nelson chain,

X_{\rm HN}=\kappa I-t_{R}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\ket{j+1}\bra{j}-t_{L}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\ket{j}\bra{j+1},

(21)

with
t_{R}>t_{L}>0
, define

r=\sqrt{\frac{t_{R}}{t_{L}}},\qquad S=\sum_{j=1}^{N}r^{j}\ket{j}\bra{j}.

(22)

Then

S^{-1}X_{\rm HN}S=\kappa I-\sqrt{t_{R}t_{L}}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(\ket{j+1}\bra{j}+\ket{j}\bra{j+1}\right),

(23)

which is a Hermitian open chain. The reference eigenfunctions and eigenvalues are

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(j)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{N+1}}\sin\frac{n\pi j}{N+1},

(24)

\displaystyle\beta_{n}=\kappa-2\sqrt{t_{R}t_{L}}\cos\frac{n\pi}{N+1}.

(25)

Transforming back gives

R_{n}(j)=r^{j}\phi_{n}(j),\qquad L_{n}(j)=r^{-j}\phi_{n}(j).

(26)

For
r>1
, right modes accumulate at the right edge and left modes at the left edge. With
Y=\Gamma\ket{s}\bra{s}
, the source dependence of the slowest mode is
\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)=\Gamma|L_{1}(s)|^{2}/(2\real\,\beta_{1})
. Thus the pump reads a left-skin envelope, while the selected orbital displays a right-skin envelope.

Discussion.

The main result of this Letter is the exact steady-state selection formula Eq. (
13
). It shows that the mixed steady state of a Gaussian open chain is organized by the same biorthogonal geometry that controls nonreciprocal relaxation, but not in a way that is exhausted by the density profile. Slow denominators, left-mode loading, and right-mode geometry enter separately. A local source selects components through the left eigenmodes, while the selected spatial profile is drawn by the right eigenmodes. The dominant natural orbital isolates that component.

The two examples demonstrate different levels of the same mechanism. The Hatano–Nelson chain verifies the source-selection law in the minimal bulk-skin geometry. The nonreciprocal SSH chain shows that natural-orbital locking can switch between different candidates when topology and nonreciprocity coexist. This provides a steady-state diagnostic that is mode-resolved, directly computable from
C_{\mathrm{ss}}
, and sharper than a density-only description.

Y. T. Wang and X. Z. Zhang

This Supplemental Material provides the technical foundation for the results presented in the main text. We first derive the closed equation of motion for the one-body correlation matrix starting from a microscopic number-conserving quadratic Lindblad equation. We then solve the resulting Lyapunov equation and obtain the exact biorthogonal decomposition of the steady-state correlator. This derivation makes explicit how slow relaxation denominators, left-mode source loading, and right-mode spatial profiles combine to determine the dominant natural orbital of the nonequilibrium steady state. We next apply the general formula to the two target relaxation matrices used in the main text. For the Hatano–Nelson chain, we give the similarity transformation to a Hermitian reference chain and derive the analytic source-loading law. For the nonreciprocal SSH chain, we state the finite-size model, define the edge and bulk-skin candidates, and specify the overlap diagnostics used to identify the edge-lock to bulk-skin-lock crossover. Finally, we present an inverse-design construction showing how the target matrices can be embedded in microscopic Gaussian Lindblad dynamics by appropriate Hamiltonians and quantum jumps. The notation follows the main text throughout.

Appendix A
Exact derivations and explicit model reductions

We collect here the derivations that support the main text. The logic proceeds from the microscopic Lindblad equation to the closed one-body equation, then to the exact steady-state solution, and finally to the explicit model reductions used in the Hatano–Nelson and nonreciprocal SSH examples. The notation is kept identical to the main text.

Consider a number-conserving quadratic Hamiltonian

H=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}h_{ij}c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{j},\qquad h=h^{\dagger},

(S1)

and linear loss and gain jumps,

L^{-}_{\mu}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(u_{\mu})_{j}c_{j},\qquad L^{+}_{\nu}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(v_{\nu})_{j}c_{j}^{\dagger}.

(S2)

The Lindblad equation is

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}={}

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}[H,\rho]+\sum_{\mu}\left(L^{-}_{\mu}\rho L^{-\dagger}_{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}\{L^{-\dagger}_{\mu}L^{-}_{\mu},\rho\}\right)+\sum_{\nu}\left(L^{+}_{\nu}\rho L^{+\dagger}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\{L^{+\dagger}_{\nu}L^{+}_{\nu},\rho\}\right).

(S3)

We define the one-body correlator

C_{ij}(t)=\Tr\!\left[\rho(t)c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{i}\right].

(S4)

The Hamiltonian part follows from

[c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{i},c_{m}^{\dagger}c_{n}]=\delta_{im}c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{n}-\delta_{nj}c_{m}^{\dagger}c_{i},

(S5)

which gives

\dot{C}\big|_{H}=-\mathrm{i}[h,C].

(S6)

For the loss and gain sectors it is convenient to define the Gram matrices

\Gamma^{-}=\sum_{\mu}\bm{u}_{\mu}\bm{u}_{\mu}^{\dagger},\qquad\Gamma^{+}=\sum_{\nu}\bm{v}_{\nu}\bm{v}_{\nu}^{\dagger}.

(S7)

A direct fermionic reduction gives

\dot{C}\big|_{-}=-\frac{1}{2}\{\Gamma^{-},C\},\qquad\dot{C}\big|_{+}=-\frac{1}{2}\{\Gamma^{+},C\}+\Gamma^{+}.

(S8)

Combining Eqs. (
S6
) and (
S8
), we arrive at

\dot{C}=-\mathrm{i}[h,C]-\frac{1}{2}\{\Gamma^{-}+\Gamma^{+},C\}+\Gamma^{+}.

(S9)

With

X=\mathrm{i}h+\frac{\Gamma^{-}+\Gamma^{+}}{2},\qquad Y=\Gamma^{+},

(S10)

this becomes the compact equation quoted in the main text,

\dot{C}=-XC-CX^{\dagger}+Y.

(S11)

The exact steady-state solution follows by introducing

\widetilde{C}(t)=\mathrm{e}^{Xt}C(t)\mathrm{e}^{X^{\dagger}t}.

(S12)

Differentiation gives

\frac{d\widetilde{C}}{dt}=\mathrm{e}^{Xt}Y\mathrm{e}^{X^{\dagger}t},

(S13)

so that

C(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-Xt}C(0)\mathrm{e}^{-X^{\dagger}t}+\int_{0}^{t}du\,\mathrm{e}^{-Xu}Y\mathrm{e}^{-X^{\dagger}u}.

(S14)

If all eigenvalues
\beta_{n}
of
X
satisfy
\real\,\beta_{n}>0
, the first term decays and the steady state is

C_{\mathrm{ss}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\mathrm{e}^{-Xt}Y\mathrm{e}^{-X^{\dagger}t}.

(S15)

Assume now that
X
is diagonalizable,

X\ket{R_{n}}=\beta_{n}\ket{R_{n}},\qquad X^{\dagger}\ket{L_{n}}=\beta_{n}^{*}\ket{L_{n}},

(S16)

with

\langle L_{m}|R_{n}\rangle=\delta_{mn},\qquad\sum_{n}\ket{R_{n}}\bra{L_{n}}=I.

(S17)

Inserting the spectral decomposition of
\mathrm{e}^{-Xt}
into Eq. (
S15
) yields

C_{\mathrm{ss}}=\sum_{m,n}\frac{\matrixelement{L_m}{Y}{L_n}}{\beta_{m}+\beta_{n}^{*}}\ket{R_{m}}\bra{R_{n}}.

(S18)

For a local pump
Y=\Gamma\ket{s}\bra{s}
this simplifies to

C_{\mathrm{ss}}=\Gamma\sum_{m,n}\frac{L_{m}^{*}(s)L_{n}(s)}{\beta_{m}+\beta_{n}^{*}}\ket{R_{m}}\bra{R_{n}}.

(S19)

If one mode is parametrically slower than the rest, then

C_{\mathrm{ss}}\approx\mathcal{A}_{0}(s)\ket{R_{0}}\bra{R_{0}},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{0}(s)=\frac{\Gamma|L_{0}(s)|^{2}}{2\real\,\beta_{0}}.

(S20)

Because

(S21)

The numerical diagnostics used in the main text are defined as follows. The normalized density is

\tilde{n}_{j}=\frac{n_{j}^{\mathrm{ss}}}{\sum_{\ell}n_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ss}}},\qquad n_{j}^{\mathrm{ss}}=(C_{\mathrm{ss}})_{jj}.

(S22)

(S23)

(S25)

(S37)

(S38)

Appendix B
Inverse-design construction of the microscopic open systems

We now summarize the inverse-design problem that reconstructs a microscopic quadratic open system from a target pair
(X_{\mathrm{tar}},Y_{\mathrm{tar}})
. The general relation is

X=\mathrm{i}h+\frac{\Gamma^{-}+\Gamma^{+}}{2},\qquad Y=\Gamma^{+}.

(S41)

Hence the formal solution is

h=\frac{X_{\mathrm{tar}}-X_{\mathrm{tar}}^{\dagger}}{2\mathrm{i}},\qquad\Gamma^{+}=Y_{\mathrm{tar}},\qquad\Gamma^{-}=X_{\mathrm{tar}}+X_{\mathrm{tar}}^{\dagger}-Y_{\mathrm{tar}}.

(S42)

The task is then to realize the positive semidefinite matrices
\Gamma^{+}
and
\Gamma^{-}
through explicit local quantum jumps. In the most general diagonal-pump implementation one may take

Y_{\mathrm{tar}}=\sum_{j}y_{j}\ket{j}\bra{j},\qquad L_{j}^{+}=\sqrt{y_{j}}\,c_{j}^{\dagger},

(S43)

with
y_{j}\geq 0
. A local pump is the special case
y_{j}=\Gamma\delta_{j,s}
. The residual onsite loss strengths in the decompositions below are then obtained by replacing the uniform pump strength
\gamma
by the corresponding site-dependent
y_{j}
. The simple uniform-pump formulas are written explicitly because they are compact and show the structure of the construction.

For the Hatano–Nelson target matrix,

X_{\mathrm{HN}}=\kappa I-t_{R}T-t_{L}T^{\dagger},\qquad T=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\ket{j+1}\bra{j},

(S44)

we choose a uniform pump,

Y_{\mathrm{tar}}=\gamma I,\qquad L_{j}^{+}=\sqrt{\gamma}\,c_{j}^{\dagger},\qquad j=1,\dots,N.

(S45)

The Hamiltonian follows from Eq. (
S42
),

H=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}(t_{R}-t_{L})\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(c_{j+1}^{\dagger}c_{j}-c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}\right).

(S46)

The remaining loss matrix is

\Gamma^{-}=(2\kappa-\gamma)I-(t_{R}+t_{L})(T+T^{\dagger}).

(S47)

A transparent local decomposition is obtained by introducing the bond jumps

B_{j}^{-}=\sqrt{t_{R}+t_{L}}\,(c_{j}-c_{j+1}),\qquad j=1,\dots,N-1,

(S48)

and the onsite losses

S_{1}^{-}=\sqrt{\delta-\beta}\,c_{1},\qquad S_{N}^{-}=\sqrt{\delta-\beta}\,c_{N},

(S49)

S_{j}^{-}=\sqrt{\delta-2\beta}\,c_{j},\qquad j=2,\dots,N-1,

(S50)

with

\delta=2\kappa-\gamma,\qquad\beta=t_{R}+t_{L}.

(S51)

This explicit construction is valid under the simple sufficient condition

2\kappa-\gamma\geq 2(t_{R}+t_{L}).

(S52)

For the nonreciprocal SSH target matrix,

\displaystyle X_{\mathrm{SSH}}={}

\displaystyle\kappa I-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(t_{1}^{R}\ket{n,B}\bra{n,A}+t_{1}^{L}\ket{n,A}\bra{n,B}\right)-\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\left(t_{2}^{R}\ket{n+1,A}\bra{n,B}+t_{2}^{L}\ket{n,B}\bra{n+1,A}\right),

(S53)

we again choose a uniform pump,

Y_{\mathrm{tar}}=\gamma I,\qquad L_{n,A}^{+}=\sqrt{\gamma}\,c_{n,A}^{\dagger},\qquad L_{n,B}^{+}=\sqrt{\gamma}\,c_{n,B}^{\dagger}.

(S54)

The Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle H={}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}(t_{1}^{R}-t_{1}^{L})\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(c_{n,B}^{\dagger}c_{n,A}-c_{n,A}^{\dagger}c_{n,B}\right)+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}(t_{2}^{R}-t_{2}^{L})\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\left(c_{n+1,A}^{\dagger}c_{n,B}-c_{n,B}^{\dagger}c_{n+1,A}\right).

(S55)

The Hermitian part that must be reproduced dissipatively is

\displaystyle\Gamma^{-}={}

\displaystyle(2\kappa-\gamma)I-(t_{1}^{R}+t_{1}^{L})\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\ket{n,B}\bra{n,A}+\ket{n,A}\bra{n,B}\right)-(t_{2}^{R}+t_{2}^{L})\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\left(\ket{n+1,A}\bra{n,B}+\ket{n,B}\bra{n+1,A}\right).

(S56)

A local decomposition is obtained from the intracell bond losses

B_{1,n}^{-}=\sqrt{t_{1}^{R}+t_{1}^{L}}\,(c_{n,A}-c_{n,B}),\qquad n=1,\dots,N,

(S57)

and the intercell bond losses

B_{2,n}^{-}=\sqrt{t_{2}^{R}+t_{2}^{L}}\,(c_{n,B}-c_{n+1,A}),\qquad n=1,\dots,N-1.

(S58)

These bond jumps generate the desired nonzero off-diagonal matrix elements and part of the diagonal ones. The remaining diagonal weights are supplied by onsite losses. Writing

\delta=2\kappa-\gamma,\qquad\beta_{1}=t_{1}^{R}+t_{1}^{L},\qquad\beta_{2}=t_{2}^{R}+t_{2}^{L},

(S59)

the required onsite terms are

S_{1,A}^{-}=\sqrt{\delta-\beta_{1}}\,c_{1,A},\qquad S_{N,B}^{-}=\sqrt{\delta-\beta_{1}}\,c_{N,B},

(S60)

S_{n,A}^{-}=\sqrt{\delta-\beta_{1}-\beta_{2}}\,c_{n,A},\qquad n=2,\dots,N,

(S61)

S_{n,B}^{-}=\sqrt{\delta-\beta_{1}-\beta_{2}}\,c_{n,B},\qquad n=1,\dots,N-1.

(S62)

A simple sufficient condition for this explicit local realization is

2\kappa-\gamma\geq(t_{1}^{R}+t_{1}^{L})+(t_{2}^{R}+t_{2}^{L}).

(S63)

The same construction applies to a diagonal local pump by using the site-dependent replacement described above. A sufficiently large uniform damping shift
\kappa
guarantees positivity of the residual loss matrix while preserving the nonreciprocal eigenvector geometry of the target family.
