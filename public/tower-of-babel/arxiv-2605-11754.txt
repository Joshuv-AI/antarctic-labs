Global Existence and Uniqueness of Strong Solutions for a Phase Transition Model in Atmospheric Dynamics
Giada Cianfarani Carnevale, Donatella Donatelli, Stefano Spirito

Abstract
. In this work, we study a phase transition model in atmospheric dynamics, inspired by the works [ 6 , 14 , 15 ] , which analyze the primitive equations governing the evolution of velocity, temperature, and specific humidity. The main difficulty arises from the presence of a multivalued discontinuous nonlinear term in the temperature and in the humidity equations, describing the formation of precipitations, which becomes active under supersaturation conditions. To overcome this issue, we introduce a regularized formulation that ensures the existence and uniqueness of approximate solutions. By employing classical compactness arguments, we then establish the existence of a strong solution to the original model. Additionally, we establish uniqueness under a conditional and physically meaningful assumption. This approach allows us to provide a rigorous justification of the tropical climate model on the whole space ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} , while avoiding the introduction of a viscosity term in the humidity equation.

1.
Introduction

The fundamental equations of atmospheric dynamics, known as the primitive equations, are derived from the Navier-Stokes equations with rotation. These equations are integrated with thermodynamic diffusion, considering that the ratio of the vertical scale to the horizontal scale is negligible. The primitive equations account for buoyancy forces and stratification effects, assuming the Boussinesq hypothesis, which implies the neglect of density variations in all terms except those multiplied by g, the acceleration due to gravity. On large scales, atmospheric flow can be uniquely decomposed into its barotropic and baroclinic components. These approaches are based on the characteristic scales of motion: horizontal scales, which extend over thousands of kilometers, are much larger than the vertical scales of the troposphere, approximately 16 km (
[
13
]
). Consequently, vertical motion is modeled through hydrostatic balance, expressed as

\frac{\partial p}{\partial z}=-\rho g,

where
p
represents pressure and
\rho
the density. Following the derivation in
[
13
]
and considering the Boussinesq and hydrostatic assumptions, the viscous, incompressible primitive equations derived from the Navier Stokes equations can be written as follows:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}V+V\cdot\nabla V+W\partial_{z}V-\mu\Delta_{h}V-\nu\partial_{z}^{2}V+\nabla_{h}\Phi=0\\
&\dive_{h}V+\partial_{z}W=0\\
&\partial_{z}\Phi=\frac{g}{\theta_{0}}\theta\\
&\partial_{t}\theta+V\cdot\nabla_{h}\theta+W\partial_{z}\theta+\frac{N^{2}\theta_{0}}{g}W=S_{\theta}\\
&\partial_{t}Q+V\cdot\nabla_{h}Q+W\partial_{z}Q=-P\end{aligned}\right.

where
V=(V_{1},V_{2})
,
U=(V,W)
is the velocity field,
\Phi
the pressure,
Q
the moisture,
h=(x,y)
,
\theta
the potential temperature (the temperature that a parcel of air would have if displaced adiabatically in the vertical from its original position to a fixed reference position). Classic references on this subject include

The global existence of the primitive viscous equations, fundamental for the large-scale description of oceanic and atmospheric dynamics, has been shown in the seminal paper
[

there is an overview regarding the most relevant results in the existence theory for the primitive equations and the recent developments in geophysical models: global stability of strong solutions (with full or partial viscosity), conditional uniqueness of weak solutions and their global stability, even in the presence of discontinuous initial data and singular perturbation limits such as small aspect ratio which rigorously justify hydrostatic equilibrium and relaxation limits.

The main difficulty lies in the presence of discontinuous multivalued nonlinear term in the temperature and humidity equations, which is active only under supersaturation conditions, and in the corresponding variations of temperature. To address the problem, both in

Strictly related to the moisture phase transition model is the so called
Tropical Climate Model
introduced in
[
10
]
.
The absence of the Coriolis force at the equator favours the formation of different types of atmospheric waves, characterised by complex vertical and meridional structures. These waves are mainly divided into two categories: barotropic waves, which propagate toward the poles, and baroclinic waves, which remain localised near the equator and extend along it.
Moreover, in the tropical zone of the earth, the wind in the lower troposphere is of equal magnitude, but with opposite sign to that in the upper troposphere, in other words, the primary effect is captured in the first baroclinic mode, i.e.on the first mode of the fluctuation of the solution about its vertical average. However, for the study of the tropical-extratropical interactions, where the transport of momentum between the barotropic (the spatial vertical average of the solution) and baroclinic (the fluctuation of the solution about the barotropic part) modes plays an important role, it is necessary to retain both the barotropic and baroclinic modes of the velocity. In
[
15
]
, the global well-posedness of strong solutions was established for this kind of model and in
[
20
]
the existence and uniqueness of global solutions for the three-dimensional tropical climate model with partial viscosity and damping are established. Techniques involving anisotropic embedding and fractional derivative interpolation inequalities are employed to obtain estimates and smooth solutions, which are compared with the classical Navier Stokes equations. An important ingredient of the tropical atmospheric circulation is the water vapour. Water vapour is the most abundant greenhouse gas in the atmosphere, and it is responsible for amplifying the long-term warming or cooling cycles. Therefore, one should also consider the coupling with an equation modeling moisture in the atmosphere. Indeed in
[
10
]
the equations are analogous to those used for shallow waters and include a precipitation term that acts as a nonlinear source. This system is also coupled with the nonlinear, incompressible barotropic flow equations. From the perspective of tropical atmospheric dynamics, this theory offers a new outlook on how large regions of humidity, associated with deep convection, can move and interact with large-scale dynamics within a near-equilibrium framework. In
[
14
]

[

H^{1}

\epsilon

H^{1}

\epsilon

H^{1}
that depends continuously on the data if they are slightly more regular than
H^{1}
.

The purpose of this paper is to fill the gap in the models
[

]
and
[

3.2.
Precipitation parameterization

The saturated adiabatic lapse rate is fundamental in understanding the structure and evolution of clouds, as well as the associated weather conditions. It influences atmospheric stability, precipitation, and other atmospheric phenomena. Air is considered saturated when the vapor pressure of humidity reaches its maximum limit, to the point where it cannot hold more water vapor without resulting in condensation, as in the case of cloud or fog formation. In this context, we will focus on adiabatic processes, meaning situations in which there is no heat exchange with the external environment. When a mass of air rises, it expands and cools; conversely, when it descends, it undergoes compression and warms up. We know from
[
6
]
(and references therein) that the general form of moisture equation is the following:

(3.13)

\frac{Dq}{Dt}=\frac{Dq_{s}}{Dt}+D_{q}=P

where
q
denotes the specific humidity, that is the ratio between the mass of water vapor and the total mass of moist air, and thus it is always assumed that
q\geq 0
. The term
q_{s}
denotes the saturation concentration (or saturation humidity) and
D_{q}
is a suitable form of dissipation accounting for horizontal and vertical diffusion.
For simplicity we impose from now on
D_{q}=0
, therefore our aim is to explicit through thermodynamic relations the form of
Dq_{s}/Dt
, hence of the precipitation term
P
.

We denote by
L
the latent heat of evaporization,
R
is the ideal gas constant,
c_{p}
is the air specific heat capacity,
R^{\nu}
the gas constants for water vapour,
k=R^{d}/R^{\nu}
,
p
the total pressure and
e_{s}
the partial pressure of water vapour in air that occurs when the vapour is in equilibrium with the liquid. If more vapour were added, it would immediately condense. Starting from Chapter 18 in
[
27
]
, in the context of atmospheric convection processes, we review the most important thermodynamic relations to explicitly express the equations satisfied by the humidity
q
and the temperature
T
. Here, we recall that in a moist atmosphere the moist static energy is conserved as a parcel ascends and this allows us to write down the equation satisfied by the temperature
T
as

c_{p}\frac{dT}{dz}=-L\frac{dq}{dz}-g.

If the air is moist but not saturated then an ascending parcel will follow the dry adiabatic lapse rate (because
dq/dz=0
) but if it is saturated, namely
q=q_{s}
, then as a parcel ascends it will cool and some water vapour will condense. Since
q_{s}
can be expressed as
q_{s}=ke_{s}/p
, and so is a function of temperature and pressure, we have

\frac{dq_{s}}{dz}=\left(\frac{dq_{s}}{dT}\right)_{p}\frac{dT}{dz}+\frac{q_{s}}{p}\rho g,

using hydrostasy. Using the Clausius–Clapeyron equation

\frac{de_{s}}{dT}=\frac{Le_{s}}{R^{\nu}T^{2}},

and the ideal gas equation of state we get the relation between
T
and
q
of an adiabatically ascending saturated parcel, that is

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{dT}{dz}=-\frac{L}{c_{p}}\frac{dq_{s}}{dz}-\frac{g}{c_{p}}\\
&\frac{dq_{s}}{dz}=\frac{Lq_{s}}{R^{\nu}T^{2}}\frac{dT}{dz}+\frac{gq_{s}}{RT}.\end{aligned}\right.

By substituting
\displaystyle{\frac{dT}{dz}}
in the equation of
\displaystyle{\frac{dq_{s}}{dz}}
we get:

\frac{dq_{s}}{dz}=\frac{g}{R}q_{s}\left(\frac{c_{p}R^{\nu}T-LR}{c_{p}R^{\nu}T^{2}+L^{2}q_{s}}\right),

and thus, by classical chain rule, we easily obtain:

\frac{Dq_{s}}{Dt}=\frac{dq_{s}}{dz}\frac{Dz}{Dt}=w\frac{g}{R}q_{s}\left(\frac{c_{p}R^{\nu}T-LR}{c_{p}R^{\nu}T^{2}+L^{2}q_{s}}\right).

Hence from (
3.13
) we get

P=-\frac{H}{\pi}\dive v\frac{g}{R}q_{s}\left(\frac{c_{p}R^{\nu}T-LR}{c_{p}R^{\nu}T^{2}+L^{2}q_{s}}\right),

where we used (
3.8
).
Finally, the equation for
q
is given by,

(3.14)

\partial_{t}q+u\nabla q+\bar{Q}\dive v=\frac{H}{\pi}\dive v\frac{g}{R}q_{s}\left(\frac{c_{p}R^{\nu}T-LR}{c_{p}R^{\nu}T^{2}+L^{2}q_{s}}\right).

Now, by recalling that the contribution of the source term
S_{T}
is given by a diffusivity part (heat flux) and a precipitation term, namely,
S_{T}=\Delta T+P
(see also
[
14
,
26
]
) we get

(3.15)

\partial_{t}T+u\nabla T-\Delta T-\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{\theta_{0}N^{2}}{g}\dive v=-\frac{H}{\pi}\dive v\frac{g}{R}q_{s}\left(\frac{c_{p}R^{\nu}T-LR}{c_{p}R^{\nu}T^{2}+L^{2}q_{s}}\right).

Finally, collecting (
3.8
), (
3.10
), (
3.12
), (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) the system for tropical atmosphere which describes humidity and moisture interactions becomes:

(3.16)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u-\mu\Delta u+\nabla p+\dive(v\otimes v)=0\\
&\partial_{t}v+u\cdot\nabla v+v\nabla u-\mu\Delta v=\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{g}{\theta_{0}}\nabla T\\
&\dive u=0\\
&\partial_{t}T+u\nabla T-\Delta T-\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{\theta_{0}N^{2}}{g}\dive v=-\frac{H}{\pi}\dive v\frac{g}{R}q_{s}\left(\frac{LR-c_{p}R^{\nu}T}{c_{p}R^{\nu}T^{2}+L^{2}q_{s}}\right)\\
&\partial_{t}q+u\nabla q+\bar{Q}\dive v=\frac{H}{\pi}\dive v\frac{g}{R}q_{s}\left(\frac{LR-c_{p}R^{\nu}T}{c_{p}R^{\nu}T^{2}+L^{2}q_{s}}\right)\end{aligned}\right.

Remark 3.1
.

We recall that
\bar{Q}
represents the gross moisture stratification and thus one has to assume that
\bar{Q}>0
. This condition is motivated by the fact that typical humidity measurements indicate an exponential decrease of moisture with altitude over scales of a few kilometers. The parameter
\bar{Q}
plays a fundamental role in atmospheric dynamics: a higher value of
\bar{Q}
tends to reduce stability in humid regions, influencing convection processes.

3.3.
Equivalent formulation

In order to simplify the formulation of (
3.16
) we impose that
\mu=1
and the humidity saturation parameter
q_{s}
is a constant and
0<q_{s}<1
.

In our setting the precipitation production
P
is given by

(3.17)

P=-\frac{H}{\pi}\dive v\frac{g}{R}q_{s}\left(\frac{LR-c_{p}R^{\nu}T}{c_{p}R^{\nu}T^{2}+L^{2}q_{s}}\right)=-\frac{H}{\pi}\dive v\frac{g}{RT}F(T)=-\frac{H}{\pi}\dive v\frac{g}{R}G(T)

where
F(T)
is defined as

F(T)=q_{s}T\left(\frac{LR-c_{p}R^{\nu}T}{c_{p}R^{\nu}T^{2}+q_{s}L^{2}}\right),

and satisfies the following properties,

(3.18)

\displaystyle|F(\xi_{1})-F(\xi_{2})|\leq M_{F}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\quad\text{ for every }\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}.

\displaystyle|F(\xi)|\leq C_{F}\quad\text{ for every }\xi\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\text{ Since }F(0)=0\text{ we have }|F(\xi)|\leq M_{F}|\xi|\quad\text{ for every }\xi\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\text{ Since }F(\xi_{0})=0\text{ where }\xi_{0}=LR/c_{p}R_{\nu}\text{ then }F(\xi)\geq 0\Longleftrightarrow\xi\in[0,\xi_{0}].

Moreover the function
G(T)
is defined as

G(T):=\frac{F(T)}{T}=q_{s}\left(\frac{LR-c_{p}R^{\nu}T}{c_{p}R^{\nu}T^{2}+L^{2}q_{s}}\right),

and the following properties hold

(3.19)

\displaystyle|G(\xi_{1})-G(\xi_{2})|\leq M_{G}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\quad\text{ for every }\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}.

\displaystyle|G(\xi)|\leq C_{G}\quad\text{ for every }\xi\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\text{ Since }G(0)=\frac{R}{L}\text{ we have }\left|G(\xi)-\frac{R}{L}\right|\leq M_{G}|\xi|\quad\text{ for every }\xi\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\text{ Since }G(\xi_{0})=0\text{ where }\xi_{0}=LR/c_{p}R_{\nu}\text{ then }G(\xi)\geq 0\Longleftrightarrow\xi\in(-\infty,\xi_{0}].

When the air is humid, the convection process, which is the vertical movement of air, occurs more easily compared to dry air. This is because when the water vapor present in the air condenses, changing from a gaseous to a liquid state, it releases heat called “latent heat”. This heat helps to warm the surrounding air, thus facilitating its uplift. However, this condition only occurs when the air is saturated. In other words, only when the air is fully moist and saturated the heat released during condensation can influence the stability of the air and promote the convection process. Therefore one assumes that the contribution by
G(T)
in (
3.17
) applies only during condensation, namely when
q\geq q_{s}
and requires the upward motion (
\frac{dz}{dt}>0
or equivalently
\omega:=\frac{dp}{dt}<0
in pressure coordinates, see
[
6
]
for instance). Since,

\frac{dz}{dt}=w=-\frac{H}{\pi}\dive v\quad\text{ then }\quad w>0\Longleftrightarrow\dive v<0,

we can write (
3.17
) in this way,

(3.20)

P=\delta\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}vG(T),

where
f^{-}=\max\{-f,0\}
denotes the negative part of
f
and
\delta
is defined as

\delta=\left\{\begin{aligned} 1\qquad&q\geq q_{s}\qquad\text{and }\dive v\leq 0\\
0\qquad&\text{ otherwise. }\end{aligned}\right.

In this paper we make use of the equivalent formulation of (
3.20
) given by

(3.21)

P=\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}\mathcal{H}(q-q_{s})G(T),

where
\mathcal{H}(q-q_{s})
is the Heaviside multivalued function defined as,

(3.22)

\mathcal{H}(r)=\left\{\begin{aligned} 0\qquad&r<0\\
(0,1)\qquad&r=0\\
1\qquad&r>0\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover, we replace
G(T)
by its positive part
G^{+}(T)
. As we will see later on, this assumption plays a central role in order to prove the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions for system (
3.24
). Taking into account the property
\eqref{prop_G}_{4}
this requirement is linked with an upper bound of the temperature
T
,

(3.23)

T\leq\xi_{0}=\frac{LR}{c_{p}R_{\nu}}.

The condition mentioned above is not overly restrictive. In Section
7
, by combining the a priori estimates from Section
6
and the parabolic structure of the temperature balance equation, we can derive that
T
is uniformly bounded in space and time (see (
7.4
)). In particular, by applying the classical maximum principle, we can conclude that if we choose an initial datum satisfying (
3.23
), then the same holds for
T
. We emphasize that the condition (
3.23
) will always be satisfied by an initial temperature since

\xi_{0}=\frac{LR}{c_{p}R_{\nu}}\simeq 1548\ \text{Kelvin},

which is never achievable for atmosphere temperatures.

Finally the system (
3.16
) can be rewritten as follows

(3.24)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u-\Delta u+\nabla p+\dive(v\otimes v)=0\\
&\partial_{t}v+u\cdot\nabla v+v\nabla u-\Delta v=\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{g}{\theta_{0}}\nabla T\\
&\dive u=0\\
&\partial_{t}T+u\nabla T-\Delta T-\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{\theta_{0}N^{2}}{g}\dive v=\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}\mathcal{H}(q-q_{s})G^{+}(T).\\
&\partial_{t}q+u\nabla q+\bar{Q}\dive v=-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}\mathcal{H}(q-q_{s})G^{+}(T).\end{aligned}\right.

Remark 3.2
.

We point out that in order to deal with the discontinuous right hand side of the equations for
T
and
q
in (
3.24
), one can rephrase it as a differential inclusions (see
[
6
]
). Therefore the system (
3.24
) becomes,

(3.25)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u-\Delta u+\nabla p+\dive(v\otimes v)=0\\
&\partial_{t}v+u\cdot\nabla v+v\nabla u-\Delta v=\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{g}{\theta_{0}}\nabla T\\
&\dive u=0\\
&\partial_{t}T+u\nabla T-\Delta T-\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{\theta_{0}N^{2}}{g}\dive v\in\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}\mathcal{H}(q-q_{s})G^{+}(T)\\
&\partial_{t}q+u\nabla q+\bar{Q}\dive v\in-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}\mathcal{H}(q-q_{s})G^{+}(T).\end{aligned}\right.

4.
Main results

In this section, we will present the main results within the context of global strong solutions, which we define as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

Given a positive time
\mathcal{T}
and initial data
(u_{0},v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then
(u,v,T,q)
is called strong solution to system (
3.24
) if it enjoys the following properties

\displaystyle(u,v,T)\in C([0,\mathcal{T}];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap L^{2}((0,\mathcal{T});H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}));

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}v,\partial_{t}T,\partial_{t}q)\in L^{2}((0,\mathcal{T});L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}));

\displaystyle q\in C([0,\mathcal{T}];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap L^{\infty}((0,\mathcal{T});H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})),

has initial value

(u,v,T,q)\big|_{t=0}=(u_{0},v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})

and satisfies the system (
3.24
) a.e on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\mathcal{T})
. More precisely,
T
and
q
satisfy a.e on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\mathcal{T})
the following equations:

(4.1)

\partial_{t}T+u\nabla T-\Delta T-\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{\theta_{0}N^{2}}{g}\dive v=\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}h_{q}G^{+}(T),

(4.2)

\partial_{t}q+u\nabla q+\bar{Q}\dive v=-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}h_{q}G^{+}(T),

where
h_{q}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\infty))
is an element of
\mathcal{H}(q-q_{s})
defined as:

(4.3)

h_{q}(x,y,t):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1\qquad q-q_{s}>0\\
&\alpha\in[0,1]\qquad q-q_{s}=0\\
&0\qquad q-q_{s}<0\end{aligned}\right.

Remark 4.2
.

If we adopt the differential inclusion formulation as in
[
6
]
(see system (
3.25
)), then (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) can be reformulated by saying that
h_{q}\in L^{\infty}_{t,x}
and satisfies the following variational inequality,

(4.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[\tilde{q}-q_{s}]^{+}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[q-q_{s}]^{+}dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}h_{q}(\tilde{q}-q)dx\qquad\text{a.e in }[0,t_{1}]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

This means that
h_{q}
is an element of the subdifferential of the function
q\rightarrow([q-q_{s}]^{+},1)
where
(\cdot,1)
is the
L^{2}
scalar product. Since it holds that

\partial([q-q_{s}]^{+},1)=\mathcal{H}(q-q_{s})

it is easy to verify that if
h_{q}\in\mathcal{H}(q-q_{s})
then it satisfies (
4.3
).

In this paper, we will not use this formulation since, as we work on an unbounded domain, we will not obtain the compactness needed to prove (
4.4
), as will be shown later.

Now we are ready to state our main results.

Theorem 4.3
.

(Global existence of strong solutions)
Suppose that
(u_{0},v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\dive u_{0}=0
. Then there exist global strong solutions
(u,v,T,q)
to system (
3.24
) in the sense of Definition
4.1
such that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}}\|(u,v,T,q)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}(\|(\Delta u,\Delta v,\Delta T,\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}v,\partial_{t}T,\partial_{t}q)\|_{2}^{2}+\|(\nabla u,\nabla v)\|_{\infty})dt\leq C

where
h_{q}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\infty))
is an element of
\mathcal{H}(q-q_{s})
defined as (
4.3
) and

C:=C(\mathcal{T},\|(u_{0},v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})\|_{H^{1}})
is a nondecreasing function on
\mathcal{T}\in[0,\infty)
.

Theorem 4.4
.

(Uniqueness of global strong solutions)
Suppose that
(u_{1},v_{1},T_{1},q_{1})
and
(u_{2},v_{2},T_{2},q_{2})
are two global strong solutions to the system (
3.24
) as provided by Theorem
4.3
, with the same initial data and satisfying
(q_{1}-q_{s})(q_{2}-q_{s})\geq 0
a.e in
(0,\mathcal{T})\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then the two solutions coincide; namely,

(u_{1},v_{1},T_{1},q_{1})\equiv(u_{2},v_{2},T_{2},q_{2})\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,\mathcal{T}),\quad\text{for every }\mathcal{T}\in[0,\infty).

Concerning the uniqueness result, we note that the result is conditional to the assumption
(q_{1}-q_{s})(q_{2}-q_{s})\geq 0
. Physically, this assumption means that both solutions remain in the same thermodynamic regime: either below saturation (
q\leq q_{s}
) or above saturation (
q\geq q_{s}
). We stress that this requirement is physically sound. Indeed, since
q_{s}
represents the saturation humidity, namely the maximal amount of water vapor sustainable before condensation processes become active, then the regions
q\leq q_{s}
and
q\geq q_{s}
correspond to distinct physical states of the atmosphere.
From the analytical viewpoint, the threshold
q=q_{s}
is precisely where the nonlinear source term becomes discontinuous. Preventing changes of sign of
q-q_{s}
allows one to control the multivalued contribution and derive a stability estimate leading to uniqueness. On the other hand, although it is natural to assume that the initial datum satisfies either
q_{0}\leq q_{s}
or
q_{0}\geq q_{s}
, we are currently not able to prove that such a condition is propagated by the dynamics for positive times. Establishing the invariance of these regimes under the evolution remains an open issue, and for this reason the uniqueness statement is conditional.

5.
Approximate Problem

In this section, we define a family of regularized problems starting from system (
3.24
). In particular, we introduce two different approximations. First, we regularize the Heaviside function by means of mollification, denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(q-q_{s})
, to obtain a single-valued function. Next, we introduce an artificial viscosity term
\eta\Delta q
in the humidity equation. This strategy is designed to ensure the necessary regularity to prove local and, subsequently, global well-posedness for each fixed pair of parameters
\epsilon,\eta>0
.

I approximation: Problem
P_{\epsilon}

Let us introduce the real function
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(\cdot)
that approximate
\mathcal{H}(\cdot)
,

(5.1)

\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(r):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0,\quad r\leq 0\\
&r/\epsilon,\quad r\in(0,\epsilon]\\
&1,\quad r>\epsilon\end{aligned}\right.

the function
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(r)
enjoys the following properties,

(5.2)

\displaystyle|H_{\epsilon}(r_{1})|\leq 1,\quad|H_{\epsilon}(r_{1})-H_{\epsilon}(r_{2})|\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}|r_{1}-r_{2}|\qquad\text{for any}\ r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{R}.

Now, the approximating system
P_{\epsilon}
is given by

(
P_{\epsilon}
)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u-\Delta u+\nabla p+\dive(v\otimes v)=0\\
&\partial_{t}v+u\cdot\nabla v+v\nabla u-\Delta v=\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{g}{\theta_{0}}\nabla T\\
&\dive u=0\\
&\partial_{t}T+u\nabla T-\Delta T-\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{\theta_{0}N^{2}}{g}\dive v=\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(q-q_{s})G^{+}(T)\\
&\partial_{t}q+u\nabla q+\bar{Q}\dive v=-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(q-q_{s})G^{+}(T),\end{aligned}\right.

such that for every fixed
\epsilon>0
is a single-valued problem. In Section
6
we will prove the global well-posedness of system (

P
ϵ

).

II approximation: Problem
P_{\epsilon,\eta}

In order to prove the existence of a global strong solution to (

P
ϵ

), one has to overcome the difficulty caused by the absence of diffusivity in the moisture equation. As already mentioned, we regularize (

P
ϵ

) by adding an artificial viscosity term
\eta\Delta q
, with a positive parameter
\eta>0
.

(
P_{\epsilon,\eta}
)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u-\Delta u+\nabla p+\dive(v\otimes v)=0\\
&\partial_{t}v+u\cdot\nabla v+v\nabla u-\Delta v=\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{g}{\theta_{0}}\nabla T\\
&\dive u=0\\
&\partial_{t}T+u\nabla T-\Delta T-\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{\theta_{0}N^{2}}{g}\dive v=\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(q-q_{s})G^{+}(T).\\
&\partial_{t}q+u\nabla q+\bar{Q}\dive v-\eta\Delta q=-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(q-q_{s})G^{+}(T).\end{aligned}\right.

For the system (

P

ϵ
,
η

) it is possible to prove the following global well-posedness result.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose that
(u_{0},v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\dive u_{0}=0
. Then, there is a unique global strong solution
(u,v,T,q)
to (

P

ϵ
,
η

) with initial data
(u_{0},v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})
, such that

\displaystyle(u,v,T,q)\in C([0,\infty);H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap L^{2}_{loc}([0,\infty);H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})),

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}v,\partial_{t}T,\partial_{t}q)\in L^{2}_{loc}([0,\infty);H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})).

In particular, we have for all
\mathcal{T}\in[0,\infty)
,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}}\|(u,v,T,q)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\|(\Delta u,\Delta v,\Delta T,\sqrt{\eta}\Delta q,\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}v,\partial_{t}T,\partial_{t}q)\|_{2}^{2}dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}(\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla v\|_{\infty})dt\leq C

where
C:=C(\mathcal{T},\|(u_{0},v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})\|_{H^{2}})
is a nondecreasing function on
\mathcal{T}\in[0,\infty)
and it is indipendent on
\eta
.

We would like to emphasize that the proof of Proposition

is largely in line with those presented in
[
14
,
15
]
. The novelty of the present work lies in the inclusion of the diffusivity term in the temperature equation and in the specific form of the precipitation term. For this reason, we will focus only on analyzing the main differences related to these aspects.

Proof of Proposition
5.1

The proof of Proposition
5.1
consists of several steps. The local existence and uniqueness of strong solutions to system (

P

ϵ
,
η

) can be proved in a standard way, and here we only sketch the main arguments. For the sake of clarity, the
\eta
–uniform energy estimates are presented in Section
6
, since they are the key ingredients used to prove Theorem
6.1
.

Contraction mapping principle

We aim to apply the contraction mapping principle to the following linear system defined on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\mathcal{T})
with initial data
(u_{0},v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}U-\Delta U+\nabla P=-u\cdot\nabla u-\dive(v\otimes v)

\displaystyle\dive U=0

\displaystyle\partial_{t}V-\Delta V=-u\cdot\nabla v+\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{g}{\theta_{0}}\nabla T-v\cdot\nabla u

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{T}-\Delta\tilde{T}=-u\nabla T+\frac{HN^{2}\theta_{0}}{\pi g}\dive v+\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(q-q_{s})G^{+}(T)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Q-\eta\Delta Q=-u\nabla q-\bar{Q}\dive v-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(q-q_{s})G^{+}(T).

Let us consider a positive time interval
\mathcal{T}
. For every
(u,v,T,q)\in L^{2}((0,\mathcal{T});H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap C([0,\mathcal{T}];H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, one can uniquely find a solution
(U,V,\tilde{T},Q)\in L^{2}((0,\mathcal{T});H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap C([0,\mathcal{T}];H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, with time derivatives
(\partial_{t}U,\partial_{t}V,\partial_{t}\tilde{T},\partial_{t}Q)\in L^{2}((0,\mathcal{T});H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, to the linear system defined above, satisfying the initial condition
(u_{0},v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})
. By performing standard energy estimates, one can show that the solution map
\mathrm{R}
defined as

\mathrm{R}:(u,v,T,q)\rightarrow(U,V,\tilde{T},Q)

is a contraction in the space
L^{2}((0,\mathcal{T});H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap C([0,\mathcal{T}];H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, for sufficiently small positive time
\mathcal{T}
depending only on the initial data
(u_{0},v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})
. By the Banach fixed-point theorem, this yields the existence of a unique fixed point
(u,v,T,q)
, corresponding to the unique local strong solution of the system with the prescribed initial data.

Global existence

In order to prove global existence, one needs to show that the maximal existence time
\mathcal{T}^{*}>0
of the unique strong solution to (

P

ϵ
,
η

) satisfies
\mathcal{T}^{*}=\infty
. We proceed by contradiction assuming that
\mathcal{T}^{*}<\infty
. Applying the operator
\nabla
to equation
\eqref{approx}_{5}
and taking the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
inner product with
-\nabla\Delta q
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta q\|_{2}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\eta}\nabla\Delta q\|_{2}^{2}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[|u||\nabla^{2}q|+|\nabla^{2}v|+|\nabla v\nabla T|+|\nabla v\nabla q|]|\nabla\Delta q|dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{4}\|\nabla^{2}q\|_{4}+\|\Delta v\|_{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{4}\|\nabla T\|_{4}+\|\nabla v\|_{4}\|\nabla q\|_{4})\|\nabla\Delta q\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|u\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\Delta q\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\nabla\Delta q\|_{2}^{1/2}+\|\Delta v\|_{2}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{1/2}\|(\nabla q,\nabla T)\|_{2}^{1/2}\|(\Delta q,\Delta T)\|_{2}^{1/2}\Big)\|\nabla\Delta q\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\sqrt{\eta}\,\nabla\Delta q\|_{2}^{2}+C_{\eta}\Big(\|u\|_{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}\|\Delta q\|_{2}\|\nabla\Delta q\|_{2}+\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla v\|_{2}\|\Delta v\|_{2}\|(\nabla q,\nabla T)\|_{2}\|(\Delta q,\Delta T)\|_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\eta}\nabla\Delta q\|^{2}_{2}+C_{\eta}(1+\|u\|_{2}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|(\nabla v,\nabla q,\nabla T)\|_{2}^{2})\|)\|(\Delta v,\Delta q,\Delta T)\|_{2}^{2}.

By repeating the same argument for
(u,v,T)
as in
[
15
]
, one can obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,\mathcal{T}^{*}]}\|(u,v,T,q)\|_{H^{2}}+\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}^{*}}\|(\nabla u,\nabla v,\nabla T,\nabla q)\|_{H^{2}}dt\leq C,

Proposition 6.6

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(q-q_{s})|\leq 1

The other terms can be treated similarly and in particular,

therefore

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|[K(t-s)*(\dive v)(s)](x)\|_{\infty}ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla v\|_{\infty}ds\leq C(t,\|(u_{0},v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})\|_{H^{1}}).

Finally, by taking the supremum over
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,\mathcal{T})
we deduce

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,\mathcal{T})}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}|T(x,t)|\leq\sup_{t\in[0,\mathcal{T})}\|T_{0}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\sup_{t\in[0,\mathcal{T})}\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K(t-s,x-y)(u\nabla T)(s,y)dy\Bigg|ds

\displaystyle+\sup_{t\in[0,\mathcal{T})}\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K(t-s,x-y)

\displaystyle\times\Big(\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{\theta_{0}N^{2}}{g}\dive v+\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(q-q_{s})G^{+}(T)\Bigg)(s,y)\,dy\Bigg|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{0}\|_{\infty}+C\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\Big\|K(t-s)*\Big(u\nabla T+\dive v\Big)(s)\Big\|_{\infty}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|T_{0}\|_{\infty}+\mathcal{T}^{\frac{1}{4}})\leq C\Big(\mathcal{T},\|(u_{0},v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\Big).

The proof is concluded.

(7.3)

\displaystyle(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon},T_{\epsilon},q_{\epsilon})\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}(u,v,T,q)\text{ in }L^{\infty}((0,\mathcal{T});H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})),

\displaystyle(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon},T_{\epsilon})\overset{}{\rightharpoonup}(u,v,T)\text{ in }L^{2}((0,\mathcal{T});H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})),

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon},\partial_{t}v_{\epsilon},\partial_{t}T_{\epsilon},\partial_{t}q_{\epsilon})\overset{}{\rightharpoonup}(\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}v,\partial_{t}T,\partial_{t}q)\text{ in }L^{2}((0,\mathcal{T});L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(q_{\epsilon}-q_{s})\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}h_{q}\text{ in }L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\mathcal{T})),

where the last convergence comes from the
L^{\infty}_{t,x}
bound of the function
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}
. In addition, we also have that

T_{\epsilon}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}T\mbox{ in }L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\mathcal{T})),

and thus the limiting temperature satisfies

(7.4)

T\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\mathcal{T})).

Applying Aubin-Lions lemma, for any positive time
\mathcal{T}
and a disk
B_{R}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
of arbitrary radius
R>0
it holds:

(7.5)

\displaystyle(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon},T_{\epsilon})\rightarrow(u,v,T)\text{ in }C([0,\mathcal{T});L^{2}(B_{R}))\cap L^{2}((0,T);H^{1}(B_{R})),

\displaystyle q_{\epsilon}\rightarrow q\text{ in }C([0,\mathcal{T});L^{2}(B_{R})).

As argued in the previous section, the convergences in (
7.3
), together with Fatou’s Lemma and the weak lower semicontinuity of the norms, allow us to pass to the limit in (
6.1
), yielding

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,\mathcal{T}]}\left(\|(u,v,T,q)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|T\|_{\infty}\right)+\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\|(\Delta u,\Delta v,\Delta T,\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}v,\partial_{t}T,\partial_{t}q)\|_{2}^{2}dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla v\|_{\infty}dt\leq\liminf_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\Bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,\mathcal{T}]}\left(\|(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon},T_{\epsilon},q_{\epsilon})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|T_{\epsilon}\|_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\|(\Delta u_{\epsilon},\Delta v_{\epsilon},\Delta T_{\epsilon},\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon},\partial_{t}v_{\epsilon},\partial_{t}T_{\epsilon},\partial_{t}q_{\epsilon})\|_{2}^{2}dt+\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\big(\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla v_{\epsilon}\|_{\infty}\big)dt\Bigg)\leq C.

It remains to establish the pointwise convergence as
\epsilon\to 0
of the temperature and moisture equations (
4.1
) and (
4.2
).

Convergence of the temperature and the moisture equations

In this section, we prove the validity of the equations (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,\mathcal{T}]
.
In other words, we need to show that the limit solution
(u,v,T,q)
satisfies

I case:

On
\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,\mathcal{T}]:q-q_{s}<0\right\}
it holds a.e that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}q+u\nabla q+\bar{Q}\dive v=0

\displaystyle\partial_{t}T+u\nabla T-\Delta T-\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{\theta_{0}N^{2}}{g}\dive v=0.

II case:

On
\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,\mathcal{T}]:q-q_{s}>0\right\}
it holds a.e that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}q+u\nabla q+\bar{Q}\dive v+\frac{Hg}{\pi R}G^{+}(T)(\dive v)^{-}=0

\displaystyle\partial_{t}T+u\nabla T-\Delta T-\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{\theta_{0}N^{2}}{g}\dive v=\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}G^{+}(T).

III case:

On
\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,\mathcal{T}]:q-q_{s}=0\right\}
it holds a.e that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}q+u\nabla q+\bar{Q}\dive v+\frac{Hg}{\pi R}G^{+}(T)(\dive v)^{-}h_{q}=0

\displaystyle\partial_{t}T+u\nabla T-\Delta T-\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{\theta_{0}N^{2}}{g}\dive v=\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v)^{-}G^{+}(T)h_{q},

where
h_{q}
is the weak* limit of
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}
and
h_{q}=\alpha\in[0,1]
when
q(x,t)=q_{s}
. For simplicity, we will focus on the moisture equation, as the argument for the temperature equation is analogous.

I case

In the same spirit of
[
14
]
, for every triplet of positive integers
j,k,l>0
we can define:

\displaystyle O^{-}=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\infty):q-q_{s}<0\right\},

\displaystyle O_{jkl}^{-}=\left\{(x,t)\in B_{k}(0)\times(0,l):q-q_{s}<-\frac{1}{j}\right\},

\displaystyle O^{-}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{l=1}^{\infty}O_{jkl}^{-},

where
B_{k}
is the usual disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of radius
k
. Let us fix
(j,k,l)
, from (
7.5
) we deduce that
q_{\epsilon}\rightarrow q
in
L^{2}(O_{jkl}^{-})
and thus there exists a subsequence
q_{\epsilon_{n}}
such that
q_{\epsilon_{n}}\rightarrow q
a.e. in
O_{jkl}^{-}
. Thanks to the Egoroff theorem
\forall\eta>0\;\exists\;E_{\eta}\subset O_{jkl}^{-}
such that
|E_{\eta}|\leq\eta
and
q_{\epsilon_{n}}\rightarrow q
uniformly on
O_{jkl}^{-}\setminus E_{\eta}
. Therefore,

\forall j>0\quad\exists\quad\bar{n}=n(j)\;\text{ such that }\;\forall n>\bar{n}\quad q_{\epsilon_{n}}-q_{s}\leq q-q_{s}+\frac{1}{2j}\leq-\frac{1}{2j}<0,

this implies that for
q_{\epsilon_{n}}

H_{\epsilon_{n}}(q_{\epsilon_{n}}-q_{s})=0
on
O_{jkl}^{-}\setminus E_{\eta}
for
\epsilon_{n}
sufficiently small (
\epsilon_{n}<\epsilon_{\bar{n}}
) and

\mathcal{G_{\epsilon}}:=\partial_{t}q_{\epsilon_{n}}+u_{\epsilon_{n}}\nabla q_{\epsilon_{n}}+\bar{Q}\dive v_{\epsilon_{n}}=0\quad\text{ on }\quad O_{jkl}^{-}\setminus E_{\eta}.

From the strong convergence of
(q_{\epsilon_{n}},u_{\epsilon_{n}},v_{\epsilon_{n}})
in
(
7.5
)
we deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{G_{\epsilon}}\overset{}{\rightharpoonup}\mathcal{G}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(O_{jkl}^{-}\setminus E_{\eta}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}:=\partial_{t}q+u\nabla q+\bar{Q}\dive v=0.

Since
\mathcal{G_{\epsilon}}=0\quad a.e\quad O_{jkl}^{-}\setminus E_{\eta}
this means that
\mathcal{G}=0\quad a.e\quad on\qquad O_{jkl}^{-}\setminus E_{\eta}
. Thanks to Lemma
2.2
we conclude that
\mathcal{G}=0
on
O_{jkl}^{-}
for any positive
(j,k,l)
and therefore it holds on
O^{-}
.

II case

In the same way we construct
O^{+}
and
O_{jkl}^{+}
such that:

\displaystyle O^{+}=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\infty):q-q_{s}>0\right\},\;O_{jkl}^{+}=\left\{(x,t)\in B_{k}(0)\times(0,l):\frac{1}{j}<q-q_{s}\right\},

\displaystyle O^{+}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{l=1}^{\infty}O_{jkl}^{+}.

As before, for every fixed
(j,k,l)
, the strong convergence of
q_{\epsilon}
in (
7.5
) and the Egoroff theorem imply that
\forall\eta>0\;\exists\;E_{\eta}\subset O_{jkl}^{-}
such that
|E_{\eta}|\leq\eta
and a subsequence
q_{\epsilon_{n}}\rightarrow q
uniformly on
O_{jkl}^{+}\setminus E_{\eta}
. In particular, for any fixed
\eta
, it holds that

\forall j>0\quad\exists\quad\bar{n}=n(j)\;\text{ such that }\;\forall n>\bar{n}\quad q-\frac{1}{2j}<q_{\epsilon_{n}}<q+\frac{1}{2j}\mbox{ and }\epsilon_{n}<\frac{1}{2j}.

Therefore for any
n>\bar{n}
it holds

0<\epsilon_{n}<\frac{1}{2j}=\frac{1}{j}-\frac{1}{2j}<q-q_{s}-\frac{1}{2j}\leq q_{\epsilon_{n}}-q_{s},

and thus

q_{\epsilon_{n}}-q_{s}>\epsilon_{n}\quad\text{ on }\quad O_{jkl}^{+}\setminus E_{\eta}.

This implies that for
q_{\epsilon_{n}}
it holds that
H_{{\epsilon}_{n}}(q_{\epsilon_{n}}-q_{s})=1
and

\mathcal{G_{\epsilon}}:=\partial_{t}q_{\epsilon_{n}}+u_{\epsilon_{n}}\nabla q_{\epsilon_{n}}+\bar{Q}\dive v_{\epsilon_{n}}+\frac{H}{\pi}G^{+}(T_{\epsilon_{n}})(\dive v_{\epsilon_{n}})^{+}=0\quad\text{ on }\quad O_{jkl}^{+}\setminus E_{\eta}.

From the strong convergence of
(q_{\epsilon_{n}},u_{\epsilon_{n}},v_{\epsilon_{n}})
in (
7.5
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G_{\epsilon}}\overset{}{\rightharpoonup}\mathcal{G}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(O_{jkl}^{-}\setminus E_{\eta}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}:=\partial_{t}q+u\nabla q+\bar{Q}\dive v+\frac{Hg}{\pi R}G^{+}(T)(\dive v)^{+}=0.

Since
\mathcal{G_{\epsilon}}=0\quad a.e\quad O_{jkl}^{+}\setminus E_{\eta}
this means that
\mathcal{G}=0\quad a.e\quad O_{jkl}^{+}\setminus E_{\eta}
. Thanks to Lemma
2.2
we conclude that
\mathcal{G}=0
on
O_{jkl}^{+}
for any positive
(j,k,l)
and therefore it holds on
O^{+}
.

III case

As in the previous case we define

\displaystyle O=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\infty):q-q_{s}=0\right\},\quad O_{kl}=\left\{(x,t)\in B_{k}(0)\times(0,l):q=q_{s},\right\},

\displaystyle O=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{l=1}^{\infty}O_{kl}.

Let us also define

\mathcal{G_{\epsilon}}:=\partial_{t}q_{\epsilon_{n}}+u_{\epsilon_{n}}\nabla q_{\epsilon_{n}}+\bar{Q}\dive v_{\epsilon_{n}}+\frac{H}{\pi}H_{\epsilon_{n}}(q_{\epsilon_{n}}-q_{s})G^{+}(T_{\epsilon_{n}})(\dive v_{\epsilon_{n}})^{+}=0\quad\text{ on }\quad O_{kl}.

By using the convergences in (
7.3
) it is easy to obtain that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G_{\epsilon}}\overset{}{\rightharpoonup}\mathcal{G}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(O_{kl}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}:=\partial_{t}q+u\nabla q+\bar{Q}\dive v+\frac{H}{\pi}h_{q}G^{+}(T)(\dive v)^{+}=0.

Since
\mathcal{G_{\epsilon}}=0\quad a.e\quad O_{kl}
, by using again Lemma
2.2
we deduce that this means that
\mathcal{G}=0\quad a.e\quad O
. Finally, since
h_{q}
is the weak start limit of
H_{\epsilon_{n}}(q_{\epsilon_{n}}-q_{s})\in[0,1]
it also holds that
h_{q}=\alpha\in[0,1]
.

7.2.
Proof of Theorem
4.4
(Uniqueness of global strong solutions)

In this section we establish the uniqueness of strong global solutions to the system (
3.24
). Let
(u_{1},v_{1},T_{1},q_{1})
and
(u_{2},v_{2},T_{2},q_{2})
be two strong solutions of (
3.24
) in the sense of Definition
4.1
, corresponding to the same initial data
(u_{0},v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})
on the time interval
[0,\mathcal{T})
for any
0<\mathcal{T}<\infty
. We define the difference as

(\delta u,\delta v,\delta T,\delta q)=(u_{1},v_{1},T_{1},q_{1})-(u_{2},v_{2},T_{2},q_{2}),

thus we have

(7.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\delta u+u_{1}\nabla\delta u+\delta u\nabla u_{2}-\Delta\delta u+\nabla\delta p+\dive(v_{1}\otimes\delta v)+\dive(\delta v\otimes v_{2})=0\\
&\partial_{t}\delta v+u_{1}\nabla\delta v+\delta u\nabla v_{2}-\Delta\delta v+v_{1}\nabla\delta u+\delta v\nabla u_{2}=\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{g}{\theta_{0}}\nabla\delta T\\
&\dive\delta u=0\\
&\partial_{t}\delta T+u_{1}\nabla\delta T+\delta u\nabla T_{2}-\Delta\delta T-\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{N^{2}\theta_{0}}{g}\dive\delta v=\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{1})^{-}h_{q_{1}}G(T_{1})^{+}\\
&\hskip 241.84842pt-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{2})^{-}h_{q_{2}}G(T_{2})^{+}\\
&\partial_{t}\delta q+u_{1}\nabla\delta q+\delta u\nabla q_{2}+\bar{Q}\dive\delta v=-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{1})^{-}h_{q_{1}}G(T_{1})^{+}\\
&\hskip 170.71652pt+\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{2})^{-}h_{q_{2}}G(T_{2})^{+}.\end{aligned}\right.

We recall the assumption that

(q_{1}-q_{s})(q_{2}-q_{s})\geq 0\quad\text{a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\mathcal{T}),

that is, the quantities
q_{1}-q_{s}
and
q_{2}-q_{s}
possibly have different sign on sets of measure zero.

As usual, we multiply the first two equations in (
7.6
) by
(\delta u,\delta v)
and integrate over
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. After standard manipulations we obtain

(7.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\delta u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\delta v\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\delta u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\|\nabla\delta v\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[(|\nabla u_{2}|+|\nabla v_{2}|+|\nabla v_{1}|+|v_{1}|^{2}+|v_{2}|^{2}](|\delta u|^{2}+|\delta v|^{2})dx+C\|\delta T\|_{2}^{2}.

Regarding the temperature and moisture equations, we decompose the domain
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\infty)
as follows:

\Omega=\sum_{i=1}^{7}\Omega_{i},

where

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}=\left\{q_{1}-q_{s}<0\right\}\cap\left\{q_{2}-q_{s}<0\right\},\qquad\Omega_{2}=\left\{q_{1}-q_{s}<0\right\}\cap\left\{q_{2}-q_{s}=0\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{3}=\left\{q_{1}-q_{s}=0\right\}\cap\left\{q_{2}-q_{s}<0\right\},\qquad\Omega_{4}=\left\{q_{1}-q_{s}=0\right\}\cap\left\{q_{2}-q_{s}=0\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{5}=\left\{q_{1}-q_{s}=0\right\}\cap\left\{q_{2}-q_{s}>0\right\},\qquad\Omega_{6}=\left\{q_{1}-q_{s}>0\right\}\cap\left\{q_{2}-q_{s}=0\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{7}=\left\{q_{1}-q_{s}>0\right\}\cap\left\{q_{2}-q_{s}>0\right\}.

We notice that on the set
\Omega_{1}
since
h_{q_{1}}=h_{q_{2}}=0
the equations for
\delta T,\delta q
satisfy

(7.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta T+u_{1}\nabla\delta T+\delta u\nabla T_{2}-\Delta\delta T-\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{N^{2}\theta_{0}}{g}\dive\delta v=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta q+u_{1}\nabla\delta q+\delta u\nabla q_{2}+\bar{Q}\dive\delta v=0.

On the set
\Omega_{2}
since
q_{2}=q_{s}
then
\partial_{t}q_{2}+u\nabla q_{2}=0
for a.e.
(x,t)
. Thus, from the equation of
q_{2}
we have

\bar{Q}\dive v_{2}=-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{2})^{-}G^{+}(T_{2})\alpha,

where
h_{q_{2}}=\alpha\in(0,1)
. This, in particular, implies that on
\Omega_{2}
,
\dive v_{2}=0
. This type of argument will be applied multiple times in the proof, even if it is not explicitly mentioned each time. Finally, on
\Omega_{2}
the equations for
\delta T,\delta q
satisfy

(7.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta T+u_{1}\nabla\delta T+\delta u\nabla T_{2}-\Delta\delta T-\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{N^{2}\theta_{0}}{g}\dive\delta v=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta q+u_{1}\nabla\delta q+\delta u\nabla q_{2}+\bar{Q}\dive v_{1}=0.

On
\Omega_{3}
we have
\dive v_{1}=0
and

(7.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta T+u_{1}\nabla\delta T+\delta u\nabla T_{2}-\Delta\delta T+\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{N^{2}\theta_{0}}{g}\dive v_{2}=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta q+u_{1}\nabla\delta q+\delta u\nabla q_{2}-\bar{Q}\dive v_{2}=0.

On
\Omega_{4}
we have
\dive v_{1}=0=\dive v_{2}
and

(7.11)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta T+u_{1}\nabla\delta T+\delta u\nabla T_{2}-\Delta\delta T=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta q+u_{1}\nabla\delta q+\delta u\nabla q_{2}=0.

On
\Omega_{5}
we have
\dive v_{1}=0
and

(7.12)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta T+u_{1}\nabla\delta T+\delta u\nabla T_{2}-\Delta\delta T+\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{N^{2}\theta_{0}}{g}\dive v_{2}=-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{2})^{-}G^{+}(T_{2}),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta q+u_{1}\nabla\delta q+\delta u\nabla q_{2}-\bar{Q}\dive v_{2}=\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{2})^{-}G^{+}(T_{2}).

On
\Omega_{6}
we have
\dive v_{2}=0
and

(7.13)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta T+u_{1}\nabla\delta T+\delta u\nabla T_{2}-\Delta\delta T-\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{N^{2}\theta_{0}}{g}\dive v_{1}=\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{1})^{-}G^{+}(T_{1}),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta q+u_{1}\nabla\delta q+\delta u\nabla q_{2}+\bar{Q}\dive v_{1}=-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{1})^{-}G^{+}(T_{1}).

On
\Omega_{7}
we have,

(7.14)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta T+u_{1}\nabla\delta T+\delta u\nabla T_{2}-\Delta\delta T-\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{N^{2}\theta_{0}}{g}\dive\delta v

\displaystyle=\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{1})^{-}G^{+}(T_{1})-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{2})^{-}G^{+}(T_{2}),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta q+u_{1}\nabla\delta q+\delta u\nabla q_{2}+\bar{Q}\dive\delta v=-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{1})^{-}G^{+}(T_{1})+\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{2})^{-}G^{+}(T_{2}).

We fix the following notation,

\delta H:=H_{1}-H_{2}=(\dive v_{1})^{-}G^{+}(T_{1})-(\dive v_{2})^{-}G^{+}(T_{2}).

Therefore, combining (
7.8
) - (
7.14
), we obtain

(7.15)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta T+u_{1}\nabla\delta T+\delta u\nabla T_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{N^{2}\theta_{0}}{g}(\dive\delta v\chi_{\Omega_{1}}+\dive\delta v\chi_{\Omega_{7}})+\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{N^{2}\theta_{0}}{g}(\dive v_{1}\chi_{\Omega_{2}}+\dive v_{1}\chi_{\Omega_{6}})

\displaystyle+\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{N^{2}\theta_{0}}{g}(-\dive v_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{3}}-\dive v_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{5}})-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{2})^{-}G^{+}(T_{2})\chi_{\Omega_{5}}

\displaystyle+\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{1})^{-}G^{+}(T_{1})\chi_{\Omega_{6}}+\frac{Hg}{\pi R}\delta H\chi_{\Omega_{7}}

\displaystyle=\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{N^{2}\theta_{0}}{g}(\dive\delta v-\dive\delta v\chi_{\Omega_{4}})

\displaystyle+\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{N^{2}\theta_{0}}{g}(\dive v_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{2}}-\dive v_{1}\chi_{\Omega_{3}}-\dive v_{1}\chi_{\Omega_{5}}+\dive v_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{6}})

\displaystyle+\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\delta H-\delta H\chi_{\Omega_{1}}-\delta H\chi_{\Omega_{2}}-\delta H\chi_{\Omega_{3}}-\delta H\chi_{\Omega_{4}})

\displaystyle+\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(-H_{1}\chi_{\Omega_{5}}+H_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{6}})

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.

(7.16)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta q+u_{1}\nabla\delta q+\delta u\nabla q_{2}=

\displaystyle-\bar{Q}(\dive\delta v\chi_{\Omega_{1}}+\dive\delta v\chi_{\Omega_{7}}+\dive v_{1}\chi_{\Omega_{2}}+\dive v_{1}\chi_{\Omega_{6}})

\displaystyle+\bar{Q}(\dive v_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{3}}+\dive v_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{5}})+\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{2})^{-}G^{+}(T_{2})\chi_{\Omega_{5}}

\displaystyle-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\dive v_{1})^{-}G^{+}(T_{1})\chi_{\Omega_{6}}-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}\delta H\chi_{\Omega_{7}}

\displaystyle=-\bar{Q}(\dive\delta v-\dive\delta v\chi_{\Omega_{4}})

\displaystyle-\bar{Q}(\dive v_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{2}}-\dive v_{1}\chi_{\Omega_{3}}+\dive v_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{6}}-\dive v_{1}\chi_{\Omega_{5}})

\displaystyle-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(\delta H-\delta H\chi_{\Omega_{1}}-\delta H\chi_{\Omega_{2}}-\delta H\chi_{\Omega_{3}}-\delta H\chi_{\Omega_{4}})

\displaystyle-\frac{Hg}{\pi R}(-H_{1}\chi_{\Omega_{5}}+H_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{6}})

\displaystyle=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4}.

Let us start by multiplying equation (
7.15
) by
\delta T
and integrate over
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

(7.17)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\delta T\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\delta T\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\delta u\nabla T_{2}\delta Tdx+\frac{H}{\pi}\frac{N^{2}\theta_{0}}{g}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\dive\delta v-\dive\delta v\chi_{\Omega_{4}})\delta Tdx

\displaystyle+\frac{Hg}{\pi R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\delta H-\delta H\chi_{\Omega_{1}}-\delta H\chi_{\Omega_{2}}-\delta H\chi_{\Omega_{3}}-\delta H\chi_{\Omega_{4}})\delta Tdx

\displaystyle\leq\|\delta u\|_{\infty}\|\nabla T_{2}\|_{2}\|\delta T\|_{2}+C(1+\|\nabla v_{2}\|_{4}^{4})\|\delta T\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla\delta v\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla\delta T\|_{2}^{2},

since
I_{2}=0
,
H_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{6}}=0
and
H_{1}\chi_{\Omega_{5}}=0
.

Finally, we multiply (
7.16
) by
\delta q
and integrate over
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have

(7.18)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\delta q\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\delta u\|_{\infty}\|\nabla q_{2}\|_{2}\|\delta q\|_{2}+C(1+\|\nabla v_{2}\|_{4}^{4})(\|\delta T\|_{2}^{2}+\|\delta q\|_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla\delta v\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla\delta T\|_{2}^{2}

since
J_{2}=0
,
H_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{6}}=0
and
H_{1}\chi_{\Omega_{5}}=0
. At this stage, by combining (
7.7
), (
7.17
), and (
7.18
), and proceeding as in Subsection
6.2
, we can apply Lemma

to conclude the proof.
