1.
The product and sum intersection sets

Let
\mathbf{N}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
be the set of positive integers.
The letter
h
will always denote a positive integer.

Let
S
be a semigroup, written multiplicatively and not necessarily abelian,
with subsets
A_{1},\ldots,A_{h}
.
The
product set
of these subsets is the set

A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{h}=\{a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{h}:a_{i}\in A_{i}\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,h\}.

If
A_{i}=A
for all
i=1,\ldots,h
, then the
h
-fold product set
is

A^{h}=\{a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{h}:a_{i}\in A\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,h\}.

If the semigroup
S
is abelian and written additively, then we have the
sumset

A_{1}+A_{2}+\cdots+A_{h}=\{a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{h}:a_{i}\in A_{i}\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,h\}

and the
h
-fold sumset

hA=\{a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{h}:a_{i}\in A\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,h\}.

Let
Q
be an infinite set and let
(A_{q})_{q\in Q}
be a family
of subsets of the semigroup
S
such the the set of sets
\{A_{q}:q\in Q\}
is infinite.
The set
Q
is called an
index set
.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{Q}(S)
be the set of all infinite families
(A_{q})_{q\in Q}

of subsets of
S
indexed by
Q
.
For all
(A_{q})_{q\in Q}\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}(S)
, we define

A=\bigcap_{q\in Q}A_{q}.

For multiplicative semigroups, we have the multiplicative inclusions

A^{h}\subseteq A_{q}^{h}
for all
q\in Q
and
h\in\mathbf{N}
and so

(1)

A^{h}\subseteq\bigcap_{q\in Q}A_{q}^{h}.

For additive abelian semigroups, we have the additive inclusions
hA\subseteq hA_{q}

for all
q\in Q
and
h\in\mathbf{N}
and so

(2)

hA\subseteq\bigcap_{q\in Q}hA_{q}.

For some families
(A_{q})_{q\in Q}
, the
relations (
1
) and (
2
)
are equalities, and for other sequences the inclusions are proper inclusions.
For a multiplicative semigroup
S
and index set
Q
,
we define the
product intersection set

H(A_{q})=H_{Q}^{S}(A_{q})=\left\{h\in\mathbf{N}:A^{h}=\bigcap_{q\in Q}A_{q}^{h}\right\}.

For an additive semigroup
S
and index set
Q
,
we define the
sum intersection set

H(A_{q})=H_{Q}^{S}(A_{q})=\left\{h\in\mathbf{N}:hA=\bigcap_{q\in Q}hA_{q}\right\}.

We have
1\in H(A_{q})
for all
(A_{q})_{q\in Q}\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}(S)
.

There are many unsolved problems about the intersections
of
h
-fold product sets of subsets
of a semigroup
S
with respect to an index set
Q
.

Problem 1
.

For a given family
(A_{q})_{q\in Q}\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}(S)
, compute
H(A_{q})
.

Problem 2
.

For a given subset
A
of
S
, determine the set

\mathcal{H}_{Q}(A)=\left\{H(A_{q}):(A_{q})_{q\in Q}\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}(S)\text{ and }A=\bigcap_{q\in Q}A_{q}\right\}.

For example, the problem
\mathcal{H}_{Q}(\emptyset)
is to determine all subsets
X
of
\mathbf{N}
such that
there exists a family
(A_{q})_{q\in Q}\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}(S)
with
\bigcap_{q\in Q}A_{q}=\emptyset

such that
\bigcap_{q\in Q}A_{q}^{h}=\emptyset
if and only if
h\in X
.

Problem 3
.

Determine the set

\mathbf{H}_{Q}(S)=\bigcup_{A\subseteq S}\mathcal{H}_{Q}(A)=\left\{H(A_{q}):(A_{q})_{q\in Q}\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}(S)\right\}.

The set
\mathbf{H}_{Q}(S)
is the “master set” of all product intersection sets
H(A_{q})

for all families
(A_{q})_{q\in Q}\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}(S)
.
The following is a special case of Problem
3
.

Problem 4
.

Is there a set
X
of positive integers with
1\in X
such that
X\notin\mathbf{H}_{Q}(S_{0})

for the semigroup
S_{0}
?

Is there a set
X
of positive integers with
1\in X
such that
X\notin\mathbf{H}_{Q}(S)

for all semigroups
S
?

Problem
4
asks if there exists a set
X
of positive integers with
1\in X

such that there exists no family
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}(S)

with
\bigcap_{q\in Q}A_{q}=A
such that
A^{h}=\bigcap_{q\in Q}A_{q}^{h}
if and only if
h\in X
.

Replacing
h
-fold product sets with
h
-fold sumsets, we have the analogous problems
for additive abelian semigroups.
Product intersection sets and sum intersection sets are of special interest in number theory
when investigated for the additive and multiplicative semigroups
\mathbf{N}
and
\mathbf{Z}
.

The purpose of this paper is to prove basic results about intersection sets and to construct
explicit examples of families of subsets of a semigroup with interesting intersection properties.

2.
Intersections of sequences of sets

We often consider intersection sets with respect to the index set
Q=\mathbf{N}

and sequences of subsets
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
.
A sequence
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
of subsets of a set
S
is
decreasing

if
A_{q}\supseteq A_{q+1}
for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
and

strictly decreasing

if
A_{q}\supseteq A_{q+1}
and
A_{q}\neq A_{q+1}
for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
.
A decreasing sequence
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
is

asymptotically strictly decreasing

if
A_{q}\neq A_{q+1}
for infinitely many
q\in\mathbf{N}
.
A decreasing sequence is asymptotically strictly decreasing if and only if it is not eventually constant.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{N}}^{*}(S)
be the set of all asymptotically strictly decreasing sequences

(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
of subsets of
S
.

Problem 5
.

For a given subset
A
of
S
, determine the set

\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{N}}^{*}(A)=\left\{H(A_{q}):(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{N}}^{*}(S)\text{ and }A=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}\right\}.

Problem 6
.

Determine the set

\mathbf{H}^{*}_{\mathbf{N}}(S)=\bigcup_{A\subseteq S}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{N}}^{*}(A)=\left\{H(A_{q}):(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{N}}^{*}(S)\right\}.

Problem 7
.

Is there a set
X
of positive integers with
1\in X
such that
X\notin\mathbf{H}^{*}_{\mathbf{N}}(S_{0})

for the semigroup
S_{0}
?

Is there a set
X
of positive integers with
1\in X
such that
X\notin\mathbf{H}^{*}_{\mathbf{N}}(S)

for all semigroups
S
?

A subset
A
of a set
S
is
co-finite
if the complement of
A
in
S
is finite
and
co-infinite
if the complement of
A
in
S
is infinite.

If the sequence
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
is strictly decreasing and
A=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}
,
then, for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
, we have

S\setminus A\supseteq A_{1}\setminus A\supseteq A_{1}\setminus A_{q+1}=\bigcup_{r=1}^{q}A_{r}\setminus A_{r+1}.

Because the sets
A_{r}\setminus A_{r+1}
are pairwise disjoint and nonempty, we have

\left|S\setminus A\right|\geq\left|\bigcup_{r=1}^{q}A_{r}\setminus A_{r+1}\right|=\sum_{r=1}^{q}\left|A_{r}\setminus A_{r+1}\right|\geq q

for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
and so
A
is a co-infinite subset of
S
.
Every asymptotically strictly decreasing sequence
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
contains a strictly decreasing subsequence and so
A=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}
is also a co-infinite subset of
S
.

For many families
(A_{q})_{q\in Q}\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}(S)
, we have
H(A_{q})=\{1\}

or
H(A_{q})=\mathbf{N}
.
For example, if
S=\mathbf{N}
and
Q=\mathbf{N}
, then the sequence of sets

(A^{\sharp}_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
defined by

A^{\sharp}_{q}=\{r\in\mathbf{N}:r\geq q\}

satisfies

A=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A^{\sharp}_{q}=\emptyset

and

hA=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA^{\sharp}_{q}=\emptyset

for all
h\in\mathbf{N}
and so
H(A^{\sharp}_{q})=\mathbf{N}
.

If
S=\mathbf{Z}
and
Q=\mathbf{N}
, then the sequence of sets

(A^{\sharp}_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
defined by

A^{\sharp}_{q}=\{r\in\mathbf{Z}:|r|\geq q\}

satisfies

A=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A^{\sharp}_{q}=\emptyset.

By Theorem
4
,

hA=\emptyset\neq\mathbf{Z}=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA^{\sharp}_{q}

for all
h\geq 2
and so
H(A^{\sharp}_{q})=\{1\}
.

Sum and product intersection sets other than
\mathbf{N}
and
\{1\}
have been constructed.

Theorem 1

(Marques and Nathanson
[
1
]
)
.

In the additive semigroup
\mathbf{Z}
, for every positive integer
h_{0}
,
there exists
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{N}}^{*}(\mathbf{Z})
such that

H(A_{q})=\{1\}\cup\{h\in\mathbf{N}:h\geq h_{0}\}.

For every integer
d\geq 2
, there exists
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{N}}^{*}(\mathbf{Z})

such that

H(A_{q})=\{h\in\mathbf{N}:h\not\equiv 0\pmod{d}\}.

Very little is known about the arithmetic structure of product and sum intersection sets.

We often use the following simple relation.

Lemma 1
.

Let
S
be a set, let
A
and
B
be subsets of
S
,
and let
(B_{q})_{q\in Q}
be a family of subsets of
S
. If

\bigcap_{q\in Q}B_{q}=B

then

\bigcap_{q\in Q}(A\cup B_{q})=A\cup B

and

\bigcap_{q\in Q}(A\cap B_{q})=A\cap B.

Proof.

Klar.

3.
Representation functions and intersection sets

Let
S
be a semigroup and let
A
be a subset of
S
.
For all
x\in S
, we define the

h
-fold representation function

r_{A,h}(x)=\card\left\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A^{h}:a_{1}\cdots a_{h}=x\right\}.

If
A\subseteq B
, then
r_{A,h}(x)\leq r_{B,h}(x)
for all
x\in S
.
We have

r_{A,1}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $x\in A$}\\
0&\text{if $x\notin A$}\end{cases}

and
r_{A,h}(x)\geq 1
if and only if
x\in A^{h}
.

Let
\mathbf{Z}^{d}
be the additive group of vectors in
\mathbf{R}^{d}
with integral coordinates,
that is, the group of
d
-dimensional lattice points.
Let
\mathbf{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
be the set of nonnegative integers
and
\mathbf{N}_{0}^{d}
the additive semigroup of
d
-dimensional lattice points with nonnegative integral coordinates.

Lemma 2
.

(1)

In the multiplicative semigroup
\mathbf{Z}^{*}=\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
,

r_{\mathbf{Z}^{*},h}(x)<\infty\qquad\text{ for all $h\geq 1$ and $x\in\mathbf{Z}^{*}$. }

(2)

In the additive semigroup
\mathbf{N}_{0}^{d}
,

r_{\mathbf{N}^{d}_{0},h}(x)<\infty\qquad\text{ for all $h\geq 1$ and $x\in\mathbf{N}_{0}^{d}$.}

(3)

In the additive semigroup
\mathbf{Z}^{d}
,

r_{\mathbf{Z}^{d},h}(x)=\infty\qquad\text{ for all $h\geq 2$ and $x\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}$.}

Proof.

(1) If
x
is a nonzero integer and and if
a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}
are nonzero integers
such that
x=a_{1}\cdots a_{h}
, then each integer
a_{i}
is a divisor of
x
and
every nonzero integer
x
has only finitely many positive or negative divisors.
It follows that
r_{\mathbf{Z}^{*},h}(x)<\infty
.

(2) If
x=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d})
is a nonnegative lattice point and if
a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}
are lattice points
such that
x=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h}
, then each summand
a_{i}
is in the finite hypercube

\left\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})\in\mathbf{N}_{0}^{d}:0\leq t_{i}\leq u_{i}\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,d\right\}

and so
r_{\mathbf{N}_{0},h}(x)<\infty
.

(3) For all lattice points
x
and
y
in
\mathbf{Z}^{d}
, we have

x=y+(x-y)+\underbrace{0+\cdots+0}_{\text{$h-2$ summands}}

and so
r_{\mathbf{Z}^{d},h}(x)=\infty
.
This completes the proof.
∎

The following result establishes a link between representation functions in number theory
and intersection sets in semigroups.

Theorem 2
.

Let
S
be a semigroup and let
A
be a subset of
S
.
Let
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
be a decreasing sequence of subsets of
S
such that

A=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}.

If
h\in\mathbf{N}
and
r_{A_{1},h}(x)<\infty
for all
x\in A_{1}
, then
h\in H(A_{q})
.

If
r_{A_{1},h}(x)<\infty
for all
h\in\mathbf{N}
and
x\in A_{1}
, then
H(A_{q})=\mathbf{N}
.

Proof.

For all
h\in\mathbf{N}
, we have
A^{h}\subseteq\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}^{h}
.
Suppose that, for some
h\in\mathbf{N}
, we have
r_{A_{1},h}(x)<\infty
for all
x\in A_{1}
.
We must prove that
\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}^{h}\subseteq A^{h}
.

If
x\in\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}^{h}
, then, for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
, we have
x\in A_{q}^{h}

and so there is an
h
-tuple
(a_{1,q},\ldots,a_{h,q})
with
a_{i,q}\in A_{q}\subseteq A_{1}

for all
i=1,\ldots,h

such that

x=a_{1,q}\cdots a_{h,q}.

Because
r_{A_{1},h}(x)<\infty
, there is an
h
-tuple
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{h})

with
b_{i}\in A_{1}
for all
i=1,\ldots,h
and there is an infinite strictly increasing
sequence of positive integers
(q_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
such that

(a_{1,q_{j}},\ldots,a_{h,q_{j}})=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{h})

for all
j=1,2,3,\ldots
.
For all
q\in Q
, there exists
q_{j}
such that
q\leq q_{j}
.
Because the sequence of sets
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
is decreasing, we have

A_{q_{j}}\subseteq A_{q}
and so

(b_{1},\ldots,b_{h})=(a_{1,q_{j}},\ldots,a_{h,q_{j}})\in A_{q_{j}}\times\cdots\times A_{q_{j}}\subseteq A_{q}\times\cdots\times A_{q}.

It follows that, for all
i=1,\ldots,h
and
q\in Q
, we have
b_{i}\in A_{q}
and so

b_{i}\in\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}=A
. Therefore,
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{h})\in A\times\cdots\times A

and
x=b_{1}\cdots b_{h}\in A^{h}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3
.

If
S
is a semigroup such that
r_{S,h}(x)<\infty
for all
h\in\mathbf{N}
and
x\in S
, then

\mathbf{H}^{*}_{\mathbf{N}}(S)=\{\mathbf{N}\}.

In particular, for the multiplicative semigroups
\mathbf{N}
and
\mathbf{Z}^{*}

and for the additive semigroup
\mathbf{N}_{0}^{d}
,

\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{N})=\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{Z}^{*})=\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{N}_{0}^{d})=\{\mathbf{N}\}.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
2

and Theorem
2
.
∎

Problem 8
.

Determine the semigroups
S
such that

(1)

for all
h\geq h_{0}
, there exists
x\in S
with
r_{S,h}(x)=\infty
, and

(2)

\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{N}}^{*}(S)=\{\mathbf{N}\}
.

The subset
A
of a semigroup
S
is a
basis of order
h
for
S

if
A^{h}=S
and a
basis of exact order
h_{0}
for
S
if
h_{0}
is the smallest
integer such that
A^{h_{0}}=S
.
The subset
A
is a
nonbasis of order
h
for
S

if
A^{h}\neq S
and a
nonbasis
for
S
if
A^{h}\neq S
for all
h=1,2,3,\ldots
.

Let
S
be a semigroup that contains an identity.
If
A
is a basis of order
h_{0}
for
S
, then
A
is a basis of order
h
for
S

for all
h\geq h_{0}
. If
A
is a nonbasis of order
h_{0}
for
S
, then
A
is a nonbasis
of order
h
for
S
for all
h\leq h_{0}
.

Additive bases and nonbases are described similarly.
For example, the set
A=\{0\}\cup\{\pm 2^{i}:i\in\mathbf{N}_{0}\}
is a nonbasis
for the additive abelian semigroup
\mathbf{Z}
.

By Theorem
3
, if
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
is any
asymptotically strictly decreasing sequence of sets of nonnegative integers, then

H(A_{q})=\mathbf{N}
, that is,

hA=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}
for all
h\in\mathbf{N}
.
This is not true for sets that contain both positive and negative integers
nor for sets of nonnegative rational numbers.
The following results construct examples of such sets.

Theorem 4
.

In the additive group
\mathbf{Z}
, for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
, let

A^{\sharp}_{q}=\{r\in\mathbf{Z}:|r|\geq q\}.

The sequence
(A^{\sharp}_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
is strictly decreasing and

\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A^{\sharp}_{q}=\emptyset.

For all
h\geq 2
,

\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA^{\sharp}_{q}=\mathbf{Z}

and
H\left(A^{\sharp}_{q}\right)=\{1\}
.

Let
A
be a co-infinite set of integers. For all
q\in\mathbf{N}
, let

A_{q}=A\cup A_{q}^{\sharp}.

The sequence
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
is asymptotically strictly decreasing and

\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}=A.

If
A
is a basis of order
h
for
\mathbf{Z}
, then
h\in H(A_{q})
.
If
A
is a basis of exact order
h_{0}
for
\mathbf{Z}
, then

H(A_{q})=\{1\}\cup\{h\in\mathbf{N}_{0}:h\geq h_{0}\}.

If
A
is a nonbasis of order
h_{0}
for
\mathbf{Z}
, then

h\notin H(A_{q})\qquad\text{ for all $h=2,3,\ldots,h_{0}$.}

If
A
is a nonbasis for
\mathbf{Z}
, then
H(A_{q})=\{1\}
.

Proof.

The sequence of sets
\left(A^{\sharp}_{q}\right)_{q=1}^{\infty}
is strictly decreasing and

A^{\sharp}=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A^{\sharp}_{q}=\emptyset.

It follows that

(3)

hA^{\sharp}=\emptyset

for all
h\in\mathbf{N}
.

Let
h\geq 2
. For all
n\in\mathbf{N}_{0}
and
q\in\mathbf{N}
, we have

\pm(n+q)\in A^{\sharp}_{q}

and

\pm(h-1)q\in A^{\sharp}_{q}.

It follows that

n=(n+q)+\underbrace{q+\cdots+q}_{\text{$h-2$ summands}}+(-(h-1)q)\in hA^{\sharp}_{q}

and

-n=-(n+q)+\underbrace{(-q)+\cdots+(-q)}_{\text{$h-2$ summands}}+((h-1)q)\in hA^{\sharp}_{q}

and so
hA^{\sharp}_{q}=\mathbf{Z}
for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
. Therefore,

(4)

\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA^{\sharp}_{q}=\mathbf{Z}.

From (
3
) and (
4
), we obtain

hA^{\sharp}\neq\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA^{\sharp}_{q}

for all
h\geq 2
and so
H\left(A_{q}^{\sharp}\right)=\{1\}
.

Because the set
A_{q}
is co-finite and the set
A
is co-infinite, the set
A_{q}\setminus A

is infinite for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
. It follows that, for all
q\in Q
,
there exists

s\in A_{q}\setminus A\subseteq A_{q}.

We have
|s|\geq q
and so
|s|+1>q
. Therefore,

s\notin A_{|s|+1},\qquad s\in A_{q},\qqand A_{|s|+1}\neq A_{q}

and so the sequence
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
is asymptotically strictly decreasing.

For all
q\in\mathbf{N}
, we have
A\subseteq A_{q}
and so

A\subseteq\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}
.
Let
x\notin A
. For all
q>|x|
, we have
x\notin A^{\sharp}_{q}
and so
x\notin A\cup A^{\sharp}_{q}=A_{q}
.
Therefore,

x\notin\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}

and

A=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}.

For all
h\geq 2
, we have

\mathbf{Z}=hA_{q}^{\sharp}\subseteq hA_{q}\subseteq\mathbf{Z}
and so

\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}=\mathbf{Z}.

If
A
is a basis of order
h
for
\mathbf{Z}
, then

hA=\mathbf{Z}=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}
and
h\in H(A_{q})
.
If
A
is a basis of exact order
h_{0}
, then

hA=\mathbf{Z}=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}
if and only if
h\geq h_{0}
and

H(A_{q})=\{1\}\cup\{h\in\mathbf{N}:h\geq h_{0}\}.

If
A
is a nonbasis of order
h_{0}
for
\mathbf{Z}
, then, for all
h=2,\ldots,h_{0}
,
we have
hA\neq\mathbf{Z}=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}

and
h\notin H(A_{q})
.

If
A
is not a basis for
\mathbf{Z}
, then

Every infinite abelian group contains an infinite proper subgroup.

The usual Euclidean length of a vector

in
\mathbf{R}^{d}

\|x_{i}\|=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{i,j}^{2}}
.
The vector

is

for all

If

are nonzero vectors such that

Lemma 5
.

If

are nonnzero nonnegative vectors in

Proof.

for

The coordinates

are nonnegative, and so

\displaystyle\|x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i,j}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i,j}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{i,j}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|x_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq k\min(\|x_{1}\|,\ldots,\|x_{k}\|)^{2}

For all
q\in\mathbf{N}
, let

A_{q}^{\sharp}=\{x\in\mathbf{N}_{0}^{d}:\|x\|\geq 2qm^{*}\}

and

A_{q}=A\cup A_{q}^{\sharp}=A\cup\{x\in\mathbf{N}_{0}^{d}:\|x\|\geq 2qm^{*}\}.

The sequences
(A_{q}^{\sharp})_{q=1}^{\infty}
and
(A_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
are
strictly decreasing, and
A\cap A_{q}^{\sharp}=\emptyset
for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
.
We have

\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}^{\sharp}=\emptyset

and so

\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}A_{q}=A.

We shall prove that

(10)

hA=\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}

for all
h\in\mathbf{N}
.

We have
hA\subseteq\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}
.
If
hA\neq\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}
, then there is a lattice point

x\in\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}hA_{q}
such that
x\notin hA
.
It follows that, for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
, there exist

k\in\{1,2,\ldots,h\}
and lattice points

a_{1,q},\ldots,a_{k,q}\in A_{q}\setminus A=A_{q}^{\sharp}

and lattice points
a_{1},\ldots,a_{h-k}\in A
such that

x=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i,q}+\sum_{i=1}^{h-k}a_{i}.

The lattice points
a_{i,q}
are nonnegative and nonzero.
Applying Lemma
5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|x-\sum_{i=1}^{h-k}a_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i,q}\right\|

\displaystyle>\min\left(\|a_{1,q}\|,\ldots,\|a_{k,q}\|\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2qm^{*}.

We also have

\displaystyle\left\|x-\sum_{i=1}^{h-k}a_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|+\sum_{i=1}^{h-k}\left\|a_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle<\|x\|+hm^{*}

and so

2qm^{*}<\|x\|+hm^{*}

for all
q\in\mathbf{N}
, which, because
m^{*}>0
, is absurd.
This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Intersections of product intersection sets

In this section we prove a global intersection relation for product intersection sets.

Lemma 6
.

Let
A
be a subset of a semigroup
S_{1}
and
B
a subset of a semigroup
S_{2}
.
Then
A\times B
is a subset of the product semigroup
S_{1}\times S_{2}
and,
for all
h\in\mathbf{N}
,

A^{h}\times B^{h}=(A\times B)^{h}.

Proof.

If
(x,y)\in A^{h}\times B^{h}
, then
x\in A^{h}
and
y\in B^{h}
and so there exist

a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}\in A
and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{h}\in B
such that

x=a_{1}\cdots a_{h}
and
y=b_{1}\cdots b_{h}
.
We have
(a_{i},b_{i})\in A\times B
for
i=1,\ldots,h
and

(x,y)=(a_{1},b_{1})\cdots(a_{h},b_{h})\in(A\times B)^{h}.

Thus,

A^{h}\times B^{h}\subseteq(A\times B)^{h}.

Conversely, if
(x,y)\in(A\times B)^{h}
, then there exist

(a_{1},b_{1}),\ldots,(a_{h},b_{h})\in A\times B
such that

(x,y)=(a_{1},b_{1})\cdots(a_{h},b_{h})=(a_{1}\cdots a_{h},b_{1}\cdots b_{h}).

We have
x=a_{1}\cdots a_{h}\in A^{h}
and
y=b_{1}\cdots b_{h}\in B^{h}

and so
(x,y)\in A^{h}\times B^{h}
and

(A\times B)^{h}\subseteq A^{h}\times B^{h}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 14
.

Let
\mathbf{Semi}
be the class of all semigroups.
For every index set
Q
, let

\mathbf{H}_{Q}=\bigcup_{S\in\mathbf{Semi}}\mathbf{H}_{Q}(S)=\bigcup_{S\in\mathbf{Semi}}\ \bigcup_{(A_{q})\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}(S)}H^{S}_{Q}(A_{q})

be the set of all product intersection sets
H_{Q}(S)

for all semigroups
S
. Let

\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{N}}^{*}=\bigcup_{S\in\mathbf{Semi}}\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{N}}^{*}(S).

The sets
\mathbf{H}_{Q}
and
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{N}}^{*}
are closed under intersection.

Proof.

Let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
be semigroups and let
S_{1}\times S_{2}
be the product semigroup.
For all
(A_{q})_{q\in Q}\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}(S_{1})
and
(B_{q})_{q\in Q}\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}(S_{2})
, we shall construct

(C_{q})_{q\in Q}\in\mathcal{F}_{Q}(S_{1}\times S_{2})
such that

H^{S_{1}}_{Q}(A_{q})\cap H^{S_{2}}_{Q}(B_{q})=H^{S_{1}\times S_{2}}_{Q}(C_{q}).

Let
C_{q}=A_{q}\times B_{q}\in S_{1}\times S_{2}
for all
q\in Q
. If

A=\bigcap_{q\in Q}A_{q}\qqand B=\bigcap_{q\in Q}B_{q}

then

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{q\in Q}C_{q}=\bigcap_{q\in Q}(A_{q}\times B_{q})

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{q\in Q}A_{q}\times\bigcap_{q\in Q}B_{q}=A\times B.

If
h\in H^{S_{1}\times S_{2}}_{Q}(C_{q})
, then

C^{h}=\bigcap_{q\in Q}C_{q}^{h}.

By Lemma
6
,

\displaystyle A^{h}\times B^{h}

\displaystyle=(A\times B)^{h}=C^{h}=\bigcap_{q\in Q}C_{q}^{h}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{q\in Q}(A_{q}\times B_{q})^{h}=\bigcap_{q\in Q}\left(A_{q}^{h}\times B_{q}^{h}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{q\in Q}A_{q}^{h}\times\bigcap_{q\in Q}B_{q}^{h}

and so

A^{h}=\bigcap_{q\in Q}A_{q}^{h}\qqand B^{h}=\bigcap_{q\in Q}B_{q}^{h}.

It follows that
h\in H^{S_{1}}_{Q}(A_{q})
and
h\in H^{S_{2}}_{Q}(B_{q})

and so
h\in H^{S_{1}}_{Q}(A_{q})\cap H^{S_{2}}_{Q}(B_{q})
and

H^{S_{1}\times S_{2}}_{Q}(C_{q})\subseteq H^{S_{1}}_{Q}(A_{q})\cap H^{S_{2}}_{Q}(B_{q}).

Conversely, if
h\in H^{S_{1}}_{Q}(A_{q})\cap H^{S_{2}}_{Q}(B_{q})
,
then

A^{h}=\bigcap_{q\in Q}A_{q}^{h}\qqand B^{h}=\bigcap_{q\in Q}B_{q}^{h}.

By Lemma
6
,

\displaystyle C^{h}

\displaystyle=(A\times B)^{h}=A^{h}\times B^{h}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{q\in Q}A_{q}^{h}\times\bigcap_{q\in Q}B_{q}^{h}=\bigcap_{q\in Q}\left(A_{q}^{h}\times\ B_{q}^{h}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{q\in Q}\left(A_{q}\times\ B_{q}\right)^{h}=\bigcap_{q\in Q}C_{q}^{h}

and
h\in H^{S_{1}\times S_{2}}_{Q}(C_{q})
.
Thus,

H^{S_{1}}_{Q}(A_{q})\cap H^{S_{2}}_{Q}(B_{q})\subseteq H^{S_{1}\times S_{2}}_{Q}(C_{q}).

It follows that
H^{S_{1}}_{Q}(A_{q})\cap H^{S_{2}}_{Q}(B_{q})=H^{S_{1}\times S_{2}}_{Q}(C_{q})

and so
\mathbf{H}_{Q}

is closed under intersection.

To prove that
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{N}}^{*}
is closed under intersection, it suffices to observe

and
(B_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
are asymptotically strictly

(C_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}=(A_{q}\times B_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}

is also asymptotically strictly decreasing.
This completes the proof.
∎

6.
R
-module transformations

Let
R
be a ring, not necessarily commutative, and let
R^{\times}
be the group of units of
R
.
Let
M
be an
R
-module and let
A
and
A^{\prime}
be subsets of
M
.
We write
A\sim A^{\prime}
if there exist

\varepsilon\in R^{\times}
and
t\in M
such that

A^{\prime}=\varepsilon A+t=\{\varepsilon a+t:a\in A\}.

For example, in
\mathbf{Z}
, we have
A=\{-7,-5,-4\}\sim A^{\prime}=\{0,1,3\}

because, with
\varepsilon=-1
and
t=-4
, there is the relation

\varepsilon A+t=(-1)A-4=\{0,1,3\}=A^{\prime}
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module and let
(A_{q})_{q\in Q}
and
(A^{\prime}_{q})_{q\in Q}

be families of subsets of
M
. We write
(A_{q})_{q\in Q}\sim(A^{\prime}_{q})_{q\in Q}

if there exist
\varepsilon\in R^{\times}
and
t\in M
such that

A^{\prime}_{q}=\varepsilon A_{q}+t
for all
q\in Q
.

Lemma 7
.

The relation
\sim
is an equivalence relation on the set of subsets of a
R
-module
M
.

Proof.

Choosing
\varepsilon=1
and
t=0
proves
A\sim A
for all
A\subseteq M
.

Let
A
and
A^{\prime}
be subsets of
M
such that
A\sim A^{\prime}
.
There exist
\varepsilon\in R^{\times}
and
t\in M
such that

A^{\prime}=\varepsilon A+t
.
Then
\varepsilon^{-1}\in R^{\times}
and
-\varepsilon^{-1}t\in M
and

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}A^{\prime}-\varepsilon^{-1}t

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-1}(\varepsilon A+t)-\varepsilon^{-1}t

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-1}\varepsilon A+\varepsilon^{-1}t-\varepsilon^{-1}t

\displaystyle=A

and so
A^{\prime}\sim A
.

Let
A
,
A^{\prime}
, and
A^{\prime\prime}
be subsets of
M
such that
A\sim A^{\prime}=\varepsilon A+t

and
A^{\prime}\sim A^{\prime\prime}=\varepsilon^{\prime}A^{\prime}+t^{\prime}
for some
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\in R^{\times}

and
t,t^{\prime}\in M
. Then

\displaystyle A^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{\prime}A^{\prime}+t^{\prime}=\varepsilon^{\prime}(\varepsilon A+t)+t^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{\prime}\varepsilon A+\varepsilon^{\prime}t+t^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}A+t^{\prime\prime}

with
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}=\varepsilon^{\prime}\varepsilon\in R^{\times}
and

t^{\prime\prime}=\varepsilon^{\prime}t+t^{\prime}\in M
, and so
A\sim A^{\prime\prime}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 15
.

Let
M
be a
R
-module and let
(A_{q})_{q\in Q}
and
(A^{\prime}_{q})_{q\in Q}
be families of subsets
of

By Lemma

and

If
