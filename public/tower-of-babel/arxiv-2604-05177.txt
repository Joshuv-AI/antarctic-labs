The Best constant in the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality for the mixed local and nonlocal Laplacian


Abstract
. In this paper, we establish the best constant of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality for the mixed local and nonlocal Laplacian. In the breakthrough paper of Weinstein [ 45 ] , he proved the best constant by establishing the relationship between the optimizers of the best constant and the ground state solutions of the underlying equation. To achieve this goal, the Pohožaev identity plays a key role. In our problem, due to the appearance of a local and a nonlocal operator and the loss of the L 2 L^{2} term, it is hard to get the regularity result. It doesn’t seem reasonable to think that all the weak solutions of equation satisfy Pohožaev identity. We overcome this difficulty by using the Nehari-Pohožaev manifold method. We not only prove that the optimizer of the best constant is the ground state solution of the equation, but we also establish the best constant.

1.1.
Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities: Historical background

In 1914, Landau
[
32
]
showed the inequality

\displaystyle\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant C\|u^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In 1949, Kolmogoroff

[
30
]
extended it for higher-order derivatives.
In 1957, Stein

[
40
]

improved Landau’s result for
p\in[1,\infty)
as

\displaystyle\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant C\|u^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In 1958, Nash
[
36
]
and Ladyzenskaja
[
31
]
independently proved, for
N=2
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leqslant C\|u\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In 1959, Gagliardo
[
28
]
and Nirenberg
[
37
]
independently showed the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\displaystyle\|u\|_{D^{j,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leqslant C\|u\|_{D^{k,r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{1-\theta},

where

\displaystyle\begin{cases}0\leqslant j<k,\penalty\ \penalty\ j,k\in\mathbb{N},\\
\theta=\frac{j}{k},\\
\frac{k}{p}=\frac{j}{r}+\frac{k-j}{q}.\end{cases}

In 2018, Brézis-Mironescu
[
9
]
improved the Gagliardo-Nirenberg’s result for
j,k\geqslant 0
.
For more related works, we refer the reader to

[
10
,
25
,
20
,
16
,
1
,

1.5.
Our main results

Based on the results in subsections 1.2-1.4, we know that the uniqueness of the ground state and the Pohožaev identity play key roles in the proof of the best constant.
In our case, we are facing the following challenges:

(i)

In

[
24
,
41
,
42
]
,
the authors showed the regularity results for mixed operators type equation.
However, due to the loss of
L^{2}
term in equation (

P

it is hard to apply their method to get the regularity result.
Without that piece of information, we cannot tell whether the weak solutions of equation (

(ii)

For equation (

P

),
it should be remarked that the delicate issue of uniqueness of solutions (modulo symmetries) as well as the non-degeneracy of the associated linearized operator are two open challenging questions.

(iii)

It is hard to establish the relationship between the optimizer of the best constant
and the ground state solution of equation (

We will overcome these challenges thanks to an innovative approach that we will describe later. Let us first state our first result:

2.
Sobolev Space and Lieb’s translation theorem

The homogeneous Sobolev space

\displaystyle D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\{u\in L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})||\nabla u|\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\},

its semi-norm is taken as

\displaystyle\|u\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

The homogeneous fractional Sobolev space
D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
under the semi-norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

The mixed Sobolev space
E
is defined by the completion of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
under the semi-norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{E}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

Lemma 2.1
.

E\hookrightarrow D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

E\hookrightarrow D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\leqslant\|u\|_{E}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\leqslant\|u\|_{E}^{2}.

Therefore
E\hookrightarrow D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

E\hookrightarrow D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

E\hookrightarrow L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
t\in[\frac{2N}{N-2s},\frac{2N}{N-2}]
.

Proof.

Using Hölder’s inequality, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{t}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2s}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{(N-2s)[2N-t(N-2)]}{4N(1-s)}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{(N-2)[t(N-2s)-2N]}{4N(1-s)}}.

From Lemma
2.1
, we know that:

\displaystyle C_{N,p,s}^{-1}=\left(\frac{2N-p(N-2)}{2p(1-s)}\right)^{\frac{2N-p(N-2)}{4(1-s)}}\left(\frac{p(N-2s)-2N}{2p(1-s)}\right)^{\frac{p(N-2s)-2N}{4(1-s)}}\left[\frac{2p}{p-2}c\right]^{\frac{p-2}{2}}.

∎
