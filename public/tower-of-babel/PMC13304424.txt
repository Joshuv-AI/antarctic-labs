Computational analysis of thermally reactive MHD thixotropic hybrid nanofluid flow under the influence of natural convection past a stretching surface
Authors: Ibrahim Mahariq, Medhat M Helal, Muhammad Shaheen, Mehreen Fiza, Hakeem Ullah, Ali Akgül, Farkhod Rakhmonov, Ilyas Khan, Abdoalrahman S A Omer, Seham M Al-Mekhlafi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigates the steady two-dimensional natural-convection flow and heat and mass transfer of a Cu–Al2O3/water thixotropic hybrid nanofluid over a vertically stretching sheet. The boundary-layer model incorporates magnetic effects, thermal radiation, heat generation, viscous dissipation, Joule heating, melting, chemical reaction, species diffusion, and motile microorganisms. By applying suitable similarity transformations, the governing partial differential equations are reduced to a system of nonlinear ordinary differential equations and solved numerically using MATLAB bvp4c. The results show that the magnetic field suppresses the velocity profile, whereas melting enhances it. Temperature increases with the heat source parameter but decreases with radiation and melting, while nanoparticle concentration declines with higher Schmidt number and chemical reaction. In addition, microorganism concentration decreases with the bioconvection Lewis number and increases with the Peclet number and microorganism concentration difference parameter.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s11671-026-04730-3.Keywords: Hybrid nanofluid, Thixotropic, Natural convection, Radiation, Stretching sheet

Although previous studies have examined hybrid nanofluids, non-Newtonian flows, magnetic effects, thermal radiation, or bioconvection in different settings, a unified analysis that combines thixotropic hybrid nanofluid behavior with natural convection, melting, chemical reaction, and gyrotactic microorganism effects over a vertically stretching surface remains limited. The novelty of the present work therefore lies in the development of a single mathematical model for Cu–Al2O3 water thixotropic hybrid nanofluid flow that simultaneously incorporates MHD, thermal radiation, heat generation, viscous dissipation, Joule heating, species diffusion, and bioconvection. In addition, the study offers a detailed parametric analysis of how these interacting mechanisms affect the velocity, temperature, nanoparticle concentration, and microorganism distributions, thereby providing physically meaningful insight into advanced heat and mass transfer processes. The findings would be useful for future developments in thermal management, cooling technologies, and transport optimization, while further research would extend the present framework to unsteady or three-dimensional configurations, alternative nanoparticle combinations, and experimental validation.

Solution of methodology

To solve the reduced model, Eqs. (8) to (11) together with the boundary conditions in Eq. (12) were first rewritten as a coupled first-order system. The resulting nonlinear boundary-value problem was then solved numerically in MATLAB using the bvp4c solver, which is based on a collocation scheme with adaptive mesh refinement. An initial guess consistent with the wall and far-field conditions was supplied, and the computational domain was truncated at a sufficiently large similarity variable, η = 7, to ensure asymptotic convergence of all profiles. Unless otherwise stated, the default values of the governing parameters were kept fixed and one parameter was varied at a time in order to isolate its influence on the velocity, temperature, concentration, and microorganism fields. The fixed values of parameters are: Lb = 0.2, Pe = 0.2, = 0.3, Pr = 0.5, Q = 0.5, Ec = 0.9, M = 0.5, Kp = 0.5, Sc = 0.2, = 0.8.

Conclusions

This study presented a numerical investigation of steady two-dimensional MHD thixotropic Cu–Al2​O3​/water hybrid nanofluid flow over a vertically stretching surface under the effects of natural convection, thermal radiation, heat generation, melting, chemical reaction, and bioconvection. The results show that the magnetic parameter and permeability parameter reduce the velocity profile, whereas melting enhances it. Temperature increases with the heat source parameter, but decreases with melting, radiation, and the Prandtl number. In addition, nanoparticle concentration decreases with increasing melting parameter, Schmidt number, and chemical reaction parameter, while microorganism concentration decreases with melting and bioconvection Lewis number but increases with the Peclet number and microorganism concentration difference parameter. These findings provide useful theoretical insight into the control of momentum, heat, mass, and microorganism transport in advanced thermal management, cooling, and transport systems involving reactive non-Newtonian hybrid nanofluids. The present study is limited by the assumptions of steady two-dimensional boundary-layer flow, a single hybrid nanofluid composition, and purely numerical analysis without experimental validation. Future work may therefore consider unsteady or three-dimensional configurations, alternative nanoparticle/base-fluid combinations, additional physical effects, and experimental or computational validation against practical engineering systems.
