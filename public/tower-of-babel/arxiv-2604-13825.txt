Contractive analytic self-mappings of the disc
Artur Nicolau

Abstract
Analytic self-maps of the unit disc whose hyperbolic derivative is uniformly bounded by a constant smaller than one, are called contractive. We describe these maps in terms of their Aleksandrov-Clark measures and in terms of their inner-outer factorization. In addition, we show that contractive inner functions can be described in terms of a certain mixing property of its boundary values. We also present other results on the boundary behavior of contractive inner functions.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb D
be the unit disc in the complex plane and let
\partial\mathbb{D}
be the unit circle. Let
d_{h}(z,w)
be the hyperbolic distance between the points
z,w\in\mathbb D
, defined by

d_{h}(z,w)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+\rho(z,w)^{2}}{1-\rho(z,w)^{2}},\qquad\rho(z,w)=\bigg|\frac{z-w}{1-\overline{w}z}\bigg|.

The Schwarz Lemma says that any analytic mapping
f:\mathbb D\rightarrow\mathbb D
contracts hyperbolic distances, that is,
d_{h}(f(z),f(w))\leq d_{h}(z,w)
for any
z,w\in\mathbb D
, or equivalently its derivative
D_{h}(f)
with respect the hyperbolic metric is bounded by
1
. A calculation shows that

D_{h}(f)(z)=\lim_{w\to z}\frac{d_{h}(f(w),f(z))}{d_{h}(w,z)}=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|f(z)|^{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb D.

Moreover if there exists a point
z_{0}\in\mathbb D
such that
D_{h}(f)(z_{0})=1
, then
D_{h}(f)(z)=1
for any
z\in\mathbb D
and
f
is an automorphism of
\mathbb D
. Hence, the hyperbolic derivative
D_{h}(f)(z)
measures how much
f
deviates from being an automorphism of
\mathbb D
near the point
z
.

An analytic mapping
f:\mathbb D\rightarrow\mathbb D
is called contractive if there exists a constant
D<1
such that
d_{h}(f(z),f(w))\leq D\,d_{h}(z,w)
for any
z,w\in\mathbb D
, or equivalently, if
D(f)=\sup\{D_{h}(f)(z):z\in\mathbb D\}<1
. One nice feature of contractive analytic mappings is conformal invariance, that is,
\tau_{1}\circ f\circ\tau_{2}
is contractive whenever
f
is contractive and
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
are automorphisms of
\mathbb D
. Moreover
D(\tau_{1}\circ f\circ\tau_{2})=D(f)
. The main goal of this paper is to describe analytic self-mappings of
\mathbb D
in different terms: their Aleksandrov-Clark measures; their inner-outer factorization and their boundary behavior.

Given an analytic mapping
f:\mathbb D\rightarrow\mathbb D
and a value
\alpha\in\partial\mathbb D
, the function
(\alpha+f)/(\alpha-f)
has positive real part. Hence there exist a positive measure
\sigma_{\alpha}=\sigma_{\alpha}(f)
on
\partial\mathbb D
and a constant
C_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\frac{\alpha+f(z)}{\alpha-f(z)}=\int_{\partial\mathbb D}\frac{\xi+z}{\xi-z}d\sigma_{\alpha}(\xi)+iC_{\alpha},\quad z\in\mathbb D,

(1.1)

or equivalently,

\frac{1-|f(z)|^{2}}{|\alpha-f(z)|^{2}}=\int_{\partial\mathbb D}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|\xi-z|^{2}}d\sigma_{\alpha}(\xi),\quad z\in\mathbb D.

(1.2)

The measures
\{\sigma_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\partial\mathbb D\}
are called the Aleksandrov-Clark measures of
f
. They were introduced by Clark in the seventies and after the work of Aleksandrov in the nineties, have become an important topic in function theory. Actually many properties of an analytic selfmapping
f
of
\mathbb D
can be translated to properties of the Aleksandrov-Clark measures of
f.
The nice surveys
[
PS
]
and
[
Sak
]
, as well as chapter 9 of
[
CMR
]
, contain many of their properties and a wide range of applications. We mention that
f
is an inner function if and only if
\sigma_{\alpha}
is a singular measure with respect to Lebesgue measure
dm
on
\partial\mathbb D
for some (and then all)
\alpha\in\partial\mathbb D
. Moreover in this case,
\sigma_{\alpha}
is concentrated on the set
f^{-1}(\{\alpha\})
.

Let
dA
denote the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb D
and for
1<p<\infty
let
L^{p}(dA)
be the standard Lebesgue spaces. The Bergman projection
P
given by

P(g)(z)=\int_{\mathbb D}\frac{g(w)}{(1-\overline{w}z)^{2}}dA(w),\quad z\in\mathbb D,\quad g\in L^{2}(dA),

is the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(dA)
onto the closed subspace of analytic functions in
\mathbb D
which are in
L^{2}(\mathbb D)
. It is well known that
P
is a bounded operator from
L^{p}(dA)
into
L^{p}(dA)
for any
1<p<\infty
. Given an arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb D
consider the Carleson box
Q=Q(I)=\{z\in\mathbb D:1-|z|\leq m(I),z/|z|\in I\}
. We will also use the standard notation
l(Q)=m(I)=|I|
if
Q=Q(I)
. Given a positive integrable function
w
in
\mathbb D
consider the weighted space
L^{p}(wdA)
of measurable functions
g
in
\mathbb D
such that
\int_{\mathbb D}|g(z)|^{p}w(z)dA(z)<\infty
. Bekollé and Bonami studied the boundedness of the Bergman projection on weighted Lebesgue spaces and proved that
P
is a bounded operator from
L^{p}(wdA)
to
L^{p}(wdA)
if and only the weight
w
satisfies

\sup\bigg(\frac{1}{A(Q)}\int_{Q}wdA\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{A(Q)}\int_{Q}w^{-1/(p-1)}dA\bigg)^{p-1}<\infty,

(1.3)

where the supremum is taken over all Carleson boxes
Q\subset\mathbb D
. See
[
BB
]
. The weights
w
satisfying (
1.3
) are called Bekollé-Bonami
B_{p}
weights.

Our first result is a description of contractive mappings in terms of their Aleksandrov-Clark measures, in which Bekollé-Bonami
B_{2}
weights play a central role.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be an analytic mapping. Let
\sigma
be an Aleksandror-Clark measure of
f
and let
u
be its Poisson integral defined by

u(z)=\int_{\partial\mathbb D}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|\xi-z|^{2}}d\sigma(\xi),\quad z\in\mathbb D.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(a)
The mapping
f
is contractive.

(b)
There exists a constant
c>0
such that

\frac{\sigma(I)}{|I|}\geqslant cu\left(z_{I}\right),

(1.4)

for any arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
. Here
z_{I}=(1-|I|)\xi_{I}
, where
\xi_{I}
denotes the center of
I
.

(c)

u
is a Bekollé-Bonami
B_{2}
weight, that is,

[u]_{B_{2}}:=\sup\left(\frac{1}{A(Q)}\int_{Q}udA\right)\left(\frac{1}{A(Q)}\int_{Q}\frac{dA}{u}\right)<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all Carleson boxes
Q\subset\mathbb{D}
.

We now mention some remarks.

1. Symmetric measures.
Recall that a bounded analytic function
f
in
\mathbb D
is called inner if it has radial limits of modulus
1
at almost every point of the unit circle. Let
f
be an inner function. In
[
AAN
]
it was shown that
D_{h}(f)(z)\rightarrow 0
as
|z|\rightarrow 1
if and only if
\sigma
is symmetric, that is,

\lim_{|I|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\sigma(I)}{\sigma\left(I^{\prime}\right)}=1.

Here
I^{\prime}
is an arc contiguous to
I
of the same length. This result can be understood as an asymptotic version of Theorem
1.1
.

2. Box kernels.
The converse estimate to (
1.4
) in (b) holds for any positive measure
\sigma
. Indeed
\sigma(I)/|I|\leq 3u(z_{I})
, for any arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
and any positive measure
\sigma
on
\partial\mathbb{D}
. This follows from the fact that
the Poisson kernel
P_{z}(\xi)=(1-|z|^{2})|\xi-z|^{-2}
,
\xi\in\partial\mathbb D
, satisfies
P_{z}(\xi)\geq 3^{-1}(1-|z|)^{-1}{\mathbb{1}}_{I(z)}(\xi)
, where
I(z)=\{\xi\in\partial\mathbb D:|\xi-z/|z||<1-|z|\}
. Hence, condition (
1.4
) in (b) is equivalent to saying that
\sigma(I)/|I|
is comparable to
u(z_{I})
.
Equivalently, integrating
\sigma
against the Poisson kernel
P_{z}
is (uniformly in
z\in\mathbb D
) comparable to integrating against the box kernel, defined as
(1-|z|)^{-1}{\mathbb{1}}_{I(z)}
.

3. Doubling measures.
A positive measure
\sigma
in
\partial\mathbb D
is called a doubling measure if there exists a constant
C=C(\sigma)>0
such that
\sigma(2I)\leq C\sigma(I)
for any arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb D
. Here
2I
refers to the arc on
\partial\mathbb D
that has the same center as
I
and length
2|I|
. The infimum of the constants
C>0
satisfying the previous condition is called the doubling constant of
\sigma
. Condition (b) of Theorem
1.1
implies that
\sigma
is a doubling measure but there are doubling measures which do not satisfy (b). However, if
\sigma
is a doubling measure with doubling constant sufficiently close to
2
, then
\sigma
satisfies condition (b). These facts will be discussed in Section
2
after the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

4.
B_{p}
weights.
It will also be shown in Section
2
that any positive harmonic function is a
B_{p}
weight for any
p>2
. On the other hand, there are positive harmonic functions
u
in
\mathbb{D}
such that
1/u
is not integrable in
\mathbb{D}
and therefore cannot be
B_{2}
weights.

5. Uniformity.
Let
\{\sigma_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\partial\mathbb D\}
be the family of Aleksandrov-Clark measures of an analytic self-mapping of
\mathbb D
. As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, if for some value
\alpha_{0}\in\partial\mathbb D
the measure
\sigma_{\alpha_{0}}
verifies the condition in part (b) of Theorem
1.1
, then
\sigma_{\alpha}
verifies condition (b) for any
\alpha\in\partial\mathbb D
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C(\alpha_{0})>0
such that
[u_{\alpha}]_{B_{2}}\leq C[u_{\alpha_{0}}]_{B_{2}}
for any
\alpha\in\partial\mathbb D
, where
u_{\alpha}
denotes the Poisson integral of
\sigma_{\alpha}
. The analogous fact for Muckenhoupt weights was proved by Sarason in
[
Sar
]
(see also
[
AAD
]
).

Recall that any bounded analytic function
f
in
\mathbb D
factors as
F=EBS
where
E
is an outer function,
B
is a Blaschke product and
S
is a singular inner function associated to a singular measure on
\partial\mathbb D
. See Chapter II of
[
Gar
]
. Our second description of contractive analytic mappings is given in terms of the inner-outer factorization, and it is inspired by the characterization of Bishop of bounded analytic functions in the little Bloch space (
[
Bis
]
). Although there is no direct connection between Aleksandrov-Clark measures and the inner-outer factorization, the conditions in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are closely related.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be an analytic mapping. Let
\nu
be the singular measure associated to its singular inner factor. Consider the measure

\mu=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{D}:f(z)=0}(1-|z|^{2})\delta_{z}+2\nu+2\log|f|^{-1}dm

and its Poisson integral

P[\mu](z)=\int_{\overline{\mathbb D}}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{2}}d\mu(w),\quad z\in\mathbb D.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(a)
f
is contractive.

(b) There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\frac{\mu(\overline{Q})}{l(Q)}\geq CP[\mu](z_{Q})\quad\text{ if }\quad P[\mu](z_{Q})\leq C.

Here
z_{Q}=(1-l(Q))\xi_{Q}
, where
\xi_{Q}
is the center of the arc
\overline{Q}\cap\partial\mathbb D
.

We now focus on contractive inner functions. It turns out that contractive inner functions can be described in terms of their mapping properties in
\partial\mathbb D
. Given a point
z\in\mathbb D
and a measurable set
E\subset\partial\mathbb D
let
\omega(z,E)
denote the harmonic measure in
\mathbb D
from the point
z
of the set
E
, given by

\omega(z,E)=\int_{E}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|\xi-z|^{2}}dm(\xi),\quad z\in\mathbb{D},\,E\subset\partial\mathbb D.

(1.5)

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f
be an inner function. The following conditions are equivalent:

(a)
f
is contractive, that is,
D=\sup\{D_{h}(f)(z):z\in\mathbb D\}<1
.

(b) There exists a constant
C>0
such that

C^{-1}\omega\left(f\left(z_{I}\right),E\right)\leqslant\frac{m\left(f^{-1}(E)\cap I\right)}{m(I)}\leqslant C\omega\left(f\left(z_{I}\right),E\right),

(1.6)

for any measurable set
E\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
and any arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
.

We make the following remarks.

1. The mixing property.
The second inequality in (
1.6
) is always fulfilled, that is, there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that for any inner function
f
, any arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb D
and any measurable set
E\subset\partial\mathbb D
, we have
m(f^{-1}(E)\cap I)\leq C\omega(f(z_{I}),E)m(I)
. This follows from the classical Loewner Lemma which says that
\omega(f(z),E)=\omega(z,f^{-1}(E))
for any
z\in\mathbb D
, any measurable set
E\subset\partial\mathbb D
and any inner function
f
(see page 12 of
[
Ahl
]
), and the estimate
P_{z_{I}}(\xi)\geq 3^{-1}{\mathbb{1}}_{I}(\xi)/m(I)
,
\xi\in\partial\mathbb D
. So, Theorem
1.3
says that the first inequality in (
1.6
) holds if and only if
f
is contractive.

2. Dependence of the constants.
Assume that (a) holds and let
C=C(D)
be the constant appearing in part (b). The proof of Theorem
1.3
shows that the constant
C=C(D)
satisfies
C(D)\longrightarrow 1
as
D\rightarrow 0
. The proof also provides local versions of the result.

A measurable set
E\subset\partial\mathbb D
is called a
B_{2}
set if
\omega(z,E)
, as defined in (
1.5
), is a
B_{2}
weight. By Theorem
1.1
, this is equivalent to the existence of a constant
C>0
such that

\frac{m(I\cap E)}{m(I)}\geq C\omega(z_{I},E),

for any arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb D
. Note that by Remark 3 after Theorem
1.1
, if
E
is a
B_{2}
set, the restriction of the Lebesgue measure to
E
is a doubling measure and hence
B_{2}
sets of positive measure must be well spread out over
\partial\mathbb D
. It turns out that there exist
B_{2}
sets
E\subset\partial\mathbb D
with
0<m(E)<1
and actually, contractive inner functions provide non-trivial examples of
B_{2}
sets. It is convenient to mention that by Lowner’s Lemma, for any inner function
f
we have
0<m(f^{-1}(E))<1
for any measurable set
E\subset\partial\mathbb D
with
0<m(E)<1
.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
f
be a contractive inner function. Then
f^{-1}(E)
is a
B_{2}
set for any measurable set
E\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
.

A related result was proved in
[
AAN
]
. More concretely, assume
D_{h}(f)(z)
tends to
0
as
|z|\to 1
. Then for any measurable set
E\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
, the restriction of Lebesgue measure to
f^{-1}(E)
is a symmetric measure, that is,

\frac{m\left(f^{-1}(E)\cap I\right)}{m\left(f^{-1}(E)\cap I^{\prime}\right)}\xrightarrow[|I|\rightarrow 0]{}1.

Here
I^{\prime}
denotes an arc contiguous to
I
of the same length.

Our three main results are now applied to obtain fine properties of the boundary behavior of contractive inner functions. For
0<s<1
let
M^{s}(E)
denote the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff content of a set
E\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
and let
\operatorname{dim}(E)
denote its Hausdorff dimension. Fernández and Pestana proved that for any
0<s<1
and for any inner function
f
there exists a constant
C=C(s,f(0))
such that
M^{s}(f^{-1}(E))\geq CM^{s}(E)
for any measurable set
E\subset\partial\mathbb D
. Therefore
\operatorname{dim}(f^{-1}(E)\geq\operatorname{dim}(E)
, for any measurable set
E\subset\partial\mathbb D
and any inner function
f
. See
[
FP
]
. Contractive inner functions have the following extreme behavior.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
f
be a contractive inner function with

D=\sup\{D_{h}(f)(z):z\in\mathbb{D}\}<1.

Then
M^{1-D}(f^{-1}(\{\alpha\})\cap I)>0
for any point
\alpha\in\partial\mathbb{D}
and any arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
.

Note that an inner function
f
which extends analytically across an arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb D
satisfies
D_{h}(f)(z)
tends to
1
, as
z\in\mathbb D
approaches any point of
I
. Hence, contractive inner functions cannot be analytically extended across any arc of the unit circle. Consequently, the set of singularities of a contractive inner function
f
is the whole unit circle. Next, we will study the size of the set

E(f)=\{\xi\in\partial\mathbb{D}:\limsup_{r\rightarrow 1}|f(r\xi)|<1\}.

Since
f
is inner,
m(E(f))=0
. However, we will show that if
f
is a contractive inner function, then
\operatorname{dim}(E(f))>0
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
f
be an inner function such that

\sup\{D_{h}(f)(z):z\in\mathbb{D}\}<D<1.

Then there exists
0<t=t(D)<1
such that

\operatorname{dim}\left\{\xi\in I:\limsup_{r\rightarrow 1}|f(r\xi)|<t\right\}>1-D,

for any arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
. In addition,
t(D)\rightarrow 0
as
D\rightarrow 0
.

Finally, we present some natural examples of contractive analytic selfmappings. A domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{D}
will be called a contractive domain if any analytic mapping
f:\mathbb D\rightarrow\Omega
satisfies
\sup\{D_{h}(f)(z):z\in\mathbb D\}<1
. We will show that a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb D
is contractive if and only if there exists a constant
R=R(\Omega)>0
such that
\Omega
does not contain any hyperbolic disc of hyperbolic radius
R
. This is analogous to the well known description of Bloch domains from
[
ACP
]
. Contractive analytic mappings also appear naturally when studying composition operators in the classical Bloch space. We will also give other natural examples of inner functions and discuss when they are contractive and of bounded compression, which were considered in
[
IN25
]
.

1.1
and the corresponding remarks are discussed in Section

1.2
and
1.3
. Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
are proved in Section
4

5

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Given an arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb D
centered at
\xi_{I}
, recall the notations
z_{I}=(1-|I|)\xi_{I}
and
Q(I)=\{z\in\mathbb D:1-|z|\leq|I|,\,z/|z|\in I\}
. We also denote by
T(Q)=\{z\in Q:1-|z|\geq l(Q)/2\}
the top part of
Q
. Harnack’s inequality provides a universal constant
C>0
such that
C^{-1}u(w)\leq u(z)\leq Cu(w)
for any pair of points
z,w\in T(Q)
, any Carleson box
Q
and any positive harmonic function
u
in
\mathbb D
. We start with an elementary auxiliary result.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists a universal constant
C>0
such that

C^{-1}u\left(z_{I}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{A(Q(I))}\int_{Q(I)}udA\leqslant Cu\left(z_{I}\right),

for any arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
and any positive harmonic function
u
in
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

The lower bound follows
from the mean value property and Harnack’s inequality.
For the upper bound, we use that

\frac{1}{|I|}\lesssim\frac{|I|}{\left|\xi-z_{I}\right|^{2}},\quad\xi\in I,

which gives

\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}u(r\xi)dm(\xi)\lesssim\int_{I}u(r\xi)\frac{|I|}{\left|\xi-z_{I}\right|^{2}}dm(\xi)\leq u\left(rz_{I}\right),\quad 0<r<1.

Applying Harnack’s inequality one obtains

\frac{1}{|I|^{2}}\int_{Q(I)}udA\lesssim\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{1-|I|}^{1}u\left(rz_{I}\right)dr\lesssim u\left(z_{I}\right),

as desired.
∎

Let
f:\mathbb D\rightarrow\mathbb D
be an analytic mapping and let
u
be the Poisson integral of an Aleksandrov-Clark measure of
f
. We will use the following identities which follow from definition (
1.1
) by a straightforward computation,

D_{h}(f)(z)=\frac{\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\left|f^{\prime}(z)\right|}{1-|f(z)|^{2}}=\frac{\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)|\nabla u(z)|}{2u(z)},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

(2.1)

and

\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)|\nabla u(z)|=2\left|\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|\xi-z|^{2}\tau_{z}(\xi)}d\sigma(\xi)\right|,

(2.2)

where
\tau_{z}(\xi)=(\xi-z)/(1-\bar{z}\xi)
,
\xi\in\partial\mathbb D
. These identities have recently been used in
[
IN24
]
and
[
IN25
]
.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

(a)\Rightarrow(b)
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Assume
I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
is an arc with

\frac{\sigma(I)}{|I|}\leq\varepsilon u\left(z_{I}\right).

We want to show that
\varepsilon
is bounded below if
f
is contractive. Let
0<\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)<1
be a constant to be fixed later and let
\delta I
denote the arc that has the same center as
I
and length
\delta|I|
. By (
2.2
),

\left(1-\left|z_{\delta I}\right|^{2}\right)\left|\nabla u\left(z_{\delta I}\right)\right|=2\left|\int_{\partial\mathbb D}\frac{1-\left|z_{\delta I}\right|^{2}}{\left|\xi-z_{\delta I}\right|^{2}\tau_{z_{\delta I}}(\xi)}d\sigma(\xi)\right|.

(2.3)

Now the elementary estimate
|\xi-z_{\delta I}|\geq 1-|z_{\delta I}|=\delta|I|
and Harnack’s inequality give

\int_{I}\frac{1-\left|z_{\delta I}\right|^{2}}{\left|\xi-z_{\delta I}\right|^{2}}d\sigma(\xi)\leqslant\frac{2\sigma(I)}{\delta|I|}\leqslant\frac{2\varepsilon}{\delta}u\left(z_{I}\right)\leqslant\frac{2\varepsilon}{\delta^{2}}u\left(z_{\delta I}\right).

(2.4)

Then

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\partial\mathbb D}\frac{1-|z_{\delta I}|^{2}}{\left|\xi-z_{\delta I}\right|^{2}\tau_{z_{\delta I}}(\xi)}d\sigma(\xi)\right|\geq

(2.5)

Doubling measures.
As explained in Remark 2 from the introduction, condition (b) is equivalent to
\sigma(I)/|I|\simeq u(z_{I})
, for any arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb D
. We now show that condition (b) implies that
\sigma
is a doubling measure. Actually, condition (b) and Harnack’s inequality give

\frac{\sigma(I)}{|I|}\simeq u(z_{I})\simeq u(z_{I^{\prime}})\simeq\frac{\sigma(I^{\prime})}{|I^{\prime}|},

for any pair
I,I^{\prime}
of adjacent arcs of
\partial\mathbb D
of equal length.

However, there are doubling measures for which (b) fails, as the following example shows. Let
\mathcal{D}_{n}
be the collection of dyadic arcs of
\partial\mathbb D
of generation
n
and consider the measure
\sigma
defined recursively by declaring its mass on any dyadic arc, as follows. Fix
0<p<1/2
. Define
\sigma(\partial\mathbb D)=1
and by induction, assume that
\sigma(I)
has been defined for any
I\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
. Fix an arc
I\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
and write
I=I_{-}\cup I_{+}
, where
I_{-}\in\mathcal{D}_{n+1}
(respectively,
I_{+}\in\mathcal{D}_{n+1}
) is the left (respectively right) half of
I
. Define

\displaystyle\sigma\left(I_{-}\right)=p\sigma(I),\quad\sigma\left(I_{+}\right)=(1-p)\sigma(I),\quad\text{if }n\text{ is odd},

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sigma\left(I_{-}\right)=(1-p)\sigma(I),\quad\sigma\left(I_{+}\right)=p\sigma(I),\quad\text{if }n\text{ is even}.

This defines the measure
\sigma
and since
0<p<1/2
,
\sigma
is singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure in
\partial\mathbb D
. Note that

\frac{p}{1-p}\leq\frac{\sigma(I)}{\sigma\left(I^{\prime}\right)}\leq\frac{1-p}{p},

(2.9)

for any pair of contiguous arcs
I,I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
,
n\geq 1
.
Since any arc in the unit circle contains a dyadic subarc of comparable length, estimates (
2.9
) imply that
\sigma
is a doubling measure. Note that for any
n=1,2,\ldots
, there exists a dyadic arc
I_{n}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
such that
\sigma\left(I_{n}\right)=p^{n}
while by Harnack’s inequality,

. Hence, condition (b) implies that
p\geq 1/4
. In Section
5
we will show that
\sigma
satisfies condition (b) if and only if
p>1/4
.

We will now show that if
\sigma
is a doubling measure with doubling constant sufficiently close to
2
, then
\sigma
satisfies condition (b). Assume that there exists
0<\varepsilon<1
such that
\sigma(2I)\leq 2(1+\varepsilon)\sigma(I)
for any arc

. Then, standard estimates of the Poisson kernel and the doubling condition give

u(z_{I})=\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\frac{1-\left|z_{I}\right|^{2}}{\left|\xi-z_{I}\right|^{2}}d\sigma(\xi)\lesssim\frac{\sigma(I)}{|I|}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma\left(2^{k}I\right)}{2^{2k}\left|I\right|}\lesssim\frac{2\sigma(I)}{(1-\varepsilon)|I|}.

Hence, condition (b) holds.

5.1
Contractive domains

Lemma 5.1

Given

, there exists

such that if an analytic mapping

satisfies

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\left|f^{\prime}(z)\right|}{1-|f(z)|^{2}}\geqslant\rho,

contains a hyperbolic disc of hyperbolic radius

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Assume there exist
M>0
and mappings
f_{n}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
,

, with

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)|f_{n}^{\prime}(z)|}{1-\left|f_{n}(z)\right|^{2}}>1-\frac{1}{n},

does not contain any hyperbolic disc of hyperbolic radius

,

converges uniformly on compacts of

to a rotation. Hence

contains hyperbolic discs of hyperbolic radius

5.2
Contractive versus bounded compression

An analytic mapping
f:\mathbb D\rightarrow\mathbb D
has bounded compression if the hyperbolic diameters of the images of hyperbolic discs of hyperbolic radius
1
are uniformly bounded below. In
[
IN25
]
several different descriptions of these mappings are presented. In this section we consider the singular doubling probability measure on
\sigma
defined by (
2.8
)
and discuss when it leads to a contractive inner function or to an inner function of bounded compression.

We first show that
\sigma
is an Aleksandrov-Clark measure of a contractive inner function if and only if
1/4<p<1/2
. By Theorem
1.1
, this amounts to showing that
P[\sigma]\left(z_{I}\right)
is comparable to
\sigma(I)/|I|
for any arc
I\subset\mathbb D
if and only if
1/4<p<1/2
.

Since
\sigma
is a doubling measure, we can restrict our attention to dyadic arcs
I\subset\partial\mathbb D
. Fix a dyadic arc
I
with
|I|=2^{-N}
. Standard estimates of the Poisson kernel show

P[\sigma](z_{I})\simeq\frac{\sigma(I)}{|I|}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\sigma\left(2^{k}I\setminus 2^{k-1}I\right)}{2^{2k}|I|}.

Let
I_{n}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
with
I\subset I_{n}
,
n=0,\cdots,N
. Since
\sigma
is doubling,
\sigma(2^{k}I\setminus 2^{k-1}I)\simeq\sigma(I_{N-k})
. Then

P[\sigma](z_{I})\simeq\frac{\sigma(I)}{|I|}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\sigma\left(I_{N-k}\right)}{2^{2k}|I|}.

By construction
\sigma(I)=p^{\alpha(k,I)}(1-p)^{k-\alpha(k,I)}\sigma\left(I_{N-k}\right)
, where
\alpha(k,I)
is the number of dyadic arcs
\left\{I_{n}:n=N-k+1,\ldots,N\right\}
that have a proportion
p
of the mass of their predecessor. Hence

P[\sigma](z_{I})\simeq\frac{\sigma(I)}{|I|}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{p^{-\alpha(k,I)}{(1-p)}^{-(k-\alpha(k,I))}}{2^{2k}}\frac{\sigma(I)}{|I|}.

We deduce that
P[\sigma]\left(z_{I}\right)
is comparable to
\sigma(I)/|I|
if and only if

\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{p^{-\alpha(k,I)}(1-p)^{-k+\alpha(k,I)}}{2^{2k}}\lesssim 1.

(5.1)

Since (
5.1
) must be verified for every dyadic arc
I
and
0<p<1/2
, the worst possible case occurs when
\alpha(k,I)=k
for all
1\leq k\leq N
, that is, when each predecessor of
I
gets proportion
p
of mass from its predecessor. Hence (
5.1
) holds for all dyadic arcs
I
if and only if

\sum_{k=1}\frac{p^{-k}}{2^{2k}}\lesssim 1,

which holds if and only if
p>1/4
.

Finally, we show that for any
0<p<1/2
, the measure
\sigma
is the Aleksandrov-Clark measure of an inner function with bounded compression. We will use the following description of these measures given in
[
IN25
]
: a positive singular measure
\sigma
on
\partial\mathbb D
is the Aleksandrov-Clark measure of an inner function of bounded compression if and only if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N=N(\varepsilon)>0
such that for any heavy arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb D
there exists a subarc
J\subset I
with
|J|\geq 2^{-N}|I|
such that
\sigma(J)/|J|\leq\varepsilon\sigma(I)/|I|
. Now observe that any dyadic arc
J\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
contains a dyadic subarc
J\subset I
,
J\in\mathcal{D}_{n+k}
with

\frac{\sigma(J)}{|J|}=(2p)^{k}\frac{\sigma(I)}{|I|},

for any
k=1,2,\ldots
. Hence, given
\varepsilon>0
we have

\frac{\sigma(J)}{|J|}<\varepsilon\frac{\sigma(I)}{|I|}

if
k\geq(\log\varepsilon)(\log(2p))^{-1}
. Since
\sigma
is a doubling measure, this shows that
\sigma
is an Aleksandrov-Clark measure of an inner function of bounded compression.
∎

5.3
Composition operators

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the classical Bloch space of analytic functions
g:\mathbb D\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
\|g\|=\sup\{(1-|z|^{2})|g^{\prime}(z)|:z\in\mathbb D\}<\infty
. Given an analytic mapping
f:\mathbb D\rightarrow\mathbb D
, consider the composition operator
C_{f}:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
defined by
C_{f}(g)=g\circ f
,
g\in\mathcal{B}
. By the chain rule,

(1-|z|^{2})|(g\circ f)^{\prime}(z)|)=D_{h}(f)(z)(1-|f(z)|^{2})|g^{\prime}(f(z))|,\quad z\in\mathbb D,

(5.2)

and hence
\|C_{f}(g)\|\leq D(f)\|g\|
for any
g\in\mathcal{B}
. Here
D(f)=\sup\{D_{h}(f)(z):z\in\mathbb D\}
. It also shows that there exists a constant
D<1
such that
\|C_{f}(g)\|\leq D\|g\|
for any
g\in\mathcal{B}
if and only if
f
is contractive. It is well known that the essential norm
\|C_{f}\|_{e}
satisfies

\|C_{f}\|_{e}=\lim_{c\to 1}\sup\{D_{h}(f)(z):|f(z)|>c\}.

See
[
Mon
]
. From Lemma
3.2
we deduce that
\|C_{f}\|_{e}<1
if and only if
f
is contractive.
