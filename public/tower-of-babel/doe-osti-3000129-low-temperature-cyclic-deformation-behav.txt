Executive Summary:
 The primary goal of the project is to explore the fundamental deformation and fatigue
failure mechanisms for magnesium with a hexagonal close packed (HCP) crystal structure. It
is important to perform the project for several reasons. Firstly, magnesium is the lightest
structural metal and its application in various structural components can reduce the final
component weight. Secondly, magnesium-based structural components in transportation
vehicles such as automobiles and airplanes can improve fuel efficiency and reduce
greenhouse gas emissions. Thirdly, structural components often experience dynamic loading
such as cyclic loading conditions. Fourthly, magnesium with a HCP crystal structure has its
special deformation responses under loading conditions. This project investigated magnesium
under various loading conditions and also explored some processing routes to manipulate the
microstructure and mechanical properties of magnesium. The research results were published
in a number of articles and also disseminated through presentations in various conferences
such as TMS annual meetings, MRS meetings, the international Plasticity conferences, ASME
Manufacturing Science and Engineering. In addition to the contribution to the
research/academic community, this project is also beneficial to the public. With the actual
usage of magnesium in the passenger cars, weight reduction and fuel consumption reduction
will save the energy bill of individual owners.

Project Goals and Objectives
 The primary goal is to investigate the underlying deformation and failure mechanisms
of magnesium, especially under cyclic loading conditions. The specific objectives are as
follows.

 1

 Processing magnesium samples to realize microstructure modification and achieve the
 control on mechanical response;
 Performing mechanical characterizations on magnesium samples to observe
 mechanical behaviors and acquire mechanical properties;
 Conducting microstructural characterizations on pre- and post-test samples to obtain
 microscopic deformation mechanisms;
 Connecting deformation behaviors/properties and microscopic mechanisms for
 different loading conditions.
