The AJ conjecture and connected sums of torus knots
Xingru Zhang

Abstract
. The set of isotopy classes of nontrivial torus knots T ⁡ ( p , q ) T(p,q) in S 3 S^{3} is in bijection with the set of coprime integer pairs ( p , q ) (p,q) satisfying | p | > q ≥ 2 |p|>q\geq 2 . We verify the AJ conjecture for the connected sums T ⁡ ( p , q ) ​ # ​ T ​ ( a , b ) T(p,q)\#T(a,b) when p p and a a have the same sign. Notably, in cases where p ​ q = a ​ b pq=ab but p ≠ a p\neq a , the recurrence polynomial α ⁡ ( t , M , L ) {\alpha}(t,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}}) of T ⁡ ( p , q ) ​ # ​ T ​ ( a , b ) T(p,q)\#T(a,b) has repeated factors involving the variable L after evaluation at t = − 1 t=-1 . These appear to be the first examples of knots exhibiting this phenomenon. Therefore, the AJ conjecture requires a slight modification to accommodate this possibility.



1.
Introduction

The AJ conjecture, formulated by Garoufalidis
[
3
, Conjecture 2]
, predicts a fundamental connection between two important knot invariants for knots in
S^{3}
: the A-polynomial and the colored Jones polynomial.

For a knot
K
in
S^{3}
,
its (normalized)
A
-polynomial
A_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}({{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})
is a two-variable polynomial
in
{\mathbb{Z}}[{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}}]
with no repeated factors and with relatively prime integer coefficients,
which is uniquely associated with
K
up to a sign (see
[
1
]
).
Note that
A_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}({{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})
always contains the factor
{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}}-1
.

For a knot
K
in
S^{3}
and each positive integer
n
, the
n
-colored Jones polynomial
J_{{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}}(n)
is a one-variable Laurent polynomial in
{\mathbb{Z}}[t^{\pm 1}]
with the zero framing (see
[
18
]
). In this paper,
J_{{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}}(n)
is normalized such that for the unknot
U
,

(1.1)

J_{{\mbox{\tiny U}}}(n)=[n]:=\frac{t^{2n}-t^{-2n}}{t^{2}-t^{-2}}.

By defining
J_{{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}}(-n):=-J_{{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}}(n)
and
J_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(0):=0
, one may treat
J_{{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}}(n)
as
a discrete function
J_{{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}}(\cdot):{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}[t^{\pm 1}]
. The quantum torus
{\mathcal{T}}
, a non-commutative ring with presentation

{\mathcal{T}}={\mathbb{Q}}[t^{\pm 1}]\left<{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}}^{\pm 1},{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}}^{\pm 1}\right>/({{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}}{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}}-t^{2}{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}}{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}}),

acts on the set of discrete functions
f:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{Q}}[t^{\pm 1}]
by

(1.2)

({{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}}f)(n):=t^{2n}f(n),\quad({{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}}f)(n):=f(n+1).

The set of
annihilators of
J_{{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}}(n)
in
{\mathcal{T}}
, denoted by

{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}:=\{P\in{\mathcal{T}}\mid PJ_{{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}}(n)=0\},

is a left ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}
called the
recurrence ideal
of
K
(as each operator
P\in{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}
defines
a recurrence relation for
J_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(n)
and vice versa).
A fundamental result proven in
[
5
]
states that for every knot
K
in
S^{3}
,
J_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(n)
has a nontrivial recurrence relation, or equivalently,
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}

is not the zero ideal.

The ring
{\mathcal{T}}
can be extended to a principal left ideal domain
\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}
by adjoining inverses of polynomials in
t
and
M
; that is,
\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}
is the set of Laurent polynomials in
L
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Q}}(t,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}})
(which is the set of
rational functions in
t
and
M
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Q}}
) under the product defined by

(1.3)

f(t,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}}){{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}}^{j}\cdot g(t,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}}){{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}}^{k}=f(t,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}})g(t,t^{2j}{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}}){{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}}^{j+k}.

The extended left ideal
\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}=\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}{\mathcal{A}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}}
is then generated by a single non-zero element in
\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}
.
Such a generator can be normalized to lie in
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}
and takes the form

\alpha_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(t,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})=\sum_{i=0}^{d}D_{i}{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}}^{i},

with
d>0
minimum (among all non-zero annihilators of
J_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(n)
) and with
D_{0},\dots,D_{d}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}}]
being coprime in
{\mathbb{Z}}[t,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}}]
. This polynomial
\alpha_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(t,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})
is uniquely determined up to a sign and is called the (
normalized
)
recurrence polynomial
of
K
.

The original AJ conjecture states that for every knot
K
in
S^{3}
,
\alpha_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(-1,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})
is equal to

A_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}({{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})
up to a non-zero factor depending on
M
only.
Based on examples found in this paper, for some knots
K
,
{\alpha}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(-1,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})

may contain repeated factors involving the variable
L
.
Consequently, the
AJ
conjecture requires a slight modification:

The AJ conjecture:
For every knot
K
in
S^{3}
,

{\alpha}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(-1,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})
, after removing all repeated factors, is equal to
A_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}({{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})

up to a non-zero factor depending on
M
only.

The conjecture remains largely open, having been proven true only for some simple classes of knots, such as some
2
-bridge knots
[
3
,
13
,
7
,
4
,
8
,
9
]
, torus knots
[
6
,
14
]
, some
pretzel knots
[
8
]
, and some cabled knots over certain knots
[
11
,
10
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
2
]
.
In this paper, we consider the AJ conjecture for connected sums of torus knots.

Recall that the set of isotopy classes of nontrivial torus knots
T(p,q)
in
S^{3}

is in one-to-one correspondence with the set of coprime integer pairs

(p,q)
satisfying
|p|>q\geq 2
.
We show:

Theorem 1.1
.

The
AJ
conjecture holds for the connected sums
T(p,q)\#T(a,b)

when
p
and
a
have the same sign.

As we will see, for a knot
K=T(p,q)\#T(a,b)
given as in Theorem
1.1
,
its recurrence polynomial
{\alpha}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(t,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})
, after evaluation at
t=-1
, has repeated factors involving the variable

L
exactly when
pq=ab
but
p\neq a
.

In Section 2, we list the formulas to be applied.
Theorem 1.1 is then proved in Sections 3-9, which correspond to seven cases.

For convenience, in this paper we often represent the
A
-polynomial

A_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}({{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})
as an element in
{\mathbb{Z}}[{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}}^{\pm 1},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}}]
that differs from the normalized

A
-polynomial only by a factor of an integer power of
M
.
Similarly, in each case of
K=T(p,q)\#T(a,b)
that we consider, we shall obtain
a recurrence polynomial
{\alpha}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(t,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})
up to normalization, i.e., up to multiplying
by a non-zero rational function in
t
and
M
.

Our proof procedure consists of the following three steps:

(1)

Produce a candidate minimum
L
-degree non-zero annihilator
{\alpha}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(t,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})

in
\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}
of the form

\displaystyle{\alpha}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(t,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})=\sum_{i=0}^{d}D_{i}{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}}^{i}
with
D_{i}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(t,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}})

such that
{\alpha}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(-1,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})
is well-defined and has the same
L
-degree as
{\alpha}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(t,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})
;

(2)

Verify that
{\alpha}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(-1,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})
, after removal of all repeated factors, is equal to
the
A
-polynomial
A_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}({{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})
up to multiplying by a non-zero rational function in
M
;

(3)

Prove that the
L
-degree
d
of our candidate annihilator
{\alpha}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(t,{{\mbox{\footnotesize M}}},{{\mbox{\footnotesize L}}})

is indeed the minimum among
all non-zero annihilators of
J_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}(n)
in
\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}_{{\mbox{\tiny K}}}
.

This procedure is sufficient to verify the AJ conjecture for the given knot.
