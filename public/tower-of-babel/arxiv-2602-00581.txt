Gaffney’s inequality and the Closed Range Property of the de Rham Complex in Unbounded Domains Thanks: Corresponding Author. Marcus Waurick
Dirk Pauly, Marcus Waurick

Abstract
. The classical Poincaré estimate establishes closedness of the range of the gradient in unweighted 𝖫 2 ​ ( Ω ) \mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega) -spaces as long as Ω ⊆ ℝ 3 \Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3} is contained in a slab, that is, Ω \Omega is bounded in one direction. Here, as a main observation, we provide closed range results for the rot \rot -operator, if (and only if) Ω \Omega is bounded in two directions. Along the way, we characterise closed range results for all the differential operators of the primal and dual de Rham complex in terms of directions of boundedness of the underlying domain. As a main application, one obtains the existence of a spectral gap near the 0 0 of the Maxwell operator allowing for exponential stability results for solutions of Maxwell’s equations with sufficient damping in the conductivity. Our results are based on the validity of Gaffney’s (in)equality and the transition of the same to unbounded (simple) domains as well as on the stability of closed range results under bi-Lipschitz regular transformations. The latter technique is well-known and detailed in the appendix; for the results concerning Gaffney’s estimate, we shall provide accessible, simple proofs using mere standard results. Moreover, we shall present non-trivial examples and a closed range result for rot \rot with mixed boundary conditions on a set bounded in one direction only.

Applications

The main property of ((skew)-selfadjoint) operators with closed range is a spectral gap around the origin. respectively. All of them are selfadjoint.

Next, we turn to consequences of the closed range property for block operators of the form mentioned in the concluding lines of the previous examples.

(Skew-)selfadjoint operators with closed range

Throughout, let
\mathsf{H}
be a Hilbert space and

\operatorname{T}\colon D(\operatorname{T})\subseteq\mathsf{H}\to\mathsf{H}

be skew-selfadjoint or self-adjoint. Recall the notation for the reduced operator
\widehat{\operatorname{T}}
, which turns out to be continuously invertible as long as
R(\operatorname{T})\subseteq\mathsf{H}
is closed. In other words, we have
0\in\rho(\widehat{\operatorname{T}})
. By the openness of the reslvent set
\rho(\widehat{\operatorname{T}})
, we obtain that
\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda
is continuously invertible in a neighbourhood of
0
. The quantified statement reads as follows.

Lemma 2.4

(spectral gap for the reduced operator)
.

Let
R(\operatorname{T})
be closed and let
|\lambda|<1/c_{\operatorname{T}}

with
c_{\operatorname{T}}\coloneqq\big\|\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-1}\big\|_{R(\operatorname{T})\to R(\operatorname{T})}
.
Then

\forall\,x\in D(\widehat{\operatorname{T}})\qquad\|x\|_{\mathsf{H}}\leq\widehat{c}_{\operatorname{T},\lambda}\big\|(\operatorname{T}-\lambda)x\big\|_{\mathsf{H}},\qquad\quad\widehat{c}_{\operatorname{T},\lambda}\coloneqq\frac{c_{\operatorname{T}}}{1-c_{\operatorname{T}}|\lambda|},

and

N(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)=\{0\},\qquad R(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)=R(\operatorname{T}),

in particular,
R(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)
is closed.
Moreover, the inverse
(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)^{-1}:R(\operatorname{T})\to D(\widehat{\operatorname{T}})
is bounded with

\big\|(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)^{-1}\big\|_{R(\operatorname{T})\to R(\operatorname{T})}\leq\widehat{c}_{\operatorname{T},\lambda}
.
In other words,

B(0,1/c_{\operatorname{T}})\subseteq\rho(\widehat{\operatorname{T}})
.

Proof.

As mentioned above,

\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-1}:R(\operatorname{T})\to D(\widehat{\operatorname{T}})
is bounded, and

\|x\|_{\mathsf{H}}\leq c_{\operatorname{T}}\|\operatorname{T}x\|_{\mathsf{H}}\leq c_{\operatorname{T}}\big\|(\operatorname{T}-\lambda)x\big\|_{\mathsf{H}}+c_{\operatorname{T}}|\lambda|\|x\|_{\mathsf{H}}

holds for
x\in D(\widehat{\operatorname{T}})
,
showing the estimate for all
|\lambda|<1/c_{\operatorname{T}}
.
Hence

N(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)=\{0\}

and
R(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)
is closed with

R(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)=N(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{*}-\overline{\lambda})^{\bot_{R(\operatorname{T})}}=N(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\overline{\lambda})^{\bot_{R(\operatorname{T})}}=R(\operatorname{T}).

Thus
\widehat{(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)}=\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda
.
Therefore,

\widehat{(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)}^{-1}=(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)^{-1}:R(\widehat{\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda})=R(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)=R(\operatorname{T})\to D(\widehat{\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda})=D(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)=D(\widehat{\operatorname{T}})

is bounded by the above estimate and Theorem
2.1
.
∎

Theorem 2.5

(spectral gap)
.

Let

closed;

. Moreover,

is bounded with

,
where

\displaystyle c_{\operatorname{T},\lambda}\coloneqq\sqrt{\widehat{c}_{\operatorname{T},\lambda}^{2}+|\lambda|^{-2}}.

In particular,

\forall\,x\in D(\operatorname{T})\qquad\|x\|_{\mathsf{H}}\leq c_{\operatorname{T},\lambda}\big\|(\operatorname{T}-\lambda)x\big\|_{\mathsf{H}}.

In other words,

B(0,1/c_{\operatorname{T}})\setminus\{0\}\subseteq\rho(\operatorname{T})

Let

with

According to the standard orthogonal decomposition,
\mathsf{H}=R(\operatorname{T})\oplus_{\mathsf{H}}N(\operatorname{T})

We see

\displaystyle D(\operatorname{T})\ni x

\displaystyle D(\widehat{\operatorname{T}})

\displaystyle=D(\operatorname{T})\cap R(\operatorname{T}),

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}\ni f

\displaystyle=f_{R}+f_{N}\in R(\operatorname{T})\oplus_{\mathsf{H}}N(\operatorname{T}),

and obtain the equation

(\operatorname{T}-\lambda)x_{R}-\lambda x_{N}=f_{R}+f_{N}

which separates into the two equations

by orthogonality. Lemma

yields

x_{R}=(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)^{-1}f_{R},\qquad x_{N}=-\frac{1}{\lambda}f_{N},

and thus

\|x\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}=\|x_{R}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}+\|x_{N}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\leq\widehat{c}_{\operatorname{T},\lambda}^{2}\|f_{R}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}+|\lambda|^{-2}\|f_{N}\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\leq c_{\operatorname{T},\lambda}^{2}\|f\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}.

We conclude

3

Note that for

Theorem 2.7

(low frequency asymptotics)
.

Let
R(\operatorname{T})
be closed
and let
0<|\lambda|<1/c_{\operatorname{T}}
.
Then

\displaystyle(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{n}\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-n-1},

\displaystyle(\operatorname{T}-\lambda)^{-1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\pi_{N(\operatorname{T})}+(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)^{-1}\pi_{R(\operatorname{T})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\pi_{N(\operatorname{T})}+\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\lambda^{n}\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-n-1}\pi_{R(\operatorname{T})}+\lambda^{k}\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-k-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{n}\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-n}\pi_{R(\operatorname{T})}

and

\big\|(\operatorname{T}-\lambda)^{-1}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\pi_{N(\operatorname{T})}-\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\lambda^{n}\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-n-1}\pi_{R(\operatorname{T})}\big\|_{\mathsf{H}\to\mathsf{H}}\leq\widehat{c}_{\operatorname{T},\lambda}c_{\operatorname{T}}^{k}|\lambda|^{k}=\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{k})\quad(\text{for }\lambda\to 0).

Proof.

We observe

(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)=\widehat{\operatorname{T}}\big(1-\lambda\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-1}\big)

and
\|\lambda\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-1}\|_{R(\operatorname{T})\to R(\operatorname{T})}=|\lambda|c_{\operatorname{T}}<1
.
Thus by Neumann’s series

(\widehat{\operatorname{T}}-\lambda)^{-1}=\big(1-\lambda\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-1}\big)^{-1}\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{n}\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-n-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\lambda^{n}\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-n-1}+\lambda^{k}\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-k-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{n}\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-n},

which, together with Remark

Moreover,

\big\|\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-k-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{n}\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-n}\pi_{R(\operatorname{T})}\big\|_{\mathsf{H}\to\mathsf{H}}\leq c_{\operatorname{T}}^{k+1}\big\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{n}\widehat{\operatorname{T}}^{-n}\big\|_{R(\operatorname{T})\to R(\operatorname{T})}\leq\frac{c_{\operatorname{T}}^{k+1}}{1-|\lambda|c_{\operatorname{T}}}=\widehat{c}_{\operatorname{T},\lambda}c_{\operatorname{T}}^{k},

concludes the proof.

Exponential Stability

We quickly introduce the operator-theoretic setting for space-time equations in the context of evolutionary equations introduced by Picard,
[
36
]
. We also refer to the monograph
[
39
]
accessible for graduate students, and, particularly, we refer to
[
39
, Chapter 11]
on exponential stability. The core result to deduce exponential stability for (nonlinear, time-nonlocal) Maxwell’s equations in
[
7
]
is an exponential stability statement for evolutionary equations that can be found in
[
39
, Corollary 11.6.1]
. In the following, we sketch the set-up and provide a corresponding stability result for Maxwell type equations. For this, we introduce for
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
, the Hilbert space

\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R};\mathsf{H})\coloneqq\{f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\textnormal{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathsf{H}):\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big\|f(t)\big\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}\exp(-2\nu t)dt<\infty\},

endowed with the obvious scalar product. Introducing the (distributional, time-) derivative
\partial_{t,\nu}
on this space with maximal domain, the following result is a standard application of Picard’s well-posedness theorem, where we understand that operators defined on
\mathsf{H}
can be lifted canonically to operators on
\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R};\mathsf{H})
retaining their properties (e.g., self-adjointness, positivity, etc.) For ease of readability, we will re-use the notation of the original operator also for the lifted one.

Theorem 2.8

(Picard’s theorem,
[
39
, Theorem 6.2.1]
)
.

Let
0\leq\operatorname{M}_{0}=\operatorname{M}_{0}^{*},\operatorname{M}_{1}\in\mathsf{L}(\mathsf{H})
,
\operatorname{S}
be a skew-selfadjoint operator in
\mathsf{H}
. Then, if there exists
\nu_{0}\geq 0
such that

\forall\phi\in\mathsf{H}\qquad\nu_{0}\langle\operatorname{M}_{0}\phi,\phi\rangle_{\mathsf{H}}+\Re\langle\operatorname{M}_{1}\phi,\phi\rangle_{\mathsf{H}}\geq c\|\phi\|_{\mathsf{H}},

the operator

\widetilde{\operatorname{B}}_{\nu}\coloneqq(\partial_{t,\nu}\operatorname{M}_{0}+\operatorname{M}_{1}+\operatorname{S})

is closable in
\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R};\mathsf{H})
for all
\nu\geq\nu_{0}
, the closure of which,
\operatorname{B}_{\nu}\coloneqq\overline{\widetilde{\operatorname{B}}_{\nu}}
, is continuously invertible. Moreover, for
\nu,\mu\geq\nu_{0}
and
f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R};\mathsf{H})\cap\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R};\mathsf{H})
, we have
\operatorname{B}_{\nu}^{-1}f=\operatorname{B}_{\mu}^{-1}f
.

A particular application can be found in the following example of Maxwell type.

Example 2.9

(Maxwell type equations)
.

Let
\operatorname{A}\colon D(\operatorname{A})\subseteq\mathsf{H}_{0}\to\mathsf{H}_{1}
be closed and densely defined,
0<c\leq\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{*},\sigma\in\mathsf{L}(\mathsf{H}_{0})
,
0<c\leq\mu=\mu^{*}\in\mathsf{L}(\mathsf{H}_{1})
in the sense of positive definiteness. Then

(\partial_{t,\nu}\operatorname{M}_{0}+\operatorname{M}_{1}+\operatorname{S})\coloneqq(\partial_{t,\nu}\begin{bmatrix}\varepsilon&0\\
0&\mu\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\sigma&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}0&-\operatorname{A}^{*}\\
\operatorname{A}&0\end{bmatrix})

satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
2.8
. Note the particular choice
\operatorname{A}=\rotc_{\Omega}
for some open
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

It has been found that evolutionary equations lead to a convenient framework to express results concerning exponential stability. In fact, an operator of the form of
\operatorname{B}_{\nu}
as provided in Theorem
2.8
is
exponentially stable
, if there exists
\eta>0
such that for all
\nu\geq\nu_{0}
and
f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R};\mathsf{H})\cap\mathsf{L}^{2}_{-\eta}(\mathbb{R};\mathsf{H})
we have

\operatorname{B}_{\nu}f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}_{-\eta}(\mathbb{R};\mathsf{H}).

The relationship of this notion of exponential stability to more classical formulations is expressed in
[
39
, Chapter 11]
. Next, as before, closed range results can help establish exponential stability statements. For simplicity, we focus on equations of the form provided in Example
2.9
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\operatorname{A}\colon D(\operatorname{A})\subseteq\mathsf{H}_{0}\to\mathsf{H}_{1}
be closed and densely defined with closed range,
\varepsilon,\sigma,\mu>0
. Then, there exists
\eta>0
such that for all
\nu>0
and
f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R};\mathsf{H}_{0})\cap\mathsf{L}^{2}_{-\eta}(\mathbb{R};\mathsf{H}_{0})
and

U\coloneqq\overline{(\partial_{t,\nu}\begin{bmatrix}\varepsilon&0\\
0&\mu\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\sigma&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}0&-\operatorname{A}^{*}\\
\operatorname{A}&0\end{bmatrix})}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}f\\
0\end{bmatrix}

we have

U\in\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R};\mathsf{H})\cap\mathsf{L}^{2}_{-\eta}(\mathbb{R};\mathsf{H}).

Proof.

Before we proceed proving the actual result, we may assume without loss of generality, that
\varepsilon=1
and
\mu=1
. Indeed, multiplying

\overline{(\partial_{t,\nu}\begin{bmatrix}\varepsilon&0\\
0&\mu\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\sigma&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}0&-\operatorname{A}^{*}\\
\operatorname{A}&0\end{bmatrix})}

from the left and the right with
\begin{bmatrix}{\varepsilon}^{-1/2}&0\\

we obtain

\overline{(\partial_{t,\nu}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{\sigma}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}0&-{\widetilde{\operatorname{A}}}^{*}\\
\widetilde{\operatorname{A}}&0\end{bmatrix})},\text{ where }\widetilde{\sigma}={\varepsilon}^{-1/2}\sigma{\varepsilon}^{-1/2}\text{ and }\widetilde{\operatorname{A}}={\mu}^{-1/2}{{\operatorname{A}}}{\varepsilon}^{-1/2}.

Note that
\operatorname{A}
has closed range, if and only if
\widetilde{\operatorname{A}}
has; moreover
\widetilde{\sigma}=\sigma/\varepsilon>0
. Henceforth, we drop
\widetilde{\cdot}
again in our notation. Considering the abstract Helmholtz decomposition into
\mathsf{H}_{0}\times\mathsf{H}_{1}=R(\operatorname{S})\oplus N(\operatorname{S})=\big(R(\operatorname{A}^{*})\times R(\operatorname{A})\big)\oplus\big(N(\operatorname{A})\times N(\operatorname{A}^{*})\big)

\overline{(\partial_{t,\nu}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}+\Sigma+\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{S}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix})},\text{ where }\Sigma=\begin{bmatrix}\Sigma_{11}&0\\
0&\Sigma_{22}\end{bmatrix}\text{ with }

Integration by Parts

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain
with boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
and outer unit normal field
\nu
.
For

\Gamma

and its corresponding tangential component on
\Gamma
(a.e.) by

E_{\mathsf{t}}\coloneqq E-E_{\mathsf{n}}\nu=\nu\times E\times\nu.

For simplicity, here, we consider only real-valued vector fields.

For
E,F\in\mathsf{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with matrix fields
\na E,\na F\in\mathsf{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
we have

point-wise

2\rot E\cdot\rot F=\big(\na E-(\na E)^{\top}\big)\cdot\big(\na F-(\na F)^{\top}\big)=2\na E\cdot\na F-2\na E\cdot(\na F)^{\top},

where the dots stand for the standard scalar product for vectors and the Forbenius scalar product for matrices, respectively. Hence we observe the (point-wise) key relation

\rot E\cdot\rot F+\operatorname{div}E\cdot\operatorname{div}F=\na E\cdot\na F-\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\Big(\p_{l}E_{k}\cdot\p_{k}F_{l}-\p_{k}E_{k}\cdot\p_{l}F_{l}\Big).

Integration over
\Omega
, Gauß’ theorem, and Schwarz’ lemma yield

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\qquad\int_{\Omega}\na E\cdot\na F-\int_{\Omega}\rot E\cdot\rot F-\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}E\cdot\operatorname{div}F\\
&=\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\p_{l}E_{k}\p_{k}F_{l}-\p_{k}E_{k}\p_{l}F_{l}\Big)\\
&=\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\int_{\Gamma}\Big(\nu_{l}E_{k}\p_{k}F_{l}-\nu_{k}E_{k}\p_{l}F_{l}\Big)=\int_{\Gamma}\Big((\p_{E}F)_{\mathsf{n}}-E_{\mathsf{n}}\operatorname{div}F\Big)=:\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(E,F).\end{aligned}

So, it remains to investigate the last two boundary integrals,
which we shall do in the following two subsections for smooth domains
\Omega

and for the special case of the unit cube

, separately.

Then we obtain by (
6
) and Gauß’ theorem

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\Omega}\na(\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}})\cdot\rot(\varphi F\times\nu)&=\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}\big(\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}\rot(\varphi F\times\nu)\big)\\
&=\int_{\Gamma}\nu\cdot\big(\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}\rot(\varphi F\times\nu)\big)=-\int_{\Gamma}E_{\mathsf{n}}\operatorname{div}F_{\mathsf{t}},\\
\int_{\Omega}\rot(\varphi E\times\nu)\cdot\na(\varphi F_{\mathsf{n}})&=\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}\big((\varphi E\times\nu)\times\na(\varphi F_{\mathsf{n}})\big)\\
&=\int_{\Gamma}\nu\cdot\big((\varphi E\times\nu)\times\na(\varphi F_{\mathsf{n}})\big)=\int_{\Gamma}\na F_{\mathsf{n}}\cdot E_{\mathsf{t}}=\int_{\Gamma}E_{\mathsf{t}}\cdot\na_{\mathsf{t}}F_{\mathsf{n}}.\end{aligned}

Finally, we plug (
7
) into (
5
)
and arrive at:

Lemma 3.1

(integration by parts for smooth domains)
.

Let
\Omega
be bounded and smooth
and let
E,F\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
.
Then

\langle\na E,\na F\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle\rot E,\rot F\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\operatorname{div}E,\operatorname{div}F\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(E,F),

where
\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}
is a boundary integral term given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(E,F)

\displaystyle=\big\langle\rot(\varphi E\times\nu),\na(\varphi F_{\mathsf{n}})\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\big\langle\na(\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}),\rot(\varphi F\times\nu)\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\big\langle E_{\mathsf{n}},(\operatorname{div}\nu)F_{\mathsf{n}}\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}-\big\langle E_{\mathsf{t}},(\na\nu)F_{\mathsf{t}}\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\|\na E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\rot E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\operatorname{div}E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(E,E),

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(E,E)

\displaystyle=2\big\langle\na(\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}),\rot(\varphi E\times\nu)\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-\big\langle E_{\mathsf{n}},(\operatorname{div}\nu)E_{\mathsf{n}}\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}-\big\langle E_{\mathsf{t}},(\na\nu)E_{\mathsf{t}}\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}.

Proof.

For
E,F\in\mathsf{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
the assertions follow by the previous considerations and computations.
By approximation, i.e.,
\overline{\mathsf{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}=\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
,
the results carry over to
E,F\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
. For this note that, as
\Omega
is smooth, the mapping
\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\ni E\mapsto E|_{\Gamma}\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)
is well-defined and continuous.
∎

Remark 3.2

(integration by parts on the boundary)
.

For
E,F\in\mathsf{C}^{\infty}(\Omega)
we have by (
7
)
the following integration by parts formula on the boundary

\langle\na_{\mathsf{t}}E_{\mathsf{n}},F_{\mathsf{t}}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}=\big\langle\na(\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}),\rot(\varphi F\times\nu)\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=-\langle E_{\mathsf{n}},\operatorname{div}F_{\mathsf{t}}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}.

For
E,F\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
this formula remains valid in the sense of traces for
the respective Sobolev spaces
\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
,
\mathsf{H}(\operatorname{div},\Omega)
, and
\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Omega)
.
More precisely, we see by the complex properties
that
\na(\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}),\,\varphi F\times\nu\in\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Omega)

as well as

\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and
\rot(\varphi F\times\nu)\in\mathsf{H}(\operatorname{div},\Omega)
.
Hence

\big\langle\!\big\langle\trt\na(\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}),\trtx(\varphi F\times\nu)\big\rangle\!\big\rangle_{\Gamma}=\big\langle\na(\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}),\rot(\varphi F\times\nu)\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\big\langle\!\big\langle\trs(\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}),\trn\rot(\varphi F\times\nu)\big\rangle\!\big\rangle_{\Gamma},

where
\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle_{\Gamma}
denotes (roughly)
the duality in the respective
\mathsf{H}^{\pm 1/2}(\Gamma)
trace spaces
without going into details.
Here,
\trs
,
\trn
,
\trt
,
\trtx

denote the scalar, normal, and tangential, twisted tangential traces, respectively.
We emphasise that modifying (or even identifying) the terms

\operatorname{div}F_{\mathsf{t}}
and
\trn\rot(\varphi F\times\nu)

to the proper surface divergence
\operatorname{div}_{\mathsf{t}}

requires some additional efforts,
which are not relevant for our needs.

Corollary 3.3

(integration by parts for smooth domains and homogeneous boundary conditions)
.

Let
\Omega
be bounded and smooth
and let
E,F\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
.
If
E,F\in\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,\Omega)
, then

\displaystyle\langle\na E,\na F\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\langle\rot E,\rot F\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\operatorname{div}E,\operatorname{div}F\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-\big\langle E_{\mathsf{n}},(\operatorname{div}\nu)F_{\mathsf{n}}\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}.

If
E,F\in\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\operatorname{div},\Omega)
, then

\displaystyle\langle\na E,\na F\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\langle\rot E,\rot F\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\operatorname{div}E,\operatorname{div}F\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-\big\langle E_{\mathsf{t}},(\na\nu)F_{\mathsf{t}}\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}.

In particular, if
E\in\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,\Omega)
, then

\displaystyle\|\na E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\rot E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\operatorname{div}E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\int_{\Gamma}\operatorname{div}\nu\,|E_{\mathsf{n}}|^{2},

and, if
E\in\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\operatorname{div},\Omega)
, then

\displaystyle\|\na E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\rot E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\operatorname{div}E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\int_{\Gamma}E_{\mathsf{t}}\cdot(\na\nu E_{\mathsf{t}}).

Proof.

For
E\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,\Omega)

we have
\varphi E\times\nu\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)

and
E_{\mathsf{t}}=0
on
\Gamma
.
Moreover, for all
\Psi\in\mathsf{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

we compute by Gauß’ theorem

\displaystyle\langle\varphi E\times\nu,\rot\Psi\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\big\langle\rot(\varphi E\times\nu),\Psi\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(\varphi E\times\nu\times\Psi),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(\varphi E\times\nu\times\Psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\nu\cdot(\varphi E\times\nu\times\Psi)=\int_{\Gamma}(\nu\times E\times\nu)\cdot\Psi=\int_{\Gamma}E_{\mathsf{t}}\cdot\Psi=0,

which shows
\varphi E\times\nu\in\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,\Omega)
.
Hence, as
\na(\varphi F_{\mathsf{n}})\in N(\rot_{\Omega})\subseteq\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Omega)
,

\big\langle\rot(\varphi E\times\nu),\na(\varphi F_{\mathsf{n}})\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=0,

and Lemma
3.1
shows the first assertion for

E,F\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,\Omega)
.

For
E\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\operatorname{div},\Omega)

we have
\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)

and
E_{\mathsf{n}}=0
on
\Gamma
.
Moreover, for all
\Psi\in\mathsf{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

we compute by Gauß’ theorem

\displaystyle\langle\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}},\operatorname{div}\Psi\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=-\big\langle\na(\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}),\Psi\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}\cdot\Psi),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}\cdot\Psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\nu\cdot(\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}\cdot\Psi)=\int_{\Gamma}E_{\mathsf{n}}\Psi_{\mathsf{n}}=0,

which shows
\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}\in\mathring{\mathsf{H}}^{1}(\Omega)
.
Hence, as
\rot(\varphi F\times\nu)\in N(\operatorname{div}_{\Omega})\subseteq\mathsf{H}(\operatorname{div},\Omega)
,

\big\langle\na(\varphi E_{\mathsf{n}}),\rot(\varphi F\times\nu)\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=0,

and Lemma
3.1
shows the second assertion for

E,F\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\operatorname{div},\Omega)
.
∎

In the remaining part of this section, we focus on the implication of the presented integration by parts formula for convex geometries.

Corollary 3.4

(integration by parts for smooth convex domains and homogeneous boundary conditions)
.

Let
\Omega
be bounded, smooth, and convex.
Then:

\forall\;E\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\big(\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,\Omega)\cup\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\operatorname{div},\Omega)\big)\qquad\|\na E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\|\rot E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\operatorname{div}E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

Proof.

As
\Omega
is convex,
\na\nu
and
\operatorname{div}\nu
are non-negative,
as mentioned in the beginning of Section
3
.
Corollary
3.3
shows the assertion.
∎

Example 3.5

(Unit ball)
.

Let
\Omega=B_{3}\coloneqq B(0,1)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the Euclidean unit ball with boundary
\Gamma=S_{2}
.
Then for
x\neq 0

\displaystyle\nu(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{|x|},

\displaystyle\na\nu(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|x|^{3}}\begin{bmatrix}x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}&-x_{1}x_{2}&-x_{1}x_{3}\\
-x_{2}x_{1}&x_{1}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}&-x_{2}x_{3}\\
-x_{3}x_{1}&-x_{3}x_{2}&x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}\end{bmatrix}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\rot\nu(x)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\nu(x)

\displaystyle=\tr\na\nu(x)=\frac{2}{|x|}>0.

Hence Corollary
3.3
, cf. (
3
), (
5
), and Lemma
3.1
,
shows, e.g., for
E\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(B_{3})\cap\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,B_{3})

\displaystyle\|\na E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(B_{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\rot E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(B_{3})}^{2}+\|\operatorname{div}E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(B_{3})}^{2}-2\int_{S_{2}}|E_{\mathsf{n}}|^{2}.

Note that, e.g., for
E=\id
we get

3|B_{3}|=9|B_{3}|-2|S_{2}|
, i.e., the well-known result
|S_{2}|=3|B_{3}|
.

Finally, we address the prototype example for geometries with non-curved faces. The example deals with the geometric setting and the subsequent lemma proves the integration by parts formula in this geometry. The remarkable fact is that no curvature term appears and, thus, the Gaffney estimate becomes a mere equality.

Example 3.6

(Unit cube)
.

Let
\Omega=Q\coloneqq(0,1)^{3}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the unit cube with boundary and faces

\Gamma=\bigcup_{k=1}^{3}\big(\Gamma_{k,+}\cup\Gamma_{k,-}\big)\qquad\Gamma_{k,\pm}\coloneqq\{x\in\overline{Q}:2x_{k}=1\pm 1\},

and (almost everywhere defined) outward unit normal
\nu
given by

\nu|_{\Gamma_{k,\pm}}=:\nu^{k,\pm}=\pm e^{k}.

Note that
\nu_{l}|_{\Gamma_{k,\pm}}=\nu^{k,\pm}_{l}=\pm\delta_{lk}
.
Then for smooth vector fields
E

the tangential and normal components, e.g., on
\Gamma_{3,\pm}
are simply

E_{\mathsf{n}}=\pm e^{3}\cdot E=\pm E_{3},\quad E_{\mathsf{t}}=E-E_{3}=\begin{bmatrix}E_{\parallel}\\
0\end{bmatrix},\qquad E_{\parallel}\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}E_{1}\\
E_{2}\end{bmatrix},\quad E_{\bot}\coloneqq E_{\mathsf{n}}.

Hence, together with the surface gradient and divergence,
we have on
\Gamma_{k,\pm}

\displaystyle E_{\parallel}

\displaystyle\coloneqq E_{\parallel,k}\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}E_{n}\\
E_{m}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}_{\parallel}E_{\parallel}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\operatorname{div}_{\parallel,k}E_{\parallel,k}\coloneqq\p_{n}E_{n}+\p_{m}E_{m},

\displaystyle E_{\bot}

\displaystyle\coloneqq E_{\bot,k}\coloneqq\pm E_{k},

\displaystyle\na_{\bot}E_{\bot}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\na_{\bot,k}E_{\bot,k}\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}\p_{n}E_{\bot}\\
\p_{m}E_{\bot}\end{bmatrix}=\pm\begin{bmatrix}\p_{n}E_{k}\\
\p_{m}E_{k}\end{bmatrix}

for
\{k,n,m\}=\{1,2,3\}
,
n<m
.

We conclude this section with an integration by parts formula particularly valid for the unit cube.

Lemma 3.7

(integration by parts for the unit cube)
.

Let
E,F\in\mathsf{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then

\langle\na E,\na F\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}=\langle\rot E,\rot F\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}+\langle\operatorname{div}E,\operatorname{div}F\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}+\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{\Gamma}(E,F),

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{\Gamma}
is a boundary integral given by

\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{\Gamma}(E,F)\coloneqq\langle E_{\parallel},\na_{\bot}F_{\bot}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}-\langle E_{\bot},\operatorname{div}_{\parallel}F_{\parallel}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}.

Proof.

By (
3
) we just have to compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(E,F)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\int_{\Gamma}\Big(\nu_{l}E_{k}\p_{k}F_{l}-\nu_{k}E_{k}\p_{l}F_{l}\Big)

\displaystyle\;=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\sum_{k\neq l=1}^{3}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{\Gamma_{j,\pm}}\Big(\nu^{j,\pm}_{l}E_{k}\p_{k}F_{l}-\nu^{j,\pm}_{k}E_{k}\p_{l}F_{l}\Big)

\displaystyle\;=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\sum_{k\neq l=1}^{3}\Big(\pm\int_{\Gamma_{l,\pm}}E_{k}\p_{k}F_{l}\mp\int_{\Gamma_{k,\pm}}E_{k}\p_{l}F_{l}\Big)

\displaystyle\;=\pm\langle E_{1},\p_{1}F_{2}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{2,\pm})}\pm\langle E_{1},\p_{1}F_{3}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{3,\pm})}\mp\langle E_{1},\p_{2}F_{2}+\p_{3}F_{3}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{1,\pm})}

\displaystyle\qquad\pm\langle E_{2},\p_{2}F_{1}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{1,\pm})}\pm\langle E_{2},\p_{2}F_{3}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{3,\pm})}\mp\langle E_{2},\p_{1}F_{1}+\p_{3}F_{3}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{2,\pm})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\pm\langle E_{3},\p_{3}F_{1}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{1,\pm})}\pm\langle E_{3},\p_{3}F_{2}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{2,\pm})}\mp\langle E_{3},\p_{1}F_{1}+\p_{2}F_{2}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{3,\pm})}

\displaystyle\;=\pm\Big\langle\begin{bmatrix}E_{2}\\
E_{3}\end{bmatrix},\na_{2,3}F_{1}\Big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{1,\pm})}\mp\Big\langle E_{1},\operatorname{div}_{2,3}\begin{bmatrix}F_{2}\\
F_{3}\end{bmatrix}\Big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{1,\pm})}

\displaystyle\qquad\pm\Big\langle\begin{bmatrix}E_{1}\\
E_{3}\end{bmatrix},\na_{1,3}F_{2}\Big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{2,\pm})}\mp\Big\langle E_{2},\operatorname{div}_{1,3}\begin{bmatrix}F_{1}\\
F_{3}\end{bmatrix}\Big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{2,\pm})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\pm\Big\langle\begin{bmatrix}E_{1}\\
E_{2}\end{bmatrix},\na_{1,2}F_{3}\Big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{3,\pm})}\mp\Big\langle E_{3},\operatorname{div}_{1,2}\begin{bmatrix}F_{1}\\
F_{2}\end{bmatrix}\Big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{3,\pm})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\Big(\langle E_{\parallel},\na_{\bot}F_{\bot}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{k,\pm})}-\langle E_{\bot},\operatorname{div}_{\parallel}F_{\parallel}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{k,\pm})}\Big)=\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{\Gamma}(E,F),

completing the proof.
∎

4.
Regularity and Gaffney’s Inequality for Convex Domains

The following result is rooted in
[
38
, Lemma 2.1]
apparently due to discussions with Rolf Leis.
It has also been used in higher-dimensional situations, see
[
41
, Lemma 3.9]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathsf{H}_{0}
,
\mathsf{H}_{1}
, and
\mathsf{H}_{2}
be Hilbert spaces.
Moreover, let
\operatorname{A}_{0}:D(\operatorname{A}_{0})\subseteq\mathsf{H}_{0}\to\mathsf{H}_{1}

and
\operatorname{A}_{1}:D(\operatorname{A}_{1})\subseteq\mathsf{H}_{1}\to\mathsf{H}_{2}

be two densely defined and closed linear operators
satisfying the complex property
R(\operatorname{A}_{0})\subseteq N(\operatorname{A}_{1})
.
Let

\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}\coloneqq\id-\operatorname{A}_{0}(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\operatorname{A}_{0}+1)^{-1}\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}:D(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*})\to D(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*})

and
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq D_{1,0}\coloneqq D(\operatorname{A}_{1})\cap D(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*})
.
Then:

(i)

\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}[D_{1,0}]\subseteq D_{1,0}
.

(ii)

If
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}
is dense in
D(\operatorname{A}_{1})
,
then
\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}[\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}]
is dense in
D_{1,0}
.

(iii)

For
y\in D_{1,0}
, we have

\|\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y\|_{D_{1,0}}\leq\|y\|_{{\operatorname{A}_{1}}}
.

Here,
D(\operatorname{A}_{1})
,
D(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*})
, and
D_{1,0}
are endowed with the graph inner products

\displaystyle\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{{\operatorname{A}_{1}}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\mathsf{H}_{1}}+\langle\operatorname{A}_{1}\,\cdot\,,\operatorname{A}_{1}\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\mathsf{H}_{2}},

\displaystyle\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{{\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\mathsf{H}_{1}}+\langle\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\,\cdot\,,\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\mathsf{H}_{0}},

\displaystyle\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{D_{1,0}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\mathsf{H}_{1}}+\langle\operatorname{A}_{1}\,\cdot\,,\operatorname{A}_{1}\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\mathsf{H}_{2}}+\langle\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\,\cdot\,,\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\mathsf{H}_{0}},

and the Hilbert space adjoints are given by

\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}:D(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*})\subseteq\mathsf{H}_{1}\to\mathsf{H}_{0}

and
\operatorname{A}_{1}^{*}:D(\operatorname{A}_{1}^{*})\subseteq\mathsf{H}_{2}\to\mathsf{H}_{1}
.

Proof.

Note that by the Riesz’ representation theorem

\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\operatorname{A}_{0}+1:D(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\operatorname{A}_{0})\to\mathsf{H}_{0}
is a topological isomorphism.
Hence
(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\operatorname{A}_{0}+1)^{-1}[R(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*})]\subseteq D(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\operatorname{A}_{0})
and

\operatorname{A}_{0}(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\operatorname{A}_{0}+1)^{-1}R(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*})\subseteq D(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*})\cap N(\operatorname{A}_{1})

by the complex property.
Thus,
\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}[D_{1,0}]\subseteq D_{1,0}
,
i.e.,
D_{1,0}
is invariant under
\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}
,
showing (i).

(ii) Before we turn to the actual proof of (ii), we establish the following equality first:

(8)

\forall y,z\in D_{1,0}\,\qquad\langle z,\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y\rangle_{D_{1,0}}=\langle z,y\rangle_{\operatorname{A}_{1}}.

Indeed, let
y,z\in D_{1,0}
and put
\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y=y-\operatorname{A}_{0}(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\operatorname{A}_{0}+1)^{-1}\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}y\in D_{1,0}
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{A}_{1}\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y

\displaystyle=\operatorname{A}_{1}y\text{ and }

\displaystyle\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y

\displaystyle=\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}y-\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\operatorname{A}_{0}(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\operatorname{A}_{0}+1)^{-1}\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}y=(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\operatorname{A}_{0}+1)^{-1}\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}y.

Thus, from

\langle\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}z,\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y\rangle_{\mathsf{H}_{0}}=\big\langle z,\operatorname{A}_{0}(\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\operatorname{A}_{0}+1)^{-1}\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}y\big\rangle_{\mathsf{H}_{1}}=\big\langle z,(1-\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}})y\big\rangle_{\mathsf{H}_{1}}

it follows that

\displaystyle\langle z,\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y\rangle_{D_{1,0}}

\displaystyle=\langle z,\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y\rangle_{\mathsf{H}_{1}}+\langle\operatorname{A}_{1}z,\operatorname{A}_{1}\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y\rangle_{\mathsf{H}_{2}}+\langle\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}z,\operatorname{A}_{0}^{*}\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y\rangle_{\mathsf{H}_{0}}

\displaystyle=\langle z,y\rangle_{\mathsf{H}_{1}}+\langle\operatorname{A}_{1}z,\operatorname{A}_{1}y\rangle_{\mathsf{H}_{2}}=\langle z,y\rangle_{\operatorname{A}_{1}},

as desired.

Next, we turn to the proof of (ii). For this let
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}
be dense in
D(\operatorname{A}_{1})
and take
z\in D_{1,0}\cap(\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}[\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}])^{\bot_{D_{1,0}}}
.
Then, for all
\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y\in\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq D_{1,0}

with
y\in\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}
, using (
8
), we get

0=\langle z,\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y\rangle_{D_{1,0}}=\langle z,y\rangle_{\operatorname{A}_{1}},

and, as
\operatorname{\mathcal{D}}
is dense in
D(\operatorname{A}_{1})
, we conclude
z=0
.

(iii)
Let
y\in D_{1,0}
.
Then by (
8
) with
z=\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y

\|\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y\|_{D_{1,0}}^{2}=\langle\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y,y\rangle_{{\operatorname{A}_{1}}}\leq\|\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y\|_{{\operatorname{A}_{1}}}\|y\|_{\operatorname{A}_{1}}\leq\|\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y\|_{D_{1,0}}\|y\|_{{\operatorname{A}_{1}}},

i.e.,
\|\operatorname{P}_{\operatorname{A}_{0}}y\|_{D_{1,0}}\leq\|y\|_{{\operatorname{A}_{1}}}
.
∎

The latter density result may now be used to prove Gaffney’s inequality in the smooth bounded domain case.

Lemma 4.2

(Gaffney’s inequality for bounded, smooth, and convex domains)
.

Let
\Omega
be bounded, smooth, and convex.
Then
\Omega
is a Gaffney domain:
If
E\in\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\mathsf{H}(\operatorname{div},\Omega)

or
E\in\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\operatorname{div},\Omega)
,
then
E\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and

\|\na E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\|\rot E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\operatorname{div}E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Proof.

Note that it suffices to prove the regularity statement. Indeed, then by Corollary
3.4
the desired estimate also follows. For the regularity statement, we use Lemma
4.1

Theorem

states that bounded and convex domains are Gaffney domains.
The next result enables us to transition from bounded to possibly unbounded domains.

Lemma 4.4

(permanence principle for (exact) Gaffney domains)
.

Let
\Omega^{\star}\subseteq\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
be open, bounded, and star-shaped
with center
x_{\star}\in\Omega^{\star}
.
Define
\Omega^{\star}_{r}\coloneqq x_{\star}+r(\Omega^{\star}-x_{\star})
for all
r>0
.

If
\Omega_{r}\coloneqq\Omega^{\star}_{r}\cap\Omega
is a (exact) Gaffney domain for all
r
,
then so is
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
E\in\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\mathsf{H}(\operatorname{div},\Omega)

or
E\in\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\operatorname{div},\Omega)
.
Without loss of generality,
x_{\star}=0
.
As
\Omega^{\star}_{1}=\Omega^{\star}
is open, bounded, and star-shaped,
so is
\Omega^{\star}_{1/2}
.
Moreover, it is compactly contained in
\Omega^{\star}_{1}
.
Thus, there exists
\varphi\in\mathring{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{3},[0,1]\big)
such that

\varphi|_{\Omega^{\star}_{1/2}}=1
and
\varphi|_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\Omega^{\star}_{2/3}}=0

Put
\varphi_{r}\coloneqq\varphi(\,\cdot\,/r)
for

.
Then
\varphi_{r}|_{\Omega^{\star}_{r/2}}=1

and
\varphi_{r}|_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\Omega^{\star}_{2r/3}}=0
.
Note that
\supp\na\varphi_{r}\subseteq\overline{\Omega}^{\star}_{2r/3}\setminus\Omega^{\star}_{r/2}

and
|\na\varphi_{r}|\leq c/r
.
For all
r
the product rules

\displaystyle\p_{j}(\varphi_{r}E)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{r}\p_{j}E+(\p_{j}\varphi_{r})E,

\displaystyle\rot(\varphi_{r}E)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{r}\rot E+(\na\varphi_{r})\times E,

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}(\varphi_{r}E)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{r}\operatorname{div}E+(\na\varphi_{r})\cdot E

imply that

\varphi_{r}E\in\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,\Omega_{r})\cap\mathsf{H}(\operatorname{div},\Omega_{r})

or
\varphi_{r}E\in\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Omega_{r})\cap\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\operatorname{div},\Omega_{r})
.
Since
\Omega_{r}
is a Gaffney domain, we get

(15)

\displaystyle\big\|\na(\varphi_{r}E)\big\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\Big(\big\|\rot(\varphi_{r}E)\big\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big\|\operatorname{div}(\varphi_{r}E)\big\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big).

In particular, we have
E\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega_{r})
for all
r
.
Then (with

15
)

(16)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\varphi_{r}\na E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}&\leq c\Big(\big\|\na(\varphi_{r}E)\big\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\big\|(\p_{j}\varphi_{r})E\big\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)\\

If (

Exact Gaffney Domains

Although not being relevant for our results,
we note the following facts about exactness:
Using the particular integration by parts result for cubes,
Lemma
3.7

which can be generalised to polyhedrons
with some additional technical and notational efforts,
and a sophisticated investigation of the
surface differential operators

\na_{\bot}
and
\operatorname{div}_{\parallel}

(continuous extensions of them),
it is possible to give proper meaning to the boundary term

\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}_{\Gamma}(E,F)=\langle E_{\parallel},\na_{\bot}F_{\bot}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}-\langle E_{\bot},\operatorname{div}_{\parallel}F_{\parallel}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}

even for vector fields

belonging merely to
\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
,
see, e.g.,
[
6
]
.
More precisely:

Lemma 4.8

(
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded polyhedron,
and let
E,F\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
. Then

\langle\na E,\na F\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}=\langle\rot E,\rot F\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}+\langle\operatorname{div}E,\operatorname{div}F\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}+\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{\Gamma}(E,F),

where

\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{\Gamma}(E,F)\coloneqq\langle E_{\mathsf{t}},\na_{\mathsf{t}}F_{\mathsf{n}}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}-\langle E_{\mathsf{n}},\operatorname{div}_{\mathsf{t}}F_{\mathsf{t}}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}

is given as sum over all faces of
\Gamma
.

It turns out that
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{\Gamma}(E,F)
still vanishes,
if
E,F
belong additionally also to
\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,\Omega)
or
\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\operatorname{div},\Omega)
,
in particular, for
E\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,\Omega)

or

we obtain

\|\na E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\|\rot E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\operatorname{div}E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Hence, bounded and convex polyhedrons are exact Gaffney domains.
Note that the convexity is still needed for the regularity part of the result.

For the cube there is another elementary way:
By the tensor structure of a cube
Q

products of sine and cosine functions,
as eigenfunctions of Laplacians on the unit interval,
form a complete orthonormal system in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)

yielding dense subsets for the different boundary conditions.
This, together with the density lemma
(the abstract result Lemma
4.1
)
shows that cubes are exact Gaffney domains.

Together with Lemma
4.4
we conclude:

Corollary 4.9

(exact Gaffney domains)
.

All (possibly unbounded) convex polyhedrons are exact Gaffney domains.
In particular,

\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,1)

We finally turn to closed range results using Gaffney’s inequality. In particular, we will carry out the strategy sketched in the introduction. Here we focus on cuboids and discuss the validity of a Friedrichs type estimate first.

Cuboids

For

\db_{Q}\coloneqq\#\big\{j\in\{1,2,3\}:\ell_{j}<\infty\big\}

with the usual convention
\infty-(-\infty)=\infty

Instrumental in the proof of the closed range result is the following variant of Friedrichs’ estimate.
For
\db_{Q}\geq 1

Lemma 5.1

(Friedrichs estimate)

Let

Proof.

By a density argument, it suffices to establish the inequality for
u\in\mathring{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(a_{3},\infty)\big)
.
Then

u(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\int_{a_{3}}^{x_{3}}\p_{3}u(x_{1},x_{2},\,\cdot\,),\qquad x_{j}\in I_{j}.

Thus

\displaystyle\big|u(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\big|^{2}\leq(x_{3}-a_{3})\int_{a_{3}}^{b_{3}}\big|\p_{3}u(x_{1},x_{2},\,\cdot\,)\big|^{2}
,
which implies

(18)

\displaystyle\int_{a_{3}}^{b_{3}}\big|u(x_{1},x_{2},\,\cdot\,)\big|^{2}\leq\frac{\ell_{3}^{2}}{2}\int_{a_{3}}^{b_{3}}\big|\p_{3}u(x_{1},x_{2},\,\cdot\,)\big|^{2}.

Integration over
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
shows

\|u\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}^{2}\leq c_{3}^{2}\|\p_{3}u\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}^{2}
.

Proof.

from

).
Then
\|u_{n}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\sim n^{N}

and
\|\na u_{n}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\sim n^{N-1}
.
Thus, a Friedrichs type estimate cannot hold,
and
R(\na_{\mathbb{R}^{N}})
is not closed.
∎

By Lemma
5.1
, Lemma
5.2
,
and Banach’s closed range theorem we have:

Theorem 5.3

(closed range of the gradient)
.

Let
Q
be as in (
17
). Then

R(\nac_{Q})\text{ closed}\;\Leftrightarrow\;R(\operatorname{div}_{Q})\text{ closed}\;\Leftrightarrow\;\db_{Q}\geq 1.

Thus, it suffices to consider
\rotc
and
\divc
in the following.

Theorem 5.4

(closed range of the rotation)
.

Let
Q
be as in (
17
). Then

R(\rotc_{Q})\text{ closed}\;\Leftrightarrow\;R(\rot_{Q})\text{ closed}\;\Leftrightarrow\;\db_{Q}\geq 2.

Proof.

The closed range theorem yields the equivalence first.
In order to show a closed range result for
\rotc
, by Theorem
1.1
,
it suffices to find
c>0
such that for all
E\in D(\rotc)\cap N(\rotc)^{\bot}

\|E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}\leq c\|\rotc E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}.

Thus, let
E\in D(\rotc)\cap N(\rotc)^{\bot}
.
As

N(\rotc)^{\bot}=\overline{R(\rot)}\subseteq N(\operatorname{div})\subseteq D(\operatorname{div})
,
we deduce that
E\in D(\rotc)\cap N(\operatorname{div})
.
By Theorem
4.5
we infer
E\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(Q)
and

(20)

\|\na E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}\leq\big(\|\rotc E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\|\operatorname{div}E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}^{2}\big)^{1/2}=\|\rotc E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}.

Next, if
\db_{Q}\geq 2
, we may assume without loss of generality, that
\ell_{2},\ell_{3}<\infty
.
We note that
E\in\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,Q)\cap\mathsf{H}^{1}(Q)
.
Hence, we may evaluate
E
at the boundary to deduce

E_{1}=E_{2}=0
on
I_{1}\times I_{2}\times\{a_{3}\}
and

E_{1}=E_{3}=0
on
I_{1}\times\{a_{2}\}\times I_{3}
.
Lemma
5.1
shows

\displaystyle\|E_{1}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq c_{3}\|\na E_{1}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)},

\displaystyle\|E_{3}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\|\na E_{3}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)},

\displaystyle\|E_{2}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq c_{3}\|\na E_{2}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)},

and thus

\|E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}\leq\max\{c_{2},c_{3}\}\|\na E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}
.
Finally, (
20
) yields the closed range estimate for
\rotc
,
which completes the main part of the proof.

For the remaining part, let
\db_{Q}<2
, and,
without loss of generality,
\ell_{1}=\ell_{2}=b_{1}=b_{2}=\infty
.
Note that, due to the Helmholtz decomposition,

(21)

\displaystyle N(\rotc)^{\bot}=\overline{R(\rot)}=N(\operatorname{div})\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{D}}(\Omega)=N(\operatorname{div}),

as
Q
is convex, by Corollary
4.6
.
To contradict the closed range, we define a sequences

(E_{n})_{n}\text{ in }\mathsf{H}^{1}(Q)\cap D(\rotc)\cap N(\operatorname{div})

for either remaining cases.

We start out with
\db_{Q}=1
, i.e.,
\ell_{3}<\infty
. We define
E_{n}
by
E_{n}(x)\coloneqq f_{n}\big(|x^{\prime}|\big)e^{3}
with
f_{n}
as in (
19
), the third unit vector
e^{3}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
x^{\prime}=[x_{1}\;x_{2}]^{\top}
.
Then
\operatorname{div}E_{n}=0
and

\rot E_{n}(x)=\frac{f_{n}^{\prime}\big(|x^{\prime}|\big)}{|x^{\prime}|}\begin{bmatrix}x_{2}\\
-x_{1}\\
0\end{bmatrix},

and
\|E_{n}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}^{2}\sim n^{2}

and
\|\rotc E_{n}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}\sim\beta n
.

Finally, we address
\db_{Q}=0
, i.e.,
\ell_{3}=\infty
.
With
f_{n}
as in (
19
) we put
g_{n}
by
g_{n}(t)\coloneqq\int^{t}_{0}f_{n}
. Define
E_{n}\coloneqq\rot H_{n}
with
H_{n}(x)\coloneqq g_{n}\big(|x|\big)e^{3}
.
Then
\operatorname{div}E_{n}=0
and

E_{n}(x)=\frac{f_{n}\big(|x|\big)}{|x|}\begin{bmatrix}x_{2}\\
-x_{1}\\
0\end{bmatrix},\qquad\rot E_{n}(x)=\frac{f_{n}\big(|x|\big)}{|x|^{3}}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}x_{3}\\
-x_{2}x_{3}\\
|x^{\prime}|^{2}-2|x|^{2}\end{bmatrix}-\frac{f_{n}^{\prime}\big(|x|\big)}{|x|^{2}}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}x_{3}\\
-x_{2}x_{3}\\
|x^{\prime}|^{2}\end{bmatrix}.

Hence

\displaystyle\|E_{n}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{Q}f_{n}^{2}\big(|x|\big)\frac{|x^{\prime}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\geq 2\pi\int_{2}^{n}\int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4}r^{2}\cos^{3}\theta\,d\theta\,dr\sim n^{3},

\displaystyle\|\rot E_{n}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q}\Big(\frac{f_{n}^{2}\big(|x|\big)}{|x|^{2}}+(f_{n}^{\prime})^{2}\big(|x|\big)\Big)\sim n^{2}.

Thus, in both cases, a closed range estimate for
\rotc
cannot hold,
and
R(\rotc_{Q})
is not closed.
∎

(closed range of the divergence)

An Example with Mixed Boundary Conditions

Another detailed look into the proof of Theorem
5.4
shows that,
if mixed boundary conditions are asked for,
then the respective
\rot
can be established to have closed range,
even though only

\db_{Q}=1<2.

In order to keep this exposition as focussed as possible, we shall introduce mixed boundary conditions in a rather ad-hoc way.
We refer to the literature for the proper set-up and the corresponding Hilbert complex structure,
see, in particular,
[
16
]
.

Let
Q\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,1)
, i.e.,
\db_{Q}=1
,
and let
\Gamma_{0}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\{0\}
and
\Gamma_{1}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\{1\}
.
With the help of test fields

\mathsf{C}^{\infty}_{\Gamma_{\ell}}(Q)\coloneqq\big\{\phi|_{Q}:\phi\in\mathring{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,\wedge\,\dist(\supp\phi,\Gamma_{\ell})>0\big\}

we define restrictions
\rot_{0}
and
\operatorname{div}_{1}
of
\rot
and
\operatorname{div}
by

D(\rot_{0})\coloneqq\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{0}}(\rot,Q)\coloneqq\overline{\mathsf{C}^{\infty}_{\Gamma_{0}}(Q)}^{\mathsf{H}(\rot,Q)},\qquad D(\operatorname{div}_{1})\coloneqq\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{1}}(\operatorname{div},Q)\coloneqq\overline{\mathsf{C}^{\infty}_{\Gamma_{1}}(Q)}^{\mathsf{H}(\operatorname{div},Q)},

realising mixed homogeneous boundary conditions.
We show that

R(\rot_{0})\text{ is closed.}

Indeed,
\rot_{0}
and
\operatorname{div}_{1}
are densely defined and closed.
It follows from
[
16
]
that
\rot_{0}^{*}=\rot_{1}

and that the complex property also holds for mixed boundary conditions, i.e.,

N(\rot_{0})^{\bot}=\overline{R(\rot_{1})}\subseteq N(\operatorname{div}_{1}).

Thus, again, it suffices to establish Gaffney’s inequality for

E\in D(\rot_{0})\cap D(\operatorname{div}_{1})
and to show that

E
satisfies boundary conditions allowing
for Friedrichs’ estimate to hold (Lemma
5.1
).

Let
E\in D(\rot_{0})\cap N(\rot_{0})^{\bot}\subseteq D(\rot_{0})\cap N(\operatorname{div}_{1})
.
Let
\phi\in\mathsf{C}^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{3},[0,1]\big)

with
\phi=1
near
\Gamma_{0}
and
\phi=0
near
\Gamma_{1}
.
Then
E=\phi E+(1-\phi)E

as well as (by mollification)

\phi E\in\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,Q)\cap\mathsf{H}(\operatorname{div},Q)

and
(1-\phi)E\in\mathsf{H}(\rot,Q)\cap\mathring{\mathsf{H}}(\operatorname{div},Q)
.
As
Q
is convex Theorem
4.5
yields

\phi E,\,(1-\phi)E\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(Q)
, that is,

E\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(Q)
.
Similar to the proof of Lemma
4.4
, let

\varphi\in\mathring{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{3},[0,1]\big)
such that

\varphi|_{B(0,1)}=1
and
\varphi|_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,2)}=0
,
and put
\varphi_{r}\coloneqq\varphi(\,\cdot\,/r)
for
r>0
.
Then
\varphi_{r}|_{B(0,r)}=1

and
\varphi_{r}|_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,2r)}=0
.
Note that
\supp\na\varphi_{r}\subseteq\overline{B(0,2r)}\setminus B(0,r)

and
|\na\varphi_{r}|\leq c/r
.
Lemma
4.8
shows

\big\|\na(\varphi_{r}E)\big\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}^{2}=\big\|\rot(\varphi_{r}E)\big\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\big\|\operatorname{div}(\varphi_{r}E)\big\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}^{2}

(integration just over
Q\cap(-3r,3r)^{3}
,
flat boundaries, and mixed boundary conditions on the particular faces).
Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem
together with the product rules yields for
r\to\infty

\|\na E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}^{2}=\|\rot E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\|\operatorname{div}E\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(Q)}^{2},

cf. (
15
) and (
16
).
The tangential boundary condition implies
E_{1}=E_{2}=0
at
\Gamma_{0}

and the normal boundary condition shows
E_{3}=0
at
\Gamma_{1}
.
Thus, in any case,
E_{j}
satisfies the Friedrichs estimate from Lemma
5.1
,
and the closed range inequality for
\rot_{0}
follows by
\operatorname{div}E=0
.

6.
Global Lipschitz Domains

In this section we turn to domains that are not necessarily cubes anymore.
Let
\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
be open, and let

\Phi:\Theta\to\Omega\coloneqq\Phi(\Theta)

be an
admissible bi-Lipschitz transformation
,
cf. Appendix
A
.
The next theorem asserts that admissible transformations preserve closedness of the range. The domains in question are called global Lipschitz domains defined as follows.
We say
\Omega
is a
global strong Lipschitz domain
,
if there exists an open cuboid
\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
and an admissible bi-Lipschitz transformation
\Phi

such that
\Phi(\Theta)=\Omega
.
Correspondingly, we define the
number of directions of boundedness
by

\db_{\Omega}\coloneqq\db_{Q}
.

Theorem 6.1

(closed range invariance)
.

Let
\Omega,\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
be open and
\Phi\colon\Theta\to\Omega
be an admissible bi-Lipschitz transformation.
Then

R(\rot_{\Omega})\text{ closed}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad R(\rot_{\Theta})\text{ closed}.

The corresponding results also hold for
R(\na_{\Omega})
,
R(\operatorname{div}_{\Omega})
,
and
R(\nac_{\Omega})
,
R(\rotc_{\Omega})
,
R(\divc_{\Omega})
.

Proof.

Assume that
R(\rot_{\Theta})
is closed, and
let
(E_{n})_{n}
in
D(\rot_{\Omega})=\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Omega)
be a sequence such that

\rot E_{n}\to F
in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
for some
F\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
By Theorem
A.1

\tau^{1}_{\Phi}E_{n}\in\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Theta)

and

\rot\tau^{1}_{\Phi}E_{n}=\tau^{2}_{\Phi}\rot E_{n}\to\tau^{2}_{\Phi}F

in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Theta)
.
As
R(\rot_{\Theta})
is closed we get

\tau^{2}_{\Phi}F=\rot H\in R(\rot_{\Theta})

with
H\in D(\rot_{\Theta})=\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Theta)
.
Then
\tau^{1}_{\Phi^{-1}}H\in\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Omega)=D(\rot_{\Omega})

and
\rot\tau^{1}_{\Phi^{-1}}H=\tau^{2}_{\Phi^{-1}}\rot H=F

by Theorem
A.1

and thus
F\in R(\rot_{\Omega})
.
Similarly, we see the corresponding results for
R(\na_{\Omega})
and
R(\operatorname{div}_{\Omega})
.

The remaining assertions follow analogously
or from the closed range theorem.
∎

It is not difficult to see that
\db_{\Omega}
does not depend on the particular choice of
Q
and
\Phi
.
In fact, this is due to the fact that bounded intervals and unbounded intervals
cannot be mapped in a bi-Lipschitz way onto another.

Theorem 6.2

(main theorem)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a global strong Lipschitz domain.
Then

\displaystyle R(\nac_{\Omega})

closed

\displaystyle\;\Leftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle R(\operatorname{div}_{\Omega})

closed

\displaystyle\;\Leftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle\db_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\geq 1;

\displaystyle R(\rotc_{\Omega})

closed

\displaystyle\;\Leftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle R(\rot_{\Omega})

closed

\displaystyle\;\Leftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle\db_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\geq 2;

\displaystyle R(\divc_{\Omega})

closed

\displaystyle\;\Leftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle R(\na_{\Omega})

closed

\displaystyle\;\Leftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle\db_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=3.

Proof.

The statements follow from Banach’s closed range theorem
and the characterisations in Section
5
together with the invariance of closed ranges from Theorem
6.1
.
∎

We provide some admissible transformations
such that Theorem
6.2
is applicable.

(convex bodies)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
be open, bounded, and convex.
Then there exists a bi-Lipschitz map
\Phi:Q\coloneqq(-1,1)^{3}\to\Omega
,
cf.
[
9
]
,
which can be extended to an admissible bi-Lipschitz transformation.
Hence,
\nac_{\Omega}
,
\operatorname{div}_{\Omega}
,
\rotc_{\Omega}
,
\rot_{\Omega}
,
\divc_{\Omega}
,
\na_{\Omega}
have closed range.

(infinite L-shaped pipe)
.

Let
\Phi:Q\to\Omega=\Phi(Q)

with

Q\coloneqq\mathbb{R}\times(0,1)^{2},\qquad\Phi(r,t,s)\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}r\\
|r|+t\\
t+s\end{bmatrix}.

Then
\det\Phi(r,t,s)=1
and
\db_{Q}=2
.
Hence,
\Phi
is admissible,
and
\nac_{\Omega}
,
\operatorname{div}_{\Omega}
,
\rotc_{\Omega}
,

have closed range.

Appendix A
The Lipschitz Transformation Theorem

Let
\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
be open
and let
\Phi\in\mathsf{C}^{0,1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
be such that its restriction to
\Theta
, still denoted by

\Phi:\Theta\to\Phi(\Theta)=:\Omega,

is bi-Lipschitz, bounded, and regular, i.e.,

\Phi\in\mathsf{C}^{0,1}_{\mathsf{bd}}(\overline{\Theta},\overline{\Omega})

and
\Phi^{-1}\in\mathsf{C}^{0,1}_{\mathsf{bd}}(\overline{\Omega},\overline{\Theta})
with

J_{\Phi}=\Phi^{\prime}=(\na\Phi)^{\top},\qquad\det J_{\Phi}>0.

Such regular bi-Lipschitz transformations
\Phi
will be called
admissible
.
For admissible
\Phi
the inverse and adjunct matrix of
J_{\Phi}
shall be denoted by

J_{\Phi}^{-1},\qquad\operatorname{adj}J_{\Phi}\coloneqq(\det J_{\Phi})J_{\Phi}^{-1},

respectively.
We denote the composition with
\Phi

by tilde, i.e., for any tensor field
v
we define

\widetilde{v}\coloneqq v\circ\Phi.

We introduce a new notation

\dot{\mathsf{H}}=\mathring{\mathsf{H}}\quad\text{or}\quad\dot{\mathsf{H}}=\mathsf{H}

to handle spaces with and without boundary conditions simultaneously.

In the following, let
\Phi
be admissible.

Theorem A.1

(transformation theorem)
.

Let
u\in\dot{\mathsf{H}}^{1}(\Omega)
,
E\in\dot{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,\Omega)
, and
H\in\dot{\mathsf{H}}(\operatorname{div},\Omega)
.
Then

\displaystyle\tau^{0}_{\Phi}u\coloneqq\widetilde{u}

\displaystyle\in\dot{\mathsf{H}}^{1}(\Theta)

and

\displaystyle\na\tau^{0}_{\Phi}u

\displaystyle=\tau^{1}_{\Phi}\na u,

\displaystyle\tau^{1}_{\Phi}E\coloneqq J_{\Phi}^{\top}\widetilde{E}

\displaystyle\in\dot{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,\Theta)

and

\displaystyle\rot\tau^{1}_{\Phi}E

\displaystyle=\tau^{2}_{\Phi}\rot E,

\displaystyle\tau^{2}_{\Phi}H\coloneqq(\operatorname{adj}J_{\Phi})\widetilde{H}

\displaystyle\in\dot{\mathsf{H}}(\operatorname{div},\Theta)

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\tau^{2}_{\Phi}H

\displaystyle=\tau^{3}_{\Phi}\operatorname{div}H

with
\tau^{3}_{\Phi}f\coloneqq(\det J_{\Phi})\widetilde{f}=(\det J_{\Phi})\tau^{0}_{\Phi}f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Theta)

for
f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
Moroever,

\displaystyle\tau^{0}_{\Phi}:\dot{\mathsf{H}}^{1}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\to\dot{\mathsf{H}}^{1}(\Theta),

\displaystyle\tau^{1}_{\Phi}:\dot{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,\Omega)

\displaystyle\to\dot{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,\Theta),

\displaystyle\tau^{3}_{\Phi}:\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\to\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Theta),

\displaystyle\tau^{2}_{\Phi}:\dot{\mathsf{H}}(\operatorname{div},\Omega)

\displaystyle\to\dot{\mathsf{H}}(\operatorname{div},\Theta)

are topological isomorphisms with norms depending on
\Theta

and
J_{\Phi}
only. The inverse operators and the
\mathsf{L}^{2}
-adjoints,
i.e., the Hilbert space adjoints of

\tau^{q}_{\Phi}:\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Theta)
,
q\in\{0,1,2,3\}
,
are given by

(\tau^{q}_{\Phi})^{-1}=\tau^{q}_{\Phi^{-1}},\qquad(\tau^{q}_{\Phi})^{*}=\tau^{3-q}_{\Phi^{-1}}.

A proof for differential forms
can be found in the appendix of
[
4
]
.

Proof.

We use Rademacher’s theorem for Lipschitz functions, that is,
any Lipschitz continuous function is differentiable almost everywhere
with uniformly bounded derivative.

in

.

\widetilde{\na u^{\ell}}\to\widetilde{\na u}
in

by the standard transformation theorem.
We have

\widetilde{u^{\ell}}\in\dot{\mathsf{C}}^{0,1}(\Theta)\subseteq\dot{\mathsf{H}}^{1}(\Theta)

by (
22

\widetilde{u^{\ell}}\to\widetilde{u},\quad\na\widetilde{u^{\ell}}=J_{\Phi}^{\top}\widetilde{\na u^{\ell}}\to J_{\Phi}^{\top}\widetilde{\na u}\qquad\text{in }\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Theta).

is closed,
we conclude
\widetilde{u}\in\dot{\mathsf{H}}^{1}(\Theta)
and

\na\widetilde{u}=J_{\Phi}^{\top}\widetilde{\na u}.

Next, we consider the

\na

\displaystyle\rot(J_{\Phi}^{\top}\widetilde{E})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\na\widetilde{E}_{n}\times\na\Phi_{n}=\sum_{n}(J_{\Phi}^{\top}\widetilde{\na E_{n}})\times\na\Phi_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\big([\na\Phi_{1}\;\na\Phi_{2}\;\na\Phi_{3}]\,\widetilde{\na E_{n}}\big)\times\na\Phi_{n}

we conclude
J_{\Phi}^{\top}\widetilde{E}\in\dot{\mathsf{H}}(\rot,\Theta)
and

\rot(J_{\Phi}^{\top}\widetilde{E})=(\operatorname{adj}J_{\Phi})\,\widetilde{\rot E}.

For the divergence,

\displaystyle(\operatorname{adj}J_{\Phi})\widetilde{H}

Remark A.2

(transformation theorem)
.

More explicitly, Theorem
A.1
shows:

\displaystyle\forall\,u

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}

\displaystyle\na\widetilde{u}
