Pell-Padovan tetranacci numbers and their Hadamard product with classical sequences
Helmut Prodinger

Abstract
. A quick way to compute generating functions related to Pell-Padovan tetranacci numbers and classical sequences of recursions of order two is provided. Eight special instances can be computed at once.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
05A15

The sequence of
Pell-Padovan tetranacci numbers

\mathscr{P}_{n+4}=\mathscr{P}_{n+2}+2\mathscr{P}_{n+1}+\mathscr{P}_{n},\quad\mathscr{P}_{0}=0,\;\mathscr{P}_{1}=1,\;\mathscr{P}_{2}=1,\;\mathscr{P}_{3}=1,

is the main interest in
[
2
]
.
The generating function is

\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{P}_{n}z^{n}=\frac{z(1+z)}{1-z^{2}-2z^{3}-z^{4}}.

The Hadamard product of two generating functions is defined by

\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}z^{n}\odot\sum_{n\geq 0}g_{n}z^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}g_{n}z^{n}.

The paper
[
2
]
computes

\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{P}_{n}z^{n}\odot\sum_{n\geq 0}X_{n}z^{n}

for any of the 8 second order sequences in Table
1
, by using a method with symmetric functions and discussing each instance separately.
We consider generally

\frac{a+bz}{1+cd+dz^{2}}

for
\sum_{n\geq 0}X_{n}z^{n}
and cover all 8 cases in one go. We obtain

\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{P}_{n}z^{n}\odot\frac{a+bz}{1+cd+dz^{2}}=\frac{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{D}}

with

\mathscr{N}=b-ac+(-ad-cb+a{c}^{2})z+db{z}^{2}+d(ad-2cb){z}^{3}\\
-d(-db+dac-{c}^{2}b){z}^{4}+{d}^{2}(ad-cb){z}^{5}

and

\mathscr{D}=1+(-{c}^{2}+2d){z}^{2}+2c({c}^{2}-3d){z}^{3}\\
+(-{c}^{4}+4{c}^{2}d-{d}^{2}){z}^{4}-2c{d}^{2}{z}^{5}+{d}^{2}({c}^{2}+2d){z}^{6}-2c{d}^{3}{z}^{7}+{d}^{4}{z}^{8}.

Our method of choice is to use the Maple package
gfun
. It has various useful programs, like
‘hadamardproduct‘
,

‘rec*rec‘
etc. The easiest way to proceed seems to be to compute a list of, say, 30 coefficients of

\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{P}_{n}z^{n}\odot\frac{a+bz}{1+cd+dz^{2}}
and use
‘guessgf‘
; the program guesses than the generating function

\mathscr{N}/\mathscr{D}
as posted. This procedure is fully legitimate, as we know
a priori
that we will get a recursion of
order
4\times 2=8
. Knowing a sufficient number of initial coefficients, one knows that the guessed answer is indeed the correct answer.
I learnt this type of argument from Doron Zeilberger. Once one
has
the rational generating function there are indeed various ways
for justification.

Of course, this approach is not restricted to the sequences discussed in this note.
