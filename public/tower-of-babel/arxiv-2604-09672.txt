Sharp mean Hadamard inequalities and polyconvex integrands that give rise to convex functionals
Jonathan J. Bevan, Martin Kružík, Jan Valdman

Abstract
. We investigate several instances of the Hadamard inequality in the mean in two dimensions. As a consequence, we prove the uniqueness of minimizers of an integral functional with a polyconvex integrand, subject to mixed Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions. The theoretical findings are complemented by computational experiments that illustrate the behavior of the minimizers.

Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and let
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. This paper is concerned with the convexity of the functional

(1)

\displaystyle I(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+f(x)\det\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x

defined on
W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, which given its quadratic dependence on
\varphi
is equivalent to the condition

(2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+f(x)\det\nabla\varphi\,\,{\rm d}x\geq 0\quad\forall\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Inequality (
2
) is immediately implied by the pointwise Hadamard inequality for matrices

|A|^{2}\geq 2|\det A|\quad A\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}

for
f
with the property that

(3)

\displaystyle|f(x)-\langle f\rangle_{\Omega}|\leq 2\quad\mathrm{a.e.}\ x\in\Omega,

which follows by coupling (
3
) with the well-known fact that the function
A\mapsto\det A
is a null Lagrangian, cf. e.g.
[
5
]
. Here,

\langle f\rangle_{\Omega}:=\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}f(x)\,\,{\rm d}x

denotes the mean value of
f
over the domain
\Omega
, and
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\Omega)
is the two-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
\Omega
.

In fact, (
2
) can also be shown to hold even when condition (
3
) fails, which is how (
2
) earns its name of a ‘Hadamard-in-the-mean’ inequality, such as when

for any

and
\Omega^{\prime}
any reasonable subdomain of
\Omega
. See
[
3
, Proposition 3.4]
for details, including the use of ideas on quasiconvexity at the boundary due to Mielke and Sprenger
[

]
that are needed to show that the condition
|M|=4
is sharp.

In this and in other examples, we stress that although the integrand

is polyconvex
[
1
]
, it does not automatically follow that
I(\varphi)\geq I(0)=0
for any
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
. One reason is that we may not assume that

\int_{\Omega}W(x,\nabla\varphi)\,\,{\rm d}x\geq\int_{\Omega}W(x,0)\,\,{\rm d}x

holds in the case of an

[
3
, Proposition 6.2]
, a clear link is made between the sequential lower semicontinuity of

in
W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
and the nonnegativity of
I(\varphi)
for
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Nor are standard devices such as studying solutions of the Euler-Lagrange equations of any use. The positivity or otherwise of

Varying the width of the insulation layer

In this section we consider the effect of varying the width of the so-called insulation layer, which in Section

Evidence for this is supplied by Theorem

and Proposition

below, a consequence of which is the result that if

and the canonical domain is replaced with

\Omega_{N}:=\left(-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2N},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2N}\right)\times\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right),

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2N},-\frac{1}{2N}\right)\times\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\ \mathrm{and}

\displaystyle R_{2,N}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2N},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2N}\right)\times\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and set

(34)

\displaystyle M_{k}:=1-\frac{1}{k}+\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}+\frac{6}{k}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and set

. Then for every
u\in W^{1,2}(R_{-2}\cup R^{\delta};\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that

(35)

\displaystyle\int_{R_{-2}}|\nabla u|^{2}-M_{k}\det\nabla u\,{\rm d}x+\int_{R^{\delta}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x\geq 0.

Proof.

Assume for a contradiction that (
35
) is false for one of the
u
in the class described. Then
u
is not zero and

(36)

\displaystyle\int_{R^{\delta}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle<\int_{R_{-2}}M_{k}\det\nabla u-|\nabla u|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{R_{-2}}M_{k}\det\nabla u-2|\det\nabla u|\,\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq(M_{k}-2)\int_{R_{-2}}|\det\nabla u|\,\,{\rm d}x

(37)

\displaystyle\int_{R^{\delta}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M_{k}-2}{2}\int_{R_{-2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x,

where we have used Hadamard’s pointwise inequality
|\nabla u|^{2}\geq 2|\det\nabla u|
twice. Now extend
u
to a function

in

by a sequence of

reflections of

in vertical lines that cross the

) and because

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{R_{-1}}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x<\frac{k(M_{k}-2)}{2}\int_{R_{-2}}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x.

Note also that by rearranging (
36

(39)

\displaystyle\int_{R_{-2}}\det\nabla\tilde{u}\,\,{\rm d}x>\frac{1}{M_{k}}\int_{R_{-2}}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x.

Now consider the quantity

I(\tilde{u})=\int_{R_{2}}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}-4\det\nabla\tilde{u}\,\,{\rm d}x+\int_{R_{-1}}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x,

and note that by (

\displaystyle I(\tilde{u})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{R_{-2}}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}-\frac{4}{M_{k}}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}+\frac{k(M_{k}-2)}{2}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x

Numerical experiments

The functional (
2
) reads

(40)

I(\varphi)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi:\nabla\varphi+\frac{1}{2}\,f\,\nabla\varphi:\operatorname{cof}\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
:
denotes the Frobenius inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
.
Using the identity
A:\operatorname{cof}A=2\det A
, the second term can be interpreted as a determinant contribution.

Let
V_{h}\subset W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a finite element space of continuous, piecewise affine vector fields over a triangular mesh aligned with the jump set of
f
; see Figure
5
.

Figure 5.
Example of a level 3 triangular mesh of
\Omega
(left) and the distribution of
f
(right).

The approximation
\varphi_{h}\in V_{h}
is represented as

\varphi_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}\phi_{i},\qquad\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{N})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is the standard finite element basis of globally continuous, piecewise affine vector-valued functions associated with the mesh nodes. These basis functions satisfy the nodal interpolation property
\phi_{i}(x_{j})=\delta_{ij}
, so that the coefficient vector
\mathbf{v}
represents the nodal values of the discrete deformation:

\varphi_{h}(x_{i})=v_{i}.

Since the basis functions are affine on each triangle, their gradients are piecewise constant, and thus

\nabla\varphi_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}\nabla\phi_{i}.

Substituting this expansion into (
40
) and exploiting bilinearity, we define the stiffness matrices (cf.
[
4
]
)

(K_{1})_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi_{j}:\nabla\phi_{i}\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad(K_{2})_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}f\,\nabla\phi_{j}:\operatorname{cof}\nabla\phi_{i}\,\mathrm{d}x,

which leads to the quadratic representation

(41)

I(\varphi_{h})=\mathbf{v}^{\top}\bigl(K_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}K_{2}\bigr)\,\mathbf{v}=:\mathbf{v}^{\top}A\,\mathbf{v}.

The matrix
A
is symmetric. The positivity of its minimal eigenvalue
\lambda_{\min}(A)
guarantees that the quadratic form (
41
) is positive definite and hence confirms Theorem
2.1
, while
\lambda_{\min}(A)<0
indicates a violation.

Equivalently, positive definiteness of
A
can be verified by the existence of a Cholesky factorization
A=LL^{\top}
. In the computations, failure of the Cholesky decomposition provides a practical indicator of the loss of positive definiteness.
