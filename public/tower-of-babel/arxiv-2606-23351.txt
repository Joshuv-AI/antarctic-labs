Fixed-density profiles for the semi-induced 4-vertex star
Jinghua Deng, Jianfeng Hou

Abstract
We study the fixed-density semi-inducibility profiles of the red-blue star S 2 , 1 S_{2,1} , which has one distinguished center, two red edges and one blue edge. For an n n -vertex graph G G , let N ⁡ ( S 2 , 1 , G ) N(S_{2,1},G) be the number of injective labeled copies in which the two red edges of S 2 , 1 S_{2,1} are mapped to edges of G G and its blue edge is mapped to a non-edge of G G , that is, N ⁡ ( S 2 , 1 , G ) = ∑ v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) d ⁡ ( v ) ​ ( d ⁡ ( v ) − 1 ) ​ ( n − 1 − d ⁡ ( v ) ) . \displaystyle N(S_{2,1},G)=\sum_{v\in V(G)}d(v)(d(v)-1)(n-1-d(v)). For every fixed red edge density β ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] \beta\in[0,1] , we determine both extremal S 2 , 1 S_{2,1} -densities. On the upper side, we prove the missing low-density range and, together with the theorem of Balogh, Lidický, Mubayi, Pfender and Volec for β ≥ 1 / 4 \beta\geq 1/4 , obtain the full four-branch profile predicted in their work. On the lower side, we show that the natural endpoint profile coming from the quasi-star and quasi-clique constructions is not universal; the correct minimum is given by a one-parameter three-class complement-split family. The proofs use a transfer argument with degree-square tie-breaking, reducing the extremal analysis to almost-regular, threshold and finite-staircase optimizations.

Introduction

The inducibility problem, introduced by Pippenger and Golumbic
[
PG75
]
, asks for the largest possible asymptotic density of induced copies of a fixed graph. Determining the inducibility of a graph appears to be non-trivial even for simple graphs such as the four-vertex path and cycles. Exact inducibility results are known for several families and sporadic small graphs; see, for example,
[
BS94
,
BEHJ95
,
HHN14
,
BP25b
,
BGL+26
]
. On the other hand, inducibility has become a standard testing ground for modern extremal methods. Graph-limit compactness turns the problem into a variational problem over graphons
[
LS06
]
; flag algebras provide semidefinite certificates
for many exact bounds
[
Raz07
]
; and symmetrization and stability
arguments are used to identify and certify extremal constructions.

The semi-inducibility problem is a red-blue analogue of inducibility. A
red-blue graph

H
is a graph whose edges are colored red or blue; pairs of vertices which are not edges of
H
are left unconstrained. Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph, viewed as a complete red-blue graph by coloring the edges of
G
red and the non-edges blue. A
semi-induced
copy of
H
in
G
is an injective map

\phi:V(H)\to V(G)
such that

\displaystyle\phi(x)\phi(y)\in E(G)\text{ whenever $xy$ is red},\quad\phi(x)\phi(y)\notin E(G)\text{ whenever $xy$ is blue.}

Writing
|V(H)|=h
, define

\displaystyle N(H,G)\coloneqq\#\{\text{semi-induced injective labeled copies of $H$ in $G$ }\}\quad\text{ and }\quad p(H,G)=\frac{N(H,G)}{n^{h}}.

The
semi-inducibility problem
asks for the maximum limiting value of
p(H,G)
over all
n
-vertex graphs
G
. In this language, ordinary induced-copy density is the special case in which
every pair of
H
is colored, while ordinary subgraph density is obtained by
leaving all non-edge pairs uncolored.

The semi-inducibility problem was first studied by Basit, Granet, Horsley, Kündgen and Staden
[
BGH+25
]
, who established sharp or nearly sharp results for alternating walks, alternating cycles of length divisible by 4, and cycles of length 4 under every colour pattern. Very recently, Chen and Noel
[
CN25
]
determined the semi-inducibility of alternating cycles of length 6 using flag algebras, thereby resolving an open problem posed in
[
BGH+25
]
.

We consider the fixed-density version of the semi-inducibility problem. For an
n
-vertex
m
-edge graph
G
, let
\beta_{G}\coloneqq\frac{2m}{n(n-1)}
be the
density
of
G
. Let
\beta\in[0,1]
and let
H
be a fixed red-blue graph. A graph sequence
(G_{n})\coloneqq(G_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
with
|V(G_{n})|=n
, has
red density
\beta
if
\beta_{G_{n}}\to\beta
. The sequence
(G_{n})
is
H
-good if

p(H,(G_{n}))\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}p(H,G_{n})

exists. Define

\displaystyle I(H,\beta)=\sup p(H,(G_{n})),\text{ and }i(H,\beta)=\inf p(H,(G_{n}))

where the supremum and infimum are taken over all
H
-good sequences with red density
\beta
.

Clearly,
I(H,\beta)
and

are the upper and the lower profiles obtained after fixing the red edge density. A fixed-red-density question in this setting appears as Problem 9.3 in
[
BGH+25
]
. This is analogous to the feasible -region framework introduced by Liu and Mubayi
[
LM21
]
, in which one optimizes one density parameter subject to another being fixed.

Most known results on determining
I(H,\beta)
concern small red-blue graphs

H
, and the problem is trivial when
H
has at most two vertices. For
three-vertex monochromatic graphs, the fixed-density values follow from
classical extremal results, namely the Kruskal–Katona
theorem
[
Kru63
,
Kat68
]
, the theorem of Reiher and
Wagner
[
RW18
]
, and the theorem of Ahlswede and
Katona
[
AK78
]
. For non-monochromatic three-vertex graphs, the
values follow from an observation of Liu, Mubayi and Reiher
[
LMR23
]
,
together with Razborov’s triangle-density theorem
[
Raz08
]
when
all three pairs are prescribed. Hence
I(H,\beta)
is completely determined
for every red-blue graph
H
on three vertices.

The next case is
|V(H)|=4
. For the usual semi-inducibility problem, this
case has largely been settled
[
BGH+25
,
BP25a
]
. In the
fixed-density setting, Balogh, Lidický, Mubayi, Pfender and
Volec
[
BLM+26
]
proved that

for the alternating path

, and gave
partial sharp results for the alternating four-cycle.

A further natural four-vertex example is the red-blue star

Lemma 2.5

Let

Definition 2.6
.

Let

be a graph and let

\displaystyle N_{G}(x)\setminus\{y\}\subseteq N_{G}(y)\setminus\{x\}.

\displaystyle N_{G}(x)\setminus\{y\}=N_{G}(y)\setminus\{x\}.

Let

\displaystyle N(u)\setminus\{v\}\subset N(v)\setminus\{u\}.

Let

\displaystyle I(G)\coloneqq\{v\in V(G):d(v)=0\},\qquad W\coloneqq V(G)\setminus I(G).

Proof of Theorem

In this section we prove Theorem

by reducing chosen upper-extremal graphs to two structural branches: an almost-regular core and a threshold graph.
Fix integers

, and Lemma

in Subsection

\displaystyle s(\beta)\coloneqq\max\{xy^{2}(1-y)+y(x+y)^{2}(1-x-y):x,y\geq 0,\ x+y\leq 1,\ 2xy+y^{2}=\beta\},

Lemma 3.1
.

Every chosen upper-extremal graph is either threshold or has the form

is a set of isolated vertices and all degrees inside

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

, and let

Suppose that, for each

,

.

For

, the chosen upper-extremal threshold graph

with desity

-density at

Assuming these three structural inputs, the low-density upper bound

Proof of Theorem

.

Let

\displaystyle\rho(G)\coloneqq\frac{N(S_{2,1},G)}{|V(G)|^{4}},

and
\beta\in[0,1/4]
. Let
(G_{n})
be any
S_{2,1}
-good graph sequence
with
\beta_{G_{n}}\to\beta
, and let

that attains the supremum. Replacing each

by a chosen upper-extremal graph with the

After passing to a subsequence, by Lemma

is an independent set of isolated vertices and the degrees in

is threshold. In the first case, Lemma

gives

.
Since the split feasible set of

contains the point

Thus

By Proposition

\displaystyle I(S_{2,1},\beta)\leq\begin{cases}s(\beta),&0\leq\beta\leq\beta_{*},\\

For the reverse inequality in the low-density range, we use the construction given by Balogh, Lidický, Mubayi, Pfender and Volec

By (

For

\displaystyle=\mu z\left(1-z-\frac{x}{\mu}\right)\left(z+\frac{x}{\mu}\right)^{2}+x\mu z^{2}(1-\mu z)+(1-z)z^{2}(z-\mu z).

\displaystyle=zx^{3}(\mu^{-2}-1)+zx^{2}(1-\mu^{-1})+3z^{2}x^{2}(\mu^{-1}-1)+xz^{2}(1-\mu)+xz^{3}(\mu^{2}-1)

(30)

We first cover the boundary of

If

\displaystyle 0<\mu<1,\qquad 0<z<1/3,\qquad 0<x<\mu(1-z).

At such an interior constrained maximum, the Lagrange multiplier equations for

\displaystyle R_{h}=\lambda\partial g/\partial h=2\lambda\eta,\qquad R_{\eta}=\lambda\partial g/\partial\eta=2\lambda h,\qquad R_{z}=\lambda\partial g/\partial z=2\lambda z.

\displaystyle=\eta(2h-3h^{2})+(z+\eta)^{2}(1-z-\eta)-z^{2}(1-z),

\displaystyle=h^{2}(1-h)+h(z+\eta)(2-3z-3\eta),

(32)

\displaystyle R_{z}

\displaystyle=h(z+\eta)(2-3z-3\eta)+z^{2}(1-z)+(z-h)(2z-3z^{2}).

\displaystyle=h(z-h)\left(zh-z+z^{2}+2\eta-6z\eta-3\eta^{2}\right)=0.

(34)

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 3x^{2}+2\mu(3z-1)x+\mu^{2}z(1-(\mu+1)z)=0.

Since

Therefore

. By (

Proof of Lemma

In this subsection, we prove Lemma

.
We translate threshold graphs into finite
staircase patterns, prove the finite dimensional inequality

, and then pass to graph
sequences.

Recall that

\displaystyle\psi(t)\coloneqq t^{2}(1-t),\qquad M(\beta)\coloneqq\min_{z\in J_{\beta}}C_{\beta}(z).

The function

is continuous on
[0,1]
. For

, this follows from continuity of

on the compact interval

, whose endpoints vary continuously with

. At
\beta=1

gives

Finite normalization.

Let

be a finite threshold graph on

be its positive twin classes, ordered as in Lemma
2.8
, and write

\displaystyle s_{i}\coloneqq|V_{i}|,\qquad S_{i}\coloneqq s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i},\qquad x_{i}^{(n)}\coloneqq\frac{S_{i}}{n},\qquad x_{0}^{(n)}\coloneqq 0.

Here the

are cumulative endpoints, not normalized class sizes; the normalized size of

is the gap
x_{i}^{(n)}-x_{i-1}^{(n)}
. For every finite

\displaystyle 0=x_{0}^{(n)}<x_{1}^{(n)}<\cdots<x_{r}^{(n)}\leq 1,

where the final inequality reflects the possible presence of isolated vertices:

, with equality exactly when

has no isolated vertices.
The strict inequalities between consecutive endpoints hold for the finite

, a vertex of

has degree

\displaystyle d_{i}=S_{r+1-i}-\mathbf{1}_{\{2i\leq r+1\}}.

\displaystyle\frac{2e(G)}{n^{2}}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}d_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{s_{i}}{n}\frac{S_{r+1-i}}{n}+O(1/n)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(x_{i}^{(n)}-x_{i-1}^{(n)})x_{r+1-i}^{(n)}+O(1/n),

(50)

and, by the identity (
8

\displaystyle\frac{N(S_{2,1},G)}{n^{4}}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(x_{i}^{(n)}-x_{i-1}^{(n)})\psi(x_{r+1-i}^{(n)})+O(1/n).

(51)

For the
S_{2,1}

\displaystyle\frac{d_{i}(d_{i}-1)(n-1-d_{i})}{n^{3}}=\psi(x_{r+1-i}^{(n)})+O(1/n).

Multiplying by

and summing over

again gives a total error

, independently of the number of point classes.

For a weak closed staircase pattern
\mathbf{x}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})
satisfying

\displaystyle 0=x_{0}\leq x_{1}\leq\cdots\leq x_{r}\leq 1

put

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}\coloneqq x_{i}-x_{i-1},\quad\beta_{r}(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\Delta_{i}x_{r+1-i},\quad F_{r}(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\Delta_{i}\psi(x_{r+1-i}).

For

we use the empty-list convention
\beta_{0}=F_{0}=0
. The list

\displaystyle(\Delta_{1},x_{r}),(\Delta_{2},x_{r-1}),\ldots,(\Delta_{r},x_{1})

\beta_{r}
is the total edge-density contribution of this list, and

is the corresponding total
S_{2,1}
-density contribution.

Lemma 4.6

(zero-gap endpoint deletion)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})
be a weak closed staircase pattern satisfying

0=x_{0}\leq x_{1}\leq\cdots\leq x_{r}\leq 1

and assume that
\Delta_{j}=x_{j}-x_{j-1}=0
. Put
q\coloneqq r+1-j
. Remove endpoints from this finite endpoint list, not vertices from a graph, according to

\displaystyle D_{j}=\begin{cases}\{j\},&q=j\text{ or }q=j-1,\\
\{j,q\},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(52)

Let
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}
be the remaining endpoint list. Then
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}
is a weak closed staircase pattern of smaller length and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\beta}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})=\beta_{r}(\mathbf{x}),\qquad\widetilde{F}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})=F_{r}(\mathbf{x}).

For orientation, the deletion rule has the following local effect on the size-degree list. This table is only a guide to the case check in the proof.

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}\text{case}&\text{endpoint(s) removed}&\text{only possible local change}\\
\hline\cr q\notin\{j,j-1\},\ j>1&j,q&(\Delta_{q},x_{j}),(\Delta_{q+1},x_{j-1})\mapsto(\Delta_{q}+\Delta_{q+1},x_{j})\\
j=1&j,q=r&\text{last pair has degree label }x_{1}=0\\
q=j&j&\text{removed pair has zero weight}\\
q=j-1&j&(\Delta_{q},x_{j})\mapsto(\Delta_{q},x_{j-1})\end{array}

Proof.

For the pattern
\mathbf{x}
, write its size-degree list as

\displaystyle L(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq((\Delta_{1},x_{r}),(\Delta_{2},x_{r-1}),\ldots,(\Delta_{r},x_{1})).

The pair
(\Delta_{i},x_{r+1-i})
contributes
\Delta_{i}x_{r+1-i}
to
\beta_{r}(\mathbf{x})
and
\Delta_{i}\psi(x_{r+1-i})
to
F_{r}(\mathbf{x})
. Since
\Delta_{j}=0
, we have
x_{j}=x_{j-1}
, and the
j
-th pair in
L(\mathbf{x})
has zero weight. Thus it contributes neither to
\beta_{r}(\mathbf{x})
nor to
F_{r}(\mathbf{x})
. The endpoint
x_{j}
also appears on the anti-diagonal side of the list: since
q=r+1-j
, the
q
-th pair has degree label
x_{r+1-q}=x_{j}
. Thus one must also account for the degree-label side when
x_{j}
is removed.

If
r=1
, then the zero-gap assumption forces

\Delta_{1}=x_{1}-x_{0}=x_{1}=0
, so
\mathbf{x}=(0,0)
. The remaining endpoint list
is
(0)
, and both
\beta
and
F
are empty sums equal to
0
. Hence assume

r\geq 2
. The deletion of
x_{j}
removes the zero-weight pair

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}x_{r+1-j}=0,\qquad\Delta_{j}\psi(x_{r+1-j})=0.

We now check the only other possible local changes.

First assume that
q\notin\{j,j-1\}
and
j>1
. Then
q<r
. In the
size-degree list, the two pairs affected by deleting the endpoint
x_{q}
are

(\Delta_{q},x_{r+1-q}),(\Delta_{q+1},x_{r-q}).

Since
q=r+1-j
, these are

\displaystyle(\Delta_{q},x_{j}),\qquad(\Delta_{q+1},x_{j-1}).

Deleting
x_{q}
does not remove any class weight from the graph-theoretic list; it only
merges the two adjacent gaps
\Delta_{q}
and

\Delta_{q+1}

into the single gap
x_{q+1}-x_{q-1}=\Delta_{q}+\Delta_{q+1}
. The new pair has
weight
\Delta_{q}+\Delta_{q+1}
and degree label
x_{j}
. Since the deleted

x_{j}
is a zero-gap endpoint, we have
x_{j}=x_{j-1}
. Hence the

\displaystyle\Delta_{q}x_{j}+\Delta_{q+1}x_{j-1}=(\Delta_{q}+\Delta_{q+1})x_{j},\quad\text{ and }\quad\Delta_{q}\psi(x_{j})+\Delta_{q+1}\psi(x_{j-1})=(\Delta_{q}+\Delta_{q+1})\psi(x_{j}).

If
j=1
, then
q=r
. Here
x_{1}=x_{0}=0
, and the endpoint
x_{q}=x_{r}
corresponds to the last pair, whose degree label is
x_{1}=0
. Deleting this last pair also changes neither sum, since

\displaystyle\Delta_{r}x_{1}=0,\qquad\Delta_{r}\psi(x_{1})=0.

It remains to consider the two central cases, where the zero-weight pair and the anti-diagonal role of

overlap. If

, then
x_{j}
is the degree label of the same zero-weight pair, and deleting

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}x_{j}=0,\qquad\Delta_{j}\psi(x_{j})=0.

If

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}x_{j-1}=0,\qquad\Delta_{j}\psi(x_{j-1})=0.

The only nonzero pair whose degree label crosses the deleted endpoint is the

\Delta_{q}x_{j-1}

. Likewise,

\displaystyle\Delta_{q}\psi(x_{j})=\Delta_{q}\psi(x_{j-1}).

The remaining endpoint list is still weakly increasing, so it is a weak closed staircase pattern. The cases above account for every pair whose weight or degree label can change; all other pairs keep the same weight and the same degree label, up to relabeling. Therefore the two sums are unchanged.

s=0

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\partial\beta_{r}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial\beta_{r}}{\partial x_{k}}\eta^{\prime}(0).

Eliminating
\eta^{\prime}(0)
gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{r}/\partial x_{j}}{\partial\beta_{r}/\partial x_{j}}=\frac{\partial F_{r}/\partial x_{k}}{\partial\beta_{r}/\partial x_{k}}.

Using (
4.5
) and (
56
), this becomes

\displaystyle A(x_{r-j},x_{r+1-j})+\psi^{\prime}(x_{j})=A(x_{r-k},x_{r+1-k})+\psi^{\prime}(x_{k}).

Since
j,k\in J
were arbitrary, all these quantities are equal to a common constant
\Lambda
.

Lemma 4.9
.

If a strict threshold pattern
\mathbf{x}
with
x_{r}<1
has at most two positive gaps, then
F_{r}(\mathbf{x})\geq M(\beta_{r}(\mathbf{x}))
.

Proof.

If there is one positive gap, the graph is a clique plus isolated vertices. Then
\beta=x_{1}^{2}
and

\displaystyle F=x_{1}\psi(x_{1})=x_{1}^{3}(1-x_{1})=c(\beta)\geq M(\beta),

where the last inequality follows from Fact
2.4
.
Now assume that there are two positive gaps. Write
0<x_{1}<x_{2}<1
. Then

\displaystyle\beta(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}x_{2}+(x_{2}-x_{1})x_{1}=2x_{1}x_{2}-x_{1}^{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad F(x_{1},x_{2})=(x_{2}-x_{1})\psi(x_{1})+x_{1}\psi(x_{2}).

For fixed
\beta
, regard
x_{1}
as the free first level and set

\displaystyle x_{2}(x_{1})\coloneqq\frac{\beta+x_{1}^{2}}{2x_{1}}=\frac{x_{1}}{2}+\frac{\beta}{2x_{1}}.

The corresponding one-variable two-gap value is

\displaystyle F_{\beta}(x_{1})\coloneqq F(x_{1},x_{2}(x_{1}))=\left(\frac{\beta-x_{1}^{2}}{2x_{1}}\right)\psi(x_{1})+x_{1}\psi\left(\frac{\beta+x_{1}^{2}}{2x_{1}}\right).

(57)

The conditions
x_{1}<x_{2}(x_{1})<1
are equivalent to

\displaystyle 1-\sqrt{1-\beta}<x_{1}<\sqrt{\beta}.

(58)

Indeed,
x_{1}<x_{2}(x_{1})
if and only if
x_{1}^{2}<\beta
. Moreover,

x_{2}(x_{1})<1
if and only if
x_{1}>1-\sqrt{1-\beta}.
If
x_{2}=x_{1}
, then
x_{1}=\sqrt{\beta}
. If
x_{2}=1
, then
x_{1}=1-\sqrt{1-\beta}
.
Thus the two endpoints are not strict two-gap points; they are boundary
degenerations of the closed interval. Their boundary values are

\displaystyle F(1-\sqrt{1-\beta},1)=(1-\beta)(1-\sqrt{1-\beta})^{2}=\operatorname{cc}(\beta),\qquad F(\sqrt{\beta},\sqrt{\beta})=\beta^{3/2}(1-\sqrt{\beta})=c(\beta).

By Facts
2.2
and
2.4
,

\displaystyle M(\beta)\leq\min\{\operatorname{cc}(\beta),c(\beta)\}.

(59)

It remains to prove that there is no
x_{1}
in (
58
) such
that

\displaystyle F_{\beta}(x_{1})<\min\{\operatorname{cc}(\beta),c(\beta)\}.

Suppose otherwise. Since the two boundary degenerations have values

\operatorname{cc}(\beta)
and
c(\beta)
, the function
F_{\beta}
has an interior minimizer

u
with

\displaystyle F_{\beta}(u)<\min\{\operatorname{cc}(\beta),c(\beta)\}.

Put
v\coloneqq x_{2}(u)
. Then

\displaystyle F_{\beta}^{\prime}(u)=0,\qquad F_{\beta}^{\prime\prime}(u)\geq 0,\qquad 0<u<v<1.

Differentiation gives

\displaystyle F_{\beta}^{\prime}(x_{1})=\frac{(x_{1}^{2}-\beta)R_{\beta}(x_{1})}{4x_{1}^{3}},

where

\displaystyle R_{\beta}(x_{1})=-\beta^{2}-\beta x_{1}^{2}+\beta x_{1}+6x_{1}^{4}-3x_{1}^{3}.

At
x_{1}=u
, we have
u^{2}-\beta=2u(u-v)<0
. Hence
R_{\beta}(u)=0
. Substituting
\beta=2uv-u^{2}
into
R_{\beta}(u)=0
gives

\displaystyle R_{2uv-u^{2}}(u)=2u^{2}(-2v^{2}+uv+v+3u^{2}-2u)=0.

Since
u>0
, this gives

\displaystyle-2v^{2}+uv+v+3u^{2}-2u=0.

(60)

Write
H_{\beta}(x_{1})=(x_{1}^{2}-\beta)/(4x_{1}^{3})
. Since
F_{\beta}^{\prime}(x_{1})=H_{\beta}(x_{1})R_{\beta}(x_{1})
and
R_{\beta}(u)=0
, we have

\displaystyle F_{\beta}^{\prime\prime}(x_{1})=H_{\beta}(x_{1})R_{\beta}^{\prime}(x_{1})+H_{\beta}^{\prime}(x_{1})R_{\beta}(x_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad F_{\beta}^{\prime\prime}(u)=H_{\beta}(u)R_{\beta}^{\prime}(u).

Since
u^{2}-\beta=2u(u-v)<0
, we have
H_{\beta}(u)<0
. Note that
F_{\beta}^{\prime\prime}(u)\geq 0
. Therefore
R_{\beta}^{\prime}(u)\leq 0
. Since
\beta=2uv-u^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle R_{\beta}^{\prime}(u)=-2\beta u+\beta+24u^{3}-9u^{2}=2u(v(1-2u)+13u^{2}-5u)\leq 0,

(61)

We claim that
u+v<1
. First
u<1/2
. Indeed, if
u\geq 1/2
, then
1-2u\leq 0
, and since
v<1
, we have

\displaystyle(1-2u)+13u^{2}-5u\geq(1-2u)+13u^{2}-5u=13\left(u-\frac{7}{26}\right)^{2}+\frac{3}{52}\geq\frac{3}{4}>0,

a contradiction to (
61
). Hence
u<1/2
. Let

\displaystyle Q(z)\coloneqq-2z^{2}+uz+z+3u^{2}-2u.

Then (
60
) says
Q(v)=0
, while

Q(1-u)=2u-1<0,

and for every
z\geq 1-u
,

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}(z)=-4z+u+1\leq-4(1-u)+u+1=5u-3<0.

So
Q(z)<0
for every
z\geq 1-u
. Since
Q(v)=0
, we must have
v<1-u
, that is,
u+v<1
.

Therefore
1-v>u
, and

\displaystyle F_{\beta}(u)=(v-u)u^{2}(1-u)+uv^{2}(1-v)\geq uv^{2}(1-v)>u^{2}v^{2}.

Also
\beta=2uv-u^{2}\leq 2uv
, so

\displaystyle u^{2}v^{2}\geq\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}.

By Fact
2.3
,
\operatorname{cc}(\beta)\leq\beta^{2}/4
. Thus

\displaystyle F_{\beta}(u)>\operatorname{cc}(\beta)\geq\min\{\operatorname{cc}(\beta),c(\beta)\},

contradicting the choice of
u
. Consequently every
x_{1}
in (
58
) satisfies

\displaystyle F_{\beta}(x_{1})\geq\min\{\operatorname{cc}(\beta),c(\beta)\}\geq M(\beta).

This proves the two-gap case.

Lemma 4.10
.

If a strict threshold pattern
\mathbf{x}
has three positive gaps and
x_{3}<1
, then
F_{3}(\mathbf{x})\geq M(\beta_{3}(\mathbf{x}))
.

Proof.

Let
0<x_{1}<x_{2}<x_{3}<1
. Then

\displaystyle\beta=x_{1}x_{3}+(x_{2}-x_{1})x_{2}+(x_{3}-x_{2})x_{1}=x_{2}^{2}+2x_{1}(x_{3}-x_{2}).

(62)

Since
\beta-x_{2}^{2}=2x_{1}(x_{3}-x_{2})>0
,

\displaystyle x_{3}=x_{2}+\frac{\beta-x_{2}^{2}}{2x_{1}},\qquad\frac{\beta-x_{2}^{2}}{2(1-x_{2})}<\frac{\beta-x_{2}^{2}}{2(x_{3}-x_{2})}=x_{1}<x_{2}.

(63)

The normalized
S_{2,1}
-density, namely the value of
F_{3}(\bf x)
, is

\displaystyle\Phi_{x_{2},\beta}(x_{1})\coloneqq\frac{\beta-x_{2}^{2}}{2x_{1}}\psi(x_{1})+x_{1}\psi\left(x_{2}+\frac{\beta-x_{2}^{2}}{2x_{1}}\right)+(x_{2}-x_{1})\psi(x_{2}).

(64)

We compare
\Phi_{x_{2},\beta}
with the two endpoint degenerations of the interval in (
63
). By (
3
), if

\displaystyle x_{1}=\frac{\beta-x_{2}^{2}}{2(1-x_{2})}=h_{\beta}(x_{2}),

then
x_{3}=1
. Using the definition of
C_{\beta}(x)
in (
4
) and
\psi(1)=0
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{x_{2},\beta}(h_{\beta}(x_{2}))=(1-x_{2})\psi(h_{\beta}(x_{2}))+(x_{2}-h_{\beta}(x_{2}))\psi(x_{2})=C_{\beta}(x_{2}).

At the right endpoint
x_{1}=x_{2}
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{x_{2},\beta}(x_{2})=\frac{\beta-x_{2}^{2}}{2x_{2}}\psi(x_{2})+x_{2}\psi\left(\frac{\beta+x_{2}^{2}}{2x_{2}}\right)=F_{\beta}(x_{2}),

where
F_{\beta}
is the two-gap value from (
57
).
Set

\displaystyle T\coloneqq(\beta-x_{2}^{2})(1-2x_{2})+6x_{2}^{2}(1-x_{2})^{2}.

Over the common denominator
8x_{2}^{2}(1-x_{2})^{2}
, expansion gives

\displaystyle F_{\beta}(x_{2})-C_{\beta}(x_{2})=\frac{(\beta-x_{2}^{2})(2x_{2}(1-x_{2})-(\beta-x_{2}^{2}))T}{8x_{2}^{2}(1-x_{2})^{2}}.

(65)

Indeed,
\beta-x_{2}^{2}>0
by (
62
), and (
63
) gives

\beta-x_{2}^{2}<2x_{1}(1-x_{2})<2x_{2}(1-x_{2}).

Hence the sign of
F_{\beta}(x_{2})-C_{\beta}(x_{2})
is the sign of
T
.

First assume
T\geq 0
. Then
C_{\beta}(x_{2})\leq F_{\beta}(x_{2})
. Expanding (
64
) and subtracting
C_{\beta}(x_{2})
gives

\displaystyle\Phi_{x_{2},\beta}(x_{1})-C_{\beta}(x_{2})=\frac{(\beta-x_{2}^{2})(2x_{1}(1-x_{2})-(\beta-x_{2}^{2}))N}{8x_{1}^{2}(1-x_{2})^{2}},

(66)

where

\displaystyle N={}

\displaystyle(\beta-x_{2}^{2})((1-x_{2})^{2}-x_{1}^{2})+2x_{1}^{2}(1-x_{2})(1-x_{1})+4x_{1}x_{2}(1-x_{2})^{2}.

The factors outside
N
in (
66
) are nonnegative by (
62
) and (
63
). If
x_{1}\leq 1-x_{2}
, then
N\geq 0
. It remains in this case to consider
x_{1}>1-x_{2}
. Then
x_{2}>1/2
, and
N
decreases as the quantity
\beta-x_{2}^{2}
increases. From
T\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\beta-x_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{6x_{2}^{2}(1-x_{2})^{2}}{2x_{2}-1}.

Therefore

\displaystyle N\geq\left.N\right|_{\beta-x_{2}^{2}=\frac{6x_{2}^{2}(1-x_{2})^{2}}{2x_{2}-1}}=\frac{2(x_{2}-x_{1})(x_{2}-1)M_{0}}{2x_{2}-1},

(67)

where

\displaystyle M_{0}=-(2x_{2}-1)x_{1}^{2}-x_{1}(1-x_{2})\left(3\left(x_{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right)^{2}+\frac{2}{3}\right)-3x_{2}(1-x_{2})^{3}<0.

The lower bound in (
67
) is nonnegative, since
x_{2}-x_{1}>0
,
x_{2}-1<0
,
2x_{2}-1>0
and
M_{0}<0
. Hence
N\geq 0
, and
\Phi_{x_{2},\beta}(x_{1})\geq C_{\beta}(x_{2})
.

Now assume
T\leq 0
. Then
F_{\beta}(x_{2})\leq C_{\beta}(x_{2})
. Expanding (
64
) and subtracting
F_{\beta}(x_{2})
gives

\displaystyle\Phi_{x_{2},\beta}(x_{1})-F_{\beta}(x_{2})=\frac{(\beta-x_{2}^{2})(x_{2}-x_{1})W}{8x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}},

(68)

where

\displaystyle W={}

\displaystyle-(x_{1}+x_{2})(\beta-x_{2}^{2})^{2}+2(\beta-x_{2}^{2})x_{1}x_{2}(1-3x_{2})+4x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}(x_{1}+x_{2}-1).

Since
T\leq 0
,
\beta-x_{2}^{2}\geq 0
and
6x_{2}^{2}(1-x_{2})^{2}\geq 0
, we have
x_{2}>1/2
. The assumption
T\leq 0
gives

\displaystyle\beta-x_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{6x_{2}^{2}(1-x_{2})^{2}}{2x_{2}-1}.

Together with (
63
), this places
\beta-x_{2}^{2}
in the interval

\displaystyle\frac{6x_{2}^{2}(1-x_{2})^{2}}{2x_{2}-1}\leq\beta-x_{2}^{2}\leq 2x_{1}(1-x_{2}).

(69)

The expression
W
is a concave quadratic in
\beta-x_{2}^{2}
, so its minimum on this interval is attained at an endpoint. Put

\displaystyle P\coloneqq x_{1}(2x_{2}-1)-3x_{2}^{2}(1-x_{2}).

Substituting the two endpoint values of
\beta-x_{2}^{2}
into

gives

\displaystyle\left.W\right|_{\beta-x_{2}^{2}=\frac{6x_{2}^{2}(1-x_{2})^{2}}{2x_{2}-1}}=-\frac{4x_{2}^{2}PM_{0}}{(2x_{2}-1)^{2}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\left.W\right|_{\beta-x_{2}^{2}=2x_{1}(1-x_{2})}=4x_{1}^{2}P.

Since
1>x_{2}>1/2
, we have
2x_{2}-1>0
and
2(1-x_{2})>0
. Comparing the two
endpoints in (
69
) and multiplying by

(2x_{2}-1)/(2(1-x_{2}))
gives

3x_{2}^{2}(1-x_{2})\leq x_{1}(2x_{2}-1).

Thus
P\geq 0
.
Since
M_{0}<0
, both endpoint values of
W
are nonnegative. Therefore
W\geq 0
throughout the interval, and
\Phi_{x_{2},\beta}(x_{1})\geq F_{\beta}(x_{2})
.

Thus in all cases

\displaystyle\Phi_{x_{2},\beta}(x_{1})\geq\min\{C_{\beta}(x_{2}),F_{\beta}(x_{2})\}.

Here
x_{2}\in J_{\beta}
: from
\beta-x_{2}^{2}>0
we get
x_{2}<\sqrt{\beta}
, while
x_{3}<1
and (

\displaystyle\beta<x_{2}^{2}+2x_{1}(1-x_{2})<x_{2}^{2}+2x_{2}(1-x_{2})=2x_{2}-x_{2}^{2},

equivalently
x_{2}>1-\sqrt{1-\beta}
. Thus
C_{\beta}(x_{2})\geq M(\beta)
by definition, and
F_{\beta}(x_{2})\geq M(\beta)
by Lemma
4.9
. Hence
\Phi_{x_{2},\beta}(x_{1})\geq M(\beta)
.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})
be a strict pattern satisfying
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{r}<1
. If
\mathbf{x}
is an interior local minimum of
F_{r}
among
r
-class patterns with fixed edge density
\beta_{r}
, then

F_{r}(\mathbf{x})\geq M(\beta_{r}(\mathbf{x})).

Proof.

For
r\leq 3
, Lemmas
4.9
and
4.10
show that no such counterexample exists. Assume
r\geq 4
and suppose, for a contradiction, that such a counterexample is an interior local minimum at fixed edge density.

Keep
x_{2},\ldots,x_{r-1}
fixed and vary only the two outside endpoints
x_{1}
and
x_{r}
. In the edge-density
\beta_{r}
, we have

\displaystyle\beta_{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(x_{i}-x_{i-1})x_{r+1-i}=x_{1}x_{r}-x_{1}x_{r-1}+x_{1}(x_{r}-x_{r-1})+x_{2}x_{r-1}+\sum_{i=3}^{r-1}(x_{i}-x_{i-1})x_{r+1-i}.

Thus, preserving the edge density is equivalent to keeping

K\coloneqq x_{1}(x_{r}-x_{r-1})

constant. Thus, as
x_{r}
varies,

\displaystyle x_{1}(x_{r})=\frac{K}{x_{r}-x_{r-1}},\qquad\frac{dx_{1}}{dx_{r}}=-\frac{x_{1}}{x_{r}-x_{r-1}},\qquad\frac{d^{2}x_{1}}{dx_{r}^{2}}=\frac{2x_{1}}{(x_{r}-x_{r-1})^{2}}.

(70)

For the normalized
S_{2,1}
-density,

\displaystyle F_{r}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(x_{i}-x_{i-1})\psi(x_{r+1-i}).

Under the same move, all terms are constant except

\displaystyle G(x_{r})\coloneqq(x_{r}-x_{r-1})\psi(x_{1}(x_{r}))+x_{1}(x_{r})\psi(x_{r})-x_{1}(x_{r})\psi(x_{r-1})

where
x_{1}=K/(x_{r}-x_{r-1})
. Differentiating twice and substituting

\psi^{\prime\prime}(t)=2-6t
gives

\displaystyle G^{\prime\prime}(x_{r})=\frac{2x_{1}(3x_{1}^{2}-x_{1}+(x_{r}-x_{r-1})^{2})}{x_{r-1}-x_{r}}.

(71)

Since
x_{r-1}<x_{r}
and local minimality forces
G^{\prime\prime}(x_{r})\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle 3x_{1}^{2}-x_{1}+(x_{r}-x_{r-1})^{2}\leq 0.

(72)

Hence
3x_{1}^{2}-x_{1}<0
and
x_{1}<1/3
. Also, (
72
) gives

\displaystyle(x_{r}-x_{r-1})^{2}\leq x_{1}-3x_{1}^{2}\leq\max_{0\leq t\leq 1/3}(t-3t^{2})=\frac{1}{12}.

Therefore

\displaystyle x_{r}-x_{r-1}\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}.

(73)

By Lemma
4.8
, the quantities
A(x_{r-j},x_{r+1-j})+\psi^{\prime}(x_{j})
are all equal. Taking
j=1
and
j=r
gives

\displaystyle A(x_{r-1},x_{r})+\psi^{\prime}(x_{1})=A(0,x_{1})+\psi^{\prime}(x_{r}).

(74)

Equation (
74
) becomes

\displaystyle(x_{r}-x_{r-1})(1-3x_{r}+x_{r}-x_{r-1})=x_{1}(1-2x_{1}).

(75)

We have
x_{1}(1-2x_{1})\geq 0
since
0<x_{1}<1/3
, and
x_{r}-x_{r-1}>0
. Hence

1-3x_{r}+x_{r}-x_{r-1}>0.

Combining this with (
73
) gives

\displaystyle x_{r}<\frac{1+x_{r}-x_{r-1}}{3}\leq\frac{1+1/(2\sqrt{3})}{3}<\frac{1}{2}.

(76)

All positive degree levels are at most
x_{r}
, and their total weight is
\sum_{i}\Delta_{i}=x_{r}
. Hence
1-x_{r+1-i}\geq 1-x_{r}
, and

\displaystyle F_{r}=\sum_{i}\Delta_{i}x_{r+1-i}^{2}(1-x_{r+1-i})\geq(1-x_{r})\sum_{i}\Delta_{i}x_{r+1-i}^{2}.

By Cauchy’s inequality,

\displaystyle\beta_{r}^{2}=\left(\sum_{i}\Delta_{i}x_{r+1-i}\right)^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{i}\Delta_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{i}\Delta_{i}x_{r+1-i}^{2}\right)=x_{r}\sum_{i}\Delta_{i}x_{r+1-i}^{2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle F_{r}\geq\frac{1-x_{r}}{x_{r}}\beta_{r}^{2}>\beta_{r}^{2},

(77)

where the last inequality uses (
76
). On the other hand, Facts
2.2
and
2.3
give

\displaystyle M(\beta_{r})\leq\operatorname{cc}(\beta_{r})\leq\frac{\beta_{r}^{2}}{4}<\beta_{r}^{2}<F_{r},

where
\beta_{r}>0
since the pattern is strict. This contradicts the counterexample assumption
F_{r}<M(\beta_{r})
.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})
be a strict pattern satisfying
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{r-1}<x_{r}=1
. If
\mathbf{x}
is a relative interior local minimum of

, at fixed edge density

, then

F_{r}(\mathbf{x})\geq M(\beta_{r}(\mathbf{x})).

We first spell out the cases

. Recall that

If

, then
x_{1}=1
and

. If

, write

. Then

,

in the complement-split construction. The

\displaystyle\beta=z^{2}+2h(1-z).

Hence

,
(
3
) gives

. Using (
4
), the

\displaystyle F_{3}({\bf x})=(z-h)z^{2}(1-z)+(1-z)h^{2}(1-h)=C_{\beta}(z)\geq M(\beta).

It remains to exclude relative interior local-minimum counterexamples with

.

Four positive gaps.

Let
0<x_{1}<x_{2}<x_{3}<x_{4}=1
. Since
x_{4}=1
, the edge-density formula gives

\displaystyle\beta\coloneqq\beta_{4}({\bf x})

\displaystyle=x_{1}+(x_{2}-x_{1})x_{3}+(x_{3}-x_{2})x_{2}+(1-x_{3})x_{1}=2x_{1}-2x_{1}x_{3}+2x_{2}x_{3}-x_{2}^{2}.

(78)

Writing
x_{2}=x_{1}+\Delta_{2}
and
x_{3}=x_{1}+\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}
, this becomes

\displaystyle\beta_{4}({\bf x})=2x_{1}-x_{1}^{2}+\Delta_{2}^{2}+2\Delta_{2}\Delta_{3}.

(79)

Also

\displaystyle F_{4}({\bf x})=\Delta_{3}\psi(x_{2})+(1-x_{3})\psi(x_{1})+\Delta_{2}\psi(x_{3}).

(80)

First, we claim that
x_{2}>1/3
. By Lemma
4.8
, with
x_{4}=1
fixed, the free-coordinate balance equations give

\displaystyle A(x_{3},1)+\psi^{\prime}(x_{1})=A(x_{2},x_{3})+\psi^{\prime}(x_{2}).

(81)

Substituting
A(a,b)=a+b-a^{2}-ab-b^{2}
and
\psi^{\prime}(t)=2t-3t^{2}
into
(

\displaystyle x_{3}(1-x_{2})=2x_{1}-3x_{1}^{2}+4x_{2}^{2}-3x_{2}.

(82)

Suppose instead that
x_{2}\leq 1/3
. Since
0<x_{1}<x_{2}\leq 1/3
and

\psi^{\prime}(t)=2t-3t^{2}
is increasing on
[0,1/3]

2x_{1}-3x_{1}^{2}<2x_{2}-3x_{2}^{2}.

Hence the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle 2x_{1}-3x_{1}^{2}+4x_{2}^{2}-3x_{2}<2x_{2}-3x_{2}^{2}+4x_{2}^{2}-3x_{2}=x_{2}^{2}-x_{2}<0.

But the left-hand side of (

.

Next we prove that
\Delta_{2}>\Delta_{3}
. Again by Lemma

, the factor in parentheses is positive, and

\lambda-1\leq 0
. Hence

T_{1/2}(\lambda)\leq 0,

and so
T_{\tau}(\lambda)\leq 0
.

Thus every feasible point has value at most
c(\tau^{2})
. The endpoint

\lambda=1
, equivalently
(x,y)=(0,\tau)
, is feasible and gives
equality. Therefore

s(\tau^{2})=c(\tau^{2})\qquad(t_{*}\leq\tau\leq 1/2).

Since
c(\tau^{2})=\tau^{3}(1-\tau)=\beta^{3/2}(1-\sqrt{\beta})
with

\beta=\tau^{2}
, the desired comparison follows.
