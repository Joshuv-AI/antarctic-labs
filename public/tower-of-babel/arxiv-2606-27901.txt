Abstract
. We identify the zonal and character spherical functions for quantum symmetric pairs with the symmetric Koornwinder–Macdonald polynomials. To this end, the methods of Letzter’s 2004 paper are translated to modern conventions and right coideal subalgebras, and extended to non-standard cases and cases with non-reduced restricted root systems. For the last elusive Satake type, 𝖥𝖨𝖨 \mathsf{FII} , a conjecture is provided.

Primary 33D80; Secondary 33D52, 17B37

1.
Introduction

The study of quantum symmetric pairs (QSP) and their spherical functions has advanced vastly in conventions, generality, and knowledge since
one of its most foundational papers, Gail Letzter’s
[
Let04
]
, was written.
This paper’s aim is to translate the contents of loc.cit. into modern
notation, provide more details in the proofs and a better
understanding of the techniques that Letzter used, and also deliver
on Letzter’s promise from loc.cit.’s introduction: show that these
techniques work in the general case as well.
We then use these techniques and combine them with results on rank-1 cases from the literature to identify the zonal spherical functions
of QSPs with twisted Macdonald–Koornwinder polynomials for all finite-type QSPs except for
type
\mathsf{FII}
for which we provide a conjecture.
This happens in Theorem
7.2
(and Conjecture
6.4
).

In particular, we determine the zonal spherical functions for all
Hermitean QSPs, i.e. those
(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{B})
where

\mathbf{B}
has characters besides the counit
\epsilon
.
This allows us to extend the results from
[
Mee25
]
to
non-standard parameters, to different parameters on the left and the right, and to different characters on the left and the right
(Theorem
8.7
).
For specific choices of characters, the resulting functions can also
be interpreted as
\epsilon
-symmetric Macdonald–Koornwinder polynomials (Proposition
8.9
).

Lastly, we provide a survey about which Koornwinder polynomials
(i.e. Macdonald for a
(\mathsf{C}_{n}^{\vee},\mathsf{C}_{n})
affine root system)
can be realised as character spherical functions of a quantum symmetric
pair.

1.1.
Structure

In Section
2
we introduce standard notation around
QSPs, their root systems, and their spherical functions.
In particular, we introduce the restriction procedure that produces
symmetric polynomials from spherical functions.

In Section
3
we study the centraliser

Z_{\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{B}})
and use it to construct a
commutative algebra of symmetric difference operators (the radial parts of
Z_{\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{B}})
) that are
diagonalised by the restrictions of zonal spherical functions.
Our goal is to show that these symmetric difference operators
are Macdonald operators.

In Section
4
we establish the existence of a power
series
p
that determines the
leading terms of the radial parts of
Z_{\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{B}})
in a uniform manner.

In Section
5
we establish that
p
can be
computed from the
p
-functions of rank-1 QSPs.
In Section
6
we compute these rank-1

p
-functions from cases that are known from literature.

In Section
7
we consider small cases (that
always exist) in which the leading term of a radial part actually
determines the entire radial part, so that we can compute these
radial parts using our knowledge of
p
.
This knowledge is sufficient to identify the joint eigenfunctions, i.e.
the restrictions of ZSF, as symmetric Macdonald–Koornwinder polynomials.

In the next section (Section
8
), we complete
the computation of character spherical functions from
[
Mee25
]

by also considering non-standard cases and by considering
different parameters and characters on the left and right.
For specific non-standard cases, this also gives us an interpretation
of character spherical functions as
\epsilon
-symmetric
Koornwinder polynomials.
For specific standard cases, this also allows us to classify the
zonal spherical functions for quantisations of several non-symmetric
spherical pairs.

Finally, in Section
9
, we classify which
parameter values of rank-
n
Koornwinder polynomials are

“geometric”
, i.e. can be realised as restrictions of character
spherical functions.

2.
General Definitions and Notations

2.1.
Root System

Let
I:=\left\{1,\dots,n\right\}
and let
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j=1,\dots,n}
be a
symmetrisable Cartan matrix of finite type and let
X,Y
be two lattices equipped with
a perfect pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:Y\times X\to\mathbb{Z}
, and
with linearly independent elements

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in X,\qquad h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\in Y

such that

\langle h_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle=a_{ij}\qquad(i,j\in I).

Let there be a symmetric bilinear form
\cdot
on
X
such that

\forall i,j\in I\colon\quad\langle h_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle=2\frac{\alpha_{i}\cdot\alpha_{j}}{\alpha_{i}\cdot\alpha_{i}}.

This corresponds to a choice
(I,\cdot)
of Cartan datum
(
[
Lus10
, §1.1.1]
), and a choice
(X,Y)
of
X
- and
Y
-regular
root datum (
[
Lus10
, §2.2.1]
), where the embeddings of

I\to X,Y
are denoted by
i\mapsto\alpha_{i},h_{i}
, respectively.
Furthermore, we write
h:X\to Y\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
for the linear map mapping

\lambda\in X
to the unique element
h\in Y\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
satisfying

\forall\mu\in X\colon\quad\lambda\cdot\mu=\langle h,\mu\rangle.

In particular,
h_{\alpha_{i}}=\frac{\absolutevalue{\alpha_i}^{2}}{2}h_{i}
.
Assume that
\absolutevalue{\alpha_i}^{2}\in 2\mathbb{Z}
, so that
h_{\alpha_{i}}\in Y
.

Write
Q,Q^{\vee}
for the sublattices of
X,Y
generated by

\alpha_{i},h_{i}
(
i\in I
), respectively.
Write
P^{\vee},P
for the corresponding dual lattices (which are then
quotients of
Y,X
, respectively).

Let
W
be the corresponding Weyl group; it acts on
X,Y
and their
sub- and quotient lattices
Q,Q^{\vee},P,P^{\vee}
.
Let
R=\bigcup_{i\in I}W\alpha_{i}\subseteq Q
be the root system associated
to
A
, and let
Q^{+}:=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{N}_{0}\alpha_{i}
and

R^{+}:=R\cap Q^{+}
(similarly,
R^{\vee},Q^{\vee+},R^{\vee+}
).
Write furthermore
X^{+}:=\left\{\mu\in X\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\langle Q^{\vee+},\mu\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}
for the set of dominant elements of
X
.

Let
2\rho\in X
be the sum of positive roots.

2.2.
Quantum Group

Let
q
be an indeterminate and consider
k:=\overline{\mathbb{C}(q)}
.
Let
K
be an invertible element of a
k
-algebra
A
; for

a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
we define

[a]_{q^{b}}:=\frac{q^{ab}-q^{-ab}}{q^{b}-q^{-b}},\qquad[K;a]_{q^{b}}:=\frac{Kq^{ab}-K^{-1}q^{-ab}}{q^{b}-q^{-b}}

as well as
[K]_{q^{b}}:=[K;0]_{q^{b}}
if this does not lead to confusion.
Define
q_{i}:=q^{\frac{\absolutevalue{\alpha_i}^{2}}{2}}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
Drinfel’d–Jimbo quantum group

\mathbf{U}
is the associative

k
-algebra generated by

(E_{i})_{i\in I},\qquad(F_{i})_{i\in I},\qquad(K_{h})_{h\in Y}

subject to the relations

\displaystyle K_{0}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle K_{h}K_{h^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=K_{h+h^{\prime}}

\displaystyle K_{h}E_{i}

\displaystyle=q^{\langle h,\alpha_{i}\rangle}E_{i}K_{h}

\displaystyle K_{h}F_{i}

\displaystyle=q^{-\langle h,\alpha_{i}\rangle}F_{i}K_{h}

\displaystyle\commutator{E_i}{F_j}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}[K_{\alpha_{i}}]_{q_{i}}

\displaystyle F_{ij}(E_{i},E_{j})

\displaystyle=0\qquad(i\neq j)

\displaystyle F_{ij}(F_{i},F_{j})

\displaystyle=0\qquad(i\neq j),

where

F_{ij}(x,y):=\sum_{p+p^{\prime}=1-a_{ij}}\frac{(-1)^{p^{\prime}}}{[p]^{!}_{q_{i}}[p^{\prime}]^{!}_{q_{i}}}x^{p}yx^{p^{\prime}},

h,h^{\prime}\in Y,i,j\in I
,
and where
K_{\mu}
is understood to mean
K_{h_{\mu}}
for

\mu\in X
with
h_{\mu}\in Y
.
Here, we used
[p]^{!}_{q}:=[p]_{q}[p-1]_{q}\cdots[1]_{q}
for

p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

We also define the completion
\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}
of

\mathbf{U}
to be the algebra generated by

(E_{i})_{i\in I},\qquad(F_{i})_{i\in I},\qquad(K_{h})_{h\in Y\otimes\mathbb{Q}}

subject to the same relations.

Write
\mathcal{A}
for the vector space of matrix elements
of finite-dimensional representations.

There is a well-established way (e.g.
[
Lus10
, §3.3.4]
or

[
MS25
, Lemma 3.2]
) of turning
\mathbf{U}
and
\mathcal{A}
into dual Hopf
algebras with comultiplication
\Delta
, counit
\epsilon
, and antipode
S
.
Sweedler notation will be used whenever appropriate.

We define
\mathbf{U}^{0}
(resp.
\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}^{0}
) to be the commutative

k
-subalgebra of
\mathbf{U}
(resp.
\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}
) generated by
K_{h}

for
h\in Y
(resp.
h\in Y\otimes\mathbb{Q}
).

Write
\mathbf{U}^{\pm}
for the subalgebras generated by the
E_{i}

(resp.
F_{i}
) for
i\in I
.
We have

\mathbf{U}\cong\mathbf{U}^{+}\otimes\mathbf{U}^{0}\otimes\mathbf{U}^{-},\qquad\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}\cong\mathbf{U}^{+}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}^{0}\otimes\mathbf{U}^{-}.

Write
\ad,\mathrm{ad}_{\mathrm{r}}
for the left- and right-adjoint actions of
\mathbf{U}

(resp.
\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}
) on itself.
They are given as follows:

\ad(x)(y):=x_{(1)}yS(x_{(2)}),\qquad\mathrm{ad}_{\mathrm{r}}(x)(y):=S(x_{(1)})yx_{(2)}.

For any
\mathbf{U}
-module
V
and
\mu\in X
let

V_{\mu}:=\left\{v\in V\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\forall h\in Y:K_{h}v=q^{\langle h,\mu\rangle}v\right\},

the
\mu
-weight space
.
Applying this to the left-adjoint action of
\mathbf{U}
on itself,
we obtain a decomposition
\mathbf{U}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in Q}\mathbf{U}_{\mu}

into weight spaces.

For a group homomorphism
\xi:Q\to k^{\times}
, write

\ad(\xi)
for the automorphism of
\mathbf{U}
(resp.
\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}
)
that acts as
\xi(\mu)
on
\mathbf{U}_{\mu}
.
Write
\tilde{H}
for the group generated by such
\ad(\xi)
.
In particular, we can view
X
as subgroup of
\tilde{H}

by having
\mu\in X
correspond to
\ad(\xi)
where

\xi:h\mapsto q^{\langle h,\mu\rangle}
.

For
w\in W
write
T_{w}
for the automorphism
T^{\prime\prime}_{w,1}\mathbf{U}\to\mathbf{U}
(resp.

\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}\to\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}
) from

[
Lus10
, §37.1.3, §39.4.7]
.
It satisfies
T_{w}(K_{h})=K_{wh}
for
h\in Y
(resp.
h\in Y\otimes Q
).

For
\lambda\in X^{+}
we write
L(\lambda)
for the
finite-dimensional
\mathbf{U}
- (resp.
\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}
-) module with
highest weight
\lambda
.

Quantum Symmetric Pairs

Notation 2.2
.

For any subset

write
w_{\bullet}
for the
longest element of the parabolic subgroup of

generated by

I_{\bullet}

Definition 2.3
.

A

(I_{\bullet},\tau)

of a subset
I_{\bullet}\subseteq I
and an involutive diagram

(i)

\tau|_{I_{\bullet}}=-w_{\bullet}

(ii)

\rho_{\bullet}^{\vee}
is the

I_{\bullet}
); and

(iii)

there is an involution
\tau
of
X
(and hence

Y

\tau\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{\tau(i)}

Write

(

(

)

.

and

for the set of elements

Note that the quotient map

Write
\mathbf{U}_{\bullet}

For

Let

Definition 2.8

Lemma 2.14

Both

3.
Centraliser of
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mu\in A
and define

\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{\mu}=\mathbf{U}^{-}K_{\mu}k[T]S(\mathbf{U}^{+})

and

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}):=\bigoplus_{\lambda-\mu\in Q(\Sigma)^{+}}\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{\mu}.

Then
(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}))_{\lambda\in A}
is
a filtration of rings.

Proof.

To show this, we note that the vector space grading assigns to the
generators
F_{i},E_{i},K_{\mu}
the degrees
0,\tilde{\alpha}_{i},\tilde{\mu}
, respectively.
Furthermore, the relations
K_{\lambda}K_{\mu}=K_{\lambda+\mu}

K_{\mu}E_{i}K_{-\mu}=q^{\langle\mu,\alpha_{i}\rangle}E_{i},\qquad K_{\mu}F_{i}K_{-\mu}=q^{-\langle\mu,\alpha_{i}\rangle}F_{i},\qquad

and the Serre relations are homogeneous, and the relation

\commutator{E_i}{F_j}=\delta_{ij}\frac{K_{i}-K_{i}^{-1}}{q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}}

is homogeneous modulo terms of order lower than
\tilde{\alpha}_{i}
.
Consequently,
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{\lambda+\mu}
.
∎

Lemma 4.20

There is

Proof.

Write

\displaystyle\widehat{\Upsilon}(v_{\lambda}b^{*}\otimes bv_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in Q^{+}}\Upsilon(v_{\lambda}b_{\alpha}^{*}\otimes b_{-\alpha}v_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in Q^{+}}v_{\lambda}^{*}(b_{\alpha}^{*}b_{-\alpha}v_{\lambda})e^{\lambda-\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in Q^{+}}v_{\lambda}^{*}(P(b_{\alpha}^{*}b_{\alpha})v_{\lambda})e^{\lambda-\alpha}

∎

Having now constructed the restriction

Proposition 4.21

[
Let08

We can write

With an analogous proof we can show loc.cit. in the associated

Lemma 4.22
.

Let

On one hand we have

∎

Theorem 4.23
.

There exists a power series

Moreover, writing

\Rad(x)=pT_{\mu}p^{-1}+\lot

Proof.

Write

\Rad^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{\nu\leq\mu}a_{\nu}T_{\nu}

(possible by
[
Let08
, Lemma 5.8]

For every
\lambda\in X^{+}

of the Casimir element from the proof of

\gamma(ac_{\lambda})=K_{2\tilde{\lambda}}+\lot

We write
\Rad^{\prime}(ac_{\lambda})=\sum_{\nu\leq 2\tilde{\lambda}}b_{\nu}T_{\nu}

for appropriate rational functions

Note that
b_{2\tilde{\lambda}}=\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}(\cdot+2\tilde{\lambda})}

Since

and

commute, so do their radial parts,

D_{-\lambda}
,
it must be a symmetric Koornwinder–Macdonald polynomial
by
[
Sto21
, Remark 9.3.23]
.

To show the claim, note that by definition

c_{\tau(\lambda)}=\prod_{a\in R^{\mathrm{t},+}\cap\tau(-\lambda)(R^{\mathrm{t},-})}c_{a}=\prod_{a\in R^{\mathrm{t},+}\setminus\tau(-\lambda)(R^{\mathrm{t},+})}c_{a}.

We also have

R^{\mathrm{t},+}=\left\{\alpha+\mu_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{t}}mc\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in R_{0},m\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\cup R_{0}^{+}

and

\tau(-\lambda)(\alpha+\mu_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{t}}mc)=\alpha+\quantity(\mu_\alpha^{\mathrm{t}}m + \lambda\cdot\alpha)c.

We obtain

R^{\mathrm{t},+}\setminus\tau(-\lambda)(R^{\mathrm{t},+})=\left\{\alpha+\mu_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{t}}mc\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in R_{0}^{+},0\leq m<\lambda\cdot\alpha^{\vee}\right\}

Moreover, for
a=\alpha+\mu_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{t}}mc
we have

c_{a}=\kappa_{a}^{2}\frac{\quantity(1-\kappa_a^{-1}\kappa_{2a}^{-1}e^{-\alpha}q_\alpha^{-m})\quantity(1+\kappa_a^{-1}\kappa_{2a}e^{-\alpha}q_\alpha^{-m})}{1-e^{-2\alpha}q^{-2m}}.

If
R
is reduced, we obtain
\kappa_{a}=\kappa_{2a}
for all
a\in R

and
\kappa_{\alpha+\mu_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{t}}mc}=\kappa_{\alpha}
,
so that

\displaystyle c_{\tau(\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\tau(\lambda)}^{2}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{0}^{+}}\prod_{m=0}^{\lambda\cdot\alpha^{\vee}-1}\frac{1-\kappa_{\alpha}^{-2}e^{-\alpha}q_{\alpha}^{-m}}{1-e^{-\alpha}q_{\alpha}^{-m}}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\tau(\lambda)}^{2}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{0}^{+}}\frac{\quantity(\kappa_\alpha^{-2}e^{-\alpha};q_\alpha^{-1})_{\lambda\cdot\alpha^{\vee}}}{\quantity(e^{-\alpha};q_\alpha^{-1})_{\lambda\cdot\alpha^{\vee}}}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\tau(\lambda)}^{2}\frac{p}{p(\cdot+\lambda)}.

Meanwhile, for
R
non-reduced, we have

c_{\tau(\lambda)}=\kappa_{\tau(\lambda)}^{2}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{0}^{+}}\tilde{p}_{\alpha}

for

\tilde{p}_{\alpha}=\prod_{m=0}^{\lambda\cdot\alpha^{\vee}-1}\frac{\quantity(1-\kappa_{\alpha+\mu_\alpha^{\mathrm{t}}mc}^{-1}
\kappa_{2\alpha+2\mu_\alpha^{\mathrm{t}}mc}^{-1}e^{-\alpha}q_\alpha^{-m})\quantity(1+\kappa_{\alpha+\mu_\alpha^{\mathrm{t}}mc}^{-1}\kappa_{2\alpha+2\mu_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{t}}mc}e^{-\alpha}q_\alpha^{-m})}{1-e^{-2\alpha}q_{\alpha}^{-2m}}.

If
\alpha\in R_{0}^{+}
is long (so that
\alpha\in R
is medium),

\tilde{p}_{\alpha}
reduces to

\tilde{p}_{\alpha}=\frac{\quantity(\kappa_{\alpha}^{-2}e^{-\alpha};q_\alpha)_{\lambda\cdot\alpha^{\vee}}}{\quantity(e^{-\alpha};q_\alpha)_{\lambda\cdot\alpha^{\vee}}}.

Otherwise, if
\alpha\in R_{0}^{+}
is short,
we have that
\mu\cdot\alpha^{\vee}
is even, and we have

\kappa_{\alpha+\mu_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{t}}mc}=\begin{cases}\kappa_{\theta}&m\in 2\mathbb{Z}\\
\kappa_{a_{0}}&m\in 2\mathbb{Z}+1\end{cases}

and similarly for
\kappa_{2\alpha+2\mu_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{t}}mc}
.
Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\prod_{m=0}^{\lambda\cdot(2\alpha)^{\vee}-1}\frac{\quantity(1-\kappa_\theta^{-1}\kappa_{2\theta}^{-1}e^{-\alpha}q_\alpha^{-2m})\quantity(1+\kappa_\theta^{-1}\kappa_{2\theta}e^{-\alpha}q_\alpha^{-2m})}{\quantity(1-e^{-2\alpha}q_\alpha^{-4m})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\frac{\quantity(1-\kappa_{a_0}^{-1}\kappa_{2a_0}^{-1}e^{-\alpha}q^{-2m-1})\quantity(1+\kappa_{a_0}^{-1}\kappa_{2a_0}e^{-\alpha}q_\alpha^{-2m-1})}{\quantity(1-e^{-2\alpha}q_\alpha^{-4m-2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\quantity(a^{-1}e^{-\alpha},b^{-1}e^{-\alpha},c^{-1}e^{-\alpha}, d^{-1}e^{-\alpha}; q_\alpha^{-2})_{\lambda\cdot(2\alpha)^{\vee}}}{\quantity(e^{-2\alpha};q_\alpha^{-2})_{\lambda\cdot\alpha^{\vee}}}.

Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle c_{\tau(\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\tau(\lambda)}^{2}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{0}^{+,\mathrm{s}}}\frac{\quantity(a^{-1}e^{-\alpha},b^{-1}e^{-\alpha},c^{-1}e^{-\alpha}, d^{-1}e^{-\alpha}; q_\alpha^{-2})_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda\cdot\alpha^{\vee}}}{\quantity(e^{-2\alpha};q_\alpha^{-2})_{\lambda\cdot\alpha^{\vee}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\prod_{\alpha\in R^{+,\mathrm{l}}}\frac{\quantity(\kappa_{\alpha}^{-2}e^{-\alpha};q_\alpha)_{\lambda\cdot\alpha^{\vee}}}{\quantity(e^{-\alpha};q_\alpha)_{\lambda\cdot\alpha^{\vee}}}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\tau(\lambda)}^{2}\frac{p}{p(\cdot+\lambda)}.

We have thus shown that
c_{\tau(\lambda)}
is proportional to

\frac{p}{p(\cdot+\lambda)}
in every case.
And hence by the argument above, the claim follows.
∎

Lemma 8.1

(
\mathsf{AI}_{1}
)
.

Let

\Res(\Phi^{\omega_1}_{\chi_{\tau,\delta},\chi_{\sigma,\epsilon}})=e^{\omega_{1}}+\epsilon\delta q^{\epsilon\sigma+\delta\tau+1}e^{-\omega_{1}}.

Proof.

We start by constructing spherical vectors.
With respect to the basis
v_{\omega_{1}},v_{-\omega_{1}}
, the
operator
B_{1}=F_{1}+q^{-1}E_{1}K_{1}^{-1}+[\sigma]_{q}K_{1}^{-1}

\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix[\sigma]_qq^{-1} & 1\\1 & [\sigma]_qq\endlx@physics@matrix),

which has eigenvalues
[\sigma\pm 1]_{q}
with eigenvector

v_{\pm}=v_{\omega_{1}}\pm q^{\pm\sigma}v_{-\omega_{1}}

Similarly, replacing
\sigma
by
\tau
, we obtain the following
eigenform for the eigenvalue
[\tau\pm 1]_{q}
:

f_{\pm}=v_{\omega_{1}}^{*}\pm q^{\pm\tau}v_{-\omega_{1}}^{*}.

We therefore obtain

f_{\epsilon}(K_{h}v_{\delta})=q^{\langle h,\omega_{1}\rangle}+\epsilon\delta q^{\epsilon\sigma+\delta\tau-\langle h,\omega_{1}\rangle},

so that
\Res(f_\epsilon(\cdot v_\delta))=q^{-1/2}e^{\omega_{1}}+\epsilon\delta q^{\epsilon\sigma+\delta\tau+1/2}e^{-\omega_{1}}
.
We conclude that a properly normalised character spherical function
reads

e^{\omega_{1}}+\epsilon\delta q^{\epsilon\sigma+\delta\tau+1}e^{-\omega_{1}}.\qed

Remark 8.10
