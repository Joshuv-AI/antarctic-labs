On the p p -part of the conductor of a generalised character
Markus Linckelmann

Abstract
. We show that the p p -part of the conductor of a generalised character of a finite group is equal to the conductor of its generalised decomposition numbers. We use this to show that p p -parts of conductors of irreducible characters are preserved under isotypies and perfect isometries that arise in the context of stable equivalences of Morita type with endopermutation source. We apply this to blocks with abelian defect and Frobenius inertial quotient.

We use without further comment the well-known fact that for any two positive

Proof of Theorem

.

Denote by

the non-negative integer satisfying

.
The right side in the statement of Theorem

is a power of

since all generalised decomposition numbers are contained in a cyclotomic
field of a

-power order root of unity. Denote by

the non-negative integer
such that
p^{d}=c\left(\{d^{u}_{\chi,\varphi}\ |\ u\in G_{p},\ \varphi\in\mathrm{IBr}(C_{G}(u))\}\right)
.
We need to show that

.

In the defining equation above of the
generalised decomposition numbers
d^{u}_{\chi,\varphi}
, the numbers

\varphi(s)
are contained in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta^{\prime})
, where
\zeta^{\prime}
is a root of unity
of order the

-part of

. Thus

is contained in

{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{p^{d}},\zeta^{\prime})
, and hence all vlaues of

are

{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{p^{d}n^{\prime}})
, where

.
Since the values of

are also all contained in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{p^{a}m})

and since

is minimal with this property, it follows that

.

For the other inequality we use Brauer’s reciprocity, as descibed in

[
11
, 5.14.7, 5.14.8]

Let

, and denote by
\langle-,-\rangle^{\prime}
the
bilinear map on
K

p^{\prime}
-elements of
C_{G}(u)
given by

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle^{\prime}=

\frac{1}{|C_{G}(u)|}\sum_{s\in C_{G}(u)_{p}^{\prime}}\ \alpha(s)\beta(s^{-1}
, where
\alpha
,
\beta
are
K
-valued class funtions
defined on
G_{p^{\prime}}
. Let
\psi\in

\mathrm{IBr}(C_{G}(u)
, and let
\Psi
be the character
of a projective cover of a simple

-module with Brauer character

.
Brauer’s reciprocity, applied to

, states that for any
\varphi\in

\mathrm{IBr}(C_{G}(u))

the number
\langle\varphi,\Psi\rangle^{\prime}
is equal to

and equal to

for all

\frac{1}{|C_{G}(u)|}\sum_{s\in C_{G}(u)_{p^{\prime}}}\chi(us)\Psi(s^{-1})\ =\ \langle d^{u}_{G}(\chi),\Psi\rangle^{\prime}\ =\ \sum_{\varphi\in\mathrm{IBr}(C_{G}(u)}\ d^{u}_{\chi,\varphi}\langle\varphi,\Psi\rangle^{\prime}\ =\ d^{u}_{\chi,\psi}.

Now the values

are all contained in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta^{\prime})

on the left side shows that the numbers

are contained in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{p^{a}}\zeta^{\prime})

and all
\psi\in

\mathrm{IBr}(C_{G}(u))

{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{p^{d}})

3.
On generalised decomposition numbers

We refer to
[
11

for the Brauer construction, and for
basic terminology regarding Puig’s notion of pointed groups we refer
to the exposition in
[

Let
G
be a finite group,
B
a block of
{\mathcal{O}G}
, and suppose that
K
is a splitting
field for

. We deote by

the subgroup of

generated
by the characters of projective indecomposable

of

consists of all generalised characters which are perpendicular
to

with respect to the standard scalar product on
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Irr}(B)

We will use Puig’s description
of generalised decomposition numbers (cf.
[
11
, Theorem 5.15.3]
):
for
\chi\in

{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Irr}(B)
,

a

p
-element in
G

\varphi\in

\mathrm{IBr}({\mathcal{O}}C_{G}(u))
we have

where

({\mathcal{O}G})^{\langle u\rangle}

\langle u\rangle

on

with the property that
\mathrm{Br}_{\langle u\rangle}(j)
is a primitive
idempotent in

which does not annihilate the simple

-modules with Brauer character
\varphi
. One immediate
consequence of this description of generalised decomposition numbers is the
following fact, stated for future reference:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
\chi\in

{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Irr}(G)
. For any
u\in G_{p}

and any idempotent
f
in
{\mathcal{O}}C_{G}(u)
we have
c(\chi)_{p}\geq

c(\chi(uf))
.

We mention another immediate consequence of standard properties of idempotents.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\chi\in

{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Irr}(B)
,
u\in G_{p}
, and
\varphi\in

\mathrm{IBr}(C_{G}(u))
.
Let

be a primitive idempotent in
{\mathcal{O}}C_{G}(u)
such that
{\mathcal{O}}C_{G}(u)f

is a projective cover of a simple
kC_{G}(u)
-module with Brauer character

.
Then
d^{u}_{\chi,\varphi}=\chi(uf)
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathrm{Br}_{\langle u\rangle}(f)
is the image of
f
in
kC_{G}(u)
, hence
a primitive idempotent. By standard lifting theorems of idempotents, there is
a primitive idempotent

in
{\mathcal{O}G}^{\langle u\rangle}
such that

\mathrm{Br}_{\langle u\rangle}(j)=

\mathrm{Br}_{\langle u\rangle}(f)
.
It follows from

that we have

\chi(uj)=

d^{u}_{\chi,\varphi}
.

-modules
j^{\prime}M\otimes_{B}U
and

V_{Q}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}jU
coincide when evaluated at
u
. Moreover, the character of
V_{Q}

evaluated at
u
is in
\{1,-1\}
. (We use here the hypothesis that
V
has a

{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued character.) Thus the evaluations at
u
of the characters
of
V_{Q}\otimes jU
and
jU
differ at most by a sign.
Together it follows that the generalised decomposition numbers

\chi(uj)
and
\psi(uj^{\prime})
differ at most by a sign. The result follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

By Lemma
5.5
, given an
{\mathcal{O}}
-free
B
-module of finite

{\mathcal{O}}
-rank, the characters of
U
and of
M\otimes_{B}U
have the same
generalised non-ordinary decomposition numbers. Thus the first
statement follows from Theorem
1.1
.
Let
\psi\in

{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Irr}(B)
. Denote by
-\cdot_{B}-
the maps on Grothendieck
groups induced by the functor
-\otimes_{B}-
.
By
[
8
, Proposition 3.3]
the generalised character

\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(B)}\ \Phi(\chi)\chi^{*}-\chi_{M}
is in fact a generalised
projective character of
G^{\prime}\times G
. Here
\chi^{*}
is defined by

\chi^{*}(g)=\chi(g^{-1})
for all
g\in G
.
Thus

\Phi(\psi)-\Phi_{M}(\psi)=(\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(B)}\ \Phi(\chi)\chi^{*}-\chi_{M})\cdot_{B}\psi

is a generalised projective character in
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Irr}(B^{\prime})
.
Therefore, if
u
is a nontrivial
p
-element in
G^{\prime}
and
j
an idempotent belonging to
a local point of
\langle u\rangle
on
B^{\prime}
, then this character evaluated at elements
of the form
vj
, with
v\in\langle u\rangle

yields a generalised projective character of
{\mathcal{O}}\langle u\rangle
, hence vanishes
for
u
a nontrivial
p
-element in
G^{\prime}
. Thus the generalised characters

\Phi(\psi)=(\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(B)}\ \Phi(\chi)\chi^{*})\cdot_{B}\psi
and

\Phi_{M}(\psi)=\chi_{M}\cdot_{B}\psi
have the same generalised non-ordinary
decomposition numbers. As before, Theorem
1.1
implies that they have
conductors whose
p
-parts are equal.
Combined with the first statement and
applied with
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(B)
instead of
\psi
implies
the last statement in Theorem
1.5
.
∎

As mentioned in the Introduction, the Corollaries
1.6
and
1.7

can be played back more directly to Theorem
1.1
by making use of
long standing results on generalised decomposition numbers in these cases;
we give some references in the proofs below, which circumvent the need to use
Theorem
1.5
. In the case of blocks with a cyclic defect group, the
generalised decomposition numbers are due to Dade
[

]
,
except for the explicit connection between the signs that appear there on the one hand,
and the endopermutation module
V
and perfect isometry on the other hand.

Proof of Corollary
1.6
.

Assume that
P
is cyclic. By
[
10
, Théorème (13.2), Théorème (13.6)]

there is an isometry such that the non-ordinary generalised decomposition numbers
of corresponding irreducible characters are equal up to signs. This isometry is
automatically perfect by
[
8
, Proposition 3.3]
. The fact that this isometry
is perfect is also shown in
[
9
]
; see

[
12
, Theorem 11.1.14]
for a broader expository account.
The result follows from Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.7
.

Note that
C
has
{\mathcal{O}}(P\rtimes E)
as source algebra. Thus the hypotheses
and
[

, Theorem 5.10.5]
imply the existence of a perfect isometry
which preserves generalised decomposition numbers up to signs.
Thus the result follows again from Theorem

.

6.
Some remarks on conductors and restriction to blocks of subgroups

Restriction and induction functors between blocks of finite groups
are given by bimodules with trivial source, so they differ in general from the
stable equivalences of Morita type with endopermutation source considered
in the main results of this paper. We mention some cases where we do get
results on restrictions and truncated restrictions.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Assume that
K
contains a primitive
|G|
-th root
of unity. Let
\chi\in

{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, and let
u\in G_{p}
and
\varphi\in

\mathrm{IBr}(C_{G}(u))
such that
c(\chi)_{p}=c(d^{u}_{\chi,\varphi})
.
Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
containing
C_{G}(u)
. We have

c(\chi)_{p}=

c(\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi))_{p}
.

Proof.

Clearly
c(\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi))_{p}\leq

c(\chi)_{p}
. For the other inequality, note that Lemma
3.2
and
Theorem
1.1
imply that
c(\chi)_{p}
is the maximum of the numbers

\chi(vf)
, with
v\in G_{p}
and
f
running over the primitive idempotents
in
{\mathcal{O}}C_{G}(v)
. Applied to
v=u
and using the hypotheses, there is
a primitive idempotent
f\in

{\mathcal{O}}C_{G}(u)
such that

c(\chi)_{p}=

c(d^{u}_{\chi,\varphi})=

c(\chi(uf))\leq

c(\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi))_{p}
.
∎

Given a generalised character
\chi
of a finite group algebra
{\mathcal{O}G}

and a central idempotent
b
in
Z({\mathcal{O}G})
we denote by
b\cdot\chi
the
component of
\chi
belonging to
{\mathcal{O}Gb}
; that is,
(b\cdot\chi)(g)=

\chi(bg)
for all
g\in G
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
b
,
b^{\prime}
be orthogonal idempotents
in
Z({\mathcal{O}G})
. Let
\chi\in

{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Irr}(G)
such that
\chi=

(b+b^{\prime})\cdot\chi
.
We have
c(\chi)_{p}=

\max\{c(b\cdot\chi)_{p},c(b^{\prime}\cdot\chi)_{p}\}
.

Proof.

The generalised decomposition numbers are partitioned according to
blocks (by Brauer’s Second Main Theorem, which is implied directly by Puig’s
description of these numbers). Thus Theorem
1.1
implies the Lemma.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
B
a block of
{\mathcal{O}G}
,
P
a defect group of

B
, and
H
a subgroup of
G
containing
N_{G}(P)
. Let
C
be the block
of
{\mathcal{O}H}
with
P
as defect group which is the Brauer correspondent of
B
.
Let
\chi\in

{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Irr}(B)
. If
c(\chi)_{p}=

c(1_{C}\cdot\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi))_{p}
,
then
c(\chi)_{p}=

c(\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi))_{p}
.

Proof.

We clearly have
c(\chi)_{p}\geq

c(\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi))_{p}\geq

c(1_{C}\cdot\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi))_{p}
, where the last inequality
follows from Lemma
6.2
. The result follows from the
hypotheses.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
B
a block of
{\mathcal{O}G}
, and
P
a defect group
of
B
. Suppose that
H
is a subgroup of
G
containing
P
such that
for all
g\in

G\setminus H
we have
P\cap{{}^{g}P}=\{1\}
.
Then for every
\chi\in

{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Irr}(B)
we have

c(\chi)_{p}=c(\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi))_{p}=c(1_{C}\cdot\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi))_{p}.

Proof.

It is well-known (see e.g.
[
12
, Theorem 9.8.6]
) that the hypotheses
imply that the truncated induction functor
1_{B}\cdot\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{H}
and the truncated
restriction functor
1_{C}\cdot\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}

given by the
B
-
C
-bimodule
B\cdot 1_{C}
and its dual induces a stable
equivalence of Morita type whose indecomposable summands have trivial
source. Thus Theorem
1.5
implies that

c(\chi)_{p}=

c(1_{C}\cdot\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi))_{p}
.
We clearly have
c(\chi)_{p}\geq

c(\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi))_{p}
.
Since
H
contains
C_{G}(u)
for any nontrivial element
u
in
P
,
we also have
c(\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi))_{p}\geq

c(1_{C}\cdot\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi))_{p}
, by Lemma
6.2
.
The result follows.
∎

The following Corollary is a special case of
[
7
, Theorem C]

(and is in one of the steps in the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 3.4]
);
we mention this just to illustrate the use of Theorem
1.5
.

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
B
a block of
{\mathcal{O}G}
with a nontrivial cyclic
defect group
P
. Denote by
P_{1}
the unique subgroup of order
p
of
P

and set
H=N_{G}(P_{1})
. Denote by
C
the block of
{\mathcal{O}H}
which is the Brauer
correspondent of
B
. There is a bijection
\gamma:\mathrm{Irr}(B)\to

\mathrm{Irr}(C)

and there are signs
\delta_{\chi}\in

\{1,-1\}
such that

{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Irr}(B)\cong

{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Irr}(C)
sending
\chi\in

\mathrm{Irr}(B)
to
\delta_{\chi}\gamma(\chi)

is a perfect isometry, and such that

c(\chi)_{p}=\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi))_{p}=c(1_{C}\cdot\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi))_{p}=c(\gamma(\chi))_{p}.

Proof.

It is well-known and easy to check that for
g\in G\setminus H
we have

P\cap{{}^{g}P}=\{1\}
. Moreover, by

[
12
, Theorem 11.10.2]
,
there is a bijection
\gamma:\mathrm{Irr}(B)\to

\mathrm{Irr}(C)
such that

1_{C}\cdot\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi)=

\delta_{\chi}\gamma(\chi)+\psi_{\chi}
for some
projective character
\psi
in
\Pr(C)
and some
\delta_{\chi}\in

\{1,-1\}
.
Thus the Corollary follows from Proposition
6.4
and Theorem
1.5
.
∎
