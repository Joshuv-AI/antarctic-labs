Revised Demailly’s Affineness Criterion and Algebraization of Entire Grauert Tubes
Kyobeom Song

Abstract
We provide a partial answer to Burns’ 1982 conjecture on the affineness of entire Grauert tubes: the complement of a codimension-one subset of an entire Grauert tube is affine. This result is obtained by establishing a generalized version of Demailly’s criterion for affineness of Stein manifolds, which may be of independent interest.



1
Introduction

A complex manifold
X
is called a Stein manifold if it satisfies one of the following equivalent conditions:

•

The holomorphic function ring
\mathcal{O}(X)
yields a proper holomorphic embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{N}
for some
N
.

•

There exists a strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion function
\rho
on
X
, i.e.,
dd^{c}\rho>0
and
\{\rho\leq c\}
is compact for all
c\geq 0
.

•

Cartan’s Theorems A and B hold, i.e.,

(A)

For any coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
and any open set
U\subset X
, the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{F}(U)
is generated by global sections.

(B)

H^{i}(X,\mathcal{F})=0
for all
i>0
.

One may notice that Stein manifolds in complex geometry correspond to affine varieties in algebraic geometry. Indeed, the analytification of any smooth affine variety is a Stein manifold. However, since Stein manifolds are defined in terms of arbitrary holomorphic functions rather than regular functions, they admit a much broader and more flexible class of examples than affine varieties. For instance, any pseudoconvex open subset of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, any domain of holomorphy, as well as any closed complex submanifold of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, is Stein.

Another example illustrating the flexibility of Stein manifolds is given by Grauert tubes. For any real-analytic Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of dimension
n
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
T^{\varepsilon}M:=\{v\in TM\mid\|v\|_{g}<\varepsilon\}
admits a Stein manifold structure called a
Grauert tube
, satisfying the following properties
[
13
,
18
,
11
,
6
]
:

1.

\rho:=\|v\|_{g}^{2}
is strictly plurisubharmonic and the Kähler metric associated to
i\partial\bar{\partial}\rho
restricts to
g
on
M
,

2.

(i\partial\bar{\partial}\sqrt{\rho})^{n}=0
(the Homogeneous Complex Monge–Ampère equation, HCMA).

In particular,
any
real-analytic manifold admits a Stein manifold that deformation retracts onto it, showing that the class of Stein manifolds is as vast as that of arbitrary real-analytic manifolds. If
\varepsilon=\infty
, i.e., if such a Stein structure exists on the entire tangent bundle
TM
, we call
T^{\infty}M:=TM
an
entire Grauert tube
, and the triple
(TM,M,\rho)
a Monge–Ampère model.

In 1982, Burns
[
5
]
proposed the following conjecture.

Burns Conjecture.
Is an entire Grauert tube an affine variety?

This question is also mentioned in Demailly’s 1985 paper, where necessary and sufficient conditions for a Stein manifold to be affine are established
[
9
, Problème 9.3]
.

The interest of this problem lies both in the statement itself and in its broader connection to complex geometry. Intrinsically, a Stein manifold
X
with maximal-dimensional real center
M:=\{\rho=0\}
can be highly flexible, as seen in the case of Grauert tubes. In contrast, affine varieties with the same property are much more rigid. There is no a priori reason that the algebraic property of affineness should be characterized purely by an analytic condition such as the Monge–Ampère equation. If true, this would reveal a nontrivial link between algebraic and analytic geometry, in the spirit of GAGA.

Moreover, this question is closely related to the Good Complexification Conjecture proposed by Totaro
[
19
]
. A closed smooth real manifold
M
is said to admit a
good complexification
if there exists an affine algebraic variety
U
defined over
\mathbb{R}
such that
U(\mathbb{R})=M
and the inclusion
U(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow U(\mathbb{C})
is a homotopy equivalence.

Good Complexification Conjecture.
A closed manifold admits a metric of nonnegative curvature if and only if it admits a good complexification.

This conjecture implies several known conjectures on nonnegatively curved manifolds. Kulkarni
[
12
]
showed that if a manifold
M
admits a good complexification, then
\chi(M)\geq 0
, in accordance with the Hopf sign conjecture. Totaro
[
19
]
further proved that stronger restrictions are imposed on the rational cohomology ring. Thus, good complexifications can be viewed as an attempt to understand the rigidity of nonnegatively curved manifolds through underlying algebraic structures.

It is known that if
(M,g)
admits an entire Grauert tube
TM
, then it should be nonnegatively curved
[
13
]
, and
TM
is homotopy equivalent to
M
. Hence, as observed by Totaro
[
19
]
, the Burns conjecture can be interpreted as asking whether an entire Grauert tube provides a good complexification. It is also remarkable that most known nonnegatively curved manifolds admit entire Grauert tubes, which also provide good complexification
[
2
]
.

Over the past 44 years, this problem has been approached from various perspectives. In 2000, Aguilar and Burns
[
1
]
claimed a proof of the conjecture; however, Totaro
[
19
]
pointed out that Proposition 2.1, which is central to their argument, is not generally accepted. Later, in 2015 (published in 2018), Burns and Leung
[
7
]
proved that if the base manifold
(M,g)
is Zoll, i.e., all geodesics are periodic with the same period, then its entire Grauert tube is affine. In a series of works in the 2010s, Zelditch
[
20
,
21
]
showed that Laplacian eigenfunctions on
(M,g)
admit analytic continuations to the entire Grauert tube, and that the
\limsup
of their absolute values with respect to
\sqrt{\rho}
is exponentially bounded in
\sqrt{\rho}
. This suggests the abundance of functions on the entire Grauert tube with controlled growth, analogous to regular functions on affine varieties. In addition, it has recently been shown that entire Grauert tubes satisfy several strong topological restrictions (e.g., rational ellipticity
[
8
,
16
]
, zero topological entropy of geodesic flow
[
17
]
).

Among these, Aguilar and Burns
[
1
]
introduced a crucial nontrivial idea that motivates our approach. They attempted to prove the conjecture using the following affineness criterion of Demailly
[
9
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Demailly’s Affineness Criterion)
.

Let
X
be a Stein manifold of dimension
n
(possibly with finitely many singularities). The following are equivalent:

1.

X
is biholomorphic to an affine variety.

2.

There exists a strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion function
\phi
on
X
such that, writing
\alpha:=dd^{c}\phi
and
\beta:=dd^{c}e^{\phi}
, the following conditions hold:

(a)

X
has finite Monge–Ampère volume, i.e.,
\int_{X}\alpha^{n}<\infty
,

(b)

there exists
\psi\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X,\mathbb{R})\cap C^{0}(X_{\mathrm{reg}},\mathbb{R})
and constants
A,B>0
such that

\psi\leq A\phi+B\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{Ric}(\beta)+\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\psi\geq 0,

(c)

\dim H^{2k}(X;\mathbb{R})<\infty
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Aguilar and Burns proposed to construct the function
\psi
in condition (b) using the
(n,0)
-part of the analytic continuation of the volume form of the base manifold
(M,g)
to the entire Grauert tube. However, for this approach to work, the resulting
(n,0)
-form
V^{n,0}
must be globally well-defined. As we explain in Section
9
, even when such a form can be defined, there exist explicit examples of entire Grauert tubes for which
V^{n,0}
has poles along a singular set
S
. In this case, the function
\psi
constructed from
V^{n,0}
takes the value
-\infty
on
S
, and hence Theorem
1.1
cannot be applied.

To overcome this difficulty, we first establish a revised version of Demailly’s affineness criterion (Theorem
1.1
), which is of independent interest.

Theorem 1.2

(Revised Affineness Criterion)
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional Stein manifold equipped with a strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion
\phi
.
Assume that
X
has finite Monge–Ampère volume

\mathrm{Vol}(X)\;:=\;\int_{X}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}\;<\;\infty.

(i)

Assume that there exist constants
A,B>0
and a function
\psi\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X,\mathbb{R})
such that, for
\beta:=dd^{c}(e^{\phi})
, in the sense of
(1,1)
-currents, one has

\mathrm{Ric}(\beta)+\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\psi\;\geq\;0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\psi~\leq~A\phi+B

Then, writing
S:=\{x~|~e^{-2\psi}\notin L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(x)\}
, for any
p\in X\setminus S
, there exists
b_{p}\in\mathcal{O}(X)
with

b_{p}(p)=1
and
b_{p}|_{S}=0
such that
X\setminus b_{p}^{-1}(0)
is biholomorphic to a smooth affine variety that has
1/b_{p}
as a regular function.

Furthermore, for any finitely many points
p_{i}\in X\setminus S
, there exists a normal affine variety
\mathcal{A}
such that each
X\setminus b_{p_{i}}^{-1}(0)
embeds as a Zariski open subset of
\mathcal{A}
.

(ii)

If
\dim H^{2k}(X\setminus S;\mathbb{R})<\infty
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
X\setminus S
is biholomorphic to a quasi-affine variety, i.e., a Zariski open set of an affine variety.

(iii)

If
\dim H^{2k}(X\setminus S;\mathbb{R})<\infty
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
X\setminus S
is a Stein manifold, then there exists an irreducible normal affine variety
\mathcal{A}
and its algebraic hypersurface
H
such that
X\setminus S
is biholomorphic to
\mathcal{A}\setminus H
.

(iv)

If
S=\emptyset
and
\dim H^{2k}(X;\mathbb{R})<\infty
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
X
is biholomorphic to an affine variety.

We then show that the metric
g
on the base manifold
(M,g)
extends meromorphically to a nondegenerate symmetric
(2,0)
-tensor
g_{\mathbb{C}}
on the entire Grauert tube
TM
. Using this, we prove that for the meromorphic nonvanishing section
\det(g_{\mathbb{C}})\in H^{0}(TM,K_{TM}^{2})
,

\phi:=\log(1+\cosh(\|v\|_{g})),\qquad\beta:=dd^{c}(e^{\phi}),\qquad\psi:=-\log\|\det(g_{\mathbb{C}})\|_{\beta}

satisfy the assumption of Theorem
1.2
(i), and the set
S:=\{x~|~e^{-2\psi}\notin L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(x)\}
is exactly the pole set of
\det(g_{\mathbb{C}})
. Hence, we obtain the following: an entire Grauert tube is affine outside a codimension-one subset.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold admitting an entire Grauert tube
TM
. Then the metric
g
extends to a nondegenerate meromorphic
(2,0)
-tensor
g_{\mathbb{C}}
on
TM
. Let
S
denote the pole set of
g_{\mathbb{C}}
. For any
p\in TM\setminus S
, there exists a holomorphic function
b_{p}\in\mathcal{O}(TM)
such that
b_{p}(p)=1
and
b_{p}|_{S}=0
, and the open set
TM\setminus b_{p}^{-1}(0)
is biholomorphic to a smooth affine variety on which
1/b_{p}
is a regular function.

Furthermore, for any finitely many points
p_{i}\in TM\setminus S
, there exists a normal affine variety
\mathcal{A}
such that each
TM\setminus b_{p_{i}}^{-1}(0)
embeds as a Zariski open subset of
\mathcal{A}
.

For a detailed discussion of Theorem
1.3
, see Section
9
.

1.1
Discussion on Theorem
1.2

Demailly’s affineness criterion (Theorem
1.1
) provides a necessary and sufficient condition for a Stein manifold to be affine, and serves as a cornerstone connecting Stein and affine geometry. Among its hypotheses, the most essential is condition (b), a Ricci curvature bound. Conditions (a) finite volume, and (c) finite-dimensional cohomology are properties that are naturally expected for a Stein manifold presumed to be affine.

Condition (b) eliminates non-affine behavior in the Stein setting as follows. An affine variety admits a coordinate ring of regular functions and comes with a divisor at infinity, yielding strong control on both its function theory and boundary behavior. In contrast, for a Stein manifold, the analogue of the coordinate ring is
\mathcal{O}(X)
, which consists of arbitrary holomorphic functions, and the boundary can be highly pathological.

The requirement that there exists a function
\psi
with controlled growth
\psi\leq A\phi+B
such that
dd^{c}\psi
bounds the Ricci form implies, via Hörmander’s
L^{2}
estimates, that weighted
L^{2}
holomorphic functions are dense in
\mathcal{O}(X)
. Moreover, control of the
L^{2}
norms of holomorphic functions provides a way to regulate pathological behavior at the boundary of the Stein manifold.

However, condition (b) is also the most difficult to verify in practice. The usual strategies for constructing such a function
\psi
are as follows:

1.

Finding a global Ricci potential
\psi
with controlled growth
\psi\leq A\phi+B
such that
dd^{c}\psi=-\mathrm{Ric}(\beta)
.

2.

Alternatively, one may use multiple sections of the determinant line bundle
K_{X}^{-m}
. Recall that such bundles carry a natural norm induced by the Kähler form
\beta
. Since
\mathrm{Ric}(\beta)
is locally expressed as
-i\partial\bar{\partial}\log\det(\beta)
, it follows that for a section
s_{i}\in H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{-m})
which does not vanish at a given point, the function

\psi_{i}:=\frac{1}{m}\log\|s_{i}\|_{\beta}^{2}

satisfies
i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{i}=-\mathrm{Ric}(\beta)
away from the zero set of
s_{i}
. Therefore, by choosing finitely many sections
s_{i}
with no common zero and such that
\|s_{i}\|_{\beta}\leq e^{A\phi+B}
, one may define
\psi:=\max_{i}\psi_{i}
.

Both approaches are extremely difficult to realize on a general Stein manifold. The first condition would imply the vanishing of the first Chern class, which is a strong restriction not expected even for general affine varieties. The second approach may appear more accessible in light of Cartan’s Theorem A; however, even constructing a single section satisfying the growth condition
\|s\|_{\beta}\leq e^{A\phi+B}
is in general highly nontrivial.

Indeed, the approach of Aguilar and Burns ultimately amounts to finding a nonvanishing
(n,0)
-form with controlled growth, i.e., a special section of the canonical bundle. This in turn yields a nonvanishing holomorphic section of
K_{X}\otimes\overline{K_{X}}
via
V^{n,0}\wedge\overline{V^{n,0}}
. However,
V^{n,0}
may in general have poles, therefore this construction provides only a single section in the second strategy described above. We refer to Section
9
for further details.

In the context of this usual strategy, Theorem
1.2
provides a criterion that is more broadly applicable than Demailly’s original criterion. Indeed, even in situations where it is difficult or impossible to construct more than one section
s
of
K_{X}^{-m}
with controlled growth arising from the second strategy, by setting
\psi:=\frac{1}{m}\log\|s\|_{\beta}^{2}
, one still obtains a certain degree of algebraicity of
X
.

The statements in the theorem can be interpreted as follows.

(i)

Without any cohomological assumptions
, the existence of such a
\psi
ensures that
X\setminus S
is covered by birationally equivalent affine Zariski open subsets.

(ii), (iii)

As additional reasonable conditions are imposed, stronger global algebraicity of
X\setminus S
follows.

(iv)

In the case
S=\emptyset
, one recovers Demailly’s Affineness Criterion
1.1
under a relaxed regularity condition on
\psi
.

Finally, we remark that the additional assumptions appearing at each stage of Theorem
1.2
are, in a certain sense, essential. First, the finite-dimensionality of cohomology required to pass from (i) to (ii) is already noted to be necessary for global algebraicity in Demailly’s original work
[
9
, Chapter 13]
. For the additional assumption from (ii) to (iii), note that
\psi
can be quite arbitrary. For instance, let
X=\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\phi=\log(1+\|Z\|^{2})
. Then
\beta:=dd^{c}e^{\phi}
is Ricci-flat, and any plurisubharmonic function
\psi
satisfying
\psi\leq A\phi+B
meets the condition of the theorem. If we take
\psi=n\log\|Z\|
, then
X\setminus S=\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, so all the conditions in (ii) are satisfied, but this space is not Stein. This shows that the Stein assumption in (iii) cannot be omitted.

Part I  Revised Demailly’s Criterion

Our main observations for the proof of Theorem
1.2
are as follows, and the proof will proceed accordingly.

1.

If
e^{-2\psi}
is integrable, then most of the
L^{2}
estimates essential for controlling the function theory on
X
become available.

2.

The singularity set

S:=\{x\mid e^{-2\psi}\notin L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(x)\}

to which the above argument does not apply is a zero locus of a multiplier ideal. Hence, using functions
b_{p}
obtained via suitable weighted
L^{2}
estimates, one can “cut out”
S
and reduce the situation to one analogous to Demailly’s original criterion. More precisely, using weighted
L^{2}
functions
b_{p}
satisfying
b_{p}|_{S}=0
, we consider the open set
X\setminus b_{p}^{-1}(0)
. This allows us to carry over much of the argument of Demailly’s criterion to the present setting.

3.

Moreover, the function theory on
X\setminus b_{p}^{-1}(0)
enjoys many remarkable properties. By exploiting these, one can prove the affineness of
X\setminus b_{p}^{-1}(0)
without imposing any cohomological conditions.

Conventions and notation.

Throughout this paper, we define
d^{c}=i(\bar{\partial}-\partial)
and
dd^{c}=2i\,\partial\bar{\partial}
. For a real-valued function
V
, we write
V_{+}:=\max(0,V)
.

2
Demailly’s Monge–Ampère measure theory

2.1
\phi
-polynomials

A fundamental difference between holomorphic functions on a Stein manifold and regular functions on an affine variety is that the former allow exponential growth (for instance
e^{z}
) and hence need not be algebraic. Consequently, in order to prove that a given Stein manifold is in fact affine, one must select candidates for coordinate functions whose growth is sufficiently controlled so that they behave similarly to regular functions.

We therefore introduce the following definitions from
[
9
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional Stein manifold and let
\phi
be a strictly psh exhaustion function on
X
. Set
\alpha:=dd^{c}\phi
.

1.

For
r>0
, consider the hypersurface
S(r):=\{\phi=r\}
equipped with the measure

\mu_{r}:=\alpha^{n-1}\wedge d^{c}\phi,\qquad\mu_{r}(V):=\int_{S(r)}Vd\mu_{r}~,

where
V
is a measurable function. For a measurable function
F
, define the
\phi
-degree of
F
by

\delta_{\phi}(F):=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{r}\int_{S(r)}\log_{+}|F|\,d\mu_{r}=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{r}\mu_{r}(log_{+}|F|)\in[0,\infty],

where
\log_{+}t:=\max(0,\log t)
.

2.

Define

A_{\phi}(X):=\{F\in\mathcal{O}(X)\mid\delta_{\phi}(F)<\infty\}.

Elements of
A_{\phi}(X)
are called
\phi
-polynomials
. By the properties of
\limsup
and
\log
,
A_{\phi}(X)
is a ring.

3.

The fraction field

\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(X):=\left\{\frac{F}{G}\mid F,G\in A_{\phi}(X)\right\}

is called the
\phi
-rational function field
, and its elements are called
\phi
-rational functions
.

The following result is a consequence of
[
9
, Theorem 8.5]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional Stein manifold with finite Monge–Ampère volume

\mathrm{Vol}(X):=\int_{X}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}<\infty.

Then the following hold:

1.

0\leq\mathrm{trdeg}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(X)\leq n
.

2.

If
\mathrm{trdeg}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(X)=n
, then
\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(X)
is a finite type extension of
\mathbb{C}
.

2.2
Weighted
\phi
-polynomials

Let
X
be a Stein manifold endowed with a strictly psh exhaustion
\phi
and set

\beta:=dd^{c}(e^{\phi}).

Since the main arguments to follow rely on weighted
L^{p}
estimates (in particular
L^{2}
estimates) with respect to the measure
\beta^{n}
, we introduce an analogue of
\phi
-polynomials adapted to this weighted setting, again from
[
9
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Define

L_{\phi}^{p}(X):=\left\{f\in L^{0}(X)\;\middle|\;\int_{X}|f|^{p}e^{-C\phi}\beta^{n}<\infty\text{ for some }C>0\right\},

L_{\phi}^{\infty}(X):=\left\{f\in L^{0}(X)\;\middle|\;|f|\leq e^{C(1+\phi)}\text{ a.e. for some }C>0\right\},

L_{\phi}^{0}(X):=\bigcup_{p>0}L_{\phi}^{p}(X),\qquad A_{\phi}^{p}(X):=L_{\phi}^{p}(X)\cap\mathcal{O}(X).

The following properties follow from
[
9
, Lemmas 11.2 and 11.3]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

1.

1\in A_{\phi}^{p}(X)
for all
p\geq 0
, and for
p\geq q>0
,

L_{\phi}^{p}(X)\subset L_{\phi}^{q}(X),\qquad A_{\phi}^{p}(X)\subset A_{\phi}^{q}(X).

2.

L_{\phi}^{0}(X)
is a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, and
A_{\phi}^{0}(X)
is an integrally closed subalgebra of
L_{\phi}^{0}(X)
.

3.

If
V
is plurisubharmonic and
e^{V}\in L_{\phi}^{0}(X)
, then
\delta_{\phi}(e^{V})<\infty
. In particular,
A_{\phi}^{0}(X)\subset A_{\phi}(X)
and elements of
A_{\phi}^{0}(X)
are
\phi
-polynomials.

4.

For
f\in A_{\phi}^{p}(X)
, one has
|df|_{\beta}\in L_{\phi}^{p}(X)
.

Note that in
[
9
, Lemma 11.3(a)]
, Demailly proved that if a holomorphic function
f
belongs to
L_{\phi}^{0}(X)
, then
\delta_{\phi}(f)<\infty
. However, the argument essentially proves Lemma
2.4
(3). This observation will be used later in Section
4
for the Siegel-type vanishing order counting argument of holomorphic functions. Moreover, statement (3) allows us to restrict future constructions to functions in
A_{\phi}^{0}(X)
while retaining the key properties of
\phi
-polynomials.

3
Construction of
b_{p}
and
X\setminus b_{p}^{-1}(0)

First, note that
S:=\{x\mid e^{-2\psi}\notin L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(x)\}
coincides with the zero set of the multiplier ideal

\mathcal{J}(\psi)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{O}(X)\;\middle|\;|f|^{2}e^{-2\psi}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X)\right\}.

In particular, the existence of
\psi
yields functions in
\mathcal{O}(X)
that vanish along
S
and have controlled
L^{p}
norm. More precisely, this follows from the following Bombieri–Hörmander–Skoda type lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\tau\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X)
satisfy

i\partial\bar{\partial}\tau+\mathrm{Ric}(\beta)\;\geq\;\lambda\,\beta

for some positive continuous function
\lambda
.
Let
u
be a
(0,1)
-form on
X
with locally
L^{2}
coefficients such that
\bar{\partial}u=0
and

\int_{X}\lambda^{-1}\,|u|_{\beta}^{2}\,e^{-\tau}\,\beta^{n}\;<\;\infty.

Then there exists
g\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X)
with
\bar{\partial}g=u
and

\int_{X}|g|^{2}e^{-\tau}\beta^{n}\;\leq\;\int_{X}\lambda^{-1}|u|_{\beta}^{2}e^{-\tau}\beta^{n}\;<\;\infty.

Using this lemma, we control the function theory of
X
as follows.

Proposition 3.2
.

The following statements hold.

(a)

Given finitely many points
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\notin S^{\prime}:=\{x~|~e^{-\psi}\notin L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(x)\}\subset S
, and prescribed
s_{i}
-jets at each

p_{i}
, there exists
f\in A_{\phi}^{0}(X)
realizing the prescribed
s_{i}
-jet at each
p_{i}
.

(b)

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in A_{\phi}^{0}(X)
satisfy

\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}df_{i}(p)\neq 0\qquad\text{for some }p\notin S.

Then there exists
b_{p}\in A_{\phi}^{0}(X)
such that
b_{p}(p)=1
and

\{\textstyle\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}df_{i}=0\}\subset\{b_{p}=0\}.

(c)

\mathrm{trdeg}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(X)=n
, and
\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(X)
is a finite type extension of
\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

(a)
First, note that
S
and
S^{\prime}
are closed. Hence, we can fix finitely many points
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
and choose local coordinate systems

z^{(1)},\dots,z^{(k)}
near
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
, respectively, such that these coordinate neighborhoods are disjoint
from
S^{\prime}
.
Let
\chi_{i}
be a cutoff function supported in the coordinate neighborhood of

4
Algebraization of

We now prove that the map

constructed in Proposition

sends

biholomorphically onto an affine subvariety of

. The argument consists of two main steps.

1.

After slight modification of

into

, we prove the existence of a meromorphic rational
(1,0)
-form

whose pole divisor coincides with

is an algebraic hypersurface of

.

2.

We show that

is rationally convex, i.e., for any compact set

and the regular function ring

, the rational convex hull

should be a compact subset of

. Together with a theorem of Mok to be recalled later, this will imply that

is affine.

4.1

is quasi-affine

First, we need the following modification of

,

, and

as follows.

Construction 4.1
.

Let

Choose a polynomial

Review on Grauert tube theory

In this section we review the basic concepts of Grauert tube theory required for the proof of Theorem

Definition 6.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a real
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with tangent bundle
TM
. Define

E(v):=\frac{1}{2}\|v\|_{g}^{2},\qquad u:=\sqrt{2E}.

Let
T^{r}M:=\{v\in TM~|~\|v\|_{g}<r\}
. Suppose that
T^{r}M
admits a Stein manifold structure with strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion
u^{2}
such that
M=\{u^{2}=0\}
and that
u
satisfies the Homogeneous Complex Monge–Ampère equation (HCMA)

(i\partial\bar{\partial}u)^{n}=0

on
T^{r}M\setminus M
. Then the triple
(T^{r}M,M,u^{2})
(sometimes
(T^{r}M,M,u)
) is called a
Monge–Ampère model
. If the complex structure
J
on
T^{r}M
is such that the metric associated with the Kähler form
i\partial\bar{\partial}u^{2}
restricts to the original metric
g
on
M
, then we call
(T^{r}M,J,u)
a
Grauert tube
. If the above structure exists for
r=\infty
, i.e. on
T^{\infty}M:=TM
, then we call
(TM,J,u)
the
entire Grauert tube
.

Even though the definition of a Grauert tube appears highly restrictive, it is remarkable that for any real-analytic Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
T^{\varepsilon}M
admits a unique integrable complex structure
J
making it into a Grauert tube.

Since a Grauert tube
T^{r}M
is a subset of the tangent bundle, it carries a natural symplectic structure. Let
\pi:TM\to M
be the natural projection. Using its differential
d\pi:TTM\to TM
, we define the Liouville
1
-form
\theta
and the corresponding Poincaré
2
-form
\Omega
at
(p,v)\in TM
by

\theta_{(p,v)}(-):=g_{p}(d\pi(-),v),\qquad\Omega:=-d\theta.

Thus
TM
always carries a canonical symplectic structure. On the domain where the Grauert tube is defined, the complex structure
J
together with
\Omega
forms a Kähler structure, and the resulting Kähler metric coincides with the original Riemannian metric
g
along
M
.

The Grauert tube
T^{r}M
carries a natural foliation, which can be described in two equivalent ways. First observe that
u^{2}
is strictly plurisubharmonic and

\partial\bar{\partial}u^{2}=2u\,\partial\bar{\partial}u+2\,\partial u\wedge\bar{\partial}u,

while
(i\partial\bar{\partial}u)^{n}=0
. Hence a rank inequality implies
\mathrm{rank}(i\partial\bar{\partial}u)=n-1
. Therefore
T^{r}M\setminus M
admits a foliation whose leaves are complex
1
-dimensional; we call this the
Monge–Ampère foliation
.

This foliation is equivalent to the following structure on the tangent bundle of the Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, called the
Riemann foliation
. Let
N_{\tau}:TM\to TM
be the dilation
N_{\tau}(v)=\tau v
,
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to M
be a unit-speed geodesic, and
S_{r}:=\{\sigma+i\tau\in\mathbb{C}~|~\tau<r\}
. Define

\phi_{\gamma}:S_{r}\to TM,\qquad\phi_{\gamma}(\sigma+i\tau)=N_{\tau}(\gamma^{\prime}(\sigma)).

If
T^{r}M
admits a complex structure for which this map becomes holomorphic, then the complex structure is called the
adapted complex structure
. It is known that on
T^{r}M
the adapted complex structure is equivalent to the Grauert tube structure. We call the map
\phi_{\gamma}
(or its image in
T^{r}M
) the
complexified leaf
(sometimes simply the
leaf
).

As extensions of Jacobi fields along a geodesic, the leaves carry vector fields called
parallel vector fields
. These can be defined in two equivalent ways.

First, we give a formal definition. Viewing
TM\setminus M
as a smooth manifold, recall that the geodesic flow
\Phi_{t}
and the dilation
N_{\tau}
are smooth self-maps of
TM\setminus M
. A vector field
\xi:\phi_{\gamma}\to T\phi_{\gamma}\subset TTM
is called a parallel vector field if it is invariant under
d\Phi_{t}
and
dN_{\tau}
, i.e.

d\Phi_{t}\,\xi(\alpha+i\beta)=\xi(\alpha+t+i\beta),\qquad dN_{\tau}\,\xi(\alpha+i\beta)=\xi(\alpha+i\tau\beta).

Note that any point of
\phi_{\gamma}
away from the real locus can be moved to any other point of
\phi_{\gamma}
by a combination of the geodesic flow and dilation. Hence, the value of a parallel vector field at a single non-real point determines it everywhere.

Parallel vector fields can also be described concretely in analogy with Jacobi fields. Consider a one-parameter family of geodesics
\gamma_{s}
with
\gamma_{0}=\gamma
. This induces a one-parameter family of leaves
\phi_{\gamma_{s}}
. Then a parallel vector field
\xi:\phi_{\gamma}\to T\phi_{\gamma}\subset TTM
can be defined by

\xi(\sigma+i\tau):=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\phi_{\gamma_{s}}(\sigma+i\tau)\right|_{s=0}=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial s}N_{\tau}(\gamma_{s}^{\prime}(\sigma))\right|_{s=0}.

Note that when
\tau=0
this coincides with a Jacobi field. The vector field defined in this way is invariant under geodesic flow and dilation, and conversely every parallel vector field arises in this manner.

The analogy between Jacobi fields on the real part
M
and parallel vector fields on the Grauert tube
T^{r}M
is remarkable. First, the leaves of the Monge–Ampère foliation are totally geodesic and flat in
TM\setminus M
(
[
13
]
, Proposition 3.4). Thus, along any geodesic contained in the leaf, any parallel vector field restricts to a Jacobi field. Combining this with the second definition above, we obtain a one-to-one correspondence between normal Jacobi fields along
\gamma
and parallel vector fields normal to the leaf
\phi_{\gamma}
. Consequently the space of (normal) parallel vector fields is a real
2n
-dimensional (respectively
2n-2
-dimensional) vector space. Since a concrete basis of this space will be recalled frequently, we record the following setting.

\Xi:=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})

be the

.

Using the complex structure

In this setting,

(2)

The results of

7
Construction of complexified metric
g_{\mathbb{C}}

As a key ingredient in constructing the Ricci curvature bound
\psi
, we prove that the metric
g
on the base manifold
(M,g)
admits a meromorphic nondegenerate
(2,0)
-extension to the Grauert tube
T^{r}M
.

Definition and Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold and
T^{r}M:=\{v\in TM~|~\|v\|_{g}<r\}
be a Grauert tube. There exists the nondegenerate meromorphic
(2,0)
-tensor
g_{\mathbb{C}}
on
T^{r}M
obtained as the extension of
g
to the Grauert tube. We call
g_{\mathbb{C}}
the
complexified metric
.

The construction of
g_{\mathbb{C}}
proceeds as follows. First, since any metric admitting an entire Grauert tube is real-analytic, the metric
g
extends at least to a small neighborhood
T^{\varepsilon}M
of
M
(Proposition
7.3
). Second, on each leaf, parallel vector fields form a holomorphic basis, which allows one to extend
g
meromorphically along each leaf (Proposition
7.4
). Third, as the leaves vary, the extension
g_{\mathbb{C}}
depends real-analytically on the leaf parameter, and hence is globally real-analytic away from the pole set; since it is holomorphic on
T^{\varepsilon}M
, it follows that
g_{\mathbb{C}}
is holomorphic (Proposition
7.6
).

Before giving the precise proof, we recall the following key facts.

Remark 7.2
.

Recall the following two basic facts.

(1)

Let
D
be a connected complex domain. If two real-analytic functions on
D
agree on a nonempty open subset
U\subset D
,
then they agree on all of
D
.

(2)

Let
D
be a connected complex domain and let
S\subset D
be a totally real subset of maximal dimension.
If two holomorphic functions on
D
agree on
S
, then they agree on all of
D
.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
g
be the metric tensor on
M
. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that on the Grauert tube
T^{\varepsilon}M
there exists a holomorphic
(2,0)
-tensor
g_{\mathbb{C}}
extending
g
.

Proof.

Using that
M
is a real-analytic totally real submanifold of
TM
, for each point of
M
choose a local holomorphic coordinate system
\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
on
TM
such that
x_{i}:=\Re(z_{i})
restricts to a real-analytic coordinate system on
M
.
In these coordinates we may write

g=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}g_{ij}(x)\,dx_{i}\otimes dx_{j}.

Since
M
is real-analytic, after shrinking the chart if necessary each coefficient
g_{ij}
admits a holomorphic extension to a neighborhood of
M
in
TM
. Denote these extensions by
g_{\mathbb{C}ij}(z)
and define

g_{\mathbb{C}}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}g_{\mathbb{C}ij}(z)\,dz_{i}\otimes dz_{j}.

Then
g_{\mathbb{C}}|_{M}=g
because
dz_{i}|_{M}=dx_{i}
.

Next choose
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small so that
T^{\varepsilon}M
is covered by such neighborhoods. It remains to check that
g_{\mathbb{C}}
is globally well-defined on
T^{\varepsilon}M
. On the overlap of two charts
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, the locally defined tensors restrict to the same
g
on
M
. Hence the difference
g_{\mathbb{C}}^{(1)}-g_{\mathbb{C}}^{(2)}
vanishes on
M\cap(C_{1}\cap C_{2})
. By Remark
7.2
(2) applied componentwise, it vanishes on all of
C_{1}\cap C_{2}
. Therefore the transition is consistent and
g_{\mathbb{C}}
is globally well-defined on
T^{\varepsilon}M
.
∎

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
\gamma
be a geodesic in
M
and let
\phi_{\gamma}
denote its complexified geodesic in
T^{r}M
. Then
g_{\mathbb{C}}
extends to all of
\phi_{\gamma}
as a meromorphic nondegenerate
(2,0)
-tensor.

Proof.

We first consider the geometry on the real locus
(M,g)
. For
n
-tuples of vector fields
V=(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
and
U=(U_{1},\dots,U_{n})
, define the matrix
g(V,U)
as

g(V,U)_{ij}:=g(V_{i},U_{j}).

If
V
and
U
consist of Jacobi fields, then their Wronskian

W(V,U):=g(V^{\prime},U)-g(V,U^{\prime})

is constant. Indeed, writing
R
for the Jacobi operator, we compute

\displaystyle W^{\prime}(V,U)

\displaystyle=\bigl(g(V^{\prime},U)-g(V,U^{\prime})\bigr)^{\prime}=g(V^{\prime\prime},U)+g(V^{\prime},U^{\prime})-g(V^{\prime},U^{\prime})-g(V,U^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=g(RV,U)-g(V,RU)=0.

Now consider Construction
6.1
. On the real locus at
\sigma=0
, we note that
W(\Xi,\Xi)=0
and
W(H,\Xi)=I
. Since
H=\Xi A
, we have

\displaystyle W(H,\Xi)

\displaystyle=g(H^{\prime},\Xi)-g(H,\Xi^{\prime})=g(\Xi^{\prime}A+\Xi A^{\prime},\Xi)-g(\Xi A,\Xi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=g(\Xi,\Xi)A^{\prime}-W(\Xi,\Xi)A=I.

Thus, on the real locus (where defined), we obtain

g(\Xi,\Xi)=(A^{\prime})^{-1}.

Recall that the entries of
\Xi^{1,0}
form a holomorphic frame along each leaf
\phi_{\gamma}
away from the real locus. For
\Phi
, the holomorphic extension of
A
, define

k(\Xi^{1,0},\Xi^{1,0}):=(\Phi^{\prime})^{-1},

on the non-real locus of
\phi_{\gamma}
, and set
k|_{\mathbb{R}}=g
. Since
\Phi^{\prime}
is well-defined on the non-real locus, this defines
g_{\mathbb{C}}
as a meromorphic nondegenerate
(2,0)
-tensor.

Finally, this extension does not depend on the choice of Construction
6.1
. For any such choice, on
T^{\varepsilon}M\setminus M
, the resulting extension of

agrees with the tensor
g_{\mathbb{C}}
already defined on
T^{\varepsilon}M
from Proposition
7.3
.
By Remark
7.2
(1), any two extensions obtained from different choices coincide on the whole leaf.
∎

Remark 7.5
.

When

is expressed as a matrix with respect to the parallel vector fields

, the essential information is contained in the

and

.

Accordingly, when explicitly mentioned, we will also denote this

.

Proposition 7.6
.

The tensor
g
extends to all of
T^{r}M
as a meromorphic nondegenerate
(2,0)
-tensor

.

Proof.

Since
T^{r}M\setminus M
is uniquely covered by the leaf foliation given by complexified geodesics
\phi_{\gamma}
, we define
g_{\mathbb{C}}
on each leaf by the extension constructed in Proposition
7.4
and verify that the resulting tensor is meromorphic on
T^{r}M
.
Fix a unit-speed geodesic
\gamma
with
\gamma(0)=p
,
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=v
, and an orthonormal frame
v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1}
perpendicular to

.
Geodesics near
\gamma
can be described by the following
(2n-2)
-parameter family.
Let
N_{v}M
be the normal space to

in
T_{p}M
and take

.
Let
P_{u}
denotes parallel transport along the curve

from

.
Define

\gamma(u,w)(s):=\exp_{\exp_{p}u}\Bigl(s\,P_{u}\!\left(\frac{v+w}{\sqrt{1+\|w\|^{2}}}\right)\Bigr).

Using this parametrization we can choose the nearby geodesic

and its initial point

Next select an orthonormal frame

\frac{v+w}{\|v+w\|},\;v_{1}^{\prime},\dots,v_{n-1}^{\prime}.

8
Algebraization of entire Grauert tube

We now construct a strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion
\phi
and a Ricci curvature bound
\psi
for Theorem
1.2
.

8.1
Strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion
\phi

Definition 8.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold admitting an entire Grauert tube
TM
.
For
E(v):=\tfrac{1}{2}\|v\|_{g}^{2}
and
u(v):=\sqrt{2E(v)}
on
TM
, define

\phi:=\log\bigl(1+\cosh(u)\bigr)=\log\bigl(1+\cosh(\sqrt{2E})\bigr).

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
\mathrm{Vol}(B_{1}^{n})
denote the Euclidean volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\int_{TM}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}=n!\,\mathrm{Vol}(M,g)\,\mathrm{Vol}(B_{1}^{n}).

Proof.

A direct computation gives

dd^{c}\phi=2i\partial\bar{\partial}\phi=\frac{\sinh u}{u(1+\cosh u)}\,2i\partial\bar{\partial}E+\frac{u-\sinh u}{u^{3}(1+\cosh u)}2i\,\partial E\wedge\bar{\partial}E.

Using the Monge–Ampère equation
(i\partial\bar{\partial}u)^{n}=(i\partial\bar{\partial}\sqrt{2E})^{n}=0
, we have

\displaystyle(i\partial\bar{\partial}u)^{n}

\displaystyle=(i\partial\bar{\partial}\sqrt{2E})^{n}=\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E}}i\partial\bar{\partial}E-\frac{1}{2E\sqrt{2E}}\,i\partial E\wedge\bar{\partial}E\Bigr)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{u^{n}}(i\partial\bar{\partial}E)^{n}-\frac{n}{u^{n+2}}(i\partial\bar{\partial}E)^{n-1}\wedge(i\partial E\wedge\bar{\partial}E)=0.

(3)

In addition,
2i\partial\bar{\partial}E=\Omega
(Theorem
6.2
, (2)), hence we have

(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}=\frac{\sinh^{\,n-1}u}{u^{\,n-1}(1+\cosh u)^{n}}(2i\partial\bar{\partial}E)^{n}=\frac{\sinh^{\,n-1}u}{u^{\,n-1}(1+\cosh u)^{n}}\Omega^{n}.

The symplectic volume form
\frac{\Omega^{n}}{n!}
decomposes at
(x,v)\in TM
as

\frac{\Omega^{n}}{n!}=d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(x)\,dV_{x}(v),

where
dV_{x}
denotes the Euclidean measure on
T_{x}M
. Writing
dV_{x}
in radial form and noting that
u
corresponds to the fiberwise norm, we obtain

dV_{x}=u^{n-1}\,du\,dS_{x},

where
dS_{x}
is the standard area measure on the unit sphere
S_{x}^{n-1}
.

Combining these expressions yields

\displaystyle\int_{TM}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\sinh^{\,n-1}u}{(1+\cosh u)^{n}}\,n!\,du\,dS_{x}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=n!\,\mathrm{Vol}(S^{n-1}_{1})\,\mathrm{Vol}(M,g)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh^{\,n-1}u}{(1+\cosh u)^{n}}du

\displaystyle=n!\,\mathrm{Vol}(S^{n-1}_{1})\,\mathrm{Vol}(M,g)\left[\frac{1}{n}\tanh^{n}\frac{u}{2}\right]_{u=0}^{\infty}=n!\,\mathrm{Vol}(B_{1}^{n})\,\mathrm{Vol}(M,g).

∎

8.2
Ricci curvature bound
\psi

Finally, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
by constructing the following function
\psi
and applying Theorem
1.2
(i).

Definition 8.3
.

Let
\beta:=dd^{c}e^{\phi}=dd^{c}(1+\cosh(u))=dd^{c}(1+\cosh(\sqrt{2E}))
. Let
g_{\mathbb{C}}
be the complexified metric. For the meromorphic nonvanishing section
\det(g_{\mathbb{C}})\in H^{0}(TM,K_{TM}^{2})
, We define the Ricci curvature bound function
\psi
by

\psi:=-\log\|\det(g_{\mathbb{C}})\|_{\beta}.

Locally,
\psi
can be written as
\psi=\log|\det(\beta)|-\log|\det(g_{\mathbb{C}})|
. Then
i\partial\bar{\partial}\log|\det(\beta)|=-\mathrm{Ric}(\beta)
, while the second term is pluriharmonic away from the poles of
\det(g_{\mathbb{C}})
, i.e. the zero locus of
\det(\Phi^{\prime})
, and defines a plurisubharmonic function in the sense of
(1,1)
-currents globally. Therefore,
i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi+\mathrm{Ric}(\beta)\geq 0
. Hence, in order to apply Theorem
1.2
(i), it remains to estimate the growth of
\psi
.

Proposition 8.4
.

There exist constants
A,B>0
such that
\psi\leq A\phi+B
.

Proof.

Assume Construction
6.1
. It suffices to show that there exist constants
A,B>0
independent of the choice of leaf
\phi_{\gamma}
.

Locally,
\psi=\log|\det(\beta)|-\log|\det(g_{\mathbb{C}})|
. On each leaf, away from the real locus (and additionally near
\sigma+i\tau=0
), the vector fields
\xi_{1}^{1,0},\dots,\xi_{n}^{1,0}
form a holomorphic frame. We compute each term with respect to this basis.

(1) The term
-\log|\det(g_{\mathbb{C}})|
.
By Proposition
7.4
, on the basis
\xi_{1}^{1,0},\dots,\xi_{n}^{1,0}
, we have
\det(g_{\mathbb{C}})=\det((\Phi^{\prime})^{-1})=1/\det(\Phi^{\prime})
.

(2) The term
\log|\det(\beta)|
.
Writing
\beta
in terms of
E
and
u=\sqrt{2E}
, we have

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=dd^{c}e^{\phi}=2i\partial\bar{\partial}(1+\cosh\sqrt{2E})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{u}\sinh(u)\,2i\partial\bar{\partial}E+\Bigl(\frac{1}{u^{2}}\cosh(u)-\frac{1}{u^{3}}\sinh(u)\Bigr)\,2i\partial E\wedge\bar{\partial}E.

Combining the Equation
3
with
2i\partial\bar{\partial}E=\Omega
(Theorem
6.2
(2)), a direct computation yields

\beta^{n}=\frac{\cosh u\,\sinh^{n-1}u}{u^{\,n-1}}\,\Omega^{n}.

Next, we compute
\Omega(\xi_{i}^{1,0},\xi_{j}^{0,1})
. Since
i\partial\bar{\partial}E=\tfrac{1}{2}\Omega
, the form
\Omega
has the same symmetry as a Kähler form. Together with
\Omega(\xi_{i},\xi_{j})=0
(Theorem
6.2
(3)), we obtain

\Omega(\xi_{i}^{1,0},\xi_{j}^{0,1})=\frac{i}{2}\Omega(\xi_{i},J\xi_{j}).

Recall that at
\phi_{\gamma}(i)
, one has
\Omega(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})=1
and
\Omega(\xi_{i},\eta_{j})=0
for
i\neq j
(Theorem
6.2
(4)). Since the geodesic flow is Hamiltonian,
\Omega
is invariant under the flow, and by definition,
\Omega
scales by a factor
\tau
under the dilation
N_{\tau}
. Parallel vector fields are also invariant under both operations. Hence, at
\phi_{\gamma}(\sigma+i\tau)
we obtain
\Omega(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})=\tau
and
\Omega(\xi_{i},\eta_{j})=0
for
i\neq j
.

Note that on
\phi_{\gamma}(\sigma+i\tau)
,
u=\tau
. Using
J\Xi=(H-\Xi A)B^{-1}
(Equation
2
) and
\Omega(\xi_{i},\xi_{j})=0
, we conclude that on the frame
\{\xi_{i}^{1,0}\}
,

\det(\Omega)=2^{-n}u^{n}\det(B^{-1}),\qquad\det(\beta)=2^{-n}u\cosh u\,\sinh^{n-1}u\,\det(B^{-1}).

Therefore, on
\phi_{\gamma}(\sigma+i\tau)
, the function
\psi
can be expressed as

\psi=\log\Bigl(2^{-n}u\cosh u\,\sinh^{n-1}u\left|\frac{\det(\Phi^{\prime})}{\det(B)}\right|\Bigr).

(4)

Finally, the upper bound of

\bigl|\det(\Phi^{\prime})/\det(B)\bigr|=\bigl|\det(\Phi^{\prime})/\det(\Im\Phi)\bigr|

is obtained from the following matrix-valued Schwarz–Pick lemma.

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
\mathbb{C}^{+}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\Im z>0\}
, and let

\Phi:\mathbb{C}^{+}\to M_{n}(\mathbb{C})

be a symmetric, matrix-valued Herglotz function, i.e., a matrix-valued holomorphic function such that
B:=(\Phi-\Phi^{*})/(2i)
is positive definite. Then for
z=\sigma+i\tau
,

\left|\frac{\det(\Phi^{\prime}(z))}{\det(B(z))}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\tau^{n}}.

Proof.

By the matrix-valued Herglotz representation
[
10
]
,

\Phi(z)=C+Dz+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(\frac{1}{t-z}-\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\bigr)\,d\mu(t),

where
C,D
are Hermitian matrices,
D\geq 0
, and
\mu
is a positive semidefinite matrix-valued Borel measure. Since
\Phi
is symmetric, they are real symmetric. Hence, we have the following.

\Phi^{\prime}(z)=D+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{(t-z)^{2}}\,d\mu(t)\qquad B(z)=\tau D+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\tau}{|t-z|^{2}}\,d\mu(t).

For any
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

|v^{*}\Phi^{\prime}(z)v|\leq|v^{*}Dv|+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{|t-z|^{2}}\,d(v^{*}\mu(t)v)=\frac{1}{\tau}\,v^{*}B(z)v.

Setting
v=B^{-1/2}w
gives

|w^{*}B^{-1/2}\Phi^{\prime}(z)B^{-1/2}w|\leq\frac{1}{\tau}\|w\|^{2}
,
so the absolute values of all eigenvalues of
B^{-1/2}\Phi^{\prime}(z)B^{-1/2}
are bounded by
1/\tau
.
Therefore,

\left|\det(B^{-1/2}\Phi^{\prime}(z)B^{-1/2})\right|=\left|\frac{\det(\Phi^{\prime}(z))}{\det(B(z))}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\tau^{n}}.

∎

On the real locus of Construction
6.1
, the Jacobi field systems
\Xi
and
H
have vanishing Wronskian. Hence the transition matrix
A
relating them is symmetric. Consequently, its extension
\Phi
is also symmetric, and by
[
13
, Lemma 6.7]
,
B=\Im\Phi
is positive definite. Therefore the above lemma applies.

Since both the bound
1/\tau^{n}=1/u^{n}
and Equation
4
are independent of the choice of leaf
\phi_{\gamma}
, it follows that
\psi
is bounded above linearly in
\phi\sim u
.
∎

9
Tube singularity
S
and Theorem
1.3

We conclude this paper with a discussion of Theorem
1.3
and possible directions for future improvement. We begin by introducing the following definition.

Definition 9.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold, and let
T^{r}M
be its Grauert tube equipped with the complexified metric
g_{\mathbb{C}}
. The pole set

S\subset T^{r}M\setminus M

of
\det(g_{\mathbb{C}})
is called the
tube singularity
.

The tube singularity
S
is a natural analytic subset of a Grauert tube: it depends only on the underlying metric
g
. In addition, for
\psi:=-\log\|\det(g_{\mathbb{C}})\|_{\beta}
, note that the tube singularity
S
coincides with the set

S:=\{x\mid e^{-2\psi}\notin L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(x)\}

appearing in Theorem
1.2
. Indeed, since
e^{-2\psi}=\|\det(g_{\mathbb{C}})\|_{\beta}^{2}
and
\det(g_{\mathbb{C}})
is locally represented by a meromorphic function, its pole set is precisely the set where
\|\det(g_{\mathbb{C}})\|_{\beta}^{2}
fails to be locally integrable.

Theorem
1.3
shows that an entire Grauert tube
TM
is affine outside a codimension-one subset of the form
b_{p}^{-1}(0)
. Moreover,
TM\setminus S
is covered by the affine open sets
TM\setminus b_{p}^{-1}(0)
, which are all birational to one another. Since the tube singularity
S
is locally given as the zero locus of a holomorphic function at each point, namely by
(\det g_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}=0
, the complement
TM\setminus S
is Stein
[
22
, Corollary 4]
. Therefore, Theorem
1.3
can be roughly rephrased as follows.

Theorem 1.3. (rephrased)

For an entire Grauert tube
TM
with tube singularity
S
, the complement
TM\setminus S
is a Stein manifold covered by birationally equivalent affine Zariski open subsets.

However, each hypersurface
b_{p}^{-1}(0)
necessarily contains the entire tube singularity
S
. In addition, Theorem
1.3
is obtained from the first step (i) of Theorem
1.2
; hence, to apply the additional statements of that theorem, one needs sufficient control over
S
. Therefore, a complete resolution of the Burns conjecture depends on understanding the structure of the tube singularity
S
.

Indeed, if
S
vanishes, then by Theorem
1.2
(iv), the entire Grauert tube is affine. However, there exist explicit examples where
S
is nonempty, which constitutes the main obstruction to the Burns conjecture. Moreover, if the
(n,0)
-part
V^{n,0}
of the analytic continuation of the volume form
V
of the real locus
(M,g)
exists, then one must have

\|V^{n,0}\wedge\overline{V^{n,0}}\|_{\beta}=C_{n}\|\det(g_{\mathbb{C}})\|_{\beta},

for a positive dimensional constant
C_{n}
. Therefore, the presence of
S
also reflects the difficulty encountered in the approach of Aguilar and Burns
[
1
]
. We first describe such an example and then suggest a known control on
S
.

Szőke
[
18
, Section 4]
constructed a family of surfaces of revolution
M_{d}
that all admit entire Grauert tubes, and proved that, up to rescaling, these exhaust all such surfaces of revolution.

Example 9.2
.

Let
C_{d}=(\chi_{1},0,\chi_{2})\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a curve defined by

\chi_{1}(s):=\frac{\sin s}{\sqrt{d\sin^{2}s+1}},\qquad(\chi_{1}^{\prime})^{2}+(\chi_{2}^{\prime})^{2}=1,\qquad-\chi_{2}(0)=\chi_{2}(\pi).

Let
M_{d}
be the surface of revolution obtained by rotating
C_{d}
around the third coordinate axis.

As observed in Proposition
7.4
, on each complexified leaf
\phi_{\gamma}
, the tube singularity
S
corresponds to the zero set of
\det(\Phi^{\prime})
, where
\Phi
is the transition matrix between parallel vector fields. The above example provides an explicit case where
\Phi^{\prime}
degenerates outside the real locus when
d\neq 0
.

Recall that on a surface of revolution, meridian curves are geodesics. Szőke showed the following fact
[
18
, Proposition 4.2]
.

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
\xi
be the unit normal vector field along the meridian curve
C_{d}
in
M_{d}
. Then

X_{1}(s):=\frac{\sin s}{\sqrt{d\sin^{2}s+1}}\,\xi(s),\qquad X_{2}(s):=\frac{ds\sin s-\cos s}{\sqrt{d\sin^{2}s+1}}\,\xi(s)

are Jacobi fields.

Interpreting this in the framework of Construction
6.1
and Remark
7.5
, note that since the base manifold is two-dimensional, the transition matrix
\Phi
between two normal Jacobi field systems reduces to the ratio of two Jacobi fields. In this case,
X_{1}
corresponds to
\eta_{1}
and
X_{2}
to
\xi_{1}
, so that the
1\times 1
matrix
\Phi^{\prime}
is given by

\Phi^{\prime}(z)=\left(\frac{\sin z}{dz\sin z-\cos z}\right)^{\prime}.

A direct computation shows that
\Phi^{\prime}
vanishes at

z=n\pi\pm i\,\mathrm{arcsinh}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\right),\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Thus, except for the round sphere
M_{0}
, every surface in the family
M_{d}
has a nonempty tube singularity
S
.

Lempert
[
14
, Proposition 3.1, Lemma 3.1, Lemma 4.1]
showed that the absence of zeros of
\Phi^{\prime}
near
M
can be partially controlled by the geometry of
(M,g)
, providing an estimate on the region where
S
does not appear.

Proposition 9.4
.

Let
\rho
be the conjugate radius of
(M,g)
, i.e., the minimal distance between distinct conjugate points along any geodesic. Then

T^{\rho/2}M\cap S=\emptyset.

However, to the best of the author’s knowledge, there is currently no known method to control the topology of
S
sufficiently to apply the global argument in Theorem
1.2
. Therefore, in view of the Burns conjecture and its applications to the good complexification conjecture, we propose the following questions.

Question 9.5
.

Let
TM
be an entire Grauert tube with tube singularity
S
. Can one guarantee that
\dim H^{2k}(TM\setminus S;\mathbb{R})<\infty
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
?

As mentioned above,
TM\setminus S
is a Stein manifold. Therefore, if the above question admits an affirmative answer, then Theorem
1.2
(iii) would imply the following global affineness statement for entire Grauert tubes: there exists an irreducible normal affine variety
X
and an algebraic hypersurface
H\subset X
such that
TM\setminus S
is biholomorphic to
X\setminus H
.

Question 9.6
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold admitting an entire Grauert tube. Does there exist a deformation of the metric, preserving the existence of the entire Grauert tube, such that the resulting tube singularity
S
vanishes?

Note that the entire Grauert tube
TM
has the same cohomology as the compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
. Therefore, if the tube singularity set
S
vanishes, then
TM
is affine by Theorem
1.2
(iv). Consequently, if the above question admits an affirmative answer, we obtain that any manifold
(M,g)
admitting an entire Grauert tube has a good complexification.
