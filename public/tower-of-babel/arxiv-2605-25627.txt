Functoriality and Weyl Groupoids of Ample C*-Diagonal Pairs
Ali Jabbari

Abstract
. We initiate a functorial study of ample C ∗ -diagonal pairs and their Weyl groupoids, focusing on how certain well-behaved ∗ * -homomorphisms induce geometric maps between the associated groupoids. Given a morphism between diagonal pairs satisfying compatibility conditions with the diagonal and the canonical conditional expectations, we construct an induced partial morphism between the associated Weyl groupoids and analyze its properties. This provides a way to transfer certain structural information between Cartan-type inclusions. As applications, we study the behaviour of expectation-compatible ideals, faithful conditional expectations, and dynamical comparison under diagonal-preserving morphisms. We further investigate tensor products of ample C ∗ -diagonal pairs and prove that the Weyl groupoid of a tensor product is naturally identified with the product of the corresponding Weyl groupoids. Under suitable hypotheses, we obtain a subadditivity result for diagonal dimension via dynamic asymptotic dimension. We also prove that the Weyl functor is faithful on a natural subcategory of untwisted pairs, providing a concrete invariant that distinguishes non-isomorphic diagonal pairs. The theory is illustrated through examples arising from AF algebras, graph C ∗ -algebras, crossed products, and recent constructions of exotic diagonals in UHF and Cuntz algebras.

1.
Introduction

Cartan subalgebras of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, introduced by Kumjian and refined by Renault, play a role in the structure and classification of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. A unital inclusion
(D\subset A)
is called a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pair
if
D
is a maximal abelian, regular subalgebra of
A
possessing the unique extension property (UEP). Renault’s celebrated reconstruction theorem shows that every such pair arises from a twisted étale groupoid
(\mathcal{G},\Sigma)
with topologically principal
\mathcal{G}
, and that the pair is
ample
precisely when the unit space is totally disconnected.

In recent years, Kopsacheilis and Winter developed a systematic theory of ample
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pairs, establishing deep results on dynamical comparison, tracial almost divisibility, and the equivalence between diagonal comparison and the conjunction of dynamical comparison and strict comparison
[
10
]
. However, the functorial behavior of such pairs under
*
-homomorphisms — particularly those that respect the Cartan structure has remained largely unexplored. Understanding this behavior is essential for applications to classification, rigidity, and the construction of exotic diagonals in tensor products and inductive limits.

Very recently, Sibbel and Winter
[
17
]
constructed an explicit C*-diagonal with Cantor spectrum in the Cuntz algebra
\mathcal{O}_{2}
, answering a long-standing question. Their construction proceeds via crossed products by minimal homeomorphisms on Cantor spaces and infinite tensor products, ultimately relying on Kirchberg’s
\mathcal{O}_{2}
-absorption theorem. The present work provides a complementary perspective: the functorial framework developed here (specifically, Theorem
5.8
and Theorem
4.6
) offers a systematic language for understanding how such diagonals behave under homomorphisms and quotients.

Additionally, Kopsacheilis and Winter
[
11
]
have constructed, for each
n\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,\infty\}
, a C*-diagonal in the CAR algebra
M_{2^{\infty}}
with Cantor spectrum and diagonal dimension exactly
n
, showing in particular that non-AF diagonals with amenable Weyl groupoids exist. These examples provide natural test cases for the functorial techniques developed in Section
5
.

This paper addresses three interrelated themes:

(1)

Quotient permanence.
We characterize when a surjective
*
-homomorphism
\Phi:A\to B
between ample
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pairs yields a new such pair
(\Phi(D)\subset B)
. In particular, we show that if
\Phi
is a morphism in the sense of Definition
2.4
(i.e., respects the diagonal, the conditional expectation, and the normalizer semigroup), then the quotient is again an ample
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pair, and the associated Weyl groupoid is a reduction of the original one (in the untwisted case; see Remark
3.10
for the twisted situation).

(2)

Geometric realization of quotient diagonals.
Using the Exel–Pitts ideal structure theorem, we prove that for an
untwisted
ample
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pair
A\cong C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})
, there is a bijection between closed ideals generated by their intersection with the diagonal and open invariant subsets of
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
. The quotient pair corresponds to the restriction of
\mathcal{G}
to the complement, yielding an untwisted diagonal pair.

(3)

Partial functoriality of Weyl groupoids.
Let
\Phi:(A,D)\to(B,C)
be a unital
*
-homomorphism between ample
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pairs such that
\Phi|_{D}:D\to C
is an isomorphism. We construct an open subgroupoid
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{B}
and a continuous, open, injective groupoid homomorphism
\rho_{\Phi}:\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\longrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{A}
that extends the induced homeomorphism on unit spaces. This result provides a concrete functorial link between the groupoid models, which is not automatic from Renault’s theory.

The central theme of the paper is that ample
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pairs exhibit structured behavior under quotient constructions and diagonal-preserving morphisms, and that this behavior admits a natural description in terms of partial groupoid morphisms. The associated Weyl groupoids interact geometrically through reductions and induced subgroupoid morphisms, unifying the three themes above and highlighting the interplay between algebraic and groupoid dynamics. We develop the categorical framework for such pairs in Proposition
2.5
and further establish functorial properties in Theorem
5.12
, Proposition
6.5
, and Corollary
4.7
.

Remark 1.1
.

The present paper is foundational in nature. While we establish a functor
\mathcal{W}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}\to\mathsf{Grpd}_{\mathrm{part}}
and prove several permanence properties, we do not claim that this functor is an equivalence or that it yields a complete classification. Rather, we develop the necessary categorical and geometric language to study diagonal-preserving morphisms systematically. A concrete application demonstrating the faithfulness of this invariant for
untwisted
pairs is given in Section
7
, where we show that the Weyl functor distinguishes the exotic diagonals of Kopsacheilis and Winter. Further applications remain a direction for future work.

After recalling necessary preliminaries (Section
2
), we define morphisms of ample
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pairs and establish basic transfer properties (Section
3
) and study quotients that are again groupoid
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. Section
4
gives a geometric characterization of quotient diagonals via invariant open sets, under the assumption of untwistedness. Section
5
contains the main functoriality result: the construction of
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
and
\rho_{\Phi}
, valid in the twisted or untwisted setting. Section
6
provides examples and applications, Section
7
proves faithfulness of the Weyl functor for untwisted principal pairs, and Section
9
lists open problems.

Throughout, all
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras are separable and unital. Groupoids are second-countable, locally compact, Hausdorff, étale, with compact unit space. A
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pair
(D\subset A)
is called
ample
if
\operatorname{Spec}(D)
is totally disconnected.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Groupoid conventions

For a second
-
countable locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}
:

(i)

topologically principal
(also called
essentially principal
) means that the set of units with trivial isotropy is dense in
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.

(ii)

effective
means that the interior of the isotropy bundle
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\{g\in\mathcal{G}:s(g)=r(g)\}
equals
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.

It is well known that topological principality implies effectiveness

[
16
, Proposition 3.6]
. The converse may fail for general
étale groupoids, but for second countable Hausdorff étale groupoids the
two notions coincide for those arising from C*-diagonal pairs (which satisfy the UEP). In particular, the Weyl groupoids arising from
the ample C
∗
-diagonal pairs considered in this paper are
topologically principal, and hence effective.

Remark 2.1
.

For a Weyl groupoid
\mathcal{G}
arising from an ample C*-diagonal pair, the UEP forces topological principality (see
[
16
]
). Consequently,
\mathcal{G}
is also effective by
[
16
, Proposition 3.6]
. This equivalence justifies our occasional interchangeable use of the two terms in the sequel. In particular, all results from
[
6
]
that require effectiveness apply directly.

Remark 2.2
.

A crucial subtlety:
topological principality
of
\mathcal{G}
does
not
imply that the Kumjian–Renault twist is trivial. A principal groupoid (all isotropy trivial) may still carry a nontrivial twist — a 2-cocycle with values in
\mathbb{T}
representing a nontrivial class in
H^{2}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{T})
. The resulting twisted groupoid C*-algebra
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G},\Sigma)
is then a Cartan pair that is
not
isomorphic to
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
.

In this paper, we say a C*-diagonal pair is
untwisted
if its associated twist is trivial (cohomologous to 1), i.e.,
A\cong C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
with
\mathcal{G}
ample and topologically principal.
Principality alone does not imply untwistedness
. Our results in Sections
4
and
7
assume untwistedness explicitly; the partial groupoid morphism construction in Section
5
and the Weyl functor in Section
7
are valid in the twisted setting as well, but faithfulness of the functor holds only for untwisted principal pairs (see Remark
5.13
).

2.2.
Reconstruction tools and their logical dependencies

•

Kumjian
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
: Every
C^{*}
-diagonal pair
(D\subset A)
gives rise to a twist
\Sigma
over a principal groupoid
R
such that
A\cong C_{r}^{*}(R,\Sigma)
and
D\cong C(R^{(0)})
. Conversely, every such twist yields a
C^{*}
-diagonal pair.

•

Renault
[
16
, Theorem 4.1]
: If
\mathcal{G}
is second
-
countable, locally compact Hausdorff étale and topologically principal, then
(C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})\subseteq C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}))
is a Cartan pair. Every Cartan pair arises from a twisted groupoid in this way. When the Cartan pair additionally has the UEP, it is a
C^{*}
-diagonal pair.

•

Bönicke–Li
[
6
, Theorem 3.10 and Corollary 3.12]
: For a Hausdorff étale effective groupoid, every closed ideal of
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
is generated by its intersection with
C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
. This holds for the reduced C*-algebra of a
twisted
groupoid as well, because effectiveness is preserved under twisting.

•

A regular abelian subalgebra with UEP is automatically maximal abelian (see
[
12
, Proposition 4]
); this follows from the definition of a diagonal and the fact that a faithful conditional expectation with UEP forces the relative commutant to be contained in the subalgebra.

All groupoids we encounter from unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pairs have compact unit space, so
C_{0}
becomes
C
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
C^{*}
-diagonal pair
is a unital inclusion
(D\subseteq A)
together with a faithful conditional expectation
E_{A}:A\to D
such that

(i)

D
is a maximal abelian subalgebra of
A
,

(ii)

D
is regular, i.e.
C^{*}(\mathcal{N}_{A}(D))=A
, where

\mathcal{N}_{A}(D):=\{n\in A:nDn^{*}\cup n^{*}Dn\subseteq D\},

(iii)

D
has the unique extension property (UEP): every pure state on
D
extends uniquely to a state on
A
.

The pair is
ample
if
\operatorname{Spec}(D)
is totally disconnected.
If
A
is separable, Kumjian’s reconstruction
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
(or Renault’s reconstruction
[
15
]
together with the UEP) yields a twist over an ample principal groupoid. The Weyl groupoid is topologically principal, hence effective.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(A,D,E_{A})
and
(B,C,E_{B})
be ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pairs.
A
morphism

\Phi:(A,D,E_{A})\to(B,C,E_{B})

is a unital
*
-homomorphism
\Phi:A\to B
satisfying:

(D)

\Phi(D)\subseteq C
;

(E)

\Phi\circ E_{A}=E_{B}\circ\Phi
;

(N)

\Phi(\mathcal{N}_{A}(D))\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{B}(C)
, and the restriction
\Phi|_{\mathcal{N}_{A}(D)}
is injective (equivalently, if
\Phi(n)=0
then
n=0
for
n\in\mathcal{N}_{A}(D)
).

When
\Phi
is surjective and
\Phi|_{D}
is an isomorphism, condition (N) simplifies to
\Phi(\mathcal{N}_{A}(D))=\mathcal{N}_{B}(C)
because injectivity on normalizers follows from
\ker\Phi\cap D=\{0\}
and Lemma
3.4
.

Proposition 2.5

(Category of ample diagonal pairs)
.

Ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pairs together with the morphisms defined in Definition
2.4
form a category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}
.

Proof.

Let

\Phi:(A,D_{A})\to(B,D_{B})\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi:(B,D_{B})\to(C,D_{C})

be morphisms. Since both

\Phi
and
\Psi
are unital
*
-homomorphisms, so is the composition

\Psi\circ\Phi
.

Moreover, conditions (D), (E), and (N) are preserved
under composition. Indeed,

(\Psi\circ\Phi)(D_{A})\subseteq\Psi(D_{B})\subseteq D_{C},

so (D) holds. Compatibility with the conditional expectations
follows from

E_{C}\circ(\Psi\circ\Phi)=(E_{C}\circ\Psi)\circ\Phi=(\Psi\circ E_{B})\circ\Phi=\Psi\circ(E_{B}\circ\Phi)=\Psi\circ(\Phi\circ E_{A})=(\Psi\circ\Phi)\circ E_{A},

establishing (E). Finally, if
n\in N_{A}(D_{A})
, then

\Phi(n)\in N_{B}(D_{B})\quad\text{and hence}\quad\Psi(\Phi(n))\in N_{C}(D_{C}),

so (N) also holds.

The identity map on any ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pair trivially satisfies
(D), (E), and (N). Associativity of composition follows
from associativity of composition of
*
-homomorphisms. Therefore the
collection of ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pairs together with these morphisms
forms a category.
∎

2.3.
Naturality of the morphism definition

The definition of a morphism arises naturally from the structure of a C*-diagonal pair. Condition (D) is the minimal requirement that the diagonal be preserved. Condition (E) ensures that the conditional expectation, which encodes the Cartan structure, is compatible with the map; without it, the induced map on Gelfand spectra would not be well-defined. Condition (N) is necessary for the Weyl groupoid construction to be functorial, as normalizers generate the algebra and their images under
\Phi
must remain normalizers to define a groupoid morphism.

To see that condition (N) is not automatic from (D) and (E), consider the following example. Let
A=M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
with diagonal
D=\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}
and the usual conditional expectation. Let
B=\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}
with diagonal
C=B
and identity expectation. Define
\Phi:A\to B
by
\Phi\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)=(a,d)
. Then (D) and (E) hold, but the normalizer
\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}
maps to
(0,0)
, which violates injectivity on normalizers. Thus (N) fails, and indeed there is no induced map on Weyl groupoids because the germ of this normalizer would be lost.

A nontrivial example where (N) is satisfied occurs for maps between graph algebras that send vertex projections to sums of vertex projections; such maps preserve the normalizer semigroup of partial isometries.

3.
Transfer of the faithful conditional expectation and dynamical comparison

The purpose of this section is to connect the functorial behaviour of C*-diagonal pairs with dynamical properties of their Weyl groupoids. We show that, under suitable hypotheses, faithful conditional expectations and dynamical comparison can be transferred along diagonal-preserving morphisms, thereby linking operator-algebraic structures with the underlying groupoid geometry.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Phi:(A,D,E_{A})\to(B,C,E_{B})
be a morphism of
C^{*}
-diagonal pairs. Assume that:

(i)

\Phi
is surjective,

(ii)

there exists a
*
-homomorphism
\Psi:B\to A
such that
\Phi\circ\Psi=\operatorname{id}_{B}
,

(iii)

E_{A}\circ\Psi=\Psi\circ E_{B}
.

If
E_{A}
is faithful, then
E_{B}
is faithful.

Proof.

If
E_{B}(b)=0
for a positive
b\in B
, then
\Psi(b)\geq 0
and
E_{A}(\Psi(b))=\Psi(E_{B}(b))=0
. Since
E_{A}
is faithful,
\Psi(b)=0
, and applying
\Phi
yields
b=0
.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

[
10
, Definition 1.5]

For a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pair
(D\subset A)
with
\mathrm{T}(A)\neq\emptyset
, for projections
p,q\in D
write
p\preceq_{N}q
if there exists
n\in\mathcal{N}_{A}(D)
with
n^{*}n=p,\;nn^{*}\leq q
. The pair has
dynamical comparison
if
\tau(p)<\tau(q)
for all
\tau\in\mathrm{T}(A)
implies
p\preceq_{N}q
.

Remark 3.3
.

A natural setting for transferring dynamical comparison is via the geometric correspondence in Theorem
4.6
: if
(D\subset A)
has dynamical comparison and
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is an open invariant subset, then the quotient pair
(D/(I\cap D)\subseteq A/I)
with
I=C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{U})
inherits dynamical comparison because its Weyl groupoid is a reduction
\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U}
, and dynamical comparison is preserved under reductions (see
[
10
, Proposition 4.3]
). This argument assumes the quotient pair is untwisted; for twisted reductions, dynamical comparison must be re-examined (see Remark
3.10
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(A,D)
be an untwisted ample
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pair with Weyl groupoid
\mathcal{G}
. Then every closed two-sided ideal
I\triangleleft A
is generated by
I\cap D
.

Proof.

Renault’s reconstruction gives
A\cong C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
and
D\cong C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
.
Since
\mathcal{G}
is topologically principal, it is effective by
[
16
, Proposition 3.6]
and Remark
2.1
.

Therefore, by
[
6
, Theorem 3.10 and Corollary 3.12]
, every closed two-sided ideal of
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
is generated by its intersection with

C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
. Pulling this characterization back to the pair
(A,D)
, we conclude that every ideal
I\triangleleft A
is generated by
I\cap D
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Phi:A\to B
be a surjective unital
*
-homomorphism between unital
C^{*}
-algebras.
Let
D\subset A
be an abelian
C^{*}
-subalgebra with the UEP (pure state version), and set
C:=\Phi(D)
. Assume that
\Phi|_{D}:D\to C
is an isometric
*
-isomorphism. Then
C\subset B
also has the UEP.

Proof.

A pure state on the abelian algebra
C
is a character
\chi
. Because
\Phi|_{D}
is an isometric isomorphism,
\tilde{\chi}:=\chi\circ(\Phi|_{D})
is a character on
D
, hence a pure state. If
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
are state extensions of
\chi
to
B
, then
\rho_{1}\circ\Phi
and
\rho_{2}\circ\Phi
are state extensions of
\tilde{\chi}
to
A
. By the UEP of
D\subset A
, they coincide. Surjectivity of
\Phi
forces
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}
. Thus every pure state on
C
extends uniquely to a state on
B
, so
C\subseteq B
has the UEP.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(A,D)
be a
C^{*}
-diagonal pair,

\Phi:A\to B
a surjective unital *-homomorphism,
and suppose there exists a conditional expectation

E_{B}:B\to C
with
C=\Phi(D)
satisfying

\Phi\circ E_{A}=E_{B}\circ\Phi.

Assume further that
\ker\Phi\subseteq\ker E_{A}
. Then
E_{B}
is uniquely determined by

E_{B}(\Phi(a))=\Phi(E_{A}(a)).

Moreover, if
\ker\Phi\cap D=\{0\}
and
E_{A}
is faithful,
then
E_{B}
is faithful.

Proof.

We first verify well-definedness. Suppose
\Phi(a_{1})=\Phi(a_{2})
. Then
a_{1}-a_{2}\in\ker\Phi\subseteq\ker E_{A}
, so
E_{A}(a_{1})=E_{A}(a_{2})
. Hence
\Phi(E_{A}(a_{1}))=\Phi(E_{A}(a_{2}))
, so the definition
E_{B}(\Phi(a)):=\Phi(E_{A}(a))
is independent of the lift
a
. For any
b\in B=\Phi(A)
, choose
a
with
\Phi(a)=b
. Then the commuting relation implies that
E_{B}(b)=E_{B}(\Phi(a))=\Phi(E_{A}(a))\in C
. Hence the expectation is uniquely determined.

We now investigate the faithfulness of
E_{B}
. Suppose that
E_{B}(bb^{*})=0
for some
b\in B
. Since
\Phi
is onto, there exists
a\in A
such that
\Phi(a)=b
. Then

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle E_{B}(bb^{*})=E_{B}\left(\Phi(a)\Phi(a^{*})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{B}\left(\Phi(aa^{*})\right)=E_{B}\circ\Phi\left(aa^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi\circ E_{A}\left(aa^{*}\right).

This means that
E_{A}\left(aa^{*}\right)\in\ker\Phi
. However, we have
\ker\Phi\cap D=\{0\}
, and
E_{A}(aa^{*})\in D
, so this demonstrates that
E_{A}\left(aa^{*}\right)=0
. Faithfulness of
E_{A}
implies
a=0
and hence
b=0
. Thus,
E_{B}
maps onto
C
and is faithful.
∎

Lemma 3.7

(Characterization of
C^{*}
-diagonals)
.

Let
C\subseteq B
be a unital inclusion of
C^{*}
-algebras with a faithful conditional expectation

. Suppose

, and has the UEP. Then
(C,B,E)

By

, a diagonal subalgebra (Definition 3,

) automatically has the UEP and is maximal abelian. Conversely, a regular abelian subalgebra with a faithful conditional expectation and the UEP satisfies the conditions of

Lemma 3.8

(Ideal structure for untwisted groupoids)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an ample Hausdorff étale groupoid that is topologically principal. Then there is a bijection between open invariant subsets
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
and closed ideals
I\triangleleft C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
given by
U\mapsto C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{U})
. Moreover,
I\cap C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})=C_{0}(U)
.

Proof.

By
[
6
, Theorem 3.10 and Corollary 3.12]
, since
\mathcal{G}
is ample, Hausdorff, étale, and topologically principal (hence effective by Remark
2.1
), every closed ideal

I\triangleleft C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
is generated by its intersection with the diagonal algebra
C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
.
Now ideals of the commutative algebra
C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
are precisely of the form
C_{0}(U)
, where
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is open. Moreover, the ideal generated by
C_{0}(U)
coincides with
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{U})
, and
U
must be invariant. Thus the assignment
U\longmapsto C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{U})
defines a bijection between open invariant subsets of
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
and closed ideals of
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
. Finally, for the ideal corresponding to
U
, we have
I\cap C_{0}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})=C_{0}(U)
.
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a second-countable, ample, Hausdorff, étale, topologically principal groupoid, and set

A=C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}),\qquad D=C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}),

so that
(D\subset A)
is an
untwisted

\Phi:A\to B
be a unital surjective
*
-homomorphism and set

. Assume:

(i)

there exists a faithful conditional expectation
E_{B}:B\to C
such that
\Phi\circ E_{A}=E_{B}\circ\Phi
, where
E_{A}:A\to D
is the canonical expectation;

(ii)

\ker\Phi\cap D=\{0\}
;

(iii)

\Phi(\mathcal{N}_{A}(D))\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{B}(C)
(condition (N) from Definition

Then

(a)

(C\subseteq B)
is an ample
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pair;

(b)

\mathcal{H}
and a twist

such that

is induced by a continuous homomorphism of groupoids), then

is trivial;

(c)

if additionally
\mathcal{G}

Proof.

We first note that
A
is separable because
\mathcal{G}
is second-countable (see
[
15
, Remark (iii), p. 59]
). Hence the quotient
B
is also separable. The pair
(D\subset A)
is a Cartan pair by
[
16
, Theorem 5.9]
because
\mathcal{G}
is topologically principal.

Step 1: Regularity of
C
in
B
.

By hypothesis (iii),
\Phi(\mathcal{N}_{A}(D))\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{B}(C)
. Since
\mathcal{N}_{A}(D)
generates
A
as a
C^{*}
-algebra and
\Phi
is surjective,
\Phi(\mathcal{N}_{A}(D))
generates
B
. Hence
B=C^{*}(\mathcal{N}_{B}(C))
, so
C
is regular in
B
.

Step 2: UEP for
C\subset B
.

Recall that the
unique extension property (UEP)
for an abelian subalgebra
C\subset B
means: every pure state on
C
extends uniquely to a state on
B
(see
[
12
]
).
Since
\Phi|_{D}:D\to C
is injective (by
\ker\Phi\cap D=\{0\}
) and surjective onto
C
, it is an isometric
*
-isomorphism. Lemma
3.5
(which preserves the pure-state UEP under isomorphisms) then gives the UEP for
C\subset B
.

Step 3: Faithful conditional expectation.

We first verify
\ker\Phi\subseteq\ker E_{A}
. For any
a\in\ker\Phi
, we have
\Phi(E_{A}(a))=E_{B}(\Phi(a))=E_{B}(0)=0
, so
E_{A}(a)\in\ker\Phi\cap D=\{0\}
. Thus
E_{A}(a)=0
, i.e.,
a\in\ker E_{A}
. Hence
\ker\Phi\subseteq\ker E_{A}
, satisfying the hypothesis of Lemma
3.6
. That lemma then guarantees that
E_{B}
is faithful.

Step 4: Maximal abelianness of
C
in
B
.

By
[
16
, Proposition 4.8]
, an abelian subalgebra with the unique extension property is maximal abelian. Since we have established the UEP for
C\subset B
in Step 2, it follows that
C
is a masa in
B
.

Step 5: Cartan pair.

We have shown:
C
is abelian (as an image of an abelian algebra), regular in
B
(Step 1), maximal abelian in
B
(Step 4), and admits a faithful conditional expectation
E_{B}:B\to C
(Step 3). By definition
[
16
, Definition 5.1]
,
(C,B,E_{B})
is a
Cartan pair
.

Step 6: From Cartan pair to
C^{*}
-diagonal.

A Cartan pair
(C,B,E_{B})
is a
C^{*}
-diagonal precisely when it has the UEP. Indeed, Kumjian
[
12
, Definition 1.3]
defines a diagonal by conditions (i) faithful conditional expectation, (ii)
\ker P=\overline{\operatorname{span}}N_{f}(B)
, where
N_{f}(B)
denotes the set of free normalizers of
B
, which is equivalent to regularity together with the UEP (see
[
12
, Proposition 1.4]
). Since we have established regularity (Step 1), the UEP (Step 2), and faithfulness of
E_{B}
(Step 3), it follows that
(C,B,E_{B})
is a
C^{*}
-diagonal pair. Moreover, because
\mathcal{G}
is ample,
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is totally disconnected. Since
\Phi|_{D}:D\to C
is an isomorphism,
\operatorname{Spec}(C)\cong\operatorname{Spec}(D)\cong\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is totally disconnected. Hence
(C\subset B)
is ample. This proves (a).

Step 7: Reconstruction of the quotient groupoid (possibly twisted).

Since
(C,B)
is a unital ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pair and
B
is separable, Kumjian’s reconstruction theorem
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
yields a second-countable, ample, Hausdorff, étale, topologically principal groupoid
\mathcal{H}
and a twist
\Sigma
over
\mathcal{H}
such that
B\cong C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{H},\Sigma)
and
C\cong C(\mathcal{H}^{(0)})
. This proves (b). If the original pair is untwisted and
\Phi
is induced by a groupoid homomorphism (i.e.,
\Phi=C_{r}^{*}(\phi)
for some
\phi:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
), then the twist
\Sigma
is the pushforward of the trivial twist and hence trivial.

Step 8: Principality with untwistedness forces isomorphism.

Assume additionally that
\mathcal{G}
is principal and that
A
is untwisted. By Lemma
3.8
, the ideal
I=\ker\Phi
corresponds to an open invariant subset
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
such that
I=C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{U})
and
I\cap D=C_{0}(U)
. The hypothesis
\ker\Phi\cap D=\{0\}
gives
C_{0}(U)=\{0\}
, which forces
U=\emptyset
because
C_{0}(U)
is nonzero for any nonempty open set. Hence
I=\{0\}
. Therefore
\Phi
is injective, and since it is already surjective, it is an isomorphism. This proves (c).
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Theorem
3.9
(c) shows that for
untwisted
principal groupoids, a surjective morphism with trivial kernel on the diagonal must be an isomorphism. However, if the original pair is
twisted
(i.e.,
A\cong C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G},\Sigma)
with
\Sigma
nontrivial), then principality of
\mathcal{G}
does
not
force
\Phi
to be an isomorphism. For example, a quotient by an ideal corresponding to an invariant open set may collapse the twist in a nontrivial way, yielding a quotient pair with a different twist. The Weyl groupoid of the quotient may be a reduction of
\mathcal{G}
, but the twist may change. The untwisted case is thus special.

4.
Quotient diagonals via invariant open sets

Convention for this section:
All C*-diagonal pairs in this section are assumed to be
untwisted
. Consequently, they are of the form
(C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})\subset C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}))
for an ample, Hausdorff, étale, topologically principal groupoid
\mathcal{G}
.

The following result is fundamental because it converts geometric equality of germs into algebraic equality in
A
. This is essential in Renault reconstruction, Weyl groupoid construction, proving Hausdorffness, identifying quotient groupoids, constructing cocycles and twists. It is one of the key algebra-to-geometry bridges in diagonal theory.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(A,D)
be a
C^{*}
-diagonal pair with Weyl groupoid
\mathcal{G}
. Let

n,m\in\mathcal{N}_{A}(D)
and
x\in\operatorname{dom}(n)\cap\operatorname{dom}(m)
.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

[n,x]=[m,x]
in
\mathcal{G}
;

(ii)

there exists
d\in D
such that
d(x)\neq 0
and

nd=md
.

Proof.

Recall that the germ relation defining the Weyl groupoid identifies

(n,x)\sim(m,x)
precisely when there exists an open neighbourhood

U
of
x
such that
\alpha_{n}|_{U}=\alpha_{m}|_{U}
,
and such that
n
and
m
agree modulo multiplication by a diagonal
element supported near
x
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii).
Assume
[n,x]=[m,x]
. By definition of the germ relation, there exists
an open neighbourhood
U
of
x
and a function
d\in D
such that

d|_{U}=1
and
nd=md
. Since
d(x)=1\neq 0
, condition (ii) follows.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i).
Assume that there exists
d\in D
such that

d(x)\neq 0\quad\text{and}\quad nd=md.

By continuity, there exists an open neighbourhood
U
of
x
on which

d
does not vanish. Hence
d|_{U}
is invertible in
D|_{U}
.
Choose
e\in D
supported in
U
such that
e(x)\neq 0
. Since

d
is invertible on
U
, there exists
f\in D
with

fd=e
. Then

ne=nfd=mfd=me.

Thus
n
and
m
agree after multiplication by a diagonal element
nonvanishing at
x
, which is precisely the germ equivalence relation.
Hence

[n,x]=[m,x].

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
A=C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
with
\mathcal{G}
ample Hausdorff étale and topologically principal. Let
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
be an open invariant set. Set
I=C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{U})
. Then under the natural isomorphism
A/I\cong C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U})
, the image of
D=C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
is
C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U)
. That is,

(D+I)/I\cong C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U).

Proof.

Since
I\cap D=C_{0}(U)
, the restriction map
\rho:D=C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})\to C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U)
is a surjective
*
-homomorphism with kernel
I\cap D
.
Hence, by the first isomorphism theorem,
D/(I\cap D)\cong C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U)
. Using the canonical identification
D/(I\cap D)\cong(D+I)/I
, we obtain
(D+I)/I\cong C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U)
. Moreover, by the standard quotient theorem for reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras (see
[
15
]
),
A/I\cong C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U})
, and under this identification the image of
D
is precisely
C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U)
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(A,D)
be an untwisted ample
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pair with Weyl groupoid
\mathcal{G}
. Let
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
be an open invariant subset, and set
I=C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{U})
. Assume that the quotient pair
(D/(I\cap D)\subseteq A/I)
is a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pair. Then for
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{A}(D)
, the following are equivalent for any
x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U
:

(i)

[n_{1},x]=[n_{2},x]
in
\mathcal{G}
;

(ii)

[q(n_{1}),q(x)]=[q(n_{2}),q(x)]
in the Weyl groupoid of
A/I
, where
q:A\to A/I
is the quotient map.

Moreover, the map
[n,x]\mapsto[q(n),q(x)]
induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U}\cong\mathcal{G}_{A/I}
.

Proof.

We prove each direction using Lemma
4.1
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii). Assume
[n_{1},x]=[n_{2},x]
. By Lemma
4.1
, there exists
d\in D
such that
d(x)\neq 0
and
n_{1}d=n_{2}d
. Since
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is totally disconnected, there exists a clopen neighbourhood
V
of
x
on which
d
is nonvanishing. Let
p=1_{V}\in D
. Then
p(x)=1
and
n_{1}p=n_{2}p
. Applying
q
gives
q(n_{1})q(p)=q(n_{2})q(p)
with
q(p)(q(x))=1
because
x\notin U
. Hence Lemma
4.1
in the quotient yields
[q(n_{1}),q(x)]=[q(n_{2}),q(x)]
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i). Suppose
[q(n_{1}),q(x)]=[q(n_{2}),q(x)]
. By Lemma
4.1
applied in the quotient, there exists
\dot{d}\in D/(I\cap D)
such that
\dot{d}(q(x))\neq 0
and
q(n_{1})\dot{d}=q(n_{2})\dot{d}
. Choose a lift
d_{0}\in D
of
\dot{d}
. Because
\dot{d}(q(x))\neq 0
and
x\notin U
, we have
d_{0}(x)\neq 0
(any two lifts differ by an element of
C_{0}(U)
, which vanishes at
x
). Now pick a clopen neighbourhood
V
of
x
disjoint from
U
(possible because the space is totally disconnected). Define a function
f\in D
such that
f|_{V}=1
and
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subseteq V
. Then
f(x)=1
and
f
vanishes on
U
. Set
d=d_{0}f
. Then
d(x)=d_{0}(x)\neq 0
. Moreover,
q(d)=q(d_{0})q(f)=\dot{d}\cdot 1=\dot{d}
. Since
q
is injective on functions supported in
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U
, from
q(n_{1}d)=q(n_{2}d)
we get
n_{1}d=n_{2}d
. Now Lemma
4.1
gives
[n_{1},x]=[n_{2},x]
.

Thus the map
[n,x]\mapsto[q(n),q(x)]
is well-defined and bijective. Compatibility with multiplication and inversion follows from the fact that
q
is a
*
-homomorphism, and continuity follows from the definition of the germ topology.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
A=C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
with
\mathcal{G}
ample Hausdorff étale and topologically principal. Let
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
be an open invariant set, and set
I=C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{U})
. Then

(i)

if
n\in\mathcal{N}_{A}(D)
, then its image
\dot{n}=q(n)
in
A/I
belongs to
\mathcal{N}_{A/I}(D/(I\cap D))
.

(ii)

if
\mathcal{G}
is principal and
\dot{n}\in\mathcal{N}_{A/I}(D/(I\cap D))
lifts to some
n\in A
, then
n
can be chosen to be a normalizer of
D
.

Proof.

(i) Since
nDn^{*}\subseteq D
, applying the quotient map
q:A\to A/I
gives

\dot{n}\,q(D)\,\dot{n}^{*}=q(nDn^{*})\subseteq q(D).

Similarly,
\dot{n}^{*}q(D)\dot{n}\subseteq q(D)
. Because
q(D)\cong D/(I\cap D)
, we conclude
\dot{n}\in\mathcal{N}_{A/I}(D/(I\cap D))
.

(ii) Let
\dot{n}\in\mathcal{N}_{A/I}(D/(I\cap D))
. When
\mathcal{G}
is principal, every normalizer in
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U})
corresponds to a compact open bisection
S\subseteq\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U}
(see
[
12
]
). Since
\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is an open subgroupoid, the same set
S
is a compact open bisection of
\mathcal{G}
. Therefore the characteristic function
1_{S}\in C_{c}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq A
is a normalizer of
D
. Moreover,
q(1_{S})=\dot{n}
. Hence
\dot{n}
admits a lift that normalizes
D
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
A=C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
with
\mathcal{G}
ample Hausdorff étale and topologically principal. Let
I
be an ideal generated by
I\cap D
, corresponding to the open invariant set
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
(so
I=C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{U})
). Assume that the quotient pair
(D/(I\cap D)\subseteq A/I)
is a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pair. Then its Weyl groupoid is naturally isomorphic to the reduction
\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U}
. The isomorphism identifies the diagonal with
C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U)
and the normalizer germs with those of the reduction.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, we have

\frac{D+I}{I}\cong C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U).

For a normalizer
n\in\mathcal{N}_{A}(D)
, Lemma
4.4
guarantees that
\dot{n}=q(n)
normalizes the quotient diagonal. The associated partial homeomorphism on
\operatorname{Spec}(D/(I\cap D))
is the restriction of
\alpha_{n}
because for any character
\chi
of
D/(I\cap D)
lifted to
\widetilde{\chi}
of
D
,

\alpha_{\dot{n}}(\chi)(\dot{d})=\frac{\chi(\dot{n}^{*}\dot{d}\dot{n})}{\chi(\dot{n}^{*}\dot{n})}=\frac{\widetilde{\chi}(n^{*}dn)}{\widetilde{\chi}(n^{*}n)}=\alpha_{n}(\widetilde{\chi})(d).

Lemma
4.3
provides an explicit bijection between germs in
\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U}
and germs in the Weyl groupoid of the quotient. This bijection respects composition and inverses because
q
is a homomorphism.

Hence the Weyl groupoid of
(D/(I\cap D)\subseteq A/I)
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U}
. By Renault’s uniqueness theorem
[
15
, Theorem 5.9]
, this isomorphism is canonical.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
A=C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
, and
D=C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
, where
\mathcal{G}
is an
untwisted
ample Hausdorff étale principal groupoid.
Let
I\triangleleft A
be a closed ideal, and let
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
be the open invariant subset satisfying
I\cap D=C_{0}(U)
.
The following are equivalent

(i)

the reduction
\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U}
is topologically principal;

(ii)

the quotient pair
(D/(I\cap D)\subseteq A/I)
is an ample
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pair;

(iii)

I=C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{U})
and the reduction to the complement of the support of
I\cap D
is topologically principal.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.4
, every ideal is generated by
I\cap D
, so
I=C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{U})
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) From (i), we obtain
A/I\cong C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U})
by Lemma
4.2
, and
(D+I)/I\cong C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U)
. Since
\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U}
is ample, Hausdorff, étale, and topologically principal, the canonical inclusion

C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U)\subseteq C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U})

is an untwisted ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pair. Total disconnectedness follows because
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U
is closed in
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
. Hence (ii) holds.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) If the quotient is a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pair, Lemma
4.5
(which applies because (ii) gives the necessary hypothesis) demonstrates its Weyl groupoid is
\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U}
, which must be topologically principal. Moreover,
I
is generated by
I\cap D
by Lemma
3.4
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) If
I
is generated by
I\cap D
and
\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U}
is topologically principal, since
\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U}
is ample, Hausdorff, étale, and topologically principal, the canonical inclusion

C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U)\subseteq C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U})

is an untwisted ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pair. By Lemma
4.2
, this pair is isomorphic to the quotient pair, hence (i) holds.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an untwisted ample Hausdorff étale principal groupoid with compact unit space. Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
be the poset of open invariant subsets of
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, ordered by inclusion. Define a contravariant functor
\mathcal{Q}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}
as follows

(i)

For
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
open invariant, set

\mathcal{Q}(U)=\big(C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U)\subset C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U})\big).

(ii)

If
U\subseteq V
are open invariant subsets, define
\mathcal{Q}(U\subseteq V):\mathcal{Q}(V)\to\mathcal{Q}(U)
to be the quotient map

C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus V})\longrightarrow C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus V})\big/C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{V\setminus U})\cong C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U}),

where the isomorphism is induced by the reduction
\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U}\cong(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus V})|_{(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus V)\setminus(V\setminus U)}
.

Then
\mathcal{Q}
is a well-defined contravariant functor.

Proof.

We must show that
\mathcal{Q}(U)
is indeed an object of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}
and that
\mathcal{Q}(U\subseteq V)
is a morphism in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}
, and that functoriality holds.

Step 1:
\mathcal{Q}(U)
is an ample C*-diagonal pair.

Since
\mathcal{G}
is principal and ample, the reduction
\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U}
is again ample, Hausdorff, étale, and principal (restrictions preserve these properties). By Kumjian’s theorem, the inclusion
C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U)\subseteq C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U})
is an untwisted ample C*-diagonal pair. The unit space is totally disconnected because it is a closed subset of
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
. Hence
\mathcal{Q}(U)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}
.

Step 2:
\mathcal{Q}(U\subseteq V)
is a morphism.

The map is the composition of two quotient maps:

C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus V})\xrightarrow{\pi}C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus V})/C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{V\setminus U})\xrightarrow{\cong}C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U}).

We verify conditions (D), (E), (N) for
\pi
(the isomorphism clearly preserves them).

(D)

By Lemma
4.2
,
\pi(C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus V))=C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus V)/C_{0}(V\setminus U)\cong C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U)
. Hence the diagonal maps onto the diagonal of the quotient.

(E)

The conditional expectation on the quotient is defined by
E_{U}(\pi(f))=\pi(E_{V}(f))
, where
E_{V}
is the canonical conditional expectation from
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus V})
onto
C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus V)
. This is exactly the commuting relation.

(N)

If
n\in\mathcal{N}(C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus V))
, then by Lemma
4.4
(i),
\pi(n)
normalizes the quotient diagonal because
\pi
is a *-homomorphism and
\pi(D)=C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U)
. Thus
\pi
preserves normalizers.

Therefore
\pi
is a morphism in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}
. The isomorphism in the definition preserves the structure by Lemma
4.4
(ii).

Step 3: Functoriality.

For inclusions
U\subseteq V\subseteq W
, we have

\mathcal{Q}(U\subseteq W)=\mathcal{Q}(V\subseteq W)\circ\mathcal{Q}(U\subseteq V)

because the quotient map
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus W})\to C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus U})
factors uniquely through the intermediate quotient by
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{V\setminus U})
. The identity map
U\subseteq U
corresponds to the identity morphism
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{Q}(U)}
. Hence
\mathcal{Q}
is a contravariant functor.
∎

5.
Partial groupoid morphisms from diagonal isomorphisms

5.1.
Why partial groupoid morphisms?

A naive approach to functoriality would be to seek an ordinary continuous groupoid homomorphism
\rho:\mathcal{G}_{B}\to\mathcal{G}_{A}
extending the unit space homeomorphism
h
. However, this fails for two fundamental reasons.

First, a
*
-homomorphism
\Phi:A\to B
that is an isomorphism on diagonals need not map every normalizer of
C
to a normalizer of
D
. Only the normalizers in the image
\Phi(\mathcal{N}_{A}(D))
are guaranteed to lift. Consequently, only those germs in
\mathcal{G}_{B}
represented by this specific subset of normalizers can be mapped back to
\mathcal{G}_{A}
. This inevitably produces an
open subgroupoid

\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{B}
as the natural domain of the induced map.

Second, this phenomenon is already familiar in Renault’s theory: a Cartan subalgebra inclusion
(C\subset B)
corresponds to a twisted groupoid
(\mathcal{G},\Sigma)
, but a morphism of Cartan pairs does not generally yield a morphism of the underlying groupoids. Instead, it yields a
partial morphism
or a
morphism of extensions
, which is precisely captured by an open subgroupoid of the target. Our construction
\rho_{\Phi}:\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\to\mathcal{G}_{A}
is the concrete manifestation of this principle, bridging the gap between the algebraic map
\Phi
and the geometric groupoid models. It aligns with the philosophy of partial actions and inverse semigroups, where the normalizer semigroup
\mathcal{N}(D)
naturally acts partially on the spectrum of
D
.

5.2.
Partial groupoid morphisms

We now fix the hypotheses of the main theorem. Throughout this section, all ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pairs are assumed to be untwisted for simplicity; however, the construction works equally well for twisted pairs if one works with the underlying Weyl groupoid (ignoring the twist). The twist only affects the algebra isomorphism, not the germ construction.

Let
(A,D)
and
(B,C)
be ample
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pairs with topologically principal Weyl groupoids
\mathcal{G}_{A},\mathcal{G}_{B}
. Suppose that
\Phi:A\to B
is a unital
*
-homomorphism satisfying (D), (E), (N), and assume that
\Phi|_{D}:D\to C
is an isomorphism. Denote by
h:\mathcal{G}_{B}^{(0)}\to\mathcal{G}_{A}^{(0)}
the homeomorphism induced by
\Phi|_{D}
. For achieving the main result in this section, we give some auxiliary lemmas on normalizers. The following result demonstrates the most basic compatibility; i.e., normalizers are preserved by the homomorphism. This is essential because Weyl groupoids are built from normalizers.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
n\in\mathcal{N}_{A}(D)
, then
\Phi(n)\in\mathcal{N}_{B}(C)
.

Proof.

For any
c\in C
, write
c=\Phi(d)
with
d\in D
(using surjectivity of
\Phi|_{D}
). Then

\Phi(n)\,c\,\Phi(n)^{*}=\Phi(n)\Phi(d)\Phi(n^{*})=\Phi(ndn^{*})\in\Phi(D)=C,

because
ndn^{*}\in D
. Similarly for
\Phi(n)^{*}c\Phi(n)
. Hence
\Phi(n)
normalizes
C
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
n\in\mathcal{N}_{A}(D)
, let
\alpha_{n}
be its partial homeomorphism on
\mathcal{G}_{A}^{(0)}
and
\alpha_{\Phi(n)}
the corresponding partial homeomorphism on
\mathcal{G}_{B}^{(0)}
. Then

\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{\Phi(n)})=h^{-1}(\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{n})).

Proof.

Recall that the domain of
\alpha_{n}
consists of those characters
\chi
of
D
for which
\chi(n^{*}n)\neq 0
. Under the isomorphism
\Phi|_{D}
, characters of
C
correspond to characters of
D
via
\chi\mapsto\chi\circ\Phi|_{D}
. For a character
\psi
of
C
, let
\chi=\psi\circ\Phi|_{D}
be the corresponding character of
D
. Then

\psi((\Phi(n))^{*}\Phi(n))=\psi(\Phi(n^{*}n))=\chi(n^{*}n).

Thus
\psi\in\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{\Phi(n)})
if and only if
\chi\in\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{n})
. The homeomorphism
h
is exactly the map
\psi\mapsto\chi
, so
\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{\Phi(n)})=h^{-1}(\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{n}))
.
∎

Definition 5.3
.

Define

\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}:=\{n\in\mathcal{N}_{B}(C):\exists\,m\in\mathcal{N}_{A}(D),\;\Phi(m)=n\}.

For each
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}
, the element
m
is unique because
\Phi
is injective (Lemma
5.4
below); write
L(n)=m
.

Lemma 5.4
.

\Phi
is injective.

Proof.

\Phi
is a unital
*
-homomorphism between
C^{*}
-algebras, so its kernel is a closed two-sided ideal. Assume towards a contradiction that
\ker\Phi\neq\{0\}
. Then by Lemma
3.4
,
\ker\Phi\cap D\neq\{0\}
because every ideal of
A
is generated by its intersection with
D
. But
\Phi|_{D}
is an isomorphism, hence injective, so
\ker\Phi\cap D=\{0\}
, a contradiction. Thus
\ker\Phi=\{0\}
. (Note: Lemma
3.4
assumes untwistedness; however, the same conclusion holds for twisted pairs because effectiveness still gives ideal generation by
I\cap D
.)
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

The map
L:\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}\to\mathcal{N}_{A}(D)
is a bijection onto its image and satisfies:

(i)

L(n^{*})=L(n)^{*}
;

(ii)

If
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}
are composable, then
n_{1}n_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}
and
L(n_{1}n_{2})=L(n_{1})L(n_{2})
;

(iii)

L
preserves support projections:
\Phi(L(n)^{*}L(n))=n^{*}n
and
\Phi(L(n)L(n)^{*})=nn^{*}
.

Proof.

(i) and (iii) are immediate from
\Phi
being a
*
-homomorphism and injective.

(ii) Since
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{B}(C)
, their product
n_{1}n_{2}
is in
\mathcal{N}_{B}(C)
. Moreover,
\Phi(L(n_{1})L(n_{2}))=n_{1}n_{2}
, hence by definition
n_{1}n_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}
and
L(n_{1}n_{2})=L(n_{1})L(n_{2})
by uniqueness.
∎

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{B}
be the set of germs represented by elements of
\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}
:

\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}=\{[n,y]:n\in\mathcal{N}_{\Phi},\;y\in\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{n})\}.

Lemma 5.6
.

\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
is an open subgroupoid of
\mathcal{G}_{B}
.

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}
, the set

\mathcal{Z}(n,\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{n}))=\{[n,y]:y\in\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{n})\}

is a basic open bisection in the Weyl topology on
\mathcal{G}_{B}
. Since

\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}}\mathcal{Z}(n,\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{n})),

it follows that
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
is open. For every
y\in\mathcal{G}_{B}^{(0)}
,
[1_{B},y]=y
. Since
1_{B}=\Phi(1_{A})
, we have
1_{B}\in\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}
. Thus every unit belongs to
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
. If
[n,y]\in\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
, then
[n,y]^{-1}=[n^{*},\alpha_{n}(y)]
. By Lemma
5.5
,
n^{*}\in\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}
, hence
[n,y]^{-1}\in\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
.
Suppose that
[n_{2},y_{2}],[n_{1},y_{1}]\in\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
are composable. By the definition of multiplication in the Weyl groupoid,

[n_{2},y_{2}][n_{1},y_{1}]=[n_{2}n_{1},y_{1}].

Again by Lemma
5.5
, we have
n_{2}n_{1}\in\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}
. Therefore
[n_{2}n_{1},y_{1}]\in\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
. Hence
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
is an open subgroupoid of
\mathcal{G}_{B}
.
∎

Define
\rho_{\Phi}:\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\to\mathcal{G}_{A}
by

\rho_{\Phi}([n,y])=[L(n),h(y)].

Lemma 5.7
.

The map
\rho_{\Phi}:\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\to\mathcal{G}_{A}
defined as above is a well-defined injective continuous groupoid homomorphism which is open onto its image. Moreover,

\rho_{\Phi}|_{\mathcal{G}_{B}^{(0)}}=h
.

Proof.

Suppose that
[n_{1},y]=[n_{2},y]
in
\mathcal{G}_{B}
with
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}
. By Lemma
4.1
applied to
(B,C)
, there exists
e\in C
with
e(y)\neq 0
and
n_{1}e=n_{2}e
. Since
\Phi|_{D}
is an isomorphism, there is a unique
d\in D
with
\Phi(d)=e
. Then
d(h(y))\neq 0
and

\Phi(L(n_{1})d)=n_{1}e=n_{2}e=\Phi(L(n_{2})d).

Since
\Phi
is injective, it follows that
L(n_{1})d=L(n_{2})d
. Because
d(h(y))\neq 0
, the standard germ criterion (Lemma
4.1
) applied to
(A,D)
implies
[L(n_{1}),h(y)]=[L(n_{2}),h(y)]
. Hence
\rho_{\Phi}([n_{1},y])=\rho_{\Phi}([n_{2},y])
. This demonstrates
\rho_{\Phi}
is well-defined.

Assume that
\rho_{\Phi}([n_{1},y_{1}])=\rho_{\Phi}([n_{2},y_{2}])
. Then
h(y_{1})=h(y_{2})
and
[L(n_{1}),h(y_{1})]=[L(n_{2}),h(y_{1})]
in
\mathcal{G}_{A}
. By Lemma
4.1
in
\mathcal{G}_{A}
, there exists
d\in D
with
d(h(y_{1}))\neq 0
and
L(n_{1})d=L(n_{2})d
. Apply
\Phi
:
n_{1}\Phi(d)=n_{2}\Phi(d)
with
\Phi(d)(y_{1})\neq 0
. Lemma
4.1
in
\mathcal{G}_{B}
now yields
[n_{1},y_{1}]=[n_{2},y_{1}]
. Since
h(y_{1})=h(y_{2})
and
h
is injective,
y_{1}=y_{2}
. Thus the germs are equal and this means that
\rho_{\Phi}
is injective.

We now verify the homomorphism properties. For any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{\Phi}(y)=

\displaystyle\rho_{\Phi}([1_{B},y])=[L(1_{B}),h(y)]=[1_{A},h(y)]=h(y).

For any
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}
and
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
,

\displaystyle\rho_{\Phi}([n,y]^{-1})=

\displaystyle\rho_{\Phi}([n^{*},\alpha_{n}(y)])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[L(n^{*}),h(\alpha_{n}(y))]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[L(n)^{*},\alpha_{L(n)}(h(y))]=\rho_{\Phi}([n,y])^{-1}.

If
[n_{2},y_{2}][n_{1},y_{1}]=[n_{2}n_{1},y_{1}]
is composable, then

\displaystyle\rho_{\Phi}([n_{2}n_{1},y_{1}])=

\displaystyle[L(n_{2}n_{1}),h(y_{1})]=[L(n_{2})L(n_{1}),h(y_{1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{\Phi}([n_{2},y_{2}])\rho_{\Phi}([n_{1},y_{1}]).

We verify continuity using a conceptual argument. Observe that the map
\rho_{\Phi}
is the unique continuous extension of the inverse of the isomorphism
C(\mathcal{G}_{B}^{(0)})\to C(\mathcal{G}_{A}^{(0)})
induced by
\Phi|_{D}
. More concretely, for a basic open bisection
\mathcal{Z}(m,V)\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{A}
with
m\in\mathcal{N}_{A}(D)
and
V\subseteq\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{m})
open, we have

\rho_{\Phi}^{-1}(\mathcal{Z}(m,V))=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}\\
L(n)=m\end{subarray}}\mathcal{Z}\!\big(n,\;h^{-1}(V\cap\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{m}))\big).

For each such
n
, Lemma
5.2
gives
\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{n})=h^{-1}(\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{m}))
. Since
h^{-1}
is continuous,
h^{-1}(V\cap\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{m}))
is open. Hence each
\mathcal{Z}(n,h^{-1}(V\cap\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{m})))
is a basic open set in
\mathcal{G}_{B}
, and its intersection with
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
is open. The union over all such
n
is therefore open, proving continuity.

Let
\mathcal{Z}(n,W)
be a basic open set in
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
, with
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}
and
W\subseteq\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{n})
open. Then

\rho_{\Phi}(\mathcal{Z}(n,W))=\{[L(n),h(y)]:y\in W\}=\mathcal{Z}(L(n),h(W)).

Since sets of the form
\mathcal{Z}(L(n),V)
form a basis for the topology on the image subgroupoid, and
h(W)
is open (because
h
is a homeomorphism),
\mathcal{Z}(L(n),h(W))
is a basic open set in
\mathcal{G}_{A}
. It follows that the image of every basic open set is open, proving openness onto its image.
Finally, for the restriction to unit spaces, for
y\in\mathcal{G}_{B}^{(0)}
,
\rho_{\Phi}(y)=h(y)
by the unit case above.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
(A,D)
and
(B,C)
be ample
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pairs with topologically principal Weyl groupoids
\mathcal{G}_{A},\mathcal{G}_{B}
. Suppose
\Phi:A\to B
is a unital
*
-homomorphism satisfying (D), (E), (N), and assume that
\Phi|_{D}:D\to C
is an isomorphism. Define

\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}:=\{n\in\mathcal{N}_{B}(C):\exists\,m\in\mathcal{N}_{A}(D),\;\Phi(m)=n\},

and let
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{B}
be the set of germs represented by elements of
\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}
. Then:

(i)

\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
is an open subgroupoid of
\mathcal{G}_{B}
;

(ii)

there exists a continuous open injective groupoid homomorphism
\rho_{\Phi}:\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\longrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{A}
which restricts on unit spaces to the homeomorphism
h:\mathcal{G}_{B}^{(0)}\cong\mathcal{G}_{A}^{(0)}
induced by
\Phi|_{D}
;

(iii)

the image
\rho_{\Phi}(\mathcal{H}_{\Phi})
is an open subgroupoid of
\mathcal{G}_{A}
.

Proof.

(i) Apply Lemma
5.6
.

(ii) Apply Lemma
5.7
.

(iii) Each set
\mathcal{Z}(L(n),h(\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{n})))
is a basic open bisection in
\mathcal{G}_{A}
. Hence

\rho_{\Phi}(\mathcal{H}_{\Phi})=\bigcup_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}}\mathcal{Z}(L(n),h(\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{n})))

is open.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

Theorem
5.8
has the following geometric interpretation.

Why partiality appears?

The
*
-homomorphism
\Phi:A\to B
lifts to a map on normalizers:
\Phi(\mathcal{N}_{A}(D))\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{B}(C)
. However, the converse inclusion fails in general: not every normalizer of
C
lies in the image of
\Phi
. Consequently, only those germs in
\mathcal{G}_{B}
represented by normalizers that
do
lift can be mapped back to
\mathcal{G}_{A}
. This subset
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{B}
is exactly the domain of the induced map.

Why subgroupoids are inevitable?

The set
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
is not arbitrary: it is an open subgroupoid. This follows from the fact that liftable normalizers are closed under multiplication (if
n_{1},n_{2}
lift, so does
n_{1}n_{2}
) and under taking adjoints. Moreover, every unit lifts because
1_{B}=\Phi(1_{A})
. Thus
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
inherits the groupoid structure from
\mathcal{G}_{B}
.

Contrast with Kumjian’s equivalence?

When
\Phi
is an isomorphism,
\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}=\mathcal{N}_{B}(C)
and
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}=\mathcal{G}_{B}
. In that case,
\rho_{\Phi}
is an ordinary groupoid isomorphism, recovering Kumjian’s equivalence of categories between twists and
C^{*}
-diagonals. For non-isomorphisms, the domain must shrink to the largest subgroupoid on which a well-defined map exists. This is analogous to passing from global actions to partial actions in inverse semigroup theory.

5.3.
Functoriality of the Weyl groupoid

We now formulate a functorial version of the Weyl groupoid construction for diagonal-preserving morphisms. Since a morphism of diagonal pairs induces a pullback map on spectra, the resulting Weyl groupoid construction is naturally contravariant.

Definition 5.10
.

Let
\mathsf{Grpd}_{\mathrm{part}}
denote the category whose objects are ample Hausdorff étale topologically principal groupoids.
A morphism from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{G}
is a triple
(\mathcal{K},\rho,h)
where

(i)

\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
is an open subgroupoid containing
\mathcal{H}^{(0)}
;

(ii)

\rho:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{G}
is a continuous, open, injective groupoid homomorphism;

(iii)

h:\mathcal{H}^{(0)}\to\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is a homeomorphism satisfying

\rho|_{\mathcal{K}^{(0)}}=h|_{\mathcal{K}^{(0)}}.

If
(\mathcal{K}_{1},\rho_{1},h_{1}):\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
and
(\mathcal{K}_{2},\rho_{2},h_{2}):\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{F}
are morphisms, define

\mathcal{K}=\rho_{1}^{-1}\big(\mathcal{K}_{2}\cap\rho_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{1})\big)\subseteq\mathcal{H},

and define the composition by

(\mathcal{K}_{2},\rho_{2},h_{2})\circ(\mathcal{K}_{1},\rho_{1},h_{1})=(\mathcal{K},\rho_{2}\circ\rho_{1},h_{2}\circ h_{1}).

The identity morphism on
\mathcal{G}
is
(\mathcal{G},\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}},\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}})
.

Proposition 5.11
.

\mathsf{Grpd}_{\mathrm{part}}
is a category.

Proof.

We verify the axioms explicitly.

Step 1:
\mathcal{K}
is an open subgroupoid of
\mathcal{H}
containing
\mathcal{H}^{(0)}
.

-
Open:

\mathcal{K}_{2}
is open in
\mathcal{G}
and
\rho_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{1})
is open in
\mathcal{G}
(since
\rho_{1}
is open onto its image and
\mathcal{K}_{1}
is open). Their intersection
\mathcal{K}_{2}\cap\rho_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{1})
is open in
\mathcal{G}
. Since
\rho_{1}
is continuous,
\rho_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{K}_{2}\cap\rho_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{1}))
is open in
\mathcal{K}_{1}
, hence open in
\mathcal{H}
.

-
Contains units:
For any unit
u\in\mathcal{H}^{(0)}
, we have
\rho_{1}(u)=h_{1}(u)\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
. Since
\mathcal{K}_{2}
contains
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
(as it is a subgroupoid containing all units of
\mathcal{G}
), we have
\rho_{1}(u)\in\mathcal{K}_{2}
. Also
\rho_{1}(u)\in\rho_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{1})
because
u\in\mathcal{K}_{1}
(since
\mathcal{K}_{1}
contains
\mathcal{H}^{(0)}
). Hence
\rho_{1}(u)\in\mathcal{K}_{2}\cap\rho_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{1})
, so
u\in\mathcal{K}
. Thus
\mathcal{H}^{(0)}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
.

-
Subgroupoid:
If
x,y\in\mathcal{K}
are composable in
\mathcal{H}
, then
\rho_{1}(x),\rho_{1}(y)\in\mathcal{K}_{2}
are composable in
\mathcal{G}
(since
\rho_{1}
is a homomorphism). Their product
\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{1}(y)
lies in
\mathcal{K}_{2}
because
\mathcal{K}_{2}
is a subgroupoid. Also
\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{1}(y)=\rho_{1}(xy)
(since
\rho_{1}
is a homomorphism), and
xy\in\mathcal{K}_{1}
(since
\mathcal{K}_{1}
is a subgroupoid). Thus
xy\in\mathcal{K}
. Similarly, if
x\in\mathcal{K}
, then
\rho_{1}(x^{-1})=\rho_{1}(x)^{-1}\in\mathcal{K}_{2}
, so
x^{-1}\in\mathcal{K}
. Hence
\mathcal{K}
is a subgroupoid.

Step 2:
\rho
is a continuous, open, injective groupoid homomorphism.

-
Homomorphism:
For composable
x,y\in\mathcal{K}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho(xy)=

\displaystyle\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}(xy))=\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{1}(y))=\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}(x))\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x)\rho(y).

-
Injectivity:
If
\rho(x)=\rho(y)
, then
\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}(x))=\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}(y))
. Since
\rho_{2}
is injective,
\rho_{1}(x)=\rho_{1}(y)
. Since
\rho_{1}
is injective,
x=y
.

-
Continuity:

\rho=\rho_{2}\circ\rho_{1}|_{\mathcal{K}}
is the composition of continuous maps.

-
Openness onto image:
For any open
U\subseteq\mathcal{K}
,
\rho_{1}(U)
is open in
\rho_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{1})
(since
\rho_{1}
is open onto its image). Then
\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}(U))
is open in
\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{1}))
because
\rho_{2}
is open onto its image. Hence
\rho(U)
is open in
\rho(\mathcal{K})
.

Step 3:
\rho|_{\mathcal{K}^{(0)}}=h|_{\mathcal{K}^{(0)}}
.

For any unit
u\in\mathcal{K}^{(0)}=\mathcal{H}^{(0)}\cap\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{H}^{(0)}
(since
\mathcal{K}
contains all units), we have:

\rho(u)=\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}(u))=\rho_{2}(h_{1}(u))=h_{2}(h_{1}(u))=h(u).

Thus the triple
(\mathcal{K},\rho,h)
is indeed a morphism from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{F}
.

Step 4: Identity morphisms.

For any object
\mathcal{G}
, define
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}}:=(\mathcal{G},\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}},\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}})
. This satisfies the conditions:
\mathcal{G}
is an open subgroupoid of itself containing
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
;
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}}
is continuous, open, injective, and a homomorphism; and
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}}|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}
.

Verification that
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}}\circ(\mathcal{K}_{1},\rho_{1},h_{1})=(\mathcal{K}_{1},\rho_{1},h_{1})
:
- Compute
\mathcal{K}=\rho_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}\cap\rho_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{1}))=\rho_{1}^{-1}(\rho_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{1}))=\mathcal{K}_{1}
(since
\rho_{1}
is injective).
-
\rho=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}}\circ\rho_{1}=\rho_{1}
.
-
h=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\circ h_{1}=h_{1}
.

Similarly,
(\mathcal{K}_{1},\rho_{1},h_{1})\circ\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{H}}=(\mathcal{K}_{1},\rho_{1},h_{1})
.

Step 5: Associativity.

Consider three morphisms:

(\mathcal{K}_{1},\rho_{1},h_{1}):\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G},\quad(\mathcal{K}_{2},\rho_{2},h_{2}):\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{F},\quad(\mathcal{K}_{3},\rho_{3},h_{3}):\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{E}.

First compute
(\mathcal{K}_{3},\rho_{3},h_{3})\circ((\mathcal{K}_{2},\rho_{2},h_{2})\circ(\mathcal{K}_{1},\rho_{1},h_{1}))
:

Let
(\mathcal{K}_{21},\rho_{21},h_{21})=(\mathcal{K}_{2},\rho_{2},h_{2})\circ(\mathcal{K}_{1},\rho_{1},h_{1})
where

\mathcal{K}_{21}=\rho_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{K}_{2}\cap\rho_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{1})),\quad\rho_{21}=\rho_{2}\circ\rho_{1}|_{\mathcal{K}_{21}},\quad h_{21}=h_{2}\circ h_{1}.

Now compose with
(\mathcal{K}_{3},\rho_{3},h_{3})
:

\mathcal{K}_{(31)21}=\rho_{21}^{-1}(\mathcal{K}_{3}\cap\rho_{21}(\mathcal{K}_{21})).

Now compute
((\mathcal{K}_{3},\rho_{3},h_{3})\circ(\mathcal{K}_{2},\rho_{2},h_{2}))\circ(\mathcal{K}_{1},\rho_{1},h_{1})
:

Let
(\mathcal{K}_{32},\rho_{32},h_{32})=(\mathcal{K}_{3},\rho_{3},h_{3})\circ(\mathcal{K}_{2},\rho_{2},h_{2})
where

\mathcal{K}_{32}=\rho_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{K}_{3}\cap\rho_{2}(\mathcal{K}_{2})),\quad\rho_{32}=\rho_{3}\circ\rho_{2}|_{\mathcal{K}_{32}},\quad h_{32}=h_{3}\circ h_{2}.

Now compose with
(\mathcal{K}_{1},\rho_{1},h_{1})
:

\mathcal{K}_{1(32)}=\rho_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{K}_{32}\cap\rho_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{1})).

Claim:

\mathcal{K}_{(31)21}=\mathcal{K}_{1(32)}
and the resulting maps coincide.

*Proof of claim.* An element
x\in\mathcal{H}
belongs to
\mathcal{K}_{(31)21}
iff:
1.
x\in\mathcal{K}_{21}
(so
x\in\mathcal{K}_{1}
and
\rho_{1}(x)\in\mathcal{K}_{2}
), and
2.
\rho_{21}(x)=\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}(x))\in\mathcal{K}_{3}
.

Thus
x\in\mathcal{K}_{1}
,
\rho_{1}(x)\in\mathcal{K}_{2}
, and
\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}(x))\in\mathcal{K}_{3}
.

An element
x\in\mathcal{H}
belongs to
\mathcal{K}_{1(32)}
iff:
1.
x\in\mathcal{K}_{1}
, and
2.
\rho_{1}(x)\in\mathcal{K}_{32}
, i.e.,
\rho_{1}(x)\in\mathcal{K}_{2}
and
\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}(x))\in\mathcal{K}_{3}
.
These conditions are identical. Hence
\mathcal{K}_{(31)21}=\mathcal{K}_{1(32)}
. Moreover, both compositions yield the map
\rho_{3}\circ\rho_{2}\circ\rho_{1}
on this common domain, and the unit space map
h_{3}\circ h_{2}\circ h_{1}
.

Thus associativity holds. Since all category axioms are satisfied,
\mathsf{Grpd}_{\mathrm{part}}
is a category.
∎

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
denote the category whose objects are untwisted ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pairs
(A,D)
, and whose morphisms
\Phi:(A,D)\to(B,C)
are diagonal-preserving
∗
-homomorphisms such that
\Phi|_{D}:D\to C
is an isomorphism. Then there exists a contravariant functor
\mathcal{W}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Grpd}_{\mathrm{part}}
defined as follows

(i)

On objects,
\mathcal{W}(A,D)=\mathcal{G}_{(A,D)}
, the Weyl groupoid of the diagonal pair.

(ii)

Let
\Phi:(A,D)\to(B,C)
be a morphism. Define

\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}=\{n\in\mathcal{N}_{B}(C):n=\Phi(m)\text{ for some }m\in\mathcal{N}_{A}(D)\}.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{B}
be the set of all germs
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}=\{[n,y]:n\in\mathcal{N}_{\Phi},\;y\in\operatorname{dom}(\alpha_{n})\}
. Since
\Phi|_{D}:D\to C
is an isomorphism, Gelfand duality yields a homeomorphism
h_{\Phi}:\mathcal{G}_{B}^{(0)}\to\mathcal{G}_{A}^{(0)}
. Define
\rho_{\Phi}:\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\to\mathcal{G}_{A}
by
\rho_{\Phi}([n,y])=[m,h_{\Phi}(y)]
, where
m\in\mathcal{N}_{A}(D)
is the unique normalizer satisfying
\Phi(m)=n
.
Set
\mathcal{W}(\Phi)=(\mathcal{H}_{\Phi},\rho_{\Phi},h_{\Phi})
.

Proof.

The proof is identical to that in the original manuscript, noting that the construction only uses the Weyl groupoids (which are independent of the twist) and the fact that
\Phi|_{D}
is an isomorphism. The functor lands in
\mathsf{Grpd}_{\mathrm{part}}
by Theorem
5.8
. Contravariance follows from the composition formula verified in the original proof.
∎

Remark 5.13
.

The functor
\mathcal{W}
is not faithful on the full category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
because two different twists over the same principal groupoid give non-isomorphic diagonal pairs but identical Weyl groupoids (the twist is not recorded by
\mathcal{W}
). However, when restricted to the subcategory of
untwisted
pairs,
\mathcal{W}
becomes faithful (see Theorem
7.5
below). Thus faithfulness is a feature of the untwisted setting only.

6.
Tensor products and applications

In this section we study tensor-product constructions for ample C
∗
-diagonal pairs and the corresponding behaviour of their Weyl groupoids. In particular, we show that the tensor product of ample C
∗
-diagonal pairs again carries a natural diagonal structure whose Weyl groupoid is the product of the original groupoids.

We then apply these results to questions concerning diagonal dimension, AF diagonals, crossed products, graph algebras, and recently constructed exotic diagonals in UHF and Cuntz algebras.

Remark 6.1
.

If
\mathcal{G}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}
are second-countable topologically principal groupoids, then their product
\mathcal{G}_{1}\times\mathcal{G}_{2}
is again topologically principal. This follows because the set of points with trivial isotropy is the product of the corresponding dense sets, and the product of dense subsets is dense in the product topology.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
E_{i}:A_{i}\to D_{i}
be faithful conditional expectations. Then
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}:A_{1}\otimes_{\min}A_{2}\to D_{1}\otimes_{\min}D_{2}
is a faithful conditional expectation.

Proof.

The map is a conditional expectation by standard tensor product theory. For faithfulness, let
x\in A_{1}\otimes_{\min}A_{2}
be positive with
(E_{1}\otimes E_{2})(x)=0
. Choose faithful states
\varphi_{i}
on
D_{i}
; then
\varphi_{i}\circ E_{i}
are faithful on
A_{i}
. The product state
(\varphi_{1}\circ E_{1})\otimes(\varphi_{2}\circ E_{2})
is faithful on the minimal tensor product
[
2
, Chapter 3]
. Applying it to
x
gives zero, hence
x=0
.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
(A_{1},D_{1},E_{1})
and
(A_{2},D_{2},E_{2})
be ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pairs with Weyl groupoids
\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}
and
\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}}
. Then

(D_{1}\otimes D_{2}\subseteq A_{1}\otimes_{\min}A_{2})
together with
E_{A_{1}\otimes A_{2}}:=E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
is an ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pair. Moreover, its Weyl groupoid is naturally isomorphic to

\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}\times\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}}
, and its associated twist is
\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
(A_{i},D_{i})
are ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pairs, Kumjian’s reconstruction theorem
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
yields twists
\Sigma_{i}
over
\mathcal{G}_{A_{i}}
together with isomorphisms

A_{i}\cong C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{A_{i}},\Sigma_{i}),\qquad D_{i}\cong C(\mathcal{G}_{A_{i}}^{(0)}).

By
[
3
, Lemma 5.1]
, we obtain

C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}},\Sigma_{1})\otimes_{\min}C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}},\Sigma_{2})\cong C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}\times\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}},\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2}).

Hence, we obtain

A_{1}\otimes_{\min}A_{2}\cong C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}\times\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}},\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2}).

Under this identification,
D_{1}\otimes D_{2}
corresponds to

C(\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}^{(0)})\otimes C(\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}}^{(0)})\cong C(\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}^{(0)}\times\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}}^{(0)}),

which is precisely
C((\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}\times\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}})^{(0)})
. Faithfulness of
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
follows from Lemma
6.2
. Since
\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}\times\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}}
is again an ample Hausdorff étale topologically principal groupoid (the product of topologically principal groupoids is topologically principal when both are second-countable, which holds here; see the remark above), the canonical inclusion

C((\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}\times\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}})^{(0)})\subseteq C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}\times\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}},\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2})

is an ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pair. Finally, since products of étale groupoids are étale and products of compact open bisections are compact open bisections, the groupoid

\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}\times\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}}

is ample.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Under the hypotheses of Proposition
6.3
, if additionally the Weyl groupoids
\mathcal{G}_{A_{i}}
are principal and have finite dynamic asymptotic dimension, then

\dim_{\mathrm{diag}}(D_{1}\otimes D_{2}\subset A_{1}\otimes A_{2})\leq\dim_{\mathrm{diag}}(D_{1}\subset A_{1})+\dim_{\mathrm{diag}}(D_{2}\subset A_{2}).

Proof.

By Proposition
6.3
, the Weyl groupoid of
(D_{1}\otimes D_{2}\subset A_{1}\otimes A_{2})
is
\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}\times\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}}
. Since
\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}
and
\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}}
are principal, their product is also principal (the isotropy of
(g_{1},g_{2})
is the product of the isotropy groups, each trivial). Therefore, by
[
13
, Theorem E]
, we have the equality

\dim_{\mathrm{diag}}(D_{1}\otimes D_{2}\subset A_{1}\otimes A_{2})=\operatorname{dad}(\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}\times\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}}).

Applying the same theorem to each factor (using that
\mathcal{G}_{A_{i}}
are principal) gives

\dim_{\mathrm{diag}}(D_{i}\subset A_{i})=\operatorname{dad}(\mathcal{G}_{A_{i}}),\qquad i=1,2.

Now by
[
5
, Theorem A(3)]
, the dynamic asymptotic dimension satisfies the subadditivity inequality:

\operatorname{dad}(\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}\times\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}})\leq\operatorname{dad}(\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}})+\operatorname{dad}(\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}}).

Combining these equalities/inequality yields the desired result.
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

The full subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}
admits a natural symmetric monoidal structure defined as follows:

(i)

Tensor product on objects:
For objects
(A_{1},D_{1},E_{1}),(A_{2},D_{2},E_{2})
in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
, define

(A_{1},D_{1},E_{1})\otimes(A_{2},D_{2},E_{2})=(A_{1}\otimes_{\min}A_{2},\ D_{1}\otimes_{\min}D_{2},\ E_{1}\otimes E_{2}).

(ii)

Tensor product on morphisms:
For
\Phi_{1}:(A_{1},D_{1})\to(B_{1},C_{1})
and
\Phi_{2}:(A_{2},D_{2})\to(B_{2},C_{2})
in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
, define

(\Phi_{1}\otimes\Phi_{2})(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})=\Phi_{1}(a_{1})\otimes\Phi_{2}(a_{2}).

(iii)

Unit object:

\mathbb{1}=(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C},\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

(iv)

Associator, unitors, symmetry:
The usual associativity, unit, and symmetry isomorphisms of the spatial tensor product.

Then
(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}},\otimes,\mathbb{1})
is a symmetric monoidal category.

Proof.

We must verify that:

1. The tensor product of objects lies in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
(i.e., it is an ample C*-diagonal pair with
\Phi|_{D}
an isomorphism for the structure maps).
2. The tensor product of morphisms is a morphism in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
.
3. The structural isomorphisms are morphisms in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
.
4. The coherence axioms hold.

Step 1: Tensor product of objects is in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
.

By Proposition
6.3
,
(D_{1}\otimes D_{2}\subseteq A_{1}\otimes A_{2})
with expectation
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
is an ample C*-diagonal pair. Moreover, since
\Phi_{i}|_{D}
are isomorphisms, their tensor product is also an isomorphism. Hence the tensor product of objects is well-defined.

Step 2: Tensor product of morphisms.

Let
\Phi_{1}:(A_{1},D_{1})\to(B_{1},C_{1})
and
\Phi_{2}:(A_{2},D_{2})\to(B_{2},C_{2})
be morphisms in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
. Then
\Phi_{1}\otimes\Phi_{2}
is a unital *-homomorphism. Condition (D) holds because
(\Phi_{1}\otimes\Phi_{2})(D_{1}\otimes D_{2})=\Phi_{1}(D_{1})\otimes\Phi_{2}(D_{2})\subseteq C_{1}\otimes C_{2}
. Condition (E) follows from
(E_{1}\otimes E_{2})\circ(\Phi_{1}\otimes\Phi_{2})=(\Phi_{1}\circ E_{1})\otimes(\Phi_{2}\circ E_{2})=(E_{1}^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{1})\otimes(E_{2}^{\prime}\circ\Phi_{2})=(E_{1}^{\prime}\otimes E_{2}^{\prime})\circ(\Phi_{1}\otimes\Phi_{2})
. Condition (N) holds because normalizers tensor to normalizers, and the isomorphism property on diagonals is preserved. Thus
\Phi_{1}\otimes\Phi_{2}
is a morphism in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
.

Step 3: Structural isomorphisms.

The associator
\alpha_{A,B,F}:(A\otimes B)\otimes F\to A\otimes(B\otimes F)
is an isomorphism in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
because it preserves diagonals, expectations, and normalizers (as verified in the original manuscript). Similarly for the unitors and symmetry.

Step 4: Coherence axioms.

The pentagon, triangle, and hexagon axioms hold because they hold in the category of C*-algebras with the spatial tensor product, and all arrows are morphisms in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
.

Thus
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
is a symmetric monoidal category. Note that the larger category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}
is not monoidal because the tensor product of arbitrary morphisms may not satisfy condition (N).
∎

Remark 6.6
.

It is expected that if the Weyl groupoid G of an ample C*-diagonal pair is non-amenable, then the pair cannot be AF. Indeed, AF C*-algebras are nuclear and admit Bratteli diagram models, and it is plausible that the associated Weyl groupoids admit AF-type descriptions in the sense of Renault
[
15
]
, hence are amenable. A complete proof would require establishing that AF diagonal pairs have AF Weyl groupoids in the precise groupoid-theoretic sense, which is beyond the scope of this paper. Nevertheless, the converse fails: Kopsacheilis and Winter
[
11
]
constructed non-AF diagonals with amenable Weyl groupoids (Example
8.5
), showing that amenability does not characterize AF diagonals.

7.
Faithfulness of the Weyl functor

We now show that the Weyl functor
\mathcal{W}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}\to\mathsf{Grpd}_{\mathrm{part}}

is **faithful** when restricted to the subcategory of
untwisted
pairs. This provides a concrete invariant that distinguishes non-isomorphic diagonal pairs.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{fd}}
denote the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
consisting of untwisted ample C*-diagonal pairs
(D\subset A)
such that:

(i)

the Weyl groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{A}
is principal;

(ii)

the diagonal dimension
\dim_{\mathrm{diag}}(D\subset A)<\infty
;

(iii)

the conditional expectation
E_{A}:A\to D
is faithful and has the unique extension property.

The next result records the functoriality of the Weyl groupoid construction.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
\Phi:(A_{1},D_{1})\longrightarrow(A_{2},D_{2})
be an isomorphism of
C^{*}
-diagonal pairs. Then
\Phi
induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{W}(\Phi):\mathcal{G}_{(A_{1},D_{1})}\longrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{(A_{2},D_{2})}
between the associated Weyl groupoids.

Proof.

Since
\Phi(D_{1})=D_{2}
, the restriction
\Phi|_{D_{1}}:D_{1}\to D_{2}
is a
C^{*}
-isomorphism. By Gelfand duality, this induces a homeomorphism
h:\widehat{D_{2}}\to\widehat{D_{1}}
. Moreover, if
n\in\mathcal{N}_{A_{1}}(D_{1})
, then
\Phi(n)D_{2}\Phi(n)^{*}=\Phi(nD_{1}n^{*})\subseteq D_{2}
, and similarly
\Phi(n)^{*}D_{2}\Phi(n)\subseteq D_{2}
. Hence
\Phi(n)\in\mathcal{N}_{A_{2}}(D_{2})
. Define
\mathcal{W}(\Phi)([n,x])=[\Phi(n),h^{-1}(x)]
. We verify that this is well defined. Suppose that
[n,x]=[m,x]
. By Lemma
4.1
, there exists
d\in D_{1}
such that

d(x)\neq 0
and
nd=md
. Applying
\Phi
, we obtain
\Phi(n)\Phi(d)=\Phi(m)\Phi(d)
. Since
\Phi(d)(h^{-1}(x))=d(x)\neq 0
, Lemma
4.1
implies that
[\Phi(n),h^{-1}(x)]=[\Phi(m),h^{-1}(x)]
. Thus
\mathcal{W}(\Phi)
is well defined.

It is straightforward to verify that
\mathcal{W}(\Phi)
preserves range, source, multiplication and inversion, and is continuous with continuous inverse induced by
\Phi^{-1}
. Therefore
\mathcal{W}(\Phi):\mathcal{G}_{(A_{1},D_{1})}\to\mathcal{G}_{(A_{2},D_{2})}
is an isomorphism of étale groupoids.
∎

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
(A_{1},D_{1})
and
(A_{2},D_{2})
be
C^{*}
-diagonal pairs.
If their Weyl groupoids are not isomorphic, then the diagonal pairs are
not isomorphic.

Proof.

Assume that
(A_{1},D_{1})\cong(A_{2},D_{2})
as
C^{*}
-diagonal pairs. Then there exists an isomorphism
\Phi:(A_{1},D_{1})\to(A_{2},D_{2})
. By Proposition
7.2
,

\Phi
induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{W}(\Phi):\mathcal{G}_{(A_{1},D_{1})}\to\mathcal{G}_{(A_{2},D_{2})}
. Hence the Weyl groupoids are isomorphic. Therefore, if the Weyl groupoids are not isomorphic, the diagonal pairs cannot be isomorphic.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

The converse of Corollary
7.3
is false in general: non-isomorphic Cartan pairs can have isomorphic Weyl groupoids (e.g., different twists over the same groupoid). However, for untwisted ample principal groupoids, the Weyl functor is faithful, as shown below.

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
(A_{1},D_{1})
and
(A_{2},D_{2})
be untwisted ample C*-diagonal pairs with principal Weyl groupoids
\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}
and
\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}}
. If
\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}\cong\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}}
as topological groupoids, then
(A_{1},D_{1})\cong(A_{2},D_{2})
as C*-diagonal pairs.

Proof.

Let
\phi:\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}\to\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}}
be an isomorphism of topological groupoids.
By Kumjian’s construction
[
12
, Theorem 2.9]
, an (untwisted) principal groupoid
\mathcal{G}
gives rise to a C*-algebra
C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G})
with a diagonal subalgebra
C(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})
. Moreover, the isomorphism
\phi
induces an isomorphism
\Phi:C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}})\to C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}})
defined on the dense subalgebra
C_{c}(\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}})
by
\Phi(f)(\gamma)=f(\phi^{-1}(\gamma))
. This map is an isometric *-isomorphism because
\phi
is a homeomorphism preserving the Haar systems. It satisfies
\Phi(C(\mathcal{G}_{A_{1}}^{(0)}))=C(\mathcal{G}_{A_{2}}^{(0)})
since
\phi
restricts to a homeomorphism of the unit spaces.

Since
(A_{i},D_{i})
are untwisted ample C*-diagonal pairs, Kumjian’s reconstruction theorem
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
yields isomorphisms
\psi_{i}:A_{i}\to C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{A_{i}})
with
\psi_{i}(D_{i})=C(\mathcal{G}_{A_{i}}^{(0)})
. Note that for untwisted pairs, the twist is trivial and the C*-algebra is the reduced groupoid C*-algebra. Composing, we obtain an isomorphism

\psi_{2}^{-1}\circ\Phi\circ\psi_{1}:A_{1}\to A_{2}
that sends
D_{1}
onto
D_{2}
and intertwines the conditional expectations (because
\Phi
preserves the restriction map to the unit space). Hence
(A_{1},D_{1})\cong(A_{2},D_{2})
as C*-diagonal pairs.
∎

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{K},\rho,h):\mathcal{G}_{1}\to\mathcal{G}_{2}
be an isomorphism in
\mathsf{Grpd}_{\mathrm{part}}
. Then
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{G}_{1}
and
\rho:\mathcal{G}_{1}\to\mathcal{G}_{2}
is an isomorphism of topological groupoids.

Proof.

Since
(\mathcal{K},\rho,h)
is an isomorphism, there exists an inverse morphism
(\mathcal{L},\sigma,k):\mathcal{G}_{2}\to\mathcal{G}_{1}
such that

(\mathcal{L},\sigma,k)\circ(\mathcal{K},\rho,h)=(\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}},\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}^{(0)}})

and

(\mathcal{K},\rho,h)\circ(\mathcal{L},\sigma,k)=(\mathcal{G}_{2},\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}},\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}^{(0)}}).

Recall the composition law in
\mathsf{Grpd}_{\mathrm{part}}
: for morphisms
(\mathcal{K}_{1},\rho_{1},h_{1}):\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
and
(\mathcal{K}_{2},\rho_{2},h_{2}):\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{F}
, their composition is
(\mathcal{K},\rho_{2}\circ\rho_{1}|_{\mathcal{K}},h_{2}\circ h_{1})
where

\mathcal{K}=\rho_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{K}_{2}\cap\rho_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{1}))\subseteq\mathcal{H}.

Applying this to the composition
(\mathcal{L},\sigma,k)\circ(\mathcal{K},\rho,h)
, we obtain:

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=\rho^{-1}(\mathcal{L}\cap\rho(\mathcal{K}))\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{1},

and the composition equals
(\mathcal{K}^{\prime},\sigma\circ\rho|_{\mathcal{K}^{\prime}},k\circ h)
.

By the isomorphism condition, this must equal
(\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}},\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}^{(0)}})
. Hence:

1.
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=\mathcal{G}_{1}
;
2.
\sigma\circ\rho|_{\mathcal{K}^{\prime}}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{K}^{\prime}}
;
3.
k\circ h=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}^{(0)}}
.

Now observe that
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=\rho^{-1}(\mathcal{L}\cap\rho(\mathcal{K}))\subseteq\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{1}
. Since
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=\mathcal{G}_{1}
, we obtain

\mathcal{G}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{1},

hence
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{G}_{1}
. With
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{G}_{1}
, condition (2) becomes
\sigma\circ\rho=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}}
.

Similarly, composing in the reverse order gives
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{G}_{2}
and
\rho\circ\sigma=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}
, together with
h\circ k=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}_{2}^{(0)}}
.

Thus
\rho:\mathcal{G}_{1}\to\mathcal{G}_{2}
is bijective, with inverse
\sigma
. By definition of morphisms in
\mathsf{Grpd}_{\mathrm{part}}
,
\rho
is continuous and open onto its image. Since
\rho
is continuous, bijective, and open onto its image, it follows that
\rho
is a homeomorphism. Hence
\rho
is an isomorphism of topological groupoids.
∎

Kopsacheilis and Winter
[
11
]
constructed,
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, a diagonal pair

(D^{(n)}\subset M_{2^{\infty}})

inside the CAR algebra
M_{2^{\infty}}
.

Theorem 7.7

(Exotic diagonals are distinguished by diagonal dimension)
.

For every
n,m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, the following hold:

(i)

\dim_{\mathrm{diag}}(D^{(n)}\subset M_{2^{\infty}})=n
;

(ii)

if
n\neq m
, then
(D^{(n)}\subset M_{2^{\infty}})\not\cong(D^{(m)}\subset M_{2^{\infty}})
as
C^{*}
-diagonal pairs;

(iii)

if
n\neq m
, then
\mathcal{W}(D^{(n)}\subset M_{2^{\infty}})\not\cong\mathcal{W}(D^{(m)}\subset M_{2^{\infty}})
as Weyl groupoids.

Proof.

Part (i) is the main construction theorem of Kopsacheilis–Winter
[
11
, Theorem 3.4]
.

(ii) Diagonal dimension is an invariant of diagonal pairs under isomorphism: if
\Phi
is an isomorphism, then
\dim_{\mathrm{diag}}(D_{1}\subset A_{1})=\dim_{\mathrm{diag}}(D_{2}\subset A_{2})
because approximations can be transferred via
\Phi
and
\Phi^{-1}
. Hence
n=\dim_{\mathrm{diag}}(D^{(n)})=\dim_{\mathrm{diag}}(D^{(m)})=m
, so
n=m
. Thus distinct
n,m
yield non-isomorphic diagonal pairs.

(iii) Suppose the Weyl groupoids were isomorphic. Since the Weyl groupoids are principal (as shown in
[
11
]
), Theorem
7.5
(the reflection property for untwisted principal pairs) would imply the diagonal pairs are isomorphic, contradicting part (ii). Hence the Weyl groupoids are pairwise non-isomorphic.
∎

8.
Examples

We conclude by recording several examples illustrating the scope of the preceding constructions.

Example 8.1
.

Let
E=(E^{0},E^{1},r,s)
be a row-finite directed graph satisfying Condition (L). Then the graph groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{E}
is ample and topologically principal, and hence the canonical inclusion
(D_{E}\subset C^{*}(E))
is an untwisted ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pair
[
12
,
15
]
. Recall that the diagonal algebra is given by
D_{E}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{s_{\mu}s_{\mu}^{*}:\mu\in E^{*}\}
, where
E^{*}
denotes the set of finite paths in
E
. Under the groupoid identification
C^{*}(E)\cong C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{E})
, the diagonal corresponds to the commutative algebra
D_{E}\cong C(\mathcal{G}_{E}^{(0)})
.

Now let
H\subseteq E^{0}
be a saturated hereditary subset. By the standard ideal theory of graph
C^{*}
-algebras
[
4
,
14
]
, there is a gauge-invariant ideal
I_{H}\triangleleft C^{*}(E)
generated by the vertex projections
\{p_{v}:v\in H\}
. Explicitly,

I_{H}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{s_{\mu}s_{\nu}^{*}:r(\mu)=r(\nu)\in H\}.

The quotient graph
E\setminus H
is obtained by removing the vertices in
H
together with all incident edges. The quotient algebra satisfies
C^{*}(E)/I_{H}\cong C^{*}(E\setminus H)
, and the quotient diagonal is naturally identified with the canonical diagonal of the quotient graph
D_{E}/(D_{E}\cap I_{H})\cong D_{E\setminus H}
. From the groupoid viewpoint, saturated hereditary subsets correspond to invariant open subsets of the boundary-path unit space
\mathcal{G}_{E}^{(0)}
. Hence the quotient graph algebra corresponds to a reduction of the graph groupoid:

C^{*}(E\setminus H)\cong C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{E}|_{Y}),

for a suitable invariant subset
Y\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{E}^{(0)}
. Since graph groupoids are naturally untwisted ample étale groupoids, their reductions remain untwisted and ample. Moreover, Condition (L) passes to the quotient graph, so the reduced groupoid remains topologically principal. Consequently,

(D_{E\setminus H}\subset C^{*}(E\setminus H))

is again an untwisted ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pair. Thus quotients of graph Cartan pairs by gauge-invariant ideals associated to saturated hereditary subsets remain untwisted ample diagonal pairs.

Example 8.2
.

Let
X
be a compact totally disconnected Hausdorff space, and let
\alpha:\mathbb{Z}\curvearrowright X
be a free minimal action. Consider the crossed product
A=C(X)\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
with its canonical diagonal
D=C(X)
. The transformation groupoid associated to the action is
\mathcal{G}=X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
, whose elements are pairs
(x,n)\in X\times\mathbb{Z}
with

s(x,n)=x,\qquad r(x,n)=\alpha^{n}(x).

Since
X
is totally disconnected,
\mathcal{G}
is an ample Hausdorff étale groupoid. Moreover, freeness of the action implies that
\mathcal{G}
is principal: indeed, if
(x,n)\in\mathcal{G}^{x}_{x}
, then
\alpha^{n}(x)=x
, and freeness forces
n=0
. Hence every isotropy group is trivial. By Kumjian’s reconstruction theorem
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
,

(C(X)\subset C(X)\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z})

is therefore an untwisted ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pair. Now let
U\subseteq X
be an open invariant subset. Then
I:=C_{0}(U)\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
is an ideal of
A
. By Lemma
4.2
,
A/I\cong C_{r}^{*}(\mathcal{G}|_{X\setminus U})
, and the quotient diagonal is naturally identified with
D/(D\cap I)\cong C(X\setminus U)
. Furthermore, by Lemma
4.5
, the Weyl groupoid of the quotient pair
(D/(D\cap I)\subset A/I)
is naturally isomorphic to the reduction
\mathcal{G}|_{X\setminus U}
. Since reductions of principal ample groupoids remain principal and ample, the reduced groupoid
\mathcal{G}|_{X\setminus U}
is again an ample principal étale groupoid. Consequently,
(D/(D\cap I)\subset A/I)
is again an untwisted ample
C^{*}
-diagonal pair. Thus quotients of crossed-product Cartan pairs by invariant open subsets preserve the untwisted diagonal structure.

Example 8.3
.

Sibbel and Winter
[
17
]
recently constructed a C*-diagonal
D^{\otimes\infty}\subset\mathcal{O}_{2}
whose spectrum is homeomorphic to the Cantor space. Their construction proceeds via crossed products by minimal homeomorphisms on Cantor spaces and infinite tensor products, ultimately relying on Kirchberg’s
\mathcal{O}_{2}
-absorption theorem. This provides an interesting test case for the functorial techniques developed in Section
5
: any
*
-homomorphism from another C*-diagonal pair into
\mathcal{O}_{2}
that restricts to an isomorphism on diagonals would induce a groupoid morphism as in Theorem
5.8
and functorially in Theorem
5.12
. Moreover, the methods of Theorem
4.6
could potentially be applied to analyze quotients of this diagonal.

Example 8.4
.

Let
(D\subset A)
be an ample
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pair. For any UHF algebra
M_{\mathfrak{s}}
with canonical diagonal
D_{\mathfrak{s}}
, Proposition
6.3
guarantees that
(D\otimes D_{\mathfrak{s}}\subset A\otimes M_{\mathfrak{s}})
is again an ample
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-diagonal pair. This construction is used in
[
11
]
and
[
7
]
to produce exotic diagonals in the CAR algebra and Cuntz algebras, respectively. By Corollary
6.4
, the diagonal dimension satisfies
\dim_{\mathrm{diag}}(D\otimes D_{\mathfrak{s}}\subset A\otimes M_{\mathfrak{s}})\leq\dim_{\mathrm{diag}}(D\subset A)
because
\dim_{\mathrm{diag}}(D_{\mathfrak{s}}\subset M_{\mathfrak{s}})=0
.

Example 8.5
.

Kopsacheilis and Winter
[
11
]
constructed, for each
n\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,\infty\}
, a C*-diagonal
(D^{(n)}\subset M_{2^{\infty}})
with Cantor spectrum and diagonal dimension exactly
n
.
In particular, they exhibited a non-AF diagonal in
M_{2^{\infty}}
.
Since
M_{2^{\infty}}
is nuclear and amenable, the Weyl groupoid of their diagonal is amenable (by Renault’s theorem on amenable Cartan subalgebras).
Thus, while one might have hoped that AF diagonals are characterized by amenability of the Weyl groupoid, these examples show that amenable Weyl groupoids can arise from non-AF diagonals.
Hence amenability does **not** imply AF.
The family
\{(D^{(n)}\subset M_{2^{\infty}})\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
further demonstrates that diagonal dimension can distinguish non-conjugate diagonals.

9.
Further questions

We conclude with several questions suggested by the preceding results.

Question 9.1
.

Characterise exactly which inverse semigroup homomorphisms between normaliser semigroups arise from a morphism
\Phi
with
\Phi|_{D}
an isomorphism.

Question 9.2
.

Given a continuous open groupoid homomorphism
\rho:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}_{A}
where
\mathcal{H}
is an open subgroupoid of an effective ample Weyl groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{B}
, find conditions under which there exists a morphism
\Phi:A\to B
inducing
\rho
.

Question 9.3
.

Give a complete characterisation of expectation
-
compatible ideals in terms of the geometry of the groupoid, allowing for possibly twisted groupoids.

Question 9.4
.

Kopsacheilis and Winter
[
10
]
characterized diagonal comparison for a single ample C*-diagonal pair. Can the functorial map
\rho_{\Phi}
of Theorem
5.8
and the functor
\mathcal{W}
of Theorem
5.12
be used to prove that diagonal comparison is preserved under surjective morphisms satisfying (D), (E), (N)? More precisely, if
\Phi:(A,D)\to(B,C)
is a surjective morphism and
(A,D)
has diagonal comparison, does
(B,C)
also have diagonal comparison?

Question 9.5
.

Does the symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
from Proposition
6.5
interact with the functor
\mathcal{W}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\mathrm{iso}}\to\mathsf{Grpd}_{\mathrm{part}}
to give a monoidal functor? That is, is there a natural isomorphism
\mathcal{W}((A_{1},D_{1})\otimes(A_{2},D_{2}))\cong\mathcal{W}(A_{1},D_{1})\times\mathcal{W}(A_{2},D_{2})
in
\mathsf{Grpd}_{\mathrm{part}}
?
