Establishment and Characterization of the Murine Liver Tumor Cell Line ΔCN60 with Conditional Caspase-8 and IKKγ/NEMO Inactivation and a Hepatocyte-Biliary Progenitor-like Phenotype
Authors: Alejandro Cornejo Müller, Thomas Liehr, Prahlad Balakrishnan, Stefanie Kankel, Eva M. Buhl, Diandra T. Keller, Katharina S. Hardt, Sarah K. Schröder-Lange, Christian Liedtke, Ralf Weiskirchen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights What are the main findings? A new murine hepatoma cell line (ΔCN60) derived from DEN-induced tumors in Casp8f/fNemof/f mice was established, retaining floxed alleles that allow efficient Cre-mediated simultaneous deletion of Caspase-8 and NEMO.Combined Casp8/Nemo deletion reduces proliferation, impairs TNF-α–induced NF-κB p65 nuclear translocation, and promotes a progenitor-like hepatoma phenotype with increased AFP and CD133 expression and reduced albumin levels. What are the implications of the main findings? ΔCN60 provides a genetically defined and karyotypically stable in vitro system to study Caspase-8/NEMO-dependent TNF-α signaling, NF-κB activation, and cell death pathways in hepatocytes.The model bridges conditional mouse genetics with mechanistic cell culture experiments and may reduce reliance on animal studies while enabling investigation of hepatocyte plasticity and inflammatory signaling in liver disease. AbstractCaspase-8 and the NF-κB essential modulator (NEMO, also referred to as IKKγ) play critical roles in controlling TNF-α-induced cell death and survival in hepatocytes. The aim of this study was to generate a hepatocyte-derived cell line in which the Casp8 and Nemo genes can be conditionally inactivated simultaneously to investigate the significance of the corresponding signaling pathways. To this end, we induced hepatocellular carcinoma in Casp8f/fNemof/f mice using diethylnitrosamine and established an immortalized hepatoma cell line from explanted liver tumors, which is subsequently referred to as ΔCN60. ΔCN60 cells still retain floxed Casp8 and Nemo alleles, allowing for efficient Cre-mediated deletion to generate Casp8ΔNemoΔ derivatives. Loss of both Caspase-8 and NEMO inhibits cell proliferation, increases the expression of tumor and progenitor markers (AFP, CD133), reduces albumin expression, and blocks TNF-α-induced NF-κB p65 nuclear translocation. ΔCN60 Casp8ΔNemoΔ cells display altered sensitivity to prolonged TNF-α exposure, suggesting a potential shift in necroptosis-associated signaling pathways, although necroptotic cell death was not directly demonstrated. ΔCN60 serves as a versatile hepatoma model for investigating Caspase-8/NEMO-dependent TNF-α signaling and hepatocyte plasticity and may help prioritize future in vivo experiments by enabling selected mechanistic questions to be addressed first in vitro.

1. Introduction

Immortalized cell lines are essential tools in biomedical research, providing standardized, renewable, and experimentally tractable in vitro systems to study cellular and molecular mechanisms under controlled conditions [1]. They allow high-throughput drug and toxicant screening, detailed analysis of signaling pathways, and stable genetic manipulation that is challenging in primary cells or in vivo models. In liver research, robust hepatocytic cell lines are particularly valuable because primary hepatocytes rapidly lose their differentiated phenotype and metabolic competence in culture. Moreover, their availability is limited by donor material and ethical considerations [1]. Immortalized hepatocytic cell lines offer a reproducible platform for long-term experiments, complementing animal models, and align with the 3Rs principles (replacement, reduction, and refinement) in experimental science [2,3].

Several human and murine hepatocytic cell lines are commonly used in hepatology and toxicology research. Human hepatoma-derived lines such as HepG2 [4], Hep3B [4], and Huh7 [5,6] are widely used for studying hepatic metabolism, lipotoxicity, viral hepatitis, and cancer biology. HepaRG cells [7], which are capable of differentiating into hepatocyte-like cells, are often used as substitutes for primary human hepatocytes in xenobiotic metabolism and cholestasis studies. In mouse models, cell lines such as AML12 (derived from non-tumorigenic hepatocytes of a transgenic mouse) [8] and Hepa1-6 (a murine hepatoma line) [9] are employed to explore species-specific liver physiology, oncogenesis, and the effects of genetic manipulation. Together, these models have provided valuable insights into hepatocyte biology, liver regeneration, and the development of liver diseases such as steatosis, fibrosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).

However, currently available hepatocytic cell lines have important limitations that restrict their translational relevance. Most widely used lines are tumor-derived and have complex, often incompletely defined genetic and chromosomal abnormalities that can alter signaling pathways, stress responses, and metabolic capacity in ways that do not accurately mimic normal hepatocytes [6,10]. Many lines have reduced expression or activity of drug-metabolizing enzymes, transporters, and nuclear receptors, and show altered bile acid and lipid handling, limiting their suitability for predictive toxicology and pharmacokinetic modeling. Long-term culture can lead to genetic drift and clonal selection, resulting in heterogeneity between laboratories and even between passages within the same laboratory [2]. Furthermore, only a subset of existing lines is directly linked to genetically engineered mouse models, which limits the ability to integrate in vitro findings with in vivo studies conducted in defined genetic backgrounds. These constraints highlight the need for well-characterized hepatocytic cell lines from mice that are closely linked to specific genetic configurations and can serve as reliable in vitro counterparts to in vivo models.

Caspase-8 and the NF-κB regulatory subunit NEMO/IKKγ are central nodes in tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) signaling and play a critical role in determining whether hepatocytes undergo apoptosis, necroptosis, or survival and proliferation in response to inflammatory stimuli [11,12]. Disruption of this regulatory network of Caspase-8 and NEMO has previously been associated with activation of receptor-interacting serine/threonine-protein kinase (RIPK1/RIPK3)-dependent inflammatory and cell death pathways in hepatocytes [4,13,14]. The genetic loss of Casp8 in hepatocytes increases sensitivity to TNF-α, stabilizes RIPK1, and accelerates liver regeneration [15,16,17,18]. Conversely, the combined inactivation of Casp8 and Nemo leads to severe liver injury, underscoring the importance of their interaction in controlling cell death and regeneration [18]. To study these pathways in a hepatocyte-specific and genetically manipulable context, free from interference by immune or non-parenchymal cells, a hepatoma cell line carrying conditional alleles of Casp8 and Nemo is particularly appealing.

In this study, we describe the generation of a hepatoma cell line derived from Casp8f/fNemof/f mice, allowing simultaneous Cre-mediated deletion of both genes in vitro. In particular, we describe the morphology and growth characteristics of the ΔCN60 cell line using light microscopy and we analyzed their ultrastructural features with electron microscopy to evaluate hepatocyte-like organelle architecture and differentiation status. The authenticity of the cell line was confirmed through short tandem repeat (STR) profiling, and its chromosomal landscape was characterized using conventional karyotyping and spectral karyotyping (SKY). Additionally, bulk mRNA next-generation sequencing (RNA-seq) was performed to demonstrate the translational potential of this line. Selected mRNA expression findings were evaluated at the protein level through Western blot analysis. By integrating structural, cytogenetic, and genomic data with targeted protein validation, we provide a comprehensive characterization of this cell line and establish it as a genetically defined, hepatocyte-derived model system. This system will be valuable for studying Caspase-8/NEMO-dependent signaling, TNF-α-induced liver injury, and HCC biology, ultimately providing an in vitro system that may complement in vivo models and help prioritize future animal experiments.

2. Materials and Methods

2.1. Cell Line Origin and Establishment

In our study, we used C57BL/6 mice with LoxP-flanked (i.e., “floxed”) alleles of the Casp8 and Nemo genes [16,17]. Casp8f/fNemof/f mice were treated with diethylnitrosamine (DEN) according to a standard protocol, leading to the development of HCC approximately 40 weeks after injection. Primary hepatocytes were isolated from HCC-bearing livers using a well-established method and cultured until reaching confluence over a period of approximately two months. This process resulted in the establishment of a stable hepatoma cell line with a Casp8f/fNemof/f genotype, designated as ΔCN60.

The cell line ΔCN60 was derived from a DEN-induced liver tumor isolated from a male Casp8f/fNemof/f mouse. The cell line was established by serial culture and passaging of adherent tumor-derived cells and was not generated by single-cell cloning or limiting dilution. Thus, ΔCN60 represents a stable tumor-derived adherent cell population rather than a formally single-cell-derived clone. Unless stated otherwise, experiments were performed between passages 5 and 10 after establishment of this cell line, which was assumed to have occurred after approximately 50 passages.

The cells were routinely cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM, #D6171) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, #F7524), 2 mM L-glutamine (#G7513), 1 mM sodium pyruvate (#S8636), and 1× penicillin/streptomycin (#P0781). All reagents were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich (Taufkirchen, Germany).

Mycoplasma contamination in cell culture supernatants was assessed using the Venor®GeM OneStep kit (#11-8050; Minerva Biolabs GmbH, Berlin, Germany) essentially as reported previously [19]. Amplified products were separated on a conventional 2% agarose gel prepared in 1× TAE buffer containing 40 mM Tris, 20 mM acetic acid, and 1 mM EDTA (pH 8.6). Electrophoresis was performed for 50 min at 90 V, and DNA bands were visualized by GelRed Nucleic Acid Stain (#41003, Biotium GmbH, Hamburg, Germany). Gel images were acquired using a standard documentation system from Intas Science Imaging GmbH (Göttingen, Germany).

The animal experiments used to generate the tumor material from which ΔCN60 was established were performed under the animal use approval granted on 6 February 2009 by the Review Board for the Care of Animal Subjects of the district government, LANUV, Recklinghausen, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, under permit number AZ 8.87-50.10.35.08.349. Specifically, Casp8f/fNemof/f mice were treated with DEN under this approval, liver tumors were allowed to develop, and tumor-derived hepatocytes were isolated from HCC-bearing livers for subsequent establishment of the ΔCN60 cell line. No additional mice were used for the present study after establishment of the cell line, and no new DEN-induced tumor experiments, tumor isolations, transplantation experiments, or other animal experiments were performed at a later date. All experiments reported in the present manuscript after the initial cell line establishment were conducted exclusively with the established ΔCN60 in vitro cell line, including adenoviral Cre-mediated gene deletion, proliferation and signaling assays, cytogenetic characterization, transcriptomic profiling, microscopy, RT-qPCR, and Western blot analyses. Therefore, the cited 2009 approval applies only to the original animal work required to generate the tumor-derived cell line, whereas the subsequent characterization described here did not require additional animal ethics approval.

2.2. Adenoviral Cre-Mediated Gene Deletion

For Cre-mediated recombination, ΔCN60 cells were infected with adenoviral vectors encoding Cre recombinase. Where indicated, Ad-CMV-Cre/RSV-eGFP (#FVQ005, Kerafast, Newark, CA, USA), hereafter referred to as adv-Cre/GFP, was used to express Cre recombinase and enhanced green fluorescent protein (eGFP) from the same adenoviral construct. In experiments not requiring GFP visualization, an adenoviral Cre vector without eGFP, hereafter referred to as adv-Cre, was used. Control infections were performed with Ad-CMV-eGFP (#FVQ002, Kerafast), hereafter referred to as adv-GFP, or with Ad-CMV-LacZ, hereafter referred to as adv-LacZ. Cre-mediated recombination resulted in deletion of exons 3–4 of Casp8 and exon 2 of Nemo, as determined by PCR using genomic DNA as a template, leading to premature termination and loss or strong reduction in Caspase-8 and NEMO protein expression at the population level. Deletion efficiency was assessed by genomic PCR and Western blotting; however, these bulk assays do not provide a single-cell-based percentage of successfully recombined cells. For Nemo, a 460 bp fragment represented the floxed allele, while a 260 bp fragment indicated the recombined knockout allele.

2.3. Functional Assays

Cell growth was monitored for up to 72 h in parental Casp8f/fNemof/f ΔCN60 cells and adv-Cre-treated Casp8/Nemo-deficient ΔCN60 cell populations. For growth-curve analyses, three independent biological replicates were analyzed. Stimulation experiments with TNF-α (#410-MT, Novus Biologicals, Centennial, CO, USA) were performed using a concentration of 10 ng/mL for time-course analyses. Activation of NF-κB was assessed through Western blotting of nuclear extracts using antibodies against p65 and TATA-box binding protein (TBP), as well as by p65 immunofluorescence with nuclear (DAPI) counterstaining using standard procedures. Expression levels of Afp, Cd133, and Alb were quantified relative to ileum and normal mouse liver. Cell cycle analysis was performed by comparing mRNA expression of cyclin A2 and cyclin D1 in Casp8f/fNemof/f and Casp8ΔNemoΔ ΔCN60 cell populations, with the latter referring to the adv-Cre-treated bulk population analyzed in the present study, which does not imply single-cell clonal derivation or complete recombination in every cell. The primers used in these assays are listed in Table S1.

2.4. Short Tandem Repeat (STR) Profiling

The ΔCN60 cell line underwent STR profiling using the CellCheck™ Mouse system, which employs a set of 19 consensus markers specifically designed for mouse species as outlined by the Consortium for Mouse Cell Line Authentication. To analyze the STR profile of ΔCN60, we utilized the Cellosaurus STR Similarity Search Tool CLASTR version 1.4.4 (release 41.0) along with the corresponding mouse STR database from Cellosaurus. The analysis parameters included a scoring algorithm based on Tanabe, a focus on non-empty markers, a score threshold of 40%, and a requirement for at least eight markers to be present.

2.5. Preparation of ΔCN60 Metaphase Chromosomes, Karyotyping, and Molecular Cytogenetics

For karyotype analysis, metaphase chromosomes were isolated from ΔCN60 cells following established protocols involving the induction of mitotic arrest using KaryoMAX colcemid solution (#15212012, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Darmstadt, Germany). After hypotonic treatment and fixation in a methanol:acetic acid mixture, multicolor fluorescence in situ hybridization (mFISH) with different probe sets specific to mouse chromosomes was performed. The probe set 21XMouse (MetaSystems, Altlußheim, Germany) was used according to the manufacturer’s instructions; it contains all 21 murine whole chromosome paints as probes, labeled in individual color combinations. To detect intrachromosomal aberrations, 21 chromosome-specific murine multicolor banding (mcb) probe sets were applied as previously described [20]. Twenty metaphases per probe set were thoroughly examined using a fluorescence microscope equipped with a CCD camera, and images were processed through ISIS software (version 5.9.2) to construct the karyotype representation of ΔCN60 cells. The unique features of the blue counterstain 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) were utilized by the same software to create an inverted DAPI-banding pattern.

2.6. Next-Generation Sequencing and Data Analysis

Next-generation sequencing (NGS) of ΔCN60 cells was performed following established laboratory protocols designed for RNA sequencing studies [21]. Cells were cultured until they reached about 80% confluence before being lysed with guanidine thiocyanate for total RNA extraction using the cesium chloride cushion method. The quality and quantity of isolated RNA were assessed using UV spectrophotometry and an Agilent TapeStation system before rRNA depletion. This was followed by reverse transcription and indexing with NEBNext Multiplex Oligos designed for Illumina platforms. The datasets generated and/or analyzed during the current study are available in the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) repository under accession number GSE325078 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE325078, last accessed on 26 June 2026).

2.7. Electron Microscopic Cell Analysis

To investigate the ultrastructural characteristics of ΔCN60 cells, samples were fixed in glutaraldehyde and then post-fixed in osmium tetroxide. Dehydration was carried out through an ethanol series before embedding in epoxy resin for further processing. Ultrathin sections approximately 70–90 nm thick were prepared using an ultramicrotome and mounted on copper grids. They were then contrasted with uranyl acetate and lead citrate solutions for examination by transmission electron microscopy (TEM).

2.8. Reverse Transcription Polymerase Chain Reaction

RNA was isolated using the PureLinkTM RNA Mini Kit (12183025, Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher Scientific) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, including additional DNase digestion (12185010, Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher Scientific) as described previously [22]. Conventional RT-PCR was performed on cDNA synthesized from ΔCN60 cells using a thermal cycling protocol optimized for various target genes, such as Hnf4a and Rn18s among others. This process included an initial denaturation followed by multiple cycles at gene-specific annealing temperatures before final extension steps were taken to ensure complete amplification. All primers used in this analysis are listed in Table S1.

RT-qPCR experiments were performed using three independent biological replicates per cell line or condition and three technical qPCR replicates per biological replicate. Statistical testing was performed on biological replicate values after averaging technical replicates. Transcripts with undetermined Ct values or signals below the detection limit in all replicates of one group were reported as not detected and were not subjected to conventional parametric significance testing based on substituted Ct values.

2.9. Western Blot Analysis

Protein extracts from ΔCN60 cells and primary mouse hepatocytes were analyzed using standardized methodologies to ensure uniform loading across all gel electrophoresis lanes. The proteins were then transferred onto a 0.45 µm nitrocellulose membrane (GE10600001, Amersham, Cytiva, Marlborough, MA, USA) and probed with specific primary antibodies. Chemiluminescent signals were visualized using SuperSignal West Dura Extended Duration Substrate (#34076, Thermo Fisher Scientific) and the iBrightTM FL500 imaging system (Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher Scientific) All antibodies used for Western blot analysis are listed in Table S2.

2.10. Phalloidin Stain

For F-actin visualization, ΔCN60 cells were seeded at 4 × 104 cells per well in four-chamber glass slides suitable for tissue culture (#354114, Corning Inc., Falcon, Corning, NY, USA). After allowing time for attachment, cells were fixed with 3.7% paraformaldehyde and permeabilized for F-actin staining using phalloidin-Alexa Fluor 488 (#A12379 Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher Scientific) and 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI, #D1306, Thermo Fisher Scientific) as a nuclear counterstain before imaging by fluorescence microscopy using a Nikon Eclipse E80i fluorescence microscope (Nikon, Düsseldorf, Germany) equipped with the NIS-elements Vis software (Software version 3.22.01), following procedures described previously [23].

2.11. Statistical Analysis

Data are presented as the mean ± SD unless indicated otherwise. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (version 8.4.2). Normally distributed data were analyzed using Shapiro–Wilk test and comparisons between two groups were done using an unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05. The number of biological and technical replicates is indicated in the respective figure legends.

3. Results

3.1. Establishment of ΔCN60 and Conditional Casp8/Nemo Deletion

The ΔCN60 cell line was established from DEN-induced HCC arising in Casp8f/f Nemof/f mice and maintained in vitro until stable proliferation was achieved (Figure 1A). We confirmed that ΔCN60 cultures are mycoplasma-free, as routine PCR-based screening using the Venor®GeM OneStep kit consistently showed no detectable mycoplasma contamination (Figure S1). Genetically, ΔCN60 retains floxed Casp8 and Nemo alleles, enabling conditional gene deletion by Cre recombinase. Adenoviral delivery of Cre (adv-Cre) efficiently generated detectable Casp8Δ and NemoΔ alleles at the bulk population level, as demonstrated by the appearance of the 260 bp Nemo knockout fragment alongside the 460 bp floxed fragment (Figure 1B). Western blot analysis confirmed a reduction in NEMO protein levels after infection (Figure 1C), demonstrating that this system provides a suitable cellular platform to analyze the biological functions of the Caspase-8/NEMO network and its downstream signaling. Efficient adenoviral transduction of ΔCN60 cells was further verified by fluorescence microscopy following infection with adv-GFP or adv-Cre/GFP at different multiplicities of infection, demonstrating robust GFP expression after 24 and 48 h (Figure 1D). In addition, Western blot analysis confirmed a marked reduction in Caspase-8 protein levels in adv-Cre/GFP-infected cells compared with adv-GFP controls, validating efficient Cre-mediated deletion of the floxed Casp8 alleles at the protein level (Figure 1E).

At the transcriptional level, parental ΔCN60 cells expressed Casp8 and Nemo/Ikbkγ transcripts at the TPM values shown in Table 1. These values indicate detectable expression of both genes in the ΔCN60 bulk transcriptome, without implying a universal threshold-based classification as low, moderate, or high expression (Table 1).

3.2. Proliferation, Cell Cycle Control, and Tumor Differentiation Marker Expression

Adv-Cre-treated Casp8/Nemo-deficient ΔCN60 cell populations displayed significantly reduced proliferation compared with their Casp8f/fNemof/f counterparts when monitored over 72 h (Figure 2A). Cell-cycle analysis after adv-Cre infection showed impaired progression in Casp8/Nemo-deleted cells, indicating a decreased fraction of cells entering the S-phase. At the transcriptional level, the induction of cyclin D1 (Ccnd1) and cyclin A2 (Ccna2) was lower in Casp8ΔNemoΔ hepatoma cells relative to floxed controls (Figure 2B). Additionally, parental ΔCN60 cells expressed the tumor-associated markers alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) and CD133, while showing lower albumin levels compared to normal liver, indicating a hepatoma phenotype. Upon deletion of Casp8 and Nemo, Afp and Cd133 mRNA levels increased further, while Alb expression decreased, suggesting additional dedifferentiation and acquisition of progenitor-like features (Figure 2C). Therefore, ΔCN60 not only models HCC biology, but also indicates that Casp8/Nemo loss may promote dedifferentiation in hepatoma cells. In addition, the altered signaling environment created by combined Casp8/Nemo deletion may influence TNF-α-dependent cell death pathways that have previously been linked to RIPK1/RIPK3 signaling [3,13,14,24]. However, the present study did not directly investigate necroptotic signaling or necroptotic cell death mechanisms in ΔCN60 cells.

3.3. NF-κB p65 Nuclear Translocation

Stimulation with TNF-α (10 ng/mL) in Casp8f/fNemof/f ΔCN60 cells led to a rapid nuclear accumulation of NF-κB p65, whereas Casp8ΔNemoΔ cells exhibited significantly reduced TNF-α-induced nuclear p65, both biochemically (Figure 3A) and by microscopy (Figure 3B). Quantification of p65-positive nuclei confirmed a notable decrease in NF-κB activation in adv-Cre-infected cells compared with adv-LacZ-infected cells (Figure 3C). While Casp8/Nemo deletion has previously been linked to RIPK1/RIPK3-dependent cell death signaling in hepatocytes, the present study primarily demonstrates altered TNF-α responsiveness in ΔCN60 cells rather than directly characterizing RIPK1/RIPK3 activation.

3.4. Morphological, Ultrastructural, and Cytoskeletal Characterization of ΔCN60 Cells

Under phase-contrast light microscopy, ΔCN60 cells formed a tightly adherent monolayer of predominantly polygonal cells with an epithelial-like morphology and moderate size heterogeneity, consistent with their hepatocytic origin from DEN-induced HCC (Figure 4A). Individual cells exhibited abundant cytoplasm and centrally to eccentrically located round nuclei with one or more prominent nucleoli, features typical of hepatocyte-derived tumor cells. At higher confluence, cells established close cell–cell contacts and organized into compact clusters and sheet-like arrangements, while occasionally less differentiated, spindle-shaped cells were observed at the periphery of colonies, reflecting the tumor-derived and partially progenitor-like nature of the line. Fluorescence staining with Alexa Fluor-conjugated phalloidin further revealed a well-developed F-actin cytoskeleton with intense cortical actin along cell borders and a fine submembranous network, underscoring the epithelial organization of ΔCN60 and the integrity of intercellular contacts (Figure 4B). Nuclear counterstaining with DAPI confirmed a largely uniform nuclear size and chromatin distribution, without evidence of contaminating non-parenchymal cell populations, in agreement with the hepatocyte-derived and genetically defined origin of the cell line.

TEM provided further insight into the ultrastructural organization of ΔCN60 cells and confirmed their hepatocytic identity. Ultrastructural examination revealed a high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio with round to oval nuclei containing dispersed euchromatin and distinct nucleoli, indicative of active transcriptional activity typical of tumor cells (Figure 4C). The cytoplasm contained numerous mitochondria and well-developed rough endoplasmic reticulum cisternae, consistent with the preserved protein-synthetic and metabolic functions of hepatocyte-derived cells. The overall organelle organization and abundance suggested a partially dedifferentiated hepatoma phenotype rather than fully mature hepatocytes. In higher-magnification TEM images, intercellular clefts resembling canaliculus-like lumina were observed between adjacent cell membranes, and short microvillus-like membrane protrusions were visible within these structures (Figure S2). Electron-dense membrane appositions at lateral cell borders were consistent with junction-like cell–cell contacts and supported the epithelial organization observed by light microscopy, although definitive assignment to specific tight or adherens junction subtypes would require additional junction-marker analyses. Together, the light microscopic, ultrastructural, and cytoskeletal analyses confirm that ΔCN60 represents a hepatocyte-derived hepatoma line with epithelial organization, preserved hepatocyte-like organelle architecture, and a well-developed cortical actin network, reflecting the partial dedifferentiation and plasticity inferred from its transcriptional and functional characteristics.

3.5. Genetic Characterization of ΔCN60 Cells

STR profiling was conducted to assess the genetic identity of ΔCN60. The STR analysis revealed distinct profiles that differentiate this cell line from other murine hepatocytic lines, such as AML12 and Hepa1-6, used in our laboratory (Table 2).

The karyotypic analysis of the ΔCN60 cell line based on inverted DAPI-banding revealed an unaltered chromosomal structure, indicating stability in its genetic makeup (Figure 5A). Representative metaphase spreads demonstrated a normal diploid complement of 40 murine chromosomes without obvious structural abnormalities or aneuploidy. To further investigate chromosomal integrity, we utilized multicolor fluorescence in situ hybridization (mFISH) analysis, which allowed visualization of the distinct spectral color signature of each chromosome (Figure 5B). This analysis confirmed the findings obtained by inverted DAPI-banding and demonstrated the absence of numerical or interchromosomal rearrangements in ΔCN60 cells. The stability of the karyotype suggests that the cellular behavior observed in experimental settings is likely to remain consistent and reproducible over time. Finally, high-resolution mcb analysis was performed to further refine the cytogenetic characterization (Figure 5C). This approach allows the detection of intra- and interchromosomal rearrangements larger than approximately 5 Mb. Consequently, no molecular-cytogenetically detectable chromosomal aberrations, including inversions, insertions, translocations, or large copy-number alterations, were identified in the ΔCN60 cell line.

3.6. Transcriptomic Profiling Reveals a Core Hepatocytic Program with Partial Loss of Mature Metabolic Functions

To further confirm the hepatocytic origin of ΔCN60 cells, we conducted bulk mRNA NGS (Table S3) and examined curated marker panels for hepatocytes (Table S4), hepatic stellate cells (Table S5), cholangiocytes (Table S6), and Kupffer cells (Table S7). ΔCN60 cells showed strong expression of numerous hepatocyte-enriched transcription factors, such as Hnf4a, Foxa1-3, Hhex, and Prox1, along with classic hepatocyte products like Alb, Apob, Cp, Ambp, Gc, and genes related to the urea cycle (Asl, Ass1, Glul). Additionally, essential enzymes involved in mitochondrial β-oxidation and cholesterol/lipid biosynthesis, including Acadm, Acat2, Acly, Hmgcs1, Msmo1, and Scd, were expressed at high TPM values, indicating that ΔCN60 maintains a significant hepatocyte-like metabolic program. However, several characteristics of fully differentiated adult hepatocytes exhibited either low or absent expression, such as the gluconeogenic genes G6pc and Pck1, the glycolytic regulator Gck, the bile acid synthesis enzyme Cyp7a1, and the bile acid transporter Slc10a1. There was also minimal expression of the glucose transporter Slc2a2 (Glut2), suggesting a partial metabolic dedifferentiation commonly seen in hepatoma cell lines. ΔCN60 also expressed fetal/tumor-associated hepatocyte markers like Afp and Gpc3, consistent with previous findings of AFP and CD133 expression at the mRNA and protein levels, further confirming its classification as a hepatoma line with progenitor-like characteristics.

3.7. Minimal Hepatic Stellate and Kupffer Cell Signatures but Pronounced Biliary/Progenitor Marker Expression

Analysis of hepatic stellate cell markers showed that canonical myofibroblastic genes indicative of hepatic stellate cells, such as Acta2, Des, Gfap, Tagln, Pnpla3, Fap, and Hgf were not detected in ΔCN60 cells. Fibrillar collagens (Col1a1, Col1a2, Col3a1) and proteoglycans (Bgn, Dcn) were present but at low TPM values compared with Gapdh, indicating some extracellular matrix production. In contrast, several matricellular and secreted factors typically associated with stellate cells and tumor stroma, including Cyr61/Ccn1, Sparc, Ctgf/Ccn2, Igfbp3, and particularly Igfbp7, showed moderate to very high transcript levels, suggesting that ΔCN60 hepatoma cells themselves produce components that, in vivo, would partly derive from non-parenchymal cells.

The cholangiocyte marker panel revealed a surprisingly strong biliary/progenitor gene signature in ΔCN60 cells. ΔCN60 showed very high expression of the ductular keratin Krt19 and the secreted phosphoprotein Spp1 (osteopontin), together with substantial expression of the transcription factors Sox9, Hnf1b, and Onecut2 that are characteristic of biliary epithelial cells and liver progenitors. Additional cholangiocyte-associated genes were also clearly expressed, including Aqp1, Itgb4, Slc4a2, Lcn2, Elf3, Muc5b, and the trefoil factors Tff1 and Tff2, further supporting a biliary/progenitor-like transcriptional component. By contrast, some markers of mature large bile ducts such as Agr2 and Kcnn2 were not detected, and Krt7, Cftr, and Cldn4 were present only at low levels, indicating a partial rather than fully cholangiocytic differentiation state. Taken together with the hepatocyte marker profile, these data support a hybrid hepatocyte-cholangiocyte or liver-progenitor-like phenotype of ΔCN60 cells.

Similarly, Kupffer cell-specific genes were largely absent from the ΔCN60 transcriptome. Key macrophage markers, including Adgre1 (F4/80), Adgre4, C1qa, C1qb, C1qc, Cd163, Clec4f, Marco, Msr1, Tnf, and Il1b, were not expressed, which argues against contamination with liver-resident macrophages or a macrophage-like identity. Instead, ΔCN60 expressed a subset of innate immune and iron-handling genes that are known to be shared between hepatocytes and macrophages, such as Cd14, Tlr4, Slc40a1, and Ftl, consistent with the established role of hepatocytes in sensing inflammatory stimuli and regulating iron metabolism rather than indicating a Kupffer cell origin. Additional stress- and signaling-related genes including Cd38, Mmp13, Ppard, Stard5, and Hfe were also expressed, highlighting that ΔCN60 is an inflammation-responsive cell line. Overall, the NGS data confirm the hepatocytic origin of ΔCN60, reveal a loss of several functions typical of fully differentiated hepatocytes, and uncover pronounced cholangiocytic/progenitor and inflammatory signatures that likely contribute to the specific behavior of this Casp8/Nemo-conditional hepatoma model.

To validate our NGS data, we next performed quantitative real-time RT-qPCR and Western blot analysis for representative markers. In accordance with the RNA-seq data, RT-qPCR analysis demonstrated that ΔCN60 cells maintain robust expression of core hepatocytic markers such as Alb, Hnf4a, Foxa2, Krt18, Apob, and Apoe, while also expressing high levels of fetal/progenitor and iron-handling genes, including Afp, Sox9, Fth1, and Ftl1. Several transcripts differed significantly from AML12 hepatocytes, a non-tumorigenic murine hepatocyte cell line commonly used as a reference model for differentiated hepatocytes, further supporting the idea of a hepatocyte-derived yet progenitor-biased tumor phenotype (Figure 6A). Western blot analysis confirmed the presence of HNF4α and β-catenin in ΔCN60 cells. It also showed expression of the cell cycle regulator cyclin D1, indicating a proliferative hepatoma state. Detection of vimentin and ferritin light chain (FTL1) further supported partial epithelial plasticity and altered iron metabolism characteristic of transformed, dedifferentiated liver cells (Figure 6B). The consistent mRNA and protein expression profiles validate the transcriptomic characterization of ΔCN60, confirming its mixed mature-fetal hepatocytic and biliary/progenitor signature. This underscores its value as a genetically defined hepatoma model that combines preserved hepatocyte identity with cancer-associated dedifferentiation.

4. Discussion

In this study, we established ΔCN60, a DEN-induced murine hepatoma cell line derived from Casp8f/f/Nemof/f mice that preserves floxed alleles of both genes and enables efficient Cre-mediated generation of Casp8ΔNemoΔ derivatives in an isogenic hepatocyte-derived background. This genetic configuration allows ΔCN60 to bridge observations from Casp8/Nemo conditional mouse models with mechanistically tractable in vitro hepatocyte systems. Functionally, combined Casp8 and Nemo deletion in ΔCN60 reproduced key features of hepatocyte responses observed in Casp8- and Nemo-deficient mouse livers [14,17,18,27]. Casp8ΔNemoΔ ΔCN60 cells showed reduced proliferation, impaired cell-cycle progression, and decreased expression of cyclin A2 (Ccna2) and cyclin D1 (Ccnd1), supporting non-apoptotic roles of Caspase-8 and NEMO in hepatocyte proliferation, consistent with previous links between Caspase-8, RIPK1, NF-κB, and JNK during liver regeneration [18,27,28]. In parallel, loss of Casp8 and/or Nemo increased AFP and CD133 expression while reducing albumin levels, indicating enhanced dedifferentiation and acquisition of progenitor-like features.

ΔCN60 also recapitulates central aspects of TNF-α signaling, including NF-κB activation and TNF-induced cell death responses [29,30]. In Casp8f/fNemof/f ΔCN60 cells, TNF-α induced nuclear accumulation of NF-κB p65, whereas Casp8ΔNemoΔ cells showed markedly impaired p65 nuclear translocation, confirming the importance of NEMO for canonical TNF-α-induced NF-κB activation in this model. The increased sensitivity of Casp8/Nemo-deficient ΔCN60 cells to prolonged TNF-α exposure is consistent with reports linking loss of Caspase 8 or NEMO to RIPK1/RIPK3-dependent cell death signaling in hepatocytes [3,31,32]. However, because RIPK1/RIPK3 activation and downstream necroptotic effectors were not directly analyzed, these findings indicate potential susceptibility to necroptosis-associated signaling rather than definitive proof of necroptotic cell death. Future studies should therefore assess TNF-α-induced necroptosis using dedicated assays, including Annexin V/propidium iodide-based cell-death analyses, RIPK1/RIPK3/MLKL activation, and genetic or pharmacological inhibition of necroptotic signaling components.

Morphological, ultrastructural, and cytoskeletal analyses confirmed the hepatocytic origin and epithelial organization of ΔCN60 while revealing features of a partially dedifferentiated hepatoma. ΔCN60 cells formed adherent polygonal epithelial-like monolayers with occasional spindle-shaped cells at colony margins, consistent with phenotypic heterogeneity. TEM showed euchromatic nuclei, abundant mitochondria, well-developed rough endoplasmic reticulum, canaliculus-like lumina with microvilli, and junction-like contacts, indicating preserved hepatocyte-like organelle architecture and partial polarization. F-actin staining further demonstrated a prominent cortical actin network supporting epithelial cell–cell adhesion.

A major strength of ΔCN60 is its favorable genetic profile. Inverted DAPI banding, mFISH, and mcb analyses revealed an apparently unaltered diploid complement of 40 murine chromosomes without detectable structural rearrangements, gains, losses, or aneuploidy, contrasting with the chromosomal complexity frequently reported for cancer cell lines [33,34]. STR profiling further demonstrated a distinct genetic signature separating ΔCN60 from AML12 [21] and Hepa1-6 [25], excluding cross-contamination with these commonly used murine liver cell lines. Together, the diploid karyotype in the analyzed passages and unique STR profile support the reliability and reproducibility of the model, although stability should not be overgeneralized beyond the examined culture conditions and passages.

Bulk RNA-seq and RT-qPCR showed that ΔCN60 retains a core hepatocyte identity while displaying partial metabolic dedifferentiation and a strong biliary/progenitor signature. ΔCN60 expressed hepatocyte-associated regulators and products, including Hnf4a, Foxa1-3, Hhex, Prox1, Alb, Apob, Cp, Ambp, and Gc, as well as genes involved in lipid, amino acid, and urea metabolism, such as Acadm, Acat2, Acly, Hmgcs1, Msmo1, Glul, Ass1, and Asl. These data, supported by Western blot validation of markers including Alb, Hnf4a, Foxa2, Krt18, Apob/Apoe, Fabp1, and β-catenin, confirm a hepatocyte-derived protein profile. At the same time, low or absent expression of adult hepatocyte markers, such as G6pc, Pck1, Gck, Cyp7a1, Slc10a1, and Slc2a2, indicates loss of mature gluconeogenic and bile acid-related functions, consistent with a transformed hepatoma phenotype. Expression of Afp, Gpc3, Fth1, and Ftl1 further supports a fetal/progenitor-like and iron-responsive state.

Beyond its hepatocytic core, ΔCN60 showed high expression of biliary/progenitor-associated genes, including Krt19, Spp1, Sox9, Hnf1b, Onecut2, Aqp1, Itgb4, Slc4a2, Elf3, Muc5b, Tff1, and Tff2, whereas markers of mature large bile ducts, such as Agr2 and Kcnn2 were absent, and Krt7 and Cldn4 remained low. This indicates a partial hybrid hepatocyte-cholangiocyte or liver-progenitor-like state rather than full cholangiocytic differentiation. In addition, ΔCN60 expressed matricellular and stromal factors, such as Ccn1/Cyr61, Sparc, Ctgf/Ccn2, Igfbp3, and Igfbp7, which are unusual for fully differentiated hepatocytes but relevant to liver cancer and apoptosis [32,35,36,37,38]. The absence of canonical myofibroblastic stellate cell markers (Acta2, Des, Gfap, Tagln, Pnpla3, Fap, Hgf) [39,40] and Kupffer cell-specific genes (Adgre1, Clec4f, Marco, Cd163, C1qa-c) [41,42] argues against contamination by hepatic stellate cells or resident macrophages. Instead, expression of shared inflammatory and iron-regulatory genes, such as Cd14, Tlr4, Slc40a1, Ftl, Mmp13, Ppard, and Hfe is consistent with hepatocyte-intrinsic inflammatory and iron-sensing functions. Overall, ΔCN60 represents a hepatocyte-derived, progenitor-biased tumor line combining hepatocytic, biliary, stromal-like, and inflammatory traits, reminiscent of ductular reaction or combined HCC-biliary phenotypes observed in chronic liver injury [43].

Compared with commonly used murine hepatocytic lines such as AML12 [8,21] and Hepa1-6 [9] and human hepatoma lines including HepG2 [4], Hep3B [4], Huh7 [5,6], and HepaRG [7], ΔCN60 offers the advantage of direct derivation from a defined conditional Casp8f/fNemof/f mouse model. This facilitates integration of in vitro experiments with in vivo data from the same genetic background. Its authenticated STR profile, diploid karyotype in the passages analyzed, TNF-α-responsive NF-κB axis, conditional Caspase-8/NEMO/RIPK1/RIPK3 signaling context, and intrinsic biliary/progenitor signature distinguish it from many long-established hepatic tumor lines with complex or incompletely defined chromosomal alterations [6,21]. Although primary hepatocytes remain the gold standard for fully differentiated liver functions [44], their limited lifespan, rapid dedifferentiation, and resistance to stable genetic manipulation make ΔCN60 useful for gain- and loss-of-function studies, high-content signaling analyses, and chronic stimulation experiments. Thus, ΔCN60 complements existing liver cell models and supports the 3R principles by enabling mechanistic in vitro studies that may reduce and refine subsequent animal experiments [45].

At the same time, ΔCN60 should not be considered a universal surrogate for fully differentiated murine hepatocytes. Its partial loss of adult metabolic functions limits its suitability for studies requiring precise modeling of gluconeogenesis, xenobiotic metabolism, bile acid synthesis, or bile acid transport. Such studies should be complemented by primary hepatocytes or more differentiated models. Conversely, the progenitor-like and plastic phenotype of ΔCN60 makes it particularly suitable for investigating how inflammatory cues, Caspase-8/Nemo signaling, and necroptosis-associated pathways influence hepatocyte identity, ductular reactions, and tumor progression.

In summary, ΔCN60 combines an authenticated genetic background, a preserved but partially dedifferentiated hepatocytic identity, a pronounced biliary/progenitor and inflammatory transcriptional profile, and a conditional Casp8/Nemo system that reproduces key aspects of TNF-α-induced NF-κB activation, cell-cycle regulation, and cell-death signaling. These features establish ΔCN60 as a versatile in vitro platform for studying Caspase-8/NEMO/RIPK1/RIPK3 signaling, hepatocyte plasticity, and TNF-α-mediated liver injury while supporting the reduction and refinement of in vivo experimentation.

5. Limitations of This Study

Several limitations should be considered when using ΔCN60. First, all functional conclusions are based on a single murine DEN-induced HCC-derived cell line from a Casp8f/fNemof/f background, and the in vivo tumorigenicity of ΔCN60 after establishment in culture was not assessed by transplantation or comparable tumor-forming assays. Therefore, although ΔCN60 is referred to as a DEN-induced HCC-derived hepatoma cell line based on its tumor origin, epithelial hepatoma-like morphology, tumor/progenitor marker expression, and hepatocyte-derived transcriptional program, the capacity of cultured ΔCN60 cells to form tumors in vivo remains to be experimentally demonstrated. Consequently, the term “hepatoma cell line” in this study denotes the DEN-induced HCC-derived origin and molecular phenotype of the cells rather than direct confirmation of transplantation-based tumorigenicity. The findings may therefore not be directly generalizable to other tumor etiologies, mouse strains, donor sexes, or human hepatocellular carcinoma.

Second, ΔCN60 was established as a serially passaged adherent tumor-derived cell population and was not generated by single-cell cloning. Although STR profiling and cytogenetic analyses support a distinct and stable identity in the passages examined, subclonal heterogeneity and long-term genetic or epigenetic drift cannot be excluded.

Third, ΔCN60 is maintained as a two-dimensional (2D) monoculture and does not reproduce the full liver microenvironment, including immune, stromal, endothelial, and vascular signals. Fourth, the pronounced fetal/biliary-progenitor signature and partial loss of mature adult hepatocyte metabolic functions limit its use for studies requiring precise modeling of gluconeogenesis, xenobiotic metabolism, bile acid synthesis, or bile acid transport. Fifth, although Casp8/Nemo-deficient ΔCN60 cells show altered TNF-α responsiveness and increased sensitivity to prolonged TNF-α exposure, necroptosis was not directly demonstrated in this study. Future experiments should assess RIPK1/RIPK3/MLKL activation, Annexin V/propidium iodide profiles, and the effects of pharmacological or genetic inhibition of necroptotic signaling.

Another limitation is that recombination efficiency was assessed only at the bulk population level, and future studies should quantify or enrich successfully recombined cells using approaches such as single-cell cloning, digital PCR, flow cytometry-based enrichment, or allele-specific quantitative PCR.

Finally, the RNA-seq analysis was performed as a bulk transcriptomic characterization and does not resolve possible subpopulations within the culture. Single-cell transcriptomics, systematic low- and high-passage comparisons, and in vivo transplantation assays would further strengthen the characterization of ΔCN60. Nevertheless, the current data establish ΔCN60 as a genetically defined and experimentally useful hepatoma model for investigating Caspase-8/NEMO-dependent TNF-α signaling and hepatocyte plasticity in vitro.

6. Conclusions

ΔCN60 is a murine DEN-induced HCC-derived hepatoma cell line established from Casp8f/fNemof/f mice. It retains floxed Casp8 and Nemo alleles and can be converted by adenoviral Cre treatment into Casp8/Nemo-deficient derivatives at the bulk population level. Combined Casp8/Nemo deletion reduces proliferation, impairs TNF-α-induced NF-κB p65 nuclear translocation, and promotes a more fetal/progenitor-like marker profile. Together with its distinct STR identity, diploid karyotype in the passages examined, and mixed hepatocytic/biliary-progenitor phenotype, ΔCN60 represents a useful in vitro platform for studying Caspase-8/NEMO-dependent TNF-α signaling and hepatocyte plasticity. The model may support future reduction and refinement of animal experiments by enabling focused mechanistic studies in cell culture before selected hypotheses are tested in vivo. However, because systematic long-term comparisons between low- and high-passage ΔCN60 cultures were not performed, conclusions regarding genetic and phenotypic stability should be restricted to the passages analyzed in this study and should not be overgeneralized to extended culture periods.
