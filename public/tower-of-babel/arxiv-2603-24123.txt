On relation of the genus one Moore-Seiberg identity to the Baxter Q-operator in the hyperbolic Ruijsenaars model


Abstract
In this paper we show how the Baxter Q-operator and the product formula for eigenfunctions of two-particle hyperbolic Ruijsenaars system can be derived from the genus one Moore-Seiberg duality identity in two-dimensional Liouville conformal field theory. We expect that this relation would reveal genuine role of the Moore-Seiberg identity in integrable systems.

Introduction

The last years witnessed outburst of interest to Ruijsenaars wave function

\displaystyle F^{g}_{\lambda}(x)={1\over S_{b}(g)}\int_{-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\mathrm{i}\infty}S_{b}(\pm z+\lambda/2+g/2)S_{b}(\pm z-\lambda/2+g/2)e^{2\mathrm{i}\pi zx}{dz\over\mathrm{i}}\,.

(1)

Here we use notations
f(x\pm y)=f(x+y)f(x-y)
and

S_{b}(x)
is hyperbolic gamma function reviewed in appendix A. This function appears in many cases in various branches of the theoretical and mathematical physics, e.g. in theory of integrable systems
[
23
,
5
]
, in two-dimensional conformal field theory
[
26
]
, in
4D
and
3D
supersymmetric gauge theories
[
12
,
8
]
etc. It is eigenfunction of the Hamiltonian of the relativistic Calogero-Sutherland model given by the
finite-difference operator
[
19
,
22
,
23
]
:

\displaystyle H={\sin\pi b(x-g)\over\sin\pi xb}e^{-b\partial_{x}}+{\sin\pi b(x+g)\over\sin\pi bx}e^{b\partial_{x}}\,,

(2)

\displaystyle H\left[F^{g}_{\lambda}\right](x)=2\cos\pi b\lambda F^{g}_{\lambda}(x)\,.

(3)

The function
F^{g}_{\lambda}(x)
possesses the nice self-duality property

[
23
]
:

F^{g}_{\lambda}(x)=F^{Q-g}_{x}(\lambda).

(4)

It is shown in
[
11
]
that the function (
1
) enjoys the following product formula:

\displaystyle 4F^{g}_{\lambda}(x_{1})F^{g}_{\lambda}(x_{2})=S_{b}(g)\int_{-\textup{i}\infty}^{\textup{i}\infty}{S_{b}(g\pm z)\over S_{b}(\pm z)}S_{b}((\bar{g}\pm z\pm x_{1}\pm x_{2})/2)F^{g}_{\lambda}(z){dz\over\mathrm{i}}\,,

(5)

where
\bar{g}=b+b^{-1}-g
.
Multiplying both sides of (
5
) by
e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\rho x_{1}}
and integrating over
x_{1}
, one can rewrite formula (
5
) in the form:

\displaystyle{4S_{b}(\pm\rho\pm\lambda/2+g/2)\over\left(S_{b}(g)\right)^{2}}F^{g}_{\lambda}(x_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{-\textup{i}\infty}^{\textup{i}\infty}\int_{-\textup{i}\infty}^{\textup{i}\infty}e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\rho x_{1}}{S_{b}(g\pm z)\over S_{b}(\pm z)}S_{b}((\bar{g}\pm z\pm x_{1}\pm x_{2})/2)F^{g}_{\lambda}(z)dzdx_{1}.

(6)

This shows that if we define an integral operator

[Q^{\rho}\phi](x)=\int_{-\textup{i}\infty}^{\textup{i}\infty}\int_{-\textup{i}\infty}^{\textup{i}\infty}e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\rho y_{1}}{S_{b}(g\pm y_{2})\over S_{b}(\pm y_{2})}S_{b}((\bar{g}\pm y_{2}\pm y_{1}\pm x)/2)\phi(y_{2})dy_{1}dy_{2}\,,

(7)

the function (
1
) appears to be its eigen-vector:

[Q^{\rho}F^{g}_{\lambda}](x)={4S_{b}(\pm\rho\pm\lambda/2+g/2)\over\left(S_{b}(g)\right)^{2}}F^{g}_{\lambda}(x)\,.

(8)

It is found in
[
4
,
5
]
, that
Q^{\rho}
operators commute with the Hamiltonian (
2
)
and with each other

[Q^{\rho},H]=0\quad{\rm and}\quad[Q^{\rho},Q^{\lambda}]=0\,,

(9)

and therefore can be considered as the Baxter
Q
-operators. It is shown in
[
11
,
5
]
, that
in certain nonrelativistic limit the formula (
5
) yields the product formula and the Baxter
Q
-operator
for eigenfunctions of the Calogero-Sutherland model. It was demonstrated in
[
7
]
, that in the complex limit
b\to\mathrm{i}
eq. (
5
) becomes product formula and the Baxter
Q
-operator for the complex hypergeometric functions
[
18
]
.

On the other hand the function (
1
) appears in two-dimensional conformal Liouville field theory as matrix
of modular transformation
S_{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}(\beta_{3})
of one-point conformal blocks on a torus
[
25
]
.
It was established in
[
14
,
16
,
15
]
that the matrix of modular transformations of the one-point blocks
should satisfy the genus one Moore-Seiberg (MS) identity:

\displaystyle S_{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}(\beta_{3})\int_{\mathbb{S}}d\beta_{4}F_{\beta_{3},\beta_{4}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\beta_{2}&\alpha_{1}\\
\beta_{2}&\alpha_{2}\end{array}\right]e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(\Delta_{\beta_{4}}-\Delta_{\beta_{2}})}F_{\beta_{4},\beta_{5}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha_{2}&\alpha_{1}\\
\beta_{2}&\beta_{2}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}}d\beta_{6}F_{\beta_{3},\beta_{6}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\beta_{1}&\alpha_{1}\\
\beta_{1}&\alpha_{2}\end{array}\right]e^{\pi\mathrm{i}(\Delta_{\alpha_{1}}+\Delta_{\alpha_{2}}-\Delta_{\beta_{5}})}F_{\beta_{1},\beta_{5}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{2}\\
\beta_{6}&\beta_{6}\end{array}\right]S_{\beta_{6}\beta_{2}}(\beta_{5})\,,

where
F_{\beta_{3},\beta_{4}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\beta_{2}&\alpha_{1}\\
\beta_{2}&\alpha_{2}\end{array}\right]
is fusion matrix and
\Delta_{\alpha}
is conformal weight of the primary labelled by
\alpha
.
In the case of
2D
conformal Liouville field theory fusion matrix was computed in
[
20
,
21
]
:

F_{\alpha_{s},\alpha_{t}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha_{3}&\alpha_{2}\\
\bar{\alpha_{4}}&\alpha_{1}\end{array}\right]={S_{b}(\alpha_{s}+\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1})S_{b}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{t}-\alpha_{4})\over S_{b}(\alpha_{t}+\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{3})S_{b}(\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{s}-\alpha_{4})}|S_{b}(2\alpha_{t})|^{2}J_{h}(\beta_{a}^{\circ},\gamma_{a}^{\circ})

(20)

where
\bar{\alpha_{4}}=Q-\alpha_{4}
,
Q=b+{1\over b}
,
\Delta_{\alpha}=\alpha(Q-\alpha)
and
\mathbb{S}={Q\over 2}+\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R}
.

Here
J_{h}
is the hyperbolic hypergeometric function, introduced in
[
22
,
21
,
20
]
:

J_{h}(\underline{\beta}^{\circ},\underline{\gamma}^{\circ})=\int_{-\textup{i}\infty}^{\textup{i}\infty}\prod_{a=1}^{4}S_{b}(z+\gamma_{a}^{\circ})S_{b}(-z+\beta_{a}^{\circ}){dz\over\mathrm{i}}\,.

(21)

The variables
\gamma_{a}^{\circ},\beta_{a}^{\circ},a=1,2,3,4
,
in the Ponsot-Teschner parametrization are given by the formulae:

\begin{aligned} &\gamma_{1}^{\circ}=-Q/2+\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{4}\,,\\
&\gamma_{2}^{\circ}=-Q/2+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\,,\\
&\gamma_{3}^{\circ}=Q/2-\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{4}\,,\\
&\gamma_{4}^{\circ}=Q/2-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\,,\end{aligned}\qquad\begin{aligned} &\beta_{1}^{\circ}=Q/2+\alpha_{s}\,,\\
&\beta_{2}^{\circ}=Q/2-\alpha_{t}+\alpha_{4}+\alpha_{2}\,,\\
&\beta_{3}^{\circ}=-Q/2+\alpha_{t}+\alpha_{4}+\alpha_{2}\,,\\
&\beta_{4}^{\circ}=3Q/2-\alpha_{s}\,.\end{aligned}

(22)

They satisfy the balancing condition
\sum_{a=1}^{4}(\gamma_{a}^{\circ}+\beta_{a}^{\circ})=2Q
.

Now we are going to explain how the equations (
1

3
) can be derived as particular cases of the relation (
1

1
)

\displaystyle S_{0\beta_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{S}}d\beta_{4}F_{0,\beta_{4}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\beta_{2}&\alpha\\
\beta_{2}&\alpha\end{array}\right]e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\Delta_{\beta_{4}}}F_{\beta_{4},\beta_{5}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\alpha\\

\displaystyle=e^{\pi\mathrm{i}(2\Delta_{\alpha}-\Delta_{\beta_{5}}+2\Delta_{\beta_{2}})}F_{0,\beta_{5}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\alpha\\
\alpha&\alpha\end{array}\right]S_{\alpha\beta_{2}}(\beta_{5})\,.

Here we used that
S_{\alpha\beta_{2}}(\beta_{5})
in the limit

(31)

where

S_{0\alpha}={1\over\sqrt{2}S_{b}(\pm 2(\alpha-Q/2))}=-2\sqrt{2}\sin{\pi(2\alpha-Q)\over b}\sin\pi(2\alpha-Q)b\,.

(32)

So we see that the one-point matrix of modular transformations is given indeed essentially by function (
1
).
If now we set in (
1

equal to degenerate value
\alpha_{1}=-b/2

and take into account

\beta_{2}&\alpha_{2}\end{array}\right]e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}(\Delta_{\beta_{4}}-\Delta_{\beta_{2}})}

\displaystyle\times F_{\beta_{2}+s_{2}b/2,\alpha_{2}+s_{3}b/2}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha_{2}&-b/2\\
\beta_{2}&\beta_{2}\end{array}\right]\quad\quad

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{4}=\pm}F_{\alpha_{2}+s_{1}b/2,\beta_{1}+s_{4}b/2}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\beta_{1}&-b/2\\
\beta_{1}&\alpha_{2}\end{array}\right]e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}(\Delta_{-b/2}+\Delta_{\alpha_{2}}-\Delta_{\beta_{5}})}

\displaystyle\times F_{\beta_{1},\alpha_{2}+s_{3}b/2}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}-b/2&\alpha_{2}\\
\beta_{1}+s_{4}b/2&\beta_{1}+s_{4}b/2\end{array}\right]S_{\beta_{1}+s_{4}b/2\beta_{2}}(\beta_{5})\,.

Inserting here the corresponding values of the fusion matrix elements with one degenerate entry, which are explicitly calculable, see for example

(46)

4
Conclusion

In this paper we have shown that the product formula of the two-particle Ruijsenaars wave functions, which coincides in fact with the
equation describing action of the Baxter Q-operator on the wave functions, can be derived as particular case of the genus one
Moore-Seiberg identity in the Liouville field theory.
Since general N-particle hyperbolic Ruijsenaars system is related to the two-dimensional conformal Toda field theory
[
12
,
8
]

we can expect that similar relation should exist also between
Moore-Seiberg identity (
1
) in the conformal Toda field theory and N-particle hyperbolic Ruijsenaars system.
Another direction is the supersymmetric generalization of the hyperbolic Ruijsenaars system.
In our previous paper
[
1
]
, an attempt to construct supersymmetric generalization was made, where
we suggested a model whose eigen-functions are given by elements of one-point matrix of the modular transformation in
N=1
super
Liouville conformal field theory. We think that similar studies of the identity (
1
) in

N=1
super Liouville conformal field theory will bring to the corresponding product formula for the elements of one-point matrix of the modular transformation in
N=1
super
Liouville conformal field theory.
We hope that deep understanding of the relation between the Moore-Seiberg identity and the Q-Baxter operator
can reveal genuine role of the Moore-Seiberg identity in integrable systems.
