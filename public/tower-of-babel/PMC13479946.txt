Research on equity and utilization efficiency of health resource allocation in Anhui Province
Authors: Haili Li, Yujing Shu, Jiegen Yu, Hairong Liu, Penghui Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo analyze the equity and utilization efficiency of health resource allocation in Anhui Province, providing methods for rational resource layout.MethodsThe entropy-weighted TOPSIS method and rank-sum ratio (RSR) method were adopted to conduct comprehensive evaluation and grading analysis of health resource allocation in Southern, Central, and Northern Anhui from 2019 to 2023.ResultsThe entropy-weighted TOPSIS analysis indicated that since 2021, the comprehensive evaluation index (C-value) of Central Anhui surpassed that of Southern Anhui, reaching 0.745 in 2023, while Northern Anhui consistently exhibited low C-values. RSR grading results classified Southern Anhui as “excellent,” Central Anhui as “moderate,” and Northern Anhui as “poor.”ConclusionSignificant disparities exist in health resource allocation across Anhui Province. Southern Anhui boasts abundant resources but faces weaker grassroots service capacity; Central Anhui shows rapid growth yet poor alignment between resources and population density; Northern Anhui suffers from inadequate infrastructure and high equipment idle rates. Recommendations include:strengthening mobile medical services and resource allocation to grassroots in Southern Anhui; implementing hierarchical diagnosis and treatment coupled with medical consortium collaboration in Central Anhui; enhancing grassroots talent and equipment management in Northern Anhui through targeted training programs and incentive policies.

Introduction

The report of the 19th National Congress of the Communist Party of China emphasized that as socialism with Chinese characteristics has entered a new era, the principal contradiction facing Chinese society has transformed into the contradiction between the people’s growing needs for a better life and unbalanced and inadequate development. Unbalanced and inadequate development is a direct manifestation of low-quality development. In response to this new principal contradiction, the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China has proposed a new development philosophy: high-quality development. High-quality development is development that can effectively meet the people’s growing needs for a better life and reflects the new development philosophy. The people’s aspiration for a better life includes the need for high-quality and efficient medical services. As a strategic foundation for safeguarding the health rights and interests of the entire population, the equity and efficiency of health resource allocation directly determine the accessibility and quality of medical and health services. It is also a crucial support for implementing the “Healthy China” strategy [1]. The Chinese government has consistently prioritized the optimization of health resource allocation to address the contradiction between the growing demand for high-quality medical services and the unbalanced, insufficient development across regions [2]. We must focus on the quantitative expansion of medical services, but more importantly, we must address the qualitative aspects of medical services, achieving effective quantitative growth through substantial qualitative improvement, thereby enhancing the people’s sense of gain, well-being, and security. The development of China’s health sector in the new era faces numerous challenges. On the one hand, the people continue to raise new and higher demands regarding the allocation of health resources and the provision of services. On the other hand, there is a severe imbalance in the distribution of health resources between urban and rural areas and among different regions, coupled with a relatively rigid health management system and relatively backward health service delivery. These factors have led to multiple problems in the allocation of health resources, including waste, shortages, and imbalances. Therefore, it is of great significance to identify the existing problems in the allocation of health resources and service provision, to enrich the connotation of health resources, to improve the efficiency of health resource allocation, and to explore effective approaches and models for their allocation.

Anhui Province, a key component of the Yangtze River Delta integration strategy, displays a characteristic regional development gradient. The region exhibits three distinct patterns: the northern plains, characterized by a large population base but a scarcity of high-quality resources; central Anhui, an economically vibrant urban agglomeration grappling with structural supply–demand mismatches; and southern Anhui, an ecologically advantaged mountainous area where service accessibility remains limited. This three-dimensional disparity of “natural geography, economic development, and population distribution” has led to a pattern of “central polarization and a north-south fault” in the allocation of health resources. The problem is further complicated by the paradox of structural surplus coexisting with absolute shortage [3]. In response, the Anhui Provincial Government issued the “Implementation Plan for the Healthy Anhui Action” in 2021, explicitly proposing to optimize the structure of health resource allocation, strengthen regional coordination, and focus on improving the capacity of primary medical services [4]. Systematically evaluating the efficiency and fairness of health resource allocation is therefore of great practical value for alleviating supply-demand contradictions, promoting balanced urban-rural development, and enhancing the public’s sense of health acquisition.

Literature review

Health resource allocation has long been a central focus of global health systems research. The core challenge of achieving both equity and efficiency in allocation has attracted extensive academic attention, leading to a rich body of literature.

First, studies on the efficiency of health resource allocation often employ methods such as Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA) and Stochastic Frontier Analysis (SFA). Robles Enciso et al. (2025) found that healthcare efficiency in Mexico varied regionally, being higher in the north and center but lower in the south due to resource and technology constraints [5]. Ibrahim (2023) evaluated maternal and child health service efficiency in Sub-Saharan Africa using DEA [6], while Jukka and Timo (2024) reported higher efficiency in Helsinki compared to sparsely populated, aging northern Finland [7]. Within China, Shi et al. (2023) identified an inverted U-shaped relationship between local health spending and efficiency, suggesting that over-investment could reduce effectiveness [8]. Wang et al. (2024) applied network DEA to analyze efficiency across eastern, central, and western China [9], whereas Cao et al. (2024) focused on tertiary hospitals in Shandong Province from a micro-level perspective using bootstrap DEA [10].

Second, research on equity frequently utilizes the Gini coefficient and Theil index to measure distributional disparities [11, 12]. Provincial studies reveal persistent inequalities. For example, Zhou (2024) and Wei et al. (2024) identified significant inequities in Sichuan and Chongqing, respectively [11, 12]. Su et al. (2022) analyzed the dual challenges of equity and efficiency in Guangdong’s public hospitals [13]. Research into specific sectors shows similar patterns, such as inequities in maternal and child health resources in Hunan [14] and ongoing allocation challenges in primary care in Jiangsu and Liaoning provinces [15, 16]. Sun et al. (2018) provided a broader measurement of health equity in Zhejiang [17], while Wu et al. (2022) examined the strain on tiered healthcare resources during the COVID-19 pandemic in Shanxi [18].

Despite the valuable insights from these studies, several research gaps remain. First, most existing efficiency studies rely on single methods like DEA or Gini coefficients, which often fail to systematically account for the interplay between multiple complex factors such as population density, terrain characteristics, and economic level. Second, the research perspective is often fragmented, with few studies integrating efficiency and fairness into a unified analytical framework. Third, while Anhui Province exhibits a unique and pronounced regional disparity, there is a lack of a comprehensive and systematic investigation that quantifies both the equity and efficiency of its health resource allocation using an integrated methodology. This study aims to fill these gaps by employing a combined model of entropy-weighted TOPSIS and rank-sum ratio (RSR) methods to seamlessly connect quantitative fairness evaluation with efficiency grading. This approach allows for a comprehensive evaluation of health resource allocation in Anhui Province from 2019 to 2023 and an in-depth analysis of the underlying differences among the three development models in Southern, Central, and Northern Anhui.

Materials and methods

Data source

The data were extracted from the Statistical Yearbook of Anhui Province (2020–2024) and the annual health development reports of various cities. Key indicators include the number of health institutions, the number of beds in health institutions, the number of health technicians, the number of licensed (assistant) doctors, the number of registered nurses, and the total population at year-end. For regional comparison, Anhui Province is divided into three regions: Southern Anhui, Central Anhui, and Northern Anhui. This division is based on the geographical boundaries of the Yangtze and Huaihe Rivers-a classification widely used in regional studies in Anhui. Northern Anhui comprises seven cities: Huaibei, Suzhou, Bozhou, Bengbu, Fuyang, Huainan, and Chuzhou; Central Anhui includes four cities: Lu’an, Hefei, Maanshan, and Anqing; Southern Anhui covers Wuhu, Tongling, Xuancheng, Chizhou, and Huangshan (Fig. 1).Fig. 1The distribution of the three regions of Southern Anhui, Central Anhui, and Northern Anhui

Research method

This study employs a combination of the entropy-weighted TOPSIS method and the rank-sum ratio (RSR) method to systematically evaluate the equity and efficiency characteristics of health resource allocation in Anhui Province. Entropy-weighted TOPSIS method

This study applies the entropy-weighted TOPSIS method to assess the equity of interregional health resource allocation. Using raw data from three major regions in Anhui Province, the information entropy value of each indicator is derived. A lower entropy value signifies greater discriminatory power of the indicator in reflecting regional disparities, warranting a higher assigned weight. The TOPSIS model is then employed to establish positive and negative ideal solutions, and the relative closeness of each region’s resource allocation scheme to the optimal solution is calculated. A closeness value closer to 1 indicates that the region’s allocation approaches the ideal state.

Rank-sum ratio (RSR) method

The RSR method is a multi-indicator decision-making technique that extracts a dimensionless RSR statistic from multiple evaluation indicators through rank transformation, enabling the comparison of the performance of n evaluation objects. Based on the data characteristics of the three major regions of southern Anhui, central Anhui, and northern Anhui, a rank transformation was first performed on five indicators to eliminate the interference of dimensional differences-such as the extensive service radius in the mountainous areas of southern Anhui and the prolonged bed turnover cycle in northern Anhui-on the evaluation results. Subsequently, the rank sum ratio of each region was calculated, and a probability distribution model was employed to set grading thresholds, classifying the efficiency of resource allocation into three levels: high, medium, and low. This efficiency classification enables an intuitive presentation of the structural contradictions among the regions.

Results

Descriptive analysis of health resource allocation

Between 2019 and 2023, the overall level of health resource allocation in Anhui Province exhibited an upward trend; nevertheless, pronounced regional disparities persisted. Southern Anhui consistently recorded the highest provincial levels across all six indicators per 1,000 population, namely the number of health institutions, beds, health technicians, licensed physicians, and registered nurses. In 2023, these figures reached 0.52 institutions, 5.85 beds, 5.60 health technicians, 2.15 licensed physicians, and 3.05 registered nurses per 1,000 population, substantially exceeding those of other regions. Central Anhui followed in rank order. From 2019 to 2023, the number of health institutions per 1,000 population in central Anhui increased from 0.40 to 0.45, and the number of beds per 1,000 population rose from 4.77 to 5.20, while the counts of health technicians and registered nurses also demonstrated steady growth. Although northern Anhui showed improvements across all indicators, its overall level remained relatively low. Of note, the numbers of health technicians and registered nurses per 1,000 population in northern Anhui were only 4.20 and 1.80, respectively, lagging considerably behind those in southern and central Anhui.

Figures 2, 3, 4 illustrate the trends of these key indicators. From 2019 to 2023, the total health resource allocation index (HRI) across the three major regions of Anhui Province exhibited an increasing trend; nevertheless, regional disparities remained significant. Southern Anhui continued to lead in health resource levels, with its HRI rising from 3.264 to 3.434, representing an increase of 5.2%. Central Anhui experienced the fastest growth, with its HRI increasing from 2.836 to 3.090, an increase of 8.9%. Northern Anhui, starting from a weaker baseline, showed a modest increase in HRI from 2.336 to 2.450, an increase of only 4.9%. Discussion and Conclusion

Discussion

This study provides a comprehensive analysis of the equity and efficiency of health resource allocation in Anhui Province from 2019 to 2023 using an integrated methodological approach. The findings reveal significant regional disparities and offer insights into the underlying mechanisms.

The driving factors behind regional disparities

The study confirms a substantial imbalance in health resource distribution. While Southern Anhui maintains a leading position in per capita resources, the emergence of Central Anhui as the top-ranked region in the C-value since 2021 is a notable trend. This shift can be attributed to several factors. Firstly, provincial policy has heavily prioritized the development of Hefei, the capital city in Central Anhui, as a regional hub for high-quality medical services. This has led to a concentrated influx of capital, infrastructure, and top-tier talent, driving rapid growth in the number of institutions and beds, as well as a significant increase in the weight of health technicians and doctors in the evaluation (Table 3). Secondly, the infrastructure in Southern Anhui, while abundant, is aging in some mountainous areas. The region faces challenges in service accessibility and preventive care capacity, which may have limited the efficiency gains from its high resource quantity. Thirdly, the impact of economic development cannot be overlooked. Central Anhui’s active economy has allowed for more sustained health investment, while Northern Anhui’s traditional agricultural economy has struggled to keep pace with growing healthcare demands. This analysis highlights that the “central overtaking” phenomenon is not simply a matter of resource quantity but a complex outcome of policy prioritization, economic capacity, and the differential utilization efficiency of resources.

Talent shortage as a core barrier to Equity

The increasing weights of health technicians and practicing doctors from 2019 to 2023 underscore the growing importance of human resources as a core determinant of equity. The analysis shows that talent shortage is a primary barrier, particularly in Northern Anhui, where per capita health technicians and doctors remain far below the provincial average. This shortage directly undermines the quality of primary care. For instance, the low proportion of village clinic doctors with standardized qualifications in Northern Anhui (under 55%) challenges effective chronic disease management. In Central Anhui, while the quantity of doctors has increased, their distribution is uneven. The severe patient load in Hefei’s tertiary hospitals (exceeding reasonable capacity by over 30%) compared to underutilized county hospitals reveals a structural mismatch. This pattern, where high-quality human resources are concentrated in a few central facilities, creates a “siphoning effect,” exacerbating inequities rather than alleviating them. The loss of over 120 intermediate-to-senior professionals from Southern Anhui’ s grassroots centers in five years further illustrates the nationwide challenge of retaining talent in underserved areas.

Management inefficiency and its implications

The RSR grading confirms that Northern Anhui has the lowest allocation efficiency (RSR = 0.551). The consistently low bed occupancy rates in its primary institutions (below 65%) and high equipment idle rates (e.g., 23% of equipment idle) are clear indicators of underutilization. This inefficiency is not solely due to a lack of resources but also stems from organizational management shortcomings, such as the absence of a unified scheduling platform for medical equipment. In Central Anhui, the efficiency (RSR = 0.696) is hampered by the slow development of the medical consortium, with only 15% of community centers having established two-way referral channels with tertiary hospitals. This lack of integration fuels patient preference for large city hospitals, increasing costs and straining tertiary facilities. Even in Southern Anhui, the highest efficiency region (RSR = 0.705), there is room for improvement, particularly in mobile medical service frequency for remote villages. These findings indicate that achieving optimal resource allocation requires not only increasing investment but also fundamentally reforming the organizational and management models to ensure resources are deployed and utilized effectively.

Conclusion

Health resources are the cornerstone of the healthcare system and play a central role in implementing the “Healthy China” strategy. This research reveals significant equity issues in health resource allocation in Anhui Province, characterized by pronounced regional gradient differences. The southern Anhui region is known for its concentration of high-quality resources but lacks effective grassroots coverage. The central Anhui region has prominent advantages in scale but suffers from a mismatch in resource utilization. The northern Anhui region has a weak foundation and low efficiency. This study demonstrates that an integrated approach of entropy-weighted TOPSIS and RSR can effectively capture these complex dynamics.

Suggestion

Based on these findings, the following specific and tailored policy recommendations are proposed:

For Northern Anhui: The focus should be on infrastructure strengthening and talent retention. The Anhui Provincial Government should establish a special fund for village doctor training and equipment updates. Given the flat terrain of the Northern Anhui Plain, it is suitable to promote the construction of county-level medical communities. A compensation mechanism for talent mobility should be established, encouraging senior experts from tertiary hospitals in other regions to reside in county-level hospitals in Northern Anhui. To address the high equipment idle rate, relevant departments should implement an intelligent scheduling system for county-level medical resources, enabling cross- township allocation of high-value medical equipment like CT and MRI scanners.

For Central Anhui: The policy focus should shift from simply expanding quantity to optimizing structure and promoting integration. To mitigate the “capital highland, peripheral depression” effect, the government must control the excessive expansion of provincial capital hospitals and focus on improving the service capacity of county-level medical centers. To address the mismatch in resource utilization, efforts must concentrate on improving the hierarchical diagnosis and treatment system and accelerating the development of medical consortia. A concrete target should be to allocate at least 20% of the ordinary outpatient slots in tertiary hospitals to patients referred by grassroots institutions. This would help decompress tertiary hospitals and strengthen primary care.

For Southern Anhui: The strategy should prioritize enhancing service accessibility in mountainous areas. Given the dispersed population in Southern Anhui’s mountainous terrain, the government should strengthen mobile medical services and promote the sinking of specialized resources. This includes deploying medical teams to provide on-site services during the high-incidence season for epidemics and expanding the use of remote informationization and specialty alliances. To retain talent in remote areas, the incentive mechanism for grassroots medical personnel should be improved by adding job subsidies and creating green channels for professional title promotion for those serving in remote township health centers.
