The aim of these notes is twofold: We develop notions of support for
triangulated categories, and we apply them to classify thick and
localising tensor ideals of categories that arise in modular
representation theory of finite groups.

There are two basic situations: Either the triangulated category
admits a monoidal structure, or it admits an action of a
graded-commutative ring. In order to cover both we introduce the
notion of a triangulated category that is fibred over a space. The space may
be the prime ideal spectrum of a tensor triangulated category or the
prime ideal spectrum of a ring that is acting.

There is some further dichotomy: The triangulated categories we study
are either essentially small, or they admit set-indexed coproducts and
are compactly generated. Both settings are related; so we study the
support for a compactly generated category by introducing first the
support for its subcategory of compact objects.

The ultimate goal is to establish a stratification of a triangulated
category via an appropriate space, and to actually compute it. This
amounts to classifying thick and localising subcategories. For
instance, we achieve this for derived categories of modular
representations. More specifically, for any finite group the
stratification of its derived category involves the spectrum of the
cohomology ring. This builds on Quillen’s stratification of group
cohomology, and one may think of this process as a categorification of
the Quillen stratification. This prototypical example explains the
term ‘stratification’ in the triangulated context.

These notes are not exhaustive, though fairly self-contained. In
particular, only few references are included. The guiding principle
has been to develop all concepts that are need for our applications in
modular representation theory, and to do it in sufficient generality
that the techniques can be easily used in other contexts.

The lattice of thick subcategories

The thick subcategories of a triangulated category form a
lattice. This is a fundamental object of interest and we collect some
basic properties. A useful tool is the category of cohomological
functors from a triangulated category to abelian groups, and any thick
subcategory yields a localisation sequence for the category of
cohomological functors. There is a notion of commutativity for pairs
of thick subcategories, which is equivalent to having Mayer–Vietoris
type exact sequences for cohomological functors.

[
10
,
19
]
.

Lattice structure

Let
(L,\leq)
be a poset. For any subset
U\subseteq L
we write

\bigvee_{x\in U}x\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sup U

for the
supremum
(or

join
) and

\bigwedge_{x\in U}x\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\inf U

for the
infimum
(or

meet
) of the elements in
U
, provided they exist.

Definition 1.1
.

A poset
L
is a

lattice
if
x\vee y
and
x\wedge y
exists for any pair
x,y

in
L
. A lattice is
complete
if
\sup U
and
\inf U
exist for any

U\subseteq L
.
An element

x\in L
is
finite
or
compact
if
x\leq\sup U
implies

x\leq\sup V
for some finite subset
V\subseteq U
.

For the definition of a complete lattice it suffices to request the
existence of all suprema, since

\inf U=\sup\{x\in L\mid x\leq u\text{ for all }u\in U\}.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category. The thick subcategories of

\mathcal{T}
are partially ordered via inclusion. For a class of objects

\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
let
\operatorname{thick}(\mathcal{X})
denote the smallest thick
subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
that contains
\mathcal{X}
. There is an inductive
procedure to construct the thick closure:

\operatorname{thick}(\mathcal{X})=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{X}(n)

where
\mathcal{X}(0)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{X}

and
\mathcal{X}(n+1)
is obtained from
\mathcal{X}(n)
by adding
extensions, (de)suspensions, and direct summands of objects in
\mathcal{X}(n)
.

We write
\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
for the lattice of thick subcategories of

\mathcal{T}
. This may not be a set, but the operations
\vee
and

\wedge
are well defined. In fact, for each pair of thick
subcategories
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}
one has

\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{V}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}=\operatorname{thick}(\mathcal{U}\cup\mathcal{V})\,.

More generally, for each
family
(\mathcal{U}_{i})_{i\in I}
in
\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})

\bigwedge_{i\in I}\mathcal{U}_{i}=\bigcap_{i\in I}\mathcal{U}_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{U}_{i}=\operatorname{thick}\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{U}_{i}\right).

Lemma 1.2
.

The lattice
\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
is complete. An element

\mathcal{U}\in\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
is finite if and only if

\mathcal{U}=\operatorname{thick}(x)
for some object
x\in\mathcal{U}
.∎

For an exact functor
f\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
there is a pair of maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}(f)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}^{-}(f)}

which are defined by

\operatorname{Thick}(f)(\mathcal{U})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{thick}(f(\mathcal{U}))\qquad\text{and}\qquad\operatorname{Thick}^{-}(f)(\mathcal{V})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f^{-1}(\mathcal{V})

for
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. These maps
form an adjoint pair, because one has

\operatorname{Thick}(f)(\mathcal{U})\subseteq\mathcal{V}\qquad\iff\qquad\mathcal{U}\subseteq\operatorname{Thick}^{-}(f)(\mathcal{V}).

We may think of a poset as category. The objects are the elements and
there is a unique morphism
x\to y
if and only if
x\leq y
. Then for
any set
U
one has

\bigvee_{x\in U}x=\coprod_{x\in U}x\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bigwedge_{x\in U}x=\prod_{x\in U}x.

Thus a left adjoint preserves all joins, while a right adjoint
preserves all meets.

Localisation and cohomology

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category with suspension

\varSigma\colon\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{T}
. Given objects
x,y
in
\mathcal{T}
set

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(x,y)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,\varSigma^{i}y)\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(x,x)\,.

These are graded abelian groups. Composition makes
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(x)
a graded ring and
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(x,y)
a
left-
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(y)
right-
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(x)
module.

Fix a triangulated subcategory
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
. The composite
of the inclusion
\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{T}
and the quotient functor

\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{U}
induces a long exact sequence which we
describe as follows.

For
x\in\mathcal{T}
consider the
slice category

\mathcal{U}/x
; the
objects are pairs
(u,\phi)
given by a morphism
\phi\colon u\to x

with
u\in\mathcal{U}
and a morphisms
(u,\phi)\to(u^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
is given by
a morphism
\alpha\colon u\to u^{\prime}
such that

\phi^{\prime}\alpha=\phi
. Analogously, one defines the category

x\backslash\mathcal{U}
of all morphisms
x\to y
with cone in
\mathcal{U}
.

Lemma 1.3
.

The canonical functor
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{U}
induces for each object

x\in\mathcal{T}
a functorial exact sequence:

\cdots\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{U}}(-,\varSigma^{-1}x)\longrightarrow\operatorname{colim}_{u\to x}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,u)\longrightarrow\\
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,x)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{U}}(-,x)\longrightarrow\cdots

Proof.

From the definition of
\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{U}
we have

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{U}}(-,x)=\operatorname{colim}_{x\to y}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,y)

where
x\to y
runs through all morphisms with cone in
\mathcal{U}
, so
the objects in
x\backslash\mathcal{U}
. Given such an exact triangle

u\to x\to y\to\varSigma u
in
\mathcal{T}
yields an exact sequence:

\cdots\to\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,\varSigma^{-1}y)\to\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,u)\to\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,x)\to\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,y)\to\cdots

Taking the colimit over the filtered category of all such
triangles yields the desired exact sequence, because there are
forgetful functors to
\mathcal{U}/x
and
x\backslash\mathcal{U}
which induce
isomorphisms when taking colimits.
∎

Now assume that
\mathcal{T}
is essentially small and let
\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}

denote the category of cohomological functors
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Ab}
into
the category of graded abelian groups. The assignment

x\longmapsto H_{x}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,x)

yields an embedding

\mathcal{T}\to\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}
.

Lemma 1.4
.

The cohomological functors
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Ab}
are preciseley the
filtered colimits of representable functors.

Proof.

Any filtered colimit of cohomological functors is again
cohomological. On the other hand, any additive functor
F\colon\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Ab}

equals the colimit of representable functors indexed by the category

\mathcal{T}/F
of all morphisms
H_{x}\to F
, so

\operatorname{colim}_{H_{x}\to F}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,x)=F.

The category
\mathcal{T}/F
is filtered when
F
is cohomological.
∎

There are three essential properties of
\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}
that we are going
to use:

(1)

the category
\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}
has filtered colimits,

(2)

the category
\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}
admits a canonical exact structure,
and

(3)

any filtered colimit of exact sequences is again exact.

By definition a sequence
0\to F\to G\to H\to 0
in
\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}
is

exact
if
0\to F(x)\to G(x)\to H(x)\to 0
is exact in
\mathrm{Ab}
for
all
x\in\mathcal{T}
.

An exact functor
f\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
between essentially small
triangulated categories induces an adjoint pair of functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{*}}

where for
F\in\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}
and
F^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
one defines

f^{*}(F)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{colim}_{H_{x}\to F}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(-,f(x))\qquad\text{and}\qquad f_{*}(F^{\prime})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}F^{\prime}\circ f.

Using Yoneda’s lemma the adjointness is easily checked:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}(f^{*}(F),F^{\prime})

\displaystyle\cong\lim_{H_{x}\to F}\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(-,f(x)),F^{\prime})

\displaystyle\cong\lim_{H_{x}\to F}F^{\prime}(f(x))

\displaystyle\cong\lim_{H_{x}\to F}\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,x),f_{*}(F^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}(F,f_{*}(F^{\prime})).

Note that
f^{*}
and
f_{*}
both are exact and preserve filtered
colimits.

For a pair of exact functors

\mathcal{U}\xrightarrow{\ i\ }\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\ q\ }\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{U}

given by the inclusion of a triangulated subcategory and the quotient
functor, set

\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}i^{*}\circ i_{*}\qquad\text{and}\qquad L_{\mathcal{U}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}q_{*}\circ q^{*}\,.

Both functors are idempotent, so the
adjunctions induce isomorphisms
\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}^{2}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}

and
L_{\mathcal{U}}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}L^{2}_{\mathcal{U}}
. For
x\in\mathcal{T}
one computes:

\displaystyle\varGamma_{U}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,x))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{colim}_{u\to x}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,u))

\displaystyle L_{U}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,x))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{U}}(-,x)

The equivalence
\varSigma\colon\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{T}
extends to an equivalence

\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}
by taking
F
to

\varSigma F\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}F\circ\varSigma^{-1}
.

Proposition 1.5
.

The sequence of functors
\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{U}

induces an exact sequence of functors
\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}\to\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}

(1.1)

\cdots\longrightarrow\varSigma^{n-1}L_{\mathcal{U}}\longrightarrow\varSigma^{n}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\longrightarrow\varSigma^{n}\longrightarrow\varSigma^{n}L_{\mathcal{U}}\longrightarrow\varSigma^{n+1}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\longrightarrow\cdots

which is functorial in
\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

The morphism
\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\to\mathrm{id}
is the counit which is given by
the construction of
\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}
. Analogously,
\mathrm{id}\to L_{\mathcal{U}}

is the unit. For a representable functor
F=\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,x)
the
sequence identifies with the one from Lemma
1.3
. It
remains to observe that any cohomological functor is a filtered
colimit of representable functors and that each functor in the above
sequence preserves filtered colimits.

An inclusion
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
of triangulated subcategories
induces morphisms
\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\to\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}
and

L_{\mathcal{U}}\to L_{\mathcal{V}}
which yield a morphism of exact sequences of
functors
\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}\to\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}
.
∎

We capture the situation in the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Coh}(\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{U})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{*}}

The lower row is a
localisation sequence
, which means the following:

(L1)

(i^{*},i_{*})
and
(q^{*},q_{*})
are adjoint pairs,

(L2)

i^{*}
and
q_{*}
are fully faithful (equivalently,

\mathrm{id}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}i_{*}i^{*}
and
q^{*}q_{*}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathrm{id}
),

(L3)

\operatorname{Im}i^{*}=\operatorname{Ker}q^{*}
(equivalently,
i^{*}i_{*}(x)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}x
if and only if
q^{*}(x)=0
).

An analogue of this localisation sequence arises when
i
and
q
admit right
adjoints; see (
3.1
). However, for
essentially small triangulated categories it is rare that the
inclusion of a thick subcategory admits an adjoint, and the
above localisation sequence is a useful substitute.

Commutativity

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category, and we assume that
\mathcal{T}
is essentially small.

Definition 1.6
.

A pair of thick subcategories

\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}
of
\mathcal{T}
is called
commuting
if the inclusions

\mathcal{V}\leftarrow\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{U}
induce isomorphisms

\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}\xleftarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{V}}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\,.

A thick subcategory is
central
if it commutes with all other
thick subcategories.

There is an equivalent and more elementary way to describe
commutativity: A pair
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}
of thick subcategories is
commuting if and only if all morphisms
\mathcal{U}\ni u\to v\in\mathcal{V}
and

\mathcal{V}\ni v\to u\in\mathcal{U}
factor through objects in

\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{V}
. The following lemma provides the explanation.

Lemma 1.7
.

For a pair
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}\in\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
the following are equivalent.

(1)

Every morphism
\mathcal{U}\ni u\to v\in\mathcal{V}
factors through an
object in
\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}
.

(2)

The canonical morphism

\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}\to\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}
is an
isomorphism.

Proof.

When
\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{U}
is viewed as a full subcategory of
\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}
, then the
objects in
\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{U}
are precisely the filtered colimits of objects

H_{u}
with
u\in\mathcal{U}
. From this it follows that
F\in\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}
belongs
to
\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{U}
if and only if every morphism
H_{x}\to F
with
x\in\mathcal{T}

factors through
H_{u}
for some
u\in\mathcal{U}
.

The above general observation shows that the two conditions are
equivalent to the property that each object of the form

\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}F
belongs to
\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}
.
∎

For a pair of commuting subcategories
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}
there are further canonical
isomorphisms:

(1.2)

\begin{gathered}L_{\mathcal{U}}L_{\mathcal{V}}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}L_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}\xleftarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}L_{\mathcal{V}}L_{\mathcal{U}}\\
\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}L_{\mathcal{U}}\xleftarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}L_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}\cong L_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}L_{\mathcal{U}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}\\
\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}L_{\mathcal{U}}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}L_{\mathcal{U}}\cong L_{\mathcal{U}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}\xleftarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}L_{\mathcal{U}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}\end{gathered}

It is straightforward to deduce these isomorphisms from the long exact sequence
(
1.1
), and details are left to the reader.

A pair of commuting subcategories gives rise to a pair of

Mayer–Vietoris sequences
. It turns out that commutativity is
actually equivalent to having such long exact sequences.

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}\in\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
be commuting. Then there are canonical exact
sequences

\cdots\longrightarrow\varSigma^{-1}L_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}\longrightarrow L_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}\longrightarrow L_{\mathcal{U}}\oplus L_{\mathcal{V}}\longrightarrow L_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}\longrightarrow\varSigma L_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}\longrightarrow\cdots

and

\cdots\longrightarrow\varSigma^{-1}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}\longrightarrow\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}\longrightarrow\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\oplus\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}\longrightarrow\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}\longrightarrow\varSigma\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}\longrightarrow\cdots

where the morphisms are induced by the inclusions

\mathcal{U}\leftarrow\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}\leftarrow\mathcal{V}.

Proof.

The long exact sequence (
1.1
) given by
\mathcal{U}
and composed
with
L_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}\to L_{\mathcal{V}}

yields a morphisms of exact sequences.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varSigma^{-1}L_{\mathcal{U}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}L_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\mathcal{U}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varSigma\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}L_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varSigma^{-1}L_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}L_{\mathcal{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\mathcal{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varSigma\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}L_{\mathcal{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

Here we use the identification

L_{\mathcal{U}}L_{\mathcal{V}}=L_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}
and the isomorphisms from
(
1.2
). Inverting them yields the connecting morphism

L_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}\to\varSigma L_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}
, and then a
standard argument turns the diagram into an exact sequence of the
above form; cf.
[
26
, Lemma 10.7.4]
. The proof for the
second sequence is analogous.
∎

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}\in\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
be commuting.
Then for each object
x\in\mathcal{T}
there is a canonical exact sequence

\cdots\to\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}/(\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V})}(-,x)\to\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{U}}(-,x)\oplus\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{V}}(-,x)\to\\
\to\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}/(\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V})}(-,x)\to\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}/(\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V})}(-,\varSigma x)\to\cdots\,.

Proof.

Let
F=\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,x)
. Then
L_{\mathcal{U}}F=\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{U}}(-,x)
. Thus the sequence follows from the Mayer–Vietoris sequence of Proposition
1.8
.
∎

Distributivity

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category. We consider the lattice

\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
of thick subcategories and recall the following definitions.

Definition 1.10
.

A lattice is
distributive
if for all elements

x,y,z
there is an equality

(x\wedge z)\vee(y\wedge z)=(x\vee y)\wedge z.

An equivalent
condition is that for all elements
x,y,z
there is an equality

(x\vee z)\wedge(y\vee z)=(x\wedge y)\vee z.

A
frame
is a
complete lattice where arbitrary joins distribute over finite meets.

The lattice
\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
is rarely distributive; cf. Example
1.16
. But
distributivity is obtained when one restricts to appropriate classes
of thick subcategories. For instance, these can be tensor ideals or
subcategories given via central localisation. For establishing
distributivity a crucial ingredient is commutativity.

Our goal is to show that the central subcategories form a sublattice
of
\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
which is distributive. We begin with a sequence of
lemmas.

Lemma 1.11
.

Let
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W}\in\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
and suppose
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}
.
If
\varGamma_{\mathcal{W}}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}
then
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}
. Then
\varGamma_{\mathcal{W}}H_{x}\cong\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}H_{x}\cong H_{x}
. Thus
H_{x}\in\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{W}
and therefore

x\in\mathcal{W}
.
∎

The next pair of lemmas shows that meet and join preserve central
elements.

Lemma 1.12
.

Let
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}\in\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
and suppose that
\mathcal{U}
is central.

(1)

The element
\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}
is central in
\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{V})
.

(2)

The element
(\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V})/\mathcal{V}
is central in
\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{V})
.

Proof.

(1) Let
\mathcal{X}\in\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{V})
. Then any morphism between objects of
\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}

and
\mathcal{X}
(in either direction) factors through an object of

\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{X}=(\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V})\wedge\mathcal{X}
.

(2) Observe that for any
\mathcal{X}\in\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
containing

\mathcal{V}
the functor
\varGamma_{\mathcal{X}}
restricts to a functor

\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{V}\to\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{V}
where it identifies with

\varGamma_{\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{V}}
, since

\varGamma_{\mathcal{X}}L_{\mathcal{V}}\cong L_{\mathcal{V}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{X}}
. Using
(
1.2
) we obtain

\varGamma_{\mathcal{X}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}L_{\mathcal{V}}\cong\varGamma_{\mathcal{X}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}L_{\mathcal{V}}\cong\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{X}}L_{\mathcal{V}}\cong\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}L_{\mathcal{V}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{X}}\cong\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}L_{\mathcal{V}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{X}}\cong\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{X}}L_{\mathcal{V}},

and therefore

\varGamma_{\mathcal{X}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}\cong\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{X}}
when restricted to

\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{V}
.
∎

Lemma 1.13
.

Let
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}\in\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
be central. Then

\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}
are central. Moreover, we have for all
\mathcal{W}\in\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})

(\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{W})\vee(\mathcal{V}\wedge\mathcal{W})=(\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V})\wedge\mathcal{W}

and

(\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{W})\wedge(\mathcal{V}\vee\mathcal{W})=(\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V})\vee\mathcal{W}

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}
is central since

\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{W}}\cong\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{W}}\cong\varGamma_{\mathcal{W}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}\cong\varGamma_{\mathcal{W}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}.

Now we show that
\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}
is central and combine this with the proof
of the first identity. Consider the following Mayer–Vietoris
sequences, where the middle one uses that
\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{W}
and

\mathcal{V}\wedge\mathcal{W}
are commuting by Lemma
1.12
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{W}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{W}}\oplus\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{W}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{W}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}\wedge\mathcal{W}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{W}}\oplus\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}\wedge\mathcal{W}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{(\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{W})\vee(\mathcal{V}\wedge\mathcal{W})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{\mathcal{W}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{\mathcal{W}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}\oplus\varGamma_{\mathcal{W}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{\mathcal{W}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

The vertical isomorphisms are clear since
\mathcal{U}
and

\mathcal{V}
central. The inclusion

(\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{W})\vee(\mathcal{V}\wedge\mathcal{W})\subseteq(\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V})\wedge\mathcal{W}

is automatic. Thus we can apply
Lemma
1.11
, and then the five lemma yields the
assertion for
\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}
. The proof of the second identity
is similar, using the other Mayer–Vietoris sequence involving functors of the form
L_{\mathcal{U}}
instead of

\varGamma_{\mathcal{U}}
.
∎

An immediate consequence of Lemma
1.13
is the fact that
the central elements form a sublattice of
\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
which is
distributive. The next step is to extend the above lemma and to
establish the same assertions for arbitrary joins, using some standard
arguments. Thus the central subcategories form a frame.

Proposition 1.14
.

The central subcategories of
\mathcal{T}
are closed under finite meets
and arbitrary joins. Let
(\mathcal{U}_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of
central elements in
\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
and
\mathcal{V}\in\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
. Then the following holds:

\displaystyle\bigvee_{i\in I}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\wedge\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle=\left(\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{U}_{i}\right)\wedge\mathcal{V}\qquad(I\text{ arbitrary})

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{i\in I}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\vee\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle=\left(\bigwedge_{i\in I}\mathcal{U}_{i}\right)\vee\mathcal{V}\qquad(I\text{ finite})

Proof.

Set
\mathcal{U}=\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{U}_{i}
. The element

\mathcal{U}_{J}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigvee_{i\in J}\mathcal{U}_{i}
is central for all finite
J\subseteq I

by Lemma
1.13
. Now choose objects
u\in\mathcal{U}
and

v\in\mathcal{V}
. Then
u\in\mathcal{U}_{J}
for some finite
J\subseteq I
and
therefore any morphism between
u
and
v
factors through an object
in
\mathcal{U}_{J}\wedge\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}
. Thus
\mathcal{U}
is central.

The inclusion

\bigvee_{i\in I}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\wedge\mathcal{V})\subseteq\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}

is clear. For the other inclusion we use again
Lemma
1.13
. In fact any object

x\in\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}
belongs to

\mathcal{U}_{J}\wedge V=\bigvee_{i\in J}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\wedge\mathcal{V})
for some
finite
J\subseteq I
, and therefore
x
belongs to

\bigvee_{i\in I}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\wedge\mathcal{V})
.

The second equality is already covered by Lemma
1.13
.
∎

In specific examples we may restrict to a sublattice

T\subseteq\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
which is closed under arbitrary joins, and then
look only at elements which commute with all other elements from
T
.
For instance,
T
could be the lattice of thick tensor ideals when

\mathcal{T}
is a tensor triangulated category; cf. Proposition
2.23
. The above Proposition
1.14

generalises to this situation; so the central elements in
T
form a
frame.

Example 1.15
.

For the category of perfect complexes over a commutative ring all
thick subcategories are central. One way of seeing this is that the
category of perfect complexes is tensor triangulated. Then one uses
that each thick subcategory is a tensor ideal, because the tensor
unit is generating, and that tensor ideals commute with each other
by Proposition
2.23
.

Example 1.16
.

Consider the path algebra
A=k(\circ\to\circ)
over a field
k
and
the bounded derived category
\mathcal{T}=\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}A)

of finitely generated
A
-modules, which identifies with the
category of perfect complexes over
A
. There are three
isoclasses of indecomposable
A
-modules and each generates a proper thick
subcategory. Thus the Hasse diagram of the
lattice of thick subcategories is the following:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\circ}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\circ}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\circ}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\circ}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\circ}

In particular, the lattice is not distributive. Central subcategories are
\{0\}
and
\mathcal{T}
.

2.
Support via central actions

We consider the action of a graded-commutative ring on a triangulated
category. This provides a notion of
cohomological support
for
objects, and we collect its basic properties. A useful tool are Koszul
objects. For a tensor triangulated category there is a natural notion of

tensor triangulated support
which is based on its spectrum of
prime ideals. For any ring action on a tensor triangulated category
there is a canonical comparison map which relates both notions of
support.

References are
[
2
,
6
,
15
]
.

Support for modules

Let
R
be a
graded-commutative ring
; thus
R
is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
and satisfies

rs=(-1)^{|r||s|}sr\quad\text{for}\quad r,s\in R,

where
|r|
denotes the degree of a homogeneous element
r
.
We consider only homogeneous elements or ideals
of
R
, and
\operatorname{Spec}R
denotes the set of homogeneous prime ideals of

R
with the Zariski topology. Analogously, only graded
R
-modules
are considered. We write
\operatorname{Mod}R
for the category of graded

R
-modules with morphisms given by degree zero
R
-linear maps.

For
R
-modules
M,N
we set

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}^{*}(M,N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N[i])\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{End}_{R}^{*}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}^{*}(M,M)\,.

Scalar
multiplication yields a homomorphism

\phi_{M}\colon R\to\operatorname{End}^{*}_{R}(M)
such that the induced left and right
actions of
R
on
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}^{*}(M,N)
are compatible: For any
r\in R

and
\alpha\in\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R}(M,N)
, one has

\phi_{N}(r)\alpha=(-1)^{|r||\alpha|}\alpha\phi_{M}(r)\,.

For a multiplicative set
S\subseteq R
of homogeneous elements we
write
R[S^{-1}]
for the localisation and set

M[S^{-1}]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}M\otimes_{R}R[S^{-1}]
for any
R
-module

M
. This yields an exact functor
\operatorname{Mod}R\to\operatorname{Mod}R[S^{-1}]
, and an

R
-module is
S
-local
if the canonical map
M\to M[S^{-1}]

is an isomorphism.

For
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
set
R_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R[(R\smallsetminus\mathfrak{p})^{-1}]

and
M_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}M\otimes_{R}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for any
R
-module
M
. An

R
-module
M
is called
\mathfrak{p}
-local
if the canonical map

M\to M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is an isomorphism.

Definition 2.1
.

The
support
of an
R
-module
M
is the
subset

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\mid M_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0\}.

The support of a module is a subset
V\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
that is

specialisation closed
, so for primes
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
we have
that
\mathfrak{p}\in V
implies
\mathfrak{q}\in V
. Further basic properties of

\operatorname{supp}_{R}
are derived from the fact that
(-)_{\mathfrak{p}}
is exact and
preserves all coproducts.

For a multiplicative set
S\subseteq R
of homogeneous elements the
canonical map
R\to R[S^{-1}]
identifies
\operatorname{Spec}R[S^{-1}]
with the
subset
\{\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\mid\mathfrak{p}\cap S=\varnothing\}
. In particular,
for
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
the canonical map
R\to R_{\mathfrak{p}}
identifies

\operatorname{Spec}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
with the subset
\{\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\mid\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\}
. This has the following consequence.

Lemma 2.2
.

For an
R
-module
M
, we have

M[S^{-1}]=0\quad\iff\quad\operatorname{supp}_{R}(M)\subseteq\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\mid\mathfrak{p}\cap S\neq\varnothing\}.\qed

For an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
set

V(\mathfrak{a})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\mid\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\}.

If
\mathfrak{a}
is
generated by a single element
r
we write
V(r)
for
V(\mathfrak{a})
. Let

\operatorname{ann}_{R}(M)
denote the kernel of the canonical map
R\to\operatorname{End}^{*}_{R}(M)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

We have
\operatorname{supp}_{R}(M)\subseteq V(\operatorname{ann}_{R}(M))
, and equality holds when

M
is finitely generated.∎

An
R
-module
M
is called
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion
if for each
homogeneous
x\in M
we have
\mathfrak{a}^{n}x=0
for
n\gg 0
. An equivalent
condition is
\operatorname{supp}_{R}(N)\subseteq V(\mathfrak{a})
for each finitely generated
submodule
N\subseteq M
, which means that
\operatorname{supp}_{R}(M)\subseteq V(\mathfrak{a})
.

Let
\alpha\colon R\to S
be a homomorphism of graded-commutative
rings. The assignment
\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\alpha^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})
yields a map

\alpha^{*}\colon\operatorname{Spec}S\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}R

which is also denoted by
\operatorname{Spec}\alpha
.

Lemma 2.4
.

¡ We have
\operatorname{supp}_{R}(S)=V(\operatorname{Ker}\alpha)
.

Proof.

This follows from the above lemma. For one inclusion observe that

\operatorname{Ker}\alpha=\operatorname{ann}_{R}(S)
. For the other inclusion observe that

R/(\operatorname{Ker}\alpha)
identifies with an
R
-submodule of
S
.
∎

The graded centre

For a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
with suspension
\varSigma\colon\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{T}
we introduce its graded centre and actions of graded-commutative rings.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
R
be a graded-commutative ring. We say that the triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
is

R
-linear
, or that
R

acts
on
\mathcal{T}
, if for each

x
in
\mathcal{T}
there is a homomorphism of graded rings

\phi_{x}\colon R\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(x)

such that the induced left and right actions of
R
on
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(x,y)
are compatible: For any
r\in R
and

\alpha\in\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,y)
, one has

\phi_{y}(r)\alpha=(-1)^{|r||\alpha|}\alpha\phi_{x}(r)\,.

This means that the representable functors
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,-)
and

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,y)
are
R
-linear cohomological functors
\mathcal{T}\to\operatorname{Mod}R
.

There is an alternative way to describe a central ring action on

\mathcal{T}
via its centre.

Definition 2.6
.

The
graded centre
of
\mathcal{T}
is the graded-commutative ring

Z^{*}(\mathcal{T})=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\{\eta\colon\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\varSigma^{n}\mid\eta\varSigma=(-1)^{n}\varSigma\eta\}

with multiplication given by
composition of morphisms in
\mathcal{T}
. Note that
Z^{*}(\mathcal{T})
may not
be a set.

If the ring
R
acts on
\mathcal{T}
via homomorphisms

\phi_{x}\colon R\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(x)
, then this yields a
homomorphism
\phi\colon R\to Z^{*}(\mathcal{T})
by setting

\phi(r)_{x}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\phi_{x}(r)
for
x\in\mathcal{T}
and
r\in R
. Conversely, any homomorphism
R\to Z^{*}(\mathcal{T})
gives an

R
-action on
\mathcal{T}
.

For example, one has a homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}\to Z^{*}(\mathcal{T})
which sends
n\in\mathbb{Z}

to
n\cdot\mathrm{id}\colon\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\varSigma^{0}
. Thus any triangulated
category is
\mathbb{Z}
-linear.

Central localisation

Let
R
be a graded-commutative ring and
\mathcal{T}
an
R
-linear
triangulated category. For a multiplicative set
S\subseteq R
of
homogeneous elements we define a triangulated
category
\mathcal{T}[S^{-1}]
as follows. The objects are the same as in

\mathcal{T}
. For the morphisms set

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}[S^{-1}]}(x,y)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,y)[S^{-1}].

The exact triangles in
\mathcal{T}[S^{-1}]
are
given by the ones isomorphic to images of exact triangles in
\mathcal{T}

under the canonical functor
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}[S^{-1}]
. The kernel of this
functor is the thick subcategory

\mathcal{T}_{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in\mathcal{T}\mid\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(x)[S^{-1}]=0\}

and it is not difficult to check that
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}[S^{-1}]

induces a triangle equivalence

\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{T}_{S}\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\mathcal{T}[S^{-1}].

The notation
x\mapsto x[S^{-1}]
is used for the canonical functor
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}[S^{-1}]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

An
R
-linear cohomological functor
H\colon\mathcal{T}\to\operatorname{Mod}R
factors through the
canonical functor
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}[S^{-1}]
if and only if
H(x)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}H(x)[S^{-1}]
for all
x\in\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

For any pair
x,y\in\mathcal{T}
we have the following commutative square:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,y)[S^{-1}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R}(H(x),H(y))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R}(H(x),H(y))[S^{-1}]}

The assertion follows from the fact that the map
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,y)\to\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R}(H(x),H(y))
induced by
H
factors through
the top horizontal map if and only if the bottom horizontal map is invertible.
∎

A special case of the above construction arises for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
denote the triangulated category given by
localising the morphisms at
\mathfrak{p}
,
so

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}}(x,y)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,y)_{\mathfrak{p}}.

The notation
x\mapsto x_{\mathfrak{p}}
is used for the canonical functor
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

The next lemma provides a supply of central subcategories.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
R
be a graded-commutative ring
acting on
\mathcal{T}
and
S\subseteq R
a multiplicative subset. Then

\mathcal{T}_{S}
is a central thick subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

To simplify notation we set
\varGamma_{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{T}_{S}}
and

L_{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}L_{\mathcal{T}_{S}}
. Let
F\in\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}
. Then
F(x)
is an

R
-module for all
x\in\mathcal{T}
and we have an isomorphism

(L_{S}F)\cong F[S^{-1}].

This holds for a
representable functor
F=\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,x)
by the definition of
\mathcal{T}[S^{-1}]
, and the general case follows since
L_{S}
preserves
filtered colimits.

Now fix a thick subcategory

Koszul objects

Let
R
be a graded-commutative ring and
\mathcal{T}
an
R
-linear
triangulated category.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
r\in R
be homogeneous of degree
d
and
x
an object in

\mathcal{T}
. A
Koszul object of
r
on
x
is any object

{x}/\!\!/{r}
that appears in an exact triangle

x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\longrightarrow}}\varSigma^{d}x\longrightarrow{x}/\!\!/{r}\longrightarrow\varSigma x\,.

For a sequence of elements
\mathbf{r}=r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
in
R
, consider
objects
x_{i}
defined by

x_{i}=\begin{cases}x&\text{for $i=0$,}\\
{x_{i-1}}/\!\!/{r_{i}}&\text{for $i\geq 1$.}\end{cases}

Set
{x}/\!\!/{\mathbf{r}}=x_{n}
; this is a
Koszul object
of
\mathbf{r}
on
x
.
A Koszul object is well defined up to (nonunique) isomorphism.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
r\in R
and set
S=\{r^{i}\mid i\geq 0\}
. If

\mathcal{T}=\operatorname{thick}(\mathcal{X})
for some class
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
,
then we have

\mathcal{T}_{S}=\operatorname{thick}(\{{x}/\!\!/{r}\mid x\in\mathcal{X}\}).

Proof.

We use the fact that a thick subcategory
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
is
determined by the class of
R
-linear cohomological functors

H\colon\mathcal{T}\to\operatorname{Mod}R
such that
H(\mathcal{U})=0
. For
x\in\mathcal{T}

let
r_{x}
denote the morphism
x\to\varSigma^{d}x
given by
multiplication with
r
, where
d=|r|
. Given a cohomological functor

H\colon\mathcal{T}\to\operatorname{Mod}R
, the exact triangle defining
{x}/\!\!/{r}

induces an exact sequence

\cdots\longrightarrow H(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\longrightarrow}}H(x)[d]\longrightarrow H({x}/\!\!/{r})\longrightarrow H(x)[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots

and therefore
H({x}/\!\!/{r})=0
if and only if
H(r_{x})
is
invertible. Now
H(r_{x})
is invertible for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
if and
only if
H(x)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}H(x)[S^{-1}]
for all
x\in\mathcal{T}
. From
Lemma
2.7
it follows that the latter condition is
equivalent to
H
factoring through the canonical functor

\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}[S^{-1}]
, which means that
H
annihilates

\mathcal{T}_{S}
.
∎

For a finitely generated ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
set

\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in\mathcal{T}\mid x_{\mathfrak{p}}=0\text{ for }\mathfrak{p}\in(\operatorname{Spec}R)\smallsetminus V(\mathfrak{a})\}.

Choose a sequence of homogeneous generators

\mathbf{r}=r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
of
\mathfrak{a}
and set
{x}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{x}/\!\!/{\mathbf{r}}
. This Koszul object depends on the choice of generators but
the thick subcategory generated by it does not.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=\operatorname{thick}(\mathcal{X})
for some class
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
.
Then we have

\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}=\operatorname{thick}(\{{x}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}}\mid x\in\mathcal{X}\}).

Moreover,
\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}
is a central thick subcategory.

Proof.

For
r\in R
and
S=\{r^{i}\mid i\geq 0\}
Lemma
2.10
implies

\mathcal{T}_{V(r)}=\mathcal{T}_{S}=\operatorname{thick}(\{{x}/\!\!/{r}\mid x\in\mathcal{X}\})\,.

Now choose a sequence of homogeneous generators
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
of

\mathfrak{a}
, so

V(\mathfrak{a})=V(r_{1})\cap\cdots\cap V(r_{n}).

Let
\mathfrak{b}
denote the ideal
generated by
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n-1}
. Then

\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}=(\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{b})})_{V(r_{n})}
and an induction on

yields the assertion, since
{x}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}}={({x}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{b}})}/\!\!/{r_{n}}
.

Finally, we have

\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}=\mathcal{T}_{V(r_{1})}\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{T}_{V(r_{n})}

and therefore

is central by
Proposition
1.14
and Lemma
2.8
.
∎

Cohomological support

Let
R
be a graded-commutative ring and
\mathcal{T}
an
R
-linear
triangulated category. The subsets
V(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
given by finitely generated
ideals form a distributive lattice with

V(\mathfrak{a})\cap V(\mathfrak{b})=V(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b})\qquad\text{and}\qquad V(\mathfrak{a})\cup V(\mathfrak{b})=V(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}).

Lemma 2.12
.

For
U=V(\mathfrak{a})
and
V=V(\mathfrak{b})
we have

\mathcal{T}_{U\cap V}=\mathcal{T}_{U}\wedge\mathcal{T}_{V}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{T}_{U\cup V}=\mathcal{T}_{U}\vee\mathcal{T}_{V}.

Proof.

The first equality is clear. For the second equality choose
homogeneous generators

r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}
of
\mathfrak{a}
and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
of
\mathfrak{b}
such that

V(\mathfrak{a})=V(r_{1})\cap\cdots\cap V(r_{m})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\text{V}(\mathfrak{b})=V(s_{1})\cap\cdots\cap V(s_{n}).

For a homogeneous element

r\in R
one has

\mathcal{T}_{V(r)}=\operatorname{thick}(\{{x}/\!\!/{r}\mid x\in\mathcal{T}\})

by Lemma
2.10
. For homogeneous elements
r,s\in R
an
application of the octahedral axiom shows that
{x}/\!\!/{rs}
is an
extension of
{x}/\!\!/{r}
and
{\varSigma^{|r|}x}/\!\!/{s}
. Thus

\mathcal{T}_{V(r)\cup V(s)}=\mathcal{T}_{V(rs)}=\mathcal{T}_{V(r)}\vee\mathcal{T}_{V(s)}.

We may assume that
\mathcal{T}
is essentially small
because for
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
we have
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}=\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}\cap\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. Thus applying the distributivity discussed in
Proposition
1.14
together with Lemma
2.8
we
obtain

\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}\vee\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{b})}=\bigwedge_{i,j}(\mathcal{T}_{V(r_{i})}\vee\mathcal{T}_{V(s_{j})})=\bigwedge_{i,j}\mathcal{T}_{V(r_{i}s_{j})}=\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b})}=\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a})\cup V(\mathfrak{b})}

since

V(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b})=\bigcap_{i,j}V(r_{i}s_{j}).\qed

Definition 2.13
.

A subset
V\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
is said to be
Thomason
if it can
be written as
V=\bigcup_{i}V_{i}
such that each

(\operatorname{Spec}R)\smallsetminus V_{i}
is Zariski open and quasi-compact. When
R

is noetherian, Thomason subsets are precisely the specialisation
closed subsets of
\operatorname{Spec}R
. In general, the Thomason subsets are
by definition the open subsets for the
Hochster dual topology

on
\operatorname{Spec}R
; see
[
15
]
.

Definition 2.14
.

The open subsets of any space form a
frame
, that is to say, a
complete lattice where arbitrary joins distribute over finite meets.
A morphism of frames is a map that preserves arbitrary joins and
finite meets. Given a frame
F
, one calls
x\in F

finite
or

compact
if
x\leq\bigvee_{i\in I}y_{i}
implies

x\leq\bigvee_{i\in J}y_{i}
for some finite subset
J\subseteq I
. A
frame is
coherent
when every element can be written as a join
of finite elements and the finite elements form a sublattice.

Example 2.15
.

The Thomason sets of
\operatorname{Spec}R
form a coherent frame and the finite
elements are precisely the sets of the form
V(\mathfrak{a})
given by a
finitely generated ideal of
R
.

We consider the lattice of thick subcategories in

\mathcal{T}
. For a Thomason subset
V\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
we set

\tau_{R}(V)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigvee_{V(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq V}\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a})},

where
\mathfrak{a}
runs through all finitely generated ideals of
R
.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an essentially small triangulated category and
R
a ring acting on
it. Then the assignment
V\mapsto\tau_{R}(V)
induces a lattice morphism

\{\text{Thomason subsets of }\operatorname{Spec}R\}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})\,.

which preserves arbitrary joins. Moreover, each
\tau_{R}(V)
is a
central thick subcategory.

Proof.

We use the fact that the map
V(\mathfrak{a})\mapsto\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}
is
a lattice morphism by Lemma
2.12
. Let
V=\bigcup_{i}V_{i}
be a
join of Thomason sets. We need to show that

\tau_{R}\left(\bigcup_{i}V_{i}\right)\subseteq\bigvee_{i}\tau_{R}(V_{i});

the other inclusion is automatic. Let
V(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq V
. Then there is a finite set of finitely
generated ideals
\mathfrak{a}_{i}
such that
V(\mathfrak{a}_{i})\subseteq V_{i}
and

V(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\bigcup_{i}V(\mathfrak{a}_{i})
. Thus

\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}\subseteq\tau_{R}\left(\bigcup_{i}V(\mathfrak{a}_{i})\right)=\bigvee_{i}\tau_{R}(V(\mathfrak{a}_{i}))\subseteq\bigvee_{i}\tau_{R}(V_{i}).

Now choose Thomason sets

. We need to show that

\tau_{R}(V_{1})\wedge\tau_{R}(V_{2})\subseteq\tau_{R}(V_{1}\cap V_{2});

the other inclusion is automatic. For any object
x\in\tau_{R}(V_{1})\wedge\tau_{R}(V_{2})
we find finitely
generated ideals
\mathfrak{a}_{i}
such that
V(\mathfrak{a}_{i})\subseteq V_{i}
and

x\in\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a}_{1})}\wedge\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a}_{2})}=\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a}_{1}+\mathfrak{a}_{2})}\subseteq\tau_{R}(V_{1}\cap V_{2}),

since
V(\mathfrak{a}_{1}+\mathfrak{a}_{2})=V(\mathfrak{a}_{1})\cap V(\mathfrak{a}_{2})\subseteq V_{1}\cap V_{2}
.

The final assertion follows from Propositions
1.14
and
2.11
.
∎

In view of the next definition it is useful to recall for

and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R

\displaystyle x_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0

\displaystyle\iff\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,x)_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0

\displaystyle\iff\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(x)_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0

\displaystyle\iff\mathfrak{p}\in V(\operatorname{Ker}\phi_{x})

where the last equivalence follows Lemma
2.4
.

Definition 2.17
.

For an object

the
cohomological support
is

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\mid x_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0\}.

For
a class of objects
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
set

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(\mathcal{X})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x).

We say that
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is
finite
if for each
x\in\mathcal{T}
there
is a finitely generated ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
such that

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)=V(\mathfrak{a})
.

The cohomological support is a specialisation closed subset of

\operatorname{Spec}R
, but not necessarily a Thomason set. Also,
\operatorname{supp}_{R}

preserves arbitrary joins in
\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})
, but not necessarily
finite meets. The following lemma explains the finiteness condition.

Lemma 2.18
.

The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The cohomological support
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is finite.

(2)

The maps
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
and
\tau_{R}
provide an adjoint pair

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\text{Thomason subsets of }\operatorname{Spec}R\}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{supp}_{R}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{R}}

In this case one has for each Thomason set

\tau_{R}(V)=\{x\in\mathcal{T}\mid\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)\subseteq V\}.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is finite. Let
V
be Thomason and

x\in\mathcal{T}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)\subseteq V

\displaystyle\iff\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)\subseteq V(\mathfrak{a})\text{ for some }V(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq V

\displaystyle\iff x\in\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}\text{ for some }V(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq V

\displaystyle\iff x\in\tau_{R}(V).

The first equivalence uses the assumption on
\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)
and the
other ones are immediate from the definitions.

Now suppose there is an adjoint pair of maps. This implies that

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)
is Thomason for each
x\in\mathcal{T}
. A standard argument
shows that the left adjoint preserves finiteness if the right
adjoint preserves all joins. It remains to note that finiteness in

\operatorname{Spec}R
means that there
is a finitely generated ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
such that

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)=V(\mathfrak{a})
.
∎

Example 2.19
.

Let
R
be noetherian ring. Then for any
R
-linear triangulated
category the cohomological support
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is finite.

Lemma 2.20
.

For a finitely generated ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
and
x\in\mathcal{T}
we have

\operatorname{supp}_{R}({x}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}})=\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)\cap V(\mathfrak{a}).

Proof.

The inclusion

follows from
Proposition
2.11
. For the other inclusion we use an
induction on the number of generators of
\mathfrak{a}
, as in the proof of
Proposition
2.11
. Thus we claim for

that

\operatorname{supp}_{R}({x}/\!\!/{r})\supseteq\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)\cap V(r)
. Choose
\mathfrak{p}
in

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)\cap V(r)
. If
({x}/\!\!/{r})_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
, then the canonical map

sends

to a unit, which is impossible since

.
∎

Proposition 2.21
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an essentially small triangulated category and
R
a ring acting on
it. Then cohomological support induces a lattice isomorphism

\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T})\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\{\text{Thomason subsets of }\operatorname{Spec}R\}

if and only if the following holds:

(1)

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(\mathcal{T})=\operatorname{Spec}R
;

(2)

\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is finite;

(3)

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{R}(y)
implies

\operatorname{thick}(x)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}(y)
for all

.

Proof.

The forward direction is clear. For the other direction we claim

\text{(1)--(2)}\implies{\operatorname{supp}_{R}}\circ\tau_{R}=\mathrm{id}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\text{(2)--(3)}\implies\tau_{R}\circ{\operatorname{supp}_{R}}=\mathrm{id}.

First suppose that (1)–(2) hold. Then

\operatorname{Spec}R=\operatorname{supp}_{R}(\mathcal{T})=\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{T}}\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)

implies
that there is an object

with
\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x_{0})=\operatorname{Spec}R
, since
\operatorname{Spec}R
(with
the Hochster dual topology) is quasi-compact. This implies

\operatorname{supp}_{R}({x_{0}}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}})=V(\mathfrak{a})
for any ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of

by

. Thus
\operatorname{supp}_{R}\tau_{R}=\mathrm{id}
.
Now suppose that (2)–(3) hold. This implies for

that

, and therefore

. Thus (1)–(3) imply that
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
induces a lattice isomorphism.
∎

.

Tensor triangulated support

Let
\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{T},\otimes,\mathds{1})
be a
tensor triangulated
category
. Thus the triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
admits a closed
symmetric monoidal structure

\otimes\colon\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
. In particular, there are
natural isomorphisms
x\otimes y\cong y\otimes x
, which we may treat
as identities, and a unit
\mathds{1}
for the tensor product, so

\mathds{1}\otimes x\cong x
. Moreover, there exists an adjoint

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}\colon\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
such that

(2.1)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(x\otimes y,z)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(y,z)).

The functors
\otimes
and
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}
are assumed to be exact in each
variable.

An object
x
is
dualisable
if for each

y\in\mathcal{T}
the natural map

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(x,\mathds{1})\otimes y\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(x,y)

is an isomorphism. We assume that the category
\mathcal{T}
is

rigid
, which means that every object is dualisable. Finally,
assume that
\mathcal{T}
is essentially small and let

\operatorname{Thick}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{T})
denote the lattice of thick tensor ideals of

\mathcal{T}
.

Proposition 2.23
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{T},\otimes,\mathds{1})
be an essentially small rigid tensor triangulated
category. Then any pair of thick tensor ideals is commuting and

\operatorname{Thick}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{T})
is a frame.

Proof.

Fix a pair of thick tensor ideal
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}
. For
x\in\mathcal{T}

we set
Dx\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(x,\mathds{1})
. Given
u\in\mathcal{U}
and
v\in\mathcal{V}
, any
morphism
\phi\colon u\to v
admits a factorisation

u\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathds{1}\otimes u\xrightarrow{\text{coev}\otimes u}u\otimes Du\otimes u\xrightarrow{\phi\otimes Du\otimes u}v\otimes Du\otimes u\xrightarrow{v\otimes\text{ev}}v\otimes\mathds{1}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}v

with
v\otimes Du\otimes u
in
\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{V}
. By symmetry
the same holds for a morphism
v\to u
. Thus the pair

\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}
is commuting. The (infinite) distributivity now
follows, as in the proof of Proposition
1.14
.
∎

Remark 2.24
.

(1) For Proposition
2.23
the assumption on the tensor
triangulated category to be rigid is needed. On the other hand, the
commutativity of the tensor product is not needed.

(2) There are examples of thick subcategories
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
such that
\mathcal{U}
is tensor ideal but not
central, and conversely that
\mathcal{U}
is central but not tensor ideal.

Definition 2.25
.

A thick tensor ideal
\mathcal{P}\subsetneq\mathcal{T}
is
prime
if

x\otimes y\in\mathcal{P}
implies
x\in\mathcal{P}
or
y\in\mathcal{P}
for each
pair
x,y\in\mathcal{T}
. The
Balmer spectrum
of
\mathcal{T}
is the
set
\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}
of prime thick tensor ideals, endowed with the

Zariski topology
. The basic open sets are of the form

\{\mathcal{P}\in\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}\mid x\in\mathcal{P}\}
for some
x\in\mathcal{T}
.

Definition 2.26
.

For an object
x\in\mathcal{T}
its
tensor triangulated support
is

\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathcal{P}\in\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}\mid x\not\in\mathcal{P}\}\,.

[

.

Let
\mathcal{T}

\mathcal{T}

via

\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1})\longrightarrow Z^{*}(\mathcal{T}),\quad\alpha\longmapsto\alpha\otimes-.

Now fix a graded-commutative ring
R
that acts on
\mathcal{T}

canonically
, that is to say, via a map of rings

\phi\colon R\to\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1})
. In many cases
R
is the
graded endomorphism ring of
\mathds{1}
and
\phi=\mathrm{id}
, but not
always. The Balmer spectrum is related to the spectrum of the ring
R

that acts on
\mathcal{T}
, via the
comparison map

(2.3)

\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\ \rho_{\mathcal{T}}\ }\operatorname{Spec}\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1})\xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{Spec}\phi\ }\operatorname{Spec}R

where
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
takes a prime ideal
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
to
the preimage of the unique maximal ideal of

\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{P}}(\mathds{1})
under the canonical homomorphism

\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1})\to\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{P}}(\mathds{1})
; see

[
3
]
.

We wish to compare tensor triangulated and cohomological
support. This requires a sequence of lemmas.

Lemma 2.28
.

For objects
x,y
in
\mathcal{T}
we have

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x\otimes y)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)\cap\operatorname{supp}_{R}(y).

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
. Then
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)
if and only if

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,-)_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0
. From (
2.1
) it
follows that
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(x\otimes y,-)_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0
implies

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,-)_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0
.
∎

The lemma implies that for any subset
V\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
the thick
subcategory consisting of objects
x\in\mathcal{T}
with
\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)\subseteq V
is a
tensor ideal. In particular,
\tau_{R}(V)
is a tensor ideal when
V
is Thomason.

Lemma 2.29
.

For a finitely generated ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
we have
\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}=\operatorname{thick}_{\otimes}({\mathds{1}}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}})
.

Proof.

The equality

\mathcal{T}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}=\operatorname{thick}(\{{x}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}}\mid x\in\mathcal{T}\})
follows from
Proposition
2.11
and it remains
to note that
{x}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}}={\mathds{1}}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}}\otimes x
.
∎

Lemma 2.30
.

For
r\in\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1})
we have
\rho^{-1}_{\mathcal{T}}(V(r))=\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}({\mathds{1}}/\!\!/{r})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}\in\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}
. Then
\mathcal{P}\in\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}({\mathds{1}}/\!\!/{r})
if and only if the morphism

\mathds{1}\xrightarrow{r}\varSigma^{|r|}\mathds{1}
is not invertible in
\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{P}
. This
means
r\in\rho_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{P})
, so
\mathcal{P}\in\rho^{-1}_{\mathcal{T}}(V(r))
.
∎

The following result provides a comparison between cohomological and
tensor triangulated support.

Proposition 2.31
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an essentially small rigid tensor triangulated
category. Then the comparison map (
2.3
) induces a
commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\text{Thomason subsets of }\operatorname{Spec}R\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{T})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\text{Thomason subsets of }\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{T})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{R}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho^{-1}_{\mathcal{T}}(\operatorname{Spec}\phi)^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\otimes}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

In particular, the comparison map (
2.3
) is a homeomorphism if and only if the
map
\tau_{R}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The map
\rho^{-1}_{\mathcal{T}}
is given by taking preimages along

\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
, and
(\operatorname{Spec}\phi)^{-1}
is defined analogously. We
need to check that these maps are well defined and that they make
the diagram commutative. First observe that for any
r\in R
the
preimage of
V(r)
under
\operatorname{Spec}\phi
equals
V(\phi(r))
, and its
preimage under
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
equals
\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}({\mathds{1}}/\!\!/{r})

by Lemma
2.30
. Thus

(\rho^{-1}_{\mathcal{T}}(\operatorname{Spec}\phi)^{-1})(V(\mathfrak{a}))=\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}({\mathds{1}}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}})

for any finitely generated ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
, since

\rho^{-1}_{\mathcal{T}}(\operatorname{Spec}\phi)^{-1}
and
\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}
preserve finite
intersections. Using Lemma
2.29
this yields the
commutativity, keeping in mind that all maps in the diagram preserve
arbitrary joins. The diagram implies that
\tau_{R}
is a bijection if
and only if
\rho^{-1}_{\mathcal{T}}(\operatorname{Spec}\phi)^{-1}
is a bijection. Since the
spaces in question are sober, so determined up to homeomorphism by
the lattice of open subsets,
\rho^{-1}_{\mathcal{T}}(\operatorname{Spec}\phi)^{-1}
is a
bijection if and only if
(\operatorname{Spec}\phi)\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
is a
homeomorphism.
∎

The tensor triangulated analogue of Proposition
2.21
is a consequence.

Corollary 2.32
.

For an essentially small rigid tensor triangulated category
\mathcal{T}

and a ring
R
acting on it the following are equivalent.

(1)

Cohomological support induces a lattice isomorphism

\operatorname{Thick}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{T})\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\{\text{Thomason subsets of }\operatorname{Spec}R\}.

(2)

The comparison map (
2.3
) is a homeomorphism.

(3)

We have
\operatorname{supp}_{R}(\mathds{1})=\operatorname{Spec}R
,
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is finite, and for all
x,y\in\mathcal{T}

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{R}(y)\quad\implies\quad\operatorname{thick}_{\otimes}(x)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}_{\otimes}(y).

Proof.

We apply Proposition
2.31
. The equivalence (1)

\Leftrightarrow
(2) uses the adjointness of the pair

(\operatorname{supp}_{R},\tau_{R})
; see Lemma
2.18
. For the
equivalence (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(3) one adapts the proof of
Proposition
2.21
.
∎

The prototypical example is the category of perfect complexes over a
commutative ring
A
, on which
A
acts canonically.

Example 2.33
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring. The classification of thick tensor
ideals of the category of perfect complexes via
\operatorname{Spec}A
means that
the equivalent conditions in Corollary
2.32
are satisfied;
it is due to Hopkins, Neeman, and Thomason

[
16
,
20
,
25
]
.

3.
Local cohomology and support

We study compactly generated triangulated categories and the basic
properties of localising subcategories that are generated by compact
objects. Local cohomology functors are introduced when the category
of compact objects is fibred over a space, and this provides a notion
of support.

References are
[
6
,
17
]
.

Localisation and adjoints

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category and
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
a triangulated
subcategory. Suppose that the canonical functor
q\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}

admits a right adjoint
q_{\rho}\colon\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{T}
.
Then
q_{\rho}
is fully
faithful and induces an equivalence

\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\qquad\text{with quasi-inverse}\qquad\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\ q\ }\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S},

where

\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in\mathcal{T}\mid\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(s,x)=0\text{ for all
}s\in\mathcal{S}\}

and

{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in\mathcal{T}\mid\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,s)=0\text{ for all
}s\in\mathcal{S}\}\,.

The
unit of the adjunction yields for
x
in
\mathcal{T}
an exact triangle

x^{\prime}\longrightarrow x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eta}}{{\longrightarrow}}q_{\rho}q(x)\longrightarrow\varSigma x^{\prime}

with
x^{\prime}
a direct summand of an object in
\mathcal{S}
since
q(\eta)
is
invertible.

The following proposition expresses the symmetry which arises from
localising a triangulated category with respect to a thick
subcategory; see for example
[
18
, Proposition 3.2.8]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
be a thick subcategory. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The canonical functor
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}
admits a right adjoint.

(2)

The inclusion
\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{T}
admits a right adjoint.

(3)

For each
x\in\mathcal{T}
there exists an exact triangle

x^{\prime}\to x\to x^{\prime\prime}\to\varSigma x^{\prime}
with
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
and
x^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{\perp}
.

(4)

The composite
\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{T}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}
is a triangle equivalence.

In that case the right
adjoint
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{S}
induces a triangle
equivalence

\mathcal{T}/(\mathcal{S}^{\perp})\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\mathcal{S}\qquad\text{and}\qquad^{\perp}(\mathcal{S}^{\perp})=\mathcal{S}.\qed

We capture the situation in the following diagram

(3.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{\rho}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{\rho}}

which is a localisation sequence. We set

\varGamma_{\mathcal{S}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}i\circ i_{\rho}\qquad\text{and}\qquad L_{\mathcal{S}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}q_{\rho}\circ q\,.

Then the adjunctions yield for each object
x\in\mathcal{T}
an
exact triangle

(3.2)

\varGamma_{\mathcal{S}}(x)\longrightarrow x\longrightarrow L_{\mathcal{S}}(x)\longrightarrow\varSigma\varGamma_{\mathcal{S}}(x).

In particular, there are natural isomorphisms

\varGamma_{\mathcal{S}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{S}}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\varGamma_{\mathcal{S}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad L_{\mathcal{S}}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}L_{\mathcal{S}}L_{\mathcal{S}}\,.

Compactly generated triangulated categories

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category that admits arbitrary
coproducts. An object
x
in
\mathcal{T}
is called
compact
(or

small
) if for any morphism

\phi\colon x\to\coprod_{i\in I}y_{i}
in
\mathcal{T}
there is a finite
set
J\subseteq I
such that
\phi
factors through

\coprod_{i\in J}y_{i}
. It is easily checked that
x
is compact if and
only if the canonical map

\coprod_{i\in I}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,y_{i})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,\coprod_{i\in I}y_{i})

is bijective for all coproducts
\coprod_{i\in I}y_{i}
in
\mathcal{T}
. It
follows that the compact objects form a thick subcategory of
\mathcal{T}

which is denoted by
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
.

Definition 3.2
.

A set
\mathcal{C}
of compact objects is called
compactly
generating
if
\mathcal{T}
has no proper localising subcategory
containing
\mathcal{C}
. In this case
\mathcal{T}
is called
compactly
generated
.

We record some basic facts. For the following, see

[
18
, Proposition 3.4.15]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be compactly generated by a set
\mathcal{C}
of compact
objects. Then

\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}=\operatorname{thick}(\mathcal{C})\,.\qed

We consider the
restricted Yoneda functor

h_{\mathcal{T}}\colon\mathcal{T}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}},\quad x\mapsto h_{x}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,x)|_{\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}}

which is in general neither full nor
faithful. Nonetheless, it is a useful tool. For instance, the functor

reflects isomorphisms
, so a morphism
\phi
in
\mathcal{T}
is an
isomorphism if
h_{\mathcal{T}}(\phi)
is an isomorphism.

Let us fix an adjoint pair of exact functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

between triangulated categories that admit arbitrary coproducts, and
suppose that
\mathcal{T}
is compactly generated. Then the following
holds.

Lemma 3.4
.

The left adjoint
f
preserves compactness if and only the right
adjoint
g
preserves all coproducts.∎

Proof.

Fix objects
x\in\mathcal{T}
and
\coprod_{i\in I}y_{i}\in\mathcal{U}
, and suppose that
x
is
compact. We consider the following commutative diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\coprod_{i}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{U}}(f(x),y_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{U}}(f(x),\coprod_{i}y_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\coprod_{i}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,g(y_{i}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,\coprod_{i}g(y_{i}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,g\left(\coprod_{i}y_{i}\right))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wr}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wr}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}

Suppose that
g
preserves coproducts. Then
\beta
is an isomorphism,
and therefore
\alpha
is an isomorphism. Thus
f(x)
is compact. The
converse requires that the compact objects of
\mathcal{T}
are generating.
∎

Now assume that
f
preserves compactness. Thus there is an adjoint
pair of functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{c}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{*}}

where
f^{*}=(f^{\mathrm{c}})^{*}
and
f_{*}=(f^{\mathrm{c}})_{*}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

The pair
(f,g)
induces the following square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Coh}{\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Coh}{\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{c}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{\mathcal{T}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{\mathcal{U}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{*}}

which commutes up to isomorphisms.

Proof.

For
x\in\mathcal{T}
we have

h_{\mathcal{T}}(x)=\operatorname{colim}_{C\to X}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,c)

where

c\to x
runs through all morphisms with
c\in\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
. From this we
get a functorial morphism

(f^{*}h_{\mathcal{T}})(x)=f^{*}(\operatorname{colim}_{C\to X}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,c))\cong\operatorname{colim}_{C\to X}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{U}}(-,f(c))\longrightarrow h_{\mathcal{U}}(f(x)).

The objects
x\in\mathcal{T}
where this is an isomorphism form a localising
subcategory which contains
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
. Thus
f^{*}h_{\mathcal{T}}\cong h_{\mathcal{U}}f
. On the other hand, we have for
y\in\mathcal{U}

(h_{\mathcal{T}}g)(y)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,g(y))|_{\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{U}}(f^{\mathrm{c}}-,y)=(f_{*}h_{\mathcal{U}})(y).\qed

The lattice of localising subcategories

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category and suppose that arbitrary
coproducts exists in
\mathcal{T}
. Let
\operatorname{Loc}(\mathcal{T})
denote the lattice
of localising subcategories of
\mathcal{T}
. This may not be a set, but
the operations
\vee
and
\wedge
are well defined. For a class of
objects
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
let
\operatorname{loc}(\mathcal{X})
denote the smallest
localising subcategory containing
\mathcal{X}
. Then for a family

(\mathcal{S}_{i})_{i\in I}
in
\operatorname{Loc}(\mathcal{T})
we have

\bigwedge_{i\in I}\mathcal{S}_{i}=\bigcap_{i\in I}\mathcal{S}_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{S}_{i}=\operatorname{loc}\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{S}_{i}\right).

Now assume that
\mathcal{T}
is compactly generated. We are interest are
in the localising subcategories that are generated by compact objects.

Recall that an additive functor
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is an

equivalence up to direct summands
if the functor is fully
faithful and every object in
\mathcal{D}
is isomorphic to a direct
summand of an object in the image.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a compactly generated triangulated category and

\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
a localising subcategory that is generated by a set
of compact objects in
\mathcal{T}
.

(1)

There is a localisation sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}}

and the category
\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}
is compactly generated.

(2)

We have
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{c}}=\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
and the canonical functor

\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}\to\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}
induces a triangle equivalence up to direct
summands

(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}})/(\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{c}})\longrightarrow(\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S})^{\mathrm{c}}\,.

(3)

The functors
\varGamma_{\mathcal{S}}
and
L_{\mathcal{S}}
preserve all
coproducts.

Proof.

The category
\mathcal{S}
is compactly generated and therefore the
inclusion
\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{T}
admits a right adjoint, by Brown
representability. Thus we are in the situation of
Proposition
3.1
. We apply
Lemma
3.4
. The inclusion
\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{T}

preserves compactness and therefore the right adjoint preserves all
coproducts. It follows that the right adjoint of

\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}
preserves all coproducts, so

\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}
preserves compactness. Thus the canonical
functor
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}\to\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}
induces a functor

(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}})/(\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{c}})\to(\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S})^{\mathrm{c}}
, and a cofinality
argument shows that this is fully faithful. Its image is a full
triangulated subcategory consisting of compact objects generating

\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}
. Thus every compact object in
\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}
is a
direct summand of an object in this image, by
Lemma
3.3
. The same lemma yields that

\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{c}}=\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
.
∎

The assignments

\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}\supseteq\mathcal{C}\longmapsto\bar{\mathcal{C}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{loc}(\mathcal{C})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{T}\supseteq\mathcal{S}\longmapsto\operatorname{comp}(\mathcal{S})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}

induce an adjoint pair of order-preserving maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Loc}(\mathcal{T})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{loc}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{comp}}

which plays a significant role. We collect some basic facts. First
observe that
\bar{\mathcal{C}}
is generated by objects that are compact
in
\mathcal{T}
; so Proposition
3.6
applies.

Lemma 3.7
.

For a thick subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
the following holds.

(1)

\bar{\mathcal{C}}
consists of all objects
x\in\mathcal{T}
such that every morphism

\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}\ni c\to x
factors through an object in
\mathcal{C}
.

(2)

\bar{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{c}}=\mathcal{C}=\bar{\mathcal{C}}\cap\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
.

(3)

\bar{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
consists of all objects
x\in\mathcal{T}
such that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(c,x)=0
for all

c\in\mathcal{C}
.

(4)

\bar{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
is a localising subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

(1) Fix
x\in\mathcal{T}
. We may view
\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{C}
as a subcategory of

\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
. Then we have
x\in\bar{\mathcal{C}}
if and only if

h_{x}\in\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{C}
; see Lemma
3.5
. This condition
means that we can write
h_{x}=\operatorname{colim}h_{x_{i}}
with all

x_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
. From this the assertion follows.

(2) Apply Lemma
3.3
.

(3) Fix
x\in\mathcal{T}
. The kernel of
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,x)
is a
localising subcategory; so it contains
\bar{\mathcal{C}}
if it contains

\mathcal{C}
.

(4) From (3) it follows that
\bar{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
is closed under
coproducts.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

For a thick subcategory

\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
we have

\varGamma_{\mathcal{C}}\circ h_{\mathcal{T}}\cong h_{\mathcal{T}}\circ\varGamma_{\bar{\mathcal{C}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad L_{\mathcal{C}}\circ h_{\mathcal{T}}\cong h_{\mathcal{T}}\circ L_{\bar{\mathcal{C}}}\,.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
3.5
, applied to the pair of canonical functors

\bar{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}/\bar{\mathcal{C}}
.
∎

Next we show that the assignment
\mathcal{C}\mapsto\bar{\mathcal{C}}
preserves
arbitrary joins, while preserving meets requires an assumption.

Lemma 3.9
.

For any set of thick subcategories

\mathcal{C}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
we have

\operatorname{loc}\left(\bigvee_{i}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right)=\bigvee_{i}\operatorname{loc}(\mathcal{C}_{i}).

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
3.3
that both localising
subcategories have the same class of compact objects; so they agree.
∎

Proposition 3.10
.

For a pair of commuting thick subcategories

\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
of
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
there are isomorphisms

Let
\mathcal{T}

\mathcal{T}

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
Y=V\cap(X\smallsetminus U)
be locally closed. Then the functor

\varGamma_{Y}
and the localising subcategory
\mathcal{T}_{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{T}

not depend on the choice of
U
and
V
.

Proof.

We use that for any inclusion
V\subseteq U
we have

\varGamma_{V}L_{U}=0
. Let

V=(V\smallsetminus U)\cup(V\cap U)\subseteq V^{\prime}\cup U

L_{V^{\prime}}\varGamma_{V}L_{U}\cong\varGamma_{V}L_{V^{\prime}\cup U}=0
. Using the triangle
(
3.2
) given by

\varGamma_{V^{\prime}}\varGamma_{V}L_{U}\cong\varGamma_{V}L_{U}\,.

Analogously,
U^{\prime}\cap V\subseteq U
and therefore

\varGamma_{V}L_{U}\varGamma_{U^{\prime}}\cong\varGamma_{U^{\prime}\cap V}L_{U}=0
, which implies

\varGamma_{V}L_{U}L_{U^{\prime}}\cong\varGamma_{V}L_{U}\,.

We conclude that

\varGamma_{V^{\prime}}L_{U^{\prime}}\cong\varGamma_{V}\varGamma_{V^{\prime}}L_{U^{\prime}}L_{U}\cong\varGamma_{V^{\prime}}\varGamma_{V}L_{U}L_{U^{\prime}}\cong\varGamma_{V}L_{U}\,.

\varGamma_{Y}
does not depend on the choice of
U
and
V
. For

if and only

.
It follows that
\mathcal{T}_{Y}
does not depend on the choice of

and

.

Let us collect a few basic properties of the functor

\mathcal{T}

\mathcal{T}

\mathcal{T}[S^{-1}]

is compact, and holds for
an arbitrary object because it can be built from compacts using
coproducts, suspensions, and extensions. The same argument is used to
show that
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(c,x)
is
S
-local when
x\in\mathcal{T}[S^{-1}]
.
The assertion about objects in
\mathcal{T}_{S}
implies
that applying

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(c,-)[S^{-1}]

to the exact triangle

\varGamma_{S}(x)\to x\to x[S^{-1}]\to
induces an
isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(c,x)[S^{-1}]\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(c,x[S^{-1}])[S^{-1}].

The assertion about objects in
\mathcal{T}[S^{-1}]
implies
that

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(c,x[S^{-1}])\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(c,x[S^{-1}])[S^{-1}].

(2) One implication has already been shown. So it remains to show that

x\in\mathcal{T}[S^{-1}]
when
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(c,x)
is
S
-local for
all
c\in\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
. For this it suffices to show that
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(c,x)=0

when
c\in\mathcal{T}_{S}
. But this is clear since any

S
-local
R
-module with support in
\operatorname{Spec}R\smallsetminus\operatorname{Spec}R[S^{-1}]
is zero.

(3) This follows from (1).
∎

Lemma 3.28
.

Let
V=V(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
be given by a finitely generated
ideal
\mathfrak{a}
. Then
x\in\mathcal{T}
belongs to
\mathcal{T}_{V}
if and only if

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(c,x))\subseteq V\quad\text{for all}\quad c\in\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}.

Proof.

Let
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
be generators of
\mathfrak{a}
, so

V(\mathfrak{a})=V(r_{1})\cap\cdots\cap V(r_{n}).

The case
n=1
follows from Lemma
3.27
, and then
the general case by an induction on
n
using Proposition
3.18
.
∎

Proposition 3.29
.

Suppose that
R
is noetherian and let
x\in\mathcal{T}
. Then

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)=\varnothing\quad\implies\quad x=0.

Proof.

Let
x\neq 0
and choose a prime
\mathfrak{p}
that is minimal in

\bigcup_{c\in\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}}\operatorname{supp}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(c,x)).

This exists
since
R
is noetherian. Then it follows from
Lemmas
3.27
and
3.28
that

\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)=x_{p}\neq 0
, because

\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)=\varGamma_{V(\mathfrak{p})}(x_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Here, one uses that a

\mathfrak{p}
-local
R
-module
M
has
\operatorname{supp}_{R}(M)\subseteq V(\mathfrak{p})
if
M_{\mathfrak{q}}=0
for each
prime
\mathfrak{q}
strictly contained in
\mathfrak{p}
.
∎

Corollary 3.30
.

Suppose that
R
is noetherian and let
V\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
be a
Thomason subset. Then for
x\in\mathcal{T}
the following are
equivalent.

(1)

x\in\mathcal{T}_{V}
.

(2)

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)\subseteq V
.

(3)

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(c,x))\subseteq V
for all
c\in\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
.

Proof.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2) Combine Lemma
3.25
and
Proposition
3.29
.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(3) Apply Lemma
3.28
.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1) Consider the exact triangle

\varGamma_{V}(x)\to x\to y\to
. We need to show that
y=0
. First
observe that
\operatorname{supp}_{R}(y)\cap V=\varnothing
by
Lemma
3.25
. On the other hand,

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(c,y))\subseteq V
for all

c\in\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
, and therefore
y_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
when

\mathfrak{p}\not\in V
, by Lemma

. Thus

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(y)=\varnothing
, and therefore

by

.
∎

4.
Stratification

For a compactly generated triangulated category that is fibred over a
space, we wish to classify its localising subcategories via subsets of
the corresponding space. This leads to the notion of a stratification,
which reduces the classification to a local problem via a local-global
principle. Of prime interest are tensor triangulated categories, but
for actual calculations we employ appropriate ring actions.

References are
[
4
,
7
,
9
]
.

A local-global principle

We keep a compactly generated triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
, fibred
over a space
X
via
\tau\colon\varOmega(X)\to\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}})
. We
assume that each point in
X
is locally closed.

In general, there is no hope to classify all localising subcategories
of
\mathcal{T}
via the support of objects. For that reason we restrict to a
class of localising subcategories that are compatible with the
additional structure given by
\tau
.

Definition 4.1
.

A localising subcategory
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
is

\tau
-localising
, if
\varGamma_{U}\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
for
every open subset
U\subseteq X
, equivalently if

\varGamma_{Y}\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T}_{Y}

for every locally closed subset

Y\subseteq X
.

The next lemma collects some basic facts about
\tau
-localising subcategories.

Lemma 4.2
.

The
\tau
-localising subcategories are closed
under all joins, all meets, and also under the assignment

\mathcal{S}\mapsto\varGamma_{Y}\mathcal{S}
for every locally closed subset

Y\subseteq X
. In this case

\varGamma_{Y}\left(\bigvee_{i}\mathcal{S}_{i}\right)=\bigvee_{i}\varGamma_{Y}(\mathcal{S}_{i})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varGamma_{Y}\left(\bigwedge_{i}\mathcal{S}_{i}\right)=\bigwedge_{i}\varGamma_{Y}(\mathcal{S}_{i}).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\bigvee_{i}\mathcal{S}_{i}
be a join of localising subcategories
of
\mathcal{T}
and
Y\subseteq X
a locally closed subset. If each

\mathcal{S}_{i}
is
\tau
-localising, then

\varGamma_{Y}\left(\bigvee_{i}\mathcal{S}_{i}\right)\subseteq\bigvee_{i}\varGamma_{Y}(\mathcal{S}_{i})\subseteq\bigvee_{i}\mathcal{S}_{i}

since
\varGamma_{Y}
is exact and preserves all
coproducts. Thus
\mathcal{S}
is
\tau
-localising. The full subcategory

\varGamma_{Y}\mathcal{S}
is localising, and therefore the reverse inclusion

\bigvee_{i}\varGamma_{Y}(\mathcal{S}_{i})\subseteq\varGamma_{Y}\left(\bigvee_{i}\mathcal{S}_{i}\right)

holds. Now consider a meet
\bigwedge_{i}\mathcal{S}_{i}
of
\tau
-localising
subcategories. It is clear that this is
\tau
-localising, and then

\varGamma_{Y}\left(\bigwedge_{i}\mathcal{S}_{i}\right)=\left(\bigwedge_{i}\mathcal{S}_{i}\right)\wedge\mathcal{T}_{Y}=\bigwedge_{i}\left(\mathcal{S}_{i}\wedge\mathcal{T}_{Y}\right)=\bigwedge_{i}\varGamma_{Y}(\mathcal{S}_{i}).

If
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
is
\tau
-localising, then
\varGamma_{Y}\mathcal{S}
is

\tau
-localising, by Proposition
3.18
.
∎

For a class
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
let
\operatorname{loc}_{\tau}(\mathcal{X})
denote the smallest

\tau
-localising subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
that contains
\mathcal{X}
. One
easily checks that

\operatorname{loc}_{\tau}(\mathcal{X})=\bigvee_{Y\text{ loc.\ cl.}}\operatorname{loc}(\varGamma_{Y}(\mathcal{X})).

In particular,
\varGamma_{p}\mathcal{S}
is
\tau
-localising for each
localising
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
and
p\in X
.

Definition 4.3
.

The
local-global principle
holds for
(\mathcal{T},\tau)
if for each

\tau
-localising subcategory
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
one has

\mathcal{S}=\bigvee_{p\in X}\varGamma_{p}\mathcal{S}\,.

There are sufficient conditions for the local-global principle
to hold in terms of the space
X
, but this is not needed for our discussion.
Let us reformulate the condition and then we look at its consequences.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
(\mathcal{T},\tau)
the following are equivalent.

(1)

The local-global principle holds.

(2)

For each
x\in\mathcal{T}
one has

x\in\bigvee_{p\in X}\operatorname{loc}(\varGamma_{p}(x)).

(3)

For each
x\in\mathcal{T}
and each
\tau
-localising subcategory

\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
one has

x\in\mathcal{S}\qquad\iff\qquad\varGamma_{p}(x)\in\mathcal{S}\;\text{ for all
}\;p\in X.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) Apply the local-global principle to
\mathcal{S}=\operatorname{loc}_{\tau}(x)
.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3) If
\varGamma_{p}(x)\in\mathcal{S}
for all
p\in X
,
then (2) implies that
x\in\mathcal{S}
.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1) Set

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigvee_{p\in X}\varGamma_{p}\mathcal{S}
. Clearly,

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
. Applying (3) to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
yields the other inclusion.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

The local-global principle holds for
(\mathcal{T},\tau)
if and only if the assignments

\mathcal{S}\mapsto(\varGamma_{p}\mathcal{S})_{p\in X}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(\mathcal{S}_{p})_{p\in X}\mapsto\bigvee_{p\in X}\mathcal{S}_{p}

induce mutually inverse bijections between

(1)

\tau
-localising subcategories
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
, and

(2)

families of
\tau
-localising
subcategories
(\mathcal{S}_{p}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{\{p\}})_{p\in X}
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
4.2
that both maps are well
defined. That one composite equals the identity is precisely the
statement of the local-global
principle. For the other composite note that

\varGamma_{q}\left(\bigvee_{p\in X}\mathcal{S}_{p}\right)=\bigvee_{p\in X}\varGamma_{q}\mathcal{S}_{p}=\mathcal{S}_{q}

for each
q\in X
. Thus this composite equals the identity.
∎

The result suggests the additional assumption that for each point

p\in X
there are no proper nonzero
\tau
-localising subcategories
of
\mathcal{T}_{\{p\}}
, because then the
\tau
-localising subcategories
are classified via their support. This leads to the notion of
‘stratification’ which we discuss in the setting of compactly
generated tensor triangulated categories.

Compactly generated tensor triangulated categories

Let
\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{T},\otimes,\mathds{1})
be a
compactly generated
tensor triangulated category
. To be more precise, the triangulated
category
\mathcal{T}
is compactly generated and admits a closed symmetric
monoidal structure
\otimes\colon\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
with
unit
\mathds{1}
. Moreover, there exists an adjoint

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}\colon\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
such that

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(x\otimes y,z)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(x,\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(y,z))\,.

The functors
\otimes
and
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}
are assumed to be exact in each
variable.

Definition 4.6
.

An object
x\in\mathcal{T}
is called
dualisable
or
rigid
if,
for every
y\in\mathcal{T}
, the natural map

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(x,\mathds{1})\otimes y\to\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(x,y)
is an isomorphism. We say that

\mathcal{T}
is
rigidly-compactly generated
if
\mathds{1}
is compact
and every compact object is dualisable, which is equivalent to saying
that compact objects coincide with dualisable objects.

Now suppose that
\mathcal{T}
is rigidly-compactly generated. Then

\mathcal{T}
is fibred over
(\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}})^{\vee}
via the bijection

\tau_{\otimes}
in (
2.2
); see Proposition
2.23
. We write

\operatorname{Loc}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{T})
for the lattice of localising tensor ideals,
and for any class
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
let
\operatorname{loc}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{X})

denote the smallest localising tensor ideal containing
\mathcal{X}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
U\subseteq(\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}})^{\vee}
be an open subset. Then
\mathcal{T}_{U}
and
(\mathcal{T}_{U})^{\perp}
are localising tensor
ideals of
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

The subcategory
\mathcal{U}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\tau_{\otimes}(U)
is a thick
tensor ideal of
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
. By definition we have

\mathcal{T}_{U}=\operatorname{loc}(\mathcal{U})
and
(\mathcal{T}_{U})^{\perp}=\mathcal{U}^{\perp}
. We need
to show that
x\otimes\mathcal{T}_{U}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{U}
and

x\otimes(\mathcal{T}_{U})^{\perp}\subseteq(\mathcal{T}_{U})^{\perp}
for all

x\in\mathcal{T}
. We may assume that
x
is compact since
\mathcal{T}
is
compactly generated. The first inclusion is clear since

x\otimes\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
. For the second inclusion we may
assume in addition that
x
is dualisable, so
x\cong\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(x,\mathds{1}),\mathds{1})
.
Then the inclusion
follows from the fact that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(u,y)=0
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}

implies

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(u,x\otimes y)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(u,\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(x,\mathds{1}),y))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(u\otimes\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(x,\mathds{1}),y)=0

for all
u\in\mathcal{U}
.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
U\subseteq(\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}})^{\vee}
be an open subset. Then there are isomorphisms

\varGamma_{U}\cong\varGamma_{U}(\mathds{1})\otimes-\qquad\text{and}\qquad L_{U}\cong L_{U}(\mathds{1})\otimes-\,.

For a locally closed subset

Y\subseteq(\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}})^{\vee}
one has

\varGamma_{Y}\cong\varGamma_{Y}(\mathds{1})\otimes-\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{T}_{Y}=\operatorname{loc}_{\otimes}(\varGamma_{Y}(\mathds{1}))\,.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{T}
and apply
-\otimes x
to the exact triangle

\varGamma_{U}(\mathds{1})\longrightarrow\mathds{1}\longrightarrow L_{U}(\mathds{1})\longrightarrow\,.

Then

\varGamma_{U}(\mathds{1})\otimes x
is in
\mathcal{T}_{U}
and
L_{U}(\mathds{1})\otimes x

is in
(\mathcal{T}_{U})^{\perp}
, since both subcategories are tensor ideals
by Lemma
4.7
. Applying
\varGamma_{U}
to

\varGamma_{U}(\mathds{1})\otimes x\longrightarrow x\longrightarrow L_{U}(\mathds{1})\otimes x\longrightarrow

yields the
isomorphism
\varGamma_{U}(\mathds{1})\otimes x\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\varGamma_{U}(x)
, while applying

L_{U}
yields
L_{U}(x)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}L_{U}(\mathds{1})\otimes x
, since

\varGamma_{U}L_{U}=0=L_{U}\varGamma_{U}
.

Now let
Y
be locally closed and choose open subsets
U,V
such that

Y=V\smallsetminus U
. Then
\varGamma_{Y}=\varGamma_{V}L_{U}
, and the
isomorphism
\varGamma_{Y}\cong\varGamma_{Y}(\mathds{1})\otimes-
follows from the
first part. The identity for
\mathcal{T}_{Y}
is an immediate consequence.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Each localising tensor ideal of
\mathcal{T}
is
\tau_{\otimes}
-localising.

Proof.

When
U\subseteq(\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}})^{\vee}
is open we have

\varGamma_{U}\cong\varGamma_{U}(\mathds{1})\otimes-
by
Lemma
4.8
. Thus for any tensor ideal

\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
one has
\varGamma_{U}\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
.
∎

Tensor triangulated support

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a rigidly-compactly generated tensor triangulated
category. We consider
\mathcal{T}
as fibred over
(\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}})^{\vee}

via
\tau_{\otimes}
. In addition we assume that each point in

(\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}})^{\vee}
is locally closed. In particular, the local
cohomology functor
\varGamma_{p}
is defined for each point
p
.

For the support of objects in
\mathcal{T}
we recall
Definition
3.21
which extends
Definition
2.26
for compact objects.

Definition 4.10
.

For an object
x\in\mathcal{T}
its
tensor triangulated support
is

\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{p\in\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}\mid\varGamma_{p}(x)\neq 0\}\,.

This definition yields an adjoint pair of inclusion preserving maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Loc}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{T})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\text{subsets of }\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}\}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\otimes}}

given by

\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{S})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{\otimes}(U)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in\mathcal{T}\mid\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(x)\subseteq U\}\,.

This adjoint pair of maps extends (
2.2
). More
precisely, we obtain the following diagram of poset
morphisms

(4.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Loc}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{T})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\text{subsets of }\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\text{Thomason subsets of }\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}\}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{comp}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\otimes}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{int}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{loc}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\otimes}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{inc}}

which commutes in the following sense.

Proposition 4.11
.

The square of left adjoints and the square of right
adjoints in (
4.1
) both commute.
In particular, for each compact
x\in\mathcal{T}
there is an equality

\{\mathcal{P}\in\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}\mid x\not\in\mathcal{P}\}=\{p\in\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}\mid\varGamma_{p}(x)\neq 0\}\,.

Proof.

The commutativity of the square of left adjoints means that

\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(\operatorname{loc}(\mathcal{U}))=\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{U})

for each thick
tensor ideal
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
, where the definition of

\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}
depends on the ambient category. This commutativity
follows as in Proposition
3.23
, and then the
commutativity of right adjoints is a formal consequence. In
particular, the assertion about
\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(x)
for a compact
object
x
follows since

\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(\operatorname{thick}_{\otimes}(x))=\{\mathcal{P}\in\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}\mid x\not\in\mathcal{P}\}

and

\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(\operatorname{loc}_{\otimes}(x))=\{p\in\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}\mid\varGamma_{p}(x)\neq 0\}\,.\qed

In general, the support map
\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}
for tensor ideal
localising subcategories is not bijective, though it is surjective.

Lemma 4.12
.

For each subset
U\subseteq\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
we have

\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(\tau_{\otimes}(U))=U
.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
3.24
.
∎

Stratification

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a rigidly-compactly generated tensor triangulated
category, fibred over
(\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}})^{\vee}
via
\tau_{\otimes}
. Assume
that each point in
(\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}})^{\vee}
is locally closed.

Definition 4.13
.

The
local-global principle
holds for localising tensor ideals of
\mathcal{T}
if one has

\mathcal{S}=\bigvee_{p\in\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}}\varGamma_{p}\mathcal{S}

for every localising tensor ideal
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
.
An equivalent condition is that
x\in\mathcal{T}
belongs to a localising
tensor ideal
\mathcal{S}
if
and only if
\varGamma_{p}(x)\in\mathcal{S}
for each point
p
.

The local-global principle for
(\mathcal{T},\tau_{\otimes})
implies the
local-global principle for localising tensor ideals, because any
localising tensor ideal is
\tau_{\otimes}
-localising by
Lemma
4.9
.

Definition 4.14
.

The tensor triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
is
stratified
via

\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
provided that

(1)

the local-global principle holds for
localising tensor ideals of
\mathcal{T}
, and

(2)

for every

p\in\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
there are no proper nonzero localising tensor
ideals of
\mathcal{T}_{\{p\}}
.

The minimality condition (2) has an equivalent formulation:

(4.2)

p\in\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(x)\qquad\iff\qquad\operatorname{loc}_{\otimes}(\varGamma_{p}(\mathds{1}))=\operatorname{loc}_{\otimes}(\varGamma_{p}(x))

for all
x\in\mathcal{T}
and
p\in\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
.

The next result shows that the stratification of
\mathcal{T}
yields a
classification of all localising tensor ideals via the map

\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}
. Note that
\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}
is surjective; so we ask
when the map is injective.

Recall that
P(X)
denotes the power set
of a set
X
.

Proposition 4.15
.

For a rigidly-compactly generated tensor triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
, the
following are equivalent.

(1)

The category
\mathcal{T}
is stratified via
\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}}
.

(2)

The map
\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}
induces
a lattice isomorphism
\operatorname{Loc}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{T})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}P(\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}})
.

(3)

\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(x)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(y)
implies

\operatorname{loc}_{\otimes}(x)\subseteq\operatorname{loc}_{\otimes}(y)
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) The assignment

U\mapsto\bigvee_{p\in U}\mathcal{T}_{\{p\}}
yields an inclusion
preserving map
P(\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{c}})\to\operatorname{Loc}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{T})
which
satisfies
\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(\bigvee_{p\in U}\mathcal{T}_{\{p\}})=U
.
Assuming that
\mathcal{T}
is stratified, this map is an inverse for

\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}

5.
Differential graded algebras and modular representations

For a finite group one is interested in its modular representations
over a field of prime characteristic, and we may assume that the prime
divides the order of the group. Otherwise, the group algebra is
semisimple by Maschke’s theorem.

In this context we study either the essentially small bounded derived category of
finitely generated modules over the group algebra, or we pass to a
compactly generated triangulated category which contains the bounded derived
category as its subcategory of compact objects. The big category
equals the category of complexes of injective modules, up to homotopy.
Note that projective and injective modules agree over a group algebra,
because it is self-injective. However, we prefer the
injectives since representations are identified with their injective
resolutions. A tensor structure on modular representations is given by
the tensor product over the field and the diagonal action of the
group.

We begin with a classification of the thick tensor ideals of the
bounded derived category. This is done in two steps, so first for
elementary abelian
p
-groups via a Bernstein–Gelfand–Gelfand
correspondence, and then for an arbitrary finite group. The reduction
involves a subgroup theorem for cohomological support which uses
Quillen’s stratification of group cohomology. For the group algebra of
an elementary abelian
p
-group we pass to its Koszul complex; this is
a differential graded Hopf algebra which is quasi-isomorphic to an
exterior algebra. So a brief discussion of differential graded
algebras and their derived categories is included.

[

Theorem 5.9

Finite groups

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p>0
and
G
a finite group. The
comultiplication of the group algebra
kG
yields a monoidal structure
on the category of
kG
-modules, which is given by the tensor product
over
k
and therefore exact. Thus
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{fin}}(kG)
is a
tensor triangulated category with unit
k
and a canonical action
of

H^{*}(G,k)=\operatorname{Ext}_{kG}^{*}(k,k).

We write

V_{G}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,k)

and for
X\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{fin}}(kG)
we set

V_{G}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{supp}_{H^{*}(G,k)}(X)=V(\operatorname{Ker}\phi_{X})

where
\phi_{X}\colon H^{*}(G,k)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{kG}(X,X)
is given by
\alpha\mapsto\alpha\otimes X
.
Note that
H^{*}(G,k)
is a noetherian ring, by a theorem of Evens, Venkov, and
Golod. In particular, the cohomological support
\operatorname{supp}_{H^{*}(G,k)}
is finite.

In this section we establish the classification of thick tensor ideals
of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{fin}}(kG)
. The proof reduces this to the corresponding
classification for elementary abelian
p
-groups. This reduction uses
some results from modular representation theory.

Let
H\leq G
be a subgroup and consider the induced homomorphism

kH\to kG
. This yields a bimodule structure
{}_{kH}(kG)_{kG}

which gives rise to
restriction
and
induction
. For a

kG
-module
X
and a
kH
-module
Y
we set

X{\downarrow}_{H}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}(kG,X)\qquad\text{and}\qquad Y{\uparrow}^{G}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}Y\otimes_{kH}kG,

where the actions of
kH
and
kG
are given by the above bimodule
structure of
kG
.
The following natural
isomorphism is known as
Frobenius reciprocity
(or

projection formula
) and follows from the associativity of the
tensor product:

(X{\downarrow}_{H}\otimes_{k}Y){\uparrow}^{G}\cong X\otimes_{k}Y{\uparrow}^{G}.

When specialising
Y=k
we get

X{\downarrow}_{H}{\uparrow}^{G}\cong X\otimes_{k}k{\downarrow}_{H}{\uparrow}^{G}.

Restriction and
induction yield exact functors which extend to complexes of modules
and their derived categories. Note that the induction functor is faithful.

Restriction provides a homomorphism

\operatorname{res}_{G,H}\colon H^{*}(G,k)\longrightarrow H^{*}(H,k)

which induces a map

\operatorname{res}_{G,H}^{*}\colon V_{H}\longrightarrow V_{G}.

The next result is known as
subgroup theorem
and is based on
Quillen’s stratification of group cohomology.

Theorem 5.11
.

For
X\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{fin}}(kG)
we have

V_{H}(X{\downarrow}_{H})=(\operatorname{res}^{*}_{G,H})^{-1}V_{G}(X).

Proof.

See for example Corollary 9.3.3 in
[
14
]
.
∎

Theorem 5.13
.

There are elementary abelian subgroups

such that

\operatorname{thick}_{\otimes}(X)=\operatorname{thick}\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}X{\downarrow}_{E_{i}}{\uparrow}^{G}\right).

Proof.

Given

, we have

X{\downarrow}_{E_{i}}{\uparrow}^{G}\otimes_{k}Y\cong X\otimes_{k}(k{\downarrow}_{E_{i}}{\uparrow}^{G})\otimes_{k}Y\cong X\otimes_{k}(Y{\downarrow}_{E_{i}}{\uparrow}^{G})

by Frobenius reciprocity. This yields one inclusion when we

On the other

with quotients

and therefore

(X{\downarrow}_{E_{i}}\otimes_{k}W_{i}){\uparrow}^{G}\in\operatorname{thick}(X{\downarrow}_{E_{i}}{\uparrow}^{G}).

Thus,

X\in\operatorname{thick}\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}(X{\downarrow}_{E_{i}}\otimes_{k}W_{i}){\uparrow}^{G}\right)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}X{\downarrow}_{E_{i}}{\uparrow}^{G}\right)

and this yields the other inclusion.

Stratification for finite groups

For a group algebra we consider the category of complexes of injective
modules up to homotopy. This is the appropriate compactly generated
tensor triangulated category, because its subcategory of compact
objects identifies with the bounded derived category of finitely
generated modules over the group algebra. The strategy for the
stratification of this tensor triangulated category is completely
analogous to the one for the bounded derived category. So we study
first the case of an elementary abelian
p
-group using a BGG
correspondence, and then we consider an arbitrary finite group. The
reduction uses a subgroup theorem for cohomological support, and the
way Quillen’s stratification enters is carefully explained.

[
8
,
21
,
22
]
.

For a dg algebra

Given a dg Hopf algebra

Let

be an elementary abelian

to a classification of all localising subcategories of

As before, we use the exterior algebra

\varLambda=k\langle\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{r}\rangle
and the Koszul complex
B

on the sequence of generators of

. We view those as dg algebras
and note that
\varLambda
,

, and

satisfy the conditions

shows that the pair of

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{fin}}(S)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{fin}}(\varLambda)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{fin}}(B)

More precisely, the above equivalences are explicitly given and extend

Proposition 6.3

The compactly generated triangulated category

Proof.

We use the above equivalence
\mathbf{D}(S)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}B)
, and then the
assertion follows from the stratification of
\mathbf{D}(S)
via the
canonical action of
S
; see Theorem
5.1
.
∎

Next consider the dg algebra homomorphism
\iota\colon A\to B
which
induces an adjoint pair of
exact functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}B).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{*}=-\otimes_{A}B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{*}=\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(B,-)}

This restricts to a pair of functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{c}}(\operatorname{Inj}A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{c}}(\operatorname{Inj}B).}

The proof of Theorem
5.9
shows that there is an
induced pair of lattice isomorphisms

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}(\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{c}}(\operatorname{Inj}A))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}(\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{c}}(\operatorname{Inj}B)).}

The above properties of the pair
(\iota^{*},\iota_{*})
imply that

\iota_{*}
preserves the localising subcategories which are given by a
single prime.

For a localising subcategory
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}B)

set

\operatorname{Loc}(\iota_{*})(\mathcal{U})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{loc}(\iota_{*}(\mathcal{U})).

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
correspond to
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Spec}S
via the
homomorphism
S\to R
. Then

\operatorname{Loc}(\iota_{*})(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}B))=\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}A).

Proof.

The construction of
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}B)
shows that it
is generated by compact objects which are mapped by
\iota_{*}
to
compact generators of
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}A)
.
∎

The following is the analogue of Lemma
5.8
for
localising subcategories.

Lemma 6.5
.

For
X\in\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}A)
one has

\operatorname{loc}(X)=\operatorname{loc}(\iota_{*}\iota^{*}(X)).

Proof.

From Lemma
5.8
we know that

\operatorname{thick}(A)=\operatorname{thick}(B)
. Then the assertion follows by applying
X\otimes_{A}-
, since

X\otimes_{A}A=X
and
X\otimes_{A}B=\iota_{*}\iota^{*}(X)
.
∎

The following result extends Theorem
5.9
and its
proof uses a similar strategy.

Theorem 6.6
.

The compactly generated triangulated category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}A)
is
stratified via the canonical action of
R=\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(k,k)
. In
particular, cohomological support induces a lattice isomorphism

\operatorname{Loc}(\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}A))\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\{\text{Subsets of }\operatorname{Spec}R\}\,.

Proof.

We consider the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Loc}(\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}B))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\text{Subsets of }\operatorname{Spec}S\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Loc}(\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}A))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\text{Subsets of }\operatorname{Spec}R\}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{supp}_{S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Loc}(\iota_{*})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\operatorname{Spec}\iota_{k})^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{supp}_{R}}

and wish to show that the map
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is a lattice isomorphism.

First observe that the top horizontal map is an isomorphism by
Proposition
6.3
. Likewise, the vertical map on the
right is an isomorphism by Lemma
5.7
. Furthermore,
the map
\operatorname{Loc}(\iota_{*})
is surjective, since

\operatorname{Loc}(\iota_{*})\circ\operatorname{Loc}(\iota^{*})=\mathrm{id}

by
Lemma
6.5
. It remains to show that the diagram
commutes, because that would imply that all maps are bijections and
even lattice isomorphisms. All maps in the diagram preserve
arbitrary joins. So it suffices to pick
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
corresponding
to
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Spec}S
. Then Lemma
6.4
tells us that

\operatorname{Loc}(\iota_{*})(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}B))=\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}A).

From

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}A))=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
and

\operatorname{supp}_{S}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}B))=\{\mathfrak{q}\}
it follows that

\operatorname{supp}_{R}\circ\operatorname{Loc}(\iota_{*})=(\operatorname{Spec}\iota_{k})^{-1}\circ\operatorname{supp}_{S}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Quillen stratification

In
[
21
,
22
]
Quillen described the

maximal
ideal spectrum of

H^{*}(G,k)
in terms of elementary abelian subgroups. We are really
interested in
prime
ideals, but let us first describe Quillen’s
original theorem. Note that in a finitely generated graded-commutative

k
-algebra such as
H^{*}(G,k)
every prime ideal is the intersection
of the maximal ideals containing it, by a version of Hilbert’s
Nullstellensatz.

Let
G
be a finite group, and
k
a field of characteristic
p
. If

H
is a subgroup of
G
, there is a
restriction
map

H^{*}(G,k)\to H^{*}(H,k)
which is a ring
homomorphism. Writing

\bar{V}_{G}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{MaxSpec}H^{*}(G,k)

this induces a map of
varieties
\bar{V}_{H}\to\bar{V}_{G}
.

Theorem 6.7
.

An element
u\in H^{*}(G,k)
is nilpotent if and only if
\operatorname{res}_{G,E}(u)
is
nilpotent for every elementary abelian
p
-subgroup
E\leq G
. ∎

It thus makes sense to look at the product of the restriction maps

H^{*}(G,k)\longrightarrow\prod_{E}H^{*}(E,k).

The theorem implies that the kernel of this map is nilpotent. What is the image?

If an element
(u_{E})
is in the image then

(C1)

for each conjugation
c_{g}\colon E^{\prime}\to E
in
G
,

c_{g}^{*}(u_{E})=u_{E^{\prime}}
and

(C2)

for each inclusion
i\colon E^{\prime}\to E
in
G
,
i^{*}(u_{E})=u_{E^{\prime}}
.

Conversely, if
(u_{E})
satisfies these conditions, Quillen showed
that for some
t\geq 0
the element
(u_{E}^{p^{t}})
is in the image.

Definition 6.8
.

We define
\varprojlim_{E}H^{*}(E,k)
to be the elements
(u_{E})
of the direct product
\prod_{E}H^{*}(E,k)
satisfying
conditions (C1) and (C2) above.

Definition 6.9
.

A homomorphism
\phi\colon R\to S
of graded-commutative
k
-algebras
is an
F
-isomorphism
or

inseparable isogeny
if

(F1)

the kernel of
\phi
consists of nilpotent elements, and

(F2)

for each
s\in S
there exists
t\geq 0
such that
s^{p^{t}}\in\operatorname{Im}\phi
.

We can now rephrase Quillen’s theorem as follows:

Theorem 6.10
.

The restriction maps induce an
F
-isomorphism

H^{*}(G,k)\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{E}H^{*}(E,k)\,.\qed

Now if
R\to S
is an
F
-isomorphism of finitely generated
graded-commutative
k
-algebras then the induced map
\operatorname{MaxSpec}S\to\operatorname{MaxSpec}R

is a
bijection
.

For the moment, let us assume that
k
is algebraically
closed. Then this says that

\varinjlim_{E}\bar{V}_{E}\to\bar{V}_{G}
, as a map of varieties, is
bijective

(but not necessarily invertible).

Here,
\varinjlim_{E}\bar{V}_{E}
is the quotient of the disjoint union
of the
\bar{V}_{E}
by the equivalence relation induced
by the conjugations and inclusions.

Prime ideals and subgroup theorems

Now we discuss prime ideals in
H^{*}(G,k)
, and we drop the assumption
that
k
is algebraically closed. We write

V_{G}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,k)

and for
X\in\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)
we set

V_{G}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{supp}_{H^{*}(G,k)}(X).

By Quillen’s theorem, for each

\mathfrak{p}\in V_{G}
there exists an elementary abelian
p
-subgroup

E\leq G
such that
\mathfrak{p}
is in the image of
\operatorname{res}_{G,E}^{*}
. We say
that
\mathfrak{p}

originates
in such an
E
if there
does not exist a proper subgroup
E^{\prime}
of
E
such that
\mathfrak{p}
is in the
image of
\operatorname{res}_{G,E^{\prime}}^{*}
. In this language,
[
22
, Theorem 10.2]
reads:

Theorem 6.11
.

For each
\mathfrak{p}\in V_{G}
, the pairs
(E,\mathfrak{q})
where
\mathfrak{q}\in V_{E}
,
\mathfrak{p}=\operatorname{res}_{G,E}^{*}(\mathfrak{q})
and
such that
\mathfrak{p}
originates in
E
are all
G
-conjugate. ∎

For any subgroup
H\leq G
, formal properties of restriction and induction
prove the following.

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in V_{G}
and set
U=(\operatorname{res}_{G,H}^{*})^{-1}\{\mathfrak{p}\}
. If
X\in\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)
and
Y\in\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kH)
then

\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(X){\downarrow}_{H}\cong\varGamma_{U}(X{\downarrow}_{H})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(Y{\uparrow}^{G})\cong\varGamma_{U}(Y){\uparrow}^{G}.

Proof.

The first isomorphism follows from Proposition
4.22
.
The second isomorphism follows from the first and Frobenius
reciprocity, since

\displaystyle\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(Y{\uparrow}^{G})

\displaystyle\cong\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(k)\otimes_{k}Y{\uparrow}^{G}

\displaystyle\cong(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(k){\downarrow}_{H}\otimes_{k}Y){\uparrow}^{G}

\displaystyle\cong(\varGamma_{U}(k)\otimes_{k}Y){\uparrow}^{G}

\displaystyle\cong\varGamma_{U}(Y){\uparrow}^{G}.\qed

Using Mayer–Vietoris triangles one can actually show that

\varGamma_{U}=\coprod_{\mathfrak{q}\in U}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}
since
U
is discrete; see
Proposition
3.19
. But this is not needed for the
following.

Proposition 6.13
.

For
Y\in\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kH)
we have

V_{G}(Y{\uparrow}^{G})=\operatorname{res}_{G,H}^{*}V_{H}(Y).

Proof.

It follows from
Lemma
6.12
and faithfulness of induction that the condition
\mathfrak{p}\in V_{G}(Y{\uparrow}^{G})

is equivalent to: there exists
\mathfrak{q}\in V_{H}
such that

\operatorname{res}^{*}_{G,H}(\mathfrak{q})=\mathfrak{p}
and
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}(Y)\neq 0
.
∎

The following proof of the subgroup theorem for a pair
H\leq G
uses
that
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)
is stratified; so at this stage it works when
G

is elementary abelian.

Theorem 6.14
.

Let
H\leq G
and suppose that
G
is an elementary abelian
p
-group. For
X
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)
we have

V_{H}(X{{\downarrow}_{H}})=(\operatorname{res}_{G,H}^{*})^{-1}V_{G}(X)\,.

The proof shows that the inclusion
\subseteq
is purely
formal, while there are other tensor triangulated categories where the
analogue of
\supseteq
does not hold.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{q}\in V_{H}
and
\mathfrak{p}=\operatorname{res}_{G,H}^{*}(\mathfrak{q})
. Then
Proposition
6.13
yields

V_{G}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}(k){\uparrow}^{G})=\{\mathfrak{p}\}=V_{G}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(k)),

and the classification of localising subcategories for
kG

(Theorem
6.6
) implies

\operatorname{loc}((\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}k){\uparrow}^{G})=\operatorname{loc}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(k)).

Thus,

\displaystyle\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}(X{\downarrow}_{H})\neq 0

\displaystyle\iff\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}(k)\otimes_{k}X{\downarrow}_{H}\neq 0

\displaystyle\iff(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}(k)\otimes_{k}X{\downarrow}_{H}){\uparrow}^{G}\neq 0

\displaystyle\iff\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}(k){\uparrow}^{G}\otimes_{k}X\neq 0

\displaystyle\iff\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(k)\otimes_{k}X\neq 0

\displaystyle\iff\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(X)\neq 0,

where the second equivalence uses that
(-){\uparrow}^{G}
is faithful, the
third equivalence uses Frobenius reciprocity, and the fourth
equivalence uses the above equality of localising subcategories.
∎

The following result is the analogue of
Theorem
5.13
for bounded complexes of
finitely generated
kG
-modules.

Theorem 6.15
.

There are elementary abelian subgroups
E_{1},\ldots,E_{t}
such that
for each
X\in\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)

\operatorname{thick}_{\otimes}(X)=\operatorname{thick}\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}X{\downarrow}_{E_{i}}{\uparrow}^{G}\right).

Proof.

The proof of Theorem
5.13
carries over
without any changes.
∎

We continue with a variant of the subgroup theorem which is sufficient
for us; it uses Quillen’s stratification of group cohomology, as
formulated in Theorem
6.11
.

Theorem 6.16
.

Let
E\leq G
be an elementary abelian
p
-subgroup. For
X,Y
in

\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)
we have

V_{G}(X)\subseteq V_{G}(Y)\qquad\implies\qquad V_{E}(X{\downarrow}_{E})\subseteq V_{E}(Y{\downarrow}_{E}).

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{q}\in V_{E}(X{\downarrow}_{E})
and set
\mathfrak{p}=\operatorname{res}^{*}_{G,E}(\mathfrak{q})
. Then

\mathfrak{p}\in V_{G}(X)
by Theorem
6.14
. Let
E_{0}\leq E

be a subgroup such that
\mathfrak{p}
originates in
E_{0}
, and pick

\mathfrak{q}_{0}\in V_{E_{0}}
such that
\mathfrak{q}=\operatorname{res}^{*}_{E,E_{0}}(\mathfrak{q}_{0})
. Then

\mathfrak{q}_{0}\in V_{E_{0}}(X{\downarrow}_{E_{0}})
, again by Theorem
6.14
.

We wish to show that
\mathfrak{q}\in V_{E}(Y{\downarrow}_{E})
and may assume that

Y=\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(Y)
, since

V_{E}((\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(Y)){\downarrow}_{E})\subseteq V_{E}(Y{\downarrow}_{E})
. Choose an
elementary abelian
p
-subgroup
E^{\prime}\leq G
such that

Y{\downarrow}_{E^{\prime}}\neq 0
; this exists by
Theorem
6.15
. Let

\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\in V_{E^{\prime}}(Y{\downarrow}_{E^{\prime}})
. Then
\operatorname{res}^{*}_{G,E^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime})=\mathfrak{p}
by
Theorem
6.14
. Choose a subgroup
E^{\prime}_{0}\leq E^{\prime}
such that

\mathfrak{p}
originates in
E^{\prime}_{0}
, together with a prime

\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}_{0}\in V_{E^{\prime}_{0}}(Y{\downarrow}_{E^{\prime}_{0}})
such that

\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}=\operatorname{res}^{*}_{E^{\prime},E^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}_{0})
. The pairs
(E_{0},\mathfrak{q}_{0})
and

(E^{\prime}_{0},\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}_{0})
are
G
-conjugate by Theorem
6.11
. Thus

\mathfrak{q}_{0}\in V_{E_{0}}(Y{\downarrow}_{E_{0}})
. Here, one uses that conjugation by
an element
g\in G
induces an automorphism of
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)
which
takes
Y
to
Y^{g}
, and that multiplication with
g
induces an
isomorphism
Y\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}Y^{g}
. With Theorem
6.14
we
conclude that
\mathfrak{q}\in V_{E}(Y{\downarrow}_{E})
.
∎

Stratification for finite groups

We are ready to establish the stratification of
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)
for an
arbitrary finite group, which is due to Benson, Iyengar, and Krause

[
8
]
. The proof uses the same strategy
as the one for the classification of thick tensor ideals of

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{fin}}(kG)
(Theorem
5.14
).

Theorem 6.17
.

For a finite group
G
and a field
k
, the rigidly-compactly generated tensor triangulated category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)
is
stratified via the canonical action of
H^{*}(G,k)
. In
particular, cohomological support induces a lattice isomorphism

\operatorname{Loc}_{\otimes}(\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG))\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\{\text{Subsets of }\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,k)\}\,.

Proof.

Fix
X,Y
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)
and suppose that

V_{G}(X)\subseteq V_{G}(Y)

holds. For an elementary abelian
p
-subgroup
E\leq G
,
Theorem
6.16
implies

V_{E}(X{\downarrow}_{E})\subseteq V_{E}(Y{\downarrow}_{E})

and therefore

\operatorname{loc}(X{\downarrow}_{E})\subseteq\operatorname{loc}(Y{\downarrow}_{E})

by
Theorem
6.6
. This implies

\operatorname{loc}(X{\downarrow}_{E}{\uparrow}^{G})\subseteq\operatorname{loc}(Y{\downarrow}_{E}{\uparrow}^{G})

and then Theorem
6.15
implies

\operatorname{loc}_{\otimes}(X)=\operatorname{loc}\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}X{\downarrow}_{E_{i}}{\uparrow}^{G}\right)\subseteq\operatorname{loc}\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}Y{\downarrow}_{E_{i}}{\uparrow}^{G}\right)=\operatorname{loc}_{\otimes}(Y).

It remains to apply
Proposition
4.18
which establishes the
isomorphism between
\operatorname{Loc}_{\otimes}(\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG))
and the subsets of

\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,k)
.
∎

Remark 6.18
.

The full subcategory of objects in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)
that are supported
at the maximal ideal
H^{+}(G,k)
of positive degree elements
identifies with the derived category
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}kG)
. On the other
hand, the stable category
\operatorname{StMod}kG
of the module category over

kG
identifies with the full subcategory of acyclic complexes in

\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)
, by sending a module to a
complete resolution
, which
is obtained by splicing together a projective resolution and an injective
coresolution. Thus the tensor triangulated category
\operatorname{StMod}kG
is
stratified via

\operatorname{Proj}H^{*}(G,k)=\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,k)\smallsetminus\{H^{+}(G,k)\}.
