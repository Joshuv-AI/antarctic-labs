Synergistic application of taurine and spermidine enhances wheat tolerance to neodymium stress through redox homeostasis, metabolic adjustment, and nutrient regulation
Authors: Rashid Iqbal, Hassan Mehmood, Asma Majeed, Ghulam Murtaza, Nazih Y. Rebouh, Mohammed S. Alotaibi, Saqer S. Alotaibi, Sajid Ullah, Aziza Huseynova, Sajad Ali
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractNeodymium (Nd) contamination is an emerging environmental challenge that threatens crop productivity and food quality in agroecosystems. Although taurine (Tau) and spermidine (Spd) are recognized as important regulators of plant stress responses, their interactive role in mitigating rare-earth-element toxicity remains largely unexplored. The present study evaluated the potential of foliar-applied (Tau 10 mM) and (Spd 1 mM), applied individually and in combination, to alleviate Nd toxicity (150 mg kg−1 NdCl3·6H2O) in wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) cv. Faisalabad-2008. Nd exposure significantly (p ≤ 0.05) impaired soil physico-chemical properties, depressed microbial biomass carbon and dehydrogenase, urease, alkaline phosphatase and β-glucosidase activities, restricted root architecture and shoot biomass, suppressed gas exchange and pigment biosynthesis and triggered oxidative damage, as evidenced by elevated malondialdehyde (MDA) and hydrogen peroxide (H2O2). The combined Tau + Spd foliar spray restored the soil quality and microbial functionality, enhanced the activities of superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase (CAT), peroxidase (POX), and ascorbate peroxidase (APX), increased the proline, glutathione and total soluble sugars and improved the photosynthetic pigments and stomatal conductance relative to the Nd-only treatment. The dual application also reduced Nd accumulation in roots, shoots, and grains, restored the K+/Na+ ratio and macronutrient acquisition and improved the grain yield, protein, starch, and micronutrient density. Pearson correlation, radar, and principal component analyses confirmed coordinated recovery across soil, physiological, and grain-quality traits. These findings demonstrate that taurine and spermidine act synergistically to enhance wheat tolerance to Nd toxicity through the modulation of antioxidant defenses, osmotic adjustment, nutrient homeostasis, and metal exclusion mechanisms, highlighting their potential as sustainable biostimulants for crop production in rare-earth-element-contaminated soils.

Introduction

The increasing accumulation of REEs in agricultural soil due toREE mining, e-waste recycling, phosphate fertilizer application and technological industries has become a major threat to world food security and agro-ecosystems. 1 Neodymium (Nd), a light lanthanide with extensive applications in the manufacturing of permanent magnets, rechargeable batteries, and wind energy producers, is released into agricultural soils through industrial effluents, atmospheric deposition and soil additives rich in REEs. 2 , 3 Wheat (Triticum aestivum L.), the leading cereal utilized by over 35% of the world population, is produced under various pedo-climatic conditions and is, thus, susceptible to REE contamination of soils. 4 , 5 Elevated levels of Nd within the rhizosphere interfere with nutrient availability by displacing critical cations bound to soil exchange complexes and change root system structure, thereby affecting wheat grain productivity. 6

Nd overdosage results in complex phytotoxic effects in cereal grains. The Nd3+ cation interferes with the transmembrane movement of calcium (Ca2+) and potassium (K⁺) ions because of its ionic size, leading to disruption of their ratios and osmoregulatory processes. 6 Nd causes excessive production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), resulting in disruption of thylakoid membranes, impairment of the photosynthetic electron transfer chain and lipid and protein peroxidation. 2 , 7 In addition to direct toxicity in plants, REE build-up in soil negatively affects the structure of microbial communities and represses the enzymatic activities that facilitate C, N, and P cycles. 8 , 9 However, knowledge of the molecular and biochemical basis of Nd³⁺ toxicity in wheat plants is incomplete. Traditional approaches to remediation using chemical chelators or single-agent treatment are expensive, ineffective, non-selective, and potentially hazardous. 10 , 11 Therefore, there is an urgent need to apply low-cost, environment-friendly additives that can simultaneously correct ionic homeostasis, provide antioxidative protection and maintain crop yield architecture.

Polyamines and sulfur-containing compounds, such as taurine, have been extensively studied for their roles in plant abiotic stress tolerance. 12 , 13 Exogenous taurine (Tau), a sulfur-containing non-protein amino acid, promotes osmotic adjustment, stabilizes membranes and regulates the glutathione redox pool under heavy metal and salinity stress. 14 Similarly, polycationic polyamines, such as spermidine (Spd), stabilize membrane and nucleic acid structures, activate ion channels and upregulate detoxification enzymes. 15 , 16 While individual applications of these compounds have shown promise, and their co-application is hypothesized to confer complementary protection: Tau supplies precursors for sulfur-rich antioxidants and stabilizes the cellular milieu, while Spd enhances ROS scavenging and ion homeostasis. However, to the best of our knowledge, the combined effects of foliar Tau and Spd on Nd-stressed soil–plant systems and how this synergy advances beyond single-agent remediation, have not previously been evaluated.

The present study evaluated the combined effects of foliar-applied taurine (10 mM) and spermidine (1 mM) on wheat grown under Nd stress (150 mg kg−1 as NdCl3·6H2O). Specifically, we tested whether co-application would (i) restore soil microbial function and enzyme activity in Nd-contaminated soils; (ii) recover photosynthetic performance and antioxidant capacity in wheat; (iii) limit Nd translocation to grains while restoring the K+/Na+ ratio and macronutrient homeostasis and (iv) improve grain yield and nutritional quality. By integrating soil biochemistry, plant physiology and grain-quality parameters within a single experimental framework, this study provides a mechanistic basis for the development of biocompatible amendment strategies to sustain cereal production in REE-contaminated soils and a foundation for subsequent field-scale validation.

Materials and methods

Experimental setup and treatments

The experiment was conducted during the Rabi growing season (November–April) at the controlled wire-house facility, Punjab, Pakistan (29.3890° N, 71.6597° E). The experimental area was enclosed with stainless steel mesh of 1 × 1 mm size, which prevented entry of birds and reptiles but permitted entry of solar radiation, temperature, and moisture into the enclosure. Environmental factors, such as ambient temperature, relative humidity, photosynthetically active radiation (PAR), and wind velocity, were measured continuously at an hourly interval using data loggers. The experiment lasted for approximately 135 d from the date of sowing until physiological maturity. Artificial light or climate modification was not used in the experiment so that the natural environment would remain realistic. It comprised a randomized complete block design (RCBD) with seven treatments and four replicates, thus making a total of 28 experimental units. There were four types of treatment combinations, as presented in Table 1.

Crop management and agronomic practices

Seeds of wheat (Triticum aestivum L. cv. Faisalabad-2008) were surface sterilized by treatment with 2% sodium hypochlorite for 5 min. These seeds were then thoroughly washed with distilled water and air-dried before sowing. The experimental pots (25 cm diameter × 30 cm deep) contained 10 kg of uniform sandy loam soil. Ten seeds were sown at a depth of 2 cm in each pot. After 10 d of emergence, the seedlings were thinned to five plants per pot. The basal dose of the recommended fertilizer was 120-90-60 kg N-P-K ha−1, where the complete doses of P and K and half doses of nitrogen were mixed in the soil at the time of sowing, while the remaining nitrogen was top-dressed at tillering. The moisture content of the soil was maintained between 70% and 75% of the field capacity based on the weight method. The weeds in the pots were eliminated every 15 d, while insecticidal soap at 0.5% level was sprayed when aphids exceeded economic injury levels. At physiological maturity (grain moisture 12%–14%), the plants were harvested and partitioned into roots, shoots and grains. All the samples were oven-dried to a constant weight, ground to a fine powder in a stainless-steel mill and stored in desiccators pending laboratory analysis. Prior to sowing, the Nd treatment (150 mg kg−1 NdCl3·6H2O) was thoroughly mixed into the air-dried soil. The soil was then incubated for 14 d with moisture maintained at 70% field capacity to ensure the equilibration and stabilization of Nd ions within the soil matrix. This concentration was selected to simulate severe contamination levels reported in agricultural soils adjacent to rare-earth mining sites, waste recycling facilities and long-term phosphate-fertilized fields, where Nd concentrations can exceed 100–200 mg kg−1. 6 , 17

Post-harvest soil physicochemical properties analysis

Post-harvest soil samples were collected from each pot, air-dried, homogenized and passed through a 2 mm sieve. The soil pH was measured in a 1:2.5 (w/v) soil-to-water suspension using a calibrated glass electrode, while electrical conductivity (EC) was determined at a 1:5 (w/v) soil-to-water extract. 18 Soil organic matter was quantified using the Walkley–Black wet-oxidation method 19 and the cation exchange capacity (CEC) was determined by ammonium acetate extraction (pH 7.0). 20 Microbial biomass carbon was estimated using the chloroform fumigation–incubation method. 21 Available nitrogen was extracted with alkaline potassium permanganate and quantified by micro-Kjeldahl distillation; available phosphorus was extracted with 0.5 M NaHCO₃ (Olsen method) 22 and determined colorimetrically; and available potassium was extracted with 1 M NH₄OAc and measured by flame photometry. 23 Total soil neodymium was determined following microwave-assisted aqua-regia digestion and quantified by inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES), with reagent blanks and certified standards included for quality control. Bioavailable Nd was extracted with 0.005 M DTPA (pH 7.3) and quantified by ICP–OES. 24

Soil enzyme activity assessment

Soil enzyme activities were quantified colorimetrically using fresh soil samples. Dehydrogenase activity was assessed using the triphenyl tetrazolium chloride (TTC) reduction method after 24 h of incubation at 30 °C. Urease activity was determined by quantifying the ammonium released after 2 h of incubation with a urea substrate at 37 °C using the indophenol blue method. Alkaline phosphatase and β-glucosidase activities were measured as the rate of p-nitrophenol (pNP) liberation after 1 h of incubation with the respective substrates at 37 °C. All enzyme activities were expressed on a dry-weight basis following moisture correction. 25

Plant growth and morphological characteristics

At harvest, plant height was measured from the soil surface to the tip of the longest leaf, and root and shoot lengths were recorded using a digital scale. The root fresh mass, shoot fresh mass, root dry mass, and shoot dry mass were measured using an analytical balance (accuracy ± 0.001 g). The leaf area was measured using an automated leaf area meter (WinFOLIA Pro (Model T221B, 2016) automated leaf area meter (Regent Instruments Inc., Canada). 26 The ratio between the root dry mass and shoot dry mass was calculated by dividing the root dry mass by that of the shoots. All morphometric parameters were measured on a per-plant basis for each species.

Root architectural parameters measurement

Architectural features of the root system were measured using a WinRHIZO Pro root scanner (Regent Instruments Inc., Canada). The roots were thoroughly cleaned to remove all adhering soil and then positioned inside an opaque box filled with water. The root length density, root volume, root diameter, root surface area, and root tip number were calculated using the WinRHIZO program. Calibration was performed using appropriate standards and overlapping roots were separated using segmentation techniques based on a threshold value. 27

Physiological and photosynthetic parameters evaluation

The relative water content (RWC) of the leaves was calculated using the following formula:

RWC(%)=FW−DWTW−DW×100, where FW represents the fresh weight, DW represents the dry weight obtained after drying in an oven and TW refers to the turgid weight recorded after immersion in distilled water for 24 h. 28

MSI was computed using the following equation:

MSI(%)=1−(EC1EC2)×100, where EC₁ refers to the electrical conductivity of leaf-disc leachate when incubated at 40 °C for one hour and EC₂ denotes the electrical conductivity after incubation at 100 °C for one hour. 29 While EL was valuated according to the following formula:

EL(%)=(EC1EC2)×100. Concentrations of chlorophyll-a, chlorophyll-b and total carotenoids in fresh leaf samples were measured using spectroscopy with an extraction solvent containing 80% (v/v) acetone. 30 Leaf greenness was measured at the midpoint of the flag leaf using a SPAD-502 + chlorophyll meter. 31 , 32 The maximum quantum yield of photosystem II (Fv/Fm) was determined on dark-adapted leaves using a pulse-amplitude-modulated (PAM) fluorometer. 33

Gas exchange attributes

Leaf gas exchange parameters were measured on fully expanded flag leaves between 09:00 and 11:00 h using a portable infrared gas analyzer (LI-6400XT, LI-COR Biosciences, USA). The cuvette conditions were maintained at a photosynthetic photon flux density of 1200 μmol m−2 s−1, a reference CO₂ concentration of 400 μmol mol−1, a relative humidity of 60% and a leaf temperature of 25 °C. Net photosynthetic rate (A), transpiration rate (E), stomatal conductance (gs ), and intercellular CO₂ concentration (Ci ) were recorded after the leaves had equilibrated within the chamber for 3 min. 34

Oxidative stress marker quantification

Malondialdehyde (MDA) concentration was determined using the thiobarbituric acid reactive substances (TBARS) assay, measuring absorbance at 532 nm with correction for non-specific absorbance at 600 nm. 35 Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) was quantified using the titanium sulfate method, in which the yellow peroxide–titanium complex was measured at 410 nm. 36 Superoxide radical content (O2 −) was determined by the method of nitrite production from hydroxylamine oxidation, with the absorption value measured at 530 nm. 37

Antioxidant enzyme activity assay

Freshly excised leaf tissues were ground in ice-cold buffer solution of 50 mM phosphate buffer pH 7.0 and 1 mM EDTA along with 1% (w/v) polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP). This was centrifuged at 15,000 × g for 20 min at 4 °C. Superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity was determined by monitoring the inhibition of nitroblue tetrazolium (NBT) photoreduction at 560 nm. 38 Peroxidase (POD) activity was assayed by measuring guaiacol oxidation at 470 nm, 39 whereas catalase (CAT) activity was quantified from the rate of H₂O₂ decomposition at 240 nm. 40 Ascorbate peroxidase (APX) activity was determined from the rate of ascorbate oxidation at 290 nm. 41 Glutathione peroxidase (GPX) and glutathione reductase (GR) activities were assayed using coupled enzymatic reactions that monitored NADPH oxidation at 340 nm, as described by Tietze 42 and Carlberg and Mannervik, 43 respectively. All enzyme activities were expressed on a soluble-protein basis, with protein content determined by the Bradford assay. 44

Osmolyte accumulation and glutathione redox metabolism

Free proline was extracted with 3% (w/v) sulfosalicylic acid and quantified using the ninhydrin–acetic acid method, with the absorbance of the chromophore measured at 520 nm. 45 Glycine betaine was extracted with ice-cold sulfuric acid and determined using the periodide reaction at 365 nm. 46 Reduced (GSH) and oxidized (GSSG) glutathione were extracted in metaphosphoric acid and quantified using the DTNB recycling assay at 412 nm. The glutathione redox ratio was calculated as GSH/GSSG, and the glutathione redox state was calculated as GSH/(GSH + GSSG). 47

Carbon metabolism and biochemical constituents

Soluble sugars were extracted from shoot tissues using 80% (v/v) ethanol at 80 °C. Reducing sugars were quantified using the dinitrosalicylic acid (DNS) method at 540 nm. Non-reducing sugars were determined after acid hydrolysis of sucrose and total soluble sugars were computed as the sum of the reducing and non-reducing fractions. 48 Fructose and sucrose were separated and quantified using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) coupled with a refractive index detector. 49 Shoot crude protein content was estimated using the Kjeldahl method, applying a conversion factor of 5.7 to total nitrogen. 50

Yield and yield-related traits assessment

At physiological maturity, spike length was measured as the linear distance from the basal node of the rachis to the apex of the spike. Grains per spike were determined by manual counting after threshing. The 1000-grain weight was obtained as the mean of three independent replicate counts. The grain yield (g pot−1) and straw yield (g pot−1) were recorded after oven-drying the respective samples to constant weight. The biological yield per pot was determined as the total oven-dried aboveground biomass. The harvest index was calculated as follows: Harvestindex(HI)=EconomicyieldBiologicalyield×100.

Grain quality attributes determination

The concentration of grain crude protein was analyzed using the micro-Kjeldahl technique. 50 The amounts of reducing and non-reducing sugars were measured by standardized colorimetry, and the amount of total soluble sugars was determined as the sum of both. 48 Total phenolics were extracted from methanolic solutions and measured using the Folin‒Ciocalteu reagent, expressed as gallic acid equivalent (GAE). 51 Flavonoid content was also analyzed using a colorimetric assay utilizing aluminum chloride, with the result expressed as the quercetin equivalent (QE). 52

Endogenous polyamine uptake and accumulation

Endogenous taurine and spermidine levels in root, shoot and leaf tissues were measured using HPLC. The tissue samples were extracted using 5% (v/v) perchloric acid solution, centrifuged and then derivatized using dansyl chloride. The derivatized tissue extract was separated on a C18 reversed-phase column using gradient elution. The detection of taurine and spermidine was carried out using fluorescence detection. 53

Neodymium accumulation, translocation, and bioconcentration

The Nd concentration in the roots, shoots, leaves and grains was determined after digestion in HNO3:HClO4 at a 4:1 (v/v) ratio using microwave-assisted digestion, followed by the measurement of Nd levels via (ICP–OES; Optima 8300, PerkinElmer, Norwalk, CT, USA). 24 The translocation factor (TF) was calculated as: TF=[Nd]shoot[Nd]root, while tissue-specific BCFs were estimated as:

BCFtissue=[Nd]tissue[Nd]soil.

Nutrient composition and ion homeostasis analysis

The shoots were subjected to digestion using nitric acid (HNO3) and perchloric acid (HClO4), after which their contents were evaluated for nutrients. 54 Nitrogen levels were obtained using micro-Kjeldahl distillation. 55 The phosphorus content was obtained using colorimetry, utilizing the molybdenum-blue procedure at 882 nm wavelength. Potassium (K⁺) and sodium (Na⁺) levels were determined using flame photometry, while chloride (Cl⁻) level was determined using argentometric titration. 56 The concentrations of iron (Fe), zinc (Zn), manganese (Mn), and copper (Cu) were determined using ICP–OES. 57 The K⁺:Na⁺ ratio was estimated by dividing potassium concentration by sodium concentration. 58

Statistical analysis

Prior to analysis, data were screened for outliers using the Grubbs' test. Treatment effects were evaluated using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Although a factorial design was considered, one-way ANOVA was employed to compare the seven discrete, pre-defined treatment combinations, which is standard for evaluating specific amendment protocols. The assumptions of normality and homogeneity of variance were confirmed using the Shapiro–Wilk and Levene's tests, respectively 59 through Statistix 8.1. 60 Mean separation was performed using Tukey's honestly significant difference (HSD) test at p ≤ 0.05. 61 Pearson's correlation coefficients were used to assess pairwise relationships among measured parameters. Principal component analysis (PCA) 62 and radar-chart visualization 63 were performed to integrate multivariate responses across soil, physiological, biochemical and grain-quality traits. Exact p-values are reported where p > 0.001 and partial eta-squared (η²p) was computed to provide effect-size estimates for the observed treatment differences.

Results

Post-harvest soil physico-chemical properties

Foliar applications of taurine and spermidine significantly modulated post-harvest soil physico-chemical properties under both unstressed and Nd-stressed conditions (Table 2). Under non-stress conditions, sole taurine application increased available N, P, and K by 7.61%, 6.72%, and 4.10%, respectively, together with the soil organic matter (7.72%), cation exchange capacity (CEC, 2.32%), and microbial biomass carbon (MBC, 7.85%); concurrently, it lowered total Nd (−2.41%), DTPA-extractable Nd (−2.69%), electrical conductivity (EC, −1.27%), and soil pH (−0.81%) relative to the control. Sole spermidine produced comparable but more pronounced effects, raising available N, P, and K by 10.21%, 9.34%, and 8.13% and soil organic matter, CEC and MBC by 13.19%, 3.52%, and 7.90%, respectively, with negligible changes in total Nd, available Nd, EC, and soil pH. Sole Nd stress markedly degraded soil quality, increasing the total Nd by 5175.12%, the available Nd by 9088.85%, and the EC by 86.13%, raising soil pH by 5.33% and depressing available N (−33.82%), P (−30.30%) and K (−26.82%), along with organic matter (−33.41%), CEC (−19.34%), and MBC (−43.15%) relative to the control. Co-application of taurine with Nd stress reduced available Nd (−16.01%), EC (−17.00%), and soil pH (−2.40%) relative to sole Nd, while restoring available N, P, and K by 24.21%, 21.75%, and 16.34%, organic matter by 23.80%, CEC by 9.25%, and MBC by 32.71%. The Spd + Nd combination further lowered available Nd (−20.75%), EC (−19.31%), and pH (−3.27%) relative to sole Nd, with concomitant increases in available N (28.96%), P (25.16%), K (20.27%), organic matter (31.81%), CEC (10.36%), and MBC (36.31%). The combined Tau + Spd + Nd treatment elicited the most pronounced ameliorative effects, decreasing available Nd by 30.32%, EC by 30.97% and pH by 3.59%, while elevating available N, P, and K by 42.82%, 38.32%, and 30.45%, organic matter by 42.84%, CEC by 18.67%, and MBC by 61.09% compared with sole Nd stress.

Soil enzyme activity responses

Soil enzyme activities responded markedly to both Nd exposure and foliar treatments (Table 3). Compared to the unamended control, sole taurine application increased dehydrogenase, urease, alkaline phosphatase, and β-glucosidase activities by 9.23%, 10.58%, 11.51%, and 9.69%, respectively. Sole spermidine produced larger gains of 13.82%, 12.84%, 15.03%, and 13.14% for the same enzymes. In contrast, sole Nd stress severely suppressed enzymatic activity, with reductions of 52.04% (dehydrogenase), 47.45% (urease), 45.24% (alkaline phosphatase), and 50.37% (β-glucosidase) relative to the control. Co-application of taurine with Nd stress restored dehydrogenase, urease, alkaline phosphatase, and β-glucosidase activities by 41.27%, 37.62%, 39.83%, and 43.03%, respectively, relative to sole Nd stress. The spermidine + Nd combination yielded further increases of 52.38%, 44.12%, 47.01%, and 50.38% for the same enzymes. The combined Tau + Spd + Nd treatment produced the greatest recovery, increasing dehydrogenase by 85.13%, urease by 69.95%, alkaline phosphatase by 65.87%, and β-glucosidase by 77.39% relative to sole Nd stress.

Growth and morphological characteristics

Foliar applications of taurine and spermidine significantly modified the growth and morphological attributes of wheat under Nd stress (Table 4). Sole Nd stress alone severely impaired growth, reducing root, and shoot biomass, plant height and leaf area by 32%–52% compared to the control. The co-application of taurine or spermidine with Nd stress partially restored these traits. Notably, the combined Tau + Spd + Nd treatment elicited the greatest amelioration, significantly increasing the root length, shoot fresh weight, and leaf area by 59.13%, 67.95%, and 54.20%, respectively, relative to sole Nd stress, demonstrating a strong synergistic protective effect.

Sole spermidine produced strong increases in endogenous spermidine in the roots, shoots, leaves, and grains by 983.53%, 1014.36%, 1013.10%, and 911.93%, respectively, with marginal increases in endogenous taurine of 9.03%, 9.15%, 4.86%, and 14.42%. Sole Nd stress modestly increased endogenous taurine in roots, shoots, leaves, and grains by 2.89%, 5.45%, 1.39%, and 7.32%, while reducing endogenous spermidine in the same tissues by 5.89%, 6.32%, 6.13%, and 8.35% relative to the control. Co-application of taurine with Nd stress strongly enhanced endogenous taurine in the roots, shoots, leaves, and grains by 674.94%, 849.67%, 937.23%, and 750.71% relative to sole Nd stress, along with concomitant increases in endogenous spermidine of 18.69%, 18.19%, 18.54%, and 25.08%. The Spd + Nd combination produced corresponding increases in endogenous spermidine of 842.04%, 855.98%, 858.96%, and 785.11% across the same tissues, with smaller gains in endogenous taurine of 6.50%, 7.24%, 6.78%, and 9.97%. The combined Tau + Spd + Nd treatment elicited the strongest accumulation of both polyamines, raising endogenous taurine in roots, shoots, leaves, and grains by 717.78%, 916.15%, 1012.92%, and 828.71% and endogenous spermidine by 892.69%, 938.89%, 931.12%, and 867.27%, respectively, relative to sole Nd stress.

Foliar applications of taurine and spermidine significantly modulated Nd accumulation, translocation and bioconcentration in wheat (Figure 8). Compared to the control, sole taurine application marginally decreased the Nd concentrations in roots, shoots, leaves, and grains by 3.94%, 5.75%, 6.20%, and 10.72%, respectively, along with reductions in TF (0.31%) and BCF values for roots, shoots, leaves, and grains by 0.08%, 6.11%, 3.56%, and 8.16%, respectively. Sole spermidine produced negligible changes in tissue Nd and BCFs relative to the control. Sole Nd stress markedly elevated the Nd concentrations in the roots, shoots, leaves, and grains by 185597.50%, 137827.03%, 128059.63%, and 103674.37%, respectively, with substantial increases in BCF root (3420.85%), BCF shoot (2484.80%), BCF in the leaves (2276.36%), and BCF grain (1812.02%), and a 25.94% reduction in TF relative to the control. Pearson correlation analysis

As can be observed from Pearson's correlation matrix, there were unique multivariate correlations between soil and plant parameters within the seven treatments (Figure 9). The total soil Nd and its DTPA extractable forms were highly correlated with one another but showed negative correlations with the available nitrogen, P, K, soil organic matter, and CEC. The soil dehydrogenase activity, soil urease activity, alkaline phosphatase activity and β-glucosidase activity were highly correlated with the microbial biomass C and available nutrients but were negatively correlated with the soil EC and Nd concentration. The plant physiological parameters, including root length, shoot length, root biomass, shoot biomass, and leaf area, were positively correlated with the photosynthesis rate, stomata conductance, chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, total chlorophyll content, and carotenoids but were negatively correlated with MDA and H2O2. SOD and CAT antioxidant enzyme activities were positively correlated with proline and glycine betaine, and the GSH/GSSG ratio was positively correlated with grain yield and the harvest index. The total phenolic content in the grains showed a positive correlation with the shoot Nd concentration, but the shoot K+/Na+ ratio was negatively correlated with Nd accumulation.

Pearson correlation matrix was formed to understand the inter-relationship between soil property variables and their effect on plant growth, physical traits, antioxidant activity, yield parameters, and nitrogen content in wheat when sprayed with taurine (10 mM) and spermidine (1.0 mM), with Nd (150 mg Nd kg−1 soil) as well.

Discussion

Rare earth element contamination has emerged as a pressing global environmental concern, with Nd accumulation in agricultural soils threatening crop productivity and food safety in contaminated regions worldwide. 6 , 17 The present study investigated the mechanisms by which foliar applications of taurine and spermidine, alone and in combination, alleviate Nd-induced toxicity in wheat, addressing the scarcity of mechanistic evidence for polyamine-mediated mitigation of rare earth element stress in cereals.

The current study is the first comprehensive investigation of the use of the combination of taurine and spermidine in a synergetic manner for alleviating the stress caused by Nd. It includes a comprehensive study of the changes in soil physical and chemical properties, soil enzymes, root architectural flexibility, photosynthesis, oxidative defenses, carbon metabolism, ions, and grain quality. In contrast to previous studies that have investigated mainly individual polyamines or transition metal stressors, the present study highlights how a sulfur-derived amino acid (taurine) acts in synergy with an aliphatic polyamine (spermidine) to alter the availability, uptake and physiological responses to Nd stress. 11 , 64 The study of the endogenous accumulation of polyamines in the roots, shoots, leaves, and grains is new in the context of Nd stress.

However, Nd pollution caused notable adverse effects on soil fertility through decreases in the levels of N, P, and K nutrients by 33.82%, 30.30%, and 26.82%, as well as reductions in soil organic matter, cation exchange capacity (CEC) and microbial biomass carbon content. This decrease indicates disturbances at the mechanistic level in soil colloidal stability, the microbial population, and the catalytic ability of enzymes. Rare earth element cations compete with essential nutrient ions for exchange sites on soil colloids, driving the displacement of Ca2+, Mg2+, and K+ from their binding sites and decreasing both CEC and nutrient retention. 11 The concomitant increases in EC and soil pH amplify ionic displacement and proton exchange processes characteristic of rare earth elements, 2 , 5 with soil alkalinization arising from Nd hydrolysis releasing hydroxyl ions and displacing exchangeable acidity. 7 Sole foliar applications of 10 mM taurine and 1.0 mM spermidine produced modest improvements in soil nutrient availability and microbial biomass under non-stress conditions, likely through stimulation of root exudation patterns and rhizosphere microbial activity, 65 as polyamines are known to enhance nutrient mineralization rates and microbial activity by acting as signaling molecules and stimulating soil enzymes. 66 The combined Tau + Spd + Nd treatment produced markedly greater improvements increasing available N, P, and K by 42.82%, 38.32%, and 30.45%, respectively and MBC by 61.09% relative to sole Nd stress, indicative of a synergistic interaction in which taurine and spermidine reduce Nd bioavailability and microbial toxicity through chelation. 11 , 67 Both molecules can form stable complexes with Nd³⁺ via carboxyl and amino functional groups, lowering its bioavailability and toxicity to soil microorganisms. 68

There was significant susceptibility of soil enzyme activity towards Nd stress, with reductions in enzyme activity levels by 52.04% (dehydrogenase), 47.45% (urease), 45.24% (alkaline phosphatase), and 50.37% (β-glucosidase) due to changes in enzyme conformation and competitive inhibition of substrate binding. 2 , 66 The interaction of heavy metals such as sulfhydryl and histidine residues in enzymes causes denaturation of protein structure, leading to blockage of substrate interaction with enzymes. Moreover, Nd3+ may cause displacement of cofactors needed for proper functioning of metalloenzymes such as Mg2+, Zn2+, and Mn2+. 7 , 10 Polyamines in combination with Nd stress showed significant restoration of soil enzyme activity, with increases in activity levels by 85.13% (dehydrogenase) compared to sole Nd treatment. This was achieved due to the electrostatic binding of positively charged polyamines with negatively charged enzyme surfaces, along with ROS removal by polyamines, which helps prevent enzyme inactivation due to oxidation. 66 , 68

It was noted that stress caused by Nd exerted a substantial inhibitory effect on plant growth, where there was a reduction in root length, shoot length, root fresh weight, and shoot fresh weight of 42.46%, 37.00%, 50.64%, and 47.54%, respectively. The above-mentioned reductions are due to the negative impact of Nd on cell division, elongation and the transportation of auxins. 10 , 11 Rare earth ions affect the organization of microtubules and the activities of cyclin-dependent kinases, causing arrest during the G2/M phases of the cell cycle. 2 Furthermore, rare earth ions impair Ca2+ signaling, which is responsible for cell wall relaxation and turgor-driven cell elongation. 7 The synergistic effect of the three treatments, Tau + Spd + Nd, restored plant growth significantly, with the root length improving by 59.13% and a shoot fresh weight improving by 67.95%, compared to the single stress condition of Nd. This was due to the effects on cell-wall extensibility, cytoskeleton arrangement, and hormone signaling by the polyamines. 64 , 65 Polyamines play an important role in plant elongation when stressed through expansin gene regulation and Ca²⁺ signaling. 68

Root architecture characteristics were negatively influenced by Nd treatment, which decreased root density, root volume, root surface area and root tip number but increased average root diameter. This was probably an evolutionary change in the morphology of plants to adapt to the toxic effects of the elements on resource absorption. 2 , 10 Nd interferes with lateral root initiation and apical meristem development by disrupting the formation of maximum auxin at the root tip. 7 It triggers early differentiation in cells with decreased cell division rate in root apical meristems, resulting in thick and short roots with fewer branches. 5 It might be possible that the thickening is an avoidance strategy for minimizing the surface area exposed to toxic ions. 69 , 70 The application of Tau + Spd + Nd restored root architecture by significantly increasing root surface area (by 59.04%) and root tip number (by 70.93%). These results demonstrate that the control of root meristem function, lateral root formation and apical dominance by polyamines is achieved by influencing auxin transport and distribution. 71 , 72

Under stress, physiological and photosynthetic performances were severely affected, as shown by decreased values for RWC, MSI, chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, total chlorophyll content, SPAD, carotenoids, and Fv/Fm of 28.02%, 35.93%, 47.81%, 48.29%, 46.67%, 37.37%, 42.89%, and 22.00%, respectively. This is consistent with the damage to thylakoid membranes, pigment degradation, and photoinhibition of PSII. 2 , 68 Rare earth elements localize in chloroplasts and disrupt the photosynthetic electron transfer chain owing to their resemblance with plastoquinone and plastocyanin. 10 ROS production induced by Nd damages the thylakoids, leading to impaired photosynthetic efficiency. 73 , 74 The application of foliar polyamines showed high restoration of photosynthetic ability, where the co-application of Tau + Spd + Nd resulted in a 69.38% increase in chlorophyll a and 64.95% in total chlorophyll Compared to treatment by Nd only. The results imply that chloroplast integrity is due to the effects of polyamines on photosynthetic pigment and light harvesting complex stability. 75

Photosynthesis was suppressed through stomatal, mesophyll and biochemical limitations by Nd. The photosynthesis rate (A), transpiration rate (E) and stomatal conductance (gs ) decreased by 46.37%, 46.91%, and 48.76%, respectively, while intercellular CO₂ concentration (Ci ) increased, which showed that stomata were closed due to reduced mesophyll conductance and Rubisco activity. 2 , 10 Carbonic anhydrase is inhibited by heavy metals to modify and reduce Rubisco activation state through oxidative damage of catalytic cysteines. Meanwhile, Nd disturbs signaling processes inside guard cells, thus causing stomatal closure by limiting the diffusion of CO₂ within intercellular spaces. 5 Reduction in mesophyll conductance happens because of thickened cell walls and a reduction in the chloroplast surface area within intercellular spaces. 76 , 77 Simultaneous application of polyamines substantially improved gas exchange functions; the combination of Tau + Spd + Nd enhanced A and gs by 69.23% and 72.70%, respectively, compared to individual Nd stress. Such reactions result from the regulatory role of polyamines in the modulation of stomatal aperture physiology, aquaporins expression and enzymes responsible for carbon fixation activities, 78 , 79 namely, the upregulation of Rubisco activase genes and prevention of enzyme degradation. 68 Moreover, spermidine also improved mesophyll conductance by ensuring the flexible state of the cell walls and chloroplasts position. 80 , 81

Nd toxicity greatly increased the levels of oxidative stress biomarkers, with increases in MDA, H2O2, O2 −, and electrolyte leakage of 216.55%, 278.44%, 214.34%, and 197.09%, respectively, in line with increased ROS production, lipid peroxidation and membrane disruption caused by Nd. The presence of heavy metals facilitates both Fenton and Haber-Weiss-type reactions, resulting in the generation of hydroxyl radicals, which cause lipid peroxidation and membrane injury. 82 , 83 In addition, exposure to Nd hampers the electron flow of chloroplasts and mitochondria, thus leading to increased electron leakage and superoxide radicals, while simultaneously inhibiting the action of antioxidant enzymes, causing an increase in the ROS concentration. 7 Membrane lipids containing polyunsaturated fatty acids are highly susceptible to lipid peroxidation, where MDA represents the end product of oxidation. 84 , 85 The application of polyamines via the leaves greatly reduced the damage and the application of Tau + Spd + Nd reduced the MDA and H2O2 levels by 53.01% and 55.04%, respectively. This is due to the increased ability for antioxidant scavenging, electrostatic stabilization of membranes through polyamines and the induction of enzymes that detoxify ROS. 64 , 65

There was a positive compensation effect on antioxidant enzymes, leading to an increase in SOD, POX, CAT, APX, GPX, and GR activities by 74.19%, 75.80%, 48.98%, 81.36%, 92.45%, and 66.90%, respectively, suggesting the existence of an adaptive antioxidant mechanism to counteract oxidative effects. 2 The transcription of antioxidant genes is regulated by stress-responsive transcription factors in cases of metal toxicity. 86 , 87 The combination of foliar polyamines and Nd resulted in an improvement in the activities of SOD and APX by 60.51% and 88.71%, respectively, compared to the application of Nd alone. This is likely due to the transcriptional induction of antioxidant genes by polyamines, post-translational stabilization of enzymes and synergism with the enzymatic and non-enzymatic antioxidant systems. 64 , 65 Polyamines control the expression of superoxide dismutase, catalase and ascorbate peroxidase using stress response signaling pathways, 68 while spermidine stabilizes the enzyme structure and protects enzyme active sites from oxidative damage. 88 , 89

Exposure to Nd affected proline and glutathione metabolism, leading to increased levels of proline and glycine betaine and decreased concentration of GSH along with the GSH/GSSG redox ratio, signifying impairment in redox balance and osmoregulation. 2 Proline acts as an osmoprotectant, which helps maintain membrane stability and protein structures under environmental stress, 90 , 91 Whereas oxidative stress triggered by Nd exposure leads to the oxidation of glutathione, resulting in a decrease in the GSH/GSSG redox ratio, which acts as a cellular redox buffers. 5 The application of a combination of Tau + Spd + Nd significantly restored osmolyte status and redox homeostasis, enhancing the level of proline and GSH/GSSG ratio by 70.38% and 202.46%, respectively, compared to Nd alone. These effects represent improvement in glutathione redox signaling and regeneration along with the synthesis of osmoprotectants due to polyamines. 64 , 65 Polyamines have shared biosynthetic precursors and regulating mechanisms similar to those of osmolytes, increasing the activity of P5CS. 92 , 93

Nd significantly alters plant shoot carbon metabolism and biochemistry. The accumulation of soluble sugars under Nd stress likely serves a dual purpose: acting as compatible osmoprotectants to maintain cellular turgor and resulting from the stress-induced inhibition of starch synthesis enzymes or reduced sink demand due to impaired reproductive development. 2 , 5 Concurrently, Nd prevents protein synthesis and promotes proteolysis. The Tau + Spd + Nd combination partly corrected these effects, improving protein concentrations by 47.57% relative to sole Nd stress. This suggests that spermidine protects ribosomal integrity and facilitates stress-adapted translation, while taurine supports efficient nitrogen assimilation. 68 , 94

Nd demonstrated a strong negative effect on yield and associated parameters. Nd decreased the spike length, grains per spike, 1000-grain weight, grain yield, straw yield, biological yield and harvest index by 35.05%. 41.03%, 36.47%, 57.07%, 46.56%, 48.38%, and 14.27%, respectively. The decrease in these yield parameters was due to Nd’s ability to hinder grain filling and the partitioning of assimilates, as well as reproductive development. 2 Heavy metals also affect flowering, pollination and fertilization and initiate oxidative damage to reproductive tissues. 7 Nd inhibits grain filling by decreasing the supply of photosynthate and by inhibiting the enzymes of starch synthesis. 5 The yield attributes improved, and the grain yield and harvest index jumped 97.51% and 11.54%, respectively, with the application of polyamines when mixed with taurine and spermidine and placed under the same context as neodymium stress. These yield improvements also show the role of polyamines in the preservation of floral initiation, pollen viability and development of the grain. 95 Zhang and Turgeon 96 and Broussard, Abadie 97 also mentioned that polyamines improve the loading and unloading of phloem. Spermidine also increased grain development and reduced oxidative stress. 98

Grain quality attributes were significantly altered by Nd, with reductions in crude protein, reducing sugars, non-reducing sugars and total soluble sugars, alongside increases in total phenolics and flavonoids. These changes indicate that stress diminished nutritional quality while inducing the accumulation of secondary metabolites as a defense response. Heavy metals reduce grain protein content by impairing nitrogen assimilation and amino acid biosynthesis, 7 while elevated phenolics and flavonoids accumulate under stress because of their antioxidant and metal-chelating activities. 5 Foliar polyamine application substantially restored grain nutritional quality; combined Tau + Spd + Nd treatment increased crude protein and total phenolics by 34.45% and 41.89%, respectively, relative to sole Nd stress. The increased activity is due to better carbohydrate metabolism, phenylpropanoid metabolism and efficient nitrogen assimilation, 65 as well as improved nitrogen metabolism and protein synthesis. 68 In addition, spermidine contributed to higher-quality grains by stimulating phenolics production. 99

Endogenous polyamine uptake and accumulation exhibited tissue-specific kinetics in response to Nd stress. Minor increases in endogenous taurine and minor reductions in endogenous spermidine levels in roots, shoots, leaves and grains suggested that Nd exposure affects polyamine synthesis and metabolism. 65 Heavy metals modulate the transcription of polyamine biosynthesis genes, increase polyamine oxidase activity and decrease endogenous polyamine content. 68 Foliar exogenous application led to increased endogenous polyamine levels; under Tau + Spd + Nd stress conditions, there was an increase in the concentration of endogenous taurine and spermidine in shoots by 916.15% and 938.89%, respectively, compared to Nd stress alone. This suggests that polyamines regulate biosynthetic enzyme activity, transport efficiency and tissue-specific distribution. 100 The exogenous application of polyamines involves their entry into plant cells via specific active transport processes and subsequent transport through the phloem and xylem. 93 , 101

Nd bioaccumulation, translocation and bioconcentration showed remarkable tissue specificity. The high levels of Nd concentration within various tissues, such as roots, shoots, leaves and grains, alongside low translocation factors and varying bioconcentration factors, were an outcome of the influence of the kinetics of Nd uptake, root-to-shoot translocation and grain loading. 2 Nd bioaccumulated specifically within plant roots through cell wall adsorption and vacuole precipitation. 7 The application of foliar polyamines greatly inhibited Nd bioaccumulation within tissues; the Tau + Spd + Nd treatment decreased tissue Nd concentrations by 39.50% (roots), 54.93% (shoots), and 63.57% (grains) compared to the sole Nd stress conditions. This was achieved through polyamine-induced chelation of Nd ions within the rhizosphere, competitive inhibition of Nd transporters and increased vacuolar accumulation. Polyamines chelated Nd ions into complexes that limited Nd uptake, 68 whereas spermidine and taurine induced the upregulation of vacuolar metal transporters. 11

The nutrient composition and ion homeostasis of wheat shoots were greatly affected by the presence of Nd. The reduction in the levels of nitrogen, phosphorous, potassium, iron, zinc, manganese, and copper, along with increased sodium and chloride ions (Figure 12), indicated the detrimental effects of Nd ion selectivity and nutrient uptake by affecting the functioning of membrane transporters. 2 In addition, rare earth elements compete with vital nutrients for absorption and affect the membrane potential, which plays a key role in selecting the ions transported through membranes. 7 Furthermore, Nd also inhibits plasma membrane H+-ATPase activity, thereby interfering with the proton gradient necessary for nutrient transport. 5 Foliar spray with polyamines significantly improved ion homeostasis.

Multivariate analyses highlighted unique response signatures to individual treatments within soil and plant traits. Pearson correlation analysis indicated significant positive correlations between total and available Nd but negative correlations with available nutrients (N, P, and K), soil organic carbon and CEC. Significant positive correlations were observed between soil enzymes (dehydrogenase, urease and alkaline phosphatase) and microbial biomass carbon and available nutrients but were negatively correlated with EC and Nd concentration. 2 , 7 Growth traits (root, shoot length and biomass) showed significant positive correlations among themselves but had negative correlations with the MDA and H2O2. SOD and CAT activities showed significant positive correlations with proline and glycine betaine, while the GSH/GSSG ratio showed positive correlations with grain yield and the harvest index. 65 , 68 It was evident from the radar chart visualization that only Nd stress created an imbalanced profile dominated by stress parameters, growth and soil fertility-related parameters, whereas taurine and spermidine alone broadened the profile towards photosynthesis and growth indices. Treatment with Tau + Spd + Nd produced resulted in the best balance of all the parameters with regard to increased grain yield, harvest index, osmolyte concentration and glutathione redox metabolism, as well as decreased tissue Nd content. 11 , 67 The PCA biplot was able to explain 99.71% of the variance (PC1 = 87.81%, PC2 = 11.53%, and PC3 = 0.37%), with separation of the control, taurine only and spermidine only treatments by agronomic properties (grain yield, A, total chlorophyll content, and biomass) and the sole Nd treatment by stress factors (shoot Nd, MDA, EC, and pH). Treatments with amendments × Nd (Tau + Nd, Spd + Nd and Tau + Spd + Nd) moved along the PC1 axis into the unstressed zone, resulting in metabolic benefits.

Limitations and future perspectives

While this controlled pot experiment provides robust mechanistic insights into Tau-Spd-mediated mitigation of Nd toxicity, it is subject to the inherent limitations of containerized studies, such as restricted root exploration and simplified microbial communities. Future research must prioritize field-scale validation to account for complex soil-plant-microbe interactions, environmental variability and the long-term fate of applied polyamines in diverse agro-ecosystems.

Conclusions

Rare earth element pollution in agricultural soils has been recognized as a global issue, posing potential risks to crop production and food security. In this regard, the current study aimed to assess the impact of spray treatment with taurine and spermidine on the reduction in Nd-induced toxicity in wheat plants, considering the limited data on the amelioration of rare earth element stress in cereals using polyamines. Treatment with 150 mg Nd kg−1 soil caused severe damage to soil physiochemical conditions, root development, photosynthetic efficiency, the antioxidant system, ionic balance and yield characteristics. While the sole spray treatments of 10 mM taurine and 1.0 mM spermidine improved plant physiological status without stress, their combination with Nd gradually alleviated stress effects via osmotic adjustment and the activation of enzymatic antioxidant systems. The most remarkable improvement was achieved from the spray combination of 10 mM taurine, 1.0 mM spermidine, and 150 mg Nd kg−1 soil, which showed significantly decreased the absorption and translocation of Nd, improved the activity of enzymes and microbial biomass in the soil and enhanced the antioxidant status as well as the maintenance of yield and quality characteristics. The uniqueness of this experiment is its ability to prove that the application of foliar sprays of taurine and spermidine together can effectively protect wheat from toxicity caused by rare earth elements, which opens wide prospects for further investigations in the field of stress response mechanisms in plants and their relationships with the soil. The results of the present study can be used as a starting point for developing polyamine-containing foliar sprays to protect cereals grown in Nd-polluted soils.
