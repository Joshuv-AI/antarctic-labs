The effect of the liquid motion bubbler timer soothing sensory toy on children’s fear and anxiety levels during blood sampling: a randomized clinical trial
Authors: Arzu Sariali̇oğlu, Tuğba Oluç
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBlood sampling is one of the invasive procedures in a hospital setting that can cause fear, anxiety, and stress in children.AimThe study aimed to determine the effect of the Liquid Motion Bubbler Timer Soothing Sensory Toy on children’s fear and anxiety levels during blood sampling.MethodsThe study was conducted with a total of 90 children aged 4–6 years, divided into two groups (45 in the intervention group and 45 in the control group). The data were collected by face-to-face interviews using the “Questionnaire Form”, “Children’s Fear Scale”, and “Children’s State Anxiety”. Children in the intervention group were made to play “Liquid Motion Bubbler Timer Soothing Sensory Toy” before and during the blood sampling procedure. The Dependent Groups T-Test, the Wilcoxon Test, the Independent Groups T-Test, and the Mann-Whitney U-Test were used in the analysis of the data.ResultsThe Liquid Motion Bubbler Timer Soothing Sensory Toy group had significantly lower fear and anxiety scores compared to the control group (p<0.05). The Liquid Motion Bubble Timer Soothing Sensory Toy has a significant effect on children’s fear and anxiety.ConclusionThe innovative “Liquid Motion Bubble Timer Soothing Sensory Toy” is effective against fear and anxiety in children, and can be used by healthcare professionals working in pediatric units during invasive procedures with children.Trial registrationClinicalTrials.gov (No: NCT06476418, Date: 03/05/2024, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06476418?locStr=Erzurum,%20T%C3%BCrkiye&country=T%C3%BCrkiye&state=Erzurum&city=Erzurum&viewType=Card&cond=child&rank=17)Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-04943-2.

Introduction

In the hospital environment, children encounter many procedures that cause fear and anxiety. Blood sampling is one of these procedures. Blood drawing is one of the most stressful invasive procedures for children, often causing fear, anxiety, pain, and a lack of trust in healthcare professionals or medical procedures [1, 2]. Children who frequently experience these feelings may feel more pain, fear, and stress during subsequent invasive procedures [3]. The anxiety, fear, and pain experienced during blood sampling depend on the child’s age; younger children are generally more fearful of needles than older children. Research suggests that reported needle fear and pain decrease with age, suggesting an inverse relationship between age and these experiences [4]. Furthermore, approximately 75% of children under the age of 9 have reported fear of needles [4]. Factors that cause children to experience anxiety and fear in hospitals include age, previous hospital experiences, social adjustment problems, inadequate coping skills, and parental anxiety [5].

The preschool period is a crucial developmental stage in which children’s cognitive and psychosocial development progresses rapidly. According to Piaget, the preschool period is the pre-operational stage in which children make sense of their environment through symbolic play and imagination [6]. According to Erikson’s Theory, the preschool period is the “initiative versus guilt” phase, where children’s tendencies towards initiative and interaction increase [7]. When preschool children develop self-awareness, they fear that their body integrity will be disrupted, and they might lose their strength when invasive procedures are performed on them with medical instruments [8, 9]. They also fear losing control over their bodies [10].

Nurses must be aware of the characteristics of a child’s developmental stage during invasive procedures and perform the procedure in a way that is appropriate for the child’s developmental stage [11]. Nurses use various methods to manage the negative emotions that children face as a result of invasive procedures and to prevent these emotions from causing trauma in children [12, 13]. These methods are known as non-pharmacological methods [14–19]. They are cost-effective and easy to apply [16]. A frequently used non-pharmacological method is distraction [20]. Distraction has become a widespread technique in child health in recent years [21]. Distraction is handled under two subheadings: active and passive distraction. The active method refers to the child’s active participation in the process and cooperation with the child. In the passive method, on the contrary, the child remains calm and quiet, and a distracting stimulus is given [21, 22]. Recent systematic reviews and meta-analyses have shown that active and passive distraction methods are more effective in reducing pain and anxiety experienced by children during the procedure [22, 23]. Studies comparing active and passive distraction methods show that active methods have a higher effect size; however, passive methods are more advantageous in terms of ease of application, cost, and accessibility [24].Nurses should know how to apply these methods [11].

In the literature review, various attention diversion applications were made, such as the use of puppets [19], cartoons and musical toys [15], virtual reality [25], buzzy and distraction cards [26], clowns [27], and kaleidoscope interventions [28]. Distraction methods during blood sampling can make significant contributions to nursing practices. By applying these techniques, nurses can support children in accessing healthcare services without fear and prevent traumatic experiences related to the procedure. One of the key strengths of this study is the use of a soothing and moving toy as a distraction method to reduce children’s fear and anxiety levels during blood sampling. Literature reports the use of methods such as puppets [19], virtual reality [25], music, or cartoons [15], Pinwheel and stress ball [29] and teddy bear [30] for distraction during invasive procedures in children. However, studies using a soothing and moving toy as a distraction method are limited. In this respect, the study contributes to the literature by offering an innovative and easily applicable approach that can be used during blood sampling in pediatric patients. The “Liquid Motion Bubble Timer Soothing Sensory Toy” used in our research is a novel distraction toy, and no previous research has been conducted on this toy. The aim of this study was to determine the effect of the Liquid Motion Bubbler Timer Soothing Sensory Toy on children’s fear and anxiety levels during blood sampling.

Methods

Design and setting

The study had a randomised controlled experimental design (NCT06476418) and was conducted between July and October, 2024, at the pediatric blood sampling unit in Erzincan, Türkiye. The flowchart of the research is summarized in Fig. 1.

Sample size and randomisation

A priori power analysis was conducted using the G Power Programme to determine the sample size. The post-procedure Children’s Fear Scale score averages were utilised in the Oluç & Sarıalioglu (2023) [19] study to calculate the effect size. The effect size was calculated as 0.71 (f = 0.71) with 5% margin of error (α = 0.05) and 95% power (1-β = 0.95). The sample size for each group was calculated as 40, and the study was planned to be conducted with 80 children. Data were collected from 90 children, with 45 assigned to the intervention group and 45 to the control group. A 10% backup sample was included to account for potential data losses. A post hoc power analysis conducted using the G Power programme indicated that the actual power of the study was 0.99, with an effect size of 0.77. The study found that both the achieved effect size and power exceeded the initially determined values of 0.50 for effect size and 0.95 for power. This indicates that the results are clinically significant [31]. The principles stated in the CONSORT 2010 guideline were followed during the study process (see Fig. 2). The intervention and control groups were randomised using a specific programme (https://www.randomizer.org/).

Data collection tools

Questionnaire Form, Children’s Fear Scale, and Children’s State Anxiety were used to collect the research data. “Liquid Motion Bubbler Timer Soothing Sensory Toy” was used as an intervention tool.

Questionnaire form

This form, prepared by the researcher by reviewing the literature, included questions such as “age, gender, previous hospitalisation experience, blood sampling in the last 6 months, preparation before blood sampling, reaction to blood sampling, and reaction to the materials used” [15, 19, 32]. This form is a combined version of the parent and child profile forms. Since the survey form contains introductory information, this section was filled out by asking the parent questions.

Children’s Fear Scale (CFS)

The scale was developed by McMurtry et al. in 2011 [33] to measure fear in children undergoing painful medical procedures. In 2018, its validity and reliability were assessed in Turkiye by Gerçeker et al. [34]. It is a scale developed for children aged 4–10 years. The CFS is a 0–4 scale with five faces ranging from a neutral statement (0 = no concern at all) to a feared face (4 = severe anxiety). Since the scale is in the form of a scoring system, item-total correlation and Cronbach’s alpha coefficient could not be calculated.

Children’s State Anxiety (CSA)

The scale was developed by Ersig et al. in 2013 [35]. The Turkish validity and reliability study of the scale was conducted by Özalp Gerçeker et al. in 2018 [34]. Like a thermometer with horizontal lines, the CSA is scored between 0 and 10 as a scale developed for children aged 4–10 years. When the scale is introduced to the child, the anxiety increases as it goes upwards, and at the top the level of anxiety is very high. Afterwards, the child is asked to put a line on the part that shows how anxious or nervous they are. Since the scale is in the form of a scoring system, item-total correlation and Cronbach’s alpha coefficient could not be calculated.

Liquid motion bubbler timer soothing sensory toy

“Liquid Motion Bubbler Timer Soothing Sensory Toy” was used as an intervention tool in the research. Guided by the cute rabbit pouffe inside, there is a fascinating view of the falling balls. It is used for stress relief, anxiety relief, and anger management. It is made of a unique material that is resistant to breakage. In this way, it does not carry any risk for children. It takes approximately 4 min for the rabbit in the toy to go down. The toy can be used upside down like an hourglass. The toy is available for preschool children. Its fascinating design and mobility attracts the attention of children. It contains non-toxic materials and is safe for children as it is made of hard, solid plastic by injection moulding with non-toxic mineral oil and water (Fig. 3).

Data collection

After informing the children and their parents about the study and obtaining verbal consent and written permission from the parents, data were collected through face-to-face interviews in the blood sampling room with the children who agreed to participate. The same nurse collected blood from the children in the study and collected the data.

Blood sampling room

The blood sampling room is an intervention room located in the corridor of the children’s outpatient clinic. Children who will undergo blood sampling procedures are brought to this room upon the doctor’s request. Due to the physical infrastructure of the room, blood can only be taken from one child at a time. The door of the children’s blood sampling room is kept closed during the blood collection process to prevent other children from being affected. An initial interview is conducted with the family and child at the entrance before they are taken into the blood sampling room. Pens are provided for children to use for marking. After the blood sampling is completed, the child leaves the room with a toy as a gift.

Blinding

In this study, both the intervention and data collection procedures were carried out by the same nurse to ensure standardization. Due to the nature of the intervention, blinding was not feasible. The same nurse administered the intervention and collected the outcome data; therefore, assessor blinding was not possible.

Pilot implementation

The pilot implementation was carried out with four children. The children included in this phase were not part of the research group. Based on their feedback, the researcher determined the ideal play time length of the toy.

Procedure

Control group

The researcher filled out Questionnaires and scales 5 min before blood sampling. Only routine blood sampling was performed. The scales were re-administered 5 min after the procedure was completed. Filling out the forms took an average of 5–10 min. The toy was given to the child after the procedure.

Intervention group

The researcher administered the questionnaire and scales 5 min before the blood sampling. The child was introduced to the toy and allowed to play with it 3 min before the blood sampling. It was explained that the child was free to watch the fascinating rabbit movements throughout the procedure and to count the feast of droplets occurring in the toy flow if they wished. The children were told to give the toy a name if they wanted, and that this toy would accompany them during the procedure. The children were mesmerised by the toy and transformed the scary hospital environment into fun. After the procedure was completed, the scales were reapplied after waiting 5 min. Filling out the forms took an average of 5–10 min. The toy was presented to the child after the procedure (Fig. 4, 5).

Data analysis

The SPSS package program was used to analyze the data. Participants’ sociodemographic characteristics were summarized using percentage and frequency distributions. The Chi-Square Test was used to assess the sociodemographic similarity between the intervention and control groups. Normal distribution of the data was determined based on skewness and kurtosis values (ranging from + 3 to -3). The fear and anxiety posttest measurements of the intervention group were not normally distributed, while the other measurements were usually distributed. In comparing two dependent score averages, the Dependent Groups T-Test was used for the measurements showing normal distribution, and the Wilcoxon Test was used for the groups that did not show normal distribution. In comparing two independent mean scores, the Independent Groups T-Test was used in the measurements showing normal distribution, and the Mann-Whitney U-Test was used in the groups not showing normal distribution. The effect size was given by the Cohen’s d value. Statistical significance value was accepted as p < 0.05.

Ethical principles

This study received 03/05/2024 dated, and B.30.2.ATA.0.01.00/272 numbered approval was taken from Atatürk University Non-Interventional Clinical Research Ethics Committee. The purpose of the study was explained to the children who met the participation criteria, and their questions were answered. Verbal consent was obtained from the children, and written informed consent was obtained from their parents or legal guardians for participants under the age of 16. Ethical principles were followed in all stages of the study. The research process was conducted in accordance with the principles of respect for autonomy, confidentiality, and the Declaration of Helsinki; verbal consent was obtained from participants, and data were coded anonymously and made accessible only to the research team. Participants had the right to withdraw from the study at any time.

The control group’s mean fear score was 1.24 ± 1.61, and after the procedure, it increased significantly and became 1.98 ± 1.60 (t=-2.606, p = 0.012, d = 1.888). Discussion

Venipuncture is one of the most commonly used needle applications in the diagnosis and treatment processes in children [36, 37]. This procedure can cause significant fear, anxiety, and pain in children [38]. This study showed that distraction with the “Liquid Motion Bubbler Timer Soothing Sensory Toy” during blood sampling reduced children’s fear and anxiety. These results support the research hypotheses H1.1 and H1.2. The specialized toy used in this study represents the first research of its kind in this field.

The literature found that different methods during blood sampling reduced the level of fear after blood sampling [15, 19, 39, 40]. It has been found that using cartoon character stickers and a vacuum gun during blood sampling can reduce children’s fear [41]. It was found that the light toy given to children during blood sampling reduced their fear levels [42]. The use of virtual reality and distraction cards was found to reduce the level of fear in children during painful procedures [43]. The use of buzzers and whistles during blood sampling has also been found to reduce children’s fear [40]. Animal characters (butterfly, peacock, and rabbit) attached to the vacutainer were found to be effective in reducing fear levels in children during blood sampling [11]. Therapeutic play using a doll during blood sampling has reduced children’s fear levels [44]. Using storytelling caterpillars during blood collection has reduced children’s fear levels [45]. The findings of the current study are generally consistent with the results of studies in the literature using different intervention methods.

In the literature, different methods have been found to reduce anxiety levels after blood sampling [1, 14, 17, 39]. It has been found that using virtual reality during blood sampling reduces anxiety in children [25, 46]. Finger puppets, abeslang puzzles, and wind rose were found to reduce anxiety during the blood sampling process [14]. It was found that watching cartoons during the blood sampling effectively reduced anxiety scores in children [39]. Therapeutic play with a teddy bear has been found to reduce anxiety in children during invasive procedures [17]. Therapeutic play with a doll during blood sampling has reduced anxiety levels in children [44]. Using storytelling caterpillars during blood collection reduced anxiety levels in children [45]. Anxiety levels in children decreased during blood sampling procedures that used storytelling [47]. The findings of the current study are generally consistent with the results of studies in the literature using different intervention methods.

Strengths

This study is the first of its kind to investigate the effects of distraction using a “Liquid Motion Bubbler Timer Soothing Sensory Toy” on children’s fear and anxiety levels during blood sampling. Other notable features of the study include the fact that the research was conducted with a randomized controlled experimental design and that the analyses were performed by an independent statistician to ensure the security of the data.

Limitations

The study’s limitations include the fact that it was conducted only in one province in Eastern Turkiye. It is also important to consider that environmental and family factors may influence children’s responses. The fact that data was collected only from children, without additional data from parents or healthcare professionals, may limit the interpretation of the results. It should also be considered that children may perceive and respond to questions differently depending on their age. Furthermore, data collection via self-report may lead to response bias. Moreover, the generalizability of the results may be limited because the study was conducted in a single center. Additionally, the study design focused on results obtained immediately after blood sampling. Therefore, the long-term effects of the intervention on children’s fear and anxiety levels could not be assessed. Additionally, the lack of blinding and the use of a single researcher for both intervention and outcome assessment may have introduced measurement bias.

Implication for practice

The results of this study demonstrate that distraction methods, such as using toys during blood sampling, can be effective in reducing children’s fear and anxiety levels. Therefore, healthcare professionals, especially nurses, can incorporate the use of toys into their clinical practice to redirect children’s attention to different stimuli during invasive procedures. The ease of use, low cost, and familiarity of toys to children can facilitate the integration of this method into routine care practices in pediatric clinics. Furthermore, such distraction methods can increase children’s cooperation with healthcare professionals during the procedure and contribute to a less stressful environment.

Conclusion

This study marks the first time a liquid motion bubbler timer soothing sensory toy has been used. Thanks to its calming properties, this toy helps reduce fear and anxiety in children during challenging situations. In conclusion, the findings suggest that this fun and calming toy may be beneficial, and its use in different settings and populations is recommended for future research to enhance the evidence on its effectiveness and applicability.

How might this information affect nursing practice?

Thanks to the innovative Liquid Motion Bubbler Timer Soothing Sensory Toy used, fear and anxiety experienced during blood sampling have decreased significantly. The use of such cheap, easily accessible, and effective methods in child-related clinics will both increase the quality of care and increase the satisfaction level of the care provided by the nurse. Pediatric nurses will help themselves feel happy and energetic by reducing the workload that will occur by using the methods used in the research. As a result of the evaluation made with pediatric nurses at the end of the research, they stated that the Liquid Motion Bubbler Timer Soothing Sensory Toy application made them happy and should be widespread.
