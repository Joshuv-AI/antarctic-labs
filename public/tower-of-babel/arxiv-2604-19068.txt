Abstract
We recently introduced a novel architecture for the design of validated IVP algorithms. This architecture forms the basis of our complete validated algorithm for IVP. A key subroutine in our algorithm is the Euler Tube : it gave a technique for refining end- and full-enclosures and is also key to deriving a complexity bound of our IVP solver. In this paper, we generalize it to Taylor Tube of degree p ≥ 1 p\geq 1 . As expected, higher-degree Taylor Tubes improve accuracy. But surprisingly, our experiments show that it can also lead to an overall speedup when combined with bisection.

Recently, we introduced an algorithm

By “complete” we mean that our algorithm is a halting algorithm.
Most validated algorithms are only

2
2

2

This is a standard terminology in theoretical computer science.

in the sense that, the output is

We note some features in our solution:
(1) We guarantee that the “over-approximation” of

(2) Our algorithm is fully automatic; it does not require

(3) In preliminary implementations, our algorithm
has proved to be practical.
It is reasonable to expect that the algorithm takes a performance hit
in order to ensure completeness. But surprisingly, it
often performs better than the incomplete algorithms.

Our previous papers
[
9
,
10
]

introduced a novel architecture for

Within this framework, many of our original

The case

Our experiments show that a higher degree for

surprisingly, they can also lead to overall speedup.
We remark that such tube techniques were critical
for our ability to prove complexity bounds for our

Appendix A: Proofs

Proof of Lemma
2
.

For simplicity, assume that
h=m{\overline{h}}
for some integer
m
, and write

t_{i}=i{\overline{h}},\qquad i=0,\dots,m.

The proof below is easily adjusted if the last step size is
<{\overline{h}}
.

For each
i=0,\dots,m-1
, let

{\bm{q}}_{i}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathrel{\,:=\,}}T^{p}_{{\bm{q}}_{0},{\overline{h}}}(t_{i}),\qquad g_{i}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathrel{\,:=\,}}\|{\bm{q}}_{i}-{\bm{x}}(t_{i})\|

and

{\bm{x}}_{i}:[0,{\overline{h}}]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

denotes the exact solution of

{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\prime}(s)={\bm{f}}({\bm{x}}_{i}(s))
,
{\bm{x}}_{i}(0)={\bm{q}}_{i}
.
By hypothesis,
\mbox{Image}({\bm{q}}_{i},{\overline{h}})\subseteq B_{1}
.
Since
B_{1}
is a full enclosure of
({\bm{q}}_{0},h)
, we also have

{\bm{x}}([t_{i},t_{i+1}])\subseteq B_{1}
.
Hence, we can invoke Lemma
1
to
bound the error between
{\bm{x}}
and
{\bm{x}}_{i}

over their common time interval:

\|{\bm{x}}_{i}(s)-{\bm{x}}(t_{i}+s)\|\leq\|{\bm{q}}_{i}-{\bm{x}}(t_{i})\|e^{{\overline{\mu}}s}=g_{i}e^{{\overline{\mu}}s}.

(16)

Next, we bound the error

E(t)
between the Taylor curve and
{\bm{x}}
at
time
t\in[0,h]
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{llllll}E(t)&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathrel{\,:=\,}}&\|T^{p}_{{\bm{q}}_{0},{\overline{h}}}(t)-{\bm{x}}(t)\|\\
&=&\|T^{p}_{{\bm{q}}_{i}}(s)-{\bm{x}}(t_{i}+s)\|\\
&&\text{\hskip 18.49988pt(where $i=\left\lfloor{t/{\overline{h}}}\right\rfloor$,
$t=s+t_{i}$ and $0\leq s<{\overline{h}}$)}\\
&\leq&\|T^{p}_{{\bm{q}}_{i}}(s)-{\bm{x}}_{i}(s)\|+\|{\bm{x}}_{i}(s)-{\bm{x}}(t_{i}+s)\|\\
&&\text{\hskip 18.49988pt(by triangular inequality and
${\bm{q}}_{i}={\bm{x}}_{i}(0)={\bm{x}}(t_{i})$)}\\
&\leq&\|T^{p}_{{\bm{q}}_{i}}(s)-{\bm{x}}_{i}(s)\|+g_{i}e^{{\overline{\mu}}s}\\
&&\text{\hskip 18.49988pt(by ({\ref{eq:error-i}}))}\\
&=&\|f^{[p+1]}({\bm{q}}_{i})\xi^{p+1}\|+g_{i}e^{{\overline{\mu}}s}\\
&&\text{\hskip 18.49988pt(Taylor remainder, $0\leq\xi\leq s$)}\\
&\leq&{\overline{M}}s^{p+1}+g_{i}e^{{\overline{\mu}}s}\\
&&\text{\hskip 18.49988pt(${\bm{q}}_{i}\in B_{1}$ and
$\|{\bm{f}}^{[p+1]}(B_{1})\|\leq{\overline{M}}$).}\end{array}

Let
\phi(s)
be the last expression in this derivation:

\phi_{i}(s){\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathrel{\,:=\,}}{\overline{M}}s^{p+1}+g_{i}e^{{\overline{\mu}}s}.

(17)

Note that
\phi_{i}(s)
is convex,
being the sum of convex functions

{\overline{M}}s^{p+1}
and
g_{i}e^{{\overline{\mu}}s}
,
and so its extremal values are obtained at
\phi_{i}(0)

and
\phi_{i}({\overline{h}})
. Let
\phi:[0,h]\to{\mathbb{R}}
where

\phi(t){\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathrel{\,:=\,}}\phi_{i}(s)
if
t=s+t_{i}
,
i=\left\lfloor{s/{\overline{h}}}\right\rfloor
.
Thus
\phi
is an upper bound on the error function,

E(t)\leq\phi(t).

By construction
\phi(t)
is a continuous function
whose extremal values are attained at
\phi(t_{i})

(
i=0,\ldots,m
). Next define

G_{i}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathrel{\,:=\,}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllllllllllllllllllllllll}0&\mbox{{\ if}}\ i=0,\\
{\overline{M}}{\overline{h}}^{p+1}+G_{i-1}e^{{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}}}&\mbox{{\ if}}\ i\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

(18)

From (
17
), we see that

\phi(t_{i})\leq G_{i}
for each
i
.
We claim that

G_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllllllllllllllllllllllll}{\overline{M}}{\overline{h}}^{p+1}&\mbox{{\ if}}\ {\overline{\mu}}=0,\\
{\overline{M}}{\overline{h}}^{p+1}\left(\frac{e^{i{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}}}-1}{e^{{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}}}-1}\right)&\mbox{{\ if}}\ {\overline{\mu}}\neq 0.\end{array}\right.

(19)

The case
{\overline{\mu}}=0
follows from the formula
for
{\overline{\mu}}\neq 0
by applying L’Hôpital’s rule.
Observe that the sequence

G_{i}
is non-decreasing with
i
(even
if
{\overline{\mu}}<0
).
We prove (
19
) by induction on
i
.
The case
i=0
is clear, and for
i\geq 1
:

\begin{array}[]{llllll}G_{i}&=&{\overline{M}}{\overline{h}}^{p+1}+G_{i-1}e^{{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}}}&\text{(from ({\ref{eq:Gi}}))}\\
&=&{\overline{M}}{\overline{h}}^{p+1}+{\overline{M}}{\overline{h}}^{p+1}\left(\frac{e^{(i-1){\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}}}-1}{e^{{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}}}-1}\right)e^{{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}}}&\text{(inductive hypothesis for $G_{i-1}$)}\\
&\vdots&\\
&=&{\overline{M}}{\overline{h}}^{p+1}\left(\frac{e^{i\cdot{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}}}-1}{e^{{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}}}-1}\right)&\text{(as claimed).}\end{array}

Finally, for any
\delta>0
,
we find an upper bound on
{\overline{h}}
in order to
ensure that the Taylor curve
T^{p}_{{\bm{q}}_{0},{\overline{h}}}

stays inside the
\delta
-tube of
{\bm{x}}
.
There are 3 cases, depending on
{\overline{\mu}}
:

CASE
1
(
{\overline{\mu}}>0
)
:
The sequence
G_{i}
is increasing for
i=0,\ldots,m
, and
so it suffices to choose
{\overline{h}}
so that
G_{m}\leq\delta
:

\begin{array}[]{llllll}G_{m}&=&{\overline{M}}{\overline{h}}^{p+1}\left(\frac{e^{m{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}}}-1}{e^{{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}}}-1}\right)\\
&=&{\overline{M}}{\overline{h}}^{p+1}\left(\frac{e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}-1}{e^{{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}}}-1}\right)&\text{(since $h={\overline{h}}m$)}\\
&\leq&{\overline{M}}{\overline{h}}^{p+1}\left(\frac{e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}-1}{{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}}}\right)&\text{(since $e^{x}-1\geq x$ for $x\geq 0$)}\\
&=&{\overline{M}}{\overline{h}}^{p}\left(\frac{e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}-1}{{\overline{\mu}}}\right).\end{array}

To ensure
G_{m}\leq\delta
,
it suffices to require

\begin{array}[]{llllll}{\overline{M}}{\overline{h}}^{p}\left(\frac{e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}-1}{{\overline{\mu}}}\right)&\leq&\delta\\
{\overline{h}}^{p}&\leq&\frac{\delta}{{\overline{M}}}\frac{{\overline{\mu}}}{(e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}-1)}\\
{\overline{h}}&\leq&\big(\frac{\delta}{{\overline{M}}}\frac{{\overline{\mu}}}{(e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}-1)}\big)^{1/p}.\end{array}

CASE
0
(
{\overline{\mu}}=0
):

This case can be derived from CASE 1 by
taking the limit as
{\overline{\mu}}\to 0
, and applying
L’Hôpital’s rule.

CASE
-1
(
{\overline{\mu}}<0
)
:
In this case, the above derivation
must use this bound
e^{x}-1<x+\tfrac{1}{2}x^{2}
when
x<0
.
We need some preliminary facts:

(a)

Suppose
\delta>0
and
B<B^{+}<0
.
Then
\delta>\frac{A}{B^{+}}
implies
\delta>\frac{A}{B}
.
If
A\geq 0
, the conclusion clearly holds.
If
A<0
then

\delta>\frac{A}{B^{+}}=\big|\frac{A}{B^{+}}\big|>\big|\frac{A}{B}\big|=\frac{A}{B}

since
|B^{+}|>|B|
.

(b)

Suppose
{\overline{\mu}}<0
and
{\overline{h}}<2/|{\overline{\mu}}|
,
then
{\overline{\mu}}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\overline{\mu}}^{2}{\overline{h}}<0
.

{\overline{\mu}}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\overline{\mu}}^{2}{\overline{h}}={\overline{\mu}}(1+\tfrac{1}{2}{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}})<{\overline{\mu}}

since
0>\tfrac{1}{2}{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}}>-1
.

(c)

If follows from (a) and (b)
that to prove that
\delta\geq G_{m}
, it is
sufficient to prove

\delta\geq{\overline{M}}{\overline{h}}^{p+1}\left(\frac{e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}-1}{{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}}+\tfrac{1}{2}({\overline{\mu}}{\overline{h}})^{2}}\right)={\overline{M}}{\overline{h}}^{p}\left(\frac{e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}-1}{{\overline{\mu}}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\overline{\mu}}^{2}{\overline{h}}}\right)

Rearranging the above inequality, we obtain

{\overline{h}}^{p}\leq\frac{\delta\bigl({\overline{\mu}}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\overline{\mu}}^{2}{\overline{h}}\bigr)}{{\overline{M}}\,(e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}-1)}=\frac{\delta{\overline{\mu}}}{{\overline{M}}\,(e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}-1)}+\frac{\delta{\overline{\mu}}^{2}}{2{\overline{M}}\,(e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}-1)}\,{\overline{h}}.

Define

A{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathrel{\,:=\,}}\frac{\delta{\overline{\mu}}}{{\overline{M}}\,(e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}-1)},\qquad B{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathrel{\,:=\,}}-\,\frac{\delta{\overline{\mu}}^{2}}{2{\overline{M}}\,(e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}-1)}.

Since
{\overline{\mu}}<0
and
e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}-1<0
, both
A
and
B
are positive.
Hence it is enough to prove

{\overline{h}}^{p}+B{\overline{h}}\leq A.

Now the function
{\overline{h}}^{p}+B{\overline{h}}
is increasing for
{\overline{h}}\geq 0
since
B
is positive.
Therefore, if we choose

{\overline{h}}\leq\min\!\left\{\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)^{1/p},\,\frac{A}{2B}\right\},

then

{\overline{h}}^{p}\leq\frac{A}{2},\qquad B{\overline{h}}\leq\frac{A}{2},

and thus

{\overline{h}}^{p}+B{\overline{h}}\leq A.

This proves
\delta\geq G_{m}
.

Finally, note that

\frac{A}{2B}=\frac{\delta{\overline{\mu}}/({\overline{M}}(e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}-1))}{-\,\delta{\overline{\mu}}^{2}/({\overline{M}}(e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}-1))}=-\frac{1}{{\overline{\mu}}},

so our choice also implies
{\overline{h}}\leq-1/{\overline{\mu}}
, hence

{\overline{\mu}}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\overline{\mu}}^{2}{\overline{h}}<0
, as required.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let

{\bm{x}}_{0}=\mbox{{{{IVP}}}}({\bm{q}}_{0},h),\qquad{\bm{x}}\in\mbox{{{{IVP}}}}(E_{0},h).

We want to show that

\|{\bm{x}}(t)-T^{p}_{{\bm{q}}_{0},{\overline{h}}}(t)\|_{2}\leq\|{\bm{x}}(t)-{\bm{x}}_{0}(t)\|_{2}+\|{\bm{x}}_{0}(t)-T^{p}_{{\bm{q}}_{0},{\overline{h}}}(t)\|_{2}\leq r_{0}e^{{\overline{\mu}}t}+\delta

(20)

for all
t\in[0,h]
.

Hence all hypotheses of Lemma
2
are satisfied, and therefore

\|T^{p}_{{\bm{q}}_{0},{\overline{h}}}(t)-{\bm{x}}_{0}(t)\|\leq\delta\qquad\text{for all }t\in[0,h].

(21)

Because
{\overline{\mu}}
is a lognorm-bound on
F_{1}
, Lemma
1
gives

\|{\bm{x}}(t)-{\bm{x}}_{0}(t)\|_{2}\leq e^{{\overline{\mu}}t}\,\|{\bm{p}}-{\bm{q}}_{0}\|_{2}\leq r_{0}e^{{\overline{\mu}}t}\qquad\text{for all }t\in[0,h].

(22)

Combining (
21
) and (
22
), we obtain (
20
).

(a)

Taking
t=h
in (
20
), we get

\|{\bm{x}}(h)-T^{p}_{{\bm{q}}_{0},{\overline{h}}}(h)\|_{2}\leq r_{0}e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}+\delta.

Hence

{\bm{x}}(h)\in\mathrm{Ball}_{T^{p}_{{\bm{q}}_{0},{\overline{h}}}(h)}\left(r_{0}e^{{\overline{\mu}}h}+\delta\right).

This ball is an end-enclosure for

\mbox{{{{IVP}}}}(E_{0},h)
.

(b)

Let

r{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathrel{\,:=\,}}\max_{h^{\prime}\in[0,h]}\left(r_{0}e^{{\overline{\mu}}h^{\prime}}+\delta\right).

Then by (
20
) for every
t\in[0,h]
,

\|{\bm{x}}(t)-T^{p}_{{\bm{q}}_{0},{\overline{h}}}(t)\|_{2}\leq r_{0}e^{{\overline{\mu}}t}+\delta\leq r.

Therefore,

{\bm{x}}(t)\in\mathrm{Ball}_{T^{p}_{{\bm{q}}_{0},{\overline{h}}}(t)}(r)\subseteq T^{p}_{{\bm{q}}_{0},{\overline{h}}}(t)\pm[-r,r]^{n}.

Taking the union over
t\in[0,h]
, we obtain

{\bm{x}}([0,h])\subseteq T^{p}_{{\bm{q}}_{0},{\overline{h}}}([0,h])\pm[-r,r]^{n}.

This proves that

T^{p}_{{\bm{q}}_{0},{\overline{h}}}([0,h])\pm[-r,r]^{n}

is a full-enclosure for
\mbox{{{{IVP}}}}(E_{0},h)
.
