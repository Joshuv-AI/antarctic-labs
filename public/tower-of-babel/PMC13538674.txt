Experimental and 3D finite element modeling of line heating effect on thin mild steel sheets
Authors: Galana Abay Kebede, Gamachis Ragasa Gutata, Gurmessa Horata Abbera, Bikila Debela Gemeda, Chala Abdissa Duressa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Forming sheet metals by a moving heat source (i.e., line heating) is an efficient and economical method of forming metal sheets into desired shapes, including curvatures, which nowadays needs to replace convenient mechanical forming processes. However, only a few studies have been carried out on deforming steel sheets into a required shape by using an oxy-acetylene gas torch as a heat source using a numerical finite element model. This is mainly due to the difficulty of numerically modeling the amount of heat flux from the gas torch to the sheet metal, temperature distribution on the surface of the sheet, deformation resulting from different amounts of heat, analyzing the effect of the heat path, and experimentally maintaining the surface temperature of the sheet metal on a given path. Due to these difficulties, there is a weakness in judgment of where and how much to heat the material to obtain the required shape of the sheets. In this study for different amounts of heat flux from a gas torch to the surface of thin mild steel sheets, the temperature distribution on the surface of thin mild steel sheets is numerically modeled by 3D FEM software called ANSYS, and experimentally the surface temperature is controlled by a professional infrared thermometer on thin mild steel sheets of size 200 × 200 × 4 mm for straight, rectangular, and parallel path line heating to determine where and how much to heat the sheet to deform into desired shapes. Finally, the numerical and experimental results are compared, and the results obtained were fairly acceptable with the error less than 5–7% and in good agreement.

Introduction

Line heating (LH) is a thermo-mechanical method of forming plate or sheet metal by means of local heat treatments. There are two mechanisms commonly used to form steel plates/sheets into curved shells: mechanical forming and thermo-mechanical forming. In mechanical forming, a load is applied through a hydraulic press or a set of rolls to produce the required designed shape. Whereas in thermo-mechanical forming, thermal load is applied to obtain the desired shape1. When a plate is heated on one side, the through-thickness temperature gradient generated can cause different expansions across the thickness of the metal plate and bending of the plate/sheet2. Plastic deformation occurs in the region directly under the heat source where the temperature is high and the yield stress of the metal is low. This method is specially used in shipyard industries, the automotive industry, and with sheet metal forming technologies and other plate/sheet forming requirements3. Despite its utility, the process is difficult to control and generally regarded as art performed by the most skilled shipwrights. Therefore, it is essential to develop a scientific deterministic methodology of modeling the LH process using a heat source like an oxy-acetylene flame, which is widely used in heavy industry for metal cutting operations.

Until today, a number of research have addressed the topic of line heating in search of better control of the process.

Still today further research is required for the automation and reduction of problems in the manual application of line heating by using an oxy-acetylene flame, as the process is difficult to control and generally regarded as an art performed by the most skilled workers. But less research is carried out on LH by oxy-acetylene flame, as a gas torch it is mostly available in almost all workshops of industries, and it’s economical. Principle of line heating

When a steel sheet is heated locally by a moving heat source the heated region will expand more and also become soft enough to be distorted. The heated region is prevented from expanding side wards due to the surroundings are cold and there is a temperature gradient across the thickness. It will only be able to expand upwards, and will do so readily because it is softened, thereby making a lump. When the steel subsequently cools and hardens, this lump will remain, so that the surrounding steel will be pulled in to fill the space the lump has left. As a result, the steel sheet will distort. If a line on a sheet has been heated and cooled, there will be a line of such lumps, and the sheet will bend14. The process consists of increasing locally and quickly the temperature of one side of the sheet with a heating source, such as a torch, to induce plastic strains in the plate. During cooling, the sheet starts to deform such that the material in the heated region bends locally, resulting in permanent deformation in the sheet metal. The edges will rise towards the lump. This is the basic principle of line heating. Figure 1 shows the basic principle of how the deformation occurred by line heating.

Similarly, thermally induced stresses can be used to remove strain, i.e., to eliminate distortion. Herein lies the idea of controlled heating and cooling for regulating strain so as to achieve a specified curvature or for removing undesired curvature.

To generate the required shape of the mild steel sheet, the surface temperature (for mild steel it should not exceed 700℃, or recrystallization would happen), the speed of torch movement, the gap between the heating torch and the sheet surface, the nozzle diameter, and the heating pattern are important additional parameters.

Numerical analysis

A numerical model is developed for thermo-mechanical analysis of the process of sheet metal forming by line heating and yields computerized calculations that are an important supplement to experiments. This numerical model has been built by use of the finite element code ANSYS release 16. The objective of the simulation in numerical analysis is to acquire the heating information necessary to form a given initial surface to a given target surface: heat regions, heating order, number of passes required, etc. In this numerical analysis: Rezoning technique is adopted to greatly reduce the simulation time.

All the thermal and mechanical properties are considered as a function of temperature.Linear Newtonian convection cooling is considered on all the surfaces.The maximum temperature attained does not exceed 700 °C, phase change is not considered.A small strain and small deflection formulation has been applied. This is justified by the fact that the deformations induced by line heating are very small.The temperature field is unaffected by the structural response so that the thermal and structural problems can be solved in sequence.

The heat input and the heating speed determine the thermal cycle, which has an important effect on the heat distribution pattern in the vicinity of the heat source. It is in this region that the heated zone is formed, characterized by temperature distribution over a time domain leading to the formation of a thermal strain field.

The temperature distribution in a plate during the line for evaluating the residual deformation pattern of a line heated plate, heat transfer analysis is first carried out to find the nodal temperatures over a time domain. Then in the second part elasto-plastic analysis is carried out using the results obtained from the heat transfer analysis as inputs. The moving of the heat source was divided into several load steps. In the mechanical analysis the results from the thermal analysis were input as load. For each load step of thermal analysis corresponding rate independent structural analysis was carried out. The structural analysis involved thermo-elastic-plastic material model with temperature dependent material properties incorporated into the modelling.

Numerical test for different path of line heating

One most important factor controlling the final shape of the sheet is t heating line position. By considering the sheets of identical material and the same thickness, h, torch speed, v, and amount of heat, Q. during forming process the quantity of h and v are easily measured, but how much heat is actually absorbed by the sheet is difficult to say. Meanwhile the maximum temperature is very important as there must be some proportionality between the temperature and the local deformation. The surface temperature can be measured and is the function of other parameters Q, v and h.

For these reasons, only maximum temperature is measured on the heated side and the plate thickness and torch velocity are considered in this simulation program. Along the torch non-contact type, surface measuring temperature device called professional infrared thermocouple (PIT) is used and shown in Fig. 8 below. Result and discussion

From numerical analyses of line heating effect on thin mild steel sheet the valuable information obtained to compare the results with that of experimental effects of line heating on sheets. Under this the effect changing the amount of heat on surface temperature of the sheet, the relation between temperature and deformation of the sheet, temperature profile versus time graph, the effect of changing the heating path and validation of numerical deformation by experiment are discussed for center, rectangular and parallel path line heating.

Conclusion

From the experimental and 3D finite element modeling of line heating effect on thin mild steel sheet the conclusion reached are as described below.

The maximum temperature at the top surface of the sheet increased with increasing input power. Furthermore, temperature gradients between the upper and lower surfaces of the sheet increased significantly as the input power increased; this could be a primary factor in increasing the deformation.The heating path determined by numerical model to give a certain shape of the sheet has a good agreement with experimental result, so one can get required information for heating path from numerical results before proceeding to actual line heating of the sheet.The transient temperature distributions of the sheet used in the line heating process is responsible for the strain gradient and residual deformation.Fine meshing of heated zone been employed to greatly reduce the simulation time yet preserve the required accuracy.The required amount of deformation of sheet may not achieved by a single pass line heating. Therefore, more number of pass is needed to get that deformation.Results obtained from numerical analysis matched well with experimental results.Line heating metal forming techniques are applicable in realistic applications to solve real problems.The findings demonstrates that line heating is an effective and practical metal forming technique with direct relevance to industrial applications such as shipbuilding plate forming, repair and correction of distorted steel structures, and low-cost forming operations in workshop environments where advanced forming equipment may not be available. Specifically, the method addresses key industrial challenges including controlled bending of large thin plates, correction of welding-induced distortions and achieving desired curvature without extensive mechanical loading or expensive tooling.
