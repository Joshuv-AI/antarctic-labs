Abstract

Under certain conditions, we characterize the Gorenstein flat-cotorsion modules over T R ​ ( M ) T_{R}(M) , showing that a T R ​ ( M ) T_{R}(M) -module ( X , u ) (X,u) is Gorenstein flat-cotorsion if and only if u u is monomorphic and Coker ​ u \mathrm{Coker}u is a Gorenstein flat-cotorsion R R -module. 1
Introduction

The study of Gorenstein homological algebra stems from finitely generated modules of G-dimension zero over any noetherian ring, introduced by Auslander and
Bridger as a generalization of finite generated projective modules
[
1
]
.
Later, Enochs and Jenda introduced Gorenstein projective modules (not necessarily finitely generated) over any associative ring
[
10
]
, and dually, the Gorenstein injective modules were defined. Another pursuit
of this analogy led to the concept of the Gorenstein flat modules introduced by Enochs et al. in
[
12
]
. Modeled on the classical projective, injective and flat modules, the three types of Gorenstein homological modules have become a cornerstone of Gorenstein homological algebra
[
11
,
16
,
17
]
.

Recently, Gorenstein flat modules that are also cotorsion have attracted increasing investigation. This focus is largely due to the fact that the category of Gorenstein flat modules is not Frobenius, whereas the subcategory of Gorenstein flat and cotorsion modules is.
Motivated by this, Christensen et al. introduced the notion of Gorenstein flat-cotorsion modules (GFC, for short), and they showed that over a right coherent ring, GFC are precisely
the modules that are Gorenstein flat and cotorsion
[
6
]
. We refer the readers to
[
3
,
5
,
18
,
19
,
20
]

for more discussions on Gorenstein flat-cotorsion modules.

Throughout the paper, all rings are nonzero associative rings with identity and all modules are unitary. For a ring
R
, we adopt the convention that an
R
-module is a left
R
-module; right
R
-modules are viewed as modules over the opposite ring
R^{op}
. Let
R
be such a ring and
M
be an
R
-bimodule. Following
[
7
]
, a tensor ring of an
R
-bimodule
M
is defined as
T_{R}(M)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}M^{i}
, where
M^{0}=R
and
M^{i+1}=M\otimes_{R}M^{i}
for
i\geqslant 0
. The addition of elements in
T_{R}(M)
is componentwise,
and the multiplication is induced by the isomorphism

(\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}M^{n})\otimes_{R}(\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}M^{m})\cong\bigoplus_{n,m=0}^{\infty}M^{n+m}
. Examples of tensor rings include but are not limited to trivial
extension rings, Morita contex trings, triangular matrix rings and so on. In this paper, we focus on the case that
M
is nilpotent, that is,
M^{N+1}=0
for some
N\geq 0
. Then a module over
T_{R}(M)
can be written as a pair
(X,u)
with
X
an
R
-module and
u\in\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}X,X)
.
The classical homological properties of the tensor ring
T_{R}(M)
were studied in
[
21
,
23
]
. Recently, various Gorenstein homological modules over
T_{R}(M)
have been characterized, including Gorenstein projective (injective, flat) modules
[
4
,
9
]
, Ding projective (injective) modules
[
29
]
and projectively coresolved Gorenstein flat modules
[
25
]
. On the other hand, Wang-He-Jin investigated Gorenstein flat-cotorsion modules over trivial extension rings
[
26
]
. This development naturally motivates the central question of this work.

Question.
How to describe Gorenstein flat-cotorsion module over
T_{R}(M)
for an
N
-nilpotent
R
-bimodule
M
?

The main purpose of the paper is to answer this question and to provide further applications. We need the following Tor-vanishing
condition on
M
introduced by Chen-Lu
[
4
]
:

{\rm(P)}\quad\quad\quad\ \ \ \ \mathrm{Tor}_{\geq 1}^{R}(M,M^{i})=0\ {\rm for\ all}\ i\geq 1.

Under this condition, our results can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

( Theorem
3.9
)

Let
M
be an
N
-nilpotent
R
-bimodule which satisfies the condition
(P)
. If
M\otimes_{R}-
perserves cotorsion modules,

\mathrm{fd}_{R}M<\infty
and
\mathrm{fd}_{R^{op}}M<\infty
, then a
T_{R}(M)
-module
(X,u)
is Gorenstein flat-cotorsion if and only if
u
is monomorphic and
\mathrm{Coker}u
is a Gorenstein flat-cotorsion
R
-module.

Example
3.10
shows that there exists a bimodule
M
over some algebra satisfying
all the conditions in the above theorem. The idea of our proof of Theorem
1.1
is similar to that
in
[
9
]
, but additional ingredients are needed.
Indeed, the proof of
[
9
, Proposition 2.11]
relies
on the fact that every module is an image of a projective module and that the subcategory of
Gorenstein projective modules is closed under kernels of epimorphisms. However,
these properties do not hold for Gorenstein flat-cotorsion modules, and thus new arguments must be introduced to establish Theorem
1.1
.
As an application, we characterize the Gorenstein flat-cotorsion modules over some
1
-nilpotent trivial
extension rings and Morita context rings.

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section 2 collects basic material on tensor rings and introduces notation used throughout the paper. Section 3 is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Finally, Section 4 presents the applications.

\mathrm{Mod}(R)
the category of left
R
-modules,
and by
\mathrm{Proj}(R)
(resp.
\mathrm{Flat}(R)
) the subcategory of
\mathrm{Mod}(R)
consisting
of all projective (resp. flat)
R
-modules. For an
R
-module

, we denote by

\mathrm{pd}(X)

.

2.1 Tensor rings.
Let
R
be a ring and
M
an
R
-bimodule, a tensor ring of an R-bimodule
M
is defined by
T_{R}(M)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}M^{i}
, where
M^{0}=R
and
M^{i+1}=M\otimes_{R}M^{i}

for
i\geqslant 0
. In this paper, we assume that
M
is
N
-nilpotent
,
that is,
M^{N+1}=0
for some
N\geqslant 0
, and
we denote by
T_{R}(M)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}M^{i}
, the
tensor ring
with respect to
M
.

From
[
4
]
, a module over
T_{R}(M)
can be written as a pair
(X,u)
, where
X\in\mathrm{Mod}(R)
and
u:M\otimes_{R}X\rightarrow X
is an
R
-homomorphism. A morphism from
(X,u)
to
(X^{\prime},u^{\prime})
is an
R
-homomorphism
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(X,X^{\prime})
such that
f\circ u=u^{\prime}\circ(M\otimes f)
.
A sequence

(X,u)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X^{\prime},u^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X^{\prime\prime},u^{\prime\prime})

in
\mathrm{Mod}(T_{R}(M))
is exact if and only if the sequence
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}X^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\longrightarrow}}X^{\prime\prime}
is exact in
\mathrm{Mod}(R)
.

2.2 Adjoint functors.
From
[
4
]
, there are two adjoint pairs as follows:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Mod}(R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Mod}(T_{R}(M))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Mod}(R).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm Ind}}

Here,
U
is the forgetful functor
which maps a
T_{R}(M)
-module
(X,u)
to the underlying
R
-module
X
,
and for an
R
-module
X
,

{\rm Ind}(X)
is defined as
(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}(M^{i}\otimes_{R}X),c_{X})
, where
c_{X}
is the inclusion from
M\otimes_{R}(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}M^{i}\otimes_{R}X)\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{N}(M^{i}\otimes_{R}X)

to
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}(M^{i}\otimes_{R}X)
. More explicitly,

c_{X}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&1&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1\end{pmatrix}_{(N+1)\times N}.

For an
R
-homomorphism
f:X\to Y
, the
T_{R}(M)
-homomorphism

{\rm Ind}(f):{\rm Ind}(X)\to{\rm Ind}(Y)
can be viewed as a formal diagonal matrix with
diagonal elements
M^{i}\otimes f
for
0\leqslant i\leqslant N
.
Moreover, the functor
S

sends an
R
-module
X
to the
T_{R}(M)
-module
(X,0)
,
and for a
T_{R}(M)
-module
(X,u)
,
C((X,u))
is defined as
\mathrm{Coker}u
.
For a morphism
f:(X,u)\to(Z,w)
in
\mathrm{Mod}(T_{R}(M))

C(f):\mathrm{Coker}u\to\mathrm{Coker}w
is determined by the universal property of cokernels.

It is straightforward to verify that
C\circ{\rm Ind}=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathrm{Mod}(R)}
, and by the Eilenberg-Watts theorem, the functor

{\rm Ind}
is naturally isomorphic to

T_{R}(M)\otimes_{R}-
, so one has
{\rm Ind}(X)\cong T_{R}(M)\otimes_{R}X

for each
X\in\mathrm{Mod}(R)
. By
[
9
, Remark 1.5]
,
a
T_{R}(M)
-module
(X,u)
gives rise to the following short exact sequence:

0\to{\rm Ind}(M\otimes_{R}X)\xrightarrow{\phi_{(X,u)}}{\rm Ind}(X)\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_{(X,u)}}(X,u)\to 0

of

-modules, where

\phi_{(X,u)}=\begin{pmatrix}-u&0&0&\cdots&0\\
1&-M\otimes u&0&\cdots&0\\
0&1&-M^{2}\otimes u&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1\end{pmatrix}_{(N+1)\times N},

and

\varepsilon_{(X,u)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&u&u\circ(M\otimes u)&\cdots&u\circ(M\otimes u)\circ\cdots\circ\left(M^{N-1}\otimes u\right)\end{pmatrix}.

2.3 Gorenstein flat-cotorsion modules.

From
[
27
]
, an
R
-module
C
is said to be
cotorsion
if
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{R}(F,C)=0
for any
F\in\mathrm{Flat}(R)
. For simplicity, we denote by

{\rm FC(R)}=\{M\in\mathrm{Mod}(R)\mid M\text{ is flat and cotorsion}\}.

Recall from
[
6
]
that an exact complex
T^{\bullet}

with
T^{i}\in\mathrm{FC}(R)

is a
totally acyclic complex of flat-cotorsion
modules
if it remains exact after applying the functor

\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(-,W)
for every
W\in\mathrm{FC}(R)
. An
R
-module

X
is called

Gorenstein flat-cotorsion
(
\mathrm{GFC}
, for short) if there is a totally acyclic complex

T^{\bullet}
of flat-cotorsion modules with

X\cong\mathrm{Ker}(T^{0}\to T^{1})
. By
[
5
,
6
]
, a Gorenstein flat and cotorsion module is
GFC, and the converse also holds if
R
is right coherent. However, this is not true for arbitrary rings.

We now record several auxiliary results that will be used frequently.

Lemma 2.1
.

For a tensor ring
T_{R}(M)
, the following equalities hold.

(1)(
[
9
, Lemma 1.9]
)

\mathrm{Proj}(T_{R}(M))={\rm Ind}(\mathrm{Proj}(R))
.

(2)(
[
29
, Lemma 2.8]
)

\mathrm{Flat}(T_{R}(M))={\rm Ind}(\mathrm{Flat}(R))
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
8
, Lemma 3.7]
)

Let
R
and
S
be rings,
(F,G)
an adjoint pair with
F:\mathrm{Mod}(R)\to\mathrm{Mod}(S)
.
For an
X\in\mathrm{Mod}(R)
, if there is a short exact sequence
0\to K\to P\to X\to 0
with
P\in\mathrm{Proj}(R)
such that
0\to FK\to FP\to FX\to 0
is exact with
FP\in\mathrm{Proj}(S)
, then

\operatorname{Ext}_{S}^{1}(FX,Y)\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(X,GY)

for any
Y\in\mathrm{Mod}(S)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
F\in\mathrm{Flat}(R)
and
(X,u)\in\mathrm{Mod}(T_{R}(M))
, we have

\mathrm{Ext}_{T_{R}(M)}^{1}({\rm Ind}(F),(X,u))\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(F,X).

Proof.

Since
({\rm Ind},U)
is an adjoint pair with
U

exact, we get that the functor
{\rm Ind}
preserves projective modules.
For any
F\in\mathrm{Flat}(R)
, consider the following short exact sequence

0\to K\to P\to F\to 0

with
P\in\mathrm{Proj}(R)
. Applying the right exact
functor
{\rm Ind}=T_{R}(M)\otimes_{R}-
, and noting that
\mathrm{Tor}_{1}^{R}(T_{R}(M),F)=0
, we have a short exact sequence

0\to{\rm Ind}(K)\to{\rm Ind}(P)\to{\rm Ind}(F)\to 0.

Then the assertion follows from Lemma
2.2
.
∎

As a consequence, we get the following characterizations of flat and cotorsion
modules over
T_{R}(M)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

(X,u)
is a cotorsion
T_{R}(M)
-module if and only if
X
is a cotorsion
R
-module.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.1
and
2.3
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

If
(X,u)\in\mathrm{FC}(T_{R}(M))
, then
(X,u)\cong\operatorname{Ind}(F)
for some
F\in\mathrm{FC}(R)
.

Proof.

Since
(X,u)
is flat, it follows from Lemma
2.1

that there exists
F\in\operatorname{Flat}(R)
such that

(X,u)\cong\operatorname{Ind}(F)
. It remains to show that

F
is cotorsion.
Since
(X,u)\cong(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}(M^{i}\otimes_{R}F),c_{F})

and
(X,u)
is cotorsion, it follows from Lemma
2.4
that
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}(M^{i}\otimes_{R}F)
is also cotorsion, that is,
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(F^{\prime},\,\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}(M^{i}\otimes_{R}F))=0
for any
F^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Flat}(R)
. Therefore, we have

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(F^{\prime},F)=0
for any
F^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Flat}(R)
, and thus

F
is a cotorsion
R
-module.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

If
M\otimes_{R}-
preserves cotorsion modules, then
\mathrm{FC}(T_{R}(M))={\rm Ind}(\mathrm{FC}(R))
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
, we have the inclusion
\mathrm{FC}(T_{R}(M))\subseteq{\rm Ind}(\mathrm{FC}(R))
. It remains to establish the converse inclusion, that is,
{\rm Ind}(\mathrm{FC}(R))\subseteq\mathrm{FC}(T_{R}(M))
. To this end, let
X\in\mathrm{FC}(R)
. Then, by Lemma
2.1
, the induced module

{\rm Ind}(X)
is flat. Now we claim that
{\rm Ind}(X)
is also cotorsion.
Since
{\rm Ind}(X)=(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}(M^{i}\otimes_{R}X),\ c_{X})
, it suffices, in view of Lemma
2.4
, to prove that
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}(M^{i}\otimes_{R}X)
is cotorsion, and this is due to the hypothesis that

M\otimes_{R}-
preserves cotorsion modules.
∎

Next, we will give a sufficient condition under which the functor

M\otimes_{R}-
preserves cotorsion modules.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
M
is a finitely-generated projective right
R
-module, then
M\otimes_{R}-
preserves cotorsion modules.

Proof.

Since
M
is a finitely-generated projective right
R
-module, we have an isomorphism of functors

M\otimes_{R}-\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathrm{Hom}_{R^{op}}(M,R),-)
, and thus
M\otimes_{R}-
has a left adjoint

\mathrm{Hom}_{R^{op}}(M,R)\otimes_{R}-
. Let
F=\mathrm{Hom}_{R^{op}}(M,R)\otimes_{R}-
and
G=M\otimes_{R}-
.
Since
G
is exact, we infer that
F
preserves projective modules. Moreover,
F

preserves the exactness of the exact sequence
0\rightarrow K\rightarrow P\rightarrow W\rightarrow 0

with
W\in\mathrm{Flat}(R)
since
\mathrm{Tor}_{1}^{R}(\mathrm{Hom}_{R^{op}}(M,R),W)=0
. Then it follows from
Lemma
2.2
that

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(FW,X)\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(W,GX),

for any
W\in\mathrm{Flat}(R)
and
X\in\mathrm{Mod}(R)
. Since
F
preserves projective modules and filtered colimits,
we have that
F
preserves flat modules. Now the above isomorphism implies that
G
preserves cotorsion modules.
∎

3
Gorenstein flat-cotorsion modules over tensor rings

In this section, we will give the proof Theorem
1.1
.
In order to describe the Gorenstein flat-cotorsion modules over tensor rings, we need the following compatible and admissible conditions, which were first
defined in
[
4
,
28
]
for
finite generated Gorenstein projective modules, and were further considered for infinite generated
Gorenstein projective (injective) modules
[
9
]
and
\mathcal{X}
-Gorenstein projective modules
[
29
]
.
Now we need the version of Gorenstein flat-cotorsion modules.

Definition 3.1
.

An
R
-bimodule
M
is said to be
compatible
if the following two conditions hold for a totally acyclic complex
T^{\bullet}
of flat-cotorsion
T_{R}(M)
-modules:

(C1) The complex
M\otimes_{R}T^{\bullet}
is acyclic;

(C2) The complex
\mathrm{Hom}_{T_{R}(M)}(T^{\bullet},{\rm Ind}(M\otimes_{R}F))
is acyclic for each
F\in\mathrm{FC}(R)
.

Definition 3.2
.

An
R
-bimodule
M
is said to be
admissible
if

\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(W,M^{i}\otimes_{R}F)=0=\mathrm{Tor}_{1}^{R}(M,M^{i}\otimes_{R}W)

for each
W\in\mathrm{GFC}(R)
,
F\in\mathrm{FC}(R)
and
i\geq 0
.

From
[
4
]
, the condition (P) for an
R
-bimodule
M
is defined as follow:

{\rm(P)}\quad\quad\quad\ \ \ \ \mathrm{Tor}_{\geq 1}^{R}(M,M^{i})=0\ {\rm for\ all}\ i\geq 1.

Inspired by
[
9
, Proposition 2.7]
, we will give a sufficient condition for a module
M
to be compatible and admissible. We need the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.3
.

(
[
24
, Theorem 9.48]
)

Let
R
and
S
be rings, and
U
an
R
-
S
-bimodule. If
\mathrm{fd}(_{R}U)<\infty
, then
\mathrm{fd}(_{R}(U\otimes_{S}F))<\infty
for any
F\in\mathrm{Flat}(S)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
M
be an
R
-bimodule satisfying the condition
(P)
. If
\mathrm{fd}_{R}M<\infty
,
then
\mathrm{fd}_{T_{R}(M)}({\rm Ind}(M\otimes_{R}F))<\infty
for any
F\in\mathrm{Flat}(R)
.

Proof.

Since
M
satisfies
the condition (P), it follows from
[
4
, Lemma 4.2]
that
\mathrm{Tor}_{\geqslant 1}^{R}(M^{s},M\otimes_{R}F)=0
for all
s\geq 1

and
F\in\mathrm{Flat}(R)
, and thus

\mathrm{Tor}_{\geqslant 1}^{R}(T_{R}(M),M\otimes_{R}F)=0
.
On the other hand, it follows from Lemma
3.3
that
\mathrm{fd}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}F\bigr)\leq n
for some
n
. Considering the flat resolution

0\to F^{-n}\to\cdots\to F^{-1}\to F^{0}\to M\otimes_{R}F\to 0

and applying
{\rm Ind}=T_{R}(M)\otimes_{R}-
, we obtain a complex of
T_{R}(M)
-modules

0\to{\rm Ind}(F^{-n})\to\cdots\to{\rm Ind}(F^{-1})\to{\rm Ind}(F^{0})\to{\rm Ind}(M\otimes_{R}F)\to 0,

which is also exact since
\mathrm{Tor}_{\geqslant 1}^{R}(T_{R}(M),M\otimes_{R}F)=0
.
Now the fact
{\rm Ind}(F^{-i})\in\mathrm{Flat}(T_{R}(M))
implies that
\mathrm{fd}_{T_{R}(M)}({\rm Ind}(M\otimes_{R}F))<\infty
.
∎

Now we are ready to give a sufficient condition for a module
M
to be compatible and admissible.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that
M
satisfies the condition
{\rm(P)}
and
M\otimes_{R}-

preserves cotorsion modules. If
\mathrm{fd}_{R}M<\infty

and
\operatorname{fd}_{R^{\mathrm{op}}}M<\infty
, then
M
is compatible and admissible.

Proof.

Let
T^{\bullet}
be a totally acyclic complex of flat-cotorsion
T_{R}(M)
-modules.
Since
T^{i}\in\mathrm{FC}(T_{R}(M))
, it follows from Lemma
2.5
that

T^{\bullet}
can be written as:

T^{\bullet}:\cdots\to{\rm Ind}(F^{-1})\to{\rm Ind}(F^{0})\to{\rm Ind}(F^{1})\to\cdots,

where
F^{i}\in\mathrm{FC}(R)
for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Applying the functor
U
, we get the following exact sequence

Q^{\bullet}:\cdots\to\textstyle\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}(M^{i}\otimes_{R}F^{-1})\to\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}(M^{i}\otimes_{R}F^{0})\to\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}(M^{i}\otimes_{R}F^{1})\to\cdots.

To prove the condition (C1), we have to prove that
M\otimes_{R}Q^{\bullet}
is exact. Since
\operatorname{fd}_{R^{\mathrm{op}}}M<\infty
, there is an exact sequence of
right
R
-modules

0\to\widetilde{F^{n}}\to\widetilde{F^{n-1}}\to\cdots\to\widetilde{F^{0}}\to M\to 0

with
\widetilde{F^{i}}\in\mathrm{Flat}(R^{\mathrm{op}})
.
Since
M
satisfies
the condition (P), it follows from
[
4
, Lemma 4.2]
that
\mathrm{Tor}^{R}_{\geqslant 1}(M,M^{i}\otimes_{R}F)=0
for all
F\in\mathrm{Flat}(R)
and
i\geq 0
, and thus

\mathrm{Tor}^{R}_{\geqslant 1}(M,\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}(M^{i}\otimes_{R}F))=0
. Then there exists an exact sequence of complexes

0\to\widetilde{F^{n}}\otimes_{R}Q^{\bullet}\to\widetilde{F^{n-1}}\otimes_{R}Q^{\bullet}\to\cdots\to\widetilde{F^{0}}\otimes_{R}Q^{\bullet}\to M\otimes_{R}Q^{\bullet}\to 0.

Since
\widetilde{F^{k}}\otimes_{R}-
is exact, one gets that the complex
\widetilde{F^{k}}\otimes_{R}Q^{\bullet}
is acyclic for
0\leq k\leq n
, and thus
M\otimes_{R}Q^{\bullet}
is exact, as desired.

We then prove the condition (C2). For any
F\in\mathrm{FC}(R)
,
it follows from the fact
\mathrm{fd}_{R}M<\infty
and Lemma
3.4
that
\mathrm{fd}_{T_{R}(M)}({\rm Ind}(M\otimes_{R}F))<\infty
. Now we claim that
{\rm Ind}(M\otimes_{R}F)
is cotorsion, and then
the complex

is exact by
[

, Lemma 2.1]
.
Since
{\rm Ind}(M\otimes_{R}F)=(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{N}(M^{i}\otimes_{R}F),\ c_{M\otimes_{R}F})
, it suffices, in view of Lemma
2.4
, to prove that

is cotorsion, and this is due to the hypothesis that

M\otimes_{R}-
preserves cotorsion modules.

Next, we prove that
M
is admissible. Since
M
satisfies
the condition (P), it follows from
[
4
, Lemma 4.2]
that
\mathrm{Tor}^{R}_{\geqslant 1}(M,M^{i}\otimes_{R}P)=0
for all

and
i\geq 1
, and then
[
9
, Lemma 2.6]

yields that

\mathrm{fd}_{R}(M^{i})<\infty
for any
i\geq 0
. Applying Lemma
3.3
, we have
\mathrm{fd}_{R}(M^{i}\otimes_{R}F)<\infty
for all
F\in\mathrm{FC}(R)
.
Since
M\otimes_{R}-

preserves cotorsion modules, we get that
M^{i}\otimes_{R}F
is also cotorsion.
Then it follows from
[
26
, Lemma 2.1]
that

\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(W,M^{i}\otimes_{R}F)=0
for each
W\in\mathrm{GFC}(R)
,
F\in\mathrm{FC}(R)
and
i\geq 0
.

On the other hand, since
\mathrm{fd}_{R^{\mathrm{op}}}(M^{s})<\infty
by
[
9
, Lemma 2.6]
, a standard dimension shifting argument yields
that
\mathrm{Tor}^{R}_{\geq 1}(M^{s},W)=0
for any
W\in\mathrm{GFC}(R)
.
Thus, by
[
4
, Lemma 4.2]
, one gets that
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\geq 1}(M,M^{s}\otimes_{R}W)=0
for any
W\in\mathrm{GFC}(R)
and
s\geqslant 0
. This yields that
M
is admissible.
∎

Suppose that
J
is the ideal of
kQ
generated by all the arrows. Then
R=kQ/J^{h}
is a self-injective algebra for
2\leqslant h\leqslant n
. Denote by
e_{i}
the idempotent element corresponding to the vertex
i
. Then one has
e_{j}Re_{i}=0
whenever
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
and
j-i\geqslant h
. Let
M=Re_{i}\otimes_{k}e_{j}R
. Then
M
is an
R
-bimodule,
which is finitely-generated projective as a right
R
-module. Therefore,

M
satisfies the condition
{\rm(P)}
and
M\otimes_{R}-

preserves cotorsion modules; see Lemma
2.7
.
Moreover,
M
is nilpotent since
M\otimes_{R}M\cong Re_{i}\otimes_{k}(e_{j}R\otimes_{R}Re_{i})\otimes_{k}e_{j}R=0
.

4
Applications

In this section, we present some applications to trivial ring extensions and Morita context rings.

4.1 The trivial extension of rings.

Let
R
be a ring and
M
an
R
-bimodule. The
trivial extension
ring of
R
by
M
,
defined as
R\ltimes M
, is the Cartesian product
R\times M

with the natural addition and multiplication given by

(r_{1},m_{1})(r_{2},m_{2})=(r_{1}r_{2},\;r_{1}m_{2}+m_{1}r_{2}),

for all
r_{1},r_{2}\in R
and
m_{1},m_{2}\in M
. We refer to
[
13
,
22
]
for more details
on trivial extension rings.

Suppose that the
R
-bimodule
M
is
1
-nilpotent, that is,
M\otimes_{R}M=0.
Then the tensor ring
T_{R}(M)
is nothing but the
trivial extension ring
R\ltimes M.
One can immediately get the following results by Theorem
3.9
.

Corollary 4.1
.

Suppose that
M
is a
1
-nilpotent
R
-bimodule. If

\mathrm{Tor}_{\geq 1}^{R}(M,M)

=0
,

\mathrm{fd}_{R}M<\infty
,
\mathrm{fd}_{R^{op}}M<\infty
and
M\otimes_{R}-

preserves cotorsion modules, then
(X,u)\in\mathrm{GFC}(R\ltimes M)
if and only if
u
is monomorphic and
\mathrm{Coker}u\in\mathrm{GFC}(R)
.

Remark 4.2
.

In
[
26
]
, Wang et al. established some sufficient and necessary
conditions for Gorenstein flat-cotorsion modules over

R\ltimes M
. When
M
is
1
-nilpotent,
the sequence
M\otimes_{R}M\otimes_{R}X\xrightarrow{M\otimes u}M\otimes_{R}X\xrightarrow{u}X

is exact if and only if
u
is a monomorphism. In this case,
the conditions in Corollary
4.1
improve upon those in
[
26
, Theorem 2.6]
.
Indeed, we weaken the assumption that
M_{R}
is flat by only requiring that
\mathrm{fd}(M_{R})<\infty
and
\mathrm{Tor}_{\geq 1}^{R}(M,M)=0
.

4.2 Morita context rings.

Let
A
,
B
be rings,
N
an
A
-
B
-bimodule and
M
a
B
-
A
-bimodule. Consider the Morita context ring

\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}A&N\\
M&B\end{pmatrix}

with zero bimodule homomorphisms, i.e. multiplication is given by

\begin{pmatrix}a&n\\
m&b\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&n^{\prime}\\
m^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}aa^{\prime}&an^{\prime}+nb^{\prime}\\
ma^{\prime}+bm^{\prime}&bb^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}.

Recall that
\text{Mod}(\Lambda)
is equivalent to a category with objects tuples
(X,Y,f,g)
where
X\in\text{Mod}(A)
,
Y\in\text{Mod}(B)
,
f\in\text{Hom}_{B}(M\otimes_{A}X,Y)
and
g\in\text{Hom}_{A}(N\otimes_{B}Y,X)
. For a detailed exposition of this we refer to
[
14
,
15
]
.

It follows from
[
15
, Proposition 2.5]
that the ring
\Lambda
is isomorphic to the trivial extension
ring
(A\times B)\ltimes(M\oplus N)
. We mention that
(M\oplus N)\otimes_{A\times B}(M\oplus N)\cong(M\otimes_{A}N)\oplus(N\otimes_{B}M)
.
Then the
A\times B
-bimodule
M\oplus N
is
1
-nilpotent if and only if
M\otimes_{A}N=0=N\otimes_{B}M
. Thus, we obtain the next result by Corollary

4.1
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\Lambda
be a Morita context ring with
M\otimes_{A}N=0=N\otimes_{B}M
, and let
(X,Y,f,g)
be a
\Lambda
-module.
Assume that both
M\otimes_{A}-
and
N\otimes_{B}-

preserve cotorsion modules.
If
\mathrm{Tor}_{\geqslant 1}^{B}(N,M)=0=\mathrm{Tor}_{\geqslant 1}^{A}(M,N)
,

\mathrm{fd}_{B}M<\infty
,
\mathrm{fd}_{A^{\mathrm{op}}}M<\infty
,
\mathrm{fd}_{A}N<\infty
and
\mathrm{fd}_{B^{\mathrm{op}}}N<\infty
,
then
(X,Y,f,g)\in\mathsf{GFC}(\Lambda)
if and only if both
f
and
g
are monomorphisms, and
\mathrm{Coker}(f)\in\mathsf{GFC}(B)
and
\mathrm{Coker}(g)\in\mathsf{GFC}(A)
.

In the setting where
M\otimes_{A}N=0=N\otimes_{B}M
, Corollary
4.3
refines
[
26
, Proposition 3.1]
,
as highlighted in Remark
4.2
. Specifically, it weakens the flatness assumptions on

M_{A}
and
N_{B}
by only requiring that
\mathrm{fd}(M_{A})<\infty
,

\mathrm{fd}(N_{B})<\infty
,
\mathrm{Tor}_{\geq 1}^{A}(M,N)=0
and
\mathrm{Tor}_{\geq 1}^{B}(N,M)=0
.

Example 4.4
.

Suppose that the ring
R
and the
R
-bimodule
M
as in Example
3.10
. Then the Morita context ring

\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}R&M\\
M&R\end{pmatrix}

satisfies all conditions in Corollary
4.3
.
