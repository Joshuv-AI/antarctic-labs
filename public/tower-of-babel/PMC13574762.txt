Hybrid-domain attention learning for lightweight underwater image enhancement and real-time vision deployment
Authors: Shan Jiang, Chenfeng Sun, Xuan Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Underwater image enhancement is critical for marine exploration and robotic vision. However, real-world underwater images suffer from color distortion, low contrast, and detail loss, while existing deep learning methods are often computationally heavy. This work presents a lightweight hybrid-domain enhancement network that jointly models frequency and spatial information for effective underwater image restoration. The architecture integrates frequency-spatial attention, channel attention pointwise, and multi-shape convolution to balance performance and efficiency. Experiments on public datasets show that the proposed method achieves higher PSNR and SSIM than mainstream methods with lower computational cost, supporting real-time deployment on resource-constrained devices. This work provides an effective solution for high-quality and efficient underwater vision applications. The source code and implementation details are publicly available at https://github.com/cfengsun16/HDAMS-Net to ensure reproducibility.

Introduction

Underwater image enhancement (UIE) plays an essential role in ocean exploration, underwater navigation, and robotic perception. Images captured underwater suffer from complex optical degradation-including color distortion, low contrast, and detail blurring-that significantly hinders high-level vision tasks and reduces the reliability of underwater visual systems. Addressing these degradations efficiently and robustly remains an open challenge.

Traditional physics-based methods, such as DCP1 and Retinex-based models2, attempt to compensate for color distortion and visibility degradation through handcrafted priors. Although these approaches have achieved partial success, their performance is limited by strong assumptions about water conditions and lighting environments. Recent advances in deep learning have introduced CNN-based3 and GAN-based4 architectures, demonstrating strong capability in modeling complex visual degradation. Similar deep learning paradigms have also shown effectiveness in other image-based diagnostic tasks5,25, further highlighting the generalization ability of data-driven feature learning. while recent lightweight hybrid CNN models with attention mechanisms further improve restoration performance and efficiency6, learning end-to-end mappings from degraded to clear images. Models such as Ucolor7, Water-Net8, and PUGAN9 have demonstrated improved visual quality but often at the expense of high computational cost and network complexity. However, Transformer-based10 frameworks enhance global feature modeling capability but are difficult to deploy on resource-constrained underwater devices, while recent structure-aware Transformer enhancement models attempt to balance performance and efficiency11,12.

To overcome these challenges, this paper proposes Hybrid-Domain Attention with Multi-shape Structure (HDAMS-Net), a lightweight hybrid-domain architecture for underwater image enhancement. Unlike existing hybrid-domain methods such as SFGNet, which process frequency and spatial features in separate sequential stages without explicit cross-domain interaction, our FSA module performs pixel-level frequency modulation and spatial channel recalibration jointly within a single unified block, enabling tighter coupling between global spectral correction and local texture recovery. Unlike standard channel attention mechanisms such as SE blocks or CBAM, which operate purely on spatial feature statistics, the CAP module decomposes spatial convolution along horizontal and vertical axes before applying channel reweighting, allowing it to capture directional long-range dependencies with significantly lower computational cost. Unlike prior multi-scale bottleneck designs that rely on isotropic square kernels of varying sizes, the MSC module introduces anisotropic stripe kernels (1×31 and 31×1) alongside a large square kernel (31×31), specifically targeting the directional scattering and streak illumination patterns characteristic of underwater degradation. The network integrates these three complementary modules-Frequency-Spatial Attention (FSA), Channel Attention Pointwise (CAP), and Multi-Shape Convolution (MSC)-to jointly capture multi-scale spatial structures, frequency-domain cues, and long-range directional dependencies within a compact encoder-decoder framework. The proposed architecture is designed to balance enhancement quality and computational efficiency, enabling real-time deployment in embedded or mobile underwater imaging systems. Comprehensive evaluations across various benchmarks verify that HDAMS-Net achieves superior performance in terms of PSNR and SSIM13 across the evaluated benchmarks, while concurrently maintaining a compact model size, indicating its potential for practical underwater vision applications such as autonomous navigation and marine resource inspection. Related work

UIE methods

Underwater image enhancement (UIE) aims to restore visual quality from images degraded by wavelength-dependent light attenuation and backscattering, which produce characteristic artifacts including color distortion, low contrast, and hazy appearance. Research in this area has evolved through three broad paradigms, each addressing the limitations of its predecessor.

Physics-based methods were the earliest systematic approach to UIE. Methods such as UNTV14, MLLE15, and ADPCC16 exploit handcrafted priors-such as the Dark Channel Prior or Retinex theory-to estimate transmission maps or illumination components. While these methods improve visibility under controlled conditions, they rely on strong assumptions about scene depth and water optical properties that rarely hold in practice. Their performance degrades significantly under dynamic illumination, mixed water types, or complex scattering environments, and they offer no mechanism to adapt to scene-specific degradation patterns, motivating the shift toward data-driven approaches.

Deep learning-based methods overcame many of these limitations by learning nonlinear enhancement mappings directly from paired data. CNN-based frameworks such as Ucolor, UIE-WD17, and PUGAN demonstrated substantially improved robustness and perceptual quality compared to physics-based predecessors. Subsequent Transformer-based models, including U-Transformer18 and URSCT19, leveraged self-attention to capture long-range dependencies and global contextual relationships, further improving color consistency and structural fidelity. However, these gains come at a significant computational cost: Transformer-based models typically require tens of gigaflops and tens of millions of parameters, making them poorly suited for deployment on resource-constrained underwater platforms such as embedded imaging systems or autonomous underwater vehicles. Moreover, most CNN and Transformer methods process features entirely in the spatial domain, lacking an explicit mechanism to address the frequency-domain nature of global color attenuation-a fundamental characteristic of underwater degradation.

Hybrid and lightweight methods have more recently attempted to bridge the gap between restoration quality and computational efficiency. Physics-guided networks20 and illumination-guided enhancement models combine data-driven learning with physical priors to improve interpretability and generalization, but their increased structural complexity often reintroduces the deployment challenges of earlier deep models. Lightweight architectures reduce parameter count through depthwise separable convolutions and MLP-based designs, but they typically do not address the frequency-spatial coupling inherent in underwater degradation.

These limitations collectively motivate the design of HDAMS-Net: a lightweight hybrid-domain architecture that explicitly models both frequency-domain color attenuation and spatial-domain texture degradation within a compact, deployable framework. By jointly leveraging frequency-spatial attention, axial channel perception, and multi-shape convolution, HDAMS-Net addresses the core technical bottlenecks of prior work while remaining suitable for real-time embedded deployment.

Lightweight U-net architectures for image enhancement

U-Net, originally developed for biomedical image segmentation21, introduced a symmetric encoder-decoder structure with skip connections, which later became a widely adopted paradigm in various image enhancement tasks. Its ability to combine multi-scale contextual information with fine-grained spatial details makes it a strong baseline for underwater image enhancement as well. However, the standard U-Net architecture is computationally expensive, limiting its applicability to real-time or embedded enhancement scenarios.

To overcome these limitations, recent studies have focused on lightweight modifications of the U-shaped framework. For example, Unext22 incorporates multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) to reduce convolutional redundancy, while U-Lite23 employs depthwise separable convolutions to significantly lower computational cost without sacrificing enhancement quality. These lightweight strategies improve parameter efficiency, facilitate deployment on mobile or edge devices, and maintain robust performance across diverse visual tasks.

Building on these advancements, our work follows similar lightweight design principles by integrating hybrid-domain attention mechanisms and multi-scale feature extraction within an efficient U-shaped topology. This provides a compact yet powerful architectural foundation for HDAMS-Net, enabling high-quality underwater image enhancement under resource-constrained conditions.

Methodology

We present HDAMS-Net, a lightweight encoder-bottleneck-decoder architecture designed for underwater image restoration by jointly leveraging spatial, frequency, and multi-scale information, inspired by recent hybrid representation learning frameworks24. As shown in Fig. 2, the overall framework follows a U-shaped topology in which the encoder progressively abstracts spatial and frequency features, the bottleneck aggregates multi-scale contextual information, and the decoder reconstructs high-fidelity details via skip-connection fusion. U-shape network architecture

As illustrated in Fig. 2, the proposed HDAMS-Net employs a simplified U-shaped encoder-decoder framework. This configuration allows the network to holistically integrate multi-scale features, achieving an effective balance between high restoration fidelity and computational economy without relying on extensive module stacking. The architecture comprises three core components: a progressive encoder, a bottleneck layer, and a symmetrical decoder.

The primary role of the encoder is to hierarchically reduce spatial dimensions and abstract high-level semantic representations. Conversely, the decoder is tasked with progressively reconstructing fine-grained textures and spatial details through a series of upsampling operations and the fusion of complementary low-level features from the encoder pathway. Skip connections are integral to this design, serving as conduits to transfer rich, high-resolution spatial information from the initial encoder stages to the corresponding deeper decoder stages. This mechanism enhances model performance by facilitating multi-stage feature fusion and mitigating the loss of structural consistency.

In contrast to conventional U-Net blocks, which typically consist of stacked standard convolutional layers, the proposed model introduces novel, specialized building blocks for both the encoder and decoder. These customized modules leverage advanced attention mechanisms and depthwise convolutions to broaden the effective receptive field and improve the adaptive weighting of contextual features. Consequently, this specialized block design enables HDAMS-Net to deliver robust performance and superior processing speed within a streamlined architectural footprint.

MSC module

The MSC Block constitutes the bottleneck stage of HDAMS-Net, serving as a critical bridge between the encoding and decoding paths. It is designed to capture multi-scale contextual information and restore geometric consistency by employing convolutions with diverse kernel shapes and orientations.

Inspired by the bottleneck architectures in OKNet26 and LU2Net27, our approach utilizes multiple parallel convolutional operations, specifically integrating point-wise convolution, large kernel convolution, and both horizontal and vertical stripe convolutions. The large kernel convolution (31× 31) enables the model to effectively capture long-range contextual dependencies within a single stage. The 1× 1 point-wise convolution calibrates pixel-level weights, enhancing the module’s adaptability to local image differences. Furthermore, the horizontally (1× 31) and vertically (31× 1) extended kernels are particularly effective in modeling the anisotropic structures commonly found in underwater images, such as streaking illumination patterns and directional scattering. The core architecture of the MSC module’s bottleneck is composed of four parallel convolutional branches: 31× 31, 1× 1, 1× 31, and 31× 1 (excluding the identity mapping/residual connection). FSA module

The FSA module is strategically divided into two complementary subdomains: a Frequency-Domain branch, which captures global spectral correlations, and a Spatial-Domain branch, which executes channel-wise reweighting and local spatial refinement. These two branches operate jointly to correct holistic color and contrast biases while simultaneously preserving fine-grained textural detail.

This dual-domain design reflects the characteristics of underwater degradation: global color attenuation and contrast imbalance are frequency-domain phenomena, whereas texture loss and edge blurring are inherently spatial. By separating feature processing into these domains, the FSA module corrects global color shifts through spectral modulation while restoring structural details via spatial refinement, enabling joint handling of global illumination distortion and local texture degradation.

Since \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathcal {L}}_{PSNR}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathcal {L}}_{SSIM}$$\end{document} differ in numerical scale, their relative contributions are balanced by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _{3}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _{4}$$\end{document} to maintain optimization stability.

This multi-domain formulation jointly constrains pixel accuracy, structural integrity, and perceptual consistency, leading to visually natural and quantitatively superior underwater image restoration results. In practice, the coefficients are set to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _1=1.0$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _2=0.5$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _3=0.2$$\end{document}, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _4=0.1$$\end{document}, which are empirically found to provide the best balance between quantitative fidelity and perceptual realism.

Experiments

Datasets and evaluation metrics

Underwater image datasets are generally divided into synthetic and real-world types, with models trained on synthetic data often struggling to adapt to real scenes. To address this, we selected training data from three high-quality real-world datasets: UIEB28, EUVP29, and LSUI30, which provide diverse and high-quality underwater images. Specifically, we adopted an 8:2 ratio for data partitioning, where 712 images from UIEB, 18296 from EUVP, and 4183 from LSUI were used for training, while the remaining 178, 4574, and 857 images were reserved for testing, respectively. This ensures a balanced and representative distribution of samples across different underwater conditions, enabling comprehensive evaluation of both the performance and generalization capability of the proposed method.

We employ three widely recognized evaluation metrics: PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. PSNR and SSIM measure pixel fidelity and structural similarity, while LPIPS31 measures perceptual similarity using deep network activations with learned linear weighting, which are widely adopted in modern deep vision tasks. These metrics together provide a thorough and balanced evaluation of each method’s performance in underwater image restoration tasks.

Preprocess all datasets, adjust the size of LSUI images to 256 × 256, UIEB images to 384 × 384, and EUVP images to 128 × 128, and normalize the pixel values to the range of [0,1].

Implementation details

All experiments were implemented in Python using the PyTorch framework on an Ubuntu 22.04 system with an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU (24 GB VRAM). The proposed HDAMS-Net was trained for 1500 epochs with a batch size of 8 using the Adam optimizer (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta _1$$\end{document}=0.5, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta _2$$\end{document}=0.999) and an initial learning rate of 2× 10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^{-4}$$\end{document}, which was halved every 100 epochs. All input images were resized to 256× 256 and normalized to [0,1], with data augmentation including random flipping, rotation, cropping, and scaling to enhance generalization.

Evaluation metrics

For a more detailed quantitative evaluation, we further present the formal definitions of the three metrics used in this study: PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS.

PSNR evaluates pixel-level reconstruction fidelity using the logarithmic ratio between the maximum pixel intensity and the mean squared error (MSE) between the enhanced image and the ground truth:8\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} PSNR = 10 \log _{10} \left( \frac{MAX_I^2}{MSE} \right) , \quad MSE = \frac{1}{N} \sum _{i=1}^{N} (I_i - I_i^{gt})^2. \end{aligned}$$\end{document}SSIM measures the similarity of structural information by comparing luminance, contrast, and local texture:9\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} SSIM(x, y) = \frac{(2\mu _x \mu _y + C_1)(2\sigma _{xy} + C_2)}{(\mu _x^2 + \mu _y^2 + C_1)(\sigma _x^2 + \sigma _y^2 + C_2)}. \end{aligned}$$\end{document}LPIPS reflects perceptual similarity by computing deep feature distances extracted from pretrained neural networks. Lower LPIPS values indicate higher perceptual quality.

These three metrics jointly assess pixel accuracy, structural consistency, and perceptual realism, ensuring a comprehensive evaluation of underwater image enhancement performance.

Generalization discussion

To further assess the generalization capability of HDAMS-Net across diverse underwater conditions, we analyze its behavior from three perspectives: cross-dataset performance, sensitivity to water type variation, and robustness under challenging degradation patterns.

As reported in Table 1, HDAMS-Net achieves consistent improvements over competing methods across all three benchmark datasets-UIEB, EUVP, and LSUI-which differ substantially in image resolution, water type, and degradation severity. UIEB contains real-world images with diverse and unpredictable color casts, EUVP covers both structured and unstructured underwater scenes captured at varying depths, and LSUI comprises large-scale real scenes with mixed illumination and turbidity levels. The fact that HDAMS-Net ranks first or competitively on all three datasets without dataset-specific tuning indicates that the proposed modules generalize effectively across different underwater imaging conditions. The FSA module is designed to address the two dominant degradation modes in underwater imagery: global color attenuation, which manifests as low-frequency spectral imbalance, and local texture blurring, which is a spatial-domain artifact. By explicitly decoupling these two degradation types into frequency and spatial processing branches, HDAMS-Net avoids the common failure mode of methods that apply a uniform enhancement strategy across all degradation types. This architectural choice contributes to the model’s stable performance across greenish, bluish, and turbid underwater scenes, as observed in the qualitative comparisons in Fig. 6. Despite these strengths, HDAMS-Net is trained and evaluated on paired datasets, and its performance under severe domain shift-for instance, when applied to deep-sea footage captured by novel sensor types not present in the training data-has not been fully characterized. Additionally, the current model does not explicitly model temporal consistency, which may limit its performance on underwater video sequences where flickering artifacts can arise. Future work will investigate lightweight domain adaptation and knowledge distillation strategies to extend the model’s robustness to these more challenging deployment scenarios.

Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed HDAMS-Net, a lightweight hybrid-domain attention network for underwater image enhancement. The model integrates the Frequency-Spatial Attention, Channel Attention Pointwise, and Multi-Shape Convolution modules within an encoder-decoder framework to effectively capture global context, inter-channel relationships, and fine structural details. HDAMS-Net efficiently suppresses color distortion and haze while preserving natural textures and illumination balance. Extensive experiments conducted on multiple real-world underwater datasets demonstrate that our method achieves competitive quantitative metrics and favorable visual quality compared to existing state-of-the-art approaches, while maintaining a compact model size and fast inference speed. HDAMS-Net demonstrates consistent performance across multiple real-world benchmark datasets covering diverse water types and degradation conditions, confirming its generalization capability beyond any single training distribution. The explicit decoupling of frequency-domain color correction and spatial-domain texture refinement within the FSA module contributes to this robustness by allowing the network to adapt its enhancement strategy to the dominant degradation mode present in each scene. These properties make HDAMS-Net a viable candidate for real-time deployment on autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) and remotely operated platforms. In future work, we plan to further enhance the generalization of HDAMS-Net by incorporating lightweight model compression, knowledge distillation, and cross-domain adaptation, as well as extending the framework to handle underwater video enhancement with temporal consistency constraints.
