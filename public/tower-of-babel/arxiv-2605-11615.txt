Quillen-McCord theorem for persistence finite posets
Vitalii Guzeev, Kohei Tanaka

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish a persistence version of the Quillen-McCord theorem for persistence finite posets. Given a map f : P → Q f\colon\thinspace P\to Q between persistence finite posets P P and Q Q with weakly ε \varepsilon -contractible homotopy fibers, we provide an upper bound for the homotopy commutative interleaving distance between P P and Q Q .



1.
Introduction

For a continuous map
f\colon\thinspace X\to Y
, studying its homotopy fiber
F_{f}
is essential for understanding the relationship between
X
and
Y
.
In particular, if
F_{f}
is weakly contractible (homotopically trivial), then it follows that
f
is a weak homotopy equivalence.

From a combinatorial viewpoint, the version of this statement applied to (finite) posets is known as the Quillen–McCord theorem (or Quillen fiber lemma)
[
McC66
,
Qui72
]
.
When a finite poset is regarded as a finite
T_{0}
-space whose open sets are upper sets, the Quillen–McCord theorem can be described as follows since the McCord weak homotopy equivalence
\mathcal{B}(P)\to P
for a finite poset
P
and the classifying space
\mathcal{B}(P)

[
McC66
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Quillen-McCord’s theorem
[
McC66
,
Qui72
]
)
.

Let
f\colon\thinspace P\to Q
be an order preserving map between finite posets.
Assume that one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

f^{-1}(L_{v})
is weakly contractible for each
v\in Q
, where
L_{v}=\{x\in Q\mid x\leq v\}
.

(2)

f^{-1}(U_{v})
is weakly contractible for each
v\in Q
, where
U_{v}=\{x\in Q\mid x\geq v\}
.

Then
f
is a weak homotopy equivalence.

The following result is also known as a homological version of the above theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(Homological Quillen-McCord’s theorem
[
Bar11a
]
)
.

Let
R
be a PID and let
H_{*}
denote the homology with coefficients in
R
.
For an order preserving map
f\colon\thinspace P\to Q
between finite posets
P
and
Q
, we assume that one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

f^{-1}(L_{v})
is acyclic, i.e.
H_{*}(f^{-1}(L_{v}))
is trivial for each
v\in Q
.

(2)

f^{-1}(U_{v})
is acyclic, i.e.
H_{*}(f^{-1}(U_{v}))
is trivial for each
v\in Q
.

Then
f
induces an isomorphism
H_{*}(P)\cong H_{*}(Q)
.

On the other hand, in the framework of TDA (Topological Data Analysis), one considers a sequence of spaces indexed by a numeric parameter and computes its persistence homologies.

One established technique of building a sequence of spaces is to start with a collection of data equipped with numeric indexes and consider spaces built from the said collection filtered by different threshold values of index (see
[
SG07
,
KCM+21
]
for examples).
In this technique, resulting object is naturally a filtration. The other known approach involves indexing by time (
[
SSG+18
]
).

In either case, researchers face two problems.

(1)

How to measure proximity of results of different experiments and when to consider them essentially equivalent.

(2)

How to actually compute the results: computation of invariants like persistent homology heavily depends on the data size, for coefficients in a field specifically on dimension of spaces, and may have unacceptable costs.

It seems possible that a persistence version of a Quillen-McCord theorem is useful for resolution of both problems.
Specifically, given a suitable persistence version of a theorem one could design an algorithm to reduce dimension of persistence homology at the cost of sharpness of the result (
[
Ayz
,
Guz
]
).

One of the tools used to compare results, namely persistent homology, is the interleaving distance denoted by
d_{\mathrm{I}}(M,N)
for persistence modules
M,N
.
As a persistence version of the homological Quillen-McCord theorem (Theorem
1.2
), the previous work
[
Guz
]
provides an estimate on the interleaving distance of the associated persistent homologies
H_{*}(P)
and
H_{*}(Q)
for a map
f\colon\thinspace P\to Q
with
\varepsilon
-acyclic homotopy fibers.

Theorem 1.3

(Persistence homological Quillen-McCord’s theorem
[
Guz
]
)
.

For a map
f\colon\thinspace P\to Q
between suitable persistence finite posets
P
and
Q
, we assume that one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

f^{-1}(L_{v})
is
\varepsilon
-acyclic for each persistence point
v
in
Q
.

(2)

f^{-1}(U_{v})
is
\varepsilon
-acyclic for each persistence point
v
in
Q
.

Then we have the following inequality for each
i
:

d_{\mathrm{I}}(H_{i}(P),H_{i}(Q))\leq 4\varepsilon|Q|,

where
|Q|
denotes the number of persistence points in
Q
.

The aim of this paper is to establish a persistence version of the Quillen-McCord theorem (Theorem
1.1
).
The primary focus is on comparisons at the level of persistence spaces, prior to passing to homology.

For persistence spaces, several notions of interleaving distance up to homotopy are known
[
BL23
,
LS23
]
.
A natural one is the homotopy commutative interleaving distance
d_{\mathrm{HC}}
, defined using interleavings in the homotopy category of topological spaces.

Theorem 1.4

(Persistence Quillen-McCord’s theorem (Theorem
3.8
))
.

For a map
f\colon\thinspace P\to Q
between suitable persistence finite posets
P
and
Q
, we assume that one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

f^{-1}(L_{v})
is weakly
\varepsilon
-contractible for each persistence point
v
in
Q
.

(2)

f^{-1}(U_{v})
is weakly
\varepsilon
-contractible for each persistence point
v
in
Q
.

Then we have the following inequality:

d_{\mathrm{HC}}(P,Q)\leq 2\varepsilon|Q|.

As a consequence of our main result, one can improve the upper bound for the interleaving distance of homology.

Corollary 1.5

(Corollary
3.10
)
.

For a map
f\colon\thinspace P\to Q
between suitable persistence finite posets
P
and
Q
, we assume that one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

f^{-1}(L_{v})
is weakly
\varepsilon
-contractible for each persistence point
v
in
Q
.

(2)

f^{-1}(U_{v})
is weakly
\varepsilon
-contractible for each persistence point
v
in
Q
.

Then we have the following inequality for each

:

d_{\mathrm{I}}(H_{i}(P),H_{i}(Q))\leq 2\varepsilon|Q|.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows:
In Section 2, we review persistence theory and the homotopy theory of simplicial complexes.
Since our arguments mainly rely on the order complexes associated with finite spaces (posets), we also provide the necessary preliminaries.

In Section 3, we prove the main theorem (Theorem
3.8
).
The key ingredient in the proof is an estimate of how the distance
d_{\mathrm{HC}}
changes when a reducible point is removed, as in Proposition
3.4
.
Combining this estimate with the reduction method based on mapping cylinders developed in
[
Bar11a
,
Guz
]
, we obtain the result.

2.
Preliminaries on persistence objects

To treat indexed objects such as vector spaces and topological spaces in a unified manner, we introduce the notions of persistence objects and interleavings in a category.

2.1.
Persistence objects

A poset
P
can be regarded as a category whose object set is
P
and there exists at most one morphism between two objects.
Let
\mathbb{N}
denote the poset of natural numbers with the standard total order.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a category. A
persistence object
in
\mathscr{C}
(indexed by
\mathbb{N}
) is a functor
X\colon\thinspace\mathbb{N}\to\mathscr{C}
.
In other words, a persistence object
X
consists of a family of objects
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and morphisms
\varphi^{X}_{i,j}\colon\thinspace X_{i}\to X_{j}
in
\mathscr{C}
for
i\leq j
satisfying the following condition:

(1)

\varphi^{X}_{i,i}=\mathrm{id}_{X_{i}}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

(2)

\varphi^{X}_{j,k}\circ\varphi^{X}_{i,j}=\varphi^{X}_{i,k}
for
i\leq j\leq k
.

The morphisms
\varphi^{X}_{i,j}
are called
structure maps
and are denoted simply by
\varphi_{i,j}
when there is no risk of confusion.
For a persistence object
X
in
\mathscr{C}
, the structure maps are determined by a family of maps
\{\varphi_{i,i+1}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
because any
\varphi_{i,j}
can be represented as

\varphi_{i,j}=\varphi_{j-1,j}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi_{i+1,i+2}\circ\varphi_{i,i+1}.

Remark 2.2
.

When dealing with continuously varying objects, it is common to regard
\mathbb{R}
, rather than
\mathbb{N}
, as a totally ordered set and to consider functors
\mathbb{R}\to\mathscr{C}
as persistence objects in
\mathscr{C}
.
However, since we mainly focus on discrete finite spaces (posets), we take
\mathbb{N}
as the indexing category.

Example 2.3
.

We give several typical examples of persistence objects.

(1)

Let
R
be a PID. A functor
\mathbb{N}\to R{\operatorname{-Mod}}
to the category
R{\operatorname{-Mod}}
of
R
-modules is called a
persistence module
over
R
.

(2)

A functor
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbf{Top}
to the category
\mathbf{Top}
of topological spaces is called a
persistence space
.

(3)

A functor
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbf{Pos}
to the category
\mathbf{Pos}
of posets is called a
persistence poset
.

A persistence object that stabilizes after some point is said to be of finite type.

Definition 2.4
.

A persistence object
X
in
\mathscr{C}
is said to be of
finite type
if there exists
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that the structure map
\varphi_{j,k}\colon\thinspace X_{j}\to X_{k}
is an isomorphism for all
i\leq j\leq k
.

We introduce morphisms between persistence objects that take shifts into account.

.

For
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{N}
and two persistence objects

and

in
\mathscr{C}
, an
\varepsilon
-map (or morphism)

is a family of morphisms
\{f_{i}\colon\thinspace X_{i}\to Y_{i+\varepsilon}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

making the following diagram commute for any

\scriptstyle{f_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\varphi^{X}_{i,j}}
\textstyle{Y_{i+\varepsilon}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi^{Y}_{i+\varepsilon,j+\varepsilon}}
\textstyle{X_{j}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{j}}

In particular, a
0
-map agrees with a natural transformation between the functors
X
and
Y
, and we simply call it a
map (or morphism)
of persistence objects.

A particularly important self
\varepsilon
-map is the shift map defined using the structure maps.
Let
X
be a persistence object in
\mathscr{C}
. The
\varepsilon
-map
\mathrm{Id}^{\varepsilon}\colon\thinspace X\to X
is defined by
\mathrm{Id}^{\varepsilon}_{i}=\varphi_{i,i+\varepsilon}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Persistence objects
X
and
Y
in
\mathscr{C}
are
\varepsilon
-interleaved
if there exist
\varepsilon
-maps

f\colon\thinspace X\to Y
and
g\colon\thinspace Y\to X
such that
g\circ f=\mathrm{Id}^{2\varepsilon}
and
f\circ g=\mathrm{Id}^{2\varepsilon}
.
That is, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the composition
g_{i+\varepsilon}\circ f_{i}\colon\thinspace X_{i}\to X_{i+2\varepsilon}
coincides with the structure map
\varphi^{X}_{i,i+2\varepsilon}
, and the composition
f_{i+\varepsilon}\circ g_{i}\colon\thinspace Y_{i}\to Y_{i+2\varepsilon}
coincides with the structure map
\varphi^{Y}_{i,i+2\varepsilon}
.

Note that
0
-interleaved persistence objects are isomorphic as functors.

For two persistence objects
X
and
Y
in a category
\mathscr{C}
, the
interleaving distance

d_{\mathrm{I}}(X,Y)
is defined as

d_{\mathrm{I}}(X,Y)=\inf\{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\mid\textrm{$X$ and $Y$ are $\varepsilon$-interleaved}\}.

This is an extended pseudometric on persistence objects.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
F\colon\thinspace\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
be a functor.
If two persistence objects
X
and
Y
in
\mathscr{C}
are
\varepsilon
-interleaved, then
F(X)
and
F(Y)
are
\varepsilon
-interleaved in
\mathscr{D}
. Moreover, we have
d_{\mathrm{I}}(F(X),F(Y))\leq d_{\mathrm{I}}(X,Y)
.

Proof.

This immediately follows from the fact that
F
preserves compositions and identities.

There are several notions of interleavings up to homotopy for persistence spaces, or more generally persistence objects in a model category (see
[
BL23
,
LS23
]
).
One simple idea is to consider interleavings in the homotopy category of topological spaces.
Here, the homotopy category
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathbf{Top})
is the localization of
\mathbf{Top}
with respect to weak homotopy equivalences.

Definition 2.10
.

Persistence spaces
X
and
Y
are
homotopy commutative
\varepsilon
-interleaved
if they are
\varepsilon
-interleaved in the homotopy category
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathbf{Top})
.

More precisely, if
\Gamma\colon\thinspace\mathbf{Top}\to\mathrm{Ho}(\mathbf{Top})
is the localization, which is the identity on objects, then two persistence spaces
X
and
Y
are homotopy commutative
\varepsilon
-interleaved if and only if
\Gamma(X)
and
\Gamma(Y)
are
\varepsilon
-interleaved.
We then define the
homotopy interleaving distance

d_{\mathrm{HC}}
by

d_{\mathrm{HC}}(X,Y):=d_{\mathrm{I}}(\Gamma(X),\Gamma(Y)).

Remark 2.11
.

In the case that
X
and
Y
are persistence CW complexes, the homotopy commutative interleavings between
X
and
Y
can be translated into lax maps by the Whitehead theorem.
Namely,
X
and
Y
are homotopy commutative
\varepsilon
-interleaved if and only if
there exist lax
\varepsilon
-maps

f\colon\thinspace X\to Y
and
g\colon\thinspace Y\to X
such that
g\circ f\simeq\mathrm{Id}^{X}_{2\varepsilon}
and
f\circ g\simeq\mathrm{Id}_{2\varepsilon}^{Y}
.

That is, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the composition
g_{i+\varepsilon}\circ f_{i}\colon\thinspace X_{i}\to X_{i+2\varepsilon}
is homotopic to the structure map
\varphi^{X}_{i,i+2\varepsilon}
in
X
, and the composition
f_{i+\varepsilon}\circ g_{i}\colon\thinspace Y_{i}\to Y_{i+2\varepsilon}
is homotopic to the structure map
\varphi^{Y}_{i,i+2\varepsilon}
in

.

Definition 2.15
.

A

is a persistence space satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

(2)

Definition 2.16
.

Let

of

\varphi_{i,j}^{-1}(x_{j})=\{x_{i}\}

Remark 2.17
.

For a persistence point

(X\backslash x)_{i}=X_{i}\backslash x_{i}

.
Note that the restriction of structure map

.

A persistence space
X
is called a

if every structure map
\varphi^{X}_{i,j}
is injective.

Example 2.18
.

Let
X
be a filtration. A point

determines a persistence point

in

x_{j}=\begin{cases}\varphi_{i,j}(x_{i})&\textrm{if $i\leq j$,}\\
\varphi_{j,i}^{-1}(x_{i})&\textrm{if $j<i$.}\end{cases}

Conversely, any persistence point

in a filtration

is uniquely determined by a point

Remark 2.19

A filtration

as a persistence set.
This does not hold in general for persistence spaces (sets) that are not filtrations.
This fact is essential to the reduction method for persistence points used in the proof of Theorem

2.3.
Simplicial complexes

In developing a combinatorial homotopy theory, simplicial complexes are convenient to work with.
For basic notions concerning (abstract) simplicial complexes, we refer the reader to
[
Spa66

.

Definition 2.20
.

A
simplicial complex

K
consists of the set of vertices
V(K)
and the set of simplices
\Sigma(K)
which is a subset of the power set

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\{v\}\in\Sigma(K)
for any

,

(2)

\sigma\subset\tau
and
\tau\in\Sigma(K)
imply
\sigma\in\Sigma(K)
.

We deal only with finite simplicial complexes which have finitely many vertices in this paper.

Definition 2.21
.

The

Let

3.
A persistence Quillen–McCord theorem for finite persistence posets

3.1.
Persistence finite posets (spaces)

A (finite) poset
P
can be regarded as a topological space with the Alexandroff topology that consists of upper sets.
Here, an upper set (resp. a lower set) of
P
is a subset closed upward (resp. downward) in
P
.

Conversely, a finite
\mathrm{T}_{0}
-space
X
can be regarded as a poset with the specialization order
x\leq y
defined by
U_{x}\supset U_{y}
, where
U_{x}
and
U_{y}
are the minimal open neighborhoods of
x
and
y
respectively.
Furthermore, a map
f\colon\thinspace X\to Y
between finite
\mathrm{T}_{0}
-spaces
X,Y
is continuous if and only if
f
is order-preserving.

From this perspective, we identify finite posets with finite
\mathrm{T}_{0}
-spaces (see
[
Bar11b
,
McC66
]
for details).
Hence, persistence finite posets can be viewed as persistence spaces that we have seen in Section
2.2
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Persistence finite posets
P
and
Q
are homotopy commutative
\varepsilon
-interleaved if and only if the induced persistence spaces
\mathcal{B}(P)
and
\mathcal{B}(Q)
are homotopy commutative
\varepsilon
-interleaved.

Proof.

The result follows from the fact that
R
and
\mathcal{B}(R)
are isomorphic in
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathbf{Top})
for any finite poset
R
by McCord’s weak homotopy equivalence
\mathcal{B}(R)\to R

[
McC66
]
.
This map gives natural isomorphisms
\mathcal{B}(P)\cong P
and
\mathcal{B}(Q)\cong Q
as functors
\mathbb{N}\to\mathrm{Ho}(\mathbf{Top})
.
In other words,
\mathcal{B}(P)
and
P
(
\mathcal{B}(Q)
and

) are homotopy commutative
0
-interleaved.
Hence,
P
and

are homotopy commutative
\varepsilon
-interleaved if and only if
\mathcal{B}(P)
and
\mathcal{B}(Q)
are homotopy commutative
\varepsilon
-interleaved.
∎

Concluding remarks and future directions

In this paper, we established a persistence version of the Quillen–McCord theorem for finite persistence posets.
Our main result (Theorem
3.8
) implies that if there is a map
P\to Q
from a persistence poset
P
to a finite type filtration
Q
with weakly
\varepsilon
-contractible homotopy fibers, then we have

d_{\mathrm{HC}}(P,Q)\leq 2\varepsilon|Q|.

Based on the present results, we would like to outline several directions for future investigation.

(1)

It is worthwhile to examine the sharpness of the estimates for
d_{\mathrm{HC}}
obtained in this work.
Although we have provided an upper bound for
d_{\mathrm{HC}}
, it remains to determine how tight this bound is.
To this end, one should investigate concrete examples of persistence finite posets
P,Q
, and establish methods for computing
d_{\mathrm{HC}}
.

(2)

For persistence spaces
X,Y
, Proposition
3.9
induces the following inequality:

d_{\mathrm{I}}(H_{*}(X),H_{*}(Y))\leq d_{\mathrm{HC}}(X,Y).

The difference between
d_{\mathrm{I}}
and
d_{\mathrm{HC}}
is of interest as a way to explicitly capture the change in information after passing to homology.

Since computations in general settings are expected to be difficult, it may be worthwhile to consider special cases—for instance, when
X
and
Y
are finite spaces (posets) satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
3.8
—where this difference can be computed.

(3)

For a given finite poset (space)
P
, it is known that there exists, up to isomorphism, a unique minimal finite poset
P^{\prime}
that has the same homotopy type as
P

[
Sto66
]
.
From the viewpoint of applications to TDA, it is of particular interest to formulate and construct a minimal model (in an appropriate sense) for a given persistence finite poset.
In fact, in our main theorem (Theorem
3.8
), the upper bound depends on the size of
Q
; therefore, it may be possible to improve the estimate by replacing
Q
with a smaller persistence finite poset
Q^{\prime}
.

Furthermore, for a given persistence finite poset
Q
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
, it is of practical interest to construct a smaller model
P
than
Q
satisfying the conditions of Theorem
3.8
.
