Semiorthogonal decompositions for stacks
Chenjing Bu, Tudor Pădurariu, Yukinobu Toda

Abstract
We give a systematic construction of semiorthogonal decompositions of derived categories of coherent sheaves on quasi-smooth derived algebraic stacks over C \mathbb{C} , where the summands are subcategories defined by weight conditions, and the inclusion functors are given by parabolic induction. The summands are indexed by the component lattice of the stack, a central combinatorial structure in intrinsic Donaldson–Thomas theory. As examples, we obtain semiorthogonal decompositions for moduli stacks of semistable G G -bundles or G G -Higgs bundles on a curve, and moduli stacks of de Rham or Betti G G -local systems on a curve, for reductive groups G G not necessarily of type A.

1.1.3

Next, we explain our main motivations, and we review related prior work.

Our main motivation for our construction comes from Donaldson–Thomas (DT) theory,
which studies, among other topics, numerical, cohomological, and categorical structures
associated to
(-1)
-shifted symplectic stacks

defined by
[
74
]
.

Originally, Donaldson–Thomas theory
[
25
,
81
]

focused on virtual counts of ideal sheaves on complex threefolds,
called
Donaldson–Thomas invariants
,
and their relationship to curve-counting invariants as conjectured by
[
58
,
59
]
.
Later,
[
50
]
and

[
54
]

defined invariants which are virtual counts of semistable sheaves on Calabi–Yau threefolds and,
more broadly, of semistable objects in
3
-Calabi–Yau abelian categories.
They also defined invariants in the presence of strictly semistable objects,
which allowed for advances in wall-crossing formulas for DT invariants
and in the study of cohomological Hall algebras
[
55
]
.
Although these invariants can be rational, they are related through multiple cover formulas to integer-valued invariants known as BPS invariants, named after Bogomol’nyi–Prasad–Sommerfield. In the absence of stricly semistable objects, BPS invariants coincide with DT invariants.

It is now understood that DT invariants can be defined
for more general stacks, namely for certain
(-1)
-shifted symplectic stacks,
not necesarily moduli of objects in a
3
-Calabi–Yau category,
thanks to the new framework of

intrinsic Donaldson–Thomas theory
, developed in

[
17
,
19
,
18
]
.

Further, since the works of
[
11
]
,
[
55
]
,
and
[
13
]
,
it is understood that BPS invariants
are the Euler characteristic of graded vector spaces
obtained as the cohomology of certain perverse sheaves.
This is the subject of study of cohomological Donaldson–Thomas theory.
A central result in this subject is cohomological integrality,
proposed by
[
55
]

and proved in increasing generality by

[
29
]
,

[
22
]
,
and more recently in the intrinsic DT theory framework in

[
16
,
44
]
.
Next, we briefly recall this result.

For a
(-1)
-shifted symplectic stack
\mathcal{X}

satisfying certain assumptions, as in
[

,
44
]
,
including symmetricity, orientability, and the existence of a good moduli space
in the sense of
[
5
]
,
one considers a perverse sheaf

on

obtained by gluing vanishing cycles sheaves for functions on smooth stacks
whose critical loci are local models of the stack

,
see
[

]
.
In general, the
critical cohomology

\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{crit}}(\mathcal{X})=\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{X},\varphi_{\mathcal{X}})

is a graded rational vector space whose total dimension is infinite.
However, under the above assumptions, as shown in

[
16
,

]

this critical cohomology can be decomposed into finite-dimensional pieces,
called

whose Euler characteristic gives the BPS invariant.

1.1.4

The current paper is concerned with a further refinement of these enumerative invariants,
which is the subject of study of categorical Donaldson–Thomas theory initiated in

[
83
]
.
We are interested in categorical analogues of the critical cohomology,
which might be called critical categories,
and in semiorthogonal decompositions of such categories,
whose summands are window subcategories called quasi-BPS categories

[
73
,
72
,
64
,
63
,
67
,
70
,
65
,
66
,
69

Such decompositions may be viewed as categorical analogues of cohomological integrality.
However, this relationship is less direct than one might hope.
For instance, if one applies an additive invariant directly to our decomposition,
such as periodic cyclic homology,
one does not expect to recover the cohomological integrality decomposition.
There are two reasons for this.

First, additive invariants of stacks, including periodic cyclic homology,
do not directly recover the Borel–Moore homology of the stack;
this is already evident in the example of the classifying stack
*/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
.
Second, even after applying topological or algebraic
K
-theory
to the semiorthogonal decomposition,
the resulting decomposition is not as refined as
the decomposition appearing in BPS cohomology.
One expects that a finer decomposition should instead be constructed
directly in rational topological or algebraic
K
-theory,
which does not originate from a categorical decomposition.
We do not pursue this direction further here,
and refer the reader to
[
63
]

New phenomena appear in
K

not visible in cohomology,
such as the dependence on an auxiliary parameter
\delta

1.1.1.2

which controls the positions of the windows.
This also affects the size of quasi-BPS categories,
which has the effect that only some of these categories (those of the correct size)
recover the two-periodic version of the BPS cohomology;
see
[
66
]

for details in the case of symmetric quivers with potential.

Currently, there is no known construction of critical categories
for general
(-1)
-shifted symplectic stacks,
although they are known in some cases.
Important progress towards the general case has been made in recent works of

[

,

In this paper, we restrict to the following two situations,

We start with a quasi-smooth derived algebraic stack

Secondly, we assume that

2 The linear quotient case

2.1   The decomposition

We start by constructing semiorthogonal decompositions
for quotient stacks of the form
V/G
,
where
V
is a representation of a reductive group
G
.
Note that
\mathsf{Perf}(V/G)=\mathsf{Coh}(V/G)
.
The main result of this section is
Theorem

2.1.6
,
and it will serve as a local model for
semiorthogonal decompositions for more general stacks later on.

2.1.1
The setting

Throughout this section, we work with the following setting:

•

Let
G
be a connected reductive group over
\mathbb{C}
,
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

•

Let
T\subset G
be a maximal torus,
and let
W=\mathrm{N}_{G}(T)/T
be the Weyl group.

•

Let
\Lambda_{T}=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},T)
and

\Lambda^{T}=\mathrm{Hom}(T,\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})

be the cocharacter and character lattices.

•

Let
V
be a
G
-representation, and let

\mathrm{wt}(V)\in\mathbb{N}[\Lambda^{T}]

be the multiset of
T
-weights in
V
.

We say that

•

V
is
symmetric

if
V\simeq V^{\vee}
as
G
-representations.

•

V
is
quasi-symmetric

if for any rational line

\ell\subset\Lambda^{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}

through the origin, the weighted sum of elements of the multiset

\mathrm{wt}(V)\cap\ell

is
0
.

See
[
80
, §7]

for the latter notion.

2.1.2
Hall induction

In the above setting,
let
\lambda\in\Lambda_{T}
be a cocharacter.
Let
G^{\lambda}\subset G
and
V^{\lambda}\subset V

be the
\lambda
-fixed loci, where
G
acts on itself by conjugation,
and let
G^{\lambda,+}\subset G
and
V^{\lambda,+}\subset V

be the
\lambda
-attracted loci.
The subgroups

G^{\lambda}\subset G^{\lambda,+}\subset G

are called the
Levi subgroup

and the
parabolic subgroup

associated to
\lambda
, respectively.

We have a natural correspondence

V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}\overset{\mathrm{gr}_{\lambda}}{\longleftarrow}V^{\lambda,+}/G^{\lambda,+}\overset{\mathrm{ev}_{\lambda}}{\longrightarrow}V/G\ ,

(2.1.2.1)

and we define the
Hall induction
functor

{\star}_{\lambda}=(\mathrm{ev}_{\lambda})_{*}\,\mathrm{gr}_{\lambda}^{*}\colon\mathsf{Perf}(V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda})\longrightarrow\mathsf{Perf}(V/G)\ .

(2.1.2.2)

This is well-defined as the morphism

\mathrm{ev}_{\lambda}
is proper.

Since the correspondence

Eq.

2.1.2.1

does not change when
\lambda
is scaled by a positive factor,
the functor
{\star}_{\lambda}

can also be defined for rational cocharacters

\lambda\in\Lambda_{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.

2.1.3
Weight polytopes

Define the multiset

\mathrm{wt}(V/G)=\mathrm{wt}(V)-\mathrm{wt}(\mathfrak{g})\in\mathbb{Z}[\Lambda^{T}]\ ,

(2.1.3.1)

seen as a multiset with possibly negative multiplicities,
where the minus sign denotes taking the difference of multiplicities,
and
\mathfrak{g}
denotes the adjoint representation of
G
.

Define the
weight polytope

\nabla_{V/G}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in\mathrm{wt}(V)}{}[0,v]-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in\mathrm{wt}(\mathfrak{g})}{}[0,v]\quad{\subset}\quad\Lambda^{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}\ ,

(2.1.3.2)

where we take a Minkowski difference of Minkowski sums.
Equivalently,

\nabla_{V/G}
is the set of rational weights

w\in\Lambda^{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}

such that

w(\lambda)\in\Biggl[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in\mathrm{wt}(V/G)^{\lambda<0}}v(\lambda)\ ,\ \frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in\mathrm{wt}(V/G)^{\lambda>0}}v(\lambda)\Biggr]

(2.1.3.3)

for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
,
where
w(\lambda)
is the natural pairing, we sum with multiplicities,

\mathrm{wt}(V/G)^{\lambda<0}

denotes the part of
\mathrm{wt}(V/G)

consisting of those
v
with
v(\lambda)<0
,
and similarly for
\mathrm{wt}(V/G)^{\lambda>0}
.

For example, we have

\nabla_{\mathfrak{g}/G}=\{0\}
,
and

\nabla_{*/G}=\varnothing

unless
G
is a torus.

When
V
is quasi-symmetric, we have the symmetry

\nabla_{V/G}=-\nabla_{V/G}
.

2.1.4
Window subcategories

For each rational weight

\delta\in(\Lambda^{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q})^{W}
,
define the
window subcategory

\mathsf{W}_{V/G}(\delta)\subset\mathsf{Perf}(V/G)

to be the subcategory generated by the vector bundles

\Gamma_{V/G}(w)=p^{*}(V_{w})\ ,\qquad w\in(\nabla_{V/G}+\delta)\cap\Lambda^{T}_{+}\ ,

where
p\colon V/G\to{*}/G
is the projection,

\Lambda^{T}_{+}\subset\Lambda^{T}
is the set of dominant weights,
and
V_{w}
is the irreducible
G
-representation of highest weight
w
.

2.1.5
Semiorthogonal decompositions

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a pretriangulated dg-category,
let
I
be a totally ordered set,
and let
(\mathcal{D}_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family
of pretriangulated full subcategories of
\mathcal{D}
.
We say that the subcategories
\mathcal{D}_{i}

form a
semiorthogonal decomposition
of
\mathcal{D}
,
denoted by

\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{D}_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle\ ,

if the following conditions hold:

(i)

(
Generation
)
The subcategories
\mathcal{D}_{i}
generate
\mathcal{D}
.

(ii)

(
Semiorthogonality
)
For any
i,j\in I
with
i>j
,
and any
x_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{i}
and
x_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{j}
,
we have
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(x_{i},x_{j})=0
.

The subcategories
\mathcal{D}_{i}

are not required to be admissible,
although in the semiorthogonal decompositions that we obtain,
we will often show that this is the case.

Theorem 2.1.6

In the setting of
“S

2.1.1
,
assume that
V
is quasi-symmetric,
and suppose we are given the following extra data:

•

A
W
-invariant positive-definite quadratic form

q\in(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\Lambda^{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}))^{W}
.

•

A
W
-invariant rational weight

\delta\in(\Lambda^{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q})^{W}
.

Then there is a semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathsf{Perf}(V/G)=\Bigl<\star_{\lambda}\,\mathsf{W}_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}(\delta_{\lambda})\Bigm|\lambda\in(\Lambda_{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q})/W\Bigr>\ ,

(2.1.6.1)

where

\delta_{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}

is given by

\delta_{\lambda}=\delta-v_{\lambda}-q(\lambda)\ ,\qquad v_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in\mathrm{wt}(V/G)^{\lambda>0}}{}v\ ,

(2.1.6.2)

where
q
is seen as an isomorphism

q\colon\Lambda^{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}\mathrel{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}\Lambda_{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.
The rational weight
\delta_{\lambda}
is invariant under the Weyl group of
G^{\lambda}
.
Each functor

{\star}_{\lambda}\colon\mathsf{W}_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}(\delta_{\lambda})\to\mathsf{Perf}(V/G)

is fully faithful.

The order of the semiorthogonal decomposition
can be chosen to be any order such that for any two terms labelled
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
,
if
|\lambda|_{q}<|\lambda^{\prime}|_{q}
,
then
\lambda
is on the right of
\lambda^{\prime}
,
where
|\lambda|_{q}=q(\lambda,\lambda)^{1/2}

is the
q
-norm of
\lambda
.

The proof of the theorem will be given in
Section

2.3
.

Here, the choice of
\delta_{\lambda}

can be understood
as choosing a partition

\Lambda^{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}}{}(\nabla_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}+\delta_{\lambda})\ ,

(2.1.6.3)

as illustrated in
Fig.

1

in the introduction.

Remark 2.1.7

In
Theorem

2.1.6
, the notation

\lambda\in(\Lambda_{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q})/W

should be precisely understood as follows: if

\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}

are in the same
W
-orbit,
so that
\lambda^{\prime}=\pi\cdot\lambda

for some
\pi\in W
,
then there is an induced isomorphism

V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}\mathrel{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\to}}V^{\lambda^{\prime}}/G^{\lambda^{\prime}}
,
which identifies the window subcategory

\mathsf{W}_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}(\delta)

with

\mathsf{W}_{\smash{V^{\lambda^{\prime}}/G^{\lambda^{\prime}}}}(\pi\cdot\delta)
,
and is compatible with the Hall induction, so that

{\star}_{\lambda}\,\mathsf{W}_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}(\delta_{\lambda})={\star}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\,\mathsf{W}_{\smash{V^{\lambda^{\prime}}/G^{\lambda^{\prime}}}}(\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}})

as subcategories of
\mathsf{Perf}(V/G)
,
where we note that
\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}}=\pi\cdot\delta_{\lambda}
.

, Section 4.6]
.

In fact,
Theorem

2.1.6
can be applied to
any symmetric quiver,
and we will further generalize this to any quiver with a generic stability condition
in
Example

3.3.2
below.

2.3   Proof of the decomposition

This subsection is dedicated to the proof of
Theorem

2.1.6
.

2.3.1
The Borel–Weil–Bott theorem

We recall a corollary of the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem
from
[
64
, Proposition 2.1]

and
[
40
, Proposition 3.8]
,
which gives an explicit description of the Hall induction functor
on vector bundles induced from irreducible representations.

For an anti-dominant rational cocharacter

\lambda\in\Lambda_{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}

and a dominant weight
w\in\Lambda^{T}
, let

\Gamma_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}(w)\in\mathsf{Perf}(V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda})

be the vector bundle on
V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}

given by the irreducible
G^{\lambda}
-representation
with highest weight
w
.
Then there is an explicit presentation

\star_{\lambda}\,\Gamma_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}(w)\simeq\biggl(\bigoplus_{J}\Gamma_{V/G}\bigl((w-v_{J})^{+}\bigr)[|J|-\ell_{J}]\ ,\ d\biggr)\ ,

(2.3.1.1)

where
J
runs through sub-multisets

J\subset\mathrm{wt}^{\lambda<0}(V)=\{v\in\mathrm{wt}(V)\mid\langle\lambda,v\rangle<0\}

such that
v_{J}=\sum_{v\in J}v

satisfies that
w-v_{J}

has a trivial stabilizer under the shifted action of
W
defined by

\pi*w=\pi\cdot(w+\rho)-\rho

for
\pi\in W
and
w\in\Lambda^{T}
,
where
\rho
is the half sum of positive roots.
In this case, there exists a unique
\pi\in W

such that
\pi*(w-v_{J})=(w-v_{J})^{+}

is dominant,
and
\ell_{J}
denotes the length of
\pi
.

2.3.2
Semiorthogonality

We first show that for anti-dominant rational cocharacters

\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}

such that
|\lambda|_{q}\leqslant|\lambda^{\prime}|_{q}
,
and any

E_{\lambda}\in\mathsf{W}_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}(\delta_{\lambda})

and

E_{\lambda^{\prime}}\in\mathsf{W}_{\smash{V^{\lambda^{\prime}}/G^{\lambda^{\prime}}}}(\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}})
,
we have

\mathrm{Hom}_{V/G}(\star_{\lambda}\,E_{\lambda},\star_{\lambda^{\prime}}\,E_{\lambda^{\prime}})\simeq 0

(2.3.2.1)

in
\mathsf{Perf}(V/G)
.

Indeed, by adjunction, this is equivalent to

\mathrm{Hom}_{\smash{V^{\lambda^{\prime},+}/G^{\lambda^{\prime},+}}}(\mathrm{ev}_{\smash{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{*}\,{\star_{\lambda}}\,E_{\lambda},\mathrm{gr}_{\smash{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{*}\,E_{\lambda^{\prime}})\simeq 0
.
We may assume

E_{\lambda}=\Gamma_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}(w)
and

E_{\lambda^{\prime}}=\Gamma_{\smash{V^{\lambda^{\prime}}/G^{\lambda^{\prime}}}}(w^{\prime})

for
w\in\nabla_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}+\delta_{\lambda}

and
w^{\prime}\in\nabla_{\smash{V^{\lambda^{\prime}}/G^{\lambda^{\prime}}}}+\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}}
.
By
Eq.

2.3.1.1
and

[
73
, Proposition 4.2]
,
it is enough to show that

\langle\lambda^{\prime},(w-v_{J})^{+}\rangle>\langle\lambda^{\prime},w^{\prime}\rangle

(2.3.2.2)

for all
J\subset\mathrm{wt}^{\lambda<0}(V)

such that
(w-v_{J})^{+}
is defined.

Write
w=u+\delta_{\lambda}

and
w^{\prime}=u^{\prime}+\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}}

for some
u\in\nabla_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}

and
u^{\prime}\in\nabla_{\smash{V^{\lambda^{\prime}}/G^{\lambda^{\prime}}}}
,
so that

\langle\lambda,u\rangle=\langle\lambda^{\prime},u^{\prime}\rangle=0
,
and write
(w-v_{J})^{+}=\pi*(w-v_{J})
for some
\pi\in W
.
Let

\rho_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\smash{v\in\mathrm{wt}^{\lambda<0}(\mathfrak{g})}}v
,
which is a half sum of some positive roots.
Then we have

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}\langle\lambda^{\prime},(w-v_{J})^{+}\rangle-\langle\lambda^{\prime},w^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\lambda^{\prime},\delta+(u-v_{\lambda}-q(\lambda)-v_{J})^{+}\rangle-\langle\lambda^{\prime},\delta-v_{\lambda^{\prime}}\rangle+|\lambda^{\prime}|_{q}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\lambda^{\prime},\pi\cdot(u-v_{\lambda}-q(\lambda)-v_{J}+\rho)-\rho+v_{\lambda^{\prime}}\rangle+|\lambda^{\prime}|_{q}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\langle\lambda^{\prime},\pi\cdot(-q(\lambda))-\rho+\rho_{\lambda^{\prime}}\rangle+|\lambda^{\prime}|_{q}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\lambda^{\prime},\pi\cdot(-q(\lambda))\rangle+|\lambda^{\prime}|_{q}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant{-|\lambda^{\prime}|_{q}}\cdot|\lambda|_{q}+|\lambda^{\prime}|_{q}^{2}\geqslant 0\ ,

(2.3.2.3)

where the first inequality used the fact that

\langle\lambda^{\prime},\pi\cdot(u-v_{\lambda}-v_{J}+\rho)\rangle\geqslant\langle\lambda^{\prime},-v_{\lambda^{\prime}}+\rho_{\lambda^{\prime}}\rangle
,
because
\langle\lambda^{\prime},-\rangle

attains its minimum at
-v_{\lambda^{\prime}}+\rho_{\lambda^{\prime}}

in
\nabla_{V}
,
and
u-v_{\lambda}-v_{J}+\rho\in\nabla_{V}
,
since
u+\rho-\rho_{\lambda}\in\nabla_{\smash{V^{\lambda}}}

and

-(v_{\lambda}-\rho_{\lambda})-v_{J}\in\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\smash{v\in\mathrm{wt}^{\lambda\neq 0}(V)}}{}[0,v]
,
which follows from the quasi-symmetry of
V
.

It remains to show that the equal sign cannot be achieved.
Indeed, the second inequality being an equality implies that

\pi\cdot q(\lambda)=q(\lambda^{\prime})
,
so that
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
are conjugate,
and hence equal since they are anti-dominant,
which is a contradiction.

2.3.3
Full faithfulness

We now show that

\star_{\lambda}\colon\mathsf{W}_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}(\delta_{\lambda})\to\mathsf{Perf}(V/G)

is fully faithful.
Again, we may check this on generators,
and it is enough to show that

\mathrm{Hom}_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}(\Gamma_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}(w),\Gamma_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}(w^{\prime}))\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{V/G}({\star}_{\lambda}\,\Gamma_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}(w),{\star}_{\lambda}\,\Gamma_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}(w^{\prime}))

(2.3.3.1)

for all
w,w^{\prime}\in\nabla_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}+\delta_{\lambda}

that are dominant integral weights.
This follows from

[
80
, Propositions 3.2.1 and 3.5.1]
,
where the condition

[
80
, Definition 3.1.1 (5)]

follows from the estimate

Eq.

2.3.2.3

with
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
and
w=w^{\prime}
,
together with the fact that the equal sign cannot be achieved
when
J\neq\varnothing
.
To see this, suppose the contrary.
Then we must have
\pi\cdot\lambda=\lambda

by the argument below
Eq.

2.3.2.3
,
so that
\pi\cdot v_{\lambda}=v_{\lambda}

and
\pi\cdot\rho_{\lambda}=\rho_{\lambda}
.
The first inequality in
Eq.

2.3.2.3

being an equality now implies that

\langle\lambda,\pi\cdot(u-v_{J}+\rho)-\rho_{\lambda}\rangle=0
,
so that

\langle\lambda,u-v_{J}+\rho-\rho_{\lambda}\rangle=0
,
and hence

\langle\lambda,v_{J}\rangle=0
,
so that
J=\varnothing
.

2.3.4
Generation

Finally, we show that the terms in

Eq.

2.1.6.1

generate

\mathsf{Perf}(V/G)
.
For this, let
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathsf{Perf}(V/G)

be the subcategory generated by the terms in

Eq.

2.1.6.1
.
Since
\mathsf{Perf}(V/G)

is generated by the vector bundles

\Gamma_{V/G}(w)

for dominant weights
w
,
it is enough to show that

\Gamma_{V/G}(w)\in\mathcal{D}

for all such
w
.

Recall the partition
Eq.

2.1.6.3

of
\Lambda^{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.
By induction on
|\lambda|_{q}
,
we show that if

w\in\nabla_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}+\delta_{\lambda}

is a dominant integral weight,
then
\Gamma_{V/G}(w)\in\mathcal{D}
.
The induction is possible since those
\lambda

such that
\nabla_{\smash{V^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}}}+\delta_{\lambda}

contains an integral weight are well-ordered under the
q
-norm.

When
\lambda=0
, the claim holds by the definition of

\mathsf{W}_{V/G}(\delta)
.
For general
\lambda
, let
w
be as above.
By
Eq.

2.3.1.1
,
it is enough to show that

\Gamma_{V/G}\bigl((w-v_{J})^{+}\bigr)\in\mathcal{D}

for all
\varnothing\neq J\subset\mathrm{wt}^{\lambda<0}(V)

such that
(w-v_{J})^{+}
is defined.
For such
J
, let
\lambda^{\prime}
be an anti-dominant rational cocharacter such that

(w-v_{J})^{+}\in\nabla_{\smash{V^{\lambda^{\prime}}/G^{\lambda^{\prime}}}}+\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}}\ ,

and it is enough to show that

|\lambda^{\prime}|_{q}<|\lambda|_{q}
.
Suppose for a contradiction that
|\lambda^{\prime}|_{q}\geqslant|\lambda|_{q}
.
Then, repeating the calculation
Eq.

2.3.2.3

with
w^{\prime}=(w-v_{J})^{+}
,
we see that the equal sign is attained,
and the same computation as in the last part of
“S

2.3.3

shows that
J=\varnothing
.

This completes the proof of
Theorem

2.1.6
.
∎

2.4   Proof of the decomposition in one example

2.4.1
Preliminaries

For the convenience of the reader, we discuss the proof of
Theorem

2.1.6
in one example,
which already contains most of the ideas present in the proof for the general case.
Denote by
\mathbb{C}(w)
the one dimensional representation of

T=\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
of weight
w\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Consider the
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-representation
V=\mathbb{C}(1)^{\oplus n}
.
We reprove the semiorthogonal decompositions from
Theorem

2.1.6
for the stack

\mathcal{X}=(V\oplus V^{\vee})/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\ .

Let
\Lambda^{T}=\mathbb{Z}\cdot\beta
,
where
\beta
is the tautological character of
T
,
and so
\Lambda^{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Q}\cdot\beta
.
The Weyl group is trivial,
and the semiorthogonal decomposition is independent of the choice of the quadratic form
q
.
Let
\delta\in\Lambda^{T}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
,
which we identify with a rational number
\delta\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Consider the fixed and attracting stacks:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}=V/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Z}=0/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=V^{\vee}/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\hbox to0.0pt{ .\hss}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q^{\prime}}

The Hall induction functors are

\displaystyle\star

\displaystyle=p_{*}\,q^{*}\colon

\displaystyle\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{Z})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{X})\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\star^{\prime}

\displaystyle=p^{\prime}_{*}\,q^{\prime*}\colon

\displaystyle\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{Z})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{X})\ .

The window subcategory

\mathsf{W}(\delta)=\mathsf{W}_{\mathcal{X}}(\delta)\subset\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{X})

is the full subcategory generated by the equivariant line bundles

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(w)
for
\delta-n/2\leqslant w\leqslant\delta+n/2
.

2.4.2
The decomposition

The subcategory
\mathsf{Perf}(0/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})_{w}

is generated by the representation
\mathbb{C}(w)
,
and we write
\langle\mathbb{C}(w)\rangle=\mathsf{Perf}(0/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})_{w}
.
We now show directly that is a semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{X})=\Bigl<{\star}\,\langle\mathbb{C}(a)\rangle,{\star^{\prime}}\,\langle\mathbb{C}(b)\rangle,\mathsf{W}(\delta)\Bigm|a<\delta-n/2\text{ and }b>\delta+n/2\Bigr>\ ,

(2.4.2.1)

where the order is as follows between the summands coming from Hall induction:

•

\text{Hom}(\star\,\mathbb{C}(a),\star\,\mathbb{C}(a^{\prime}))\simeq 0\text{ for }a^{\prime}<a
,

•

\text{Hom}(\star^{\prime}\,\mathbb{C}(b),\star^{\prime}\,\mathbb{C}(b^{\prime}))\simeq 0\text{ for }b^{\prime}>b
,

•

The complexes
\star\,\mathbb{C}(a)
and
\star^{\prime}\,\mathbb{C}(b)
are mutually orthogonal for
a
and
b
as above.

Further, the functors
\star\colon\langle\mathbb{C}(a)\rangle\to\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
and
\star^{\prime}\colon\langle\mathbb{C}(b)\rangle\to\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
are fully faithful.

2.4.3
Generation

The category
\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
is generated by the line bundles
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(v)
for
v\in\mathbb{Z}
, so it suffices to show that these are generated by the summands of
Eq.

2.4.2.1
.

Consider the Koszul resolution for the structure sheaf of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{X}
,
which is a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-equivariant version of the Koszul resolution for the affine subspace
V^{\vee}\subset V\oplus V^{\vee}
:

\left[\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(-n)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(-n+1)^{\oplus n}\to\ldots\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(-1)^{\oplus n}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\right]\mathrel{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\ .

(2.4.3.1)

There is thus a resolution:

\left[\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(b-n)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(b-n+1)^{\oplus n}\to\ldots\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(b-1)^{\oplus n}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(b)\right]\mathrel{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\longrightarrow}}\star^{\prime}\,\mathbb{C}(b)\ .

(2.4.3.2)

There is a similar resolution for
\star\,\mathbb{C}(a)

obtained from the Koszul resolution for the structure sheaf of

\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{X}
:

\left[\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(a+n)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(a+n-1)^{\oplus n}\to\ldots\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(a+1)^{\oplus n}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(s)\right]\mathrel{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\longrightarrow}}\star\,\mathbb{C}(a)\ .

(2.4.3.3)

Any line bundles
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(v)
for
v\in\mathbb{Z}
may be obtained, via distinguished triangles, from:

•

the line bundles
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(w)
for
\delta-n/2\leqslant w\leqslant\delta+n/2
,

•

the complexes
Eq.

2.4.3.2
for
b>\delta+n/2
, and

•

the complexes
Eq.

2.4.3.3
for
a<\delta-n/2
,

and so the generation part of the semiorthogonal decomposition follows.

2.4.4
Vanishings involving the window subcategory

In the rest of this subsection, we fix

b>\delta+n/2
,
a<\delta-n/2
, and

\delta-n/2\leqslant w\leqslant\delta+n/2
.

We now discuss vanishing of morphisms involving the window subcategory.
We have

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(w),p_{*}\,q^{*}\,\mathbb{C}(a))\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}(w),\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}(a))\simeq\bigl(\mathrm{Sym}(V^{\vee})(a-w)\bigr)^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}\simeq 0\ ,

because
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
acts with negative weights on
V^{\vee}
.
Similarly, we get that

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(w),p^{\prime}_{*}\,q^{\prime*}\,\mathbb{C}(b))\simeq\bigl(\mathrm{Sym}(V)(b-w)\bigr)^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}\simeq 0\ .

2.4.5
Vanishings for the same attracting locus

Let
a^{\prime}<a
. We show that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}}(\star\,\mathbb{C}(a),\star\,\mathbb{C}(a^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}}(p_{*}\,q^{*}\,\mathbb{C}(a),p_{*}\,q^{*}\,\mathbb{C}(a^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{S}}(p^{*}\,p_{*}\,q^{*}\,\mathbb{C}(a),q^{*}\,\mathbb{C}(a^{\prime}))\simeq 0\ .

The analogous statement for
{\star^{\prime}}=p^{\prime}_{*}\,q^{\prime*}
follows similarly.
The complex
p^{*}\,p_{*}\,q^{*}\,\mathbb{C}(a)

has a resolution by locally free sheaves that are direct sums of

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}(a+i)
for
0\leqslant i\leqslant n
.
The desired vanishing follows from

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}(a+i),\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}(a^{\prime}))\simeq\bigl(\mathrm{Sym}(V^{\vee})(a^{\prime}-a-i)\bigr)^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}\simeq 0\ .

Note that the above argument also shows the isomorphism required in the full faithfulness of

\star\colon\langle\mathbb{C}(a)\rangle\to\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{X})
, namely that

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}}(\star\,\mathbb{C}(a),\star\,\mathbb{C}(a))\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{S}}(q^{*}\,\mathbb{C}(a),q^{*}\,\mathbb{C}(a))\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\mathbb{C}(a),\mathbb{C}(a))\ .

Indeed, the natural map
p^{*}\,p_{*}\,q^{*}\,\mathbb{C}(a)\to q^{*}\,\mathbb{C}(a)
has cone generated by the locally free sheaves
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}(a+i)
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
.

2.4.6
Vanishings for different attracting loci

The vanishing

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}}(\star\,\mathbb{C}(a),\star^{\prime}\,\mathbb{C}(b))\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}}(\star^{\prime}\,\mathbb{C}(b),\star\,\mathbb{C}(a))\simeq 0

can be shown by considering the natural sections

s\colon\mathcal{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}
and

s^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

of
q
and
q^{\prime}
, respectively,
then using proper base change for the cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}=V/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Z}=0/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=V^{\vee}/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\hbox to0.0pt{ ,\hss}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{\prime}}

and using standard functoriality, including the upper shriek functor.
We leave the details to the reader.

Remark 2.4.7

We note that for a more general stack

\mathcal{X}=V/G
with

quasi-symmetric, the argument above becomes combinatorially more involved due to the potentially higher-dimensional lattice

Semiorthogonal decompositions via GIT

For the convenience of the reader,

using the choice of a stability condition.
There are two such semiorthogonal decompositions,
corresponding to the two possible chambers of non-trivial linearizations

on

\displaystyle\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{X})

\displaystyle=\Bigl<{\star}\,\langle\mathbb{C}(a)\rangle,\mathsf{W}(\delta),{\star}\,\langle\mathbb{C}(a^{\prime})\rangle\Bigm|a<\delta-n/2<a^{\prime}\Bigr>\ ,

3 The smooth case

The goal of this section is to globalize the construction in

3.1   The component lattice

To globalize the construction in

Theorem

2.1.6

from linear quotient stacks to general smooth stacks,
we use the formalism of component lattices from

[
17
]
.
Here, we recall their definition and basic properties
for general stacks, not necessarily smooth.
For more details, see
[
17
]
.

3.1.1
The component lattice

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a derived algebraic stack over
\mathbb{C}
.
The
component lattice
of
\mathcal{X}
is defined as the functor

\displaystyle\mathrm{CL}(\mathcal{X})\colon\mathsf{Lat}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathsf{Set}\ ,

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle\longmapsto\uppi_{0}\,{\mathcal{M}\mkern-3.0mu\mathit{ap}\mkern 1.0mu}(*/T_{\Lambda},\mathcal{X})\ ,

(3.1.1.1)

where
\mathsf{Lat}(\mathbb{Z})

is the category of finitely generated free
\mathbb{Z}
-modules,

\uppi_{0}
denotes taking the set of connected components,

{\mathcal{M}\mkern-3.0mu\mathit{ap}\mkern 1.0mu}(-,-)
denotes the derived mapping stack,
and
T_{\Lambda}
is the algebraic torus
whose cocharacter lattice is
\Lambda
.
The component lattice
\mathrm{CL}(\mathcal{X})

only depends on the classical truncation
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{cl}}

of
\mathcal{X}
.

Such a functor is called a
formal lattice
,
and thought of as an object glued from copies of

\mathbb{Z}^{n}
(for possibly different
n
)
along arbitrary
\mathbb{Z}
-linear maps.
Under very mild conditions on
\mathcal{X}
,
as in
[
17
, Theorem 6.2.8]
,

\mathrm{CL}(\mathcal{X})

can be glued from finitely many copies of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}

along finitely many maps.

The
underlying set
of
\mathrm{CL}(\mathcal{X})

is defined to be

|\mathrm{CL}(\mathcal{X})|=\mathrm{CL}(\mathcal{X})(\mathbb{Z})=\uppi_{0}\,{\mathcal{M}\mkern-3.0mu\mathit{ap}\mkern 1.0mu}(*/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},\mathcal{X})\ .

(3.1.1.2)

Morphisms of formal lattices

\alpha\colon\Lambda\to\mathrm{CL}(\mathcal{X})

for
\Lambda\in\mathsf{Lat}(\mathbb{Z})

are in bijection with elements of

\mathrm{CL}(\mathcal{X})(\Lambda)
,
and are called
faces
.
Such a face is
non-degenerate

if it does not factor through a lattice

\Lambda^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Lat}(\mathbb{Z})

of lower rank than
\Lambda
.

There is also a rational version,

\mathrm{CL}{Q}(\mathcal{X})=\mathrm{CL}(\mathcal{X})\otimes\mathbb{Q}\colon\mathsf{Lat}(\mathbb{Q})^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Set}\ ,

where
\mathsf{Lat}(\mathbb{Q})

is the category of finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces,
with its underlying set

|\mathrm{CL}{Q}(\mathcal{X})|=\mathrm{CL}{Q}(\mathcal{X})(\mathbb{Q})
.

Similarly, morphisms of formal lattices

\alpha\colon F\to\mathrm{CL}{Q}(\mathcal{X})

for
F\in\mathsf{Lat}(\mathbb{Q})

are called
faces
,
and a face is
non-degenerate

if it does not factor through a vector space of lower dimension.

Example 3.1.2
(Linear quotients)

In the setting of
“S

2.1.1

3.1.4

Let
\mathcal{X}

3.1.6
The central rank

When proving properties of stacks related to the component lattice,
a useful technique is to use induction on the
central rank
of the stack,
based on the idea that the stack
\mathcal{X}_{\lambda}

is always ‘simpler’ than
\mathcal{X}
measured by the central rank,
except when it is the same as
\mathcal{X}
,
and the induction allows us to
we always assume that the property holds for all
\mathcal{X}_{\lambda}

that are different from
\mathcal{X}
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a stack as in
“S

3.1.4
,
and assume that
\mathcal{X}
is connected.

An element
\lambda\in|\mathrm{CL}{Q}(\mathcal{X})|

is said to be
central
, if the forgetful morphism

\mathrm{tot}_{\lambda}\colon\mathcal{X}_{\lambda}\to\mathcal{X}

is an isomorphism.
By

[
17
, Theorem 4.1.5]
,
the locus of central elements in

|\mathrm{CL}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{X})|

is the injective image of the
maximal central face

\alpha_{0}\colon F_{0}\to\mathrm{CL}{Q}(\mathcal{X})
.
There is also an integral maximal central face

\Lambda_{0}\to\mathrm{CL}(\mathcal{X})
,
which can be similarly described.

The
central rank
of
\mathcal{X}
,
denoted
\mathrm{crk}(\mathcal{X})
,
is defined as the dimension of the maximal central face
\alpha_{0}
.
Also, when
\mathcal{X}
is quasi-compact,
the
rank
of
\mathcal{X}
,
denoted
\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{X})
,
is defined as the maximal dimension of a non-degenerate face of

\mathrm{CL}{Q}(\mathcal{X})
.

It follows from

[
17
, Theorem 4.1.4]

that for all
\lambda\in|\mathrm{CL}{Q}(\mathcal{X})|
,
we have

\mathrm{crk}(\mathcal{X})\leqslant\mathrm{crk}(\mathcal{X}_{\lambda})\leqslant\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{X}_{\lambda})\leqslant\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{X})\ ,

(3.1.6.1)

and the first inequality is strict unless
\lambda
is central.
Therefore, if we use the invariant

\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{X})-\mathrm{crk}(\mathcal{X})

to measure the complexity of
\mathcal{X}
,
then
\mathcal{X}_{\lambda}
is either strictly simpler than
\mathcal{X}

(when
\lambda
is non-central),
or isomorphic to
\mathcal{X}

(when
\lambda
is central).

Let
\mathcal{X}

“S

Example 3.1.10

We give an example of a quadratic norm

on

when
\mathcal{X}
is a

[
17

carries the structure of a commutative monoid

,

3.5   For matrix factorizations

We now show a variant of

3.2.3
,
which gives semiorthogonal decompositions
for categories of

on smooth stacks. The main results are

and

The purpose is two-fold:
First, these categories and their semiorthogonal summands
describe the categorical Donaldson–Thomas theory
for the derived critical locus of a function on a smooth stack,
which is a local model for

Second, the results obtained here will be later used via Koszul duality

{\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{P^{\vee}}\to{\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{L^{\vee}}
,
where
{\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{L^{\vee}}(\chi)^{\mathrm{ss}}
is the union of the components

{\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{L^{\vee}}(\chi^{\prime})^{\mathrm{ss}}
for
\chi^{\prime}\in\uppi_{1}(L^{\vee})

whose image in
\uppi_{1}(L^{\vee})^{\mathrm{free}}
is equal to
\chi
.

Suppose that
g(C)\leqslant 1
.
Then by
[
28
, Theorem 9.1.2]
,
the stratification
Eq.

4.4.8.3
defines a sequence of open substacks of
{\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{G^{\vee}}

that are co-truncative in the sense of
[
28
]
.
Consequently, by
[
28
, Proposition 3.1.2]
and Kashiwara’s
lemma
[
36
, Proposition 2.5.6]
,
we obtain the semiorthogonal decomposition

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}({\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{G^{\vee}})=\Bigl<\mathsf{D}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}({\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{P^{\vee}}(\chi)^{\mathrm{ss}})\Bigm|\chi\in\uppi_{1}(L^{\vee})^{\mathrm{free}}_{+}\Bigr>\ .

(4.4.8.4)

We have an equivalence by
[
26
, Lemma 10.3.6]

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}({\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{L^{\vee}}(\chi)^{\mathrm{ss}})\mathrel{\overset{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\sim$}}\mspace{3mu}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathsf{D}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}({\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{P^{\vee}}(\chi)^{\mathrm{ss}})

since
{\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{P^{\vee}}(\chi)^{\mathrm{ss}}\to{\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{L^{\vee}}(\chi)^{\mathrm{ss}}
is a gerbe of some semidirect product of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
’s by the condition
g(C)\leqslant 1
.
Moreover, the semiorthogonal decomposition
Eq.

4.4.8.4
preserves compact objects, therefore we have the semiorthogonal decomposition

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}({\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{G^{\vee}})^{\mathrm{cp}}=\Bigl<\mathsf{D}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}({\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{L^{\vee}}(\chi)^{\mathrm{ss}})^{\mathrm{cp}}\Bigm|\chi\in\uppi_{1}(L^{\vee})^{\mathrm{free}}_{+}\Bigr>\ .

(4.4.8.5)

Using the compatibility of the equivalence
Eq.

4.4.8.1
with Eisenstein functors

]
,

4.4.4.2

4.4.8.5

Eq.

4.4.8.2
.
In particular, we obtain an equivalence

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}_{{\mathcal{C}\mkern-3.0mu\mathit{onn}\mkern 1.0mu}_{G},\,\mathcal{N}}(-w)\simeq\mathsf{D}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}({\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{G^{\vee}}(w)^{\mathrm{ss}})^{\mathrm{cp}}\ .

g(C)
, we expect the following.

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{micro}}(\mathcal{U})=\mathsf{D}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}({\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{G^{\vee}}){\hbox{$\left/\vbox to10.0pt{}\right.$}}\mathsf{D}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}({\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{G^{\vee}})_{\mathcal{Z}}\ .

]

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}({\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{G^{\vee}})=\Bigl<\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{micro}}({\mathcal{H}\mkern-4.0mu\mathit{iggs}\mkern 1.0mu}_{L^{\vee}}(\chi)^{\mathrm{ss}})\Bigm|\chi\in\uppi_{1}(L^{\vee})^{\mathrm{free}}_{+}\Bigr>\ ,

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}({\mathcal{B}\mkern-2.0mu\mathit{un}\mkern 1.0mu}_{G^{\vee}})^{\mathrm{cp}}=\Bigl<\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{micro}}({\mathcal{H}\mkern-4.0mu\mathit{iggs}\mkern 1.0mu}_{L^{\vee}}(\chi)^{\mathrm{ss}})^{\mathrm{cp}}\Bigm|\chi\in\uppi_{1}(L^{\vee})^{\mathrm{free}}_{+}\Bigr>\ .
