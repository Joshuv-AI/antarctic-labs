Chromosome-scale Genome Assembly and Annotation of Anise Hyssop (Agastache foeniculum)
Authors: Yeonjun Sung, Donghyun Jeon, Changsoo Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We present a chromosome-scale genome assembly and annotation of anise hyssop (Agastache foeniculum), an aromatic perennial herb widely used for medicinal, horticultural, and ornamental purposes. The genome was assembled using PacBio HiFi long-read sequencing, Illumina short-read sequencing, and Omni-C proximity ligation data, with gene annotation supported by RNA-seq data from leaf tissue. The final assembly spans 482.39 Mb, of which 434.47 Mb (90.06%) were anchored into nine chromosome-scale pseudomolecules. Structural annotation identified 28,193 protein-coding genes. Genome completeness was assessed using BUSCO, yielding scores of 98.0% (embryophyta_odb10, genome mode) and 95.5% (protein mode). This chromosome-scale genome assembly provides a foundational genomic resource for comparative and functional genomics within the genus Agastache and the Lamiaceae family.

Background & Summary

Agastache foeniculum (Pursh) Kuntze (Lamiaceae), commonly known as anise hyssop (giant blue hyssop), is an aromatic perennial herb cultivated for medicinal, culinary, and ornamental purposes. Species within the genus Agastache are well known for producing a diverse array of specialized metabolites, with phytochemical profiles frequently dominated by phenylpropanoids and terpenoids, which has driven sustained interest in their bioactivity and potential applications in natural product research1,2. In addition, A. foeniculum is widely cultivated for its strong floral fragrance and prolonged flowering period, and its pronounced attractiveness to pollinators has made it a species of interest in ecological and horticultural studies3.

Although genomic resources within the family Lamiaceae have expanded in recent years, high-quality chromosome-scale genome assemblies remain unevenly distributed across many lineages of applied and biological interest, constraining comparative genomic analyses and investigations into the genetic basis of traits such as specialized metabolism, environmental adaptation, and horticultural performance. In this context, A. foeniculum is a particularly suitable target for chromosome-scale genomics: it is a diploid species with a well-defined sporophytic chromosome number (2n = 2x = 18)4 and low heterozygosity (0.218%), features that facilitate haplotype-resolved assembly, and its moderately sized genome (<500 Mb) is well suited to high-coverage PacBio HiFi sequencing. Moreover, although chromosome-scale assemblies in Lamiaceae are concentrated in a few genera such as Salvia, Mentha, and Perilla, the genus Agastache — economically and chemically distinct within subfamily Nepetoideae — has lacked a chromosome-scale reference until now. To address this gap, we generated a chromosome-scale genome assembly and annotation for A. foeniculum using PacBio long-read sequencing, Illumina short-read data, Omni-C proximity-ligation scaffolding, and RNA-seq evidence.

The final curated assembly totals 482.39 Mb and anchors 434.47 Mb (90.06%) into nine pseudomolecules (chr1–chr9), consistent with the expected base chromosome number (x = 9). The assembly is highly contiguous, with a scaffold N50 of 44.49 Mb and a contig N50 of 43.79 Mb, individual pseudomolecules ranging from 33.93 to 63.33 Mb, a GC content of 37.26%, and a near-gapless sequence (only ~100 bp of ambiguous bases). This assembly size is consistent with previous cytological estimates for this species (≈485–520 Mb) but larger than the k-mer–based estimate (≈346.8 Mb), the latter reflecting the collapse of high-copy repetitive k-mers in repeat-rich genomes. The remaining 47.92 Mb comprises 385 unplaced scaffolds that are predominantly short (median length 64.16 kb; 90th percentile 170.56 kb) and highly repetitive. The transposable elements and repetitive content of the unplaced fraction (84.21%) was substantially higher than in the anchored pseudomolecules (63.85%), indicating enrichment of repeat-rich, low-uniqueness sequences among regions that are difficult to anchor unambiguously.

Structural annotation identified 28,193 protein-coding genes. Of these, 26,172 gene models (92.83%) and 96.52% of the total predicted coding sequence length (26.09 Mb) are located on the anchored pseudomolecules, indicating that the vast majority of coding potential is captured within the chromosome-scale assembly. Genome completeness assessment using BUSCO (embryophyta_odb10) recovered 98.0% complete BUSCOs in genome mode (C: 98.0% [S: 91.3%, D: 6.7%]) and 95.5% complete BUSCOs in protein mode for the predicted gene set (C: 95.5% [S: 88.6%, D: 6.9%]), supporting the high completeness of both the assembly and annotation. Together, this dataset provides a foundational genomic resource for Agastache and will facilitate comparative, functional, and evolutionary studies within Lamiaceae, including investigations of specialized metabolism and trait-associated genetic variation.

Methods

Plant material and sampling

Seeds of Agastache foeniculum (Pursh) Kuntze were obtained from “Jekas” (51.584369° N, 2.512253° W). The seed lot was maintained at the Plant Computational Genomics Laboratory, College of Agriculture and Life Sciences, Chungnam National University, where seeds were germinated and cultivated in plastic pots filled with a commercial potting mix in a glass greenhouse under natural light. When seedlings reached the 3–4 true-leaf stage, fresh young leaves were harvested, immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C until downstream genome sequencing and assembly.

DNA extraction and quality assessment

High-molecular-weight (HMW) genomic DNA was extracted from frozen young leaf tissue using a CTAB-based protocol with minor modifications for long-read sequencing5. Genomic DNA for Illumina short-read sequencing was extracted from the frozen young leaves using the Plant DNA(III) extraction kit (SmartGene, Korea). DNA concentration and purity were evaluated using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer and a Qubit fluorometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), and DNA integrity was assessed using a TapeStation system prior to library preparation.

For Illumina short-read sequencing, paired-end libraries were prepared using the TruSeq Nano DNA Kit and sequenced by Macrogen (Seoul, Korea) with a read length of 151 bp. Raw paired-end reads were generated and inspected using seqkit v2.3.0 (stats -a)6, yielding 199,860,829 reads per mate (i.e., 199,860,829 read pairs) and 30,178,985,179 bp per file (~30.18 Gbp), totaling 60,357,970,358 bp (~60.36 Gbp; Table 1). The raw data showed Q30 rates of 96.94% for Read 1 and 96.17% for Read 2, with GC contents of 37.55% and 37.81%, respectively. For downstream analyses, reads were additionally processed using fastp v0.23.47 to remove adapter sequences and low-quality bases.

Omni-C library preparation and sequencing

An Omni-C proximity-ligation library was prepared using the Dovetail Omni-C Kit (v2.0) according to the manufacturer’s protocol for plant tissues. The Omni-C library was sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq X Plus (2 × 151 bp). BCL files were converted to FASTQ format and demultiplexed using BCL Convert v4.4.4 (Illumina). The sequencing yielded 82,311,887,840 bases (545,111,840 reads) for sample AF (index: TTCCAAGG–CCTTGTAG), with 92.32% of bases at Q30 or higher (mean quality score 38.21). FASTQ-level summary metrics computed from the raw paired files using seqkit (stats -a)6 indicated 272,555,920 reads and 41,155,943,920 bp per mate (i.e., 272,555,920 read pairs and 82,311,887,840 bp in total; Table 1). The raw data showed Q30 rates of 94.17% for Read 1 and 90.47% for Read 2, with GC contents of 38.78% and 39.97%, respectively.

RNA library construction, sequencing, and read processing

For transcriptome sequencing, RNA-seq libraries were prepared from young leaf tissue with three biological replicates and sequenced by Macrogen (Seoul, Korea) in paired-end mode (2 × 151 bp). Demultiplexed FASTQ files were summarized using seqkit (stats -a)6, yielding 24,815,697–26,918,378 reads per mate and 3,747,170,247–4,064,675,078 bp per mate across replicates, totaling 23,632,682,028 bases (~23.63 Gbp; Table 1). The raw data showed Q30 rates of 94.7–95.1%, with GC contents of 48.3–48.8%. For downstream analyses, reads were additionally processed using fastp v0.23.47 to remove adapter sequences and low-quality bases.

Genome size estimation

Genome size and characteristics were estimated from Illumina short-read data using a k-mer–based approach. Canonical k-mers (k = 21) were counted with Jellyfish v2.3.18 to generate a k-mer frequency histogram, which was subsequently modeled with GenomeScope29. The fitted GenomeScope2 model estimated a haploid genome length of 346,824,605 bp (≈346.8 Mb). Model-derived parameters indicated that 56.1% of the genome corresponded to unique sequence, with an estimated heterozygosity of 0.218% and a sequencing error rate of 0.159%. The model also suggested a duplication level of 0.81 and a main k-mer coverage (kcov) of 43.1. The k-mer profile was best explained by a diploid GenomeScope2 model (p = 2), showing a dominant peak and a minor lower-coverage component consistent with heterozygous k-mers (Fig. 1). This k-mer–based estimate (≈346.8 Mb) is smaller than both the final assembly size (482.39 Mb) and previous estimates for this species (≈485 Mb, Kew Plant DNA C-values database10; ≈520 Mb, Talebi et al.11). Such underestimation is expected for repeat-rich genomes, as high-copy k-mers from repetitive sequences (67.65% of the assembly) are collapsed in the GenomeScope2 model12. Genome assembly, and Chromosome-scale genome scaffolding

PacBio HiFi reads were assembled de novo using hifiasm v0.25.0-r72613 to generate a primary contig assembly along with phased haplotype-resolved contigs. To prevent organellar sequences from confounding downstream nuclear-genome scaffolding and evaluation, assembled contigs were screened for chloroplast- and mitochondrial-derived sequences using HiMT v1.1.114. Reference sequences included in HiMT were used to identify candidate organelle-associated contigs by sequence similarity searches against the contig assembly. Contigs classified as chloroplast or mitochondrial contaminants were excluded, yielding a filtered set of nuclear contigs.

To achieve a chromosome-scale genome, Omni-C paired-end reads were mapped to the filtered nuclear contig assembly using bwa-mem2 v2.2.115 with the −5 and -SP options. Alignments were filtered to retain confident proximity contacts (MAPQ ≥ 1 and NM ≤ 3) and formatted as BAM for downstream analysis. Proximity-guided scaffolding was conducted with HapHiC v1.0.716 in diploid mode, with the expected haploid chromosome number set to nine (nchrs = 9). The HapHiC pipeline was run with–correct_nrounds 2,–max_inflation 5, and parallel execution (–threads 80–processes 80). Resulting contact maps and scaffold structures were inspected in Juicebox17, and manual edits were applied where interaction patterns indicated potential mis-joins or inconsistencies in contig order/orientation. Transposable elements and repetitive sequences annotation

A de novo, species-specific repeat library was generated from the curated A. foeniculum genome assembly using RepeatModeler v2.0.718 under default settings, leveraging the configured dependencies (including TRF v4.1019 and RepeatScout v1.0.720). To improve the resolution of LTR retrotransposons, the repeat library was further refined by combining the RepeatModeler-derived library with a high-quality intact LTR library. Intact LTR retrotransposons were identified de novo with LTR_FINDER_parallel and gt ltrharvest and filtered with LTR_retriever v3.0.6, and the resulting non-redundant LTR library was merged with the RepeatModeler library using CD-HIT-EST to build a unified, non-redundant repeat library. This unified library was then used for repeat identification and masking with RepeatMasker v4.2.221 in default mode. In total, 67.65% of the assembly was masked as repetitive, dominated by LTR retrotransposons (34.55%; Copia 12.92% and Gypsy 14.49%), followed by a substantial proportion of unclassified repeats (23.26%); the remaining classes each accounted for less than 5% of the genome (Table 4). Gene prediction and annotation

Gene models were generated by integrating ab initio, transcript-based and homology-based evidence. Ab initio predictions were produced with AUGUSTUS v3.5.023, and RNA-seq–based gene structures were inferred with BRAKER v3.0.824. Homology-based prediction was performed using GeMoMaPipeline v1.925 on the repeat-masked assembly, leveraging six reference genomes and annotations retrieved from National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) Assembly: Arabidopsis thaliana (GCF_000001735.4), Erythranthe guttata (GCA_000504015.1), Phlomoides rotata (GCA_051938595.1), Salvia miltiorrhiza (GCF_028751815.1), Salvia splendens (GCF_004379255.1), and Salvia hispanica (GCF_023119035.1). GeMoMa was executed with default pipeline behavior without RNA-seq evidence within GeMoMa (RNA-seq mode = NO; tblastn = false). All evidence tracks were subsequently merged into a consensus gene set using EvidenceModeler (EVM) v2.1.026.

The final structural annotation contained 28,193 protein-coding genes and an equal number of transcript models (mRNA = 28,193), with 127,988 CDS features in total, corresponding to an average of 4.54 CDS segments per transcript. The predicted proteome comprised 28,193 proteins totaling 11,521,694 amino acids (mean length 408.7 aa; maximum 5,361 aa). The genomic and transcriptomic sequencing data for Agastache foeniculum are available in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) under BioProject PRJNA140491035. PacBio HiFi long reads are available under NCBI SRA experiment SRX31855442 (run SRR36883717)36. The Illumina whole-genome shotgun (WGS) dataset was deposited in the NCBI SRA as SRR3688379237, and the Illumina RNA-seq dataset was deposited in the NCBI SRA as SRR3688402338. The chromosome-scale genome assembly has been submitted to the NCBI Assembly/GenBank databases and was publicly released under an assembly accession (GCA_ 054883455.1)40. Genome annotation files and supporting resources are available from Figshare41.

Technical Validation

Omni-C–guided scaffolding anchored 434.47 Mb (90.06%) of the 482.39 Mb assembly into nine chromosome-scale pseudomolecules. The remaining 47.92 Mb across 385 unplaced scaffolds is dominated by short sequences (median 64.16 kb), which commonly yield ambiguous long-range linkage patterns and are therefore difficult to place with high confidence. Transposable elements and repetitive sequence content assessed by RepeatMasker was substantially higher in unplaced scaffolds (84.21%) than in anchored pseudomolecules (63.85%), indicating enrichment of repeat-derived, low-uniqueness sequence in the unplaced fraction (Supplementary Table 1).

To evaluate representation of the gene space, we compared the distribution of predicted genes and coding sequence between anchored and unplaced scaffolds. The anchored pseudomolecules contained 92.83% of predicted genes and 96.52% of total CDS length, and exhibited a higher CDS density (60.05 kb/Mb) than the unplaced fraction (19.62 kb/Mb) (Supplementary Table 1).

Read-based validation further supported assembly accuracy. Illumina paired-end reads aligned to the assembly using Bowtie v2.5.442 showed an overall alignment rate of 98.04% (Supplementary Table 1). Genome completeness was evaluated using BUSCO v6.0.043 with the embryophyta_odb10 dataset (n = 1,614). In genome mode (euk_genome_min with miniprot v0.18-r28144), BUSCO recovered 98.0% complete BUSCOs (91.3% single-copy and 6.7% duplicated), with 0.1% fragmented and 1.9% missing BUSCOs. In protein mode, the predicted proteome recovered 95.5% complete BUSCOs (88.6% single-copy and 6.9% duplicated), with 1.4% fragmented and 3.0% missing BUSCOs (Fig. 5).Fig. 5BUSCO assessment of assembly and annotation completeness.

BUSCO assessment of assembly and annotation completeness.

Beyond gene-space completeness, three complementary reference-free metrics were used to assess the base-level and structural quality of the assembly. Structural accuracy assessed with CRAQ47 yielded a regional assembly quality index (R-AQI) of 92.31 and a structural assembly quality index (S-AQI) of 93.44 (overall AQI = 92.87).
