Exploiting Out-of-Band Information for Millimeter-Wave MIMO Channel Estimation: Performance in Static and Dynamic Scenarios
Faruk Pasic 1 , Mariam Mussbah 1 2 , Stefan Schwarz 1 , Markus Rupp 1 and Christoph F. Mecklenbräuker 1

Abstract
To support the high data rates for latency-critical applications, future wireless systems will employ fully digital beamforming MIMO ( MIMO ) architectures at MMW ( MMW ) frequencies. Moreover, MMW MIMO deployments will coexist with conventional sub-6 GHz MIMO systems, creating opportunities to exploit out-of-band sub-6 GHz information to enhance channel estimation at MMW frequencies. In this work, we analyze the pilot-aided channel estimation performance of MMW MIMO systems under various pilot configurations in both static and dynamic environments. We evaluate the system performance in terms of SE ( SE ) for line-of-sight and non-line-of-sight propagation conditions. Simulation results show that incorporating out-of-band sub-6 GHz information yields notable SE gains in both static and dynamic scenarios.

I
Introduction

MMW
communication systems are a promising solution for accommodating the rapidly growing demand for high data rates, enabled by the availability of large bandwidths in the
MMW
spectrum
[
17
,
9
,
21
]
.
To compensate for the severe path loss inherent at these frequencies,
MMW
systems rely on large-scale antenna arrays in conjunction with
MIMO
techniques
[
10
]
.
Fully digital beamforming architectures are essential to fully exploit the spatial multiplexing and beamforming capabilities of
MIMO
systems
[
32
,
27
]
.
Unlike analog and hybrid architectures, digital beamforming enables simultaneous multi-directional channel estimation within a single time interval, thereby significantly reducing link establishment overhead and supporting latency-critical applications
[
15
]
.

A major challenge in the realization of digital
MMW

MIMO
systems is reliable link establishment, which depends on accurate channel estimation
[
10
]
.
At
MMW
frequencies, channel estimation is particularly demanding due to the low pre-beamforming
SNR
(
SNR
).
In contrast, sub-6 GHz systems experience significantly lower propagation losses, resulting in a higher pre-beamforming
SNR

[
11
,
26
]
.
As
MMW
systems are increasingly deployed alongside sub-6 GHz systems
[
28
]
, this reliable out-of-band information can be leveraged to assist and enhance
MMW
channel estimation.
Several recent studies have investigated the use of out-of-band information for
MMW
channel estimation
[
23
,
24
]
, proposing pilot-aided methods that exploit the
LOS
(
LOS
) channel component acquired with the support of the sub-6 GHz band.

The performance of digital
MMW
beamforming systems has been studied in static environments
[
7
,
29
,
4
,
30
]
, as well as in dynamic scenarios
[
14
,
20
,
31
]
. However, existing studies on digital
MMW
beamforming systems that exploit out-of-band information have largely focused on static environments
[
3
,
24
,
22
]
, while dynamic scenarios remain largely unexplored.
Moreover, to the best of the author’s knowledge, the impact of different pilot configurations on the performance of digital
MMW

MIMO
systems in dynamic scenarios has not yet been studied.

Contribution:

In this paper, we evaluate the performance of digital beamforming
MMW

MIMO
systems in both static and dynamic environments.
We consider both conventional channel estimation and out-of-band aided channel estimation as proposed in
[
24
]
.
Our analysis considers various pilot configurations, a wide range of receiver velocities, different
SNR
levels and both
LOS
and
NLOS
(
NLOS
) propagation conditions characterized by the Rician

The performance of the considered methods is assessed through simulations in terms of

II
System Model

We consider a point-to-point multi-band
MIMO
communication system in which sub-6 GHz and
MMW
systems operate simultaneously under far-field propagation conditions.
The transmitter employs
M_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm s}\right)}
antenna elements at sub-6 GHz and
M_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}
antenna elements at
MMW
.
Correspondingly, the receiver is equipped with
M_{\rm Rx}^{\left({\rm s}\right)}
sub-6 GHz and
M_{\rm Rx}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}

MMW
antenna elements.
Both frequency bands utilize
ULA
composed of dipole antennas, which are approximated as isotropic radiators.
The sub-6 GHz and
MMW
arrays are assumed to be spatially co-located and aligned, such that the
LOS
path is characterized by the same
AoD
(
AoD
)
\vartheta
and
AoA
(
AoA
)
\varphi
in both bands
[
16
]
.
The spacing between adjacent antenna elements in each array is chosen as
\Delta d^{\left({\rm b}\right)}=\,
0.5
\,\lambda^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
, where
\lambda^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
denotes the wavelength of the respective system.
We further assume perfect time and frequency synchronization across all transmitting and receiving antennas.
The transmitter and receiver are equipped with one
RF
(
RF
) chain per antenna, enabling fully digital beamforming in both the sub-6 GHz and
MMW
systems.
The system follows a
TDD
(
TDD
) operation, thereby ensuring reciprocal channel responses.

We consider an
OFDM
(
OFDM
) transmission system employing
N^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
subcarriers and
K^{\left({\rm b}\right)}

OFDM
time-symbols.
For each
OFDM
time-symbol, a block of
N^{\left({\rm b}\right)}

QAM
(
QAM
) symbols is assigned to
N^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
distinct subcarriers.
The
OFDM
system converts a broadband frequency-selective channel into narrowband frequency-flat channels with the help of a
DFT
(
DFT
) and application of a cyclic prefix
[
6
]
.
The cyclic prefix length is assumed to exceed the maximum excess delay of the multipath channel, thereby eliminating inter-symbol interference.
Furthermore, inter-carrier interference, such as that caused by Doppler shifts or carrier frequency offsets, is considered negligible
[
19
]
.

II-A

Channel Model

For a given
OFDM
subcarrier
n
and time-symbol
k
, the propagation channel is represented by an
M_{\rm Rx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}\times M_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
dimensional complex-valued channel matrix
\mathbf{H}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}[n,k]
, based on the equivalent complex baseband model of the
OFDM
system.
These matrices characterize the small-scale fading behavior of the wireless channel incorporating the
OFDM
processing at the transmitter and receiver.
In particular,
\mathbf{H}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}[n,k]
corresponds to a sampled version of the underlying
CTF
(
CTF
), following the formulation in
[
5
]
.
In what follows, if the description of a quantity is independent of the subcarrier index
n
and/or the time-symbol index
k
, the respective index is omitted for notational simplicity.

We adopt a time-varying frequency-selective channel model with Rician fading, modeled as
[
18
]

\begin{split}\mathbf{H}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}[n,k]&=\sqrt{\eta^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}\sqrt{\frac{\kappa^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}{1+\kappa^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}}\underbrace{\mathbf{H}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}_{\rm fs}[k]}_{\rm free-space}\\
&+\sqrt{\eta^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{1+\kappa^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}}\underbrace{\mathbf{H}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}_{\rm sp}[n,k]}_{\rm stochastic\,part},\end{split}

(1)

where
\eta^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
accounts for large-scale attenuation effects, including path loss and shadowing, while
\kappa^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
denotes the Rician
K
-factor associated with frequency band
{\rm b}\in\{{\rm s},{\rm m}\}
.
To reflect the different propagation characteristics at
MMW
and sub-6 GHz frequencies, the
MMW

K
-factor is assumed to scale with that of the sub-6 GHz band according to
\kappa^{\left({\rm m}\right)}=c_{\kappa}\kappa^{\left({\rm s}\right)}
, where
c_{\kappa}
denotes the scaling factor.
The deterministic
LOS
channel component corresponds to a free-space propagation model and is defined by

\mathbf{H}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}_{\rm fs}[k]=e^{j\chi^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}e^{j\frac{2\pi\nu^{\left({\rm b}\right)}k}{\bigtriangleup f^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}}\mathbf{a}_{\rm Rx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}\left(\varphi\right)\left(\mathbf{a}_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}\left(\vartheta\right)\right)^{\rm H}

(2)

where

\mathbf{a}_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}\left(\vartheta\right)=\begin{bmatrix}1&\cdots&e^{-j2\pi\left(M_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}-1\right)\frac{\Delta d^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}{\lambda^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}\sin\left(\vartheta\right)}\end{bmatrix}^{\rm T}

(3)

and

\mathbf{a}_{\rm Rx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}\left(\varphi\right)=\begin{bmatrix}1&\cdots&e^{-j2\pi\left(M_{\rm Rx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}-1\right)\frac{\Delta d^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}{\lambda^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}\sin\left(\varphi\right)}\end{bmatrix}^{\rm T}

(4)

denote the array steering vectors associated with the
AoD

\vartheta

and
AoA

\varphi
, respectively.
In
Eq.

2
, the term
\chi^{\left({\rm b}\right)}~\sim~\mathcal{U}~\left(-\pi,\pi\right)
models the random phase offset at the first (reference) antenna element,
\nu^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
denotes the Doppler shift and
\bigtriangleup f^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
represents the subcarrier spacing.
The stochastic channel matrix
\mathbf{H}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}_{\rm sp}[n,k]
is generated according to the
3GPP
(
3GPP
) channel model
[
2
]
.
Given the large difference between the sub-6 GHz and
MMW
frequencies, the stochastic channels
\mathbf{H}^{\left({\rm s}\right)}_{\rm sp}[n,k]
and
\mathbf{H}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}_{\rm sp}[n,k]
are assumed to be statistically independent.
Furthermore, a channel model following Jakes sum-of-sinusoids approach
[
12
]
is employed to determine the temporal evolution of the wireless channel
[
33
]
.

II-B

Link Establishment

The establishment of a communication link consists of a training phase, followed by a data transmission phase.
During the training phase, the wireless channel is estimated independently at sub-6 GHz and
MMW
frequency bands.
The channel estimates from both bands are then utilized jointly for data transmission at the
MMW
band.

II-B
1
Training Phase

Fig. 1:
Pilot symbols at each antenna occupy a certain number of subcarriers such that they do not overlap in the frequency domain.
The 1-symbol pattern (top) confines all pilot transmissions to a single OFDM time-symbol, the 2-symbol pattern (middle) expands the pilot allocation over two OFDM time-symbols, while the 4-symbol pattern (bottom) spans four OFDM time-symbols per transmission direction.

In the training phase, known pilot symbols are transmitted from each antenna element and are assumed to be perfectly known at the receiver.
The pilot symbols
\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}[n,k]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}\times 1}
generated from the
QAM
constellation are distributed across
N^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
subcarriers such that each transmit antenna is assigned a distinct subset of non-overlapping subcarriers.

For the pilot allocation, we consider three pilot patterns similar to those defined by the
3GPP
for the
CSI-RS
(
CSI-RS
)
[
1
]
.
These pilot patterns are designed to facilitate efficient and scalable channel estimation for different antenna configurations and deployment scenarios.
Note that the pilot patterns considered in this work are provided as illustrative examples and are not fully compliant with the
3GPP
standard.
These pilot patterns are intended for potential use in future systems and may be adapted based on the specific configuration and deployment scenario.
In this work, we refer to them colloquially as the 1-symbol, 2-symbol, and 4-symbol pilot patterns, based on the number
K_{\rm P}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
of
OFDM
time-symbols they span.
Note that the system operates using the
TDD
protocol, which requires the pilot symbols to be allocated for both the
Tx-to-Rx
(
Tx-to-Rx
) and
Rx-to-Tx
(
Rx-to-Tx
) directions.
Since we typically use the same number of transmit and receive antennas in this paper, we set the number of time-symbols
K_{\rm P}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
to be identical for both the
Tx-to-Rx
and
Rx-to-Tx
directions.
This constraint can, however, be relaxed when the numbers of transmit and receive antennas differ.
An example pilot allocation for a system with
M_{\rm Tx}^{\left(\rm b\right)}=8
transmit antennas is illustrated in
Fig.

1
.
The pilot allocation at the
t
-th transmit antenna is given by

\mathrm{\phi}_{t}^{\left(\rm b\right)}[n,k]=\begin{cases}\mathrm{\phi}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}[n,k],\quad\text{if }(n,k,t)\in\mathcal{P}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}\\
0,\quad\quad\quad\quad\,\,\text{else}\end{cases}

(5)

where the set of pilot positions
\mathcal{P}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}
is defined by

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}=\left\{\right.&\left.(n,k,t):\right.\\
&\left.n\in\left\{t_{m},t_{m}+\frac{M_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}{K_{\rm P}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}},\ldots,N^{\left({\rm b}\right)}-\frac{M_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}{K_{\rm P}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}+t_{m}\right\},\right.\\
&\left.k\in\{1,2,\ldots,2K_{\rm P}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}\},\quad t\in\left\{1,2,\ldots,M_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}\right\},\right.\\
&\left.t_{m}=\left(t-1\bmod\frac{M_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}{K_{\rm P}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}\right)+1\right\}.\end{split}

(6)

and
t\in\{1,\ldots,M_{\rm Tx}^{\left(\rm b\right)}\}
denotes the transmit antenna index.

The 1-symbol pilot pattern
\left(K_{\rm P}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}=1\right)
confines all pilot transmissions to a single
OFDM
time-symbol per transmission direction.
This enables fully parallel pilot transmission and offers minimal time-domain overhead, making it attractive for latency-critical applications.
However, the limited number of pilot resource elements restricts its applicability to systems with few transmit antennas and may result in reduced estimation accuracy in frequency-selective channels due to sparse pilot density in the frequency domain.

The 2-symbol pilot pattern
\left(K_{\rm P}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}=2\right)
expands the pilot allocation over two
OFDM
time-symbols per transmission direction.
This extension increases the available pilot resources, supporting a larger number of antennas and improving frequency-domain resolution.
This denser pilot placement leads to more reliable channel estimation in frequency-selective environments, at the expense of higher time-domain overhead compared to the 1-symbol pattern.

In the 4-symbol pilot pattern
\left(K_{\rm P}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}=4\right)
, pilots span four
OFDM
time-symbols, providing high pilot density and robust channel estimation, particularly for large-scale
MIMO
systems.
The improved accuracy comes with a significant increase in overhead, which reduces resources available for data transmission and increases latency.

The input-output relationship of the training phase is given by

\mathbf{y}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}[n,k]=\mathbf{H}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}[n,k]\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}[n,k]+\mathbf{w}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}[n,k],

(7)

where the received signal is denoted by
\mathbf{y}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}[n,k]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Rx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}\times 1}
and the
AWGN
(
AWGN
) with the power of
\sigma_{w^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}^{2}
is denoted by
\mathbf{w}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}[n,k]\sim\mathcal{CN}\left(0,\sigma_{w^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M_{\rm Rx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}\right)
.
At the receiver,
LS
(
LS
) channel estimates are first obtained at the pilot subcarriers.
The channel response at the remaining subcarriers is then obtained via linear interpolation, yielding the estimated channel matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Rx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}\times M_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}}
.
Since the channel estimation spans over
K_{\rm P}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}
symbols to obtain a full
MIMO
channel estimate, we consider the estimated quantities as time-invariant and therefore omit the time-index
k
.
However, it is important to note that the actual channel
\mathbf{H}^{\left({\rm b}\right)}[n,k]
may still vary over time.

Since channel estimation is performed at the receiver, the corresponding
CSI
(
CSI
) has to be available also at the transmitter to enable optimal beamforming.
Owing to the use of a
TDD
scheme, the transmitter acquires the
CSI
by leveraging channel reciprocity in both frequency bands.
Specifically, the procedure begins with the transmission of training symbols from the transmitter to the receiver (
Tx-to-Rx
), allowing the receiver to acquire the
CSI
.
This is followed by the transmission of training symbols in the reverse direction (
Rx-to-Tx
), enabling the transmitter to acquire the
CSI
.

II-B
2
Data Transmission

During the data transmission phase, only the
MMW
link is considered.
Data symbols are transmitted over
N^{\left({\rm m}\right)}
subcarriers and
K_{\rm D}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}=K^{\left({\rm m}\right)}-K_{\rm P}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}

OFDM
time-symbols.
The channel estimates obtained in the training phase are further processed using the methods which will be detailed in
Section

III
.
The resulting
MMW
channel estimate
\overline{\mathbf{H}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]
is then employed to design the precoding and combining matrices.
To maximize
SE
,
SVD
(
SVD
) is adopted.
The compact-form
SVD
of
\overline{\mathbf{H}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]
is given by

\overline{\mathbf{H}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]=\overline{\mathbf{Q}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\,\overline{\mathbf{\Sigma}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\left(\overline{\mathbf{F}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\right)^{\rm H},

(8)

where
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Rx}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}\times{\ell_{\rm max}}}
contains the left singular vectors and serves as the combining matrix,
\overline{\mathbf{F}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}\times{\ell_{\rm max}}}
comprises the right singular vectors and defines the precoder,
\overline{\mathbf{\Sigma}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]
denotes the diagonal matrix of singular values
{\rm diag}\left(\overline{\sigma}_{\rm(1)}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n],\ldots,\overline{\sigma}_{(\ell_{\rm max})}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\right)
and
\ell_{\rm max}={\rm min}\left(M_{\rm Rx}^{\left({\rm m}\right)},M_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}\right)
denotes the maximum number of spatial streams.
The power loading matrix is defined by
\overline{\mathbf{P}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]={\rm diag}\left(\overline{p}_{(1)}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n],\ldots,\overline{p}_{(\ell_{\rm max})}^{\left(\rm m\right)}[n]\right)
and is optimized via the water-filling algorithm to maximize the achievable rate under a total transmit power constraint

\left\lVert\overline{\mathbf{F}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\left(\overline{\mathbf{P}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\right)^{1/2}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}=P_{\rm\rm T}^{\left({\rm m}\right)},

(9)

where
P_{\rm\rm T}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}
denotes the total transmit power.
With this notation, the input-output relationship for the data transmission phase is then given by

\begin{split}\mathbf{y}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n,k]&=\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\right)^{\rm H}\mathbf{H}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n,k]\,\overline{\mathbf{x}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n,k]\\
&+\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\right)^{\rm H}\mathbf{w}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n,k],\end{split}

(10)

where the precoded transmit signal is denoted by
\overline{\mathbf{x}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n,k]=\overline{\mathbf{F}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\left(\overline{\mathbf{P}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\right)^{1/2}\mathbf{x}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n,k]
, the unprecoded transmit signal is denoted by
\mathbf{x}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n,k]\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell_{\rm max}\times 1}
, the received signal is denoted by
\mathbf{y}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n,k]\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell_{\rm max}\times 1}
and the
AWGN
added at the
MMW
receiver is denoted by
\mathbf{w}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n,k]\sim\mathcal{CN}\left(0,\sigma_{w^{\left({\rm m}\right)}}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M_{\rm Rx}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}}\right)
.
Since
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]
is semi-unitary,
\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]\right)^{\rm H}\mathbf{w}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n,k]
has the same statistical distribution as
\mathbf{w}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n,k]
.

III
Channel Estimation Methods

This section outlines the
MMW
channel estimation schemes considered in this work.

III-A

Conventional

The conventional approach uses only the in-band
MMW
channel estimate
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]
, obtained via
LS
estimation at pilot subcarriers and subsequent linear interpolation, as described in
Section

II-B1
.
The resulting
MMW
channel estimate is given by

\overline{\mathbf{H}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]=\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n].

(11)

Note that this approach does not leverage any additional side information or advanced signal processing techniques.

III-B

Out-of-band Aided MRC (OOBA-MRC)

This method applies
OOBA-MRC
(
OOBA-MRC
) to optimally combine the in-band channel estimate
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]
(detailed in
Section

II-B1
) with the out-of-band aided channel estimate
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]
(introduced in
[
24
]
).
The combined
MMW
channel estimate is given by

\overline{\mathbf{H}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]=\widehat{w}\,\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]+(1-\widehat{w})\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}[n]

(12)

where

\widehat{w}\approx\frac{M_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}M_{\rm Rx}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}\sigma_{w^{\left(\rm m\right)}}^{2}}{\frac{M_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}M_{\rm Rx}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}}{1+\widetilde{\kappa}^{\left({\rm s}\right)}}+\left(1+M_{\rm Tx}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}M_{\rm Rx}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}\right)\sigma_{w^{\left(\rm m\right)}}^{2}}.

(13)

denotes the approximated optimal combining factor, whose derivation is detailed in
[
24
]
.
In
Eq.

13
,
\widetilde{\kappa}^{\left({\rm s}\right)}

Two counteracting effects are of particular interest here.
Increasing the number of pilot symbols enhances the estimation accuracy by providing more reliable channel information.
However, increasing the number of pilot symbols causes channel aging, where the estimates lose relevance as the channel changes over time.
Thus, it is important to evaluate the resulting trade-off between estimation accuracy and channel aging.
As expected, the achievable

-

For low-velocities (up to approximately 50 km/h), the

K_{\rm P}^{\left({\rm m}\right)}

In the time-invariant case, the 1-symbol pilot pattern yields an

With increasing velocity, however, channel aging becomes dominant, leading to a faster

In the

In contrast, the conventional method is affected by channel aging, causing performance to deteriorate as the number of pilot symbols increases.
When the receiver velocity reaches 200 km/h, the

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigate the channel estimation performance of digital beamforming
MMW

MIMO
systems and the impact of different pilot schemes in both static and dynamic scenarios.
Simulation results confirm the effectiveness of the
OOBA-MRC
method compared to the conventional method.
The
SNR
and Rician
K
-factor affect the performance of the
OOBA-MRC
method to a great extent.

In static scenarios, increasing the number of pilot symbols improves the quality of the channel estimates.
For low
K
-factors, where the channel is highly frequency-selective, this results in an overall increase in
SE
.
However, for high
K
-factors where the channel is predominantly flat, increasing the number of pilot symbols does not improve the
SE
performance.

In dynamic scenarios, channel aging becomes a dominant factor.
As the number of pilot symbols increases, the channel may change significantly during the estimation interval, causing older estimates to lose relevance.
Consequently, for low
K
-factors, lower velocities benefit from additional pilots, leading to increased
SE
, while higher velocities experience a decline in
SE
as more pilots are used.
For high
K
-factors, the conventional method exhibits decreasing
SE
with an increasing number of pilots, whereas
OOBA-MRC
remains robust and maintains high performance even at high mobility.
