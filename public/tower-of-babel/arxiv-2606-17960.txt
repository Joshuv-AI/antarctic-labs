Average entropy of Bogoliubov-Kubo-Mori random state ensemble
Sohail ​ and Lu Wei

Abstract
Random states play a foundational role in different branches of modern quantum science. In this work, we study a recently proposed random state ensemble induced from von Neumann entropy through the Bogoliubov-Kubo-Mori (BKM) metric. In particular, we derive an exact yet explicit formula of average entanglement entropy over BKM ensemble. In obtaining the formula, we only make use of properties of normalization constant of the ensemble in the absence of its correlation kernel, contrary to average entropy computation of other ensembles. This new framework paves the way for calculating higher-order cumulants of BKM ensemble beyond the average.

Introduction and main results

Crucial to successful exploitation of revolutionary advances of quantum science is the understanding of quantum entanglement. Entanglement is the physical phenomenon, the medium, and, most importantly, the resource underpinning quantum technologies. Mathematical description of entanglement is naturally formulated in terms of generic states, which are random states generated from information-geometric metrics. Random states find various applications in contemporary quantum science such as complexity of quantum circuits

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C(\alpha)}\det\left(A(\alpha)\right)\,\mathrm{tr}\left(A^{-1}(\alpha)A(\alpha+l)\right)

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{tr}\left(A^{-1}(\alpha)A(\alpha+l)\right),

3
Other generic state ensembles

In this appendix, we introduce two major random state ensembles and compare their average entropy formulas with that of the BKM ensemble.

3.1
Hilbert-Schmidt ensemble

Hilbert-Schmidt ensemble is considered as the baseline model of generic quantum states. Randomness of the states comes from the assumption of Gaussian distributed coefficients. The probability density function of Hilbert-Schmidt ensemble and its unconstrained version, up to normalization constants, are given respectively by
Page93

f(\lambda)\propto\delta\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\right)\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}

(94)

and

g(x)\propto\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{e}^{-x_{i}}.

(95)

Comparing the above unconstrained ensemble (
95
) to BKM unconstrained ensemble (
16
), the only difference is that the interaction

\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)

(96)

in (
95
) is replaced by the interaction

\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\left(\ln x_{i}-\ln x_{j}\right)

(97)

in (
16
). This in fact leads to substantial differences of average entropy formulas as discussed below.

The average von Neumann entropy (
2
) over the Hilbert-Schmidt ensemble (
94
) was conjectured in the seminal work
Page93
as

\mathbb{E}\!\left[S\right]=\psi_{0}\left(m^{2}+m\alpha+1\right)-\psi_{0}\left(m+\alpha+1\right)-\frac{m-1}{2(m+\alpha)},

(98)

which was subsequently proved, among other proofs, in
Foong94
;
Ruiz95
. It is observed that the above average entropy (
98
) only involves digamma function
\psi_{0}
, whereas the average entropy (
10
) of BKM ensemble of dimension
m
involves polygamma functions
\psi_{0},\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{m}
.

Beyond the mean formula (
98
), higher-order cumulants up to the fourth were reported in
VPO16
;
Wei17
;
Wei20
;
HWC21
, whereas a simplified procedure to derive cumulants of any order was recently proposed in
HW25
. Finding structures underlying higher-order cumulants of BKM ensemble remains an interesting open question.

3.2
Bures-Hall ensemble

Bures-Hall ensemble is an improved variant of the Hilbert-Schmidt ensemble that satisfies a few additional information-theoretic properties
Sommers03
. The ensemble is often used as a prior distribution known as Bures prior in reconstructing quantum states from measurements. The probability density function of Bures-Hall ensemble and its unconstrained version, up to normalization constants, are given respectively by
Kumar19

f(\lambda)\propto\delta\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\right)\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\frac{\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)^{2}}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}

(99)

and

g(x)\propto\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\frac{\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)^{2}}{x_{i}+x_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{e}^{-x_{i}}.

(100)

The average von Neumann entropy (
2
) over the Bures-Hall ensemble (
99
) was conjectured in
Kumar19
as

\mathbb{E}\!\left[S\right]=\psi_{0}\left(\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+m\alpha+1\right)-\psi_{0}\left(m+\alpha+1\right),

(101)

which was first proved in
Wei20BHA
and a recent proof based on spectral moments was found in
WHW26
. It is seen that the above formula (
101
) shares the same first term

\psi_{0}(\beta+1)=\psi_{0}\left(\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+m\alpha+1\right)

(102)

with that of the BKM ensemble (
10
). This is due to the fact that trace of the two unconstrained ensembles (
100
) and (
16
) follows a gamma density (
22
) with the same shape parameter
\beta
in (
5
), where the first terms in (
101
) and (
10
) are produced from the same trace statistics (
26
). It is also of interest to point out that the average entropy formulas (
101
) and (
98
) share the same term

\psi_{0}\left(m+\alpha+1\right),

(103)

although the reason for this remains unclear.

Beyond the mean formula (
101
), higher-order cumulants of variance and skewness formulas were obtained in
Wei20BH
and
WHW25
, respectively. However, finding cumulant structures of Bures-Hall ensemble that enable the derivation of cumulant of any order is still an open problem. Finally, we note that besides entanglement entropy (
2
), other entanglement measures such as entanglement capacity
Wei23
and relative entropy
Wei26
have also been studied for the Hilbert-Schmidt and Bures-Hall ensembles.
