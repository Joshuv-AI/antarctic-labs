Direct Product of Picture Fuzzy Subgroups
Taiwo O. Sangodapo,

Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, the concept of a picture fuzzy subgroup of a group is studied, and the notion of the direct product of picture fuzzy subgroups is introduced. Several characterisations of the direct product of picture fuzzy subgroups are established using the
(r,s,t)
-cut sets of picture fuzzy sets.

2.
Preliminaries

This section gives the basic definitions and existing results relating to picture fuzzy subgroups.

Definition 2.1
.

[
8
]

A picture fuzzy set Q of
Y
is defined as

Q=\{(y,\sigma_{Q}(y),\tau_{Q}(y),\gamma_{Q}(y))|y\in Y\},

where the functions

\sigma_{Q}:Y\rightarrow[0,1],~\tau_{Q}:Y\rightarrow[0,1]~\text{and}~\gamma_{Q}:Y\rightarrow[0,1]

are called the positive, neutral and negative membership degrees of
y\in Q
, respectively, and
\sigma_{Q},\tau_{Q},\gamma_{Q}
satisfy

0\leq\sigma_{Q}(y)+\tau_{Q}(y)+\gamma_{Q}(y)\leq 1,~\forall y\in Y.

For each
y\in Y
,
S_{Q}(y)=1-(\sigma_{Q}(y)-\tau_{Q}(y)-\gamma_{Q}(y))
is called the refusal membership degree of
y\in Q
.

Definition 2.2
.

[
13
]

Let
(G,\ast)
be a crisp group and
Q=\{(y,\sigma_{Q}(y),\tau_{Q}(y),\eta_{Q}(y))~|~y\in G\}
be a PFS in
G
. Then,
Q
is called picture fuzzy subgroup of
G
(PFSG) if

(i)
\sigma_{Q}(a\ast b)\geq\sigma_{Q}(a)\wedge\sigma_{Q}(b),~\tau_{Q}(a\ast b)\geq\tau_{Q}(a)\wedge\tau_{Q}(b),~\eta_{Q}(a\ast b)\leq\eta_{Q}(a)\vee\eta_{Q}(b)

(ii)
\sigma_{Q}(a^{-1})\geq\sigma_{Q}(a),~\tau_{Q}(a^{-1})\geq\tau_{Q}(a),~\eta_{Q}(a^{-1})\leq\eta_{Q}(a)
for all
a,b\in G
.

Notice that
a^{-1}
is the inverse of
a\in G
,

or equivalently,
Q
is a PFSG of
G
if and only if

\sigma_{Q}(a\ast b^{-1})\geq\sigma_{Q}(a)\wedge\sigma_{Q}(b),~\tau_{Q}(a\ast b^{-1})\geq\tau_{Q}(a)\wedge\tau_{Q}(b),~\eta_{Q}(a\ast b^{-1})\leq\eta_{Q}(a)\vee\eta_{Q}(b).

.

[
13
]

Let
(G,\ast)
be a crisp group and
Q=(\sigma_{Q},\tau_{Q},\eta_{Q})
be a PFSG of
G
. Then,
Q
is called picture fuzzy normal subgroup of
G,
denoted by PFNSG if

\sigma_{Qa}(b)=\sigma_{aQ}(b),~\tau_{Qa}(b)=\tau_{aQ}(b),~\eta_{Qa}(b)=\eta_{aQ}(b)

for all
a,~b\in G
.

Definition 2.4
.

A PFSG
Q=(\sigma_{Q},\tau_{Q},\eta_{Q})
of a group
G
is said to be a PFNSG of
G
if
\sigma_{Q}(ab)=\sigma_{Q}(ba),~\tau_{Q}(ab)=\tau_{Q}(ba)~\text{and}~\eta_{Q}(ab)=\eta_{Q}(ba)~\forall~a,b\in G,
or equivalently,
Q
a PFSG of
G
is said to be normal if and only if

\sigma_{Q}(b^{-1}ab)=\sigma_{Q}(a),~\sigma_{Q}(b^{-1}ab)=\sigma_{Q}(a)~\text{and}~\eta_{Q}(b^{-1}ab)=\eta_{Q}(a)~\forall~a,b\in G.

Definition 2.5
.

[
13
]

Let
Q=\{(x,\sigma_{Q},\tau_{Q},\eta_{Q})|a\in X\}
be PFS over the universe
X
. Then,
(r,s,t)
-cut set of
Q
is the crisp set in
Q
, denoted by
C_{r,s,t}(Q)
and is defined by

C_{r,s,t}(Q)=\left\{a\in X|\sigma_{Q}(a)\geq r,~\tau_{Q}(a)\geq s,\eta_{Q}(a)\leq t\right\}

r,s,t\in[0,1]
with the condition
0\leq r+s+t\leq 1

Theorem 2.1
.

[
13
]

Let
(G,\ast)

Q=(\sigma_{Q},\tau_{Q},\eta_{Q})
be a PFSG of
G

Q
is a PFSG (PFNSG) if and only if
C_{r,s,t}(Q)
is a crisp subgroup (normal) of

.

[

(G,\ast)

for all

.

(G,\ast)

G

Qa=(\sigma_{Qa},~\tau_{Qa},~\eta_{Qa})

\sigma_{Qa}(y)=\sigma_{aQ}(y),~\tau_{Qa}(y)=\tau_{aQ}(y),~\eta_{Qa}(y)=\eta_{aQ}(y)

Proposition 2.1
.

[
23
]

Let
Q
be a PFSG of
G
and
a
be any fixed element of
G.
Then,

(i.)

a\ast C_{r,s,t}(Q)=C_{r,s,t}(a\ast Q).

(ii.)

C_{r,s,t}(Q)\ast a=C_{r,s,t}(Q\ast a),~\forall~r,s,t\ [0,1]

Definition 2.9
.

[
27
]

Let
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
be two nonempty sets and
f:Y_{1}\rightarrow Y_{2}
be a mapping. Let
P
and
Q
be two PFSs of
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2},
respectively. Then, the image of
P
under
f
denoted by
f(P)
is defined as

f(P)(y_{2})=(\sigma_{f(P)}(y_{2}),~\tau_{f(P)}(y_{2}),~\eta_{f(P)}(y_{2})),

where

\sigma_{f(P)}(y_{2})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vee\{\sigma_{P}(y_{1}):y_{1}\in f^{-1}(y_{2})\}\\
\\
0,\;otherwise\end{array}\right.,

\tau_{f(P)}(y_{2})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vee\{\tau_{P}(y_{1}):y_{1}\in f^{-1}(y_{2})\}\\
\\
0,\;otherwise\end{array}\right.,

and

\eta_{f(P)}(y_{2})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\wedge\{\eta_{P}(y_{1}):y_{1}\in f^{-1}(y_{2})\}\\
\\
1,\;otherwise\end{array}\right.

Thus,

f(P)(y_{2})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\vee\{\sigma_{P}(y_{1}):y_{1}\in f^{-1}(y_{2})\},\vee\{\tau_{P}(y_{1}):y_{1}\in f^{-1}(y_{2})\},\wedge\{\eta_{P}(y_{1}):y_{1}\in f^{-1}(y_{2})\})\\
\\
(0,0,1),\;\;\;\;\;otherwise\end{array}\right.

The pre-image of
Q
under
f,
denoted by
f^{-1}(Q)
is also defined as

f^{-1}(Q)(y_{1})=(\sigma_{f^{-1}(Q)}(y_{1}),\tau_{f^{-1}(Q)}(y_{1}),\eta_{f^{-1}(Q)}(y_{1}))

where

\sigma_{f^{-1}(Q)}(y_{1})=\sigma_{Q}(f(y_{1})),\tau_{f^{-1}(Q)}(y_{1})=\tau_{Q}(f(y_{1})),\eta_{f^{-1}(Q)}(y_{1})=\eta_{Q}(f(y_{1})).

Thus,

f^{-1}(Q)(y_{1})=(\sigma_{Q}(f(y_{1})),\tau_{Q}(f(y_{1})),\eta_{Q}(f(y_{1}))).

Remark 2.1
.

[
27
]

For any
y_{1}\in Y_{1},
we have
\sigma_{f(P)}(f(y_{1}))\geq\sigma_{P}(y_{1}),~\tau_{f(P)}(f(y_{1}))\geq\tau_{P}(y_{1})
and
\eta_{f(P)}(f(y_{1}))\leq\eta_{P}(y_{1}).

Theorem 2.2
.

[
27
]

Let
f:Y_{1}\rightarrow Y_{2}
be a mapping. Then,

(i)

f(C_{r,s,t}(P))\subseteq C_{r,s,t}(f(P)),~\text{for~ all}~P\in PFS(Y_{1})

(ii)

f^{-1}(C_{r,s,t}(Q))=C_{r,s,t}(Q)(f^{-1}(Q)),~\text{for~ all}~Q\in PFS(Y_{2}).

Theorem 2.3
.

If
P=(\sigma_{P},\tau_{P},\eta_{P})
be a PFSG of
G.
Then,
P
is PFNSG of
G
if and only if
C_{r,s,t}(P)
is a PFNSG of
G,
for all
0\leq r,s,t\leq 1
with
0\leq r+s+t\leq 1.

Proof.

Suppose that
P
is a PFNSG of
G

\iff~aP=Pa
for all
a\in G

\iff C_{r,s,t}(aP)=C_{r,s,t}(Pa),
for all
0\leq r,s,t\leq 1
with
0\leq r+s+t\leq 1

\iff~a*C_{r,s,t}(P)=C_{r,s,t}(P)*a,
for all
a\in G

\iff~C_{r,s,t}(P)
is a PFNSG of
G.

∎

Definition 2.10
.

Let
P=(\sigma_{P},\tau_{P},\eta_{P})
and
Q=(\sigma_{Q},\tau_{Q},\eta_{Q})

G.
Then,
P
and

are said to be picture fuzzy conjugate subgroups (PFCSG) of

\sigma_{P}(g)=\sigma_{Q}(a^{-1}ga),~\tau_{P}(g)=\tau_{Q}(a^{-1}ga)~\text{and}~\eta_{P}(g)=\eta_{Q}(a^{-1}ga),~\text{for~ every}~g\in G.

3.
Direct Product of Picture Fuzzy Subgroups

Definition 3.1
.

Let
P=\{\langle p,\sigma_{P}(p),\tau_{P}(p),\eta_{P}(p)\rangle~|~p~\in Y_{1}\}
and
Q=\{\langle q,\sigma_{Q}(q),\tau_{Q}(q),\eta_{Q}(q)\rangle~|~q~\in Y_{2}\}
be two PFSs. Then, the Cartesian product of
P
and
Q
is the PFS

P\times Q=\{\langle(p,q),\sigma_{P\times Q}(p,q),\tau_{P\times Q}(p,q),\eta_{P\times Q}(p,q)\rangle~|~(p,q)~\in Y_{1}\times Y_{2}\},

where
\sigma_{P\times Q}(p,q)=\bigwedge\{\sigma_{P}(p),\sigma_{Q}(q)\}
,
\tau_{P\times Q}(p,q)=\bigwedge\{\tau_{P}(p),\tau_{Q}(q)\}

and
\eta_{P\times Q}(p,q)=\bigvee\{\eta_{P}(p)\vee\eta_{Q}(q)\}
for all
(p,q)\in Y_{1}\times Y_{2}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
P=(\sigma_{P},\tau_{P},\eta_{P})
and
Q=(\sigma_{Q},\tau_{Q},\eta_{Q})
be PFSs of
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2},
respectively. Then,

C_{r,s,t}(P\times Q)=C_{r,s,t}(P)\times C_{r,s,t}(Q),~\forall~0\leq r,s,t\leq 1~\text{with}~r+s+t\in[0,1].

Proof.

Let
(p,q)\in C_{r,s,t}(P\times Q)
be any element

\iff~(p,q)\geq r,~(p,q)\geq s~\text{and}~(p,q)\leq t

\iff~\bigwedge\{\sigma_{P}(p),~\sigma_{Q}(q)\},~\bigwedge\{\tau_{P}(p),~\tau_{Q}(q)\}~\text{and}~\bigvee\{\eta_{P}(p),~\eta_{Q}(q)\}

\iff~\sigma_{P}(p)\geq r,~\sigma_{Q}(q)\geq r,~\tau_{P}(p)\geq s,~\tau_{Q}(q)\geq s~\text{and}~\eta_{P}(p)\leq t,~\eta_{Q}(q)\leq t

\iff~\sigma_{P}(p)\geq r,~\tau_{P}(p)\geq s,~\eta_{P}(p)\leq t~\text{and}~\sigma_{Q}(q)\geq r,~\tau_{Q}(q)\geq s,~\eta_{Q}(q)\leq t

\iff~p\in C_{r,s,t}(P)~\text{and}~q\in C_{r,s,t}(Q)

\iff~(p,q)\in C_{r,s,t}(P)\times C_{r,s,t}(Q).

Hence,

C_{r,s,t}(P\times Q)=C_{r,s,t}(P)\times C_{r,s,t}(Q).

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
P=(\sigma_{P},\tau_{P},\eta_{P})
and
Q=(\sigma_{Q},\tau_{Q},\eta_{Q})
be PFSGs of groups
G
and
G^{*},
respectively. Then,
P\times Q
is also a PFSG of group
G\times G^{*}.

Proof.

Since
P
and
Q
are PFSG of
G
and
G^{*},
respectively, then
C_{r,s,t}(P)
and
C_{r,s,t}(Q)
are PFSG of
G
and
G^{*},
respectively,
\forall~r,s,t\in[0,1]
with
r+s+t\in[0,1]
by Theorem
2.1
. This shows that
C_{r,s,t}(P)\times C_{r,s,t}(Q)
is a PFSG of
G\times G^{*}
which also implies that
C_{r,s,t}(P\times Q)
is a PFSG of
G\times G^{*}
by Theorem
2.1
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
P=(\sigma_{P},\tau_{P},\eta_{P})
and
Q=(\sigma_{Q},\tau_{Q},\eta_{Q})
be PFNSGs of groups
G
and
G^{*},
respectively. Then,
P\times Q
is also a PFNSG of group
G\times G^{*}.

Proof.

Since
P
and
Q
are PFNSG of
G
and
G^{*},
respectively. Then, by Theorem
2.3
,
C_{r,s,t}(P)
and
C_{r,s,t}(Q)
are PFNSG of
G
and
G^{*},
respectively. This means that
C_{r,s,t}(P)\times C_{r,s,t}(Q)
is a PFNSG of
G\times G^{*}
which also implies that
C_{r,s,t}(P\times Q)
is a PFSG of
G\times G^{*}
by Theorem
2.3
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
P=(\sigma_{P},\tau_{P},\eta_{P})
and
Q=(\sigma_{Q},\tau_{Q},\eta_{Q})
be PFSGs of groups
G
and
G^{*},
respectively. Assume that
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are the identities of
G
and
G^{*},
respectively and if
P\times Q
is a PFSG of
G\times G^{*},
then at least on of the following must holds:

(i)
\sigma_{Q}(e_{2})\geq\sigma_{P}(g),~\tau_{Q}(e_{2})\geq\tau_{P}(g),~\text{and}~\eta_{Q}(e_{2})\leq\eta_{P}(g),~\forall~g\in G.

(ii)
\sigma_{Q}(e_{1})\geq\sigma_{P}(g^{*}),~\tau_{Q}(e_{1})\geq\tau_{P}(g^{*}),~\text{and}~\eta_{Q}(e_{1})\leq\eta_{P}(g^{*}),~\forall~g^{*}\in G^{*}.

Proof.

Let
P\times Q
be a PFSG of
G\times G^{*}.
Suppose none of (i) and (ii) holds. Then, there exists
g\in G
and
g^{*}\in G^{*}
such that

\sigma_{P}(g)>\sigma_{Q}(e_{2}),~\tau_{P}(g)>\tau_{Q}(e_{2}),\eta_{P}(g)<\eta_{Q}(e_{2})
and

\sigma_{Q}(g^{*})>\sigma_{P}(e_{1}),~\tau_{Q}(g^{*})>\tau_{P}(e_{1}),\eta_{Q}(g^{*})<\eta_{P}(e_{1}).
So,

\sigma_{P\times Q}(g,g^{*})=\bigwedge\{\sigma_{P}(g),\sigma_{Q}(g^{*})\}>\{\sigma_{P}(e_{1}),\sigma_{Q}(e_{2})\}=\sigma_{P\times Q}(e_{1},e_{2}),

\tau_{P\times Q}(g,g^{*})=\bigwedge\{\tau_{P}(g),\tau_{Q}(g^{*})\}>\{\tau_{P}(e_{1}),\tau_{Q}(e_{2})\}=\tau_{P\times Q}(e_{1},e_{2})

and

\eta_{P\times Q}(g,g^{*})=\bigvee\{\eta_{P}(g),\eta_{Q}(g^{*})\}<\{\eta_{P}(e_{1}),\eta_{Q}(e_{2})\}=\eta_{P\times Q}(e_{1},e_{2}).

This means that
P\times Q
is not a PFSG of
G\times G^{*},
which is a contradiction. Therefore, either
\sigma_{Q}(e_{2})\geq\sigma_{P}(g),~\tau_{Q}(e_{2})\geq\tau_{P}(g),~\text{and}~\eta_{Q}(e_{2})\leq\eta_{P}(g),~\forall~g\in G
or
\sigma_{Q}(e_{1})\geq\sigma_{P}(g^{*}),~\tau_{Q}(e_{1})\geq\tau_{P}(g^{*}),~\text{and}~\eta_{Q}(e_{1})\leq\eta_{P}(g^{*}),~\forall~g^{*}\in G^{*}.

∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
P=(\sigma_{P},\tau_{P},\eta_{P})
and

be PFSGs of groups

and
G^{*},
respectively such that

Proof.

Let

be any element. Then,
(g,e_{2})~\text{and}~(g^{*},e_{2})\in G\times G^{*}.

we have,

\displaystyle\sigma_{P}(g(g^{*})^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{\sigma_{P}(g(g^{*})^{-1}),\sigma_{Q}(e_{2},e_{2})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{P\times Q}(g(g^{*})^{-1},(e_{2},e_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{P\times Q}[(g,e_{2})((g^{*})^{-1},e_{2})]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{\sigma_{P\times Q}(g,e_{2}),\sigma_{P\times Q}((g^{*})^{-1},e_{2})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{\bigwedge\{\sigma_{P}(g),\sigma_{Q}(e_{2})\},\bigwedge\{\sigma_{P}(g^{*})^{-1},\sigma_{Q}(e_{2})\}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{\sigma_{P}(g),\sigma_{P}((g^{*})^{-1}\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{\sigma_{P}(g),\sigma_{P}(g^{*})\}.

\displaystyle\tau_{P}(g(g^{*})^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{\tau_{P}(g(g^{*})^{-1}),\tau_{Q}(e_{2},e_{2})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{P\times Q}(g(g^{*})^{-1},(e_{2},e_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{P\times Q}[(g,e_{2})((g^{*})^{-1},e_{2})]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{\tau_{P\times Q}(g,e_{2}),\tau_{P\times Q}((g^{*})^{-1},e_{2})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{\bigwedge\{\tau_{P}(g),\tau_{Q}(e_{2})\},\bigwedge\{\tau_{P}(g^{*})^{-1},\tau_{Q}(e_{2})\}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{\tau_{P}(g),\tau_{P}((g^{*})^{-1}\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{\tau_{P}(g),\tau_{P}(g^{*})\}.

and

\displaystyle\eta_{P}(g(g^{*})^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee\{\eta_{P}(g(g^{*})^{-1}),\eta_{Q}(e_{2},e_{2})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{P\times Q}(g(g^{*})^{-1},(e_{2},e_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{P\times Q}[(g,e_{2})((g^{*})^{-1},e_{2})]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigvee\{\eta_{P\times Q}(g,e_{2}),\eta_{P\times Q}((g^{*})^{-1},e_{2})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee\{\bigvee\{\eta_{P}(g),\eta_{Q}(e_{2})\},\bigvee\{\eta_{P}(g^{*})^{-1},\eta_{Q}(e_{2})\}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee\{\eta_{P}(g),\eta_{P}((g^{*})^{-1}\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigvee\{\eta_{P}(g),\eta_{P}(g^{*})\}.

Therefore,
P
is a PFSG of
G.

∎

G\times G^{*}
is conjugate to the PFSG
P_{2}\times Q_{2}
of
G\times G^{*}.

Proof.

Since
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are PFCSGs of
G.
Therefore, there exists
a\in G
such that

\sigma_{P_{1}}(a)=\sigma_{P_{2}}(a^{-1}ga),~\tau_{P_{1}}(a)=\tau_{P_{2}}(a^{-1}ga)~\text{and}~\eta_{P_{1}}(a)=\eta_{P_{2}}(a^{-1}ga),~\forall~g\in G.

Similarly, since
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
are PFCSGs of
G^{*}.
Therefore, there exists
b\in G^{*}
such that

\sigma_{Q_{1}}(b)=\sigma_{Q_{2}}(b^{-1}g^{*}b),~\tau_{Q_{1}}(b)=\tau_{Q_{2}}(b^{-1}g^{*}b)~\text{and}~\eta_{Q_{1}}(b)=\eta_{Q_{2}}(b^{-1}g^{*}b),~\forall~g^{*}\in G^{*}.

Now,

\displaystyle\sigma_{P_{1}\times Q_{1}}(g,g^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{\sigma_{P_{1}}(g),\sigma_{Q_{1}}(g^{*})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{\sigma_{P_{2}}(a^{-1}ga),\sigma_{Q_{2}}(b^{-1}g^{*}b)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{P_{2}\times Q_{2}}((a^{-1}ga),(b^{-1}g^{*}b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{P_{2}\times Q_{2}}((a^{-1},b^{-1})(g,g^{*})(a,b)).

\displaystyle\tau_{P_{1}\times Q_{1}}(g,g^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{\tau_{P_{1}}(g),\tau_{Q_{1}}(g^{*})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{\tau_{P_{2}}(a^{-1}ga),\tau_{Q_{2}}(b^{-1}g^{*}b)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{P_{2}\times Q_{2}}((a^{-1}ga),(b^{-1}g^{*}b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{P_{2}\times Q_{2}}((a^{-1},b^{-1})(g,g^{*})(a,b)).

and

\displaystyle\eta_{P_{1}\times Q_{1}}(g,g^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee\{\eta_{P_{1}}(g),\eta_{Q_{1}}(g^{*})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee\{\eta_{P_{2}}(a^{-1}ga),\eta_{Q_{2}}(b^{-1}g^{*}b)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{P_{2}\times Q_{2}}((a^{-1}ga),(b^{-1}g^{*}b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{P_{2}\times Q_{2}}((a^{-1},b^{-1})(g,g^{*})(a,b)).

Therefore, the PFSG
P_{1}\times Q_{1}
of
G\times G^{*}
is conjugate to the PFSG
P_{2}\times Q_{2}
of
G\times G^{*}.

∎

Conclusion

In this paper, we have established some characterisations of direct product of picture fuzzy subgroups of a group via
(r,s,t)
-cut sets of picture fuzzy sets.
