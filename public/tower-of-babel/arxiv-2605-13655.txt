Hardy spaces and quasiregular mappings: averaged derivatives and the 𝔹 ​ 𝕄 ​ 𝕆 \mathbb{BMO} case Thanks: 1 The research of I. C. is partially supported by grant PID2022-138758NB-I00 (Spain).
Tomasz Adamowicz, Iván Caamaño 1

Abstract
. We study the Hardy spaces ℋ p \mathcal{H}^{p} , 0 < p < ∞ 0<p<\infty of quasiregular mappings on the unit ball 𝔹 n \mathbb{B}^{n} in ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} under the appropriate growth and multiplicity conditions. Our focus is on the averaged derivatives of maps and their Harnack and quantitative Harnack estimates. The averaged derivatives are employed to study the non-tangential limit functions and non-tangential maximal functions of quasiregular mappings and to characterize ℋ p \mathcal{H}^{p} in the case of finite multiplicity of f f . Moreover, we study relations between quasiregular mappings, averaged derivatives, BMO spaces and Carleson measures on 𝔹 n \mathbb{B}^{n} and the role of the multiplicity of a map. We also apply our results to the second order elliptic PDEs and 𝒜 \mathcal{A} -harmonic equations. Our paper extends results by Astala and Koskela [ AK ] and Nolder [ No1 ] to the setting of quasiregular maps.

1.
Introduction

Hardy spaces are one of the key tools in geometric and harmonic analysis and their studies have led to several profound
results. Originally defined in the setting of complex analytic functions, see Hardy
[
H
]
, have been only recently systematically studied for quasiconformal mappings in Euclidean spaces beyond the planar case, see Astala–Koskela
[
AK
]
. Recall that quasiconformal mappings are one of the most fruitful generalizations of planar conformal mappings and are studied in various settings including the Euclidean, the Riemannian and the sub-Riemannian settings, as well as, the metric measure spaces. Moreover, recently the studies of Hardy spaces has been initiated also for the quasiregular mappings, see
[
AG1
,
AG2
]
. On the contrary to quasiconformal mappings, the quasiregular ones need not be injective and, therefore, the interplay between the topology, geometry and analysis lies at the heart of their studies. In particular, one has to control the multiplicity of mappings and their growth in order to ensure the existence of the boundary limits, a problem solved for harmonic and complex analytic functions by the classical Fatou theorem. Nevertheless, a counterpart of the seminal result by Hardy–Littlewood
[
HL
]
characterizing the Hardy spaces in terms of the integrability of the nontangential maximal function and the non-tangential limit map can be proven in the setting of quasiregular maps as well, see
[
AG2
]
and also Theorem
2.3
in Section 2 below.

In this work, we would like to shed a new light on another important tool in the geometric mapping theory, the so-called
averaged derivative of a map
, and apply it to the studies of Hardy spaces. In order to give a wider perspective on our results, let us mention that they correspond to an important line of research in harmonic analysis where geometry of the domain intertwines with the studies of non-tangential maximal functions and the boundary behaviour of functions, see e.g.
[
GMT
]
,
[
HMM
]
,
[
HMMTZ
]
,
[
HT
]
to mention only few. In our work we consider the domain, classical for the studies of Hardy spaces, namely the unit ball. However, we hope that in the long run our results can be extended to other more general types of domains, such as Lipschitz or NTA domains.

In order to motivate the studies of the averaged derivative for quasiregular mappings, recall that the derivative of an analytic function in plane is analytic, and so a number of results in the
H^{p}
-theory for the derivative of an analytic function follow naturally. This is not the case already in the setting of quasiconformal mappings. In particular, the image of a circle under a quasiconformal mapping of the disk can fail to be rectifiable. Hence, in the quasiconformal studies a notion of the
averaged derivative
is introduced in Astala–Gehring
[
AsGe2
]
for domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that generalizes
|Df|
for conformal mappings
f
and is subsequently studied in
[
AK
]
in the context of Hardy spaces, see
[
AsGe1
, Formula (1.5)]
which in our notation reads:

(1)

a_{f}(x):=\exp\left(\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{|2B_{x}|}\int_{2B_{x}}\log J_{f}\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{L}}^{n}\right),

where
2B_{x}
stands for an open hyperbolic ball centered at point
x
with the radius equal to the distance of
x
to the boundary of the underlying domain. As a comment aside let us remark, that the notion of the averaged derivative is quite robust and can be studied also beyond the Euclidean setting, for example in the first Heisenberg group, see
[
AFW
, Definition 1.3]
. However, this definition for the not necessarily injective quasiregular mappings is difficult to study even in the planar setting. Indeed, if
f
is conformal in the plane, then
\operatorname{a}_{f}=|f^{\prime}|
due to the harmonicity of
\log|f^{\prime}|
, but it is not the case for general analytic functions (which are
1
-quasiregular). Therefore, in the lack of injectivity we define the following counterpart of
a_{f}
:

(2)

\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda}(x):=\left(\frac{1}{|2B_{x}|}\int_{\lambda B_{x}}|Df|^{n}\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{L}}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}},\quad x\in\mathbb{B}^{n},\,0<\lambda<2.

In the special
\lambda=1
this notion is introduced in Nolder
[
No1
]
. Moreover, in order to avoid confusion with the weak Jacoby matrix
Df
, we changed the notation comparing to
[
No1
]
, where the author uses
D_{f}
instead. If
f
is quasiconformal, then both notions of
a_{f}
coincide, see
[
No1
, Lemma 2.7]
. Nevertheless, in general it trivially holds that

\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda}\leq D_{f},\hbox{ for }0<\lambda<1.

Our key general observation in this work is that by studying the family of averaged derivatives parameterized by
\lambda
instead of a single averaged derivative for
\lambda=1
, as in
[
No1
]
, we may obtain a good deal of results corresponding to those in Sections 2, 5 and 7 in
[
AK
]
.

In particular, we present various pointwise and quantitative Harnack estimates for
\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda}
and show how the interplay between different values of
\lambda
can be utilized in the studies of Hardy spaces and quasiregular mappings, see Lemma
3.2
and Corollary
3.3
and, moreover, Theorem
3.7
, Lemma
3.9
and Corollary
3.10
.

Let us also add that, in fact, several of the results in Sections 3.2-3.3 hold under mild assumptions on maps, such as the local
L^{n}
-integrability of the Jacoby matrix and, therefore, can potentially be extended beyond the setting of quasiregular mappings, also for more general metric measure spaces.

Furthermore, our results point to the role of multiplicity of a map, since stronger results can be proven if the multiplicity of a map in a ball is finite, see for instance Corollary
3.1
. As presented below, controlling the multiplicity function becomes important in our main results, see the condition (
M
) and the discussion in Section 2 below.
Another crucial condition is the growth of the mapping, see Section 2 for the discussion on the Miniowitz class (
Min
) and Theorem
2.2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

) and in the Miniowitz class (

Then, it holds that

Moreover, if the multiplicity of

Preliminaries

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
we denote the ball centered at
x
with radius
r
by
B(x,r)
and define
\lambda B(x,r):=B(x,\lambda r)
for any
\lambda>0
. Denote
\mathbb{B}^{n}=B(0,1),\mathbb{S}^{n-1}=\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}
and for
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
we define the (Euclidean) hyperbolic ball as follows:

B_{x}:=B\Big(x,\frac{1}{2}(1-|x|)\Big).

Next, we define the
non-tangential region in
\mathbb{B}^{n}
with parameter
\alpha
centered at
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
as follows:

(3)

\Gamma_{\alpha}(\omega):=\{x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}:\;|x-\omega|<(1+\alpha)(1-|x|)\}.

When
\alpha
is fixed, we write for simplicity
\Gamma:=\Gamma_{\alpha}
.

For a point
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
we define its
shadow

S(x)\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
as follows

S(x):=B(x,(1+\alpha)(1-|x|))\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

By the definition,
\omega\in S(x)\Leftrightarrow x\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(\omega)
, and so
S(x)
denotes the maximal set of points on the unit sphere such that a non-tangential region with apex in
S(x)
contains point
x
.

Quasiregular mappings.
Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a domain. A continuous mapping
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
K
-
quasiregular
for
K\geq 1
, if
f
belongs to the Sobolev space
W_{loc}^{1,n}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and the distortion inequality

|Df(x)|^{n}\leq KJ_{f}(x)

holds for almost every
x\in\Omega
. Here
|Df(x)|
denotes the operator norm of the formal derivative of
f
at
x\in\Omega
and
J_{f}(x)
stands for the Jacobian determinant of
Df
at
x
.

If in addition we require
f
to be a homeomorphism, then we say that
f
is
K
-
quasiconformal
. For comprehensive introductions to the topic and further references we refer, for instance, to
[
Ri
]
, Chapter 14 in
[
HKM
]
,
[
Re
]
for results in
n\geq 2
, and
[
AIM
]
for
n=2
. Let us comment that the quasiregular mappings can be defined in various other equivalent ways, e.g. via the modulus of curve families, see e.g.
[
Va
,
Vu2
]
.

Hardy spaces of mappings.
We say that a mapping
f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
belongs to
the Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{p}
for a
0<p<\infty
, if

\sup_{0<r<1}\left(\mathop{\mathchoice{\kern 1.28333pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.78336pt}{\kern 1.16664pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.66666pt}{\kern 1.02428pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.07292pt}\kern-4.09721pt}{\kern 0.96526pt\vbox{\hrule width=2.89586pt}\kern-3.86111pt}}\mathopen{}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|f(r\omega)|^{p}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\omega\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}:=\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}<\infty.

The classical Hardy spaces of holomorphic functions on unit disc are due to Hardy and Littlewood
[
HL
]
. Their importance in the complex analysis follows, for instance, from the relations to harmonic analysis, the studies of the boundary behaviour of functions, Carleson measures and in the Nevanlinna theory, see e.g.
[
D
,
Ga
]
.

The systematic studies of the Hardy spaces of quasiconformal maps in the Euclidean spaces for
n\geq 2
are presented in
[
AK
]
, see also
[
BK
,
BKL
]
,
[
Zi
]
,
[
No2
]
; see
[
AF
]
for the setting of first Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}_{1}
. The lack of injectivity for quasiregular maps leads to the necessity of some additional assumptions as discussed in
[
AG1
,
AG2
]
. We recall them now.

Multiplicity condition (
M
).
One of the fundamental problems to handle in our studies is the existence of non-tangential boundary values for quasiregular functions. Even the special case of bounded quasiregular mappings, which in fact belong to any Hardy space, already illustrates the difficulty of the problem and is not yet fully solved, see
[
Ra08
]
. Moreover, already in the planar setting, the existence of the radial limits requires additional assumptions, see
[
Ma
,
AG1
]
and the discussion in the introduction to
[
AG2
]
. On the other hand, bounded quasiconformal mappings have boundary limits almost everywhere. Therefore, the control on the multiplicity of the quasiregular mapping turns out to be crucial in several of the known results regarding the existence of boundary values.

Recall that by following the discussion in
[
HKM
]
(see pg. 254), for a subset
E\subset\mathbb{B}^{n}
we denote by
N(y,f,E)

the crude multiplicity
of mapping
f
and define it as follows:
N(y,f,E):=\#\{x\in E:f(x)=y\}
. Similarly, we define

the multiplicity function
of
f
:

N(f,E)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}N(y,f,E).

In
[
KMV
, Theorem 4.1]
, see also condition (3) in
[
Äk
]
, the authors impose on a quasiregular mapping
f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
the following multiplicity growth condition:

(
{\rm M}_{0}
)

N(f,B(0,r))\leq\frac{C}{(1-r)^{a}}\quad\hbox{for all }0<r<1,

for some constant
C>0
and an exponent
a\in[0,n-1)
. For bounded quasiregular mappings such growth restriction allows to show the existence of radial limits almost everywhere on the unit sphere, see
[
MR
]
; see also
[
Vu2
, Ch. IV]
and
[
Ri
, Ch. VII]
for further results and references.

In order to study the nontangential maximal function for quasiregular mappings we introduce in
[
AG2
]
a stronger condition on the multiplicity of the function. Namely, we require estimate (

M
0

) to hold not only for balls centered at zero, but for any hyperbolic ball in
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. To this end, let
T_{x}
denote the self-conformal map in
B
, such that
T_{x}(x)=0
, see (
8
), and let
B_{\rho}(x,r)
stand for the hyperbolic ball defined by
B_{\rho}(x,r):=T_{x}^{-1}(B(0,r))
. We refer the reader to e.g.
[
Vu2
, Ch. I.2]
for more information on the properties of hyperbolic balls and their relations with Euclidean balls.

Definition 2.1

(Definition 1.1 in
[
AG2
]
)
.

We say that a quasiregular mapping
f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
satisfies
the multiplicity condition (M)
, if there exist constant
C>0
and an exponent
a\in[0,n-1)
, such that

(M)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}}N(f,B_{\rho}(x,r))\leq\frac{C}{(1-r)^{a}}\quad\hbox{for all }0<r<1.

In particular, for
x=0
we retrieve condition (

M
0

). Notice that since the Möbius transformations of a unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
are the isometries of
\mathbb{B}^{n}
considered with respect to the hyperbolic metric, it holds that
T_{x}^{-1}(B(0,r))
equals to an Euclidean ball whose center and radius can be computed directly, see (2.22)-(2.24) in
[
Vu2
, Ch. I.2]
. In a consequence, condition (
M
) reads:

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}}N(f\circ T_{x}^{-1},B(0,r))

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}}N(f,B_{\rho}(x,r))

(4)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}}N\left(f,B\left(\frac{x(1-r^{2})}{1-|x|^{2}r^{2}},\frac{(1-|x|^{2})r}{1-|x|^{2}r^{2}}\right)\right)\leq\frac{C}{(1-r)^{a}}.

Let us analyze the behaviour of
R
, the radius of the (Euclidean) ball in (
4
), as the function of
|x|
and
r
:

\bullet
for any fixed
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
we have that
R\to 1
, as
r\to 1
and
R\to 0
, as
r\to 0
, and thus the growth of
N
is similar in nature to the condition (

M
0

);

\bullet
for a fixed
r
, it holds that
R\to r
, when
|x|\to 0
and the growth of
N
is again similar to (

M
0

).

However, the nature of the condition (
M
) differs from the condition (

M
0

) for the (Euclidean) hyperbolic balls, as the following observation shows. Recall that Lemma 2.2 in
[
AG2
]
asserts that if
0<\lambda<2(\sqrt{2}-1)\approx 0,83
, then

\lambda B_{x}\subset T_{x}^{-1}B\left(0,\left(\lambda/2\right)^{2}+\lambda\right):=B_{\rho}\left(x,\left(\lambda/2\right)^{2}+\lambda\right).

Therefore, by the (
M
) condition we have for all
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
0<\lambda<2(\sqrt{2}-1)
that

(5)

1\leq N(f,\lambda B_{x})\leq\frac{C}{(1-(\lambda/2)^{2}-\lambda)^{n-1}},

due to
a<n-1
. On the other hand, if
\lambda\in(0,2)
then for each
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
we can cover
\lambda B_{x}
by balls
\{\frac{1}{2}B_{x_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, where
N
only depends on
n
and
\lambda
, see the covering Lemma
3.6
. Then, by the subadditivity of the multiplicity for finite coverings and (
5
) applied to balls
\frac{1}{2}B_{x_{i}}
, we get

(6)

1\leq N(f,\lambda B_{x})\leq C(\lambda,n).

Remark 2.1
.

If
\lambda
approaches
2
, then in the above estimate the constant
C(n,\lambda)
grows unbounded, since then the cardinality

of the
\lambda B_{x}
covering grows. Indeed, the closer to
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}
the ball
\lambda B_{x}
is, the smaller the covering balls become and, thus,

grows large.

Miniowitz class (
Min
).
Let us now recall the following estimate by Miniowitz and the related class of mappings, see
[
AG2
]
for more details. Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be such that

(7)

E\cap S^{n-1}(r)\not=\emptyset,\quad\hbox{for all }r\geq 0.

Examples of sets

encompass an unbounded curve starting at the origin and the nonnegative part of the

-axis in

for any

.

Theorem 2.2

(Theorem 3 in
[
Mn
]
)

Let
f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus E
be a
K
-quasiregular mapping for set

(Min)

\frac{1}{C}\left(\frac{1-r}{1+r}\right)^{m}\leq\frac{|f(y)|}{|f(0)|}\leq C\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right)^{m},\quad r=|y|,

where
m:=2^{n-1}K_{I}
and
C:=2^{8m}
are constants depending only on the inner distortion
K_{I}
and
n
.

Moreover, Theorem
2.2
has counterparts for quasiregular mappings with bounded multiplicity omitting the origin and spherically mean
1
-valent quasiregular mappings, see
[
Mn
]
. Observe that all of these classes of quasiregular functions are invariant by conformal self-maps
T_{x}
of the unit ball. This observation motivates the following definition of the class of mappings.

Definition 2.2

(Definition 1.2 in
[
AG2
]
)
.

We say that a
K
-quasiregular mapping
f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
is in the
Miniowitz class
, denoted by
{\rm(Min)}
, if for any
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
, the quasiregular map
g_{x}:=f\circ T_{x}^{-1}
satisfies the growth estimate (
Min
) with some positive constants
C
and
m
depending only on
n
and
K
.

We also recall if a quasiregular mapping
f
satisfies condition (
M
) and is in the Miniowitz class (
Min
), then
g_{x}:=f\circ T_{x}^{-1}
satisfies the multiplicity condition (

M
0

), i.e. the multiplicity condition (
M

satisfies the growth estimate (
Min
).

It is worthy noticing that the class of quasiregular mappings in Miniowitz class is rich and enclosses the following examples:

(1)

f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
with bounded multiplicity,

(2)

f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus E

(3)

f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and is spherically mean

.

Growth condition.
Following the discussion in
[
AG2
]
we recall the following

on a quasiregular mapping

|f(x)|\leq\frac{C}{(1-|x|)^{\beta}}.

Observation 3.1 in

Radial limits.
We define the

\tilde{f}
of a mapping
f
as follows. Let
f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then

\tilde{f}(\omega):=\lim_{r\to 1}f(r\omega)\quad\hbox{for }\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

whenever this limit exists. Theorem 4.1 in
[

]
asserts that a

.
Notice further, that since existence of the non-tangential limit is independent of choice of a curve nontangentially approaching the given boundary point, we can reduce our investigations to the radial limits only and justify definition of
\tilde{f}
.

Non-tangential maximal functions.
If
f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is any map, then the

\mathcal{N}_{\alpha}f(\omega):=\sup_{x\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(\omega)}|f(x)|,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

where

We are now in a position to recall Theorem 1.1 in

Theorem 2.3
.

Let

.

BMO spaces.
The space of functions with bounded mean oscillations (BMO) plays an important role in the harmonic analysis, for example due to their duality to the real Hardy space

[

In our work the BMO spaces will appear in Sections 4 and 5, especially in the context of the boundary BMO spaces, whose definition we now recall, but will not directly appeal to in what follows.

For a given
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
r>0
we set
\Delta(\omega,r):=B(\omega,r)\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, where
B(\omega,r)
is a ball in

equipped with the surface measure
\sigma
in

\sup_{\Delta(x,r)}\frac{1}{\sigma(\Delta(x,r))}\int_{\Delta(x,r)}|v(y)-v_{\Delta(x,r)}|\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)<\infty,

where
v_{\Delta(x,r)}
denotes the mean-value of a function
v:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
on
\Delta(x,r)
, i.e.
v_{\Delta(x,r)}:=\mathop{\mathchoice{\kern 1.28333pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.78336pt}{\kern 1.16664pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.66666pt}{\kern 1.02428pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.07292pt}\kern-4.09721pt}{\kern 0.96526pt\vbox{\hrule width=2.89586pt}\kern-3.86111pt}}\mathopen{}\int_{\Delta(x,r)}v(y)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)
.

3.
Characterization of Hardy spaces via the averaged derivative

The main goal of this section is to discuss and prove Theorem
1.1
, a characterization of the
L^{p}
-integrability of
the non-tangential limit map
of a quasiregular mapping in terms of its
averaged derivative
; see also Corollary
1.2
for the related characterization of the Hardy spaces. Therefore, we generalize the corresponding result for quasiconformal mappings, see Theorem 5.1 in
[
AK
]
. In order to present a slightly wider perspective on main results of this section, let us recall that they belong to the line of research in harmonic analysis studying relations between the geometry of the domain, the
L^{p}
-integrability of boundary data for the harmonic Dirichlet problem and the
N\approx S
estimates for the square function/nontangential maximal function. See, for instance, Section 1 in
[
HMM
]
and Theorem 1.1 in
[
AHMMT
]
for the case of Lipschitz domains and domains with uniformly rectifiable boundary, respectively.

In Section 3.1 we discuss some difficulties related to showing the Harnack estimates for the operators
\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda}
and the role of the finite multiplicity of mappings in proving such estimates. Then, in Section 3.2 we discuss various pointwise Harnack inequalities for the averaged operators
\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda}
without the multiplicity conditions and even without the quasiregularity assumption. Sections 3.3 and 3.4 are devoted to the quantitative (integral) versions of the Harnack estimates for
\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda}
, see Theorem
3.7
, Lemma
3.9
and Corollary
3.10
. Moreover, we also discuss partial Koebe distortion theorems in Lemma
3.11
and in Remark
3.13
. Finally, Section 3.5 contains the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

3.1.
Harnack inequalities: motivations and the finite multiplicity case

Let us recall that one of the basic tools employed throughout the work
[
AK
]
are the following results:

(1) Lemma 2.1, giving the equivalence between the diameter of the image of a hyperbolic ball under a quasiconformal map
f
and the distance to the boundary of the target space, and

(2) Lemma 2.3, the Koebe distortion theorem for
a_{f}
(as defined in (
1
)), which plays an important role in understanding the behavior of
a_{f}
and controlling its values near the boundary (see also
[
AsGe1
, Theorem 1.8]
).

An important consequence of these results is the Harnack inequality for
a_{f}
when
f
is quasiconformal, i.e.

a_{f}(x)\simeq_{n,K}a_{f}(y),\,\hbox{ for each }x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}\hbox{ and }y\in B_{x}.

For quasiregular maps we get a weaker Harnack estimate, see Lemma
3.2
below. In fact, the result is obtained under mild assumptions on
f
in order to emphasize the generality of
\operatorname{a}_{f}
and its potential broad applications. However, as we will discuss in Corollary
3.1
, a stronger Harnack estimate is available provided that a quasiregular map
f
has finite multiplicity
N(f,\mathbb{B}^{n})<\infty
.

Let us briefly argue that the Harnack inequality for more general maps with
N(f,\mathbb{B}^{n})=\infty
appears to be a more delicate problem. Recall, that the proof of the Koebe distortion theorem in
[
AsGe1
]
relies on the BMO estimate for
\log J_{f}
, where
J_{f}
denotes the Jacobian of the quasiconformal map, which in turn is related to the Muckenhoupt condition for
J_{f}
and, therefore, to the Harnack estimate for the average derivative
a_{f}
. As observed in Section 2.4(c) in
[
BKR
]
and in Remark 3.6 in
[
HK
]
, a Harnack estimate for quasiregular maps (in particular, in the lack of the injectivity), follows from two facts:

(1) the doubling property of the weighted measure
\mathrm{d}\mu=J_{f}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}
, and

(2) the finite multiplicity assumption on
f
, i.e.
N(f,\mathbb{B}^{n})<\infty
.

Recall that, for instance by 15.5 and 15.7 in
[
HKM
]
, the Muckenhoupt condition
J_{f}\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
implies the doubling property for
\mu
on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
(but only for such balls
B\subset\mathbb{B}^{n}
with
B\subset 2B\subset\mathbb{B}^{n}
). Furthermore, by the discussion in
[
HK
, Remark 3.6(b)]
, the same condition
J_{f}\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
also implies the uniform boundedness of the local index of
f
, but the map
g(z)=e^{z}
in the plane is a counterexample that the opposite implication need not hold and, moreover, observe that
N(g,\mathbb{R}^{2})=\infty
. On the other hand, direct computations imply the Harnack estimate for
\operatorname{a}_{g}
in
\mathbb{B}^{n}
.

Toward a Koebe estimate in the setting of quasiregular mappings, it turns out that a careful scrutiny of the proof of Theorem 1.5 in
[
HK
]
leads to the following proposition, a counterpart of the formula (3.7) in
[
HK
]
.

Observation 3.1
.

If
f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a
K
-quasiregular map with bounded multiplicity
N(f,\mathbb{B}^{n})<\infty
, then the Koebe estimate holds for all
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
0<\lambda\leq 1
:

(9)

\lambda^{c(n)}\frac{\operatorname{diam}f(B_{x})}{1-|x|}\lesssim\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda}(x)\lesssim\frac{\operatorname{diam}f(B_{x})}{1-|x|}.

The comparison constants in both inequalities depend on
n,K,N(f,\mathbb{B}^{n})
and, moreover,
c(n)>1
.

Proof.

In order to show the claim, we first note that as on pages 259-260 in
[
HK
]
one can show that for
1\leq t<2
it holds that

|f(tB_{x})|\leq C(n,K,t)|f(B_{x})|,

and so

(10)

\int_{tB_{x}}J_{f}\leq C(n,K,t)\int_{B_{x}}J_{f},

due to the bounded multiplicity and the quasiregular change of variables formula. Next, we fix
1<t<2
and by the Sobolev embedding theorem and the reverse Hölder inequality we get for
y\in B_{x}
the following estimate

\displaystyle|f(y)-f(x)|\lesssim_{n,K}\operatorname{diam}B_{x}\left(\mathop{\mathchoice{\kern 1.28333pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.78336pt}{\kern 1.16664pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.66666pt}{\kern 1.02428pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.07292pt}\kern-4.09721pt}{\kern 0.96526pt\vbox{\hrule width=2.89586pt}\kern-3.86111pt}}\mathopen{}\int_{tB_{x}}J_{f}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,K,N(f,\mathbb{B}^{n}),t,\lambda}\frac{2}{\lambda}\operatorname{diam}B_{x}\left(\mathop{\mathchoice{\kern 1.28333pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.78336pt}{\kern 1.16664pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.66666pt}{\kern 1.02428pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.07292pt}\kern-4.09721pt}{\kern 0.96526pt\vbox{\hrule width=2.89586pt}\kern-3.86111pt}}\mathopen{}\int_{\lambda B_{x}}J_{f}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\lambda,n,K,N(f,\mathbb{B}^{n}),t}\frac{1}{\lambda}(1-|x|)\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda}(x),

and so, the left hand-side in estimate (
9
) follows. In fact, the dependence on
\lambda
can be found more explicitly, again by computing the appropriate moduli of curve families at the end of the proof of
[
HK
, Theorem 1.5]
. Namely, it turns out that in (
9
) we have
\lambda^{c}
for the constant
c=c(n)>1
.

The right-hand side estimate follows immediately from the definition of
\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda}
and the quasiregular change of variables formula as follows:

\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda}^{n}(x)\leq\frac{K}{|2B_{x}|}\int_{f(B_{x})}N(f,B_{x})\leq\frac{KN(f,B_{x})}{|2B_{x}|}\,\big(\operatorname{diam}f(B_{x})\big)^{n}.

∎

An important consequence of the observation is the Harnack estimate for the averaged derivative, whose proof follows from the doubling property (
10
).

Corollary 3.1
.

If
f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a
K
-quasiregular map with bounded multiplicity
N(f,\mathbb{B}^{n})<\infty
, then for all
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
,
0<\lambda\leq 1
and
y\in\frac{\lambda}{3}B_{x}
, it holds that:

\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda}(x)\approx\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda}(y).

The comparison constant depends on
n,K,N(f,\mathbb{B}^{n})
and
\lambda
.

Proof.

If
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
y\in\frac{\lambda}{3}B_{x}
, then the following estimations give that
\lambda B_{x}\subset\lambda_{2}B_{y}
for
\lambda_{2}:=\frac{8\lambda}{6-\lambda}
. Indeed, we have for
z\in\lambda B_{x}
that

|z-y|\leq|z-x|+|x-y|\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}(1-|x|)+\frac{\lambda}{6}(1-|x|)\leq\frac{8\lambda}{6-\lambda}\frac{1}{2}(1-|y|),

as

1-|x|\leq|x-y|+1-|y|<\frac{\lambda}{6}(1-|x|)+1-|y|\Rightarrow 1-|x|\leq\frac{6}{6-\lambda}(1-|y|).

Then, the doubling property of the Jacobian (
10
) implies the following

\displaystyle\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda}^{n}(x)\leq\frac{K}{|2B_{x}|}\int_{\lambda B_{x}}J_{f}(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K}{|2B_{x}|}\int_{\frac{8\lambda}{6-\lambda}B_{y}}J_{f}(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,K}\frac{1}{|2B_{x}|}\int_{\frac{4\lambda}{6-\lambda}B_{y}}J_{f}(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,K}\frac{1}{|2B_{x}|}\int_{\lambda B_{y}}|Df(z)|^{n}\mathrm{d}z\approx_{n,K}\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda}^{n}(y).

The opposite estimate follows from the similar reasoning upon noticing that
\lambda B_{y}\subset\frac{\lambda}{3}(4+\frac{\lambda}{2})B_{x}\subset\frac{3}{2}\lambda B_{x}
, since for
z\in\lambda B_{y}
and
y\in\frac{\lambda}{3}B_{x}
it holds that

|z-x|\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}(1-|y|)+\frac{\lambda}{6}(1-|x|)\leq\frac{\lambda}{6}\Big[1+3\Big(\frac{\lambda}{6}+1\Big)\Big](1-|x|).

∎

3.2.
Harnack inequalities for averaged derivatives of differentiable mappings

In this section we discuss several Harnack estimates for the differential operators
\operatorname{a}_{f}
under mild assumption on the map
f
, i.e. without requiring quasiregularity of
f
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let map
f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be such that its Jacoby matrix satisfies
{D\!f\in L^{n}_{loc}(\mathbb{B}^{n})}
. Then for any
\lambda\in(0,1/2)
there exist constants
C_{H}=C_{H}(n,\lambda)>0
and
0<\lambda_{1}<1<\lambda_{2}<2
, with
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
depending only on
\lambda
, such that for all
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
y\in\lambda B_{x}

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{H}}\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda_{1}}(y)\leq\operatorname{a}_{f}(x)\leq C_{H}\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda_{2}}(y),

(12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{H}}\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda_{1}}(x)\leq\operatorname{a}_{f}(y)\leq C_{H}\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda_{2}}(x).

Moreover,
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
in fact can be chosen so that
0<\lambda_{1}<1-\frac{3}{2}\lambda
and
\frac{2+2\lambda}{2-\lambda}<\lambda_{2}<2
.

Let us remark that both the right-hand side estimates in Lemma
3.2
will be used in what follows: (
11
) in Theorem
1.1
and (
12
) to obtain Corollary
3.3
(b).

Proof.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
y\in\lambda B_{x}
. If
z\in\lambda_{1}B_{y}
for some
\lambda_{1}>0
, then by the triangle inequality and since
1-|y|\leq d(y,x_{min})
, where
x_{min}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
stands for a point realizing the distance
\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathbb{S}^{n-1})=d(x,x_{min})
, we have the following estimate

\displaystyle|x-z|

\displaystyle\leq|x-y|+|y-z|

\displaystyle<\frac{\lambda}{2}(1-|x|)+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}(1-|y|)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}(1-|x|)+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}(|x-y|+(1-|x|))

\displaystyle<\frac{\lambda}{2}(1-|x|)+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}+1\right)(1-|x|)=\left[\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}+1\right)+\frac{\lambda}{2}\right](1-|x|).

Therefore, we have shown that
z\in B_{x}
, provided that
\left[\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}+1\right)+\frac{\lambda}{2}\right]<\frac{1}{2}
, i.e., when

\lambda_{1}<\frac{2-2\lambda}{2+\lambda}\in\left(\frac{2}{5},1\right).

Hence, we get that, regardless of the value of
\lambda
, for any
\lambda_{1}\leq\frac{2}{3}
it holds that
\lambda_{1}B_{y}\subset B_{x}
.

Now suppose
z\in B_{x}
. Then

|y-z|\leq|y-x|+|x-z|<\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)(1-|x|).

On the other hand

1-|x|\leq|x-y|+1-|y|<\frac{\lambda}{2}(1-|x|)+1-|y|\Rightarrow 1-|x|\leq\frac{2}{2-\lambda}(1-|y|).

As consequence, we get
|y-z|<\frac{2+2\lambda}{2-\lambda}\frac{1}{2}(1-|y|)
, proving that
B_{x}\subset\lambda_{2}B_{y}
for any

(13)

\lambda_{2}\geq\frac{2+2\lambda}{2-\lambda}\in\left(1,2\right).

Let
C_{d}
be the doubling constant of the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle|2B_{y}|\geq|\lambda_{2}B_{y}|\geq|B_{x}|\geq C_{d}|2B_{x}|,

\displaystyle|2B_{x}|\geq|B_{x}|\geq|\lambda_{1}B_{y}|\geq C_{d}^{j(\lambda_{1})}|2B_{y}|,

where
j(\lambda_{1})
is the smallest integer such that
2B_{y}\subset 2^{j(\lambda_{1})}\lambda_{1}B_{y}
. From this we easily conclude the first assertion (
11
) of the lemma, namely:

\frac{1}{C_{d}^{1/n}}\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda_{1}}(y)\leq\operatorname{a}_{f}(x)\leq C_{d}^{\frac{j(\lambda_{1})}{n}}\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda_{2}}(y).

Similarly we show the second Harnack inequality in (
12
). Let
z\in\tilde{\lambda}_{1}B_{x}
for some
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}>0
. Then

|y-z|\leq|y-x|+|x-z|<\frac{\lambda+\tilde{\lambda}_{1}}{2}(1-|x|)<\frac{\lambda+\tilde{\lambda}_{1}}{2-\lambda}(1-|y|),

proving that
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}B_{x}\subset B_{y}
whenever
\frac{\lambda+\tilde{\lambda}_{1}}{2-\lambda}<\frac{1}{2}
, i.e.,

\tilde{\lambda}_{1}<\frac{2-3\lambda}{2}\in\left(\frac{1}{4},1\right).

Finally, suppose that
z\in B_{y}
. Then

\displaystyle|x-z|

\displaystyle\leq|x-y|+|y-z|

\displaystyle<\frac{\lambda}{2}(1-|x|)+\frac{1}{2}(1-|y|)\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}(1-|x|)+\frac{1}{2}(|x-y|+(1-|x|))

\displaystyle<\frac{\lambda}{2}(1-|x|)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}+1\right)(1-|x|)=\frac{1}{4}(3\lambda+2)(1-|x|).

Hence,
B_{y}\subset\tilde{\lambda}_{2}B_{x}
, provided that

\tilde{\lambda}_{2}\geq\frac{3\lambda+2}{2}\in\left(1,\frac{5}{4}\right).

Thus, we arrive at the estimate (
12
)

\frac{1}{C_{d}^{j(\lambda_{1})/n}}\operatorname{a}_{f,\tilde{\lambda}_{1}}(x)\leq\operatorname{a}_{f}(y)\leq C_{d}^{1/n}\operatorname{a}_{f,\tilde{\lambda}_{2}}(x).

In order to unify constants in both Harnack estimates we set
C_{H}:=C_{d}^{j(\lambda_{1})/n}
and notice that
C_{H}
only depends on
n
and
\lambda_{1}
(thus, indirectly on
\lambda
). Moreover,

(14)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}:=\min\{\lambda_{1},\tilde{\lambda}_{1}\}<\min\left\{\frac{2-2\lambda}{2+\lambda},\frac{2-3\lambda}{2}\right\}=\frac{2-3\lambda}{2},

(15)

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}:=\max\{\lambda_{2},\tilde{\lambda}_{2}\}>\max\left\{\frac{2+2\lambda}{2-\lambda},\frac{3\lambda+2}{2}\right\}=\frac{2+2\lambda}{2-\lambda}.

∎

Corollary 3.3

Let map

be such that its Jacoby matrix satisfies
{D\!f\in L^{n}_{loc}(\mathbb{B}^{n})}

Then the following Harnack estimates hold for the average derivatives

(a)

For every

and

it holds for all

(16)

\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda}(x)\leq\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda_{1}}(y).

(b)

For every

there exist parameters
\lambda\in(0,1/2)
and
\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{2}^{\prime}

and

\operatorname{a}_{f}(y)\lesssim\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda_{2}}(x)\lesssim\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda_{2}^{\prime}}(y).

(c)

For every

there exist parameters
\lambda\in(0,1/2)
and
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{1}^{\prime}
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
and

\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda_{1}^{\prime}}(y)\lesssim\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda_{1}}(x)\lesssim\operatorname{a}_{f}(y).

Proof.

Part (a).
In order to prove assertion (
16
) let us fix
\lambda_{1}>0
. Let further

and
\lambda,\lambda_{1}^{\prime}\in(0,1)
for
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}
depending on

.
Thus, in order to get that

.

Part (b).
Let

and
\lambda_{2}\in(1,2)

\lambda_{2}
depends on

Part (c).

3.3.
Reverse quantitative Harnack inequality for averaged derivative

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
3.7
, an integral variant of the Harnack estimate for
\operatorname{a}_{f}
, which we call the
reverse quantitative Harnack inequality
. The justification of this name comes from the fact that in the assertion of Theorem
3.7
the parameter
\lambda_{2}
in
\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda_{2}}
on the left-hand side is larger than
\lambda_{1}
in
\operatorname{a}_{f,\lambda_{1}}
appearing on the right-hand side of the estimate. Theorem
3.7
leads to a handy integral estimates relating powers of norms
|Df|
and
\operatorname{a}_{f}
, see Corollary
3.10
, and is also employed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Moreover, as remarked after the statement of the theorem, it holds without the quasiregularity assumption on the map.

In order to improve the clarity of the proof of the theorem, we first show the following three auxiliary lemmas.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any point
z\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
\lambda_{2}\in(1,2)
there exist
\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(\lambda_{2})\in(0,\frac{2}{3}),\tau_{1}=\tau_{1}(\tau_{0},\lambda_{2})\in(1,2)
, both independent of
z
, such that for every
x\in\tau_{0}B_{z}
it holds that
\lambda_{2}B_{x}\subset\tau_{1}B_{z}
.

Moreover, for each
\kappa
such that
\tau_{1}<\kappa<2
there exists
\eta=\eta(\tau_{0},\kappa)
such that
\eta B_{x}\subset\kappa B_{z}
for all
x\in\tau_{1}B_{z}
.

The lemma is the key auxiliary tool for Theorem
3.7
and captures the following geometric observations (see also Figure
1
):

•

First assertion of the lemma says that for a given point
z\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
we can find a slightly (smaller) hyperbolic ball
\tau_{0}B_{z}
contained in the hyperbolic ball
B_{z}
, such that when considering hyperbolic balls
\lambda_{2}B_{x}
centered at points in that smaller ball
\tau_{0}B_{z}
, as close to
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}
as possible (since
\lambda_{2}
is allowed to be close to
2
), there is a (larger) hyperbolic ball
\tau_{1}B_{z}
that contains all balls
\lambda_{2}B_{x}
. Moreover, constants
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
can be chosen uniformly with respect to points
z
and
x
. Observe further, that
\tau_{0}
is chosen to be sufficiently small in order to handle the case when the ball
\tau_{0}B_{z}
contains points
x
close to the origin, and so the ball
\lambda_{2}B_{x}
could a priori be large.

•

We can enlarge the above ball
\tau_{1}B_{z}
to the ball
\kappa B_{z}
, which can be as close to
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}
as possible and, similarly as above, be able to find that hyperbolic balls
\eta B_{x}
are contained in
\kappa B_{z}
, now for points
x
in the (larger) hyperbolic ball
\tau_{1}B_{z}
. Furthermore, the constant
\eta
is independent of
z
and
x
.

Let

\displaystyle\leq|y-x|+|x-z|\leq\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}(1-|x|)+\frac{\tau_{0}}{2}(1-|z|)\leq\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}(|x-z|+1-|z|)+\frac{\tau_{0}}{2}(1-|z|)

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
,
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
y,z\in\lambda B_{x}
. Then
y\in 4\lambda B_{z}
.

Proof.

Let
y,z\in\lambda B_{x}
. It holds that

1-|x|\leq|x-z|+1-|z|\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}(1-|x|)+1-|z|,\,\,\hbox{and so}\,\,1-|x|\leq\frac{2}{2-\lambda}(1-|z|).

.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in(0,2)
. Then, there exists a finite covering of the ball
\eta_{1}B_{x}
by balls
\{\eta_{2}B_{x_{i}(x)}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, where
N
depends only on
n,\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
.

Proof.

By applying metrically doubling condition
m

\eta_{1}B_{x}
can be covered by the following collection of

B_{i}(x):=B\left(x_{i}(x),2^{-m-1}\eta_{1}(1-|x|)\right),\quad x_{i}(x)\in\eta_{1}B_{x}\quad\hbox{ for }1\leq i\leq 2^{mn}.

Since

, the similar argument as in the proof of the previous two lemmas gives the following estimate

1-|x|\leq|x-x_{i}(x)|+1-|x_{i}(x)|\leq\frac{\eta_{1}}{2}(1-|x|)+1-|x_{i}(x)|,\hbox{ and hence }1-|x|\leq\frac{2}{2-\eta_{1}}(1-|x_{i}(x)|).

.

.

Let

.

We remark that Theorem
3.7
holds under the weaker assumption, due to Lemma

{D\!f\in L^{n}_{loc}(\mathbb{B}^{n})}

Let function

3.4.

Lemma 3.9

\lambda_{2}^{\prime}\in(1,2)
and for some

we have that for any

and

3.6.
The Riesz theorem

The classical Riesz conjugate function theorem for an analytic function in the plane asserts it belongs to the Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{p}
for
{1<p<\infty}
if and only if its real part belongs to
\mathcal{H}^{p}
. The Riesz theorem fails for quasiconformal mappings, see
[
AK
, Section 6]
. However, it has counterparts for the harmonic quasiconformal mappings in the plane, see Theorems 1.1-1.2 in
[
LZ
]
, and for invariant harmonic quasiregular mappings in
\mathbb{B}^{n}
, see Theorem 1.3 in
[
LZ
]
. Nevertheless, a theorem by Burkholder–Gundy–Silverstein
[
BGS
]
shows that the
L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
-norm of a harmonic conjugate function
v
of a harmonic one
u
is bounded by the
L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
-norm of
\mathcal{N}(u)
, up to a constant depending only on
p>0
. Similar result holds for quasiconformal mappings in
\mathbb{B}^{n}
, see Theorem 6.1 in
[
AK
]
. The following observation is a counterpart of this result and follows from Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 3.17
.

Let
f=(f^{1},\ldots,f^{n}):\mathbb{B}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
a
K
-quasiregular mapping satisfying (
M
) and (
Min
); moreover, let the multiplicity of
f
in ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
be finite
N(f,\mathbb{B}^{n})<\infty
. Then
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p}
if and only if
\mathcal{N}(f^{i})\in L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
for one of the component functions of
f
and
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

If
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p}
, then
\mathcal{N}(f)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
by Theorem
2.3
and, hence, also
\mathcal{N}(f^{i})\in L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.

For the opposite implication, let
\mathcal{N}(f^{i})\in L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
for some
i=1,\ldots,n
. Since
|f^{i}(x)|\leq\mathcal{N}(f^{i})(\omega)
for all
x\in\Gamma(\omega)
, it holds for
t>0
that

\mathop{\mathchoice{\kern 1.28333pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.78336pt}{\kern 1.16664pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.66666pt}{\kern 1.02428pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.07292pt}\kern-4.09721pt}{\kern 0.96526pt\vbox{\hrule width=2.89586pt}\kern-3.86111pt}}\mathopen{}\int_{tB_{x}}|f^{i}(y)|^{n}\mathrm{d}y\leq\mathcal{N}(f^{i})(\omega)^{n},

for any ball
tB_{x}\subset\Gamma_{\alpha}(\omega)
with
\omega:=\frac{x}{|x|}
,
\alpha>2
and all
1<t\leq\frac{2\alpha}{2+\alpha}
. The latter follows immediately from the definition of the non-tangential region, since if

, then

|y-\omega|\leq|y-x|+|x-\omega|\leq(1+\frac{t}{2})(1-|x|)\leq\frac{2+t}{2-t}(1-|y|)\leq(1+\alpha)(1-|y|).

On the other hand, the Caccioppoli inequality for component functions of a quasiregular map, see
[
No1
, Proposition 2.6]
, gives that
for almost all
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}

(41)

\int_{B_{x}}|Df|^{n}\leq C(n,K)\left(\frac{t}{t-1}\right)^{n}\mathop{\mathchoice{\kern 1.28333pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.78336pt}{\kern 1.16664pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.66666pt}{\kern 1.02428pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.07292pt}\kern-4.09721pt}{\kern 0.96526pt\vbox{\hrule width=2.89586pt}\kern-3.86111pt}}\mathopen{}\int_{tB_{x}}|f^{i}|^{n}\lesssim_{n,K,t}\mathcal{N}(f^{i})(\omega)^{n},

The estimate (
41

\operatorname{a}_{f}(x)(1-|x|)\leq C(n,K,t)\mathcal{N}(f^{i})(\omega),

which in turn gives that
\mathcal{N}(\operatorname{a}_{f}(x)(1-|x|))\in L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
for all

. Therefore, by Theorem
1.1
and the assumption

we have that assertion (3) implies (1), meaning that

and hence

by Theorem
2.3
.
∎

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
be a
K
-quasiregular mapping satisfying the multiplicity condition (

M
0

) with some

. Moreover, let us assume that

satisfies the growth condition (

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|Df(x)|^{n}(1-|x|)^{n-1+a}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c<\infty,

Furthermore, if instead of condition (

Proof.

Let us represent the unit ball

R_{j}:=\{x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}:1-2^{-j}\leq|x|\leq 1-2^{-(j+1)}\}\,\hbox{ for }j=0,1,\ldots.

Then, by the distortion inequality and the quasiregular change of variables it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|Df(x)|^{n}(1-|x|)^{n-1+a}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\phantom{AAAA}\lesssim_{n,K,a}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j(n-1+a)}N(f,B(0,1-2^{-j}))|f(R_{j})|.

(49)

|f(R_{j})|\leq|f(B(0,1-2^{-j})|\leq C2^{j\beta}.

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|Df(x)|^{n}(1-|x|)^{n-1+a}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim_{K,a,n}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j(n-1+a)}2^{ja}C2^{j\beta}\lesssim_{K,a,n}C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j(n-1-\beta)}.

6.
Applications to elliptic PDEs

The purpose of this section is to briefly address how some of the above results transfer to the properties of elliptic PDEs in the plane and beyond it. Our discussion serves as an illustration of relations between PDEs and quasiregular mappings.

6.1.
Case
n=2

Recall that if
u\in W^{2,2}_{loc}(\Omega)
is a solution to the planar uniformly elliptic PDE with bounded measurable coefficients
a,b,c
, i.e.
Lu=au_{xx}+2bu_{xy}+cu_{yy}=0
, then its complex gradient
f:=u_{x}-{\rm i}u_{y}=(u_{x},-u_{y})
is a
K
-quasiregular mapping with
K=K(\lambda,\Lambda)
, where
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
are the bounds in the uniform ellipticity condition for
L
. Moreover, the opposite relation holds as well, namely a
K
-quasiregular map defines a uniformly elliptic operator
L
, see
[
GT
, Chapter 12]
and
[
Mn
, Theorem 3]
. Let us also add that the Stoilow factorization and the theory of generalized analytic functions, see
[
V
]
and
[
BJS
, Chapter 6]
, allow to observe that the complex gradient
f
is quasiregular also for
W^{1,p}_{loc}
-solutions of elliptic equations in the divergence form
\mathcal{L}u={\rm div}(A(x,y)\nabla u)=0
, see e.g.
[
Mg
, Section 2.3]
and even for degenerate quasilinear equations, such as the
p
-harmonic one, see
[
Mn
,
AL
]
and
[
AIM
, Chapter 16]
.

In order to apply our results to a quasiregular map
f
given by the complex gradient of the elliptic equation, we need to ensure the three conditions to hold:

(1)
f\not=0
,

(2) the multiplicity condition (
M
), and

(3) the Miniowitz condition (
Min
).

The first condition reads that the gradient
\nabla u\not=0
in
\mathbb{B}^{2}
.

Upon applying the Stoilow factorization, we obtain that
f=h\circ g
for holomorphic function
h
and quasiconformal change of variables
g
in
\mathbb{B}^{2}
, and so by Definition
2.1
the multiplicity condition reads for all
0<r<1
and all
x\in\mathbb{B}^{2}

N(f,B_{\rho}(x,r))=N(h\circ g,B_{\rho}(x,r))=N(h,g(B_{\rho}(x,r)))\leq\frac{C}{(1-r)^{a}},\quad 0\leq a<1.

Therefore, the multiplicity conditions transfers naturally to the planar holomorphic function
h
and images of hyperbolic balls under quasiconformal map
g
. Similar growth results appeared in the literature for the value distribution theory, see e.g.
[
L
]
,
[
Ri
, Chapter V]
,
[
Vu2
, Chapter 11]
and
[
Äk
]
.

It amounts to checking the Miniowitz condition, which as presented below follows from the gradient estimates for elliptic PDEs.
Suppose that the coefficients
a,b,c
are Hölder regular in
\mathbb{B}^{2}
for the non-divergence equation
Lu
=0 (respectively, the matrix
A
has

-entries for the above equation in the divergence form
\mathcal{L}u=0
). By the Schauder theory and Theorem 12.4 (or Corollary 6.3) in
[

]
, applied to balls

and

(57)

\frac{|\nabla u(z)|}{|\nabla u(0)|}\leq\frac{C(\lambda,\Lambda)\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}}{(1-|z|)^{s}},\quad s=s(\lambda,\Lambda).

The analogous estimate holds for solutions of the divergence type equation
\mathcal{L}u=0
, see
[
GT
, Theorem 13.1]
.

In order to show the lower part of condition (
Min
) we will appeal to the fact that
\nabla u\not=0
in
\mathbb{B}^{2}
and so we will have that

(58)

\frac{|\nabla u(z)|}{|\nabla u(0)|}\geq C\geq C(1-|z|)^{s},\quad z\in\mathbb{B}^{2},

for the constant

depending on

and the geometry of the space. The estimates similar to (

[
Al2
]
, see pg. 240-242 there and of Theorem 1.3 in
[
Al1
]
, see pg. 273-275 there. By the direct formal computations we find that

is a solution in

of the following equation

L|\nabla u|^{2}={\rm tr}(A\cdot[H(u)^{2}]):=F,

where

is the symmetric matrix with entries

and

stands for the Hessian matrix of

. The justification that

can be evaluated on

follows the same lines as the proofs of Lemma 2.1 and 2.3 in
[
Al2
]
and, therefore, we omit it. Moreover, by the uniform ellipticity condition we have that

As in the proof of
[
Al2
, Theorem 2.1]
, see the paragraph following (2.32) on pg. 241, we appeal to the Morrey inequality, the Schauder estimates for the equation

and get that for balls

it holds

\displaystyle\inf_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|\gtrsim(R-r)^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\|H(u)\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}

for the appropriate choice of

and

and

.
Similar argument gives us also the lower bound for divergence type equations, see the proof of Theorem 1.3 in
[
Al1
]
, pg. 273-275.

As consequence of the above discussion we arrive at the following observations:

(1)

Since the upper estimate in (

]

has non-tangential limits at

exists a.e. and is well-defined on
\mathbb{S}^{1}

(2)

in Theorem

applied to

(3)

M
points
x_{1},\dots,x_{M}
which are
\frac{\eta}{5}d(x_{1})
-separated. Notice that

2^{4+\log_{2}\tau}\frac{\eta}{5}\geq 3\eta\tau
, and thus by
[
HKST
, Lemma 4.1.12]
we obtain
M\leq N^{4+\log_{2}\tau}
, where
N
is the Assuad dimension of
X
.

Consider now the family
\mathcal{W}:=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{W}_{k}
and, as before, assume
x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{M}
are such that
x_{0}\in B(x_{i},\tau\eta d(x_{i}))
and
d(x_{1})\geq d(x_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,M
. Let
k_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
B_{1}\in\mathcal{\mathcal{}}W_{k_{1}}
, and thus we also have that
x_{1}
is a center of a ball in
\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}}
. By (
61
) we also have
d(x_{i})\geq\frac{1-\eta\tau}{1+\eta\tau}d(x_{1})\geq 2^{k_{1}-s-1}
, where
s
is the biggest integer so that
2^{-s}\leq\frac{1-\eta\tau}{1+\eta\tau}
. Then

x_{i}\in\bigcup_{m=0}^{s+1}\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}-m}\;\mbox{ for all }i=1,\dots,M.

This yields that
M\leq(s+1)N^{4+\log_{2}\tau}
, proving the uniformly bounded overlap property.

∎

Appendix B

First, we express the Morrey estimate in terms of averaged derivatives. Then, we prove Lemmas
3.15
and
3.16
, see the paragraph before the statement of Lemma
3.15
for the geometric-analytic meaning of the lemma.

Proposition B.1

(Morrey’s inequality)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{B}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be
K
-quasiregular. Let
0<\eta\leq 1
, then for almost every
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
y\in\frac{\eta}{2}B_{x}
we have

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C(n,K,\eta)(1-|x|)^{\beta}\operatorname{a}_{f,\eta}(x),

for any
\beta\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

The proof follows immediately from
[
BI
, Theorem 5.2]
. In particular, the Sobolev embedding argument and the Gehring estimate for quasiregular mappings with the exponent
p=p(n,K)>n
give the following inequality for
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
y\in\frac{\eta}{2}B_{x}
:

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(y)|

\displaystyle\leq C(n,K)\left(\frac{|x-y|}{\operatorname{diam}(\eta B_{x})}\right)^{1-\frac{n}{p(n,K)}}(1-|x|)\left(\frac{1}{|2B_{x}|}\int_{\eta B_{x}}|Df|^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,K,\eta)(1-|x|)\operatorname{a}_{f,\eta}(x).

Since
1-|x|<1
, the assertion follows from the trivial observation that
1-|x|\leq(1-|x|)^{\beta}
for any
\beta\in[0,1]
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.15
.

First let us prove the estimate at
x=0
, namely that

(62)

\sigma\left(\left\{\omega\in S^{n-1}:|f(0)-\tilde{f}(\omega)|\geq M\mathrm{a}_{f,\eta}(0)\right\}\right)\leq C(n,K,\eta)(\log M)^{1-n}.

Let

E:=\left\{\omega\in S^{n-1}:|f(0)-\tilde{f}(\omega)|\geq M\mathrm{a}_{f,\eta}(0)\right\}.

For
x\in\frac{\eta}{2}B_{0}=B(0,\frac{\eta}{4})
we have, by Proposition
B.1
, that
|f(0)-f(x)|\leq C(n,K,\eta)a_{f,\eta}(0)
, and thus
f(B(0,\frac{\eta}{4}))\subset B(f(0),C\mathrm{a}_{f,\eta}(0))
. Here, for simplicity of the presentation we denote by
C=C(n,K,\eta)
.

On the other hand, by the definition of
E
we have that
f(E)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(f(0),Ma_{f,\eta}(0))
. Suppose first that
M>C
and let

\displaystyle\Gamma_{E}:=\Gamma(B(0,\frac{\eta}{4}),E,\mathbb{B}^{n}),

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\prime}_{E}=f\Gamma_{E}.

By definition
\Gamma^{\prime}_{E}
consists of curves joining points in
f(B(0,\frac{\eta}{4}))
and in
f(E)
. Thus, by the above discussion,
\Gamma^{\prime}_{E}
majorizes the curve family consisting of curves in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with one endpoint in the ball
B(f(0),C\operatorname{a}_{f,\eta}(0))
and the other one in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B(f(0),Ma_{f,\eta}(0))
, in the sense of Definition 6.3 in
[
Va
]
. The latter family of curves contains the spherical ring for
M>C
. Therefore, we have the following modulus estimate

\mathrm{Mod}(\Gamma^{\prime}_{E})\lesssim_{n}(\log\frac{M}{C})^{1-n}\lesssim_{n}(\log M)^{1-n}.

The last inequality holds by the price of increasing admissible
M>C
. However, this is not a restriction for our investigations, as in what follows we will apply Lemma
3.15
only for
M
big enough, see the computations for
\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}
at the end of the proof of Proposition
3.14
.

On the other hand, by the definition of
\Gamma_{E}
we also have the following modulus estimate, cf. Remark 7.7 in
[
Va
]
:

\mathrm{Mod}(\Gamma_{E})=\sigma(E)(\log\frac{4}{\eta})^{1-n}.

Upon combining the above estimates for
\mathrm{Mod}(\Gamma^{\prime}_{E})
and
\mathrm{Mod}(\Gamma_{E})
, together with the modulus definition of quasiregular map and the bound
N(f,\mathbb{B}^{n})\leq N<\infty
, we arrive at the estimate

\displaystyle\sigma(E)(\log\frac{4}{\eta})^{1-n}=\mathrm{Mod}(\Gamma_{E})\leq N\mathrm{Mod}(\Gamma^{\prime}_{E})\lesssim N(\log M)^{1-n}.

Thus,

\sigma(E)\lesssim N(\log\frac{4}{\eta})^{n-1}(\log M)^{1-n}\approx_{n,N,\eta}(\log M)^{1-n}.

For second case, let us assume that
1<M\leq C
(we can always increase constant
C
in Proposition
B.1
, if
C<1
). Then, trivially
1\leq\left(\frac{\log C}{\log M}\right)^{n-1}
and so

\sigma(E)=\int_{E}1\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq\sigma(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})(\log C)^{n-1}(\log M)^{1-n}\approx_{n,C}(\log M)^{1-n}.

Hence, the assertion (
62
) is proven for
x=0
.

Now let
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
and set
g=f\circ T_{x}^{-1}
, which will still have uniformly bounded multiplicity. Since
g(0)=f(x)
, applying the previous case to
g
at the origin and
\tilde{\eta}\in(0,1)
, whose value will be determine later in the proof, we obtain

\sigma\left(\left\{\omega\in S^{n-1}:|f(x)-\tilde{g}(\omega)|\geq M\mathrm{a}_{g,\tilde{\eta}}(0)\right\}\right)\leq C(\log M)^{1-n}.

Moreover, by the definition of
g
this also reads as

(63)

\sigma\left(T_{x}\left(\left\{y\in S(x):|f(x)-\tilde{f}(y)|\geq M\mathrm{a}_{g,\tilde{\eta}}(0)\right\}\right)\right)\leq C(\log M)^{1-n},

since we directly check that given
\omega=T_{x}(y)
, a point in the set on the left-hand side above, for

M\mathrm{a}_{g,\tilde{\eta}}(0)\leq|f(x)-\tilde{f}(y)|=|f(x)-\tilde{f}(T_{x}^{-1}(\omega))|=|f(x)-\tilde{g}(\omega)|.

On the other hand, the following relation holds

\mathrm{a}_{g,\tilde{\eta}}(0)\approx_{\eta}(1-|x|)\mathrm{a}_{f,\eta}(x).

In order to see that formula we note that by the change of variables and the definition of a conformal map

\displaystyle|2B_{0}|\,\mathrm{a}_{g,\tilde{\eta}}^{n}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{\eta}B_{0}}|D(f\circ T_{x}^{-1})(y)|^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{\eta}B_{0}}|Df(T_{x}^{-1}(y))|^{n}|\,|DT_{x}^{-1}(y)|^{n}J_{T_{x}^{-1}}(y)J_{T_{x}}(T_{x}^{-1}(y))\mathrm{d}y

Proof of Lemma
3.16
.

For a fixed parameter
c>0
consider
x\in E
, so that
d(S(x),\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus U)\simeq_{c}1-|x|
, and let also
\tau>1
. Note that this assumption excludes the case
x=0
, as then
d(S(0),\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus U)=d(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus U)=0
for any
U\subsetneq\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Therefore, we define

(64)

y_{\tau}(x):=\left(|x|-\frac{\tau}{2}(1-|x|)\right)\frac{x}{|x|},

which satisfies

\displaystyle|y_{\tau}(x)-x|=\left|x-\frac{\tau}{2}\frac{x}{|x|}(1-|x|)-x\right|=\frac{\tau}{2}(1-|x|),\,\hbox{ hence }y_{\tau}(x)\in\partial(\tau B_{x})\hbox{ and moreover},

\displaystyle\left|\frac{x}{|x|}-y_{\tau}(x)\right|=\left(1+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)(1-|x|).

Note that
y_{\tau}(x)
may belong to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{B}^{n}
. On the other hand, since
U
and
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus S(x)
ar closed, there exist
w_{1}\in S(x)
and
w_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus U
such that
d(S(x),\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus U)=d(w_{1},w_{2})\simeq_{n}|w_{1}-w_{2}|
. In particular, by the property of
x\in E
we have
|w_{1}-w_{2}|\approx_{c,n}1-|x|
with
c
independent of
x
. Recall also that, since
w_{1}\in S(x)
, it holds that
|w_{1}-x|\leq(1+\alpha)(1-|x|)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle|y_{\tau}(x)-w_{2}|\leq|y_{\tau}(x)-x|+|x-w_{1}|+|w_{1}-w_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\tau}{2}+(1+\alpha)+C\right)(1-|x|)

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{\tau}{2}+(1+\alpha)+C}{1+\frac{\tau}{2}}\left|\frac{x}{|x|}-y_{\tau}(x)\right|.

Notice that the fraction on the right-hand side above is a decreasing function of
\tau
and

\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\frac{\frac{\tau}{2}+C+\alpha+1}{\frac{\tau}{2}+1}=1.

Therefore, for sufficiently large
\tau:=\tau_{0}(\alpha)
we have
|y_{\tau_{0}}(x)-w_{2}|\leq(1+\alpha)\left|\frac{x}{|x|}-y_{\tau_{0}}(x)\right|
. The following two cases may occur:

(1)
\left|\frac{x}{|x|}-y_{\tau_{0}}(x)\right|\leq 1
. Then it holds that
\left|\frac{x}{|x|}-y_{\tau_{0}}(x)\right|=1-|y_{\tau_{0}}(x)|
and, hence,
|y_{\tau_{0}}(x)-w_{2}|\leq(1+\alpha)(1-|y_{\tau_{0}}(x)|)
. This, in particular, means that
w_{2}\in S(y_{\tau}(x))
. Furthermore, recall that
w_{2}\notin U
as assumed above. We set
y_{x}:=y_{\tau_{0}}(x)
and directly verify that
y_{x}
satisfies (
35
) and (
36
).

(2) Otherwise, let
\left|\frac{x}{|x|}-y_{\tau_{0}}(x)\right|>1
. Then, upon setting
y_{x}:=0
, we clearly get (
35
), since
S(0)=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. As for (
36
) observe that if
\left|\frac{x}{|x|}-y_{\tau_{0}}(x)\right|>1
, then by the construction of
y_{\tau}(x)
in (
64
), the ball
\overline{\tau_{0}B_{x}}
contains the origin and this holds regardless whether
