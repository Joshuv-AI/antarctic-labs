Studies of \PZ → 4 ​ \Pell \PZ\to 4\Pell decays in proton-proton collisions at s = 8 \sqrt{s}=8 and 13 ​ \TeV 13\TeV


Abstract
Decays of \PZ \PZ bosons to four charged leptons (electrons and muons) are studied in proton-proton collisions at s = 8 \sqrt{s}=8 and 13 \TeV . The analysis is based on data collected with the CMS detector at the LHC corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 19.7 \fbinv at 8 \TeV and 138 \fbinv at 13 \TeV . The measured value of the inclusive branching fraction for all four-lepton decay modes, ℬ ⁡ ( \PZ → 4 ​ \Pell ) \mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell) , is [ 4.67 ± 0.11 ​ \stat ± 0.10 ​ \syst ] × 10 − 6 [4.67\pm 0.11\stat\pm 0.10\syst]\times 10^{-6} , which has a precision of about 3% limited by both statistical and systematic uncertainties. Measurements of the individual branching fractions for the decays \PZ → 4 ​ \Pgm \PZ\to 4\Pgm , \PZ → 4 ​ \Pe \PZ\to 4\Pe , and \PZ → 2 ​ \Pgm ​ 2 ​ \Pe \PZ\to 2\Pgm 2\Pe are also reported. Differential decay rates are presented as functions of kinematic and angular quantities in the \PZ \PZ boson rest frame. Measurements of triple-product asymmetries, which are sensitive to possible violations of charge conjugation and parity invariance, are performed for \PZ → 4 ​ \Pell \PZ\to 4\Pell decays. The results are compared with standard model predictions and used to set limits on the production of new gauge bosons.

0.2
Analysis strategy

The LHC and the CMS detector serve as the source of a very large number of

\PZ
bosons—far more than produced at the Large Electron-Positron Collider, LEP—enabling
the measurement of the branching fraction
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)
as well as differential decay rates.
However,
\mathcal{B}({\PZ\to 6\Pell})\approx 10^{-10}
, which remains beyond the sensitivity of current CMS data.

The physics quantities of interest are defined in the rest frame of the
\PZ
boson and are
practically independent of the center-of-mass energy. The phase space of
the
\PZ\to 4\Pell
decay process, with respect to which theoretical predictions of branching fractions
and differential decay rates are calculated, is defined by two requirements. First, the
four-lepton invariant mass must satisfy
80<m_{4\Pell}<100\GeV
. Second, any pair
of same-flavor, opposite-sign leptons must satisfy
m_{\Pellp\Pellm}>4\GeV
. This second requirement
removes contributions from low-mass resonances such as
\JPsi\to\Pellp\Pellm
,
which has been observed in
\PZ
boson decays
[
35
]
.
Theoretical predictions of branching fractions and differential decay rates are calculated
with respect to this phase space. When working
with simulated event samples, generator-level leptons are “dressed” by summing
all photons falling within a cone of
\Delta R=\sqrt{\smash[b]{(\Delta\eta)^{2}+(\Delta\phi)^{2}}}=0.1

centered on the charged lepton, where
\Delta\eta
is the difference in pseudorapidity and
\Delta\phi
is
the difference in azimuthal angle measured in radians.
The
\PZ\to\Pellp\Pellm
decay process is used for normalizing
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)
, as explained below,
and its phase space is defined simply by the requirement
80<m_{\Pellp\Pellm}<100\GeV
.
The phase space should not be confused with the fiducial region defined by
the event selection requirements stated in Section
0.5.2
.

The number of selected signal events is

N^{\text{sig}}=\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)\frac{{(A\,\epsilon)_{4\Pell}}}{(1-f_{\text{nr}})}\,\sigma(\Pp{}\Pp\to\PZ+X)\lumi,

(1)

where
\lumi
is the integrated luminosity,

A
is the acceptance for events in the phase space region to fall in the
fiducial region,
\epsilon
is the efficiency for selecting events in the fiducial region
that pass the event selection requirements,
f_{\text{nr}}
is a small correction
for nonresonant production of
4\Pell
events, and
\sigma(\Pp{}\Pp\to\PZ+X)
is the cross
section for the inclusive production of
\PZ
bosons.

The branching fraction
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)
could be extracted directly from Eq. (
1
).
Significant uncertainties, however, would ensue from the measured values of the cross section and integrated luminosity.
In order to eliminate these uncertainties, the production of lepton pairs,

\Pp{}\Pp\to\PZ\to\Pellp\Pellm
, is used as a precise measure of
\sigma(\Pp{}\Pp\to\PZ+X)\lumi
.
Since

N_{\Pellp\Pellm}=\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to\Pellp\Pellm){(A\,\epsilon)_{\Pellp\Pellm}}\,\sigma(\Pp{}\Pp\to\PZ+X)\lumi,

the percent-level uncertainties of the quantity
\sigma(\Pp{}\Pp\to\PZ+X)\lumi
are exchanged
for extremely small statistical uncertainties for
N_{\Pellp\Pellm}
and the well-measured value
for
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to\Pellp\Pellm)

[
58
]
. By using similar fiducial regions for
\PZ\to 4\Pell
and
\PZ\to\Pellp\Pellm
,
the relative uncertainty in
(A\,\epsilon)_{4\Pell}
and
(A\,\epsilon)_{\Pellp\Pellm}
are also rather small.
On this basis, we infer
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)
using the formula

\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)=\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to\Pellp\Pellm)(1-f_{\text{nr}})\frac{(A\,\epsilon)_{\Pellp\Pellm}}{(A\,\epsilon)_{4\Pell}}\frac{(N^{\text{obs}}-N^{\text{bkg}})_{4\Pell}}{(N^{\text{obs}}-N^{\text{bkg}})_{\Pellp\Pellm}}.

(2)

In this expression,
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to\Pellp\Pellm)=(3.3658\pm 0.0023)\%
is the world-average dilepton branching fraction
[
58
]
.
The quantity
f_{\text{nr}}=0.04\pm 0.01
is the fraction
[
34
]
of
4\Pell
events from nonresonant processes
such as
\PZ\PZ\to 4\Pell
and is calculated using
\MGvATNLO
v.3.5.0
[
5
]
.
The quantity
(A\,\epsilon)
is the product of theoretical acceptance and experimental efficiency for the given final state.
The
N^{\text{obs}}
is the observed number of selected events and
N^{\text{bkg}}
is the estimated number of background events.
These quantities are defined separately for the
4\Pell
signal channel and the
\Pellp\Pellm
normalization channel.
The formulation of Eq. (
2
) is founded on two ratios of similar quantities—
(A\,\epsilon)

for
\Pellp\Pellm
and
4\Pell
, and the ratio of background-corrected yields—and consequently is experimentally
robust. The constants in the formula,
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to\Pellp\Pellm)
and
(1-f_{\text{nr}})
, are precisely known.

The flavor-specific branching fractions
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pgm\!)
,
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pe\!)
, and
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 2\Pgm 2\Pe\!)
are measured using a
choice of normalization channels that reduces the systematic uncertainties coming from lepton
reconstruction and identification. For
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pgm\!)
, only
\MM
events are used in
Eq. (
2
), and for
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pe\!)
only
\EE
events are used.
With this choice,

\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pgm\!)\propto\left.\frac{(N^{\text{obs}}-N^{\text{bkg}})_{4\Pgm}}{(A\,\epsilon)_{4\Pgm}}\middle/\frac{(N^{\text{obs}}-N^{\text{bkg}})_{\MM}}{(A\,\epsilon)_{\MM}}\right.

and

\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pe\!)\propto\left.\frac{(N^{\text{obs}}-N^{\text{bkg}})_{4\Pe}}{(A\,\epsilon)_{4\Pe}}\middle/\frac{(N^{\text{obs}}-N^{\text{bkg}})_{\EE}}{(A\,\epsilon)_{\EE}}\right..

Both
\MM
and
\EE
events are used to measure
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 2\Pgm 2\Pe\!)
:

\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 2\Pgm 2\Pe\!)\propto 2\,\frac{(N^{\text{obs}}-N^{\text{bkg}})_{2\Pgm 2\Pe}}{(A\,\epsilon)_{2\Pgm 2\Pe}}\left/\middle(\frac{(N^{\text{obs}}-N^{\text{bkg}})_{\MM}}{(A\,\epsilon)_{\MM}}+\frac{(N^{\text{obs}}-N^{\text{bkg}})_{\EE}}{(A\,\epsilon)_{\EE}}\right).

The inclusive branching fraction
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)
is defined to be the sum of the measurements of flavor-specific final states:

\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)\equiv\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pgm\!)+\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 2\Pgm 2\Pe\!)+\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pe\!).

0.2.1
Differential decay rates

Differential decay rates,
\ddinline{\Gamma_{\PZ\to 4\Pell}}{x}
, where
\Gamma_{\PZ\to 4\Pell}
is the partial width for
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)
and

x
is one of nine kinematic quantities listed below, are defined in the
\PZ
boson rest frame,
which is well-approximated by the four-lepton rest frame.
For the sake of clearly defining the differential decay rates,
we label the more (less) massive same-flavor, opposite-sign lepton pair in the event as
\PZ_{1}
(
\PZ_{2}
).
This notation does not imply that we necessarily view
\PZ\to 4\Pell
as a
1\to 2
decay process.
Furthermore, we order the momenta of the four leptons in the
\PZ
boson rest frame, so that

p_{\Pell_{1}}>p_{\Pell_{2}}>p_{\Pell_{3}}>p_{\Pell_{4}}
; in general,
this will not correspond to the transverse momentum (
\pt
) ordering of leptons, and the leading
lepton in the
\PZ
boson rest frame is not necessarily the lepton with highest
\pt
.
For the differential distributions,
x
will refer to one of the following kinematic quantities:

•

m_{\PZ_{1}}
(
m_{\PZ_{2}}
), the invariant mass of the
\PZ_{1}
(
\PZ_{2}
) lepton pair;

•

p_{\Pell_{1}}
, the momentum of the leading lepton in the
\PZ
rest frame;

•

m_{\Pell_{2,3,4}}
, the invariant mass of the three trailing leptons;

•

\alpha_{\PZ_{1}}
(
\alpha_{\PZ_{2}}
), the angle between the momentum vectors of the leptons of pair
\PZ_{1}
(
\PZ_{2}
);

•

\beta
, the angle between the momentum vector of the less-energetic lepton of the
\PZ_{1}
pair and the momentum vector of
\PZ_{2}
;

•

\theta_{\PZ_{1}}
(
\theta_{\PZ_{2}}
), the angle between the momentum vector of the positively-charged lepton of pair
\PZ_{1}
(
\PZ_{2}
) and the momentum vector of
\PZ_{2}
(
\PZ_{1}
), measured in the rest frame of
\PZ_{1}
(
\PZ_{2}
).

0.2.2
A probe for the violation of
C\!P
invariance

We probe for a violation of
C\!P
invariance in
\PZ\to 4\Pell
decays by measuring a triple-product asymmetry.
In the SM,
\PZ\to 4\Pell
decays are expected to conserve
C\!P
.
References
[
15
,
16
]
suggest a probe of
C\!P
invariance for the
\PZ\to 4\Pell
decay process, and define the observable

\phi
as the angle between the decay planes of
\PZ_{1}
and
\PZ_{2}
in the
\PZ
boson rest frame.
The “decay plane” of a pair is defined by the momentum vectors of its constituent leptons,
which in general are not collinear.
The angle
\phi
is measured from the “front” face of the
\PZ_{1}
plane to the “front” face of
the
\PZ_{2}
plane, where the “front“ of the
\PZ_{1}
plane is defined by the outward-pointing unit normal vector

\hat{n}_{\PZ_{1}}=\frac{\hat{p}_{\PZ_{1}}^{\Pellp}\times\hat{p}_{\PZ_{1}}^{\Pellm}}{\lvert\hat{p}_{\PZ_{1}}^{\Pellp}\times\hat{p}_{\PZ_{1}}^{\Pellm}\rvert}

and similarly for the
\PZ_{2}
plane. Here,
\hat{p}_{\PZ_{1}}^{\Pellp}
is the unit vector in the direction
of the momentum of the positively charged
\PZ_{1}
lepton. It is convenient to define
\phi
as

\sin\phi=(\hat{n}_{\PZ_{1}}\times\hat{n}_{\PZ_{2}})\cdot\hat{p}_{\PZ_{1}},

where
\hat{p}_{\PZ_{1}}
is the unit vector in the direction of the momentum of
\PZ_{1}
.

The quantity
\sgn(\sin\phi)
, where
\sgn
is the sign function,
is a triple-product asymmetry observable that may be used to test for
\mathit{C}
,
\mathit{P}
, and
C\!P
asymmetries.
It is equivalent to
\sgn(\psi)
, where
\psi=(\vec{p}_{\PZ_{1}}^{\,\Pellp}\times\vec{p}_{\PZ_{1}}^{\,\Pellm})\cdot\vec{p}_{\PZ_{2}}^{\,\Pellp}

is a scalar triple-product uniquely defined by the four lepton momenta in the
\PZ
boson rest frame
[
15
]
.
The asymmetry
A_{\sin\phi}
is given by

A_{\sin\phi}=\frac{N_{+}-N_{-}}{N_{+}+N_{-}},

(3)

where
N_{+}
(
N_{-}
) is the number of events for which
\sin\phi>0
(
<0
) after subtracting expected
background contributions. The distribution of
\sin\phi
might itself show evidence of an asymmetry,
which would constitute a contradiction of SM expectations.
Similar probes have been applied to four-body meson decays such as

\PDz\to\PKp\PKm\Pgpp\Pgpm
studied by the Belle Collaboration
[
51
]

and
{\HepParticle{\PK}{L,S}{0}}\Xspace\to\Pgpp\Pgpm\EE
studied by the NA48 Collaboration
[
52
]
.

0.2.3
Physics beyond the SM

We consider possible contributions from a new neutral scalar or vector boson
{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace
which may mediate
the
\PZ\to 4\Pell
decay, as shown in Fig.
2
. This
{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace
boson couples to electrons
and muons, potentially with different strengths. Couplings to other fermions and to gauge bosons are not considered.
The scalar and vector models, respectively, are described by the following additions to the SM Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}\supset\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace\partial^{\mu}{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace-\frac{1}{2}m_{{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace}^{2}{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace^{2}+g_{\Pell}\bar{\Pell}\Pell{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace

(4)

and

\mathcal{L}\supset\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace_{\nu}\partial^{\mu}{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace^{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}m_{{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace}^{2}{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace_{\mu}{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace^{\mu}+g_{\Pell}\bar{\Pell}\gamma_{\mu}\Pell{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace^{\mu},

(5)

where
m_{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace
is the mass of the
{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace
boson and
g_{\Pell}
(
=g_{\Pe},g_{\PGm}
) is the strength
of its couplings to leptons.

Figure 2:
The
\PZ\to 4\Pell
process mediated by a scalar (left) or vector (right) boson
{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace
.

The combined value of published
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)
measurements is used in Ref.
[
59
]
to set limits on
the production of
{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace
bosons. A similar approach is taken in Ref.
[
47
]
.
The flavor-specific branching fractions
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pgm\!)
and
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pe\!)
enable us
to set limits on the presence of
{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace
, a new neutral scalar or vector boson
which couples only to muons (
\ie
, with
g_{\Pe}=0
)
or only to electrons (
g_{\PGm}=0
). Our
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 2\Pgm 2\Pe\!)
measurement could be used to constrain the case
where
g_{\Pe},g_{\PGm}>0
but
g_{\Pe}\neq g_{\PGm}
, although this case is not investigated
in this paper.

0.3
The CMS detector and event reconstruction

The central feature of the CMS apparatus is a superconducting solenoid of 6
\unit
m internal diameter, providing a magnetic field of 3.8
\unit
T. Within the solenoid volume are a silicon pixel and strip tracker, a lead tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter (ECAL), and a brass and scintillator hadron calorimeter (HCAL), each composed of a barrel and two endcap sections. Forward calorimeters extend the
\eta
coverage provided by the barrel and endcap detectors. Muons are detected in gas-ionization detectors embedded in the steel flux-return yoke outside the solenoid.
A more detailed description of the CMS detector, along with a definition of the coordinate system used and
relevant kinematic variables, is reported in Ref.
[
22
]
.

The particle-flow (PF) algorithm
[
60
]
uniquely identifies all reconstructed particles in an event
based on an optimized combination of information from many different elements of the CMS detector. PF objects include
charged and neutral hadrons, photons, electrons, and muons.

Muons are identified
[
36
]
as tracks in the central tracker consistent with either a track or several hits in the
muon system, and with energy deposits consistent with the expectation for a muon traversing the calorimeters.
Muons are detected in the range
\abs{\eta}<2.4
using detection planes employing
three technologies: drift tubes, cathode strip chambers, and resistive plate chambers.
For muons with
\pt
up to 100
\GeV
, the
\pt
resolution is determined mainly by the tracker, and amounts
to 1% in the barrel and 3% in the endcaps
[
26
,
36
]
.

An electron is identified
[
43
,
28
]
in the range
\abs{\eta}<2.5

as a charged particle track and one or more ECAL energy clusters consistent with the
extrapolation of this track to the ECAL surface and with bremsstrahlung photons emitted as the electron propagates
through the tracker material. The momentum of the electron is estimated by combining the ECAL cluster energy measurements
with the momentum measurement in the tracker. The momentum resolution for electrons with
\pt\approx 45\GeV

ranges 1.7–4.5%. It is generally better in the barrel region than in the endcaps, and depends on the
amount of energy emitted by the electron as bremsstrahlung
[
28
]
.

The energy of photons is obtained from the measurement of ECAL clusters. The energy of charged hadrons
is determined from a combination of their momentum as measured in the tracker and the matching ECAL and HCAL
energy deposits.
The energy of neutral hadrons is obtained from ECAL and HCAL cluster energies after
the deposits from charged hadrons, photons, electrons, and muons have been subtracted.

The imbalance of the total
\pt
, known as missing
\pt
, is computed as the opposite
of the vector sum of the
\ptvec
of all charged and neutral PF candidates
[
38
]
. The impact of additional
\Pp{}\Pp
collisions
in the same or adjacent bunch crossings (“pileup”) is handled as described in Refs.
[
23
,
42
]
.

The reconstructed vertex with the largest sum of
\pt^{2}
of all physics objects associated to that vertex
is taken to be the primary
\Pp
\Pp
interaction vertex in the event. These physics objects may include jets reconstructed
using the anti-
\kt
clustering algorithm
[
19
,
20
]
taking tracks associated with the
given vertex and the associated missing momentum vector as input.

0.4
Data and simulated event samples

The results of this analysis are based on the CMS data sets collected at
\sqrt{s}=8
and 13
\TeV
.
The data are collected using triggers that require the presence of one muon candidate, one electron candidate,
or two same-flavor lepton candidates
[
32
,
41
]
.
The trigger path
\pt
requirements vary from one data-taking period to another.
The single-muon (single-electron) paths used in this analysis require a candidate lepton with

\pt^{\PGm}>24
–27
\GeV
(
\pt^{\Pe}>27
–35
\GeV
). The dimuon (dielectron) paths require one candidate with

\pt^{\PGm_{1}}>17
–23
\GeV
(
\pt^{\Pe_{1}}>17
–23
\GeV
) and another one with

\pt^{\PGm_{2}}>8\GeV
(
\pt^{\Pe_{2}}>8
–12
\GeV
).

Samples of simulated signal and background events are used to optimize the event selection criteria,
evaluate the detector acceptance and efficiency of each selection, verify the procedure for
a background estimate based on control samples in data, and assess systematic uncertainties.
The available Monte Carlo (MC) event generators vary somewhat from one data-taking period to the next.
Most simulated event samples are generated at next-to-leading order (NLO) in perturbative quantum chromodynamics (QCD)
for
\Pp
\Pp
collisions at

0.5
Object and event selection

The lepton identification used in these studies is based on
Refs.
[
28
,
43
,
26
,
36
]
.
The event selection involves only kinematic quantities based on either two or four well-reconstructed leptons.

0.5.1
Object selection

Electrons and muons are selected on the basis of kinematic, impact parameter, and reconstruction criteria.
A first set of criteria are referred to as the “loose” identification requirements, and a second, more restrictive
one is described in the next paragraph.
A loosely-identified electron (muon) candidate must satisfy
\pt>7
(5)
\GeV
and
\abs{\eta}<2.5
(2.4).
A candidate of either flavor must satisfy the transverse and longitudinal impact parameter requirements
\abs{d_{xy}}<0.5\cm
and

\abs{d_{z}}<1\cm
with respect to the event’s primary vertex. The three-dimensional impact parameter
(
IP
) of each candidate must also satisfy the requirement
\abs{\text{IP}/\sigma_{\text{IP}}}<4

with respect to the primary vertex, where
\sigma_{\text{IP}}
is the uncertainty in the
IP
measurement.

Further “tight” identification requirements are defined that select leptons originating from the hard process of an event
and suppress leptons resulting from decays of hadrons containing a
\PQb
or
\PQc
quark;
these requirements are applied to lepton candidates that pass
the loose identification criteria. Tightly-identified muons must be identified as such by the PF algorithm described in
Section
0.3
, and the tight identification requirement for electrons is defined by a multivariate classifier.
Each tightly-identified lepton must be isolated in the sense that the sum of energies of particles close to the candidate
lepton is small compared with the lepton energy. Leptons emitted in
\PZ
boson decays are generally expected to be
isolated, in contrast to “nonprompt” leptons emitted in heavy hadron decays that are found inside jets;
the isolation requirement suppresses unwanted nonprompt leptons. The relative isolation of a lepton is defined by

I_{\text{rel}}^{\Pell}=\left.\left(\sum\pt^{\text{charged hadrons}}+\max\!\left[\sum\pt^{\text{neutral hadrons}}-\pt^{\text{pileup}},0\right]\right)\middle/\pt^{\Pell}\right.,

where the sum is over PF objects within a cone of size
\DR
about the lepton’s direction.
A small value of
I_{\text{rel}}
indicates an isolated lepton. For events collected at 8 (13)
\TeV
,
\DR=0.4
(
0.3
).
For muons,
\pt^{\text{pileup}}=\sum_{\text{PU}}\pt^{\text{charged hadrons}}/2-\sum\et^{\text{photons}}

where
PU
represents the pileup vertices, and the factor of
1/2
is motivated by the fact that
two charged hadrons are expected for each neutral one. We require
I_{\text{rel}}^{\PGm}<0.4
(
<0.35
) at 8 (13)
\TeV
.
For electrons,
\pt^{\text{pileup}}=\rho A_{\text{eff}}
, where
\rho
is the mean energy density in the event and the
effective area
A_{\text{eff}}
is the ratio of the slope of the average isolation and the slope of
\rho

as functions of the number of pileup vertices
[
21
]
. We require
I_{\text{rel}}^{\Pe}<0.4
at 8
\TeV
.
The multivariate classifier applied to the 13
\TeV
data samples includes the object relative isolation as an input variable,
so we impose no explicit isolation requirement on electrons in those samples.

The energies and momenta of leptons are corrected on a per-lepton basis for any bias related to detector misalignment or calibration.
In simulated events, the object energies are further smeared such that the energy distributions for leptons of both flavors match
those observed in data. Unless otherwise specified, all leptons mentioned in the following pass the tight identification requirements.

and also defines the fiducial region for acceptance in the CMS detector.
These requirements are stated below and summarized in Table

In order to be selected, an event must satisfy the requirements for both the fiducial and
the phase space regions. The phase space is defined above, in Section
0.2
.
The fiducial regions are as similar as possible for the
\PZ\to 4\Pell
and
\PZ\to\Pellp\Pellm

\abs{\eta^{\Pell}}<2.5
for all leptons, and
\pt^{\Pell_{1}}>20\GeV
and
\pt^{\Pell_{2}}>10\GeV
for
the leading and subleading leptons, respectively.
The trailing two leptons of a four-lepton event are both required to have
\pt^{\Pell}>5\GeV

0.6
Background estimation

The largest sources of background events come from the
\PZ\to\Pellp\Pellm
and
\ttbar\to 2\Pell 2\PGn
production processes.
In both cases, the
4\Pell
requirements outlined in Section
0.5.2
select the two prompt leptons and two
additional, nonprompt leptons despite the isolation requirements stated in Section
0.5
.
These backgrounds make up just over 1% of the
4\Pell
signal yield, and their contributions are estimated using
control samples in data, as described in Section
0.6.1
.

The nonprompt background that we estimate from data includes only the contributions coming from
\PZ\to\Pellp\Pellm
and

\ttbar\to 2\Pell 2\PGn
events.
Other processes such as diboson production also contribute events with one or more nonprompt leptons,
but their impact is much smaller and better modeled by simulation than the
\PZ\to\Pellp\Pellm
and
\ttbar
contributions.
Therefore, the diboson backgrounds are estimated together with the prompt processes that we estimate from simulation.
These prompt backgrounds include triboson, Higgs boson, and
\ttbar\PZ
production,
and altogether they comprise about 1% of the
4\Pell
yield.
Backgrounds from leptonic
\tau
decays, estimated from MC simulations, are found to be negligible.

For the
\PZ\to\Pellp\Pellm
process, the total background contribution is below 0.5% in both the
\MM
and
\EE

final states. We estimate all
\PZ\to\Pellp\Pellm
backgrounds from simulation, including diboson,
\ttbar(+\PZ)
,
triboson,
\PZ\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
, and Higgs boson production.

0.6.1
Nonprompt-lepton background estimate

Events with nonprompt leptons will not necessarily lead to the combination of charges in an
\Pellp\Pellm\Pellp\Pellm
final state.
Therefore, we use a same-sign method to estimate the number of selected events with nonprompt leptons.
A control region of events with one same-flavor, opposite-sign lepton pair
\PZ_{1}
(consisting of prompt leptons)
and one same-flavor, same-sign lepton pair
\PZ_{2}
(consisting of nonprompt leptons) is defined. With the exception
of the same-sign requirement for
\PZ_{2}
, the selection requirements for this control region are identical to those
described in Section
0.5
for the
4\Pell
signal region. In order to match the signal region as closely
as possible, the requirement
m_{\PZ_{2}}>4\GeV
is imposed.

We estimate the nonprompt background,
N^{\text{npr}}
, as
N^{\text{npr}}=\lambda(N^{\text{obs}}-N^{\text{pr}})

where
\lambda
scales the number of nonprompt events from the control region to the signal region,

N^{\text{obs}}
is the number of observed data events in the control region, and
N^{\text{pr}}
is the total number
of expected events from the simulated prompt processes in the control region. We take
\lambda=1
;
that is, we assume all charge configurations of nonprompt leptons are equally probable.
This assumption is supported by studies of simulated
\PZ\to\Pellp\Pellm
and
\ttbar\to 2\Pell 2\PGn
events,
and we assign a systematic uncertainty of 30% to
\lambda
, based on these studies.
The statistical uncertainty of
N^{\text{npr}}
is assessed by propagating the statistical uncertainties of

N^{\text{pr}}
and
N^{\text{obs}}
.

When measuring the differential decay rates, it is necessary to characterize the shapes of the nonprompt
contributions in order to subtract them and this requires a sufficiently large sample of background events.
For this purpose, an additional “loose” control region is defined. This region is identical to the
one described above—which, for clarity, we will refer to as the “tight” control region—except that
we also allow events with one lepton that passes loosened identification criteria. This lepton must pass
the “loose” criteria listed in Section
0.5.1
and it must be in the
\PZ_{2}
pair. The other
three leptons in the event are required to pass the tight identification criteria. Events with more than
one lepton candidate passing the loose but failing the tight identification criteria are rejected.

We apply an overall normalization to the loose control region to correct for the different selection
efficiencies of loosely and tightly identified leptons. The yield of the loose control region is scaled
by the factor
\lambda_{\text{loose}}={N^{\text{npr}}}/{N^{\text{npr}}_{\text{loose CR}}}
,
where
N^{\text{npr}}
and
N^{\text{npr}}_{\text{loose CR}}
are the numbers of nonprompt events in the tight and loose control regions.

0.8.1
Branching fraction measurements

The individual branching fractions
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pgm\!)
,
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 2\Pgm 2\Pe\!)
, and
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pe\!)
are measured using each of the 2012, 2016, 2017,
and 2018 data samples. The 8
\TeV
data, taken in 2012, are expected to contribute 8.4% of the total yield.
Simultaneous fits to these 12 measurements are performed with a procedure that is
equivalent to the best linear unbiased estimation method for combining measurements
[
55
]
.
The free parameters in the fit are signal strength parameters that scale the SM expected value of
the branching fraction to the observed one. The measured value is the product of the signal strength
and the expected value.
Two fits are performed: one in which a single signal strength is used to parameterize all

4\Pell
final states simultaneously (the “SM” parameterization), and another
in which the signal strengths for the
4\Pgm
,
2\Pgm 2\Pe
, and
4\Pe

final states are allowed to vary independently (the “BSM” parameterization).
The SM parameterization assumes lepton flavor universality in the
\PZ\to 4\Pell
process and is the nominal result.
The BSM parameterization is used for the new physics constraints reported in Section
0.8.4
.

The fitting procedure includes correlations of systematic uncertainties among the measurements.
We consider each source to be completely correlated for all measurements within a particular data-taking period.
Lepton identification and reconstruction efficiencies are fully correlated among the 13
\TeV
data and uncorrelated with the 8
\TeV
data,
with the exception of a 50% correlation for electron identification efficiency between 8 and 13
\TeV
.
In general, the dimuon and dielectron trigger efficiencies are uncorrelated with each other,
and all trigger efficiencies are uncorrelated among the data-taking periods.
The systematic uncertainty from background estimation is fully correlated across data-taking periods,
as are all uncertainties from theory sources.

The fit results are listed in Table
0.8.1
. The values for the individual channels

4\Pgm
,
2\Pgm 2\Pe
, and
4\Pe
are statistically independent and are based on the BSM parameterization.
The value for the combined channel
4\Pell
is not simply the sum of the values for the individual channels

0.8.2
Differential decay rates

We report the differential decay rates
\ddinline{\Gamma_{\PZ\to 4\Pell}}{x}
, where
x
is one of nine kinematic and angular quantities.
These quantities are, unless otherwise specified, measured in the rest frame of the
\PZ
boson, which is taken to be
the center-of-mass frame of the reconstructed four-lepton system.

For each observed data distribution, we subtract the expected background bin-by-bin.
The shape of the nonprompt-lepton background is taken from the loose control region described in Section
0.6.1

and the shapes of other backgrounds are taken from simulations. After background subtraction,
each distribution is unfolded to the dressed lepton level in order to correct for the effects of detector resolution.
The software package
PyUnfold

[
18
]
is used, which implements the D’Agostini method of iterative
unfolding
[
48
]
with a customizable initial prior distribution. We begin with a flat prior and
iterate until the result converges. Convergence is achieved when the covariance matrix remains stable between iterations
within a 1% tolerance, which is met before 50 iterations for each distribution. For the quantities considered here,
resolution effects are small and unfolding has a minor effect on the distributions.
The unfolded distributions are corrected to the full phase space by taking into account the acceptance and efficiency
for each bin in the distribution. Each distribution is normalized such that its integral is equal to

\Gamma_{\PZ\to 4\Pell}=\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)\Gamma_{\PZ}=12.83\pm 0.41\keV,

where
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)
is the fit result given in Table
0.8.1
and
\Gamma_{\PZ}=2.4955\pm 0.0023\GeV
is
the world-average total
\PZ
boson width
[
58
]
.

The differential decay rates for the invariant masses of the lepton pairs,
\ddinline{\Gamma_{\PZ\to 4\Pell}}{m_{\PZ_{1}}}
and
\ddinline{\Gamma_{\PZ\to 4\Pell}}{m_{\PZ_{2}}}
, are shown in
Fig.

.

(lower left) shows
\ddinline{\Gamma_{\PZ\to 4\Pell}}{p_{\Pell_{1}}}
, where

is the momentum of the leading lepton in the

peaks sharply just below 45
\GeV
, which is approximately half the
nominal mass of the
\PZ

0.8.3
Measurement of the triple-product asymmetry

The SM does not predict observable violations of
C\!P
invariance in decays of
\PZ
bosons,
so any evidence for
C\!P
violation would constitute a clear signal of BSM physics.
Generally,
C\!P
violation in decays of unstable particles can
appear as a difference between the amplitudes for the decay to a specific final state
and to its
C\!P
-conjugate state
[
14
,
15
]
. Assuming that all gauge interactions
respect
\mathit{CPT}
invariance, where
\mathit{T}
is time-reversal symmetry,
a T-odd observable such as the scalar triple product

X=(\vec{p}_{a}\times\vec{p}_{b})\cdot\vec{p}_{c}
can be used to test for
C\!P
invariance
violation that would be manifested as an asymmetry in
X

[
14
]
.
In the present case of four-body
\PZ
boson decays, decomposed as two-body decays
to
\PZ_{1}
and
\PZ_{2}
, the angle
\phi
between the respective decay planes provides
an opportunity to test for
C\!P
invariance violation by measuring the asymmetry

A_{\sin\phi}
defined in Section
0.2.2
.

The measurements of
A_{\sin\phi}
for all
4\Pell
final states are given in Table
0.8.3
. Every measured value
is consistent with the SM expectation of zero, hence there is no evidence of
C\!P
invariance violation.
The table also lists
N_{+}
and
N_{-}
, the numbers of events with

\sin\phi>0
or
<0
, respectively, after subtracting background contributions
determined from the loose control region described in Section
0.6
. The background estimation is the only
nonnegligible source of systematic uncertainty for
A_{\sin\phi}
, which is much smaller than the statistical uncertainty.
Both the statistical and systematic uncertainties for
A_{\sin\phi}
are computed taking
A_{\sin\phi}=0
.
The distributions of
\sin\phi
are shown in Fig.
6
for all
\PZ\to 4\Pell
events and for each final state studied.

0.8.4
Physics beyond the SM

A light gauge boson that couples to leptons and has a mass below approximately 70
\GeV
may be produced on shell in
\PZ
boson decays. If such a particle exists, it must modify
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)
with respect
to its SM predicted value.

Without focusing on specific models, we address this general possibility by hypothesizing a new light boson coupling significantly to leptons. This minimal extension of the SM was explored in Ref.
[
59
]
, where the boson is labeled
\HepParticle
U
\Xspace
. A scalar or vector
\HepParticle
U
\Xspace
boson may mediate the
\PZ\to 4\Pell
decay, as shown in Fig.
2
. The relevant parameters of
\HepParticle
U
\Xspace
are its mass
m_{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace
and the strengths of its couplings to leptons,
g_{\Pell}
. We consider cases where the
\HepParticle
U
\Xspace
boson couples to electrons and muons equally (
\ie
,
g_{\Pe}=g_{\PGm}
) and where it couples exclusively to leptons of one flavor (
\ie
,
g_{\PGm}=0
or
g_{\Pe}=0
).
In contrast to Ref.
[
39
]
, which targets a narrow peak in the dilepton invariant mass spectrum,
our procedure is insensitive to assumptions about the
\HepParticle
U
\Xspace
boson total width
\Gamma_{{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace}
because
we do not search for narrow structures in dilepton mass spectra.
Under the assumption that new physics appears as a narrow peak, the results
from Ref.
[
39
]
are expected to be more sensitive than the results presented here.

The 95% confidence level (
\CL
) upper limit (UL) on individual channels
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pgm\!)
,
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 2\Pgm 2\Pe\!)
,
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pe\!)

and on the combined channel
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)
are calculated using the measured branching fractions given
in Table
0.8.1
.
The expected and observed upper limits are reported in Table
0.8.4
.

In order to derive constraints on model parameters
m_{{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace}
and
g_{\Pell}
, signal events are generated at LO
with
\MADGRAPH

[
5
]
implementing the Lagrangian terms in Eqs. (
4
)
and (
5
) using the
FeynRules
package
[
4
]
.
The values for
m_{{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace}
and
g_{\Pell}
vary in the ranges
4<m_{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace<90\GeV
and
0.005<g_{\Pell}<0.45
.
Each signal sample has
10^{4}
events, including contributions from both the SM and the new physics process
and their interference. The
\HepParticle
U
\Xspace
boson total width
\Gamma_{{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace}
is calculated by
\MADGRAPH
.

For each sampled point in the
(m_{{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace},g_{\Pell})
parameter space, we calculate the acceptance
A^{\text{NP}}

of the fiducial region as defined in Section
0.5.2
. A sampled point is excluded when

A^{\text{NP}}>\frac{\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)^{\text{95\% CL UL}}}{\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)}A^{\text{SM}},

(6)

where
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)^{\text{95\% \CL UL}}
is the 95%
\CL
upper limit of
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)
listed in Table
0.8.4

and
A^{\text{SM}}
is the acceptance for SM events generated at LO with
\MADGRAPH
.

The results are presented as curves in the
(m_{{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace},g_{\Pell})
plane. These curves are obtained by
performing a fit in
(m_{{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace},g_{\Pell})
space and obtaining a smooth mapping
to
A^{\text{NP}}
, that is,
f(m_{{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace},g_{\Pell})=A^{\text{NP}}
.
The exclusion curve is given by the contour

f(m_{{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace},g_{\Pell})=\frac{\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)^{\text{95\% CL UL}}}{\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)}A^{\text{SM}},

where the right side of the equality is the right side of the inequality in Eq. (
6
).

The excluded regions for the six models we consider are displayed in Fig.
7
.
These constraints, based on the current analysis, are more stringent than those presented in Ref.
[
59
]
,
which employed a world average for
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)
.
The strongest limits are found for the case of a vector
{\HepParticle{U}{}{}}\Xspace
with
g_{\Pe}=0
, which complement the
CMS direct search for an
{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
vector gauge boson in the
4\Pgm
channel
[
39
]
.
The excluded regions from both analyses are shown together in Fig.
7
(lower right).
Similar results are obtained in Ref.
[
47
]
.

0.9
Summary

A detailed study of
\PZ\to 4\Pell
decays (
\Pell
=

\Pe
or
\PGm
)
in proton-proton collisions at
\sqrt{s}=8
and 13
\TeV
has been presented.
The data correspond to an integrated luminosity of 19.7
\fbinv
collected at 8
\TeV
and
138
\fbinv
at 13
\TeV
with the CMS detector. A total of 1,877 events is observed in the
\PZ\to 4\Pell
signal region.
The signal purity for each of the
4\Pgm
,
2\Pgm 2\Pe
, and
4\Pe
final states is above 97%.

A measurement of the branching fraction for the sum of all four-lepton decay modes has been reported:

\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)=(4.67\pm 0.11\stat\pm 0.10\syst)\times 10^{-6}
.
This measurement is more precise than all previous CMS and ATLAS results
[
25
,
31
,
34
,
6
,
7
,
8
]

and has a combined statistical and systematic precision of 3.2%. Measurements of the branching fractions for
the decays
\PZ\to 4\Pgm
,
\PZ\to 4\Pe
, and
\PZ\to 2\Pgm 2\Pe
have also been reported. All measured branching fractions are consistent with
standard model (SM) expectations.

Measurements of differential decay rates for the
\PZ\to 4\Pell
decay process as functions of nine kinematic and angular
quantities were presented. These measurements are reported in terms of the
\PZ\to 4\Pell
partial width
\Gamma_{\PZ\to 4\Pell}
. They are
unfolded to correct for the effects of detector resolution and scaled to the full phase space region defined by
the four-lepton invariant mass
80<m_{4\Pell}<100\GeV
and the dilepton invariant mass
m_{\Pellp\Pellm}>4\GeV
.
These differential decay rates characterize the behavior of the
\PZ\to 4\Pell

decay process and are consistent with simulations based on the SM.

In the interest of exploring possible contributions of new physics to the
\PZ\to 4\Pell
channel,
a triple-product asymmetry measurement
was performed. The measurement was made possible by the high statistical precision of the analysis, and may be used
to probe for possible violations of charge conjugation and parity invariance in this decay.
This asymmetry is observed to be consistent with zero for all
4\Pell
channels, as expected in the SM.
Finally, the
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pgm\!)
,
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pe\!)
, and
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 2\Pgm 2\Pe\!)
measurements were used to set limits on
new physics in the form of a scalar or vector boson that may mediate the
\PZ\to 4\Pell
process. These limits are more stringent
than those set using previous measurements of
\mathcal{B}(\PZ\to 4\Pell)

[
59
]
and complement direct searches for a narrow-width vector
boson decaying to a
\MM
pair
[
39
]
.
