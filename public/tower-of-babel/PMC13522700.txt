Response to the Letter to the Editor, “Reappraising the role of CMRI in HFpEF: A tiny step in the black and white world of HFpEF”
Authors: Sudipta Mondal, Swasthi S Kumar, Garikapati Vrandha, Valaparambil Kumaran Ajit Kumar, Bijulal Sasidharan, Harikrishnan Sivadasanpillai, Ayyappan Anoop, Jayakrishnan Radhakrishnan, Suprakash Mandal
Published: 2026

Dear Sir,

We thank the authors for their insightful analysis and comments regarding our recently published article.1 As the authors correctly noted, the role of cardiac MRI (CMR) in Heart Failure with preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF)—for both diagnostic and prognostic evaluation—is expanding and is expected to grow further. Myocardial T1 values and Extracellular Volume (ECV) are considered surrogate markers of interstitial fibrosis not captured by routine Late Gadolinium Enhancement (LGE), although their specific role in the prognostication of HFpEF patients has been a subject of recent debate (especially native T1).

The formula for calculating ECV is2:

ECV = (1 – hematocrit) x [(1/T1MYO POST – 1/T1MYO PRE)/(1/T1BLOOD POST – 1/T1BLOOD PRE)], where T1MYO PRE/POST indicates native and post-contrast T1 of the myocardium and T1BLOOD PRE/POST indicates native and post-contrast T1 of the blood pool. This specific ratio was fundamentally designed to mitigate systematic bias from a technical standpoint, as native T1 is highly sensitive to variations in imaging sequences and field strengths.

While the role of native T1 in diagnostic evaluation is well established, its prognostic significance has shown varying impacts across different trials. For instance, while Garg et al concluded that native T1 values were among the strongest predictors of all-cause mortality, Roy et al found no such correlation.3,4 Conversely, a meta-analysis by Zhang et al demonstrated that higher native T1 is associated with clinical endpoints in all heart failure patients combined.5 This heterogeneity in the predictive value of native T1 is likely contributed by the following factors:

1.Significant heterogeneity of study populations: For example, Garg et al included all patients with diastolic dysfunction according to guidelines, whereas Roy et al excluded cases of amyloidosis, sarcoidosis, HCM, and severe valvular heart disease.3,4 Importantly, our study also excluded significant CAD, pericardial disease, and congenital heart disease, in addition to the exclusions made by Roy et al.1

2.Variation in Region of Interest (ROI) analysis: The methods for data sampling differed across researchers. Roy et al averaged six ROIs at the mid-ventricular level, whereas Garg et al sampled only the mid-ventricular interventricular septum (IVS).3,4 In contrast, our study utilized the average of all segments in an 18-segment MRI model.1 Furthermore, field strength affects native T1 values (3T MRI by Philips for Roy et al; 1.5T MRI by Siemens for Garg et al and our study), which can significantly impact observations.5

These practical considerations likely lead to variations in the overall impact of T1 in prognosticating HFpEF. Large, multicenter, prospective randomized controlled trials are needed to further clarify the complexities of HFpEF, which remains as clinically significant as HFrEF.

As the authors rightly pointed out, MRI Global Longitudinal Strain (GLS) could have served as an additional prognostic marker; however, this was not performed due to technical constraints. Regarding LGE, our study found it to be patchy, with no instances exceeding 5% of the total myocardium. In contrast, Kato et al reported a mean LGE of 13.5% (±8.2% SD) in their population, with an ROC threshold of 6% for 80% sensitivity and 77% specificity.6 We believe this difference nullified the prognostic significance of LGE in our specific cohort.

Given these limitations, we advocate for large multicenter trials with standardized inclusion/exclusion criteria—targeting “pure HFpEF"—and uniform measurement protocols to eliminate technical bias. We hope our study represents a meaningful step toward ushering in a new era of cardiac MRI utilization for HFpEF in this region.
