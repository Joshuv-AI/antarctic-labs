SCOPE
The scope of this study is to evaluate the arc start capabilities of various tungsten electrode alloys—2%
Thoriated, 2% Ceriated, 2% Lanthanated, and a proprietary Tri-Mix WS2 on Stainless Steel 304 and
Molybdenum. Each electrode maintained consistent geometry: 0.040" diameter, 2.000" length, and a
0.0010" flat tip with a 40° tip angle. The testing procedure aimed to determine the minimum starting
amperage required to achieve 10 consecutive successful arc starts per electrode type.

An automated Miller/AMET welding system located in TA-03-SM-39 was used for this study. Spot welds
were performed at increasing amperage intervals, and electrode performance was evaluated based on arc
initiation reliability and weld consistency. Metallographic analysis was conducted where allowed,
assessing weld diameter, penetration depth, and microstructural integrity—including grain structure,
porosity, and potential defects.

Results show that the 2% Lanthanated electrode exhibited the most reliable arc starts on Stainless Steel
304. Thoriated electrode consistently underperformed struggling to initiate and producing poor arc
characteristics. These findings support the potential adaptation of safer electrode materials into production
environments.

INTRODUCTION
GTAW Basics

Gas Tungsten Arc Welding (GTAW) is a unique process in comparison to other forms of welding (such
as SMAW, Stick, MIG, etc.). The electrode is primarily composed of tungsten (W), often alloyed with
small amounts of other elements that influence arc characteristics and weld quality. GTAW provides
precise control over weld and heat input. When performed without filler metal, it helps prevent spatter
formation. Shielding gas such as Ar and He are used in GTAW. The function of shielding gas is to
displace atmospheric gases that prevent oxidation and maintain a clean arc and welding environment.

GTAW is typically performed using DC electrode negative (DCEN) polarity, which allows for deeper
weld penetration due to increased heat input from electron bombardment on the workpiece. This setup
also minimizes heat transfer into the electrode, thereby extending the lifespan of the tungsten. In contrast,
using DC electrode positive (DCEP) would result in significant heating of the electrode and produce
shallower weld profiles.

Figure 1: Effect of polarity with the gas tungsten arc welding process. Photo produced by the American Welding Society.

The electrodes used in this study were supplied by Diamond Ground Products and arrived pre-ground to a
40° tip angle. No modifications to the electrode geometry were required. However, the significance of tip
geometry is well understood—accurate electrode dimensions are critical for producing a stable arc and
consistent weld profile. A general rule of thumb is that a smaller tip angle results in a wider arc with
shallower penetration, while a larger (blunter) tip angle produces a more concentrated arc, leading to
deeper penetration.

Process Set-Up

The electrode study is designed to support production and process improvement with the goal of
producing cleaner welds and replicating autogenous welding. Autogenous welding is a fusion welding
process that does not require filler material, the edges of the materials are heated to a suitable temperature
and fused together.

This experimental study focuses on understanding and developing arc start qualities using four different
electrodes with unique compositions: Thorium, Lanthanum, Cerium and a proprietary mixture from
Diamond Ground Product.

Two main systems were engaged for the welding process:

 • AMET/Miller welding system that was designed for conducting autonomous welding in a glove
 box. The AMET controller manages automation aspects of the process. The AMET executes
 logic and welding sequences designed by the team. The AMET has the capacity to conduct
 multiple actions; in our instance we have to linear rails which form our gantry. The gantry
 actuates in an x-axis and z-axis direction. To change the y-axis, there is a micrometer that is
 controlled manually by the operator.

 • The Miller power supply is the electrical generation device that supplies the current output to the
 torch.

There were 2 possible ways to successfully initiate an arc - lift arc and high frequency (HF) start. For
this study only HF was engaged. Prior to welding, four key parameters must be configured: start time,
slope time, start amperage, and sustained amperage.

 • Start time is the duration the initial amperage is held steady before transitioning into the main
 weld sequence.

 • Slope time defines how long the AMET controller allows the Miller power supply to ramp up
 from the start amperage to the sustained amperage.

 • Start amperage is the initial electrical current applied at the moment of arc initiation.

 • Sustained amperage is the steady-state current maintained throughout the welding process.

To initiate the high -frequency start, the AMET system performs a touch-retract step, where the
electrode briefly contacts the workpiece and then withdrawn to a precise distance. The AMET then closes
the contactor on the Miller power supply, which emits a high-frequency electrical current. This current
ionizes the surrounding gas forming a conductive path and initiating the arc. Simultaneously, the Miller
applies the programmed arc start current and timing. Once the arc is detected, the AMET controller
begins executing the automated welding sequence.

 The welding sequences are programmable segments that control all aspects of the welding operation.
These sequences incorporate various parameters that influence the overall weld quality, including pulse-
width modulation (PWM), which helps maintain a consistent current while managing heat input to avoid
excessive thermal inputs – leading to distortion and/or weld defects.

GTAW Drawbacks

In addition to the positive aspects of GTAW there are also notable disadvantages that could cause the
operators to choose another welding process.

 • GTAW requires a significant amount of operator skill to perform welding operations effectively.
 • It is a slow process compared to other welding methods.
 • The process is limited to welding in narrow gaps.
 • Welding thick materials is generally ineffective, as GTAW lacks the heat efficiency required for
 deep penetration.
 • The process is highly sensitive to contaminants, making inclusions more visible and impactful.
 • In outdoor manual welding, torch gas shielding can be lost due to wind.
 • Argon, being a light inert gas, is easily blown away, which can compromise weld quality.

Electrode Composition

It is important to understand and note the various applications of each electrode type. Each electrode
composition allows the operator to customize the process as required. The welding industry is
increasingly exploring alternative electrode types—such as Ceriated, Zirconiated, Lanthanated, and mixed
formulations.

Thoriated still has its place in industry and experimental research, for multiple reasons it is still preferred:

 • Excellent arc starting capabilities – Known for reliable and stable arc initiation, especially in
 DC applications.

 • High current-carrying capacity – Can handle up to 20% more current compared to some non-
 thoriated types.

Los Alamos National Laboratory Page 5

 • Long electrode life – Resists wear and maintains shape well under high-load conditions.

 • Good performance at high temperatures – Suitable for demanding applications where heat
 resistance is critical.

 • Consistent electron emission – Enhances arc stability and helps produce clean, precise welds.

Thorium Additions:

Thorium is an elemental inclusion that improves electron flow from the tip to the work area. Thorium
content can range from 1.7% to 2.2%, most of the variation comes from manufacturer control. The desire
to move away from Thorium comes from the radioactive nature of this element. Thoriated tungsten
contains thorium oxide (ThO2 or thoria) and is the most used electrode in America. Thoriated is a general
tungsten and has one of the lowest required amperage settings. This electrode also performs well with
overloading and extra amperage. According to DGP the thoria delivers a 20 percent higher current
carrying capacity, so more electrons will bombard the plate and ionize those material compounds. It is
also claimed to have a longer life span and greater resistance to contamination. Arc starting is also said to
be easier and more stable than pure tungsten and Zirconiated electrodes. DGP’s thoriated electrodes were
designed to be run with DCEN settings.

Cerium oxide (CeO2) additions:

Ceriated electrodes are the first non-radioactive electrodes that the team will explore. Ceriated electrodes
contain 2% cerium oxide (CeO2). This electrode type is known to excel at DC welding due to its low
working conditions. DGP states that their electrodes require 20 percent less current than thoriated.
Ceriated is also preferred for small and thin parts. Due to higher migration rates (mainly due to grain
boundary growth), these electrodes perform more desirable at the beginning of the life cycle. Something
to note is that when operated at lower amperages, they will last longer than thoriated. For higher
amperage applications, thoriated and Lanthanated electrode perform better. During the team’s study with
stainless steel, it was evident that the Ceriated electrodes seemed to hit a performance wall at 70 amperes:
with Lanthanated outperforming at every amperage setting. Interesting to note, the Ceriated electrode did
far and away, better than the thoriated electrode at all settings.

Lanthanum oxide (La2O3) additions:

Lanthanated electrodes contain 2% lanthanum oxide (La2O3) referred to as Lanthana. Lanthanated are
chosen over 2% thoriated electrodes in most application all across Europe and Japan. Lanthanated
trioxide has the lowest work requirements and starts the easiest at lower amperages, this claim is also
reflected in the SS-L-1 dataset, discussed below. Since the requirements for a functioning electrode are
lower, there is less required heat at the tip which helps to prevent unwanted grain growth. DGP gives their
2%L three desirable characteristics.

 • It excels at resisting the thermal shock of pulse welding.

 • It excels in situations where there are multiple re-ignitions with short weld cycles.

 • It excels at resisting contamination and inclusions.

DGP states that their specific electrode requires 15% less amps to start and sustain low current arcs.
Lanthanum is also non-radioactive and for some unknown reason, has not been marketed heavily in the
US domestic market.

System Set-up:
By design, operations in the development lab are intended to closely mimic those in production. As a
result, the setup phase was structured to align closely with production practices. During the first few
weeks, the team focused on learning to operate Big Blue and establishing stable environmental
conditions. To achieve this, a fully sealed argon environment was implemented within Big Blue, and the
team was successful in maintaining it.

 Big Blue (BB), a Rocky Flats-era glove box (GB) relocated to
 Los Alamos National Laboratory, features a critical component
 known as the autoregulatory Dri-Train. This system maintains

 from the atmosphere, allowing the substance of oxygen levels

 critical part of the study

 The Dri-Train, a part of the GB system, which is used to cycle
 the atmosphere, is composed of two copper beds that strip the

 considered as contaminants, severely interfere with our GTAW
 process. These elements promote oxidation, porosity, or even
 unwanted inclusions, which are detrimental to the welding
 process. Dri-Train must be engaged only when the O2 content is
 less than 250 ppm in Big Blue, if we were to open the Dri-Train
 above 250 ppm, it would saturate the bed immediately.

Workings of the Dri-Train System:
The Dri-Train operates by continuously circulating inert gas through a series of purification components.
The first component in the system is a blower—a turbine that moves gas through a closed loop composed
of the glovebox and a purification column containing catalyst beds. As the inert gas flows through these
beds, contaminants are stripped from the glovebox atmosphere through chemical reactions with copper.
When oxygen reacts with the copper, it forms copper oxide, which is then expelled through the
regeneration process.
                                      2 Cu (s) + O₂ (g) → 2 CuO (s)

The Dri-Train regeneration process restores the glovebox’s oxygen and moisture filtration capacity. It
uses a gas mixture of 5% nitrogen and 95% hydrogen. These regeneration gas reduce the oxidized copper
(CuO) back to metallic copper (Cu) at high temperatures (200-300 Celsius). The hydrogen reacts with the
CuO, which forms water vapor (H2O) and then is heated and evaporated with the systems drying
columns. The beds would be regenerated every three to five days, to maintain and stabilize the
atmosphere.

Once the desired atmosphere was achieved in the glovebox, the team was able to begin the welding
process.

RESULTS FOR STAINLESS STEEL 304
ARC AND WELD EVALUATION

A 2% thoriated tungsten electrode served as the benchmark for both performance and weld quality and
therefore was chosen to establish the baseline. During the parameterization phase, all preliminary testing
was conducted using this electrode type.

We noticed that the arc initiations were inconsistent and regularly exhibited a range of undesirable
behaviors, including arc dancing, plasma cloud buildup, plasma cloud dancing, plasma backflow, edge
plate welding, and audible fizzling. These characteristics were observed optically—each weld attempt
was monitored in real time through welding shield glass by the operator, who recorded qualitative
descriptive notes in a dedicated welding spreadsheet.

Below are some definitions for the terminology used along this report:

 • Arc dancing occurs when the arc fails to establish a stable path to the workpiece, causing it to
 jump erratically across the plate surface.
 • Plasma cloud buildup refers to the formation of a reactive argon-arc gas cloud.
 • Plasma cloud dancing describes this cloud forming into a funnel shape and wandering across the
 plate surface—visually resembling a plasma “tornado.”
 • Plasma backflow is when this plasma cloud is pulled back into the torch cup, interfering with the
 gas lens screens.
 • Edge plate welding happens when the arc fails to strike at the intended location and instead
 initiates along the edge of the plate.

SUMMARY
Thoriated
Produced the lowest performance overall, with only ~53% successful starts (non-filing) and ~68% with
filing. Welds showed poor initiation, discoloration, and instability. Perfect starts dropped to ~32% in non-
filing and ~48% with filing. Overall, thoriated electrodes underperformed across all amperage ranges.

Lanthanated
Best-performing electrode, with 80% success (non-filing) and 88.3% (filing). Welds were consistent,
clean, and stable across the amperage range. Results showed minimal discoloration and uniform
penetration. Lanthanated consistently outperformed all other electrodes in arc stability and weld quality.

Ceriated
Performed moderately, improving from 63.3% (non-filing) to 86.7% (filing). The best results were at
lower amperages, though arc instability appeared at higher currents. Welds showed raised solidifications
at mid-range conditions. Overall, Ceriated was effective but less consistent than Lanthanated.

Mixed
Second-best performer, achieving 47 (non-filing) and 52 (filing) successful starts. Welds were stable
overall but showed oxidation and pitting at higher amperages. Performance was comparable to Ceriated at

lower ranges but more consistent across the gradient. The mixed electrode ranked just below Lanthanated
in overall reliability.
