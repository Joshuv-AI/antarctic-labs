Project Overview
In this project we sought to elucidate the interstellar origin for heavy metal isotopes relevant to
dating interstellar timescales like the age of our solar system and galaxy. We fielded
accelerator-based experiments to study hafnium and rhenium cosmochronometer isotopes
intending to utilize the resulting data to constrain the production rates using the so-called Oslo
method. Deuterium bombardment of hafnium-180 was performed at Florida State University
(FSU) in preparation for future tritium beam experiments which allowed us to demonstrate
significant elements of the experiment feasibility of the tritium reaction for producing
cosmochronometer hafnium-182. In addition, alpha particle bombardment of tungsten-186 was
performed at the University of Oslo to constrain nuclear properties relevant to the interstellar
production rate of rhenium-187, a galactic cosmochronometer. The outcomes of the project are
a predictive simulation tool for future tritium experiment planning at FSU, and results of the Oslo
experiment on the production rate of rhenium-187. Both analyses are nearing completion and
publication.

Mission Impact
While driven by fundamental science motivations, the experimental, analytical techniques and
collaborations developed and strengthened during this project directly impact national security
missions and will support the NNSA goal to advance the science, technology, and engineering
competencies that are the foundation of those missions. This research advances core
competencies in Nuclear, Chemical, and Isotopic Science and Technology and High Energy
Density Science.
