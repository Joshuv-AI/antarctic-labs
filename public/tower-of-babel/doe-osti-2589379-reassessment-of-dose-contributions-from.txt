 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
During a recent revision to the groundwater and inadvertent human intruder screening analysis
report (Aleman and Hamm, 2023), weaknesses within the screening logic were observed resulting
in the addition of U-235 being brought back into the list of radionuclides requiring inventory limits
within the Paducah Cask special waste form (SWF). In addition, while reviewing the earlier vadose
zone transport analysis efforts, an inconsistency was observed in the initial U-235P inventory that
was supplied to the Consolidated Waste Tracking System (CWTS) back in 2009.
This report documents the groundwater (GW) and inadvertent human intruder (IHI) updated
analyses for the Paducah Cask SWF. GW and IHI inventory limits and doses were computed for
U-235P, but U-238P was included in the GW analysis due to its role in the total uranium solubility-
controlled release mechanism.
The GW analysis in the revised E-Area Low-Level Waste Facility (ELLWF) Performance
Assessment (PA) was performed using a total uranium solubility-controlled release mechanism in
the vadose zone, along with updated aquifer flow and transport models. For IHI inventory limits
and doses the standard generic assumptions are applied with exceptions: no dynamic compaction
of the waste: and waste zone located at the bottom of ST05.

1 Introduction
The Paducah Cask was considered to no longer be viable as a transportation cask in 2003 and was
slated for burial as activated scrap. To take full advantage of the disposal of this cask with its
existing shielding and confinement capabilities, a variety of waste constituents to be loaded within
this cask were considered. Once waste was selected, confirmed, and loaded, this Paducah Cask
was buried in 2005 within Slit Trench 5 (ST05).
Solid Waste Management (SWM) proposed the disposal of several waste forms within the shielded
Paducah Cask and requested SRNL to perform an evaluation for disposing of the Paducah Cask in
a slit trench. The evaluation was done by Hiergesell and Cook (2003) and later SRNL performed
a Special Analysis (SA) for disposal of the Paducah Cask by Hiergesell (2004). In this SA more
recently available transport properties were employed. In the follow-on 2008 Performance
Assessment (PA), updated analyses for the Paducah Cask were performed where a solubility-
controlled release mechanism was chosen making this a special waste form (SWF) analysis. In the
revised E-Area Low-Level Waste facility (ELLWF) PA the vadose zone (VZ) model (solubility-
controlled source terms) was reused along with the updated aquifer flow and transport models.
During a recent revision to the Groundwater (GW) and Inadvertent Human Intruder (IHI)
screening analysis report (Aleman and Hamm, 2023), weaknesses within the screening logic were
observed resulting in the addition of U-235 being brought back into the list of radionuclides
requiring inventory limits within the SWF referred to as the Paducah Cask. In addition, while
reviewing the earlier (VZ) transport analysis efforts, an inconsistency was observed in the initial
U-235 inventory that was supplied to the Consolidated Waste Tracking System (CWTS) back in
2009. This SWF resides within the ELLWF in ST05. Note that radionuclides involving a SWF
have a letter designation appended to its name (e.g., U-235 within the SWF Paducah Cask is
referred to as U-235P). For the Air and Radon screening no such findings were observed.
Based on these recent assessments the following list of parent radionuclides have been included
back into the required list of radionuclides requiring inventory limits:
 • GW analyses: U-235P
   •   IHI analyses: Th-232P, U-235P, and U-238P
For GW inventory limits within the ELLWF PA, the Paducah Cask SWF radionuclides of interest
are handled where the release mechanism is assumed to be solubility controlled. Due to the
magnitude of material present and the rates of estimated dissolution, this solubility-controlled
assumption applies for many thousands of years. For IHI inventory limits and doses the standard
generic assumptions are applied with exceptions: no dynamic compaction of the waste; and waste
zone located at the bottom of ST05.
For each of these parent radionuclides, inventory limits for the GW and IHI pathways are created.
For GW limits, the solubility-controlled source terms (in tabular form) are input to PORFLOW
VZ transport simulations creating flux-to-the-water table (WT) sources. These fluxes are then
input to PORFLOW aquifer simulations to compute concentrations of the parent and its short-
chain daughters at the 100-m Point of Assessment (POA). GW and IHI maximum dose factors are
generated where inventory limits per pathway and time widows are created. Each of these
computational pieces are discussed within this report and this report serves as the new technical
baseline for their inventory limits.

2 Paducah Cask Details
The Paducah Cask is a transportation cask that utilizes uranium metal as its primary gamma shield.
The Paducah Cask is comprised of Type 304 Stainless Steel (SS304), cast depleted uranium (DU)
shield plugs for axial shielding, and rolled and laminated depleted uranium (LDU) sheets for the
radial shield. The Paducah cask utilizes twelve laminated uranium metal sheets that are rolled into
a cylindrical geometry as its radial shielding. The top and bottom shields are cast uranium metal.
The cask has 52 circumferential stainless-steel fins that are three inches in length. The base region
of the cask is composed primarily of stainless-steel plates and a 7.75-inch extension of the cask
outer stainless-steel shell, thus leaving considerable free volume within the base region of the cask.
The cask cavity is 50.5-inches in length and is 17-inches in diameter. The overall length of the
cask is ~7 feet with an outer diameter of ~3 feet.
The Paducah Cask is a very structurally sound container that is expected to remain structurally
intact over thousands of years. However, due to potential local corrosion pitting at the seals,
hydraulic integrity is conservatively assumed to fail at the end of institutional control (EIC) (at the
Year 171). At seal failure it’s further assumed that all hydraulic resistance associated with the
metal structure components is completely lost.
The following discussions provide details regarding the Paducah Cask SWF and its burial within
ST05 that are employed in the GW and IHI inventory analyses that follow.

2.3 Time Dependent Flow Information
Based on the structural integrity of the Paducah Cask, the likelihood of collapse within the first
few thousand years is considered low. Since there will be no other waste package placed near this
structure, the likelihood on a local subsidence event is low as well. Thus, for limit analysis
purposes only the intact scenario needs consideration.
Time dependent GW flow information is required for the region locally surrounding the location
of the Paducah Cask. The average flowrate through the local WZ is required. This information was
extracted from the 3-D flow simulations performed using PORFLOW. These multidimensional
flow field parameters were averaged over the DC Waste zone where the Paducah Cask was placed.
The DC Waste zone represents the bottom 11.9 feet of the excavated trenches. The Paducah Cask
makes up a small volume fraction of the WZ and for the VZ modeling local soil properties
dominant.
The average value of Darcy velocity was computed across ST05 within its material zone referred
to as DC Waste. Relative time in years beyond the opening of E-Area in 1995 is employed. The
resulting values are shown in Figure 2-6 where the following key time periods can be seen:
 • 0-10 years – ST05 is uncovered, and no waste is buried
 • At year 10 – waste is buried within ST05
 • 10-16 years – ST05 is uncovered and has buried waste
 • 16-71 years - ST05 is covered with an operational cover and maintained
 • 71-171 years - ST05 is covered with an interim cover and maintained

 6

 • 171-beyond – final cover is placed over entire units with outyear degradation
The Darcy velocity values are shown in Figure 2-6.

Figure 2-6. Average Darcy velocity across the DC Waste zone region for the intact scenario.
The local WZ is restricted to only having the Paducah Cask and no other generic waste (i.e., neither
crushable nor non-crushable containers are present).
As such, dynamic compaction will not be performed over this local WZ region, and the Paducah
Cask is assumed to be an intact vessel throughout the 1000-year compliance period (CP). Only the
intact scenario for limits analyses is considered since subsidence appears very unlikely.
The initial state of the Paducah Cask is a structurally strong stainless-steel vessel that is
hydraulically sealed. Over time due to corrosion pitting at joints, the hydraulic seal will fail but
estimates of such failure places that point in time many hundreds of years after burial.
Conservatively, we are assuming that complete hydraulic failure occurs at the end of institutional
control (EIC) in the year 2165 (i.e., ~160 years after the first burial in ST05). This 160-year period
is a “bake” period where the initial uranium isotopes decay, building in ingrowth inventory of their
daughters within the open volume inside the Paducah Cask. Beyond the EIC period the region
containing the Paducah Cask acts as a source term without any hydraulic resistance to GW
movement.

3.2 Source Term Conceptual Model
For GW purposes a simple conceptual solubility-controlled dissolution model is employed to
address radionuclide migration exiting a local WZ that contains the SWF referred to as the
“Paducah Demonstration Cask” or more simply the “Paducah Cask.” The simple conceptual model
is highlighted in Figure 3-1.

  Figure 3-1. Conceptual model for dissolution and migration through a local waste zone
 region.
Within the VZ clean GW enters at the top of the local WZ and flows vertically through this WZ
absorbing radionuclides from surrounding solid (i.e., soil and metal) materials. It is assumed that
negligible amounts of the radionuclides exist within the gas phase. It is also assumed that local

 8

thermodynamic equilibrium is maintained at the liquid (w - water) and solid (s - soil) phase
interfaces (I).
During the burial years, up to the EIC, the Paducah Cask is assumed to be hydraulically sealed.
During this time (referred to as a bake time) the Paducah Cask is isolated within the local WZ, and
only radioactive decay and ingrowth of chain members is considered. Beyond the EIC the
interfacial dissolution process proceeds under the constraint of a solubility-controlled release
mechanism.
Based on the geometric information provided in Table 2-1, a local WZ model was established.

4 Inadvertent Human Intruder Analyses
Performance objectives are assessed for the IHI who may physically disrupt the facility at some
time after the end of IC and when the POA is at the DUs.

 30

The compliance period is 1,000 years (calendar years 2165 to 3165), commencing after the 100-
year IC period, which runs from operational closure in calendar year 2065 to 2165. The applicable
performance measures are 500 mrem EDE for acute exposure scenarios and 100 mrem yr-1 for
chronic exposure scenarios. The POs for IHI scenarios are radionuclide and DU specific inventory
limits or trigger values that will be monitored during ELLWF operations based on a SOF approach.

4.1 Intruder Human Intruder Scenarios
The following six stylized exposure scenarios are considered for inadvertent human intruders in
the ELLWF (Smith et al., 2019):
 • Acute Basement Construction: IHI constructs a basement and encounters waste during
 excavation which is inadvertently mixed with clean soil and diluted.
 • Acute Well Drilling: IHI drills a water well through waste and is exposed to drill cuttings
 mixed with clean soil that are brought to the surface.
 • Acute Discovery: IHI begins constructing a basement but stops when encountering the
 riprap in the final closure cap and is exposed to photon radiation from unexcavated material
 residing in the undisturbed waste zone.
 • Chronic Agriculture: Resident IHI is exposed to waste that was excavated for basement
 construction and mixed with native soil in the intruder’s vegetable garden.
 • Chronic Post-Drilling: Resident IHI is exposed to waste from drill cuttings mixed with
 native soil and scattered in the garden area.
 • Chronic Residential: Resident IHI is exposed to external radiation while in home located
 above waste with shielding provided by the concrete basement floor and any soil or
 engineered material remaining between the basement and waste.
These scenarios assume that institutional control and memory are lost in calendar year 2165 (100
years after operational closure in calendar year 2065) and that the IHI is on site after that time.
Waste is assumed to be indistinguishable from, and mistaken for, soil. However, an IHI is assumed
to be able to recognize concrete covers and erosion barriers when they are encountered. These
scenarios and their associated exposure pathways are summarized in Table 1-2 (Smith et al., 2019)
The total acute dose to an IHI is the sum of individual doses due to the three acute scenarios, as
applicable. Therefore, the individual constructing the basement is the same person who drills the
well, and who discovers the waste. Likewise, the IHI chronic dose is the sum of the three individual
chronic exposure scenarios. Note that these scenarios do not include direct ingestion of
contaminated GW or the use of contaminated GW for irrigation.

4.2 Inadvertent Human Intruder Model Details
The intruder model consists of the U-235 parent radionuclide uniformly distributed throughout the
depleted uranium shielding volume of the Paducah cask with no depletion of the parent or progeny
inventories over time due to leaching from infiltration. The Paducah Cask is lying on its side in
the bottom 3-foot section of Trench 5F within ST05. There is a bounding region surrounding the
Paducah Cask where no other forms of waste can be buried, and no dynamic compaction of waste
is allowed. The full (un-collapsed) 16-foot waste height of ST05 was assumed in determining
whether waste zone contaminants were within the standard 3-meter excavation for the acute

 31

basement construction scenario. The waste zone represents the areal footprint (polygon EFGH)
and waste height (3-foot diameter) of the Paducah Cask. The initial inventory of U-235 is set equal
to 1012 pCi at a burial time of 9.664 years (5/27/2004) after the start of the ELLWF. The initial
activities of progeny are set to zero.
IHI inventory limits and doses are sensitive to the 3-D geometry of ST05 as well as the overlying
material. Critical dimensions of DUs are shown in Figure 4-1. Vertical profiles are presented for
the various types of DUs, along with the final depths below backfill and the engineered cover. The
Paducah Casks resides in the bottom 3 feet of the uncompacted ST05 nominal waste zone. The
initial depth below the CAP (ground surface) to the top of the Paducah Cask is 24 feet.

 Figure 4-1. Critical Vertical Dimensions of ELLWF Disposal Units for IHI Analysis.
Residential basements are assumed to be 3 meters deep; therefore, the acute basement construction
and chronic agricultural scenarios do not apply if the top of the waste zone is deeper than 3 meters
(9.84 feet) bgs. However, it is assumed that 0.9144 meters (3 feet) of soil in the post-closure cover

5 Dose and Inventory Limit Analyses
The analyses presented earlier provide the necessary information to compute:
 • GW preliminary inventory limits – these inventory values are incorporated into the
 revised ELLWF PA where final inventory limits per GW pathway are created using the
 most recent GW Plume Interaction Factors (GW PIFs).
 • IHI final inventory limits – for IHI analyses the preliminary and final inventory limits are
 equivalent since the POA is at the surface of each DU and no plume interaction takes place.
Note that inventory limits for the Air and Radon pathways are not required for these radionuclide
parents since they were screened out.

5.1 GW Pathways
In this section we are focused on the calculational aspects associated with determining the
“preliminary” inventory limits and corresponding doses for the various GW pathways of concern.
The following subsections describe the calculational sequences involved.
The preliminary DU-specific GW inventory limits are provided in this section for each parent
radionuclide of interest. These are not generic limits since specific engineered barriers are

employed within the computational vadose zone model (total uranium solubility-controlled source

subsidence potential; however, for the portion of trench 5F where the Paducah Cask resides no
non-crushable containers are allowed. Thus, for the SWF Paducah Cask only the intact scenario is
considered and by default this scenario becomes the maximum case, CaseMX.
