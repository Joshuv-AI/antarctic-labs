Ishii’s conjecture and Bridgeland stability conditions for dihedral reflection groups
Shu Nimura

Abstract
. We provide a new proof of Ishii’s conjecture for any dihedral reflection group G ⊂ G ​ L 2 ​ ( ℂ ) G\subset GL_{2}(\mathbb{C}) from the viewpoint of Bridgeland stability conditions. Our strategy is to reduce the problem, via the derived McKay correspondence, to a geometric construction of Bridgeland stability conditions on the root stack of the maximal resolution along the strict transform of the discriminant divisor.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

One of the central themes in the McKay correspondence is the relationship between birational geometry and moduli spaces of representations of noncommutative algebras.
Craw and Ishii proposed a conjecture
[
11
]
on the crepant resolutions of
\mathbb{C}^{3}/G
for any finite subgroup
G\subset SL_{3}(\mathbb{C})
: every crepant resolution arises as a moduli space of
\theta
-stable representations by varying King stability
\theta\in\Theta(G)

[
22
]
. The conjecture was proved for abelian groups in
SL_{3}(\mathbb{C})
by
[
11
]
and for general finite groups in
SL_{3}(\mathbb{C})
by
[
39
]
. Furthermore, inspired by the DK hypothesis, Ishii proposed the following conjecture in dimension two, extending the Craw–Ishii conjecture to the non-crepant setting:

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
G\subset GL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be any finite subgroup. Then, a resolution of
\mathbb{C}^{2}/G
is dominated by the maximal resolution of the pair
(\mathbb{C}^{2}/G,B)
if and only if
X\cong M_{\theta}
for some generic
\theta\in\Theta(G)
.

For the definitions, see §2. Here, we only note that if
B=0
(equivalently, if
G
acts freely in codimension one), the crepant resolution, when it exists, is both minimal and maximal. Ishii proved the “if” part in full generality, and also the “only if” part for arbitrary abelian subgroups and for small finite subgroups
G\subset GL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, i.e., when
G
acts freely in codimension one. Later,
[
9
]
proved the conjecture for dihedral reflection groups. This paper aims to give a new proof of Ishii’s conjecture in the case of dihedral reflection groups via Bridgeland stability conditions and wall-crossing phenomena.

1.2.
Bridgeland stability

Bridgeland stability conditions were introduced by Bridgeland
[
6
]
inspired by Douglas’s work on
\Pi
-stability
[
13
]
. Bridgeland stability conditions play a central role in the study of triangulated categories arising in birational geometry, representation theory, enumerative geometry, and mirror symmetry. Bridgeland stability can be regarded as a generalization of classical notions of stability, such as Gieseker stability for sheaves on a variety and King stability for representations of a noncommutative algebra.

In the context of birational geometry of surfaces, Toda interpreted the surface MMP in terms of wall-crossing phenomena for moduli spaces of Bridgeland stable objects
[
36
]
. We briefly review Toda’s work. Let
Y
be a smooth projective surface. The statement is formulated in the ‘real part’ of
\mathrm{Stab}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}
of the stability manifold
\mathrm{Stab}(Y)
. This space fits into the following cartesian diagram (see also §2)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Stab}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Stab}(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{NS}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K^{\mathrm{num}}(Y)_{\mathbb{C}}^{\vee}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi_{\mathbb{R}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi}

Here, we fix the notation: for a Bridgeland stability condition
\sigma=(Z,\mathcal{A})
,
M_{\sigma}([\mathcal{O}_{y}])
denotes the algebraic space parametrizing
Z
-stable objects
E\in\mathcal{A}
with
[E]=[\mathcal{O}_{y}]
in
K^{\mathrm{num}}(Y)
for
y\in Y
.
Toda proved the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2

(
[
36
]
)
.

Let
Y
be any smooth projective surface. For every smooth contraction
Y\to X
, we can associate a connected open subset
U(X)\subset\mathrm{Stab}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(1)

For any
\sigma\in U(X)
,
M_{\sigma}([\mathcal{O}_{x}])\cong X
.

(2)

If
X,X^{\prime}
are related by a single blowup, then
\overline{U(X)}\cap\overline{U(X^{\prime})}
is nonempty and has real codimension one.

To prove this, Toda constructs a local section of
\Pi_{\mathbb{R}}

\mu^{\prime}_{\mathrm{geom}}:A^{\mathrm{tot}}:=\bigcup_{Y\to X}\overline{A^{\dagger}}(X)\to\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathbb{R}}(Y),

where the union runs over all smooth contractions

Y\to X
, and
\overline{A^{\dagger}}(X)
is a subset of the Neron-Severi group
\mathrm{NS}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}
.
U(X)
is defined to be the image of the interior
A^{\dagger}(X)
.

On the other hand, in the context of representation theory of algebras, Bayer–Craw–Zhang constructed a continuous map
[
2
]
,

\mu^{\prime}_{\mathrm{alg}}:\Theta(G)\times\Lambda_{1}\to\mathrm{Stab}([\mathbb{C}^{2}/G]),

where
\Lambda_{1}
is a certain space of additional parameters. Via
\mu^{\prime}_{\mathrm{alg}}
, one can identify King stability and Bridgeland stability, allowing one to interpret Ishii’s conjecture in terms of Bridgeland moduli (see §4.1 for the details.)

1.3.
Main results

Our strategy for Ishii’s conjecture is to construct an analogous map to
\mu^{\prime}_{\mathrm{geom}}
for a certain DM stack
\mathcal{Y}
and to compare
\mu^{\prime}_{\mathrm{geom}}
and
\mu^{\prime}_{\mathrm{alg}}
via the McKay correspondence.

To state our results more precisely, we prepare some notations. Let
G
be a dihedral reflection group and set
H:=G\cap SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Let
Y_{H}:=H\text{-Hilb}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
be the
H
-Hilbert scheme defined by
[
19
]
.
Y_{H}
admits a natural action of
G/H\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, and we consider
\mathcal{Y}:=[Y_{H}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}]
, which can be regarded as the second root stack over the quotient variety
Y:=Y_{H}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
along the branch locus.
Y
is the maximal resolution in Ishii’s conjecture. We write
\pi:\mathcal{Y}\to Y
for the coarse moduli map, or the quotient map. In this situation, the universal family of
H\text{-Hilb}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
with
G/H
-action induces an exact equivalence

\Phi:D_{c}(\mathcal{Y})\cong D_{c}([\mathbb{C}^{2}/G]),

where
D_{c}(-)
denotes the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves whose cohomology sheaves are compactly supported. This provides the natural setting for Bridgeland stability for our purposes since the corresponding Bridgeland moduli spaces are well-behaved (unlike the derived category
D_{Z}(-)
supported on a fixed compact subscheme
Z
).

To formulate the analogue of Toda’s work, we introduce
\mathrm{NS}_{{\mathrm{orb}}}(\mathcal{Y})_{\mathbb{R}}
, the orbifold Néron-Severi group (see §3.3 for the definition). For our purposes, we consider

\mathrm{NS}_{{\mathrm{orb}}}(\mathcal{Y})_{\mathbb{C}}:=\mathrm{NS}_{{\mathrm{orb}}}(\mathcal{Y})_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathbb{C}.

We define
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{n}}(\mathcal{Y})
to be the space of normalized stability conditions, which fits into the following cartesian diagram (see §3.3)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{n}}(\mathcal{Y})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Stab}(\mathcal{Y})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{NS}_{{\mathrm{orb}}}(\mathcal{Y})_{\mathbb{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K^{\mathrm{num}}_{c}(\mathcal{Y})_{\mathbb{C}}^{\vee}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi_{\mathrm{n}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi}

As in the ordinary surface case, we consider the numerical class of skyscraper sheaves on
\mathcal{Y}
, which is explicitly described as
[\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{y}]\in K_{c}^{\mathrm{num}}(\mathcal{Y})
for any
y\in Y
.

Theorem A
.

For any smooth contraction
Y\to X
of the maximal resolution
Y
, we can associate a connected open subset
\mathcal{U}(X)\subset\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{n}}(\mathcal{Y})
such that

(1)

For any
\sigma\in\mathcal{U}(X)
,
M_{\sigma}([\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{y}])\cong X
.

(2)

If
X
and
X^{\prime}
are related by a single blowup, then
\overline{\mathcal{U}(X)}\cap\overline{\mathcal{U}(X^{\prime})}
is nonempty and has real codimension one.

As a result, the birational geometry of the maximal resolution
Y
is encoded by wall-crossing phenomena of Bridgeland moduli spaces. More precisely, we will construct a continuous section of
\Pi_{\mathrm{n}}

\mu_{\mathrm{geom}}:A^{\mathrm{tot}}\times\mathcal{B}:=\left(\bigcup_{Y\to X}\overline{A^{\dagger}}(X)\right)\times\mathcal{B}\to\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{n}}(\mathcal{Y})

where
\overline{A^{\dagger}(X)}\subset\mathrm{NS}_{{\mathrm{orb}}}(\mathcal{Y})_{\mathbb{R}}
is the counterpart of
\overline{A^{\dagger}(X)}
appearing in Toda’s construction, and
\mathcal{B}
is a small open neighborhood of
0
in
\mathrm{NS}_{{\mathrm{orb}}}(\mathcal{Y})_{\mathbb{R}}
that is independent of the contractions. We can think of
\mu_{\mathrm{geom}}
as a small deformation of
\mu^{\prime}_{\mathrm{geom}}
inside
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{n}}(\mathcal{Y})
.
A subtle but crucial point is that the real slice
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{Y})
is too small to compare
\mu^{\prime}_{\mathrm{geom}}
with
\mu^{\prime}_{\mathrm{alg}}
. The two local sections do not admit a nonempty common domain inside
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{Y})
. The deformation by the additional parameter space
\mathcal{B}
is introduced to produce such a common domain. The main point is that, after the deformation, the two local sections glue along the resulting overlap:

Theorem B
.

After identifying the two stability manifolds
\mathrm{Stab}(\mathcal{Y})
and
\mathrm{Stab}([\mathbb{C}^{2}/G])
via the derived equivalence
\Phi
, an explicit change of parameters, and a rotation action, the local sections of
\Pi_{\mathrm{n}}

\mu_{\mathrm{geom}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu^{\prime}_{\mathrm{alg}}

coincide on their common domain.

See Theorem
4.13
for the precise statement. Combining Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
, we obtain the following corollary (Corollary
4.14
, Corollary
4.19
):

Corollary 1.3
.

For each contraction
Y\to X
, we can associate a GIT chamber
V(X)
in
\Theta(G)
such that

(1)

For any
\theta\in V(X)
,
M_{\theta}\cong X
.

(2)

If
X
and
X^{\prime}
are related by a single blowup, then
\overline{V(X)}\cap\overline{V(X^{\prime})}
is nonempty and has real codimension one.

In particular, we recover Capellan’s theorem
[
9
]

that Ishii’s conjecture holds for dihedral reflection groups. An advantage of our approach is that the description of the Bridgeland moduli spaces is very simple: for a contraction
f:\mathcal{Y}\to X
, we construct the moduli space
\{\mathbf{L}f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{x}\mid x\in X\}
. The main difficulty is to realize it as a Bridgeland moduli space and to identify it with a King moduli space. This suggests that Bridgeland stability provides a new perspective on Ishii’s conjecture in a more general setting.

1.4.
Relations with existing works

First, we discuss the relation with Ishii’s approach. Our approach to Ishii’s conjecture is analogous to his. His essential case is when
G\supset\{\pm 1\}=:N
. He considered the crepant resolution
Y_{N}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}/N
, equipped with an action of
\overline{G}
. Given a smooth contraction
Y\to X
, he proved that
X
can be realized as a moduli space of
\overline{G}
-constellations on
Y_{N}
. In other words, he realized
X
as the moduli space of objects in
\operatorname{coh}_{c}[Y_{N}/\overline{G}]\subset D_{c}([Y_{N}/\overline{G}])
, which is derived equivalent to
D_{c}([\mathbb{C}^{2}/G])
.
On the other hand, our first aim is to realize
X
as the moduli space of complexes in
D_{c}(\mathcal{Y})
, which is derived equivalent to
D_{c}([\mathbb{C}^{2}/G])
. To put it simply, we aim to modify Ishii’s approach to a more flexible framework, namely, Bridgeland moduli spaces.
Ishii’s proof cannot be applied directly to our case. Indeed, Ishii uses the fact that minimal resolution dominates
Y_{N}/\overline{G}
, which is not true in our situation since our
\mathbb{C}^{2}/G
is already the ‘minimal resolution’.

Next, we discuss the relation with existing works on Bridgeland stability. While Bridgeland stability conditions have been extensively studied for many classes of smooth projective surfaces and certain threefolds, relatively few works treat stability conditions on
D_{c}(X)
for non-projective varieties
X
; see, for example,
[
2
]
. Our formulation mainly follows the framework developed in
[
2
]
.

On the other hand, there are several works on stability conditions for Deligne–Mumford stacks, including projective Kleinian orbisurfaces whose coarse moduli spaces are projective surfaces with only Kleinian singularities
[
25
]
. Stability conditions have also been studied for root stacks over projective curves
[
32
]
and over projective surfaces
[
26
]
. We note that
[
26
]
mainly constructs stability conditions corresponding to the large volume limit. Although the class of stacks considered here is rather special, our setting admits a rich family of stability conditions. Some wall-crossing phenomena appearing in our construction (cf. Lemma
3.22
, Lemma
4.12
) already appeared implicitly in the one-dimensional projective settings
[
32
]
. Other wall-crossing phenomena can be regarded as direct generalizations of Toda’s work
[
36
]
.

It is also worth mentioning that induced stability provides a useful method for studying Bridgeland stability conditions on
G
-equivariant derived categories, as developed in
[
27
]

[
31
]
. Such induced stability conditions are often easier to analyze. This viewpoint plays a key role in the comparison between the two constructions of stability conditions carried out in §4.

1.5.
Plan of the paper

In §2, we review the precise statement of Ishii’s conjecture and several related notions. We also recall some preliminary results on Bridgeland stability conditions, especially for (non-projective) surfaces.
In §3, we construct the map
\mu_{\mathrm{geom}}
using Toda’s construction. We also prove Theorem
A
except for the proof of the existence of the Bridgeland moduli spaces.
In §4, we prove Theorem
B
. Using the gluing result established there, we also resolve the existence problem of the moduli spaces, thereby completing the proof of Theorem
A
.
Finally, in §5, for the reader’s convenience, we sketch a proof of the support property for stability conditions on non-projective surfaces.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Representations of dihedral groups

For
n\geq 1
, we consider

G:=\left\langle\alpha,\beta\right\rangle\subset GL(2,\mathbb{C})

where

\alpha:=\begin{pmatrix}\zeta_{n}&0\\
0&\zeta_{n}^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\,\,\beta:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\,\,\zeta_{n}:=\exp{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{n}}.

Put
H:=G\cap SL(2,\mathbb{C})=\left\langle\alpha\right\rangle
. Then
G/H=\langle\beta\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. We write the representation of
H
by

\rho_{i}:H\to\mathbb{C}^{*},\alpha\mapsto\zeta_{n}^{i}.

We denote the set of irreducible representations modulo isomorphism by
\mathrm{Irr}H,\mathrm{Irr}G
.
There is an action of
G/H
on
\mathrm{Irr}H
by

\beta\cdot\rho(h):=\rho(\beta^{-1}h\beta).

Then the action fixes
\rho_{0}
if
n
is odd or
\rho_{0},\rho_{\frac{n}{2}}
if
n
is even. Any other irreducible representation
\rho_{i}

belongs to the free orbit
\{\rho_{i},\rho_{n-i}\}
. Let
\operatorname{sgn}
denote the nontrivial irreducible representation of
G/H
and regard it as an irreducible representation of
G
. By the Clifford theory, the irreducible representations of
G
are given by

\mathrm{Irr}G=\begin{cases}\{\rho_{0}^{+},\rho_{0}^{-},\rho_{i}\oplus\rho_{n-i}\mid i=1,\dots,\frac{n-1}{2}\}&\text{if $n$: odd}\\
\{\rho_{0}^{+},\rho_{0}^{-},\rho_{\frac{n}{2}}^{+},\rho_{\frac{n}{2}}^{-},\rho_{i}\oplus\rho_{n-i}\mid i=1,\dots,\frac{n}{2}-1\}&\text{if $n$: even}\end{cases}

where
\rho_{i}^{+}:=\rho_{i},\,\,\rho_{i}^{-}:=\rho_{i}^{+}\otimes\operatorname{sgn}
for
i=0,\frac{n}{2}
.

2.2.
Moduli space of G-constellations

For a finite subgroup
G\subset GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
G
-Hilbert scheme
[
29
]

[
19
]
is defined to be the moduli space of
G
-clusters
, where a
G
-cluster is a
G
-invariant 0-dimensional subscheme

of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

is isomorphic to the regular representation
\mathbb{C}[G]
. As a generalization of

-clusters, Craw-Ishii introduced the notion of
G
-constellations

[
11
]
. A
G
-constellation is a 0-dimensional
G
-equivariant sheaf

on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
H^{0}(E)\cong\mathbb{C}[G]
. We write

v:=[\mathbb{C}[G]\otimes\mathcal{O}_{0}]\in K_{c}^{\mathrm{num}}([\mathbb{C}^{2}/G])

(see also Section
2.5
). For any
\theta\in\Theta(G):=v^{\perp}\subset\operatorname{Hom}(K_{c}^{\mathrm{num}}([\mathbb{C}^{2}/G]),\mathbb{R})
, the notion of
\theta
-stability for
G
-constellation is defined. We note that any
\theta
-stable

-constellation is
\theta
-semistable by definition. We say that
\theta\in\Theta(G)
is

if any
\theta
-semistable
G
-constellation is
\theta
-stable. We consider the moduli functor defined as follows:

\mathcal{M}_{\theta}(v)(S):=\{\text{flat families of $\theta$-stable $G$-constellations on $\mathbb{C}^{n}$}\}/\sim

Here, a

\theta
-stable
G
-constellations
is a
G
-equivariant sheaf
E_{S}
on
S\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
flat over

such that each fiber

is a
\theta
-stable

-constellation for every closed point

. For two flat families

, we write

. Using King’s construction of the moduli space of representations of a quiver

is represented by a quasi-projective scheme

[

We also note that the set of generic King stability parameters forms a dense open subset of

consisting of finitely many open convex polyhedral cones (e.g.
[

). We call this decomposition the GIT wall-and-chamber structure.

2.3.
Maximal resolutions and Ishii’s conjecture

We briefly review Ishii’s conjecture and the geometry of maximal resolutions for dihedral reflection groups.

We first recall the definition of maximal resolution. Let
(X,B)
be a klt pair. A resolution
f:Y\to X
is a
maximal resolution
if the exceptional divisors of
f
are precisely the prime divisors over
X
with non-positive discrepancies. The maximal resolution exists uniquely in dimension two as in
[
23
]
. In the case of
X=\mathbb{C}^{2}/G
, we define the boundary divisor
B
by the equality
\nu^{*}(K_{\mathbb{C}^{2}/G}+B)=K_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}
for
\nu:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}/G
. If
G
is a dihedral reflection group, we can write
B=\frac{1}{2}D
by the discriminant divisor
D
. Now, we restate the conjecture by Ishii

Conjecture 2.1

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
G\subset GL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be a finite subgroup and consider the
quotient
\mathbb{C}^{2}/G
with the boundary divisor
B
defined as above. For any resolution of singularities
f:X\to\mathbb{C}^{2}/G
, there is a generic
\theta\in\Theta(G)
such that
X\cong M_{\theta}(v)
if and only if there is a morphism
Y\to X\to\mathbb{C}^{2}/G
.
Here,
Y
is the maximal resolution of
(\mathbb{C}^{2}/G,B)
.

Ishii’s conjecture for dihedral reflection groups was proved by
Capellan
[
9
]
by using the variation of GIT for

G
-constellations on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
(see Remark
4.20
). We next review the geometry of maximal
resolutions in this case, following
[
17
]
.
Let
Y_{H}
be the minimal resolution of the quotient singularity
\mathbb{A}^{2}/H
of type
A_{n-1}
, whose exceptional locus consists of
n-1
exceptional curves
E_{1},\dots,E_{n-1}
such that

E_{i-1}\cap E_{i}
consists of a point.
Connected components of the fixed point locus of the action of
G/H
on
Y_{H}

are smooth divisors as in
[
17
, Lemma 2.1(1)]
.
Moreover, the action of
G/H
exchanges the strict transforms in
Y_{H}
of the images of the two coordinate axes of
\mathbb{A}^{2}
, one intersecting
E_{1}

and the other intersecting
E_{n-1}
.
Then, it also exchanges
E_{i}
with
E_{n-i}
,
and no exceptional curve
E_{i}
is contained in the fixed point set
R:=(Y_{H})^{G/H}
.
Therefore,
R
coincides with the strict transform
{\widetilde{D}}\subset Y
of the discriminant divisor
D\subset X
.
It follows that
Y:=Y_{H}/(G/H)
is smooth and is obtained by blowing up
\mathbb{A}^{2}/G\cong\mathbb{A}^{2}
along
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor

[
17
, Lemma 2.1]
,

is the maximal resolution of
(\mathbb{C}^{2}/G,\frac{1}{2}D)
(see also
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
).
We also note that any proper birational model
X\to\mathbb{A}^{2}/G

dominated by
Y
has only one
(-1)
-curve. In particular, the MMP of
Y
to
\mathbb{C}^{2}/G
is unique in our case.

2.6.
Bridgeland stability on smooth surfaces

In this subsection, we adapt the standard construction of Bridgeland stability conditions on smooth projective surfaces to the compactly supported setting. Although this formulation for non-compact surfaces does not seem to be readily available in the literature, the arguments are essentially identical to the projective case.

Throughout this subsection, we assume that
f:X\to S
is a smooth surface that is projective birational over an affine surface
S
. We define
\mathrm{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
to be the space of numerical divisor classes, namely, the quotient of
\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}

E

for any proper curve
C
on
X
. We note that

\displaystyle\mathrm{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\cong(K^{\mathrm{num}}_{c}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}/[\mathcal{O}_{x}])^{\vee}\hookrightarrow K^{\mathrm{num}}(X)_{\mathbb{R}},

3.3.
Construction of central charge

We define

\mathrm{NS}_{\mathrm{orb}}(\mathcal{Y})_{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathrm{NS}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus K^{\mathrm{num}}(R)_{\mathbb{R}}

as the orbifold analogue of
\mathrm{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
.
First, we explain the motivation for the definition.
By the semiorthogonal decomposition (
2.1
), we have an isomorphism

(3.7)

\displaystyle\psi:K^{\mathrm{num}}_{c}(\mathcal{Y})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}K^{\mathrm{num}}_{c}(Y)\oplus K_{c}^{\mathrm{num}}(R),

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\mapsto(\pi_{*}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}E,(\mathbf{L}i^{*}(\operatorname{sgn}\otimes E))^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}).

Indeed, the semiorthogonal decomposition induces a decomposition of Grothendieck groups, which descends to a surjection on numerical Grothendieck groups. Since both sides are free of the same rank (deduced from the finer semiorthogonal decomposition
[
17
, Example 3.5]
, for example), it is an isomorphism. Via the Euler pairing, this allows us to regard
\mathrm{NS}_{\mathrm{orb}}(\mathcal{Y})_{\mathbb{R}}
as the subspace of
K^{\mathrm{num}}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus K^{\mathrm{num}}(R)_{\mathbb{R}}\cong K^{\mathrm{num}}(\mathcal{Y})_{\mathbb{R}}
with
\chi(E,\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{y})=0
. This is analogous to (
2.3
).

Remark 3.7
.

A similar definition also appears in
[
12
, Definition 8.1]
and
\mathrm{NS}_{{\mathrm{orb}}}(\mathcal{Y})_{\mathbb{R}}
can be naturally viewed as a subset of the Chen-Ruan cohomology (orbifold cohomology)
H^{*}_{\mathrm{CR}}(\mathcal{Y},\mathbb{R})
. It is known that the Chen-Ruan cohomology group can be regarded as the direct sum of the ordinary cohomology groups of connected components of the inertia stack. In our case, we have

H^{*}_{\mathrm{CR}}(\mathcal{Y},\mathbb{R})\cong H^{*}(Y,\mathbb{R})\oplus H^{*}(R,\mathbb{R}).

where the second summand corresponds to the twisted sectors of
\mathcal{Y}
. In this paper, we only use the ordinary intersection theory and will not discuss any intersection products on
\mathrm{H}^{*}_{\mathrm{CR}}(\mathcal{Y},\mathbb{R})
.

Next, we define a homomorphism
Z_{\omega,B}^{t,s}\in K^{\mathrm{num}}_{c}(\mathcal{Y})^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}
for
((\omega,t),(B,s))\in\mathrm{NS}_{{\mathrm{orb}}}(\mathcal{Y})_{\mathbb{C}}:=\mathrm{NS}_{{\mathrm{orb}}}(\mathcal{Y})_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathbb{C}
as follows. Since

is generated by skyscraper sheaves

on each connected component
{R_{i}}
of

as follows:

\displaystyle Z^{t,s}:K_{c}^{\mathrm{num}}(R)

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\sum_{[R_{i}]\in\pi_{0}(R)}a_{i}[\mathcal{O}_{p_{i}}]

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{i}a_{i}(-\frac{1}{2}+s_{i}+\sqrt{-1}t_{i}).

Using the above homomorphisms, for

\displaystyle Z_{\omega,B}^{t,s}:=(Z_{\omega,B}+Z^{t,s})\circ\psi\in K^{\mathrm{num}}_{c}(\mathcal{Y})^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}}.

For example, for

4.3.
Bridgeland moduli and King moduli

Finally, we prove Ishii’s conjecture in our setting.
Let

f:Y\to X
be a projective birational morphism over
\mathbb{C}^{2}/G
.
First,
we prove the existence of the Bridgeland moduli space for our stability conditions
\sigma_{\omega,B}^{t,s}
via King moduli spaces, bypassing
Corollary
4.14
.

Corollary 4.16
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{U}(X)=\mu_{\mathrm{geom}}(A^{\dagger}(X)\times\mathcal{B})
. Then the moduli functor
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}([\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{y}])
is represented by a quasi-projective variety
M_{\sigma}([\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{y}])
.

Proof.

Using Corollary
4.14
, by deforming
\sigma
inside a chamber in
\mathrm{Stab}(\mathcal{Y})
, we may assume that
\sigma=\mu_{\mathrm{alg}}((\gamma\times\delta)^{-1}(\theta,\lambda))
for some
(\theta,\lambda)\in\Theta(G)\times\Lambda_{1}
. Since the King moduli functor is representable, it remains to show that, for any separated scheme of finite type
S
,

\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(v)(S)\to\mathcal{M}_{\theta}(v)(S),\,\,\,E_{S}\mapsto\Phi_{S}(E_{S}),

is a bijection, where
\Phi_{S}:=\Phi\times\operatorname{id}_{S}:D(\mathcal{Y}\times S)\to D([\mathbb{C}^{2}/G]\times S)
is the relative Fourier-Mukai functor. This follows from the same argument as in
[
2
, Proposition 7.3.1]
.
∎

The following proposition completes the proof of Theorem
A
:

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{U}(X)
. Then,
M_{\sigma}([\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{y}])
is isomorphic to
X
.

Proof.

In Proposition
3.23
, we already proved that the map

X\to M_{\sigma}([\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{y}])

sending
x
to
\mathbf{L}\widetilde{f}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{x}
is a bijection at the level of closed points. This map can be upgraded to a morphism of varieties since
(\mathbf{L}\widetilde{f}\times\operatorname{id}_{X})^{*}\Delta_{X}\in D(\mathcal{Y}\times X)
is a flat family of
\sigma
-stable objects with the class
[\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{y}]
and
M_{\sigma}([\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{y}])
is the fine moduli space (Corollary
4.16
).
Since

\mathbf{L}\widetilde{f}^{*}:D_{c}(X)\to D_{c}(\mathcal{Y})

is fully faithful, the map induced on the tangent space is an isomorphism. Thus, the map is an isomorphism.
∎

Remark 4.18
.

Toda uses the finiteness of the generator to prove the existence of moduli (especially the openness in Inaba’s moduli of simple complexes). This is not obvious in our case. Instead, we prove the existence problem by using the moduli of representations, which is easier to construct.

(

For any projective birational morphism

Proof.

Let

be such a contraction. By Corollary
4.14
, there is a GIT chamber

Proof.
