Nonexistence of Whirling-Knight Tours at Half Coil Count for n ≡ 4 , 6 ( mod 8 ) n\equiv 4,6\pmod{8}
Shisheng Li

Abstract
A whirling knight’s tour is a Hamiltonian cycle in the digraph of counter-clockwise knight steps about the centre of an n × n n\times n board; its coil count c c is the winding number around the centre. We prove that no such tour with c = n / 2 c=n/2 exists when n ≡ 4 ( mod 8 ) n\equiv 4\pmod{8} ( n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 ) or n ≡ 6 ( mod 8 ) n\equiv 6\pmod{8} ( n ≥ 6 n\geq 6 ), settling a conjecture of Beluhov. For each residue class we exhibit a closed-form Farkas certificate for infeasibility of a cycle-cover LP relaxation; the two certificates are structurally distinct. Keywords: knight’s tour, whirling knight, Hamiltonian cycle, Farkas certificate, linear programming relaxation, parity counting. MSC 2020: 05C45 (Eulerian and Hamiltonian graphs); 05C38 (paths and cycles); 05C20 (directed graphs); 90C05 (linear programming).

Introduction

The
whirling knight’s digraph
on an
m\times n
board has the

mn
cells as vertices and an arc
(i,j)\to(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
whenever

(i-i^{\prime})^{2}+(j-j^{\prime})^{2}=5
and the step proceeds counter-clockwise (CCW)
about the pivot
p=\bigl(\tfrac{m-1}{2},\,\tfrac{n-1}{2}\bigr)
. A

whirling tour
is a Hamiltonian cycle in this digraph; its

coil count

c
is the winding number of the cycle about
p
.
Equivalently,
c
is the number of arcs of the cycle that cross the
open vertical ray above
p
(the
north plumb-line
): the
winding number around
p
counts signed crossings of any ray from

p
, and since every arc is CCW each crossing contributes
+1
,
giving the equivalence. This object was introduced
by Bennett
[
1
]
and studied
in
[
4
, Exercises 227–231]
.

Any
m\times n
whirling tour with
m\leq n
satisfies
m/2\leq c\leq m
.
The lower endpoint
c=n/2
on square boards is the focus of this
paper. An integer-programming search by Beluhov produced
c=n/2

tours at
n=16
and
n=18
but none for
n\equiv 4,6\pmod{8}
,
leading him to conjecture nonexistence in those residue classes; the
complementary classes
n\equiv 0,2\pmod{8}
admit
c=n/2
tours, so
any uniform argument must single out residues
4
and
6
. For
related work on leaper Hamilton tours
see
[
3
,
2
]
.

Main results.

Theorem 1
.

For every
n\equiv 6\pmod{8}
with
n\geq 6
, no
n\times n
whirling
knight’s tour with
c=n/2
coils exists.

Theorem 2
.

For every
n\equiv 4\pmod{8}
with
n\geq 4
, no
n\times n
whirling
knight’s tour with
c=n/2
coils exists.

Together, Theorems
1
and
2
settle Beluhov’s
conjecture.

Method.

We use the cycle-cover LP relaxation: doubly-stochastic edge
assignments on the CCW knight digraph subject to a single coil-count
equation
\sum_{e}w_{e}x_{e}=c
, where
w_{e}\in\{0,1\}
marks crossings of
the north plumb-line. Infeasibility of this LP rules out a whirling
tour at the given
c
.

For each residue class we exhibit a closed-form Farkas dual

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\mathbb{R}^{V}\times\mathbb{R}^{V}\times\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\alpha_{v}+\beta_{u}+\gamma w_{e}\leq 0
for every arc
e=(u,v)
and

\sum_{v}\alpha_{v}+\sum_{v}\beta_{v}+c\gamma>0
. Both certificates have
small integer support, but are structurally distinct:

•

For
n\equiv 6\pmod{8}
(Theorem
1
), the certificate
puts
+1
-weights on two short blocks of cells flanking the pivot
column. The combinatorial reading reduces the proof to three
elementary geometric facts about the CCW knight digraph,
contradicting the coil count by a single excess crossing.

•

For
n\equiv 4\pmod{8}
(Theorem
2
), the certificate
combines an NE-triangle of cells carrying
\pm 1
weights according to

(i+j)
-parity, plus an analogous block-row correction. The crucial
arithmetic is a parity-counting identity stating that, on the chosen
triangle, the number of cells with odd coordinate sum exceeds the
even count by exactly
2m+1
, where
n=8m+4
.

and

The cycle-cover LP relaxation

Each whirling tour
T
on the
n\times n
board with
c
coils
corresponds to an indicator vector
x_{e}=\mathbf{1}_{e\in T}\in\{0,1\}^{E}

satisfying the cycle-cover constraints

\sum_{e\,\text{into}\,v}x_{e}=1,\quad\sum_{e\,\text{out of}\,v}x_{e}=1,\quad\forall v\in V,

together with the coil-count equation
\sum_{e}w_{e}\,x_{e}=c
.
Replacing
\{0,1\}
by
[0,1]
yields the linear-programming
relaxation
(\mathrm{LP}_{n,c})
:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{find }x\in\mathbb{R}^{E}\\
\text{s.t.}&\displaystyle\sum_{e\,\text{into}\,v}x_{e}=1,\;\displaystyle\sum_{e\,\text{out of}\,v}x_{e}=1,\quad\forall v\in V;\\[6.0pt]
&\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}w_{e}\,x_{e}=c,\\[6.0pt]
&0\leq x_{e}\leq 1,\quad\forall e\in E.\end{array}

The following lemma is immediate.

Lemma 1

(Reduction Lemma)
.

If a whirling tour with
c
coils exists at
(n,c)
, then

(\mathrm{LP}_{n,c})
is feasible. Equivalently,

(\mathrm{LP}_{n,c})
infeasible implies that no whirling tour
with
c
coils on the
n\times n
board exists.

Proof.

Let
T
be such a tour and set
x_{e}=\mathbf{1}_{e\in T}\in\{0,1\}\subset[0,1]
;
the box constraint holds trivially. In a Hamiltonian cycle every
vertex has exactly one incoming and one outgoing arc, so the in/out
degree constraints hold. The coil-count constraint is the definition
of “
c
coils.”
∎

Farkas certificates

A
Farkas certificate
(cf.
[
5
, §7]
) for

(\mathrm{LP}_{n,c})
is a triple

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\mathbb{R}^{V}\times\mathbb{R}^{V}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
\alpha

multiplies the in-degree row of each vertex,
\beta
the out-degree
row, and
\gamma
the single coil-count row. Define the
per-arc and scalar quantities

\mathrm{LHS}_{e}:=\alpha_{v}+\beta_{u}+\gamma\,w_{e}\quad\text{(for $e=(u,v)$)},\qquad\mathrm{RHS}:=\sum_{v}\alpha_{v}+\sum_{v}\beta_{v}+c\,\gamma.

The certificate is required to satisfy

\displaystyle\mathrm{LHS}_{e}

\displaystyle\leq 0,\qquad\forall e\in E,

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHS}

\displaystyle>0.

(2)

Multiplying the LP equalities by their multipliers and summing
arc-by-arc yields, for any LP-feasible
x
,

\sum_{e}\mathrm{LHS}_{e}\,x_{e}=\mathrm{RHS}.

For
x\geq 0
and a certificate satisfying (
1
)
and (
2
), the left side is
\leq 0
while the right side
is
>0
, a contradiction. Hence the existence of such a certificate
implies infeasibility of
(\mathrm{LP}_{n,c})
.

To illustrate, for
n=3
,
c=2
the digraph has eight cells (the
centre
(1,1)
is excluded for odd

) and its unique cycle cover

T_{3}
has coil count

, so
(\mathrm{LP}_{3,2})
is infeasible by
direct inspection. A
two-cell certificate
\alpha_{v}=\mathbf{1}_{v\in H}
,

\beta_{v}=0
,
\gamma=-1
with
H=\{(0,0),(1,0),(2,0)\}
confirms
this; see Figures
4
and
4
.

A geometric lemma

Both proofs invoke the following geometric fact about CCW knight
arcs touching the pivot column from the north.

Lemma 2

(Geometric Lemma)
.

Let
n
be even,
p=\bigl(\tfrac{n-1}{2},\tfrac{n-1}{2}\bigr)
the
pivot,
h=n/2
, and let
w=(i,j)
be a cell with
i\leq h-2
.

(a)

If
j=h-1
(column directly west of the pivot), then every
CCW knight arc
u\to w
satisfies
j_{u}\in\{h,h+1\}
(in particular

j_{u}\geq h
, strictly east of the pivot column) and crosses the north
plumb-line.

(b)

If
j=h
(column directly east of the pivot), then every
CCW knight arc
w\to v
satisfies
j_{v}\in\{h-1,h-2\}
and crosses the
north plumb-line.

Proof.

The CCW test is
(u-p)\times(v-p)>0
, where
\times
denotes the

2\times 2
determinant
(a,b)\times(c,d)=ad-bc
. Substituting

u=v-\Delta
for a knight step

\Delta\in\{(\pm 1,\pm 2),(\pm 2,\pm 1)\}
and expanding,

\Delta_{j}\,(i_{v}-p_{i})-\Delta_{i}\,(j_{v}-p_{j})>0.

(3)

For (a) take
w=v=(i,h-1)
, so
j_{v}-p_{j}=-\tfrac{1}{2}
and

i_{v}-p_{i}=i-(h-\tfrac{1}{2})\leq-\tfrac{3}{2}
(since
i\leq h-2
).
Plugging each of the eight knight steps into (
3
)
shows that exactly the four steps

\Delta\in\{(1,-2),(-1,-2),(2,-1),(-2,-1)\}
are CCW (the bound

i_{v}-p_{i}\leq-\tfrac{3}{2}
enters monotonically, so the same four

\Delta
’s remain CCW for every
i\leq h-2
); all four have

\Delta_{j}\in\{-1,-2\}
, so
j_{u}=j_{v}-\Delta_{j}\in\{h,h+1\}\geq h
.
Since
j_{u}\geq h>h-\tfrac{1}{2}=p_{j}>h-1=j_{v}
, the arc segment crosses the
vertical line
x=p_{j}
; the crossing height is the unique

i
-coordinate at
j=p_{j}
along the segment, which by linear
interpolation equals
i_{v}+\frac{\Delta_{i}}{\Delta_{j}}\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}
. For
the four CCW
\Delta
’s this gives heights
i\pm\tfrac{1}{4}
(from

\Delta_{j}=-2
) and
i\pm 1
(from
\Delta_{j}=-1
); since

i\leq h-2
, each is at most
h-1<h-\tfrac{1}{2}=p_{i}
, so the crossing
lies above the pivot and the arc crosses
N
. Part (b) follows by the symmetric calculation
with
j_{v}-p_{j}=+\tfrac{1}{2}
and
i_{w}-p_{i}\leq-\tfrac{3}{2}
, identifying the
same four CCW steps and giving

j_{v}=j_{w}+\Delta_{j}\in\{h-1,h-2\}
.
∎

Remark
.

The hypothesis
i\leq h-2
is sharp for both (a) and (b): for

w=(h-1,h-1)
(just NW of the pivot), the arc
(h-3,h-2)\to w

is CCW (test:

(-\tfrac{5}{2})(-\tfrac{1}{2})-(-\tfrac{3}{2})(-\tfrac{1}{2})=\tfrac{1}{2}>0
) yet
has
j_{u}=h-2<h
, violating both the column conclusion of (a) and
its symmetric counterpart in (b). Lemma
2
therefore
applies to block interiors but not to cells adjacent to the pivot
row.

Direct combinatorial restatement.

The cases (i)–(iii) above amount to three structural facts about
the CCW knight digraph:

(A)

Every CCW arc into
N^{\mathrm{in}}
crosses the north plumb-line.

(B)

Every CCW arc out of
N^{\mathrm{out}}
crosses the north plumb-line.

(C)

No CCW arc joins
N^{\mathrm{out}}
to
N^{\mathrm{in}}
.

Suppose for contradiction that a whirling tour
T
on the
n\times n

board has
c=n/2
coils. Each cell has exactly one in-arc and one
out-arc in
T
, so
T
contains
|N^{\mathrm{in}}|
arcs ending in
N^{\mathrm{in}}
and

|N^{\mathrm{out}}|
arcs starting in
N^{\mathrm{out}}
. By (A)–(B) all these arcs
cross
N
, and by (C) the two collections are disjoint. Hence
T

has at least
|N^{\mathrm{in}}|+|N^{\mathrm{out}}|=4(m+1)=4m+4
N-crossings. But by
definition
T
has exactly
c=4m+3
N-crossings, contradiction.

\square

.

Sub-case

(i.e.
\alpha=0
).

. Of its four CCW knight arcs, the one

Structural remark.

Both certificates achieve
\mathrm{RHS}=1
, the smallest positive integer:

c=n/2
lies at the LP-feasibility boundary in both classes. The
two certificates are structurally distinct: T1’s
\{0,1\}
-cert
admits the brief combinatorial restatement (A)–(C), whereas T2
requires sign-mixing on an NE-triangle, replacing the König-style
counting of T1 with an alternating-sum identity.
