Ground State Solutions for local-nonlocal Shrödinger equations in the presence of two critical exponents Thanks: Yu Su is supported by the Natural Science Research Project of Anhui Educational Committee (Grant No. 2023AH040155).


Abstract
. In this paper, we address the existence of ground state solutions for Schrödinger equations in the presence of local and nonlocal operators and two critical nonlinearities associated with each operator. The situation is completely solved in the critical case both in the local and in the nonlocal settings. However, methods developed in these cases cannot extend to local-nonlocal operators when we have two critical power nonlinearities. This unprecedented situation in PDEs presents some challenges, and its resolution will open the door to solve similar problems. Our approach is essentially based on a subtle generalization of the Lieb translation theorem. We will also discuss the positivity of the ground states as well as their regularity.

2.
Sobolev Space

Let us define the homogeneous Sobolev space as follows

\displaystyle D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\{u\in L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})||\nabla u|\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\},

its semi-norm is taken as

\displaystyle\|u\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

For
N\geqslant 3
and
s\in(0,1)
, let
D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be the homogeneous fractional Sobolev space, which is the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with the semi-norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

The mixed Sobolev space
X
defined by the completion of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
under the semi-norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

Lemma 2.1
.

X\hookrightarrow D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

X\hookrightarrow D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\leqslant\|u\|_{X}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\leqslant\|u\|_{X}^{2}.

These show
X\hookrightarrow D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

X\hookrightarrow D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

X
is continuously embedding
L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
t\in[2_{s}^{*},2^{*}]
, and
2_{s}^{*}:=\frac{2N}{N-2s}
and
2^{*}:=\frac{2N}{N-2}
.

Proof.

Using Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{t}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{(tN-2N-2t)(N-2s)}{4N(s-1)}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{(tN-2N-2ts)(N-2)}{4N(1-s)}}.

From Lemma
2.1
, we know

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2_{s}^{*}}}\leqslant\|u\|_{D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\leqslant\|u\|_{X}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\leqslant\|u\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\leqslant\|u\|_{X}^{2}.

Then we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{t}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{(tN-2N-2t)(N-2s)}{4N(s-1)}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{(tN-2N-2ts)(N-2)}{4N(1-s)}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X}^{t}<\infty.

The proof is completed.
∎

[

Lieb’s translation theorem

In this section,
we recall the Lieb translation theorem and we prove an extention of it.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

Let

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(S_{s}^{-1}\int_{B(z,1)}\int_{B(z,1)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2_{s}^{*}}{2}\frac{2^{*}-q}{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}}}\left(S_{1}^{-1}\int_{B(z,1)}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2^{*}}{2}\frac{q-2_{s}^{*}}{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle S_{s}^{-\frac{2_{s}^{*}}{2}\frac{2^{*}-q}{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}}}S_{1}^{-\frac{2^{*}}{2}\frac{q-2_{s}^{*}}{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}}}\left(\int_{B(z,1)}\int_{B(z,1)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B(z,1)}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}.

Applying (
3.1

we know that

\displaystyle\int_{B(z,1)}|u|^{q}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B(z,1)}|u|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}\left(\int_{B(z,1)}|u|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{q-2}{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{B(z,1)}\int_{B(z,1)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B(z,1)}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\int_{B(z,1)}|u|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{q-2}{q}}.

Covering

by balls of radius

is contained in at most

We find that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant C(N+1)\left(\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{B(z,1)}|u|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\|u\|_{X}^{2}.

∎

Theorem 3.1
.

(Lieb’s translation theorem)

,

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\penalty\ \mathrm{in}\penalty\ X,\penalty\ u_{n}\rightarrow u\penalty\ \mathrm{a.e.\penalty\ in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N},u_{n}\rightarrow u\penalty\ \mathrm{in}\penalty\ L^{q}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}^{-2}\left(\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N}\sigma_{n}^{N}\right)^{\frac{2_{s}^{*}-2}{2_{s}^{*}}}\left(\int_{B(x_{n},\sigma_{n})}|u_{n}(y)|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{2}{2_{s}^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\sigma_{n}^{-2+N(1-\frac{2}{2_{s}^{*}})}\to 0,\ \text{as}\ n\rightarrow\infty.

This yields to a contradiction.
By the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem, up to a subsequence, still denoted by
\{\sigma_{n}\}
,
there exists
\bar{\sigma}\in[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{n}=\bar{\sigma}.

Second, we show that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{n}=\bar{\sigma}\not=0
.
Suppose on the contrary that

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{n}=\bar{\sigma}=0
.
Using the boundedness of
\{u_{n}\}
,
we have that

\displaystyle C\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{2}{2_{s}^{*}}}\leqslant\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{n}\|_{X}^{2}\leqslant\bar{C}.

It follows from Hölder’s and Sobolev’s inequalities that

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,\sigma_{n})}|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}y\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B(0,\sigma_{n})}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}}{2^{*}}}\left(\int_{B(0,\sigma_{n})}|u_{n}|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{2_{s}^{*}}{2^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle S_{1}^{-\frac{2_{s}^{*}}{2}}\left(\int_{B(0,\sigma_{n})}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}}{2^{*}}}\left(\int_{B(0,\sigma_{n})}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}{\rm d}y\right)^{\frac{2_{s}^{*}}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle S_{1}^{-\frac{2_{s}^{*}}{2}}\left(\int_{B(0,\sigma_{n})}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}}{2^{*}}}\|u_{n}\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2_{s}^{*}-2}\int_{B(0,\sigma_{n})}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle CS_{1}^{-\frac{2_{s}^{*}}{2}}\left(\int_{B(0,\sigma_{n})}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}}{2^{*}}}\int_{B(0,\sigma_{n})}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}{\rm d}y.

Similarly, for each
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{B(z,\sigma_{n})}|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}y\leqslant CS_{1}^{-\frac{2_{s}^{*}}{2}}\left(\int_{B(0,\sigma_{n})}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}}{2^{*}}}\int_{B(z,\sigma_{n})}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}{\rm d}y.

Covering

\mathbb{R}^{N}

with balls of radius

\sigma_{n}
,
in such a way that each point of

\mathbb{R}^{N}

is contained in at most

N+1

balls, one gets

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}y\leqslant

\displaystyle C(N+1)S_{1}^{-\frac{2_{s}^{*}}{2}}\left(\int_{B(0,\sigma_{n})}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}}{2^{*}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C(N+1)S_{1}^{-\frac{2_{s}^{*}}{2}}\left(\int_{B(0,\sigma_{n})}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}}{2^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C(N+1)S_{1}^{-\frac{2_{s}^{*}}{2}}\sigma_{n}^{N(1-\frac{2_{s}^{*}}{2^{*}})}.

By our assumption
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{n}=0
, it follows that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}y\leqslant

\displaystyle C(N+1)S_{1}^{-\frac{2_{s}^{*}}{2}}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{n}^{N(1-\frac{2_{s}^{*}}{2^{*}})}=0,

which contradicts the facts that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x>0
.

Step 3.

Using
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{n}=\bar{\sigma}\not=0
,
up to a subsequence,
we have

\{\sigma_{n}\}\subset(\frac{\bar{\sigma}}{2},2\bar{\sigma})

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2}}{\bar{\sigma}^{2}}\int_{B(x_{n},2\bar{\sigma})}|u_{n}(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y\geqslant C_{1}>0,

which gives us:

\displaystyle\int_{B(x_{n},2\bar{\sigma})}|u_{n}(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y\geqslant\frac{C_{1}\bar{\sigma}^{2}}{2^{2s}}>0.

Set
\bar{u}_{n}:=u_{n}(x+x_{n})
. Then

is a bounded sequence satisfying:

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\bar{u}_{n}|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x>0\penalty\ {\rm and}\penalty\ \lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\bar{u}_{n}|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x>0.

and from (

Using the combinations of the embedding

4.
Mountain pass geometry and Nehari manifold

The energy functional corresponding to the equation (

D

) is

\displaystyle I(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{X}^{2}-\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\beta}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x.

For any
\phi\in X
, the critical points of
I
satisfy

\displaystyle 0=\langle I^{\prime}(u),\phi\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\nabla\phi\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(\phi(x)-\phi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}-1}\phi\mathrm{d}x-\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p-1}\phi\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}-1}\phi\mathrm{d}x.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
N\geqslant 3
and
0<s<1
.
Then the functional
I
has mountain pass geometric structure.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.2
, one has

\displaystyle I(u)\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{X}^{2}-C\|u\|_{X}^{p}-C\|u\|_{X}^{2_{s}^{*}}-C\|u\|_{X}^{2^{*}}.

Recall that
2<2_{s}^{*}<p<2^{*}
. There exists a sufficiently small positive number
\rho
such that

\displaystyle\varsigma:=\inf_{\|u\|_{X}=\rho}I(u)>0=I(0).

For
u\in X\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\displaystyle I(tu)=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|u\|_{X}^{2}-\beta\frac{t^{p}}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{t^{2_{s}^{*}}}{2_{s}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{t^{2^{*}}}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x.

From
2<2_{s}^{*}<p<2^{*}
, it follows that
I(tu)<0
for
t
large enough.

From the above,
we can choose
t_{0}>0
corresponding to
u
such that
I(t_{0}u)<0
for
t>t_{0}
and
\|t_{0}u\|_{X}>\rho
.
∎

We set the mountain pass level

\displaystyle c=\inf\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,1]}I(\gamma(t))>0,

and

\displaystyle\Gamma=\{\gamma\in C\left([0,1],X\right)|\gamma(0)=0,I(\gamma(1))<0\}.

We recall the
(PS)_{c}
sequence as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

If sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset X
satisfies the condition

\displaystyle I(u_{n})\rightarrow c\penalty\ \mathrm{and}\penalty\ I^{\prime}(u_{n})\rightarrow 0\penalty\ \mathrm{in}\penalty\ X^{-1},\penalty\ {\rm as}\penalty\ n\to\infty.

Then
\{u_{n}\}
is called the Palais-Smale sequence of
I
with respect to
c
, short for
(PS)_{c}
sequence,
where
X^{-1}
is the dual space of
X
.

It follows from the mountain pass Theorem
[
2
]
and Lemma
4.1
that there exists a
(PS)_{c}
sequence.

We now set the Nehari manifold as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=\{u\in X\setminus\{0\}|\langle I^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=0\}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
N\geqslant 3
and
0<s<1
.
Then for any
u\in X\setminus\{0\}
, there exists a unique
t_{u}>0
such that
t_{u}u\in\mathcal{N}
and
I(t_{u}u)=\max\limits_{t>0}I(tu)
.

Proof.

For any
u\in X\setminus\{0\}
and
t\in(0,\infty)
, we define

\displaystyle f_{1}(t)=I(tu)=

\displaystyle\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|u\|_{X}^{2}-\beta\frac{t^{p}}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{t^{2_{s}^{*}}}{2_{s}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{t^{2^{*}}}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x.

We compute

\displaystyle f_{1}^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle t\|u\|_{X}^{2}-\beta t^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x-t^{2_{s}^{*}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x-t^{2^{*}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x.

We know that
f_{1}^{\prime}(\cdot)=0
iff

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X}^{2}=\beta t^{p-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+t^{2_{s}^{*}-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x+t^{2^{*}-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x.

Let

\displaystyle f_{2}(t)=\beta t^{p-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+t^{2_{s}^{*}-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x+t^{2^{*}-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x.

Clearly,
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}f_{2}(t)\rightarrow 0
,
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}f_{2}(t)\rightarrow+\infty
.
By using the intermediate value theorem, we know that there exists a
0<t_{u}<\infty
such that

\displaystyle f_{2}(t_{u})=\|u\|_{X}^{2}.

Furthermore, it is easy to see that
f_{2}(\cdot)
is strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
.
Then we get the uniqueness of
t_{u}
.
And then,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X}^{2}=\beta t_{u}^{p-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+t_{u}^{2_{s}^{*}-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x+t_{u}^{2^{*}-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x,

which implies that

\displaystyle\|t_{u}u\|_{X}^{2}=\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|t_{u}u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|t_{u}u|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|t_{u}u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x.

This implies that
t_{2}u\in\mathcal{N}
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
N\geqslant 3
and
0<s<1
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\bar{c}=\inf\limits_{u\in\mathcal{N}}I(u)>0.

Proof.

By applying
\langle I^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=0
,
we know that

\displaystyle 0=\langle I^{\prime}(u),u\rangle\geqslant\|u\|_{X}^{2}-C\|u\|_{X}^{p}-C\|u\|_{X}^{2_{s}^{*}}-C\|u\|_{X}^{2^{*}},

which implies that

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{X}^{p-2}+C\|u\|_{X}^{2_{s}^{*}-2}+C\|u\|_{X}^{2^{*}-2}\geqslant 1,

and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X}^{2}\geqslant C.

Then,
for
u\in\mathcal{N}
,
we get that

\displaystyle I(u)=

\displaystyle I(u)-\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}}\langle I^{\prime}(u),u\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{X}^{2}-\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\beta}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}}\left(\|u\|_{X}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x-\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}}\right)\|u\|_{X}^{2}+\beta\left(\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\left(\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}}\right)\|u\|_{X}^{2}\geqslant C.

Hence,

is bounded from below on
\mathcal{N}
. And then
\bar{c}>0
.
∎

Set

\displaystyle\bar{\bar{c}}:=\inf\limits_{u\in X\setminus\{0\}}\sup\limits_{t\geqslant 0}I(tu).

From
[
46
]
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
N\geqslant 3
and
0<s<1
.
Then we have

.

Proof.

By virtue of Lemma
4.2
, we have the following result directly

\displaystyle\bar{c}=\bar{\bar{c}}.

For any
u\in X\setminus\{0\}
,
there exists some
\tilde{t}>0

Define a path

On the other hand,

.
Then,

A direct calculation gives us

Thus, there exists

and

.
This shows that

.

Lemma 4.5

\displaystyle(a-b)(\bar{u}_{L}(a)-\bar{u}_{L}(b))\geqslant 0\penalty\ \mbox{for any}\penalty\ a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Using Jensen’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}(a-b)(\bar{u}_{L}(a)-\bar{u}_{L}(b))\geqslant|Y(a)-Y(b)|^{2}\penalty\ \mbox{for any}\penalty\ a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

from which it follows that

\displaystyle|Y(u)(x)-Y(u)(y)|^{2}\leqslant(u(x)-u(y))((uu_{L}^{2(\mu-1)})(x)-(uu_{L}^{2(\mu-1)})(y)).

Note that

Y(u)\geqslant\frac{1}{\mu}uu_{L}^{\mu-1}
.
By Lemma
2.3
, one has

(6.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\nabla\bar{u}_{L}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(\bar{u}_{L}(x)-\bar{u}_{L}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\|Y(u)\|^{2}_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|Y(u)\|^{2}_{D^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle C\|Y(u)\|^{2}_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\mu^{2}}\|uu_{L}^{\mu-1}\|^{2}_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

By virtue of (
6.5
), (
6.6
) and
(F_{1})
, we have

\displaystyle\|uu_{L}^{\mu-1}\|^{2}_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mu^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\nabla\bar{u}_{L}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(\bar{u}_{L}(x)-\bar{u}_{L}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\mu^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u)\bar{u}_{L}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mu^{2}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}}|u_{L}|^{2(\mu-1)}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}|u_{L}|^{2(\mu-1)}\mathrm{d}x\right].

The proof is completed.
∎

We are now ready to show the
L^{\infty}
estimation of solution
u
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Step 1.

From Lemma
6.2
, we have

\displaystyle\|uu_{L}^{\mu-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leqslant C\mu^{2}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}}|u_{L}|^{2(\mu-1)}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}|u_{L}|^{2(\mu-1)}\mathrm{d}x\right].

Let
\bar{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
be chosen later.
By using Hölder’s inequality,
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}-2}|uu_{L}^{\mu-1}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{\{u(x)\leqslant\bar{d}\}}|u|^{2^{*}-2}|uu_{L}^{\mu-1}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\{u(x)>\bar{d}\}}|u|^{2^{*}-2}|uu_{L}^{\mu-1}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\bar{d}^{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}}\int_{\{u(x)\leqslant\bar{d}\}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}-2}|uu_{L}^{\mu-1}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\{u(x)>\bar{d}\}}|u|^{2^{*}-2}|uu_{L}^{\mu-1}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\bar{d}^{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}}\int_{\{u(x)\leqslant\bar{d}\}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}-2}|uu_{L}^{\mu-1}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\left(\int_{\{u(x)>\bar{d}\}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2^{*}-2}{2^{*}}}\left(\int_{\{u(x)>\bar{d}\}}|uu_{L}^{\mu-1}|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}.

We can fix
\bar{d}
such that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\{u(x)>\bar{d}\}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2^{*}-2}{2^{*}}}\leqslant\frac{1}{2C\mu^{2}}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\|uu_{L}^{\mu-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leqslant C\mu^{2}(1+\bar{d}^{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}}|u_{L}|^{2(\mu-1)}\mathrm{d}x.

Taking the limit as

L\rightarrow\infty

in the above inequality,
we deduce

(6.7)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}\mu}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\leqslant C\mu^{2}(1+\bar{d}^{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}+2\mu-2}\mathrm{d}x.

Step 2.

Let
\mu_{1}-1=\frac{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}}{2}
. Then from (
6.7
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}\mu_{1}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\leqslant C\mu_{1}^{2}(1+\bar{d}^{2^{*}-2_{s}^{*}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x<+\infty,

which gives

\displaystyle\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}\mu_{1}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}(\mu_{1}-1)}}<+\infty.

Step 3.
We choose
\mu_{2}-1=\frac{2^{*}}{2}(\mu_{1}-1)
. Then

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}\mu_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\leqslant C\mu_{2}^{2}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}+2\mu_{2}-2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}+2\mu_{2}-2}\mathrm{d}x\right].

Let

\displaystyle a=\frac{2^{*}(2^{*}-2_{s}^{*})}{2(\mu_{2}-1)},\penalty\ \penalty\ b=2_{s}^{*}+2\mu_{2}-2-a.

Then
\frac{2^{*}b}{2^{*}-a}=2^{*}+2\mu_{2}-2
. By applying Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2_{s}^{*}+2\mu_{2}-2}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{a}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{2^{*}-a}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}+2\mu_{2}-2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}+2\mu_{2}-2}\mathrm{d}x\right).

Thus

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}\mu_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\leqslant C\mu_{2}^{2}\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}+2\mu_{2}-2}\mathrm{d}x\right).

Using
\mu_{2}>1
, we deduce

\displaystyle\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}\mu_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\leqslant

\displaystyle 1+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}\mu_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mu_{2}^{2}\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}+2\mu_{2}-2}\mathrm{d}x\right).

Then

\displaystyle\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}\mu_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}(\mu_{2}-1)}}\leqslant

\displaystyle(C\mu_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{\mu_{2}-1}}\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}+2\mu_{2}-2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{\mu_{2}-1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(C\mu_{2})^{\frac{2}{\mu_{2}-1}}\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}\mu_{1}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}(\mu_{1}-1)}}

Step 4.

Iterating this process and recalling that

\displaystyle\mu_{i+1}-1=\frac{2^{*}}{2}(\mu_{i}-1),

and

\displaystyle\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}\mu_{i+1}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}(\mu_{i+1}-1)}}\leqslant

\displaystyle(C\mu_{i+1})^{\frac{2}{\mu_{i+1}-1}}\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}\mu_{i}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}(\mu_{i}-1)}}.

Denoting
C_{i+1}:=C\mu_{i+1}
and
K_{i}=\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}\mu_{i}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}(\mu_{i}-1)}}
, we know that there exists a constant
C>0
independent of

\displaystyle K_{m+1}\leqslant\prod_{i=1}^{m}C_{i+1}^{\frac{2}{\mu_{i+1}-1}}K_{1}\leqslant CK_{1}<+\infty.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leqslant CK_{1}<+\infty.
