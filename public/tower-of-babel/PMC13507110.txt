Multi-scenario simulation of land use change and its effects on ecosystem service value: a case study of the three provinces in the middle reaches of the Yangtze River, China
Authors: Nijad Emer, Lanzhou Chen, Haijun Wang, Youcheng Song, Quan Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Land use and land cover change (LUCC) exerts substantial influence on ecosystem service values (ESV). But conventional ESV valuation approaches frequently neglect temporal shifts in crop economic returns. Integrating high-resolution land use data (2000–2020) with the Patch-generating Land Use Simulation (PLUS) model—and augmenting it with spatial econometric analysis—we project 2030 land use configurations under four policy-relevant scenarios: Business-as-Usual (BAU), Economic Development Priority (EDP), Ecological Protection Priority (EPP), and Ecological Economic Balance (EEB). Under EDP, construction land expands by 5.52%, critically low-ESV areas increase by 476.83 km2 (a 2.7-fold surge relative to the 2010–2020 period), and net ESV declines by 1.04%. By contrast, EPP achieves a 10.19% reduction in urban land through targeted ecological restoration, effectively arresting ESV degradation. High-value ESV zones are concentrated in water bodies and forests, which dominate regional regulating services. The three-province in the Middle Reaches of the Yangtze River epitomizes the inherent tension between rapid urbanization and ESV conservation. These findings provide a basis for assessing the social, economic and environmental factors. Furthermore, the results provide a new solution approach for formulating differentiated ecological environment protection policies in the study area and addressing key technical challenges in land use planning for large-scale ecological functional area.

Introduction

The influence of escalating global urbanization and concomitant land transitions on ESV has become a central concern for regional sustainability1,2. Ecosystem services encompass the benefits and outcomes that humans derive from ecosystems through their organization, operation, and inherent mechanisms, encompassing both direct and indirect advantages. The sustenance of human productivity and enhanced quality of life are intrinsically linked to these crucial services3,4. The three provinces in the Middle Reaches of the Yangtze River, a pivotal economic and ecological composite region in China5, has experienced sustained impairment of its ecosystem integrity due to uncoordinated urbanization and intensive agricultural practices6. This threatens regional ecological security and long-term developmental resilience7,8. Costanza and colleagues underscored the importance of ESV assessment and pioneered the global ESV factor, a monetary quantification of ESV fluctuations. This innovation garnered widespread acceptance and has been extensively applied to guide ecological restoration and sustainable development policy formulation, solidifying its core role in the field.

The maintenance of ecosystem service functions is largely contingent on the complex dynamics of LUCC, thus establishing LUCC as a key determinant of these services. Serving as a critical nexus between human activities and the natural environment, LUCC assumes paramount importance in the pursuit of sustainable development objectives9. Intensified human activities can disrupt LUCC patterns10, potentially triggering rapid urbanization, environmental degradation, and adverse impacts on natural ecosystems. Numerous studies have employed LUCC data to evaluate dynamic changes in ESV. Achieving spatially accurate simulation and prediction of LUCC and ESV is essential for promoting regional sustainable development and enhancing the effectiveness of environmental planning and management. LUCC transformation is a constantly evolving process, shaped by urbanization and agricultural development, resulting in diverse temporal and spatial variations in land use patterns. To quantify the integrated influence of environmental and anthropogenic factors on LUCC spatial distribution, various LUCC models incorporate parameters such as landform, boundary characteristics, and unique attributes. LUCC change models have therefore emerged as valuable and reproducible tools, playing a crucial role in environmental impact assessment and the development of effective land use planning strategies. Researchers have deployed a range of methodologies to investigate LUCC. Recognizing that the dynamic coupling between ESV and LUCC is crucial for the co-evolution of human and natural systems11–13, this study leverages the Millennium Ecosystem Assessment framework, using ESV monetization to inform policy decisions. The interconnected provinces of Hubei, Hunan, and Jiangxi within the Yangtze Economic Belt exemplify this relationship in the context of China’s ecological civilization initiative. Faced with rapid urbanization, shifting demographics, and resource constraints, the LUCC transformation in this tri-provincial region and its impacts on ESV merit in-depth scrutiny. Landscape fragmentation weakens the regulation of ecosystem services, while ESV changes reflect topographic gradients. Furthermore, interventions aimed at enhancing ESV also feed back into land management practices. Prior research has emphasized ESV assessment and management; given the pronounced influence of LUCC on ESV, this study aims to determine the strength of this relationship. However, human activities can also disrupt LUCC patterns14, leading to trade-offs. Analyzing the LUCC-ESV relationship can inform land management, provide theoretical underpinnings, and demonstrate the effectiveness of LUCC models in assessing environmental impacts. Leveraging land use data from two time periods, the PLUS model15,16 introduces a novel framework for mining land use transition rules. As a CA model based on a multi-type patch generation strategy, it is better suited to simulate realistic land landscapes. In recent years, the PLUS model has been employed to simulate and predict urban land use under different scenarios17, estimate ESV18 and integrate ecosystem service functions19. Land use data can reflect the impact of human activities on ESV20. Because different land types confer different ecological benefits, numerous scholars use ecological capability21,22 or ESV23,24 to assess ecological risk25. Studies have found that the Figure of Merit (FOM) value of the PLUS model (0.26) surpasses that of the FLUS model (0.19). Moreover, the simultaneously used the CA–Markov model and the PLUS model to simulate land use changes in the Aksu Basin26, and the results indicated that the Kappa value of the PLUS model (90.8%) was higher than that of the CA–Markov model (88.9%)27. However, several challenges remain: (1) In the study area (Hubei, Hunan, and Jiangxi), comprehensive analyses of the advantages and disadvantages of various CA-based models remain limited, despite independent studies demonstrating the potential of the PLUS model; (2) Existing studies typically focus on the quantitative structure or spatial optimization of LUCC, overlooking key interactions between LUCC and ESV, especially their spatial synergistic changes; (3) Prior research on LUCC and ESV has been largely confined to single cities or provinces, thus limiting the scope of investigation.

While existing studies have addressed the influence of land use change on ESV28,29, most are limited to static analyses or single-scenario predictions, lacking a systematic assessment of future land use dynamics and their spatial heterogeneity under multiple policy drivers. Furthermore, traditional models are limited in their ability to simulate complex land use transitions, hindering the capture of interactive effects between policy interventions and natural constraints, thereby causing the deviations between predicted outcomes and actual planning needs.

This study focuses on the three provinces in the Middle Reaches of the Yangtze River, covering an area of 564,600 km2, and employs the PLUS model to simulate LUCC, estimate ESV, and subsequently validate and compare the simulation results. A “policy-space-value” linked dynamic evaluation framework is proposed, which combines high-resolution land use data and a spatially explicit PLUS model to simulate the evolution of land use patterns and their impact on ESV under four scenarios in 2030: BAU, EDP, EPP, and EEB. By integrating the random forest algorithm, cellular automata, and Markov chains, this study quantifies the spatial differentiation characteristics of construction land expansion, farmland degradation, and forest degradation under different policy orientations were quantified, and the interactive impact of terrain slope and policy intervention on ESV spatial heterogeneity was revealed. The innovations of this study lie in: (1) constructing a multi-model coupled with a dynamic evaluation framework that overcomes the limitations of traditional models in simulating complex land use transitions and enables a linked analysis of macro policies and micro spatial patterns; (2) Four alternative scenarios were designed to simulate future LULC dynamics and their impacts on ESV. This enables the identification of trade-offs between economic development and ecological development under different policy preferences; (3) By analyzing the ESV losses and gains under different development models, the research results provide spatial decision support for optimizing land use planning, improving ecosystem management, and promoting the ecological-economic coordinated development of the Middle Reaches of the Yangtze River urban agglomeration.

These findings not only have practical significance for optimizing land use planning but also provide a methodological reference for countries in the Global South facing similar challenges, particularly by offering an empirical basis for reconciling the objectives of “economic growth” and “ecological protection” within the framework of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

Materials and methods

Study area

Located in the heart of central China (110°–116° E, 27°–32° N), the study area experiences a subtropical monsoon climate characterized by annual precipitation of 1000–1400 mm and an average annual temperature of 16–18 °C, fostering favorable conditions for both agriculture and industry. Encompassing Hubei, Hunan, and Jiangxi provinces (Fig. 2), the study area features a polycentric spatial structure anchored by the Wuhan metropolitan area, the Changsha-Zhuzhou-Xiangtan urban cluster, and the Yichang-Jingzhou integrated area. In 2021, the study area accounted for 3.4% of China’s land area, supported 9.2% of its population, and contributed 9.3% of its GDP. Specifically, Wuhan, leveraging its strategic location on the Yangtze River and its integration into the “eight vertical and eight horizontal” high-speed rail network, is evolving into a comprehensive international transportation hub, solidifying its central role in Midwest China30. The engineering machinery industry cluster in the Changsha-Zhuzhou-Xiangtan urban agglomeration boasts a world-leading scale (targeted output value of 500 billion RMB by 2025), continues to expand its global influence. Abundant reserves of lithium mica in Yichun, Jiangxi, accounting for 28–40% of China’s total, underpin the city’s status as the “Lithium Capital of Asia”. Meanwhile, Hubei province is accelerating its New Energy Vehicle (NEV) industry through policy support, with a planned production capacity of 1.75 million units (approximately 9% of the national total) and target of exceeding 1 million units by 2025.

Integrating ESV into economic decision-making can systematically promote a low-carbon industrial transition. Quantifying ESV enables prioritized support for eco-friendly industries while incentivizing traditional industries to upgrade. Spatial ESV mapping further optimizes land development, as exemplified by Wuhan’s incorporation of Donghu Wetland ESV assessments into urban planning, limiting development intensity.

Data preprocessing was conducted using ArcGIS 10.8. First, we masked the Chinese Academy of Sciences LUCC data to the study area boundaries to ensure a consistent spatial extent. All the data were converted to the WGS-1984-UTM-Zone-49N coordinate system, and resampled to a cell size of 150 m by 150 m to match the resolution of the land use data for the prediction. The LUCC data were subsequently reclassified according to the six land cover types. Following LUCC processing, ArcGIS environment settings were configured using the 2020 LUCC as a reference. Driving factors were masked, and proximity variables were generated using Euclidean distance calculations. This ensured a consistent format for all input variables. Most importantly, the road network data were derived from the publicly accessible and validated open-source repositories https://www.openstreetmap.org34. The population and GDP data vere obtained from the Chinese Academy of Sciences http://www.resdc.cn, ESV data were obtained from http://tjj.hubei.gov.cn/, http://tjj.hunan.gov.cn/, and http://tjj.jiangxi.gov.cn35. These reliable and traceable data sources guaranteed overall data quality. All preprocessed datasets were further used to construct a grid within ArcGIS for spatial analysis, ultimately generating an ESV distribution map. This map was converted to a raster format to support quantitative analyses.

PLUS model

This study employs the PLUS model, which was developed by the HMSCIL@CUG laboratory at the China University of Geosciences. As a spatially explicit tool, PLUS is designed to forecast future land use dynamics and assess their impact on ecosystem integrity25. Building upon traditional cellular automata (CA) methods, the PLUS model incorporates both the Land Expansion Analysis Strategy (LEAS) and CA based on Multiple Random Seeds (CARS) modules. This structure allows for a more nuanced simulation of land conversion processes and complex landscape responses. The PLUS model workflow begins by identifying land use expansion areas from multi-temporal datasets. The LEAS module utilizes a Random Forest algorithm to quantify the relationships between land use transitions and environmental and socioeconomic drivers, generating spatially explicit probability surfaces for each land cover type. The contribution degree of driving factors is calculated through the LEAS module of the PLUS model. Specifically, by superimposing the land use data of two periods to extract the change raster and inputting it into the LEAS module, after importing the driving factor data, this model can automatically complete coordinate alignment and spatial range matching. In this study, the random forest parameters are set as follows: uniform sampling, 20 decision trees, a sampling rate of 0.05, the number of features does not exceed the total number of driving factors, the running parameters are set to 12 to improve efficiency, and finally, the development probability maps of each land use type and the contribution degree of driving factors are obtained38. Finally, CARS integrates a random patch generation and a decreasing threshold mechanism to simulate future land use patterns. To evaluate the sensitivity of land use trajectories to policy choices, we implemented a scenario-based analysis to predict land use patterns under four distinct future development pathways: BAU, EDP, EPP, and EEB. This framework enables a systematic assessment of competing land use objectives and their impact on ESV. The robustness of the PLUS model predictions was rigorously evaluated using established metrics (OA > 0.95) and a modified FoM > 0.10, ensuring confidence in the simulated land use patterns and their downstream effects39.These validation metrics allowed us to quantify the model’s ability to reproduce observed land use changes and assess the reliability of the scenario-based projections. The application of the PLUS model provides a foundation for land planning and policy. As demonstrated in the study area, the analysis reveals the spatial differentiation of land use patterns under different scenarios. Although the driving factors analysis is essential for this process, however the need for careful consideration of model parameters underscores the sensitivity of spatial planning tools. Furthermore, this method still needs to be improved, such as monetizing the value of ecological projects. Accuracy evaluation and neighborhood weight parameter setting

We validated the 2020 LUCC data from our cellular automata model for the Middle Yangtze River region by comparing it with pre-existing, independently validated LUCC data. The model achieved a high OA of 0.95, and a Kappa coefficient of 0.9148, indicating strong predictive capability. To assess model performance in more detail, we evaluated individual land cover types using Producer’s Accuracy (PA) and User’s Accuracy (UA), providing a granular analysis of the model’s prediction accuracy. Meanwhile, we defined the neighborhood weight parameter, which reflects the expansion intensity of land use types under internal and external driving factors. This parameter ranges from 0 to 1, with values closer to 1 indicating stronger expansion capacity. Ecosystem service valuation assessment

The equivalent coefficient method is a commonly used approach in ecological studies.

Discussion

Uniqueness of the study area

The Middle Yangtze River region is distinguished by its intricate geographic setting and pronounced heterogeneity in climate and geomorphology52. As the core of the Yangtze River Economic Belt and strategically situated along a major inland waterway, this region holds unparalleled economic and geopolitical significance53,54. Functioning as a linchpin in China’s “Rise of Central China” strategy, the Middle Yangtze River region also serves as a critical transportation corridor linking eastern and western, as well as northern and southern, China. The land expansion in the cities of the Yangtze River Delta and the Pearl River Delta has entered a relatively stable and continuous development stage. The economic growth and poverty reduction effects are moderate and continuously strengthening. However, in the Middle Reaches of the Yangtze River, it is still in a rapid expansion stage with high annual growth rate of construction land and continuous decline of ESV, and the land expansion rate of the central cities Wuhan, Changsha, and Nanchang is still faster than that of the Yangtze River Delta. The quality of foreign investment is relatively high, the industrial structure leans towards intermediate products, and the technological autonomy is relatively strong. The growth rate of the secondary industry has a significant effect on poverty reduction. In the Pearl River Delta, foreign investment mainly focuses on labor-intensive processing and export, with the industrial chain biased towards final products and relying on external intermediate products. The growth rate of the tertiary industry has a more significant effect on poverty reduction55,56. At present, the Middle Reaches of the Yangtze River mainly rely on land expansion to drive employment, and the acceleration of urbanization and industrialization processes has led to a major transformation in the model, seriously damaging the key ecosystem services57. The continuous reduction of farmland20, the intensification of water resource pressure, and the increasingly serious environmental pollution jointly threaten the long-term sustainability of this region. The findings obtained in this study not only provide empirical support for the sustainable development strategies of the Middle Reaches of the Yangtze River, but also offer a transferable spatial governance model for other rapidly urbanizing regions.

Land use simulation for 2030 using the plus model

To support sustainable land governance in the Middle Yangtze River region, we applied the PLUS model, which integrates the LEAS and a cellular automata framework based on multi-type random seeds (CARS). The PLUS model largely compensates for the deficiencies of existing CA models (such as CA-Markov, CLUE-S, FLUS) in terms of the mining strategies for transformation rules and the simulation strategies for landscape dynamic changes58. It can dynamically simulate the patch-level changes of various land use types, and this has already been confirmed by existing research59,60. Compared with models such as FLUS and CA-Markov, the PLUS model has higher simulation accuracy. The commonly used models for land use change research include the Markov model, CA, and FLUS model. Existing studies have shown that these models are difficult to achieve both quantitative prediction and spatial pattern prediction simultaneously2,61,62. Unlike conventional land use models, PLUS incorporates a random forest algorithm to strengthen the relationship between land use transitions and their driving forces, allowing spatially explicit simulation of spatial dynamics. Model validation, based on OA = 0.95 and the Kappa coefficient (0.91), verified the model’s high accuracy and reliability for projecting land use patterns. Using historical land use data from two time points, the model derived land use transition rules through the LEAS module and simulated future configurations with the CARS model. Land use projections were conducted for 2030 under four policy-relevant scenarios—BAU, EDP, EPP, and EEB—to evaluate divergent development trajectories and associated trade-offs between urban growth and ecosystem integrity. Results indicate that the EDP scenario results in a dramatic expansion of construction land (+ 5.52%, or + 54,077 hm2), accompanied by declines in farmland (− 0.45%) and forestland cover (− 0.13%), reflecting intensified development pressure. Industrial land expands at an annual rate of 4.2%, surpassing other scenarios. While ecological degradation is partially alleviated through urban densification strategies (floor area ratio > 2.0) and brownfield redevelopment, the scenario underscores the risk of ecological pressure. In contrast, the EPP scenario—aligned with restoration-focused policies—results in a 10.19% reduction in construction land (− 99,740 ha), with corresponding gains in farmland (+ 0.68%, or + 51,288 ha) and forest area (+ 0.31%, or + 44,154 ha). Ecological reserves occupy 30% of the landscape, and construction land is restricted to less than 7%, approximately 35% lower than in EDP, demonstrating the effectiveness of strict ecological protection policies. In the EPP scenario, the reduction of construction land63–65 and the increase of cultivated land/forests occur simultaneously, and this is highly dependent on cross-regional quota trading mechanisms (such as construction land indicators trading, balance of cultivated land occupation and compensation, and large-scale reclamation of inefficient/unused construction land). In the short term, this is feasible only in specific regions. Moreover, this scenario creates favorable conditions for the implementation of carbon sequestration trading schemes and ecological restoration mechanisms. The EEB scenario presents a more moderate trajectory, with construction land decreasing by 2.53% and farmland increasing by 0.24%, indicating a feasible path to harmonize development with ecological objectives. These scenario-based simulations reveal the spatial heterogeneity of land use responses to policy choices and emphasize the importance of terrain-informed, policy-aligned planning frameworks. The PLUS model proves effective in identifying critical trade-offs and synergies, offering actionable insights for land use optimization and sustainable urban-regional planning in fast-developing contexts.

Scenario-based assessment of land use change and ecosystem service value in the Middle Yangtze River Region

Shifts in land use patterns exert a profound influence on ESV, particularly in rapidly urbanizing regions where construction land encroachment accelerates ESV degradation66,67. Our findings reveal a complex and spatially heterogeneous trade-off between economic expansion and ecological sustainability across the Middle Yangtze River region. Although agricultural land remains a dominant land use type, it has become a focal point of land use conflict in high-growth urban centers such as Wuhan, Ezhou, and Qianjiang. Between 2010 and 2020, the region experienced a net ESV loss of CNY 26.871 billion (− 1.04%), largely driven by the conversion of arable and unused land into construction zones, an outcome that reflects the systemic undervaluation of ecological assets in development strategies. Scenario-based projections for 2020–2030 under four alternative trajectories,namely BAU, EDP, EPP, and EEB, suggest divergent futures for ESV across the region. The EDP scenario yields the sharpest ESV decline, reinforcing the view that unmitigated economic intensification undermines ecosystem functionality. In contrast, EPP and EEB scenarios demonstrate that targeted conservation policies can stabilize or even reverse ESV losses, particularly through the strategic restoration of green infrastructure and ecosystem corridors. Spatial patterns consistently show that water bodies, which are subject to strict land-conversion restrictions, function as enduring ESV hotspots. Meanwhile, construction and unused lands persist as areas of low ecological value. Under EPP and EEB, the proportion of high-ESV areas (ESV indices between 11.34 and 18.12) increases markedly by 2030, highlighting the potential of ecological prioritization to enhance multifunctional landscapes. Notably, the EPP scenario achieves the smallest ESV reduction, while EDP registers the most significant loss, underscoring the ecological cost of growth-first planning paradigms. The conflicts between land development and ecological degradation across scenarios can be explained by the “metabolic rift” theory68–70: the EDP scenario widens this rift through unregulated occupation of ecological and agricultural land, while EPP and EEB alleviate it by strengthening ecological protection and regulating land conversion. To address this tension, the Middle Yangtze River region must shift from linear, extractive land-use models to circular, regenerative ones. Institutionalizing ecosystem service accounting within territorial spatial planning frameworks offers a viable pathway to reconcile urbanization with ecological resilience. This includes placing stringent limits on farmland-to-construction conversion in ecologically sensitive zones and embedding ESV valuation into the logic of land governance. Future research should prioritize the quantification of long-term socio-ecological returns from maintaining or enhancing ESV, thereby equipping policymakers with robust metrics to support evidence-based, SDG-aligned planning.

Shortcomings and future prospects

This study applies the PLUS model to evaluate the impacts of land-use change on ecosystem service value (ESV) in the study area. Although the model achieves high (OA = 0.95) and strong agreement (Kappa = 0.91), several methodological limitations constrain its explanatory power and policy relevance. First, the absence of systematic benchmarking against alternative land-use modeling frameworks—such as FLUS and CA-Markov—hampers robust assessment of model sensitivity and obscures the interplay among key drivers, especially the synergistic or antagonistic effects of policy interventions and biophysical constraints. Second, reliance on static socioeconomic proxies—GDP and population—overlooks temporally evolving processes, including climate-driven variability (precipitation anomalies) and adaptive policy shifts (carbon neutrality targets). Third, the spatial resolution mismatch between 30-m land-cover maps and 1-km2 socioeconomic layers risks aggregating out fine-scale urban–rural gradients, thereby diluting spatially explicit insights into ESV distribution and vulnerability. Fourth, the use of static ESV equivalence factors fails to capture the temporal dynamics of ecosystem functionality, including feedback effects from ecological restoration and functional transitions in response to land use shifts. Finally, the scenario framework does not incorporate key emerging dynamics, such as extreme weather events (floods) or innovative instruments like blue carbon markets, both of which are increasingly relevant under climate change. Future research should address these gaps through four key directions. Integrate CA-Markov, FLUS, and GEP71 to dissect cascading drivers, for instance, tracing the pathway from road density to forest fragmentation and ultimately, the loss of ESV. Employ high-frequency, multi-source data such as nighttime satellite imagery, social media indicators, and artificial intelligence approaches (convolutional neural networks), alongside CMIP6 climate projections, to enhance scenario granularity and predictive robustness. Develop region-specific, temporally dynamic ESV equivalence factors that reflect unique ecological functions—such as subtropical forest regulation capacity—and incorporate them into process-based tools like InVEST to capture nonlinear feedbacks and inform “low-carbon, smart growth” strategies. Construct a macro-level policy framework grounded in community-level participation; pilot cross-sector ecological restoration schemes in critical ESV hotspots such as the east lake wetland; and institutionalize cross-watershed governance mechanisms to reconcile ecological protection with urban expansion. Together, these future directions offer a pathway toward more precise, dynamic, and policy-relevant spatial planning frameworks for sustainable urban development in rapidly transforming megaregions.

Conclusion

This study applies the PLUS model to simulate land-use change and associated ESV under four contrasting scenarios—BAU, EDP, EPP, and EEB—using pixel-level allocation of land-cover types. Key findings are as follows: Between 2000 and 2010, construction land expanded by 4,253.62 km2, accompanied by declines in farmland (− 5,299.76 km2) and grassland (− 1,209.36 km2). From 2010 to 2020, construction increased further by 3,831.73 km2. while water bodies (− 549.68 km2), farmland (− 2,033.42 km2), and forest (− 1,477.08 km2) all contracted. Under the EDP scenario, construction land is projected to expand by 5.52%. The EPP scenario, which emphasizes environmental protection, led to an increase in farmland and forest, while construction land decreased significantly. By prioritizing ecology (with 30% of its area designated as ecological protection zones), EPP limited construction land to less than 7%, a 35% reduction compared to EDP. By 2030, ESV under EDP reaches 5,105.34 CNY/ha, whereas the EEB scenario exhibits the strongest positive correlation with ESV. Historical analysis reveals a 1.04% decline in regional ESV from 2010 to 2020, and it is predicted that the EDP scenario will continue to cause the largest decline in ESV (1.04%) during the forecast period. Spatial analysis indicates that the EPP and EEB scenarios can generate the highest ecosystem service value (11.34 and 18.12), while the EDP scenario consistently shows the lowest ESV level and the weakest temporal change. By simulating land use and ecosystem service value under multiple scenarios, this study provides a reference for the sustainable development of cities in the study area and beyond. Future research should design systems based on regional characteristics, integrate urban underground construction comprehensively, and build a more objective ESV.
