Adipose precursor cell-derived clusterin promotes homeostasis in subcutaneous adipose tissue
Authors: Zong Chen, Zhiyou Chen, Jiuzuo Huang, Nanze Yu, Xinyan Chen, Yuming Chong, Hao Liu, Xiaolei Jin, Xueying Hou, Xiao Long
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryAdipose precursor cells (APCs), present within the stromal vascular fraction (SVF) of adipose tissue, play an important role in adipose biology. However, its regulatory functions in adipogenesis and tissue homeostasis remain incompletely characterized. Here, we isolated APC from the subcutaneous adipose tissue of Prx1-Cre; Rosa26-tdTomato mice and confirmed that APC has a high adipogenic differentiation efficiency. Next, RNA sequencing analysis indicates that APC may have dual effects of promoting adipogenesis and anti-inflammatory effects. Subsequent in vivo studies identified clusterin (Clu) as a key secretory factor through which APC exerts its regulatory functions. By utilizing gene regulation and APC/adipocyte co-culture, we further demonstrated that APC-derived Clu promotes its own adipogenic differentiation and improves local resistance to oxidative stress. Collectively, data from conditional Clu knockout mice establish its key function in preserving adipose tissue homeostasis. These findings highlight Clu as a potential therapeutic target for interventions aimed at adipose tissue homeostasis and regeneration.Keywords: adipose precursor cells, stromal vascular fraction, adipogenesis, Prx1, clusterin

Introduction

Adipose tissue, as an important part of connective tissue, regulates various physiological activities (Zwick et al., 2018). The steady-state maintenance and regeneration ability of adipose tissue play a crucial role in tissue repair and reconstruction (Wang et al., 2013). Previous studies have established the important roles of distinct adipocyte types and adipocyte browning in maintaining adipose tissue homeostasis and metabolic regulation, while the role of adipose precursor cells (APCs) in these processes is not yet fully understood (Berry and Rodeheffer 2013; Berry et al., 2014; Rodeheffer et al., 2008; Liu et al., 2022). APCs are a substantial subset of adipose-derived mesenchymal cells residing in the stromal vascular fraction (SVF) (Rodeheffer et al., 2008; Sanchez-Gurmaches et al., 2015). SVF has been widely reported for its adipogenic and regulatory effects in vitro and in vivo, and its potential differentiation and paracrine effects contribute to its effects (Bora and Majumdar 2017). It should be noted that SVF is a highly heterogeneous cell population, including adipocyte progenitor cells, precursor adipocytes, and other components such as endothelial progenitor cells, pericytes, fibroblasts, and immune cells (Bora and Majumdar 2017). At present, the contributions and mechanisms of action of each component in SVF are not very clear. Previous studies suggested that APC was capable of generating adipose tissue and establishing functional fat deposits following transplantation, indicating their potential significance in regenerative medicine (Rodeheffer et al., 2008; Berry et al., 2014). APCs derived from subcutaneous fat are a rich source of regenerative medicine cells, so the ability to purify, culture, and transplant them may lead to new cell-based therapies (Sanchez-Gurmaches et al., 2015). In addition, APCs may also play an important role in the local microenvironment of adipose tissue. Elucidating the regulatory mechanism of APCs is of great significance for understanding and treating adipose tissue-related metabolic diseases (Berry et al., 2014; Sanchez-Gurmaches and Guertin 2014).

An important advancement in the study of adipose tissue is the establishment of a technique for isolating APC from mice (Krueger et al., 2014; Rodeheffer et al., 2008). Previous studies have isolated and characterized distinct stromal subpopulations from white adipose tissue based on cell surface markers (Sanchez-Gurmaches et al., 2015; Rodeheffer et al., 2008; Berry and Rodeheffer 2013). In addition, investigations into specific Cre drivers with preferential activity in precursors revealed that Prx1-Cre labels subcutaneous APC (Krueger et al., 2014; Sanchez-Gurmaches et al., 2015). This labeling is largely confined to precursor cells and mature subcutaneous white adipocytes within the fat depot, with minimal recombinase activity detected in other cell types in the examined adipose tissue (Krueger et al., 2014; Sanchez-Gurmaches et al., 2015).

In this study, we isolated APC (lineage−CD31−CD45−tdTomato+) from the SVF of Prx1-Cre; Rosa26-tdTomato mice and assessed their adipogenic potential and gene expression profile. RNA sequencing analysis was employed, leading to the preliminary identification of clusterin (Clu) as a paracrine factor through which APCs have dual effects of regulating adipogenesis and inflammation. Fat transplantation is an ideal model for exploring adipogenesis and inflammatory stress. Using two different fat transplantation models, we validated that Clu secretion by APC exerts both anti-inflammatory and pro-adipogenic effects in vivo. Further experiments have confirmed that upregulating the expression of Clu can significantly enhance the adipogenic ability and efficiency of APCs, and improve the tolerance of oneself and adjacent tissues to oxidative stress. Evidence from adipose-specific Clu knockout models underscored its vital role in adipose tissue homeostasis.

Results

Prx1 lineage APC exhibit high adipogenic efficiency

Based on established methods for the isolation and identification of APC (Krueger et al., 2014; Sanchez-Gurmaches et al., 2015; Rodeheffer et al., 2008), we first enriched APC from SVF using fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) and cultured the sorted cells for subsequent experiments. The remaining SVF (APC-free SVF) were also collected (Figures 1A and 1B). The 3T3-L1 cell line, a fibroblastic preadipocyte lineage derived from mouse embryos, is widely used as a model system in studies of adipogenesis and adipocyte biology due to its robust capacity to differentiate into adipocytes (Zebisch et al., 2012). To investigate the role and differences of APC or SVF in adipogenesis, we first compared the adipogenic potential of APC and SVF (without APC) against 3T3-L1 cells as a control. After 8 days of adipogenic induction, Oil Red O staining was performed to assess lipid accumulation. The results demonstrated a larger positively stained area in APC, indicating a higher level of lipid deposition and suggesting superior adipogenic differentiation efficiency. On the other hand, SVF without APC has almost no ability to differentiate and generate adipocytes (Figure 1C). Furthermore, we harvested both cell types and quantified the expression of key adipogenic marker genes (Cebpa, Pparg). The APC group showed significantly elevated expression levels of these markers compared to the control cells, with Pparg expression increasing more than 4-fold (Figure 1D). The expression level of adipogenic-related genes in SVF without APC is quite low (Figure 1D).

Although Prx1 is known to label multipotent stem cells in multiple tissues, including bone and dermis, the functional properties of Prx1+ cells may vary depending on their tissue origin. To investigate this, we isolated Prx1+ cells from cranial sutures (suture precursor cells, SPC) of Prx1-Cre; Rosa26-tdTomato mice using the same FACS strategy and compared their proliferation and differentiation capacities with those of subcutaneous APC. EdU incorporation assays showed no significant difference in proliferation between APC and SPC (Figure S1A). However, under standardized differentiation conditions, APC exhibited significantly higher adipogenic potential than SPC as assessed by Oil Red O staining, whereas SPC displayed markedly stronger osteogenic (Alizarin Red) and chondrogenic (Alcian blue) differentiation capacities (Figure S1B). These results suggest that while Prx1+ cells from different tissues share a common lineage marker, their differentiation preferences are strongly influenced by the tissue-specific microenvironment (niche).

APC may play a role in adipogenic/inflammatory regulation

To investigate the differences in gene expression patterns between APC and SVF, we collected APC and APC-free SVF (SVF without APC, as a control) for RNA sequencing. Differential gene expression analysis revealed 798 significantly upregulated and 835 significantly downregulated genes in the APC group compared to the control. Notably, the expression of the Clu gene was markedly elevated, while inflammation-related genes such as Slc37a2, Mmp13, and Srxn1 were significantly suppressed (Figure 2A).

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses of upregulated genes indicated strong associations with lipid biosynthesis, adipocyte differentiation, PPAR signaling, and adipokine signaling pathways (Figures 2B and 2C). Conversely, downregulated genes were predominantly enriched in biological processes related to IL-1 and IL-6 production, inflammatory response regulation, as well as the NF-κB and TNF signaling pathways (Figures 2D and 2E). Collectively, these results suggest that APC may play a key role in attenuating inflammatory responses and promoting adipogenic differentiation. This inference was further supported by gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of adipogenesis and inflammation-related pathways. GSEA suggested that genes related to adipogenesis were significantly upregulated in APC, while genes related to inflammatory factors were significantly downregulated (Figure 2F).

APC co-transplantation significantly improves adipose tissue survival, alleviates local inflammation

The results of the previous section suggest that APC may play an important role in adipogenesis and inflammation regulation. Previous studies have confirmed that adipose tissue transplantation is an appropriate model for exploring adipogenesis and inflammation (Trotzier et al., 2023; Zhan et al., 2017). Based on the fat transplantation model, we next investigated the in vivo adipogenic characteristics of APC and preliminarily explored the role of APC in adipogenesis/inflammation regulation.

We first constructed a C57 mouse fat transplantation model and added appropriate amounts of APC or remaining SVF for co-transplantation (Figure 3A). PBS was designed as a blank control. Results demonstrated that co-transplantation with APC significantly improved long-term (three months after transplantation) volume retention and graft quality compared to both PBS and SVF groups (Figures 3B and 3C). Perilipin1 is a lipid-droplet-associated protein widely used as a marker of mature and viable adipocytes (Sun et al., 2013; Zhang et al., 2021). We performed Perilipin1 immunostaining on grafts harvested at 1, 4, and 12 weeks post-transplantation to evaluate adipocyte survival and distribution. The results revealed that at both 4 and 12 weeks, the APC co-transplantation group exhibited a higher Perilipin1+ area ratio than the control groups, indicating enhanced adipocyte survival and regeneration (Figure 3D).

Furthermore, histological analysis via HE staining was conducted on grafts at multiple time points. Tissue morphology was scored to objectively evaluate the regenerative and regulatory effects of APC supplementation. The results showed that the APC group exhibited significantly reduced inflammatory infiltration in the early stages post-transplantation. Long-term assessments also revealed improved structural integrity of grafts, along with the reduced formation of oil cysts, vacuoles, and fibrotic areas (Figure 3E). Compared with the blank control, co-transplantation of SVF without APC improved inflammatory infiltration and fibrosis levels in the graft to a certain extent (Figure 3E).

APC enhances paracrine secretion of Clu, promoting adipogenesis and alleviating local inflammation

RNA sequencing differential expression analysis had identified significant upregulation of the Clu gene in the APC. Clu (also known as apolipoprotein J) is a novel adipokine whose secreted form has been implicated in anti-inflammatory and stress responses (Rodríguez-Rivera et al., 2021; De Miguel et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2024). Previous studies indicate that its overexpression promotes adipogenic differentiation in 3T3-L1 cells (Oh et al., 2020). It should be noted that the cellular localization of Clu exhibits heterogeneity. In part of tumor cells and immune cells, Clu is localized in the nucleus and plays a role in promoting cell apoptosis (Du et al., 2025). Histologic evidence and RNA sequencing analysis suggest that APC could improve adipocyte survival/adipogenesis, relieve local inflammation, and fibrosis. We therefore hypothesized that APC co-transplantation enhances graft adipogenesis and attenuates inflammation through the upregulation of paracrine Clu secretion.

We first measured Clu mRNA expression in the grafts over time and observed significant upregulation in the APC group after transplantation (Figure 4A). Subsequent Clu immunostaining revealed markedly elevated protein levels in the APC co-transplantation group, with Clu localized predominantly in the intercellular spaces and cytoplasm. In the control group, the nuclear localization of Clu significantly increased (Figure 4B). Furthermore, using immunofluorescence co-staining, we observed clear co-localization of Clu with tdTomato-labeled Prx1 lineage APC, suggesting that Clu production and secretion originate mainly from APC lineage (Figure 4C). To further confirm the cellular source of Clu, we performed co-immunofluorescence staining for Clu and PdgfRα, a well-established marker of APCs (Krueger et al., 2014). The results showed clear co-localization of Clu with PdgfRα in the grafts, supporting that Clu is predominantly produced and secreted by the APC lineage (Figure S2). Furthermore, we performed co-staining with Perilipin1 and tdTomato to demonstrate the temporal distribution and patterns of surviving adipocytes (Perilipin1+ and tdTomato−) and APC regenerating adipocytes (Perilipin1+ and tdTomato+) after transplantation. The staining results indicate that the number of adipocytes regenerated from APCs after transplantation gradually increases, and a considerable number of transplanted adipocytes survive (Figure 4D).

Then, we quantitatively assessed the expression levels of adipogenic genes (Fabp4, Cebpa, and Pparg) and pro-inflammatory cytokines (Il1, Il6, and Tnfa) in grafts at various time points. The results aligned with prior data and interpretations, demonstrating significantly reduced inflammatory factor expression (Figure 4E) and enhanced adipogenic gene expression (Figure 4F) in the APC co-transplantation group.

Clu enhances APC-dependent adipocyte regeneration

In order to further investigate the direct effects of APC and APC-dependent Clu secretion on adipocyte survival and regeneration, and to explore the impact of Clu on APC outcomes at the in vivo level, we established another fat transplantation model. In this model, we transplanted subcutaneous fat from Prx1-Cre; Rosa26-tdTomato mice subcutaneously into the back of C57BL/6 mice. After transplantation, recombinant Clu protein was injected subcutaneously (every three days for one month) to simulate Clu secretion and investigate its effects, with a PBS-injected group serving as the control (Figure 5A). Graft volume retention was assessed at 30 and 90 days, along with the evaluation of inflammatory cytokine and adipogenic marker gene expression within the transplants.

The results demonstrated that Clu treatment significantly improved graft volume retention (Figure 5B), reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine levels, and enhanced adipogenesis within the grafts (Figure 5C). Furthermore, immunostaining for Perilipin1 and tdTomato revealed a substantially higher proportion of Perilipin1+tdTomato+ cells in the Clu-treated group compared to PBS control, indicating that Clu effectively promotes APC-dependent adipocyte regeneration (Figure 5D).

Upregulation of Clu promotes APC adipogenic differentiation and improves its oxidative stress tolerance

To further investigate the functional role of Clu in APC biology, we generated Clu-overexpressing and Clu-knockdown APCs. In brief, Clu overexpression and knockdown were achieved by transfecting APC with cDNA3.1-Clu or Clu-specific shRNA plasmids. The efficiency of genetic modulation was confirmed by measuring Clu secretion levels in the supernatant using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) (Figure S3). We first examined how Clu expression influences the adipogenic potential of APC. Cells from the overexpression, knockdown, and control groups were subjected to adipogenic induction for 8 days. Lipid accumulation was evaluated by Oil Red O staining on days 4 and 8, together with quantitative analysis of adipogenic gene expression. The results demonstrated that Clu overexpression enhanced adipogenic differentiation, as indicated by a significant increase in lipid droplet size (Figure 6A). Consistent with this, the expression of the adipogenic markers Pparg and Cebpa was markedly upregulated in Clu overexpressing APC, whereas their transcript levels were suppressed in the Clu knockdown group (Figure 6B).

Since oxidative stress is a key trigger of inflammation in cells and tissues (Hybertson et al., 2011), we also asked whether Clu influences the oxidative stress response in APC. A hydrogen peroxide (H2O2)-based model of oxidative stress was established, and a concentration of 200 μmol/L was selected after gradient testing (Figure S4). Clu overexpressing, knockdown, and control APC were treated with H2O2, and cell viability was assessed at 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9 h post-treatment. Intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels were measured at 1, 5, and 9 h. We found that Clu overexpression significantly improved APC tolerance to oxidative stress and reduced H2O2-induced cell death (Figure 6C). ROS imaging further confirmed that Clu overexpressing cells maintained lower intracellular ROS levels under stress (Figure 6D).

Upregulation of Clu expression in APC can enhance oxidative stress tolerance in adjacent adipocytes and preserve adipocyte genetic characteristics

To explore how Clu expression in APC influences neighboring adipocytes, we established an in vitro co-culture model using APC and mature adipocytes. Adipocytes were cultured following the membrane-based adipocyte aggregate culture (MAAC) system (Harms et al., 2019), in which adipocytes are placed on a permeable membrane rather than in floating culture, with APC seeded in an upper chamber (Figure S5A). First, adipocytes in the MAAC system were exposed to conditioned medium derived from control, Clu overexpressing, or Clu knockdown APC. To simulate oxidative stress, adipocytes were treated with 50 μmol/L H2O2, and intracellular ROS levels were assessed via live imaging and quantitative measurement. The results indicated that conditioned medium from Clu overexpressing APC significantly reduced oxidative stress in adipocytes (Figures S5B).

We next evaluated the expression of key adipocyte-related genes—including Pparg, Fabp4, and Adipoq—as well as the subcutaneous adipocyte marker Shox2 and the browning-related gene Ucp1 under each coculture condition (Harms et al., 2019). Upregulation of Clu in APC enhanced the expression of adipocyte identity genes in adjacent adipocytes, whereas Clu knockdown suppressed their expression (Figures S5C and S5D). This effect was time-dependent, with a gradual decline in marker expression observed over time in the Clu knockdown group (Figures S5C and S5D). Additionally, Clu expression in APC did not significantly affect Ucp1 levels, suggesting it does not modulate adipocyte browning under these conditions (Figure S5E).

Clusterin secreted by APC promotes the maintenance of subcutaneous adipose tissue homeostasis

To further investigate the role of APC-derived Clu in subcutaneous adipose tissue homeostasis, we generated Clu conditional knockout (Clu-cKO, Cluf/f) mice (Prx1-Cre; Rosa26-tdTomato; Clu-flox), enabling specific Clu deletion in Prx1-lineage cells. We first examined the effect of Clu ablation on the proliferative capacity of APCs within the inguinal adipose stromal compartment, marked by Ki67+tdTomato+ cells. The results showed that Clu knockout significantly inhibited APC proliferation (Figures 7A and 7B). Previous in vitro experiments indicated that APC-derived Clu enhances cellular resistance to oxidative stress. 8-OHdG is a well-established marker of oxidative DNA damage, reflecting oxidative stress levels in tissues, and we performed 8-OHdG staining to assess whether Clu knockout leads to increased oxidative damage in adipocytes. Consistently, our in vivo findings demonstrated that Clu knockout markedly increased oxidative damage in subcutaneous adipocytes, with this effect becoming more pronounced with age (Figures 7C and 7D). To determine whether exogenous Clu could functionally compensate for the loss of endogenous APC-derived Clu, we administered recombinant Clu protein locally into the inguinal fat pads of Clu-cKO mice. This approach tests whether Clu acts primarily as a paracrine factor and whether its effects can be rescued by exogenous supplementation. Specifically, recombinant Clu protein was administered via subcutaneous injection into the inguinal fat pad every three days for 30 days. Following the treatment period, inguinal adipose tissue was harvested from control, Clu-cKO, and Clu-supplemented Clu-cKO mice for the analysis of inflammatory and adipogenic marker gene expression (Figure 7E). Compared with controls, Clu-cKO adipose tissue exhibited significantly elevated mRNA levels of the pro-inflammatory cytokines Il1 and Il6, alongside reduced expression of key adipogenic markers (Pparg, Fabp4, Adipoq, and Shox2) (Figures 7F and 7G). Exogenous local Clu supplementation effectively attenuated the increased inflammatory gene expression and, to varying degrees, restored the expression of adipocyte signature genes (Figures 7F and 7G).

Discussion

In recent years, the application of genetic lineage tracing technologies has enabled the identification of APC in vivo (Krueger et al., 2014). These advances have established a foundation for exploring the role of APC in repair, reconstruction, and regenerative medicine (Rivera-Gonzalez et al., 2025). APC is the main component residing in SVF, and previous studies have confirmed that SVF plays a critical role in adipogenesis, regeneration, and inflammation regulation (Nguyen et al., 2016). But the role of APC in these processes is still unclear. In this study, using Prx1-Cre; Rosa26-tdTomato reporter mice in conjunction with various fat transplantation models, RNA sequencing, and experimental analyses, we provide evidence that APC can regulate its own and adjacent tissue adipogenesis and oxidative stress by paracrine Clu. Moreover, we generated Prx1-Cre; Rosa26-tdTomato; Clu-flox (Clu-cKO) mice to specifically ablate Clu in Prx1-lineage cells (APC). This model confirmed the essential role of APC-dependent Clu in maintaining adipose tissue homeostasis.

Ongoing progress in APC research relies on the availability of reliable tools for in vivo targeting (Sanchez-Gurmaches et al., 2015). Rodeheffer et al., (2008) were among the first to identify surface markers that distinguish APC from other stromal populations within adipose tissue. Subsequent work by Krueger et al., (2014) using lineage-tracing approaches demonstrated that Prx1-Cre specifically labels the majority of adipocyte precursors and mature white adipocytes in the inguinal fat pad. Their study compared the targeting specificity of Fabp4-Cre, PdgfRα-Cre, and Prx1-Cre within adipose-derived mesenchymal stromal cells and quantitatively assessed Cre recombinase activity among stromal subpopulations. The results indicated that Prx1-Cre activity is highly specific to APC derived from subcutaneous fat depots, particularly in male mice. It is noteworthy that recent studies have also revealed that beige adipose tissue within the inguinal depot originates from the Prx1+ lineage (Liu et al., 2022). Moreover, work by Liu et al., (2022) also suggests that Prx1-expressing cells may function as stem cells within white adipose tissue, playing an important role in maintaining tissue homeostasis. It has been proposed that Prx1-Cre may mark a developmental progenitor population that gives rise to subcutaneous white adipocytes. In summary, Prx1-Cre-mediated recombination enables long-term, restricted lineage tracing within subcutaneous white adipose tissue APC, making it a valuable tool for investigating genes with adipose-specific functions (Sanchez-Gurmaches et al., 2015).

In this study, we used flow cytometry to isolate APC along with the remaining SVF components and performed RNA sequencing to compare their transcriptional profiles. The results revealed that APCs exhibit stronger adipogenic potential and greater capacity to regulate inflammatory responses compared with the APC-depleted SVF. Fat transplantation represents a promising approach for soft tissue reconstruction; however, its clinical application is often hampered by poor adipocyte survival, limited regeneration, and inflammatory complications (Trotzier et al., 2023). Given these challenges, we considered fat transplantation a suitable model for investigating the regulatory functions of APC. To evaluate the role of APC in vivo, we established two models of fat grafting. Our findings demonstrate that APC supplementation significantly enhances adipocyte survival and regeneration within the grafts. Furthermore, using a co-culture system of APC and mature adipocytes, we obtained evidence that APC not only help maintain the expression of key adipogenic genes but also improve adipocyte resistance to oxidative stress.

Interestingly, the findings highlight Clu as a potentially significant therapeutic target in adipogenesis and inflammation regulation. Clu appears to exert both anti-inflammatory and pro-adipogenic effects and shows dependence on the Prx1+ APC lineage. Previous studies have indicated that Clu plays a protective role against endoplasmic reticulum stress and has been shown to mitigate inflammatory gene expression in models of neurodegenerative diseases and acute brain inflammation (Satapathy and Wilson 2021). Currently, data regarding Clu expression in adipose tissue remain limited. However, a study by Oh et al., (2020) reported increased Clu expression during the differentiation of 3T3-L1 preadipocytes. Specifically, Clu overexpression enhanced adipogenic differentiation and elevated mRNA levels of key adipogenic markers such as Pparg and Cebpa, whereas Clu knockdown impaired adipogenesis and reduced the expression of these genes. These observations are consistent with RNA sequencing and other experimental results. Our data suggest that secreted Clu regulates adipocytes through dual mechanisms: promoting cell survival and facilitating adipogenesis. Furthermore, these effects are APC-dependent, as demonstrated by APC co-transplantation, Clu (2020)overexpression/knockdown, and Clu-cKO.

Although the present study identifies APC-derived Clu as a key paracrine regulator of subcutaneous adipose tissue homeostasis, the downstream signaling pathways through which Clu exerts its pro-adipogenic, anti-inflammatory, and anti-oxidative stress effects remain to be fully elucidated. Based on previous literature, several candidates are worthy of future investigation. First, the PI3K/AKT pathway, particularly the Akt/GSK-3β axis, has been shown to mediate Clu’s cytoprotective effects against oxidative stress, which aligns with our observation that Clu reduces ROS accumulation in APCs and adjacent adipocytes (Jun et al., 2011). Second, Clu can modulate NF-κB activity; its ability to suppress NF-κB nuclear translocation and reduce pro-inflammatory cytokine expression provides a plausible mechanism for the reduced inflammation (Shim et al., 2011). Third, a direct link between Clu and adipogenesis has been established through KLF5 stabilization, which enhances Pparg and Cebpa expression during preadipocyte differentiation (Oh et al., 2020). Additionally, as an ER stress-responsive chaperone, Clu may alleviate ER stress and contribute to its overall protective functions (Satapathy et al., 2023). Future studies using cell-specific gene knockout or pharmacological inhibition of these pathways in APC-adipocyte co-culture systems are needed to establish the causal molecular mechanisms underlying Clu-mediated adipose tissue homeostasis.

Several limitations of the present study should also be considered. First, APC likely comprises heterogeneous functional subpopulations (Rodeheffer et al., 2008), and investigating the distinct roles and mechanisms of APC subtypes at various stages of adipogenesis and their regulation represents an important direction for future research. Additionally, although this study preliminarily elucidated the role of Clu as a key factor in APC regulation, there is tissue and cellular heterogeneity in the subcellular localization of Clu (Du et al., 2025), which tends to act as a secretory factor in the APC lineage. However, further research is needed to clarify the target cells and downstream regulatory mechanisms of its secretory phenotype.

In summary, our study elucidated the in vitro and in vivo adipogenic properties of APC and its regulatory role, and identified Clu as a promising target for therapeutic intervention.

Experimental model and study participant details

Animals

All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and Peking Union Medical College. All procedures were conducted in accordance with the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. Mice were maintained on a C57BL/6J background under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions, housed in standard cages at 23 ± 2°C with a 12-hour light/dark cycle and 50–60% humidity. Prx1-Cre mice (Stock No. 005584) were obtained from The Jackson Laboratory. Rosa26-lsl-tdTomato mice (Cat. No. NM-KI-225042) and Clu-Flox mice (Cat. NO. NM-CKO-210035) were purchased from Southern Model Organisms (Shanghai, China). Wild-type C57BL/6J male mice (8 weeks old) were acquired from Charles River Laboratories (Beijing, China).

Cell preparation

Cell isolation and fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) protocols were adapted from established methods (Krueger et al., 2014; Berry and Rodeheffer 2013; Sanchez-Gurmaches et al., 2015; Rodeheffer et al., 2008). Following euthanasia, mice were surface-sterilized with 75% ethanol for 3 minutes. The inguinal fat pads were aseptically dissected, cleared of fascia and visible blood vessels, minced, and digested in 0.1% collagenase type I at 37°C for 15 (to collect the upper layer for adipocytes) or 45 minutes. The SVF was collected by centrifugation. APC (defined as lineage-CD31-CD45-tdTomato+) were isolated using a BD FACS Aria III cell sorter. Single-cell suspensions were prepared in ice-cold staining buffer (PBS containing 2% FBS and 1 mM EDTA). Sorted cells were cultured for subsequent experiments.

Method details

Differentiation induction and staining

The adipogenic differentiation potential of APC, SVF and 3T3-L1 (#CL-006, Procell; ATCC: CL-173) cells was assessed using a commercial adipogenic differentiation kit (OriCell, Guangzhou) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. After 4 or 8 days of induction, cells were fixed and stained with Oil Red O. The osteogenic and chondrogenic differentiation of APC and SPC were also assessed using the commercial differentiation kit (OriCell, Guangzhou) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. After 2 or 3 weeks of induction, cells were fixed and stained with Alizarin Red and Alcian Blue.

Plasmid transfection of APC

Clu overexpression and knockdown plasmids were designed using the VectorBuilder platform (Guangzhou, China) and constructed by OBiO Technology (Shanghai, China). For Clu overexpression, the full-length mouse Clu coding sequence was cloned into the vector. For Clu knockdown, a short hairpin RNA (shRNA) targeting mouse Clu was inserted into the vector. APCs were seeded in 6-well plates and transfected with the corresponding plasmids using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. At 48 hours post-transfection, cells were harvested or subjected to subsequent experiments. Transfection efficiency was confirmed by measuring Clu secretion levels in the supernatant via enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA).

Fat grafting models and treatments

Two fat grafting models were established to investigate the role of APC in transplantation and regeneration: C57BL/6J Model: A total of 250 μL of donor inguinal adipose tissue mixed with 50 μL of PBS, SVF (5 × 105 cells), or APC (5 × 105 cells) was transplanted subcutaneously into the dorsal flank of recipient mice. Grafts were harvested at 1, 4, and 12 weeks post-transplantation for histological and molecular analyses. Graft volume retention and weight were measured at endpoint. Prx1-Cre; Rosa26-tdTomato Model: Inguinal fat from Prx1-Cre; Rosa26-tdTomato donors was transplanted into wild-type C57BL/6J recipients. Mice received subcutaneous injections of either PBS or recombinant mouse Clu protein (10 μg/mL) in the graft area (or inguinal adipose area) every three days until day 30. Grafts were collected at 30 or 90 days for analysis, and volume retention rates of grafts were evaluated.

Histological analysis

Grafts were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned at 5 μm thickness. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was performed using standard protocols. Two blinded investigators independently evaluated the H&E sections using a semi-quantitative scoring system (0–5) for adipocyte integrity, inflammation, cyst/vacuole formation, and fibrosis (Otani et al., 2024). Detailed scoring criteria are provided in Table S1.

Immunofluorescence

For immunofluorescence staining, sections were incubated with primary antibodies against Perilipin1 (1:300; Abcam), Clu (1:5000; Invitrogen), Ki-67 (1:400, CST), 8-OHdG(1:100, Santa-Cruz), PdgfRα (1:100, Santa-Cruz) or tdTomato (1:200; Arigo) overnight at 4°C. Alexa Fluor 488-, 594- or 647-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:1000; Abcam) were applied for 1 hour at room temperature. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. Perilipin1+ areas were quantified using ImageJ software (NIH).

Quantitative Real-Time Polymerase Chain Reaction (qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted from samples using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen) and reverse-transcribed with the HiScript III 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Vazyme). qPCR was performed on a CFX Connect system (Bio-Rad) with Taq Pro Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme). The 2ˆ–ΔΔCt method was used to calculate relative mRNA expression, normalized to Actb. Primer sequences are listed in key resources table.

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) detection

Use a reactive oxygen species detection kit (Beyotime, Shanghai) to measure APC or adipocyte ROS levels. For APC: Dilute DCFH-DA with MDEM basic medium solution at a ratio of 1:1000 to achieve a final concentration of 10 micromoles per liter. Remove the cell culture medium and add an appropriate volume of diluted DCFH-DA. Incubate in a 37°C cell culture incubator for 20 minutes. Wash the cells three times with PBS solution to thoroughly remove DCFH-DA that has not entered the cells. For adipocyte: Dilute DCFH-DA to a final concentration of 10 micromoles per liter. Collect 50 μL of adipocytes suspended in 450 μL of diluted DCFH-DA and mix thoroughly. Incubate in a 37°C cell culture incubator for 20 minutes. Wash the cells three times with PBS solution to thoroughly remove DCFH-DA that has not entered the cells. Observe with fluorescence microscope, or collect cells and detect them with fluorescence microplate reader.

Bulk RNA sequencing and analysis

Total RNA was isolated from APC and APC-free SVF using TRIzol. RNA integrity was assessed using a Bioanalyzer 2100 (Agilent). Library preparation and sequencing were performed by Novogene (Beijing, China). Differential gene expression analysis was conducted using DESeq2 in R, and functional enrichment analysis was carried out with the clusterProfiler package.

Quantification and statistical analysis

All data are presented as mean ± SD. For comparisons between two groups, unpaired two-tailed student's t test was used. For comparisons of three or more groups, one-way or two-way ANOVA was used if the normality test passed, followed by Sidak's /Tukey's/Dunnett's multiple comparison test for all groups, unless otherwise stated. Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism (v9.5.0). P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. ∗P < 0.05, ∗∗P < 0.01, ∗∗∗P < 0.001, ∗∗∗∗P < 0.0001. ns, not significant. Alphabet method was also used to indicate significance.
