A reliability analysis method for Christmas tree in operating conditions based on FEMCA, FTA and numerical simulation

Qiong Wang, Huimin Li, Guanlong Ren

ABSTRACT
The installation and recovery process of Christmas tree is complex and technically difficult, and it bears high internal and external loads during production and has extremely high requirements for operation reliability. To ensure the safe operation of China’s first domestically produced deepwater subsea Christmas tree installation and production conditions, a reliability analysis method for Christmas tree in operating conditions based on FEMCA, FTA and numerical simulation is established. Firstly, the FEMCA method is used to analyze the detailed operation steps of drill pipe installation method. The severity and hazard degree of the failure modes of each component during the installation and recovery process of Christmas tree are determined. The main failure modes are evaluated as deformation of the tree body, leakage of valve groups, leakage or blockage of connecting pipe fittings. Secondly, using the FTA method and based on the OREDA database, statistical analysis is conducted on the failure data and related failure modes. A fault tree model is established for 11 main component structures during the production operation, and the basic event probability, importance, and ranking of each component structure are calculated. The results shows that the average failure probability during production is 25.82 times per 106 hours, and its reliability parameters has an average failure interval of 4.42 years. The overall reliability level meets the production safety requirements. Thirdly, the stability numerical simulation analysis shows that the maximum stress ratio of the Christmas tree system under different ocean current conditions is 0.529, and the maximum bending moment is 7292 kN·m, and the safety operation window is established with the overall reliability level of the system meeting production safety requirements.

1. Introduction The subsea Christmas tree is the core component of the subsea oil and gas production system, and also the basis for building the subsea oil and gas well production system. Its main function is to control the flow of oil and gas from the wells, and it can also be used to control the injection of gas or water into non production wells. Due to the complex structure and high quality of the Christmas tree system, its design, manufacturing and installation operations are extremely difficult. In addition, the complex and changing working environment on the sea and the increasing water depth in future operations. Firstly, during the installation process of Christmas tree it may be damaged due to strong nonlinear environmental loads, ship movements, human factors and other reasons. In severe cases, it may endanger the installation and construction operations and cause major disasters such as installation overturning or later production leakage [ 1 , 2 ]. Secondly, the Christmas tree is connected to the subsea wellhead and shallow wellbore during its operational production phase, involving complex internal processes such as high-temperature and high-pressure oil and gas production, chemical reagent injection, and subsea control system power/communication for extended periods. Each component has a dual effect of internal production and external environmental loads. When the load borne by the Christmas tree exceeds the design limit, major safety accidents such as valve failure, pipeline erosion and even oil and gas leakage will occur [ 3 , 4 ]. Therefore, the operating risk of subsea Christmas tree during installation and operation is high and to improve the operation safety and reliability of the Christmas tree is crucial. The safety risk analysis is to select appropriate analysis methods for key components or operating processes of the Christmas tree, and obtain the risk factors, failure probability and risk impact of corresponding components at the system level, and in order to combine corresponding measures and suggestions to improve reliability. According to the purpose of risk analysis, available reliable data and decision-making needs, risk assessment methods are mainly divided into three categories: qualitative, semi quantitative and quantitative. At present, there are many risk analysis methods used for the installation and recovery process, the installation process of tubing hanger, the sea trial process, and the flow safety guarantee of overflow channels of the Christmas tree. The main qualitative methods include Fault Tree Analysis (FTA), Failure Mode, Effects and Criticality Analysis (FMECA), Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), Hazard and Operability Analysis (HAZOP), and quantitative methods such as Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution(TOPSIS), Bayesian Network (BN), and Mechanical Modeling Analysis(MMA), etc [ 5 ]. DUAN and Luo et al. [ 6 ] conducted a risk analysis during the installation process of horizontal Christmas trees using the fuzzy fault tree analysis method. Based on the qualitative analysis of the fault tree, they established the AHP-TOPSIS comprehensive evaluation method to quantitatively assess the multiple risk factors during the installation and leakage process of Christmas tree. Xiao et al. [ 7 ] used the AHP method to construct an evaluation index system for Christmas tree sea trials, and used fuzzy analysis method to conduct risk assessment on the comprehensive performance of Christmas tree sea trials. A. S. Cheliyan and Liu et al. [ 8 , 9 ] conducted a risk analysis of oil and gas leakage in Christmas tree system using a combination of fault trees and Bayesian networks. Wang et al. [ 5 ] addressed the shortcomings in the reliability analysis of Christmas tree and analyzed the applicability of four typical risk assessment methods for shallow water subsea Christmas tree. They also proposed practical engineering recommendations for shallow water subsea Christmas tree. Ping et al. used the Layer of Protection Analysis (LOPA) method to semi quantitatively analyze the Safety Integrity Level (SIL) classification of typical Christmas tree processes, which ensuring the safe and efficient operation of the subsea production system. Zhu et al. [ 10 ] used the fuzzy fault tree analysis method to conduct reliability analysis on the Christmas tree system, and further studied the failure mechanism of the Christmas tree. They established a Markov model for the Christmas tree system and quantitatively analyzed its reliability. Liu et al. [ 8 ] analyzed the system reliability of the Christmas tree during operation using a combination of FTA and FMEA methods. Det Norske Veritas has developed a series of assessment software for offshore oil engineering, mainly including Neptune Offshore® for risk assessment and hazard assessment in offshore oil and gas development equipment, Leak® for calculating the frequency of various petrochemical plant accidents, and SAFETI® for conducting multifunctional risk assessment and hazard assessment [ 11 , 12 ]. In general, scholars at China and abroad have conducted extensive analysis on the reliability of Christmas tree under typical operating conditions, involving various reliability analysis methods. However, there has been no use combined with FEMCA and FTA method to conduct risk analysis during the installation and recovery process of the Christmas tree, nor has there been a specific calculation of the failure probability and safety risk assessment of various components of the Christmas tree during operation conditions. And there is a lack of comprehensive risk analysis method from system to components of the Christmas tree in operating conditions [ 13 , 14 ]. Therefore, based on previous research and combined with the operational experience and installation resources in the South China Sea, firstly, this article uses the FMECA analysis method to conduct a risk analysis for the installation and recovery process of China’s first domestically produced deepwater Christmas tree, and the main failure modes are evaluated. Secondly, the FTA fault tree analysis method is used to calculate the failure probability of each major component of the Christmas tree system combined with the system failure statistical data of the in-service working conditions of the Christmas tree. Thirdly, numerical simulation analysis methods are used to analyze the stability of the system composed of Christmas tree and wellhead under different operating conditions, ultimately forming an a reliability analysis method based on FEMCA, FTA and numerical simulation. Corresponding suggestions and measures are proposed using the Bow model, which can provide technical support for the safe installation and recovery of the Christmas tree, as well as safe operation during production. 2. Under the pressure of the formation, the main body of the Christmas tree is subjected to load changes caused by temperature and pressure. The Christmas tree contains many valve channels inside, and the production fluid may generate local turbulence inside the tree, which may cause vortex induced vibration fatigue damage to the tree. The tubing hanger inside the Christmas tree needs to consider the stress effects of temperature and pressure changes and tubing weight under normal production conditions. At the same time, the wellhead connector connected to the Christmas tree will also generate axial separation force and mechanical load due to internal pressure. During oil and gas production, sand may be produced, and solid particles mixed in the oil and gas may cause erosion and wear on the pipeline and wall when passing through the internal oil and gas flow channels, throttle valves, etc. of the Christmas tree at a certain speed. Severe erosion may lead to problems such as leakage and reduced external pressure resistance. There are multiple electro-hydraulic crossing channels installed on the Christmas tree [ 18 ]. During the production process, it is necessary to inject hydrate inhibitors such as methanol and ethylene glycol from the outside, conduct pressure tests on the upper and lower plugs, control the downhole safety valve, and transmit downhole pressure and temperature data. Method establishment 3.1 Failure mode, effects, and criticality analysis (FMECA) Failure Mode, Effects and Criticality Analysis (FMECA) is a method to improve product quality and safety by analyzing the potential failure modes of various system components and their impact on product functionality, as well as the severity of the consequences. It proposes possible preventive and improvement measures. Its function is to verify the safety and reliability of the system, determine the causes of failure modes, and evaluate the potential risks that exist in the system. Due to the complex composition of the Christmas tree system, this method can discover various defects and weak links in the Christmas tree from the perspectives of product design, process, and overall system. Therefore, for all possible failures of the Christmas tree, based on the analysis of the failure modes, the impact of each failure mode on product operation is determined, single point failures are identified, and their harmfulness is determined according to the severity and probability of occurrence of the failure mode [ 19 , 20 ]. 3.1.1 Purpose and principle of FMECA. The purpose of FMECA analysis includes: (1) identifying harmful effects and failures on system operation, such as system termination, significant degradation of system operation, or impact on user’s safety. (2) determine the harmfulness of each failure mode related to the normal function or performance of the system, locate/mitigate the priority order of each failure mode and its related process impact. (3) improve the reliability and security of the system. (4) improve the maintainability of the system. The commonly used method for hazard analysis is the Risk Priority Number (RPN) method, which uses certain statistical methods to estimate factors such as the severity S (Severity), occurrence O (Occurrence), and detection D (Detection) of the consequences when a failure occurs, then calculates the RPN and determines whether improvement is necessary or the priority of improvement based on the RPN size, thereby reducing post event losses at a lower cost and improving system reliability. The calculation of RPN is as follows: R P N = O × S × D (1) Where O is the frequency of failure modes occurring within a predetermined or prescribed time period; S is the severity of the failure occurrence; D is a detectable measure and an estimated probability of identifying and eliminating failures when the system or user is affected. These three factors are scored using a 10 point grading scale, and failure modes can be classified based on RPN values. The higher the RPN value, the lower the reliability level of the evaluated object. The probability of failure mode occurrence should be considered from the following aspects: (1) experimental data from component life. (2) available failure rate database. (3) field failure data. (4) failure data of similar products or components. The failure probability classification table is shown in Table 3 . 10.1371/journal.pone.0359208.t003 Table 3 Classification of failure probability. By using failure detection method to analyze whether there are specific detection methods for each failure mode, we can provide a basis for the system’s failure detection and isolation design. Fault Tree Analysis (FTA) technology was developed by Bell Telegraph Company’s telephone laboratory in 1962. It uses logical methods to visually analyze hazards. Fault tree is a special type of inverted tree logical causal relationship diagram that describes the causal relationships between various events in a system using event symbols, logic gate symbols, and transition symbols [ 23 , 24 ]. There are two methods for fault tree analysis: qualitative analysis and quantitative analysis. Qualitative analysis identifies all possible fault modes that lead to the occurrence of the top event, and calculates all the minimum cut sets (MCS) of the faults. Quantitative analysis mainly involves two aspects: one is to calculate the failure probability of the system by inputting the failure probabilities of each unit (bottom event) of the system. The second step is to determine the structural importance, probability importance, and critical importance of each unit (bottom event). Finally, the optimal fault diagnosis and repair sequence can be sorted based on the magnitude of critical importance, and it can also be used as the first step to improve the data of relatively unreliable units. This method is a “descending form” analysis approach from the system to the components, and then to the parts. It starts from the system and analyzes the probability of a fault event (also known as a top event) occurring through a branching diagram drawn by logical symbols, gradually expanding into a tree. It can also be used to analyze the impact of part, component, or subsystem failures on system failures, including human factors and environmental conditions. During the operation of the Christmas tree, various components work together. Using this method to analyze the risks and reliability of various working components during high-temperature oil and gas production has great advantages. Moreover, this method can not only qualitatively but also quantitatively analyze system failures [ 25 ]. It can not only analyze system failures caused by a single component, but also analyze system failures caused by different modes of failure of multiple components. 3.2.2 Steps of FTA. The FTA analysis steps are as follows: (1) Familiar with Christmas tree system, define analysis objectives. (2) Determine the top event for fault tree analysis of Christmas tree system. (3) Identify all underlying events that caused the occurrence of the top event. (4) Establish a fault tree and determine the minimum cut set that causes the top event to occur. (5) Calculate the probability of the top event occurrence based on the probability of each bottom event in the fault tree. Quantitative analysis of fault trees can be divided into solving the probability of occurrence of top events and conducting importance analysis of bottom events. Results and discussion 4.1 FMECA analysis of installation and recovery process 4.1.1 Define system boundary. This article focuses on the testing of whether the components and functions of the Christmas tree are complete during the installation and recovery process, without considering factors in equipment manufacturing, processing, and transportation. In the specific FMECA analysis process, although there are multiple connections of components within the boundary, do not cross the boundary and only analyze the components within the boundary. In addition, the analysis is divided into subsystems based on each component, which are structurally interconnected and functionally interdependent, and are exactly in line with the installation sequence of key components of the Christmas tree. 4.1.2 Divide agreed level. When conducting risk assessment during the installation and recovery process of the Christmas tree, it is necessary to divide the agreed levels, and the basic principles for dividing the agreed levels depend on the expected results and the availability of design information. In the specific evaluation process, FMECA mainly analyzes the possible failure modes of key components during the installation and recovery process, which determines that the Christmas tree and its installation system are the initial agreed level for FMECA analysis, with the lowest agreed level being the components of the Christmas tree and related tools of the installation system ( Fig 6 ). 10.1371/journal.pone.0359208.g006 Fig 6 Hierarchical diagram of the Christmas tree system. The Christmas tree and installation system can be divided into several major parts, including the Installation system, Tree body, Tubing hanger, Top blocker, and Tree cap. Each part can be further divided into more detailed sub-levels according to the installation hierarchy. 4.1.3 Fault diagnosis criteria. The fault criteria should generally be determined based on the specified product functional requirements, corresponding performance parameters, and allowable limits. The fault criteria for the Christmas tree during installation and recovery are as follows: (1) The Christmas tree cannot be installed successfully. (2) The installation of the Christmas tree fails the test and is unable to complete the specified function. (3) The installation and recovery process of the Christmas tree system has caused oil and material losses or impacts on personnel, environment, and other facilities beyond the allowed range. 4.1.4 RPN acceptable standards. The final RPN value is the product of probability of occurrence, severity, and detectability. The size of the RPN value is a measure of the overall risk of failure modes and an important reference for FMECA analysis results. Therefore, referring to relevant materials, the analysis of RPN values is limited as follows: (1) RPN > 100, Preventive or improvement measures must be taken. (2) 100 > RPN > 80, Key considerations, try to avoid as much as possible. (3) RPN < 80, Consider both factors. In addition, measures must also be taken for high severity faults with a severity (S) ≥ 8. 4.1.5 FMECA analysis of installation and recovery process of the Christmas tree body. Conclusions This article focuses on the reliability issues of subsea Christmas trees during installation and operation. The significant contribution of this paper lies in proposing a comprehensive method that appropriately integrates Failure Mode, Effects and Criticality Analysis (FMECA), Fault Tree Analysis (FTA), and numerical simulation. The combination of these methods enables the assessment of equipment reliability from multiple perspectives and constructs a comprehensive framework for risk assessment throughout the entire lifecycle of the equipment. The article systematically identifies key failure states of various components, determines their severity levels, and subsequently verifies these states through quantitative analysis using the OREDA database, which is a highly respected operational data source in the field of marine technology. The research results not only provide an evaluation of system reliability but also offer practical suggestions for the safe installation, operation, and maintenance of the equipment. The specific conclusions are as follows: (1) A survey is conducted on the installation and recovery methods of existing Christmas tree, and the detailed operational steps of the drill pipe installation method are analyzed. Based on the operational experience and installation resources in the South China Sea, the drill pipe installation method is adopted for the installation and recovery of the Christmas tree, with low installation costs, simple operation, and mature technology. (2) The purpose, principle, and steps of failure mode, effects, and hazard analysis are analyzed, and the FMECA analysis flowchart for the Christmas tree is determined based on the severity and hazard level of failure modes of various components during installation and recovery. The evaluation shows that the main failure modes are deformation of the Christmas tree body, leakage of valve groups, leakage or blockage of connecting pipe fittings. The failure reasons are uneven force on both sides of the tree body, loosening of the valve or connecting flange, loosening and leakage of hydraulic joints or channel cracks. The impact on the system is mainly the overall loosening of the Christmas tree, which may cause major accidents and affect production. (3) An analysis is conducted on the loads experienced by the Christmas tree under operational conditions. Based on the OREDA database, statistical analysis is conducted on the failure data and related failure modes of the Christmas tree, providing a foundation for FTA analysis of operational conditions of the Christmas tree. A fault tree of 11 main components is established based on the failure of the Christmas tree during production as the top event, and the probability, importance, and ranking of each basic event are calculated. The calculated average failure probability of the Christmas tree system is 25.82 times per 10 6 hours, therefore its reliability parameter has an average failure interval of 4.42 years, and the overall reliability level meets the requirements. (4) A numerical simulation analysis is conducted on the overall stability of the Christmas tree system under installation and production conditions. The maximum stress ratio of the system under different ocean current conditions is 0.529, and the maximum bending moment is 7292 kN·m, which meets the overall stress and safety production requirements of the equipment. At the same time, the allowable drilling platform offset for normal installation and operation under one-year ocean current conditions is obtained, and the safety operation window for the installation and production conditions of the underwater Christmas tree system is formulated. (5) The installation and recovery of the Christmas tree is complex and technically difficult. It is recommended to conduct sufficient strength and sealing performance tests before installation, select suitable operating conditions, strictly follow the installation and recovery process during the operation, strengthen the technical training of operators, and ensure the smooth installation and recovery of the Christmas trees. The safety operation requirements for the Christmas tree during production are high. It is recommended to regularly conduct systematic maintenance and comprehensive evaluation based on reliability analysis results, and carry out corresponding maintenance or repair work.
