 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 HyET, established in 2008, is a small business developing electro-chemical hydrogen
 compressors. HyET’s goals are for a low-cost, efficient, high-pressure compressor ready for
 market and installation into hydrogen infrastructure stations servicing mobile fuel cell
 applications. NLR has been working on hydrogen infrastructure research for over a decade and
 includes electrolyzer production via electrolysis, storage, compression, dispensing, safety, and
 performance validation. Accelerating technologies into the marketplace is one goal of NLR’s
 hydrogen research. Therefore, HyET and NLR have teamed up to provide one year of support for
 HyET’s modeling and small-scale experiments optimization efforts for HyET’s next-generation
 electro-chemical compressor.

 Summary of Research Results:

 CRADA (CRD-17-00719) with HyET Hydrogen USA LLC was an opportunity for collaboration
 between HyET and the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR). For completion of this project
 a staff member from NLR spent a period of 12 months on assignment working directly with
 HyET’s research staff and engineers. NLR’s engineer was immersed in an industry environment
 while completing testing and supporting the development of HyET’s electrochemical hydrogen
 compressor (EHC) technology. This collaboration with the US National Laboratory system was
 designed to push HyET’s state of the art EHC technology toward maturity. The CRADA with
 HyET Hydrogen USA LLC had three subtasks. (1.1) Complete build-out, install, and deploy a
 new EHC test system, (1.2) provide data for model/simulation validation, measure experimental
 parameters for model input, and sub-scale testing of representative geometry under operating
 conditions, and (1.3) transfer results from sub-scale to full scale up.

 Task 1: One-year staff loan

 In April 2018, Kevin Hartmann will relocate to Berkeley, CA, and specifically, Lawrence
 Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL), the current location of HyET’s U.S. based operations.

 The results of these experiments will be used to determine the most promising design for a novel

 Task 1 Results:

 Prior to the initiation of this CRADA between HyET and NLR installation of an EHC test system
 was under way at the Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL). Following the
 deployment of an NLR staff member to Berkeley, California installation was completed, and a
 training period began. Members of HyET’s research team trained the NLR researcher on HyET’s
 EHC system. This training included completion of the system set up and shakedown. Part of the
 training was assembly of membrane electrode assemblies, compressor flow channel assembly,
 and HyET’s test protocols and testing procedures. The initial assembly of HyET’s HCS 10 EHC
 at LBNL was completed as part of this training. The HCS 10 compressor is a benchtop
 compressor that is used to validate system performance and functionality before scaleup to the
 HCS 100 or other public refueling sized system. Membrane Electrode Assemblies (MEA) for the
 test system were assembled on site at LBNL. Final assembly consisted of correctly aligning the
 MEA with the flow channels and torqueing down the assembly bolts. Insulation was added to the
 balance of plants and along inlet and outlet lines of the compressor to assist with control of
 anode and cathode gas temperature and relative humidity. The control system, including data
 logging and safety shutdowns, was checked for functionality. Following training and shakedown
 of controls, the HCS 10 compressor was deemed functional and ready for validation testing by
 HyET, LBNL, and NLR personnel. HyET’s software engineers made software and control
 hardware revisions to improve the functionality of the compressor control system over the
 duration of the project.

 3

 Data produced over the duration of the 12-month project performance period was provided to
 HyET Hydrogen USA LLC to be used for validation of the models and simulations being
 developed. Balance of plant data including anode and cathode temperature, dewpoint, flow rate,
 pressure, and stack temperature was captured for each test on HyET’s proprietary data logging
 and control system. Electrochemical performance data was collected through a Biologic
 potentiostat. Electrochemical testing included polarization curves, impedance spectroscopy, and
 measuring the electrochemically active surface area. A complete test sequence including each of
 these electrochemical tests was completed to determine MEA performance. A single complete
 test series took between 4 and 10 hours. The test duration was dependent upon the parameters of
 interest. A MATLAB script was written to link the balance of plant and electrochemical data.
 This MATLAB script enabled rapid data workup and visualization of compressor performance.
 Test data was collected and shared for each test completed. Over the duration of the project
 improvements were made to control software and hardware. These upgrades enabled more
 streamlined long-term testing and improved data aquisition.

 The main goal of the bench top testing with the HCS 10 system was model validation and
 development. LBNL and HyET have been developing an EHC model that that can be used to
 predict performance under variable gas conditions, geometries, and MEA material composition.
 Results of experimental testing with varying gas conditions, geometries, and materials were
 utilized to guide experimental performance and validate model development. Variations in gas
 conditions were the most changed parameter. Changing the gas conditions can be completed
 quickly for head-to-head performance comparison. Changes in the gas pressure, relative
 humidity, and stack temperature are critical in understanding performance of EHCs. Changing
 these critical operating conditions provided sensitivity data to identify optimal operating regimes.
 Experimental performance of the EHC was compared to modeled data. The differences between
 the experimental and simulated results clearly showed how well the model fit the experimental
 data and helped identify where further improvement in the model was needed.

 Representative subscale testing was also completed to refine model input parameters. Sub-
 system samples of MEA components were prepared at LBNL and tested in-house or sent out for
 external testing. The focus of these sub-scale tests was to quantify membrane conductivity, water
 diffusivity, and water permeability under compressor test conditions. Quantifying the water
 transport properties through the MEA is a key parameter of EHC that is closely tied to the stack
 operating conditions. At LBNL membrane conductivity testing was completed as well as
 visualization of the gas diffusion layers and gas diffusion electrodes. LBNL researchers
 completed the analysis on samples provided by the NLR researcher. MEA component samples
 were prepared and sent out for third party testing to confirm results found though visualization.
 In the second half of the project HyET developed further capabilities in the Netherlands to more
 extensively test water transport through their system geometry. Testing completed by HyET was
 able to be compared with more theoretical results produced at LBNL.

 A full-scale EHC was not deployed at LBNL but HyET researchers were able to validate the
 small scale experiments on a full-scale system. Materials from the same production batches were
 shared between facilities allow direct comparison of the EHC performance at different scales.
 This allowed small scale testing to inform the selection of parameters and materials for large
 scale longer duration experiments. This practical decision allowed the project team to put time
 into experimental work as compared to system build out.

 Task 2: Testing HyET’s 10 kg/day Electrochemical Hydrogen Compressor at NLR

 NLR engineers will work with HyET to develop a conceptual design for integration of HyET’s
 10 kg/day electrochemical hydrogen compressor within NLR’s Hydrogen Infrastructure Testing
 Research Facility (HITRF). This conceptual test interface design will identify potential test stand
 locations and any required ESIF facility upgrades. The conceptual design requires information
 from HyET regarding the equipment interfaces and operating conditions. The conceptual test
 interface design will be utilized in preparation of NLR’s process hazards analysis of the research
 system. The conceptual test interface design will include equipment location, layout, and NLR to
 equipment interfaces (mechanical, electrical, controls, and data). This conceptual test interface
 design will include safety envelope, safety requirements, and identify potential safety gaps in the
 HyET system.

 Task 2 Results:

 Task 2 could not be completed due to reprioritization of objectives by the partner and unforeseen
 challenges posed by the COVID-19 pandemic, which provided significant uncertainty and
 disrupted operations. The task two scope was to develop a conceptual design for integration of
 HyET’s electrochemical compression technology in to NLR’s Hydrogen Infrastrucutre Testing
 and Research Facility (HITRF). During the height of the pandemic, restrictions and uncertainties
 made it impractical to proceed with the planned work.

 Conclusion:

 Over a period of 12 months an NLR researcher worked with HyET Hydrogen USA LLC. This
 opportunity for collaboration allowed NLR to support HyET with testing and development of
 their EHC technology. Testing done by NLR at LBNL has helped move HyET toward full
 characterization of their technology. More testing needs to be done to continue to push EHC
 technology towards maturity. This collaboration also gave NLR an opportunity to get industry
 like experience working on developing technologies. This experience will now be applied to
 NLR’s research in Golden, Colorado as NLR continues to help strengthen and push technology
 in the hydrogen industry.
