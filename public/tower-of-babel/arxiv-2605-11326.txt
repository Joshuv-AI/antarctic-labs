Almost Disjointness Principles and Q Q -Space Cardinals
Vinicius de Oliveira Rodrigues

Abstract
Banakh and Bazylevych introduced separation-axiom variants 𝔮 i \mathfrak{q}_{i} , for i = 1 , 2 , 2 ⁤ 1 2 i=1,2,2\frac{1}{2} , of the cardinal 𝔮 \mathfrak{q} , together with a cardinal 𝔞 ​ 𝔡 ​ 𝔭 \mathfrak{adp} lying between 𝔡 ​ 𝔭 \mathfrak{dp} and 𝔞 ​ 𝔭 \mathfrak{ap} . They asked whether 𝔞 ​ 𝔡 ​ 𝔭 \mathfrak{adp} coincides with either of these two cardinals. We prove in ZFC that 𝔞 ​ 𝔡 ​ 𝔭 = 𝔡 ​ 𝔭 \mathfrak{adp}=\mathfrak{dp} . We define a dual variant 𝔞 ​ 𝔡 ​ 𝔭 2 \mathfrak{adp}_{2} and show that 𝔞 ​ 𝔡 ​ 𝔭 2 = 𝔞 ​ 𝔭 \mathfrak{adp}_{2}=\mathfrak{ap} . We further study the relation between 𝔞 ​ 𝔭 \mathfrak{ap} and the weakened Q Q -space cardinals. We introduce a tree analogue 𝔞 ​ 𝔱 \mathfrak{at} of 𝔞 ​ 𝔭 \mathfrak{ap} and prove 𝔮 1 ≤ 𝔞 ​ 𝔱 ≤ 𝔮 2 ⁤ 1 2 \mathfrak{q}_{1}\leq\mathfrak{at}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}} , hence 𝔞 ​ 𝔭 ≤ 𝔮 2 ⁤ 1 2 \mathfrak{ap}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}} . Assuming the Generalized Continuum Hypothesis, we construct ccc forcing extensions with 𝔞 ​ 𝔭 = ω 1 < 𝔞 ​ 𝔱 = 𝔮 2 ⁤ 1 2 = 𝔠 \mathfrak{ap}=\omega_{1}<\mathfrak{at}=\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}}=\mathfrak{c} , so 𝔞 ​ 𝔭 < 𝔞 ​ 𝔱 \mathfrak{ap}<\mathfrak{at} is consistent with ZFC. Introduction

Cardinal characteristics of the continuum are cardinals between
\aleph_{1}
,
the first uncountable cardinal, and
\mathfrak{c}
, the cardinality of the
continuum. They measure combinatorial properties of the real numbers; see

[
3
]
for background.

The symbol
\omega
denotes the set of natural numbers, which includes
0
and is identified with the first infinite ordinal.

The cardinal characteristics
\mathfrak{ap}
and
\mathfrak{dp}
, both related
to the pseudointersection number
\mathfrak{p}
, were studied by Brendle in

[
4
]
. We recall the relevant definitions.

•

For a set
S
,
[S]^{<\omega}
denotes the collection of all finite subsets of
S
, and
[S]^{\omega}
denotes the collection of all countably infinite subsets of
S
.

•

Sets
a,b\in[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}
are orthogonal, written
a\perp b
, if
a\cap b
is finite.

•

Collections
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}
are orthogonal, written
\mathcal{A}\perp\mathcal{B}
, if
a\perp b
for every
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
b\in\mathcal{B}
.

•

If
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}
, then
\mathcal{A}
is weakly separated from
\mathcal{B}
if there exists
X\subseteq\omega
such that
a\not\perp X
for every
a\in\mathcal{A}
, and
b\perp X
for every
b\in\mathcal{B}
. In this case, we say that
X
weakly separates
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
.

•

\mathcal{A}\subseteq[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}
is an almost disjoint family if
a\perp b
for every two distinct
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
.

Our convention regarding the order of weak separation follows Banakh and Bazylevych
[
1
]
. It is the reverse of Brendle’s convention for ordered pairs
[
4
]
.

Since weak separation is not symmetric, the order of the two families matters in
the following definitions.

•

\mathfrak{dp}
is the least cardinal
\kappa
for which there exist
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}
such that
\mathcal{A}\perp\mathcal{B}
,
|\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}|=\kappa
, and
\mathcal{A}
is not weakly separated from
\mathcal{B}
.

•

\mathfrak{ap}
is the least cardinal
\kappa
for which there exist
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}
such that
\mathcal{A}\perp\mathcal{B}
,
|\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}|=\kappa
,
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
is an almost disjoint family, and
\mathcal{A}
is not weakly separated from
\mathcal{B}
.

It is immediate that
\mathfrak{dp}\leq\mathfrak{ap}
.
This inequality can be strict: Brendle showed by forcing that

\mathfrak{dp}<\mathfrak{ap}
is consistent
[
4
]
.
Both cardinals are greater than or equal to
\mathfrak{p}
, and consistently
strictly greater than
\mathfrak{p}

[
4
]
. Here
\mathfrak{p}
is the least
cardinality of a centered family of infinite subsets of
\omega
with no
pseudointersection; that is, every nonempty finite subfamily has infinite
intersection, but there is no single infinite set
P
such that

P\setminus A
is finite for every member
A
of the family.

If
X
is a topological space and
Y\subseteq X
, we say that
Y
is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
if there are open subsets
(U_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of
X
such that
Y=\bigcap_{n<\omega}U_{n}
.
Dually,
Y
is an
F_{\sigma}
subset of
X
if it is a countable union of closed subsets of
X
.
We say that
X
has a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal if the set
\Delta_{X}=\{(x,x):x\in X\}
is a
G_{\delta}
as a subset of
X^{2}
.

A
Q
-set is classically defined as an uncountable set of reals all of whose subsets are

G_{\delta}
sets in the subspace topology (equivalently, all of its subsets are

F_{\sigma}
sets in the subspace topology)
[
8
,
5
]
. The existence of
Q
-sets is independent of the Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory with the axiom of choice (ZFC)
[
5
,
8
]
. Indeed, if
there is a
Q
-set, then it has at least
2^{\aleph_{1}}
subsets, all realized as
traces of
G_{\delta}
subsets of the reals. Since there are only
\mathfrak{c}

many
G_{\delta}
subsets of the reals, no
Q
-set can exist under the Continuum
Hypothesis (CH). On the other hand, every set of reals of cardinality less than

\mathfrak{ap}
has all of its subsets
G_{\delta}
in the subspace topology

[
4
]
; hence, if

\aleph_{1}<\mathfrak{ap}=\mathfrak{c}
, as happens for example under Martin’s
Axiom together with the negation of CH
[
3
]
, then
Q
-sets
exist.

Q
-sets are also related to the separable case of the Normal Moore Space
Conjecture: a non-metrizable separable normal Moore space exists if and only if
there is a
Q
-set
[
6
]
.

The smallest cardinality of a non-
Q
-set is denoted by
\mathfrak{q}

[
8
,
4
]
.
More generally, a
Q
-space is a topological space in which every subset is a

G_{\delta}
set. In this terminology,
\mathfrak{q}
is the least cardinality of
a metrizable separable non-
Q
-space, equivalently, of a regular second-countable
non-
Q
-space.

These notions have been studied in more general settings. There are two natural
directions to pursue: weaken regularity to weaker separation axioms, or weaken
second countability. In
[
1
]
, Banakh and Bazylevych followed the
first direction and defined the cardinals
\mathfrak{q}_{1}
,
\mathfrak{q}_{2}
and

\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}}
by considering second-countable non-
Q
-spaces
which are, respectively,
T_{1}
, Hausdorff and Urysohn. Recall that a topological
space is
T_{1}
if every singleton is closed; Hausdorff, or
T_{2}
, if every two
distinct points have disjoint neighborhoods; and Urysohn, or

T_{2\frac{1}{2}}
, if every two distinct points have disjoint closed
neighborhoods.

Banakh and Bazylevych also introduced a cardinal
\mathfrak{adp}
, which lies
between
\mathfrak{dp}
and
\mathfrak{ap}
and is useful in the study of

Q
-spaces.

Definition 1
.

\mathfrak{adp}
is the least cardinal
\kappa
for which there exist
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}
such that
\mathcal{A}\perp\mathcal{B}
,
|\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}|=\kappa
,
\mathcal{A}
is an almost disjoint family, and
\mathcal{A}
is not weakly separated from
\mathcal{B}
.

They asked the following.

Problem 2

(
[
1
, Problem 12]
)
.

Is
\mathfrak{adp}=\mathfrak{dp}
? Is
\mathfrak{adp}=\mathfrak{ap}
?

In Section
2
, we answer the first part by proving

\mathfrak{adp}=\mathfrak{dp}
in ZFC. Together with the consistency of

\mathfrak{dp}<\mathfrak{ap}
, this shows that

\mathfrak{adp}=\mathfrak{ap}
is not provable in ZFC.

The asymmetry of weak separation also suggests asking what happens if the
almost disjointness requirement in the definition of
\mathfrak{adp}
is imposed
on
\mathcal{B}
instead of on
\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 3
.

\mathfrak{adp}_{2}
is the least cardinal
\kappa
for which there exist
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}
such that
\mathcal{A}\perp\mathcal{B}
,
|\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}|=\kappa
,
\mathcal{B}
is an almost disjoint family, and
\mathcal{A}
is not weakly separated from
\mathcal{B}
.

Using the same technique, we show that
\mathfrak{adp}_{2}=\mathfrak{ap}
.
Thus, in every model in which
\mathfrak{dp}<\mathfrak{ap}
, this modified
cardinal is distinct from
\mathfrak{dp}
.

Banakh and Bazylevych also asked:

Problem 4

(
[
1
, Problem 11]
)
.

Is
\mathfrak{ap}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2}
?

This problem remains open, but we prove several related bounds.
In Section
3
, we introduce a tree analogue of

\mathfrak{ap}
, denoted by
\mathfrak{at}
. This cardinal is obtained by
replacing almost disjoint families of subsets of
\omega
with almost
disjoint families of infinite subtrees of an
\omega
-tree. It satisfies

\mathfrak{ap}\leq\mathfrak{at}
, and we prove

\mathfrak{q}_{1}\leq\mathfrak{at}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}}.

In particular, this gives the ZFC bound

\mathfrak{ap}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}}.

The proof of the Urysohn bound shows that the extra separation available in
Urysohn spaces can be encoded by almost disjoint subtrees. In
Section
4
, we isolate another form of this extra
structure by considering Hausdorff spaces with
G_{\delta}
-diagonals. Let

\mathfrak{q}_{2\Delta}
be the least cardinality of a second-countable
Hausdorff non-
Q
-space with a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal. We prove

\mathfrak{ap}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2\Delta}.

Thus, every second-countable Hausdorff space of cardinality
<\mathfrak{ap}

with a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal is a
Q
-space. We also record that every
second-countable Urysohn space has a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal, and that every
second-countable
T_{i}
space of cardinality
<\mathfrak{q}_{i}
, for

i\in\{1,2\}
, has a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal.

Finally, in Section
5
, we show that the tree cardinal

\mathfrak{at}
is not simply another presentation of
\mathfrak{ap}
.
Assuming the Generalized Continuum Hypothesis (GCH), for every regular cardinal
\lambda>\omega_{1}
we construct a ccc
forcing extension in which

\mathfrak{ap}=\omega_{1}<\mathfrak{at}=\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}}=\mathfrak{c}=\lambda.

In particular,
\mathfrak{ap}<\mathfrak{at}
is consistent.
The construction is based on Brendle’s model for
\mathfrak{ap}<\mathfrak{q}

and uses forcing notions associated with almost disjoint families of subtrees.

2
On
\mathfrak{adp}
and
\mathfrak{adp}_{2}

We will need the following result, which is a particular case of
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
.
For completeness, we sketch a direct proof for this specific case. Recall that almost disjoint families of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
exist: for instance, for each
x\in 2^{\omega}
, consider
a_{x}=\{x\restriction n:n\in\omega\}
.
Then
\{a_{x}:x\in 2^{\omega}\}
is an almost disjoint family over the infinite countable set of all finite binary sequences.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\lambda<\mathfrak{c}
and
(a_{\alpha}:\alpha<\lambda)
be a family in
[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}
.
Then there exists a family
(a^{\prime}_{\alpha}:\alpha<\lambda)
such that
a^{\prime}_{\alpha}\in[a_{\alpha}]^{\aleph_{0}}
for every
\alpha<\lambda
, and
a^{\prime}_{\alpha}\perp a^{\prime}_{\beta}
whenever
\alpha\neq\beta
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\lambda
is infinite.

For each
\alpha<\lambda
, let
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=(b^{\alpha}_{\xi}:\xi<\lambda^{+})
be an injective indexing of an almost disjoint family of cardinality
\lambda^{+}
over the infinite countable set
a_{\alpha}
.

Let
F:\lambda\rightarrow\lambda
be such that
F(\alpha)=\min\{\beta\leq\alpha:|\{\xi<\lambda^{+}:b^{\beta}_{\xi}\not\perp a_{\alpha}\}|=\lambda^{+}\}
. Let
Y=\bigcup_{\alpha,\beta<\lambda}\{\xi<\lambda^{+}:b_{\xi}^{\beta}\not\perp a_{\alpha}\,\wedge\,F(\alpha)>\beta\}
. By the minimality of
F(\alpha)
, the regularity of
\lambda^{+}
, and the fact that
\lambda
is infinite,
|Y|\leq\lambda
.

For each
\alpha<\lambda
, let
X_{\alpha}=F^{-1}[\{\alpha\}]
. Recursively define, for
\alpha<\lambda
, an injective
G_{\alpha}:X_{\alpha}\rightarrow\lambda^{+}
and
(a_{\delta}^{\prime}:\delta\in X_{\alpha})
so that
G_{\alpha}(\delta)\in\{\xi<\lambda^{+}:b_{\xi}^{\alpha}\not\perp a_{\delta}\}\setminus Y
and
a_{\delta}^{\prime}=a_{\delta}\cap b^{\alpha}_{G_{\alpha}(\delta)}
.
This is possible because, for each
\delta\in X_{\alpha}
, the set
\{\xi<\lambda^{+}:b_{\xi}^{\alpha}\not\perp a_{\delta}\}
has size
\lambda^{+}
, while at each stage at most
\lambda
indices have been excluded.
If
\delta,\gamma\in X_{\alpha}
are distinct, then the injectivity of
G_{\alpha}
and the almost disjointness of
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
give
a^{\prime}_{\delta}\perp a^{\prime}_{\gamma}
.
If
\delta\in X_{\alpha}
,
\gamma\in X_{\beta}
, and
\alpha<\beta
, then
G_{\alpha}(\delta)\notin Y
implies
b^{\alpha}_{G_{\alpha}(\delta)}\perp a_{\gamma}
, and hence
a^{\prime}_{\delta}\perp a^{\prime}_{\gamma}
; the case
\beta<\alpha
is symmetric.
∎

First, we settle Question 12 of
[

]
.

Theorem 6
.

In ZFC,

\mathfrak{dp}=\mathfrak{adp}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathfrak{dp}\leq\mathfrak{adp}
. For the converse, assume
\kappa<\mathfrak{adp}
.
We show that
\kappa<\mathfrak{dp}
.

Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}
be such that
\mathcal{A}\perp\mathcal{B}
and
|\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}|\leq\kappa
.
We show that
\mathcal{A}
is weakly separated from
\mathcal{B}
.
Let
\lambda=|\mathcal{A}|\leq\kappa
and enumerate it as
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{\alpha}:\alpha<\lambda\}
.

Since
\mathfrak{adp}\leq\mathfrak{ap}\leq\mathfrak{c}
, we have

\lambda\leq\kappa<\mathfrak{adp}\leq\mathfrak{c}
.
Thus, by Theorem
5
, there exists
(a^{\prime}_{\alpha}:\alpha<\lambda)
such that
a^{\prime}_{\alpha}\subseteq a_{\alpha}
and for every two distinct

\alpha,\beta<\lambda
,
a^{\prime}_{\alpha}\perp a^{\prime}_{\beta}
. Let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\{a^{\prime}_{\alpha}:\alpha<\lambda\}
.
Then
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\perp\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is an almost disjoint family and

|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{B}|\leq\kappa<\mathfrak{adp}
, so
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is weakly separated from
\mathcal{B}
.

Let
X\subseteq\omega
be such that
a^{\prime}_{\alpha}\not\perp X
for every
\alpha<\lambda
, and
b\perp X
for every
b\in\mathcal{B}
.
Since
a^{\prime}_{\alpha}\subseteq a_{\alpha}
, we have
a^{\prime}_{\alpha}\cap X\subseteq a_{\alpha}\cap X
for every
\alpha<\lambda
.
Thus,
a_{\alpha}\not\perp X
for every
\alpha<\lambda
.
Therefore,
X
weakly separates
\mathcal{A}
from
\mathcal{B}
.
∎

As discussed in the introduction, it is natural to ask whether the asymmetric version of
\mathfrak{adp}
, which we named
\mathfrak{adp}_{2}
, is also equal to
\mathfrak{dp}
.
We show that it is equal to the well-studied cardinal
\mathfrak{ap}
.
Consequently, in every model in which
\mathfrak{dp}<\mathfrak{ap}
, this variant is distinct from
\mathfrak{dp}
.

Theorem 7
.

In ZFC,
\mathfrak{adp}_{2}=\mathfrak{ap}
.

It is clear that
\mathfrak{adp}_{2}\leq\mathfrak{ap}
. For the converse, assume
\kappa<\mathfrak{ap}
.
We show that
\kappa<\mathfrak{adp}_{2}
.

Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}
be such that
\mathcal{A}\perp\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{B}
is an almost disjoint family and
|\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}|\leq\kappa
.
We show that
\mathcal{A}
is weakly separated from
\mathcal{B}
.
Let
\lambda=|\mathcal{A}|\leq\kappa
and enumerate it as
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{\alpha}:\alpha<\lambda\}
.

Since
\mathfrak{ap}\leq\mathfrak{c}

\lambda\leq\kappa<\mathfrak{ap}\leq\mathfrak{c}
.
Thus, by Theorem

, there exists
(a^{\prime}_{\alpha}:\alpha<\lambda)

a^{\prime}_{\alpha}\subseteq a_{\alpha}
and for every two distinct

,
a^{\prime}_{\alpha}\perp a^{\prime}_{\beta}

,
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{B}

, and

.

.

3
On almost disjointness and Urysohn
Q
-spaces

As discussed in the introduction, the cardinal
\mathfrak{q}
is the least cardinality of a non-
Q
-set of reals. For
f,g\in\omega^{\omega}
, write
f\leq^{*}g
if
f(n)\leq g(n)
for all but finitely many
n\in\omega
. It is known that
\mathfrak{p}\leq\mathfrak{q}\leq\mathfrak{b}
, where
\mathfrak{b}
is the bounding number, namely the least cardinality of a family in
\omega^{\omega}
that is not bounded by any single function with respect to
\leq^{*}

[
8
]
.

In
[
1
]
, Banakh and Bazylevych introduced the following separation-axiom variants of
\mathfrak{q}
(with the classical invariant denoted by
\mathfrak{q}_{0}
in their notation).

Definition 8
.

The cardinal
\mathfrak{q}_{1}
is the least cardinal
\kappa
for which there exists a second-countable,
T_{1}
, non-
Q
-space of cardinality
\kappa
.

The cardinal
\mathfrak{q}_{2}
is the least cardinal
\kappa
for which there exists a second-countable, Hausdorff, non-
Q
-space of cardinality
\kappa
.

The cardinal
\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}}
is the least cardinal
\kappa
for which there exists a second-countable, Urysohn, non-
Q
-space of cardinality
\kappa
.

They proved that
\mathfrak{adp}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{1}
. It is also immediate from the separation axioms that
\mathfrak{q}_{1}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}}\leq\mathfrak{q}
; together with
\mathfrak{q}\leq\mathfrak{b}
, this gives, by Theorem
6
,

\mathfrak{p}\leq\mathfrak{dp}=\mathfrak{adp}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{1}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}}\leq\mathfrak{q}\leq\mathfrak{b}.

Banakh and Bazylevych asked whether any of these inequalities between different versions of
\mathfrak{q}
can be strict.
They also proved that every submetrizable space, that is, every space admitting a coarser metrizable topology, of cardinality less than
\mathfrak{q}
is a
Q
-space.
Thus, any separation of these cardinals from
\mathfrak{q}
must use non-submetrizable examples
[
1
]
.

More specifically, they asked whether
\mathfrak{ap}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2}
.
The relation between
\mathfrak{ap}
and these new cardinals was left open, and it is natural to ask whether
\mathfrak{ap}
is less than or equal to any of them.
We show in this section that
\mathfrak{ap}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}}
.

The first step is to extract, from the Urysohn separation axiom, a countable base arranged in finite levels.
The levels still refine neighborhoods of each point, but distinct points can occur together in closures only finitely often.

Lemma 9
.

Let
X
be a second-countable Urysohn space.
Then
X
has a countable base
\mathcal{B}
and a sequence
(\mathcal{B}_{n})_{n<\omega}
such that:

(i)

\mathcal{B}=\bigcup_{n<\omega}\mathcal{B}_{n}
;

(ii)

for every
n<\omega
,
\mathcal{B}_{n}
is finite;

(iii)

for every two distinct points
x,y\in X
, the set
\{n\in\omega:\exists B\in\mathcal{B}_{n}\,(x\in\overline{B}\text{ and }y\in\overline{B})\}
is finite;

(iv)

for every
x\in X
and every open neighborhood
O
of
x
, there is some
m<\omega
such that for every
n\geq m
there is some
B\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
such that
x\in B\subseteq O
(so, in particular,
\mathcal{B}
is a base for
X
);

(v)

for every
n<\omega
and
x\in X
, there is some
B\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
such that
x\in B
(that is,
\mathcal{B}_{n}
covers
X
).

Proof.

Since
X
is second-countable,
X^{2}\setminus\Delta
is Lindelöf.
Since
X
is Urysohn, there exists a sequence of pairs of open subsets of
X
,
(U_{k},V_{k})_{k<\omega}
,
such that

X^{2}\setminus\Delta\subseteq\bigcup_{k<\omega}U_{k}\times V_{k}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\forall k<\omega\,\overline{U_{k}}\cap\overline{V_{k}}=\emptyset.

For each
k<\omega
, let
C_{k}^{0}=X\setminus\overline{U_{k}}
and
C_{k}^{1}=X\setminus\overline{V_{k}}
.
Since
\overline{U_{k}}\cap\overline{V_{k}}=\emptyset
, the pair
\{C_{k}^{0},C_{k}^{1}\}
is an open cover of
X
.

Fix a countable base
(G_{m})_{m<\omega}
for
X
with
G_{0}=X
.
For each
n<\omega
, define

\mathcal{B}_{n}=\left\{G_{m}\cap\bigcap_{k\leq n}C_{k}^{\varepsilon(k)}:m\leq n,\ \varepsilon\in 2^{n+1}\right\}.

Each
\mathcal{B}_{n}
is a finite collection of open sets.
Let
\mathcal{B}=\bigcup_{n<\omega}\mathcal{B}_{n}
.
We first verify (iv).
Let
x\in X
, and let

O\subseteq X
be open with
x\in O
.
Choose
m<\omega
such that

x\in G_{m}\subseteq O
.
Fix
n\geq m
.
For every
k\leq n
, choose
\varepsilon(k)\in\{0,1\}
such that

x\in C_{k}^{\varepsilon(k)}
. Then

x\in G_{m}\cap\bigcap_{k\leq n}C_{k}^{\varepsilon(k)}\subseteq G_{m}\subseteq O,

and this set belongs to
\mathcal{B}_{n}
.
Thus, (iv) holds.
The same argument with
G_{0}=X
shows that (v) holds.

It remains to verify (iii).
Let
x\neq y
. Choose

k<\omega
such that
(x,y)\in U_{k}\times V_{k}
.
Then

x\notin\overline{C_{k}^{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad y\notin\overline{C_{k}^{1}}.

Now let
n>k
and
B\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
. By construction, either

B\subseteq C_{k}^{0}
or
B\subseteq C_{k}^{1}
. In the first case,

x\notin\overline{B}
; in the second case,
y\notin\overline{B}
. Hence no
member of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
, for
n>k
, has both

and
y
in its closure.

Therefore,

\{n\in\omega:\exists B\in\mathcal{B}_{n}\,(x\in\overline{B}\text{ and }y\in\overline{B})\}\subseteq\{0,\ldots,k\},

which is finite.
∎

The preceding lemma turns Urysohn separation into a countable combinatorial object.
This motivates the following tree variant of
\mathfrak{ap}
, which keeps track of weak separation for almost disjoint subtrees rather than for almost disjoint subsets of
\mathbb{N}
.

In this paper, an
\omega
-tree is a rooted tree of height
\omega
with finite levels, and subtrees are assumed to be nonempty and downward closed. For
m<\omega
, write

T_{m}=\{t\in T:\htt(t)=m\}\quad\text{and}\quad T_{<m}=\{t\in T:\htt(t)<m\}.

Two subtrees
S,R
of
T
are almost disjoint if
|S\cap R|<\omega
.
A family of subtrees of
T
is almost disjoint if any two distinct members are almost disjoint.
An infinite subtree means a subtree with infinitely many nodes.

Recall that a branch of a tree
T
is a maximal linearly ordered subset of
T
.

Definition 10
.

Let
\mathfrak{at}
be the least cardinal
\kappa
for which there is an

\omega
-tree
T
, an almost disjoint family
\mathcal{X}
of infinite subtrees of
T
with
|\mathcal{X}|\leq\kappa
, and a subcollection

\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
such that
\mathcal{Y}
cannot be weakly
separated from
\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{Y}
.
Here a set
D\subseteq T

weakly separates
(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{Y})
if

\forall Y\in\mathcal{Y}\ |D\cap Y|=\omega,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\forall Z\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{Y}\ |D\cap Z|<\omega.

It follows immediately that
\mathfrak{ap}\leq\mathfrak{at}
, since every
\omega
-tree is countably infinite and, in the definition of
\mathfrak{ap}
,
\omega
can be replaced by any infinite countable set.

The next theorem gives the lower comparison with
\mathfrak{q}_{1}
.
The idea is to topologize an almost disjoint family of branches and subtrees by declaring finite containment to be basic open information.

Theorem 11
.

\mathfrak{q}_{1}\leq\mathfrak{at}
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa
be a cardinal such that
\kappa<\mathfrak{q}_{1}
.
We show that
\kappa<\mathfrak{at}
.
Since
\mathfrak{q}_{1}\leq\mathfrak{q}\leq\mathfrak{b}
, it follows that

\kappa<\mathfrak{b}.

Let
T
be an
\omega
-tree, and let
\mathcal{X}
be an almost disjoint
family of infinite subtrees of
T
with
|\mathcal{X}|\leq\kappa
.
Fix
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and let
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{Y}
.

For each
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
, choose an infinite branch
b_{Y}\subseteq Y
, which is possible by König’s lemma. Put

H=\{b_{Y}:Y\in\mathcal{Y}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad X=H\cup\mathcal{Z}.

Thus,
X\subseteq[T]^{\omega}
, and
X
is an almost disjoint family of infinite subsets of
T
with
|X|\leq\kappa<\mathfrak{q}_{1}
.

For each finite
F\subseteq T
, let
[F]=\{A\in X:F\subseteq A\}
.
The family

\mathcal{U}=\{[F]:F\in[T]^{<\omega}\}

is a base for a topology on
X
, since
[F]\cap[G]=[F\cup G]
for every
F,G\in[T]^{<\omega}
.
Thus,
X
is second-countable because
T
is countable.
Moreover,
X
is
T_{1}
: let
A,A^{\prime}\in X
be distinct. Since
X
is
almost disjoint and
A
is infinite, choose
t\in A\setminus A^{\prime}
.
Then
[\{t\}]
is an open set containing
A
and not containing
A^{\prime}
.
Interchanging
A
and
A^{\prime}
, we obtain an open set containing
A^{\prime}
and not
containing
A
.

By the definition of
\mathfrak{q}_{1}
,
X
is a
Q
-space. In particular,
H
is a

G_{\delta}
subset of
X
.

Choose open sets
U_{n}\subseteq X
such that

H=\bigcap_{n<\omega}U_{n}.

Replacing
U_{n}
by
\bigcap_{i\leq n}U_{i}
, we may assume that
U_{n+1}\subseteq U_{n}
for every
n<\omega
.

Fix
b\in H
and
n<\omega
. Since
b\in U_{n}
and
U_{n}
is open, there is
a finite set
F\subseteq b
such that
b\in[F]\subseteq U_{n}
.
As
b
is a branch, there is
m<\omega
such that, whenever
t\in b
and

\htt(t)\geq m
, the node
t
lies above every member of

F
. Since every element of
X
is downward closed, any
A\in X
with

t\in A
must contain
F
. Hence

[\{t\}]\subseteq[F]\subseteq U_{n}.

Define
f_{b}\in\omega^{\omega}
by letting
f_{b}(n)
be the least
m<\omega

such that

\forall t\in b\,\bigl(\htt(t)\geq m\Rightarrow[\{t\}]\subseteq U_{n}\bigr).

As
|\{f_{b}:b\in H\}|\leq\kappa<\mathfrak{b}
, the family
\{f_{b}:b\in H\}
is bounded in
\omega^{\omega}
with respect to
\leq^{*}
.
Let
g\in\omega^{\omega}
be such that
f_{b}\leq^{*}g
for every
b\in H
.
Increasing
g
pointwise if necessary, we may assume that
g
is strictly increasing.
Define

D=\{t\in T:\exists n<\omega\,(\htt(t)=g(n)\text{ and }[\{t\}]\subseteq U_{n})\}.

We show that
D
weakly separates
\mathcal{Y}
from
\mathcal{Z}
.

First fix
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
, and let
b=b_{Y}
. For all but finitely many

n<\omega
,
f_{b}(n)\leq g(n)
.
Let
t_{n}
be the unique node of
b
on level
g(n)
. By the definition of

f_{b}(n)
, for all but finitely many
n
we have
[\{t_{n}\}]\subseteq U_{n}
.

Thus,
t_{n}\in D\cap b
for infinitely many
n
. Since
g
is strictly
increasing, these nodes are distinct.
Therefore
|D\cap b|=\omega
.
As
b=b_{Y}\subseteq Y
, it follows that
|D\cap Y|=\omega
.

Now fix
Z\in\mathcal{Z}
.
Since
Z\notin H
and
H=\bigcap_{n<\omega}U_{n}
, there is
N<\omega
such that
Z\notin U_{N}
.
As the sequence
(U_{n})_{n<\omega}
is decreasing, we have
Z\notin U_{n}
for every
n\geq N
.

Suppose that
t\in Z\cap D
and
\htt(t)=g(n)
for some

n\geq N
.
By the definition of
D
,
[\{t\}]\subseteq U_{n}
.
But
t\in Z
, so
Z\in[\{t\}]
, and hence
Z\in U_{n}
, a contradiction.
Therefore

Z\cap D\subseteq\bigcup_{n<N}T_{g(n)}.

Each level of
T
is finite, so
|Z\cap D|<\omega
.
Thus,
D
weakly separates
\mathcal{Y}
from
\mathcal{Z}
.

As
T
,
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
were arbitrary, we conclude that
\kappa<\mathfrak{at}
.
∎

Lemma 12
.

\mathfrak{at}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}}
.
Consequently,
\mathfrak{ap}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a second-countable Urysohn space with
|X|<\mathfrak{at}
. We show
that every subset of
X
is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
.

Apply Lemma
9
to get a countable sequence of finite
covers of
X
,
(\mathcal{B}_{n}:n<\omega)
, as in the statement of the lemma.
Let

T=\bigcup_{n<\omega}\prod_{k<n}\mathcal{B}_{k},

ordered by inclusion (that is, initial-segment extension).
This is a countable tree with finite levels.
For
x\in X
put

T_{x}=\{t\in T:\forall k<|t|\ (x\in t(k))\}.

Each
T_{x}
is an infinite subtree of
T
. If
x\neq y
, then by
Lemma
9
there is
m<\omega
such that, for all

n\geq m
, no member of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
contains both
x
and
y
in its closure,
and hence no member of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
contains both
x
and
y
. Therefore,

T_{x}\cap T_{y}
has height at most
m
and is finite. Thus,

\mathcal{T}_{X}=\{T_{x}:x\in X\}

is an almost disjoint family of subtrees of
T
.
The map
x\mapsto T_{x}
is injective, since
T_{x}\cap T_{y}
is finite for

x\neq y
, whereas each
T_{x}
is infinite.

Fix
A\subseteq X
.
Since
|\mathcal{T}_{X}|<\mathfrak{at}
, there exists
D\subseteq T
such that

\forall x\in A\ |D\cap T_{x}|=\omega,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\forall y\in X\setminus A\ |D\cap T_{y}|<\omega.

For
t\in T
let
O_{t}=\bigcap_{k<|t|}t(k)
, with the convention

O_{\emptyset}=X
. Also, for each finite
F\subseteq D
define

W_{F}=\bigcup_{t\in D\setminus F}O_{t}
.
Each
W_{F}
is open, and
[D]^{<\omega}
is countable. We claim that

A=\bigcap_{F\in[D]^{<\omega}}W_{F}.

If
x\in A
and
F\in[D]^{<\omega}
, choose

t\in(D\cap T_{x})\setminus F
. Then
x\in O_{t}\subseteq W_{F}
. Conversely, let

y\in X\setminus A
and put
F_{y}=D\cap T_{y}
. This set is finite. If

t\in D\setminus F_{y}
, then
t\notin T_{y}
, so for some
k<|t|
we have

y\notin t(k)
, and hence
y\notin O_{t}
. Thus
y\notin W_{F_{y}}
.

Therefore every subset of
X
is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
. Applying this to
complements, every subset is also an
F_{\sigma}
subset of
X
. Hence
X
is a

Q
-space.
∎

4
G_{\delta}
diagonals

The preceding sections show that the Urysohn assumption gives enough
combinatorial structure to obtain the bound

\mathfrak{at}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}}
, and hence
\mathfrak{ap}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}}
.

It is natural to ask where
\mathfrak{q}_{2}
stands relative to
\mathfrak{ap}
.
We do not answer this question here, but, as we will see, a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal supplies the diagonal separation needed in the Hausdorff context.

Definition 13
.

The cardinal
\mathfrak{q}_{2\Delta}
is the least cardinality of a
second-countable Hausdorff non-
Q
-space with a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal.

The cardinal
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
, where
\mathcal{M}
is the ideal of meager subsets of
\mathbb{R}
(countable unions of nowhere dense sets), is the least cardinality of a subfamily of
\mathcal{M}
whose union is
\mathbb{R}
.
For the next lemma, recall that
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
is the least cardinality of a collection
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\omega^{\omega}
such that there is no
g\in\omega^{\omega}
satisfying

\forall f\in\mathcal{F}\quad|\{n<\omega:f(n)=g(n)\}|=\omega.

For a proof, see
[
3
, Theorem 5.9]
.

Lemma 14
.

Let
\kappa<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
. Let
Y
be a set with

|Y|\leq\kappa
, and let
(\mathcal{U}_{n})_{n<\omega}
be a sequence of
countable covers of
Y
. Then there exist
U_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}
, for

n<\omega
, such that

\forall y\in Y\quad|\{n<\omega:y\in U_{n}\}|=\omega.

Proof.

For each
n<\omega
, enumerate
\mathcal{U}_{n}
as

\{U^{n}_{i}:i<\omega\}
, repeating members if necessary. For each
y\in Y
, define
f_{y}\in\omega^{\omega}

by choosing
f_{y}(n)
so that
y\in U^{n}_{f_{y}(n)}
.

Since
|\{f_{y}:y\in Y\}|<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
, by the
characterization of
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
mentioned above, there exists

g\in\omega^{\omega}
such that

|\{n<\omega:g(n)=f_{y}(n)\}|=\omega

for every
y\in Y
. Put
U_{n}=U^{n}_{g(n)}
. Then, for each
y\in Y
,

y\in U_{n}
for infinitely many
n
.
∎

We do not know whether ZFC proves a comparison between
\mathfrak{q}_{2}
and either
\mathfrak{ap}
or
\mathfrak{at}
.
The next theorem shows that adding a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal to the Hausdorff hypothesis is already enough to recover the
\mathfrak{ap}
bound.

Theorem 15
.

\mathfrak{ap}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2\Delta}
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a second-countable Hausdorff space with a

G_{\delta}
-diagonal, and assume that
|X|<\mathfrak{ap}
. We prove
that every subset of
X
is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
.

As
\mathfrak{ap}\leq\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
(see
[
4
]
), we have
|X|<\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.
Fix a countable base
\mathcal{B}
for
X
. Since
X
has a

G_{\delta}
-diagonal, there is a decreasing sequence
(W_{n})_{n<\omega}
of
open subsets of
X^{2}
such that

\Delta_{X}=\bigcap_{n<\omega}W_{n}.

For each
n<\omega
, define
\mathcal{B}_{n}=\{G\in\mathcal{B}:G\times G\subseteq W_{n}\}
.
Clearly,
\mathcal{B}_{n}
is a cover of
X
.
Apply Lemma
14
to the sequence

(\mathcal{B}_{n})_{n\in\omega}
. We obtain sets
V_{n}\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
, for

n<\omega
, such that

\forall x\in X\quad\bigl|\{n<\omega:x\in V_{n}\}\bigr|=\omega.

In particular,
V_{n}\times V_{n}\subseteq W_{n}
for every
n\in\omega
.
For each
x\in X
, define

H_{x}=\{n<\omega:x\in V_{n}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{H}=\{H_{x}:x\in X\}.

Each
H_{x}
is infinite by the choice of the sets
V_{n}
.
We claim that
\mathcal{H}
is an almost disjoint family.
Let
x,y
be distinct elements of
X
. Since

\Delta_{X}=\bigcap_{n<\omega}W_{n}
, there is
N<\omega
such that
(x,y)\notin W_{N}
.
As the sequence
(W_{n})_{n<\omega}
is decreasing,
(x,y)\notin W_{n}
for
all
n\geq N
. If
n\in H_{x}\cap H_{y}
, then
x,y\in V_{n}
, and hence

(x,y)\in V_{n}\times V_{n}\subseteq W_{n}
.

Thus,
n<N
. Therefore
H_{x}\cap H_{y}\subseteq N
, so
H_{x}\cap H_{y}
is
finite and the map
x\mapsto H_{x}
is injective.

Now let
A\subseteq X
. Since
|\mathcal{H}|=|X|<\mathfrak{ap}
, the
set
\mathcal{H}_{A}=\{H_{x}:x\in A\}

can be weakly separated from
\mathcal{H}_{X\setminus A}=\{H_{y}:y\in X\setminus A\}
.
Thus, there exists
D\subseteq\omega
such that

\forall x\in A\quad|D\cap H_{x}|=\omega\qquad\text{and}\qquad\forall y\in X\setminus A\quad|D\cap H_{y}|<\omega.

For each finite
F\subseteq D
, define the open set
O_{F}=\bigcup_{n\in D\setminus F}V_{n}
.
We show that
A=\bigcap_{F\in[D]^{<\omega}}O_{F}
.

First let
x\in A
, and let
F\in[D]^{<\omega}
.
Since
D\cap H_{x}
is infinite, there is
n\in(D\cap H_{x})\setminus F
.
Then
x\in V_{n}\subseteq O_{F}
.
Therefore,
x\in\bigcap_{F\in[D]^{<\omega}}O_{F}
.

Conversely, let
y\in X\setminus A
.
Put
F_{y}=D\cap H_{y}
, which is finite.
If
n\in D\setminus F_{y}
, then
n\notin H_{y}
, and
therefore
y\notin V_{n}
. Hence
y\notin\bigcup_{n\in D\setminus F_{y}}V_{n}=O_{F_{y}}
.
Thus,
y\notin\bigcap_{F\in[D]^{<\omega}}O_{F}
.

This proves that
A=\bigcap_{F\in[D]^{<\omega}}O_{F}
. Since

[D]^{<\omega}
is countable,
A
is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
.
As
A\subseteq X
was arbitrary,
X
is a

Q
-space.
∎

The preceding theorem applies to spaces with a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal without
assuming the Urysohn axiom. Conversely, the combinatorial base constructed in
Section
3
shows directly that second-countable Urysohn

-diagonals. For the sake of completeness, we include the

Lemma 16
.

Every second-countable Urysohn space has a

-diagonal.

Proof.

Let
X
be a second-countable Urysohn space. Apply
Lemma
9
and let
(\mathcal{B}_{n})_{n<\omega}
be the
sequence of finite open covers obtained there. If
x\neq y
, then there is

m<\omega
such that, for every
n\geq m
, no member of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
contains
both
x
and
y
in its closure, and hence no member of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
contains
both
x
and
y
. Therefore

\Delta_{X}=\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\bigcup_{U\in\mathcal{B}_{n}}U\times U.

∎

The last lemma explains why
\mathfrak{q}_{2\Delta}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}}
. The next observation goes in a different
direction: below the corresponding
Q
-space threshold, even the weaker
separation axioms force a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal.

Lemma 17
.

Every second-countable
T_{i}
space of cardinality
<\mathfrak{q}_{i}
has a

G_{\delta}
-diagonal, for

i\in\{1,2\}
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a second-countable
T_{i}
space such that
|X|<\mathfrak{q}_{i}
.
By the definition of
\mathfrak{q}_{i}
, the space
X
is a
Q
-space. Hence
every subset of
X
is an
F_{\sigma}
subset of
X
.

Fix a countable base
\mathcal{B}
for
X
. For each
B\in\mathcal{B}
, choose
a countable collection
\mathcal{F}_{B}
of closed subsets of

such that

, and put

\mathcal{F}=\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{F}_{B}.

Then
\mathcal{F}
is a countable closed network for

, meaning that for every

and every

there is

such that

.
For each pair
(F,B)\in\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{B}
such that

,
let

\mathcal{U}_{F,B}=\{B,X\setminus F\}.

This is an open cover of

. Also, the countable family of covers

\mathcal{V}=\{\mathcal{U}_{F,B}:F\in\mathcal{F},\ B\in\mathcal{B},\ F\subseteq B\}

separates points in the following sense: if
x\neq y
, choose

B\in\mathcal{B}
with

, and then choose

F\in\mathcal{F}_{B}
with

; for this pair
(F,B)
, no member of

\mathcal{U}_{F,B}
contains both

and

.
Thus,

\Delta_{X}=\bigcap_{\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{V}}\bigcup_{U\in\mathcal{U}}U\times U.

∎

5
A model with
\mathfrak{ap}<\mathfrak{at}

In this section we prove a consistency result separating
\mathfrak{ap}
from

\mathfrak{at}
, and therefore from
\mathfrak{q}_{2\frac{1}{2}}
.
The construction is based on Brendle’s model for

\mathfrak{ap}<\mathfrak{q}

[
4
]
.

We use the forcing convention that
q\leq p
means that
q
is stronger than

p
. A subset of a forcing preorder is centered if every finite subfamily has
a common extension; a forcing is
\sigma
-centered if it is the union of
countably many centered sets. The countable chain condition (ccc) means that
every antichain is countable. If
\mathbb{P}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}
are forcing
preorders, then
\mathbb{P}
is a complete suborder of
\mathbb{Q}
if every
maximal antichain of
\mathbb{P}
remains maximal in
\mathbb{Q}
.

Following
[
7
]
, if
\mathbb{P}
is a forcing preorder and
\tau
is a
\mathbb{P}
-name, a
\mathbb{P}
-nice name for a subset of
\tau
is a
\mathbb{P}
-name of the form
\bigcup_{\sigma\in\dom\tau}\{\sigma\}\times A_{\sigma}
for some family
(A_{\sigma}:\sigma\in\dom\tau)
of antichains of
\mathbb{P}
.

For undefined forcing concepts and standard facts about forcing, we refer the reader to
[
7
]
.

5.1
Intertwined families

The following notion, due to
Brendle
[
4
]
, is the small almost disjoint configuration that will be preserved
throughout the iteration.

Definition 18
.

A pair
\langle\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}\rangle
of disjoint subcollections of

[\omega]^{\omega}
, each of cardinality
\aleph_{1}
, is
intertwined
if

\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{C}
is an almost disjoint family and, whenever

E\in[\omega]^{\omega}
has infinite intersection with uncountably many members
of
\mathcal{C}
, then
E
has infinite intersection with all but countably many
members of
\mathcal{B}
.

The role of intertwined families is to keep
\mathfrak{ap}
small throughout the forcing
iteration. Indeed, if such a family survives to the final extension, then

\mathfrak{ap}=\omega_{1}
: the family
\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{C}
is an almost
disjoint family of size
\omega_{1}
, and the defining implication for intertwinedness
prevents
\mathcal{C}
from being weakly separated from
\mathcal{B}
.

5.2
Forcing blocks

In this section, we define the forcing posets that will be used as building blocks for the iteration.

The forcing used here is the tree analogue of Brendle’s forcing

Q(\mathcal{A})
from
[
4
]
. In Brendle’s argument, conditions
choose a finite increasing sequence of natural numbers while avoiding finitely
many members of an almost disjoint family
\mathcal{A}
of branches of
2^{<\omega}
. Here the
ambient object is not the fixed tree
2^{<\omega}
and the members of the almost
disjoint family are not necessarily branches; instead, it is an arbitrary
finitely branching
\omega
-tree
T
, and
\mathcal{A}
is an almost disjoint family of
infinite subtrees of
T
. The generic object is a subset of
T

which is almost disjoint from every member of
\mathcal{A}
, but still meets
infinitely every ground-model set outside the ideal generated by
\mathcal{A}
.

For an
\omega
-tree
T
, write

T^{\uparrow<\omega}=\{\sigma\in T^{<\omega}:i<j<|\sigma|\Rightarrow\htt(\sigma(i))<\htt(\sigma(j))\}.

Thus
T^{\uparrow<\omega}
consists of finite sequences of nodes whose heights
strictly increase; no compatibility in the tree order is required. The
following forcing is a tree version of Brendle’s forcing.

Definition 19
.

Let
T
be an
\omega
-tree and let
\mathcal{X}
be an almost disjoint family of subtrees of
T
.
The forcing poset
Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
consists of triples
p=(\sigma_{p},h_{p},\mathcal{X}_{p})
with

\sigma_{p}\in T^{\uparrow<\omega},\qquad h_{p}:T^{\uparrow<\omega}\to\omega,\qquad\mathcal{X}_{p}\in[\mathcal{X}]^{<\omega},

such that, for every
i<|\sigma_{p}|
,
h_{p}(\sigma_{p}\restriction i)\leq\htt(\sigma_{p}(i))
.

For
p,q\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
, we define

q\leq p
if, and only if:

(1)

\sigma_{p}\subseteq\sigma_{q}
;

(2)

h_{q}\geq h_{p}
pointwise;

(3)

\mathcal{X}_{q}\supseteq\mathcal{X}_{p}
;

(4)

\sigma_{q}(i)\notin\bigcup\mathcal{X}_{p}
for all

|\sigma_{p}|\leq i<|\sigma_{q}|
.

Given
p\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
, the
stem
of
p
is
\sigma_{p}
and the
side condition
of
p
is
\mathcal{X}_{p}
.

In the notation above, the stem
\sigma_{p}
is a finite initial approximation to a generic set of nodes, the
function
h_{p}
imposes height lower bounds for future extensions, and the side
condition
\mathcal{X}_{p}
lists the subtrees that future nodes must avoid.

We first record that these forcing posets are
\sigma
-centered. In particular,
they have the countable chain condition and preserve cardinals and cofinalities.

Lemma 20
.

Let
T
be an
\omega
-tree and let
\mathcal{X}
be an almost disjoint family of subtrees of
T
. Then:

(1)

if
p,q\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
have the same stem, then there exists

r\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
such that
r\leq p,q
and
r
has the same stem as

p
and
q
;

(2)

Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
is
\sigma
-centered.

Proof.

For (1), let
p=(\sigma,h_{p},\mathcal{X}_{p})
and

q=(\sigma,h_{q},\mathcal{X}_{q})
.
Define
r=(\sigma,h_{r},\mathcal{X}_{r})
, where

\mathcal{X}_{r}=\mathcal{X}_{p}\cup\mathcal{X}_{q}
and
h_{r}(\tau)=\max\{h_{p}(\tau),h_{q}(\tau)\}
for every
\tau\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}
.
Then
r\leq p,q
and
r
has stem
\sigma
.

For (2), for each
\sigma\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}
let
C_{\sigma}=\{p\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X}):\text{the stem of }p\text{ is }\sigma\}
.
By (1) and a straightforward induction, each
C_{\sigma}
is centered.
Since
T
is countable,
T^{\uparrow<\omega}
is countable, and
Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})=\bigcup_{\sigma\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}}C_{\sigma}
.
Thus,
Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
is
\sigma
-centered.
∎

Given an almost disjoint family
\mathcal{X}
of subsets of a countable set
T
,
the
ideal generated by
\mathcal{X}
on
T
is defined as follows.

\mathcal{I}_{T}(\mathcal{X})=\{X\subseteq T:\exists\mathcal{F}\in[\mathcal{X}]^{<\omega}|X\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{F}|<\omega\}.

We say that the ideal
\mathcal{I}_{T}(\mathcal{X})
is
proper
if
T\notin\mathcal{I}_{T}(\mathcal{X})
.
Notice that if
\mathcal{X}
is infinite, then
\mathcal{I}_{T}(\mathcal{X})
is proper.

The following lemma records the basic separation property of
Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
.
Provided the ideal generated by
\mathcal{X}
is proper, the generic set is
almost disjoint from every member of
\mathcal{X}
and meets every

\mathcal{I}_{T}(\mathcal{X})
-positive set infinitely often.

Lemma 21
.

Let
T
be an
\omega
-tree and let

\mathcal{X}
be an almost disjoint family of subtrees of
T
.
Assume that
\mathcal{I}_{T}(\mathcal{X})
is proper.
Let
\dot{D}
be a
Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
-name such that

Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})\Vdash\dot{D}=\{t\in\check{T}:\exists p\in\dot{G}\ \exists i<|\sigma_{p}|\ (t=\sigma_{p}(i))\}.

Then:

(1)

for every
A\in\mathcal{X}
,
Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})\Vdash|\dot{D}\cap\check{A}|<\omega
;

(2)

for every
B\in[T]^{\omega}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{T}(\mathcal{X})
,
Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})\Vdash|\dot{D}\cap\check{B}|=\omega
.

Proof.

For (1), fix
A\in\mathcal{X}
. The set

\mathcal{D}_{A}=\{p\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X}):A\in\mathcal{X}_{p}\}
is dense in

Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
.
Working in a countable transitive model
M
, let
G
be
Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
-generic over
M
.
Let
p\in G\cap\mathcal{D}_{A}
.
We claim that
\dot{D}_{G}\cap A\subseteq\{\sigma_{p}(i):i<|\sigma_{p}|\}
.

Given
t\in\dot{D}_{G}\cap A
, there are
q\in G
and
i<|\sigma_{q}|
such that
t=\sigma_{q}(i)
.
There exists
r\in G
such that
r\leq p,q
.
If
i\geq|\sigma_{p}|
, then

t=\sigma_{q}(i)=\sigma_{r}(i)\notin\bigcup\mathcal{X}_{p}
, contradicting

t\in A\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal{X}_{p}
. Hence
i<|\sigma_{p}|
.

For (2), fix
B\in[T]^{\omega}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{T}(\mathcal{X})
and
m\in\omega
, and define

\mathcal{D}_{B,m}=\{p\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X}):\exists i<|\sigma_{p}|(\sigma_{p}(i)\in B\land\htt(\sigma_{p}(i))\geq m)\}.

Then
\mathcal{D}_{B,m}
is dense in
Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
: given
p\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
, let
\bar{m}\in\omega
be such that

\bar{m}\geq\max(\{m,h_{p}(\sigma_{p})\}\cup\{\htt(\sigma_{p}(i)):i<|\sigma_{p}|\})+1.

As
B\notin\mathcal{I}_{T}(\mathcal{X})
,
B\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{X}_{p}
is infinite.
Since every level of
T
is finite,
T_{<\bar{m}}
is finite.
Thus, there is
t\in B\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{X}_{p}
such that
\htt(t)\geq\bar{m}
.
Then
q=(\sigma_{p}^{\frown}\langle t\rangle,h_{p},\mathcal{X}_{p})
is a condition extending
p
and belonging to
\mathcal{D}_{B,m}
.

Now, given
B
and
m
, every
p\in\mathcal{D}_{B,m}
forces

\dot{D}\cap\check{B}\not\subseteq\check{T_{<m}}
.
As
\mathcal{D}_{B,m}
is dense, this means that
Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})\Vdash\dot{D}\cap\check{B}\not\subseteq\check{T_{<m}}
.
Since this holds for every
m\in\omega
and each
T_{<m}
is finite, we have
Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})\Vdash|\dot{D}\cap\check{B}|=\omega
.
∎

If
T
has height
\omega
, then the infinite branches of
T
are exactly the
chains that meet every level of
T
.
Such a branch meets each level in exactly one node.

We will use the following notation.

Definition 22
.

Let
T
be an
\omega
-tree, and let
\mathcal{X}
be an almost disjoint
family of subtrees of
T
.
Let
p\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
and let

\tau\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}
.
We say that
p
is extensible to
\tau
if
\sigma_{p}\subseteq\tau
and, for every

|\sigma_{p}|\leq i<|\tau|
,

h_{p}(\tau\restriction i)\leq\htt(\tau(i))\quad\text{and}\quad\tau(i)\notin\bigcup\mathcal{X}_{p}.

Thus,
p=(\sigma_{p},h_{p},\mathcal{X}_{p})
is extensible to
\tau
exactly when
one can replace the stem of
p
by
\tau
, keeping the same height function and
side condition, and obtain an extension of
p
.

Lemma 23
.

Let
T
be an
\omega
-tree, let
\mathcal{X}
be an almost disjoint family of
subtrees of
T
, and let

q_{\beta}=(\sigma,h_{\beta},\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{S}_{\beta})\qquad(\beta\in I)

be an uncountable family of conditions in
Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
with the same stem
\sigma
.
Assume that the finite sets
\mathcal{S}_{\beta}
are pairwise
disjoint and disjoint from
\mathcal{R}
.
Let
h^{\prime}:T^{\uparrow<\omega}\to\omega
be such that
(\sigma,h^{\prime},\mathcal{R})
is a condition and
h_{\beta}\geq h^{\prime}
for every
\beta\in I
.
Then there is
h\geq h^{\prime}
such that
q=(\sigma,h,\mathcal{R})
is a condition
and, for every
\tau\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}
to which
q
is extensible, the set

\{\beta\in I:q_{\beta}\text{ is extensible to }\tau\}

is uncountable.

Proof.

For each
n<\omega
, put

L_{n}=\{\tau\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}:|\tau|=n\}
and

L_{<n}=\bigcup_{m<n}L_{m}
.
We define a sequence of functions
(g_{n}:n<\omega)
such that, for every

n<\omega
:

(1)

g_{n}:T^{\uparrow<\omega}\to\omega
;

(2)

g_{n}|(T^{\uparrow<\omega}\setminus L_{<n})=h^{\prime}|(T^{\uparrow<\omega}\setminus L_{<n})
;

(3)

for all
m<n
,
g_{n}|L_{<m}=g_{m}|L_{<m}
and
g_{m}\leq g_{n}
pointwise;

(4)

(\sigma,g_{n},\mathcal{R})\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
;

(5)

for all
\tau\in L_{n}
, if
(\sigma,g_{n},\mathcal{R})
is not extensible to
\tau
, then
g_{n+1}(\tau)=h^{\prime}(\tau)
;

(6)

for all
\tau\in L_{n}
, if
(\sigma,g_{n},\mathcal{R})
is extensible to
\tau
, then for every
t\in T
such that

\tau^{\frown}\langle t\rangle\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}
,

t\notin\bigcup\mathcal{R}
, and

\htt(t)\geq g_{n+1}(\tau)
, the set

\{\beta\in I:q_{\beta}\text{ is extensible to }\tau^{\frown}\langle t\rangle\}
is uncountable.

We proceed by recursion on
n
.
For
n=0
, define
g_{0}=h^{\prime}
. Then (1)–(4) hold, and there is nothing to check for (5) and (6).

Assume that
(g_{m}:m\leq n)
has been defined so that conditions (1)–(6) hold up to this point.
We define
g_{n+1}
as follows.
For
\tau\in L_{<n}
, put
g_{n+1}(\tau)=g_{n}(\tau)
.
For
\tau\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}\setminus L_{<n+1}
, put
g_{n+1}(\tau)=h^{\prime}(\tau)
.

Now fix
\tau\in L_{n}
. If
(\sigma,g_{n},\mathcal{R})
is not extensible to

\tau
, put
g_{n+1}(\tau)=h^{\prime}(\tau)
. Otherwise, we first show that

I_{\tau}=\{\beta\in I:q_{\beta}\text{ is extensible to }\tau\}

is uncountable. If
\tau=\sigma
, this is immediate. If

\tau\supsetneq\sigma
, write
\tau=\eta^{\frown}\langle t\rangle
, where

\eta\in L_{n-1}
. Since
(\sigma,g_{n},\mathcal{R})
is extensible to
\tau
,
we have

t\notin\bigcup\mathcal{R}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\htt(t)\geq g_{n}(\eta).

Moreover,
(\sigma,g_{n-1},\mathcal{R})
is extensible to
\eta
, because

g_{n-1}
and
g_{n}
agree on all proper initial segments of
\eta
.
By the induction hypothesis, applied to
\eta\in L_{n-1}
, the set

\{\beta\in I:q_{\beta}\text{ is extensible to }\eta^{\frown}\langle t\rangle\}

is uncountable.
Hence,
I_{\tau}
is uncountable.

Since the map
\beta\in I_{\tau}\longmapsto h_{\beta}(\tau)\in\omega

has countable range, there is an uncountable set
J_{\tau}\subseteq I_{\tau}
and a number

j_{0}<\omega
such that
h_{\beta}(\tau)\leq j_{0}
for every
\beta\in J_{\tau}
.

For
\beta\in J_{\tau}
, put

U_{\beta}=\bigcup\mathcal{S}_{\beta}.

The family
(U_{\beta}:\beta\in J_{\tau})
is pairwise almost disjoint, since the

\mathcal{S}_{\beta}
’s are pairwise disjoint finite subsets of the almost
disjoint family
\mathcal{X}
.
Let

E_{\tau}=\{t\in T:\{\beta\in J_{\tau}:t\notin U_{\beta}\}\text{ is countable}\}.

Thus,
E_{\tau}
is the set of nodes which are forbidden by all but countably many
of the side conditions
\mathcal{S}_{\beta}
.

We claim that
E_{\tau}
is finite.
For each
t\in E_{\tau}
, the set
\{\beta\in J_{\tau}:t\in U_{\beta}\}

is cocountable in
J_{\tau}
.
Since
E_{\tau}
is countable,
\bigcap_{t\in E_{\tau}}\{\beta\in J_{\tau}:t\in U_{\beta}\}
is cocountable in
J_{\tau}
.
Choose distinct

\beta,\gamma
in this intersection.
Then
E_{\tau}\subseteq U_{\beta}\cap U_{\gamma}
.
Since
U_{\beta}
and
U_{\gamma}
are almost disjoint,
E_{\tau}
is finite, as claimed.
Let

g_{n+1}(\tau)=\max(\{h^{\prime}(\tau),j_{0}\}\cup\{\htt(t):t\in E_{\tau}\})+1.

This completes the definition of
g_{n+1}
.

We now check the requirements. Requirements (1)–(3) are immediate from the
definition of
g_{n+1}
and the induction hypothesis. Requirement (4) also
holds.
Indeed, let
i<|\sigma|
and put
\eta=\sigma\restriction i
.
We must show that

g_{n+1}(\eta)\leq\htt(\sigma(i)).

If
|\eta|<n
, then
g_{n+1}(\eta)=g_{n}(\eta)
, and the inequality follows from

(\sigma,g_{n},\mathcal{R})\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
.
If
|\eta|>n
, then
g_{n+1}(\eta)=h^{\prime}(\eta)
, and the inequality follows from

(\sigma,h^{\prime},\mathcal{R})\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
.
Finally, if
|\eta|=n
, then
\eta
is a proper initial segment of
\sigma
,
so
(\sigma,g_{n},\mathcal{R})
is not extensible to
\eta
. Hence

g_{n+1}(\eta)=h^{\prime}(\eta)
, and the inequality again follows from

(\sigma,h^{\prime},\mathcal{R})\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
.
Thus,
(\sigma,g_{n+1},\mathcal{R})\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
.

Requirement (5) was arranged by definition. For requirement (6), let

\tau\in L_{n}
be such that
(\sigma,g_{n},\mathcal{R})
is extensible to
\tau
,
and let
t\in T
satisfy

\tau^{\frown}\langle t\rangle\in T^{\uparrow<\omega},\qquad t\notin\bigcup\mathcal{R},\qquad\htt(t)\geq g_{n+1}(\tau).

Then
t\notin E_{\tau}
, and therefore
K_{t}=\{\beta\in J_{\tau}:t\notin U_{\beta}\}
is uncountable.
For every
\beta\in K_{t}\subseteq I_{\tau}
, we have
q_{\beta}
extensible to

\tau
,

h_{\beta}(\tau)\leq j_{0}\leq g_{n+1}(\tau)\leq\htt(t)\qquad\text{and}\qquad t\notin\bigcup(\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{S}_{\beta}).

Hence,
q_{\beta}
is extensible to
\tau^{\frown}\langle t\rangle
.
Therefore,
K_{t}\subseteq\{\beta\in I:q_{\beta}\text{ is extensible to }\tau^{\frown}\langle t\rangle\}
, and the latter set is uncountable, concluding the verification of (6).
This completes the recursive construction.

Finally, define

h(\tau)=g_{n+1}(\tau)\qquad\text{whenever }\tau\in L_{n}.

By construction, for every
\tau\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}
,
h(\tau)=g_{n+1}(\tau)\geq g_{0}(\tau)=h^{\prime}(\tau)
, where
n=|\tau|
.

We claim that
q=(\sigma,h,\mathcal{R})\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
.
Let
i<|\sigma|
.
Since
\sigma\restriction i
is a proper initial segment of
\sigma
,

(\sigma,g_{i},\mathcal{R})
is not extensible to
\sigma\restriction i
.
Hence, by requirement (5),

h(\sigma\restriction i)=g_{i+1}(\sigma\restriction i)=h^{\prime}(\sigma\restriction i)\leq\htt(\sigma(i)).

Therefore,
q\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
.

It remains to verify the desired property. Let
\tau\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}

be such that
q
is extensible to
\tau
. If
\tau=\sigma
, then every

q_{\beta}
is extensible to
\tau
. Otherwise, write

\tau=\eta^{\frown}\langle t\rangle
and let
|\eta|=n
. Since
q
is
extensible to
\tau
, we have

t\notin\bigcup\mathcal{R}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\htt(t)\geq h(\eta)=g_{n+1}(\eta).

Also,
(\sigma,g_{n},\mathcal{R})
is extensible to
\eta
, because
g_{n}
agrees
with
h
on all proper initial segments of
\eta
. By requirement (6), the
set

\{\beta\in I:q_{\beta}\text{ is extensible to }\eta^{\frown}\langle t\rangle\}

is uncountable. Since
\tau=\eta^{\frown}\langle t\rangle
, this concludes the
proof.
∎

5.3
Ranks and finite-rank approximations

We now introduce the rank machinery used to analyze names for subsets of
\omega
.
This is the point at which the proof most closely follows Brendle’s rank argument,
with natural numbers replaced by nodes of high enough height in
T
.

Definition 24
.

Let
T
be an
\omega
-tree, let
\mathcal{X}
be an almost disjoint family of
subtrees of
T
, let
\mathcal{R}\in[\mathcal{X}]^{<\omega}
, and let
\dot{D}

be a
Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
-name for a subset of
\omega
.
We define:

(1)

for each
\eta\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}
,

\operatorname{Succ}_{\mathcal{R}}(\eta)=\{\eta^{\frown}\langle t\rangle:t\in T\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{R}\text{ and }\eta^{\frown}\langle t\rangle\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}\}
.

(2)

for each
\ell<\omega
,
B_{\ell}=\{\eta\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}:\exists r\in Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})\,(\sigma_{r}=\eta\text{ and }r\Vdash\ell\in\dot{D})\}
.

For each
\ell<\omega
, define, by recursion on
\alpha<\omega_{1}
, sets

W^{\ell}_{\alpha}\subseteq T^{\uparrow<\omega}
as follows:

•

W^{\ell}_{0}=B_{\ell}
;

•

for
0<\alpha<\omega_{1}
,

W^{\ell}_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}W^{\ell}_{\xi}\cup\{\eta\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}:|\operatorname{Succ}_{\mathcal{R}}(\eta)\cap\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}W^{\ell}_{\xi}|=\omega\}
.

Let
W^{\ell}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}W^{\ell}_{\alpha}
.
For
\eta\in W^{\ell}
, the rank of
\eta
with respect to
(\ell,\mathcal{R},\dot{D})
is

\rho_{\ell}(\eta)=\min\{\alpha<\omega_{1}:\eta\in W^{\ell}_{\alpha}\}.

If
\eta\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}\setminus W^{\ell}
, write
\rho_{\ell}(\eta)=\infty
.
Finally, for
\eta\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}
and
i<\omega
, put

D_{\eta,i}=\{\ell<\omega:\exists t\in T\,(\eta^{\frown}\langle t\rangle\in\operatorname{Succ}_{\mathcal{R}}(\eta)\cap W^{\ell}\text{ and }\htt(t)\geq i)\}.

The sets
W^{\ell}_{\alpha}
measure how close a stem is to being able to force

\ell\in\dot{D}
.
At level zero, we put all stems from which
\ell\in\dot{D}

can already be forced.
At later stages, a stem is admitted once it has infinitely many one-step
extensions that avoid the fixed finite set
\mathcal{R}
and were admitted earlier.
Thus
\rho_{\ell}(\eta)
is the
first stage at which
\eta
enters this inductive closure, while

\rho_{\ell}(\eta)=\infty
means that this process never reaches
\eta
.

The sets
D_{\eta,i}
are ground-model approximations to the name

\dot{D}
above the stem
\eta
.
An integer
\ell
belongs to
D_{\eta,i}
if some allowed one-step extension
of
\eta
by a node of height at least
i
has countable
\ell
-rank.
These approximations are designed to reflect the possible decisions about

\dot{D}
made by extensions of the forcing condition in the ground model, and will be used in the proof of Lemma
29
.

Lemma 25
.

Let
T
be an
\omega
-tree, let
\mathcal{X}
be an almost disjoint family of
subtrees of
T
, and assume that
\mathcal{I}_{T}(\mathcal{X})
is proper.
Let
\mathcal{R}\in[\mathcal{X}]^{<\omega}
and let
\dot{D}
be a

Q_{T}(\mathcal{X})
-name for a subset of
\omega
.
Let
\rho_{\ell}=\rho_{\ell,\mathcal{R},\dot{D}}
,

and

be as in Definition
24
.
Then, for every
\eta\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}
and every
\ell<\omega
,

(1)

if
\eta\in W^{\ell}
, then
\ell\in D_{\eta,i}
for every

i<\omega
;

(2)

if
(\mathcal{S}_{\beta}:\beta\in I)
is a pairwise disjoint family of

\mathcal{X}
, then, for every

\eta\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}
with

, for all
but at most one

the set

\{t\in T\setminus\bigcup(\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{S}_{\beta}):\eta^{\frown}\langle t\rangle\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}\text{ and }\rho_{\ell}(\eta^{\frown}\langle t\rangle)<\rho_{\ell}(\eta)\}

Proof.

For (1), fix

.

First assume that

, and put

\alpha=\rho_{\ell}(\eta)
. Since

is the first stage at which

\eta
belongs to
W^{\ell}_{\alpha}
, the definition of

says that

L=\{t\in T\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{R}:\eta^{\frown}\langle t\rangle\in T^{\uparrow<\omega}\text{ and }\eta^{\frown}\langle t\rangle\in\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}W^{\ell}_{\xi}\}

is infinite. Since the levels of
T
are finite,
L
contains nodes of
arbitrarily large height. Choose

with

\htt(t)\geq i
. Then

\eta^{\frown}\langle t\rangle\in W^{\ell}
,

, and

\htt(t)\geq i
, so
\ell\in D_{\eta,i}
.

Now assume that
\rho_{\ell}(\eta)=0

\eta\in B_{\ell}
, so there is a
condition

such that
\sigma_{r}=\eta
