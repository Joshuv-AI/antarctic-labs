On the Axioms of Arboreal Categories Thanks: This is an extended version of a paper due to appear in the proceedings of the 18th International Workshop on Coalgebraic Methods in Computer Science (CMCS 2026).
Tomáš Jakl, Luca Reggio

Abstract
Arboreal categories were introduced as an axiomatic framework for game comonads, which provide a comonadic view on many model-comparison games in logic. We demonstrate the inadequacy of the axiom stating that paths are connected. We then propose the notion of “tree-connectedness” to address this deficiency, and show that all the essential properties of arboreal categories that we are aware of remain valid under this new definition. Furthermore, we show that the path functor is a Street fibration.

Introduction

Game comonads were introduced in
[

,
9

to give a categorical and comonadic account of several concepts central to finite model theory.
The key insight is that model-comparison games, a basic tool of (finite) model theory,
can be naturally organised into endofunctors on categories of relational structures that carry a comonad structure.
In fact, these endofunctors encode the possible plays or positions in a given relational structure according to the rules of the game, and the relational structure on the set of plays is derived from the winning conditions.
The principal examples are:

Over the years, the term

The category of Eilenberg–Moore coalgebras is central to the theory.

To capture the common features of the categories of Eilenberg–Moore coalgebras of game comonads, the axiomatic framework of arboreal categories was proposed in

Relational structures.

that extracts the last element of a sequence, i.e., it sends
[\bullet,\tau_{1},a_{1},\,\dots,\,\tau_{n},a_{n}]
to
a_{n}
.

•

The distinguished point of
\mathbb{M}_{k}A
is the shortest sequence, that is,
[\bullet]
.

•

For a unary symbol
P\in\sigma
, define
P^{\mathbb{M}_{k}A}
as
\{s\mid P^{A}(\varepsilon_{A}(s))\}.

For a binary symbol
R_{\tau}\in\sigma
, define
R^{\mathbb{M}_{k}A}_{\tau}
as the set of pairs
(s,s\mathbin{+\mkern-10.0mu+}[\tau,a])
, with
a\in A
, such that
R^{A}_{\tau}(\varepsilon_{A}(s),a)
. Here,
\mathbin{+\mkern-10.0mu+}
denotes the concatenation of sequences.

Note that
\mathbb{M}_{k}
is an endofunctor; its action on morphisms is given by

\mathbb{M}_{k}(f)\colon[\bullet,\tau_{1},a_{1},\,\dots,\,\tau_{n},a_{n}]\mapsto[\bullet,\tau_{1},f(a_{1}),\,\dots,\,\tau_{n},f(a_{n})].

The comonad structure is defined as follows:

•

The counit is the map
\varepsilon_{A}\colon\mathbb{M}_{k}A\to A
defined above.

•

The comultiplication
\delta_{A}\colon\mathbb{M}_{k}A\to\mathbb{M}_{k}^{2}A
sends a sequence to the
sequence of its prefixes
, i.e.
[\bullet,\tau_{1},a_{1},\,\dots,\,\tau_{n},a_{n}]
is mapped to

[[\bullet],\tau_{1},[\bullet,\tau_{1},a_{1}],\tau_{2},\,\dots,\,\tau_{n},[\bullet,\tau_{1},a_{1},\,\dots,\,\tau_{n},a_{n}]].

Observe that, in general,
\varepsilon_{A}
is not a p-morphism since the length of sequences in
\mathbb{M}_{k}A
is bounded by
k
. However, the structures
A
and
\mathbb{M}_{k}A
are indistinguishable in the fragment
\mathrm{ML}_{k}
of modal logic, consisting of formulas of
modal depth
at most
k
(i.e., with at most
k
nested modalities). In particular,

A\equiv_{\mathrm{ML}_{k}}B\quad{\iff}\quad\mathbb{M}_{k}A\sim\mathbb{M}_{k}B

(4)

where
\sim
denotes bisimilarity or, equivalently, the existence of a winning strategy of Duplicator in the bisimulation game.

These facts are well known in the literature on modal logic, see e.g.
[
22
]
, except perhaps for the observation that
\mathbb{M}_{k}
is a comonad. Interestingly, a similar pattern emerges in many other model-comparison games occurring in (finite) model theory.
In the case of other comonads, however, the category of coalgebras typically plays a more prominent role, as it must be explicitly invoked to express bisimilarity.

Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé comonad.

The next two comonads that we define are on

, for a given relational signature
\sigma
.
The first comonad can be viewed as an unravelling of a structure based on the rules of the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game.
For a fixed

•

•

maps
[a_{1},\dots,a_{n}]
to
a_{n}
.

•

\delta_{A}\colon\mathbb{E}_{k}A\to\mathbb{E}_{k}^{2}A

•

For an
n

3.1
The categories of coalgebras

For the following, it is useful to recall from
[
10
]
the concrete description of the categories of coalgebras of our running examples of game comonads.
To this end, we say that a poset
(F,\leq)
is a
forest
if, for every
x\in F
, the set

{\downarrow}\,x\coloneqq\{y\in F\mid y\leq x\}

is a finite chain. A function between forests
f\colon F\to F^{\prime}
is a
forest morphism
if it is monotone and the chains
{\downarrow}\,x
and
{\downarrow}\,f(x)
have the same length for each
x\in F
.

If
x
is an element of a forest
F
, its
height
, denoted by
\htf(x)
, is the cardinality of the set
{\downarrow}\,x\setminus\{x\}
.
The elements of height
0
are called
roots
.
A
tree
is a forest with precisely one root.
The
height of the forest

F
is the supremum of the set
\{\htf(x)\mid x\in F\}
in
\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
.

The coalgebras for our three game comonads have an inherent forest order. Namely, for a comonad
\mathbb{C}
among
\mathbb{M}_{k},\mathbb{E}_{k},\mathbb{P}_{k}
and a coalgebra
\alpha\colon A\to\mathbb{C}A
, set

a\sqsubseteq_{\alpha}b\quad{\iff}\quad\kern 5.0pt\text{the sequence}\kern 5.0pt\alpha(a)\kern 5.0pt\text{is a prefix of}\kern 5.0pt\alpha(b).

Then, the poset
(A,\sqsubseteq_{\alpha})
is a forest and coalgebra morphisms are forest morphisms.
This allows us to give a concrete characterisation of the category
\mathsf{EM}(\mathbb{C})
as a class of forest-ordered structures:

•

Since
\mathbb{M}_{k}
is an idempotent comonad, the category
\mathsf{EM}(\mathbb{M}_{k})
is isomorphic to the full subcategory of
\mathsf{Str}_{\bullet}(\sigma)
consisting of
synchronisation trees

[
24
]
of height
{\leq}\,k
, i.e. tree-ordered
\sigma
-structures
(A,\leq)
such that
a
is the parent of
b
just when
R^{A}(a,b)
for some relation
R
in
\sigma
.
In particular, Kripke morphisms between synchronisation trees are automatically forest morphisms.

•

The objects of
\mathsf{EM}(\mathbb{E}_{k})
can be described as forest-ordered
\sigma
-structures
{(A,\leq)}
of height
{\leq}\,k
such that if
R^{A}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, then
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
are pairwise comparable in the order
\leq
.
The latter condition follows from the definition of the relation
R^{\mathbb{E}_{k}A}
given above.

Coalgebra morphisms correspond precisely to homomorphisms of the underlying relational structures that are also forest morphisms.

•

Objects in
\mathsf{EM}(\mathbb{P}_{k})
can be described as forest-ordered
\sigma
-structures
{(A,\leq,p)}
with a “pebbling function”
p\colon A\to\{1,\dots,k\}
such that
R^{A}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

implies that (i)
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
are pairwise comparable and (ii) if
x_{i}<x_{j}
then
p(x_{i})\not\in\{p(y)\mid x_{i}<y\leq x_{j}\}
.
Morphisms in
\mathsf{EM}(\mathbb{P}_{k})
are the homomorphisms of relational structures that preserve the pebbling functions and are also forest morphisms.

These categories of coalgebras are very well-behaved. For instance, they are complete and cocomplete, admit an (epi, regular mono) factorisation system, and are even locally finitely presentable. Cf.
[
18
,
23
,
31
]
.

3.2
Game comonads and logic

Using the concrete characterisation of the category

\mathbb{E}_{k}

To this end, we say that a coalgebra

is a

.
Then, following
[

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

with

Using these notions, we can introduce three types of equivalence in

For coalgebras

•

if there is a bisimulation

If the relations

Furthermore, in the setting of arboreal categories, which we introduce below, back-and-forth equivalence and bisimilarity coincide, as shown in

4
Arboreal categories and the failure of connectedness

The
raison d’être
of arboreal categories is to provide a general language for working with back-and-forth equivalence, bisimilarity, and so forth, for categories of coalgebras for game comonads. This is achieved uniformly, without relying on any specific feature of the comonads or the underlying category of structures.

An essential ingredient in the definition of arboreal categories is an axiomatic notion of path, which extends the concrete notion of path considered in Section
3.2
to any category with a proper factorisation system.

Definition 4.1

Let
\C
be a well-powered category equipped with a proper factorisation system
(\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{M})
.
An object
X
of
\C
is a
path
if its poset of
\mathscr{M}
-subobjects
\Emb{X}
is a finite linear order. If
P
is a path, its
height
, denoted by
\htf(P)
, is the height of the finite chain
\Emb P
.
A
path embedding
is an embedding
P\rightarrowtail X
whose domain is a path.

The following is the definition of an arboreal category as given in
[
6
]
.

Definition 1
(“Old definition” of arboreal category)

Let
\A
be a well-powered category equipped with a stable proper factorisation system.
We say that
\A
is
arboreal
if it satisfies the following conditions:

Paths are connected

Coproducts of sets of paths exist in
\A
and each path
P
is
connected
; that is, every arrow
P\to\coprod_{i\in I}{Q_{i}}
into a coproduct of a non-empty set of paths
\{Q_{i}\mid i\in I\}
factors through some coproduct arrow
Q_{j}\to\coprod_{i\in I}{Q_{i}}
.

2-out-of-3 property

Given arrows
f\colon P\to Q
and
g\colon Q\to R
between paths, if
g\circ f
is a quotient, then so is
f
.
3
3

3

This condition is equivalent to saying that if any two of
f
,
g
and
g\circ f
are quotients, then so is the third; see
[
7
, Remark 3.9]
. Hence the term “2-out-of-3 property”.

Path-generation

The full subcategory of paths is dense in
\A
.
4
4

4

A category equipped with a stable proper factorisation system satisfies the path-generation property if, and only if, each object is
path-generated
; that is, for each object
X
, the cocone consisting of all path embeddings
P\rightarrowtail X
is a colimit cocone

[
7
, Lemma 5.1 and Remark 5.2]
.

The paper
[
7
]
, in which arboreal categories were introduced, laid out the foundations for an axiomatic approach to logical equivalences in finite model theory. In fact, even game comonads introduced later still fit within the scope of arboreal categories, highlighting the robustness of this concept.
Consequently, results proved in the general setting of arboreal categories (cf. e.g.
[
2
,
5
,
8
,
23
,
30
]
) also apply to these newly introduced comonads.

However, there is an issue. The categories of coalgebras for game comonads defined over
pointed
Kripke structures may fail to be arboreal. This mistake has gone unnoticed until now. Below, we demonstrate that the connectedness axiom generally fails for modal comonads.

Firstly, we apply Lemma
1
to show that the (epi, regular mono) factorisation system of
\mathsf{Str}_{\bullet}(\sigma)
induces a stable proper factorisation system on the category
\mathsf{EM}(\mathbb{M}_{k})
. It is straightforward to check that
\mathbb{M}_{k}
preserves regular monomorphisms, so it remains to show that it sends pullbacks of quotients along embeddings to weak pullbacks.

Lemma 2

\mathbb{M}_{k}
sends pullbacks to weak pullbacks.

Proof

Let
f\colon A\to B
and

\mathsf{Str}_{\bullet}(\sigma)
, and let their pullback be as shown below.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime}}

5
Arboreal categories redefined: tree-connectedness

We propose amending the definition of arboreal category by replacing the connectedness condition with a
tree-connectedness
condition. The 2-out-of-3 and path-generation axioms remain unchanged.

Definition 2

Let
\C
be a category equipped with a stable proper factorisation system. A
tree-diagram
in
\C
is a functor
D\colon T\to\C
where
T
is a tree order, and the image of
D
consists of embeddings in
\C
. We say that
D
is a
tree-diagram of paths
if, in addition, its image consists of embeddings between paths.

Definition 3
(“New definition” of arboreal category)

Let
\A
be a well-powered category equipped with a stable proper factorisation system.
We say that
\A
is
arboreal
if it satisfies the following conditions:

Paths are tree-connected

\A
admits an initial object and colimits of tree-diagrams of paths, and each path
P
is
tree-connected
; i.e., for every tree-diagram of paths
{D\colon T\to\A}
and every arrow
f\colon P\to\operatornamewithlimits{colim}{D}
, there is a least
t\in T
such that
f
factors through the colimit arrow
D(t)\to\operatornamewithlimits{colim}{D}
.

2-out-of-3 property

Given arrows
f\colon P\to Q
and
g\colon Q\to R
between paths, if
g\circ f
is a quotient, then so is
f
.

Path-generation

The full subcategory of paths is dense in
\A
.

The diagram of paths in Example
1
, which showed the failure of connectedness of paths in the category
\mathsf{EM}(\mathbb{M}_{k})
, is not a tree-diagram of paths and cannot be turned into one.
In fact, we have the following:

Lemma 3

Every path in the category
\mathsf{EM}(\mathbb{M}_{k})
is tree-connected.

Proof

Let
D\colon T\to\mathsf{EM}(\mathbb{M}_{k})
be a tree-diagram of paths in
\mathsf{EM}(\mathbb{M}_{k})
, with
T
a tree. First, note that for every
u\in T
the colimit map
i_{u}\colon D(u)\to\operatornamewithlimits{colim}{D}
is an embedding. It is injective because it is a forest morphism from a chain. To see that it reflects unary relations, observe that the full inclusion
\mathsf{EM}(\mathbb{M}_{k})\to\mathcal{\mathsf{Str}_{\bullet}(\sigma)}
is left adjoint and so it preserves colimits. This means that
\operatornamewithlimits{colim}{D}
is computed by first taking the coproduct of the
\sigma
-structures
D(u)
, for
u\in T
, which is given by glueing the structures
D(u)
at their distinguished elements, and then quotienting according to the embeddings in the diagram. Hence, if
x\in D(u)
and
i_{u}(x)
satisfies a relation
r
, there is
x^{\prime}\in D(u^{\prime})
that satisfies
r
and such that
i_{u}(x)=i_{u^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})
. But this can only happen if there are
t\leq u,u^{\prime}
and

A similar argument shows that paths in the categories of coalgebras for the pebbling and Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé comonads are tree-connected. Since the categories

The categories
\mathsf{EM}(\mathbb{M}_{k})

and
\mathsf{EM}(\mathbb{P}_{k})
are arboreal in the sense of Definition

.

Regarding the relationship between connectedness and tree-connectedness, the following fact holds:

Lemma 4

Let

be a category equipped with a stable proper factorisation system and admitting an initial object
\mathbf{0}
. The following statements are equivalent:

1.

\mathbf{0}
has no proper quotients, i.e. any quotient with domain
\mathbf{0}
is an isomorphism;

2.

for every object

, the unique arrow
\mathbf{0}\to X
is an embedding.

\C
is cocomplete, then any tree-connected object of

is connected.

The equivalence of items
1
and
2
is easily verified from basic properties of orthogonal factorisation systems. Now, let
X
be a tree-connected object in
\C
and let
\{Q_{i}\mid i\in I\}
be a non-empty set of paths. Consider a tree
T
consisting of a root with
I

be the functor that sends the root of

to
\mathbf{0}
, and the

-th immediate successor of the root to

. This is a tree-diagram of paths whose colimit coincides with
\coprod_{i\in I}{Q_{i}}
. Since
X
is tree-connected, any arrow
X\to\coprod_{i\in I}{Q_{i}}
factors through some colimit arrow
Q_{j}\to\coprod_{i\in I}{Q_{i}}
.
∎

The following is an immediate consequence of Lemma

.

Proposition 2

Let
\C
be an arboreal category in the new sense (Definition
3
). If
\C
admits coproducts of sets of paths, and its initial object has no proper quotients, then
\C
is arboreal in the old sense (Definition

).

Since both in
\mathsf{EM}(\mathbb{E}_{k})
and
\mathsf{EM}(\mathbb{P}_{k})
the initial object has no proper quotients, the above proposition provides another, less direct, proof that both categories are arboreal in the old sense. However, the proposition does not apply to

. Note that as soon as the modal signature

contains a unary symbol

, any structure consisting of one element that satisfies

is a proper quotient of the initial object, which is a one-element structure with no relations.

Combining Lemmas

Remark 6

We could weaken the tree-connectedness condition in Definition

by considering only tree-diagrams of paths

7
The path functor is a Street fibration

In this final section, we demonstrate an important consequence of the new definition of an arboreal category.
Let us fix an arboreal category
\A
and write

\Path\colon\A\to\T

for the associated

into the category
\T
of trees and forest morphisms between them (cf. Theorem

). Recall that
\Path
assigns to an object
X
of
\A
the tree
\Path{X}
of (equivalence classes of) path embeddings into
X
; the order on
\Path X
is the one induced by the poset

of
\mathscr{M}
-subobjects of

.

We wish to show that
\Path
is a Street fibration (Theorem

To this end, we review some basic definitions related to fibrations.

A morphism

is

if for all

in

such that

and
\Path\overline{w}=w
.

is an embedding for every path embedding

One might ask whether Theorem

Example 2

Let
\A\coloneqq\mathsf{EM}(\mathbb{P}_{k})
for
k\geq 2
.
Recall from Section
3.1
that objects in this category can be described as tuples
(A,\leq,p)
where
(A,\leq)
is a forest-ordered structure and
p\colon A\to\{1,2\}
is a pebbling function. Consider
A_{1},A_{2}
discrete structures on
\{\star\}
equipped with
p\colon A_{i}\to\{1,2\}
such that
p(\star)=i
.
Then, both
\Path A_{1}
and
\Path A_{2}
are isomorphic to the two-element chain
\mathbf{2}=(\bot<\top)
, whose bottom element corresponds to the empty subpath of
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, respectively.

However, the span of forest morphisms
(f_{i}\colon\mathbf{2}\to\Path A_{i})_{i=1,2}
in
\T
cannot be lifted to a span
(g_{i}\colon X\to A_{i})_{i=1,2}
in
\A
such that
\Path(g_{i})=f_{i}
, since

would have to be a structure

maps

and

’s, and so

It remains an open problem to identify which Street fibrations over the category
\T
of trees arise from arboreal categories.
Finally, we present an example of a category that is arboreal in the old sense whose path functor is not a fibration.

Example 3

Let
\sigma
be a non-empty relational signature consisting only of unary relations, which we regard as colours. Let
\A
be the category
\mathsf{Str}(\sigma)
, equipped with the factorisation system (regular epi, mono), rather than the factorisation system (epi, regular mono) considered earlier. Then
\A
is arboreal in the old sense and the path functor
{\Path\colon\A\to\T}
is not a fibration.

For the sake of this example, assume
\sigma
contains two colours,
blue
and
red
.
The non-empty paths in
\A
are the one-element structures with at most one colour.
Hence, up to isomorphism, there is one path of height 0 (the empty structure), one path of height 1 (the one-element structure with no colours), and two paths
P_{blue}
and
P_{red}
of height 2, one for each colour. There are no paths of height 3 or more.
The category
\A
is arboreal in the old sense, but not in the new sense, since the tree-diagram of paths
P_{blue}\leftarrow\{\star\}\to P_{blue}
has colimit
P_{blue}
, but the identity
P_{blue}\to P_{blue}
does not factor through a minimal element.

Furthermore, the path functor
\Path\colon\A\to\T
is not a fibration because the trees in the image of
\Path
are coproducts of trees of the shapes below.

If
T
is the tree consisting of a root with three children, there is no
Y
such that
T\cong\Path(Y)
. Thus, no morphism of the form
T\to\Path X
admits a Cartesian lift.
