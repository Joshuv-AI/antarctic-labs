A Lagrangian framework for canonical analysis for the Holst model with β = 0 \beta=0
Lorenzo Fatibene

Abstract
We perform a canonical analysis of the Holst model for General Relativity, within the framework laid out in [ 9 , 18 ] , distinguishing our approach by setting the Barbero parameter to β = 0 \beta=0 and leaving the lapse and shift functions unconstrained. The β = 0 \beta=0 choice is of particular interest because it is viable across all dimensions, providing a necessary foundation for extending the Loop Quantum Gravity formalism beyond 3 + 1 3+1 dimensions. Through field decomposition and the projection of the field equations, we derive a system of 37 equations (10 differential constraints , 21 algebraic constraints , and 6 evolution equations ) exactly matching the 37 field components to be determined. Moreover, leaving the gauge unfixed reveals that three equations, which are typically identically satisfied under normal evolution, are actually differential constraints whose triviality depends on specific gauge choices. The resulting framework remains fully consistent with the standard 3 + 1 3+1 decomposition of the Einstein equations without requiring any constraints on the lapse and shift functions.

Introduction

General Relativity (GR) is the (classical) theory of gravitation, that is based on the idea that the gravitational attraction arises as a consequence of the geometry of spacetime.
In this framework, spacetime is described by a manifold

Holst model

.
Here

, i.e. its 1st and 2nd Stiefel-Whitney class are zero),

and

\hat{\omega}^{ab}

to

2.1
Ashtekar-Barbero-Immirzi variables on spacetime

The group

\mathrm{Spin}(3,1)

of

\mathrm{SU}(2)
-principal bundle

\iota(p\cdot U)=\iota(p)\cdot i(U)\ \forall\,p\in\Sigma,U\in\mathrm{SU}(2)
. Such a reduction is, in general, not guaranteed to exist due to topological obstructions; however a reduction from
\mathrm{Spin}(n,1)
to

is proven to exist, provided that the spacetime is a spin manifold, for every dimension

(see
[
18
]
).

The existence of the reduction allows us to define the
\mathrm{SU}(2)
spin frame

as

. However, see
[
18
]
, it is not sufficient to deal with the principal connection

and obtain a

connection, because the horizontal subspaces defined by
\hat{\omega}
might not be tangent to
\iota(\Sigma)
. This fact is assured in case a

exists. For the purpose of this work, it is sufficient to state that a reductive splitting is a map

such that its image in
\mathfrak{spin}(n,1)
is invariant with respect to the adjoint action of
\mathrm{Spin}(n,1)
restricted to
\mathrm{Spin}(n,0)
. For a detailed treatment, see
[
16
]
. Indeed, for
n=3
there exists a whole family of reductive splittings

, while if

, is available.

The maps

allow us to split

into a
\mathrm{SU}(2)

(2.3)

Let us remark that, following
[
6
,
5
,

]
we define

on spacetime. When we restrict to space we obtain exactly the ABI connection. The reduction
