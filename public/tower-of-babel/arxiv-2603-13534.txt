Nonlinear parabolic problem with time fractional derivative
Nikolai Kutev, Tsviatko Rangelov

Abstract
Time fractional parabolic problem for p-Laplacian with double singular Hardy-type potential is considered. Comparison principle and appriory estimates for the weak solutions are proved. Existence of global weak solutions and finite-time blow-up are investigated depending on the optimal Hardy constant.

Introduction

Let
\displaystyle D_{t}^{\alpha}
,
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
be the Liouville-Caputo fractional derivative with respect to
\displaystyle t\in[0,\infty)
,

D_{t}^{\alpha}h(t)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\left[h(s)-h(0)\right]ds,

where
\displaystyle\Gamma
is Gamma function, see
Kiryakova 1994
and
Kilbas et al. 2006
.

Denote by
\displaystyle\Omega=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},|x|\leq\varphi(x)\}
a bounded star-shaped domain with respect to a small ball centered at the origin and
\displaystyle\varphi(x)\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a positive homogeneous function of 0-th order.

We consider the parabolic problem with time fractional derivative

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&D_{t}^{\alpha}u-\hbox{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)-\mu W(x)|u|^{p-2}u=0,\hbox{ in }Q=(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
&u(t,x)=0,\hbox{ for }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\partial\Omega,\\
&u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\hbox{ for }x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},u_{0}(x)\in L^{2}(\Omega),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\displaystyle n>p
,
\displaystyle p>2
,
\displaystyle\mu>0
,
\displaystyle\Delta_{p}=\hbox{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)
is p-Laplacian and the potential
\displaystyle W(x)
is singular on the boundary
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
and at the origin

W(x)=|x|^{-p}\left[1-\left(\frac{|x|}{\varphi(x)}\right)^{\frac{n-p}{p}}\right]^{-p}.

(1.2)

The theory of fractional evolutionary PDEs is intensively investigated in the last decades. Fractional time derivative PDEs, including Laplace, p-Laplace, porous medium, mean curvature, Kirchhoff operator and slow diffusion are studied in
Dipierro et al. 2019
,
Tarasov 2013
and
Uchaikin 2000
. In
Allen et al. 2016
,
Djida et al. 2019
,
Dong and Kim 2021
,
Kubica and Yamamoto 2018
,
Li et al. 2014
,
Luchko 2009
,
Vergara and Zacher 2010
,
Vergara and Zacher 2015
existence and uniqueness of the solutions is proved, while in
Smadiyeva and Torebek 2023
optimal decay estimates are established.

Let us mention that evolutionary PDEs with fractional Laplace and p-Laplace operator are considered in
Mazón et al. 2016
,
Fu and Pucci 2016
,
Boudjeriou 2022
where existence of local solutions, global solvability and final time blow-up by means of the potential well method are proved.

The applications of fractional calculus appear in the theory of mechanics of fluids, probability and statistics, dynamical processes in self-similar and and porous structures, viscoelasticity, electrochemistry of corrosion, optic and signal processing and so on, see
Kilbas et al. 2006
. For example, in mathematical physics when describing dynamic processes in material with memory in the theory of heat conduction, models of anomalous diffusion, in the context of spatially disordered systems, porous media, fractional media, sites of micro-molecular crowding, stock price movements (
Ben-Avraham and Havlin 2000
,
Kiselev et al. 2007
,
Metzler and Klafter 2000
), magneto-thermoelastic heat conduction (

) and hydrodynamics (

).

As for classical parabolic problems with singular potentials, they are models in molecular physics (

), quantum cosmology (

), non-relativistic quantum mechanics (

) and others.

Global existence or finite time blow-up is proved for inverse-square potential in

,

and for more general singularities at an interior point in

,
Fernandez-Cara 1997

, while double singular potentials at an interior point and on the boundary are investigated in

The goal of the present paper is to extend the result for classical parabolic problems in the works mention above to time fractional evolutionary PDEs with p-Laplace operator and double singular potential. We prove finite time blow-up or global existence of the weak solutions to (
1.1
) and thresholds between them is the optimal Hardy constant, see Theorem 9.3 in

Note that for potential
\displaystyle W(x)
the following Hardy inequality holds

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\geq\Lambda(n,p)\int_{\Omega}W(x)|u|^{p}dx,

(1.3)

with optimal constant
\displaystyle\Lambda(n,p)=\left(\frac{n-p}{p}\right)^{p}
.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&D_{t}^{\alpha}u_{N}-\Delta_{p}u_{N}-\mu W_{N}(x)|u_{N}|^{p-2}u_{N}=0,\hbox{ in }Q,\\
&u_{N}(t,x)=0,\hbox{ for }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\partial\Omega,\\
&u_{N}(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\hbox{ for }x\in\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

for every positive integer
\displaystyle N\geq 1
, where
\displaystyle W_{N}(x)=\min\{N,W(x)\}
.

Definition 1.1
.

The function
\displaystyle u(t,x)
is a weak solution in distributional sense of (

u(t,x)\in C^{0}\left([0,\tau];L^{2}(\Omega)\right)\cap L^{p}\left([0,\tau];W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\right),\ \ \hbox{ for some }\tau>0,

and is a limit of the solutions
\displaystyle u_{N}(t,x)
of the truncated problem (
1.4
), for
\displaystyle N\rightarrow\infty
.

Definition 1.2
.

The solution of (
1.1
) defined in
\displaystyle[0,\tau)
does not blow-up at
\displaystyle\tau
if there exists a constant
\displaystyle C_{0}
such that

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{2}(Q_{\tau})}\leq C_{0},\quad\|u(t,x)\|_{W^{1,p}(Q_{\tau})}\leq C_{0}\hbox{ for }t\in[0,\tau),x\in\Omega,

(1.5)

where
\displaystyle Q_{\tau}=(0,\tau)\times\Omega

Let
\displaystyle\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle 0\in\Omega

Suppose

2
Time fractional ordinary differential equations

2.1
Comparison principle

Consider the ordinary fractional differential equation

D^{\alpha}_{t}w(t)=f(t,w(t)),\hbox{ for }t\in[0,T].

(2.1)

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
\displaystyle u(t),v(t)
are sub- and supersolutions of (
2.1
) and

\displaystyle|u(t)|\leq M,\quad|v(t)|\leq M,\hbox{ for }t\in[0,T]
,

D^{\alpha}_{t}u(t)\leq f(t,u(t)),\quad D^{\alpha}_{t}v(t)\geq f(t,v(t)),\hbox{ for }t\in[0,T],

(2.2)

|f_{\omega}(t,\omega)|\leq K,\quad\hbox{ for }t\in[0,T],|\omega|\leq M.

(2.3)

If
\displaystyle u(0)\leq v(0)
then

u(t)\leq v(t),\hbox{ for }t\in[0,T].

(2.4)

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that (
2.4
) fails. Define the function

z(t)=\left(u(t)-v(t)\right)_{+}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}&u(t)-v(t)&\hbox{ for }u(t)-v(t)>0,\\
&0&\hbox{ for }u(t)-v(t)\leq 0,\end{array}\right.

Then there exists
\displaystyle T_{0}
,
\displaystyle 0\leq T_{0}<T
,
\displaystyle T_{0}=\inf\{t;z(t)>0\}
and function
\displaystyle z(t)=0
for
\displaystyle t\in[0,T_{0}]
. Moreover, from (
2.2
)
\displaystyle D^{\alpha}_{t}z(t)
satisfies the inequality

D^{\alpha}_{t}z(t)\leq f(t,u(t))-f(t,v(t))\hbox{ for }t\in[0,T].

(2.5)

Since

f(t,u(t))-f(t,v(t))=\left(u(t)-v(t)\right)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\omega}\left(t,(1-\theta)v(t)+\theta u(t)\right)d\theta,

and from (
2.3
) and
\displaystyle|(1-\theta)v(t)+\theta u(t)|\leq M
,
\displaystyle|f(t,u(t))-f(t,v(t))|\leq Kz(t)
for
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
inequality (
2.5
) becomes

D^{\alpha}_{t}z(t)\leq Kz(t),z(0)=0.

Thus

D^{\alpha}_{t}z(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}z(s)ds\leq Kz(t),\hbox{ for }t\in[0,T],

(2.6)

and integrating (
2.6
) from
\displaystyle 0
to
\displaystyle T_{1}\in(T_{0},T]
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{T_{1}}(T_{1}-s)^{-\alpha}z(s)ds-\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}z(s)ds\\[1.0pt]
\\
\leq K\int_{0}^{T_{1}}z(s)ds.\end{array}

(2.7)

From

\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}z(s)ds\leq M\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}ds=M\frac{t^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha},

it follows that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}z(s)ds=0
and from (
2.7
)

\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{T_{0}}^{T_{1}}(T_{1}-s)^{-\alpha}z(s)ds=\int_{0}^{T_{1}}(T_{1}-s)^{-\alpha}z(s)ds\leq K\Gamma(1-\alpha)\int_{0}^{T_{1}}z(s)ds\\[1.0pt]
\\
=K\Gamma(1-\alpha)\int_{0}^{T_{1}}z(s)ds.\end{array}

Thus

(T_{1}-T_{0})^{-\alpha}\int_{T_{0}}^{T_{1}}z(s)ds\leq K\Gamma(1-\alpha)\int_{T_{0}}^{T_{1}}z(s)ds,

and for every
\displaystyle T_{1}<\min\left\{T,T_{0}+\left(K\Gamma(1-\alpha)\right)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right\}
we get

0<\left[(T_{1}-T_{0})^{-\alpha}-K\Gamma(1-\alpha)\right]\int_{T_{0}}^{T_{1}}z(s)ds\leq 0,

i.e.
\displaystyle z(t)=0
for
\displaystyle t\in[T_{0},T_{1}]
which contradict the choice of
\displaystyle T_{0}
.
∎

2.2
Blow-up solutions

Following
Shan and Lv 2024
we consider initial value problem for ordinary fractional differential equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&D^{\alpha}u(t)=u^{p}(t),\alpha\in(0,1),p>1,\hbox{ for }t>0,\\
&u(0)=u_{0}>0\end{array}\right.

(2.8)

The solution of (
2.8
) is equivalent to the solution of the integral equation, see
Kilbas et al. 2006

u(t)=u(0)+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}u^{p}(s)ds,\hbox{ for }t\geq 0,

(2.9)

Let us recall the comparison principle for integral equation (
2.9
).

Theorem 2.2

(Lemma 2.1 in
Lakshmikantham and Vatsala 2008
)
.

Let
\displaystyle v(t),w(t)\in C([0,T])
,
\displaystyle F\in C([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
and let
one of the following inequalities being strict.

\begin{array}[]{ll}&v(t)\leq v(0)+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}F(s,v(s))ds,\\[1.0pt]
\\
&w(t)\leq w(0)+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}F(s,w(s))ds.\end{array}

Suppose
\displaystyle F(t,x)
is non decreasing in
\displaystyle x
for every
\displaystyle t
and
\displaystyle v(0)<w(0)
.

Then
\displaystyle v(t)<w(t)
for
\displaystyle 0\leq t\leq T
.

The following theorem that we will use in the proof of Theorem
1.2
gives an existence of a blow-up solution of problem (
2.8
) and improves the result in
Shan and Lv 2024
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose
\displaystyle u_{0}
is strictly positive,
\displaystyle p>1
and
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then the solution of (
2.8
) blows-up for finite time
\displaystyle T_{m}<\infty
.

Proof.

For a constant
\displaystyle\delta>0
, which will be chosen later on, we consider the problem.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&w^{\prime}(t)=\frac{w^{p}(t)}{\Gamma(\alpha)(t+\delta)^{p(1-\alpha)}},\hbox{ for }t>0,\\
&w(0)=\frac{1}{2}\delta^{1-\alpha}u_{0}.\end{array}\right.

(2.10)

Integrating (
2.10
) we get

w(t)=w(0)+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{w^{p}(s)}{(s+\delta)^{p(1-\alpha)}}ds,\hbox{ for }t\geq 0,

(2.11)

as well as

\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{dw}{w^{p}}=\frac{dt}{\Gamma(\alpha)((t+\delta)^{p(1-\alpha)})},\\
\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dw}{w^{p}}=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)((s+\delta)^{p(1-\alpha)})}ds,\hbox{and}\\
\frac{1}{1-p}\left[w^{1-p}(t)-w^{1-p}(0)\right]\\
=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)(1-p(1-\alpha))}\left[(t+\delta)^{1-p(1-\alpha)}-\delta^{1-p(1-\alpha)}\right]\end{array}

Finally,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&w^{1-p}=F(t,\delta),\hbox{ for }p(1-\alpha)-1\neq 0,\\
&w^{1-p}=F_{0}(t,\delta),\hbox{ for }p(1-\alpha)-1=0,\end{array}\right.

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}&F(t,\delta)=w^{1-p}(0)+\frac{(p-1)\delta^{1-p(1-\alpha)}}{\Gamma(\alpha)[1-p(1-\alpha)]}-\frac{(p-1)(t+\delta)^{1-p(1-\alpha)}}{\Gamma(\alpha)[1-p(1-\alpha)]},\\
&F_{0}(t,\delta)=w^{1-p}(0)-\frac{p-1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\ln\frac{t+\delta}{\delta}.\end{array}

From (
2.9
) the function
\displaystyle v(t)=(t+\delta)^{1-\alpha}u(t)
satisfies the equality

v(t)=(t+\delta)^{1-\alpha}u_{0}+\frac{(t+\delta)^{1-\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}v^{p}(s)}{(s+\delta)^{p(1-\alpha)}}ds.

(2.12)

Since
\displaystyle 0\leq t-s\leq t<t+\delta
for
\displaystyle 0\leq s\leq t
, we get from (
2.11
), (
2.12
) the inequality

\begin{array}[]{ll}v(t)>&\delta^{1-\alpha}u_{0}+\frac{(t+\delta)^{1-\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}v^{p}(s)}{(s+\delta)^{p(1-\alpha)}}ds\\
&=2w(0)+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{v^{p}(s)}{(s+\delta)^{p(1-\alpha)}}ds.\end{array}

We will chose
\displaystyle\delta
in order to prove the existence of blow-up solution of (
2.8
).

Let us consider the cases

i)

\displaystyle 1-p(1-\alpha)\geq 0
,

i1)

\displaystyle 1-p(1-\alpha)>0
,

i2)

\displaystyle 1-p(1-\alpha)=0
,

ii)

\displaystyle 1-p(1-\alpha)<0
.

We will show that in both cases there exists
\displaystyle T_{m}<\infty
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{m},t<T_{m}}w(t)=\infty
.

In the case i1) and i2) we chose
\displaystyle\delta=1
.

In the case ii) denote
\displaystyle A=\frac{p-1}{\Gamma(\alpha)[p(1-\alpha)-1]}
and we chose

\displaystyle\delta=\left[\frac{3}{2}Aw^{p-1}(0)\right]^{\frac{1}{p(1-\alpha)-1}}>0
.

Indeed, in case i) we have
\displaystyle F(0,1)=F_{0}(0,1)=w^{1-p}(0)>0
and
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}F(t,1)=-\infty
as well as
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}F_{0}(t,1)=-\infty
. So there exists

\displaystyle T_{m}<\infty
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{m},t<T_{m}}w^{1-p}(t)=0
because
\displaystyle p>1
and hence
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{m},t<T_{m}}w(t)=\infty
.

In the case i1)
\displaystyle F(T_{m},1)=0
means

w^{1-p}(0)+\frac{p-1}{\Gamma(\alpha)[1-p(1-\alpha)]}=\frac{(p-1)(T_{m}+1)^{1-p(1-\alpha)}}{\Gamma(\alpha)[1-p(1-\alpha)]},

(T_{m}+1)^{1-p(1-\alpha)}=w^{1-p}(0)\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)[1-p(1-\alpha)]}{p-1}+1,

and

T_{m}=\left(w^{1-p}(0)\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)[1-p(1-\alpha)]}{p-1}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{p(1-\alpha)-1}}-1.

(2.13)

Formulae (
2.13
) is the same as in
Shan and Lv 2024
for the case i1)

In the case i2) solving the equation
\displaystyle F_{0}(T_{m},1)=0
we obtain

T_{m}=e^{w^{1-p}(0)\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{p-1}}-1.

In the case ii) we have that
\displaystyle F(T_{m},\delta)=0
if

\frac{1}{w^{p-1}(0)}-\frac{A}{\delta^{p(1-\alpha)-1}}=\frac{A}{(T_{m}+\delta)^{p(1-\alpha)-1}}.

(2.14)

With
\displaystyle\delta=\left(\frac{3Aw^{p-1}(0)}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p(1-\alpha)-1}}
, we get
\displaystyle\frac{1}{w^{p-1}(0)}=\frac{3A}{2\delta^{p(1-\alpha)-1}}
and from (
2.14
)

\frac{A}{2\delta^{p(1-\alpha)-1}}=\frac{A}{(T_{m}+\delta)^{p(1-\alpha)-1}}.

then
\displaystyle T_{m}=\left(2^{\frac{1}{p(1-\alpha)-1}}-1\right)\delta
and finally

T_{m}=\left(2^{\frac{1}{p(1-\alpha)-1}}-1\right)\left(\frac{3(p-1)w^{p-1}(0)}{2\Gamma(\alpha)[p(1-\alpha)-1]}\right)^{\frac{1}{p(1-\alpha)-1}},

so
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{m},t<T_{m}}w(t)=\infty
.

From the comparison principle, Theorem
2.2
, for (
2.11
) and (
2.13
) it follows that

v(t)>w(t),\hbox{ for }t\in[0,T_{m}),

or equivalently

u(t)\geq(t+\delta)^{\alpha-1}w(t),\hbox{ for }t\in[0,T_{m}).

Hence, from what is proved above in both cases i) and ii) we obtain

\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{m},t<T_{m}}u(t)=\infty.

∎

3
Time fractional parabolic problems

3.1
Comparison principle

We consider the problem

D^{\alpha}_{t}w-\Delta_{p}w=f(x)|w|^{p-2}w,\quad\hbox{ for }t\in[0,T],x\in\Omega,

(3.1)

where
\displaystyle\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\alpha\in(0,1),\quad p>2,\hbox{ and }|f(x)|\leq C,\hbox{ for }x\in\Omega.

(3.2)

Theorem 3.1

(Comparison principle)
.

Suppose

u(t,x),v(t,x)\in C^{0}\left([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)\right)\cap L^{p}\left([0,T];W_{0}^{1,p}\right),

satisfy the inequality

D^{\alpha}_{t}u-\Delta_{p}u-f(x)|u|^{p-2}u\leq\partial^{\alpha}_{t}v-\Delta_{p}v-f(x)|v|^{p-2}v,\quad\hbox{ for }t\in[0,T],x\in\Omega.

If (
3.2
) holds and
\displaystyle u(0,x)\leq v(0,x)
for
\displaystyle x\in\Omega
then

u(t,x)\leq v(t,x)\quad\hbox{ for }t\in[0,T],x\in\Omega.

(3.3)

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that (
3.3
) fails. Then the function

z(t,x)=\left(u(t,x)-v(t,x)\right)_{+}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}&u(t,x)-v(t,x)&\hbox{ for }u(t,x)-v(t,x)>0,\\
&0&\hbox{ for }u(t,x)-v(t,x)\leq 0,\end{array}\right.,

satisfies the inequality

D^{\alpha}_{t}z-\Delta_{p}u+\Delta_{p}v-f(x)\left(|u|^{p-2}u-|v|^{p-2}v\right)\leq 0,

(3.4)

for
\displaystyle(t,x)\in Q_{+}=\left\{(t,x);t\in[0,T],x\in\Omega_{+}(t)\right\}
and ,

\displaystyle\Omega_{+}(t)=\left\{x\in\Omega,u(t,x)>v(t,x)\right\}
.
Let us recall the so called fundamental identity, proved in the next Lemma.

Lemma 3.1

(Lemma 4.1 in
Zacher 2019
)
.

Let
\displaystyle T>0
,
\displaystyle J=(0,T)
and
\displaystyle U
be an open subset of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
. Let further
\displaystyle k\in H^{1}_{1}(J)
,
\displaystyle H\in C^{1}(U)
, and
\displaystyle u\in L^{1}(J)
with
\displaystyle u(t)\in U
for a.a.
\displaystyle t\in J
.Suppose that the functions
\displaystyle H(u),H^{\prime}(u)u
and
\displaystyle H^{\prime}(u)(k\ast u)
belong to
\displaystyle L^{1}(J)
. Then we have for a.a.
\displaystyle t\in J
the equality

\begin{array}[]{l}H^{\prime}(u(t))\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}(k\ast u)(s)ds\\
=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}(k\ast H(u))(s)ds+\left(-H(u(t))+H^{\prime}(u(t))\right)k(t)\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\left(H(u(t-s))-H(u(t))-H^{\prime}(u(t))[u(t-s)-u(t)]\right)[-k(s)]ds.\end{array}

(3.5)

From the fundamental identity (
3.5
) for
\displaystyle k=g_{1-\alpha}(t)=\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}
and
\displaystyle H(z)=z^{2}(t,x)
we get

\begin{array}[]{l}2z(t,x)\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}z(s,x)ds\\
=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}z^{2}(s,x)ds+\left[-z^{2}(t,x)+2z^{2}(t,x)\right]g_{1-\alpha}(t)\\
-\int_{0}^{t}\left[z^{2}(t-s,x)-z^{2}(t,x)-2z(t,x)\left(z(t-s,x)-z(t,x)\right)\right]\frac{d}{ds}g_{1-\alpha}(s)ds\\
=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}z^{2}(s,x)ds+\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}z^{2}(t,x)\\
+\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\alpha-1}\left[z(t-s,x)-z(t,x)\right]^{2}ds.\end{array}

(3.6)

Multiplying (
3.4
) with
\displaystyle z(t,x)
and integrating in
\displaystyle\Omega
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}z(t,x)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}z^{2}(s,x)dsdx\\
+\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}\nabla z\left[|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\right]dx\\
-\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}f(x)\left[|u|^{p-2}u-|v|^{p-2}v\right]z(t,x)dx\leq 0\end{array}

Simple computation give us

\begin{array}[]{l}\left[|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\right]\nabla z=\frac{1}{2}\left[|\nabla v|^{p-2}+|\nabla u|^{p-2}\right]|\nabla(u-v)|^{2}\\
+\frac{1}{2}\left[|\nabla u|^{p-2}+|\nabla v|^{p-2}\right]\left[|\nabla u|^{2}-|\nabla v|^{2}\right]\geq 0,\end{array}

0\leq\left(|u|^{p-2}u-|v|^{p-2}v\right)z\leq(p-1)(v-u)^{2}\left(|v|+|u|\right)^{p-2},

and hence

\begin{array}[]{l}0\leq\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}\left[|u(t,x|^{p-2}u(t,x)-|v(t,x)|^{p-2}v(t,x)\right]z(t,x)dx\\
\leq(p-1)\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}|z(t,x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}(|v|^{p}+|u|^{p})dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\\
\leq(p-1)2^{\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}(|v|^{p}+|u|^{p})dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}|z(t,x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}.\end{array}

(3.7)

If

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\Omega}|u(t,x)|^{p}dx\leq M,\quad\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\Omega}|v(t,x)|^{p}dx\leq M,

then (
3.7
) becomes

\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}\left[|u|^{p-2}u-|v|^{p-2}v\right]z(t,x)dx\\
\leq(p-1)2^{\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{p}}(2M)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}|z(t,x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\\
=(p-1)2^{p-2}M^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}|z(t,x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}.\end{array}

(3.8)

From (
3.6
) - (
3.8
) and
\displaystyle z(0,x)\equiv 0
we get the inequalities

\begin{array}[]{l}0\geq\frac{1}{2}D^{\alpha}_{t}\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}z^{2}(t,x)dx-\frac{1}{2}D^{\alpha}_{t}\int_{\Omega_{+}(0)}z^{2}(0,x)dx\\
+\frac{1}{2\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}t^{-\alpha}z^{2}(t,x)dx\\
+\frac{\alpha}{2\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\alpha-1}\left[z(t-s,x)-z(t,x)\right]^{2}dsdx\\
+\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}\left[|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\right]\nabla zdx\\
-C\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}\left[|u|^{p-2}u-|v|^{p-2}v\right]zdx\\
\geq\frac{1}{2}D^{\alpha}_{t}\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}z^{2}(t,x)dx+\frac{1}{2\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}t^{-\alpha}z^{2}(t,x)dx\\
+\frac{\alpha}{2\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\alpha-1}\left[z(t-s,x)-z(t,x)\right]^{2}dsdx\\
+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}\left[\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}+|\nabla v|^{p-2}\right)|\nabla z|^{2}\right.\\
\left.+\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\right)\left(|\nabla u|^{2}-|\nabla v|^{2}\right)\right]dx\\
-C(p-1)2^{p-2}M^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}|z(t,x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\\
\geq\frac{1}{2}D^{\alpha}_{t}\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}z^{2}(t,x)dx-K_{1}\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}|z(t,x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}},\end{array}

(3.9)

where

K_{1}=C(p-1)2^{p-2}M^{\frac{p-2}{p}}.

(3.10)

Since

\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}|z(t,x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}=\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}z^{2}(t,x)dx\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}|z(t,x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\\
\times\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}|z(t,x)|^{2}dx\right)^{-1}=\theta\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}|z(t,x)|^{2}dx\end{array}

(3.11)

where
\displaystyle\theta(z)=\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}|z(t,x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}|z(t,x)|^{2}dx\right)^{-1}
, is a homogeneous function of 0-th order, i.e.
\displaystyle\theta(\lambda z)=\theta(z)
for every
\displaystyle\lambda\neq 0
we have

0\leq\theta(z)\leq C_{1}=|\Omega_{+}(\delta)|^{\frac{p-2}{p}}.

Here
\displaystyle C_{1}=\theta(\delta)
for some
\displaystyle\delta\in(0,T]
such that
\displaystyle\Omega_{+}(\delta)\neq 0
.

Thus, from (
3.10
), (
3.11
), inequality (
3.9
) becomes for
\displaystyle w(t)=\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}|z(t,x)|^{2}dx
,

D^{\alpha}_{t}w(t)\leq 2K_{1}C_{1}w(t)\hbox{ for }t\in[0,T],w(0)=0.

Since

0\leq\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}z^{2}(t,x)dx\leq\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}|z|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}(t)}1dx\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\leq|\Omega_{+}(t)|^{\frac{p-2}{p}}M,

(3.12)

and (
3.2
) holds, from Theorem
2.1
for
\displaystyle u(t)=w(t),v(t)=0
, it follows that
\displaystyle w(t)\leq 0
, i.e.
\displaystyle z(t,x)=0
for
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
,
\displaystyle x\in\Omega
which contradicts our assumption.
∎

3.2
Apriori estimates

In this section we consider equation (
3.1
) with initial and boundary data

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&D^{\alpha}_{t}u_{N}-\Delta_{p}u=f_{N}(x)|u_{N}|^{p-2}u_{N},\quad\hbox{ for }t\in[0,T],x\in\Omega,\\
&u_{N}(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\hbox{ for }x\in\Omega,\\
&u_{N}(t,x)=0,\hbox{ for }t\in[0,T],x\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(3.13)

where
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\displaystyle n>p>2
,
\displaystyle f_{N}(x)=\mu W_{N}(x)=\mu\min\{N,W(x)\},N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Here
\displaystyle W(x)
is defined in (
1.2
) and
\displaystyle\mu<\Lambda(n,p)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

There exist constants
\displaystyle A_{1}
,
\displaystyle A_{2}
and the weak solution
\displaystyle u_{N}(t,x)
of (
3.13
) satisfies the estimates

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{N}(t,x)|^{p}dtdx\leq A_{1},\quad\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|u_{N}(t,x)|^{p}dtdx\leq A_{2}.

(3.14)

Proof.

From (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and (
3.6
) for
\displaystyle z=u_{N}(t,x)
we get

\begin{array}[]{l}0=2u_{N}(t,x)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}u_{N}(s,x)ds\\
-2u_{N}(t,x)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}u_{0}(x)ds-2u(t,x)\Delta_{p}u_{N}(t,x)\\
+2u_{N}(t,x)D^{\alpha}_{t}u_{0}(x)-2u_{N}(t,x)\mu W_{N}(x)|u_{N}(t,x|^{p-2}u_{N}(t,x)\\
=D^{\alpha}_{t}u^{2}_{N}(t,x)+2u^{2}_{N}(t,x)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}u^{2}_{0}(x)ds\\
+\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}u^{2}_{N}(t,x)+\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\alpha-1}\left[u_{N}(t-s,x)-u_{N}(t,x)\right)^{2}ds\\
-2u_{N}(t,x)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}u_{0}(x)ds\\
-2u_{N}(t,x)\Delta_{p}u_{N}(t,x)-2u(t,x)\mu W_{N}(x)|u_{N}(t,x|^{p-2}u_{N}(t,x).\end{array}

(3.15)

Integrating (
3.15
) in
\displaystyle\Omega
we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}0=D_{t}^{\alpha}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{N}(t,x)dx+\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{N}(t,x)dx\\
+\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}s^{-\alpha-1}\left[u_{N}(t-s,x)-u_{N}(t,x)\right]^{2}dsdx\\
+2\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{N}(t,x)|^{p}dx-2\mu\int_{\Omega}W_{N}(x)|u_{N}(t,x)|^{p}dx\\
+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}u^{2}_{0}(x)dsdx\\
-2u_{N}(t,x)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}u_{0}(x)dsdx\end{array}

(3.16)

Since
\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}u^{j}_{0}(x)ds=\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}u_{0}^{j}(x)
,
\displaystyle j=1,2
and

\begin{array}[]{l}2\left|\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{N}(t,x)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}u_{0}(x)dxds\right|=\frac{2t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left|\int_{\Omega}u_{N}(t,x)u_{0}(x)dx\right|\\
\leq\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{2\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{N}(t,x)dx+\frac{2t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{0}(x)dx\end{array}

we get from (
3.16
) and Hardy inequalities (
1.3
)

\begin{array}[]{l}0\geq D^{\alpha}_{t}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{N}(t,x)dx+\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{2\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{N}(t,x)dx+2\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{N}(t,x)|^{p}dx\\
-2(\mu+\varepsilon)\Lambda^{-1}(n,p)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{N}(t,x)|^{p}dx\\
+2\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}W_{N}(x)|u_{N}(t,x)|^{p}dx-\frac{3t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{2}(x)dx,\end{array}

(3.17)

where

0<\varepsilon<\min\left(\omega_{0},\Lambda(n,p)-\mu\right),\quad\omega_{0}=\min_{\Omega}W(x)>0.

Integrating (
3.17
) on
\displaystyle t
from
\displaystyle 0
to
\displaystyle T
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}0\geq\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{N}(s,x)dsdxdt\\
+\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{N}(t,x)|^{p}dxdt+2\varepsilon\omega_{0}\int_{\Omega}|u_{N}(t,x)|^{p}dxdt\\
+\frac{1}{2\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{T}t^{-\alpha}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{N}(t,x)dxdt-\frac{3}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{T}t^{-\alpha}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{0}(x)dxdt,\end{array}

(3.18)

where
\displaystyle\gamma=2\left[1-(\mu+\varepsilon)C^{-1}\right]>0
.

From(
3.12
) it follows that

0\leq\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{N}(t,x)dx\leq M|\Omega|^{\frac{p-2}{p}}=C_{2}<\infty\hbox{ fof }t\in[0,T],

and hence

\begin{array}[]{l}0\leq\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{1-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{N}(t,x)dsdx\\
\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}ds=\frac{C_{2}}{(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}t^{1-\alpha}=0.\end{array}

Thus (
3.18
) becomes

\begin{array}[]{l}0\geq\int_{0}^{T}\frac{(T-s)^{1-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{N}(s,x)dsdx+\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{N}(t,x)|^{p}dxdt\\
+\frac{1}{2\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{T}t^{-\alpha}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{N}(t,x)dxdt+2\varepsilon\omega_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|u_{N}(t,x)|^{p}dxdt\\
-\frac{T^{1-\alpha}}{(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{2}(x)dx\\
\geq\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{N}(t,x)|^{p}dxdt+2\varepsilon\omega_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|u_{N}(t,x)|^{p}dxdt\\
-\frac{3T^{1-\alpha}}{(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{2}(x)dx.\end{array}

and hence we obtain (
3.14
) with
\displaystyle A_{1}=\frac{C_{3}}{\gamma}
,
\displaystyle A_{2}=\frac{C_{3}}{2\varepsilon\omega_{0}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}

and
\displaystyle C_{3}=\frac{3T^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{2}(x)dx
.
∎

4
Proof of the main results

In this section we prove global boundedness or finite time blow-up of the solutions in distributional sense of the singular fractional parabolic problem (
1.1
)

Proof of theorem
1.1
.

From the appriori estimate (
3.14
) it follows that the solution
\displaystyle u_{N}(t,x)
of problem (
1.4
) satisfies (
1.5
). After the limit
\displaystyle N\rightarrow\infty
in (
3.14
) by means of Theorem 4.1 in
Boccardo and Murat 1992
the limit solution
\displaystyle u(t,x)
fulfilled (
1.5
) and does not blow-up at
\displaystyle T_{m}
.
∎

Proof of theorem
1.2
.

We suppose by contradiction that
\displaystyle u(t,x)
is globally defined for every
\displaystyle t>0
. Hence
\displaystyle u_{N}(t,x)
is globally defined for every
\displaystyle t>0
. If
\displaystyle\lambda_{N}
is the first eigenvalue of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta_{p}v(x)=\lambda_{N}W_{N}(x)|v(x)|^{p-2}v(x)\hbox{ for }x\in\Omega,\\
v(x)=0\hbox{ for }x\in\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.,

then from Lemma 9.2 in
Kutev and Rangelov 2022
we have

\lambda_{N}\geq\Lambda(n,p),\quad\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{N}=\Lambda(n,p),

and problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta_{p}X(x)-\mu W_{N}(x)|X(x)|^{p-2}X(x)=-X(x)\hbox{ for }x\in\Omega,\\
X(x)=0\hbox{ for }x\in\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.,

has a positive solution.

From Theorem
2.3
problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}D^{\alpha}_{t}T(t)=T^{p-1}(t),t>0,\\
T(0)=T_{0}>0\end{array}\right.,

has a positive solution which blows-up for finite time
\displaystyle T_{max}
. We chose
\displaystyle T_{0}=\varepsilon>0
where
\displaystyle\varepsilon
is small enough constant such that

\varepsilon X(x)<u_{0}(x)\hbox{ for }x\in\Omega.

Since
\displaystyle v(t,x)=T(t)X(x)
is a positive subsolution of problem (
1.4
) for
\displaystyle t\in(0,T_{max})
from the comparison principle Theorem
2.2
it follows that

u_{N}(t,x)\geq T(t)X(x),\hbox{ for }x\in\Omega,t>0.

Hence
\displaystyle u_{N}(t,x)
blows-up at
\displaystyle t=T_{max}
and the solution
\displaystyle u(t,x)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}u_{N}(t,x)
of (
1.1
) blows-up at
\displaystyle t=T_{max}
.
∎
