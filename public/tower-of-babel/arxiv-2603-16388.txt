An analytic function

of

is univalent in
N(z_{0})
. The Jacobian of a complex-valued function

is defined by
J_{f}(z)=|f_{z}|^{2}-|f_{\overline{z}}|^{2}

It is well-known that the non-vanishing of the Jacobian is necessary and sufficient conditions for local univalence of analytic mappings (see
[
13

Let

f

\mathbb{D}

in

has the following Taylor series expansion:

\displaystyle f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}.

(1.1)

Let

be the class of all analytic functions
\omega:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
and

Functions in

Strict inequality holds in both estimates unless

Lemma 1.1

[

Let

\displaystyle\left|\omega^{\prime}(z_{0})-\frac{\omega(z_{0})}{z_{0}}\right|\leq\frac{|z_{0}|^{2}-\left|\omega(z_{0})\right|^{2}}{|z_{0}|(1-|z_{0}|^{2})}.

The equality occurs if, and only if,

[

Suppose

\displaystyle\frac{\left|f^{(n)}(z)\right|}{n!}\leq\frac{1-|f(z)|^{2}}{(1-|z|)^{n-1}(1-|z|^{2})}\quad\text{and}\quad|a_{n}|\leq 1-|a_{0}|^{2}\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 1,~~|z|<1.

The Dieudonné’s lemma is an improvement of the Schwarz’s lemma as well as the Schwarz-Pick lemma. A Blaschke product of degree

\displaystyle f(z)=e^{i\theta}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{z-z_{j}}{1-\overline{z_{j}}z},\quad z,z_{j}\in\mathbb{D},~\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

One of the most important and useful tools in geometric function theory is the differential subordination technique. With the help of differential subordination, many problems in geometric function theory can be solved in a simple and precise manner.
An analytic function
f
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
is said to be subordinate to an analytic function

\mathbb{D}

Using the notion of subordination, Ma and Minda
[

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}:\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\varphi(z)\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{C}(\varphi)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}:1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec\varphi(z)\right\},

1.1.
Pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of analytic functions

For a locally univalent analytic function
f
defined in a simply connected domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
, the pre-Schwarzian derivative
P_{f}
and the Schwarzian derivative
S_{f}
of
f
are, respectively, defined as follows:

\displaystyle P_{f}(z)=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{f}(z)=P_{f}^{\prime}(z)-\frac{1}{2}P_{f}^{2}(z)=\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{2}.

(1.3)

Moreover, the pre-Schwarzian and the Schwarzian norms of
f
are, respectively, given by

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f}(z)|\quad\text{and}\quad\|S_{f}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|.

(1.4)

Some important global univalence criteria for a locally univalent analytic function have been obtained using the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms.
For a univalent analytic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
, it is well-known that
\|P_{f}\|\leq 6
and the equality
is attained for the Koebe function or its rotation.
One of the most used univalence criterion for locally univalent analytic functions is the Becker’s univalence criterion
[
7
]
,
which states that if
f
is a locally univalent analytic function and
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\left|zP_{f}(z)\right|\leq 1
, then
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. In a subsequent study,
Becker and Pommerenke
[
8
]
have proved that the constant
1
is sharp.
In 1976, Yamashita
[
43
]

proved that
\|P_{f}\|
is finite if, and only if,
f
is uniformly locally univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, if
\|P_{f}\|<2
, then
f
is bounded in
\mathbb{D}
(see
[
22
]
).

In terms of the Schwarzian derivative, it is well-known that for any univalent analytic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
, we have the sharp inequality
\|S_{f}\|\leq 6
and
the equality is attained for the Koebe function or its rotation (see
[
24
]
).
In 1949, Nehari
[
30
]
established important criteria for global univalence, expressed in terms of the Schwarzian derivative, by virtue of the connection with linear differential equations.
For instance, if
f
is locally univalent and analytic in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
and satisfies
\|S_{f}\|\leq 2
, then
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. The bound

is sharp
[

. This result is known as Nehari’s criterion for univalence.

f_{1}(z)
defined by

\displaystyle f_{1}(z)=\dfrac{1-(1-z)^{\gamma e^{-i\theta}\cos\theta+1}}{\gamma e^{-i\theta}\cos\theta+1}.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}(\gamma)
, where
|\theta|<\pi/2
and
\gamma>0
. By the definition of the class
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}(\gamma)
and from (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec\frac{1-e^{-i\theta}\left(\gamma\cos\theta+e^{i\theta}\right)z}{1-z}.

Thus, there exist a Schwarz function
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{1-e^{-i\theta}\left(\gamma\cos\theta+e^{i\theta}\right)\omega(z)}{1-\omega(z)},

i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{\left(1-e^{-i\theta}\left(\gamma\cos\theta+e^{i\theta}\right)\right)\omega(z)}{z(1-\omega(z))}.

For simplicity, let
\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)=-e^{-i\theta}\gamma\cos\theta
.
Therefore, the Schwarzian derivative for the function
f\in S_{\theta}(\gamma)
is

\displaystyle S_{f}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)\left(\frac{z\omega^{\prime}(z)-\omega(z)}{z^{2}(1-\omega(z))^{2}}+\frac{(2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta))\omega^{2}(z)}{2z^{2}(1-\omega(z))^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)\left(\frac{\omega^{\prime}(z)-\omega(z)/z}{z(1-\omega(z))^{2}}+\frac{(2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta))\omega^{2}(z)}{2z^{2}(1-\omega(z))^{2}}\right).

Let us consider the transformation
h(z)=\omega^{\prime}(z)-\omega(z)/z
. In view of Lemma
1.1
, the function
h(z)
varies over the closed disk

\displaystyle|h(z)|\leq\frac{|z|^{2}-|\omega(z)|^{2}}{|z|\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)}\quad\text{ for fixed}\quad|z|<1.

(2.1)

Using (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle|S_{f}(z)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)\right|\left|\left(\frac{h(z)}{z(1-\omega(z))^{2}}+\frac{(2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta))\omega^{2}(z)}{2z^{2}(1-\omega(z))^{2}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)\right|\left(\frac{|h(z)|}{|z|(1-|\omega(z)|)^{2}}+\frac{|2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)||\omega(z)|^{2}}{2|z|^{2}(1-|\omega(z)|)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)\right|\left(\frac{|z|^{2}-|\omega(z)|^{2}}{|z|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})(1-|\omega(z)|)^{2}}+\frac{|2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)||\omega(z)|^{2}}{2|z|^{2}(1-|\omega(z)|)^{2}}\right).

For
0<t:=|\omega(z)|\leq|z|<1
, we have

\displaystyle|S_{f}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)\right|\left(\frac{|z|^{2}-t^{2}}{|z|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})(1-t)^{2}}+\frac{|2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)|t^{2}}{2|z|^{2}(1-t)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)\right|\left(\frac{2|z|^{2}-2t^{2}+|2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)|(1-|z|^{2})t^{2}}{2|z|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})(1-t)^{2}}\right).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle|S_{f}(z)|\leq\left|\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)\right|F_{1}(|z|,t),

(2.2)

where

\displaystyle F_{1}(r,t)=\frac{2r^{2}+\left(|2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)|(1-r^{2})-2\right)t^{2}}{2r^{2}(1-r^{2})(1-t)^{2}}\quad\text{for}\quad r=|z|.

The objective is to determine the maximum of
F_{1}(r,t)
on
\Omega=\{(r,t):0<t\leq r<1\}
.
Differentiating partially
F_{1}(r,t)
with respect to
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}F_{1}(r,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4r^{2}+2\left(|2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)|(1-r^{2})-2\right)t^{2}}{2r^{2}(1-r^{2})(1-t)^{3}}+\frac{2\left(|2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)|(1-r^{2})-2\right)t}{2r^{2}(1-r^{2})(1-t)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4r^{2}+2\left(|2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)|(1-r^{2})-2\right)t^{2}+2\left(|2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)|(1-r^{2})-2\right)t(1-t)}{2r^{2}(1-r^{2})(1-t)^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2r^{2}+\left(|2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)|(1-r^{2})-2\right)t}{r^{2}(1-r^{2})(1-t)^{3}}.

Note that
|2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)|=\sqrt{4+(\gamma^{2}+4\gamma)\cos^{2}\theta}>2
for all
\gamma>0
and
|\theta|<\pi/2
. For
\gamma>0
and
|\theta|<\pi/2
, we have

\displaystyle|2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)|(1-r^{2})-2>-2r^{2}~~\text{and}~~2r^{2}+\left(|2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)|(1-r^{2})-2\right)t>2r^{2}(1-t)>0

for all
(r,t)\in\Omega
.
Thus, we have
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}F_{1}(r,t)\geq 0
for all
(r,t)\in\Omega
, which shows that
F_{1}(r,t)
is a monotonically increasing function of
t\in(0,r]
. Consequently,
F_{1}(r,t)\leq F_{1}(r,r)
and hence, we have

\displaystyle|S_{f}|\leq\frac{\left|\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)\right|\left(|2-\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)|\right)}{2(1-r)^{2}}=\frac{\gamma\cos\theta\sqrt{4+(\gamma^{2}+4\gamma)\cos^{2}\theta}}{2(1-r)^{2}}.

To show that the estimate is sharp, we consider the function
f_{1}
given by

\displaystyle f_{1}(z)=\dfrac{1-(1-z)^{\gamma e^{-i\theta}\cos\theta+1}}{\gamma e^{-i\theta}\cos\theta+1}\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}(\gamma).

(2.3)

The Schwarzian derivative of
f_{1}
is given by

\displaystyle S_{f_{1}}(z)=\left(\frac{f_{1}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f_{1}^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{f_{1}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f_{1}^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{2}=-\frac{\left(\gamma e^{-i\theta}\cos\theta+2\right)\gamma e^{-i\theta}\cos\theta}{2(1-z)^{2}},

i.e.,

\displaystyle\left|S_{f_{1}}(z)\right|=\frac{\left|\gamma e^{-i\theta}\cos\theta+2\right|\left|\gamma e^{-i\theta}\cos\theta\right|}{2|1-z|^{2}}.

On the positive real axis, we note that

\displaystyle\left|S_{f_{1}}(z)\right|=\frac{\gamma\cos\theta\sqrt{4+(\gamma^{2}+4\gamma)\cos^{2}\theta}}{2(1-|z|)^{2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}(\gamma)
, then in view of Theorem
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|zP_{f}(z)\right|=\left|\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|=\left|\frac{-e^{-i\theta}\gamma\cos\theta~\omega(z)}{1-\omega(z)}\right|\leq\frac{\gamma\cos\theta~|\omega(z)|}{1-|\omega(z)|}\leq\frac{\gamma\cos\theta~|z|}{1-|z|}.

Thus, we have
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\left|zP_{f}(z)\right|\leq 2\gamma\cos\theta
.
According to Becker’s univalence criterion, the function
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}(\gamma)
is univalent in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
for those values of
\gamma>0
and
|\theta|<\pi/2
such that the condition
2\gamma\cos\theta\leq 1
holds. In the case of
\theta=0
, the functions belonging to the class
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}(\gamma)
are univalent in

\mathbb{D}
, provided that
\gamma\leq 1/2
. It is evident that as
|\theta|\to\pi/2
, the range of values for
\gamma
increases.

In the following result, we establish the sharp estimate of the Schwarzian norm for functions
f
in the class
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}(\gamma)
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
|\theta|<\pi/2
and
\gamma>0
, let
f
be in the class
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}(\gamma)
. Then the Schwarzian norm satisfies the following inequality

\displaystyle\|S_{f}\|\leq 2\gamma\cos\theta\sqrt{4+(\gamma^{2}+4\gamma)\cos^{2}\theta}

and the estimate is best possible.

Proof.

As
f
is in the class
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}(\gamma)
for
|\theta|<\pi/2
and
\gamma>0
, in view of Theorem
2.1
, the Schwarzian derivative of
f
satisfies the following inequality

\displaystyle|S_{f}|\leq\frac{\gamma\cos\theta\sqrt{4+(\gamma^{2}+4\gamma)\cos^{2}\theta}}{2(1-|z|)^{2}}.

Therefore, the Schwarzian norm of
f
is

\displaystyle\|S_{f}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)^{2}\left|S_{f}(z)\right|\leq 2\gamma\cos\theta\sqrt{4+(\gamma^{2}+4\gamma)\cos^{2}\theta}.

To show that the estimate is sharp, we consider the function
f_{1}
given in (
2.3
) and thus, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)^{2}\left|S_{f_{1}}(z)\right|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)^{2}\frac{\gamma\cos\theta\sqrt{4+(\gamma^{2}+4\gamma)\cos^{2}\theta}}{2|1-z|^{2}}.

On the positive real axis, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq z<1}\left(1-z^{2}\right)^{2}\frac{\gamma\cos\theta\sqrt{4+(\gamma^{2}+4\gamma)\cos^{2}\theta}}{2(1-z)^{2}}=2\gamma\cos\theta\sqrt{4+(\gamma^{2}+4\gamma)\cos^{2}\theta}.

This completes the proof.
∎

In the following result, we obtain the estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm for functions in the class
\mathcal{HC}_{\theta}(\gamma)
.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
|\theta|<\pi/2
and
\gamma>0
, let
f=h+\overline{g}
be in the class
\mathcal{HC}_{\theta}(\gamma)
. Then the pre-Schwarzian norm satisfies the following inequality

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|\leq 1+2\gamma\cos\theta.

and the estimate is best possible whenever
\theta=0
.

Proof.

As
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{HC}_{\theta}(\gamma)
, thus,
h
satisfies the following condition

\displaystyle\textrm{Re}\left(e^{i\theta}\left(1+\frac{zh^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}\right)\right)<\left(1+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\cos\theta\quad\text{for}~z\in\mathbb{D},~\gamma>0~\text{and}~|\theta|<\frac{\pi}{2}.

In view of (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zh^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}\prec\frac{1-e^{-i\theta}\left(\gamma\cos\theta+e^{i\theta}\right)z}{1-z}.

Thus, there exist a Schwarz function
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)\omega(z)}{z(1-\omega(z))}.

where
\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)=-e^{-i\theta}\gamma\cos\theta
. For
0<t:=|\omega(z)|\leq|z|<1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}\right|\leq\frac{\gamma t\cos\theta}{|z|(1-t)}:=G_{1}(|z|,t).

Differentiating partially
G_{1}(|z|,t)
with respect to
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G_{1}(|z|,t)=\frac{\gamma\cos\theta}{|z|(1-t)^{2}}>0,

which shows that
G_{1}(|z|,t)
is a monotonically increasing function
t\in(0,|z|]
and hence, we have
G_{1}(|z|,t)\leq G_{1}(|z|,|z|)
,
i.e.,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}\right|\leq\frac{\gamma\cos\theta}{1-|z|}.

Therefore, the pre-Schwarzian norm for the function
f\in\mathcal{HC}_{\theta}(\gamma)
is

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{h^{\prime}}-\frac{\overline{\omega}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\right|,

where
\omega=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
is the dilatation of
f
. Since
\omega\in\text{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, in view of the Schwarz-Pick lemma and triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\left(\left|\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{h^{\prime}}\right|+\left|\frac{\overline{\omega}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\left(\frac{\gamma\cos\theta}{1-|z|}+\frac{|\overline{\omega}|}{1-|z|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(\gamma\cos\theta(1+|z|)+|\omega|\right)=1+2\gamma\cos\theta.

To prove the sharpness of the result, we consider the function
f_{2}=h_{2}+\overline{g_{2}}\in\mathcal{H}
with dilatation
\omega(z)=(a-z)/(1-az)
,
a\in(0,1)
and
h_{2}
be such that

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zh_{2}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h_{2}^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{1-e^{-i\theta}\left(\gamma\cos\theta+e^{i\theta}\right)z}{1-z}.

It is evident that
f_{2}\in\mathcal{HC}_{\theta}(\gamma)
and
\omega^{\prime}(z)=(a^{2}-1)/(1-az)^{2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle 1-|\omega(z)|^{2}=1-\frac{(a-z)}{(1-az)}\frac{(a-\overline{z})}{(1-a\overline{z})}=\frac{(1-a^{2})(1-|z|^{2})}{(1-az)(1-a\overline{z})}

and

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\omega}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}=\frac{(a^{2}-1)}{(1-az)^{2}}\cdot\frac{(a-\overline{z})}{(1-a\overline{z})}\cdot\frac{(1-az)(1-a\overline{z})}{(1-a^{2})(1-|z|^{2})}=\frac{\overline{z}-a}{(1-az)(1-|z|^{2})}.

Therefore, the pre-Schwarzian norm of
f_{1}
is

\displaystyle\|P_{f_{2}}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{h^{\prime}}-\frac{\overline{\omega}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)}{1-z}-\frac{\overline{z}-a}{(1-az)(1-|z|^{2})}\right|,

where
\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)=-e^{-i\theta}\gamma\cos\theta
. Let
\theta=0
, then
\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)=-\gamma
.
On the positive real axis, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq r<1}(1-r^{2})\left|\frac{\Gamma(\gamma,\theta)}{1-r}-\frac{r-a}{(1-ar)(1-r^{2})}\right|=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\left|\gamma(1+r)+\frac{r-a}{(1-ar)}\right|.

Let
F_{2}(r):=\gamma(1+r)+(r-a)/(1-ar)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle F_{2}^{\prime}(r)=\gamma+\frac{1-a^{2}}{(1-ar)^{2}}\geq 0,

which shows that
F_{2}(r)
is a monotonically increasing function of
r\in[0,1)
and hence, we have

\|P_{f_{2}}\|=\sup_{0\leq r<1}F_{2}(r)=2\gamma+1
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{HC}_{\theta}(\gamma)
. Then, in view of Theorem
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}\right|\leq\frac{\gamma\cos\theta}{1-|z|},\quad\text{which shows that}\quad\|P_{h}\|\leq 2\gamma\cos\theta.

Using the function given in (
2.3
), we easily show that the estimation is sharp.
Hence, we have
\left|\|P_{f}\|-\|P_{h}\|\right|\leq 1
for functions
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{HC}_{\theta}(\gamma)
and equality occurs when
\theta=0
.
Therefore, the class of functions
\mathcal{HC}_{\theta}(\gamma)
supports the result of Liu and Ponnusamy
[
28
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
|\theta|<\pi/2
and
\gamma>0
, let
f=h+\overline{g}
be in the class
\mathcal{HC}_{\theta}(\gamma)
. Then the Schwarzian norm satisfies the following inequality

\displaystyle\|S_{f}\|\leq 2\gamma\cos\theta\sqrt{4+(\gamma^{2}+4\gamma)\cos^{2}\theta}+2\gamma\cos\theta+11/2.

Proof.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{HC}_{\theta}(\gamma)
, then in view of Theorem
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zh^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}\prec\frac{1-e^{-i\theta}\left(\gamma\cos\theta+e^{i\theta}\right)z}{1-z}.

Using the argument as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we obtain the Schwarzian derivative of
h
satisfies the following inequality

\displaystyle|S_{h}|\leq\frac{\gamma\cos\theta\sqrt{4+(\gamma^{2}+4\gamma)\cos^{2}\theta}}{2(1-|z|)^{2}}.

Again, in view of Theorem
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}\right|\leq\frac{\gamma\cos\theta}{1-|z|}.

Therefore, the Schwarzian derivative of
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{HC}_{\theta}(\gamma)
is

\displaystyle S_{f}=S_{h}+\frac{\overline{\omega}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\left(\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{h^{\prime}}\omega^{\prime}-\omega^{\prime\prime}\right)-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\omega^{\prime}\overline{\omega}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\right)^{2}

i.e.,

\displaystyle|S_{f}(z)|\leq|S_{h}|+\left|\frac{\overline{\omega}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\left(\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{h^{\prime}}\omega^{\prime}-\omega^{\prime\prime}\right)\right|+\left|\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\omega^{\prime}\overline{\omega}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\right)^{2}\right|.

Since
\omega\in\text{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, in view of Lemma
1.2
, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\frac{\left|\omega(z)\omega^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}\leq 2(1+|z|)|\omega(z)|.

In view of the Schwarz-Pick lemma, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{h}(z)|+(1-|z|^{2})\frac{\left|\overline{\omega(z)}\omega^{\prime}(z)\right|}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}\cdot(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}\right|

\displaystyle+(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\frac{\left|\overline{\omega(z)}\omega^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}+\frac{3}{2}\left((1-|z|^{2})\frac{|\omega^{\prime}(z)\overline{\omega(z)}|}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+|z|)^{2}\frac{\gamma\cos\theta\sqrt{4+(\gamma^{2}+4\gamma)\cos^{2}\theta}}{2}+(1+|z|)|\omega(z)|\gamma\cos\theta

\displaystyle+2(1+|z|)|\omega(z)|+\frac{3}{2}|\omega(z)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+|z|)^{2}\frac{\gamma\cos\theta\sqrt{4+(\gamma^{2}+4\gamma)\cos^{2}\theta}}{2}+(1+|z|)|z|\gamma\cos\theta

\displaystyle+2(1+|z|)|z|+\frac{3}{2}|z|^{2}.

Therefore, the Schwarzian norm of
f\in\mathcal{HC}_{\theta}(\gamma)
is

\displaystyle\|S_{f}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\;(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|\leq 2\gamma\cos\theta\sqrt{4+(\gamma^{2}+4\gamma)\cos^{2}\theta}+2\gamma\cos\theta+11/2.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

The bound in Theorem 2.4 is not necessarily sharp. Finding the sharp bound for
\|S_{f}\|
in
\mathcal{HC}_{\theta}(\gamma)

Corollary 2.3
.

For
|\theta|<\pi/2
and
\gamma>0
, let
f=h+\overline{g}
be in the class
\mathcal{HC}_{\theta}(\gamma)
such that
g^{\prime}(z)/h^{\prime}(z)=z
. Then the Schwarzian norm satisfies the following inequality

\displaystyle\|S_{f}\|\leq 2\gamma\cos\theta\sqrt{4+(\gamma^{2}+4\gamma)\cos^{2}\theta}+2\gamma\cos\theta+3/2.

Proof.

Using argument as in the proof of Theorem
2.4
, we obtain the Schwarzian derivative of
f=h+\overline{g}
as follows:

\displaystyle S_{f}=S_{h}+\frac{\overline{z}}{1-|z|^{2}}\cdot\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\overline{z}}{1-|z|^{2}}\right)^{2}

i.e.,

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|\leq(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{h}|+|z|\cdot(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}\right|+\frac{3}{2}|z|^{2}.

Therefore, the Schwarzian norm of
f=h+\overline{g}

\displaystyle\|S_{f}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\;(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|\leq 2\gamma\cos\theta\sqrt{4+(\gamma^{2}+4\gamma)\cos^{2}\theta}+2\gamma\cos\theta+3/2.
