Abstract
. In this article, we derive explicit combinatorial formulas, depending only on q q , for the Wedderburn decomposition of the rational group algebras of the finite linear groups SL 2 ⁡ ( q ) \operatorname{SL}_{2}(q) and PSL 2 ⁡ ( q ) \operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q) . Furthermore, we also determine the number of pairwise non-isomorphic simple ℚ ​ G \mathbb{Q}G -modules of each possible dimension for G G being either SL 2 ⁡ ( q ) \operatorname{SL}_{2}(q) or PSL 2 ⁡ ( q ) \operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q) .

primary 20C05; secondary 20G15, 20G05

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a group, and let
\mathbb{F}
be a number field. Then the
group algebra
of
G
over
\mathbb{F}
is

\mathbb{F}G=\{\,f:G\to\mathbb{F}\,:\,f(g)=0\text{ for all but finitely many }g\in G\,\},

where addition, scalar multiplication, and multiplication are defined by

(f+h)(g)=f(g)+h(g),\,\,\,\,(af)(g)=a\,f(g),\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,(fh)(g)=\sum_{xy=g}f(x)h(y)

for
f,h\in\mathbb{F}G
,
a\in\mathbb{F}
, and
g\in G
, respectively. Note that
\mathbb{F}G
is a free
\mathbb{F}
-module with basis
G
. Describing group algebras is a classical problem in algebra, allowing many approaches. When considering semisimple algebras, i.e., the case of a finite group over a field whose characteristic does not divide the group order, this involves describing the Wedderburn components of the group algebra. By the Wedderburn–Artin theorem, a semisimple group algebra
\mathbb{F}G
decomposes as a direct sum of matrix algebras over division rings

\mathbb{F}G\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathrm{M}_{n_{i}}(D_{i}),

where each
\mathrm{M}_{n_{i}}(D_{i})
is a full matrix algebra of size
n_{i}
over a division ring
D_{i}
,
which is
finite-dimensional over its center. These are called the
simple components
of
\mathbb{F}G
. Moreover, by the Brauer–Witt theorem
[
25
]
, each simple component is Brauer equivalent to a cyclotomic algebra. The study of the Wedderburn decomposition of rational group algebras has received significant attention due to its importance in understanding various algebraic structures (see
[
12
,
15
,
22
]
).

This decomposition can be achieved explicitly using character-theoretic methods or by exploiting subgroup structures via Shoda pairs for certain classes of rational group algebras, in particular for rational group algebras of monomial groups. See the works on rational group algebras based on Shoda pair theory presented in
[
19
,
2
,
1
,
17
,
10
,
18
]
, which have been done in recent years. Computational approaches, such as the
Wedderga
package in
GAP

[
9
]
, implement these methods to compute the Wedderburn decomposition of rational group algebras of such groups; however, exact computations remain challenging, especially for groups of large order.

For finite abelian groups, Perlis and Walker
[
20
]
provided a combinatorial formula for the Wedderburn decomposition of their rational group algebras based on counting cyclic subgroups. For several classes of finite
p
-groups, the first author and Prajapati have developed combinatorial techniques to determine the Wedderburn decomposition explicitly, either by reducing to abelian sections or using parameters from group presentations, as detailed in
[
4
,
3
,
6
,
5
,
7
,
8
]
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denote the finite field of order
q
, where
q=p^{m}
for some prime
p
and positive integer
m
. Let
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
and
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)
denote the groups of
2\times 2
matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with determinant
1
and their projective version, respectively, i.e.,

\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)=\{A\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\,:\,\det(A)=1\}\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)/Z(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)),

where
Z(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q))
is the center of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
. In this article, we derive combinatorial formulas for the Wedderburn decomposition of the rational group algebras of the finite classical matrix groups
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
and
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)
.

For a positive integer
d
, let
\zeta_{d}
denote a primitive
d
-th root of unity in
\mathbb{C}
. Note that
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
for
q=2^{m}
. Theorem
1
determines a formula, depending only on
q
, for the Wedderburn decomposition of the rational group algebras of
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
, where
q
is a power of
2
.

1
.

Let
G=\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
, where
q
is a power of
2
. Suppose that

A=\{\,2<d\leq q+1\,:\,d\mid q+1\,\}\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,B=\{\,2<d\leq q-1\,:\,d\mid q-1\,\}.

Then the Wedderburn decomposition of
\mathbb{Q}G
is given by

\mathbb{Q}G\cong\mathbb{Q}\bigoplus_{d\in A}\mathrm{M}_{q-1}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1}))\bigoplus\mathrm{M}_{q}(\mathbb{Q})\bigoplus_{d\in B}\mathrm{M}_{q+1}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1})).

Next, we prove Theorem
2
, which provides a formula, depending only on
q
, for the Wedderburn decomposition of the rational group algebra of
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)
, where
q
is a power of an odd prime
p
.

2
.

Let
G=\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)
, where
q
is a power of an odd prime
p
. Suppose that

A=\left\{\,2<d\leq\tfrac{q+1}{2}\,:\,d\mid\tfrac{q+1}{2}\,\right\}\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,B=\left\{\,2<d\leq\tfrac{q-1}{2}\,:\,d\mid\tfrac{q-1}{2}\,\right\}.

Then we have the following.

(1)

Case (
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
).
In this case, the Wedderburn decomposition of
\mathbb{Q}G
is given by

\mathbb{Q}G\cong\mathbb{Q}\bigoplus\mathrm{M}_{\frac{q-1}{2}}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-q}))\bigoplus_{d\in A}\mathrm{M}_{q-1}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1}))\bigoplus\mathrm{M}_{q}(\mathbb{Q})\bigoplus_{d\in B}\mathrm{M}_{q+1}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1})).

(2)

Case (
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
).
In this case, we have the following two subcases.

(a)

Subcase (
q
is not a square)
. In this subcase, the Wedderburn decomposition of
\mathbb{Q}G
is given by

\mathbb{Q}G\cong\mathbb{Q}\bigoplus\mathrm{M}_{\frac{q+1}{2}}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q}))\bigoplus_{d\in A}\mathrm{M}_{q-1}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1}))\bigoplus\mathrm{M}_{q}(\mathbb{Q})\bigoplus_{d\in B}\mathrm{M}_{q+1}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1})).

(b)

Subcase (
q
is a square)
. In this subcase, the Wedderburn decomposition of
\mathbb{Q}G
is given by

\mathbb{Q}G\cong\mathbb{Q}\bigoplus 2\mathrm{M}_{\frac{q+1}{2}}(\mathbb{Q})\bigoplus_{d\in A}\mathrm{M}_{q-1}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1}))\bigoplus\mathrm{M}_{q}(\mathbb{Q})\bigoplus_{d\in B}\mathrm{M}_{q+1}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1})).

A quaternion algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
with
\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{F})\neq 2
is a central simple algebra of dimension
4
, denoted
(a,b)_{\mathbb{F}}
, generated by elements
i,j
satisfying

i^{2}=a,\quad j^{2}=b,\quad ij=-ji,\quad a,b\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}.

If
q
is a power of
2
, then
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)=\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)
. Thus, with the above setup of division rings, we finally prove Theorem
3
, which provides a formula depending only on
q
for the Wedderburn decomposition of the rational group algebra of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
, where
q
is a power of an odd prime
p
.

3
.

Let
G=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)
, where
q
is a power of an odd prime
p
. Suppose that

\displaystyle A=\left\{\,2<d\leq\tfrac{q+1}{2}\,:\,d\mid\tfrac{q+1}{2}\,\right\},

\displaystyle A^{\prime}=\left\{\,2<d\leq q+1\,:\,d\mid(q+1),\ d\nmid\tfrac{q+1}{2}\,\right\},

\displaystyle B=\left\{\,2<d\leq\tfrac{q-1}{2}\,:\,d\mid\tfrac{q-1}{2}\,\right\},\,\,\,\,\text{and}

\displaystyle B^{\prime}=\left\{\,2<d\leq q-1\,:\,d\mid(q-1),\ d\nmid\tfrac{q-1}{2}\,\right\}.

Furthermore, let us define the division algebras
D_{d}\cong(\zeta_{d}-\zeta_{d}^{-1},-q)_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1})}
,
D^{\prime}_{d}\cong(\zeta_{d}-\zeta_{d}^{-1},-1)_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1})}
,
D^{\prime}\cong(-1,-1)_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q})}
, and

D^{\prime\prime}\cong\begin{cases}(-1,-p)_{\mathbb{Q}}&\text{ for }\,\,\,\,q=p^{2m}\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,p\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\
(-\ell,-p)_{\mathbb{Q}}&\text{ for }\,\,\,\,q=p^{2m}\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,p\equiv 3\pmod{4},\end{cases}

where
\ell
is a prime satisfying
\ell\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and the Legendre symbol
\left(\dfrac{\ell}{p}\right)
takes value
-1
. Then we have the following.

(1)

Case (
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
).
In this case, the Wedderburn decomposition of
\mathbb{Q}G
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}G\cong

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}\bigoplus\mathrm{M}_{q}(\mathbb{Q})\bigoplus\mathrm{M}_{\frac{q-1}{2}}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-q}))\bigoplus_{d\in A^{\prime}}\mathrm{M}_{\frac{q-1}{2}}(D_{d})\bigoplus_{d\in A}\mathrm{M}_{q-1}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1}))

\displaystyle\bigoplus\mathrm{M}_{\frac{q+1}{2}}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-q}))\bigoplus_{d\in B^{\prime}}\mathrm{M}_{\frac{q+1}{2}}(D_{d})\bigoplus_{d\in B}\mathrm{M}_{q+1}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1})).

(2)

Case (
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
).
In this case, we have the following two subcases.

(a)

Subcase (
q
is not a square)
. In this subcase, the Wedderburn decomposition of
\mathbb{Q}G
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}G\cong

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}\bigoplus\mathrm{M}_{q}(\mathbb{Q})\bigoplus\mathrm{M}_{\frac{q-1}{4}}(D^{\prime})\bigoplus_{d\in A^{\prime}}\mathrm{M}_{\frac{q-1}{2}}(D_{d})\bigoplus_{d\in A}\mathrm{M}_{q-1}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1}))

\displaystyle\bigoplus\mathrm{M}_{\frac{q+1}{2}}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q}))\bigoplus_{d\in B^{\prime}}\mathrm{M}_{\frac{q+1}{2}}(D_{d})\bigoplus_{d\in B}\mathrm{M}_{q+1}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1})).

(b)

Subcase (
q
is a square)
. In this subcase, the Wedderburn decomposition of
\mathbb{Q}G
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}G\cong

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}\bigoplus\mathrm{M}_{q}(\mathbb{Q})\bigoplus 2\mathrm{M}_{\frac{q-1}{4}}(D^{\prime\prime})\bigoplus_{d\in A^{\prime}}\mathrm{M}_{\frac{q-1}{2}}(D_{d})\bigoplus_{d\in A}\mathrm{M}_{q-1}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1}))

\displaystyle\bigoplus 2\mathrm{M}_{\frac{q+1}{2}}(\mathbb{Q})\bigoplus_{d\in B^{\prime}}\mathrm{M}_{\frac{q+1}{2}}(D_{d})\bigoplus_{d\in B}\mathrm{M}_{q+1}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d}+\zeta_{d}^{-1})).

\mathbb{C}

2.1.
Schur theory

Let
G
be a finite group. Define an equivalence relation on
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
by Galois conjugacy over
\mathbb{Q}
. Two irreducible characters
\chi,\psi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
are called
Galois conjugates
over
\mathbb{Q}
if there exists
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\chi)/\mathbb{Q})
such that
\chi^{\sigma}=\psi
, where
\chi^{\sigma}(g)=\sigma(\chi(g))
for each
g\in G
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
13
, Lemma 9.17]

Let
G
be a finite group and
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
. Denote by
E(\chi)
the Galois conjugacy class of
\chi
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then

|E(\chi)|=[\mathbb{Q}(\chi):\mathbb{Q}].

Let
G
be a finite group, and let
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
. Then Schur theory implies that there exists an irreducible
\mathbb{Q}
-representation
\rho
of
G
that affords the character
\Omega(\chi)
. Moreover, we have

\rho(1)=\Omega(\chi)(1)=m_{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)\,[\mathbb{Q}(\chi):\mathbb{Q}]\,\chi(1).

It follows that distinct Galois conjugacy classes correspond to distinct irreducible rational representations of
G
. Reiner
[
21
, Theorem 3]
further describes the structure of the simple components in the Wedderburn decomposition of the rational group algebra
\mathbb{Q}G
associated with these rational representations. By the direct sum of representations, we mean the standard construction given by the block-diagonal sum in matrix form.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
21
, Theorem 3]

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic zero and let
\overline{\mathbb{K}}
denote its algebraic closure. Suppose
T
is an irreducible
\mathbb{K}
-representation of
G
, extended linearly to a
\mathbb{K}
-representation of
\mathbb{K}G
. Set

A=\{T(x):x\in\mathbb{K}G\}.

Then
A
is a simple
\mathbb{K}
-algebra and hence is isomorphic to
M_{n}(D)
for some positive integer
n
and some division algebra
D
over
\mathbb{K}
.

Moreover, after extension of scalars to
\overline{\mathbb{K}}
, the representation
T
decomposes as a direct sum

T\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\overline{\mathbb{K}}\;\cong\;m_{\mathbb{K}}(\chi)\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{K}(\chi)/\mathbb{K})}U^{\sigma},

where
U
is an irreducible
\overline{\mathbb{K}}
-representation of
G
with character
\chi
,

U^{\sigma}
affords the character
\chi^{\sigma}
, and

m_{\mathbb{K}}(\chi)
denotes the Schur index of
\chi
over
\mathbb{K}
.
In this case, the center of
D
satisfies

Z(D)\cong\mathbb{K}(\chi),

and the degree of
D
over its center is given by

[D:Z(D)]=\bigl(m_{\mathbb{K}}(\chi)\bigr)^{2}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

be a prime power with

A=\{\,1<d\leq q+1\,:\,d\mid q+1\,\}
and
B=\{\,1<e\leq q-1\,:\,e\mid q-1\,\}

where

denotes the number of integers in
[1,m]
coprime to
m

Proof.

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exist
d\in A
and

such that

(1)

\frac{(q-1)\varphi(d)}{2}=\frac{(q+1)\varphi(e)}{2}.

Let
g=\gcd(q-1,q+1)
. Then for a prime power
q=p^{m}
, we have

g=\begin{cases}1&q\text{ even},\\
2&q\text{ odd}.\end{cases}

Write
q-1=ga
and
q+1=gb
with
\gcd(a,b)=1
. Equation (
1
) becomes

a\varphi(d)=b\varphi(e).

Since
\gcd(a,b)=1
, there exists an integer
k\geq 1
such that
\varphi(d)=bk
and
\varphi(e)=ak
. Because
\varphi(d)<d\leq q+1=gb
and
\varphi(e)<e\leq q-1=ga
, we obtain
bk<gb
and
ak<ga
, hence
k<g
. If
g=1
, this is impossible since
k\geq 1
. Thus no solutions exist in this case. Therefore,
g=2
, so
q
is odd and
k=1
. Hence,
\varphi(d)=\frac{q+1}{2}
and
\varphi(e)=\frac{q-1}{2}
. Note that
\varphi(n)=n/2
if and only if
n=2^{r}
for some positive integer
r
. In this way, we obtain
d=q+1=2^{k}
for some positive integer
k
and
e=q-1=2^{k^{\prime}}
for some positive integer
k^{\prime}
which is impossible since they differ by
2
.
This contradiction shows that no such
d
and
e
exist. Therefore,
S_{A}\cap S_{B}=\varnothing
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

, where

is a power of an odd prime

\tau(d)

\displaystyle A=\left\{\,2<m\leq\tfrac{q+1}{2}\,:\,m\mid\tfrac{q+1}{2}\,\right\},

\displaystyle A^{\prime}=\left\{\,2<m\leq q+1\,:\,m\mid(q+1),\ m\nmid\tfrac{q+1}{2}\,\right\},

\displaystyle B=\left\{\,2<m\leq\tfrac{q-1}{2}\,:\,m\mid\tfrac{q-1}{2}\,\right\},\,\,\,\,\text{and}

\displaystyle B^{\prime}=\left\{\,2<m\leq q-1\,:\,m\mid(q-1),\ m\nmid\tfrac{q-1}{2}\,\right\}.

Then the following statements hold.

(1)

There is a unique trivial
\mathbb{Q}G
-module of dimension
1
.

(2)

There is a unique (up to isomorphism) Steinberg
\mathbb{Q}G
-module of dimension
q
.

(3)

For each
d\in A
, there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) simple
\mathbb{Q}G
-module of dimension
\frac{q-1}{2}\varphi(d)
, and these modules are pairwise non-isomorphic for distinct
d
.

(4)

For each
d\in A^{\prime}
, there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) simple
\mathbb{Q}G
-module of dimension
(q-1)\varphi(d)
, and these modules are pairwise non-isomorphic for distinct
d
, and are non-isomorphic to any modules arising from part (3).

(5)

For each
d\in B
, there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) simple
\mathbb{Q}G
-module of dimension
\frac{q+1}{2}\varphi(d)
, and these modules are pairwise non-isomorphic for distinct
d
.

(6)

For each
d\in B^{\prime}
, there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) simple
\mathbb{Q}G
-module of dimension
(q+1)\varphi(d)
, and these modules are pairwise non-isomorphic for distinct
d
, and are non-isomorphic to any modules arising from part (5).

(7)

The remaining simple
\mathbb{Q}G
-modules arising from the principal series are as follows, which are non-isomorphic to any modules arising from parts (3)-(6):

•

If
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) simple
\mathbb{Q}G
-module of dimension
2(q-1)
.

•

If
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and
q
is not a square, there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) simple
\mathbb{Q}G
-module of dimension
2(q-1)
.

•

If
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and
q
is a square, there exist two (up to isomorphism) simple
\mathbb{Q}G
-modules of dimension
q-1
.

(8)

The remaining simple
\mathbb{Q}G
-modules arising from the complementary series are as follows, which are non-isomorphic to any modules arising from parts (3)-(7):

•

If
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) simple
\mathbb{Q}G
-module of dimension
q+1
.

•

If
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and
q
is not a square, there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) simple
\mathbb{Q}G
-module of dimension
q+1
.

•

If
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and
q
is a square, there exist two (up to isomorphism) simple
\mathbb{Q}G
-modules of dimension

.

(9)

|\mathrm{Irr}_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)|=\begin{cases}\tau(q-1)+\tau(q+1)+2&\text{if }q\equiv 1\pmod{4}\text{ and }q\text{ is a square},\\[4.30554pt]
\tau(q-1)+\tau(q+1)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

From Table
2-1

\displaystyle=[\mathbb{Q}(\psi^{(j)}_{q-1}):\mathbb{Q}]

(q+1)

Similarly, for each such

\tfrac{q+1}{2}

\operatorname{SL}_{2}(q)

Observe that Schur theory ensures the existence of a unique irreducible representation

D^{\prime\prime}\cong\begin{cases}(-1,-p)_{\mathbb{Q}}&\text{ for }\,\,\,\,q=p^{2m}\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,p\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\
(-\ell,-p)_{\mathbb{Q}}&\text{ for }\,\,\,\,q=p^{2m}\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,p\equiv 3\pmod{4},\end{cases}

where
\ell
is a prime satisfying
\ell\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and the Legendre symbol
\left(\dfrac{\ell}{p}\right)
takes value
-1
. Then by Lemma
4.3
, the simple components in the Wedderburn decomposition of
\mathbb{Q}G
corresponding to the irreducible
\mathbb{Q}
-representations of
G
affording the character
\Omega(\chi)
with
\chi(1)=\frac{q-1}{2}
are isomorphic to
2\mathrm{M}_{\frac{q-1}{4}}(D^{\prime\prime})
.

The remaining part of the proof proceeds by arguments analogous to those in the previous case. Finally, in both subcases when
q
is not a square and when
q
is a square, by collecting the simple components corresponding to all inequivalent irreducible
\mathbb{Q}
-representations of
G
affording the character
\Omega(\chi)
with
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, we obtain the respective result in each subcase.

This completes the proof of Theorem
3
.
