A Fourier spectral method for the cutoff Boltzmann equation: Convergence analysis and numerical simulation
Yanzhi Gui, Ling-Bing He, Liu Liu

Abstract
. This work addresses a central challenge in the numerical analysis of the cutoff spatially homogeneous Boltzmann equation: the development of rigorously justified, accurate numerical schemes. We present (i) a novel Fourier spectral method for the equation with Maxwellian and hard potentials, (ii) the derivation of the first rigorous error estimates for the proposed schemes. Comprehensive numerical experiments validate the theory, confirming the predicted accuracy and illustrating the method’s capability to capture solution dynamics, including the approach to equilibrium. The study thus provides a complete framework–from theoretical analysis to practical implementation–for the reliable computation of solutions to this foundational kinetic model.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35Q20, 65M15, 65M70, 65M12, 35R09, 82C40

1.
Introduction

The main purpose of this work is to conduct a thorough numerical investigation of the spatially homogeneous Boltzmann equation for two important classes of interactions: Maxwellian potentials and hard potentials with angular cutoff. This involves:

1.

The development of a novel numerical approximation method tailored to these potential types;

2.

Rigorous error estimates for the proposed numerical scheme, establishing its accuracy and reliability;

3.

Implementation of simulations to illustrate the method’s performance and to explore quantitative properties of solutions.

This study is significant because it advances the computational toolkit for the Boltzmann equation—a fundamental model in kinetic theory that describes the evolution of particle systems through binary collisions. Obtaining accurate numerical solutions is essential for analyzing complex phenomena in rarefied gas dynamics, plasma physics, and other areas of statistical physics where analytical solutions are scarce or unavailable. By developing a novel numerical method with rigorous error analysis, this work provides a reliable framework for simulating the behavior and fundamental properties of solutions, thereby bridging theoretical kinetic theory with practical computational science.

1.1.
A brief review on the Boltzmann equation

The Boltzmann equation, formulated by Ludwig Boltzmann in the 1870s, governs the time evolution of the particle distribution function
f(t,x,v)
in phase space, representing the probability density of finding a particle at position
x
with velocity
v
at time
t
. In the absence of external forces and spatial inhomogeneity, the spatially homogeneous Boltzmann equation reads:

(1.1)

\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(t,v)=Q(f,f)(t,v),\quad v\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where the collision operator
Q
is a bilinear integral operator that accounts for binary elastic collisions between particles. It can be expressed in its gain-loss form as:

Q(g,f)(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}B(|v-v_{*}|,\cos\theta)\big[g(v^{\prime}_{*})f(v^{\prime})-g(v_{*})f(v)\big]\,d\sigma\,dv_{*}=:Q^{+}(g,f)-Q^{-}(g,f).

The term
Q^{+}(g,f)
represents the gain term, modeling particles that enter the velocity state
v
after a collision, while
Q^{-}(g,f)
represents the loss term, modeling particles that leave velocity
v
due to a collision.

Several remarks are in order to clarify the components of this operator:

(1)

Here
v,v_{*}
are the pre-collision velocities of two particles, and
v^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{*}
are their corresponding post-collision velocities. These are determined by the conservation laws of momentum and kinetic energy for elastic collisions. More precisely, they satisfy the following parametric relations:

v^{\prime}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\frac{\left|v-v_{*}\right|}{2}\sigma,\quad v_{*}^{\prime}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}-\frac{\left|v-v_{*}\right|}{2}\sigma,\quad\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},

where the unit vector
\sigma
parameterizes all possible post-collision directions for a given relative velocity.

(2)

The angle
\theta
is the deflection angle, defined by the relation
\cos\theta:=\frac{v-v_{*}}{|v-v_{*}|}\cdot\sigma
, where
\cdot
denotes the Euclidean dot product. This angle characterizes the scattering direction.

(3)

The function
B
is the collision kernel or cross-section, which encodes the microscopic details of the interaction potential between particles. It is a non-negative function of the relative speed
|v-v_{*}|
and the scattering angle
\theta
. In this work, we consider a specific class of kernels often used in theoretical and numerical studies:

B\left(\left|v-v_{*}\right|,\cos\theta\right)=\Phi(|v-v_{*}|)\,b(\cos\theta),\quad\text{with}\quad\Phi(|v-v_{*}|)=|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}\,\text{and}\,b\equiv 1.

where
\gamma
is a parameter in
[0,1]
. The function
b(\cos\theta)
, which governs the angular scattering, is typically assumed to be integrable over the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
(this is known as Grad’s angular cut-off assumption). For simplicity in our numerical analysis and simulations, we often take the isotropic case
b\equiv 1
.

Remark 1.1
.

Two key families of collision kernels considered in this work, which fall under the general form
\Phi(|v-v_{*}|)b(\cos\theta)
, are:

•

Maxwellian molecules:
In this idealized case, the kernel’s dependence on relative speed vanishes. We have
\gamma=0
, so that
\Phi(|v-v_{*}|)=1
. Thus,
B=b(\cos\theta)
, independent of the relative speed.

•

Hard-sphere model:
This corresponds to the case
\gamma=1
, where the collision frequency is proportional to the relative speed. Thus, the kernel is
B=|v-v_{*}|
. We note that the standard hard-sphere model also has a specific angular dependence
b(\cos\theta)\propto|\cos\theta|
, but under the cut-off assumption,
b
is taken to be an integrable function, often a constant for simplicity. Models with
\gamma\in(0,1)
are typically referred to as
hard potentials
(with cut-off).

The Maxwellian case (
\gamma=0
) often admits analytical simplifications, while the hard-sphere/hard potential cases (
\gamma>0
) present greater numerical challenges due to the velocity dependence of the collision frequency, making them crucial benchmarks for any proposed numerical method.

The fundamental properties of the spatially homogeneous Boltzmann equation underpin both its physical relevance and the mathematical challenges it presents. These properties are summarized as follows:

1.1.1.
Conservation Laws

Due to the microscopic conservation of mass, momentum, and energy during binary elastic collisions, solutions to the Boltzmann equation formally conserve these macroscopic quantities. For any solution
f(t,v)
with initial data
f_{0}(v)
, the following moments remain constant in time:

(1.2)

\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}f(t,v)\,\varphi(v)\,dv=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}f_{0}(v)\,\varphi(v)\,dv\quad\text{for the collision invariants}\quad\varphi(v)=1,v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},\frac{|v|^{2}}{2},

where
\varphi(v)=1,v_{i},\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}
correspond to mass, the three components of momentum, and kinetic energy, respectively. This conservation law can be derived directly from the weak formulation of the collision operator. For any suitable test function
\varphi(v)
, we have:

(1.3)

\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}Q(f,f)(v)\,\varphi(v)\,dv=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}B\left(\left|v-v_{*}\right|,\sigma\right)ff_{*}\big(\varphi(v^{\prime})+\varphi(v^{\prime}_{*})-\varphi(v)-\varphi(v_{*})\big)\,d\sigma\,dv_{*}\,dv.

The right-hand side vanishes identically precisely when
\varphi(v)
is a linear combination of the five collision invariants
1,v,|v|^{2}
, due to the microscopic conservation laws
\varphi(v^{\prime})+\varphi(v^{\prime}_{*})=\varphi(v)+\varphi(v_{*})
.

1.1.2.
Entropy Structure and H-Theorem

The Boltzmann equation exhibits a profound irreversible thermodynamic structure. Defining the macroscopic hydrodynamic fields associated with the distribution
f(t,v)
—namely, the density
\rho_{f}
, bulk velocity
u_{f}
, and temperature
T_{f}
—we have:

(1.4)

\rho_{f}(t):=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}f(t,v)\,dv,\quad u_{f}(t):=\frac{1}{\rho_{f}(t)}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}v\,f(t,v)\,dv,\quad T_{f}(t):=\frac{1}{3\rho_{f}(t)}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}|v-u_{f}(t)|^{2}\,f(t,v)\,dv.

These quantities are conserved according to (
1.2
). Given these fields, we can define the local Maxwellian (or Gibbs equilibrium state):

(1.5)

\mu_{\rho_{f},u_{f},T_{f}}(v)=\frac{\rho_{f}}{(2\pi T_{f})^{3/2}}\,\exp\left({-\frac{|v-u_{f}|^{2}}{2T_{f}}}\right),

which shares the same mass, momentum, and energy as
f
.

The system’s irreversibility is captured by the
relative entropy
(or Boltzmann
H
-functional relative to the local Maxwellian):

(1.6)

H(f|\mu_{\rho_{f},u_{f},T_{f}})(t):=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\left[f(t,v)\log\left(\frac{f(t,v)}{\mu_{\rho_{f},u_{f},T_{f}}(v)}\right)-f(t,v)+\mu_{\rho_{f},u_{f},T_{f}}(v)\right]\,dv.

This functional is non-negative and serves as a measure of how far the distribution
f
is from its local equilibrium
\mu_{\rho_{f},u_{f},T_{f}}
.

Boltzmann’s celebrated
H-Theorem
states that for solutions of the homogeneous equation, this relative entropy is non-increasing:

(1.7)

\frac{d}{dt}H(f|\mu_{\rho_{f},u_{f},T_{f}})(t)=-D(f(t,\cdot))\leq 0,

where
D(f)
is the
entropy dissipation rate
, defined by:

(1.8)

D(f):=-\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}Q(f,f)(v)\,\log f(v)\,dv\geq 0.

Using the symmetries of the collision operator,
D(f)
can be rewritten in the following symmetric and revealing form:

D(f)=\frac{1}{4}\iint_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}B\left(\left|v-v_{*}\right|,\sigma\right)\left(f^{\prime}f^{\prime}_{*}-ff_{*}\right)\log\frac{f^{\prime}f^{\prime}_{*}}{ff_{*}}\,d\sigma\,dv_{*}\,dv.

This expression makes clear that
D(f)\geq 0
because the sign of the factor
(f^{\prime}f^{\prime}_{*}-ff_{*})
is always opposite to the sign of
\log({f^{\prime}f^{\prime}_{*}}/{ff_{*}})
. The dissipation vanishes,
D(f)=0
, if and only if
f^{\prime}f^{\prime}_{*}=ff_{*}
for almost all collisions, which is precisely the condition that
f
is a local Maxwellian (Boltzmann’s Lemma). Consequently, solutions are expected to converge (in entropy sense) to the unique global Maxwellian equilibrium
\mu_{\rho_{0},u_{0},T_{0}}
determined by the initial mass, momentum, and energy as
t\to\infty
.

The interplay between the conservation laws (
1.2
) and the entropy dissipation inequality (
1.7
) forms the core of the equation’s qualitative theory. These properties are not only physically essential but also provide critical guidelines for the design and analysis of robust numerical methods, which must preserve or correctly approximate these invariants and the entropy decay structure.

In what follows, we will provide a brief review of previous work from two complementary perspectives: the analytical perspective, focusing on well-posedness, regularity, and moment bounds; and the numerical perspective, focusing on discretization strategies, conservation properties, and convergence analysis.

1.1.3.
Existing Analytical Results

The rigorous mathematical study of the spatially homogeneous Boltzmann equation for hard potentials with angular cutoff began with Carleman
[
6
,
7
]
, who established the existence and uniqueness of solutions in the function space
L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
, assuming initial data with pointwise moment bounds, specifically for hard-sphere interactions. A more general Cauchy theory was subsequently developed by Arkeryd
[
1
,
2
]
, who extended these results to solutions in the space
L^{1}\cap L\log L
, requiring only
L^{1}
moment bounds on the initial data. This framework significantly broadened the class of admissible initial distributions. The theory reached a level of optimality in the work of Mischler and Wennberg
[
21
]
(see also Lu
[
19
]
), where sharp conditions for well-posedness and moment propagation were established. Their results effectively close the classical theory for cutoff hard potentials.

The study of moment bounds for the spatially homogeneous equation has a rich history. The seminal work of Elmroth
[
9
]
first proved the propagation of existing polynomial moments for “variable hard sphere” models, utilizing Povzner-type inequalities, a fundamental analytical tool introduced by Povzner
[
27
]
. A key result was then established by Desvillettes
[
8
]
, who demonstrated that for the same model, any polynomial moment appears instantaneously (is generated) provided the initial datum possesses a moment of order strictly greater than two. This “moment generation” phenomenon underscores the qualitative smoothing effect of hard potential collisions. These results were later refined to optimality by Mischler and Wennberg
[
21
]
, who established sharp growth rates and conditions for both the propagation and appearance of polynomial moments.

1.1.4.
Existing Numerical Results

It has been a challenging task to numerically solve the Boltzmann equation, owing to its high dimensionality, complex, non-linear and non-local collision terms. The numerical approximation for the Boltzmann equation mainly falls into two mainstream numerical methods: direct simulation of Monte-Carlo and deterministic methods. The Direct Simulation Monte-Carlo (DSMC) method
[
3
,
24
]
is a stochastic, particle-based approach that has been widely used, thanks to its easy implementation and low computational cost. However, due to its strong statistical noise, the DSMC method suffers from slow convergence and becomes prohibitively expensive when simulating non-steady and low-speed flows. In contrast, deterministic approaches yield more accurate results compared to stochastic ones.

Among the deterministic approaches, a broad range of numerical schemes has been developed, including discrete velocity methods (DVM) and spectral methods. This paper focuses specifically on Fourier spectral methods pioneered by
[
25
,
26
]
, which provide a noise-free framework by discretizing the collision operator over a truncated and periodized velocity domain, achieving a computational complexity of
O(N^{2d})
. Subsequent work
[
22
,
12
]
accelerated the algorithm to
O(M\,N^{d}\log N)
by exploiting a Carleman-like representation of the collision operator, where
M
denotes the total number of discretization points on the sphere. This acceleration, however, is restricted to certain special collision kernels, such as the three-dimensional hard-sphere model. For a general class of collision models, including non-cutoff kernels, efficient Fourier spectral schemes with computational complexity
O(M\,N^{d+1}\log N)
have been developed in
[
13
,
15
]
through the use of low-rank approximations. We also mention that due to the truncation of the domain, some properties such as momentum and energy conservation are lost, which the authors in
[
14
]
handle it by using Lagrange multipliers after every time step.

Despite the progress in algorithmic development, the theoretical analysis of Fourier spectral methods for the Boltzmann equation remains limited. Most existing theoretical studies confine their attention to bounding the approximation error of the collision operator itself, without addressing the stability and error propagation of the fully discrete solution. In particular, prior works
[
11
,
16
]
establish convergence toward the solution of the
periodized
problem on the torus, rather than toward the physically relevant whole-space solution. A recent work
[
10
]
on the spatially homogeneous Landau equation demonstrates that rigorous error estimates comparing the numerical and exact whole-space solutions are achievable for kinetic equations of this type, providing a key motivation for the present work.

Notations and function spaces

We begin with the following notation:

\bullet
We use the notation
a\lesssim b(a\gtrsim b)
to indicate that there is a uniform constant
C
, which may be different on different lines, such that
a\leq Cb(a\geq Cb)
. We use the notation
a\sim b
whenever
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
.

\bullet
We denote
C_{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n}}
(or
C\left(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n}\right)
) by a constant depending on the parameters
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n}
.

\bullet

\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}
is the characteristic function of the set
\Omega
. We use
\left\langle{f,g}\right\rangle:=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}f(v)g(v)dv
to denote the inner product of
f,g
. The Japanese bracket is denoted as
{\langle v\rangle}:=\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}
.

\bullet
For a multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha^{1},\alpha^{2},\alpha^{3})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{3}
, we denote the partial derivative operator with respect to
\alpha
as
\partial^{\alpha}=\partial^{\alpha^{1}}_{1}\partial^{\alpha^{2}}_{2}\partial^{\alpha^{3}}_{3}
. Also, we denote the order
|\alpha|=|\alpha_{1}|+|\alpha_{2}|+|\alpha_{3}|
, and for two multi-indices
\alpha
and
\beta
, we define
\alpha\leq\beta
by
\alpha^{1}\leq\beta^{1},\alpha^{2}\leq\beta^{2},\alpha^{3}\leq\beta^{3}
.

In the subsequent sections, we present various necessary function spaces used throughout this work. In what follows, we assume that
f=f(v)
.

(1).

The normalized space
L^{1}_{1,0,1}
is defined as

L_{1,0,1}^{1}:=\left\{0\leq f\in L^{1}\mid\rho_{f}=1,u_{f}=0,T_{f}=1\right\}.

The
L\log L
space is defined as

L\log L:=\left\{f(v)\left|\|f\|_{L\log L}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f|\log(1+|f|)dv<\infty\right.\right\}.

(2).

For
p\in[1,\infty)
and
l\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the weighted
L_{l}^{p}
space as follows:

L_{l}^{p}:=\left\{f(v)\,\bigg|\,\|f\|_{L_{l}^{p}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f(v)|^{p}\langle v\rangle^{lp}\,dv\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\right\},

and for an integer
N\geq 0
, the general weighted Sobolev space
W_{l}^{N,p}
is given by:

W_{l}^{N,p}:=\left\{f(v)\,\bigg|\,\|f\|_{W_{l}^{N,p}}=\left(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial_{v}^{\alpha}f(v)\|_{L^{p}_{l}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\partial_{v}^{\alpha}f(v)|^{p}\langle v\rangle^{lp}\,dv\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\right\},

with the multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
, where
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}
and
\partial_{v}^{\alpha}=\partial_{v_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{v_{2}}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{v_{3}}^{\alpha_{3}}
. In particular, if
p=2
, we define
H^{N}_{l}:=W_{l}^{N,2}
and
\|f\|_{H^{N}_{l}}:=\|f\|_{W_{l}^{N,2}}
.

(3).

For real numbers
k
and
l
, the weighted Sobolev space
H_{l}^{k}
is defined as follows:

H_{l}^{k}:=\left\{f(v)\mid\|f\|_{H_{l}^{k}}=\left\|\langle D\rangle^{k}\langle\cdot\rangle^{l}f\right\|_{L^{2}}<+\infty\right\},

where
a(D)
represents a pseudo-differential operator characterized by the symbol
a(\xi)
, given by:

(a(D)f)(x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i(x-y)\xi}a(\xi)f(y)\,dy\,d\xi.

Similarly, the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{k}
is defined as:

\dot{H}_{l}^{k}:=\left\{f(v)\mid\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{l}^{k}}=\left\||D|^{k}\langle\cdot\rangle^{l}f\right\|_{L^{2}}<+\infty\right\},

indicating that the relationship
\|f\|_{H^{k}_{l}}\sim\|f\|_{L^{2}_{l}}+\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k}_{l}}
holds when
k>0
.

1.3.
Goals and difficulties.

This work investigates a central challenge in the numerical analysis of the spatially homogeneous Boltzmann equation: the development and rigorous analysis of accurate numerical schemes with provable error estimates. Specifically, we aim to derive the first error estimates for the proposed schemes, quantifying their convergence rates in appropriate norms, and validate the theoretical results through numerical experiments, demonstrating the schemes’ accuracy in practical computations.

Mathematically, the above problems can be formulated as follows:

Question (I).
Suppose
f=f(t,v)
is a global smooth solution to (
1.1
) with smooth initial data
f_{0}
. Given a final time
T>0
and a tolerance
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, can we systematically construct a numerical solution
f^{Num}=f^{Num}(t,v)
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|f(t,\cdot)-f^{Num}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim\epsilon?

Question (II).
How can we design a numerical scheme that strictly mirrors its theoretical formulation while remaining computationally realizable?

To answer these questions, one must overcome a fundamental structural mismatch: the exact solution of the Boltzmann equation is natively defined on the unbounded whole space
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}
, whereas the Fourier spectral method mathematically requires a bounded periodic domain (a torus
{\mathcal{D}}_{L}=[-L,L]^{3}
). Before presenting our approach, we briefly review how previous works have addressed these issues.

1.3.1.
Fourier-Galerkin spectral methods for the periodic Boltzmann equation

Previous Fourier-Galerkin spectral methods for the Boltzmann equation, extensively developed since the original work
[
25
,
26
]
, are typically formulated directly on the torus. More precisely, they consider the periodic problem on the torus
{\mathcal{D}}_{L}=[-L,L]^{3}
and formulated the periodic Boltzmann operator
Q_{p}
as follows:

(1.9)

Q_{p}(g,h):=\int_{[-L,L]^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}B(|z|,\sigma)\mathbf{1}_{|z|\leq 2R}(g(v+z^{-})h(v+z^{+})-g(v+z)h(v))\,dz\,d\sigma=:Q_{p}^{+}(g,h)-Q_{p}^{-}(g,h),

where

z=v_{*}-v,\ \ \ z^{+}=\frac{z+|z|\sigma}{2},\ \ \ z^{-}=\frac{z-|z|\sigma}{2},

with
L=\frac{3+\sqrt{2}}{2}R
for any functions
g,h
defined on the torus
{\mathcal{D}}_{L}
. Here the subscript
p
indicates periodicity. It has been established that for functions compactly supported in the ball
B(0,R)
, the periodic operator
Q_{p}
exactly coincides with the original operator
Q
. The Fourier-Galerkin spectral method is then applied to this periodic operator as:

(1.10)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f_{N}\,=\,{\mathcal{P}}_{N}Q_{p}(f_{N},f_{N}),\\
f_{N}|_{t=0}={\mathcal{P}}_{N}(f_{0}\,\mathbf{1}_{{\mathcal{D}}_{L}}),\end{cases}

where
{\mathcal{P}}_{N}
is the orthogonal projection onto the finite-dimensional space
\mathbb{P}_{N}:=span\{e^{-i\frac{\pi}{L}k\cdot v}|k\in{\mathcal{J}}_{N}\}
in
L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
with
{\mathcal{J}}_{N}:=\llbracket-N,N-1\rrbracket^{3}
. More precisely, for any periodic function
f\in L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
, we have

(1.11)

{\mathcal{P}}_{N}f=(2L)^{-3}\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{J}}_{N}}\hat{f}(k)e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}k\cdot v},\quad\text{where}\quad\hat{f}(k):=\int_{{\mathcal{D}}_{L}}f(v)\,\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{L}k\cdot v\right)\,dv.

By doing so, one can exactly calculate the Fourier coefficients of the collision operator
Q_{p}
, ensuring the numerical solution always remains within the trigonometric polynomial space
\mathbb{P}_{N}
.

Later theoretical advances
[
11
,
16
]
established stability and convergence results for the numerical solution towards the exact solution of the
periodized
problem
\partial_{t}f^{R}=Q_{p}(f^{R},f^{R})
. However, the asymptotic long-time behavior of the periodic equation leads to a constant equilibrium state, which differs essentially from the Maxwellian equilibrium of the whole-space problem. Furthermore, standard estimates for the whole-space collision operator rely heavily on the regular/singular pre-post collisional change of variables
(v,v_{*})\to(v^{\prime},v_{*}^{\prime})
. On the periodic torus
{\mathcal{D}}_{L}
, this coordinate transformation is geometrically invalid.

1.3.2.
Progress on numerical analysis of spatially homogeneous Landau equation

To bridge the gap between
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{L}
, a “two-step” framework was recently proposed for the Landau equation in
[
10
]
. The first step introduces an artificially truncated equation, where the collision operator is modified by a smooth truncation function
\psi^{R}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B(0,R))
that localizes the interactions to a ball of radius
R
:

(1.12)

\partial_{t}f^{R}=Q(f^{R}\,\psi^{R}\,,\,f^{R}\,\psi^{R})\,\psi^{R},

where
\psi^{R}
is a radial bump function satisfying
\mathbf{1}_{\{|v|\leq R/2\}}\leq\psi^{R}\leq\mathbf{1}_{\{|v|\leq R\}}
. By carefully analyzing the error introduced by this truncation, the authors showed that the solution
f^{R}
to the truncated equation approximates the original solution
f
in suitable polynomial weighted norms in
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}
as
R\to\infty
. Constrained by the equation, the solution
f^{R}
is strictly supported in
B(0,R)
and automatically coincides with the solution
f_{p}^{R}
to the analogous truncated equation formulated on the torus:

(1.13)

\partial_{t}f^{R}_{p}=Q_{p}(f^{R}_{p}\,\psi^{R}\,,\,f^{R}_{p}\,\psi^{R})\,\psi^{R}.

Thus, this first step provides a bridge between the whole-space problem and a localized version that can be treated on the torus.

The second step involves the discretization and construction of the numerical scheme, followed by bounding the distance between the solution of the truncated equation and the numerical approximation on the torus. The numerical scheme is proposed as:

(1.14)

\partial_{t}f^{R}_{N}={\mathcal{P}}_{N}\left({\mathcal{P}}_{N}\left(Q_{p}\left({\mathcal{P}}_{N}(f^{R}_{N}\,\psi^{R})\,,\,{\mathcal{P}}_{N}(f^{R}_{N}\,\psi^{R})\right)\right)\,\psi^{R}\right).

Through this approach, the numerical solution
f^{R}_{N}
is confined to the finite space
\mathbb{P}_{N}
, and the discrete collision operator can be evaluated exactly. For any fixed torus domain, it was proved that the error between
f^{R}_{N}
and
f^{R}_{p}
converges to zero as
N\to\infty
. By managing the errors in both steps, the convergence of the numerical solution to the whole-space Landau solution is established as
R\to\infty
and
N\to\infty
.

While this “two-step” strategy elegantly connects the domains, the intermediate truncated system necessitates a substantial amount of highly complex technical analysis. The polynomial weighted norms used in the first step destroy the periodic structure required in the second step, making it difficult to apply the similar analysis to the second step.

1.4.
Strategies and main results.

In this work, we propose a
refined, direct strategy
that significantly streamlines the proof. Instead of constructing an complex intermediate truncation PDE, we directly utilize the truncated whole-space exact solution,
f^{R}:=f\psi^{R}
, as our continuous reference function on the torus
{\mathcal{D}}_{L}
. By adopting
f\psi^{R}
directly, we entirely bypass the heavy analytical analysis of the first step encountered in
[
10
]
. The theoretical effort is then focused on a single step: bounding the difference between the numerical solution and the truncated solution
f\psi^{R}
on the periodic domain.

Remark 1.2
.

Two remarks are in order:

(i).

The operators

are valid only for the functions defined on the torus

with the geometric relation

1.4.2.
Strategy

Our main strategy relies on the following two aspects.

\bullet

Novel tools on the torus:
{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}
and
m_{k}
.

To transfer analytical techniques from the whole space
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}
to the periodic torus
{\mathcal{D}}_{L}
, we introduce two mathematical tools: a smoothing projection operator
{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}
and a smoothly patched periodic weight function
m_{k}
.
The ingenuity of
m_{k}
lies in the fact that it coincides with the standard polynomial weight
{\langle v\rangle}^{k}
within the domain
B(0,R)
, while remaining a smooth and strictly periodic function on
{\mathcal{D}}_{L}
. This structure enables us to rigorously estimate the commutator between
{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}
and
m_{k}
(see Lemma
2.1
). Consequently, we can avoid heavy intermediate truncation equations and directly execute polynomial-weighted norm estimates on the torus, generalizing classical whole-space methods for the propagation of moments to the periodic setting.

\bullet

Intrinsic analytical bounds for the periodic collision operator
Q_{p}
.

The standard weighted estimates for the Boltzmann collision operator heavily rely on the regular and singular pre/post-collisional changes of variables
(v,v_{*})\to(v^{\prime},v_{*}^{\prime})
, which are invalid on a periodic torus. To bypass this incompatibility, we establish analytical bounds for the periodic collision operator
Q_{p}
using its
discrete Fourier convolution structure
(see Lemma
2.4
). This innovation allows us to estimate the collision operator within the periodic domain. Remarkably, for functions compactly supported in
B(0,R)
, these derived Fourier-based bounds perfectly match the classical polynomial-weighted estimates of the original collision operator
Q
on
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}
.

2.2.4.
Estimates for the commutator

We establish the following estimate for the commutator between a weight function and the smoothing projection operator

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\in L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
and let
m\in C^{1}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
be a periodic function. When
N>L
, the following estimates for
{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(fm)-({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}f)m
hold:

(1)

It follows that

(2.12)

\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(fm)-({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}f)\,m\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|f\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla m\|_{L^{\infty}},

where the constant depends only on the bump function
\Phi
.

(2)

When
m=m_{k}
is the weight function defined by (
1.17
) with integer
k\geq 1
,

(2.13)

\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(fm_{k})-({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}f)\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|fm_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}},

where the constant depends only on
k
and the bump function
\Phi
.

Proof.

Step 1. Kernel representation of
{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}
.

Consider the Fourier kernel of the operator
{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}
. For
x\in{\mathcal{D}}_{L}
, by substituting the definition of the Fourier coefficients
\hat{f}(k)
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2L)^{3}}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\hat{f}(n)\,\Phi\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}n\cdot x}=\frac{1}{(2L)^{3}}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathcal{D}}_{L}}f(v)\,e^{-i\frac{\pi}{L}n\cdot v}\,\Phi\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}n\cdot x}\,dv

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2L)^{3}}\int_{{\mathcal{D}}_{L}}f(v)\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\Phi\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}n\cdot(x-v)}\,dv=\int_{{\mathcal{D}}_{L}}f(v)\,D_{N}(x-v)\,dv,

where we define the Fourier kernel of
{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}
by

(2.14)

D_{N}(v):=\frac{1}{(2L)^{3}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\Phi\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}k\cdot v}.

Thus
{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}f=f*D_{N}
, i.e., the smoothing projection operator can be represented as a convolution with the kernel
D_{N}
. (Remark: For the projection operator
{\mathcal{P}}_{N}
, where
\Phi=\mathbf{1}_{[-1,1]^{3}}
, this Fourier kernel becomes the Dirichlet kernel
D_{N}(v)=\frac{1}{(2L)^{3}}\sum_{|k|\leq N}e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}k\cdot v}
.)

Step 2. Estimates for the kernel
D_{N}
.

We first apply the Poisson summation formula to rewrite
D_{N}(v)
. Define the inverse Fourier transform of
\Phi
on
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}
by

\phi(x)=\Phi^{\vee}(x)=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\Phi(\xi)\,e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}\,d\xi.

Since
\Phi
is a Schwartz function,
\phi
is also a Schwartz function. By the Poisson summation formula,

D_{N}(v)=\frac{1}{(2L)^{3}}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\Phi\left(\frac{2L}{N}\cdot\frac{n}{2L}\right)\,e^{2\pi in\cdot\frac{v}{2L}}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{2L}{N}\ \cdot\right)\right)^{\vee}(v+2Ln)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\phi_{2L/N}\left((v+2Ln)\right),

where we have used the scaling property of the Fourier transform. Here we denote
\phi_{t}(x)=t^{-3}\phi(t^{-1}x)=(\Phi(t\ \cdot\,))^{\vee}(x)
for any
t>0
. Consequently,

\int_{{\mathcal{D}}_{L}}D_{N}(v)\,dv=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\phi_{2L/N}(v)\,dv=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\phi(v)\,dv=\Phi(0)=1,

and

\|D_{N}(v)\|_{L^{1}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}=\int_{{\mathcal{D}}_{L}}|D_{N}(v)|\,dv=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}|\phi_{2L/N}(v)|\,dv=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}|\phi(v)|\,dv=\|\phi\|_{L^{1}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

For any
s>0
, consider the function
|v|^{s}D_{N}(v)
defined on the torus. For
v\in{\mathcal{D}}_{L}
and
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
, when
n\neq 0
we have
|v+2Ln|\geq L\geq|v|/\sqrt{3}
(here we regard
v+2Ln\in\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}
, so
v+2Ln
lies outside the cube
{\mathcal{D}}_{L}
except when
n=0
). Thus

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{D}}_{L}}|v|^{s}\,|D_{N}(v)|\,dv=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathcal{D}}_{L}}|v|^{s}\,|\phi_{2L/N}(v+2Ln)|\,dv\lesssim\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathcal{D}}_{L}}|v+2Ln|^{s}\,|\phi_{2L/N}(v+2Ln)|\,dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}|v|^{s}\,|\phi_{2L/N}(v)|\,dv=\left(\frac{2L}{N}\right)^{s}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}|v|^{s}\,|\phi(v)|\,dv=\left(\frac{2L}{N}\right)^{s}\|\,|\cdot|^{s}\,\phi(\cdot)\,\|_{L^{1}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

Since
\phi
is a Schwartz function,
\|\,|\cdot|^{s}\,\phi(\cdot)\,\|_{L^{1}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}<\infty
is a finite constant depending only on
s
and
\Phi
. Therefore,

(2.15)

\|\,|\cdot|^{s}D_{N}(\cdot)\,\|_{L^{1}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}\lesssim\left(\frac{L}{N}\right)^{s}.

Step 3. Estimates for the commutator.

For any
x\in{\mathcal{D}}_{L}
,

({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(fm)-({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}f)\,m)(x)=\int_{{\mathcal{D}}_{L}}f(v)\,(m(v)-m(x))\,D_{N}(x-v)\,dv.

Point (1).
For any
m\in C^{1}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
, the mean value theorem implies
|m(v)-m(x)|\leq\|\nabla m\|_{L^{\infty}}|v-x|
(this also holds for
x,v
on the torus when
m
is a periodic function with continuous periodic derivative). Hence,

|({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(fm)-({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}f)\,m)(x)|\leq\|\nabla m\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{{\mathcal{D}}_{L}}|f(v)|\,|(x-v)\,D_{N}(x-v)|\,dv.

By Young’s convolution inequality and (
2.15
),

\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(fm)-({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}f)\,m\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\nabla m\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}\|\,|\cdot|\,D_{N}(\cdot)\,\|_{L^{1}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|f\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla m\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Point (2).
When
m=m_{k}
for integer
k\geq 1
, we apply the multivariate Taylor expansion with remainder: for any
x,v\in{\mathcal{D}}_{L}
,

m_{k}(x)-m_{k}(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\sum_{|\alpha|=i}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}m_{k}(v)}{\alpha!}(x-v)^{\alpha}+\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}m_{k}(\xi)}{\alpha!}(x-v)^{\alpha},

where
\xi=v+\theta(x-v)
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
, and
\alpha
denotes a multi-index. Since
|\partial^{\alpha}m_{k}(v)|\lesssim m_{k-|\alpha|}(v)
and
\|\partial^{\alpha}m_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C
when
|\alpha|=k
, with
C
depending only on
k
, we obtain

\displaystyle|m_{k}(x)-m_{k}(v)|\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}|\nabla^{i}m_{k}(v)|\,|x-v|^{i}+\|\nabla^{k}m_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}|x-v|^{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim m_{k-1}(v)\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}|x-v|^{i}+|x-v|^{k}\lesssim m_{k-1}(v)(|x-v|+|x-v|^{k}),

where the implicit constant depends only on
k
. Using the same method as in Point (1), we derive

|({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(fm_{k})-({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}f)\,m_{k})(x)|\lesssim\int_{{\mathcal{D}}_{L}}|f(v)|\,m_{k-1}(v)\,(|x-v|+|x-v|^{k})\,|D_{N}(x-v)|\,dv.

By Young’s convolution inequality, (
2.15
), and the condition
N>L
,

\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(fm_{k})-({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}f)\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\,|\cdot|\,D_{N}(\cdot)\,\|_{L^{1}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}+\|\,|\cdot|^{k}\,D_{N}(\cdot)\,\|_{L^{1}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}\right)\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|f\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}},

with some constant depending only on
k
and the bump function
\Phi
.
∎

The commutator estimate in Lemma
2.1
provides a fundamental tool for controlling the interaction between the smoothing projection operator and weight functions. As a direct consequence, we obtain the following corollary which will be essential for estimating the weighted norms of the truncated collision operator:

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
g\in L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
and let
m_{k}
be defined by (
1.17
). Then the following estimates hold:

(1)

When
L\leq N
with
k\geq 1
,

(2.16)

\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}},

where the constant depends only on
k
.

(2)

When
k\geq 1
,

(2.17)

\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k})-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|g\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}},

where the constant depends only on
k
.

Proof.

Point (1).

Applying Lemma
2.1
(2) yields

\displaystyle\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,m_{k}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,m_{k})\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,m_{k})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|g\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left({\frac{L}{N}+1}\right)\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

Since
L\leq N
, we obtain

(2.18)

\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}},

where the constant depends only on
k
. This completes the proof of Point (1).

Point (2).
Since

\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k})-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k})-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,m_{k})\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,m_{k})\,\psi^{R}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}},

applying Lemma
2.1
(1) to the first term, and noting that
\|\nabla\psi^{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\frac{1}{R}
and
L/R=\frac{3+\sqrt{2}}{2}
is a constant, we obtain

\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k})-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,m_{k})\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\psi^{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\frac{1}{N}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|g\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}.

Here we use
m_{k}=m_{k-1}m_{1}\lesssim L\,m_{k-1}
. For the second term, applying Lemma
2.1
(2) and
0\leq\psi^{R}\leq 1
yields

\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,m_{k})\,\psi^{R}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,m_{k})-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|g\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}.

This completes the proof of Point (2).
∎

The estimates in Lemma
2.1
and Corollary
2.1
provide the necessary control, and will be used extensively in the subsequent stability analysis, particularly when estimating the error terms arising from the discretization of the Boltzmann collision operator. The key observation is that the smoothing projection operator preserves weighted norms up to a small error of order
L/N
, which can be made arbitrarily small by choosing a sufficiently large truncation parameter
N
.

2.3.
Estimates for periodic Boltzmann collision operator

From
[
26
]
, we have the following proposition:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
l,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
. Then

(2.19)

Q_{p}^{+}(e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}l\cdot v},e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}m\cdot v})=\beta(l,m)e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}(l+m)\cdot v},\ \ \ Q_{p}^{-}(e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}l\cdot v},e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}m\cdot v})=\beta(l,l)e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}(l+m)\cdot v},

where

(2.20)

\beta(l,m)=(2R)^{3+\gamma}(4\pi)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}r^{2+\gamma}\operatorname{sinc}(\pi\lambda r|l-m|)\,\operatorname{sinc}(\pi\lambda r|l+m|)\,dr.

Here
\lambda=R/L=\frac{2}{3+\sqrt{2}}
, and the sinc function is defined by
\operatorname{sinc}(x)=\frac{\sin x}{x}
for
x\neq 0
and
\operatorname{sinc}(0)=1
.

As a direct corollary, we have

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
g,h,f\in L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
,

Q_{p}(g,h)=(2L)^{-6}\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\hat{g}(l)\hat{h}(m)Q_{p}(e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}l\cdot v},e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}m\cdot v})=(2L)^{-6}\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\hat{g}(l)\hat{h}(m)(\beta(l,m)-\beta(l,l))e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}(l+m)\cdot v}.

In particular,

(2.21)

\hat{Q}^{+}_{p}(g,h)(k)=(2L)^{-3}\sum_{l+m=k}\hat{g}(l)\hat{h}(m)\beta(l,m),\ \ \hat{Q}^{-}_{p}(g,h)(k)=(2L)^{-3}\sum_{l+m=k}\hat{g}(l)\hat{h}(m)\beta(l,l).

We also state the following estimate for
\beta(l,m)
:

Lemma 2.3
.

For the
\beta(l,m)
defined in (
2.20
), the following estimates hold:

(1)

For any
l,m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
,

(2.22)

|\beta(l,m)|\lesssim R^{3+\gamma}\left\langle{l+m}\right\rangle^{-1}\left\langle{l-m}\right\rangle^{-1}\left\langle{|l+m|-|l-m|}\right\rangle^{-1}.

(2)

For any
0\leq\delta<1
and
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
,

(2.23)

\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}|l|^{\delta}|\beta(l,m)|^{2}\lesssim R^{2(3+\gamma)}\left\langle{m}\right\rangle^{-2+\delta}.

For any
0\leq\delta<1
and
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
,

(2.24)

\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}|m|^{\delta}|\beta(l,m)|^{2}\lesssim R^{2(3+\gamma)}\left\langle{l}\right\rangle^{-2+\delta}.

The proof is given in Section
A.2
.

We now establish estimates for the periodic Boltzmann operator. Note that our results hold for general periodic functions
g,h\in L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
; therefore, we cannot directly apply the results for the
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}
case.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
g,h\in L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
, for
0\leq s<\frac{1}{2},\frac{6}{5}<p\leq 2
such that
s+\frac{3}{p}<\frac{5}{2}
the following upper bound holds:

(2.25)

\|Q_{p}^{+}(g,h)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim L^{3-\frac{3}{p}-s+\gamma}\|g\|_{L^{2}}\|h\|_{L^{p}},\ \ \ \|Q_{p}^{+}(g,h)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim L^{3-\frac{3}{p}-s+\gamma}\|g\|_{L^{p}}\|h\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof of of Lemma
2.4
.

By (
2.21
) and (
2.8
), for functions
g,h\in L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
,

\displaystyle\|Q^{+}_{p}(g,h)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(2L)^{-3}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}|\hat{Q}^{+}_{p}(g,h)(k)|^{2}\left({\frac{\pi|k|}{L}}\right)^{2s}=(2L)^{-9-2s}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}|k|^{2s}\left|\sum_{l+m=k}\hat{g}(l)\,\hat{h}(m)\,\beta(l,m)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2L)^{-9-2s}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\left|\sum_{l+m=k}\left({|l|^{2s}+|m|^{2s}}\right)|\hat{g}(l)|\,|\hat{h}(m)|\,|\beta(l,m)|\right|^{2}\lesssim L^{-9-2s}({\mathcal{I}}_{1}+{\mathcal{I}}_{2}),

where
{\mathcal{I}}_{1},{\mathcal{I}}_{2}
are defined by

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{1}:=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\left|\sum_{l+m=k}|l|^{2s}\,|\hat{g}(l)|\,|\hat{h}(m)|\,|\beta(l,m)|\right|^{2},\\[15.00002pt]
\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{2}:=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\left|\sum_{l+m=k}|m|^{2s}\,|\hat{g}(l)|\,|\hat{h}(m)|\,|\beta(l,m)|\right|^{2}.\end{cases}

For
{\mathcal{I}}_{1}
, Lemma
2.3
(2) and Cauchy’s inequality give

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\left({\sum_{l+m=k}|l|^{2s}\,|\hat{h}(m)|^{2}\,|\beta(l,m)|^{2}}\right)\left({\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}|\hat{g}(l)|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left({\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}|\hat{g}(l)|^{2}}\right)\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\left({|\hat{h}(m)|^{2}\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}|l|^{2s}\,|\beta(l,m)|^{2}}\right)\lesssim L^{3}\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}|\hat{h}(m)|^{2}\left({R^{2(3+\gamma)}\left\langle{m}\right\rangle^{-2+2s}}\right).

Let
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
and
q^{\prime}=\frac{p}{2-p}
. Then
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and
\frac{2}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
. By Hölder’s inequality and the Hausdorff-Young inequality (
2.7
),

\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}|\hat{h}(m)|^{2}\left\langle{m}\right\rangle^{-2+2s}\leq\left({\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}|\hat{h}(m)|^{q}}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}\left({\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\left({\left\langle{m}\right\rangle^{-2+2s}}\right)^{q^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|\hat{h}\|_{\ell^{q}}^{2}\lesssim L^{6/q}\|h\|_{L^{p}}^{2}.

Here we use that when
s+\frac{3}{p}<\frac{5}{2}
,
(-2+2s)\,q^{\prime}<-3
, and thus
\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\left\langle{m}\right\rangle^{(-2+2s)\,q^{\prime}}\lesssim 1
. Hence,

{\mathcal{I}}_{1}\lesssim L^{3}\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left({L^{6/q}\|h\|_{L^{p}}^{2}}\right)\left({R^{2(3+\gamma)}}\right)\lesssim L^{9+6/q+2\gamma}\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|h\|_{L^{p}}^{2}.

For
{\mathcal{I}}_{2}
, a similar argument yields

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\left({\sum_{l+m=k}|m|^{2s}\,|\hat{h}(m)|^{2}\,|\beta(l,m)|^{2}}\right)\left({\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}|\hat{g}(l)|^{2}}\right)=\left({\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}|\hat{g}(l)|^{2}}\right)\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\left({|m|^{2s}|\hat{h}(m)|^{2}\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\,|\beta(l,m)|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim L^{3}\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}|m|^{2s}|\hat{h}(m)|^{2}\left({R^{2(3+\gamma)}\left\langle{m}\right\rangle^{-2}}\right)\lesssim L^{9+6/q+2\gamma}\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|h\|_{L^{p}}^{2}.

Therefore, for
Q^{+}_{p}
,

\|Q^{+}_{p}(g,h)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}\lesssim L^{-9-2s}({\mathcal{I}}_{1}+{\mathcal{I}}_{2})\lesssim L^{-9-2s}\left({L^{9+6/q+2\gamma}\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|h\|_{L^{p}}^{2}}\right)=L^{2(3-\frac{3}{p}-s+\gamma)}\|g\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\|h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By the symmetry of
Q^{+}_{p}
, the proof is complete.
∎

Taking
s=0
and
p=\frac{6}{5-2\varepsilon}
for some
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, we obtain the following corollary for the
L^{2}
norm of
Q_{p}^{+}
:

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
g,h\in L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
. For
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and
p=\frac{6}{5-2\varepsilon}
,

(2.26)

\|Q_{p}^{+}(g,h)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim L^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma+\varepsilon}\|g\|_{L^{2}}\|h\|_{L^{p}},\ \ \ \|Q_{p}^{+}(g,h)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim L^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma+\varepsilon}\|g\|_{L^{p}}\|h\|_{L^{2}}.

Note that this result holds for general periodic functions
g,h\in L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
and does not require compact support of
g,h
. In this periodic domain, the regular/singular change of variables
v\to v^{\prime}
and
v_{*}\to v^{\prime}
(Lemma
A.1
) cannot be applied when estimating
Q^{+}
.

Using this result, we establish the following estimate for the
m_{k}
-weighted norm of the truncated periodic collision operator:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
g,h\in L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
and
k\geq 8
. Then

(2.27)

\|Q_{p}(g,h)\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|h\,m_{k+1/2+\gamma+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|g\,m_{k+1/2+\gamma+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}},

where the constant depends only on
k
and
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

We split the functions
g,h
by truncation: define
g_{i},g_{o}\in L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
by

g_{i}=g\,\mathbf{1}_{|v|<R},\ \ \ g_{o}=g\,\mathbf{1}_{|v|\geq R}.

The subscripts
i
and
o
denote the inner and outer parts of the function, respectively. Similarly, define
h_{i},h_{o}\in L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
. Then

g=g_{i}+g_{o},\ \ \ h=h_{i}+h_{o}.

Thus,
Q_{p}(g,h)
can be written as three parts:

Q_{p}(g,h)=Q_{p}(g_{i},h_{i})+Q_{p}(g_{i},h_{o})+Q_{p}(g_{o},h).

For the first part, since
g_{i},h_{i}
are both compactly supported on
B(0,R)
,

Q_{p}(g_{i},h_{i})=Q(g_{i},h_{i}),

where we do not distinguish between
g_{i},h_{i}
and their zero extensions on
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}
. By (
2.2
),

\displaystyle\|Q_{p}(g_{i},h_{i})\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Q(g_{i},h_{i}){\langle v\rangle}^{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim\|g_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\|h_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma}}+\|g_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma}}\|h_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle=\|g_{i}\,m_{k+\gamma}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}\|h_{i}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}+\|g_{i}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}\|h_{i}\,m_{k+\gamma}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}.

For the other two parts, the property of
m_{k}
gives
R^{k}\|g_{o}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|g_{o}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}
and
R^{k}\|h_{o}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|h_{o}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}
. First, consider the
Q^{+}
part. By (
2.26
), for any
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and
p=\frac{6}{5-2\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle\|Q^{+}_{p}(g_{i},h_{o})\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{k}\|Q^{+}_{p}(g_{i},h_{o})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim R^{k+1/2+\gamma+\varepsilon}\|g_{i}\|_{L^{p}}\|h_{o}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g_{i}\|_{L^{p}}\|h_{o}\,m_{k+1/2+\gamma+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}.

Since
6/5<p\leq 2
, we have
\|g_{i}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|g_{i}\,m_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|g_{i}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}
, and therefore

\|Q_{p}^{+}(g_{i},h_{o})\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|g_{i}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|h_{o}\,m_{k+1/2+\gamma+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}.

Similarly we have
\|Q^{+}_{p}(g_{o},h)\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|g_{o}\,m_{k+1/2+\gamma+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\|h\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}
.

For the
Q^{-}
part, by definition we know
Q^{-}(g,h)\,m_{k}
is still a convolution:

Q^{-}(g,h)\,m_{k}(v)=\int_{{\mathcal{D}}_{L}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|z|^{\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{|z|\leq 2R}\,g(v+z)\,h(v)\,m_{k}(v)\,d\sigma\,dz.

We then apply Young’s convolution inequality to obtain

\|Q^{-}(g,h)\,m_{k}(v)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim R^{\gamma}\|g\|_{L^{1}}\|h\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

This inequality holds for general periodic functions
g,h\in L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
. Consequently, for the latter two terms we obtain

\|Q^{-}(g_{i},h_{o})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim R^{\gamma}\|g_{i}\|_{L^{1}}\|h_{o}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|g_{i}\,m_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|h_{o}\,m_{k+\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|g_{i}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|h_{o}\,m_{k+\gamma}\|_{L^{2}},

\|Q^{-}(g_{o},h)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim R^{\gamma}\|g_{o}\|_{L^{1}}\|h\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim R^{\gamma}\|g_{o}\,m_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|h\,m_{k+\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|g_{o}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|h\,m_{k+\gamma}\|_{L^{2}},

here we use that
k\geq 8>2+\gamma
.
By definition,
\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}=\|g_{i}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}+\|g_{o}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}
, so summing the three parts yields

\displaystyle\|Q_{p}(g,h)\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|g_{i}\,m_{k+\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}\|h_{i}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}+\|g_{i}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|h_{i}\,m_{k+\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|g_{i}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|h_{o}\,m_{k+1/2+\gamma+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}+\|g_{o}\,m_{k+1/2+\gamma+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\|h\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|g_{i}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|h_{o}\,m_{k+\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}+\|g_{o}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|h\,m_{k+\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|h\,m_{k+1/2+\gamma+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|g\,m_{k+1/2+\gamma+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we prove the main stability estimate for the spectral method applied to the Boltzmann equation. The proof follows an energy method approach: we compare the numerical solution
f_{N}^{R}
with the truncated exact solution
f^{R}=f\psi^{R}
, establish an energy inequality for the error function
g=f_{N}^{R}-f^{R}
, and control the resulting error terms through careful estimation of three main components
{\mathcal{I}}_{1}
,
{\mathcal{I}}_{2}
, and
{\mathcal{I}}_{3}
.

We recall that the truncated periodic collision operator in Fourier mode
Q_{N}^{R}
is defined by

(3.1)

Q_{N}^{R}(g,h)\,:=\,{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(Q_{p}\left(G_{N},H_{N}\right)\right)\,\psi^{R}\right),

with
G_{N}:={\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}\left(g\,\psi^{R}\right)
and
H_{N}:={\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}\left(h\,\psi^{R}\right)
. This definition is nearly identical to that in our previous work, except that we use the smoothing projection operator
{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}
instead of the projection operator
{\mathcal{P}}_{N}
. We note that the outermost operator is the standard projection operator
{\mathcal{P}}_{N}
. Therefore, we construct the numerical solution
f_{N}^{R}
via equation (
1.18
):

(3.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f_{N}^{R}\,=\,Q^{R}_{N}\left(f_{N}^{R},\,f_{N}^{R}\right),\\[5.0pt]
f_{N}^{R}|_{t=0}\,=\,{\mathcal{P}}_{N}\left(f_{0}\,\psi^{R}\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{D}_{L}}\right).\end{cases}

We then compare the numerical solution
f_{N}^{R}
with the truncated exact solution
f^{R}=f\psi^{R}
, which satisfies the equation

(3.3)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f^{R}\,=\,Q\left(f,\,f\right)\psi^{R},\\[5.0pt]
f_{N}^{R}|_{t=0}\,=\,f_{0}\,\psi^{R}.\end{cases}

Here
f
denotes the solution of the Boltzmann equation (
1.1
) given by Proposition
1.1
.

Since
f^{R}
is compactly supported on
B(0,R)\subset{\mathcal{D}}_{L}
, we may regard
f^{R}
as a function defined on the torus
{\mathcal{D}}_{L}
and compare
f_{N}^{R}
with
f^{R}
on this torus.

Now we are in a position to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
: Part
(i)
.

Define the error function
g=f_{N}^{R}-f^{R}
on
{\mathcal{D}}_{L}
, then we have

(3.4)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}g\,=\,Q^{R}_{N}\left(f_{N}^{R},\,f_{N}^{R}\right)-Q\left(f,\,f\right)\psi^{R},\\[5.0pt]
g|_{t=0}\,=\,({\mathcal{P}}_{N}-I)(f_{0}\,\psi^{R}\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{D}_{L}}).\end{cases}

For some positive integer
k>8
, we apply the energy method to
g
in the weighted space
L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L};m_{k})
, where the weight function
m_{k}
is defined by (
1.17
). We obtain

(3.5)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}^{2}={\mathcal{I}}_{1}+{\mathcal{I}}_{2}+{\mathcal{I}}_{3},

where we decompose the right-hand side into three terms:

\begin{cases}{\mathcal{I}}_{1}\,:=\left\langle{Q^{R}_{N}\left(f_{N}^{R}\,,\,f_{N}^{R}\right)\,,\,g\,m_{k}^{2}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{Q_{p}({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R}\,,\,{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R})\,,\,{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,m_{k}^{2}\,\psi^{R}}\right\rangle,\\[5.0pt]
{\mathcal{I}}_{2}\,:=\left\langle{Q_{p}({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R}\,,\,{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R})-Q_{p}({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f^{R})\,\psi^{R}\,,\,{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f^{R})\,\psi^{R})\,,\,{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,m_{k}^{2}\,\psi^{R}}\right\rangle,\\[5.0pt]
{\mathcal{I}}_{3}\,:=\left\langle{Q_{p}({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f^{R})\,\psi^{R}\,,\,{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f^{R})\,\psi^{R})\,,\,{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,m_{k}^{2}\,\psi^{R}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{Q(f,f)\,\psi^{R}\,,\,g\,m_{k}^{2}}\right\rangle.\end{cases}

Estimate for
{\mathcal{I}}_{1}
.
For simplicity, let
F_{N}:={\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R}\,\psi^{R})
. We then split
{\mathcal{I}}_{1}
as
{\mathcal{I}}_{1}={\mathcal{I}}_{11}+{\mathcal{I}}_{12}+{\mathcal{I}}_{13}+{\mathcal{I}}_{14}
, where

\begin{cases}{\mathcal{I}}_{11}\,:=\left\langle{Q^{R}_{N}\left(f_{N}^{R},\,f_{N}^{R}\right)\,,\,g\,m_{k}^{2}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}\left({Q_{p}(F_{N},F_{N})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}}\right),\,g\,m_{k}}\right\rangle\\[5.0pt]
{\mathcal{I}}_{12}\,:=\left\langle{Q_{p}(F_{N},F_{N})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k},\,{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,m_{k})}\right\rangle-\left\langle{Q_{p}(F_{N},F_{N})\,\psi^{R}\,,\,{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,m_{k}^{2}}\right\rangle,\\[5.0pt]
{\mathcal{I}}_{13}\,:=\left\langle{Q_{p}(F_{N},F_{N})\,\psi^{R}-Q_{p}({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R},F_{N})\,\psi^{R}\,\,,\,{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,m_{k}^{2}}\right\rangle,\\[5.0pt]
{\mathcal{I}}_{14}\,:=\left\langle{Q_{p}({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R},F_{N})\,\psi^{R}-Q_{p}({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R},{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R})\,\psi^{R}\,,\,{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,m_{k}^{2}}\right\rangle.\end{cases}

For
{\mathcal{I}}_{11}
, using the definition of
Q_{N}^{R}
in (
3.1
), we can write

{\mathcal{I}}_{11}=\left\langle{{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(Q_{p}\left(F_{N},F_{N}\right)\right)\,\psi^{R}\right)m_{k}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}\left({Q_{p}(F_{N},F_{N})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}}\right),\,g\,m_{k}}\right\rangle

considering the first argument of the inner product, we decompose it as

\left\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(Q_{p}\left(F_{N},F_{N}\right)\right)\,\psi^{R}\right)m_{k}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}\left({Q_{p}(F_{N},F_{N})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq{\mathcal{J}}_{1}+{\mathcal{J}}_{2},

with

(3.6)

\begin{cases}{\mathcal{J}}_{1}:=\left\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(Q_{p}\left(F_{N},F_{N}\right)\right)\,\psi^{R}\right)m_{k}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(Q_{p}\left(F_{N},F_{N}\right)\right)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}},\\[5.0pt]
{\mathcal{J}}_{2}:=\left\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(Q_{p}\left(F_{N},F_{N}\right)\right)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}\right)-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}\left({Q_{p}(F_{N},F_{N})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}},\end{cases}

For
{\mathcal{J}}_{1}
, noting that
{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
is also a smoothing operator, we apply Lemma
2.1
(2) to obtain

{\mathcal{J}}_{1}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(Q_{p}\left(F_{N},F_{N}\right)\right)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}.

Since we assume
N>L
, we may apply Corollary
2.1
(1) to obtain

{\mathcal{J}}_{1}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(Q_{p}\left(F_{N},F_{N}\right)\right)\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|Q_{p}\left(F_{N},F_{N}\right)m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}.

Applying Lemma
2.5
yields

{\mathcal{J}}_{1}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|F_{N}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}.

For
{\mathcal{J}}_{2}
, since
{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\circ{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}={\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}
, Corollary
2.1
(2) gives
have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(Q_{p}\left(F_{N},F_{N}\right)\right)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left({Q_{p}(F_{N},F_{N})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}}\right)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(Q_{p}\left(F_{N},F_{N}\right)\right)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left({Q_{p}(F_{N},F_{N})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|Q_{p}(F_{N},F_{N})\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}.

Applying Lemma
2.5
again, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we obtain

{\mathcal{J}}_{2}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|F_{N}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k-1/2+\gamma+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}.

Combining the estimates for
{\mathcal{J}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{J}}_{2}
yields

{\mathcal{I}}_{11}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|F_{N}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k-1/2+\gamma+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{R^{1+\varepsilon}}{N}\|F_{N}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

For
{\mathcal{I}}_{12}
, we write

{\mathcal{I}}_{12}=\left\langle{Q_{p}(F_{N},F_{N})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k-1},{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,m_{k})m_{1}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,m_{k+1}}\right\rangle.

For the first argument, applying Lemma
2.5
as before yields

\|Q_{p}(F_{N},F_{N})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|F_{N}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k-1/2+\gamma+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}.

For the second argument, applying Lemma
2.1
(2) twice gives

\displaystyle\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,m_{k})m_{1}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,m_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,m_{k})\,m_{1}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,m_{k+1})\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,m_{k+1})-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,m_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

This yields the estimate for
{\mathcal{I}}_{12}
:

{\mathcal{I}}_{12}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|F_{N}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

For
{\mathcal{I}}_{13}
and
{\mathcal{I}}_{14}
, we first estimate the weighted difference between
F_{N}={\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R}\,\psi^{R})
and
{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R}
:

\left\|\left({F_{N}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R}}\right)\,m_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|F_{N}\,m_{k}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R}\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k})\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R}\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k})-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

Applying Lemma
2.1
(2) to the first term yields

\|F_{N}\,m_{k}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R}\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|f_{N}^{R}\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{L}{N}\|f_{N}^{R}\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}},

and using Corollary
2.1
for the second term gives

\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R}\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k})-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|f_{N}^{R}\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence

(3.7)

\left\|\left({F_{N}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R}}\right)\,m_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|f_{N}^{R}\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}.

From this result, similarly to the proof of Corollary
2.1
(1), when
L<N
we also have

(3.8)

\|F_{N}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f_{N}^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}

Returning to
{\mathcal{I}}_{13}
and
{\mathcal{I}}_{14}
, Lemma
2.5
implies

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{I}}_{13}=\left\langle{Q_{p}(F_{N}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R},F_{N})\,m_{k-\gamma/2}\,,\,{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Q_{p}(F_{N}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R},F_{N})\,m_{k-\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}.

Applying Lemma
2.5
again to
Q_{p}
and using (
3.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|Q_{p}(F_{N}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R},F_{N})\,m_{k-\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left({F_{N}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R}}\right)\,m_{k-\gamma/2}\right\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k+1/2+\gamma/2+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\left({F_{N}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R}}\right)\,m_{k+1/2+\gamma/2+\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k-\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|f_{N}^{R}\,m_{k-\gamma/2-1}\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k+1/2+\gamma/2+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{L}{N}\|f_{N}^{R}\,m_{k-1/2+\gamma/2+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k-\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{L}{N}R^{1/2+\varepsilon}\|f_{N}^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\|f_{N}^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}.

Here we use (
3.8
). Thus we obtain the bound for
{\mathcal{I}}_{13}
:

{\mathcal{I}}_{13}\lesssim\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\|f_{N}^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}.

By the same reasoning, we obtain the same bound for
{\mathcal{I}}_{14}
:

{\mathcal{I}}_{14}\lesssim\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\|f_{N}^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}.

Combining these terms and using (
3.8
), we obtain the estimate for
{\mathcal{I}}_{1}
:

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{I}}_{1}={\mathcal{I}}_{11}+{\mathcal{I}}_{12}+{\mathcal{I}}_{13}+{\mathcal{I}}_{14}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{R^{1+\varepsilon}}{N}\|f_{N}^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\|f_{N}^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\|f_{N}^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|F_{N}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}\left({\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}}\right).

We introduce the notation
g_{N}={\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)
. Since
\psi^{R}
is compactly supported in
B(0,R)
, we may write
\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}=\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}
.

Furthermore, since
F_{N}={\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R}\,\psi^{R})
and
f_{N}^{R}=f^{R}+g
, using (
3.7
) and
L\leq N
we obtain

\displaystyle\|F_{N}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{L}{N}\|f_{N}^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2-1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f^{R})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|f_{N}^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

Using
\psi^{R}\leq 1
and Corollary
2.1
(1), we have

\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f^{R})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f^{R})\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}=\|f\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+1}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

Similarly, we have
\|f_{N}^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}
. Combining these results, we obtain the estimate for
{\mathcal{I}}_{1}
:

(3.9)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{I}}_{1}\lesssim\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\left({\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k}}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}}\right)\left({\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+1}}+\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}}\right)\left({\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+1}}^{2}\left({\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}}\right)+\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\left({\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+1}}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}}\right)\left({\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\right).

Estimate for
{\mathcal{I}}_{2}
.
Since
\psi^{R}
is compactly supported in
B(0,R)
, we have
Q_{p}({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\psi^{R},{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\psi^{R})=Q({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\psi^{R},{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\psi^{R})
on
[-L,L]^{3}\subset\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}
. (Here we regard
{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})
as its zero extension to
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}
, so
\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}=\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}
, although
{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})
is not continuous.) Therefore, with
g=f_{N}^{R}-f^{R}
, we split
{\mathcal{I}}_{2}
as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{Q({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R},{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R})-Q({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f^{R})\,\psi^{R},{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f^{R})\,\psi^{R})\,,\,{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)\,m_{k}^{2}\,\psi^{R}}\right\rangle={\mathcal{I}}_{21}+{\mathcal{I}}_{22}+{\mathcal{I}}_{23},

where, introducing the notation
g_{N}={\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g)
,

\begin{cases}{\mathcal{I}}_{21}\,:=\left\langle{Q(f^{R}\,\psi^{R},g_{N}\,\psi^{R})\,,\,g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\,{\langle v\rangle}^{2k}}\right\rangle,\\[5.0pt]
{\mathcal{I}}_{22}\,:=\left\langle{Q(({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}-I)(f^{R})\,\psi^{R},g_{N}\,\psi^{R})\,,\,g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\,{\langle v\rangle}^{2k}}\right\rangle,\\[5.0pt]
{\mathcal{I}}_{23}\,:=\left\langle{Q(g_{N}\,\psi^{R},{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R})\,,\,g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\,{\langle v\rangle}^{2k}}\right\rangle,\end{cases}

since
m_{k}^{2}={\langle v\rangle}^{2k}
on
B(0,R)
.

For
{\mathcal{I}}_{21}
, applying Theorem
2.2
and noting that
f^{R}
is non-negative, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle{Q(f^{R}\,\psi^{R},g_{N}\,\psi^{R})\,,\,g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\,{\langle v\rangle}^{2k}}\right\rangle+K\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{k}}\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{1}_{\gamma}}\|f^{R}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}+C_{k}\|f^{R}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k}}^{2},

where the constant
K
depends only on a lower bound of
\|f^{R}\psi^{R}\|_{L^{1}}
and an upper bound of
\|f^{R}\psi^{R}\|_{L^{1}_{2}}+\|f^{R}\psi^{R}\|_{L\log L}
. Since
f^{R}=f\,\psi^{R}
with
f
being the solution to the Boltzmann equation satisfying
\|f\|_{L^{1}}=1,\|f\|_{L^{1}_{2}}=3,\|f\|_{L\log L}\leq\mathsf{H}
, for
R>3
we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|f^{R}\psi^{R}\|_{L^{1}}=\|f\|_{L^{1}}-\|f\,(1-(\psi^{R})^{2})\|_{L^{1}}>\frac{1}{2},

\displaystyle\|f^{R}\psi^{R}\|_{L^{1}_{2}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}_{2}}=3,\ \|f^{R}\psi^{R}\|_{L\log L}\leq\|f\|_{L\log L}\leq H.

Hence, the constant
K
depends only on
\mathsf{H}
.

For
{\mathcal{I}}_{22}
, we return to the torus and use an estimation similar to that for
{\mathcal{I}}_{13}
. Applying Lemma
2.5
yields

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{22}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{Q_{p}(({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}-I)(f^{R})\,\psi^{R},g_{N}\,\psi^{R})\,m_{k-\gamma/2},\,g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\|({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}-I)(f^{R})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+1/2+\gamma/2+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k-\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}-I)(f^{R})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k-\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+1/2+\gamma/2+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}.

To estimate the projection error
\|({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}-I)(f^{R})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}
for general
k>0
, we decompose it as follows:

\|({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}-I)(f^{R})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}-I)(f^{R})\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}-I)(f^{R}\,m_{k})\|_{L^{2}}+\|({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}f^{R})\,m_{k}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f^{R}\,m_{k})\|_{L^{2}}.

For the first term, since
f^{R}=f\,\psi^{R}\in H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})
, the weighted function
f^{R}\,m_{k}
inherits the same regularity. Therefore, by the spectral approximation property,
\|({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}-I)(f^{R}\,m_{k})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{R}{N}\|f^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}
. As
f^{R}=f\,\psi^{R}
is supported in
B(0,R)
, we have
\|f^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{H^{1}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}=\|f^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{H^{1}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}
. For the second term, Lemma
2.1
(2) gives

\|({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}f^{R})\,m_{k}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f^{R}\,m_{k})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{R}{N}\|f^{R}\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{R}{N}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k-1}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

Combining these estimates yields

(3.11)

\|({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}-I)(f^{R})\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{R}{N}\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

Substituting this bound into the estimate for
{\mathcal{I}}_{22}
gives

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{22}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{R}{N}\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+1/2+\gamma/2+\varepsilon}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+1/2+\gamma/2+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The term
{\mathcal{I}}_{23}
involves the bilinear collision operator with the numerical solution
g_{N}
. Applying Theorem
2.1
yields

\displaystyle\left\langle{Q(g_{N}\,\psi^{R},{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\,\psi^{R})\,,\,g\,\psi^{R}\,{\langle v\rangle}^{2k}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{k}}\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\psi^{R}\|_{L^{1}_{\gamma}}\|g_{N}\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}+C_{2}\|g_{N}\psi^{R}\|_{L^{1}_{\gamma}}\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}\|g_{N}\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}

\displaystyle+C_{k}\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\|g_{N}\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k}}^{2},

where we use the fact that
g=f_{N}^{R}-f^{R}
and hence
{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f_{N}^{R})={\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f^{R})+g_{N}
.

We now combine the three terms
{\mathcal{I}}_{21}
,
{\mathcal{I}}_{22}
, and
{\mathcal{I}}_{23}
. For
k\geq 8
, the embedding

holds. Since
g\psi^{R}
is compactly supported in

, we have

\|g_{N}\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}=\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}\leq\|g_{N}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}.

Applying (
2.16
) gives
\|g_{N}\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}
and
\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f^{R})\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\lesssim\|f^{R}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}=\|f\,\psi^{R}\,{\langle v\rangle}^{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{2}

\displaystyle+K\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{k}}\|f\|_{L^{1}_{\gamma}}\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}+\left({\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{k}}+C_{2}}\right)\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|g_{N}\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left({\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{k}}+C_{2}}\right)\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}+C_{k}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{3}

\displaystyle+C_{k}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{3}\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Since
\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}\leq\epsilon\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}+C_{\epsilon}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}
for some sufficiently small positive
\epsilon
to be chosen, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{2}

\displaystyle+\left({K-\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{k}}\|f\|_{L^{1}_{\gamma}}-(C_{1}+C_{2})\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}-\epsilon-C_{3}\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}}\right)\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}\left({1+\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}}\right)\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{k}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{3}.

Here we also use that for
k\geq 8
,
\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{k}}+C_{2}\leq C_{1}+C_{2}
. We denote

, an absolute constant.
Since

, we choose
k
sufficiently large such that

, and select
\epsilon<K/4
sufficiently small. This yields

(3.12)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{2}

\displaystyle+\left({\frac{K}{2}-C_{0}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}-C_{3}\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}}\right)\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}\left({1+\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}}\right)\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{k}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{3}.

Since the constant
K
depends only on
\mathsf{H}
, the choice of

also depends only on
\mathsf{H}
.

Estimate for

.
We denote

and split
{\mathcal{I}}_{3}

For
{\mathcal{I}}_{31}
, applying Corollary
2.1
(2) gives

\|g_{N}\,m_{k}\,\psi^{R}-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(g\,m_{k}\,\psi^{R})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|g\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}.

Using Lemma
2.5
and noting that
1/2+\gamma+\varepsilon<2
since

, we obtain

{\mathcal{I}}_{31}\leq\frac{L}{N}\|Q_{p}(F^{\prime}_{N},F^{\prime}_{N})\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|g\,m_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|F^{\prime}_{N}\,m_{k+2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

Since
\psi^{R}\leq 1
and
F^{\prime}_{N}={\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f^{R})\,\psi^{R}
, Corollary
2.1
(1) yields

\|F^{\prime}_{N}\,m_{k+2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f^{R})\,m_{k+2}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f^{R}\,m_{k+2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

Consequently, the estimate for
{\mathcal{I}}_{31}

{\mathcal{I}}_{31}\lesssim\frac{L}{N}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}^{2}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

For
{\mathcal{I}}_{32}
, we use a technique similar to that for
{\mathcal{I}}_{22}
to handle
(I-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}})
. First, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{32}\leq\|(I-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}})\left({Q_{p}(F^{\prime}_{N},F^{\prime}_{N})\,m_{k}}\right)\|_{L^{2}}\|g\,m_{k}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}}.

Applying (
2.11
) yields

\|(I-{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}})\left({Q_{p}(F^{\prime}_{N},F^{\prime}_{N})\,m_{k}}\right)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{R}{N}\|Q_{p}(F^{\prime}_{N},F^{\prime}_{N})\,m_{k}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\lesssim\frac{R}{N}\|Q_{p}(F^{\prime}_{N},F^{\prime}_{N})\,m_{k}\|_{H^{1}}.

Since
F_{N}^{\prime}
is compactly supported in

, the periodic collision operator coincides with the original one on
[-L,L]^{3}\subset\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}
, i.e.,
Q_{p}(F^{\prime}_{N},F^{\prime}_{N})=Q(F^{\prime}_{N},F^{\prime}_{N})
. Therefore,

\|Q_{p}(F^{\prime}_{N},F^{\prime}_{N})\,m_{k}\|_{H^{1}}=\|Q(F_{N}^{\prime},F_{N}^{\prime})\|_{H^{1}_{k}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim\|F_{N}^{\prime}\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}^{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

Consequently, we obtain the estimate

{\mathcal{I}}_{32}\lesssim\frac{R}{N}\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}^{2}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

For
{\mathcal{I}}_{33}
, we rewrite it as an inner product on
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}
:

{\mathcal{I}}_{33}=\left\langle{\left({Q(F^{\prime}_{N},F^{\prime}_{N})-Q(f,f)}\right)\,{\langle v\rangle}^{k}\,,\,g\,\psi^{R}\,{\langle v\rangle}^{k}}\right\rangle\leq\|Q(F^{\prime}_{N},F^{\prime}_{N})-Q(f,f)\|_{L^{2}_{k}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

Since
Q
is a bilinear operator, applying Theorem
2.1
(2) yields

\displaystyle\|Q(F^{\prime}_{N},F^{\prime}_{N})-Q(f,f)\|_{L^{2}_{k}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\leq\|Q(F^{\prime}_{N}-f,F^{\prime}_{N})\|_{L^{2}_{k}}+\|Q(f,f-F^{\prime}_{N})\|_{L^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|F^{\prime}_{N}-f\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma}}(\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma}}+\|F^{\prime}_{N}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma}}).

By (
2.16
), we have

\|F^{\prime}_{N}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}=\|{\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}(f^{R})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}\lesssim\|f^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}=\|f^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

To estimate
F^{\prime}_{N}-f
, we decompose the difference as

\displaystyle\|F^{\prime}_{N}-f\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\leq\|(F^{\prime}_{N}-f^{R}\,\psi^{R})m_{k+\gamma}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}+\|f^{R}\psi^{R}-f\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}

\displaystyle=\|({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}-I)(f^{R})\,\psi^{R}\,m_{k+\gamma}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}+\|f\,((\psi^{R})^{2}-1)\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

Since
|(\psi^{R})^{2}-1|\leq\mathbf{1}_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}\backslash B(0,R/2)}
, we have

(3.13)

\|f\,((\psi^{R})^{2}-1)\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim R^{-l}\|f\,((\psi^{R})^{2}-1)\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma+l}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\leq R^{-l}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+l+\gamma}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

Thus the estimate for
F^{\prime}_{N}-f
becomes

(3.14)

\|F^{\prime}_{N}-f\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\lesssim\|({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}-I)(f^{R})\,m_{k+\gamma}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}+R^{-l}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+l+\gamma}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

For the first term, applying (
3.11
) yields

\|({\tilde{{\mathcal{P}}}_{N}}-I)(f^{R})\,m_{k+\gamma}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{L})}\lesssim\frac{R}{N}\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}.

Consequently, the estimate for
{\mathcal{I}}_{33}
is

(3.15)

{\mathcal{I}}_{33}\lesssim\left({\frac{R}{N}\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}+R^{-l}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+l+\gamma}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}}\right)\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma}}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

Combining these three parts yields

(3.16)

{\mathcal{I}}_{3}\lesssim\left({\frac{R}{N}+R^{-l}}\right)\left({\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+l+\gamma}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}}\right)^{2}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

Having established estimates for the three error terms
{\mathcal{I}}_{1}
,
{\mathcal{I}}_{2}
, and
{\mathcal{I}}_{3}
, we now combine them to obtain the energy inequality for the error function
g
. Summing (
3.9

3.12

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left({\frac{K}{2}-C_{0}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}-C_{3}\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}}\right)\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2})\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{3}

\displaystyle+\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+1}}^{2}\left({\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\left({\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+1}}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}}\right)\left({\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\right).

\displaystyle+\left({\frac{R}{N}+R^{-l}}\right)\left({\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+l+\gamma}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}}\right)^{2}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

where the constant depends only on
k,l,\varepsilon
. Then, applying Young’s inequality, we estimate the following terms:

\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+1}}^{2}\left({\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}}\right)\leq\frac{K}{4}\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{K}\frac{R^{3+2\varepsilon}}{N^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+1}}^{4},

Consequently, (
3.17
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left({\frac{K}{4}-C_{0}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}-C_{3}\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\left({\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}}\right)}\right)\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2})\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{3}

\displaystyle+\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\left({\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+1}}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}}\right)\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left({\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}+R^{-l}}\right)^{2}\left({\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}}+\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+l+\gamma}}}\right)^{4}

\displaystyle+\left({\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}+R^{-l}}\right)^{2}\left({\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}}+\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+l+\gamma}}}\right)^{4}.

To proceed, we assume that

is sufficiently large such that

3.17
) as

(3.18)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left({\frac{K}{4}-C_{0}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}-C_{3}\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\left({\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}}\right)}\right)\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left({\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}+R^{-l}}\right)^{2}\left({\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}}+\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+l+\gamma}}}\right)^{4}.

We now establish uniform bounds for the exact solution
f
. Since the initial data

satisfies Assumption
1.21
, Proposition

guarantees that

(3.19)

\sup_{t\geq 0}\left({\|f\|_{H^{1}_{k+2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+l+\gamma}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}}\right)<M.

Treating
M
as a constant depending only on
k,l
, we simplify (
3.18
) to

(3.20)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left({\frac{K}{4}-C_{0}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}-C_{3}\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\left({M+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}}\right)}\right)\|g_{N}\,\psi^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+M^{2})\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{3}+\left({\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}+R^{-l}}\right)^{2}M^{4}.

For the initial data

) yields

\|g|_{t=0}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{R}{N}\|f_{0}\,\psi^{R}\|_{H^{1}_{k}}\lesssim\frac{R}{N}M.

Since

is a constant, we can express this estimate as

where

(3.21)

c\,\frac{R}{N}\leq\min\left({\frac{K}{8C_{0}},1}\right),\ \ \ \frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\leq\frac{K}{8C_{3}\,(M+1)}

the continuity of

guarantees the existence of

On the interval
[0,T_{*}]

\frac{K}{4}-C_{0}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}-C_{3}\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}\left({M+\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}}\right)\geq 0,

holds, and treating

as a constant, (
3.20
) reduces to

\frac{d}{dt}\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\kappa\,\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{4}\left({\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}+R^{-l}}\right)^{2},

for some constants

depending only on

. Applying Gronwall’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\|g(t)\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,\left({\|g|_{t=0}\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{4}\left({\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}+R^{-l}}\right)^{2}\,\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\kappa s}\,ds}\right)\,e^{2\kappa t}

\displaystyle\leq\,\mathbf{C}^{2}\left({\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}+R^{-l}}\right)^{2}\,e^{2\kappa t},\quad\,\forall t\,\in[0,T_{*}]\,,

where
\mathbf{C}^{2}=c+\frac{C_{4}}{2\kappa}
. This estimate demonstrates that by choosing
R
and
N
sufficiently large such that

\mathbf{C}\left({\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}+R^{-l}}\right)\exp(\kappa T)\leq\min\left({\frac{K}{8C_{0}},1}\right).

A sufficient condition for this bound is
R>\mathbf{R}(T)
and
N>\mathbf{N}(R,T)
, where

\mathbf{R}(t)\,:=\,\left({\frac{2\mathbf{C}\,\exp\left(\kappa\,t\right)}{\min\{K/(8C_{0}),1\}}}\right)^{1/l},

and

\mathbf{N}(R,t):=\frac{2\mathbf{C}\,R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}\,\exp\left(\kappa\,t\right)}{\min\{K/(8C_{0}),1\}}.

These conditions also guarantee
R>3
,
L\leq N
, and
N>R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}
when the constants are chosen appropriately. Moreover, by taking
\mathbf{C}
sufficiently large, condition (
3.21
) is satisfied. Consequently, when
R>\mathbf{R}(T)
and

, we obtain the estimate:

\|g(t)\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\mathbf{C}\left({\frac{R^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{N}+R^{-l}}\right)\,e^{\kappa t}\leq 1,\ \ \forall\,t\in[0,T].

Using a classical iteration scheme together with the previous estimate, we establish the well-posedness of (
3.4
), and hence of the numerical equation (
1.18
).

To complete the proof, we need to extend the error estimate from the torus domain
{\mathcal{D}}_{L}
to the whole space
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}
. The numerical solution
f_{N}
is defined as the zero extension of

outside
{\mathcal{D}}_{L}
. We will estimate the error on two regions: the inner region

where the numerical solution is well-resolved, and the outer region
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}\backslash B(0,R/2)
where the error is controlled by the decay of the exact solution. We now consider the zero extension
f_{N}
defined by (

). Since
m_{k}={\langle v\rangle}^{k}
on

and
g|_{B(0,R)}=(f^{N}_{R}-f^{R})|_{B(0,R)}=(f_{N}-f)|_{B(0,R/2)}

\|f_{N}-f\|_{L^{2}_{k}(B(0,R/2))}=\|g\,m_{k}\|_{L^{2}(B(0,R/2))},

for all

. For the domain
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}\backslash B(0,R/2)

\|f^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}\backslash B(0,R/2))}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}\backslash B(0,R/2))}\leq(R/2)^{-l}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k+l}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3})}\leq(R/2)^{-l}M\lesssim R^{-l}.

On the domain
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}\backslash B(0,R/2)

\displaystyle\|f_{N}-f\|_{L^{2}_{k}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}\backslash B(0,R/2))}

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{N}-f^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}\backslash B(0,R/2))}+\|f^{R}\|_{L^{2}_{k}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}\backslash B(0,R/2))}+\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}\backslash B(0,R/2))}

where

With the new constant

(i)
. ∎

In this section, we collect two classical results concerning the propagation properties of solutions to the Boltzmann equation (

The first result establishes the uniform-in-time propagation of Sobolev regularity with polynomial weights:

Theorem A.1
.

(
[
23

Explicitly, we may take

Next, we state a result regarding the propagation of stretched exponential moments, which guarantees the persistence of rapid decay for the initial data:

Theorem A.2
.

(
[
20
]
Corollary 1.4) Suppose that
