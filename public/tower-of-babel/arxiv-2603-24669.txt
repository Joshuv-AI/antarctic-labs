The Universe Originating from an Empty Planck-Size Torus
Bartosz Fornal

Abstract
We consider a Universe with a three-torus topology which before inflation is devoid of any matter or radiation. Its pre-inflationary evolution is driven solely by Casimir energies of the existing fields, with a radiation-like equation of state. We show that, quite strikingly, with the appropriate number of fermions and bosons in the theory, such a Universe evolving from Planck size at Planck time has the correct critical energy density today. Moreover, assuming typical parameters for inflation and reheating, the three-torus Universe can be several Hubble radii across at present time, which is precisely the size hinted by the anomaly in the cosmic microwave background observed at low multipole moments. We derive a relation between the size of the Universe, the number of e e -folds of inflation, and the energy density decrease during reheating, which we then use to determine the parameter values consistent with the anomaly and the lower bound on the size of the Universe from Planck.



1. Introduction

The question about the size and shape of the Universe is one of the most intriguing open problems in modern cosmology.
Although astrophysical observations provide accurate information about the geometry of spacetime, its topology remains a mystery. Moreover, it is not even known if the Universe is compact, and thus finite, or infinite.

Currently, the best testing ground for the topology of the Universe is the cosmic microwave background (CMB), with data provided by WMAP
Bennett et al. 2013
and Planck
Ade et al. 2016
;
Aghanim et al. 2020
. A nontrivial topology would manifest itself as correlations between various regions in the sky. Searches for the resulting repeated patterns, such as
circles in the sky
, have been proposed and conducted
Cornish et al. 1998
;
Levin et al. 1998
;
Cornish et al. 2004
;
Shapiro Key et al. 2007
;
Aslanyan and Manohar 2012
;
Vaudrevange et al. 2012
;
Aslanyan et al. 2013
;
Bernui et al. 2018
;
Akrami et al. 2024
, however, without a strong conclusion.

In this paper, we consider a Universe with a general three-torus topology. Interestingly, it was argued that such a toroidal universe should dominate the quantum creation of universes from nothing
Zeldovich and Starobinsky 1984
;
Coule and Martin 2000
;
Linde 2004
, although recently this claim has been challenged
Guth and Vilenkin 2025
. Here we will not be concerned with how a three-torus Universe was created, and we will just consider its evolution starting from the Planck time, with the appealing assumption that its initial size was the Planck length.

The compact topology of the three-torus provides an extra contribution to the energy density of the Universe in the form of Casimir energies. The general formula for those Casimir energies was derived in
Fornal and Wise 2011
, where it was shown that for a three-torus topology there exist micron-sized vacuum configurations stable with respect to the shape moduli, and unstable with respect to the volume modulus.

Those results were applied to the evolution of the three-torus Universe and used to calculate the particle production rate from the dynamical Casimir effect
Fornal 2012
. In that reference it was shown that, starting from a Planck size and with the pre-inflationary evolution driven only by Casimir energies, assuming 72 e-fold of inflation ending at
10^{-33}
s leads to the current size of the Universe of approximately ten Hubble radii.

Here we extend that analysis with a more careful treatment of reheating and by investigating the implications of the anomaly in the CMB observed at low multipole moments, which provides hints of an upper bound on the size of the three-torus Universe.
We also leave the number of
e
-folds and the energy density drop during reheating as free parameters, using Planck’s lower bounds on the size of the Universe from the search for repeated patterns in the CMB spectrum and the aforementioned upper bound suggested by the low-
\ell
anomaly to set limits on inflation and reheating. Matching the critical energy density at Planck time leads to a striking prediction for the number of degrees of freedom at high energies.

5. Evolution of the Universe

A Universe with a three-torus topology is flat, so there is no curvature term. Therefore, throughout its evolution the energy density is equal to the critical density, as governed by the Friedmann equation,

\displaystyle H^{2}=\bigg(\frac{\dot{b}}{b}\bigg)^{2}=\frac{\rho}{3M_{P}^{2}}\ .

(27)

This ensures that the energy density will be critical also at the present time, as observations indicate
Aghanim et al. 2020

We start with the assumption, seemingly natural, that the three-torus Universe is a Planck length across at Planck time,

\displaystyle b(t_{P})=\ell_{P}\ ,

(28)

\displaystyle\rho(t_{P})=\frac{3M_{P}^{2}}{t_{P}^{2}}\approx 2.6\times 10^{75}\ \rm GeV^{4}\ .

6. Implications of CMB data

Measurements of the CMB such as those made by WMAP and Planck

Ade et al. 2016

Planck’s search for matched circles in the sky yields the lower bound on the radius of the largest sphere inscribed in the fundamental domain as

7. Conclusions

It is quite remarkable that Casimir energies in a Universe with a three-torus topology can alone account for the correct critical energy density at Planck time, which then evolves according to the Friedmann equation to the energy density observed today.
This provides a prediction for the difference between the fermionic and bosonic degree of freedom of
(n_{F}-n_{B})\approx 220
in the high energy extension of theStandard Model at the Planck scale.

We traced the evolution of the volume modulus throughout the different epochs, expressing the current size of the Universe in terms of only two parameters: the number of
e
-folds during inflation and the energy density drop during reheating. For a natural choice of values for those parameters, a three-torus Universe that started from a Planck size at the Planck time can have several Hubble radii across today.

Using the results of existing searches for repeated patterns in the sky and investigating the suppressed contribution to the quadrupole moment in the CMB spectrum, we determined the preferred range for the size of the Universe and the resulting constraints on inflation and reheating, which are very restrictive. It would be interesting to investigate the impact of the three-torus topology with the metric considered here on other low-
\ell
moments, especially since the numerical analyses conducted in the literature assumed only a rectangular torus.

In
Vaudrevange et al. 2012
;
Aslanyan et al. 2013
;
Bernui et al. 2018
;
Akrami et al. 2024
it was shown that the best fit to the CMB data is provided by the
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
topology, where the diameter of the circle is
1.4\,\chi_{\rm rec}
today. In the spirit of our work, one could investigate the constraints on inflation and reheating obtained by starting the evolution from a Planck size circle and ending it on this best fit value. The formulas for Casimir energies simplify considerably in that case.

Very recently it has been demonstrated in
Negro et al. 2026
that Casimir energies in a Universe with a three-torus topology may have an impact on the post-inflationary anisotropy, providing novel measurable signals in the CMB. Since the calculation was done for a rectangular torus, it would be intriguing to investigate whether the metric considered here provides additional contributions to those anisotropies.
