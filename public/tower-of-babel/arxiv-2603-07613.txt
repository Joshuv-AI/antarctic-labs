Inverse Robin Spectral Problem for the p p -Laplace Operator
Farid Bozorgnia, Olimjon Eshkobilov

Abstract
We investigate an inverse Robin spectral problem for the p p -Laplace operator on a bounded domain with mixed Dirichlet-Robin boundary conditions. The aim is to identify an unknown Robin coefficient on an inaccessible boundary portion from spectral information and boundary flux data measured on an accessible part. We first establish a thin-coating asymptotic limit that extends the classical result of Friedlander and Keller from the linear Laplacian to the nonlinear p p -Laplacian. The analysis yields an effective Robin law in which the induced coefficient depends on the coating thickness through a p p –dependent power, making explicit how the nonlinearity enters via conductivity scaling. We then prove uniqueness of the Robin coefficient by linearizing the forward map and combining the resulting linearized equation with a boundary Cauchy unique continuation principle. Finally, we obtain a conditional local Hölder-type stability estimate (with an explicit nonlinear remainder) by combining Fréchet differentiability of the solution/measurement maps with a quantitative stability bound for the linearized inverse problem.



Keywords
: Inverse spectral problem, p-Laplacian, Robin boundary condition, Nonlinear eigenvalue problems, Stability estimate.

Introduction

Elliptic eigenvalue problems play a central role in science and engineering, with applications ranging from mechanical vibrations and quantum mechanics to biological and chemical diffusion.
Their analysis lies at the heart of both theoretical and applied mathematics, and nonlinear versions of these problems have attracted increasing attention over the past decades due to their rich mathematical structure and physical relevance.

Recovering information about a physical system from spectral data has a long history dating back to the classical works of Kac
[
25
]
and Gel’fand
[
19
]
.
In applications such as non-destructive testing
[
20
,
6
]
, electrical impedance tomography (EIT)
[
35
,
5
]
, and corrosion detection
[
3
]
, one is often faced with the task of determining unknown boundary interactions from indirect measurements. For foundational boundary-measurement methods based on singular solutions in inverse conductivity, see
[
2
]
.

A particularly important class of such problems involves
Robin boundary conditions
, which arise naturally when a domain is coated by a thin layer or interacts with an external medium.
For the classical Laplacian (
p=2
),
[
18
,
17
]
showed that a thinly coated domain can be approximated by an effective Robin boundary condition

\partial_{\nu}u+hu=0,

where the coefficient
h
depends on the thickness and conductivity of the coating.
This result underlies many inverse methods for corrosion detection
[
3
]
, non-destructive evaluation
[
20
]
, heat transfer
[
12
]
, and electromagnetic shielding
[
16
]
.
For
p=2
, inverse Robin problems are now well understood: uniqueness, reconstruction, and stability results are available even when only partial boundary data are accessible
[
38
,
21
,
11
,
10
,
9
]
. In contrast, much less is known for the nonlinear
p
-Laplacian

-\Delta_{p}u:=-\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u),\qquad p\neq 2,

which arises in the modeling of non-Newtonian fluids
[
7
]
, nonlinear elasticity, and related problems.
The parameter
p
encodes essential physical properties:
p<2
corresponds to shear-thinning fluids (such as blood or polymer solutions),
p=2
recovers the classical Newtonian case, and
p>2
describes shear-thickening fluids.
The limiting regimes
p\to 1
and
p\to\infty
lead to the total variation flow and the infinity Laplacian, respectively
[
24
,
22
]
.

Despite the extensive literature on eigenvalue optimization and spectral theory for the
p
-Laplacian
[
4
,
26
,
27
]
, inverse problems for nonlinear operators remain far less developed than their linear counterparts.
The nonlinearity destroys the superposition principle, eigenfunctions do not form an orthogonal basis, and even the structure of the spectrum is poorly understood beyond the principal eigenvalue
[
15
]
.
As a result, while inverse Robin problems for
p=2
are classical, for
p\neq 2
both uniqueness and stability of the Robin coefficient from spectral or boundary data have remained largely open.

The present work aims to close this gap. We study eigenvalue problems for the
p
-Laplacian under mixed Dirichlet–Robin boundary conditions and investigate how the Robin coefficient on an inaccessible portion of the boundary can be recovered from spectral and boundary measurements on an accessible part.
Our motivation comes from impedance and thin-coating models, where an effective Robin boundary term emerges on the exterior of a coated or corroded object.

Our main contributions are threefold. First, we establish a rigorous thin-coating limit for the
p
-Laplacian, extending the classical result of Friedlander and Keller from
p=2
to general
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Second, we prove the uniqueness of the Robin coefficient in an inverse spectral problem with partial boundary data.
Third, we derive a conditional local
Hölder-type
stability estimate with an explicit nonlinear remainder term.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 collects preliminary material on the
p
-Laplacian and its eigenvalue problem.
Section
3
establishes the thin-coating asymptotics.
Section
4
is devoted to the inverse problem, where uniqueness and stability are proved.

) that extends Friedman’s classical result to the

Thin Coating Asymptotics and Effective Robin Conditions

Thin coating problems are connected with surface reaction models and interface homogenization, and they arise in the analysis of spectral asymptotics and shape sensitivity for nonlinear operators; see
[
13
]

Such problems appear in the mathematical modeling of heterogeneous media, where a solid domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is covered by a thin layer (or coating) composed of a material with distinct physical characteristics.
Even a coating of negligible thickness may substantially modify the global response of the system in applications such as heat conduction, diffusion, and elasticity.

-\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=f
, then
u_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda u(x)
satisfies

-\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\lambda})=\lambda^{p-1}f.

This homogeneity property is preserved in the thin coating problem by the choice
\sigma_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{p-1}
, which ensures that the effective boundary condition inherits the correct scaling behavior.

The interface
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
is an interior interface in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. Across the interface
\Gamma
, the transmission conditions are

[\![\Phi_{\varepsilon}]\!]=0,\qquad[\![\sigma_{\varepsilon}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{\,p-2}\,\partial_{\nu}\Phi_{\varepsilon}]\!]=0,

where
\nu
is the unit normal on
\Gamma
pointing from
\Omega
into the coating
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with
C^{2}
boundary

\Gamma=\partial\Omega
. Let
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
be the thin layer attached to
\Gamma

of thickness
\varepsilon\rho(\xi)
, where
\rho\in C^{2}(\Gamma)
and
\rho>0
.
Fix
p\in(1,\infty)
and let
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Let
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
be an extension of
u
to
\Omega\cup\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
with

\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega\cup\Sigma_{\varepsilon})
.

(A)

If the extension satisfies a uniform
full-gradient
bound

\sup_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|\leq C
for some constant
C>0

independent of
\varepsilon
, then

\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=O(\varepsilon)\qquad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

and more precisely
\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\leq C^{p}\,|\Sigma_{\varepsilon}|
.

(B)

If
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
is the affine extension defined by

\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(\xi,t)=u(\xi)\left(1-\frac{t}{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}\right),\qquad 0<t<\varepsilon\rho(\xi),

(9)

which vanishes on the outer boundary
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, then

\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{p-1}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|u(\xi)|^{p}}{\rho(\xi)^{p-1}}\,d\sigma+O(\varepsilon)\qquad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

In particular, the right-hand side equals

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|u(\xi)|^{p}}{\rho(\xi)^{p-1}}\,d\sigma+o(1)
.

Proof.

We use boundary normal coordinates: for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, each

x\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
can be written uniquely as

x=\Phi(\xi,t):=\xi+t\,\nu(\xi),\qquad\xi\in\Gamma,\quad t\in(0,\varepsilon\rho(\xi)),

where
\nu(\xi)
is the outward unit normal to
\Gamma
.
The Jacobian satisfies (uniformly in
\xi
for
t
small)

dx=J(\xi,t)\,dt\,d\sigma(\xi),\qquad J(\xi,t)=1+O(t),

(10)

and since
0\leq t\leq\varepsilon\rho(\xi)
with
\rho
bounded on
\Gamma
, we also have

J(\xi,t)=1+O(\varepsilon)
uniformly on
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
.

Proof of (A).

Using (
10
),

|\Sigma_{\varepsilon}|=\int_{\Gamma}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}(1+O(t))\,dt\,d\sigma=\varepsilon\int_{\Gamma}\rho(\xi)\,d\sigma+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

If
\sup_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|\leq C
, then

\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\leq C^{p}\,|\Sigma_{\varepsilon}|=O(\varepsilon),

which proves (A).

Proof of (B).

Let
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
be given by (
9
). Then

\partial_{t}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(\xi,t)=-\frac{u(\xi)}{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}.

For tangential derivatives, note that on
\Gamma
(with
\nabla_{\Gamma}
denoting the
surface gradient),

\nabla_{\Gamma}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(\xi,t)=\Bigl(1-\frac{t}{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}\Bigr)\nabla_{\Gamma}u(\xi)+u(\xi)\,\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\,\nabla_{\Gamma}\!\Bigl(\frac{1}{\rho(\xi)}\Bigr).

Since
0\leq t/\varepsilon\leq\rho(\xi)
and
\rho,\nabla_{\Gamma}\rho
are bounded on
\Gamma
,
there exists
C_{0}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
such that

\sup_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla_{\Gamma}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|\leq C_{0}.

(11)

Moreover, the Euclidean gradient in the tubular neighborhood satisfies

\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(\xi,t)=\partial_{t}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(\xi,t)\,\nu(\xi)+\bigl(I+O(t)\bigr)\nabla_{\Gamma}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(\xi,t),

hence, using (
11
) and
t=O(\varepsilon)
,

|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(\xi,t)|^{p}=\Bigl|\frac{u(\xi)}{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}\Bigr|^{p}+R_{\varepsilon}(\xi,t),

(12)

where
R_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the pointwise bound

|R_{\varepsilon}(\xi,t)|\leq C\Bigl(\Bigl|\frac{u(\xi)}{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}\Bigr|^{p-1}+1\Bigr)

for a constant
C
independent of
\varepsilon
(this follows from the inequality

(a+b)^{p}\leq a^{p}+C_{p}(a^{p-1}b+b^{p})
with
a,b\geq 0
and the bound (
11
)).

Multiply (
12
) by
\varepsilon^{p-1}
and integrate using (
10
):

\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{p-1}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=I_{\varepsilon}+E_{\varepsilon},

where

I_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\Gamma}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}\varepsilon^{p-1}\Bigl|\frac{u(\xi)}{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}\Bigr|^{p}J(\xi,t)\,dt\,d\sigma(\xi),\qquad E_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\Gamma}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}\varepsilon^{p-1}R_{\varepsilon}(\xi,t)J(\xi,t)\,dt\,d\sigma(\xi).

For the main term, since
J(\xi,t)=1+O(\varepsilon)
,

I_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\Gamma}\varepsilon^{p-1}\frac{|u(\xi)|^{p}}{(\varepsilon\rho(\xi))^{p}}\left(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}(1+O(\varepsilon))\,dt\right)d\sigma(\xi)=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|u(\xi)|^{p}}{\rho(\xi)^{p-1}}\,d\sigma(\xi)+O(\varepsilon).

For the error term, using the bound on
R_{\varepsilon}
, the uniform boundedness of
J
,
and the fact that
\rho
is bounded above/below on
\Gamma
, we obtain

|E_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\int_{\Gamma}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}\varepsilon^{p-1}\Bigl(\Bigl|\frac{u(\xi)}{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}\Bigr|^{p-1}+1\Bigr)\,dt\,d\sigma(\xi)\leq C\varepsilon\int_{\Gamma}(|u(\xi)|^{p-1}+1)\,d\sigma(\xi)=O(\varepsilon).

Combining the estimates yields

\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{p-1}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|u(\xi)|^{p}}{\rho(\xi)^{p-1}}\,d\sigma(\xi)+O(\varepsilon),

which proves (B).
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Consider the two-phase problem (
8
) with coating thickness
\varepsilon\rho(\xi)
and
\sigma=\varepsilon^{p-1}
in the coating
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
. As
\varepsilon\to 0
, the effective Robin coefficient is given by

h(\xi)=\frac{1}{\rho(\xi)^{p-1}}.

For constant thickness
\rho\equiv 1
, this reduces to
h=1
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
(B), the energy contribution from the coating layer for the affine extension of a function with trace
u(\xi)
on
\Gamma
satisfies

\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{p-1}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\to\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|u(\xi)|^{p}}{\rho(\xi)^{p-1}}\,d\sigma\qquad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

The limiting Robin problem (with boundary condition
|\nabla u|^{p-2}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+h(x)|u|^{p-2}u=0
on
\Gamma
) has an associated boundary energy

E_{\text{Robin}}=\int_{\Gamma}h(\xi)|u(\xi)|^{p}\,d\sigma.

To determine the effective Robin coefficient, we match the coating energy with the limiting Robin energy. Setting

\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|u(\xi)|^{p}}{\rho(\xi)^{p-1}}\,d\sigma=\int_{\Gamma}h(\xi)|u(\xi)|^{p}\,d\sigma

and noting that this must hold for all admissible traces
u(\xi)
, we conclude

h(\xi)=\frac{1}{\rho(\xi)^{p-1}}.

∎

To compare with the classical result, we note that for
p=2
, the result by Friedman
[
18
]
establishes an effective Robin coefficient by asymptotic analysis of thin-coating problems. Our Theorem
3.6
recovers this classical result: when
p=2
, we obtain
h=1/\rho
, which matches Friedman’s conclusion.

Corollary 3.5
.

When
\rho\equiv 1
, i.e., uniform coating thickness, the effective Robin coefficient simplifies to
h=1
, and the limiting problem becomes:

\begin{cases}-\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=\mu|u|^{p-2}u&\text{in }\Omega,\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{p-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{\Gamma}}+|u|^{p-2}u=0&\text{on }\Gamma.\end{cases}

(13)

The next theorem shows the asymptotic behavior of eigenvalues.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)
denote the principal eigenvalue of the two-phase problem (
8
), and let
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
be the corresponding normalized eigenfunction. Let
(\mu_{1},u_{1})
denote the principal eigenpair of the Robin eigenvalue problem

\begin{cases}-\nabla\cdot(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=\mu|u|^{p-2}u&\text{in }\Omega,\\[4.0pt]
|\nabla u|^{p-2}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{\Gamma}}+\dfrac{1}{\rho(\xi)^{p-1}}|u|^{p-2}u=0&\text{on }\Gamma,\end{cases}

(14)

with normalization
\|u_{1}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
and
u_{1}>0
in
\Omega
. Then

\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)=\mu_{1}+o(1)\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

and
\Phi_{\varepsilon}|_{\Omega}\rightharpoonup u_{1}
weakly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

The eigenvalue
\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)
admits the Rayleigh–Ritz characterization

\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\\
\Phi\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}|\nabla\Phi|^{p}\,dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\Phi|^{p}\,dx}.

(15)

The corresponding eigenfunction
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
is normalized by
\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}=1
and satisfies

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx=\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\Phi_{\varepsilon}\psi\,dx,\,\,\,\forall\psi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}).

(16)

For the limiting Robin problem (
14
), the principal eigenvalue is given by

\mu_{1}=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\\
u\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Gamma}h(\xi)|u|^{p}\,d\sigma}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,dx},\quad\text{where }h(\xi)=\frac{1}{\rho(\xi)^{p-1}}.

(17)

Let
u_{1}
be the corresponding positive eigenfunction normalized by
\|u_{1}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
. We show
\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)\leq\mu_{1}+o(1)
by constructing a test function. Define

\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}u_{1}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\[6.0pt]
u_{1}(\xi)\left(1-\dfrac{t}{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}\right),&x=\xi+t\nu_{\Gamma}(\xi)\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon},\end{cases}

(18)

where
0\leq t\leq\varepsilon\rho(\xi)
denotes the distance from
\Gamma
along the outer normal
\nu_{\Gamma}(\xi)
. We decompose the integral:

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|u_{1}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=1+\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx.

In the thin layer, using boundary normal coordinates, we have
dx=(1+t\kappa(\xi)+O(t^{2}))\,dt\,d\sigma(\xi)
for
t\in[0,\varepsilon\rho(\xi)]
. Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}\left|u_{1}(\xi)\left(1-\frac{t}{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}\right)\right|^{p}{\bigl(1+t\kappa(\xi)+O(t^{2})\bigr)}\,dt\,d\sigma(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}|u_{1}(\xi)|^{p}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}\left(1-\frac{t}{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}\right)^{p}\bigl(1+{O(t)}\bigr)\,dt\,d\sigma(\xi).

With
s=t/(\varepsilon\rho(\xi))
, this becomes

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle={\varepsilon\int_{\Gamma}|u_{1}(\xi)|^{p}\rho(\xi)\left[\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{p}\,ds+O(\varepsilon)\right]d\sigma(\xi)}

\displaystyle={\varepsilon\int_{\Gamma}|u_{1}(\xi)|^{p}\rho(\xi)\left[\frac{1}{p+1}+O(\varepsilon)\right]d\sigma(\xi)}=O(\varepsilon).

Since
\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{p}\,ds=1/(p+1)
and
\int_{0}^{1}s(1-s)^{p}\,ds=1/((p+1)(p+2))
, and since
\rho\in C^{2}(\Gamma)
and
\kappa\in C^{0}(\Gamma)
are bounded on the compact set
\Gamma
, we obtain
\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=\varepsilon\cdot C_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}\cdot C_{2}=O(\varepsilon)
, where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are constants depending only on
u_{1}
,
\rho
,
\kappa
, and
p
. Therefore,

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=1+O(\varepsilon).

(19)

The energy in
\Omega
satisfies
\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{\varepsilon}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{1}|^{p}\,dx
, since
\sigma_{\varepsilon}=1
in
\Omega
and
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=u_{1}
in
\Omega
. By Lemma
3.3
(B), the energy in the coating layer
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{p-1}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|u_{1}(\xi)|^{p}}{\rho(\xi)^{p-1}}\,d\sigma(\xi)+o(1).

The total energy of the test function is

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{1}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{p-1}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{1}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|u_{1}(\xi)|^{p}}{\rho(\xi)^{p-1}}\,d\sigma(\xi)+o(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{1}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Gamma}h(\xi)|u_{1}|^{p}\,d\sigma+o(1),

where we used the definition
h(\xi)=1/\rho(\xi)^{p-1}
. By the Rayleigh quotient characterization (
15
) and relation (
19
),

\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}|\nabla\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx}=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{1}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Gamma}h(\xi)|u_{1}|^{p}\,d\sigma+o(1)}{1+O(\varepsilon)}.

(20)

Since
u_{1}
is the principal eigenfunction of the Robin problem (
14
) and
\|u_{1}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
, we have

{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{1}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Gamma}h(\xi)|u_{1}|^{p}\,d\sigma=\mu_{1}.}

(21)

Substituting (
21
) into (
20
):
\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)\leq(\mu_{1}+o(1))/(1+O(\varepsilon))
. Since
(1+O(\varepsilon))^{-1}=1-O(\varepsilon)+O(\varepsilon^{2})=1+O(\varepsilon)
for small
\varepsilon
, we obtain

\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)\leq(\mu_{1}+o(1))(1+O(\varepsilon))=\mu_{1}+o(1)\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

(22)

To obtain the reverse inequality
\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)\geq\mu_{1}+o(1)
, we use a compactness argument. From (
15
) and the normalization
\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}=1
, we have

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon).

(23)

From the upper bound (
22
), we have
\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)\leq\mu_{1}+1
for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
. This indicates that the sequence
\{\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)\}
is bounded: there exists
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
such that
\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)\leq C
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
.

The restrictions
\Phi_{\varepsilon}|_{\Omega}
are bounded in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. From (
23
), we have

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{p-1}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)\leq C,

where we used
\sigma_{\varepsilon}=1
in
\Omega
and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{p-1}
in
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
. Since each term is nonnegative:

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\leq C.

(24)

For the
L^{p}
-norm, we have

\int_{\Omega}|\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=1.

(25)

Combining (
24
) and (
25
), we obtain the uniform bound

\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}|_{\Omega}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\leq C+1,

hence
\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}|_{\Omega}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C^{\prime}
for some constant
C^{\prime}
independent of
\varepsilon
.

We also show that the
L^{p}
-mass of
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
in the coating is negligible:

\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=O(\varepsilon)\qquad(\varepsilon\to 0).

(26)

Indeed, fix
\xi\in\Gamma
and set
g_{\varepsilon,\xi}(t):=\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\xi+t\nu_{\Gamma}(\xi))
for
t\in(0,\varepsilon\rho(\xi))
.
Since
\Phi_{\varepsilon}=0
on the outer boundary
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, we have
g_{\varepsilon,\xi}(\varepsilon\rho(\xi))=0
. Thus for
t\in(0,\varepsilon\rho(\xi))
,

|g_{\varepsilon,\xi}(t)|=\Bigl|\int_{t}^{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}g^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,\xi}(s)\,ds\Bigr|\leq(\varepsilon\rho(\xi))^{1/p^{\prime}}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}|g^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,\xi}(s)|^{p}\,ds\Bigr)^{1/p},

and integrating in
t
yields

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}|g_{\varepsilon,\xi}(t)|^{p}\,dt\leq C\,\varepsilon^{p}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}|g^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,\xi}(t)|^{p}\,dt\leq C\,\varepsilon^{p}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\xi+t\nu_{\Gamma}(\xi))|^{p}\,dt,

where
C
depends only on
\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}
and
p
.
Using the Jacobian formula (
10
) and integrating over
\xi\in\Gamma
, we obtain

\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\leq C\,\varepsilon^{p}\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx.

Finally, since
\varepsilon^{p-1}\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\leq\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{p-1}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\leq\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)\leq C
by (
23
) and the uniform upper bound, we get

\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\leq C\,\varepsilon^{1-p}
,
hence (
26
) follows. Consequently,

\int_{\Omega}|\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=1-\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}|\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=1+O(\varepsilon),

so in particular
\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}|_{\Omega}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\to 1
.

By the Rellich–Kondrachov theorem, there exists a subsequence (still denoted
\{\Phi_{\varepsilon}\}
) and a function
\hat{u}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that, as
\varepsilon\to 0
:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\varepsilon}|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\hat{u}\quad\text{weakly in }W^{1,p}(\Omega),

(27)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\varepsilon}|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\to\hat{u}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{p}(\Omega),

(28)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\varepsilon}|_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\to\hat{u}|_{\Gamma}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{p}(\Gamma).

(29)

From (
28
) and (
26
) (which implies
\int_{\Omega}|\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=1+O(\varepsilon)
), we obtain

\|\hat{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}|_{\Omega}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1.

(30)

Set
\hat{\lambda}:=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)
(possibly along a subsequence) so
\hat{\lambda}
is finite. We establish that the limit function
\hat{u}
satisfies the weak formulation of the Robin problem (
14
) with eigenvalue
\hat{\lambda}
.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be an arbitrary test function with compact support in
\Omega
. For sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, the support of
\phi
is contained in
\Omega\subset\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, and we can extend
\phi
by zero to
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. From the weak formulation (
16
) for
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
, since
\text{supp}(\phi)\subset\Omega
and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}=1
in
\Omega
:

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx=\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega}|\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\Phi_{\varepsilon}\phi\,dx.

(31)

We pass to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
in (
31
). Since
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\to\hat{u}
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
by (
28
), and the map
s\mapsto|s|^{p-2}s
is continuous, we have for any
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
:

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega}|\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\Phi_{\varepsilon}\phi\,dx=\hat{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}|\hat{u}|^{p-2}\hat{u}\phi\,dx.

(32)

The map
F(\xi)=|\xi|^{p-2}\xi
is monotone and maps
L^{p}
into
L^{p^{\prime}}
. Hence, up to a subsequence,

F(\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon})\rightharpoonup\eta
weakly in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
By monotonicity,

\int_{\Omega}\bigl(F(\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon})-F(w)\bigr)\cdot\bigl(\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}-w\bigr)\,dx\geq 0\qquad\forall\,w\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Passing to the limit using
\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\nabla\hat{u}
in
L^{p}
and
F(\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon})\rightharpoonup\eta
in
L^{p^{\prime}}
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\bigl(\eta-F(w)\bigr)\cdot\bigl(\nabla\hat{u}-w\bigr)\,dx\geq 0\qquad\forall\,w\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

This is the Minty characterization of the graph of
F
, and it implies
\eta=F(\nabla\hat{u})=|\nabla\hat{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\hat{u}
a.e. in
\Omega
.
Therefore, for every
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\hat{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\hat{u}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx.

(33)

Combining (
32
) and (
33
) in (
31
):

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\hat{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\hat{u}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx=\hat{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}|\hat{u}|^{p-2}\hat{u}\phi\,dx

(34)

for all
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. By density, (
34
) holds for all
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Next, we show
\hat{u}
satisfies the Robin boundary condition through an energy comparison. By (
23
) we have:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{p-1}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx.

(35)

We now control the coating contribution using the one-dimensional variational principle. Define the trace
a_{\varepsilon}(\xi):=\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\xi)
for
\xi\in\Gamma
, so that
a_{\varepsilon}\to\hat{u}|_{\Gamma}
strongly in
L^{p}(\Gamma)
by (
29
). For each
\xi\in\Gamma
, consider the one-dimensional profile along the normal direction: the eigenfunction restricted to the normal segment from
\xi
to the outer boundary satisfies
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\xi)=a_{\varepsilon}(\xi)
and
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\xi+\varepsilon\rho(\xi)\nu_{\Gamma})=0
.
More precisely, for a.e.
\xi\in\Gamma
define
g_{\xi}(t):=\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\xi+t\nu_{\Gamma}(\xi))
on
[0,\varepsilon\rho(\xi)]
. Then
g_{\xi}(0)=a_{\varepsilon}(\xi)
and
g_{\xi}(\varepsilon\rho(\xi))=0
, hence by the fundamental theorem of calculus and Hölder,

|a_{\varepsilon}(\xi)|^{p}=|g_{\xi}(0)-g_{\xi}(\varepsilon\rho(\xi))|^{p}\leq(\varepsilon\rho(\xi))^{p-1}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\rho(\xi)}|g_{\xi}^{\prime}(t)|^{p}\,dt.

Since
|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\geq|\partial_{t}\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}=|g_{\xi}^{\prime}(t)|^{p}
and the normal-coordinate Jacobian equals
1+O(\varepsilon)
, integrating over
\Gamma
yields the stated slice-wise lower bound (up to
o(1)
).

By the one-dimensional variational principle for the affine profile (cf. Lemma
3.3
(B)), the minimal energy along each normal slice is achieved by the affine extension, giving the lower bound

\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{p-1}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\geq\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|a_{\varepsilon}(\xi)|^{p}}{\rho(\xi)^{p-1}}\,d\sigma+o(1)=\int_{\Gamma}h(\xi)|a_{\varepsilon}(\xi)|^{p}\,d\sigma+o(1),

where
h(\xi)=1/\rho(\xi)^{p-1}
and the
o(1)
term arises from corrections due to the boundary normal coordinate Jacobian.

By the weak lower semicontinuity of the Dirichlet energy:

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\hat{u}|^{p}\,dx\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx.

(36)

From (
23
), (
35
), and (
36
):

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left[\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{p-1}|\nabla\Phi_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\right]

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\hat{u}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Gamma}h(\xi)|\hat{u}|^{p}\,d\sigma.

(37)

By the variational characterization (
17
) and using
\|\hat{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
from (
30
):

\mu_{1}\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\hat{u}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Gamma}h(\xi)|\hat{u}|^{p}\,d\sigma}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\hat{u}|^{p}\,dx}\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\hat{u}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Gamma}h(\xi)|\hat{u}|^{p}\,d\sigma.

(38)

From (
3
) and (
38
):
\mu_{1}\leq\hat{\lambda}=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)
. Combined with the upper bound (
22
):

\mu_{1}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)\leq\mu_{1}.

Therefore
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Lambda_{1}(\varepsilon)=\mu_{1}
. Moreover, all inequalities in the chain must be equalities. In particular, equality in (
38
) shows that
\hat{u}
attains the infimum in (
17
). Hence
\hat{u}
is a principal eigenfunction for the Robin problem (
14
) with eigenvalue
\mu_{1}
.

By the simplicity of the principal eigenvalue (Proposition
2.2
),
\hat{u}=u_{1}
(up to sign, which we fix by positivity). Since the limit is unique, the entire sequence converges (not just a subsequence):
\Phi_{\varepsilon}|_{\Omega}\rightharpoonup u_{1}
weakly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. This completes the proof of Theorem
3.6
.
∎

3.1
Limiting Cases;
p\to 1^{+}
and
p\to\infty

As
p
deviates from 2, the asymptotic behavior of the eigenvalue problem becomes complex. Two key limiting regimes are particularly interesting: the degeneracy at
p=1
(total variation) and the singular limit
p\to\infty
(infinity Laplacian).

The limit
p\to 1^{+}
requires a different framework,
as the
p
-Laplacian degenerates into the 1-Laplacian (total variation operator),
which is no longer a differential operator in the classical sense. We provide
here a
formal
discussion of this limit; a fully rigorous treatment in the mixed/Robin setting requires BV trace theory and a boundary
\Gamma
–convergence
statement for the Robin term, which we do not develop here.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open. The space of functions of bounded
variation is

BV(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{1}(\Omega):|Du|(\Omega)<\infty\right\},

where the total variation is defined as

|Du|(\Omega):=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}u\operatorname{div}\phi\,dx:\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,|\phi|\leq 1\right\}.

For
u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)
, we have
|Du|(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|\,dx
.

The space of bounded variation functions provides the natural setting for problems
involving total variation (1-Laplacian), where classical derivatives may not exist.
This framework is essential for understanding the limit
p\to 1^{+}
.

Definition 3.8
.

The eigenvalue problem for the
1
-Laplacian with Robin boundary conditions is formally written as

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\right)=\lambda\frac{u}{|u|}&\text{in }\Omega,\\[8.61108pt]
u=0&\text{on }\Gamma_{D},\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{\nu}u}{|\nabla u|}+\operatorname{sign}(u)=0&\text{on }\gamma,\end{cases}

or equivalently in calibration form:
z\cdot\nu+\operatorname{sign}(u)=0
on
\gamma
, where
z\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a calibration vector field satisfying
|z|\leq 1
a.e. and
(z,Du)=|Du|
as measures.

The boundary condition
\frac{\partial_{\nu}u}{|\nabla u|}+\operatorname{sign}(u)=0
arises as the limit of the
p
-Laplacian Robin condition

|\nabla u|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u+h_{p}|u|^{p-2}u=0

as
p\to 1^{+}
, where
h_{p}=\rho^{-(p-1)}\to 1
. Indeed, dividing by
|\nabla u|^{p-2}
and
|u|^{p-2}
respectively and taking
p\to 1^{+}
yields the stated condition.

Intuitively, as
p\to 1^{+}
, eigenfunctions concentrate on lower-dimensional sets
(curves or points) where they achieve maximum efficiency. The eigenvalue problem
becomes a geometric optimization problem: finding the set that minimizes the ratio
of perimeter to area (the Cheeger problem).

Definition 3.9
.

A function
u\in BV(\Omega)
with
u=0
on
\Gamma_{D}

(in the trace sense) is an eigenfunction for the 1-Laplacian with eigenvalue

\lambda
if there exists a vector field
\mathbf{z}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})

with
|\mathbf{z}|\leq 1
a.e. such that:

(i)

(\mathbf{z},Du)=|Du|
as measures (i.e.,
\mathbf{z}
is a calibration for
u
);

(ii)

-\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{z})=\lambda\operatorname{sign}(u)
in the sense of distributions;

(iii)

The boundary condition holds:
[\mathbf{z}\cdot\nu]+h\operatorname{sign}(u)=0
on
\gamma
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\lambda_{1}(p)
denote the principal eigenvalue of problem (
1
)
with Robin coefficient
h_{p}:=1/\rho^{p-1}
. Then
h_{p}\to 1
uniformly on
\gamma
as
p\to 1^{+}
.
Moreover,
assuming
the standard equi-coercivity and
\Gamma
–convergence (in
L^{1}(\Omega)
) of the associated energies with the Robin boundary term, the eigenvalues converge to the BV-ground-state level

\lambda_{1}^{(1)}\;:=\;\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in BV(\Omega),\,u|_{\Gamma_{D}}=0\\
u\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\frac{|Du|(\Omega)+\int_{\gamma}|u|\,d\sigma}{\int_{\Omega}|u|\,dx},

which is the natural “first eigenvalue” for the 1-Laplacian with Robin contribution on
\gamma
.

Proof.

The uniform convergence
h_{p}\to 1
follows from
\rho>0
and continuity on the compact set
\gamma
.
The convergence
\lambda_{1}(p)\to\lambda_{1}^{(1)}
is a standard consequence of

\Gamma
–convergence (or Mosco convergence) of the energies

u\mapsto\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\gamma}h_{p}|u|^{p}\,d\sigma

to the BV-energy
u\mapsto|Du|(\Omega)+\int_{\gamma}|u|\,d\sigma
, together with stability of minimizers.
Since the full justification in the mixed/Robin setting requires a precise boundary
\Gamma
–convergence statement,
we record this as a formal/conditional limit (cf.
[
29
]
for related Dirichlet results).
∎

Remark 3.11
.

For the pure Dirichlet case (
\gamma=\emptyset
,
\Gamma_{D}=\partial\Omega
), the BV ground-state level

\lambda_{1}^{(1)}
from Theorem
3.10
reduces to the classical Cheeger constant

h(\Omega):=\inf_{E\subset\Omega}\frac{P(E,\Omega)}{|E|},

and minimizers are given (up to normalization) by characteristic functions of Cheeger sets; see, e.g.,
[
29
]
.

With a Robin contribution on
\gamma
, the functional in Theorem
3.10
suggests a

Robin–Cheeger
type quantity of the form

h_{\mathrm{R}}(\Omega;\gamma)\ :=\ \inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\subset\Omega\\
E\ \text{finite perimeter}\end{subarray}}\frac{P(E,\Omega)+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\!\big(\partial^{\ast}E\cap\gamma\big)}{|E|},

where
\partial^{\ast}E
denotes the reduced boundary. Establishing an identity

\lambda_{1}^{(1)}=h_{\mathrm{R}}(\Omega;\gamma)
requires a careful BV-trace interpretation of the boundary term

\int_{\gamma}|u|\,d\sigma
(and depends on the precise mixed/Robin setting). Since this identification is not used below,
we record it only as heuristic motivation.

Proposition 3.12
.

As
p\to 1^{+}
, the effective Robin coefficient satisfies

h_{p}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\rho(\xi)^{p-1}}\to 1

uniformly on
\gamma
, independent of the coating thickness function
\rho(\xi)>0
.

Proof.

For any
\rho(\xi)>0
, we have
\lim_{p\to 1^{+}}\rho(\xi)^{p-1}=\rho(\xi)^{0}=1
. Since
\rho\in C^{2}(\gamma)
is bounded away from zero and infinity on the
compact set
\gamma
, the convergence is uniform.
∎

The following behavior is evident from the definition of
h
.

Proposition 3.13
.

As
p\to\infty
, the asymptotics of the Robin coefficient

h(\xi)=\rho(\xi)^{-(p-1)}

are entirely determined by the local thickness
\rho(\xi)
:

(i)

If
\rho(\xi)>1
, then
h(\xi)\to 0
, corresponding to a
Neumann-type limit.

(ii)

If
\rho(\xi)=1
, then
h(\xi)\to 1
.

(iii)

If
\rho(\xi)<1
, then
h(\xi)\to\infty
, corresponding to a
Dirichlet-type limit.

We now describe the limit as
p\to\infty
for case
h\equiv 1
, in two parts: the interior equation and the boundary condition. For
\infty
-eigenvalue scaling, the interior equation takes the form

\min\bigl\{|\nabla u|-\Lambda_{\infty}u,\;-\Delta_{\infty}u\bigr\}=0\qquad\text{in }\Omega,

(39)

where
\Delta_{\infty}u:=\nabla u^{\top}D^{2}u\,\nabla u
is the infinity-Laplacian.

The finite-
p
Robin condition on
\gamma
with
h\equiv 1
reads

|\nabla u_{p}|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u_{p}+u_{p}^{p-1}=0\qquad\text{on }\gamma,

(40)

for a nonnegative principal eigenfunction. As
p\to\infty
there are three
pointwise regimes:

1.

If
|\nabla u|>u
: dividing (
40
) by
|\nabla u_{p}|^{p-2}

and sending
p\to\infty
annihilates the second term, yielding

\partial_{\nu}u=0.

2.

If
|\nabla u|=u
: the scaling produces

\partial_{\nu}u+u=0.

3.

If
|\nabla u|<u
: dividing by
u_{p}^{p-2}
annihilates the first term, giving

u=0
, which is incompatible with a positive principal eigenfunction.

Since only regimes (i) and (ii) are admissible, the formal limiting boundary condition
is the constraint

|\nabla u|\geq u\qquad\text{on }\gamma,

(41)

together with the splitting rule

\begin{cases}\partial_{\nu}u=0&\text{on }\gamma\cap\{|\nabla u|>u\},\\[2.84526pt]
\partial_{\nu}u+u=0&\text{on }\gamma\cap\{|\nabla u|=u\}.\end{cases}

(42)

For
h\equiv 1
, the
L^{p}
boundary energy satisfies

\left(\int_{\gamma}|u_{p}|^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right)^{1/p}\;\xrightarrow{p\to\infty}\;\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma)},

and the rigorous
L^{\infty}
Rayleigh quotient is

\Lambda_{\infty}=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in W^{1,\infty}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)\\[1.0pt]
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1\end{subarray}}\max\Bigl\{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\;\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma)}\Bigr\}.

(43)

In particular,
\Lambda_{\infty}
depends only on the geometry
(\Omega,\Gamma_{D},\gamma)

and not on the value
h\equiv 1
.

Combining the interior equation and the boundary conditions, the limit is

\begin{cases}\min\bigl\{|\nabla u|-\Lambda_{\infty}u,\;-\Delta_{\infty}u\bigr\}=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u=0&\text{on }\Gamma_{D},\\[5.69054pt]
|\nabla u|\geq u&\text{on }\gamma,\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{\nu}u=0&\text{on }\gamma\cap\{|\nabla u|>u\},\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{\nu}u+u=0&\text{on }\gamma\cap\{|\nabla u|=u\}.\end{cases}

(44)

For details on infinity Laplacian eigenvalue problems, see
Juutinen, Lindqvist, and Manfredi
[
23
,
24
]
.
For numerical approximations, see Bozorgnia, Bungert, and Tenbrinck
[
8
]
.

We establish continuity of the
eigenvalue and eigenfunction with respect to
p
in the interior
of the parameter range.

Theorem 3.14
.

Fix
p_{0}\in(1,\infty)
and
q\in(1,p_{0})
. For
\rho\equiv 1
and a domain
\Omega
with
C^{2}
boundary, the map

\mu_{1}(p)\to\mu_{1}(p_{0})\quad\text{and}\quad u_{1}(p)\to u_{1}(p_{0})\text{ in }W^{1,q}(\Omega).

Here, for each
p\in(1,\infty)
,
(\mu_{1}(p),u_{1}(p))
denotes the principal eigenpair of the Robin problem
(
13
) (equivalently (
14
) with
\rho\equiv 1
), with
u_{1}(p)>0
in
\Omega
,
chosen uniquely by the normalization
\|u_{1}(p)\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)}=1
(any fixed normalization would do).

Proof.

For
p

, the

are uniformly bounded above and below by constants

\Omega
,

, and

for any

Equivalently, one may invoke Mosco/

[

We omit the technical details.

4
Inverse Problem: Uniqueness and Stability

We first establish a boundary non-degeneracy property of the principal eigenfunction: away from the interface where the boundary condition changes, its gradient remains uniformly bounded away from zero in a thin boundary collar. Although interior critical points may cause degeneracy in the linearization of the
p
-Laplacian, all inverse information in our setting is transmitted through boundary identities on the measured Dirichlet portion
\Gamma_{D}
away from
\zeta
, where Hopf’s lemma ensures
|\nabla u|>0
and the Cauchy data are well-defined.

We therefore introduce a localized regularization that acts only where
|\nabla u|
may be small (in the interior) and is identically inactive in the boundary collar, preserving the boundary relations exactly while ensuring the uniform ellipticity needed for the subsequent analysis.

4.1
Boundary Non-Degeneracy and Localized Regularization

Let
(\lambda,u)
be the principal eigenpair of (
1
), normalized by
\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
, with
u>0
in
\Omega
(Proposition
2.2
). Assume
h\in C^{1}(\gamma)
and
h\geq 0
on
\gamma
(whenever
\inf_{\gamma}h>0
, the conclusions below on
\gamma
strengthen to strict non-degeneracy).

Let
\zeta:=\overline{\Gamma_{D}}\cap\overline{\gamma}\subset\partial\Omega
denote the (possibly nonempty) interface set where the boundary condition switches; all pointwise boundary statements below are understood on
\partial\Omega\setminus\zeta
(i.e. on the relatively open pieces
\Gamma_{D}
and
\gamma
).

Assumption 1
.

The interface
\zeta:=\overline{\Gamma_{D}}\cap\overline{\gamma}\subset\partial\Omega
is closed and satisfies

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\zeta)=0,

so boundary integrals over
\partial\Omega
are insensitive to modifications on
\zeta
.
This condition is satisfied, for example, when
\Gamma_{D}
and
\gamma
have Lipschitz boundaries as subsets of
\partial\Omega
(in which case
\zeta
is typically
(n\!-\!2)
–dimensional).

Proposition 4.1
.

For the mixed Dirichlet–Robin problem, the principal eigenfunction
u
satisfies
|\nabla u(x)|>0
for all
x\in\Gamma_{D}\setminus\zeta
. Moreover, for any
x\in\gamma\setminus\zeta
with
h(x)>0
one has
|\nabla u(x)|>0
(in particular if
\inf_{\gamma}h>0
then
|\nabla u|>0
on
\gamma\setminus\zeta
).

Proof.

Hopf’s lemma guarantees
\partial_{\nu}u<0
since
u>0
in
\Omega
and
u=0
on
\Gamma_{D}
. Since
u\equiv 0
on
\Gamma_{D}
, the gradient is purely normal:
\nabla u=(\partial_{\nu}u)\nu
, hence
|\nabla u|=|\partial_{\nu}u|>0
.

On
\gamma
, note that
u
cannot vanish on the relatively open set
\gamma
: if
u(x_{0})=0
at some
x_{0}\in\gamma
, then
x_{0}
is a boundary minimum of a nontrivial nonnegative solution of
-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda u^{p-1}\geq 0
, so Hopf’s lemma gives
\partial_{\nu}u(x_{0})<0
, whereas the Robin condition yields
|\nabla u|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u(x_{0})=-h(x_{0})u(x_{0})^{p-1}=0
, a contradiction. Hence
u>0
on
\gamma
. The Robin condition gives

|\nabla u|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u=-h\,u^{p-1}\leq 0\quad\text{on }\gamma,

so
\partial_{\nu}u\leq 0
on
\gamma
. At any point
x\in\gamma
with
h(x)>0
we obtain the strict inequality

|\nabla u(x)|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u(x)<0
, hence
\partial_{\nu}u(x)<0
and therefore
|\nabla u(x)|>0
.
∎

Even in the pure Dirichlet case (
\gamma=\emptyset
), the principal eigenfunction attains its maximum in
\Omega
, hence has at least one interior critical point where
\nabla u=0
. For
p=2
on convex domains, the classical log-concavity theory (see, e.g.,
[
37
,
28
]
) forces uniqueness of the maximum point. For the inverse problem considered here, the key point is that all information transfer between
\Gamma_{D}
and
\gamma
is mediated through boundary identities on the smooth, relatively open pieces away from
\zeta
, where
|\nabla u|>0
by Proposition
4.1
.

Definition 4.2
.

Fix
\eta>0
and set
\Gamma_{D,\eta}:=\{x\in\Gamma_{D}:\mathrm{dist}(x,\zeta)\geq\eta\}
. Since
|\nabla u|>0
on
\Gamma_{D}\setminus\zeta
(Proposition

) and
\nabla u
extends continuously to
\Gamma_{D}\setminus\zeta
(Lemma

), there exists
c_{0}=c_{0}(\eta)>0
and a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}\subset\Omega

such that
|\nabla u(x)|\geq c_{0}
for all
x\in\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
. Let
0<\delta<c_{0}/2
.

Define the

by

|\nabla u|_{\delta,\mathrm{loc}}:=\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\delta^{2}\chi_{\delta}(|\nabla u|)},

(45)

where
\chi_{\delta}:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
is a smooth cutoff function satisfying:

1.

\chi_{\delta}(t)=1
for

,

2.

\chi_{\delta}(t)=0
for

3.

\chi_{\delta}

|\chi_{\delta}^{\prime}(t)|\leq 2/\delta

The corresponding regularized coefficient matrix is

A_{\delta}(x):=|\nabla u|_{\delta,\mathrm{loc}}^{p-2}I+(p-2)|\nabla u|_{\delta,\mathrm{loc}}^{p-4}\nabla u\otimes\nabla u.

(46)

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
u
be the principal eigenfunction. Fix
\eta>0
and set

\Gamma_{D,\eta}:=\{x\in\Gamma_{D}:\mathrm{dist}(x,\zeta)\geq\eta\}.

By Lemma

\Gamma_{D}
,
\nabla u
extends continuously to
\Gamma_{D,\eta}
and satisfies
|\nabla u|=|\partial_{\nu}u|>0

c_{0}:=\min_{\Gamma_{D,\eta}}|\nabla u|>0
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
be a neighborhood of
\Gamma_{D,\eta}
in
\overline{\Omega}
such that
|\nabla u(x)|\geq c_{0}/2
for all

.

For any

:

1.

|\nabla u|_{\delta,\mathrm{loc}}=|\nabla u|
in
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
,

2.

A_{\delta}(x)=A(x)
in
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
, where
A(x)=|\nabla u|^{p-2}I+(p-2)|\nabla u|^{p-4}\nabla u\otimes\nabla u

3.

The coefficient matrix

is uniformly elliptic and bounded on

(47)

Moreover, in
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
the ellipticity bounds can be taken independent of
\delta
: there exist
0<\theta_{0}\leq\Theta_{0}<\infty
(depending only on

) such that

\theta_{0}|\xi|^{2}\leq A_{\delta}(x)\xi\cdot\xi\leq\Theta_{0}|\xi|^{2}\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathcal{U}_{\eta},\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(48)

Proof.

(i)–(ii) If
x\in\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
, then
|\nabla u(x)|\geq c_{0}>2\delta
, hence
\chi_{\delta}(|\nabla u(x)|)=0

and therefore
|\nabla u|_{\delta,\mathrm{loc}}=|\nabla u|
and
A_{\delta}=A
on
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
.

(iii) Set
s(x):=|\nabla u(x)|_{\delta,\mathrm{loc}}
. By construction

for all

, and also

s(x)\leq\sqrt{|\nabla u(x)|^{2}+\delta^{2}}\leq M+\delta,

where

Moreover,

A_{\delta}(x)\xi\cdot\xi=s^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}+(p-2)s^{p-4}(\nabla u\cdot\xi)^{2}=s^{p-2}\Big(|\xi|^{2}+(p-2)\frac{(\nabla u\cdot\xi)^{2}}{s^{2}}\Big).

\max(1,p-1)|\xi|^{2}
. Hence

\min(1,p-1)\,s^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}\leq A_{\delta}(x)\xi\cdot\xi\leq\max(1,p-1)\,s^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}.

Using
s\in[\delta,M+\delta]
yields (
47
) with, for example,

\theta_{\delta}=\min(1,p-1)\times\begin{cases}\delta^{\,p-2},&p\geq 2,\\
(M+\delta)^{\,p-2},&1<p<2,\end{cases}\quad\Theta_{\delta}=\max(1,p-1)\times\begin{cases}(M+\delta)^{\,p-2},&p\geq 2,\\
\delta^{\,p-2},&1<p<2.\end{cases}

In
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
we have
s=|\nabla u|\in[c_{0},M]
, so the same eigenvalue computation gives the
\delta
–independent bounds (
48
).
∎

The localized regularization (
45
) has the key feature that it is
inactive
in the fixed collar
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
of
\Gamma_{D,\eta}
where
|\nabla u|\geq c_{0}/2>2\delta
, hence
A_{\delta}=A
on
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
. Consequently, boundary identities used in the inverse problem on the measured Dirichlet portion
\Gamma_{D}
at positive distance from
\zeta
are independent of
\delta
.
Any use of
A_{\delta}
is therefore a technical device to obtain globally bounded uniformly elliptic coefficients, while leaving the boundary information channel unchanged on the measured boundary part.

4.2
Uniqueness

For the uniqueness theorem, we reduce the inverse problem to a
linear
divergence–form equation for the difference
w=u_{1}-u_{2}
. To conclude from vanishing Cauchy data on
\Gamma_{D}
, we apply the boundary Cauchy unique continuation theorem (Theorem
2.5
). Boundary non-degeneracy guarantees uniform ellipticity
locally
near
\partial\Omega
, but it is not automatic in the interior because the linearized
p
–Laplacian may degenerate at interior critical points. Accordingly, we state an explicit path-ellipticity assumption that ensures the coefficients are globally uniformly elliptic and regular enough to invoke Theorem
2.5
.

Assumption 2

(Uniform ellipticity)
.

Let
p\geq 2
and let
(\lambda_{j},u_{j})
be principal eigenpairs corresponding to Robin coefficients
h_{j}

(j=1,2)
(with the usual normalization and
u_{j}>0
in
\Omega
).

Define
F(\xi):=|\xi|^{p-2}\xi
for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The coefficient matrix

\bar{A}(x):=\int_{0}^{1}DF\!\left(\nabla u_{2}(x)+t(\nabla u_{1}(x)-\nabla u_{2}(x))\right)dt

(49)

is uniformly elliptic on
\overline{\Omega}
: there exist constants
0<\theta\leq\Theta<\infty
such that

\theta|\xi|^{2}\leq\bar{A}(x)\xi\cdot\xi\leq\Theta|\xi|^{2}\quad\text{for all }x\in\overline{\Omega},\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Moreover, the regularity required by Theorem
2.5
holds for
\bar{A}
, i.e.

\bar{A}\in C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})\quad(\text{i.e.\ }\bar{A}\text{ is Lipschitz on }\overline{\Omega}).

Boundary non-degeneracy yields uniform ellipticity of
\bar{A}

in a boundary collar of the measured Dirichlet part
(since
|\nabla u_{j}|
are bounded away from
0
on
\Gamma_{D}\setminus\zeta
and hence near
\Gamma_{D}
away from
\zeta
).
However, to invoke Theorem
2.5
we need
global
uniform ellipticity and the stated regularity on
\overline{\Omega}
. Thus Assumption
2
makes explicit the requirement that the linearized coefficients in (
49
) do not degenerate in the interior along the segment connecting
\nabla u_{2}
and
\nabla u_{1}
. In the special case
p=2
, one has
DF\equiv I
, so the ellipticity/regularity requirements are automatically satisfied.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
p\geq 2
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
) be a bounded domain with
C^{2}
boundary satisfying
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{D}\cup\gamma
, where
\Gamma_{D}
and
\gamma
are disjoint, relatively open, and nonempty subsets.
Let
h_{1},h_{2}\in C^{1}(\gamma)
with
h_{j}\geq 0
, and let
(\lambda_{j},u_{j})
be the corresponding principal eigenpairs for problem (
1
), normalized by
\|u_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
with
u_{j}>0
in
\Omega
. Suppose that Assumption
2
holds for the pair
(u_{1},u_{2})
.
If

\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\left.|\nabla u_{1}|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u_{1}\right|_{\Gamma_{D}}=\left.|\nabla u_{2}|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u_{2}\right|_{\Gamma_{D}}

(50)

then
h_{1}=h_{2}
on
\gamma
.

Proof.

Set
\lambda:=\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
and define
w:=u_{1}-u_{2}
. By Lemma
2.4
,
since
h_{j}\in C^{1}(\gamma)
and
h_{j}\geq 0
, and
\partial\Omega\in C^{2}
, each eigenfunction
u_{j}
belongs to
C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\zeta)
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
(with
\zeta:=\overline{\Gamma_{D}}\cap\overline{\gamma}
). In particular,
u_{j}
and
\nabla u_{j}
extend continuously to
\partial\Omega\setminus\zeta
(hence on
\Gamma_{D}
and on
\gamma
).

Since both
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
vanish on
\Gamma_{D}
, we have
w|_{\Gamma_{D}}=0
. Moreover, since
u_{j}\equiv 0
on the relatively open set
\Gamma_{D}
, the tangential derivatives vanish there: for any
x\in\Gamma_{D}
and tangent vector
\tau
,
the tangential derivative
\partial_{\tau}u_{j}(x)=0
(the trace of
u_{j}
is constant on
\Gamma_{D}
).
Therefore
\nabla u_{j}(x)=(\partial_{\nu}u_{j})\,\nu(x)
for all
x\in\Gamma_{D}
, which gives
|\nabla u_{j}(x)|=|\partial_{\nu}u_{j}(x)|
. Applying Hopf’s lemma to
u_{j}
on
\Gamma_{D}
—noting that
u_{j}>0
in
\Omega
and
-\Delta_{p}u_{j}=\lambda_{j}u_{j}^{p-1}>0
in
\Omega
—yields
\partial_{\nu}u_{1}<0
and
\partial_{\nu}u_{2}<0
on
\Gamma_{D}
. Writing
a_{j}:=-\partial_{\nu}u_{j}>0
on
\Gamma_{D}
, the flux condition in (
50
) gives
a_{1}^{p-1}=a_{2}^{p-1}
on
\Gamma_{D}
. Since the map
t\mapsto t^{p-1}
is strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
for
p>1
, we conclude
a_{1}=a_{2}
, hence

\partial_{\nu}w|_{\Gamma_{D}}=0.

(51)

Since
w\equiv 0
on the relatively open set
\Gamma_{D}
, its tangential derivatives vanish on
\Gamma_{D}
,
hence
\nabla w=(\partial_{\nu}w)\nu
there. Moreover, for
x\in\Gamma_{D}
the vectors

\nabla u_{2}(x)+t(\nabla u_{1}(x)-\nabla u_{2}(x))
are parallel to
\nu(x)
for all
t\in[0,1]
,
so
DF(\cdot)
maps
\nu(x)
to a multiple of
\nu(x)
and therefore
\bar{A}(x)\nu(x)=\kappa(x)\nu(x)

on
\Gamma_{D}
for some
\kappa(x)>0
. Consequently,

(\bar{A}\nabla w)\cdot\nu=\kappa\,\partial_{\nu}w=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{D}.

Subtracting the eigenvalue equations satisfied by
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
gives

-\operatorname{div}\!\left(|\nabla u_{1}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{1}-|\nabla u_{2}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{2}\right)=\lambda\left(|u_{1}|^{p-2}u_{1}-|u_{2}|^{p-2}u_{2}\right)\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Define
F(\xi):=|\xi|^{p-2}\xi
for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
G(s):=|s|^{p-2}s
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
. By the fundamental theorem of calculus,

F(\nabla u_{1})-F(\nabla u_{2})=\left(\int_{0}^{1}DF\!\left(\nabla u_{2}+t(\nabla u_{1}-\nabla u_{2})\right)dt\right)(\nabla u_{1}-\nabla u_{2})=\bar{A}(x)\nabla w,

where
\bar{A}(x)
is defined by (
49
) and

DF(\xi)=|\xi|^{p-2}I+(p-2)|\xi|^{p-4}\xi\otimes\xi,

with the convention
|\xi|^{p-4}\xi\otimes\xi:=0
when
\xi=0
. Similarly, defining

\bar{B}(x):=\int_{0}^{1}G^{\prime}(u_{2}(x)+t(u_{1}(x)-u_{2}(x)))\,dt=(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}|u_{2}+t(u_{1}-u_{2})|^{p-2}\,dt,

we have
G(u_{1})-G(u_{2})=\bar{B}(x)w
.

Since
u_{1},u_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
(by standard
L^{\infty}
bounds/maximum principle for nonnegative eigenfunctions of
-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda u^{p-1}
with mixed Dirichlet–Robin boundary conditions; see, e.g.,
[
33
,
39
]
), we have
\bar{B}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Consequently,
w
satisfies the linear divergence-form equation

-\operatorname{div}(\bar{A}(x)\nabla w)=\lambda\bar{B}(x)\,w\quad\text{in }\Omega.

(52)

By Assumption
2
,
\bar{A}
is uniformly elliptic on
\overline{\Omega}
with the regularity required by Theorem
2.5
, while
\bar{B}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Since
p\geq 2
and
\Omega
is bounded, the embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{1,2}(\Omega)
holds; hence
w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
and Theorem
2.5
is applicable to (
52
). Since
w=0
and
(\bar{A}\nabla w)\cdot\nu=0
on the relatively open set
\Gamma_{D}
,
Theorem
2.5
(with
\Gamma_{0}=\Gamma_{D}
) yields
w\equiv 0
in
\Omega
. Hence
u_{1}=u_{2}
in
\Omega
and (by
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity) on
\partial\Omega
, in particular on
\gamma
.
In particular, on
\gamma
we have
u_{1}=u_{2}
and
\nabla u_{1}=\nabla u_{2}
pointwise (since
u_{j}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\zeta)
), hence
|\nabla u_{1}|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u_{1}=|\nabla u_{2}|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u_{2}
on
\gamma
.
Both functions satisfy the Robin boundary condition on
\gamma
:

|\nabla u_{1}|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u_{1}+h_{1}u_{1}^{p-1}=0,\quad|\nabla u_{2}|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u_{2}+h_{2}u_{2}^{p-1}=0.

Subtracting these conditions and using
u_{1}=u_{2}
and
|\nabla u_{1}|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u_{1}=|\nabla u_{2}|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u_{2}
on
\gamma
yields
(h_{1}-h_{2})u_{1}^{p-1}=0
on
\gamma
.

To conclude, we use that
u_{1}>0
on
\gamma
: if
u_{1}(x_{0})=0
for some
x_{0}\in\gamma
, then
x_{0}
is a boundary minimum of the nonnegative solution of
-\Delta_{p}u_{1}=\lambda u_{1}^{p-1}\geq 0
, and the Hopf boundary point lemma (boundary minimum version) gives
\partial_{\nu}u_{1}(x_{0})<0
. On the other hand, the Robin condition gives
|\nabla u_{1}(x_{0})|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u_{1}(x_{0})=-h_{1}(x_{0})\,u_{1}(x_{0})^{p-1}=0
, which is incompatible with
\partial_{\nu}u_{1}(x_{0})<0
. Hence
u_{1}>0
on
\gamma
, and
(h_{1}-h_{2})u_{1}^{p-1}=0
implies
h_{1}=h_{2}
on
\gamma
.
∎

Note that for
p=2
, the coefficient matrix simplifies to
\bar{A}(x)=I
(the identity), which is trivially uniformly elliptic. In this case, Theorem
4.4
recovers the uniqueness result of Santacesaria and Yachimura
[
38
]
. Our contribution is the extension to the nonlinear case
p\neq 2
, which requires careful attention to possible degeneracy of the linearized
p
-Laplacian at interior critical points.

4.3
Fréchet Differentiability of the Solution Map

The stability estimate requires understanding how the eigenpair
(u(h),\lambda(h))

depends on the Robin coefficient
h
. In this subsection we establish
local
Fréchet
differentiability of the solution map by differentiating the original eigenvalue
problem. Throughout this subsection we assume
p\geq 2
, which is the regime used in the stability theorem; the case
1<p<2
requires additional weighted-space arguments and is not needed below.

The linearization involves the coefficient matrix

A(x)=DF(\nabla u(x))=|\nabla u|^{p-2}I+(p-2)|\nabla u|^{p-4}\nabla u\otimes\nabla u,

which degenerates where
|\nabla u|=0
when
p>2
. This degeneracy does not prevent defining the Fréchet derivative (the Nemytskii map
F(\xi)=|\xi|^{p-2}\xi
is
C^{1}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
p\geq 2
), but it
does
obstruct proving invertibility of the linearized operator without an explicit nondegeneracy assumption. The localized regularization from Definition
4.2
will be employed later as a technical tool for boundary unique continuation; it does not enter the definition of the Fréchet derivative.
Since by Lemma
4.3
the
regularization is inactive in the boundary collar
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
where

|\nabla u|\geq c_{0}>2\delta
, all boundary identities relevant to the inverse problem
remain unaffected.

The key analytic input for the implicit function theorem is the invertibility of the augmented linearization (eigenvalue equation + normalization). Because
A(x)
may lose uniform ellipticity at interior critical points of
u
when
p>2
, we state this as an explicit hypothesis at the reference coefficient.

Assumption 3
.

Let
p\geq 2
, fix
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{ad}:=\{h\in C^{1}(\gamma):\inf_{\gamma}h>0\}
, and let

(\lambda_{0},u_{0})
be the associated principal eigenpair of (
1
), normalized by

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
. Set

V:=\{v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega):v=0\ \text{on }\Gamma_{D}\},\qquad V_{\perp}:=\left\{v\in V:\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{p-2}u_{0}\,v\,dx=0\right\},

and define
\mathcal{M}_{0}:V\to V^{*}
by

\langle\mathcal{M}_{0}[v],\phi\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}A_{0}(x)\nabla v\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx+(p-1)\int_{\gamma}h_{0}|u_{0}|^{p-2}v\,\phi\,d\sigma-\lambda_{0}(p-1)\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{p-2}v\,\phi\,dx,

(53)

where
A_{0}(x):=DF(\nabla u_{0}(x))
.
Assume that the
augmented
linearization

\mathcal{L}_{0}:V\times\mathbb{R}\to V^{*}\times\mathbb{R},\qquad\mathcal{L}_{0}(v,\mu):=\big(\mathcal{M}_{0}[v]-\mu\,|u_{0}|^{p-2}u_{0},\ \int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{p-2}u_{0}\,v\,dx\big)

is a bounded linear isomorphism. Equivalently, there exists
C_{\rm lin}>0
such that
for all
(v,\mu)\in V\times\mathbb{R}
,

\|v\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}+|\mu|\leq C_{\rm lin}\,\Big(\|\mathcal{M}_{0}[v]-\mu|u_{0}|^{p-2}u_{0}\|_{V^{*}}+\Big|\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{p-2}u_{0}\,v\,dx\Big|\Big).

(54)

The orthogonality condition defining
V_{\perp}
arises naturally from the normalization
constraint. Differentiating
\|u(h)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}=1
with respect to
h
in
direction
\xi
yields

0=\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}(\int_{\Omega}|u(h+\varepsilon\xi)|^{p}dx)\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}=p\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}u\cdot u^{\prime}(h)[\xi]\,dx,

so the derivative
u^{\prime}(h)[\xi]
necessarily lies in
V_{\perp}
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
p\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{H}_{ad}:=\{h\in C^{1}(\gamma):\inf_{\gamma}h>0\}

denote the admissible set. Fix
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{ad}
and let
(\lambda_{0},u_{0})
be its normalized principal eigenpair.
Assume that Assumption
3
holds at
h_{0}
.
Then there exists a
C^{1}
–neighborhood
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{H}_{ad}
of
h_{0}
such that the solution operator

\mathcal{S}:\mathcal{U}\to V,\quad h\mapsto u(h),

(where
u(h)
is the normalized principal eigenfunction) is Fréchet differentiable on
\mathcal{U}
.
Similarly, the eigenvalue map
h\mapsto\lambda(h)
is Fréchet differentiable on
\mathcal{U}
.
Moreover, there exists a nondecreasing function
\omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
\omega(t)\to 0
as
t\to 0
such that

\|u(h)-u(h_{0})-u^{\prime}(h_{0})[h-h_{0}]\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}+|\lambda(h)-\lambda(h_{0})-\lambda^{\prime}(h_{0})[h-h_{0}]|\leq\omega(\|h-h_{0}\|_{C^{1}(\gamma)})\,\|h-h_{0}\|_{C^{1}(\gamma)}

for all
h
sufficiently close to
h_{0}
in
C^{1}(\gamma)
.

For
h\in\mathcal{U}
and
\xi\in C^{1}(\gamma)
, the directional derivative

u^{\prime}(h)[\xi]\in V_{\perp}
satisfies the linearized problem

\begin{cases}-\operatorname{div}(A(x)\nabla u^{\prime})-\lambda(p-1)|u|^{p-2}u^{\prime}=\lambda^{\prime}|u|^{p-2}u&\text{in }\Omega,\\[3.00003pt]
u^{\prime}=0&\text{on }\Gamma_{D},\\[3.00003pt]
A(x)\nabla u^{\prime}\cdot\nu+(p-1)h|u|^{p-2}u^{\prime}=-\xi|u|^{p-2}u&\text{on }\gamma,\\[3.00003pt]
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}u\,u^{\prime}\,dx=0,&\end{cases}

(55)

where we abbreviate
u=u(h)
,
u^{\prime}=u^{\prime}(h)[\xi]
,
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda^{\prime}(h)[\xi]
,
and
A(x)=DF(\nabla u(x))
.
The eigenvalue derivative is given by

\lambda^{\prime}(h)[\xi]=\int_{\gamma}\xi\,|u(h)|^{p}\,d\sigma.

(56)

Here the PDE is understood in the weak sense in
V^{*}
, and the boundary term

A\nabla u^{\prime}\cdot\nu
on
\gamma
is the conormal trace associated with the
divergence-form operator
\operatorname{div}(A\nabla\cdot)
.
On
\partial\Omega\setminus\zeta
(in particular on
\overline{\Gamma_{D}}
) the boundary regularity
from Lemma
2.4
yields the classical pointwise interpretation.

Proof.

We apply the implicit function theorem to an augmented system encoding both the
eigenvalue equation and the normalization constraint. Define the residual map

R:\mathcal{H}_{ad}\times V\times\mathbb{R}\to V^{*}

by

\langle R(h,u,\lambda),\phi\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}u\,\phi\,dx+\int_{\gamma}h|u|^{p-2}u\,\phi\,d\sigma,\qquad\forall\phi\in V,

and set

G(u):=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}-1.

Define the augmented map

\mathbb{H}(h,u,\lambda):=(R(h,u,\lambda),\,G(u)):\mathcal{H}_{ad}\times V\times\mathbb{R}\to V^{*}\times\mathbb{R}.

Then
\mathbb{H}(h,u,\lambda)=(0,0)
if and only if
(u,\lambda)
is a
normalized eigenpair for (
1
).

For
p\geq 2
, the Nemytskii map
\xi\mapsto|\xi|^{p-2}\xi
is
C^{1}
on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
(including at
\xi=0
). Hence
R
and
G
are continuously
Fréchet differentiable, and so is
\mathbb{H}
on

\mathcal{H}_{ad}\times V\times\mathbb{R}
.

The partial Fréchet derivatives at a solution
(h,u,\lambda)
are

\displaystyle\langle D_{h}R(h,u,\lambda)[\xi],\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma}\xi\,|u|^{p-2}u\,\phi\,d\sigma,

\displaystyle\langle D_{u}R(h,u,\lambda)[w],\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}A(x)\nabla w\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx-\lambda(p-1)\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}w\,\phi\,dx+(p-1)\int_{\gamma}h\,|u|^{p-2}w\,\phi\,d\sigma,

\displaystyle\langle D_{\lambda}R(h,u,\lambda)[\mu],\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\mu\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}u\,\phi\,dx,

\displaystyle DG(u)[w]

\displaystyle=p\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}u\,w\,dx,

where

A(x)=DF(\nabla u(x))=|\nabla u(x)|^{p-2}I+(p-2)|\nabla u(x)|^{p-4}\nabla u(x)\otimes\nabla u(x).

Comparing with (
53
), we recognize that

D_{u}R(h,u,\lambda)=\mathcal{M}_{h}.

Assumption
3
(applied at
h_{0}
) implies that the augmented linearization

D_{(u,\lambda)}\mathbb{H}(h_{0},u_{0},\lambda_{0}):V\times\mathbb{R}\to V^{*}\times\mathbb{R}

is an isomorphism (the normalization removes the scaling direction).

The implicit function theorem now guarantees the existence of neighborhoods

\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{H}_{ad}
and Fréchet differentiable maps

h\mapsto(u(h),\lambda(h))
such that

\mathbb{H}(h,u(h),\lambda(h))=(0,0)\qquad\text{for all }h\in\mathcal{U}.

Differentiating the identity
\mathbb{H}(h,u(h),\lambda(h))=(0,0)
in the direction

\xi\in C^{1}(\gamma)
yields the linearized system (
55
).

Finally, (
56
) follows by testing the weak formulation with

\phi=u
and using the orthogonality condition

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}u\,u^{\prime}(h)[\xi]\,dx=0.

Equivalently, differentiating the Rayleigh quotient representation

\lambda(h)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(h)|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\gamma}h\,|u(h)|^{p}\,d\sigma

under the normalization
\|u(h)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}=1
yields the same formula.
∎

4.4
Stability Estimate

We establish a conditional stability estimate for recovering
h
near
h_{0}
from the measurement map.
The estimate combines (i) a quantitative stability bound for the
linearized
inverse problem
(Assumption
4
) with (ii) an explicit control of the
nonlinear remainder

coming from Fréchet differentiability (Theorem
4.5
). The resulting bound is stated in a form that keeps this nonlinear remainder explicit, without adding any additional assumptions.

We first define the measurement operator that maps the Robin coefficient to the
observable data.

Definition 4.6
.

Define the measurement map
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{ad}}\to\mathbb{R}\times W^{-1/p,p^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
by

\mathcal{F}(h):=\Bigl(\lambda(h),\;\big(|\nabla u(h)|^{p-2}\nabla u(h)\big)\cdot\nu\big|_{\Gamma_{D}}\Bigr),

(57)

here
(u(h),\lambda(h))
denotes
the principal eigenpair of (
1
)
corresponding to the Robin coefficient
h
, normalized by
\|u(h)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
.

Let
F(\xi):=|\xi|^{p-2}\xi
and set
g(h):=F(\nabla u(h))
. Since
u(h)
solves (
1
) in the weak sense, we have
g(h)\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\dive\,g(h)=-\lambda(h)\,|u(h)|^{p-2}u(h)\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\quad\text{in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega).

Hence

g(h)\in W^{\dive,p^{\prime}}(\Omega):=\{g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}):\dive\,g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\},

and its normal trace
g(h)\cdot\nu
is well-defined (in the weak sense) as an element of
W^{-1/p,p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)
; we denote its restriction to
\Gamma_{D}
by
\big(g(h)\cdot\nu\big)\big|_{\Gamma_{D}}\in W^{-1/p,p^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
. On
\Gamma_{D}\setminus\zeta
, Lemma
2.4
yields the classical pointwise interpretation, and by Assumption
1
all identities on
\Gamma_{D}
are understood a.e. (up to modification on
\zeta
).

The key analytical input is a quantitative estimate for the linearized problem.
When the Robin coefficient is perturbed by
\xi
on the inaccessible boundary

\gamma
, the resulting change in the measured data on
\Gamma_{D}
is given by

D\mathcal{F}(h_{0})[\xi]
. The estimate (
58
) quantifies
how effectively the smallness of
D\mathcal{F}(h_{0})[\xi]
(measured on the accessible
boundary) propagates back to control
\xi
(on the inaccessible boundary). This
“propagation of smallness” from
\Gamma_{D}
to
\gamma
necessarily incurs a loss,
reflected by the Hölder exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
: control of
\xi
in
L^{2}(\gamma)

requires both the linearized data
and
an a priori bound on
\xi
in the stronger

C^{1}(\gamma)
norm. For
p=2
, estimates of this type are derived from Carleman
inequalities for the Laplacian
[
2
,
11
]
.

Assumption 4

(Quantitative linearized stability)
.

Let
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{ad}}
and let
(u_{0},\lambda_{0})

be the corresponding normalized principal eigenpair. There exist constants

C_{0}>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that for all
\xi\in C^{1}(\gamma)
,

\|\xi\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\leq C_{0}\,\|D\mathcal{F}(h_{0})[\xi]\|_{\mathbb{R}\times W^{-1/p,p^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})}^{\alpha}\,\|\xi\|_{C^{1}(\gamma)}^{1-\alpha},

(58)

where
D\mathcal{F}(h_{0}):C^{1}(\gamma)\to\mathbb{R}\times W^{-1/p,p^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
is the Fréchet derivative of
\mathcal{F}
at
h_{0}
, given by

D\mathcal{F}(h_{0})[\xi]=\biggl(\int_{\gamma}\xi\,|u_{0}|^{p}\,d\sigma,\;\bigl[(A_{0}\nabla u^{\prime})\cdot\nu\bigr]\big|_{\Gamma_{D}}\biggr),

(59)

with
u^{\prime}=u^{\prime}(h_{0})[\xi]
solving the linearized problem (
55
).

Remark 4.7
.

Assumption
4
is weaker than assuming a bounded left
inverse for
D\mathcal{F}(h_{0})
. The interpolation structure in (
58
)
reflects the severe ill-posedness: small measurement residuals
\|D\mathcal{F}(h_{0})[\xi]\|

can only control weak norms of
\xi
when combined with a priori bounds on
stronger norms. For
p=2
, estimates of this form are established
in
[
11
]
using Carleman inequalities for the Laplacian.
The extension to
p\neq 2
requires Carleman estimates for the linearized

p
-Laplacian, which we do not develop here; we therefore state (
58
)
as a hypothesis.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{ad}
and
let
\mathcal{F}
be the measurement map from Definition
4.6
.
Assume that Assumption
3
holds at
h_{0}
(so that Theorem
4.5
applies) and that Assumption
4
is satisfied.

Fix
M>0
. Then there exists
\rho_{0}>0
such that for any
h\in\mathcal{H}_{ad}
with

\|h-h_{0}\|_{C^{1}(\gamma)}\leq M,\qquad\|h-h_{0}\|_{C^{1}(\gamma)}<\rho_{0},

and letting

\delta:=\|\mathcal{F}(h)-\mathcal{F}(h_{0})\|_{\mathbb{R}\times W^{-1/p,p^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})},

one has the stability estimate

\|h-h_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\leq C_{0}\,M^{1-\alpha}\,\Big(\delta+\omega\big(\|h-h_{0}\|_{C^{1}(\gamma)}\big)\,\|h-h_{0}\|_{C^{1}(\gamma)}\Big)^{\alpha},

(60)

where
\omega
is the modulus from Theorem
4.5
.

Proof.

Set
\xi:=h-h_{0}
. By Theorem
4.5
(Fréchet differentiability of
(u,\lambda)
at
h_{0}
) and by the continuity of the normal trace operator
W^{\dive,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\ni g\mapsto(g\cdot\nu)|_{\Gamma_{D}}\in W^{-1/p,p^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
, the measurement map
\mathcal{F}
is Fréchet differentiable at
h_{0}
as a map into
\mathbb{R}\times W^{-1/p,p^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})
.

Hence

\|\mathcal{F}(h)-\mathcal{F}(h_{0})-D\mathcal{F}(h_{0})[\xi]\|_{\mathbb{R}\times W^{-1/p,p^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})}\leq\omega(\|\xi\|_{C^{1}(\gamma)})\,\|\xi\|_{C^{1}(\gamma)}.

Therefore, by the triangle inequality,

\|D\mathcal{F}(h_{0})[\xi]\|_{\mathbb{R}\times W^{-1/p,p^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{D})}\leq\delta+\omega(\|\xi\|_{C^{1}(\gamma)})\,\|\xi\|_{C^{1}(\gamma)}.

Applying Assumption
4
and using
\|\xi\|_{C^{1}(\gamma)}\leq M
gives (
60
).
∎

The conditional Hölder/interpolation rate in (
60
) reflects the severe ill-posedness of the infinite-dimensional inverse problem.
In our forthcoming work, we show that Lipschitz stability is recoverable when the Robin coefficient is parameterized by a finite-dimensional subspace, as is the case in practical numerical reconstructions.

4.5
Verification of Assumptions

In this subsection, we state sufficient criteria and a representative model under which
the standing hypotheses used in Sections
4.2
–
4.4
are satisfied.

If
\Gamma_{D}
and
\gamma
are relatively open in
\partial\Omega
and each has a Lipschitz boundary
as a subset of

\partial\Omega
(equivalently, in local charts on
\partial\Omega
), then
\zeta

is contained in a countable union of
(n-2)
–dimensional Lipschitz graphs in
\partial\Omega
.
Hence

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\zeta)=0.

Boundary non-degeneracy and the collar
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
.

Proposition
4.1
follows from Hopf’s lemma (Lemma
2.3
) and the mixed boundary
conditions: since
u=0
on
\Gamma_{D}
and
u>0
in
\Omega
, one has
\partial_{\nu}u<0
on
\Gamma_{D}\setminus\zeta
,
hence
|\nabla u|=|\partial_{\nu}u|>0
there.
Moreover
u
cannot vanish on
\gamma
(otherwise Hopf contradicts the Robin condition), so
u>0
on
\gamma
,
and at points where
h>0
the Robin condition forces
\partial_{\nu}u<0
and thus
|\nabla u|>0
.
Combining Proposition
4.1
with boundary
C^{1,\beta}
regularity (Lemma
2.4
)
gives: for every fixed
\eta>0
, setting

\Gamma_{D,\eta}:=\{x\in\Gamma_{D}:\mathrm{dist}(x,\zeta)\geq\eta\},

there exist
c_{0}=c_{0}(\eta)>0
and a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}\subset\overline{\Omega}
of
\Gamma_{D,\eta}

such that
|\nabla u|\geq c_{0}
on
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
.
This is the key input behind Definition
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
.

Lemma
4.3
shows that for any
0<\delta<c_{0}/2
the localized regularization

|\nabla u|_{\delta,\mathrm{loc}}
from (
45
) satisfies

|\nabla u|_{\delta,\mathrm{loc}}=|\nabla u|\qquad\text{and}\qquad A_{\delta}=A\quad\text{on }\mathcal{U}_{\eta},

where
A_{\delta}
is defined in (
46
) and
A=DF(\nabla u)
.
Consequently, all Cauchy-data relations on
\Gamma_{D,\eta}
(and any measured subportion

\Gamma_{\mathrm{meas}}\Subset\Gamma_{D,\eta}
) are
independent of
\delta
, while
A_{\delta}
is uniformly elliptic
on
\overline{\Omega}
with bounds (
47
).

For convenience, we recall the three analytically nontrivial inputs. Assumption
2
states that the path matrix
\bar{A}
in (
49
) must be uniformly elliptic on
\overline{\Omega}
and belong to the coefficient class required by the boundary unique continuation theorem (here
C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})
in Theorem
2.5
). Assumption
3
indicates that the linear map
L_{0}:V\times\mathbb{R}\to V^{*}\times\mathbb{R}
is a bounded linear isomorphism. In Assumption
4
, a conditional propagation-of-smallness (observability) inequality of the form (
58
) holds for the linearized map
D\mathcal{F}(h_{0})
.

Assumption
2
:

Write
F(\xi)=|\xi|^{p-2}\xi
for
p\geq 2
. For
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

DF(\xi)\eta\cdot\eta=|\xi|^{p-2}|\eta|^{2}+(p-2)|\xi|^{p-4}(\xi\cdot\eta)^{2},

so
DF(\xi)
has eigenvalues
|\xi|^{p-2}
(multiplicity
n-1
) and
(p-1)|\xi|^{p-2}
(in the
\xi
–direction).
Consequently, for the path matrix

\bar{A}(x)=\int_{0}^{1}DF\!\left((1-t)\nabla u_{2}(x)+t\nabla u_{1}(x)\right)\,dt,

one has the pointwise lower bound

\bar{A}(x)\eta\cdot\eta\;\geq\;\min\{1,p-1\}\,|\eta|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\Big|(1-t)\nabla u_{2}(x)+t\nabla u_{1}(x)\Big|^{p-2}\,dt.

(61)

Hence,
uniform ellipticity
follows once

\inf_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\int_{0}^{1}\Big|(1-t)\nabla u_{2}(x)+t\nabla u_{1}(x)\Big|^{p-2}\,dt\;>\;0.

(62)

A simple sufficient condition is the following “no simultaneous vanishing” bound:
if there exists
m_{0}>0
such that

|\nabla u_{1}(x)|+|\nabla u_{2}(x)|\geq m_{0}\quad\text{for all }x\in\overline{\Omega},

(63)

then (
62
) holds with a quantitative lower bound depending only on
(p,m_{0})

(e.g. by comparing the integral in
t
to a one-dimensional segment estimate, as in the explicit radial/1D computations).

The additional requirement
\bar{A}\in C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})
is
automatic for
p=2
(since
DF\equiv I
),
and is also satisfied in symmetric ODE-reduced settings (radial annuli, one-dimensional intervals) where
u_{j}
are
classical
C^{2}
solutions and
\nabla u_{j}
are Lipschitz.
For genuinely multidimensional degenerate problems (
p>2
) the eigenfunction typically has interior critical points,
and global Lipschitz regularity of
\bar{A}
may fail unless one assumes additional nondegeneracy/regularity ensuring that the coefficient in the linearization falls into the UCP class of Theorem
2.5
.
In applications where one only needs UCP from a measured boundary portion, one may instead invoke the localized
regularization
A_{\delta}
(Definition
4.2
), which preserves all boundary identities on
\Gamma_{D,\eta}

but enforces global uniform ellipticity; then the remaining input is a boundary UCP theorem compatible with the chosen
coefficient class (Lipschitz).

Canonical examples.

The above assumptions are fulfilled in several standard settings. In the linear case
p=2
, one has
\bar{A}\equiv I
, and hence Assumption
2
is immediate. For a radial annulus with constant Robin data, if
u_{j}(x)=U_{j}(|x|)
and each profile
U_{j}
has a unique interior critical radius
r_{j}^{*}
, then the condition
r_{1}^{*}\neq r_{2}^{*}
guarantees the no-simultaneous-vanishing mechanism (
63
), and therefore implies (
62
). In this radial ODE framework, the path matrix
\bar{A}
is automatically Lipschitz continuous. The one-dimensional interval case is entirely analogous: the same argument reduces to a scalar coefficient, and the regularity of
\bar{A}
follows directly from classical ODE theory.

Assumption
3
(invertibility of the augmented linearization).

Assumption
3
is needed to apply the implicit function theorem
in Theorem
4.5
. In standard uniformly elliptic regimes, it is consistent with coercivity on the
constrained space
V_{\perp}
together with the simplicity of the principal eigenvalue
(Proposition
2.2
).
Concretely, if the bilinear form associated with
\mathcal{M}_{0}
in (
53
) is coercive on
V_{\perp}

(e.g. under uniform ellipticity of
A_{0}
and
\inf_{\gamma}h_{0}>0
), then the augmented mapping

(v,\mu)\mapsto\Big(\mathcal{M}_{0}[v]-\mu|u_{0}|^{p-2}u_{0},\ \int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{p-2}u_{0}\,v\,dx\Big)

is an isomorphism by a standard Lax–Milgram/Fredholm alternative argument on
V_{\perp}
.
In particular, in the linear case
p=2
, this follows directly from classical Fredholm theory, and in the 1D/radial ODE settings, it can be checked by direct solvability of the corresponding augmented boundary value problem.

Assumption
4
(quantitative linearized stability).

Assumption
4
encodes the quantitative propagation-of-smallness input needed for
Theorem
4.8
. For
p=2
, inequalities of the form (
58
) are obtained
from Carleman estimates and quantitative unique continuation for second-order elliptic equations
(see, e.g.,
[
11
,
30
]
and references therein).

4.6
Compactness and Convergence

We record a standard compactness result for families of Robin coefficients and their associated eigenpairs. This result justifies passage to limits along bounded sequences of reconstructed coefficients, as arise in numerical approximations or regularization schemes. We emphasize that the forward eigenvalue problem (
1
) is formulated without any regularization: the auxiliary parameter
\delta
from Definition
4.2
enters only in linearized operators used for sensitivity analysis and unique continuation arguments. In particular, the principal eigenpair
(u(h),\lambda(h))
depends only on
h
, not on
\delta
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\{h_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{H}_{ad}
be a sequence of Robin coefficients satisfying

\|h_{k}\|_{C^{1}(\gamma)}\leq M\quad\text{and}\quad\inf_{k}\inf_{\gamma}h_{k}\geq h_{*}\geq 0

for some constants
M>0
and
h_{*}\geq 0
. Let
(u_{k},\lambda_{k})
denote the principal eigenpair of (
1
) corresponding to
h_{k}
, normalized by
\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
with
u_{k}>0
in
\Omega
.

Then there exists a subsequence (not relabeled), a function
h_{0}\in C^{0,1}(\gamma)
with
h_{0}\geq h_{*}
on
\gamma
, and a pair
(u_{0},\lambda_{0})
such that

1.

h_{k}\to h_{0}
uniformly on
\gamma
, and in fact
h_{k}\to h_{0}
in
C^{0,\alpha}(\gamma)
for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
;

2.

u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
weakly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
u_{k}\to u_{0}
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
and in
L^{p}(\gamma)
;

3.

u_{k}\to u_{0}
strongly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
;

4.

\lambda_{k}\to\lambda_{0}
;

5.

\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}(h_{0})
and
(u_{0},\lambda_{0})
is the normalized principal eigenpair of (
1
) with Robin coefficient
h_{0}
; in particular
u_{0}>0
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Since
\|h_{k}\|_{C^{1}(\gamma)}\leq M
, the family
\{h_{k}\}
is equi-Lipschitz on the compact set
\gamma
. By Arzelà–Ascoli, there exists a subsequence (not relabeled) and
h_{0}\in C^{0,1}(\gamma)
such that
h_{k}\to h_{0}
uniformly on
\gamma
. Moreover, for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
one has the interpolation estimate

[h_{k}-h_{0}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\gamma)}\;\leq\;C(\gamma,\alpha)\,[h_{k}-h_{0}]_{C^{0,1}(\gamma)}^{\alpha}\,\|h_{k}-h_{0}\|_{C^{0}(\gamma)}^{1-\alpha},

and
[h_{k}-h_{0}]_{C^{0,1}(\gamma)}
is uniformly bounded, hence
h_{k}\to h_{0}
in
C^{0,\alpha}(\gamma)
. Finally, from
h_{k}\geq h_{*}
we get
h_{0}\geq h_{*}
on
\gamma
.

To obtain uniform
W^{1,p}
bounds on
\{u_{k}\}
, we test the weak formulation of (
1
) with
\phi=u_{k}
. Using the normalization
\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
gives the energy identity

\lambda_{k}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\gamma}h_{k}|u_{k}|^{p}\,d\sigma\geq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}|^{p}\,dx.

For an upper bound on
\lambda_{k}
, fix any nonzero
v\in V:=\{w\in W^{1,p}(\Omega):w|_{\Gamma_{D}}=0\}
. The Rayleigh quotient characterization yields

\lambda_{k}\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\gamma}h_{k}|v|^{p}\,d\sigma}{\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p}\,dx}\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\,dx+\|h_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma)}\int_{\gamma}|v|^{p}\,d\sigma}{\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p}\,dx}\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\,dx+M\int_{\gamma}|v|^{p}\,d\sigma}{\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p}\,dx},

so
\sup_{k}\lambda_{k}<\infty
. Combined with the energy identity and
\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
, this gives a uniform bound
\sup_{k}\|u_{k}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}<\infty
.

By reflexivity of
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we extract a subsequence with
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
weakly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. The Rellich–Kondrachov theorem gives
u_{k}\to u_{0}
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, and since the trace map
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)
is continuous and
W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)
embeds compactly into
L^{p}(\partial\Omega)
, we also have
u_{k}\to u_{0}
strongly in
L^{p}(\gamma)
. In particular,
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
, so
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
. The sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}
is bounded, hence (after extracting a further subsequence)
\lambda_{k}\to\lambda_{0}
for some
\lambda_{0}\geq 0
.

It remains to show that
(u_{0},\lambda_{0})
solves (
1
) with coefficient
h_{0}
and that
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}(h_{0})
. Note that the weak problem (
1
) is well-defined for any
h_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\gamma)
with
h_{0}\geq h_{*}
, hence in particular for
h_{0}\in C^{0,1}(\gamma)
.
For any
\phi\in V
, the weak formulation reads

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx+\int_{\gamma}h_{k}|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}\phi\,d\sigma=\lambda_{k}\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}\phi\,dx.

The strong convergence
u_{k}\to u_{0}
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
and
L^{p}(\gamma)
implies
|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}\to|u_{0}|^{p-2}u_{0}
strongly in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and
L^{p^{\prime}}(\gamma)
. Together with
h_{k}\to h_{0}
uniformly on
\gamma
and
\lambda_{k}\to\lambda_{0}
, we can pass to the limit in the boundary and right-hand side terms.
For the gradient term, by boundedness, we may assume

|\nabla u_{k}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{k}\rightharpoonup\eta
weakly in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. By a standard Minty–Browder (monotonicity) argument for the map
\xi\mapsto|\xi|^{p-2}\xi
, using the weak formulation and
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, one identifies
\eta=|\nabla u_{0}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{0}
.

Hence passing to the limit gives that
(u_{0},\lambda_{0})
is an eigenpair for (
1
) with Robin coefficient
h_{0}
.

To identify
\lambda_{0}
as the
principal
eigenvalue, use the Rayleigh quotient.
For any fixed
v\in V\setminus\{0\}
,

\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{1}(h_{k})\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\gamma}h_{k}|v|^{p}\,d\sigma}{\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p}\,dx}.

Letting
k\to\infty
and using
h_{k}\to h_{0}
uniformly yields

\lambda_{0}\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\gamma}h_{0}|v|^{p}\,d\sigma}{\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p}\,dx}
.
Taking the infimum over
v
gives
\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}(h_{0})
.
Conversely, since
(u_{0},\lambda_{0})
is an eigenpair and
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
, the variational characterization gives
\lambda_{1}(h_{0})\leq\lambda_{0}
.
Thus
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}(h_{0})
.

Since
u_{0}\geq 0
as a limit of positive functions and
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
, the strong maximum principle implies
u_{0}>0
in
\Omega
, confirming that
(u_{0},\lambda_{0})
is the principal eigenpair.

Finally, the strong
W^{1,p}
convergence follows from the convergence of the gradient norms. Indeed,

\int_{\gamma}h_{k}|u_{k}|^{p}\,d\sigma\to\int_{\gamma}h_{0}|u_{0}|^{p}\,d\sigma

by uniform convergence of
h_{k}
and strong convergence of
u_{k}
in
L^{p}(\gamma)
, and
\lambda_{k}\to\lambda_{0}
by construction. Using

\lambda_{k}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\gamma}h_{k}|u_{k}|^{p}\,d\sigma
,
we obtain
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}|^{p}\,dx\to\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}|^{p}\,dx
.
Together with
\nabla u_{k}\rightharpoonup\nabla u_{0}
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
and uniform convexity of
L^{p}
, this yields
\nabla u_{k}\to\nabla u_{0}
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, hence
u_{k}\to u_{0}
strongly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
.
∎
