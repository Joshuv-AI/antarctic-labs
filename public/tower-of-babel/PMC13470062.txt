Bifurcation, quasi-periodic dynamics, chaos, and soliton waves in the van der Waals normal form for fluidized granular matter
Authors: Md Nur Alam, Muhammad Ziaul Haque, Shams Forruque Ahmed, Md Nurul Islam, Kok Swee Sim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe fluidized granular media is a complex phenomenon with sharp regime changes, pattern formation, and coherent density-wave propagation that is usually treated by nonlinear evolution equations, which are reduced. Of these, the van der Waals normal form, a fourth-order nonlinear oscillator with viscous/frictional dissipation and cubic nonlinearity, offers a general model with which to investigate such phenomena. Although there has been a considerable amount of effort on the construction of precise travelling-wave solutions to van der Waals-type models, in past work, systematic comparisons of the auxiliary-equation choices and their effects on admissible parameter regimes, as well as combining solution construction with an extensive dynamical-systems viewpoint are commonly not made. This study closes such gaps by creating a unified travelling-wave solution, based on Bernoulli-type and Riccati-type auxiliary equations, and an explicit parameterized family of wave solutions, consisting of kink-type, bright-dark soliton, and complex-valued solitons. They are then connected to a normalized nonlinear oscillator to analyze phase-space structures, stability, bifurcations, and sensitivity to perturbations. Symmetry-driven pitchfork bifurcations, dissipation-driven transitions, and an abundance of periodic, quasi-periodic, and chaotic behavior become apparent as key results through equilibrium analysis, 2D/3D phase portraits, time series, and Poincaré diagnostics. The high-dimensional parameter space and model idealism are drawbacks. However, analytical wave solutions coupled with dynamical diagnostics offer strong benchmarks to the numerical dynamics of the propagation of density waves in fluidized granular systems and have been used in augmenting predictive insights about their propagation. Both the theoretical and practical research in the areas of granular flow, material processing, and pattern formation have important implications for these findings. In contrast to previous works, which use one framework for auxiliary equations, a systematic comparison of four ansatz schemes within one common Bernoulli–Riccati framework. The analytical solutions are additionally connected to the dynamics of nonlinear oscillators via the tools of equilibria, bifurcation, and chaos diagnostics, and a direct link is established between exact travelling waves and the structures of phase space.Keywords: Exact travelling-wave solutions, Bernoulli auxiliary equation, Riccati auxiliary equation, Nonlinear oscillator dynamics, Dynamical systems, Phase-space structures, Poincaré diagnosticsSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Granular materials are macroscopic particles (i.e., large enough to be seen with the naked eye), aggregates of particles (powders, grains, pellets, or sand, etc.), which are central to industrial processes, like storage, mixing, and granulation, and to many geophysical processes. Their predictive models are still difficult due to the fact that the granular assemblies may act as a quasi-solid, dense flowing continuum, or even dilute collisional gases, depending on confinement, agitation, and forcing. According to recent syntheses, simple models can be effective when based on combining continuum reasoning with particle-size scale information, as opposed to being based on one theoretical level. In the case of dense granular media, Kamrin et al.1 offered a contemporary outlook on constitutive modeling that has developed out of the primitive rheologies of the early 1990s to nonlocal and reduced-order modeling, capturing creeping regions, size effects, and necessitating macro predictions to be tied to grain physics. Computational fluidization-based methods like coupling computational fluid dynamics with the discrete element method (CFD-DEM) have become useful in fluidization-dominated regimes, in which particle-fluid interactions dominate. Zhao et al.2 survey recent developments and current issues in the field of CFD-DEM simulation of fluidized bed and remark significant improvements in the field and the price of the calculations that encourage the use of reduced-modeling approaches. Fluidized and vibrated granular systems are an ongoing research field since rich and pattern-forming behaviors can be induced by external driving. Watson et al.3 presented a summary of the main findings on vibrated granular matter and transport, and one of the main aspects of these phenomena is the variation of dynamics with parameters over the different regimes. The second common theme in continuum descriptions is nonlocality: the stresses and shear rates at any point are often based on the material state of the surrounding material, especially around yielding or heterogeneous shear interactions. Thus, Fazelpour et al.4 experimentally showed that nonlocal rheology could be used to model quasi-2D annular shear flows, but the nonlocal parameters used must be dependent on the shape of the grain and material, and continuum closures must be interpreted with respect to particle properties. In addition to first-order nonlocal models, Kim and Kamrin5 proposed a second-order nonlocal constitutive model that uses DEM data to model the stress anisotropies and the secondary flows. At a small scale, Dumont et al.6 used DEM simulations to obtain scaling relations of dense granular flows at inclined planes with a combination of macroscopic laws and transiently organized microstructure. Taken together, these works highlight a practical viewpoint: in cases where full multiscale prediction is intractable, the reduced models are useful; however, their interpretation should be informed by the diagnostics that elucidate the qualitative regimes that surround a particular parameter set. In this context, reduced nonlinear evolution equations are commonly used to isolate mechanism-driven behaviours that include clustering, phase-separation-like transitions, and density wave propagating waves. The van der Waals normal form (VDWNF) of fluidized granular matter is a fourth-order nonlinear dissipative nonlinear cubic nonlinear model that is an analytically treatable testbed. It captures sharp spatial fronts, local structures, and pattern-forming dynamics whilst subject to travelling-wave reduction. Between 2021 and 2025, there has again sparked interest in building specific travelling-wave solutions to the VDWNF and related van der Waals-type models using a variety of algebraic-analytic methods. Tools integrability based on, e.g., Painlevé analysis, Bäcklund transformations, tanh-function construction, and elliptic-function representation are combined by Morad et al. 7 to interpret the obtained waves in terms of bubble-like interactions and phase-portrait dynamics. Zafar et al.8 used either the simplest equation and Exp-function techniques to produce new solitary as well as periodic travelling waves with graphical demonstrations. Zhu et al.9 introduced a general auxiliary-equation approach based on the Riccati, where families of solitary waves of the VDWNF are obtained. Li et al.10 generalized the model to a time-fractional version of the van der Waals model, and developed analytic solutions in a truncated version of the M-fractional derivative formulation, where the generalization of exact-solution methods to generalized operators is emphasized. The S-expansion technique is modified by Parvin et al.11 that highlights the applicability of kink, bright, and dark wave structures to the phenomenon of granular waves propagation and clustering. Lastly, Butt et al.12 offered a comparative analysis of solitary waves of van der Waals equations, clearly putting their study in the context of method/operator choice and the morphology of the solutions. Taken together, these works highlight the ongoing development of methods of analysis of the VDWNF, which are both to show the variety of the specific modes of construction of exact waves and to show the increasing interest in relating forms of solutions to dynamical-system representations of the dynamics of a granular media.

Methodologically, auxiliary-equation methods are of particular interest since they give us a systematic path between a nonlinear PDE and a system of parameter constraints that is algebraic. The usual algorithmic steps are: (i) travelling-wave reduction, (ii) pick a finite ansatz which is an auxiliary function satisfying a first-order sub-equation, (iii) balancing nonlinear and derivative terms to choose the degree of the ansatz, and (iv) solving the resultant algebraic system to give explicit parameter constraints. The modern trends focus more and more on verification based on the symbolic substitution and graphical interpretation as a part and parcel of the whole workflow. As an example, Mehdi et al.13 used Riccati-Bernoulli and generalised Bernoulli sub-ODE methods to build up precise travelling waves and complement their results with phase-portrait studies. Sirisubtawee et al.14 introduced a free-access auxiliary-equation model and noted that in modified procedures, the Bernoulli-type sub-equations occur naturally. In another sense, Wu et al.15 applied methods of extended Riccati equations to pulse propagation models and showed the ongoing relevance of Riccati-based transformations in computing analytic wave families. Taken together, these studies encourage the head-on comparison of the test of auxiliary-equations options on the same reduced ODE, and not in isolation. No less important, explicit travelling-wave solutions are not sufficient to describe the underlying dynamics. When travelling-wave reduction occurs, the Van der Pol-type Nonlinear Function (VDWNF) decreases into a nonlinear oscillator with a phase portrait that structures admissible trajectories and explains equilibrium structures as well as stability properties. Modern granular simulations focus on the fact that regime boundaries, e.g. smooth transitions between jamming and flow, are associated with qualitative stability and constitutive behavior changes. Rubin et al.16 suggested a continuum model that describes the transition between elastic jamming and fast inelastic flow, which shows that constitutive structure dominates the formation of a new regime. Nicot et al.17 discussed bifurcation modes and unjamming phenomena from the viewpoint of configurational mechanisms. In applied dynamical systems, equilibria, stability, and bifurcations are described systematically by the formalism of these phenomena; a revised edition by Kuznetsov is a classic reference of the bifurcation theory18, and Ahsan et al. showed how continuation platforms, including COCO, create a platform that allows parameter exploration of nonlinear dynamical systems in a reproducible way19. In cases where external perturbations are introduced, such as an external forcing of the environment, control inputs, or a parameter modulation, classical nonlinear-dynamics diagnostics are popular in order to make the distinction between periodic, quasi-periodic, and chaotic regimes. Here, a few complementary analysis tools have been found useful in detection of qualitative changes in dynamical behavior. As an example, Urenda-Cázares et al.20 used bifurcation diagrams, Lyapunov exponents, phase portraits, and Poincaré maps to study regimes transitions in Duffing-type systems when stochastically perturbed. Their work brings into focus the fact that several diagnostic measures enable a concurrent description of the dynamical complexity of driven nonlinear oscillators. Simultaneously, methodological advances are still made to enhance the reliability of these diagnostics. According to Zhou et al.21, the interpretation of Poincaré sections is subjective in automated or large-scale analysis, and suggests entropy-based improvements that do not lose the geometric intuition of Poincaré mapping but enhance a quantitative distinction. On a comparable note, Wang et al.22 studied bifurcation structures and paths to chaos in a time-delayed, fractional-order Duffing system with harmonic excitation (using harmonic excitation) showing how the variation of the parameter can trigger period-doubling cascades and periodic-chaotic alternating regimes. To characterize the chaotic dynamics quantitatively, the Lyapunov exponents have been a fundamental diagnostic. Dabrowski et al.23 talked about the other computational plans of the largest Lyapunov exponent and explain how they apply to nonlinear oscillators, such as Duffing systems. Escot et al.24 also discussed the more practical issue of computing Lyapunov exponents under noise and recommends Jacobian-based indirect algorithms which enhance robustness in experimental and numerical contexts. Besides these methods, recurrence-based methods have become a formidable family of nonlinear time series analysis techniques, which can be used to identify complex dynamical structures. Recent advances in this direction are summarized in the proceedings of Hirata et al.25, which emphasizes the rising popularity of analysis of recurrence to identify state changes between regular and chaotic stages. A combination of these tools offers a generalized model of the diagnosis of perturbed nonlinear system dynamics.

Recent advances have also broadened the use of nonlinear wave analysis beyond the conventional auxiliary-equation techniques. The dynamics of lump, breather, M-shaped, and optical soliton structures, as well as the stochastic and noise-driven nonlinear systems, have been the subject of growing interest26–31. The work of these studies highlights the usefulness of the hybrid modelling approach of using exact solutions, numerical simulations, stochastic modelling, and dynamical diagnostics to understand the behaviour of complex nonlinear wave phenomena. A systematic study of multiple auxiliary-equation models and their relevance to bifurcation and phase-space structures in models of granular evolution of the van der Waals type, however, is still largely underdeveloped. Within the last few years, analytical techniques using artificial intelligence have appeared as a new approach to the study of nonlinear differential equations and complex dynamical systems. The latter include the use of artificial intelligence for mathematical derivation, bilinear neural network approaches, fractional sub-equation neural networks, bilinear residual networks, neural network-based symbolic computation, and multimodal neurosymbolic reasoning frameworks. These techniques show good ability in symbolic discovery and pattern recognition, but are not widely applicable to models of granular evolution that are of the van der Waals type. The ability to use artificial intelligence-enhanced mathematical derivation (AIEMD) techniques to merge symbolic reasoning with machine learning to automate analytical derivations and mathematical discovery has been shown32. Analytical representations of nonlinear fractional differential equations have been proposed using neural network guided symbolic structures, called Fractional Sub-Equation Neural Networks (fSENNs)33. Recently, Bilinear Neural Network (BNN) methods and Bilinear Residual Network (BRN) frameworks have further extended the capability of neural architectures to capture nonlinear wave interactions and obtain exact solutions of nonlinear evolution Eqs.34,35. In parallel, multi-modal neurosymbolic reasoning algorithms, and neural-network-based symbolic calculation approaches combine data-driven learning with symbolic mathematical reasoning to afford novel possibilities for automated theorem discovery, symbolic calculation, and analysis of nonlinear models36,37. The techniques here are new examples of how artificial intelligence and nonlinear mathematical analysis are merging. While they have shown potential in symbolic discovery and construction of solution, the adaptation to the granular evolution models of van der Waals type has not received much attention so far. Therefore, precise analytical frameworks are still necessary to develop benchmark solutions, to check computation-based methods, and to give a strict dynamical interpretation, which is what this is all about.

Notwithstanding these progresses, this recent literature still has a number of gaps when addressing the van der Waals normal form (VDWNF). Most of the literature has focused on the creation of large compendia of travelling-wave solutions, but has not synthesized the findings. Specifically, it is commonly not clear which solutions describe actually distinct wave structures, and which are due to re-naming of parameters or similar transformations. Moreover, parameter regimes that ensure the presence of physically meaningful, real valued profiles are not necessarily systematically discovered. A second issue of significance is the effect of the selected framework of auxiliary equations: various constructions of the ansatz often result in various sets of admissible parameter roots found by matching coefficients, and these sets are hardly ever compared within a single framework. Furthermore, precise travelling-wave solutions are frequently given without any reference to the dynamical-systems interpretation of the reduced travelling-wave oscillator. Therefore, the connection between the analytical wave structures and underlying phase-space structure, including equilibrium structures, stability, and bifurcation structures, has not been adequately understood. Likewise, in a study where the dynamics are forced or perturbed, standard diagnostic means like phase portraits or Poincaré sections are usually introduced, but not linked explicitly to the equilibrium structure of the underlying unforced system upon which the dynamics is organized. In order to overcome these shortcomings, the current research has a number of objectives. First, we form and hierarchically arrange explicit travelling-wave families of the VDWNF in terms of a single auxiliary-equation framework in the form of four intimately related classes of ansatzes which are founded on the use of Bernoulli-type and Riccati-type sub-equations. Second, we give a comparative study of the ensuing parameter root, and how varying auxiliary-equations choices can affect admissible families in solutions. Third, the travelling-wave reduction can be understood as a normalized nonlinear oscillator, in which equilibrium structures, stability features and bifurcation behavior, including the symmetry-driven pitchfork bifurcation of the cubic nonlinearity, can be studied in detail. Lastly, representative perturbations are provided to demonstrate how stroboscopic Poincaré diagnostics can be used to differentiate between periodic and multi-periodic responses of the resulting non-autonomous system. The resulting analytical-dynamical complex offers a better and more organized explanation of the VDWNF. Besides providing a standard to analyze the precise exact-solution methods and numerical approximators in nonlinear PDEs, the findings provide useful information to understand and regulate the dynamics of density waves and fronts in fluidized granular materials and associated pattern-forming systems.

Novelty and main contributions

The present study builds on the literature on evolutionary models of van der Waals-type granular media in the following important respects. First, we develop a unified framework for four-ansatz analytical approaches to systematically construct and compare exact traveling-wave solutions within a single analytical framework, using both Bernoulli-type and Riccati-type auxiliary equations. Second, different auxiliary-equation formulations’ impact on the types of permissible parameter constraints and wave structures is examined, offering a comparison that is not done extensively in prior works. Third, explicit traveling-wave families, such as kink-type fronts, pulse-type structures, bright-dark solitons, and complex-valued wave branches, are derived and classified. Fourth, the travelling-wave reduction is reformulated as a nonlinear oscillator, making it possible to study the equilibrium structures, stability properties, phase-space organization, bifurcation transitions, sensitivity characteristics, and chaotic dynamics within a common framework. Lastly, a direct correspondence is set up between exact analytical wave solutions and the dynamics of the underlying dynamical system, thus gaining a fuller picture of the propagation of nonlinear waves in fluidized granular matter.

Problem formulation

A sophisticated framework that expands on the traditional van der Waals equation to simulate the complex behavior of granular flows is the van der Waals normal form applied to fluidized granular materials. Phase transitions, density changes, and particle clustering within these systems are among the key characteristics it records. This model was not created by a single person, but rather as a result of ongoing advancements in hydrodynamic theories and granular physics. It offers significant insights into the mechanics of fluidized granular materials by equating it with liquid-gas phase behavior in typical fluids. It is also a reliable tool for theoretical research and real-world applications38. Through nonlinear partial differential equations (NLPDEs), the van der Waals normal form provides insights into crucial characteristics like phase separation, jamming, and wave propagation in granular media. Advantages and limitations of the unified four-ansatz framework

A large variety of analytical methods have been devised for nonlinear evolution equations, but they all have their own merits and demerits. The Hirota bilinear method is very useful for generating multi-soliton solutions, and is usually used in conjunction with a proper bilinear representation of the basic equation. Sardar sub-equation approaches offer an efficient way to construct exact solutions, but are restricted to using one auxiliary-equation structure. More recently, Bilinear Neural Network (BNN) methods, Bilinear Residual Network (BRN) frameworks, and other artificial-intelligence-aided symbolic methodologies have shown great potential to find exact solutions and complex wave structures by means of symbolically computing via a neural network26–31,39–42. Most of these approaches, however, have been developed for the purpose of solution discovery and are not typically capable of a thorough and systematic comparison of several different formulations of the auxiliary equation in a single framework. The present study, on the contrary, presents a unified four-ansatz algorithm, which at the same time includes both Bernoulli-type and Riccati-type auxiliary equations, enabling a direct comparison of the constraints on the parameters, the families of solutions, and the corresponding dynamical interpretations. This feature offers a more comprehensive analytical view and allows for the seamless combination of the exact traveling-wave construction with phase space, stability, bifurcation and chaos analysis. In this study, a unified four-ansatz framework was chosen as it allows for the systematic comparison of several different formulations of the auxiliary equation in the same analytical framework. The methodology is a combination of both Bernoulli-type sub-equations and Riccati-type sub-equations and as such offers a larger class of admissible travelling-wave structures that would be derived from either one of the auxiliary-equation frameworks alone. In addition, the solutions obtained are explicitly represented in an analytical form and can be immediately related to the diagnostics of a dynamical system such as equilibrium analysis, stability classification, bifurcation structures, phase portraits, sensitivity analysis, and Poincaré sections. The present framework offers complete analytical transparency and parameters constraints as compared to analytical approaches using neural networks. It has certain drawbacks, however. The method is suitable for travelling-wave reductions and can not necessarily resolve arbitrary initial-value dynamics or higher dimensional wave interactions. Furthermore, the contemporary neural-network assisted methods can find more complicated structures like rogue waves, lump interactions and multidimensional wave phenomena. Therefore, the proposed framework is regarded as complementary methods that are developing, not a replacement.

Result and discussions

The current paper investigates the van der Waals-type model of granular evolution by using four sub-ODE methodologies of the form of the development of the model, obtaining a comparative study of the solutions in a systematic manner and graphical visualization, with the help of dynamical diagnostic analysis.

Comparisons

The current paper presents a major extension of the four sub-ODE ansatz-based methods of dynamical diagnostics of dynamical van der Waals-style granular evolution models, finding a rich and varied group of perfectly accurate solutions. In Subsections 5.1 and 5.2, one will find comparisons and graphical representations of the studied model. With the aim of contextualizing the current work, recent works of van der Waals-type granular evolution models and exact travelling-wave analyses are considered, emphasizing models’ focus, key analysis techniques, type of solutions, and the degree of phase-space, bifurcation, and forced-response diagnostics. As an example, Morad et al.7 used Painlevé analysis and Bäcklund transformations to study bubble-type interactions in fluidized granular media, which included phase-portrait classifications but not forced-response diagnostics. In other cases, solitary and periodic travelling waves were reported by Zafar et al.8 and Zhu et al.9 using simplified equation methods, and little or no dynamical interpretation. The two studies by Li et al.10 and Butt et al.12 were also based on the idea of fractional or dark/bright/periodic solution families without focusing on dynamical diagnostics. On the base level, the four sub-ODE ansatz techniques are compared in the current research. Ansatz I and III make use of Bernoulli-type auxiliary equations, and Ansatz II and IV of Riccati-type formulations. Each of the methods defines a trial structure admissible parameter-constraint sets. Ansatz I generates real kink/front-type travelling-wave (TW) profiles, whereas Ansatz II and IV generate families of complex value functions, the real and complex parts of which are viewed independently. Ansatz III extends to other real TW families of shifted structures. Each method has representative solution labels, which are used to connect the auxiliary sub-equation with the TW branches. The paper contributes to the literature through integrating the four-ansatz framework, the use of both Bernoulli- and Riccati-type auxiliary equations whose coefficient is equal, and explicit constraints. The resulting TW solutions include front/kink and pulse-type families, and complex branches to the solutions, as viewed in terms of their real and imaginary parts. Systematic inclusions of dynamical diagnostics are also made, such as equilibrium analysis, trace-determinant stability classification, bifurcation framing, and representative periodic/localized forcing are investigated through Poincaré stroboscopic sampling. Phase-space structure and bifurcation analysis of the reduced oscillator

In this section, phase portraits, chaotic behaviour, and sensitivity analysis of the study model were graphically illustrated, with a special focus on the qualitative dynamical behaviour on the change of parameters43.

Recently, bifurcation analysis, sensitivity diagnostics, and chaos detection have been proven to be the most useful tools for understanding the evolution of nonlinear equations. Nawaz et al.44 investigated stochastically perturbed nonlinear Schrödinger equations driven by Brownian motion, for instance, and showed that stochastic perturbations can modify the solitary-wave structures and the dynamical stability. Hassaballa et al.45 discussed travelling-wave solutions and the associated dynamical structures of the generalized Benjamin–Ono equation with particular focus on the interplay between exact solutions and phase-space dynamics. Baber et al.46 studied the bifurcation structures, chaotic dynamics, and noise-induced phenomena in the stochastic Jaulent–Miodek hierarchy model and observed rich transitions between regular and chaotic regimes. Furthermore, Taskeen et al.47 investigated bifurcation, chaos, sensitivity analysis, and the dynamics of optical solitons of the Spin Hirota–Maxell–Bloch equation in erbium-doped fibers. These studies all show that equilibrium structures, bifurcation transitions, sensitivity to perturbations, and chaotic attractors are fundamental in organizing the nonlinear wave dynamics. Inspired by this progress, the present study examines similar dynamic mechanisms by studying the equilibrium points, stability classification, bifurcation interpretation, phase portraits, Poincaré diagnostics, and sensitivity investigations of the van der Waals normal form for fluidized granular material.

This diffusive process implies that the system is not moving to a stable attractor, but rather continuously drifting due to the exponentially growing external excitation. The above results indicate that the nonlinear oscillator, when perturbed by an exponentially growing perturbation, will show an increase in energy and instability. The fact that the damping () is zero and the sign of the linear term () is negative, which also makes the system unable to dissipate the injected energy, makes the restoring mechanism of the oscillator weaker. The interaction between nonlinear stiffness and exponential forcing then results in a progressive oscillatory motion and a widening out phase-space path showing the destabilizing nature of the effects of swiftly growing external perturbations. It can be seen that the nonlinear dynamical system forced exponentially to large values is not approaching any standard periodic attractor, but rather is growing in amplitude and is expanding its phase-space, which is the indication of the system undergoing unstable dynamical behavior.

A few qualitative features of the dynamical behavior found in the present van der Waals normal form are similar to those of some recently reported nonlinear systems44–47. In the present system, the variation of the parameters causes significant changes in the organization and stability of the phase space similar to the stochastic resonance Schrödinger model44 and the stochastic Jaulent–Miodek hierarchy model45. Furthermore, the bifurcation transitions presented here are similar to those in the generalized Benjamin–Ono equation46 and the Spin Hirota–Maxwell–Bloch system47, where equilibrium structures are organizing centers for complex dynamical responses. In contrast with the stochastic models discussed in44–47, however, the analysis of the present work is based on a deterministic granular-matter model, where the intrinsic mechanisms of bifurcation associated with the underlying non-linear oscillator structure can be isolated and directly studied.

Conclusion

This work gives a detailed analytical-dynamical exploration of the van der Waals normal form of fluidized granular matter, which responds to the necessity to integrate explicit travelling-wave constructions with the perspective of a complete dynamical-systems approach. The main task was to build up precisely the travelling-wave solutions in a systematic way, and at the same time derive the interpretation of their meaning in the phase-space dynamics. We used a travelling-wave reduction to do this and came up with four closely related classes of auxiliary-equation ansatzes with Bernoulli- and Riccati-type sub-equations in them. These methods gave explicit, parameterized families of waves, both of real kink and anti-kink fronts, and more complicated complex-valued waves, whose real and complex components can be studied separately. Graphical presentations, including 3D plots, contour diagrams, and time-slice representations, were useful in a finer analysis of the spatial-temporal organization of such waves. The travelling-wave ODE was normalised to a damped cubic oscillator to relate the precise solutions to the system dynamics underlying the solution. This expression allowed an in-depth phase-portrait analysis based on equilibrium structures, the classification of linear stability, and bifurcation diagnostics. Moreover, forced perturbations in the form of representatives were also used to investigate stroboscopic Poincaré sections, which in this case emphasized periodic, quasi-periodic, and multi-periodic responses. Important results indicate how critical symmetry-based pitchfork bifurcations, dissipation-based transitions, as well as the selection of auxiliary equations, are in defining the admissible solution space and system behavior. The results have offered analytical benchmarks - in terms of explicit travelling-wave solutions with explicitly defined parameter constraints - as well as regime-sensitive diagnostics, which can be used to check numerical solvers and interpret reduced model dynamics of pattern-forming dynamics in fluidized granular and other dispersive-dissipative media. The major limitations are due to the high-dimensional parameter space and the idealization of the model, which might not be able to reflect all granular interactions and stochastic effects. However, the combined dynamical and analytical framework provides a solid basis for researching density-wave propagation and intricate oscillatory forms. Systematic parameter sweeps, particularly of chaotic quantification, and investigation of multi-scale interactions should further build on this effort in the future, enhancing predictive abilities and practical use of the granular flow engineering, material processing, and complex fluidized systems.

The present framework can be extended in a number of directions in the future. First, the proposed unified four-ansatz method can be extended to fractional-order and variable-coefficient van der Waals-type models for studying memory effects and heterogeneous granular environments. Second, the introduction of stochastic perturbations and random forcing could be considered to more accurately describe realistic fluidized granular systems. Third, the analytical framework can be integrated with symbolic computation techniques that are now available using modern methods of artificial intelligence, such as neural-network-based symbolic discovery and the neurosymbolic reasoning method, to support automated construction and classification of exact travelling-wave solutions. Finally, the developed approach can be extended to apply to other types of nonlinear evolution equations that are important in fluid dynamics, plasma physics, optical systems, and complex material science. Additional future research is possible by combining the present unified auxiliary-equation framework with the recent advancements of neural-network-based analytical methods, such as enhanced bilinear neural network methods, to examine higher-dimensional (coordinate) nonlinear evolution equations, interactions between lump waves and rogue waves, rogue-wave phenomena, stochastic granular systems, and the symbolic discovery of exact solutions with assistance from AI tools39–42.
