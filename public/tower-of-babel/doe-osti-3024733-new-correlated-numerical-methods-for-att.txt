Final Report on Early Career, grant N◦ DE-SC0020311
 New correlated numerical methods
 for attosecond molecular single and double ionization

Summary: This project resulted in the development of ASTRA, a new molecular ionization code
capable of describing single and double ionization processes in polyatomic molecules with corre-
lated electrons and time-dependent fields. Key achievements include: (i) the development of the
PRISM hybrid-integral library enabling core-ionization and high-angular-momentum convergence;
(ii) implementation of time-dependent Schrödinger equation solvers for pump–probe spectroscopies;
(iii) validation against experimental and theoretical benchmarks for atoms and molecules; and (iv)
extension to two-photon double ionization using the finite-pulse virtual-sequential model. These
developments establish ASTRA as a versatile platform for attosecond molecular dynamics.

 I. ASTRA, A NEW MOLECULAR IONIZATION CODE

Continuous advances in the XUV and soft-x-ray ultrafast technologies, pursued at large free-electron-
laser facilities and in attosecond laboratories worldwide, have reached shorter attosecond-pulse
durations, larger intensities, energies, and repetition rates. New XUV-pump XUV/soft-x-ray-probe
schemes, in which the duration of both light-matter interaction stages is short, have enhanced the
time resolution with which ionization can be steered and monitored. Soft-x-ray probes can excite
core electrons, thus monitoring the valence dynamics with high spatial resolution. X-ray pulses
in the water window have extended the scope of attosecond spectroscopy to aqueous matrices.
Photoelectrons from core orbitals are scattered by neighboring nuclei, thus encoding the geometry
of the target molecule. In molecules with tightly bound pairs of equivalent atoms, such as N2
or C2H2, core photoelectrons give rise to characteristic double-slit interference fringes that mirror
the evolution of molecular geometry and charge migration. XUV pulses can even release two or
more electrons, which, if detected in coincidence, give direct information on the concerted motion
of electrons in excited states. The theoretical description of these processes is essential to track the
motion of correlated electron pairs, and, ultimately, to control ultrafast dynamics in matter.
This project concerned the merging of hybrid-basis close-coupling approaches with numerical tech-
niques for the single- and double-ionization continuum, and for correlated ion bound states, to
describe both single- and double-escape processes from poly-electronic molecules. At the project’s
inception, recent interfaces of quantum-chemistry codes to hybrid Gaussian-B-spline close-coupling
(CC) space started giving promising results, but more advanced methods were required to achieve
1keV energies for single core photoionization, and to describe the multi-photon double-ionization
processes targeted by upcoming attosecond experiments. In the project, the PI developed and
integrated in the new ASTRA code (AttoSecond TRAnsitions) the numerical tools for the repre-
sentation of free-electron and electron pairs with electronic-structure molecular packages to lay the
foundation of a new hybrid CC program for the description of molecular single and double-ionization
processes, with a quantitative account of light-driven static and dynamic electronic correlation.
When this project started, most of the fundamental quantities needed to carry out this program
(hybrid integrals and high-order transition density matrices) were not available yet. Computing
and incorporating them in scalable programs, therefore, was a major theoretical and computational
goal. In the course of this project, we have reached several of these goals, bringing us closer to
directly imaging correlated motion in matter, a necessary step to expand attosecond science.

 II. PROGRESS IN ASTRA

The new ASTRA [1] molecular ionization code is capable of accurately reproducing existing exper-
imental and theoretical results for atomic and molecular ionization observables within the fixed-
nuclei approximation, such as resonance parameters [2] total [1] and partial photoionization cross
sections [3]. ASTRA represents the electronic continuum within the close-coupling (CC) ansatz,
treating electronic correlation in the molecular ions at the Complete-Active-Space (CAS) level. Un-
like comparable codes, ASTRA uses transition density matrices to efficiently evaluate interchannel
coupling, enabling simulations for larger molecules.
ASTRA can compute multichannel scattering states of a molecular system within the fixed-nuclei
approximation. The necessary hybrid Gaussian-Bspline integrals, initially determined using the
GBTOlib library [4], are now computed with PRISM (Polycentric Routines for Ionization and
Scattering in Molecules), a new library developed in collaboration with NIST [5]. PRISM allows us
to converge orbital angular momentum, reproduce core ionization processes, and describe separating
fragments. The implementation of finite-pulse virtual-sequential model for molecular two-photon
double ionization processes was carried out under a dedicated supplemental award, as detailed in a
separate section at the end of this report.
ASTRA includes explicit solvers for the Time-Dependent Schrödinger equation (TDSE), both in
an essential-state basis, for optical observables such as attosecond transient absorption (ATAS)
and four-wave mixing (FWM) spectroscopies, and in the full basis, for photoelectron observables,
relevant to pump-probe photoelectron spectroscopies. The current TDSE solver partitions the wave
function into internal and external components, where the latter are propagated analytically. This
approach allows us to describe interaction with moderately intense (≤ 1 TW/cm2 ) long pulses.
The electronic part of ASTRA has reached a high level of sophistication, meeting the goals set in
the DOE CAREER program, establishing the basis for including the remaining degrees of freedom
relevant to current ultrafast spectroscopies.

Supplemental award. Molecular two-photon double ionization
X-ray free-electron lasers have enabled time-resolved studies of multiple vacancies, including double
core holes (DCHs), in polyelectronic molecules. However, theoretical capabilities have not kept pace
with these advances. To address this gap, we developed the finite-pulse virtual-sequential model
(FPVSM), which simulates the time-resolved two-photon double ionization (TPDI) processes in
polyelectronic atoms and shows excellent agreement with accurate benchmarks [15, 16].

Figure 8: Joint photoelectron energy distribution in the one-pulse two-photon double ionization of
the H2 molecule in fixed nuclei approximation. Top row: ab initio simulations by X. Guan et al.,
Phys. Rev. A 88, 043402 (2013). Bottom row: results from the finite-pulse virtual sequential model
implemented for this project.

As a part of the DOE project, we extended FPVSM to molecular systems using astra [1], enabling
simulations of x-ray pump–probe schemes for DCH, core–valence, and double-valence hole formation
with attosecond temporal resolution. The formalism was generalized from atoms to polyatomic
molecules within the fixed-nuclei approximation. While NewStock exploits full SO(3) symmetry,
astra is limited to abelian point groups up to D2h . The resulting algorithms compute molecular
TPDI observables and also provide a cross-check for atomic implementations.
Validation of the FPVSM implementation with linearly-polarized light was first performed for atomic
magnesium and neon using transition amplitudes from astra. For atomic magnesium, with a
single-reference intermediate ionic state and a final 1 S doubly charged state, the molecular FPVSM
expressions correctly reproduce known analytical atomic formulas. In this process, we have further
tested astra for a single-reference closed-shell target state. The transition amplitudes obtained
from the astra is validated against the results obtained from NewStock. The joint energy
distributions agree with results from atomic implementation. In this formalism, the algorithm is
designed to efficiently handle the large number of highly-correlated intermediate ionic channels

populated via one-photon transitions that leads to multiple doubly charged states which preserve
the symmetry and accessible by linearly polarized pulses.

Figure 9: Joint photoelectron energy distribution for the two-photon double ionization of the N2
molecule.

As a starting point for molecular FPVSM implementation, we applied the method to molecular
N2 (see Fig. 9), a benchmark diatomic system. Transition amplitudes for neutral and intermediate
ionic states show reasonable agreement with the literature. The joint energy distribution exhibits
expected TPDI behavior: strong correlation near the non-sequential threshold and a two-peak
structure approaching the sequential regime. To the best of our knowledge, no angularly integrated
or resolved TPDI results are available for N2 for direct comparison. Most prior molecular TPDI
studies have focused on H2 due to the computational cost of solving the TDSE for larger systems.
Further validation was therefore carried out for H2 (see Fig. 8), for which extensive theoretical results
are available [17–21]. We computed transition amplitudes for both H2 and H+ 2 , providing a stringent
test of astra. The resulting resonance parameters and photoionization cross sections agree well
with prior calculations. The joint energy distribution reproduces known qualitative features and
shows signatures of two-particle interference in pump–probe scheme as the photoionization spectrum
is absent of any autoionizing states where the condition of the two-particle interference satisfies.
