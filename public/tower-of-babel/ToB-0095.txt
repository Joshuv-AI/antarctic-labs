# The Wonderful Discoverie of the Witchcrafts of Margaret and Phillip Flower (1619)

## What Is This?

This is a genuine contemporary broadside and trial account from 1619, documenting the case of Margaret and Phillip Flower of Lincoln, England. They were two sisters executed for allegedly bewitching the Bekenwell family of nearby Beck Hall. This publication is a direct, contemporaneous account — not a retelling or a modern interpretation.

## The Flower Family Context

Joan Flower (mother) had been a servant at Beck Hall and was dismissed. She reportedly died cursing the Bekenwell family. Her two daughters — Margaret and Phillip — were later accused of continuing her maleficia, bewitching the Bekenwell children to death (three children allegedly died). The girls confessed under examination, claiming they had compacted with the devil at the urging of their mother.

## Contents of the Trial Documents

- **The original 1619 broadside** printed immediately after the trial
- **Full trial record** — examination questions and the girls' answers
- **Confessions** — detailed accounts of alleged compact-making with the devil
- **Background on Joan Flower** — including her threats against the Bekenwell family before her death

## Why It Matters

This is a rare example of a contemporary, firsthand trial document from the witch trial era. Unlike the theoretical treatises (Malleus, Bodin, Weyer), this is raw primary material — the actual words used to accuse, examine, and convict real people of witchcraft in 17th century England. The Flower case is frequently cited in scholarship on English witch trials.
