A Criterion for Phantomness of dg-categories
Keiho Matsumoto

Abstract
. We study the question of whether the vanishing of additive invariants characterizes phantomness for smooth proper dg categories admitting geometric realizations. More precisely, let X X be a smooth proper variety over a field k k , and let 𝒯 ⊂ perf dg ⁡ ( X ) \mathcal{T}\subset\operatorname{perf}_{\operatorname{dg}}(X) be a k k -linear admissible full dg subcategory. We construct a non-compact motive ℳ ⁡ ( 𝒯 ) ∈ 𝐃𝐌 ⁡ ( k , ℚ ) \mathcal{M}(\mathcal{T})\in\operatorname{\mathbf{DM}}(k,\mathbb{Q}) and show that its l l -adic realization recovers the K ⁡ ( 1 , l ) K(1,l) -local algebraic K K -theory of 𝒯 \mathcal{T} . Analogous statements are obtained for Betti and de Rham realizations, which recover topological K K -theory and periodic cyclic homology, respectively. As a consequence, assuming that the Chow motive of X X is Kimura-finite, we prove a criterion for phantomness: the vanishing of L K ⁡ ( 1 , l ) ​ K ​ ( 𝒯 k ¯ ) ℚ L_{K(1,l)}K(\mathcal{T}_{\overline{k}})_{\mathbb{Q}} , of Hochschild homology in characteristic zero, or of rational topological K K -theory over ℂ \mathbb{C} implies that the rational noncommutative motive of 𝒯 \mathcal{T} vanishes. In this way, our results provide a partial answer to a question raised by Sosna. We also establish a deformation-invariance result for phantomness in smooth proper families.



1.
Introduction

The notion of a
phantom category
, studied in noncommutative algebraic geometry, has become increasingly important due to its connections with the minimal model program and mirror symmetry (see
[
5
]
). Throughout, let
k
be a field. A
k
-linear dg category
\mathcal{T}
is said to
admit a geometric realization
if it is a
k
-linear admissible full dg subcategory of
\operatorname{perf}_{\operatorname{dg}}(X)
for some smooth proper
k
-variety
X
.

For a nonzero smooth proper dg category
\mathcal{T}
that admits a geometric realization, Gorchinskiy and Orlov
[
10
]
define
\mathcal{T}
to be a
quasi-phantom
if
\operatorname{HH}_{*}(\mathcal{T}/k)=0
and
K_{0}(\mathcal{T})
is finite. They also define
\mathcal{T}
to be a
universal phantom
if

K_{0}(\mathcal{T}\otimes Y)=0

for every smooth proper
k
-variety
Y
. In this paper we introduce the following closely related notion.

Definition 1.1
.

We call
\mathcal{T}
a motivic quasi-phantom if

K_{0}(\mathcal{T}\otimes Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}=0

for every smooth proper
k
-variety
Y
.

If
\mathcal{T}
admits a geometric realization, then
\mathcal{T}
is a motivic quasi-phantom if and only if the rational noncommutative motive of
\mathcal{T}
vanishes. When the base field
k
is
\mathbb{C}
(or, more generally, an algebraically closed field of infinite transcendence degree over its prime subfield), one has

\text{quasi-phantom}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{motivic quasi-phantom}

(see
[
10
, Lemma 5.3]
). Conversely, if
\mathcal{T}
is an admissible full dg subcategory of
\operatorname{perf}_{\operatorname{dg}}(X)
and
K_{0}(X)_{\mathrm{tor}}
is finitely generated (for instance, this holds under the Bass conjecture when
k
is a number field or a finite field), then

\text{motivic quasi-phantom}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{quasi-phantom}.

These implications can be summarized as follows:

quasi-phantom
universal phantom
\textstyle{K_{0}(\mathcal{T})\text{ is finite}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{k=\overline{k}\text{ and }\mathrm{tr.deg}(k)=\infty}
motivic quasi-phantom
\scriptstyle{{\operatorname{ch}}(k)=0\text{ and }K_{0}(X)_{\mathrm{tor}}\text{ is f.g.\ as a }\mathbb{Z}\text{-module}}

If
\mathcal{T}
is a motivic quasi-phantom, then every additive invariant with rational coefficients vanishes. In particular, for any prime
l
invertible in
k
, one has
{L_{K(1,l)}}K(\mathcal{T}_{\overline{k}})_{\mathbb{Q}}=0
. Furthermore, if
{\operatorname{ch}}(k)=0
, then
\operatorname{HH}_{*}(\mathcal{T}/k)=0
. The main result of this paper is that this converse implication holds. It may also be viewed as providing a partial answer to a question raised by Sosna
[
18
, Question 1]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k
be a field, and let
\mathcal{T}
be a non-zero
k
-linear smooth proper dg category admitting a geometric realization
\mathcal{T}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{perf}_{\operatorname{dg}}(X)
. Assume that
X
has Kimura-finite Chow motive. Then:

(1)

Let
l
be a prime such that
l\in k^{*}
. If
{L_{K(1,l)}}K(\mathcal{T}_{\overline{k}})_{\mathbb{Q}}=0
, then
\mathcal{T}
is a motivic quasi-phantom.

(2)

Assume
{\operatorname{ch}}(k)=0
. If
\operatorname{HH}_{*}(\mathcal{T}/k)=0
, then
\mathcal{T}
is a motivic quasi-phantom.

(3)

Assume
k=\mathbb{C}
. If
K^{\operatorname{top}}(\mathcal{T})_{\mathbb{Q}}=0
, then
\mathcal{T}
is a motivic quasi-phantom.

Here the notion of
Kimura finiteness
for Chow motives plays a central role. Kimura finiteness is a property of Chow motives introduced independently by Kimura
[
12
]
and O’Sullivan. They conjectured that every Chow motive is Kimura-finite.
For smooth projective surfaces with vanishing geometric genus, the Kimura-finiteness of the Chow motive is equivalent to Bloch’s conjecture.

As a corollary of this theorem, we can establish the deformation invariance of the property of admitting a phantom category, as follows.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
S
be a Noetherian connected scheme, and let
f:\mathcal{X}\to S
be a smooth proper morphism. Assume that the Chow motive of each fiber is Kimura-finite. Suppose that for some point
0\in S
, the fiber
\mathcal{X}_{0}
admits a motivic quasi-phantom category
\mathcal{T}_{0}
, and that there exists a prime number
l\in\kappa(0)^{*}
such that
L_{K(1,l)}K(\mathcal{T}_{0})\neq 0
. Then there exists an étale morphism
\mathcal{U}\to S
whose image contains
0
and such that every fiber of
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{U}}\to\mathcal{U}
admits a motivic quasi-phantom category. Moreover, under the Belmans–Okawa–Ricolfi conjecture
[
3
, Conjecture 9.9]
, the nonvanishing condition
L_{K(1,l)}K(\mathcal{T}_{0})\neq 0
is unnecessary.

This corollary may be viewed as a generalization of the argument of Böhning–Bothmer–Katzarkov–Sosna in
[
4
, Section 10]
: for families of
\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}
-torsion numerical Godeaux surfaces, they prove a corresponding deformation-invariance statement by combining Bloch’s conjecture with the deformation invariance of the Grothendieck group. In our setting, the Kimura-finiteness assumption plays the role that allows one to extend this strategy to a broader class of families.

2.5.

F_{1}
and
F_{2}

Denote by

the functor

Proposition 2.5
.

Functors

and

Proof.

The category
\operatorname{\mathbf{DM}}(k,\mathbb{Q})

and the Tate twist becomes an autoequivalence on

(see

and

are symmetric monoidal, it suffices to show that

and

are equivalent. By
[

, there is an equivalence

\operatorname{\mathbf{DA}}^{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},{\operatorname{eff}}}(k,\mathbb{Q})\simeq\operatorname{\mathbf{DM}}^{{\operatorname{eff}}}(k,\mathbb{Q}).

Here,
\operatorname{\mathbf{DA}}^{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},{\operatorname{eff}}}(k,\mathbb{Q})
is obtained from the category of complexes
\mathrm{Cpl}(\mathrm{Shv}_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Sm}}(k),\mathbb{Q}))
by localizing at
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy equivalences. By
[
2
, Theorem B.13]
,

is compactly generated by
\mathbb{Q}(X)[i]
for smooth variety

and

. Since
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
preserves colimit, it is suffices to show that

\operatorname{\mathbf{Sm}}(k)\to\operatorname{\mathbf{DA}}^{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},{\operatorname{eff}}}(k,\mathbb{Q})\simeq\operatorname{\mathbf{DM}}^{\operatorname{eff}}(k,\mathbb{Q})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{\mathbf{DM}}(k,\mathbb{Q})\overset{F_{1}\circ a}{\underset{F_{2}\circ a}{\rightrightarrows}}D(\mathbb{Q}_{l}[\beta^{\pm}]\text{-}\mathrm{mod})^{op}

For a smooth variety

K(1)
-local algebraic

-theory
[
19
]
.
More precisely, for a prime

E_{2}^{p,2q}=H_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}^{p}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{l}(q))\Longrightarrow\pi_{2q-p}({L_{K(1,l)}}K(X)),

After tensoring with

Proposition 2.6
.

The functors
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are equivalent.

Proof.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be right adjoints of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
, respectively.

We first note that for any

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{SH}}_{KGL_{\mathbb{Q}}}}(aA,aB)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{SH}}_{H\mathbb{Q}}}(A,I(aB))

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{SH}}_{H\mathbb{Q}}}(A,B(i)[2i]),

where we used (
2.1
). In particular, if
\mathcal{G}
is a set of compact generators of

is a set of compact generators of
\operatorname{\mathbf{SH}}_{KGL_{\mathbb{Q}}}

Hence, for every

and every object

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{SH}}_{KGL_{\mathbb{Q}}}}(aC,G_{1}(M))

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{SH}}_{KGL_{\mathbb{Q}}}}(aC,G_{2}(M)).

In this section, we fix a field

\mathcal{T}

Lemma 3.1
.

The motive
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{T})\in\operatorname{\mathbf{DM}}(k,\mathbb{Q})
is zero if and only if
\mathcal{T}
is motivic quasi-phantom.

Proof.

Now

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{T})=H^{-1}\circ I\circ\mathcal{M}_{KGL_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{T}))

is a direct summand of
\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}(i)[2i]

Let

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{SH}}_{KGL_{\mathbb{Q}}}}\bigl(M(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes KGL_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathcal{M}_{KGL_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{T}))\bigr)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{DM}}(k,\mathbb{Q})}\bigl(M(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{T})\bigr).

On the other hand, by Kontsevich’s formula for mapping spectra in noncommutative motives, proved by Robalo

Since

Therefore

K_{0}(\mathcal{T}\otimes_{k}\operatorname{perf}_{\operatorname{dg}}(Y))_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{SH}}_{KGL_{\mathbb{Q}}}}\bigl(M(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes KGL_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathcal{M}_{KGL_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{T}))\bigr).

If

for every smooth projective

Conversely, assume that

Then we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{DM}}(k,\mathbb{Q})}\bigl(M(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}(n)[2n],\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{T})\bigr)=0.

Theorem 3.3

We shall prove only (1), as the proofs of (2) and (3) are entirely analogous. By Lemma

As explained above, there exists an idempotent endomorphism

Equivalently, since the category is pseudo-abelian,

\textstyle{M(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}(m)[2m]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\beta\in\pi_{2}K/l^{v}(A)
the Bott element, where
v\geq 1
if
l>3
,
v\geq 2
if
l=3
, and
v\geq 4
if
l=2
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
S
be a locally Noetherian integral scheme, and let
l
be a prime number invertible on
S
. Let

f:\mathcal{X}\to S

be a smooth proper morphism, and let
\mathcal{T}\subset\operatorname{perf}(\mathcal{X})
be an
S
-linear admissible full subcategory. Then, for any two geometric points
\overline{s},\overline{t}\to S
, there is a non-canonical isomorphism

\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{T}_{\overline{s}})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr)\simeq\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{T}_{\overline{t}})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr)

for every
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
v\geq 1
if
l>3
,
v\geq 2
if
l=3
, and
v\geq 4
if
l=2
.

Proof.

We may therefore assume that
S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
for a Noetherian strictly henselian discrete valuation ring
A
. Let
K
denote the fraction field of
A
. Since
cd_{l}(K)=1
, Thomason’s theorem
[
19
, Theorem 2.45 and Theorem 3.1]
yields a strongly convergent spectral sequence

E_{2}^{i,j}(\mathcal{X})=H_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}^{i-j}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}(j))\Longrightarrow\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{X})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr).

Likewise, we have strongly convergent spectral sequences

E_{2}^{i,j}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{s}})=H_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}^{i-j}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{s}},\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}(j))\Longrightarrow\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{s}})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr),

and

E_{2}^{i,j}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{t}})=H_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}^{i-j}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{t}},\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}(j))\Longrightarrow\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{t}})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr).

The natural morphisms
\mathcal{X}_{\overline{s}}\to\mathcal{X}
and

\mathcal{X}_{\overline{t}}\to\mathcal{X}
induce morphisms of spectral sequences

\textstyle{H_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}^{i-j}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{s}},\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}(j))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{s}})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr)}
\textstyle{H_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}^{i-j}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}(j))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\overline{s}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\overline{t}^{*}}
\textstyle{\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{X})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}^{i-j}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{t}},\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}(j))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{t}})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr)}

By smooth proper base change, the pullback maps

\overline{s}^{*}:H_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}^{i-j}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}(j))\longrightarrow H_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}^{i-j}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{s}},\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}(j))

and

\overline{t}^{*}:H_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}^{i-j}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}(j))\longrightarrow H_{{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}}^{i-j}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{t}},\mathbb{Z}/l^{v}(j))

are isomorphisms. Since the above spectral sequences are strongly convergent, it follows that

\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{s}})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr)\simeq\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{X})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr)\simeq\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{t}})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr).

Let

i:\mathcal{T}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{perf}(\mathcal{X})

be the inclusion, and let

l:\operatorname{perf}(\mathcal{X})\to\mathcal{T}

be its left adjoint. Set

e:=i\circ l:\operatorname{perf}(\mathcal{X})\to\operatorname{perf}(\mathcal{X}).

Then
e
is an idempotent exact endofunctor. By base change,
e
induces idempotent exact endofunctors

e_{\overline{s}}:\operatorname{perf}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{s}})\to\operatorname{perf}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{s}})\qquad\text{and}\qquad e_{\overline{t}}:\operatorname{perf}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{t}})\to\operatorname{perf}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{t}}),

whose essential images are precisely
\mathcal{T}_{\overline{s}}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\overline{t}}
, respectively.

By functoriality of Thomason’s spectral sequence with respect to exact functors, the endofunctors

e,\qquad e_{\overline{s}},\qquad e_{\overline{t}}

induce endomorphisms of the above spectral sequences, and these endomorphisms are compatible with the pullback maps
\overline{s}^{*}
and
\overline{t}^{*}
. In particular, the isomorphism

\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{s}})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr)\simeq\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{t}})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr)

intertwines the endomorphisms induced by
e_{\overline{s}}
and
e_{\overline{t}}
.

Now, since
\mathcal{T}_{\overline{s}}\subset\operatorname{perf}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{s}})
and
\mathcal{T}_{\overline{t}}\subset\operatorname{perf}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{t}})
are admissible,
K
-theory splits accordingly, and the image of the endomorphism induced by
e_{\overline{s}}
(resp.
e_{\overline{t}}
) on

\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{s}})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr)\text{ (resp. }\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{X}_{\overline{t}})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr))

is naturally identified with

\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{T}_{\overline{s}})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr)\text{ (resp. }\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{T}_{\overline{t}})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr)\bigr)).

Therefore the above isomorphism restricts to an isomorphism

\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{T}_{\overline{s}})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr)\simeq\pi_{j}\bigl(K/l^{v}(\mathcal{T}_{\overline{t}})[\beta^{-1}]\bigr).

This proves the proposition.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let

be a Noetherian connected scheme, and let

be a smooth proper morphism. Assume that the Chow motive of each fiber is Kimura-finite. Suppose that for some point

\mathcal{U}\to S
whose image contains
0
and such that every fiber of
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{U}}\to\mathcal{U}
admits a motivic quasi-phantom category. Moreover, under the Belmans–Okawa–Ricolfi conjecture
[
3
, Conjecture 9.9]
, the nonvanishing condition
L_{K(1,l)}K(\mathcal{T}_{0})\neq 0
is unnecessary.
