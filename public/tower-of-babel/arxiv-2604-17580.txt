The Witt ring of the real sphere
Heng Xie

Abstract
. We calculate the Witt ring of the real sphere.



1.
Introduction

In the 1970s, Knebusch introduced the Witt ring
W(X)
of quadratic forms over a scheme
X
and pointed out that the Witt ring
W(S^{n})
remained uncomputed (cf.
[
12
, p. 105]
), where

S^{n}:=\textnormal{Spec\,}\,\mathbb{R}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]/(\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}^{2}-1)

denotes the real sphere. Nearly fifty years have passed, it might be surprising to the non-specialist that, even the group structure of the Witt ring of such an elementary variety has not been fully determined. This is striking since the analogous computations for
K
-theory and Chow groups are well-known. The aim of this article is to solve this problem of computing the ring
W(S^{n})
.

The underlying group of
W(S^{n})

In the 1990s, Knus
[
13
]
made the computation of
W(S^{n})
for
n=1,2
(See also
[
4
, Example 1.2.6]
for a result of Ojanguren on this). In the 1980s, Brumfiel
[
5
]
constructed the signature map (a ring homomorphism)

\gamma:W(X)\to KO(X_{\mathbb{R}})

for any algebraic variety over
\mathbb{R}
and its associate space
X_{\mathbb{R}}
of
\mathbb{R}
-points, and showed
\gamma
is an isomorphism away from two torsions, i.e.
\gamma:W(X)[\frac{1}{2}]\xrightarrow{\cong}KO(X_{\mathbb{R}})[\frac{1}{2}]
. More recently, another proof of Brumfiel’s theorem was given by Karoubi, Schlichting, and Weibel
[
11
]
. In 2007, Dell’Ambrigio and Fasel computed
W(S^{n})[\frac{1}{2}]
via Balmer’s localization sequence away from two, which is independent of Brumfiel, (cf.
[
7
]
).

Unfortunately, none of these results reveal the two torsion information of
W(S^{n})
, except for
n=1,2
. Therefore, the two torsion problem for
W(S^{n})
(
n\geq 3
) is exactly what Theorem
1.1
resolves. Experts immediately see that
W(S^{n})
has the pattern of the classical 8-Bott periodicity, i.e. share the same pattern with Atiyah’s
KO
-theory
KO(S^{n}_{\mathbb{R}})\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\pi_{n}(O^{\infty})
. This leads us to show that

Theorem 1.2
.

The Brumfiel’s map
\gamma:W(S^{n})\to KO(S^{n}_{\mathbb{R}})
is a ring isomorphism.

Since the ring structure of
KO(S^{n}_{\mathbb{R}})
is well-known, Theorem
1.2
settles the problem of computing the ring
W(S^{n})
.
Our search for explicit generators of
W(S^{n})
leads us to a condensed version of the Clifford-module description of the non-trivial generator of
KO(S^{n}_{\mathbb{R}})
due to Atiyah-Bott-Shapiro
[
2
]
and Fossum
[
8
]
(see Formula (
1
) and Definition
2.1
, and compare with
\ker(\alpha_{M})
in
[
8
]
).
Together with Swan’s result
[
18
, Theorem 3]
, we see that Witt-theory,
KO
-theory and
K
-theory are unified in the case of real spheres, i.e.

W(S^{n})\xrightarrow{\cong}KO(S^{n}_{\mathbb{R}})\xleftarrow{\cong}K_{0}(S^{n}).

It is commonly believed that Brumfiel’s map
\gamma
is not an isomorphism integrally (neither injective nor surjective), but only becomes an isomorphism away from two torsions, since there could be real vector bundles on
X_{\mathbb{R}}
that can not be realised as an algebraic quadratic form over
X
. This is the case for projective spaces
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. It might be surprising that Theorem
1.2
shows that quadratic forms over the real spheres behave nicely, so that all the quadratic forms (up to hyperbolic forms) over real sphere
S^{n}
precisely comes from topological real vector bundles stably. It leads to the following question:

Question 1.3
.

For which real algebraic varieties is Brumfiel’s map
\gamma
an isomorphism?

For
X
to be a point, Question
1.3
has a trivial positive answer. Our real sphere computation provides an interesting non-trivial example to the positive answer of Question
1.3
. In general, the answer of Question
1.3
might depend on the development of the computation of Witt rings of algebraic varieties, and measures the gap between the algebraic theory of quadratic forms and the topology of real vector bundles.

2.1.
Construct the non-trivial generator

We will show that the non-trivial generator of
W(S^{n})
occurs only when
n\equiv 1,2,4,8\mod 8
in Section
5
. The aim of this section is to construct them explicitly. For Clifford algebras, we follow the notation
\mathrm{C}^{p,q}
used in Karoubi’s book
[
10

(See Section
3
for a summary).

2.1.1.

Let
A_{n}
be the coordinate ring
\mathbb{R}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]/(\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2}-1)
. Take the generators
e_{0},\ldots,e_{n}\in\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}
with
e_{i}^{2}=1
and
e_{i}e_{j}=-e_{j}e_{i}
for
i\neq j
. Consider the endomorphism

\Phi:A_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}\to A_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}

given by

(1)

\Phi(a\otimes u)=\frac{1}{2}(\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}a\otimes e_{i}u+a\otimes u)=:\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{1})\cdot(a\otimes u)

where
\mathbf{x}:=\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}\otimes e_{i}
is the left multiplication. It is not hard to see that
\Phi
is an idempotent operator, since
\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2}=1
in
A_{n}
. Let
\sigma:\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}\to\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}
be the canonical involution, i.e.
\sigma(e_{i})=e_{i}
and
\sigma(xy)=\sigma(yx)
. Note that the trace form

\beta:\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}\times\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}\to\mathbb{R},(x,y)\mapsto\textnormal{Tr}(\sigma(x)y)

is positive definite and non-degenerate. Moreover, the trace form
\beta
is compatible with
\Phi
, i.e. the diagram

\textstyle{A_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\hat{\beta}}
\textstyle{A_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hat{\beta}}
\textstyle{\textnormal{Hom}_{A}(A_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1},A_{n})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi^{\vee}}
\textstyle{\textnormal{Hom}_{A}(A_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1},A_{n})}

commutes where
\hat{\beta}(a\otimes x,b\otimes y):=ab\beta(x,y)
. In other words,
\hat{\beta}(\Phi(x),y)=\hat{\beta}(x,\Phi(y))
.

2.1.2.

The algebra
\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}
is semi-simple, and we can always find a primitive idempotent element
\wp
so that
\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}\wp
is an irreducible left ideal in
\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Define

P_{n}:=\ker(\Phi:A_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}\wp\to A_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}\wp).

Since
\Phi
is an idempotent operator,
P_{n}
is a projective
A_{n}
-module.

Lemma 2.2
.

The form
\hat{\beta}
restricting to
P_{n}
is non-degenerate.

Proof.

The trace form

\beta:\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}\times\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}\to\mathbb{R},(x,y)\mapsto\textnormal{Tr}(\sigma(x)y)

is positive definite. Hence, it restricts a non-degenerate symmetric form
\beta:E\times E\to\mathbb{R}
on the subspace
E:=\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}\wp
, which gives rise to a non-degenerate form on
A_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}E
by tensoring the trivial form on
A_{n}
. Write

A_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}E=\ker(\Phi)\oplus\ker(1-\Phi)=P_{n}\oplus Q_{n}

Since
\hat{\beta}(\Phi(x),y)=\hat{\beta}(x,\Phi(y))
, we must have
\hat{\beta}(p,q)=0
for any
p\in P_{n}
and
q\in Q_{n}
. It follows that
\hat{\beta}
restricts to a non-degenerate form on
P_{n}
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

The module
P_{n}
provides another construction of the projective module over
A_{n}
studied by Fossum
[
8
]
. Later, we will show that the form
(P_{n},\hat{\beta})
represents the non-trivial generator in
W(S^{n})
for
n\equiv 1,2,4,8\mod 8
.

2.2.
On the Brumfiel’s map

Let
X
be a smooth algebraic variety over
\mathbb{R}
. We may assume that
X_{\mathbb{R}}
is compact which is enough for us. Recall the Brumfiel’s map

\displaystyle\gamma:W(X)

\displaystyle\to KO(X_{\mathbb{R}})

\displaystyle[V,\phi]

\displaystyle\mapsto[V_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}]-[V_{\mathbb{R}}^{-}]

where the form
(V_{\mathbb{R}},\phi_{\mathbb{R}})
on
X_{\mathbb{R}}
yields a decomposition
V_{\mathbb{R}}=V_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\oplus V_{\mathbb{R}}^{-}
such that the restriction of form
\phi_{\mathbb{R}}
to
V_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
(resp.
V_{\mathbb{R}}^{-}
) is positive (resp. negative), cf.
[
10
, Chapter I.8]
.
The aim of this section is to prove that

Proposition 2.4
.

The map
\gamma:W(S^{n})\to KO(S^{n}_{\mathbb{R}})
is surjective.

Proof.

If
n\equiv 3,5,6,7\mod 8
, then
KO(S_{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
generated by the trivial line bundle
S^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
. It is clear that
\gamma
sends the trivial form
(\mathcal{O},\textnormal{id})
of
S^{n}
to
S_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
. The result follows.

If
n\equiv 1,2,4,8\mod 8
, then
KO(S_{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
has two generators. One is represented by the trivial bundle
S_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, and
\gamma[\mathcal{O},\textnormal{id}]=[S_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}]
as before. The other one
\xi
which is non-trivial can be however desribed by an algebraic process (cf. Atiyah-Bott-Shapiro
[
2
]
). Fossum
[
8
]
further studied this program which is what we use here.

Let
P:=P_{n}
and
E:=\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}\wp
. Since
e_{0}\in\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}
satisfies
e_{0}^{2}=1
, we have a decomposition

E=E^{0}\oplus E^{1}

with
E^{0}:=\ker(e_{0}+1)
and
E^{1}:=\ker(e_{0}-1)
.
Set
f_{i}=e_{0}e_{i}
(for
i\neq 0
) which serves as the generators of
\mathrm{C}^{n,0}
. It is clear that
f_{i}^{2}=-1
and
f_{i}f_{j}=-f_{j}f_{i}
for
i\neq j
. If
(u^{0},u^{1})\in E^{0}\oplus E^{1}
, then
f_{i}u^{0}\in E^{1}
, since
e_{0}f_{i}u^{0}=-f_{i}e_{0}u^{0}=f_{i}u^{0}
, and similarly
f_{i}u^{1}\in E^{0}
. Therefore,
E=E^{0}\oplus E^{1}
is a graded left
\mathrm{C}^{n,0}
-module in view of the basis
f_{i}
. The irreducibility of
E
as a left
\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}
implies that
E
is a graded left irreducible
\mathrm{C}^{n,0}
-module.

The form
\beta_{\mathbb{R}}
is positive definite, i.e. a metric on
P_{\mathbb{R}}
, therefore
\gamma([P,\beta])=[P_{\mathbb{R}}]
. Note that, via realization, the operator (
1
) induces a map
S_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times E\xrightarrow{\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}}S_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times E
by

\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbf{a},u)=(\mathbf{a},\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{a_{e}})u)

where
\mathbf{a}:=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
\mathbf{a_{e}}:=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}e_{i}
such that
\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}^{2}=1
. Hence,
P_{\mathbb{R}}=\ker(\Phi_{\mathbb{R}})
. It suffices to show that
\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}
coincide with
\alpha_{E}
in
[
8
]
. Note that

e_{0}u^{0}=-u^{0},\quad e_{0}u^{1}=u^{1},\quad e_{i}u^{0}=f_{i}u^{0},\quad e_{i}u^{1}=-f_{i}u^{1}

for
i\geq 1
. Let
a^{*}
denote
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}f_{i}
as in
[
8
]
. The map
\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}
then acts as

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbf{a},u^{0}+u^{1})

\displaystyle=\big(\mathbf{a},\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{a_{e}})(u^{0}+u^{1})\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\mathbf{a},\frac{1}{2}(u^{0}+u^{1}-a_{0}u^{0}+a_{0}u^{1}+a^{*}u^{0}-a^{*}u^{1})\big)

which coincide with Fossum’s
\alpha_{E}(\mathbf{a},u^{0}+u^{1})
via the automorphism

T:S_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times E\xrightarrow{\Phi}S_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times E,\quad(\mathbf{a},u^{0}+u^{1})\mapsto(\mathbf{a},u^{0}-u^{1}).

Hence,
P_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\ker(\alpha_{E})
which is the non-trivial generator of
KO(S^{n})
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

The orthogonal sum

(P_{n}^{+},\hat{\beta}|_{P_{n}^{+}})\perp(P_{n}^{-},\hat{\beta}|_{P_{n}^{-}})

is isometric to the trivial form
2^{\delta(n+1)}\langle 1\rangle
over
S^{n}
.

Proof.

Set
M_{\pm}:=A_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}E_{\pm}.

Since
\tau(\wp_{+})=\wp_{-}
, the map

T:M_{-}\longrightarrow M_{+},\quad T(a\otimes u):=a\otimes\tau(u),

is an
A_{n}
-module isomorphism. We first check that
T
is an isometry if we input the form
\hat{\beta}
over both sides of
T
. Note that we have

\textnormal{Tr}(\tau(z))=\textnormal{Tr}(z)

for all
z\in\mathrm{C}^{0,n+1}
.
Since
\tau
commutes with the canonical involution
\sigma
, we have

\beta(\tau(u),\tau(v))=\textnormal{Tr}\bigl(\sigma(\tau(u))\,\tau(v)\bigr)=\textnormal{Tr}\bigl(\tau(\sigma(u)v)\bigr)=\textnormal{Tr}(\sigma(u)v)=\beta(u,v).

for
u,v\in E_{+}
. Therefore,
T
is an isometry.

Furthermore, since
\tau(e_{i})=-e_{i}
for all
i
, we have

T(\mathbf{x}\cdot(a\otimes u))=-\mathbf{x}\cdot T(a\otimes u).

Thus,
T\circ\Phi=(1-\Phi)\circ T.

Consequently,

T(\textnormal{ker}(\Phi))=\textnormal{ker}(1-\Phi),

that is,

T(P_{n}^{-})=Q_{n}^{+}:=\textnormal{ker}(1-\Phi:M_{+}\to M_{+})

which yields an isometry

(P_{n}^{-},\hat{\beta}|_{P_{n}^{-}})\cong(Q_{n}^{+},\hat{\beta}|_{Q_{n}^{+}}).

The proof of Lemma
2.2
applied to
(M_{+},\hat{\beta})
gives that

(P_{n}^{+},\hat{\beta}|_{P_{n}^{+}})\perp(P_{n}^{-},\hat{\beta}|_{P_{n}^{-}})\cong(P_{n}^{+},\hat{\beta}|_{P_{n}^{+}})\perp(Q_{n}^{+},\hat{\beta}|_{Q_{n}^{+}})\cong(M_{+},\hat{\beta}).

Finally,
\beta
is positive definite on
E_{+}
which must be the trivial form. If follows that

(M_{+},\hat{\beta})

is the trivial form of rank
\dim(E_{+})=2^{\delta(n+1)}
.
This proves the lemma.
∎

The result that
W(S^{n})\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
for
n\equiv 0\mod 4
follows. ∎
