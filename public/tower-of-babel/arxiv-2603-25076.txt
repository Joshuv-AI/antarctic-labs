Analytical continuation of prime zeta function for ℜ ⁡ ( s ) > 1 2 \Re(s)>\frac{1}{2} assuming (RH)
Artur Kawalec

Abstract
We derive a simple expression to analytically continue the prime zeta function to the domain ℜ ⁡ ( s ) > 1 2 \Re(s)>\frac{1}{2} assuming (RH) and taking into account a proper branch cut. We also verify the formula numerically and provide several plots.



1
Introduction

Let
p=\{2,3,5,7,\ldots\}
be a sequence of primes and the prime zeta function

P(s)=\sum_{p}\frac{1}{p^{s}}

(1)

is defined as a generalized zeta series over primes, which converges absolutely for
\Re(s)>1
. Another representation of the prime zeta function is the very well-known Möbius inversion formula

P(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}\log\zeta(ns)

(2)

which further continues
P(s)
for
\Re(s)>0
(see [2]). And also, there is a natural boundary line at
\Re(s)=0
, after which
P(s)
cannot be further continued to
\Re(s)\leq 0
because of a dense accumulation of singularities that form near
\Re(s)=0
[5, p. 215]. In our previous article [3] we derived a series expansion of the prime zeta about
s=1
using Stieltjes integration. In this article, we also show by similar Stieltjes integration, that

Theorem 1
.

P(s)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\Bigg\{\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p^{s}}+\operatorname{E_{1}}[(s-1)\log(x)]\Bigg\}

(3)

which is analytic for
\Re(s)>\tfrac{1}{2}
with a branch cut on
(\tfrac{1}{2},1]
assuming (RH).

And the exponential integral is defined by

\operatorname{E_{1}}(z)=\int_{z}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t}dt,\quad|\arg(z)|<\pi

(4)

valid for complex
z
with a branch cut on
(-\infty,0]
, and its series expansion is

\operatorname{E_{1}}(z)=-\gamma-\log(z)-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-z)^{k}}{k\cdot k!}

(5)

We now prove this Theorem in the next Section.

2
Proof of Theorem 1

To show that, let us review some basic definitions. The prime counting function is defined by

\pi(x)=\sum_{p\leq x}1

(6)

for positive integer argument
x>0
, and by the average value

\pi(x)=\frac{1}{2}\Bigg[\sum_{p<x}1+\sum_{p\leq x}1\Bigg]

(7)

for positive real argument
x>0
. This means that its value is defined as an average of the two sides of the step when
x
is an integer. The asymptotic formula is

\pi(x)=\operatorname{li}(x)+f(x)

(8)

where the logarithmic integral is defined by the P.V. integral function

\operatorname{li}(x)=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{\log t}dt

(9)

for real
x>0
. One also has the relation

\frac{d}{dx}\operatorname{li}(x)=\frac{1}{\log x}.

(10)

And as for the remainder
f(x)
the best estimate is

f(x)=O(\sqrt{x}\log x)

(11)

assuming (RH).
We prove Theorem
1
by Stieltjes integration of the remainder term as follows:

\displaystyle P(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p^{s}}+\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{s}}\,d\pi(t)

(12)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p^{s}}+\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{s}}d\operatorname{li}(x)\,+\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{s}}df(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p^{s}}+\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{s}\log(t)}dt\,+\Biggl[\frac{f(t)}{t^{s}}\Biggr]_{x}^{\infty}+s\int_{x}^{\infty}t^{-s-1}f(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p^{s}}+\operatorname{E_{1}}[(s-1)\log(x)]-\frac{f(x)}{x^{s}}+s\int_{x}^{\infty}t^{-s-1}f(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p^{s}}+\operatorname{E_{1}}[(s-1)\log(x)]-\frac{\pi(x)-\operatorname{li}(x)}{x^{s}}+O\Big(x^{\tfrac{1}{2}-s}\log x\Big)\,

The integral can be expressed in terms of the exponential integral as

\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{s}\log t}dt=\operatorname{E_{1}}[(s-1)\log(x)]

(13)

by substituting
u=\log t
and
t=e^{u}
and
dt=e^{u}du
. And also the size of the boundary term

O\Big(\frac{\pi(x)-\operatorname{li}(x)}{x^{s}}\Big)=O\Big(x^{\tfrac{1}{2}-s}\log x\Big)

(14)

is also the same as the last integral in (12). As a result, when taking the limit
x\to\infty
, we have that

P(s)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\Bigg\{\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p^{s}}+\operatorname{E_{1}}[(s-1)\log(x)]-\frac{\pi(x)-\operatorname{li}(x)}{x^{s}}\Bigg\}

(15)

for
\Re(s)>\tfrac{1}{2}
, but the boundary term (14) doesn’t improve convergence of this equation, hence it may be dropped. And we must introduce at branch cut
(\tfrac{1}{2},1]
because of log in the exponential integral (see series expansion (5)).
