Monotonicity Property of Non-Archimedean Heat Equation
Alexandra V. Antoniouk, Anatoly N. Kochubei

Abstract
We prove the monotone dependence of the heat kernel corresponding to the Vladimirov-Taibleson non-Archimedean fractional differentiation operator D α D^{\alpha} on the order α \alpha .



1
Introduction

Let
K
be a non-Archimedean local field,
\Omega=\left\{x\in K^{n}:\ \|x\|_{K^{n}}\leq q^{N}\right\}
be an ultrametric ball. Here
N\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\|(x_{1},\ldots x_{n})\|_{K^{n}}=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}|x_{j}|_{K}
,
q
is the residue cardinality of
K

(for the basic notions regarding local fields see Section 2 below and
[
7
,
10
,
18
]
). The best known example of a non-Archimedean local field is the field of
p
-adic numbers; here
p
is a prime number.

A model example of a pseudo-differential operator acting on real- or complex-valued functions on
\Omega
is the Vladimirov-Taibleson fractional differential operator

\ \big(D^{\alpha,n}u\big)(x)=c_{n,\alpha}\int\limits_{K^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{\|x-y\|_{K^{n}}^{n+\alpha}}\,dy,\quad x\in\Omega,

(1)

where
dy
is the Haar measure,
c_{n,\alpha}=\dfrac{q^{\alpha}-1}{1-q^{-\alpha-n}},\quad\alpha>0,
and a function
u
is extended onto
K^{n}
by a nonlocal Dirichlet condition, that is (for the homogeneous case)
u(y)=0
, as
y\in\Omega^{c}=K^{n}\setminus\Omega
. The precise understanding of the integral in (
1
) depends on smoothness assumptions for
u
; the simplest case is that of its boundedness and local constancy when
u(y)=u(x)
, as
y
belongs to a neighbourhood of a point
x
. Note that
K
is totally disconnected, and, in contrast to classical analysis, the set of such functions is quite rich.

The choice of the constant
c_{n,\alpha}
is motivated by the representation of
D^{\alpha,n}
in terms of the Fourier analysis on
K^{n}
; see
[
10
,
17
]
.

The operator (
1
) and related equations have been studied by many authors, see the monographs
[
17
,
10
,
1
,
16
,
9
,
19
,
20
]
and many recent papers. Its form, especially its nonlocality, resembles that of the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
of real analysis, though the actual meaning of the objects of a non-Archimedean theory is quite different.

In this note we consider an analog of the first boundary value problem for the heat equation, that is

\ \left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial u(t,x)}{\partial t}+\left(D^{\alpha,n}u\right)(t,x)=0,\quad x\in\Omega,\ t>0;\\
u(t,x)=0,\quad x\in\Omega^{c};\\
u(0,x)=\varphi(x),\quad x\in\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(2)

The non-Archimedean ball
\Omega
is simultaneously open and closed, so that it has no topological boundary. Though one cannot speak, in the classical style, about ‘‘boundary value problems’’, there is a well-developed theory of parabolic equations of this kind. See
[
10
,
2
,
19
,
20
,
11
,
3
,
4
]
and references therein. Our aim here is to prove a monotone dependence, in a certain sense, of a solution of (2) on
\alpha
. A similar problem for the fractional Laplacian was studied in
[
6
]
.

2
Local fields
[
7
,
10
,
18
]

A (non-Archimedean) local field is a non-discrete totally disconnected locally compact topological field.

An important related notion is that of a field extension. If
k
is a subfield of a field
K
, then
K
is called an algebraic extension of
k
. In this situation, the field
K
is a vector space over
k
, and we call
K
a finite extension, if this vector space is finite-dimensional. This dimension is called a degree of this extension. A local field
K
is isomorphic either to a finite algebraic extension of the field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of
p
-adic numbers, if
K
has characteristic
0
, or to the field of formal Laurent series with coefficients from a finite field, if
K
has a positive characteristic. By Ostrowski’s theorem,
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is the only possible alternative to
\mathbb{R}
as a completion of the field of rational numbers. This shows the importance of non-Archimedean mathematics as one of the principal branches of mathematical science.

Any local field is endowed with absolute value
|\cdot|_{K}
, such that: 1)
|x|_{K}=0
, if and only if
x=0
; 2)
|xy|_{K}=|x|_{K}\cdot|y|_{K}
; 3)
|x+y|_{K}\leq\max(|x|_{K},|y|_{K})
. The last property is called the ultrametric inequality. It implies the equality
|x+y|_{K}=|x|_{K}
, if
|y|_{K}<|x|_{K}
.

The ring of integers
O=\{x\in K\colon|x|_{K}\leq 1\}
contains an ideal
P=\{x\in K\colon|x|_{K}<1\}
. There exists such an element
\beta
that
P=\beta O
. The quotient ring
\bar{K}=O/P
is actually a finite field. The absolute value is called normalized, if
|\beta|_{K}=q^{-1}
, where
q
is the cardinality of the finite field
O/P
. Unless stated otherwise, this property is assumed. A normalized absolute value takes exactly the values
q^{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
L
be a finite extension of a local field
K
. An operator of multiplication in
L
by an element
\xi
can be considered as a linear operator on a
K
-vector space. If the linear function
\xi\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(\xi)
does not vanish identically, then the extension is called separable. Here
\operatorname{Tr}
is the trace of a linear operator in a finite-dimensional vector space. All finite extensions of the field of characteristic zero are separable. On the other hand, the notion of separability makes sense also for the finite fields
\bar{K},\bar{L}
.

A finite extension
L
of a local field
K
is called unramified, if
\bar{L}
is a separable extension of
\bar{K}
of the same degree, as
L
over
K
. Any local field
K
has a unique (up to the isomorphism) unramified extension of each given degree
n\geq 2
. The cardinality of the residue field
\bar{L}
in this extension equals
q^{n}
where
q
is the residue cardinality for
K
.

The unramified extension of degree
n
has, as a
K
-vector space, a canonical basis consisting of representatives of a basis in
\bar{L}
over
\bar{K}
. Let
x\in L
have the coefficients
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in K
of its expansion with respect to the canonical basis. Then the normalized absolute value
|x|_{L}
has the representation
[
15
]
:

\ |x|_{L}=\Big(\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}|x_{j}|_{K}\Big)^{n}.

The comparison of
|x|_{L}
with another absolute value on
L
,
\|x\|_{L}=|x|^{1/n}_{L}\equiv\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}|x_{j}|_{K}
, which extends the absolute value from
K
, shows that the above canonical basis is an orthogonal basis (in the non-Archimedean sense
[
14
]
) in
L
considered as Banach space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L}
. Therefore this basis defines an isometric linear isomorphism
U:\ L\to K^{n}
,
Ux=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
.

The above isomorphism identifies the operator
D^{\alpha,n}
defined by (1) with the operator

\left(D_{L}^{\gamma}u\right)(x)=\frac{1-q^{n\gamma}}{1-q^{-n(\gamma+1)}}\int\limits_{L}\frac{u(z)-u(x)}{|z-x|_{L}^{\gamma+1}}\,dz,\quad\gamma=\dfrac{\alpha}{n};

(3)

see
[
12
]
for the details.

Thus, a multi-dimentional operator over a local field
K
is equivalent to a one-dimensional operator over a bigger field, the unramified extension
L
. Such properties are absent in real analysis (except some two-dimensional operators admitting complex representation).

3
The non-Archimedean heat equation

Let us consider the problem (2). We begin with the one-dimensional case (
n=1
).

Using the ultrametric property of the absolute value
|\cdot|_{K}
, we see that for
x\in\Omega
,

\left(D^{\alpha,1}u\right)(t,x)=\left(\Delta_{\Omega}^{\alpha}\right)u(t,x)+c_{1,\alpha}u(t,x)\int\limits_{|y|_{K}>q^{N}}\frac{dy}{|y|_{K}^{\alpha+1}}-c_{1,\alpha}\int\limits_{|y|_{K}>q^{N}}\frac{u(t,y)dy}{|y|_{K}^{\alpha+1}}

(4)

where the operator

\left(\Delta_{\Omega}^{\alpha}\right)u(t,x)=c_{1,\alpha}u(t,x)\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{u(t,x)-u(t,y)}{|x-y|_{K}^{\alpha+1}}\,dy,\quad x\in\Omega,

called a local Vladimirov-Taibleson operator in
[
5
]
, is an analog of the regional fractional operator
[
8
]
. The second term on the right in (4) equals
\lambda_{N}^{(\alpha)}u(t,x)
where

\lambda_{N}^{(\alpha)}=\dfrac{q-1}{q(1-q^{-\alpha-1})}q^{-\alpha N}

(see
[
11
]
). The third term on the right in (4) equals 0, due to the ‘‘boundary condition’’ in (2).

Thus, the problem (2) is reduced to a kind of a Cauchy problem (on the open and closed set
\Omega
) for the equation

\frac{\partial u(t,x)}{\partial t}+\left(\Delta_{\Omega}^{\alpha}u\right)(t,x)+\lambda_{N}^{(\alpha)}u(t,x)=0.

(5)

Setting
u(t,x)=e^{-\lambda_{N}^{(\alpha)}t}v(t,x)
we come to the equation

\frac{\partial v(t,x)}{\partial t}+\left(\Delta_{\Omega}^{\alpha}v\right)(t,x)=0,\quad x\in\Omega,

(6)

the simplest case of a heat equation on a compact
p
-adic manifold
[
5
]
.

An orthonormal basis in
L^{2}(\Omega)
can be constructed of Kozyrev’s
p
-adic wavelets
\psi:\ \Omega\to\mathbb{C}
, which are eigenfunctions of
\Delta_{\Omega}^{\alpha}
corresponding to the eigenvalues
[
13
,
5
]

\rho_{\psi}=c_{1,\alpha}\left\{\int\limits_{\Omega\setminus B}|y|_{K}^{-\alpha-1}dy+(\operatorname{mes}B)^{-\alpha}\right\},

(7)

where
\psi
is supported on a subball
B\subset\Omega
. Note that the eigenfunctions
\psi
do not depend on
\alpha
.

It is shown in
[
5
]
that the heat kernel function (a fundamental solution of the Cauchy problem) corresponding to the equation (6) has the form

H_{\alpha}(t,x,y)=1+\sum\limits_{\psi}e^{-t\rho_{\psi}}\psi(x)\overline{\psi(y)},\quad x\neq y\in\Omega,t>0,

(8)

where
\psi
runs the set of all the above wavelets. If
\alpha>1
,
H_{\alpha}
exists also for
x=y
,
t>0
and
0<H(t,\cdot,\cdot)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Returning to the equation (5) we get the corresponding heat kernel

G_{\alpha}(t,x,y)=e^{-\lambda_{N}^{(\alpha)}t}H_{\alpha}(t,x,y)

(9)

where
H_{\alpha}
is given by (8). The above constructions make sense also for the multi-dimensional situation, with an appropriate constant in (9).

4
Monotonicity

Now we formulate the main result of this paper.

Theorem
.

Suppose that
N\leq 0
,
1<\alpha_{0}<\alpha_{1}
. Then

G_{\alpha_{0}}(t,x,x)>G_{\alpha_{1}}(t,x,x)

for all
t>0
,
x\in\Omega
.

Proof.
We begin with the case where
n=1
. We have

\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\lambda_{N}^{(\alpha)}=\frac{q-1}{q}q^{-\alpha N}\frac{q^{-\alpha-1}\log q}{1-q^{-\alpha-1}}\left(\frac{1}{1-q^{-\alpha-1}}-N\right)>0,

so that
\lambda_{N}^{(\alpha)}
increases in
\alpha
, as
\alpha>1
Thus
\exp(-\lambda_{N}^{(\alpha)}t)
decreases in
\alpha
.

Similarly,

\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}c_{1,\alpha}=\frac{q^{\alpha}(1-q^{-\alpha-1})\log q-(q^{\alpha}-1)q^{-\alpha-1}\log q}{(1-q^{-\alpha-1})^{2}}

and

q^{\alpha}(1-q^{-\alpha-1})-(q^{\alpha}-1)q^{-\alpha-1}=q^{\alpha}-2q^{-1}+q^{-\alpha-1}=q^{-1}(q^{\alpha+1}-2)+q^{-\alpha-1}>0,

since
q\geq 2
, so that
c_{1,\alpha}
increases in
\alpha
, thus the eigenvalues
\rho_{\psi}
increase, while the expressions
\exp\{-t\rho_{\psi}\}
decrease in
\alpha
, which implies the decrease of the heat kernel.

Turning to the multi-dimensional case, we notice that the isometric isomorphism
U:\ L\to K^{n}
transforms the heat kernel of the operator over
K^{n}
into a similar one-dimensional operator over the unramified
extension
L
, so that the multi-dimensional case is a consequence of the one-dimensional one considered for a bigger field.
\blacksquare
