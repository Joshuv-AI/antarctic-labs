Calculation of a regularized Teukolsky Green function in Schwarzschild spacetime
David Q. Aruquipa, Marc Casals, Brien C. Nolan

Abstract
We obtain exact expressions for various factors involved in the Hadamard form of the retarded Green function for the (Bardeen-Press-)Teukolsky equation on Schwarzschild spacetime. We use these to improve on previous results for the calculation of this Green function. We work in a spacetime ℳ 2 × 𝕊 2 \mathcal{M}_{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2} conformal to Schwarzschild, in which the metric takes a direct product form. This allows us to derive a separable form for the direct (i.e., singular) part of the Hadamard form of the retarded Green function. The angular factor in this quantity is calculated explicitly. This shows an interesting interplay between geodesics of 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} , spin-weighted spherical harmonics, and Euler angles. The ℳ 2 \mathcal{M}_{2} factor equates to a spin-dependent factor that satisfies a transport equation along geodesics, times the square root of the van Vleck determinant. Both terms are calculated in an exact form for constant radius orbits (which includes the cases of circular timelike geodesics and static worldlines of Schwarzschild spacetime). This separable form also allows us to obtain the multipolar ℓ \ell -modes of the direct part for electromagnetic and gravitational field perturbations. We then use these ℓ \ell -modes to calculate, in the gravitational case, the retarded Green function minus its direct part: this is a better representation in practise of the retarded Green function for points near coincidence.



I
Introduction

Performing practical calculations of the
4
-dimensional retarded Green function (GF) for field perturbations of black hole spacetimes is notoriously difficult. One of the main technical difficulties is that the GF is a (two-point) distribution on spacetime, with singular support at coincidence and along any null geodesic connecting the two points
12
. It is often useful to calculate the GF by decomposing it into multipolar
\ell
-modes, which obey a differential equation of dimension lower than that obeyed by the full GF. This can be extremely effective in some regimes. However, as this approach necessarily involves in practice a
finite
mode-sum, it can only provide an approximation that is qualitatively different to the GF on its singular support: for example, Dirac
-\delta
distributions become smeared to Gaussian peaks. This smearing ‘contaminates’ the calculation away from the singularities of the GF calculated via a multipolar decomposition.
As an example of a consequence of that, in the scalar field case, this contamination can hinder the calculation of the scalar self-field and self-force, when obtained as worldline integrals of, respectively, the GF and its derivatives (see, e.g.,
23
for a review of the self-force and
7
;
29
for the calculation of the scalar self-field and self-force via worldline integrals and using a multipolar decomposition of the GF).

Fortunately, the explicit form of the GF (including its singular part) near coincidence is provided by the
Hadamard form

15
;
13
: the singular part is given by a Dirac-
\delta
distribution with support at coincidence and when the points are lightlike-separated. The Hadamard form thus allows for finding alternative methods for calculating the GF near coincidence.
One of these methods is to calculate the multipolar modes of the divergent Dirac-
\delta
term (called the direct term) in the Hadamard form and subtract them from the modes of the full GF.
We refer to the summation of the modes resulting from this subtraction as the non-direct part of the GF.
Since the Dirac-
\delta
term has sharp support where its argument is zero, the non-direct part of the GF
should provide a better approximation to the GF at any pairs of points except for exactly at coincidence (or for points connected by a direct null geodesic). The singular part of the GF exactly at coincidence is not needed for, e.g., self-field/force calculations since it is removed as part of a regularization procedure. If the singular part of the GF at coincidence is needed (via, e.g., an integral of the GF) for other applications, then this can be calculated separately (using, e.g., the Hadamard form again) and surgically added separately (this was done, e.g., for communication between quantum particle detectors in
18
).

The described method for improving the calculation of the GF away from coincidence was suggested and implemented in Ref.
8
in the case of scalar field perturbations of Schwarzschild spacetime. This was achieved by leveraging the fact that Schwarzschild spacetime is conformal to the direct product of a
2
-dimensional spacetime (
\mathcal{M}_{2}
) and the
2
-dimensional sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. In the scalar case, this property was exploited in
11
for finding a representation for the GF which makes explicit its complete singularity structure and then used in
8
for obtained the multipolar modes of the direct part in the Hadamard form and then calculating the non-direct part of the (scalar) GF.

In this paper we carry out a similar calculation for the case of
generic
-spin field perturbations of Schwarzschild space-time, which satisfy the (Bardeen-Press-)Teukolsky (BPT) equation
4
;
26
;
25
.
Specifically, we factor out the direct Hadamard term into two factors, one coming from
\mathcal{M}_{2}
and the other from
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. The factor coming from the
\mathbb{S}^{2}
we obtain in closed form in terms of Euler angles (see Eq. (
68
)). As for the factor coming from the

\mathcal{M}_{2}
, we express it in terms of its value in the scalar case (which is the square root of the van Vleck determinant
23
;
11
in
\mathcal{M}_{2}
) times a coefficient which is written as an integral along geodesics in
\mathcal{M}_{2}
(see Eq. (
85
)).
We thus provide a fully geometrical expression for the direct Hadamard term for the BPT equation lifted to the conformal Schwarzschild spacetime. We then use this representation in order to obtain an analytic expression for the multipolar modes of the direct Hadamard term. We calculate these modes in the cases of electromagnetic (spin
s=-1
) and gravitational (
s=-2
) perturbations. We finally use this calculation together with a calculation provided in
2
of the multipolar modes of the full GF for
s=-2
to calculate the non-direct part of this gravitational GF.
In certain restrictive cases, we calculate the direct term in the Hadamard form directly in Schwarzschild spacetime (without resorting to the mentioned
2+2
decomposition) either fully in closed form or else in terms of the van Vleck determinant in Schwarzschild; the above
2+2
decomposition is instead useful for generic spacetime points and for calculating the
\ell
-modes (with the consequent improvement in the representation of the GF away from coincidence).

The rest of this paper consists of the following. In Sec.
II
we present the Hadamard form for the GF in Schwarzschild spacetime, provide the equations satisfied by its various biscalars and obtain analytic expressions for its direct term along specific worldlines. We start using the
2+2
approach in Sec.
III

2+2
form. In Sec.

\mathcal{M}_{2}
- and
\mathbb{S}^{2}
-factors in the direct part of the Hadamard form. We use these expressions for the factors in Sec.
V

in order to obtain an analytic expression for the
\ell
-modes of the direct part, calculate these modes explicitly for spin
s=-1
and
-2
and then use the latter to calculate the non-direct part of the BPT GF for
s=-2
in Schwarzschild spacetime.
Finally, in appendices
A
–
C

\mathcal{M}_{2}
; in App.
D

Throughout this paper we use geometric units
c=G=1

II
Hadamard form of the BPT retarded Green function

The exterior Schwarzschild spacetime
(M,g)
is given by
M=\mathbb{R}\times(2M,+\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
, and the line element, in Schwarzschild coordinates
(x^{\mu})=(t,r,\theta,\phi)
,

ds^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=-fdt^{2}+f^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}_{2},

(1)

where
f=f(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}
and
d\Omega_{2}^{2}=d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}
is the line element of
\mathbb{S}^{2}

Helicity
-s
fields (
s=0
,
\pm 1
and
\pm 2

;
26
;
25

The spin of the field is
|s|
, but following common usage, we shall refer to

The retarded Green function (GF)

of the BPT equation satisfies

{}_{s}\mathcal{T}(_{s}G_{\textrm{ret}}(x,x^{\prime}))=-4\pi\delta_{4}(x,x^{\prime}),

(2)

where the Teukolsky operator is

{}_{s}\mathcal{T}=\Box+A^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+B=\Box+\frac{2s}{r^{2}}(a^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+b),

(3)

and
\delta_{4}(x,x^{\prime})
is the Dirac delta function on
M
.
Here,
\Box
is the d’Alembertian of the Schwarzschild metric tensor
g
, and we have defined

A^{\mu}=\frac{2s}{r^{2}}a^{\mu},\quad B=\frac{2s}{r^{2}}b,

(4)

with

\displaystyle a^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{r}{f}\left(1-\frac{3M}{r}\right)\partial_{t}+(r-M)\partial_{r}+i\cot\theta\csc\theta\partial_{\phi},

(5)

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(1-s\cot^{2}\theta).

(6)

The “retarded” boundary condition on
{}_{s}G_{\textrm{ret}}(x,x^{\prime})
requires that it vanishes when the field point
x
does not lie to the causal future of the base point
x^{\prime}
.

In local, normal neighbourhoods the so-called Hadamard form for the GF
{}_{s}G_{\textrm{ret}}(x,x^{\prime})
provides a representation which makes explicit its singular nature when the points
x
and
x^{\prime}
are connected via a null geodesic.
A normal neighbourhood
\mathcal{N}(x)
of a point
x
is a neighbourhood of
x
such that
every
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}(x)
is connected to
x
by a unique geodesic which
lies in
\mathcal{N}(x)
.
Within a normal neighbourhood of
x^{\prime}
, the Hadamard form for
{}_{s}G_{\textrm{ret}}(x,x^{\prime})
is (see e.g. Theorem 4.5.1 of
13
)

_{s}G_{\textrm{ret}}(x,x^{\prime})={}_{s}U(x,x^{\prime})\delta_{+}(\sigma)+{}_{s}V(x,x^{\prime})\theta_{+}(-\sigma),

(7)

where
{}_{s}U(x,x^{\prime})
and
{}_{s}V(x,x^{\prime})
are smooth biscalars,

\sigma=\sigma(x,x^{\prime})
is Synge’s world function
(i.e., it is equal to half of the (signed) squared distance along the unique geodesic connecting
x
and
x^{\prime}
).

\delta_{+}
and
\theta_{+}
are the spacetime delta- and step-functions, respectively, supported in the region
\{t>t^{\prime}\}
.
The terms
{}_{s}U\delta_{+}
and
{}_{s}V\theta_{+}
in (
7
) are referred to as, respectively, the direct and tail Hadamard terms.

Following Ref.
13
(see also
2
;
17
) the direct Hadamard biscalar
{}_{s}U
is taken to satisfy the transport equation

\left(2\sigma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+{\sigma^{\mu}}_{\mu}+\sigma_{\mu}A^{\mu}-4\right){}_{s}U=0.

(8)

As usual
23
, we use
\sigma_{\mu}=\nabla_{\mu}\sigma
and
{\sigma^{\mu}}_{\mu}=\Box\sigma
. Also, the tail Hadamard biscalar
{}_{s}V
satisfies the homogeneous Teukolsky equation,

{}_{s}\mathcal{T}\,({}_{s}V)=0,

(9)

complemented by the following boundary condition as a transport equation (this agrees with the corresponding equation of
17
, but note that
{}_{s}V
in this paper has the opposite sign to the corresponding term in the present paper):

\displaystyle 2\sigma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\left({}_{s}\check{V}\right)+({\sigma^{\mu}}_{\mu}+\sigma_{\mu}A^{\mu}-2){}_{s}\check{V}=\left.\left({}_{s}\mathcal{T}\,({}_{s}U)\right)\right|_{\sigma=0},

(10)

where
{}_{s}\check{V}\equiv\left.{}_{s}V\right|_{\sigma=0}
.
In their turn, the transport equations (
8
) for
{}_{s}U
and (
10
) for
{}_{s}V
are complemented by the initial conditions, respectively,

\left[{}_{s}U\right]=1

(11)

and

\left[{}_{s}\check{V}\right]=\left[{}_{s}V\right]=\frac{1}{2}\left[\Box{}_{s}U\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left[A^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\left({}_{s}U\right)\right]+\frac{1}{2}B.

(12)

We use square brackets around a bitensor
A(x,x^{\prime})
to denote its value at coincidence (i.e.,
[A]=A(x,x)
).

In the scalar case
s=0
, the direct biscalar is simply equal to the square root of the van Vleck determinant
\Delta
on Schwarzschild spacetime:

{}_{0}U(x,x^{\prime})=\Delta^{1/2}(x,x^{\prime}).

(13)

We note that, whereas for
s=0

{}_{s}U(x,x^{\prime})

{}_{s}V(x,x^{\prime})
and
{}_{s}G_{\textrm{ret}}(x,x^{\prime})
are both symmetric and real-valued and

is a self-adjoint operator, none of these properties need to hold for

In App.
D
we use Eqs. (
12
) and (
8
) to derive a covariant expansion (i.e., in terms of powers of
\sigma^{\mu}
) for
{}_{s}U
and to obtain the coincidence limit

[{}_{s}V]=-s^{2}\frac{2M}{r^{3}}.

(14)

\displaystyle 2\ln\left(\frac{{}_{s}U(x,x^{\prime})}{\Delta^{1/2}(x,x^{\prime})}\right)

\displaystyle-\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{2}}\dot{z^{\mu}}A_{\mu}(z(\lambda))\textrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\,\ln\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{s(3\epsilon+1)}+\ln\left(\frac{r^{\prime}-2M}{r-2M}\right)^{s(1-\epsilon)}.

III
2+2 form of the BPT equation

We work in the conformally rescaled spacetime with line element

The

VI
Conclusions

In this paper we have first analyzed the direct part of the Hadamard form for the GF of the Bardeen-Press-Teukolsky equation in Schwarzschild spacetime.
We started by obtaining closed-form expressions for this direct part for points along some specific worldlines (see Eqs. (
19
) and (
21
)).

For
generic
spacetime points and in order to obtain the
\ell
-modes, we instead exploited the fact that Schwarzschild is conformal to
\hat{M}=\mathcal{M}_{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
. In particular, we have obtained an analytic and geometrical expression (Eq. (
100
)) for this direct part in terms of the world function
\hat{\sigma}
in
\hat{M}
,
a factor admitting a closed-form expression in terms of the Euler angles (see Eq. (
68
)), the van Vleck determinant of
\mathcal{M}_{2}
and and a factor admitting an integral representation along geodesics in
\mathcal{M}_{2}
. In the case of constant radius worldlines of Schwarzschild, explicit formulas for these terms are given - see Eqs. (
69
), (
84
) and Eq. (
85
). These exact expressions - which can be generalised to essentially all pairs of points of Schwarzschild spacetime - are obtained by using the fact that
\mathcal{M}_{2}
is a causal domain, and by introducing coordinates on
\mathcal{M}_{2}
comprising proper time along the geodesics and the initial radial velocity
21
.
We then used this expression for the direct part in order to obtain an expression (Eq. (
107
)) for its multipolar
\ell
-modes and explicitly calculated them in the electromagnetic
s=-1
(Fig.
6
) and gravitational
s=-2
(see Fig.
7
) cases.
We have derived (App.
C
) small coordinate distance expansions for the
\ell
-modes of the direct part and various other quantities in
\mathcal{M}_{2}
.

The
\ell
-modes of the direct part of the GF may be useful in themselves for, e.g.,
modelling the early stage of the ringdown of a gravitational waveform (e.g.,
22
;
24
).
In our case, we used these
\ell
-modes in order to calculate the non-direct part of the GF of the BPT equation for
s=-2
(see Figs.
8
and
9
).
We have shown that this
\ell
-mode calculation of the non-direct part is a better representation than the corresponding
\ell
-mode calculation of the GF.
The improvement, however and similarly to what already happened for
