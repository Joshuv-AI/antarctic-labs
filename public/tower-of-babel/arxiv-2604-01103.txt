A Framework for Coalgebraic Reward-Sensitive Bisimulation
Pedro H. Azevedo de Amorim, Mayuko Kori, Koko Muroya

Abstract
In this paper we present a framework for modelling reward-sensitive bisimulations , that is, bisimulations that account for quantitative differences such as accumulated rewards. To capture both qualitative and quantitative aspects uniformly, we consider two interacting notions of bisimulation: a graded variant that tracks bounded reward differences, and an ungraded one that abstracts from them. Our characterization of these notions is done in the fibrational and coalgebraic approach to (bi)simulation initiated by Hermida and Jacobs. To formally relate the graded and ungraded notions, we deploy categorical gluing, a standard technique in categorical logic. Furthermore, we show that this construction interacts well with standard coalgebra concepts, such as final coalgebras, and that it yields a unified characterization in terms of combined notions of bisimulations under mild assumptions. In order to demonstrate the versatility of our approach, we show how it encompasses various bisimulation notions for different kinds of systems, including relation-based bisimulations for automata with rewards and metric-based notions of bisimulations for labelled Markov processes.



1
Introduction

Quantitative extensions of (bi)simulations have been extensively studied
for systems equipped with quantitative data.
For example, over the past few years there has been much
work on behavioural metrics and relaxations thereof to reason about distances
of states in transition systems
[
8
,
4
,
24
]
.
Similarly, there has been work on defining
notions of transitions systems where transitions may incur cost
[
28
,
15
,
16
,
2
,
23
]
and
bisimulation for such transition systems
[
18
]
.

In this paper, we focus on reward-sensitive (bi)simulations,
which capture not only whether two states are just similar, but also similar up to a relative reward.
For example, imagine a variant of finite automata where each transition
has a reward associated with it. In such a scenario, one might be
interested in understanding whether one state can accept the same
string as a state similar to it which receives more reward.
While traditional notions of bisimulation relations
are effective at reasoning about qualitative properties of transition
systems, such as whether two states accept the same language, they
cannot cope with quantitative properties such as distances and relative cost.
This motivates
a graded refinement of the usual notion of bisimulation,
where the grade indexes the tolerance difference in accumulated reward.
Such graded refinement has appeared in the literature as

amortised bisimulations

[
18
]

for labelled transition systems with costs. Yet, it is still uncertain
if there is a common abstraction that encompasses these reward-sensitive
notions of bisimulation with more metric-based examples. Furthermore,
there is no categorical/coalgebraic theory that explains when such
graded bisimulations can be seen as a refinement of an ungraded
notion of bisimulation.

Motivated by these kinds of applications,
we explore coalgebraic approaches to graded refinements
of bisimulation relations and its interactions with ungraded
bisimulations.
In particular,
we propose a coalgebraic and
fibred axiomatization of graded and ungraded notions of bisimulation,
obtained by applying
categorical gluing—an important construction from
categorical logic—to the coalgebra world. We use this
construction to define the “coherent” pairing of two different notions
of coalgebra along a well-behaved morphism between them.
We show that it
provides
a purely formal way to relate the coalgebraic structure of graded and ungraded bisimulations,
and to identify
minimal structural assumptions for
ensuring that an ungraded notion of bisimulation can be seen as the “collapse”
of a graded notion of bisimulation, i.e. that the graded bisimulation is a
refinement of the ungraded one.

In order to substantiate the canonicity of our construction, we prove
an abstract soundness theorem that relates the graded and ungraded
notions of coalgebra. We also show that, in a way we make precise in

Theorem

5.2
, these “glued coalgebras” are the universal way
of combining the notions of graded and ungraded bisimulations. Then,
prove
Theorem

5.4
, which means that such coalgebras form a
section-retraction pair of graded and ungraded coalgebras. We conclude
by characterizing the final coalgebra in the glued model.

Our work builds on the line of work initiated by Hermida and
Jacobs
[
11
]
, who showed that fibrations
provide rich structures for defining abstract notions of
bisimulation as lifting functors up fibrations.
As a general framework, we adopt a standard setting
based on fibrations and fibred adjunctions,
which we call an
adjoint gluing doctrine
.
This axiomatization
provide a common language for explaining traditional notions of bisimulations
purely coalgebraically and generalizing them beyond binary relations to quantitative structures, such as
pseudometric.
We illustrate that our framework instantiates to
a range of examples,
including
simulations on deterministic automata with rewards (DAwR), which is a new notion as far as the authors know,
and existing ones such as
amortised bisimulations and approximate bisimulations
[
7
,
8
]
.

Related work

As discussed above,
there has been extensive research on quantitative refinements of classical bisimulation relations,
and many coalgebraic frameworks have been proposed to capture such refinements.
For probabilistic systems,
several coalgebraic and fibrational accounts of behavioural metrics have been developed
[
3
,
24
]
.
Another line of work concerns

Graded semantics

[
21
,
9
]
,
which has been proposed to organise behavioural equivalences along the linear-time and branching-time spectrum
[
27
]
.
Although our notion of graded bisimulation shares the idea of grading,
the usage is different:
while grades in graded semantics usually represent the depth of transitions,
grades in our graded bisimulation represent tolerances on the differences of rewards.
In this paper, we focus on reward-sensitive bisimulations,
which differ from the above quantitative refinements.
A closely related development is the recent coalgebraic framework for
amortised analysis

by Grodin and Harper
[
10
]
.
Their work mainly focuses on generalizing
potential functions
,
rather than on bisimulations. A point of convergence between their work
and the notions of bisimulation considered here is their use of 2-dimensional
data to bound the cost between two different systems. They encoded this as
categories enriched in partial orders while we adopt an even finer grained
approach by making the relative cost be a part of the data.

Organization

We start by studying a concrete example based on deterministic automata
with rewards,
showing how it naturally gives rise to a graded notion of simulations
that
bound
the relative reward between states.
We then formulate DAwR and their simulations coalgebraically.

Motivated by the categorical structures that emerge from this coalgebraic treatment, we
introduce an adjoint gluing doctrine, as a general framework capturing the interactions between graded and ungraded (bi)simulations.
Once we set this up, we show how the two settings can be glued together,
and
we establish a general soundness theorem
relating the graded (bi)simulation with
its ungraded variant
within the glued setting.

Finally, we illustrate the generality of our framework by showing that it encompasses several useful examples.

2
Motivating example: simulations on deterministic automata with rewards

We start with a motivating example, namely simulations
for deterministic automata and their reward-sensitive extensions.

A
deterministic automaton (or DA)
is a tuple
(S,\Sigma,\delta,\alpha)
consisting of
a set of states
S
, an input alphabet
\Sigma
, a transition function
\delta\colon S\to S^{\Sigma}
,
and a set of accepting states
\alpha\subseteq S
.
It is well known that DAs admit a notion of simulations that characterises language inclusion.

Definition 1

An
(ungraded) simulation for a DA
(S,\Sigma,\delta,\alpha)
is a binary relation
R\subseteq S\times S
such that for every
(s,s^{\prime})\in R
:
s\in\alpha
implies
s^{\prime}\in\alpha
and
for every
a\in\Sigma
,
(\delta(s,a),\delta(s^{\prime},a))\in R
. The
similarity
is defined as the largest simulation.

Proposition 1

The similarity is the set of pairs such that every word accepted from the first is also accepted from the second.
Hence,
if
R
is a simulation on a DA,
then every
(s,s^{\prime})\in R
satisfies this language inclusion property.

We then move on to a reward-sensitive setting.
A
deterministic automaton with rewards (or DAwR)
is a deterministic automaton equipped with a reward function
\text{rew}\colon S\to\mathbb{N}^{\Sigma}
.
The acceptance of words is defined as in DAs.
For a state
s\in S
and a word
w\in\Sigma^{*}
, the
accumulated reward

\text{rew}(s,w)
is defined as the natural extension of the reward function by

\text{rew}(s,\epsilon)=0
and
\text{rew}(s,aw)=\text{rew}(s,a)+\text{rew}(\delta(s,a),w)
for each
a\in\Sigma
and
w\in\Sigma^{*}
.
A DAwR with an initial state
s
thus induces a partial function
\text{rew}(s,-)\colon\Sigma^{*}\rightharpoonup\mathbb{N}
that assigns to each accepted word its accumulated reward and is undefined for words outside the language.
We adopt this partial function as the semantic basis for defining simulations between DAwRs.

These kinds of automata has been studied in the classical literature
[
15
,
28
]
.
It is closely related to
deterministic weighted automata
over
(\mathbb{N},+,0)
.
A key difference lies in the presence of the acceptance condition:
a DAwR assigns rewards only to accepted words, while a weighted automaton assigns rewards to all words regardless of acceptance.

For DAwRs, we extend the classical notion of simulation so as to
capture quantitative bounds on relative rewards
between similar states.
In this setting, we do not require that rewards are preserved exactly.
Instead, for similar states
s
and
s^{\prime}
,
we require that every word
w
accepted from
s
is also accepted from
s^{\prime}
,
and
moreover,

the difference in accumulated rewards never exceeds a bound
n
along any partial run.
This motivates the introduction of a grading by natural numbers, where the index
n
indicates the maximal tolerated reward difference.

Definition 2

A
graded simulation on a DAwR
(S,\Sigma,\delta,\text{rew},\alpha)
is a family of binary relations
\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}=\{\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
over
S
such that if
(s,s^{\prime})\in\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
:

•

s\in\alpha
implies
s^{\prime}\in\alpha
,

•

for every
a\in\Sigma
,
\text{rew}(s,a)\leq n+\text{rew}(s^{\prime},a)
,

•

for every
a\in\Sigma
,
(\delta(s,a),\delta(s^{\prime},a))\in\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{n-\text{rew}(s,a)+\text{rew}(s^{\prime},a)}
.

The
graded similarity
is defined as the largest simulation.

It is easy to see that this notion of graded simulation is sound with respect to language inclusion with rewards:

Proposition 2

The graded similarity of a DAwR is the graded binary relation
\{\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
collecting all pairs
(s,s^{\prime})
such that

(\star)
: every word
w
accepted from
s
is also accepted from
s^{\prime}

and
\text{rew}(s,w^{\prime})\leq n+\text{rew}(s^{\prime},w^{\prime})
for each prefix
w^{\prime}
of
w
.

Hence,
if
\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}
is a graded simulation on a DAwR,
then

(s,s^{\prime})\in\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
implies that it satisfies the condition
(\star)
.

A graded simulation on a DAwR induces an ungraded simulation on the underlying DA by taking the union of all relations in the family.

Proposition 3

A graded simulation
\{\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\}_{n}
on a DAwR induces a simulation
\bigcup_{n}\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
on the underlying DA.

In
Section

5
, we use comma objects to give a more conceptual framing of this property.

Observing that the indices of a graded simulation behave like tolerances, it is natural to impose the following additivity conditions on a graded simulation:

1.

For each
s\in S
,
(s,s)\in\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
,

2.

For each
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\{(x,y)\mid\exists z.~(x,z)\in\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\text{ and }(z,y)\in\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{m}\}\subseteq\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{n+m}
.

The first condition reflects that the reward difference between a state and itself is always
0
,
and the second condition reflects
that if the reward difference from
x
to
z
is at most
n
, and from
z
to
y
is at most
m
, then the reward difference from
x
to
y
is at most
n+m
.
A graded simulation satisfying these condition is called
lax monoidal
.
In fact, from a categorical perspective, these conditions mean that
\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}
is a lax monoidal functor
from the monoidal category
(\mathbb{N},0,+)
to the monoidal category of endorelations with
relation compositions as the monoidal product.
We will introduce fibrational frameworks for these ungraded and (lax monoidal) graded simulations in
Section

4
.

Background: coalgebraic framework for simulations

We briefly recall a coalgebraic account of (bi)simulations, which generalizes the classical definitions for transition systems.
In the coalgebraic framework, a transition system is modelled as a coalgebra
c\colon S\to FS
of an endofunctor
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
.
To capture relational reasoning, one often employs a fibration
p\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{C}
whose total category
\mathcal{E}
is a category of relations over objects in
\mathcal{C}
.
This perspective follows the established framework of fibrational approaches to coalgebraic (bi)simulations,
see e.g.
[
11
,
24
]
.
For details of fibrations in general,
we refer the reader to Chapters 1 and 9 of
[
17
]
.
In this paper, we focus in particular on a class of
posetal fibrations known as
\mathbf{CLat}_{\sqcap}
-fibrations, which are a special case of topological functors
[
14
]
.

Definition 3
(
\mathbf{CLat}_{\sqcap}
-fibration)

A
\mathbf{CLat}_{\sqcap}
-fibration
is a fibration
p\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{C}

such that each fibre
\mathcal{E}_{X}
is a complete lattice and for each morphism

f\colon X\to Y
in
\mathcal{C}
, the reindexing functor
f^{*}\colon\mathcal{E}_{Y}\to\mathcal{E}_{X}

preserves all meets.

We use
\sqsubseteq
for the order in each fibre,
and
\leq
for standard orders, such as the usual order on natural numbers
or the order on booleans
2\coloneqq\{\mathrm{False},\mathrm{True}\}
satisfying
\mathrm{False}\leq\mathrm{True}
and
\mathrm{True}\not\leq\mathrm{False}
.
It is known that a
\mathbf{CLat}_{\sqcap}
-fibration is faithful so that
for each
P,Q\in\mathcal{E}
and
f\colon pP\to pQ
, there is at most one morphism
P\to Q
in
\mathcal{E}
above
f
.

Therefore, we often write
f\colon P\mathrel{\overset{\scalebox{0.4}{\ $\bullet$}}{\rightarrow}}Q
for the existence of a morphism
P\to Q
above
f\colon pP\to pQ
, which is equivalent to
P\sqsubseteq f^{*}Q
in
\mathcal{E}_{pP}
.

Definition 4

Let
p\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{C}
and
q\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{D}
be fibrations.
Let
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
and
\hat{F}\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}
be functors. We say that
\hat{F}
is a
lifting of
F
along
p
and
q
if the equality
q\circ\hat{F}=F\circ p
holds.
If moreover
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{F}
and
p=q
,
we simply say that
\hat{F}
is a
lifting of
F
along
p
.
A functor
\hat{F}\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}
is said to be
fibred
if
it is a lifting of some
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
and it preserves cartesian morphisms.

Given a
\mathbf{CLat}_{\sqcap}
-fibration
p\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{C}
, we fix a lifting
\hat{F}\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
of
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
along
p
.
A coalgebra
c\colon S\to FS
in
\mathcal{C}
models a transition system,
and
a coalgebra
c\colon R\mathrel{\overset{\scalebox{0.4}{\ $\bullet$}}{\rightarrow}}\hat{F}R
(or such
R
)
is called a
simulation
on the coalgebra
c\colon S\to FS
.
In general,
the lifting
\hat{F}
is often instantiated by specific constructions suited for simulations, such as relation liftings or codensity liftings
[
20
,
24
]
.
However, since the present paper does not rely on particular structures of liftings,
we place no such restriction and work with arbitrary liftings.

Since the fibre
\mathcal{E}_{S}
is a complete lattice and the endofunction
c^{*}\hat{F}
on this fibre is monotone,
the Knaster–Tarski theorem
[
26
]
guarantees
the existence of the greatest fixed point
\nu(c^{*}\hat{F})
.
The fixed point represents the largest simulation on
c
,
which is called the
similarity
.
We often refer to simulations and similarities as
bisimulations
and
bisimilarities

when they are symmetric in an appropriate sense
depending on the context.

Example 1
((Ungraded) simulations on DAs)

Let

(S,\Sigma,\delta,\alpha)
be a deterministic automaton (DA).
It can be represented as a coalgebra
c\coloneqq\langle\alpha,\delta\rangle\colon S\to 2\times S^{\Sigma}
of the functor
F_{\text{DA}}\coloneqq 2\times(-)^{\Sigma}\colon\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Set}
.

Consider the category
\mathbf{Rel}

whose objects are endorelations
(X,R\subseteq X\times X)
and morphisms are relation preserving functions, i.e.

are
functions

satisfying
(x_{1},x_{2})\in R

.
We often simply write

for

The forgetful functor

is

\mathbf{CLat}_{\sqcap}
-fibration: the fibre
\mathbf{Rel}_{X}
over a set

We define a lifting

With this setup,

on a DA

(see

For an endofunctor

4
Coalgebraic framework for graded simulations

In this section we provide a canonical way of coalgebraically formulating
the graded notion of simulation presented in the previous section. We do this
by refining an “ungraded” notion of coalgebraic simulation. In the broader
context of this paper, the goal of this section is to explore the categorical
structures that naturally emerge when making ungraded and graded notions of
relations interact. Then, in the next section, we use such structures as a
starting point for defining more general notions of graded and ungraded simulations.

We start by stating some definitions and results about fibred limits. These can
very naturally capture the kinds of relations we are interested in.
Then, we provide
a coalgebraic proof of
Proposition

2

5
Refinements Through Glued Fibrations

The previous section shows that there is plenty of
categorical structure present in such graded refinements
of notions of simulation. In this section we abstract
away from the DAwR example and show that we can prove
a generalization of
Proposition

4
by using categorical
gluing, a technique coming from categorical logic
and programming language theory.

We begin by defining the adjoint gluing doctrine, a fibred adjunction that
generalizes
Example

2
, providing an abstract framework for
representing graded/ungraded notions of simulation. An important
insight from our work is that neither the ungraded nor the graded
notions of simulation are the “ground truth”. Instead, they should
be thought of as different analyses that serve different purposes.

In order to unify these analyses we combine both fibrations into a single
one by using a fibred generalization of the comma category, often used in
proofs by logical relations in categorical logic and programming language theory.

This comma construction allows for a fairly conceptual and simple proof of a
generalization of
Proposition

4
. Finally, in order to substantiate the
claim that this glued model is a natural setting to do coalgebra in, we
study some of its structural properties such as final coalgebras and comma object
preservation.

5.1

5.3
Final coalgebra

Final coalgebras provide useful coinductive reasoning principles and
are widely used in coalgebraic automata theory. When it
comes to fibred bisimulation, the final coalgebra provides a useful
way of characterizing which invariants are kept track of by
bisimulation relations. For example, in the DFA example, the final
coalgebra has languages over the alphabet as its underlying set and
equality is the relational component, i.e. bisimulation is
equivalent to language equality.

In some cases, we can explicitly characterize the final coalgebra. A
useful result due to Adámek

6
Examples

In this section we present a few examples that illustrate the range of
adjoint gluing doctrine as an abstraction. We begin by presenting a first example
based on reward-sensitive notions of simulations on labelled transition systems with rewards. Then,
we present a reward-sensitive variant of simulations on Markov Decision
Processes (MDPs), and then we move on to a pseudometric and
relation-based bisimulations on labelled Markov processes.

6.1
Amortised bisimulations on labelled transition systems

Kiehn and Arun-Kumar
[
18
]
have introduced

amortised bisimulations
for labelled transition systems
where certain actions have a reward
associated with it.
Their definition is remarkably similar to our notion of graded
simulation for DAwR.
Here, we slightly generalize their setting
by allowing rewards to be defined locally for each transition,
rather than globally for each action as in their automata class,
which
also refines the
usual notion of LTS bisimulation.

Let
\mathcal{P}\colon\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Set}
be the covariant powerset functor.
Consider a labelled transition system with rewards (LTS)

\delta\colon S\to\mathcal{P}(S\times\mathbb{N})^{\Sigma}
,
where
\Sigma
is a set of labels and
(s^{\prime},n)\in\delta(s)(a)
means a possible transition

s\xrightarrow{a}s^{\prime}
with a reward
n
.
We write
F_{\mathrm{LTS}}
for the functor
\mathcal{P}(-\times\mathbb{N})^{\Sigma}\colon\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Set}
.

For liftings of
\mathbf{CLat}_{\sqcap}
-fibrations, we only provide
the action of functors on objects because any
\mathbf{CLat}_{\sqcap}
-fibration is faithful.

Ungraded bisimulations on LTSs

Define a lifting
\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LTS}}\colon\mathbf{Rel}\to\mathbf{Rel}
of
F_{\mathrm{LTS}}
along
p\colon\mathbf{Rel}\to\mathbf{Set}

by

\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LTS}}(R\subseteq X\times X)\coloneqq\left\{(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\ \middle|\ \begin{array}[]{l}\forall i\in\{0,1\}.~\forall a\in\Sigma.~\forall(x_{i},r_{i})\in\tau_{i}(a).\\
\quad\exists(x_{1-i},r_{1-i})\in\tau_{1-i}(a).~(x_{0},x_{1})\in R\end{array}\right\}.

A coalgebra
\delta\colon R\mathrel{\overset{\scalebox{0.4}{\ $\bullet$}}{\rightarrow}}\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LTS}}(R)

is an ungraded bisimulation on the LTS
\delta
,
which is a standard bisimulation relation on the underlying system
(\mathcal{P}\pi)^{\Sigma}\circ\delta
where
\pi\colon S\times\mathbb{N}\to S
is the projection.

Graded bisimulations on LTSs

Using the fibration
\mathbb{N}
-graded
\Delta^{*}p^{{\mathbb{N}}}\colon\Delta^{*}\mathbf{Rel}^{{\mathbb{N}}}\to\mathbf{Set}
introduced in
Example

2
,
we define a lifting
\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LTS}}^{\mathbb{N}}\colon\Delta^{*}\mathbf{Rel}^{{\mathbb{N}}}\to\Delta^{*}\mathbf{Rel}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
of
F_{\mathrm{LTS}}
. Its actions on objects
\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LTS}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathrel{\mathcal{A}})_{n}
is
defined as.

\displaystyle\left\{(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\ \middle|\ \begin{array}[]{l}\forall i\in\{0,1\}.~\forall a\in\Sigma.~\forall(x_{i},r_{i})\in\tau_{i}(a).~\exists(x_{1-i},r_{1-i})\in\tau_{1-i}(a).\\
\quad\big(r_{0}\leq n+r_{1}\text{ and }(x_{0},x_{1})\in\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{n-r_{0}+r_{1}}\big)\end{array}\right\}.

A coalgebra
\delta\colon\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}\mathrel{\overset{\scalebox{0.4}{\ $\bullet$}}{\rightarrow}}\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LTS}}^{\mathbb{N}}(\mathrel{\mathcal{A}})
is a graded bisimulation on the LTS, known as a amortised bisimulation
[
18
]
.
It is a reward-sensitive refinement of bisimulations given as
\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LTS}}
-coalgebras.

Adjoint gluing doctrine

The structures introduced above, together with the appropriate fibred adjunction (

Note that
there exists a canonical natural transformation

because
\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LTS}}=(\exists g.-)\circ\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LTS}}^{\mathbb{N}}\circ\Delta

Hence,
Theorem

5.3
yields the following result.

For an LTS

if
\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}

6.2
Probabilistic simulations and their reward-sensitive variant

Let

{\mathcal{D}_{\leq 1}}
be the subdistribution monad over
\mathbf{Set}
.
Consider a Markov Decision Process

\langle\delta,\text{rew}\rangle\colon S\to({\mathcal{D}_{\leq 1}}S\times{\mathbb{R}^{+}})^{\Sigma}
,
where

\mathbb{R}^{+}
is the set of positive real numbers,

\delta\colon S\to({\mathcal{D}_{\leq 1}}S)^{\Sigma}
describes probabilistic transitions and
\text{rew}\colon S\to(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{\Sigma}
assigns an expected reward for each transition.
We write
F_{\mathrm{MDP}}
for the functor
({\mathcal{D}_{\leq 1}}(-)\times{\mathbb{R}^{+}})^{\Sigma}\colon\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Set}
.

Ungraded simulations on MDPs

We first introduce simulations that ignore rewards,
capturing only the probabilistic behaviour of the underlying MDP
\delta
.
To capture probabilistic simulations,
we lift
F_{\mathrm{MDP}}
along the fibration
p\colon\mathbf{Rel}\to\mathbf{Set}
.
For a relation
R\subseteq I\times I
and a subset
X\subseteq I
,
We write
R(X)
for the set
\{x^{\prime}\mid\exists x\in X.~(x,x^{\prime})\in R\}
,
and for
f\in{\mathcal{D}_{\leq 1}}(S)
and
X\subseteq S
,
we write
f(X)
for
\sum_{x\in X}f(x)
.
Define a lifting
\hat{F}_{\mathrm{MDP}}\colon\mathbf{Rel}\to\mathbf{Rel}
of
F_{\mathrm{MDP}}
along
p

by

\hat{F}_{\mathrm{MDP}}(R\subseteq I\times I)=\{(f,g)\mid\forall a\in\Sigma.~\forall X\subseteq I.~(\pi_{1}f(a))(X)\leq(\pi_{1}g(a))(R(X))\}.

A coalgebra
\langle\delta,\text{rew}\rangle\colon R\mathrel{\overset{\scalebox{0.4}{\ $\bullet$}}{\rightarrow}}\hat{F}_{\mathrm{MDP}}(R)

is an ungraded simulation on the MDP, which
coincides with
0
-simulations on labelled markov processes
[
7
]
.

Graded simulations on MDPs

To account for rewards, we introduce a graded extension of the above lifting.
We do this by instantiating the fibration introduced in
Example

2
with

\mathbb{R}^{+}
as our grading monoid, resulting in a fibration
\Delta^{*}p^{{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}\colon\Delta^{*}\mathbf{Rel}^{{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}\to\mathbf{Set}
.
We define a lifting
\hat{F}_{\mathrm{MDP}}^{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\colon\Delta^{*}\mathbf{Rel}^{{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}\to\Delta^{*}\mathbf{Rel}^{{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}

of
F_{\mathrm{MDP}}
. Its action on objects
\hat{F}_{\mathrm{MDP}}^{\mathbb{R}^{+}}(\mathrel{\mathcal{A}})_{r}
is.

\displaystyle\left\{(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\ \middle|\ \begin{array}[]{l}\forall a\in\Sigma.~\forall X\subseteq I.~\big(r_{0}\leq r+r_{1}\text{ and }(\tau_{0}(X)\leq\tau_{1}(\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{r-r_{0}+r_{1}}(X))\big)\\
\quad\text{where }(\tau_{i},r_{i})=\tau_{i}(a)\text{ for each }i\in\{0,1\}.\end{array}\right\}

There the grade
r
represents the admissible reward difference between corresponding transitions.
A coalgebra
\langle\delta,\text{rew}\rangle\colon\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}\mathrel{\overset{\scalebox{0.4}{\ $\bullet$}}{\rightarrow}}\hat{F}_{\mathrm{MDP}}^{\mathbb{R}^{+}}(\mathrel{\mathcal{A}})
is a graded simulation on the MDP, which can be regarded as a reward-sensitive refinement of simulations given as
\hat{F}_{\mathrm{MDP}}
-coalgebras.

Adjoint gluing doctrine.

The structures introduced above, together with the fibred adjunction (
1
) for this setting, form an adjoint gluing doctrine.
Note that
there exists a canonical natural transformation

\exists g.-\circ\hat{F}_{\mathrm{MDP}}^{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\Rightarrow\hat{F}_{\mathrm{MDP}}\circ\exists g.-

because
\hat{F}_{\mathrm{MDP}}=(\exists g.-)\circ\hat{F}_{\mathrm{MDP}}^{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\circ\Delta
holds.
Hence,
Theorem

5.3
implies the following.

Proposition 7

For an MDP
\langle\delta,\text{rew}\rangle\colon S\to({\mathcal{D}_{\leq 1}}S\times{\mathbb{R}^{+}})^{\Sigma}
,
if
\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}
is a graded simulation on
\langle\delta,\text{rew}\rangle
then
\exists g.\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}
is a simulation on the underlying MDP
\delta
.

6.3
Pseudometric and relational approximate notions of bisimulations

We next turn to approximate notions of bisimulations for labelled Markov processes (LMPs), which can be represented as
coalgebras
\delta\colon X\to({\mathcal{D}_{\leq 1}}X)^{\Sigma}
,
where
{\mathcal{D}_{\leq 1}}
is the subdistribution monad and
\Sigma
is a set of labels.
We write
F_{\mathrm{LMP}}
for the functor
({\mathcal{D}_{\leq 1}}(-))^{\Sigma}\colon\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Set}
.

In recent work, Desharnais and Sokolova
[
8
]

investigated how the
Lévy-Prokhorov pseudometrics

[
22
]
characterises
\epsilon
-bisimulations, relating the metric and relational viewpoints.
We now show how this is modeled by our framework.

Throughout this subsection,
we employ the unit interval
([0,1],\oplus,0)
,
where
\oplus
is truncated addition, i.e.,
a\oplus b=\min(1,a+b)
,
as a grading quantale.
We note, however, that our framework (excluding results
Theorem

6.1
and
Proposition

8
) can be applied to any quantale.

(Pseudometric-based) ungraded bisimulations on LMPs

We define
\mathbf{PMet}_{1}
to be the category of
1
-bounded pseudometric spaces.
Its objects are pairs of a set
X
and a function
d\colon X\times X\to[0,1]

such that
d
satisfies
d(x,x)=0
,
d(x,z)\leq d(x,y)+d(y,z)
, and
d(x,y)=d(y,x)
for each
x,y,z\in X
.
A morphism
f\colon(X,d_{X})\to(Y,d_{Y})
is a
1
-Lipschitz function, i.e. a function
f\colon X\to Y
such that
d_{Y}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})
for each
x,x^{\prime}\in X
.
The forgetful functor
\mathbf{PMet}_{1}\to\mathbf{Set}
is a
\mathbf{CLat}_{\sqcap}
-fibration
[
3
]
.

Define
\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LMP}}\colon\mathbf{PMet}_{1}\to\mathbf{PMet}_{1}
of
F_{\mathrm{LMP}}
along the fibration by

\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LMP}}(d)(f_{0},f_{1})\coloneqq\inf\{r\mid\forall a\in\Sigma.~\forall X\subseteq S.~f_{i}(a)(X)\leq f_{1-i}(a)(X_{r})+r,i=0,1\},

where
X_{r}=\{y\mid\exists x\in X.~d(x,y)\leq r\}
.
It coincides with the Lévy-Prokhorov lifting
[
22
,
8
]
,
except that we use closed balls instead of open ones.
This change allows the lifting
\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LMP}}
to be expressed as a composition
L\circ\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LMP}}^{[0,1]}\circ R
, where
L\dashv R
is an adjunction and
\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LMP}}^{[0,1]}
is a lifting for graded relations defined below.

A coalgebra
\delta\colon(S,d)\mathrel{\overset{\scalebox{0.4}{\ $\bullet$}}{\rightarrow}}\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LMP}}(S,d)
is an ungraded bisimulation on the LMP.

(Relation-based) graded bisimulations on LMPs

In the same way as
Example

3
with letting
I\coloneqq([0,1],\oplus,0)
,
we obtain the lax monoidal
[0,1]
-graded fibrations
\overline{\Delta^{*}\mathbf{Rel}^{[0,1]}}\to\mathbf{Set}
of
p\colon\mathbf{Rel}\to\mathbf{Set}
(see
Definition

6
).

We write
\mathbf{IRel}
for
the total category
\overline{\Delta^{*}\mathbf{Rel}^{[0,1]}}
,
whose objects
are
([0,1],\leq)
-graded binary relations
\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}
on a set
X
such that

•

For every
x\in X
,
x\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{0}x
, and

•

If
x_{1}\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{r}x_{2}
and
x_{2}\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{r^{\prime}}x_{3}
then

x_{1}\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{r\oplus r^{\prime}}x_{3}
.

A morphism between such relations is a function between their underlying
sets such that they laxly preserve the relation.

We define a lifting

\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LMP}}^{[0,1]}\colon\mathbf{IRel}\to\mathbf{IRel}
of
F_{\mathrm{LMP}}
by specifying its action on
objects
\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LMP}}^{[0,1]}(\mathrel{\mathcal{A}})_{r}
as

\{(f_{0},f_{1})\mid\forall a\in\Sigma.~\forall X\subseteq S.~f_{i}(a)(X)\leq f_{1-i}(a)(\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{r}(X))+r,i=0,1\}.

A coalgebra
\delta\colon\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}\mathrel{\overset{\scalebox{0.4}{\ $\bullet$}}{\rightarrow}}\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LMP}}^{[0,1]}(\mathrel{\mathcal{A}})
is a graded bisimulation on the LMP.
A component
\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}_{\epsilon}
of a graded bisimulation
\mathrel{\mathcal{A}}

coincides with the so-called
\epsilon
-bisimulation introduced in
[
7
]
.

Adjoint gluing doctrine

The following structures form an adjoint gluing doctrine.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{PMet}_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{IRel}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{Set}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{F}_{\mathrm{LMP}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}

where in the fibred adjunction

the right adjoint uses the closed balls indexed by their radius

. Note that in the definition of

due to the order on distances

There exists a canonical natural transformation

Note that the Lévy-Prokhorov lifting (using open balls) also fits into our framework.
By

If

If

7
Conclusion

In this paper we have introduced a framework for axiomatizing the interaction
between graded and ungraded notions of bisimulation. In order to be able to
treat such situations coalgebraically, we have proposed the application of
categorical gluing to coalgebraic automata theory. This technique
allows for the canonical construction of coalgebraic models that account for
the coherent integration of these different notions of bisimulation. We corroborates
the naturality of this model by using it to prove an abstract soundness theorem,
as well as showing that is had nice coalgebraic properties and
has interesting concrete examples.
