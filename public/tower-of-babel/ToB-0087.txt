## 📖 OVERVIEW

**Full Title:** Heptameron, or Magical Elements (also known as The Fourth Book of Occult Philosophy)
**Author:** Attributed to Peter de Abano (c.1250–1315), Italian philosopher and physician
**Date:** Earliest known print c.1657; this edition transcribed from the 1657 edition
**Pages:** ~100
**Language:** Latin / English
**Category:** Grimoire / Ceremonial Magic / Planetary Magic

---

## 📚 CONTENTS

### The Seven Days / Planetary Hours

The Heptameron organizes magic around the seven days of the week, each governed by a planetary intelligence:

- **Sunday** — Sol (The Sun) — Divine and angelic operations
- **Monday** — Luna (The Moon) — Lunar magic, water, dreams
- **Tuesday** — Mars — War, courage, destruction
- **Wednesday** — Mercury — Wisdom, travel, commerce
- **Thursday** — Jupiter — Expansion, luck, law
- **Friday** — Venus — Love, beauty, pleasure
- **Saturday** — Saturn — Limitation, binding, death

### Ritual Structure

- Conjuring circles and their construction
- Consecration of magical tools
- Planetary hour calculations
- Names and seals of the angels/demons of each day
- The nature and powers of each spirit

---

## 📌 HISTORICAL NOTES

**Attribution:** Peter de Abano (c.1250–1315) was an Italian philosopher and physician who was actually accused of heresy twice. The Heptameron was attributed to him but likely dates from much later.

**Companion Texts:** Often bound together with Agrippa's Fourth Book of Occult Philosophy and the Arbatel of Magic. The full "Fourth Book" collection is available at archive.org/details/cu31924006718757
