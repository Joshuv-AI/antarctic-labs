Impact of TACE combined with targeted and immune therapy on liver function and prognosis in unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma patients: the significance of baseline albumin-bilirubin grade
Authors: Yiwan Guo, Xiaocui Liu, Bo Sun, Fan Pu, Xin Li, Chuansheng Zheng, Xuefeng Kan, Fan Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:Transarterial chemoembolization combined with tyrosine kinase inhibitors and immune checkpoint inhibitors (TTI) has emerged as a promising strategy for unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (uHCC); however, its impact on liver function and the prognostic role of baseline albumin–bilirubin (ALBI) grade remain insufficiently defined.Objectives:To compare the effects of combined therapy and transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) alone on liver function in uHCC patients, and to evaluate the prognostic value of baseline ALBI grade.Design:This retrospective study enrolled uHCC patients treated with TTI or TACE alone between January 2018 and December 2023.Methods:The primary outcome was the time to progression of baseline ALBI grade, with secondary outcomes including overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) of patients. Survival outcomes were analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method.Results:A total of 162 patients (TTI group: 68; TACE alone group: 94) were included. The TTI group showed a significantly longer median time to progression of baseline ALBI grade than the TACE alone group (19.0 months vs 11.6 months, p = 0.004). Subgroup analyses revealed similar benefits of the TTI group (11.3 months vs 5.0 months for baseline ALBI grade 1, p = 0.015; 20.0 months vs 14.7 months for baseline ALBI grade 2, p = 0.025). Patients in the TTI group demonstrated superior median OS (42.2 months vs 22.0 months for baseline ALBI grade 1, p = 0.077; 22.6 months vs 13.5 months for baseline ALBI grade 2, p = 0.001) and longer median PFS (11.7 months vs 6.8 months for baseline ALBI grade 1, p = 0.148; 8.4 months vs 4.9 months for baseline ALBI grade 2, p = 0.022) compared to the TACE alone group.Conclusion:TTI therapy preserved liver function for a longer duration and achieved better survival outcomes than TACE alone in uHCC patients, particularly benefiting those with baseline ALBI grade 2.Keywords: albumin-bilirubin grade, hepatocellular carcinoma, immune checkpoint inhibitors, liver function, transarterial chemoembolization, tyrosine kinase inhibitors

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the sixth most common malignant tumor and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. 1 Since the onset of HCC is asymptomatic, most patients are initially diagnosed at the intermediate or advanced stage, consequently losing the opportunity for curative treatments. 2

Current clinical guidelines recommend transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) as a therapeutic approach for intermediate and advanced HCC.3,4 However, it is difficult to achieve complete tumor necrosis with TACE alone. 5 In addition, TACE can induce a hypoxic tumor microenvironment following embolization, which may upregulate the expression of programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), potentially driving tumor recurrence and metastasis.6,7

In recent years, some studies have explored the efficacy of combining TACE with systemic therapy for patients with advanced HCC and obtained promising results. CHANCE001 study suggested that TACE combined with PD-L1 blockade and molecular targeted therapy could significantly improve overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), and objective response rate (ORR) versus TACE alone for Chinese patients with mainly advanced HCC. 8 In addition, the CHANCE2201 study demonstrated that advanced HCC patients receiving TACE combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) and anti-VEGF antibody/tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) showed better OS, PFS, and ORR than those receiving ICIs and anti-VEGF antibody/TKIs alone. 9

Liver function has been regarded as a critical determinant for the prognosis of patients with HCC. 10 In clinical practice, Child-Pugh (CP) classification is commonly used to assess liver function. However, the accuracy of this method can be influenced by some subjective variables, including ascites and hepatic encephalopathy. 11 The albumin-bilirubin (ALBI) grade is a method that uses only serum bilirubin and albumin levels to evaluate liver function. It has demonstrated comparable or superior performance to the CP classification in assessing liver function.12,13 In addition, baseline ALBI grade has been identified as an independent predictor of survival outcomes in HCC patients treated with sorafenib or pembrolizumab.14,15 Several previous studies have also used ALBI grade changes as a dynamic marker to evaluate hepatic functional deterioration and treatment-related liver impairment in HCC patients.15,16 In this context, time to baseline ALBI grade progression may represent a clinically meaningful endpoint reflecting the duration of maintained hepatic functional reserve during treatment.

To our knowledge, there are currently few studies directly investigating the impact of TACE combined with TKI and ICI (TTI) versus TACE alone on liver function of unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (uHCC) patients. Therefore, the aim of the present study was to evaluate the impact of TTI therapy on liver function in uHCC patients compared with TACE alone, and to assess the prognostic value of baseline ALBI grade in uHCC patients.

Methods

Study design and patients

This retrospective study received approval from the Ethics Committee of Union Hospital, Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology (protocol code: 2025-0191), and the requirement for written informed consent was waived. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The reporting of this study conformed to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guideline 17 (Supplemental File 1).

Between January 2018 and December 2023, the medical records of uHCC patients were reviewed. HCC was diagnosed pathologically or clinically according to the European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL) guidelines. 3 Treatment decisions were made by a multidisciplinary team based on clinical practice. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) 18–75 years of age; (2) patients with HCC confirmed by histopathological or clinical findings and who were not eligible for resection; (3) Child-Pugh A or B; (4) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) scores ⩽1; (5) receiving TTI therapy or TACE alone. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) prior treatments for HCC, such as liver resection, systemic therapy, local ablation therapy, or hepatic arterial infusion; (2) combined with other malignancies; (3) Child-Pugh C; (4) incomplete clinical or imaging data.

Treatment protocol

TACE was performed using a conventional TACE (c-TACE) protocol or a drug-eluting beads TACE (d-TACE) protocol, which was carried out before systemic therapy. The Seldinger technique and angiography were used to locate tumor-feeding arteries and evaluate tumor burden. Depending on this assessment, an emulsion of 5–15 mL containing 10–20 mg of doxorubicin hydrochloride (from Hisun Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd in Zhejiang, China) was mixed with 5–10 mL of lipiodol (Lipiodol Ultrafluido from Guerbet, Villepinte, France) and administered into the tumor-feeding arteries via a 3-F microcatheter. Subsequently, suitable quantities of gelatin sponge particles (350–560 µm from Cook) were injected into these arteries to achieve embolization. For patients undergoing d-TACE, the embolization was performed using drug-eluting beads (Callispheres®; Suzhou, Hengrui Callisyn Biomedical CO., Ltd., Suzhou, China) carrying chemotherapeutic drugs. The procedure was considered complete when blood flow in the tumor-feeding arteries was almost completely stopped.

TKI and ICI therapy was typically initiated approximately 1 week after the TACE procedure, allowing adequate time for recovery from the intervention and clinical reassessment before starting systemic treatment. In this study, lenvatinib (8 or 12 mg), as the TKI protocol, was administered orally daily; while camrelizumab (200 mg), as the ICI protocol, was administered intravenously every 3 weeks until disease progression or unexpected toxicity occurred. The TKI dosage and schedule were modified based on toxicity and disease status. TKI and ICI were stopped if intolerable toxicity arose or no clinical benefits were observed. All patients included in the TTI group successfully initiated systemic therapy after TACE, and no deaths or disease progression events occurred during the interval between TACE and the initiation of systemic therapy.

Treatment exposure data were retrospectively collected from the medical records, including the number of TACE sessions, the interval between TACE sessions, and whether additional locoregional therapies were performed during follow-up. For patients in the TTI group, information on systemic therapy exposure was also collected, including the starting dose of lenvatinib (8 or 12 mg according to body weight), dose modifications (dose reduction or temporary interruption), and the number of camrelizumab cycles administered. This information is summarized in Table S1.

Outcomes and follow-up

The monitoring of tumor response and progression was conducted through contrast-enhanced CT and/or MRI every 2–3 months, following the modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (mRECIST). 18 The laboratory and imaging data were collected at each patient visit. ALBI scores were calculated as follows: (log10 bilirubin [in µmol/L] × 0.66) − 0.085 × albumin (in g/L). According to the ALBI grading system, patients were stratified into three grades: grade 1 (scores ⩽ −2.60), grade 2 (scores −2.60 to −1.39), and grade 3 (scores > −1.39). 12 Two abdominal radiologists with over 10 years of experience independently assessed the imaging data, and they were blinded to the patients’ clinical details. Discrepancies in their assessments were resolved through consensus. TACE was performed if a residual tumor or disease progression was observed during follow-up. The endpoint of the follow-up was June 1, 2024.

Time to progression of baseline ALBI grade, reflecting the duration of preserved hepatic functional reserve, was used as the primary outcome. It was defined as the time from the initial ALBI grade assessment to the first observation of at least one-grade increase during the follow-up. The secondary outcomes included OS and PFS. OS was defined as the time from the first TACE procedure (time zero for both groups) to death or the last follow-up. PFS was defined as the duration between the first TACE procedure and disease progression, death, or the last follow-up.

Safety assessment

Treatment-related adverse events were also recorded and evaluated according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE, version 5.0). 19 Particular attention was given to hepatic decompensation events, including ascites, hepatic encephalopathy, and variceal bleeding, as well as immune-related adverse events.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (Version 25.0, Chicago, IL, USA) and GraphPad Prism (Version 8.0.1, San Diego, CA, USA). Continuous variables were presented as the mean and standard deviation or median and interquartile range and were compared using the t-test or Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages and were compared using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test when appropriate. To further reduce the potential confounding associated with the retrospective study design, univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed to identify factors associated with time to progression of baseline ALBI grade, OS, and PFS. Variables with p < 0.05 in univariable analysis were entered into the multivariable analysis. Hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed using log-minus-log survival plots, and no obvious violations were observed. Kaplan–Meier curves were generated for time to progression of baseline ALBI grade, OS, and PFS, and were compared using the log-rank tests. A two-tailed p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics of enrolled patients

A total of 68 and 94 patients treated with TTI and TACE alone, respectively, were enrolled in this study (Figure 1). The baseline characteristics of these patients (n = 162) were presented in Table 1. There were no significant differences in baseline characteristics between the two groups (all p > 0.05). According to the baseline ALBI scores, 37 patients were stratified as ALBI grade 1 (n = 19 for the TTI group; n = 18 for the TACE alone group), and 125 patients were stratified as ALBI grade 2 (n = 49 for the TTI group; n = 76 for the TACE alone group). Subgroup analysis suggested that no significant differences in these baseline characteristics were observed between the two groups when stratified by baseline ALBI grade (Table 2).

Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses

To further evaluate whether treatment strategy was independently associated with survival outcomes, univariate and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed. After adjusting for potential confounders including age, sex, largest tumor size, tumor number, vascular invasion, biliary obstruction, extrahepatic metastasis, baseline ALBI grade, AFP level, and cirrhosis, TTI therapy remained independently associated with improved time to progression of baseline ALBI grade (HR = 0.41 (95% CI 0.25–0.68); p < 0.001; Table 3), OS (HR = 0.38 (95% CI 0.24–0.60); p < 0.001; Table 4), and PFS (HR = 0.49 (95% CI 0.33–0.71); p < 0.001; Table 5) compared with TACE alone.

Time to progression of baseline ALBI grade

In our study, the median time to progression of baseline ALBI grade was longer in the TTI group than that in the TACE alone group (19.0 (95% CI 15.1–22.9) vs 11.6 (95% CI 9.2–13.9) months, p = 0.004; Figure 2(a)). Further subgroup analysis indicated that, for both baseline ALBI grade 1 and grade 2 patients, those who underwent TTI therapy exhibited a longer time to progression of baseline ALBI grade compared to those receiving TACE alone (11.3 (95% CI 4.8–17.9) vs 5.0 (95% CI 2.0–7.9) months for ALBI grade 1, p = 0.015, Figure 2(b); 20.0 (95% CI 18.3–21.7) vs 14.7 (95% CI 11.8–17.7) months for ALBI grade 2, p = 0.025, Figure 2(c)).

OS and PFS stratified by baseline ALBI grade

Overall, the median OS (mOS) and median PFS (mPFS) in the TTI group were both longer than those in the TACE alone group (23.5 (95% CI 14.7–32.4) vs 13.6 (95% CI 10.5–16.7) months for mOS, p < 0.001; 9.3 (95% CI 6.1–12.6) vs 5.0 (95% CI 3.2–6.9) months for mPFS, p = 0.004). For patients with baseline ALBI grade 1, the mOS was 42.2 (95% CI 6.3–78.1) months in the TTI group, compared to 22.0 (95% CI 1.8–42.1) months in the TACE alone group (p = 0.077, Figure 3(a)). Similarly, the mPFS was longer in the TTI group than in the TACE alone group (11.7 (95% CI 7.9–15.5) vs 6.8 (95% CI 0.0–14.0) months; p = 0.148; Figure 3(b)). For patients with baseline ALBI grade 2, those in the TTI group had significantly longer mOS compared to the TACE alone group (22.6 (95% CI 14.0–31.2) vs 13.5 (95% CI 10.0–16.9) months; p = 0.001; Figure 4(a)). Consistent with the trend observed for mOS, the mPFS was also superior for patients in the TTI group than in the TACE alone group [(8.4 (95% CI 6.9–9.9) vs 4.9 (95% CI 3.9–6.0) months; p = 0.022; Figure 4(b)].

Safety

Adverse events were generally manageable (Table S2). Hepatic decompensation events were uncommon and occurred at similar rates between groups, including ascites (13.2% vs 17.0%), hepatic encephalopathy (2.9% vs 4.3%), and variceal bleeding (1.5% vs 3.2%). Immune-related adverse events were observed only in the TTI group and were mostly mild. The incidence of grade ⩾3 adverse events was low and comparable between the two groups (10.3% vs 9.6%).

Discussion

Our study suggested that the median time to progression of baseline ALBI grade was longer in the TTI group than in the TACE alone group. In addition, the mOS and mPFS were both longer in the TTI group irrespective of baseline ALBI grade. These results indicated that the combination of TACE with TKI and ICI may preserve liver function for a longer duration and achieve better survival outcomes than TACE alone.

TACE has long been considered the standard treatment for intermediate and advanced HCC. However, in clinical practice, HCC patients classified as Child-Pugh C cannot be candidates for TACE according to the guidelines,3,20 highlighting the critical role of baseline liver function in determining treatment options and prognosis. ALBI grading has been used to evaluate the survival outcomes in HCC after liver resection,21,22 radiotherapy,23,24 and systemic treatment,25,26 showing better prognostic efficacy than the CP classification. In recent years, the therapeutic landscape of HCC has evolved rapidly with the development of targeted therapies and ICIs. Emerging evidence suggests that combining TACE with systemic agents may enhance treatment efficacy of uHCC by integrating the local tumor-control effect of TACE with systemic anti-tumor activity.27–29

In the present study, we found that the median time to progression of baseline ALBI grade in the TTI group was longer than that in the TACE alone group. Furthermore, similar results were observed in both the baseline ALBI grade 1 and ALBI grade 2 subgroups (p < 0.05). These results suggested that TTI therapy may better preserve liver function compared to TACE alone. Previous studies suggested that compared with placebo, ramucirumab, or pembrolizumab did not negatively impact liver function and improved OS regardless of baseline ALBI grade.15,26 In addition, a phase III IMbrave150 study found that atezolizumab + bevacizumab preserved liver function for a numerically longer duration than sorafenib. 16 These studies suggested that molecular targeted agents and ICIs may not negatively affect liver function, which may partly explain our study results.

Another key finding in our study was that the mOS and mPFS were superior in the TTI group compared to those in the TACE alone group, irrespective of baseline ALBI grade. This result was consistent with previous studies.8,27–29 However, for patients with baseline ALBI grade 1, although both mOS and mPFS were longer in the combination therapy group, there was no statistically significant difference between the two groups (p = 0.077 for mOS; p = 0.148 for mPFS). Several factors may account for this observation: (1) the sample size for patients with baseline ALBI grade 1 was small in the two groups, which might lead to statistical bias; and (2) patients with ALBI grade 1 had sufficient liver function reserve, and they were tolerable to the side effects caused by the treatments. Notably, the HRs for both OS (HR = 0.38) and PFS (HR = 0.49) still favored the TTI group, suggesting a potentially meaningful clinical benefit despite the lack of statistical significance.

For patients with baseline ALBI grade 2, whose liver function was worse than that of those with baseline ALBI grade 1, our results suggested that the combination therapy significantly improved survival benefits compared to TACE monotherapy, with statistically significant differences observed in both mOS (p = 0.001) and mPFS (p = 0.022). It is known that TACE can induce tumor ischemia and necrosis, and increase the expression of PD-L1 and VEGF due to the hypoxic microenvironment after embolization, leading to tumor recurrence and metastasis.6,7 However, TKIs can inhibit signaling pathways such as VEGF and play a crucial role in both anti-angiogenesis and anti-tumor effects. 30 In addition, ICIs can block the interaction between immune checkpoints and corresponding ligands, helping to overcome the negative regulatory mechanisms imposed by immune checkpoints and promoting an effective anti-tumor response. 31 Therefore, TACE plus TKIs and ICIs may exert a synergistic effect on anti-tumor activity, which could contribute to the improved survival outcomes observed in this study. Safety outcomes further contextualize these efficacy findings. In the present study, treatment-related adverse events were generally manageable, and the incidence of hepatic decompensation events was comparable between the two groups. Importantly, the addition of systemic therapy to TACE was not associated with an apparent increase in severe adverse events or liver-related complications.

Taken together, our study suggested that in addition to improving tumor response, preservation of liver function is a critical therapeutic goal in patients with uHCC. Progressive deterioration of hepatic reserve function often limits the continuation of anticancer therapy and is strongly associated with poor prognosis. Recent experimental studies have suggested that immune-related cytokine signaling pathways may also contribute to hepatic tissue regulation. In particular, the IL-17B-IL-17RB signaling axis has been implicated in liver biology and HCC. Experimental data indicate that IL-17B can inhibit the proliferation of HCC cells through an AKT-dependent signaling mechanism and may influence tumor growth dynamics. 32 Moreover, previous studies have suggested that IL-17RB-mediated signaling may participate in liver regeneration and hepatic tissue homeostasis, 33 indicating a potential link between immune signaling and preservation of liver function. Although the clinical relevance of these pathways in patients receiving combination therapy for HCC remains to be fully elucidated, such immune-inflammatory signaling networks may represent a biologically plausible mechanism contributing to hepatic functional preservation during anticancer treatment.

This study also had several limitations. First, it was a single-center, retrospective study, which may introduce selection biases and unmeasured confounding. Although we performed multivariable Cox regression analyses to adjust for potential baseline confounders, residual bias cannot be completely excluded. Second, due to the retrospective design, some degree of treatment heterogeneity was unavoidable. Different TACE techniques, including c-TACE and drug-eluting bead TACE, were used according to institutional practice and physician preference. In addition, the timing of systemic therapy initiation in the TTI group was not completely uniform, which may have introduced potential confounding effects. Third, the sample size of the ALBI grade 1 subgroup was relatively small, which may have limited the statistical power of subgroup analyses and partly explains why some comparisons did not reach statistical significance. We also did not enroll patients with baseline ALBI grade 3 since the number of cases was too small for analysis. Finally, death may occur before the observation of baseline ALBI grade progression in patients with uHCC. Such cases were treated as censored observations in the Kaplan–Meier analysis, which may introduce potential bias. Future studies with larger cohorts should consider applying competing-risk models, such as the Fine–Gray subdistribution hazard model, to further validate these findings.

Conclusion

Our study suggested that TTI therapy was associated with prolonged preservation of liver function and improved survival outcomes compared with TACE alone. Baseline ALBI grade appeared to be prognostic for survival outcomes in patients with uHCC. Notably, patients with baseline ALBI grade 2 showed greater benefit from TTI therapy, although these findings should be interpreted with caution and require validation in prospective studies.
