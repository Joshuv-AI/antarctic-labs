Galois Covers of Calabi–Yau Quivers and BPS State Counting
Johannes Aspman 1 , Cyril Closset 2 , Elias Furrer 2,3 , Jan Manschot 1 {}^{1}\ Department of Computer Science, Czech Technical University in Prague, Karlovo nam. 13, Prague 2, Czech Republic 2 {}^{2}\ School of Mathematics, University of Birmingham, Watson Building, Edgbaston, Birmingham B15 2TT, United Kingdom 3 {}^{3}\ NHETC and Department of Physics and Astronomy, Rutgers University, 126 Frelinghuysen Rd., Piscataway NJ 08855, USA School of Mathematics and Hamilton Mathematical Institute, Trinity College, Dublin 2, Ireland

Abstract
BPS quivers are central to our understanding of BPS states in 4d 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 supersymmetric field theories and of D-branes at Calabi–Yau threefold singularities. The two subjects are deeply interrelated through geometric engineering in Type II string theory, where a CY 3 quiver, also known as a 5d BPS quiver, describes fractional branes at a threefold singularity 𝐗 {\bf X} . We study the Galois cover Q ~ → Q \tilde{Q}\rightarrow Q of any BPS quiver Q Q by a finite abelian group 𝔾 \mathbb{G} , leading to a covering quiver Q ~ \tilde{Q} . The Galois cover is determined by a 𝔾 \mathbb{G} -grading of the arrows of the quiver Q Q , which can be understood as an orbifolding procedure. In particular, if Q Q is a CY 3 quiver for 𝐗 {\bf X} , then the Galois cover Q ~ \tilde{Q} is the CY 3 quiver for the orbifold singularity 𝐗 / 𝔾 {\bf X}/\mathbb{G} . We explore such Galois covering procedures in the language of supersymmetric quiver quantum mechanics, in terms of fixed loci under 𝔾 \mathbb{G} actions on moduli spaces of quiver representations, and in terms of homomorphisms between the Kontsevich–Soibelman algebras of Q Q and Q ~ \tilde{Q} . Our main result is an explicit covering formula for the BPS invariants of 4d 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 field theories, wherein the rational BPS invariant Ω ¯ Q ​ ( γ ) \bar{\Omega}^{Q}(\gamma) of Q Q is expressed as a sum of BPS invariants of Q ~ \tilde{Q} . We derive this formula in various special cases, which include the case when γ \gamma is a primitive charge vector, the case of general charge vectors for quivers without loops, and the case of CY 3 quivers for some simple geometries such as the conifold or local del Pezzo surfaces. The general formula is presented as a conjecture that can be verified in many examples. August 10, 2026

Quiver representations.

2.2
BPS invariants from quiver quantum mechanics

As already reviewed in the introduction, a

The existence (or not) of quiver points (or loci) for a given SQFT is a hard question, but there are many known cases in which we can find BPS quivers by indirect arguments

Given a BPS quiver

This quiver SQM is the 1d gauge-theory description of the worldline theory of a BPS particle — such descriptions often arise from wrapped D-branes in Type II string theory. The actual BPS states are the supersymmetric ground states of this SQM. In the Higgs phase (that is, typically at large FI parameters), one can find the ground states semi-classically in two steps. First, we consider the classical Higgs branch of the
\mathcal{N}=4

(2.20)

BPS invariants as Witten indices.
The basic quantity of interest for us is the BPS invariant

(2.22)

\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}^{Q}=\Omega(\gamma;\zeta)\penalty\ ,

(2.23)

This interpretation of

2.3
The Kontsevich–Soibelman monodromy operator

BPS spectra and their wall-crossing properties are famously encoded in the KS monodromy operator
75
;
55
;
52
. We let
\mathfrak{g}_{Q}
be the
\Gamma
-graded Lie algebra, with elements
e_{\gamma}\in\mathfrak{g}_{Q}
for each charge
\gamma\in\Gamma
, satisfying the commutation relation,

[e_{\gamma},e_{\gamma^{\prime}}]=\left<\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\right>(-1)^{\left<\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\right>}e_{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}\penalty\ .

(2.32)

We then define the formal quantities:

\mathcal{K}_{\gamma}=\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}e_{n\gamma}\right)\penalty\ .

(2.33)

The BPS spectrum is encoded in the KS monodromy operator:

\mathbb{M}_{Q}(\zeta)=\prod^{\curvearrowright}_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{+}}\mathcal{K}_{\gamma}^{\Omega^{Q}(\gamma;\zeta)},

(2.34)

where the product is over all BPS particles with central charges
Z_{\gamma}
valued in a given half-plane of the complex plane (giving us a customary separation between particles and anti-particles). The ordering of the non-commutative product is determined by clockwise ordering of the phases of the central charges — for BPS invariants, this corresponds to decreasing slopes (
2.10
) for the stable representations in
\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{\gamma}
. The KS wall-crossing formula is the statement that
\mathbb{M}_{Q}\equiv\mathbb{M}_{Q}(\zeta)
is actually independent of the choice of chamber in the space of stability conditions — this gives us very intricate constraints on how the spectrum can change as we cross a wall of marginal stability.

It is also useful to reorder the product (
2.34
) in terms of the (so-called) symplectomorphism
\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}
defined by

\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}=\exp\left(\bar{\Omega}(\gamma)\,e_{\gamma}\right)\penalty\ ,

(2.35)

written now in terms of the rational invariants
\bar{\Omega}
defined in (
2.30
), so that:

\mathbb{M}_{Q}=\prod^{\curvearrowright}_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{+}}\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}=\prod^{\curvearrowright}_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{+}}\exp\left(\bar{\Omega}^{Q}(\gamma)\,e_{\gamma}\right)\penalty\ .

(2.36)

Note that, even if
\Omega(\gamma)
vanishes for a specific
\gamma
,
\bar{\Omega}(\gamma)
may be non-vanishing if
\gamma
is non-primitive.

The monodromy operator provides us with a way to determine invariants at a degenerate stability condition or on a wall of marginal stability. In that case, we require that the charges
\gamma
with aligned central charges appear in the same exponent:

\exp\left(\sum_{\gamma\,|\,{\rm Arg}(Z_{\gamma})=\phi}\bar{\Omega}(\gamma)\,e_{\gamma}\right)\penalty\ .

(2.37)

We consider the coefficients of
e_{\gamma}
so obtained as the definition of the BPS invariants at such a non-generic stability condition. These can be determined using the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula

\begin{split}&\exp\!\left(X\right)\,\exp\!\left(Y\right)=\\
&\quad\exp\left(X+Y+\tfrac{1}{2}[X,Y]+\tfrac{1}{12}[X,[X,Y]]+\tfrac{1}{12}[Y,[Y,X]]+\dots\right)\penalty\ ,\end{split}

(2.38)

where the dots represent higher commutators and
X
,
Y
are any Lie algebra elements. We expect that the BPS invariants for such a degeneric stability parameters can be
alternatively derived using Harder–Narasimhan
filtrations and wall-crossing for invariants of moduli stacks
96
;
65
;
64
. We leave this as an interesting challenge for future work.

Refined monodromy operator.
The quantum monodromy operator admits a refinement, denoted by

\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}=\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Omega(\gamma,y^{n})}{n}\frac{\hat{e}_{n\gamma}}{y^{n}-y^{-n}}\right)\penalty\ ,

(2.39)

\hat{e}_{\gamma}\hat{e}_{\gamma^{\prime}}=(-y)^{\left<\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\right>}\hat{e}_{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}\penalty\ .

(2.40)

In terms of the refined rational invariant (

\mathcal{W}_{\gamma}=\exp\left(\bar{\Omega}(\gamma,y)\,\frac{\hat{e}_{\gamma}}{y-y^{-1}}\right).

(2.41)

The quantum monodromy operator then reads

(2.42)

The unrefined case is reproduced in the limit

.

3

It has long been known that there exists BPS spectra which can be roughly interpreted as several copies of the BPS spectrum of another theory

4
Graded representations and BPS states

In this section, we explore the relationship between quiver representations of Galois pairs
Q
and
\tilde{Q}
. As a simple consequence of this discussion, we will see that Galois coverings of 5d BPS quivers correspond to orbifolds in M-theory. Finally, we will conjecture a precise formula relating BPS invariants of distinct BPS quivers related by a Galois covering.

4.1
Induced actions on quiver representations

Galois covers of quivers induce relationships between quiver representations — more precisely, we have induced functors between the corresponding categories of quiver representations.
There are two distinct Galois functors to consider, covariant and contravariant with respect to the Galois cover (
3.3
), which correspond neatly to the two distinct definitions of Galois covering given in section
3.1

4.1.1
Galois functors on quiver representations

Without any real loss of generality, we may fix
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. For
\tilde{Q}
any
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
Galois cover of a quiver

\tilde{R}
of

\tilde{N}=(N_{j,\alpha})=(N_{1,0},\dots,N_{1,n-1};N_{2,0},\dots,N_{2,n-1};\dots;N_{|Q_{0}|,0},\dots,N_{|Q_{0}|,n-1})\penalty\ .

Given a Galois cover (
3.3

Push-down functor.

The push-down (
i.e.
pushforward) functor

F_{\ast}\;:\;{\bf Rep}(\tilde{Q})\longrightarrow{\bf Rep}(Q)

(4.2)

maps a representation
\tilde{R}
of
\tilde{Q}

V_{j}=\oplus_{\alpha}\tilde{V}_{j,\alpha}\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003ptX_{a}=\oplus_{\alpha}\tilde{X}_{a,\alpha}\penalty\ .

(4.3)

The entries of the dimension vector

N_{j}=F_{\ast}(\tilde{N})_{j}=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{n-1}N_{j,\alpha}\penalty\ .

(4.4)

Given the identification (
2.8
) between dimension vectors

and

F_{\ast}\;:\;\tilde{\Gamma}\rightarrow\Gamma\;:\;\tilde{\gamma}\mapsto\gamma=F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})=\sum_{j\in Q_{0}}\left(\sum_{\alpha=0}^{n-1}N_{j,\alpha}\right)\gamma_{j}\penalty\ .

(4.5)

Pull-up functor.

The pull-up (
i.e.

F^{\ast}\;:\;{\bf Rep}(Q)\longrightarrow{\bf Rep}(\tilde{Q})

(4.6)

maps a representation

The entries of the dimension vector

The superpotentials

4.2
Galois covers of 5d BPS quivers as orbifolds

Consider a quiver

We are interested in determining relations between the BPS invariants of the BPS quiver

Gauging and ungauging in
\mathcal{N}=4
SQM.
To the Galois covering quiver
\tilde{Q}

{\rm MSQM}[\tilde{Q}]=\bigoplus_{\tilde{\gamma}\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{+}}{\rm SQM}_{\tilde{\gamma}}[\tilde{Q}]\penalty\ .

(4.22)

Here we use the notation
\tilde{\gamma}\cong(\tilde{N})
for the dimension vectors of the quiver
\tilde{Q}
, corresponding to
\tilde{N}_{j,g}\geq 0
for all
(j,g)\in\tilde{Q}_{0}

\mathbb{G}
acts as a discrete symmetry of this MSQM by permuting the charge vectors:

\mathbb{G}^{(0)}\,:\,{\rm MSQM}[\tilde{Q}]\rightarrow{\rm MSQM}[\tilde{Q}]\;:\;\tilde{N}_{j,g}\mapsto\tilde{N}_{j,g+h}\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for any }\,h\in\mathbb{G}\penalty\ .

(4.23)

In particular, each specific
{\rm SQM}_{\tilde{\gamma}}
with a fixed charge
\tilde{\gamma}
sits in a
\mathbb{G}
-orbit
[\tilde{\gamma}]
of SQMs of cardinality
|[\tilde{\gamma}]|
and with stabiliser
{\rm Stab}(\tilde{\gamma})\subseteq\mathbb{G}
.

The MSQM (

) only admits the discrete symmetry
\mathbb{G}
if the stability parameters
\tilde{\zeta}_{j,g}
are chosen to be
\mathbb{G}
-covariant inside each orbit

. This amounts to choosing the FI parameters
\tilde{\zeta}_{j,g}=\zeta_{j}
for all

, where
\zeta_{j}
will be identified with the FI parameters of
Q=\tilde{Q}/\mathbb{G}
— in other words,
\tilde{\zeta}=F^{\ast}(\zeta)
. Equivalently, we identify the central charges as:

\tilde{Z}(\tilde{\gamma}_{j,g})=Z(\gamma_{j})\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for all }\,g\in\mathbb{G}\penalty\ ,

(4.24)

which then determines the FI parameters as in (
2.19
). It is important to note that this is a fine-tuned locus in the larger space of stability conditions for
\tilde{Q}
. In particular, since central charges align according to (
4.24
), we are generally on walls of marginal stability for
\tilde{Q}
. Such fine-tuned loci are essentially the collimation chambers studied in the 5d BPS quiver context in

;
33
;
34

quivers, these

-invariant stability conditions were recently established in

, where they were also recast in the language of Bridgeland stability conditions for triangulated categories.

In

\mathbb{G}
-invariant stability conditions of

correspond to the
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
-invariant stability conditions of the quiver

. For our Galois cover with
\mathbb{G}
abelian, the group of characters

Given the MSQM (
4.22
) with a discrete symmetry
\mathbb{G}
, we can consider gauging the symmetry to obtain the MSQM for
Q=\tilde{Q}/\mathbb{G}
. We can certainly consider this gauging at the level of the BPS Hilbert space — that is, the supersymmetric ground states of (
4.22

\mathcal{H}^{(\tilde{Q})}_{\rm BPS}\equiv\bigoplus_{\tilde{\gamma}\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{+}}\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\gamma}}^{(\tilde{Q})}\penalty\ ,

(4.25)

on which
\mathbb{G}
acts as
\tilde{\gamma}\rightarrow g\cdot\tilde{\gamma}
. The symmetry (
4.23
) is an ordinary symmetry of the quantum mechanics — that is, a zero-form symmetry
\mathbb{G}^{(0)}
in modern terminology
51
. Gauging
\mathbb{G}^{(0)}
amounts to projecting over the
\mathbb{G}
-invariant states, which are simply the orbits
[\tilde{\gamma}]
. We then have:

\mathcal{H}_{\rm BPS}^{(Q)}\cong\mathcal{H}_{\rm BPS}^{(\tilde{Q})}\big/\mathbb{G}^{(0)}\penalty\ .

(4.26)

Working at the level of the (formal) Witten indices (
2.22
) written as:

\mathcal{I}_{\rm BPS}^{Q}\equiv\Tr_{\mathcal{H}_{\rm BPS}^{(Q)}}\left((-1)^{\rm F}\right)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{+}}\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}^{Q}\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}^{Q}\equiv\Tr_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}^{(Q)}}\left((-1)^{\rm F}\right)\penalty\ ,

(4.27)

the gauging of the one-form symmetry corresponds to the insertion of the
\mathbb{G}^{(0)}
symmetry operators as follows:

\mathcal{I}_{\rm BPS}^{Q}={1\over|\mathbb{G}|}\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}}\Tr_{\mathcal{H}_{\rm BPS}^{(\tilde{Q})}}\left(g(-1)^{\rm F}\right)\penalty\ .

(4.28)

More generally, the
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant states are indexed by the characters of
\mathbb{G}^{(0)}

\lvert[\tilde{\gamma}];\chi\rangle\equiv{1\over\sqrt{|\mathbb{G}|}}\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}}\chi(g)\,\lvert g\cdot\tilde{\gamma}\rangle\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with }\;\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}}={\rm Hom}(\tilde{\mathbb{G}},{\rm U}(1))\penalty\ .

(4.29)

The invariant states correspond to
\chi=1
. Like for any discrete symmetry in quantum mechanics, the group characters index superselection sectors and
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}\cong\mathbb{G}^{(-1)}
is the ‘quantum’
(-1)
-form symmetry of the gauged theory. When gauging with the
\chi
-twist, we simply project onto the corresponding superselection sector — the insertion of the character corresponds to the insertion of a 1d SPT phase for
\mathbb{G}^{(0)}

\mathcal{I}_{\rm BPS}^{Q,\,\chi}\equiv{1\over|\mathbb{G}|}\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}}\chi(g)\Tr_{\mathcal{H}_{\rm BPS}^{(\tilde{Q})}}\left(g(-1)^{\rm F}\right)\penalty\ .

(4.30)

The original Hilbert space (
4.25

\mathbb{G}
symmetry. From this perspective, the grading action (
3.4

\mathbb{G}^{(-1)}

\mathbb{G}
-equivariant

Relations between 1d Witten indices.
Given a Galois pair
(Q,\tilde{Q})
, the physical picture of
\mathbb{G}
gauging and ungauging suggests simple relations between BPS states and, hence, between invariants of the two distinct quivers. First, consider decomposing (
4.25

\mathcal{H}^{(\tilde{Q})}_{\rm BPS}\equiv\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{+}}\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}^{(\tilde{Q})}\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with }\;\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}^{(\tilde{Q})}\equiv\bigoplus_{\tilde{\gamma}\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{+}\,|\,F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma}\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\gamma}}^{(\tilde{Q})}\penalty\ .

(4.31)

In each
\gamma
-sector, we then have

\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}^{(Q)}\cong\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}^{(\tilde{Q})}\big/\mathbb{G}^{(0)}\penalty\ ,

(4.32)

and we are now dealing with finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. We can then compute the Witten index of the
\gamma
-sector in the 1d gauged theory as

\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}^{Q}={1\over|\mathbb{G}|}\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}}\Tr_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}^{(\tilde{Q})}}\left(g(-1)^{\rm F}\right)={1\over|\mathbb{G}|}\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}\,|\,F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma}\sum_{g\in{\rm Stab}(\tilde{\gamma})}\mathcal{I}^{\tilde{Q}}_{\tilde{\gamma}}\penalty\ ,

(4.33)

where we used the fact that the insertion of
g
yields zero unless it is in the stabiliser of
\tilde{\gamma}
since
\langle\tilde{\gamma}|\mathopen{}g\cdot\tilde{\gamma}\rangle=0
if
g\cdot\gamma\neq\gamma
. We then find:

\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}^{Q}={1\over|\mathbb{G}|}\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}\,|\,F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma}|{\rm Stab}(\tilde{\gamma})|\,\mathcal{I}^{\tilde{Q}}_{\tilde{\gamma}}=\sum_{[\tilde{\gamma}]\,|\,F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma}\mathcal{I}_{[\tilde{\gamma}]}^{\tilde{Q}}\penalty\ .

(4.34)

In the last equality, we used the
\mathbb{G}^{(0)}
symmetry which implies that the Witten index
\mathcal{I}_{[\tilde{\gamma}]}^{\tilde{Q}}\equiv\mathcal{I}_{\tilde{\gamma}}^{\tilde{Q}}
must be the same for every
\tilde{\gamma}\in[\tilde{\gamma}]
, together with the orbit-stabiliser theorem. In other words, the gauging (
4.32
) gives us BPS states of
Q
which are indexed by the
\mathbb{G}^{(0)}
orbits of BPS states of the quiver
\tilde{Q}
, as expected.
We therefore derived a precise relationship between the Witten indices of the Galois pair, in principle. The difficulty in using such a formula, however, is that we generally do not have a good way to compute the 1d
\mathcal{N}=4
Witten index (
2.22
) of the quiver SQM

is not primitive — see

\mathcal{N}=2
Coulomb-branch physics, this is related to the possible presence of bound states at threshold, and from the perspective of moduli spaces of quiver representations, this is closely related to the fact that the 1d target spaces should generally be understood as moduli stacks.

Relations between rational BPS invariants.

To obtain a more useful relation, we need to recast the above discussion in terms of standard BPS invariants, which we can do whenever (
2.23

of Galois pairs:

\bar{\Omega}^{Q}(\gamma;\zeta)={1\over|\mathbb{G}|}\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}\,|\,F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma}{\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})}\;\bar{\Omega}^{\tilde{Q}}(\tilde{\gamma};F^{\ast}(\zeta))\penalty\ ,

(4.35)

where
\bar{\Omega}
was defined in (

\tilde{\zeta}=F^{\ast}(\zeta)
are as discussed above (

is a sign that tracks the relative complex dimension of the moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{\gamma}(\zeta)
and
\mathcal{M}_{\tilde{\gamma}}^{\tilde{Q}}(\tilde{\zeta})

\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})=(-1)^{{\dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{M}^{\tilde{Q}}_{\tilde{\gamma}})-{\dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{\gamma})}\in\{\pm 1\}\penalty\ .

(4.36)

Recall that, for a quiver without superpotential, the virtual dimension of the
moduli space of charge
\gamma
is given by (
2.14
), and
that this formula remains true mod 2 for generic superpotential and FI
parameters, hence (
4.36

2.14

The relative sign (
4.36

4.35

2.25
). The fact that the rational invariant
\bar{\Omega}
appears instead of
\Omega
is related to the non-trivial stabilisers. In the special case when
\gamma
is primitive, then all
\tilde{\gamma}
involved are primitive and have trivial stabiliser

This is easily seen by contradiction. If some
\tilde{\gamma}
such that

\gamma

\tilde{\gamma}
had a non-trivial stabiliser

would be divisible by
|H|

and therefore the formula (
4.35

\bar{\Omega}

4.4
Refined covering relation for symmetric Galois covers

One of the difficulties encountered when relating BPS invariants of a Galois cover as in (
4.35
) is that the stability condition
\tilde{\zeta}=F^{*}(\zeta)
is non-generic and in many cases sits on a wall of marginal stability for
\tilde{Q}
. Such cases arise, in particular, for a charge
\tilde{\gamma}
such that
F_{*}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma\in\Gamma
is non-primitive, since two constituent charges
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}\in\tilde{\Gamma}
with
F_{*}(\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})=F_{*}(\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime\prime})
have the same slope
\mu
(
2.10
) for
\tilde{\zeta}=F^{*}(\zeta)
.

It is therefore useful to single out Galois covers for which the BPS invariants
\Omega(\tilde{\gamma},F^{*}(\zeta))
are independent of a small generic perturbation away from
F^{*}(\zeta)
.
To this end, we introduce the notion of a
symmetric

\mathbb{G}
Galois cover as follows. A symmetric Galois cover is a cover for which the set
\{d_{a}\}_{i,j}
of gradings
d_{a}\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
of the set of arrows
a\in Q_{1}
with equal tail
i
and head
j
is
\mathbb{G}
symmetric. In other words, the gradings are symmetrically distributed among the arrows. A necessary condition for a Galois cover to be
\mathbb{G}
symmetric is that the number of arrows
(a:i\to j)
between each pair of nodes
i,j\in Q_{0}
is a multiple of
|\mathbb{G}|
. Moreover, the inner products for
Q
and
\tilde{Q}
of a symmetric Galois cover are related by

|\mathbb{G}|\langle\tilde{N},\tilde{N}^{\prime}\rangle_{\tilde{Q}}=\langle F_{\ast}(\tilde{N}),F_{\ast}(\tilde{N}^{\prime})\rangle_{Q}\penalty\ ,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }\tilde{N},\tilde{N}^{\prime}\penalty\ .

(4.43)

Then, for all
\tilde{N}
,
\tilde{N}^{\prime}
such that
F_{\ast}(\tilde{N})=F_{\ast}(\tilde{N}^{\prime})
, the anti-symmetric product vanishes:

F_{\ast}(\tilde{N})=F_{\ast}(\tilde{N}^{\prime})\hskip 10.00002pt\Rightarrow\hskip 10.00002pt\langle\tilde{N},\tilde{N}^{\prime}\rangle=0.

(4.44)

The vanishing of these inner products implies that the BPS invariant
\Omega(\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\zeta})
is independent for a small, generic perturbation away from
\tilde{\zeta}=F^{*}(\zeta)
. This aids the evaluation of the BPS invariants and ensures that these are integers.

For such covers, we propose a generalisation of the covering relation (
4.35
) to refined rational invariants. To this end, we assign to the vertices in a block of the quiver
\tilde{Q}
a non-trivial single-centred invariant
\Omega_{S}^{\tilde{Q}}
, which is an input in the Coulomb branch formula
82
;
84
;
87
;
88
. In that context, the single-centred invariant
\Omega_{S}^{\tilde{Q}}
represents the number of quantum states of a single black hole centre. We will explain in later sections that the general form of the motivic KS monodromy
\mathbb{M}(y)
, leads us, for
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, to the assignment

\Omega_{S}^{\tilde{Q}}(\tilde{\gamma}_{j,\alpha},\tilde{y})=\tilde{y}^{(2\alpha+1-|\mathbb{G}|)/|\mathbb{G}|},\hskip 20.00003pt\alpha=0,\dots,|\mathbb{G}|-1.

(4.45)

Due to the
\mathbb{G}
-symmetry, we can of course permute the
\alpha
on the right-hand side. This assignment thus mixes the space-time rotation symmetry and the
\mathbb{G}
-symmetry of the quiver. We then propose the following refinement of (
4.35
):

\bar{\Omega}^{Q}(\gamma,\zeta;y)=\frac{y-y^{-1}}{y^{|\mathbb{G}|}-y^{-|\mathbb{G}|}}\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}\,|\,F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma}\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})\,\bar{\Omega}^{\tilde{Q}}(\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\zeta};\tilde{y})\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\tilde{y}^{1/|\mathbb{G}|}=y\penalty\ .

(4.46)

The phase of
y
and
\tilde{y}
is determined by requiring that the
\tilde{y},y\to 1
limits agree. We will discuss examples where this formula holds in section
7
.

The
K_{2}
cover of figure
2(b)
discussed above is an example of a symmetric
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
cover, while the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
cover of figure
2(c)
is not symmetric. Sections
7.1
to
7.4
discuss more examples of symmetric
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
covers and the evaluation of their BPS invariants, while section
7.5
considers non-symmetric examples.

Circle action from the arrow grading and its fixed loci

For a fixed

\ \widehat{F}\;:\;\widehat{Q}\longrightarrow Q\penalty\ ,

\widehat{\gamma}
such that
F_{\ast}(\widehat{\gamma})=\gamma
, we have

\widehat{F}_{\ast}\;:\;\mathcal{M}_{\widehat{\gamma}}^{\widehat{Q}}\mapsto\widehat{F}_{\ast}(\mathcal{M}_{\widehat{\gamma}}^{\widehat{Q}})\cong\mathcal{M}_{\widehat{\gamma}}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{Q}\penalty\ .

(5.12)

The above argument establishes this embedding if all orbits
[R]\in\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{Q}
are
\zeta
-stable.
If
R
is strictly semi-stable —
i.e.
polystable, assuming that
[R]\in\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{Q}
— we have a decomposition into marginally bound constituents and a non-trivial stabiliser:

R\cong\oplus_{l}R_{l}^{\oplus m_{l}}\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm Stab}(R)=\prod_{l}GL(m_{l})\Big/GL(1)\penalty\ .

(5.13)

We can still run the above argument for each
R_{l}
independently, and we conclude that polystable orbits of representations of
Q
fixed by
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
simply give us polystable orbits of representations of
\widehat{Q}
.

We therefore conclude that fixed loci of the circle action (
5.2
) on semi-stable representations are encoded in the
\mathbb{Z}
-covering quiver
\widehat{Q}
. Note that, while
\widehat{Q}
has an infinite number of nodes, only a finite number are populated by any representation
\widehat{R}
corresponding to fixed-points
[R]
in
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{Q}
, for any finite
\gamma
.

\mathbb{G}
action versus
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
action.
To relate the above discussion to the
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
covering
\tilde{Q}
of
Q
, we should restrict the circle action (
5.2
) to the multiplicative
\mathbb{G}
action on
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{Q}
given by:

\mathbb{G}\;:\;[R]\mapsto g(\lambda_{n})\cdot[R]=[g(\lambda_{n})\cdot R]\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda_{n}\equiv e^{2\pi i\over n}\penalty\ .

(5.14)

The fixed loci under this
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
action are generally larger than under the circle action, but it is clear that the latter are a subset of the former:

(\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{\gamma})^{\mathbb{C}^{\ast}}\subseteq(\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{\gamma})^{\mathbb{G}}\penalty\ .

(5.15)

It is also clear that the fixed loci under
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
can be found using the same logic as above. The weight-space decomposition (
5.5
) can be restricted to the
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
characters in
{\rm Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{\ast})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, which we write as:

V_{i}\cong\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}\tilde{V}_{i,\alpha_{i}}\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003ptN_{i}=\sum_{\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}N_{i,\alpha_{i}}\penalty\ ,

(5.16)

with
V_{i,\alpha_{i}}\cong\mathbb{C}^{N_{i,\alpha_{i}}}
.
The fixed-point condition (
5.8
) on arrows now reads:

\alpha_{j}=\alpha_{i}+d_{a}\;({\rm mod}\,n)\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,(a:i\rightarrow j)\in Q_{1}\penalty\ .

(5.17)

Therefore, we find that the Galois cover
\mathbb{G}:\tilde{Q}\rightarrow Q
and the representation vectors
\tilde{\gamma}
such that
F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma
precisely encode the fixed loci in the moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{Q}
under the induced
\mathbb{G}
action (
5.14
) — any representation
\tilde{R}
of
\tilde{Q}
gives us a representation
R=F_{\ast}(\tilde{R})
of
Q
that sits in a
\mathbb{G}
-invariant orbit
[R]
. For each
\tilde{\gamma}
, We have the corresponding SQM gauge group

G_{\tilde{\gamma}}=\prod_{(i,\alpha)\in\tilde{Q}_{0}}{\rm U}(N_{i,\alpha})\penalty\ .

(5.18)

There are many cases where we can argue that, actually, the fixed points under
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
and the
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
action coincide — for instance, this is the case in any quiver without superpotential with
d_{a}\in\{0,1,\cdots,n-1\}
. More generally, the fixed loci under
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
are a strict subset of those under
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. There is a
\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}_{n}
Galois covering of
\tilde{Q}
by
\widehat{Q}
that corresponds to the decomposition of the vector spaces of any representation
\tilde{R}
of
\tilde{Q}
into weight spaces for
\widehat{Q}
:

\tilde{V}_{i,\alpha}=\bigoplus_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{i}^{(w+ln)}\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall(i,\alpha)=i_{\alpha}\in\tilde{Q}_{0}\penalty\ .

(5.19)

A necessary condition for having
(\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{\gamma})^{\mathbb{C}^{\ast}}=(\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{\gamma})^{\mathbb{G}}
is that the decomposition (
5.19
) is trivial (
i.e.
with a single summand
\tilde{V}_{i,\alpha}=V_{i}^{(w)}
for each node
(i,\alpha)\in\tilde{Q}_{0}
).

\mathbb{Z}_{n}
orbits and stacky fixed points.
It is important to emphasise that the cocharacter
\sigma
in (
5.4
) is only defined up to a simultaneous rescaling of the weights,
w_{i}\rightarrow w_{i}+1
, for all
i\in Q_{0}
, and similarly when restricted to the multiplicative action by
\mathbb{G}
. In other words, the
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
transformation

\mathbb{G}^{(0)}\;:\;\tilde{\gamma}=(N_{i,\alpha})\mapsto\lambda_{n}\cdot\tilde{\gamma}=(N_{i,\alpha+1\,({\rm mod}\,n)})

(5.20)

on the gauge ranks of the
\tilde{Q}
quiver descends to a gauge transformation
\sigma|_{\lambda=\lambda_{n}}
in the
\mathcal{N}=4
SQM for the quiver
Q
. This means that two
\tilde{Q}
moduli spaces for two distinct dimension vectors related by (
5.20
) are not just isomorphic (this much is clear from the
\mathbb{G}
symmetry of
\tilde{Q}
), they also descend to the same fixed locus in
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{Q}
.

As in section
4.3
, let us denote by
[\tilde{\gamma}]
the orbit of
\tilde{\gamma}
under
\mathbb{G}^{(0)}
, and by
\mathcal{M}^{\tilde{Q}}_{[\tilde{\gamma}]}\cong\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{\tilde{Q}}
the corresponding moduli space.
In general, for a fixed dimension vector
\tilde{\gamma}
of
\tilde{Q}
, the action (
5.20
) might have a non-trivial stabiliser
{\rm Stab}(\tilde{\gamma})\subseteq\mathbb{G}^{(0)}
. The corresponding fixed points inside
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{Q}
are then understood as stacky points. Physically, we simply have the finite gauge group
{\rm Stab}(\tilde{\gamma})\subset G_{\gamma}^{0}
which remains un-Higgsed on the classical Higgs branch. We then formally write down the fixed loci of
\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{\gamma}
under the
\mathbb{G}
action as a sum over distinct
\mathbb{G}^{(0)}
orbits
[\tilde{\gamma}]
:

(\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{\gamma})^{\mathbb{G}}=\sum_{[\tilde{\gamma}]\;|\;F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma}{1\over|{\rm Stab}(\tilde{\gamma})|}\mathcal{M}^{\tilde{Q}}_{[\tilde{\gamma}]}={1\over n}\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}\;|\;F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma}\mathcal{M}^{\tilde{Q}}_{\tilde{\gamma}}\penalty\ ,

(5.21)

where the second equality follows from the orbit-stabiliser theorem. This should be compared to (
4.34
).

BPS index from Atiyah–Bott–Kirwan localisation.
Our main conjecture (
4.35
) is intimately related to the decomposition (
5.21
) of the fixed locus. In favourable cases, we can see this explicitly. For instance, let us assume that
(\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{\gamma})^{\mathbb{C}^{\ast}}=(\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{\gamma})^{\mathbb{G}}
, and that all the moduli spaces involved are smooth. Then, viewing the moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}^{\tilde{Q}}_{\tilde{\gamma}}
as fixed loci of the circle action (
5.1
) inside
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{Q}
, one can recover the homology of
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{Q}
from Atiyah–Bott–Kirwan localisation
72
— see also
106
for a related discussion. Indeed, in this case, the topological Euler characteristic can be written as

\chi(\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{\gamma})=\sum_{[\tilde{\gamma}]}\chi(\mathcal{M}^{\tilde{Q}}_{[\tilde{\gamma}]})={1\over n}\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}}\chi(\mathcal{M}^{\tilde{Q}}_{\tilde{\gamma}})\penalty\ .

(5.22)

It directly follows from (
2.25
) that, for
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{Q}
smooth,

\Omega^{Q}(\gamma;\zeta)={1\over n}\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}\,|\,F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma}\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})\,\Omega^{\tilde{Q}}(\tilde{\gamma};\tilde{\zeta})\penalty\ ,

(5.23)

with the sign
\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})
defined in (
4.36
). More generally, it is natural to conjecture that the similar relation (
4.35
) for
\bar{\Omega}
also follows from the decomposition (
5.21
) when properly interpreted in terms of moduli stacks. We hope to return to this point in future work.

5.2
Refined indices from fixed loci and their normal bundles

The non-trivial distinction between fixed loci under
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
and under
\mathbb{G}
is further illuminated by considering how fixed-point theorems give us new relations between refined BPS indices of
Q
and of their
\mathbb{Z}
Galois covers. Recall that the refined BPS index is obtained as the
\chi_{\bf y}
-genus (
2.28
), assuming the moduli space is smooth, and it can therefore be computed by taking advantage of the circle action. Indeed, the
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
action (
5.2
) on the Kähler manifold
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{Q}
is naturally Hamiltonian, with the moment map schematically given by:

\mu_{\mathbb{C}^{\ast}}=\sum_{a\in Q_{1}}d_{a}\,|X_{a}|^{2}\penalty\ .

(5.24)

This gives us a Morse-Bott function whose critical points are the fixed loci
\mathcal{M}^{\tilde{Q}}_{\widehat{\gamma}}\subset\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{\gamma}
. We then have an explicit localisation of the
\chi_{\bf y}
-genus as

\chi_{-y^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{Q})=\sum_{\widehat{\gamma}\,|\,\widehat{F}_{\ast}(\widehat{\gamma})=\gamma}y^{2\,{\rm rank}(N_{\widehat{\gamma}}^{+})}\,\chi_{-y^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{\widehat{\gamma}}^{\widehat{Q}})\penalty\ ,

(5.25)

where the sum is over all fixed loci as indexed by dimension vectors of
\widehat{Q}
, and with
N_{\widehat{\gamma}}^{+}
denoting the positive-weight part of the normal bundle to
\mathcal{M}_{\widehat{\gamma}}^{\widehat{Q}}\subset\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{\gamma}
. This formula is most easily derived in the language of Morse theory
72
, where
2\,{\rm rank}(N_{\widehat{\gamma}}^{+})
is also the real Morse index at the fixed loci.
17
17

17

In our conventions,
{\rm rank}(N_{\widehat{\gamma}}^{+})
gives the number of complex directions in
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}
along which
\mu_{\mathbb{C}^{\ast}}
increases; that is the Morse index if we choose
f=-\mu_{\mathbb{C}^{\ast}}
to be the Morse function.
The rank of
N_{\widehat{\gamma}}^{+}
can be easily derived from the weight decomposition, at least in terms of virtual dimensions, by counting representations of positive weights minus the residual gauge transformations in
GL_{\gamma}/GL_{\widehat{\gamma}}
acting on them:

{\rm rk}(N^{+}_{\widehat{\gamma}})=\sum_{a\in Q_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w_{t(a)},w_{h(a)}\\
w_{h(a)}<w_{t(a)}+d_{a}\end{subarray}}N_{t(a),w_{t(a)}}N_{h(a),w_{h(a)}}-\sum_{j\in Q_{0}}\sum_{w^{\prime}>w}N_{j,w}N_{j,w^{\prime}}\penalty\ .

(5.26)

We therefore obtain a completely explicit formula relating the refined BPS indices of
Q
and of its
\mathbb{Z}
Galois covering
\widehat{Q}
, as follows:

\Omega^{Q}(\gamma,y;\zeta)=\sum_{\widehat{\gamma}\,|\,\widehat{F}_{\ast}(\widehat{\gamma})=\gamma}\,(-y)^{{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{M}_{\widehat{\gamma}})-{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{M}_{\gamma})}\,y^{2\,{\rm rank}(N_{\widehat{\gamma}}^{+})}\,\Omega^{\widehat{Q}}(\widehat{\gamma},y;\widehat{\zeta})\penalty\ ,

(5.27)

with
\widehat{\zeta}=\widehat{F}^{\ast}(\zeta)
.
Note that this formula depends crucially on the weight decomposition (
5.5
)-(
5.6
) and on the fact that the gradings and weights are
\mathbb{Z}
-valued. There is no such formula in terms of the
\mathbb{G}
fixed loci only — that is, even in cases where
(\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{\gamma})^{\mathbb{C}^{\ast}}=(\mathcal{M}^{Q}_{\gamma})^{\mathbb{G}}
so that
\mathcal{M}_{\widehat{\gamma}}^{\widehat{Q}}=\mathcal{M}_{[\tilde{\gamma}]}^{\tilde{Q}}
, we still need to consider the
\mathbb{Z}
covering quiver
\widehat{Q}
to compute the Morse indices (
5.26
). Note also that setting
y=1
in (
5.27
) in cases when
\mathcal{M}_{\widehat{\gamma}}^{\widehat{Q}}=\mathcal{M}_{[\tilde{\gamma}]}^{\tilde{Q}}
for every
\widehat{\gamma}
reproduces the unrefined relation (
5.23
).

6
Galois Covers and homomorphisms of graded Lie algebras

In this section, given a Galois pair of quivers
Q
and
\tilde{Q}
, we discuss several homomorphisms between the Kontsevich–Soibelman graded Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}_{Q}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\tilde{Q}}
, or between subalgebras of these algebras, which help us to establish Galois covering relations between BPS invariants. In subsection
6.1
we consider general Galois pairs, while in subsection
6.2
we consider additional homomorphisms that can be defined for symmetric Galois covers. In subsection
6.3
, we explain how these homomorphisms imply non-trivial relations between BPS invariants of Galois pairs.

6.1
Homomorphism for general Galois covers

The KS algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{Q}
for
Q
is the
\Gamma
-graded Lie algebra with commutation relations (
2.32
). Similarly, the KS algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\tilde{Q}}
is the
\tilde{\Gamma}
-graded Lie algebra with commutation relations:

[\tilde{e}_{\tilde{\gamma}},\tilde{e}_{\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}}]=\left<\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\right>(-1)^{\left<\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\right>}\,\tilde{e}_{\tilde{\gamma}+\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}}\penalty\ .

(6.1)

Recall that the Galois group
\mathbb{G}
acts on
\tilde{\Gamma}
as:

\mathbb{G}\;:\;\tilde{\Gamma}\rightarrow\tilde{\Gamma}\;:\;\tilde{\gamma}\mapsto g\tilde{\gamma}=\sum_{(j,h)\in\tilde{Q}_{0}}\tilde{N}_{j,h}\tilde{\gamma}_{j,h+g}\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall g\in\mathbb{G}\penalty\ .

(6.2)

One can easily show that, for
\gamma=F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})
and
\gamma^{\prime}=F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})
, we have the identities:

\langle g\tilde{\gamma},g\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}}\langle\tilde{\gamma},g\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\rangle\penalty\ .

(6.3)

\mathbb{G}
-invariant subalgebra.
For our purpose, it is very useful to define the subalgebra

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{Q}}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{\tilde{Q}}

(6.4)

generated by the
\mathbb{G}
-invariant elements:

\tilde{e}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{\gamma}}\equiv\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}}\tilde{e}_{g\tilde{\gamma}}.

(6.5)

Note this definition only depends on the orbit
[\tilde{\gamma}]
of
\tilde{\gamma}
, and that the right hand side is a sum of
|\mathbb{G}|
elements, even if
\tilde{\gamma}
has non-trivial stabiliser group.
18
18

18

Thus we should rather write this element as
\tilde{e}^{\mathbb{G}}_{[\tilde{\gamma}]}
, but prefer to write it as
\tilde{e}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{\gamma}}
to avoid clutter.

To prove that the elements (
6.5
) indeed generate a subalgebra, we compute the commutator using (
6.1
):

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\left[\tilde{e}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{\gamma}},\tilde{e}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}}\sum_{g^{\prime}\in\mathbb{G}}\langle g\tilde{\gamma},g^{\prime}\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle(-1)^{\langle g\tilde{\gamma},g^{\prime}\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle}\,\tilde{e}_{g\tilde{\gamma}+g^{\prime}\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}}

(6.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}}\sum_{g^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{G}}\langle g\tilde{\gamma},gg^{\prime\prime}\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle(-1)^{\langle g\tilde{\gamma},gg^{\prime\prime}\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle}\tilde{e}_{g(\tilde{\gamma}+g^{\prime\prime}\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}}\langle\tilde{\gamma},g\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle(-1)^{\langle\tilde{\gamma},g\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle}\,\tilde{e}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{\gamma}+g\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}}\penalty\ .

Here we set
g^{\prime}=g\,g^{\prime\prime}
in the second line and we then use the first identity in (
6.3
), so that the sum over
g
simply returns the
\mathbb{G}
-invariant elements
\tilde{e}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{\gamma}+g^{\prime\prime}\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}}
.

The KS algebra homomorphism
\mathfrak{f}_{\ast}^{\mathbb{G}}
.
It is natural to try and define a graded Lie algebra homomorphism between
\mathfrak{g}_{\tilde{Q}}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{Q}
that would somehow correspond to the pull-down functor
F_{\ast}
that acts on quiver representations. In the general case, the relevant homomorphism actually acts on the
\mathbb{G}
-invariant elements only. We define:

\mathfrak{f}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\ast}\;:\;\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{Q}}\to\mathfrak{g}_{Q}\;:\;\tilde{e}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{\gamma}}\mapsto\mathfrak{f}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\ast}(\tilde{e}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{\gamma}})=\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})\,e_{\gamma}\penalty\ ,

(6.7)

where
\gamma=F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})
and the sign
\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})
was defined in (
4.36
).
This is well-defined on
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{Q}}
since the parity of
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{M}^{\tilde{Q}}_{\tilde{\gamma}})
is independent of
\tilde{\zeta}
and
\xi
is
\mathbb{G}
-invariant — that is:

\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})=\xi(\gamma,g\tilde{\gamma})\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall g\in\mathbb{G}\penalty\ .

(6.8)

It also follows from (
2.14
) and (
2.16
) that
\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})
satisfies the cocycle relation:

\xi(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}+\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})\,\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})\,\xi(\gamma^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})=(-1)^{\langle\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\rangle+\langle\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle}\penalty\ .

(6.9)

As an alternative to (
4.36
), one may define
\xi
iteratively through this cocycle condition after setting
\xi(\gamma_{j},\tilde{\gamma}_{j,g})=1
for the vertices
j\in Q_{0}
and
(j,g)\in\tilde{Q}_{0}
.

To prove that (
6.7
) is indeed a Lie algebra homomorphism, we first note that

\mathfrak{f}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\ast}\left(\left[\tilde{e}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{\gamma}},\tilde{e}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}}\right]\right)=\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})\xi(\gamma^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})\left[e_{\gamma},e_{\gamma^{\prime}}\right].

(6.10)

On the other hand,
\mathfrak{f}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\ast}
acting on the third line of (
6.6
) gives us

\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}}\langle\tilde{\gamma},g\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle\,(-1)^{\langle\tilde{\gamma},g\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle}\,\xi(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}+g\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})\,e_{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}\penalty\ .

(6.11)

Using the cocycle identity (
6.9
) and the property (
6.8
), this reduces to:

\begin{split}&\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})\xi(\gamma^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}}\langle\tilde{\gamma},g\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle(-1)^{\langle\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\rangle}e_{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}\\
&=\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})\xi(\gamma^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})\langle\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\rangle(-1)^{\langle\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\rangle}e_{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}\penalty\ ,\end{split}

(6.12)

which is the
\mathfrak{g}_{Q}
commutator for the right-hand side of (
6.10
).

6.2
Homomorphisms for a symmetric Galois cover

There exists a richer structure of algebra homomorphisms if the Galois cover is symmetric, that is to say if the cover satisfies the constraint (
4.43
). In this case, it is not necessary to project
\mathfrak{g}_{\tilde{Q}}
to the
\mathbb{G}
-invariant subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{Q}}
considered above. Instead, we can directly apply the forgetful map
F_{\ast}:\tilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
provided by the push-down functor on the charge lattice, as defined in (
4.5
).
Let us introduce the
\Gamma
-graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{\underline{g}}_{\tilde{Q}}
generated by
\underline{e}_{\gamma}
,
\gamma\in\Gamma
, with the commutation relations

\left[\underline{e}_{\gamma},\underline{e}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\right]=\left<\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\right>\,(-1)^{\left<\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\right>}\,\underline{e}_{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}\penalty\ ,

(6.13)

where
\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\in\tilde{\Gamma}
are charges such that
F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma\in\Gamma
and
F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})=\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
. The symmetric condition (
4.43
) ensures that
\left<\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\right>
is independent of the choice of
\tilde{\gamma}
,
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}
. We then have the forgetful homomorphism:

\mathfrak{{\underline{h}}}\;:\;\mathfrak{g}_{\tilde{Q}}\to\mathfrak{\underline{g}}_{\tilde{Q}}\;:\;\tilde{e}_{\tilde{\gamma}}\mapsto\mathfrak{{\underline{h}}}(\tilde{e}_{\tilde{\gamma}})={\underline{e}}_{\gamma}\penalty\ ,

(6.14)

with
F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma
.
Indeed, the commutation relation (
6.1
) implies (
6.13
).
The algebra
\mathfrak{\underline{g}}_{\tilde{Q}}
can be realised as a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{\tilde{Q}}
in many ways. For example, for each
j\in Q_{0}
,
\underline{e}_{\gamma_{j}}
can be identified with one of the elements
\tilde{e}_{\tilde{\gamma}_{j,g}}
, giving us
|Q_{0}|^{|\mathbb{G}|}
distinct realizations. Note that the image of the
|\mathbb{G}|
-invariant element (
6.5
) is

\mathfrak{\underline{h}}(\tilde{e}_{\tilde{\gamma}}^{\mathbb{G}})=|\mathbb{G}|\,\underline{e}_{\gamma}\penalty\ .

(6.15)

It follows from (
6.13
) that there is a bijective homomorphism
\mathfrak{\underline{f}}_{*}
between the two
\Gamma
-graded algebras
\mathfrak{\underline{g}}_{\tilde{Q}}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{Q}
, with

\mathfrak{\underline{f}}_{*}({\underline{e}}_{{\gamma}})=\frac{\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})}{|\mathbb{G}|}\,e_{\gamma}\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003ptF_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma\penalty\ ,

(6.16)

with
\xi
as discussed in the previous subsection. This is again independent of the choice of
\tilde{\gamma}\in\tilde{\Gamma}
by the properties of a symmetric cover.

Surjective homomorphism and motivic lift.

The composition
\mathfrak{f}_{*}=\mathfrak{\underline{f}}_{*}\circ\mathfrak{\underline{h}}
provides the surjective algebra homomorphism:

\mathfrak{f}_{*}=\mathfrak{\underline{f}}_{*}\circ\mathfrak{\underline{h}}\;:\;\mathfrak{g}_{\tilde{Q}}\to\mathfrak{g}_{Q}\;:\;\tilde{e}_{\tilde{\gamma}}\mapsto\mathfrak{f}_{*}(\tilde{e}_{\tilde{\gamma}})=\frac{\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})}{|\mathbb{G}|}\,e_{\gamma}\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\gamma=F_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})\penalty\ .

(6.17)

This is a homomorphism since it maps the commutation relation (
6.1
) to:

\frac{\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})\xi(\gamma^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})}{|\mathbb{G}|}\,[e_{\gamma},e_{\gamma^{\prime}}]=\frac{\xi(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}+\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})}{|\mathbb{G}|}\langle\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle\,(-1)^{\left<\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\right>}\,e_{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}\penalty\ ,

(6.18)

which reproduces (
2.32
) upon using the symmetric property (
4.43
) and the cocycle relation (
6.9
). This relation for symmetric covers may be lifted to a relation between quantum tori. We let
\hat{e}_{\gamma}
be the generators of the quantum torus
\mathcal{R}_{Q}
associated to
\mathfrak{g}_{Q}
satisfying (
2.40
), and
\hat{\underline{e}}_{\gamma}
the generators of the quantum torus
\underline{\mathcal{R}}_{\tilde{Q}}\subset\mathcal{R}_{\tilde{Q}}
associated to
\mathfrak{\underline{g}}_{\tilde{Q}}
, satisfying

\left[\hat{\underline{e}}_{\gamma},\hat{\underline{e}}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\right]=(-\tilde{y})^{\langle\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle}\,\hat{\underline{e}}_{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}},\hskip 20.00003ptF_{*}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma,\,\,F_{*}(\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})=\gamma^{\prime}.

(6.19)

The homomorphism
\mathfrak{\underline{f}}_{*}
then acts on the quantum tori as follows:

\mathfrak{\underline{f}}_{*}:\hskip 20.00003pt\hat{\underline{e}}_{\gamma}\to\xi(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})\,\hat{e}_{\gamma},\hskip 20.00003pt\tilde{y}^{1/|\mathbb{G}|}=y\penalty\ .

(6.20)

Injective homomorphism.
Finally, symmetric covers also allow for the injective homomorphism:

\mathfrak{\underline{f}}^{*}:\mathfrak{g}_{Q}\to\mathfrak{\underline{g}}_{\tilde{Q}},\hskip 20.00003pt\mathfrak{\underline{f}}^{*}({e}_{\gamma})=\frac{\xi(\gamma,|\mathbb{G}|\tilde{\gamma})}{|\mathbb{G}|}\,{\underline{e}}_{{|\mathbb{G}|\gamma}},\hskip 20.00003ptF_{\ast}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma\penalty\ .

(6.21)

Note this maps only to elements
{\underline{e}}_{{\gamma}}
with charges
\gamma
divisible by
|\mathbb{G}|
, and is thus not surjective.

6.3
Homomorphisms and covering relations for BPS invariants

The homomorphisms described above will act on the KS monodromy operator
\mathbb{M}_{\tilde{Q}}
. For any Galois cover, the BPS invariants
\Omega(\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\zeta})
are
\mathbb{G}
-invariant if
\tilde{\zeta}=F^{\ast}(\zeta)
, namely:

\Omega(g\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\zeta})=\Omega(\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\zeta})\penalty\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall g\in\mathbb{G}\penalty\ .

(6.22)

Even for a generic
\zeta
, the choice
\tilde{\zeta}=F^{\ast}(\zeta)
is a highly non-generic stability condition in
\tilde{Q}
: many quiver representations will have the same slope even though their dimension vectors are not necessarily parallel. The prescription (
2.37
) for such states sitting on the same ray implies that
\mathbb{M}_{\tilde{Q}}
can be written in terms of the
\mathbb{G}
-invariant elements (
6.5
), as we now explain. Firstly, assuming that
\zeta
is a generic stability condition for
Q
, the monodromy operator of
\tilde{Q}
for the fine-tuned stability condition can be written in terms of a product over primitive elements of
\Gamma_{+}
, as:

\mathbb{M}_{\tilde{Q}}(\tilde{\zeta}=F^{*}(\zeta))=\prod^{\curvearrowright}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{+}\\
\gamma\,\,\text{primitive}\end{subarray}}\exp\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}|F_{*}(\tilde{\gamma})=\ell\gamma}\bar{\Omega}(\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\zeta})\,\tilde{e}_{\tilde{\gamma}}\right).

(6.23)

Here we put in the same exponent the generators
\tilde{e}_{\tilde{\gamma}}
for the charges
\tilde{\gamma}
that are sent to the same
\gamma
of
Q
, allowing for the possibility that the latter might not be primitive. Then, using the orbit-stabiliser theorem, we can write the exponent as a sum over
\mathbb{G}
-orbits
[\tilde{\gamma}]
as

\exp\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{[\tilde{\gamma}]|F_{*}(\tilde{\gamma})=\ell\gamma}\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}}\frac{\bar{\Omega}(g\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\zeta})}{|\text{Stab}(\tilde{\gamma})|}\,\tilde{e}_{g\tilde{\gamma}}\right),

(6.24)

with
\text{Stab}(\tilde{\gamma})\subset\mathbb{G}
the stabilizer group of
\tilde{\gamma}
. Using (
6.22
), we can express this in terms of the
\mathbb{G}
-invariant elements
\tilde{e}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{\gamma}}
:

\exp\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{[\tilde{\gamma}]|F_{*}(\tilde{\gamma})=\ell\gamma}\frac{\bar{\Omega}(\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\zeta})}{|\text{Stab}(\tilde{\gamma})|}\,\tilde{e}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{\gamma}}\right).

(6.25)

Finally, writing the sum over orbits
[\tilde{\gamma}]
as a sum over distinct pre-images
\tilde{\gamma}
of
\ell\gamma
gives us

\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{G}|}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}|F_{*}(\tilde{\gamma})=\ell\gamma}\bar{\Omega}(\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\zeta})\,\tilde{e}^{\mathbb{G}}_{\tilde{\gamma}}\right)\penalty\ .

(6.26)

Relating the monodromy operators.

Given the above, acting with the surjective homomorphism (
6.7
) on
\mathbb{M}_{\tilde{Q}}
and using the fact that the
e_{\ell\gamma}
’s commute, we find:

\mathfrak{f}^{\mathbb{G}}_{*}(\mathbb{M}_{\tilde{Q}})=\prod^{\curvearrowright}_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{+}}\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbb{G}|}\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}|F_{*}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma}\bar{\Omega}(\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\zeta})\,e_{\gamma}\right).

(6.27)

Comparing with
\mathbb{M}_{Q}
given in (
2.36
), we see that this is precisely of the desired form to derive the covering relation (
4.35
) for rational BPS invariants.
To complete the argument, we need to demonstrate that

\mathfrak{f}^{\mathbb{G}}_{*}(\mathbb{M}_{\tilde{Q}}(F^{\ast}(\zeta)))=\mathbb{M}_{Q}(\zeta)

(6.28)

for at least one choice
\zeta
. Since
\mathbb{M}_{\tilde{Q}}
and
\mathbb{M}_{Q}
are independent of the stability condition, this would establish (
4.35
) for every
\zeta
.
While proving (
6.28
) is an open question for Galois covers of arbitrary quivers, it can be established for large families of quivers
Q
where ‘easy’ stability conditions exist, including:

•

Quivers without loops
: One can choose the FI parameters such that
\langle\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}\rangle\,(\zeta_{i}-\zeta_{j})<0
for all pairs
i,j\in Q_{0}
. As a result, the only non-vanishing BPS invariants are
\Omega(\gamma_{j})
with
\gamma_{j}
the charge associated to
j\in Q_{0}
. Since the Galois cover
\tilde{Q}
has precisely
|\mathbb{G}|
nodes for each node of
Q
, the relation (
4.35
) is satisfied and
\mathfrak{f}^{\mathbb{G}}_{*}
indeed relates the KS monodromies
\mathbb{M}_{\tilde{Q}}
and
\mathbb{M}_{Q}
for this choice of
\zeta
. Then, by wall-crossing invariance, it holds for any choice
\zeta
.

•

Quivers for which empty attractor chambers can be established
: The attractor point
\zeta^{\star}

2
;
11
, or self-stability
18
, is a special choice of FI parameter
\zeta
determined by the quiver
Q
and dimension vector
N
,

\zeta^{\star}_{i}=-\sum_{j\in Q_{0}}\langle\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}\rangle\,N_{j}\penalty\ .

(6.29)

For Calabi–Yau quivers based on del Pezzo surfaces or
D_{S^{1}}E_{n}
quivers, it can be shown that the expected dimension of the moduli space is negative for
\zeta^{\star}_{i}
, such that the corresponding BPS invariant vanishes
11
. Since the wall-crossing away from
\zeta^{\star}_{i}
is governed by the Lie algebra, the Lie algebra homomorphism again ensures the relation (
4.35
). This argument does not hold for charges in the kernel of
\langle-,\gamma\rangle
for all
\gamma
, whose BPS invariants are independent of
\zeta
. These charges include multiples of
\delta=\sum_{j\in Q_{0}}\gamma_{j}
, or D0-branes from the string theory perspective. For these charges, one can compare the attractor invariants determined in
92
to verify (
4.35
).

Symmetric Galois covers and monodromy operators.

Symmetric Galois covers are a special case, where we expect that further relations can be established using the homomorphisms of subsection
6.2
. Recall that the BPS invariants of symmetric Galois covers
\tilde{Q}
are invariant under small perturbation of
\tilde{\zeta}
, which facilitates their evaluation. Moreover, the homomorphism
\mathfrak{f}_{*}^{\mathbb{G}}
factorizes,
\mathfrak{f}_{*}^{\mathbb{G}}=\mathfrak{\underline{f}_{*}}\circ\mathfrak{\underline{h}}
. Denoting by
\underline{\mathbb{M}}_{\tilde{Q}}
the KS monodromy operator in terms of the element
\underline{e}_{\gamma}
, we have the maps

\begin{split}&\mathfrak{\underline{h}}:\qquad{\mathbb{M}}_{\tilde{Q}}\to\underline{\mathbb{M}}_{\tilde{Q}}\penalty\ ,\\
&\mathfrak{\underline{f}_{*}}:\qquad\underline{\mathbb{M}}_{\tilde{Q}}\to{\mathbb{M}}_{Q}\penalty\ .\end{split}

(6.30)

For symmetric covers, we have proposed the relation (
4.46
) for the refined BPS invariants. Similarly to the above discussion leading to (
6.28
), this relation can be understood from a homomorphism mapping the refined monodromy operator
{\mathbb{M}}_{\tilde{Q}}(\tilde{y})
to
{\mathbb{M}}_{Q}(y)
. This map is induced by the homomorphism of non-commutative tori defined in (
6.20
). To see this, let us fix
\tilde{\zeta}=F^{*}(\zeta)
and introduce the product
\tilde{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\gamma}
over all charges
\tilde{\gamma}
with
F_{*}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma
defined as

\tilde{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\gamma}=\exp\left(\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}|F_{*}(\tilde{\gamma})=\gamma}\frac{\Omega(\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{y};\tilde{\zeta})}{n}\frac{\hat{\underline{e}}_{n\gamma}}{\tilde{y}^{n}-\tilde{y}^{-n}}\right)\penalty\ .

(6.31)

Combined with the assignment (
4.45
) of non-trivial single-centred indices and the following relation for
\tilde{y}^{1/|\mathbb{G}|}=y
:

\frac{\sum_{\alpha=0}^{|\mathbb{G}|-1}\tilde{y}^{(2\alpha+1-|\mathbb{G}|)/|\mathbb{G}|}}{\tilde{y}-\tilde{y}^{-1}}=\frac{1}{y-y^{-1}}\penalty\ ,

(6.32)

one verifies that, for
\gamma_{j}\in Q_{0}
,

\mathfrak{f}_{*}(\tilde{\underline{\mathcal{U}}}_{\gamma_{j}})=\mathcal{U}_{\gamma_{j}}\penalty\ ,

(6.33)

since
\Omega^{Q}(\gamma_{j},\zeta)=1
. This confirms the relation (
4.46
) for these simple dimension vectors, and indeed this was our original motivation to assign single-centred invariants as given in (
4.45
). Using the same arguments as for the bullet points above, one can then argue that (
4.46
) also holds for general charge vectors of those families of quivers.

Finally, we comment on the action of
\mathfrak{\underline{f}}^{*}
. Since this map is not surjective, it does not provide a map from
\mathbb{M}_{Q}
to
{\mathbb{\underline{M}}}_{\tilde{Q}}
, in general, and therefore cannot be used as a general strategy to verify relations between BPS invariants. Nonetheless, it can be useful in specific instances where the support of the BPS invariants of
\tilde{Q}
is such that all the relevant information is encoded in an appropriate subset of the KS algebra. We see in section
7.1
below that the Galois pair of the 4d
\mathcal{N}=2

SU(2)

N_{f}=0
and
N_{f}=2
BPS quivers gives one such example.
