Entropy and Minimax Risk of Hypoelliptic Pseudodifferential Operators
Thomas Allard, Helmut Bölcskei

Abstract
We characterize the entropy and minimax risk of a broad class of compact pseudodifferential operators. Under suitable decay and regularity conditions on the symbol, we combine a Weyl-type asymptotic relation between the eigenvalue-counting function and the phase-space volume of the symbol with a general correspondence between spectral quantities, entropy, and minimax risk for compact operators. This approach yields explicit asymptotic formulae for both entropy and minimax risk directly in terms of the symbol. As an application, we derive sharp entropy and minimax risk asymptotics for unit balls in Sobolev spaces on unbounded domains, thereby extending Pinsker’s theorem for Sobolev classes beyond the bounded-domain setting, and showing that the sharp asymptotic constants are determined by phase-space geometry rather than domain geometry.

Introduction

Metric entropy provides a natural measure of the massiveness of classes of mathematical objects—such as functions, dynamical systems, or statistical estimators—by quantifying the number of bits required to approximate their elements uniformly to a given precision.
This concept plays a central role in a wide range of mathematical fields, including approximation theory

Minimax risk in non-parametric estimation provides a complementary notion of complexity, quantifying the intrinsic difficulty of recovering elements of a class from noisy observations; see, e.g.,
[

36
,
47

. While metric entropy and minimax risk are closely related, existing characterizations typically treat them through separate analytical frameworks and under restrictive structural assumptions. The present paper develops a unified framework for characterizing the
entropy and minimax risk of a large class of compact pseudodifferential operators. Explicit asymptotic characterizations are derived in terms of the associated operator symbols. These quantities characterize operator compactness by describing, respectively, the effective size of the operator image of the unit ball and the intrinsic difficulty of recovering elements of this image from noisy measurements

We build on the well-known observation that images of pseudodifferential operators are often localized in phase space, with localization properties governed by the associated operator symbols; see, for example, the discussion of uncertainty principles and phase-space concentration in
[
25

. This observation motivates characterizing the metric entropy and minimax risk of operator images as a natural and effective way to study the complexity of sets of phase-space-localized signals. Such a viewpoint was adopted by the authors in
[

This approach yields improvements over the previously best-known entropy characterizations for both the Landau–Pollak–Slepian operator and Sobolev balls.

The central contribution of the present paper is to expand this localization-based methodology by dispensing with explicit ellipsoidal structure and developing a symbol-based framework that applies to broad classes of compact pseudodifferential operators, thereby enabling a unified analysis of entropy and minimax risk under general phase-space localization conditions encoded by symbol decay and regularity.

For compact pseudodifferential operators with sufficiently regular symbols, phase-space localization at the symbol level is reflected in the spectral
properties of the operator; see, e.g.,
[
25
]
for the underlying microlocal principle.
A spectral formulation is therefore natural. In the analysis of entropy and minimax risk for compact linear operators, it suffices to restrict attention to operators that are positive and self-adjoint. This follows from the fact that any compact operator admits a polar decomposition, and that metric entropy and minimax risk depend only on the geometry of the image of the unit ball, which is preserved under the isometry induced by the polar decomposition

The goal of the present paper is to extend this spectral-reduction strategy to operator classes for which the asymptotic behavior of the eigenvalue-counting function can be derived from symbol-level information, notably compact pseudodifferential operators.

An important consequence of our results is a conceptual extension of Pinsker’s

In pure mathematics, pseudodifferential operators play a fundamental role in partial differential equations and microlocal analysis

The mapping (

Many properties of pseudodifferential operators relevant to spectral asymptotics are naturally expressed at the level of the symbol. In particular, for symbols that are positive and decay at infinity,

Entropy and Minimax Risk of Compact Hypoelliptic Pseudodifferential Operators

We recall the definition of metric entropy for compact sets and the associated notion of entropy for compact linear operators.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable real Hilbert space and let
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{H}
be a compact set.
For
\varepsilon>0
, an
\varepsilon
-covering of
\mathcal{K}
is a finite set

\{x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\}\subset\mathcal{H}
with the property that, for every

x\in\mathcal{K}
, there exists an index
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
such that

\|x-x_{i}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\varepsilon.

The
\varepsilon
-covering number
N(\varepsilon;\mathcal{K},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}})
is defined as the cardinality of a smallest such
\varepsilon
-covering; the metric entropy of
\mathcal{K}
is the natural logarithm of the
\varepsilon
-covering number.

Given a compact linear operator
T\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
, the entropy of
T
is defined as the metric entropy of the closure of the image of the unit ball
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{H}}\subset\mathcal{H}
under
T
, namely

H_{T}(\varepsilon)\coloneqq\ln N\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\varepsilon;\overline{T(\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{H}})},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}}}\right).

(12)

Since
T
is compact, the set
\overline{T(\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{H}})}
is compact in
\mathcal{H}
, and the right-hand side of (
12
) is therefore finite for every
\varepsilon>0
.

The symbols treated in the classical literature on spectral asymptotics for
pseudodifferential operators are predominantly drawn from the class

\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
of positive-order hypoelliptic symbols;
see Theorem
7
and the review of the standard spectral theory for
hypoelliptic pseudodifferential operators in
Appendix
A
. In this regime, the associated operators are necessarily
non-compact. Consequently, their entropy is not well defined and their minimax
risk does not converge to zero.
By contrast, operators whose symbols lie in
\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{-}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})

can be extended to compact operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
; see, for example,
[
53
, Theorem 24.4]
.
The results developed here build on spectral asymptotics for such compact
hypoelliptic operators, recalled in Theorem
8
.

The asymptotic formulae derived below are obtained under the assumption that the
volume function
V_{\sigma}
is regularly varying at zero with negative index.
This assumption excludes highly irregular decay behavior while encompassing
the polynomial decay rates that arise naturally in hypoelliptic
pseudodifferential calculus, as well as mild logarithmic perturbations thereof. Its role is to ensure sufficient
regularity of the associated eigenvalue-counting function to allow for a precise
evaluation of the leading-order asymptotics in the integral characterizations of entropy
and minimax risk.

More concretely, regular variation of the volume function
V_{\sigma}
implies
condition (RC) in
[
5
, Theorem 2]
, allowing one to pass from spectral information to the entropy and minimax-risk asymptotics
in (
9
)–(
11
).
Background on regular variation and a detailed proof of the implication

‘
V_{\sigma}
regularly varying
\Rightarrow
condition (RC)
’
can be found in

[
9
]
and in

[
5
, Appendix C]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{-}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
be a strictly positive hypoelliptic symbol, and
assume that the associated volume function
V_{\sigma}
is regularly varying at
zero. Then,

H_{\sigma}(\varepsilon)\sim\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\frac{V_{\sigma}(\lambda)}{\lambda}\,\mathop{}\!d\lambda,\qquad\varepsilon\to 0,

(13)

where
H_{\sigma}
denotes the entropy of the operator associated with
\sigma
,
independently of whether
\sigma
is quantized using the left, right, or Weyl
quantization. Moreover, if the operator
T_{\sigma^{-1}}
is invertible, then

H_{T^{-1}_{\sigma^{-1}}}(\varepsilon)\sim H_{\sigma}(\varepsilon),\qquad\varepsilon\to 0.

(14)

Proof.

See Section
4.1
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1
is carried out within the standard
hypoelliptic pseudodifferential operator framework summarized in
Appendix
A
, following
[
53
]
.
The strict positivity assumption on the symbol
\sigma
in
Theorem
1
is not essential, but it simplifies the proof by
ensuring that the symbol is invertible. A strategy for removing this assumption
proceeds as follows. By hypoellipticity, the symbol
\sigma
has a fixed sign
outside a sufficiently large ball in phase space; without loss of generality,
this sign may be taken to be positive. One may therefore modify
\sigma
inside
this ball so as to make it strictly positive everywhere, without affecting the
asymptotic behavior of the associated volume function
V_{\sigma}
, and hence
without altering the resulting entropy asymptotics. Consequently, the additional (strict) positivity assumption in
Theorem
1
entails no loss of generality for the problems
considered here. Moreover, all symbols arising in the applications discussed in
Section
3
are strictly positive.

The following heuristic motivates the use of (
8
) in the
derivation of the entropy and minimax-risk asymptotics
(
9
) and (
10
). Guided by the
uncertainty principle, one expects the eigenfunctions of
T_{\sigma}
to be
essentially localized in phase space on regions of unit volume. Within such a
localized region centered at a point
(x_{0},\omega_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, the symbol

\sigma
may be regarded as approximately constant, with value

\sigma(x_{0},\omega_{0})
.

Under this approximation, the number of eigenvalues exceeding a level

\lambda>0
is expected to be proportional to the number of disjoint unit-volume
regions of phase space centered at points
(x,\omega)
for which

\sigma(x,\omega)>\lambda
. This leads naturally to the volume-based relation
(
8
). A related heuristic for elliptic operators is
discussed by Fefferman in
[
25
]
. Of course, the
argument above is purely heuristic; a central contribution of the present work is
to identify precise conditions under which the insertion of
(
8
) into the entropy and minimax-risk integrals can be
rigorously justified in the compact, symbol-decay setting considered here.

The volume principle underlying (
8
) is reminiscent of the
approximate diagonalization results in underspread operator theory

[
37
,
8
,
48
,
22
,
30
,
45
,
44
]
, in that
both frameworks interpret the Weyl symbol as an effective time–frequency transfer
function and relate spectral quantities to its pointwise values through a
phase-space localization principle.
In that setting, one expects entropy formulae of the same log-integral form as (
9
), paralleling Shannon-type expressions for frequency-selective channels. Developing a rigorous analogue of Theorem
1
for underspread operators is an interesting direction for future work.

that makes the
connection between entropy and the symbol explicit. Again, the

in Corollary

Corollary 2
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{-}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
be a strictly positive hypoelliptic symbol
and assume that the associated volume function
V_{\sigma}
is regularly varying at
zero with negative index. Then, the entropy admits the
asymptotic representation

H_{\sigma}(\varepsilon)\sim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\ln_{+}\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\sigma(x,\omega)}{\varepsilon}}}\right)\mathop{}\!dx\,\mathop{}\!d\omega,\qquad\varepsilon\to 0.

(15)

Proof.

See Section
4.2
.
∎

Recall that, after normalization by a factor
\ln(2)
, the metric entropy of a
compact set admits an information-theoretic interpretation as the number of bits
required to uniformly encode the set with accuracy
\varepsilon
.
From this perspective, relation (
15
) suggests the
following interpretation.
The phase space
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
may be partitioned into regions of unit volume, centered
at points
(x,\omega)
, each contributing to the total entropy the number of bits
required to quantize the local symbol value
\sigma(x,\omega)
.
At the heuristic level, this contribution is given by

\lceil\log_{+}(\sigma(x,\omega)/\varepsilon)\rceil
, which is equivalent in the
limit
\varepsilon\to 0
to
\log_{+}(\sigma(x,\omega)/\varepsilon)
.
Corollary
2
shows that this phase-space heuristic is in fact
correct and yields a sharp characterization of the leading-order term of the
entropy.

Relation (
15
) admits a direct phase-space
interpretation in terms of activation and deactivation of degrees of freedom.
Specifically, only those phase-space cells for which the local symbol value

\sigma(x,\omega)
exceeds the threshold
\varepsilon
contribute to the entropy,
while cells with
\sigma(x,\omega)\leq\varepsilon
are effectively inactive.
For each active cell, the contribution to the entropy is logarithmic in the
ratio
\sigma(x,\omega)/\varepsilon
. From this perspective, Corollary
2
shows that the overall entropy is
obtained by aggregating the contributions of all active phase-space cells.

Minimax risk has been studied extensively in nonparametric estimation for function
classes and compact subsets of Hilbert spaces, in particular through the analysis
of the spectral decay of associated compact operators, such as covariance
operators, embedding operators, or diagonal operators arising from basis
expansions; see, for example,

[
35
,
51
,
59
]
.
In these settings, the function class is typically represented implicitly as the
image of a unit ball under a compact linear operator, and minimax risk is
characterized in terms of the eigenvalues or singular values of that operator.

In contrast, the present framework treats minimax risk as a functional of the
operator itself and aims to characterize its asymptotic behavior directly at the
symbol level. This operator-centric perspective makes it possible to link minimax
risk explicitly to phase-space geometry and spectral asymptotics, in parallel with
the corresponding entropy analysis developed above.

To make this operator-level perspective precise, let

T\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
be a compact linear operator on a separable
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. The minimax risk of
T
is defined over the compact
set

\mathcal{K}=\overline{T(\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{H}})}\subset\mathcal{H}.

Specifically, for a noise level
\kappa>0
, it is given by

R_{T}(\kappa)\coloneqq\inf_{\hat{x}_{\kappa}}\sup_{x\in\overline{T(\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{H}})}}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim x}\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\bigl\|\hat{x}_{\kappa}(y)-x\bigr\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}}}\right],

where the observation model is

y=x+\kappa\xi,

with
\xi
a Gaussian random element in
\mathcal{H}
whose coordinates with
respect to some (and hence any) orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
are i.i.d. standard normal random variables. Here,
\mathbb{E}_{y\sim x}
is expectation with respect to the law of
y
for fixed

x
, and
\hat{x}_{\kappa}
is a measurable estimator based on the observation
y
.

The asymptotic behavior of the minimax risk then follows from
Theorem
1
via the general correspondence between metric entropy
and minimax risk developed in
[
5
]
.

Corollary 3
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{-}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
be a strictly positive hypoelliptic symbol
and assume that the associated volume function

is regularly varying at

R_{\sigma}(\kappa)\sim\kappa^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon_{\kappa}}{\sigma(x,\omega)}}}\right)_{+}\,\mathop{}\!dx\,\mathop{}\!d\omega,\qquad\kappa\to 0,

3
Applications to Sobolev Spaces

We now apply the results developed in the preceding sections to characterize the
metric entropy of unit balls in Sobolev spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This extends the
corresponding analysis for Sobolev spaces on bounded domains presented in

[
3
, Section 3.2]
. In contrast to the bounded-domain
setting, the unit ball of a classical Sobolev space on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
—as defined, for
example, in
[
56
, Definition 3.1]
or

[
11
, Section 9.1]
—is not compact with
respect to the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
metric due to translation invariance
(cf.
[
1
, Example 6.11]
). As a result, its metric
entropy is infinite for every
\varepsilon>0
.

Compactness-restoring variants of Sobolev spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such as those
involving confining potentials or weights, are classical and have been studied
extensively from the perspectives of functional analysis and spectral theory.

By modifying the Sobolev structure so as to restore compactness while
preserving the underlying notion of regularity, one obtains function classes
that fall within the scope of the present theory. Two such modifications are
particularly natural and widely used in practice: Sobolev spaces with a
confining potential (Theorem
4
) and weighted Sobolev spaces
(Theorem
5
). In both cases, the resulting spaces admit
compact embeddings into
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and can be treated directly within our framework.

We begin with Sobolev spaces equipped with a confining potential.
Fix
s>0
, let
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
be a potential function, and define

T_{\mathcal{S}}=(I+U-\Delta)^{s/2},

(18)

where
I
is the identity operator,
U
acts by pointwise multiplication with
u(\cdot)
,
and
-\Delta
is the Laplacian operator.
Throughout,
T_{\mathcal{S}}
is understood as the self-adjoint operator on

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
obtained as the closure of its action on
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
We note that
I+U-\Delta
is a Schrödinger operator; see, for example,

[
40
, Chapter 11.2]
.

When
s
is an even integer, say
s/2=k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, the operator
T_{\mathcal{S}}

coincides with the
k
-fold composition of
I+U-\Delta
with itself and is a
differential operator of order
2k
. Its principal Kohn–Nirenberg symbol is

\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}(x,\omega)=\bigl(1+u(x)+(2\pi\|\omega\|_{2})^{2}\bigr)^{s/2}.

(19)

For general
s>0
, the operator
T_{\mathcal{S}}
is defined via functional
calculus and is a pseudodifferential operator whose principal Kohn–Nirenberg
symbol is given by (
19
); see, for instance,

[
31
, Definition 3.13]
. As a concrete and widely studied example, we work with the quadratic
potential (cf.
[
53
, Section 25.3]
),

u(x)=c\,\|x\|_{2}^{2},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

with a fixed constant
c>0
.

The Sobolev space of order
s
induced by
T_{\mathcal{S}}
is defined as

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(s)}\;\coloneqq\;\overline{C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\,\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{S}}^{(s)}},\qquad\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{S}}^{(s)}\coloneqq\|T_{\mathcal{S}}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where the closure is taken with respect to the topology induced by

\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{S}}^{(s)}
, so that

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(s)}
embeds continuously into
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Equivalently,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(s)}
coincides with the domain of the
operator
T_{\mathcal{S}}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{S}}^{(s)}=\|T_{\mathcal{S}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

In this sense,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(s)}
may be viewed as a Sobolev space
of order
s
with a confining potential, encoding
s
degrees of smoothness
together with spatial localization.
The corresponding unit ball is

\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(s)}\coloneqq\bigl\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(s)}:\|T_{\mathcal{S}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1\bigr\}.

(20)

We are now in a position to characterize the metric entropy of

\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(s)}
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
s,c>0
.
The metric entropy of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(s)}
obeys

H\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\varepsilon;\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(s)}}}\right)\sim\frac{s\,\omega_{2d}}{2d(2\pi\sqrt{c})^{d}}\,\varepsilon^{-2d/s},\qquad\varepsilon\to 0,

(21)

and the corresponding minimax risk admits the asymptotic expansion

R_{\kappa}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(s)}}}\right)\sim\frac{d+s}{d}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{d\,s\,\omega_{2d}\,\kappa^{2}}{(2\pi\sqrt{c})^{d}(d+s)(2d+s)}}}\right)^{\frac{s}{d+s}},\qquad\kappa\to 0.

(22)

Proof.

See Section
4.4
.
∎

Theorem
4
, in particular the minimax-risk asymptotic
(
22
), extends Pinsker’s theorem to Sobolev spaces on
unbounded domains. Pinsker’s original result was established in

[
51
]
; general reviews can be found in

[
47
,
36
,
59
]
,
and an extension to arbitrary bounded domains is provided in

[
5
, Theorem 9]
. The result obtained here
shows that Pinsker’s principle is not confined to bounded domains: after
restoring compactness through spatial confinement, the minimax risk remains
governed by the same structural mechanism identified by Pinsker, with the sharp
asymptotic constant now determined by phase-space volume rather than boundary
effects. This indicates that Pinsker’s theorem reflects an underlying geometric
principle of statistical estimation, rather than an artifact of bounded domains
or specific boundary conditions.

As a second application, we consider weighted Sobolev spaces, defined following

[
31
, Section 3.3]
.
Fix
s,r,c>0
, set
u(x)=c\|x\|_{2}^{2}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let

T_{\mathcal{W}}\coloneqq(I-\Delta)^{s/2}(I+U)^{r/2},

where
I
is the identity operator,
U
acts by pointwise multiplication with
u(\cdot)
,
and
-\Delta
is the Laplacian operator.
As before,
T_{\mathcal{W}}
is interpreted as a pseudodifferential operator; in
this case, its principal Kohn–Nirenberg symbol is given by

\sigma_{\mathcal{W}}(x,\omega)=\bigl(1+(2\pi\|\omega\|_{2})^{2}\bigr)^{s/2}\bigl(1+c\|x\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{r/2}.

(23)

The weighted Sobolev space of regularity
s
and weight exponent
r
induced by

T_{\mathcal{W}}
is defined as

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{W}}^{(s,r)}\;\coloneqq\;\overline{C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\,\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{W}}^{(s,r)}},\qquad\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{W}}^{(s,r)}\coloneqq\|T_{\mathcal{W}}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where the closure is taken with respect to the topology induced by

\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{W}}^{(s,r)}
, so that

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{W}}^{(s,r)}
embeds continuously into
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Equivalently,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{W}}^{(s,r)}
coincides with the domain of the
operator
T_{\mathcal{W}}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}}^{(s,r)}=\|T_{\mathcal{W}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

In this sense,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{W}}^{(s,r)}
may be viewed as a weighted
Sobolev space of regularity
s
, where the weight exponent
r
enforces spatial
localization.
The corresponding unit ball is

\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{W}}^{(s,r)}\coloneqq\bigl\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{W}}^{(s,r)}:\|T_{\mathcal{W}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1\bigr\}.

(24)

We are now ready to characterize the metric entropy of

\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{W}}^{(s,r)}
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
s,r,c>0
.
The metric entropy of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{W}}^{(s,r)}
obeys

H\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\varepsilon;\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{W}}^{(s,r)},\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}}\right)\sim\frac{\omega_{d}^{2}}{(2\pi\sqrt{c})^{d}}\begin{cases}\Xi_{r,s,d}\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{d}{\min\{r,s\}}},\quad&\text{if }r\neq s,\\[7.11317pt]
\varepsilon^{-\frac{d}{s}}\ln\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\varepsilon^{-1}}}\right),\quad&\text{if }r=s,\end{cases}\quad\varepsilon\to 0,

where, for
r\neq s
,

\Xi_{r,s,d}\coloneqq\frac{\min\{r,s\}\,\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{d\,|s-r|}{2\min\{r,s\}}}}\right)\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{d}{2}}}\right)}{2\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{d\max\{r,s\}}{2\min\{r,s\}}}}\right)},

(25)

and
\Gamma
denotes Euler’s Gamma function.

Proof.

See Section
4.5
.
∎

The expression (
25
) for the constant appearing in
Theorem
5
can often be simplified by exploiting standard
identities for the Gamma function
[
7
]
.
In particular, when the dimension is even, say
d=2k
with
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
the Gamma factors reduce to factorials and one obtains

\Xi_{r,s,2k}=\frac{(k-1)!\,\min\{r,s\}^{\,k+1}}{2\prod_{j=1}^{k}\bigl(k\max\{r,s\}-j\min\{r,s\}\bigr)},\qquad r\neq s.

For example, in dimension
d=2
this yields

\Xi_{r,s,2}=\frac{\min\{r,s\}^{2}}{2\,(\max\{r,s\}-\min\{r,s\})}.

Theorem

also yields sharp entropy asymptotics for
compact embeddings between weighted Sobolev spaces defined through the
operators
T_{\mathcal{W}}
. In particular, for parameters
s_{1}>s_{2}
and

r_{1}>r_{2}
, consider the inclusion map

I\colon\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{W}}^{(s_{1},r_{1})}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{W}}^{(s_{2},r_{2})},

which is compact by Rellich’s theorem
(see
[
31
, Theorem 2.17 and Exercise 2.12]
).
The special case treated in Theorem
5

corresponds to the choice
s_{2}=r_{2}=0
. The same analysis as in the proof of Theorem
5
applies to the
embedding operator

with the parameters
(r,s)
replaced by

(r_{1}-r_{2},s_{1}-s_{2})

H_{I}(\varepsilon)\sim\frac{\omega_{d}^{2}}{(2\pi\sqrt{c})^{d}}\begin{cases}\Xi_{r_{1}-r_{2},s_{1}-s_{2},d}\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{d}{\min\{r_{1}-r_{2},s_{1}-s_{2}\}}},&\text{if }r_{1}+s_{2}\neq r_{2}+s_{1},\\[7.11317pt]
\varepsilon^{-\frac{d}{r_{1}-r_{2}}}\ln\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\varepsilon^{-1}}}\right),&\text{if }r_{1}+s_{2}=r_{2}+s_{1},\end{cases}\qquad\varepsilon\to 0,

where the constant
\Xi_{r,s,d}
is defined as in
Theorem

in a more general setting—for instance for weighted Besov and
Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
[

.
However, these results do not identify the leading constants.
The present approach provides these constants explicitly in the weighted
Sobolev setting.

To derive the entropy asymptotics, we first reduce the Weyl quantization

By the polar decomposition

(26)

Our strategy is to invoke the spectral asymptotics of

To this end, we choose the weight functions

\phi(z)=\varphi(z)=\bigl(1+\|z\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{\rho/2},\qquad w(z)=\sigma(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}.

(27)

Once it is verified that this choice satisfies conditions

|g^{\prime}(t)|\leq K_{0},\qquad t\in[0,1].

(41)

If
\|z\|_{2}<1
, then the assumption of this case implies

\|z^{\prime}\|_{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}.

Consequently, for all
t\in[0,1]
, the points
z+tz^{\prime}
remain in a fixed compact
subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
.
Since
\sigma\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is strictly positive, the function

\nabla\sigma/\sigma
is continuous and therefore bounded on this set.
It follows that
|g^{\prime}(t)|
is uniformly bounded for all
t\in[0,1]
,
which yields (
41
).
Integrating (
41
)
over the interval
[0,1]

gives

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\ln\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\sigma(z+z^{\prime})}{\sigma(z)}}}\right)}}\right|\leq K_{0},

(42)

and therefore

\sigma(z+z^{\prime})\leq e^{K_{0}}\,\sigma(z).

Writing
z=(x,\omega)
and
z^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
, and recalling that
w=\sigma
, we hence get

w(x+x^{\prime},\omega+\omega^{\prime})\leq e^{K_{0}}\,w(x,\omega)\leq e^{K_{0}}\,w(x,\omega)\,\bigl[1+\|x^{\prime}\|_{2}\,\phi(x,\omega)+\|\omega^{\prime}\|_{2}\,\varphi(x,\omega)\bigr],

which is exactly (
128
) with
C=e^{K_{0}}
and
\zeta=1
.

Case 2:

\|z^{\prime}\|_{2}\geq\|z\|_{2}^{\rho}/2
. By hypoellipticity, there exist constants
R>0
and
C_{1}>0

with

\sigma(z)\geq C_{1}\,\bigl(1+\|z\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{-m_{+}/2},\qquad\|z\|_{2}\geq R.

Since
\sigma
is continuous and strictly positive on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, it attains a
strictly positive minimum on the closed ball

B_{R}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}:\|z\|_{2}\leq R\}
.
Hence, there exists
C_{2}>0
satisfying

\sigma(z)\geq C_{2}\,\bigl(1+\|z\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{-m_{+}/2},\qquad\|z\|_{2}\leq R.

These estimates imply that there exists a constant
K_{1}>0
such that

\sigma(z)\geq K_{1}\,\bigl(1+\|z\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{-m_{+}/2},\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}.

Using this lower bound in conjunction with the corresponding upper bound from
(
124
), we obtain

\frac{\sigma(z+z^{\prime})}{\sigma(z)}\leq K_{2}\,\frac{\bigl(1+\|z+z^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{-m_{-}/2}}{\bigl(1+\|z\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{-m_{+}/2}},

for some constant
K_{2}>0
.
Applying Peetre’s inequality (
32
) to the numerator gives

\frac{\sigma(z+z^{\prime})}{\sigma(z)}\leq K_{3}\bigl(1+\|z^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{m_{-}/2}\bigl(1+\|z\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{(m_{+}-m_{-})/2},

for some constant
K_{3}>0
. Using the assumption of this case,

\|z^{\prime}\|_{2}\geq\|z\|_{2}^{\rho}/2
, we can bound

\bigl(1+\|z\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{(m_{+}-m_{-})/2}\leq C\,\bigl(1+\|z^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{(m_{+}-m_{-})/(2\rho)}
,
which leads to

\sigma(z+z^{\prime})\leq K_{4}\,\sigma(z)\bigl(1+\|z^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{m_{-}}{2}+\frac{m_{+}-m_{-}}{2\rho}},

(43)

for some constant
K_{4}>0
. Writing
z=(x,\omega)
and
z^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
, we note that

1+\|z^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}=1+\|x^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\omega^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\bigl(1+\|x^{\prime}\|_{2}+\|\omega^{\prime}\|_{2}\bigr)^{2}\leq\bigl(1+\|x^{\prime}\|_{2}\,\phi(x,\omega)+\|\omega^{\prime}\|_{2}\,\varphi(x,\omega)\bigr)^{2}.

Recalling that
w=\sigma
, this implies

w(x+x^{\prime},\omega+\omega^{\prime})\leq K_{4}\,w(x,\omega)\bigl[1+\|x^{\prime}\|_{2}\,\phi(x,\omega)+\|\omega^{\prime}\|_{2}\,\varphi(x,\omega)\bigr]^{\,m_{-}+\frac{m_{+}-m_{-}}{\rho}},

which is precisely the
(\phi,\varphi)
-temperateness condition
(
128
) with

and
\zeta=m_{-}+(m_{+}-m_{-})/\rho
.

Verifying (N).

By hypoellipticity of
\sigma\in\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{-m_{+},-m_{-}}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})

(recall (

there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2},R>0

C_{1}\bigl(1+\|z\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{-m_{+}/2}\leq\sigma(z)\leq C_{2}\bigl(1+\|z\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{-m_{-}/2},\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\ \text{with}\ \|z\|_{2}\geq R.

(44)

With the choice

, the bounds in (

C_{2}^{-\frac{2\rho}{m_{-}}}\,w(z)^{\frac{2\rho}{m_{-}}}\leq(1+\|z\|_{2}^{2})^{-\rho}\leq C_{1}^{-\frac{2\rho}{m_{+}}}\,w(z)^{\frac{2\rho}{m_{+}}},\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\ \text{with}\ \|z\|_{2}\geq R.

(45)

Since

is continuous and strictly positive, its restriction to the
closed ball
\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}:\|z\|_{2}\leq R\}
attains a positive minimum and a finite

C_{-}\coloneqq\min_{\|z\|_{2}\leq R}w(z),\qquad C_{+}\coloneqq\max_{\|z\|_{2}\leq R}w(z).

Then, for all

with
\|z\|_{2}\leq R

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+R^{2}}}\right)^{-\rho}C_{+}^{-\frac{2\rho}{m_{-}}}w(z)^{\frac{2\rho}{m_{-}}}\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+R^{2}}}\right)^{-\rho}\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+\|z\|_{2}^{2}}}\right)^{-\rho}\leq 1\leq C_{-}^{-\frac{2\rho}{m_{+}}}w(z)^{\frac{2\rho}{m_{+}}}.

(46)

Combining (
45
) and (
46
), and setting

K^{\prime}\coloneqq\min\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{C_{2}^{-\frac{2\rho}{m_{-}}},(1+R^{2})^{-\rho}C_{+}^{-\frac{2\rho}{m_{-}}}}}\right\},\qquad K\coloneqq\max\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{C_{1}^{-\frac{2\rho}{m_{+}}},C_{-}^{-\frac{2\rho}{m_{+}}}}}\right\},

\gamma^{\prime}=2\rho/m_{-}
, and
\gamma=2\rho/m_{+}
,
we obtain

K^{\prime}w(z)^{\gamma^{\prime}}\leq\phi^{-1}(z)\,\varphi^{-1}(z)\leq Kw(z)^{\gamma},\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d},

which is exactly condition (

This completes the verification of conditions
(H1)
–
(N)
and hence
establishes (
13
) for the Weyl quantization.
We next show that the same entropy asymptotics hold for the left and right

To this end, we first state a technical lemma, which is proved at the end of this

Lemma 6
.

Let
\rho_{1}>0
and
m>0
.
Let
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
be compact linear operators on

such that

T_{1}=T_{2}(I+K)+K_{-\infty},

for some

and
K_{-\infty}\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

Assume further that there exists a strictly positive hypoelliptic symbol

\sigma\in\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{-}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
whose associated volume function

is
regularly varying at zero, and that either
T_{1}
or
T_{2}
is the Weyl quantization
of
\sigma
.
Then

H_{T_{1}}(\varepsilon)\sim H_{T_{2}}(\varepsilon),\qquad\varepsilon\to 0.

(47)

Invariance under change of quantization.

We establish invariance of the entropy asymptotics under a change of
quantization by treating explicitly the Weyl–left case; the Weyl–right case
follows by the same arguments. Let
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
denote the left and Weyl quantizations of the symbol
\sigma
,
respectively, and let
\sigma_{1}
be the Weyl symbol of
T_{1}
.
Since
T_{1}
is the left quantization of a symbol

\sigma\in\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{-m_{+},-m_{-}}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
,
Proposition 25.1 in
[
53
]
implies that
the corresponding Weyl symbol
\sigma_{1}
also belongs to

\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{-m_{+},-m_{-}}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.

Since
T_{2}
is the Weyl quantization of a hypoelliptic symbol in

\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{-m_{+},-m_{-}}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, Theorem 25.1 in

[
53
]
guarantees the existence of a right
parametrix
B_{2}
with symbol
b_{2}\in\text{H}\Gamma^{m_{-},m_{+}}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
such that

T_{2}B_{2}=I+S,\qquad S\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(48)

We may assume, without loss of generality, that the symbol
b_{2}
is nowhere vanishing on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
.
Indeed, by hypoellipticity and the lower bound in (
124
), there exists
R>0
such that

b_{2}(z)\neq 0
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
with
\|z\|_{2}\geq R
. If
b_{2}
vanishes at some point in the compact region

\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\mid\|z\|_{2}<R\}
, we can construct a function

b_{2}^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, supported in

\{\,z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\mid\|z\|_{2}\leq 2R\,\}
, such that

b_{2}^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq b_{2}+b_{2}^{\prime}\in\text{H}\Gamma^{m_{-},m_{+}}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})

and
b_{2}^{\prime\prime}(z)\neq 0
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. Let
B_{2}^{\prime}
and
B_{2}^{\prime\prime}
denote the Weyl quantizations of

b_{2}^{\prime}
and
b_{2}^{\prime\prime}
, respectively. Replacing
B_{2}
by
B_{2}^{\prime\prime}
in (
48
)
yields

T_{2}B_{2}^{\prime\prime}=I+S+T_{2}B_{2}^{\prime},

where
T_{2}B_{2}^{\prime}\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
since
b_{2}^{\prime}
is compactly supported. Absorbing this term
into the remainder, we obtain a representation of the form

T_{2}B_{2}^{\prime\prime}=I+S^{\prime},\qquad S^{\prime}\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

with a parametrix whose symbol does not vanish on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
.
Composing (
48
) on the right with
(T_{1}-T_{2})
yields

T_{2}B_{2}(T_{1}-T_{2})=(T_{1}-T_{2})+S(T_{1}-T_{2}).

Because
S\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
T_{1}-T_{2}
is a pseudodifferential operator,
and since
\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a two-sided ideal in the
pseudodifferential calculus, the composition
S(T_{1}-T_{2})
belongs to

\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
; see, for example,
[
31
, Theorem 4.22]
or
[
53
, Theorem 23.6]
.
Letting

S^{\prime}\coloneqq-\,S(T_{1}-T_{2})\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

we may therefore rewrite the above identity as

T_{2}B_{2}(T_{1}-T_{2})=(T_{1}-T_{2})-S^{\prime}.

Rearranging terms yields

T_{1}=T_{2}\bigl(I+B_{2}(T_{1}-T_{2})\bigr)+S^{\prime}.

(49)

With
K\coloneqq B_{2}(T_{1}-T_{2})
and
K_{-\infty}=S^{\prime}
, we see that
(
49
) is of the form required in
Lemma
6
.
Therefore, once it is shown that

K\in\Psi^{-m}_{\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
\rho_{1}>0
and
m>0
,
the lemma applies and yields

H_{T_{1}}(\varepsilon)\sim H_{T_{2}}(\varepsilon),\qquad\varepsilon\to 0.

(50)

We next prove that in fact
K\in\Psi^{-2\rho}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
This will be done by first showing that
\sigma^{-1}(\sigma_{1}-\sigma)\in\Gamma^{-2\rho}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.
Recall that
\sigma_{1}
denotes the Weyl symbol of the left quantization
T_{1}
.
By
[
53
, Theorem 23.3]
, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
there
exists a remainder

r_{N}\in\Gamma^{-m_{-}-2(N+1)\rho}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
such that

\sigma_{1}(x,\omega)-\sigma(x,\omega)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\\
1\leq|\alpha|\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\alpha!\,2^{|\alpha|}}\partial_{\omega}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\sigma(x,\omega)+r_{N}(x,\omega),\qquad x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(51)

Since
\sigma
is strictly positive, division by
\sigma
is well-defined, and
(
51
) yields

\frac{\sigma_{1}-\sigma}{\sigma}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\\
1\leq|\alpha|\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\alpha!\,2^{|\alpha|}}\frac{\partial_{\omega}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\sigma}{\sigma}+\frac{r_{N}}{\sigma}.

(52)

We treat the two terms on the right-hand side of (
52
) separately.
First, since
\sigma^{-1}\in\text{H}\Gamma^{m_{-},m_{+}}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, application of

[
53
, Lemma 25.1]
allows us to conclude that

\frac{r_{N}}{\sigma}\in\Gamma^{\,m_{+}-m_{-}-2(N+1)\rho}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}).

Choosing
N
sufficiently large ensures

\frac{r_{N}}{\sigma}\in\Gamma^{-2\rho}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}).

(53)

Second, consider the derivative terms appearing in the finite sum in
(
52
). For each multi-index
\alpha\neq 0
, the
Leibniz rule together with the derivative bounds in
(
124
) imply that

\sigma^{-1}\,\partial_{\omega}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\sigma\in\Gamma^{-2\rho|\alpha|}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}).

Since
|\alpha|\geq 1
, we have
-2\rho|\alpha|\leq-2\rho
, and hence

\sigma^{-1}\,\partial_{\omega}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\sigma\in\Gamma^{-2\rho}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}).

(54)

Combining (
53
) and (
54
) in (
52
) yields

\sigma^{-1}(\sigma_{1}-\sigma)\in\Gamma^{-2\rho}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),

as claimed.

Returning to the parametrix identity
T_{2}B_{2}=I+S
, let
s
denote the Weyl
symbol of the operator
S
.
By the composition formula for Weyl quantization

[
53
, Theorem 23.6]
and the hypoellipticity of both

b_{2}
and
\sigma
, for every
M\in\mathbb{N}
there exist constants

C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
and a remainder

r^{\prime}_{M}\in\Gamma^{m_{+}-m_{-}-2(M+1)\rho}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
such that

\displaystyle 1+s(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\\
0\leq|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq M\end{subarray}}C_{\alpha,\beta}\,\partial_{z}^{\alpha}b_{2}(z)\,\partial_{z}^{\beta}\sigma(z)+r^{\prime}_{M}(z)

(55)

\displaystyle=b_{2}(z)\,\sigma(z)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\\
1\leq|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq M\end{subarray}}C_{\alpha,\beta}\,\frac{\partial_{z}^{\alpha}b_{2}(z)}{b_{2}(z)}\,\frac{\partial_{z}^{\beta}\sigma(z)}{\sigma(z)}+\frac{r^{\prime}_{M}(z)}{b_{2}(z)\,\sigma(z)}}}\right),

(56)

for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
.
Recall that
\sigma(z)
is non-vanishing for all

by assumption,

has been chosen to be non-vanishing on

without loss of generality.

Choosing
M
sufficiently large ensures that
r^{\prime}_{M}\in\Gamma^{-2\rho}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.
Moreover, by the Leibniz rule and hypoellipticity,

b_{2}^{-1}\,\partial^{\alpha}b_{2}\in\Gamma^{-\rho|\alpha|}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),\qquad\sigma^{-1}\,\partial^{\beta}\sigma\in\Gamma^{-\rho|\beta|}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),

for all multi-indices
\alpha,\beta
.
Consequently, the expression in parentheses in (
56
)
is of the form
1+r^{\prime}
with
r^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{-2\rho}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.
Since
r^{\prime}(z)\to 0
as
\|z\|_{2}\to\infty
, there exists
R>0
such that

|r^{\prime}(z)|\leq\tfrac{1}{2},\qquad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\text{ with }\|z\|_{2}\geq R.

We may now modify
r^{\prime}
on
\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\mid\|z\|_{2}<R\}
as follows.
Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
satisfy

\chi(z)=1\quad\text{for }z\in\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\mid\|z\|_{2}\leq R\},

and

\chi(z)=0\quad\text{for }z\in\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\mid\|z\|_{2}\geq 2R\},

and let
G\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a smooth truncation of the identity satisfying

G(t)=t\quad\text{for }|t|\leq\tfrac{1}{2},\qquad|G(t)|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\quad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

Define

\widetilde{r}^{\prime}(z)\coloneqq(1-\chi(z))\,r^{\prime}(z)+\chi(z)\,G(r^{\prime}(z)),\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}.

Then

\widetilde{r}^{\prime}-r^{\prime}=\chi\bigl(G(r^{\prime})-r^{\prime}\bigr)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),

and hence

\widetilde{r}^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{-2\rho}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),

since
r^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{-2\rho}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and

C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\subset\Gamma^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\subset\Gamma^{-2\rho}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.

\widetilde{s}-s=b_{2}\,\sigma(\widetilde{r}^{\prime}-r^{\prime})\in\Gamma^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),

since
\widetilde{r}^{\prime}-r^{\prime}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.
Hence
\widetilde{s}\in\Gamma^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, while

55
)–(
56
).

by

, and

It follows that

b_{2}(z)=\sigma(z)^{-1}\,\frac{1+s(z)}{1+r^{\prime}(z)},\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}.

Moreover,

(1+r^{\prime})^{-1}=1+q,\qquad q=-\frac{r^{\prime}}{1+r^{\prime}}.

Writing

with
H(t)\coloneqq-\frac{1}{1+t}

Therefore,

(57)

(58)

for some

,

Invertibility formula.

We now prove the second statement of Theorem

and

Using the assumed invertibility of

with that of

Applying the Weyl composition formula

[

to

and

with

.

Since

(60)

By
[

, the operator

.
Identity (

,

,

Proof of Lemma
6
.

Let
k
denote the Weyl symbol of
K
.
Since
K\in\Psi^{-m}_{\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
m>0
, we have

, hence
k
is of strictly negative order.
The constant symbol
1
belongs to
\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{0,0}_{\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.
Applying Shubin’s stability result for hypoelliptic symbols under lower-order
additive perturbations
[
53
, Lemma 25.1 (1c)]

yields

1+k\in\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{0,0}_{\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}).

Consequently, by
[
53
, Theorem 25.1]
,
the operator
I+K
admits a right parametrix, that is, there exists a
pseudodifferential operator
P\in\Psi^{0}_{\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

(I+K)P=I+R_{-\infty},

(61)

for some residual operator
R_{-\infty}\in\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

By the Weyl composition formula
[
53
, Theorem 23.6]
,
the Weyl symbol of
(I+K)P
has the form

(1+k)p+q,

where
p
is the Weyl symbol of
P
and

q\in\Gamma^{-2\rho_{1}}_{\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.
On the other hand, by (

(I+K)P

is
1+r_{-\infty}
, where
r_{-\infty}
is the symbol of
R_{-\infty}
.
Using that
k\in\Gamma^{-m}_{\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and comparing both expressions,
it follows that there exists a symbol

r_{1}\in\Gamma^{-\gamma}_{\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),\qquad\gamma\coloneqq\min\{2\rho_{1},m\},

such that

Equivalently, in operator form,

P=I+R_{1},\qquad R_{1}\in\Psi^{-\gamma}_{\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We now multiply the identity

\displaystyle T_{1}P

\displaystyle=T_{2}(I+K)P+K_{-\infty}P

\displaystyle=T_{2}(I+R_{-\infty})+K_{-\infty}P

\displaystyle=T_{2}+T_{2}R_{-\infty}+K_{-\infty}P.

Rearranging terms yields

(62)

where

since

is a two-sided ideal in the pseudodifferential

of the same structural form as the hypothesis of

Although the order

the lemma only requires the perturbation to be of strictly negative order.

We now turn to the comparison of the entropy numbers of

and

Recall that the entropy numbers

We refer to

We now estimate the term

Using the factorization

\displaystyle e_{4\lfloor n^{1/2}\rfloor}\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(R^{\prime}_{-\infty}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq e_{\lfloor n^{1/2}\rfloor}(T_{\bar{\sigma}})\,e_{\lfloor n^{1/2}\rfloor}\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(QR^{\prime}_{-\infty}}}\right)+e_{\lfloor n^{1/2}\rfloor}\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(R^{\prime\prime}_{-\infty}}}\right)e_{\lfloor n^{1/2}\rfloor}\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(R^{\prime}_{-\infty}}}\right)

Proof of Corollary

From the definition (
7

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\frac{V_{\sigma}(\lambda)}{\lambda}\,d\lambda

Appendix A
Review of Spectral Theory for Pseudodifferential Operators

This appendix reviews the spectral-theoretic material required for the
development of our results.
We begin by recalling the Kohn–Nirenberg representation of a
pseudodifferential operator.
With the Fourier transform defined by

\hat{f}(\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(y)\,e^{-2\pi i\,y\cdot\omega}\,\mathop{}\!dy,\qquad\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(120)

the operator
T_{\sigma}
associated with a symbol
\sigma
admits the integral
representation

(T_{\sigma}f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\sigma(x,\omega)\,e^{2\pi i(x-y)\cdot\omega}\,f(y)\,\mathop{}\!dy\,\mathop{}\!d\omega,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(121)

Following standard practice (see, e.g.,

[
53
, Definition 23.1]
),
we assume throughout that the symbols under consideration satisfy appropriate
regularity and growth conditions.
Specifically, for
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
(typically
N=2d
or
N=3d
below),
m\in\mathbb{R}
, and

\rho\in(0,1]
, we define the symbol class

\Gamma^{m}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as the set of real-valued functions

\sigma\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that, for every multi-index

\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
, there exists a constant
C_{\alpha}>0
with

\bigl|\partial^{\alpha}\sigma(z)\bigr|\leq C_{\alpha}\,\bigl(1+\|z\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{m-\rho|\alpha|}{2}},\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(122)

For later use, it is convenient to work with a slightly more general integral
representation than (
121
), allowing the symbol to depend
on the integration variable
y
as well.
Specifically, we consider operators of the form

(T_{\bar{\sigma}}f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\bar{\sigma}(x,y,\omega)\,e^{2\pi i(x-y)\cdot\omega}\,f(y)\,\mathop{}\!dy\,\mathop{}\!d\omega,

(123)

where
\bar{\sigma}\in\Gamma^{m}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{3d})
is referred to as the
amplitude of the operator. The precise definition of an amplitude varies somewhat across the literature;
see, for example,
[
53
, Definition 23.3]
and

[
57
, Definition 2.1]
.
The choice adopted here—namely, requiring
\bar{\sigma}
to belong to

\Gamma^{m}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{3d})
—is slightly more restrictive than

[
53
, Definition 23.3]
, but this distinction plays
no role in the arguments below. Since
\bar{\sigma}\in\Gamma^{m}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{3d})
, it satisfies
(
122
) with
N=3d
.
Operators of the form (
123
) with amplitudes
\bar{\sigma}\in\Gamma^{m}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{3d})
define the
class
\Psi^{m}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Finally, we set

\Psi^{-\infty}\;\coloneqq\;\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{R}}\Psi^{m}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

which can be shown to be independent of the parameter
\rho
; see the discussion following

[
53
, Definition 23.4]
.

The amplitude formulation (
123
) provides a
unified framework encompassing several standard quantization schemes.
The Kohn–Nirenberg representation (
121
) is recovered when
the amplitude is independent of the variable
y
, that is, when there exists a
symbol
\sigma_{L}\in\Gamma_{\rho}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
such that

\bar{\sigma}(x,y,\omega)=\sigma_{L}(x,\omega).

In this case,
\sigma_{L}
is referred to as the Kohn–Nirenberg symbol (or
left symbol) of the operator
T_{\bar{\sigma}}
.
If
\sigma_{W}\in\Gamma_{\rho}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and

\bar{\sigma}(x,y,\omega)=\sigma_{W}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\tfrac{x+y}{2},\omega}}\right),

then
T_{\bar{\sigma}}
coincides with the Weyl quantization of the symbol

\sigma_{W}
, which is therefore called the Weyl symbol of the operator.
Similarly, when the amplitude depends only on the second spatial variable,

\bar{\sigma}(x,y,\omega)=\sigma_{R}(y,\omega),\qquad\sigma_{R}\in\Gamma_{\rho}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),

the function
\sigma_{R}
is referred to as the right symbol of

T_{\bar{\sigma}}
.
Conversely, every operator
T_{\bar{\sigma}}\in\Psi_{\rho}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
admits equivalent
representations in terms of a left symbol, a Weyl symbol, and a right symbol; see

[
53
, Theorem 23.1]
.

A central theme in the spectral theory of pseudodifferential operators is that the
asymptotic behavior of the eigenvalue-counting function can be characterized in
terms of the associated symbol, independently of the chosen quantization
(Kohn–Nirenberg, Weyl, or right).
We highlight two results of this type.
The first, stated as Theorem
7
, is a classical Weyl-type result for
hypoelliptic pseudodifferential operators and is included here for context.
The second, Theorem
8
, concerns compact pseudodifferential
operators and provides the spectral characterization used in the proof of our
main result.

To state Theorem
7
, we first introduce the notion of a
hypoelliptic symbol; see, for example,

[
53
, Definition 25.1]
.
A real-valued function
\sigma\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is said to be hypoelliptic if
there are constants
C_{1},C_{2},\rho,R>0
and real numbers
m_{+}\geq m_{-}
such that,
for every multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2d}
, one can find a constant
C_{\alpha}>0

for which

C_{1}\bigl(1+\|z\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{m_{-}}{2}}\leq|\sigma(z)|\leq C_{2}\bigl(1+\|z\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{m_{+}}{2}},\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{|\partial^{\alpha}\sigma(z)|}{|\sigma(z)|}\leq C_{\alpha}\bigl(1+\|z\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)^{-\frac{\rho|\alpha|}{2}},

(124)

for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
with
\|z\|_{2}\geq R
.
We denote this class of symbols by
\text{H}\Gamma^{m_{-},m_{+}}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.
By direct comparison of (
124
) with the estimate
(
122
), every hypoelliptic symbol is a symbol in the
sense of
\Gamma_{\rho}^{m_{+}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, that is,

\text{H}\Gamma^{m_{-},m_{+}}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\subset\Gamma_{\rho}^{m_{+}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.
A hypoelliptic operator is a pseudodifferential operator whose symbol is hypoelliptic.

Depending on the asymptotic behavior of the symbol at infinity,
hypoelliptic symbols exhibit qualitatively different spectral properties.
We therefore distinguish between symbols that grow at infinity and symbols
that decay at infinity.

Positive-order hypoelliptic symbols.

A hypoelliptic symbol
\sigma
is said to be of positive order if it belongs
to the class

\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\;\coloneqq\;\bigcup_{\rho\in(0,1]}\ \bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{+},m_{-}>0\\
m_{+}\,\geq\,m_{-}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{m_{-},m_{+}}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}).

That is,
\sigma
is of positive order if there exist parameters
\rho\in(0,1]

and real numbers
m_{+}\geq m_{-}>0
such that

\sigma\in\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{m_{-},m_{+}}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}).

For symbols in this class, we have

|\sigma(z)|\to\infty,\qquad\text{as }\|z\|_{2}\to\infty.

Negative-order hypoelliptic symbols.

A hypoelliptic symbol
\sigma
is said to be of negative order if it belongs
to the class

\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{-}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\;\coloneqq\;\bigcup_{\rho\in(0,1]}\ \bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{+},m_{-}>0\\
m_{+}\,\geq\,m_{-}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{-m_{+},-m_{-}}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}).

(125)

That is,
\sigma
is of negative order if there exist parameters
\rho\in(0,1]

and real numbers
m_{+}\geq m_{-}>0
such that

\sigma\in\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{-m_{+},-m_{-}}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}).

For symbols in this class, we have

|\sigma(z)|\to 0,\qquad\text{as }\|z\|_{2}\to\infty.

The classical spectral theory of hypoelliptic pseudodifferential operators
predominantly addresses the
positive-order case. This stands in contrast to the negative-order setting of the present paper, where the symbols of
interest decay at infinity, so that the volume integral in
(
7
) is finite.
Nevertheless, in order to place our results in context and to recall the
classical Weyl-type asymptotics available in the
positive-order case, it is
convenient to introduce counterparts of the eigenvalue-counting function
(
1
) and of the volume function
(
7
), which we denote by a superscript

\uparrow
.
Specifically, for a Weyl symbol
\sigma_{W}\in\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, we define the
upward eigenvalue-counting function of the pseudodifferential operator

T_{\sigma_{W}}
by

M^{\uparrow}_{\sigma_{W}}(\lambda)\;\coloneqq\;\#\bigl\{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}:\lambda_{n}(T_{\sigma_{W}})<\lambda\bigr\},\qquad\lambda>0,

where
\{\lambda_{n}(T_{\sigma_{W}})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}

denotes the eigenvalues
of
T_{\sigma_{W}}
.
It follows from
[
53
, Corollary 23.3 and Theorem 26.3]

that
T_{\sigma_{W}}
has purely discrete spectrum.
The upward volume associated with the same symbol
\sigma_{W}
is defined by

V^{\uparrow}_{\sigma_{W}}(\lambda)\;\coloneqq\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma_{W}(x,\omega)\,<\,\lambda\}}\,\mathop{}\!dx\,\mathop{}\!d\omega,\qquad\lambda>0.

(126)

With these notions in place, a classical Weyl-type result in the spectral theory
of hypoelliptic pseudodifferential operators can be stated as follows.

Theorem 7

(
[
53
, Theorem 30.1]
)
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathrm{H}\Gamma^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.
Assume that there exist constants
R>0
and
C>0
such that

|z\cdot\nabla\sigma(z)|\geq C\,|\sigma(z)|,\qquad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\text{ with }\|z\|_{2}\geq R.

(127)

Then the upward eigenvalue-counting function
M^{\uparrow}_{\sigma}
of the
corresponding Weyl-quantized operator satisfies

M^{\uparrow}_{\sigma}(\lambda)\sim V^{\uparrow}_{\sigma}(\lambda),\qquad\text{as }\lambda\to\infty.

Note that our definition of the upward volume in
(
126
) differs from that used in

[
53
, Theorem 30.1]
by a multiplicative factor of

(2\pi)^{d}
.
This discrepancy reflects the different normalization conventions for the
Fourier transform; see (
120
) and

[
53
, eq. (1.2)]
.

The second result we invoke is due to Dauge and Robert
[
18
]
.
Their work concerns compact pseudodifferential operators of negative order and
establishes a Weyl-type asymptotic formula for the eigenvalue-counting function,
including a remainder estimate. Earlier results in this direction are due to Birman and Solomyak

[
10
]
, who analyzed compact pseudodifferential
operators with symbols that are homogeneous or quasi-homogeneous with respect to
the phase variable.
A typical example of such behavior is given by symbols
\sigma
for which there
exists
\zeta<0
such that

\sigma(x,t\omega)=t^{\zeta}\sigma(x,\omega),

for all
t\geq 1
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\|\omega\|_{2}
sufficiently
large. Dauge and Robert relax the requirement of exact homogeneity and work under more
general decay assumptions at infinity, thereby extending Weyl-type spectral
asymptotics to a broader class of compact pseudodifferential operators. This
flexibility makes their result applicable to the negative-order symbol regime
considered in the present paper.

Dauge–Robert formulate their results in terms of general weight functions

\phi,\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
taking values in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
; see

[
18
, Definition 1.1]
.
In this framework, a function
w\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
with values in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}

is called
(\phi,\varphi)
-continuous if there exist constants
c,C>0
such that

C^{-1}\leq\frac{w(x+x^{\prime},\omega+\omega^{\prime})}{w(x,\omega)}\leq C

for all
x,x^{\prime},\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

\frac{\|x^{\prime}\|_{2}}{\varphi(x,\omega)}+\frac{\|\omega^{\prime}\|_{2}}{\phi(x,\omega)}\leq c.

The weight
w
is said to be
(\phi,\varphi)
-temperate if there exist constants

C,\zeta>0
so that

w(x+x^{\prime},\omega+\omega^{\prime})\leq C\,w(x,\omega)\bigl[1+\|x^{\prime}\|_{2}\,\phi(x,\omega)+\|\omega^{\prime}\|_{2}\,\varphi(x,\omega)\bigr]^{\zeta},

(128)

for all
x,x^{\prime},\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Further, given a
(\phi,\varphi)
-temperate weight
w
, Dauge–Robert define the symbol
class
\Gamma(\mathbb{R}^{2d};w,\phi,\varphi)
as the set of real-valued functions

\sigma\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
such that, for every pair of multi-indices

\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, one has

\bigl|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\,\partial_{\omega}^{\beta}\,\sigma(x,\omega)\bigr|\;\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{w(x,\omega)}{\phi(x,\omega)^{|\alpha|}\,\varphi(x,\omega)^{|\beta|}},\qquad x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(129)

with a constant
C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
.

In order to control the spectral asymptotics of compact pseudodifferential
operators with symbol decay, Dauge–Robert impose a set of structural conditions
on the weight functions
w,\phi,\varphi
governing localization and scaling in
phase space. For ease of reference, we reproduce these assumptions below, using
the same labels as in
[
18
]
.

(H1)

The functions
\phi^{-1}
and
\varphi^{-1}
are

(\phi,\varphi)
-continuous,
(1,1)
-temperate, and bounded on

\mathbb{R}^{2d}
.

(H2)

There exist constants
C,\zeta>0
such that

C\,(1+\|x\|_{2}+\|\omega\|_{2})^{\zeta}\leq\phi(x,\omega)\,\varphi(x,\omega),\qquad x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(130)

(W)

The weight
w
belongs to the symbol class

\Gamma(\mathbb{R}^{2d};w,\phi,\varphi)
and is

(\phi,\varphi)
-temperate.

(N)

There exist constants
K,K^{\prime},\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}>0
such that

K^{\prime}\,w(x,\omega)^{\gamma^{\prime}}\leq\phi^{-1}(x,\omega)\,\varphi^{-1}(x,\omega)\leq K\,w(x,\omega)^{\gamma},\qquad x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(131)

Under assumptions
(H1)
–
(N)
, Dauge–Robert establish the following Weyl-type
asymptotic formula for the eigenvalue-counting function of compact
pseudodifferential operators whose symbol

\sigma
belongs to
\Gamma(\mathbb{R}^{2d};w,\phi,\varphi)
.

Theorem 8

(
[
18
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Let
w,\phi,\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})

be weight functions satisfying (H1), (H2), (W), and (N).
Let
\sigma\in\Gamma(\mathbb{R}^{2d};w,\phi,\varphi)

be non-negative
and assume that the maps
\lambda\mapsto V_{\sigma}(\lambda)
and
\lambda\mapsto V_{w}(\lambda)
are regularly varying at zero with
V_{w}(\lambda)=O_{\lambda\to 0}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V_{\sigma}(\lambda)}}\right)
.
Then,
M_{|T_{\sigma}|}(\lambda)\sim M_{T_{\sigma}}(\lambda)\sim V_{\sigma}(\lambda)
, as
\lambda\to 0
, where
T_{\sigma}
is the Weyl quantization of
\sigma
.

We remark that both Theorems
7
and
8
are stated
here in slightly simplified forms relative to their original counterparts in

[
53
, Theorem 30.1]
and

[
18
, Theorem 1.3]
, respectively.
More precisely,
[
53
, Theorem 30.1]
replaces the
condition (
127
) by the weaker requirement

|z\cdot\nabla\sigma(z)|\geq C\,|\sigma(z)|^{1-\delta},\qquad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\text{ with }\|z\|_{2}\geq R,

(132)

for some
\delta\in[0,\rho^{\prime})
, where
\rho^{\prime}>0
is a parameter depending on the
symbol
\sigma
(with the universal lower bound
\rho^{\prime}\geq\rho/m_{+}
).
Under this assumption, Shubin establishes the refined asymptotic expansion

M^{\uparrow}_{\sigma}(\lambda)=V^{\uparrow}_{\sigma}(\lambda)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+O_{\lambda\to\infty}\bigl(\lambda^{\delta-\rho^{\prime}+\eta}\bigr)}}\right),\qquad\text{for every }\eta>0.

Similarly,
[
18
, Theorem 1.3]
provides a remainder estimate of the
form

M_{\sigma}(\lambda)=V_{\sigma}(\lambda)+O_{\lambda\to 0}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\lambda^{\zeta}\,V_{w}(\lambda)}}\right),\qquad\text{for some }\zeta>0,

under their original set of assumptions. In the present paper, only first-order asymptotics are required, and the finer
remainder estimates available in the original results are not needed.
For this reason, the simplified formulations given in
Theorems
7
and
8
are sufficient for our
purposes.
Additionally, we note that the regular-variation assumption imposed in
Theorem
8
is stronger than the original condition (T) in

[
18
]
. This strengthening is introduced here in order to enable the
subsequent connection between eigenvalue-counting functions and metric entropy,
and therefore does not entail any loss of generality in the context of our
results. Finally, the non-negativity assumption on the symbol
\sigma
can in principle be
removed. In
[
18
]
, this is achieved by separating the asymptotic
analysis of the positive and negative parts of the spectrum of
T_{\sigma}
.
When

\sigma
is strictly positive and of negative order, the negative spectrum
does not contribute to the leading asymptotics,
and
M_{|T_{\sigma}|}(\lambda)\sim M_{T_{\sigma}}(\lambda)
. Since our analysis
focuses exclusively on the leading-order behavior, the formulation of
Theorem
8
for non-negative symbols is compatible with the
assumptions of Theorem
1
.
