The integral Chow ring of ℳ 0 ​ ( ℙ r , 2 ) \mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},2)
Renzo Cavalieri, Damiano Fulghesu

Abstract
. We compute a presentation for the integral Chow rings of the moduli stacks of degree 2 2 maps from smooth rational curves to projective space ℙ r \mathbb{P}^{r} , as a quotient of a three-variable polynomial ring. The relations as r r varies have rich combinatorial structure: all non-trivial relations are encoded by two generating functions which are rational functions.

1.
Introduction

This article is the second step in the authors’ program to compute the Chow rings with integral coefficients of the stacks
\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
. We refer to the introduction of
[
1
]
for history, context and relevance of the broader problem. Here we focus on the work presented in this manuscript, and explain why it is a natural self-contained step in the program. We begin by stating the result.

Theorem 1

(generating function form)
.

For
r
a positive integer,
a presentation for the integral Chow ring of the stack
\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},2)
is given by:

A^{\ast}(\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},2))=\frac{\mathbb{Z}[T,c_{2},c_{3}]}{(2c_{3},(T^{3}+c_{2}T+c_{3})^{r+1},\rho_{1,r},\rho_{1,r+1},(T^{3}+c_{2}T+c_{3})\rho_{2,2r-2},\rho_{2,2r},\rho_{2,2r+2})},

where
T
is a degree one variable,
c_{i}
is a graded variable of degree
i
and
\rho_{i,n}
is the degree
n
homogeneous term of the expansion at
0
of the generating function
\mathcal{R}_{i}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{(1-T)^{2}+c_{2}}

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-T^{2})((1-T^{2}-c_{2})^{2}+c_{2})-c_{2}^{2}+(T-c_{3})c_{3}}

The informal paraphrasis of the result is: the integral Chow ring of the space of degree two rational maps to any projective space is presented as a quotient of a three-variable polynomial ring; the ideal of relations can be generated by (at most) seven relations, two of which are boring. The interesting relations can be extracted from the homogeneous coefficients of two rational functions in the three variables of the polynomial ring.

An alternative description of the main theorem presents a generating set for the ideal of relations in closed form.

Theorem 1

(closed form)
.

A^{\ast}(\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},2))\cong\frac{\mathbb{Z}[T,c_{2},c_{3}]}{(2c_{3},(T^{3}+c_{2}T+c_{3})^{r+1},\{\alpha_{i,k}^{r}\}_{i=1,2\ k=0,1,2})},

where

\alpha_{1,k}^{r}=\frac{(-l_{0})^{k}2l_{0}(T+l_{0})^{r+1}}{(l_{1}-l_{0})(l_{2}-l_{0})}+\frac{(-l_{1})^{k}2l_{1}(T+l_{1})^{r+1}}{(l_{0}-l_{1})(l_{2}-l_{1})}+\frac{(-l_{2})^{k}2l_{2}(T+l_{2})^{r+1}}{(l_{0}-l_{2})(l_{1}-l_{2})}.

\alpha_{2,k}^{r}={l_{0}^{k}}\cdot\frac{(T+l_{1})^{r+1}(T+l_{2})^{r+1}}{(l_{0}-l_{1})(l_{0}-l_{2})}+{l_{1}^{k}}\cdot\frac{(T+l_{2})^{r+1}(T+l_{0})^{r+1}}{(l_{1}-l_{2})(l_{1}-l_{0})}+{l_{2}^{k}}\cdot\frac{(T+l_{0})^{r+1}(T+l_{1})^{r+1}}{(l_{2}-l_{0})(l_{2}-l_{1})}

The broad strategy for computing the presentation parallels
[
1
]
: present
\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},2)
as a global quotient stack by a special group, thus identifying the Chow ring of the quotient with the group equivariant cohomology of the space being quotiented
[
4
,
9
]
; show
\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},2)
is an open substack of some manageable ambient space, which provides a set of generators; construct an equivariant envelope for the complement and compute the pushforward of the Chow rings of the components of the envelope to obtain the relations. The localization computation on the equivariant envelope produces the presentation in the closed form version of Theorem
Theorem 1
. We verify the equivalence of the two forms of Theorem
Theorem 1
in Section
5
.

The stacks
\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
admit a natural presentation as global quotients by
\mathbb{P}\text{GL}_{2}
: informally, considering the source of a map to be a projective line with homogeneous coordinates
(s:t)
, a point of
\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
can be represented by
(r+1)
homogeneous polynomials in
s,t
of degree
d
. All other representatives of the given moduli point are obtained as the orbit of the
\mathbb{P}\text{GL}_{2}
action reparameterizing the source curve, thus exhibiting
\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
as the global quotient of an open subset
U
of projective space (where the homogeneous coordinates are the coefficients of the
(r+1)
polynomials) by
\mathbb{P}\text{GL}_{2}
.

For degree
d
odd, a
\text{GL}_{2}
action on the affine cone (minus the zero section) over
U
lifts the
\mathbb{P}\text{GL}_{2}
action and presents
\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
as a global quotient stack by the special group
\text{GL}_{2}
(
[
1
, Prop. 2.4]
).

When
d
is even such a lift does not exist. We make use of a general construction from
[
2
]
to exhibit
\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
as a global quotient stack by the special group
\text{GL}_{3}
. Informally, for a moduli point
[f:C\to\mathbb{P}^{r}]\in\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
, we view the source curve not as a parameterized
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, but as a smooth conic in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. The map is then determined by
(r+1)
sections of the restriction to the conic of the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1)
, with all other representatives obtained as the orbit of a
\text{GL}_{3}
action lifting the standard linear action on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

We give a slicker, albeit less intuitive, description of this construction: denoting by
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(2)))\cong\mathbb{P}^{5}
the locus of smooth conics, the map
\mathcal{S}\to[Spec(k)/\mathbb{P}\text{GL}_{2}]
is a
\text{GL}_{3}
torsor. The pull-back
Y
of
[U/\mathbb{P}\text{GL}_{2}]\cong\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)\to[Spec(k)/\mathbb{P}\text{GL}_{2}]
via this morphism is therefore also a
\text{GL}_{3}
torsor, implying
[Y/\text{GL}_{3}]\cong\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
.

We thus need to compute the
\text{GL}_{3}
equivariant Chow ring of
Y
. One can observe that
Y
is an open subset of a projective bundle over
\mathcal{S}
, and we can therefore compute a presentation for its Chow ring by constructing an equivariant envelope for the complement of
Y
. For this task the case
d=2
stands out with respect to all other even degrees for its simplicity, which led us to write its treatment as a standalone paper. In this case, there are two components of the envelope: one is a projective bundle over the universal conic, which is itself a projective bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. The second is isomorphic to a product of projective spaces. The localization computation, which can in both cases be performed relative to a morphism to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, is then extremely simple and elegant.

We conclude with a brief discussion of our observations and expectations for the remaining cases: when
d\geq 4
, the components of an equivariant envelope should be describable in terms of the geometry of either a subvariety of the absolute Hilbert scheme of points in
Hilb^{n}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
, or the relative Hilbert scheme of the universal smooth conic over its base
\underline{Hilb}^{n}_{\mathcal{U}/\mathcal{S}}
. Atiyah-Bott localization is not immediately available in these cases, either because the space is non-proper or too singular, but we are exploring an approach through virtual intersection theory which we believe might allow for applying the localization theorem again; if this approach succeeds, then the combinatorics of localization and of trying to treat uniformly all even cases should make the computations an interesting challenge for future work.

The paper is organized as follows: in Section
2.1
we provide some background on
\text{GL}_{3}
counterparts (
[
2
]
) to make this work more self-contained and set notation. In Section
3
we apply this construction to give a presentation of the stack
\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},2)
as a global
\text{GL}_{3}
quotient stack. In Section
4
we set up a
\text{GL}_{3}
equivariant envelope and compute the pushforward of the Chow rings of its components. In Section
5
we show how these computations give rise to the efficient generating function form of Theorem
Theorem 1
. We conclude by showing some explicit computations in low dimensions.

(r+1)
copies of
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1))
, i.e. of the standard representation of
\text{GL}_{3}
. It follows that

R(T)=(T^{3}+c_{1}T^{2}+c_{2}T+c_{3})^{r+1}.

Restricting the projective bundle to the part over the locus
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{S})\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
, one obtains the relations computed in Section
2.3
:
Q=c_{1}
and
c_{1}=2c_{3}=0
. Plugging these into (
18
) one obtains:

A^{\ast}_{\text{GL}_{3}}(\left.\mathbb{P}(\check{F}^{r}\right|_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{S})}))\cong\frac{\mathbb{Z}[T,c_{2},c_{3}]}{(2c_{3},P_{2}(0),(T^{3}+c_{2}T+c_{3})^{r+1})}.

The proof of the proposition is concluded by observing that
P_{2}(0)
contains a factor of
8c_{3}
coming from the weights of the quadratic forms
x_{0}^{2},x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2}
which are
2l_{0},2l_{1}
and
2l_{2}
;
P_{2}(0)
is therefore a redundant relation as the polynomial is already contained in the ideal generated by
2c_{3}
.
∎

4.2.
The first component
Z_{1}
of the envelope

We construct a projective variety
Z_{1}
together with a birational, regular,
\Delta
-equivariant morphism
\pi_{1}:Z_{1}\to{Z}
; this gives the first component of the envelope.

We define
Z_{1}
as a subset of
\mathbb{P}(\text{pr}_{1}^{\ast}(\check{F}^{r}))
:

Z_{1}:=\{(C,p\in C,\sigma_{0},\ldots,\sigma_{r})|\sigma_{i}(p)=0\ \ \mbox{for all $i$}\},

where
C
denotes a (not necessarily smooth) conic, the pair
(C,p)
denotes a point in the universal conic
C_{2,2}
, and
\sigma_{i}
is a linear form restricted to the conic, i.e. a global section of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1)_{|C}
. The map
\pi_{1}:Z_{1}\to Z
is the pull-back of the natural morphism that forgets the point
p
, i.e. the universal family from the universal conic
C_{2,2}
.

We observe that
Z_{1}
may be described as a projective bundle over a projective bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. First, define
K_{2}
to be the rank
5
bundle obtained as the kernel of the evaluation sequence:

0\to K_{2}\to H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(2))\times\mathbb{P}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle ev_{2}}}{{\to}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(2)\to 0.

The universal conic
C_{2,2}
is naturally identified with
\mathbb{P}(K_{2})
. Analogously, we consider the bundle
K_{1}
defined as the kernel of the evaluation sequence:

0\to K_{1}\to H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1))\times\mathbb{P}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle ev_{1}}}{{\to}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1)\to 0.

The affine cone
\widehat{Z}_{1}
is naturally identified with
(r+1)
copies of the pullback of
K_{1}
, so:

{Z}_{1}=\mathbb{P}(\text{pr}_{2}^{\ast}K_{1}^{\oplus(r+1)}).

Presenting
Z_{1}
as a projective bundle over a projective bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{2}
allows us to identify a set of generators for
A^{\ast}_{\Delta}(Z_{1})
:

•

the equivariant parameters
l_{0},l_{1},l_{2}
generating
A^{\ast}_{\Delta}(\text{Spec}(k))
;

•

the (pullback of the) equivariant hyperplane class of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
H=c_{1}^{eq}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{can}(1))
;

•

the relative (equivariant) hyperplane class of the projective bundle
\mathbb{P}(K_{2})\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
, which we denote by
Q
;

•

the relative (equivariant) hyperplane class of the projective bundle
\mathbb{P}(\text{pr}_{2}^{\ast}K_{1}^{\oplus(r+1)})\to\mathbb{P}(K_{2})
, which we denote by
T
.

The names of the generators have been purposefully chosen: one may show via some standard diagram chasing that the classes
Q,T
are the pull-back via
\pi_{1}
of the homonymous classes in (
18
). It follows by the projection formula that
\pi_{1\ \ast}(A^{\ast}_{\Delta}(Z_{1}))
is generated as an ideal by the pushforwards of the classes
1,H,H^{2}
. We observe that these classes are pulled-back via the morphism
\Pi_{1}:Z_{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
obtained by composing the two successive projective bundle projections.

By the Atiyah-Bott localization isomorphism (
[
5
, Theorem 2]
),
the class
H^{k}
in
A^{*}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{P}^{2})\otimes\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{l_{0}},\frac{1}{l_{1}},\frac{1}{l_{2}}]
may be expressed in the fixed point basis as:

(19)

H^{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{2}\frac{i_{p_{j}}^{*}(H^{k})}{c_{top}^{\Delta}(T_{p_{j}}\mathbb{P}^{2})}[p_{j}]=\frac{(-l_{0})^{k}}{(l_{1}-l_{0})(l_{2}-l_{0})}[p_{0}]+\frac{(-l_{1})^{k}}{(l_{0}-l_{1})(l_{2}-l_{1})}[p_{1}]+\frac{(-l_{2})^{k}}{(l_{0}-l_{2})(l_{1}-l_{2})}[p_{2}],

where
[p_{j}]
denotes the equivariant cohomology class of the torus fixed point where the
j
-th coordinate is non-zero.

Denote by
L_{j}=\Pi_{1}^{-1}(p_{j})
the inverse image in
Z_{1}
of the
j
-th fixed point of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. The map
\pi_{1|L_{j}}
is an isomorphism onto its image, which consists of the torus invariant subvariety of
\mathbb{P}(\check{F}^{r})
defined by the vanishing of:

•

the homogeneous coordinate of
W_{2,2}
corresponding to the conic
x_{j}^{2}
;

•

the homogeneous coordinates in
\mathbb{P}^{3r+2}
that are congruent to
j
modulo
3
, corresponding to choosing
r+1
times the linear form
x_{j}
.

As a consequence, we have

(20)

\pi_{1\ast}(\Pi_{1}^{\ast}([p_{j}]))=(Q+2l_{j})(T+l_{j})^{r+1}.

Combining (
19
),(
20
) we obtain:

(21)

\pi_{1\ast}(H^{k})=\frac{(-l_{0})^{k}(Q+2l_{0})(T+l_{0})^{r+1}}{(l_{1}-l_{0})(l_{2}-l_{0})}+\frac{(-l_{1})^{k}(Q+2l_{1})(T+l_{1})^{r+1}}{(l_{0}-l_{1})(l_{2}-l_{1})}+\frac{(-l_{2})^{k}(Q+2l_{2})(T+l_{2})^{r+1}}{(l_{0}-l_{2})(l_{1}-l_{2})}.

It is immediate to see that (
21
) is a symmetric rational function in
l_{0},l_{1},l_{2}
. In order to see that it is in fact a polynomial expression with integer coefficients it suffices to show that it does not have poles along the loci
l_{i}=l_{j}
. Picking, without loss of generality,
l_{0}=l_{1}
one sees that the third summand is clearly regular along this locus. As for the first two summands, consider their product by
(l_{1}-l_{0})
, thus clearing the factor in the denominator; it is immediate to see that setting
l_{1}=l_{0}
the sum of the two terms vanishes, meaning that the original two summands added to a function regular along
l_{1}=l_{0}
. By symmetry, it follows that (
21
) are homogeneous polynomials of degree
r+k
, symmetric in the
l_{i}
’s.

4.3.
The second component
Z_{2}
of the envelope

We define
Z_{2}
to be

Z_{2}=\mathbb{P}(W_{2,2})\times(\mathbb{P}^{2})^{\vee}\times\mathbb{P}^{r},

with the action of the torus
\Delta
scaling linearly the
x_{i}
’s in the first factor and the homogeneous coordinates of the second, and acting trivially on the third factor
\mathbb{P}^{r}
.
The equivariant map
\pi_{2}:Z_{2}\to Z
is given by:

(C,(a_{0}:a_{1}:a_{2}),(b_{0}:\ldots:b_{r}))\mapsto(C,(b_{0}\cdot(a_{0}x_{0}+a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}):\ldots:b_{r}\cdot(a_{0}x_{0}+a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}))).

Generically, the image
\pi_{2}(Z_{2})
parameterizes a choice of a conic and
r+1
linear forms vanishing at two points: generically the two points are distinct and determine a unique line, and therefore the linear forms are all scalar multiples of each other.

The
\Delta
-equivariant Chow ring of
Z_{2}
is generated by the equivariant classes
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
and the equivariant hyperplane classes of the factors
Q,H,T_{2}
, where the relationship with the pullbacks of the hyperplane classes via
\pi_{2}
are:
\pi_{2}^{\ast}(Q)=Q
and
\pi_{2}^{\ast}(T)=H+T_{2}
.
By the projection formula, the ideal generated by the image of
\pi_{2\ast}
is generated by
\pi_{2\ast}(H^{k})
for
k=0,1,2
.

Consider the projection function
\Pi_{2}:Z_{2}\to(\mathbb{P}^{2})^{\vee}
. As in Section
4.2
, the class of
H^{k}
may be written in the fixed point basis as:

(22)

H^{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{2}\frac{i_{p^{\vee}_{j}}^{*}(H^{k})}{c_{top}^{\Delta}(T_{p^{\vee}_{j}}(\mathbb{P}^{2})^{\vee})}[p_{j}^{\vee}]=\frac{l_{0}^{k}}{(l_{0}-l_{1})(l_{0}-l_{2})}[p_{0}^{\vee}]+\frac{l_{1}^{k}}{(l_{1}-l_{0})(l_{1}-l_{2})}[p_{1}^{\vee}]+\frac{l_{2}^{k}}{(l_{2}-l_{0})(l_{2}-l_{1})}[p_{2}^{\vee}],

where
[p_{j}]^{\vee}
is the class of the linear form
x_{j}
in the dual plane.

The restriction of
\pi_{2}
to
\Pi_{2}^{-1}(p_{j}^{\vee})
maps isomorphically onto its image, consisting of (the inverse image via the projection to
\mathbb{P}^{3r+2}
of) the linear subspace defined by the vanishing of the homogeneous coordinates
not congruent to
j
modulo
3
. It follows that:

(23)

\pi_{2\ast}(H^{k})=\frac{l_{0}^{k}(T+l_{1})^{r+1}(T+l_{2})^{r+1}}{(l_{0}-l_{1})(l_{0}-l_{2})}+\frac{l_{1}^{k}(T+l_{0})^{r+1}(T+l_{2})^{r+1}}{(l_{1}-l_{0})(l_{1}-l_{2})}+\frac{l_{2}^{k}(T+l_{0})^{r+1}(T+l_{1})^{r+1}}{(l_{2}-l_{0})(l_{2}-l_{1})}.

Expression (
23
) is clearly symmetric, as any element of
S_{3}
permutes the three summands. For any
i\not=j
, it is immediate to see that
(l_{i}-l_{j})\pi_{2\ast}(H^{k})
vanishes when setting
l_{i}=l_{j}
, implying that
\pi_{2\ast}(H^{k})
is in fact a degree
2r+k
homogeneous polynomial which is symmetric in the
l_{i}
’s.

4.4.
A presentation for
A^{\ast}(\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},2))

We have all the ingredients to exhibit explicitly the presentation from Lemma
3.3
.
Denote by
\alpha^{r}_{i,k}=\pi_{i\ast}(H^{k})_{|Q=0}
the relations obtained from the
i
-th component of the envelope, as computed in (
21
),(
23
). From (
14
)
and Proposition
4.1
we obtain:

(24)

A^{\ast}(\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{r},2))\cong\frac{\mathbb{Z}[T,c_{2},c_{3}]}{(2c_{3},(T^{3}+c_{2}T+c_{3})^{r+1},\{\alpha_{i,k}^{r}\}_{i=1,2\ k=0,1,2})},

proving the closed form version of Theorem
Theorem 1
.

5.
Proof of generating function form of Theorem
Theorem 1

In this section we perform algebraic manipulations that allow us to describe the relations from the presentation in Section
4.4
as coefficients of appropriate generating functions, and thus give a proof of the generating function version of Theorem
Theorem 1
. As a starting point we define generating functions for relations of the form
\alpha_{i,k}^{r}
, as
r
varies.
Define

(25)

\mathcal{A}_{i,k}:=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{i,k}^{r}.

For the generating functions
\mathcal{R}_{j}
from (
Theorem 1
), we let
\rho_{j,n}
denote the degree
n
part of the formal geometric series expansion of
\mathcal{R}_{j}
.

We first consider the relations coming from the first component of the envelope.

Claim 5.1
.

We have the following equality of ideals in
\mathbb{Z}[T,c_{2},c_{3}]/(2c_{3})
:

(\alpha_{1,0}^{r},\alpha_{1,1}^{r},\alpha_{1,2}^{r})=(\rho_{1,r},\rho_{1,r+1}).

Proof.

We explicitly compute the generating function
\mathcal{A}_{1,0}
. Setting
Q=0
in (
21
), and recognizing that each summand gives rise to a geometric series, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1,0}

\displaystyle=\frac{2l_{0}}{(l_{1}-l_{0})(l_{2}-l_{0})(1-T-l_{0})}+\frac{2l_{1}}{(l_{0}-l_{1})(l_{2}-l_{1})(1-T-l_{1})}+\frac{2l_{2}}{(l_{0}-l_{2})(l_{1}-l_{2})(1-T-l_{2})}

(26)

\displaystyle=\frac{2(1-T)}{(1-T-l_{0})(1-T-l_{1})(1-T-l_{2})}=\frac{2(1-T)}{(1-T)^{3}+(1-T)c_{2}+c_{3}}.

Viewing the denominator of
\mathcal{A}_{1,0}
as having the shape
1+P(T,c_{2},c_{3})
, with
P
a polynomial vanishing at
(0,0,0)
, the relations are obtained by expanding the denominator as a geometric series. Because of the factor of
2
in the numerator, any term containing
c_{3}
appears with an even coefficient and therefore vanishes in the ring
\mathbb{Z}[T,c_{2},c_{3}]/(2c_{3})
.
It follows that replacing
P(T,c_{2},c_{3})
by
P(T,c_{2},0)
does not change any of the relations. Setting
c_{3}=0
, it is immediate to observe that
\mathcal{A}_{1,0}
reduces to
\mathcal{R}_{1}
. We have therefore shown that, for every
r
,

(27)

\alpha_{1,0}^{r}=\rho_{1,r},

where we recall that the second lower index for the
\rho
’s indicates the degree of the relation rather than the power of the hyperplane class pushed forward.

Resumming in an analogous fashion all the relations
\alpha_{1,1}^{r}
one obtains:

(28)

\mathcal{A}_{1,1}=(1-T)\mathcal{A}_{1,0},

from which it follows that

(29)

\alpha_{1,1}^{r}=\alpha_{1,0}^{r+1}-T\alpha_{1,0}^{r}=\rho_{1,r+1}-T\rho_{1,r}

Finally, resumming over
r
the relations
\alpha_{1,2}^{r}
one obtains:

(30)

\mathcal{A}_{1,2}=-c_{2}\mathcal{A}_{1,0},

implying

(31)

\alpha_{1,2}^{r}=-c_{2}\alpha_{1,0}^{r}=-c_{2}\rho_{1,r}.

The claim immediately follows from (
27
),(
29
),(
31
).
∎

We proceed analogously with the relations from the second envelope.

Claim 5.2
.

We have the following equality of ideals in
\mathbb{Z}[T,c_{2},c_{3}]/(2c_{3})
:

(\alpha_{2,0}^{r},\alpha_{2,1}^{r},\alpha_{2,2}^{r})=(\rho_{2,2r},\rho_{2,2r-2}(T^{3}+c_{2}T+c_{3}),\rho_{2,2r+2}).

Proof.

We adopt a similar strategy as in the previous claim. We begin by summing over all
r
the relations
\alpha_{2,0}^{r}
from (
23
), to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2,0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(l_{0}-l_{1})(l_{0}-l_{2})(1-(T+l_{1})(T+l_{2}))}+\frac{1}{(l_{1}-l_{0})(l_{1}-l_{2})(1-(T+l_{0})(T+l_{2}))}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(l_{2}-l_{0})(l_{2}-l_{1})(1-(T+l_{0})(T+l_{1}))}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{((T^{2}+(l_{0}+l_{1})T+l_{0}l_{1}-1)(T^{2}+(l_{0}+l_{2})T+l_{0}l_{2}-1)(T^{2}+(l_{1}+l_{2})T+l_{1}l_{2}-1))}

(32)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-T^{2})((1-T^{2}-c_{2})^{2}+c_{2})-c_{2}^{2}+(T+c_{3})c_{3}}=\mathcal{R}_{2}.

This computation shows that for any
r

(33)

\alpha_{2,0}^{r}=\rho_{2,2r}

Analogously resumming all the relations
\alpha_{2,k}^{r}
, for
k=1,2
, one obtains:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2,1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{T^{3}+(c_{2}-1)T+c_{3}}{(1-T^{2})((1-T^{2}-c_{2})^{2}+c_{2})-c_{2}^{2}+(T+c_{3})c_{3}}=(T^{3}+c_{2}T+c_{3}-T)\mathcal{R}_{2},

(34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2,2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{T^{4}+(c_{2}-2)T^{2}+c_{3}T-c_{2}+1}{(1-T^{2})((1-T^{2}-c_{2})^{2}+c_{2})-c_{2}^{2}+(T+c_{3})c_{3}}=(T^{4}+c_{2}T^{2}+c_{3}T-2T^{2}-c_{2}+1)\mathcal{R}_{2}.

At the level of coefficients, (
5
) gives:

\displaystyle\alpha_{2,1}^{r}=(T^{3}+c_{2}T+c_{3})\rho_{2,2r-2}-T\rho_{2,2r},

(35)

\displaystyle\alpha_{2,2}^{r}=T\alpha_{2,1}^{r}-(T^{2}+c_{2})\rho_{2,2r}+\rho_{2,2r+2}.

Equations (
33
),(
5
) together immediately establish the Claim.
∎

Claims
5.1
and
5.2
allow to substitute the relations
\alpha_{i,k}^{r}
’s with the corresponding (multiples of)
\rho
’s in presentation (
24
), thus concluding the proof of Theorem
Theorem 1
.
