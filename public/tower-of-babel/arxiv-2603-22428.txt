Abstract
. Let Y → X Y\to X be a proper map between proper hyperbolic metric spaces. A Cannon–Thurston map is a continuous extension ∂ Y → ∂ X \partial Y\to\partial X . We prove that in most known settings in which a Cannon–Thurston map exists it is uniformly finite-to-one. This answers a question of Swarup and generalizes previous results of Cannon–Thurston, Kapovich–Lustig, Dowdall–Kapovich–Taylor and Ghosh.

1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a closed hyperbolic 3-manifold fibering over the circle with fiber
F
. Cannon and Thurston
[
CT07
]
proved that the inclusion map
i:\widetilde{F}\to\widetilde{M}
extends continuously to
\partial i:\partial\widetilde{F}\to\partial\widetilde{M}
. Further, they identified point-preimages of
\partial i
precisely in terms of stable and unstable laminations on
F
. In particular, they showed that
\partial i
is uniformly finite-to-one.

More generally, let
Y\to X
be a proper map between proper hyperbolic spaces. A continuous extension
\partial Y\to\partial X
, if exists, is called a
Cannon–Thurston map
. In
[
Bes00
, Question 1.20]
, Swarup asked the following:

Question 1.1
.

Let

\Pi:X\to T
be a tree of hyperbolic metric spaces such that the inclusion of edge spaces into vertex spaces are quasi-isometric (qi) embeddings
as in Bestvina–Feighn’s combination theorem
[
BF92
]
.
Further, assume that
X
is hyperbolic. Let
v
be
a vertex of
T
and
(X_{v},d_{v})
denote the hyperbolic vertex space
corresponding to
v
. It was proven in
[
Mit98b
]
that
i:X_{v}\to X
extends continuously to

\partial i:\partial X_{v}\to\partial X
.
Is
\partial i_{v}:\partial X_{v}\to\partial X
finite-to-one?

The main theorem of this paper answers Question
1.1
affirmatively. In fact we prove the following more general statement
(see Theorems
3.13
,
4.13
and Corollaries
3.15
,
4.15
).

Theorem A
.

Let
X,Y
be bounded valence graphs, satisfying one of the following:

(1)

X
is the Cayley graph of a hyperbolic group;
Y
is the Cayley graph of a normal hyperbolic subgroup.

(2)

X
is a hyperbolic tree of hyperbolic spaces such that the vertex spaces are uniformly hyperbolic and the inclusions of edge spaces into vertex spaces are uniformly qi embeddings
(as in Bestvina–Feighn’s combination theorem
[
BF92
]
);
Y
is a vertex space.

(3)

X
is a hyperbolic metric graph bundle of uniformly hyperbolic spaces with uniformly coarse surjective barycenter maps as in Mj–Sardar
[
MS12
]
;
Y
is a fiber space.

Then the Cannon–Thurston map
\partial Y\to\partial X
of the natural inclusion
Y\to X
is uniformly finite-to-one.

The existence of a Cannon–Thurston map in the cases of the theorem was proven respectively in
[
Mit98a
, Theorem 4.3]
,
[
Mit98b
, Theorem 3.10]
and
[
MS12
, Theorem 5.3]
.
Case (1) follows from Case (3), and generalizes previous results of Kapovich and Lustig
[
KL15
, Theorem A]
, Dowdall, Kapovich and Taylor
[

Case (2) answers a question of Swarup
[
Bes00
, Question 1.20]
.

Unlike previous approaches, we prove uniform finiteness of fibers of the Cannon–Thurston map directly without going through the description of Cannon–Thurston laminations as in
[

,

,

,
Gho20

. We sketch the main idea in the case of a hyperbolic metric bundle over the ray:

Sketch of the main idea:

Let
T=[0,\infty)
be a ray with vertices at integer points
\{0\}\cup{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
X\to T
be a tree of (uniformly)
\delta-
hyperbolic spaces such that edge to vertex space maps are uniform quasi-isometries (and not just qi embeddings). Hence there exist uniform
quasi-isometries
\phi_{n}:X_{n}\to X_{n+1}
. Let
\partial\phi_{n}:\partial X_{n}\to\partial X_{n+1}
denote the induced boundary homomorphisms.
Let
\partial\Phi_{n}:\partial X_{0}\to\partial X_{n}
be given by
\partial\Phi_{n}=\partial\phi_{n-1}\circ\cdots\circ\partial\phi_{0}
.
Suppose
X
is hyperbolic.

Let
i:X_{0}\to X
denote the inclusion of the base vertex space. Let

\partial i:\partial X_{0}\to\partial X
denote the Cannon–Thurston map
[
Mit98b
]
.
Let
\zeta\in\partial X
. Our goal is to uniformly bound the size
|\partial i^{-1}(\zeta)|.
We will do so by uniformly bounding the size of any finite subset
A\subseteq\partial i^{-1}(\zeta)
.

Let
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}\in A
be distinct points.
The triple of points
\partial\Phi_{n}(\xi_{1}),\partial\Phi_{n}(\xi_{2}),\partial\Phi_{n}(\xi_{3})
defines a coarse barycenter

Bary_{X_{n}}(\partial\Phi_{n}(\xi_{1}),\partial\Phi_{n}(\xi_{2}),\partial\Phi_{n}(\xi_{3}))
(Definition
2.2
) in each fiber
X_{n}
.
The ray

r=r(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}):\{0\}\cup{\mathbb{N}}\to X\quad\text{ defined by}\quad r(n)=Bary_{X_{n}}(\partial\Phi_{n}(\xi_{1}),\partial\Phi_{n}(\xi_{2}),\partial\Phi_{n}(\xi_{3}))

is a quasigeodesic ray (with uniform quasi-geodesic constants).
Moreover, since
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}\in\partial i^{-1}(\zeta)
we have
r(\infty)=\zeta
.

By the previous paragraph, it follows that for any two triples (of distinct points)
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}
and
\xi^{\prime}_{1},\xi^{\prime}_{2},\xi^{\prime}_{3}
in
A
the rays

and

are asymptotic.
That is, there exists

(independent of
A
) and

such that for all

,
d_{X_{n}}(r(n),r^{\prime}(n))\leq D
.
Since
A
is finite there exists

such that the distance between barycenters of distinct triples in

\partial\Phi_{M}(A)
is bounded above by
D
in

\partial X_{M}
.
By compactness of
\partial X_{M}
and bounded valence of
X_{M}
, it then follows that

is of bounded size (see Proposition
2.4
for further detail).

A historical note:
Swarup’s question
[
Bes00
, Question 1.20]
had two parts. The first part asked for a description of point-preimages, i.e. Cannon–Thurston laminations in the terminology of
[

]
. The second part is addressed by Theorem

. The question was stated
originally in

[
Bes00
]
in the somewhat more restrictive group-theoretic setting of a finite graph of hyperbolic groups satisfying the qi-embedded condition.
The recent exhaustive monograph
[

]
(especially Chapter 8) does furnish a description, building on related work in
[

,
MS12
]

[

and

and geodesic laminations.
The key new contribution of this paper is that it largely circumvents the use of any lamination-related machinery and addresses Question

2.2.
Cannon–Thurston maps

Definition 2.5
.

[
Mit98a
,
Mit98b
]

Let
i:(Y,d_{Y})\to(X,d_{X})
be a proper injective map of hyperbolic metric spaces.
We say that
i:Y\rightarrow X
admits a
Cannon–Thurston map
if
i
extends continuously to a map
\partial i:\partial Y\to\partial X
.

Suppose
H<G
are hyperbolic groups. Let
\partial H,\partial G
denote their Gromov boundaries.
Choose a finite generating set for
G
containing a
finite generating set for
H
, so that we have an inclusion
i:\Gamma_{H}\to\Gamma_{G}
of Cayley graphs
\Gamma_{H},\Gamma_{G}
with respect to these generating sets. We say that the inclusion
H\rightarrow G
admits a
Cannon–Thurston map
if
i:\Gamma_{H}\to\Gamma_{G}
admits a Cannon–Thurston map.

Notation.
When we want to emphasize the spaces in question, we denote the inclusion of
Y
into
X
by
i_{Y,X}:Y\to X
. We shall denote the corresponding Cannon–Thurston map, if it exists, by
\partial i_{Y,X}:\partial Y\to\partial X
.

The following lemma asserts the basic fact that a composition of Cannon–Thurston maps is a Cannon–Thurston map.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
Z\subseteq Y\subseteq X
be inclusions of hyperbolic graphs. (Here, all spaces are equipped with their natural graph metrics.) Assume that the inclusions
i_{Z,Y}:Z\rightarrow Y
and
i_{Y,X}:Y\rightarrow X
admit Cannon–Thurston maps. Then the inclusion
i_{Z,X}:Z\rightarrow X
admits a Cannon–Thurston map given by
\partial i_{Z,X}=\partial i_{Y,X}\circ\partial i_{Z,Y}
. ∎

3.3.
Finite fibers of Cannon–Thurston maps for trees of spaces

We are now in a position to prove the main theorem of the paper.

.

Metric graph bundles were introduced in

Definition 4.1

4.2.
Cannon–Thurston fibers For metric graph bundles

We are now in a position to prove the analog of Theorem
3.13
for metric graph bundles.

Theorem 4.13
.

Given
\delta_{0}\geq 0
,
L^{\prime}\geq 0
,
D_{0}\geq 0
and a proper map
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
there is a constant
N=N(\delta_{0},L^{\prime},D_{0},f)
satisfying the following. Let
p:X\to B
be an
f
-metric graph bundle with controlled fibers satisfying the following.

(1)

Each
F_{b}
is a graph where all vertices have valence bounded by
D_{0}
.

(2)

The total space
X
is
\delta_{0}
-hyperbolic.

Then for any
b\in V(B)
, the Cannon–Thurston map
\partial i:\partial F_{b}\to\partial X
is uniformly finite-to-one, i.e. for all
\xi\in\partial X
,

|\partial i^{-1}(\xi)|\leq N.

Proof.

For the upper bound, we first deal with the case that
B
is a geodesic ray
[0,\infty)
where
[0,\infty)
has the standard graph structure with vertices at the integer points.
By Remark
4.11
,
p:X\to[0,\infty)
is quasi-isometric to a tree of spaces, whose vertex spaces are exactly those of
p:X\to[0,\infty)
.
This special case is then an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.13
(I).

We now deal with the upper bound in the general case. The argument is essentially the same as that for Theorem
3.13
(II). We include a sketch for completeness.

Assume that
\partial i^{-1}(\xi)
contains more than two elements. Also, let
\xi_{1}\in\partial F_{b}
be such that
\partial i(\xi_{1})=\xi
.
Let
\eta\in\partial B
be the boundary point of
B
determined by
\xi
as per the last statement of Proposition
4.9
.
By Corollary
4.12
, it suffices to consider a metric bundle
p:X_{\eta}\to\alpha
over a geodesic ray
\alpha\subset B
, where
\alpha(\infty)=\eta
. By Theorem
4.5
,
X_{\alpha}:=p^{-1}(\alpha)
is
\delta_{1}
-hyperbolic for some
\delta_{1}
depending only on
\delta_{0}
,
L^{\prime}
and
f
. Thus the restricted metric graph bundle
p:X_{\alpha}\to\alpha\subseteq B
over the geodesic ray
\alpha
satisfies all the conditions of the present theorem with constants
\delta_{1}
,
D_{0}
and
f
.
By the special case dealt with at the beginning of the proof, the cardinality of the set
\partial i^{-1}_{F_{b},X_{\alpha}}(\partial i_{F_{b},X_{\alpha}}(\xi_{1}))
is bounded by an integer
N=N(\delta_{1},D_{0},f)
.
Since
\delta_{1}
depends only on
\delta_{0},L^{\prime},f
the theorem follows.
∎

Applications and generalizations

5.1.
New proofs of old theorems

We give a new and short proof of
[
LMM24
, Theorem B]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
p:X\to[0,\infty)
be a metric graph bundle with controlled hyperbolic fiber
F
, where
F
is quasi-isometric to a one-ended hyperbolic group
H
.
Then
\partial F
admits a local cut-point. Hence,
H
virtually splits over a 2-ended subgroup.

Proof.

The boundary
\partial X
is a dendrite
[
Bow98
]
. Let
i:F\to X
denote the inclusion map, and

\partial i:\partial F\to\partial X
denote the associated Cannon–Thurston map. Since
H
is one-ended,
\partial F
is a compact, connected, locally connected set. Hence, so is

\partial i(\partial F)
. In fact,
\partial i(\partial F)
is
equal to
\partial X

[
Hal25
]
.
For any cut-point
\xi
of

\partial X=\partial i(\partial F)
,
\partial i^{-1}(\xi)
disconnects
\partial F
. By Theorem
4.13
,
\partial i^{-1}(\xi)
is finite.
Hence

\partial F
admits a local cut-point. The last statement now follows from Bowditch’s theorem on JSJ decomposition of hyperbolic groups
[
Bow98
]
.
∎

The notion of
3-manifolds that slither over the circle
[
Thu97
,
Thu98
,
CD03
,
CL24
]

can be translated
to the context of metric graph bundles. We use this to recover Thurston’s theorem that the associated Cannon-Thurston map is finite-to-one.

We refer the reader to
[
CL24
, Section 4]
for details on uniform quasimorphisms and

[
CL24
, Example 4.12]
for an elucidation of the fact that 3-manifolds that slither in the sense of

[
Thu97
,
Thu98
]
give examples of
uniform quasimorphisms. In fact the explanation in
[
CL24
, Example 4.12]
shows a bit more.
Let
M
be a closed hyperbolic 3-manifold slithering over the circle, and let
G=\pi_{1}(M)
. Let
\phi:G\to\mathbb{Z}
denote the associated uniform quasimorphism.
Let
\Gamma
denote a Cayley graph of
G
with respect to a finite generating set.
Then there exists a projection
\Pi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}

such that (after possibly adding some edges to
\Gamma
while retaining its quasi-isometry type)
\Pi^{-1}(n)
is a connected graph. Further,
by Candel’s theorem
[
Can93
]

\Pi^{-1}(n)
is uniformly quasi-isometric to the hyperbolic plane. Thus,

\Pi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}
gives rise to a metric graph bundle
in the sense of Definition
4.1
with controlled hyperbolic fibers.
In fact the projection map
\Pi
in the metric graph bundle is quasi-equivariant with respect to the
action by
G
on the total space and the quasi-action by
G
on the base
\mathbb{Z}

via the quasimorphism
\phi
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
denote any leaf of the pulled back foliation on
\widetilde{M}
and we assume by Candel’s theorem
[
Can93
]

that
\mathcal{L}
is isometric to the hyperbolic plane.
It now follows from Theorem
4.6
that

i:\mathcal{L}\to\widetilde{M}
admits a Cannon–Thurston map. Further,
by Theorem
4.13
this map is finite-to-one.

We also note that Theorem
5.1
shows that there cannot exist analogs of Thurston’s slithering examples in higher dimensions in the following sense. Let
M
be a closed manifold of pinched negative curvature
of dimension greater than 3, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a codimension one uniform
foliation in the sense of Thurston, i.e.
\Pi:\widetilde{M}\to\mathbb{R}
is a metric
bundle in the sense of
[
MS12
]
.
Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
denote the
pulled back foliation to

\widetilde{M}
.
Then no (simply connected) leaf
\mathcal{L}
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
can itself be
of pinched negative curvature
with respect to the metric induced from
\widetilde{M}
. Else, the boundary
\partial{\mathcal{L}}
is a sphere of dimension greater than one, and hence cannot have local cut-points, violating Theorem
5.1
.
This generalizes the well-known fact that a closed negatively curved manifold of dimension greater than 3 cannot fiber over the circle with fibers of pinched negative curvature.
