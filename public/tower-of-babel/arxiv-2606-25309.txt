Sharp Residual-Work Criteria for Positive-Cone Oldroyd–B and FENE-P Reynolds States

Abstract
We prove sharp residual-work criteria for entropy-admissible Reynolds states in viscoelastic models whose elastic variables are constrained by a positive cone or by a finite-extensibility domain. The argument is formulated for an entropy-dual class of closures in which the entropy lever G = D ​ Φ G=D\Phi and the elastic stress T T satisfy G ⁡ ( C ) ​ C = T ⁡ ( C ) G(C)C=T(C) . The criterion gives the optimal pointwise cost, the unique aligned minimizer, a windowed three-channel alternative, and closed exclusion tests for structured families. We compute one such test for a finite-thickness shear layer and show that its positive pressure-free work outruns the available lever–residual budget. 1
Introduction

The stress-diffusion-free Oldroyd–B system has a robust free-energy identity,
but this identity does not automatically close the large-data problem. At the
relaxed level a sharper question appears: which Reynolds defects are compatible
with the positive definite conformation constraint and the physical
energy–entropy inequality?

We place the conformation tensor in logarithmic variables,

A=e^{B},\qquad B(t,x)\in\mathbb{S}^{2},

so the positive cone
A\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{2}
is automatic. A relaxed positive-cone
state contains two residual channels: a momentum residual and a conformation
residual. The momentum residual may be represented by a symmetric
antidivergence, but the conformation residual is not free. It must have the
right signed projection against the entropy-dual lever

G(A)=I-A^{-1}.

The basic residual-work estimate is this. After removing the pressure, let

R_{\mathcal{N}}
be a symmetric antidivergence of the zero-mean momentum residual,
and define

P(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}-R_{\mathcal{N}}(t):D(u(t))\,dx,\qquad D(u)=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla u+\nabla u^{T}).

On every time window
E
,

\int_{E}[P(t)]_{+}\,dt\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\left\lVert\mathcal{S}[u,B]\right\rVert_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})},

where
\eta_{E}\in[0,1]
measures negative alignment between
G
and

\mathcal{S}
. Thus positive Reynolds production can be paid only by a large
lever, a large conformation residual, or efficient negative alignment.

The main result is a sharp admissibility theorem for positive-cone Reynolds
states. A structured ansatz, reduced model, or finite-thickness stress layer
that fails the residual-work inequality cannot be energy–entropy admissible.
Conversely, at the algebraic level, a signed surplus in the direction
-G

is the component a positive-cone correction must contain. The mechanism is
entropy-dual rather than tied to one constitutive closure: the Hookean
Oldroyd–B lever
I-A^{-1}
and the FENE-P lever
D\Phi_{b}(C)
enter the
same pressure-free work law. The result is a necessary residual-level
criterion, distinct from the compactness or realization problems that arise in
weak-solution and convex-integration approaches.

The admissibility condition used below is not an additional constitutive
postulate. It is the local-in-time defect form of the standard free-energy
inequality after the exact Oldroyd–B stress work has been canceled. Thus a
Reynolds state is admissible when its unresolved residuals do not inject net
energy–entropy into the physical balance. The theorem identifies
which part of this defect budget survives after pressure, mean forcing, and
representative choices have been removed.

This point is different from the usual weak-solution framework. The
energy–entropy inequality in the sense of Lions and Masmoudi

[
13
]
records the global dissipation law, but it does not
by itself isolate which residual directions are compatible with that law after
pressure and mean modes have been removed. The present paper extracts the
corresponding signed condition. It gives a computable and sharp test for
energy–entropy admissibility at the smooth residual-profile level.

The residual-work inequality is an exact criterion for pressure-free residual
admissibility on smooth profiles, but it is not, in general, a realization
theorem. Showing that an algebraically admissible residual can be produced by
true solutions, by a positive-cone convex-integration scheme, or by a compact
approximating sequence requires additional localization and compactness
hypotheses. This realization problem is kept separate from the residual-level
result proved here.

1.1
The admissibility problem

The full large-data problem for non-diffusive Oldroyd–B contains several
interacting difficulties: positivity of
A
, absence of stress diffusion,
possible high-frequency cascades in
\operatorname{Log}A
, and the lack of an obvious
compactness principle for nonlinear stress work. The residual-work framework
separates one part of the question that can be answered exactly. It asks:
given a smooth proposed profile
(u,B)
, is there enough signed conformation
work to pay for the positive pressure-free Reynolds production generated by
the momentum residual?

This admissibility problem is independent of any particular construction
scheme. It is algebraic enough to be sharp at each time, but it is still tied
to the physical energy–entropy law and the positive cone. Thus it gives a
coordinate-free test that every weak-solution ansatz, reduced model, or
localized correction has to pass before one asks whether it can be realized by
a more delicate construction.

We prove three linked facts. First, after pressure and mean modes are
removed, the defect-work identity reduces admissibility to a signed pairing
between the conformation residual and the entropy-dual lever
G=I-A^{-1}
.
Second, this signed inequality has an optimal
L^{2}
cost and a unique aligned
minimizer whenever positive pressure-free work is present. Third, the
integrated form gives a three-channel alternative: positive work can be paid
only by a large lever, a large conformation residual, or strong negative
alignment. These three facts are then tested on power-law families and on an
explicit finite-thickness positive-cone shear layer.

1.2
Meaning of sharpness

There are three sharpness statements in the paper. First, the constant in the
pointwise residual-work inequality is forced by the energy–entropy identity.
Second, for fixed
G
and positive work
P
, the minimal
L^{2}

conformation residual is exactly the aligned field

-\frac{2P}{\alpha\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}G.

Third, the windowed inequality has the correct three-channel form:

\hbox{positive work}\leq\hbox{alignment}\times\hbox{lever}\times\hbox{residual}.

No channel can be removed. Near the cone tip
A=I
, the lever vanishes; for
nearly orthogonal residuals, the alignment vanishes; and for small residuals,
there is no work budget. Proposition
4.10
states
these optimality properties in residual-level form.

1.3
Scope of the result

The residual-work criterion is formulated for smooth profiles and smooth
residuals. On this class it is an exact admissibility criterion for the
pressure-free work balance: after the gauge reduction, no further hidden work
channel remains. What the theorem does not assert is that every residual
satisfying the signed work inequality can be generated by a genuine
positive-cone iteration or weak solution. That stronger realization problem
would require localization, commutator control in the logarithmic chart, and
compactness of the resulting energy–entropy inequality. Thus the theorem is
exact at the residual level and necessary for any later realization.

1.4
Canonical origin of defect admissibility

For a smooth exact solution, the Oldroyd–B free energy satisfies the usual
energy–entropy equality. If the same equations are relaxed by a symmetric
momentum defect
R
and a conformation defect
S
, the only new terms in
the equality are

-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}R:D(u)\,dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(I-A^{-1}):S\,dx.

Requiring this defect contribution to be non-positive is the direct analogue
of the Leray energy inequality for the relaxed system. This is the meaning of
Definition
3.2
. The definition does not prescribe a
particular representative of the momentum residual; the pressure-free
reduction below removes precisely that gauge freedom.

1.6
Comparison with unconstrained Reynolds defects

For incompressible Euler-type Reynolds states, a symmetric stress is constrained
primarily by its divergence and by the chosen admissibility inequality. In the
positive-cone Oldroyd–B setting there is a second constitutive channel: the
stress must arise from a positive conformation tensor and its residual must
interact with the entropy derivative
G=I-A^{-1}
. This produces a signed
restriction that is invisible if one treats the elastic stress as an arbitrary
symmetric tensor.

The residual-work criterion isolates exactly this restriction. The momentum
residual may be represented by a pressure-free symmetric antidivergence, but
positive pressure-free production is admissible only if the conformation
residual has a sufficiently negative projection onto
G
. Orthogonal
residuals cannot pay positive work, and aligned residuals are the unique
efficient way to do so. This is the sense in which the positive cone changes
the relaxed problem: it does not merely constrain eigenvalues of
A
, but
also constrains the signed direction of admissible residual work.

This also explains why the criterion is not a reformulation of the standard
energy estimate. The combined free-energy identity only sees the sum

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\bigl[-R:D(u)+(\alpha/2)G:S\bigr]\,dx.

Large positive kinetic defect work can therefore be hidden at the level of the
total balance if the conformation channel is allowed to compensate it. The
pressure-free reduction separates the observable work
P(t)
from the
available entropy payment
-(\alpha/2)\int G:S
, and so produces an algebraic
constraint on relaxed states. In this respect the result plays the same role
as sign or cone restrictions in differential-inclusion approaches: before a
defect can be realized by an iteration, it must first lie in the admissible
algebraic class.

Main theorem, informal form.

For every smooth positive-cone residual-matching Oldroyd–B Reynolds state,
energy–entropy admissibility forces the pressure-free positive work on each
time window
E
to obey

W_{E}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}L_{E}S_{E}.

Here
W_{E}
is the positive pressure-free work,
L_{E}
is the size of the
entropy lever
G=I-A^{-1}
,
S_{E}
is the conformation-residual size, and

\eta_{E}
is the negative-alignment coefficient. The constant and the
aligned minimizing residual are sharp. Hence any structured family violating
this inequality is not energy–entropy admissible.

The terminology used in the statements below is made precise in

Anatomy of the theorem.

The proof has four independent pieces. The defect-work identity converts the
energy–entropy inequality into a signed work balance. The pressure-free
reduction removes isotropic and mean modes, leaving a gauge-invariant number

P(t)
. The sharp pointwise cost is then a Hilbert-space minimization
against the lever
G=I-A^{-1}
. Finally, the windowed estimate follows by
integrating the pointwise inequality and taking the worst negative-alignment
coefficient on the time window. The structured-family and power-law tests are
consequences of this chain, not additional assumptions.

Theorem 1.1

(Residual-work obstruction)
.

Let
(u,p,B)
be a residual-matching profile on a time interval
I
, and
let
P(t)
be its pressure-free work in
Definition
4.2
. Define

A=e^{B},\qquad G=I-A^{-1}.

For a.e.
t
, set

\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\left[-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\,dx\right]_{+}}{\left\lVert G(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}},&\left\lVert G(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}>0,\\[11.99998pt]
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

If the associated residual-matching state is energy–entropy admissible, then

[P(t)]_{+}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t)\left\lVert G(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Consequently, on every measurable
E\subset I
,

W_{E}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}L_{E}S_{E},

where

W_{E}=\int_{E}[P(t)]_{+}\,dt,\quad L_{E}=\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})},\quad S_{E}=\left\lVert\mathcal{S}[u,B]\right\rVert_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})},\quad\eta_{E}=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in E}\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t).

For each fixed time, among all conformation residuals with the same lever

G(t)
, the smallest possible
L^{2}
-size needed to balance positive work is

\frac{2[P(t)]_{+}}{\alpha\left\lVert G(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}},

with the convention that no finite residual can balance positive work when

G(t)\equiv 0
. If
P(t)>0
and
G(t)\not\equiv 0
, the unique minimizer is

S_{\min}=-\frac{2P(t)}{\alpha\left\lVert G(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}G(t).

(Windowed residual-work data)
.

For a residual-matching profile and a measurable time window
E\subset I
,

The convention in the degenerate case is the one in

Theorem 1.3

(Closed structured-family test)
.

Let
(u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon})
be smooth residual-matching states on a
time window
E
, and let

W_{E}^{\varepsilon},\quad L_{E}^{\varepsilon},\quad S_{E}^{\varepsilon},\quad\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}

be their residual-work data. If there exist constants

such that

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}\geq W_{*},\qquad L_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq L_{*},\qquad S_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq S_{*},\qquad\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq\eta_{*},

and

W_{*}>\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{*}L_{*}S_{*},

then no member of the family is energy–entropy admissible on
E
.
In the algebraic converse direction, if the exact residual matching equations
hold,
P^{\varepsilon}\leq 0
whenever
G^{\varepsilon}\equiv 0
, and

\mathcal{S}[u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon}]=S_{\rm pay}^{\varepsilon}+Z^{\varepsilon},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G^{\varepsilon}:Z^{\varepsilon}\,dx\leq 0,

for a.e. time, where

S_{\rm pay}^{\varepsilon}=-\frac{2[P^{\varepsilon}]_{+}}{\alpha\left\lVert G^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}G^{\varepsilon}

on the set
\{G^{\varepsilon}\not\equiv 0\}
and

S_{\rm pay}^{\varepsilon}=0

\{G^{\varepsilon}\equiv 0\}
,

E
.

Proof.

The obstruction direction is immediate from
Theorem
1.1
. If an admissible member existed, then

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}L_{E}^{\varepsilon}S_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{*}L_{*}S_{*},

For the algebraic converse, insert

\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G:S_{\rm pay}\,dx=-[P]_{+}.

Definition 3.2

(Defect admissibility)
.

A smooth positive-cone Reynolds state is energy–entropy admissible if

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left[-R:D(u)+\frac{\alpha}{2}(I-A^{-1}):S\right]\,dx\leq 0\quad\hbox{for a.e. }t.

Definition 3.3

(Free energy and entropy lever)
.

For
A\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{2}
, define

\Phi(A)=\operatorname{tr}A-\log\det A-2,\qquad G(A)=D_{A}\Phi(A)=I-A^{-1}.

The free energy of a positive-cone state is

\mathcal{E}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\Phi(A(t))\,dx.

Lemma 3.4

(Lever bounds on spectral windows)
.

If the spectrum of
A
lies in
[m,M]
,
0<m<M<\infty
, then

|G(A)|\leq C_{m,M}|A-I|,\qquad|A-I|\leq C_{m,M}|G(A)|.

Near equilibrium,
G(A)=A-I+O(|A-I|^{2})
.

Proof.

The map
A\mapsto I-A^{-1}
is smooth on the compact spectral set

\{mI\leq A\leq MI\}
. Its derivative at

is the identity map on
symmetric matrices, which gives the local expansion.

Proposition 3.5

(Defect-work identity)
.

Every smooth positive-cone Reynolds state satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(t)

\displaystyle+\nu\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\alpha}{2\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\operatorname{tr}(A+A^{-1}-2I)\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}R:D(u)\,dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(I-A^{-1}):S\,dx.

Proof.

Testing (
1
) by
u
, integrating over the torus, and
using
\operatorname{div}u=0
, gives

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|u|^{2}\,dx+\nu\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx=-\alpha\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(A-I):\nabla u\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}R:D(u)\,dx.

The pressure and transport terms vanish, and
R:D(u)=R:\nabla u
because

R
is symmetric.

For the elastic part, since
D_{A}\Phi(A)=G=I-A^{-1}
,

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\Phi(A)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G:\partial_{t}A\,dx.

The transport term satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G:(u\cdot\nabla A)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u\cdot\nabla\Phi(A)\,dx=0.

Moreover
G
commutes with
A
, and

G:(\nabla u\,A+A(\nabla u)^{T})=2(A-I):\nabla u.

Finally,

G:(A-I)=\operatorname{tr}(A+A^{-1}-2I).

Pairing (
2
) with
\alpha G/2
therefore yields

\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\Phi(A)\,dx=\alpha\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(A-I):\nabla u\,dx-\frac{\alpha}{2\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\operatorname{tr}(A+A^{-1}-2I)\,dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G:S\,dx.

Adding the kinetic and elastic balances cancels the principal stress work and
gives the stated identity.
∎

4
Residual Matching and Sharp Cost

For smooth
u,p,B
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}[u,p,B]

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}u+\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)-\nu\Delta u+\nabla p-\alpha\operatorname{div}(A-I),

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}[u,B]

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}A+u\cdot\nabla A-\nabla u\,A-A(\nabla u)^{T}+\lambda^{-1}(A-I).

(4)

The residual matching equations are

\operatorname{div}R=\mathcal{M}[u,p,B],\qquad S=\mathcal{S}[u,B].

Definition 4.1

(Residual-matching profile)
.

A smooth triple
(u,p,B)
, with
\operatorname{div}u=0
and
A=e^{B}
, is called a
residual-matching profile if the mean of
\mathcal{M}[u,p,B]
vanishes for a.e.
time and if
S=\mathcal{S}[u,B]
is used as the conformation residual. A symmetric
tensor
R
is an admissible momentum representative for the profile if, for
some scalar pressure correction
\pi
,

\operatorname{div}R+\nabla\pi=\mathcal{M}[u,p,B].

Two representatives are identified if their difference is a divergence-free
symmetric tensor plus an isotropic tensor
\pi I
, since the latter changes
only the pressure correction in the momentum equation.

Definition 4.2

(Pressure-free work of a profile)
.

For a residual-matching profile, set

\mathcal{N}[u,B]=\partial_{t}u+\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)-\nu\Delta u-\alpha\operatorname{div}(A-I).

Let

\mathcal{N}_{0}(t,x)=\mathcal{N}(t,x)-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\mathcal{N}(t,y)\,dy

be the zero-mean part. Let
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{N}}(t)
be the affine space of
all symmetric tensors
R
for which there exists a scalar
\pi
satisfying

\operatorname{div}R+\nabla\pi=\mathcal{N}_{0}.

We quotient this affine space by the linear subspace

\{Q+pI:\ Q\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};\mathbb{S}^{2}),\ \operatorname{div}Q=0,\ p\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\}.

A pressure-free representative is any element of the resulting quotient class.
The pressure-free work is

P(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}-R_{\mathcal{N}}(t):D(u(t))\,dx.

By Lemma
4.5
, this number is independent of the representative.
The removed mean is the harmless mean-velocity forcing mode and does no work
against periodic gradients.

Remark 4.3

(Mean mode)
.

On the torus, a constant vector field cannot be represented as the divergence
of a periodic tensor. This is why the spatial mean of
\mathcal{N}
is removed in
Definition
4.2
. The residual-work obstruction is
concerned with deformation work, and
D(u)
only sees the gradient part of
the velocity. Fixing the mean velocity therefore removes no admissibility
information relevant to the pressure-free work.

Proposition 4.4

(Symmetric pressure-free antidivergence)
.

If a vector field
m
on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
has zero mean, then there is a symmetric
trace-free tensor
R_{m}
with

\operatorname{div}R_{m}=m,\qquad\left\lVert R_{m}\right\rVert_{H^{s+1}}\leq C_{s}\left\lVert m\right\rVert_{H^{s}}.

Proof.

Let
\Delta\phi=m
with zero mean and set

(R_{m})_{ij}=\partial_{i}\phi_{j}+\partial_{j}\phi_{i}-\delta_{ij}\operatorname{div}\phi.

Then
R_{m}
is symmetric, trace-free in two dimensions, and

\operatorname{div}R_{m}=m
.
∎

Lemma 4.5

(Gauge invariance of pressure-free work)
.

Let
R_{1},R_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};\mathbb{S}^{2})
satisfy

\operatorname{div}R_{1}=\operatorname{div}R_{2}

and assume
R_{1}-R_{2}
differs from a divergence-free symmetric tensor by an
isotropic pressure tensor. Then, for every divergence-free
u
,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}R_{1}:D(u)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}R_{2}:D(u)\,dx.

Proof.

Write
R_{1}-R_{2}=Q+pI
, with
\operatorname{div}Q=0
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}Q:D(u)\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\operatorname{div}Q)\cdot u\,dx=0,

and

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}pI:D(u)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}p\,\operatorname{div}u\,dx=0.

∎

Proposition 4.6

(Canonical pressure-free reduction)
.

For every residual-matching profile, the value of
P(t)
in
Definition
4.2
is independent of the chosen
pressure-free representative. Moreover, the energy–entropy admissibility of
the profile depends only on
P(t)
,
G(A)
, and
\mathcal{S}[u,B]
.

Proof.

The independence of
P(t)
is Lemma
4.5
. Substituting

S=\mathcal{S}[u,B]
into the defect-work identity shows that all representatives
with the same pressure-free work produce the same right-hand side in the
energy–entropy balance. Therefore admissibility is determined by

P(t)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\,dx\leq 0.

∎

Proposition 4.7

(Pressure-free residual work)
.

For a residual-matching profile, the admissibility condition is

P(t)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\,dx\leq 0,

for a.e.
t
.

Proof.

Adding an isotropic pressure tensor
pI
changes the divergence constraint
but not the work pairing, since
pI:D(u)=p\operatorname{div}u=0
. The remaining
gauge freedom is removed by Lemma
4.5
. Equivalently, this is the
canonical pressure-free reduction in
Proposition
4.6
.
∎

Proposition 4.8

(Sharp residual-work cost)
.

Assume
G(t)\not\equiv 0
. Among all conformation residuals
S
satisfying

P(t)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):S(t)\,dx\leq 0,

the minimal
L^{2}
norm is

\frac{2[P(t)]_{+}}{\alpha\left\lVert G(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}},

and, for
P(t)>0
, the unique minimizer is

-\frac{2P(t)}{\alpha\left\lVert G(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}G(t).

Proof.

The constraint requires

-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G:S\,dx\geq\frac{2P_{+}}{\alpha}.

Cauchy’s inequality gives

\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{2P_{+}}{\alpha},

with equality exactly when
S
is a negative multiple of
G
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The pressure-free identity (
4.7
) implies

[P(t)]_{+}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\,dx\right]_{+}.

Indeed, if
P(t)\leq 0
there is nothing to prove, while for
P(t)>0
the
admissibility inequality forces the negative signed projection of

\mathcal{S}[u,B]
onto
G
to pay
P(t)
. The positive part of this projection
is bounded by its absolute value, and the definition of
\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t)

therefore gives the pointwise bound in Theorem
1.1
, with

\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t)=0
on the zero-lever or zero-residual set.

Integrating the pointwise bound on
E
and using

\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t)\leq\eta_{E}=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in E}\eta_{\mathrm{al}}(t)
, we get

W_{E}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}\int_{E}\left\lVert G(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,dt.

Cauchy’s inequality in time gives

\int_{E}\left\lVert G(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,dt\leq L_{E}S_{E},

which is the windowed estimate in Theorem
1.1
. The sharp cost is
Proposition
4.8
.
∎

Corollary 4.9

(Three-channel alternative)
.

Fix a time window
E
and assume
W_{E}>0
. If a residual-matching state is
energy–entropy admissible, then at least one of the following must hold:

L_{E}\geq\left(\frac{2W_{E}}{\alpha\eta_{E}}\right)^{1/2},\qquad S_{E}\geq\left(\frac{2W_{E}}{\alpha\eta_{E}}\right)^{1/2},\qquad\eta_{E}\geq\frac{2W_{E}}{\alpha L_{E}S_{E}}.

Equivalently, a family with positive work can remain admissible only by
developing a large entropy lever, a large conformation residual, or a strong
negative alignment between them.

Proof.

The statement is a direct rewriting of

W_{E}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}L_{E}S_{E}.

If all three alternatives fail, then

\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}L_{E}S_{E}<W_{E},

because
\eta_{E}\leq 1
,
L_{E},S_{E}\geq 0
, and at least one of the three
displayed inequalities is then strict. This contradicts the windowed
residual-work estimate.
∎

Proposition 4.10

(Optimality of the residual-work channels)
.

The residual-work criterion is sharp in the following residual-level sense.

1.

The constant
\alpha/2
in

P(t)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):S(t)\,dx\leq 0

cannot be decreased.

2.

If
P(t)>0
and
G(t)\not\equiv 0
, the unique
L^{2}
-minimizing
paying residual is the aligned field

S_{\min}(t)=-\frac{2P(t)}{\alpha\left\lVert G(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}G(t).

3.

None of the three windowed channels
L_{E}
,
S_{E}
, and
\eta_{E}
can
be omitted from a necessary admissibility condition depending only on

(W_{E},L_{E},S_{E},\eta_{E})
.

Proof.

The first two assertions are the equality case in
Proposition
4.8
. Indeed, for
P(t)>0
, admissibility is
equivalent to

-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):S(t)\,dx\geq\frac{2P(t)}{\alpha}.

Cauchy’s inequality gives the minimal
L^{2}
norm and equality occurs only
when
S(t)
is a negative multiple of
G(t)
. Substituting the displayed

S_{\min}
gives equality in the admissibility inequality, so any smaller
constant than
\alpha/2
would exclude an exactly balanced residual.

For the third assertion, fix
W_{E}>0
. If
L_{E}
is allowed to be arbitrarily
large, the estimate can be satisfied with bounded
S_{E}
and
\eta_{E}=1
.
If
S_{E}
is allowed to be arbitrarily large, it can be satisfied with bounded

L_{E}
and
\eta_{E}=1
. If
\eta_{E}
is allowed to approach
1
, the
aligned residual in the second assertion realizes equality. Conversely, if
any one of the three quantities is kept too small while the other two are
fixed, Corollary
4.9
gives an inadmissible residual-work
tuple. Thus the product
\eta_{E}L_{E}S_{E}
is the complete windowed budget at
this level of information.
∎

Remark 4.11

(Cone-tip degeneracy)
.

When
A\approx I
, the lever
G=I-A^{-1}
is small. Thus a profile that
tries to keep the conformation close to equilibrium while generating positive
pressure-free Reynolds work must compensate by either producing a large
conformation residual or arranging nearly perfect negative alignment. This is
the algebraic form of the positive-cone obstruction near equilibrium.

5
Application: Structured Family Tests and a Concrete Obstruction

The next statements translate the residual-work obstruction into scale
criteria. They are useful because a localized ansatz usually comes with
separate estimates for work, lever, residual size, and alignment. The theorem
below says that no compensation outside these four channels is available at the
level of energy–entropy admissibility.

Corollary 5.1

(Bulk power-law exclusion test)
.

Let
(u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon})
,
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, be
smooth residual-matching states on the same time window
E
. Suppose

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}\geq c_{W}\varepsilon^{-w},\quad L_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{L}\varepsilon^{-\ell},\quad S_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{S}\varepsilon^{-r},\quad\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{\eta}\varepsilon^{-q}.

If
w>\ell+r+q
, then the family is not energy–entropy admissible on
E

for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. The same conclusion holds when

w=\ell+r+q
if

c_{W}>\frac{\alpha}{2}C_{\eta}C_{L}C_{S}.

Proof.

For small
\varepsilon
, (
5.1
) gives

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}>\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}L_{E}^{\varepsilon}S_{E}^{\varepsilon},

which contradicts Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Remark 5.2

(Exponent bookkeeping)
.

The bulk test is intentionally one-sided. The exponent
w
measures how fast
positive pressure-free work grows. The exponents
\ell,r,q
measure how much
budget is available through the lever, conformation residual, and alignment.
The inequality

w>\ell+r+q

means that the work grows faster than the entire admissible budget. In the
borderline case, the constants matter. This is useful for finite-thickness
ansatzes because the powers are usually robust under small changes of cutoff,
whereas the constants record the exact shape.

Definition 5.3

(Line-to-bulk data package)
.

Let
(u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon})
be a finite-thickness lift of a line state.
A data package on
E
consists of numbers

W_{\rm line},L_{\rm line},S_{\rm line},\eta_{\rm line},\delta_{W},\delta_{L},\delta_{S},\delta_{\eta}

such that

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}\geq W_{\rm line}-\delta_{W},\qquad L_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq L_{\rm line}+\delta_{L},

and

S_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{\rm lift}S_{\rm line}+\delta_{S},\qquad\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq\eta_{\rm line}+\delta_{\eta}.

Theorem 5.4

(Power-law active-line test)
.

Assume a finite-thickness lift admits a line-to-bulk package. If

W_{\rm line}^{\varepsilon}\geq c_{W}\varepsilon^{-w},\quad\delta_{W}^{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{c_{W}}{2}\varepsilon^{-w},

L_{\rm line}^{\varepsilon}+\delta_{L}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{L}\varepsilon^{-\ell},\quad C_{\rm lift}S_{\rm line}^{\varepsilon}+\delta_{S}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{R}\varepsilon^{-r},

and

\eta_{\rm line}^{\varepsilon}+\delta_{\eta}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{\eta}\varepsilon^{-q},

then
w>\ell+r+q
excludes energy–entropy admissibility for small

\varepsilon
. In the borderline case
w=\ell+r+q
, the same conclusion
holds if
c_{W}>\alpha C_{\eta}C_{L}C_{R}
.

Proof.

The data package converts the active-line bounds into the bulk bounds of
Corollary
5.1
, with a factor
1/2
in the work lower
bound.
∎

Remark 5.5

(What the active-line test does not assume)
.

Theorem
5.4
does not assume that the line equation is
strictly equivalent to the full Oldroyd–B system. It assumes only that a
finite-thickness lift has computable bulk residual-work data. This is why the
test remains meaningful even when the reduced model is used as a mechanism
detector rather than as an exact reduction.

5.1
Model examples and non-examples

We record two elementary uses of the criterion before the concrete
finite-thickness construction. The point is to distinguish failure of
admissibility from mere largeness of a residual.

Proposition 5.6

(Orthogonal residuals cannot pay positive work)
.

Let
(u,p,B)
be a residual-matching profile on
E
. Suppose

P(t)>0\quad\hbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\,dx=0

on a subset of
E
of positive measure. Then the profile is not
energy–entropy admissible on that subset.

Proof.

The pressure-free work identity gives

P(t)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\,dx=P(t)>0,

which directly violates

P(t)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)\,dx\leq 0.

∎

Proposition 5.7

(Aligned residuals are exactly efficient)
.

Let
G(t)\not\equiv 0
and
P(t)>0
. If

\mathcal{S}[u,B](t)=-\frac{2P(t)}{\alpha\left\lVert G(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}G(t),

then the pressure-free admissibility inequality is saturated at time
t
.
If an additional residual
Z(t)
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t):Z(t)\,dx\leq 0,

then replacing
\mathcal{S}
by
\mathcal{S}+Z
preserves admissibility.

Proof.

Substitution gives

\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G:\mathcal{S}\,dx=-P.

The additional term contributes a non-positive quantity to the left-hand side
of the admissibility inequality.
∎

5.2
A concrete finite-thickness obstruction

We now give a fully bulk example, not a formal one-dimensional reduction. The
profile is supported in a layer of thickness
\varepsilon
, remains inside the
positive cone by construction, and has an explicitly matched momentum and
conformation residual. The point of the example is that all four quantities
in the residual-work criterion can be computed on the same scale. The positive
pressure-free work grows faster than the entire lever–residual budget, so the
family fails admissibility for a structural reason rather than because of a
choice of gauge or a missing pressure mode.

The example is a residual-level construction. It is not used as an approximate
solution generated by the Oldroyd–B dynamics, and it does not by itself
produce a weak solution or a blow-up scenario. It shows instead that the
residual-work inequality has nontrivial content on explicit positive-cone bulk
profiles.

Lemma 5.8

(Finite-thickness scaling)
.

Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}((-1/4,1/4))
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, and

f_{\varepsilon}(x_{2})=\varepsilon^{-a}f(x_{2}/\varepsilon)

on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, for
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, by viewing the expression as a periodic
function of the coordinate
x_{2}
. Then

\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}=\varepsilon^{1/2-a}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},\qquad\left\lVert\partial_{2}f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}=\varepsilon^{-1/2-a}\left\lVert f^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

If
F
is a smooth bounded function with
F(0)=0
, then

\left\lVert F(f(x_{2}/\varepsilon))\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leq C_{F}\varepsilon^{1/2}.

Proof.

The first two identities follow from the change of variables

y=x_{2}/\varepsilon
. Since
f
is supported in
(-1/4,1/4)
, the layer is
contained in an interval of length
O(\varepsilon)
; for small
\varepsilon
, its
periodic copies do not overlap on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. The last estimate follows from
boundedness and the same change of variables.
∎

Proposition 5.9

(Concrete finite-thickness shear-layer obstruction)
.

Assume
\nu>0
. Fix a time window
E\subset[0,T]
of positive measure and

\beta>0
. Let
\theta,\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((-1/4,1/4))
, with

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\theta(y)\,dy=0,\qquad\theta^{\prime}\not\equiv 0,\qquad\chi\not\equiv 0.

Define

\theta_{\varepsilon}(x_{2})=\theta(x_{2}/\varepsilon),\qquad\chi_{\varepsilon}(x_{2})=\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon),

and set

H=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad u^{\varepsilon}(x)=\left(\varepsilon^{-\beta}\theta_{\varepsilon}(x_{2}),0\right),

B^{\varepsilon}(x)=b\chi_{\varepsilon}(x_{2})H,\qquad A^{\varepsilon}=e^{B^{\varepsilon}}.

Let

R^{\varepsilon}=-2\nu D(u^{\varepsilon})-\alpha(A^{\varepsilon}-I),\qquad S^{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{S}[u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon}].

Then

(u^{\varepsilon},0,B^{\varepsilon},R^{\varepsilon},S^{\varepsilon})
is a smooth
positive-cone Reynolds state with fixed mean velocity. Its residual-work data
obey

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}\geq c_{W}\varepsilon^{-(2\beta+1)},\qquad L_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{L}\varepsilon^{1/2},

S_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{S}\varepsilon^{-(\beta+1/2)},\qquad\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq 1.

Thus the bulk exponents may be taken as

w=2\beta+1,\qquad\ell=0,\qquad r=\beta+\frac{1}{2},\qquad q=0,

and the family is not energy–entropy admissible for all sufficiently small

\varepsilon
.

Proof.

The support and zero-mean assumptions on
\theta
make

u^{\varepsilon}
a smooth periodic divergence-free velocity with zero mean.
Since
B^{\varepsilon}
is symmetric,
A^{\varepsilon}=e^{B^{\varepsilon}}
is
positive definite. More explicitly,

A^{\varepsilon}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{b\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)}&0\\
0&e^{-b\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)}\end{pmatrix},

so
A^{\varepsilon}
,
(A^{\varepsilon})^{-1}
, and all smooth functions of
them are uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}
, independently of
\varepsilon
.
Writing
u^{\varepsilon}=(U^{\varepsilon}(x_{2}),0)
, one has

\operatorname{div}(u^{\varepsilon}\otimes u^{\varepsilon})=0,\qquad\operatorname{div}(2D(u^{\varepsilon}))=\Delta u^{\varepsilon}.

Therefore

\operatorname{div}R^{\varepsilon}=-\nu\Delta u^{\varepsilon}-\alpha\operatorname{div}(A^{\varepsilon}-I)=\mathcal{N}[u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon}],

and the conformation residual is matched by the definition of

S^{\varepsilon}
. Moreover
D(u^{\varepsilon})
and

A^{\varepsilon}-I
are symmetric, hence
R^{\varepsilon}
is a symmetric
representative of the zero-mean momentum residual.

The deformation tensor is purely off-diagonal:

\nabla u^{\varepsilon}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\partial_{2}U^{\varepsilon}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad D(u^{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&\partial_{2}U^{\varepsilon}\\
\partial_{2}U^{\varepsilon}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Since
A^{\varepsilon}-I
is diagonal, its Frobenius product with

D(u^{\varepsilon})
vanishes:

(A^{\varepsilon}-I):D(u^{\varepsilon})=0.

Thus

P^{\varepsilon}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}-R^{\varepsilon}:D(u^{\varepsilon})\,dx=2\nu\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|D(u^{\varepsilon})|^{2}\,dx.

Since

\partial_{2}U^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-\beta-1}\theta^{\prime}(x_{2}/\varepsilon),

and
|D(u^{\varepsilon})|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{2}U^{\varepsilon}|^{2}
, Lemma
5.8

gives

P^{\varepsilon}(t)=\nu\left\lVert\partial_{2}U^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}=\nu\varepsilon^{-(2\beta+1)}\left\lVert\theta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

Thus

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}=|E|\nu\left\lVert\theta^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\varepsilon^{-(2\beta+1)}.

The lever is

G^{\varepsilon}=I-(A^{\varepsilon})^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1-e^{-b\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)}&0\\
0&1-e^{b\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)}\end{pmatrix}.

It is a bounded smooth function of
b\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)
, vanishing outside a
layer of thickness
O(\varepsilon)
. Lemma
5.8
gives

L_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{L}\varepsilon^{1/2}.

Finally,

\mathcal{S}[u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon}]=-\nabla u^{\varepsilon}A^{\varepsilon}-A^{\varepsilon}(\nabla u^{\varepsilon})^{T}+\lambda^{-1}(A^{\varepsilon}-I),

because the state is steady and
u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla A^{\varepsilon}=0
.
The matrices
-\nabla u^{\varepsilon}A^{\varepsilon}
and

-A^{\varepsilon}(\nabla u^{\varepsilon})^{T}
are off-diagonal and contain the
factor
\partial_{2}U^{\varepsilon}
multiplied by the uniformly bounded
entries
e^{\pm b\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)}
. Hence their
L^{2}
norm is bounded by

C\varepsilon^{-(\beta+1/2)}.

The relaxation term is a bounded smooth function of
b\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)
, so its

L^{2}
norm is
O(\varepsilon^{1/2})
. Therefore

S_{E}^{\varepsilon}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-(\beta+1/2)}.

Using the weaker lever bound
L_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{L}
corresponds to

\ell=0
. Since
2\beta+1>\beta+1/2
, the bulk power-law exclusion test
applies.
∎

Corollary 5.10

(Scale table for the shear layer)
.

For the family in Proposition
5.9
, the leading powers are

\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}\hbox{quantity}&\hbox{definition}&\hbox{scale}\\
\hline\cr W_{E}^{\varepsilon}&\displaystyle\int_{E}[P^{\varepsilon}(t)]_{+}\,dt&\varepsilon^{-(2\beta+1)}\\[3.99994pt]
L_{E}^{\varepsilon}&\left\lVert I-(A^{\varepsilon})^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}&\varepsilon^{1/2}\\[3.99994pt]
S_{E}^{\varepsilon}&\left\lVert\mathcal{S}[u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon}]\right\rVert_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}&\varepsilon^{-(\beta+1/2)}\\[3.99994pt]
\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon}&\hbox{alignment coefficient}&\leq 1\end{array}

Consequently,

\frac{W_{E}^{\varepsilon}}{L_{E}^{\varepsilon}S_{E}^{\varepsilon}}\gtrsim\varepsilon^{-(\beta+1)}\to\infty.

If one uses only the rougher bound
L_{E}^{\varepsilon}\leq C
, the ratio still diverges
like
\varepsilon^{-(\beta+1/2)}
.

Proof.

The table collects the estimates proved in
Proposition
5.9
. Dividing the work scale by the
lever–residual scale gives

\frac{\varepsilon^{-(2\beta+1)}}{\varepsilon^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-(\beta+1/2)}}=\varepsilon^{-(\beta+1)}.

The rough lever estimate replaces
\varepsilon^{1/2}
by
1
.
∎

Remark 5.11

(Why viscosity makes the obstruction explicit)
.

The explicit layer uses the viscous part of the momentum equation to generate
large positive pressure-free work:

P^{\varepsilon}=2\nu\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|D(u^{\varepsilon})|^{2}\,dx.

Thus the obstruction is clearest when
\nu>0
. In the inviscid case, a
different source of positive work would be needed, typically coming from
transport or from an imposed Reynolds stress. The residual-work criterion
itself is independent of this choice; only the concrete ansatz changes.

Corollary 5.12

(Gauge-invariant failure of the shear layer)
.

The obstruction is not produced by choosing an arbitrary unfavorable defect.
The fields
(u^{\varepsilon},B^{\varepsilon})
determine the bulk momentum and
conformation residuals. The tensor
R^{\varepsilon}
in
(
5.9
) is only an explicit symmetric antidivergence of the
momentum residual. Any other symmetric representative gives the same
pressure-free work. Hence failure of admissibility is a property of the bulk
residual-work tuple of the layer, not a gauge artifact.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{R}^{\varepsilon}
be another symmetric representative of the
same momentum residual, modulo an isotropic pressure tensor and a
divergence-free symmetric tensor. Lemma
4.5
gives

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\widetilde{R}^{\varepsilon}:D(u^{\varepsilon})\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}R^{\varepsilon}:D(u^{\varepsilon})\,dx.

Thus the pressure-free work
P^{\varepsilon}
, and hence

(W_{E}^{\varepsilon},L_{E}^{\varepsilon},S_{E}^{\varepsilon},\eta_{E}^{\varepsilon})
, is
unchanged by the choice of representative. Corollary
5.10

therefore gives the same violation of the residual-work inequality for every
representative in the gauge class.
∎

6
Entropy-Dual Closures

The residual-work argument is an entropy-dual statement. Once the elastic
stress work cancels against the stretching part of the entropy identity, the
pressure-free admissibility proof uses only the pairing between the
conformation residual and the corresponding entropy lever. This section
records the abstract closure structure behind this cancellation and then
specializes it to FENE-P.

Definition 6.1

(Entropy-dual closure)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{S}_{++}^{2}
be an open matrix domain. A pair

(\Phi,T)
, with
\Phi\in C^{2}(\mathcal{D})
and

T:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
, is called an entropy-dual closure if

G(C):=D\Phi(C),\qquad G(C)C=T(C)

for all
C\in\mathcal{D}
, and if
G(C)
commutes with
C
. A relaxation

\mathcal{R}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
is
entropy-dissipative for this closure if

G(C):\mathcal{R}(C)\geq 0,\qquad C\in\mathcal{D}.

Theorem 6.2

(Residual-work law for entropy-dual closures)
.

Let
(\Phi,T,\mathcal{R})
be an entropy-dual closure with
entropy-dissipative relaxation on
\mathcal{D}
. Let
C(t,x)\in\mathcal{D}
and let

R,S\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
be smooth residuals in

\partial_{t}u+\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)-\nu\Delta u+\nabla p=\alpha\operatorname{div}T(C)+\operatorname{div}R,

\partial_{t}C+u\cdot\nabla C-\nabla u\,C-C(\nabla u)^{T}+\mathcal{R}(C)=S.

Then

\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\Phi(C(t))\,dx

satisfies

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}(t)+\nu\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(C):\mathcal{R}(C)\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}R:D(u)\,dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(C):S\,dx.

Consequently, after the pressure-free reduction, every admissible
residual-matching profile satisfies on each measurable
E\subset I

W_{E}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}^{\Phi}L_{E}^{\Phi}S_{E}^{\Phi},

where

L_{E}^{\Phi}=\left\lVert G(C)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})},\qquad S_{E}^{\Phi}=\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})},

and

\eta_{E}^{\Phi}=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in E}\frac{\left[-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(C(t)):S(t)\,dx\right]_{+}}{\left\lVert G(C(t))\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert S(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}},

with value
0
when the denominator vanishes. For fixed
t
,
P(t)>0
,
and
G(C(t))\not\equiv 0
, the sharp paying residual is

S_{\min}^{\Phi}=-\frac{2P(t)}{\alpha\left\lVert G(C(t))\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}G(C(t)).

Proof.

The kinetic energy identity gives the stress work as

-\alpha\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}T(C):\nabla u\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}R:D(u)\,dx.

Testing the conformation equation by
\alpha G(C)/2
gives

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\Phi(C)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(C):\bigl(\nabla u\,C+C(\nabla u)^{T}\bigr)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(C):\mathcal{R}(C)\,dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(C):S\,dx.

Since
G(C)C=T(C)
and
C
commutes with
G(C)
, the stretching term equals

\alpha\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}T(C):\nabla u\,dx,

and cancels the stress work. This proves the defect-work identity. The
pressure-free reduction, the pointwise Cauchy–Schwarz estimate, and the
time-window estimate are the same as in Theorem
1.1
,
with
G(A)
replaced by
G(C)=D\Phi(C)
.
∎

Remark 6.3

(Commutation hypothesis)
.

The identity
G(C)C=T(C)
is the compatibility relation that cancels stress
work. In the isotropic closures used below,
G(C)
and
C
commute because
both are functions of
C
. For a non-isotropic closure, this commutation is
part of the entropy-dual structure.

Corollary 6.4

(Hookean Oldroyd–B closure)
.

For

\mathcal{D}=\mathbb{S}_{++}^{2},\qquad\Phi(C)=\operatorname{tr}C-\log\det C-2,\qquad T(C)=C-I,\qquad\mathcal{R}(C)=\lambda^{-1}(C-I),

one has
G(C)=I-C^{-1}
,
G(C)C=T(C)
, and

G(C):\mathcal{R}(C)=\lambda^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(C+C^{-1}-2I)\geq 0.

Thus Theorem
6.2
recovers the Oldroyd–B
residual-work law.

With
b>2
, set

\mathcal{D}_{b}=\{C\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{2}:\operatorname{tr}C<b\},\qquad f_{b}(C)=\frac{b-2}{b-\operatorname{tr}C},

and use the Peterlin stress

T_{b}(C)=f_{b}(C)C-I.

The finite-extension boundary
\operatorname{tr}C=b
is now part of the admissibility
geometry, not a coordinate artifact.

Definition 6.5

(FENE-P entropy lever)
.

For
C\in\mathcal{D}_{b}
, define

\Phi_{b}(C)=-(b-2)\log\!\left(1-\frac{\operatorname{tr}C}{b}\right)-\log\det C,

and

G_{b}(C)=D\Phi_{b}(C)=\frac{b-2}{b-\operatorname{tr}C}I-C^{-1}.

For
K<\infty
and
\delta>0
, the compact FENE-P envelope is

\mathcal{K}_{K,\delta}^{\rm FENE}=\{C\in\mathcal{D}_{b}:\left\lVert\operatorname{Log}C\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq K,\ b-\operatorname{tr}C\geq\delta\}.

Corollary 6.6

(FENE-P residual-work criterion)
.

For the FENE-P closure on
\mathcal{D}_{b}
,

\Phi_{b}(C)=-(b-2)\log\!\left(1-\frac{\operatorname{tr}C}{b}\right)-\log\det C,\qquad T_{b}(C)=f_{b}(C)C-I,

and
\mathcal{R}_{b}(C)=\lambda^{-1}T_{b}(C)
, the compatibility relation

G_{b}(C)C=T_{b}(C)

holds with

G_{b}(C)=D\Phi_{b}(C)=f_{b}(C)I-C^{-1}.

Let
(u,p,C)
be a smooth FENE-P residual-matching profile on a time interval

I
, and assume that
C(t,x)\in\mathcal{D}_{b}
. Let
P(t)
be the same
pressure-free work as in Definition
4.2
, but with

\alpha\operatorname{div}(A-I)
replaced by
\alpha\operatorname{div}T_{b}(C)
. Define

L_{E}^{\rm FENE}=\left\lVert G_{b}(C)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})},\qquad S_{E}^{\rm FENE}=\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{L^{2}(E\times\mathbb{T}^{2})},

and

\eta_{E}^{\rm FENE}=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in E}\frac{\left[-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G_{b}(C(t)):S(t)\,dx\right]_{+}}{\left\lVert G_{b}(C(t))\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert S(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}},

with value
0
when the denominator vanishes. If the FENE-P residual state
is energy–entropy admissible, then on every measurable
E\subset I
,

W_{E}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}^{\rm FENE}L_{E}^{\rm FENE}S_{E}^{\rm FENE}.

For each fixed time, the sharp paying residual is aligned with

-G_{b}(C)
:

S_{\min}^{\rm FENE}=-\frac{2P(t)}{\alpha\left\lVert G_{b}(C(t))\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}G_{b}(C(t)),\qquad P(t)>0.

Proof.

The formula for
G_{b}
is Definition
6.5
. The relation

G_{b}(C)C=T_{b}(C)
follows immediately. Moreover

G_{b}(C):T_{b}(C)=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(C^{-1}T_{b}(C)^{2}\bigr)\geq 0,

because
G_{b}(C)=T_{b}(C)C^{-1}
and
C
commutes with
T_{b}(C)
. The
windowed estimate and sharp minimizer are then Theorem
6.2
.
∎

Remark 6.7

(Finite extensibility in the residual-work law)
.

The FENE-P criterion differs from the Hookean one in two places. First,
admissibility requires the finite-extension margin
b-\operatorname{tr}C>0
. Second, the
entropy-dual lever is

G_{b}(C)=f_{b}(C)I-C^{-1}.

On compact FENE-P envelopes
\mathcal{K}_{K,\delta}^{\rm FENE}
, this is a smooth
finite-extensibility analogue of the Hookean lever. As
b\to\infty
away
from the FENE boundary,
f_{b}(C)\to 1
and
G_{b}(C)\to I-C^{-1}
, recovering
the Oldroyd–B residual-work law.

Corollary 6.8

(FENE-P structured-family obstruction)
.

For a family of smooth FENE-P residual profiles on a time window
E
, suppose

W_{E}^{\varepsilon}\geq c_{W}\varepsilon^{-w},\quad L_{E,{\rm FENE}}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{L}\varepsilon^{-\ell},\quad S_{E,{\rm FENE}}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{S}\varepsilon^{-r},\quad\eta_{E,{\rm FENE}}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{\eta}\varepsilon^{-q}.

If
w>\ell+r+q
, then the family is not FENE-P
energy–entropy admissible for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. The
same conclusion holds at equality of exponents if

c_{W}>\frac{\alpha}{2}C_{\eta}C_{L}C_{S}.

Proof.

This is the bulk power-law test applied to
(
6.6
).
∎

7
Discussion: Admissibility vs. Realization

7.1
Positive-cone correctors

The residual-work law also gives a precise necessary condition for any proposed
repair of an inadmissible profile. Changing the pressure or the
pressure-free representative cannot help; positive work can be paid only by a
negative signed component in the conformation residual.

Proposition 7.1

(Necessary signed component of any corrector)
.

Let
P(t)>0
and
G(t)\not\equiv 0
. If a correction

\widetilde{S}
makes the pressure-free work admissible, then its projection
onto
-G
must satisfy

\left\langle\widetilde{S},-\frac{G}{\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{L^{2}}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{2P(t)}{\alpha\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{L^{2}}}.

In particular, a correction that is
L^{2}
-orthogonal to
G
cannot pay any
positive work.

Proof.

The admissibility inequality requires

-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G:\widetilde{S}\,dx\geq\frac{2P(t)}{\alpha}.

Dividing by
\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
gives the claimed projection lower bound.
∎

Remark 7.2

(Constructive meaning)
.

The proposition is only a necessary direction. It identifies the signed
component a correction must contain, but it does not construct a localized
positive-cone iteration realizing such a component.

7.2
Residual-level exactness and the realization problem

The results above are exact at the residual level. Given a smooth
positive-cone profile and its residuals, the theorem decides whether the
pressure-free work can be paid by the conformation residual in the
energy–entropy balance. This does not imply realization by genuine solutions.
Realization would require producing the residual through
A=e^{B}
, localizing
the correction without losing the work balance, and passing to a limit while
preserving the energy–entropy inequality.

Remark 7.3

(Open problem)
.

Does there exist a smooth positive-cone Reynolds state satisfying the
residual-work admissibility inequality, but which cannot be approximated by
any sequence of energy–entropy admissible Oldroyd–B weak solutions? An
affirmative answer would show that the present criterion is sharp as a
necessary residual-level condition but not sufficient for realization. A
negative answer would amount to a positive-cone realization theorem.

Remark 7.4

(Connection with large-data closure)
.

For the large-data regularity problem, the theorem gives a necessary
diagnostic rather than a closure theorem. Any proposed closure mechanism must
prevent positive pressure-free work from outrunning the available
lever–residual–alignment budget. Conversely, a blow-up or non-compactness
construction that is visible at the residual level must force either a growing
lever, a growing conformation residual, or increasingly efficient negative
alignment. The theorem does not assert that every such residual scenario can
be dynamically realized.

8
Conclusion

The positive-cone parametrization
A=e^{B}
removes the positivity constraint
from the relaxed Oldroyd–B problem. The remaining admissibility condition is
a signed residual-work law. The main theorem says that positive pressure-free
Reynolds production can be admissible only if the conformation residual has
sufficient size and negative alignment against
G=I-A^{-1}
:

W_{E}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\eta_{E}L_{E}S_{E}.

This gives an exact residual-level answer to the admissibility problem for
smooth positive-cone Reynolds profiles. It yields sharp pointwise costs,
closed structured-family tests, bulk and active-line power-law exclusions, and
a concrete finite-thickness shear-layer obstruction.

The entropy-dual closure theorem places the Hookean and FENE-P cases in the
same residual-work language. In the FENE-P case the state space is

\mathcal{D}_{b}=\{C>0:\operatorname{tr}C<b\}
, the stress is the Peterlin stress
T_{b}(C)
, and
the entropy-dual lever is

G_{b}(C)=\frac{b-2}{b-\operatorname{tr}C}I-C^{-1}.

Thus the finite-extension margin is part of the admissibility geometry, while
the pressure-free work law itself remains a signed entropy-dual alignment
condition. In the Hookean limit, away from the FENE boundary, this lever
converges to
I-C^{-1}
, recovering the Oldroyd–B criterion.

The finite-thickness layer shows that the law has genuine content in bulk
variables: positive work can outrun all available
lever–residual–alignment budget even when the conformation tensor remains
positive by construction. This necessary admissibility test remains distinct
from the realization problem. A profile may satisfy the residual-work
inequality and still fail to arise from a genuine weak solution,
convex-integration scheme, or localized positive-cone correction.

Two questions remain open. First, the realization problem in
Remark
7.3
asks whether residual-work admissibility can be
upgraded to approximation by true admissible Oldroyd–B states. Second, can
one construct localized logarithmic correctors whose leading conformation
residual realizes the optimal direction

-G/\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
while keeping the full energy–entropy inequality stable
under passage to the limit? These questions mark the boundary between the
exact residual-level criterion proved here and a future realization theory for
positive-cone Oldroyd–B Reynolds states.
