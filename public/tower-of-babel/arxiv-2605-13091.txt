Orbits of subgroups of codimension one to four of the Iwahori group in the affine flag variety of SL 2 \SL_{2}
Claude Eicher

Abstract
. We describe how each finite dimensional Schubert cell in the affine flag variety of SL 2 \SL_{2} decomposes into orbits for a chain of subgroups of codimension one to four of the Iwahori group.



1.
Introduction

In this article we describe how each finite dimensional Schubert cell in the affine flag variety of
\SL_{2}
over
\C

decomposes into orbits for the subgroups of the standard Iwahori group
I
in the chain

(1.1)

\displaystyle I\supset I_{\{\alpha_{1}\}}\supset I_{\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{0}\}}\supset I_{\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{0},\delta+\alpha_{1}\}}\supset I_{\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{0},\delta,\delta+\alpha_{1}\}}\;.

(Recall that in the affine flag variety the finite dimensional Schubert cells
are precisely the
I
-orbits.) Here
I_{S}\subset I
is the subgroup obtained by
removing from
I
the root subgroup associated to each element of
S
, a finite set of positive affine
roots of the corresponding affine Kac-Moody Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
. As usual the simple roots are denoted by
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{0}

and
\delta=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{0}
.
In the case of the last subgroup
I_{\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{0},\delta,\delta+\alpha_{1}\}}
we include the multiplicative group of loop rotations in
order for each Schubert cell to decompose into finitely many orbits.
We find that for each containment in (
1.1
) an orbit
O

is either again an orbit for the subgroup or decomposes as
O=O^{\hyp}\sqcup O^{\open}
, where
O^{\hyp}
is the locus of a hyperplane in natural coordinates of
O
and
O^{\open}
is its open complement. The natural
coordinates are restrictions of the natural coordinates describing the Schubert cell as an affine space.
If we label the orbits in this way, each
I_{S}
-orbit is named by the Schubert cell in which it is contained.

In the subsequent text we abbreviate the subgroups under consideration by
I_{1}=I_{\{\alpha_{1}\}}
,

I_{2}=I_{\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{0}\}}
,
I_{3}=I_{\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{0},\delta+\alpha_{1}\}}
, and
I_{4}=I_{\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{0},\delta,\delta+\alpha_{1}\}}
,
the index being the codimension in
I
.

We consider the loop group

The following lemma is well-known.

Lemma 2.1

For each

there is a unique

\displaystyle gI=\begin{pmatrix}t^{n}&p\\

We abbreviate this

Proof.

Existence

we are in the second case if and only if
\nu(d)-\nu(c)\geq 0

Case

Then

and we have

\displaystyle gI

\displaystyle=g\begin{pmatrix}d_{(0)}&0\\
-t^{-\nu(d)}c&d_{(0)}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}I=\begin{pmatrix}t^{-\nu(d)}&bd_{(0)}^{-1}\\

0&1\end{pmatrix}I

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}t^{-\nu(d)}&bd_{(0)}^{-1}+t^{-\nu(d)}s\\

for

is as in the first case indicated.

Case

Then

and we have

\displaystyle gI

\displaystyle=g\begin{pmatrix}-c_{(0)}^{-1}&t^{-\nu(c)}d\\

-t^{\nu(c)}&0\end{pmatrix}I=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{a}{c_{(0)}}&t^{-\nu(c)}\\
-t^{\nu(c)}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
ts&1\end{pmatrix}I

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{a}{c_{(0)}}+t^{-\nu(c)+1}s&t^{-\nu(c)}\\
-t^{\nu(c)}&0\end{pmatrix}I

with

is as in the second case indicated.

Uniqueness
.
Consider the case when
g_{1}I=g_{2}I
are in the first case. Thus we assume for some

\lx@nobreakspace tc&d\end{pmatrix}\in I

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\lx@nobreakspace t^{n}&p\\
\lx@nobreakspace 0&t^{-n}\end{pmatrix}g=\begin{pmatrix}\lx@nobreakspace t^{n}a+tpc&t^{n}b+pd\\
\lx@nobreakspace t^{-n+1}c&t^{-n}d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\lx@nobreakspace t^{m}&q\\

Thus

,

. Thus

.

Next, consider the case when

g_{1}I

in the first case. Thus we assume for some

,

3.

-Orbits

For

Lemma 3.1

Every point of
\SL_{2}((t))/I

or

Proof.

By Lemma

1.

We indeed have

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&-t^{m+n}p_{(0)}\\
\lx@nobreakspace 0&1\end{pmatrix}\lx@nobreakspace\begin{pmatrix}\lx@nobreakspace t^{n}&p\\
\lx@nobreakspace 0&t^{-n}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}t^{n}&0\\

-t^{-m-n}&p_{(0)}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}t^{n}&p\\

-p_{(0)}^{-1}t^{n-m}&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&t^{m}\\

3.
[n,p]^{\prime}\in O_{n}

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&p_{(0)}t^{m+n}\\

-t^{-n}&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&t^{n}\\
-t^{-n}&0\end{pmatrix}\;.

4.

We indeed have

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}p_{(0)}^{-1}&0\\
t^{-m-n}&p_{(0)}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p&t^{n}\\
