LIPSCHITZ REGULARITY FOR PARABOLIC FRACTIONAL p p -LAPLACE EQUATIONS
HARSH PRASAD

Abstract
We prove that local weak solutions to nonlocal parabolic p p -Laplace equations are locally Lipschitz continuous in space, uniformly in time for every 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty and s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) whenever s ​ p > p − 1 sp>p-1 . Our results hold for symmetric, translation-invariant kernels satisfying standard ellipticity bounds, including kernels that may be discontinuous and require only that the tail of the solution be bounded. In the linear case, our proof provides a different route avoiding blow up arguments and Liouville theorems.

1.  Introduction

We are interested in studying gradient regularity for weak solutions to the following non-linear, nonlocal parabolic equation:

\partial_{t}u+L_{K}u=0\quad\text{in }\Omega\times I,

(1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is open,
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an open
interval, and

L_{K}u(x,t):=\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))\,K(x-y)\,dy.

The kernel
K:\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\to(0,\infty)
is
symmetric and satisfies

\frac{\lambda}{|z|^{N+sp}}\leq K(z)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|z|^{N+sp}}

(2)

for fixed constants
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda<\infty
,

p\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in(0,1)
. We do
not
assume

K
to be continuous. The model case is the fractional

p
-Laplace equation

\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=0,

which corresponds to
K(z)=|z|^{-(N+sp)}
.

1.1. Background and Novelty

Local regularity for nonlocal, nonlinear equations has been
intensively studied over the past decade. Local boundedness and
Hölder continuity for equations with kernels
satisfying (
2
) were established in the
elliptic case in
[
12
,
10
,
1
]
and in the parabolic case
in
[
2
,
25
,
13
,
26
]
. These results hold for
the full class of kernels satisfying (
2
) including kernels that are not translation invariant. However, as is typically the case with DeGiorgi-Nash-Moser techniques, they yield only a
small, non-explicit Hölder exponent. It is natural to ask if we can get sharp exponents under less general assumptions on the kernel.

For the fractional
p
-Laplacian, sharp higher Hölder regularity in space was
obtained in
[
8
]
for
p\geq 2

and in
[
17
]
for
1<p<2
, with the
optimal spatial exponent

\gamma^{\circ}=\min\{1,sp/(p-1)\}
. In particular, when

sp>p-1
, solutions were shown to be
almost
Lipschitz. The
parabolic analogues, including higher Hölder regularity in
both space and time, appear
in
[
9
,
16
]
.

Whether solutions are actually Lipschitz continuous in space
when
sp>p-1
remained open in both the elliptic and
parabolic settings. In the elliptic case, this was recently
settled by Biswas–Topp
[
7
]
, who introduced
the Ishii–Lions method
[
19
]
into the nonlocal
nonlinear context. These methods were extended to yield better regularity for fractional
p-
harmonic functions in
[
6
]
and subsequently,
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity for fractional
p-
harmonic functions was
established
in
[
18
]
. Earlier applications
of Ishii–Lions methods to nonlocal
equations
[
4
,
3
,
5
]
relied
on a sub-additive (Pucci-type) structure that leads to useful
cancellations; this structure is unavailable for the fractional

p
-Laplacian, and
[
7
]
handles this
difficulty through a careful treatment of the
I_{3}
integral (see
Section

5.3
and
Section

5.3
.)

Very recently, and independently of the present work,
Jesus–Sobral–Urbano
[
20
]
established
spatial Lipschitz regularity for weak solutions to the
parabolic fractional
p
-Laplace equation in the degenerate
range
p\geq 2
, as well as time regularity via a
barrier argument. Our results overlap with theirs in this range
for the explicit kernel
K(z)=|z|^{-(N+sp)}
, but differ in
several significant respects.

(i)

Subquadratic case.
We treat the full range

p>1
, including
1<p<2
. The subquadratic case is not a simple extension
of the superquadratic case since in the latter case we can evaluate the operator on smooth
functions whereas in the former case we cannot do so without running into singularities. This is the main reason why in
[
21
]
, one needs to work with an auxilliary class of test functions in the definition of viscosity solutions. In particular, extending
[
20
]
directly to the subquadratic case would need the introduction of such special class of test functions in the parabolic setup. On the other hand, we circumvent the issue entirely.

(ii)

Discontinuous kernels.
We work with a more general class of kernels including discontinuous kernels. Doing so brings it in line with known results in the elliptic case where spatial Lipschitz
regularity is known for discontinuous, translation invariant, symmetric kernels
[
27
,
14
]
. Whereas in the linear case, such a result proceeds via heat kernel estimates, Liouville theorems and blow up arguments
[
15
, Theorem 2.4.3]
, our proof is different.

(iii)

Weaker tail assumption.
We impose only

u\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I;\,L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
,
that is, the tail of
u
is merely bounded in time.
The paper
[
20
]
requires the
strictly stronger condition

u\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}(I;\,L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
,
i.e. continuity of the tail in time. Such an assumption also occurs when studying linear
equations
[
24
]
. We bypass the requirement.

(iv)

A parametrised family of Ishii-Lions functionals
The
paper
[
20
]
relies on the the Jensen–Ishii
lemma
[
11
, Theorem 8.3]
to produce limiting
jets. We eschew the usage of the
viscosity machinery by working with a parametrised family of Ishii-Lions functionals (cf.
Section

5.1
)

We also note that
[
20
]
establishes a
full viscosity solution framework for the parabolic fractional

p
-Laplacian when
p>2
, including the equivalence between weak and
viscosity solutions and a comparison principle for viscosity
solutions.

1.2. Main Theorem

For
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
and
R,S>0
we write

Q_{R,S}(x_{0},t_{0}):=B_{R}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-S,t_{0}]
.
Given a weak solution
u
in
Q_{2R,2R^{sp}}(x_{0},t_{0})
,
define

M_{R}:=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2R,2R^{sp}}(x_{0},t_{0}))}+\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(u;\,x_{0},R,\,(t_{0}-2R^{sp},t_{0}])+1.

(3)

Theorem 1.1

(Spatial Lipschitz regularity)
.

Let
u
be a local weak solution to (
1
). Suppose
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Omega\times I

and
R>0
are such that

Q_{2R,2R^{sp}}(x_{0},t_{0})\Subset\Omega\times I
.

Then
u(\cdot,t)
is locally Lipschitz in space,
uniformly in time and with
M_{R}
as
in (
3
), the following estimates hold for
all
t\in(t_{0}-R^{sp}/2,\,t_{0}]
:

\displaystyle\sup_{t}\,[u(\cdot,t)]_{C^{0,1}(B_{R/2}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\,M_{R}\,R^{-1}

\displaystyle\text{if }p\geq 2,

(4)

\displaystyle\sup_{t}\,[u(\cdot,t)]_{C^{0,1}(B_{R/2}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\,M_{R}^{1+\frac{2-p}{sp}}\,R^{-1}

\displaystyle\text{if }1<p<2.

(5)

The constant
C>0
depends only on
N,s,p,\lambda,\Lambda
.

The proof is given in Section
5
. It combines
the touching lemma
(Proposition
3
),
the scaling reduction
(Section
4
), and the Ishii–Lions
argument (Section
5.1
).

Remark 1.2

(Sharpness of the condition)
.

Our result extends to equations with a bounded source term (cf.
Section

6
) and thus the condition
sp>p-1
is sharp for the method
[
8
, Example 1.6]
. However, as in
[
6
]
, it may be possible to extend the range of exponents if we assume higher regularity on the kernel.

Remark 1.3

(Time regularity)
.

The spatial Lipschitz estimate of Theorem
1.1

can be used to improve time regularity (for more general kernels) via a barrier
argument; we do not pursue it here.

Remark 1.4

(Discontinuous kernels)
.

. All prior higher regularity results
(
[
8
,
17
,
9
,
16
]
) worked with the explicit
kernel
K(z)=|z|^{-(N+sp)}
.
Theorem
1.1
holds for any
K

satisfying (
2
), including
discontinuous ones. Our method extends to proving higher Hölder regularity and thus generalises these previous works (cf.
Section

6
).

6
we state (without proofs)

(p,q)
-Laplacian

2.  Preliminaries

Notation

We write
B_{R}(x_{0}):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|x-x_{0}|<R\}
and

B_{R}:=B_{R}(0)
. For
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
and

R,S>0
we set

Q_{R,S}(x_{0},t_{0}):=B_{R}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-S,\,t_{0}],\qquad Q_{R,S}:=Q_{R,S}(0,0).

The conjugate exponent of
p\in(1,\infty)
is

p^{\prime}:=p/(p-1)
. We write
J_{p}(t):=|t|^{p-2}t
for

t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Function spaces

Fractional Sobolev spaces.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set,
1<p<\infty
and
s\in(0,1)
. The fractional Sobolev space

W^{s,p}(E)
consists of all
u\in L^{p}(E)
such that

[u]_{W^{s,p}(E)}^{p}:=\int_{E}\int_{E}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dx\,dy<\infty.

It is a reflexive Banach space under the norm
\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(E)}:=\|u\|_{L^{p}(E)}+[u]_{W^{s,p}(E)}
. The local
space
W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(E)
consists of all
u

with
u\in W^{s,p}(U)
for every open
U\Subset E
.

Tail space.

The tail space
L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined by

L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\Bigl\{u\in L^{p-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}}{1+|x|^{N+sp}}\,dx<\infty\Bigr\}.

For
u(\cdot,t)\in L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

and
r>0
, we set

\mathrm{Tail}(u(\cdot,t);\,x_{0},r):=\Bigl(r^{sp}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(x,t)|^{p-1}}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+sp}}\,dx\Bigr)^{\!\frac{1}{p-1}}.

For a time interval
I=(t_{1},t_{2})
we define the
parabolic tail

\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(u;\,x_{0},r,I):=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in I}\,\mathrm{Tail}(u(\cdot,t);\,x_{0},r).

Hölder seminorms.

For an open set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
,

[u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(E)}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in E\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

The case
\alpha=1
is the Lipschitz seminorm.

Definition of weak solution

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open,
I=(t_{1},t_{2})
,

1<p<\infty
,
s\in(0,1)
, and let
K

satisfy (
2
). A function

u\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I;\,W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I;\,L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap C_{\mathrm{loc}}(I;\,L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega))

is a
local weak solution
to (
1
) if, for
every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega\times I)
,

-\int_{I}\int_{\Omega}u\,\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}J_{p}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))\,(\varphi(x,t)-\varphi(y,t))\,K(x-y)\,dx\,dy\,dt=0.

(6)

Local weak supersolutions and subsolutions are defined by
replacing
=0
with
\geq 0
and
\leq 0
respectively,
and restricting to
\varphi\geq 0
.

3.  The Touching Lemma

After the scaling reduction of
Section
4
, we work in the domain

B_{2}\times(-2,0]
and seek Lipschitz regularity in

B_{1}\times(-1,0]
. We decompose the operator as

L_{K}u=L_{K}^{B_{1}}u+L_{K}^{B_{1}^{c}}u,\qquad L_{K}^{B_{1}}u(x,t):=\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{B_{1}(x)}J_{p}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))\,K(x-y)\,dy,

(7)

and set
f(x,t):=-L_{K}^{B_{1}^{c}}u(x,t)
. By the tail
bound
u\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I;\,L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))

and the normalisation (
11
), the
function
f
satisfies

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}\times(-1,0])}\leq C_{0},

(8)

where
C_{0}>0
depends only on
N,s,p,\lambda,\Lambda

and the normalisation bound. Hence
u
satisfies

\partial_{t}u+L_{K}^{B_{1}}u=f\quad\text{in }B_{1}\times(-1,0],

(9)

with
-C_{0}\leq f\leq C_{0}
. The main result of this
section is
Proposition
3
,
which proves the touching lemma for
equation (
9
). The key advantage of
working with
L_{K}^{B_{1}}
rather than
L_{K}
is that the
truncated operator integrates only over
B_{1}(x)
and thus no tail
condition on the test function
\phi
is required for the
continuity of the map
(x,t)\mapsto L_{K}^{B_{1}}\phi(x,t)
. The bounded
right-hand side
f
enters the contradiction argument
as a fixed additive constant
\pm C_{0}
and does not
affect the divergence argument for large
L
(cf.
Section

5.1
.)

The argument proceeds in four steps. First
(Lemma
3
), we show the principal value
integral of the truncated operator is well-defined and
bounded at points where the gradient does not vanish.
Second
(Lemma
3
), we
establish continuity of the truncated parabolic operator.
Third
(Lemmas
3
–
3
),
we show that a smooth strict supersolution of the
truncated equation remains a weak strict supersolution
under small perturbations. Finally, the touching
lemma
(Proposition
3
)
follows by a contradiction argument (cf.
[
7
, Proposition 1.5]
) using the weak
comparison principle
(Lemma
3
).
The results of this section are parabolic analogues of
[
21
]
adapted to our setup.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open,
p\in(1,\infty)
,

s\in(0,1)
. Let
\mathcal{K}\Subset\Omega
be compact
and
I\subset\mathbb{R}
a bounded interval. Suppose there exists
an open set
U
with
\mathcal{K}\Subset U\Subset\Omega

and a function
\phi\in C^{2}_{x}(U\times I)
with

\nabla_{x}\phi(x,t)\neq 0
for every

(x,t)\in\mathcal{K}\times I
. Then there exists a
constant
\mathbf{C}>0
such that for every

(x,t)\in\mathcal{K}\times I
and every

0<\delta<\varepsilon<\min\bigl\{\mathrm{dist}(\mathcal{K},\partial U),\,1\bigr\},

we have

\left|\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\setminus B_{\delta}(x)}J_{p}(\phi(x,t)-\phi(y,t))\,K(x-y)\,dy\right|\leq\mathbf{C}\,\varepsilon^{p(1-s)}.

The constant
\mathbf{C}
depends only on
N,p,s,\Lambda
,
\sup_{U\times I}|D_{x}^{2}\phi|
,

\inf_{\mathcal{K}\times I}|\nabla_{x}\phi|
, and

\sup_{\mathcal{K}\times I}|\nabla_{x}\phi|
; in particular
it is independent of
x\in\mathcal{K}
,
t\in I
,
\varepsilon>0
and

\delta
.

Proof.

Fix
x\in\mathcal{K}
,
t\in I
and

0<\delta<\varepsilon<\mathrm{dist}(\mathcal{K},\partial U)
.
Set
z=y-x
and consider the tangent hyperplane

\ell(y):=\phi(x,t)+\nabla_{x}\phi(x,t)\cdot(y-x).

Note that
\ell(x)-\ell(x+z)=-\nabla_{x}\phi(x,t)\cdot z
.
Since
K
is symmetric and
J_{p}
is odd, the integrand

z\mapsto J_{p}(-\nabla_{x}\phi(x,t)\cdot z)K(z)
is odd,
hence

\int_{B_{\varepsilon}\setminus B_{\delta}}J_{p}(\ell(x)-\ell(x+z))\,K(z)\,dz=0.

In particular,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\setminus B_{\delta}(x)}J_{p}(\phi(x,t)-\phi(y,t))\,K(x-y)\,dy

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{B_{\varepsilon}\setminus B_{\delta}}\bigl(J_{p}(\phi(x,t)-\phi(x+z,t))-J_{p}(\ell(x)-\ell(x+z))\bigr)\,K(z)\,dz.

Let
a=\phi(x,t)-\phi(x+z,t)
and

b=\ell(x)-\ell(x+z)=-\nabla_{x}\phi(x,t)\cdot z
. Then,

|a-b|\leq\tau|z|^{2}

where
\tau:=\sup_{U\times I}|D_{x}^{2}\phi|
. Since

\nabla_{x}\phi
is continuous on
\mathcal{K}\times I
,
there exist
0<m\leq M<\infty
with

m\leq|\nabla_{x}\phi(x,t)|\leq M
on

\mathcal{K}\times I
. Using

|J_{p}(a)-J_{p}(b)|\leq c_{p}(|a-b|+|b|)^{p-2}|a-b|
we get:

•

for
p\geq 2
:

|J_{p}(a)-J_{p}(b)|\leq C|z|^{p}
for
|z|\leq 1
.

•

for
1<p<2
:

|J_{p}(a)-J_{p}(b)|\leq C|\nabla_{x}\phi(x,t)\cdot z|^{p-2}|z|^{2}
.

In either case, integrating against

K(z)\leq\Lambda|z|^{-N-sp}
and switching to polar coordinates yields

\left|\int_{B_{\varepsilon}\setminus B_{\delta}}(J_{p}(a)-J_{p}(b))K(z)\,dz\right|\leq\mathbf{C}\,\varepsilon^{p(1-s)},

where for
1<p<2
the angular integral

\int_{S^{N-1}}|e\cdot\omega|^{p-2}\,d\omega
is finite
since
p>1
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open,
p\in(1,\infty)
,

s\in(0,1)
. Let
\mathcal{K}\Subset\Omega
be compact
and
I\subset\mathbb{R}
a bounded interval. Suppose there exists
an open set
U
with
\mathcal{K}\Subset U\Subset\Omega
and
\rho>0
is such that

B_{\rho}(x)\subset U
for all
x\in\mathcal{K}
.
If
\phi\in C^{2}_{x}(U\times I)\cap C^{1}_{t}(U\times I)

satisfies
\nabla_{x}\phi(x,t)\neq 0
for every

(x,t)\in\mathcal{K}\times I
, then the map

(x,t)\mapsto\partial_{t}\phi(x,t)+L_{K}^{B_{\rho}}\phi(x,t)

is continuous on
\mathcal{K}\times I
, where

L_{K}^{B_{\rho}}\phi(x,t):=\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}J_{p}(\phi(x,t)-\phi(y,t))\,K(x-y)\,dy.

Proof.

Let
(x,t),(y,\tau)\in\mathcal{K}\times I
with

(x,t)\to(y,\tau)
. Fix

\varepsilon\in(0,\min\{\rho/2,1\})
.
Decompose

L_{K}^{B_{\rho}}\phi=L_{K}^{B_{\varepsilon}}\phi+L_{K}^{B_{\rho}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}
.
By Lemma
3
,

|L_{K}^{B_{\varepsilon}}\phi(x,t)|+|L_{K}^{B_{\varepsilon}}\phi(y,\tau)|\leq 2\mathbf{C}\varepsilon^{p(1-s)}

uniformly. For the annular part, the domain

B_{\rho}(x)\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(x)
is bounded and
bounded away from the singularity, so the measure

K(x-z)\,dz
is finite there. The integral

\int_{B_{\rho}(x)\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(x)}J_{p}(\phi(x,t)-\phi(x^{\prime},t))\,K(x-x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}=\int_{B_{\rho}(0)\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(0)}J_{p}(\phi(x,t)-\phi(x+z,t))\,K(z)\,dz

converges
pointwise as
(x,t)\to(y,\tau)
to

\int_{B_{\rho}(0)\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(0)}J_{p}(\phi(y,\tau)-\phi(y+z,\tau))\,K(z)\,dz=\int_{B_{\rho}(y)\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(y)}J_{p}(\phi(y,\tau)-\phi(y^{\prime},\tau))\,K(y-y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}

by continuity of

\phi
and dominated convergence; the dominating function being

C\|\phi\|_{C^{1}(U\times I)}^{p-1}|z|^{p-1-N-sp}
,
which is integrable on
B_{\rho}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}
. Thus,

\limsup_{(x,t)\to(y,\tau)}|\partial_{t}\phi(x,t)+L_{K}^{B_{\rho}}\phi(x,t)-\partial_{t}\phi(y,\tau)-L_{K}^{B_{\rho}}\phi(y,\tau)|\leq 2C\varepsilon^{p(1-s)}.

We now let
\varepsilon\to 0+
to conclude.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The continuity of
L_{K}^{B_{\rho}}\phi
requires only local
regularity of
\phi
and the absence of critical points. In particular, no further conditions on the
behaviour of
\phi
outside
U
are needed, in contrast
to the full operator
L_{K}\phi
. This is the key
simplification provided the truncation
strategy (
7
) which lets us bypass any tail continuity condition and, together with translation invariance, any continuity assumption on the kernel.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open,
p\in(1,\infty)
,

s\in(0,1)
. Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
a bounded interval. Suppose there exists
open sets
U,V

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

Fix

\varphi\geq 0

\sigma>0

\delta_{\sigma}(x,t)\to 0

Using

\varphi\geq 0

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I}\int_{V}(\partial_{t}\phi+L_{K}^{B_{\rho}}\phi)\,\varphi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle L_{K,\sigma}^{B_{\rho}}\phi\,\varphi\,dx\,dt

5.  Proof of Main Theorem

We prove spatial Lipschitz regularity for the unknown
u
normalised as in Section
4
. Throughout the section,
p>1
,
sp>p-1
, and
u
is a continuous local weak solution to

\partial_{t}u+L_{K}u=0\quad\text{in }B_{2}\times(-2,0],

(10)

satisfying the normalisation bound

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2}\times(-2,0])}+\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(u;\,0,2,\,(-2,0])\leq 1.

(11)

Following the truncation strategy outlined in Section
3
, we decompose the operator as
L_{K}u=L_{K}^{B_{1}}u+L_{K}^{B_{1}^{c}}u
. Setting
f(x,t):=-L_{K}^{B_{1}^{c}}u(x,t)
, the normalisation bounds imply that
f\in L^{\infty}(B_{1}\times(-1,0])
with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{0}
for a universal constant
C_{0}>0
. Hence,
u
acts as a weak subsolution (and supersolution) to the truncated problem:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+L_{K}^{B_{1}}u

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\quad\text{in }B_{1}\times(-1,0],

(12)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+L_{K}^{B_{1}}u

\displaystyle\geq-C_{0}\quad\text{in }B_{1}\times(-1,0].

(13)

Under the above setup, the arguments now largely follow along the lines of
[
7
]
with the difference that
we use a parametrized family of Ishii-Lions functionals to ensure that
|a_{\nu}|\neq 0
(cf.
Figure

1
) and also since we have truncated the operator, some of the computations simplify and in particular, the final tail integral in
[
7
]
has already been taken care of in the form of
C_{0}
above.

Penalisation profiles

We work with two penalisation profiles. For the Hölder bootstrap we use:

\varphi_{\gamma}(r):=r^{\gamma},\quad\gamma\in(0,1).

(14)

For the Lipschitz step we work with the almost-linear profile:

\tilde{\varphi}(r):=r+\frac{r}{\log r},\quad r\in(0,r_{\circ}],

(15)

for
r_{\circ}\in(0,e^{-2})
small enough, with
\tilde{\varphi}(0):=0
. On
(0,r_{\circ}]
, this profile satisfies

\tfrac{r}{2}\leq\tilde{\varphi}(r)\leq r,\quad\tfrac{1}{2}\leq\tilde{\varphi}^{\prime}(r)\leq 1,\quad-\tfrac{2}{r\log^{2}r}\leq\tilde{\varphi}^{\prime\prime}(r)\leq-\tfrac{1}{2r\log^{2}r}<0.

(16)

The strict concavity
\tilde{\varphi}^{\prime\prime}<0
ensures the cone estimates for the integral bounds (cf.
Figure

1
) still holds, which would fail for the purely linear profile
\varphi(r)=r
.

5.1. The Ishii–Lions framework

Fix radii

, and set
\tilde{\varrho}:=\frac{1}{4}(\varrho_{2}-\varrho_{1})<1

and choose

such that

for all

(17)

If

We define the time-doubled functional on

(18)

Let

be a global maximiser of

. Since

, standard arguments yield that as

5.3. Integral estimates

Lemma 5.2
.

We have the following bounds:

•

Hölder profile:

I_{1}+I_{2}\geq\tfrac{C_{\varepsilon}}{2}L^{p-1}|a_{\nu}|^{\gamma(p-1)-ps}
.

•

Lipschitz profile:

I_{1}+I_{2}\geq\tfrac{C_{\varepsilon}}{2}L^{p-1}|a_{\nu}|^{p-1-ps}(\log^{2}|a_{\nu}|)^{-\beta}
, where
\beta:=\tfrac{N+1}{2}+p-sp
.

Proof.

We note that for
z\in B_{\delta_{\nu}}
, the point
x_{\nu}+z
lies in
B_{\delta_{\nu}}(x_{\nu})
. We compute the spatial increment directly:

\displaystyle\Theta_{1}w_{1}(x_{\nu},t_{\nu},z)

\displaystyle=w_{1}(x_{\nu},t_{\nu})-w_{1}(x_{\nu}+z,t_{\nu})

\displaystyle=\Bigl[L\varphi(|a_{\nu}|)+m_{1}\psi(x_{\nu})+u(y_{\nu},\tau_{\nu})+\tfrac{b_{\nu}^{2}}{2\nu}+M_{\nu}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad-\Bigl[L\varphi(|x_{\nu}+z-y_{\nu}|)+m_{1}\psi(x_{\nu}+z)+u(y_{\nu},\tau_{\nu})+\tfrac{b_{\nu}^{2}}{2\nu}+M_{\nu}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=L[\varphi(|a_{\nu}|)-\varphi(|a_{\nu}+z|)]+m_{1}[\psi(x_{\nu})-\psi(x_{\nu}+z)],

to see that the time-dependent terms

(t_{\nu}-\tau_{\nu})^{2}/(2\nu)
, and
M_{\nu}
cancel exactly. Hence
\Theta_{1}w_{1}(x_{\nu},t_{\nu},z)

[

]
. An identical computation shows the same for
\Theta_{1}w_{2}(y_{\nu},\tau_{\nu},z)
on

[

, Lemmas 3.1, 3.2, 4.1, 4.2]
, and for the Lipschitz profile from
[
7

.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
p\geq 2
and suppose

•

Hölder profile:

I_{3}\geq-C_{\kappa,\varepsilon_{1}}|a_{\nu}|^{\gamma(p-1)-sp}

Lipschitz profile:

6.  Extensions and Further Remarks

The Ishii–Lions method adapted to the nonlocal, nonlinear context, as observed in
[
7
]
, is widely applicable as we survey below in brief.

Higher Hölder regularity for
discontinuous kernels

Using
\varphi_{\gamma}
with
\gamma<\min\{1,sp/(p-1)\}

and running the bootstrap of
Proposition
5.4
from

\kappa=0
, any local weak solution
to (
1
) belongs to
C^{0,\gamma}_{x}

locally, uniformly in time. When
sp/(p-1)\neq 1
the
endpoint
\gamma=\min\{1,sp/(p-1)\}
is also attained,
with both cases handled by the Lipschitz-profile argument
of Section
5.1
. These are
parabolic analogues of
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]

and extend those
of
[
9
,
16
]
to all kernels
satisfying (
2
).

Equations with
L^{\infty}
source

For
\partial_{t}u+L_{K}u=f
with

f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times I)
, the master
inequality (
19
) acquires a term

2\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}
on the right. Since this is bounded
while the left side diverges as
L\to\infty
, the same
Lipschitz conclusion holds;
cf.
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
and

[
29
]
.

Lower-order terms

For
\partial_{t}u+L_{K}u=b(x,t,u,\nabla u)
with

|b|\leq C_{b}(1+|\xi|^{\alpha})
,
\alpha\in[0,1)
, the
gradient of the test function at the touching point

x_{\nu}
satisfies

|\nabla w_{1}(x_{\nu},t_{\nu})|\leq CL\varphi^{\prime}(|a_{\nu}|)
,
so the lower-order term contributes at most

C_{b}(1+(L\varphi^{\prime}(|a_{\nu}|))^{\alpha})
to the master
inequality. Since
\varphi^{\prime}(|a_{\nu}|)
grows strictly
slower than
L^{(p-1-\alpha)/(1-\alpha)}
for
\alpha<1
,
this is of lower order than

L^{p-1}|a_{\nu}|^{p-1-sp}
and the Lipschitz conclusion
extends. The precise admissible range of
\alpha
is left
to future work.

Fractional
(p,q)
-Laplacian

For
\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s_{1}}u+(-\Delta_{q})^{s_{2}}u=0
, the Ishii–Lions argument
applies to each operator separately. The Lipschitz
threshold is
\min\{s_{1}p/(p-1),\,s_{2}q/(q-1)\}>1
,
that is, both operators must individually satisfy the
Lipschitz condition; cf.
[
7
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Nonlocal double-phase operator

For
\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s_{1}}u+\xi(x)(-\Delta_{q})^{s_{2}}u=0
with

\xi\in C^{0,\alpha}
,
s_{2}q\leq s_{1}p+\alpha
, the
additional term
(\xi(x_{\nu})-\xi(y_{\nu}))L_{q}[D_{2}]w_{2}
is controlled by
|a_{\nu}|^{\alpha}
and is of
lower order; cf.
[
7
, Theorem 5.2]
.
