On Variational Approximations For Wave Maps
Zhiyuan Geng, Changyou Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we revisit the existence of global weak solutions of wave maps from ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} into the sphere 𝕊 L − 1 \mathbb{S}^{L-1} , □ ​ u ⟂ T u ​ 𝕊 L − 1 \Box u\perp T_{u}\mathbb{S}^{L-1} , by establishing it as a singular limit of maps from ℝ n × ℝ + \mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+} to 𝕊 L − 1 \mathbb{S}^{L-1} that minimize elliptic regularized variational functionals that contain an exponential weight in the time direction with a small parameter ε \varepsilon , where the initial data of the Cauchy problem serve as the boundary condition. The idea went back to De Giorgi [ 1 ] , which has been implemented by Serra and Tilli [ 6 , 7 ] for certain class of nonlinear wave equations. This approach is also applicable to the S ​ O ​ ( m ) SO(m) -target manifold.

1.
Introduction

Recall that for
n,k\geq 1
and a given a smooth,
k
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold
(N,h)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{L}

(isometrically embedded into
the euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{L}
for a sufficiently lareg
L
),
a map
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\to N\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{L}
is called a wave map, if it solves the wave map equation:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Box u:=\partial_{tt}u-\Delta u\perp T_{u}N\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty),

(1.2)

\displaystyle(u,\partial_{t}u)\big|_{t=0}=(\phi,\psi)\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
and
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\phi^{*}TN
, the induced tangent bundle
TN
by
\phi
, are given initial data. Observe that a wave map
u
can be viewed as a harmonic map from
(M,g_{0})=(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty),g_{0})
to
N
,
with the Minkowski metric given by
g_{0}=dx^{2}-dt^{2}
, that is,

u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\to N
is a critical point of the functional

H(u)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)}(|\partial_{t}u|^{2}-|\nabla u|^{2})\,dxdt.

However, since the functional
H
is neither convex nor coercive, it is impossible to find a wave map through the method of calculus of variations on
H
. When
n\geq 2
and
N=\mathbb{S}^{L-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{L}
is the unit sphere, Shatah
[
8
]
was the first to establish the existence of global weak solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) for
(\phi,\psi)\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{S}^{L-1})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\phi^{*}T\mathbb{S}^{L-1})
through
approximation by semi-linear wave equations, namely, the Ginzburg-Landau approximation. Later, Freire
[
2
]
and Zhou
[
10
]

extended the global existence of weak solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) to Riemannian homogeneous spaces by Ginzburg-Landau approximations and viscous approximations of wave maps respectively. For general target manifolds
N
, in a very important work
[
3
]
(cf. also
[
4
]
) Freire-Müller-Struwe successfully
established, in dimension
n=2
, the compensated-compactness property for weakly convergent sequences of wave maps by adapting
Hélein’s method of moving frames on
N
and the duality
between Hardy and BMO spaces for harmonic maps. Utilizing

[
3
]
and Zhou’s method of viscous approximations, Müller-Struwe
[
5
]
obtained the global existence
of weak wave maps into general manifolds when
n=2
.
It has remained an open problem that whether there exists
global weak weak solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
)
for general smooth target manifolds
N
, when
n\geq 3
. For interested readers, we refer to the book
[
9
]
by Shatah and Struwe that contains many interesting development on geometric wave equations.

In this article, we revisit the wave map equation (
1.1
) by investigating the elliptic regularization approach proposed by De Giorgi
[
1
]
and Serra-Tilli
[
6
,
7
]
.

Now, we describe the approach. For
\varepsilon>0
, define a family of
energy functionals

(1.3)

\displaystyle{E}_{\varepsilon}(v)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{e^{-t/\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\Big\{\varepsilon|\partial_{t}^{2}v|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}|\nabla v|^{2}\Big\}\,dxdt,

for maps
v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\to N
subject to the boundary condition:

(1.4)

\displaystyle v(x,0)=\phi(x),\ \ v_{t}(x,0)=\psi(x),\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N

and
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{L}
,
with
\psi(x)\in T_{\phi(x)}N
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, are given data.

By calculating the first order variation, any minimizer
v_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\to N
for
{E}_{\varepsilon}
, provided it exists in suitable configuration function spaces,
solves the following Euler-Lagrange equation

(1.5)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}^{4}v_{\varepsilon}-2\varepsilon\partial_{t}^{3}v_{\varepsilon}+\partial_{t}^{2}v_{\epsilon}-\Delta v_{\varepsilon}\perp T_{v_{\varepsilon}}N.

Heuristically, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, a subsequence of solutions
v_{\varepsilon}
of (
1.5
) would converge to a map
v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\to N
, that satisfies the wave map equation (
1.1
). On the other hand, how to justify the validity of such a heuristics turns out to be a very challenging problem, thanks to the supercritical nonlinearities appearing within the corresponding analytic form of the equation (
1.5
).

Neverthless, when
N=\mathbb{S}^{L-1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{L}
or
the special orthogonal group
SO(m)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{L}

(with
L={m^{2}}
),
we are able to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
N=\mathbb{S}^{L-1}
or

SO(m)
. Let
\phi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},N)

and
\psi\in(L^{\infty}\cap{H}^{1})(\mathbb{R}^{n},\phi^{*}TN)
. For
\varepsilon>0
, let

v_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\to N

be a minimizer of the energy

{E}_{\varepsilon}
over the admissible space
\mathcal{H}
together with boundary data
(\phi,\psi)
. Then there exists a subsequence
\varepsilon_{i}\to 0
such that
v_{i}=v_{\varepsilon_{i}}
weakly converges to
a map
v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to N
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
so that

(\partial_{t}v,\nabla v)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).

And
v
is a weak solution of the wave map equation:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}v-\Delta v\perp T_{v}N\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty),

along with the initial condition

(1.7)

\displaystyle v(x,0)=\phi(x),\ \partial_{t}v(x,0)=\psi(x),\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Furthermore,
v
satisfies the following energy inequality:

(1.8)

\displaystyle E(v(t))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(|\partial_{t}v|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2})(x,t)\,dx\leq E(v(0))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(|\psi|^{2}+|\nabla\phi|^{2})(x)\,dx.

We would like to point out that, due to the variational approach
of specific forms, the condition on
\psi
is stronger than the standard assumption
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for wave maps.
We will present the proof in detail for
N=\mathbb{S}^{L-1}
in Section 2, and point out the necessary changes needed for
the proof of
N=SO(m)
in Section 3.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
for
N=\mathbb{S}^{L-1}

In this section, we will establish uniform energy estimates
of
v_{\varepsilon}
and then provide a proof of Theorem
1.1
for

N=\mathbb{S}^{L-1}.

First, by defining
u(x,t)=v(x,\varepsilon t)
, one can check that

E_{\varepsilon}(v)=\varepsilon^{-3}I_{\varepsilon}(u)
, where

I_{\varepsilon}
is defined by

(2.1)

I_{\varepsilon}(u)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}\Big\{|\partial_{t}^{2}u|^{2}+{\varepsilon^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\Big\}\,dxdt,

and the boundary condition for
u
becomes

(2.2)

u(x,0)=\phi(x),\ \ \partial_{t}u(x,0)=\varepsilon\psi(x),\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

2.1.
Existence of minimizer for
I_{\varepsilon}

Define the function space

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\Big\{u:\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{S}^{L-1}:(\partial_{t}^{2}u,\nabla u)\in L^{2}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})\ \ {\rm{and}}

\displaystyle\qquad\big[u\big]_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}\big(|\partial_{t}^{2}u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2})(x,t)\,dxdt<\infty\Big\}.

Since
|u|=1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
for any

u\in\mathcal{H}
, it is not difficult to verify that for any

0<T<\infty
and compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow H^{2}([0,T],L^{2}(K)),

so that

(u,\partial_{t}u)\in C([0,T],L^{2}(K)),\ \forall u\in\mathcal{H}.

Hence for each
u\in\mathcal{H}
, the traces
u(\cdot,0)

and
\partial_{t}u(\cdot,0)
are well defined as elements of
L^{2}(K)
, see also
[
6
, Remark 2.1]
.

Given
\phi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{S}^{L-1})

and
\psi\in({H}^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{\phi}^{*}T\mathbb{S}^{L-1})
, we define

\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\varepsilon\psi}=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{H}:\ u(x,0)=\phi(x),\ \partial_{t}u(x,0)=\varepsilon\psi(x)\ {\rm{for}}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\Big\}.

It follows from Lemma
2.2
that

\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\varepsilon\psi}\not=\emptyset
.
Now we can show the
existence of minimizer of
I_{\varepsilon}
over
\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\varepsilon\psi}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
\phi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{S}^{L-1})

and
\psi\in({H}^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{\phi}^{*}T\mathbb{S}^{L-1})
. Then there exists a map
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\varepsilon\psi}
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq I_{\varepsilon}(v),\ \forall v\in\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\varepsilon\psi}.

Proof.

First, it follows from Lemma
2.2
below that

\alpha:=\inf\Big\{I_{\varepsilon}(v):\ v\in\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\varepsilon\psi}\Big\}\leq\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+C\varepsilon^{3}<\infty,

where
C>0
depends on

\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
,

\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
,
and
\|\psi\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.

Now let
\{u_{i}\}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\varepsilon\psi}
be a minimizing sequence of
\alpha
, i.e.,

I_{\varepsilon}(u_{i})\rightarrow\alpha.

By the weak compactness of
\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\varepsilon\psi}
, there exists a map
u_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{L}
,
with
\big[u\big]_{\mathcal{H}}<\infty
,
such that, after passing to a subsequence if necessary,

(\partial_{t}^{2}u_{i},\nabla u_{i})\rightharpoonup(\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon},\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

and

(u_{i},\partial_{t}u_{i})\rightarrow(u_{\varepsilon},\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon})\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ L^{2}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})\ \ {\rm{and\ a.e.\ in}}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.

In particular,
|u_{\varepsilon}|=1
a.e. in

\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and
(u_{\varepsilon},\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon})\big|_{t=0}=(\phi,\varepsilon\psi)
, that is,
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\varepsilon\psi}
.
Hence

I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\alpha.

On the other hand, by the lower semi continuity of
I_{\varepsilon}

on
\mathcal{H}
, we have

I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq\liminf_{i}I_{\varepsilon}(u_{i})=\alpha.

Thus
I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\alpha
. This
completes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Uniform Energy Estimates

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume
\phi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{S}^{L-1})

and
\psi\in({H}^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{\phi}^{*}T\mathbb{S}^{L-1})
.
Then

(2.4)

\displaystyle\alpha:=\inf\Big\{I_{\varepsilon}(u):\ u\in\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\varepsilon\psi}\Big\}\leq\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+C\varepsilon^{3}.

Proof.

Since

\phi(x)+t\varepsilon\psi(x)
satisfies

|\phi(x)+t\varepsilon\psi(x)|^{2}=|\phi(x)|^{2}+t^{2}\varepsilon^{2}|\psi(x)|^{2}\geq 1,

we can define the
\mathbb{S}^{L-1}
-valued map by

w_{\varepsilon}(x)=\displaystyle\frac{\phi(x)+t\varepsilon\psi(x)}{|\phi(x)+t\varepsilon\psi(x)|},\ (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.

By the definition of
\alpha
, we have

\alpha\leq I_{\varepsilon}(w_{\varepsilon}).

To estimate
I_{\varepsilon}(w_{\varepsilon})
,
we first calculate

\displaystyle\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\nabla\phi+t\varepsilon\nabla\psi)|\phi+t\varepsilon\psi|^{2}-(\phi+t\varepsilon\psi)\langle\phi+t\varepsilon\psi,\nabla\phi+t\varepsilon\nabla\psi\rangle}{|\phi+t\varepsilon\psi|^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\phi+t\varepsilon\psi|}\Big((\nabla\phi+t\varepsilon\nabla\psi)-\langle\nabla\phi+t\varepsilon\nabla\psi,\frac{\phi+t\varepsilon\psi}{|\phi+t\varepsilon\psi|}\rangle\frac{\phi+t\varepsilon\psi}{|\phi+t\varepsilon\psi|}\Big)

so that

|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|(x,t)\leq|\nabla\phi+t\varepsilon\nabla\psi|\leq|\nabla\phi|+t\varepsilon|\nabla\psi|.

Also we calculate

\partial_{t}w_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)=\frac{\varepsilon\psi}{|\phi+t\varepsilon\psi|}-\frac{\varepsilon(\phi+t\varepsilon\psi)\langle\phi+t\varepsilon\psi,\psi\rangle}{|\phi+t\varepsilon\psi|^{3}}

so that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}w_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\psi\langle\phi+t\varepsilon\psi,\psi\rangle}{|\phi+t\varepsilon\psi|^{3}}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}|\psi|^{2}(\phi+t\varepsilon\psi)}{|\phi+t\varepsilon\psi|^{3}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +3\frac{\varepsilon^{2}(\phi+t\varepsilon\psi)\langle\phi+t\varepsilon\psi,\psi\rangle^{2}}{|\phi+t\varepsilon\psi|^{5}}.

Hence

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{2}w_{\varepsilon}|(\cdot,t)

\displaystyle\leq 6\varepsilon^{2}|\psi|^{2}

This implies that

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}(w_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}(|\partial_{t}^{2}w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2})\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+C\varepsilon^{3}|\nabla\phi||\nabla\psi|+C\varepsilon^{4}(|\nabla\psi|^{2}+|\psi|^{4}))\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx+O(\varepsilon^{3}).

Here we have used the fact that

\|\psi\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This completes the proof. ∎

To proceed, we introduce a few notations now.
For any minimizer
u_{\varepsilon}
of
I_{\varepsilon}
over
\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\varepsilon\psi}
, define

L_{\epsilon}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(|\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})(x,t)\,dx,

H_{\varepsilon}(t)=\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-s}L_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds,

I_{\epsilon}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}(x,t)\,dx,

D_{\varepsilon}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}(x,t)\,dx,

and

E_{\varepsilon}(t)=I_{\varepsilon}(t)-I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)+\frac{1}{2}e^{t}H_{\varepsilon}(t).

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume
\phi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{S}^{L-1})

and
\psi\in({H}^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{\phi}^{*}T\mathbb{S}^{L-1})
.
Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a minimizer of
I_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\varepsilon\psi}
. Then

E_{\varepsilon}(t)
satisfies

(2.5)

E_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)=-2D_{\varepsilon}(t).

In particular,
E_{\epsilon}\in W^{1,1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, and is monotonically non-increasing with respect to
t
.

Proof.

The idea of proof is based on the inner variation of
u_{\varepsilon}
along the
t
-direction.
For the detail, we follow closely the argument by
[
6
, Proposition 3.1]
.
For any
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, define

g(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\eta(s)\,ds.

Then
g\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
vanishes near
t=0
.

Write
u
for
u_{\varepsilon}
. For a sufficiently small
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, define

\alpha_{\delta}(t)=t-\delta g(t)\ \ {\rm{and}}\ \ U_{\delta}(x,t)=u(x,\alpha_{\delta}(t)),\ (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Then
U_{\delta}
satisfies the boundary condition (2.2), and
u\equiv U_{0}
so that by the minimality, we have

I_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq I_{\varepsilon}(U_{\delta}),

and hence

\frac{d}{d\delta}\big|_{\delta=0}I_{\varepsilon}(U_{\delta})=0.

Since
\alpha_{\delta}
is invertible when

\delta
is sufficiently small, we denote
\beta_{\delta}
as the inverse so that
\beta_{\delta}\circ\alpha_{\delta}(t)=t
.
By change of variables, we can rewrite

I_{\varepsilon}(U_{\delta})
as

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}(U_{\delta})

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}(e^{-\beta_{\delta}(s)}\beta_{\delta}^{\prime}(s))\big(|\partial_{t}^{2}u(\alpha_{\delta}^{\prime})^{2}+\partial_{t}u\alpha_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}(x,s)\big)\,dxds.

Direct calculations yield

\frac{d}{d\delta}\big|_{\delta=0}\big(e^{-\beta_{\delta}}\beta_{\delta}^{\prime}\big)=e^{-t}(g^{\prime}(t)-g(t)),

and

\frac{d}{d\delta}\big|_{\delta=0}\alpha^{\prime}_{\delta}(t)=g^{\prime}(t),\ \ \frac{d}{d\delta}\big|_{\delta=0}\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{\delta}(t)=g^{\prime\prime}(t).

Using these identities, we can deduce

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d\delta}\big|_{\delta=0}I_{\varepsilon}(U_{\delta})

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}(g^{\prime}-g)(|\partial_{t}^{2}u|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})(t,x)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\ \ -\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}(4|\partial_{t}^{2}u|^{2}g^{\prime}+2\langle\partial_{t}^{2}u,\partial_{t}u\rangle g^{\prime\prime})(t,x)\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}(g^{\prime}(t)-g(t))L_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}(4D_{\varepsilon}(t)g^{\prime}(t)+2I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)g^{\prime\prime}(t))\,dt.

Observe that

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}g(t)L_{\epsilon}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t)H_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=g(t)H_{\varepsilon}(t)\big|_{0}^{\infty}-\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{\varepsilon}(t)g^{\prime}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{\varepsilon}(t)g^{\prime}(t)\,dt,

since
g(0)=H_{\varepsilon}(\infty)=0
.
Observe also that

g^{\prime}(t)=\eta(t)\ \ {\rm{and}}\ \ g^{\prime\prime}(t)=\eta^{\prime}(t).

Therefore, we obtain that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(L_{\varepsilon}(t)-e^{t}H_{\varepsilon}(t)-4D_{\varepsilon}(t)\big)e^{-t}\eta(t)\,dt-2\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)e^{-t}\eta^{\prime}(t))\,dt=0.

By integration by parts, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)e^{-t}\eta^{\prime}(t)\,dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)e^{-t}\eta(t)\,dt-\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t)e^{-t}\eta(t)\,dt.

Thus we arrive at

\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\big(L_{\varepsilon}(t)-e^{t}H_{\varepsilon}(t)-4D_{\varepsilon}(t)-2I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)\big)+2I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t)\Big]e^{-t}\eta(t)\,dt=0.

This, combined with

(e^{t}H_{\epsilon}(t))^{\prime}=e^{t}H_{\varepsilon}(t)-L_{\varepsilon}(t)
,
yields

(2.7)

E_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)=\Big(\frac{1}{2}e^{t}H_{\epsilon}(t)+I_{\varepsilon}(t)-I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)\Big)^{\prime}=-2D_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq 0.

In particular,
E_{\varepsilon}(t)
is monotonically decreasing with respect to
t
. This completes the proof.
∎

Since

-(e^{-t}I_{\varepsilon}(t))^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}H_{\varepsilon}(t)=e^{-t}E_{\varepsilon}(t),

it follows from (
2.7
) that we can integrate from
t
to
\infty
to get

e^{-t}I_{\varepsilon}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{\infty}H_{\varepsilon}(\tau)\,d\tau=\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\tau}E_{\varepsilon}(\tau)\,d\tau\leq E_{\varepsilon}(t)\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\tau}\,d\tau=e^{-t}E_{\varepsilon}(t),

this yields

(2.8)

I_{\varepsilon}(t)+\frac{e^{t}}{2}\int_{t}^{\infty}H_{\varepsilon}(\tau)\,d\tau\leq E_{\varepsilon}(t).

From the global energy inequality (
2.4
), we have that

H_{\varepsilon}(0)=I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq C\varepsilon^{2}

Since
H_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq H_{\varepsilon}(0)
for all

t\geq 0
, we then have

(2.9)

H_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq C\varepsilon^{2},\ \forall t\geq 0.

Since

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}D_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt\leq I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq C\varepsilon^{2},

we see that

(2.10)

\int_{0}^{1}D_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt\leq e\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}D_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

This, combined with
\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}(x,0)=\varepsilon\psi(x)
, implies

(2.11)

\int_{0}^{1}I_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq C\big(\int_{0}^{1}D_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}|\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}(x,0)|^{2}\,dx\big)\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

Hence, by Hölder inequality, we have

(2.12)

\int_{0}^{1}|I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)|\,dt\leq\big(\int_{0}^{1}D_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\int_{0}^{1}I_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

Putting (
2.9
),
(
2.11
), and (
2.12
) together, one has

(2.13)

\int_{0}^{1}E_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}(I_{\varepsilon}(t)-I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)+\frac{1}{2}e^{t}H_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

Observe that from (
2.5
), we have
that

(2.14)

E_{\varepsilon}(0)=E_{\varepsilon}(t)+2\int_{0}^{t}D_{\varepsilon}(\tau)\,d\tau,

so that by integrating
t\in[0,1]
, we obtain that

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}E_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt+2\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{t}D_{\varepsilon}(\tau)\,d\tau dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}E_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt+2\int_{0}^{1}D_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2},

where we have applied (
2.13
) and (

) in the last step. In particular, we also have that

(2.15)

\displaystyle 0\leq E_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq E_{\varepsilon}(0)\leq C\varepsilon^{2},

and

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}|\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)|^{2}\,dxdt=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}E_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(E_{\varepsilon}(0)-E_{\varepsilon}(\infty))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}E_{\varepsilon}(0)\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

Observe that by the monotonic decreasing property of

(

\displaystyle e^{t}H_{\varepsilon}(t+1)\leq e^{t}\int_{t}^{t+1}H_{\varepsilon}(\tau)\,d\tau\leq e^{t}\int_{t}^{\infty}H_{\varepsilon}(\tau)\,d\tau\leq 2E_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq C\varepsilon^{2},\ \forall t>0.

This implies

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x,\tau)|^{2}\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}e^{t+1}\int_{t}^{t+1}e^{-s}L_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds

(2.17)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-2}e^{t+1}H_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq C,\ \forall t\geq 0.

2.3.
Dual estimates of
(\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\times u_{\varepsilon})(\cdot,t)

In this subsection, we prove the following.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume
\phi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{S}^{L-1})

and
\psi\in({H}^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{\phi}^{*}T\mathbb{S}^{L-1})

Let

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\times u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\cdot h(x)\,dx\Big|\leq Ce^{t}\varepsilon^{2}\Big(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big).

Proof.

Since

is a minimizer of

over

\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\psi}^{\varepsilon}

(2.19)

\displaystyle\Big[\partial_{t}^{2}(e^{-t}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon})-\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon^{2}e^{-t}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\Big]\times u_{\varepsilon}=0\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,+\infty).

Assume

. As in
[
6
, Theorem 4.2]
, let
\eta\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
be given by

\eta(t)=\begin{cases}0&t\leq 0,\\
t^{2}&0<t<1,\\
2t-1&t\geq 1.\end{cases}

For

and
\delta>0

\eta_{\delta}(t)=\delta\eta(\frac{t-T}{\delta}).

Testing (
2.19
) by
\eta_{\delta}(t)h(x)
, we have that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Big[e^{-t}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\times\partial_{t}^{2}(u_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}h)+\varepsilon^{2}e^{-t}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\times\nabla(u_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}h)\Big]\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}[\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\times(\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}+u_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}+2\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}^{\prime})h+\varepsilon^{2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\times(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}h+u_{\varepsilon}\nabla h)\eta_{\delta}]\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}[\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\times(u_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}+2\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}^{\prime})h+\varepsilon^{2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\times(u_{\varepsilon}\nabla h)\eta_{\delta}]\,dxdt.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\times u_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}h\,dxdt

\displaystyle+2\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\times\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}^{\prime}h\,dxdt

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\times u_{\varepsilon}\nabla h\eta_{\delta}\,dxdt=0.

Since
\eta_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(t)=2\delta^{-1}\chi_{(T,T+\delta)}(t)
, the first term of (
2.3

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\times u_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}h\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\delta}\int_{T}^{T+\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-t}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\times u_{\varepsilon}h\,dxdt

\displaystyle\rightarrow 2e^{-T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\times u_{\varepsilon}h\,dx,\ {\rm{as}}\ \ \delta\to 0.

The second term of (
2.3
) can be estimated as follows.

\displaystyle 2\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\times\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}^{\prime}h\,dxdt

\displaystyle=4\int_{T}^{T+\delta}\frac{t-T}{\delta}e^{-t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\times\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}h\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+4\int_{T+\delta}^{\infty}e^{-t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\times\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}h\,dxdt,

hence we have

\displaystyle\Big|2\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\times\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}^{\prime}h\,dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle\leq 4\int_{T}^{\infty}e^{-t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\times\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}||h|\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}|\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}|\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}|\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\Big(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}|\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dxdt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}(x,0)|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The third term of (
2.3
) can be
estimated by

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\Big|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\times u_{\varepsilon}\nabla h\eta_{\delta}\,dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle=2\Big|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}(t-T)^{+}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\times u_{\varepsilon}\nabla h\,dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle\leq 2\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-t}(t-T)^{+}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}||\nabla h|\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-k}(k+1)\Big(\int_{T+k}^{T+k+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{T+k}^{T+k+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla h|^{2}\,dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\sup_{k\geq 0}\Big(\int_{T+k}^{T+k+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Substituting these estimates into (
2.3

2.18
)
holds for a.e.
T>0
.
∎

2.4.
Passage to the limit

In this subsection, we illustrate how to pass to the limit
v
of
v_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=u_{\varepsilon}(x,t/\varepsilon)
so that

v
is a weak solution of the wave map equation which satisfies the weak-type
global energy inequality.

First, let us collect all the estimates for
v_{\varepsilon}
after translating those for
u_{\varepsilon}
. They are as follows.

(2.21)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}(x,t)\,dxdt\leq CT.

(2.22)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}(x,t)\,dx\leq C,\ {\rm{a.e.}}\ t>0,

(2.23)

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}|\partial_{t}^{2}v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}(x,t)\,dxdt\leq C\varepsilon^{-1},

and

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}^{2}v_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\times v_{\varepsilon}(x,t)h(x)\,dx\Big|

(2.24)

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\big(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\big),\ {\rm{a.e.}}\ 0<t<T.

After passing to a subsequence, we may assume that there exists a map
v\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{S}^{L-1})
such that

(\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon},\nabla v_{\varepsilon})\rightharpoonup(\partial_{t}v,\nabla v)\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}).

Since
v_{\varepsilon}
is a weak solution of

\Big[\partial_{t}^{2}\big(e^{-t/\varepsilon}\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}v_{\varepsilon})-\nabla\cdot(e^{-t/\varepsilon}\nabla v_{\varepsilon})\Big]\times v_{\varepsilon}=0\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},

we have, for any test function
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
we have

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\big(e^{-t/\varepsilon}\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}v_{\varepsilon}\big)\times\partial_{t}^{2}(v_{\varepsilon}\eta)+(e^{-t/\varepsilon}\nabla v_{\varepsilon})\times\nabla(v_{\varepsilon}\eta)\,dxdt=0.

After expansion and cancellations of certain terms, this identity reduces to

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\big(e^{-t/\varepsilon}\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}v_{\varepsilon}\big)\times(2\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}\eta^{\prime}+v_{\varepsilon}\eta^{\prime\prime})+(e^{-t/\varepsilon}\nabla v_{\varepsilon})\times(v_{\varepsilon}\nabla\eta)\,dxdt=0.

Let
\hat{\eta}=e^{-t/\varepsilon}\eta
. Then we have

2\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}\eta^{\prime}+\partial_{t}^{2}v_{\varepsilon}\eta=e^{t/\varepsilon}\Big[2\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}\big(\hat{\eta}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\hat{\eta}\big)+v_{\varepsilon}\big(\hat{\eta}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\hat{\eta}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\hat{\eta}\big)\Big],\ \ \nabla\eta=e^{t/\varepsilon}\nabla\hat{\eta}.

Substituting this into the equation above, we obtain that

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\big[-\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}\times\partial_{t}\big(v_{\varepsilon}\hat{\eta}\big)+\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\times(v_{\varepsilon}\nabla\hat{\eta})\big]\,dxdt

\displaystyle=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\partial_{t}^{2}v_{\varepsilon}\times\Big[2\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}\big(\varepsilon^{2}\hat{\eta}^{\prime}+\varepsilon\hat{\eta}\big)+v_{\varepsilon}\big(\varepsilon^{2}\hat{\eta}^{\prime\prime}+2\varepsilon\hat{\eta}^{\prime}\big)\Big]\,dxdt.

From (
2.22
) and
(
2.23
), it is easy to see that

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\partial_{t}^{2}v_{\varepsilon}\times\Big[2\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}\big(\varepsilon^{2}\hat{\eta}^{\prime}+\varepsilon\hat{\eta}\big)+v_{\varepsilon}\big(\varepsilon^{2}\hat{\eta}^{\prime\prime}+2\varepsilon\hat{\eta}^{\prime}\big)\Big]\,dxdt\to 0

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Thus, after sending
\varepsilon\to 0
in the equation above, we arrive at

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\big[-\partial_{t}v\times\partial_{t}\big(v\hat{\eta}\big)+\nabla v\times(v\nabla\hat{\eta})\big]\,dxdt=0,

this is,

v
is a weak solution of

\partial_{t}^{2}v\times v-\nabla\cdot(\nabla v\times v)=0\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.

This is equivalent to that
v
is a weak solution of the wave map equation:

v_{tt}-\Delta v\perp T_{v}\mathbb{S}^{L-1}\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Next, we want to verify that
v
satisfies the initial condition

v(x,0)=\phi(x),\ \ \partial_{t}v(x,0)=\psi(x)\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}.

The first condition follows from the compactness of traces. For any
T>0
and
R>0
,

H^{1}(B_{R}\times[0,T])\hookrightarrow L^{2}(B_{R}\times\{0\}),

and
v_{\varepsilon}(x,0)=\phi(x)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The second condition follows from (
2.4
) and the following argument, similar to
[
6
, page 1571]
. For
\eta(t)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}(\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}\times v_{\varepsilon})h\,dxdt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}\times v_{\varepsilon}h\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad-\eta(0)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi(x)\times\phi(x)h(x)\,dx.

Passing to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
in this identity and applying (
2.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\partial_{t}(\partial_{t}v\times v)h\,dxdt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}v\times vh\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad-\eta(0)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi(x)\times\phi(x)h(x)\,dx.

On the other hand, by integration by parts we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}(\partial_{t}v\times v)h\,dxdt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}v\times vh\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad-\eta(0)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}v(x,0)\times\phi(x)h(x)\,dx.

Hence

\eta(0)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi(x)\times\phi(x)h(x)\,dx=\eta(0)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}v(x,0)\times\phi(x)h(x)\,dx.

Since
\eta
and
h
are arbitrarily chosen,
we conclude that

(\partial_{t}v(x,0)-\psi(x))\times\phi(x)=0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Since
\partial_{t}v(x,0)-\psi(x)\in T_{\phi(x)}\mathbb{S}^{L-1}
, or equivalently,

\langle\partial_{t}v(x,0)-\psi(x),\phi(x)\rangle=0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Thus we obtain

\partial_{t}v(x,0)=\psi(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

This completes the proof, except for part of the global energy inequality.
∎

In this section, we will indicate the necessary changes from the proof presented in Section 2 in order to show Theorem

3.1.
\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\psi}^{\varepsilon}
is non-empty

Define

.

Taking

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}U_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\varepsilon^{2}\psi(x)\Omega(x)\textup{exp}_{e}\big(t\varepsilon\Omega(x)\big).

Taking

It follows from (

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}(U_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+C\varepsilon^{3}(|\nabla\phi||\nabla\psi|+|\nabla\phi|^{2}|\psi|)+C\varepsilon^{4}(|\psi|^{4}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}+|\nabla\phi|^{2}|\psi|^{2})\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+C\varepsilon^{3},

with
C
depends on
\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
,

\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
,
and
\|\psi\|_{(L^{\infty}\cap L^{4})(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.

It follows from the calculation above that both Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.1
remain true.
Since Lemma
2.3
is obtained through inner variations in the
t
-direction, it remains true.

Therefore, it suffices to show any weak limiting map
V

solves the wave map equation along with the Cauchy data.

3.2.
Passing to the limit

Observe that if
U_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\varepsilon\psi}

minimizes
I_{\varepsilon}
, then
V_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=U_{\varepsilon}(x,\frac{t}{\varepsilon})

would minimize
E_{\varepsilon}
over
\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\psi}
,
that is,
(V_{\varepsilon},\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon})\big|_{t=0}=(\phi,\psi)
. Then we can derive the Euler-Lagrange equation
for
V_{\varepsilon}
as follows.

For small
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+},{\it so}(m))
, consider

V_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(x,t)=(V_{\varepsilon}\textup{exp}_{e}(\delta\phi))(x,t)
.
Then it is easy to check that
V_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}\in\mathcal{H}

and satisfies the boundary condition
(V_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})\big|_{t=0}=(\phi,\psi)
.
Hence, by the minimality of
U_{\varepsilon}
, we have

E_{\varepsilon}(V_{\varepsilon})\leq E_{\varepsilon}(V_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})

so that

\frac{d}{d\delta}\big|_{\delta=0}E_{\varepsilon}(V_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})=0.

By direct calculations, using
\phi^{T}+\phi=0
and the fact

\frac{d}{d\delta}\big|_{\delta=0}V_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}=V_{\varepsilon}\phi,

we obtain that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\Big(\varepsilon^{2}\langle\partial_{t}^{2}V_{\varepsilon},\partial_{t}^{2}(V_{\varepsilon}\phi)\rangle+\langle\nabla V_{\varepsilon},\nabla(V_{\varepsilon}\phi)\rangle\Big)\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\Big(\varepsilon^{2}\langle\partial_{t}^{2}V_{\varepsilon},2\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}\phi+V_{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}^{2}\phi\rangle+\langle\nabla V_{\varepsilon},V_{\varepsilon}\nabla\phi)\rangle\Big)\,dxdt,

where we have used the skew-symmetry of
\phi
that guarantees

\langle\partial_{t}^{2}U_{\varepsilon},\partial_{t}^{2}U_{\varepsilon}\phi\rangle=0\ \ {\rm{and}}\ \ \langle\nabla V_{\varepsilon},\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\phi\rangle=0.

Now, we substitute
\phi=e^{t/\varepsilon}\psi
with

\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+},{\it so}(m))
to obtain

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\Big(\big\langle\partial_{t}^{2}V_{\varepsilon},2\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon}\big(\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}\psi+{\varepsilon}\psi\big)+V_{\varepsilon}\big({\varepsilon}\partial_{t}\psi+\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}\psi+\psi\big)\big\rangle+\big\langle\nabla V_{\varepsilon},V_{\varepsilon}\nabla\psi\big\rangle\Big)\,dxdt=0.

This is equivalent to say that
V_{\varepsilon}
is a weak solution of

(3.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(V_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon})-{\rm{div}}(V_{\varepsilon}^{T}\nabla V_{\varepsilon})=G_{\epsilon},

where

(3.6)

\displaystyle G_{\varepsilon}:=-(\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}-\varepsilon\partial_{t})(V_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}^{2}V_{\varepsilon})+2\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}(\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}^{2}V_{\varepsilon})-2\varepsilon\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}^{2}V_{\varepsilon}.

Since
V_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the energy bounds similar to (
2.21
), (
2.22
), and (
2.23
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(|\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}|^{2})\,dxdt\leq CT,

and

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}|\partial_{t}^{2}V_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dxdt\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}.

Hence, after passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
there exists a map
V\in H^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty),SO(m))
such that

V_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow V\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ L^{2}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)),

and

(\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon},\ \nabla V_{\varepsilon})\rightharpoonup(\partial_{t}V,\ \nabla V)\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)).

It is not hard to verify that for
any
\phi\in C_{0}^{(}\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty),{\it so}(m))
,

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\langle G_{\varepsilon},\phi\rangle\,dxdt

\displaystyle=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\langle V_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}^{2}V_{\varepsilon},(\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}+\varepsilon\partial_{t})\phi\rangle\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad-2\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\langle\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}^{2}V_{\varepsilon},(\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}+2\varepsilon)\phi\rangle\,dxdt.

Hence

\displaystyle\Big|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\langle G_{\varepsilon},\phi\rangle\,dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\Big(1+\big\|\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon}\big\|_{L^{2}({\rm{supp}}\phi)}\Big)\big\|\partial_{t}^{2}V_{\varepsilon}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty))}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\to 0.

While

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\langle\partial_{t}(V_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon})-{\rm{div}}(V_{\varepsilon}^{T}\nabla V_{\varepsilon}),\phi\rangle\,dxdt

\displaystyle=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\big(\langle V_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon},\partial_{t}\phi\rangle-\langle V_{\varepsilon}^{T}\nabla V_{\varepsilon},\nabla\phi\rangle\big)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\rightarrow-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\big(\langle V^{T}\partial_{t}V,\partial_{t}\phi\rangle-\langle V^{T}\nabla V,\phi\rangle\big)\,dxdt

Putting these estimates together, we obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\Big(\langle\nabla V,V\nabla\psi\rangle-\langle\partial_{t}V,V\partial_{t}\psi\rangle\Big)\,dxdt=0.

Or equivalently,
V
is a weak solution of the wave map into
SO(m)
:

(3.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(V^{T}\partial_{t}V)-\nabla\cdot(V^{T}\nabla V)=0\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty).

It is easy to see that
V
satisfies the trace condition

V(x,0)=\phi(x)
. To show
\partial_{t}V(x,0)=\psi(x)
, we need
the following estimate on
V_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}^{2}V_{\varepsilon}
, stated for
U_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}^{2}U_{\varepsilon}
similar to Lemma
2.4
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume
\phi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},SO(m))

and
\psi\in({H}^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{n},\phi^{*}T(SO(m)))
.
Let
U_{\varepsilon}
be a minimizer of
I_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\varepsilon\psi}
. There exists
C>0
such that for any
h\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},{\it so}(m))\cap\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},{\it so}(m))
,
it holds that for a.e.
t>0
,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\partial_{t}^{2}U_{\varepsilon}(x,t),U_{\varepsilon}(x,t)h(x)\rangle\,dx\Big|\leq Ce^{t}\varepsilon^{2}\Big(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big).

Proof.

It is similar to that of Lemma
2.4
. Here we give a sketch. First, as in the previous subsection, we know that
U_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Big[\langle e^{-t}\partial_{t}^{2}U_{\varepsilon},\partial_{t}^{2}(U_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}h)\rangle+\varepsilon^{2}\langle e^{-t}\nabla U_{\varepsilon},\nabla(U_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}h)\rangle\Big]\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Big[\langle e^{-t}\partial_{t}^{2}U_{\varepsilon},(U_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}+2\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}^{\prime})h\rangle+\varepsilon^{2}\langle e^{-t}\nabla U_{\varepsilon},U_{\varepsilon}\nabla h\rangle\eta_{\delta}\Big]\,dxdt,

where
h\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},{\it so}(m))
and
\eta_{\delta}\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
is given by Lemma
2.4
.

As in Lemma
2.4
, we can bound

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)}e^{-t}\langle\partial_{t}^{2}U_{\varepsilon},U_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}h\rangle\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\delta}\int_{T}^{T+\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-t}\langle\partial_{t}^{2}U_{\varepsilon},U_{\varepsilon}h\rangle\,dxdt

\displaystyle\rightarrow 2e^{-T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\partial_{t}^{2}U_{\varepsilon},U_{\varepsilon}h\rangle\,dx,\ {\rm{as}}\ \delta\to 0,

\displaystyle\Big|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)}e^{-t}\langle\partial_{t}^{2}U_{\varepsilon},2\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\delta}^{\prime}h\rangle\,dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|4\int_{T}^{T+\delta}\frac{t-T}{\delta}e^{-t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\partial_{t}^{2}U_{\varepsilon},\partial_{t}U_{\epsilon}h\rangle\,dxdt+4\int_{T+\delta}^{\infty}e^{-t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\partial_{t}^{2}U_{\varepsilon},\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}h\rangle\,dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\big\|h\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)}e^{-t}|\partial_{t}^{2}U_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)}e^{-t}|\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\big\|h\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

and

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\Big|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)}e^{-t}\langle\nabla U_{\varepsilon},U_{\varepsilon}\nabla h\rangle\,dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle=2\Big|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)}e^{-t}(t-T)^{+}\langle\nabla U_{\varepsilon},U_{\varepsilon}\nabla h\rangle\,dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\big\|\nabla h\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\sup_{k\geq 0}\Big(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[T+k,T+k+1]}|\nabla U_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big\|\nabla h\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Putting all these estimates together yields (
3.1
).
∎

Note that translating back to
V_{\varepsilon}
,
(
2.4
) yields that for a.e.
t>0
,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\partial_{t}^{2}V_{\varepsilon}(x,t),V_{\varepsilon}(x,t)h(x)\rangle\,dx\Big|\leq Ce^{t}\Big(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big).

With the help of (
3.10
), we can sketch the proof of
\partial_{t}V(x,0)=\psi(x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows.
For
\eta\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}\langle\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon},V_{\varepsilon}h\rangle\,dx\,dt=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon},V_{\varepsilon}h\rangle\,dx\,dt-\eta(0)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\psi,\phi\rangle h\,dx.

Since

\partial_{t}\langle\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon},V_{\varepsilon}h\rangle=\langle\partial_{t}^{2}V_{\varepsilon},V_{\varepsilon}h\rangle+\langle\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon},\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon}h\rangle=\langle\partial_{t}^{2}V_{\varepsilon},V_{\varepsilon}h\rangle,

it follows from (
3.10
) that we can pass to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
in the above identity to achieve

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}\langle\partial_{t}V,Vh\rangle\,dx\,dt=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\partial_{t}V,Vh\rangle\,dx\,dt-\eta(0)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\psi,\phi h\rangle\,dx.

On the other hand, one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}\langle\partial_{t}V,Vh\rangle\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\partial_{t}V,Vh\rangle\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad-\eta(0)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\partial_{t}V(x,0),\phi(x)h(x)\rangle\,dx.

Therefore, by choosing
\eta(0)\not=0
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\partial_{t}V(x,0)-\psi(x),\phi(x)h(x)\rangle\,dx=0.

Since
\partial_{t}V(x,0)-\psi(x)\in T_{\phi(x)}{SO(m)}
, it follows
that
(\partial_{t}V(x,0)-\psi(x))^{T}\phi(x)
is skew-symmetric.
Since
h\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},{\it so}(m))
is an arbitrary skew-symmetric matrix, we must have that

\partial_{t}V(x,0)-\psi(x)=0\ \ {\rm{for\ a.e.}}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Finally, as in Theorem
2.5
the global energy inequality (
2.25
) can be proved
once we show the following improved estimate, which is analogous to Lemma
2.6
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
\phi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},SO(m))

and
\psi\in({H}^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{n},\phi^{*}T(SO(m)))
.
Let
U_{\varepsilon}
be a minimizer of
I_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\phi,\varepsilon\psi}
.
Then

(3.11)

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(0)=I_{\varepsilon}(0)-I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0)+\frac{1}{2}H_{\varepsilon}(0)\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(|\nabla\phi|^{2}+|\psi|^{2})(x)\,dx+C\varepsilon^{3}.

Proof.

Similar to Lemma
2.6
. First, by definition

I_{\varepsilon}(0)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varepsilon^{2}|\psi|^{2}\,dx,

and by (
3.1
),

\frac{1}{2}H_{\varepsilon}(0)=\frac{1}{2}I_{\varepsilon}(U_{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{1}{2}(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx+C\varepsilon^{3}).

Thus, it remains to show

|I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0)|\leq C\varepsilon^{3}.

Note that
U_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\to SO(m)
, one has

I_{\varepsilon}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|U_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx

Hence, for
\delta>0
small, we can estimate

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{\delta}I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)\,dt\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\frac{2}{\delta}\int_{0}^{\delta}\langle\partial_{t}(U_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}),U_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}-U_{\varepsilon}^{T}(x,0)\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}(x,0)\rangle\,dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle+\Big|\frac{2}{\delta}\int_{0}^{\delta}\langle\partial_{t}(U_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}),U_{\varepsilon}^{T}(x,0)\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}(x,0)\rangle\,dxdt\Big|.

Now we can apply the same estimates as in Lemma
2.6
, with (
2.18
) replaced by
(
3.9
), to bound

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{2}{\delta}\int_{0}^{\delta}\langle\partial_{t}(U_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}),U_{\varepsilon}^{T}(x,0)\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}(x,0)\rangle\,dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{3}\big(\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\big)\leq C\varepsilon^{3},

and

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{2}{\delta}\int_{0}^{\delta}\big\langle\partial_{t}(U_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}),U_{\varepsilon}^{T}\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}-U_{\varepsilon}^{T}(x,0)\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}(x,0)\big\rangle\,dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}^{2}U_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dxdt\rightarrow 0,

as
\delta\to 0
. Putting all these estimates together yields (
3.11
).
∎

Finally, we would like to raise the question whether
Theorem
1.1
holds for Riemannian homogeneous
spaces, or even general compact smooth target manifolds.
