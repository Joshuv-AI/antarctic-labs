Abstract
Using the worldline in-in effective action obtained in Porto:2024cwd , we derive the total even-in-velocity relative impulse, scattering angle, and time delay at fifth post-Newtonian (5PN) order, including radiation-reaction and hereditary contributions at 𝒪 ⁡ ( G 5 ​ ν 2 ) {\cal O}(G^{5}\nu^{2}) and 𝒪 ⁡ ( G 6 ​ ν 2 ) {\cal O}(G^{6}\nu^{2}) . We introduce an isotropic-like description which, together with the associated losses of energy and angular momentum, fixes the evolution of the system entirely from scattering data. This framework opens the door to an unambiguous characterization of the underlying two-body dynamics solely in terms of scattering observables. Following Porto:2024cwd , we isolate a conservative component using Feynman’s i ​ 0 + i0^{+} prescription. This sector contains both “tail-like” and “memory-like” contributions, the latter being intrinsically nonlocal in time and described by a double Principal-Value integral. Owing to the local-in-time character of the corresponding (in-in) action, we establish a systematic procedure that is consistent with Feynman’s prescription while preserving the complete local dynamics. This provides a universal contribution to the conservative (isotropic) Hamiltonian at 5PN order and, as a byproduct, also fixes the value of the Effective One Body coefficients { d ¯ 5 ​ l ​ o ​ c , a 6 ​ l ​ o ​ c } \{{\bar{d}}_{5{\rm loc}},a_{6{\rm loc}}\} consistently with the Tutti-Frutti framework. For completeness, we analyse the “ γ ​ - ​ 3 \gamma\text{-}3 ” prescription introduced in recent post-Minkowskian computations. When implemented in our formalism, we find exact agreement over the overlapping regime of validity. In contrast, Feynman’s prescription yields a (local) memory-like contribution with the opposite sign at 𝒪 ⁡ ( G 5 ​ ν 2 ) {\cal O}(G^{5}\nu^{2}) . We also find that an analogous γ ​ - ​ 3 \gamma\text{-}3 rerouting at 𝒪 ⁡ ( G 6 ​ ν 2 ) {\cal O}(G^{6}\nu^{2}) would be incompatible with the conjecture that all π 2 \pi^{2} terms arise solely from the potential region, while Feynman’s formulation preserves this expectation.

4.2.2
Hamiltonian

The existence of an isotropic form of a conservative-like force does not, in general, guarantee the existence of a (local) Hamiltonian. In such an isotropic coordinate system, the latter takes the form,

\displaystyle H(r,{\boldsymbol{p}}^{2})=\frac{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{2}}{2M\nu}+\sum_{n}c_{n}({\boldsymbol{p}}^{2})\left(\frac{G}{r}\right)^{n}\;,

(4.33)

such that the equations of motion read

\displaystyle\dot{{\boldsymbol{r}}}=\mathcal{A}(r,{\boldsymbol{p}}^{2}){\boldsymbol{p}}\;,\qquad\dot{{\boldsymbol{p}}}=\mathcal{B}(r,{\boldsymbol{p}}^{2}){\boldsymbol{n}}\,,

(4.34)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(r,{\boldsymbol{p}}^{2})\equiv 2\frac{\partial H}{\partial{\boldsymbol{p}}^{2}}=\frac{1}{M\nu}+2\sum_{n}G^{n}\frac{c^{\prime}_{n}({\boldsymbol{p}}^{2})}{r^{n}}\;,\quad\mathcal{B}(r,{\boldsymbol{p}}^{2})\equiv-\frac{\partial H}{\partial r}=\sum_{n}nG^{n}\frac{c_{n}({\boldsymbol{p}}^{2})}{r^{n+1}}\;.

(4.35)

Using that
{\boldsymbol{p}}^{2}
is a function of
{\boldsymbol{v}}^{2}

5
Alternative Memories

Building on the manifest locality of the associated in-in forces in the isotropic-like representation, in the previous section we constructed a separation between conservative and dissipative effects without the need to invoke nonlocal-in-time structures (beyond what is already inherent from standard tail terms, see (
2.12
)). In what follows we explore other possible options.

5.1
Origin of nonlocality

The nonlocal-in-time structure of the action can be traced directly to the appearance of a product of absolute values, namely
|\omega_{1}|\,|\omega_{2}|
, in the (real) Feynman effective action. This feature is not optional: it is enforced by the choice of propagator that renders the symmetry
\omega\to-\omega
manifest at the level of the frequency integrand. Indeed, the reader will immediately recognize that
|\omega|
is essentially the unique parity-even function that is linear in
\omega
in magnitude (any putative odd-in-
\omega
linear term would be eliminated by the
\omega\to-\omega
symmetry). The ensuing time nonlocality is therefore nothing but the Fourier-space imprint of this parity-symmetrized linearity, amplified by the specific convolution of Green’s functions that arises in nonlinear gravitational contributions to the effective action.

As discussed already in
Porto:2024cwd
, we may isolate a tail-like region within the class of propagator convolutions in which the two frequencies effectively collapse onto a single one through an intermediate
static
propagator. As we demonstrated, this prescription yields perfect agreement with the corresponding results across the existing PM literature
Dlapa:2021npj
;
Dlapa:2021vgp
;
Driesse:2024xad
;
Driesse:2026qiz
. Moreover, the resulting contribution to the scattering angle can be shown to follow from a manifestly local action [cf. (
2.65
) in
green
]. However, in contrast to genuine tail (and failed-tail) terms—where the multipoles couple through a conserved quantity—the tail-like sector is characterized by two crucial features:
i)
one must keep one of the three multipoles
unperturbed
; and
ii)
the
\omega\to-\omega
symmetry, implemented through absolute values, discards some of the contributions. The latter mechanism is essential for obtaining the one-to-one match with the PM results. As a result, the tail-like action can only be understood in a PM-expanded sense, in which we remove certain otherwise admissible contributions (see (
2.4
) and Eq. (7.2) in
Porto:2024cwd
).

Another crucial aspect of the derivation is that Feynman’s prescription does more than drop some of the corrections associated with
static
multipoles, it also eliminates from the tail-like region an extra term arising from the product of three quadrupole moments in the action. Concretely, the piece proportional to the frequency structure [cf. (
2.52
)]

\textrm{Sign}(\omega_{1})\textrm{Sign}(\omega_{3})\omega_{1}^{2}\,\omega_{3}^{2}\,\omega_{2}^{4}\,\delta(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})\,,

(5.1)

which, for the tail-like region, is identically zero once the conditions in (
2.56
)-(
2.57
) are imposed. This constitutes another essential ingredient in securing agreement with both 4PM
Dlapa:2021npj
;
Dlapa:2021vgp
and 5PM
Driesse:2024xad
;
Driesse:2026qiz
results. The extra term contributes, starting at
{\cal O}(G^{5})
, yielding to the nonlocal-in-time part of the memory-like effective action
Porto:2024cwd
, and becomes a key ingredient of the full conservative sector [cf. (
3.44
)-(
3.45
) in
violet
]. These observations underscore the various subtle aspects of Feynman’s prescription.

5.2
Feynman & Wightman

As shown in
Kalin:2022hph
, the use of Feynman propagators as a vehicle to construct conservative quantities follows directly from the in-in construction, notably in the original
(1,2)
basis, combined with the relationship between Feynman and retarded/advanced propagators

\displaystyle\Delta_{\rm ret/adv}(x)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{F}(x)+\Delta_{\mp}(x)\,,\quad\Delta_{\rm ret/adv}(x)=\int_{k}\frac{e^{ik\cdot x}}{(k^{0}\pm i0^{+})^{2}-{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}}\,,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{F}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{k}\frac{e^{ik\cdot x}}{k^{2}+i0^{+}}\,,\quad\Delta_{\pm}(x)=(2\pi i)\int_{k}e^{ik\cdot x}\delta(k^{2})\theta(\pm k^{0})\,.

For the PN-type computations that are relevant for the present work, the relevant Green’s functions are obtained after
integrating over
{\mathrm{d}}^{3}{\boldsymbol{k}}

\displaystyle\Delta_{\rm ret/adv}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{i}{4\pi}\omega\;,\quad\Delta_{F}(\omega)=-\frac{i}{4\pi}\textrm{Sign}(\omega)\omega\;,

\displaystyle\quad\Delta_{\pm}(\omega)=\pm\frac{i}{2\pi}\theta(\pm\omega)\omega\;.

(5.3)

From here it follows that the relation in (
5.2

\textrm{Sign}(\omega)=1-2\theta(-\omega)
, which the reader will recall is also behind the identification of a local part through the PB prescription. The apparent nonlocality in the memory-like integral is therefore paired with a corresponding nonlocal
dissipative
contribution that arises from products of Wightman correlators. For illustrative purposes, let us return to the memory contribution in (
2.10
). Upon a few manipulations, it can be rewritten as

\displaystyle S_{\rm(M)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{16\pi^{2}G^{2}}{70}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{2}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\mathcal{F}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})\bigg[I^{ij}_{-}(\omega_{1})I^{jk}_{+}(\omega_{2})I^{ki}_{+}(\omega_{3})\Delta_{\rm adv}(\omega_{1})\Delta_{\rm ret}(\omega_{3})

\displaystyle+I^{ij}_{-}(\omega_{3})I^{jk}_{+}(\omega_{1})I^{ki}_{+}(\omega_{2})\Delta_{\rm adv}(\omega_{3})\Delta_{\rm ret}(\omega_{1})+I^{ij}_{-}(\omega_{2})I^{jk}_{+}(\omega_{3})I^{ki}_{+}(\omega_{1})\Delta_{\rm ret}(\omega_{1})\Delta_{\rm ret}(\omega_{3})\bigg]\,,

(5.4)

with

\mathcal{F}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})=\left(7\omega_{1}^{3}\omega_{3}^{3}-2\omega_{1}^{2}\omega_{3}^{2}\omega_{2}^{2}+2\omega_{1}\omega_{3}\omega_{2}^{4}\right)\delta\!\!\!{}^{-}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})\;.

(5.5)

From here we obtain for the acceleration,

{\boldsymbol{a}}^{\ell}_{\rm(M)}(t)=-\frac{16\pi^{2}G^{2}}{35}\delta^{\ell\langle i}{\boldsymbol{r}}^{j\rangle}(t)F^{ij}_{\rm(M)}(t)\;,

(5.6)

where
F^{ij}_{\rm(M)}(t)
is the Fourier transform of

\displaystyle F^{ij}_{\rm(M)}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{2}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega,\omega_{2},\omega_{3})I^{jk}(\omega_{2})I^{ki}(\omega_{3})\Delta_{\rm adv}(\omega)\Delta_{\rm ret}(\omega_{3})

(5.7)

\displaystyle+\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega)I^{jk}(\omega_{1})I^{ki}(\omega_{2})\Delta_{\rm adv}(\omega)\Delta_{\rm ret}(\omega_{1})

\displaystyle+\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega_{1},\omega,\omega_{3})I^{jk}(\omega_{3})I^{ki}(\omega_{1})\Delta_{\rm ret}(\omega_{1})\Delta_{\rm ret}(\omega_{3})\,.

The conservative part is controlled instead by

\displaystyle S^{\rm cons}_{\text{(M)}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{16\pi^{2}G^{2}}{70}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{2}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta\!\!\!{}^{-}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})\Delta_{F}(\omega_{1})\Delta_{F}(\omega_{3})

(5.8)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times I_{ij}(\omega_{1})I_{jk}(\omega_{3})I_{ki}(\omega_{2})\mathcal{F}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})\,.

from which we find

{\boldsymbol{a}}^{{\rm cons},\ell}_{\rm(M)}=-\frac{16\pi^{2}G^{2}}{35}\delta^{\ell\langle i}{\boldsymbol{r}}^{j\rangle}(t)F^{ij\,\rm cons}_{\rm(M)}(t)\;,

(5.9)

where

\displaystyle F^{ij\,\rm cons}_{\rm(M)}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{2}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega,\omega_{2},\omega_{3})I^{jk}(\omega_{2})I^{ki}(\omega_{3})\Delta_{F}(\omega)\Delta_{F}(\omega_{3})

(5.10)

\displaystyle+\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega)I^{jk}(\omega_{1})I^{ki}(\omega_{2})\Delta_{F}(\omega)\Delta_{F}(\omega_{1})

\displaystyle+\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega_{1},\omega,\omega_{3})I^{jk}(\omega_{3})I^{ki}(\omega_{1})\Delta_{F}(\omega_{1})\Delta_{F}(\omega_{3})\,.

It is straightforward to show that

Off-shell

The above separation into conservative and dissipative terms is
independent
of the trajectories. This allows us, in principle, to implement a decomposition of the force into local and nonlocal contributions at the level of the
off-shell
action, as we demonstrate below.

Using the following relationships,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\rm adv}(\omega)\Delta_{\rm ret}(\omega_{3})-\Delta_{F}(\omega)\Delta_{F}(\omega_{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega\omega_{3}}{8\pi^{2}}\bigg(\theta(-\omega)\theta(-\omega_{3})+\theta(\omega)\theta(\omega_{3})\bigg)

(5.11)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\rm ret}(\omega)\Delta_{\rm ret}(\omega_{3})-\Delta_{F}(\omega)\Delta_{F}(\omega_{3})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\omega\omega_{3}}{8\pi^{2}}\bigg(\theta(\omega)\theta(-\omega_{3})+\theta(-\omega)\theta(\omega_{3})\bigg)\;,

(5.12)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\omega\omega_{3}}{8\pi^{2}}\bigg(1-\theta(\omega)\theta(\omega_{3})-\theta(-\omega)\theta(-\omega_{3})\bigg)\;,

the dissipative force can be written as (for simplicity we drop the (M)-label from here on)

\displaystyle F^{ij\,\rm diss(off)}_{{\rm loc}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{2}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega_{1},\omega,\omega_{3})I^{jk}(\omega_{1})I^{ki}(\omega_{3})\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{3}}{8\pi^{2}}

(5.13)

\displaystyle F^{ij\,\rm diss(off)}_{{\rm nloc}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{2}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega,\omega_{2},\omega_{3})I^{jk}(\omega_{2})I^{ki}(\omega_{3})\frac{\omega\omega_{3}}{8\pi^{2}}\Big(\theta(\omega)\theta(\omega_{3})+\theta(-\omega)\theta(-\omega_{3})\Big)

\displaystyle+\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega)I^{jk}(\omega_{1})I^{ki}(\omega_{2})\frac{\omega\omega_{1}}{8\pi^{2}}\Big(\theta(\omega)\theta(\omega_{1})+\theta(-\omega)\theta(-\omega_{1})\Big)

\displaystyle+\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega_{1},\omega,\omega_{3})I^{jk}(\omega_{3})I^{ki}(\omega_{1})\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{3}}{8\pi^{2}}\Big(\theta(\omega_{1})\theta(\omega_{3})+\theta(-\omega_{1})\theta(-\omega_{3})\Big)\,.

(5.14)

Likewise, following similar manipulations we can decompose the conservative force as

\displaystyle F^{ij\,\rm cons(off)}_{{\rm loc}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{2}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega,\omega_{2},\omega_{3})I^{jk}(\omega_{2})I^{ki}(\omega_{3})\frac{\omega\omega_{3}}{16\pi^{2}}

(5.15)

\displaystyle+\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega)I^{jk}(\omega_{1})I^{ki}(\omega_{2})\frac{\omega\omega_{1}}{16\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle+\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega_{1},\omega,\omega_{3})I^{jk}(\omega_{3})I^{ki}(\omega_{1})\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{3}}{16\pi^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle F^{ij\,\rm cons(off)}_{{\rm nloc}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{2}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega,\omega_{2},\omega_{3})I^{jk}(\omega_{2})I^{ki}(\omega_{3})\frac{\omega\omega_{3}}{8\pi^{2}}\Big(\theta(\omega)\theta(\omega_{3})+\theta(-\omega)\theta(-\omega_{3})\Big)

\displaystyle-\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega)I^{jk}(\omega_{1})I^{ki}(\omega_{2})\frac{\omega\omega_{1}}{8\pi^{2}}\Big(\theta(\omega)\theta(\omega_{1})+\theta(-\omega)\theta(-\omega_{1})\Big)

\displaystyle-\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega_{1},\omega,\omega_{3})I^{jk}(\omega_{3})I^{ki}(\omega_{1})\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{3}}{8\pi^{2}}\Big(\theta(\omega_{1})\theta(\omega_{3})+\theta(-\omega_{1})\theta(-\omega_{3})\Big)\,.

(5.16)

\displaystyle=-F^{ij\,\rm diss(off)}_{{\rm nloc}}(\omega)\,.

The sum of local conservative and dissipative terms then reproduces the total (in-in) force.

From the above analysis we conclude that time nonlocality is therefore just a direct consequence of how the Feynman prescription reorganizes the support of the frequency integrals. These manipulations imply that, within an off-shell prescription, we may simply retain the local contributions from both dissipative and conservative parts without ever referring to the intermediate nonlocal terms. Although suggestive, the PM components of the (relative) conservative scattering angle under such a prescription depart from our previous derivation already at
{\cal O}(G^{4})
. We will comment on this point in §
6
.

On-shell

The above procedure should be contrasted with our earlier decomposition of the effective action, where the
on-shell
local conservative part is isolated only
after
substituting the solution to the equations of motion as an expansion in
G
, see (
2.56
). The two key distinctions are:
i)
the on-shell approach discards (consistently with PM derivations) certain terms (via (
2.57
)) that are instead retained in (
5.15
); and moreover
ii)
it allows us to naturally incorporate 2RR effects into the conservative sector. These differences imply that, to reproduce the on-shell impulse one must include contributions which, from the off-shell perspective, are encoded in a subset of the (a priori) nonlocal part of the force (although involving
I^{(2)ij}_{(0)}
pieces).

An essential point of the on-shell prescription is that only the full Feynman action admits a well-defined variational principle. Once we substitute the solution to the equations of motion and subsequently split the result into local and nonlocal pieces, this decomposition no longer corresponds to a separation at the level of a well-defined action principle. Yet, owing to the local-in-time nature of the resulting in-in force, in §
4
we reconstructed a local action that reproduces the local part of the (relative) impulse and associated scattering angle to the desired PN order [cf. (
3.43
)-(
3.45
)]. The dissipative counterpart is then unambiguously defined as the (local) remainder. Time locality is preserved throughout and the in-in dynamics is recovered by construction. We return to this point in §
6
.

5.3
Symmetrizing & (Re)routing

As we mentioned earlier, the natural way to isolate the conservative sector of the in-in effective theory is to undo the Keldysh representation, expressed in terms of the
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{\pm}
variables, and return to the original
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{1,2}
basis. As discussed in
Galley:2012hx
;
Kalin:2022hph
, rewriting the action as

S_{\rm inin}=\int{\mathrm{d}}t\big[L({\boldsymbol{x}}_{a,1},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{a,1})-L({\boldsymbol{x}}_{a,2},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{a,2})+K({\boldsymbol{x}}_{a,1},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{a,1},{\boldsymbol{x}}_{a,2},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{a,2})\big]\,,

(5.17)

leads to equations of motion that naturally decompose into conservative and dissipative sectors: the former derives from an ordinary Lagrangian,
L({\boldsymbol{x}}_{a},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{a})
, while the latter is encoded in the nonconservative kernel
K
. As shown in
Kalin:2022hph
, Feynman’s prescription furnishes precisely such a decomposition. It is therefore not surprising that, when this procedure is implemented directly at the level of the final off-shell action, written in terms of retarded propagators, one recovers a conservative contribution that is fully equivalent to the one obtained from the off-shell Feynman prescription, in which the nonlocal pieces cancel among themselves. In contrast, if one performs the symmetrization at the level of the decomposition in (
5.2
), while keeping
\Delta_{F}
throughout, one is naturally led back to the same sequence of steps that gave rise to the PB prescription.

There are in principle other ways to reorganize the full in-in computation. For instance, let us return to the memory contribution to the force in (
5.7
), which can be written as

\displaystyle F^{ij}_{\rm(M)}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{2}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega,\omega_{2},\omega_{3})I^{jk}(\omega_{2})I^{ki}(\omega_{3})\frac{\omega\omega_{3}}{16\pi^{2}}

(5.18)

\displaystyle+\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega)I^{jk}(\omega_{1})I^{ki}(\omega_{2})\frac{\omega\omega_{1}}{16\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.75,0.16}-\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{1}{\mathrm{d}}\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\omega_{1},\omega,\omega_{3})I^{jk}(\omega_{3})I^{ki}(\omega_{1})\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{3}}{16\pi^{2}}}\,.

We have highlighted [in
green
] the term that involves both retarded propagators. We immediately noticed that
rerouting
this term in the Feynman diagrams, namely replacing

\Delta_{\rm ret}(\omega_{1})\Delta_{\rm ret}(\omega_{3})\to\Delta_{\rm ret}(\omega_{1})\Delta_{\rm ret}(-\omega_{3})=\Delta_{\rm ret}(\omega_{1})\Delta_{\rm adv}(\omega_{3})\,,

(5.19)

would flip the sign of the respective contribution, yielding a symmetrized total version (with three
\Delta_{\rm ret}\Delta_{\rm adv}
combinations). The resulting force is therefore conservative by construction and furthermore it coincides with the value given in (
5.15
). Unfortunately, similarly to the off-shell prescription, when applied across all regions (without taking into account Feynman’s symmetries) the associated conservative sector is already modified at
{\cal O}(G^{4})
. Nonetheless, when restricted to the memory-like region, the above rerouting produces the same structure as the local version obtained through the PB prescription. This has important implications, in particular when combined with the tail-like contributions, as we discuss next.
