On Regret Bounds of Thompson Sampling for Bayesian Optimization
Shion Takeno, Shogo Iwazaki

Abstract
We study a widely used Bayesian optimization method, Gaussian process Thompson sampling (GP-TS), under the assumption that the objective function is a sample path from a GP. Compared with the GP upper confidence bound (GP-UCB) with established high-probability and expected regret bounds, most analyses of GP-TS have been limited to expected regret. Moreover, whether the recent analyses of GP-UCB for the lenient regret and the improved cumulative regret upper bound can be applied to GP-TS remains unclear. To fill these gaps, this paper shows several regret bounds: (i) a regret lower bound for GP-TS, which implies that GP-TS suffers from a polynomial dependence on 1 / δ 1/\delta with probability δ \delta , (ii) an upper bound of the second moment of cumulative regret, which directly suggests an improved regret upper bound on δ \delta , (iii) expected lenient regret upper bounds, and (iv) an improved cumulative regret upper bound on the time horizon T T . Along the way, we provide several useful lemmas, including a relaxation of the necessary condition from recent analysis to obtain improved regret upper bounds on T T .

Introduction

Bayesian optimization (BO)
(
27
;
29
)
is a powerful framework for black-box optimization problems.
BO aims to optimize an expensive-to-evaluate black-box function using a small number of input-output pairs by adaptively querying input points based on a Bayesian model, typically a Gaussian process (GP) model.
BO has been applied to a wide range of applications, such as materials informatics
(
50
)
, AutoML
(
39
)
, and drug discovery
(
24
)
.
Alongside these applications and algorithmic developments, theoretical properties of BO have also been studied.
This paper focuses on regret analysis under the assumption that the objective function is a sample path from a GP.

Gaussian process upper confidence bound (GP-UCB)
(
27
;
40
)
is a BO algorithm with well-established theoretical guarantees.
The theoretical performance of BO methods is often measured by cumulative regret
(
40
)
, for which both high-probability and expected upper bounds of GP-UCB have been derived
(
40
;
46
)
.
High-probability upper bound of an alternative criterion called
lenient regret

(
28
)
, which counts a regret exceeding a given tolerance, has also been obtained by
6
;
18
.
Furthermore,
18
has shown a tighter high-probability upper bound for the cumulative regret.
These results position GP-UCB as the most extensively analyzed algorithm in the BO literature.

GP Thompson sampling (GP-TS)
(
37
)
is another well-known BO algorithm with regret guarantees.
GP-TS achieves a similar expected cumulative regret upper bound as GP-UCB
(
23
;
37
;
44
)
.
However, its high-probability regret upper bound is limited to the direct consequence of the expected regret bound, leading to worse dependence on probability
\delta
than GP-UCB.
In addition, neither high-probability lenient regret bounds nor tighter high-probability cumulative regret bounds have been established for GP-TS, which has been explicitly described as an open problem in
(
18
)
.
Despite these limitations, GP-TS remains a promising alternative among BO methods with regret guarantees, since GP-UCB often relies on a carefully tuned confidence width parameter that lacks such guarantees in practice.
Therefore, more refined regret analyses of GP-TS are needed to support its empirical effectiveness.

This paper aims to bridge the gap between the regret analyses of GP-UCB and GP-TS discussed above.
First, we investigate the dependence on probability
\delta
in high-probability regret bounds.
While GP-UCB enjoys logarithmic dependence on
\delta
, existing analyses of GP-TS require polynomial dependence of
1/\delta
, raising the question of whether logarithmic dependence is achievable by GP-TS.
For this question, we construct a problem instance in which GP-TS incurs
\Omega(1/\delta^{c})
regret, which implies that GP-TS cannot obtain
O(\log(1/\delta))
regret upper bounds in general.
Second, we derive an upper bound on the second moment of the cumulative regret, which directly leads to an improved cumulative regret upper bound on
\delta
.
Third, we show that GP-TS achieves polylogarithmic expected upper bounds on lenient regrets, the first such bounds for any algorithm.
Our proof for the lenient regret is different from
(
6
;
18
)
and readily suggests polylogarithmic expected lenient regret upper bounds for GP-UCB by combining the known proof techniques
(
37
;
46
)
.
Finally, by adapting the recent analysis of GP-UCB by
18
and our lenient regret upper bounds, we obtain an improved cumulative regret bound on the time horizon
T
for GP-TS.
Our refined analysis, applicable to both GP-UCB and GP-TS, relaxes the conditions on Matérn kernels.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:

1.

Theorem
3.1
provides the problem instance in which GP-TS incurs
O(1/\delta^{c})
regret with probability
\delta
.

2.

In Theorem
3.2
, we derive the upper bound on the second moment of the cumulative regret and the high-probability cumulative regret bound that improves dependence on probability
\delta
by a factor of
1/\sqrt{\delta}
.

3.

In Theorem
3.3
, we obtain the expected lenient regret upper bound for GP-TS, which is polylogarithmic on the time horizon

as with the high-probability bound of GP-UCB.

4.

Theorem
3.5
presents the
\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})
high-probability cumulative regret upper bound, where
\tilde{O}
hides polylogarithmic factors on
T
, for squared exponential and Matérn kernels under a looser condition on Matérn kernels by the refined analysis shown in Lemma

Along the way, we obtain useful lemmas, which are discussed alongside the most relevant theorems.

Preliminaries

This section provides problem setup, regularity assumptions, and background knowledge for GP-TS.

2.1
Problem Setup

We consider a sequential decision-making problem to optimize an unknown black-box function
f
:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}^{*}=\argmax_{\boldsymbol{x}\in{\cal X}}f(\boldsymbol{x}),

where
{\cal X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an input domain and
d
is an input dimension.
Here, “black-box” means that we can obtain only (contaminated) function values, and other information, such as derivatives, is unknown.
Furthermore, we generally assume that
f
is expensive to evaluate.
Therefore, our goal is to optimize
f
with fewer function evaluations.

In this problem, BO sequentially evaluates the maximizer of an acquisition function (AF), which is designed so that beneficial inputs are chosen based on a Bayesian model.
That is, for all iterations
t\geq 1
, BO algorithms sequentially evaluate
f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})
, where
\boldsymbol{x}_{t}=\argmax_{\boldsymbol{x}\in{\cal X}}\alpha_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})
with some AF
\alpha_{t}:{\cal X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
based on the Bayesian model.
By this procedure, BO aims to achieve a sample-efficient optimization.

In the BO literature, the performance of algorithms is often measured by cumulative regret
(
40
)
:

\displaystyle R_{T}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t}).

In particular, if the cumulative regret is sublinear
R_{T}=o(T)
, then we can see that the solution of the BO algorithm converges to the optimal solution.
This is because we can choose the solution
\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}_{t}
so that
f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}_{t})\leq\frac{1}{T}R_{T}=o(1)
.
Hence, we focus on sublinear cumulative regret bounds.

In addition, we consider the performance measure called lenient regret
(
6
;
28
)
:

\displaystyle LR_{T}=\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t}),

where
{\cal T}=\{t\in[T]\mid f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\geq\Delta\}
and
\Delta>0
is a predefined constant.
The lenient regret measures regret under a given tolerance
\Delta
, where the input
\boldsymbol{x}
that satisfies
f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x})\leq\Delta
is regarded as a sufficiently good action.
We further analyze upper bounds of
|{\cal T}|
as with
(
6
;
28
)
.

2.2
Gaussian Process Regression

In this paper, we assume the following regularity condition on the objective function
f
:

Assumption 2.1
.

The objective function
f:{\cal X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
follows
{\cal G}{\cal P}(0,k)

{\cal X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
k:{\cal X}\times{\cal X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

{\cal G}{\cal P}(0,k)
denotes a zero-mean GP with a covariance function
k
.
Furthermore, the kernel function satisfies
k(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime})\leq 1
for all
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}\in{\cal X}
.
Observation noise
\{\epsilon_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are mutually independent and
\epsilon_{i}\sim{\cal N}(0,\sigma^{2})
with
\sigma^{2}>0
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
The noise contaminates observations as
y_{i}=f(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})+\epsilon_{i}
.

Let the training dataset
{\cal D}_{t}=\{(\boldsymbol{x}_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{t}
.
Under Assumption
2.1
, the posterior distribution of
f
given
{\cal D}_{t}
is also a GP, whose mean and variance are derived as follows:

\begin{split}\mu_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})&=\boldsymbol{k}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})^{\top}\bigl(\boldsymbol{K}_{t}+\sigma^{2}\boldsymbol{I}_{t}\bigr)^{-1}\boldsymbol{y}_{t},\\
\sigma_{t}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x})&=k(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x})-\boldsymbol{k}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})^{\top}\bigl(\boldsymbol{K}_{t}+\sigma^{2}\boldsymbol{I}_{t}\bigr)^{-1}\boldsymbol{k}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}),\end{split}

where
\boldsymbol{k}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})\coloneqq\bigl(k(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}_{1}),\dots,k(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\bigr)^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{t}
,
\boldsymbol{K}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
is the kernel matrix whose
(i,j)
-element is
k(\boldsymbol{x}_{i},\boldsymbol{x}_{j})
,
\boldsymbol{I}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
is the identity matrix, and
\boldsymbol{y}_{t}\coloneqq(y_{1},\dots,y_{t})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{t}

(
34
)
.

For continuous input domains, we consider the following additional assumption
(
23
;
40
;
44
;
48
)

Assumption 2.2
.

Let
{\cal X}\subset[0,r]^{d}
be a compact set with some
r>0
.
There exist constants
a,b>0
such that the kernel
k
satisfies the following condition on the derivatives of a sample path

\displaystyle\Pr\left(\sup_{\boldsymbol{x}\in{\cal X}}\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{j}}\right|>L\right)\leq a\exp\left(-\left(\frac{L}{b}\right)^{2}\right),\text{ for }j\in[d],

where

This condition is satisfied by the common kernel functions

, such as

Lemma 2.3

Let

\displaystyle L_{\sigma}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{if $k=k_{\rm Lin}$},\\

Regret Analysis

This section describes our regret analyses.

3.1
Regret Lower Bound

First, we show the simple two-armed problem instance where GP-TS must incur
(1/\delta)^{c}
cumulative regret, where
c\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
\delta\in(0,1)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
{\cal X}=\{\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)},\boldsymbol{x}^{(2)}\}
,
\epsilon_{t}\sim{\cal N}(0,1)
for all
t\in[T]
, and

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)})\\
f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(2)})\end{array}\right)\sim{\cal N}\left(\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1/2\\
1/2&1\end{array}\right)\right).

Then, if Algorithm
1
runs and
T>e
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\Pr\left(R_{T}\geq T/2\right)\geq\frac{c_{1}}{T^{c_{2}}},

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are strictly positive constants.

This theorem implies that we cannot obtain
O(\log(1/\delta))
cumulative regret upper bounds in general.
This is because we can show a contradiction from Theorem
3.1
if we set
\delta=c_{1}/T^{c_{2}}
.
This result motivates us to improve the exponent of
1/\delta
in the regret upper bound.
See the following proof sketch and Appendix
A
for the proof.

Proof sketch.

We derive a lower bound on probability

\displaystyle\Pr(E_{f}\land E_{\epsilon}\land E_{\rm TS})\geq c_{1}/T^{c_{2}},

where the events are defined as follows:

\displaystyle E_{f}

\displaystyle=\left\{f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)})\geq 4\sqrt{\log T}\text{ and }f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(2)})\geq f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)})+1\right\},

\displaystyle E_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\left\{\forall t\in[\lceil T/2\rceil],\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{t}\epsilon_{i}\geq-1\right\},

\displaystyle E_{\rm TS}

\displaystyle=\left\{\forall t\in[\lceil T/2\rceil],\boldsymbol{x}_{t}=\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)}\right\},

for which we leverage Lemma 5.1 of
(
47
)
shown in Lemma
A.1
.
Obviously, under these events, GP-TS suffers from
T/2
regret by querying the suboptimal arm
\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)}
for
T/2
times.

Contradictions from the existing study.

3
;
2
(for example, Remark 4 of
(
2
)
) have reported the

regret upper bound that appears to contradict Theorem

.
Their analysis in the Bayesian setting closely follows the proof technique of

)
, originally developed for the frequentist setting.
We conjecture that this transfer may not be fully justified, as the randomness of

In particular, Eq. (11) in

Proof sketch.

The following theorem provides the expected lenient regret upper bound for GP-TS:

Theorem 3.3
.

Fix
\Delta>0

\delta\in(0,1)

in terms of

In the next section, we leverage Theorem

to derive an improved upper bound on the cumulative regret with respect to

18

to obtain a bound on the expectation, as an analysis of expected lenient regret has not previously appeared in the BO literature.

for the proof.

Proof sketch.

For simplicity, we here denote the case of
|{\cal X}|<\infty

\displaystyle|{\cal T}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\min\left\{1,\frac{f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})}{\Delta}\right\}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]\lesssim\frac{\beta_{T}}{\Delta^{2}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\right]\right),

Regarding the upper bound of

Proof sketch.

4
Conclusion and Discussion

In this paper, we showed several regret bounds of GP-TS in the Bayesian setting.
First, we constructed the simple two-armed problem instance where GP-TS suffers from
1/\delta^{c}
regret with probability
\delta
, which implies that
O\bigl(\log(1/\delta)\bigr)
regret upper bounds cannot be obtained by GP-TS in general.
Second, we showed the
\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T\gamma_{T}/\delta})
cumulative regret upper bound tighter by a
1/\sqrt{\delta}
factor than the existing result.
Third, we proved the polylogarithmic expected lenient regret upper bounds by the different proof from
(
6
;
18
)
.
Lastly, we showed the
\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})
high-probability cumulative regret upper bound by extending the analysis by
18
, in which we relaxed the condition of
\nu
and
d
for Matérn kernels as only
\nu>2
, which matches the condition needed for Lemma
2.4
.
Note that this refined analysis can readily be adapted to that of GP-UCB.
Hence, Lemma
3.4
and Theorem
3.5
partially address the limitations discussed as the “Smoothness condition” and the “Extension to other algorithms” in
(
18
)
.

However, our analyses still have the limitations listed below:

•

Optimality for
\delta
.

Although we show the regret lower bound for GP-TS in Theorem
3.1
, it does not imply the tightness of our Theorem
3.2
in terms of
\delta
.
In addition, whether a similar result can be obtained for continuous
{\cal X}
is unclear.
Therefore, showing the tightness on
\delta
and
T
or a sharper concentration property of
R_{T}
incurred by GP-TS for both finite and continuous input domains remains as future work.

•

Analysis of GP-TS with variance inflation.

We conjecture that GP-TS with variance inflation
(
7
)
can attain an
O(\sqrt{T\gamma_{T}\log(T/\delta)})
cumulative regret upper bound by almost the same proof as that in the frequentist setting
(
7
)
.
However, we expect that extending the analysis by
7
to the polylogarithmic lenient regret upper bound is not straightforward because of the use of the Azuma–Hoeffding inequality that causes
\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})
dependence.
This difficulty also hinders the application of the analysis of the improved regret upper bound on
T
.
An analysis of such a modified algorithm may be an important direction for achieving a regret upper bound with improved dependence on
\delta
of GP-TS.

•

Condition on
\nu
and
d
for Matérn kernels.

We have relaxed the condition for Matérn kernels on
\nu
and
d
from
2\nu+d\leq\nu^{2}

(
18
)
to
\nu>2
in Lemma
3.4
and Theorem
3.5
.
This condition
\nu>2
is also currently needed as the sufficient condition of Assumption
2.2
and Lemma
2.4
, which is leveraged in most prior works
(
18
;
38
;
40
)
.
However, this condition
\nu>2
cannot be satisfied by the widely used value
\nu=1/2
or
3/2
.
Therefore, it is crucial to determine whether the condition
\nu>2
cannot be avoided or if this condition can be further relaxed.

•

Extension to other algorithms.

As discussed in Section
1.1
, there are several algorithms except for GP-UCB and GP-TS with the
\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T\gamma_{T}})
expected cumulative regret upper bounds
(
46
;
44
;
45
;
47
)
.
Therefore, extending our analyses to those algorithms would be an intriguing future direction.

•

Extension to other problem setup.

Although we and
18
focused on the analysis of vanilla BO algorithms, BO has been extended to various problem settings, including multi-fidelity
(
21
;
22
;
42
;
43
)
, multi-objective
(
15
;
32
;
56
)
, constrained
(
9
)
, and parallel BO
(
31
;
23
)
.
Thus, extending our analysis to these settings would be of interest to investigate the theoretical performance of BO algorithms.

•

Limitations discussed in prior work.

The limitations named as “Optimality,” “Extension to the expected regret,” and “Instance dependence analysis in the frequentist setting” discussed in Section 4 of
(
18
)
still remain as interesting directions for future work.

Appendix A
Proof of Theorem
3.1

Theorem
3.1
.

Assume that
{\cal X}=\{\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)},\boldsymbol{x}^{(2)}\}
,
\epsilon_{t}\sim{\cal N}(0,1)
for all
t\in[T]
, and

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)})\\
f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(2)})\end{array}\right)\sim{\cal N}\left(\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1/2\\
1/2&1\end{array}\right)\right).

Then, if Algorithm
1
runs and
T>e
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\Pr\left(R_{T}\geq T/2\right)\geq\frac{c_{1}}{T^{c_{2}}},

(7)

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are strictly positive constants.

Proof.

We show that the probability that the following events simultaneously hold can be bounded from below by
\frac{c_{1}}{T^{17}}
:

\displaystyle E_{f}

\displaystyle=\left\{f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)})\geq 4\sqrt{\log T}\text{ and }f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(2)})\geq f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)})+1\right\},

(8)

\displaystyle E_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\left\{\forall t\in[\lceil T/2\rceil],\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{t}\epsilon_{i}\geq-1\right\},

(9)

\displaystyle E_{\rm TS}

\displaystyle=\left\{\forall t\in[\lceil T/2\rceil],\boldsymbol{x}_{t}=\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)}\right\}.

(10)

If these events simultaneously hold, we can see that
\sum_{t=1}^{T}f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\geq T/2
, which concludes the proof.

Probability of
E_{f}
:

From the assumption, we can see that
\bigl(f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)}),f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(2)})\bigr)
and
(Z_{0}+Z_{1},Z_{0}+Z_{2})
are identically distributed, where
Z_{0},Z_{1}
, and
Z_{2}
are independent and identically distributed as
{\cal N}(0,1/2)
.
Thus, we can rephrase the probability as

\displaystyle\Pr\left(f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)})\geq 4\sqrt{\log T}\text{ and }f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(2)})\geq f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)})+1\right)

\displaystyle=\Pr\left(Z_{0}+Z_{1}\geq 4\sqrt{\log T}\text{ and }Z_{2}-Z_{1}\geq 1\right)

(11)

\displaystyle\geq\Pr\left(Z_{0}\geq 4\sqrt{\log T}\right)\Pr\left(Z_{2}-Z_{1}\geq 1\text{ and }Z_{1}\geq 0\right).

(12)

Since
\Pr\left(Z_{2}-Z_{1}\geq 1\text{ and }Z_{1}\geq 0\right)
does not depend on any variables, we treat it as a constant.
Furthermore, using Gaussian anti-concentration inequality
1-\Phi(c)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{c}{c^{2}+1}\exp(-c^{2}/2)
, we can obtain the lower bound of
\Pr\left(\sqrt{2}Z_{0}\geq 4\sqrt{2\log T}\right)
:

\displaystyle\Pr\left(Z_{0}\geq 4\sqrt{\log(T)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{4\sqrt{2\log T}}{32\log T+1}\exp(-16\log T)

(13)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{4\sqrt{2\log T}}{32\log T+1}T^{-16}.

(14)

Hence, by choosing sufficiently small

\displaystyle E=\left\{\forall t\geq 1,\forall\boldsymbol{x}\in{\cal X},|f(\boldsymbol{x})-\mu_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x})|\leq\beta_{t}^{1/2}\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}),|g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})-\mu_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x})|\leq\beta_{t}^{1/2}\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x})\right\},

(78)

where
\beta_{t}=2\log(2|{\cal X}|T^{2}t(t+1))
.
We denote a complementary event of
E
as
E^{c}
.
Then, using Lemma
E.1
(Lemma 5.1 of
[
40
]
) and the union bound,
\Pr(E^{c})\leq 1/T^{2}
.
Therefore, we can see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|\mid E]\Pr(E)+\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|\mid E^{c}]\Pr(E^{c})

(79)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|\mid E]+\frac{1}{T},

(80)

where the inequality holds because
\Pr(E)\leq 1
,
\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|\mid E^{c}]\leq T
, and
\Pr(E^{c})\leq 1/T^{2}
.

Regarding
\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|\mid E]
, as with the proof of the elliptical potential count lemma
[
10
,
16
]
, we can obtain the upper bound as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|\mid E]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\min\left\{1,\frac{f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})}{\Delta}\right\}\biggm|E\right]

(81)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\min\left\{1,\frac{\bigl(f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\bigr)^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}\right\}\biggm|E\right]

(82)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\frac{\bigl(f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\bigr)^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}\biggm|E\right]

(83)

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}4\beta_{t}\bigl(\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})+\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\bigr)^{2}\biggm|E\right]

(84)

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\leq}\frac{8\beta_{T}}{\Delta^{2}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\biggm|E\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\bigr)\biggm|E\right]\right)

(85)

\displaystyle\overset{(d)}{\leq}\frac{16\beta_{T}}{\Delta^{2}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\right]\right),

(86)

where the inequalities hold because of
(a)
\frac{f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})}{\Delta}\geq 1
for
t\in{\cal T}
,
(b)
f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})+2\beta_{t}^{1/2}\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\geq g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\geq g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\geq f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-2\beta_{t}^{1/2}\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})
due to the conditioning by
E
and the nature of GP-TS,
(c)
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,
and (d) the definition of the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}[X|E]=\mathbb{E}[X\mathbbm{1}_{E}]/\Pr(E)
(Example 34.1 of
[
4
]
),
\Pr(E)\geq 1-1/T^{2}\geq 1/2
due to
T\geq 2
, and
\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\geq 0
.

Therefore, combining Lemma
E.7
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|\mid E]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{32C_{1}\beta_{T}\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}}{\Delta^{2}}.

(87)

Thus, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]\leq\frac{32C_{1}\beta_{T}\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}}{\Delta^{2}}+\frac{1}{T}

(88)

Hence,
\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]\in\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mid t\leq\frac{32C_{1}\beta_{T}\tilde{\gamma}_{t}}{\Delta^{2}}+\frac{1}{T}\}
and
\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|\leq T_{\rm max}
from the definition of
T_{\rm max}
.

Next, we consider the case of
{\cal X}\subset[0,r]^{d}
.
Purely for the sake of analysis, we consider equally divided grid points
{\cal X}_{t}
, where the grid size for each dimension is

.

We define

\displaystyle E_{\rm Lip}=\left\{\|f\|_{\rm Lip}\leq b\sqrt{\log(2adT^{2})}\right\},

(89)

where
\|f\|_{\rm Lip}
is the Lipschitz constant of

.
From Assumption
2.2
,
\Pr(E_{\rm Lip}^{c})\leq 1/(2T^{2})

In addition, we define the event for the credible intervals of

and

\displaystyle E=\left\{\forall t\geq 1,\forall\boldsymbol{x}\in{\cal X}_{t},|f(\boldsymbol{x})-\mu_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x})|\leq\beta_{t}^{1/2}\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}),|g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})-\mu_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x})|\leq\beta_{t}^{1/2}\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x})\right\},

where

.
From Lemma
E.1
and the union bound,
\Pr(E^{c})\leq 1/(2T^{2})
.
Hence, we see
\Pr(E^{c}\lor E_{\rm Lip}^{c})\leq 1/T^{2}

Therefore, we can see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|\mid E\land E_{\rm Lip}]\Pr(E\land E_{\rm Lip})+\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|\mid E^{c}\lor E_{\rm Lip}^{c}]\Pr(E^{c}\lor E_{\rm Lip}^{c})

(91)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|\mid E\land E_{\rm Lip}]+\frac{1}{T},

(92)

where the inequality holds because
\Pr(E\land E_{\rm Lip})\leq 1
,
|{\cal T}|\leq T
a.s., and
\Pr(E^{c}\lor E_{\rm Lip}^{c})\leq 1/T^{2}
.
Then, as with the case of discrete
{\cal X}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|\mid E\land E_{\rm Lip}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\min\left\{1,\frac{f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})}{\Delta}\right\}\biggm|E\land E_{\rm Lip}\right]

(93)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\min\left\{1,\frac{\bigl(f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\bigr)^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}\right\}\biggm|E\land E_{\rm Lip}\right]

(94)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\frac{\bigl(f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\bigr)^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}\biggm|E\land E_{\rm Lip}\right]

(95)

where the first inequalities hold because

due to the definition of
{\cal T}
.

Since the event
E
and
E_{\rm Lip}
holds and discretization error can be bounded from above by
1/t
from Lemma
E.4
, for all
t\geq 1
,

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq|f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})|+|f([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})-\mu_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})|+\mu_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})-\mu_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})

(96)

\displaystyle\qquad+|f([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})-\mu_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})|+|f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})-f([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})|

(97)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})-\mu_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})+\beta_{t}^{1/2}\bigl(\sigma_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})+\sigma_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})\bigr)+\frac{2}{t}.

(98)

Furthermore, we can obtain

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq 2\beta_{t}^{1/2}\bigl(\sigma_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})+\sigma_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})\bigr)+\frac{4}{t},

(99)

because

\displaystyle g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\geq g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\quad\text{(from the nature of GP-TS)}

(100)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\mu_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})+\beta_{t}^{1/2}\sigma_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})+\frac{1}{t}\geq\mu_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})-\beta_{t}^{1/2}\sigma_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})-\frac{1}{t}

(101)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\mu_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})-\mu_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})\leq\beta_{t}^{1/2}\bigl(\sigma_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})+\sigma_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})\bigr)+\frac{2}{t}.

(102)

Finally, we see that

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq 2\beta_{t}^{1/2}\bigl(\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})+\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\bigr)+\frac{4(\beta_{t}^{1/2}+1)}{t},

(103)

because

\displaystyle\sup_{\boldsymbol{x}\in{\cal X}}|\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x})-\sigma_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}]_{t})|\leq L_{\sigma}\|\boldsymbol{x}-[\boldsymbol{x}]_{t}\|_{1}\leq\frac{1}{t},

(104)

due to Lemma
2.3
.

Hence, noting
f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\geq 0
for all
t\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|\mid E\land E_{\rm Lip}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\left(2\beta_{t}^{1/2}\bigl(\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})+\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\bigr)+\frac{4(\beta_{t}^{1/2}+1)}{t}\right)^{2}\biggm|E\land E_{\rm Lip}\right]

(105)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\frac{12\beta_{T}}{\Delta^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\left\{\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})+\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\right\}\biggm|E\land E_{\rm Lip}\right]+\frac{48}{\Delta^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\frac{(\beta_{t}^{1/2}+1)^{2}}{t^{2}}\biggm|E\land E_{\rm Lip}\right]

(106)

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\frac{12\beta_{T}}{\Delta^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\left\{\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})+\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\right\}\biggm|E\land E_{\rm Lip}\right]+\frac{8\pi^{2}(\beta_{T}^{1/2}+1)^{2}}{\Delta^{2}},

(107)

where the inequalities hold because of the monotonicity of
\beta_{t}
,
(a) the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
and (b)
\sum_{t=1}^{T}1/t^{2}\leq\pi^{2}/6
.

Finally, for the term
\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\left\{\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})+\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\right\}\biggm|E\land E_{\rm Lip}\right]
, since we can apply the same proof as the case of
|{\cal X}|<\infty
(with
\Pr(E\land E_{\rm Lip})\geq 1-1/T^{2}\geq 3/4
due to
T\geq 2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|\mid E\land E_{\rm Lip}]\leq\frac{32C_{1}\beta_{T}\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}}{\Delta^{2}}+\frac{8\pi^{2}(\beta_{T}^{1/2}+1)^{2}}{\Delta^{2}},

(108)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]\leq\frac{32C_{1}\beta_{T}\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}}{\Delta^{2}}+\frac{8\pi^{2}(\beta_{T}^{1/2}+1)^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}+\frac{1}{T}.

(109)

Hence,
\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]\in\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mid t\leq\frac{32C_{1}\beta_{T}\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}}{\Delta^{2}}+\frac{8\pi^{2}(\beta_{T}^{1/2}+1)^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}+\frac{1}{T}\}
and
\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|\leq T_{\rm max}
from the definition of
T_{\rm max}
.
∎

\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})

Theorem C.2
.

Fix
\Delta>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
and let
{\cal T}=\{t\mid f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\geq\Delta\}
.
Then, if Algorithm
1
runs, the following inequality holds for any
T\geq 2
:

•

If Assumption
2.1
holds and
|{\cal X}|<\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\right]\leq 4\sqrt{C_{1}\beta_{T}\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}}+1\leq 4\sqrt{C_{1}\beta_{T}T_{\rm max}\tilde{\gamma}_{T_{\rm max}}}+1.

(110)

where
\beta_{t}=\max\bigl\{1,2\log(4|{\cal X}|T/\sqrt{2\pi})\bigr\}
for all
t\in[T]
and

T_{\rm max}
is defined as in Theorem
C.1

•

If Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\right]

\displaystyle\leq 4+4\beta_{T}^{1/2}+4\sqrt{C_{1}\beta_{T}\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}}\leq 4+4\beta_{T}^{1/2}+4\sqrt{C_{1}\beta_{T}T_{\rm max}\tilde{\gamma}_{T_{\rm max}}}.

(111)

where

C_{1}=2/\log(1+\sigma^{-2})
,

\beta_{t}=\max\bigl\{1,2\log\bigl(4\lceil drLT\rceil^{d}T/\sqrt{2\pi}\bigr)\bigr\}

2.3

Proof.

Let
U_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})=\mu_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x})+\beta_{t}^{1/2}\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x})

(c)_{+}=\max\{0,c\}
.

Since
g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\geq g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})
and
\beta_{t}
is monotonically increasing, we can derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\right]

(112)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\biggl(f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-U_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})+2\beta_{t}^{1/2}\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})+L_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})+g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})-U_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})+2\beta_{t}^{1/2}\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})+L_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\biggr)\biggr]

(113)

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\biggl(\bigl(f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-U_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\bigr)_{+}+\bigl(L_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\bigr)_{+}+\bigl(g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})-U_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\bigr)_{+}+\bigl(L_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\bigr)_{+}\biggr)\biggr]}_{A_{1}}

(114)

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{2\beta_{T}^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\biggl(\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})+\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\biggr)\biggr]}_{A_{2}}.

(115)

For the terms
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, by applying Lemma
E.5
and Lemma
E.7
respectively, we have

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{4}{4T}=1,

(116)

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{C_{1}\beta_{T}\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}},

(117)

which is the desired result.
Note that, for the terms
\bigl(L_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\bigr)_{+}
and
\bigl(L_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\bigr)_{+}
, we apply Lemma
E.5
to
-g_{t}
and
-f
.

Next, we consider the case of
{\cal X}\subset[0,r]^{d}
.
Purely for the sake of analysis, we consider equally divided grid points
{\cal X}_{t}
, where the grid size for each dimension is
\tau_{t}=\lceil drLT\rceil
and
|{\cal X}_{t}|=\tau_{t}^{d}
, where
L=\max\{b\bigl(\sqrt{\log(ad)}+\sqrt{\pi}/2\bigr),L_{\sigma}\}

L_{\sigma}
defined in Lemma
2.3
.
We denote
[\boldsymbol{x}]_{t}=\argmin_{\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}\in{\cal X}_{t}}\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}\|_{1}
.

\sup_{\boldsymbol{x}\in{\cal X}}\|\boldsymbol{x}-[\boldsymbol{x}]_{t}\|_{1}\leq\frac{1}{LT}
.

Since
g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\geq g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})
and
\beta_{t}
is monotonically increasing, we can derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\right]

(118)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\biggl(f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})+f([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})-U_{t}([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})+2\beta_{t}^{1/2}\sigma_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})+L_{t}([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})-g_{t}([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})

(119)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})-g_{t}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})+g_{t}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})-U_{t}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})+2\beta_{t}^{1/2}\sigma_{t-1}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})+L_{t}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})-f([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})+f([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\biggr)\biggr]

(120)

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\biggl(|f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})|+|g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})-g_{t}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})|+|f([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})|\biggr)\biggr]}_{A_{1}}

(121)

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\biggl(\bigl(f([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})-U_{t}([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})\bigr)_{+}+\bigl(L_{t}([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})-g_{t}([\boldsymbol{x}^{*}]_{t})\bigr)_{+}+\bigl(g_{t}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})-U_{t}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})\bigr)_{+}+\bigl(L_{t}([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})-f([\boldsymbol{x}_{t}]_{t})\bigr)_{+}\biggr)\biggr]}_{A_{2}}

Regarding the term

, we derive

Regarding the term

2.3

\displaystyle A_{3}\leq 4\beta_{T}^{1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{T}=4\beta_{T}^{1/2}.

Finally, regarding the term

\displaystyle A_{4}\leq 4\sqrt{C_{1}\beta_{T}\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}}.

(127)

Consequently, we obtain the desired result:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\right]

Finally, we show the kernel-specific results shown below.

3.3
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]

\displaystyle\leq T_{\rm max},

\displaystyle T_{\rm max}=O\left(\frac{\beta_{T}\gamma_{T}}{\Delta^{2}}\right).

Since

[

,
51
]
, we obtain the desired result.

[
18
]
, we see that

\displaystyle T_{\rm max}=O\left(\frac{\beta_{T}T_{\rm max}^{\frac{d}{2\nu+d}}\log^{\frac{4\nu+d}{2\nu+d}}T}{\Delta^{2}}\right)

(253)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{{\cal D}_{t-1}}\left[\sum_{t\in[T]}\mathbb{E}\left[\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\mathbbm{1}_{g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})-g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\geq\Delta}\mid{\cal D}_{t-1}\right]\right]

(254)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in[T]}\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\mathbbm{1}_{g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})-g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\geq\Delta}\right]

(255)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{C_{1}|{\cal T}_{g}|\gamma_{|{\cal T}_{g}|}}\right]

(256)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{C_{1}\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}_{g}|]\gamma_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}_{g}|]}}

(257)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{C_{1}\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]\gamma_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}}.

(258)

∎

Lemma E.8
.

Suppose Assumption
2.1
holds and
k=k_{\rm SE}
or
k=k_{\rm Mat}
with
\nu>1/2
.
Fix
\Delta,\rho>0
and let
{\cal T}=\left\{t\in[T]\mid f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\geq\Delta\land\boldsymbol{x}_{t}\in{\cal B}\left(\rho;\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\right)\right\}
.
Then, if Algorithm
1
runs, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;0\right)\right),

(259)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{C_{1}\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;0\right)\right)},

(260)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;0\right)\right),

(261)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{C_{1}\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;0\right)\right)},

(262)

where
C_{1}=2/\log(1+\sigma^{-2})
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{T}({\cal X})
is the MIG over the domain
{\cal X}
.

Proof.

First, we can see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}C_{1}\mathbb{E}[\gamma_{|{\cal T}|}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\right)\right)]

(263)

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{=}C_{1}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\gamma}_{|{\cal T}|}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;0\right)\right)]

(264)

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\leq}C_{1}\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;0\right)\right).

(265)

The inequalities hold because of
(a)
\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\leq C_{1}\gamma_{|{\cal T}|}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\right)\right)
from Lemma
E.3
and
\boldsymbol{x}_{t}\in{\cal B}\left(\rho;\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\right)
,
(b)
\gamma_{T}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\right)\right)=\tilde{\gamma}_{T}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;0\right)\right)
for any
T\in\mathbb{N}
due to the definition of
\tilde{\gamma}
and the shift-invariant property of the SE and Matérn kernels, and
(c) Jensen’s inequality and the concavity of
\tilde{\gamma}_{T}
.

Second, we can obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{|{\cal T}|\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})}\right]

(266)

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{C_{1}|{\cal T}|\gamma_{|{\cal T}|}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\right)\right)}\right]

(267)

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\leq}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{C_{1}|{\cal T}|\tilde{\gamma}_{|{\cal T}|}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;0\right)\right)}\right]

(268)

\displaystyle\overset{(d)}{\leq}\sqrt{C_{1}\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\gamma}_{|{\cal T}|}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;0\right)\right)\right]}

(269)

\displaystyle\overset{(e)}{\leq}\sqrt{C_{1}\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;0\right)\right)}.

(270)

The inequalities hold because of
(a) Cauchy–Schwarz inequality
(b)
\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\leq C_{1}\gamma_{|{\cal T}|}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\right)\right)
from Lemma
E.3
and
\boldsymbol{x}_{t}\in{\cal B}\left(\rho;\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\right)
,
(c)
\gamma_{T}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\right)\right)=\tilde{\gamma}_{T}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;0\right)\right)
for any
T\in\mathbb{N}
due to the definition of
\tilde{\gamma}
and the shift-invariant property of the SE and Matérn kernels,
(d) Cauchy–Schwarz inequality (
|\mathbb{E}[XY]|\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]\mathbb{E}[Y^{2}]}
for any random variables
X
and
Y
),
and (e) Jensen’s inequality and the concavity of
\tilde{\gamma}_{t}
.

For the proof with respect to
\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})
, we use the property of

{\cal T}_{g}=\left\{t\in[T]\bigm|g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})-g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\geq\Delta\land\|\boldsymbol{x}_{t}-\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\rho\right\}
.
Let
\mathbbm{1}_{E}
be the indicator function that is 1 if
E
is true, and 0 otherwise.
We can see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|{\cal T}\right|\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\geq\Delta\land\|\boldsymbol{x}_{t}-\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\rho}\mid{\cal D}_{t-1}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})-g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\geq\Delta\land\|\boldsymbol{x}_{t}-\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\rho}\mid{\cal D}_{t-1}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|{\cal T}_{g}\right|\right],

where the second equality holds since
\bigl(\mathbbm{1}_{f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\geq\Delta\land\|\boldsymbol{x}_{t}-\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\rho}\mid{\cal D}_{t-1}\bigr)
and
\bigl(\mathbbm{1}_{g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})-g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\geq\Delta\land\|\boldsymbol{x}_{t}-\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\rho}\mid{\cal D}_{t-1}\bigr)
are identically distributed.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in[T]}\mathbb{E}\left[\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\mathbbm{1}_{f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\geq\Delta\land\boldsymbol{x}_{t}\in{\cal B}\left(\rho;\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\right)}\biggm|{\cal D}_{t-1}\right]\right]

(271)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in[T]}\mathbb{E}\left[\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\mathbbm{1}_{g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})-g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\geq\Delta\land\|\boldsymbol{x}_{t}-\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\rho}\biggm|{\cal D}_{t-1}\right]\right]

(272)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}_{g}}\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\right]

(273)

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}C_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[\gamma_{|{\cal T}_{g}|}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\right)\right)\right]

(274)

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\leq}C_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\gamma}_{|{\cal T}_{g}|}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;0\right)\right)\right]

(275)

\displaystyle\overset{(d)}{\leq}C_{1}\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}\left[|{\cal T}_{g}|\right]}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;0\right)\right)

(276)

\displaystyle=C_{1}\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}\left[|{\cal T}|\right]}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;0\right)\right)

(277)

where the equality and inequality hold because
(a)
\bigl(\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\mathbbm{1}_{f(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})-f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\geq\Delta\land\boldsymbol{x}_{t}\in{\cal B}\left(\rho;\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\right)}\mid{\cal D}_{t-1}\bigr)
and
\bigl(\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\mathbbm{1}_{g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})-g_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\geq\Delta\land\|\boldsymbol{x}_{t}-\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\rho}\mid{\cal D}_{t-1}\bigr)
are identically distributed,
(b)
\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\leq C_{1}\gamma_{|{\cal T}|}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\right)\right)
from Lemma
E.3
and
\boldsymbol{x}_{t}\in{\cal B}\left(\rho;\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\right)
,
(c)
\gamma_{T}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\right)\right)=\tilde{\gamma}_{T}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;0\right)\right)
for any
T\in\mathbb{N}
due to the definition of
\tilde{\gamma}
and the shift-invariant property of the SE and Matérn kernels,
and (d) Jensen’s inequality and the concavity of
\tilde{\gamma}_{T}
.
By almost the same arguments shown above and Lemma
E.7
, we further obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in{\cal T}}\sigma_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{C_{1}\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}[|{\cal T}|]}\left({\cal B}\left(\rho;0\right)\right)},

which concludes the proof.
∎
