Computing the Skyscraper Invariant
Marc Fersztand, Jan Jendrysiak

Abstract
We develop the first algorithms for computing the Skyscraper Invariant [FJNT24]. This is a filtration of the classical rank invariant for multiparameter persistence modules defined by the Harder-Narasimhan filtrations along every central charge supported at a single parameter value. Cheng’s algorithm [Cheng24] can be used to compute HN filtrations of arbitrary acyclic quiver representations in polynomial time in the total dimension, but in practice, the large dimension of persistence modules makes this direct approach infeasible. We show that by exploiting the additivity of the HN filtration and the special central charges, one can get away with a brute-force approach. For d d -parameter modules, this produces an FPT ε \varepsilon -approximate algorithm with runtime dominated by 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / ε d ⋅ T dec ) \mathcal{O}(1/\varepsilon^{d}\cdot T_{\textup{{dec}}}) , where T dec T_{\textup{{dec}}} is the time for decomposition, which we compute with aida [DJK25]. We show that the wall-and-chamber structure of the module can be computed via lower envelopes of degree d − 1 d-1 polynomials. This allows for an exact computation of the Skyscraper Invariant whose runtime is roughly 𝒪 ⁡ ( n d ⋅ T dec ) \mathcal{O}(n^{d}\cdot T_{\textup{{dec}}}) for n n the size of the presentation of the modules and enables a faster hybrid algorithm to compute an approximation. For 2-parameter modules, we have implemented not only our algorithms but also, for the first time, Cheng’s algorithm. We compare all algorithms and, as a proof of concept for data analysis, compute a filtered version of the Multiparameter Landscape for biomedical data.

Introduction.

Multiparameter Persistence Modules (MPM) provide a way to capture topological features of multifiltered data. Unlike the one parameter case, the decomposition of an MPM is, in general, neither easy to interpret nor robust to noise
[
5
]
. To use MPM in practice, one needs invariants that are informative, stable, computable, and easy either to interpret or to integrate into a statistical or Machine Learning pipeline. A first example is given by the

[

[

In

(

Given a

[

We will describe multiple algorithms to compute the Skyscraper Invariant and also touch on the computation of Harder-Narasimhan Filtrations with respect to other stability conditions.

2.1  Filtrations of simplicial complexes

We denote by

2.2  Persistence Modules

Let
\mathbf{k}
be any field. We denote by
\mathrm{Pers}(P)\coloneqq\fun(P,\textbf{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}})
the category of functors from a poset
P
to the category of finite-dimensional
\mathbf{k}
-vector spaces and call the objects of any such category
Persistence Modules
. We say that
V\in\mathrm{Pers}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is
bounded
if its support is bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
A
d
-parameter persistence module can also be seen as a module over the
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-graded algebra
A\coloneqq\mathbf{k}\{x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\}

of polynomials in
d
variables with
real
exponents
[
15
, Theorem 1]
.

Presentations.

A module
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\textbf{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}}
is
free
if it is isomorphic to a direct sum of interval modules whose support is
{\left\{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\beta\geq\alpha\right\}}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A
finite presentation
of
V
is a map

F_{1}\overset{\phi}{\longrightarrow}F_{0}

between free modules such that
V\simeq\coker\phi
. When
V
admits a finite presentation, we say that
V
is
finitely presentable
.
When a Persistence Module
V
is obtained by the homology of a filtered
finite
simplicial complex, then
V
must be finitely presentable.
Henceforth, we will restrict ourselves to the category
\mathrm{Pers}_{\text{fp}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of finitely presentable persistence modules. We further denote by
\mathrm{Pers}_{\text{fp}}^{\text{b}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the category of bounded and finitely presentable modules.

Each finitely presentable persistence module
V\in\mathrm{Pers}_{\text{fp}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
has a finite minimal presentation, unique up to natural isomorphism. This minimal presentation can be extended to a
minimal resolution
that is at most of length
d+1
. The degrees of the generators, relations, higher relations etc. in a minimal resolution are independent of the chosen resolution and form multi-subsets
b_{0}(V),\ b_{1}(V),\dots,\ b_{d}(V)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
– the graded
Betti numbers
of
V
.

A presentation (or rather a map between free modules) can be encoded in a
graded matrix

M
. Each row of
M
corresponds to a generator and each column to a relation. The rows and columns are endowed with the parameter value – or
degree
– of the corresponding generator or relation, making
M
a
graded matrix
or
monomial matrix

[
54
]
. If we write
A[G]
for the free module generated by a tuple of degrees
G
, then a graded matrix is nothing but a linear map
M\colon A[R]\to A[G]
and it presents
V
if
V\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}A[G]/\Img(M)
.

Invariants.

In order to compare or vectorise Persistence Modules, we will consider invariants: assignments
I
, from Persistence Modules to some set
\mathcal{P}
that satisfy

V\simeq W\implies I(V)=I(W).

We say that
I
is an
additive
invariant if
\mathcal{P}
is an abelian group and for every
V,\ W
, we have

I(V\oplus W)=I(V)+I(W).

(7)

The
Hilbert function
of
V
, given by the pointwise dimension
\udim V\colon\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto\dim_{\mathbf{k}}V_{\alpha}
, is an additive invariant; and so is the
rank invariant
, which assigns to
V\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\textbf{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}}
, the collection
\rank_{V}
of the integers
(\rank V_{\alpha\to\beta})_{\alpha\leq\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.

2.3  Change of Poset Domain.

In the course of this paper, we will restrict our attention to sub-posets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in multiple ways. To restrict or induce persistence modules supported on one poset to another, we make liberal use of Kan extensions because they make book-keeping easier. This comes at the price of losing some clarity in the exposition, so we will briefly review the theory here. More details on Kan extensions can be found in
[
51
, §X.3]
.

Kan Extensions.

Let
h\colon P\to Q
be a map of posets. We denote by
h^{*}
the restriction functor
-\circ h\colon\mathrm{Pers}(Q)\to\mathrm{Pers}(P)
. We denote by, respectively,
h_{!}\colon\mathrm{Pers}(P)\to\mathrm{Pers}(Q)
and
h_{*}\colon\mathrm{Pers}(P)\to\mathrm{Pers}(Q)
the left and right
Kan extensions
along
h
. Equivalently, we can define them as the left adjoint
h_{!}\dashv h^{*}
and right adjoint
h^{*}\dashv h_{*}
of
h^{*}
.

Remark 2.1

(
[
51
, X.3, Theorem 1]
)
.

For
V\in\mathrm{Pers}_{\text{fp}}(P)
, the vector space at
\gamma\in Q
of a Kan extension along

can be computed via (co)limits with the formulas

h_{!}(V)_{\gamma}\simeq\colim\limits_{h(\alpha)\leq\gamma}V_{\alpha}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad h_{*}(V)_{\gamma}\simeq\lim\limits_{h(\alpha)\geq\gamma}V_{\alpha}.

(8)

Observe that when
h
is fully faithful, the first unit
u^{!}_{h}\colon\Id_{\mathrm{Pers}(P)}\to h^{*}h_{!}
and the second counit
c^{*}_{h}\colon h^{*}h_{*}\to\Id_{\mathrm{Pers}(P)}
are isomorphisms.

Definition 2.2
.

For any subset
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we also write
\langle S\rangle\coloneqq\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\exists s\in S\colon s\leq\alpha\}
for the induced sub-poset.
Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be any subset, then we treat it as an induced sub-poset and denote by
\iota_{B}\colon B\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
the corresponding fully faithful functor. In particular, the unit
u^{!}_{\iota_{B}}
and counit
c^{*}_{\iota_{B}}
are isomorphisms by the previous
Subsection 2.3
.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we write
\iota_{\alpha}
as shorthand for
\iota_{\langle\alpha\rangle}\colon\langle\alpha\rangle\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

. (
Equation 8
) immediately verifies that
({\iota_{\alpha}})_{!}
extends

by

, whereas
({\iota_{\alpha}})_{*}
extends

via isomorphisms along its values on the boundary of
\langle\alpha\rangle
. That is, for every
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\left((\iota_{\alpha})_{*}V\right)_{\gamma}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}V_{\gamma\vee\alpha}
, where
\vee
denotes the join in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Grids, Discretisation, and Kan extensions.

A
grid

\mathcal{G}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the product of
d
finite subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. A grid
\mathcal{G}
is called
regular
of width
\varepsilon
if it is the intersection of a cube of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Let
\mathcal{G}
be a grid, we denote by
\mathcal{G}^{+}
the extension of
\mathcal{G}
where we formally add
\pm\infty
as a value in every coordinate. We define for

\lfloor\alpha\rfloor_{\mathcal{G}}\coloneqq\max{\left\{g\in\mathcal{G}^{+},\ g\leq\alpha\right\}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\lceil\alpha\rceil_{\mathcal{G}}\coloneqq\min{\left\{g\in\mathcal{G}^{+},\ g\geq\alpha\right\}}.

The fibres of
\lfloor-\rfloor_{\mathcal{G}}
define a tiling of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into rectangular cells:

\mathbb{R}^{d}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{G}^{+}}{\left\{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\lfloor\gamma\rfloor_{\mathcal{G}}=\alpha\right\}}

and we will say that
\beta,\gamma
are in the same cell iff
\lfloor\beta\rfloor_{\mathcal{G}}=\lfloor\gamma\rfloor_{\mathcal{G}}

We treat a grid

as a sub-poset. Since, as a sub-poset, a grid has all limits and colimits, the formulas for Kan extensions become easy for the inclusion
\iota_{\mathcal{G}}
:

Proposition 2.4

(cf.
[
10
,
17
,
9
]
)
.

Let

. The left Kan extension wrt.

satisfies

((i_{\mathcal{G}})_{!}V)_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}V_{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor_{\mathcal{G}}}&\text{if }\lfloor\alpha\rfloor_{\mathcal{G}}\in\mathcal{G}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\quad\text{ and }\quad((i_{\mathcal{G}})_{!}V)_{\alpha\to\beta}=V_{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor_{\mathcal{G}}\to\lfloor\beta\rfloor_{\mathcal{G}}}

and the right satisfies the dual statement.

Proof.

Follows directly from
Subsection 2.3
, because the comma categories will have a final (cofinal) element given by the maximum (minimum).
∎

Definition 2.5
.

The
induced

, which we denote by

, is the smallest grid of

containing

.

The induced grid of a module contains all of its graded Betti numbers

, Theorem 3.1]
. Finitely presentable persistence modules can be discretised over a grid (e.g.

, Lemma 7.13]

containing

, we have that the counit

Lemma 2.6
.

Given

and

belonging to the same cell of

, the map

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\grid(V)
, we have
(i_{\mathcal{G}})_{!}i_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}V\overset{c^{!}_{\iota_{\mathcal{G}}}}{\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}}V
and since

is an isomorphism.
∎

More Kan extensions.

When the sub-poset

2.4  Classical definition of HN filtrations

We now describe why (
Equation 2
) is a special case of the classical definition of HN filtrations. Given an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
, the
Grothendieck group
of
\mathcal{A}
is defined as the isomorphism classes of objects in
\mathcal{A}
subject to the relations
[V]=[U]+[W]
for every short exact sequence
0\to U\to V\to W\to 0
in
\mathcal{A}
. A
stability condition
is a group morphism
Z\colon K(\mathcal{A})\to\mathbb{C}
such that for every nonzero
V\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
\mathrm{Real}Z([V])>0
. The slope
\mu_{Z}
assigns to a nonzero
V\in\mathcal{A}
the number
\mathrm{Imag}Z([V])/\mathrm{Real}Z([V])
. Moreover,
V
is said to be
Z
-
semistable
if for
0\subsetneq W\subsetneq V
we have
\mu_{Z}(W)\leq\mu_{Z}(V)
. If
V\in\mathcal{A}
admits a filtration
0=F^{0}\subsetneq F^{1}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq F^{\ell}=V
such that the successive subquotients
(F^{i}/F^{i-1})_{1\leq i\leq\ell}
are
Z
-semistable and are sorted by decreasing
Z
-slope, then the filtration
F^{\bullet}
is unique and is called the
classical HN filtration
of
V
along
Z

[
12
]
.

In this paper, we are mainly interested in the setting introduced in
Subsection 1.2
and in its discretization:

1.

Bounded finitely presented
d
-parameter persistence modules:
\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\mathrm{Pers}_{\text{fp}}^{\text{b}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we consider the stability condition
Z_{\alpha}
defined for
W\in\mathrm{Pers}_{\text{fp}}^{\text{b}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

Z_{\alpha}([W])\coloneqq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\udim W\right)+i\dim_{\mathbf{k}}W_{\alpha}.

(9)

2.

Persistence modules over a finite poset
P
:
\mathcal{A}
is the category
\mathrm{Pers}(P)
and for
\alpha\in P
we consider the stability condition
\widehat{Z}_{\alpha}
defined for
\widehat{V}\in\mathrm{Pers}(P)
by

\widehat{Z}_{\alpha}([\widehat{V}])\coloneqq\left(\sum_{\beta\in P}\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\widehat{V}_{\beta}\right)+i\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\widehat{V}_{\alpha}.

(10)

One can go from one setting to the other because HN filtrations behave well with restrictions and extensions:

(
[

, Theorem 2.9]
)

Let

and let

containing

along the stability condition

(

along

(

) is given by

3.2  Storing the Skyscraper Invariant

Given

and

, we will represent

by the Hilbert functions of the factors

of the HN filtration of
\langle V_{\alpha}\rangle
, together with their slope.

To store the Hilbert function of a module

,
we will use a collection of intervals whose indicator functions sum up to
\udim V
. Since the filtration factors are always uniquely generated, these intervals will be too. Such modules are called

, because of their shape. That is, even if the HN filtration itself is not always built from intervals, the Skyscraper Invariant is represented by collections of intervals, which we store via their graded Betti numbers.

One could also represent the Hilbert function of a factor directly via its graded Betti numbers without cutting it up into intervals. The advantage of our approach is that over
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the relations of an interval form an anti-chain by ordering with respect to
x
or

Definition 3.2
.

of

For each

Lemma 4.16

Let

be matrix subspaces of respectively

Conclusion.

Our algorithms, together with the filtered Landscapes give researchers a new invariant for topological data analysis that is robust to noise, finer than the rank invariant, efficiently computable, and directly interpretable from a visual representation.

To develop fast algorithms, we studied the wall-and-chambers structure on skyscraper stability conditions for persistence modules and obtained a full description in
Theorem A
.

Our experiments with Cheng’s algorithm empirically show that higher blow-up sizes are necessary for finite fields compared to infinite ones, confirming the intuition behind the results of
[
37
]
.

8.2  Open Problems.

Improving the runtime.

Figure 8
and
Figure 13
make it clear that, in practice, we are still computing the HN filtration too often. In
Subsection 6.1
, we outline a strategy to prove that it is enough to compute the slope subdivision only for the equivalence classes of
\sim_{t,V}
. These are coarser than the grid by
Subsection 6.1
(ii), but we would probably need a condition that is easy to compute and that guarantees that two points in
\grid(V)
are related by
\sim_{t,V}
to experience a significant speed up.

Differentiability.

To incorporate invariants of MPM into Machine Learning pipelines, the authors of
[
62
]
define a framework, which needs to be extended to include the Skyscraper Invariant.
Instead of Landscapes, one could also filter other vectorizations of the rank invariant.
There are qualitatively different HN filtrations of MPM via changing the definition of the slope
[
29
]
. Can our methods be adapted to this general setting?

Other Stability Conditions.

Subsection 2.4
points towards a possible generalisation to another stability condition. Consider a positive locally integrable function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
. One can replace the real part of the stability condition
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\udim V
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\cdot\udim V
. For each
J\subset[d]
, we can compute the slopes
\mu(\langle V_{\alpha}\rangle_{|F_{J}})
and construct the slope polynomial with these coefficients. Using
Subsection 6.2
, this polynomial will approximate the slope. If
f
is Lipschitz or has other bounds on its growth, then we can bound the error of this approximation. If for some parameter value
\alpha
, one slope polynomial is not just minimal, but even minimal up to this error, we have again found the unique submodule of highest slope for the stability condition at
\alpha
.

Möbius Inversion.

In
[
52
]
, McCleary and Patel Möbius-inverted the rank invariant (
[
61
]
) to represent it as a signed set of rectangles.
For each
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
the same can be done with
s^{\theta}\colon(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{op}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}^{\text{op}}
, yielding a path in the space of signed
d
-cells in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\Hom(\text{Int}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathbb{Z})
. What does it look like?

For a specific
\theta
,
s^{\theta}
is not always represented by a persistence module, but it sometimes is. An example was given in
Subsection 1.2
and we can see that these modules are not finitely presented anymore, but they are finitely encoded over a semi-algebraic set in the sense of Miller’s work
[
56
]
.
If
s^{\theta}
can be represented by a module, we conjecture that this is always the case. In general, this implies that the Möbius inversion will not be finite. The question is therefore: Is there a signed semi-algebraic barcode or persistence diagram
[
43
,
10
]
that represents each
s^{\theta}
and can this be used to construct an efficient data structure to store the Skyscraper Invariant?
