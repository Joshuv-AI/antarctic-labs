Asymptotic stability of strong rarefaction waves to a parabolic-hyperbolic system arising from chemotaxis 0 0 footnotetext: *Corresponding author. 0 0 footnotetext: E-mail addresses: liust533@nenu.edu.cn (S. Liu), lijy645@nenu.edu.cn (J. Li)
Sitong Liu, Jingyu Li[2mm] School of Mathematics and Statistics, Northeast Normal University,Changchun 130024, PR China

Abstract




Abstract:

We are interested in the asymptotic behavior of solutions toward strong rarefaction waves for a parabolic-hyperbolic system arising from chemotaxis. Suppose that the Riemann problem to the corresponding inviscid system admits rarefaction waves. We show that if the initial data is a small perturbation of an approximate rarefaction wave, then the Cauchy problem has a unique global solution that converges to the rarefaction wave asymptotically in time.
The waves can be either a single rarefaction wave or a superposition of two rarefaction waves. Furthermore, the stability results hold regardless of the wave strengths. The proofs are based on the energy method, where the key observations are the monotonicity of the approximate rarefaction waves with respect to both space and time, and the appropriate replacement of spatial derivative of the hyperbolic component with the parabolic component in proper forms.

Key words
: Chemotaxis; parabolic-hyperbolic system; strong rarefaction wave; composite wave; asymptotic stability.

Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the large time behaviors of solutions to the Cauchy problem of the following parabolic-hyperbolic system:

\begin{cases}n_{t}-(nq)_{x}=n_{xx},&\ x\in\mathbb{R},t>0,\\
q_{t}-n_{x}=0,&\ x\in\mathbb{R},t>0,\end{cases}

(1.1)

with the initial data

(n,q)(x,0)=(n_{0},q_{0})(x)\rightarrow(n_{\pm},q_{\pm})\ \text{ as }x\rightarrow\pm\infty.

(1.2)

The system (
1.1
) is derived from the following PDE-ODE hybrid chemotaxis model with logarithmic sensitivity

\left\{\begin{aligned} &n_{t}=Dn_{xx}-\chi(n(\ln c)_{x})_{x},\\
&c_{t}=-nc+\beta c,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

which was first proposed by Levine et al.
[
15
]
to model the interactions between vascular endothelial cells and the signaling molecule vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) during the initiation of tumor angiogenesis. In this system, the unknowns
n(x,t)>0
and
c(x,t)>0
denote the density of vascular endothelial cells and the concentration of VEGF, respectively. In contrast to random diffusion, chemotaxis represents a biased movement of organisms in response to chemical stimuli. The parameter
D>0
denotes the diffusion coefficient of vascular endothelial cells. The parameter
\chi>0
stands for the chemotactic coefficient measuring the strength of chemotaxis, and the parameter
\beta\geq 0
denotes the growth rate of chemical VEGF. The logarithmic sensitivity function
\ln c
of the model (
1.3
) reflects that the chemotactic response of cells to the chemical signal adheres to the Weber-Fechner law, a principle with prominent implications in biological modelings (cf.
[
1
,
5
,
13
]
). Since the sensitivity function
\ln c
is singular at
c=0
, it is challenging to study the model (
1.3
) directly. To resolve this singularity, Levine and Sleeman
[
14
]
introduced an effective Hopf-Cole transformation

\displaystyle q:=-(\ln c)_{x}=-\frac{c_{x}}{c},

which transform the chemotaxis model (
1.3
) to the following parabolic-hyperbolic system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &n_{t}-\chi(nq)_{x}=Dn_{xx},\\
&q_{t}-n_{x}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Then applying the scaling transformations

\displaystyle\tilde{x}=\frac{x}{\sqrt{\chi}},\quad\tilde{q}=\sqrt{\chi}q,\quad\tilde{D}=\frac{D}{\chi},

and taking
\tilde{D}=1
, one can obtain (
1.1
) by dropping the tildes.

The first analytical work on the well-posedness of the system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) was established by Guo et al.
[
9
]
, where it was shown that the system admits a unique global strong solution for large initial data. Subsequently, by taking a positive constant state as the background solution, Zhang et al.
[
33
]
constructed global classical small solutions and obtained an algebraic convergence rate. By establishing an entropy type estimate relative to a constant state, Li et al.
[
16
]
further proved the global well-posedness of large classical solutions. In the multi-dimensional case, owing to the complexity of higher dimensionality, all existing results on global well-posedness have been established for small solutions around a constant state. See
[
16
,
6
,
10
]
for results concerning global well-posedness in various working spaces.

All the above works are concerned with the stability of constant steady states. Once
n_{+}\neq n_{-}
, it turns out that the large time behaviors of solutions to (
1.1
) are intrinsically related to the Riemann problem for inviscid system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &n_{t}-(nq)_{x}=0,\\
&q_{t}-n_{x}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

with initial data

(n,q)(x,0)=(n_{0}^{r},q_{0}^{r})(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(n_{-},q_{-}),\ \ \ \ x<0,\\
&(n_{+},q_{+}),\ \ \ \ x>0.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.5)

To analyze its mathematical structure, we rewrite (
1.4
) as

\left(\begin{aligned} &n\\
&q\end{aligned}\right)_{t}+\left(\begin{aligned} &-q\ \ -n\\
&-1\ \ \ \ \ 0\end{aligned}\right)\left(\begin{aligned} &n\\
&q\end{aligned}\right)_{x}=0.

(1.6)

A direct calculation shows that the matrix

J=\left(\begin{aligned} &-q\ \ -n\\
&-1\ \ \ \ \ 0\end{aligned}\right)

has two distinct eigenvalues

\lambda_{1}(n,q)=\frac{-q-\sqrt{q^{2}+4n}}{2}\ <\ \frac{-q+\sqrt{q^{2}+4n}}{2}=\lambda_{2}(n,q),

(1.7)

if
q^{2}+4n>0
, and the corresponding right eigenvectors are given by

r_{k}(n,q)=(-\lambda_{k}(n,q),1)^{T},\ k=1,2.

It is easy to verify that

\displaystyle\nabla\lambda_{1}(n,q)\cdot r_{1}(n,q)=-\frac{q}{\sqrt{q^{2}+4n}}-1\neq 0,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\nabla\lambda_{2}(n,q)\cdot r_{2}(n,q)=\frac{q}{\sqrt{q^{2}+4n}}-1\neq 0,

where
\nabla\lambda_{k}(n,q)\triangleq(\frac{\partial\lambda_{k}}{\partial n},\frac{\partial\lambda_{k}}{\partial q})^{T},k=1,2
.
Thus the system (
1.4
) is strictly hyperbolic and the characteristic fields are genuinely nonlinear if
q^{2}+4n>0
.

According to the theory of hyperbolic conservation laws
[
28
]
, the solution to the Riemann problem (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) consists of shock waves, rarefaction waves, and their linear superpositions. Wang and Hillen
[
29
]
were the first to construct shock waves for the inviscid system (
1.4
) and explicitly derived the traveling waves for the parabolic-hyperbolic system (
1.1
). Subsequently, using the energy method, Li and Wang
[
21
]
proved the asymptotic stability of traveling waves for (
1.1
) away from the vacuum state under zero-mass perturbations. Li et al.
[
17
]
generalized the work of
[
21
]
to composite two traveling waves. On the basis of weighted energy estimates, Jin et al.
[
12
]
further derived the asymptotic stability of traveling waves with vacuum end state under zero mass perturbations. The first multidimensional study was conducted by Chae et al.
[
2
]
, who obtained the stability of planar traveling waves on an infinite strip domain under zero-mass perturbations. All these works were restricted to zero-mass perturbations as their methods essentially rely on the anti-derivative approach. Very recently, a breakthrough was achieved by Choi et al.
[
3
,
4
]
where the so-called
L^{2}
contraction property was successfully derived for general perturbations of traveling waves via the relative entropy method. Based on such relative entropy method, along with some intrinsic cancellation mechanisms of the chemotaxis model, the authors of the current paper
[
22
]
proved the nonlinear stability of planar traveling waves in the three-dimensional case under general perturbations.

Compared with the fruitful works available for traveling waves of (
1.1
), the understanding of rarefaction waves is quite limited. To our knowledge, Rascle
[
27
]
was the first to construct rarefaction waves for the inviscid system (
1.4
), where a strictly hyperbolic region and a linearly degenerate region were found. Very recently, Li and Mathur
[
20
]
as well as He and Wang
[
11
]
presented a comprehensive study of the Riemann problem for (
1.4
) and constructed rarefaction waves, shock waves, contact discontinuities, and their superpositions. However, it remains an open question whether these basic waves are stable under appropriate perturbations. Furthermore, for the parabolic-hyperbolic system (
1.1
), it is unknown whether its large-time behaviors could include a single rarefaction wave and its superposition with rarefaction waves or shock waves from other fields.

The purpose of this paper is to prove the asymptotic stability of rarefaction waves and the superposition of two rarefaction waves. We note that as early as the 1980s, Xin
[
30
,
31
]
presented a general stability theory of weak rarefaction waves and the superposition of two weak rarefaction waves for
2\times 2
viscous conservation laws. In contrast to the works in
[
30
,
31
]
, we are interested in the strong rarefaction waves.

We now state our main results. Define the rarefaction curve
R_{k}
in a suitable neighborhood of
(n_{-},q_{-})
as

\displaystyle R_{k}(n_{-},q_{-})=\{(n,q)\in\mathbb{R}^{2};\ h_{k}(n,q)=h_{k}(n_{-},q_{-});\ \lambda_{k}(n,q)\geq\lambda_{k}(n_{-},q_{-})\},

(1.9)

where

is a
k
-Riemann invariant. Following
[
11
]
or
[

]
, we derive our Riemann invariants as follows:

\displaystyle h_{1}(n,q)=(\sqrt{q^{2}+4n}+q)(\sqrt{q^{2}+4n}-2q)^{2},

(1.10)

\displaystyle h_{2}(n,q)=(\sqrt{q^{2}+4n}-q)(\sqrt{q^{2}+4n}+2q)^{2}.

It is easy to verify that the Riemann invariants satisfy

\displaystyle\nabla h_{k}\cdot r_{k}=0,\ k=1,2.

(1.11)

Our first result is about the asymptotic stability of single rarefaction waves.

.

Let
k=1,2
. For each fixed

with

, there exists a positive constant

, such that
if

-rarefaction curve

,

then the initial value problem (

)-(
1.2
) has a unique global solution

satisfying

(n-n^{r}_{k},q-q^{r}_{k})\in C^{0}([0,+\infty);L^{2}),\ (n,q)_{x}\in C^{0}([0,+\infty);L^{2}),\ q_{xx}\in L^{2}((0,+\infty);L^{2}),

(1.13)

and

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|(n,q)(x,t)-(n^{r}_{k},q^{r}_{k})(x,t)\right|=0,

(1.14)

where
(N_{k0},Q_{k0})(x)
is the initial value of smooth approximate rarefaction wave

constructed in (
2.12
).

Remark 1.1
.

In biology our results imply that the bacterial distribution will become increasingly sparse in the form of the rarefaction wave if initially it is close to
(N_{0},Q_{0})
.

Remark 1.2
.

Our stability results hold true regardless of the strengths of the rarefaction waves, i.e. the amplitude
|n_{-}-n_{+}|+|q_{-}-q_{+}|
can be arbitrarily large.

Yang and Zhao
[
32
]
proposed a general stability theory of strong rarefaction wave for
2\times 2
conservation laws with positive definite viscosity coefficient matrix. Since the system (
1.1
) only exhibits partial viscosity, its viscosity coefficient matrix is not positive definite but degenerate. Hence, the theory established in
[
32
]
cannot be applied. We prove our two theorems by observing two key ingredients: One is the monotonicity of the approximations of waves in both
x
and
t
, which provides a “good” sign in the proof of basic energy estimate (see Lemma
3.2
). The other is the management of
(q-Q_{k})_{x}
, i.e.
\psi_{x}
in the perturbation equations. Since
\psi
satisfies a first-order equation (see (
3.2
)), it is challenging to estimate
\psi_{x}
directly. Instead, thanks to the specific coupling structure of the perturbation equations, we replace
\psi_{x}
in terms of
\phi
in the
L^{2}
estimate (see (
3.14
)) and
H^{1}
estimate (see (
3.37
)). Then, by exploring the strong dissipation of the parabolic equation of
\phi
, we successfully close the
a priori
estimate.

has been extensively refined by numerous researchers in the context of more complex systems, including the one dimensional full Navier-Stokes equations

7

and the Navier-Stokes-Poisson equations
[

]
. In particular, the work in
[

]
also includes the global stability of strong rarefaction waves of isentropic Navier-Stokes equations with a very general pressure. By observing some essential cancellations in the perturbation system, Li and Wang

and Li et al.

Proof.

Solving (
3.10

F^{1/2}(t)\leq F^{1/2}(0)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\beta(\tau)d\tau.

Taking a square of this inequality, one obtains the desired estimate (

).
∎

We now derive the
L^{2}

Lemma 3.2
.

Let the assumptions of Proposition
3.3
hold. There exist two constants
C>0
and
\chi_{0}>0
such that if
E(T)\leq\chi_{0}
, then

\displaystyle\left\|\phi(\cdot,t)\right\|^{2}+\left\|\psi(\cdot,t)\right\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\||Q_{kx}|^{1/2}\phi(\cdot,\tau)\|^{2}+\||N_{kt}|^{1/2}\psi(\cdot,\tau)\|^{2}+\left\|\phi_{x}(\cdot,\tau)\right\|^{2}\Big)

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\phi_{0}\|^{2}+\|\psi_{0}\|^{2}+\varepsilon)\ \text{ for any }t\in[0,T].

Proof.

Multiplying the first equation of (
3.2
) by
\phi
and the
second one by
\psi N_{k}
, summing them up and integrating the result over
\mathbb{R}\times[0,t]
, we have by integration by parts,

\displaystyle\left.\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{\phi^{2}}{2}+\frac{\psi^{2}}{2}N_{k}\right)dx\right|_{0}^{t}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{kx}\phi^{2}dxd\tau-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}N_{kt}\psi^{2}dxd\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{x}^{2}dxd\tau

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi N_{kxx}dxd\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\phi^{2}}{2}\psi_{x}dxd\tau.

To estimate the last term on the right hand side (RHS) of (
3.13
), we rewrite the first equation of (
3.2
) as

\displaystyle\psi_{x}=(N_{k}+\phi)^{-1}[\phi_{t}-(Q_{k}+\psi)\phi_{x}-N_{kx}\psi-\phi Q_{kx}-\phi_{xx}-N_{kxx}],

(3.14)

which implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\phi^{2}}{2}\psi_{x}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-1}\frac{\phi^{2}}{2}[\phi_{t}-Q_{k}\phi_{x}-\psi\phi_{x}-(N_{kx}\psi+\phi Q_{kx})-\phi_{xx}-N_{kxx}].

(3.15)

We next estimate each term on the RHS of (
3.15
). Integrating by parts gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\phi^{2}}{2}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-1}\phi_{t}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left.(N_{k}+\phi)^{-1}\frac{\phi^{3}}{6}\right|_{\tau=0}^{\tau=t}dx-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t}\left((N_{k}+\phi)^{-1}\right)\frac{\phi^{3}}{6}.

(3.16)

By (
3.6
) and the fact that
\frac{\partial N_{k}}{\partial x}<0

N_{k}(x,t)+\phi(x,t)\geq\frac{n_{+}}{2}\ \text{ if }E(t)\ll 1,

which implies the first term on the RHS of (
3.15
) satisfies

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left.(N_{k}+\phi)^{-1}\frac{\phi^{3}}{6}\right|_{\tau=0}^{\tau=t}dx\right|\leq CE(t)\|\phi(t)\|^{2}+CE(0)\|\phi_{0}\|^{2}.

(3.17)

A direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t}\left((N_{k}+\phi)^{-1}\right)\frac{\phi^{3}}{6}\right|\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|N_{kt}||\phi|^{3}+\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-2}\phi_{t}\frac{\phi^{3}}{6}\right|.

(3.18)

By Lemma
2.3
-(4) and (
3.6

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|N_{kt}||\phi|^{3}dxd\tau\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{kx}|\phi^{2}dxd\tau.

(3.19)

Owing to the first equation of (
3.2
), we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-2}\phi_{t}\frac{\phi^{3}}{6}\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-2}N_{kxx}\frac{\phi^{3}}{6}\right|

(3.20)

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-2}(\phi\psi+\phi Q_{k}+N_{k}\psi+\phi_{x})_{x}\frac{\phi^{3}}{6}\right|.

It follows from (
3.6
) that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-2}N_{kxx}\frac{\phi^{3}}{6}\right|\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi N_{kxx}|dxd\tau.

(3.21)

Integrating by parts gives rise to

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-2}(\phi\psi+\phi Q_{k}+N_{k}\psi+\phi_{x})_{x}\frac{\phi^{3}}{6}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-3}(N_{kx}+\phi_{x})(\phi\psi+\phi Q_{k}+N_{k}\psi+\phi_{x})\frac{\phi^{3}}{3}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-2}(\phi\psi+\phi Q_{k}+N_{k}\psi+\phi_{x})\frac{\phi^{2}}{2}\phi_{x}\right|

(3.22)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|N_{kx}(\phi\psi+\phi Q_{k}+N_{k}\psi+\phi)\phi^{3}\right|+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\phi_{x}\phi^{4}\psi+\phi_{x}\phi^{4}Q_{k}+\phi_{x}N_{k}\psi\phi^{3}\right|

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\phi_{x}\phi^{3}\psi+\phi_{x}\phi^{3}Q_{k}+\phi_{x}N_{k}\psi\phi^{2}+\phi_{x}^{2}\phi^{2}+\phi_{x}^{2}\phi^{3}\right|.

By Lemma
2.3
-(4) and (
3.6
) again, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|N_{kx}(\phi\psi+\phi Q_{k}+N_{k}\psi+\phi_{x})\phi^{3}\right|\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{kx}|\phi^{2}+CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi_{x}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau.

(3.23)

By Hölder’s inequality, Sobolev inequality
\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\phi\|^{1/2}\|\phi_{x}\|^{1/2}
and (
3.6
), the second term on the RHS of (
3
) can be estimated as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\phi_{x}\phi^{4}\psi+\phi_{x}\phi^{4}Q_{k}+\phi_{x}N_{k}\psi\phi^{3}\right|

\displaystyle\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\phi_{x}\phi^{3}\right|

(3.24)

\displaystyle\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\phi\|\|\phi_{x}\|

\displaystyle\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi\|^{2}\|\phi_{x}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi_{x}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau,

Similarly, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\phi_{x}\phi^{3}\psi+\phi_{x}\phi^{3}Q_{k}+\phi_{x}N_{k}\psi\phi^{2}+\phi_{x}^{2}\phi^{2}+\phi_{x}^{2}\phi^{3}\right|

\displaystyle\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\phi_{x}\phi^{3}\right|+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\phi_{x}\phi^{3}\right|+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\phi_{x}\psi\phi^{2}\right|+CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi_{x}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\phi\|\|\phi_{x}\|+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\psi\|\|\phi_{x}\|+CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi_{x}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau

(3.25)

\displaystyle\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi\|^{2}\|\phi_{x}\|^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi\|\|\psi\|\|\phi_{x}\|^{2}+CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi_{x}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi_{x}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau.

Substituting (
3.23
)-(
3
) into (
3
), and combing the result with (
3.21
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-2}\phi_{t}\frac{\phi^{3}}{6}\right|\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{kx}|\phi^{2}+CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi_{x}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau.

(3.26)

Then substituting (
3.19
) and (
3.26
) into (
3.18
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t}\left((N_{k}+\phi)^{-1}\right)\frac{\phi^{3}}{6}\right|\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|Q_{kx}|\phi^{2}+\phi_{x}^{2}+|\phi N_{kxx}|),

which along with (
3.17
) and (
3.16
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\phi^{2}}{2}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-1}\phi_{t}\leq

\displaystyle CE(t)\|\phi(t)\|^{2}+CE(0)\|\phi_{0}\|^{2}+CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{kx}|\phi^{2}dxd\tau

(3.27)

\displaystyle+CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi_{x}(\tau)\|^{2}+CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi N_{kxx}|dxd\tau.

After integration by parts, by Lemma
2.3
-(4), one finds that

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-1}\frac{\phi^{2}}{2}Q_{k}\phi_{x}dxd\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\left((N_{k}+\phi)^{-1}Q_{k}\right)\frac{\phi^{3}}{6}dxd\tau

(3.28)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{kx}||\phi|^{3}dxd\tau+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi_{x}||\phi|^{3}dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{kx}||\phi|^{3}dxd\tau+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\phi\|\|\phi_{x}\|d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{kx}||\phi|^{3}dxd\tau+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi\|^{2}\|\phi_{x}\|^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{kx}|\phi^{2}dxd\tau+CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi_{x}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau,

where we have used Sobolev inequality in the third inequality and (
3.6
) in the last inequality.
Noting
N_{k}(x,t)+\phi(x,t)>\frac{n_{+}}{2}
when
E(t)\ll 1
, we have

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-1}\frac{\phi^{2}}{2}\psi\phi_{x}dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\phi^{2}\psi\phi_{x}\right|dxd\tau

(3.29)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\psi\|\|\phi_{x}\|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi\|\|\psi\|\|\phi_{x}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi_{x}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau,

where we have used (
3.6
) in the last inequality.
By Lemma
2.3
-(4) again, one has

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-1}\frac{\phi^{2}}{2}(N_{kx}\psi+\phi Q_{kx})dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{kx}|(|\phi|+|\psi|)\frac{|\phi|^{2}}{2}dxd\tau

(3.30)

\displaystyle\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{kx}|\phi^{2}dxd\tau.

A straightforward calculation by integration by parts yields

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-1}\frac{\phi^{2}}{2}\phi_{xx}dxd\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\left((N_{k}+\phi)^{-1}\frac{\phi^{2}}{2}\right)\phi_{x}dxd\tau

(3.31)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(|\phi|^{2}|\phi_{x}||N_{kx}|+\phi^{2}|\phi_{x}|^{2}+|\phi||\phi_{x}|^{2}\right)dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{kx}|\phi^{2}dxd\tau+CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi_{x}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau,

where we have used
|N_{kt}|\leq C|Q_{kx}|
from Lemma
2.3
-(4). By the fact that
N_{k}(x,t)+\phi(x,t)>\frac{n_{+}}{2}
and (
3.6
), the following estimate holds

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(N_{k}+\phi)^{-1}\frac{\phi^{2}}{2}N_{kxx}\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi|^{2}|N_{kxx}|\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi N_{kxx}|.

(3.32)

Substituting (
3.27
)-(
3.32
) into (
3.14
) leads to

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\phi^{2}}{2}\psi_{x}dxd\tau\right|\leq

\displaystyle CE(t)\|\phi(t)\|^{2}+CE(0)\|\phi_{0}\|^{2}+CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi N_{kxx}|dxd\tau

(3.33)

\displaystyle+CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{kx}|\phi^{2}dxd\tau+CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi_{x}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau.

Combining (
3.13
) and (
3.33
), we have

\displaystyle\|\phi(\cdot,t)\|^{2}+\|\psi(\cdot,t)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{kx}|\phi^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|N_{kt}|\psi^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi_{x}(\tau)\|^{2}

(3.34)

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{0}\|^{2}+\|\psi_{0}\|^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi N_{kxx}|dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{0}\|^{2}+\|\psi_{0}\|^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi(\cdot,\tau)\|\|N_{kxx}(\cdot,\tau)\|d\tau,

provided that
E(t)
is suitably small. Now set

\displaystyle F(t)\triangleq\|\phi_{0}\|^{2}+\|\psi_{0}\|^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi(\cdot,\tau)\|\|N_{kxx}(\cdot,\tau)\|d\tau,\ \beta(t)\triangleq C\|N_{kxx}(\cdot,t)\|.

(3.35)

It follows from (
3.34

(F^{\prime}(t))^{2}\leq\beta^{2}(t)F(t),\ \forall t\in[0,T].

Therefore, by (
2.16
) and Lemma
3.1
,
we obtain the
estimate (
3.12
) and complete the proof.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let the assumptions of Proposition
3.3
hold. There exist two constants
C>0
and
\chi_{0}>0
such that if
E(T)\leq\chi_{0}
, then

\displaystyle\|\psi_{x}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi_{x}(\cdot,\tau)\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C(\|\phi_{0}\|^{2}+\|\psi_{0}\|_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon)\ \text{ for any }t\in[0,T].

(3.36)

Proof.

Substituting
\psi_{t}=\phi_{x}
into the first equation of (
3.2
) yields

\displaystyle\psi_{xt}=\phi_{t}-(\phi\psi+\phi Q_{k}+N_{k}\psi)_{x}-N_{kxx}.

(3.37)

Multiplying (
3.37
) by
\psi_{x}
and integrating the result over
\mathbb{R}\times[0,t]
, noting

\displaystyle\phi_{t}\psi_{x}

\displaystyle=(\phi\psi_{x})_{t}-\phi\psi_{xt}=(\phi\psi_{x})_{t}-\phi\phi_{xx}=(\phi\psi_{x})_{t}-(\phi\phi_{x})_{x}+\phi_{x}^{2},

we have

\displaystyle\left.\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\psi_{x}^{2}}{2}-\phi\psi_{x}dx\right)\right|_{0}^{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}N_{k}\psi_{x}^{2}dxd\tau

(3.38)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{x}^{2}dxd\tau-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{x}(\phi Q_{k})_{x}dxd\tau-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{x}(\phi\psi)_{x}dxd\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\psi^{2}}{2}N_{kxx}dxd\tau-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{x}N_{kxx}dxd\tau.

We next estimate each term on the RHS of (
3.38
). By Young’s inequality, one has

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{x}(\phi Q_{k})_{x}dxd\tau=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{x}\phi_{x}Q_{k}dxd\tau-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{x}\phi Q_{kx}dxd\tau

(3.39)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{k}||\psi_{x}|^{2}dxd\tau+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{k}||\phi_{x}|^{2}dxd\tau

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{kx}|\phi^{2}dxd\tau+C_{p}\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi_{x}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau,

where we have used
\left\|N_{kx}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{p}\varepsilon
from (
2.14
). Using Young’s inequality
and (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{x}(\phi\psi)_{x}dxd\tau

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{x}\phi_{x}\psi dxd\tau-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi\psi_{x}^{2}dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\psi_{x}\|^{2}+\|\phi_{x}\|^{2}\right)d\tau+CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi_{x}\|^{2}d\tau.

Thanks to the Sobolev inequality and (
3.6

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\psi^{2}}{2}N_{kxx}dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi\|\|\psi_{x}\|\|N_{kxx}\|_{L_{1}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq CE\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi_{x}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau+CE\int_{0}^{t}\|N_{kxx}\|_{L_{1}}^{2}d\tau.

2.3
-(3), one has

\|N_{kxx}\|_{L_{1}}^{2}\leq C_{p}\min\{\varepsilon^{2},\ t^{-2}\}
. If
t<\varepsilon^{-1}\triangleq t_{0}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|N_{kxx}\|_{L_{1}}^{2}d\tau\leq\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\|N_{kxx}\|_{L_{1}}^{2}d\tau\leq C_{p}\varepsilon^{2}t_{0}\leq C_{p}\varepsilon.

(3.42)

If
t>t_{0}
, i.e.
t^{-1}<\varepsilon
, the inequality
\|N_{kxx}\|_{L_{1}}^{2}\leq C_{p}\ t^{-2}
holds, which implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|N_{kxx}\|_{L_{1}}^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\|N_{kxx}\|_{L_{1}}^{2}d\tau+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|N_{kxx}\|_{L_{1}}^{2}d\tau

(3.43)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\varepsilon^{2}t_{0}+C_{p}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\tau^{-2}d\tau\leq C_{p}\varepsilon.

A combination of (
3.42
) and (
3.43
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|N_{kxx}\|_{L_{1}}^{2}d\tau\leq C_{p}\varepsilon.

(3.44)

Substituting (
3.44
) into the RHS of (
3.41

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\psi^{2}}{2}N_{kxx}dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq CE(t)\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi_{x}(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau+CE(t)\varepsilon.

(3.45)

Therefore, combining the estimates (
3.39
), (
3.40
), (
3.45
) and Lemma
3.2

\displaystyle\|\psi_{x}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi_{x}(\cdot,\tau)\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C(\|\phi_{0}\|^{2}+\|\psi_{0}\|^{2}+\|\psi_{0x}\|^{2})+CE\varepsilon+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi_{x}N_{kxx}|,

(3.46)

provided that
E(t)
is suitably small. As in (
3.35
), by Lemma
3.1
and (
2.16
), we obtain the estimate (
3.36
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let the assumptions of Proposition
3.3
hold. There exist two constants
C>0
and
\chi_{0}>0
such that if
E(T)\leq\chi_{0}
, then for any
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{xx}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|Q_{k}||\phi_{x}|^{2}+|Q_{kx}|^{2}|\phi|^{2}+|N_{kx}|^{2}|\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|N_{kx}|^{2}|\psi|^{2}dxd\tau\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi\|\|\psi_{x}\|\|N_{kx}\|^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi\|\left(\|\psi_{x}\|^{2}+\|N_{kx}\|^{4}\right)d\tau

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi_{x}|^{2}|\psi|^{2}dxd\tau\leq C\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi_{x}|^{2}d\tau\leq CE^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi_{x}\|^{2}d\tau,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi|^{2}|\psi_{x}|^{2}dxd\tau\leq C\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi_{x}|^{2}d\tau\leq CE^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi_{x}\|^{2}d\tau.

Owing to (
2.15

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|N_{kxx}|^{2}dxd\tau\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\min\{\varepsilon^{3},\varepsilon^{1/2}\tau^{-5/2}\}d\tau\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

(3.52)

3.49
)-(

3.48

3.2

.

Let the assumptions of Proposition
4.2
hold.
If
\chi_{1}\ll 1
, then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{x}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Phi_{xx}(\cdot,\tau)\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C(\|\Phi_{0}\|_{1}^{2}+\|\Psi_{0}\|_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon)\text{ for }t\in[0,T].

(4.31)

Proof.

Multiplying the first equation of (
4.1
) by
-\Phi_{xx}
and integrating the result, we have

\displaystyle\left.\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\Phi_{x}^{2}}{2}dx\right|_{0}^{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi_{xx}^{2}dxd\tau

(4.32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi_{xx}\left[(\Phi\Psi+\Phi Q+N\Psi)_{x}+N_{xx}+g(N,Q)_{x}\right]dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi_{xx}^{2}dxd\tau+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|N_{xx}|^{2}dxd\tau+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|Q||\Phi_{x}|^{2}+|Q_{x}|^{2}|\Phi|^{2}+|N_{x}|^{2}|\Psi|^{2}

\displaystyle+|N||\Psi_{x}|^{2}+|\Phi_{x}|^{2}|\Psi|^{2}+|\Phi|^{2}|\Psi_{x}|^{2})dxd\tau+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|g(N,Q)_{x}|^{2}dxd\tau.

As in the proof of Lemma
3.4
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{x}|^{2}|\Phi|^{2}dxd\tau\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{x}||\Phi|^{2}dxd\tau,

(4.33)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|N_{x}|^{2}|\Psi|^{2}dxd\tau\leq CE\int_{0}^{t}\|\Psi_{x}\|^{2}d\tau+CE\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\Phi_{x}|^{2}|\Psi|^{2}dxd\tau\leq CE^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\Phi_{x}\|^{2}d\tau,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\Phi|^{2}|\psi_{x}|^{2}dxd\tau\leq CE^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\Psi_{x}\|^{2}d\tau.

By (
2.39
) and
\theta>\frac{3}{2}
, the last term of (
4.32
) satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|g(N,Q)_{x}|^{2}dxd\tau\leq C_{p\theta}\int_{0}^{t}\varepsilon^{3}(1+(\varepsilon\tau)^{2})^{-2\theta/3}d\tau\leq C_{p\theta}\varepsilon^{2}.

(4.34)

Substituting (
4.33
) and (
4.34
) into (
4.32
), by

4.1
and
4.2

) and finish the proof of Lemma
4.3

∎
