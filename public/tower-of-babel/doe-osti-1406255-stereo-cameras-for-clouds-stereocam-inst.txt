1.0 Instrument Description
 The U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM) User Facility’s
 stereo camera setups aim to provide synchronized and stereo calibrated time series of images. Images
 from both cameras of the same stereo setup can be paired together to obtain three-dimensional (3D)
 reconstruction by triangulation. This handbook describes the features and operation of ARM stereo
 camera setups and introduces data collected by them.

2.0 Technical Specifications
 ARM stereo camera setups are sited in pairs. Each member of a pair consists of a high-resolution
 surveillance camera, a minicomputer, a cellular modem, and a solar power unit (if grid power is not
 accessible at the site). The specifications of these components and the requirements for their mounting are
 as follows:
  • Currently, 5MP StarDot SD500BN Netcam cameras that have outdoor rating and are equipped with
 4.5-13 mm varifocal StarDot 5MV4513CS lenses are used. The cameras are set at 2592x1944 pixel
 resolution and a zoom factor that provides 65 o -75 o horizontal field of view (FoV).
  • Each camera is connected to a minicomputer, Moxa V2201, which controls image capture and
 temporarily stores data files. The computer establishes network connectivity through a CradlePoint
 IBR 600LPE cellular modem for data transfer and monitoring.
  • Precise time synchronization is ensured by use of a Garmin 18x PC GPS puck.
  • Solar power unit is composed of two 145 W panels, a 12V 200Ah battery and a Morningstar MPPT
 25A charge controller.
  • Stability of the cameras is highly crucial. Clear FoV and minimum possible vibration must be ensured
 while mounting the cameras.
  • Cameras must be pitched 16o -20o up from the ground. This range is to satisfy a trade-off between the
 following constraints:
 – avoid nearby low clouds that appear in one camera’s FoV but not the other
 – capture part of the horizon that may provide landmarks when necessary
 – have the area we are most interested in at the center of the pictures where distortion is minimum
 – increase the area of the sky that is being observed.
  • Typical separation between the cameras of a pair (baseline) is chosen to be about an order of
 magnitude shorter than the distance from the cameras to the objects being observed. This reflects a
 trade-off between a larger baseline that is required for more precise reconstruction, and the increased
 difficulty of identifying matching points with a larger baseline. For example, stereo camera pairs sited
 at the ARM Southern Great Plains (SGP) facilities form a ring to observe shallow cumulus clouds
 from approximately 6-km distance. Therefore, SGP stereo camera pairs have a 500-600-m baseline.
 The stereo camera pair installed at the Houston, Texas (HOU) Tracking Aerosol Convection
 Interactions Experiment (TRACER) site was to observe clouds that were at least 10 km away from
 the cameras. Therefore, the HOU stereo camera pair had a 1.2-km baseline.

3.0 Setup and Operation of Instrument
 Each stereo camera operates as part of a standalone, solar-powered unit. Two 145 W solar panels charge a
 deep-cycle battery, providing continuous power (Figure 1). For network access, a CradlePoint IBR
 600LPE cellular modem is used. A dedicated minicomputer manages data collection, storage, and power
 monitoring.

 Figure 1. One of the six cameras ringing the SGP Central Facility. Two solar panels are mounted with a
 top-of-pole rack, an electrical enclosure is placed beneath the solar panels, and the camera
 enclosure is pitched approximately 18 o up from the ground.

 The minicomputer instructs the camera to capture JPEG images at regular intervals of 20 seconds during
 the daytime and six minutes during the nighttime. It temporarily stores these images along with auxiliary
 data from the solar panels’ charge controller. Figure 2 displays an electrical enclosure that houses the
 charge controller (which regulates power to the system), cellular modem, and the minicomputer.

 3

 Figure 2. Interior of the electrical enclosure of a camera setup. A charge controller, a cellular modem, a
 minicomputer, and circuit breakers and fuses are enclosed in this box, which is placed
 beneath the solar panels as shown in Figure 1.

 Auxiliary data is collected from the charge controller through a meter bus with an RS232 interface.
 Images from both cameras of the stereo pair (all cameras in a stereo ring) must be time-synchronized
 within one second. A GPS puck is a part of the setup to ensure precise time synchronization. The
 minicomputer resets its time through the GPS puck every day at midnight.

 Ideally, the stereo cameras in a pair are oriented such that they face roughly perpendicular to the baseline
 (Figure 3). To maximize the common FoV, the yaw angle of the cameras are typically tilted 3°-5° inward
 from perpendicular to the baseline.

4.0 Data
 Each setup acquires a raw datastream consisting of JPEG images at a 2592x1944-pixel resolution. Images
 are captured at 20-second intervals during the day and every six minutes at night. Nighttime images are
 essential for extrinsic camera calibration using stellar objects (see Section 5). In addition to these images,
 auxiliary measurements summarizing the power system's status as listed in Table 2 are recorded hourly.
 To give a daily overview, a video is generated for each day, comprising daytime images sampled at
 two-minute intervals.

 An automatic cloud point matching and 3D cloud point reconstruction algorithm has been developed by
 the mentors (Romps and Öktem 2023). This algorithm generates the Point Cloud of Cloud Points (PCCP)
 Value-Added Product (VAP), which can be accessed from the ARM Data Center
 (http://dx.doi.org/10.5439/1531325). The PCCP VAP is a set of 3D positions representing the locations of
 cloud features in the sky. When synchronous PCCP data are available from multiple views surrounding a
 cloud, it becomes possible to extract the representation of the whole cloud surface. Building on the PCCP
 datastreams from the ARM SGP stereo camera ring (Figure 3), Clouds Optically Gridded by Stereo

(COGS) VAP is generated (Romps and Öktem 2018). COGS provides a four-dimensional (4D) map of
cloudiness and this data product can be accessed from the ARM Data Center
(https://doi.org/10.5439/1877293).

4.1 Data Object Description
Datastreams are named as follows:
 • xstereocamyz.YYYYMMDD.hhmmss.jpg, for the JPEG image files.
 • xstereocamymoviez.YYYYMMDD.hhmmss.mp4, for a movie generated from images at one-frame-
 per-two-minutes interval.
 • xstereocamyauxz.YYYYMMDD.hhmmss.nc, for the auxiliary power system status measurements
 (see Table 2).

“x” refers to the site name and is “sgp” for the SGP site, “cor” for the first ARM Mobile Facility (AMF1)
site for the Cloud, Aerosol, and Complex Terrain Interactions (CACTI) campaign in Córdoba, Argentina,
and “hou” for the TRACER AMF1 Houston, Texas site. “y” is either a or b, referring to one of the two
camera pairs. “z” refers to the facility name, “E43”, “E44”, “E45” for SGP extended facilities (EFs), “S4”
for COR and “S5” for HOU.

4.4 Data Quality
The image data quality is judged subjectively by the sharpness of images and metadata information
collected with the images. Environmental factors such as rain, sun (when in FoV), or insufficient daylight
may temporarily impact the data quality during the day depending on the season. In case of rainy weather
conditions, rain drops on the camera screen may distort images. Heavy rain or fog may result in complete
loss of visibility. Dust or debris accumulation on the camera enclosure screen is another factor that may
affect image quality.

5.0 Historical Background

 5.1 Deployment at the Southern Great Plains (SGP)
 Stereo camera pairs were initially deployed at ARM’s SGP extended facilities: E43, E44, and E45. These
 pairs started operation in August 2017. Each camera pair is positioned at approximately 120 degrees from
 the others, with a 17 o -19 o pitch angle from the ground, and at 5-6-km distance from the SGP Central
 Facility to cover the region from northeast, northwest, and southern views. This “ring” of three stereo
 pairs allows for combining their 3D reconstructions to generate a 3D mask of cloudiness from
 surrounding views.

 Note the following camera replacements at the SGP site:
  • On 8 December 2020 sgpstereocama at site E45 was replaced.
  • On 18 June 2021 sgpstereocamb at site E43 was replaced.
  • On 12 June 2025 sgpstereocamb at site E43 was replaced.

 Camera intrinsic parameters changed at each camera replacement (refer to Section 5 for details).

 7

 5.2 Deployment for the CACTI Campaign
 During the CACTI campaign, a stereo camera pair became a part of the AMF1 instrument set. The pair
 (corstereocam) was deployed at 1-km distance from the AMF1 M1 site in Córdoba, Argentina
 (Varble et al. 2021). corstereocama and corstereocamb were sited at 32.1274o S, 64.7139 o W and
 32.1185 oS, 64.7237 oW, respectively, and they operated from 1 October 2018 through 30 April 2019. The
 cameras had a 1.3-km baseline in between and were facing 22o (for corstereocama) and 25o (for
 corstereocamb) south from west.

 5.3 Deployment for the TRACER Campaign
 During the TRACER campaign, the AMF1 stereo camera pair (houstereocam) was deployed 12 km east
 of the AMF1 M1 site in Houston, Texas (Jensen et al. 2023). houstereocama and houstereocamb were
 sited at 29.6939o N, 94.9366 o W, and 29.6836 o N, 94.9356 o W, respectively, and they operated from
 1 October 2021 through 30 September 2022. The cameras had a 1.2-km baseline in between and were
 facing 12 o (for houstereocama) and 7 o (for houstereocamb) south from west.

6.0 Maintenance Plan
 Setups need to be checked periodically to identify problems by inspecting:
  • image videos for camera focus/heading/view change and for spider webs in front of the camera
 enclosure screen (daily)
  • battery monthly for cracked or bulging cases, and corroding terminals (monthly)
  • all wiring (annually)
  • enclosures for nesting insects (annually).

7.0 Calibration Plan
 Cameras of the stereo setups need to be configured for optimal visual quality and calibrated for stereo
 reconstruction. This is performed during initial deployment and then as required by the camera mentors.
 Further details on camera configuration and calibration requirements are provided below.

 7.1 Camera Configuration for Visual Quality
 To ensure optimal visual quality, each camera lens is manually adjusted to focus on distant objects. Focus
 adjustment is performed after the FoV of each (varifocal) lens is set to 65o-75o . Once adjusted, zoom and
 focus are expected to stay stable over time. However, they must be readjusted if either the camera or the
 lens is replaced.

 At this 65 o -75 o field of view, lens distortion becomes highly noticeable in the captured images. Images are
 stored as captured, i.e., without any lens distortion correction applied. Table 3 lists intrinsic camera
 parameters, which can be used with software tools such as OpenCV (Bradski and Kaehler 2008) to
 correct for lens distortion. The intrinsic camera parameters are obtained through intrinsic camera

 8

calibration by use of a checkerboard pattern (Bradski and Kaehler 2008, Hartley and Zisserman 2004).
These parameters are tied to the specific zoom/focus settings. Therefore, intrinsic camera calibration
needs to be updated only if a camera is changed, or its zoom/focus is readjusted.

7.2 Camera Calibration for Stereo Reconstruction
For stereo processing, each stereo camera pair has to be stereo calibrated, i.e., extrinsic camera parameters
have to be identified (Hartley and Zisserman 2004, Romps 2024, Öktem et al. 2014). A stereo camera
calibration script is developed by the mentors for this purpose. The script makes use of nighttime camera
images and the position of bright stellar objects (that can be identified in the captured images) to
determine a camera’s orientation accurately. Table 4 lists the extrinsic camera parameters for the initial
deployment of the SGP stereo cameras, where the focus and principal point parameters are specified for
distortion-corrected images.

Extrinsic camera parameters require updating whenever the camera positions or headings change, in
addition to the times when the camera or the lens are replaced or readjusted. After the initial deployment,
incidents like a heavy bird landing on a camera, a vehicle striking the camera, or ground softening due to
heavy rain altered the camera's orientation several times. Following each such incident, the camera's
orientation was readjusted to maintain its initially set azimuth and pitch angles within a ±2 degree
accuracy. Every such readjustment necessitates a new extrinsic camera calibration for stereo processing.

8.0 User Notes and Known Problems
 See Section 2.4 on data quality.

 Beyond the issues listed in Section 2.4, external factors such as heavy bird landing on the camera, vehicle
 striking the camera, or ground softening from heavy rain have altered the camera orientation at irregular
 times. These incidents significantly shifted the camera's FoV until it was manually readjusted by the field
 operations team. Consequently, the PCCP VAP is not produced during any periods when the camera's
 FoV is out of adjustment.
