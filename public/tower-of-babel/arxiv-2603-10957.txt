Abstract
. Stone duality is an indispensable tool for the study of compact, zero-dimensional, Hausdorff spaces. In the case of general compact Hausdorff spaces one can get quite a bit of mileage by considering the ‘Wallman duality’ between compact spaces and lattices of closed sets. I will argue that the Gelfand–Naimark duality between compact Hausdorff spaces and unital, commutative C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebras provides great insight into compact Hausdorff spaces, and Čech–Stone remainders and their autohomeomorphisms in particular.



Let me start by stating that there is nothing new here. Perhaps it is a mere coincidence that a 1960 classic on (among other things) Čech–Stone compactifications is called ‘Rings of continuous functions’ (
[
33
]
), while the series of four papers some two decades prior by Murray and von Neumann introduced operator algebras (now known as von Neumann algebras) under the name of ‘Rings of operators’ (
[
41
]
and references thereof).
Some readers may object that every theorem about compact Hausdorff spaces proved by using the Gelfand–Naimark duality can be also proved by using Wallman duality. Yes, but all of those theorems can also be proved from the scratch, by using definitions only. The insight provided by the Gelfand–Naimark duality is, in my opinion, well worth the trouble.

1.1.
Stone duality

To a compact, Hausdorff, zero-dimensional space
X
one associates
\Clop(X)
, the Boolean algebra of its clopen sets. If
f\colon X\to Y
is a continuous function between compact Hausdorff spaces, then the function
f_{*}\colon\Clop(Y)\to\Clop(X)
defined by

f_{*}(U)=f^{-1}(U)

is a Boolean algebra homomorphism.

Theorem 1.1
.

The functor
X\mapsto\Clop(X)
is an equivalence of categories of compact, Hausdorff, zero-dimensional spaces and Boolean algebras.
This functor is contravariant. More precisely, we have the following for every continuous function
f\colon X\to Y
between compact, Hausdorff, zero-dimensional spaces.

(1)

f
is surjective if and only if
f_{*}
is injective.

(2)

f
is injective if and only if
f_{*}
is surjective. ∎

This duality is very useful in the study of
\beta{\mathbb{N}}\setminus{\mathbb{N}}
and Čech–Stone remainders of other zero-dimensional spaces (see
[

]
,
[

,

In the category of all compact Hausdorff spaces, the ‘Stone functor’ is forgetful—e.g., all continua are sent to the trivial two-element Boolean algebra. The lattice of closed sets serves as a surprisingly good replacement in the general case (e.g.,

This is

Given a commutative, unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra

is closed in the weak*-topology (e.g., if

has a weak*-open neighbourhood consisting of functionals that satisfy the same inequality). Therefore

is compact with respect to the weak*-topology.

To prove that

is an equivalence of categories, we need to verify that every *-homomorphism

is of the form

(see (
1.1

Every

Now assume

The fact that if

If

is surjective, then

is injective because continuous functions on

∎

-algebras is countable, the space of all sentences is separable, and we can choose an infinite
I\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
such that for every sentence
\varphi
the limit
\lim_{n\in I,n\to\infty}\varphi^{C(X_{n})}
exists. This limit clearly does not change when passing to a further infinite
J\subseteq I
.
By a corollary to Ghasemi’s Feferman–Vaught theorem (
[
32
]
,
[
24
, Theorem 16.6.3]
), the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras

\displaystyle C(\beta X_{I}\setminus X_{I})

\displaystyle=\textstyle\prod_{n\in I}C(X_{n})/\sum_{n\in I}C(X_{n})\text{ and }

\displaystyle C(\beta X_{J}\setminus X_{J})

\displaystyle=\textstyle\prod_{n\in J}C(X_{n})/\sum_{n\in J}C(X_{n})

are elementarily equivalent. Since they are both countably saturated, CH implies that they are isomorphic, and by the Gelfand–Naimark duality
X_{I}
and
X_{J}
have homeomorphic remainders.
∎

The Čech–Stone remainder of the half-line
\mathbb{H}=[0,\infty)
is a well-studied continuum (
[
35
]
).
The Continuum Hypothesis implies that
\beta\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{H}
has
2^{\aleph_{1}}
nontrivial autohomeomorphisms (
[
35
, §9]
,
[
57
]
).

Theorem 3.4
.

The
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
C(\beta\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{H})
is countably saturated. In particular, CH implies that it has
2^{\aleph_{1}}
nontrivial autohomeomorphisms.

Proof.

Countable saturation follows by a special case of
[
29
, Theorem 2.5]
: take
X_{n}=[0,n]
; then
\mathbb{H}=\bigcup_{n}X_{n}
and
\partial X_{n}
is a singleton for all
n
. As before, CH implies that
C(\beta\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{H})
, as a saturated model, has
2^{\aleph_{1}}
automorphisms and the conclusion follows by Theorem
1.7
.
∎

Countable saturation can get us slightly further.
By
[
29
, Theorem 2.5]
, if
X
is a locally compact, non-compact Polish space that can be presented as an increasing union of compact subspaces
X_{n}
such that (denoting the topological boundary of
X_{n}
by
\partial X_{n}
)
\sup_{n}|\partial X_{n}|<\infty
, then
C(\beta X\setminus X)
is countably saturated.
[
29
, Theorem 2.2]
suggests that this condition on
X
may be necessary for countable saturation of
C(\beta X\setminus X)
.

Is there a pair of connected (locally compact, non-compact, Polish) spaces
X
and
Y
such that the assertion that
\beta X\setminus X
and
\beta Y\setminus Y
are homeomorphic is independent from ZFC? In order to have these remainders homeomorphic for nontrivial reason, one would have to prove that the associated
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras are elementarily equivalent, and at present we do not have an efficient way of computing the theory of a connected space. One route to this would be via an extension of the Feferman–Vaught theorem; such an extension would be of great interest in its own right.

If
X
and
Y
satisfy the assumptions of
[
29
, Theorem 2.5]
, then
a simpler route could use a combination of Ghasemi’s trick with the methods of
[
15
, §3]
, where the Čech–Stone remainder of a continuum
X
is considered as a continuous image of the Čech–Stone remainder of the space
[0,1]\times{\mathbb{N}}
(known as
\mathbb{M}
).

3.1.1.
Nontrivial autohomeomorphisms without countable saturation.

If
X
can be presented as an increasing union of compact subspaces, then
C(\beta X\setminus X)
satisfies a weak form of countable saturation called
countable degree-1 saturation
(see
[
24
, §15]
). While fairly useful for analyzing properties of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, this property does not provide a sufficient amount of interesting information about Čech–Stone remainders to justify introducing it here.

The following inconvenient fact is
[
30
, Corollary 2]
(see also Theorem 1 of the same paper for more bad news).

Theorem 3.5
.

If
\mathbb{R}^{2}
embeds as a closed subspace into
X
, then
C(\beta X\setminus X)
is not countably saturated. ∎

Nevertheless,
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras provide a good vantage point for analyzing autohomeomorphisms of Čech–Stone remainders of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 2
and other locally compact, non-compact manifolds.
The following is
[
53
, Theorem 1]
.

Theorem 3.6
.

If
X
is a locally compact, metrizable, non-compact manifold, then CH implies that
\beta X\setminus X
has
2^{\aleph_{1}}
autohomeomorphisms. ∎

The proof uses a clever stratification of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras associated to coronas of the so-called ‘flexible’ spaces (see
[
53
, Definition 2.1]
).

3.2.
Forcing axioms

Considerably more interesting (in the author’s opinion) and deeper (absolutely) than the trivializing effect of CH on Čech–Stone remainders and other quotients is the fact that under forcing axioms, coronas of locally compact, non-compact, Polish spaces have only trivial autohomeomorphisms.
In the case of zero-dimensional spaces this is a result of Shelah (
[
45
]
) asserting that all automorphisms of
\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{N}})/\Fin
are (consistently with ZFC) trivial, as adapted to the context of forcing axioms (in decreasing order of strength) in
[
46
,
52
,
12
]
.

The following ultimate commutative generalization of these results is taken from
[
54
, Theorem C]
(
\OCAT
is Todorcevic’s version of the Open Colouring Axiom,
[
48
]
, and
\MA
is Martin’s Axiom,
[
40
]
; see Definition
3.1
for (non)trivial autohomeomorphisms).

Theorem 3.7
.

\OCAT
and
\MA
imply that for all second-countable, locally compact, non-compact spaces
X
and
Y
, every homeomorphism between
\beta X\setminus X
and
\beta Y\setminus Y
is trivial. ∎

In addition to forcing axioms, the proof of Theorem
3.7
relies on Ulam-stability of approximate *-homomorphisms between commutative
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras proved in
[
44
]
.

To give an illustration of these results, for an infinite
I\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
let

\textstyle Z_{I}=\bigsqcup_{n\in I}[0,1]^{n}.

Corollary 3.8
.

There is an infinite set
I\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
such that for every infinite
J\subseteq I
the assertion that
Z_{I}
and
Z_{J}
have homeomorphic Čech–Stone remainders is independent from ZFC.

Proof.

By Ghasemi’s trick (Theorem
3.3
), there exists an infinite
I\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
such that for every infinite
J\subseteq I
, CH implies that
\beta Z_{I}\setminus Z_{I}
and
\beta Z_{J}\setminus Z_{J}
are homeomorphic.
By Theorem
3.7
and elementary dimension theory,
\OCAT
and
\MA
imply that these spaces are homeomorphic if and only if
I\setminus J
is finite.
∎

See
[
26
]
for more on rigidity results along these lines.
It is known to suffice for the analogous conclusion in the case of the Calkin algebra (
[
23
]
,
[
24
, §17]
).

3.3.
ZFC

The following is the main result of
[
16
]
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Every continuum of weight not greater than
\aleph_{1}
is a continuous image of
\beta\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{H}
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a continuum of weight
\leq\aleph_{1}
.
By Proposition
2.1
,
C(X)
and

have the same universal theory.

Since

is countably saturated by Theorem

by Proposition

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra

with the same universal theory as

By the Löwenheim–Skolem theorem (
[
24

such that

is elementarily equivalent to

If CH holds, then

is isomorphic to the ultrapower of

associated with any nonprincipal ultrafilter

on

4.
Reflection

Elementary submodels are routinely used to prove reflection results, often in conjunction with nontrivial set-theoretic assumptions.
With Gelfand–Naimark duality this is possible, but rather subtle for more than one reason.

Before delving into this section, the reader should be warned that results along similar lines had been obtained without using
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras in for example
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,

[

\mathrm{C}^{*}

I will assume that the reader is familiar with the structure
H_{\theta}
of all sets whose transitive closures have cardinality

Suppose that

Suppose that

(and not even

is closed under

[

for a more detailed analysis of the relation between

and

On the brighter side, the set

is a club (closed unbounded set)

5.
An open-ended concluding section

The
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
C(2^{\mathbb{N}})
is injectively universal in the class of separable, unital, commutative,
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras; this is just a reformulation of the familiar fact that the Cantor space is surjectively universal in the class of compact metrizable spaces, using Lemma
1.8
.
On the other hand, the class of compact metrizable continua has no surjectively universal object (
[
56
]
), and therefore there is no injectively universal unital, abelian,
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra without nontrivial projections.

One often needs to analyze not just a single compact Hausdorff space but a continuous function
f\colon X\to Y
between such spaces. For such analysis using elementary submodels of
H_{\theta}
(as in §
4
) seee.g.,
[
21
]
and
[
3
]
. By Theorem
1.7
, such triple
f\colon X\to Y
corresponds to a triple
(C(X),C(Y),f_{*})
of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras and a *-homomorphism between them. The class of such triples is easily shown to be axiomatizable.

One interesting situation is the following. Using the notation from §
1.3.2
, given compact metrizable spaces
X_{n}
, for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and a nonprincipal ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
on
{\mathbb{N}}
, the space
\sum_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}
is a subspace of
\sum_{\Fin}X_{n}
(where
\Fin
is the Fréchet filter). Equivalently,
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}C(X_{n})
is a quotient of
\prod_{\Fin}C(X_{n})
. Theorem
5.1
below is (modulo Theorem
1.7
) a special case of
[
25
, Theorems E and C]
, minus the notation (
2^{{\mathbb{N}}}
stands for the Cantor space).

Recall that
\beta(X\times{\mathbb{N}})\setminus(X\times{\mathbb{N}})
is homeomorphic to
\sum_{\Fin}X
(Lemma
1.18
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
X
is a compact Hausdorff space and
{\mathcal{U}}
is a nonprincipal ultrafilter on
{\mathbb{N}}
. Then the following holds.

(1)

\sum_{\Fin}X
is coelementarily equivalent to
\sum_{\mathcal{U}}(X\times 2^{{\mathbb{N}}})
.

(2)

If CH holds and
X
is metrizable, then
\sum_{\Fin}X
and
\sum_{\mathcal{U}}(X\times 2^{{\mathbb{N}}})
are homeomorphic.

(3)

If CH holds and
X
is metrizable, then the natural copy of
\sum_{\mathcal{U}}X
inside
\sum_{\Fin}X
is a retract if and only if
{\mathcal{U}}
is a P-point.

Proof.

In
[
25
]
the notation
C(X)^{\infty}
,
C(X)^{\mathcal{U}}
is used for
\prod_{\Fin}C(X)
and
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}C(X)
, respectively.

In
[
25
, §3]
, the functor
K
is defined by
K(C(X))=C(X\otimes 2^{{\mathbb{N}}})
. Thus
[
25
, Proposition 3.5]
implies (
1
) and
[
25
, Theorem E]
gives (
2
).
Finally, there is a *-homomorphism
\Psi\colon\prod_{\mathcal{U}}C(X)\to\prod_{\Fin}C(X)
such that the quotient map
\pi_{\mathcal{U}}\colon\prod_{\Fin}C(X)\to\prod_{\mathcal{U}}C(X)
satisfies
\Psi\circ\pi_{\mathcal{U}}=\id_{\prod_{\Fin}}C(X)
(
[
25
, Theorem C]
). This, together with Theorem
1.7
, gives (
3
).
∎
