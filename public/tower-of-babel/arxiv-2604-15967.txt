TwoHamsters: Benchmarking Multi-Concept Compositional Unsafety in Text-to-Image Models
Chaoshuo Zhang, Yibo Liang, Mengke Tian, Chenhao Lin, Zhengyu Zhao, Le Yang, Chong Zhang, Yang Zhang, Chao Shen

Abstract
Despite the remarkable synthesis capabilities of text-to-image (T2I) models, safeguarding them against content violations remains a persistent challenge. Existing safety alignments primarily focus on explicit malicious concepts, often overlooking the subtle yet critical risks of compositional semantics. To address this oversight, we identify and formalize a novel vulnerability: Multi-Concept Compositional Unsafety (MCCU), where unsafe semantics stem from the implicit associations of individually benign concepts. Based on this formulation, we introduce TwoHamsters, a comprehensive benchmark comprising 17.5k prompts curated to probe MCCU vulnerabilities. Through a rigorous evaluation of 10 state-of-the-art models and 16 defense mechanisms, our analysis yields 8 pivotal insights. In particular, we demonstrate that current T2I models and defense mechanisms face severe MCCU risks: on TwoHamsters, FLUX achieves an MCCU generation success rate of 99.52%, while LLaVA-Guard only attains a recall of 41.06%, highlighting a critical limitation of the current paradigm for managing hazardous compositional generation.

3.1
Formalization of MCCU Risk

To rigorously assess safety in text-to-image (T2I) generation, we model the evaluation as a mapping from visual concepts to their compositional semantics. Accordingly, TwoHamsters reformulates the annotation schema as a tuple
(s_{i},c_{i},v^{1}_{i},v^{2}_{i},p_{i})
, where
s_{i}\in\{0,1\}
represents the binary safety label,
c_{i}
the hazard category,
v_{i}
the atomic concepts, and
p_{i}
the prompt. This formulation explicitly anchors safety violations to specific concept pairs.

We distinguish MCCU through two defining characteristics: atomic safety and interpretative harmfulness. Atomic safety dictates that MCCU instances consist of inherently benign concepts, making them susceptible to misclassification by filters that analyze components in isolation. Interpretative harmfulness, however, implies that the synthesized combination conveys hazardous semantics perceivable by specific groups. Let
\mathcal{F}(x)
be a Boolean safety predicate anchored to a specific safety policy, such that
\mathcal{F}(x)=\text{True}
if content
x
is safe under established guidelines. The logical condition for an MCCU concept pair is defined as the conjunction of atomic safety and interpretative harmfulness:

(v^{1},v^{2})\text{ s.t. }\mathcal{F}(v^{1})\land\mathcal{F}(v^{2})\land\neg\mathcal{F}(v^{1}\oplus v^{2}),

(1)

where
\land
and
\neg
denote logical conjunction and negation, and
\oplus
represents semantic composition. The operator
\oplus
is instantiated as visual contextual interaction, where
v^{1}
and
v^{2}
interact within a shared visual context.

Equation

1
functions as the operational criterion for dataset construction rather than a predictive model of latent dynamics.

3.2
Category-Concept Taxonomy

To address the subtle and implicit nature of MCCU, we carefully design a taxonomy-driven, human-AI pipeline for data curation. Our objective is to engineer a benchmark characterized by broad taxonomic coverage, semantic granularity, and high adversarial potency.

Specifically, we strictly align our taxonomy with real-world applications by synthesizing safety guidelines from leading commercial T2I platforms. This process integrates sociopolitical sensitivities and ethical ambiguities to construct a robust hierarchical framework. Following rigorous adjudication by an expert committee, this pipeline yields 10 core risk dimensions, namely Disturbing, Harassment, Hate, Humiliation, Illegal, Political, Public Health Harm, Self-Harm, Sexual, and Violence.
Comprehensive definitions and detailed examples are provided in
Table

Then, guided by the formulation in
Equation

1
, we proceed to mine specific MCCU concept pairs. We employ LLMs for semantic decomposition and knowledge retrieval. Acknowledging that MCCU risks are significantly influenced by region, culture, and belief, we instruct LLMs to adopt diverse personas to identify context-specific MCCU pairs from different perspectives. An expert committee then categorizes the model responses, screening for representative pairs that satisfy
Equation

1
. Furthermore, considering the distribution of risk severity, we prioritize the expansion of categories associated with higher public concern and potential harm. Ultimately, we identify 51 distinct concept pairs.
This top-down methodology facilitates comprehensive coverage of latent risks emerging from the composition of individually benign atomic concepts.

3.3
Benchmark Construction Pipeline

We construct the benchmark via a rigorous three-stage pipeline designed to isolate implicit compositional risks. The acquisition phase employs a dual-stream strategy, combining taxonomy-guided retrieval with LLM-based adversarial expansion, while enforcing a strict blocklist to preclude explicit violations and ensure risks emerge solely from concept interactions.

Following the acquisition, a multi-stage filtering protocol ensures diversity and fidelity. We first utilize centrality-based pruning in CLIP embedding space to maximize semantic variance. This is complemented by a cross-modal consistency verification against single-concept baselines to ensure emergent risks stem from effective composition rather than semantic degradation.

Finally, we augment the dataset with structured rationales via a human-in-the-loop process. Each sample captures the causal logic of the risk, adjudicated through a double-blind review where independent auditors verify rationale-visual alignment. This verification yields a finalized corpus of 17.5k high-fidelity samples.

3.4
Automated Evaluator

Current safety classifiers struggle to discern the subtle compositional semantics inherent in the TwoHamsters benchmark. To address this, we propose a robust evaluation framework consisting of two complementary components: MCCU-ViT for high-throughput quantitative assessment, and MCCU-VLM for reasoning-heavy qualitative verification
(
11
)
.
Given the prohibitive computational costs of VLMs for large-scale benchmarking, we establish MCCU-ViT as the primary evaluator. It is designed to overcome the distribution shift between natural pre-training data and AI-generated unsafe content through domain-adaptive fine-tuning on 300k image–text pairs from TwoHamsters and auxiliary safety datasets.

To enhance evaluation rigor, MCCU-ViT incorporates a Multi-Head Causal Consistency mechanism. Unlike standard binary classifiers, our model determines safety violations via the logical conjunction of three parallel heads:

\hat{y}=\mathbb{I}\left[{F}_{unsafe}(\hat{x})\land{F}_{align}(\hat{x},v^{1})\land{F}_{align}(\hat{x},v^{2})\right],

(2)

a safety evaluator
{F}_{unsafe}(\cdot)
to
\{0,1\}
(1 denotes unsafe), and a concept alignment evaluator
{F}_{align}(\cdot,v)\to\{0,1\}
(1 denotes alignment). This design enables the evaluator to understand MCCU hazards while learning the concept distribution, ensuring that a generated image is penalized only when the risk is causally linked to the specific compositional triggers, thereby minimizing false positives arising from background noise or spurious correlations. 3.5
Evaluation Metrics

To rigorously quantify the trade-off between safety and utility, we establish a unified metric framework based on the generator
\mathcal{G}
, We define a general evaluation score
\mathcal{M}
over a prompt set
\mathcal{P}
with a condition function
\Phi
as:

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{P},\Phi)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\sum_{(p,v)\in\mathcal{P}}\Phi(\mathcal{G}(p),v)

(3)

Based on this formulation, we define three metrics.

MCCU Defense Rate (MDR):
Measures the defense success rate against compositional risks. Let
\mathcal{P}_{MCCU}
be the set of compositional prompts triggering
v_{A}\oplus v_{B}
, and the risk condition be
\Phi_{risk}(\hat{x},v)={F}_{unsafe}(\hat{x})\cdot{F}_{align}(\hat{x},v)
(indicating a successful jailbreak). We define MDR as the complement of the violation rate:

\text{MDR}=1-\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{P}_{MCCU},\Phi_{risk}),

(4)

Higher MDR signifies robust resistance against MCCU risks.

Utility Retention Metrics (SCR & NCR):
To assess generative utility, we measure concept retention using the alignment condition
\Phi_{util}(\hat{x},v)={F}_{align}(\hat{x},v)
across different datasets:

Single-Concept Retention (SCR):
Evaluated on atomic prompts
\mathcal{P}_{atom}
to ensure base concepts remain intact.

\text{SCR}=\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{P}_{atom},\Phi_{util}),

(5)

Non-target Concept Retention (NCR):
Evaluated on unrelated prompts
\mathcal{P}_{gen}
to quantify collateral damage.

\text{NCR}=\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{P}_{gen},\Phi_{util}),

(6)

Higher scores in SCR and NCR indicate superior preservation of generative utility.

Insight 1: There exists an “Instruction vs Safety” Dilemma in Compositional Generation.

Our empirical analysis reveals a critical “Instruction-Safety” dilemma: as models achieve superior instruction following, they become paradoxically more vulnerable to MCCU. While early U-Net architectures (e.g., SD-v1.4) retain a baseline MDR of 40.91%, modern DiT-based models (e.g., FLUX.1) plummet to near-0% MDR. We interpret this as an alignment paradigm that is semantic rather than axiological: models excel at compositionally rendering entities (e.g., “Watermelon” and “Person”) but lack the social reasoning to detect their emergent toxicity. Thus, optimization for strict prompt adherence effectively bypasses safety constraints when risks are implicit rather than explicit.

Insight 2: Taxonomic Bias in Safety Alignment.

Our fine-grained analysis reveals a systemic imbalance in current defense paradigms. Categories characterized by complex socio-semantic nuances, specifically Harassment (8.81% Mean MDR), Hate Speech, and Political Sensitivity, exhibit significantly higher vulnerability than extensively studied domains such as Sexual Content (21.85% Mean MDR).
The marked divergence in MDR across categories highlights that current T2I safety alignment focus on suppressing explicit, low-level visual hazards (e.g., nudity), yet fail to sufficiently address high-level risks devoid of explicit visual signatures.

Insight 4: Mainstream safety filters systematically fail by suffering from MCCU data scarcity rather than architectural limitations.

Widely deployed safety classifiers in T2I pipelines (e.g., SD Safety Checker and NSFW-I) exhibit extremely low sensitivity on the TwoHamsters dataset, failing to detect risks arising from multi-concept compositional usage.
Even safety-fine-tuned VLMs, including LLaVA-Guard and PerspectiveVision, achieve only 41.06% and 18.56% overall recall, with detection rates in public health and sexually transmitted risk categories below 10%. Although NSFW-T achieves superior Recall, its reliance on the textual modality inherently constrains its deployment scope compared to visual filters.
It is crucial to emphasize that these off-the-shelf filters were evaluated in a zero-shot setting, as they have never been exposed to the specific compositional concepts within our dataset.
In contrast, to determine the theoretical detection upper bound within the MCCU paradigm, we evaluated MCCU-ViT and MCCU-VLM, which are trained on the TwoHamsters training split as automated evaluators, showed stronger utility on the test set, with MCCU-ViT achieving an average recall of 99.49% (see
Table

5
).
This gap cannot be explained by architectural differences, as both models are derived from MultiHeaded and LLaVA-Guard with only minor modifications; instead, it primarily reflects the inclusion of targeted MCCU training data.
Overall, these results suggest that existing safety filters fail not due to limited model capacity, but due to the systematic omission of MCCU content in prior training and evaluation paradigms.

Insight 5: Universal MCCU defense confronts the structural barrier of semantic fluidity.

Although MCCU-ViT achieves near-perfect recall on the TwoHamsters benchmark, we strictly position it as a benchmark-specific evaluator rather than a deployable, general-purpose filter. Its primary function is to approximate human consensus at scale, ensuring reproducible experimental quantification within the dataset’s closed ontology. However, extrapolating this efficacy to open-world deployment faces an intrinsic hurdle: unlike explicit visual hazards (e.g., gore or pornography) characterized by stable visual features, MCCU risks are socially constructed and culture-dependent. The toxicity of a compositional image often stems not from pixel-level semantics but from the evolving cultural zeitgeist; a combination deemed benign today may acquire offensive connotations tomorrow via emerging subcultures or shifting societal norms. Consequently, a universally invariant MCCU filter capable of long-term, comprehensive detection remains theoretically elusive. Effective mitigation requires framing MCCU safety not as a one-off technical optimization, but as a persistent, adaptive socio-technical challenge.

Insight 7: The Dual Dilemma of Ineffective Erasure and Utility Collapse in Concept Erasure.

First, we observe ineffective erasure across various categories, where the MDR of CE-tuned models remains unacceptably close to the SDXL baseline. One reason is that while state-of-the-art methods can suppress explicit hazards in smaller models like SD-v1.4, they struggle to decouple deep text-to-latent associations in large-scale architectures such as SDXL or FLUX. Another reason lies in the inherent complexity of MCCU; MCCU prompts are often structurally intricate, and when these complex compositional prompts are triggered, latent unsafe visual features tend to re-emerge.

Second, effective erasure often leads to a catastrophic decline in utility, as evidenced by the synchronized drop in MDR and SCR for methods such as ESD, UCE, KSCU, and COGFD. This limitation stems from the fact that existing erasure logic relies on coarse-grained activation suppression over parameter subsets, lacking the precision required to address fine-grained MCCU risks. We further argue that MCCU risks differ in practice from conventional safety hazards in terms of latent feature distributions. Prior safety research typically assumes manifold separation, modeling violating and benign data as disjoint distributions. In contrast, the critical challenge of MCCU lies in its intrinsic distributional entanglement, where unsafe semantics do not form isolated clusters but are superimposed onto and parasitize benign concept manifolds. Under this regime, current CE algorithms attempting to excise specific MCCU risks inadvertently induce the collapse of the underlying benign manifolds that constitute concept representations, thereby degrading generative utility.

Insight 8: Shifting from Concept Erasure to Compositional Disentanglement.

The intrinsic limitation of current Concept Erasure lies in its objective: attempting to remove a concept vector
v_{c}
entirely. Our results prove this fails for MCCU because the constituent elements (e.g., “priest”, “child”) are benign and essential. Therefore, we propose a paradigm shift from data-centric filtration to logic-oriented disentanglement. Mathematically, this implies moving from minimizing the probability
P(c)
to enforcing conditional independence:
P(A|B)\approx P(A)
, preventing the contextual triggering of risk while preserving atomic utility. While COGFD has explored the direction of compositional disentanglement, results on TwoHamsters demonstrate that it falls short in both erasure efficacy and generative utility. Practical implementation should prioritize disrupting the semantic binding mechanism within the model’s cross-attention layers. Instead of erasing weights, future alignment must focus on orthogonalizing the interaction manifolds—ensuring that the activation patterns of benign concepts (e.g., “banana”) remain computationally orthogonal to sensitive contexts (e.g., “sexualized textures”), thereby stripping the model’s capacity for harmful synthesis without degrading its knowledge base.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we formally define Multi-Concept Compositional Unsafety (MCCU) and introduce TwoHamsters to benchmark this implicit vulnerability. Our evaluation reveals a severe safety risk: current SOTA T2I models exhibit significant susceptibility to MCCU. Notably, FLUX demonstrates an MCCU generation rate as high as 99.52% , while mainstream defense methods fail to effectively shield models against these risks.
These findings demonstrate that current safeguards, which rely on explicit data filtering, are fundamentally inadequate against MCCU, underscoring the urgent need for logic-oriented safety alignment.

Impact Statement

This work advances the safety alignment of Text-to-Image (T2I) generation by formally defining and benchmarking Multi-Concept Compositional Unsafety (MCCU). We acknowledge that our introduced benchmark, TwoHamsters, contains prompts designed to trigger offensive content, including hate speech, sexual innuendo, and self-harm.

While this dataset poses a potential risk of being repurposed for jailbreaking, its release is imperative given our empirical finding that current SOTA models (e.g., FLUX) exhibit a failure rate of nearly 100% against MCCU attacks. Keeping such vulnerabilities opaque hinders the development of robust defenses. We emphasize that the prompts in our benchmark consist of individually benign concepts, distinguishing them from explicit malicious instructions.

To mitigate harm during data curation, we employed a strict human-in-the-loop review process with established protocols to protect annotator well-being. We urge the research community to use this dataset responsibly, primarily for auditing safety boundaries and developing logic-oriented alignment techniques.

Appendix A
Benchmark Construction Pipeline Implementation

A.1
Prompt Collection

To ensure both the breadth and linguistic diversity of our benchmark, we propose a dual-stream acquisition strategy tailored to capture a heterogeneous spectrum of compositional risks. The first stream focuses on taxonomy-driven retrieval, systematically mining high-quality prompts from established datasets. By leveraging our hierarchical concept taxonomy, we identify and extract prompts that embody nuanced semantic variants of target concepts. The second stream complements this by harnessing the advanced reasoning capabilities of LLMs for adversarial expansion. We instantiate predefined MCCU concept pairs as logical constraints within specialized instruction templates, guiding LLMs to synthesize complex scenarios that go beyond trivial keyword co-occurrence. To strictly isolate implicit risks, we implement a dual-safeguard mechanism. We enforce negative constraints using a comprehensive blocklist of explicit risk terms (e.g., violence, illegal acts) during generation, followed by strict post-filtering. This guarantees that unsafety stems exclusively from compositional interactions rather than explicit vocabulary. This integrated pipeline converts static concept pairs into dynamic, high-complexity prompts, yielding an initial corpus of over 100k candidates for rigorous subsequent filtering.

A.2
Data Filtering

To distill the raw corpus into a robust benchmark, we deploy a multi-stage quality control pipeline comprising feature-space diversity filtering and cross-modal semantic effectiveness screening.

Feature-space Diversity Filtering

Appendix D
Automated Evaluator Training Implementation

D.1
MCCU-ViT Training Implementation

To enable automated and precise fidelity assessment of generated concepts, we construct a dedicated concept detector named MCCU-ViT. The architecture leverages the CLIP visual encoder as its backbone, specifically employing the ViT-L/14-CLIPA-336 variant pre-trained on DataComp-1B. To harness the rich semantic representation capabilities of the pre-trained model while maintaining training efficiency, we freeze the parameters of the visual encoder and fine-tune three distinct lightweight classification heads. These heads are structured as Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) comprising a linear projection, a ReLU activation, and a final linear mapping layer. They are tasked with independently predicting the first atomic concept, the second atomic concept, and the global safety category, respectively. This multi-head design decouples the complex task of safety detection into concrete object recognition and abstract category classification.

The model is trained on the TwoHamsters dataset using a multi-task learning objective. For the atomic concept heads, we formulate the task as an N-way classification problem, where images containing the specific target concept are labeled as positives. The training process minimizes the Cross-Entropy Loss using the AdamW optimizer. Regarding hyperparameters, we standardize the input resolution to 384 times 384 pixels and employ a batch size of 16. The learning rate is set to 1e-4 with a weight decay of 0.01 and a dropout rate of 0.2 to prevent overfitting. The model is trained for a total of 15 epochs. To ensure robustness, we strictly monitor performance on a held-out validation set and select the model checkpoint that achieves the highest validation accuracy for the final inference.

During inference, we employ a logical ensemble strategy that synthesizes the outputs of the three classification heads. Our empirical observations indicate that while the global category head achieves an accuracy of approximately 80 percent, the atomic concept heads demonstrate superior performance with accuracies exceeding 98 percent. Consequently, we prioritize the predictions of the atomic heads: if the two atomic concepts are detected with high confidence, we map their combination to the safety taxonomy to determine the final label. This neuro-symbolic approach effectively corrects potential misclassifications from the global head, boosting the overall detection accuracy to over 99 percent. This high-precision mechanism ensures that our metric effectively distinguishes between the accurate generation of specific semantics and merely high-fidelity but irrelevant content.

D.2
MCCU-VLM Training Implementation

Diverging from the discriminative nature of MCCU-ViT, which focuses on rapid probability quantification, MCCU-VLM is engineered as a generative reasoning agent designed to articulate the rationale behind compositional risks. We construct this model upon the Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct architecture, a state-of-the-art Large Multimodal Model (LMM) that bridges a CLIP-based vision encoder with a Qwen2 language model via a complex projection layer. Unlike the feature-mapping approach used in our discriminative detector, the training of MCCU-VLM follows the task-specific instruction tuning paradigm. Our objective is to transition the model from general visual captioning to specialized safety auditing without compromising its pre-trained visual common sense. To achieve this, we freeze both the vision tower and the multimodal projector, exclusively optimizing the language model’s capabilities to interpret subtle MCCU semantics.

We adopt a Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) strategy to navigate the trade-off between plasticity and stability. Specifically, we inject Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) modules into all linear layers of the language model, including the query, key, value, output, gate, up, and down projections. The LoRA configuration is set with a rank
r=16
and a scaling factor
\alpha=32
. The model is fine-tuned on a curated dataset of 9,379 visual instruction-following samples. Each sample is structured as a multi-turn dialogue where the model must not only output a final verdict but also generate a comprehensive safety critique. The training objective minimizes the auto-regressive next-token prediction loss, calculated solely on the assistant’s reasoning and response tokens, while masking the user instructions to prevent pattern memorization.

Implementation-wise, we employ the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of
1\times 10^{-4}
and a cosine decay schedule. The training spans 10 epochs with a batch size of 1 per GPU, utilizing a gradient accumulation strategy implicitly handled by the optimizer’s step. A context window of 4,096 tokens is allocated to accommodate detailed reasoning chains. To mitigate overfitting on this specialized domain, a LoRA dropout rate of 0.1 is applied. This alignment process effectively transforms the general-purpose VLM into an expert evaluator capable of explaining why a benign composition triggers an unsafe perception.

Appendix E
LLM-driven Prompts Data Synthesis

To scale the benchmark beyond manual annotation while maintaining high semantic quality, we implement an automated data synthesis pipeline leveraging LLMs. Specifically, we employ the Qwen-Flash model to function as an expert prompt engineer. For each identified hazardous concept pair
(v^{1}_{i},v^{2}_{i})
belonging to a specific risk category, the pipeline is tasked with generating a triplet of prompts: a compositional prompt where the two concepts interact to trigger the risk, and two atomic prompts describing each concept in isolation. This generation process is governed by three critical constraints to ensure the validity of the MCCU evaluation.

First, to guarantee Semantic Independence, we enforce a strict mutual exclusivity constraint: the description for the first atomic concept
v^{1}_{i}
must strictly exclude any mention or semantic implication of the second concept
v^{2}_{i}
, and vice versa. This ensures that the atomic prompts serve as clean, uncontaminated baselines for measuring single-concept fidelity. Second, to ensure Risk Activation, the compositional prompt is explicitly instructed to manifest the target risk category through the interaction of the two concepts, rather than simply listing them. Third, to mitigate the “template bias” often observed in synthetic data, we introduce a Stylistic Diversity mechanism. For each generation query, we randomly sample from a predefined pool of 24 distinct stylistic directives (e.g., “cinematic”, “clinical”, “poetic”, “technical blueprint”). This stochastic sampling strategy ensures that the generated prompts cover a wide distribution of linguistic patterns, preventing the evaluation from overfitting to a specific sentence structure.

Finally, we impose technical constraints to align the outputs with standard text-to-image architectures. All generated descriptions are restricted to a maximum length of 77 tokens to fit within the context window of the CLIP text encoder. We generate multiple variations for each concept pair, varying the random seeds and stylistic templates, resulting in a robust and diverse evaluation set that rigorously tests the model’s behavior under various contextual conditions.
