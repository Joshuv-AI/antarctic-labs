Abstract
Matrix measures induced by vector norms are widely used in contraction theory of nonlinear dynamical systems. A natural and important robustness question is whether negativity of a matrix measure is preserved under arbitrary nonnegative diagonal damping. Matrix measures with this property have been called admissible. We show that an induced matrix measure is admissible if and only if the underlying norm is orthant-monotonic. Equivalently, these are precisely the induced matrix measures satisfying μ ⁡ ( D ) = max i ⁡ { d i ​ i } \mu(D)=\max_{i}\{d_{ii}\} for every nonnegative diagonal matrix D D . We further show that this class is maximal for uniform preservation of contraction under nonnegative diagonal perturbations. The result gives a new geometric characterization of admissibility and clarifies the role of orthant-monotonicity in additive D D -stability and diffusion-induced instability.

I
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be the set of
n\times n
diagonal matrices with nonnegative entries.
Suppose that a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

is Hurwitz.
Then the system
\dot{x}=Ax
is asymptotically stable,
and we may naturally expect that

\displaystyle\dot{x}=Ax-Dx,\text{ with }D\in\mathbb{D}^{n}_{\geq 0},

(1)

is also asymptotically stable, as we merely add an independent dissipation term for each state variable.
In other words, we may expect that
A-D
is also Hurwitz for any
D\in\mathbb{D}^{n}_{\geq 0}
.
However, this is not so. For example, the
2\times 2
matrix
A=\begin{bmatrix}1&-3\\
1&-2\end{bmatrix}

is Hurwitz (as
\det(A)>0
and
\operatorname{tr}(A)<0
), yet for any
d_{2}>1
we have that
\det(A-\operatorname{diag}(0,d_{2}))=1-d_{2}<0
, so
A-\operatorname{diag}(0,d_{2})
is not Hurwitz. In general,
stability is a global property of the interaction pattern between the state-variables, and
not a monotone function of diagonal damping.

The term
-Dx
in (
1
) models independent dissipation (or leakage) in each state variable. This is
common in many linear and nonlinear
models representing for example
natural death rate in population dynamics, molecular degradation in chemical reaction networks and systems biology, drug clearance in pharmacokinetics,
damping in mechanical systems, and more. Often one expects that if
D
is
large enough then the term
-Dx
dominates the other terms in the vector field and guarantees stability, but as we saw in the example above this is not necessarily true.

A similar stability problem arises in the context of systems with diffusion, as shown for the first time in a famous paper by A. M. Turing
[
26
]
. The theory is for systems of PDEs, but following Sontag
[
24
]
we present the basic ideas using a set of ODEs. Consider two systems:

\dot{x}=Ax\text{ and }\dot{z}=Az,

(2)

with
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
Hurwitz, that are coupled via a diagonal diffusion term, i.e.,

\displaystyle\dot{x}=Ax+D(z-x),

\displaystyle\dot{z}=Az+D(x-z),

(3)

with
D\in\mathbb{D}^{n}_{\geq 0}
.
Define a new state-vector
p:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{2n}
by
p:=T\begin{bmatrix}x\\
z\end{bmatrix}
, with
T:=\begin{bmatrix}I_{n}&I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&I_{n}\end{bmatrix}
. Then

\dot{p}=\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\
0&A-2D\end{bmatrix}p.

Since
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is Hurwitz, the coupled system (
I
) will be asymptotically stable iff
A-2D
is Hurwitz. Furthermore, since
p=\begin{bmatrix}z+x&z-x\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
, the two systems in (
2
)
synchronize (i.e,
x(t)-z(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
) iff
A-2D
is Hurwitz.

Diffusion terms appear in many dynamical models representing for example migration in ecological networks, kinetic interactions in chemical reaction networks, electrical or chemical interactions between neurons, information flow in cooperative systems,
and more.

The discussion above motivates the following definition:
A matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is called additively
D
-stable if
A-D
is Hurwitz for any
D\in\mathbb{D}^{n}_{\geq 0}
. According to Kushel
[
13
]
, this notion first appeared in
[
5
]
(see also
[
16
]
).

Example 1
.

If
A\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
then
A-D
is Hurwitz iff

\operatorname{tr}(A-D)=a_{11}-d_{1}+a_{22}-d_{2}<0

and

\det(A-D)=\det(A)+d_{1}d_{2}-d_{1}a_{22}-d_{2}a_{11}>0,

so
A\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
will be additively
D
-stable iff

\operatorname{tr}(A)<0,\;\det(A)>0,\text{ and }a_{11},a_{22}\leq 0.

In general, the problem of characterizing the class of additively
D
-stable matrices is open, except for low orders of
n

[
11
]
. In some special cases,
there are also several known necessary conditions and sufficient conditions for additive
D
-stability
[
13
,
11
]
. For example, if
A
is Metzler (i.e.,
a_{ij}\geq 0
for all
i\not=j
) then
A
is
additively
D
-stable iff it is Hurwitz.

Additive
D
-stability has found many applications in the stability analysis of continuous-time
reaction-diffusion models
(see, e.g.,
[
8
]
and the references therein).
In this context, the
matrix
A
represents the
linearization of the reaction dynamics at a steady-state, and additive
D
-stability rules out
the possibility of diffusion-driven instabilities.

Li and Wang
[
27
]

introduced the related notion of an
admissible
matrix measure, namely,
a matrix measure
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\mu(-D)\leq 0\text{ for any }D\in\mathbb{D}^{n}_{\geq 0},

(4)

and used it to analyze
additive D-stability. Indeed,

\mu(A)<0,\text{ with }\mu\text{ admissible},

(5)

implies that
for any
D\in\mathbb{D}^{n}_{\geq 0}
we have

\displaystyle\mu(A-D)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(A)+\mu(-D)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(A)

\displaystyle<0,

(6)

where we used the fact that matrix measures are sub-additive.
This implies in particular
that
A-D
is Hurwitz, so
A
is additively
D
-stable. However, the condition in (
5
) is in fact stronger than additive
D
-stability, as it implies negativity of
\mu(A-D)
for all
D\in\mathbb{D}^{n}_{\geq 0}
with respect to the
same measure

\mu
.

Here, we give a new and complete characterization of such matrix measures by proving that a matrix measure is admissible if and only if it is induced by an orthant-monotonic norm.
This links the geometric
notion of orthant-monotonicity with the stability-motivated notion of admissible matrix measures.
Moreover, we show that the class of matrix measures induced by orthant-monotonic norms
is the maximal set of matrix measures that are relevant for additive
D
-stability (see Theorem
1
below).

Induced matrix norms and matrix measures play an important role in many fields of applied mathematics.

For

II-A

Monotonic and absolute norms

A norm
|\cdot|:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is called
monotonic
if for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

\operatorname{abs}(x_{i})\leq\operatorname{abs}(y_{i})\text{ for all }i\implies|x|\leq|y|.

A norm
|\cdot|:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is called
absolute
if

|x|=|\operatorname{abs}(x)|\text{ for any }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\operatorname{abs}(x):=\begin{bmatrix}\operatorname{abs}(x_{1})&\dots&\operatorname{abs}(x_{n})\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
.

It is well-known that a vector norm is monotonic if and only if it is absolute
[
2
]
. In other words, a monotonic norm only depends on the absolute values of the vector entries.
For example, it is clear from the definition that any
\ell_{p}
norm is absolute and thus monotone. Furthermore, if
D\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is diagonal and non-singular
and
p\in[0,\infty]

then the scaled norm
|x|_{p,D}:=|Dx|
is an absolute (and thus monotonic) norm, and the induced matrix measure is
\mu_{p,D}(A)=\mu_{p}(DAD^{-1})
.

Geometrically, a monotonic norm has a unit ball that is convex, centrally symmetric (about the origin), and invariant under sign changes.

II-A
1
Order-preserving properties of monotonic norms

If
|\cdot|:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is absolute (i.e., monotonic) then the induced matrix norm and matrix measure enjoy some “order-preserving” properties that have found interesting applications (see for example
[
21
, Proposition 2]
[
22
]
).

For example, for any
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle|Ax|

\displaystyle=|\operatorname{abs}(Ax)|

\displaystyle\leq|\operatorname{abs}(A)\operatorname{abs}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\|\operatorname{abs}(A)\||x|.

and thus

\displaystyle\|A\|\leq\|\operatorname{abs}(A)\|\quad\text{for all }A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}.

(9)

Also, if
0\leq A\leq B
then

\displaystyle|Ax|

\displaystyle=|\operatorname{abs}(Ax)|

\displaystyle\leq|A\operatorname{abs}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|B\operatorname{abs}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\|B\||\operatorname{abs}(x)|

\displaystyle=\|B\||x|,

so we conclude that for a matrix norm induced by a monotonic norm, we have

0\leq A\leq B\implies\|A\|\leq\|B\|.

(10)

A similar
argument shows that if
\mu
is a matrix measure induced by a monotonic norm,
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
are Metzler and
A\leq B
then

\mu(A)\leq\mu(B)
.

Monotonic norms also have a kind of “Perron-Frobenius” property. To explain this, fix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
with
A\geq 0
. Then
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\operatorname{abs}(Ax)\leq A\operatorname{abs}(x),

so

\displaystyle|Ax|=|\operatorname{abs}(Ax)|\leq|A\operatorname{abs}(x)|.

(11)

This implies that

A\geq 0\implies\|A\|:=\max_{|x|=1}|Ax|=\max_{|x|=1,\;x\geq 0}|Ax|.

(12)

In other words, it is always possible to find a maximizing vector
x
in the non-negative orthant.

II-B

Orthant-monotonic norms

A more general class of norms are orthant-monotonic norms
[
10
]
(also called weak monotonic norms, see, e.g.
[
15
]
).
A norm
|\cdot|:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is

orthant-monotonic

if for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

x_{i}y_{i}\geq 0\text{ and }|x_{i}|\leq|y_{i}|\text{ for all }i\implies|x|\leq|y|.

Note that inside one orthant, moving away from the origin coordinatewise means moving outward.
An orthant-monotone norm respects this order: going outward in every coordinate cannot reduce your distance from the origin as measured by the norm.

.

Consider the norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined by

|x|:=\begin{cases}|x|_{\infty},&\text{if }\>x_{1}x_{2}\geq 0,\\
|x|_{1},&\text{if }\>x_{1}x_{2}<0.\end{cases}

(13)

This norm is orthant-monotonic.
The unit circle in this norm is shown in Fig.

.
This norm is not absolute, as

2=|\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\end{bmatrix}^{\top}|\neq|\begin{bmatrix}1&1\end{bmatrix}^{\top}|=1.

(14)

Hence,
this norm is orthant-monotonic, but not monotonic.

Geometrically, an orthant-monotonic norm is characterized by a unit ball that is
convex, centrally symmetric (about the origin) and whose boundary is monotone inside each orthant, but may have different slopes in different orthants (and, therefore, the norm is not necessarily monotone).
Orthant-monotonic norms have found
applications in
approximation theory
[
29
]
,
sparse optimization
[
4
]
, and norm-induced preferences in economic theory
[
9
]

A norm
|\cdot|:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is orthant-monotonic if and only if

|\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}&\dots&&x_{j-1}&0&x_{j+1}&\dots&x_{n}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}|\leq|\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}&\dots&x_{j-1}&x_{j}&x_{j+1}&\dots&x_{n}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}|

(15)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. In other words, projecting
a vector on a coordinate axis can
only lead to a smaller norm. For example, consider the norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}

with unit ball defined by
the parallelogram with vertices
\begin{bmatrix}2&2\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
,

\begin{bmatrix}-2&-2\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
,

\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
, and

\begin{bmatrix}-1&1\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
.
This norm is not orthant-monotonic, as

\left|\begin{bmatrix}2\\
0\end{bmatrix}\right|>1=\left|\begin{bmatrix}2\\
2\end{bmatrix}\right|.

Summarizing the relations between the norms reviewed above, we have

\ell_{p}\text{ norms }\subset\text{ monotonic norms }=\text{ absolute norms }\subset\text{ orthant-monotonic norms, }

where
\subset
denotes strict inclusion.

II-C

Induced matrix norms of diagonal matrices

Monotonic and orthant-monotonic norms can also be characterized
by the effect of their induced matrix norms on diagonal matrices.

An induced
matrix norm
\|\cdot\|:\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is
monotonic
if and only if

\|D\|=1\text{ for any }D\text{ such that }\operatorname{abs}(D)=I_{n},

(see
[
18
]
),
and
if and only if

\|D\|=\max_{i}\{\operatorname{abs}(d_{ii})\}\text{ for any diagonal matrix }D,

(see
[
2
, Theorem 3]
).
The second characterization implies that if
\mu
is induced by a monotonic
matrix measure and
D\in\mathbb{D}^{n}_{\geq 0}

then
\mu(-D)=\max_{i}\{-d_{ii}\}\leq 0
, so
\mu
is admissible
[
23
]
.
In particular, a matrix measure induced by a (diagonally scaled)
\ell_{p}
norm is admissible.

An induced matrix norm
\|\cdot\|:\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is orthant-monotonic norm if and only if

\|D\|=\max_{i}\{d_{ii}\}\text{ for any }D\in\mathbb{D}^{n}_{\geq 0}

(16)

(see
[
7
]
).

Remark 1
.

In this paper, we only consider matrix norms and matrix measures that are induced by a norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
To shorten the notation, from now on we say that a matrix norm or a matrix measure
is monotonic [orthant-monotonic] if it is induced by a monotonic [orthant-monotonic] norm.

Main results

III-A

Characterization of
orthant-monotonic matrix measures

Our first main result in this section provides a new characterization of orthant-monotonic matrix measures and shows that this class coincides with the class of admissible matrix measures. introduced in
[
27
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a matrix measure. The following four conditions are equivalent.

1.

\mu
is orthant-monotonic;

2.

\mu
is admissible;

3.

\mu
satisfies

\mu(D)=\max_{i}\{d_{ii}\}\text{ for any }D\in\mathbb{D}^{n}_{\geq 0};

4.

there exists a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
such that
\mu(A-D)<0
for all
D\in\mathbb{D}^{n}_{\geq 0}
.

Proof:

1
)
\implies
3
):
Suppose that
\mu
is orthant-monotonic. Fix
D\in\mathbb{D}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. Then using (
16
) gives

\mu(D)=\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{\|I_{n}+hD\|-1}{h}=\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{\max_{i}\{1+hd_{ii}\}-1}{h}=\max_{i}\{d_{ii}\}.

(17)

3
)
\implies
1
):
Suppose that
\mu
satisfies property
3
) in the theorem. Fix
D\in\mathbb{D}^{n}_{>0}
. Define a diagonal matrix
\ln(D)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
by
\ln(D):=\operatorname{diag}(\ln(d_{11}),\dots,\ln(d_{nn}))
.
Then

\|D\|=\|\exp(\ln(D))\|\leq\exp(\mu(\ln(D)))=\exp(\max_{i}\ln({d_{ii}}))=\max_{i}\{d_{ii}\},

for any

(see e.g.
[

]

We conclude that (

3
)
\implies

\implies

Assume that
\mu

2
) implies
3
.

2
)
\implies

Suppose that

Li and Wang

\mu_{\infty,T}:\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\to\mathbb{R}
induced by the scaled
\ell_{\infty}
norm
|x|_{\infty,T}:=|Tx|_{\infty}
, with

1&3\end{bmatrix}

is not admissible. Indeed, for

, we have

that the scaled norm

so (

The proof that

Corollary 1
.

If
\mu
satisfies any of the equivalent conditions in Theorem

\mu(D)=\max_{i}\{d_{ii}\}\text{ for any diagonal matrix }D\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}.

III-B

Explicit bounds for the matrix measure under diagonal perturbations

We next show that for orthant-monotonic matrix measures a
diagonal perturbation shifts the matrix measure in a controlled way.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
be an orthant-monotonic matrix measure. Then for any
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and any diagonal matrix
D\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, we have

\mu(A)-\max_{i}\{d_{ii}\}\leq\mu(A-D)\leq\mu(A)-\min_{i}\{d_{ii}\}.

(20)

Note that taking
D=I_{n}
and using (
8
) shows that the bounds are sharp.

Proof:

First note that by Corollary
1
,

\mu(D)=\max_{i}\{d_{ii}\}\text{ and }\mu(-D)=-\min_{i}\{d_{ii}\}.

Thus,

\mu(A-D)\leq\mu(A)+\mu(-D)=\mu(A)-\min_{i}\{d_{ii}\}.

To prove the second bound in (
20
), note that

\displaystyle\mu(A)

\displaystyle=\mu(A-D+D)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(A-D)+\mu(D)

\displaystyle=\mu(A-D)+\max_{i}\{d_{ii}\},

and this completes the proof.
∎

In the remainder of this section, we prove two more
properties of orthant-monotonic measures.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{p}
denote the matrix norm induced by the
\ell_{p}
norm, which is absolute and thus monotonic. Using the explicit expression for
\|\cdot\|_{1}
,
\|\cdot\|_{2}
, and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
(see Table
I
), it can be shown that all these three norms satisfy
\|A\|\geq\max\{\operatorname{abs}(a_{1,1}),\dots,\operatorname{abs}(a_{n,n})\}
for any
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. The next result generalizes this to any orthant-monotonic norm.

Lemma 1
.

Let
|\cdot|:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be an
orthant-monotonic norm. Then the induced matrix norm satisfies
\|A\|\geq\max\{\operatorname{abs}(a_{1,1}),\dots,\operatorname{abs}(a_{n,n})\}
for any
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
.

Proof:

Fix
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Let
e^{i}
denote the
i
th unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then

\displaystyle\|A\||e^{i}|

\displaystyle\geq|Ae^{i}|

\displaystyle=\left|\begin{bmatrix}a_{1,i}&\dots&a_{i-1,i}&a_{i,i}&a_{i+1,i}&\dots&a_{n,i}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\begin{bmatrix}0&\dots&0&a_{i,i}&0&\dots&0\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\right|

\displaystyle=\operatorname{abs}(a_{i,i})|e^{i}|,

where the inequality follows from (
15
). We conclude that
\|A\|\geq\operatorname{abs}(a_{i,i})
for any
i
.
∎

Let
\mu_{p}
denote the matrix measure induced by the
\ell_{p}
norm, which is absolute and thus monotonic. Using the explicit expression for the
\ell_{1}
,
\ell_{2}
, and
\ell_{\infty}
matrix measures (see Table

), it can be shown that if
\mu_{i}(A)<0
for

then every diagonal entry of

is negative. The next result generalizes this to any orthant-monotonic norm.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
be an
orthant-monotonic matrix measure, and let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. If
\mu(A)<0
then every diagonal entry of
A
is negative.

Proof:

Since
\mu(A)<0
, we have

\|I_{n}+hA\|<1

for any sufficiently small
h>0
.
Lemma
1
implies that
\operatorname{abs}(1+ha_{i,i})<1
for all
i
,
so
a_{i,i}<0
for all
i
.
∎

Example
1
shows that a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}

can be additively
D
-stable
even if one of its diagonal entries is zero. Thus, the condition
\mu(A)<0
, with
\mu
orthant-monotonic (i.e., admissible) is strictly stronger than the requirement that

is additively
D
-stable.

IV
Conclusion

We proved that a matrix measure is admissible if and only if
it is induced by an orthant-monotonic norm. This links the geometric notion of orthant-monotonicity with the stability-motivated notion of admissible matrix measures. We further showed that
orthant-monotonic norms are the maximal class preserving contraction under arbitrary diagonal damping.

Questions for further research include the following.
First, in proving contraction and, more generally,
k
-contraction, it is often useful to use matrix measures induced by scaled norms (see, e.g.
[
1
,
20
,
28
]
). If
|\cdot|:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a norm and
T\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times}
is non-singular then
|x|_{T}:=|Tx|
is a scaled norm. In our context, it is important to understand when a scaled norm is monotonic or orthant-monotonic. For some partial results along these lines, see
[
14
]
.

As noted above, matrix norms induced by monotonic norms have some special properties over the set of nonnegative matrices
(see (
10
) and (
12
)).
It is well-known that induced matrix norms have special minimality properties (see
[
12
, Thm. 5.6.32]
), and an interesting problem
is to find necessary and sufficient conditions for a matrix norm to be minimal over the set of nonnegative matrices.
