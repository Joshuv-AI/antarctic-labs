Air quality prediction via hybrid BiGRU-MLP using binary ant colony optimization and firefly algorithm for hyperparameter tuning
Authors: Amira A Mahmoud, Sarah M Alhammad, Yasser Fouad, Ahmed M Osman, Hazem M El-Bakry, Ahmed M Elshewey
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAir quality prediction is an important environmental task that supports pollution monitoring, public health protection, and sustainable urban decision-making. This study proposes a hybrid BiGRU-MLP prediction framework enhanced using Binary Ant Colony Optimization (BACO) for feature selection and the Firefly Algorithm (FA) for hyperparameter tuning. The proposed model was evaluated using the public Air Quality dataset from Kaggle, which contains 9,357 records and multiple pollutant and meteorological attributes, including CO(GT), NOx(GT), NO2(GT), temperature, relative humidity, and sensor-based air quality indicators. The dataset was divided into 70% training, 20% validation, and 10% testing subsets to ensure reliable model development and unbiased performance evaluation. Several baseline models were implemented for comparison, including BiGRU, MLP, Neural Network (NN), LSTM, and BiLSTM. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed BiGRU-MLP model achieved the best predictive performance, obtaining an MSE of 0.0001, RMSE of 0.0101, MAE of 0.0081, MedAE of 0.0070, MAPE of 0.0008, and R² of 99.99%. In comparison, the standalone BiGRU achieved an R² of 96.87%, while MLP, NN, LSTM, and BiLSTM achieved R² values of 95.17%, 92.71%, 90.88%, and 89.29%, respectively. These results confirm that combining BiGRU’s temporal learning capability with MLP’s nonlinear feature representation significantly improves prediction accuracy. The integration of BACO and FA further enhances the model by selecting informative features and optimizing model parameters. Therefore, the proposed framework provides an accurate and efficient solution for air quality prediction and can support intelligent environmental monitoring systems.Keywords: Air quality prediction, Hybrid deep learning, BiGRU-MLP model, Binary ant colony optimization, Firefly algorithm, Feature selection, Hyperparameter tuning, Regression modeling, Environmental monitoringSubject terms: Engineering, Environmental sciences, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Air pollution is one of the most critical environmental and public health challenges facing modern cities, particularly because pollutant concentrations are influenced by complex interactions among traffic emissions, industrial activities, meteorological conditions, seasonal variation, and sensor-based environmental measurements. Accurate air quality prediction is therefore essential for early warning systems, urban planning, environmental policy, and public health protection. Recent studies have shown that machine learning and deep learning models are increasingly effective for air quality forecasting because they can learn nonlinear relationships from high-dimensional environmental and meteorological data. Comprehensive reviews have confirmed the growing role of deep learning in air quality forecasting, especially for modeling temporal dependencies and pollutant variation patterns that are difficult to capture using conventional statistical models1,2.

Traditional air quality prediction methods, including statistical and shallow machine learning models, often show limited ability to represent long-term temporal dependencies, nonlinear pollutant interactions, and hidden sequential patterns in environmental time-series data. For this reason, recurrent neural network architectures such as LSTM, GRU, BiLSTM, and BiGRU have been widely adopted for air pollution forecasting. For example, previous studies have used LSTM-based and hybrid deep learning models to improve prediction accuracy for AQI and pollutant concentration forecasting in urban environments3–5. Moreover, hybrid architectures such as CNN-LSTM and stacked CNN-BiLSTM have demonstrated that combining different neural components can improve feature extraction and temporal representation compared with standalone deep learning models6,7.

Despite these advances, air quality prediction remains a challenging research problem. Environmental datasets usually contain noisy sensor readings, missing or abnormal values, redundant variables, and strong nonlinear correlations among pollutants and meteorological attributes. In addition, many existing models depend heavily on manually selected input variables or fixed hyperparameter settings, which may reduce generalization performance. Recent works have emphasized that air quality forecasting models should not only improve prediction accuracy but also address model robustness, optimization, feature relevance, and practical reliability in real-world monitoring systems8,9. Attention-based and optimized deep learning frameworks have also been introduced to enhance AQI prediction by improving feature weighting, temporal learning, and model adaptability10,11.

The main research gap is that many existing air quality prediction studies focus either on standalone recurrent models or on hybrid models without fully integrating automated feature selection and hyperparameter optimization. Although models such as LSTM, GRU, BiLSTM, CNN-LSTM, and attention-based networks have achieved promising results, several studies still suffer from high prediction error, weak optimization of model parameters, limited feature selection, and insufficient comparison with strong baseline neural models. Furthermore, redundant or irrelevant environmental variables may increase computational complexity and reduce predictive stability. Therefore, there is a need for a hybrid optimized model that can combine temporal learning, nonlinear mapping, automatic feature selection, and metaheuristic-based hyperparameter tuning within a unified framework.

To address these limitations, this study proposes a hybrid BiGRU-MLP model for air quality prediction, supported by BACO for feature selection and the FA for hyperparameter tuning. The BiGRU component is used to learn bidirectional temporal dependencies from air quality data, while the MLP component enhances nonlinear feature representation and final prediction capability. BACO is employed to select the most informative air quality and meteorological features, reducing redundancy and improving model efficiency. FA is used to optimize model hyperparameters and improve predictive performance. The proposed model is evaluated using a public Kaggle air quality dataset containing 9,357 records, divided into 70% training, 20% validation, and 10% testing subsets. The proposed BiGRU-MLP achieved superior performance compared with BiGRU, MLP, NN, LSTM, and BiLSTM, obtaining MSE = 0.0001, RMSE = 0.0101, MAE = 0.0081, MAPE = 0.0008, MedAE = 0.0070, and R² = 99.99%.

Related works

The related work section reviews recent studies on air quality and AQI prediction using statistical models, machine learning, deep learning, hybrid neural architectures, and optimization-based approaches. Previous research has explored several techniques, including ANN, ARIMA-based hybrids, CNN-LSTM, BiLSTM, GRU-based models, Vision Transformers, graph neural networks, and attention mechanisms to improve pollutant forecasting accuracy. Many studies have also incorporated feature engineering, spatial–temporal modeling, imputation methods, transfer learning, and metaheuristic optimization to address the nonlinear and dynamic nature of air pollution data. This section highlights the main contributions, strengths, and limitations of these existing approaches and identifies the research gap that motivates the proposed BiGRU-MLP model integrated with BACO-based feature selection and Firefly Algorithm-based hyperparameter tuning.

Kalaiselvi et al.12 proposed a hybrid deep learning framework for air quality forecasting that integrates CNN and LSTM components within a Multi-Modal Attention-based Spatio-Temporal Network (MAST-Net). The study’s main contribution lies in combining multi-source remote sensing data from Sentinel-5P, MODIS, and Landsat-8 with meteorological variables and ground-based observations to predict key pollutants such as PM2.5, PM10, NO₂, and O₃. The model further improves prediction reliability through dynamic feature selection and uncertainty quantification. Experimental results across metropolitan regions showed notable improvements over conventional methods, reducing RMSE by 23–31% and achieving correlation coefficients between 0.91 and 0.94, demonstrating its effectiveness for real-time and spatially adaptive air quality monitoring systems.

Madan et al.13 developed a short-term AQI forecasting framework that combines structured feature engineering with deep learning models. The study’s key contribution is the design of enriched temporal and pollutant-based features, including lag variables, moving averages, seasonal cyclic encodings, pollutant ratios, and date-related indicators, to better capture nonlinear dependencies and interactions among air quality variables. Using OpenWeather API data, the authors evaluated CNN, LSTM, and hybrid CNN-LSTM models optimized with Adam and RMSprop. The results showed that the CNN-LSTM model achieved the best performance, with an F1-score of about 91%, outperforming standalone LSTM and CNN models.

Fusiripong et al.14 introduced AQNet, a spatiotemporal deep learning framework designed to improve urban air quality forecasting by jointly modeling spatial and temporal dependencies. The main contribution of the study is the integration of adaptive mesh-based graph construction with attention-guided temporal learning, allowing the model to represent nonuniform monitoring-station distributions and dynamic pollutant patterns more effectively. Using the Beijing Multi-Site Air Quality Dataset, AQNet applies graph convolution to capture spatial relationships among monitoring stations, gated recurrent units to learn temporal variations, and a cross-fusion layer to combine both representations. The model achieved strong predictive performance, with an MAE of 2.50, RMSE of 3.35, R² of 0.96, and classification-related metrics above 0.95.

Alsholi et al.15 proposed a robust PM2.5 forecasting framework for Kuwait by combining advanced time-series imputation, dimensionality reduction, image-based representation, and hybrid deep learning. The study’s main contribution is the integration of three missing-value imputation methods—SAITS, CSDI, and linear interpolation—with PCA to reduce the feature space from 10 variables to 4 principal components. The processed data were transformed into visual representations using line graph images for time-domain and principal-component patterns and spectrograms for frequency-domain information. These images were then used as inputs to a hybrid ViT-BiLSTM model, where the Vision Transformer extracted informative visual features, and the BiLSTM modeled sequential dependencies for PM2.5 prediction. The proposed model achieved strong forecasting accuracy, with MAE, MSE, and RMSE values of 0.0191, 0.0031, and 0.0562, respectively. Its evaluation on additional datasets from Northern Ireland and Beijing further demonstrated its generalizability and effectiveness compared with previous approaches.

Somboonmark et al.16 proposed hybrid statistical–neural forecasting models for daily AQI prediction in Hat Yai, Songkhla, Thailand, using long-term AQI records collected from 2013 to 2023. The study’s main contribution is the development and comparison of ARIMA-ANN-REG and ARIMA-ANN-QREG models against conventional ARIMA, ANN, and ARIMA-ANN approaches. By integrating linear regression and quantile regression with the ARIMA-ANN structure, the proposed models improved prediction accuracy and reduced forecasting bias. The ARIMA-ANN-QREG model achieved the best overall performance, recording the lowest MAPE, minimal fractional bias, and reduced fractional error, indicating more stable and reliable AQI forecasting. In addition to the modeling contribution, the study developed a user-friendly web application that enables users to fit these forecasting models to their own time-series data, supporting practical environmental monitoring and decision-making.

Gilik et al.17 proposed a CNN-LSTM-based air pollution prediction framework that uses real sensor data to model spatial–temporal relationships across multiple urban locations. The study’s main contribution is the development of both univariate and multivariate prediction models for different pollutants using publicly available datasets from Barcelona, Kocaeli, and İstanbul. The proposed model combines CNN layers for spatial feature extraction with LSTM layers for temporal dependency learning, while extensive hyperparameter tuning is applied to optimize model architecture and reduce prediction error. The results showed that the CNN-LSTM model improved RMSE performance over conventional one-hidden-layer LSTM models by 11–53% for particulate matter, 20–31% for ozone, 9–47% for nitrogen oxides, and 18–46% for sulfur dioxide. The study also explored transfer learning between cities, showing that transferring model weights from Kocaeli to İstanbul improved prediction accuracy due to similarities in their pollution and meteorological patterns.

Duan et al.18 developed a hybrid AQI prediction model that combines statistical modeling, deep learning, and metaheuristic optimization to improve forecasting accuracy across four cities. The study’s key contribution is the integration of ARIMA for capturing the linear component of AQI time-series data and CNN-LSTM for modeling nonlinear temporal patterns. To overcome the difficulty of manually selecting CNN-LSTM hyperparameters, the authors employed the Dung Beetle Optimizer algorithm to automatically identify the optimal model configuration. The proposed optimized ARIMA-CNN-LSTM framework was compared with nine commonly used forecasting models and demonstrated superior performance across RMSE, MAE, and R² metrics. The model achieved R² values of 0.989, 0.962, 0.953, and 0.953 for the four cities, confirming its effectiveness in handling both linear and nonlinear air quality patterns and improving multi-city AQI forecasting reliability.

Hu et al.19 proposed a CNN-LSTM-KAN hybrid framework for AQI time-series prediction across five Chinese cities representing different climatic, geographic, and topographic conditions. The main contribution of the study is the integration of CNN and LSTM layers with Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks and attention mechanisms to improve feature extraction, temporal dependency learning, and adaptive feature weighting. The authors also applied Shapiro–Wilk normality testing to justify Gaussian filtering for noise reduction and designed a multi-regional validation strategy to assess model generalizability beyond a single-city setting. Experimental results showed that the proposed model reduced RMSE by 23.6–59.6% compared with baseline LSTM models and consistently outperformed conventional CNN-LSTM architectures. The model achieved strong cross-regional performance with R² values ranging from 0.92 to 0.99, while correlation analysis identified PM2.5 and PM10 as the most influential predictors, confirming its suitability for regionally adaptive air quality early-warning systems.

Guo et al.20 developed an ANN-based forecasting model for hourly PM2.5 and PM10 concentration prediction in Chongqing City using pollutant concentrations, temporal variables, and meteorological factors as input features. The main contribution of the study is the systematic comparison of thirteen ANN training functions to identify the most effective configuration for short-term particulate matter forecasting. The results showed that the Bayesian regularization training function, trainbr, provided the best prediction performance for both PM2.5 and PM10, achieving high correlation values of 0.9783 and 0.9773, respectively, with low RMSE and MAE values. The Levenberg–Marquardt function, trainlm, also showed strong performance as the second-best training method.

Cui et al.21 proposed a multi-distance spatio-temporal graph neural network with ensemble fusion, named MD-STGNN-EN, for AQI forecasting. The study’s main contribution is the construction of multiple spatial dependency structures using six heterogeneous time-series distance metrics, enabling the model to capture more detailed relationships among monitoring stations. The framework combines graph-convolution and temporal-convolution layers to learn hidden spatio-temporal patterns, while an ensemble fusion module based on CNN and MLP layers generates the final forecasts within an end-to-end learning structure. Evaluated on AQI data from 12 monitoring stations in Xi’an, China, the model achieved strong performance with MAE of 7.0866, MAPE of 14.3329%, and RMSE of 11.6184. Additional validation on another dataset confirmed the robustness of the method, demonstrating its effectiveness in modeling complex spatial and temporal dependencies for accurate AQI prediction.

Recent studies have increasingly emphasized the importance of combining machine learning, feature selection, and metaheuristic optimization to improve the accuracy and efficiency of environmental prediction models. Sawah et al.22 proposed a hybrid optimization framework for air quality prediction that integrates Binary Particle Swarm Optimization (BPSO) with Binary Whale Optimization Algorithm (BWAO) for feature selection and hyperparameter optimization. Their study demonstrated that optimized feature selection can reduce redundant environmental variables and improve air quality prediction performance, confirming the importance of metaheuristic optimization in pollution-related prediction tasks. This work is closely related to the present study because both approaches aim to improve air quality prediction through automated feature selection and optimization; however, the present study introduces a different optimization strategy by integrating BACO for feature selection with the Firefly Algorithm for BiGRU-MLP hyperparameter tuning.

In another environmental prediction study, Sawah23 introduced a feature-selection-driven machine learning framework for sustainable rainfall classification. The study highlighted the role of selecting the most informative meteorological attributes to improve classification accuracy, reduce computational complexity, and support sustainability-oriented environmental modeling. Although the task focused on rainfall classification rather than air quality prediction, it is methodologically relevant because it confirms that feature selection is essential for improving machine-learning-based environmental prediction systems.

Elkamhawy et al.24 developed ensemble neural network models for predicting and optimizing hydraulic-structure stability considering uplift pressure and exit gradient. Their work demonstrated the effectiveness of optimized neural-network-based modeling in complex engineering and environmental systems where nonlinear relationships exist among input variables and target responses. In addition to studies have increasingly used hybrid deep learning and optimization-based models to improve forecasting accuracy for example Ahmad and Kumar25 proposed a hybrid BiGRU-1DCNN model for spatio-temporal PM2.5 forecasting across multiple monitoring stations in Delhi, showing the effectiveness of combining convolutional feature extraction with bidirectional recurrent learning. Other recent studies introduced quantum-inspired neural networks26, hybrid RNN-BiGRU models with random imputation27, and hybrid spatio-temporal PM2.5 models to improve time-series air pollution prediction and handle missing or irregular environmental data28. In addition, WaveNet-based 1DCNN-BiLSTM-XGBoost29 and decomposed-recomposed 1DCNN-BiGRU models were developed for PM2.5 prediction using US Embassy monitoring data30, demonstrating the value of integrating convolutional, recurrent, decomposition, and residual-correction strategies. In addition to Aljanabi et al.31 compared multiple machine-learning algorithms for urban air-quality forecasting and showed that benchmark ML models remain important references for evaluating new prediction frameworks. Kulkarni et al.32 introduced a decentralized air-quality forecasting framework that integrates IoT and federated learning to support privacy-preserving AQI prediction across distributed urban sensors. More recently, Narkhede et al.33 proposed an air-pollution prediction framework based on advanced preprocessing and deep ensemble learning, confirming the importance of robust data preparation and ensemble-based modeling for improving prediction reliability. This supports the motivation for using hybrid neural architectures and optimization strategies in the present study. Compared with these recent studies, the proposed framework combines BACO-based feature selection, FA-based hyperparameter tuning, BiGRU-based temporal representation, and MLP-based nonlinear prediction within a unified model for air quality prediction.

Materials and methods

Dataset description

This study uses the publicly available Air Quality dataset obtained from Kaggle34. The dataset contains 9,357 observations collected from air quality monitoring sensors and includes several pollutant-related and meteorological attributes. These variables describe the concentration of harmful gases, sensor responses, temperature, relative humidity, and the final air quality target value. The dataset was adopted to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed BiGRU-MLP model for air quality prediction because it provides continuous environmental measurements that are suitable for regression-based forecasting tasks.

The dataset includes important air pollution indicators such as CO(GT), C6H6(GT), NOx(GT), and NO2(GT), in addition to sensor-based variables such as PT08.S1(CO), PT08.S2(NMHC), PT08.S3(NOx), PT08.S4(NO2), and PT08.S5(O3). Meteorological variables, including temperature (T) and relative humidity (RH), are also included because weather conditions can strongly affect pollutant dispersion and concentration levels. The prediction target in this study is Air Quality, which represents the overall air quality indicator estimated from the available pollutant and environmental measurements.

In this study, the variable “Air Quality” was used as the target variable for the regression task. This variable represents the air quality response value available in the adopted dataset and was not used as an input feature during model training. The input variables consisted only of pollutant-related measurements, sensor-response attributes, and meteorological factors, including CO(GT), NMHC(GT), C6H6(GT), NOx(GT), NO2(GT), PT08 sensor variables, temperature, and relative humidity. To avoid possible data leakage, the target column was separated from the feature matrix before preprocessing, feature selection, scaling, sequence construction, and model training. Therefore, BACO-based feature selection was applied only to the independent input variables, and the “Air Quality” column was used only as the output label for supervised learning and final evaluation.

Data preprocessing

Data preprocessing was performed to transform the raw air quality dataset into a clean, consistent, and model-ready format. This stage is important because the dataset contains heterogeneous pollutant measurements, sensor responses, meteorological variables, and invalid values that may negatively affect the learning process if directly used. The preprocessing pipeline included initial inspection, invalid-value handling, missing-value imputation, normalization, data splitting, leakage prevention, and final sequence preparation for the proposed BiGRU-MLP model.

In the implemented preprocessing pipeline, all invalid sensor readings with a value of − 200 were first replaced with missing values because these values indicate unavailable or erroneous sensor measurements rather than valid environmental observations. After separating the input features from the target variable, the dataset was divided into training, validation, and testing subsets using a 70:20:10 ratio. All preprocessing parameters were then fitted using the training subset only. Specifically, the median value of each input feature was computed from the training data and used to impute missing values in the training, validation, and testing subsets. No median values were estimated from the validation or testing data. This ensured that information from unseen data did not influence the imputation process.

After median imputation, min–max normalization was applied to scale the input features. The minimum and maximum values of each feature were estimated only from the training subset. The same training-derived minimum and maximum values were then applied unchanged to transform the validation and testing subsets. The target variable “Air Quality” was kept separate from the feature matrix and was not used during imputation fitting, normalization fitting, BACO-based feature selection, or model input construction. This training-only fitting strategy was adopted to prevent data leakage and to provide an unbiased evaluation of the proposed model on unseen testing data.

Conclusion and future work

This study proposed an optimized hybrid BiGRU-MLP model for air quality prediction using Binary Ant Colony Optimization (BACO) for feature selection and the Firefly Algorithm (FA) for hyperparameter tuning. The proposed framework was designed to improve prediction accuracy by combining bidirectional temporal learning, nonlinear feature representation, automated selection of informative variables, and metaheuristic-based parameter optimization. The air quality dataset was first preprocessed by handling invalid sensor readings, treating missing values, analyzing outliers, normalizing features, and dividing the data into training, validation, and testing subsets using a 70:20:10 split. BACO was then applied to select the most relevant pollutant, sensor, and meteorological features, while FA was used to optimize the main hyperparameters of the BiGRU-MLP model.

The experimental results confirmed the superiority of the proposed model compared with several baseline models, including BiGRU, MLP, NN, LSTM, and BiLSTM. The proposed BiGRU-MLP achieved the best overall performance, with MSE = 0.0001, RMSE = 0.0101, MAE = 0.0081, MedAE = 0.0070, MAPE = 0.0008, and R² = 99.99%. These results indicate that the hybrid architecture can effectively capture complex temporal and nonlinear relationships in air quality data. The training and validation curves also demonstrated stable convergence and good generalization behavior, while the actual-versus-predicted analysis showed a very strong agreement between predicted and real air quality values. Overall, the findings show that integrating BACO-based feature selection and FA-based hyperparameter tuning with the BiGRU-MLP architecture provides an effective and accurate solution for air quality prediction.

Future research may also explore advanced explainable artificial intelligence techniques, such as SHAP or attention-based interpretation, to identify the most influential pollutants and environmental factors affecting air quality prediction. Moreover, uncertainty quantification can be incorporated to improve the reliability of predictions in real-world monitoring systems. Another promising direction is to develop a real-time deployment framework that integrates the proposed model with IoT-based air quality sensors and cloud computing platforms. Finally, future studies can compare BACO and FA with other optimization algorithms, such as Particle Swarm Optimization, Grey Wolf Optimizer, Bayesian Optimization, and Optuna, to further examine the computational efficiency, convergence behavior, and predictive stability of the proposed air quality prediction framework.
