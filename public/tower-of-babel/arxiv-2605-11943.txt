1.

Throughout this paper, all rings are assumed to be commutative Noetherian with identity. Let
R=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}R_{n}
be a standard graded ring, i.e.,
R_{0}
is a commutative Noetherian ring with identity, and

is generated as an
R_{0}
-algebra by finitely many elements of degree 1. Let

denote the irrelevant ideal of

and

be a finitely generated graded
R
-module. We denote by
\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{N}_{0}
and

the sets of rational numbers, integers, non-negative integers, and positive integers, respectively.

Let

be a ring and

an ideal of
S
. For any

,

denote the

which is the
i
-th right derived functor of the
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion functor
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(-)
. For any
S
-module

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(X):=\cup_{t\in X}(0:_{X}\mathfrak{a}^{t})=\{x\in X\mid\exists t\in\mathbb{N},\mathfrak{a}^{t}x=0\}.

Reference
[
3
]
provides a comprehensive treatment of the basic properties of local cohomology modules.

The vanishing and finiteness properties of local cohomology modules are central and highly interesting problems in this theory; see for example
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
7
]
and
[

]
.

It is well-known that if

is a graded
R
-module and
\mathfrak{a}
is a homogeneous ideal of
R
, then for any

naturally inherits a grading. In particular (

):

(1)

H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n}
is a finitely generated
R_{0}
-module for any

, and

(2)

H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0
; that is, there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n}=0
for all

In
[
6
]
and
[
11
]
, the above results have been studied for ideals that either contain or are contained in the irrelevant ideal.

Although, the graded components of local cohomology modules

are trivial in almost all positive degrees, they can behave quite irregularly in negative degrees. For example in
[

the authors show that if

is a prime number with

\operatorname{dim}_{K}(H^{2}_{R_{+}}(R)_{-n})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }n\equiv 0\,(mod\,p+1),\\
1&\text{if }n=p^{t}\text{for some odd integer}\,t,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Nevertheless, it is possible to predict the asymptotic behavior of the graded components

H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n}
as

in certain cases, for example:

(1)

i=\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(M)
, the cohomological dimension of

with respect to

(
[

]
) (see (

(2)

with respect to

(
[

]
) (see (

) for definition of finiteness dimension.)

(3)

When

is of small dimension (
[
2
]
).

(4)

When

is a regular ring and

is a polynomial ring over

; in this case, one may consider any homogeneous ideal instead of

(

In this paper we consider the asymptotic behavior of the sequence

This paper is devoted in to 4 sections.
In Section 2, we present some preliminaries, with a primary focus on the graded components of *injective modules.
We show that if

via

of

In Section 3, we study the Bass numbers of graded components of local cohomology modules

(see

To be more precise, it is shown (Theorem

we have:

(1)

(2)

\mu^{1}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\leq\operatorname{dim}_{\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(H^{j+1}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n}),\,\,\,\text{ for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

In Section 4, we consider the Bass numbers

where

(see

for definition of

).
More precisely, among other results, we show (Theorem

) that if
(R_{0},\mathfrak{m}_{0})
is a regular local ring of dimension
d:=\operatorname{dim}(R_{0})
and

then
the following statements hold.

(1)

There exists a polynomial
p(x)
of degree
<c
such that

\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})=p(n)\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

In particular, if

, then the sequence
\{\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{1}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of integers is ultimately constant; that is, there exists

such that

\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{1}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})=\alpha,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\ \text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

(2)

If

, then

\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},M)\neq 0
and the sequence
\{\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is asymptotically decreasing. Moreover, if

(3)

If

is finitely generated then there exists a polynomial
p(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
of degree

such that

\mu^{d-1}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\leq p(n),\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

If, in addition,
\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{c-1}_{R_{+}}(M))
is finitely generated then the equality holds.

We also study the Bass numbers
\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})
in the case where

is relative Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
R_{+}
(see
4.18
for definition of relative Cohen-Macaulay modules). In particular, we show (Corollar

is local,

of degree

and

is flat as an

-module, then the following statements holds.

(1)

For any

and

,
\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})=\operatorname{dim}_{\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(H^{i+c}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n}).

(2)

Let
(R_{0},\mathfrak{m}_{0})
be regular of dimension

. Then,

(a)

there exists a polynomial

of degree
<c-1
such that

\mu^{d-1}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\geq p(n),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0,

(b)

if

may be infinite. Moreover, if

is finitely generated, then by

,

f_{\mathfrak{a}}(X)=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{N}\arrowvert\mathfrak{a}\nsubseteq\sqrt{0:_{S}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X)}\}.

Graded components of local cohomology modules, at the level of finiteness dimension, exhibit favorable asymptotic behavior. For example:

•

(
[
11
, Theorem 3. 6]
) Let
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
be an ideal of
R_{0}
and set

. Then the set
\{\operatorname{Ass}_{R_{0}}(H^{f}_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}+R_{+}}(M)_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

\operatorname{Ass}_{R_{0}}(H^{f}_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}+R_{+}}(M)_{n})=\operatorname{Ass}_{R_{0}}(H^{f}_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}+R_{+}}(M)_{t}),\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\leq t.

In this section we study the asymptotic behavior of the Bass numbers of the graded components

H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n}
as
n\rightarrow-\infty
when
i\leq f_{R_{+}}(M)
.

Recall that, if
S
is a ring,
X
is an
S
-module and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(S)
, then for any
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the
i
-th Bass number of
X
with respect to
\mathfrak{p}
is defined by

(3.2)

\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{p},X):=\operatorname{dim}_{\kappa(p)}(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{S}(\tfrac{S}{\mathfrak{p}},X)_{\mathfrak{p}}).

Here,
\kappa({\mathfrak{p}_{0}}):=\tfrac{(R_{0})_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}}{\mathfrak{p}_{0}(R_{0})_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}},
is the residue field of the local ring
(R_{0})_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}
.
The really important property of the

i
-th Bass number of
X
with respect to
\mathfrak{p}
is that it gives, when the (uniquely determined)
i
-th term in the minimal injective resolution of
X
is expressed as a direct sum of indecomposable injective
S
-modules, the number of those direct summands which
are isomorphic to
E_{S}(\tfrac{S}{\mathfrak{p}})
, the injective envelope of the
S
-module
\tfrac{S}{\mathfrak{p}}
.

The following lemma will be used repeatedly, we include it here for the reader’s convenience.This lemma provides a characterization of Noetherian and Artinian graded modules over standard graded algebras.

Lemma 3.1
.

(
[
14
, Theorem 1]
)
Let
N
be a graded
R
-module. Then

(1)

N
is Noetherian if and only if there exist integers
t
and
s
such that

(a)

N_{n}=0
for all
n\leq t
,

(b)

N_{n+1}=R_{1}N_{n}
for all
n\geq s
and

(c)

N_{n}
is a finitely generated
R_{0}
-module for all
t\leq n\leq s
.

(2)

N
is Artinian if and only if there exist integers
u
and
v
such that

(a)

N_{n}=0
for all
n\geq u
,

(b)

0:_{N_{n}}R_{1}=0
for all
n\leq v
and

(c)

N_{n}
is an Artinian
R_{0}
-module for all
v\leq n\leq u
.

In the following theorem, which constitute one of the main results of this paper, we study the asymptotic behavior of the Bass numbers
\{\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, as
n\rightarrow-\infty
, where
(R_{0},\mathfrak{m}_{0})
is local. We show that these Bass numbers exhibit favorable properties in the case where

j\leq f_{R_{+}}(M)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that
(R_{0},\mathfrak{m}_{0})
is local and
R
is a flat
R_{0}
-module via
R_{0}\hookrightarrow R
. Set
f:=f_{R_{+}}(M)
. Then, for any
l\leq f
, the following statements hold:

(1)

\mu^{0}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{l}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})=\operatorname{dim}_{\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(H^{l}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n}),\,\,\,\text{ for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

(2)

\mu^{1}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{l}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\leq\operatorname{dim}_{\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(H^{l+1}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n}),\,\,\,\text{ for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

Proof.

Let
l\leq f
.

(1)

Using Lemma
2.5
(
1
), there exists the following homogeneous convergence of spectral sequences

(3.3)

E_{2}^{i,j}=\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M))\underset{i}{\Rightarrow}H^{i+j}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M).

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
k<f
. Then

\displaystyle(E_{2}^{i,k})_{n}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{k}_{R_{+}}(M))_{n}

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R_{0}}(\frac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}},H^{k}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})

(by Lemma
2.3
)

\displaystyle=0\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0

\displaystyle\text{(by\,\,Lemma \ref{kirby})}.

Therefore,

(E_{\infty}^{i,k})_{n}=0,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,i\in\mathbb{N}_{0},k<f\,\,\text{and all}\,\,n\ll 0.

In particular,

(3.4)

\operatorname{Hom}_{R_{0}}(\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}},H^{l}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})=(E_{2}^{0,l})_{n}=(E_{\infty}^{0,l})_{n}.

By convergence of the spectral sequence, for all
n\ll 0
, there exists a filtration

0\subseteq\Phi_{l}^{n}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\Phi_{1}^{n}\subseteq\Phi_{0}^{n}=H^{l}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n},

of submodules of
H^{l}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n}
such that

\displaystyle(E_{\infty}^{0,l})_{n}\cong\tfrac{\Phi_{0}^{n}}{\Phi_{1}^{n}},\,\,\text{and}

\displaystyle 0=(E_{\infty}^{i,k})_{n}\cong\tfrac{\Phi_{i}^{n}}{\Phi_{i+1}^{n}}\,\,\text{for all }\,\,i,k\,\text{with}\,\,i+k=l\,\,\text{and}\,\,k<l.

Hence, the filtration reduces to

0=\Phi_{l}^{n}=\cdots=\Phi_{1}^{n}\subseteq\Phi_{0}^{n}=H^{j}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n}.

Therefore, by
3.4
, for all
n\ll 0
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{R_{0}}(\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}},H^{l}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{l}_{R_{+}}(M))_{n}

\displaystyle=\Phi_{0}^{n}=H^{l}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n},

and the result follows.

(2)

Using
3.3
and Lemma
3.1
, for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
k<l
and all
n\ll 0
we have

\displaystyle(E_{2}^{i,k})_{n}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{k}_{R_{+}}(M))_{n}

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R_{0}}(\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}},H^{k}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})

\displaystyle=0=(E_{\infty}^{i,k})_{n},

and hence

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{l}_{R_{+}}(M))_{n}

\displaystyle=(E_{2}^{1,l})_{n}

\displaystyle\cong(E_{\infty}^{1,l})_{n},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

Again, by convergence of the spectral sequence, for all
n\ll 0
, there exists a filtration

0\subseteq\Phi_{l+1}^{n}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\Phi_{2}^{n}\subseteq\Phi_{1}^{n}\subseteq\Phi_{0}^{n}=H^{l+1}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n},

of submodules of
H^{l+1}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n}
such that

\displaystyle(E_{\infty}^{1,l})_{n}\cong\tfrac{\Phi_{1}^{n}}{\Phi_{2}^{n}},\,\,\text{and}

\displaystyle 0=(E_{\infty}^{i,k})_{n}\cong\tfrac{\Phi_{i}^{n}}{\Phi_{i+1}^{n}}\,\,\text{for all }\,\,i,k,\,\text{with}\,\,i+k=l+1\,\,\text{and}\,\,k<l.

Hence,

(E_{\infty}^{1,l})_{n}\cong\Phi_{1}^{n}\subseteq H^{l+1}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n}.

Therefore, for all
n\ll 0
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R_{0}}(\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}},H^{l}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{l}_{R_{+}}(M))_{n}

\displaystyle\cong\Phi_{1}^{n}\subseteq H^{l+1}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n}.

Consequently,

\mu^{1}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{l}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\leq\operatorname{dim}_{\frac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(H^{l+1}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n}),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

∎

. Therefore, the following corollary is an immediate consequence of the preceding theorem.

.

Let
(R_{0},\mathfrak{m}_{0})
be local and
R
is a flat
R_{0}
-module via
R_{0}\hookrightarrow R
. Then,

(1)

\mu^{0}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{1}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})=\operatorname{dim}_{\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(H^{1}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n}),\,\,\,\text{ for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

(2)

\mu^{1}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{1}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\leq\operatorname{dim}_{\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(H^{2}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n}),\,\,\,\text{ for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

4.
Bass numbers of
H^{\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(M)}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n}

Let
S
be a ring. The cohomological dimension
of an
S
-module
X
with respect to an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is defined by

(4.1)

\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X):=\sup\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\arrowvert H^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X)\neq 0\}.

Note that, in view of
[
3
, Corollary 3. 3. 3]
, if
S
is Noetherian, then
\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X)<\infty.

The cohomological dimension is an important invariant in local cohomology theory and has attracted considerable interest; see, for example,
[
7
,
16
]
and
[
17
]
.

Graded components of local cohomology modules, at the level of cohomological dimension exhibit favorable asymptotic behavior. For example:

•

(
[
2
, Corollary 3. 7]
) The set
\{\operatorname{Supp}_{R_{0}}(H^{\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(M)}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is decreasing; that is,

\operatorname{Supp}_{R_{0}}(H^{\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(M)}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n-1})\supseteq\operatorname{Supp}_{R_{0}}(H^{\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(M)}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n}),\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Moreover, there exists
t\in\mathbb{Z}
such that, for all
n\leq t
,

\operatorname{Supp}_{R_{0}}(H^{\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(M)}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})=\{\mathfrak{p}_{0}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R_{0})|\operatorname{dim}_{R}(\kappa({\mathfrak{p}_{0}})\otimes_{R_{0}}M)=\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(M)\}.

In other words, for all sufficiently small integers
n
, the set
\operatorname{Supp}_{R_{0}}(H^{\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(M)}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})
depends only on
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(M)
.

In this section we study the asymptotic behavior of the Bass numbers of the graded components

H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n}
as
n\rightarrow-\infty
when
i=\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(M)
.

The following lemma from
[
7
]
will be used in the subsequent theorem.

Lemma 4.1
.

(
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
)
Let
S
be a ring,
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
S
and
X
and
Y
be two finitely generated
S
-modules with

\operatorname{Supp}_{S}X\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}_{S}Y
. Then
\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X)\leq\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(Y)
. In particular,
\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X)=\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(Y)
whenever
\operatorname{Supp}_{S}X=\operatorname{Supp}_{S}Y
.

In the following theorem, which constitute one of the main results of this paper, we study the asymptotic behavior of the Bass numbers
\{\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, as
n\rightarrow-\infty
, where
(R_{0},\mathfrak{m}_{0})
is a regular local ring of dimension
d
. We show that these Bass numbers exhibit favorable properties in the case where

i\in\{d,d-1\}
and
j=\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(M)
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that
(R_{0},\mathfrak{m}_{0})
is a regular local ring of dimension
d
and
R
is flat as an
R_{0}
-module
via
R_{0}\hookrightarrow R
. Set
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(M)
. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

There exists a polynomial
p(x)
of degree
<c
such that

\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})=p(n)\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

In particular, if
c=1
then the sequence
\{\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{1}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of integers is eventually constant; that is, there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{1}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})=\alpha,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\ \text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

(2)

Suppose that
c>0
and
\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M))\neq 0
, then

\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},M)\neq 0
and the sequence
\{\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is asymptotically decreasing. Moreover, if
c\geq 2
it is asymptotically strictly decreasing.

(3)

If
H^{c}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))
is finitely generated then there exists a polynomial
p(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
of degree
<c-1
such that

\mu^{d-1}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\leq p(n),\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

If, in addition,
\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\frac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{c-1}_{R_{+}}(M))
is finitely generated, then the equality holds.

Proof.

The homomorphism
R_{0}\hookrightarrow R
is faithfully flat. Since
R_{0}
is regular, we have

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R})=\operatorname{pd}_{R_{0}}(\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}})=\operatorname{dim}(R_{0})=d.

(1)

Consider the homogeneous convergence of spectral sequences

E_{2}^{i,j}=\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M))\underset{i}{\Rightarrow}H^{i+j}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)\ \ \ \ \ (\ref{s1}).

Since

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M))=0,\,\,\text{for }\,\,i>d\,\,\text{ or}\,\,j>c,

it follows that

E_{\infty}^{i,j}=E_{2}^{i,j}=0,\,\,\,\text{ for}\,\,i>d\,\,\text{or}\,\,j>c.

Hence, by convergence of the spectral sequence,

(4.2)

\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M))=E_{2}^{d,c}=E_{\infty}^{d,c}\cong H^{d+c}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M).

On the other hand, by Lemma
2.5
(
2
), there is a second homogeneous convergent spectral sequence:

(4.3)

E_{2}^{i,j}=H^{i}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{j}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))\underset{i}{\Rightarrow}H^{i+j}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M).

By Lemma
4.1
,
\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{j}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))\leq c
, and hence

H^{i}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{j}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))=0,\,\,\text{for}\,\,i>c\,\,\text{or}\,\,j>d.

Thus

(4.4)

E_{\infty}^{i,j}=E_{2}^{i,j}=0,\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,i>c\,\,\text{or}\,\,j>d,

and therefore,

(4.5)

H^{d+c}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)\cong E_{\infty}^{d,c}=E_{2}^{d,c}=H^{c}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)).

Combining
4.2
,
4.5
and Lemma
2.3
, we obtain, for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle H^{c}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))_{n}

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M))_{n}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R_{0}}(\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n}).

Let
\mu(X)
denote the minimum number of generators of an

R_{0}
-module
X
. It is well-known that
\mu(X)=\operatorname{dim}_{\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(\tfrac{X}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}X})
. Thus,
1
yields

\displaystyle\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(H^{c}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))_{n})

(4.7)

\displaystyle=\mu(H^{c}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))_{n}).

By Lemma
4.1
,
\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))\leq\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(M)=c.

If
\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))<c,
Then

0=\mu(H^{c}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))_{n})=\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n}),\,\,\text{for all}\,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Otherwise, if equality holds, then by

2

of degree
<c

\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})=p(n),\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

In particular, if

\{\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{1}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

(2)

Assume that

\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M))\neq 0

\displaystyle\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R_{0}}(\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)))

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{dim}_{\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R_{0}}(\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})),

(by Lemma
2.1
(
3
))

such that

\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{t})\neq 0
. By
1

\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\frac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)\neq 0
and hence
\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},M)\neq 0
. Again by
1

H^{c}_{R_{+}}(Ext^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))_{t}\neq 0.

Therefore, in view of Lemma
4.1
,
\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))=c>0

Now, by
[
2

and
1

\displaystyle\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{t-1})

\displaystyle=\mu(H^{c}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\frac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))_{t-1})

\displaystyle\geq\mu(H^{c}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))_{t})+c-1

\displaystyle=\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{t})+c-1

(3)

Assume that
H^{c}_{R_{+}}(Ext^{d-1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))

(4.9)

H^{c}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))_{n}=0,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{ for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

H^{c-1}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))_{n}=(E_{2}^{c-1,d})_{n}\cong(E_{\infty}^{c-1,d})_{n},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

H^{c-1}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))_{n}\cong\tfrac{\Phi^{n}_{c-1}}{\Phi^{n}_{c}}.

3.3

\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M))\cong\tfrac{\Phi_{d-1}}{\Phi_{d}}.

4.12
implies

(4.13)

\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M))\cong\tfrac{H^{d+c-1}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)}{\Phi_{d}}.

Moreover, by
4.11
, for all
n\ll 0
we obtain

\displaystyle\mu^{d-1}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{dim}_{\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(H^{d+c-1}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n})

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(H^{c-1}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))_{n}).

Now, consider the standard graded ring
{R^{\prime}}:=\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}\bigotimes_{R_{0}}R
with base ring
\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}
. By the Independence Theorem
[
3
, Theorem 14. 1. 7]
, there is a homogeneous isomorphism

H^{c-1}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))\cong H^{c-1}_{R^{\prime}_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)).

Hence, by
[
3
, Theorem 17.1.11]
, there exists a polynomial
p(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
of degree
<c-1
such that

\operatorname{dim}_{\frac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(H^{c-1}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))_{n})=p(n),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

Therefore,
3
implies that

\mu^{d-1}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\leq p(n),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0,

as desired.

Finally, assume in addition that
\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{c-1}_{R_{+}}(M))
is a finitely generated
R
-module. Then, by lemmas
3.1
and
2.3
,

\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R_{0}}(\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}},H^{c-1}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})=0,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all }\,\,n\ll 0.

Using again the spectral sequences
3.3
we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}_{R_{0}}(\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})=(E_{2}^{d-1,c})_{n}

\displaystyle=(E_{\infty}^{d-1,c})_{n}

(4.15)

\displaystyle\cong H^{d+c-1}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n}.

Therefore, the only inequality in
3
is in fact an equality and the result follows using the same argument as used above.

∎

Note that, in view of Lemma
4.1
, for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))\leq\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(M).

So, the condition that
H^{c}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))
is finitely generated in the item 3 of the above theorem is not very restrictive.

The following corollary is immediate by the above theorems.

Corollary 4.3
.

Assume that
R
is flat as an
R_{0}
-module
via
R_{0}\hookrightarrow R
, let
\mathfrak{p}_{0}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R_{0})
and set
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(M)
and
f:=f_{R_{+}}(M)
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

If
l\leq f
, then

\mu^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{0},H^{l}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})=\operatorname{dim}_{\kappa{(\mathfrak{p}_{0})}}((H^{l}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{p}_{0}R},M)_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}),\,\,\,\text{ for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

Moreover,

\mu^{1}(\mathfrak{p}_{0},H^{l}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\leq\operatorname{dim}_{\kappa{(\mathfrak{p}_{0})}}((H^{l+1}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{p}_{0}R},M)_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}),\,\,\,\text{ for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

(2)

Assume that
R_{0}
is regular and set
p:=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p}_{0})
. Then

(a)

There exists a polynomial
p(x)
of degree
<c
such that

\mu^{p}(\mathfrak{p}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})=p(n)\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

In particular, if
c=1
then the sequence
\{\mu^{p}(\mathfrak{p}_{0},H^{1}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is eventually constant.

(b)

If
c>0
and
\mu^{p}(\mathfrak{p}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M))\neq 0
, then

\mu^{p}(\mathfrak{p}_{0},M)\neq 0
and the sequence
\{\mu^{p}(\mathfrak{p}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is asymptotically decreasing.
Moreover, if
c\geq 2
it is asymptotically strictly decreasing.

(c)

If
H^{c}_{R_{+}}(Ext^{p-1}_{R}(\frac{R}{\mathfrak{p}_{0}R},M))
is finitely generated, then there exists a polynomial
p(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
of degree
<c-1
such that

\mu^{p-1}(\mathfrak{p}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\leq p(n)\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

If, in addition,
Ext^{p}_{R}(\frac{R}{\mathfrak{p}_{0}R},H^{c-1}_{R_{+}}(M))
is finitely generated then the equality holds.

Proof.

Consider the flat extension
R^{\prime}:=(R_{0})_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}\otimes_{R_{0}}R
of
R
and let
M^{\prime}:=M\otimes_{R}R^{\prime}
. Then
R^{\prime}
is a standard graded ring with local base ring
((R_{0})_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}},\mathfrak{p}_{0}(R_{0})_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}})
.

Moreover, in view of the graded Flat Base Change Theorem
[
3
, Theorem 14. 1. 9]
, for any
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we have

\displaystyle(H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}

\displaystyle\cong(H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M)_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}})_{n}

\displaystyle\cong(H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M)\otimes_{R_{0}}(R_{0})_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}})_{n}

\displaystyle\cong(H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M)\otimes_{R}R^{\prime})_{n}

\displaystyle\cong H^{i}_{R^{\prime}_{+}}(M^{\prime})_{n}.

Therefore, for any
i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we obtain

\displaystyle\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{p}_{0},H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{0})}(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{(R_{0})_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{0}),(H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{{\frac{R^{\prime}_{0}}{\mathfrak{p}_{0}R^{\prime}_{0}}}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R^{\prime}_{0}}(\tfrac{R^{\prime}_{0}}{\mathfrak{p}_{0}R^{\prime}_{0}},H^{j}_{R^{\prime}_{+}}(M^{\prime})_{n})).

In addition, it is straightforward to verify that

\operatorname{cd}_{R^{\prime}_{+}}(M^{\prime})\leq\operatorname{cd}_{R_{+}}(M)
,
f_{R_{+}}(M)\leq f_{R^{\prime}_{+}}(M^{\prime})
and that
\operatorname{pd}_{R^{\prime}}(\tfrac{R^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{p}_{0}R^{\prime}})\leq\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{p}_{0}R})
.

Finally, if
R
is flat over
R_{0}
, then
R^{\prime}
is flat as an
R^{\prime}_{0}
-module via
R^{\prime}_{0}\hookrightarrow R^{\prime}
.
Now, the results follow from the Theorems
3.2
and
4.2
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

(1)

In view of
[
2
, Lemma 3. 8]
, for any
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M))=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\{\mathfrak{p}_{0}+R_{+}\arrowvert\mathfrak{p}_{0}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R_{0}}(H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\}.

E_{R}(H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M))=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\oplus_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R_{0}}(H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})}E_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{p}_{0}+R_{+}})^{\mu^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{0}+R_{+},H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M))}.

In other words,

\mu^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{0}+R_{+},H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M))>0\Leftrightarrow\exists n\in\mathbb{Z}\,\,\text{such that }\,\,\mu^{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{0},H^{i}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})>0.

(2)

For any

and
\mathfrak{p}_{0}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R_{0})

\displaystyle\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{p}_{0},H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{0})}(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{(R_{0})_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{0}),H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M)_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{dim}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{0})}(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{(R_{0})_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{0}),(H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}))

(by
2.1
(
3
))

(4.16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{p}_{0},H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n}).

Therefore, by
[

,

\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{p}_{0},H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M))<\infty\Longleftrightarrow\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{p}_{0},H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})=0\,\,\text{for all }\,\,n\ll 0.

We recall from
[

]
that if

is a ring and
\mathfrak{a}

then a non-zero finitely generated
S
-module

is said to be relative Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
of degree

if

\operatorname{grade}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X)=\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X)=c,

where

denotes the length of a maximal

-regular sequence in

. Equivalently,

The following corollary, consider the Bass numbers of local cohomology modules of relative Cohen-Macaulay modules.

Corollary 4.5
.

Assume that
(R_{0},\mathfrak{m}_{0})
is local,
M
is relative Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
R_{+}
of degree
c
and
R
is flat as an
R_{0}
-module
via
R_{0}\hookrightarrow R
. Then the following statements holds.

(1)

For any
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})=\operatorname{dim}_{\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(H^{i+c}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)_{n}).

(2)

Let
(R_{0},\mathfrak{m}_{0})
be regular of dimension
d
. Then,

(a)

There exists a polynomial
p(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
of degree
<c-1
such that

\mu^{d-1}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\geq p(n),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

(b)

If
H^{c-1}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))
is not finitely generated, then

\mu^{d-1}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})>0,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

Therefore,
\mu^{d-1}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M))
is not finite.

Proof.

(1)

Consider the following homogeneous convergence of spectral sequences

E_{2}^{i,j}=\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M))\underset{i}{\Rightarrow}H^{i+j}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M).\,\,\,\,\,\,(\ref{s1})

Since
M
is relative Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
R_{+}
of degree
c
, we have

E_{2}^{i,j}=\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{j}_{R_{+}}(M))=0,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\,\,\text{with}\,\,j\neq c.

Therefore, using the concept of convergence of spectral sequences, we have the following homogeneous isomorphisms of graded
R
- modules

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M))

\displaystyle=E_{2}^{i,c}

\displaystyle\cong E_{\infty}^{i,c}

(4.19)

\displaystyle\cong H^{i+c}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M),

\displaystyle\text{for all}\,\,i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

This proves
(1)
.

(2)

Assume that
R_{0}
is regular of dimension
d
. Then,

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R})=\operatorname{pd}_{R_{0}}(\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}})=d.

(a)

Considering the convergence of spectral sequences
4.3
,

E_{2}^{i,j}=H^{i}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{j}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))\underset{i}{\Rightarrow}H^{i+j}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M),

we have

E_{\infty}^{i,j}=E_{2}^{i,j}=0,\,\,\,\text{when}\,\,i>c\,\,\text{or}\,\,j>d.

Hence, by the convergence of spectral sequences,

H^{c-1}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))=E_{2}^{c-1,d}\cong E_{\infty}^{c-1,d},

and this module is a subquotient of
H^{d+c-1}_{R_{+}}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M)
.
Now, by
1
, for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(4.20)

\mu^{d-1}(\mathfrak{m}_{0},H^{c}_{R_{+}}(M)_{n})\geq\operatorname{dim}_{\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(H^{c-1}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))_{n}).

Moreover, by
[
3
, Theorem 17. 1. 11]
, there is a polynomial
p(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
of degree
<c-1
such that

\operatorname{dim}_{\tfrac{R_{0}}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}}(H^{c-1}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))_{n})=p(n),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

(b)

Assume that
H^{c-1}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))
is not finitely generated. Then, using
3.1
,

H^{c-1}_{R_{+}}(\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R},M))_{n}\neq 0,\,\,\,\,\,\ \text{for all}\,\,n\ll 0.

Hence, the result follows from
4.20
together with
4.17
.
