Pharmacometric Targets During Anti-TNF Induction and Their Association with Deep Remission in Pediatric Crohn’s Disease
Authors: Alexander Nasr, Tomoyuki Mizuno, Kei Irie, Jonathan R. Dillman, Kimberly Jackson, Kimberly Lynch, Alexa Horrell, Thomas Aviles, Kelly Chun, Jane Yang, Brendan Boyle, Jeffrey S. Hyams, Joshua Noe, Lee. A. Denson, Phillip Minar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and ObjectivePersonalizing anti-tumor necrosis factor (TNF) therapy in pediatric Crohn’s disease (CD) remains challenging due to variable drug clearance and response. Identifying pharmacokinetic (PK) and pharmacodynamic (PD) predictors of deep remission could enable precision dosing strategies. The primary aim was to define PK metrics and PD biomarkers associated with deep remission.MethodsPatients initiating infliximab or adalimumab were prospectively enrolled from four pediatric centers with longitudinal blood and stool biospecimens collected for one year. Deep remission was defined as a combination of weighted pediatric CD activity index (wPCDAI) < 12.5 and Simple Endoscopic Score-CD < 3. Trough concentrations (cTrough) were measured throughout the study. Drug exposure (area under the curve, AUC) and clearance were estimated using drug-specific population PK models and Bayesian estimation using nonlinear mixed-effects modeling (NONMEM).ResultsDeep remission was achieved in 34/70 (48.6%) with no difference in outcomes between biologics. Infliximab cTrough thresholds associated with deep remission were 33 µg/mL (Week 2), 15 µg/mL (Week 6), and 4.7 µg/mL (Month 3) with Week 6 cTrough targets ranging 15–26 µg/mL depending on the outcome of interest. Higher AUC during induction for both infliximab and adalimumab was associated with deep remission, whereas rapid infliximab clearance at various timepoints was inversely associated with deep remission. Baseline predictors of rapid infliximab clearance included older age, low albumin, and elevations in body mass index, C-reactive protein, soluble CD64, and wPCDAI.ConclusionsWe identified PK/PD cut-points associated with deep remission and rapid infliximab clearance, providing actionable metrics to individualize induction dosing and optimize maintenance therapy for children starting anti-TNF therapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s40262-026-01685-7.

Introduction

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), including both Crohn’s disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis, is a chronic inflammatory condition associated with substantial morbidity and represents a growing challenge in pediatric gastroenterology [1]. Crohn’s disease incidence continues to rise and its relapsing and remitting course not only drives persistent intestinal inflammation but also increases the risk of cumulative intestinal damage and long-term complications during critical stages of growth and development [2–4].

Biologic therapies, primarily the anti-tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) inhibitors, have emerged as a cornerstone in the management of children with moderate to severe CD. In the USA, infliximab and adalimumab remained the only Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved advanced therapies for pediatric CD for nearly two decades. More recently, both the FDA and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) approved ustekinumab for children with CD. Although anti-TNF biologics achieve high rates of initial clinical response, remission is often not sustained, with only 44.5–55.8% of patients achieving clinical remission at one year [5–7]. These limitations have prompted preemptive strategies, including the use of proactive therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM), dose optimization during the maintenance phase, concomitant immunomodulator therapy, and routine assessment for endoscopic response, to improve drug durability and lower rates of CD-related complications [8, 9].

While prior investigations have identified an association between anti-TNF pharmacometrics and clinical remission or other surrogates of endoscopic response using blood and stool pharmacodynamic (PD) biomarkers, robust assessments evaluating pharmacokinetics (PK) with endoscopic remission (ER) or transmural healing (TH) remain scarce [10–12]. In one of the largest pediatric studies assessing ER and TH after one year of anti-TNF (n = 116), 59.5% achieved ER, 38.8% had TH, and 35.3% achieved both with most patients (88.8%) receiving combination anti-TNF and azathioprine therapy [13]. More recently, Grabovski et al reported a mucosal healing (MH, resolution of visible gut inflammation) rate of 29%, with no difference in maintenance anti-TNF trough concentrations (cTrough) between MH and non-MH groups [14]. Therefore, a critical knowledge gap remains between exposure-response for the anti-TNF biologics, underscoring the need for contemporary, prospective studies evaluating ER and TH in patients receiving proactive, dose-optimized therapy and the association between PK/PD metrics and these outcomes.

To address these gaps, we conducted a post hoc analysis of the ENvISION cohort, a real-world study that included extensive PK and PD assessments with ER and TH assessed after one year of anti-TNF therapy. The primary objective was to identify PK and PD parameters associated with deep remission, which was defined by achieving a combination of both clinical remission (CR) and ER at Month (M)12.

Methods

Study Design and Patient Eligibility

The ENvISION study (Precision Crohn’s disease Management Utilizing Predictive Protein Panels, NCT04131504) was a multicenter, observational cohort of children and young adult patients with CD who were enrolled before starting either infliximab or adalimumab. The primary objective of the ENvISION parent study was to conduct a comprehensive proteomic analysis to identify inflammatory endotypes associated with deep remission. Patients were eligible if they were diagnosed with CD, aged between 2–19 years, and were anti-TNF naïve. Enrollment occurred either at the time of the diagnostic colonoscopy or immediately before starting the anti-TNF biologic. At baseline, demographic characteristics, Paris classification [15] and severity, and prior exposure to corticosteroids or immunomodulators were documented. Participants were prospectively followed for 12 months or until discontinuation of the index anti-TNF biologic or underwent CD-related surgery, whichever occurred first. At study end, participants were assessed for clinical, biochemical, radiologic (TH), and endoscopic outcomes. In this post hoc analysis, we used the same eligibility criteria as the parent study but only included participants who had a M12 colonoscopy, had CD-related surgery, or discontinued therapy before M12 (those who refused the follow-up colonoscopy were excluded).

For both biologics, the initial dose selection and maintenance dosing and intervals were at the discretion of the treating physician at their respective medical center. Dose or interval optimizations occurred throughout maintenance and were largely based on clinical assessments and proactive TDM. Subject recruitment occurred between October 2019 and December 2022 at four academic centers; Cincinnati Children’s Hospital Medical Center, Connecticut Children’s Medical Center, Nationwide Children’s Hospital, and the Medical College of Wisconsin. All subjects or their guardians provided informed consent for this study before collecting any study data. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board at all four participating centers.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of the ENvISION parent study was deep remission at M12, defined as both ER and CR. Endoscopic remission was defined as a Simple Endoscopic Score-Crohn’s Disease (SES-CD) < 3 and CR characterized by a weighted pediatric CD activity index (wPCDAI) < 12.5 and discontinuation of prednisone for at least 8 weeks [16]. Patients who stopped the anti-TNF before M12 or had a CD-related surgery were recorded as treatment failures (no deep remission). The SES-CD was scored by three independent reviewers (AN, TA, and PM) who were blinded to treatments, outcomes and the endoscopy report. Comparison across reviewers was performed to assess classification consistency. Discrepant cases were subsequently re-evaluated in a blinded fashion and followed by in-person review to achieve consensus.

Secondary Outcomes

At M12, ER alone (SES-CD < 3), endoscopic healing (SES-CD = 0) and biochemical remission (fecal calprotectin < 250 µg/g) were assessed.

Research-only magnetic resonance enterography (MRE) at M12 was optional for participants at all four centers. The MRI protocol is included in the Supplemental Methods. Each research MRE exam was independently scored by a single radiologist (JRD) using the simplified Magnetic Resonance Index of Activity (sMaRIA), which provides segmental qualitative assessments of intestinal inflammation [17]. Assessed features included bowel wall thickening (> 3 mm), intramural edema, perienteric fat stranding, and radiologic ulcers. Wall thickening, edema, and fat stranding were each assigned 1 point when present, while ulcers were assigned 2 points, resulting in segmental scores (jejunum, ileum, and colon) ranging from 0 to 5. Transmural healing (TH) was defined as a global sMaRIA score < 2 and deep TH was defined as a sMaRIA < 2 in conjunction with ER and CR.

Anti-TNF Pharmacokinetics

Research-only blood specimens were obtained to determine cTrough for infliximab before infusion 2 (Week 2), infusion 3 (Week 6), and infusion 4 (Week 14), as well as before the infusion closest to M6 and M12. Adalimumab cTrough were assessed prior to injection 3 (M1) and prior to injections at M3, M6, and M12. Infliximab, antibodies to infliximab, adalimumab, and antibody to adalimumab concentrations were measured with a drug-tolerant electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (Esoterix, LabCorp specialty lab, Calabasas, CA). With serial dilutions, the infliximab assay had an upper detection limit of 600 μg/mL, a lower detection limit of 0.4 μg/mL, an inter-assay coefficient of variation (CV) of 6.07–8.51% and a lower limit of antibody to infliximab detection of 22 ng/mL. The adalimumab assay had a lower detection limit of 0.6 µg/mL with an inter-assay CV of < 10% and an antibody to adalimumab limit of detection of 25 ng/mL. As with missing outcome data, missing trough concentrations were not imputed, and analyses were based on the available data at each timepoint.

Infliximab cumulative exposure was defined as the area under the curve (AUC, µg*h/mL) of the predicted individual infliximab concentration-time profile from therapy initiation until the next infusion (Weeks 0–2, Weeks 0–6, and Weeks 0–14, Weeks 0–26 and Weeks 0–52) and determined by applying population PK models and Bayesian estimation using nonlinear mixed-effects modeling (NONMEM) software (version 7.5.1, ICON Development Solutions, Ellicott City, MD). Adalimumab cumulative AUC was calculated using the same approach, assessing individual drug exposure profiles from therapy initiation until the next injection (Weeks 0–4, Weeks 0–12, Weeks 0–26 and Weeks 0–52). Infliximab and adalimumab PK parameters (AUC and clearance) were estimated using the population PK models reported by Xiong et al [12] and Sharma et al [18], respectively. Model adequacy was evaluated using goodness-of-fit plots and prediction-corrected visual predictive checks. Following model evaluation, Bayesian post hoc estimation was performed using all available concentration measurements, and an inter-occasion variability of 20% on clearance was incorporated for each infusion occasion [19]. For example, baseline clearance for the treatment initiation occasion was informed primarily by the earliest available concentration measurements obtained after treatment initiation, while all available concentration measurements contributed to the final Bayesian post hoc estimates. The corresponding NONMEM code is available in the Supplemental Materials.

Anti-TNF Pharmacodynamic Biomarkers

In addition to evaluating PK predictors of deep remission, we evaluated the optimal cut-points for PD biomarkers from both blood and stool. The peripheral-blood PD biomarkers of interest were neutrophil surface expression of Fcγ Receptor I (nCD64) and soluble CD64 (sCD64). Measurement of nCD64 was performed as previously described and calculated as the ratio of the mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) of granulocyte CD64 expression to lymphocyte CD64 MFI determined by quantitative flow cytometry (FlowJo, BD Life Sciences) [20]. The sCD64 concentrations were determined with the FCGRI/CD64 enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) with an inter-assay precision CV < 6.93% (LifeSpan BioSciences, Inc., Seattle, WA) [20, 21]. Research-only stool samples were collected at predefined timepoints for each biologic to quantify fecal calprotectin. Fecal calprotectin was measured using ELISA (Buhlmann, Switzerland), with an inter-assay precision CV ranging from 6.6–14.5%.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile range (IQR, 25th–75th percentile), depending on their distribution. Categorical variables were summarized as counts and percentages. Comparisons of cTrough, AUC and drug clearance between subjects with deep remission and those without deep remission were performed using Student’s t-test for normally distributed data or the Mann-Whitney test for non-normally distributed data. Similar analyses were conducted to evaluate differences in the PD biomarkers between those with deep remission and those without deep remission.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated for each PK parameter and PD biomarker in relation to deep remission, with optimal thresholds determined using Youden’s J statistic. For each cut-point, we calculated the area under the ROC curve (AUROC) with 95% confidence interval (CI), along with sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value.

Inter-rater reliability for endoscopic severity scoring was assessed by the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). Univariate logistic regression was used to examine the association between deep remission and pre-treatment (or M3) predictors, with odds ratios (OR) and 95% CI reported. For each PK and PD biomarker, the optimal cut-point associated with deep remission was first determined, and then the baseline and M3 predictor ORs were then estimated using these cut-points defined for this cohort. We used the same approach to identify pre-treatment factors linked to subtherapeutic drug exposure during induction as well as for assessing predictors of rapid baseline infliximab clearance. Variables with a p < 0.05 in the univariate analysis for rapid clearance were considered for the inclusion in a multivariable model. To identify the most parsimonious set of predictors for rapid clearance, backward stepwise selection was applied using the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC). The selection procedure evaluated the impact of removing each predictor at each step, using k = log(n). The OR and 95% CI were reported for predictors retained in the final model. Multicollinearity was assessed to evaluate the relationship between predictors. Model performance was assessed internally by comparing predicted probabilities to observed outcomes and using 1000 bootstrap iterations to obtain bias-corrected estimates of discrimination and calibration.

Statistical analyses were performed and figures generated using GraphPad Prism (v8.0.1, GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA) or R (v.4.3.1, R Core Team, 2020) using the following packages, OptimalCutpoints, MASS, boot, rpart and tidyverse.

Primary Outcome

Among the 70 participants with an evaluable M12 outcome (endoscopy, CD surgery, or drug discontinuation), 34 (48.6%) achieved deep remission. Baseline characteristics were largely comparable between anti-TNF therapies, although patients receiving infliximab were younger at enrollment and had lower body mass index (Supplemental Table 1). Rates of deep remission did not differ between infliximab (50.9%) and adalimumab (38.5%, p = 0.56, Fig. 2). The majority of baseline characteristics were similar between patients with and without deep remission. M12 Secondary Outcomes

The percentage of patients achieving ER was similar between adalimumab (38.5%) and infliximab (52.6%, p = 0.54, Fig. 2). Similarly, the proportion of patients achieving CR (73.8%), biochemical remission (53.1%), endoscopic healing (40%), TH (44.9%), and deep TH (29.6%) were similar between patients treated with infliximab or adalimumab (Supplemental Figure 1). There was no difference in any M12 outcomes between patients receiving anti-TNF monotherapy and those receiving combination therapy with methotrexate.

In a sensitivity analysis of the cohort who remained on therapy without surgery and had a M12 colonoscopy, ER was achieved in 59.3% (35/59). The intraclass correlation coefficient between the three blinded reviewers performing SES-CD scoring was 0.89 (95% CI 0.82–0.93). Among 24 of the 59 patients with ongoing endoscopic inflammation (SES-CD ≥ 3), 62.5% were in CR. Likewise, of the 31 of 44 patients without deep TH, 51.6% were in CR.

Pharmacokinetics and Deep Remission

The PK parameter of greatest clinical interest is cTrough, while AUC (cumulative exposure) and estimated clearance provide complementary metrics that may offer a more comprehensive assessment of the patient’s PK profile. Because AUC and clearance were derived using population PK models, model performance was evaluated in the ENvISION dataset. As shown in Supplemental Figures 2 and 3, goodness-of-fit diagnostics and prediction-corrected visual predictive checks indicated that both models adequately described the observed concentration data. The ETA shrinkage for clearance was 11% for infliximab and 25% for adalimumab, suggesting that the concentration data were sufficiently informative for individual clearance estimation and that the Bayesian post hoc estimates were not primarily driven by population priors.

Trough Concentrations (cTrough)

Patients achieving deep remission had significantly higher infliximab cTrough at Week 2, Week 6 and M3 compared to those without deep remission, but no difference was observed at M12 (Fig. 3a). However, further evaluation of infliximab cTrough quartiles showed that 68.2% of patients with a M12 cTrough ≥ 8.8 µg/mL (the upper two quartiles) achieved deep remission (Supplemental Figure 4). The infliximab cut-points for deep remission were 33 µg/mL, 15 µg/mL, and 4.7 µg/mL at Week 2, Week 6, and M3, respectively (Supplemental Figure 5). Although most guidelines recommend a M3 cTrough > 5 µg/mL for luminal-only CD, we evaluated the optimal Week 6 (infusion 3) cTrough that was associated with a cTrough of > 7.5 or > 10 µg/mL. For adalimumab, we found that patients in deep remission had higher adalimumab cTrough at M1 and M3 with no differences at M6 and M12 (Fig. 3b). The optimal M3 adalimumab cTrough associated with deep remission was 14 µg/mL (AUROC 0.9, 95% CI 0.72-1.00).

Drug Exposure (AUC)

Patients who achieved deep remission had higher infliximab AUC at Week 6 and M3 with a similar pattern observed among those receiving adalimumab, with higher AUC at M1, M3 and M6 in patients who achieved deep remission compared to those who did not (Supplemental Figure 6). The infliximab AUC target at M3 for deep remission was ≥ 85,043 µg*h/mL (AUROC 0.67, 95% CI 0.53–0.82). The rate of deep remission for patients achieving and exceeding the infliximab AUC target was 68.8% (22/32) compared to 29% (7/24) when the AUC target was not met (OR 5.3, 95% CI 1.8–17, p = 0.006).

We evaluated the relationship between AUC and early cTrough targets by determining cTrough cut-points corresponding to the newly established M3 AUC target (85,043 µg*h/mL). These cTrough cut-points were 24 µg/mL (AUROC 0.85, 95% CI 0.74–0.95), 14 µg/mL (AUROC 0.90, 95% CI 0.82–0.97), and 6.9 µg/mL (AUROC 0.95, 95% CI 0.88–1.0) at Week 2, Week 6 and M3, respectively.

Although the sample size for adalimumab was small, we found the ideal M3 AUC deep remission was 41,339 µg*h/mL (AUROC 0.98, 95% CI 0.91–1.00). At M3, patients with an AUC ≥ 41,339 µg*h/mL had a mean cTrough of 17.7 (2.1) µg/mL, compared to 9.6 (3) µg/mL in those below the AUC target (p < 0.001). The optimal adalimumab M3 cTrough cut-point associated with an AUC ≥ 41,339 µg*h/mL was 14 µg/mL (AUROC 0.98, 95% CI 0.94–1.00).

Drug Clearance

Infliximab clearance was consistently higher at baseline, Week 6, M3, M6 and M12 in those who did not achieve deep remission (Fig. 4a). For adalimumab, only M1 clearance differed, with more rapid clearance seen in patients without deep remission (Fig. 4b). Receiver operating characteristic analysis identified that infliximab clearance thresholds associated with deep remission varied by timepoint; < 0.009 L/h at baseline (AUROC 0.69, 95% CI 0.55–0.83), ≤ 0.012 L/h at Week 6 (AUROC 0.79, 95% CI 0.67–0.91) and ≤ 0.011 L/h at M3 (AUROC 0.80, 95% CI 0.68–0.91, Fig. 4c–e). PK and Deep Transmural Healing

As the subgroup with deep TH assessed by MRE and colonoscopy was relatively limited in size (n = 44), cTrough, AUC, and clearance results were only evaluated for patients receiving infliximab (n = 35). For most parameters, those that were statistically different occurred early in therapy, during induction and at the start of maintenance (Supplementary Figure 7). The optimal cTrough thresholds for deep TH at Week 6 and M3 were 15 µg/mL (AUROC 0.69, 95% CI 0.52–0.87) and 6.2 µg/mL (AUROC 0.83, 95% CI 0.67–0.99), respectively.

Auto-drug Antibodies

Antibodies to infliximab were detected in 0%, 3%, 18.5% and 50% at Week 2, Week 6, M3 and M12, respectively. Although most antibodies to infliximab detected before the first maintenance dose were clinically insignificant (< 200 ng/mL, 66.7%), we did find 25% of patients with detected antibodies to infliximab at M12 were ≥ 395 ng/mL. Three of the seven patients who received combination infliximab and methotrexate developed antibodies to infliximab during the study.

Patients with antibodies to infliximab at M3 had markedly lower median cTrough (4.4 µg/mL vs 18 µg/mL, p < 0.001) and lower median AUC (27,478 µg*h/mL vs 52,139 µg*h/mL, p < 0.001) at Week 6. Similarly, patients with antibodies to infliximab at M12 had a lower median M3 cTrough (4.1 µg/mL vs 9.3 µg/mL, p = 0.017) and a lower mean M3 AUC (77,968 µg*h/mL vs 101,956 µg*h/mL, p = 0.021). We found a M3 cTrough < 5 µg/mL was associated with antibody development at M12 (OR 8.5, 95% CI 2.2–39.3, p = 0.003).

Antibodies to adalimumab were detected in 12.5%, 12.5%, 20% and 28.6% at M1, M3, M6 and M12, respectively. Of all those detected, only one patient (5.9%) had an antibody to adalimumab > 200 ng/mL.

Pharmacodynamic Biomarkers and Deep Remission

In our univariate analysis, we found that no baseline patient characteristics (demographics or CD phenotype) were associated with a higher likelihood of achieving deep remission. However, baseline wPCDAI < 25 (OR 4.8, 95% CI 1.7–15.6) and baseline erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) < 30 mm/h (OR 5.6, 95% CI 1.7–21.7) were associated with deep remission.

We then assessed the relationship between deep remission and the neutrophil-related PD biomarkers, including nCD64, sCD64, and fecal calprotectin. Baseline values of the peripheral blood nCD64 and sCD64 did not differ between patients who achieved deep remission and those who did not, while a baseline fecal calprotectin < 2400 µg was associated with deep remission (Table 3). The median percent improvement in nCD64 from baseline to M3 was greater in patients who achieved deep remission (45%, IQR 25–62%) than in those without deep remission (20%, IQR 1–32%, p = 0.0074). Although not statistically significant, changes in the median sCD64 (37.5% vs 32.6%, p = 0.079) and fecal calprotectin showed a similar pattern of improvement, with a median reduction of fecal calprotectin of 92% (IQR 76–96%) in deep remitters versus 68% (51–88%) in patients without deep remission (p = 0.056).

Using ROC analysis, the optimal percent improvement from baseline to M3 that was associated with deep remission was 53% for sCD64 (AUROC 0.62, 95% CI 0.49–0.76), 38% for nCD64 (AUROC 0.74, 95% CI 0.58–0.90) and 83% for fecal calprotectin (AUROC 0.65, 95% CI 0.49–0.81). When evaluated as a binary threshold (rounded for application in routine clinical practice), a 40% improvement in nCD64, 50% improvement in sCD64, and an 80% improvement in fecal calprotectin were significantly associated with deep remission (Table 3). In our cohort, 50% improvement in fecal calprotectin at M3 was not associated with a higher likelihood of deep remission (OR 1.2, 95% CI 0.33–4.5, p = 0.77).

Baseline Predictors for Pre-treatment Rapid Infliximab Clearance

As we found accelerated baseline infliximab clearance was inversely associated with deep remission, we assessed the relationship between rapid clearance at baseline and patient characteristics. Rapid infliximab clearance was defined as ≥ 0.009 L/h, based on the optimal cut-point identified by ROC analysis using the Youden index for deep remission. Six pre-treatment variables were significant in the univariate analysis (Table 4). Backward model selection using the BIC identified age > 14.5 years at the start of infliximab and baseline wPCDAI ≥ 25 as independent predictors of rapid infliximab clearance in our multivariable analysis (BIC, 57.9). The predicted probability of rapid infliximab clearance can be calculated using the following formula: probability = 1/1+exp[− (−5.8 + 0.363*AGE + 0.047*wPCDAI)], with the heatmap and table illustrating predicted probabilities across age and baseline wPCDAI (Supplemental Figure 8). For example, a 16-year-old patient with a baseline wPCDAI of 45 has a predicted probability of rapid clearance of 0.89. Discussion

In this contemporary cohort of pediatric patients with CD treated primarily with anti-TNF monotherapy, we found 48.6% achieved deep remission and 29.6% attained both deep remission and TH. Several PK factors were associated with deep remission, including baseline infliximab clearance and end of induction infliximab or adalimumab cTrough, AUC and clearance. Among PD biomarkers, a baseline fecal calprotectin < 2400 µg/g and a lower nCD64 expression and fecal calprotectin at the end of induction were strong predictors of deep remission.

Prior studies have identified end of induction anti-TNF cTrough as a predictor of M12 outcomes but these investigations relied largely on clinical indices or surrogate biomarkers of intestinal healing [12, 22]. Using repeat colonoscopy to assess deep remission, we identified Week 6 and M3 infliximab cTrough targets of 15 and 4.7 µg/mL, respectively. While the adalimumab cohort was small, we identified a M3 cTrough target of 14 µg/mL for deep remission.

As clinical practice shifts from conducting proactive TDM at M3 to TDM at Week 6 (especially in pediatrics) [23], it will be important to better define Week 6 target concentrations. In our prior investigations, we identified Week 6 targets of 13.9 µg/mL for biologic response, 15.9 µg/mL for clinical response and 18 µg/mL for M3 cTrough > 5 µg/mL [11]. In this current study, we found Week 6 targets of 15 µg/mL for deep remission and deep TH, while targets of 22 µg/mL and 26 µg/mL were associated with M3 concentrations > 7.5 µg/mL and > 10 µg/mL, respectively. Thus, depending on patient-specific goals and disease severity, it is reasonable to consider an early cTrough target range from 15–26 µg/mL at Week 6 (dose 3).

Although higher anti-TNF exposure during induction was associated with deep remission, cTrough and AUC (for either drug) at the time of endoscopy did not differ between deep remitters and non-remitters, suggesting that by M12, most patients had been dose optimized. These findings highlight the value of alternative biomarkers, particularly drug clearance or PD biomarkers, in assessing treatment response, which had been previously shown in a cross-sectional study [24]. While real-time calculation of drug clearance at the bedside can be challenging, clearance can be estimated by integrating a drug-specific population PK model, patient weight and PD biomarkers such as serum albumin, ESR, nCD64, CRP, and anti-drug antibodies to provide a more dynamic assessment of dosing requirements. Moreover, with the growing availably of precision dosing dashboards [25], drug clearance could be a central metric to guide whether a patient requires further dose optimization (PK failure) or a switch to a different advanced therapy (PD failure).

In the current era of frequent use of proactive TDM in children with CD, drug clearance is emerging as a particularly informative PK metric linked to therapeutic response [24, 26–28]. Given its importance, we explored patient-specific predictors of rapid clearance prior to starting infliximab, reasoning that such predictors could help guide higher (off-label) starting doses when warranted. Several univariate predictors of baseline rapid infliximab clearance were identified, and multivariable analysis revealed two independent predictors. We subsequently developed a simplified formula using only pre-infliximab age (years) and baseline wPCDAI as a practical clinical tool to estimate the probability of rapid clearance. This approach requires external validation and prospective clinical evaluation. While this equation cannot currently be used as a clinical decision-making tool until these additional studies are conducted, it may offer pragmatic guidance for selecting initial infliximab dosing strategies.

Interestingly, we observed that increasing age was associated with baseline rapid drug clearance, which contrasts with our prior work showing that young age (< 10 years) and lower weight (< 30 kg) were associated with poor infliximab exposure during induction [12] as well as recent findings by Zhao et al demonstrating more rapid clearance in children aged < 10 years compared with those aged > 10 to < 17 years [28]. These differences may reflect heterogeneity in study populations with inclusion of both patients with CD and ulcerative colitis (43%) in the Zhao et al study (conditions that may differ in inflammatory burden and the presence of protein losing enteropathy), as well as the relatively small number of children aged < 10 years included in our current study (n = 15). Further studies in larger, more homogeneous cohorts are needed to confirm these results and to control for baseline inflammatory burden (such as the extent and severity of endoscopic or transmural inflammation) when assessing clearance prior to anti-TNF therapy.

The investigation into neutrophil PD biomarkers has also provided important insights. While not widely available in the clinic, we found baseline sCD64 was associated with rapid infliximab clearance. While baseline nCD64 was not associated with deep remission, reductions in nCD64 > 40%, sCD64 > 50% and fecal calprotectin > 80% by M3 were associated with deep remission, underscoring the value of longitudinal PD monitoring [26]. Moreover, these findings suggest that while a 50% reduction in fecal calprotectin may be associated with some disease outcomes, it may be insufficient for deep remission [29, 30]. Importantly, while these results remain exploratory, they may further inform trial designs that integrate PK- and PD-guided dose optimization strategies.

Strengths of this study include multicenter enrollment of 80 children and young adults, longitudinal assessment of blood and stool PD biomarkers, and the use of endoscopy and MRE for M12 outcomes. Blinded endoscopic scoring by three reviewers also demonstrated high reliability. We evaluated several pharmacometrics, including cTrough, AUC, drug clearance and identified an objective approach to assess baseline rapid clearance that may inform initial infliximab dosing strategies in the future. Although access to sCD64 is limited, this PD biomarker shows promise for identifying patients with rapid baseline clearance.

Limitations of our analysis include a relatively homogeneous cohort (predominately White, non-Hispanic) and a small number of patients receiving adalimumab, which precluded the identification of cut-points for certain PK metrics and increased uncertainty around ROC-derived thresholds. Consequently, findings related to adalimumab should be interpreted cautiously, as ROC-derived cut-points may be sensitive to small changes in the underlying data. As a post hoc analysis of the primary ENvISION study, we were limited to evaluating PK and PD metrics in patient who completed the M12 primary outcome assessments, including a limited number of patients undergoing MRE. We also acknowledge that multiple PK and PD parameters were evaluated across several time points, and ROC-derived cut-points were not adjusted for multiple comparisons. Therefore, the reported thresholds, particularly those derived from patients on adalimumab, should be viewed as hypothesis-generating and require external validation in larger, independent cohorts before being considered definitive therapeutic targets. Finally, given the observational nature of this study, residual confounding by disease severity and other unmeasured factors cannot be excluded. Accordingly, the identified PK and PD markers should be interpreted as factors associated with clinical outcomes rather than established causal determinants of treatment response.

Additionally, anti-TNF dose selection, use of combination therapy, and dose optimization strategies were not standardized across centers. These limitations underscore the need for larger, pragmatic trials (such as REMODEL-CD [31]) evaluating rates of deep remission in patients receiving the same anti-TNF biologic with standardized, protocol-driven dose selection and routine proactive TDM to further validate our findings. Importantly, rapid baseline clearance may identify patients at risk for poor outcomes under conventional dosing strategies rather than representing an irreversible determinant of treatment failure. Consistent with this interpretation, recent evidence from PREDICT-UC suggests that the adverse prognostic impact of high infliximab clearance may be overcome, at least in part, through sustained intensified dosing in selected patients [32].

Conclusions

In conclusion, adequate drug exposure during induction and early maintenance was associated with deep remission and deep TH. Our novel findings include the identification of Week 6 cTrough targets for various outcomes, the inverse association between rapid baseline infliximab clearance and deep remission, and the identification of various PD biomarkers associated with rapid clearance. It is noteworthy that the identified pharmacometric thresholds in this study should be interpreted as exposure-response benchmarks associated with a higher likelihood of deep remission rather than definitive therapeutic targets. While larger validation studies are needed, the proposed prediction model for rapid baseline clearance may help identify patients at increased risk for accelerated drug clearance early in treatment. Likewise, as precision dosing platforms continue to evolve, future studies should evaluate whether individualized dosing strategies informed by clearance prediction, drug exposure, and pharmacodynamic biomarkers can improve clinical outcomes in pediatric CD.
