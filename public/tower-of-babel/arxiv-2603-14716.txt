Introduction

The observed late–time acceleration of the Universe admits two broad explanatory directions: modifications of gravity on cosmological scales or additional cosmic components with negative pressure. Bounds from the binary–neutron–star merger GW170817 and its electromagnetic counterpart enforce a luminal tensor speed and an effectively constant Planck mass at low redshift, disfavoring large sectors of modified–gravity space
[
1
,
57
]
. In this context it is natural to isolate dark–energy constructions that preserve the Einstein–Hilbert metric sector and alter only the matter content.

This work develops a minimal, effective dark–energy framework—
Quantum–Kinetic Dark Energy
(QKDE)—in which only the normalization of the scalar kinetic term varies slowly in time while the metric sector remains that of General Relativity (GR). The defining effective action is

S=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\Big[\frac{1}{2}M_{\rm pl}^{2}R+K(\chi_{\rm c})\,X-V(\phi)\Big],\qquad X\equiv-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial_{\nu}\phi,

(1.1)

with minimally coupled matter and radiation, where
\chi_{\rm c}
is a clock (Stückelberg) scalar field whose background value defines cosmic time. Working in unitary gauge
\chi_{\rm c}=t
, the action reduces to the effective background form with a time–dependent kinetic normalization
K(t)\equiv K(\chi_{\rm c})|_{\chi_{\rm c}=t}
used throughout the numerical analysis.

Written in this way, the theory admits a covariant completion with a conserved total energy–momentum tensor; the explicit time dependence arises only after gauge fixing. The effective description is therefore interpreted as a background–level unitary–gauge realization of an underlying diffeomorphism–invariant theory, rather than as a manifestly covariant scalar–tensor model.

2.1
Stress–energy tensor and fluid variables

For a general scalar Lagrangian
P(X,\phi,\chi)
, the stress–energy tensor is

T_{\mu\nu}=P_{,X}\,\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial_{\nu}\phi+g_{\mu\nu}P
(see
[
30
]
).
Applying this to
P=K(\chi)X-V(\phi)
gives

T_{\mu\nu}^{(\phi)}=K(\chi)\,\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial_{\nu}\phi+g_{\mu\nu}\!\left[K(\chi)\,X-V(\phi)\right].

(2.2)

On a spatially flat FRW background
\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-\mathrm{d}t^{2}+a^{2}(t)\mathrm{d}\vec{x}^{2}
, homogeneity implies
X=\tfrac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}
and therefore

\rho_{\phi}=K\,\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}+V(\phi),\qquad p_{\phi}=K\,\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}-V(\phi),\qquad w_{\phi}=\frac{K\dot{\phi}^{2}/2-V}{K\dot{\phi}^{2}/2+V}.

(2.3)

Here and below,
K\equiv K(\chi)|_{\chi=t}
once unitary gauge is imposed.
The quantities
\rho_{\phi}
and
p_{\phi}
represent the scalar contribution to the total stress–energy tensor and do not correspond to a separately conserved fluid when
K
varies.

2.2
Scalar equation of motion and modified friction

The Euler–Lagrange equation for
P(X,\phi,\chi)
is
\nabla_{\mu}(P_{,X}\nabla^{\mu}\phi)-P_{,\phi}=0

[
30
]
.
For
P=K(\chi)X-V(\phi)
one obtains

K\,\Box\phi+(\nabla_{\mu}K)\,\nabla^{\mu}\phi-V_{,\phi}=0.

(2.4)

Since
K=K(\chi)
, fixing unitary gauge
\chi=t
gives
(\nabla_{\mu}K)\nabla^{\mu}\phi=g^{00}\dot{K}\,\dot{\phi}=-\dot{K}\,\dot{\phi}
.
Using
\Box\phi=-(\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi})
on FRW gives the background equation

K(t)\big(\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}\big)+\dot{K}(t)\,\dot{\phi}+V_{,\phi}=0,

(2.5)

i.e. the canonical Klein–Gordon relation with an additional, time–dependent friction term
\dot{K}\,\dot{\phi}

2.3
Einstein equations, Raychaudhuri form, and Ricci scalar

Because the metric sector is pure GR (no non-minimal coupling and constant
M_{\rm pl}
),

H^{2}=\frac{1}{3M_{\rm pl}^{2}}\big(\rho_{m}+\rho_{r}+\rho_{\phi}\big),\qquad\dot{H}=-\frac{1}{2M_{\rm pl}^{2}}\Big(\rho_{m}+\tfrac{4}{3}\rho_{r}+\rho_{\phi}+p_{\phi}\Big).

(2.6)

Substituting
\rho_{\phi}+p_{\phi}=K\,\dot{\phi}^{2}
into the second relation yields the Raychaudhuri form

\dot{H}=-\frac{1}{2M_{\rm pl}^{2}}\left(\rho_{m}+\tfrac{4}{3}\rho_{r}+K\,\dot{\phi}^{2}\right),

(2.7)

and spatial flatness implies the FRW Ricci scalar

R=6\left(2H^{2}+\dot{H}\right).

(2.8)

2.4
Continuity relations and background source

Minimal coupling implies the standard matter and radiation continuity equations,

\dot{\rho}_{m}+3H\rho_{m}=0,\qquad\dot{\rho}_{r}+4H\rho_{r}=0.

(2.9)

Because matter and radiation couple only to the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
and not to

In the covariant completion, the total stress–energy tensor is covariantly conserved,
\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu}{}_{\nu(\mathrm{tot})}=0

After fixing unitary gauge
\chi=t

\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu}{}_{\nu(\phi)}\;=\;-\,\partial_{\nu}P(X,\phi,t)\;=\;-\,X\,\partial_{\nu}K,

(2.10)

which on FRW reduces to the exact background relation

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\dot{K}\,\dot{\phi}^{2}+K\,\dot{\phi}\,\ddot{\phi}+V_{,\phi}\dot{\phi},

(2.11)

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\phi}+3H(\rho_{\phi}+p_{\phi})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\dot{K}\,\dot{\phi}^{2}+K\,\dot{\phi}\,(\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi})+V_{,\phi}\dot{\phi}=-\,\tfrac{1}{2}\,\dot{K}\,\dot{\phi}^{2},

(2.12)

5.2
Perturbed Klein–Gordon equation (derived)

Starting from the covariant equation (

K\,\Box\phi+(\nabla_{\mu}K)\nabla^{\mu}\phi-V_{,\phi}=0

and expanding to first order, note that
K=K(t)\Rightarrow\delta K=0

\displaystyle K\!\left(\delta\ddot{\phi}+3H\,\delta\dot{\phi}+\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\delta\phi\right)+\dot{K}\,\delta\dot{\phi}+V_{,\phi\phi}\,\delta\phi\;=\;4K\,\dot{\phi}_{0}\,\dot{\Phi}-2\,V_{,\phi}\,\Phi.

For completeness, the scalar-fluid perturbations read (cf.

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{\phi}

\displaystyle=K\left(\dot{\phi}_{0}\,\delta\dot{\phi}-\dot{\phi}_{0}^{2}\,\Phi\right)+V_{,\phi}\,\delta\phi,

5.3

Define the gauge-invariant Mukhanov–Sasaki (MS) variable and pump field

Such plots highlight the observational pathways of QKDE while avoiding any

8
Numerical implementation and validation

This section specifies a
reproducible
procedure to integrate the background system (
3.12
), evaluate observables (Sec.
7
), and verify identities implied by the covariant formulation (Secs.
2
–
5
). Every step is a direct evaluation of previously stated equations—no phenomenological shortcuts are introduced. The presentation is algorithmic so that distinct codes produce numerically indistinguishable results within controlled tolerances.

8.1
State vector, domain, and solver

For numerical stability and clarity, evolve the state vector

\boxed{\mathbf{y}(N)\equiv\big(\phi(N),\;s(N),\;y_{H}(N)\big)\equiv\big(\phi,\;\phi^{\prime},\;\ln H\big),\qquad N\equiv\ln a\in[N_{\mathrm{ini}},\,0],}

(8.1)

with
H(N)=\exp\{y_{H}(N)\}
and
E(N)\equiv H^{\prime}/H=y_{H}^{\prime}(N)
. The evolution equations are the closed system (
3.12
) written in
(\phi,s,y_{H})
:

\displaystyle s^{\prime}\;

\displaystyle=\;-\Big(3+y_{H}^{\prime}+\frac{K^{\prime}}{K}\Big)\,s\;-\;\frac{V_{,\phi}}{\,e^{2y_{H}}K\,},

(8.2a)

\displaystyle y_{H}^{\prime}\;

\displaystyle=\;-\frac{1}{2M_{\rm pl}^{2}e^{2y_{H}}}\Big(\rho_{m}+\tfrac{4}{3}\rho_{r}+K\,e^{2y_{H}}s^{2}\Big),

(8.2b)

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}\;

\displaystyle=\;K\,\frac{e^{2y_{H}}}{2}\,s^{2}+V(\phi),\qquad X=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{2y_{H}}s^{2}.

(8.2c)

with
\rho_{m}(N)=\rho_{m0}e^{-3N}
and
\rho_{r}(N)=\rho_{r0}e^{-4N}
.

Units and scaling (practical).

A dimensionless implementation improves conditioning: work with
\hat{\phi}\equiv\phi/M_{\rm pl}
,
\hat{V}\equiv V/(M_{\rm pl}^{2}H_{0}^{2})
,
\hat{\rho}_{i}\equiv\rho_{i}/(3M_{\rm pl}^{2}H_{0}^{2})
, and retain
y_{H}=\ln(H/H_{0})
. Equations (
8.2
) are invariant under this rescaling after the trivial substitutions
M_{\rm pl}\!\to\!1
and
H\!\to\!H_{0}e^{y_{H}}
.

Integrator and tolerances.

Use a standard embedded Runge–Kutta method with adaptive stepsize (e.g. Dormand–Prince 4(5)
[
27
]
or an equivalent implementation
[
34
]
). Recommended componentwise tolerances:

\mathrm{rtol}\in[10^{-10},10^{-7}],\qquad\mathrm{atol}\in[10^{-12},10^{-9}].

Late–time thawing solutions are typically nonstiff; if large
|V_{,\phi\phi}|/H^{2}
induces stiffness, a stiff solver may be used
without
changing equations.

Admissible initial data.

Choose
N_{\mathrm{ini}}
deep in matter domination (any epoch with
\rho_{m}\gg\rho_{r},\rho_{\phi}
). Initialize

s(N_{\mathrm{ini}})=0,\qquad y_{H}(N_{\mathrm{ini}})=\ln\!\Big[H_{0}\sqrt{\Omega_{m0}e^{-3N_{\mathrm{ini}}}+\Omega_{r0}e^{-4N_{\mathrm{ini}}}}\Big],

(8.3)

and determine
\phi(N_{\mathrm{ini}})
by a one-dimensional shoot (bisection/Newton) so that the closure at
N=0
holds:

\Omega_{\phi}(0)=1-\Omega_{m0}-\Omega_{r0},\qquad\Omega_{\phi}(N)=\frac{K\,s^{2}}{6M_{\rm pl}^{2}}+\frac{V(\phi)}{3M_{\rm pl}^{2}e^{2y_{H}}}.

(8.4)

The shoot uses only (
8.2c
) (no extra priors).

8.2
Evaluation of
K^{\prime}/K

Phenomenological running.

For
K(N)=1+K_{0}e^{-pN}
the identity (
4.14
) is exact:

\boxed{\,\frac{K^{\prime}}{K}=-p\,\frac{K-1}{K}\,}.

(8.5)

Curvature–motivated running.

For
K(N)=1+\alpha R/M^{2}
, use the
algebraic, non-iterative
form (
4.10
). With
E=y_{H}^{\prime}
and
H=e^{y_{H}}
,

\displaystyle\frac{R}{H^{2}}

\displaystyle=6(2+E),\qquad\frac{R^{\prime}}{H^{2}}=A+B\,K^{\prime},\quad B=\frac{3s^{2}}{M_{\rm pl}^{2}},

(8.6)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=24E+\frac{18K\,s^{2}}{M_{\rm pl}^{2}}+\frac{9\rho_{m}+16\rho_{r}+6s\,V_{,\phi}}{M_{\rm pl}^{2}e^{2y_{H}}},\qquad c\equiv\frac{(\alpha/M^{2})\,e^{2y_{H}}}{\,1+(\alpha/M^{2})\,R\,}.

(8.7)

Then

\boxed{\frac{K^{\prime}}{K}=\frac{c\,A}{\,1-c\,B\,K\,}=\frac{\displaystyle\frac{\alpha e^{2y_{H}}}{M^{2}}\,\frac{A}{1+\alpha R/M^{2}}}{\displaystyle 1-\frac{\alpha e^{2y_{H}}}{M^{2}}\,\frac{3K\,s^{2}}{M_{\rm pl}^{2}\left(1+\alpha R/M^{2}\right)}}}\!,

(8.8)

evaluated pointwise from
(\phi,s,y_{H})
; no recursion in
R^{\prime}
or
K^{\prime}
is required.

Admissibility guards (cheap to enforce).

Enforce for all
N\in[N_{\mathrm{ini}},0]
:

K(N)>0\quad\text{(ghost freedom)},\qquad\big|\,1-cBK\,\big|>\epsilon\;\;\text{(non-vanishing denominator)},

(8.9)

with a user-visible
\epsilon
(e.g. a few
\times
machine precision). If one wishes to confine running to late times, impose the simple prior
|\alpha R/M^{2}|\ll 1
for
N\leq N_{\rm drag}
so that
K\to 1
prior to the baryon drag epoch (Sec.
7.2
).

With

Use an adaptive Gauss–Kronrod routine

8.4
Linear growth implementation

Integrate the GR growth equation (
7.9
) with

:

D^{\prime\prime}+\big(2+E\big)D^{\prime}-\frac{3}{2}\,\Omega_{m}(N)\,D=0,\qquad\Omega_{m}(N)=\frac{\rho_{m}(N)}{3M_{\rm pl}^{2}e^{2y_{H}(N)}}.

(8.12)

Initial conditions in the deep matter era:

D(N_{\mathrm{ini}})=e^{N_{\mathrm{ini}}},\qquad D^{\prime}(N_{\mathrm{ini}})=1,

(8.13)

followed by a renormalization

to set
D(0)=1

and verify consistency with

9.2

Write the closed background system as

\mathbf{y}^{\prime}=\mathbf{F}\big(N,\mathbf{y};\boldsymbol{\theta}\big),\qquad\mathbf{y}\equiv\big(\phi,\,\phi^{\prime},\,H\big)^{\!\top},

(9.2)

with

\boxed{\mathbf{s}_{i}^{\prime}\;=\;\mathbf{J}_{y}\,\mathbf{s}_{i}+\mathbf{J}_{\theta_{i}},\qquad\mathbf{J}_{y}\equiv\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\mathbf{y}},\quad\mathbf{J}_{\theta_{i}}\equiv\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial\theta_{i}}}\,,

with initial data obtained by differentiating the initialization rules in Sec.
3.4

(9.4)

3.12c

Jacobian entries.

(9.5)

Using (
3.12a
)–(
3.12b

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{\phi}}{\partial\phi}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{\phi}}{\partial\phi^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{\phi}}{\partial H}

\displaystyle=0,

(9.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{\phi^{\prime}}}{\partial\phi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{V_{,\phi\phi}}{H^{2}K},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{\phi^{\prime}}}{\partial\phi^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-\!\left(3+E+\frac{K^{\prime}}{K}\right)-\phi^{\prime}\!\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial\phi^{\prime}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi^{\prime}}\frac{K^{\prime}}{K}\right),

\displaystyle=-\phi^{\prime}\!\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial H}+\frac{\partial}{\partial H}\frac{K^{\prime}}{K}\right)+\frac{2V_{,\phi}}{H^{3}K},

\displaystyle=E+H\,\frac{\partial E}{\partial H}.

(9.8)

\boxed{\,\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\!\left(\frac{K^{\prime}}{K}\right)=-\,\frac{K-1}{K}+pN\,\frac{K-1}{K}-pN\,\frac{(K-1)^{2}}{K^{2}}\,}\,.

(9.10)

Curvature–motivated

Appendix E
Quadratic action and the Mukhanov–Sasaki system

This appendix presents the explicit derivation of the quadratic action for scalar
perturbations in the QKDE framework and the resulting Mukhanov–Sasaki (MS)

covariantly completed scalar sector evaluated in unitary gauge
written in covariant notation
in Eq. (
1.1

scalar field, and eliminates all lapse and shift variables through the
Hamiltonian and momentum constraints. No phenomenological assumptions are
introduced; every step follows from the structure of

P(X,\phi,\chi_{\rm c})=K(\chi_{\rm c})X-V(\phi)

with unitary gauge
\chi_{\rm c}=t
imposed only after variation and
K(\chi_{\rm c})>0
.

E.1
Perturbation variables and gauge invariants

Scalar perturbations around a spatially flat FRW background are written in
Newtonian gauge:

ds^{2}=-(1+2\Psi)\,dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)\,(1-2\Phi)\,d\vec{x}^{2},\qquad\phi(t,\vec{x})=\phi_{0}(t)+\delta\phi(t,\vec{x}),

(E.1)

with the potentials satisfying
\Phi=\Psi
in the present framework because the
anisotropic stress of the scalar vanishes at linear order
(see Sec.
5.1
).

A gauge-invariant scalar fluctuation is constructed as in

[

,
46
]
:

v\equiv a\left(\delta\phi+\frac{\dot{\phi}_{0}}{H}\,\Phi\right),\qquad\mathcal{R}\equiv\frac{v}{z},

(E.2)

where
z
is the pump field determined below.
The combination is gauge-invariant under infinitesimal coordinate
transformations

[

,
68
]
.

E.2
ADM expansion and solution of constraints

The covariant action is

S=\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\Big[\tfrac{1}{2}M_{\rm pl}^{2}R+P(X,\phi,\chi_{\rm c})\Big],\qquad P(X,\phi,\chi_{\rm c})=K(\chi_{\rm c})X-V(\phi),

All variations are performed covariantly; unitary gauge
\chi_{\rm c}=t
is fixed only after deriving the constraint equations.
To obtain the quadratic action, the metric is decomposed using ADM variables,
and the total action is expanded to second order in
\{\Phi,\delta\phi\}
.
The lapse and shift appear as nondynamical variables; their equations of motion
give the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints.
Solving these and substituting back yields a quadratic action in a single
dynamical degree of freedom

For general

theories, the resulting action is

,

]

S^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2}\int d\eta\,d^{3}x\left[(v^{\prime})^{2}-c_{s}^{2}(\nabla v)^{2}+\frac{z^{\prime\prime}}{z}\,v^{2}\right],\qquad v^{\prime\prime}+\left(c_{s}^{2}k^{2}-\frac{z^{\prime\prime}}{z}\right)v=0,

(E.3)

where primes denote differentiation with respect to conformal time
\eta

The general expressions for the sound speed and pump field are

c_{s}^{2}=\frac{P_{,X}}{P_{,X}+2XP_{,XX}},\qquad z^{2}=a^{2}\frac{2XP_{,X}}{c_{s}^{2}H^{2}}.

(E.4)

For QKDE,

P_{,X}=K(\chi_{\rm c}),\qquad P_{,XX}=0,

so the theory remains exactly canonical. Evaluated in unitary gauge,

c_{s}^{2}=1,\qquad z^{2}=a^{2}\frac{K(t)\,\dot{\phi}_{0}^{2}}{H^{2}}.

(E.5)

Clarification.

Within the covariant completion evaluated in unitary gauge,
the EFT–DE parameters satisfy

does not kinetically mix with the metric.
Thus the quadratic action reduces identically to the standard single-field
Mukhanov form, with the only modification appearing through the
time-dependent normalization

in the background kinetic energy.
No additional operators are generated.

Constraints on
K(\chi_{\rm c})

Ghost freedom requires
K(\chi_{\rm c})>0
.
Gradient stability follows from

H.3
Parameter-source vectors
\mathbf{J}_{\theta_{i}}

With the pieces above,

\displaystyle J_{\theta_{i},\phi}

H.4

Initial data are set in the matter era by

\phi^{\prime}(N_{\rm ini})=0,\qquad H(N_{\rm ini})=H_{0}\sqrt{\Omega_{m0}e^{-3N_{\rm ini}}+\Omega_{r0}e^{-4N_{\rm ini}}},

\boxed{\;\partial_{\theta_{i}}\phi(N_{\rm ini})=-\,\frac{\left.\dfrac{\partial g}{\partial\mathbf{y}}\right|_{0}\!\cdot\mathbf{s}_{i}^{(0)}(0)}{\left.\dfrac{\partial g}{\partial\mathbf{y}}\right|_{0}\!\cdot\mathbf{e}(0)}\;.\;}
