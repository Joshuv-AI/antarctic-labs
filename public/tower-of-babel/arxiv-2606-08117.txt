Markovian dynamics of single-rebit open quantum systems with applications to colour perception
Michel Berthier, Gabriel Niebel, Edoardo Provenzi

Abstract
This paper investigates the Markovian dynamics of open two-state quantum systems defined over the real numbers (rebits). Two main objectives are pursued. First, we present a comprehensive classification of Markovian rebit quantum channels, i.e. one-parameter semigroups of completely positive, trace-preserving (CPTP) maps acting on the rebit state space. We show that a full characterisation of their action can be achieved and that describing these channels as solutions of the GKSL equation allows us to explicitly identify the associated Lindblad generators and conditions for complete positivity. Second, we present an original application of this classification to colour perception. Using a recent model in which perceived colours arise from Lüders measurements on the rebit state space, we show how chromatic distortion induced by a non-neutral illuminant can be modelled by a Markovian rebit channel that progressively diminishes colour distinguishability. Other types of channels could be used to study colour vision deficiencies. These phenomena are illustrated by simulations on digital images, highlighting the relevance of rebit Markovian dynamics in modelling colour vision.

Markovian dynamics, Quantum channels, Relative entropy, Chromatic distortion

Introduction

The study of open quantum systems has led to the development of a rich mathematical framework for describing the dynamical evolution of quantum states, among which Markovian dynamics plays a central role. In standard quantum information theory, such studies are carried out in the complex context, where qubits (two-level quantum systems) evolve under completely positive, trace-preserving (CPTP) linear maps, hereafter referred to as channels, and are governed by master equations such as the Gorini-Kossakowski-Sudarshan-Lindblad (GKSL) equation.

However, recent years have seen a growing interest in studying real-valued quantum systems, where the qubit is replaced by its analogue over the real numbers, known as the rebit. Several works, including
Wootters 1990
;
McKague et al. 2009
;
Aleksandrova et al. 2013
;
Wootters 2014
;
Moretti and Oppio 2017
;
Koh et al. 2018
;
Renou et al. 2021
;
Fuchs et al. 2022
;
Chiribella et al. 2023
, provide insightful theoretical contributions to the understanding of rebit dynamics. It is worth noting that the rebit is not a simple by-product of the qubit, and that there are dramatic differences between the real and complex settings. One of these differences is illustrated by the fact that, when
n\geq 2
, the dimension of the space of
d^{n}\times d^{n}
real symmetric matrices is strictly greater than the
n
th power of the dimension of the space of
d\times d
real symmetric matrices
Caves et al. 2002
:
d^{n}(d^{n}+1)/2>(d(d+1)/2)^{n}
, whereas these dimensions agree for Hermitian matrices in the complex settings. According to
Wootters 1990
, in real vector space quantum mechanics, one cannot in general determine the density matrix using only local measurements.

Another difference comes from the fact that entanglement over the real numbers differs from that of standard quantum mechanics over the complex numbers. For instance, the real state

\rho=\left(I\otimes I+\sigma_{y}\otimes\sigma_{y}\right)/4

is complex separable because it can be written as

\rho=\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(I+\sigma_{y}\right)\otimes\frac{1}{2}\left(I+\sigma_{y}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(I-\sigma_{y}\right)\otimes\frac{1}{2}\left(I-\sigma_{y}\right)\right)/2
in the complex framework, but real entangled because its concurrence is equal to 1, see
Caves et al. 2001
.

In the first, purely theoretical part of this work, we derive a comprehensive classification of Markovian rebit channels. We begin by analysing the case in which the affine component of the dynamics is itself Markovian, for which the classification admits a relatively simple and explicit form, and then extend the analysis to the general situation. By establishing a precise connection with the GKSL master equation adapted to rebit systems, we are able to formulate clear conditions ensuring complete positivity of the resulting dynamical maps.

The second part of the paper is devoted to an application to a recent quantum information-based model of colour perception, in which chromatic states are represented by rebit states. We show that chromatic distortion arising from illuminant changes can be coherently modelled by non-unital Markovian rebit channels acting on states. Such dynamics naturally accounts for the progressive reduction of chromatic distinguishability when human observers are exposed to non-neutral illuminants. Other types of channels could be used to model colour vision deficiencies.

The paper is structured as follows. For clarity of exposition, the complete classification of Markovian rebit channels is developed in separate subsections of Section
2
. In
2.1
, we begin by introducing the general setting of rebit systems and recall the basic notions concerning rebit states, quantum channels, and Markovian dynamics. Subsection
2.2
is devoted to the classification of Markovian rebit channels whose affine component is itself Markovian. The technically more involved case of general Markovian rebit channels is addressed by first considering unital Markovian dynamics in
2.3
, which provides the key structural insight leading naturally to the full classification presented in
2.4
.

In Section
3
, we establish a direct connection between Markovian rebit channels and the GKSL master equation adapted to real two-level quantum systems, deriving explicit and operational conditions for complete positivity in terms of the coefficients of the Lindblad matrix.

Finally, in Section
4
, we apply these results to a quantum information-based model of colour perception, in which chromatic states are represented as rebit states. We show how illuminant-induced chromatic distortion can be coherently modelled by suitable non-unital Markovian rebit channels, and illustrate the resulting dynamics through numerical simulations.

2
Classification of Markovian rebit channels

Markovian rebit channels form a subclass of general rebit channels, whose classification has been established in
Aldé et al. 2023
. The main results of that work which are relevant for the present study are briefly recalled in the following subsection, in which we also define precisely the concept of Markovian behaviour for rebit channels.

2.1
Classification of general rebit channels and Markovian behaviour

The rebit is the two-state quantum system with Hilbert space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The density matrices
\rho_{\bf s}
representing rebit states form a convex subset
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H}(2,\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}))
of
\mathcal{H}(2,\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$})
, the 3-dimensional
\mathbb{R}
-vector space of
2\times 2
real symmetric matrices. Every
\rho_{\bf s}
is a unit-trace, positive semi-definite matrix which can be explicitly expressed as:

\rho_{\bf s}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1+s_{1}&s_{2}\\
s_{2}&1-s_{1}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{0}+s_{1}\sigma_{1}+s_{2}\sigma_{2}),

(1)

where
s_{1}^{2}+s_{2}^{2}\leq 1
,
\sigma_{0}
is the identity matrix
I_{2}
and
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
are the Pauli matrices with real entries,

\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\;\sigma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(2)

The density matrix
\rho_{\bf s}
is univocally associated to a vector
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}=(s_{1},s_{2})
belonging to the unit disk
\cal D
in
\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}^{2}
. In this context,
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}
and
\cal D
are referred to as
Bloch vector
and
Bloch disk
, respectively.
Throughout this paper, a rebit state
\mathbf{s}
will be equivalently identified with its density matrix
\rho_{\mathbf{s}}
or its associated Bloch vector
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}
, depending on the context.

It is shown in
Aldé et al. 2023
that the quantum channels, i.e. the CPTP linear maps, of the rebit transform the Bloch disk through the following maps

\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}})=R_{1}{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{A}}R_{2}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}=R_{1}DR_{2}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}+R_{1}{\bf w},

(3)

where
R_{i}
is a rotation matrix with angle
\theta_{i}\in[0,2\pi)
,
i=1,2
, and the affine component
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{A}}
of
{\mathcal{C}}
acts as follows

{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}})=D\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}+{\bf w},

(4)

with
D=\text{diag}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
and
{\bf w}=(w_{1},w_{2})\in\cal D
. The complete positivity (CP) of these maps is expressed by the following conditions

\begin{cases}1+\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\geq 0\\[4.30554pt]
1+\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\geq 0\\[4.30554pt]
1-\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\geq 0\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\frac{w_{1}^{2}}{(1+\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})(1+\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})}+\frac{w_{2}^{2}}{(1+\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})(1-\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})}\leq 1,\end{cases}

(5)

together with the requirement that if
\max\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}=1
, then
{\bf w}=\bf{0}
in order to preserve
\cal D
.

The first three constraints imply that
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are confined to a subset
\cal A
of the square defined by
|\lambda_{i}|\leq 1
,
i=1,2
.
\cal A
is called
admissibility region
and is illustrated in Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
Admissibility region
\cal A
for the parameters
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
.

The action of
C_{\mathbb{A}}
on the Bloch disk
\mathcal{D}
is twofold. First,
\mathcal{D}
is contracted into a two-dimensional ellipsoidal region with semiaxes
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
. Second, the centre of
\mathcal{D}
is translated by the vector
\mathbf{w}
. Accordingly,
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are referred to as the
scaling coefficients
, while
\mathbf{w}
is called the
shift vector
. A rebit channel is
unital
, that is, it preserves the centre of
\mathcal{D}
, if and only if
\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{0}
.

We now define the rebit channels that will be analysed in this paper.

Definition 1
.

A Markovian rebit channel is a continuous one-parameter semigroup
\mathcal{C}(t)
,
t\geq 0
, of rebit channels. More precisely, the family of quantum channels

\mathcal{C}(t)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}})=R_{1}(t)\,\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{A}}(t)\,R_{2}(t)\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}=R_{1}(t)D(t)R_{2}(t)\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}+R_{1}(t)\mathbf{w}(t),\qquad t\geq 0,

(6)

with

D(t)=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1}(t),\lambda_{2}(t)),\qquad\mathbf{w}(t)=(w_{1}(t),w_{2}(t)),\qquad t\geq 0,

(7)

satisfies the semigroup conditions

\begin{cases}\mathcal{C}(t_{1})\circ\mathcal{C}(t_{2})=\mathcal{C}(t_{1}+t_{2})\\
\mathcal{C}(0)=I_{2},\end{cases}

(8)

for all
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0
, the map
t\mapsto\mathcal{C}(t)
is continuous, and the scaling and shift functions
\lambda_{i}(t)
and
w_{i}(t)
,
i=1,2
, satisfy the complete positivity conditions (
5
) for every
t\geq 0
.

For the sake of brevity, in the following we shall simply refer to the CP conditions (
5
), with the implicit understanding that they are required to hold for the scaling and the shift functions for all
t\geq 0
. Notice that a Markovian rebit channel
{\cal C}(t)
is unital if
{\bf w}(t)={\bf 0}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Thanks to a standard result, see e.g.
Engel and Nagel 2000
, we have that

\mathcal{C}(t)=\exp\left(\dot{\mathcal{C}}(0)t\right),\quad t\geq 0,

(9)

where
\dot{\mathcal{C}}(0)
is the infinitesimal generator,

\dot{\mathcal{C}}(0)=\left.\frac{d\mathcal{C}(t)}{dt}\right|_{t=0}.

(10)

In the next subsection, we show that the classification of Markovian rebit channels is quite simple when the affine component

\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{A}}(t)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}})=D(t)\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}+\mathbf{w}(t)

(11)

of the channel
\mathcal{C}(t)
is itself Markovian.

2.2
Classification of Markovian rebit channels with a Markovian affine component

The following proposition provides the exhaustive classification of Markovian rebit channels
\mathcal{C}(t)
whose affine component
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{A}}(t)
is itself Markovian.

Proposition 1
.

A Markovian rebit channel
\mathcal{C}(t)
whose affine component
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{A}}(t)
is itself Markovian admits one of the following two representations.

1.

Given
-a:=\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)=\dot{\lambda}_{2}(0)\leq 0
and
\omega:=\dot{\theta}_{1}(0)+\dot{\theta}_{2}(0)
,

\mathcal{C}(t)=\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{A}}(t)\,R(\omega t)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-at},e^{-at})\,R(\omega t),

(12)

where
R(\omega t)
denotes the rotation matrix of angle
\omega t
.

2.

Given
a_{i}:=-\dot{\lambda}_{i}(0)\geq 0
and
b_{i}:=\dot{w}_{i}(0)
,
i=1,2
,

\mathcal{C}(t)=R_{2}(0)^{-1}\,\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{A}}(t)\,R_{2}(0),

(13)

where

\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{A}}(t)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}})=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-a_{1}t},e^{-a_{2}t})\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}+\mathbf{w}(t),

(14)

and

w_{i}(t)=\frac{b_{i}}{a_{i}}\left(1-e^{-a_{i}t}\right),\quad i=1,2,

(15)

are such that the CP conditions (
5
) are fulfilled for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}(t)
be a Markovian channel whose affine component
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{A}}(t)
is Markovian. The condition

{\cal C}_{\mathbb{A}}(t_{1}+t_{2})={\cal C}_{\mathbb{A}}(t_{1})\circ{\cal C}_{\mathbb{A}}(t_{2}),\quad t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0,

(16)

together with eq. (
6
) implies

D(t_{1}+t_{2})\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}+{\bf w}(t_{1}+t_{2})=D(t_{1})D(t_{2})\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}+D(t_{1}){\bf w}(t_{2})+{\bf w}(t_{1}),

(17)

i.e.

\lambda_{i}(t_{1}+t_{2})=\lambda_{i}(t_{1})\lambda_{i}(t_{2})

(18)

and

w_{i}(t_{1}+t_{2})=w_{i}(t_{1})+\lambda_{i}(t_{1})w_{i}(t_{2}),

(19)

i=1,2
, for all
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0
. Moreover, the initial condition
{\cal C}_{\mathbb{A}}(0)=I_{2}
implies

{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{A}}(0)=R_{1}^{-1}(0)R_{2}^{-1}(0)=I_{2},

(20)

so
{\bf w}(0)={\bf 0}
,
\theta_{1}(0)=-\theta_{2}(0)
and, by the CP conditions (
5
),
\lambda_{1}(0)=\lambda_{2}(0)=1
, so
D(0)=I_{2}
.

Eq. (
18
) implies that
\lambda_{1}(t)
and
\lambda_{2}(t)
are continuous characters of the additive semigroup
[0,+\infty)
, so

\lambda_{i}(t)=e^{\dot{\lambda}_{i}(0)t},

(21)

with
\dot{\lambda}_{i}(0)\leq 0
,
i=1,2
, to respect the admissibility region. Introducing this result in (
19
) and differentiating with respect to
t_{2}
, evaluated at
t_{2}=0
, we obtain

\dot{w}_{i}(t)=\dot{w}_{i}(0)\,e^{\dot{\lambda}_{i}(0)t},

(22)

which, considering the initial condition
{\bf w}(0)={\bf 0}
, gives

w_{i}(t)=\frac{\dot{w}_{i}(0)}{\dot{\lambda}_{i}(0)}\left(e^{\dot{\lambda}_{i}(0)t}-1\right).

(23)

We denote, as in the statement of the proposition,
-a_{i}=\dot{\lambda}_{i}(0)\leq 0
, and
b_{i}=\dot{w}_{i}(0)
, for
i=1,2
. Then, by eq. (
23
),
{\mathcal{C}(t)}
is unital if and only if
b_{1}=b_{2}=0
. Let us first analyse such unital rebit channels in the case
a_{1}=a_{2}=:a
, then

\mathcal{C}(t)=R_{1}(t)D(t)R_{2}(t)=e^{-at}R_{1}(t)R_{2}(t),

(24)

so

\mathcal{C}(t_{1}+t_{2})=e^{-a(t_{1}+t_{2})}R_{1}(t_{1}+t_{2})R_{2}(t_{1}+t_{2})=e^{-a{t_{1}}}e^{-a{t_{2}}}R_{1}(t_{1})R_{1}(t_{2})R_{2}(t_{1})R_{2}(t_{2}),

(25)

and
R_{1}(t_{1}+t_{2})R_{2}(t_{1}+t_{2})=R_{1}(t_{1})R_{2}(t_{1})R_{1}(t_{2})R_{2}(t_{2})
. Consequently,

\theta_{1}(t_{1}+t_{2})+\theta_{2}(t_{1}+t_{2})=(\theta_{1}(t_{1})+\theta_{2}(t_{1}))+(\theta_{1}(t_{2})+\theta_{2}(t_{2})),

(26)

so, the continuous angular function
\theta(t)=\theta_{1}(t)+\theta_{2}(t)
satisfies the Cauchy functional equation
\theta(t_{1}+t_{2})=\theta(t_{1})+\theta(t_{2})
for all
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0
, which implies that
\theta(t)=\omega t
, with
\omega=\dot{\theta}_{1}(0)+\dot{\theta}_{2}(0)
, thus giving the expression (
12
).

Suppose now that
a_{1}\neq a_{2}
. By the Markovian property of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{A}}
we have

R_{1}(t_{1})D(t_{1})R_{2}(t_{1})R_{1}(t_{2})D(t_{2})R_{2}(t_{2})\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}=R_{1}(t_{1}+t_{2})D(t_{1}+t_{2})R_{2}(t_{1}+t_{2})\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}},

(27)

for all
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathcal{D}
, hence

\|D(t_{1})R_{2}(t_{1})R_{1}(t_{2})D(t_{2})R_{2}(t_{2}){\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}}\|=\|D(t_{1}+t_{2})R_{2}(t_{1}+t_{2}){\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}}\|.

(28)

The diagonal matrix
D(t)
has eigenvalues
e^{-a_{1}t}
and
e^{-a_{2}t}
, with eigenvectors of coordinates
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
, respectively. So, if we consider

{\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}}=R_{2}^{-1}(t_{1}+t_{2})\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},

(29)

then we have

\|D(t_{1}+t_{2})R_{2}(t_{1}+t_{2}){\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}}\|=e^{-a_{1}(t_{1}+t_{2})},

(30)

and the equality

\|D(t_{1})R_{2}(t_{1})R_{1}(t_{2})D(t_{2})R_{2}(t_{2}){\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}}\|=e^{-a_{1}(t_{1}+t_{2})}

(31)

can be fulfilled if and only if

R_{2}(t_{2})=\pm R_{2}(t_{1}+t_{2}),

(32)

and

R_{2}(t_{1})R_{1}(t_{2})=\pm I_{2},

(33)

for all
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0
.
Equation (
32
) implies
\theta_{2}(t_{2})=\theta_{2}(t_{1}+t_{2})
or
\theta_{2}(t_{2})=\theta_{2}(t_{1}+t_{2})+\pi
, for all
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0
. This means that
R_{2}(t)
is a constant rotation and, by eq. (
33
),
R_{2}(t)=R_{2}(0)=\pm R_{1}^{-1}(t)
. Since
C(0)
is the identity,
R_{1}(t)=R_{2}^{-1}(0)
, and we obtain expression (
13
) in the unital case.

Suppose now that
\mathcal{C}(t)
is non-unital. By Brouwer’s fixed point theorem, together with the uniqueness of solutions to ordinary differential equations,
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{A}}(t)
and
{\mathcal{C}}(t)
have a fixed point, that we indicate with
{\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{0}}
and
{\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{1}}
, respectively, both contained in
\mathcal{D}
for all
t\geq 0
. From

{\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{0}}={\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{A}}(t){\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{0}}=D(t){\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{0}}+{\bf w}(t),

(34)

we get

{\bf w}(t)={\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{0}}-D(t){\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{0}},

(35)

so

{\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{1}}=\mathcal{C}(t){\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{1}}=R_{1}(t)\left[D(t)R_{2}(t){\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{1}}+{\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{0}}-D(t){\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{0}}\right],

(36)

or, equivalently

D(t)(R_{2}(t){\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{1}}-{\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{0}})=R_{1}^{-1}(t){\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{1}}-{\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{0}},\quad t\geq 0.

(37)

Since
D(t)
is invertible, the left-hand side of (
37
) vanishes if and only if
R_{2}(t)
is constant, that is,
R_{2}(t)=R_{2}(0)
for all
t\geq 0
, with
R_{2}(0)\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}}=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}_{0}}
. In this case, it coincides with the right-hand side of (
37
) if and only if
R_{1}(t)
is constant and satisfies
R_{1}(t)=R_{2}^{-1}(0)
for all
t\geq 0
. The channel
\mathcal{C}(t)
is therefore given by the expression (
13
) in the non-unital case.

Finally, we remark that eq. (
37
) cannot hold whenever one side of the equation is non-null. To prove this, consider the set of points defined by the right-hand side, that is

A=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid(x,y)=R_{1}^{-1}(t){\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{1}}-{\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{0}},\,t\geq 0\}.

(38)

The subset
A
is included in the circle with equation

(x+{\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{0},1})^{2}+(y+{\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{0},2})^{2}=\|{\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{1}}\|^{2}.

(39)

Instead, the set of points defined by the left-hand side is

B=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid(x,y)=D(t)(R_{2}(t){\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{1}}-{\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{0}}),\,t\geq 0\},

(40)

which is a subset of the ellipse with equation

\frac{(x+e^{-a_{1}t}{\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{0},1})^{2}}{e^{-2a_{1}t}}+\frac{(y+e^{-a_{2}t}{\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{0},2})^{2}}{e^{-2a_{2}t}}=\|{\bf v}_{{\bf s}_{1}}\|^{2}.

(41)

Since no point remains in the intersection of
A
and
B
for all
t\geq 0
, eq. (
37
) cannot hold whenever one side is non-null. This completes the proof of the Proposition
1
.

The Markovian rebit channels described by eq. (

(42)

An explicit counterexample showing that the affine component of a Markovian channel need not be Markovian in general is given by

\mathcal{C}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-t}(1+t)&-e^{-t}t\\
e^{-t}t&e^{-t}(1-t)\end{pmatrix}=R(t)\,\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{A}}(t)\,R(t),

One readily checks that

\lambda_{1}(t)=e^{-t}\big(\sqrt{1+t^{2}}+t\big),\qquad\lambda_{2}(t)=e^{-t}\big(\sqrt{1+t^{2}}-t\big).

These functions coincide with those appearing in (

\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{A}}(t)
does not define a Markovian semigroup.

2.3
Classification of unital Markovian rebit channels

The counterexample discussed above motivates the analysis developed in this subsection and in the next, where we do not assume that the affine component
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{A}}(t)
is itself Markovian. In the present subsection we provide a complete classification of
unital
Markovian rebit channels, while in
2.4
we remove the unitality assumption and treat the general case.

The following notation will simplify the subsequent computations:

l_{0}:=\frac{\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)+\dot{\lambda}_{2}(0)}{2},\quad l_{1}:=\frac{\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)-\dot{\lambda}_{2}(0)}{2},\quad l_{3}:=\dot{\theta}_{1}(0)+\dot{\theta}_{2}(0),\quad q:=l_{3}^{2}-l_{1}^{2},

(46)

\sigma_{3}:=\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(47)

In what follows, without loss of generality, we assume that
\dot{\lambda}_{2}(0)\leq\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)
, i.e.

l_{1}\geq 0.

(48)

Since
\lambda_{1}(0)=1
and
\lambda_{1}(t)\leq 1
for all
t\geq 0
by the CP conditions (
5
), one necessarily has

l_{1}+l_{0}=\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)\leq 0.

(49)

This is coherent with assumption (
48
) only if

l_{0}\leq 0.

(50)

Proposition 2
.

Given the notations above and the unital Markovian rebit channel

\mathcal{C}_{0}(t)=R_{1}(t)D(t)R_{2}(t)=\exp\left(\dot{\mathcal{C}_{0}}(0)t\right),

(51)

with
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
, and
D
continuously differentiable, it holds that

\mathcal{C}_{0}(t)=R_{2}^{-1}(0)\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)R_{2}(0),

(52)

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)
has null initial rotation angles and can be written in one of the following ways.

1.

If
q=0
, then

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)=e^{l_{0}t}\left[\sigma_{0}+l_{1}t(\sigma_{1}\pm\sigma_{3})\right].

(53)

2.

If
q<0
, then

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)=e^{l_{0}t}\left[\cosh(t\sqrt{-q})\,\sigma_{0}+\frac{\sinh(t\sqrt{-q})}{\sqrt{-q}}(l_{1}\sigma_{1}+l_{3}\sigma_{3})\right].

(54)

3.

If
q>0
, then

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)=e^{l_{0}t}\left[\cos(t\sqrt{q})\,\sigma_{0}+\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{q})}{\sqrt{q}}(l_{1}\sigma_{1}+l_{3}\sigma_{3})\right].

(55)

In all three cases, the necessary condition (
49
), i.e.
l_{1}+l_{0}\leq 0
, or equivalently
\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)\leq 0
, is also sufficient for the validity of the CP Conditions (
5
).

Proof.

Modulo an orthogonal conjugation, the initial rotation angles can always be assumed to vanish. In fact, eq. (
20
) and the initial condition imply

D(0)=I_{2},\quad\theta_{1}(0)=-\theta_{2}(0),\quad R_{1}(0)=R_{2}^{-1}(0),

(56)

therefore

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t):=R_{2}(0)\,\mathcal{C}_{0}(t)\,R_{2}^{-1}(0)

(57)

is a unital Markovian rebit channel with null initial angles,
\theta_{1}(0)=\theta_{2}(0)=0
, and such that

\mathcal{C}_{0}(t)=R_{2}^{-1}(0)\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)R_{2}(0).

(58)

If we write
D(t)
and
R_{i}(t)
,
i=1,2
, as follows

D(t)=\frac{\lambda_{1}(t)+\lambda_{2}(t)}{2}\sigma_{0}+\frac{\lambda_{1}(t)-\lambda_{2}(t)}{2}\sigma_{1},\quad R_{i}(t)=\cos\theta_{i}(t)\sigma_{0}+\sin\theta_{i}(t)\sigma_{3},

(59)

by direct computation we obtain

\begin{split}\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)&=\frac{\lambda_{1}(t)+\lambda_{2}(t)}{2}\cos\left(\theta_{1}(t)+\theta_{2}(t)\right)\sigma_{0}+\frac{\lambda_{1}(t)-\lambda_{2}(t)}{2}\cos\left(\theta_{1}(t)-\theta_{2}(t)\right)\sigma_{1}\\
&+\frac{\lambda_{1}(t)-\lambda_{2}(t)}{2}\sin\left(\theta_{1}(t)-\theta_{2}(t)\right)\sigma_{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1}(t)+\lambda_{2}(t)}{2}\sin\left(\theta_{1}(t)+\theta_{2}(t)\right)\sigma_{3}.\end{split}

(60)

Assuming that
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
, and
D
are continuously differentiable, by differentiating

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)
at
t=0
, using
\lambda_{1}(0)=\lambda_{2}(0)=1
and

\theta_{1}(0)=\theta_{2}(0)=0
, we can write the generator of
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)
as follows

\dot{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}}_{0}(0)=\frac{\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)+\dot{\lambda}_{2}(0)}{2}\sigma_{0}+\frac{\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)-\dot{\lambda}_{2}(0)}{2}\sigma_{1}+\bigl(\dot{\theta}_{1}(0)+\dot{\theta}_{2}(0)\bigr)\sigma_{3},

(61)

or, taking into account the notation (
46
), as follows

\dot{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(0)=l_{0}\sigma_{0}+l_{1}\sigma_{1}+l_{3}\sigma_{3}.

(62)

The absence of the Pauli matrix
\sigma_{2}
in the expression of the generator imposes a strong structural constraint, which will be exploited in the sequel.

The semigroup generated by
\dot{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(0)
is

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)=\exp\left(\dot{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(0)t\right)=e^{l_{0}t}\exp\left[(l_{1}\sigma_{1}+l_{3}\sigma_{3})t\right].

(63)

Since the matrix
(l_{1}\sigma_{1}+l_{3}\sigma_{3})
belongs to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
, the semigroup
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)
decomposes into the product of a contractive homothety with ratio
\exp(l_{0}t)
(since
l_{0}\leq 0
) and an area-preserving linear map in
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. It can be verified that, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(l_{1}\sigma_{1}+l_{3}\sigma_{3})^{2k}=(-q)^{k}\sigma_{0},\quad(l_{1}\sigma_{1}+l_{3}\sigma_{3})^{2k+1}=(-q)^{k}(l_{1}\sigma_{1}+l_{3}\sigma_{3}),

(64)

which leads to

\exp\left[(l_{1}\sigma_{1}+l_{3}\sigma_{3})t\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-q)^{k}}{(2k)!}t^{2k}\,\sigma_{0}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-q)^{k}}{(2k+1)!}t^{2k+1}(l_{1}\sigma_{1}+l_{3}\sigma_{3}).

(65)

Following
Rubilar and Schultz 2020
, the matrix
(l_{1}\sigma_{1}+l_{3}\sigma_{3})
belongs to one and only one of the adjoint orbits of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
, which are characterized by the sign of
q=l_{3}^{2}-l_{1}^{2}
. This observation and eq. (
65
) lead, after straightforward computations, to the following possible explicit forms of
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)
:

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)=e^{l_{0}t}\cdot\begin{cases}\displaystyle\cos(t\sqrt{q})\,\sigma_{0}+\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{q})}{\sqrt{q}}\,(l_{1}\sigma_{1}+l_{3}\sigma_{3}),&q>0\\[5.16663pt]
\displaystyle\cosh(t\sqrt{-q})\,\sigma_{0}+\frac{\sinh(t\sqrt{-q})}{\sqrt{-q}}\,(l_{1}\sigma_{1}+l_{3}\sigma_{3}),&q<0\\[5.16663pt]
\displaystyle\sigma_{0}+t\,l_{1}(\sigma_{1}\pm\sigma_{3}),&q=0.\end{cases}

(66)

Note in particular that if
l_{1}=l_{3}=0
, then
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)=e^{l_{0}t}\sigma_{0}
. This verifies the explicit expressions of
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)
appearing in Proposition
2
.

The only claim that remains to be established concerns the complete positivity (CP) conditions.
We first observe that, for the unital channel
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)
, the CP conditions (
5
) are equivalent to

\lambda_{1}(t)\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{2}(t)\leq 1,\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

(67)

Indeed,
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)
is invertible for all
t\geq 0
, which implies
\det D(t)\neq 0
, or equivalently
\lambda_{1}(t)\lambda_{2}(t)\neq 0
for all
t\geq 0
. Since
\lambda_{1}(0)=\lambda_{2}(0)=1
, continuity ensures that both
\lambda_{1}(t)
and
\lambda_{2}(t)
remain strictly positive for all
t\geq 0
. Under this condition, the CP constraints (
5
) reduce precisely to the inequalities in (
67
).

In the remainder of the proof, we derive the explicit expressions of
\lambda_{1}(t)
and
\lambda_{2}(t)
by equating (
60
) and (
66
) in each of the three cases distinguished by the sign of
q
. We then show that, in every case, the CP conditions (
67
) are equivalent to the inequality
l_{1}+l_{0}\leq 0
, that is,
\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)\leq 0
.

Case 1

(
q=0
)
.

Suppose
q=l_{3}^{2}-l_{1}^{2}=0
, and consider the expression

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)=e^{l_{0}t}\left(\sigma_{0}+l_{1}t(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{3})\right),

(68)

corresponding to the choice of the positive sign between
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{3}
, the case of the negative sign will be discussed below. Equating this expression with (
60
) yields the system

\begin{cases}(\lambda_{1}(t)+\lambda_{2}(t))\cos\left(\theta_{1}(t)+\theta_{2}(t)\right)=2e^{l_{0}t}\\
(\lambda_{1}(t)-\lambda_{2}(t))\cos\left(\theta_{1}(t)-\theta_{2}(t)\right)=2l_{1}te^{l_{0}t}\\
(\lambda_{1}(t)-\lambda_{2}(t))\sin\left(\theta_{1}(t)-\theta_{2}(t)\right)=0\\
(\lambda_{1}(t)+\lambda_{2}(t))\sin\left(\theta_{1}(t)+\theta_{2}(t)\right)=2l_{1}te^{l_{0}t}.\end{cases}

(69)

We have that
\lambda_{1}(t)=\lambda_{2}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
if and only if
l_{1}=0
and, in this case,
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)=e^{l_{0}t}\sigma_{0}
. Instead, if
l_{1}\neq 0
, the third equation implies
\theta_{1}(t)=\theta_{2}(t)=:\theta(t)
by continuity and the initial condition on
\theta_{i}(t)
. Taking the ratio of the last and the first equation
gives

\theta(t)=\frac{1}{2}\arctan(l_{1}t).

(70)

So,
\cos(2\theta(t))=1/\sqrt{1+(l_{1}t)^{2}}
and by the first equation we obtain

\lambda_{1}(t)+\lambda_{2}(t)=2e^{l_{0}t}\sqrt{1+(l_{1}t)^{2}}.

(71)

Moreover,

\lambda_{1}(t)\lambda_{2}(t)=\det(D(t))=\det(\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t))=\det(e^{l_{0}t}\exp\left[(l_{1}\sigma_{1}+l_{3}\sigma_{3})t\right])=e^{2l_{0}t},

(72)

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{3}
are traceless. (
71
) and (
72
) imply

\lambda_{1}(t)=e^{l_{0}t}\left(\sqrt{1+(l_{1}t)^{2}}+l_{1}t\right),\quad\lambda_{2}(t)=e^{l_{0}t}\left(\sqrt{1+(l_{1}t)^{2}}-l_{1}t\right).

(73)

Let us now show that the necessary condition (
49
), namely
\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)\leq 0
, is also sufficient for complete positivity.
From (
48
) we have
l_{1}\geq 0
, hence
\lambda_{2}(t)\leq\lambda_{1}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
, and it is therefore enough to prove that (
49
) implies
\lambda_{1}(t)\leq 1
for all
t\geq 0
. Since
\lambda_{1}(0)=1
,
\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)\leq 0
, and

\lambda_{1}(t)\to 0
as
t\to+\infty
, it is sufficient to verify that
\dot{\lambda}_{1}(t)
does not vanish for any
t>0
. We have

\begin{split}\dot{\lambda}_{1}(t)&=e^{l_{0}t}\left[l_{0}\left(\sqrt{(l_{1}t)^{2}+1}+l_{1}t\right)+l_{1}\left(\frac{l_{1}t}{\sqrt{(l_{1}t)^{2}+1}}+1\right)\right]\\
&=e^{l_{0}t}\left[l_{0}{\sqrt{(l_{1}t)^{2}+1}}\left(1+\frac{l_{1}t}{\sqrt{(l_{1}t)^{2}+1}}\right)+l_{1}\left(\frac{l_{1}t}{\sqrt{(l_{1}t)^{2}+1}}+1\right)\right]\\
&=e^{l_{0}t}\left[\left(l_{0}{\sqrt{(l_{1}t)^{2}+1}}+l_{1}\right)\left(\frac{l_{1}t}{\sqrt{(l_{1}t)^{2}+1}}+1\right)\right].\end{split}

(74)

The derivative
\dot{\lambda}_{1}(t)
vanishes if and only if
l_{1}=-l_{0}{\sqrt{(l_{1}t)^{2}+1}}
, but from (
50
) we know that
l_{0}\leq 0
, so
\dot{\lambda}_{1}(t)=0
if and only if
l_{1}\geq-l_{0}
, with equality only at
t=0
. However, this violates (
49
) and so
\dot{\lambda}_{1}(t)\neq 0
for all
t>0
.

The case of the negative sign between
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{3}
simply amounts to exchanging
\lambda_{1}(t)
with
\lambda_{2}(t)
and reversing the sign of the angle function
\theta(t)
, without affecting the validity of the argument.

Case 2

(
q<0
)
.

Suppose now
q=l_{3}^{2}-l_{1}^{2}<0
, then

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)=e^{l_{0}t}\left(\cosh(t\sqrt{-q})\,\sigma_{0}+\frac{\sinh(t\sqrt{-q})}{\sqrt{-q}}(l_{1}\sigma_{1}+l_{3}\sigma_{3})\right).

(75)

Note that if
l_{3}=0
, then
q=-l_{1}^{2}<0
and
\sqrt{-q}=l_{1}
.
In this case the generator reduces to
l_{1}\sigma_{1}
, and therefore (
63
) gives

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}e^{(l_{0}+l_{1})t}&0\\
0&e^{(l_{0}-l_{1})t}\end{pmatrix},

(76)

so, if
q<0
and
l_{3}=0
,
\lambda_{1}(t)=e^{(l_{0}+l_{1})t}
and
\lambda_{2}(t)=e^{(l_{0}-l_{1})t}
are both
\leq 1
for all
t\geq 0
if
l_{1}+l_{0}\leq 0
.

If
l_{3}\neq 0
, equating (
75
) with (
60
) yields the system

\begin{cases}\dfrac{\lambda_{1}(t)+\lambda_{2}(t)}{2}\cos\left(\theta_{1}(t)+\theta_{2}(t)\right)=e^{l_{0}t}\cosh(t\sqrt{-q})\\
\dfrac{\lambda_{1}(t)-\lambda_{2}(t)}{2}\cos\left(\theta_{1}(t)-\theta_{2}(t)\right)=\dfrac{l_{1}\sinh(t\sqrt{-q})}{\sqrt{-q}}e^{l_{0}t}\\
\dfrac{\lambda_{1}(t)-\lambda_{2}(t)}{2}\sin\left(\theta_{1}(t)-\theta_{2}(t)\right)=0\\
\dfrac{\lambda_{1}(t)+\lambda_{2}(t)}{2}\sin\left(\theta_{1}(t)+\theta_{2}(t)\right)=\dfrac{l_{3}\sinh(t\sqrt{-q})}{\sqrt{-q}}e^{l_{0}t}.\end{cases}

(77)

We set

S(t)=\frac{\lambda_{1}(t)+\lambda_{2}(t)}{2},\;D(t)=\frac{\lambda_{1}(t)-\lambda_{2}(t)}{2},\;A(t)=\theta_{1}(t)+\theta_{2}(t),\;B(t)=\theta_{1}(t)-\theta_{2}(t).

(78)

Notice that the case
D(t)=0
, i.e.
\lambda_{1}(t)=\lambda_{2}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
would force the second equation of the system to give

l_{1}=0
, which contradicts
q=l_{3}^{2}-l_{1}^{2}<0
, therefore the system can be analysed under the assumption
\lambda_{1}(t)\neq\lambda_{2}(t)
.
We have
\theta_{1}(t)=\theta_{2}(t)=:\theta(t)
, with

\tan(2\theta(t))=\frac{l_{3}}{\sqrt{-q}}\tanh(t\sqrt{-q}).

(79)

Then, the third equation implies
\sin B(t)=0
, that is
\cos B(t)=\pm 1
. The choice of the plus sign is equivalent to the condition
\lambda_{1}(t)\geq\lambda_{2}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
. Without loss of generality, assuming this choice we get

D(t)=\frac{l_{1}\sinh(t\sqrt{-q})}{\sqrt{-q}}e^{l_{0}t}.

(80)

Moreover, by squaring and adding the first and fourth equations, and using
-q=l_{1}^{2}-l_{3}^{2}
and
\cosh^{2}x=1+\sinh^{2}x
, we obtain, after taking the square root,

S(t)=\frac{e^{l_{0}t}}{\sqrt{-q}}\sqrt{l_{1}^{2}\sinh^{2}(t\sqrt{-q})-q}.

(81)

Finally, by adding and subtracting
S(t)
and
D(t)
we get

\lambda_{1}(t)=\frac{e^{l_{0}t}}{\sqrt{-q}}\left[\,\sqrt{\sinh^{2}\bigl(t\sqrt{-q}\bigr)l_{1}^{2}-q}+\sinh\bigl(t\sqrt{-q}\bigr)l_{1}\,\right],

(82)

\lambda_{2}(t)=\frac{e^{l_{0}t}}{\sqrt{-q}}\left[\,\sqrt{\sinh^{2}\bigl(t\sqrt{-q}\bigr)l_{1}^{2}-q}-\sinh\bigl(t\sqrt{-q}\bigr)l_{1}\,\right].

(83)

We now proceed as we have done in the case
q=0
. The derivative
\dot{\lambda}_{1}(t)
in this case vanishes if and only if the function
\dot{f}(t)+l_{0}f(t)
vanishes, where

\displaystyle f(t)=\sqrt{\sinh^{2}(t\sqrt{-q})(l_{1})^{2}-q}+\sinh(t\sqrt{-q})\,l_{1},

(84)

\displaystyle\dot{f}(t)=\frac{(l_{1})^{2}\sqrt{-q}\cosh(t\sqrt{-q})\sinh(t\sqrt{-q})}{\sqrt{\sinh^{2}(t\sqrt{-q})(l_{1})^{2}-q}}+\sqrt{-q}\cosh(t\sqrt{-q})\,l_{1}.

(85)

By setting

\alpha(t)=\sqrt{\sinh^{2}(t\sqrt{-q})(l_{1})^{2}-q},\quad\beta(t)=\sinh(t\sqrt{-q}),\quad\gamma(t)=\cosh(t\sqrt{-q}),

(86)

we have

\begin{split}\dot{f}(t)+l_{0}f(t)&=\frac{l_{1}^{2}}{\alpha(t)}\sqrt{-q}\gamma(t)\beta(t)+l_{1}\sqrt{-q}\gamma(t)+l_{0}\left(\alpha(t)+l_{1}\beta(t)\right)\\
&=l_{1}\sqrt{-q}\gamma(t)\left(1+\frac{l_{1}\beta(t)}{\alpha(t)}\right)+l_{0}\alpha(t)\left(1+\frac{l_{1}\beta(t)}{\alpha(t)}\right)\\
&=\left(1+\frac{l_{1}\beta(t)}{\alpha(t)}\right)\left(l_{1}\sqrt{-q}\gamma(t)+l_{0}\alpha(t)\right).\end{split}

(87)

Since

1+\frac{l_{1}\beta(t)}{\alpha(t)}=1+\frac{l_{1}\sinh(t\sqrt{-q})}{\sqrt{\sinh^{2}(t\sqrt{-q})(l_{1})^{2}-q}}\neq 0,

(88)

the derivative
\dot{\lambda}_{1}(t)
vanishes if and only if
l_{1}\sqrt{-q}\gamma(t)+l_{0}\alpha(t)
vanishes. Writing

l_{1}\sqrt{-q}\gamma(t)+l_{0}\alpha(t)=l_{1}\sqrt{-q}\sqrt{\sinh^{2}(t\sqrt{-q})+1}+l_{0}\sqrt{-q}\sqrt{\frac{\sinh^{2}(t\sqrt{-q})(l_{1})^{2}}{-q}+1},

(89)

if
t\geq 0
verifies
\dot{\lambda}_{1}(t)=0
, then

l_{1}\sqrt{\sinh^{2}(t\sqrt{-q})+1}=-l_{0}\sqrt{\frac{\sinh^{2}(t\sqrt{-q})(l_{1})^{2}}{-q}+1}.

(90)

But
q=l_{3}^{2}-l_{1}^{2}<0
implies
-l_{1}^{2}/q\geq 1
and consequently
l_{1}\geq-l_{0}
, with equality only if
t=0
, since
l_{3}\neq 0
.

Case 3

(
q>0
)
.

Finally, suppose
q=l_{3}^{2}-l_{1}^{2}>0
, then

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)=e^{l_{0}t}\left(\cos(t\sqrt{q})\,\sigma_{0}+\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{q})}{\sqrt{q}}(l_{1}\sigma_{1}+l_{3}\sigma_{3})\right).

(91)

Equating this expression with (
60
) yields the system

\begin{cases}\dfrac{\lambda_{1}(t)+\lambda_{2}(t)}{2}\cos\left(\theta_{1}(t)+\theta_{2}(t)\right)=\cos(t\sqrt{q})e^{l_{0}t}\\
\dfrac{\lambda_{1}(t)-\lambda_{2}(t)}{2}\cos\left(\theta_{1}(t)-\theta_{2}(t)\right)=\dfrac{l_{1}\sin(t\sqrt{q})}{\sqrt{q}}e^{l_{0}t}\\
\dfrac{\lambda_{1}(t)-\lambda_{2}(t)}{2}\sin\left(\theta_{1}(t)-\theta_{2}(t)\right)=0\\
\dfrac{\lambda_{1}(t)+\lambda_{2}(t)}{2}\sin\left(\theta_{1}(t)+\theta_{2}(t)\right)=\dfrac{l_{3}\sin(t\sqrt{q})}{\sqrt{q}}e^{l_{0}t}.\end{cases}

(92)

From the second and the third equation, it follows that
\lambda_{1}(t)=\lambda_{2}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
if and only if
l_{1}=0
. In this case
q=l_{3}^{2}>0
,
\sqrt{q}=|l_{3}|
, and from (
66
) we get

\begin{split}\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)&=e^{l_{0}t}\left(\cos(|l_{3}|t)\,\sigma_{0}+\sin(|l_{3}|t)\,\sigma_{3}\right)\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}e^{l_{0}t}&0\\
0&e^{l_{0}t}\end{pmatrix}R(|l_{3}|t)=\begin{pmatrix}e^{l_{0}t}&0\\
0&e^{l_{0}t}\end{pmatrix}R(|\dot{\theta}_{1}(0)+\dot{\theta}_{2}(0)|t),\end{split}

(93)

so, if
q>0
and
l_{1}=0
,
\lambda_{1}(t)=\lambda_{2}(t)=e^{l_{0}t}\leq 1
for all
t\geq 0
.

We assume now that
l_{1}\neq 0
, so
\lambda_{1}(t)\neq\lambda_{2}(t)
and
\theta_{1}(t)=\theta_{2}(t)=:\theta(t)
, with

\tan(2\theta(t))=\frac{l_{3}}{\sqrt{q}}\tan(t\sqrt{q}).

(94)

Solving the system as in the case
q<0
, we obtain

\lambda_{1}(t)=\frac{e^{l_{0}t}}{\sqrt{q}}\left[\sqrt{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{q})(l_{1})^{2}+q}+\sin(t\sqrt{q})\,l_{1}\right],

(95)

\lambda_{2}(t)=\frac{e^{l_{0}t}}{\sqrt{q}}\left[\sqrt{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{q})(l_{1})^{2}+q}-\sin(t\sqrt{q})\,l_{1}\right].

(96)

Contrary to the previous situations, it is no longer true that
\lambda_{1}(t)\geq\lambda_{2}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
, see Figure
3
for an illustration. It follows that, in this case, we must consider both scaling functions.

\lambda_{1}(t)
and
\lambda_{2}(t)
in (

) and (

), with

.

We have that
\dot{\lambda}_{1}(t)
vanishes if and only if

\left(1+\frac{l_{1}\beta(t)}{\alpha(t)}\right)\left(l_{1}\sqrt{q}\gamma(t)+l_{0}\alpha(t)\right)=0.

(97)

where

\alpha(t)=\sqrt{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{q})l_{1}^{2}+q},\quad\beta(t)=\sin(t\sqrt{q}),\quad\gamma(t)=\cos(t\sqrt{q}).

(98)

Since

1+\frac{l_{1}\beta(t)}{\alpha(t)}=1+\frac{l_{1}\sin(t\sqrt{q})}{\sqrt{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{q})l_{1}^{2}+q}}\neq 0,

(99)

\dot{\lambda}_{1}(t)
vanishes if and only if

l_{1}=-l_{0}\frac{1}{\cos(t\sqrt{q})}\sqrt{\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{q})l_{1}^{2}}{q}+1}.

(100)

If
t\geq 0
is such that
\dot{\lambda}_{1}(t)=0
, then
\cos(t\sqrt{q})>0
and
l_{1}\geq-l_{0}
, with equality if and only if
t\sqrt{q}=0
modulo
2\pi
. But at these points

\lambda_{1}\left(\frac{2k\pi}{\sqrt{q}}\right)=\exp\left(l_{0}\frac{2k\pi}{\sqrt{q}}\right)\leq 1,

(101)

with equality if and only if
k=0
, i.e.
t=0
. In the same way
\dot{\lambda}_{2}(t)
vanishes if and only if

\left(1-\frac{l_{1}\beta(t)}{\alpha(t)}\right)\left(l_{0}\alpha(t)-l_{1}\sqrt{q}\gamma(t)\right)=0,

(102)

that is, if and only if

l_{1}=l_{0}\frac{1}{\cos(t\sqrt{q})}\sqrt{\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{q})l_{1}^{2}}{q}+1}.

(103)

If
t\geq 0
is such that
\dot{\lambda}_{2}(t)=0
, then
\cos(t\sqrt{q})<0
and
l_{1}\geq-l_{0}
, with equality if and only if
t\sqrt{q}=\pi
modulo
2\pi
. But at these points

\lambda_{2}\left(\frac{\pi+2k\pi}{\sqrt{q}}\right)=\exp\left(l_{0}\frac{\pi+2k\pi}{\sqrt{q}}\right)<1.

(104)

With the analysis of the three cases
q=0,q<0,
and
q>0
, Proposition
2
is fully proven.
∎

,

2.4
Classification of Markovian rebit channels: the general case

In the previous subsection, we classified unital Markovian rebit channels, as stated in Proposition
2
. Here, we extend that result to include the non-unital case, thereby obtaining the most general classification of Markovian rebit channels.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}(t)=R_{1}(t)\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{A}}(t)R_{2}(t)
be a Markovian rebit channel with

{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{A}}(t)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}})=D(t)\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}+{\bf w}(t),\quad\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}\in\cal D,

(106)

where
D(t)=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1}(t),\lambda_{2}(t))
and
{\bf w(t)}\in\cal D
for all
t\geq 0
.
Assume that the scaling functions
\lambda_{1}(t)
and
\lambda_{2}(t)
and
{\bf w}(t)
satisfy the CP conditions in eq. (
5
) for all
t\geq 0
and that
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
, and
D
are continuously differentiable.
Then, there exist a unital Markovian rebit channel

\mathcal{C}_{0}(t)=R_{2}^{-1}(0)R(t)D(t)R(t)R_{2}(0)

(107)

belonging to the classification appearing in Proposition
2
and Corollary
2.1
, and
{\bf W}(t)\in\cal D
for all
t\geq 0
, with
{\bf W}(0)=\bf 0
, such that the action of
\mathcal{C}(t)
can be written as

{\mathcal{C}}(t)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}})={\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}})+{\bf W}(t),\quad\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}\in\cal D,

(108)

with

R_{1}(t)=R_{2}^{-1}(0)R(t),\quad R_{2}(t)=R(t)R_{2}(0),

(109)

and, whenever
\dot{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(0)
is invertible,

{\bf W}(t)=R_{2}^{-1}(0)R(t){\bf w}(t)=\left[\dot{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(0)\right]^{-1}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}(t)(\dot{\bf W}(0))-\dot{\bf W}(0)\right),

(110)

otherwise,
{\bf W}(t)=\bf 0
for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

By eq. (
6
), the general action of a Markovian rebit channel is

{\mathcal{C}}(t)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}})={\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}})+R_{1}(t){\bf w}(t).

(111)

By (
58
) and Corollary
2.1
, the unital Markovian channel
\mathcal{C}_{0}(t)=R_{1}(t)D(t)R_{2}(t)
is given by

\mathcal{C}_{0}(t)=R_{2}^{-1}(0)\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(t)R_{2}(0)=R_{2}^{-1}(0)R(t)D(t)R(t)R_{2}(0).

(112)

In homogeneous coordinates, the affine action of
\mathcal{C}(t)
is represented by the
3\times 3
matrix

C(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathbf{0}^{T}\\
{\bf W}(t)&\mathcal{C}_{0}(t)\end{pmatrix},

(113)

where
{\bf W}(t)=R_{2}^{-1}(0)R(t){\bf w}(t)
is such that

\mathcal{C}(t)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}})=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathbf{0}^{T}\\
{\bf W}(t)&\mathcal{C}_{0}(t)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}\end{pmatrix}.

(114)

Since
{\bf w}(0)=\mathbf{0}
, the infinitesimal generator
\dot{\mathcal{C}}(0)
of the channel
\mathcal{C}(t)
is then given by the matrix

\dot{\mathcal{C}}(0)=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbf{0}^{T}\\
\dot{{\bf W}}(0)&\dot{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(0)\end{pmatrix},

(115)

with
\dot{{\bf W}}(0)=R_{2}^{-1}(0)R(0){\dot{\bf w}}(0)
. It can be checked the powers of
\dot{\mathcal{C}}(0)
can be written as

\left[\dot{\mathcal{C}}(0)\right]^{k}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbf{0}^{T}\\
[\dot{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(0)]^{k-1}(\dot{{\bf W}}(0))&[\dot{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(0)]^{k}\end{pmatrix},

(116)

thus the exponential series gives

{\bf W}(t)=\left[\dot{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(0)\right]^{-1}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}(t)-I_{2}\right)\dot{\bf W}(0),

(117)

provided that
\dot{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(0)
is invertible. By (
62
), we have

\det\left(\dot{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(0)\right)=\det\left(\dot{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(0)\right)=0

(118)

if and only if
\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)\dot{\lambda}_{2}(0)+l_{3}^{2}=0
. If the CP conditions hold, then
\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)\dot{\lambda}_{2}(0)\geq 0
and so the determinant is null if and only if
\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)\dot{\lambda}_{2}(0)=l_{3}=0
. But then that at least one of
\lambda_{1}(t)
and
\lambda_{2}(t)
is 1 for all
t\geq 0
, so the CP conditions imply
{\bf W}(t)=\bf 0
for all
t\geq 0
.
∎

and

,

, or

and

3
Markovian rebit channels and GKSL master equation

As already noted in Remark
1
, deriving explicit constraints on
{\bf w}(t)
and on the scaling functions
\lambda_{i}(t)
,
i=1,2
, that guarantee complete positivity is not straightforward, even in the simplified setting where the affine components are Markovian.

Here we show that it is possible to obtain a simple characterisation of complete positivity entirely determined by the parameters
\dot{\lambda}_{i}(0)
and
\dot{w}_{i}(0)
,
i=1,2
. To this end, we must establish a connection between the analysis of the previous section and the GKSL master equation.

We start quoting the following theorem, see e.g.
Breuer and Petruccione 2002
;
Gorini et al. 1976
;
Lindblad 1976
, and then we adapt it to rebit systems.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a complex Hilbert space of dimension
d\geq 2
. We denote
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
the sets of trace-class and bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
be a linear operator. Then,
\mathcal{L}
is the infinitesimal generator of a completely positive dynamical semigroup on
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
if and only if there exist:

•

H\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
with
H^{\dagger}=H
and Tr
(H)=0

•

F_{k}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, with Tr
(F_{k})=0
and Tr
(F_{k}F_{\ell}^{\dagger})=\delta_{k\ell}
for all
k,\ell=1,\dots,d^{2}-1

•

\mathcal{M}=\left(\alpha_{k\ell}\right)_{k,\ell=1}^{d^{2}-1}
, a self-adjoint positive semi-definite matrix,

such that for all
X
in
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
, we have:

\mathcal{L}(X)=-i\left[H,X\right]+\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{d^{2}-1}\alpha_{k,\ell}\left(2F_{k}XF_{\ell}^{\dagger}-\{F_{\ell}^{\dagger}F_{k},X\}\right).

(119)

\mathcal{L}
is referred to as the
Lindbladian
,
M
and
F_{k}
are called
Lindblad matrix
and
operators
, respectively, and
\{A,B\}:=AB+BA
is the anti-commutator.

The differential equation

\dot{X}(t)=\mathcal{L}(X(t)),\quad t\geq 0,

(120)

is the GSKL master equation.

If
X
is a density matrix
\rho
of a quantum system with Hilbert space
\cal H
and Hamiltonian
H
, and if all the coefficients
\alpha_{k\ell}
vanish, then the GKSL master equation reduces to the Liouville-von Neumann equation:
\dot{\rho}(t)=-i[H,\rho(t)]
. For this reason, the first term of the Lindbladian in (
119
) is associated with
unitary evolution
, while the second term, called
dissipator
, takes into account the interaction between the system and its environment.

For a single rebit:
\mathcal{H}=\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}^{2}
and
X(t)=\rho_{\bf s}(t)=(\sigma_{0}+s_{1}(t)\sigma_{1}+s_{2}(t)\sigma_{2})/2
, so the presence of the imaginary unit in (
119
) lacks a fundamental justification and must be replaced in order to obtain a Linbladian term responsible for the
orthogonal evolution
of the rebit.

Since the only meaningful orthogonal dynamics of
\rho_{\bf s}
is given by a rotation of the Bloch vector
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}(t)=(s_{1}(t),s_{2}(t))
, we replace the term
-i[H,X]
in (
119
) with
[\omega\sigma_{3},\rho_{\bf s}]
, with
\omega\in\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}
.

Indeed, if one retains only this contribution, the GKSL equation reduces to

\dot{\rho}_{\bf s}(t)=\frac{1}{2}(\dot{s}_{1}(t)\sigma_{1}+\dot{s}_{2}(t)\sigma_{2})=[\omega\sigma_{3},\rho_{\bf s}(t)]=\omega(-s_{2}(t)\sigma_{1}+s_{1}(t)\sigma_{2}),

(121)

which yields the differential system

\begin{cases}\dot{s}_{1}(t)=-\omega s_{2}(t)\\
\dot{s}_{2}(t)=\omega s_{1}(t),\end{cases}

(122)

solved by

\begin{cases}s_{1}(t)=s_{1}(0)\cos(\omega t)-s_{2}(0)\sin(\omega t)\\
s_{2}(t)=s_{2}(0)\cos(\omega t)+s_{1}(0)\sin(\omega t),\end{cases}

(123)

thereby showing that the bracket
[\omega\sigma_{3},\rho_{\bf s}]
generates an orthogonal evolution.

A further, independent justification for this choice follows from the observation that
\rho_{\bf s}
belongs to the Jordan algebra
\mathcal{H}(2,\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$})
of symmetric
2\times 2
real matrices, endowed with the Jordan product defined by half the anti-commutator, see e.g.
Baez 2012
for further information.
As proven in
Yao et al. 2019
, the only derivations, namely Leibniz-type endomorphisms, of
\mathcal{H}(2,\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$})
act precisely through the bracket
[\omega\sigma_{3},\rho_{\bf s}]
.

Let us now analyse the dissipator of the Lindbladian for a rebit. A natural choice for the Lindblad operators is the following family of traceless matrices:

F_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sigma_{k},\quad k=1,2,3,

(124)

in fact,
\text{Tr}(F_{k}F_{\ell}^{T})=\delta_{k\ell}
. With direct computations, we obtain that the diagonal terms of
\cal L
, i.e.

{\mathcal{L}}_{k}(\rho_{\bf s}):=2F_{k}\rho_{\bf s}F_{k}^{T}-\{F_{k}^{T}F_{k},\rho_{\bf s}\},\quad k=1,2,3,

(125)

are given by

\mathcal{L}_{1}(\rho_{\bf s})=-s_{2}\sigma_{2},\quad\mathcal{L}_{2}(\rho_{\bf s})=-s_{1}\sigma_{1},\quad\mathcal{L}_{3}(\rho_{\bf s})=-(s_{1}\sigma_{1}+s_{2}\sigma_{2}).

(126)

Similarly, the off-diagonal terms of
\cal L

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{k,\ell}(\rho_{\bf s}):={\mathcal{L}}_{k,\ell}(\rho_{\bf s})+{\mathcal{L}}_{\ell,k}(\rho_{\bf s}),

(127)

with

{\mathcal{L}}_{k,\ell}(\rho_{\bf s})=2F_{k}\rho_{\bf s}F_{\ell}^{T}-\{F_{k}^{T}F_{\ell},\rho_{\bf s}\},\quad{\mathcal{L}}_{\ell,k}(\rho_{\bf s})=2F_{\ell}\rho_{\bf s}F_{k}^{T}-\{F_{\ell}^{T}F_{k},\rho_{\bf s}\},

(128)

k,\ell=1,2,3
,
k\neq\ell
, are given by

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1,2}(\rho_{\bf s})=s_{2}\sigma_{1}+s_{1}\sigma_{2},\quad\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1,3}(\rho_{\bf s})=2\sigma_{2},\quad\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{2,3}(\rho_{\bf s})=-2\sigma_{1}.

(129)

Proposition 5
.

The CP conditions (
5
) are satisfied if and only if there
exist
\alpha_{i,j}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}
,
i,j=1,2,3
, satisfying

\begin{cases}\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)=-2(\alpha_{2,2}+\alpha_{3,3})\\
\dot{\lambda}_{2}(0)=-2(\alpha_{1,1}+\alpha_{3,3})\\
\dot{w}_{1}(0)=-4\alpha_{2,3}\\
\dot{w}_{2}(0)=4\alpha_{1,3},\end{cases}

(130)

and such that the matrix

\mathcal{M}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1,1}&0&\alpha_{1,3}\\
0&\alpha_{2,2}&\alpha_{2,3}\\
\alpha_{1,3}&\alpha_{2,3}&\alpha_{3,3}\end{pmatrix}

(131)

is positive semi-definite.

Proof.

The key to proving the proposition lies in comparing the dynamics of a rebit density matrix
\rho_{\mathbf{s}}
under the action of a Markovian channel
\mathcal{C}(t)
with its evolution as a solution of the GKSL equation with Lindbladian
\mathcal{L}
.

Since
\mathcal{C}(t)
is completely positive if and only if
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(t)=R_{2}(0)\mathcal{C}(t)R_{2}^{-1}(0)
is, it is enough to determine the complete positivity conditions for the semigroup

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(t)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}})={\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}}_{0}(t)\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}+\widetilde{\bf w}(t),

(132)

with
\widetilde{\bf w}(t)=R(t){\bf w}(t).
We have

\dot{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}}(0)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}})=\dot{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}}_{0}(0)\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}+R(0)\dot{\bf w}(0),

(133)

which, using eq. (
62
), can be rewritten as

\dot{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}}(0)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}})=\begin{pmatrix}l_{0}+l_{1}&-l_{3}\\
l_{3}&l_{0}-l_{1}\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}+R(0)\dot{\bf w}(0).

(134)

By Corollary
2.1
,
\theta_{1}(t)=\theta_{2}(t)=\theta(t)
,
\dot{\theta}(0)=\omega
and
\theta(0)=0
, so
l_{3}=2\dot{\theta}(0)
and

\dot{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}}(0)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}})=\begin{pmatrix}\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)&-2\dot{\theta}(0)\\
2\dot{\theta}(0)&\dot{\lambda}_{2}(0)\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}+\dot{\bf w}(0).

(135)

If
\rho_{\mathbf{s}}(t)
evolves under the action of the channel
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(t)
, then

\rho_{\bf s}(t)=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{0}+s_{1}(t)\sigma_{1}+s_{2}(t)\sigma_{2})=\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(t)\rho_{\bf s}(0)=e^{\dot{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}}(0)t}\rho_{\bf s},

(136)

which gives

\dot{\rho}_{\bf s}(t)=\frac{\dot{s}_{1}(t)\sigma_{1}+\dot{s}_{2}(t)\sigma_{2}}{2}=\dot{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}}(0)\rho_{\bf s}(t)=\dot{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}}(0)\frac{(\sigma_{0}+s_{1}(t)\sigma_{1}+s_{2}(t)\sigma_{2})}{2},

(137)

that, thanks to eq. (
135
), can be written as the following differential system

\begin{cases}\dot{s}_{1}(t)=\dot{\lambda}_{1}(0)s_{1}(t)-2\dot{\theta}(0)s_{2}(t)+\dot{w}_{1}(0)\\
\dot{s}_{2}(t)=2\dot{\theta}(0)s_{1}(t)+\dot{\lambda}_{2}(0)s_{2}(t)+\dot{w}_{2}(0),\end{cases}

(138)

with
s_{1}(0)=s_{1}
, and
s_{2}(0)=s_{2}
.

Now we describe the evolution of
\rho_{\bf s}
via the GKSL master equation

\dot{\rho}_{\bf s}(t)=\frac{\dot{s}_{1}(t)\sigma_{1}+\dot{s}_{2}(t)\sigma_{2}}{2}=\mathcal{L}(\rho_{\bf s}(t)),

(139)

with Lindbladian

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(\rho_{\bf s})&=[\dot{\theta}(0)\sigma_{3},\rho_{\bf s}]+\sum_{k=1}^{3}\alpha_{k,k}\mathcal{L}_{k}(\rho_{\bf s})+\alpha_{1,3}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1,3}(\rho_{\bf s})+\alpha_{2,3}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{2,3}(\rho_{\bf s})\\
&=-\left((\alpha_{2,2}+\alpha_{3,3})s_{1}(t)+2\alpha_{2,3}+\dot{\theta}(0)s_{2}(t)\right)\sigma_{1}\\
&\quad-\left((\alpha_{1,1}+\alpha_{3,3})s_{2}(t)-2\alpha_{1,3}-\dot{\theta}(0)s_{1}(t)\right)\sigma_{2}.\end{split}

(140)

Using eqs. (
126
) and (
129
), we get the differential system

\begin{cases}\dot{s}_{1}=-2(\alpha_{2,2}+\alpha_{3,3})s_{1}(t)-4\alpha_{2,3}-2\dot{\theta}(0)s_{2}(t)\\
\dot{s}_{2}=-2(\alpha_{1,1}+\alpha_{3,3})s_{2}(t)+4\alpha_{1,3}+2\dot{\theta}(0)s_{1}(t),\end{cases}

(141)

with
s_{1}(0)=s_{1}
, and
s_{2}(0)=s_{2}
. Comparing the two differential systems that we have obtained, we have that
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(t)
is a Markovian rebit channel if and only if the statement of the theorem holds. ∎

4
Markovian rebit channels and colour perception

Besides its intrinsic theoretical interest, the analysis of Markovian rebit channels presented in the previous sections has a relevance in the study of the effect of illuminant changes on human colour perception, which is the main motivation behind our theoretical analysis.

The mathematical model presented in
Berthier and
Provenzi 2022
;
Berthier et al. 2022
, describes perceived colours as the result of quantum measurements performed by quantum effects on rebit states. If
\rho_{\bf s}
is a density matrix, then we can write

\rho_{\bf s}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\sigma_{0}+r\cos\vartheta\left(\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(1,0)}-\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(-1,0)}\right)+r\sin\vartheta\left(\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(0,1)}-\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(0,-1)}\right)\right],

(142)

with
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}=(s_{1},s_{2})=(r\cos\vartheta,r\sin\vartheta)
,
r\in[0,1]
,
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
.

The state
\rho_{0}:=\sigma_{0}/2
is the state of maximal von Neumann entropy which is natural to assume as the
achromatic state
. Moreover, since orthogonal pure states are represented by antipodal points on the boundary of the Bloch disk,
(\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(1,0)},\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(-1,0)}
) and
(\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(0,1)},\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(0,-1)})
are two pairs of mutually exclusive states.

These considerations show that the representation of
\rho_{\bf s}
by eq. (
142
) is the exact quantum analogue of Hering’s representation of colour
Berthier 2020
: the
chromatic state

\rho_{\bf s}
decomposes as a sum of an
achromatic state

\rho_{0}
with
two chromatic opponency axes

\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(1,0)}
vs.
\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(-1,0)}
and
\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(0,1)}
vs.
\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(0,-1)}
, with respective
degrees of opponency
given by
r\cos\vartheta
and
r\sin\vartheta
.

The pure chromatic states
\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(1,0)}
,
\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(-1,0)}
,
\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(0,1)}
, and
\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(0,-1)}
are
the four unique hues
, usually denominated as red, green, yellow and blue.

Since
\sigma_{1}=\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(1,0)}-\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(-1,0)}
and
\sigma_{2}=\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(0,1)}-\rho_{\mathbf{s}}{(0,-1)}
, the chromatic opponency is encoded by the two Pauli matrices
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
.

A quantum effect of the rebit is represented by a matrix

\eta_{\bf e}=\begin{pmatrix}e_{0}+e_{1}&e_{2}\\
e_{2}&e_{0}-e_{1}\end{pmatrix}=2e_{0}\rho{(e_{1}/e_{0},e_{2}/e_{0})},

(143)

with
0<e_{0}\leq 1
, and such that the effect vector
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{e}}=(e_{1}/e_{0},e_{2}/e_{0})
belongs to the Bloch disk. Given an effect
\eta_{\bf e}
and a density matrix
\rho_{\bf s}
, one can perform a Lüders measurement, see e.g.
Kraus et al. 1983
;
Busch et al. 1997
, that gives rise to the following post-measurement generalised state

\psi_{\bf e}(\mathbf{s}):=\eta_{\bf e}^{1/2}\rho_{\bf s}\eta_{\bf e}^{1/2}=\langle{\bf e}\rangle_{\bf s}\frac{\psi_{\bf e}(\mathbf{s})}{\langle{\bf e}\rangle_{\bf s}}=:\langle{\bf e}\rangle_{\bf s}\varphi_{\bf e}(\mathbf{s}),

(144)

where
\varphi_{\bf e}(\mathbf{s})
is the post-measurement chromatic state, and

0\leq\langle{\bf e}\rangle_{\bf s}=e_{0}(1+{\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{e}}}\cdot{\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}})\leq 1

(145)

is the expectation value of the measurement.

In the colour model, chromatic states are associated with the preparation of a visual scene and effects represent observers. Thus,
the generalized state
\psi_{\bf e}(\mathbf{s})
is the colour perceived by the observer
{\bf e}
from a visual scene prepared in the chromatic state
\mathbf{s}
. It is shown in
Berthier and
Provenzi 2022
that

{\bf v}_{\varphi_{\mathbf{e}}(\mathbf{s})}=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{e}}\hbox{\hskip 6.94438pt\hskip-6.94438pt\hbox{$\,\circlearrowleft\,$}\hskip-6.94438pt\hskip-3.8889pt\raisebox{0.0pt}{\hbox{$+$}}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip 6.94438pt}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}},

(146)

where
{\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{e}}}{\hbox{\hskip 6.94438pt\hskip-6.94438pt\hbox{$\,\circlearrowleft\,$}\hskip-6.94438pt\hskip-3.8889pt\raisebox{0.0pt}{\hbox{$+$}}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip 6.94438pt}}{\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}}
is the relativistic sum of the two vectors: if
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{e}}=\bf 0
then
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{e}}\hbox{\hskip 6.94438pt\hskip-6.94438pt\hbox{$\,\circlearrowleft\,$}\hskip-6.94438pt\hskip-3.8889pt\raisebox{0.0pt}{\hbox{$+$}}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip 6.94438pt}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}:=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}
, otherwise

{\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{e}}}\hbox{\hskip 6.94438pt\hskip-6.94438pt\hbox{$\,\circlearrowleft\,$}\hskip-6.94438pt\hskip-3.8889pt\raisebox{0.0pt}{\hbox{$+$}}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip 6.94438pt}{\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}}:=\frac{1}{1+{\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{e}}}\cdot{\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}}}\left[{\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{e}}}+\frac{{\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}}}{\gamma_{\mathbf{e}}}+\frac{\gamma_{\bf e}({\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{e}}}\cdot{\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}}){\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{e}}}}{1+\gamma_{\bf e}}\right],

(147)

with

\gamma_{\bf e}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\|{\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{e}}}\|^{2}}}.

(148)

This new description of perceived colours makes it possible to give well-founded mathematical definitions of their perceptual attributes: brightness, lightness, saturation, colourfulness, chroma, and hue, see
Berthier et al. 2022
for details. It also enables the efficient implementation of white-balance algorithms by means of Lorentz boosts, see
Berthier et al. 2024
.

A crucial concept in quantum information is the
relative entropy
between two quantum states
{\bf s}
and
{\bf t}
. It is defined as

R(\rho_{{\bf s}}||\rho_{{\bf t}}):={\rm Tr}\left(\rho_{{\bf s}}\log_{2}\rho_{{\bf s}}-\rho_{{\bf s}}\log_{2}\rho_{{\bf t}}\right),

(149)

and it represents an important measure of distinguishability between states. In the specific case of a rebit, the relative entropy has an analytical expression given by
Cortese 2002

R(\rho_{{\bf s}}||\rho_{{\bf t}})=\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}(1-r_{\bf s}^{2})+\frac{r_{\bf s}}{2}\log_{2}\left(\frac{1+r_{\bf s}}{1-r_{\bf s}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}(1-r_{\bf t}^{2})-\frac{r_{\bf s}\cos\theta_{{\bf s},{\bf t}}}{2}\log_{2}\left(\frac{1+r_{\bf t}}{1-r_{\bf t}}\right).

(150)

The interplay between quantum relative entropy and quantum channels is encoded by the well-known Data Processing Inequality (DPI), see e.g.
Wilde 2017
;
Witten 2020
,

R(\mathcal{C}(\rho_{\bf s})\,\|\,\mathcal{C}(\rho_{\bf t}))\leq R(\rho_{\bf s}\,\|\,\rho_{\bf t}),\quad\forall\,\mathbf{s},{\bf t},

(151)

which implies that channels tend to decrease the distinguishability between quantum states.

In the following subsections, we analyse the role of Markovian rebit channels within the colour perception model outlined above.

4.1
Illuminant channels and chromatic distortion

Let us consider a visual scene prepared in such a way that an observer
\mathbf{e}
is exposed to the chromatic state
\mathbf{s}
of a colour stimulus in a room illuminated by an
achromatic
illuminant
\imath_{a}
. Physically,
\imath_{a}
corresponds to a light source with a broadband spectrum. Mathematically,
\imath_{a}
is described by a null Bloch vector
{\bf v}_{\imath_{a}}=\mathbf{0}
.

Empirically, after a sufficiently long exposure, the observer
\mathbf{e}
becomes adapted to
\imath_{a}
, in the sense that the observer’s effect vector vanishes. Denoting by
\mathbf{e}_{a}
an observer adapted to an achromatic illuminant, one has
{\bf v}_{\mathbf{e}_{a}}=\mathbf{0}
and therefore
{\bf v}_{\mathbf{e}_{a}}\hbox{\hskip 6.94438pt\hskip-6.94438pt\hbox{$\,\circlearrowleft\,$}\hskip-6.94438pt\hskip-3.8889pt\raisebox{0.0pt}{\hbox{$+$}}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip 6.94438pt}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}
. Consequently,
\mathbf{e}_{a}
perceives chromatic states as they intrinsically are, namely
\varphi_{\mathbf{e}_{a}}(\mathbf{s})=\mathbf{s}
.

Suppose now that the scene preparation is altered by replacing
\imath_{a}
with a
non-neutral
illuminant
\imath
, with Bloch vector
{\bf v}_{\imath}\neq\mathbf{0}
. Empirically, the observer’s colour perception is reported to undergo a sudden distortion for two reasons: first, chromatic states are no longer perceived in the same manner as before; second, the ability to discriminate between colours is reduced.

This condition persists for a finite amount of time, until the chromatic adaptation mechanisms of the human visual system intervene to counteract the distortion, progressively steering perception toward a new adapted regime.

Thus, chromatic distortion can be understood as a perceptual phenomenon arising from a change in the overall configuration of the scene (the original chromatic state of the stimulus perceived under a novel illuminant), to which the observer is not yet adapted. Accordingly, at the mathematical level, it is natural to model chromatic distortion by means of a dynamical map acting
on states
, and chromatic adaptation by one acting
on effects
.

Here, we focus on modelling chromatic distortion by means of Markovian rebit channels. The use of quantum channels is motivated by the fact that they capture the two key features highlighted above: they are dynamical maps acting on rebit states and, by virtue of the DPI (
151
), they necessarily reduce state distinguishability. The restriction to Markovian dynamics is further justified by the validity, in the present context, of the Born assumptions underlying the GKSL equation, namely the weak coupling between the system and its environment
Auletta et al. 2009
. Indeed, the illuminant influences colour perception by the observer, whereas the observer’s perceptual state does not affect the illuminant.

In what follows, the four unique hues are assumed to be fixed, thereby defining a reference frame for colour perception. Accordingly, we restrict our attention to Markovian rebit channels
\mathcal{C}(t)
of the form (
6
) with
R_{1}(t)=R_{2}(t)=I_{2}
for all
t\geq 0
. More general scenarios could in principle be envisaged, however their analysis from the viewpoint of colour perception becomes substantially more involved.

We denote by
\mathbf{s}(t)
the evolution of an initial chromatic state
\mathbf{s}
under the action of
\mathcal{C}(t)
, with
\mathbf{s}(0)=\mathbf{s}
. Accordingly,

\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}(t):=\mathcal{C}(t)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}),\quad t\geq 0,

(152)

is the Bloch vector of the density matrix
\rho_{{\mathbf{s}}(t)}
, with
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}(0)=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}
and
\rho_{{\mathbf{s}}(0)}=\rho_{\mathbf{s}}
.

Before chromatic adaptation takes place, the observer remains adapted to the achromatic illuminant, so that
\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{e}_{a}
and

{\bf v}_{\mathbf{e}_{a}}\hbox{\hskip 6.94438pt\hskip-6.94438pt\hbox{$\,\circlearrowleft\,$}\hskip-6.94438pt\hskip-3.8889pt\raisebox{0.0pt}{\hbox{$+$}}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip 6.94438pt}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}(t)=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}(t),\qquad t\geq 0.

(153)

Consequently, the corresponding evolution of the post-measurement chromatic state is given by

\varphi^{t}_{\mathbf{e}_{a}}(\mathbf{s})=\rho_{\mathbf{s}(t)},\qquad t\geq 0.

(154)

A distinctive feature of chromatic distortion induced by an illuminant change is that, during the distortion phase, achromatic colour stimuli are no longer perceived as such, but instead
acquire a chromatic component resembling that of the illuminant
. This entails that the channel
\mathcal{C}(t)
must induce a displacement of the centre of the Bloch disk, and therefore cannot be unital.

From eqs. (
126
) and (
129
) one sees that the diagonal dissipative terms of
\cal L
, i.e.

\mathcal{L}_{k}
,
k=1,2,3
, are all proportional to the Bloch components
s_{1}
,
s_{2}
, and therefore vanish when evaluated on the achromatic state. By contrast, the mixed dissipative terms
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1,3}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{2,3}(\rho_{\bf s})

are independent of the Bloch vector
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{s}}
. As a consequence, non-zero coefficients
\alpha_{1,3}
,
\alpha_{2,3}
produce a constant contribution to the Lindbladian that generates a shift of the centre of the Bloch disk.

Hence, in order for the channel
\mathcal{C}(t)
to displace the centre of the Bloch disk, the coefficients
\alpha_{1,3}
and
\alpha_{2,3}
cannot both vanish. In the purely dissipative setting considered here, these coefficients can be interpreted as coupling parameters linking the illuminant component encoded by
\sigma_{3}
to the chromatic opponent degrees of freedom associated with
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
. Concretely, they quantify how the presence of a non-neutral illuminant induces systematic drifts along the chromatic opponent directions. This effect is not a rotation of the opponency axes, but a direct dissipative coupling that converts the illuminant bias into a chromatic displacement, affecting even the achromatic state.

The above considerations motivate the following definition.

Definition 2
.

An illuminant channel is a Markovian rebit channel generated by the Lindbladian

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(\rho_{\bf s})&=\alpha_{1,1}\mathcal{L}_{1}(\rho_{\bf s})+\alpha_{2,2}\mathcal{L}_{2}(\rho_{\bf s})+\alpha_{3,3}\mathcal{L}_{3}(\rho_{\bf s})+\alpha_{1,3}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1,3}(\rho_{\bf s})+\alpha_{2,3}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{2,3}(\rho_{\bf s})\\
&=-\left((\alpha_{2,2}+\alpha_{3,3})s_{1}+2\alpha_{2,3}+\dot{\theta}(0)s_{2}\right)\sigma_{1}\\
&\quad-\left((\alpha_{1,1}+\alpha_{3,3})s_{2}-2\alpha_{1,3}-\dot{\theta}(0)s_{1}\right)\sigma_{2}.\end{split}

(155)

The GKSL equation corresponding to
\cal L
leads to the following differential system

\begin{cases}\dot{s}_{1}=-2(\alpha_{2,2}+\alpha_{3,3})s_{1}(t)-4\alpha_{2,3}\\
\dot{s}_{2}=-2(\alpha_{1,1}+\alpha_{3,3})s_{2}(t)+4\alpha_{1,3},\end{cases}

(156)

solved by

\begin{cases}s_{1}(t)=s_{1}(0)e^{-2(\alpha_{2,2}+\alpha_{3,3})t}-\dfrac{2\alpha_{2,3}}{\alpha_{2,2}+\alpha_{3,3}}\left(1-e^{-2(\alpha_{2,2}+\alpha_{3,3})t}\right)\\[8.53581pt]
s_{2}(t)=s_{2}(0)e^{-2(\alpha_{1,1}+\alpha_{3,3})t}+\dfrac{2\alpha_{1,3}}{\alpha_{1,1}+\alpha_{3,3}}\left(1-e^{-2(\alpha_{1,1}+\alpha_{3,3})t}\right).\end{cases}

(157)

The stationary state
\mathbf{s}_{0}
has Bloch vector

{\bf v}_{\mathbf{s}_{0}}=(s_{0,1},s_{0,2})=\left(-\frac{2\alpha_{2,3}}{\alpha_{2,2}+\alpha_{3,3}},\frac{2\alpha_{1,3}}{\alpha_{1,1}+\alpha_{3,3}}\right).

(158)

The state
{\bf s}_{0}
characterizes the asymptotic behaviour of the purely dissipative dynamics. In the absence of chromatic adaptation, the initial chromatic components
s_{i}(0)
are exponentially suppressed and progressively replaced by the fixed offsets determined by the Lindblad coefficients.

By definition, an illuminant channel is a Markovian rebit channel whose affine component is Markovian, see the system (
157
). Moreover, the differential system (
156
) shows that an illuminant channel is unital if and only if
\alpha_{1,3}=\alpha_{2,3}=0
, which is equivalent to the fact that the illuminant
\imath
is achromatic.

4.2
Opponency channels and colour vision deficiencies

Besides illuminant channels, one may also consider unital Markovian rebit channels whose Lindbladian satisfies
\alpha_{1,3}=\alpha_{2,3}=\alpha_{3,3}=0
, i.e. channels for which
\sigma_{3}
does not appear in
\mathcal{L}
. Since the Pauli matrices encode Hering’s chromatic opponency, such channels act exclusively on the chromatic degrees of freedom, modifying the representation of chromatic states in terms of the opponency axes associated with the unique hues. For this reason, these channels may be used to simulate colour perception deficiencies.

The following definition formalises this notion.

Definition 3
.

An opponency channel is a unital Markovian rebit channel generated by the Lindbladian

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(\rho_{\bf s})&=\alpha_{1,1}\mathcal{L}_{1}(\rho_{\bf s})+\alpha_{2,2}\mathcal{L}_{2}(\rho_{\bf s})+\alpha_{1,2}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1,2}(\rho_{\bf s})\\
&=(\alpha_{1,2}s_{2}-\alpha_{2,2}s_{1})\sigma_{1}+(\alpha_{1,2}s_{1}-\alpha_{1,1}s_{2})\sigma_{2}.\end{split}

(159)

An opponency channel that leaves the unique hues fixed is thus given by

\mathcal{L}(\rho_{\bf s})=\alpha_{1,1}\mathcal{L}_{1}(\rho_{\bf s})+\alpha_{2,2}\mathcal{L}_{2}(\rho_{\bf s})=-(\alpha_{2,2}s_{1}\sigma_{1}+\alpha_{1,1}s_{2}\sigma_{2}).

(160)

The differential system arising from the GKSL equation with this Lindbladian is

\begin{pmatrix}\dot{s_{1}}\\
\dot{s_{2}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-2\alpha_{2,2}&2\alpha_{1,2}\\
2\alpha_{1,2}&-2\alpha_{1,1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}s_{1}(t)\\
s_{2}(t)\end{pmatrix}.

(161)

The system matrix can be diagonalized, with eigenvalues given by

\lambda_{\pm}=-(\alpha_{2,2}+\alpha_{1,1})\pm\sqrt{(\alpha_{2,2}-\alpha_{1,1})^{2}+4\alpha_{1,2}^{2}}.

(162)

Since the Lindblad matrix

\mathcal{M}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1,1}&\alpha_{1,2}&0\\
\alpha_{1,2}&\alpha_{2,2}&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}

(163)

is symmetric positive semi-definite, the two eigenvalues in eq. (
162
) are negative, and the corresponding eigenvectors are orthogonal. For instance, the channel defined by

\dot{\rho}_{\bf s}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\rho_{\bf s})+\mathcal{L}_{2}(\rho_{\bf s})\right)+\frac{1}{4}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{1,2}(\rho_{\bf s})

(164)

is also defined by

\dot{\rho}_{\bf s^{\prime}}=\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\prime}(\rho_{{\bf s^{\prime}}})+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\prime}(\rho_{{\bf s^{\prime}}}),

(165)

with

\displaystyle\rho_{\bf s}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sigma_{0}+s_{1}\sigma_{1}+s_{2}\sigma_{2}\right)=\rho_{{\bf s^{\prime}}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sigma_{0}+s^{\prime}_{1}\sigma^{\prime}_{1}+s^{\prime}_{2}\sigma^{\prime}_{2}\right),

(166)

\sigma^{\prime}_{1}=\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}
,
\sigma^{\prime}_{2}=\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}
,
s^{\prime}_{1}=(s_{1}+s_{2})/2
,
s^{\prime}_{2}=(s_{1}-s_{2})/2
, and
F_{1}^{\prime}=\sigma^{\prime}_{1}/2
,
F_{2}^{\prime}=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}/2
.

Note that this type of channels does not appear in the classification results of Section

Indeed, by imposing
R_{1}(t)=R_{2}(t)=I_{2}
for all
t\geq 0
, those results are valid in the fixed standard Bloch basis

in which the matrix
D(t)
of the affine part of
\mathcal{C}(t)
is diagonal.

4.3
Numerical experiments

Here we present illustrative experiments showing how chromatic distortions induced by Markovian rebit dynamics may affect digital images. Our discussion is intentionally limited to a few basic examples, as a comprehensive analysis would require the introduction of advanced colorimetric notions that fall outside the scope of the present work.

A preliminary observation is in order: at present, we do not possess a faithful numerical model of the chromatic state space of the rebit. Constructing such a model would require dedicated psycho-visual experiments aimed at identifying the four unique hues and the two chromatic opponency mechanisms. These experiments could be carried out by reproducing, with modern quantum optical devices and protocols, classical procedures such as hue-cancellation experiments originally introduced by Jameson and Hurvich more than seventy years ago, see
Hurvich and
Jameson 1955
.

Such an experimental programme would make it possible to define a novel colour space fully consistent with both Hering’s opponency theory and the quantum information-based framework adopted here, thereby avoiding the need to rely on the CIE (Commission International de l’Éclairage) colour spaces.
Nevertheless, it is possible to approximate the state space of Hering’s rebit starting from the Hue-Chroma-Value (HCV) conical colour space, by suitably reshaping it so as to respect chromatic opponency. A detailed account of this construction is provided in
Berthier et al. 2024

5
Conclusions

We have analysed in detail the structure of Markovian channels of rebit systems. When the affine component of such channels is itself Markovian, their classification turns out to be relatively simple and can be obtained in a direct and explicit manner. By contrast, when the affine component does not form a Markovian semigroup, the classification becomes technically more involved and leads to the emergence of three distinct classes of Markovian channels.

We have also connected the resulting classification to the GKSL master equation adapted to rebit systems, thereby providing a clearer characterisation of the complete positivity conditions.

Finally, we have applied these results to a quantum information-based model of colour perception, in which chromatic states are represented as states of a rebit. Within this setting, we have shown how changes in the illuminant can be modelled by Markovian rebit channels acting on states, giving rise to chromatic distortion and to an inherent reduction of chromatic distinguishability, captured by the monotonicity of quantum relative entropy. We have also seen that other types of channels could be used to study colour vision deficiencies.

In future work, we plan to investigate the complementary phenomenon of chromatic adaptation. Our aim is to describe adaptation as a dynamical map acting on effects rather than on states, counteracting chromatic distortion and thus its associated loss of chromatic distinguishability.
