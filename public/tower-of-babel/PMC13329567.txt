Comparative trends in idiopathic epilepsy burden in China and worldwide based on Global Burden of Disease Study 2021
Authors: Xueshi Tian, Yu Geng, Xiang Chen, Zuolin Shi, Zhonghui Zhao, Guangzhi Hao, Haihui Liu, Yushu Dong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIdiopathic epilepsy, defined by the absence of identifiable structural or metabolic etiologies, remains a significant contributor to the global neurological disease burden. However, long-term comparisons between China and the global level are limited. This study analyzed and compared trends in incidence, prevalence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) of idiopathic epilepsy between China and the global level from 1990 to 2021, and included predictive analysis of future trends.MethodsThe key metrics including incidence, prevalence, mortality, and DALYs, along with their corresponding age-standardized rates, were extracted and analyzed. Joinpoint regression was employed to assess temporal trends, Bayesian age-period-cohort models were used for future projections, and decomposition analysis quantified the contributions of population growth, population aging, and epidemiological changes.ResultsFrom 1990 to 2021, both China and the world showed increasing trends in incidence and prevalence. In contrast, DALYs and mortality decreased significantly, with China showing a more pronounced decline (AAPC: −1.790%; −2.649%) than the global level (AAPC: −0.525%; −0.535%). Decomposition analysis revealed that epidemiologic changes played a greater role in China's DALY and mortality reductions. Projection analysis suggested continued increases in incidence and prevalence, but ongoing declines in DALYs and mortality by 2045, especially in China.DiscussionThe burden of idiopathic epilepsy has evolved differently in China compared with global trends, with China experiencing a more substantial reduction in disease severity and mortality. These findings highlight the need for targeted prevention strategies and health system enhancements tailored to specific demographic and epidemiological profiles.Keywords: Idiopathic epilepsy, Global Burden of Disease, Temporal trends, China, Epidemiological analysis

Introduction

Epilepsy is a common chronic neurological disorder affecting millions of individuals worldwide, posing a significant public health challenge and contributing to substantial disability, premature mortality, and social stigma [1], [2], [3]. In 2021, approximately 51.7 million individuals worldwide were living with epilepsy [4]. In 2022, recognizing the substantial global burden of this neurological disorder, the 75th World Health Assembly and the World Health Organization (WHO) designated epilepsy as a top priority in the prevention and control of non-communicable diseases. Accordingly, the Intersectoral Global Action Plan on Epilepsy and Other Neurological Disorders 2022–2031 was adopted to guide comprehensive global strategies for prevention, diagnosis, treatment, and care [5].

Epilepsy encompasses a spectrum of disorders with diverse etiologies and clinical manifestations [6]. Among these, idiopathic epilepsy is defined as epilepsy without identifiable structural, metabolic, infectious, or other known causes and is often presumed to have a genetic basis. It accounts for a substantial proportion of cases, particularly among children and adolescents [7], [8], [9]. Despite significant advances in diagnosis, treatment, and disease management, idiopathic epilepsy remains a major global health burden, particularly in low- and middle-income countries where treatment gaps persist [4].

China, as the most populous country in the world, provides a unique and important context for examining the evolving burden of idiopathic epilepsy. Rapid urbanization, accelerated population aging, and substantial disparities in healthcare resource distribution between urban and rural areas have collectively shaped the country's epidemiological landscape [10]. Additionally, increased public awareness, improved diagnostic capacity, and enhanced disease surveillance in recent decades may have influenced the overall pattern of epilepsy burden in China. However, systematic and long-term comparative analyses between national and global trends remain limited.

To address this gap, the present study employed the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 data to perform a comprehensive analysis of temporal trends in the burden of idiopathic epilepsy in China and globally from 1990 to 2021. This study aims to identify both shared patterns and notable differences between China and the global landscape, thereby providing essential evidence to inform targeted prevention strategies and optimize healthcare policy planning.

Method

Data source

Data for this study were obtained from the GBD 2021 database, which provides comprehensive, standardized, and comparable estimates of the burden of 371 diseases and injuries across 204 countries and territories from 1990 to 2021 [11]. The GBD database is publicly accessible via the Global Health Data Exchange platform (http://ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-results-tool) and integrates data from diverse sources, including vital registration, verbal autopsy, surveys, surveillance systems, censuses, sample registration, among others [12].

The present study focused specifically on idiopathic epilepsy and included both Chinese and global data from 1990 to 2021. Data were extracted for four key indicators of disease burden: incidence, prevalence, mortality, and DALYs. For each indicator, both the absolute number of cases and age-standardized rates (ASRs) per 100,000 population were obtained. Age-standardized rates were calculated using the GBD world population standard, allowing for valid comparisons over time and across regions by adjusting for differences in population size and age structure. DALYs, as a composite measure of disease burden, were calculated as the sum of years of life lost (YLLs) due to premature mortality and years lived with disability (YLDs), reflecting the combined impact of both fatal and non-fatal health outcomes [13]. The DALYs metric enables a comprehensive assessment of the overall health loss attributable to idiopathic epilepsy.

Given the heterogeneity in data quality, case definitions, and reporting systems across countries and over time, the GBD study employs the Bayesian meta-regression modeling tool DisMod-MR 2.1 to generate internally consistent estimates of disease burden [14]. DisMod-MR synthesizes epidemiological data from multiple sources, adjusts for definitional and methodological differences, addresses data gaps, and reduces potential biases. This approach enhances both the comparability and validity of the burden estimates used in the present study. Additionally, data on idiopathic epilepsy were stratified into 5-year age groups, in accordance with the standard GBD reporting framework. This stratification enabled a detailed evaluation of disease burden across age groups and provided deeper insights into age-specific patterns and temporal trends of idiopathic epilepsy in both China and globally.

Statistical analysis

In this study, key metrics related to idiopathic epilepsy were obtained from the GBD 2021 database, including incidence, prevalence, mortality, DALYs, and their corresponding age-standardized rates: the age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR), prevalence rate (ASPR), mortality rate (ASMR), and DALY rate (ASDR). ASR were calculated based on the WHO world standard population, as applied in the GBD study. This standardization minimizes the influence of variations in population size and age structure across regions and time periods, thereby improving the comparability of disease burden estimates.

To quantify temporal trends in idiopathic epilepsy burden from 1990 to 2021, Joinpoint regression analysis was performed using the Joinpoint Regression Program (version 5.4.0.0) (https://surveillance.cancer.gov/joinpoint/). This method identifies points where significant changes in trend occur and estimates the annual percentage change (APC) for each time segment [15]. Based on the APC values from all segments, the average annual percentage change (AAPC) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated to describe the overall trend over the study period. The AAPC was used to quantify the overall trend in each indicator over the study period, with an AAPC greater than 0 indicating an increasing trend, an AAPC less than 0 indicating a decreasing trend, and an AAPC with a 95% confidence interval.

Additionally, to predict the future burden of idiopathic epilepsy, a Bayesian age-period-cohort (BAPC) model was applied [16]. The BAPC model incorporates temporal, cohort, and age-related effects to generate robust projections of disease burden indicators. Bayesian inference was used to estimate model parameters and quantify uncertainty, providing probabilistic forecasts of incidence, prevalence, mortality, and DALYs for idiopathic epilepsy beyond 2021. All statistical analyses and projections were performed using R software (version 4.4.2), with a P value of <0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

The burden of idiopathic epilepsy in China and globally

Between 1990 and 2021, both China and the world experienced increasing trends in the incidence and prevalence of idiopathic epilepsy (Table 1). Globally, the number of incident cases rose from 2.12 million (95% CI: 1.52 to 2.78 million) to 3.27 million (95% CI: 2.40 to 4.13 million), with the ASIR increasing from 38.12 to 42.82 per 100,000 population (AAPC = 0.367%). In China, the ASIR increased from 22.36 to 28.19 per 100,000 (AAPC = 0.781%). Trends in prevalence exhibited a similar upward pattern. Globally, the number of prevalent cases increased from 15.32 million to 24.22 million, and the ASPR rose from 287.46 to 307.38 per 100,000 (AAPC = 0.21%). In China, prevalent cases increased from 2.18 million to 3.09 million, with the ASPR rising from 189.28 to 214.71 per 100,000 (AAPC = 0.432%). In contrast, both DALYs and mortality due to idiopathic epilepsy declined significantly during the same period. The global DALYs increased in absolute numbers from 11.38 million to 13.88 million, the ASDR declined from 208.08 to 177.84 per 100,000 (AAPC = −0.525%). China showed a more substantial reduction, with ASDR decreasing from 178.61 to 101.39 per 100,000 (AAPC = −1.79%). Similarly, global deaths due to idiopathic epilepsy increased from 104,356 to 139,851, while the ASMR declined from 2.07 to 1.74 per 100,000 (AAPC = −0.535%). In China, both absolute deaths and ASMR declined markedly, with ASMR dropping from 1.86 to 0.81 per 100,000 (AAPC = −2.649%), representing a more pronounced reduction compared to the global trend.

Joinpoint regression analysis of the burden of idiopathic epilepsy in China and globally

The Joinpoint regression analysis identified statistically significant changes in the temporal trends of idiopathic epilepsy burden at both the global and Chinese levels from 1990 to 2021 (Fig. 1). Compared with the global estimates, the trend patterns in China exhibited greater variability and more distinct inflection points.

For incidence, a rapid increase in the global ASIR was observed from 1990 to 1994 (APC = 1.10%, P < 0.05), followed by a relatively stable period with mild fluctuations. In China, the ASIR increased more markedly during the same period (APC = 4.48%, P < 0.05). After 2002, a slight decline was observed, followed by a sharp increase from 2019 to 2021 (APC = 6.07%, P < 0.05). For prevalence, the global ASPR showed a moderate upward trend in the early years, with a slight decline from 2010 to 2019 (APC = −0.32%, P < 0.05). In China, the ASPR increased significantly from 1990 to 1994 (APC = 4.43%, P < 0.05), followed by a decreasing trend between 2005 and 2019 (APC = −2.33% and − 1.19%, both P < 0.05). A sharp rebound was noted from 2019 to 2021 (APC = 6.87%, P < 0.05). For DALYs, the global ASDR declined gradually over time, with the most substantial decrease occurring between 2004 and 2011 (APC = −0.94%, P < 0.05). In China, after a brief increase from 1990 to 1993 (APC = 1.24%, P < 0.05), the ASDR showed a consistent downward trend, with the most marked reductions observed between 2003 and 2007 (APC = −4.26%, P < 0.05) and 2007 to 2013 (APC = −2.64%, P < 0.05). A modest rebound was observed from 2019 to 2021 (APC = 2.51%, P < 0.05). For mortality, the ASMR of China and globally exhibited a general declining trend. In China, the most pronounced decreases were observed during 2003 to 2007 (APC = −5.72%, P < 0.05) and 2010 to 2013 (APC = −4.12%, P < 0.05).

Gender disparities in the burden of idiopathic epilepsy in different age groups in China and globally

In 2021, marked gender- and age-specific disparities in the burden of idiopathic epilepsy were observed in both China and globally (Fig. 2, Supplementary Fig. 1). In China, the incidence peaked in children under 5 years, followed by 5–9 and 10–14 age groups, with a secondary peak in adults aged 30–34 years. After age 40, incidence declined, especially in those ≥60 years. The prevalence showed a different pattern, peaking in adults aged 30–34 years, with a secondary peak at 65–69 years. Prevalence also remained higher in males across all age groups. DALYs and mortality followed a similar pattern, with the highest burden among adults aged 30–39 years and a steady decline thereafter. Globally, the incidence peaked in children under 5 years and decreased steadily with age. Prevalence peaked in those aged 5–14 years, then declined after puberty, with no adult secondary peak, in contrast to China. DALYs were concentrated in children under 5 and adolescents aged 15–19 years. Mortality peaked among adolescents (15–19 years), with elevated rates also in children under 5 and young adults. Males consistently bore a higher burden than females across all metrics both China and globally (Fig. 3, Supplementary Fig. 2).

Burden of idiopathic epilepsy in different age groups in China and globally in 1990 and 2021

In China, a clear age-specific shift in the burden of idiopathic epilepsy was observed from 1990 to 2021 (Fig. 4, Supplementary Fig. 3). The age group with the highest incidence remained among children under 5 years of age across both time points. For prevalence, the burden shifted toward adults, with the highest prevalence observed in the 30–34 years group in 2021. DALYs and mortality also reflected these trends, with the burden in 2021 becoming more concentrated in middle-aged and older adults compared to the younger age peaks observed in 1990. These patterns collectively indicate a progressive shift of the disease burden toward older populations in China, underscoring the growing public health challenge posed by idiopathic epilepsy in the context of an aging society.

Decomposition analysis of changes in the burden of idiopathic epilepsy in China and globally from 1990 to 2021

Decomposition analysis was conducted to quantify the relative contributions of population growth, population aging, and epidemiological changes to the overall changes in idiopathic epilepsy burden from 1990 to 2021 in China and globally (Fig. 5). For incidence, at the global level, the increase from 1990 to 2021 was primarily driven by population growth (90.30%) and epidemiological changes (26.70%), while population aging contributed negatively (−17.00%). In China, the pattern was similar in direction but differed in magnitude. Population growth (61.34%) and epidemiological changes (73.13%) both contributed to the increase in incidence, while population aging had a larger negative effect (−34.47%) compared to the global level. For prevalence, global trends showed that population growth (85.21%) and epidemiological changes (14.44%) were the main contributors to the overall increase, with a minimal impact from population aging (0.35%). In China, all three factors contributed positively to the increase in prevalence. Notably, the contributions of epidemiological changes (41.21%) and population aging (4.41%) were more pronounced than those observed at the global level. For DALYs, global results demonstrated an overall increase, driven primarily by population growth (198.45%), which was partially offset by negative contributions from population aging (−16.79%) and epidemiological changes (−81.66%). In contrast, China experienced an overall decrease in DALYs, with strong negative contributions from epidemiological changes (−120.76%) and population aging (−23.48%), outweighing the positive contribution from population growth (44.24%). For mortality, the global burden increased, with population growth (133.83%) and population aging (30.35%) contributing positively, while epidemiological changes (−64.18%) partially offsetting this increase. In China, however, mortality decreased overall, with epidemiological changes (−137.95%) and population growth (−33.94%) contributing to the reduction, while population aging contributed positively (4.01%).

Projections of the burden of idiopathic epilepsy in China and globally from 2022 to 2045

For incidence, both China and the global level are projected to experience an overall increasing trend in the absolute number of incident cases and ASIR from 2022 to 2045, reflecting a sustained increase in the burden of idiopathic epilepsy incidence (Fig. 6, Supplementary Fig. 4–6, Supplementary Table 1). For prevalence, the number of prevalent cases in China is projected to decline from 3125, 897.46 (95% CI: 2,923,199.46 to 3,328,595.45) in 2022 to 2,686,375.83 (95% CI: 1,598,712.06 to 3,774,039.60) in 2045, with a corresponding decrease in ASPR from 218.59 (95% CI: 204.42 to 232.76) to 203.06 (95% CI: 120.84 to 285.27) per 100,000. In contrast, global prevalence is projected to increase from 24,500,176.06 (95% CI: 24,151,266.66 to 24,849,085.46) to 27,536,477.20 (95% CI: 24,531,789.47 to 30,541,164.94), while the ASPR is expected to decrease slightly from 304.52 (95% CI: 300.19 to 308.86) to 293.24 (95% CI: 261.24 to 325.23) per 100,000. For DALYs, both China and the global level are projected to experience a continuous downward trend from 2022 to 2045. Notably, the decline in both the absolute number of DALYs and the ASDR is expected to be more pronounced in China compared to the global level. For mortality, the number of deaths in China is projected to decline substantially from 10,719.47 (95% CI: 10,137.65 to 11,301.29) in 2022 to 3820.11 (95% CI: 2951.09 to 4689.13) in 2045, with the ASMR decreasing from 0.75 (95% CI: 0.71 to 0.79) to 0.29 (95% CI: 0.22 to 0.35) per 100,000. In contrast, the decline in global mortality is expected to be relatively modest.

Discussion

This study conducted a comprehensive, systematic analysis of the trends and disparities in the burden of idiopathic epilepsy in China and at the global level from 1990 to 2021, based on the GBD 2021 data. Our findings demonstrate several key patterns. First, both China and the global level exhibited an overall increasing trend in the incidence and prevalence of idiopathic epilepsy, while DALYs and mortality declined. Notably, the decline in DALYs and mortality was substantially more pronounced in China compared to the global level. Second, significant gender and age-specific disparities were identified, with males consistently exhibiting a higher burden than females across all indicators. The age distribution of the burden in China shifted toward middle-aged and older adults over the study period, a pattern distinct from the global level, where the burden predominantly affects younger populations. Third, decomposition analysis revealed that population growth and epidemiological changes were the primary contributors to the increasing burden of idiopathic epilepsy globally, whereas in China, improvements in disease prevention and management played a more substantial role in reducing DALYs and mortality. Finally, our projections suggest that although the absolute number of incident cases will continue to rise, the overall burden of idiopathic epilepsy, particularly DALYs and mortality, is expected to decline both in China and globally by 2045, with a more substantial reduction expected in China.

Compared with previous studies, our results confirm and extend existing knowledge regarding the epidemiological trends of idiopathic epilepsy. A recent GBD-based analysis reported an increasing trend in the global incidence and prevalence of epilepsy, with persistent regional and gender disparities [4]. Our study focused specifically on idiopathic epilepsy and demonstrated a consistent pattern of increasing incidence globally, in line with previous findings on the global burden of idiopathic epilepsy [7]. Importantly, the decline in DALYs and mortality we observed in China was considerably more pronounced compared to the global level, which may reflect the combined effects of improved healthcare infrastructure, expanded access to epilepsy diagnosis and treatment, and increased public awareness over recent decades.

In terms of gender differences, our finding that males consistently experience a higher burden of idiopathic epilepsy is consistent with previous epidemiological studies [17], [18], [19]. Several biological mechanisms may contribute to these sex differences. First, sex-specific variations in brain structure and connectivity have been proposed to influence seizure susceptibility. Males tend to exhibit greater intrahemispheric connectivity, whereas females demonstrate stronger interhemispheric connectivity, which may partly account for differential epilepsy risk and seizure patterns [20]. Second, sex hormones play a pivotal role in modulating neuronal excitability. Experimental and clinical studies have shown that progesterone and its neurosteroid metabolites possess potent anticonvulsant properties through positive allosteric modulation of GABAA receptors, whereas testosterone and its metabolites exert more complex, bidirectional effects on seizure susceptibility [21], [22], [23]. The relatively lower circulating progesterone levels in males may partly contribute to their increased seizure susceptibility and higher epilepsy burden. In addition, sex chromosome-linked gene expression and epigenetic modifications have been recognized as important contributors to sex differences in epilepsy. Genes that escape X-chromosome inactivation in females may lead to gene dosage differences between sexes, potentially conferring neuroprotective effects against hyperexcitability and seizures in females [21], [22]. Conversely, Y chromosome-linked gene expression may increase neuronal hyperexcitability and seizure risk in males. These genetic and epigenetic mechanisms, together with structural and hormonal factors, may act synergistically to shape the observed sex differences in idiopathic epilepsy burden.

With respect to age-specific patterns, our study revealed that the burden of idiopathic epilepsy in China has progressively shifted toward middle-aged and older adults, in contrast to the global level, where the burden remains predominantly concentrated in younger populations, particularly children and adolescents [18], [24]. This difference is likely attributable to China's rapidly aging population, coupled with an increasing burden of cerebrovascular disease, which is a major contributor to epilepsy in older adults [25], [26], [27]. It is estimated that by 2040, the total number of older adults in China will reach 400 million, accounting for 28% of the total population [28]. This demographic shift is expected to further increase the burden of epilepsy. Additionally, improvements in the diagnosis and management of pediatric epilepsy in China may have contributed to the relative decline in the burden among younger age groups [29].

In terms of disease burden, our study revealed a continuous downward trend in DALYs and mortality for idiopathic epilepsy in both China and globally from 1990 to 2021, with a more pronounced decline observed in China. Despite this encouraging trend, the overall burden of idiopathic epilepsy remains substantial. Notably, epilepsy ranked among the top ten causes of age-standardized DALYs worldwide in 2021 [30], underscoring its ongoing public health significance and the need for sustained prevention and management efforts. Interestingly, our Joinpoint regression analysis and burden estimates from 2020 to 2021 showed a temporary rebound in incidence, prevalence, DALYs, and mortality, particularly in China. This fluctuation may be partially explained by the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic. Several studies have reported that the pandemic disrupted routine healthcare services, delayed diagnosis and treatment for epilepsy, and increased psychological stress and seizure risk among people with epilepsy [31], [32], [33]. Moreover, pandemic-related restrictions may have temporarily altered healthcare-seeking behavior, resulting in delayed disease detection and a short-term increase in reported cases and burden. These findings underscore the importance of ensuring continuity of care for people with epilepsy during public health crises.

Our study has important public health implications. The growing burden of idiopathic epilepsy, particularly the increasing incidence and prevalence, highlights the need for enhanced prevention, early detection, and treatment strategies, especially targeting high-risk groups such as males and older adults. The more pronounced decline in DALYs and mortality observed in China suggests that investments in healthcare infrastructure, public health initiatives, and epilepsy management programs have been effective in mitigating disease severity and fatal outcomes. These successful experiences may provide valuable insights for other low- and middle-income countries facing similar challenges. Moreover, our findings emphasize the critical need to address healthcare resource disparities between urban and rural regions in China, as unequal access to care remains a key barrier to further reducing the burden of idiopathic epilepsy.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the GBD estimates are subject to uncertainty due to cross-country and temporal variations in data quality, case definitions, and reporting systems. Although the DisMod-MR model addresses these issues to some extent, residual bias cannot be completely excluded. Second, while the decomposition analysis quantified the contributions of demographic and epidemiological factors, unmeasured factors such as environmental exposures, socioeconomic status, and healthcare utilization patterns could not be fully captured. Finally, although we provided projections of idiopathic epilepsy burden using the BAPC model, these forecasts are based on historical trends and assume the continuation of current patterns, which may be influenced by future changes in healthcare policies, medical technologies, and unforeseen events such as pandemics.
