Graphs with girth 8 and without longer even holes are 3-colorable
Yan Wang, Rong Wu

Abstract
For an integer ℓ ≥ 2 \ell\geq 2 , let ℋ ℓ {\cal{H}}_{\ell} denote the family of graphs which have girth 2 ​ ℓ 2\ell and have no even hole of length greater than 2 ​ ℓ 2\ell . Wu, Xu and Xu conjectured that every graph in ⋃ ℓ ≥ 2 ℋ ℓ \bigcup_{\ell\geq 2}{\cal{H}}_{\ell} is 3 3 -colorable. Chen showed that every graph in ⋃ ℓ ≥ 5 ℋ ℓ \bigcup_{\ell\geq 5}{\cal{H}}_{\ell} is 3 3 -colorable. In this paper, we prove that every graph in ℋ 4 {\cal{H}}_{4} is 3 3 -colorable.

The

For a graph

For a graph

The concept of

[
8
]
.
Studying which family of graphs can be

-bounded, and finding the optimal binding function for
\chi
-bounded class are important problems in this area.
Since clique number is a trivial lower bound of chromatic number, if a family of
\chi
-bounded graphs has a linear binding function, then this function must be the asymptotically optimal binding function of this family.
For recent progress on
\chi
-bounded problems, see
[
14
]
.

A hole in a graph is an induced cycle of length at least
4
.
A hole is said to be

Addario-Berry, Chudnovsky, Havet, Reed and Seymour
[

]
, and Chudnovsky and Seymour

]
, proved that every even hole free graph has a vertex whose neighbours are the union of two cliques, which implies that

.
However, the situation becomes much more complicated for odd hole free graphs.
The Strong Perfect Graph Theorem
[

asserts that a graph is perfect if and only if it induces neither odd holes nor their complements.
Confirming a conjecture of Gyárfás
[
8
]
,

[

proved that odd hole free graphs are
\chi
-bounded with binding function
\frac{2^{2^{\omega(G)+2}}}{48(\omega(G)+2)}

Hoàng and McDiarmid
[

conjectured for an odd hole free graph

A related direction concerns graphs with restricted hole lengths.
A graph is
\ell

\ell

Cook, Horsfield, Preissmann, Robin and Seymour

Sivaraman

Let

It is natural to study the chromatic number of every graph in

More recently, Chen

In contrast to the case of odd girth, the even girth problem becomes more difficult for small
\ell

When

is large, the girth condition and the exclusion of longer even holes impose stronger distance constraints on induced theta graphs and induced

K_{4}
-subdivisions.
However, for small even girth, extremal configurations such as short jumps and tight theta subgraphs may exist simultaneously without immediately creating either a shorter cycle or a forbidden longer even hole, leaving much less structural information.
In this paper, we prove that Conjecture

Theorem 1.3

Graphs in
{\cal H}_{4}
are
3

In fact, we prove the following structural characterization, which implies Theorem

Theorem 1.4

All graphs in
{\cal H}_{4}
have either a degree-
2

Proof of Theorem

Suppose that Theorem

be a minimal counterexample.
Then

.
By Theorem

-cut or a

-cut. Consequently,

By the minimality of

Assume for contradiction that the statement is false.
Let
C
be an even hole of a graph
G\in{\cal H}_{4}
and write
C=v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{8}v_{1}
.
Let
S
be the set consisting of ends of short jumps over
C
.

Claim 3.1

S\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.
Suppose it is false.
That is, there is no short jump over
C
.
By Lemma
2.5
(1),
every pair of local jumps across one vertex either share an end or cross.
So there is a
5
-vertex path
Q
on
C
containing the ends of all local jumps across one vertex over
C
.
Then there exists a
3
-vertex path of
C\backslash V(Q)
that contradicts Lemma
2.2
.

Claim 3.2

|S|\neq 2
.

Proof.
Suppose it is false.
If there exists a short jump of type-o,
then we may assume that
v_{1}v_{2}v_{3}v_{4}v_{5}
contains the ends of all short jumps.
Now,
\{v_{6},v_{7},v_{8}\}\cap S=\emptyset
, which contradicts Lemma
2.6
.
Hence, every short jump is of type-e.

Suppose that there exists a short
(v_{1},v_{5})
jump of type-e, that is,
S=\{v_{1},v_{5}\}
.
Since
S=\{v_{1},v_{5}\}
, by Lemma
2.2
,
for
3
-vertex path
v_{6}-v_{7}-v_{8}
,
either there is a
v_{7}-v_{8}-v_{1}
local jump, or there is a
v_{6}-v_{7}-v_{8}
local jump, or there is a
v_{5}-v_{6}-v_{7}
local jump.
Similarly, for
3
-vertex path
v_{2}-v_{3}-v_{4}
,
either there is a
v_{1}-v_{2}-v_{3}
local jump, or there is a
v_{2}-v_{3}-v_{4}
local jump, or there is a
v_{3}-v_{4}-v_{5}
local jump.
Now, we show that there is no
v_{7}-v_{8}-v_{1}
local jump, or
v_{5}-v_{6}-v_{7}
local jump, or
v_{1}-v_{2}-v_{3}
local jump, or
v_{3}-v_{4}-v_{5}
local jump.
Suppose otherwise.
We may assume that there is a
v_{7}-v_{8}-v_{1}
local jump, then there is no
v_{1}-v_{2}-v_{3}
local jump.
Otherwise, there exists a short jump with an end
v_{7},v_{8}
,
v_{2}
, or
v_{3}
or there exists an even hole containing
v_{3}v_{4}\cdots v_{7}
of length at least
10
,
a contradiction.
If there is a
v_{2}-v_{3}-v_{4}
local jump, then by Lemma
2.5
, there exists a short jump with an end
v_{8}
or
v_{3}
;
and if there is a
v_{3}-v_{4}-v_{5}
local jump, then by Lemma
2.5
, there exists a short jump with an end
v_{8}
or
v_{4}
.
A contradiction.
So, by Lemma
2.2
, there exist a
v_{6}-v_{7}-v_{8}
local jump and a
v_{2}-v_{3}-v_{4}
local jump.
However, by Lemma
2.5
,
v_{3}
or
v_{7}
is an end of some short jumps, which contradicts
S=\{v_{1},v_{5}\}
.

Claim 3.3

|S|\neq 3
.

Proof.
Suppose not.
Then there exist two short jumps sharing an end.
Clearly, these two short jumps are parallel and one of the short jumps is of type-o.
Assume that the shared end is
v_{1}
.
We may assume that one of the short jumps, say
P_{1}
, is a
(v_{1},w_{1})
-short jump, and the other short jump, say
P_{2}
, is a
(v_{1},w_{2})
-short jump where
w_{1},w_{2}\in\{v_{3},v_{4},\cdots,v_{7}\}
and
w_{1}\neq w_{2}
.
Then
S=\{v_{1},w_{1},w_{2}\}
.
By Lemma
2.6
,
\{v_{6},v_{7}\}\cap S\neq\emptyset
and
\{v_{3},v_{4}\}\cap S\neq\emptyset
.
Since
\{v_{6},v_{7}\}
is anticomplete to
\{v_{3},v_{4}\}
, by Lemma
2.4
,
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
cannot both be of type-o.
So we may assume that
P_{1}
is of type-o and
P_{2}
is of type-e, then

w_{2}=v_{5}
, a contradiction.

Therefore, we have
|S|\geq 4
.

(s,t)
-short jump over
