The cohesive and stable Ramsey theorems and proof size over a weak base theory
Leszek Aleksander Kołodziejczyk and Mengzhou Sun

Abstract
We show that over the weak base theory RCA 0 ∗ \mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*} , cohesive Ramsey’s theorem for pairs CRT 2 2 \mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2} implies exponential closure of the definable cut I 1 0 \mathrm{I}^{0}_{1} , which is the intersection of all Σ 1 0 \Sigma^{0}_{1} -definable cuts. Consequences include non-elementary proof speedup of RCA 0 ∗ + CRT 2 2 \mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2} over RCA 0 ∗ \mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*} for Π 1 \Pi_{1} sentences and the unprovability of CRT 2 2 \mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2} in RCA 0 ∗ + CAC \mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\mathrm{CAC} . On the other hand, we show that RCA 0 ∗ + SRT 2 2 \mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2} , where SRT 2 2 \mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2} is stable Ramsey’s theorem for pairs, is polynomially simulated by RCA 0 ∗ \mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*} w.r.t. proofs of ∀ Π 3 0 \forall\Pi^{0}_{3} sentences. Nevertheless, SRT 2 2 \mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2} also implies a nontrivial property of I 1 0 \mathrm{I}^{0}_{1} , specifically closure under functions of quasipolynomial growth rate.



1
Introduction

The logical strength of combinatorial theorems
formalized in second-order arithmetic is a topic of considerable interest in reverse mathematics, and in the foundations of mathematics at large. A particularly interesting set of problems concerns the first-order, or more precisely
\Pi^{1}_{1}
, consequences
of Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and two colours,
\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}
,
and of its natural weakenings and variants.
(See
[
4
,
22
,
19
,
20
]
for some research milestones in this area from the last decade.)

Starting with
[
27
]
,
the strength of combinatorial statements has also been studied over the weak axiom system
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
, which differs from the usual base theory for reverse mathematics,
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}
, by disallowing uses of mathematical induction for non-computable (more precisely,
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
) properties. The study of
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
has led to new insights into reverse mathematics in the traditional setting of
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}
– see
[
2
,
10
]
. However, the strength of
\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}
and its cousins over
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
is also a natural and intriguing topic in its own right.

It was shown in
[
9
]
that even though typical weakenings of
\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}
differ in terms of their first-order consequences over
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
, in some sense almost all of them have similar strength: they are
\forall\Pi^{0}_{3}
- but not
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-conservative over
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
,
and thus also over the theory
\mathrm{B}\Sigma^{0}_{1}+\mathrm{exp}
, which captures the
\Pi^{1}_{1}
consequences of
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
.
(See
[
26
]
for a curious exception.)
That suggests the need for sharper tools to differentiate between the strength of various principles, and one such tool is provided by proof size analysis.

The study of proof size in axiomatic theories has a long tradition dating back to Gödel
[
11
]
. It was
explicitly related to reverse mathematics by Avigad
[
1
]
, who introduced the
method later called forcing interpretations in order to show
that the well-known
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-conservativity of
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}

over
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}
can be witnessed by a polynomial proof transformation. More recently, proof size analysis has been
applied to the reverse mathematics of Ramsey theory
[
17
,
14
,
21
]
.

In the specific area of reverse mathematics over
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
,
it turns out that the effect on proof size is a good way of distinguishing between some Ramsey-theoretic statements
with the same
\forall\Pi^{0}_{3}
consequences.
It was shown in
[
17
]
that translating proofs of some
\forall\Pi^{0}_{3}
(in fact, simpler) sentences from
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}
into
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
results in iterated exponential (“tower function”) blowup. A similar speedup phenomenon still holds for some weakenings of
\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}
, like
the Erdös-Moser principle
\mathrm{EM}
. On the other hand,
Kowalik
[
14
]
proved that the chain-antichain principle
\mathrm{CAC}
leads to no superpolynomial speedup over
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
for proofs of
\forall\Pi^{0}_{3}
sentences.

The proofs of both the speedup and the non-speedup results mentioned above make use of the definable cut
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
, which is the intersection of all
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-definable cuts and thus measures the available amount of
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
induction. In general,
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
is only closed under multiplication. Adding
\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}
to
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
is enough to show closure of
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
under exponentiation, and that results in proof speedup, while
\mathrm{CAC}
does not imply any such additional closure properties, which plays a major role in the non-speedup argument. In turn, the effect of a Ramsey-theoretic principle on
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
depends on the Ramsey numbers associated with its finite-combinatorial analogue. For example, Ramsey numbers for arbitrary 2-colourings of pairs are roughly exponential, and that is
why
\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}
implies exponential closure of
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
, but by Dilworth’s theorem, Ramsey numbers for colourings corresponding to posets are only polynomial, and because of this
\mathrm{CAC}
does not prove superpolynomial closure of
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
.

A well-known pair of Ramsey-theoretic statements that has so far resisted attempts at such classification consists
of cohesive Ramsey’s theorem for pairs,
\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
, and stable
Ramsey’s theorem for pairs,
\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
. The principle
\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
claims the existence of a homogeneous set for any colouring of pairs from
\mathbb{N}
that is
stable
, meaning that for any given element, all but finitely many pairs involving that element have the same colour;
\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
complements that by saying that for any colouring of pairs from
\mathbb{N}
, there is an infinite set on which the colouring is stable. Intuitively, the reason why
\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
and
\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
have been difficult to analyze in terms of proof size is that it is not obvious what the finite analogue of a stable colouring should be. (On the syntactic level, this is reflected in the greater first-order quantifier complexity of
\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
and
\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
compared to many other variants of
\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}
.)

In this paper, we resolve the conundrum concerning the effect of
\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
and
\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
on proof size over
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
. We show that
\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
behaves like
\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}
in that it implies exponential closure of
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
and thus leads to iterated exponential proof speedup on rather simple sentences.
An interesting corollary of this result is that the implication
\mathrm{CAC}\to\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
, known to hold over
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}
,
requires
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
induction in the sense that it is not probable in
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
. In contrast,
\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
has no superpolynomial proof speedup over
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
for
\forall\Pi^{0}_{3}
sentences. Unlike
\mathrm{CAC}
, however,
\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
does have nontrivial consequences for
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
, as it implies the closure of that cut under functions of quasipolynomial growth rate.

The proofs of these facts once again involve finite combinatorics, though, as expected, this time the passage between infinite and finite is less direct than in the case of
\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}
and
\mathrm{CAC}
. Roughly speaking, a colouring
f
of pairs from
\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
that is in a loose sense “stable” should have the property that for many
i<m
, the sequence
\langle f(i,i+1),f(i,i+2),\ldots,f(i,m-1)\rangle
contains long constant subsequences, and the related property that the same sequence rarely changes value. The former property is helpful in the analysis of
\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
, the latter – in the analysis of
\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
, which is more subtle and requires proving bounds on Ramsey numbers for a parametrized family of classes of colourings.

The rest of this paper is structured as follows. We review the relevant background material in Section
2
. We prove the results on the strength of
\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
in Section
3
. Passing to
\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
, we deal with the finite-combinatorial aspects and the effect on
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
in Section
4
, and in Section
5
we use that to prove the non-speedup result.

The symbol
\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of natural numbers formalized in an arithmetic theory, while
\omega
denotes the set of standard natural numbers. We use the convention that the letter
n
always denotes a standard number.

The formula classes
\Sigma_{n}^{0},\Pi_{n}^{0}
, and
\Delta_{0}^{0}
are defined as usual by counting alternations of blocks of first-order quantifiers, allowing second-order variables but not second-order quantifiers.
Notation without the superscript
0
, that is
\Sigma_{n},\Pi_{n},\Delta_{0}
,
represents analogously defined classes of purely first-order formulas, in which second-order parameters are not allowed.
Given a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, we write
\Sigma_{n}(A),\Pi_{n}(A)
for the classes of
\Sigma_{n}^{0}
and
\Pi_{n}^{0}
formulas, respectively, in which
A
is the only second-order parameter.
The class
\forall\Pi^{0}_{n}
contains formulas that consist of a block of universal (first- and/or second-order) quantifiers
followed by a
\Pi^{0}_{n}
formula.

It is well-known that the graph of exponentiation, viewed as a binary relation
2^{x}=y
, is
\Delta_{0}
-definable.
The axiom
\mathrm{exp}
denotes the
\Pi_{2}
statement
\forall x\,\exists y\;2^{x}=y
asserting the totality of exponentiation,
and the formula class
\Delta_{0}(\mathrm{exp})
is defined analogously to
\Delta_{0}
but allowing the use of
2^{x}
as a function symbol.
We write
2_{a}(x)
to denote the
a
-th iteration of exponentiation, i.e.,

2_{a}(x):=2^{2^{\scriptstyle 2^{\iddots^{\scriptstyle x}}}},

where the stack of exponents contains
a
appearances of
2
.
The expression
2_{a}
abbreviates
2_{a}(1)
. Functions like the logarithm
\log y
and the iterated logarithm
\log^{*}y
are treated as integer-valued, with
value equal to the greatest
x\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{x}\leq y
resp.
2_{x}\leq y
. As a result, some equalities in the proof of Corollary
4.3
hold only approximately. This causes no problems as what really matters for the proof is the
in
equalities, and these hold as soon as the numbers involved are large enough. The function
\omega_{1}

is defined by
\omega_{1}(x)=x^{\log x}
. A function is said to have
quasipolynomial
growth rate if it is bounded by some finite iteration of
\omega_{1}
, or equivalently by

x\mapsto x^{\log^{\ell}x}
for some constant
\ell
.

The theory
\mathrm{RCA}^{*}_{0}
, originally introduced in

[
24
]
, is obtained from
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}
by weakening the
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
induction axiom
\mathrm{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}

to
{\mathrm{I}\Delta^{0}_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}}
.
The theory
\mathrm{WKL}^{*}_{0}
is obtained from
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}
in an analogous way: in other words,
\mathrm{WKL}^{*}_{0}
is
\mathrm{RCA}^{*}_{0}
plus Weak König’s Lemma
\mathrm{WKL}
.

Let
M
be a model of
\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}+\mathrm{exp}
.
We say that a subset
I
of
M
is a
cut
if it is closed under successor and downward closed; we denote this by
I\subseteq_{\mathrm{e}}M
.
A cut
I\subseteq_{\mathrm{e}}M
is
proper
if
I\neq M
.
If
(M,\mathcal{X})
is a model of
\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}^{0}
, then no proper cut appears in the second-order universe
\mathcal{X}
, and the model fails to satisfy
\mathrm{I}\Sigma_{1}^{0}
if and only if a proper
\Sigma_{1}^{0}
-definable cut exists.

We write
\log I
for the definable set
\{\log x\mid x\in I\}
, which need not be a cut unless the cut
I
is closed under addition. The set
\log^{*}I
, which is a cut only if
I
is closed under exponentiation, is defined similarly.

We can view an element
c
of a model
M
as representing the set
\mathrm{Ack}(c)
, where
x\in\mathrm{Ack}(c)
is defined to mean that the
x
-th digit in the binary expansion of
c
is
1
. We refer to sets of the form
\mathrm{Ack}(c)
for
c\in M
as
M
-finite sets
.
Let
I
be a proper cut of
M
.
Given a set
X\subseteq I
, we say that
X
is
coded
in
I
if there is some
c\in M
such that
X=\mathrm{Ack}(c)\cap I
.
We define
\mathrm{Cod}(M/I)=\{\mathrm{Ack}(c)\cap I\mid c\in M\}
.
It is well-known
[
24
]
that if
I
is exponentially closed, then
(I,\mathrm{Cod}(M/I))
is a model of
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}^{*}
.

We say that a set
A
is
unbounded
in
\mathbb{N}

(or
infinite
)
if
\forall b\,\exists x\!>\!b\;x\in A
.
In a model
(M,\mathcal{X})\vDash\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
,
the sets in
\mathcal{X}
that are not unbounded are
exactly the
M
-finite sets. Over
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
, an unbounded set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
need not be in bijective correspondence with
\mathbb{N}
.
Instead, there is a unique cut
J
, called the

of
A
, such that there is an increasing enumeration
\{a_{i}\mid i\in J\}
of the elements of
A
.
Moreover, the cut
J
is
\Sigma_{1}^{0}
-definable, namely,

i\in J\text{ iff }\exists x\!\in\!A\;x=a_{i}.

Conversely, every
\Sigma_{1}^{0}
-definable cut can be obtained in this way for some unbounded set
A
.

The cut
\mathrm{I}_{1}^{0}
is defined to consist of those
x\in\mathbb{N}

such that every unbounded
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
contains a finite subset of cardinality

.
By the previous paragraph,
\mathrm{I}_{1}^{0}
is exactly the intersection of all
\Sigma_{1}^{0}
-definable cuts.
In particular, it is provable in
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
that
for any
x\in\mathbb{N}
and
v\in\mathrm{I}_{1}^{0}
, the number
2_{v}(x)
exists; also,
\mathrm{I}_{1}^{0}=\mathbb{N}
if and only if
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}
holds.

We use the letters
\sigma,\tau
to denote finite sequences, which are represented by numbers in the usual way.
We use the symbol
\emptyset
to stand for the empty sequence.
The notation
\sigma\sqsubseteq\tau
means that
\sigma
is an initial segment of
\tau
. For concatenation with a one-element sequence, we write
\sigma i

instead of
\sigma^{\smallfrown}\langle i\rangle
.
We will use vertical lines, as in
|\sigma|
or
|S|
,
to stand for the length of a finite sequence or for
the cardinality of a finite set, depending on the context.

In contrast to the standard finite-combinatorial convention, we write
[m]
for the set
\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
instead of
\{1,\dots,m\}
. For an arbitrary set
A
, the notation
[A]^{2}
stands for the set of unordered pairs of distinct elements of
A
, represented as usual by (codes of) ordered pairs with the first element smaller than the second one.
We write
[m]^{2}
instead of
[[m]]^{2}
.
If a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is enumerated in increasing order as
A=\{a_{0}<a_{1}<\ldots\}
and

f\colon[A]^{2}\to 2
, then

f(a_{i},-)
stands for the binary sequence

\langle f(a_{i},a_{i+1}),f(a_{i},a_{i+2}),\ldots\rangle.

This convention applies also when
A
is finite,
in which case the enumeration is
\{a_{0}<a_{1}<\ldots<a_{k}\}
for some
k
, and
f(a_{i},-)
is the finite sequence

\langle f(a_{i},a_{i+1}),\ldots,f(a_{i},a_{k})\rangle
.
If
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is unbounded,
we say that a colouring
f\colon[A]^{2}\to 2
is
stable
if for every
x\in A
, the sequence
f(x,-)
is eventually constant.

Ramsey’s Theorem for pairs and two colours,
\mathrm{RT}_{2}^{2}
,
asserts that for any colouring
f\colon[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
there is an unbounded
homogeneous
set
H
, i.e.,

\forall{{x,y,z,w}\in{H}}\ f(x,y)=f(z,w).

Cholak, Jockusch, and Slaman
[
3
]
decomposed
\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}
into two weaker principles, Cohesive Ramsey’s Theorem for pairs,
\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
, and Stable Ramsey’s Theorem for pairs,
\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
:

\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
:

For every
f\colon[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
there exists an unbounded set
C\subseteq\mathbb{N}

such that
f\mathclose{\restriction_{[C]^{2}}}
is stable.

\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
:

For every stable
f\colon[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
there exists an unbounded

homogeneous set
H\subseteq\mathbb{N}
.

Two other Ramsey-theoretic statements that will be mentioned are the chain-antichain principle
\mathrm{CAC}
, which says that any partial order on
\mathbb{N}
contains an infinite chain or an infinite antichain, and the ascending-descending sequence principle
\mathrm{ADS}
, which says that for any linear order on
\mathbb{N}
there is an infinite set on which the order either agrees with the usual natural number ordering
\leq

or agrees with the inverse of
\leq
.

Provably in
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
, each of the above Ramsey-theoretic principles is equivalent to its generalization
to instances defined on arbitrary unbounded subsets of
\mathbb{N}
.
Over
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}
, we have the following sequence of implications:

\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}\to\mathrm{CAC}\to\mathrm{ADS}\to\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2},

proved mostly in
[
13
]
. All of these except
\mathrm{ADS}\to\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
and
\mathrm{CAC}\to\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
are known to hold over
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
as well.

When considering questions related to proof size, formally speaking we should fix a specific proof system, but the exact choice is immaterial as there are polynomial-time translations between typical Hilbert-style, natural deduction, and sequent calculus systems with cut
[
6
]
. For definiteness, we assume that our proof system is like the one in
[
7
, Section 2.4]
except that propositional tautologies are derived from a finite number of axiom schemes rather than assumed as axioms. As in the system from
[
7
]
,
our official set of logical symbols consists only of
of
\neg
,
\to
, and
\forall
, with the others treated as abbreviations.

A theory
S

polynomially simulates
a theory
T
with respect to sentences from the class
\Gamma
if there is a polynomial-time procedure that takes a
T
-proof of a sentence

\gamma\in\Gamma
and outputs an
S
-proof of
\gamma
.
In contrast,
T
has
non-elementary speedup
over
S
with respect to
\Gamma
if there is no elementary recursive function
f
such that if
\pi
is a
T
-proof of a sentence
\gamma\in\Gamma
, then there exists an
S
-proof of
\gamma
of size at most
f(|\pi|)
.

3
The strength of Cohesive Ramsey’s Theorem

In this section, we study the behaviour of
\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
over
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
. We show that
\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}

shares some features of
\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}
that make it appear “strong” in this context, distinguishing it from other weakenings of
\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}
like
\mathrm{CAC}
or
\mathrm{ADS}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The theory
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
proves that
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
is closed under
\mathrm{exp}
.

Proof.

We present the proof in a model-theoretic setting,
working with a model of
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
rather than
in the theory, but we emphasize that this is a matter of convenience rather than necessity. The argument could be given a (perhaps somewhat unwieldy) purely syntactic form.

Let
(L,\cal X)
be a model of
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
with
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
not closed under
\mathrm{exp}
.
Then obviously
(L,\mathcal{X})\not\vDash\mathrm{RCA}_{0}
, since
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}
proves
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}=\mathbb{N}
. As a consequence (see
[
18
, Lemma 9]
) there is a proper exponential
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-cut
M
in
(L,\cal X)
.
As mentioned in the Preliminaries, the structure

(M,\mathrm{Cod}(L/M))
is a model of
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}^{*}
.
One can check that
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}(M,\mathrm{Cod}(L/M))=\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}(L,\cal X)
; in the remainder of this argument, we will refer to

\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}(M,\mathrm{Cod}(L/M))
simply as
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
. Moreover,
by
[
9
, Corollary 3.10]
,
(M,\mathrm{Cod}(L/M))
satisfies
\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
if and only if
(L,\cal X)
does. So, to prove that

(L,\mathcal{X})\not\vDash\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
it suffices to show

(M,\mathrm{Cod}(L/M))\not\vDash\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
.
Given an
L
-finite set
S
, we will write
\widehat{S}
for
S\cap M
, which is an element of
\mathrm{Cod}(L/M)
.

Since
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
is not closed under
\mathrm{exp}
,
there is some
k\in M
such that
k\in\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}<2^{k/4}
. Fix some such
k
.
By the assumption that
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}<2^{k/4}
, we can also fix
an
L
-finite set
A
of cardinality
2^{k/4}
such that
\widehat{A}
is cofinal in
M
.

To prove that
(M,\mathrm{Cod}(L/M))\not\vDash\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
, we will use a probabilistic argument in
L
. More precisely, we will isolate a
finite-combinatorial (from the point of view of
L
) property of colourings
f\colon[A]^{2}\to 2

that occurs with non-zero probability for random
f

and has the infinite-combinatorial (from the point of view of
M
) consequence that
\widehat{f}
is an instance of
\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
with no solution in
(M,\mathrm{Cod}(L/M))
.

Consider a fixed
f\colon[A]^{2}\to 2
and a hypothetical set
C\subseteq A
such that
\widehat{C}
is a solution to
\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}

on instance
\widehat{f}
. We make the following observations:

1.

As
\widehat{C}
is unbounded in
M
and
C\subseteq A
, the cardinality of
\widehat{C}
is a
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-cut
J_{C}
such that
k\in J_{C}<2^{k/4}
. Moreover,
J_{C}
is not closed under addition, because otherwise
\log J_{C}
would be a
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-cut with
\log J_{C}<k
, contradicting the assumption that

2.

Thus, by possibly taking some elements out of

3.

By the assumption that

k+1
consecutive 0’s.

Let us now assume that we specify

by a random process in which

.
As mentioned, in order to prove that
(M,\mathrm{Cod}(L/M))\not\vDash\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
, and thus complete the argument, we want to find an event with probability strictly less than 1 that is implied by the existence of a solution to
\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
for
\widehat{f}
in
\mathcal{X}
.

Fix some

and a set

as above.
By observation (iii), if
\widehat{C}
is a solution to
\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
for

\widehat{f}

occurs: the string

contains a run of 1’s or a run of 0’s of length

We claim that for any fixed

is less than
\frac{j}{2^{k}}

To see this, note that the probability that we get a run of length

is exactly
\frac{1}{2^{k}}
, and
s_{i}
has length

, so there are fewer than

, as claimed.

For fixed

, the events

with

ranging in

are independent, so we get

\Pr\left(\bigcap_{i}E_{j,C,i}\right)<\left(\frac{j}{2^{k}}\right)^{j}.

Now, for

:= “there is

\binom{2^{k/4}}{2j}\leq\left(2^{k/4}\right)^{2j}=2^{kj/2},

so, by the union bound again, we get

\Pr(E_{j})<2^{kj/2}\left(\frac{j}{2^{k}}\right)^{j}=\left(\frac{j2^{k/2}}{2^{k}}\right)^{j}\leq\left(\frac{2^{3k/4}}{2^{k}}\right)^{j}=\left(\frac{1}{2^{k/4}}\right)^{j}\leq\frac{1}{2^{k^{2}/4}}.

Here, the second inequality holds because

, and the last one holds because

.

Finally, using the union bound one more time, we get

\Pr\left(\bigcup_{j=k}^{2^{k/4}}E_{j}\right)<{2^{k/4}}\frac{1}{2^{{k^{2}}/{4}}}={2^{-\frac{k^{2}-k}{4}}}\ll 1.

But if

\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
for

must hold for

∎

. Firstly,

.

Corollary 3.2
.

\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\mathrm{CRT}^{2}_{2}
has non-elementary speedup over
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
with respect to proofs of
\Delta_{0}(\mathrm{exp})
sentences.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as for
\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}
in
[

, Section 3]
– in
[

]
, a detailed argument is given for proofs of
\Sigma_{1}
sentences, but it remarked there that the speedup applies also to finite consistency statements that can be expressed in a
\Delta_{0}(\mathrm{exp})
way.

Let
\mathrm{Con}_{n}(T)
be the statement

with at most

mean “there is no inconsistency proof in

4
Stable Ramsey’s Theorem: upper and lower bounds

We would now like to investigate the effect of

\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}

We start by isolating the relevant class of finite colourings – in fact, not a single class but a parametrized family of classes – and proving a lower bound on the associated Ramsey numbers.

Definition 4.1
.

Let

Corollary

tells us that
there is a nontrivial, namely quasipolynomial, lower bound on Ramsey numbers even for the restricted situation in which we only consider somewhat stable colourings: more precisely, when looking for a
homogeneous set of size
k

k
-unstable.
Our aim is now to show that there is a corresponding quasipolynomial upper bound as well.

There is a well-known class of colourings for which Ramsey numbers are at most quadratic. This class was given a definition in
[
8
]
in graph-theoretic terminology, which is probably more natural in this context, but we reformulate it in terms
of sets and colourings to maintain notational uniformity.

Definition 4.4

(Erdös–Hajnal
[
8
]
)
.

A colouring
f\colon[S]^{2}\to 2
for a finite

is

if it belongs to the smallest class satisfying the following conditions:

1.

if
S
has one element, then the unique colouring of
[S]^{2}
is very simple,

2.

if
f_{1}\colon[S_{1}]^{2}\to 2
and

f_{2}\colon[S_{2}]^{2}\to 2
are both very simple, then each of the two colourings
g_{0},g_{1}
of
[S_{1}\cup S_{2}]^{2}
is very simple, where
g_{b}
acts as
f_{i}
on
[S_{i}]^{2}
and has constant value
b
on all pairs with one element in
S_{1}
and one in
S_{2}
.

The following lemma is stated and proved
(of course, in terms of graphs and without mention of provability in
{\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}}
)
in
[
8
]
, where it is referred to
as well-known.

Lemma 4.5

(
[
8
, Lemma 1.4]
)
.

({\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}})
If
f:[S]^{2}\to 2
is a very simple colouring, then there is a homogeneous set for
f

of size at least
\sqrt{|S|}
.

Proof.

It is proved by induction on
|S|
that
the product of the size of the largest homogeneous set with colour
0
and the size of the largest homogeneous set with colour
1
must be at least
|S|
. The inductive step is a straightforward case analysis that easily formalizes in
{\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}}
.
∎

To prove our upper bound on Ramsey
numbers for stable colourings, we will show that
if a colouring is at most
k
-unstable but defined on a set of size considerably larger than
k
, then we can inductively construct a reasonably big subset on which the colouring is very simple. The following lemma provides the core of the inductive step in that construction.

Lemma 4.6
.

({\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}})
Let
c\geq 2
, and let
k
be larger than some fixed standard constant.
Then for any
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
with
|A|=k^{c}
the following holds: if
f\colon[A]^{2}\rightarrow 2
is at most
k
-unstable, then there exist some
A_{0},A_{1}\subseteq A
with
\max A_{0}<\min A_{1}
such that
|A_{0}|=|A_{1}|=k^{c-2}
and
f\mathclose{\restriction_{A_{0}\times A_{1}}}
is constant.

We identify
A
with
[k^{c}]
.
Let
A_{0}^{\prime}=[4k^{c-2}]
and
A_{1}^{\prime}=[k^{c}]\setminus A_{0}^{\prime}
.

Assume that we sample a subinterval
I\subseteq A_{1}^{\prime}

uniformly at random. For each fixed
x\in A_{0}^{\prime}

be the event “

does not change value within

Then

1-\Pr_{I}(E_{x,I})\leq\frac{k(|I|-1)}{|A_{1}^{\prime}|-|I|+1}\leq\frac{k^{c-1}}{k^{c}-5k^{c-2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

The first inequality above holds because if a given point at which

changes value lies within

must lie in one of at most

So,

and the interval

\Pr_{x,I}(E_{x,I})\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
Let
A_{1}
be the subinterval

of

that maximizes

Let

holds.
Then by the lower bound on

.

Now,

. We choose

Then

has constant value

by the choice of

. Finally, we may leave out some elements of

so as to ensure

.

({\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}})

By Lemma

, it is enough to show

5
Polynomial simulation

The aim of this section is to prove the following result.

Theorem 5.1
.

The theory
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}^{*}+\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
is polynomially simulated
by
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
w.r.t.
\forall\Pi^{0}_{3}
sentences.

The proof of Theorem
5.1
– or, at least,
the proof we present here –
relies on Corollary
4.8

and the technique of forcing interpretations, introduced originally (
avant la lettre
) in
[
1
]
.

The high-level structure of our argument is modelled closely on the proof of an analogous polynomial simulation result
for
\mathrm{CAC}
given by Kowalik in
[
14
]
(which is based on
[
15
, Chapter 4]
). However, there are two significant modifications, due to the fact that
the first-order part of
\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
is more complex than that
of
\mathrm{CAC}
and to the quasipolynomial rather than polynomial nature of our bounds in Section
4
.
Since the routine details of forcing interpretations are often highly tedious to describe even when the idea is quite clear, we present the proof in the form of a somewhat lengthy sketch focusing on the novel aspects of our argument.
More precisely, we first review the concept of forcing interpretations, and then we give a rather careful summary of the polynomial simulation proof, providing full details only in those places where our reasoning diverges from
the argument of
[
14
]
.

Forcing interpretations.

In general, the idea of a forcing interpretation of a theory
T
in a theory
S
is that one defines in
S
a notion of ‘
\psi
is forced’ for
\mathcal{L}_{T}
-formulas
\psi
, in such a way that
S
can prove that each axiom of
T
is forced. Then a
T
-proof of a sentence
\theta
can be transformed into an
S
-proof of ‘
\theta
is forced’.
If, in addition,
\theta
is actually in
\mathcal{L}_{S}\cap\mathcal{L}_{T}
and we can construct an
S
-proof
of (‘
\theta
is forced’
\rightarrow\theta
),
then we obtain an
S
-proof of
\theta
by modus ponens. Assuming that proofs verifying various properties of the forcing interpretation can be constructed in polynomial time, the transformation of the
T
-proof of
\theta
into the
S
-proof is polytime as well.

In the form proposed in
[
17
, Section 1]
and then used in
[
14
]
, to provide a forcing interpretation
\tau
of
T
in
S
one has to define in
S
the following: a set
\Cond_{\tau}
of forcing conditions, a relation
\trianglelefteqslant_{\tau}
on the conditions, a set
\Name_{\tau}
of names (intended to name elements of the generic
\mathcal{L}_{T}
-structure being described by the forcing),
and the forcing relations

p\Vdash_{\tau}v\mathclose{\downarrow}
and
p\Vdash_{\tau}\alpha(\overline{v})
, where
p
is a condition,
v,\overline{v}
are names, and
\alpha
is an
atomic

\mathcal{L}_{T}
-formula (the intuitive meaning of
p\Vdash_{\tau}v\mathclose{\downarrow}
is that
v
will in fact name an element of the generic structure as long as
p
is in the generic filter). These definitions have to satisfy a number of well-behavedness requirements
provably in
S
:
roughly,
\trianglelefteqslant_{\tau}
should be a preorder,
the forcing relation(s) should be monotone w.r.t.
\trianglelefteqslant_{\tau}
, the equality axioms should be forced, and
p
should force a statement (be it
v\mathclose{\downarrow}
or
\alpha(\overline{v})
) if it is densely forced below
p
.
(For a precise formulation of the requirements, see

[
14
, Definition 3.2]
; or
[
17
, Definition 1.8]
, where, however, the treatment of atomic formulas has to be more careful due to greater intended generality).

The definition of
p\Vdash_{\tau}\theta
is then extended to non-atomic formulas
\theta
using standard inductive conditions:

1.

p\Vdash_{\tau}\neg\theta
iff
q\not\Vdash_{\tau}\theta
for all
q\trianglelefteqslant_{\tau}p
;

2.

p\Vdash_{\tau}(\theta\to\eta)
iff for every
q\trianglelefteqslant_{\tau}p
such that
q\Vdash_{\tau}\theta
there is
r\trianglelefteqslant_{\tau}q
such that
r\Vdash_{\tau}\eta
;

3.

p\Vdash_{\tau}\forall x\,\theta(x)
iff for every name
w
and every
q\trianglelefteqslant_{\tau}p
such that
q\Vdash_{\tau}w\mathclose{\downarrow}
, there is
r\trianglelefteqslant_{\tau}q
such that
r\Vdash_{\tau}\theta(w)
.

(For simplicity, we suppress names corresponding to potential free variables in
\theta,\eta
other than
x
in (iii)).
The final requirement that has to be satisfied
for
\tau
to be a forcing interpretation of
T

is that
S
proves
\Vdash_{\tau}\psi
for each axiom
\psi
of
T
.
Here
\Vdash_{\tau}\ldots
stands for
\forall p\!\in\!\Cond_{\tau}\,(p\Vdash_{\tau}\ldots)
.

Remark 5.2
.

As discussed in the Preliminaries,
our official language does not contain connectives other than
\neg,\to,\forall
, but typical inductive conditions also apply to forcing formulas involving other connectives if they
are defined from
\neg,\to,\forall
in the usual way. In particular,
p
forces an existential statement iff densely below
p
a specific instance is forced.

If the existence of
\tau
is to imply that

T
is polynomially simulated by
S
w.r.t. a class of sentences
\Gamma
, two additional requirements have to be met. Firstly,
\tau
has to be a
polynomial forcing interpretation of
T
, which in the simple case of finitely axiomatized
T
means that the
S
-proofs of some well-behavedness properties of
\Vdash_{\tau}
on atoms
(for example, that the equality axioms are forced) can be constructed in polytime. Secondly,
\tau
has to be
polynomially
\Gamma
-reflecting
: there has to be a polytime procedure which, given a sentence
\gamma\in\Gamma
, outputs an
S
-proof of
(\Vdash_{\tau}\gamma)\rightarrow\gamma
.
(The fact that the existence of a
polynomially
\Gamma
-reflecting forcing interpretation
of
T
in
S
implies polynomial simulation of
T
by
S
w.r.t.
\Gamma
is stated as
[
14
, Theorem 3.8]

or
[
17
, Theorem 1.18]
, though the idea goes back to
[
1
, Section 10]
.)

Structure of the polynomial simulation argument.

We now discuss the structure of the proof of Theorem
5.1
.
The result of
[
17
]
that
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}+\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}
is polynomially simulated by
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}
w.r.t.
\forall\Pi^{0}_{3}
sentences means that for any consequence
\xi
of
\mathrm{RT}^{2}_{2}
, polynomial simulation of
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}^{*}+\xi
by
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}

follows from polynomial simulation of
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}^{*}+\xi

by
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\neg\mathrm{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}

(all still at the
\forall\Pi^{0}_{3}
level).
To achieve this for
\xi
equal to
\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
,
we introduce an intermediate auxiliary theory

{{\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}}}+{\mathrm{SC}_{\omega_{1}}}
, where
\mathrm{SC}_{\omega_{1}}
is the following axiom in the language
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PA}}\cup\{\mathbb{I}\}
for a unary predicate
\mathbb{I}
:

\mathbb{I}
is a proper cut closed under
\omega_{1}

such that for any
x
there is some
v>\mathbb{I}
for which
2_{v}(x)
exists.

This is a stronger version of the axiom
\mathrm{SC}
considered in

[
14
]
, which required only closure of
\mathbb{I}

under multiplication rather than under
\omega_{1}
. The reason why this strengthening turns out to be relevant is that the bound from Corollary
4.8
is quasipolynomial, in contrast to the polynomial bound on Ramsey numbers for
\mathrm{CAC}
that follows from Dilworth’s Theorem.

We need polynomial forcing interpretations of
{{\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}}}+{\mathrm{SC}_{\omega_{1}}}

in
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\neg\mathrm{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
and of
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}^{*}+\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
in
{\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}}+{\mathrm{SC}_{\omega_{1}}}
. We begin with the former.

Interpreting the auxiliary theory.

In order to (forcing-)interpret
{\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}}+{\mathrm{SC}}
(without the additional requirement on closure of
\mathbb{I}
under
\omega_{1}
)
in
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\neg\mathrm{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
, we follow
[
14
]
by making a case distinction.
Let
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-
\mathrm{LPC}
be the statement “there exists a least proper
\Sigma_{1}^{0}
-definable cut”.
If
\neg\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-
\mathrm{LPC}
holds, then there is
a straightforward (non-forcing) interpretation in which all the arithmetical operations are interpreted identically and
\mathbb{I}
is interpreted as
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
. On the other hand,
under
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-
\mathrm{LPC}
we can use a forcing interpretation
\tau_{1}
that describes a
restricted ultrapower
of the first-order universe (modulo a generic ultrafilter)
with
\mathbb{I}
interpreted as the cut generated by
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
of the ground model. Conditions are unbounded subsets of
\mathbb{N}
(potential elements of the ultrafilter),
the order
\trianglelefteqslant_{\tau_{1}}
is inclusion, names are total
functions from
\mathbb{N}
to
\mathbb{N}
.
The details of the forcing
relation are exactly as in
[
14
]

and will not be referred to below.
Thus, we do not present them here except for noting that
p\Vdash_{\tau_{1}}t(\overline{v})\in\mathbb{I}
if there is
k\in\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
such that
t(\overline{v}(x))\leq k
holds for all but finitely many
x\in p
(intuitively, the element of the ultrapower given by
t(\overline{v})
, where
\overline{v}
is a tuple of functions, is bounded by an element of
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
, identified as usual with a constant function).

Making the additional step from
\mathrm{SC}
to
{\mathrm{SC}_{\omega_{1}}}
is actually quite easy, thanks to Solovay’s technique of shortening cuts.
There is a definable subcut of
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
that is provably closed under
\omega_{1}
, namely the cut defined by

\varphi(x):=\forall y\,(y\in\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}\to 2^{\log x\cdot\log y}\in\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}).

Both in the
\neg\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-
\mathrm{LPC}
and in
the
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-
\mathrm{LPC}
case, substituting
\varphi
for
the definition of
\mathrm{I}^{0}_{1}
throughout gives rise to a polynomial forcing interpretation of
{{\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}}}+{\mathrm{SC}_{\omega_{1}}}
(which does not actually involve forcing
in the
\neg\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-
\mathrm{LPC}
case).
In the
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-
\mathrm{LPC}
case, as a gesture to subscript enthusiasts, we will refer to the resulting forcing interpretation
as
\tau_{1,\omega_{1}}
.

Summarizing this part of the argument, we get:

Lemma 5.3

(cf.
[
14
]
, Lemma 4.4)
.

The forcing interpretation

\tau_{1,\omega_{1}}

is a polynomial forcing interpretation of
{{\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}}}+{\mathrm{SC}_{\omega_{1}}}
in
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\neg\mathrm{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}+\Sigma^{0}_{1}\text{-}\mathrm{LPC}
.
There is also a (non-forcing) interpretation of
{{\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}}}+{\mathrm{SC}_{\omega_{1}}}
in
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\neg\mathrm{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}+\neg\Sigma^{0}_{1}\text{-}\mathrm{LPC}
that is the identity on
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PA}}
.

Remark 5.4
.

Although the step from
\mathrm{SC}
to
\mathrm{SC}_{\omega_{1}}

described above is very simple from a technical point of view,
the possibility of shortening cuts is the main reason why a
quasipolynomial
(or even quasiquasipolynomial, etc.) bound on the finite version of a Ramsey-theoretic
principle makes it possible to prove a
polynomial
simulation over
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}
.

Interpreting
\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
.

The formulas defining our forcing interpretation
\tau_{2}
of
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}+\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
in
{\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}}+{\mathrm{SC}_{\omega_{1}}}
are exactly
the same as those used in
[
14
]
to
forcing-interpret
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}+\mathrm{CAC}
in
{{\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}}}+{\mathrm{SC}}
.
The difference is that we now have a stronger
theory of the ground model,
guaranteeing better closure properties of
\mathbb{I}
, which will
allow us to interpret the additional principle
\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
.

The forcing interpretation
\tau_{2}

describes a
generic cut
,
with the coded sets as the second-order structure,
satisfying
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}^{*}
. The forcing conditions of
\tau_{2}

are finite sets
p
such that

1.

|p|>\mathbb{I}
.

2.

\forall{{x,y}\in{p}}\ (x<y\rightarrow 2^{x}<y)
.

A condition
q
extends
p
in the sense of
\trianglelefteqslant_{\tau_{2}}
if
q\subseteq p
. Intuitively,
a condition
p
approximates a cut between
\min p
and
\max p
such that elements of
p
appear arbitrarily high in the cut.
Requirement (i) on
p
guarantees that
\mathrm{I}_{1}^{0}
of the cut contains
\mathbb{I}
(in fact, it will be exactly
\mathbb{I}
by genericity); requirement (ii) guarantees that
\mathrm{exp}
holds in the cut.

Since
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}^{*}
is a two-sorted theory, we need two kinds of names: first-order names
v
are natural numbers, second-order names
V
are also natural numbers but viewed as the finite sets they code. It is always the case that
p\Vdash_{\tau_{2}}V\mathclose{\downarrow}
, and
p\Vdash_{\tau_{2}}v\mathclose{\downarrow}
if
p\cap[0,v]
is not a condition: intuitively,
p\cap[0,v]
is then small, so the cut approximated by
p
will be above
v
. Finally, for an atom
\alpha
we have
p\Vdash_{\tau_{2}}\alpha(\overline{v},\overline{V})

if
p\Vdash_{\tau_{2}}v\mathclose{\downarrow}
and
p\Vdash_{\tau_{2}}V\mathclose{\downarrow}
and
\alpha(\overline{v},\overline{V})
holds (where an atom of the form
t\in V
is understood according to the usual Ackermann interpretation of set theory in arithmetic).

Lemma 5.5

(cf.
[
14
, Lemma 4.9]
)
.

The forcing interpretation
\tau_{2}
is a polynomial
forcing interpretation of
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}^{*}+\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
in
{{\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}}}+{\mathrm{SC}_{\omega_{1}}}
.

Proof.

The verification that
\tau_{2}
is a forcing interpretation of
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}^{*}
is just like in
[
14
, Lemma 4.9]
. (Intuitively, requirement (ii) from the definition
of
\Cond_{\tau_{2}}
ensures that the generic cut is exponentially closed, and every exponentially closed proper cut in a model of
\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}
is a model of
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}^{*}
.)

Also the fact that
\tau_{2}
is a polynomial forcing interpretation is proved like in
[
14
]
,
because the only new thing we have to do is to provide
a (necessarily constant-sized) proof in
{\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}}+\mathrm{SC}_{\omega_{1}}
that the single statement
\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
is forced.

We reason inside
{\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}}+{\mathrm{SC}_{\omega_{1}}}
, and we drop the subscript
{}_{\tau_{2}}
, writing
\Vdash
,
\trianglelefteqslant
etc.
Our arguments below will make use of the following intuitively obvious fact (see
[
17
]
or
[
14
]
for a proof): if
p\in\Cond
, then

p\Vdash\text{`}p\text{ is unbounded'}
, where
\text{`}p\text{ is unbounded'}
stands for
\forall x\,\exists y\,(y\geq x\land y\in p)
.

Since all logical equivalences are forced, we can
assume that
\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
states:

\forall f\,(\text{`}f\text{ stable'}\to\exists H\,(\text{`}H\text{ unbounded'}\land\text{`}H\text{ homogeneous for }f\text{'}).

(1)

Assume that
p
is a condition,
f
is a second-order name
and
p
forces that
f
is a function from
[\mathbb{N}]^{2}
to
2
;
this lets us also assume w.l.o.g. (by possibly shrinking
p
, thus passing to a stronger condition) that
f\mathclose{\restriction_{[p]^{2}}}

is in fact a 0-1 valued function. We want to show that

p
forces (
1
).

We make the following claim.

Claim 5.5.1
.

If
p\Vdash\text{`}f\text{ stable'}
, then
there exists some
k\in\mathbb{I}
such that
f\mathclose{\restriction_{[p]^{2}}}
is at most

-unstable.

Proof of Claim
.

Suppose otherwise. Then by
\Delta_{0}
overspill, there
is some

and
\ell>\mathbb{I}
such that

f\mathclose{\restriction_{[p]^{2}}}(x_{0},-)
changes value
\ell
times.
Let

Reflection.

This part of the argument is just like in

]

except for the additional appearance of
\omega_{1}
.
We do not prove a polynomial reflection property for each forcing interpretation separately (note that this would involve changing the class of sentences involved,

and
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}^{*}+\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
are two-sorted theories,
while the intermediate theory
{{\mathrm{I}\Delta_{0}}+{\mathrm{exp}}}+{\mathrm{SC}_{\omega_{1}}}

is one-sorted).

Lemma 5.6

(cf.
[
14
]

.

There exists a polytime procedure which, given an

sentence

{\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}}+{\neg\mathrm{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}}+{\Sigma^{0}_{1}\text{-}\mathrm{LPC}}

.

An analogous result holds with
\Sigma^{0}_{1}\text{-}\mathrm{LPC}

replaced by

\tau_{1,\omega_{1}}

Completing the argument.

We can now review how the pieces are put together to complete the proof of the polynomial simulation.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

By
[
17
, Theorem 2.1]
and an
obvious argument by cases, it
is enough to prove that
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}^{*}+\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}
is polynomially simulated by
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\neg\mathrm{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
w.r.t.
\forall\Pi^{0}_{3}
. By another case distinction, it suffices
to prove polynomial simulation by
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\neg\mathrm{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}+\Sigma^{0}_{1}\text{-}\mathrm{LPC}
and separately by

\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\neg\mathrm{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}+\neg\Sigma^{0}_{1}\text{-}\mathrm{LPC}
.

Let us consider the case of
\Sigma^{0}_{1}\text{-}\mathrm{LPC}
.
Assume that we are given a proof in
\mathrm{WKL}_{0}^{*}+\mathrm{SRT}^{2}_{2}

of a
\forall\Pi^{0}_{3}
sentence
\psi
.
By Lemmas
5.3
,
5.5

and the properties of forcing interpretations,
we have a polynomial-time procedure that outputs a proof in
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\neg\mathrm{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}+\Sigma^{0}_{1}\text{-}\mathrm{LPC}
of

\Vdash_{\tau_{1,\omega_{1}}}(\Vdash_{\tau_{2}}\psi).

On the other hand, by Lemma

, another polytime procedure produces a proof in
\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\neg\mathrm{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}+\Sigma^{0}_{1}\text{-}\mathrm{LPC}

\neg\psi\to(\Vdash_{\tau_{1,\omega_{1}}}(\not\Vdash_{\tau_{2}}\psi)).

Putting these two proofs together,

\mathrm{RCA}_{0}^{*}+\neg\mathrm{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}+\Sigma^{0}_{1}\text{-}\mathrm{LPC}

\neg\psi\to(\Vdash_{\tau_{1,\omega_{1}}}\bot)

The argument for

is replaced by Remark

.
∎
