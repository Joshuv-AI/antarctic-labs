Double phase inequalities with convolution nonlinearity in exterior domains
Marius Ghergu

Abstract
We discuss the existence of C 1 C^{1} -solutions for two related double phase inequalities: ( P ± P^{\pm} ) ℒ g ​ u ± Δ s ​ u ≥ ( | x | − α ∗ u p ) ​ u q in ​ ℝ N ∖ B ¯ 1 , N ≥ 1 , {\mathcal{L}}_{g}u\pm\Delta_{s}u\geq(|x|^{-\alpha}*u^{p})u^{q}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B}_{1},N\geq 1, in which Δ s u := div ( | ∇ u | s − 2 ∇ u ) \Delta_{s}u:={\rm div}\big(|\nabla u|^{s-2}\nabla u\big) is the s s -Laplace operator, s > 1 s>1 , and ℒ g u := − div ( | ∇ u | m − 2 g ( | ∇ u | ) ∇ u ) , m > s > 1 , {\mathcal{L}}_{g}u:=-{\rm div}\Big(|\nabla u|^{m-2}g(|\nabla u|)\nabla u\Big),\quad m>s>1, where g : [ 0 , ∞ ) → ( 0 , ∞ ) g:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty) is a C 1 ( 0 , ∞ ) ∩ C [ 0 , ∞ ) C^{1}(0,\infty)\cap C[0,\infty) non-increasing function with some specific behaviour near the origin. In the above context, the general form of ℒ g ​ u {\mathcal{L}}_{g}u includes the case of m m -Laplace and m m -mean curvature operator. Our study reveals a sharp distinction between ( P + ) (P^{+}) and ( P − ) (P^{-}) . Precisely, we show that the inequality ( P + ) (P^{+}) has solutions for all m > s > 1 m>s>1 and q > s − 1 q>s-1 . In contrast, ( P − ) (P^{-}) has solutions if and only if p p and q q are sufficiently large. We also link the solvability of ( P − ) (P^{-}) with that of the corresponding equation ℒ g ​ u − Δ s ​ u = ( | x | − α ∗ u p ) ​ u q {\mathcal{L}}_{g}u-\Delta_{s}u=(|x|^{-\alpha}*u^{p})u^{q} in ℝ N ∖ B ¯ 1 \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B}_{1} , for which we derive optimal conditions in terms of p , q , α , s p,q,\alpha,s and N N . The approach combines integral estimates with a new sub and supersolution method that accounts for the presence of the convolution term.

Lemma 2.1

Let

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{m-1}u^{1-s}\phi^{\lambda-1}|\nabla\phi|

Similarly,

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{s-1}u^{1-s}\phi^{\lambda-1}|\nabla\phi|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{s-1}{2\lambda}|\nabla u|^{s}u^{-s}\phi^{\lambda}+C_{2}(s,\lambda)|\nabla\phi|^{s}\phi^{\lambda-s}.

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}(|x|^{-\alpha}*u^{p})u^{q-s+1}\phi^{\lambda}+\frac{s-1}{2}

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}\Big(|\nabla u|^{m}g(|\nabla u|)+|\nabla u|^{s}\Big)u^{-s}\phi^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq C\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}|\nabla\phi|^{m}u^{m-s}\phi^{\lambda-m}+C\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}|\nabla\phi|^{s}\phi^{\lambda-s},

where

in

and supp

for all
R>2

For all

\displaystyle\geq\int\limits_{B_{4R}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{u^{p}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\geq\int\limits_{B_{4R}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{u^{p}(y)}{(8R)^{\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\Big(\int\limits_{B_{4R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{p+q-s+1}{2}}\phi^{\lambda}\Big)^{2}

for all
R>2

Since

Lemma 5.3
.

Let

,
\rho>1

for some

Then, for any

, there exists

We fix

\displaystyle-\Delta_{s}v

\displaystyle=\frac{|v^{\prime}(r)|^{s-1}}{r}\Big\{\frac{rv^{\prime\prime}(r)}{v^{\prime}(r)}(s-1)+N-1\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{-N}\log^{\theta(s-1)-1}r\cdot\frac{(N-s)\log r+(1-\theta)(s-1)}{\frac{N-s}{s-1}\log r-\theta}

and thus from (

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{g}v

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{m-1}g(t)}{r}\Big\{\frac{rv^{\prime\prime}(r)}{v^{\prime}(r)}\Big(m-1+\frac{tg^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}\Big)+N-1\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq c\frac{t^{m-1}}{r}\Big\{\frac{rv^{\prime\prime}(r)}{v^{\prime}(r)}(s-1)+N-1\Big\}

n\geq 2
we consider the problem

(5.22)

\begin{cases}{\mathcal{L}}_{g}u_{n}-\Delta_{s}u_{n}\displaystyle=\Big(\int\limits_{B_{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{u_{n-1}^{p}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\Big)u_{n-1}^{q}\quad\mbox{ in }B_{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{1},\\[7.22743pt]
u_{n}=\underline{u}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{n}.\end{cases}

Step 1:
Existence and uniqueness of
u_{n}\in C^{1}(B_{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{1})
,
n\geq 2
. Furthermore, we have
\underline{u}\leq u_{n-1}\leq u_{n}\leq\overline{u}
in
B_{n}\setminus B_{1}
.

In proving the above claim, we first need the result below.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B}_{1}
be a smooth bounded domain and
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that

(5.23)

0\leq f(x)\leq(|x|^{-\alpha}*\overline{u}^{p})\overline{u}^{q}(x)\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega.

Then, the problem

(5.24)

\begin{cases}{\mathcal{L}}_{g}u-\Delta_{s}u\displaystyle=f(x)\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega\,,\\
u=\underline{u}\quad\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

has a unique solution
u\in C^{1}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,m}(\Omega)
and
\underline{u}\leq u\leq\overline{u}
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
G:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
G(0)=0
and
G^{\prime}(t)=t|t|^{m-2}g(t)+t|t|^{s-2}
. By (
1.7
) we have that
G
is convex. We also claim that there exist
c_{2}>c_{1}>0
such that

(5.25)

c_{1}(t^{m}g(t)+t^{s})\leq G(t)\leq c_{2}(t^{m}g(t)+t^{s})\quad\mbox{ for all }t\geq 0.

Indeed, integrating by parts, for all
t>0
we have

G(t)=\frac{t^{s}}{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{m-1}g(\tau)d\tau=\frac{t^{s}}{s}+\frac{t^{m}g(t)}{m}-\frac{1}{m}\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{m}g^{\prime}(\tau)d\tau.

Since
g^{\prime}\leq 0
the above equality implies the first estimate in (
5.25
).
Furthermore, using (
1.7
) we have
\tau^{m}g^{\prime}(\tau)\geq-(m-1)\tau^{m-1}g(\tau)
so

G(t)\leq\frac{t^{s}}{s}+\frac{t^{m}g(t)}{m}+\frac{m-1}{m}\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{m-1}g(\tau)d\tau\leq\frac{t^{s}}{s}+\frac{t^{m}g(t)}{m}+\frac{m-1}{m}G(t),

which gives

\frac{1}{m}G(t)\leq\frac{t^{s}}{s}+\frac{t^{m}g(t)}{m}\quad\mbox{ for all }t\geq 0

and this implies the second estimate in (
5.25
).

Denote by
W^{1,G}(\Omega)
the set of all measurable functions
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
for which
D_{i}u:=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}
exists in the weak sense,
1\leq i\leq N
, and

\int_{\Omega}G(|\nabla u|)dx<\infty.

Then,
W^{1,G}(\Omega)
becomes a reflexive Banach space (see
[
1
, Theorem 8.28]
) with respect to the norm (see
[
14
, Lemma 5.7 ]
)

\|u\|_{G}=\inf\left\{k>0:\int_{\Omega}G\Big(\frac{|D_{i}u|}{k}\Big)\leq 1,\mbox{ for all }1\leq i\leq N\right\}.

Let also
W_{0}^{1,G}(\Omega)
be the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the
\|\cdot\|_{G}
norm. Set

I:\underline{u}+W_{0}^{1,G}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R},\quad I(u)=\int_{\Omega}G(|\nabla u|)dx-\int_{\Omega}f(x)u.

Then
I
is of class
C^{1}
and

\langle I^{\prime}(u),\phi\rangle=\int\limits_{\Omega}G^{\prime}(|\nabla u|)\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\cdot\nabla\phi-\int\limits_{\Omega}f(x)\phi,\quad\mbox{ for all }\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

Since
G(t)\geq\frac{1}{s}t^{s}
, we easily derive that
I
is coercive and thus has a critical point which is a solution of (
5.24
). From Lemma
2.3
we have
\underline{u}\leq u\leq\overline{u}
in
\Omega
. By
[
18
, Theorem 1.7]
we derive
u\in C^{1}(\Omega)
.
∎

To conclude the proof in this step, it remains to apply Lemma
5.4
for
\Omega=B_{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{1}
and

0\leq f(x):=\Big(\int\limits_{B_{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{u_{n-1}^{p}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\Big)u_{n-1}^{q}(x)\quad\mbox{ in }B_{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{1}.

Starting from
u_{1}=\underline{u}
we see that condition (
5.23
) is fulfilled since
\underline{u}\leq\overline{u}
in
B_{2}\setminus B_{1}
. Then, since
u_{1}=\underline{u}\leq u_{2}\leq\overline{u}
in
B_{2}\setminus B_{1}
, we may apply Lemma
5.4
to derive

\underline{u}\leq u_{2}\leq u_{3}\leq\overline{u}
in
B_{3}\setminus B_{1}
and we may further proceed by induction.

Step 2:
The sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq 2}
is bounded in
W^{1,G}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B}_{1})
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B}_{1}
be a compact set and take
R\geq 1
an integer such that
K\subset B_{R}\setminus\overline{B}_{1}
. Take
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{R}\setminus B_{1})
such that
0\leq\phi\leq 1
and
\phi=1
on
K
. We test the problem (
5.22
) with
\phi^{k}u_{n}
,
k>m>s
. Using the fact that
u_{n-1}\leq\overline{u}
in
B_{R}\setminus B_{1}
, we find

\int\limits_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}\Big(|\nabla u_{n}|^{m-2}g(|\nabla u_{n}|)+|\nabla u_{n}|^{s-2}\Big)\nabla u_{n}\cdot\nabla(\phi^{k}u_{n})\leq\int\limits_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}(|x|^{-\alpha}*\underline{u}^{p})\underline{u}^{q}(\phi^{k}\underline{u})=C<\infty.

This yields

(5.26)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}\Big(|\nabla u_{n}|^{m}

\displaystyle g(|\nabla u_{n}|)+|\nabla u_{n}|^{s}\Big)\phi^{k}

\displaystyle\leq C+\int\limits_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}\Big(|\nabla u_{n}|^{m-1}g(|\nabla u_{n}|)+|\nabla u_{n}|^{s-1}\Big)|\nabla\phi^{k}|\overline{u}

\displaystyle\leq C+C_{1}\int\limits_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}\Big(|\nabla u_{n}|^{m-1}g(|\nabla u_{n}|)+|\nabla u_{n}|^{s-1}\Big)|\nabla\phi^{k}|.

We note that
|\nabla\phi^{k}|\leq c\phi^{k-1}
, so by Young’s inequality we derive

(5.27)

\displaystyle|\nabla u_{n}|^{s-1}|\nabla\phi^{k}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2C_{1}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{s}\phi^{\frac{s(k-1)}{s-1}}+C(s,k)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2C_{1}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{s}\phi^{k}+C(s,k).

Similarly we have

|\nabla u_{n}|^{m-1}|\nabla\phi^{k}|\leq\frac{1}{2C_{1}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{m}\phi^{k}+C(m,k).

Since
g(|\nabla u_{n}|)\leq g(0)
from the last estimate we obtain

(5.28)

|\nabla u_{n}|^{m-1}g(|\nabla u_{n}|)|\nabla\phi^{k}|\leq\frac{1}{2C_{1}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{m}g(|\nabla u_{n}|)\phi^{k}+C(m,k)g(0).

We now use (
5.27
) and (
5.28
) into (
5.26
) and the fact that
\phi=1
on
K
to deduce

(5.29)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{K}\Big(|\nabla u_{n}|^{m}g(|\nabla u_{n}|)+|\nabla u_{n}|^{s}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}\Big(|\nabla u_{n}|^{m}

\displaystyle g(|\nabla u_{n}|)+|\nabla u_{n}|^{s}\Big)\phi^{k}

\displaystyle\leq 2C.

Using the estimates (
5.25
), it follows that
\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq R}
is bounded in
W^{1,G}(K)
.

Step 3:
The function
u(x):=\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}(x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B}_{1}
is a
C^{1}
-solution of (
1.8
).

Let us note that
u
is well defined as it is the pointwise limit of the monotone sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
. Let
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n\geq 2}
be a decreasing sequence that converges to zero and
0<\varepsilon_{n}<1
. Since
\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq 2}
is bounded in
W^{1,G}(B_{2}\setminus B_{1+\varepsilon_{2}})
, we may find a subsequence
\{u_{n}^{2}\}_{n\geq 2}
which converges to
u
in
W^{1,G}(B_{2}\setminus B_{1+\varepsilon_{2}})
. Next, since the sequence
\{u_{n}^{2}\}_{n\geq 3}
is bounded in
W^{1,G}(B_{3}\setminus B_{1+\varepsilon_{3}})
, we may find a subsequence
\{u_{n}^{3}\}_{n\geq 3}
which converges to
u
in
W^{1,G}(B_{3}\setminus B_{1+\varepsilon_{3}})
and so on. Inductively, for all
k\geq 2
we construct a sequence

\{u_{n}^{k-1}\}_{n\geq k}
which is bounded in
W^{1,G}(B_{k}\setminus B_{1+\varepsilon_{k}})
, and thus admits a subsequence
\{u_{n}^{k}\}_{n\geq k}
which converges to
u
in
W^{1,G}(B_{k}\setminus B_{1+\varepsilon_{k}})
. It follows that
\{u_{n}^{n}\}_{n\geq 2}
converges to
u
in
W^{1,G}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B}_{1})
. We claim that
u
is a weak solution of (
1.8
). Indeed, let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B}_{1})
and take a positive integer
R\geq 2
such that supp
\,\phi\subset B_{R}\setminus B_{1+\varepsilon_{R}}
. From (
5.22
), for all
n\geq R
we have

(5.30)

\int\limits_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1+\varepsilon_{R}}}\Big(|\nabla u_{n}^{n}|^{m-2}g(|\nabla u_{n}^{n}|)+|\nabla u_{n}^{n}|^{s-2}\Big)\nabla u_{n}^{n}\cdot\nabla\phi=\int\limits_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1+\varepsilon_{R}}}\big(|x|^{-\alpha}*(u_{n}^{n})^{p}\big)(u_{n}^{n})^{q}\phi.

The sequence
\{u_{n}^{n}\}_{n\geq R}
converges to
u
in
W^{1,G}(B_{R}\setminus B_{1+\varepsilon_{R}})
. Also, since
u_{n}^{n}\leq\overline{u}
in
B_{R}\setminus B_{1}
, we may apply the Lebesgue theorem to deduce the convergence of the right-hand side of (
5.30
). Thus, letting
n\to\infty
in (
5.30
) we deduce that
u
is a
W^{1,m}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B}_{1})
solution of (
1.8
). Finally, since
\underline{u}\leq u_{n}^{n}\leq\overline{u}
in
B_{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{1}
, we find
\underline{u}\leq u\leq\overline{u}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B}_{1}
. In particular
u\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B}_{1})
and now Theorem 1.7 in
[
18
]
yields
u\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B}_{1})
. This concludes our proof.
∎
