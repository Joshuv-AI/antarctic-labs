Abstract
. In this paper we obtain the well-posedness of the transport and continuity equations in the Heisenberg groups ℍ n {\mathbb{H}}^{n} for a class of contact vector fields 𝐛 {\bf{b}} , under natural assumptions on the regularity of 𝐛 {\bf{b}} not covered by the, now classical, Euclidean theory [ 18 ] . It is the first example of well-posedness in a genuine sub-Riemannian setting, that we obtain adapting to the ℍ n {\mathbb{H}}^{n} geometry the mollification strategy of [ 18 ] . In the final part of the paper we illustrate why our result is not covered by the Euclidean B ​ V BV case solved by the first author in [ 1 ] , and we compare it with the strategy of [ 7 ] , based on the representation of the commutator by interpolation à la Bakry-Émery and an integral representation of the symmetrized derivative of 𝐛 {\bf{b}} .

1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is the investigation of the well-posedness of the transport and continuity equations (induced by a
possibly time-dependent vector field
{\bf{b}}(\tau,p)
), respectively

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\tau}}-\left\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla u\right\rangle+cu=0,\qquad\displaystyle{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\tau}}+\divv({\bf{b}}u)=0\qquad\text{in $(0,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$,}

when a genuinely non-Euclidean state space is involved, namely the
Heisenberg group
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
. In the Euclidean setting this topic has
received a lot of attention (see e.g. the Lecture Notes
[
2
,
3
]
), starting from the seminal papers

[
18
]
and
[
1
]
, which focused on Sobolev and
BV
vector fields respectively. As explained more in detail in
Section

5
, one of the reasons of this attention is the Lagrangian part of the theory: having in mind the classical theory of characteristics for first order PDE’s, well-posedness at the
Eulerian (PDE) level can be transferred to well-posedness at the Lagrangian (ODE) level, and conversely. Indeed, this problem has
been attacked by a variety of techniques: here we focus on the Eulerian ones, via well-posedness of (
1.1
), quoting only remarkable recent contributions on the Lagrangian side
[
9
,
12
,
19
]
. As we discuss more in detail below, working with the canonical coordinates of
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, our results can also
be read as new well-posedness results for a class of vector fields in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
,
but we keep our focus on the geometric and intrinsic description of
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
.

Based on the strategy introduced in
[
18
]
, a mollification with respect to the spatial variables of the transport equation produces the appearance of a
commutator

\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}
, due to the fact that
mollification and divergence do not commute:

(1.2)

\partial_{\tau}u_{\varepsilon}-\left\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle+cu_{\varepsilon}=\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}\qquad\text{in $(-\infty,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$.}

The main ingredient of the well-posedness of (
1.1
), linked to various degrees of regularity
of the components of
{\bf{b}}
and its very structure, is the proof of
strong

L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}
vanishing of the commutators as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
. When this is the case, distributional solutions to (
1.1
) are stable with respect to composition with suitable test functions, i.e. are
renormalizable
in the sense of
[
18
]
. In turn, under appropriate integrability conditions on
{\bf{b}}
, the renormalization property grants the well-posedness of
(
1.1
). In the present paper, we are interested in (possibly time-dependent) velocity fields with
horizontal
Sobolev regularity. Differently from
[
18
]
, where convergence of the commutators follows essentially by a characterization of Sobolev functions in terms of their first-order difference quotients, in our context we have to face one more
non-commutativity aspect, due to the very geometry of
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
. Indeed, the vector fields
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{2n+1}=X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n},T
representing the natural basis of the Lie algebra of
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
have non-trivial commuting relations. Nevertheless, this lack of commutativity can be balanced when
{\bf{b}}(\tau,\cdot)
is a
contact
vector field and, in particular, it possesses the structure in (
1.3
) below.

Contact vector fields in Heisenberg groups and, more generally, in contact manifolds have been extensively studied, see e.g.
[
26
,
27
,
31
]
. Contact vector fields appear naturally in the theory: in fact, for a (smooth) vector field
{\bf{b}}
in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

the flow associated to
{\bf{b}}
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to the
Carnot-Carathéodory metric
on
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
;

(ii)

the differential of the associated flow preserves the
horizontal distribution
, i.e., the subbundle linearly generated by
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},Y_{1},\ldots Y_{n}
;

(iii)

the commutator (Lie bracket) between
{\bf{b}}
and any horizontal vector field is horizontal;

(iv)

there exists for
{\bf{b}}
a “generating” function
\psi
on
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, i.e., a function such that

(1.3)

{\bf{b}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(Y_{j}\psi X_{j}-X_{j}\psi Y_{j}\right)-4\psi T.

It is the characterization in (iv) that is relevant for our purposes: in fact, we are able to prove the well-posedness of (
1.1
) for vector fields
{\bf{b}}(\tau,\cdot)
possessing the particular structure (
1.3
) and
where the generating function
\psi(\tau,\cdot)
lies in the second-order horizontal Sobolev space
W^{2,s}_{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
.
When we try to read in Euclidean terms such a regularity for
{\bf{b}}(\tau,\cdot)
, we see that we are asking less regularity with respect to the horizontal components, as
X_{j}\psi
and
Y_{j}\psi
are only in the horizontal Sobolev space

W^{1,s}_{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
, while asking more only on the vertical component, proportional to
\psi
.
Notice that vector fields with different degrees of regularity with respect to different components are not new in the well-posedness literature for (
1.1
). We mention, for instance, those in
[
28
]
of the form
({\bf{b}}_{1}(x_{1}),{\bf{b}}_{2}(x_{1},x_{2}))
, with
{\bf{b}}_{2}

only measurable with respect to
x_{1}
, but see also
[
14
,
10
]
. However, in these papers, the
commutation of the derivatives with respect to
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
plays a role in the proof of the strong convergence of suitable commutators.

As a matter of fact, the
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
-convergence of
\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}
seems to rely on a genuinely structural phenomenon. Indeed, the specific form (
1.3
) of contact vector fields yields certain fine cancelations needed in the proof. Accordingly, the multiplicative factor of the vertical component is not a mere conventional choice, but significantly falls within this scheme. This highlights the distinguished role of contact vector fields, as similar cancellations seem not to be available for more general classes, neither natural (e.g.,
{\bf{b}}
being horizontal) nor more exotic (e.g.,
{\bf{b}}
being in the linear span of the
right
-invariant horizontal subbundle).

Our approach is structured as follows.
Section

2
provides all calculus tools in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
needed in this paper, including horizontal Sobolev spaces and the presentation of contact vector fields
{\bf{b}}(\tau,\cdot)
as in (
1.3
). Even though some computations are done in coordinates, we adopt an intrinsic notation for most of the objects involved, also bearing in mind potential extensions of our results to more general classes of sub-Riemannian spaces.

Section

3
provides the main analytic results regarding convergence of difference quotients in first and second-order horizontal Sobolev spaces. Indeed, while first-order difference quotients suffice to deal with velocity fields with Euclidean Sobolev regularity, the non-commutativity structure of
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
generates second-order remainders. In turn, the contact structure of
{\bf{b}}
allows to handle them via convergence of second-order difference quotients of the vertical component of
{\bf{b}}
. We address the convergence of difference quotients by a separate study of the horizontal and vertical contributions. Precisely, denote by

w_{\mathcal{H}}
and
w_{2n+1}
the
horizontal
and
vertical
component of
w=(w_{\mathcal{H}},w_{2n+1})
. We are interested in difference quotients with respect to the intrinsic structure of
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
. Accordingly,
\cdot
denotes its group law, while
\left(\delta_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon>0}
denotes its natural, anisotropic, homogeneous structure. If
f\in W^{1,s}_{\mathcal{H}}
, we show that

\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}},0))-f(p)}{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\searrow 0]{L^{s}}\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle,\qquad\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(0,w_{2n+1}))-f(p)}{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\searrow 0]{L^{s}}0.

Instead,
if
f\in W^{2,s}_{\mathcal{H}}
, we show that

\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}},0))-f(p)-\varepsilon\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle}{\varepsilon^{2}}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\searrow 0]{L^{s}}\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\nabla^{2,{\mathcal{H}}}f(p)w_{\mathcal{H}},w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle

and

\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(0,w_{2n+1}))-f(p)}{\varepsilon^{2}}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\searrow 0]{L^{s}}w_{2n+1}Tf(p).

With a perturbative argument, these results can be used to obtain a crucial Taylor expansion
(still in
L^{s}
-sense) in
Theorem

3.5
:

(1.4)

\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p)-\varepsilon\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle}{\varepsilon^{2}}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\searrow 0]{L^{s}}\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\nabla^{2,{\mathcal{H}}}f(p)w_{\mathcal{H}},w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle+w_{2n+1}Tf(p).

Section

4
uses this expansion to prove the strong
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
-convergence of commutators

\mathscr{C}_{\epsilon}
in (
1.2
); we emphasize once again that this computation uses in an essential way the cancellations provided by the special structure of
{\bf{b}}
in (
1.3
).
More precisely,
\mathscr{C}_{\epsilon}
admits an integral representation which splits into two components: one depending on the reaction term
c
, the other depending on the velocity field
{\bf{b}}
(cf.
Proposition

4.9
). If the former can be managed by a standard procedure, the latter needs to be handled by a careful combination of the special structure of
{\bf{b}}
provided by (
1.3
) and the Taylor expansion (
1.4
). Denote by
b_{1},\ldots,b_{2n}
the horizontal components of
{\bf{b}}
, and by
b_{2n+1}
its vertical component. Beside the first-order, horizontal contribution

(1.5)

\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\frac{b_{j}(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-b_{j}(p)}{\varepsilon}u(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))Z_{j}\varrho(w)\,dw

reminiscent of its Euclidean counterpart, the contact structure of
{\bf{b}}
crucially intervenes to put in evidence the second-order, vertical correction

(1.6)

\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\frac{b_{2n+1}(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-b_{2n+1}(p)-\varepsilon\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}b_{2n+1}(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle}{\varepsilon^{2}}u(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))T\varrho(w)\,dw,

because it grants the vanishing of the the remainder

\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\frac{\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}b_{2n+1}(p)+4{\bf{J}}({\bf{b}}(p)),\delta_{\varepsilon}(w)\right\rangle}{\varepsilon^{2}}u(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))T\varrho(w)\,dw.

In the above formula,
{\bf{J}}
is a suitable ninety-degrees rotation also known as
complex structure
(see (
2.4
)). Convergence of (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) is then ensured by (
1.4
).

Once convergence of commutators has been ensured, the consequences at the level of well-posedness of the transport equation are obtained following
the path of
[
18
]
, with essentially no modification, under natural growth conditions on
u
,
{\bf{b}}
and its spatial divergence,
see
Theorem

4.6
and
Theorem

4.7
.

In
Section

5
we derive for the sake of illustration some consequences of the renormalization property, namely the existence of renormalized solutions to the continuity equation for arbitrary measurable initial data (based on
[
18
]
) and the existence and the uniqueness of Regular Lagrangian Flows in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, see
Definition

5.3
(based on
[
1
]
, under one-sided
L^{\infty}
bounds on the spatial divergence of
{\bf{b}}
).

Section

6
provides, on the basis of the functional analytic argument of
Lemma

6.1

and the use of highly oscillating test functions, the proof of the density in
W^{2,s}_{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
of generators of contact vector fields whose horizontal components do not belong to
BV(\mathbb{R}^{2n+1})
. It follows that our result cannot be deduced from
[
18
]
or
[
1
]
. We believe that similar arguments can be used to rule out the use of other regularity classes of Euclidean vector fields considered in the literature. In the end of the section we compare our approach with that of
[
7
]
, where well-posedness results have been obtained in a large class of metric measure spaces
(X,d,\mu)
, including Riemannian manifolds and the
RCD(K,\infty)
metric measure spaces of
[
4
]
. It seems that, while the setting of
[
7
]
rules out non-horizontal vector fields, the estimate on the symmetrized derivative
\dsym{\bf{b}}
(see (
6.8
)) introduced in
[
7
]
requires a non-horizontal structure. We believe that the conciliation of these two techniques requires further investigations and we leave them for the future.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Main notation

We write
\infty=+\infty
. If
\bar{\tau}\in(0,\infty]
, the notation
[0,\bar{\tau}]
stays for the usual closed interval if
\bar{\tau}<\infty
or
[0,\infty)
if
\bar{\tau}=\infty
. Given open sets
A,\Omega
, when
\overline{A}
is a compact subset of
\Omega
, we write
A\Subset\Omega
. We denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
and by
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
the Euclidean scalar product and its induced norm. If
s\in[1,\infty]
, we denote its Hölder conjugate by
s^{\prime}\in[1,\infty]
, i.e.,
\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}=1
.
Given a real-valued function
f
, we denote its support by
\mathrm{supp}(f)
.
If
f
is differentiable at a point
q
in its domain, we denote by
df_{q}
its differential at
q
. We denote either by
\mathcal{L}^{N}
or by
|\cdot|
the
N
-dimensional Lebesgue measure. If
\mu
is a measure and
f
is a
\mu
-measurable function, we denote the associated push-forward measure by
f_{\#}\mu
.

2.2.
Heisenberg groups

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, and set
N\coloneqq 2n+1
. We endow
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the (non-abelian) group law

\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\\
t\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}x^{\prime}\\
y^{\prime}\\
t^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}x+x^{\prime}\\
y+y^{\prime}\\
t+t^{\prime}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x_{j}^{\prime}y_{j}-x_{j}y^{\prime}_{j})\end{pmatrix}\text{ \qquad for every $(x,y,t),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}$.}

In this way,
(\mathbb{R}^{N},\cdot)
is (the most relevant instance of) a
stratified Lie group
(see
[
11
]
), known as
n
-th Heisenberg group
and denoted from now on by
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
. Observe that the identity element is
0
and the inverse of
p\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
is
p^{-1}=-p
. Given
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{N})=(x,y,t)\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, we may adopt the notation

w_{\mathcal{H}}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{2n})=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad|w_{\mathcal{H}}|=\sqrt{w_{1}^{2}+\ldots+w_{2n}^{2}}.

We may also write

\begin{split}x_{j}(w)=w_{j},\qquad y_{j}(w)=w_{j+n}\qquad\text{for every $j=1,\ldots,2n$. }\end{split}

The left and right translations are, respectively, defined by

L_{q}(p)=q\cdot p,\qquad R_{q}(p)=p\cdot q\qquad\text{for every $p,\,q\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$.}

A basis of the Lie algebra of left-invariant vector fields is given by

\begin{split}X_{j}(p)&=\left(dL_{p}\right)_{0}\left(\partial_{x_{j}}\right)=\partial_{x_{j}}+2y_{j}\partial_{t},\\
Y_{j}(p)&=\left(dL_{p}\right)_{0}\left(\partial_{y_{j}}\right)=\partial_{y_{j}}-2x_{j}\partial_{t},\\
T(p)&=\left(dL_{p}\right)_{0}\left(\partial_{t}\right)=\partial_{t}\end{split}\qquad\text{for every $j=1,\ldots,n$, $p=(x,y,t)\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$.}

Observe that the only nonzero commutators are

(2.1)

[Y_{j},X_{j}]=4T\qquad\text{for every $j=1,\ldots,n$}.

Because of (
2.1
), the
horizontal distribution

{\mathcal{H}}
, defined by

{\mathcal{H}}_{p}=\spann(X_{1}(p),\ldots,X_{n}(p),Y_{1}(p),\ldots,Y_{n}(p))\qquad\text{for every $p\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$,}

satisfies the so-called
Hörmander condition
(see
[
11
]
).
We endow
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
with the unique left-invariant Riemannian metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
that makes this basis orthonormal. We may also write

Z_{j}=X_{j},\qquad Z_{j+n}=Y_{j},\qquad Z_{N}=T\qquad\text{for every $j=1,\ldots,n$.}

Denoting by
{\mathbb{X}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}
the class of locally integrable vector fields in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
,
any
{\bf{b}}\in{\mathbb{X}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}
can be canonically written as
\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}Z_{j}
and

\langle{\bf{b}},{\bf{b}}^{\prime}\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}b_{j}^{\prime}
. Accordingly, with respect to this basis, the intrinsic gradient of a function
f
(with suitable regularity) is denoted by

\nabla f=(X_{1}f,\ldots,X_{n}f,Y_{1}f,\ldots,Y_{n}f,Tf),

while occasionally we denote by
\nabla^{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f
the gradient with respect to the Euclidean structure. The Riemannian measure induced by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
coincides with the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{N}
. Accordingly, the divergence of a vector field
{\bf{b}}
, defined by the formula

\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}d\varphi({\bf{b}})\,dp=-\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\varphi\,\divv{\bf{b}}\,dp\qquad\text{for every $\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})$},

coincides with the Euclidean divergence. Moreover,

\divv\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}Z_{j}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}Z_{j}b_{j}.

In addition, if
u
is a smooth function and
{\bf{b}}\in{\mathbb{X}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}
, one has

(2.2)

\left\langle\nabla^{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u,{\bf{b}}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}=du({\bf{b}})=\left\langle\nabla u,{\bf{b}}\right\rangle.

By the above considerations, the transport equation can be equivalently formulated owing to the Riemannian structure that we have fixed.
The
horizontal gradient
and the
horizontal Hessian
(see
[
11
]
) are, respectively, defined by

\begin{split}\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f&=(X_{1}f,\ldots,X_{n}f,Y_{1}f,\ldots,Y_{n}f),\\
\left(\nabla^{2,{\mathcal{H}}}f\right)_{ij}&=Z_{i}Z_{j}f\qquad\text{for every $i,j=1,\ldots,2n$.}\end{split}

We will also consider the right-invariant vector fields

(2.3)

\begin{split}X_{j}^{r}(p)&=Z_{j}^{r}(p)=\left(dR_{p}\right)_{0}\left(\partial_{x_{j}}\right)=X_{j}(p)-4y_{j}T(p),\\
Y_{j}^{r}(p)&=Z_{n+j}^{r}(p)=\left(dR_{p}\right)_{0}\left(\partial_{y_{j}}\right)=Y_{j}(p)+4x_{j}T(p),\\
T^{r}(p)&=Z_{N}^{r}(p)=\left(dR_{p}\right)_{0}\left(\partial_{t}\right)=T(p)\end{split}\qquad\text{for $j=1,\ldots,n$, $p=(x,y,t)\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$.}

Left-invariant vector fields are complete (see
[
29
, Theorem 9.18]
). Accordingly, if
Z
is a left-invariant vector field and
\gamma
is its integral curve starting from
0
, we set

\exp(Z)=\gamma(1).

We endow
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
with a
homogeneous structure
provided by
intrinsic dilations
(see
[
11
]
).
Namely, we set

\delta_{\lambda}(x,y,t)=(\lambda x,\lambda y,\lambda^{2}t)\qquad\text{for every $\lambda\geq 0$, $(x,y,t)\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$}.

In this way,
\delta_{\lambda}
is a Lie group isomorphism of
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
for any
\lambda>0
. Moreover, we will exploit the
complex structure

{\bf{J}}:{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
(cf.
[
15
]
) defined by

(2.4)

{\bf{J}}(x,y,t)=(-y,x,0)\qquad\text{for every $(x,y,t)\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$}.

It is easy to see that

(2.5)

\begin{split}{\bf{J}}({\bf{J}}(w))&=(-w_{\mathcal{H}},0),\\
\langle{\bf{J}}(w),z\rangle&=-\langle w,{\bf{J}}(z)\rangle\end{split}\qquad\text{for every $w,z\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$.}

We equip
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
with the so-called
Carnot-Carathéodory distance

d
(see
[
34
]
). We recall that, given
p,q\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
,

d(p,q)=\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\left\langle\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle}\,d\tau\,:\,\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\text{ is absolutely continuous, horizontal, }\gamma(0)=p,\,\gamma(1)=q\right\},

where an absolutely continuous curve is
horizontal
if
\dot{\gamma}(\tau)\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\gamma(\tau)}
for a.e.
\tau
.
The Carnot-Carathéodory distance is compatible both with the group structure and the homogeneous structure, namely

(2.6)

\begin{split}d(w\cdot p,w\cdot q)&=d(p,q),\\
d(\delta_{\lambda}(p),\delta_{\lambda}(q))&=\lambda d(p,q)\end{split}\qquad\text{for every $p,q,w\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n},\,\lambda>0$.}

We indicate by

B(p,r)=\{q\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}:d(p,q)<r\}

the open ball with center
p\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
and radius
r>0
. We denote by
\mathrm{Lip}(f)
the Lipschitz constant of
f:{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
with respect to
d
.
The Lebesgue measure
|\cdot|_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}
is both a left and a right Haar measure of
({\mathbb{H}}^{n},\cdot)
(see
[
34
]
). In particular,

(2.7)

\begin{split}\left\lvert L_{p}(E)\right\rvert&=\lvert E\rvert,\\
\left\lvert R_{p}(E)\right\rvert&=\lvert E\rvert\end{split}\qquad\text{for every $E\subseteq{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$ measurable, $p\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$.}

Moreover,

(2.8)

\left\lvert\delta_{\lambda}(E)\right\rvert=\lambda^{Q}\left\lvert E\right\rvert\qquad\text{for every $E\subseteq{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$ measurable, $\lambda\geq 0$,}

where
Q\coloneqq 2n+2
is known as
homogeneous dimension
of
({\mathbb{H}}^{n},\cdot)
(see
[
34
]
). Left and right-invariant vector fields are related by the inversion map as follows.

Lemma 2.1
.

Define
\iota:{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
by
\iota(q)=q^{-1}
. Then

(2.9)

Z_{j}(\varphi\circ\iota)=-Z_{j}^{r}\varphi\circ\iota\qquad\text{for every }\varphi\in C^{1}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}),\,j=1,\ldots,N.

In particular, if
\varphi=\varphi\circ\iota
, then
Z_{j}\varphi=-Z_{j}^{r}\varphi\circ\iota
.

Proof.

For every
q\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, the identity
\iota\circ L_{q}=R_{q^{-1}}\circ\iota
holds. Differentiating it at
0
,

d\iota_{q}((dL_{q})_{0}(Z_{j}(0))=(dR_{q^{-1}})_{0}(d\iota_{0}(Z_{j}(0)),

that is, thanks to the left-invariance of
Z_{j}
and the fact that
-d\iota
is the identity map,

(2.10)

d\iota_{q}(Z_{j}(q))=-(dR_{q^{-1}})_{0}(Z_{j}(0))=-Z_{j}^{r}\left(q^{-1}\right).

Therefore,

\begin{split}Z_{j}(\varphi\circ\iota)(q)=d\varphi_{q^{-1}}(d\iota_{q}(Z_{j}(q)))\overset{\eqref{eq_differential_inversion}}{=}-d\varphi_{q^{-1}}\left(Z_{j}^{r}\left(q^{-1}\right)\right)=-Z_{j}^{r}\varphi\left(q^{-1}\right)=-(Z_{j}^{r}\varphi\circ\iota)(q),\end{split}

which is the thesis.
∎

We recall that right translations are continuous in
L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
s\in[1,\infty)
and
f\in L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
. Then
\lim_{q\to 0}\left\lVert f\circ R_{q}-f\right\rVert_{L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}=0
.

Proof.

The family of linear operators
\{f\circ R_{q}-f\}_{q\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
due to (
2.7
). Since they converge to
0
as
q\to 0
when
f\in C_{c}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
, the density of
C_{c}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
in
L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
grants that the same property holds for any
f\in L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
.
∎

A similar property holds for left translations, with the same proof. In the sequel many other convergence results, in Sobolev classes, will be achieved by a similar functional analytic argument.

2.3.
Group convolution

For an account on group convolution, we refer to
[
21
]
. Given
f,g\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
, their
group convolution

f*g
is defined by

(f*g)(p)\coloneqq\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}f(q)g\left(q^{-1}\cdot p\right)\,dq\overset{\eqref{eq_Lebesgue_Haar}}{=}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}f\left(p\cdot q^{-1}\right)g(q)\,dq\qquad\text{for every $p\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$,}

provided that the integrals converge. If the domain of
f
and
g
is an arbitrary open set
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, we extend them to be
0
outside
\Omega
, and
f*g
is still well-defined. If in addition
g\in C^{1}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
,

(2.11)

\begin{split}Z(f*g)&=f*Zg\,\qquad\text{if $Z$ is left-invariant,}\end{split}

since
f*g
can be viewed as the superposition, weighted by
f
, of left translations of
g
.
In order to introduce group mollification, we fix a mollifier
\varrho\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
such that

(2.12)

\varrho\geq 0,\quad\varrho(p)=\varrho\left(p^{-1}\right),\quad\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\varrho\,dp=1\quad\text{and}\quad\textrm{supp}(\varrho)\subseteq B(0,1).

For every
\varepsilon>0
, set

(2.13)

\varrho_{\varepsilon}(p)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{Q}}\varrho\left(\delta_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}(p)\right)\qquad\text{for every $p\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$.}

Notice that

(2.14)

\begin{split}Z^{r}_{j}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(p)&=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{Q+1}}Z^{r}_{j}\varrho\left(\delta_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}(p)\right)\qquad\text{for every $j=1,\ldots,2n$,\, $p\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$,}\\
T\varrho_{\varepsilon}(p)&=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{Q+2}}T\varrho\left(\delta_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}(p)\right)\qquad\text{for every $p\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$,}\end{split}

and analogous formulas hold for every
Z_{j}
. The following proposition collects standard convergence properties of group mollification.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
s\in[1,\infty)
. Let
u\in L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
and set
u_{\varepsilon}=u*\varrho_{\varepsilon}
for every
\varepsilon>0
. Then

(2.15)

\begin{split}\left\lVert u*\varrho_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}&\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}\end{split}\qquad\text{for every $\varepsilon>0$.}

Moreover,
u_{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{L^{s}}u
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a distribution acting on
C^{\infty}_{c}\left([0,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\right)
and
\varrho\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
, we define the
spatial group convolution of
\mathcal{F}
by
\varrho
by

(2.16)

\left\langle\mathcal{F}*\varrho,\varphi\right\rangle\coloneqq\left\langle\mathcal{F},\varphi*\check{\varrho}\right\rangle\qquad\text{for every $\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}\left([0,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\right)$,}

where
\check{\varrho}(p)\coloneqq\varrho\left(p^{-1}\right)
, so that

\qquad\left(\varphi*\check{\varrho}\right)(\tau,p)=\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\varphi(\tau,q)\check{\varrho}\left(q^{-1}\cdot p\right)\,dq=\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\varphi(\tau,q)\varrho\left(p^{-1}\cdot q\right)\,dq\qquad\text{for every $(\tau,p)\in[0,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$.}

2.4.
Horizontal Sobolev spaces and contact vector fields

As pointed out in the introduction, we will consider vector fields
{\bf{b}}\in{\mathbb{X}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}
with
horizontal
Sobolev regularity. To this end, fix an open subset
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
and
u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
. If
j=1,\ldots,2n
, we define the distribution
Z_{j}u
by

\left\langle Z_{j}u,\varphi\right\rangle=-\int_{\Omega}uZ_{j}\varphi\,dp\qquad\text{for every $\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)$}.

If
s\in[1,\infty]
, we define the
horizontal Sobolev space

W^{1,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
by

W^{1,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\coloneqq\{u\in L^{s}(\Omega)\,:\,Z_{j}u\in L^{s}(\Omega)\text{ for every $j=1,\ldots,2n$}\}.

The spaces
W^{1,s}_{{\mathcal{H}},\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and
W^{k,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
, for
k=2,3,\ldots
, are defined accordingly. It is well-known (see
[
21
]
) that the vector space
W^{k,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
, endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{k,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\|u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}+\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\|Z_{j}u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}+\cdots+\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}=1}^{2n}\|Z_{j_{1}}\cdots Z_{j_{k}}u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)},

is a Banach space for every
1\leq s\leq\infty
and
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, reflexive when
1<s<\infty
.

The following Meyers-Serrin type approximation result holds (cf.
[
23
]
, and cf.
[
22
]
when
k=1
).

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
be an open set. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
and
s\in[1,\infty)
. Let
u\in W^{k,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
. There exists a sequence
\{u_{h}\}_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{k,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
such that

\lim_{h\to\infty}\|u_{h}-u\|_{W^{k,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)}=0.

The commutation relation (
2.1
) holds as well in the horizontal Sobolev setting.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
be an open subset. Let
s\in[1,\infty]
. Then
W^{2,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\subseteq W^{1,s}(\Omega)
, and

Tu=\frac{1}{4}(Y_{j}X_{j}-X_{j}Y_{j})u\qquad\text{for every $u\in W^{2,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)$, $j=1,\ldots,n$.}

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. Let
j=1,\ldots,n
. It suffices to notice that

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}u\,T\varphi\,dp=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}u\left(Y_{j}X_{j}\varphi-X_{j}Y_{j}\varphi\right)\,dp=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\left(X_{j}Y_{j}u-Y_{j}X_{j}u\right)\varphi\,dp=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{4}(Y_{j}X_{j}-X_{j}Y_{j})u\,\varphi\,dp.\end{split}

The thesis follows.
∎

We employ horizontal Sobolev spaces to introduce vector fields with horizontal Sobolev regularity. We recall that a smooth vector field
{\bf{b}}\in{\mathbb{X}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}
is called a
contact vector field
if its flow preserves the horizontal distribution
{\mathcal{H}}
(see
[
27
]
). In this case,
{\bf{b}}
admits an explicit representation in terms of a smooth
generating function

\psi
, namely

(2.17)

{\bf{b}}=-4\psi T+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(Y_{j}\psi X_{j}-X_{j}\psi Y_{j})=-4\psi T-{\bf{J}}\left(\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}\psi\right),

where in the second equality we extended the action of
{\bf{J}}
in (
2.4
) also to gradients. Therefore, it makes sense to define contact vector fields with horizontal Sobolev regularity as those representable by a generating function
\psi\in W^{2,s}_{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
for some
s\in[1,\infty]
according to (
2.17
). In this way,
Lemma

2.5
implies that
b_{N}\in W^{1,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In particular, owing again to
Lemma

2.5
, it is easy to verify that

\divv{\bf{b}}=-4(n+1)T\psi\in L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}).

3.
Convergence of difference quotients

In this section we study the asymptotic behavior of difference quotients of horizontal Sobolev functions. However, differently e.g. from
[
13
]
, we consider difference quotients along intrinsically dilated left translations rather than along horizontal flows. This fact requires to carefully deal with suitable vertical remainders, as it will be clearer in a while. These results will be fundamental in order to prove the regularization property of the next section. Along this section,
\Omega
is a fixed open set in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
.
We begin by recalling the first and second-order behavior of smooth functions along dilated left translations.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Let
p\in\Omega
,
\tau>0
and
w\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
. Assume that
p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w)\in\Omega
. Then

(3.1)

\frac{d(f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w)))}{d\tau}=\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w)),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle+2\tau w_{N}Tf(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w))

and

(3.2)

\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}(f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w)))}{d\tau^{2}}&=\left\langle\nabla^{2,{\mathcal{H}}}f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w))w_{\mathcal{H}},w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle+2w_{N}Tf(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w))\\
&\quad+4\tau w_{N}\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}Tf(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w)),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle+4\tau^{2}w_{N}^{2}TTf(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w)).\end{split}

Proof.

Observe that, for every
\tau>0
,

\frac{d(\delta_{\tau}(w))}{d\tau}=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}w_{i}Z_{i}(\delta_{\tau}(w))+2\tau w_{N}T(\delta_{\tau}(w)).

Hence, setting
\gamma(\tau)=p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w)=L_{p}(\delta_{\tau}(w))
for every
\tau>0
, we obtain, by left-invariance,

\begin{split}\dot{\gamma}(\tau)&=\left(dL_{p}\right)_{\delta_{\tau}(w)}\left(\frac{d(\delta_{\tau}(w))}{d\tau}\right)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}w_{i}\left(dL_{p}\right)_{\delta_{\tau}(w)}(Z_{i}(\delta_{\tau}(w)))+2\tau w_{N}\left(dL_{p}\right)_{\delta_{\tau}(w)}(T(\delta_{\tau}(w)))\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}w_{i}Z_{i}(\gamma(\tau))+2\tau w_{N}T(\gamma(\tau)).\end{split}

Having this, the two formulas follow from a direct computation. First,

\begin{split}\frac{d(f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w)))}{d\tau}&=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}Z_{i}f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w))w_{i}+2\tau Tf(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w))w_{N}\\
&=\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w)),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle+2\tau w_{N}Tf(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w)).\end{split}

Furthermore,

\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}(f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w)))}{d\tau^{2}}&=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}Z_{j}Z_{i}f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w))w_{i}w_{j}+2\tau\sum_{i=1}^{2n}TZ_{i}f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w))w_{i}w_{N}\\
&\quad+2Tf(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w))w_{N}+2\tau w_{N}\sum_{i=1}^{2n}Z_{i}Tf(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w))w_{i}+4\tau^{2}w_{N}^{2}TTf(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w))\\
&=\left\langle\nabla^{2,{\mathcal{H}}}f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w))w_{\mathcal{H}},w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle+2w_{N}Tf(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w))\\
&\quad+4\tau w_{N}\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}Tf(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w)),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle+4\tau^{2}w_{N}^{2}TTf(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w)).\end{split}

∎

The next result shows that vertical difference quotients disappear in the limit.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
s\in[1,\infty)
and let
f\in W_{\mathcal{H}}^{1,s}(\Omega)
. Let
A\subseteq\Omega
be open and such that
\mathrm{dist}(A,\partial\Omega)>0
. Then

(3.3)

\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}^{L^{s}(A)}\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))}{\varepsilon}=0\qquad\text{for every $w\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$.}

In addition, if
w\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
and
\varepsilon>0
satisfy

(3.4)

\varepsilon\left(|w_{\mathcal{H}}|+2\sqrt{|w_{N}|}\right)<\mathrm{dist}(A,\partial\Omega),

one has

(3.5)

\left\|\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))}{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{s}(A)}\leq 2\sqrt{|w_{N}|}\left\|\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f\right\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that both properties hold when
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
: indeed, if this holds, then (
3.5
) extends to
all
f\in W_{\mathcal{H}}^{1,s}(\Omega)
thanks to
Theorem

2.4
(notice that the argument of the limit on the left hand side of (
3.3
) is
L^{s}
-continuous
when
\varepsilon
is fixed). Then, the operators in the left hand side result uniformly bounded in
W_{\mathcal{H}}^{1,s}(\Omega)
, and from the
density of
C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
in
W_{\mathcal{H}}^{1,s}(\Omega)
one obtains the validity of (
3.3
).
Fix
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
. Fix
p\in A
. If
w_{N}=0
, there is nothing to prove. Assume that
w_{N}>0
. Assume that
\varepsilon>0
satisfies (
3.4
). By definition of
d
,
d\left(p,p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}})\right)\leq\varepsilon|w_{\mathcal{H}}|
, whence
p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}})\in\Omega
by (
3.4
). We exhibit a horizontal curve joining
p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}})
and
p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w)
: start from
p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}})
, follow the flow of
Y_{1}
and then, in order, those of
X_{1}
,
-Y_{1}
and
-X_{1}
, with time
\tilde{\tau}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sqrt{w_{N}}
at each step. By the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula (cf.
[
36
]
),

\begin{split}\exp(\tilde{\tau}Y_{1})&\cdot\exp(\tilde{\tau}X_{1})\cdot\exp(-\tilde{\tau}Y_{1})\cdot\exp(-\tilde{\tau}X_{1})\\
&=\exp\left(\tilde{\tau}Y_{1}+\tilde{\tau}X_{1}+\frac{\tilde{\tau}^{2}}{2}[Y_{1},X_{1}]\right)\cdot\exp\left(-\tilde{\tau}Y_{1}-\tilde{\tau}X_{1}+\frac{\tilde{\tau}^{2}}{2}[Y_{1},X_{1}]\right)\\
&=\exp(\tilde{\tau}^{2}[Y_{1},X_{1}])\\
&=\exp(\varepsilon^{2}w_{N}T).\end{split}

The case
w_{N}<0
could be handled similarly. In particular, if
q
is any point along the above curve,

d(p,q)\leq d(p,p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))+d(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}),q)\leq\varepsilon\left(|w_{\mathcal{H}}|+2\sqrt{|w_{N}|}\right),

whence
q\in\Omega
by (
3.4
).
Set

\begin{split}\bar{w}_{0}&=\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}),\\
\bar{w}_{1}&=\bar{w}_{0}\cdot\exp(\tilde{\tau}Y_{1}),\\
\bar{w}_{2}&=\bar{w}_{1}\cdot\exp(\tilde{\tau}X_{1}),\\
\bar{w}_{3}&=\bar{w}_{2}\cdot\exp(-\tilde{\tau}Y_{1}),\\
\bar{w}_{4}&=\bar{w}_{3}\cdot\exp(-\tilde{\tau}X_{1})=\delta_{\varepsilon}(w).\end{split}

Then

\begin{split}f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))&=[f(p\cdot\bar{w}_{4})-f(p\cdot\bar{w}_{3})+f(p\cdot\bar{w}_{2})-f(p\cdot\bar{w}_{1})]\\
&\quad+[f(p\cdot\bar{w}_{3})-f(p\cdot\bar{w}_{2})+f(p\cdot\bar{w}_{1})-f(p\cdot\bar{w}_{0})]\\
&=\int_{0}^{\tilde{\tau}}[-X_{1}f(p\cdot\bar{w}_{3}\cdot\exp(-\tau X_{1}))+X_{1}f(p\cdot\bar{w}_{1}\cdot\exp(\tau X_{1}))]\,d\tau\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{\tilde{\tau}}[-Y_{1}f(p\cdot\bar{w}_{2}\cdot\exp(-\tau Y_{1}))+Y_{1}f(p\cdot\bar{w}_{0}\cdot\exp(\tau Y_{1}))]\,d\tau.\end{split}

Computing the
L^{s}
-norm, Minkowski’s integral inequality (cf.
[
30
, Corollary B.83]
) implies

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{A}\left\lvert f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))\right\rvert^{s}\,dp\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\\
&\quad\leq\int_{0}^{\tilde{\tau}}\left(\int_{A}\left\lvert X_{1}f(p\cdot\bar{w}_{3}\cdot\exp(-\tau X_{1}))-X_{1}f(p\cdot\bar{w}_{1}\cdot\exp(\tau X_{1}))\right\rvert^{s}\,dp\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\,d\tau\\
&\qquad+\int_{0}^{\tilde{\tau}}\left(\int_{A}\left\lvert Y_{1}f(p\cdot\bar{w}_{2}\cdot\exp(-\tau Y_{1}))-Y_{1}f(p\cdot\bar{w}_{0}\cdot\exp(\tau Y_{1}))\right\rvert^{s}\,dp\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\,d\tau.\end{split}

Dividing (
3.6
) by
\varepsilon
, since
\tilde{\tau}=O(\varepsilon)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
,
Lemma

2.2
implies (
3.3
).
In order to prove (
3.5
), just use the invariance of
\mathcal{L}^{N}
under right translations and the explicit formula of
\tilde{\tau}
.
The thesis follows.
∎

We are ready to prove the main convergence results for first-order difference quotients.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
s\in[1,\infty)
and let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
be open. Let
f\in W_{\mathcal{H}}^{1,s}(\Omega)
. Let
A\subseteq\Omega
be open and such that
\mathrm{dist}(A,\partial\Omega)>0
. Then

(3.7)

\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}^{L^{s}(A)}\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p)}{\varepsilon}=\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle\qquad\text{for every $w\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$.}

In addition, if
\varepsilon>0
and
w\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
satisfy (
3.4
), one has

(3.8)

\left\|\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p)}{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{s}(A)}\leq\left(|w_{\mathcal{H}}|+2\sqrt{w_{N}}\right)\left\|\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f\right\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

As in the previous proof, it suffices to assume
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
. If
p\in A
and
\varepsilon>0
satisfies (
3.4
), one has

f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))-f(p)-\varepsilon\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle\overset{\eqref{derivataalongdilation}}{=}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\left[\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w_{\mathcal{H}})),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle\right]\,d\tau.

Dividing by
\varepsilon
, computing the
L^{s}
-norm and using Minkowski’s integral inequality, we get

(3.9)

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{A}\left|\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))-f(p)}{\varepsilon}-\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle\right|^{s}\,dp\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\\
&\quad\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{A}\left\lvert\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))-\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{s}\,dp\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\,d\tau.\end{split}

By
Lemma

2.2
, this clearly implies (
3.7
). Then, (
3.8
) follows combining
(
3.5
) with

(3.10)

\left\|\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))-f(p)}{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{s}(A)}\leq|w_{\mathcal{H}}|\left\|\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f\right\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}.

In turn, this inequality can be obtained from the same argument leading to (
3.9
), starting from

f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))-f(p)=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w_{\mathcal{H}})),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle\,d\tau

and using the invariance of
\mathcal{L}^{N}
under right translations.
∎

Next, we deal with second-order difference quotients.
We first compute the contribution of vertical difference quotients.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
s\in[1,\infty)
and let
f\in W_{\mathcal{H}}^{2,s}(\Omega)
. Let
A\subseteq\Omega
be open and such that
\mathrm{dist}(A,\partial\Omega)>0
. Then

(3.11)

\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}^{L^{s}(A)}\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))}{\varepsilon^{2}}=w_{N}Tf(p)\qquad\text{for every $w\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$.}

In addition, if
\varepsilon>0
and
w\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
satisfy (
3.4
), one has

(3.12)

\left\|\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\|_{L^{s}(A)}\leq|w_{N}|\|Tf\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Again, it suffices to assume
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{2,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
. We know that

(3.13)

f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))=2w_{N}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\tau Tf(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}})\cdot\delta_{\tau}(\exp(w_{N}T)))\,d\tau.

Hence,

\begin{split}\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))}{\varepsilon^{2}}-w_{N}Tf(p)=\frac{2w_{N}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\tau[Tf(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}})\cdot\delta_{\tau}(\exp(w_{N}T)))-Tf(p)]\,d\tau.\end{split}

Computing the
L^{s}
-norm and applying Minkowski’s integral inequality, we obtain

(3.14)

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{A}\left|\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))}{\varepsilon^{2}}-w_{N}Tf(p)\right|^{s}\,dp\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\\
&\quad\leq\frac{2\left\lvert w_{N}\right\rvert}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\tau\left(\int_{A}\left\lvert Tf(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}})\cdot\delta_{\tau}(\exp(w_{N}T)))-Tf(p)\right\rvert^{s}\,dp\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\,d\tau.\end{split}

This and
Lemma

2.2
clearly imply (
3.11
). Then, (
3.12
) follows
by the same argument leading to (
3.14
), starting from (
3.13
)
and using the invariance of
\mathcal{L}^{N}
under right translations.
∎

The next result describes the asymptotic behavior of second-order difference quotients.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
s\in[1,\infty)
and let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
be open. Let
f\in W_{\mathcal{H}}^{2,s}(\Omega)
. Let
A\subseteq\Omega
be open and such that
\mathrm{dist}(A,\partial\Omega)>0
. Then

(3.15)

\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}^{L^{s}(A)}\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p)-\varepsilon\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle}{\varepsilon^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\nabla^{2,{\mathcal{H}}}f(p)w_{\mathcal{H}},w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle+w_{N}Tf(p)

for every
w\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
. In addition, if
\varepsilon>0
and
w\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
satisfy (
3.4
), one has

(3.16)

\left\|\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p)-\varepsilon\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\|_{L^{s}(A)}\leq|w_{N}|\|Tf\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}+|w_{\mathcal{H}}|^{2}\left\|\nabla^{{\mathcal{H}},2}f\right\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

As customary, As we assume
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{2,s}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
such that (
3.4
) holds. One has

f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))-f(p)-\varepsilon\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle\overset{\eqref{derivataalongdilation_2nd}}{=}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon-\tau)\left\langle\nabla^{2,{\mathcal{H}}}f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))w_{\mathcal{H}},w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle\,d\tau,

so that

\begin{split}f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))-&f(p)-\varepsilon\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\left\langle\nabla^{2,{\mathcal{H}}}f(p)w_{\mathcal{H}},w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle\\
&=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon-\tau)\left\langle\left[\nabla^{2,{\mathcal{H}}}f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))-\nabla^{2,{\mathcal{H}}}f(p)\right]w_{\mathcal{H}},w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle\,d\tau.\end{split}

Dividing by
\varepsilon^{2}
and computing the
L^{s}
-norm we infer (as in the proof of
Lemma

3.2
)

(3.17)

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{A}\left|\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))-f(p)-\varepsilon\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle}{\varepsilon^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\nabla^{2,{\mathcal{H}}}f_{k}(p)w_{\mathcal{H}},w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle\right|^{s}\,dp\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\\
&\quad\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon-\tau)\left(\int_{A}\left\lvert\left\langle\left[\nabla^{2,{\mathcal{H}}}f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))-\nabla^{2,{\mathcal{H}}}f(p)\right]w_{\mathcal{H}},w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{s}\,dp\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\,d\tau\\
&\quad\leq\frac{\left\lvert w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rvert^{2}}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{A}\left\lvert\nabla^{2,{\mathcal{H}}}f(p\cdot\delta_{\tau}(w_{\mathcal{H}}))-\nabla^{2,{\mathcal{H}}}f(p)\right\rvert^{s}\,dp\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\,d\tau.\end{split}

Therefore, (
3.15
) follows by
Lemma

2.2
and by
Lemma

3.4
. In addition (
3.12
) and (
3.17
)
grant (
3.16
).
∎

Finally, we address the case
s=\infty
. As in the Euclidean case, the uniform convergence of difference quotients is in general false. However, it is still possible to prove their uniform boundedness. For instance, fix
f\in W^{1,\infty}_{{\mathcal{H}},\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and fix bounded open sets
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
and
\tilde{\Omega}\Subset\Omega
. By
[
24
]
,
f
is Lipschitz continuous on
\Omega
with respect to
d
. Denoting its Lipschitz constant by
\mathrm{Lip}(f,\Omega)
, there exists
c_{1}=c_{1}(\tilde{\Omega})>0
such that

(3.18)

\left|\frac{f\left(p\cdot\delta_{\epsilon}(w)\right)-f(p)}{\varepsilon}\right|\leq\mathrm{Lip}(f,\Omega)d(0,w)\qquad\text{for every $p\in\tilde{\Omega}$, $w\in B(0,1)$, $0<\varepsilon<c_{1}$.}

Similar bounds hold for second-order difference quotients. For instance, in the following we may exploit the following result.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
f\in W_{{\mathcal{H}},\mathrm{loc}}^{2,\infty}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
. Let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
be a bounded open set. Let
R=R(\Omega)>0
be such that
\Omega\Subset B(0,R)
. Then there exists
c_{1}=c_{1}(\Omega)>0
such that

\left|\frac{f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p)-\varepsilon\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right|\leq\mathrm{Lip}\left(\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f,B(0,R+c_{1})\right)d(0,w)^{2}

for every
p\in\Omega
,
w\in B(0,1)
,
0<\varepsilon<c_{1}
.

Proof.

Fix
w\in B(0,1)
.
Since
\Omega\Subset B(0,R)
, there exists
c_{1}=c_{1}(\Omega)>0
such that
p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w)\in B(0,R)
for every
p\in\Omega
and every
0<\varepsilon<c_{1}
.
Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
be an absolutely continuous, horizontal curve such that
\gamma(0)=p,\gamma(1)=p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w)
and

(3.19)

d(p,p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\left\langle\dot{\gamma}(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau)\right\rangle}\,d\tau.

The existence of such a curve is ensured e.g. by
[
34
, Theorem 2.13]
.
Since
f\in W^{2,\infty}_{{\mathcal{H}},\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
, then
f\in C({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
and
Z_{j}f\in C({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
for any
j=1,\ldots,2n
(cf.
[
24
]
). In particular, by
[
33
, Proposition 2.6]
,

f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p)=\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(\gamma(\tau)),\dot{\gamma}(\tau)\right\rangle\,d\tau.

Moreover,

\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),\dot{\gamma}(\tau)\right\rangle\,d\tau=\sum_{j=1}^{2n}Z_{j}f(p)\int_{0}^{1}\dot{\gamma}_{j}(\tau)\,d\tau=\sum_{j=1}^{2n}Z_{j}f(p)(p_{j}+\varepsilon w_{j}-p_{j})=\varepsilon\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),w_{H}\right\rangle,

whence

f(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p)-\varepsilon\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),w_{H}\right\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(\gamma(\tau))-\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),\dot{\gamma}(\tau)\right\rangle\,d\tau.

Notice that
\gamma(\tau)\in B(0,R+c_{1})
for any
\tau\in(0,1)
. Indeed, being
\gamma
length-minimizing by (
3.19
),

d(0,\gamma(\tau))\leq d(0,p)+d(p,\gamma(\tau))\leq d(0,p)+d(p,p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))<R+\varepsilon d(0,w)<R+c_{1}.

Hence, since
\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f
is Lipschitz continuous on
B(0,R+c_{1})
and
\gamma
is length-minimizing,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(\gamma(\tau))-\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f(p),\dot{\gamma}(\tau)\right\rangle\,d\tau&\leq\mathrm{Lip}\left(\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f,B(0,R+c_{1})\right)\int_{0}^{1}d(p,\gamma(\tau))\left\lvert\dot{\gamma}(\tau)\right\rvert\,d\tau\\
&\leq\mathrm{Lip}\left(\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f,B(0,R+c_{1})\right)d(p,p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))\int_{0}^{1}\left\lvert\dot{\gamma}\right\rvert\,d\tau\\
\overset{\eqref{eq_CC_distance}}{}&{=}\mathrm{Lip}\left(\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f,B(0,R+c_{1})\right)\varepsilon^{2}d(w,0)^{2},\end{split}

which is the thesis.
∎

4.
Distributional solutions are renormalizable

In this section we prove that contact velocity fields with horizontal Sobolev regularity enjoy the renormalization property.

4.1.
Transport equation and distributional solutions

We begin by recalling some basic definitions and properties of transport equations.
If
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is an open interval, we consider Borel functions
u:I\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
up to
\mathcal{L}^{1+N}
-negligible sets. Inside this class, given

\bar{\tau}\in(0,\infty]
and
\alpha,\beta\in[1,\infty]
, we consider (see
[
30
]
) the space
L^{\alpha}\left(0,\bar{\tau};L^{\beta}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\right)
of those
functions
u
such that

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}(0,\bar{\tau};L^{\beta}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}))}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}\left\lVert u(\cdot,\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}^{\alpha}\,d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}&\text{if $\alpha\in[1,\infty)$}\\
\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\limits_{\tau\in(0,\bar{\tau})}\,\left\lVert u(\cdot,\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}&\text{if $\alpha=\infty$}\end{cases}

is finite.
Its local version
L^{\alpha}\left(0,\bar{\tau};L^{\beta}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\right)
is defined similarly. Moreover, we recall that when
\alpha=\beta
,
L^{\alpha}\left(0,\bar{\tau};L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\right)
naturally identifies with
L^{\alpha}\left((0,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\right)
.
If
{\bf{b}}\in{\mathbb{X}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}
is a velocity field,
c
is a reaction term and
u_{0}
is an initial condition, the Cauchy problem for the
transport equation
associated with
{\bf{b}},c
and
u_{0}
reads formally as

(
\mathscr{T}
)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\tau}-\left\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla u\right\rangle+cu=0}&\text{in $(0,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$}\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}&\text{in ${\mathbb{H}}^{n}$.}\end{cases}

The natural notion of solution to (

T

) is that of
distributional solution
.

Definition 4.1

(Distributional solutions)
.

Fix
s\in[1,\infty]
and assume that

(4.1)

{\bf{b}}\in L^{1}\left(0,\bar{\tau};L^{s^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n};{\mathbb{H}}^{n})\right),\qquad c,\divv{\bf{b}}\in L^{1}\left(0,\bar{\tau};L^{s^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\right),\qquad u_{0}\in L^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}).

We say that
u\in L^{\infty}\left(0,\bar{\tau};L^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\right)
(
u\in L^{\infty}\left((0,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\right)
if
s=\infty
) is a
distributional solution
to (

T

) if

\left\langle\mathscr{T}_{u_{0},{\bf{b}},c}(u),\varphi\right\rangle\coloneqq-\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u_{0}(p)\varphi(0,p)\,dp+\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u\left[-\partial_{\tau}\varphi+\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla\varphi\rangle+(c+\divv{\bf{b}})\varphi\right]\,dp\,d\tau=0

for every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n})
.

Notice that, thanks to (
4.1
) and to the
L^{s}-L^{s^{\prime}}
duality,
Definition

4.1
is well-posed. Existence of solutions to (

T

) is guaranteed, in great generality, by
[
18
, Proposition II.1]
(even though the result is stated in Euclidean spaces, we may apply it thanks to (
2.2
) and the invariance of the divergence in the Euclidean and Heisenberg structures).

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
s\in[1,\infty]
and assume (
4.1
). Assume, in addition, that
u_{0}\in L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
and

\begin{cases}c+\frac{1}{s}\divv b\in L^{1}(0,\bar{\tau};L^{\infty}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}))&\text{if $s\in(1,\infty]$}\\
c,\divv b\in L^{1}(0,\bar{\tau};L^{\infty}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}))&\text{if $s=1$}.\end{cases}

Then there exists a distributional solution
u\in L^{\infty}\left(0,\bar{\tau};L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\right)
to (

T

).

The following simple remark will be crucial in the following (cf. for instance
[
16
]
).

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
u\in L^{\infty}\left(0,\bar{\tau};L^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\right)
be any distributional solution to (

T

). Extend
{\bf{b}},c,u
to
(-\infty,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
by

(4.2)

{\bf{b}}(\tau,p)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $\tau<0$}\\
{\bf{b}}(\tau,p)&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\qquad c(\tau,p)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $\tau<0$}\\
c(\tau,p)&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\qquad u(\tau,p)=\begin{cases}u_{0}(p)&\text{if $\tau<0$}\\
u(\tau,p)&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then

\partial_{\tau}u-\left\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla u\right\rangle+cu=0\qquad\text{in $(-\infty,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$}

in the sense of distributions, namely
\langle\mathscr{T}_{{\bf{b}},c}(u),\varphi\rangle=0
for every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((-\infty,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n})
, with

(4.3)

\left\langle\mathscr{T}_{{\bf{b}},c}(u),\varphi\right\rangle\coloneqq\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u\left[-\partial_{\tau}\varphi+\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla\varphi\rangle+(c+\divv{\bf{b}})\varphi\right]\,dp\,d\tau.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((-\infty,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n})
. Then

\begin{split}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u[-\partial_{\tau}\varphi+&\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla\varphi\rangle+(c+\divv{\bf{b}})\varphi]\,dp\,d\tau\\
\overset{\eqref{extensiondelellis}}{}&{=}-\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u_{0}(p)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{0}\partial_{\tau}\varphi\,d\tau\right)\,dp+\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u\left[-\partial_{\tau}\varphi+\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla\varphi\rangle+(c+\divv{\bf{b}})\varphi\right]\,dp\,d\tau\\
&=-\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u_{0}(p)\varphi(0,p)dp+\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u\left[-\partial_{\tau}\varphi+\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla\varphi\rangle+(c+\divv{\bf{b}})\varphi\right]\,dp\,d\tau\\
\overset{\eqref{eq_transport_equation}}{}&{=}0.\end{split}

∎

4.2.
Renormalized solutions

Distributional solutions are in general not stable with respect to composition with test functions, not even in the divergence-free case,
see for instance
[
17
]
for a beautiful example. DiPerna-Lions’ notion of
renormalized solution
to (

T

) formalizes such a property.

Definition 4.4

(Renormalized solutions)
.

Let
s\in[1,\infty]
and assume that (
4.1
) holds with
u_{0}\in L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
.
Then a function
u\in L^{\infty}\left(0,\bar{\tau};L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\right)
is a
renormalized solution
to (

T

) if, for every
\beta\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
with

\beta^{\prime}
bounded, the function
\beta(u)
is a distributional solution to

(4.4)

\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial\beta(u)}{\partial\tau}-\left\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla\beta(u)\right\rangle+cu\beta^{\prime}(u)=0&\text{in $(0,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n}$}\\
\beta(u)(0,\cdot)=\beta(u_{0})&\text{in ${\mathbb{H}}^{n}$,}\end{cases}

namely if, for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}\left([0,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\right),

-\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\beta(u_{0})(p)\varphi(0,p)\,dp+\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\beta(u)\left[-\partial_{\tau}\varphi+\left\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla\varphi\right\rangle+\divv{\bf{b}}\,\varphi\right]+cu\beta^{\prime}(u)\varphi\,dp\,d\tau.

If
s=\infty
the condition that
\beta^{\prime}
is bounded is not required.

Choosing
\beta
to be the identity map in
Definition

4.4
, it is clear that renormalized solutions are distributional solutions. When the converse implication holds,
{\bf{b}}
is said to enjoy the
renormalization property
.

Definition 4.5

(Renormalization property)
.

Assume that
{\bf{b}}\in{\mathbb{X}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}
satisfies (
4.1
) with
s=\infty
. We say that
{\bf{b}}
has the
renormalization property
if bounded distributional solutions to (

T

) with

u_{0}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
and
c\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n})
are renormalized solutions.

The renormalization property of contact vector fields with horizontal Sobolev regularity will follow as a corollary of the main result of the paper.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
s\in[1,\infty]
and
u_{0}\in L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
. Assume that
{\bf{b}}\in L^{1}\left(0,\bar{\tau};{\mathbb{X}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\right)
is a time-dependent contact vector field, induced by
\psi\in L^{1}\left(0,\bar{\tau};W^{2,s^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\right)
according to (
2.17
). Then, if
c\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n})
, any distributional solution
u\in L^{\infty}(0,\bar{\tau};L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}))
to (

T

)
is a renormalized solution. In particular,
{\bf{b}}
has the renormalization property.

As a consequence of
Theorem

4.6
and
Proposition

4.2
, we get existence and uniqueness of distributional solutions to (

T

).

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
s\in[1,\infty]
,
u_{0}\in L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
and let
{\bf{b}}
as in
Theorem

4.6
. Assume, in addition, that
c,\divv{\bf{b}}\in L^{1}\left(0,\bar{\tau};L^{\infty}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\right)
and that

(4.5)

\frac{|{\bf{b}}|}{1+d(p,0)}\in L^{1}\left(0,\bar{\tau};L^{1}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\right)+L^{1}\left(0,\bar{\tau};L^{\infty}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\right),

where
|{\bf{b}}|
is the norm of
{\bf{b}}
with respect to the Riemannian metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
Then there exists a unique distributional solution
u
to (

T

) in
L^{\infty}(0,\bar{\tau};L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}))
corresponding to the initial condition
u_{0}
.

Remark 4.8
.

The integrability condition (
4.5
) is the natural adaptation to our framework, due to both the Riemannian norm of
{\bf{b}}
and the sub-Riemannian distance
d(p,0)
, of the
Euclidean
growth assumption imposed in
[
18
]
. Nevertheless, we stress that the latter would have worked as well in our setting: in that case, the proof of
Theorem

4.7
follows
verbatim
that of
[
18
, Theorem II.2]
.

4.3.
Regularization

As customary, the key step in the proof of the renormalization property consists in showing that the spatial group mollification
u*\varrho_{\varepsilon}
of a distributional solution

u
to (

T

) still satisfies (

T

) up to a remainder distribution, the so-called
commutator

\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}
. As the latter admits an integral representation, the main effort is to prove that this error tends to
0
not only in the sense of distributions, but actually in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
.
We begin by providing an integral representation for the above-mentioned error term.

Proposition 4.9
.

For
s\in[1,\infty]
, assume (
4.1
). Fix a distributional solution
u\in L^{\infty}\left(0,\bar{\tau};L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\right)
to (

T

) and extend
u,{\bf{b}},c
to
(-\infty,\bar{\tau})
as in (
4.2
). For every
\varepsilon>0
, let
u_{\varepsilon}=u*\varrho_{\varepsilon}
be the spatial group mollification of
u
by
\varrho_{\varepsilon}
, with
\varrho_{\varepsilon}
as in (
2.13
). Then, the distribution
\mathscr{T}_{{\bf{b}},c}(u_{\varepsilon})
in (
4.3
) is representable by integration of the commutator
\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}
, namely

\left\langle\mathscr{T}_{{\bf{b}},c}(u_{\varepsilon}),\varphi\right\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\varphi(\tau,p)\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}(\tau,p)\,dp\,d\tau\qquad\text{for every $\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((-\infty,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n})$,}

where

\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}=\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}^{1}+\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}^{2}\in L^{1}\left(-\infty,\bar{\tau},L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\right)
is defined, for a.e.
(\tau,p)\in(-\infty,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, by

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(\tau,p)

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\left((u\divv{\bf{b}})*\varrho_{\varepsilon}\right)(\tau,p)-\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u\left(\tau,p\cdot q^{-1}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[b_{j}(\tau,p)Z_{j}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(q)-b_{j}\left(\tau,p\cdot q^{-1}\right)Z^{r}_{j}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(q)\right]\,dq,

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\tau,p)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u\left(\tau,p\cdot q^{-1}\right)\varrho_{\varepsilon}(q)\left[c(\tau,p)-c\left(\tau,p\cdot q^{-1}\right)\right]\,dq.

Proof.

Fix
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}\left((-\infty,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\right)
. Since
u
solves (

T

), then
u
extended as in (
4.2
) solves (
4.3
) by
Proposition

4.3
. Therefore,

(4.6)

\left\langle\mathscr{T}_{{\bf{b}},c}(u)*\varrho_{\varepsilon},\varphi\right\rangle\overset{\eqref{deficonvdistr}}{=}\left\langle\mathscr{T}_{{\bf{b}},c}(u),\varphi*\check{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle=0.

Moreover, since
u_{\varepsilon}
is smooth in the spatial variable and
Z_{j}
is left-invariant,

(4.7)

\begin{split}\left\langle\mathscr{T}_{{\bf{b}},c}(u_{\varepsilon}),\varphi\right\rangle&=-\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u_{\varepsilon}\partial_{\tau}\varphi\,dp\,d\tau+\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\varphi\left[-\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\rangle+cu_{\varepsilon}\right]\,dp\,d\tau\\
&=-\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u_{\varepsilon}\partial_{\tau}\varphi\,dp\,d\tau+\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\varphi\left[-\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}\left(u*Z_{j}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\right)+cu_{\varepsilon}\right]\,dp\,d\tau.\end{split}

On the other hand, one has

\begin{split}0\overset{\eqref{deficonvdistr},\eqref{mollizero}}{}&{=}\left\langle\mathscr{T}_{{\bf{b}},c}(u)*\varrho_{\varepsilon},\varphi\right\rangle\\
&=-\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u(\tau,w)\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\varphi(\tau,p)\check{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}\left(p^{-1}\cdot w\right)\,dp\,dw\,d\tau\\
&\quad+\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u(\tau,w)\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}(\tau,w)Z_{j}\left(\varphi*\check{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}\right)(\tau,w)\,dw\,d\tau\\
&\quad+\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u(\tau,w)(c+\divv{\bf{b}})(\tau,w)\varphi(\tau,p)\varrho_{\varepsilon}\left(w^{-1}\cdot p\right)\,dp\,dw\,d\tau\\
\overset{\eqref{eq_derivatives_convolution},\eqref{scambiarederivateeinversione}}{}&{=}-\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u_{\varepsilon}(\tau,p)\partial_{\tau}\varphi(\tau,p)dp\,d\tau-\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u(\tau,w)\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}(\tau,w)\left(\varphi*\widecheck{Z_{j}^{r}\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\right)(\tau,w)\,dw\,d\tau\\
&\quad+\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u(\tau,w)(c+\divv{\bf{b}})(\tau,w)\varphi(\tau,p)\varrho_{\varepsilon}\left(w^{-1}\cdot p\right)\,dp\,dw\,d\tau.\end{split}

In particular, we can replace in (
4.7
) the term
-\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u_{\varepsilon}\partial_{\tau}\varphi\,dp\,d\tau
by the sum of two other terms resulting from the previous identity to get

\begin{split}&\left\langle\mathscr{T}_{{\bf{b}},c}(u_{\varepsilon}),\varphi\right\rangle=-\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\varphi(\tau,p)\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u(\tau,w)\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}(\tau,p)Z_{j}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\left(w^{-1}\cdot p\right)\,dw\,dp\,d\tau\\
&\quad+\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\varphi(\tau,p)\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u(\tau,w)\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}(\tau,w)Z_{j}^{r}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\left(w^{-1}\cdot p\right)\,dw\,dp\,d\tau\\
&\quad-\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\varphi(\tau,p)\left(\left(u\divv{\bf{b}}\right)*\varrho_{\varepsilon}\right)(\tau,p)\,dp\,d\tau+\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\varphi(\tau,p)\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u(\tau,w)c(\tau,p)\varrho_{\varepsilon}\left(w^{-1}\cdot p\right)\,dw\,dp\,d\tau\\
&\quad-\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\varphi(\tau,p)\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u(\tau,w)c(\tau,w)\varrho_{\varepsilon}\left(w^{-1}\cdot p\right)\,dw\,dp\,d\tau.\end{split}

Recalling (
2.7
), the thesis follows by the change of variables
w\mapsto p\cdot q^{-1}
.
∎

\begin{split}-\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(p)&=\left({(u\divv{\bf{b}})}*\varrho_{\varepsilon}\right)(p)+\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}u\left(p\cdot q^{-1}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[b_{j}(p)Z_{j}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(q)-b_{j}\left(p\cdot q^{-1}\right)Z^{r}_{j}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(q)\right]\,dq\\

In particular, recalling (
2.4
),

\begin{split}-\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(p)&=((u\divv{\bf{b}})*\varrho_{\varepsilon})(p)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left[b_{j}(p)-b_{j}\left(p\cdot q^{-1}\right)\right]u\left(p\cdot q^{-1}\right)Z_{j}^{r}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(q)\,dq\\
&\quad-4\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\langle{\bf{b}}(p),{\bf{J}}(q)\rangle u\left(p\cdot q^{-1}\right)T\varrho_{\varepsilon}(q)\,dq\\

Performing the change of variables

-limit of

for every

\begin{split}\int_{K}&\left\lvert f_{\varepsilon}(p,w)u(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-f(p,w)u(p)\right\rvert\,dp\\

&\leq\mathrm{Lip}(f,\Omega)d(0,w)\left\lVert u\circ R_{\delta_{\varepsilon}(w)}-u\right\rVert_{L^{1}(K)}+\int_{K}\left\lvert f_{\varepsilon}(p,w)-f(p,w)\right\rvert\left\lvert u(p)\right\rvert\,dp.\end{split}

The first term converges to

In any case, we have obtained that

\begin{split}&\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}^{L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}}A_{1}(p)=\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}b_{j}(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle u(p)Z_{j}\varrho(w)\,dw\\
&\quad=\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{2n}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}Z_{i}b_{j}(p)w_{i}u(p)Z_{j}\varrho(w)\,dw+\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}Z_{j}b_{j}(p)w_{j}u(p)Z_{j}\varrho(w)\,dw\\
&\quad=\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{2n}Z_{i}b_{j}(p)u(p)\underbrace{\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}Z_{j}(w_{i}\varrho(w))\,dw}_{=0}+u(p)\sum_{j=1}^{2n}Z_{j}b_{j}(p)\Bigg(\underbrace{\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}Z_{j}(w_{j}\varrho(w))\,dw}_{=0}-\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\varrho(w)\underbrace{Z_{j}w_{j}}_{=1}\,dw\Bigg)\\
&\quad=-u(p)\sum_{j=1}^{2n}Z_{j}b_{j}(p)=-u(p)\divv{\bf{b}}(p)+u(p)Tb_{N}(p).\end{split}

Next, we focus on

. Notice that

\begin{split}A_{2}(p)&=\underbrace{\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\frac{b_{N}(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-b_{N}(p)-\varepsilon\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}b_{N}(p),w_{\mathcal{H}}\rangle}{\varepsilon^{2}}u(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))T\varrho(w)\,dw}_{\eqqcolon B_{1}(p)}\\

Recall that, since
{\bf{b}}
is a contact vector field,
b_{N}\in W^{2,s^{\prime}}_{{\mathcal{H}},\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
. Therefore, arguing as above, we get, by
Theorem

3.5
if

or
Theorem

3.6
if

,

\begin{split}\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}^{L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}}B_{1}(p)&=\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\nabla^{2,{\mathcal{H}}}b_{N}(p)w_{\mathcal{H}},w_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle+w_{N}Tb_{N}(p)\right)u(p)T\varrho(w)\,dw\\
&=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}Z_{i}Z_{j}b_{N}(p)w_{i}w_{j}u(p)T\varrho(w)\,dw+\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}w_{N}Tb_{N}(p)u(p)T\varrho(w)\,dw\\

&=-Tb_{N}(p)u(p).\end{split}

Finally, since

is a contact vector field generated by

,

Then

. In conclusion,

(4.9)

-\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}^{L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}}\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}^{1}=\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}^{L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}}(u\divv{\bf{b}})*\varrho_{\varepsilon}+\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}^{L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}}(A_{1}+A_{2})=u\divv{\bf{b}}-u\divv{\bf{b}}=0,

where the second equality holds due to

.

Step 2. Convergence of

For

\begin{split}\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}^{2}(p)&=\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left[c(p)u\left(p\cdot q^{-1}\right)\varrho_{\varepsilon}(q)-c\left(p\cdot q^{-1}\right)u\left(p\cdot q^{-1}\right)\varrho_{\varepsilon}(q)\right]\,dq\\

Therefore, fixing

, we obtain by Hölder’s inequality

\begin{split}\left\lVert\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(K)}&\leq\int_{K}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left\lvert c(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))-c(p)\right\rvert\left\lvert u(p\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}(w))\right\rvert\varrho(w)\,dw\,dp\\

&\leq\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\varrho(w)\left\lVert c\circ R_{\delta_{\varepsilon}(w)}-c\right\rVert_{L^{s^{\prime}}(K)}\left\lVert u\circ R_{\delta_{\varepsilon}(w)}\right\rVert_{L^{s}(K)}\,dw\\
\overset{\eqref{eq_Lebesgue_Haar}}{}&{=}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{s}(K)}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\varrho(w)\left\lVert c\circ R_{\delta_{\varepsilon}(w)}-c\right\rVert_{L^{s^{\prime}}(K)}\,dw.\end{split}

Since

by

and

\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}^{2}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\searrow 0]{L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}}0
by the dominated convergence theorem.

\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left(-\beta(u_{\varepsilon})\partial_{\tau}\varphi+\beta(u_{\varepsilon})\left(\left\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla\varphi\right\rangle+\divv{\bf{b}}\,\varphi\right)+cu_{\varepsilon}\beta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\varphi\right)\,dp\,d\tau=\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\beta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\mathscr{C}_{\epsilon}\varphi\,dp\,d\tau.

First, by
Lemma

and recalling that
\beta^{\prime}
is bounded,

\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\beta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}\varphi\,dp\,d\tau\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\searrow 0]{}0.

For the left-hand side, observe first that, by the dominated convergence theorem,

\begin{split}\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left(\beta(u_{\varepsilon})-\beta(u)\right)\partial_{\tau}\varphi\,dp\,d\tau\right|\leq M\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\left\lVert\partial_{\tau}\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{s^{\prime}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}\left\lVert u_{\varepsilon}-u\right\rVert_{L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}\,d\tau\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\searrow 0]{}0\end{split}

since
u_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\searrow 0]{L^{s}}u(\tau,\cdot)
for a.e.
\tau\in(-\infty,\bar{\tau})
by
Proposition

2.3
and

\left\lVert\partial_{\tau}\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{s^{\prime}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}\left\lVert u_{\varepsilon}-u\right\rVert_{L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}\overset{\eqref{eq_Young_inequality}}{\leq}2\left\lVert\partial_{\tau}\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{s^{\prime}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}\in L^{1}(0,\bar{\tau})\qquad\text{for every $\varepsilon>0$.}

Let
\Omega\Subset{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
be such that
\mathrm{supp\,}\varphi\subseteq(-\infty,\bar{\tau})\times\Omega

\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left(\beta(u_{\varepsilon})-\beta(u)\right)\left\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla\varphi\right\rangle\,dp\,d\tau\right|\leq M\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\left\lVert|{\bf{b}}|\right\rVert_{L^{s^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\left\lVert|\nabla\varphi|\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\lVert u_{\varepsilon}-u\right\rVert_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\,d\tau\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\searrow 0]{}0

Finally, fix any subsequence of
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}

\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}

\beta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})u_{\varepsilon}-\beta^{\prime}(u)u\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\searrow 0]{}0

\|\beta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})u_{\varepsilon}-\beta^{\prime}(u)u]c\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq 3M\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}\left\lVert c\right\rVert_{L^{s^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{s}(\Omega)},

whence

\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left[\beta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})u_{\varepsilon}-\beta^{\prime}(u)u\right]c\varphi\,dp\,d\tau\right|\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\searrow 0]{}0.

In conclusion, we proved that, for every

(4.13)

\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left[-\beta(u)\partial_{\tau}\varphi+\beta(u)\left(\left\langle b,\nabla\varphi\right\rangle+\divv b\varphi\right)+cu\beta^{\prime}(u)\varphi\right]\,dp\,d\tau=0.

Fix

\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}\left((-\infty,\bar{\tau})\times{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\right)

\begin{split}0\overset{\eqref{prefineblim}}{}&{=}\int_{-\infty}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left[-\beta(u)\partial_{\tau}\varphi+\beta(u)\left(\left\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla\varphi\right\rangle+\divv{\bf{b}}\,\varphi\right)+cu\beta^{\prime}(u)\varphi\right]\,dp\,d\tau\\

&\quad-\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\beta(u_{0})(p)\varphi(0,p)\,dp+\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left[-\beta(u)\partial_{\tau}\varphi+\beta(u)\left(\left\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla\varphi\right\rangle+\divv{\bf{b}}\,\varphi\right)+cu\beta^{\prime}(u)\varphi\right]\,dp\,d\tau,\end{split}

where the last equality follows by (

6.
Counterexamples and connection with existing results

In this section, we emphasize the relevance of our approach by showing how the existing results in the literature cannot generally be applied to our case.

6.1.
Contact velocity fields without Euclidean regularity

First, we show that contact velocity fields with horizontal Sobolev regularity are,
generically
, not of Euclidean bounded variation, whence the renormalization results obtained in
[
1
,
18
]
do not apply. By generically, we refer to the vocabulary of
Baire’s categories
(see e.g.
[
35
]
).
We recall that, given a metric space
(M,d)
, a set
A\subseteq M
is of the
first category
if
A
is contained in a countable union of
nowhere dense
sets. In turn, a set
E\subseteq M
is said to be nowhere dense if its closure has empty interior. The complement of a set of the first category is called
generic
. If
(M,d)
is complete and non-empty, Baire’s category theorem asserts that generic sets are dense, whence in particular non-empty.

In order to prove that vector fields with horizontal Sobolev regularity are, generically, not of locally bounded variation, we use the following basic functional analytic lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a normed space and let
Y\subseteq X
be a subspace, endowed with a norm
\|\cdot\|_{Y}
such that

(6.1)

\sup_{y\in Y:\|y\|_{X}=1}\|y\|_{Y}=\infty.

If the function
g:X\to[0,\infty]
defined by

(6.2)

g(x):=\begin{cases}\|x\|_{Y}&\text{if $x\in Y$}\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

is lower semicontinuous in
X
, then
X\setminus Y
is generic in
X
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
Y
is dense in
X
(since any closed proper subspace of
X
is of first
category). It is sufficient to prove that the closed sets
A_{k}=\{x:g(x)\leq k\}
, whose union is
Y
, have empty interior. If for some integer
k
this does not happen, there exist
x_{0}\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
such that
\|x-x_{0}\|_{X}<\varepsilon
implies
g(x)\leq k
. The density of
Y
in

X
grants the existence of
y_{0}\in Y
such that
\|x-y_{0}\|_{X}<\varepsilon/2
implies
g(x)\leq k
. In particular,

\|x-y_{0}\|_{X}<\varepsilon/2
implies
g(x-y_{0})\leq k+\|y_{0}\|_{Y}
. This last implication gives that the supremum in (
6.1
) is bounded by
(2k+2\|y_{0}\|_{Y})/\varepsilon
, a contradiction.
∎

In the class of contact vector fields
{\bf{b}}={\bf{b}}(\psi)
parametrized by
\psi\in W^{2,s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
as in (
2.17
) and with the induced topology we can prove the following genericity result. The strategy of proof is to use highly oscillating test functions to provide estimates on different scales for derivatives along different directions. In particular, we will produce competitors whose oscillation in the vertical direction is predominant compared to horizontal derivatives. Accordingly, this approach grants the existence of contact vector fields with horizontal Sobolev regularity and unbounded vertical variation. In connection with Baire’s category arguments, by contrast, this could be compared with the main result of
[
32
]
, which roughly speaking asserts that the class of renormalizable Euclidean vector fields is generic in
L^{1}
. The strategy of proof of
[
32
]
is to consider the class of vector fields which can be approximated in
L^{1}
by Lipschitz ones at a rate faster than the inverse of their Lipschitz constant.

Theorem 6.2
.

The set

\left\{\psi\in W^{2,s}_{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\,:\,\,b_{1}(\psi),\ldots,b_{2n}(\psi)\notin BV_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\right\}

is generic, and in particular dense, in
W^{2,s}_{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
.

Proof.

We fix a bounded open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,2n\}
. We assume without loss of generality that

0\in\Omega
and we are going to apply
Lemma

6.1
with
X=W^{2,s}_{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
and the subspace

Y\coloneqq\left\{\psi\in W^{2,s}_{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}):b_{i}(\psi)\in BV(\Omega)\right\}

endowed with the norm
\|\psi\|_{W^{2,s}_{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}+|Db_{i}(\psi)|(\Omega)
.
Notice that the standard formula of the
BV
theory

|Db_{j}(\psi)|(\Omega)=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}b_{i}(\psi)\divv F\,dp:F\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,\,|F|\leq 1\right\}

grants that the lower semicontinuity assumption of
Lemma

6.1
is satisfied. Hence, it will be sufficient to show that there is no constant
C
such that

(6.3)

|Db_{i}(\psi)|(\Omega)\leq C\|\psi\|_{W^{2,s}_{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}\qquad\forall\psi\in W^{2,s}_{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n}).

Let an open Euclidean ball

of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
and an open interval

, both centered at

, be chosen in such a way that
\overline{B}\times\overline{I}\subseteq\Omega
. Fix

such that
\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)\subseteq B
,

and
|\partial_{w_{i}}\varphi\|_{L^{1}(B)}\neq 0
. Fix

such that
\mathrm{supp}(g)\subseteq I
and

. Denote points in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
by

For
\beta
and

sufficiently small,
\mathrm{supp}(\psi)\subseteq B\times I
. Recall that
Z_{j}=\partial_{w_{j}}-2{\bf{J}}(w)_{j}\partial_{t}
for every

. For every

, set

if

and

if

otherwise. Then

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\left(\partial_{w_{k}}\partial_{w_{j}}\varphi\right)\left(\frac{w}{\beta}\right)g\left(\frac{t}{\delta}\right)-\frac{2}{\delta}c_{jk}\varphi\left(\frac{w}{\beta}\right)g^{\prime}\left(\frac{t}{\delta}\right)-\frac{2}{\beta\delta}{\bf{J}}(w)_{j}\left(\partial_{w_{k}}\varphi\right)\left(\frac{w}{\beta}\right)g^{\prime}\left(\frac{t}{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2}{\beta\delta}{\bf{J}}(w)_{k}\left(\partial_{w_{j}}\varphi\right)\left(\frac{w}{\beta}\right)g^{\prime}\left(\frac{t}{\delta}\right)+\frac{4}{\delta^{2}}{\bf{J}}(w)_{j}{\bf{J}}(w)_{k}\varphi\left(\frac{w}{\beta}\right)g^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{t}{\delta}\right),

\displaystyle TZ_{i}\psi(w,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta\delta}\left(\partial_{w_{i}}\varphi\right)\left(\frac{w}{\beta}\right)g^{\prime}\left(\frac{t}{\delta}\right)-\frac{2}{\delta^{2}}{\bf{J}}(w)_{i}\varphi\left(\frac{w}{\beta}\right)g^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{t}{\delta}\right).

for every

and every

F_{1}=1+\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\|\partial_{w_{j}}\varphi\|_{L_{1}(B)},\qquad F_{2}=F_{1}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{2n}\|\partial_{w_{k}}\partial_{w_{j}}\varphi\|_{L_{1}(B)},\qquad G_{1}=\|g^{\prime}\|_{L_{1}(I)},\qquad G_{2}=\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{1}(I)}.

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\beta^{2n}\delta,

\displaystyle\|Z_{j}\psi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\beta^{2n-1}\delta F_{1}+2\beta^{2n+1}G_{1},

\displaystyle\|Z_{k}Z_{j}\psi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\beta^{2n-2}\delta F_{2}+2\beta^{2n}\left(G_{1}+2F_{1}G_{1}\right)+\frac{4\beta^{2n+2}}{\delta}G_{2},

\displaystyle\|TZ_{i}\psi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\beta^{2n-1}\|\partial_{w_{i}}\varphi\|_{L^{1}(B)}G_{1}-\frac{2\beta^{2n+1}}{\delta}G_{2}.

In particular, if we choose

, with

, we see that all the terms in the first three lines above are

) holds.
∎

(r,s)

In this section, we show that our results cannot be recovered by employing the metric approach of

As already mentioned in the introduction, the authors of

On the metric measure space

\mathscr{E}(u)\coloneqq\displaystyle{\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}u,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}u\right\rangle\,dp&\text{ if }u\in W^{1,2}_{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\\

\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}u,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}v\right\rangle\,dp=-\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}v\Delta^{\mathcal{H}}u\,dp\qquad\text{ for every $u,v\in W^{1,2}_{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})$ such that $\Delta^{\mathcal{H}}v\in L^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})$},

-gradient flow associated with
\mathscr{E}
is the classical sub-Riemannian
heat semigroup

P_{\tau}
(see
[
21
]
). Finally, a suitable algebra of test functions could be the class
C^{1}_{c}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
. The uniqueness scheme of
[
7
]
relies on suitable regularization properties of the heat semigroup, as well as on density assumptions on the algebra and on regularity conditions on the velocity field. First,
P_{\tau}
is required to satisfy the so-called
L^{s}
-
\Gamma
inequality
for any
s\in[2,\infty)
, i.e.,

(6.5)

\left\|\sqrt{\Gamma(P_{\tau}f)}\right\|_{L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}\leq\frac{c_{s}}{\sqrt{\tau}}\|f\|_{L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}\qquad\text{for every $f\in L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})$, $\tau\in(0,1)$,}

where
c_{s}>0
is independent of
f
. As pointed out in
[
7
, Corollary 6.3]
, (
6.5
) is a typical consequence of the
Bakry-Émery condition

\mathrm{BE}_{2}(K,\infty)
. Namely, we recall that
\mathrm{BE}_{2}(K,\infty)
holds if there exists
K\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\Gamma\left(P_{\tau}f\right)\leq e^{-2K\tau}P_{\tau}\left(\Gamma f\right)\qquad\text{ for every $f\in W^{1,2}_{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})$.}

However,
({\mathbb{H}}^{n},d,\mathcal{L}^{N})
is a prototypical setting in which
\mathrm{BE}_{2}(K,\infty)
does not hold for any
K
. Indeed, it is well-known that
({\mathbb{H}}^{n},d,\mathcal{L}^{N})
fails to satisfy the so-called
curvature-dimension condition

\mathrm{CD(K,\infty)}
for any
K\in\mathbb{R}
(cf.
[
6
,
25
]
). In turn, when the Cheeger energy is a quadratic form,
\mathrm{CD(K,\infty)}
is equivalent to
\mathrm{BE_{2}(K,\infty)}
(cf.
[
4
,
5
]
), whence the latter fails in
({\mathbb{H}}^{n},d,\mathcal{L}^{N})
for every
N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
and every
K\in\mathbb{R}
. Nevertheless, it is still possible to prove the validity of (
6.5
).

Proposition 6.3
.

For every
s\in[2,\infty)
, (
6.5
) holds.

Proof.

Fix
s\in[2,\infty)
,
\tau\in(0,1)
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
. Then, by
[
8
, Remark 3.3]
,

\Gamma(P_{\tau}f)\leq\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\frac{n+1}{2n}\right)\left[P_{\tau}(f^{2})-\left(P_{\tau}f\right)^{2}\right]\leq\frac{1}{\tau}P_{\tau}(f^{2}).

In particular,

(6.6)

\left\|\sqrt{\Gamma(P_{\tau}f)}\right\|_{L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau}}\left\|P_{\tau}\left(f^{2}\right)\right\|_{L^{\frac{s}{2}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})},

where the last inequality follows by the
L^{\frac{s}{2}}
-contractivity of
P_{\tau}
(cf.
[
21
]
). Fix
f\in L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
, and let
\{f_{h}\}_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
be such that
f_{h}\to f
strongly in
L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
. By linearity and contractivity,
P_{\tau}f_{h}\to P_{\tau}f
strongly in
L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
, whence, by the strong
L^{s}
-lower-semicontinuity (cf.
[
22
,
37
]
) of
u\mapsto\|\sqrt{\Gamma\left(\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}u\right)}\|_{L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}
,
we conclude that

\left\|\sqrt{\Gamma(P_{\tau}f)}\right\|_{L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}\leq\liminf_{h\to\infty}\left\|\sqrt{\Gamma(P_{\tau}f_{h})}\right\|_{L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}\overset{\eqref{eq_gammaineqsmoothproof}}{\leq}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau}}\liminf_{h\to\infty}\left\|f_{h}\right\|_{L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}.

∎

Coming to the density properties of the algebra of test functions
C^{1}_{c}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
, it is easy to check that the latter satisfies the basic assumptions
[
7
, (2-16)]
and
[
7
, (2-17)]
. In addition (cf.
[
7
, (4-3)]
), the algebra is required to contain a sequence
\{f_{h}\}_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

(6.7)

0\leq f_{h}\leq 1\text{ for every $h\in\mathbb{N}$,}\qquad f_{h}\nearrow 1\quad\text{ a.e.~on ${\mathbb{H}}^{n}$,}\qquad\sqrt{\Gamma(f_{h})}\to 0\text{ weakly${}^{\star}$ in $L^{\infty}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})$.}

A sequence satisfying (
6.7
) can be easily constructed by choosing a sequence of smooth cut-off functions between
B(0,h+1)
and
B(0,h+2)
. Finally, we turn to the regularity assumptions on the velocity field. For the sake of simplicity, we focus on the autonomous case. From now on, we fix
s\in(1,\infty]
and a vector field
{\bf{b}}\in{\mathbb{X}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}
with
b_{i}\in W^{1,s}_{{\mathcal{H}}}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
,
i=1,\ldots,2n
and
Tb_{N}\in L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
,
so that
\divv{\bf{b}}\in L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
. Following
[
7
]
,
{\bf{b}}
induces a
derivation
acting on a test function
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
as

df({\bf{b}})\coloneqq\langle{\bf{b}},\nabla f\rangle.

Again, according to
[
7
]
, the
deformation

\dsym{\bf{b}}
is defined for every
f,g\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
by

(6.8)

\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\dsym{\bf{b}}(f,g)\,dp\coloneqq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left[df({\bf{b}})\Delta^{\mathcal{H}}g+dg({\bf{b}})\Delta^{\mathcal{H}}f-\divv{\bf{b}}\,\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}g\right\rangle\right]\,dp.

Clearly, the above definition extends to any couple
f,g
for which the right hand side is meaningful.
For what concerns
{\bf{b}}
, the approach of
[
7
]
relies on the following two assumptions.

•

{\bf{b}}
needs to satisfy an a.e. upper bound of the form

(6.9)

|df({\bf{b}})|\leq|{\bf{b}}|\left|\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f\right|

for every
f
belonging to the algebra of test functions.

•

{\bf{b}}
needs to have a
deformation of type
(r,s)
.
Namely, there exists
c\geq 0
such that

(6.10)

\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\dsym{\bf{b}}(f,g)\,dp\leq c\left\|\sqrt{\left(\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f\right)}\right\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}\left\|\sqrt{\left(\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}g\right)}\right\|_{L^{s}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})}

for every
f,g
for which the above expression is meaningful.

On the one hand, it is clear that (
6.9
) rules out non-horizontal vector fields. On the other hand, (
6.10
) rules out vector fields which do not possess a contact structure. Since a non-trivial contact vector field cannot be horizontal, the above two restrictions prevent the approach of
[
7
]
from being applicable to our case. To justify the validity of the second restriction (independently of the first one, hence even considering a non-horizontal field
{\bf{b}}
), we assume for the sake of simplicity that all components
b_{i}
belong to
C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
, and we make (
6.8
) more explicit. Precisely, if
f,g\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
,

(6.11)

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\dsym{\bf{b}}(f,g)\,dp&=\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}\left(\frac{Z_{i}b_{j}+Z_{j}b_{i}}{2}\right)Z_{i}fZ_{j}g\,dp\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}b_{N}+4{\bf{J}}({\bf{b}}),Tg\,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f+Tf\,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}g\right\rangle\,dp.\end{split}

The validity of (
6.11
) follows by applying the divergence theorem to the first term on the right hand side and by exploiting the commutation relations (
2.1
). Indeed, notice that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}&\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}Z_{i}b_{j}\left(Z_{i}fZ_{j}g+Z_{j}fZ_{i}g\right)\,dp=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}\underbrace{\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}Z_{i}\left(b_{j}\left(Z_{i}fZ_{j}g+Z_{j}fZ_{i}g\right)\right)\,dp}_{=0}\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}\Bigl(\underbrace{b_{j}Z_{i}Z_{i}fZ_{j}g}_{\mathrm{I}}+\underbrace{b_{j}Z_{i}fZ_{i}Z_{j}g}_{\mathrm{II}}+\underbrace{b_{j}Z_{i}Z_{j}fZ_{i}g}_{\mathrm{III}}+\underbrace{b_{j}Z_{j}fZ_{i}Z_{i}g}_{\mathrm{IV}}\Bigr)\,dp\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left[df({\bf{b}})\Delta^{\mathcal{H}}g+dg({\bf{b}})\Delta^{\mathcal{H}}f\right]\,dp+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left(\Delta^{\mathcal{H}}f\,b_{N}\,Tg+\Delta^{\mathcal{H}}g\,b_{N}\,Tf\right)\,dp\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}\left(b_{j}Z_{i}fZ_{j}Z_{i}g+b_{j}Z_{j}Z_{i}fZ_{i}g\right)\,dp+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}\left(b_{j}Z_{i}f[Z_{j},Z_{i}]g+b_{j}[Z_{j},Z_{i}]fZ_{i}g\right)\,dp.\end{split}

In the last equality, we exploited
\mathrm{I}
and
\mathrm{IV}
to make the first two terms on the right hand side of (
6.8
) explicit, and rewrote
\mathrm{II}
and
\mathrm{III}
highlighting commutators. Applying again the divergence theorem,

\begin{split}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}&\left(b_{j}Z_{i}fZ_{j}Z_{i}g+b_{j}Z_{j}Z_{i}fZ_{i}g\right)\,dp=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}\underbrace{\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}Z_{j}\left(b_{j}Z_{i}fZ_{i}g\right)\,dp}_{=0}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}Z_{j}b_{j}Z_{i}fZ_{i}g\,dp+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}b_{j}Z_{j}Z_{i}fZ_{i}g\,dp-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}b_{j}Z_{j}Z_{i}fZ_{i}g\,dp\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\divv{\bf{b}}\,\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}g\right\rangle\,dp-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}Tb_{N}\,\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}g\right\rangle\,dp.\end{split}

Moreover, by (
2.1
),

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}&b_{j}Z_{i}f[Z_{j},Z_{i}]g\,dp+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}b_{j}[Z_{j},Z_{i}]fZ_{i}g\,dp\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(b_{j}Y_{j}f[X_{j},Y_{j}]g+b_{n+j}X_{j}f[Y_{j},X_{j}]g+b_{j}Y_{j}g[X_{j},Y_{j}]f+b_{n+j}X_{j}g[Y_{j},X_{j}]f\right)\,dp\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[4b_{j}\left(-Y_{j}f\right)\,Tg+4b_{n+j}X_{j}f\,Tg+4b_{j}\left(-Y_{j}g\right)\,Tf+4b_{n+j}X_{j}g\,Tf\right]\,dp\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left[\left\langle 4{\bf{b}},{\bf{J}}\left(\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f\right)\right\rangle\,Tg+\left\langle 4{\bf{b}},{\bf{J}}\left(\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}g\right)\right\rangle\,Tf\right]\,dp.\end{split}

Therefore, by the above computations,

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}&\dsym{\bf{b}}(f,g)\,dp=\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}\left(\frac{Z_{i}b_{j}+Z_{j}b_{i}}{2}\right)Z_{i}fZ_{j}g\,dp+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left\langle 4{\bf{J}}({\bf{b}}),Tg\,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f+Tf\,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}g\right\rangle\,dp\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left(\Delta^{\mathcal{H}}f\,b_{N}\,Tg+\Delta^{\mathcal{H}}g\,b_{N}\,Tf\right)\,dp+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}Tb_{N}\,\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}g\right\rangle\,dp\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}\left(\frac{Z_{i}b_{j}+Z_{j}b_{i}}{2}\right)Z_{i}fZ_{j}g\,dp+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}b_{N}+4{\bf{J}}({\bf{b}}),Tg\,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f+Tf\,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}g\right\rangle\,dp\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left[\divv\left(b_{N}\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f\right)\,Tg+\divv\left(b_{N}\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}g\right)\,Tf\right]+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}Tb_{N}\,\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}g\right\rangle\,dp,\\
\end{split}

where in the last equality we just added and subtracted the quantity

\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}b_{N},Tg\,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f+Tf\,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}g\right\rangle\,dp.

Applying once more the divergence theorem, and since
T
commutes with
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{2n}
, we conclude that

\begin{split}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}\left[\divv\left(b_{N}\,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f\right)\,Tg+\divv\left(b_{N}\,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}g\right)\,Tf\right]\,dp&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}b_{N}\left(\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}Tg\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}Tf,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}g\right\rangle\right)\,dp\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}b_{N}\,T\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}g\right\rangle\,dp\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}Tb_{N}\,\left\langle\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla^{\mathcal{H}}g\right\rangle\,dp,\end{split}

whence (
6.11
) follows.
Due to the presence of the vertical derivatives
Tf
and
Tg
on the right hand side of (
6.11
), we conclude that
{\bf{b}}
has deformation of type
(r,s)
provided that it is a contact vector field. Indeed, in this case the second integral on the right hand side of (
6.11
) vanishes by (
4.8
), and (
6.10
) follows by choosing
r=s=2(s^{\prime})^{\prime}
and
c=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}\|Z_{i}b_{j}\|_{L^{s^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.

Appendix A
Chain rule for weak derivatives along vector fields

The following chain rule for weak derivatives along vector fields is surely well-known. Being typically stated for Sobolev functions (cf.
[
20
,
30
]
), we include a proof for the sake of completeness.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
. Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be open. Let
X
be a locally Lipschitz continuous vector field over
A
.
Let
u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
be such that
Xu\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
and let
\beta\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
\beta^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Then
\beta(u)\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
,
X(\beta(u))\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
and

(A.1)

X(\beta(u))=\beta^{\prime}(u)Xu\qquad\text{a.e.~on $A$.}

Moreover, if
u\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
, the above facts hold for every
\beta\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Set
C=\|\beta^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
. Fix an open set
B\Subset A
. Then

(A.2)

\int_{B}|\beta(u)|\,dp\leq C\int_{B}|u|\,dp+\beta(0)|B|<\infty,

whence
\beta(u)\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
. Notice that
\beta^{\prime}(u)Xu\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
. To conclude, it suffices to show that

\int_{A}\left(\beta^{\prime}(u)Xu\right)\varphi\,dp=-\int_{A}\divv X\,\beta(u)\varphi\,dp-\int_{A}\beta(u)X\varphi\,dp\qquad\text{for every $\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(A)$.}

Fix
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(A)
. Let
B\subseteq A
be open and such that
\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)\Subset B\Subset A
. Then
X
is Lipschitz continuous on
B
, and moreover
u,Xu\in L^{1}(B)
. By
[
22
, Theorem 1.2.3]
, there exists a sequence
\{u_{h}\}_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq C^{\infty}(B)
such that
u_{h},Xu_{h}\in L^{1}(B)
for any
h\in\mathbb{N}
,
u_{h}\to u
,
Xu_{h}\to Xu
strongly in
L^{1}(B)
,
u_{h}\to u
a.e. on
B
and
|Xu_{h}|\leq g
a.e. on
B
for some
g\in L^{1}(B)
. Since every
u_{h}
is smooth,
\beta^{\prime}(u_{h})Xu_{h}=X(\beta(u_{h}))
on
B
for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
,

(A.3)

\int_{A}\beta^{\prime}(u_{h})Xu_{h}\varphi\,dp=-\int_{A}\divv X\,\beta(u_{h})\varphi\,dp-\int_{A}\beta(u_{h})X\varphi\,dp.

Notice that, since
\beta^{\prime}
is continuous,
\beta^{\prime}(u_{h})\to\beta^{\prime}(u)
a.e. on
B
, and moreover,
|\beta^{\prime}(u_{h})-\beta^{\prime}(u)|\leq 2C
. Therefore, by the dominated convergence theorem,

\begin{split}\int_{A}|\beta^{\prime}(u_{h})Xu_{h}\varphi-\beta^{\prime}(u)Xu\varphi|\,dp&\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\int_{B}|\beta^{\prime}(u)||Xu_{h}-Xu|\,dp+\int_{B}|\beta^{\prime}(u_{h})-\beta^{\prime}(u)||Xu_{h}||\varphi|\,dp\\
&\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\int_{B}|Xu_{h}-Xu|\,dp+\int_{B}|\beta^{\prime}(u_{h})-\beta^{\prime}(u)||Xu_{h}||\varphi|\,dp\to 0.\end{split}

Furthermore,

\int_{A}|\divv X\,\beta(u_{h})\varphi-\divv X\,\beta(u)\varphi|\,dp\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\|\divv X\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\int_{B}|u_{h}-u|\,dp\to 0.

Finally,

\int_{A}|\beta(u_{h})X\varphi-\beta(u)X\varphi|\,dp\leq C\|X\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\int_{B}|u_{h}-u|\,dp\to 0.

Hence, (
A.2
) follows by letting
h\to\infty
in (
A.3
). In conclusion, when
u\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
, the above arguments apply for every
\beta\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
∎
