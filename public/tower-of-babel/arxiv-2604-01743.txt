In this paper, we search for Liouville integrable Lotka-Volterra (LV) systems, i.e. ordinary differential equations of the form

LV systems have been studied extensively and they found applications in e.g. biology and chemistry

14

16
,
18

. One of the interesting things about LV systems is that they can exhibit regular or chaotic behaviour, or both. Of course, systems with maximum regularity are integrable.

A Darboux Polynomial (DP) is a polynomial
P
whose derivative,
\dot{P}
, is proportional to
P
. The entire
n(n+1)
-parameter class of LV-systems (
1
) admits
n
such polynomials, namely the variables
x_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. By specialisation of the parameters, we obtain subclasses with additional DPs, which give rise to integrals, or other mathematical structures such as a Poisson bracket. Recently, the authors have established large classes of integrable cases. This includes:

•

the study
[
22
]
, of inhomogeneous LV systems of the form

\dot{x}_{i}=x_{i}\,\big(r_{i}+\sum_{j>i}x_{j}-\sum_{j<i}x_{j}\big),\qquad r_{i}=\begin{cases}b,&i=1,\ldots,k,\\
c,&i=k+1,\ldots,k+l,\\
d,&i=k+l+1,\ldots,k+l+m=n,\end{cases}

(2)

where
c=b+d
and
km\neq 0
. The 2-parameter class (
2
) contains Liouville integrable, Liouville superintegrable, nonholonomically integrable, and ’integrable-or-not’ subclasses. For the latter we were not able to find a Hamiltonian structure or more than
n-3
integrals. Still the entire class was shown to be solvable, using the solutions to contracted 2- and 3-component systems given in terms of the Lambert W function.

•

the construction of LV tree-systems in
[
17
,
23
]
. These
are homogeneous superintegrable
(3n-2)
-parameter classes of
n
-component LV systems. They admit
n-1

n-1

•

the classification of classes of
n
-component LV systems with linear DPs in any number of variables, for
n<6
, up to linear transformations. These systems are associated to (LV admissible) hypergraphs. One new superintegrable
3n-2=13

In this paper, we assume that
\mathbf{A}
is an anti-symmetric constant
n\times n
matrix, starting with
n=2m
. In the homogeneous case, this implies that the LV-system is a Hamiltonian system, with Poisson bracket

\{x_{i},x_{j}\}=A_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}

(3)

and Hamiltonian
H=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}
. In other words, the equation

\dot{x}_{i}=\{x_{i},H\},\quad i=1,\ldots,n,

(4)

equals (
1
) with
\mathbf{r}=0
. We follow a similar approach to the one taken in
[
17
,
20
,
23
]
, i.e., we require that certain linear functions are DPs of the system (each of them giving rise to an integral). Note that we do not need as many as before. Whereas for superintegrability
n-1
were needed, here, besides the Hamiltonian,
n/2-1

Definition 1
.

An
n
-dimensional Hamiltonian system is Liouville integrable if there are
s
Casimir functions and
\frac{n-s}{2}
commuting and functionally independent integrals (including the Hamiltonian).

We present a large number of homogeneous and inhomogeneous Liouville integrable multi-parameter families of
n
-dimensional Lotka-Volterra systems, for each

\frac{m^{2}}{4}+\frac{m}{2}+\frac{1-\left(-1\right)^{m}}{8}

. In odd dimensions, this is not the case. In Section

The number of different Liouville integrable classes of LV systems we provide in this paper grows quadratically, whereas the number of superintegrable classes of tree-systems grows exponentially with

3n/2-2

Homogeneous Liouville integrable LV systems

In this section, we consider homogeneous
n
-dimensional Hamiltonian systems (
4
), with anti-symmetric matrix
\mathbf{A}
and Hamiltonian

H=x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}.

(5)

Integrals for (

F

\dot{F}=\{F,H\}=0

Darboux Polynomial
(DP) is a polynomial

\dot{P}=\{P,H\}=CP,

(6)

in which case

is called the cofactor of

, also denoted

function

which satisfies

is called a
second integral

[
1
, Definition 2.14]
. A product of powers of DPs,

\Pi=\prod_{i}P_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}

(7)

is a second integral with cofactor
C[\Pi]=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}C[P_{i}]
.

We will require linear combinations of the form

P_{I}=\alpha_{1}x_{i_{1}}+\alpha_{2}x_{i_{2}}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}x_{i_{k}},

(8)

where
I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{N}_{n}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}

\mathbf{A}
, as given in
[
17
]
for

, or in
[
20
]
for

. Due to the antisymmetry of

, the coefficients

in

are all equal; we take
\alpha_{i}=1
for all

. If the matrix
\mathbf{A}
has full rank, then each DP gives rise to a integral. Indeed, if
\mathbf{B}
is the
1\times n
vector such that
C[P_{I}]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}B_{i}x_{i}
, and

\mathbf{Z}=-\mathbf{B}.\mathbf{A}^{-1},

then

P_{I}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{Z_{i}}

(9)

is an integral, cf.
[
17
, Section 2]
. Further conditions on the matrix
\mathbf{A}
are imposed by requiring the integrals to Poisson commute. A homogeneous
n
-dimensional Hamiltonian LV system with
k

9

n

For example, the Hamiltonian LV system with matrix

-b_{1}&-b_{1}&-a_{2}&0&a_{4}&a_{5}\\
-b_{1}&-b_{1}&-a_{3}&-a_{4}&0&a_{5}\\

\displaystyle\{P_{2m-1,2m},P_{2j+1,n-2l-1}\}

\displaystyle=b_{m}P_{2m-1,2m}P_{2j+1,n-2l-1},

\displaystyle\{P_{2m-1,2m},P_{n-2l,n}\}

\displaystyle=c_{m}P_{2m-1,2m}P_{n-2l,n},

\displaystyle\{P_{2m-1,2m},P_{2t-1,2t}\}

\displaystyle=a_{m,t}P_{2m-1,2m}P_{2t-1,2t},

\displaystyle\{P_{2j+1,n-2l-1},P_{n-2l,n}\}

\displaystyle=a_{j+3k+1}P_{2j+1,n-2l-1}P_{n-2l,n},

where
a_{m,t}
is defined by (

G^{j}_{m},G^{j,k}_{t}
do not depend on the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{j}
the only relevant part of the matrix
\mathbf{A}
is the bottom-right part
\mathbf{A}^{k,l}_{j+1}
which is the same as in Prop.
4
but shifted. Hence the
G
-integrals Poisson commute. They also commute with the

\overline{F}
-integrals because for all

,

,

as in both cases the derivation

acts as a scaling and the

∎

2.2
Odd-dimensional homogeneous Liouville integrable LV systems

If the matrix
\mathbf{A}
does not have full rank, Casimirs can be found by determining the null-space of
\mathbf{A}
(which, as
\mathbf{A}
is antisymmetric, equals the null-space of its transpose). Indeed, if
\sum_{j}A_{i,j}q_{j}=0
for all
i
, and
\mathcal{C}=\prod_{h}x_{h}^{q_{h}}
, then

\{x_{i},\mathcal{C}\}=x_{i}\mathcal{C}\left(\sum_{j}A_{i,j}q_{j}\right)=0.

We will obtain odd
n=2m-1
dimensional systems from even
n+1=2m
dimensional systems by reduction, i.e. setting
x_{2m}=0
. Integrals of the
2m
-dimensional system which do not depend on
x_{2m}
, will be integrals of the reduced system. The reduced system has one less integral, but it makes up for that by acquiring a Casimir.

Proposition 6
.

The odd-dimensional type
[j,0,0]
LV system is Liouville integrable, with

mutually in involution. The Casimir is given by

where

for

Proof.

The commutativity of the

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{2k-2}A_{2k-1,l}q_{l}\Big)+A_{2k-1,2k-1}q_{2k-1}+A_{2k-1,2k}q_{2k}+\Big(\sum_{l=2k+1}^{n-1}A_{2k-1,l}q_{l}\Big)+A_{2k-1,n}q_{n}

\displaystyle=0+0+a_{k}(-\frac{b_{k}}{a_{k}})+0+b_{k}

as the terms in the summations cancel each other pairwise. The proof that

3
Nonhomogeneous Liouville integrable LV systems

The family of Lotka-Volterra equations (
1
) is Hamiltonian, or Poisson, if
\mathbf{A}
is skew and
\mathbf{r}\in\text{Im}(\mathbf{A})
. In terms of the Poisson bracket (
3
), the system (
1
) can be written as
\dot{\mathbf{x}}=\{\mathbf{x},H\}
, where the Hamiltonian is given by

H=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}+k_{i}\ln x_{i}),\qquad\text{ with }\mathbf{A}\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{r}.

(50)

A function
P(\mathbf{x})
is homogeneous of weight
d
if
P(\lambda\mathbf{x})=\lambda^{d}P(\mathbf{x})
.
The following theorem is an improved version of
[
21
, Theorem 3.1]
. Let
I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{N}_{n}
, and denote
\mathbf{x}_{I}=(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}})
.

Theorem 10
.

Let
P(\mathbf{x}_{I})
be a homogeneous second integral of weight
d
with cofactor
C(\mathbf{x})
for the system of ODEs
\dot{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})
. Then
P(\mathbf{x}_{I})
is a second integral for the system
\dot{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})+r(\mathbf{x},t)\mathbf{x}
, with cofactor
C(\mathbf{x})+dr(\mathbf{x},t)
, where
r
is a scalar function of
\mathbf{x},t
.

It follows that if a homogeneous LV system admits an integral
F
which is homogeneous of weight 0, then the inhomogeneous LV system obtained by adding
\mathbf{r}=r\mathbf{x}
to the right-hand-side admits the same integral. Note that, in Theorem
10
, the only relevant equations are the ones for
\mathbf{x}_{I}
, the equations for the complementary variables
\mathbf{x}_{I^{c}}
can be altered differently.

For odd-dimensional

of the form

3.2.1
The odd-dimensional inhomogeneous
[j,0,0]
LV system

The
n=2j+1
dimensional inhomogeneous LV system of type
[j,0,0]
has matrix

\mathbf{A}=\begin{pmatrix}J_{1}&K_{1,2}&K_{1,3}&\cdots&K_{1,j}&L_{1}\\
-K_{1,2}&J_{2}&K_{2,3}&\cdots&K_{2,j}&L_{2}\\
-K_{1,3}&-K_{2,3}&J_{3}&\cdots&K_{3,j}&L_{3}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
-K_{1,j}&-K_{2,j}&-K_{3,j}&\cdots&J_{j}&L_{j}\\
-L_{1}^{T}&-L_{2}^{T}&-L_{3}^{T}&\cdots&-L_{j}^{T}&J_{j+1}\end{pmatrix}

(54)

where

J_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{i}\\
-a_{i}&0\end{pmatrix},\ K_{i,k}=(c_{i}-c_{k})\mathbf{1}^{2,2},\ L_{i}=a_{j+1}\mathbf{1}^{2,1}.

(55)

It is obtained by deleting the last row and column of (
14
), and the substitution
b_{i}=a_{j+1}
,
i=1,\ldots,j
. We obtain 1 Casimir,
\mathcal{C}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{q_{i}}
, with

q_{i}=\begin{cases}1/a_{m}&i=2m-1,\quad m=1,\ldots,j+1,\\
-1/a_{m}&i=2m,\quad m=1,\ldots,j.\end{cases}

The vector
\mathbf{r}=r\mathbf{1}
is not in the image of
\mathbf{A}
, but we can take

r_{i}=\begin{cases}r&i<n\\
0&i=n.\end{cases}

The Hamiltonian is
H=\sum_{i=1}^{2j+1}x_{i}+k_{i}\ln(x_{i})
, with

k_{i}=\begin{cases}-rq_{i}&i<n\\
0&i=n.\end{cases}

and the functions

\displaystyle R_{i}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}\left(\frac{F_{i}}{F_{i+1}}\right)^{1/(a_{j+1}(c_{i}-c_{i+1}))}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{x_{2i-1}+x_{2i}}{x_{2i+1}+x_{2i+2}}\right)^{1/(c_{i}-c_{i+1})}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left(\frac{x_{2i-1}}{x_{2i}}\right)^{1/a_{i}}

(56)

are pairwise commuting integrals that do not depend on the variable
x_{n}
. This yields the following result.

Proposition 13
.

The inhomogeneous
n=2j+1
dimensional
[j,0,0]
LV system is Liouville integrable.

We note that there is one additional DP,
P_{1,n-1}
, which yields an extra integral,

\frac{P_{1,n-1}}{P_{1,2}}\prod_{i=2}^{j}\left(\frac{x_{2i-1}}{x_{2i}}\right)^{(c_{i}-c_{1})/a_{i}}.

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&a_{1}&\frac{b_{1}c_{2}-b_{2}c_{1}}{a_{3}}&\frac{b_{1}c_{2}-b_{2}c_{1}}{a_{3}}&b_{1}\\
-a_{1}&0&\frac{b_{1}c_{2}-b_{2}c_{1}}{a_{3}}&\frac{b_{1}c_{2}-b_{2}c_{1}}{a_{3}}&b_{1}\\
-\frac{b_{1}c_{2}-b_{2}c_{1}}{a_{3}}&-\frac{b_{1}c_{2}-b_{2}c_{1}}{a_{3}}&0&a_{2}&b_{2}\\
-\frac{b_{1}c_{2}-b_{2}c_{1}}{a_{3}}&-\frac{b_{1}c_{2}-b_{2}c_{1}}{a_{3}}&-a_{2}&0&b_{2}\\
-b_{1}&-b_{1}&-b_{2}&-b_{2}&0\end{array}\right],

integral
\dfrac{\left(x_{1}+x_{2}\right)^{b_{2}}}{\left(x_{3}+x_{4}\right)^{b_{1}}}x_{5}^{\frac{c_{2}b_{1}-c_{1}b_{2}}{a_{3}}}
and Casimir

\left(\dfrac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\right)^{b_{1}a_{2}}\left(\dfrac{x_{3}}{x_{4}}\right)^{a_{1}b_{2}}x_{5}^{a_{1}a_{2}}
.

The inhomogeneous [2,0,0] system, with
\mathbf{r}=(r,r,r,r,0)
, has matrix

\mathbf{A}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&a_{1}&c_{2}-c_{1}&c_{2}-c_{1}&a_{3}\\
-a_{1}&0&c_{2}-c_{1}&c_{2}-c_{1}&a_{3}\\
c_{1}-c_{2}&c_{1}-c_{2}&0&a_{2}&a_{3}\\
c_{1}-c_{2}&c_{1}-c_{2}&-a_{2}&0&a_{3}\\
-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{3}&0\end{array}\right],

Hamiltonian

H=x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}+x_{5}+\frac{r}{a_{1}}\ln\left(\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\right)+\frac{r}{a_{2}}\ln\left(\frac{x_{4}}{x_{3}}\right),

integrals

\left(\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{x_{3}+x_{4}}\right)^{1/(c_{1}-c_{2})}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\right)^{1/a_{1}}\left(\frac{x_{3}}{x_{4}}\right)^{1/a_{2}},\quad\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}}{x_{1}+x_{2}}\left(\frac{x_{3}}{x_{4}}\right)^{(c_{2}-a_{1})/a_{2}}

and Casimir

\left(\dfrac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\right)^{1/a_{1}}\left(\dfrac{x_{3}}{x_{4}}\right)^{1/a_{2}}x_{5}^{1/a_{3}}
.

2.

The homogeneous [1,1,0] system has matrix

\mathbf{A}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&a_{1}&b_{1}&b_{1}&b_{1}\\
-a_{1}&0&b_{1}&b_{1}&b_{1}\\
-b_{1}&-b_{1}&0&a_{2}&a_{3}\\
-b_{1}&-b_{1}&-a_{2}&0&a_{4}\\
-b_{1}&-b_{1}&-a_{3}&-a_{4}&0\end{array}\right],

integral
\frac{x_{4}^{a_{3}}\left(x_{3}+x_{4}+x_{5}\right)^{a_{2}-a_{3}+a_{4}}}{x_{3}^{a_{4}}x_{5}^{a_{2}}}
and Casimir
\left(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\right)^{\frac{b_{1}}{a_{1}}}\left(\frac{x_{3}^{a_{4}}x_{5}^{a_{2}}}{x_{4}^{a_{3}}}\right)^{1/(a_{2}-a_{3}+a_{4})}
.
One can not add a inhomogeneous term of the form
\mathbf{r}=(r,\ldots,r,s)
. However, one can take
\mathbf{r}=(r,r,0,0,0)
(without constraints on the parameters) and then the Hamiltonian is

H=x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}+x_{5}+\frac{r}{a_{1}}\ln\left(\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\right).

3.

The [0,1,1] system has matrix

\mathbf{A}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&a_{1}&a_{2}&a_{4}&a_{4}\\
-a_{1}&0&a_{3}&a_{4}&a_{4}\\
-a_{2}&-a_{3}&0&a_{4}&a_{4}\\
-a_{4}&-a_{4}&-a_{4}&0&a_{5}\\
-a_{4}&-a_{4}&-a_{4}&-a_{5}&0\end{array}\right],

integral
\frac{x_{2}^{a_{2}}\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}\right)^{a_{1}-a_{2}+a_{3}}}{x_{1}^{a_{3}}x_{3}^{a_{1}}}
and Casimir
\left(\frac{x_{1}^{a_{3}}x_{3}^{a_{1}}}{x_{2}^{a_{2}}}\right)^{a_{5}}\left(\frac{x_{5}}{x_{4}}\right)^{a_{4}(a_{1}-a_{2}+a_{3})}
.
The Hamiltonian
H=x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}+x_{5}+r\frac{a_{5}-a_{4}}{a_{4}a_{5}}\ln(x_{4})+\frac{r}{a_{5}}\ln(x_{5})
yields a inhomogeneous term with
\mathbf{r}=\left(r,r,r,r,r\frac{a_{4}-a_{5}}{a_{4}}\right)
.

4.

The [0,2,0] system (which contains the [0,1,1] case) has matrix

\mathbf{A}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&a_{1}&a_{2}&a_{4}&a_{5}\\
-a_{1}&0&a_{3}&a_{4}&a_{5}\\
-a_{2}&-a_{3}&0&a_{4}&a_{5}\\
-a_{4}&-a_{4}&-a_{4}&0&a_{6}\\
-a_{5}&-a_{5}&-a_{5}&-a_{6}&0\end{array}\right],

integral
\frac{x_{2}^{a_{2}}\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}\right)^{a_{1}-a_{2}+a_{3}}}{x_{1}^{a_{3}}x_{3}^{a_{1}}}
and Casimir
\left(\frac{x_{1}^{a_{3}}x_{3}^{a_{1}}}{x_{2}^{a_{2}}}\right)^{a_{6}}\left(\frac{x_{5}^{a_{4}}}{x_{4}^{a_{5}}}\right)^{a_{1}-a_{2}+a_{3}}
.
The inhomogeneous term is
\mathbf{r}=\left(r,r,r,r,r\frac{\left(a_{5}-a_{6}\right)}{a_{4}}\right)
with Hamiltonian

H=x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}+x_{5}+b\frac{a_{6}-a_{5}}{a_{4}a_{6}}\ln\left(x_{4}\right)+\frac{b}{a_{6}}\ln\left(x_{5}\right)
.

4.5
Examples of Liouville integrable LV systems not of type
[j,k,l]

There exist Liouville integrable LV systems with associated hypergraphs which are not in the
[j,k,l]
family displayed in Figure
2
. These systems seem to have less parameters, and additional DPs and/or integrals.

In this section, the hyperedges correspond to the DPs of the system, not to associated commuting integrals (which does not even make sense when the matrix
\mathbf{A}
is singular). We give three examples of
10
-component classes of LV systems.

1.

The 10-component LV system with rank 8 matrix

\mathbf{A}=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{1}&a_{1}&a_{1}&a_{2}&a_{2}&a_{2}&a_{3}&a_{3}&a_{3}\\
-a_{1}&0&a_{1}&a_{1}&a_{2}&a_{2}&a_{2}&a_{3}&a_{3}&a_{3}\\
-a_{1}&-a_{1}&0&0&a_{2}&a_{2}&a_{2}&a_{3}&a_{3}&a_{3}\\
-a_{1}&-a_{1}&0&0&a_{2}&a_{2}&a_{2}&a_{3}&a_{3}&a_{3}\\
-a_{2}&-a_{2}&-a_{2}&-a_{2}&0&a_{4}&a_{5}&a_{7}&a_{7}&a_{7}\\
-a_{2}&-a_{2}&-a_{2}&-a_{2}&-a_{4}&0&a_{6}&a_{7}&a_{7}&a_{7}\\
-a_{2}&-a_{2}&-a_{2}&-a_{2}&-a_{5}&-a_{6}&0&a_{7}&a_{7}&a_{7}\\
-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{7}&-a_{7}&-a_{7}&0&a_{8}&a_{9}\\
-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{7}&-a_{7}&-a_{7}&-a_{8}&0&a_{10}\\
-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{7}&-a_{7}&-a_{7}&-a_{9}&-a_{10}&0\end{pmatrix}

(63)

admits (apart from the Hamiltonian
H=\sum_{i=1}^{10}x_{i}
) the following DPs

x_{1}+x_{2},x_{3}+x_{4},x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4},x_{5}+x_{6}+x_{7},x_{8}+x_{9}+x_{10},x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4},

that is, it has the associated hypergraph depicted in Fig.
6
.

Figure 6
:
Hypergraph on
10
vertices associated to matrix (
63
).

The matrix (
63
) has rank
10-2=8
. The functions

\frac{x_{3}}{x_{4}},\quad x_{1}^{-a_{7}}x_{2}^{a_{7}}x_{3}^{-a_{7}}x_{5}^{\frac{a_{3}a_{6}}{a_{4}-a_{5}+a_{6}}}x_{6}^{-\frac{a_{3}a_{5}}{a_{4}-a_{5}+a_{6}}}x_{7}^{\frac{a_{3}a_{4}}{a_{4}-a_{5}+a_{6}}}x_{8}^{-\frac{a_{10}a_{2}}{a_{8}-a_{9}+a_{10}}}x_{9}^{\frac{a_{2}a_{9}}{a_{8}-a_{9}+a_{10}}}x_{10}^{-\frac{a_{8}a_{2}}{a_{8}-a_{9}+a_{10}}}

are Casimirs, and the set of integrals
H
,

\frac{\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}\right)x_{2}}{x_{1}x_{3}},\quad\frac{\left(x_{5}+x_{6}+x_{7}\right)^{a_{4}-a_{5}+a_{6}}x_{6}^{a_{5}}}{x_{5}^{a_{6}}x_{7}^{a_{4}}},\quad\frac{\left(x_{8}+x_{9}+x_{10}\right)^{a_{8}-a_{9}+a_{10}}x_{9}^{a_{9}}}{x_{8}^{a_{10}}x_{10}^{a_{8}}}

is pairwise commuting. Although the hypergraph in Fig.
6
contains the type
[2,1,1]
hypergraph, the LV system with matrix (
63
) is not a subsystem of the corresponding LV system.

2.

The LV system with rank 8 matrix

\mathbf{A}=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{1}&a_{2}&a_{2}&a_{3}&a_{3}&a_{3}&a_{3}&a_{3}&a_{3}\\
-a_{1}&0&a_{2}&a_{2}&a_{3}&a_{3}&a_{3}&a_{3}&a_{3}&a_{3}\\
-a_{2}&-a_{2}&0&a_{4}&a_{5}&a_{5}&a_{5}&a_{5}&a_{5}&a_{5}\\
-a_{2}&-a_{2}&-a_{4}&0&a_{5}&a_{5}&a_{5}&a_{5}&a_{5}&a_{5}\\
-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{5}&-a_{5}&0&a_{6}&a_{7}&a_{7}&a_{7}&a_{7}\\
-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{5}&-a_{5}&-a_{6}&0&a_{7}&a_{7}&a_{7}&a_{7}\\
-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{5}&-a_{5}&-a_{7}&-a_{7}&0&a_{7}&a_{7}&a_{7}\\
-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{5}&-a_{5}&-a_{7}&-a_{7}&-a_{7}&0&a_{8}-a_{9}&a_{8}\\
-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{5}&-a_{5}&-a_{7}&-a_{7}&-a_{7}&-a_{8}+a_{9}&0&a_{9}\\
-a_{3}&-a_{3}&-a_{5}&-a_{5}&-a_{7}&-a_{7}&-a_{7}&-a_{8}&-a_{9}&0\end{pmatrix}

(64)

admits the DPs

x_{1}+x_{2},\ x_{3}+x_{4},\ x_{5}+x_{6},\ x_{5}+x_{6}+x_{7},\ x_{8}+x_{9}+x_{10},\ x_{7}+x_{8}+x_{9}+x_{10},\ x_{5}+x_{6}+x_{7}+x_{8}+x_{9}+x_{10},

see associated hypergraph in Fig.

.

It possesses 8 functionally independent integrals

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}+x_{5}+x_{6}+x_{7}+x_{8}+x_{9}+x_{10}

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\right)^{\frac{a_{3}}{a_{1}}}\left(\frac{x_{3}}{x_{4}}\right)^{\frac{a_{5}}{a_{4}}}\left(\frac{x_{5}}{x_{6}}\right)^{\frac{a_{7}}{a_{6}}}x_{7}\left(\frac{x_{10}}{x_{8}}\right)^{\frac{a_{7}}{a_{8}}},\quad C_{2}=\frac{x_{9}^{a_{8}}x_{10}^{a_{9}-a_{8}}}{x_{8}^{a_{9}}}

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\right)^{\frac{a_{3}}{a_{1}}}\left(\frac{x_{4}}{x_{3}}\right)^{\frac{a_{5}}{a_{4}}}\frac{\left(x_{1}+x_{2}\right)^{\frac{a_{5}}{a_{2}}}}{\left(x_{3}+x_{4}\right)^{\frac{a_{3}}{a_{2}}}}

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\right)^{\frac{a_{3}}{a_{1}}}\left(\frac{x_{3}}{x_{4}}\right)^{\frac{a_{5}}{a_{4}}}\left(x_{5}+x_{6}+x_{7}+x_{8}+x_{9}+x_{10}\right)

\displaystyle R_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(x_{5}+x_{6}+x_{7}\right)^{a_{6}}x_{6}^{a_{7}}}{x_{5}^{a_{7}}x_{7}^{a_{6}}},\quad R_{4}=\frac{\left(x_{7}+x_{8}+x_{9}+x_{10}\right)^{a_{8}}x_{8}^{a_{7}}}{x_{7}^{a_{8}}x_{10}^{a_{7}}}

\displaystyle R_{5}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\right)^{\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}}\left(x_{3}+x_{4}\right)\left(\frac{x_{6}}{x_{5}}\right)^{\frac{a_{5}}{a_{6}}}\left(\frac{x_{8}+x_{9}+x_{10}}{x_{7}}\right)^{\frac{a_{5}}{a_{7}}},

where

are Casimirs, and the following brackets vanish

\{R_{1},R_{2}\}=\{R_{1},R_{3}\}=\{R_{1},R_{4}\}=\{R_{2},R_{3}\}=\{R_{2},R_{4}\}=\{R_{3},R_{5}\}=0.

3.

The LV system with nonsingular matrix

\mathbf{A}=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{1}&a_{2}&a_{4}&a_{4}&a_{4}&a_{5}&a_{5}&a_{5}&a_{5}\\
-a_{1}&0&a_{3}&a_{4}&a_{4}&a_{4}&a_{5}&a_{5}&a_{5}&a_{5}\\
-a_{2}&-a_{3}&0&a_{4}&a_{4}&a_{4}&a_{5}&a_{5}&a_{5}&a_{5}\\
-a_{4}&-a_{4}&-a_{4}&0&a_{6}&a_{7}&a_{9}&a_{9}&a_{9}&a_{9}\\
-a_{4}&-a_{4}&-a_{4}&-a_{6}&0&a_{8}&a_{9}&a_{9}&a_{9}&a_{9}\\
-a_{4}&-a_{4}&-a_{4}&-a_{7}&-a_{8}&0&a_{9}&a_{9}&a_{9}&a_{9}\\
-a_{5}&-a_{5}&-a_{5}&-a_{9}&-a_{9}&-a_{9}&0&a_{11}&a_{11}&a_{11}\\
-a_{5}&-a_{5}&-a_{5}&-a_{9}&-a_{9}&-a_{9}&-a_{11}&0&a_{10}&a_{11}\\
-a_{5}&-a_{5}&-a_{5}&-a_{9}&-a_{9}&-a_{9}&-a_{11}&-a_{10}&0&a_{11}\\
-a_{5}&-a_{5}&-a_{5}&-a_{9}&-a_{9}&-a_{9}&-a_{11}&-a_{11}&-a_{11}&0\end{pmatrix}

(65)

admits the DPs

x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3},x_{4}+x_{5}+x_{6},x_{7}+x_{8}+x_{9},x_{8}+x_{9},x_{8}+x_{9}+x_{10},x_{7}+x_{8}+x_{9}+x_{10},

as depicted in Fig.
8
.

:

).

The system has the following set of pairwise commuting integrals

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}\right)^{-a_{1}+a_{2}-a_{3}}x_{1}^{a_{3}}x_{2}^{-a_{2}}x_{3}^{a_{1}},\quad T_{2}=\left(x_{4}+x_{5}+x_{6}\right)^{-a_{6}+a_{7}-a_{8}}x_{4}^{a_{8}}x_{5}^{-a_{7}}x_{6}^{a_{6}},

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(x_{7}+x_{8}+x_{9}\right)\left(x_{8}+x_{9}+x_{10}\right)}{x_{7}x_{10}},\quad H=x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{10},

\displaystyle T_{4}

\displaystyle=\left(x_{8}+x_{9}\right)^{-a_{4}}x_{1}^{\frac{a_{9}a_{3}}{a_{1}-a_{2}+a_{3}}}x_{2}^{-\frac{a_{2}a_{9}}{a_{1}-a_{2}+a_{3}}}x_{3}^{\frac{a_{1}a_{9}}{a_{1}-a_{2}+a_{3}}}x_{4}^{-\frac{a_{8}a_{5}}{a_{6}-a_{7}+a_{8}}}x_{5}^{\frac{a_{5}a_{7}}{a_{6}-a_{7}+a_{8}}}x_{6}^{-\frac{a_{6}a_{5}}{a_{6}-a_{7}+a_{8}}}x_{7}^{a_{4}}x_{10}^{a_{4}},

and 1 additional integral

T_{5}=x_{8}^{-a_{11}}x_{9}^{a_{11}}x_{10}^{-a_{10}}\left(x_{8}+x_{9}+x_{10}\right)^{a_{10}}

which commutes with
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
.

The system is not equivalent to a subsystem of the 10-component LV system of type
[2,1,1]
.

We note that the linear transformations that preserve the Lotka-Volterra structure (and induce the equivalence relation introduced in
[
20
]
) do not preserve the antisymmetry of the matrix
\mathbf{A}
.
