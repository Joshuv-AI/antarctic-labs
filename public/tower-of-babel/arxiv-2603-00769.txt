An Inexact Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers for Constrained Parabolic Optimal Distributed Control Problems
Haiming Song, Jinda Yang, Yuran Yang, Jianhua Yuan

Abstract
Solving parabolic optimal control problems can be inherently challenging in the field of science and engineering, especially with constraints on the nonsmooth distributed control. Motivated by the extensive applicability of the alternating direction method of multipliers, in this paper we develop a novel inexact algorithmic framework for parabolic optimal distributed control problems with control constraints. By decoupling the control constraint and possible nonsmooth objective from the optimal control problem, our aim is to efficiently solve the subproblem constrained by the parabolic state equation, for which computing a sufficiently accurate numerical solution can be prohibitively expensive. Given this high computational cost, we consider that it may not always be justifiable to compute a highly accurate solution of the subproblem at every iteration. Hence, we propose an inexact strategy for solving the parabolic equation constrained subproblem. Under mild and flexible conditions on the parameters, we prove global convergence and a linear convergence rate for the resulting algorithmic framework. In practice, our inexact algorithmic framework is easily implementable with applicable nested iterations. Numerical experiments are performed on different cases, and the results demonstrate the validity and trustworthy performance of the proposed methods. Keywords : Parabolic optimal distributed control, alternating direction method of multipliers, inexact framework, linear convergence rate. MSC 49M41, 35Q90, 35Q93, 65K05, 90C25

Introduction

Optimal control of partial differential equations (PDEs) has important applications in various scientific and industrial fields, such as physics, chemistry, medicine, finance, and engineering. We refer to, for instance,

In such problems, the state is governed by a linear parabolic equation, and the distributed control enters as a source term subject to additional constraints. Specifically, the control is allowed to be sparse and may act either on the whole spatial domain or only on a prescribed subdomain.

From an algorithmic perspective, we are facing nonsmooth optimization problems that couple state equation constraint with additional control constraints, and that after numerical discretization, typically lead to large and complicated algebraic systems, especially in the context of time-dependent PDE-constrained problems. Numerically, such problems are better solved by algorithms that carefully exploit the structure and properties of the underlying model, rather than by generic off-the-shelf methods.

In this paper, we consider the following constrained parabolic optimal distributed control problem:

\min\limits_{u}\ \mathcal{J}(u)=\dfrac{\gamma_{d}}{2}\displaystyle\iint_{Q}|y(u)-y_{d}|^{2}{\rm d}x{\rm d}t+\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\iint_{G}|u|^{2}{\rm d}x{\rm d}t+\gamma_{s}\displaystyle\iint_{G}|u|{\rm d}x{\rm d}t,

(1.1)

where
Q=\Omega\times(0,T)
and
G=\Omega_{\text{sub}}\times(0,T)
, with
\Omega_{\text{sub}}
an open subset of the spatial domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}(d\geq 1)
, and
0<T<\infty

\gamma_{d}

\gamma_{s}

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}-\nu\Delta y+a_{0}y=\chi_{G}u,&&\text{\,in}\quad\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&y=0,&&\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&y(0)=\phi,&&\text{\,in}\quad\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

Here
\chi_{G}

G

\Omega_{\text{sub}}
. The initial datum
\phi
is given in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, the positive coefficient
a_{0}\in L^{\infty}(Q)
and
\nu

\mathcal{C}
defined by

\mathcal{C}=\left\{u|u\in L^{2}(G),~a\leq u(x,t)\leq b\text{~a.e.~}(x,t)\in G\right\},

(1.3)

In the special case that the control is unconstrained and the sparsity term is absent, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}=L^{2}(G)
and
\gamma_{s}=0

Moreover, the projected gradient descent (PGD) methods, the semismooth Newton (SSN) methods
[
6
]
, and the augmented Lagrangian methods (ALM)
[
38
]
are popular approaches for constrained and nonsmooth optimal control problems.

Since they are easy to implement, PGD methods remain among the methods of choice for complex problems. Note also that the parabolic optimal control problems require much more computational effort due to the additional time variable, and thus PGD methods are suitable for numerical simulations and constitute a valuable alternative to methods with higher orders of convergence; see, e.g.,
[
15
,
34
,
9
]
. A crucial step in the implementation of PGD methods is the calculation of the descent stepsizes, which are usually expensive to calculate exactly or up to high accuracy. To estimate proper stepsizes, a common idea is to employ a line search strategy, such as a backtracking technique based on the Armijo or Wolfe conditions; see, e.g.,
[
5
,
34
,
24
]
. It should be mentioned, however, that enforcing these line search conditions demands the repeated evaluation of the objective functional or its derivative, which may become very costly in practice because each evaluation involves solving the state equation and the corresponding adjoint equation repeatedly.

The interest in SSN methods is driven by the increased complexity of the optimality conditions of the problem, due to the nonsmooth part of the objective and the indicator function associated with the control constraint, a situation in which PGD methods are not applicable; see, e.g.,
[
18
,
34
]
. With a semismooth Newton direction constructed using the Clarke generalized Jacobian, SSN methods employ Newton-type iterations based on active-set strategies that iteratively identify active and inactive components with respect to the nonsmooth objective term and the control constraints. It was shown in
[
17
,
16
]
that SSN methods exhibit local superlinear convergence for suitably chosen initial values, under the standard assumption that all Newton systems are solved exactly. Nevertheless, the Newton systems encountered in SSN methods are typically ill-conditioned, which underscores the need for appropriate preconditioners to ensure efficiency; see, e.g.,
[
32
,
28
,
35
]
. When employing SSN methods, the nonsmooth objective term and the control constraint are handled together with the associated parabolic equation; hence one has to recompute the preconditioner at each iteration as the active set varies. Furthermore, we often face unavoidable large-scale Newton systems when fine meshes are used to discretize time-dependent problems. Although the above issues can be mitigated by adaptive strategies
[
2
]
and inner iterations
[
26
]
, the computational load associated with solving the Newton systems still remains substantial; see, e.g.,
[
33
,
32
]
, and may impede the practical efficiency of SSN methods.

As a fundamental approach to various constrained problems, the augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) has also been applied to optimal control problems. By handling the inequality constraints directly, the resulting iteration schemes become rather elaborate and complicated, which makes it challenging to analyze their convergence rate. The alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM), which may be regarded as a splitting version of the classical ALM, has also been used to solve parabolic optimal control problems
[
13
,
30
]
.

Inspired by the above considerations, we aim to apply ADMM and to develop a uniform iteration scheme that can effectively solve the constrained parabolic optimal distributed control problem (
1.1
) with a nonsmooth part of the objective and control constraints. The key advantage of ADMM lies in the fact that the main subproblem in ALM is decomposed and the resulting subproblems can be solved more easily at each iteration in a Gauss–Seidel fashion. Keeping this in mind, our first motivation is to separate the parabolic equation constraint (
1.2
) from the nonsmooth part of the objective in (
1.1
) and the control constraints (
1.3
) into two different subproblems. As a result, one of the subproblems should be tackled using iteration-independent coefficient matrices derived from the numerical discretization of the parabolic equation, whereas the other can be solved explicitly. However, it is important to note that the subproblem constrained by the parabolic equation is time-dependent and thus remains high-dimensional after space–time discretization. Although this subproblem can be solved by inner iterations, achieving high accuracy comes at a high computational cost. Hence, our second motivation is to design an adaptive inexact strategy for this subproblem, in order to alleviate the high cost of solving it exactly at each iteration. Numerically, the adaptive inexact strategy should be readily implementable, independent of the discretization mesh sizes, and capable of achieving sufficient accuracy. Theoretically, the adaptive inexact strategy must be robust enough to guarantee the convergence of the resulting inexact ADMM.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we propose the algorithmic framework of the inexact ADMM based on a systematic inspection of the model problem. Section
3
is devoted to the convergence analysis of the inexact ADMM framework. The numerical implementation and experiments are presented in Section
4
. Finally, conclusions are drawn in Section
5
.

2
Algorithmic framework

In this section, we introduce some notation and elaborate on the essential concept of our inexact ADMM for the constrained parabolic distributed optimal control problem (
1.1
).

For clarity and convenience, we use
U
and
Y
for the spaces
L^{2}(G)
and
L^{2}(Q)
, respectively. We let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\|\cdot\|
denote the
L^{2}
inner product and norm with respect to
U
or
Y
without causing confusion. The
L^{1}
-norm on
U
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{1}
. To simplify the presentation, we introduce the control-to-state operator
S:U\rightarrow Y
defined by
y=S(u)
, associated with the state equation (
1.2
). By the superposition principle
[
34
]
, it is evident that the affine operator
S
can be represented as follows:

S(u)=\bar{S}u+S(0),

where the linear part
\bar{S}\in\mathcal{L}(U,Y)
solves (
1.2
) with zero initial data (i.e.,
\phi=0
). We let
\bar{S}^{\ast}:Y\rightarrow U
defines the adjoint operator of
\bar{S}
.

By introducing an auxiliary variable
z
, the problem (
1.1
) can be reformulated as:

\displaystyle\min\limits_{u,z\in U}

\displaystyle J(u)+R(z)

(2.1)

\displaystyle\text{s.~t.}

\displaystyle u=z,

where

\displaystyle J(u)=\frac{\gamma_{d}}{2}\|S(u)-y_{d}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2},\quad R(z)=\gamma_{s}\|z\|_{1}+I_{\mathcal{C}}(z).

and
I_{\mathcal{C}}
denotes the extended-valued indicator function of the admissible set
\mathcal{C}
for the control constraints.
With a penalty parameter
\beta>0
, the associated augmented Lagrangian functional is given by

L_{\beta}(u,z,\lambda):=J(u)+R(z)-\langle\lambda,u-z\rangle+\frac{\beta}{2}\|u-z\|^{2},

(2.2)

where
\lambda\in U
is the Lagrange multiplier associated with the linear constraint
u=z
. Then, we can directly apply the classic ADMM to (
2.1
) as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{u}^{k+1}=\mathop{\arg\min}\limits_{u\in U}L_{\beta}(u,\hat{z}^{k},\hat{\lambda}^{k}),

(2.3a)

\displaystyle\hat{z}^{k+1}=\mathop{\arg\min}\limits_{z\in U}L_{\beta}(\hat{u}^{k+1},z,\hat{\lambda}^{k}),

(2.3b)

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}=\hat{\lambda}^{k}-\beta(\hat{u}^{k+1}-\hat{z}^{k+1}).

(2.3c)

Despite the concision of the above scheme, it is ideational that each subproblem requires exact minimization. In practice, not all subproblems can be easily solved in a high precision.

Let us take a close look at the subproblems (
2.3a
) and (
2.3b
). From (
2.2
), it follows that the
u
-subproblem (
2.3a
) takes the form of the following smooth parabolic optimal control problem without control constraint:

\hat{u}^{k+1}=\underset{u\in U}{\arg\min}\left\{J(u)-\langle\hat{\lambda}^{k},u-\hat{z}^{k}\rangle+\frac{\beta}{2}\|u-\hat{z}^{k}\|^{2}\right\}.

With the constraint of state equation (
1.2
) presented by
y=S(u)
, the solution
\hat{u}^{k+1}
satisfies

\hat{u}^{k+1}+\gamma_{d}\hat{p}^{k+1}-\hat{\lambda}^{k}+\beta(\hat{u}^{k+1}-\hat{z}^{k})=0,

(2.4)

where the adjoint variable
\hat{p}^{k+1}=\bar{S}^{\ast}(S(\hat{u}^{k+1})-y_{d})
satisfies the following adjoint equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\frac{\partial\hat{p}^{k+1}}{\partial t}-\nu\Delta\hat{p}^{k+1}+a_{0}\hat{p}^{k+1}=\hat{y}^{k+1}-y_{d},&\quad&\text{\,in}\quad\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&\hat{p}^{k+1}=0,&\quad&\text{on}\quad\Gamma\times(0,T),\\
&\hat{p}^{k+1}(T)=0,&\quad&\text{\,in}\quad\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

in which
\hat{y}^{k+1}
is the solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial\hat{y}^{k+1}}{\partial t}-\nu\Delta\hat{y}^{k+1}+a_{0}\hat{y}^{k+1}=\chi_{G}\hat{u}^{k+1},&\quad&\text{\,in}\quad\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&\hat{y}^{k+1}=0,&\quad&\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&\hat{y}^{k+1}(0)=\phi&\quad&\text{\,in}\quad\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.6)

For the
z
-subproblem (
2.3b
), we have

\hat{z}^{k+1}=\underset{z\in U}{\arg\min}\left\{R(z)-\langle\hat{\lambda}^{k},\hat{u}^{k+1}-z\rangle+\frac{\beta}{2}\|\hat{u}^{k+1}-z\|^{2}\right\},

and it is easy to check that this subproblem admits the explicit solution

\hat{z}^{k+1}=P_{\mathcal{C}}\left(S_{\frac{\gamma_{s}}{\beta}}(\hat{u}^{k+1}-\beta^{-1}\hat{\lambda}^{k})\right),

where the projection operator
P_{\mathcal{C}}
and the soft-thresholding operator
S_{\frac{\gamma_{s}}{\beta}}
are defined pointwise by

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{C}}(v(x,t))=\min\left\{b,\max\{v(x,t),a\}\right\},

\displaystyle S_{\frac{\gamma_{s}}{\beta}}(v(x,t))=\text{sign}(v(x,t))\max\{|v(x,t)|-\beta^{-1}\gamma_{s},0\}.

Based on the above observations, it can be seen that the computational difficulties of solving the subproblems (
2.3a
) and (
2.3b
) are quite different. We can obtain the closed-form solution of
z
-subproblem (
2.3b
) by the soft-thresholding operator and projection onto the set
\mathcal{C}
. Nevertheless, as a standard optimization problem constrained by the parabolic equation (
1.2
), the solution of
u
-subproblem (
2.3a
) is essentially determined by the coupled time-dependent system (
2.4
)-(
2.6
) at
k
-th iteration, and consequently, attaining a solution to this system with high accuracy will spend expensive cost. Hence, it is advisable to solve the
u
-subproblem (
2.3a
) iteratively and inexactly.

A naive idea is to perform nested iterations such that

\|u^{k+1}-\tilde{u}^{k+1}\|\leq\theta_{k},

(2.7)

where
u^{k+1}
and
\tilde{u}^{k+1}
denote, respectively, the inexact solution and the auxiliary solution of the
u
-subproblem associated with
(u^{k},z^{k},\lambda^{k})^{\top}
at
k
-th iteration. Note that the auxiliary solution
\tilde{u}^{k+1}
is distinct from the exact solution
\hat{u}^{k+1}
, because here the solution
\tilde{u}^{k+1}
depends on the inexact triplet
(u^{k},z^{k},\lambda^{k})^{\top}
.

Clearly, the above criterion can not be applied directly in practical implementations, and we should employ an executable criterion for the inexact iteration. To this end, we define
\sigma_{k}(u)
by

\sigma_{k}(u)=(1+\beta)u+\gamma_{d}\bar{S}^{\ast}(S(u)-y_{d})-(\beta z^{k}+\lambda^{k}).

(2.8)

From the optimality condition of
\tilde{u}^{k+1}
and the definitions of operators
S
and
\bar{S}^{\ast}
, it follows that
\sigma_{k}(\tilde{u}^{k+1})=0
for any
k>0
. Given this, we propose the following criterion for solving
u
-subproblem inexactly:

\|\sigma_{k}(u^{k+1})\|\leq\theta_{k},

(2.9)

where
\theta_{k}
is a criterion parameter. In addition, we let
\Theta_{k}
denotes the set

\Theta_{k}=\{u|~\|\sigma_{k}(u)\|\leq\theta_{k}\}.

Then, for the problem (
1.1
), the framework of inexact ADMM can be given by

\displaystyle u^{k+1}\in\Theta_{k},

(2.10a)

\displaystyle z^{k+1}=P_{\mathcal{C}}\left(S_{\frac{\gamma_{s}}{\beta_{k}}}(u^{k+1}-\beta^{-1}_{k}\lambda^{k})\right),

(2.10b)

\displaystyle\lambda^{k+1}=\lambda^{k}-\beta_{k}(u^{k+1}-z^{k+1}),

(2.10c)

where the penalty parameter
\beta_{k}
can be adjusted at each iteration.

Remark 1
.

It is apparent that, in the above framework, the equation constraint have been decoupled from the nonsmooth objective term and the control constraint at each iteration, and the subproblems (
2.10b
) and (
2.10c
) can be solved easily. For the subproblem (
2.10a
), the criterion parameter
\theta_{k}
does not need to be prescribed a priori as a fixed small constant and is independent of the discretization mesh sizes. Indeed, the criterion parameter
\theta_{k}
can be set specified according to an appropriate rule (see Section
4
for details), and the inexact iterations can be performed automatically. In addition, the subproblem (
2.10a
) can be solved by a suitable nested iteration, such as the conjugate gradient (CG) method described in Section
4
. With these features, the framework (
2.10a
)-(
2.10c
) can be easily implemented.

3
Convergence analysis

In this section, we first present some mild assumptions and useful results for the subsequent analysis. Then we establish the convergence of the proposed inexact ADMM framework (
2.10a
)-(
2.10c
).

3.1
Preliminaries

We begin this subsection with the following assumptions that will be used throughout the paper.

Assumption 1
.

The sequence of augmented penalty parameters
\{\beta_{k}\}_{0}^{\infty}
satisfies

\beta_{0}>0,\quad\frac{1}{1+\eta_{k}}\beta_{k}\leq\beta_{k+1}\leq(1+\eta_{k})\beta_{k},\quad\forall~k\geq 0,

where
\{\eta_{k}\}_{0}^{\infty}
is a non-negative sequence and
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\eta_{k}<+\infty
.

Assumption 2
.

The sequence of criterion parameters
\{\theta_{k}\}_{0}^{\infty}
satisfies

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\theta_{k}<+\infty.

Note that under Assumption
1
the condition
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\eta_{k}<+\infty
implies that
\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+\eta_{k})<+\infty
, and hence that the sequence
{\beta_{k}}
is uniformly bounded. More precisely, we have

0<\underline{\beta}=\left(\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+\eta_{k})\right)^{-1}\beta_{0}\leq\beta_{k}\leq\left(\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+\eta_{k})\right)\beta_{0}=\overline{\beta}.

Next, we state some properties of the functional
J
in (
2.1
).
Let
DJ(u)
denote the Fréchet gradient of
J
at
u
. Then the gradient operator
DJ
is Lipschitz continuous and there exists a constant
L=1+\gamma_{d}\|\bar{S}^{*}\bar{S}\|
such that

\|DJ(u_{1})-DJ(u_{2})\|\leq L\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|,\quad\forall~u_{1},u_{2}\in U.

(3.1)

As a standard result (see, e.g.,
[
23
]
), the following inequality holds:

J(u_{1})\geq J(u_{2})+\langle DJ(u_{2}),u_{1}-u_{2}\rangle+\dfrac{1}{2L}\|DJ(u_{1})-DJ(u_{2})\|^{2},\quad\forall~u_{1},u_{2}\in U.

(3.2)

Moreover, the functional
J
is also strongly convex and the gradient operator
DJ
is strongly monotone, that is,

\langle DJ(u_{1})-DJ(u_{2}),u_{1}-u_{2}\rangle\geq\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|^{2},\quad\forall~u_{1},u_{2}\in U,

(3.3)

with modulus
1
. Consequently, it holds that

J(u_{1})\geq J(u_{2})+\langle DJ(u_{2}),u_{1}-u_{2}\rangle+\dfrac{1}{2}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|^{2},\quad\forall~u_{1},u_{2}\in U.

(3.4)

To simplify the presentation, we use
\omega
for
(u,z,\lambda)^{\top}
,
\upsilon
for
(z,\lambda)^{\top}
, and denote
W
and
V
as
U\times\mathcal{C}\times\Lambda
and
\mathcal{C}\times\Lambda
, respectively. For any
v\in V
, we define
M_{k}
-induced norm associated with the matrix

M_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}\beta_{k}I&0\\
0&\beta_{k}^{-1}I\end{pmatrix},

by

\|v\|_{M_{k}}=\langle v,M_{k}v\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}=\left(\beta_{k}\|z\|^{2}+\beta_{k}^{-1}\|\lambda\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.5)

By the definition of the augmented Lagrangian functional (
2.2
), a point
\omega^{\ast}=(u^{\ast},z^{\ast},\lambda^{\ast})^{\top}\in W
is an optimal solution to problem (
2.1
) if and only if it satisfies the following variational inequality
[
19
]
:

J(u)+R(z)-J(u^{\ast})-R(z^{\ast})-\langle\lambda^{\ast},u-z\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall~\omega\in W.

(3.6)

Recall that in the classic ADMM (
2.3a
)-(
2.3c
), all subproblems are assumed to be solved exactly. Hence, we obtain from the optimality conditions for
\hat{\omega}^{k+1}=(\hat{u}^{k+1},\hat{z}^{k+1},\hat{\lambda}^{k+1})^{\top}
that

\displaystyle DJ(\hat{u}^{k+1})-(\hat{\lambda}^{k}-\beta_{k}(\hat{u}^{k+1}-\hat{z}^{k}))=0,

(3.7a)

\displaystyle R(z)-R(\hat{z}^{k+1})+\left\langle z-\hat{z}^{k+1},\hat{\lambda}^{k}-\beta_{k}(\hat{u}^{k+1}-\hat{z}^{k+1})\right\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall~z\in U,

(3.7b)

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}=\hat{\lambda}^{k}-\beta_{k}(\hat{u}^{k+1}-\hat{z}^{k+1}).

(3.7c)

We denote by
\tilde{\omega}^{k+1}=(\tilde{u}^{k+1},\tilde{z}^{k+1},\tilde{\lambda}^{k+1})^{\top}
the auxiliary solution, which is the exact solution obtained at
k
-th by updating the inexact solution
\omega^{k}
according to the classic ADMM (
3.7a
)-(
3.7c
). Clearly, for the auxiliary “exact solution”
\tilde{\omega}^{k+1}
, the optimality conditions are given by

\displaystyle DJ(\tilde{u}^{k+1})-(\lambda^{k}-\beta_{k}(\tilde{u}^{k+1}-z^{k}))=0,

(3.8a)

\displaystyle R(z)-R(\tilde{z}^{k+1})+\left\langle z-\tilde{z}^{k+1},\tilde{\lambda}^{k}-\beta_{k}(\tilde{u}^{k+1}-\tilde{z}^{k+1})\right\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall~z\in U,

(3.8b)

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}^{k+1}=\lambda^{k}-\beta_{k}(\tilde{u}^{k+1}-\tilde{z}^{k+1}).

(3.8c)

In addition, we have the following relation between the auxiliary solution and the inexact solution.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\omega^{k}
be the sequence generated by the inexact ADMM framework (
2.10a
)-(
2.10c
), and
\tilde{\omega}^{k+1}
be the corresponding auxiliary solution satisfying (
3.8a
)-(
3.8c
). Then, for all
k\geq 0
, we have

\|u^{k+1}-\tilde{u}^{k+1}\|\leq\theta_{k}.

Proof.

From the definition (
2.8
) and the positivity of
\beta_{k}
, it is easy to check that
\sigma_{k}
is strongly monotone with modulus
\mu_{k}\geq 1
.

For any
u^{k+1},~\tilde{u}^{k+1}\in U
, using the identity

\displaystyle\left\langle b-a,a-c\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(\|b-c\|^{2}-\|a-c\|^{2}-\|b-a\|^{2}),

(3.9)

it holds that

\displaystyle 2\langle u^{k+1}-\tilde{u}^{k+1},\sigma_{k}(u^{k+1})\rangle=\|u^{k+1}-\tilde{u}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|\sigma_{k}(u^{k+1})\|^{2}-\|u^{k+1}-\sigma_{k}(u^{k+1})-\tilde{u}^{k+1}\|^{2}.

Based on the above equation, the fact that
\sigma_{k}(\tilde{u}^{k+1})=0
, and the strongly monotone property of
\sigma_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{k}(u^{k+1})\|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle 2\langle u^{k+1}-\tilde{u}^{k+1},\sigma_{k}(u^{k+1})\rangle-\|u^{k+1}-\tilde{u}^{k+1}\|^{2}

(3.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\langle u^{k+1}-\tilde{u}^{k+1},\sigma_{k}(u^{k+1})-\sigma_{k}(\tilde{u}^{k+1})\rangle-\|u^{k+1}-\tilde{u}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(2\mu_{k}-1)\|u^{k+1}-\tilde{u}^{k+1}\|^{2}.

Since the modulus
\mu_{k}\geq 1
, applying (
2.9
), the proof is completed.
∎

is introduced solely for convergence analysis and is not required in practical computations. While Proposition

provides a bound on the inexactness of

, a completed convergence proof for the inexact ADMM (

)-(

) requires additional properties of the exact ADMM sequence

generated by (

)-(
3.7c
).

Lemma 1
.

Let

(3.12)

Further, using the relations (
3.12

\displaystyle\left\langle DJ(\hat{u}^{k+1}),\hat{u}^{k+1}-u^{\ast}\rangle+\langle\partial R(\hat{z}^{k+1}),\hat{z}^{k+1}-z^{\ast}\right\rangle-\langle\lambda^{\ast},\hat{u}^{k+1}-\hat{z}^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\langle DJ(\hat{u}^{k+1}),\hat{u}^{k+1}-u^{\ast}\rangle+\left\langle\partial R(\hat{z}^{k+1}),\hat{z}^{k+1}-z^{\ast}\right\rangle-\langle\lambda^{\ast},\hat{u}^{k+1}-\hat{z}^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|\hat{v}^{k}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\|\hat{v}^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\|\hat{v}^{k+1}-\hat{v}^{k}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}).

∎

(3.16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|\hat{v}^{k}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\|\hat{v}^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\|\hat{v}^{k+1}-\hat{v}^{k}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\dfrac{1}{L}\|DJ(\hat{u}^{k+1})-DJ(u^{\ast})\|^{2}).

\displaystyle J(\hat{u}^{k+1})+R(\hat{z}^{k+1})-J(u^{\ast})-R(z^{\ast})-\langle\lambda^{\ast},\hat{u}^{k+1}-\hat{z}^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\langle DJ(\hat{u}^{k+1}),\hat{u}^{k+1}-u^{\ast}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial R(\hat{z}^{k+1}),\hat{z}^{k+1}-z^{\ast}\right\rangle-\left\langle\lambda^{\ast},\hat{u}^{k+1}-\hat{z}^{k+1}\right\rangle-\dfrac{1}{2}\|\hat{u}^{k+1}-u^{\ast}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|\hat{v}^{k}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\|\hat{v}^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\|\hat{v}^{k+1}-\hat{v}^{k}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\|\hat{u}^{k+1}-u^{\ast}\|^{2}).

Similarly, we can obtain (

).
∎

Theorem 1
.

Let
\{\hat{\omega}^{k}\}
be the sequence generated by (
3.7a
)-(
3.7c
). Under Assumption 1, there exists
0<q_{1}<1
, such that

\|\hat{v}^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}\leq q_{1}\|\hat{v}^{k}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}.

Proof.

First, from equation (
3.7a
) and the optimality of
\omega^{\ast}
, we derive

\displaystyle\|DJ(\hat{u}^{k+1})-DJ(u^{\ast})\|^{2}

(3.17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}-\lambda^{\ast}+\beta_{k}(\hat{z}^{k}-\hat{z}^{k+1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}-\lambda^{\ast}\|^{2}+\beta_{k}^{2}\|\hat{z}^{k}-\hat{z}^{k+1}\|^{2}+2\beta_{k}\langle\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}-\lambda^{\ast},\hat{z}^{k}-\hat{z}^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}-\lambda^{\ast}\|^{2}+\beta_{k}^{2}\|\hat{z}^{k}-\hat{z}^{k+1}\|^{2}-\epsilon_{1}\|\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}-\lambda^{\ast}\|^{2}-\beta_{k}^{2}{\epsilon_{1}^{-1}}\|\hat{z}^{k}-\hat{z}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\epsilon_{1})\|\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}-\lambda^{\ast}\|^{2}+\beta_{k}^{2}(1-{\epsilon_{1}^{-1}})\|\hat{z}^{k}-\hat{z}^{k+1}\|^{2},

where the last line follows from Young’s inequality with a parameter
1/(1+L/\bar{\beta})<\epsilon_{1}<1
.

Next, from the multiplier update (
3.7c
), we obtain a similar estimate for the primal variable:

\displaystyle\|\hat{u}^{k+1}-u^{\ast}\|^{2}

(3.18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\hat{u}^{k+1}-\hat{z}^{k+1}+\hat{z}^{k+1}-z^{\ast}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\epsilon_{2})\|\hat{z}^{k+1}-z^{\ast}\|^{2}+(1-{\epsilon_{2}^{-1}})\|\hat{u}^{k+1}-\hat{z}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\epsilon_{2})\|\hat{z}^{k+1}-z^{\ast}\|^{2}+{\beta_{k}^{-2}}(1-{\epsilon_{2}^{-1}})\|\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}-\hat{\lambda}^{k}\|^{2},

where
1/(1+\underline{\beta})<\epsilon_{2}<1
.

We now combine the above estimates with the inequalities from Lemma
2
. Substituting (
3.17
) into (
3.16
) gives

\displaystyle J(\hat{u}^{k+1})+R(\hat{z}^{k+1})-J(u^{\ast})-R(z^{\ast})-\langle\lambda^{\ast},\hat{u}^{k+1}-\hat{z}^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|\hat{v}^{k}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\|\hat{v}^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{v}^{k+1}-\hat{v}^{k}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\dfrac{(1-\epsilon_{1})}{2L}\|\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}-\lambda^{\ast}\|^{2}-\dfrac{\beta_{k}^{2}(1-{\epsilon_{1}^{-1}})}{2L}\|\hat{z}^{k}-\hat{z}^{k+1}\|^{2}

(3.19)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|\hat{v}^{k}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\|\hat{v}^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2})

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta_{k}^{-1}}{2}\|\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}-\hat{\lambda}^{k}\|^{2}-\dfrac{(1-\epsilon_{1})}{2L}\|\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}-\lambda^{\ast}\|^{2}+\left(\dfrac{\beta_{k}^{2}({\epsilon_{1}^{-1}}-1)}{2L}-\dfrac{\beta_{k}}{2}\right)\|\hat{z}^{k}-\hat{z}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|\hat{v}^{k}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\|\hat{v}^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2})-\dfrac{(1-\epsilon_{1})}{2L}\|\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}-\lambda^{\ast}\|^{2}.

Similarly, substituting (
3.18
) into (
3.15
) yields

\displaystyle J(\hat{u}^{k+1})+R(\hat{z}^{k+1})-J(u^{\ast})-R(z^{\ast})-\langle\lambda^{\ast},\hat{u}^{k+1}-\hat{z}^{k+1}\rangle

(3.20)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|\hat{v}^{k}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\|\hat{v}^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2})

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta_{k}}{2}\|\hat{z}^{k+1}-\hat{z}^{k}\|^{2}-\dfrac{(1-\epsilon_{2})}{2}\|\hat{z}^{k+1}-z^{\ast}\|^{2}+\left(\dfrac{({\epsilon_{2}^{-1}}-1)\beta_{k}^{-2}}{2}-\dfrac{\beta_{k}^{-1}}{2}\right)\|\hat{\lambda}^{k}-\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|\hat{v}^{k}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\|\hat{v}^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2})-\dfrac{(1-\epsilon_{2})}{2}\|\hat{z}^{k+1}-z^{\ast}\|^{2}.

Since the left-hand sides of both (
3.19
) and (
3.20
) are nonnegative by the inequality (
3.6
), we obtain two key inequalities:

\displaystyle\dfrac{(1-\epsilon_{1})}{2L}\|\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}-\lambda^{\ast}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\dfrac{\beta_{k}^{-1}}{2}\|\hat{\lambda}^{k}-\lambda^{\ast}\|^{2}-\dfrac{\beta_{k}^{-1}}{2}\|\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}-\lambda^{\ast}\|^{2}+\dfrac{\beta_{k}}{2}\|\hat{z}^{k}-z^{\ast}\|^{2}-\dfrac{\beta_{k}}{2}\|\hat{z}^{k+1}-z^{\ast}\|^{2},

(3.21)

\displaystyle\dfrac{(1-\epsilon_{2})}{2}\|\hat{z}^{k+1}-z^{\ast}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\dfrac{\beta_{k}^{-1}}{2}\|\hat{\lambda}^{k}-\lambda^{\ast}\|^{2}-\dfrac{\beta_{k}^{-1}}{2}\|\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}-\lambda^{\ast}\|^{2}+\dfrac{\beta_{k}}{2}\|\hat{z}^{k}-z^{\ast}\|^{2}-\dfrac{\beta_{k}}{2}\|\hat{z}^{k+1}-z^{\ast}\|^{2}.

(3.22)

To combine these estimates into a single contraction for the weighted norm
\|\cdot\|_{M_{k}}
, we take a convex combination of (
3.21
) and (
3.22
). Specifically, multiply (
3.21
) by
t_{k}
and (
3.22
) by
(1-t_{k})
, then add them to obtain

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{(1-\epsilon_{1})t_{k}}{2L}+\dfrac{\beta_{k}^{-1}}{2}\right)\|\hat{\lambda}^{k+1}-\lambda^{\ast}\|^{2}+\left(\dfrac{(1-\epsilon_{2})(1-t_{k})}{2}+\dfrac{\beta_{k}}{2}\right)\|\hat{z}^{k+1}-z^{\ast}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\dfrac{\beta_{k}^{-1}}{2}\|\hat{\lambda}^{k}-\lambda^{\ast}\|^{2}+\dfrac{\beta_{k}}{2}\|\hat{z}^{k}-z^{\ast}\|^{2}.

For this inequality to imply a contraction
\|\hat{v}^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}\leq q_{1,k}\|\hat{v}^{k}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}
, we require the coefficients on the left to be exactly
1/(2\beta_{k}q_{1,k}^{2})
and
\beta_{k}/(2q_{1,k}^{2})
respectively. This condition gives the system:

\left(\dfrac{(1-\epsilon_{1})t_{k}}{2L}+\dfrac{\beta_{k}^{-1}}{2}\right)\Big/{\dfrac{\beta_{k}^{-1}}{2}}=\left(\dfrac{(1-\epsilon_{2})(1-t_{k})}{2}+\dfrac{\beta_{k}}{2}\right)\Big/{\dfrac{\beta_{k}}{2}}=\dfrac{1}{q_{1,k}^{2}}.

Solving for
t_{k}
and
q_{1,k}
yields

t_{k}=\dfrac{L(1-\epsilon_{2})}{L(1-\epsilon_{2})+\beta_{k}^{2}(1-\epsilon_{1})},\quad q_{1,k}=\sqrt{1\Big/\left(1+\dfrac{(1-\epsilon_{1})(1-\epsilon_{2})\beta_{k}}{(1-\epsilon_{1})\beta_{k}^{2}+(1-\epsilon_{2})L}\right)}.

Finally, noting that
\underline{\beta}\leq\beta_{k}\leq\overline{\beta}
under Assumption 1, we define

q_{1}=\sqrt{{1}\Big/\left(1+\dfrac{(1-\epsilon_{1})(1-\epsilon_{2})\underline{\beta}}{(1-\epsilon_{1})\bar{\beta}^{2}+(1-\epsilon_{2})L}\right)}.

It follows that
0<q_{1,k}\leq q_{1}<1
for all
k
, and consequently

\|\hat{v}^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}\leq q_{1,k}\|\hat{v}^{k}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}\leq q_{1}\|\hat{v}^{k}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}},

which completes the proof.
∎

By using similar arguments for Lemma

, from the definition (

Lemma 3

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|v^{k}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\|v^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\|v^{k+1}-v^{k}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\|u^{k+1}-u^{\ast}\|^{2})

\displaystyle+\langle\sigma_{k}(u^{k+1}),u^{k+1}-u^{\ast}\rangle.

In addition to the above lemma, the following boundedness result for the sequence

Lemma 4
.

Let
\{{\omega}^{k}\}
be the sequence generated by the inexact ADMM (

)-(
2.10c
). Under Assumptions
1
and
2

Proof.

From (
2.10b
), (
2.10c
), (
3.8b
) and (
3.8c
), it follows that

\displaystyle\|v^{k+1}-\tilde{v}^{k+1}\|_{M_{k+1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta_{k+1}\|z^{k+1}-\tilde{z}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\beta_{k+1}^{-1}\|\lambda^{k+1}-\tilde{\lambda}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\beta_{k+1}\|z^{k+1}-\tilde{z}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\beta_{k+1}^{-1}\|\beta_{k}(\tilde{u}^{k+1}-\tilde{z}^{k+1})-\beta_{k}(u^{k+1}-z^{k+1})\|^{2}

Theorem 2
.

Let
\{{\omega}^{k}\}

)-(

According to Assumption 1 and the definition of

\displaystyle\|v^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|\leq

\displaystyle\|v^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\max\{\bar{\beta},\underline{\beta}^{-1}\}}\|v^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|\leq\max\{\bar{\beta},\underline{\beta}^{-1}\}C_{v},

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\beta_{k}}^{-1}(\|\lambda^{k+1}-\lambda^{\ast}\|+\|\lambda^{k}-\lambda^{\ast}\|)+\|z^{k+1}-z^{\ast}\|

\displaystyle\max\{2\underline{\beta}^{-1},1\}\sqrt{\max\{\bar{\beta},\underline{\beta}^{-1}\}}C_{v}.

For simplicity, the upper bound of the sequences
\|v^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k+1}}
,
\|v^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|
,
\|v^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}
and

is uniformly denoted by

.
Further, from (

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\|v^{k}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}-\|v^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k+1}}^{2}+\|v^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k+1}}^{2}-\|v^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{K}\|v^{k+1}-v^{k}\|_{M_{k}}^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{K}\|u^{k+1}-u^{\ast}\|^{2}\leq\|v^{0}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{0}}^{2}+C^{2}_{w}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{K}\eta_{k}\right)+2C_{w}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{K}\theta_{k}\right)<\infty,

which implies that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|u^{k+1}-u^{\ast}\|=0,\qquad\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|v^{k+1}-v^{k}\|=0.

(3.30)

From the equation (
2.10c
) and the boundedness of
\beta_{k}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|z^{k+1}-z^{\ast}\|\leq\|z^{k+1}-u^{k+1}\|+\|u^{k+1}-u^{\ast}\|\leq\underline{\beta}^{-1}\|\lambda^{k+1}-\lambda^{k}\|+\|u^{k+1}-u^{\ast}\|.

Therefore, combining the convergence properties in (
3.30
), we obtain

\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|z^{k+1}-z^{\ast}\|=0.

In addition, combining the definition of
\sigma_{k}(u)
in (
2.8
) with the equation (
2.10c
),
and applying the optimality of
\omega^{\ast}
and the property of
\sigma_{k}(u)
in (
2.9
), we have

\displaystyle\|\lambda^{k+1}-\lambda^{\ast}\|=

\displaystyle\|DJ(u^{k+1})+\beta_{k}(z^{k+1}-z^{k})-\sigma_{k}(u^{k+1})-DJ(u^{\ast})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|DJ(u^{k+1})-DJ(u^{\ast})\|+\beta_{k}\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\|+\|\sigma_{k}(u^{k+1})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\|u^{k+1}-u^{\ast}\|+\beta_{k}\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\|+\theta_{k}.

Combining the convergence properties in (
3.30
) and Assumption 2, we have

\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\lambda^{k+1}-\lambda^{\ast}\|=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

With a suitable choice of
\theta_{k}
, we obtain a linear rate of convergence for the inexact ADMM framework (
2.10a
)–(
2.10c
).

Theorem 3
.

Let
\{{\omega}^{k}\}
be the sequence generated by (
2.10a
)-(
2.10c
) with
\theta_{k}=q_{2}^{k}
, where
q_{2}\in(0,1)
is a given constant distinct from
q_{1}
. Under Assumption 1, there exist positive constants
C
and
q=\max(q_{1},q_{2})\in(0,1)
such that

\|v^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k+1}}\leq Cq^{k+1}.

Proof.

Substituting
\theta_{k}=q_{2}^{k}
into the second-to-last inequality in (
3.26
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|v^{k+1}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{k+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+\eta_{k})\right)q_{1}^{k+1}\|v^{0}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{0}}+C_{\beta}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+\eta_{k})\right)\sum_{i=0}^{k}q_{1}^{k-i}q_{2}^{i}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+\eta_{k})\right)\left(\|v^{0}-v^{\ast}\|_{M_{0}}+C_{\beta}|q_{1}-q_{2}|^{-1}\right)q^{k+1},

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2
.

Although the choice
\theta_{k}=q_{2}^{k}
guarantees linear convergence of the proposed framework, it may not be the most computationally efficient strategy in practice. There is an inherent trade-off between the inner-iteration accuracy and outer-iteration convergence speed: solving the subproblem more accurately (smaller
\theta_{k}
) yields a convergence speed closer to that of the exact algorithm, but at the cost of increased inner-loop computational effort. Consequently, the overall computation time may not be minimized. Therefore, striking an appropriate balance between inner accuracy and outer convergence speed remains an important and challenging issue. Besides the geometric decay
\theta_{k}=q_{2}^{k}
, another admissible choice satisfying Assumption 2 is the algebraic decay
\theta_{k}=1/k^{\alpha}
with
\alpha>1
. The relative effectiveness of these inexact stopping strategies is problem-dependent and requires careful consideration in implementation; see the numerical results shown in next section.

4.1
Implementation of the framework (
2.10a
)-(
2.10c
)

In this subsection, we describe in detail the implementation of the InADMM framework (
2.10a
)–(
2.10c
) and present a practical algorithm for the numerical solution of the model problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
).

Recalling the definition of
\bar{S}^{\ast}
, the evaluator
\sigma_{k}(u^{k+1})
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(u^{k+1})=((1+\beta_{k})I+\gamma_{d}\bar{S}^{\ast}\bar{S})u^{k+1}-(\beta_{k}z^{k}+\lambda^{k})+\gamma_{d}\bar{S}^{\ast}(S(0)-y_{d}),

which can be expressed as

\sigma_{k}(u^{k+1})=H^{k}u^{k+1}-d^{k},

where
H^{k}=(1+\beta_{k})I+\gamma_{d}\bar{S}^{\ast}\bar{S}
and
d^{k}=\beta_{k}z^{k}+\lambda^{k}-\gamma_{d}\bar{S}^{\ast}(S(0)-y_{d})
.
Thus we can reformulate the inexact criterion (
2.9
) as

\|H^{k}u^{k+1}-d^{k}\|\leq\theta_{k}.

It is easy to check that the operator
H^{k}\in\mathcal{L}(U,Y)
is self-adjoint and positive definite. Then for solving the
u
-subproblem, we can apply the CG method as described in Algorithm
1
.

Algorithm 1

CG for the
u
-subproblem

Input:

u^{(0)}=u^{k}
,
m=0
,
r^{(0)}=-\sigma_{k}(u^{(0)})
,
q^{(0)}=r^{(0)}
.

do

1:

Compute
u^{(m+1)}
by

u^{(m+1)}=u^{(m)}+\alpha_{m}q^{(m)},\quad\text{with}\quad\alpha_{m}=\dfrac{\langle r^{(m)},q^{(m)}\rangle}{\langle H^{k}q^{(m)},q^{(m)}\rangle},

2:

Compute
r^{(m+1)}
by

r^{(m+1)}=r^{(m)}-\alpha_{m}H^{k}q^{(m)},

3:

Compute
q^{(m+1)}
by

q^{(m+1)}=r^{(m+1)}+\rho_{m}q^{(m)}\quad\text{with}\quad\rho_{m}=\dfrac{\langle r^{(m+1)},r^{(m+1)}\rangle}{\langle r^{(m)},r^{(m)}\rangle}.

while

\|\sigma_{k}(u^{(m+1)})\|\leq\theta_{k}
.

Output

u^{k+1}=u^{(m+1)}.

In the
u
-subproblem of the framework (
2.10a
)–(
2.10c
), the operator
H^{k}
is almost unchanged throughout the iterations, with the only variation coming from the parameter
\beta_{k}
, which in turn enables an efficient execution of Algorithm 1. Now, based on the above discussion of the
u
-subproblem, we can instantiate the InADMM framework (
2.10a
)–(
2.10c
) in the following Algorithm
2
.

Algorithm 2

InADMM for the optimal control problem
(\ref{eq:obj})-(\ref{eq:control constraint})

Input:

\omega^{0}=(u^{0},z^{0},\lambda^{0})^{T}
,
\{\beta_{k}\}_{0}^{\infty}
, and
\{\theta_{k}\}_{0}^{\infty}
.

for

k\geq 0

1:

Update
u^{k+1}
by Algorithm 1 with
\theta_{k}
.

2:

Update
z^{k+1}
by

z^{k+1}=P_{\mathcal{C}}\left(S_{\frac{\gamma_{s}}{\beta_{k}}}\left(u^{k+1}-\beta^{-1}_{k}\lambda^{k}\right)\right).

3:

Update
\lambda^{k+1}
by

\lambda^{k+1}=\lambda^{k}-\beta_{k}(u^{k+1}-z^{k+1}).

end for

In practice, the value of penalty parameter
\beta_{k}
usually has an important effect on the behavior of the algorithm: values that are too large or too small tend to slow down convergence. In our numerical implementation, we adopt the strategy in
[
14
]
to adjust
\beta_{k}
as follows:

\beta_{k+1}=\begin{cases}(1+\eta_{k})\beta_{k}&\text{if}\quad\beta_{k-1}\|z^{k-1}-z^{k}\|<\frac{1}{4}\|u^{k}-z^{k}\|,\\
\frac{\beta_{k}}{1+\eta_{k}}&\text{if}\quad\beta_{k-1}\|z^{k-1}-z^{k}\|>\frac{1}{4}\|u^{k}-z^{k}\|,\\
\beta_{k}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

which ensures that the sequence
{\beta_{k}}
satisfies Assumption 1.

4.2
Experiment settings

To demonstrate the efficiency of our method, in consideration of the feasible iterative
\{z^{k}\}
, we define the state relative distance SRD and the objective value Obj as

\text{SRD}=\frac{\|S(z^{k})-y_{d}\|}{\|y_{d}\|},\quad\text{and}\quad\text{Obj}=\dfrac{\gamma_{d}}{2}\|S(z^{k})-y_{d}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|z^{k}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}+\gamma_{s}\|z^{k}\|_{L^{1}(G)},

respectively. The primal residual PR and dual residual DR are defined as

\text{PR}=\frac{\beta_{k}\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\|}{\|z^{k-1}\|},\quad\text{and}\quad\text{DR}=\frac{\|u^{k}-z^{k}\|}{\max\{\|u^{k}\|,\|z^{k}\|\}},

respectively. We let CG-Ave/Max denote the average and maximum steps of the CG method.

For all experiments, we adopt

\max\left\{\text{PR},\text{DR}\right\}\leq\text{tol}

as the stopping criterion uniformly, where
\text{tol}=10^{-4}
. In addition, the subproblem (
2.10a
) is regarded as solved “exactly” when the tolerance
\theta_{k}
falls below
10^{-6}
. The maximum number of outer iterations is set to
10^{3}
.

All numerical simulations are started from a zero initial guess. For numerical discretization, we use linear finite elements on a uniform spatial mesh of size
h
and the backward Euler scheme with temporal step size
\tau
. All experiments were performed on a desktop computer with a 13th Gen Intel Core i7-13700K 3.40 GHz processor.

Conclusions

We have proposed an inexact algorithmic framework of the alternating direction method of multipliers for solving constrained parabolic optimal distributed control problem. At each iteration, the nonsmooth part of objective and control constraint are untied from the parabolic optimal control problem, and the resulting parabolic equation constrained subproblem is solved inexactly using our efficient strategy. With the flexible parameter choices, we have established the globally strong convergence and a linear convergence rate of the proposed method. A key practical advantage of our inexact framework is that its implementation undemanding, requiring only a few internal iterations per outer step. Numerical experiments have illustrated the validness and competitive efficiency of our methods.
