Enhanced removal of tetracycline using amine-modified ZIF-11: isotherms, kinetics, and thermodynamics
Authors: Romina Roozbeh, Narjes Keramati, Mehdi Mousavi Kamazani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The impact of an amino ligand on the adsorptive capability of metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) was explored through assessment of the NH2-ZIF-11 nanostructure. This framework was utilized to adsorb tetracycline (TC) from water, and its structural and morphological integrity was verified using XRD, FTIR, FESEM, EDS, and BET. Under the optimized conditions (contact time = 60 min, adsorbent dosage = 0.5 g/L, and TC concentration = 5 ppm), the maximum tetracycline removal efficiency and adsorption capacity of NH2-ZIF-11 were 90.7% and 9.03 mg/g, respectively. The incorporation of the amine group yielded a 2.1-fold increase in adsorption capacity compared with the parent ZIF-11. The process is best described by pseudo-second-order kinetics and follows the Langmuir isotherm. The process is thermodynamically favorable: spontaneous, endothermic, and entropy-driven. The adsorbent also demonstrated robust durability, retaining its performance across three consecutive cycles. The facile, ambient synthesis of the NH2-ZIF-11 nanostructure yields a highly effective material for the remediation of TC. Its enhanced performance is a direct result of synergistic interactions of electrostatic forces, π–π stacking, and hydrogen bonds imparted by the amine functionalization. This fundamental understanding lays a critical groundwork for the practical development of MOF-based adsorbents.

Introduction

Recently, tetracycline (TC) has been classified as an emerging organic pollutant with adverse effects on living organisms1. A wide variety of techniques, including Fenton oxidation, photocatalysis, electrocoagulation, membrane systems, the electrochemical method, and ultrasonic treatment, have been employed. Meanwhile, adsorption is considered a highly effective, inexpensive, and straightforward method for its removal2. Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs), a new group of crystalline organic-inorganic porous materials composed of the coordination of organic linkers and metal ions, have been intensively investigated3,4. The main advantages of them as adsorbents include their very high specific surface area, chemical stability, and the possibility of chemical modification of the surface4. Among functional groups, amine groups form a suitable substrate for creating non-covalent bonds in the structure of MOFs5. Amine groups can be incorporated into the framework during synthesis by using amine-containing organic ligands or added after synthesis by modifying the MOF surface with amine-containing molecules6.

In other words, zeolitic imidazolate frameworks (ZIFs), a group of MOFs that are topologically similar to zeolites, are composed of tetrahedrally coordinated metal ion linkages and imidazole linkages. Ji et al. fabricated a ZnO@ZIF-8(-NH2)/cellulose nanofiber foam with a specific surface area of about 555.7 m2/g and a higher adsorption capacity of TC7. Ni et al. employed a two-solvent method involving methanol and ammonia to produce ZIF-8 as an adsorbent for dye8. Zhong et al. fabricated binary nanocomposites of metal/covalent-organic frameworks (NH2-MIL-125(Ti)@TpPa-1), which they developed as a multifunctional platform for the adsorption of radionuclides9. Zhang et al. considered the crosslinking reaction between the catechol groups in PDA and the amine groups in NH2-MIL-125(Ti). The photocatalyst showed great potential in degrading TC10. Li et al. synthesized iron-based organometallic systems (NH2-MIL-88B) with an amine functionalization to achieve detection of TC in tap water with high selectivity11.

Although ZIFs demonstrate exceptional stability under liquid conditions, MILs show instability in water. This property is essential for water treatment processes, which require the separation of organic materials from aqueous solutions. The bond between zinc and nitrogen in the imidazole ring of ZIFs exhibits better chemical stability under basic conditions than the metal-carboxylate bonds found in MILs12. Among the various types of ZIFs, ZIF-11, with forty-eight Zn2+ cations in a unit cell where these metal cations are tetrahedrally coordinated to four benzimidazole nitrogens, is one of the most common examples of ZIFs with the RHO topology13,14. ZIF-11 has larger pores than ZIF-8, which provides a specific benefit for both adsorption/catalytic applications and the extraction of large natural components. TC consists of a heavy molecule that contains multiple functional groups, including hydroxyl groups, carbonyl groups, and amino groups that attach to aromatic rings. The presence of aromatic surfaces in ZIF-11 increases π-π and van der Waals interactions with it15. Although several amine-functionalized MOFs and ZIFs, such as NH2-ZIF-8 and NH2-MIL materials, have been reported for pollutant adsorption, their practical application in aqueous systems remains limited by factors including insufficient pore size, restricted access to active sites, and poor hydrolytic stability in water. Compared with these materials, ZIF-11 possesses a distinctive RHO topology with larger pore apertures and a more hydrophobic aromatic framework, which can promote stronger π–π interactions and van der Waals attractions with tetracycline molecules. In addition, the incorporation of amine functional groups into the ZIF-11 structure provides additional active adsorption sites through hydrogen bonding and electrostatic interactions. These combined structural and chemical features make NH2-ZIF-11 a promising adsorbent for the efficient removal of tetracycline from aqueous media.

In this study, a novel amine-functionalized ZIF-11 adsorbent was synthesized and evaluated for tetracycline removal from aqueous solutions. The adsorption behavior was systematically investigated through equilibrium, kinetic, and thermodynamic analyses to elucidate the adsorption mechanism. The obtained results highlight the potential of NH2-ZIF-11 as a stable and efficient adsorbent for antibiotic-contaminated wastewater treatment and provide valuable insight into the rational design of functionalized ZIF-based materials for environmental remediation applications.

Materials and methods

The materials were obtained from Merck, including zinc acetate dihydrate (Zn (CH3CO2)2.2H2O, 99.5%), 2-aminobenzimidazole (C₇H₇N₃, 97%), methanol (CH3OH, 99.8%), toluene (C6H5CH3, 99.9%), and ammonium hydroxide (NH4OH, 30%). Furthermore, Pars Behorzan Jam Company provided the TC (C22H24N2O8, 98%).

The synthesis of ZIF‑11 followed the procedure reported in our previous work16. First, 0.12 g of benzimidazole was dissolved in 4.8 g of methanol, mixed with 1.2 g of ammonium hydroxide and 4.6 g of toluene under stirring. Then, 0.11 g of zinc acetate was added, and the resulting mixture was stirred for 3 h at room temperature. Centrifugation at 19 °C for 10 min at 10,000 rpm was the next step. The precipitate was washed three times with methanol and dried in ambient air for 12 h. Similarly, NH2-ZIF-11 was synthesized using the same procedure, except that 2-aminobenzimidazole (0.13 g) was employed as the linker (Scheme 1). In NH2-ZIF-11 synthesis, 2-aminobenzimidazole was used as the sole organic linker instead of benzimidazole, resulting in the direct incorporation of amino functional groups into the framework structure.

Results and discussion

To investigate the crystal structure of NH2-ZIF-11 and compare it with pristine ZIF-11, XRD analysis was performed (Fig. 1a). The characteristic diffraction peaks of NH2-ZIF-11 observed within the 2θ range of 3–70° appeared at 5.8, 12.3, 14.5, 17.1, 20.1, 21.5, 26.4, 28.2, and 30.2°. Compared with pristine ZIF-11, the diffraction peak intensity at 4.3° decreased significantly after amination, while new diffraction peaks emerged at approximately 10.9° and 14.4°. These changes indicate that incorporation of amino groups modifies the crystallographic arrangement and partially reduces the crystallinity of ZIF-11 without completely destroying its crystalline structure18. The observed structural changes are consistent with the SEM results and suggest the formation of a more structurally disordered framework after functionalization.

According to Fig. 1b, a peak in the 432 cm− 1 region appears when applying tensile stress to Zn-N within ZIF-11 and NH2-ZIF-11. There is also a distinct band at 750 cm− 1, corresponding to the completion of the stretching process. The peak seen at 1460 cm− 1 may arise from C-C stretching within the aromatic benzimidazole ring structure19. An adsorption band observed at 1590 cm− 1 is attributed to the stretching vibration of the C=C/C=N bonds in the imidazole ring20. The C-O bonds exhibit stretching at 1242 cm[− 116. Vibrations at 1631 cm− 1 and 916 cm− 1 increase considerably due to asymmetric aromatic C = C stretching modes and C-H bending modes beyond the plane of the ZIF-11 and NH2-ZIF-11 bond21. The C-C stretching vibrations in the benzimidazole ring produce a wavenumber of 1460 cm[− 122. Two distinct peaks were observed, which were attributed to the amine at 1571 cm− 1 and 1269 cm− 1 for the N-H rotational vibration and the C-N extension, respectively23. The characteristic peak associated with the amine group in NH2-ZIF-11 is noticeably sharper than its corresponding peak in pristine ZIF-11. The peak appears due to the merged NH2 vibrational modes of the recently incorporated ligands and O-H stretching vibrational modes18. A broad peak is present instead of scattered groups, which means that the amino groups generally enter the system at a constant distance24.

From the FESEM images, it can be seen that the amine-containing sample does not have the RHO morphology of ZIF-11 (Fig. 2a-d). NH2-ZIF-11 exhibits shell-like or plate-like particles with sizes of 300–700 nm. The surface of the amine-functionalized sample appears significantly rougher, a feature that increases the number of active sites available for pollutant adsorption, subsequently enhancing the overall adsorption performance8. The reduction in particle dimensions after amine functionalization may be attributed to partial ligand substitution during the synthesis process25. In addition, the observed morphological changes are likely associated with differences in ligand dissolution, diffusion, and coordination behavior during framework formation18. EDS analysis was performed to confirm the elemental composition of the functionalized material, showing the characteristic peaks for zinc, carbon, oxygen, and nitrogen elements (Fig. 2e). This technique primarily confirms the presence and relative abundance of expected elements, rather than detecting trace impurities. The higher nitrogen content observed in NH2-ZIF-11 compared with pristine ZIF-11 confirms the successful incorporation of amino functional groups into the framework26.

The higher nitrogen content observed in NH2-ZIF-11 compared with pristine ZIF-11 confirms the successful incorporation of amino functional groups into the framework26. Nitrogen adsorption–desorption isotherms of the NH2-ZIF-11 sample are presented in Fig. 2f. According to our previous study16, the pore size, pore volume, and specific surface area of pristine ZIF-11 were approximately 2.6 nm, 0.142 cm3/g, and 219 m2/g, respectively. After amino functionalization, these values changed to 25.6 nm, 0.34 cm3/g, and 52.7 m²/g for NH2-ZIF-11. These results indicate that the incorporation of amino groups increased the pore size and pore volume, while significantly decreasing the specific surface area.

The relatively large BJH-derived pore diameter (desorption branch) is likely associated with the formation of interparticle voids and defect-induced mesoporosity generated during the functionalization process, rather than a true expansion of the intrinsic microporous ZIF framework. This interpretation is consistent with the morphological changes observed in FESEM images and the slight variations in crystallinity observed in XRD patterns. Despite the lower specific surface area, NH2-ZIF-11 exhibited significantly enhanced tetracycline adsorption performance compared with pristine ZIF-11. This improvement can be attributed to the presence of amino functional groups, which provide additional active adsorption sites and strengthen hydrogen bonding, electrostatic interactions, and π–π interactions with tetracycline molecules26. In addition, the presence of mesoporosity can facilitate the mass transfer of TC molecules and improve the accessibility of active sites. Under the optimized conditions (contact time = 60 min, adsorbent dosage = 0.5 g/L, and TC concentration = 5 ppm), NH2-ZIF-11 exhibited a maximum tetracycline removal efficiency of 90.7% and an adsorption capacity of 9.03 mg/g, corresponding to an adsorption performance approximately 2.1-fold greater than that of pristine ZIF-11. Previous studies have similarly demonstrated that hierarchical porosity can enhance both adsorption kinetics and adsorption capacity30. Despite the lower surface area of NH2-ZIF-11 compared with pristine ZIF-11, the adsorption performance improved considerably. This enhancement can be attributed to the presence of amino functional groups, which provide additional active sites and stronger binding interactions with tetracycline molecules26.

In NH2-ZIF-11 synthesis, 2-aminobenzimidazole was used as the sole organic linker instead of benzimidazole, resulting in the direct incorporation of amino functional groups into the framework structure27.

The effect of solution pH on the efficiency of TC adsorption by NH2-ZIF-11 was investigated. The efficiency of adsorption and removal of TC at pH 5, 7, and 9 is 50.8, 90.7, and 72.6%, respectively. Considering the pHpzc= 7.2 of the adsorbent, the adsorbent surface has a positive charge at values less than this number and a negative charge at values greater than this number. The chemical structure of TC includes multiple active components, which possess a complex ring system that contains several functional groups that can undergo ionization28. The pka values of TC are 3.3, 7.7, 9.7, respectively28. Therefore, at a pH of 5, the adsorbent surface has a positive charge, and TC is present in the solution as a Zwitterion, meaning it has a net charge close to zero. The amine functional group of the adsorbent material will receive complete protonation to achieve maximum positive charge at neutral pH because of its presence on both the material surface and the internal pore spaces. At a pH of 7, the adsorbent surface has a positive charge, and TC is present in the solution as a negatively charged molecule; at a pH of 9, both the adsorbent surface and TC have a negative charge. Therefore, it is expected that at a pH of 7, due to the electrostatic attraction between the adsorbent and the pollutant, we will have the highest removal efficiency, which is what happened. However, in the comparison between pH 5 and 9, despite the electrostatic repulsion at pH 9, we have seen a better efficiency than at pH 5, because the adsorption is not limited to simple electrostatic interactions. At high pH, other factors can play a decisive role, especially in the present amine structure. The oxygen and nitrogen functional groups in TC (especially in the anionic form) can form strong hydrogen bonds with adsorbent surface groups (such as hydroxyl, amino, or carboxyl groups on the adsorbent). At alkaline pH, these groups are in a better position to form hydrogen bonds on both the adsorbent and the adsorbate2,29.

The main factors involved in TC adsorption onto an NH2-ZIF-11 adsorbent include coordinate bonds, electrostatic interactions, π-π stacking interactions, and hydrogen bonding (Fig. 3). The most powerful element that determines the outcome is electrostatic interaction. The process that attracts TC to amine-functionalized surfaces operates through solid electrostatic attraction, which develops between positive surface charges on the adsorbent and negative charges of the TC particle30.The acidic hydrogen atoms of the protonated amino group can establish connections with the TC acceptor atoms. The amino group can function as a hydrogen bond donor when a certain portion of it stays in its unprotonated state. TC contains multiple oxygen atoms, which exist in its carbonyl and hydroxyl groups as excellent hydrogen bond acceptors. Hydrogen bonds develop between the TC ring structure oxygen groups and both protonated and unprotonated N − H groups on the NH2-ZIF-11 surface, which stabilize the adsorbed molecule31. In NH2-ZIF-11, the amine group is covalently bonded to one of the carbon atoms of the benzene ring attached to the imidazole nucleus. According to FTIR spectra, the observation of the C−N peak is a direct confirmation of the presence of this covalent bond in the structure. In addition to the C − N peak, the presence of amine groups and N−H bonds is also confirmed. Therefore, the attachment of the amine functional group to ZIF-11 using the aminobenzimidazole ligand is a stable covalent bond in which the amine group is attached to the carbon skeleton of the ligand. This functionalized ligand then formed the ZIF network during the synthesis process.

The TC particle uses its π orbitals to interact with the π orbitals that exist in the fragrant rings of the ZIF ligand when it comes closer to the adsorbent surface. The intelligent systems achieve their purpose by maintaining atomic stability within larger pore spaces and at exterior surface locations. Van der Waals forces in all intermolecular interactions are especially vital in adsorption at low concentrations and in nonpolar situations. At extremely close distances between two particles that reach their ideal electrostatic and hydrogen bonding configuration, the weak transitory forces produce these binding strengths, which lead to the final adsorption process and stabilization30.

The Langmuir isotherm can describe the adsorption isotherm better than others, according to the R2 coefficient32. It describes the monolayer adsorption process, and that further adsorption is not possible after this layer is occupied. On the other hand, the Freundlich isotherm assumes that the adsorbent surface is heterogeneous and supports adsorption in multiple layer2. Previous researchers have also reported the fitting of the Langmuir isotherm model for TC removal32,33. In cases when the adsorption coefficient, either KL or RL, is between zero and one, it suggests that adsorption processes shall be advantageous and preferential34. For comparison, the adsorption isotherm and kinetics were also investigated with the original sample. The adsorption behavior of ZIF-11 also fits well with the Langmuir isotherm model. This indicates a monolayer coverage and a chemisorption process. The parameters of this model are RL, qm, and KL, which were determined to be 4.25 mg/g, 0.72, and 0.075 L/mg, respectively. According to Table 1, the functionalized sample has a higher maximum monolayer adsorption capacity than the plain ZIF-11. This means that either it has more active sites that are actually useful, or its surface is simply better tuned for monolayer adsorption. The parameter KL represents the affinity/strength of the binding between the adsorbent and the adsorbate. A larger KL means a stronger adhesion. And when the two samples were compared side by side, the difference was clearly seen.

Figure 5 and Table 2 show the results of fitting pseudo-first-order, pseudo-second-order, Elovich, and intraparticle diffusion models using NH2-ZIF-11. The pseudo-second-order kinetic model had higher correlation coefficients. The initial TC concentration affects the adsorption characteristic of NH2-ZIF-11, under a pseudo-second-order kinetic model, which uses chemisorption as the rate-determining factor and the initial rate-controlling step35. The pseudo-second-order model expects that adsorption occurs at a specific rate, which depends on two factors: the availability of active adsorption sites and the way adsorbate particles interact with the adsorbent surface. The demonstration shows that chemisorption functions as the main rate-limiting process, which involves strong interactions between hydrogen and other chemical bonds, including π–π interactions and electrostatic forces, weak covalent bonds, electron transfer, and surface chemical reactions. The adsorption rate constant determines the adsorption rate because higher values result in faster contaminant adsorption. Researchers have frequently reported the simultaneous observation of Langmuir isotherm behavior together with pseudo-second-order energy during TC adsorption experiments using ZIF36. Zhang et al. synthesized an alginate-encapsulated ZIF-67 composite gel for TC adsorption. Batch experiments demonstrated TC adsorption, following pseudo-second-order kinetics and the Langmuir isotherm, indicating chemisorption-driven monolayer adsorption via π-π interactions, hydrogen bonding, and coordination with unsaturated Co²⁺ sites36.

On the other hand, the adsorption of TC onto ZIF-11 follows Elovich kinetics. This is another indication that the process is chemically controlled. From the model fit, α = 0.13 and β = 0.93 were obtained. This low α value for ZIF-11 indicates that at the very beginning, the adsorption rate is very slow or the surface reaction starts slowly.

The thermodynamic data in Table 3 show that at all three temperatures, ΔG0 is negative. The positive change in entropy suggests an increase in disorder at the interface during the adsorption process. These results correlate well with the significant increase in TC adsorption capacity at elevated temperatures37. The positive ΔH0 value indicates the endothermic nature of the adsorption phenomenon. Since the assigned ΔH0 value is quite high (> 40 kJ/mol), this strengthens and supports the view of chemisorption as being the principal adsorption method33.

The stability of the synthesized sample without regeneration between cycles was investigated over three consecutive cycles (Fig. 6a). The minimal reduction in TC adsorption efficiency throughout these cycles confirms the excellent reusability and robust nature of the synthesized adsorbent. Part of the efficiency loss could be due to physical loss of the adsorbent during collection, and the other part could be due to the occupation of the active adsorption sites of the sample, because no regeneration operation was performed between cycles. FTIR analysis was employed to investigate the structural stability and possible interactions between NH2-ZIF-11 and tetracycline after adsorption (Fig. 6b). According to the FTIR results, a decrease in the intensity of the broad band observed in the 3200–3600 cm−1 region was detected after adsorption. This change is attributed to the involvement of hydroxyl/amino functional groups in weak hydrogen-bonding interactions with tetracycline molecules38. Importantly, the main characteristic absorption bands of the framework remained observable after adsorption, suggesting that the overall chemical structure of NH2-ZIF-11 was largely preserved during the adsorption process.

These findings indicate acceptable structural stability of the synthesized NH2-ZIF-11 after adsorption and suggest its potential applicability in repeated adsorption processes, which is advantageous for practical wastewater treatment applications. Finally, NH2-ZIF-11 achieved a maximum TC removal efficiency of 90.7% within only 60 min, demonstrating competitive adsorption performance compared with previously reported ZIF-based adsorbents (Table 4). Although the adsorption experiments were conducted at a relatively low initial TC concentration (5 mg/L), the obtained adsorption capacity remained consistent with values reported for comparable ZIF-derived materials. The enhanced adsorption behavior can be attributed to the synergistic effects of amine functionalization, hierarchical porosity, and improved accessibility of active adsorption sites.

Conclusions

Surface modification of ZIF-11 with amino groups was performed. To better investigate the effect of the amine ligand, the performance of NH2-ZIF-11 was compared with that of the parent ZIF-11. The structural and morphological characteristics of the synthesized material were verified using XRD, FTIR, FESEM, EDS, and BET analysis. NH2-ZIF-11 achieved a TC removal efficiency of 90.7%, approximately twice that of pristine ZIF-11. The adsorption mechanism involves H-bonding, electrostatic, and π-π interactions. Amino groups are crucial for TC binding, significantly enhancing the number of active adsorption sites. Furthermore, TC adsorption is best described by the Langmuir isotherm and pseudo-second-order kinetic model. Thermodynamic parameter calculations confirmed that the TC adsorption process is spontaneous, endothermic, and entropy-driven. The produced nanostructure also showed acceptable reusability and recycling after three consecutive cycles of operation. This study confirms that the incorporation of functional groups is an effective strategy to enhance the adsorption performance of ZIF-11 material.
