Non-existence of abelian maximal subgroups in cyclic division algebras
Huynh Viet Khanh

Abstract
. We prove that no cyclic division algebra (in the sense of Dickson) admits an abelian maximal subgroup in its multiplicative group. This settles a special case of a long-standing conjecture of Akbari–Mahdavi-Hezavehi–Mahmudi and complements earlier results on locally nilpotent maximal subgroups and provides a new malnormality criterion for maximal subgroups..



1.
Introduction

The existence of maximal subgroups in the multiplicative group
D^{*}=D\setminus\{0\}
of a division ring
D
is a notoriously difficult and still largely unsettled problem. While the general question remains open, important progress has been made in special cases, most notably by Hazrat and Wadsworth
[
6
]
, who obtained deep necessary conditions for the existence of maximal subgroups.

Maximal subgroups of
D^{*}
with additional algebraic structure — particularly those that are abelian, nilpotent, solvable, or locally nilpotent — have attracted considerable attention. A natural line of research is to investigate whether such subgroups can reflect, in some sense, the non-commutative structure of
D
itself. In this spirit, Akbari, Mahdavi-Hezavehi, and Mahmudi
[
1
]
formulated the following conjectures:

Conjecture 1
.

If
D
is a non-commutative division ring, then
D^{*}
contains no nilpotent maximal subgroups.

Conjecture 2
.

If
D
is a non-commutative division ring, then
D^{*}
contains no solvable maximal subgroups.

Conjecture 2 was disproved by Akbari et al.
[
2
]
, who exhibited a non-abelian solvable maximal subgroup in the multiplicative group of the real quaternions
\mathbb{H}
. The status of Conjecture 1 (and its natural generalization to locally nilpotent subgroups) has remained open. Ebrahimian
[
4
]
showed that every nilpotent maximal subgroup of
D^{*}
, if it exists, must be abelian. Locally nilpotent maximal subgroups were further studied by Hai
[
5
]
and Hai-Khanh
[
7
]
who ruled them out in many cases.

In this note we prove that abelian maximal subgroups do not exist in the important class of cyclic division algebras. This provides an affirmative answer to Conjecture 1 for all cyclic division algebras and constitutes a substantial step toward the general conjecture. Our approach relies on demonstrating that an abelian maximal subgroup would induce a malnormal maximal subfield, which we then show is incompatible with the automorphisms guaranteed by the Skolem-Noether theorem in cyclic algebras.

We recall the classical definition due to Dickson (see Lam
[
8
]
, §14).

Definition 1
.

Let
L/F
be a cyclic Galois extension of degree
n
with Galois group
\langle\sigma\rangle
, and let
a\in F^{*}
. The
cyclic algebra
associated to these data is the
F
-algebra

D=(L/F,\sigma,a):=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}Lx^{i},

with multiplication determined by the rules

x^{n}=a,\qquad xb=\sigma(b)x\quad\text{for all }b\in L.

When
D
is a division ring, we call
D
a
cyclic division algebra
.

In this note we always assume
D
is a division ring. The main result of the note is the following.

2.
Preliminary results

Lemma 1
.

Let
D
be a non-commutative division ring with centre
F=Z(D)
.
If
M
is an abelian maximal subgroup of
D^{*}
, then
K=M\cup\{0\}

is a maximal subfield of
D
.

Proof.

Let
K
be the sub-division ring of
D
generated by
F\cup M
. Since
M
is abelian and commutes elementwise with
F
, every pair of elements
of
K
commutes. Thus
K
is commutative. A commutative sub-division ring
of a division ring is necessarily a field, so
K
is a subfield of
D
. Clearly
M\subseteq K^{*}\subseteq D^{*}
and
K^{*}
is a subgroup of
D^{*}
.
By the maximality of
M
in
D^{*}
, either
M=K^{*}
or
K^{*}=D^{*}
. The second possibility cannot hold:
K^{*}=D^{*}
would imply
K=D
,
contradicting that
D
is non-commutative. Hence
M=K^{*}
, so
K=M\cup\{0\}
.

It remains to show that
K
is maximal among subfields of
D
. Suppose there exists a subfield
L
of
D
such that
K\subsetneq L\subsetneq D
.
Then
L^{*}
is a subgroup of
D^{*}
satisfying

M=K^{*}\subsetneq L^{*}\subsetneq D^{*},

which contradicts the maximality of
M
. Therefore no such
L
exists,
and
K
is a maximal subfield of
D
.
∎

Theorem 1
.

Let
D
be a non-commutative division ring with centre
F
.
If
A
is an abelian maximal subgroup of
D^{*}
, then
A
is self-normalizing
in
D^{*}
and the image
A/F^{*}
is malnormal in
D^{*}/F^{*}
.
Consequently,
K:=A\cup\{0\}
is a maximal subfield of
D
satisfying

K\cap x^{-1}Kx=F\qquad\text{for all }x\in D\setminus K.

Proof.

Since
A\subseteq N_{D^{*}}(A)\leq D^{*}
, either
N_{D^{*}}(A)=D^{*}
or
A=N_{D^{*}}(A)
.

Suppose first that
N_{D^{*}}(A)=D^{*}
. Then
A
is normal in
D^{*}
.
Since
A
is maximal,
D^{*}/A
is cyclic of prime order, hence abelian,
so the derived subgroup
D^{\prime}
is contained in
A
. It follows that
D^{*}
is a solvable group. This forces
D
to be commutative (see
[
8
, Corollary 13.21]
), a contradiction.

Thus
A=N_{D^{*}}(A)
. Now let
x\in D^{*}\setminus A
.
Clearly
F^{*}\subseteq A\cap A^{x}
. Since
x\notin N_{D^{*}}(A)
,
we have
A^{x}\neq A
, so there exists an element of
A^{x}
outside
A
.
Hence
A\subsetneq A\cup A^{x}
.

Both
A
and
A^{x}
are abelian, so every element of
A\cup A^{x}

commutes with every element of
A\cap A^{x}
. Therefore

A\cup A^{x}\subseteq C_{D^{*}}(A\cap A^{x})\subseteq D^{*}.

The proper containment
A\subsetneq A\cup A^{x}
together with the maximality
of
A
forces
C_{D^{*}}(A\cap A^{x})=D^{*}
, and therefore

A\cap A^{x}\subseteq Z(D^{*})=F^{*}
. Combined with the obvious inclusion

F^{*}\subseteq A\cap A^{x}
, we obtain

A\cap A^{x}=F^{*}\qquad\text{for all }x\in D^{*}\setminus A.

This means precisely that the image of
A
in the quotient
D^{*}/F^{*}

is malnormal.

The field-theoretic statement now follows immediately from
Lemma
1
:
K=A\cup\{0\}
is a maximal subfield,
and the malnormality condition translates directly into

K\cap x^{-1}Kx=F
for all
x\in D\setminus K
.
∎

3.
Main theorem

Theorem 2
.

Let
D
be a cyclic division algebra over its centre
F
. Then
D^{*}
contains no abelian maximal subgroup.

Proof.

Assume for contradiction that
A
is an abelian maximal subgroup of
D^{*}
.
By Lemma
1
,
K=A\cup\{0\}
is a maximal subfield of
D
.
By Theorem
1
,

K\cap y^{-1}Ky=F\qquad\text{for all }y\in D\setminus K.

Write
D=(L/F,\sigma,a)
for a cyclic presentation of
D
, where
L/F
is cyclic Galois of degree
n=[D:F]^{1/2}>1
and
x\in D^{*}
satisfies
x^{n}=a\in F
and
x^{-1}bx=\sigma(b)
for all
b\in L
. Then
L
is a maximal subfield of
D
and is normalized by
x
:

x\notin L,\qquad x^{-1}Lx=\sigma(L)=L.

By the Skolem–Noether theorem (see
[
3
, page 39]
), there exists
g\in D^{*}
such that

g^{-1}Lg=K.

Set
y=g^{-1}xg
. We first show that
y\notin K
. Suppose otherwise. Then

x=gyg^{-1}\in gKg^{-1}=L,

contradicting
x\notin L
. (Equivalently,
y\in K
would imply
x\in C_{D}(L)
, but
C_{D}(L)=L
in a cyclic algebra.)

Now compute the conjugate:

y^{-1}Ky=g^{-1}(x^{-1}Lx)g=g^{-1}\sigma(L)g=g^{-1}Lg=K.

Hence

K\cap y^{-1}Ky=K\supsetneq F,

which contradicts the malnormality property of
K
.

Therefore
D^{*}
contains no abelian maximal subgroup.
∎

As immediate consequences of Theorem
2
, we obtain the following two results that settle Conjecture 1 in important special cases.

Corollary 1
.

Let
D
be a division algebra of prime degree over its centre
F
. Then
D^{*}
contains no abelian maximal subgroup.

Proof.

By Albert’s theorem (see e.g.
[
8
]
), every division algebra of prime degree is cyclic. The result now follows immediately from Theorem
2
.
∎

Corollary 2
.

Let
D
be a cyclic division algebra over its centre
F
. Then
D^{*}
contains no locally nilpotent maximal subgroup.

Proof.

Suppose
M
is a nilpotent maximal subgroup of
D^{*}
. By Khanh-Hai’s theorem (
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
), any nilpotent maximal subgroup must be abelian. This contradicts Theorem
2
.
∎

As a direct generalization of Theorem
2
, we obtain the following stronger result, which applies to the significantly larger class of division algebras that admit at least one
cyclic maximal subfield

L
(that is, a maximal subfield
L
such that the extension
L/F
is cyclic Galois).

Corollary 3
.

Let
D
be a non-commutative division algebra that admits at least one cyclic maximal subfield
L
. Then
D^{*}
contains no abelian maximal subgroup.

4.
Division algebras with cyclic maximal subfields

To demonstrate that the class of division algebras that admit at least one cyclic maximal subfield is strictly larger than the class of cyclic division algebras themselves, we will present an example in this section. Before proceeding, let us make some preliminary remarks on quaternion algebras and their tensor products.

A quaternion algebra over a field
F
(with
\operatorname{char}F\neq 2
) is a central simple
F
-algebra of degree
4
, presented in the form

(a,b)_{F}=F\oplus Fi\oplus Fj\oplus Fk,\quad i^{2}=a,\ j^{2}=b,\ k=ij=-ji

with
a,b\in F^{*}
(Draxl
[
3
]
, §14, p. 103). Such an algebra is a division algebra if and only if it is not split, i.e., its Brauer class
[(a,b)_{F}]
has order exactly
2
in the Brauer group
\operatorname{Br}(F)
. Equivalently, by the explicit criterion recorded on pages 103–104 of Draxl,
(a,b)_{F}
is a division algebra if and only if the Hilbert symbol
(a,b)_{F}=-1
.

To obtain two quaternion division algebras
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
, it therefore suffices to choose pairs
(a,b)
and
(c,d)
in
F^{*}
such that

(a,b)_{F}=-1\quad\text{and}\quad(c,d)_{F}=-1.

Their tensor product
D=Q_{1}\otimes_{F}Q_{2}
is a central simple algebra of degree
4
over
F
(i.e.,
[D:F]=16
). In the Brauer group, the class of the tensor product satisfies

[D]=[Q_{1}]+[Q_{2}]

in the
2
-torsion subgroup
\operatorname{Br}(F)[2]
, which carries a natural structure of an
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-vector space (Draxl
[
3
]
, §9, pp. 59–70 and §11, pp. 77–92). By the fundamental fact recorded on page 104 of Draxl,
D
is itself a division algebra of degree
4
if and only if the classes
[Q_{1}]
and
[Q_{2}]
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
in
\operatorname{Br}(F)[2]
. In other words, neither class is zero (so both
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
are division algebras) and their sum
[Q_{1}]+[Q_{2}]\neq 0
.

A concrete choice that satisfies these conditions can be made over the rational field
\mathbb{Q}
. Take

Q_{1}=(-1,-1)_{\mathbb{Q}},\qquad Q_{2}=(-1,-7)_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Both are division algebras because their Hilbert symbols equal
-1
(Draxl
[
3
]
, §14, Example on p. 103). Their Brauer classes are linearly independent in
\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb{Q})[2]
since the algebras are ramified at different places (one is ramified at the prime
7
, the other is not). Consequently, the tensor product

D=(-1,-1)_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}(-1,-7)_{\mathbb{Q}}

is a biquaternion division algebra of degree
4
over
\mathbb{Q}
.

We now show that this
D
is not cyclic, yet still admits a cyclic maximal subfield. This demonstrates that the class of division algebras possessing at least one cyclic maximal subfield is strictly larger than the class of cyclic division algebras.

Example 1
.

Let
F
be a number field. Choose two quaternion division algebras over
F
, say

Q_{1}=(a,b)_{F}\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{2}=(c,d)_{F},

where
a,b,c,d\in F^{*}
are chosen so that the Brauer classes
[Q_{1}]
and
[Q_{2}]
are linearly independent in the Brauer group
\operatorname{Br}(F)
. Their tensor product

D=Q_{1}\otimes_{F}Q_{2}

is then a central simple algebra of degree
4
over
F
.

In general, such a biquaternion division algebra
D
is
not
cyclic. That is, there does not exist a cyclic Galois extension
L/F
of degree
4
and an element
\lambda\in F^{*}
such that
D\cong(L/F,\sigma,\lambda)
. Nevertheless,
D
always admits a cyclic Galois extension
L/F
of degree
4
as a maximal subfield. This follows from the Skolem–Noether theorem, which guarantees that any two maximal subfields are conjugate, combined with the fact that every central simple algebra of degree
4
over a number field splits over some cyclic extension of degree
4
.

Remark 1
.

Cyclic division algebras constitute an exceptionally broad and fundamental class within the theory of division rings. By Albert’s theorem, every division algebra of prime degree
p
is cyclic (of degree
p
). All quaternion algebras (including the classical real quaternions
\mathbb{H}
) are cyclic of degree
2
. Over local fields (and, more generally, over Henselian fields), every finite-dimensional division algebra is cyclic. Moreover, the explicit constructions of Dickson, Brauer, and others—such as the degree-
9
example over
\mathbb{Q}
or the infinite-family examples via indeterminate extensions—are all cyclic.

Consequently, the non-existence of abelian maximal subgroups in every cyclic division algebra (Theorem
2
) rules out such subgroups across all these large families of division rings simultaneously, thereby providing a substantial and unifying contribution toward the general conjecture of Akbari–Mahdavi-Hezavehi–Mahmudi that no non-commutative division ring admits an abelian maximal subgroup in its multiplicative group.
