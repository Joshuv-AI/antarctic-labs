2
Positive-interval preliminaries

For integers
1\leq a\leq b
, let

S(a,b)=\left|\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}):x_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq x_{3},\ x_{1}+x_{2}<x_{3},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in[a,b]\right\}\right|.

If
a>b
, we set
S(a,b)=0
by convention. Thus
S(a,b)
counts all solutions to
\mathcal{E}
in the interval
[a,b]
, without reference to any coloring.

We now show the exact value of
S(a,b)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq a\leq b
. Then

S(a,b)=\sum_{i=a}^{\left\lfloor(b-1)/2\right\rfloor}\binom{b-2i+1}{2}.

(2.1)

Equivalently,

S(a,b)=\begin{cases}0,&b-2a\leq 0,\\[5.69054pt]
\dfrac{u(u+1)(4u+5)}{6},&b-2a=2u\geq 2,\\[8.53581pt]
\dfrac{(u+1)(u+2)(4u+3)}{6},&b-2a=2u+1\geq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Fix
x_{1}=i\in[a,b]
. Since
x_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq x_{3}
and
x_{1}+x_{2}<x_{3}\leq b
, we must have

i\leq x_{2}\leq b-i-1
.
Hence
i\leq\left\lfloor(b-1)/2\right\rfloor
is necessary. Conversely, for each such
i
and each

j\in[i,b-i-1]
,
the variable
x_{3}
must satisfy
x_{3}>i+j
and
x_{3}\leq b
, so the possible values of
x_{3}
are exactly

i+j+1,i+j+2,\dots,b
,
which gives precisely
b-(i+j)
choices.

Therefore, for fixed
i
, the number of pairs
(x_{2},x_{3})
is

\sum_{j=i}^{b-i-1}(b-i-j)=1+2+\cdots+(b-2i)=\binom{b-2i+1}{2}.

Summing over all admissible
i
yields (
2.1
).

To obtain the closed form, write
i=a+r
. Then

b-2i=d-2r
.
If
b-2a\leq 0
, then there are no admissible values of
i
, so
S(a,b)=0
.

Assume
b-2a\geq 1
. If
b-2a=2u
, then
r=0,1,\dots,u-1
, and therefore

S(a,b)=\sum_{r=0}^{u-1}\binom{2u-2r+1}{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{u}\binom{2j+1}{2}=\frac{u(u+1)(4u+5)}{6}.

If
b-2a=2u+1
, then
r=0,1,\dots,u
, so

S(a,b)=\sum_{r=0}^{u}\binom{2u-2r+2}{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{u}\binom{2j+2}{2}=\frac{(u+1)(u+2)(4u+3)}{6}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3
The purely nonpositive case

We first treat the range
1\leq n\leq t
, where the interval
[1-t,n-t]
contains no positive integer.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
t\geq 2
and
1\leq n\leq t
. Then

M_{-t}(n)=\begin{cases}\dbinom{\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor+2}{3}+\dbinom{\left\lceil n/2\right\rceil+2}{3},&1\leq n<t,\\[5.69054pt]
\dbinom{\left\lfloor t/2\right\rfloor+2}{3}+\dbinom{\left\lceil t/2\right\rceil+2}{3}-1,&n=t.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
J=[1-t,n-t]
.
Because
n\leq t
, every element of
J
is nonpositive.

Consider a triple
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
with

x_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq x_{3}
and
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in J
.
If
x_{1}<0
, then

x_{1}+x_{2}\leq x_{2}-1<x_{2}\leq x_{3}
,
so
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
automatically satisfies
\mathcal{E}
. If
x_{1}=0
, then necessarily
n=t
and

0=x_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq x_{3}\leq 0
,
which forces
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=(0,0,0)
. This triple is not a solution to
\mathcal{E}
, because
0+0<0
is false.

Thus every nondecreasing triple in
J
is a solution, except for the single triple
(0,0,0)
when
n=t
.
Now fix a
2
-coloring
\Delta:J\to\{0,1\}
and let

m=\left\lvert\Delta^{-1}(0)\right\rvert
.
Then color
1
appears exactly
n-m
times. The number of nondecreasing monochromatic triples chosen from a color class of size
m
is the number of multisets of size
3
drawn from
m
ordered elements, namely
\binom{m+2}{3}
. Therefore

s(\Delta;1-t,n-t)=\binom{m+2}{3}+\binom{n-m+2}{3}-\delta_{n,t},

where
\delta_{n,t}=1
if
n=t
and
\delta_{n,t}=0
otherwise. Consequently,

M_{-t}(n)=\min_{0\leq m\leq n}\left(\binom{m+2}{3}+\binom{n-m+2}{3}\right)-\delta_{n,t}.

It remains to minimize the symmetric function

g_{n}(m)=\binom{m+2}{3}+\binom{n-m+2}{3}.

A direct calculation gives

g_{n}(m+1)-g_{n}(m)=\binom{m+2}{2}-\binom{n-m+1}{2}.

If
m<n/2
, then
m+2\leq n-m+1
, so the right-hand side is nonpositive. If
m>n/2
, the right-hand side is nonnegative. Hence
g_{n}(m)
decreases up to the midpoint and increases afterwards, and its minimum is attained when
m
is as close to
n/2
as possible, namely at

m\in\left\{\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor,\left\lceil n/2\right\rceil\right\}
.
Substituting these values yields the stated formulas.
∎

4
The case involving positive integers

Throughout this section we fix

t\geq 2,n\geq t+1,k=-t
, and
N=n-t\geq 1
.
Let
[1-t,n-t]=Q_{t}\sqcup P_{N}
,
where
Q_{t}=[1-t,0]
and
P_{N}=[1,N]
.
We further split the nonpositive part into

Q_{t}=R_{t}\sqcup\{0\}
, where
R_{t}=[1-t,-1]
.
Thus
R_{t}
is the negative block and
Q_{t}
is the nonpositive block.

Let
\Delta:[1-t,n-t]\to\{0,1\}
be any
2
-coloring of
[1-t,n-t]
. We define the following parameters associated with
\Delta
.

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\Delta^{-1}(0)\cap R_{t}\right\rvert,\qquad q=\left\lvert\Delta^{-1}(0)\cap P_{N}\right\rvert,\qquad\varepsilon=\begin{cases}1,&\Delta(0)=0,\\
0,&\Delta(0)=1.\end{cases}

Thus
a
is the number of negative integers of color
0
,
q
is the number of positive integers of color
0
, and the parameter
\varepsilon
records the color of
0
. The ranges are

0\leq a\leq t-1,\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}
, and
0\leq q\leq N
.

The following lemma describes the symmetry under exchanging the two colors, and shows that it suffices to consider colorings with
q\leq N/2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\overline{\Delta}=1-\Delta
, then
\Delta
and
\overline{\Delta}
have the same number of monochromatic solutions to
\mathcal{E}
. Moreover, if
\Delta
has parameters
(a,\varepsilon,q)
, then
\overline{\Delta}
has parameters

(t-1-a,\ 1-\varepsilon,\ N-q).

Consequently, in minimizing the number of monochromatic solutions we may restrict attention to colorings satisfying
q\leq\frac{N}{2}
.

Proof.

A triple is monochromatic under
\Delta
if and only if it is monochromatic under
\overline{\Delta}
, because passing from
\Delta
to
\overline{\Delta}
merely interchanges the two colors. The parameter transformation follows directly from the definitions.

If
q>N/2
, then
\overline{\Delta}
has
N-q<N/2
positive integers of color
0
and yields the same monochromatic solution count as
\Delta
. Therefore, when minimizing, it is enough to consider colorings with
q\leq N/2
.
∎

Because every element of
Q_{t}
is nonpositive and every element of
P_{N}
is positive, the monotonicity condition
x_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq x_{3}
forces every solution to belong to exactly one of the following four types:

PPP,\qquad QQQ,\qquad QQP,\qquad QPP.

Here, for example,
QQP
means that
x_{1},x_{2}\in Q_{t}
and
x_{3}\in P_{N}
. We now compute the contributions of the last three types exactly.
Let
N_{PPP}
,
N_{QQQ}
,
N_{QQP}
, and
N_{QPP}
denote the numbers of monochromatic solutions of the corresponding types, respectively.
We now compute
N_{QQQ}
,
N_{QQP}
, and
N_{QPP}
exactly.

We write

s_{0}=a+\varepsilon
and

s_{1}=t-s_{0}=t-a-\varepsilon
.
Hence
s_{0}
and
s_{1}
are the numbers of nonpositive integers of colors
0
and
1
, respectively.

Lemma 4.2
.

For every coloring
\Delta
with parameters
(a,\varepsilon,q)
,

N_{QQQ}=\binom{s_{0}+2}{3}+\binom{s_{1}+2}{3}-1.

Proof.

All three variables lie in
Q_{t}=[1-t,0]
. As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, every nondecreasing triple in
Q_{t}
satisfies
\mathcal{E}
, except for
(0,0,0)
.

The part of
Q_{t}
with color
0
contains
s_{0}
elements, so it contributes
\binom{s_{0}+2}{3}
nondecreasing monochromatic triples. Similarly, the part with color
1
contributes
\binom{s_{1}+2}{3}
such triples. Exactly one of the two colors contains
0
, so the exceptional triple
(0,0,0)
has been counted once and must be removed. This yields the formula.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For every coloring
\Delta
with parameters
(a,\varepsilon,q)
,

N_{QQP}=\binom{s_{0}+1}{2}\,q+\binom{s_{1}+1}{2}\,(N-q).

Proof.

Fix a color
c\in\{0,1\}
. The number of nondecreasing pairs
(u,v)
of color
c
inside
Q_{t}
equals
\binom{s_{c}+1}{2}
, because choosing a nondecreasing pair from
s_{c}
ordered values is the same as choosing a multiset of size
2
.
Let
x
be any positive integer of the same color
c
. Since
u,v\in Q_{t}
, we have

u\leq v\leq 0<x
,
and therefore

u+v\leq 0<x
.
Thus every triple
(u,v,x)
is a solution to
\mathcal{E}
.
For color
0
, there are
q
choices for
x
and
\binom{s_{0}+1}{2}
choices for
(u,v)
. For color
1
, there are
N-q
choices for
x
and
\binom{s_{1}+1}{2}
choices for
(u,v)
. Summing the two colors proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For every coloring
\Delta
with parameters
(a,\varepsilon,q)
,

N_{QPP}=a\binom{q+1}{2}+(t-1-a)\binom{N-q+1}{2}+\varepsilon\binom{q}{2}+(1-\varepsilon)\binom{N-q}{2}.

Proof.

We separate the negative first coordinates from the first coordinate equal to
0
.

Let
x_{1}<0
be of color
0
. For any pair
(x_{2},x_{3})
with color
0
satisfying

x_{2}\leq x_{3},x_{2},x_{3}\in P_{N}
,
we have

x_{1}+x_{2}<x_{2}\leq x_{3}
,
so
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
is a solution to
\mathcal{E}
. The number of nondecreasing pairs with color
0
in
P_{N}
is
\binom{q+1}{2}
. Since there are exactly
a
negative integers of color
0
, the total contribution from negative first coordinates with color
0
is

a\binom{q+1}{2}
.
Similarly, the contribution from negative first coordinates with color
1
is

(t-1-a)\binom{N-q+1}{2}
.

Then, let
x_{1}=0
.
If
\varepsilon=1
, then
0
has color
0
. A triple
(0,x_{2},x_{3})
with
x_{2},x_{3}\in P_{N}
and both of color
0
is a solution to
\mathcal{E}
if and only if

0+x_{2}<x_{3}
,
that is, if and only if
x_{2}<x_{3}
. The number of strictly increasing ordered pairs chosen from
q
positive integers is
\binom{q}{2}
. Thus the contribution from
0
is
\varepsilon\binom{q}{2}
.
Likewise, if
0
has color
1
, then the contribution is
(1-\varepsilon)\binom{N-q}{2}
.

Adding the two parts completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
t\geq 2
, let
n\geq t+1
, and put
N=n-t
. Then

M_{-t}(n)=\min_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}}\ \min_{0\leq a\leq t-1}\ \min_{0\leq q\leq\left\lfloor N/2\right\rfloor}G_{t,N,a,\varepsilon}(q),

where

\displaystyle G_{t,N,a,\varepsilon}(q)={}

\displaystyle S(1,q)+S(q+1,N)

\displaystyle+\binom{a+\varepsilon+1}{2}\,q+\binom{t-a-\varepsilon+1}{2}\,(N-q)

\displaystyle+a\binom{q+1}{2}+(t-1-a)\binom{N-q+1}{2}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\binom{q}{2}+(1-\varepsilon)\binom{N-q}{2}

\displaystyle+\binom{a+\varepsilon+2}{3}+\binom{t-a-\varepsilon+2}{3}-1.

Moreover, for every fixed triple
(a,\varepsilon,q)

0\leq q\leq N/2

[1,q]\text{ in color }0,\qquad[q+1,N]\text{ in color }1.

By Lemma

. Fix such a coloring
\Delta

(a,\varepsilon,q)
be its parameters.
We decompose the monochromatic solutions counted by

into the four types

,

,
QQP
, and
QPP
.

We now consider Type

.
All three variables lie in the positive interval
P_{N}=[1,N]
. Inside

, the coloring
\Delta
has exactly

0
and

. Since

, Theorem

N_{PPP}\geq S(1,q)+S(q+1,N)
.

Equality holds exactly when the restriction of
\Delta
to

is the two-block coloring

[1,q]\text{ in color }0,\qquad[q+1,N]\text{ in color }1.

Then we consider Types

,

, and

.
By Lemmas

,
4.3
, and

, these three contributions are

\displaystyle N_{QQQ}

\displaystyle=\binom{s_{0}+2}{3}+\binom{s_{1}+2}{3}-1,

\displaystyle N_{QQP}

\displaystyle=\binom{s_{0}+1}{2}\,q+\binom{s_{1}+1}{2}\,(N-q),

\displaystyle N_{QPP}

\displaystyle=a\binom{q+1}{2}+(t-1-a)\binom{N-q+1}{2}+\varepsilon\binom{q}{2}+(1-\varepsilon)\binom{N-q}{2},

Adding the four types gives

Since this is true for every optimal coloring with
q\leq N/2
, we obtain the lower bound

M_{-t}(n)\geq\min_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}}\ \min_{0\leq a\leq t-1}\ \min_{0\leq q\leq\left\lfloor N/2\right\rfloor}G_{t,N,a,\varepsilon}(q).

To prove the reverse inequality, fix arbitrary parameters

\varepsilon\in\{0,1\},\qquad 0\leq a\leq t-1,\qquad 0\leq q\leq\left\lfloor N/2\right\rfloor.

Construct a coloring
\Delta
as follows:

•

color exactly
a
of the
t-1
negative integers in
R_{t}=[1-t,-1]
by
0
and the remaining
t-1-a
by
1
;

•

color
0
by
\varepsilon
;

•

color the positive interval
P_{N}=[1,N]
by the two-block coloring

[1,q]\text{ in color }0,\qquad[q+1,N]\text{ in color }1.

For this coloring, the
PPP
contribution is exactly
S(1,q)+S(q+1,N)
by Theorem
1.2
, and the other three contributions are exactly the quantities in Lemmas
4.2
,
4.3
, and
4.4
. Hence

s(\Delta;1-t,n-t)=G_{t,N,a,\varepsilon}(q).

Since the parameters were arbitrary, we obtain the reverse inequality

M_{-t}(n)\leq\min_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}}\ \min_{0\leq a\leq t-1}\ \min_{0\leq q\leq\left\lfloor N/2\right\rfloor}G_{t,N,a,\varepsilon}(q).

Combining the two inequalities completes the proof.
∎

5
Cubic asymptotics

We now derive the cubic main term from the exact reduction theorem.
We write
F(N)=O_{t}(N^{\alpha})
if there exists a constant
C_{t}>0
, depending only on
t
, such that

|F(N)|\leq C_{t}N^{\alpha}

for all sufficiently large
N
; and we write
F(N)=o_{t}(N^{\alpha})
if

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{F(N)}{N^{\alpha}}=0

for each fixed
t
.

Theorem 5.1
.

For every fixed integer
k\leq-2
,

M_{k}(n)=\frac{n^{3}}{12(1+2\sqrt{2})^{2}}(1+o_{k}(1)).

Proof.

Fix
t=-k\geq 2
and put

N=n-t
.
For
n\geq t+1
, Theorem
4.5
gives

M_{-t}(n)=\min_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}}\ \min_{0\leq a\leq t-1}\ \min_{0\leq q\leq\left\lfloor N/2\right\rfloor}G_{t,N,a,\varepsilon}(q),

where

\displaystyle G_{t,N,a,\varepsilon}(q)={}

\displaystyle S(1,q)+S(q+1,N)

\displaystyle+\binom{a+\varepsilon+1}{2}\,q+\binom{t-a-\varepsilon+1}{2}\,(N-q)

\displaystyle+a\binom{q+1}{2}+(t-1-a)\binom{N-q+1}{2}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\binom{q}{2}+(1-\varepsilon)\binom{N-q}{2}

\displaystyle+\binom{a+\varepsilon+2}{3}+\binom{t-a-\varepsilon+2}{3}-1.

We now simplify this formula to its cubic main term.

Since
t
is fixed, the parameters satisfy

0\leq a\leq t-1,\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}
, and

0\leq q\leq\frac{N}{2}
.
Hence the coefficients

\binom{a+\varepsilon+1}{2},\quad\binom{t-a-\varepsilon+1}{2},\quad a,\quad t-1-a,\quad\varepsilon,\quad 1-\varepsilon

are all bounded in terms of
t
alone. Therefore the linear terms in
q
and
N-q
are
O_{t}(N)
, the binomial terms of order two are
O_{t}(N^{2})
, and the constant term is
O_{t}(1)
. Uniformly for all admissible
(a,\varepsilon,q)
,

G_{t,N,a,\varepsilon}(q)=S(1,q)+S(q+1,N)+O_{t}(N^{2}).

(5.1)

Therefore,

M_{-t}(n)=\min_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}}\ \min_{0\leq a\leq t-1}\ \min_{0\leq q\leq\left\lfloor N/2\right\rfloor}G_{t,N,a,\varepsilon}(q)=\min_{0\leq q\leq\left\lfloor N/2\right\rfloor}(S(1,q)+S(q+1,N)+O_{t}(N^{2})).

From Theorem
1.3
, we have

M_{-t}(n)=\frac{N^{3}}{12(1+2\sqrt{2})^{2}}(1+o_{t}(1)).

Since
t
is fixed, it follows that

N=n-t=n+O_{t}(1)
,
and hence

N^{3}=n^{3}+O_{t}(n^{2})
.
Therefore

M_{-t}(n)=\frac{n^{3}}{12(1+2\sqrt{2})^{2}}(1+o_{t}(1)).

Replacing
t
by
-k
gives the equivalent formulation for fixed
k\leq-2
.
∎

6
Concluding remarks

Theorem
4.5
shows that the difficulty of the case
k\leq-2
is localized in the fixed nonpositive block. Once the colors on
[1-t,0]
are encoded by the finite parameters

a=\left\lvert\Delta^{-1}(0)\cap[1-t,-1]\right\rvert\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varepsilon=\begin{cases}1,&\Delta(0)=0,\\
0,&\Delta(0)=1,\end{cases}

the entire optimization problem is reduced to a single integer parameter
q
, the number of positive integers of color
0
. For each triple
(a,\varepsilon,q)
, the positive part of an optimal coloring is exactly the KRSS two-block coloring.

In particular, the open problem left in
[
5
]
for
k\leq-2
admits an exact finite reduction and the same cubic asymptotic constant as in the nonnegative case. It would be interesting to know whether a similar phenomenon holds for more general systems of linear inequalities in which a fixed nonpositive block interacts with a long positive tail.
