ABSTRACT
PURPOSETiragolumab plus atezolizumab has shown encouraging survival outcomes in metastatic non–small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), primarily in patients with PD-L1–high tumors. We further evaluated the combination of tiragolumab plus atezolizumab in the phase III SKYSCRAPER-01 study.METHODSPatients with untreated, locally advanced unresectable/metastatic PD-L1–high (by central laboratory testing) NSCLC were randomly assigned 1:1 to receive either tiragolumab (600 mg) plus atezolizumab (1,200 mg) or placebo plus atezolizumab (1,200 mg) intravenously in 21-day cycles until disease progression, loss of clinical benefit, or unacceptable toxicity. Primary end points were investigator-assessed progression-free survival (INV-PFS) and overall survival (OS) in the primary analysis set (PD-L1–high per 22C3 assay).RESULTSFive hundred twenty-one patients were randomly assigned to either the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group (n = 262) or the placebo plus atezolizumab group (n = 259). At the primary PFS analysis (Mar 12, 2022; median follow-up 9.9 months [IQR, 6.2-13.8]), median INV-PFS was 7.0 months (95% CI, 5.6 to 9.8) with tiragolumab plus atezolizumab and 5.6 months (95% CI, 4.4 to 7.0) with placebo plus atezolizumab (hazard ratio [HR], 0.78 [95% CI, 0.63 to 0.97]; P = .02 [nonsignificant]). At the final OS analysis (Sept 24, 2024; median follow-up 17.9 months [IQR, 6.7-39.0]), median OS was 23.1 months (95% CI, 17.7 to 28.8) with tiragolumab plus atezolizumab and 16.9 months (95% CI, 14.6 to 21.3) with placebo plus atezolizumab (HR, 0.87 [95% CI, 0.7 to 1.1]; P = .22 [nonsignificant]). Overall, 41.2% (n = 110/267) and 33.8% (n = 89/263) of patients experienced grade 3-4 adverse events with tiragolumab plus atezolizumab and placebo plus atezolizumab, respectively. Four and two treatment-related deaths occurred in each group, respectively.CONCLUSIONTiragolumab plus atezolizumab did not demonstrate a statistically significant INV-PFS or OS benefit over atezolizumab in patients with previously untreated PD-L1–high NSCLC.

INTRODUCTION

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), such as anti–PD-L1 and anti–PD-1 antibodies, with or without chemotherapy, are standard first-line treatment options for patients with non–small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) without known actionable genomic alterations.1,2 However, survival benefit is modest and many patients will develop resistance after an initial response to treatment, with more than 90% of patients experiencing disease progression within 5 years.1-4 Combining immunotherapies that target complementary immune pathways may enhance the depth and durability of antitumor responses and help improve outcomes for patients.5

T-cell immunoreceptor with immunoglobulin and immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motif (TIGIT) is an immune checkpoint target expressed on natural killer (NK) cells, CD8+ and CD4+ T cells, and regulatory T cells (Tregs).6-9 TIGIT inhibits the signaling of the costimulatory CD226 receptor, expressed on T cells and NK cells, by binding to CD155 (also known as poliovirus receptor) with a higher affinity.6,7 Immune activity is inhibited by TIGIT through direct inhibition of T cells or by inducing CD155 signaling on antigen-presenting cells.6,7 TIGIT is expressed across multiple tumor types, including lung cancers, and its expression is closely associated with immune-inflamed tumors, characterized by increased PD-L1/PD-1 expression and PD-1–positive T-cell infiltration.8,10,11 Within the tumor microenvironment, TIGIT is enriched on tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, particularly CD8+ T cells; in NSCLC, approximately 60% of CD8+ tumor-infiltrating T cells coexpress PD-1 and TIGIT, consistent with an exhausted T-cell phenotype.8,10,11 Fragment crystallizable region (Fc)–active anti-TIGIT antibodies may enhance antitumor immunity through depletion of TIGIT-expressing Tregs and activation of Fc receptor–expressing myeloid cells, thereby reducing immunosuppression in the tumor microenvironment.8,10,12

It is possible that anti-TIGIT antibodies may augment the antitumor activity of anti–PD-L1/PD-1 antibodies and help improve outcomes for patients with NSCLC.6,9,11 Atezolizumab, an anti–PD-L1 monoclonal antibody, is approved as first-line monotherapy for PD-L1–high metastatic NSCLC and as adjuvant therapy for resected stage II–IIIA NSCLC.13-15 Tiragolumab is an anti-TIGIT antibody that restores CD226-mediated signaling on T cells and NK cells.7,8 In the phase II CITYSCAPE study in patients with chemotherapy-naïve, PD-L1–positive, recurrent or metastatic NSCLC, tiragolumab plus atezolizumab showed clinically meaningful improvements in objective response rate (ORR), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS) versus placebo plus atezolizumab, with the greatest benefit seen in PD-L1–high tumors (tumor proportion score [TPS] ≥50%).16 These results led to the initiation of the phase III SKYSCRAPER-01 study (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04294810) to further evaluate first-line tiragolumab plus atezolizumab in PD-L1–high NSCLC.

METHODS

Patients

Eligible patients were age 18 years and older, and had histologically or cytologically confirmed locally advanced unresectable, recurrent, or metastatic NSCLC with high tumor PD-L1 expression (defined as TPS ≥50% using the immunohistochemistry [IHC] 22C3 pharmDx assay or tumor cell [TC] ≥50% using the investigational VENTANA SP263 CDx assay, as assessed by a central laboratory). Patients had measurable disease defined by RECIST v1.1, a baseline Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status score of 0 or 1, and had not received any previous systemic therapy for NSCLC. Patients with treated CNS lesions were eligible for inclusion, provided metastases were asymptomatic and measurable disease was present outside the CNS. Patients with an Epidermal growth factor receptor genetic mutation or Anaplastic lymphoma kinase gene rearrangement were excluded from the study. Full eligibility criteria are detailed in the Data Supplement.

Study Design

In this randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial (Data Supplement, Fig S1), eligible patients were randomly assigned 1:1 to receive either tiragolumab (600 mg) plus atezolizumab (1,200 mg) or placebo (600 mg) plus atezolizumab (1,200 mg), administered via intravenous infusion on day 1 of each 21-day cycle. Site personnel and patients were blinded to treatment assignment during the study. Treatment continued until radiographic disease progression per RECIST v1.1, or for as long as patients experienced clinical benefit in the absence of unacceptable toxicity or symptomatic deterioration attributed to disease progression. Further information on the study design can be found in the Data Supplement.

The study protocol was approved by the institutional review board or ethics committee at participating centers and complied with Good Clinical Practice guidelines, the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and local laws. All patients provided written informed consent.

End Points and Assessments

The primary end points were investigator-assessed PFS (INV-PFS) and OS based on the primary analysis set (PAS), defined as all randomly assigned patients with high tumor PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%) determined by the IHC 22C3 pharmDx assay. INV-PFS was defined as the time from random assignment to the first occurrence of disease progression determined by the investigator according to RECIST v1.1 or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. OS was defined as the time from random assignment to death from any cause. Key secondary end points were INV-PFS and OS in the secondary analysis set (SAS), defined as all randomly assigned patients with high tumor PD-L1 expression (TC ≥ 50%) determined by the VENTANA SP263 CDx assay. Other secondary end points included investigator-assessed confirmed ORR (defined as the proportion of patients with a complete response [CR] or partial response [PR] on two consecutive occasions at least 4 weeks apart) and duration of response (DOR; defined as the time from the first occurrence of a documented confirmed objective response to disease progression determined by the investigator according to RECIST v1.1 or death from any cause, whichever occurred first). Tumor assessments were conducted at baseline and every 6 weeks for 48 weeks following day 1 of cycle 1, regardless of treatment delays, after which assessments were conducted every 9 weeks.

The safety and tolerability of the study drugs were continuously evaluated throughout the study by assessing adverse events (AEs), vital signs, and clinical laboratory test results. The incidence and severity of AEs were assessed according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 5.0 (NCI CTCAE v5.0).

Methods for subgroup and biomarker analyses can be found in the Data Supplement.

Statistical Analysis

Efficacy analyses were performed in the PAS and SAS, with patients grouped according to the treatment assigned at randomization. To control the overall type 1 error rate at 0.05, INV-PFS and OS in the PAS were tested with a two-sided α value of .001 and .049, respectively. Further statistical methods, including power calculations, planned analyses, the hierarchical testing procedure, and statistical tests used, are presented in the Data Supplement.

Safety analyses were performed by treatment group and conducted in all randomly assigned patients who received at least one dose of tiragolumab/placebo or atezolizumab. An independent data monitoring committee reviewed available safety data periodically to make recommendations regarding study conduct to ensure the safety of patients enrolled in the study.

RESULTS

Patients were recruited between March 4, 2020, and August 31, 2021, at 122 centers in 22 countries or regions. Of the 1,052 patients assessed, 534 eligible patients were randomly assigned to either the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group (n = 266) or the placebo plus atezolizumab group (n = 268; Fig 1). The PAS comprised 521 patients (tiragolumab plus atezolizumab, n = 262; placebo plus atezolizumab, n = 259), with 420 patients (tiragolumab plus atezolizumab, n = 211; placebo plus atezolizumab, n = 209) included in the SAS (Data Supplement, Table S1). Baseline characteristics in the PAS were balanced across treatment groups (Table 1). The median age of patients was 66.0 years (IQR, 60-72) and the majority were male (74.7%), White (69.1%), and had previously used tobacco (63.7%). Most patients had tumors that were nonsquamous (73.1%), and had no liver (82.9%) or brain (83.1%) metastases.

PFS

The final analysis of INV-PFS in the PAS took place after 337 events had been observed: 158 in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group and 179 in the placebo plus atezolizumab group. At data cutoff (Mar 12, 2022), the median duration of follow-up was 9.9 months (IQR, 6.2-13.8), with a median INV-PFS of 7.0 months (95% CI, 5.6 to 9.8) in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group and 5.6 months (95% CI, 4.4 to 7.0) in the placebo plus atezolizumab group (hazard ratio [HR], 0.78 [95% CI, 0.63 to 0.97]; P = .02; Fig 2). As INV-PFS PAS did not cross the statistical boundary, OS was tested at a two-sided α level of .049, as specified in the statistical analysis plan. Results in the SAS were consistent with the PAS (Data Supplement, Fig S2).

OS

The final OS analysis was conducted after 345 events had been observed, with 170 deaths in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group and 175 deaths in the placebo plus atezolizumab group. At data cutoff (September 24, 2024; median duration of follow-up 17.9 months [IQR, 6.7-39.1]), median OS was 23.1 months (95% CI, 17.7 to 28.8) in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group and 16.9 months (95% CI, 14.6 to 21.3) in the placebo plus atezolizumab group (HR, 0.87 [95% CI, 0.71 to 1.08]; P = .22; Fig 3); the primary end point of OS also failed to cross the statistical boundary for significance. Subgroup Analyses

An improvement in INV-PFS was observed across key subgroups for patients treated with tiragolumab plus atezolizumab, compared with placebo plus atezolizumab (Data Supplement, Fig S5). Subgroup analyses of OS did not show any significant treatment effect in favor of tiragolumab plus atezolizumab over placebo plus atezolizumab (Data Supplement, Fig S6).

Biomarker analysis showed a trend toward improved OS in patients with high TIGIT expression (≥10% and ≥15%) who were treated with tiragolumab plus atezolizumab compared with placebo plus atezolizumab (Data Supplement, Fig S7). High TIGIT expression was not prognostic or predictive of a PFS or OS benefit in the FAS or the SAS (Data Supplement, Table S6).

Tumor Response

At the time of the final OS analysis (data cutoff: September 24, 2024), investigator-assessed ORR was 45.8% (CR 1.5%; PR 44.3%) in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group and 35.1% (CR 1.2%; PR 34.0%) in the placebo plus atezolizumab group (Data Supplement, Table S2). Among patients with a confirmed response, the median DOR was 18.0 months (95% CI, 13.6 to 24.4) in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group and 14.6 months (95% CI, 9.7 to 18.6) in the placebo plus atezolizumab group (Data Supplement, Fig S4).

In the PAS, a lower proportion of patients in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group went on to receive subsequent anticancer therapy (39.7%) compared with the placebo plus atezolizumab group (50.6%), with 9.5% and 15.8% of patients going on to receive an immunotherapy, in each group, respectively (Data Supplement, Table S3).

Safety

At the clinical cutoff date for safety (September 24, 2024), the safety-evaluable population comprised 530 patients who had received at least one dose of study treatment: 267 patients in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group and 263 patients in the placebo plus atezolizumab group. The median duration of safety follow-up was 10.6 months (IQR, 4.8-24.8) and 7.8 months (IQR, 4.0-19.7) in each treatment group, respectively. In total, five patients were unblinded due to medical emergencies during the course of the study: two in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group and three in the placebo plus atezolizumab group.

Overall, 256 patients (95.9%) in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group and 240 patients (91.3%) in the placebo plus atezolizumab group experienced at least one all-cause AE (Table 2), most commonly (reported in ≥20% of patients in either group) pruritus (tiragolumab plus atezolizumab, 28.8%; placebo plus atezolizumab, 17.5%) and rash (tiragolumab plus atezolizumab, 25.5%; placebo plus atezolizumab, 13.3%). A total of 110 patients (41.2%) and 89 patients (33.8%) in each treatment group, respectively, experienced a grade 3-4 AE.

Treatment-related AEs (TRAEs), as deemed by investigators, occurred in 202 patients (75.7%) in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group and 158 patients (60.1%) in the placebo plus atezolizumab group (Table 2). Overall, TRAEs were mainly low-grade in both treatment groups, although a higher proportion of grade 3-4 TRAEs were observed in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group (19.9%) than in the placebo plus atezolizumab group (9.5%); the most common (≥5% of patients in either group) TRAEs are listed in the Data Supplement (Table S4).

Serious AEs were reported in 115 patients (43.1%) in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group and 108 patients (41.1%) in the placebo plus atezolizumab group; these were considered to be treatment-related in 13.9% and 7.6% of patients in each group, respectively. Deaths due to an AE were reported in 29 patients (10.9%) in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group, four (1.5%) of which were considered treatment-related (one case each of pneumonitis, neurologic decompensation, autoimmune hemolytic anemia, and thrombophlebitis migrans). Deaths due to an AE in the atezolizumab plus placebo group were reported in 26 patients (9.9%), two (0.8%) of which were considered treatment-related (one case each of immune-mediated encephalitis and myasthenia gravis). AEs leading to any dose interruption occurred in 49.4% of patients in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group and 41.1% of patients in the placebo plus atezolizumab group, while AEs leading to withdrawal from treatment were reported in 16.1% and 6.5% of patients in each treatment group, respectively.

Immune-mediated AEs (imAEs) occurred in 187 (70.0%) and 133 (50.6%) patients in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab and the placebo plus atezolizumab groups, respectively, and were mainly low-grade, with immune-mediated rash being the most commonly reported in each group (42.3% v 19.0%; Data Supplement, Table S5). In total, 43 patients (16.1%) in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group and 26 patients (9.9%) in the placebo plus atezolizumab group experienced a grade 3-4 imAE, with two grade 5 imAEs reported in each treatment group, respectively: one case each of pneumonitis and autoimmune hemolytic anemia in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group and one case each of encephalitis and myasthenia gravis in the placebo plus atezolizumab group. The proportion of patients requiring systemic steroid treatment due to an imAE was 24.3% and 12.5% in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group and the placebo plus atezolizumab group, respectively (Table 2).

DISCUSSION

To our knowledge, SKYSCRAPER-01 is the first phase III, randomized, global study in NSCLC to report clinical outcomes for combination treatment with an anti-TIGIT antibody and an anti–PD-L1 antibody. The primary end points of INV-PFS and OS were not met, and a statistically significant improvement in INV-PFS or OS was not seen with tiragolumab plus atezolizumab over placebo plus atezolizumab in locally advanced unresectable or metastatic PD-L1–high NSCLC. However, numerical improvements in INV-PFS (HR, 0.78 [95% CI, 0.63 to 0.97]; P = .02 [nonsignificant]) and OS (HR, 0.87 [95% CI, 0.71 to 1.08]; P = .22 [nonsignificant]) for the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group suggested potential antitumor activity with combined anti-TIGIT and anti–PD-L1 targeting in NSCLC.

In comparison with placebo plus atezolizumab, tiragolumab plus atezolizumab was associated with higher incidences of AEs, AEs leading to treatment withdrawal, imAEs, and increased steroid use, likely due to the combined effect of two active cancer immunotherapies, as well as the longer treatment duration and safety follow-up. However, imAEs were generally low-grade, most commonly immune-mediated rash, which was typically managed with topical corticosteroids. Observed AEs were consistent with the known safety profile of combined tiragolumab and atezolizumab and expectations for the underlying disease,16-18 with no new safety signals identified.

Although PD-L1 is a widely used biomarker in selecting patients who are suitable for immunotherapy in locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC,1,2 the use of different assays, scoring algorithms, and cutoff scores across studies involving first-line immunotherapy may limit our ability to make comparisons with other clinical data. The PD-L1 IHC 22C3 pharmDx assay is currently the predominant companion diagnostic test approved in the United States and the European Union to select patients for treatment with a PD-L1/PD-1 checkpoint inhibitor in the first-line NSCLC setting,19 and was also used in the CITYSCAPE study.16 In an effort to align with the commonly used IHC 22C3 pharmDx assay, and to confirm the encouraging results observed in patients with high PD-L1 expression in the CITYSCAPE study, patients with a PD-L1 TPS ≥50% per the PD-L1 IHC 22C3 pharmDx assay were enrolled into the SKYSCRAPER-01 study. As part of our study, secondary INV-PFS and OS analyses were conducted using data from patients with PD-L1–high tumors per the investigational VENTANA PD-L1 (SP263) assay and these data were concordant with the primary analysis.

Subgroup analyses did not reveal any demographic or disease characteristics that would be predictive of response to tiragolumab plus atezolizumab therapy. Although a trend toward an improved OS benefit was seen in the biomarker analysis in patients with high TIGIT expression, these subgroups had relatively small sample sizes leading to broad CIs, which limit the interpretation of our findings.

A survival benefit for combination anti–PD-L1/PD-1 plus anti-TIGIT regimens over anti–PD-L1/PD-1, with or without chemotherapy, in advanced NSCLC has not been demonstrated in other phase III, randomized clinical trials, despite promising activity seen in phase II trials.20-23 Furthermore, data from current phase II trials investigating combination regimens in advanced NSCLC also remain inconsistent. In ARC-10, the addition of domvanalimab (anti-TIGIT antibody) to zimberelimab (anti–PD-1 antibody) resulted in improved PFS and OS compared with zimberelimab alone and the regimen is being investigated further in combination with chemotherapy in the phase III STAR-121 study.24,25 In GALAXIES Lung-201, the combination regimen of dostarlimab (anti–PD-1 antibody) plus belrestotug (anti-TIGIT antibody) failed to demonstrate a significant PFS benefit over dostarlimab alone, despite demonstrating clinically meaningful antitumor activity26,27; these data, combined with a lack of clinical activity in head and neck cancer (GALAXIES H&N-202; ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT06062420), have resulted in the termination of the belrestotug program, and further enrollment into the phase III GALALXIES Lung-301 study has stopped.25 It is not yet clear why some positive early-stage studies of chemotherapy-free ICI combinations have not translated into a survival benefit in phase III studies. These studies highlight the challenges that ICI therapies, including anti-TIGIT monoclonal antibodies, face in terms of improved clinical benefit versus current ICI monotherapy. Moreover, combination therapy is also often associated with increased toxicity compared with single-agent therapy.20-23 Therefore, further research is required to better understand the underlying tumor biology in the advanced stages of NSCLC and to identify treatment strategies that can mitigate the increased toxicity associated with combination regimens. Ongoing studies of rilvegostomig, a bispecific antibody targeting TIGIT and PD-1, may help to provide further insights into the mechanistic role of TIGIT blockade and inform the development of future therapies.28,29

SKYSCRAPER-01 was designed as a randomized, blinded, placebo-controlled study, enabling robust and unbiased investigator assessment of the efficacy and safety of tiragolumab plus atezolizumab. A potential limitation of this study is that it was initiated based on the results of a selected PD-L1–high exploratory subgroup from the CITYSCAPE study; the efficacy and safety tiragolumab plus atezolizumab was not assessed in PD-L1–low or PD-L1–negative subgroups and limit our findings to a select patient population. In addition, efficacy end points were assessed by investigators rather than by an independent review facility, which could lead to bias in the reporting of our findings.

In conclusion, the primary end points of INV-PFS and OS were not met in SKYSCRAPER-01, as tiragolumab plus atezolizumab did not significantly improve clinical outcomes in patients with locally advanced unresectable or metastatic PD-L1–high NSCLC. The safety profile of combination tiragolumab plus atezolizumab was consistent with the known toxicities associated with dual checkpoint inhibition and previous observations for this regimen. A higher frequency of imAEs, including grade 3-4 imAEs, was observed in the tiragolumab plus atezolizumab group compared with the atezolizumab group. These data contribute to the body of evidence on dual checkpoint inhibition in a select patient population with NSCLC.
