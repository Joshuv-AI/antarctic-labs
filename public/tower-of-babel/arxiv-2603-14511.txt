Cosmological peculiar velocities in general relativity
Roy Maartens

Abstract
We reconsider the late-time evolution of galaxy peculiar velocities in the 1 + 3 1+3 covariant approach to cosmological perturbation theory. It has recently been claimed that this approach predicts substantially stronger growth of peculiar velocities than standard metric-based perturbation theory – on the grounds that the covariant treatment is fully relativistic whereas standard treatments are effectively Newtonian. We show that this is not the case. When the covariant equations are applied consistently, the 1 + 3 1+3 approach reproduces exactly the standard perturbative result for peculiar-velocity growth. The stronger growth laws claimed in recent work arise from an inconsistent treatment of the coupled covariant system, in which terms constrained by the field equations are treated as if they were independent sources. Further claims are made that the stronger bulk flows can mimic accelerated expansion in a dust universe. We argue that these claims rest on a confusion between the kinematics of an arbitrarily chosen observer congruence and the physical expansion of the matter congruence traced by galaxies. We conclude that the standard treatment of peculiar velocities is correct and fully relativistic – and does not lead to anomalous bulk flows or to apparent accelerated expansion.

Basic definitions

The starting point is a choice of timelike 4-velocity
u^{a}
, with
u_{a}u^{a}=-1
. This defines the projection tensor into the rest-space orthogonal to
u^{a}
:

\displaystyle h_{ab}=g_{ab}+u_{a}u_{b}\,.

(2.1)

The covariant derivative of
u^{a}
is irreducibly decomposed as

\displaystyle\nabla_{b}u_{a}=\frac{1}{3}\Theta\,h_{ab}+\sigma_{ab}+\omega_{ab}-u_{b}A_{a}\,,

(2.2)

where
A_{a}=u^{b}\nabla_{b}u_{a}
is the 4-acceleration,
\Theta=\nabla_{a}u^{a}
is the volume expansion rate,
\sigma_{ab}=h_{\langle a}^{\ \ c}h_{b\rangle}^{\ \ d}\nabla_{d}u_{c}
is the shear tensor, and
\omega_{ab}=h_{[a}^{\ \ c}h_{b]}^{\ \ d}\nabla_{d}u_{c}
is the vorticity tensor. Thus the kinematics of any congruence are described by its expansion, distortion, rotation and acceleration.

Angle brackets denote the projected, symmetric, trace-free part of a tensor:

\displaystyle J_{\langle ab\rangle}=h_{(a}^{\ \ c}h_{b)}^{\ \ d}J_{cd}-\frac{1}{3}h_{ab}h^{cd}J_{cd}\,.

(2.3)

The covariant time derivative along
u^{a}
is

\displaystyle\dot{J}_{a\cdots}^{\ \ \ \cdots b}=u^{c}\nabla_{c}J_{a\cdots}^{\ \ \ \cdots b}\,,

(2.4)

while the projected spatial derivative is

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}_{c}J_{a\cdots}^{\ \ \ \cdots b}=h_{a}^{\ d}\cdots h_{e}^{\ b}h_{c}^{\ f}\nabla_{f}J_{d\cdots}^{\ \ \ \cdots e}\,.

(2.5)

In exact FLRW spacetime, when
u^{a}
is normal to the homogeneous spatial hypersurfaces,

\displaystyle\Theta=3H\,,\qquad A_{a}=0\,,\qquad\sigma_{ab}=0\,,\qquad\omega_{ab}=0\,,

(2.6)

where
H
is the standard Hubble rate.

Relations between frames

A second 4-velocity
\tilde{u}^{a}
is related to
u^{a}
by a relative spacelike velocity field
v^{a}
:

\displaystyle\tilde{u}^{a}=\gamma(u^{a}+v^{a})\,,\qquad u_{a}v^{a}=0\,,\qquad\gamma=(1-v_{a}v^{a})^{-1/2}\,.

(2.7)

Each 4-velocity defines a congruence of worldlines and hence a frame of observers. In general a frame is not physically relevant unless it is tied to some matter content such as the radiation which defines the CMB frame, for example, though it may be of mathematical convenience. In the late-time cosmological context, following

\tilde{u}^{a}
as the matter 4-velocity, i.e. the 4-velocity of observers comoving with the galaxies. The matter is taken to be pressure-free dust in free fall. The field
u^{a}
then defines a chosen ‘cosmic rest frame’ whose ‘observers’ move with respect to the matter. This rest frame will turn out to be the coordinate frame of the longitudinal (Newtonian) gauge (see Appendix
A

Assuming

, we neglect terms nonlinear in

and its derivatives. The exact relations

\displaystyle\tilde{\Theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta+\mathrm{D}_{a}v^{a}+A_{a}v^{a}\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{a}+h_{a}^{\ b}\dot{v}_{b}+\frac{1}{3}\Theta v_{a}+\sigma_{ab}v^{b}+\omega_{ab}v^{b}+A_{b}v^{b}u_{a}\,,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}_{ab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{ab}+\mathrm{D}_{\langle a}v_{b\rangle}+A_{\langle a}v_{b\rangle}+2u_{(a}\sigma_{b)c}v^{c}\,,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{ab}

\displaystyle\omega_{ab}-\mathrm{D}_{[a}v_{b]}+A_{[a}v_{b]}-2u_{[a}\omega_{b]c}v^{c}\,.

These relations may be inverted by removing the tildes on the left-hand side, placing tildes on the right-hand side, and replacing

The important point is that these are relations between the kinematic quantities of two distinct congruences. Consequently, to relate the two sets of kinematic quantities one needs not only

Matter variables

The matter energy-momentum tensor is a physical tensor, independent of the observer congruence. In the matter frame it takes the simple dust form

\displaystyle T_{ab}=\tilde{\rho}\,\tilde{u}_{a}\tilde{u}_{b}\,.

(2.12)

Equivalently, relative to the
u^{a}
frame,

\displaystyle T_{ab}=\rho\,u_{a}u_{b}+ph_{ab}+2q_{(a}u_{b)}+\pi_{ab}\,,

(2.13)

where
\rho
,

and

are the density, pressure, momentum flux and anisotropic stress measured by the
u^{a}
observers.

To first order in

so the momentum flux seen in the cosmic rest frame is simply due to matter moving past those observers. By contrast with the kinematic relations above, here we are decomposing the

is needed to relate the fluid variables.
For the dust model considered here, the matter frame is geodesic and comoving with the fluid, while in the chosen cosmic rest frame, the only nontrivial matter variable is the momentum flux generated by the relative velocity:

\displaystyle\mbox{\bf matter frame}~\tilde{u}^{a}:~

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{a}=0\,,\qquad\tilde{p}=0\,,\qquad\tilde{q}_{a}=0\,,\qquad\tilde{\pi}_{ab}=0\,,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\mbox{\bf cosmic rest frame}~u^{a}:~

\displaystyle\rho=\tilde{\rho}\,,\qquad p=0\,,\qquad q_{a}=\rho v_{a}\,,\qquad\pi_{ab}=0\,.

(2.15)

Field equations

The evolution and constraint equations of the covariant approach follow from the Ricci identity, the Bianchi identities, Einstein’s equations, and energy-momentum conservation. For the present work we need only the equations below – we give these for a general spacetime.

The Raychaudhuri equation is

\displaystyle\dot{\Theta}=-\frac{1}{3}\Theta^{2}+\mathrm{D}_{a}A^{a}-4\pi G\rho+A_{a}A^{a}-\sigma_{ab}\sigma^{ab}+\omega_{ab}\omega^{ab}\,.

(2.17)

The shear propagation equation is

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma}_{\langle ab\rangle}=-\frac{2}{3}\Theta\sigma_{ab}+\mathrm{D}_{\langle a}A_{b\rangle}+4\pi G\pi_{ab}-E_{ab}-\sigma_{c\langle a}\sigma_{b\rangle}^{\ c}+A_{\langle a}A_{b\rangle}+\omega_{c\langle a}\omega_{b\rangle}^{\ c}\,,

(2.18)

where
E_{ab}{=C_{acbd}u^{c}u^{d}}
is the electric Weyl tensor, whose propagation equation is

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{\langle ab\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Theta E_{ab}+{\rm curl}\,H_{ab}-4\pi G(\rho+p)\sigma_{ab}-4\pi G\,\mathrm{D}_{\langle a}q_{b\rangle}-4\pi G\,\dot{\pi}_{\langle ab\rangle}-\frac{4\pi G}{3}\pi_{ab}

(2.19)

\displaystyle-A_{\langle a}q_{b\rangle}+2A^{c}\varepsilon_{cd(a}H_{b)}^{\ d}+3\sigma_{c\langle a}E_{b\rangle}^{\ c}-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{c\langle a}\pi_{b\rangle}^{\ c}-\omega_{c(a}\big(E_{b)}^{\ c}+\pi_{b)}^{\ c}\big)\,.

Here
\varepsilon_{abc}
is the projected alternating tensor,
H_{ab}=\varepsilon_{acd}C^{cd}_{~~be}u^{e}/2
is the magnetic Weyl tensor and the curl is given by

\displaystyle{\rm curl}\,J_{ab}=\varepsilon_{cd(a}\mathrm{D}^{c}J_{b)}^{\ d}\,.

(2.20)

The shear curl constraint is

\displaystyle{\rm curl}\,\sigma_{ab}=H_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{cd\langle a}\big(\mathrm{D}_{b\rangle}-2A_{b\rangle}\big)\omega^{cd}\,.

(2.21)

Finally, energy-momentum conservation gives

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\rho+p)\Theta-\mathrm{D}^{a}q_{a}-2A^{a}q_{a}-\sigma^{ab}\pi_{ab}\,,

(2.22)

\displaystyle h_{a}^{\ b}\dot{q}_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{3}\Theta q_{a}-(\rho+p)A_{a}-\mathrm{D}_{a}p-\mathrm{D}^{b}\pi_{ab}-A^{b}\pi_{ab}-(\sigma_{a}^{\ b}+\omega_{a}^{\ b})q_{b}\,.

(2.23)

These equations simplify considerably in the dust and linearised settings considered below, where
\pi_{ab}=0
and nonlinear products of perturbations are neglected.

3
Peculiar velocities: standard or stronger growth?

Peculiar velocities are not uniquely defined until a reference congruence has been chosen. In metric-based perturbation theory this choice is encoded in the gauge; in the covariant approach it is encoded in the choice of 4-velocity
u^{a}
. At first order, the two descriptions are equivalent
[
23
,
21
]
. Thus any comparison between covariant and metric-based peculiar velocities must first ensure that the same reference congruence is being used.

In the standard relativistic treatment of scalar perturbations of FLRW, peculiar velocities are often described in longitudinal gauge. The observer congruence
u^{a}
is then the unit normal to the
t=\,
const hypersurfaces, and the matter 4-velocity is
\tilde{u}^{a}=u^{a}+v^{a}
to first order. The peculiar velocity
v^{a}
is therefore the matter velocity measured relative to that observer congruence. As discussed in
Appendix A
, this is the covariant counterpart of the physical peculiar velocity in longitudinal gauge. By contrast, in matter-comoving gauges the peculiar velocity vanishes identically. Peculiar velocities are therefore gauge- or frame-dependent, but this is not problematic, provided that physical observables are treated consistently.

The analyses in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
consider models close to Einstein-de Sitter and use a covariant formulation based on
[
15
,
16
]
. For a direct comparison with the standard longitudinal-gauge result, we restrict attention to scalar first-order perturbations. In particular, the matter vorticity vanishes,

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{ab}=0\,.

(3.1)

In longitudinal gauge, the observer congruence normal to the constant-
t
hypersurfaces is shear-free and irrotational
[
11
]
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{ab}=0\,,\qquad\omega_{ab}=0\,.

(3.2)

These conditions do not impose any physical restriction on the matter. They simply characterise the chosen reference congruence. It is therefore natural to make the same choice in the covariant description, as in
[
15
,
16
]
. With this identification, the covariant setup is the first-order counterpart of longitudinal gauge.

3.1
Covariant analysis recovers the standard result

With the choice (
3.2
), the covariant equations determine the pair
(v_{a},A_{a})
as the degrees of freedom of the frame. The first relation follows immediately from momentum conservation in the
u^{a}
frame. Using

q_{a}=\rho v_{a}
,
p=\pi_{ab}=0
, and neglecting products of first-order quantities, (
2.23
) gives

\displaystyle\dot{q}_{a}+\frac{4}{3}\Theta q_{a}+\rho A_{a}=0\,,

(3.3)

so that

\displaystyle A_{a}=-\dot{v}_{a}-\frac{1}{3}\Theta v_{a}=-\dot{v}_{a}-Hv_{a}\,.

(3.4)

The second relation follows from the shear and Weyl evolution equations. Since the reference congruence is shear-free, the linearised shear propagation equation (
2.18
) reduces to

\displaystyle E_{ab}=\mathrm{D}_{\langle a}A_{b\rangle}\,.

(3.5)

Similarly, since the congruence is irrotational, the shear-curl constraint gives

\displaystyle H_{ab}=0\,.

(3.6)

Substituting these results into the linearised electric Weyl propagation equation (
2.19
), and using
q_{a}=\rho v_{a}
, yields

\displaystyle\big(\mathrm{D}_{\langle a}A_{b\rangle}\big)^{\displaystyle\cdot}=-3H\,\mathrm{D}_{\langle a}A_{b\rangle}-4\pi G\rho\,\mathrm{D}_{\langle a}v_{b\rangle}\,.

(3.7)

Using the linearised commutation relation

\displaystyle\big(\mathrm{D}_{a}J_{b}\big)^{\displaystyle\cdot}=\mathrm{D}_{a}\dot{J}_{b}-H\,\mathrm{D}_{a}J_{b}\,,

(3.8)

valid for the scalar, shear-free and irrotational setting considered here
[
15
,
16
]
, this becomes

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}_{\langle a}\Big[\dot{A}_{b\rangle}+2HA_{b\rangle}+4\pi G\rho\,v_{b\rangle}\Big]=0\,.

(3.9)

For scalar perturbations, the bracketed quantity is itself a projected gradient. Hence vanishing projected, symmetric, trace-free derivative implies that, up to a spatially homogeneous mode which is not part of the perturbations of interest, the bracket vanishes:

\displaystyle\dot{A}_{a}+2HA_{a}+4\pi G\rho\,v_{a}=0\,.

(3.10)

Equations (
3.4
) and (
3.10
) form a closed linear system for
(v_{a},A_{a})
. Eliminating
A_{a}
gives the key equation for the peculiar velocity field:

\displaystyle\ddot{v}_{a}+3H\dot{v}_{a}+\big(\dot{H}+2H^{2}-4\pi G\rho\big)v_{a}=0\,.

(3.11)

In Einstein-de Sitter,

\displaystyle\dot{H}=-\frac{3}{2}H^{2}\,,\qquad 4\pi G\rho=\frac{3}{2}H^{2}\,,

(3.12)

and therefore

\displaystyle\ddot{v}_{a}+3H\dot{v}_{a}-H^{2}v_{a}=0\,.

(3.13)

The growing solution is

\displaystyle v_{a}\propto t^{1/3}\propto a^{1/2}\,,

(3.14)

in agreement with the standard longitudinal-gauge result derived in
Appendix A
.

Equation (
3.4
) then gives the corresponding behaviour of the acceleration of the chosen reference congruence:

\displaystyle A_{a}\propto t^{-2/3}\propto a^{-1}\,.

(3.15)

Thus the acceleration required to keep the cosmic rest frame at rest relative to the matter decays with time, as expected in a decelerating dust universe.

The remaining scalar perturbations are determined once
(v_{a},A_{a})
are known. In the covariant approach we define the
density and expansion gradients as

\displaystyle\Delta_{a}=\frac{a}{\rho}\mathrm{D}_{a}\rho\,,\qquad Z_{a}=a\,\mathrm{D}_{a}\Theta\,.

(3.16)

Linearising the energy conservation equation and taking a projected gradient gives

\displaystyle\dot{\Delta}_{a}=-Z_{a}-a\,\mathrm{D}_{a}(\mathrm{D}^{b}v_{b})-3aH\,A_{a}\,,

(3.17)

while taking a projected gradient of the Raychaudhuri equation gives

\displaystyle\dot{Z}_{a}=-2H\,Z_{a}-\frac{3}{2}H^{2}\Delta_{a}-\frac{9}{2}aH^{2}A_{a}+a\,\mathrm{D}_{a}\mathrm{D}^{b}A_{b}\,.

(3.18)

Hence
(\Delta_{a},Z_{a})
form a forced linear subsystem once
(v_{a},A_{a})
are known. In particular, the source terms in (
3.17
)–(
3.18
) are fixed by the already-determined velocity and acceleration fields. The density and expansion gradients therefore follow from the closed
(v_{a},A_{a})
sector; they do not generate independent growth laws for
v_{a}
.

3.2
Ruling out faster growth

The origin of the spurious stronger growth laws claimed in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
can now be isolated. The covariant system may be rearranged in several equivalent ways to give second-order differential equations for
v_{a}
such that they appear to have a source driving them. Two examples are especially relevant.
The first is Eq. (15) of
[
2
]
, specialised to Einstein-de Sitter:

\displaystyle\ddot{v}_{a}+\frac{1}{2}H\dot{v}_{a}-2H^{2}v_{a}=\frac{1}{3aH}\Big[a\,\mathrm{D}_{a}(\mathrm{D}^{b}v_{b})^{\displaystyle\cdot}+\dot{Z}_{a}+\ddot{\Delta}_{a}\Big]\,.

(3.19)

The second is Eq. (11) of
[
3
]
:

\displaystyle\ddot{v}_{a}+H\dot{v}_{a}-\frac{3}{2}H^{2}v_{a}=\frac{1}{3aH}\Big[\ddot{\Delta}_{a}+2H\dot{\Delta}_{a}-\frac{3}{2}H^{2}\Delta_{a}\Big]\,.

(3.20)

Both equations are correct. They are simply different rearrangements of the same underlying linearised covariant system, and direct substitution of (
3.17
) and (
3.18
) confirms this.

The error arises when the right-hand side of either (
3.19
) or (
3.20
) is treated as if it were an external source that may be switched off in order to isolate a physically relevant ‘homogeneous’ solution. This is not a legitimate approximation, because the terms on the right-hand side are not independent of
v_{a}
. They are fixed by the coupled perturbation equations.

We can see the problem directly by the fact that the same truncation applied to the two equally valid equations above leads to two different growth laws. Setting the right-hand side of (
3.19
) to zero gives

\displaystyle\ddot{v}_{a}+\frac{1}{2}H\dot{v}_{a}-2H^{2}v_{a}=0\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad v_{a}\propto t^{4/3}\propto a^{2}\,,

(3.21)

whereas setting the right-hand side of (
3.20
) to zero gives

\displaystyle\ddot{v}_{a}+H\dot{v}_{a}-\frac{3}{2}H^{2}v_{a}=0\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad v_{a}\propto t\propto a^{3/2}\,.

(3.22)

Since both equations come from the same covariant system, these incompatible ‘homogeneous’ growth laws already show that the truncation of the right hand side of either has no invariant physical meaning. In fact neither growth law is allowed, because both contradict the closed subsystem result (
3.11
).

\displaystyle\ddot{v}_{a}+\Big(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\Big)H\dot{v}_{a}-\Big(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\Big)H^{2}v_{a}=\frac{1}{3aH}\Big[\ddot{\Delta}_{a}+(2-2\alpha)H\dot{\Delta}_{a}-(1-\alpha)\frac{3}{2}H^{2}\Delta_{a}+\alpha\big(a\,\mathrm{D}_{a}(\mathrm{D}^{b}v_{b})^{\displaystyle\cdot}+\dot{Z}_{a}\big)\Big]\,.

The split into a ‘homogeneous’ part for
v_{a}
and a ‘source’ term is clearly not unique. If we look for a homogeneous solution without the right-hand side, the resulting growth law would arbitrarily depend on the choice of
\alpha
.

We can see this more generally. The linearised perturbations form a coupled system for
(v_{a},A_{a},\Delta_{a},Z_{a})
. The pair
(v_{a},A_{a})
is a closed subset of that system: (
3.4
) and (
3.10
) determine its time evolution uniquely up to initial data, and give the standard growing mode (
3.14
). The remaining equations (
3.17
)–(
3.18
) then determine
(\Delta_{a},Z_{a})
once
(v_{a},A_{a})
are known.
They cannot consistently be used to infer additional growth laws for
v_{a}
, because their source terms depend on the already-determined velocity and acceleration fields.

Equivalently, (
3.17
)–(
3.18
) are a forced linear system for
(\Delta_{a},Z_{a})
, with forcing proportional to
\mathrm{D}_{a}(\mathrm{D}^{b}v_{b})
and
\mathrm{D}_{a}\mathrm{D}^{b}A_{b}
. Any manipulation of these equations that appears to yield an alternative second-order evolution equation for
v_{a}
necessarily treats those forcing terms as if they were independent or negligible, thereby enlarging the solution space beyond that allowed by the full covariant system. This is the origin of the stronger growth laws claimed in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

4
Discussion and conclusion

The results of
section 3
clearly show that the faster peculiar-velocity growth claimed in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
is
not
a solution of the full covariant system. The covariant, shear-free and irrotational reference congruence used there leads instead to the standard relativistic growth law in Einstein–de Sitter,

\displaystyle v_{a}\propto t^{1/3}\propto a^{1/2}\,,

(4.1)

as given in (
3.14
). The corresponding acceleration of the chosen cosmic rest frame decays as

A_{a}\propto a^{-1}\,,

following from (
3.4
). There is therefore no new fast-growing relativistic mode in the covariant approach. Standard perturbation theory captures all linear relativistic effects consistently at first-order.

This resolves the origin of the stronger growth laws claimed in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
. Once the shear-free condition is imposed, the pair
(v_{a},A_{a})
forms a closed subsystem: (
3.4
) and (
3.10
) determine its evolution uniquely up to initial data, leading to (
3.11
) and hence to (
3.14
). The remaining density and expansion gradients then follow from the forced system (
3.17
)–(
3.18
). They do not supply additional admissible velocity modes. The stronger laws obtained by setting the right-hand side of equations such as (
3.19
) or (
3.20
) to zero arise because terms constrained by the coupled system are treated as if they were independent sources.

These points also clarify the associated claims about bulk flows in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
. Since the faster growth laws (
3.21
) and (
3.22
) are spurious, they cannot provide an explanation for anomalously large bulk flows. Any tension between observed bulk flows and the standard cosmological model must therefore be addressed on other grounds.

A further claim in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
is that bulk motions in a matter-dominated universe can lead observers to infer accelerated expansion, even though the underlying universe is decelerating. In
[
14
]
it is claimed that a dipole found in the Pantheon+ SNIa data leads the conclusion that “The inferred cosmic acceleration cannot therefore
be due to a Cosmological Constant, but is likely a general relativistic effect due
to the anomalous bulk flow in our local Universe.” This is clearly not supported by the correct peculiar velocity evolution. Irrespective of the details of the growth law, there is a futher problem with the interpretation of different congruences. The matter congruence, with 4-velocity
\tilde{u}^{a}
, defines the
physical
expansion of the dust and hence of the late-time universe traced by galaxies. By contrast, the chosen ‘cosmic rest frame’ with 4-velocity
u^{a}
is an auxiliary observer congruence introduced in order to describe peculiar motions. Its kinematics do
not
determine the physical expansion of the matter, and has no physical meaning.

This is seen directly from the Raychaudhuri equation for the matter congruence. Writing

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathsf{H}}=\frac{1}{3}\tilde{\Theta}=\frac{1}{3}\nabla_{a}\tilde{u}^{a}\,,

(4.2)

the Ricci identity (
2.17
) gives, in the matter frame for dust

\displaystyle\tilde{u}^{a}\nabla_{a}\tilde{\Theta}=-\frac{1}{3}\tilde{\Theta}^{2}-4\pi G\tilde{\rho}-\tilde{\sigma}_{ab}\tilde{\sigma}^{ab}\,,

(4.3)

and therefore the covariant version of the deceleration parameter, as defined in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
, is

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathsf{q}}:=-\frac{3}{\tilde{\Theta}^{2}}\left(\tilde{u}^{a}\nabla_{a}\tilde{\Theta}+\frac{1}{3}\tilde{\Theta}^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{3\tilde{\mathsf{H}}^{2}}\left(4\pi G\tilde{\rho}+\tilde{\sigma}_{ab}\tilde{\sigma}^{ab}\right)>0\,.

(4.4)

Thus the matter-dominated universe is covariantly decelerating. This physical deceleration is a property of the matter worldlines and is independent of the auxiliary congruence used to define peculiar velocities.

The quantity denoted
\mathsf{q}
for the chosen cosmic rest frame describes the deceleration of the
u^{a}
congruence itself. It is not the same as the deceleration of the matter congruence as inferred observationally by observers moving with respect to the matter. Relating observations made in different states of motion requires the appropriate boost and lightcone relations. It is therefore meaningless to compare
\tilde{\mathsf{q}}
and
\mathsf{q}
as if they referred to the same physical quantity.

The same point applies to claims of a dipole in the deceleration tensor generated by bulk flows. Any dipole in the deceleration of galaxy worldlines should be defined for the matter congruence and then transformed to the frame of the observer.
It cannot be inferred directly from differences between tensors defined on different congruences. In particular, any observed dipole in a deceleration parameter must be analysed through the covariant cosmographic expansion and its transformation under boosts
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
, rather than by comparing the kinematics of two different congruences.

In summary, the covariant approach does not replace the standard relativistic treatment of peculiar velocities. When applied consistently, it reproduces it. The stronger growth laws claimed in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
arise from an inconsistent truncation of the coupled covariant system, and the associated claims about bulk flows and apparent acceleration do not follow.

Appendix A
Peculiar velocities in perturbed FLRW

Considering peculiar velocities in perturbed FLRW (Einstein-de Sitter), in order to connect with the covariant discussion it is useful to distinguish carefully between the 4-velocity of the longitudinal-gauge observers
u^{a}
(i.e. the unit normal to the constant-
t
hypersurfaces), the matter 4-velocity
\tilde{u}^{a}
, the physical peculiar 3-velocity
v^{a}
and the coordinate
velocity
\mathrm{d}x^{i}/\mathrm{d}t
.

Scalar perturbations of Einstein-de Sitter in Newtonian (longitudinal) gauge, with vanishing
anisotropic stress, are described by

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-(1+2\Phi)\,\mathrm{d}t^{2}+a^{2}(t)\,(1-2\Phi)\,\delta_{ij}\,\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}\,.

(A.1)

The Newtonian-gauge observer worldlines are normal to the
t=\,
const hypersurfaces. Their
4-velocity is therefore

u^{\mu}=\big(1-\Phi,0,0,0\big),\qquad u_{\mu}=\big(-(1+\Phi),0,0,0\big),

(A.2)

to first order.
The matter worldlines are described by a different 4-velocity
\tilde{u}^{\mu}
. Writing

\tilde{u}^{\mu}=\frac{\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}=\Big(\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}\tau},\frac{\mathrm{d}x^{i}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\Big)=\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\Big(1,\frac{\mathrm{d}x^{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big),

(A.3)

the normalisation condition
\tilde{u}^{\mu}\tilde{u}_{\mu}=-1
gives,

\tilde{u}^{\mu}=\Big(1-\Phi,\frac{\mathrm{d}x^{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big),

(A.4)

if one wishes to use the coordinate velocity
\mathrm{d}x^{i}/\mathrm{d}t
.
In the covariant decomposition one defines the peculiar velocity by

\tilde{u}^{a}=\gamma\big(u^{a}+v^{a}\big),\qquad u_{a}v^{a}=0.

(A.5)

Since
v^{a}
is orthogonal to
u^{a}
, it is a physical 3-velocity measured by the Newtonian-gauge
observers.
Using (
A.2
), this means

v^{\mu}=\tilde{u}^{\mu}-u^{\mu}=\Big(0,\frac{v^{i}}{a}\Big),

(A.6)

where
v^{i}
is the physical peculiar velocity. Therefore

\tilde{u}^{\mu}=\Big(1-\Phi,\frac{v^{i}}{a}\Big),\qquad\tilde{u}_{\mu}=\big(-(1+\Phi),a\,v_{i}\big),

(A.7)

where
v_{i}=\delta_{ij}v^{j}
.
Therefore the physical
peculiar velocity and the coordinate velocity are related by

v^{i}=a\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x^{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}.

(A.8)

Thus the covariant peculiar velocity
v^{a}
is associated with the physical peculiar velocity
v^{i}
,
not with the coordinate velocity
\mathrm{d}x^{i}/\mathrm{d}t
.

For dust,
\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0
gives the linear Euler equation in Newtonian gauge,

\dot{v}_{i}+Hv_{i}=-\frac{1}{a}\,\partial_{i}\Phi\,.

(A.9)

The linearised
0i
Einstein equation gives

\partial_{i}\big(\dot{\Phi}+H\Phi\big)=-4\pi G\,a\,\rho\,v_{i}\,,

(A.10)

where
\rho
is the background matter density.

Differentiating (
A.9
), we find

\ddot{v}_{i}+\dot{H}\,v_{i}+H\dot{v}_{i}=-\frac{1}{a}\partial_{i}\dot{\Phi}+\frac{H}{a}\partial_{i}\Phi\,.

(A.11)

Using (
A.10
), this becomes

\ddot{v}_{i}+\dot{H}\,v_{i}+H\dot{v}_{i}=\frac{2H}{a}\partial_{i}\Phi+4\pi G\rho\,v_{i}\,.

(A.12)

Eliminating
\partial_{i}\Phi
via (
A.9
),
we obtain

\ddot{v}_{i}+3H\dot{v}_{i}+\big(\dot{H}+2H^{2}-4\pi G\rho\big)v_{i}=0\,.

(A.13)

Using

\dot{H}=-\frac{3}{2}H^{2},\qquad 4\pi G\rho=\frac{3}{2}H^{2},

(A.14)

it follows that

\ddot{v}_{i}+3H\dot{v}_{i}-H^{2}v_{i}=0,

(A.15)

which is (
3.11
). Therefore the growing mode is

v_{i}\propto t^{1/3}\propto a^{1/2}.

(A.16)

By contrast, the coordinate velocity

w^{i}=\frac{\mathrm{d}x^{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{v^{i}}{a}

(A.17)

obeys

\dot{w}_{i}+2Hw_{i}=-\frac{1}{a^{2}}\partial_{i}\Phi,

(A.18)

and therefore has the dominant mode

w_{i}\propto a^{-1/2}\propto t^{-1/3}.

(A.19)
