A note on small theta lift
Jingsong Chai

Abstract
. In this note, we use certain sesquilienar form to realize small theta lift for even orthogonal-symplectic and unitary dual pairs over p-adic fields.

22E50,20C33

1.
Introduction

Let
(G^{\prime},G)
be an irreducible type I reductive dual pair inside the symplectic group
Sp(F)
, where
F
is a p-adic field. Let
\tilde{S}p(F)
be the metaplectic two fold cover of
Sp(F)
. For any subgroup
H
of
Sp(F)
we let
\tilde{H}
denote its inverse image in
\tilde{S}p(F)
. Let
(\omega,\mathcal{Y})
be the Weil representation of
\tilde{Sp}(F)
. Let
(\pi^{\prime},V_{\pi^{\prime}})
be any irreducible unitary representation of
\tilde{G}^{\prime}
. Let
\mathcal{Y}^{\infty},H_{\pi^{\prime}}^{\infty}
denote the space of smooth vectors. Then
\tilde{G}^{\prime}
acts on the algebraic tensor product
\mathcal{Y}^{\infty}\otimes V_{\pi^{\prime}}^{\infty}
by
\omega\otimes\pi^{\prime}
. The space
\mathcal{Y}^{\infty}\otimes V_{\pi^{\prime}}^{\infty}
is naturally endowed with an inner product
<,>
coming from the unitary structure of
\mathcal{Y}
and
H_{\pi^{\prime}}
. Consider the new sesquilinear form
<,>_{\pi^{\prime}}
defined by

<\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}>_{\pi^{\prime}}=\int_{\tilde{G}^{\prime}}<\Phi,(\omega\otimes\pi^{\prime})(g^{\prime})\Phi^{\prime}>dg^{\prime}

for
\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}^{\infty}\otimes V_{\pi^{\prime}}^{\infty}
. Assume
(G^{\prime},G)
and
\pi^{\prime}
are such that the above integral converges for all
\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}
. Let
R
denote the radical of
<,>_{\pi^{\prime}}
. Then
<,>_{\pi^{\prime}}
factors to define a nondegenerate sesquilinear form on

H(\pi^{\prime}):=(\mathcal{Y}^{\infty}\otimes V_{\pi^{\prime}}^{\infty})/R.

It is clear that
\tilde{G}
acting on the first factor of
\mathcal{Y}^{\infty}\otimes V_{\pi^{\prime}}^{\infty}
preserves
<,>_{\pi^{\prime}}
. Hence we have an action of
\tilde{G}
on
V_{\pi^{\prime}}
. The following conjecture is proposed by Li in
[
L90
]
.

\mathbf{Conjecture}
. Suppose
<,>_{\pi^{\prime}}
is defined and nonzero. Then

(i)
<,>_{\pi^{\prime}}
is nonnegative, hence
H(\pi^{\prime})
is unitary.

(ii)
H(\pi^{\prime})
defines an irreducible unitary representation on the completion of
H(\pi^{\prime})
with respect to the inner product given by
(i)
.

(iii) The map
\pi^{\prime}\to H(\pi^{\prime\vee})
, where
\pi^{\prime\vee}
denote the contragredient of
\pi^{\prime}
, coincides with Howe’s duality correspondence.

Now let
(G^{\prime},G)
be either even orthogonal-symplectic or unitary dual pair. Let
\pi
be an irreducible smooth representation of
G^{\prime}
with its contragredient
\pi^{\vee}
. Let’s ignore the splitting of
G^{\prime}\times G
inside
\tilde{Sp}(F)
for simplicity. Choose a nonzero element
\ell\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G^{\prime}(W)\times G^{\prime}(W)\times G(V)}(\omega\otimes\bar{\omega}\otimes\pi\otimes\pi^{\vee},{\mathbb{C}})
. Define

R:=\{\varphi\otimes v^{\vee}\in\omega\otimes\pi^{\vee}:\ell(\varphi\otimes\varphi^{\prime}\otimes v\otimes v^{\vee})=0\ \ \forall\varphi^{\prime}\in\bar{\omega},v\in\pi\}

be the radical of
\ell
. Put
H_{\ell,\psi}(\pi):=(\omega\otimes\pi^{\vee})/R
.
The main result of the note is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

H_{\ell,\psi}(\pi)
is isomorphic to the small theta lift
\theta_{\psi}(\pi)
.

\bullet
We have to restrict to even orthogonal-symplectic and unitary daul pairs since in the proof we used a result of Droschl(
[
D23
]
), which is proved only for these dual pairs. Our method works in principal for other dual pairs as well once Droschl’s result is available.

\bullet
In Li’s conjecture, the sesquilinear form
<,>_{\pi^{\prime}}
is an example of
\ell
in the above theorem. Thus our result can be viewed as an extension of part (ii) and (iii) in Li’s conjecture to general irreducible smooth representations.

E

F

F

\psi

V

W
be vector spaces over
E
equipped with nondegenerate sesquilinear forms

(\cdot,\cdot):V\times V\to E\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ <\cdot,\cdot>:W\times W\to E

of opposite signs. Put

m=\dim V\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ n=\dim W.

(G^{\prime}(W),G(V))

Let
\boldsymbol{\chi}=(\chi_{V},\chi_{W})
be a pair of characters of
E^{\times}
as in section 3.2 in
[
GI14
]
. Associated to these datum, there is a splitting

\iota=\iota_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}:G(W)\times H(V)\to Mp(\mathbb{W}),

where
\mathbb{W}=V\otimes_{E}W
.

Let
\omega_{\psi}
be the Weil representation of
Mp(\mathbb{W})
. The above splitting then induces a representation, denoted as
\omega_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}
, of
G(W)\times H(V)
. If
\pi
is an irreducible smooth (genuine) representation of
G^{\prime}(W)
, the maximal
\pi
-isotypic quotient of
\omega_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}
is of the form
\pi\otimes\Theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)
, where
\Theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)
is a smooth representation of
H(V)
. We will call
\Theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)
as the big theta lift of
\pi
. It is always of finite length. We use
\theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)
to denote the maximal semisimple quotient of
\Theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)
, and call it small theta lift of
\pi
.

The following fundamental result, called Howe duality, was conjectured by Howe (
[
How79
,
How89
]
). It was proved by Waldspuger (
[
Wal90
]
) when
p\neq 2
, and in full generality by Gan-Takeda (
[
GT16
]
) and Gan-Sun (
[
GS17
]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

\theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)
is either zero or irreducible. Moreover, if for irreducible smooth representations
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
of
G^{\prime}(W)
, we have
\theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi_{1})\cong\theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi_{2})
, then
\pi_{1}\cong\pi_{2}
.

Let
W_{-}
denote the vector space
W
over
E
equipped with form
-<\cdot,\cdot>
. Let
\textbf{W}=W\oplus W_{-}
. Use
G’(W)
to denote the isometry group of
W
, and we have an embedding

i:G^{\prime}(W)\times G^{\prime}(W)\to\textbf{G(W)}.

Note that we have

\omega_{V,\textbf{W},\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\circ i\cong\omega_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes(\bar{\omega}_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\cdot\chi_{V})

as representations of
G^{\prime}(W)\times G^{\prime}(W)
.

Consider the following see-saw diagram

G’(W)
\textstyle{G(V)\times G(V)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{G^{\prime}(W)\times G^{\prime}(W)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Delta G(V)}

Then the see-saw identity says that

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(V)}(\Theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)\otimes\Theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\bar{\psi}}(\pi^{\vee}\chi_{V}),\chi_{W})

(2.1)

\displaystyle\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G^{\prime}(W)\times G^{\prime}(W)}(\Theta_{V,\textbf{W},\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\chi_{W}),\pi\otimes\pi^{\vee}\chi_{V}).

Inside
W
, set

W^{\triangle}=\{(w,w)\in\textbf{W}:w\in W\},\ \ \ \ W^{\nabla}=\{(w,-w)\in\textbf{W}:w\in W\}.

Then
W^{\triangle}
is a maximal isotropic subspace, and we have a complete polarization

\textbf{W}=W^{\triangle}\oplus W^{\nabla}.

The stabilizer of
W^{\triangle}
in
G’(W)
is a Siegel parabolic subgroup
\textbf{P}=M_{\textbf{P}}U_{\textbf{P}}
. For
s\in{\textbf{C}}
and a character
\chi
of
E^{\times}
, let

I_{\textbf{P}}^{\textbf{G'}}(s,\chi)={\mathrm{Ind}}_{\textbf{P}}^{\textbf{G'(W)}}(\chi|\cdot|_{E}^{s})

denote the normalized induced representation of
\textbf{G'}=\textbf{G'(W)}
, where
\chi|\cdot|^{s}
is a character of
M_{\textbf{P}}
via

\textstyle{M_{\textbf{P}}=GL(W^{\triangle})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\det}
\textstyle{E^{\times}}
and is extended to
P
with
U_{\textbf{P}}
acting trivially.

The Weil representation
\omega_{V,\textbf{W},\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}
of
\textbf{G'(W)}\times G(V)
is realized on
{\mathcal{S}}(V\otimes W^{\nabla})
, here
{}^{\prime}{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
denote the space of Schwartz function space. For
h\in H,\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}(V\otimes W^{\nabla})
, the action of
G(V)
is given by

(\omega_{V,\textbf{W},\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(h))\phi(x)=\chi_{W}(\det h)\phi(h^{-1}x).

For
\textbf{g}\in\textbf{G'(W)}
, put

{\mathcal{F}}_{\phi}(\textbf{g})=(\omega_{V,\textbf{W},\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\textbf{g})\phi)(0).

One can check that
\phi\to{\mathcal{F}}_{\phi}
is a
G’
-equivariant map

\omega_{V,\textbf{W},\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\to I_{\textbf{P}}^{\textbf{G'}}(-\frac{l}{2},\chi_{V}).

Let
R_{\textbf{W},\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(V,\chi_{W})
be the image of this map. The following result is due to Rallis (
[
Ra84
]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

The map
\phi\to{\mathcal{F}}_{\phi}
induces an isomorphism

\Theta_{V,\textbf{W},\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\chi_{W})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}R_{\textbf{W},\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(V,\chi_{W})\subset I_{\textbf{P}}^{\textbf{G'}}(-\frac{l}{2},\chi_{V}).

We have the following multiplicity one result by Droschl (
[
D23
]
).

Proposition 2.3
.

For any irreducible smooth representation
\pi
of
G^{\prime}(W)
, and any character
\chi
of
E^{\times}
, we have

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G^{\prime}(W)\times G^{\prime}(W)}(I_{\textbf{P}}^{\textbf{G}}(s,\chi),\pi\otimes\pi^{\vee}\chi)=1.

Note that

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G^{\prime}(W)\times G^{\prime}(W)\times G(V)}(\omega_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\bar{\omega}_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\pi\chi_{V}^{-1}\otimes\pi^{\vee},\chi_{W})

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(V)}(\Theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)\otimes\Theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\bar{\psi}}(\pi^{\vee}\chi_{V}),\chi_{W})

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Hom}}_{G^{\prime}(W)\times G^{\prime}(W)}(\Theta_{V,\textbf{W},\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\chi_{W}),\pi\otimes\pi^{\vee}\chi_{V}).

By Rallis’ result and the above multiplicity one theorem, the dimension of these Hom spaces is at most one, and it is equal to one precisely when
\Theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)\neq 0
by Lemma 6.1 in
[
GI14
]
.

Take a nonzero element
\ell\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G^{\prime}(W)\times G^{\prime}(W)\times G(V)}(\omega_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\bar{\omega}_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\pi\chi_{V}^{-1}\otimes\pi^{\vee},\chi_{W})
. Let

R:=\{\varphi\otimes v^{\vee}\in\omega_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\pi^{\vee}:\ell(\varphi\otimes\varphi^{\prime}\otimes v\otimes v^{\vee})=0\ \ \forall\varphi^{\prime}\in\bar{\omega}_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi},v\in\pi\chi_{V}^{-1}\}

be the radical of
\ell
. Put
H_{\ell,\psi}(\pi):=(\omega_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\pi^{\vee})/R
.

Theorem 2.4
.

We have

H_{\ell,\psi}(\pi)\cong\theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi).

Proof.

Since

\Theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)=(\omega_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\pi^{\vee})_{G(V)},

it follows that
H_{\ell,\psi}(\pi)
is a quotient of
\Theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)
. Hence it is of finite length. By the Howe duality,
\Theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)
has a unique quotient
\theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)
. Thus
\theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)
is also the unique quotient of
H_{\ell,\psi}(\pi)
. Hence we can write

\theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)=(\omega_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\pi^{\vee})/R_{1},

where
R\subseteq R_{1}\subset\omega_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\pi^{\vee}
.

If
H_{\ell,\psi}(\pi)
is not irreducible, then
R_{1}
will contain
R
properly. Now consider

H_{\ell,\bar{\psi}}(\pi^{\vee}\chi_{V}):=(\bar{\omega}_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\pi\chi_{V}^{-1})/R^{\prime},

where
R^{\prime}\subset\bar{\omega}_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\pi\chi_{V}^{-1}
is defined similarly as
R
. Also
\theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\bar{\psi}}(\pi^{\vee}\chi_{V})
is a quotient of
H_{\ell,\bar{\psi}}(\pi^{\vee}\chi_{V})
and can be written as

\theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\bar{\psi}}(\pi^{\vee}\chi_{V})=(\bar{\omega}_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\pi\chi_{V}^{-1})/R_{1}^{\prime}

with
R^{\prime}\subseteq R_{1}^{\prime}\subset\bar{\omega}_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\pi\chi_{V}^{-1}
.

By Corollary 18.4 in
[
GKT
]
, there is a natural nonzero linear form in

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(V)}(\theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)\otimes\theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\bar{\psi}}(\pi^{\vee}\chi_{V}),\chi_{W}).

View this nonzero linear form as an element in

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(V)}((\omega_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\pi^{\vee})/R_{1}\otimes(\bar{\omega}_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\pi\chi_{V}^{-1})/R_{1}^{\prime},\chi_{W}),

which can be naturally extended to a nonzero liner form in

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(V)}(H_{\ell,\psi}(\pi)\otimes H_{\ell,\bar{\psi}}(\pi^{\vee}\chi_{V}),\chi_{W}).

Extend this nonzero linear form further to an element in

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G(V)}(\Theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)\otimes\Theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\bar{\psi}}(\pi^{\vee}\chi_{V}),\chi_{W}).

This nonzero element will correspond to a nonzero element
\ell^{\prime}
in

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G^{\prime}(W)\times G^{\prime}(W)\times G(V)}(\omega_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\bar{\omega}_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}\otimes\pi\chi_{V}^{-1}\otimes\pi^{\vee},\chi_{W}).

As this Hom space has dimention at most one, there exists a nonzero scalar
\lambda
, such that

\ell=\lambda\ell^{\prime}.

But, by the construction of
\ell^{\prime}
, the radical is
R_{1}
, which is strictly larger than
R
, and we got a contradiction, which will imply that
H_{\ell,\psi}(\pi)
is irreducible.

So
H_{\ell,\psi}(\pi)
is an irreducible quotient of
\Theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)
, it has to be
\theta_{V,W,\boldsymbol{\chi},\psi}(\pi)
by Howe duality, and the conclusion follows.
∎
