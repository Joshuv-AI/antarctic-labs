Solubility for families of norm equations coming from abelian number fields
Mathieu Da Silva

Abstract




Abstract :
For
F\in\mathbb{Z}[s,t]
a binary quadratic form which is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
, and
L
an abelian number field with class number
1
, we obtain the order of magnitude for the number of values
F(s,t)
which are a norm from
L
. Our result relies on the fundamental lemma of sieve theory and on geometry of numbers.

Introduction

1.1 Motivation.
Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be an abelian number field of degree
n\geqslant 2
, and
F\in\mathbb{Z}[s,t]
be a binary form which is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
. This paper is concerned with the local and global solubility of the norm equation

Y_{F,L}:\quad N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\bm{x})=F(s,t)\quad\left(\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n},(s,t)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\right),

where
N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}
is considered as a form over
\mathbb{Q}
after a choice of integral basis
(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n})
of
L
. If
x\in L
has coordinates
\bm{x}:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
in the basis
(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n})
, we write
N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\bm{x})
for
N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(x)
. More precisely, letting

\pi_{F,L}:Y_{F,L}\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}

be the projection on
(s,t)
,
we provide a lower bound for the quantity

N_{F,L}(B):=\#\left\{(s,t)\in\mathbb{A}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\max(|s|,|t|)\leqslant B,\pi_{F,L}^{-1}(\mathbb{Q})\neq\emptyset\right\},

when
\deg F=2
,
\mathcal{O}_{L}
is a principal ideal domain, and
Y_{F,L}
admits a non-trivial solution. If
L
and
K:=\mathbb{Q}[x]/(F(x,1))
are Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
, we also provide an upper bound for the quantity

N_{F,L}^{\mathrm{loc}}(B):=\#\left\{(s,t)\in\mathbb{A}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\max(|s|,|t|)\leqslant B,\pi_{F,L}^{-1}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})\neq\emptyset\right\},

where
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}
denotes the adèle ring of
\mathbb{Q}
. In particular, since
N_{F,L}^{\mathrm{loc}}(B)
is a natural upper bound for
N_{F,L}(B)
, we get that the lower bound obtained for
N_{F,L}(B)
is sharp. Under our assumptions, it yields

N_{F,L}(B)\asymp{B^{2}\over(\log B)^{1-{r\over n}}},

where
r
is the number of irreducible factors of
F
in
L[s,t]
.

A more geometric point of view on general norm equations similar to (
1
) has been studied in
[
27
]
and
[
5
]
. In particular, the Brauer group of such varieties is well understood in this setting. Note that the most recent results from Wei
[
27
]
require
L/\mathbb{Q}
to be abelian.

Our question seems to fall within the scope of the Loughran–Smeets conjecture
[
14
]
. However, some technical assumptions required in that conjecture are not satisfied here, due to the fact that we are counting integral (and not rational) points on the basis. For instance, the morphism
\pi_{F,L}
is not proper, so we cannot directly apply
[
14
, th. 1.5]
to get an upper bound for
N_{F,L}^{\mathrm{loc}}(B)
. Furthermore, our problem does not seem to reduce to a fibration

\pi:X\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}},

where
X
is a smooth variety. Indeed, if
n\neq\deg F
, taking the induced map

Y_{F,L}\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})

changes the form of the fibres, since we need to ensure that
\pi^{-1}([s:t])
is well-defined for
[s:t]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
. We get equations of the form

s^{\deg F}N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\bm{x})=F(s,t)

for which our method fails.

Nevertheless, the computation of the
\Delta
-invariant defined by
[
14
, (3.11)]
, using
[
14
, th 1.5]
in the case of
\pi_{F,L}
. This reveals that estimate (
1
) agrees with the order of magnitude predicted by conjecture
[
14
, 1.6]
. Our result differs from
[
14
, (5.6)]
by a factor
\log B
because we do not get the contribution of the point at infinity. This is proven in §1.2.

Our method and result generalise
[
24
]
, in which a sharp lower bound is obtained for the quantity
N_{F,L}(B)
in the case where
L/\mathbb{Q}
is quadratic. In this case, we recover a conic bundle and a proper model can be constructed, so that
[
24
, th. 1.1]
is relevant with regard to conjecture
[
14
, 1.6]
. For technical reasons, the degree of
F
in
[
24
, th. 1.1]
cannot be strictly greater than
3
. The obstruction to extending this result to
F
of any degree comes from the size of the error term when using the method of Daniel
[
6
]
, which relies on the classical estimates for the lattice counting problem. For the same reasons, allowing
L/\mathbb{Q}
to have any degree
n\geqslant 2
makes our strategy work only in the case
\deg F=2
.

We now state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be an abelian extension of finite degree
n\geqslant 2
and let
F\in\mathbb{Z}[s,t]
be any irreducible binary quadratic form. Assume that

(i)

the ring
\mathcal{O}_{L}
is a principal ideal domain;

(ii)

there exist
(s_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
x\in L
such that
N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(x)=F(s_{0},t_{0})
and
F(s_{0},t_{0})
is coprime to the conductor of
L
.

For
B\geqslant 2
, the quantity
N_{F,L}(B)
defined by
(
1
)
satisfies

N_{F,L}(B)\asymp{B^{2}\over(\log B)^{1-{r\over n}}},

where
r
is the number of irreducible factors of
F
in
L[s,t]
.

Note that in the case
n=2
, we recover
[
24
, th. 1.1]
.

Remark 1.2
.

It is still unknown whether there exist infinitely many algebraic number fields of class number
1

[
20
, chap. I. §6. p37]
. However, explicit examples of such number fields can be found in
[
12
]
.

Example 1.3
.

For instance, take
L:=\mathbb{Q}[x]/(x^{3}-3x-1)
for which
\mathcal{O}_{L}
is a principal ideal domain, and
F(s,t)=s^{2}-2t^{2}
. As an integral basis for
L
, we take
(1,\omega,\omega^{2})
where
\omega
is a root of
x^{3}-3x-1
. We obtain for
\bm{x}=(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})\in\penalty\ \mathbb{Q}^{3}

N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\bm{x})=x_{0}^{3}+x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}-3x_{0}x_{1}^{2}-3x_{1}x_{2}^{2}-3x_{0}x_{2}^{2}+6x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}

and Theorem
1.1
provides

\#\left\{(s,t)\in[-B,B]^{2}:\exists\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Q}^{3},N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\bm{x})=s^{2}-2t^{2}\right\}\asymp{B^{2}\over(\log B)^{2/3}}

as
B
goes to
+\infty
. This set is not empty since
(s,t)=(1,1)
and
(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})=(0,1,1)
are a solution.

We recall that the Hasse norm principle does not hold for abelian extensions in general. However, under the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
, the two quantities
N_{F,L}(B)
and
N^{\mathrm{loc}}_{F,L}(B)
have the same order of magnitude.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a Galois extension of finite degree
n\geqslant 2
and let
F\in\mathbb{Z}[s,t]
be any irreducible binary form such that
K:=\mathbb{Q}[x]/(F(x,1))
is Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then, when
B
goes to
+\infty
, we have

N^{\mathrm{loc}}_{F,L}(B)\ll{B^{2}\over(\log B)^{1-{r\over n}}},

where
r
is the number of irreducible factors of
F
in
L[s,t]
.

This result is proven in section
6
.

Remark 1.5
.

Since
F
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
, we have that
\mathbb{Q}[x]/(F(x,1))
is Galois if and only if
\mathbb{Q}[x]/(F(1,x))
is Galois. Indeed, these two polynomials have the same splitting field since their roots are reciprocals of each other.

Remark 1.6
.

In particular, under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, we have

N^{\mathrm{loc}}_{F,L}(B)\asymp N_{F,L}(B).

Following Odoni
[
21
]
and Browning–Newton
[
1
]
, we expect that

\lim_{B\to+\infty}{N^{\mathrm{loc}}_{F,L}(B)\over N_{F,L}(B)}=\#(\mathbb{Q}^{\ast}\cap N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(C_{L}))/N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}^{*})>0

where
(\mathbb{Q}^{\ast}\cap N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(C_{L}))/N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}^{\ast})
is called the knot group of
L/\mathbb{Q}
. This does not prove any kind of Hasse norm principle for
L/\mathbb{Q}
, since we make the strong assumption that there exists
(x,(s,t))\in L\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
F(s,t)=N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(x)
and
F(s,t)
is coprime to the conductor of
L
.

1.2 Link with the Loughran–Smeets conjecture.
We now explain how the problem of estimating
N_{F,L}(B)
is linked to
[
14
, conj. 1.6]
. Assume that
L/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois and choose an integral basis
(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n})
of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
. Let
X
be the variety defined by

X:\quad N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\omega_{i}\right)=F(s,t)\quad\subset\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{n+2},

and consider the projection
\pi
on
(s,t)\in\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{2}
. For
B\geqslant 2
, we have

N_{F,L}(B)=\#\left\{(s,t)\in\mathbb{A}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\max(|s|,|t|)\leqslant B:\pi^{-1}(s,t)(\mathbb{Q})\neq\emptyset\right\}.

Since
F
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
, the only codimension one point whose fibre is singular is
(F=0)
and this fibre is given by

V:\quad N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\omega_{i}\right)=0.

The variety
V
is projective, and we can compute the invariant
\delta(V)
as defined in
[
14
, §3.2]
. In our case, the residue field is
\kappa:=\mathrm{Frac}\left(\mathbb{Q}[s,t]/(F(s,t))\right)
and we consider
\kappa^{\prime}:=\kappa L\subset\overline{\kappa}
, the compositum of
\kappa
and
L
. The extension
\kappa^{\prime}/\kappa
is finite, Galois and satisfies (see
[
17
, prop. 3.19]
)

\mathrm{Gal}(\kappa^{\prime}/\kappa)\simeq\mathrm{Gal}(L/L\cap\kappa),

so it is of degree at most
[L:\mathbb{Q}]
, which depends on the number of irreducible factors of
F
in
L[s,t]
. Keeping the notation from
[
14
, §3.2]
, we get

\delta(V)={1\over\#\mathrm{Gal}(\kappa^{\prime}/\kappa)}\#\left\{\gamma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\kappa^{\prime}/\kappa):\begin{tabular}[]{c}$\gamma$ fixes a geometric irreducible component\\
of $V$ of multiplicity $1$\end{tabular}\right\}.

We now compute
\#\mathrm{Gal}(\kappa^{\prime}/\kappa)
. Let
K:=\mathbb{Q}[x]/(F(x,1))
. In the field
\kappa
, we have that
\alpha:=s/t
is algebraic with
F(x,1)
as minimal polynomial. Hence,
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)\simeq K
. Now, writing
s=\alpha t
in
\kappa
leads to
\kappa\simeq\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)(t)
, so that

\kappa\simeq K(t)

is the field of rational functions over
K
. Then, the algebraic elements of
\kappa
are precisely the elements of
K
, so it follows that
L\cap\kappa=L\cap K
and

\mathrm{Gal}(\kappa^{\prime}/\kappa)\simeq\mathrm{Gal}(L/L\cap K).

Now, let

f(s):=F(s,1)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}(s)

be the decomposition of
f
as a product of irreducible factors
f_{i}\in L[s]
.

Since
L/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois, all the polynomials
f_{i}
have the same degree
d
. To see this, write
K=\mathbb{Q}(\beta)
where
\beta
is a root of
f
. Then
\beta
is a root of one of the
f_{i}
, say
f_{1}
. Thus,

L[s]/(f_{1})\simeq L(\beta)=LK

and we get

\deg f_{1}=[L(\beta):L]=[K:K\cap L].

The same argument applies for all the conjugates of
\beta
, which are roots of the polynomials
f_{i}
, hence the result.

Therefore, we get
dr=\deg F=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
with
d=[K:K\cap L]
. It follows from the multiplicativity of the degrees that
r=[L\cap K:\mathbb{Q}]
, so that we finally get

\#\mathrm{Gal}(\kappa^{\prime}/\kappa)=[L:L\cap K]={n\over r},

where
r
is the number of irreducible factors of
F
in
L[s,t]
. We thus recover

\delta(V)={r\over n}

since the only element of
\mathrm{Gal}(\kappa^{\prime}/\kappa)\simeq\mathrm{Gal}(L/L\cap K)
which fixes a geometrically irreducible component of
V
is the identity.

1.3 Generalisations.
In another direction, Odoni
[
21
]
shows that for any algebraic number field
L/\mathbb{Q}
, there exist constants
c_{L}>0
,
\alpha_{L}\in(0,1)
depending only on
L
, and a divisor
\gamma_{L}
of the narrow class group of
L
such that

\#\left\{m\leqslant B:\exists x\in L,\;N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(x)=m\right\}\underset{B\to+\infty}{\sim}\gamma_{L}^{-1}c_{L}{B\over(\log B)^{1-\alpha_{L}}},

and
\alpha_{L}={1\over[L:\mathbb{Q}]}
if
L/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois. In particular, if
L
is abelian, we recover
\alpha_{L}={1\over n}
and the exponent that appears in Theorem
1.1
. This suggests that Theorem
1.1
should still hold in a more general setting. One could indeed expect that for any algebraic number field
L/\mathbb{Q}
and any
F\in\mathbb{Z}[s,t]
of degree
d
irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
, if there exist
(s_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
x\in L
such that
N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(x)=F(s_{0},t_{0})
is invertible modulo the conductor of
L
, then

N_{F,L}(B)\underset{B\to+\infty}{\sim}\gamma_{L}^{-1}c_{F,L}{B^{d}\over(\log B)^{1-r\alpha_{L}}},

where
\gamma_{L}
is a divisor of the narrow class group of
L
,
c_{F,L}>0
depends only on
L
and
F
,
r
is the number of irreducible factors of
F
in
L[s,t]
, and
\alpha_{L}
is as in (
1
).

1.4. Detecting the solubility.
Recall that we defined

r_{L}(k):=\#\{\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{I}_{L}:N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{a})=k\}

and the set

\mathcal{N}_{L}:=\{N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(x):x\in L\}.

We also write
G:=\mathrm{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
and, since
L/\mathbb{Q}
is abelian (see
[
20
, chap. V, theorem 1.10 and chap. VII, §11]
), we can consider
q_{L}
the conductor of
L/\mathbb{Q}
, which is defined as

q_{L}:=\min\left\{q\in\mathbb{N}:L\subset\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})\right\},

where
\zeta_{q}
denotes a primitive
q
-th root of unity. In particular,
G
is a quotient of
(\mathbb{Z}/q_{L}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, so every
\chi\in\widehat{G}:=\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\ast})
is identified with a (not necessarily primitive) Dirichlet character modulo
q_{L}
. For
\chi\in\widehat{G}
, we denote by
q(\chi)
its conductor, which is a divisor of
q_{L}
. Finally, we recall the conductor-discriminant formula
[
20
, chap. VII, 11.9]

\mathrm{disc}(L)=\prod_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}q(\chi).

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be an abelian extension of degree
n\geqslant 2
. A prime
p
ramifies in
L
if and only if
p\mid q_{L}
, and if
p\nmid q_{L}
then we have

\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{N}_{L}}(p)={1\over n}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}\chi(p).

Proof.

Let
p
be a prime ramifying in
L
. Since
q_{L}=\mathrm{lcm}_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}(q(\chi))
, by the conductor-discriminant formula, this is equivalent to
p\mid\mathrm{disc}(L)
. Hence the primes ramifying in
L
are exactly the ones dividing
q_{L}
. Let
p\nmid q_{L}
. By
[
7
, th. 6]
, we know that
\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}\chi(p)=r_{L}(p)\in\{0,n\}
is the number of ideals in
\mathcal{I}_{L}
of norm
p
. Since
\mathcal{O}_{L}
is principal, an integer
k
is the norm of an element of
L
if and only if it is the norm of a fractional ideal. If moreover
k=p
is prime, it is equivalent to being the norm of an ideal in
\mathcal{I}_{L}
, hence the conclusion.
∎

\mathrm{Gal}(LK/L)\simeq\mathrm{Gal}(K/K\cap L),

[LK:L]={[K:\mathbb{Q}]\over[K\cap L:\mathbb{Q}]}={\deg f\over[K\cap L:\mathbb{Q}]}.

Hence,

By

K
satisfies

\rho_{F}^{-}(p)=\begin{cases}\#\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{K}:\mathfrak{p}\mid p\}=[K:\mathbb{Q}]\text{ if }[K_{p}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]=1\\
0\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Therefore, we have

\sum_{p\leqslant B}{\varpi(p)\rho_{F}^{-}(p)\over p}=[K:\mathbb{Q}]\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant B\\
[L_{p}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]=1\\
[K_{p}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]=1\end{subarray}}{1\over p}+O(1).

Since a prime that is unramified in both
L
and
K
splits completely in
K
and
L
if and only if it splits completely in their compositum
KL
(see
[
15
, th. 3.1]
), it follows that

\sum_{p\leqslant B}{\varpi(p)\rho_{F}^{-}(p)\over p}=[K:\mathbb{Q}]\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant B\\
[(LK)_{p}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]=1\end{subarray}}{1\over p}+O(1).

The Chebotarev density theorem
[
22
, th. 3.4]
then yields

\sum_{p\leqslant B}{\varpi(p)\rho_{F}^{-}(p)\over p}={[K:\mathbb{Q}]\over[LK:\mathbb{Q}]}\log_{2}(B)+O(1).

Finally, equality (
1
) ensures that

{[K:\mathbb{Q}]\over[LK:\mathbb{Q}]}={r\over n},

where
r
is the number of irreducible factors of
F
in
L[s,t]
. It follows that

\sum_{p\leqslant B}{\rho_{F}(p)(\varpi(p)-1)\over p^{2}}=-\left(1-{r\over n}\right)\log_{2}B+O(1),

and

\prod_{p\leqslant B}\left(1+{\rho_{F}(p)(\varpi(p)-1)\over p^{2}}\right)\ll(\log B)^{-\left(1-{r\over n}\right)}.

This concludes the proof of Proposition
1.4
.
∎
