Unsteady phase waves in the 1D swarmalator model with inertia
Kevin O’Keeffe

Abstract
We study a one-dimensional swarmalator model with inertia. Previous studies have focused almost exclusively on the overdamped limit. We find inertia introduces a new unsteady collective state in which the rainbow order parameters undergo multiharmonic oscillations. This “thrashing” phase wave bifurcates from the model’s static phase wave state through a subcritical Hopf bifurcation that coincides with a saddle–node of limit cycles. The wave itself exists in clockwise and counterclockwise symmetric pairs. For small populations we observe attractor switching between these chiral states, while for larger systems the dynamics settle onto a single branch.

Introduction

Yet one effect remains unexplored: inertia. Most swarmalator models assume overdamped dynamics for the sake of simplicity. We know from coupled-oscillator theory, however, that inertia can qualitatively change collective behavior. The second-order Kuramoto model, for example, exhibits hysteresis, bistability, and abrupt synchronization transitions
Tanaka et al. 1997
;
Acebrón et al. 2005
. How does inertia modify swarmalator dynamics?

In this paper, we address this question using a simple model of identical swarmalators moving on a 1D domain which is especially tractable
O’Keeffe et al. 2022
;
Yoon et al. 2022
. We find that inertia fundamentally alters the phase-wave dynamics, giving rise to a new unsteady phase in which the rainbow order parameters undergo mixed–mode oscillations. This “thrashing” phase wave emerges from the static phase wave through a subcritical Hopf bifurcation that coincides with a saddle–node of limit cycles. Inertia’s main dynamical effect is thus that it generates unsteadiness; the attractors of the non-inertial swarmalator model, recall, are all static
O’Keeffe et al. 2022
.

Model

We study the 1D swarmalator model
O’Keeffe et al. 2022

\displaystyle m\ddot{x}_{i}+\dot{x}_{i}

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}^{\prime}+\frac{J^{\prime}}{N}\sum_{j}\sin(x_{j}-x_{i})\cos(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i}),

(1)

\displaystyle m\ddot{\theta}_{i}+\dot{\theta}_{i}

\displaystyle=\nu_{i}^{\prime}+\frac{K^{\prime}}{N}\sum_{j}\sin(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i})\cos(x_{j}-x_{i}),

(2)

(\omega_{i}^{\prime},\nu_{i}^{\prime})

\displaystyle m\ddot{\xi}_{i}+\dot{\xi}_{i}

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}+Kr\sin(\phi-\xi_{i})+Js\sin(\psi-\eta_{i}),

(3)

\displaystyle m\ddot{\eta}_{i}+\dot{\eta}_{i}

\displaystyle=\nu_{i}+Jr\sin(\phi-\xi_{i})+Ks\sin(\psi-\eta_{i}),

(4)

where

\displaystyle U=re^{i\phi}:=\langle e^{i\xi}\rangle,\qquad V=se^{i\psi}:=\langle e^{i\eta}\rangle,

and
(\omega,\nu)=(\omega^{\prime}+\nu^{\prime},\,\omega^{\prime}-\nu^{\prime})

Next we analyze the stability of each state.

IV
Analysis

IV.1
Sync

Without loss of generality, we take the synchronized fixed point to be

\xi_{i}^{\ast}=0,\qquad\eta_{i}^{\ast}=0,

(9)

for all
i=1,\dots,N
. At this point,

r=s=1.

(10)

To linearize the inertial dynamics, write

\xi_{i}=\delta\xi_{i},\qquad\eta_{i}=\delta\eta_{i},

(11)

with
|\delta\xi_{i}|,|\delta\eta_{i}|\ll 1
. Since

U=\langle e^{i\xi}\rangle\approx 1+i\langle\delta\xi\rangle,\qquad V=\langle e^{i\eta}\rangle\approx 1+i\langle\delta\eta\rangle,

we have, to linear order,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}(Ue^{-i\xi_{i}})

\displaystyle\approx\langle\delta\xi\rangle-\delta\xi_{i},

(12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}(Ve^{-i\eta_{i}})

\displaystyle\approx\langle\delta\eta\rangle-\delta\eta_{i}.

(13)

Therefore the linearized equations are

\displaystyle m\,\delta\ddot{\xi}_{i}+\delta\dot{\xi}_{i}

\displaystyle=K\big(\langle\delta\xi\rangle-\delta\xi_{i}\big)+J\big(\langle\delta\eta\rangle-\delta\eta_{i}\big),

(14)

\displaystyle m\,\delta\ddot{\eta}_{i}+\delta\dot{\eta}_{i}

\displaystyle=J\big(\langle\delta\xi\rangle-\delta\xi_{i}\big)+K\big(\langle\delta\eta\rangle-\delta\eta_{i}\big).

(15)

It is convenient to separate the mean modes from the shape modes. Define

\bar{\xi}:=\langle\delta\xi\rangle,\qquad\bar{\eta}:=\langle\delta\eta\rangle.

(16)

Averaging the linearized equations over
i
gives

\displaystyle m\ddot{\bar{\xi}}+\dot{\bar{\xi}}

\displaystyle=0,

(17)

\displaystyle m\ddot{\bar{\eta}}+\dot{\bar{\eta}}

\displaystyle=0.

(18)

Hence the mean sector contributes the eigenvalues

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=0,

(19)

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=-1/m.

(20)

The zero eigenvalue is the neutral mode associated with rotational invariance, while
-1/m
is a damped center-of-mass velocity mode.

Next define the zero-mean shape perturbations

\tilde{\xi}_{i}:=\delta\xi_{i}-\bar{\xi},\qquad\tilde{\eta}_{i}:=\delta\eta_{i}-\bar{\eta},

(21)

which satisfy
\langle\tilde{\xi}\rangle=\langle\tilde{\eta}\rangle=0
. Their equations are

\displaystyle m\ddot{\tilde{\xi}}_{i}+\dot{\tilde{\xi}}_{i}

\displaystyle=-K\tilde{\xi}_{i}-J\tilde{\eta}_{i},

(22)

\displaystyle m\ddot{\tilde{\eta}}_{i}+\dot{\tilde{\eta}}_{i}

\displaystyle=-J\tilde{\xi}_{i}-K\tilde{\eta}_{i}.

(23)

These decouple under the change of variables

p_{i}:=\tilde{\xi}_{i}+\tilde{\eta}_{i},\qquad q_{i}:=\tilde{\xi}_{i}-\tilde{\eta}_{i},

(24)

giving

\displaystyle m\ddot{p}_{i}+\dot{p}_{i}+(J+K)p_{i}

\displaystyle=0,

(25)

\displaystyle m\ddot{q}_{i}+\dot{q}_{i}+(K-J)q_{i}

\displaystyle=0.

(26)

Thus the remaining eigenvalues are

\displaystyle\lambda_{p,\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{\,1-4m(J+K)\,}}{2m},

(27)

\displaystyle\lambda_{q,\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{\,1+4m(J-K)\,}}{2m}.

(28)

For the scaling used in most of our numerics,
J=1
, this becomes

\displaystyle\lambda_{p,\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{\,1-4m(1+K)\,}}{2m},

(29)

\displaystyle\lambda_{q,\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{\,1+4m(1-K)\,}}{2m}.

(30)

We now determine the stability condition. The
p
-sector is stable whenever

J+K>0,

(31)

which is automatically satisfied in the synchronized regime. The potentially dangerous mode is the
q
-sector. This mode is stable iff

K-J>0.

(32)

Hence the sync state is linearly stable for

\boxed{K>K_{c}=J.}

(33)

In the normalization
J=1
, this reduces to

\boxed{K>K_{c}=1.}

(34)

Thus, just as in the non-inertial model, the stability threshold of the synchronized state is independent of the inertia
m
. The mass affects only the transient approach to sync: for sufficiently large
m
, the relaxation becomes oscillatory (Figure
3
top row), but the location of the transition remains unchanged.

IV.2
Async

Consider the phase-space density

f(\xi,\eta,u,v,t),

which evolves according to the continuity equation

\partial_{t}f+u\partial_{\xi}f+v\partial_{\eta}f+\partial_{u}(F_{\xi}f)+\partial_{v}(F_{\eta}f)=0,

(35)

where

\displaystyle F_{\xi}

\displaystyle=\frac{-u+K\,\mathrm{Im}(Ue^{-i\xi})+J\,\mathrm{Im}(Ve^{-i\eta})}{m},

(36)

\displaystyle F_{\eta}

\displaystyle=\frac{-v+J\,\mathrm{Im}(Ue^{-i\xi})+K\,\mathrm{Im}(Ve^{-i\eta})}{m}.

(37)

The order parameters are

U=\iint e^{i\xi}f\,d\xi d\eta dudv,\qquad V=\iint e^{i\eta}f\,d\xi d\eta dudv.

The asynchronous state corresponds to a distribution uniform in

(\xi,\eta)
with zero velocities,

f_{0}(\xi,\eta,u,v)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\delta(u)\delta(v).

(38)

We perturb this state as

f=f_{0}+\epsilon f_{1},\qquad 0<\epsilon\ll 1,

and assume normal-mode perturbations of the form

f_{1}\propto e^{\lambda t+i\xi}.

This mode corresponds to the first Fourier harmonic that generates the
order parameter
U
.

Linearizing the continuity equation and integrating over velocities yields

m\lambda^{2}a_{10}+\lambda a_{10}=\frac{K}{2}a_{10},

(39)

where
a_{10}
is the amplitude of the
(1,0)
Fourier mode of the spatial density.
An identical equation is obtained for the
(0,1)
mode associated with
V
.
Importantly, the cross-coupling
J
does not appear because the
corresponding terms vanish when projected onto the first Fourier harmonics.

Seeking solutions
a_{10}\propto e^{\lambda t}
gives the characteristic equation

m\lambda^{2}+\lambda-\frac{K}{2}=0,

(40)

with eigenvalues

\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{1+2mK}}{2m}.

(41)

The stability threshold follows immediately. If
K>0
, then

\lambda_{+}>0
and the asynchronous state is unstable.
If
K<0
, both eigenvalues have negative real part and the
state is linearly stable. Hence the critical coupling is

\boxed{K_{c}=0.}

(42)

Thus the stability threshold of the asynchronous state is unchanged by
inertia and is independent of the cross-coupling
J
.
Inertia affects only the transient dynamics (Figure
3
bottom row).

IV.3
Phase wave

Without loss of generality, we consider the
\xi
–wave, for which

\xi_{i}^{\ast}=0,\qquad\eta_{i}^{\ast}=\frac{2\pi i}{N},\qquad i=1,\dots,N.

(43)

In this state,

r=1,\qquad s=0,

(44)

so the swarmalators are perfectly ordered in the
\xi
direction and uniformly
distributed in
\eta
.

We perturb the phase wave as

\xi_{i}=\delta\xi_{i},\qquad\eta_{i}=\eta_{i}^{\ast}+\delta\eta_{i},

(45)

with
|\delta\xi_{i}|,|\delta\eta_{i}|\ll 1
. Expanding the order parameter

U=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{i\xi_{j}}\approx 1+i\langle\delta\xi\rangle,

gives

\mathrm{Im}(Ue^{-i\xi_{i}})\approx\langle\delta\xi\rangle-\delta\xi_{i}.

(46)

Because the base state is uniform in
\eta
, the instability is governed by the
first Fourier harmonic of the
\eta
distribution. We therefore write

\delta\xi_{i}=a\cos\eta_{i}^{\ast}+b\sin\eta_{i}^{\ast},\qquad\delta\eta_{i}=c\cos\eta_{i}^{\ast}+d\sin\eta_{i}^{\ast}.

(47)

Substituting these expressions into the linearized equations and retaining only
first-harmonic terms yields two identical
2\times 2
systems. For the cosine
sector we obtain

\displaystyle m\ddot{a}+\dot{a}

\displaystyle=-Ka+\frac{J}{2}c,

(48)

\displaystyle m\ddot{c}+\dot{c}

\displaystyle=-Ja+\frac{K}{2}c,

(49)

while the sine sector obeys the same equations with
(a,c)
replaced by
(b,d)
.

Seeking normal modes
a,c\propto e^{\lambda t}
gives

\begin{pmatrix}m\lambda^{2}+\lambda+K&-J/2\\
J&m\lambda^{2}+\lambda-K/2\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a\\
c\end{pmatrix}=0.

(50)

Nontrivial solutions require

\left(m\lambda^{2}+\lambda+K\right)\left(m\lambda^{2}+\lambda-\frac{K}{2}\right)+\frac{J^{2}}{2}=0.

(51)

Defining

x:=m\lambda^{2}+\lambda,

(52)

this can be written compactly as

2x^{2}+Kx+(J^{2}-K^{2})=0.

(53)

To determine when the phase wave loses stability we look for a Hopf
bifurcation by setting

\lambda=i\omega,\qquad\omega>0.

(54)

Then

x=-m\omega^{2}+i\omega.

Substituting into Eq. (
53
) and separating real and imaginary
parts gives

\displaystyle-4m\omega^{3}+K\omega

\displaystyle=0,

(55)

\displaystyle 2\left(m^{2}\omega^{4}-\omega^{2}\right)-Km\omega^{2}+(J^{2}-K^{2})

\displaystyle=0.

(56)

From the imaginary part the nontrivial Hopf frequency is

\omega_{c}^{2}=\frac{K}{4m}.

(57)

Substituting this into the real part yields the Hopf boundary

\boxed{m_{H}(K)=\frac{4K}{8J^{2}-9K^{2}}.}

(58)

Equivalently, for fixed
m
, the critical coupling satisfies

9mK_{c}^{2}+4K_{c}-8mJ^{2}=0,

(59)

so that the positive branch is

\boxed{K_{H}(m)=\frac{2\!\left(\sqrt{1+18m^{2}J^{2}}-1\right)}{9m}.}

(60)

In the normalization
J=1
this becomes

K_{H}(m)=\frac{2\!\left(\sqrt{1+18m^{2}}-1\right)}{9m}.

(61)

This prediction agrees well with numerics. For example, at
m=0.5
and
J=1

the formula gives

K_{H}\approx 0.598,

matching the transition observed in Fig.
2
.

The Hopf bifurcation is subcritical. We confirmed this by computing the first
Lyapunov coefficient
l_{1}
numerically for
N=3,\dots,10
and in all cases
found
l_{1}>0
. Moreover, when sweeping

through the Hopf point we observe
discontinuous jumps in both oscillation amplitude and frequency

oscillation amplitude would grow continuously from zero, and is consistent
with a nearby saddle–node of limit cycles. Numerically we find that the
critical parameter value

appears to coincide with the Hopf point

. We also scanned for bistability between the static and
thrashing phase waves but found none.

Thrashing phase wave

0<K\lesssim K_{H}(m)
. The order parameters

and

V
Discussion

We studied the 1D swarmalator model with inertia. The main
result is simple: inertia leaves the async and sync stability thresholds untouched, but
destabilizes the phase wave. A thrashing phase wave is born, which has multiharmonic oscillations and attractor switching. These findings may be relevant for scientist or engineers exploiting swarmalators for applications
O’Keeffe and Bettstetter 2019
.

What’s interesting is that inertia-induced unsteadiness does
not
occur in regular oscillators with identical natural frequencies. There, only the static sync and async are realized
Tanaka et al. 1997
;
Acebrón et al. 2005
(we emphasive this is when the oscillators are
identical
). Here, the interplay between the sync and self-assembly allows for richer dynamical coupling to the inertial effects.

Future work could add more realism into the model, such as distributed natural frequencies, random couplings, delayed communications, or thermal noise. Exploring the model in two or three spatial dimensions
O’Keeffe et al. 2024
may also be fruitful.
