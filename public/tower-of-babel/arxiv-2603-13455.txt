Strangeness neutrality and the QCD phase diagram
Wei-jie Fu, Chuang Huang, Jan M. Pawlowski, Fabian Rennecke, Rui Wen, Shi Yin

Abstract
We map out the phase structure of N f = 2 + 1 N_{f}=2+1 flavour QCD at strangeness neutrality with functional QCD. We find a critical end point at ( T CEP , μ B , CEP ) | n S = 0 = ( 92,696 ) (T_{\rm CEP},\mu_{B,{\rm CEP}})|_{n_{S}=0}=(92,696) MeV. The computation is done with the functional renormalisation group, and we systematically improve on previous works, hence reducing the systematic error significantly. Our results pass relevant QCD benchmarks: they agree well with and corroborate the QCD phase structure from functional QCD results at vanishing strangeness chemical potential. Moreover, they agree well with lattice QCD results at vanishing chemical potential. Specifically, the ratio of the second order curvature coefficient κ 2 \kappa_{2} agrees with that obtained from lattice computations, κ 2 ​ ( n S = 0 ) / κ 2 ​ ( μ S = 0 ) = 0.897 ​ ( 20 ) \kappa_{2}(n_{S}=0)/\kappa_{2}(\mu_{S}=0)=0.897(20) .



Introduction.–

The phase structure of QCD has attracted a lot of attention in recent years. Particular attention has been devoted to the regime with the onset of new phases (ONP), either a critical end point that hallmarks a first order regime or regimes with instabilities or moats
Fu et al. 2020a
;
Pisarski and Rennecke 2021
;
Pawlowski et al. 2025
. Both experiment and theory have made great efforts and significant progress in the last decades. The Beam Energy Scan (BES) program at the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) covers the region of collision energies from 200 GeV to 3.0 GeV, including the onset regime of new phases, see
Fu et al. 2020a
;
Gao and Pawlowski 2021
;
Gunkel and Fischer 2021
;
Pawlowski et al. 2025
.

Cumulants of net-proton distributions, e.g.
Adam et al. 2021
;
Abdallah et al. 2021a
;
Abdallah et al. 2021b
;
Abdallah et al. 2022
;
Aboona et al. 2023
;
Aboona et al. 2025
, fluctuations of strangeness and electric charge, e.g.
Adamczyk et al. 2018
;
Pandav 2021
, correlations of conserved charges
Adam et al. 2019
, the light nuclei production
Abdulhamid et al. 2023
;
Abdulhamid et al. 2024
, and the spin alignment
Abdallah et al. 2023
, could provide signals for the existence of the CEP, see also
Luo and Xu 2017
;
Pandav et al. 2022
;
Aparin 2022
;
Zhang et al. 2026
for reviews of recent results by the STAR collaboration. Furthermore, several other heavy-ion experiments are planned and will be devoted to further explore the QCD phase diagram at high baryon chemical potential, such as the CBM
Ablyazimov et al. 2017
and CEE+
Ruan et al. 2018
experiments.

The theoretical description of the QCD phase structure has also advanced significantly. Recently, the large density regime with
\sqrt{s}\lesssim 10
GeV has been mapped out with functional 2+1 flavour QCD,
Fu et al. 2020a
;
Gao and Pawlowski 2021
;
Gunkel and Fischer 2021
;
Pawlowski et al. 2025
. These studies either used identical quark chemical potentials,
\mu_{q}=\mu_{B}/3
(
\mu_{S}=0
), or only light quark chemical potentials
\mu_{l}=\mu_{B}/3
and
\mu_{s}=0
. Importantly, these computations have narrowed down the location of the CEP in these cases, or more generally, that of the onset of new phases:
(T_{\rm CEP},\mu_{B,{\rm CEP}})\approx(110\pm 10,630\pm 30)
MeV. This corresponds to collision energies of about 3-4 GeV per nucleon, putting it within reach of fixed-target heavy-ion collision experiments.
In addition, thermodynamic observables and fluctuations of conserved charges are available from functional methods
Fu et al. 2021
;
Fu et al. 2025a
;
Lu et al. 2024
;
Lu et al. 2025
;
Chen et al. 2025
;
Lu et al. 2026
at finite density, providing guidance for future heavy-ion experiments;
see
Fischer and Pawlowski 2026
for an overview of recent developments.
Owing to the sign problem, finite
\mu_{B}
is not directly accessible by lattice QCD. Still, estimates of the CEP location based on extrapolations of lattice data agree with the predictions from functional methods. Recent estimates are obtained from the reconstruction of Yang-Lee edge singularities
Basar 2024
;
Clarke et al. 2024
;
Adam et al. 2025
and contours of constant entropy density
Shah et al. 2024
;
Borsanyi et al. 2025
.

In this work, we provide the first functional QCD analysis of the QCD phase structure at strangeness neutrality,
n_{S}=0
. Specifically, we accommodate the dynamics of the scalar and pseudoscalar nonets, hence including open strange mesons such as the kaons and the flavour-octet
\eta
-meson. This allows us to map out the chiral crossover line, which ends in a chiral CEP at baryon chemical potential
\mu_{B}\approx 700
MeV.

Functional QCD at strangeness neutrality.–

The computations and results in the present work are achieved within the fRG-approach to QCD at finite temperature and density, set up in
Fu et al. 2020a
and extended in
Fu et al. 2025b
;
Ihssen et al. 2024
;
Pawlowski et al. 2025
. In these works the effective action of QCD is obtained by solving its flow equation from a large infrared cutoff scale
k=\Lambda_{\textrm{UV}}
with a perturbative effective action
\Gamma_{\Lambda}

down to vanishing cutoff,
k=0
with the full quantum effective action
\Gamma=\Gamma_{k=0}
. The discussion of our expansion scheme and details on the flow equations for correlation functions and their solutions is deferred to the Supplement.

The effective action of QCD, evaluated on the solution of the equations of motion, is simply the grand potential
\Omega(T,\mu_{B},\mu_{S})
. Here,
\mu_{B}
is the baryon chemical potential and the strangeness chemical potential
\mu_{S}
are given by

\displaystyle\mu_{l}=\frac{\mu_{B}}{3}\,,\qquad\mu_{S}=\mu_{l}-\mu_{s}\,,

(1)

with the quark chemical potentials
\mu_{q_{i}}
with
q=(u,d,s)
.
In
1
we have identified the
u,d
chemical potentials
\mu_{u}=\mu_{d}=\mu_{l}
, which implies a vanishing electric charge chemical potential. For more details see the Supplement.

The grand potential
\Omega
is directly related to the effective action, evaluated on the equations of motion,

\displaystyle\Omega(T,\mu_{B},\mu_{S})=\frac{T}{V}\Bigl[\Gamma(T,\mu_{B},\mu_{S})-\Gamma(0,0,0)\Bigr]\,,

(2)

with the temperature
T
and spatial volume
V
. The generalised susceptibilities are defined as

\displaystyle\chi_{ij}^{BS}=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{i+j}(p/T^{4})}{\partial(\mu_{B}/T)^{i}\partial(\mu_{S}/T)^{j}}\,,

(3)

with the pressure
p=-\Omega
in the absence of volume fluctuations.
In particular, the strangeness density reads

\displaystyle n_{S}=\chi_{1}^{S}\,.

(4)

The initial state of a heavy ion collision (HIC) is strangeness neutral,
n_{S}=0
. This property persists throughout the timeline of a HIC as strangeness is a conserved charge in QCD. For any given
T
and
\mu_{B}
, this implies a certain strangeness chemical potential,

\displaystyle\mu_{S0}=\mu_{S}(T,\mu_{B})\,,\quad\textrm{with}\quad n_{S}(T,\mu_{B},\mu_{S0})=0\,.

(5)

The resulting
T
–
\mu_{B}
phase diagram is a hypersurface in the space spanned by
\{T,\mu_{B},\mu_{S}\}
. In
Figure

1
, we show our results for
\mu_{S0}
as a function of
\mu_{B}
for various temperatures (left), and a heat map of the ratio
\mu_{S0}/\mu_{B}
in the
T-\mu_{B}
plane (right).

, and lattice QCD results from the Wuppertal-Budapest collaboration
Borsanyi et al. 2010

These results for
\mu_{S0}
are obtained within a numerical resolution of
n_{S}=0
in
5
. Alternatively one may use

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mu_{S}}{\partial\mu_{B}}=-\frac{\chi^{BS}_{11}(T,\mu_{B},\mu_{S})}{\chi^{S}_{2}(T,\mu_{B},\mu_{S})}\,,

(6)

see e.g.
Bellwied et al. 2015
;
Fu et al. 2019
;
Wen and Fu 2021
.
The second-order susceptibilities
\chi^{BS}_{11}
and
\chi^{S}_{2}
are defined in
3
. Results for
6
as well as a comparison of general susceptibilities with the respective lattice results will be done in
Fu et al. 2026
. Here we only note that
6
provides us with a direct relation between strangeness neutrality and baryon-strangeness correlations,
\chi^{BS}_{11}/\chi^{S}_{2}
. The latter are sensitive probes of the nature of QCD matter
Koch et al. 2005
. Hence,
6
carries the relation between quark number conservation and the QCD phase structure
Fu et al. 2019
.

This allows us to discuss some important properties of the curves
\mu_{S0}(\mu_{B})
shown in the left panel in
Figure

1
:

For high temperatures and baryon chemical potential, the system approaches the free quark gas limit. There, strangeness is dominated by strange quarks and hence we find
\mu_{s}=\mu_{B}/3-\mu_{S}=0
.

For low temperatures and baryon chemical potential, strangeness is dominated by open strange mesons, making the baryon-strangeness correlation in
6
very small. There,
\mu_{S}
is much smaller than
\mu_{B}/3
.

As the baryon chemical potential rises, strange baryons become increasingly relevant, leading to increasing
\chi^{BS}_{11}
and, consequently, larger
\mu_{S}
. In the deconfined phase, the free quark limit is approached.

We close this assessment of strangeness neutrality with a discussion of the lines of constant ratios
\mu_{S0}/\mu_{B}
. They are shown in the right panel of
Figure

1
, starting at
T(\mu_{B}=0)=150,160,170
MeV. Our results are in quantitative agreement with the lattice QCD results of
Borsányi et al. 2025
for small baryon chemical potential for
\mu_{B}/T\lesssim 4/3
. Note that this estimate has to be taken with a grain of salt as for different
T(\mu_{B}=0)
we expect different convergence ranges for the Taylor expansion underlying the lattice results. In any case, the lattice shows an increasingly worse convergence for
\mu_{B}/T\gtrsim 4/3
as well as an increasing deviation from our results. At present, the results achieved here are the only (functional) QCD results at strangeness neutrality for large baryon chemical potential.

Chiral condensates and thermal susceptibilities.–

We proceed with the preparation of the computation of the chiral phase structure of QCD at strangeness neutrality. The chiral crossover temperature is computed from the thermal susceptibilities of variants of the chiral condensate. The chiral condensate of a given quark flavour
q=(l,s)
and
l=(u,d)
is defined as

\displaystyle\Delta_{q_{i}}=m_{q_{i}}^{0}\frac{T}{V}\int_{x}\langle{\bar{q}}_{i}(x)q_{i}(x)\rangle\,,

(7)

where no sum over the flavour index
i
is taken. If computed from its fRG-flow,
7
is finite as the flow is finite. Put differently, the renormalisation is built-in by construction. Moreover, in the fRG-approach with emergent composites, the chiral condensates are proportional to the solutions
\sigma^{\textrm{EoM}},\sigma_{s}^{\textrm{EoM}}
of the equation of motion for the scalar modes:
\Delta_{l}\propto\sigma^{\textrm{EoM}}
with the
light scalar mode
\sigma^{\textrm{EoM}}\propto\langle\bar{l}l\rangle_{r}
, and
\Delta_{s}\propto\sigma^{\textrm{EoM}}_{s}
with the strange scalar mode
\sigma^{\textrm{EoM}}_{s}\propto\langle\bar{s}s\rangle_{r}
. Here the subscript
r
indicates that these are renormalised expectation values. For a comprehensive discussion see
Fu et al. 2020a
.

On the lattice and in other functional approaches,
7
has to be renormalised. This is commonly done or rather circumvented by computing either the
renormalised
or the
reduced
condensates. For the sake of a direct comparison we shall also compute these condensates. The renormalised condensates are defined as

\displaystyle\Delta_{q_{i},R}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{R}}[\Delta_{q_{i}}(T,\mu_{q})-\Delta_{q_{i}}(0,0)]\,,

(8)

where
\mathcal{N}_{R}
is a normalisation constant at our disposal. The reduced condensate is defined by

\displaystyle\Delta_{l,s}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\Delta_{l}(T,\mu_{B})-\Big(\frac{m_{l}^{0}}{m_{s}^{0}}\Big)^{2}\Delta_{s}(T,\mu_{B})}{\Delta_{l}(0,0)-\Big(\frac{m_{l}^{0}}{m_{s}^{0}}\Big)^{2}\Delta_{s}(0,0)}\,,

(9)

which eliminates the normalisation
{\cal N}_{R}
and the volume factor in
7
.

In
Figure

2
, we show both the renormalised light chiral condensate
\Delta_{l,R}
and the reduced condensate
\Delta_{l,s}
as functions of temperature at vanishing and finite baryon chemical potential and strangeness neutrality,
n_{S}=0
. For vanishing chemical potential,
\mu_{B}=0=\mu_{S}
, we compare our results with lattice calculations
Borsanyi et al. 2010
. We also show the results for
\mu_{S}=0
from the previous functional QCD work
Fu et al. 2020a
.
The comparison documents the quantitative agreement of the present results for the chiral condensates with lattice and the previous functional QCD results at
\mu_{B}=0
.

In the following we consider the chiral crossover temperature
T_{c}
given by the peak position of the thermal susceptibility of the renormalised light chiral condensate
\partial\Delta_{l,R}/\partial T
. For a vanishing baryon chemical potential,
\mu_{B}=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle T_{c}=157.5(5)\,\textrm{MeV}\,.

(10)

This is in quantitative agreement with the lattice results
156.5\pm 1.5
MeV (HotQCD collaboration)
Bazavov et al. 2019
,
158\pm 0.6
MeV (Wuppertal-Budapest collaboration)
Borsanyi et al. 2020
and with the other state-of-the-art functional QCD results
Fu et al. 2020a
;
Gao and Pawlowski 2021
;
Gunkel and Fischer 2021
;
Pawlowski et al. 2025
.

In summary, the present functional QCD approach at strangeness neutrality, based on the fRG-approach with emergent composites used successfully at
\mu_{S}=0
,
Fu et al. 2020a
;
Ihssen et al. 2024
;
Pawlowski et al. 2025
, passes all available benchmark tests at
\mu_{B}=0
.

We are now in the position to map out the chiral phase structure of QCD at strangeness neutrality. For that purpose we have computed the crossover temperatures for
n_{s}=0

\mu_{S}=0
, which can be compared with

Pawlowski et al. 2025

We start the discussion of the results in the low density regime, and specifically with the
\mu_{B}
-dependence of the crossover line. It has been already shown in

\kappa_{2},\kappa_{4}

\displaystyle\frac{T_{c}(\mu_{B})}{T_{c}}=1-\kappa_{2}\left(\frac{\mu_{B}}{T_{c}}\right)^{2}-\kappa_{4}\left(\frac{\mu_{B}}{T_{c}}\right)^{4}+\cdots\,,

(11)

\kappa_{2}

global

The functional QCD results for the curvature coefficient
\kappa_{2}
agree very well with each other within the systematic error estimate. The
\kappa_{4}
’s have a large numerical error due to its subleading nature as well as the coarseness of the temperature grid used for the computation of the crossover temperature: in most cases the condensates are resolved within
~1

\kappa_{2}

We proceed with a discussion of the high density regime. At strangeness neutrality,
n_{S}=0
, the CEP is located at far larger baryon chemical potential,

\displaystyle(T\,,\,{\mu_{B}})_{{}_{\text{CEP}}}=(92\,,\,696)\,\text{MeV}\,,

(13)

see
Figure

3
. Using the current setup at
\mu_{S}=0
leads us to
(T\,,\,{\mu_{B}})_{{}_{\text{CEP}}}=(102,644)\,\text{MeV}
. The latter location agrees well within a few percent with the result in
Fu et al. 2020a
, listed in
Table

1
. This is very remarkable as the location of the critical end point is very sensitive to small physics changes. It also suggests that the approximations are (still) quantitative in the regime
\mu_{B}/T\gtrsim 4
.

In
Table

1
we have compiled the predictions for the chiral critical end point of the functional QCD works shown in
Figure

3
. We emphasise in this context that in the present approximation, and those used in
Fu et al. 2020a
;
Gao and Pawlowski 2021
;
Gunkel and Fischer 2021
, the existence or absence of the CEP cannot be proven as the dominance of the scalar-pseudoscalar sector is assumed. However, the results in these works and the present one entail, that in the
absence
of novel emergent dynamics at finite density, QCD with
\mu_{S}=0
does not admit a chiral crossover beyond the CEP found in these works: the respective CEP interval is given by
\bigl(115-105\,,\,600-650\bigr)\textrm{MeV}
with a small width, see
Fischer and Pawlowski 2026
. This suggests a similar statement for QCD with strangeness neutrality with an interval of the same length roughly centred at

13
.

In any case, the large difference of the CEPs for
n_{S}=0
and
\mu_{S}=0
hints at a different dynamics in the large density regime for these cases. A natural candidate for this dynamics are additional soft modes, e.g. soft kaons. This scenario can be checked by following the masses of the scalar-pseudoscalar octet along the chiral crossover line. In
Figure

4
we show the in-medium meson masses
M^{\textrm{med}}_{\phi^{i}}
introduced in
Fu et al. 2020a
, see also the supplement. They give access to the masses of the (off-shell) mesons in the non-perturbative loops that govern the flow equation. Interestingly, the in-medium masses of the kaons even grow towards larger baryon chemical potential. Moreover, the pion in-medium mass is rather stable with a relatively large thermal part. The mass of the
\sigma
-mode is also rather stable and drops below the pion mass close to the CEP at roughly 650 MeV. This behaviour is similar to that observed at
\mu_{S}=0
, for more details see the Supplement. Moreover, it has also been seen in
Pawlowski et al. 2025
, where the full momentum dependence of pions and
\sigma
-mode has been resolved. However, with the deepening of the moat we indeed expect softer modes, which deserves further studies in particular for
\mu_{B}/T\gtrsim 4
. Such an analysis goes beyond the scope of the present work and will be provided elsewhere.

We close this discussion with the interesting observation, that in contradistinction to the large change of the CEP location, the onset regime of the moats for
\mu_{S}=0
and
n_{S}=0
is roughly the same, see
Figure

3
. The related comparison with the onset of the moat from
Fu et al. 2020a
as well as that in
Pawlowski et al. 2025
has been deferred to the supplement. There, we also show a Figure with all available data on the phase structure. We have refrained from showing all the data in
Figure

for the sake of accessibility.

Conclusions and summary.–

We have computed the 2+1 flavour QCD phase structure at strangeness neutrality within functional QCD. We have used an approximation, in which the dominance of the chiral dynamics is assumed. This assumption has been checked rather comprehensively in functional QCD, and the only alternative scenario left is that of a regime with an instability, see
Pawlowski et al. 2025
and the recent review
Fischer and Pawlowski 2026
. In turn, with the assumption, the QCD phase structure features a critical end point at
(T\,,\,{\mu_{B}})_{{}_{\text{CEP}}}=(92\,,\,696)\,\text{MeV}
, see
13
. The present computation satisfies all QCD benchmarks at small baryon chemical potential. Specifically, the reduction of the curvature of the phase boundary in comparison to
\mu_{S}=0
is in excellent agreement with the lattice results, see
12
.

Before we go into the details, we would like to provide a brief overview and embedding of the present work in the functional QCD landscape. For a comprehensive overview over functional QCD and the different approximation schemes used in different works see
Fischer and Pawlowski 2026
. The works
Fu et al. 2020a
;
Ihssen et al. 2024
;
Pawlowski et al. 2025
map out the phase structure of 2+1 flavour QCD at
\mu_{S}=0
, that is identical chemical potentials for all quarks,
\mu_{s}=\mu_{l}=\mu_{B}/3
, while
Ihssen et al. 2024
advances the approximation and the approach to systematic error estimates. The approximation to the effective action if detailed in the Supplement, here we concentrate on the most important differences and advances in comparison to
Fu et al. 2020a
;
Fu et al. 2025b
;
Ihssen et al. 2024
;
Pawlowski et al. 2025
.

The previous works took into account the resonant scalar-pseudoscalar four-quark interaction channels of the pions
\bm{\pi}
and
\sigma
-mode with emergent composites
Gies and Wetterich 2002
;
Gies and Wetterich 2004
;
Pawlowski 2007
;
Fu et al. 2020a
;
Fukushima et al. 2021
, for further applications and developments see
Mitter et al. 2015
;
Braun et al. 2016
;
Rennecke 2015
;
Cyrol et al. 2018a
. In the emergent composite approach to QCD, higher order scatterings in the resonant channels are encoded in the effective potential
V(\rho)
with
\rho=(\sigma^{2}+\bm{\pi}^{2})/2
. In
Fu et al. 2020a
;
Fu et al. 2025b
the potential was expanded in a Taylor expansion in
\rho
around the solution of the mesonic equations of motion up to order 5 - 7, and the comprehensive analysis in
Ihssen et al. 2024
with an all order potential confirmed the quantitative nature of this expansion.

In the present work we extend these work by considering the full effective potential of the mesonic composite
\phi
. Together with the potential of the temporal background gluon we are led to

\displaystyle V(\phi,A_{0})=V_{A_{0}}(A_{0})+V_{\phi}(\phi)\,.

(14)

In
14
we have dropped the interaction terms between the temporal glue background and the nonet, and in the numerical application we resort to a converging Taylor expansion, building on the analysis of
Ihssen et al. 2024
. In summary,
14
and the respective mesonic propagators takes into account multi-scattering events of the scalar-pseudoscalar nonet channels. This includes potentially important contributions involving strange mesons, especially the additional pseudo-Goldstone bosons from the pseudoscalar octet,
K
and
\eta
mesons.

S.1
Effective action and approximation scheme

In the present work we work use the fRG-approach to QCD, for QCD-related reviews see
Litim and Pawlowski 1998
;
Berges et al. 2002
;
Schaefer and Wambach 2005
;
Pawlowski 2007
;
Gies 2012
;
Braun 2012
;
Dupuis et al. 2021
;
Fu 2022
and the recent review on the functional QCD approach to the phase structure of QCD
Fischer and Pawlowski 2026
.

As already mentioned above, we use the fRG approach with emergent composites, where the effective action not only depend on the fundamental gluons, ghosts and quarks, but also emergent composite fields, in our case the scalar-pseudoscalar nonet. Moreover, we define the approximation scheme in terms of the rescaled field

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}=\bm{Z}_{\Phi}^{1/2}\,\Phi,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\Phi=\{A,\bar{c},c,\bar{q},q,\phi\}\,,\quad\textrm{where}\quad\phi=(\sigma^{a}\,,\,\pi^{a})^{T}\,,\quad a=0,...,8\,,

(15)

where the
\sigma^{a}
and
\pi^{a}
are the scalar and pseudoscalar modes in the singlet-octet basis, and
\bm{Z}_{\Phi}=\textrm{diag}(Z_{\Phi_{i}})
is the diagonal wave function matrix. The field
\bar{\Phi}
in
15
is sometimes called the ’renormalised’ field. However, it is indeed RG-invariant under the RG-transformations of the full theory at
k=0
, for a respective discussion see e.g.
Pawlowski 2007
;
Dupuis et al. 2021
and in particular
Ihssen et al. 2024
. The RG-invariance of the fields entails that all the dressings of
n
-point vertices are RG-invariant. On the present level it is simply a convenient choice which facilitates the book-keeping. However, the RG-invariant expansion scheme has better convergence property is fully momentum-dependent approximations are used, for a detailed discussion see
Ihssen et al. 2024
.

Finally, we simplify the notation and relabel
\bar{\Phi}\to\Phi
in a slight abuse of notation. Note that while the rescaled field is RG-invariant, it is not cutoff-independent,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Phi=-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\Phi}\Phi\,,\qquad\qquad\eta_{\Phi}=-\frac{1}{\bm{Z}_{\Phi}}\partial_{t}\bm{Z}_{\Phi}\,,

(16)

with the diagonal matrix of anomalous dimensions
\eta_{\Phi}=\textrm{diag}(\eta_{\Phi_{i}})
which accounts for the anomalous scaling with
k
.

S.1.1
Effective action

We use the following approximation of the effective action
\Gamma_{k}
for
N_{f}=2+1
flavour QCD,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}=

\displaystyle\,\int\limits_{0}^{\beta}\!\!dx_{0}\int\!\!d^{3}x\,\Biggr\{\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^{a}F_{\mu\nu}^{a}+(\partial_{\mu}\bar{c}^{a})D_{\mu}^{ab}c^{b}+\frac{1}{2\xi}(\partial_{\mu}A_{\mu}^{a})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}A^{a}_{\mu}(-p)\Big({\Gamma_{AA}^{(2)}}_{\mu\nu}^{ab}-\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{\perp}\delta^{ab}p^{2}\Big)A_{\nu}^{b}(p)+V_{A_{0}}(A_{0})

\displaystyle+\bar{q}\,\Big[\gamma_{\mu}D_{\mu}-\gamma_{0}\hat{\mu}_{q}+h_{q}^{1/2}\Sigma_{5}\cdot h_{q}^{1/2}\Big]\,q-\sum_{a=0}^{8}\left(\lambda_{q_{a}}\left[\bar{q}\,T^{a}\,q\right]^{2}+\lambda_{q_{a}}\left[\bar{q}\,i\gamma_{5}T^{a}\,q\right]^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+{\text{tr}}\left[\bar{D}_{\mu}\,\Sigma\,\bar{D}_{\mu}\,\Sigma^{\dagger}\right]+V_{\phi}(\Sigma,\Sigma^{\dagger})\Biggl\}\,,

(17a)

with
\beta=1/T
and the rescaled gluon fields. The transversal projector
\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{\perp}(p)=\delta_{\mu\nu}-p_{\mu}p_{\nu}/p^{2}
. The rescaled quark and meson fields are given by

\displaystyle q=(u,d,s)^{T}\,,\qquad\qquad\Sigma=T^{a}(\sigma^{a}+i\pi^{a})\,,\qquad\Sigma_{5}=T^{a}(\sigma^{a}+i\gamma_{5}\pi^{a})\,,\qquad a=0,1,...,8\,,

(17b)

where
T^{a}=\lambda^{a}/2
with the Gell-Mann matrices
\lambda^{a}
for
a=1,...,8)
are the generators of the flavour su(3)-algebra and
T^{0}=\mathbb{1}_{N_{f}\times N_{f}}/\sqrt{2N_{f}}
. The
\sigma^{a}
and
\pi^{a}
carry the quantum numbers of the respective mesons and feature the same pole masses. However, these
emergent composites
are introduced as efficient book-keeping devices for resonant four-quark interaction channels and should not be confused with on-shell asymptotic particles. Furthermore, their occurrence in
17a
does not signal an low energy effective theory, it can be shown that the QCD effective action in the fRG-approach with emergent composites reduces to the standard effective action on the equations of motion for the composite fields, similarly to the better-known two-particle irreducible (2PI) approach, where the 2PI effective action reduces to the 1PI effective action on the gap equation (equation of motion for the propagator field).

The quark chemical potential
\hat{\mu}_{q}
and the Yukawa coupling
h_{q}
in
17a
are taken diagonal,

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{q}=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{l}&0&0\\
0&\mu_{l}&0\\
0&0&\mu_{s}\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad\qquad\qquad h_{q}=\begin{pmatrix}h_{l}&0&0\\
0&h_{l}&0\\
0&0&h_{s}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(17c)

and we have identified the up and down chemical potentials and Yukawa couplings. The quark chemical potentials
\mu_{q_{i}}
can be expressed in terms of the baryon chemical potential, that of the electric charge and strangeness,

\displaystyle\mu_{u}=\frac{1}{3}\mu_{B}+\frac{2}{3}\mu_{Q}\,,\qquad\mu_{d}=\frac{1}{3}\mu_{B}-\frac{1}{3}\mu_{Q}\,,\qquad\mu_{s}=\frac{1}{3}\mu_{B}-\frac{1}{3}\mu_{Q}-\mu_{S}\,.

(18)

Hence, the identical light quark chemical potentials
\mu_{u}=\mu_{d}=\mu_{l}
entail
\mu_{Q}=0
and
\mu_{s}=\mu_{l}-\mu_{S}
. All couplings and wave functions in
17
depend on the momentum cutoff scale
k
and this dependence is computed from the flow equation of the effective action discussed in
Section

S.2
.

The first line on the right-hand side of
17a
comprises the approximation of the pure glue sector. In this work, we adopt the same truncation for the glue sector as that in
Fu et al. 2020a
;
Fu et al. 2025b
;
Ihssen et al. 2024
;
Pawlowski et al. 2025
. The quantitative nature of this approximation has been tested comprehensively in
Fu et al. 2020a
;
Ihssen et al. 2024
against fRG benchmark results
Cyrol et al. 2018a
;
Fu et al. 2025c
in the vacuum with full momentum dependence as well as lattice results (gluon propagator) at finite temperature. Further details will be provided in
Section

S.2.1
.

The two other lines comprise the approximation of the matter sector, further details will be provided in
Section

S.1.2
and
Section

S.1.3
, and we only provide a brief overview here: We only consider the baryon number and strangeness chemical potentials and ignore the isospin chemical potential, see
17c
. This is a good approximation in the context of heavy-ion collisions, as charge conservation leads to only very small isospin chemical potentials
Fu et al. 2020b
;
Fu et al. 2019
;
Wen and Fu 2021
.
\Sigma
denotes the nonets of scalar and pseudoscalar meson fields, and the meson effective potential
\tilde{V}_{\Sigma}(\Sigma,\Sigma^{\dagger})
including chiral symmetric part, the chiral symmetry breaking term (linear sigma terms) and the
U_{A}(1)
symmetry breaking term (the Kobayashi-Maskawa-’t Hooft term
Kobayashi and Maskawa 1970
;
’t Hooft 1976a
;
’t Hooft 1976b
), see
Section

S.1.2
for more details. The meson curvature masses are obtained from the eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix of the meson effective potential. The meson fields are coupled to the quarks through the Yukawa interactions. In this work, we distinguish the light and strange Yukawa couplings and the corresponding dynamical hadronisation, see
Section

S.2
.

S.1.2
Matter sector of
N_{f}=2+1
flavour QCD

The nonets of scalar and pseudoscalar meson fields have been defined in
17b
. The covariant derivative acting on the meson fields reads

\displaystyle\bar{D}_{\mu}\Sigma=\partial_{\mu}\Sigma+[\delta_{\mu 0}\hat{\mu},\Sigma]\,.

(19)

In this work, we work in the isospin-symmetric approximation. Then, there are two non-vanishing vacuum expectation values, i.e. the light and strange condensates
\sigma_{l}
,
\sigma_{s}
. In
17a
, the meson fields are coupled to the quarks through the scalar-pseudoscalar symmetric Yukawa term and the Yukawa coupling matrix is a diagonal matrix in the flavour space, see
17c
.

The meson effective potential can be divided into three parts based on the symmetry:

\displaystyle{V}_{\Sigma}(\Sigma,\Sigma^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=V_{\chi}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3})-c_{A}\xi\,,-c_{l}\sigma_{l}-c_{s}\sigma_{s}/\sqrt{2}

(20)

where

\displaystyle\xi=\det(\Sigma)+\det(\Sigma^{\dagger})\,.

(21)

In
20
,
V_{\chi}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3})
is the chirally symmetric part of the meson effective potential and has the full chiral
U_{L}(3)\times U_{R}(3)
symmetry. In general, it depends on three chiral invariants and we choose

\displaystyle\rho_{1}=\text{tr}(\Sigma\,\Sigma^{\dagger})\,,\qquad\rho_{2}=\sqrt{6\,\text{tr}\bigg(\Sigma\,\Sigma^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{3}\rho_{1}\mathbb{1}_{3\times 3}\bigg)^{2}}\,,\qquad\rho_{3}\propto\text{tr}(\Sigma\,\Sigma^{\dagger})^{3}\,.

(22)

For more details see e.g.
Jungnickel and Wetterich 1998
;
Schaefer and Wagner 2009
;
Mitter and Schaefer 2014
;
Herbst et al. 2014
;
Rennecke and Schaefer 2017
;
Wen et al. 2019
;
Fu et al. 2020b
;
Fu et al. 2020a
;
Pawlowski et al. 2025
. For vanishing pseudoscalar field
\pi^{a}=0
, the invariants
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
have simple forms in terms of the scalar fields
\sigma_{l},\sigma_{s}
. The latter fields are combinations of the fields
\sigma^{0},\sigma^{8}
in the singlet-octet basis with

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\sigma^{\ }_{l}\\[2.15277pt]
\sigma^{\ }_{s}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{2}&1\\[2.15277pt]
1&-\sqrt{2}\end{pmatrix}\,\begin{pmatrix}\sigma^{\ }_{0}\\[2.15277pt]
\sigma^{\ }_{8}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(23)

see again
Jungnickel and Wetterich 1998
;
Schaefer and Wagner 2009
;
Mitter and Schaefer 2014
;
Herbst et al. 2014
;
Rennecke and Schaefer 2017
;
Wen et al. 2019
;
Fu et al. 2020b
;
Fu et al. 2020a
. With
23
we find

\displaystyle\rho_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{l}^{2}+\sigma_{s}^{2})\,,\qquad\qquad\rho_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(2\sigma_{s}^{2}-\sigma_{l}^{2})\,.

(24)

In the following, we shall drop the invariant
\rho_{3}
for simplicity, see e.g.
Mitter and Schaefer 2014
;
Rennecke and Schaefer 2017
. This is supported by its higher order nature as well as the dominance of the contributions from the quark loops, were we follow the argument in
Ihssen et al. 2024
: The light and strange quark loops generate functions
V_{q_{l}}(\rho_{l})
and
V_{q_{s}}(\rho_{s})
respectively. Only the induced field-dependence of the Yukawa couplings mixes the invariants and these terms are subleading. For this reason we have dropped them in the present work to begin with. Further mixings are triggered by the meson contributions to the effective potential, which are subleading in a large regime in the phase structure. They become even dominant close to a critical point due to their coupling to the soft modes. For the chiral limit these are the pions, see
Braun et al. 2025
, and for the potential critical end point this is a mixture of the
\sigma
-mode, the density mode and the Polyakov loop, see
Haensch et al. 2024
. However, in this regime the subleading nature follows from the the efficient scale hierarchy in QCD and the LEGO
®
-principle. The letter entails, that relevance arguments carry over from subsectors of QCD to QCD, if the (quark-gluon) interfaces are well-controlled, see
Ihssen et al. 2024
;
Fischer and Pawlowski 2026
. In summary, this allows us to only consider the invariants
\rho_{1,2}
in
22
.

The linear breaking terms
c_{l}\sigma_{l}+c_{s}\sigma_{s}
can be understood as source terms for the zero modes (constant fields)
\sigma,\sigma_{s}
and are cutoff independent, see e.g.
Fu et al. 2020a
. They account for the explicit chiral symmetry breaking that comes with the current quark masses. As we have parametrised
20
in terms of the rescaled fields, the coefficients
c_{l,s,A}
satisfy
the flow equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(c_{l}\sigma_{l}+c_{s}\sigma_{s}\right)=0\,,\qquad\longrightarrow\qquad\partial_{t}c_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\eta_{i}\,c_{i}\,,\qquad i=l,s\,.

(25)

The
k
- and
\rho_{i}
-dependence of
V_{\chi}
is determined by its flow equation, see
Section

S.2.2
. In the present work we consider a Taylor expansion in
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
around the solutions
\kappa_{1}=\rho_{l}^{\textrm{\tiny{EoM}}}
and
\kappa_{2}=\rho_{s}^{\textrm{\tiny{EoM}}}
of the mesonic equations of motion,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(V_{\chi}-c_{A}\,\xi)}{\partial\sigma_{l}}(\sigma^{\textrm{\tiny{EoM}}}_{l},\sigma^{\textrm{\tiny{EoM}}}_{s})=c_{l}\,,\qquad\quad\frac{\partial(V_{\chi}-c_{A}\,\xi)}{\partial\sigma_{s}}(\sigma^{\textrm{\tiny{EoM}}}_{l},\sigma^{\textrm{\tiny{EoM}}}_{s})=c_{s}\,,\qquad\phi^{\textrm{\tiny{EoM}}}=(\sigma_{0}^{\textrm{\tiny{EoM}}},0,...,\sigma_{8}^{\textrm{\tiny{EoM}}},0,...,0)^{T}\,.

(26)

This leads us to

\displaystyle V_{\chi,k}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})=\sum_{i,j=0}^{i+j\leq N}\frac{\lambda_{ij,k}}{i!j!}(\rho_{1}-\kappa_{1,k})^{i}(\rho_{2}-\kappa_{2,k})^{j}\,,

(27)

with the rescaled fields
\rho_{i}
. Hence, the expansion coefficients
\lambda_{ij,k}
are RG-invariant and can be interpreted as the scattering strength of the resonant scattering of
2i+2j
fermionic bilinears. They are determined by the coupled set of flow equations for the full QCD system and we monitor the results with an increasing order of
N
with
i+j\leq N
. We find convergence with
N=5
. The
\kappa_{1,k}
,
\kappa_{2,k}
are the RG scale
k
dependent expansion points, which are always at the minimum of the effective potential for every value of
k
.

We note in passing that we could have chosen expansion points different to
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})
. Indeed, it has been shown that an expansion about the
flowing
solution
\kappa
of the equations of motion is converging slower than a
fixed
expansion about
\kappa_{k=0}
, see
Pawlowski and Rennecke 2014
. However, the latter procedure requires iterative runs of the full system and we take the more convenient choice
27
. We note that close to the critical end point a fixed expansion works qualitatively better as has been shown in
Pawlowski and Rennecke 2014
. However, for a quantitative resolution of this regime we will resort to computing the full potential without an expansion, see
Pawlowski et al. 2025
. This goes beyond the scope of the present work and will be presented elsewhere.

Notably, the choices of the expansion arguments
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
are not unique, and the physical observables should not depend on the choices. However, it would simply the mathematical expressions of meson masses and improve the stability of numerical calculations by choosing a suitable set of expansion arguments for the effective potential. We have checked that our expansion scenario converges quickly and are consistent with the results obtained from other expansion arguments in
Rennecke and Schaefer 2017
;
Mitter and Schaefer 2014
.

Finally, with
21
,
c_{A}\,\xi
in
20
is nothing but the Kobayashi-Maskawa-’t Hooft term
Kobayashi and Maskawa 1970
;
’t Hooft 1976a
;
’t Hooft 1976b
. Since
\xi
is a rescaled field,
c_{A}
is the renormalisation-group invariant ’t Hooft coupling. This term breaks
U_{A}(1)
symmetry and is related to the mass splitting of
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
meson. In this work, we introduce an instanton-induced ’t Hooft coupling
Pawlowski 1998
for
k\gtrsim 700
MeV

\displaystyle c_{A}\propto\exp\left(-\frac{2\pi}{\alpha_{k}}\right)k^{-3N_{f}+4}\,,

(28)

where
\alpha_{k}
is the strong coupling. For
k\lesssim 700
MeV it tends to a constant, see
Section

S.3
.

S.1.3
Meson and quark masses and scatterings

In the fRG-approach with emergent composites the field-dependent light and strange quark mass functions are given by

\displaystyle m_{l}(\phi)=\frac{h_{l}}{2}\sigma_{l},\qquad m_{s}(\phi)=\frac{h_{s}}{\sqrt{2}}\sigma_{s}\,,

(29)

with field-dependent Yukawa couplings. The masses
m_{l},m_{s}
in
Equation

29
are RG-invariant, and the constituent quark masses are obtained by evaluating
29
on the equations of motion
\sigma^{\textrm{\tiny{EoM}}}_{l},\sigma^{\textrm{\tiny{EoM}}}_{s}
in
26
.

The field-dependent meson mass functions are obtained from the Hessian of the meson effective potential,

\displaystyle H_{ij}(\phi)=\frac{\partial^{2}{V}_{\phi}(\phi)}{\partial\phi_{i}\partial\phi_{j}}=\frac{\partial^{2}\left({V}_{\chi}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})-c_{A}\,\xi\right)}{\partial\phi_{i}\partial\phi_{j}}\,.

(30)

The detailed expressions can be found in
Section

S.1.3
or e.g. in
Rennecke and Schaefer 2017
;
Wen et al. 2019
. The physical masses are obtained by evaluating the Hessian on the mesonic EoM
26
. There,
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\dagger}
derivatives can be converted into
\rho_{1,2}
-derivatives and the Hessian
30
can be written in terms of

\displaystyle V_{\chi}^{(n,m)}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2}):=\frac{\partial^{(n+m)}V_{\chi}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})}{(\partial\rho_{1}^{n}\partial\rho_{2}^{m})}\,,

(31)

For the sake of readability we also introduce the notation

\displaystyle H_{s,ij}=H_{\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}}\,,\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad H_{p,ij}=H_{\pi_{i}\pi_{j}}\,,\qquad\textrm{with}\qquad i,j=l,s\,.

(32)

With
32
and the parametrisation
27
, the Hessian for the scalar sector on the mesonic equation of motion reads

\displaystyle H_{s,ll}=

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{A}\sigma_{s}}{\sqrt{2}}+V_{\chi}^{(1,0)}-V_{\chi}^{(0,1)}+(V_{\chi}^{(2,0)}-2V_{\chi}^{(1,1)}+V_{\chi}^{(0,2)})\sigma_{l}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle H_{s,ls}=

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{A}\sigma_{l}}{\sqrt{2}}+(V_{\chi}^{(2,0)}+V_{\chi}^{(1,1)}-2V_{\chi}^{(0,2)})\sigma_{l}\sigma_{s}\,,

\displaystyle H_{s,ss}=

\displaystyle V_{\chi}^{(1,0)}+2V_{\chi}^{(0,1)}+(V_{\chi}^{(2,0)}+4V_{\chi}^{(1,1)}+4V_{\chi}^{(0,2)})\sigma_{s}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle H_{s,11}=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{A}\sigma_{s}}{\sqrt{2}}+V_{\chi}^{(1,0)}+\frac{7\sigma_{l}^{2}-2\sigma_{s}^{2}}{2\sigma_{s}^{2}-\sigma_{l}^{2}}V_{\chi}^{(0,1)}\,,

\displaystyle H_{s,44}=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{A}\sigma_{l}}{2}+V_{\chi}^{(1,0)}+\frac{\sigma_{l}^{2}+3\sqrt{2}\sigma_{l}\sigma_{s}+4\sigma_{s}^{2}}{2\sigma_{s}^{2}-\sigma_{l}^{2}}V_{\chi}^{(0,1)}\,,

(33)

where it is understood that
\sigma_{l,s}\to\sigma_{l,s}^{\textrm{\tiny{EoM}}}
and

\displaystyle H_{ij}=\left.H_{ij}(\phi)\right|_{\phi=\phi^{\textrm{\tiny{EoM}}}}\,,\qquad\qquad V_{\chi}^{(n,m)}=V_{\chi}^{(n,m)}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})\,.

(34)

For the pseudoscalar sector we find

\displaystyle H_{p,ll}=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{A}\sigma_{s}}{\sqrt{2}}+V_{\chi}^{(1,0)}-V_{\chi}^{(0,1)}\,,

\displaystyle H_{p,ls}=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{A}\sigma_{s}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,

\displaystyle H_{p,ss}=

\displaystyle V_{\chi}^{(1,0)}+2V_{\chi}^{(0,1)}\,,

\displaystyle H_{p,11}=

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{A}\sigma_{s}}{\sqrt{2}}+V_{\chi}^{(1,0)}-V_{\chi}^{(0,1)}\,,

\displaystyle H_{p,44}=

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{A}\sigma_{l}}{2}+V_{\chi}^{(1,0)}+\frac{\sigma_{l}^{2}-3\sqrt{2}\sigma_{l}\sigma_{s}+4\sigma_{s}^{2}}{2\sigma_{s}^{2}-\sigma_{l}^{2}}V_{\chi}^{(0,1)}\,.

(35)

The non-vanishing off-diagonal element
H_{s/p,ls}
introduce mixing angles between the light-strange basis and physical basis,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}f_{0}\\[4.30554pt]
\sigma\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\varphi_{s}&-\sin\varphi_{s}\\[4.30554pt]
\sin\varphi_{s}&\cos\varphi_{s}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{l}\\[4.30554pt]
\sigma_{s}\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad\qquad\begin{pmatrix}\eta\\[4.30554pt]
\eta^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\varphi_{p}&-\sin\varphi_{p}\\[4.30554pt]
\sin\varphi_{p}&\cos\varphi_{p}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\eta_{l}\\[4.30554pt]
\eta_{s}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(36)

with

\displaystyle\varphi_{s/p}=\frac{1}{2}\arctan\Biggl[\frac{2H_{s/p,ls}}{H_{s/p,ss}-H_{s/p,ll}}\Biggr]\,.

(37)

Then, the square of meson masses are given by

\displaystyle m_{f_{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}\varphi_{s}H_{s,ll}+\sin^{2}\varphi_{s}H_{s,ss}-2\sin\varphi_{s}\cos\varphi_{s}H_{s,ls}\,,

\displaystyle m_{\sigma}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sin^{2}\varphi_{s}H_{s,ll}+\cos^{2}\varphi_{s}H_{s,ss}+2\sin\varphi_{s}\cos\varphi_{s}H_{s,ls}\,,

\displaystyle m_{a_{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle=H_{s,11},

\displaystyle m_{\kappa}^{2}

\displaystyle=H_{s,44}\,,

\displaystyle m_{\eta}^{2}

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}\varphi_{p}H_{p,ll}+\sin^{2}\varphi_{p}H_{p,ss}-2\sin\varphi_{p}\cos\varphi_{p}H_{p,ls}\,,

\displaystyle=\sin^{2}\varphi_{p}H_{p,ll}+\cos^{2}\varphi_{p}H_{p,ss}+2\sin\varphi_{p}\cos\varphi_{p}H_{p,ls}\,,

\displaystyle m_{\pi}^{2}

\displaystyle=H_{p,11}\,,

\displaystyle m_{K}^{2}

\displaystyle=H_{p,44}\,.

(38)

The masses of

can be expressed concisely as

\displaystyle m_{f_{0}/\eta^{\prime}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\Biggl[H_{s/p,ll}+H_{s/p,ll}+\sqrt{(H_{s/p,ll}-H_{s/p,ss})^{2}+4H_{s/p,ls}^{2}}\,\,\Biggr]\,,

\displaystyle m_{\sigma/\eta}^{2}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\Biggl[H_{s/p,ll}+H_{s/p,ss}-\sqrt{(H_{s/p,ll}-H_{s/p,ss})^{2}+4H_{s/p,ls}^{2}}\,\,\Biggr]\,.

(39)

The momentum-independent mesonic
n

or rather that of
\eta_{\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle V_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}f_{0}}=

\displaystyle\,\cos\varphi_{s}\,\Big[(V_{\chi}^{(0,2)}-2V_{\chi}^{(1,1)}+V_{\chi}^{(2,0)})\sigma_{l}\Big]-\sin\varphi_{s}\,\Big[-(\frac{c_{A}}{\sqrt{2}}+(-2V_{\chi}^{(0,2)}+V_{\chi}^{(1,1)}+V_{\chi}^{(2,0)})\sigma_{s}\Big]\,,

S.2.1
Glue sector and quark-gluon interface

The glue sector is treated as in
Fu et al. 2020a
;
Ihssen et al. 2024
;
Fu et al. 2025b
. Still, for completeness, we briefly summarise here the calculation and approximation of the gluon propagator and the avatars of the strong coupling. The gluon propagator in the Landau gauge is parametrised as

\displaystyle(G_{A})^{ab}_{\mu\nu}(p)=\frac{1}{Z_{A}(p)\,p^{2}\left[1+r_{A}(x)\right]}\Pi_{\mu\nu}^{\perp}\delta^{ab}\,,

(43)

where the full momentum dependence is accounted for by the wave function
Z_{A}(p)
. For the explicit computation we we take 3d flat or Litim regulators with the shape function
r_{A}(x)=r_{\textrm{flat}}(x)
with
x=\bm{p}^{2}/k^{2}
, see
42
.

In perturbative QCD the running strong coupling can be extracted from different vertices and the RG-running of all these
avatars
agree with each other up to two-loop due to the the Slavnov-Taylor identities (STIs) and two-loop universality for mass-independent RG-schemes. Beyond two-loop this does not hold even in perturbation theory and in a non-perturbative regime the STIs feature non-trivial scattering kernels that lead to non-trivial relations between the different avatars. Moreover, one has to consider all tensor structures and the projections may not be orthogonal, for a more comprehensive discussion see e.g.
Dupuis et al. 2021
;
Pawlowski et al. 2022
;
Ihssen et al. 2024
. Therefore one has to consider different avatars of the strong coupling that agree in the perturbative regime but deviate from each other in the infrared regime. We have the avatars

\displaystyle\alpha_{A^{3}}=\frac{g^{2}_{A^{3}}}{4\pi}\,,\qquad\alpha_{A^{4}}=\frac{g^{2}_{A^{4}}}{4\pi}\,,\qquad\alpha_{c\bar{c}A}=\frac{g^{2}_{c\bar{c}A}}{4\pi}\,,\qquad\alpha_{l\bar{l}A}=\frac{g^{2}_{l\bar{l}A}}{4\pi}\,,\qquad\qquad\alpha_{s\bar{s}A}=\frac{g^{2}_{s\bar{s}A}}{4\pi}\,,

(44)

where the subscripts indicate the scattering vertex, from which the avatar of
\alpha_{s}
is extracted. In this work, we compute the
k
-dependence of the light and strange quark-gluon couplings and three-gluon coupling
\alpha_{i}=g_{i}^{2}/(4\pi)
with
i=A^{3},l\bar{l}A,s\bar{s}A
, and employ the approximation,

\displaystyle\alpha_{A^{4}}\simeq\alpha_{A^{3}},\quad\alpha_{c\bar{c}A}\simeq\alpha_{l\bar{l}A}\,.

(45)

The differences between all avatars
\alpha_{i}
with
i=A^{3},A^{4},c\bar{c}A,l\bar{l}A,s\bar{s}A
have been resolved in the vacuum,
Mitter et al. 2015
;
Cyrol et al. 2018b
;
Ihssen et al. 2024
;
Fu et al. 2025c
and at finite density,
Pawlowski et al. 2025
. All avatars agree for momenta
p\gtrsim 2
GeV and the differences grow large for
p\lesssim 1
GeV. In the latter regime the glue dynamics decouples successively, which makes the approximation
45
quantitatively reliable.

We close this discussion of the strong couplings with a discussion of the full vertices and their dressings. All full vertices discussed above,
\Gamma^{(n_{i}})_{i}
with
i=A^{3},A^{4},c\bar{c}A,q\bar{q}A
, admit more than one tensor structure. Their impact has been thoroughly investigated in pure Yang-Mills theory,
Cyrol et al. 2016
;
Cyrol et al. 2018a
and in full QCD,
Mitter et al. 2015
;
Cyrol et al. 2018b
;
Fu et al. 2025c
. Similar investigations have been performed in the DSE-method, see e.g.n
Gao et al. 2021
. In summary, for the present purpose the pure glue vertices,
i=A^{3},A^{4},c\bar{c}A
, are well approximated by their classical part. In turn, the transverse part of quark-gluon vertex is expanded in eight independent tensor structures, and three of them are important: the classical tensor structure and another chirally symmetric one,
{\cal T}_{A\bar{q}q}^{(1)},{\cal T}_{A\bar{q}q}^{(7)}
respectively, and a chirally breaking one,
{\cal T}_{A\bar{q}q}^{(4)}
. Depending on the chosen basis the two non-classical tensor structure take different forms. In the basis in
Ihssen et al. 2024
(Appendix F) we have

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{T}_{A\bar{q}q}^{(1)}\right]_{\mu}(p,q)=\,i\,\gamma_{\mu}\,,\qquad\left[\mathcal{T}_{A\bar{q}q}^{(4)}\right]_{\mu}(p,q)=\,i\,\sigma_{\mu\alpha}p_{\alpha}\,,\qquad\left[\mathcal{T}_{A\bar{q}q}^{(7)}\right]_{\mu}(p,q)=\frac{{\textrm{1}}}{3}\,\Big[\sigma_{\alpha\beta}\gamma_{\mu}+\sigma_{\beta\mu}\gamma_{\alpha}+\sigma_{\mu\alpha}\gamma_{\beta}\Big]\,,

(46)

with the dressings
\lambda^{(1,4,7)}_{q_{i}\bar{q}_{i}A}
. Within this set of three tensor structures the classical one is by far the most important, followed by the 4th tensor structure. Dropping the non-classical tensor structures, and in particular
\lambda^{(4,)}_{q_{i}\bar{q}_{i}A}
in the DSE-approach can be compensated by a significant infrared enhancement of the dressing of the classical tensor structure, see e.g. the recent review
Fischer and Pawlowski 2026
. In turn, in the fRG-approach with emergent composites, a significant part of this dynamics that is contained in the mesonic diagrams. Indeed, rewriting the loop hierarchy in the fRG in terms of DSEs, a significant part of the DSE of the vertex is carried by these loops and implicitly generate the vertex part with
\lambda^{(1)}_{q_{i}\bar{q}_{i}A}
. This entails that we can almost drop the tensor structures
\lambda^{(4,7)}_{q_{i}\bar{q}_{i}A}
while still keeping quantitative precision. We are left with a small infrared enhancement factor of roughly
1\%
which is details in
Section

S.3.2
.

This setup is sufficient for the phase structure investigation in the present work. For the computation of thermodynamic observables such as pressure, entropy and the trace anomaly as well as fluctuations of conserved charges, a parametrisation with an additional mass function
m_{A}^{2}
such as used in
Pawlowski et al. 2025
is better suited and shows better convergence properties. While we will use such a parametrisation for the computation of the respective observables in
Fu et al. 2026
.

The wave function
Z_{A}
in
43
is absorbed in the rescaled field
A_{\mu}
, and for the computation of the anomalous dimension

(47)

where

\displaystyle\eta_{A}^{i}

\displaystyle=-\frac{N_{i}}{\pi^{2}}g_{{\bar{q}}_{i}Aq_{i}}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}dx\bigg[(1-\eta_{q_{i}})\sqrt{x}+\eta_{q_{i}}x\bigg]\int_{-1}^{1}d\cos\theta\bigg[\Big(\mathcal{FF}_{(1,1)}(m_{q_{i}},m_{q_{i}})-\mathcal{FF}_{(2,1)}(m_{q_{i}},m_{q_{i}})\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\Big(\sqrt{x}\cos^{2}\!\theta-\cos\theta\Big)\Big(1+r_{F}(x^{\prime})\Big)\Big(\mathcal{FF}_{(2,1)}(m_{q_{i}},m_{q_{i}})-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{FF}_{(1,1)}(m_{q_{i}},m_{q_{i}})\Big)\bigg]\,,

We proceed with the discussion of the flows of

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{30}(5-2\eta_{q_{i}})\mathcal{FB}_{(2,2)}(m_{q_{i}},0)-\frac{4}{15}(5-\eta_{A})\mathcal{FB}_{(1,2)}(m_{q_{i}},0)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{30}(10-3\eta_{A})\mathcal{FB}_{(1,3)}(m_{q_{i}},0)\bigg\}\,.

\displaystyle{\textrm{Flow}}^{(3),\phi}_{{({l\bar{l}A})}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\bigg(\frac{g_{l\bar{l}A}{h}_{l}^{2}}{8}\bigg(3\mathcal{L}_{2}(\pi,q_{l})+3\mathcal{L}_{2}(a_{0},q_{l})+\cos^{2}\varphi_{s}\mathcal{L}_{2}(f_{0},q_{l})+\sin^{2}\varphi_{s}\mathcal{L}_{2}(\sigma,q_{l})

Meson sector

The flow of the chiral part of the effective potential reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{V}_{\Sigma,k}(\Sigma,\Sigma^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{4}}{4\pi^{2}}\bigg\{\sum_{i}l_{0}^{(B,4)}(m_{\phi_{i}},\eta_{\phi};T,\mu_{i})-4N_{c}\big[2l_{0}^{(F,4)}(m_{l},\eta_{l};T,\mu)+l_{0}^{(F,4)}(m_{s},\eta_{s};T,\mu_{s})\big]\bigg\},

The mesonic wave function

is a diagonal matrix with entries

Ihssen et al. 2024

Dupuis et al. 2021

\displaystyle Z_{\phi_{i}}=Z_{\phi}:=Z_{\pi}\,,\quad\forall i\quad\longrightarrow\eta_{\phi_{i}}=\eta_{\phi}:=\eta_{\pi}\,.

as well as those used in the following, e.g.,

,

\displaystyle\eta^{\textrm{quark}}_{\phi}(0,0)=\frac{N_{c}{h}_{l}^{2}}{6\pi^{2}}\bigg[(2\eta_{l}-3)\mathcal{F}_{(2)}({m}_{l})-4(\eta_{l}-2)\mathcal{F}_{(3)}({m}_{l})\bigg]\,.

S.2.3

In this work, we distinguish the light and strange quark wave functions, i.e.,

(61)

without summation over the flavour index

\displaystyle\eta_{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{48\pi^{2}}(4-\eta_{\phi})h_{l}^{2}\Big[3\mathcal{FB}_{(1,2)}(m_{l},m_{\pi})+3\mathcal{FB}_{(1,2)}(m_{l},m_{a_{0}})+\cos^{2}\varphi_{s}\mathcal{FB}_{(1,2)}(m_{l},m_{f0})

\displaystyle\quad+\cos^{2}\varphi_{p}\mathcal{FB}_{(1,2)}(m_{l},m_{\eta})+\sin^{2}\varphi_{s}\mathcal{FB}_{(1,2)}(m_{l},m_{\sigma})+\sin^{2}\varphi_{p}\mathcal{FB}_{(1,2)}(m_{l},m_{\eta^{\prime}})\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{24\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}^{2}-1}{2N_{c}}g_{l\bar{l}A}^{2}\Big[2(4-\eta_{A})\mathcal{FB}_{(1,2)}(m_{l},0)+3(3-\eta_{l})\big(\mathcal{FB}_{(1,1)}(m_{l},0)-2\mathcal{FB}_{(2,1)}(m_{l},0)\big)\Big],

(62)

and for the strange quark anomalous dimension

\displaystyle\eta_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{24\pi^{2}}(4-\eta_{\phi})h_{s}^{2}\Big[\sin^{2}\varphi_{s}\mathcal{FB}_{(1,2)}(m_{s},m_{f_{0}})+\sin^{2}\varphi_{p}\mathcal{FB}_{(1,2)}(m_{s},m_{\eta})+\cos^{2}\varphi_{s}\mathcal{FB}_{(1,2)}(m_{s},m_{\sigma})

\displaystyle\quad+\cos^{2}\varphi_{p}\mathcal{FB}_{(1,2)}(m_{s},m_{\eta^{\prime}})\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{24\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}^{2}-1}{2N_{c}}g_{s\bar{s}A}^{2}\Big[2(4-\eta_{A})\mathcal{FB}_{(1,2)}(m_{s},0)+3(3-\eta_{s})\big(\mathcal{FB}_{(1,1)}(m_{s},0)-2\mathcal{FB}_{(2,1)}(m_{s},0)\big)\Big].

(63)

This concludes our discussion of the quark sector.

S.2.4
Quark-meson interface and emergent composites

In this Supplement we discuss the absorption of the four-quark scattering in the scalar-pseudoscalar sector into the scattering with emergent composites. This also allows us to take into account multi-scattering events of resonant soft modes such as the pion. The flowing field
\dot{\Phi}
only has components in the light and strange sector,

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}[\Phi]

\displaystyle=(\bar{q}\,\dot{\bm{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{a}\dot{\bm{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,q\,,\,\bar{q}\,\dot{\bm{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}i\gamma_{5}T^{a}\dot{\bm{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,q\,)\,,

(64)

with the diagonal matrix
\dot{\bm{A}}_{k}
, also called the hadronisation function,

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{A}}_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}\dot{A}_{l,k}&0&0\\
0&\dot{A}_{l,k}&0\\
0&0&\dot{A}_{s,k}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(65)

For convenience, we introduce light and strange matrices
T^{l}
and
T^{s}
,

\displaystyle T^{l}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad T^{s}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\,.

(66)

Now we take a four-quark derivative of the generalised flow equation
41
and project onto the
scalar-pseudoscalar sector. Heuristically this can be understood as the second
(\bar{q}\,T^{i}q)
-derivative of
41
with
i=s,l
. This leads us to

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}\lambda_{i}+2\eta_{q_{i}}\lambda_{i}+\dot{A}_{i,k}h_{i,k}=-\text{Flow}_{(\bar{q}T^{i}q)(\bar{q}T^{i}q)}^{(4)},\qquad\text{with}\qquad i=l,s\,.

(67)

Here,
\text{Flow}_{(\bar{q}T^{i}q)(\bar{q}T^{i}q)}^{(4)}
denotes the projection of the four-quark flow onto the respective tensor structure with the coupling
\lambda_{i}
. We determine
\dot{\bm{A}}
such that the four-quark couplings
\lambda_{l,s}
vanish,

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\equiv 0\,,\quad\forall k\,,\qquad i=l,s\,.

(68)

Equation

68
is obtained with

\displaystyle\dot{A}_{l,k}=-\frac{1}{h_{l}}\text{Flow}_{(\bar{q}T^{l}q)^{2}}^{(4)}\,,

\displaystyle\dot{A}_{s,k}=-\frac{1}{h_{s}}\text{Flow}_{(\bar{q}T^{s}q)^{2}}^{(4)}\,.

(69)

For the Yukawa couplings
h_{i}
we use that they can be obtained from the mass functions by dividing out
\sigma_{i}
with
i=l,s
. Hence we project the bilinear quark derivative of
41
onto its scalar part at vanishing momentum and multiply it with
1/\sigma_{l}
and
\sqrt{2}/\sigma_{s}
respectively. Heuristically these are derivatives with respect to
(\bar{q}\,T^{i}q)
, multiplied with
1/\sigma_{i}
. With this procedure we arrive at

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h_{i}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\eta_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\phi_{i}}\bigg)h_{i}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\frac{\partial\left({V}_{\phi}(\phi)+c_{i}\,\sigma_{i}\right)}{\partial\sigma_{i}}\dot{A}_{i}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\text{Re}\left[({\text{Flow}}^{(2)}_{(\bar{q}T^{i}q)}\right]\,.

(70)

In the present work we use
\eta_{\phi_{i}}=\eta_{\phi}
, see
16
and the respective discussion.

The combination
{V}_{\phi}(\phi)+c_{i}\,\sigma_{i}
removes the explicit breaking term from the flow, see
41
and leads to manifest chiral invariance of the flow in the absence of the anomalous breaking of chiral invariance.
This term has the explicit form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{l}}\frac{\partial\left({V}_{\phi}(\phi)+c_{l}\,\sigma_{l}\right)}{\partial\sigma_{l}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{c_{A}\sigma_{s}}{\sqrt{2}}+V_{\chi}^{(1,0)}-V_{\chi}^{(0,1)}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{s}}\frac{\partial\left({V}_{\phi}(\phi)+c_{s}\,\sigma_{s}\right)}{\partial\sigma_{s}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{c_{A}\sigma_{l}^{2}}{2\sqrt{2}\sigma_{s}}+V_{\chi}^{(1,0)}+2V_{\chi}^{(0,1)}\,.

(71)

Note that
({1}/{\sigma_{l}})\,{\partial\left({V}_{\phi}(\phi)+c_{l}\,\sigma_{l}\right)}/{\partial\sigma_{l}}
is nothing but the pion mass
m_{\pi}^{2}
as in the two flavour case.

The last term on the right hand side of the light flavour case in the first line of
70
reads

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{\sigma}_{l}}\text{Re}({\text{Flow}}_{(\bar{q}T^{l}q)}^{(2)})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}h_{l}^{3}\big[3\mathcal{L}_{(1,1)}(m_{l},m_{a_{0}})-3\mathcal{L}_{(1,1)}(m_{l},m_{\pi})+\cos^{2}\varphi_{s}\mathcal{L}_{(1,1)}(m_{l},m_{f_{0}})

\displaystyle\quad-\cos^{2}\varphi_{p}\mathcal{L}_{(1,1)}(m_{l},m_{\eta})+\sin^{2}\varphi_{s}\mathcal{L}_{(1,1)}(m_{l},m_{\sigma})-\sin^{2}\varphi_{p}\mathcal{L}_{(1,1)}(m_{l},m_{\eta^{\prime}})]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{3}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}^{2}-1}{2N_{c}}g^{2}_{l\bar{l}A}h_{l}\mathcal{L}_{(1,1)}(m_{l},0)\,.

(72)

The last term on the right hand side of the strange flavour case in the second line of
70
reads

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{s}}\text{Re}({\text{Flow}}_{(\bar{q}T^{s}q)}^{(2)})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}h_{s}^{3}\big[\sin^{2}\varphi_{s}\mathcal{L}_{(1,1)}(m_{s},m_{f_{0}})-\sin^{2}\varphi_{p}\mathcal{L}_{(1,1)}(m_{s},m_{\eta})+\cos^{2}\varphi_{s}\mathcal{L}_{(1,1)}(m_{s},m_{\sigma})

\displaystyle\quad-\cos^{2}\varphi_{p}\mathcal{L}_{(1,1)}(m_{s},m_{\eta^{\prime}})]-\frac{3}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}^{2}-1}{2N_{c}}g^{2}_{s\bar{s}A}h_{s}\mathcal{L}_{(1,1)}(m_{s},0)\,.

(73)

In our calculations, we distinguish the four-quark flows for the light and strange quarks. The gluon exchange diagrams are given by

\displaystyle{\text{Flow}}^{(4),A}_{(\bar{q}T^{i}q)^{2}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\frac{N_{c}^{2}-1}{2N_{c}}\left(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}}\right)g_{q_{i}\bar{q}_{i}A}^{4}\bigg\{\frac{2}{15}(5-\eta_{A})[\mathcal{FB}_{(1,3)}(m_{q_{i}},0)

\displaystyle-(m_{q_{i}}/k)^{2}\mathcal{FB}_{(2,3)}(m_{q_{i}},0)]+\frac{1}{12}(4-\eta_{q_{i}})[\mathcal{FB}_{(2,2)}(m_{q_{i}},0)]-2(m_{q_{i}}/k)^{2}\mathcal{FB}_{(3,2)}(m_{q_{i}},0)]\bigg\}.

(74)

In the computation of the meson exchange diagrams, we have ignore the mixing angles between the light-strange basis and the physical basis. The expression of meson exchange diagrams for light quark are the same as that in
Fu et al. 2020a
. For the strange quark, the meson exchange diagrams read

\displaystyle{\text{Flow}}^{(4),\phi}_{(\bar{q}T^{s}q)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\frac{1}{N_{c}}{h}_{s}^{4}\bigg\{\frac{2}{15}(5-\eta_{\phi})\Big[\Big(\mathcal{FBB}_{(1,1,2)}({m}_{s}^{2},{m}_{\eta_{s}}^{2},{m}_{\sigma_{s}}^{2})+\mathcal{FBB}_{(1,1,2)}({m}_{s}^{2},{m}_{\sigma_{s}}^{2},{m}_{\eta_{s}}^{2})

\displaystyle\quad-2\mathcal{FB}_{(1,3)}({m}_{s}^{2},{m}_{\eta_{s}}^{2})\Big)-({m}_{s}/k)^{2}\Big(\mathcal{FBB}_{(2,1,2)}({m}_{s}^{2},{m}_{\eta_{s}}^{2},m_{\sigma_{s}}^{2})+\mathcal{FBB}_{(2,2,1)}({m}_{s}^{2},{m}_{\eta_{s}}^{2},{m}_{\sigma_{s}}^{2})

\displaystyle\quad-2\mathcal{FB}_{(2,3)}({m}_{s}^{2},{m}_{\eta_{s}}^{2})\Big)\Big]+\frac{1}{6}(4-\eta_{s})\Big[\Big(\mathcal{FBB}_{(2,1,1)}({m}_{s}^{2},{m}_{\eta_{s}}^{2},{m}_{\sigma_{s}}^{2})-\mathcal{FB}_{(2,2)}({m}_{s}^{2},{m}_{\eta_{s}}^{2})\Big)

\displaystyle\quad-2({m}_{s}/k)^{2}\Big(\mathcal{FBB}_{(3,1,1)}({m}_{s}^{2},{m}_{\eta_{s}}^{2},{m}_{\sigma_{s}}^{2})-\mathcal{FB}_{(3,2)}({m}_{s}^{2},{m}_{\eta_{s}}^{2})\Big)\Big]\bigg\}\,.

(75)

This concludes our discussion of the quark-meson interface.

S.3
Physics Parameters at the initial cutoff scale

In functional QCD, the only parameters to fix are that of fundamental QCD. In the 2+1 flavour QCD case discussed here, these are only the current quark masses
m_{i}^{0}
with
i=l,s
at the initial infrared cutoff scale chosen deep in the UV,
k=\Lambda_{\textrm{UV}}
. The current quark masses have to be adjusted such that physical QCD is approached in the infrared. This simply means that
m_{i}^{0},m_{s}^{0}
are fixed by two appropriately chosen two observables, such as the ratios
m_{\pi}/f_{\pi},m_{K}/f_{\pi}
. For a comprehensive discussion see in particular
Ihssen et al. 2024
;
Fu et al. 2025c
. Such a procedure amounts to the following: we use a small strong coupling at the initial scale
\Lambda_{\textrm{UV}}
. Then the effective action at
k=0
is computed with the flow equation. The correct ratios
m_{\pi}/f_{\pi},m_{K}/f_{\pi}
are obtained by fine-tuning the initial conditions and rerunning the flow until convergence. This procedure has been done in
Ihssen et al. 2024
;
Fu et al. 2025c
and we refer to these works for more details. Furthermore, it has been checked in
Fu et al. 2025c
that an initial scale

\Lambda_{\textrm{UV}}\approx 20
GeV allows for using a classical approximation of the effective action at this scale: all non-trivial parts of the initial effective action are set to zero at this scale, leaving us only with the parameters of fundamental QCD.

S.3.2
Phenomenological parameters

In this work, we do not resolve the full QCD dynamics. Specifically, we have to compensate for dropped tensor structures in the quark-gluon vertex, the only qualitative resolution of the running of the ’t Hooft determinant computed in
Pawlowski 1998

Haas et al. 2013

Infrared enhancement of the quark-gluon coupling
