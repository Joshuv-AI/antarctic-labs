Genomic landscape and tumor immune microenvironment of osteosarcoma: Bridging mechanistic insights to precision therapeutics
Authors: Chenchen Yang, Yiyang Yu, Yaqi Wang, Zhenyu Cai, Yuwei Zhao, Hengyue Ma, Jie Xu, Huaiqiu Zhu, Tingting Ren, Xiaodong Tang, Lu Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteosarcoma (OTS) is a highly aggressive primary bone malignancy characterized by profound genomic instability and a complex, immunosuppressive tumor immune microenvironment (TIME). Despite the established standard of neoadjuvant chemotherapy and surgical resection, the 5-year overall survival rates for metastatic or relapsed disease have remained stagnant at approximately 40% for decades. The high degree of heterogeneity and the “immune cold” nature of OTS pose significant challenges to the efficacy of conventional and emerging therapies. This review aims to summarize recent advances in the genomic and immunological landscape of OTS to inform personalized precision medicine.Main bodyGenomic profiling reveals that OTS is driven by large-scale structural variations rather than single oncogenic drivers, with chromothripsis occurring in over 70% of cases. Frequent alterations in tumor suppressor genes, such as TP53 (80%) and RB1 (40%), fuel clonal evolution and therapeutic resistance. Furthermore, epigenetic dysregulation, including aberrant DNA methylation and alternative mRNA splicing, further complicates the molecular landscape. The OTS TIME is predominantly “cold,” characterized by low T-cell infiltration and an abundance of M2-polarized tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), regulatory T cells (Tregs), and myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs). While immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) targeting PD-1/PD-L1 have shown limited clinical success, emerging precision strategies, such as antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) targeting B7-H3 or LRRC15, CAR-T cell therapies, and targeting of the CD47–SIRPα axis, demonstrate promising potential in preclinical and early-phase clinical trials.ConclusionThe transition from universal treatment to precision therapy in OTS requires a multi-omics approach for patient stratification. Future strategies must focus on overcoming the physical and immunological barriers of the TIME, reprogramming immunosuppressive cells, and exploiting novel targets like B7-H3 and MTAP deletion to improve outcomes for patients with advanced disease.Keywords: Osteosarcoma, Genomics, Immunotherapy, Precision therapy, Immune microenvironment

Introduction

High-grade Osteosarcoma (OTS) is the most common primary bone malignancy, which occurs predominantly in the metaphysis of long bones and affects adolescents mostly, with an annual incidence of approximately 2–4 per million (Arndt et al., 2012; Gatta et al., 2017; Mirabello et al., 2009). The histopathological character appears as spindle-like cells that produce osteoid matrix, and it is believed to originate from a malignant transformation of a mesenchymal progenitor (Raymond and Jaffe, 2009; Mohseny et al., 2009). Most OTSs have a complex karyotype that is caused by chromothripsis and lack a recurring gene that can be effectively targeted (Espejo Valle-Inclan et al., 2025; Cortés-Ciriano et al., 2020; Behjati et al., 2017; Chen et al., 2014; Kuijjer et al., 2013; Lau et al., 2004). Although various genetic mutations and genomic structural events have been linked to OTS, these are often unique to each patient. The genetic alterations, such as mutations in TP53 and Rb1, are considered potential contributors (Chen et al., 2014; Mirabello et al., 2020; Suehara et al., 2019). Although OTS is relatively rare, it is notorious for its high malignancy, rapid progression, marked heterogeneity, and strong propensity for metastasis and recurrence. The introduction of multi-agent chemotherapy decades ago improved 5-year event-free survival (EFS) from less than 20% to 70% in localized high-grade OTS (Fuchs et al., 1998; Luetke et al., 2014). Since the 1980s, the 5-year overall survival rates (OS) have remained stable for all OTS independent from treatment protocol, around 80% for localized OTS and 40% for metastatic disease (Bielack et al., 2002).

Nowadays, the standard treatment strategy for OTS follows a multidisciplinary therapy comprising neoadjuvant therapy, surgical resection, and adjuvant therapy. Since the 1980s, the MAP regimen (methotrexate, doxorubicin, and cisplatin) has remained the first-line standard chemotherapy for OTS, with no superior alternatives emerging over decades (Fuchs et al., 1998; Marina et al., 2016; Bielack et al., 2015; Piperno-Neumann et al., 2016; Hiraga et al., 2025; Ferrari et al., 2012; Meyers et al., 2005; Le Deley et al., 2007). Progress in adjuvant therapies has largely stagnated, and the outcomes for patients with relapsed or refractory disease remain dismal due to the absence of effective and durable treatment methods (Whelan et al., 2012; Gelderblom et al., 2011). Anti-angiogenic or multi-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) have exhibited promising efficacy in the treatment of refractory and relapsed OTS in recent years. However, responses to monotherapy are generally unsustainable, with resistance developing in 4–6 months (Xie et al., 2020a; Xie et al., 2019; Italiano et al., 2020; Duffaud et al., 2019; Grignani et al., 2015). Meanwhile, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) being carried out successfully in other tumors have little effect on OTS, as it is considered in an immunosuppressive state with low immune cell infiltration and an inhibitory tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) (Cillo et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2021a; Zhou et al., 2020). It is necessary to explore the landscape of TIME in OTS to develop more suitable immune regulation strategies. In the meantime, there is a significant deficiency of effective biomarkers for treatments, making it challenging to monitor efficacy or predict prognosis (Hegde and Chen, 2020; Herbst et al., 2014). Specific biomarkers remain to be developed to identify the population that can benefit from new treatments at an early stage for refractory cases, thereby achieving precise treatment.

This review aims to comprehensively summarize the current understanding of the genomic landscape and TIME of OTS, focusing on its implications for developing novel strategies for precision therapy. Given the limited efficacy of conventional systemic therapies and the challenges posed by high genomic instability and immunological desert, there is an urgent need to elucidate its molecular underpinnings to identify actionable alterations.

Genomic characteristics

OTS is characterized by profound genomic instability and considerable genetic heterogeneity (Truong et al., 2024). Unlike many epithelial tumors that are driven by point mutations or specific oncogenic pathways, OTS commonly exhibits genomic alterations characterized by copy number variations and large-scale chromosomal rearrangements (Chiappetta et al., 2024). These include aneuploidy, recurrent numerical chromosomal abnormalities, and extensive chromothripsis, all of which indicate a high degree of chromosomal instability (Espejo Valle-Inclan et al., 2025; Cortés-Ciriano et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2014; Wu et al., 2020; Sayles et al., 2019; Smida et al., 2017). Among the various structural aberrations, chromosomal deletions are more frequent than amplifications (Chen et al., 2014). The bias toward gene loss is clinically significant, as it often leads to the inactivation of tumor suppressor genes. Consistent findings highlight that OTS is characterized not by a single oncogenic driver but by a complex network of genomic disarray from diagnosis to relapse, fueling tumor initiation and influencing clonal evolution under therapeutic pressure (Behjati et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2024; Kinnaman et al., 2023; Pires et al., 2023).

Frequently altered genes

The frequent genetic alterations of OTS include TP53, Rb1, CDKN2A, MYC, AURKB, RUNX2, and VEGFR, among which the gene of TP53 is the most common mutation, a transcription factor of genes associated with the cell cycle, functioning as a tumor suppressor (Mirabello et al., 2020; Chiappetta et al., 2024; Kinnaman et al., 2023; Thoenen et al., 2019; Cancer Genome Atlas Research Network, 2017) (Fig. 1).

Over 80% of OTS demonstrate mutations in the TP53 gene and alterations in its regulators (Sayles et al., 2019). Most TP53 mutations are missense mutations in the DNA-binding domain, which not only lose the tumor suppressive function but also acquire oncogenic functions independent of wild-type TP53 (Thoenen et al., 2019; Saba et al., 2024a). However, instead of somatic nucleotide variants, structural variations of TP53 are in the leading position of OTS. Chen, Bahrami (Chen et al., 2014) found that 26% of OTSs have TP53 point mutations through whole-genome sequencing of 34 samples, and 55% have structural variations in the TP53 gene, the majority of which are translocations with breakpoints in intron 1. Ribi, Baumhoer (Ribi et al., 2015) found that rearrangements in intron 1 of the TP53 gene occur in 16% of OTS, whereas no other tumors exhibit such TP53 rearrangements. The loss-of-function mutation in TP53 promotes osteogenic differentiation of bone marrow stromal cells and induces OTS cells to lose the mechanism for checking and repairing DNA damage, resulting in increased gene instability and chromosomal aberrations (Danovi, 2022; Zuffa et al., 2008). Meanwhile, the mutation of the regulators of TP53 also impacts the occurrence and development of OTS. MDM2 is the principal negative regulator of p53, maintaining its homeostasis by promoting ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation, whose copy number is amplified more than 3-fold in 15%–35% of OTS (Duhamel et al., 2012).

Besides copy number deletions, copy number amplification also occurs in OTS. Leanne C. and colleagues identified six gene-targeting pathways in an OTS patient-derived xenograft (PDX) model based on whole-genome sequencing. These pathways include: MYC amplification, CCNE amplification, VEGFA amplification, CDK4 amplification, AURKB amplification, and AKT amplification (Sayles et al., 2019). Notably, all of the “genome-matched” drugs demonstrated greater than or nearly 60% inhibition of OTS growth in vivo, indicating the potential for a positive therapeutic effect in patients (Wong et al., 2012). Clinical trials of corresponding gene-targeted inhibitors have also been carried out in OTS. However, since the amplification of different genes only occurs in a small number of patients and single-agent treatment rapidly leads to the development of resistance, the efficacy of widespread medication is not clear (Macy et al., 2024).

Complex structural variations and chromothripsis

Structural variations (SVs), especially complex structural variations (CSVs), are more prevalent in OTS than single-nucleotide variations (SNVs), with chromothripsis being a significant mutation type that leads to substantial gene instability and heterogeneity (Mirabello et al., 2020; Kinnaman et al., 2023). Chromothripsis is a catastrophic event that generates extensive genetic rearrangements, causing genome instability and the occurrence and development of cancer (Simovic-Lorenz and Ernst, 2025). This phenomenon is not prevalent among pan-cancer samples, with an overall incidence between 2% and 3% (Cortés-Ciriano et al., 2020; Drews et al., 2022). However, more than 70% of OTS cases experience chromothripsis, which is the highest malignant tumors in distinct cohorts of patients (Espejo Valle-Inclan et al., 2025; Voronina et al., 2020).

Chromothripsis primarily occurs in actively replicating cells and is often triggered by the formation of micronuclei and chromosomal bridges. In a healthy cell, chromothripsis can arrest the cell cycle and lead to cell death (Crasta et al., 2012). However, malignant cells that survive these genetic events may gain various selective advantages (Wu et al., 2019; Hung et al., 2021). Shoshani, Brunner (Shoshani et al., 2021) had identified that chromothripsis is a primary mechanism that accelerates genomic DNA rearrangement and amplification into extrachromosomal DNA (ecDNA) and enables rapid acquisition of tolerance and oncogene amplifications. However, the association among ecDNA, chromothripsis, and the pathogenesis of osteosarcoma has not been evaluated yet. Gong, Zou (Gong et al., 2023) found that the inactivation of Ran GTPase-activating protein 1 (RanGAP1) is commonly observed in human OTS. This inactivation is associated with a high rate of chromothripsis, which leads to the inactivation of Rb1 and the degradation of p53, inducing a failure in DNA damage repair and contributing to the rapid tumorigenesis of OTS in preclinical models (Gong et al., 2023). Recently, Jose E. elaborated a specific mechanism, loss-translocation-amplification (LTA) chromothripsis, particularly prevalent in OTS driven by TP53 mutation, and prompts rapid clonal diversification and intra-tumor heterogeneity. Chromothripsis can induce a lot of loss of heterozygosity (LOH). High-grade OTS exhibits higher levels of LOH compared to low-grade cases, and the extent of genome-wide LOH is the only genomic event significantly correlated with progressive disease in both univariate and multivariate analyses (Espejo Valle-Inclan et al., 2025).

Epigenetic insights

In addition to genomic alterations, the transcriptome also plays a crucial role in the development and progression of OTS. Whole-genome sequencing suggests that half of OTS cases exhibit abnormal epigenetic patterns, such as those involving transcription factors or epigenetic regulators, that affect the expression of a range of downstream genes without involving DNA alterations (Truong et al., 2024; Roberts et al., 2019; Ma et al., 2018). DNA methylation and histone acetylation are two pivotal epigenetic mechanisms regulating gene transcription (Dai et al., 2021). In vitro data consistently indicated that the broad-spectrum histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitors were cytotoxic for OTS cells by impairing oxidative phosphorylation, cytoskeleton remodeling, cell cycle, angiogenesis, and ubiquitin-proteasome pathways (McGuire et al., 2020; Murahari et al., 2017). The methylation of OTS is different from that in colorectal cancer and glioma, enriched for open sea and intergenic sites. Hypomethylated OTS was more likely to have an optimal response to chemotherapy (Lietz et al., 2022).

During the splicing of precursor mRNA into mature mRNA, the spliceosome selectively includes or excludes different exons or introns, resulting in the generation of multiple mRNA isoforms from a single gene and producing diverse protein products. Skipping exon 11 of the insulin receptor (INSR) gene can increase the INSR-A: INSR-B ratio to increase cell growth, migration, and viability in the OTS (Malakar et al., 2016; Avnet et al., 2009). Venkataramany, Schieffer (Venkataramany et al., 2022) identified alternative splicing of MDM2, which induced the MDM2-A transcript, lacking its TP53 binding domain, resulting in diminished direct p53 protein interaction and contributing to the aggressive nature of tumors. The altered splice donor site of intron 11 of PI3KR1 results in exon 11 skipping and acquired PIK3R1, constitutively activating the PI3K signaling pathway to promote cell growth and proliferation in OTS (Lucas et al., 2014). Antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) may be a new treatment strategy by blocking splicing factor binding sites to switch the splicing of cancer-driving genes (Bennett, 2019). The ASOs of splicing factors SRSF3, mediating INSR alternative splicing, can reduce the levels of INSR-A, resulting in decreased angiogenesis and proliferation (Guo et al., 2018). SRSF2 is another promising target of ASO to prevent MDM2 variants by decreasing skipping of exons 4–11 (Comiskey Jr. et al., 2020).

Impact on the TIME

Osteosarcoma is defined by high chromosomal instability and extensive somatic copy number alterations. Across multiple cancer types, high mutation burdens correlate with reduced CD8+ T cell infiltration and a proportional increase in immunosuppressive M2-like macrophages (Davoli et al., 2017). Single-cell analyses in osteosarcoma confirm this pattern, showing that intratumoral genomic heterogeneity directly supports the recruitment of M2-like macrophages to form a suppressive stromal network (Zhou et al., 2020). Furthermore, the widespread chromosomal instability in osteosarcoma leads to the frequent accumulation of cytosolic DNA. Although this would typically trigger immune surveillance, chronic cGAS-STING pathway activation in highly unstable tumors paradoxically promotes downstream immunosuppressive signaling (Bakhoum et al., 2018). This creates a distinct genomic-immunological disconnect: while osteosarcoma exhibits mutational signatures similar to homologous recombination deficiency, which typically indicates elevated neoantigen loads and an “immune-hot” state, the local microenvironment remains highly “cold” (Kovac et al., 2015).

Immune microenvironment and immunosuppressive mechanism

OTS is a highly malignant primary bone tumor characterized by aggressive local invasion and a high propensity for metastasis with a suppressive TIME (Zhou et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2021; Tian et al., 2023). The TIME of OTS comprises a complex network of immune cells, stromal cells, vasculature, cytokines, and metabolic factors. Numerous studies have demonstrated that the TIME of OTS exhibits pronounced immunosuppressive characteristics, predominantly orchestrated by M2-polarized tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), Tregs, and myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) (Han et al., 2016; Buddingh et al., 2011).

Immune microenvironment

Some studies have mapped the immune-related genomic landscape of OTS. In the analysis of immune scores from four OTS cohorts found that the median immune scores for OTS were generally in the lower to middle range (ICGC/TCGA Pan-Cancer Analysis of Whole Genomes Consortium, 2020). These scores were significantly lower than those observed in immunologically “hot” tumors such as melanoma and lung adenocarcinoma, but slightly higher than those in “cold” tumors like uveal melanoma and glioblastoma, placing OTS within the category of immunologically “cold” tumors overall.

Immune cells within the TIME are broadly categorized into immunostimulatory cells that exert antitumor effects and immunosuppressive cells that promote tumor progression. The balance between these two populations is a key determinant of tumor progression and the efficacy of immunotherapeutic strategies (Wan et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2021b). Accumulating evidence suggests that the TIME of OTS is predominantly infiltrated by immunosuppressive cells, among which M2-polarized TAMs are the most abundant (Liu et al., 2021a; Zhou et al., 2020). The M2-TAMs are typically marked by the expression of CD163 and CD206 and function through the secretion of immunosuppressive cytokines such as IL-10 and TGF-β. MDSCs are also enriched in OTS and act as potent immunosuppressors by inhibiting the functions of T cells and NK cells, thereby facilitating tumor immune escape (Lasser et al., 2024; Porta et al., 2020; Joshi and Sharabi, 2022). Similarly, regulatory T cells (Tregs) increase within the TIME and suppress CD8+ T cell function via IL-10 and TGF-β secretion (Fritzsching et al., 2015; Yoshida et al., 2020).

In contrast, immunostimulatory cells are relatively scarce in the TIME of OTS. These cells include cytotoxic CD8+ T cells, Th1-type CD4+ T cells, M1-polarized macrophages, NK cells, and dendritic cells. M1-TAMs, which are characterized by the expression of CD80 and CD86, present antigens through MHC class II to activate T cells. However, M1-TAMs are significantly reduced, while M2-TAMs are prevalent in OTS (Zhou et al., 2020). This skewing can be attributed to several factors within the TIME. Elevated levels of IL-10 and TGF-β inhibit M1 polarization while promoting M2 differentiation (Peura et al., 2025). Additionally, tumor-derived macrophage colony-stimulating factor (M-CSF) plays a role, along with insufficient levels of IFN-γ and lipopolysaccharide (LPS), which are necessary to activate M1 macrophages (Song et al., 2024; Bowlby et al., 2023). Although cytotoxic CD8+ T cells, which are capable of directly killing tumor cells, are present in limited numbers, studies have demonstrated that they often exhibit an exhausted phenotype marked by high programmed death 1 (PD-1) expression (Zheng et al., 2018). NK cells, which mediate MHC-independent tumor cell lysis, are also sparsely infiltrated, and their function is hindered by TGF-β signaling (Sivori et al., 2019; Maskalenko et al., 2022). Dendritic cells within the TIME display impaired antigen-presenting capabilities, which limit their ability to activate T cells effectively (Makino et al., 2022). B cells are known to contribute to antitumor immunity, yet they are infrequently detected in OTS. Their functional relevance remains poorly understood and underexplored (Zhou et al., 2020) (Fig. 2).

Immunosuppressive mechanism

The immunosuppressive cells and cytokines present during OTS play a significant role in immune resistance. The M2-TAMs and MDSCs secrete cytokines such as TGF-β and IL-10, which inhibit the infiltration of cytotoxic immune cells that target tumor cells. TGF-β impairs the function of CD8+ T cells, promotes the expansion of Tregs, and facilitates immune evasion (Batlle and Massagué, 2019; Kureshi and Dougan, 2025). Meanwhile, IL-10 suppresses Th1 responses and enhances Treg activation, thereby dampening the body's antitumor immune responses (Salkeni and Naing, 2023). Additionally, VEGF promotes angiogenesis and tumor metastasis while also impairing the maturation of dendritic cells, further weakening immune surveillance (Chryplewicz et al., 2022). IFN-γ is an immunostimulatory cytokine that enhances the functions of CD8+ T cells and NK cells (Kureshi and Dougan, 2025). However, its expression is typically low in the TIME of OTS, which contributes to reduced antitumor immunity. IL-12 promotes Th1 immune responses and enhances T cell activity, but its expression is often suppressed by M2-polarized TAM (Liu et al., 2024; Bhatia et al., 2023). Additionally, TNF-α can induce apoptosis in tumor cells and activate M1 macrophages, while its levels in the TIME are also limited (Wang et al., 2022a; Xiao et al., 2014). Beyond immune cells and cytokines, stromal components in the TIME also influence immune regulation. Under tumor-derived stimuli, normal fibroblasts can differentiate into cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), which secrete TGF-β to suppress CD8+ T cells and enhance Treg activity (Jenkins et al., 2022; Arpinati et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2022a; Li et al., 2024). CAFs also secrete CXCL12 to recruit MDSCs and activate the IL-6/STAT3 pathway to promote drug resistance against chemotherapy and targeted therapies (Timperi et al., 2022). Moreover, CAFs produce MMPs that degrade the extracellular matrix (ECM), facilitating tumor invasion, and secrete VEGF to promote neovascularization, thereby supporting tumor growth and distant metastasis (Cavaco et al., 2017; Kim et al., 2022). In the OTS, CAFs were found with a higher infiltrating level in recurrent cases by upregulating the secretion of lysyl oxidase (LOX) to induce poor prognosis. The inhibitor of LOX reduces the M2-TAMs and increases the infiltration of CD8+ T cells of OTS in vivo (Huang et al., 2022b).

Both OTS cells and infiltrating immune cells express a range of immune checkpoint and costimulatory molecules that modulate immune responses. These include inhibitory receptors such as programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), CTLA-4, LAG-3, TIM-3, TIGIT, VISTA, and B7-H3, as well as costimulatory molecules like OX40, 4-1BB, and GITR (Kong, 2020). PD-L1, a type I transmembrane protein of the immunoglobulin superfamily, is primarily expressed on tumor cells and antigen-presenting cells. By engaging with PD-1 on T cells, PD-L1 triggers T cell apoptosis, dysfunction, exhaustion, and IL-10 overproduction (Cha et al., 2019). A study indicated that PD-1 blockade could also redirect M2 polarization and suppress OTS metastasis in murine models (Dhupkar et al., 2018). Nevertheless, clinical trials have reported disappointing outcomes for ICIs in OTS, with response rates lower than 5%, in stark contrast to the successes seen in melanoma and NSCLC (Tawbi et al., 2017; D'Angelo et al., 2018). Compared to PD-L1, B7-H3 shows much higher expression levels in OTS, reported in approximately 75%–95% of cases (Wang et al., 2013). A member of the immunoglobulin superfamily, B7-H3 suppresses CD8+ T cell proliferation and cytokine production, promotes M2 macrophage polarization, and is associated with increased metastasis, recurrence, and poor prognosis in OTS (Wang et al., 2013; Lee et al., 2017).

Precision therapy

Current situation and challenges

Gene-targeted therapy

Currently, there are no specifically effective treatment targets for OTS. Frequent gene alterations, such as TP53 and Rb1, are undruggable by direct targeting approaches. Targeted therapy for TP53 and its corresponding regulators has been carried out in OTS; most of them stagnate at the preclinical research stage (Saba et al., 2024a; Tang et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2018; Saba et al., 2024b; Stein et al., 2022). Nutlin-3a was the first report for an MDM2 inhibitor, binding MDM2 in the TP53-binding pocket and activating TP53 to arrest the cell cycle in OTS xenograft mouse models (Wang et al., 2012; Kucab et al., 2017). Many small-molecule inhibitors have been identified based on the compact and well-defined MDM2-p53 interactions, undergoing clinical assessment at different phases for cancer therapy (Liao et al., 2018). The prognostic effect of TP53 remains controversial. Wunder, Gokgoz (Wunder et al., 2005) found no evidence that TP53 mutations are associated with the histologic response to chemotherapy and predict for the development of metastases in patients with high-grade OTS. However, 1004 whole-exome sequencing from the National Cancer Institute demonstrated that a TP53 variant was more likely to be detected in patients with axial skeleton lesions who experience metastases at diagnosis, leading to a worse prognosis (Mirabello et al., 2020).

Although MYC, like RAS and TP53, has been regarded as an “undruggable” or a difficult-to-drug gene, several compounds that directly or indirectly target MYC have recently been reported to possess anticancer activity in preclinical tumor models (Duffy et al., 2021). Inhibitors of BET proteins, especially the BRD4 inhibitor JQ1, have been shown to downregulate MYC and suppress tumor growth in a diverse range of animal models exhibiting MYC activation (Delmore et al., 2011). MYC leads to replication stress and DNA damage, making cancer cells highly dependent on the DNA damage repair pathway and more sensitive to PARP inhibitors (Konecny, 2019). Targeted treatments for patients with specific gene amplifications and combination therapies may constitute an effective treatment strategy.

Therapeutic measures for chromothripsis are under exploration. Since chromothripsis is caused by chromosomal separation errors, targeting the spindle is a therapeutic strategy. RanGAP1 counteracts the activity of the spindle-assembly checkpoint during mitosis by safeguarding chromosome segregation to prevent chromosomal instability and chromothripsis. Loss of RanGAP1 is a key driver for OTS tumorigenesis, implicating that targeting RanGAP1 itself or its downstream targets is a potentially promising therapeutic intervention (Gong et al., 2023). Targeting mitotic enzymes can also selectively kill chromosomally unstable cancer cells. Inhibitors of the KIF18A motor protein activate the mitotic checkpoint and destroy the TP53-mutant cell lines with chromosomal instability features (Payton et al., 2024). The use of drugs related to DNA damage is another treatment approach. Studies have identified a strong link between chromothripsis and defective homologous recombination repair in genetic mouse models and human tumors. The combination of poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitor and particle radiotherapy extended survival and treatment efficacy in patient-derived xenografts of chromothriptic medulloblastoma (Simovic et al., 2021). The synergistic effects of the PARP inhibitor and the HDAC inhibitor occurred in several cancers irrespective of the presence of chromothripsis, preventing tumor growth and inducing apoptosis of cancer cells (Khalid et al., 2022). Since chemotherapeutic drugs can cause rearrangements initiated by chromothripsis, combining targeted therapy with DNA repair inhibitors may be an effective strategy to prevent an initial cancer from becoming more aggressive or resistant to treatment (Denisenko et al., 2016; Luo et al., 2022). However, estimates of the prevalence of chromothripsis vary widely between studies due to the inconsistent scoring criteria and different methods of detection (Simovic-Lorenz and Ernst, 2025). The use of low-resolution detection methods might underestimate the prevalence of chromothripsis. High-coverage whole genome sequencing (≥30-fold, ideally 60 to 90-fold coverage) can accurately detect chromothripsis relatively. Due to high costs, this method is limited to small-sample scientific research and has not been widely adopted in clinical tests, which hinders the clinical application of treatments related to chromothripsis.

Drugs for epigenetic regulation are also in the exploration stage. Panobiostat, an HDAC inhibitor, has additive cytotoxicity in vitro with chemotherapies commonly used to treat OTS, doxorubicin (Budman et al., 2011). La Noce, Paino (La Noce et al., 2018) also identified HDAC2 as a potential therapeutic target in human OTS treatment by preventing the acquisition of a stem phenotype and tumorigenesis. However, the current clinical trials to evaluate HDAC inhibitors in OTS are limited. A phase I study of the HDAC inhibitor vorinostat in combination with bortezomib for children with refractory or recurrent solid tumors showed good tolerability but no response in OTS (Schelman et al., 2013). Epigenetic drugs are still in the early phase of therapeutic research for OTS with uncertain clinical efficacy (Table 1).

Immunotherapy

Current analyses of the TIME in OTS consistently indicate that macrophages are the most predominant infiltrating immune cells. Consequently, targeting TAMs has emerged as one of the leading strategies in OTS immunotherapy. Early studies of muramyl tripeptide phosphatidyl-ethanolamine demonstrated its ability to activate monocytes and macrophages to kill tumor cells selectively. This effect is primarily mediated through the activation of the innate immune system via NOD2, a pattern recognition receptor highly expressed in monocytes (Gisch et al., 2011). In a Phase III clinical trial evaluating the addition of mifamurtide to standard chemotherapy for patients with resectable, non-metastatic OTS, the study did not achieve a statistically significant improvement in EFS, but it did show a benefit in OS. As a result, mifamurtide was approved by the European Medicines Agency (EMA) for clinical use (Meyers et al., 2008).

The monoclonal antibodies targeting ICIs such as PD-1/ PD-L1 and CTLA-4 have attracted widespread attention due to their promising anti-tumor efficacy (Reck et al., 2016; Larkin et al., 2015). However, clinical trial results to date have shown that most ICIs have limited effectiveness in treating solid tumors, including OTS (Tawbi et al., 2017; D'Angelo et al., 2018). The underlying reasons remain unclear, but one possibility is that T lymphocytes may not be the primary effector cells responsible for controlling OTS. A study evaluating the expression of PD-1, PD-L1, and PD-L2 in clinical samples from 234 patients with musculoskeletal tumors found that PD-1 and PD-L1 expression were negatively correlated with prognosis in OTS patients. The study also demonstrated that nivolumab could increase the proportion of CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes and enhance CD8+ T cell cytotoxicity, effectively suppressing lung metastasis in in vivo models of OTS (Zheng et al., 2018). Furthermore, reducing PD-1 expression was shown to reprogram M2-TAMs and inhibit lung metastasis in OTS. Despite these findings, clinical trials of PD-1/PD-L1 ICIs in advanced OTS have yielded disappointing results. Multiple clinical trials have demonstrated that OTS exhibits a very low response rate to ICIs. In the SARC028 trial, only a very small number of patients with OTS or soft tissue sarcoma responded to treatment with the PD-1 antibody pembrolizumab (Tawbi et al., 2017). Similarly, in the MD Anderson OTS cohort, none of the four patients who received combined anti-PD-L1 and anti-CTLA-4 therapy achieved an objective response (Somaiah et al., 2022). Xie, Xu (Xie et al., 2020a) conducted the combination of apatinib and camrelizumab in advanced osteosarcoma, showing monotherapy prolongs PFS in comparison to single-agent apatinib, but it did not reach the prespecified target. Immune infiltration levels, such as the presence of CD8+ T cells and PD-L1 expression, have been considered potential biomarkers for predicting ICI response (Park et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2024). However, several studies have pointed out that even patients with high PD-L1 expression and abundant immune cell infiltration may fail to respond to treatment. This highlights that the mechanisms of immune evasion in OTS remain poorly understood and require further investigation.

Predictive indicators of response

Osteosarcoma exhibits profound interpatient heterogeneity, making strict patient stratification essential for maximizing the efficacy of precision therapies. The predominant “immune-cold” phenotype intrinsically predicts primary resistance to single-agent ICI, which was clinically underscored by the SARC028 trial in advanced osteosarcoma (Tawbi et al., 2017). Recent pan-sarcoma analyses highlight that the presence of B cell-enriched tertiary lymphoid structures, alongside a robust baseline proportion of CD4+ T cells and high CD8+ T cell mobilization, dictates favorable responses to immunotherapy (Fritzsching et al., 2015; Petitprez et al., 2020). Identifying these functional subsets determines whether a patient might benefit from ICIs or requires prior microenvironmental priming.

Beyond immune cell infiltration, the composition of the stromal niche predicts the necessity for combinatorial microenvironmental remodeling. Tumors characterized by an “immune-suppressed or myeloid-rich” signature, featuring a high infiltration of M2-like TAMs and aberrant neoangiogenesis, pose formidable spatial barriers to T cell infiltration (Cersosimo et al., 2020). In such subsets, the presence of this fibrotic-vascular signature predicts that immunotherapy alone will fail, highlighting the necessity to integrate receptor TKIs with ICIs (Zhu et al., 2022). The precise inhibition of pro-angiogenic pathways not only normalizes the tumor vasculature but also actively reprograms M2-like TAMs, effectively dismantling the physical barriers that blunt systemic therapies and facilitating the subsequent influx of cytotoxic T cells (Xie et al., 2020b).

Future and strategies

Multi-omics-based population selection and targeted therapy

Due to extremely high heterogeneity, there is currently no universal targeted drug for OTS. Next-generation sequencing, including DNA sequencing and RNA sequencing, can divide patients into specific populations by biological information. Different populations will benefit from specific targeted treatment strategies, leading to more personalized and precise medical care.

Jiang, Wang (Jiang et al., 2022) classified OS into four subtypes, including immune-activated (S-IA), immune-suppressed (S-IS), homologous recombination deficiency dominant, and MYC-driven (S-MD). Potential therapeutic targets are identified for each subtype, including platinum-based chemotherapy, immune checkpoint inhibitors, anti-VEGFR, anti-MYC, and PARPi-based synthetic lethal strategies (Jiang et al., 2022). Wu, Beird (Wu et al., 2020) profiled the composition of infiltrating immune cells in the MDACC OS cohort and identified three immune clusters: high, intermediate, and low. The levels of all immune cell types, including CD8+ T cells, were lowest in the immune-low cluster and highest in the immune-high cluster. Known tumor-intrinsic immunosuppressive pathways, such as PD-1 signaling, CTLA-4 signaling, and IFNG signaling, were found to be dysregulated between the immune-high and immune-low clusters, indicating that tumors in the immune-low group may upregulate immunosuppressive signals that inhibit T cell activation (Wu et al., 2020). Marchais, Marques da Costa (Marchais et al., 2022) used RNA-seq to classify 79 OTS samples into two prognostically distinct subtypes based on their TIME. Group G1 was characterized by innate immune activation, such as type I interferon and inflammatory pathways, while group G2 was enriched in metabolic, stromal, and angiogenesis-related pathways, including PPARγ and VEGFR signaling. Patients in the G1 group had significantly better OS than those in the G2 group. These findings suggest that patients in the G1 group may benefit from monotherapy with ICIs, while those in the G2 group may require combination strategies targeting vasculature, stroma, and immunosuppressive mechanisms (Marchais et al., 2022). van Ewijk, Hiemcke-Jiwa (van Ewijk et al., 2025) further discovered that the G2 gene expression signature and MYC expression were independently associated with unfavorable outcomes. Wu, Gong (Wu et al., 2023) utilized the Therapeutically Applicable Research to Generate Effective Treatments (TARGET) database to create a TME index, which includes the genes MYC, P4HA1, RAMP1, and TAC4. They found that patients with a high TME index had worse outcomes, including OS, recurrence-free survival, and metastasis-free survival. Conversely, a low TME index was associated with immune-related signaling pathways, such as the inflammatory response. This suggests that patients with a higher TME index have an immune-cold TIME (Wu et al., 2023).

Overcoming the immunosuppressive microenvironment

As a member of a typical immune cold tumor, OTS has a limited presence of cytotoxic cells with unclear mechanisms involved. Targeting macrophages, the primary immunosuppressive cells, is crucial to overcoming the immunosuppressive microenvironment. A promising approach involves inhibiting the CD47–SIRPα signaling axis, which represents a specific mechanism for activating macrophage-mediated anti-tumor responses (Veillette and Chen, 2018). In OTS, CD47 expression is typically higher in tumor tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues. Anti-CD47 antibodies have been shown to enhance macrophage-mediated phagocytosis in vitro and reduce lung metastases in OTS PDX models (Xu et al., 2015). The combination of anti-GD2 and anti-CD47 in syngeneic and xenograft mouse models of OTS reduces tumor burden and extends survival (Theruvath et al., 2022). A relative clinical trial of magrolimab (anti-CD47) and dinutuximab (anti-GD-2) is recruiting. Beyond activation, reprogramming TAMs from a tumor-promoting M2 phenotype to a tumoricidal M1 phenotype is another major therapeutic avenue. Toll-like receptors (TLRs), key pathogen recognition receptors expressed on antigen-presenting cells, can also be targeted by agonists to induce M1-like polarization and enhance anti-tumor immune responses. Colony-stimulating factor 1 (CSF1) is a primary regulator of the survival, proliferation, and differentiation of monocytes and macrophages. Pexidartinib, a CSF1R inhibitor, has been approved that reduces M2 polarization and stimulates T-cell infiltration in the sarcoma. The administration of pexidartinib to the osteosarcoma orthotopic xenograft model significantly suppressed the primary tumor growth and lung metastasis (Fujiwara et al., 2021).

Another promising strategy to overcome immune resistance is to explore the mechanisms that prohibit T cells from entering the center of the tumor to kill tumor cells. After improving the level of T cells in the tumor fields, the efficacy of ICIs may be increased correspondingly. Recent discoveries have revealed new ways T cells are inhibited, in addition to the well-known suppression by TAMs and MDSCs. Unlike other cancers, OTS contains its own microenvironment, including amounts of osteoblasts, osteoclasts, and mineral matrix, which act as a natural barrier to prevent immune cell infiltration (Alfranca et al., 2015). Breaking through this barrier might be the direction for future efforts to promote immune evolution. Osteoclasts are a type of multinucleated giant cells that reside in tissue, playing a crucial role in the process of osteolysis in bone tissue and are essential for maintaining the homeostasis of the bone microenvironment under physiological conditions (Wada et al., 2006; Boyle et al., 2003; Udagawa et al., 2021). These unique giant cells have a passive effect in pancreatic cancer, breast cancer, and bone metastasis cancer, but their role in OTS remains unclear (Cyrta et al., 2022; Sajjadi et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2022b). A study has shown that osteoclasts can effectively inhibit T cell proliferation in vitro (Grassi et al., 2011). A phase III clinical trial (OS2006) of Zoledronate, a bisphosphonate inhibiting osteoclastic bone resorption, found that giant cells expressing CD163 and CD68 were detected in 9 of 29 pathological specimens of high-grade OTS and linked to a poor prognosis (Nazon et al., 2022; Gomez-Brouchet et al., 2021). However, this clinical trial has not demonstrated the superior efficacy of zoledronate when added to neoadjuvant chemotherapy for OTS (Piperno-Neumann et al., 2016). The role of this type of cell in the TIME remains to be explored. Meanwhile, the collagen-rich ECM and CAFs can influence tumor progression, metastasis, and the poor response of many solid tumors to immunotherapy (Arpinati et al., 2024). The mesoporous polydopamine nanoparticles encapsulating bromelain and the immune adjuvant can efficiently promote dendritic cell maturation, activating antitumor immunity and leading to CTL infiltration into the tumor. This offers a promising strategy for overcoming physical barriers in ECM-rich tumors (Yu et al., 2024). The tumor-targeted IL-12-armed T cells, which bind cell-surface vimentin on tumor cells, eliminate high-density CAFs and ECM in OTS xenograft tumors and reshape the tumor stroma to favor T-cell infiltration and tumor inhibition (Hu et al., 2024). After eliminating the factors that exclude T cells, the efficacy of PD1/PD-L1 inhibitors in OTS may be improved obviously in “hot” tumors.

In addition to removing the inherent T cell inhibition present in the TIME, the exogenous delivery of immune cells could be another effective strategy. A phase I/II clinical trial of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER-2) -CAR-T cells had shown certain efficacy in refractory OTS (Ahmed et al., 2015). B7-H3 targeted CAR-T cells also showed high anti-tumor efficacy against OTS both in vitro and in vivo (Zhang et al., 2022). To enhance antitumor activity, Talbot, Chabot (Talbot et al., 2024) modified B7-H3 CAR-T cells by incorporating CXCR2 and CXCR6 to improve homing toward OTS both in vitro and in vivo. Besides CAR-T cells, other related adoptive cell therapies have also been explored. Engineered T cell receptors (TCR-T cells) can identify both membrane proteins as CAR-T cells and intracellular antigens, enhancing tumor cell detection and its persistence, like MAGE-A3 (Baulu et al., 2023). Recently, innate immunocytes, including NK cells and macrophages also been considered as candidates for adoptive cell therapy. Relative trials are at the phase I/II trial stage or pre-clinical trial and focus on hematological malignancies (Pan et al., 2022). Finding the appropriate target molecules may facilitate the application of these strategies in OTS.

Exploring new targets and drug design

Compared to PD-L1 relative ICIs, targets such as B7-H3, leucine-rich repeat-containing 15 (LRRC15), HER2, and GD2 exhibit more promising characteristics for CAR-T, TCR-T, and antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) in OTS. Preclinical studies have demonstrated that targeting B7-H3 using CAR-T cells or microRNAs can improve the TIME of OTS (Talbot et al., 2024). A Phase II clinical trial (NCT05830123) demonstrated that HS-20093, an ADC targeting B7-H3, achieved an ORR of 20.8% and a median PFS of 8.4 months in the OTS (Xie et al., 2024). LRRC15, a leucine-rich repeat-containing protein superfamily member, is associated with cellular adhesion, invasion, and immune responses (Ray et al., 2022). Immunohistochemistry has shown that most OTSs (91.3%) exhibit different degrees of LRRC15 expression (Cui et al., 2020). In a Phase I trial of an ADC of LRRC15, data revealed that out of 27 sarcoma patients, including 10 with OTS, 4 (14.8%) achieved a partial response, while 8 (29.6%) experienced stable disease (Demetri et al., 2021). HER2 is expressed in approximately 40% of OTSs, and extensive research is exploring its potential as a therapeutic target in this context (Ebb et al., 2012). HER2-targeted CAR T-cell therapy has demonstrated some activity in HER2-positive recurrent or metastatic OTS, with 3 of 14 evaluable patients achieving stable disease for 12 to 15 weeks preoperatively (Ahmed et al., 2015). However, the prognostic significance of HER2 in OTS remains a matter of controversy. A prospective clinical trial indicated that HER2 overexpression was not significantly associated with EFS or OS (Gorlick et al., 2014). And a phase II trial of HER2 ADC showed that none of the nine patients enrolled at the first step responded objectively (Nakano, 2023). The glycolipid GD2 is a tumor-associated surface antigen with restricted normal tissue expression, expressed by multiple solid tumors. Although 88% of OTSs express GD2, the effect of anti-GD2 immunotherapy was limited, promoting new strategies for this target. A T cell engaging bispecific antibodies (T-BsAbs) using sequences of anti-CD3 (huOKT3) and anti-GD2 (hu3F8) exhibited a potent anti-tumor effect against OTS tumors in vitro and in vivo (Park and Cheung, 2020). A phase I trial (NCT02107963) of GD2 CAR-T has shown feasibility and safety in treating OTS and neuroblastoma, with post-treatment patients exhibiting increased T cell memory subsets (Kaczanowska et al., 2024).

The deletion of the methylthioadenosine phosphorylase (MTAP) gene is a common molecular event in OTS, found in approximately 28% of cases. This deletion leads to significant changes in the TIME, which is characterized by a reduced infiltration of CD8+ T cells and helper T cells, while there is an increase in malignantly proliferating mesenchymal stem cells (Mu et al., 2025). The accumulation of methylthioadenosine (MTA) inhibits the activity of protein arginine methyltransferase 5 (PRMT5), affecting the mRNA splicing process and leading to DNA damage and mitotic defects, which further exacerbate malignant tumor progression (Kalev et al., 2021). Recent studies indicate that interventions in methionine metabolism, such as methionine-restricted diets or the use of MAT2A inhibitors, can induce PD-L1 expression to enhance the sensitivity of MTAP-deficient osteosarcoma to immune checkpoint therapy (Belmontes et al., 2025). These findings not only elucidate the critical role of MTAP deletion in the pathogenesis and progression of osteosarcoma but also provide a rationale for developing precision therapy strategies targeting this molecular characteristic (Table 2).

There are new drugs designed to treat tumors, including bispecific antibodies or ADCs. The targets of bispecific drugs can be categorized as either immune cells, tumor cells, or both (Li et al., 2020; Tsuchikama et al., 2024). Bispecific T-cell engagers, which have a tumor-antigen binding domain on one end and a T-cell binding domain (usually CD3) on the other, are designed to create an immunological synapse between T cells and tumor cells, and can activate T cells independent of normal TCR/MHC ligand recognition (Wu and Cheung, 2018). The bispecific of B7-H3 and CD3 may be a potential candidate for immunotherapy in the OTS to shorten the distance between T cells and tumor cells. The new ADCs can be divided into bispecific ADCs, biparatopic ADCs, and dual payload ADCs (Tsuchikama et al., 2024). The preclinical trial of B7-H3 ADC had shown promising results in OTS, supporting the design of the new ADCs based on B7-H3 (Kendsersky et al., 2021) (Fig. 3).

Conclusion

OTS presents significant therapeutic challenges due to its complex metagenetics and the presence of immune exclusion. Current treatment options provide limited benefits for patients with advanced disease. However, recent advancements in genomic and immune profiling reveal potential opportunities for new interventions of specific targets and reversing immunosuppression. Future precision therapy should focus on population selection, overcoming immunosuppressive microenvironment, and exploring new targets and drug design.
