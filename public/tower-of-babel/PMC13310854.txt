An adaptive modified Newton–Raphson-based optimizer for efficient optimal power flow performance under multiple operating conditions
Authors: Ali S. Aljumah, Mohammed H. Alqahtani, Abdullah M. Shaheen, Mohamed O. Atallah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The optimal power flow (OPF) problem is essentially about finding the cheapest and safest way to operate a power system without breaking any of the operational limits that govern it. In this paper, we introduce a new Modified Newton–Raphson-Based Optimizer (MNRBO) specifically designed to tackle real-world OPF problems, integrating renewable photovoltaic sources. The NRBO integrates gradient-inspired search using the NR search rule and the trap avoidance strategy. Our MNRBO extends this framework by adding two adaptive components. An Adaptive Crossover Mechanism (ACM) is added that lets solutions dynamically exchange useful information with each other, keeping the population diverse and preventing everyone from getting stuck in the same mediocre spot too soon. Also, a Sigmoid decay mode that smoothly and gradually shifts the algorithm from broad exploration (looking around the whole search space) in the early stages to careful fine-tuning (exploitation) toward the end. This gives much steadier and more predictable convergence than the original abrupt or polynomial decay. The resulting MNRBO algorithm forms a self-evolving optimization framework that automatically adjusts its learning strategy as the search progresses. We thoroughly tested MNRBO on the standard IEEE 30-bus system across a wide range of realistic scenarios: minimizing fuel costs (with smooth quadratic models, valve-point ripples, and multi-fuel options), handling generators with prohibited operating zones, and minimizing transmission losses under normal, peak, and light-load conditions. In every single case, MNRBO delivered better solutions, faster and more consistent convergence, and dramatically lower variation across multiple runs compared to the original NRBO and several other state-of-the-art algorithms. The results clearly show that MNRBO is not only more accurate but also far more robust and dependable, exactly what operators need when solving OPF in real power systems where reliability really matters. To further validate the applicability of the proposed approach under renewable energy uncertainty, a probabilistic OPF framework incorporating photovoltaic renewable generation is developed. In this case study, the integration of renewable solar photovoltaic energy in conditions of variable irradiance is examined using the Point Estimate Method (PEM) with lognormal irradiance modeling. In addition, an ablation study is conducted to quantify the individual contributions of the ACM and sigmoid decay strategy in the presence of renewable photovoltaic sources, demonstrating their significant impact on convergence stability, robustness, and optimization accuracy.

Introduction

The Optimal Power Flow (OPF) problem remains one of the most fundamental and challenging tasks in modern power system operation and planning. At its core, it involves determining the optimal values of control variables, including real and reactive power outputs of generators, voltage magnitudes at generator buses, transformer tap settings, and shunt compensator outputs1, one or more objective functions such as fuel cost, power losses, voltage deviation, or emissions. This optimization process must simultaneously satisfy the system equality constraints represented by power balance equations as well as inequality constraints including generator limits, bus voltage limits, transmission line capacities, and security requirements2. It has long been recognized as a key optimization task in modern power system operation, introduced initially by Carpentier in the 1960s and later formalized by Dommel and Tinney3. The nonlinear, nonconvex, and highly constrained nature of OPF makes it difficult to solve using classical mathematical optimization techniques4,5, especially under multiple and uncertain operating scenarios. Because of that nasty mathematical landscape, traditional gradient-based methods, interior-point, sequential quadratic programming, and the like, often get trapped in local optima or simply fail to converge reliably, especially when the system is large, heavily loaded, or facing uncertainties. The problem is inherently complex, particularly when incorporating renewable energy sources (RES) with their inherent uncertainty and intermittency, making classical gradient-based methods prone to local optima or convergence issues. Consequently, recent research has heavily emphasized metaheuristic and nature-inspired optimization algorithms, which offer robust global search capabilities, faster convergence, and the ability to handle multi-objective formulations and large-scale systems6,7.

Recent research emphasizes bio-inspired and physics-inspired metaheuristics for applications such as reactive power compensation, renewable integration, and economic-environmental dispatch. In8, an updated hummingbird algorithm was used to optimise assigning of Static VAR Compensators (SVCs) in distribution systems, with an emphasis on maximising energy efficiency. This upgrade helped with the installation of FACTS devices for loss minimisation and voltage profile augmentation. In a related study, a modified artificial rabbits optimizer was designed for both siting and dynamic operation of Distribution-SVCs9, adapting to varying load conditions. Modifications to the original rabbit optimiser included features of increased mutation and opposition-based learning to boost performance in power-constrained systems. These technologies demonstrated reliable solutions for real-time control in distribution systems. In10, an enhanced fractional order Kepler optimizer was developed to efficiently integrate STATCOM devices to tackle multi-modal challenges in OPF with renewable PV sources. In this work, improving energy efficiency was addressed by minimizing losses or costs, maximizing PV hosting capacity, and ensuring stability in hybrid PV-STATCOM configurations. For large-scale systems, a weighted mean of vectors technique was developed for Combined Economic and Environmental Dispatch (CEED)11. In this work, the multi-objective problem of fuel costs and emissions was balanced, utilizing vector combining, rule updating, and local searching operators in solving large-scale OPF variants, focusing on scalability and constraint management.

While the primary focus is on power flow optimization, complementary works apply data-driven and optimization techniques to electric vehicle (EV) and battery systems, which interact with power grids. For instance, studies on lithium-ion battery fault diagnosis12, state-of-health estimation13, retirement prediction14, and energy consumption analysis of electric buses15 support broader smart grid and vehicle-to-grid (V2G) optimization frameworks. These can feed into OPF models by providing accurate load or storage representations, though they primarily employ machine learning rather than metaheuristics for core optimization.

Over the past two decades, the power systems community has increasingly turned to metaheuristic algorithms16. These methods don’t need derivatives, they’re remarkably flexible, and they’re designed from the ground up to hunt for good (sometimes near-global) solutions even in the messiest search spaces. Among the earliest and most popular are the Particle Swarm Optimizer (PSO) and17, Differential Evolution (DE) algorithm18, DE with spherical for search space partitioning19, PSO20, colliding bodies optimization algorithm21, Sine-Cosine algorithm22, grey wolf optimizer23, and Zebra optimisation algorithm24. These algorithms mimic natural or social phenomena to explore the solution space effectively and have been successfully applied to OPF for objectives such as real power loss minimization, fuel costs minimization, and total voltage deviation. Despite their success, many of these algorithms face issues such as premature convergence and slow exploitation capability. Consequently, various enhanced or hybrid variants have been proposed. In25, the ensemble of constraint handling techniques-DE integrated multiple constraint-handling methods to efficiently address OPF’s complex operational limits. In26, a modified Jellyfish method incorporating a quasi-reflection learning strategy was presented to solve the OPF issue. In this approach, each jellyfish randomly selected a cluster from the population to represent its social group for information sharing and validated using the IEEE standard systems beside a practical network region in Egypt.

A growing body of work from 2023 to 2025 highlighted the effectiveness of advanced metaheuristics tailored for OPF.

The whale migration algorithm, as demonstrated by Ghasemi et al. (2024), effectively modeled migration behaviors to enhance fuel cost and power loss minimization in power systems while adhering to operational constraints27. Additionally, Ghasemi et al. (2023) presented the Gaussian bare-bones Levy circulatory system-based optimization (GBLCSBO) for optimizing power flow with renewable energy sources28. In this work, the CSBO algorithm’s capacity to handle the stochastic nature of wind and solar generation was improved by incorporating Gaussian bare-bones sampling and Levy flight distributions within the circulatory system framework. Further advancements in comparative metaheuristic analysis were provided by Diab et al. (2024). In this study, six recent algorithms were evaluated, including Salp Swarm Algorithm (SSA), Gradient-Based Optimizer (GBO), Northern Goshawk Optimization (NGO), fast cuckoo search, Dynamic Control Cuckoo Search (DCCS), and Opposition Flow Direction Algorithm (OFDA)—across multiple OPF objectives on IEEE 30-bus and 118-bus systems6. The study found that GBO consistently outperformed in optimality, convergence speed, and statistical robustness across both single- and multi-objective scenarios, including fuel cost minimization, voltage profile improvement, voltage stability enhancement via L-index, and loss minimization6. Also, Aurangzeb et al. (2023) introduced a hybrid metaheuristic approach, by integrating the bird swarm algorithm and jaya optimizer for OPF analysis. This method was evaluated on standard benchmarks, showing significant performance in minimizing generation costs, emissions, voltage deviations, and line losses compared to standalone algorithms29. Most recently, Nassef et al. (2025) applied the red-tailed hawk optimization algorithm to OPF, demonstrating enhanced performance through its hunting-inspired search mechanisms30. In this work, the employed red-tailed hawk optimization demenstrated lower objective values and improved stability margins compared to established techniques, especially in systems with varying load and contingency conditions. Other notable improvements include the Quasi-Reflection-Based Symbiotic Organisms Search (QR-SOS)31, bird swarm technique with orthogonal learning strategy32, enhanced Kepler optimizer33, a composite DE approach with different constrained handling strategies34, and a modified social network algorithm35, each designed to enhance global exploration and stability in constrained optimization scenarios. These studies illustrate a clear trend toward hybrid, Levy-enhanced, and bio-inspired metaheuristics that overcome the limitations of traditional solvers.

On the other side, the cases dealing with hydro-thermal coordination modelled within the OPF framework typically extend the classical OPF formulation to jointly optimize the scheduling of hydroelectric and thermal generating units over a planning horizon. These models incorporate additional constraints36 such as water balance equations, reservoir storage limits, inflow forecasts, and hydro generation curves alongside standard power flow and operational limits, transforming the problem into a coupled hydro-thermal scheduling and OPF task. Metaheuristic approaches, such as the sine cosine algorithm applied to the IEEE 39-bus system, have been employed to solve this integrated formulation by minimizing total operating costs, including thermal fuel and hydro opportunity costs, while ensuring load-generation balance and transmission security under varying demand profiles37. Such coordinated models account for the spatiotemporal dynamics between flexible hydro resources and dispatchable thermal plants, often yielding significant reductions in fuel consumption, improved voltage profiles, and enhanced system reliability compared to decoupled solutions.

The evolution of OPF-solving methodologies has shifted from deterministic mathematical programming to adaptive, self-evolving optimization. Metaheuristic approaches are preferred due to their effectiveness in tackling large-scale, non-linear, and multi-objective problems that classical techniques struggle with38. These methods balance exploration of new solution areas with refining existing solutions39 and are widely applicable in engineering40. Each new algorithm aims to enhance the balance between global and local search behaviors while addressing the operational constraints of real-world power systems. A recently created population-based metaheuristic algorithm of NRBO was given by R. Sowmya et al.41 with succesful implementations in a variety of domains. The NRBO algorithm was succesfully utilized in solving several engineeering problems such as antenna array beam synthesis in wireless communications42, parameter identification of solar PV systems43, integration of fluid antenna systems and reconfigurable surfaces in wireless systems44, control of fuel cells45, and grid-forming with converter control in wind power networks46. In this paper, we take NRBO further and present a Modified Newton–Raphson-Based Optimizer (MNRBO). We maintain NRBO’s core strengths, including the Newton–Raphson search rule and trap avoidance strategy, while introducing two adaptive enhancements that significantly improve performance:

an Adaptive Crossover Mechanism (ACM) that lets solutions intelligently share good traits with each other, keeping the population diverse and fighting premature convergence, and.a smooth Sigmoid decay mode that gently steers the algorithm from wide-ranging exploration at the start to precise exploitation near the end.

The present study considers a generation portfolio composed exclusively of thermal units, as defined in the standard IEEE 30-bus test system adopted in this work. This consideration reflects a widely used and practically validated benchmark in OPF research, where all generating units are modeled with thermal characteristics, including quadratic, valve-point, and multi-fuel cost functions. Although hydro-thermal coordination represents another important extension in power system optimization, it introduces additional complexities such as water balance constraints, reservoir dynamics, and time-coupled scheduling, which are beyond the scope of the current study. The primary objective of this work is to rigorously evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed MNRBO algorithm under highly nonlinear and constrained thermal OPF scenarios. Therefore, focusing on thermal-only generation provides a consistent and controlled framework for benchmarking and comparative analysis with existing literature.

Constraints

The OPF problem must satisfy both equality constraints, which ensure electrical power balance, and inequality constraints, which enforce operational and security limits of power system components. These constraints guarantee that the obtained solution is not only optimal but also physically feasible and safe for practical operation.

The equality constraints \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$g\left( {SV,CV} \right)$$\end{document} represent the fundamental AC power flow equations, ensuring real and reactive power balance at the system level51. Proposed MNRBO algorithm

Standard NRBO algorithm

The NRBO is a novel population-based metaheuristic algorithm that combines elements of Newton’s method with optimization challenges41. The main concept behind Newton’s method and optimization is rooted in their shared focus on finding solutions to complex problems by iteratively improving guesses towards an optimal result.

Newton’s method, or Newton-Raphson method, is a gradient-based technique traditionally used to find the roots of a real-valued function. It starts with an initial guess for the solution. Using the function’s derivative to refine this guess iteratively52. It seeks to improve the accuracy by moving towards the root based on the slope of the tangent at the current estimate which can be mathematically formulated as follows:16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Z_{n + 1} = Z_{n} - \left( {{\raise0.7ex\hbox{${fit\left( {Z_{n} } \right)}$} \!\mathord{\left/ {\vphantom {{fit\left( {Z_{n} } \right)} {\left( {\frac{{\Delta fit\left( {Z_{n} } \right)}}{{\Delta Z_{n} }}} \right)}}}\right.\kern-0pt} \!\lower0.7ex\hbox{${\left( {\frac{{\Delta fit\left( {Z_{n} } \right)}}{{\Delta Z_{n} }}} \right)}$}}} \right)$$\end{document}

This process is repeated until the guesses converge to the root.

In optimization, the goal is to find the minimum (or maximum) of a function, often in a high-dimensional space. The challenge is similar to finding roots: identifying points where the function value is optimal.

Optimization methods inspired by NRBO, adopt three principles. The first is gradient utilization which implements first and second derivatives to find roots, as Newton’s method, to guide the search for optimal solutions. These gradients help the algorithm navigate towards regions with better objective values. The second is iterative improvement which refines the solutions over iterations by adding randomness to maintain diversity in the population, allowing exploration and avoiding local optima. Finally, the balance between exploration and exploitation is maintained while a TAS is activated to avoid getting stuck in local optima.

The NRBO follows a structured process that includes initialization, evaluation of fitness, application of Newton-Raphson-based search, and the use of operators to refine solutions.

Proposed MNRBO version

The NRBO search mechanism, primarily governed by NRSR and TAS, generates new candidate solutions independently, reducing population diversity and potentially leading to premature stagnation or local optima. The proposed MNRBO can be viewed as a hybrid between Newtonian-based local search and adaptive evolutionary population control, addressing a common weakness in NRBO of the lack of diversity regulation.

First, the ACM in MNRBO uses a crossover probability (ϕ) that changes automatically during the iterations. This probability decreases linearly from a maximum (ϕMax) to a minimum (ϕMin) value as the iterations progress, as described in Eq. (29). At the beginning, when ϕ is high, frequent crossover operations occur, allowing broad exploration of the search space through extensive information exchange between individuals. This helps the algorithm discover promising regions efficiently. As the MNRBO advances, ϕ gradually decreases, limiting random perturbations and allowing the search to concentrate on fine-tuning the best solutions.36\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\phi \left( {Iter} \right) = \phi^{Min} - \left( {\left( {\phi^{Max} - \phi^{Min} } \right) \times \left( {1 - \frac{Iter}{{Iter^{max} }}} \right)} \right)$$\end{document}

The crossover operation can be activated to each control variable as:37\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Z_{n,j} \left( {Iter + 1} \right) = \left\{ {\begin{array}{*{20}c} {Z_{n,j} \left( {Iter} \right)} & {if r_{j} < \phi \left( {Iter} \right)} \\ {Z_{n,j} \left( {Iter + 1} \right)} & {Otherwise} \\ \end{array} } \right.$$\end{document}

where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Z_{n,j} \left( {Iter} \right)$$\end{document} is the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$j^{th}$$\end{document} dimensional part of the present n^th search solution, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$r_{j} \in$$\end{document} (0,1) constitutes a randomly generated amount, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Z_{n,j} \left( {Iter + 1} \right)$$\end{document} is the freshly created value derived from the Newtonian and TAS operations.

This adaptive crossover strategy functions similarly to the recombination process in DE but with a self-regulating behavior driven by ϕ. As a result, MNRBO becomes a dynamic and self-evolving optimizer that naturally shifts its focus from exploration to exploitation. This capability enhances its stability, robustness, and effectiveness in solving complex, multimodal, and high-dimensional optimization problems.

In the proposed MNRBO, instead of Eq. (29), the Sigmoid decay mode is introduced to dynamically regulate the convergence progress during iterations. It defines the control parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta \left( {{\mathrm{Iter}}} \right)$$\end{document}as:38\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta \left( {Iter} \right) = \frac{\delta o}{{1 + exp\left( {\frac{\alpha }{{Iter^{max} }} \times \left( {Iter - \left( {\frac{{Iter^{max} }}{2}} \right)} \right)} \right)}}$$\end{document}

where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta_{0}$$\end{document} is the initial maximum value (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta_{0}$$\end{document} = 1), α controls the steepness of the curve (α = 10), and Itermax is the maximum number of iterations.

Figure 2 illustrates the variation of the control parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta \left( {{\mathrm{Iter}}} \right)$$\end{document} across iterations for both the polynomial decay function (Eq. 29), in the upper plot, and the Sigmoid decay mode (Eq. 38) displayed in the lower plot. In the polynomial decay curve, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta$$\end{document} exhibits high perturbation magnitudes at both the beginning and the end of the search process, tending toward values close to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\pm$$\end{document} 1.

This behavior implies that large search steps may occur not only during the early exploration phase but also near the final iterations. As a result, the NRBO algorithm can experience instability or oscillatory movements near convergence, reducing its precision in local refinement. In contrast, the Sigmoid decay mode produces a smooth, monotonic reduction in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta$$\end{document} as iterations progress. The value of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta$$\end{document} decreases gradually from high exploration levels to low perturbation magnitudes, concentrating the MNRBO’s adjustments within a controlled range during later stages. This adaptive transition allows the search to begin with wide, diverse exploration of the solution space and progressively shift toward focused local exploitation. Consequently, the sigmoid-based decay enhances convergence stability, prevents unnecessary large movements near the optimum, and provides a more reliable balance between exploration and exploitation.

Figure 3 displays the main steps of the MNRBO including the ACM and the Sigmoid decay mode. Also, the detailed steps is depicted in Algorithm 1. The ACM allows the algorithm to share directional knowledge among agents. Also, the Sigmoid Decay Mode provides a nonlinear time-evolution control, offering a smoother transition between exploration and exploitation phases. These additions turn NRBO into a self-evolving population framework rather than a fixed-behavior searcher. However, these modifications come at a computational cost. Each iteration requires extra vector recombination and possible re-evaluation of fitness after crossover. The adaptive parameters (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\phi \left( {Iter} \right)$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta \left( {Iter} \right)$$\end{document}) require tuning to avoid under- or over-exploitation. Despite the cost, the enhancements don’t merely “add randomness,” but introduce adaptive behavioral learning to the Newtonian mechanism.

Case study 5: RPL minimization

For Case 5, the performance of the proposed MNRBO is evaluated under three different system loading conditions that represent real grid operational scenarios: Case 5a (Base Load), Case 5b (Peak Load, 115%), and Case 5c (Off-Peak Load, 85%). In this case, peak and off-peak loading conditions are represented by standardized load-scaling factors applied to the base-case demand, with peak load at 115% and off-peak at 85% of nominal system demand. This method is commonly used in OPF studies to evaluate optimizer performance under varying loads without time-coupled constraints, emphasizing the algorithm’s robustness and adaptability rather than chronological modeling.

Table 15 presents the optimal generator dispatch, voltage setpoints, transformer tap ratios, and shunt compensator settings obtained using NRBO and the proposed MNRBO for all three loading levels. Across all operating conditions, the proposed MNRBO demonstrates more coordinated control actions than NRBO, particularly in tap ratios and capacitor settings, highlighting its superior sensitivity to reactive power management and loss-driven optimization. Under base load, MNRBO reduces losses from 2.9228 MW to 2.8517 MW (2.43% reduction). In peak load, it lowers losses from 5.2558 MW to 5.0416 MW (4.07% improvement) by effectively redistributing generation and tightening voltage profiles. The most significant enhancement occurs under off-peak load, where losses drop from 1.8591 MW to 1.7145 MW (7.78% reduction) through precise modulation of shunt compensation and tap changers. These findings confirm MNRBO’s superior ability to minimize losses while ensuring optimal voltage and reactive power coordination across varying conditions.

Figure 12 illustrates the convergence performance of MNRBO, NRBO, BPBO, PKO, and SSO under three conditions. MNRBO demonstrates the fastest and smoothest convergence, achieving a lower loss level more quickly than the other algorithms. Figure 13 highlights the variability of RPL values across 20 runs. For Case 5b under peak load, all algorithms show increased variability, but MNRBO remains the most stable. In Case 5c with off-peak load, MNRBO again showcases significant robustness, with much shorter whiskers compared to BPBO, PKO, SSO, and NRBO. The boxplots across these conditions confirm MNRBO’s superior numerical stability despite varying system loads.

Case study 6: QFC minimization in the presence of PV systems

In this regard, a PV system of 20 MW capacity is considered to be installed at bus 30. Ablation study for QFC minimization in the presence of PV systems

To further investigate the effectiveness of the proposed modifications introduced in the MNRBO framework, an ablation study is conducted for Case 6 under probabilistic PV operating conditions. The objective of this analysis is to quantify the individual and combined contributions of the ACM and the Sigmoid Decay strategy relative to the baseline NRBO algorithm.

Four optimization variants are considered in this study: (i) The original NRBO algorithm; (ii) NRBO integrated only with the ACM; (iii) NRBO integrated only with the Sigmoid Decay strategy; and (iv) The complete proposed MNRBO incorporating both ACM and Sigmoid Decay. Table 19 summarizes the statistical performance indicators obtained from multiple independent runs, including the minimum, average, and maximum QFC, in addition to the standard deviation (STD).

The results indicate that the ACM significantly enhances optimization quality and convergence robustness. When integrated with the NRBO, the minimum QFC improves from 1007.249 $/h to 1004.387 $/h (approximately 0.28% reduction), and the average QFC decreases from 1013.771 $/h to 1004.472 $/h (about 0.92% enhancement). Additionally, solution stability improves, with the standard deviation falling from 6.47067 to 0.080659, indicating a 98.75% reduction in performance fluctuations, confirming the effectiveness of ACM in enhancing population diversity and preventing premature convergence.

Conversely, the variant employing only the sigmoid decay mechanism alone does not significantly enhance optimization, as indicated by a slight increase in average QFC values from 1013.771 $/h to 1014.526 $/h and an increase in standard deviation to 6.848032. This reflects reduced robustness when used without adaptive crossover support, suggesting that its effectiveness relies on sufficient population diversity provided by ACM.

The complete MNRBO framework, integrating ACM and sigmoid decay, performs best among variants with a minimum QFC of 1004.335 $/h and an average QFC of 1004.389 $/h, marking a 0.93% improvement over the original NRBO. It also exhibits high robustness, with an STD of 0.023766, indicating a 99.63% reduction compared to NRBO, and shows narrow variation in QFC values, highlighting its consistency and convergence reliability.

These findings indicate that the enhanced performance of MNRBO results from the interaction between ACM and Sigmoid Decay. While ACM ensures exploration capability and population diversity, Sigmoid Decay facilitates a smooth transition to exploitation and stable convergence in optimization stages. This integration makes NRBO more adaptive and robust for probabilistic OPF problems involving renewable energy uncertainty.

Conclusions

This study presented an enhanced MNRBO to improve exploration-exploitation balance, increase numerical stability, and accelerate convergence when solving challenging OPF problems. The upgraded MNRBO features two adaptive mechanisms, ACM and SDM, which work together to prevent premature convergence, manage non-smooth cost functions, and dynamically adjust search behavior throughout the optimization process. Seven OPF scenarios were evaluated on the IEEE 30-bus system, covering a broad spectrum of operating conditions under base, peak, and off-peak loading. The proposed MNRBO outperformed the original NRBO and three modern algorithms of BPBO, PKO, and SSO in terms of solution quality and robustness, across all benchmarks, showing superior minimum, mean, and maximum objective values. In cost-minimization, MNRBO reduced average costs by up to 2.2% with substantial stability improvements, surpassing 80–90% reductions in standard deviation compared to NRBO. For loss minimization, it achieved reductions of 2.43% at base load, 4.07% at peak load, and 7.78% during off-peak conditions. Moreover, the proposed MNRBO effectively manages renewable generation uncertainty while enhancing voltage regulation, economic performance, and convergence stability. It combines PEM-based stochastic irradiance modeling with OPF optimization, proving capable in practical renewable-integrated power system optimization. An ablation study indicates that MNRBO’s superior performance stems from the interaction of ACM and sigmoid decay strategies, where ACM boosts exploration and diversity, and sigmoid decay ensures smoother exploitation and convergence. Otherwise, the MNRBO shows high robustness and scalability in the IEEE 118-bus system, achieving lower fuel costs and reduced variability compared to NRBO and other optimization methods, with statistical analyses revealing MNRBO’s consistent reliability and tight performance distribution.

Practical considerations

From a practical implementation perspective, the steady, dependable convergence of MNRBO highlights the effectiveness of its adaptive mechanisms in navigating complex, multimodal search landscapes. The MNRBO demonstrates strong performance in terms of solution quality and robustness; however, several aspects should be considered. First, the inclusion of the adaptive crossover mechanism and sigmoid-based control strategy introduces a moderate increase in computational effort compared to the original NRBO, representing a trade-off between accuracy and execution time. Second, while the current results on the IEEE 30-bus system validate its effectiveness, extending the method to larger-scale systems may pose additional challenges in terms of scalability and convergence speed. These aspects highlight important directions for future enhancements toward real-world deployment.

Future works

Although the proposed MNRBO has demonstrated strong performance and robustness across various OPF scenarios, several promising directions remain for future research. First, incorporating renewable energy sources along with uncertainty modeling would enable the evaluation of MNRBO in stochastic and real-time OPF frameworks. Additionally, future work may explore the integration of hydro-thermal coordination and multi-period dynamic OPF formulations to account for time-coupled constraints and storage dynamics. From an algorithmic perspective, adaptive parameter self-tuning and hybridization with machine learning techniques could further enhance convergence speed and solution accuracy. Finally, real-time implementation and hardware-in-the-loop validation may provide deeper insights into the practical deployment of MNRBO in modern smart grid environments.
