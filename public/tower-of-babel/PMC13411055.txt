From MyACR to MyμAlbumin: Clinical Validation of a High‐Precision, Real‐Time Immunoturbidimetric PoC Device for Early Chronic Kidney Disease Detection
Authors: Tullayakorn Plengsuriyakarn, Wanna Chaijaroenkul, Napaporn Youngvises, Kridsada Sirisabhabhorn, Waraporn Bubpha, Kesara Na‐Bangchang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundEarly detection of Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) is essential for preventing disease progression, with microalbuminuria serving as a key marker for renal impairment. While conventional laboratory methods are accurate, they often come with high costs and long turnaround times. Our research group previously developed the point‐of‐care (PoC) MyACR device for determining urine albumin‐to‐creatinine ratios. Building on this, we introduced MyμAlbumin, a PoC, high‐precision immunoturbidimetric device designed for rapid, real‐time quantification of urinary albumin.MethodsThe MyμAlbumin system uses a 340 nm UV LED and an integrated microcontroller to measure turbidity from specific antigen–antibody interactions. Clinical validation was performed on urine samples from 138 patients at risk for CKD, with device performance compared to a hospital‐grade immunoturbidimetric reference method through Spearman's correlation and Bland–Altman analysis.ResultsThe MyμAlbumin device demonstrated exceptional diagnostic accuracy, with a sensitivity of 99.11% and a specificity of 98.23%. It maintained a strong linear correlation (R 2 = 0.9925) across a concentration range of 0–1000 mg/L, with Bland–Altman analysis showing high agreement with the reference method (mean bias of 0.57 mg/L). Urinary albumin levels correlated significantly with elevated blood urea nitrogen (BUN) and serum creatinine. Clinical analysis indicated that microalbuminuria was more prevalent among older patients with diabetes, hypertension, and CKD.ConclusionsThe MyμAlbumin device significantly advances PoC testing, surpassing conventional tools in sensitivity and specificity. Its rapid, laboratory‐grade results enhance clinical decision‐making, making it a transformative tool for early CKD screening in primary care and resource‐limited settings.

Introduction

Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) has emerged as a critical global health challenge, currently affecting an estimated 850 million to 1 billion people, representing approximately 10% to 13% of the world's population. The disease is frequently termed a “silent killer,” as it remains largely asymptomatic until it reaches advanced stages, at which point the risk of progression to end‐stage renal disease (ESRD) and life‐threatening cardiovascular complications increases exponentially. In regions such as Southeast Asia, the burden of CKD is escalating at an alarming rate, driven by a rapid rise in the prevalence of metabolic syndromes, including type 2 diabetes and hypertension. This epidemiological shift has made early intervention and the implementation of accessible screening technologies a high‐priority public health mandate [1, 2].

Early identification of renal impairment is the single most critical factor in stopping or delaying disease progression. Microalbuminuria—defined as the excretion of small amounts of albumin in the urine (30–300 mg/day)—serves as the primary sentinel marker for early glomerular damage. It is particularly prevalent in aging populations and individuals managing chronic comorbidities such as diabetes and hypertension [3, 4]. Beyond its role in diagnosing early‐stage CKD, elevated urinary albumin is strongly correlated with other markers of renal dysfunction, including elevated blood urea nitrogen (BUN) and serum creatinine [5].

To address the limitations of centralized laboratory testing and the low sensitivity of conventional dipsticks [6], our research group has focused on developing high‐precision, point‐of‐care (PoC) diagnostic platforms. Initially, our team successfully developed MyACR, a point‐of‐care (PoC) device for determining the urine albumin‐to‐creatinine ratio (uACR). MyACR demonstrated that random urine samples could be used effectively as a marker for nephropathy, providing a more comprehensive view of albumin excretion corrected by creatinine levels [7]. Building upon this foundational success, our group recently introduced MyμAlbumin, a cutting‐edge immunoturbidity‐based system. Unlike traditional PoC tests, the MyμAlbumin device integrates 340 nm ultraviolet spectroscopy with real‐time, seamless data transmission capabilities [8]. This enables immediate diagnostic reporting and integration into digital health records, effectively overcoming the turnaround time barrier posed by centralized laboratories. While our previous work established the efficacy of uACR measurement using MyACR, this present study focuses on the clinical validation and optimized analytical performance of the MyμAlbumin device. We evaluated test performance and clinical application, compared with the gold‐standard hospital method (immunoturbidimetric assay), in a cohort of 138 patients.

Materials and Methods

Chemicals and Reagents

Goat‐derived anti‐human albumin antibodies were purchased from Sigma‐Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA). Phosphate‐buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4) and polyethylene glycol 6000 (PEG) were sourced from Power Tech Chemical (Bangkok, Thailand). Human serum albumin (HSA) was obtained from Sisco Research Laboratories (Mumbai, India).

HSA stock solution (2 mg/mL) was prepared in PBS and stored at 4°C; working standards were prepared by serial dilution in PBS immediately before use. A PEG solution (4%, w/v) was prepared by dissolving 40 g of PEG in 1 L of PBS. Artificial urine was formulated according to previously established protocols [9]. Before the assay, the anti‐albumin antibody was diluted 1:5 in the buffered PEG solution.

Instrumentation and Detection Platform

The MyμAlbumin device is a portable, turbidity‐based, point‐of‐care (PoC) spectroscopic system designed to quantify urinary albumin, as described in the original development study by [8] (Figure 1). The platform integrates an optical sensing unit controlled by a Microcontroller Unit (MCU), which regulates a constant current source to drive an ultraviolet (UV) LED at 340 nm. As light passes through the sample, it is detected by a photodiode amplifier circuit. The resulting output voltage (Vout) is digitized by an analog‐to‐digital converter (ADC) and processed using a signal‐filtering protocol to eliminate electronic noise. To reduce costs associated with expensive antibody reagents, the system utilizes disposable UV‐transparent microcuvettes (15 mm center height; BRAND, Germany). These cuvettes maintain standard external dimensions (12.5 × 12.5 × 45 mm) and a 10 mm path length but feature a reduced detection window (2 × 3.5 mm), requiring a total reaction volume of only 125 μL.

Analytical Procedure

The assay protocol is based on the immunoturbidimetric principle previously described by [8]. To ensure optical clarity, clinical urine samples were allowed to settle at room temperature (25°C) for 15 min, facilitating the sedimentation of blood components and cellular debris that could interfere with light transmission. The reaction was performed in a microcuvette containing 10 μL of a urine sample (or an albumin standard), 15 μL of phosphate‐buffered saline (PBS), and 100 μL of anti‐albumin antibody solution (diluted 1:5 in PBS). Upon mixing, the initial optical density (OD) was measured at 340 nm using the MyμAlbumin device to establish a baseline (T0). After a 5‐min incubation, the final OD was recorded (T5). The change in absorbance was then used in the following linear regression equation to calculate urinary albumin concentration: Y = 0.0036x − 0.0224.

Calibration curves were constructed to evaluate the MyμAlbumin device's performance in quantifying microalbumin levels in urine samples. The calibration analysis involved samples containing a range of microalbumin concentrations (0 to 1000 mg/L), mirroring clinical scenarios. The calibration curve demonstrated excellent linearity across the tested range, achieving a coefficient of determination (R 2) of 0.9995.

Clinical Application of MyμAlbumin

Patients

The performance of the device was validated using urine samples from patients 138 diagnosed with CKD, diabetes, hypertension, and dyslipidemia. The sample size was determined using the following formula: n=zα22×1−pd2 where n = sample size, Z = 95% confidence interval (1.96), and d (%error) = 5%. p is the average sensitivity and specificity (0.95) of the MyμAlbumin devices reported in previous studies [8].

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics for quantitative data are expressed as mean (standard deviation) and median (range) values. Spearman's rank correlation coefficient (rho) was utilized to assess the relationship between urinary microalbumin concentrations obtained by the MyμAlbumin device and those measured by the hospital‐grade immunoturbidimetric reference method. Statistical significance for all tests was set at α = 0.05.

Demographics

The gender distribution between the two groups was comparable, with males constituting 48.65% of the normal group and 54.69% of the microalbuminuria group. The mean age of patients in the microalbuminuria group was 67.12 ± 13.12 years, while the normal group had a mean age of 62.26 ± 12.21 years. The median ages were recorded as 68 years (range 32–96) for the microalbuminuria group and 64 years (range 16–89) for the normal group, indicating that older age is associated with higher urinary albumin levels.

Clinical Parameters and Diagnosis

Body weight and height measurements were similar across both groups, suggesting no significant anthropometric differences that could influence the results. Regarding clinical diagnoses, CKD and combinations of metabolic comorbidities were more prevalent in the microalbuminuria group. CKD alone was observed exclusively in patients with microalbuminuria, and combined conditions involving diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and CKD were more frequently identified in this group. Overall, patients with microalbuminuria were older and exhibited biochemical evidence of impaired renal function and a higher burden of comorbid conditions, while demographic and anthropometric characteristics were largely comparable between groups.

Laboratory Parameters and Urinary Biomarkers

Renal biochemical parameters revealed noteworthy distinctions. Patients with microalbuminuria exhibited significantly elevated blood urea nitrogen (BUN) levels of 22.28 ± 8.06 mg/dL, compared with 17.50 ± 5.72 mg/dL in the normal group. Similarly, serum creatinine levels were higher in the microalbuminuria group (1.24 ± 0.52 mg/dL) than in the normal group (1.04 ± 0.42 mg/dL), indicating compromised renal function. The analysis of urinary biomarkers demonstrated pronounced differences between the two groups. Urinary albumin concentrations were substantially higher in the microalbuminuria group (70.66 ± 44.92 mg/L) compared to the normal group (14.79 ± 6.43 mg/L). The uACR values further illustrated this disparity, with the microalbuminuria group exhibiting a mean uACR of 73.70 ± 68.36, contrasted with 13.84 ± 10.53 in the normal cohort. In terms of renal function, the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was significantly lower in the microalbuminuria group (60.29 ± 26.96 mL/min/1.73 m2) compared to the normal group (71.54 ± 21.92 mL/min/1.73 m2), suggesting a correlation between increased albumin excretion and declining renal function.

MyμAlbumin Analytical Performance: Clinical Application

Analytical Performance

The MyμAlbumin PoC device was assessed by comparing its results with those of the reference laboratory method. The sensitivity of 99.11% indicates the device's excellent ability to identify patients with microalbuminuria correctly. The specificity of 98.23% reflects the device's high capacity to classify individuals without the condition accurately. The Positive Predictive Value (PPV) of 98.23% confirms the device's reliability in affirming the presence of microalbuminuria. The Negative Predictive Value (NPV) of 99.11% indicates the device's effectiveness at ruling out microalbuminuria when results are negative. The accuracy of 98.67% indicates near‐perfect agreement with the reference method. The false‐positive rate of 1.77% suggests minimal misclassification of healthy individuals as having microalbuminuria. The false‐negative rate of 0.89% indicates that the device rarely overlooks cases of microalbuminuria. These findings collectively highlight the MyμAlbumin device's high analytical validity and clinical utility, particularly in environments where rapid and precise assessment is crucial.

Test Agreement Analysis

The agreement between the MyμAlbumin PoC device and the reference laboratory method was evaluated using Bland–Altman analysis, a widely used method for assessing agreement between two measurement techniques. The mean difference (bias) between the MyμAlbumin device and the reference method was 0.57 mg/L, indicating minimal systematic error in measurements. The 95% limits of agreement (LoA) ranged from −17.11 to 18.26 mg/L, suggesting that the majority of measurements obtained with the MyμAlbumin device fell within approximately ±18 mg/L of the reference method (Figure 2). Visual inspection of the Bland–Altman plot showed that data points were evenly distributed around the mean bias line, particularly in the lower concentration range (< 60 mg/L), which is critical for microalbumin screening. Notably, 97.83% of the values measured by the MyμAlbumin device were in agreement with those quantified by the reference method. However, a greater variability in measurements was observed at higher concentration levels (> 100 mg/L), suggesting greater variability in this range.

Bland–Altman plot analysis for the accuracy of microalbumin measured by the MyμAlbumin PoC device compared with the hospital‐based reference method.

Correlation Analysis

The resulting linear regression analysis produced the equation (y = 1.019×), with a coefficient of determination (R 2) of 0.9925, indicating a very strong correlation between the MyμAlbumin device measurements and the reference method results (Figure 3A). The slope of 1.019 suggested a minimal proportional bias, with an overestimation of approximately 1.9% relative to the reference method. The regression analysis also indicated an insignificant intercept, suggesting negligible constant bias across the concentration range analyzed. Data points were tightly clustered along the regression line throughout the concentration ranges assessed, confirming the device's consistent analytical performance. When visualized on a logarithmic scale, the linearity was maintained without any evidence of curvature or signal saturation, although slight increased dispersion was noted at higher concentrations (> 1000 mg/L) (Figure 3B). This variability is expected at extreme analyte levels and does not indicate systematic deviation.

Discussion

The results of this study demonstrate that the MyμAlbumin device is a highly effective tool for detecting microalbuminuria, positioning it as a potentially transformative PoC solution in clinical settings. With a sensitivity of 99.11% and a specificity of 98.23%, the device demonstrates diagnostic precision vital for identifying patients in the early, often asymptomatic stages of CKD. These findings align with the clinical consensus that early detection is the single most important factor in implementing interventions that can halt or delay disease progression [10]. When evaluating the device's performance against existing literature, the MyμAlbumin system consistently meets or exceeds the benchmarks set by established PoC platforms. For instance, a meta‐analysis reported that various commercially available devices exhibit sensitivities ranging from 80% to 95% [11]. Similarly, another study reported a different PoC device with 95% sensitivity and 96% specificity [12]. Our results suggest that the MyμAlbumin device provides superior reliability, which is critical for clinicians who must make high‐stakes decisions based on immediate test results. The calibration curves further support the system's analytical robustness. Achieving an R 2 value of 0.9925 across a wide range of concentrations (0 to 1000 mg/L) ensures that the device remains accurate even at the extremes of the clinical spectrum. This is particularly relevant compared with semi‐quantitative methods, which have historically shown sensitivities as low as 80% across diverse patient cohorts [6]. While our analysis showed a slight overestimation in specific instances, the Bland–Altman analysis confirmed strong agreement with hospital‐based immunoturbidimetric methods, yielding a mean bias of only 0.57 mg/L. This minimal deviation confirms that the device can serve as a trustworthy surrogate for traditional laboratory testing.

Beyond technical performance, this study's demographic and clinical finding reinforce the biological importance of microalbuminuria as a sentinel marker for systemic disease. Older patients exhibited higher frequencies of microalbuminuria, a trend that mirrors the established literature identifying age as a primary risk factor for declining renal function [4]. This correlation emphasizes the urgent need for routine screening protocols, particularly for geriatric populations and those managing chronic comorbidities like diabetes and [3]. Furthermore, the significant correlation between increased urinary albumin and elevated BUN and serum creatinine levels validates the MyμAlbumin device's role in identifying broader renal impairment [5]. By providing immediate, laboratory‐grade data at the patient's side, the MyμAlbumin device overcomes the turnaround time barrier of centralized testing. This rapid feedback loop allows healthcare providers to integrate results into a single patient visit, facilitating immediate adjustments to medication or lifestyle interventions. Such efficiency is a cornerstone of modern health promotion strategies, encouraging patient compliance and proactive kidney health management.

When examining clinical diagnoses, a clear distinction emerged. CKD and combinations of metabolic comorbidities were significantly more prevalent in the microalbuminuria group. Notably, CKD as a standalone diagnosis was observed exclusively in patients with microalbuminuria. Furthermore, complex triple‐threat conditions involving diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and CKD were more frequently identified in this cohort. These patients were typically older and exhibited clear biochemical evidence of impaired renal function, such as elevated BUN and serum creatinine levels [5]. Interestingly, while anthropometric and demographic characteristics were largely comparable across groups, albuminuria was the primary differentiator of high‐risk clinical profiles. This reinforces the need for routine screening protocols, particularly for geriatric populations managing multi‐morbid metabolic conditions [3, 4].

While this study was statistically powered with 138 patients, the single‐center design suggests that future research should transition toward multicenter trials to ensure reproducibility across more diverse populations. Additionally, longitudinal studies will be necessary to definitively correlate the early detection facilitated by the MyμAlbumin device with long‐term improvements in mortality and a reduction in the economic burden of late‐stage CKD [13]. Conduct a longitudinal study to assess changes in urinary albumin levels over time in patients using the MyμAlbumin device. This would provide insight into the progression of microalbuminuria and its correlation with clinical interventions or changes in patient management. Analyze the sensitivity of the MyμAlbumin device to dietary changes that may affect urinary albumin levels. This could involve monitoring patients' dietary habits and correlating them with changes in microalbumin levels to understand how nutrition influences renal function.

Conclusion

In summary, the MyμAlbumin device represents a significant advancement in point‐of‐care diagnostics. By successfully identifying high‐burden comorbid patients and providing laboratory‐grade accuracy in a portable format, this device has the potential to fundamentally improve the trajectory of care for millions of patients at risk for renal failure.
