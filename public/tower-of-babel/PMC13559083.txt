Clinical outcomes and determinants of intraoperative hyperlactatemia in neonates undergoing gastrointestinal surgery: a retrospective cohort analysis
Authors: Tingyi Lv, Jialian Zhao, Chunyi Jin, Binbin Cai, Yaoqin Hu, Yue Jin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe clinical significance of hyperlactatemia in neonates undergoing gastrointestinal surgery remains unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the prevalence, clinical outcomes, and associated risk factors of intraoperative hyperlactatemia (IHL) among neonates undergoing gastrointestinal surgery.MethodsA retrospective cohort analysis was conducted, including neonates who underwent gastrointestinal surgical procedures between May 2020 and October 2022. IHL was defined as a blood lactate concentration exceeding 2.0 mmol/L. The incidence of IHL was determined alongside relevant clinical characteristics and postoperative outcomes. Potential risk factors were explored using univariate analysis followed by multivariable logistic regression.ResultsOf the 570 neonates included, 34.6% developed IHL. Neonates with IHL demonstrated significantly higher 30-day mortality (15.2% vs. 2.1%, P<0.001), longer duration of mechanical ventilation (17.6 vs. 12.4 hours, P<0.001), and a greater incidence of postoperative complications, including pulmonary complications (50.3% vs. 38.9%, P=0.009). Multivariable analysis showed that younger age [odds ratio (OR) =0.917; 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.887–0.949], preoperative acidosis (OR =1.619; 95% CI: 1.061–2.470), emergency surgical intervention (OR =4.086; 95% CI: 1.877–8.890), and intraoperative blood transfusion (OR =2.018; 95% CI: 1.221–3.336) were independently associated with IHL.ConclusionsIHL is frequently observed in neonatal gastrointestinal surgery and is strongly associated with adverse clinical outcomes. Younger age, preoperative acidosis, emergency surgery, and intraoperative blood transfusion were independently associated with the occurrence of IHL.Keywords: Gastrointestinal surgery, intraoperative hyperlactatemia (IHL), neonate, outcome, risk factor

Highlight box

Key findings

• Intraoperative hyperlactatemia (IHL) occurred in approximately one-third of neonates undergoing gastrointestinal surgery. It was associated with increased mortality, prolonged mechanical ventilation, and a higher incidence of postoperative complications.

• Younger age, preoperative acidosis, emergency surgery, and intraoperative blood transfusion were independently associated with IHL occurrence.

What is known and what is new?

• Hyperlactatemia is widely recognized as a marker of tissue hypoperfusion and disease severity in critically ill patients. However, data regarding its clinical significance in neonatal gastrointestinal surgery remain limited.

• This study characterized the incidence and clinical impact of IHL in a large cohort of neonates undergoing gastrointestinal surgery. It also identified several perioperative factors that are independently associated with its occurrence.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• IHL may serve as an important marker of perioperative physiological stress and disease severity in neonatal gastrointestinal surgical patients.

• Early identification of neonates at increased risk of IHL may facilitate perioperative risk stratification and targeted monitoring.

Introduction

Abdominal surgery represents the most frequently performed operative intervention in neonates, particularly in the context of gastrointestinal malformations, necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC), and intestinal malrotation. These procedures are undertaken with the intention of improving clinical outcomes and reducing mortality (1-4). In the United States, the reported incidence of NEC ranges from 0.1% to 0.3% (5,6), with approximately 30–50% of affected neonates requiring surgical management (7-9). The associated mortality rate remains substantial, estimated at 20–30% (10). Given the low birth weight and physiological immaturity characteristic of this population, perioperative anesthetic management necessitates careful consideration. Continuous intraoperative assessment of fluid balance, blood glucose, blood gas parameters, and electrolyte levels is essential to support optimal anesthetic care (11).

Lactate is widely recognized as a biomarker of tissue hypoxia and hypoperfusion (12,13). Evidence reported by Moschino et al. indicates that elevated intraoperative lactate concentrations are associated with myocardial injury in patients undergoing non-cardiac surgery (14). Increased lactate levels have also been linked to a higher risk of mortality and morbidity in neonates (15), while even modest elevations are associated with increased mortality among critically ill pediatric patients (16). Furthermore, hyperlactatemia has been identified as an independent predictor of mortality in neonates with gastric perforation (17).

Despite these findings, the association between hyperlactatemia and clinical outcomes in the setting of neonatal gastrointestinal surgery has not been clearly established. This study therefore aims to examine the prevalence of intraoperative hyperlactatemia (IHL) and to evaluate its relationship with postoperative outcomes in this patient population. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://tp.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tp-2026-0338/rc).

Methods

Study design and population

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Children’s Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine (No. 2024-IRB-0381-P-01) and individual consent for this retrospective analysis was waived. The trial was prospectively registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (chictr.org.cn, ChiCTR2500096663, January 27, 2025). This single-center, retrospective cohort study was conducted at a specialized, tertiary children’s hospital with a dedicated neonatal surgical ward and a 60-bed neonatal intensive care unit (NICU). The hospital routinely manages neonatal gastrointestinal surgeries conditions. All neonates who underwent gastrointestinal surgery between May 2020 and October 2022 were consecutively enrolled. Gastrointestinal surgery was defined as operative procedures involving the gastrointestinal tract or related abdominal organs. Isolated hernia repair and simple peritoneal drainage procedures were excluded.

Patients were excluded if they met any of the following criteria: (I) absence of tracheal intubation; (II) preoperative cardiopulmonary resuscitation or presence of severe preoperative lactic acidosis; (III) a final preoperative serum lactate concentration exceeding 2 mmol/L; (IV) presence of severe congenital heart disease or chromosomal anomalies; and (V) incomplete clinical data for study variables.

Data collection and definition

Demographic and perioperative data were extracted from the clinical information system (Ewell, Hangzhou, China) and the institutional electronic anesthesia system (Medical System, Suzhou, China) at Children’s Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine, Hangzhou, China. Recorded demographic variables included gestational age, gender, and body weight.

Preoperative data comprised the American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status classification, comorbidities (including respiratory disease, congenital heart disease, anemia, hepatic insufficiency, and sepsis), and preoperative interventions such as mechanical ventilation, oxygen inhalation, administration of vasoactive drugs, and blood transfusions. Laboratory parameters and the classification of surgery as elective or emergency were also documented.

Intraoperative variables encompassed the type of surgical procedure, duration of surgery and anesthesia, volume of fluid administration, blood transfusions, use of vasoactive drugs, urine output, estimated blood loss, and intraoperative laboratory tests. Postoperative data included laboratory indicator complications, intensive care unit (ICU) length of stay, total postoperative hospital stay, 30-day mortality, ICU blood transfusion, and the need for unplanned intubation or reoperation.

The incidence of IHL was evaluated. Patients were stratified into IHL and non-hyperlactatemia (NHL) groups according to peak intraoperative lactate concentrations. Perioperative complications and clinical outcomes were compared between these groups, and factors associated with the development of IHL were identified. All complications were categorized in accordance with the International Classification of Diseases-9th/10th revision (ICD-9/10).

In this study, IHL was defined as a lactate concentration exceeding 2 mmol/L, as determined by arterial blood gas analysis during surgery. This threshold is widely accepted for defining hyperlactatemia in neonatal and critical care studies (18-20). In routine clinical practice, this analysis was generally performed at the beginning of surgery, approximately every hour during the procedure, at the end of surgery, and whenever was clinically indicated. For each patient, the peak intraoperative arterial blood lactate level recorded on arterial blood gas testing was extracted for analysis. Sepsis was diagnosed according to the International Consensus Criteria for Pediatric Sepsis and Septic Shock (21). Thrombocytopenia was defined as a platelet count below 150×109/L (13). Postoperative pulmonary complications included respiratory infection, respiratory failure, pleural effusion, atelectasis, pneumothorax, bronchospasm, and aspiration pneumonitis (22). Severe anemia was defined as a hemoglobin concentration ≤8 g/dL (23,24).

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or as median with interquartile range (IQR), depending on the results of normality testing. Group comparisons were conducted using Student’s t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test, as appropriate. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages, with differences assessed using the Chi-squared test.

A pre-specified multivariable logistic regression was constructed to identify factors independently associated with IHL. Variables were selected a priori based on clinical relevance and previous literature (15,25), including age, body weight, prematurity, ASA classification, emergency surgery, operation duration, disease type, preoperative sepsis, preoperative acidosis, preoperative mechanical ventilation, and intraoperative blood transfusion. Multicollinearity was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF), and all included variables demonstrated VIF <5. Patients with incomplete core data for study variables were excluded during screening; therefore, no missing data imputation was performed. Model calibration was assessed using the Hosmer-Lemeshow test, and model discrimination was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) (c-statistic). All statistical tests were two-tailed and performed using SPSS version 22.0. A P value <0.05 was considered indicative of statistical significance.

Results

Study cohort and patient characteristics

During the study period, a total of 1,365 neonatal patients underwent surgery at our institution. Of these patients, 734 underwent gastrointestinal surgery and were screened for eligibility. Following application of the exclusion criteria, 570 patients were included in the final analysis, of whom 197 (34.6%) developed IHL (Figure 1).

Flow diagram of the study population.

The IHL group had a median age of 2 days (IQR, 1–6.5) and a median body weight of 2.7 kg (IQR, 1.8–3.1), whereas the NHL group had corresponding values of 6 days (IQR, 3–12) and 2.8 kg (IQR, 2.2–3.3); these differences were statistically significant (all P<0.05). In the IHL group, 106 neonates (53.8%) were preterm, and a considerable proportion presented with compromised preoperative status, with 57 (29.0%) classified as ASA physical status III or higher. Preoperative respiratory disease, sepsis, and lactic acidosis were all significantly associated with the occurrence of IHL (all P<0.05). Emergency procedures were also more frequent among neonates in the IHL group compared with those in the NHL group (95.9% vs. 76.4%, P<0.001). The distribution of primary gastrointestinal diseases differed significantly between the two cohorts (P=0.02). Intestinal inflammation or perforation were more prevalent in the IHL group (31.0% vs. 19.3%). However, congenital gastrointestinal malformations were more prevalent among NHL patients (44.7% vs. 50.9%) (Table 1).

Impact of IHL on prognosis

Postoperative lactic acidosis and thrombocytopenia occurred more frequently among neonates in the IHL group (all P<0.05). This group also demonstrated a higher incidence of postoperative pulmonary complications (P=0.009). IHL was further associated with a prolonged duration of postoperative parenteral nutrition (16 vs. 11.5 days, P<0.001) and delayed initiation of enteral nutrition (7 vs. 6 days, P<0.001).

In addition, neonates with IHL required longer periods of mechanical ventilation and experienced extended stays in both the ICU and the hospital overall (P<0.05). The 30-day mortality rate was significantly higher in the IHL group compared with the NHL group (15.2% vs. 2.1%, P<0.001) (Table 3).

Risk factors for IHL

Multivariate logistic regression analysis identified several factors independently associated with IHL in neonates undergoing gastrointestinal surgery, included younger age [odds ratio (OR) =0.917; 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.887–0.949; P<0.001], preoperative acidosis (OR =1.619; 95% CI: 1.061–2.470; P=0.03), emergency surgical intervention (OR =4.086; 95% CI: 1.877–8.890; P<0.001), and intraoperative blood transfusion (OR =2.018; 95% CI: 1.221–3.336; P=0.006) (Table 4). The Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test confirmed an adequate model fit [χ2=6.540; degrees of freedom (df) =8; P=0.59].

Discussion

The present study demonstrated that IHL is common in neonates undergoing gastrointestinal surgery and is associated with adverse postoperative outcomes. This analysis expands the current clinical evidence base by describing statistical associations between perioperative indicators and IHL status in this patient cohort.

Lactate is widely recognized as a marker of impaired tissue perfusion in critically ill patients. Elevated lactate concentrations during the early postnatal period have been associated with markedly increased mortality (15,26). In pediatric populations, hyperlactatemia has similarly been linked to increased mortality in both emergency department settings (27) and pediatric ICU (13). In addition, neonates with IHL were more likely to require prolonged mechanical ventilation. Extended ventilatory support is associated with an increased risk of complications, including right ventricular dysfunction and respiratory muscle injury (28). These complications may, in turn, contribute to longer durations of ICU and overall hospital stay, thereby increasing healthcare-related costs (29,30). Consistent with these findings, our study demonstrated that neonates with IHL experienced significantly higher 30-day mortality, prolonged mechanical ventilation, delayed enteral feeding, and longer ICU and hospital stays. The findings suggest that IHL is associated with a more complicated postoperative course in neonates undergoing gastrointestinal surgery (14).

Several perioperative factors were independently associated with the development of IHL, most of which reflect the severity of the underlying disease and the physiological vulnerability of neonatal patients. Emergency surgery emerged as the most significant independent predictor of IHL. Neonates requiring urgent gastrointestinal procedures commonly present with conditions such as intestinal obstruction, NEC, gastrointestinal perforation, or shock, all of which are associated with severe preoperative hypoperfusion, systemic inflammatory responses, and metabolic derangements, including base deficit (31-33). In the intraoperative setting, factors such as acute surgical stress, hemodynamic instability, and limited opportunity for preoperative optimization may further exacerbate tissue hypoxia and promote lactate accumulation.

An association between preoperative acidosis and IHL was also observed. Partain et al. reported that prematurity, an ASA physical status classification greater than 3, preoperative use of vasoactive drugs, and emergency gastrointestinal procedures were linked to IHL (34). These factors generally reflect greater disease severity, in line with the present findings. Neonates with immature organ function and compromised preoperative status appear more vulnerable to intraoperative metabolic derangements. The administration of vasoactive agents and the presence of acidosis may indicate impaired microvascular tissue perfusion, with resultant microcirculatory dysfunction potentially contributing to organ impairment (12).

In the present study, those with IHL required higher rates of fluid administration, more frequent transfusions, and increased use of vasoactive drugs, reflecting greater intraoperative circulatory instability. Severe anemia and electrolyte disturbances were also prevalent, both of which may further compromise oxygen delivery at the tissue level and contribute to lactate accumulation. At the same time, the physiological characteristics of neonates, including immature renal function and low body mass, increase susceptibility to fluid overload. Excessive fluid administration may adversely affect multiple organ systems, including the lungs, heart, and kidneys (15,35,36). Accordingly, careful intraoperative fluid management remains essential to minimize the risk of tissue hypoperfusion, elevated lactate concentrations, and the development of hyperlactatemia.

Our study showed that intestinal inflammation or perforation were associated with a higher IHL prevalence than congenital gastrointestinal malformations or simple intestinal obstructions. This difference likely reflects the greater degree of systemic inflammatory activation and tissue hypoperfusion in these patients (37). In contrast, neonates with gastrointestinal malformations or intestinal obstructions generally experience less physiological derangement and exhibit a lower IHL incidence, consistent with previous neonatal surgical studies (17). Collectively, these findings suggest that IHL may represent a marker of underlying disease severity and physiological derangement rather than an isolated pathological entity.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. This was a single-center, retrospective analysis, which may introduce regional bias and limit the generalizability of the findings. In addition, lactate measurements were not obtained continuously, which limited our ability to evaluate temporal trends in lactate levels and their relationship with clinical outcomes. Intermittent sampling may have underestimated the true incidence of IHL by failing to capture transient lactate peaks during surgery. Consequently, the observed associations between IHL and postoperative outcomes may have been affected by misclassification of some patients with brief but clinically relevant elevations in lactate levels. In addition, although disease type was included in the multivariable model, the sample size within individual diagnostic categories was insufficient to determine whether the prognostic significance of IHL differed across specific diseases. Future prospective, multicenter investigations incorporating standardized lactate monitoring protocols are warranted to validate these findings and to inform evidence-based perioperative management strategies.

Conclusions

This study confirms the high prevalence of IHL during neonatal gastrointestinal surgery, with a significant association between IHL and elevated 30-day mortality. Younger age, preoperative acidosis, emergency surgery, and intraoperative blood transfusion showed independent statistical associations with IHL. Perioperative risk stratification based on these associated factors and routine intraoperative lactate monitoring may help identify neonates at increased risk of adverse outcomes. Further prospective studies are required to determine whether targeted interventions based on these findings can improve clinical outcomes.
