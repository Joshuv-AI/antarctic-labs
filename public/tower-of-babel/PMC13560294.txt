Efficacy and safety of treatment strategies for early neurological deterioration after acute ischemic stroke: a network meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
Authors: Jianhua Liu, Bing Fu, Zhaoting Zhang, Qianqian He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEarly neurological deterioration (END) is a serious complication of acute ischemic stroke and may occur spontaneously or after reperfusion therapy.ObjectiveTo compare the efficacy and safety of different interventions for acute ischemic stroke patients with END using network meta-analysis.MethodsRandomized controlled trials were identified through systematic searches of major databases up to February 2026. The primary outcome was the 90-day modified Rankin Scale score. Secondary outcomes included neurological improvement (assessed by NIHSS), intracranial hemorrhage, and mortality.ResultsFive randomized controlled trials including 1,164 patients were analyzed. Compared with standard antiplatelet therapy (ST; which included aspirin monotherapy, placebo plus aspirin, or dual antiplatelet therapy depending on the study protocol), indobufen combined with ST, and tirofiban combined with ST, significantly improved 90-day functional outcome, whereas argatroban combined with standard treatment reduced mean mRS scores but did not significantly increase the proportion of patients achieving favorable functional outcomes (mRS 0–2) or excellent functional outcomes (mRS 0–1). Indobufen demonstrated the highest probability of being the optimal treatment for reducing the mean 90-day mRS score and improving 90-day NIHSS scores based on SUCRA rankings. Indobufen and tirofiban (each added to ST) also significantly improved neurological recovery at short- and long-term follow-up. However, argatroban and tirofiban did not significantly increase the proportion of patients achieving favorable functional outcomes. None of the interventions increased the risk of intracranial or symptomatic hemorrhage.ConclusionsIndobufen was associated with lower mean 90-day mRS and NIHSS scores, whereas tirofiban added to standard antiplatelet therapy increased the proportion of patients achieving a favorable functional outcome defined as mRS 0–2. Neither tirofiban nor argatroban significantly increased the proportion achieving an excellent functional outcome defined as mRS 0–1. These findings should be interpreted cautiously because of the limited number and size of the included trials.Keywords: Acute ischemic stroke, early neurological deterioration; Efficacy; Randomized controlled trials; Meta-analysis

Introduction

Acute ischemic stroke (AIS) is a major cerebrovascular disease-causing death and disability worldwide. AIS leads to substantial long-term medical expenses, rehabilitation costs, and productivity loss, thereby imposing a significant socioeconomic burden on patients, their families, and society as a whole [1, 2]. Intravenous thrombolytic therapy with recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (rt-PA) has been established as an effective treatment for AIS. However, in clinical practice, a significant subset of patients experiences worsening neurological deficits within the acute phase, a phenomenon defined as early neurological deterioration (END), which can occur spontaneously or following reperfusion therapies such as intravenous thrombolysis [3, 4]. Previous study has demonstrated that early neurological outcomes after thrombolysis—including END and early neurological improvement—are closely associated with long-term prognosis in patients receiving intravenous thrombolysis [3].

From a pathophysiological perspective, platelet aggregation, thrombus progression, atherosclerotic plaque instability, activation of inflammatory responses, and impaired cerebral perfusion are key contributors to END. Platelet aggregation plays a pivotal role in thrombosis and disease progression. These pathological processes further exacerbate ischemic brain injury and contribute to the progressive worsening of neurological deficits [4–6]. Although aspirin and clopidogrel constitute the standard antiplatelet therapy for AIS, they often fail to achieve adequate platelet inhibition in patients with END, thereby limiting optimal disease control [4]. While atherosclerotic plaque instability and local thrombosis are primary drivers, other critical mechanisms—such as recurrent emboli leading to stroke recurrence, hemorrhagic transformation, or progressive peri-infarct brain swelling—also substantially contribute to END and require balanced consideration [4].

Currently, clinical research primarily focuses on predicting the risk of END, with inflammation-related biomarkers frequently used as predictive indicators [3, 7]. However, for patients with AIS who have developed END, there remains a lack of robust and comprehensive evidence to inform standardized and effective clinical intervention strategies. Therefore, this study aims to systematically evaluate the effectiveness and safety of various treatment strategies following the onset of END in AIS patients, with the goal of providing a reliable evidence base for precise clinical intervention.

Method

Research guide and literature search

This meta-analysis was conducted in strict accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines to ensure standardization and methodological rigor in the study design, implementation, and reporting processes. The data were retrieved from three major databases: PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library. The search period spanned from database inception to February 28, 2026. The search strategy was developed using a combination of controlled vocabulary and free-text terms. The core search terms included “acute ischemic stroke,” “early neurological deterioration,” and “random*,” with no restrictions on publication language. After the initial search, duplicate records were removed. To minimize the risk of missing relevant studies, reference lists of included articles and related reviews were manually screened for additional eligible studies.

Literature inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria

Population

Patients with AIS who developed END. Studies including this population as a subgroup were also eligible if subgroup-specific results were reported separately.

Interventions and comparators

Any clinical intervention strategies and corresponding control conditions for patients with AIS combined with END. No restrictions were placed on the types of interventions or comparators. Given the absence of a standardized intervention strategy in current clinical practice, comparative studies evaluating all potential interventions were included to maximize coverage.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score at 90-day follow-up; secondary outcomes included intracranial hemorrhage and all-cause mortality as safety indicators.

Study design

Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comparing two or more intervention groups.

Exclusion criteria

Studies meeting any of the following criteria were excluded: studies that included AIS patients with END but did not perform subgroup analyses or report separate outcome data for this population; study protocols, animal studies, and in vitro experiments; and non-original research articles, such as reviews, editorials, and commentaries.

Literature screening and data extraction

Literature screening and data extraction were conducted independently by two researchers. Disagreements arising during the screening and data extraction processes were resolved through discussion between the two researchers; if consensus could not be reached, the corresponding author made the final decision. The following key data were extracted from the included studies: first author, year of publication, study region, study design, total sample size, definition of END, patient age, details of the intervention and control groups, efficacy and safety outcomes, and follow-up duration.

Outcome measures

Primary outcome

The primary outcome was the 90-day mRS score. Given the ordinal nature of mRS, we performed additional analyses using a proportional odds (ordinal) regression model to better account for the ordered categories, rather than relying solely on mean differences. Dichotomized outcomes (mRS 0–1 and mRS 0–2) were also analyzed. All follow-up assessments occurred at 90 days post-treatment.

Secondary outcomes

Secondary efficacy outcomes included National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) scores at 7 and 90 days after treatment. Safety outcomes included the incidence of total intracranial hemorrhage, symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage, and 90-day all-cause mortality.

Risk of bias assessment

The Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool (version 5.1.0) was used to assess the methodological quality of the included RCTs. The assessment covered six domains: random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding of participants and personnel, completeness of outcome data, selective reporting, and other sources of bias. Each domain was rated as low risk, unclear risk, or high risk. Risk of bias assessment was conducted independently by two researchers, with disagreements resolved through discussion to ensure objectivity and rigor.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), with specific procedures for network meta-analysis. For continuous outcomes, mean differences (MDs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were used as effect measures. When means and standard deviations were not directly reported, data were estimated using methods recommended in the Cochrane Handbook or extracted from graphical representations. For binary outcomes, odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were used. A frequentist framework was applied using the netmeta package in R to perform the network meta-analysis. Node-splitting analysis was conducted to assess the consistency between direct and indirect evidence and to evaluate the robustness of the results. Pairwise comparison results were summarized to facilitate visual comparison of different interventions. The surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) was used to rank the efficacy of the interventions. For heterogeneity, we calculated the I² statistic and τ² for each network comparison. For inconsistency, we performed both node-splitting analysis (to compare direct and indirect evidence for each closed loop) and the design-by-treatment interaction model (to assess global inconsistency).

Results

Literature screening process and results

A literature search of three major databases was conducted according to the predefined strategy, identifying 78 records in PubMed, 144 in Embase, and 84 in the Cochrane Library. After removing duplicates using reference management software, 188 records remained. Following title and abstract screening, 154 irrelevant records were excluded, leaving 34 articles for full-text review. During full-text assessment, studies not meeting the inclusion criteria were excluded, including 13 non-randomized studies (retrospective or cohort studies), 8 study protocols, 5 studies involving AIS patients without END, and 3 reviews. Ultimately, 5 RCTs were included in the network meta-analysis [8–12] (Fig. 1).

Baseline characteristics of included studies

All five included studies were conducted in China, with a total of 1,164 patients and mean ages ranging from 62 to 68 years. As all trials were Chinese populations, the generalizability of these results to other geographic regions or ethnic groups may be limited.

The clinical timeline and criteria for defining END exhibited variations across the five included trials (Table 1). The time window for END detection predominantly occurred within the ultra-early phase of acute stroke, ranging from within 24 h [10, 11] to 48 h [9] or up to 72 h [8] from symptom onset or post-thrombolysis. Mechanistically, the threshold for defining neurological worsening also varied, with three trials utilizing an increase in NIHSS score of ≥ 2 points [8, 9, 12], one utilizing a more stringent increase of ≥ 4 points [10], and one based on general clinical worsening [11]. The elapsed time from END detection to the initiation of rescue management was consistently scheduled immediately upon the confirmation of neurological deterioration across all protocols.

Main efficacy outcome analysis

90-day mRS score

A network meta-analysis of the 90-day mRS continuous outcome included the four intervention strategies described above, with each node representing a distinct regimen or dosage. The control group (ST) was not uniform across trials; it included aspirin 100 mg, aspirin 300 mg, placebo plus aspirin, or dual antiplatelet therapy. Where data permitted, ST nodes were further subdivided by aspirin dose (100 mg vs. 300 mg) and by monotherapy vs. dual therapy. The network structure was reconstructed to display these nodes and direct comparisons (Fig. 3A). Heterogeneity across studies was low to moderate. Node-splitting analysis revealed no significant inconsistency between direct and indirect evidence for any comparison (all p > 0.05). The design-by-treatment interaction model also supported global consistency (χ² = 4.21, df = 5, p = 0.52).

The number of direct pairwise comparisons is indicated. B Forest plot displaying comparative results with standard therapy (ST) as the reference. The results showed that, compared with ST alone, indobufen significantly reduced the 90-day mRS score (MD = − 1.11, 95% CI: −1.32 to − 0.90; SUCRA = 0.91) and increased the proportion of patients achieving favorable functional outcomes. Tirofiban + ST improved both mean mRS and dichotomized outcomes (0–2) with SUCRA = 0.75 (95% CI: 0.58–0.89). Argatroban + ST reduced mean mRS (MD = − 0.45, 95% CI: −0.90 to 0.00) but did not significantly increase the proportion of patients achieving mRS 0–1 or 0–2 (OR = 1.25, 95% CI: 0.91–1.72; SUCRA = 0.33) (Fig. 3B).

The SUCRA rankings should be interpreted cautiously due to overlapping confidence intervals, small sample sizes, and wide uncertainty (Fig. 3C). The interventions showed varying effects across outcomes; indobufen and tirofiban demonstrated more consistent neurological benefits, whereas the effect of argatroban was limited primarily to the continuous mRS outcome (Fig. 3).

90-day mRS Score (0–2)

This outcome included three intervention strategies: argatroban + ST, tirofiban + ST, and ST alone. The network meta-analysis showed that the tirofiban + ST group had a significantly higher proportion of patients achieving a 90-day mRS score of 0–2, indicating a better functional outcome than the ST-alone group (OR = 3.60, 95% CI: 1.58 to 8.20; SUCRA = 0.99). In contrast, no significant difference was observed between the argatroban + ST group and the ST-alone group (OR = 1.25, 95% CI: 0.91 to 1.72; SUCRA = 0.46) (Fig. 4).

Safety outcome analysis

Incidence of intracranial hemorrhage

This outcome was analyzed across three intervention strategies: indobufen, tirofiban + ST, and ST alone. Compared with ST alone, the indobufen group showed a lower risk of intracranial hemorrhage, although the difference was not statistically significant (OR = 0.33, 95% CI: 0.03 to 3.20; SUCRA = 0.82). Heterogeneity was negligible (I² = 0%, τ² = 0) for this outcome.

The tirofiban + ST group showed a slightly higher incidence of intracranial hemorrhage than the ST-alone group, but the difference was also not statistically significant (OR = 1.20, 95% CI: 0.13 to 11.20; SUCRA = 0.31) (Fig. 8).

90-day all-cause mortality

Mortality data were reported in three studies [9, 11, 12], while two studies reported zero events and were included in a sensitivity analysis using continuity correction. Across all available studies, the 90-day all-cause mortality was numerically lower in the argatroban + ST group (4/298), tirofiban + ST group (3/196), and indobufen group (1/250) compared with their respective controls (ST alone: 12/303; ST alone for Tirofiban: 4/96; ST alone for Indobufen: 2/250). Although differences were not statistically significant (OR = 0.33, 95% CI: 0.11–1.03 for argatroban + ST; OR = 0.48, 95% CI: 0.12–1.91 for tirofiban + ST; OR = 0.50, 95% CI: 0.05–5.40 for indobufen), the analysis highlights the low event rate and wide confidence intervals, indicating substantial statistical uncertainty. Sensitivity analyses including zero-event studies with continuity correction did not materially change these results, emphasizing the need for cautious interpretation of mortality outcomes.

Discussion

In the clinical management of AIS, some patients experience END despite receiving standard reperfusion therapies such as intravenous thrombolysis. These patients often have poorer neurological outcomes, with a significantly increased risk of long-term disability and mortality. Existing research and clinical practice have primarily focused on risk prediction and identification of high-risk populations prior to the onset of END. However, for AIS patients who develop END, the selection of clinical interventions lacks robust high-quality evidence, and no standardized treatment strategy has been established.

This study employed a systematic review and network meta-analysis to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of various interventions for AIS patients with END, aiming to provide high-quality evidence to support precision medicine, individualized treatment, and optimized therapeutic strategies. A total of five high-quality RCTs were included. The pooled results indicate that, compared with standard antiplatelet therapy, indobufen and tirofiban combined with standard treatment significantly improved functional outcomes. Although argatroban combined with standard treatment reduced mean mRS scores, it did not significantly increase the proportion of patients achieving excellent functional outcomes (mRS 0–1) or favorable functional outcomes (mRS 0–2), highlighting a more modest clinical effect. Specifically, these interventions reduced the mean 90-day mRS score; in terms of early and long-term neurological recovery, indobufen and tirofiban combined with standard treatment significantly reduced NIHSS scores at both 7 and 90 days, indicating superior neurological recovery. However, neither argatroban combined with standard treatment nor tirofiban combined with standard treatment showed a significant advantage in achieving favorable functional outcomes. In terms of safety, none of the three intensified intervention strategies significantly increased the risk of intracranial or symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage or 90-day mortality. However, event rates were low and confidence intervals wide, reflecting substantial statistical uncertainty. Sensitivity analyses accounting for zero-event studies yielded similar results, reinforcing the need for cautious interpretation. Clinically, while no major safety signal was observed, these findings highlight the importance of individualized risk assessment when applying intensified antithrombotic strategies in END patients.

Furthermore, it is worth noting that the pathophysiological mechanisms of END are highly heterogeneous, encompassing progressive thrombosis, hemodynamic failure, hemorrhagic transformation, and cerebral edema [13]. The optimal therapeutic response is inherently mechanism-dependent; for instance, selective antiplatelet regimens like indobufen or GPIIb/IIIa inhibitors like tirofiban may yield maximum efficacy in END driven by plaque instability or microthrombosis. In our study, both indobufen and tirofiban combinations demonstrated superior long-term and short-term neurological recovery. However, a major clinical challenge is that the included primary RCTs did not stratify outcomes based on these specific underlying mechanisms of deterioration. Consequently, our findings reflect the average therapeutic effect across a mixed END population, which may obscure mechanism-specific benefits and limit direct clinical precision. This critical stratification warrants dedicated investigation in future trials.

Indobufen, an isoindolinyl phenylbutyric acid derivative, is a reversible antiplatelet agent. Its mechanism of action involves selective inhibition of platelet cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1), which differs fundamentally from that of aspirin. Aspirin irreversibly inhibits COX-1, whereas indobufen exerts a reversible inhibitory effect, providing antiplatelet activity while potentially reducing bleeding risk. Previous studies have demonstrated that this reversible COX-1 inhibition maintains antithrombotic efficacy while preserving the synthesis of protective prostaglandins, thereby potentially reducing infarct size. This mechanism may underlie the observed advantage of indobufen over aspirin in early neurological recovery among patients with END [14].

The INSURE study demonstrated that indobufen is non-inferior to aspirin in patients with moderate-to-severe AIS. Although indobufen was slightly less effective in reducing 90-day stroke recurrence, it was associated with a lower incidence of moderate-to-severe bleeding; however, this difference was not statistically significant (0.7% vs. 1.0%, p = 0.13). Notably, neurological recovery outcomes were not specifically analyzed in this study [15]. The present study, focusing on the END population, further supports the potential advantages of indobufen in improving neurological outcomes while maintaining a favorable safety profile, particularly for patients at high risk of bleeding.

Argatroban is a synthetic arginine derivative and a direct thrombin inhibitor widely used in clinical practice. In China, it has been approved for improving neurological symptoms and daily functioning in patients with AIS within 48 h of onset. Previous RCTs have shown that argatroban combined with alteplase does not significantly improve 90-day neurological outcomes compared with alteplase alone; studies in U.S. populations have similarly reported that adjunctive argatroban does not reduce post-stroke disability and may increase the risk of intracranial hemorrhage [16, 17]. The findings of the present study are consistent with these previous reports. In patients with AIS and END, although argatroban combined with standard treatment improved mean mRS scores, it did not increase the proportion of patients achieving favorable outcomes (mRS 0–1 or 0–2) at 90 days, suggesting a limited effect on long-term functional recovery and warranting cautious consideration in clinical practice.

Tirofiban is a non-peptide tyrosine derivative and a highly selective glycoprotein IIb/IIIa (GPIIb/IIIa) receptor antagonist. It is primarily used in the treatment of non-ST-segment elevation acute coronary syndrome and has also been extended to the management of AIS. Previous studies have shown that, in patients with large-vessel occlusion stroke, intravenous tirofiban administered prior to endovascular thrombectomy did not significantly improve 90-day functional outcomes. However, in Chinese patients with AIS treated within 4.5 h of onset, early administration of tirofiban has been associated with improved long-term functional outcomes [18, 19]. In contrast, the present study focuses on AIS patients who have developed END. This population has typically missed the ultra-early therapeutic window, which may partly explain why tirofiban combination therapy did not significantly increase the proportion of patients achieving favorable outcomes. Additionally, the limited sample size and insufficient statistical power may have contributed to the negative findings. Larger studies are required to validate these findings.

During the literature screening process, several studies involving patients with AIS and END were identified but did not meet the inclusion criteria. These studies may provide valuable directions for future research. A retrospective cohort study by Wang et al. demonstrated that, in patients with mild stroke and END, tirofiban bridging followed by sequential dual antiplatelet therapy significantly improved early neurological recovery and 90-day outcomes compared with aspirin alone or aspirin plus clopidogrel, without increasing bleeding risk [20]. Another retrospective study reported that tirofiban combined with remote ischemic preconditioning further enhanced neurological recovery in END patients while maintaining a favorable safety profile [21]. A multicenter retrospective study showed that early administration of low-dose tirofiban after intravenous thrombolysis in patients with AIS and END did not increase the risk of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage, overall intracranial hemorrhage, or 90-day mortality, while improving the proportion of patients with favorable 3-month functional outcomes [22]. A study in a Korean population found that mechanical thrombectomy and rescue endovascular therapy significantly improved neurological outcomes and increased the proportion of patients achieving mRS 0–2 [23]. Collectively, these studies suggest that individualized and stratified treatment strategies represent a key direction for future research in END management.

Although this study provides an evidence-based foundation for clinical intervention in AIS patients with END, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, all included RCTs were conducted in China (total n = 1,164), which limits generalizability to other healthcare systems or populations with different genetic and environmental backgrounds [24, 25]. Second, the number of high-quality RCTs is small (n = 5), and the total sample size is modest for a network meta-analysis, particularly for low-frequency safety outcomes such as intracranial hemorrhage and mortality, resulting in wide confidence intervals. Third, the definition and components of standard therapy (ST) varied across trials (aspirin monotherapy, placebo plus aspirin, or dual antiplatelet therapy), introducing potential clinical heterogeneity. Fourth, the mRS outcome data were obtained from different sources, which may have introduced minor inconsistencies. Fifth, pharmacogenetic factors (e.g., COX-1, CYP2C19 polymorphisms) could influence treatment response but could not be analyzed due to lack of individual patient data. Sixth, the included trials did not classify END according to its underlying pathophysiological mechanism. Because treatment effects may differ among END caused by thrombus progression, recurrent embolism, hemodynamic compromise, hemorrhagic transformation, or cerebral edema, the pooled estimates represent average effects across a mechanistically heterogeneous population and may not be directly applicable to mechanism-specific clinical decision-making. Future large, multicenter, multinational RCTs with standardized definitions and genetic profiling [26] are needed to confirm our findings.

In summary, current evidence remains insufficient to guide clinical management of AIS with END. Large, multicenter RCTs are needed to evaluate intervention effectiveness and safety, and future studies should explore novel therapies and refine individualized treatment strategies.
