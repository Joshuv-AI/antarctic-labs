Pituitary adenoma-associated with Schöpf-Schulz-Passarge syndrome
Authors: Robert Thomas Brady, Kieren Allinson, Cornelius René
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryHidrocystomas are benign cystic skin tumours of sweat gland origin which often involve the eyelids but are usually solitary. We report the case of a middle-aged man with multiple bilateral eyelid hidrocystomas, palmoplantar keratoderma, hypodontia and nail dystrophy, in keeping with a diagnosis of Schöpf-Schulz-Passarge syndrome, an exceedingly rare ectodermal dysplasia. Hyperprolactinaemia was found on testing, and radiology revealed an incidental prolactinoma. Raised serum prolactin has not been previously associated with Schöpf-Schulz-Passarge syndrome. The significance of raised serum prolactin in the context of multiple eyelid hidrocystomas is discussed.

Background

Hidrocystomas are benign sweat-filled cysts which commonly occur in the periocular area.1 They are typically solitary lesions and are not usually associated with any systemic abnormality although there are sparse reports in the medical literature of multiple bilateral eyelid hidrocystomas in patients with prolactinoma.24 Multiple bilateral eyelid hidrocystomas are also a feature of rare dysplastic syndromes, such as Schöpf-Schulz-Passarge syndrome5 and focal dermal hypoplasia (Goltz-Gorlin syndrome),6 but raised serum prolactin has not been previously associated with these syndromes. The association between raised serum prolactin and multiple bilateral eyelid hidrocystomas is interesting and highlights the role of prolactin in mammalian skin health and cutaneous disease. Case presentation

A man in his 60s was referred with multiple bilateral eyelid cysts which were present for several years. He was especially concerned about a sizeable lesion on the left lower eyelid which was slowly enlarging. A large cystic lesion, which was excised from the right upper lid margin 9 months earlier, was reported histologically as an apocrine hidrocystoma, and our dermatology colleagues previously diagnosed hypohidrotic ectodermal dystrophy due to palmoplantar keratoderma, abnormal dentition, reduced sweating and nail dystrophy, but he was otherwise well.

Ocular examination revealed good visual acuity of 6/9 in both eyes. There were multiple transilluminable cysts of varying sizes on both upper and lower lids bilaterally, extending into both medial canthi (figure 1A). Most of the lesions were located on the eyelid margin, and decapitation secretion, which is typical of apocrine hidrocystomas, was evident in the majority. However, there was no decapitation secretion in the largest lesion, which measured 8 mm in diameter and was situated on the lateral aspect of the left lower lid, in the region of the eyelid-cheek junction. Anterior segment examination was unremarkable, the intraocular pressures were normal, and fundal examination was normal in both eyes. There was a hyperkeratotic eruption on the plantar aspect of both feet, especially the heels (figure 1B) and focally on both proximal palms (figure 1C). The patient also had thin nail plates, soft nails and oral examination revealed hypodontia (figure 1D). The constellation of clinical features was in keeping with a diagnosis of Schöpf-Schulz-Passarge syndrome, which was not confirmed with genetic testing.

Investigations

Analysis of the patient’s serum prolactin revealed an elevated level of 2950 mU/L (normal range 45–375 mU/L). Subsequent MRI scanning confirmed asymmetry of the pituitary gland and sella with lateral displacement of the left internal carotid artery. The pituitary gland was of normal size and morphology, but the pituitary stalk was deviated to the left, with a mass on the left side of the gland which was T1 hypointense (figure 2A) and T2 isointense to normal pituitary tissue. The mass enhanced with gadolinium (figure 2B). There was no incursion into the left cavernous sinus or compression of the chiasm, and the radiographic appearance was consistent with a pituitary adenoma. Humphrey visual field analysis did not reveal a bitemporal hemianopia or other significant visual field deficit.

Treatment

The patient was referred to the endocrinology team and was started on cabergoline 250 micrograms twice weekly. He has reported subjective improvement in his eyelid hidrocystomas as well as improved libido, and he continues to be monitored by the endocrinologists.

The left lower lid lesion was excised intact under local anaesthesia, and the histology reported a unilocular cyst lined by an outer layer of flattened myoepithelial cells and an inner layer of flattened cuboidal cells without apical decapitation secretion, consistent with an eccrine hidrocystoma (figure 2C), in contrast to the previously excised apocrine hidrocystoma from the right upper eyelid (figure 2D).

Outcome and follow-up

The patient remains under regular review by both the ophthalmology and endocrine teams. His prolactin levels have normalised, and he remains asymptomatic. The remaining multiple small bilateral eyelid hidrocystomas have not progressed, and his visual acuity remains normal in both eyes.

Discussion

Schöpf-Schulz-Passarge syndrome (SSPS) is a very rare ectodermal dysplasia which was first described in 1971.5 The main features are multiple eyelid apocrine hidrocystomas, palmar-plantar keratoderma, hypodontia, hypotrichosis and nail dystrophy. It is known to result from mutations in the WNT10A gene.7 8 WNT10A is a member of the Wnt family of secreted signalling proteins, which play crucial roles in regulating embryonic development, cell survival, proliferation, migration and differentiation. This canonical Wnt ligand, which activates the intracellular β-catenin signal transduction pathway, is expressed during embryogenesis and is a pivotal player in ectodermal tissue development, with germline loss-of-function mutations leading to congenital disorders affecting skin, hair, teeth, nails, and sweat glands.7 Hence, mutations in this gene are also responsible for other similar ectodermal dysplasias, such as odonto-onycho-dermal dysplasia.79 Apart from being implicated in embryonic development, WNT10A is also involved in tumourigenesis, and there is evidence that overexpression or upregulation contributes to the development of certain cancers, such as renal carcinoma, colorectal carcinoma and papillary thyroid carcinoma.1013

The diagnosis of SSPS in our patient was based on the classic clinical features, and genetic testing was not performed. SSPS usually has an autosomal recessive inheritance pattern but is sometimes sporadic, and the phenotypic spectrum is extremely variable. However, the presence of multiple eyelid apocrine hidrocystomas is a universal feature and seems to be the hallmark of the condition.14 In addition to multiple apocrine hidrocystomas, our patient also had at least one eccrine hidrocystoma, which has only been reported in one previous case of SSPS.15

As far as we know, this is the first report of SSPS associated with a prolactinoma. This association is interesting, considering that the pituitary gland derives from ectoderm and SSPS is an ectodermal dysplasia. However, an embryological link between SSPS and prolactinoma is only speculative, and there is little evidence to support it. Our literature search uncovered only three published cases of prolactinoma associated with multiple bilateral eyelid hidrocystomas,24 but none had clinical features of SSPS.

The association between multiple bilateral eyelid hidrocystomas and hyperprolactinaemia itself is also interesting. Prolactin, a polypeptide hormone, is primarily responsible for lactation and breast development but has a myriad of other functions.16 17 Although it is mainly produced in the anterior pituitary gland, prolactin is also produced by the skin and, by interacting with prolactin receptors in the skin, prolactin plays an important role in mammalian skin health and disease via processes such as regulation of keratin expression, modulating hair growth, stimulating sebum production and regulating sweat gland activity in the skin.18 Hence, hyperprolactinaemia may be implicated in the causation of deranged cutaneous health. Since a prolactinoma is the the most common cause of hyperprolactinaemia, clinicians should consider the possibility of a prolactinoma in patients with multiple bilateral eyelid hidrocystomas, although the precise mechanism remains uncertain.

Patient’s perspective

Cysts began to grow around my eyes, including on the eyelids. They were unsightly, and I asked my GP to remove them. He removed one from around an eye, but referred me to the hospital clinic for surgery on the eyelid, which was performed. I was later referred to the hospital for another large cyst, only to be told initially that new NHS guidelines treated eyelid cysts as a cosmetic blemish unless they were so large that they impeded vision. The consultant I saw said that treatment could still take place on the NHS if the cysts arose from another medical condition, since he had recently read a medical paper which suggested this could be the case. He made a special funding application for my surgery and tested the prolactin level in my blood which turned out to be very high. He subsequently arranged an MRI scan of my head, which revealed the presence of a benign growth on my pituitary gland, and he referred me to the endocrine department for treatment (to shrink the pituitary growth and prevent damage to the optic nerve).The cyst around the eye was removed surgically, but the growth on my pituitary gland was treated by cabergoline (250 micrograms twice a week). As this treatment dropped the high prolactin level produced by the growth back to normal (the level had been off the roof), it was felt that surgery would not be necessary. I was warned about the possible side effects of cabergoline on behaviour (gambling and inappropriate sexual conduct) but have not suffered from either!
