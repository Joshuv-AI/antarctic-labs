Abstract
. We study affine deformations of the cotangent groupoid T ∗ ​ 𝒢 ⇉ A ∗ T^{*}\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows A^{*} , governed by a one-form γ ∈ Ω 1 ​ ( 𝒢 ( 2 ) ) \gamma\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{(2)}) , and characterize the conditions on γ \gamma under which this construction is valid. We show that these deformations arise naturally from 𝕊 1 \mathbb{S}^{1} -central extensions of Lie groupoids via symplectic reduction, and identify the reduced symplectic form as a multiplicative magnetic form. In particular, for Kac-Moody extensions, this construction yields nontrivial deformations of quotient stacks and 𝕊 1 \mathbb{S}^{1} -gerbes.

1.
Introduction

Deformations arise in Lie groupoid theory in several distinct forms. On the analytic side, tangent and adiabatic groupoids play an important role in pseudodifferential calculi, index theory, and deformation quantization

[
11
,
25
,
15
]
.
On the symplectic and Poisson side, central extensions, gauge transformations, and twisting procedures provide natural ways to modify multiplicative symplectic or presymplectic forms

[
27
,
8
,
9
,
7
,
6
]
.

More recently, cohomological approaches have clarified the deformation theory of Lie groupoids and related structures. Crainic, Mestre, and Struchiner constructed the deformation complex of Lie groupoids and proved its Morita invariance
[
13
]
. Kosmeijer and Posthuma related this complex to the Hochschild complex of the convolution algebra
[
16
]
. Cárdenas, Mestre, and Struchiner developed a deformation theory for symplectic groupoids
[
10
]
, and La Pastina and Vitagliano studied deformation complexes for VB-groupoids
[
18
]
.

The construction in this paper is more explicit. We deform the cotangent groupoid structure and the multiplicative symplectic form simultaneously. Recall that, for a Lie groupoid

\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
with Lie algebroid
A
, the cotangent groupoid

T^{*}\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows A^{*}

was introduced by Coste, Dazord, and Weinstein
[
12
]
. Together with the canonical symplectic form, it is a fundamental example of a symplectic groupoid, integrating the linear Poisson structure on
A^{*}
.

We study an explicit class of affine deformations of the cotangent groupoid. We first deform the Lie groupoid structure. This deformation is governed by a one-form

\gamma\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{(2)}).

Theorem A
.

Let
\gamma\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{(2)})
. Consider the maps

\hat{{\mathbf{s}}},\hat{{\mathbf{t}}},\hat{{\mathbf{m}}},\hat{{\mathbf{u}}},\hat{{\mathbf{\iota}}}

defined in (
2.1
)-(
2.5
), obtained from
the usual cotangent groupoid structure maps by adding affine correction terms
determined by
\gamma
. These maps make

T^{*}\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows A^{*}
into a Lie groupoid if and only if the
following conditions hold:

(1)

Cocycle condition:
\partial\gamma=0
;

(2)

Degeneracy normalization:
(\sigma_{0})^{*}\gamma=0
and

(\sigma_{1})^{*}\gamma=0
.

When the above conditions hold, we call the resulting groupoid the

\gamma
-deformed cotangent groupoid
, and denote it by

(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma}
.

We next consider the multiplicative symplectic form. The ordinary cotangent
groupoid carries the canonical multiplicative symplectic form

\omega_{\mathrm{can}}
. After the groupoid structure is deformed by

\gamma
, this form need not remain multiplicative. We therefore add a
magnetic term pulled back from
\mathcal{G}
: for

\omega_{B}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{G})
, set

\omega_{M}=\omega_{\mathrm{can}}+({\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}})^{*}\omega_{B}.

Then
\omega_{M}
is multiplicative on
(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma}
if and only if

\partial\omega_{B}=d\gamma.

Thus the failure of the canonical form to be multiplicative is measured by

d\gamma
, and it is compensated by the groupoid coboundary of the magnetic
term
\omega_{B}
.

In the rest of the paper, we focus on a geometrically important source of such
deformations:
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extensions of Lie groupoids. Central
extensions of Lie groupoids and their relation with prequantization have been
studied extensively in Poisson geometry

[
27
,
14
]
, and symplectic reduction of symplectic groupoids
is a classical construction
[
23
,
27
]
. The point relevant
here is that reduction at a nonzero moment level naturally produces the
affine deformations introduced above.

More precisely, let

M\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\longrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{G}

be an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groupoids, viewed as a
principal
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-bundle over
\mathcal{G}
. For a normalized
connection
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}})
, the forms

\partial\theta
and
d\theta
descend to forms

\gamma\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{(2)}),\qquad\omega_{B}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{G}),

and satisfy

\partial\omega_{B}=d\gamma

[
5
]
. The next theorem identifies the corresponding

\gamma
-deformed cotangent groupoid with the symplectic groupoid
obtained by reducing
T^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
at moment level
1
.

Theorem B
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathcal{G}
be an

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groupoids.

(1)

The symplectic reduction of

(T^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},\omega_{\mathrm{can}})
at moment level
1

yields a reduced symplectic groupoid

\Gamma=\mu^{-1}(1)/\mathbb{S}^{1}.

(2)

Let

\theta\in\Omega^{1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}})
be a normalized principal
connection. Then there exists a natural diffeomorphism

\Gamma\cong T^{*}\mathcal{G}
,
under which the reduced groupoid structure is identified with the

\gamma
-deformed cotangent groupoid

(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma}
, and the
reduced symplectic form is

\omega_{\mathrm{can}}+({\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}})^{*}\omega_{B}.

In Section
3.4
, we show that the deformation arising
from the Kac-Moody extension is geometrically nontrivial. As the reduction
level varies, the resulting Lie groupoids interpolate between two
non-Morita-equivalent presentations, namely the coadjoint action groupoid and
the gauge action groupoid. At the level of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbes, presented
by the central extensions of Lie groupoids

\mu^{-1}(r)\longrightarrow\mu^{-1}(r)/\mathbb{S}^{1},\quad r\in\mathbb{R},

the gerbe family interpolates between a gerbe with trivial Dixmier–Douady
class and the basic gerbe over
[G/G]
. When
G
is compact, simple, and
simply connected, the Dixmier–Douady class of the latter represents the
generator of
H^{3}_{G}(G;\mathbb{Z})
.

Finally, we discuss applications to affine Poisson geometry. In the presence
of an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groupoids, the corresponding
deformation induces an affine Poisson structure on
A^{*}
, and this affine
Poisson structure is integrated by the reduced symplectic groupoid constructed
above. Moreover, the reduced groupoid carries natural prequantization data.

2.
Deforming the cotangent groupoid

For a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
, there is a naturally associated groupoid
T^{*}\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows A^{*}
, known as the
cotangent groupoid

[
12
]
, where
A^{*}
denotes the dual of the Lie algebroid of
\mathcal{G}
. In this section, we introduce a special deformation of the standard groupoid structure on
T^{*}\mathcal{G}
, termed the
affine deformation
.

2.1.
\gamma
-deformed cotangent groupoids

Let
{\mathbf{s}},{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{m}},{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{\iota}}
denote the structure maps of
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
, see Definition
A.1
for details.
Define the space of composable pairs by
\mathcal{G}^{(2)}:=\mathcal{G}_{{\mathbf{s}}}\times_{{\mathbf{t}}}\mathcal{G}
, and fix a 1-form
\gamma\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{(2)})
. We then construct a deformation of the cotangent groupoid
T^{*}\mathcal{G}
governed by
\gamma
.

Let
{\mathbf{s}}_{0},{\mathbf{t}}_{0},{\mathbf{m}}_{0},{\mathbf{u}}_{0},{\mathbf{\iota}}_{0}
stand for the structure maps of the canonical cotangent groupoid
T^{*}\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows A^{*}
, as characterized in Theorem
A.4
. Using the 1-form
\gamma
, we equip
T^{*}\mathcal{G}
with a new groupoid structure specified as follows:

(1)

The source map
\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}\colon T^{*}\mathcal{G}\to A^{*}
is defined by

(2.1)

\langle\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\xi),a\rangle:=\langle{{\mathbf{s}}}_{0}(\xi),a\rangle+\gamma(0_{k},{\mathbf{\iota}}_{*}a),

where
k\in\mathcal{G}
,
\xi\in T^{*}_{k}\mathcal{G}
,
a\in A_{{\mathbf{s}}(k)}
and
(0_{k},{\mathbf{\iota}}_{*}a)\in T_{(k,{\mathbf{u}}{\mathbf{s}}(k))}\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
.

(2)

The target map
\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}\colon T^{*}\mathcal{G}\to A^{*}
is defined by

(2.2)

\langle\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}(\xi),b\rangle:=\langle{{\mathbf{t}}}_{0}(\xi),b\rangle-\gamma(b,0_{k}),

where
k\in\mathcal{G}
,
\xi\in T^{*}_{k}\mathcal{G}
,
b\in A_{{\mathbf{t}}(k)}
and
(b,0_{k})\in T_{({\mathbf{u}}{\mathbf{t}}(k),k)}\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
.

(3)

The multiplication map
\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}\colon T^{*}\mathcal{G}^{(2)}\to T^{*}\mathcal{G}
is determined by

(2.3)

\langle\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(w_{1},w_{2})\rangle:=\langle\xi_{1},w_{1}\rangle+\langle\xi_{2},w_{2}\rangle+\gamma(w_{1},w_{2}),

where
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathcal{G}
with
{\mathbf{s}}(k_{1})={\mathbf{t}}(k_{2})
, and
\xi_{1}\in T^{*}_{k_{1}}\mathcal{G},\xi_{2}\in T^{*}_{k_{2}}\mathcal{G}
with
\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\xi_{1})=\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}(\xi_{2})
, and
w_{1}\in T_{k_{1}}\mathcal{G},w_{2}\in T_{k_{2}}\mathcal{G}
with
{{\mathbf{s}}}_{*}(w_{1})={{\mathbf{t}}}_{*}(w_{2})
.

(4)

The unit map
\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}\colon A^{*}\to T^{*}\mathcal{G}
is defined as

(2.4)

\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}={\mathbf{u}}_{0}.

(5)

The inverse map
\hat{\iota}\colon T^{*}\mathcal{G}\to T^{*}\mathcal{G}
is defined by

(2.5)

\langle\hat{\iota}(\xi),w\rangle:=\langle\iota_{0}(\xi),w\rangle-\gamma(\iota_{*}w,w),

where
k\in\mathcal{G}
,
\xi\in T^{*}_{k}\mathcal{G}
and
w\in T_{\iota(k)}\mathcal{G}
.

Definition 2.1
.

We call
T^{*}\mathcal{G}
a
\gamma
-deformed cotangent groupoid
, if
T^{*}\mathcal{G}
is a Lie groupoid over
A^{*}
with structure maps
(\hat{{\mathbf{s}}},\hat{{\mathbf{t}}},\hat{{\mathbf{m}}},\hat{{\mathbf{u}}},\hat{\iota})
as defined above.

We now establish the following criterion for deformed cotangent groupoids.

Theorem 2.2
.

A 1-form
\gamma\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{(2)})
makes
T^{*}\mathcal{G}
a
\gamma
-deformed cotangent groupoid if and only if all the following conditions hold:

(1)

Cocycle condition:
\partial\gamma=0
,

(2)

Degeneracy normalization:
(\sigma_{0})^{*}\gamma=0
and
(\sigma_{1})^{*}\gamma=0
.

Here,
\partial
is the groupoid differential. Further,
\sigma_{0}
,
\sigma_{1}
are the degeneracy maps
\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
given by

\sigma_{0}(g)=({\mathbf{u}}({{\mathbf{t}}(g)}),g)
and
\sigma_{1}(g)=(g,{\mathbf{u}}({\mathbf{s}}(g))).

Remark 2.3
.

If
\gamma
satisfies the two conditions above, then for
r\in\mathbb{R}
, the family
(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{(r\gamma)}
forms a strict deformation of Lie groupoids, in the sense of
[
13
]
. Since the structure maps depend affinely on the parameter
r
, we refer to this as an
affine deformation of the cotangent groupoid
.

We divide the proof of Theorem
2.2
into conditions for different classes of groupoid axioms. We first derive the condition for
T^{*}\mathcal{G}
being a semigroupoid. Here, a semigroupoid means that all the groupoid identities involving
\hat{{\mathbf{s}}},\hat{{\mathbf{t}}},\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}
hold, while the existence of the unit map and the inverse map is not required. We need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathcal{G}
satisfying
{\mathbf{s}}(k_{1})={\mathbf{t}}(k_{2})
, and any tangent vectors
w_{1}\in T_{k_{1}}\mathcal{G}
,
w_{2}\in T_{k_{2}}\mathcal{G}
with
{\mathbf{s}}_{*}(w_{1})={\mathbf{t}}_{*}(w_{2})
, then
{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(w_{1},w_{2})=0
, if and only if there exists some
a\in A_{{\mathbf{s}}(k_{1})}
such that

w_{1}=(L_{k_{1}})_{*}\iota_{*}a\quad\text{and}\quad w_{2}=(R_{k_{2}})_{*}a,

where
L_{k_{1}}
is the left multiplication by
k_{1}
and
R_{k_{2}}
is the right multiplication by
k_{2}.

Proof.

Since
{\mathbf{s}}_{*}w_{2}={\mathbf{s}}_{*}{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(w_{1},w_{2})
, we have
{\mathbf{s}}_{*}w_{2}=0
.
Hence
(R_{\iota(k_{2})})_{*}w_{2}\in A_{{\mathbf{s}}(k_{1})}
, and there exists

a\in A_{{\mathbf{s}}(k_{1})}
such that
w_{2}=(R_{k_{2}})_{*}a
.

Similarly, from
{\mathbf{t}}_{*}w_{1}={\mathbf{t}}_{*}{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(w_{1},w_{2})
, we obtain

{\mathbf{s}}_{*}\iota_{*}w_{1}=0
. It follows that

(R_{k_{1}})_{*}\iota_{*}w_{1}\in A_{{\mathbf{s}}(k_{1})}
, so there exists

b\in A_{{\mathbf{s}}(k_{1})}
such that
w_{1}=(L_{k_{1}})_{*}\iota_{*}b
.

On the other hand, the condition
{\mathbf{s}}_{*}(w_{1})={\mathbf{t}}_{*}(w_{2})
implies

{\mathbf{t}}_{*}a={\mathbf{t}}_{*}b
. Combining this with the assumption

{\mathbf{m}}_{*}\big((L_{k_{1}})_{*}\iota_{*}b,(R_{k_{2}})_{*}a\big)=0,

we conclude that
a=b
. The converse follows by a similar argument.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Endowed with the structure maps
\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}
,
\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}
,
\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}
defined above,

T^{*}\mathcal{G}
is a semigroupoid if and only if

(2.6)

\partial\gamma=0,\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma(\iota_{*}a,a)=0,\quad\text{for all }a\in A.

Proof.

We first show that Equation (
2.6
) implies that
\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}
is well-defined.
By Lemma
2.4
, it suffices to verify that the pairing

(2.7)

\displaystyle\big\langle\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),\,{\mathbf{m}}_{*}\big((L_{k_{1}})_{*}\iota_{*}a,(R_{k_{2}})_{*}a\big)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\gamma\big((L_{k_{1}})_{*}\iota_{*}a,(R_{k_{2}})_{*}a\big)+\langle\xi_{1},(L_{k_{1}})_{*}\iota_{*}a\rangle+\langle(R_{k_{2}})_{*}a,\xi_{2}\rangle

vanishes for all
a\in A_{{\mathbf{s}}(k_{1})}
.

Using Equations (
2.1
), (
A.5
), and

\iota_{*}a=-a+{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{t}}_{*}a
, we compute

\displaystyle\langle\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\xi_{1}),a\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{{\mathbf{s}}}_{0}(\xi_{1}),a\rangle+\gamma(0_{k},{\mathbf{\iota}}_{*}a)

\displaystyle=\langle\xi_{1},-(L_{k_{1}})_{*}\iota_{*}a\rangle+\gamma(0_{k},{\mathbf{\iota}}_{*}a).

Similarly, by Equations (
2.2
) and (
A.6
),

\langle\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}(\xi_{2}),a\rangle=\langle\xi_{2},(R_{k_{2}})_{*}a\rangle-\gamma(a,0_{k_{2}}).

Since
\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\xi_{1})=\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}(\xi_{2})
, we obtain

(2.8)

\langle\xi_{1},(L_{k_{1}})_{*}\iota_{*}a\rangle+\langle(R_{k_{2}})_{*}a,\xi_{2}\rangle=\gamma(0_{k_{1}},\iota_{*}a)+\gamma(a,0_{k_{2}}).

Substituting (
2.8
) into (
2.7
) yields

(2.9)

\displaystyle\big\langle\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),\,{\mathbf{m}}_{*}\big((L_{k_{1}})_{*}\iota_{*}a,(R_{k_{2}})_{*}a\big)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\gamma\big((L_{k_{1}})_{*}\iota_{*}a,(R_{k_{2}})_{*}a\big)+\gamma(0_{k_{1}},\iota_{*}a)+\gamma(a,0_{k_{2}}).

Now apply the cocycle condition
\partial\gamma=0
together with
Lemma
2.4
. We have

(2.10)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(\partial\gamma)(0_{k_{1}},\iota_{*}a,(R_{k_{2}})_{*}a)

\displaystyle=\gamma(\iota_{*}a,(R_{k_{2}})_{*}a)-\gamma\big((L_{k_{1}})_{*}\iota_{*}a,(R_{k_{2}})_{*}a\big)-\gamma(0_{k_{1}},\iota_{*}a),

and

(2.11)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(\partial\gamma)(\iota_{*}a,a,0_{k_{2}})

\displaystyle=\gamma(a,0_{k_{2}})+\gamma(\iota_{*}a,(R_{k_{2}})_{*}a)-\gamma(\iota_{*}a,a).

Combining Equations (
2.10
), (
2.11
) with (
2.9
),
we arrive at

\big\langle\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),\,{\mathbf{m}}_{*}\big((L_{k_{1}})_{*}\iota_{*}a,(R_{k_{2}})_{*}a\big)\big\rangle=\gamma(\iota_{*}a,a).

Hence, if
\gamma(\iota_{*}a,a)=0
for all
a\in A_{{\mathbf{s}}(k_{1})}
, then
\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}
is well-defined. Using
\partial\gamma=0
again, it follows that
\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}
is associative.

It remains to verify the semigroupoid axioms.
Under Equation (
2.6
), we check that

\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}))=\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\xi_{2})
and

\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}(\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}))=\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}(\xi_{1})
.
For the first identity, a direct computation gives

\displaystyle\langle\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})),a\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\xi_{2}),a\rangle+\gamma(0_{k_{1}k_{2}},\iota_{*}a)-\gamma(0_{k_{1}},(L_{k_{2}})_{*}\iota_{*}a)-\gamma(0_{k_{2}},\iota_{*}a).

The last three terms vanish because
(\partial\gamma)(0_{k_{1}},0_{k_{2}},\iota_{*}a)=0
.
The second identity follows analogously from

(\partial\gamma)(b,0_{k_{1}},0_{k_{2}})=0
for
b\in A_{{\mathbf{t}}(k_{1})}
.

Thus Equation (
2.6
) implies that
T^{*}\mathcal{G}
is a semigroupoid.
Conversely, if
T^{*}\mathcal{G}
carries a semigroupoid structure, then associativity of
\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}
forces the cocycle condition

\partial\gamma=0
.
Furthermore, the well-definedness of
\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}
yields
\gamma(\iota_{*}a,a)=0
for all
a\in A_{{\mathbf{s}}(k_{1})}
.
∎

For the unit and inverse axioms to hold, we need additional conditions.

Proposition 2.6
.

Assume Condition (
2.6
) holds. Then the unit axioms for

\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}
are equivalent to

(2.12)

(\sigma_{0})^{*}\gamma=0,\qquad(\sigma_{1})^{*}\gamma=0.

Proof.

We first show that
\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}\circ\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}=\mathrm{id}_{A^{*}}
if and only if

\gamma(a,0_{{\mathbf{u}}(x)})=0
for all
a\in A_{x}
.
By Equations (
2.2
) and (
2.4
), for any
\alpha\in A^{*}_{x}
we have

\langle\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}(\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}(\alpha)),a\rangle=\langle{{\mathbf{t}}}_{0}({{\mathbf{u}}}_{0}(\alpha)),a\rangle-\gamma(a,0_{{\mathbf{u}}(x)})=\langle\alpha,a\rangle-\gamma(a,0_{{\mathbf{u}}(x)}).

Hence
\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}\circ\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}=\mathrm{id}_{A^{*}}
precisely when
\gamma(a,0_{{\mathbf{u}}(x)})=0

for all
a\in A_{x}
.

Next, we show that

\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}\big(\xi,\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}(\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\xi))\big)=\xi,\quad\text{ for all}\penalty\ \xi\in T^{*}_{k}\mathcal{G},

if and only if
(\sigma_{1})^{*}\gamma=0
.
For any
w\in T_{k}\mathcal{G}
, note that
{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(w,{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}w)=w
.
Using Equation (
A.8
), we compute

\displaystyle\langle\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\xi,\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}(\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\xi))),w\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\xi,\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}(\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\xi))),{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(w,{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}w)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\xi,w\rangle+\langle{\mathbf{u}}_{0}(\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\xi)),{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}w\rangle+\gamma(w,{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}w)

\displaystyle=\langle\xi,w\rangle+\langle\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\xi),{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}w-{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}w\rangle+\gamma(w,{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}w)

\displaystyle=\langle\xi,w\rangle+\gamma(w,{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}w).

Thus
\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\xi,\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}(\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\xi)))=\xi
for all

if and only if

\gamma(w,{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}w)=0
for all

, which is precisely
(\sigma_{1})^{*}\gamma=0
.

Note that under

, the condition
\gamma(a,0_{{\mathbf{u}}(x)})=0

follows automatically.

By the same argument,
\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}\circ\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}=\mathrm{id}_{A^{*}}
if and only if

\gamma(0_{{\mathbf{u}}(x)},\iota_{*}a)=0
for all
a\in A_{x}
, and

\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}(\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}(\xi)),\xi)=\xi
for all
\xi\in T^{*}_{k}\mathcal{G}

if and only if
(\sigma_{0})^{*}\gamma=0
.
Moreover,
(\sigma_{0})^{*}\gamma=0

\gamma(0_{{\mathbf{u}}(x)},\iota_{*}a)=0
.

Therefore, the unit axioms hold if and only if both

.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Assume
(\sigma_{0})^{*}\gamma=0
and
(\sigma_{1})^{*}\gamma=0
, then
\gamma(\iota_{*}a,a)=0
for all
a\in A
.

Proof.

Since
(\sigma_{0})^{*}\gamma=0
and
(\sigma_{1})^{*}\gamma=0
, we have

\gamma(a,0)=0
and
\gamma({\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{t}}_{*}a,a)=0
, for all
a\in A
.
Using
\iota_{*}a=-a+{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{t}}_{*}a
, we compute

\gamma(\iota_{*}a,a)=-\gamma(a,0)+\gamma({\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{t}}_{*}a,a)=0.

∎

For the inverse axioms, we make use of the following lemma.

Lemma 2.8
.

Assume that
\sigma^{*}_{0}\gamma=0
,
\sigma^{*}_{1}\gamma=0
, and
\partial\gamma=0
. Then the following inverse compatibility condition holds:

\gamma(\iota_{*}v,v)=\gamma(v,\iota_{*}v),\quad\forall\,v\in T\mathcal{G}.

Proof.

Since
T\mathcal{G}
is a Lie groupoid, the groupoid multiplication satisfies the identities

{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(v,\iota_{*}v)={\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{t}}_{*}v,\qquad{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(\iota_{*}v,v)={\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}v,\quad\forall\,v\in T_{g}\mathcal{G}.

Evaluate the simplicial cocycle condition
\partial\gamma=0
on the composable triple
(g,\iota(g),g)
and the tangent triple
(v,\iota_{*}v,v)
. This gives

0=\partial\gamma(v,\iota_{*}v,v)=\gamma(\iota_{*}v,v)-\gamma\big({\mathbf{m}}_{*}(v,\iota_{*}v),v\big)+\gamma\big(v,{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(\iota_{*}v,v)\big)-\gamma(v,\iota_{*}v).

By the pullback vanishing assumptions
\sigma_{0}^{*}\gamma=0
and
\sigma_{1}^{*}\gamma=0
, we further obtain

\gamma\big({\mathbf{m}}_{*}(v,\iota_{*}v),v\big)=\gamma({\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{t}}_{*}v,v)=\sigma_{0}^{*}\gamma(v)=0,

\gamma\big(v,{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(\iota_{*}v,v)\big)=\gamma(v,{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}v)=\sigma_{1}^{*}\gamma(v)=0.

Substituting these vanishing relations into the cocycle identity yields

\gamma(\iota_{*}v,v)=\gamma(v,\iota_{*}v),

which completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

Assume
(\sigma_{0})^{*}\gamma=0
,
(\sigma_{1})^{*}\gamma=0
and
\partial\gamma=0
. Then the inverse axioms for

\hat{\iota}
hold.

Proof.

We make use of the inverse compatibility condition in the previous lemma and verify that all inverse axioms hold.

To prove
\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}\circ\hat{\iota}=\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}
, for any
\xi\in T^{*}_{k}\mathcal{G}
and

b\in A_{{\mathbf{t}}(k)}
, we compute

\displaystyle\langle\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\hat{\iota}(\xi)),b\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\mathbf{s}}_{0}(\iota_{0}(\xi)),b\rangle+\gamma((R_{k})_{*}b,(L_{k^{-1}})_{*}\iota_{*}b)+\gamma(0_{k^{-1}},\iota_{*}b)

\displaystyle=\langle{\mathbf{t}}_{0}(\xi),b\rangle-\gamma(b,0_{k})+\gamma(b,0_{k})+\gamma((R_{k})_{*}b,(L_{k^{-1}})_{*}\iota_{*}b)+\gamma(0_{k^{-1}},\iota_{*}b)

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}(\xi),b\rangle+\gamma(b,0_{k})+\gamma((R_{k})_{*}b,(L_{k^{-1}})_{*}\iota_{*}b)+\gamma(0_{k^{-1}},\iota_{*}b).

To simplify the last three terms, we use
\gamma(\iota_{*}v,v)=\gamma(v,\iota_{*}v)
, which gives

\gamma((R_{k})_{*}b,(L_{k^{-1}})_{*}\iota_{*}b)=\gamma((L_{k^{-1}})_{*}\iota_{*}b,(R_{k})_{*}b).

Here we use the fact
\iota_{*}(R_{k})_{*}b=(L_{k^{-1}})_{*}\iota_{*}b.

By Equations (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) in the proof of
Proposition
2.5
, the sum of the last three terms vanishes:

\displaystyle\gamma((R_{k})_{*}b,(L_{k^{-1}})_{*}\iota_{*}b)+\gamma(b,0_{k})+\gamma(0_{k^{-1}},\iota_{*}b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma((L_{k^{-1}})_{*}\iota_{*}b,(R_{k})_{*}b)+\gamma(b,0_{k})+\gamma(0_{k^{-1}},\iota_{*}b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma(\iota_{*}b,b)=0,

where the last equality follows from Condition (
2.6
).
Thus
\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}\circ\hat{\iota}=\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}
. One checks similarly that

\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}\circ\hat{\iota}=\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}
.

It remains to show that
\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\hat{\iota}(\xi),\xi)=\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}(\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\xi))
.
Reversing the computation in the first part, we obtain

\langle\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\hat{\iota}(\xi),\xi),{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}v\rangle=\gamma(\iota_{*}v,v)-\gamma(v,\iota_{*}v)=0.

Hence
\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\hat{\iota}(\xi),\xi)
annihilates
{\mathbf{u}}_{*}(TM)
and therefore lies in

\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}(A^{*})={\mathbf{u}}_{0}(A^{*})
. Since
\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}\colon\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}(A^{*})\to A^{*}
is a bijection and

\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}\bigl(\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\hat{\iota}(\xi),\xi)\bigr)=\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\xi),

we conclude that
\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\hat{\iota}(\xi),\xi)=\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}(\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}(\xi))
.
The identity
\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\xi,\hat{\iota}(\xi))=\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}(\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}(\xi))
follows by an analogous argument.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

If
(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma}
is a Lie groupoid, then by Proposition
2.5
and
2.6
, we have that
(\sigma_{0})^{*}\gamma=0
,
(\sigma_{1})^{*}\gamma=0
and
\partial\gamma=0
. Conversely, if
\gamma
satisfies
(\sigma_{0})^{*}\gamma=0
,
(\sigma_{1})^{*}\gamma=0
and
\partial\gamma=0
, then by Lemma
2.7
and Proposition
2.5
, we have that
(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma}
is a semigroupoid with well-defined multiplication map. Further, by Proposition
2.6
and
2.9
, we have the unit and inverse axioms. Thus

is a groupoid. It is clear that the structure maps
\hat{{\mathbf{s}}},\hat{{\mathbf{t}}},\hat{{\mathbf{m}}},\hat{{\mathbf{u}}},\hat{{\mathbf{\iota}}}
are smooth maps and that

are submersions, making

2.2.
Example: affine action groupoids

We next restrict our attention to the case of Lie groups, i.e.
\mathcal{G}=G\rightrightarrows\{*\}.
In this case, with a specific choice of
\gamma
, we may recover the classical example of the action groupoid
G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, corresponding to the affine (coadjoint) action
[
19
,
4
]
.

Recall that an affine action of this form is determined by a
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
-valued Lie group 1-cocycle
\chi:G\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, satisfying

\chi(gh)=\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{g^{-1}}\chi(h)+\chi(g),\quad\forall g,h\in G,

Then the corresponding affine action is

g\cdot\eta=\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{g^{-1}}\eta+\chi(g)\quad\forall\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

When
\chi=0
, we recover the standard coadjoint action. In this particular case, the action groupoid is identified with the standard cotangent groupoid via left trivialization.

\displaystyle\phi^{L}:G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}

\displaystyle\rightarrow T^{*}G,

\displaystyle(g,\eta)

\displaystyle\mapsto(L_{g^{-1}})^{*}\eta.

To study
\gamma
-deformed cotangent groupoid of this form, we need to specify the form
\gamma=\gamma^{\chi}
. We define
\gamma^{\chi}\in\Omega^{1}(G\times G)
by

(2.13)

\gamma^{\chi}(v_{1},v_{2})=-\langle\chi(g_{2}),(L_{g_{1}^{-1}})_{*}v_{1}\rangle,\quad\forall v_{1}\in T_{g_{1}}G,v_{2}\in T_{g_{2}}G.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
G
be a Lie group and let
\chi:G\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be an affine coadjoint 1-cocycle. Under the left trivialization
T^{*}G\cong G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, the
\gamma^{\chi}
-deformed cotangent groupoid is precisely the affine action groupoid
G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

Proof.

We shall then describe the structure maps of the deformed groupoid, via the trivialization
\phi^{L}
. The source map
\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}\circ\phi^{L}:G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is given by, for any
a\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}\circ\phi^{L}(g,\eta),a\rangle=\langle{\mathbf{s}}_{0}((L_{g^{-1}})^{*}\eta),a\rangle+\gamma^{\chi}(0_{g},\iota_{*}a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{{\mathbf{s}}}_{0}\circ\phi^{L}(g,\eta),a\rangle=\langle\eta,a\rangle.

The target map
\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}\circ\phi^{L}:G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is given by, for any
a\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}\circ\phi^{L}(g,\eta),a\rangle=\langle{\mathbf{t}}_{0}((L_{g^{-1}})^{*}\eta),a\rangle-\gamma^{\chi}(a,0_{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{{\mathbf{t}}}_{0}\circ\phi^{L}(g,\eta)+\chi(g),a\rangle=\langle\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{g^{-1}}\eta+\chi(g),a\rangle.

For two elements
(g_{1},\eta_{1})
and
(g_{2},\eta_{2})
such that
\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}\circ\phi^{L}(g_{1},\eta_{1})=\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}\circ\phi^{L}(g_{2},\eta_{2})
, i.e.
\eta_{1}=\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{g_{2}^{-1}}\eta_{2}+\chi(g_{2})
, and for any
v_{i}\in T_{g_{i}}G
, we compute the multiplication map

\displaystyle\langle\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\phi^{L}(g_{1},\eta_{1}),\phi^{L}(g_{2},\eta_{2})),{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(v_{1},v_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\eta_{1},(L_{g_{1}^{-1}})_{*}v_{1}\rangle+\langle\eta_{2},(L_{g_{2}^{-1}})_{*}v_{2}\rangle+\gamma^{\chi}(v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{g_{2}^{-1}}\eta_{2}+\chi(g_{2}),(L_{g_{1}^{-1}})_{*}v_{1}\rangle+\langle\eta_{2},(L_{g_{2}^{-1}})_{*}v_{2}\rangle-\langle\chi(g_{2}),(L_{g_{1}^{-1}})_{*}v_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\eta_{2},\operatorname{Ad}_{g_{2}^{-1}}(L_{g_{1}^{-1}})_{*}v_{1}+(L_{g_{2}^{-1}})_{*}v_{2}\rangle=\langle(L_{(g_{1}g_{2})^{-1}})^{*}\eta_{2},{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(v_{1},v_{2})\rangle.

Thus we deduce that

\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\phi^{L}(g_{1},\eta_{1}),\phi^{L}(g_{2},\eta_{2}))=\phi^{L}(g_{1}g_{2},\eta_{2}).

Thus, the structure maps here are exactly the structure maps of affine action groupoids. One verifies similarly that the unit and inverse maps also match.

∎

Remark 2.11
.

In Section 3.4, we explain how this picture is recovered conceptually from reduction for central extensions.

2.3.
Gauge transformations

In this subsection, we discuss a more restrictive notion of equivalence among deformed cotangent groupoids, namely gauge equivalence.
It is analogous to the role of gauge transformation in the theory of symplectic groupoids
[
9
]
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma_{1}}
and
(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma_{2}}
be deformed cotangent groupoids with
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G}^{(2)})
. A gauge transformation associated with a 1-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G})
is a map
F\colon(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma_{1}}\to(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma_{2}}
satisfying

(1)

F(\xi_{g})=\xi_{g}+\alpha_{g}
, for every
g\in\mathcal{G}
, and

(2)

F
is an isomorphism of Lie groupoids.

Proposition 2.13
.

The 1-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G})
defines a gauge transformation if and only if
\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}=\partial\alpha
.

Proof.

Denote by
\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}_{1}
the groupoid multiplication on
(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma_{1}}
and by
\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}_{2}
the multiplication on
(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma_{2}}
.

Suppose that
F
is a groupoid isomorphism induced by the 1-form
\alpha
. Then the homomorphism condition reads

F\big(\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}_{1}(\xi_{g},\xi_{h})\big)=\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}_{2}\big(F(\xi_{g}),F(\xi_{h})\big),

for any
\xi_{g}\in T^{*}_{g}\mathcal{G}
and
\xi_{h}\in T^{*}_{h}\mathcal{G}
. For all
v\in T_{g}\mathcal{G}
and
w\in T_{h}\mathcal{G}
with
{\mathbf{s}}_{*}v={\mathbf{t}}_{*}w
,
pair both sides with
{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(v,w)
:

\displaystyle\big\langle F\big(\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}_{1}(\xi_{g},\xi_{h})\big),{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(v,w)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\big\langle\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}_{1}(\xi_{g},\xi_{h})+\alpha_{gh},\,{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(v,w)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\xi_{g},v\rangle+\langle\xi_{h},w\rangle+\gamma_{1}(v,w)+\langle\alpha_{gh},{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(v,w)\rangle,

\displaystyle\big\langle\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}_{2}\big(F(\xi_{g}),F(\xi_{h})\big),{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(v,w)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\big\langle\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}_{2}(\xi_{g}+\alpha_{g},\xi_{h}+\alpha_{h}),\,{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(v,w)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\xi_{g},v\rangle+\langle\xi_{h},w\rangle+\gamma_{2}(v,w)+\langle\alpha_{g},v\rangle+\langle\alpha_{h},w\rangle.

Equating the two expressions and cancelling identical terms in
\xi_{g},\xi_{h}
:

\gamma_{1}(v,w)-\gamma_{2}(v,w)=\langle\alpha_{g},v\rangle+\langle\alpha_{h},w\rangle-\langle\alpha_{gh},{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(v,w)\rangle=\partial\alpha(v,w).

Conversely, assume
\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}=\partial\alpha
. We shall verify that the map
F
yields a Lie groupoid isomorphism.

Since
(\sigma_{1})^{*}(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})=0
, for any
v\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{G})
, we have

\displaystyle(\sigma_{1})^{*}(\partial\alpha)(v)

\displaystyle=\partial\alpha\big(v,{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}v\big)=\alpha({\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}v)+\alpha(v)-\alpha\big({\mathbf{m}}_{*}(v,{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}v)\big)=\alpha({\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}v)=0.

Hence
\alpha|_{{\mathbf{u}}(M)}
annihilates
{\mathbf{u}}_{*}(TM)
and therefore defines a section in
{\mathbf{u}}_{0}(A^{*}).

Next we construct the base map
f\colon A^{*}\to A^{*}
covering
F
, defined by

f:=\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}_{2}\circ F\circ\hat{{\mathbf{u}}}_{1}.

We first check the source projection intertwining condition
\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}_{2}\circ F=f\circ\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}_{1}
. Take arbitrary
\xi\in T^{*}_{g}\mathcal{G}
and set
m={\mathbf{s}}(g)
. We compute pairings with any
a\in A_{m}
:

\big\langle(\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}_{2}\circ F)(\xi),a\big\rangle=\langle{\mathbf{s}}_{0}(\xi),a\rangle+\gamma_{2}(0_{g},\iota_{*}a)+\langle{\mathbf{s}}_{0}(\alpha_{g}),a\rangle,

\big\langle(f\circ\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}_{1})(\xi),a\big\rangle=\langle{\mathbf{s}}_{0}(\xi),a\rangle+\gamma_{1}(0_{g},\iota_{*}a)+\langle{\mathbf{s}}_{0}(\alpha_{{\mathbf{u}}(m)}),a\rangle+\gamma_{2}(0_{{\mathbf{u}}(m)},\iota_{*}a).

By the hypothesis
\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}=\partial\alpha
, evaluate the boundary operator on
(0_{g},\iota_{*}a)
:

\gamma_{1}(0_{g},\iota_{*}a)-\gamma_{2}(0_{g},\iota_{*}a)=\partial\alpha(0_{g},\iota_{*}a)=-\langle{\mathbf{s}}_{0}(\alpha_{{\mathbf{u}}(m)}),a\rangle+\langle{\mathbf{s}}_{0}(\alpha_{g}),a\rangle.

The cocycle condition
\partial\gamma_{2}=0
yields

0=\partial\gamma_{2}(0_{g},0_{{\mathbf{u}}(m)},\iota_{*}a)=\gamma_{2}(0_{{\mathbf{u}}(m)},\iota_{*}a).

Substituting these two identities back into the pairing expressions cancels all extra terms, which proves
f\circ\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}_{1}=\hat{{\mathbf{s}}}_{2}\circ F
.

Similar computations show that the pair
(F,f)
commutes with all remaining Lie groupoid structure maps. Since
F
is manifestly bijective (with inverse
\xi_{g}\mapsto\xi_{g}-\alpha_{g}
),
F
defines a Lie groupoid isomorphism. The proof is complete.
∎

As an application of gauge transformations, we obtain a normal form for the
Lie group case.

Corollary 2.14

(The Lie group case)
.

Let
G
be a Lie group, and let

\gamma\in\Omega^{1}(G\times G)
define a deformed cotangent groupoid

(T^{*}G)_{\gamma}\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Define

\chi:G\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by

\langle\chi(g),v\rangle=\gamma_{(g,e)}\bigl(0_{g},\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}v\bigr)-\gamma_{(e,g)}\bigl(v,0_{g}\bigr),\qquad v\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
\chi
is a group
1
-cocycle for the coadjoint representation.
Moreover,
(T^{*}G)_{\gamma}
is gauge isomorphic to

(T^{*}G)_{\gamma_{\chi}}
, and hence, under left trivialization, to the
affine coadjoint action groupoid determined by
\chi
.

Proof.

Define
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(G)
by

\alpha_{g}\bigl((L_{g})_{*}v\bigr)=-\gamma_{(g,e)}(0_{g},v),\qquad v\in\mathfrak{g}.

Using the cocycle and normalization conditions for
\gamma
, one verifies
that

\partial\alpha=\gamma-\gamma_{\chi}

and that
\chi
satisfies

\chi(gh)=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}^{*}\chi(h)+\chi(g).

Proposition
2.13
therefore shows that the fibre translation

F(\xi_{g})=\xi_{g}+\alpha_{g}
defines a Lie groupoid isomorphism

F:(T^{*}G)_{\gamma}\longrightarrow(T^{*}G)_{\gamma_{\chi}}.

The latter is the affine coadjoint action groupoid by
Proposition
2.10
.
∎

2.4.
Multiplicative forms on deformed groupoids

We now study multiplicative
2
-forms on the
\gamma
-deformed cotangent groupoid
(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma}
. When such a multiplicative
2
-form is additionally symplectic, the deformed cotangent groupoid
(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma}
becomes a symplectic groupoid.

In the classical undeformed setting, the canonical
2
-form
\omega_{\rm can}\in\Omega^{2}(T^{*}\mathcal{G})
is multiplicative on the standard cotangent groupoid. For the
\gamma
-deformed case, we introduce a correction
2
-form
\omega_{B}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{G})
and consider the modified candidate multiplicative
2
-form

(2.14)

\omega_{M}:=\omega_{\rm can}+{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\omega_{B},

where
{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}\colon T^{*}\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
denotes the bundle projection. The form
\omega_{M}
is referred to as the canonical
2
-form with magnetic correction, which naturally appears in the study of cotangent reductions
[
21
]
. In particular, this magnetically modified form is always nondegenerate.

We now derive the precise condition for
\omega_{M}
to be multiplicative with respect to the deformed groupoid structure
(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma}
. Let
\vartheta_{L}\in\Omega^{1}(T^{*}\mathcal{G})
denote the Liouville
1
-form on
T^{*}\mathcal{G}
. For any composable pair
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
and tangent vectors
(V_{1},V_{2})
whose base projections form
w_{1},w_{2}\in T\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
, a direct computation yields

\displaystyle({\rm pr}_{1}^{*}\vartheta_{L}+{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\vartheta_{L}-\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}^{*}\vartheta_{L})(V_{1},V_{2})

\displaystyle=\langle\xi_{1},w_{1}\rangle+\langle\xi_{2},w_{2}\rangle-\langle\hat{{\mathbf{m}}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(w_{1},w_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=-\gamma(w_{1},w_{2})

\displaystyle=-({\rm pr}^{*}_{\mathcal{G}}\gamma)(V_{1},V_{2}).

Since
\omega_{\rm can}=d\vartheta_{L}
, we obtain

\partial\omega_{\rm can}=(d\partial)\vartheta_{L}=-d({\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\gamma)=-{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}(d\gamma),

where
\partial
denotes the simplicial groupoid differential operator associated to
(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma}
. Let
{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}\colon(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
be the bundle projection, which induces a projection
q^{(2)}:(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
. As the projection
{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}\colon(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
is a Lie groupoid morphism, the differential operators commute:
\partial\circ{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}={(q^{(2)})}^{*}\circ\partial
. Consequently, the condition
\partial\omega_{M}=0
is equivalent to

\partial({\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\omega_{B})={(q^{(2)})}^{*}\partial\omega_{B}={(q^{(2)})}^{*}d\gamma.

Since
q^{(2)}
is a surjective submersion, we deduce the following characterization:

Proposition 2.15
.

The magnetically corrected canonical
2
-form
\omega_{M}
is multiplicative on
(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma}
if and only if

(2.15)

\partial\omega_{B}=d\gamma.

The above criterion immediately yields a sufficient condition for the deformed cotangent groupoid to be symplectic:

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma}
be a deformed cotangent groupoid. If
d\omega_{B}=0
and
\partial\omega_{B}=d\gamma
, then the pair
\big((T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma},\omega_{\rm can}+{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\omega_{B}\big)
forms a symplectic groupoid.

Whenever these compatibility conditions hold on a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, we call the symplectic groupoid
\big((T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma},\omega_{\rm can}+{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\omega_{B}\big)
a
magnetic cotangent groupoid
associated to the pair
(\gamma,\omega_{B})
.

Remark 2.17
.

The compatibility equations for
(\gamma,\omega_{B})
admit a natural interpretation within the deformation theory of symplectic groupoids. In the de Rham deformation model developed by Cárdenas–Mestre–Struchiner
[
10
]
, these equations are of the same form as the cocycle equations arising from the combined Bott–Shulman and de Rham differentials. Thus the family above may be viewed as an explicit realization of a distinguished class of deformation cocycles. We emphasize that this construction does not exhaust all deformation classes of the standard cotangent symplectic groupoid
(T^{*}\mathcal{G},\omega_{\rm can})
.

We next investigate the gauge transformation invariance property of magnetic forms. Given any
1
-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G})
, recall the gauge automorphism
F(\xi_{g})=\xi_{g}+\alpha_{g}
on
T^{*}\mathcal{G}
. By definition,
{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}={\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}\circ F
, which implies
F^{*}{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\omega_{B}={\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\omega_{B}
. Furthermore, a direct verification shows that the Liouville form satisfies
F^{*}\vartheta_{L}=\vartheta_{L}+{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\alpha
. Combining these identities yields the pullback formula for the magnetic symplectic form:

(2.16)

F^{*}(\omega_{\rm can}+{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\omega_{B})=\omega_{\rm can}+{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}(\omega_{B}+d\alpha).

With this formula, we upgrade the notion of gauge transformations to the setting of magnetic cotangent groupoids equipped with multiplicative symplectic forms. Consider two deformed cotangent groupoids
(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma_{1}}
and
(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma_{2}}
, endowed with magnetic multiplicative
2
-forms
\omega_{\rm can}+{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\omega_{B_{1}}
and
\omega_{\rm can}+{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\omega_{B_{2}}
respectively. A gauge transformation
F\colon(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma_{1}}\to(T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma_{2}}
is called a
gauge transformation of magnetic cotangent groupoids
if it satisfies the symplectic compatibility condition

F^{*}(\omega_{\rm can}+{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\omega_{B_{2}})=\omega_{\rm can}+{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\omega_{B_{1}}.

As in the undeformed case, such gauge transformations correspond precisely to cohomological coboundary relations. Combining the above pullback formula with Proposition
2.13
, we obtain the following characterization:

Proposition 2.18
.

A
1
-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G})
induces a gauge transformation between the magnetic cotangent groupoids

\big((T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma_{1}},\omega_{\rm can}+{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\omega_{B_{1}}\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\big((T^{*}\mathcal{G})_{\gamma_{2}},\omega_{\rm can}+{\rm pr}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\omega_{B_{2}}\big)

if and only if

(2.17)

\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}=\partial\alpha,\quad\text{and}\quad\omega_{B_{1}}-\omega_{B_{2}}=d\alpha.

3.
Central extensions and reduced symplectic groupoids

In this section we construct deformed cotangent groupoids from central
extensions and symplectic reduction, two classical techniques in symplectic
and Poisson geometry. This viewpoint also explains the geometric origin of
the forms
\gamma
and
\omega_{B}
introduced in Section

2
: they arise from the groupoid coboundary and the
curvature of a connection form, respectively.

3.1.
The reduction lemma

Let
(\mathcal{H},\omega)\rightrightarrows N
be a symplectic groupoid
equipped with a Hamiltonian
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action. We record a reduction
criterion ensuring that the Marsden–Weinstein quotient of a regular,
possibly nonzero, level set inherits a natural symplectic groupoid structure.
The key point is that the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action must be compatible with the
groupoid structure, and the chosen level set must itself be a Lie subgroupoid. Moreover, when these hypotheses hold uniformly for all levels in a regular
interval, the reduced symplectic groupoids form a deformation family.

Reduction of symplectic groupoids at the zero level is classical; see

[
23
,
27
]
. Other reduction methods for constructing symplectic groupoids appear in
the study of quotient Poisson and quasi-Poisson manifolds; see, for instance,

[
3
,
2
]
. The lemma below is the variant needed later for
the nonzero-level reductions arising from
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extensions.

Let
\mathcal{H}^{(2)}
be the set of composable pairs, and let

s,t:\mathcal{H}\to N,\qquad m:\mathcal{H}^{(2)}\to\mathcal{H},\qquad u:N\to\mathcal{H},\qquad\iota:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}

be the source, target, multiplication, unit, and inverse maps.

Definition 3.1
.

A right
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action
\phi:\mathcal{H}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathcal{H}
,

(h,r)\mapsto\phi_{r}(h)
, is called
multiplicative
on a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{H}
if

•

s\circ\phi_{r}=s
and
t\circ\phi_{r}=t
for all
r\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
;

•

for all
(h_{1},h_{2})\in\mathcal{H}^{(2)}
and
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
,

\phi_{r_{1}r_{2}}\big(m(h_{1},h_{2})\big)=m\big(\phi_{r_{1}}(h_{1}),\phi_{r_{2}}(h_{2})\big);

•

\phi_{r^{-1}}\circ\iota=\iota\circ\phi_{r}
for all
r\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

Lemma 3.2

(Reduction Lemma)
.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\omega)\rightrightarrows N
be a symplectic groupoid equipped with an

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action
\phi:\mathcal{H}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathcal{H}
such that:

(1)

\phi
is Hamiltonian with the moment map
\mu:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
, i.e. if
X
denotes
the fundamental vector field of
1\in\mathfrak{s}^{1}\simeq\mathbb{R}
, then

\omega^{\flat}(X)=-d\mu
;

(2)

c\in\mathbb{R}
is a regular value of
\mu
and the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action on
\mu^{-1}(c)

is free and proper;

(3)

\phi
is multiplicative;

(4)

\mathcal{K}:=\mu^{-1}(c)
is a Lie subgroupoid of
\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows N
over some submanifold
N_{c}
of
N
.

Then the reduced symplectic manifold
(\Gamma_{c}:=\mathcal{K}/\mathbb{S}^{1},\omega_{\Gamma})
inherits a symplectic groupoid structure over
N_{c}
. Moreover, the inclusion
\tilde{j}:\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}
and the quotient map
\tilde{p}:\mathcal{K}\to\Gamma_{c}
are Lie groupoid morphisms.

Remark 3.3
.

We may replace the assumption that
\mu^{-1}(c)
is a Lie subgroupoid by the condition:
\mu:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Lie groupoid morphism, where
\mathbb{R}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}
is the trivial groupoid. This is a stronger, yet more conceptual condition. A result of Meinrenken
[
22
]
states that if
F:H_{1}\rightarrow H_{2}
is a Lie groupoid morphism,
H_{2}^{\prime}
is a Lie subgroupoid of
H_{2}
, and
F
is transverse to
H_{2}^{\prime}
, then
F^{-1}(H_{2}^{\prime})
is a Lie subgroupoid of
H_{1}
.

In our case:
H_{1}=\mathcal{H}
,
H_{2}=\mathbb{R}
,
H_{2}^{\prime}=\{c\}
,
H_{1}^{\prime}=\mu^{-1}(c)
. This is different from classical theory, which view
\mathbb{R}
as a Lie group
\mathbb{R}\rightrightarrows\{*\}
, and one does reduction at the subgroupoid
\{0\}\subset\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Since
c
is a regular value of
\mu
and the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action on

\mathcal{K}=\mu^{-1}(c)
is free and proper, the quotient
\Gamma_{c}:=\mathcal{K}/\mathbb{S}^{1}

is a smooth manifold and we have

\tilde{p}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma}=\tilde{j}^{*}\omega.

Next we see that the groupoid structure on
\mathcal{K}
descends to the quotient
\Gamma_{c}
.
By assumption,
\mathcal{K}\rightrightarrows N_{c}
is a Lie subgroupoid.
Define structure maps on
\Gamma_{c}
by

\hat{s}\circ\tilde{p}=s|_{\mathcal{K}},\qquad\hat{t}\circ\tilde{p}=t|_{\mathcal{K}},\qquad\hat{u}=\tilde{p}\circ u|_{N_{c}},\qquad\hat{\iota}\circ\tilde{p}=\tilde{p}\circ\iota|_{\mathcal{K}}.

These are well-defined because
s\circ\phi_{r}=s
and
t\circ\phi_{r}=t
, and
\phi_{r^{-1}}\circ\iota=\iota\circ\phi_{r}
.

For multiplication, define
\hat{m}:\Gamma_{c}^{(2)}\to\Gamma_{c}
by

\hat{m}(\tilde{p}(k_{1}),\tilde{p}(k_{2})):=\tilde{p}\big(m(k_{1},k_{2})\big),\qquad(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathcal{K}^{(2)}.

This is well-defined: if
k_{1}^{\prime}=\phi_{r_{1}}k_{1}
and
k_{2}^{\prime}=\phi_{r_{2}}k_{2}
, then by multiplicativity,

m(k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime})=m(\phi_{r_{1}}k_{1},\phi_{r_{2}}k_{2})=\phi_{r_{1}r_{2}}(m(k_{1},k_{2})),

so
\tilde{p}(m(k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime}))=\tilde{p}(m(k_{1},k_{2}))
. It is routine to check the groupoid axioms.
The inclusion
\tilde{j}
and projection
\tilde{p}
are Lie groupoid morphisms.

Next we see that the reduced symplectic form on
\Gamma_{c}
is multiplicative.
Let
\partial_{\mathcal{G}}:\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{G})\to\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(2)})
be the groupoid
coboundary operator
\partial_{\mathcal{G}}\eta:=pr_{1}^{*}\eta+pr_{2}^{*}\eta-m^{*}\eta
.
Since
(\mathcal{H},\omega)
is symplectic groupoid,
\partial_{\mathcal{H}}\omega=0
.
As
\tilde{j}:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{H}
is a groupoid morphism, we have

\partial_{\mathcal{K}}(\tilde{j}^{*}\omega)=(\tilde{j},\tilde{j})^{*}(\partial_{\mathcal{H}}\omega)=0
.
Using
\tilde{p}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma}=\tilde{j}^{*}\omega
and that
\tilde{p}
is a groupoid morphism,

(\tilde{p},\tilde{p})^{*}(\partial_{\Gamma_{c}}\omega_{\Gamma})=\partial_{\mathcal{K}}(\tilde{p}^{*}\omega_{\Gamma})=\partial_{\mathcal{K}}(\tilde{j}^{*}\omega)=0.

Finally, since
(\tilde{p},\tilde{p}):\mathcal{K}^{(2)}\to\Gamma_{c}^{(2)}
is a surjective submersion,
it follows that
\partial_{\Gamma_{c}}\omega_{\Gamma}=0
. Hence
\omega_{\Gamma}
is multiplicative.

Therefore
(\Gamma_{c},\omega_{\Gamma})\rightrightarrows N_{c}
is a symplectic groupoid.
∎

3.2.
Central extensions and reduction

Let
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
and
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\rightrightarrows M
be Lie groupoids with Lie algebroids
A
and
\tilde{A}
, respectively. Throughout the remainder of this section, assume that
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
is a
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groupoids
over
\mathcal{G}
.
That is, there is a short exact sequence Lie groupoids over
{\rm id}_{M}
,

(3.1)

M\times S^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{G},

where
i
is injective and
p
is surjective.

Let
\Phi\colon\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
be the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action induced by the inclusion

i:M\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
.
Centrality means that the induced
\mathbb{S}^{1}
action from left multiplication on
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
coincides with the one induced from right multiplication, i.e.

(3.2)

\Phi_{r}(g)=i(\tilde{\mathbf{t}}(g),r)\cdot g=g\cdot i(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}(g),r),\qquad r\in\mathbb{S}^{1},\ g\in\tilde{\mathcal{G}}.

Note that
p:\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathcal{G}
is also a principal
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-bundle. In this section, we discuss how the natural
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action on the cotangent groupoid
T^{*}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
satisfies the criterion in the reduction lemma, and obtain a reduced symplectic groupoid
\Gamma:=\Gamma_{1}
at level 1 of the moment map.

3.2.1.
General setup

Differentiating (
3.1
) yields an
\mathbb{R}
-central extension of
Lie algebroids

(3.3)

0\longrightarrow M\times\mathbb{R}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\tilde{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A\longrightarrow 0.

Denote by

\varepsilon=i_{*}(1)\in\Gamma(\tilde{A})
the central section, given by

\varepsilon_{m}=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\,\Phi_{e^{t}}(\tilde{\mathbf{u}}(m))\in\ker(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}_{*})_{\tilde{\mathbf{u}}(m)}=\tilde{A}_{m}.

Moreover, since
i
is a groupoid morphism, we have
\tilde{\rho}(\varepsilon)=\tilde{\mathbf{t}}_{*}(\varepsilon)=0
.
Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathfrak{X}(\tilde{\mathcal{G}})
be the infinitesimal action given by

\psi(1)_{g}=(L_{g})_{*}\varepsilon_{\tilde{\mathbf{s}}(g)}=(R_{g})_{*}\varepsilon_{\tilde{\mathbf{t}}(g)},\quad g\in\tilde{\mathcal{G}}.

Recall that the induced action of
\Phi
on the cotangent bundle is Hamiltonian.
To be more precise, denote by
\omega_{\rm can}=d\vartheta_{L}
the canonical form on
T^{*}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
, where
\vartheta_{L}
is the Liouville form.
Let
\bar{\Phi}
be the cotangent lift of
\Phi
to
T^{*}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
, namely

\bar{\Phi}_{r}(\xi):=(\Phi_{r^{-1}})^{*}\xi,\qquad\xi\in T^{*}_{g}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}.

Let
\bar{\psi}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathfrak{X}(T^{*}\tilde{\mathcal{G}})
be the infinitesimal action.
Then the
moment map

\mu:T^{*}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by

\mu(\xi):=\langle\vartheta_{L},\bar{\psi}(1)\rangle_{\xi}=\langle\xi,\psi(1)\rangle_{g},\quad\xi\in T^{*}_{g}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}.

The moment map condition is

(3.4)

\omega_{\rm can}^{\flat}(\bar{\psi}(1))=-d\mu,

where
\omega_{\rm can}^{\flat}\colon T(T^{*}\tilde{\mathcal{G}})\to T^{*}(T^{*}\tilde{\mathcal{G}})
is the bundle map determined by
\omega_{\rm can}.

Then
(T^{*}\tilde{\mathcal{G}},\omega_{\rm can},\bar{\Phi},\mu)
is a Hamiltonian
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-space.

3.2.2.
Choice of principal connection
\theta

We need another technical input: the existence of principal connections normalized by units.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists a principal connection
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(\tilde{\mathcal{G}})
such that
\tilde{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\theta=0
.

Proof.

Take any principal connection
\theta_{0}\in\Omega^{1}(\tilde{\mathcal{G}})
for the principal bundle
p:\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
. Consider
\tilde{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\theta_{0}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.

Since
{\mathbf{u}}(M)\subset\mathcal{G}
is an embedded submanifold, there is a smooth extension
\lambda\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G})
so that
{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\lambda=\tilde{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\theta_{0}
.

Now we define
\theta:=\theta_{0}-p^{*}\lambda
. It is clear that
\theta
is
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-invariant with
\tilde{\mathbf{u}}^{*}\theta=0
. Further, we have that

\langle\theta,\psi(1)\rangle=1,

where
\psi(1)\in\mathfrak{X}(\tilde{\mathcal{G}})
is the fundamental vector field for the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

One can also work with unnormalized connections. In that case, the description of the unit map in Equation (
2.4
) would involve additional terms.

From now on, we assume the connection
\theta
satisfies the condition in Lemma
3.4
. Restricting
\theta
to the units gives a bundle map

\theta^{\flat}:\tilde{A}\longrightarrow M\times\mathbb{R},\qquad\theta_{m}(v):=(m,\langle v,\theta_{\tilde{u}(m)}\rangle),\quad\text{for\penalty\ all\penalty\ }v\in{\tilde{A}}_{m}.

Then we obtain a splitting of the sequence (
3.3
) and thus an isomorphism of vector bundles:

(3.5)

\phi\colon\tilde{A}\to A\oplus(M\times\mathbb{R}):=A\times\mathbb{R}.

Note that
\phi(v)=(p_{*}(v),\theta^{\flat}(v))
, for all
v\in\Gamma(\tilde{A})
. Then we define an affine embedding

j:A^{*}\rightarrow\tilde{A}^{*},

given by
j(\eta)=(\eta,1)
.

3.2.3.
Applying the reduction lemma

In this subsection, we shall use the reduction lemma to construct a symplectic groupoid
(\Gamma,\omega_{\Gamma})\rightrightarrows A^{*}
determined by the Hamiltonian
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-space

(T^{*}\tilde{\mathcal{G}},\omega_{\rm can},\bar{\Phi},\mu)
. We know that
\mu^{-1}(1)
is a regular level set, since
\mu
is defined by pairing with a nowhere vanishing (fundamental) vector field. Further, the lifted
\mathbb{S}^{1}

\mu^{-1}(1)
is free and proper, since the original action on
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
is principal. It remains to verify the subgroupoid and multiplicativity criteria.

Define the
\mathbb{R}
-projection map

{\rm pr}_{\mathbb{R}}\colon\tilde{A}^{*}\to\mathbb{R},\quad{\rm pr}_{\mathbb{R}}(\alpha):=\langle\alpha,\varepsilon\rangle_{m},\quad\text{for\penalty\ all\penalty\ }\alpha\in\tilde{A}^{*}_{m}.

We need the following observation.

Lemma 3.6
.

There is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{A}^{*}.}

For any

Now set

Corollary 3.7
.

The submanifold

T^{*}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}

Proof.

By Lemma

, we have that

\bar{\mathbf{s}}
and

Remark 3.8
.

The Lie subgroupoid condition can also be checked using Meinrenken’s criterion
[
22
]
.

Next we check that the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action is multiplicative.

Lemma 3.9
.

For any
\xi\in T^{*}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
and

\bar{\mathbf{t}}(\bar{\Phi}_{r}\xi)=\bar{\mathbf{t}}(\xi),\qquad\bar{\mathbf{s}}(\bar{\Phi}_{r}\xi)=\bar{\mathbf{s}}(\xi).

For any

Lemma 3.11
.

The canonical form with magnetic term

The reduced groupoid

Geometrically nontrivial examples

3.4.1.
Examples arising from Lie group central extensions

In this section, we study examples arising from Lie group extensions. Assume that there is an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groups

(3.7)

1\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow\tilde{G}\rightarrow G\rightarrow 1.

By taking derivatives, we obtain

(3.8)

0\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow 0.

We may fix a splitting of vector spaces
S:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
, so that
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=S(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathbb{R}\epsilon
, where
\epsilon
denotes the central generator. For simplicity, we may denote elements of
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
by
(v,r)

v\in\mathfrak{g}
and
r\in\mathbb{R}

The associated 1-cocycle
\chi:G\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is given by, for any

\langle\chi(g),v\rangle:=pr_{\mathbb{R}}(Ad_{{\tilde{g}}^{-1}}(v,0)),\quad\forall\tilde{g}\in\tilde{G}\text{ such that }p(\tilde{g})=g.

It follows from centrality that

By Proposition

\langle\theta,(L_{\tilde{g}})_{*}(v,r)\rangle:=r,\quad\forall(v,r)\in\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\text{ and }\tilde{g}\in\tilde{G}.

Using
\partial\theta=(pr_{1})^{*}\theta+(pr_{2})^{*}\theta-\tilde{\mathbf{m}}^{*}\theta

\tilde{\mathbf{m}}_{*}((L_{\tilde{g}_{1}})_{*}(v_{1},r_{1}),(L_{\tilde{g}_{2}})_{*}(v_{2},r_{2}))=(L_{\tilde{g}_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}})_{*}(Ad_{{\tilde{g}_{2}}^{-1}}(v_{1},r_{1})+(v_{2},r_{2}))

Here we have used that
pr_{\mathbb{R}}(Ad_{{\tilde{g}_{2}}^{-1}}(v_{1},r_{1}))=r_{1}
.

Now it follows from Equation (
2.13
) that

\partial\theta=\tilde{p}^{*}\gamma^{\chi}.

Thus we have found a connection
\theta
, which yields the reduced groupoid as the
\gamma^{\chi}
-deformed cotangent groupoid. Further, one checks that

d\theta=p^{*}\omega_{B}.

We have the following result, which is a reformulation of
[
4
]
.

Theorem 3.19
.

Assume there is an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groups
\tilde{G}\rightarrow G
. Consider the corresponding
(r\gamma^{\chi})
-deformed cotangent groupoid

, with corresponding symplectic form
\omega_{r}=\omega_{can}+r(pr_{G})^{*}\omega_{B}

Then for any

is a symplectic groupoid. At
r=0
, we get the coadjoint action groupoid; and at
r=1
, we get the affine action groupoid.

Although these objects sit in a smooth
r
-family of symplectic groupoids, the endpoint

need not be Morita equivalent to

, even as Lie groupoids. We shall see an explicit example next.

For any affine coadjoint 1-cocycle
\chi
, we denote by
G\times_{\chi}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}^{*}
the affine action groupoid corresponding to
\chi
. In particular, when
\chi=0
, we recover the coadjoint action groupoid. We have the following condition for non-Morita equivalence.

Lemma 3.20
.

If no stabilizer of the affine action corresponding to
\chi
is isomorphic to
G
, then the groupoids
G\times_{0}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
G\times_{\chi}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
are not Morita equivalent.

Proof.

By
[
5
]
, Morita-equivalent Lie groupoids present equivalent differentiable stacks, and corresponding objects have isomorphic isotropy groups. In our case, for the coadjoint action,
0\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is a fixed point for the action. Therefore, the isotropy at
0
is the Lie group
G
.

In order for
G\times_{0}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
G\times_{\chi}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
to be Morita equivalent, one must have that
G
is also the isotropy group over some point in the groupoid
G\times_{\chi}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Equivalently, if no stabilizer of the affine action is isomorphic to
G
, then the groupoids
G\times_{0}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
G\times_{\chi}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
are not Morita equivalent.
∎

The Kac-Moody example

Consider the Kac-Moody extension

4.
Affine Poisson geometry and prequantization

It is well-known that each symplectic groupoid determines a unique Poisson structure on the base manifold, so that the groupoid target map is Poisson. In this section, we discuss applications and interpretations of the deformed cotangent symplectic groupoid in Poisson geometry.

For simplicity, we shall work with examples arising from central extensions of Lie groupoids

(4.1)

M\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{G},

as discussed in Section
3.2
. We will see that in this case, the Poisson structure on the base
A^{*}
determined by the reduced symplectic groupoid is an affine Poisson structure.

Below we briefly recall affine Poisson structures on
A^{*}
. By differentiating the groupoid central extension above, we obtain an
\mathbb{R}
-central extension of
Lie algebroids

(4.2)

0\longrightarrow M\times\mathbb{R}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\tilde{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A\longrightarrow 0.

We may choose a splitting, as in Equation (
3.5
), so that
\tilde{A}=A\times\mathbb{R}
. By centrality, for
X,Y\in\Gamma(A)
, the Lie bracket on
\tilde{A}
is determined by

(4.3)

[(X,0),(Y,0)]_{\tilde{A}}=([X,Y]_{A},\lambda(X,Y)),

for some Lie algebroid 2-cocycle
\lambda\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}A^{*})
. The cocycle
\lambda
depends on the choice of splitting, but changing the
splitting changes
\lambda
by a Lie algebroid coboundary.

Denote by
q:A^{*}\to M
the bundle projection.
For
X\in\Gamma(A)
, let
l_{X}\in C^{\infty}(A^{*})
be the corresponding fiberwise linear
function. The cocycle
\lambda
defines an
affine Poisson structure

\pi_{\lambda}
on
A^{*}
, uniquely determined by

(4.4)

\displaystyle\{l_{X},l_{Y}\}_{\pi_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=l_{[X,Y]_{A}}+q^{*}\lambda(X,Y),

\displaystyle\{l_{X},q^{*}f\}_{\pi_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=q^{*}(\rho(X)f),

\displaystyle\{q^{*}f,q^{*}g\}_{\pi_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=0,

for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(A)
and
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
.
When
\lambda=0
, this reduces to the usual Lie–Poisson structure on
A^{*}
.

In order to show that the reduced symplectic groupoid determines the affine Poisson structure above, we first embed the affine Poisson structure into the Lie-Poisson manifold, as a Poisson submanifold. Let
\pi_{\rm Lie}
denote the Lie–Poisson structure on
\tilde{A}^{*}
.
Consider the affine embedding

j:A^{*}\hookrightarrow\tilde{A}^{*},\qquad a\longmapsto(a,1).

One checks directly that the map

j:(A^{*},\pi_{\lambda})\longrightarrow(\tilde{A}^{*},\pi_{Lie})

is a Poisson embedding.

Next, we employ the following observation regarding reduced symplectic groupoids: under reasonable assumptions, the Poisson structure determined by the reduced symplectic groupoid coincides with the Poisson structure inheritted by the reduced base as a Poisson submanifold. For this, we recall some terminology: let
(\Gamma,\omega_{\Gamma})\rightrightarrows N_{c}
be the reduced symplectic groupoid from
(\mathcal{H},\omega)
, then we obtain induced Poisson structures
\pi_{c}
on
N_{c}
, and
\pi_{N}
on
N
, respectively.

Recall from Poisson geometry that, for any Poisson manifold
(M,\Pi)
, let
\Pi^{\sharp}:T^{*}M\rightarrow TM
be the induced bundle map. Then a submanifold
S\subset M
is called a Poisson submanifold if

(4.5)

\Pi^{\sharp}(T^{*}M|_{S})\subset TS.

In this case,
S
inherits a unique Poisson structure such that the inclusion is a Poisson map. We postpone the proof of the following lemma to the end of this section.

Lemma 4.1

(Poisson-naturality)
.

Let
(H,\omega)\rightrightarrows(N,\pi_{N})
be a symplectic groupoid,
and suppose the reduction lemma produces a reduced symplectic groupoid

(\Gamma,\omega_{\Gamma})\rightrightarrows N_{c}.

Let
\pi_{c}^{\mathrm{red}}
be the Poisson structure on
N_{c}
induced by

(\Gamma,\omega_{\Gamma})
. If
N_{c}\subset N
is a Poisson submanifold,
then
\pi_{c}^{\mathrm{red}}
coincides with the Poisson structure inherited
from
(N,\pi_{N})
.

We now apply the above result to the extension-reduction example. Since
j(A^{*})\subset\tilde{A}^{*}
is a Poisson submanifold, we have by Lemma
4.1
that the affine Poisson structure
\pi_{\lambda}
coincides with the induced Poisson structure from
\Gamma
. That is,
\hat{{\mathbf{t}}}:(\Gamma,\pi_{\Gamma})\rightarrow(A^{*},\pi_{\lambda})
is a Poisson map.

In summary, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 4.2

\mathbb{S}^{1}
-central extension of Lie groupoids
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
over
\mathcal{G}

Appendix A
Symplectic groupoids and cotangent groupoids

We follow
[
20
]
for conventions. A groupoid is a small category such that each morphism has an inverse. More concretely, one has the following

Definition A.1
.

A
groupoid
consists of a set
\mathcal{G}
(the arrows) and a set
M
(the objects), equipped with the following structure maps,

•

the
source
and the
target
maps
{\mathbf{s}},{\mathbf{t}}:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow M,

•

the
composition
map

{\mathbf{m}}:\mathcal{G}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathcal{G},

defined on the set
\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
of composable arrows:

\mathcal{G}^{(2)}=\{(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}:{\mathbf{s}}(g)={\mathbf{t}}(h)\}.

•

the
unit
map

{\mathbf{u}}:M\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
.

•

the
inverse
map

{\mathbf{\iota}}:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
.

We often use
gh
to denote
{\mathbf{m}}(g,h)
. The structure maps satisfy:

•

law of composition: if
g,h\in\mathcal{G}
are composable, then

(A.1)

\mathbf{t}(gh)=\mathbf{t}(g),\quad\mathbf{s}(gh)=\mathbf{s}(h).

•

law of associativity: if
g,h,k\in\mathcal{G}
are composable, then

(A.2)

g(hk)=(gh)k.

•

law of units:

\forall g\in\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathbf{s}}\circ\iota={\mathbf{t}},\quad\iota(g)g={\mathbf{u}}({\mathbf{s}}(g)),

We call
\mathcal{G}
a groupoid over
M.

Definition A.2
.

A
Lie groupoid
is a groupoid
(\mathcal{G},M)
such that
\mathcal{G}
,
M
are smooth manifolds, the structure maps
{\mathbf{s}},{\mathbf{t}}
are smooth submersions, and
{\mathbf{m}},{\mathbf{u}},\iota
are smooth maps.

Define for all
p\geq 0
,

\mathcal{G}^{(p)}:=\{(g_{1},\cdots,g_{p})\in\underbrace{\mathcal{G}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{G}}_{p\penalty\ \text{times}}|\mathbf{s}(g_{i})=\mathbf{t}(g_{i+1}),\quad i=1,\cdots,p-1\},

i.e.,
\mathcal{G}^{(p)}
is the manifold of composable sequences of
p
arrows. In particular,
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}=M
and
\mathcal{G}^{(1)}=\mathcal{G}.

Given any Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, let
{\rm pr}_{1},{\rm pr}_{2}:\mathcal{G}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
be the projections into the first and the second component, respectively.

Definition A.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Lie groupoid and
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{G})
be a symplectic form.
We say
(\mathcal{G},\omega)
is a
symplectic groupoid
if
\omega
is multiplicative, i.e.,

{\rm pr}_{1}^{*}\omega+{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\omega-\mathbf{m}^{*}\omega=0.

Associated to every Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
, there are de Rham cohomology groups defined as follows.

For each
p\geq 1
and
0\leq i\leq p
, the face maps
\partial^{p}_{i}:\mathcal{G}^{(p)}\to\mathcal{G}^{(p-1)}
are defined piecewise:

\partial^{p}_{i}(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{p})=\begin{cases}(g_{2},g_{3},\dots,g_{p}),&i=0,\\
(g_{1},\dots,g_{i}g_{i+1},\dots,g_{p}),&1\leq i\leq p-1,\\
(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{p-1}),&i=p.\end{cases}

In particular,
\partial^{1}_{0}=\mathbf{s}
and
\partial^{1}_{1}=\mathbf{t}.
In fact,
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is a simplicial manifold.

For any
q
, we define the differential

\partial:=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}(\partial_{i}^{n})^{*}\colon\Omega^{q}(\mathcal{G}^{(n-1)})\rightarrow\Omega^{q}(\mathcal{G}^{(n)}).

We have that
\partial\circ\partial=0
, and therefore a complex
(\Omega^{q}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}),\partial)
, called the Čech complex of
\mathcal{G}
associated to
\Omega^{q}
. We thus obtain the Čech cohomology group
{\rm H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G},\Omega^{q})
.

We may also define de Rham cohomology of a Lie groupoid. The de Rham total complex is defined to be
C_{\rm dR}^{n}(\mathcal{G}):=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}\Omega^{q}(\mathcal{G}^{(p)})
. The total differential
\delta:C_{\rm dR}^{n}(\mathcal{G})\rightarrow C_{\rm dR}^{n+1}(\mathcal{G})
is defined by
\delta(\omega)=\partial\omega+(-1)^{p}d\omega
. We have that
\delta\circ\delta=0
. We thus obtain the de Rham cohomology of
\mathcal{G}
, denoted
{\rm H}^{\bullet}_{\rm dR}(\mathcal{G})
. Both Čech cohomology and de Rham cohomology of Lie groupoids are invariant under Morita equivalence.

Next we recall the notion of cotangent groupoids. Let
{\mathcal{G}}\rightrightarrows M
be a Lie groupoid with structure maps

{\mathbf{s}},{\mathbf{t}}:{\mathcal{G}}\to M,\qquad{\mathbf{m}}:{\mathcal{G}}^{(2)}\to{\mathcal{G}},\qquad{\mathbf{u}}:M\to{\mathcal{G}},\qquad{\mathbf{\iota}}:{\mathcal{G}}\to{\mathcal{G}}.

Let
A=\operatorname{ker}({\mathbf{s}}_{*})|_{M}
be its Lie algebroid with anchor

\rho:={\mathbf{t}}_{*}:A\to TM.

Theorem A.4

(Coste–Dazord–Weinstein,
[
12
]
)
.

There exists a Lie groupoid structure

T^{*}{\mathcal{G}}\rightrightarrows A^{*}

with structure maps
{\mathbf{s}}_{0},{\mathbf{t}}_{0},{\mathbf{m}}_{0},{\mathbf{u}}_{0},{\mathbf{\iota}}_{0}
defined as follows.

(1)

Source map.

For
\xi\in T^{*}_{g}{\mathcal{G}}
,

{\mathbf{s}}_{0}(\xi)\in A^{*}_{{\mathbf{s}}(g)}
is uniquely determined by

(A.5)

\langle{\mathbf{s}}_{0}(\xi),a\rangle=\langle\xi,(L_{g})_{*}(a-{\mathbf{u}}_{*}\rho(a))\rangle,\quad a\in A_{{\mathbf{s}}(g)}.

(2)

Target map.

For
\xi\in T^{*}_{g}{\mathcal{G}}
,

{\mathbf{t}}_{0}(\xi)\in A^{*}_{{\mathbf{t}}(g)}
is uniquely determined by

(A.6)

\langle{\mathbf{t}}_{0}(\xi),b\rangle=\langle\xi,(R_{g})_{*}b\rangle,\quad b\in A_{{\mathbf{t}}(g)}.

(3)

Multiplication.

If
(g,h)\in{\mathcal{G}}^{(2)}
and

\xi\in T^{*}_{g}{\mathcal{G}}
,

\eta\in T^{*}_{h}{\mathcal{G}}
satisfy

{\mathbf{s}}_{0}(\xi)={\mathbf{t}}_{0}(\eta)
,
then
{\mathbf{m}}_{0}(\xi,\eta)\in T^{*}_{{\mathbf{m}}(g,h)}{\mathcal{G}}
is uniquely determined by

(A.7)

\langle{\mathbf{m}}_{0}(\xi,\eta),{\mathbf{m}}_{*}(v,w)\rangle=\langle\xi,v\rangle+\langle\eta,w\rangle,

for all
(v,w)\in T_{g}{\mathcal{G}}\times T_{h}{\mathcal{G}}

such that

.

(4)

Unit map.

For
\alpha\in A_{m}^{*}
,

{\mathbf{u}}_{0}(\alpha)\in T^{*}_{{\mathbf{u}}(m)}{\mathcal{G}}
is defined by

(A.8)

\langle{\mathbf{u}}_{0}(\alpha),v\rangle=\langle\alpha,v-{\mathbf{u}}_{*}{\mathbf{s}}_{*}v\rangle,\quad v\in T_{{\mathbf{u}}(m)}{\mathcal{G}}.

(5)

Inverse map.
For
\xi\in T^{*}_{g}{\mathcal{G}}
, we have that

{\mathbf{\iota}}_{0}(\xi)=-{\mathbf{\iota}}^{*}\xi.

Moreover,

(T^{*}{\mathcal{G}},\omega_{can})\rightrightarrows(A^{*},\pi_{Lie})

is a symplectic groupoid integrating the Lie–Poisson structure on

.
