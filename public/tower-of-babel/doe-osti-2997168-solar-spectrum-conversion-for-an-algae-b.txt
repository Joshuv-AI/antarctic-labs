Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

This project focused on developing advanced optical coatings to improve solar energy utilization.
The research aimed to create lanthanide-doped upconversion nanoparticles (UCNPs) capable of
capturing unused near-infrared (NIR) light from the sun and converting it into visible light (blue
and red photons) that can be used for photosynthesis. The primary goal was to identify,
synthesize, and integrate highly efficient UCNPs into a transparent thin-film device. Through a
comprehensive workflow involving computer simulations, high-throughput robotic synthesis,
and detailed optical characterization, the project successfully developed a high-performance
material. The key technical achievement was the creation of a core-shell UCNP (NaYF₄:20%Yb³⁺,
2%Er³⁺ coated with a 10 nm NaYF₄ shell) that demonstrated a quantum yield of 3.2% for
converting 980 nm NIR light into visible light. Transparent thin films fabricated from these
nanoparticles showed excellent optical properties, confirming their potential for practical
applications. This research adds to the scientific understanding of energy transfer in lanthanide
materials and demonstrates a technically effective method for creating efficient light-converting
coatings. The primary benefit to the public lies in the potential for these coatings to enhance the
efficiency of solar-driven processes, such as boosting the growth of algae in photobioreactors for
biofuel production.

Summary of Research Results:

Project Activities and Methodology:
 • Original Hypothesis and Approach: The project's central hypothesis was that UCNPs
 could be optimized to efficiently convert NIR light into visible photons to enhance solar
 energy utilization. The approach involved a three-stage workflow: simulation, synthesis,
 and characterization.
 • Simulation and Material Selection: To guide material design, high-throughput differential
 rate-equation models were used to screen 564 different combinations of lanthanide ions
 (Yb³⁺, Er³⁺, Nd³⁺, Tm³⁺, and Ho³⁺) under various simulated solar illumination conditions.
 These simulations identified NaYF₄:20%Yb³⁺, 20%Er³⁺ as the best-performing system for
 980 nm-to-visible upconversion.
 • Synthesis: Based on the simulation results, four different UCNP formulations were
 synthesized using the WANDA high-throughput synthesis robot. A uniform 10 nm-thick
 inert NaYF₄ shell was grown around each nanoparticle core to reduce surface quenching
 and enhance quantum yield.
 • Characterization and Accomplishments: The quantum yield (QY) of the synthesized
 nanoparticles was measured using an integrating sphere. The NaYF₄:20%Yb³⁺, 2%Er³⁺ core
 UCNPs with the 10 nm shell achieved the highest QY of approximately 3.2%. This result
 validated the simulation-guided approach and met the project's primary objective. The
 best-performing UCNPs were then used to create thin films on silica substrates via a spin-
 coating process. These films were highly transparent and uniform, and they exhibited
 strong visible emission and a long luminescence lifetime (~800 µs) when excited with 980
 nm light, confirming their high quality and optical performance at the device level.

Problems Encountered and Deviations:
 • A notable departure from initial predictions occurred with co-doped samples. While
 simulations suggested that adding trace amounts of Tm³⁺ or Ho³⁺ could enhance QY, the
 experimentally synthesized samples showed a lower QY (<1.5%). This discrepancy is likely
 due to limitations in the model's accuracy at very low dopant concentrations and
 highlights the importance of experimental validation.
