On Data-Driven Koopman Representations of Nonlinear Delay Differential Equations
Santosh Mohan Rajkumar, Dibyasri Barman, Kumar Vikram Singh, Debdipta Goswami

Abstract
This work establishes a rigorous bridge between infinite-dimensional delay dynamics and finite-dimensional Koopman learning, with explicit and interpretable error guarantees. While Koopman analysis is well-developed for ordinary differential equations (ODEs) and partially for partial differential equations (PDEs), its extension to delay differential equations (DDEs) remains limited due to the infinite-dimensional phase space of DDEs. We propose a finite-dimensional Koopman approximation framework based on history discretization and a suitable reconstruction operator, enabling a tractable representation of the Koopman operator via kernel-based extended dynamic mode decomposition (kEDMD). Deterministic error bounds are derived for the learned predictor, decomposing the total error into contributions from history discretization, kernel interpolation, and data-driven regression. Additionally, we develop a kernel-based reconstruction method to recover discretized states from lifted Koopman coordinates, with provable guarantees. Numerical results demonstrate convergence of the learned predictor with respect to both discretization resolution and training data, supporting reliable prediction and control of delay systems.



I
Introduction

Delay differential equations (DDEs) arise naturally in engineering and scientific systems, including networked control systems, biological regulation, and mechanical systems with delayed feedback
[
6
,
13
,
7
,
14
]
. Unlike ordinary differential equations (ODEs), DDEs evolve on an infinite-dimensional phase space consisting of history segments, which fundamentally complicates modeling, prediction, and analysis
[
18
]
.

Recent years have witnessed increasing interest in data-driven modeling of nonlinear DDEs
[
15
,
21
,
4
,
11
]
. Adjoint-based neural DDE methods
[
15
]
enable joint learning of dynamics and delays, but suffer from high computational cost and training instability due to backpropagation through delayed trajectories. Continuous-time extensions
[
21
]
improve expressivity, yet inherit significant memory and scalability limitations. Hybrid physics–machine learning approaches
[
4
]
yield tractable finite-dimensional models, but introduce bias through basis truncation. Sparse identification methods such as SINDy
[
11
]
provide interpretable representations, but rely heavily on library design and struggle to capture complex delay interactions. Consequently, existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off between infinite-dimensional fidelity, computational tractability, and theoretical reliability.

In parallel, the Koopman operator framework has emerged as a powerful paradigm for data-driven analysis and control of nonlinear dynamical systems
[
17
]
. For nonlinear systems governed by ODEs, data-driven Koopman methods such as extended dynamic mode decomposition (EDMD)
[
20
]
and its kernel variants (kEDMD) have been widely developed, with recent works establishing deterministic error bounds for nonlinear systems
[
9
,
2
,
16
]
. Extensions to PDEs have also been explored
[
10
,
12
]
, but these works primarily focus on the spectral approximation of compact differential operators without error bounds suitable for reliable prediction and control design.

Extending Koopman-based methods to delay differential equations (DDEs) remains fundamentally challenging due to the infinite-dimensional nature of the state, which consists of history functions rather than finite-dimensional vectors. Existing Koopman-based approaches to delay systems
[
3
]
rely on heuristic delay embeddings that compress the infinite-dimensional history into finitely many samples, without explicitly quantifying the induced approximation error. As a result, these methods lack guarantees required for reliable prediction and control.

This paper addresses this gap by developing a theoretically grounded, data-driven Koopman framework for nonlinear DDEs with explicit and interpretable error guarantees. The key idea is to construct a finite-dimensional realization of the infinite-dimensional delay dynamics via history discretization and a suitable reconstruction operator, enabling a principled connection between the Koopman operator on the space of history functions and a finite-dimensional data-driven representation.

The main contributions are summarized as follows:
(i)
Koopman realization for DDEs:
We establish a rigorous
bridge between infinite-dimensional delay dynamics and
finite-dimensional Koopman representations through a
sampling–reconstruction framework, yielding a tractable
surrogate model.
(ii)
Data-driven linear surrogate:
Using kernel-based extended dynamic mode decomposition (kEDMD) with Wendland radial basis function (RBF) kernels, we construct a finite-dimensional linear surrogate model that rigorously approximates the infinite-dimensional Koopman operator defined on the Banach space of history functions through a data-driven matrix representation.
(iii)
Deterministic error guarantees:
We derive explicit error bounds for the learned Koopman predictor, decomposing the total approximation error into history discretization, kernel interpolation, and data-driven regression components, providing insight into approximation accuracy.
(iv)
State reconstruction with guarantees:
We develop a kernel-based mechanism to recover the finite-dimensional discretized state from lifted Koopman coordinates and establish deterministic bounds on the resulting state prediction error, ensuring reliability for simulation and control tasks.

The proposed framework provides a principled and control-relevant foundation for learning, prediction, and analysis of nonlinear delay systems, with potential applications in model-based control and networked systems with delays.

II-B

Finite-Dimensional Realization

To obtain a finite-dimensional representation of any state function,
let
\Theta_{M}:=\{\theta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\subset[-\tau_{d},0]
denote a set of
uniform discretization points with
\theta_{1}=-\tau_{d}
,
\theta_{M}=0
, and grid
spacing
\delta(M):=\tau_{d}/(M-1)
. Define the finite-dimensional realization of
W
as the compact operational domain
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathbb{R}^{nM}
defined as
\mathcal{Z}:=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{nM}\mid\|\zeta\|_{b,\infty}\leq\gamma\text{ and }\|\zeta_{j+1}-\zeta_{j}\|\leq L_{\theta}\delta(M),\ j=1,\cdots,(M-1)\}
, where
\zeta=[\zeta_{1}^{\top}\ \cdots\ \zeta_{M}^{\top}]^{\top}
with
\zeta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Define the sampling operator
\mathcal{Q}:W\to\mathcal{Z}
as

\mathcal{Q}(\eta):=[\eta(\theta_{1})^{\top}\ \eta(\theta_{2})^{\top}\ \cdots\ \eta(\theta_{M})^{\top}]^{\top},\ \forall\ \eta\in W.

(2)

Then
\mathcal{Q}(W)\subseteq\mathcal{Z}
, since every
\eta\in W
satisfies the defining constraints of
\mathcal{Z}
.
Now, define the reconstruction operator
\mathcal{R}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathscr{C}
for any
\zeta\in\mathcal{Z}
,
\mathcal{R}
is defined as

\mathcal{R}(\zeta)(\theta):=\zeta_{j}+\tfrac{\theta-\theta_{j}}{\delta(M)}(\zeta_{j+1}-\zeta_{j}),\quad\forall\theta\in[\theta_{j},\theta_{j+1}],

(3)

and
j\in[1:M-1]
. Although
\mathcal{R}(\zeta)(\cdot)
is piecewise affine in the argument
\theta
, the operator
\mathcal{R}
is linear with respect to
\zeta
. Specifically,
\mathcal{R}
is the restriction of a bounded linear operator defined on
\mathbb{R}^{nM}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{R}
is Fréchet differentiable with derivative
D\mathcal{R}(\zeta)=\mathcal{R}
(independent of
\zeta
) and second derivative
D^{2}\mathcal{R}=0
.

Lemma 1
.

The sampling operator
\mathcal{Q}
and the reconstruction operator
\mathcal{R}
satisfy:

(i)

\mathcal{R}
maps
\mathcal{Z}
into
W
, i.e.,
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{Z})\subseteq W
. Moreover,

\|\mathcal{R}(\zeta)-\mathcal{R}(\xi)\|_{\infty}\leq\|\zeta-\xi\|_{b,\infty},\ \forall\zeta,\xi\in\mathcal{Z}
.

(ii)

The composition
\mathcal{Q}\circ\mathcal{R}
is the identity on
\mathcal{Z}
.

(iii)

The composition
\mathcal{P}:=\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{Q}
is a projection on
W
satisfying

\|\eta-\mathcal{P}(\eta)\|_{\infty}\leq L_{\theta}\delta(M),\quad\forall\eta\in W.

Proof.

(i) Let
\zeta\in\mathcal{Z}
and the reconstructed state function be
\tilde{\eta}=\mathcal{R}(\zeta)
. It follows from (
3
) that for any
\theta\in[\theta_{j},\theta_{j+1}]
,
\tilde{\eta}(\theta)
is a convex combination of
\zeta_{j}
and
\zeta_{j+1}
. Since
\|\zeta_{j}\|\leq\gamma
for all
j
, by convexity of the norm,
\|\tilde{\eta}(\theta)\|\leq\gamma
. Thus,
\sup_{\theta\in[-\tau_{d},0]}\|\tilde{\eta}(\theta)\|=\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma
. Moreover, on each interval
[\theta_{j},\theta_{j+1}]
,
\tilde{\eta}
is affine in
\theta
and satisfies

\left\|\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta}\tilde{\eta}(\theta)\right\|=\tfrac{\|\zeta_{j+1}-\zeta_{j}\|}{\delta(M)}\leq L_{\theta}.

Since
\tilde{\eta}
is continuous and piecewise differentiable with respect to
\theta
, it follows that

\|\tilde{\eta}(\theta_{1})-\tilde{\eta}(\theta_{2})\|\leq L_{\theta}|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|,\qquad\forall\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in[-\tau_{d},0].

Therefore,
\tilde{\eta}\in W
. Since
\zeta\in\mathcal{Z}
was arbitrary, we conclude that
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{Z})\subseteq W
.

Now let
\zeta,\xi\in\mathcal{Z}
. For any
\theta\in[\theta_{j},\theta_{j+1}]
, write
\lambda=(\theta-\theta_{j})/\delta(M)\in[0,1]
. Then

\mathcal{R}(\zeta)(\theta)-\mathcal{R}(\xi)(\theta)=(1-\lambda)(\zeta_{j}-\xi_{j})+\lambda(\zeta_{j+1}-\xi_{j+1}).

By convexity of the norm,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}(\zeta)(\theta)-\mathcal{R}(\xi)(\theta)\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda)\|\zeta_{j}-\xi_{j}\|+\lambda\|\zeta_{j+1}-\xi_{j+1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\zeta-\xi\|_{b,\infty}.

Taking the supremum over
\theta\in[-\tau_{d},0]
yields

\|\mathcal{R}(\zeta)-\mathcal{R}(\xi)\|_{\infty}\leq\|\zeta-\xi\|_{b,\infty}.

(ii) By definition,
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{R}(\zeta))
samples the function
\mathcal{R}(\zeta)
at the grid points
\{\theta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}
. From the interpolation formula (
3
),
\mathcal{R}(\zeta)(\theta_{j})=\zeta_{j}
for all
j\in[1:M]
. Thus,
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{R}(\zeta))=\zeta
. Hence, the composition
\mathcal{Q}\circ\mathcal{R}
is the identity on
\mathcal{Z}
.

(iii) Let
\eta\in W
. For any
\theta\in[\theta_{j},\theta_{j+1}]
, define
\lambda:=(\theta-\theta_{j})/\delta(M)\in[0,1]
. Then,

\mathcal{P}(\eta)(\theta)=(1-\lambda)\eta(\theta_{j})+\lambda\eta(\theta_{j+1}).

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\eta(\theta)-\mathcal{P}(\eta)(\theta)\|

\displaystyle=\|(1-\lambda)\bigl(\eta(\theta)-\eta(\theta_{j})\bigr)+

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\lambda\bigl(\eta(\theta)-\eta(\theta_{j+1})\bigr)\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda)\|\eta(\theta)-\eta(\theta_{j})\|+

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\lambda\|\eta(\theta)-\eta(\theta_{j+1})\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda)L_{\theta}|\theta-\theta_{j}|+\lambda L_{\theta}|\theta-\theta_{j+1}|.

Using
|\theta-\theta_{j}|=\lambda\delta(M)
and
|\theta-\theta_{j+1}|=(1-\lambda)\delta(M)
, we obtain

\|\eta(\theta)-\mathcal{P}(\eta)(\theta)\|\leq 2\lambda(1-\lambda)L_{\theta}\delta(M)\leq L_{\theta}\delta(M).

Taking the supremum over
\theta\in[-\tau_{d},0]
yields

\|\eta-\mathcal{P}(\eta)\|_{\infty}\leq L_{\theta}\delta(M),

which completes the proof.
∎

II-D

Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS)

Consider
\mathrm{k}:\mathbb{R}^{nM}\times\mathbb{R}^{nM}\to\mathbb{R}
to be a continuous strictly positive-definite symmetric kernel and define the canonical feature
\varphi_{z}(\cdot)=\mathrm{k}(z,\cdot)
for
z\in\mathbb{R}^{nM}
. For the distinct points
\mathds{X}
, the kernel matrix is defined as

\displaystyle K_{\mathds{X}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{k}(z_{1},z_{1})&\cdots&\mathrm{k}(z_{p},z_{1})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathrm{k}(z_{1},z_{p})&\cdots&\mathrm{k}(z_{p},z_{p})\end{bmatrix}.

(9)

The kernel
\mathrm{k}
defines a Hilbert space of functions
\mathcal{H}
satisfying the reproducing property
g(z)=\langle g,\varphi_{z}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
for all
g\in\mathcal{H}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{nM}

[
2
]
. The native RKHS norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
is defined via the associated abstract inner product and is typically difficult to evaluate explicitly
[
16
,
19
]
. We consider the RKHS
\mathcal{H}
generated by Wendland radial basis function (RBF)
\mathcal{W}_{s}:\mathbb{R}^{nM}\to\mathbb{R}
with smoothness degree
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
defined as

\mathcal{W}_{s}(\xi):=\Phi_{nM,s}(\|\xi\|)
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{nM}

with
\Phi_{nM,s}\in\mathcal{C}^{2s}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R})
given as
[
19
]

\Phi_{nM,s}(r)=\begin{cases}P_{\mathfrak{d},s}(r)&0\leq r\leq 1\\
0,&r>1\end{cases},

(10)

where
P_{\mathfrak{d},s}
is a univariate polynomial of degree
\mathfrak{d}=\lfloor nM/2\rfloor+3s+1
. The induced kernel with Wendland RBF is given by
\mathrm{k}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})=\Phi_{nM,s}(\|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}\|)
for
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{nM}
. In this work, we consider the Wendland RBF
\Phi_{nM,s}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R})
with
P_{\mathfrak{d},1}(r)=(1-r)^{\mathfrak{d}-1}[(\mathfrak{d}-1)r+1]
with
\mathfrak{d}=\lfloor nM/2\rfloor+4
and

[

. For Wendland kernels, the equivalence between the native RKHS norm and the fractional Sobolev norm is established via a bounded extension operator

]

III
Koopman Formalism and kEDMD

Consider the space of Lipschitz continuous functionals on
W
be defined as
[
1
]

\mathrm{Lip}(W):=\left\{\psi:W\to\mathbb{R}\;\middle|\;\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{W}}<\infty\right\},

(11)

where
\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{W}}:=\|\psi\|_{\infty}+\mathrm{Lip}_{W}(\psi)
, with

\|\psi\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{\eta\in W}|\psi(\eta)|,\ \mathrm{Lip}_{W}(\psi):=\sup_{\eta_{1}\neq\eta_{2}}\tfrac{|\psi(\eta_{1})-\psi(\eta_{2})|}{\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\|_{\infty}}.

Further, let the space of Lipschitz continuous functions on
\mathcal{Z}
defined as

\mathrm{Lip}(\mathcal{Z}):=\left\{\tilde{\psi}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}\;\middle|\;\|\tilde{\psi}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}}<\infty\right\},

(12)

where
\|\tilde{\psi}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}}:=\|\tilde{\psi}\|_{\infty}+\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\tilde{\psi}),

with

\|\tilde{\psi}\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{\zeta\in\mathcal{Z}}|\tilde{\psi}(\zeta)|,\ \mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\tilde{\psi}):=\sup_{\zeta_{1}\neq\zeta_{2}}\tfrac{|\tilde{\psi}(\zeta_{1})-\tilde{\psi}(\zeta_{2})|}{\|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}\|_{b,\infty}}.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\psi\in\mathrm{Lip}(W)
and define the induced observable function

\tilde{\psi}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
as
\tilde{\psi}:=\psi\circ\mathcal{R}
.
Then,
\tilde{\psi}\in\mathrm{Lip}(\mathcal{Z})
and

\|\tilde{\psi}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}}\leq\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{W}}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{Z})\subseteq W
by Lemma 1(i), the composition

\tilde{\psi}=\psi\circ\mathcal{R}

is well defined on
\mathcal{Z}
.
Now,

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\psi}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\zeta\in\mathcal{Z}}|\psi(\mathcal{R}(\zeta))|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\eta\in\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{Z})}|\psi(\eta)|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\eta\in W}|\psi(\eta)|=\|\psi\|_{\infty}.

Next, let
\zeta,\xi\in\mathcal{Z}
with
\zeta\neq\xi
. Then, using
Lemma

1
(i)

\displaystyle|\tilde{\psi}(\zeta)-\tilde{\psi}(\xi)|

\displaystyle=|\psi(\mathcal{R}(\zeta))-\psi(\mathcal{R}(\xi))|

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Lip}_{W}(\psi)\,\|\mathcal{R}(\zeta)-\mathcal{R}(\xi)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Lip}_{W}(\psi)\,\|\zeta-\xi\|_{b,\infty}.

Dividing by
\|\zeta-\xi\|_{b,\infty}
and taking the supremum over all
\zeta\neq\xi
gives

\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\tilde{\psi})\leq\mathrm{Lip}_{W}(\psi).

Therefore,
\tilde{\psi}\in\mathrm{Lip}(\mathcal{Z})
and

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\psi}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}}=\|\tilde{\psi}\|_{\infty}+\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\tilde{\psi})

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi\|_{\infty}+\mathrm{Lip}_{W}(\psi)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{W}}.

∎

Lemma 4
.

We consider functions in the RKHS
\mathcal{H}
evaluated on the compact set
\mathcal{Z}
.
Then
\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Lip}(\mathcal{Z})
. In particular, there exists a constant

C_{\mathcal{H}}>0
such that

\|\Psi\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}}\leq C_{\mathcal{H}}\|\Psi\|_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad\forall\Psi\in\mathcal{H}.

Proof.

Let
\Psi\in\mathcal{H}
. Since
\mathcal{H}
is an RKHS with reproducing kernel
\mathrm{k}
, for any

\zeta\in\mathcal{Z}
,
\Psi(\zeta)=\langle\Psi,\mathrm{k}(\zeta,\cdot)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
. Hence,

|\Psi(\zeta)|\leq\|\Psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|\mathrm{k}(\zeta,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\|\Psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}\sqrt{\mathrm{k}(\zeta,\zeta)}.

Since
\mathrm{k}
is continuous and
\mathcal{Z}
is compact, there exists

C_{0}:=\sup_{\zeta\in\mathcal{Z}}\sqrt{\mathrm{k}(\zeta,\zeta)}<\infty,\text{ so that}

\|\Psi\|_{\infty}\leq C_{0}\|\Psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}
. Next, for any
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}
, the reproducing property gives

\displaystyle|\Psi(\zeta_{1})-\Psi(\zeta_{2})|

\displaystyle=|\langle\Psi,\mathrm{k}(\zeta_{1},\cdot)-\mathrm{k}(\zeta_{2},\cdot)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|

\displaystyle\leq\|\Psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}\,\|\mathrm{k}(\zeta_{1},\cdot)-\mathrm{k}(\zeta_{2},\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

Since the kernel is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
, the feature map
\zeta\mapsto\mathrm{k}(\zeta,\cdot)
is Lipschitz on
the compact set
\mathcal{Z}
, and thus there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

\|\mathrm{k}(\zeta_{1},\cdot)-\mathrm{k}(\zeta_{2},\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq C_{1}\|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}\|_{b,\infty},\qquad\forall\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}.

Therefore,

|\Psi(\zeta_{1})-\Psi(\zeta_{2})|\leq C_{1}\|\Psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}\|_{b,\infty},

which gives

\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\Psi)\leq C_{1}\|\Psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

Combining the above estimates yields

\|\Psi\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}}=\|\Psi\|_{\infty}+\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\Psi)\leq C_{\mathcal{H}}\|\Psi\|_{\mathcal{H}},

where
C_{\mathcal{H}}:=C_{0}+C_{1}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Lip}(\mathcal{Z})
.
∎

Corollary 1
.

For any
\Psi\in\mathcal{H}
,
\widehat{\Psi}:=\Psi\circ\mathcal{Q}
lies in
\mathrm{Lip}(W)
and satisfies

\|\widehat{\Psi}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{W}}\leq\|\Psi\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}}\leq C_{\mathcal{H}}\|\Psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof.

For any
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in W
, we have

|\widehat{\Psi}(\eta_{1})-\widehat{\Psi}(\eta_{2})|=|\Psi(\mathcal{Q}(\eta_{1}))-\Psi(\mathcal{Q}(\eta_{2}))|.

It follows from the definition of
\mathcal{Q}
and results from
Lemma

4
that

\displaystyle|\widehat{\Psi}(\eta_{1})-\widehat{\Psi}(\eta_{2})|\leq\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\Psi)\,\|\mathcal{Q}(\eta_{1})-\mathcal{Q}(\eta_{2})\|_{b,\infty}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\Psi)\ \left(\max_{j\in[1:M]}\|\eta_{1}(\theta_{j})-\eta_{2}(\theta_{j})\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\Psi)\ \|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\|_{\infty}.

This gives

\mathrm{Lip}_{W}(\widehat{\Psi})\leq\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\Psi)
.
Also,

\|\widehat{\Psi}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\eta\in W}|\Psi(\mathcal{Q}(\eta))|\leq\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}|\Psi(z)|=\|\Psi\|_{\infty}.

Therefore,

\|\widehat{\Psi}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{W}}=\|\widehat{\Psi}\|_{\infty}+\mathrm{Lip}_{W}(\widehat{\Psi})\leq\|\Psi\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}}.

Thus
\widehat{\Psi}\in\mathrm{Lip}(W)
. If
\Psi\in\mathcal{H}
, then by
Lemma

4
,

\|\widehat{\Psi}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{W}}\leq\|\Psi\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathcal{Z}}}\leq C_{\mathcal{H}}\|\Psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

∎

III-A

Koopman Operator

The Koopman operator is a linear but infinite-dimensional operator that enables the study of nonlinear dynamical systems through its action on observables defined on a Banach space
[
17
]
. The action of the Koopman operator
\mathcal{K}
on any
\psi\in\mathrm{Lip}(W)
is given by

(\mathcal{K}\psi)(\phi)=\psi(F(\phi))=\psi(\phi^{+}).

(13)

Suppose that
\tilde{\psi}\in\mathrm{Lip}(\mathcal{Z})
is induced by

\psi\in\mathrm{Lip}(W)
via
\tilde{\psi}=\psi\circ\mathcal{R}
. Then,
the action of the induced Koopman operator
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}
is given by

(\tilde{\mathcal{K}}\tilde{\psi})(z)=\tilde{\psi}(\tilde{F}(z))

Since
\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Lip}(Z)
, the propagation of any

\Psi\in\mathcal{H}
can be expressed, with a pointwise error bound, as
[
9
]

(\tilde{\mathcal{K}}\Psi)(z)\approx\sum_{\ell=1}^{p}\bigl(\tilde{K}\Psi_{\mathds{X}}\bigr)_{\ell}\ \varphi_{z_{\ell}}(z),

(14)

where
\Psi_{\mathds{X}}=[\Psi(z_{1})\ \cdots\ \Psi(z_{p})]^{\top}
,
\tilde{K}=K_{\mathds{X}}^{-1}K_{\tilde{F}(\mathds{X})}K_{\mathds{X}}^{-1}
, and

K_{\tilde{F}(\mathds{X})}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{k}(z_{1},\tilde{F}(z_{1}))&\cdots&\mathrm{k}(z_{p},\tilde{F}(z_{1}))\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathrm{k}(z_{1},\tilde{F}(z_{p}))&\cdots&\mathrm{k}(z_{p},\tilde{F}(z_{p}))\end{bmatrix}.

(15)

III-B

Data Driven Surrogate using kEDMD

Consider each data point
z_{\ell}\in\mathds{X}
and the corresponding local dataset
\mathds{X}_{\ell}
. To estimate the nonlinear dynamics locally, we solve the affine regression problem

\Phi_{\ell}=\argmin_{\Phi\in\mathbb{R}^{nM\times(nM+1)}}\|\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}^{+}-\Phi\bar{\mathbf{Z}}_{\ell}\|_{F},

(16)

where
\Phi_{\ell}=[\hat{F}_{\ell}\ \ \hat{B}_{\ell}]
, with

\hat{F}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{nM\times 1}
and
\hat{B}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{nM\times nM}
.
By construction,
\hat{F}_{\ell}
provides an estimate of
\tilde{F}(z_{\ell})
.
The regression problem (
16
) is well-posed under
3
. Using the estimates
\hat{F}_{\ell}\approx\tilde{F}(z_{\ell})
for each
z_{\ell}\in\mathds{X}
, we construct a linear surrogate model based on kEDMD. Consider the lifted state

\bm{\Psi}(z)=[\Psi_{1}(z)\ \cdots\ \Psi_{p}(z)]^{\top},

(17)

where
\Psi_{\ell}(\cdot)=\varphi_{z_{\ell}}(\cdot)
for
\ell\in[1:p]
. Then, from (

), the induced Koopman evolution admits the approximation

(\tilde{\mathcal{K}}\bm{\Psi})(z)\approx\mathbf{A}\bm{\Psi}(z),\text{where }\mathbf{A}=K_{\hat{F}(\mathds{X})}^{\top}K_{\mathds{X}}^{-1},

(18)

and

is constructed using the estimates
\hat{F}_{\ell}
of
\tilde{F}(z_{\ell})
for
\ell\in[1:p]
, analogously to (
15
). Using the Koopman approximation (
18
), the lifted state evolution is approximated by the data-driven surrogate model

\bm{\Psi}(z^{+})\approx\mathbf{A}\bm{\Psi}(z).

(19)

Next, we state the main theorem, which provides a bound on the approximation error of the data-driven linear surrogate (
19
).

Let Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
3

\displaystyle=C_{1}L_{F}L_{\theta}\|\bm{\Psi}\|_{\mathcal{H}},\ R_{i}=B_{1}h_{\mathds{X}}^{s-\tfrac{1}{2}}\|\bm{\Psi}\|_{\mathcal{H}},

(22a)

\displaystyle R_{d}

\displaystyle=C_{0}\sqrt{d}\ p\ L_{k}\bar{Z}\tfrac{D_{F}}{2}\|\bm{\Psi}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|K_{\mathds{X}}^{-1}\|,

(22b)

for all

,
\delta(M)
with

, and
\rho>0
. Here,

, and the positive constants

,
h_{0}

Proof.

See Appendix (

).

Equation (

), decomposed into three terms: the state discretization error

For any given

.

Proof.

See Appendix (

V-C
).
∎

Proposition 1 shows that any discretized state

can be approximately reconstructed from the lifted coordinates via the kernel interpolant in (

VI
Conclusion

This work develops a data-driven Koopman framework for nonlinear delay differential equations (DDEs), bridging infinite-dimensional delay dynamics with finite-dimensional representations via history discretization and reconstruction. Using kernel-based extended dynamic mode decomposition (kEDMD), we construct a linear surrogate model with deterministic error bounds that decompose the approximation error into discretization, kernel interpolation, and regression components, along with guarantees for state reconstruction. Numerical results demonstrate convergence with respect to discretization resolution and data density. Overall, the proposed framework provides a theoretically grounded foundation for prediction and control of delay systems, with future directions including closed-loop control synthesis and extensions to high-dimensional networked systems.
