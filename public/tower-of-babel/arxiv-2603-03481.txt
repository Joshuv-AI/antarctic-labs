Global boundedness and normalized solutions to a p p -Laplacian equation


Abstract
. In the paper, we prove the existence of radial solutions to − Δ p ​ u + ( sgn ⁡ ( p − s ) + V ⁡ ( x ) ) ​ | u | p − 2 ​ u + λ ​ | u | s − 2 ​ u = | u | q − 2 ​ u in ​ ℝ N -\Delta_{p}u+({\rm sgn}(p-s)+V(x))|u|^{p-2}u+\lambda|u|^{s-2}u=|u|^{q-2}u\qquad\text{in}\,\mathbb{R}^{N}\\ with prescribed L s ​ ( ℝ N ) L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}) -norm, where N ≥ 3 , p ∈ [ 2 , N ) , s ∈ ( 1 , p ] , q ∈ ( p ​ N + s N , N ​ p N − p ) N\geq 3,\,p\in[2,N),\,s\in(1,p],\,q\in(p\frac{N+s}{N},\frac{Np}{N-p}) and V : ℝ N → ℝ V:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R} is a suitable radial potential. We stress that V V is required to be radial but not necessarily bounded, and there are no assumptions about its sign. The case V ≡ 0 V\equiv 0 is also included. The proof is variational and relies on a min-max argument. A key-tool is the Pohozaev identity, which is shown to be true for any solution under quite weak assumptions about the potential V V . This identity is proved with the aid of a new global boundedness result for subsolutions to a suitable p p -Laplace equation.

Introduction

Let
N\geq 3
,
p\in[2,N)
,
q\in(p\frac{N+s}{N},p^{*}),\,p^{*}:=\frac{Np}{N-p}
,
s\in(1,p]
and
\rho>0
. Under these assumptions, we want to construct radial solutions to the problem

(1.1)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u+{\rm sgn}(p-s)|u|^{p-2}u+V(x)|u|^{p-2}u+\lambda|u|^{s-2}u

\displaystyle=|u|^{q-2}u\qquad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$,}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{s}=\rho,

where
V:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is a given radial potential and
\|u\|_{s}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{s}dx\right)^{1/s}
.

Consider a generalized nonlinear Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation (TDSE) where the kinetic energy term is governed by the
p
-Laplacian operator

i\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}=-\Delta_{p}\Psi-f(|\Psi|)\Psi,

where
\Psi(t,x)
is a complex-valued wave function depending on time
t
and space
x
. In quantum mechanics and optics, we are deeply interested in solitary waves or standing waves-states whose spatial profile remains constant over time, while only their phase rotates. To find these, we make the standing wave ansatz

\Psi(t,x)=e^{-i\lambda t}u(x),

where
u(x)
is a real-valued spatial profile and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is the frequency. This ansatz into the generalized TDSE gives us a stationary elliptic equation as in (
1.1
) for the case
s=2,\,V\equiv 0
.
While the case
s=2
corresponds to the physical conservation of the mass of particles, the case
s=p
corresponds to the mathematical conservation of the system’s inherent nonlinear scale.
For instance, consider an eigenvalue equation of the form

-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u.

To find solutions, we minimize the kinetic energy
\int|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx
subject to the constraint
\int|u|^{p}\,dx=c
. The first eigenvalue represents the generalized fundamental frequency of the system.
Exploring the space between them, that is
s\in(1,p]
, allows us to model strongly interacting, anomalous fluids while guaranteeing the system remains energetically stable and won’t mathematically collapse.
In complex systems like non-Newtonian fluids flowing through porous rocks (modeled by the Porous Medium Equation (state-dependent (density)) or
p
-Laplacian (gradient-dependent (flux)), the standard laws of thermodynamics are modified. In these systems, standard Boltzmann-Gibbs statistical mechanics breaks down. Instead, the system is governed by “non-extensive statistics” (Tsallis statistics
[
15
,
16
]
), where the probability density of finding a particle is not simply
|u|^{2}
, but is related to
u
raised to a power
s
to account for long-range interactions and correlations between particles. Conserving
\int|u|^{s}\,dx=\rho
physically corresponds to conserving a weighted measure of the fluid’s volume that accounts for its tendency to clump or spread in an anomalous way. In particular, when
1<s<2
, physically, this models a “droplet” or a localized blob of fluid with a hard edge, rather than a wave that slowly fades to zero at infinity, whereas for
s>2
models systems that resist high densities, forcing the wave to spread out more smoothly.
In applications like nonlinear optics and Bose-Einstein condensates, the
L^{s}
-norm represents conserved physical quantities, such as the total number of atoms or the mass of the wave. The
L^{p}
-supercritical (
q>p\frac{N+s}{N}
) regime is often associated with more complex and potentially singular physical phenomena, such as self-focusing, solitons (solitary waves) and nonlinear scattering. It can also describe optical collapse, where the wave shape becomes highly concentrated. Now we shall give the mathematical set up and links to the recent mathematical studies considering the TDSE involving
p
-Laplacian operator.

In
[
19
]
, the authors consider problem (
1.1
) with
V=0,\,s=2
and find at least a solution
u
in the space
X:=W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. However, we do not know whether their solution is radial or not. Due to the fact that the non-linearity
f(u):=|u|^{q-2}u-|u|^{p-2}u-\lambda|u|^{s-2}u
depends on
u
only and not on
x
, we expect to be able to construct radial solutions to (
1.1
) at least when
V=0
, or more generally for some suitable radial potentials
V
.

In this paper, we will prove that, in some cases, this is true. More precisely, we assume that
V\in L^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\alpha\in[\frac{N}{p},\infty]
. Using that
\frac{p\alpha}{\alpha-1}\in(p,p^{*})
for
\alpha\in(\frac{N}{p},\infty)
,
\frac{p\alpha}{\alpha-1}=p^{*}
for
\alpha=\frac{N}{p}
and introducing the convention
\frac{p\alpha}{\alpha-1}=p
for
\alpha=\infty
, the Hölder inequality and the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{\frac{\alpha p}{\alpha-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
give

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u|^{p-2}uvdx\right|\leq\|V\|_{\alpha}\|u\|_{\frac{p\alpha}{\alpha-1}}^{p-1}\|v\|_{\frac{p\alpha}{\alpha-1}}<\infty\qquad\forall\,u,\,v\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

hence problem (
1.1
) is variational. In other words, setting
X:=W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the solutions to (
1.1
) are the critical points of the functional

(1.2)

J_{V}(u):=\frac{1}{p}\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{{\rm sgn}(p-s)}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{p}+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u|^{p}dx-\frac{1}{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q},\qquad\forall\,u\in X,

constrained to the sphere
\mathcal{S}_{r,\rho}:=\mathcal{S}_{\rho}\cap X_{r}
, where

X_{r}:=W^{1,p}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\qquad\mathcal{S}_{\rho}:=\{u\in X:\,\|u\|_{s}=\rho\}

and
W^{1,p}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the space of radial functions in
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Setting

\|u\|:=(\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}+{\rm sgn}(p-s)\|u\|_{p}^{p})^{1/p}=\begin{cases}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\qquad\text{if}\,s\in(1,p)\\
\|\nabla u\|_{p}\qquad\text{if}\,p=s,\end{cases}

where
\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}:=\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}+\|u\|_{p}^{p}
.
Then our functional can be rewritten as

J_{V}(u)=\frac{1}{p}\|u\|^{p}+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u|^{p}dx-\frac{1}{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}\qquad\forall\,u\in X.

For
u\in X
and
t>0
, we define the scaling
u^{t}(x):=t^{N/s}u(tx)
. Note that this scaling preserves the
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-norm, namely
\|u^{t}\|_{s}=\|u\|_{s}
. Using such a scaling, we introduce the Pohozaev functional

(1.3)

\displaystyle P_{V}(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=1}J_{V}(u_{t})=\frac{p(N+s)-sN}{sp}\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{p}+\frac{N(p-s)}{sp}\|u\|^{p}_{p}

\displaystyle-\frac{N(q-s)}{sq}\|u\|^{q}_{q}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)\left(\frac{N}{s}|u|^{p}+|u|^{p-2}u\nabla u\cdotp x\right)dx,\qquad\forall\,u\in X.

Note that, if we assume that
\tilde{W}:=V(\cdotp)|\cdotp|\in L^{\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, with the convention that
\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}=\infty
if
\alpha=\infty
, the functional
P_{V}
is well defined on
X
. In fact, for such a potential
V
, the Hölder inequality and the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{\frac{\alpha p}{\alpha-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
yield that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u|^{p-2}u(\nabla u\cdotp x)dx\right|\leq\|\tilde{W}\|_{\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}}\|u\|_{\frac{p\alpha}{\alpha-1}}^{p-1}\|\nabla u\|_{p}<\infty\qquad\forall\,u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

It is known that the solutions constructed in Theorem
1.1
of
[
19
]
in the case
s=2,\,V\equiv 0
satisfy the Pohozaev identity

(1.4)

P_{V}(u)=0.

In that case, the Pohozaev identity is a consequence of Theorem
1.1
of
[
7
]
, which relies on some regularity properties of the solution
u
, such as boundedness. In our result the Pohozaev identity directly follows by construction, without using further regularity properties of the weak solution
u\in X
. In particular, it holds true for solutions which are not necessarily bounded.

In our forthcoming result, we will need to impose some bounds about the norms of
V
and
\tilde{W}
. For this purpose, for
p\in[2,N)
, we set

S_{p,\alpha}:=\begin{cases}\inf_{u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{\|u\|_{\frac{p\alpha}{\alpha-1}}^{p}}\qquad\text{if}\,\alpha\in[\frac{N}{p},\infty),\,s\in(1,p)\,\text{or}\,\alpha=N/p,\,s=p\\
\inf_{u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}}{\|u\|_{\frac{p\alpha}{\alpha-1}}^{p}}\qquad\text{if}\,\alpha\in(N/p,\infty),\,s=p\\
1\qquad\text{if}\,\alpha=\infty,\end{cases}

Note that, for
\alpha\in[\frac{N}{p},\infty)
,
S_{p,\alpha}^{-1/p}
is the best constant in the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{\frac{p\alpha}{\alpha-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

In our main result we assume that

(V_{1})

\alpha\in[\frac{N}{p},\infty],\,V\in L^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a radial function such that
\tilde{W}\in L^{\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,\|V\|_{\alpha}<S_{p,\alpha}
and

(1.5)

spS_{p,\alpha}^{-\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}}\|\tilde{W}\|_{\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}}+N\max\{|q-p-s|,1\}S_{p,\alpha}^{-1}\|V\|_{\alpha}<\min\left\{s\left(\frac{N}{q}-\frac{N-p}{p}\right),Nq-p(N+s)\right\},

holds.

Now we are ready to state our main Theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
(V_{1})
holds and
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)=0
if
\alpha\in\{N/p,\infty\}
. Then there exists
\rho_{0}>0
such that, for any
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
, there exists a constant
K_{\rho}>0
and a solution
(\lambda_{\rho},u_{\rho})\in(0,\infty)\times X_{r}
to problem (
1.1
) such that
J_{V}(u_{\rho})=c_{V,\rho}^{r}>0
, where

(1.6)

\displaystyle c_{V,\rho}^{r}

\displaystyle:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\rho,r}}\max_{t\in[0,1]}J_{V}(\gamma(t))

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rho,r}

\displaystyle:=\{\gamma\in C([0,1],\mathcal{S}_{\rho,r}):\,\|\gamma(0)\|\leq K_{\rho},\,J_{V}(\gamma(1))<0\}.

Moreover,
u_{\rho}
satisfies the Pohozaev identity
P_{V}(u)=0
and
\rho^{s}\lambda_{\rho}\to\infty
as
\rho\to 0^{+}
.

Our result works for
p=2
too. We note that the trivial potential
V\equiv 0
satisfies
(V_{1})
, therefore Theorem
1.1
also applies to the homogeneous case
V\equiv 0
.

It is interesting to compare the solutions constructed in Theorem

with those constructed in Theorem

of
[

]
for

. In order to do so, we set, for any

,

\displaystyle c_{V,\rho}

\displaystyle:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\rho}}\max_{t\in[0,1]}J_{V}(\gamma(t))

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rho}

\displaystyle:=\{\gamma\in C([0,1],\mathcal{S}_{\rho}):\,\|\gamma(0)\|\leq K_{\rho},\,J_{V}(\gamma(1))<0\}.

Moreover, let
c_{\rho}^{r}:=c^{r}_{0,\rho}
and
c_{\rho}:=c_{0,\rho}
.

Remark 1.2
.

•

Note that, due to the Definition of

, we have
c^{r}_{V,\rho}\geq c_{V,\rho}
.

•

Due to the properties of the radial (Schwarz) rearrangement, we have

if

, for any

. This is true since, if

and the Hardy-Littlewood inequality yields that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V|u^{*}|^{p}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V|u|^{p}dx\qquad\forall\,u\in X

.

yields that, for

. Therefore, it would be interesting to understand if the solutions constructed in

in the case

The case

is treated in Theorem

of
[

. However, our result is new, even in the case

), in which the Banach structure of the ambient space is sufficient to construct a bounded Palais-Smale sequence of

at level

provides an important generalization of Theorem

Similar results were proved in Theorem

[

]

for a non-positive potential

The idea of the proof is the following. First we construct a bounded Palais-Smale sequence
\{u_{n}\}\in\mathcal{S}_{\rho,r}
of

at level

which almost satisfies the Pohozaev identity, that is
P_{V}(u_{n})=o_{n}(1)

strongly in

. Due to the strong convergence in

P_{V}(u)=0
is also satisfied.

Finally, if
(V_{1})
is satisfied with
\alpha\in(\frac{N}{p},\infty)
, we use the compactness of the embedding
W^{1,p}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{\frac{p\alpha}{\alpha-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to prove the existence of a strongly convergent subsequence. If
\alpha=\frac{N}{p}
or

In particular, in the proof of the splitting Lemma, we need to use the fact that any weak solution
u\in X
to

(1.8)

-\Delta_{p}u+{\rm sgn}(p-s)|u|^{p-2}u+\lambda|u|^{s-2}u=|u|^{q-2}u\qquad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$,}

with
\lambda>0
, satisfies the Pohozaev identity
P_{0}(u)=0
. This fact is known for

(see
[

,
7
]
), but is new for

. In our paper, this fact follows as a corollary of a more general result.

More precisely, given an odd continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the growth condition

(1.9)

-ct^{s-1}\leq f(t)\leq ct^{q-1}\qquad\forall\,t>0,

for some constant

and a potential

, we say that

is a local weak solution to

(1.10)

if

(1.11)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdotp\nabla v\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u|^{p-2}uv\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u)v\,dx\qquad\forall\,v\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Note that, if
V\in L^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
2\leq p<N
,
1<s\leq p
,
q\in(p,p^{*})
,
\beta:=\max\{2,N/p\},\,V\in L^{\beta}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,V\geq 0
, and

is continuous, odd and fulfills (
1.9
). Let

be a local weak solution to (

) such that

(1.12)

Then

\frac{N-p}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx-N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u|^{p-2}u(x\cdotp\nabla u)dx=0,

where
F(u):=\int_{0}^{u}f(t)dt
.

Remark 1.4
.

If

,
f(u)=|u|^{q-2}u-\lambda|u|^{s-2}u-{\rm sgn}(p-s)|u|^{p-2}u

Multiplying (

) by

and subtracting to (

for weak solutions

2.
Global boundedness and the Pohozaev identity

In this section, we will assume that
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function fulfilling

(2.1)

|f(t)|\leq c(|t|^{q-1}+|t|^{s-1})\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R},

and set

.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
2\leq p<N
,

is continuous and satisfies (

) and
V\in L^{N/p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

If, in addition,

3.
A deformation Theorem

In this section, we show how to construct a Palais-Smale sequence of a given functional (along
paths
) on a Hilbert manifold at a certain given inf-max level.

The setting is the following. We consider a Banach space
X
Moreover, given a functional
G\in C^{1}(X,\mathbb{R})
, we consider the set

M:=\{u\in X:\,G(u)=0\}\subset X.

We assume that
G^{\prime}(u)\neq 0
for any
u\in M
, so that
M
is a
C^{1}
manifold of codimension
1
.

Given a functional
J\in C^{1}(X,\mathbb{R})
, we want to find a Palais-Smale sequence of
J
(along
paths
) constrained on the manifold
M
.

For any
u\in M
, the tangent space to
M
at
u
is given by

T_{u}M=\{v\in X:\,G^{\prime}(u)[v]=0\}={\rm Ker}(G^{\prime}(u))\subset X.

In these notations, for any
u\in M
,
J^{\prime}_{M}
is in the topological dual space
(T_{u}M)^{\ast}
of
T_{u}M
. Then norm of a functional
F\in(T_{u}M)^{\ast}
is given by

\|F\|_{\ast}:=\sup\{|F[v]|:\,v\in T_{u}M,\,\|v\|_{X}=1\}.

Let
T(U):=\cup_{u\in M}T_{u}M
denote the tangent bundle to
M
and let
\tilde{M}:=\{u\in M:\,J^{\prime}_{M}(u)\neq 0\}
.

Lemma 3.1

(Existence of a pseudo gradient)
.

In the above notations, there exists a vector field
Y:\tilde{M}\to T(U)
such that

(1)

\|Y(u)\|_{X}\leq 2\|J^{\prime}_{M}(u)\|_{\ast},\,\,\forall\,u\in\tilde{M}
.

(2)

J^{\prime}_{M}(u)[Y(u)]\geq\|J^{\prime}_{M}(u)\|^{2}_{\ast},\,\forall\,u\in\tilde{M}
.

Proof.

For any
u\in M
, we take a tangent vector
w\in T_{u}M
such that

\|w\|_{X}=1,\qquad J^{\prime}_{M}(u)[w]\geq\frac{3}{4}\|J^{\prime}_{M}(u)\|_{\ast}

and we set
Y(u):=\frac{3}{2}\|J^{\prime}_{M}(u)\|_{\ast}w\in T_{u}M
. It is possible to see that
Y(u)
fulfills the required properties.
∎

The vector field
Y
constructed in Lemma
3.1
is known as a
pseudo-gradient
vector field for
J
on
M
.

Remark 3.2
.

We note Lemma
3.1
is a generalization of Lemma
4
of
[
11
]
where the authors prove the existence of a pseudo-gradient vector field in the particular case
S:=\{u\in H:\,\|u\|_{H}=1\}
.

The main purpose of the Section is to find a sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset M
such that
J(u_{n})
converges to some suitable real number and
\|J^{\prime}_{M}(u_{n})\|_{\ast}\to 0
.

For our purposes, we need to introduce the notion of distance between a point
u\in M
and a given set
C\subset X
. This can be done by setting

{\rm dist}(u,C):=\inf_{v\in C}\|u-v\|_{X}.

Now we are ready to state and prove the main result of the sequence, which gives the existence of a sequence with the properties mentioned above as a corollary.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
k\geq 1
and let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a compact subset. In the above notations, let
\Gamma\subset C^{0}(K,M)
. Assume that

(3.1)

m:=\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in\partial K}J(\gamma(t))<c:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in K}J(\gamma(t)).

Moreover, assume that there exists
a\in(m,c)
such that
\Gamma
is invariant under homotopies that fix the sublevel set
J^{a}:=\{u\in M:\,J(u)<a\}
. Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
and for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that

(3.2)

\max_{t\in K}J(\gamma(t))<c+\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

there exists
u\in M
such that

(1)

|J(u)-c|<\varepsilon
,

(2)

\|J^{\prime}_{M}(u)\|_{\ast}<\sqrt{\varepsilon}

(3)

{\rm dist}(u,\gamma(K))<\sqrt{\varepsilon}
.

Remark 3.4
.

A related result was proved in Theorem
4,5
of
[
5
]
. However, some remarks are due

•

the result in
[
5
]
is stated for Hilbert a manifold only, while here we have a result for a more general Banach manifold.

•

Since we assume that
\Gamma
is invariant under homotopies which fix a certain sublevel set, not only a certain compact set, our result is much more flwxible for the applications. Note that, for example, in
[
2
]
, the authors need to start from a known solution to some limit problem, while here this technical obstacle is overcome. In other words, we can construct solutions to an equation with a potential
V
, which may be trivial, without using the existence of a solution for
V=0
. Therefore, Theorem
3.3
works both for the case
V\equiv 0
and for more a general potential.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Assume by contradiction that the statement is not true, or equivalently there exists
\varepsilon>0
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\max_{t\in K}J(\gamma(t))<c+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
and, for any
u
in the set

\Lambda_{\varepsilon}:=\{u\in M:\,|J(u)-c|<\varepsilon,\,{\rm dist}(u,\gamma(K))<\sqrt{\varepsilon}\},

we have
\|J^{\prime}_{M}(u)\|_{\ast}>\sqrt{\varepsilon}
.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
c-\varepsilon>a
.

Let
g_{\varepsilon}:M\to[0,1]
be a smooth function such that

g_{\varepsilon}(u)=\begin{cases}1\qquad\forall\,u\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon/2}\\
0\qquad\forall\,u\in M:\,|J(u)-c|\geq\varepsilon.\end{cases}

and let

W(u):=\begin{cases}-g(u)\frac{Y(u)}{\|Y(u)\|_{\ast}}\qquad\forall\,u\in\tilde{M}\\
0\qquad\forall\,u\in M\setminus\tilde{M}.\end{cases}

Then
W(u)\in T_{u}M
, for any
u\in M
.

It is known that, given
u\in M
, the associated flow
\eta
obtained by solving the Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\eta(s,u)

\displaystyle=W(\eta(s,u))

\displaystyle\eta(0,u)

\displaystyle=u,

is defined for any
s\in[0,\infty)
. Moreover,
J
is decreasing along the flow, since

\frac{d}{ds}J(\eta(s,u))=\langle J^{\prime}_{M}(\eta(s,u),W(\eta(s,u))\rangle\leq-\|J^{\prime}_{M}(\eta(s,u))\|_{\ast}\leq 0\qquad\forall\,u\in M,\,s\geq 0.

In particular,

\frac{d}{ds}J(\eta(s,u))\leq-\sqrt{\varepsilon}\qquad\forall\,u\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon},\,s\geq 0.

Moreover, for any
u\in M
and
s\geq 0
, we have

\|\eta(s,u)-u\|_{X}=\|\eta(s,u)-\eta(0,u)\|_{X}=\left\|\int_{0}^{s}\frac{d}{d\tau}\eta(\tau,u)d\tau\right\|_{X}=\left\|\int_{0}^{s}W(\tau,u)d\tau\right\|_{X}\leq\int_{0}^{s}\|W(\tau,u)\|_{X}d\tau\leq s.

This yields that, for any
u\in\gamma(K)
and
s\geq 0
, we have
{\rm dist}(\eta(s,u),\gamma(K))\leq s
, so in particular
{\rm dist}(\eta(s,u),\gamma(K))<\sqrt{\varepsilon}
if
s<\sqrt{\varepsilon}
.

As a consequence, taking
s_{0}:=\frac{3}{4}\sqrt{\varepsilon}>0
and
u\in\gamma(K)
such that
|J(u)-c|<\varepsilon
, we have

(3.3)

J(\eta(s_{0},u))=J(u)+\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\frac{d}{ds}J(\eta(s,u)ds\leq J(u)-s_{0}\sqrt{\varepsilon}<c+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-s_{0}\sqrt{\varepsilon}=c-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}<c.

Therefore, setting
\tilde{\gamma}(t):=\eta(s_{0},\gamma(t))
, we have
\max_{t\in K}J(\gamma(t))<c-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
.

On the other hand, we have
\tilde{\gamma}(t)=\gamma(t)
if
t\in K
is such that
J(\gamma(t))\leq c-\varepsilon
, so in particular

\max_{t\in\partial K}J(\tilde{\gamma}(t))=\max_{t\in\partial K}J(\gamma(t))\leq m.

Furthermore, due to the choice of
g
and
\varepsilon
, the function

H:[0,s_{0}]\times M\to M,\qquad H(s,u):=\eta(s,u)

is a homotopy which leaves the sublevel set
J^{a}
invariant. Hence this homotopy leaves
\Gamma
invariant, so that
\tilde{\gamma}(\cdotp)=H(s_{0},\gamma(\cdotp))\in\Gamma
, a contradiction.
∎

4.
The right inverse of a non-linear operator

Given
N\geq 2
,
p\in(1,N)
,
s\in(1,p]
,
\lambda>0
and
f\in X^{*}
, we want to solve the problem

(4.1)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u+|u|^{p-2}u+\lambda|u|^{s-2}u=f\qquad\text{in}\,X^{*},

\displaystyle u\in X,

where we have set
X:=W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Problem (
4.1
) has a variational structure. In fact, the solutions are the critical points of the functional

E_{f}(u):=\frac{1}{p}\|u\|^{p}+\frac{\lambda}{s}\|u\|^{s}_{s}-\langle f,u\rangle\qquad\forall\,u\in X.

The space
X
is endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{X}:=\|u\|+\|u\|_{s},\qquad\forall\,u\in X,

where we have set

\|u\|:=(\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}+{\rm sgn}(p-s)\|u\|_{p}^{p})^{1/p}=\begin{cases}(\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{p}+\|u\|_{p}^{p})^{1/p}\qquad\text{if}\,p\in(1,s)\\
\|\nabla u\|_{p}\qquad\text{if}\,p=s\end{cases}.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
p\in(1,N)
,
s\in(1,p]
,
\lambda>0
and
f\in X^{*}
. Then

(1)

there exists a unique solution
u:=\Psi_{\lambda}(f)\in X
to Problem (
4.1
). Moreover,
u
satisfies
E_{f}(u)=\min_{v\in X}E_{f}(v)
.

(2)

There exists a constant
C=C(p,q)>0
such that

(4.2)

\|u\|^{p}+\lambda\|u\|^{s}_{s}\leq C(\|f\|_{X^{*}}^{p^{\prime}}+\|f\|_{X^{*}}^{2})\qquad\forall\,f\in X^{*}.

(3)

The inverse
\Psi_{\lambda}:X^{*}\to X
is continuous with respect to the norms
\|\cdotp\|_{X^{*}}
and
\|\cdotp\|_{X}
.

Proof.

(1)

It is possible to see that the functional
E_{f}
is coercive on
X
, since, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle E_{f}(u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{p}\|u\|^{p}+\frac{\lambda}{s}\|u\|^{s}_{s}-\|u\|_{X}\|f\|_{X^{*}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Young's}}}{{\geq}}\frac{1}{p}\|u\|^{p}+\frac{\lambda}{s}\|u\|^{s}_{s}-\frac{\varepsilon^{p}}{p}\|u\|^{p}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{p^{\prime}}p^{\prime}}\|f\|^{p^{\prime}}_{X^{*}}-\frac{\varepsilon^{s}}{s}\|u\|^{s}_{s}-\frac{1}{s^{\prime}\varepsilon^{s^{\prime}}}\|f\|^{s^{\prime}}_{X^{*}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon^{p}}{p}\|u\|^{p}+\frac{\lambda-\varepsilon^{s}}{s}\|u\|^{s}_{s}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{p^{\prime}}p^{\prime}}\|f\|^{p^{\prime}}_{X^{*}}-\frac{1}{s^{\prime}\varepsilon^{s^{\prime}}}\|f\|^{s^{\prime}}_{X^{*}},

Therefore, coercivity can be proved by choosing
\varepsilon>0
small enough.

Moreover, the functional
E_{f}
is continuous with respect to the norm
\|\cdotp\|_{X}
and strictly convex. Hence it follows that it has a unique critical point, which coincides with the global minimizer.

(2)

Testing the equation with
u
we can see that, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we hvae

\|u\|^{p}+\lambda\|u\|_{s}^{s}=\langle f,u\rangle\leq\|u\|_{X}\|f\|_{X^{*}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Young's}}}{{\leq}}\frac{\varepsilon^{p}}{p}\|u\|^{p}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{p^{\prime}}p^{\prime}}\|f\|_{X*}^{p^{\prime}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{s}}{s}\|u\|^{s}_{s}+\frac{1}{s^{\prime}\varepsilon^{s^{\prime}}}\|f\|_{X*}^{s^{\prime}}

Taking
\varepsilon>0
so small enough, estimate (
4.2
) is true.

(3)

In order to show continuity, we take a sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k}\subset X^{*}
such that
f_{k}\to f
in
X^{*}
and we show that
u_{k}:=\Psi_{\lambda}(f_{k})\to u:=\Psi_{\lambda}(f)
in
X
. In order to do so, we show that any subsequence
\{f_{k_{j}}\}_{j}
admits a subsubsequence such that
u_{k_{j_{i}}}\to u
.

Let us consider a subsequence, still denoted by
\{f_{k}\}_{k}
. We note that
f_{k}
is bounded in
X^{*}
, since
f_{k}\to f
in
X^{*}
. As a consequence, estimate (
4.2
) yields that
u_{k}
is bounded in
X
. Therefore, by the weak compactness of the ball in the reflexive Banach spaces
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we can extract a subsequence, namely
\{u_{k_{j}}\}_{j}
, such that
u_{k_{j}}\rightharpoonup v
weakly in
X
, for some
v\in X
.

The weak convergence yields that
v
also solves problem (
4.1
), hence
v=u
, due to uniqueness. Since every subsequence of
\{u_{k}\}
has a further subsequence weakly converging to
u
, the entire sequence satisfies
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
X
.

It remains to prove that
u_{k}\to u
strongly in
X
. Testing the equation satisfied by
u_{k}
with
u_{k}
and the one satisfied by
u
with
u
we get

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|u_{k}\|^{p}+\lambda\|u_{k}\|^{s}_{s}

\displaystyle=\langle f_{k},u_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\|u\|^{p}+\lambda\|u\|^{s}_{s}

\displaystyle=\langle f,u\rangle

Taking the difference, we have

\|u_{k}\|^{p}-\|u\|^{p}+\lambda(\|u_{k}\|^{s}_{s}-\|u\|^{s}_{s})=\langle f_{k},u_{k}\rangle-\langle f,u\rangle=o_{k}(1),

since

\langle f_{k},u_{k}\rangle-\langle f,u\rangle=\langle(f_{k}-f),u_{k}\rangle+\langle f,(u_{k}-u)\rangle=o_{k}(1),

due to the fact that

weakly in

and
f_{k}\to f
strongly in

.

Moreover, the Brezis-Lieb Lemma yields that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{k}\|^{p}_{p}-\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{p}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla(u_{k}-u)\|^{p}_{p}+o_{k}(1)

\displaystyle\|u_{k}\|^{t}_{t}-\|u\|^{t}_{t}

\displaystyle=\|u_{k}-u\|^{t}_{t}+o_{k}(1),\qquad\forall\,t\in\{s\}\cup[p,p^{*}]

as a consequence

This concludes the proof.

∎

5.
The proof of Theorem

Note that the weak solutions to equation

2.2
of

9
]

5.1.
Symmetry

However, due to the symmetric criticality principle, namely Proposition
2.1
of
[
9
]

More precisely, the setting of

9
]

Let

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle:=\{u\in B:\,\pi(g)u=u,\,\forall\,g\in G\},

\displaystyle\Sigma_{*}

\displaystyle:=\{v^{*}\in B^{*}:\,\langle v^{*},u-\pi(g)u\rangle=0,\,\forall\,g\in G\}.

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\bot}

\displaystyle:=\{v^{*}\in B^{*}:\,\langle v^{*},u\rangle=0,\,\forall\,u\in\Sigma\}.

In these notations, the following statement holds.

(
[
9

)

If

5.2.
Mountain pass geometry

In this section we prove that
J_{V}
has the mountain-pass geometry. The proof follows the outlines of Section
2.2
of our first paper.

First let us recall the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, which asserts that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(5.3)

\|u\|_{q}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{\theta}\|u\|_{s}^{1-\theta}\qquad\forall\,u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,\theta:=\frac{pN(q-s)}{q(p(N+s)-sN)}.

We note that
\theta\in(0,1)
since
s\leq p<q<p^{*}
.

Let us denote, for
k>0
,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}:=\sup_{u\in D_{k}}J_{V}(u);\quad\beta_{k}:=\inf_{u\in\partial D_{k}}J_{V}(u),

(5.5)

\displaystyle D_{k}:=\{u\in\mathcal{S}_{\rho,r}:\ \|u\|\leq k\},

where
\|\cdotp\|
denotes the standard
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-norm.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that either
V\in L^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\alpha\in[\frac{N}{p},\infty]
and
\|V^{-}\|_{\alpha}<S_{p,\alpha}
. Then for
\frac{N+s}{N}p<q<p^{*}
, there exist
0<k_{1}<k_{2}
such that

(5.6)

\displaystyle 0<\alpha_{k_{1}}<\beta_{k_{2}}.

and
\sup_{u\in D_{k_{1}}}J_{V}(u)>0
.

Proof.

i)

Let us consider the case
s\in(1,p)
and
\alpha\in[N/p,\infty]
or
s=p
and
\alpha=N/p
.

If
V\in L^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\alpha\in[\frac{N}{p},\infty]
, by the Hölder inequality and the Sobolev embedding
D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V^{-}(x)|u|^{p}dx\right|\leq\|V^{-}\|_{\alpha}\|u\|^{p}_{\frac{p\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\leq S_{p,\alpha}^{-1}\|V^{-}\|_{\alpha}\|u\|^{p}\qquad\forall\,u\in\mathcal{S}_{\rho,r}.

As a consequence, writing
V=V^{+}-V^{-}
, we have

(5.7)

\displaystyle J_{V}(u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{p}(1-S_{p,\alpha}^{-1}\|V^{-}\|_{\alpha})\|u\|^{p}-\frac{1}{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{p}(1-S_{p,\alpha}^{-1}\|V^{-}\|_{\alpha})\|u\|^{p}-\frac{C^{q}}{q}\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{\theta q}\|u\|_{s}^{(1-\theta)q}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{p}(1-S_{p,\alpha}^{-1}\|V^{-}\|_{\alpha})\|u\|^{p}-\frac{C^{q}}{q}\rho^{(1-\theta)q}\|u\|^{\theta q}=f(\|u\|),\qquad\forall\,u\in\mathcal{S}_{\rho},

where we have set

We note that
f(0)=0
. Using that
1-S_{p,\alpha}^{-1}\|V^{-}\|_{\alpha}>0
, and
\theta q>p
, since
p\frac{N+s}{N}<q<p^{*}
, we can see that there exists a unique
t_{\rho}>0
such that

f(t_{\rho})=\max_{t\in(0,\infty)}f(t)>0

f
is strictly increasing in
(0,t_{\rho})
and strictly decreasing in
(t_{\rho},\infty)
.

As a consequence, (
5.7
) yields that, setting
k_{2}=t_{\rho}
, we have
\beta_{k_{2}}\geq f(t_{\rho})>0
and

for any

.

On the other hand, taking
k_{1}<\bar{k}_{1}:=\min\{(\frac{pf(t_{\rho})}{1+\|V^{+}\|_{\alpha}S^{-1}_{p,\alpha}})^{\frac{1}{p}},k_{2}\}
, we have

(5.8)

which concludes the proof.

•

Let us consider the case
\alpha\in(N/p,\infty]

.

In this case, we have

\displaystyle J_{V}(u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{p}(1-S_{p,\alpha}^{-1}\|V^{-}\|_{\alpha})\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{p}-\frac{C^{q}}{q}\rho^{(1-\theta)q}t^{\theta q}-\frac{\rho^{p}}{p}\|V^{-}\|_{\alpha}S^{-1}_{p,\alpha}

It is possible to see that, for any

On the other hand, taking

.

Lemma 5.9

\|u_{n}\|_{t}^{t}=\|u\|_{t}^{t}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|w^{j}\|_{t}^{t}+o_{n}(1),\qquad\forall\,t\in\{s\}\cup[p,p^{*})

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}\cdot\nabla(u_{n}-u)\varphi_{R}\,dx+\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}(u_{n}-u)\nabla u_{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{R}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{2R}}|u_{n}|^{p-2}u_{n}(u_{n}-u)\Bigl({\rm sgn}(p-s)+V(x)\Bigr)\varphi_{R}\,dx+\lambda\int_{B_{2R}}|u_{n}|^{s-2}u_{n}(u_{n}-u)\varphi_{R}\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{2R}}|u_{n}|^{q-2}u_{n}(u_{n}-u)\varphi_{R}\,dx=o_{n}(1).

The gradient and nonlinearity terms are controlled as in the proof of Lemma
2
, p-7 of
[

]
using the facts from
(V_{1})
or
(V_{2})
and
\psi_{n}^{1}\to 0
strongly in
L^{\bar{p}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ \bar{p}\in[p,p^{*})
.

Thus, the same argument gives:

\int_{B_{2R}(0)}|\nabla(u_{n}-u)|^{p}\varphi_{R}\,dx=o_{n}(1)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\nabla u_{n}\to\nabla u\text{ in }L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}\text{ and a.e.},

so in particular,
\nabla u_{n}\to\nabla u
pointwise a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Step 2. Brezis–Lieb and Vanishing of the potential term:

As a consequence, by Brezis–Lieb lemma, we obtain the following splitting as
n\to\infty
:

(5.29)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}+\|\nabla\psi_{n}^{1}\|_{p}^{p}+o_{n}(1),

(5.30)

\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|_{t}^{t}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{t}^{t}+\|\psi_{n}^{1}\|_{t}^{t}+o_{n}(1).

for any
t\in\{s\}\cup[p,p^{*})
.

Since
\psi_{n}^{1}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and the embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\subset L^{t}(\Omega)
is compact, for any bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
t\in[p,p^{*})
,
\psi_{n}^{1}\to 0
strongly in
L^{t}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for such
t
and pointwise a.e..
Therefore, in view of the assumptions
(V_{1})
(specifically that
V
vanishes at infinity),

(5.31)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|\psi_{n}^{1}|^{p}\,dx\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

This fact can be proved as follows. First we note that, since
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)=0
, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
R>0
such that
|V(x)|\leq\varepsilon
if
|x|\geq R
. As a consequence, there exists
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
and
n_{0}=n_{0}(\varepsilon)>0
such that, for any
n\geq n_{0}
and for any
M>1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\{V<M\}}V^{+}|\psi^{1}_{n}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\{V<M\}\cap B_{R}(0)}V^{+}|\psi^{1}_{n}|^{p}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)}V^{+}|\psi^{1}_{n}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq M\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\psi^{1}_{n}|^{p}dx+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\psi^{1}_{n}|^{p}dx\leq(M+C)\varepsilon.

Moreover, by the absolute continuity of the integral with respect to the measure of the integration set, there exists
\tilde{C}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
and
M_{0}=M_{0}(\varepsilon)>0
such that, for any
M\geq M_{0}
and for any
n
,

\displaystyle\int_{\{V>M\}}V^{+}|\psi^{1}_{n}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\{V>M\}}(V^{+})^{q^{+}}dx\right)^{1/q^{+}}\left(\int_{\{V>M\}}|\psi^{1}_{n}|^{\frac{pq^{+}}{q^{+}-1}}\right)^{\frac{q^{+}-1}{pq^{+}}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|\psi^{1}_{n}\|^{\frac{pq^{+}}{q^{+}-1}}_{\frac{pq^{+}}{q^{+}-1}}\leq c\varepsilon\|\psi^{1}_{n}\|^{\frac{pq^{+}}{q^{+}-1}}\leq\tilde{C}\varepsilon.

A similar argument applies to the term involving
V^{-}
. This concludes the proof of (
5.31

Consequently, the mixed term involving the potential separates:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u_{n}|^{p}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|\psi_{n}^{1}|^{p}\,dx+o_{n}(1)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u|^{p}\,dx+o_{n}(1).

Step 3. Decomposition of the Functional and Its Derivative:

Using the norm splitting and the vanishing potential term, we can decompose the energy functional

J_{V,\lambda}(u_{n})=J_{V,\lambda}(u)+I_{\lambda}(\psi_{n}^{1})+o_{n}(1),

where
I_{\lambda}
is the autonomous limit functional defined by

I_{\lambda}(w)=\frac{1}{p}\|w\|^{p}+\frac{\lambda}{s}\|w\|_{s}^{s}-\frac{1}{q}\|w\|_{q}^{q}.

Furthermore, since
u_{n}
is a Palais–Smale sequence (
J^{\prime}_{V,\lambda}(u_{n})\to 0
) and
u
is a critical point of
J_{V,\lambda}
(by weak limit properties), standard arguments in critical point theory (see
[
4

(5.32)

\langle I^{\prime}_{\lambda}(\psi_{n}^{1}),\psi^{1}_{n}\rangle\to 0\quad\text{as}\,n\to\infty.

Step 4. Analysis of the Remainder:
Note that since
\psi_{n}^{1}
is a Palais–Smale sequence for the autonomous functional
I_{\lambda}
.
Now if we assume that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{B_{r}(y)}|\psi^{1}_{n}|^{p}dx=0\qquad\forall r>0,

by Lemma I.
1
of
[

]

\psi^{1}_{n}\to 0
in

. Therefore, by (

o_{n}(1)=\langle I^{\prime}_{\lambda}(\psi^{1}_{n}),\psi^{1}_{n}\rangle=\|\psi^{1}_{n}\|^{p}+\lambda\|\psi^{1}_{n}\|^{s}_{s}+o_{n}(1),

which yields that
\psi^{1}_{n}\to 0
in strongly
X
, hence the proof is over.

Otherwise there exists a subsequence, still denoted
\psi^{1}_{n}
, a sequence of points

and

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{B_{r}(y_{n}^{1})}|\psi_{n}^{1}|^{p}\,dx>0.

Thus,

strongly in

. Define the shifted sequence

Step 5. Iteration:
Define for

\psi_{n}^{j}(x)=\psi_{n}^{j}(x)-w^{j-1}(x-y_{n}^{j-1})=u_{n}(x)-u(x)-w^{j-1}(x-y_{n}^{j-1}).

It is possible to find a sequence

\psi_{n}^{j}(x)\rightharpoonup w^{j}(x-y_{n}^{j}(x))

Summing the decompositions yields

The norm and energy identities follow directly from the iterated Brezis-Lieb splittings

J_{V,\lambda}(u_{n})=J_{V,\lambda}(u)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}I_{\lambda}(w^{j})+o_{n}(1).
