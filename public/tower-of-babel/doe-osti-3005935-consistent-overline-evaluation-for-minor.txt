1 Introduction
Following several successful prompt ν evaluations using CGMF [1], including consistent evaluations for
minor Pu isotopes [2], we detail in this report our efforts to perform a consistent ν evaluation for
minor U isotopes during FY25. Although we have not yet produced a finalized evaluation, we present
the progress that we have made towards such an evaluation for 232,233,234,236,237,239 U prompt ν. Our
milestone explicitly calls out evaluations for 233 U, 234 U, and 236 U, however, to better constrain the model
with reliable experimental ν data, we also include 235 U and 238 U in the evaluation procedure. Then, we
additionally produce evaluations for 232 U, 237 U and 239 U ν as a byproduct. Elsewhere, we will report
our efforts on a stand-alone 233 U ν evaluation.
 This report is organized in the following manner. In Sec. 2, we briefly outline the updates to CGMF
that were needed to be able to calculate all of these minor U fission reactions. The experimental data
overview is given in Sec. 3. The evaluation methodology and results are presented in Secs. 4 and 5,
respectively. Finally, we conclude and outline work for FY26 in Sec. 6.

2 CGMF Updates
A detailed description of the updates that were performed in CGMF to be able to run all minor U isotope
fission reactions has been given in [3]. Here, we just present a brief outline of the changes. We note that
the release version of CGMF is parametrized for 233 U(n,f), 234 U(n,f), 235 U(n,f), and 238 U(n,f), but there is
little consistency for the fission fragment initial conditions across these reactions, especially for the total
kinetic energy, TKE, parametrization. Here, we have updated parametrizations of the compound nuclei
to either all be the same or be dependent on the mass of the compound nucleus and updated the code
to be able to calculate those reactions that are not included in the release version.
 And overview of these updates is as follows:
 • Multi-chance fission probabilities were taken from fission calculations with an updated fission model
 in CoH [4]. As a separate 232 U(n,f) calculation was not performed, the multi-chance fission proba-
 bilities were taken to be the same as 233 U(n,f);
 • Pre-equilibrium probabilities were fit to systematics of those already included in the release version
 of CGMF;
 • Pre-neutron emission mass distributions were taken to be those of 234 U(n,f) for all fissioning sys-
 tems;

 • The total kinetic energy was assumed to be a function of the mass of the compound nucleus, and
 fit to systematics given in [5]. More details can be found on the initial parametrization in [6];
 • The excitation energy sharing parameter, RT , was taken to be a constant for the newly included
      reactions in CGMF, 232 U(n,f), 236 U(n,f), 237 U(n,f), and 239 U(n,f), with RT = 1.2; and

 • The spin cutoff parameterization was taken to be the default of α = 1.5Einc +0.071 for all compound
 nuclei.

3 Experimental Data
The experimental data as listed in Table 1 were included in the evaluation. Detailed experimental uncer-
tainty quantification was undertaken using EXFOR entries, the related literature, templates of expected
measurement uncertainties [7] and the code ARIADNE [8]. The individual data sets are described in
more detail in Refs. [6, 9, 10].

4 Evaluation Methodology
The Kalman filter method was employed to perform the evaluation. This technique yields a vector of
evaluated CGMF parameter mean values N and CovN ,

                                   N = p + CovN S t (Cove )−1 (e − Sp) ,
 CovN = Covp − Covp S t Q−1 SCovp , (1)

where
 Q = SCovp S t + Cove . (2)
Experimental mean values and covariances are denoted as e and Cove , respectively. Initial (prior) model
parameter values and their covariances are represented by p and Covp . The sensitivity vector, S, maps
from the space of ν p to model-parameter space. After the evaluation is performed, evaluated mean values
N are run through CGMF to obtain an evaluated ν.
 As in our previous ν evaluations, the experimental covariances were corrected for the effect known as
“Peelle’s Pertinent Puzzle”, where the evaluated mean value of strongly correlated experimental data lies
outside of the range of the experimental data due to an improperly formulated covariance matrix [11].
This correction was applied by converting Eqs. (1) and (2) to iteratively re-weighted least squares
following Ref. [12].
 The ARIADNE code package [8] has already been updated to evaluate multiple isotopes simultane-
ously through previous evaluations for consistent Pu ν [13, 2].

5 Evaluation Results
Two different model priors were provided for an evaluation of minor U isotopes ν using CGMF. The
first used as a starting point the TKE parametrization of [6]. Although this parametrization reasonably
reproduces the experimental TKE data as a function of incident neutron energy, the prior on ν is too
far away from the data to get a reasonable evaluation. To update the prior ν calculation (the second
model prior), we individually adjusted TKE to reproduce the ENDF/B-VIII.1 thermal ν values for each
 1/3
reaction in our optimization. We then performed a global fit for TKE as a function of Z 2 /Ac , as shown
in Fig. 1 (green dashed line). This updated parametrization was used to calculate the baseline ν values,
and then another full evaluation was performed. Only the initial model parameters and CGMF-predicted
ν values were changed for this second prior, while the sensitivities were fixed.
 Approximately a dozen evaluations were performed that are described here at a high level:
 1. When the first model prior was fit to experimental 232−239 U ν, only the evaluated 235,238 U ν below
 8 MeV are reasonably close to experimental data. Otherwise the evaluated ν coming directly out
 of ARIADNE were systematically smaller than experimental data.
 2. The assumption was that the initial model is too stiff to be able to fit ν across so many isotopes.
 Hence, a subset of isotopes were fit at one time. And, indeed better agreement could be reached
 for a few select isotopes.
 3. To further diagnose the main issue, several new priors were generated were the first initial model
 parameter values and sensitivities were used paired with ENDF/B-VIII.1 values that were gradually
 distorted. These test runs revealed that there were two issues at play to make it impossible to fit:
 It is the combination of a large offset of initial model ν values from experimental data and that
 the prior ν values have different-sized offsets across isotopes. These issues render it impossible to
 find evaluated ν that correspond to experimental data across all isotopes.

for the Pu minor isotope ν evaluation), which will be a focus for FY26. New sensitivities will have to be
calculated for this updated parametrization.

6 Conclusions and Future Work
We have performed several evaluations in fulfillment of the FY25 milestone to “attempt a consistent
nubar evaluation supported by a model code to provide better nu-bar data for minor U-isotopes”. While
we have not yet reached a final evaluation, we have performed several studies that are pointing us in the
right direction of the needed parametrization within CGMF.
 Although we include 235 U(n,f) and 238 U(n,f) in our evaluation, we do not intend to replace the
current ENDF/B-VIII.1 evaluations with the results of this consistent evaluation. These two reactions
are included to help constrain the CGMF parametrization. We also have performed a stand-alone 233 U
ν evaluation (detailed elsewhere) due to its importance for NCSP application and in conjunction with
cross section evaluations that have recently been performed.

 Finally, even though only 233 U, 234 U, and 236 U are called out explicitly in the NCSP five year plan,
we include 232 U, 237 U, and 239 U ν in our evaluation. There are limited–to no–data on these isotopes,
but our consistent modeling and evaluation framework provides for reliable results on these reactions at
no loss of quality for the isotopes called out directly. This work will continue in FY26, with a planned
finalization of the evaluation, including covariances.
