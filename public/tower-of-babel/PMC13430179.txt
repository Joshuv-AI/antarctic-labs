Development and application of a 5K targeted liquid SNP panel for Hetian chickens based on genotyping by targeted sequencing
Authors: Qiong Wu, Mengping Zhang, Ziwen Wang, Yuxing Wang, Yuming Chen, Zihao Zhang, Shini Lin, Xianghui Liang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The Hetian chicken is a nationally protected poultry genetic resource in China, possessing significant conservation value and high potential for commercial development. To facilitate the conservation and sustainable utilization of this valuable indigenous breed, we developed the first breed-specific 5K targeted liquid SNP panel for Hetian chickens using genotyping by targeted sequencing (GBTS) technology. Initially, whole-genome resequencing of 50 Hetian chickens yielded 16,433,661 single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs). Following strict quality control and filtering criteria—heterozygosity rate ≤ 50%, missing rate ≤ 10%, minor allele frequency (MAF) > 0.35, and a uniform physical distribution across the genome—5,781 core SNPs were retained to construct the conservation-specific panel. The performance of this panel was subsequently validated using 307 Hetian chicken samples. The results demonstrated a high average call rate of 99.9% and excellent genotyping performance. Furthermore, the panel exhibited robust capabilities for accurate clustering and kinship analyses. The kinship analysis findings further confirmed that this breed-specific panel holds substantial practical value for germplasm resource conservation and the implementation of molecular breeding strategies. Overall, this study highlights the technical advantages of a low-density, cost-effective, and breed-specific liquid SNP panel for precise genotyping, providing a crucial molecular tool to support the protection, genetic mining, and sustainable utilization of Hetian chicken resources.

Introduction

Recognized as a National Geographical Indication product in Fujian Province, China, the Hetian chicken is a nationally protected poultry genetic resource. It serves as a core germplasm in southern China, holding profound value for both biodiversity conservation and commercial development(Gu and Li, 2020). This breed is renowned for its exceptional meat flavor, characterized by abundant intramuscular fat (IMF) and significantly higher concentrations of umami-tasting amino acids—such as glutamic and aspartic acids—compared to commercial broilers(Deng et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2025). Beyond its premium meat quality, the Hetian chicken exhibits remarkable adaptation to the hot and humid environments typical of southern China, alongside robust disease resistance and a high tolerance for low-quality feed(Zhu et al., 2024). Despite these valuable traits, driven by the rapid commercialization of the poultry industry, the purebred Hetian chicken population is currently facing severe challenges from genetic introgression. Recent studies indicate that genetic admixture within nucleus conservation flocks has reached 8% to 12%. This introgression not only threatens the stability of its unique genetic architecture but also accelerates the degradation of its genetic purity(Deng et al., 2022; Qi et al., 2024). Consequently, it is imperative to establish highly efficient strategies for the genetic conservation and sustainable utilization of the Hetian chicken.

Driven by rapid advances in genomic technologies, marker-assisted selection (MAS) has substantially improved selection efficiency and accuracy by identifying genetic loci linked to target traits. This approach is facilitating a paradigm shift in Hetian chicken breeding—from traditional phenotype-based evaluation to precise molecular breeding. Consequently, MAS effectively overcomes the inherent limitations of conventional selection, such as extended generation intervals, susceptibility to environmental variations, and low phenotypic accuracy(Negash et al., 2021; Panigrahi et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2024). Among various molecular genetic markers, single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) are universally recognized as the preferred third-generation markers. This prominence is attributed to their high abundance, extensive whole-genome coverage, robust genetic stability, and amenability to automated high-throughput genotyping(Alemu et al., 2025; Husien et al., 2024). Currently, SNP markers serve as essential tools for genome-wide association studies (GWAS), genomic selection (GS), kinship estimation, and population structure analyses. They provide robust technical support for the genetic dissection of economically important traits and the efficient evaluation of poultry germplasm resources, thereby offering novel avenues for the genetic conservation and selective breeding of Hetian chickens(Deng et al., 2022; Guo et al., 2021; Husien et al., 2024).

As SNP genotyping technologies continuously evolve, targeted liquid SNP panels based on genotyping by targeted sequencing (GBTS) have emerged as a mainstream, highly efficient, and cost-effective alternative to WGS(Ren et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023). The GBTS framework comprises two independent yet complementary technological systems: GenoPlexs and GenoBaits. Its core methodology involves the amplification of target fragments via multiplex PCR, followed by the precise capture of target genomic regions using specific probes. This process is then coupled with next-generation sequencing (NGS) to achieve high-depth sequencing and precise genotyping at specific loci(Xu et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2023). Conventional solid-phase arrays are often hindered by inherent limitations, such as fixed marker content, extended initial development cycles, and prohibitive startup costs. In contrast, GBTS-based liquid panels successfully integrate the ultra-high throughput of NGS with the accuracy of targeted capture. They exhibit remarkable superiority in multiple dimensions, including broad adaptability, cross-platform compatibility, high operational efficiency, flexibility in marker design, analytical sensitivity, and overall cost-effectiveness(Guan et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024). To date, this technological framework has been broadly implemented across plant and livestock breeding programs, with successful applications in species such as maize, soybean, cotton, wheat, dairy goats, and cattle (Guo et al., 2021; Qiao et al., 2017; T. Zhang et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2025).

While mature commercial chicken SNP arrays—such as the Illumina 60K (Groenen et al., 2011), Axiom 600K (Kranis et al., 2013), and Phoenix Chip-I (Liu et al., 2021)—are available internationally, their direct application in Hetian chicken conservation and breeding has distinct limitations. First, they are subject to ascertainment bias. Because existing arrays were predominantly designed based on Western commercial breeds, they often fail to capture the unique genetic variants specific to the Hetian chicken. Second, there is a prominent mismatch between marker density and cost-efficiency. High-density arrays are often prohibitively expensive for large-scale, routine applications; conversely, universal low-density panels lack breed-specific loci, thereby failing to provide the analytical precision required for effective germplasm conservation (Gao et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023). Given the absence of a dedicated low-density panel for this breed, the development of a cost-effective, highly accurate, and breed-specific targeted liquid SNP panel is of paramount importance. Such a tool holds profound scientific and practical value for mitigating genetic erosion, enhancing selection efficiency within the breed, and ultimately driving the sustainable upgrading of the local poultry industry.

Materials and methods

Whole-genome resequencing

Workflow for the development and validation of the 5K liquid SNP panel for Hetian chickens.

Sample Collection and Whole-Genome Sequencing. All animal care and experimental procedures were approved by the Animal Experimental Ethics Committee of Longyan University (Approval No. LY2025010L). Blood sample collection was performed strictly with efforts made to minimize stress and discomfort to the animals.Whole blood samples were collected from 50 unrelated Hetian chickens (25 males and 25 females, aged 120 days) from a core breeding farm in Changting County, Longyan City, Fujian Province, China. These birds constituted the discovery set, with no overlapping individuals in the subsequent validation set. Genomic DNA was extracted using a high-throughput DNA extraction kit (MolBreeding Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shijiazhuang, China). The integrity of the extracted DNA and potential degradation were assessed via agarose gel electrophoresis, while precise quantification of DNA concentration was performed using a Qubit 2.0 Fluorometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). DNA samples meeting the quality criteria were subsequently utilized for sequencing library construction using the GenoBaits DNA Library Prep Kit (MolBreeding Biotechnology Co., Ltd.). Following library preparation, the concentrations were initially quantified with the Qubit 2.0 Fluorometer and further accurately quantified via quantitative PCR (qPCR). Finally, the qualified libraries were subjected to 150-bp paired-end (PE150) sequencing on a DNBSEQ-T7 platform (BGI Genomics, Shenzhen, China) to generate the raw sequencing data.

Data Processing and Variant Calling. To ensure analytical reliability, the raw sequencing reads were subjected to rigorous quality control and filtering using Fastp (v0.20.0). The filtering parameters were set to discard reads containing adapter sequences, reads with more than 10 uncalled bases (N), and reads in which over 40% of the bases had a Phred quality score below 20 (Q < 20). The resulting high-quality clean reads were subsequently aligned to the chicken reference genome (GRCg7b) utilizing the BWA-MEM algorithm (Li, 2013). Finally, SNP calling was performed using the standard pipeline within the Sentieon software suite.

Design of the Liquid SNP Panel. The panel development process comprised three stages: SNP screening, probe design, and distribution assessment. Initially, detected SNPs underwent preliminary screening (sequencing depth ≥ 5X, missing rate <50%, MAF> 0.05, retaining only biallelic SNPs and removing InDels). Subsequently, to meet strict conservation requirements, core loci were further optimized based on: heterozygosity rate ≤ 50%, missing rate ≤ 10%, MAF ≥ 0.35, and an even genomic distribution.

Capture probes were then designed for these optimized loci using the following criteria: probe length of 110 bp, GC content of 30%–70%, and ≤ 5 homologous genomic regions. The homology rules were defined as: exact sequence match > 40 bp; OR sequence similarity > 85% with a match length > 80 bp; OR sequence similarity > 95% with a match length > 70 bp. These parameters were based on empirical settings widely used in GBTS (Genotyping by Target Sequencing) panel development, which effectively balance probe capture efficiency and target specificity. Finally, the designed probes were synthesized for subsequent performance testing."

Genomic Distribution and Functional Annotation of SNPs. The chromosomal distribution of the targeted SNPs was determined by quantifying the number of markers on each chromosome. To evaluate the uniformity of this distribution, SNP densities were calculated using 100-kb sliding windows across the genome. Additionally, a gap distribution analysis was conducted by measuring the physical distance between adjacent targeted fragments (i.e., from the end coordinate of an upstream region to the start coordinate of the subsequent downstream region). A “gap” was formally defined as any genomic interval where this physical distance exceeded 10 times the theoretical average inter-marker distance (calculated as the total genome size divided by the total number of targeted markers). Visualizations of these genomic distributions were generated using the ggplot2 package (v3.5.1) in R. Finally, to facilitate downstream analyses, functional annotation of the retained variant loci was performed using ANNOVAR software, incorporating the latest reference genome annotation databases.

Validation of the targeted genotyping panel

Library Construction and Targeted Sequencing of the Liquid SNP Panel. A total of 307 Hetian chickens (40 males and 267 females, all 120 days of age) were selected as the validation cohort. Importantly, this validation cohort was strictly independent of the discovery cohort used for the aforementioned whole-genome resequencing, with zero individual overlap. Blood sample collection and genomic DNA extraction were performed following the protocol described in Section 2.1. Genotyping of these samples was conducted utilizing our custom-designed, GBTS-based liquid SNP panel. Initially, the genomic DNA was subjected to enzymatic fragmentation, end-repair, and A-tailing. Sequencing adapters were subsequently ligated to the fragments, followed by purification using magnetic beads. Next, PCR amplification was performed using barcode-indexed primers to enable sample multiplexing and precise identification. For the target enrichment phase (hybridization capture), 500 ng of the amplified products were utilized. The libraries were lyophilized and then hybridized with the custom target-specific probes at 65°C for 2 hours. Following washing steps to elute and remove unbound, off-target fragments, a second round of PCR amplification was performed to finalize the construction of the captured libraries. Upon passing strict quality and quantitative assessments using the Qubit 2.0 Fluorometer and qPCR, the final libraries were subjected to high-throughput sequencing on the designated sequencing platform.

Variant Calling and Panel Performance Evaluation. The computational pipelines for raw data filtering and sequence alignment were identical to those outlined for the whole-genome sequencing section (refer to Section 2.1.2). Subsequent variant calling was executed using the Genome Analysis Toolkit (GATK) software. To systematically evaluate the overall performance of the customized liquid panel, several genotyping metrics were calculated for each targeted locus across the 307 validation samples. These evaluated parameters included the call rate, missing rate (NA rate), heterozygosity rate (Het rate), alternative homozygous rate (Hom rate), reference homozygous rate (Ref rate), and the minor allele frequency (MAF).

Conservation analysis utilizing the GBTS liquid SNP panel

To evaluate the utility of the targeted liquid SNP panel for germplasm conservation, a systematic genetic diversity analysis was conducted using the genotyping data from 307 Hetian chickens. Initially, quality control and filtering of the raw genomic data were performed using VCFtools (v0.1.16) and PLINK (v1.90) based on the following criteria: (1) excluding insertion/deletion (InDel) variants and non-autosomal markers; (2) retaining only loci with a sequencing depth ≥ 5 ×; (3) removing SNPs with a missing rate > 10% (i.e., genotyping call rate < 90%); and (4) discarding SNPs with a minor allele frequency (MAF) < 0.05. Using the retained high-quality SNPs, key genetic parameters including observed heterozygosity (Ho), expected heterozygosity (He), MAF, and the inbreeding coefficient (FIS) were calculated. Additionally, the polymorphism information content (PIC) and Nei’s gene diversity index were computed according to standard formulas.

To ascertain the population structure, principal component analysis (PCA) was performed using GCTA software, and a Neighbor-Joining (NJ) clustering tree was constructed using MEGA-X. Linkage disequilibrium (LD) decay was evaluated by calculating the squared correlation coefficient (r²) between marker pairs using PopLDdecay (v3.42). Furthermore, runs of homozygosity (ROH) were identified utilizing PLINK, and a genomic relationship matrix (G-matrix) was constructed via GCTA to assess the genetic relatedness among individuals.

Following rigorous quality control and filtering based on the predefined criteria (detailed in the Materials and Methods section), a final set of 5,781 core SNPs was retained. These markers were subsequently utilized to construct a customized 5K targeted liquid SNP panel for the Hetian chicken. Validation of the 5K GBTS liquid SNP panel

To validate the performance of the customized liquid panel, targeted sequencing was performed on 307 Hetian chicken samples. Following fundamental quality control, the clean read rate per sample ranged from 89.68% to 99.27% (average: 97.83%). The proportions of bases with Phred quality scores of Q20 and Q30 were 98.98%–99.77% and 92.49%–96.46%, respectively. Alignment of the high-quality reads to the reference genome yielded an average mapping rate of 99.45%. These excellent metrics indicate that the sequencing data are stable, reliable, and fully satisfy the prerequisites for subsequent downstream analyses.

We further evaluated the genotyping efficacy of the 5K panel across all samples. During variant calling, a strict sequencing depth threshold of ≥ 5 × was applied to filter out low-coverage loci and individuals. Analysis of this quality-controlled dataset revealed exceptional genotyping call rates ranging from 99.91% to 100%, with corresponding missing rates (no-call rates) of only 0%–0.64%. The genotype distributions were as follows: heterozygosity rate, 34.60%–81.60%; alternate allele homozygous rate, 8.64%–37.84%; and reference homozygous rate, 9.77%–31.69% (Fig. 6).Fig. 6Statistical summary of the variant genotypes. Discussion

Optimization of SNP panel design based on high-polymorphism marker selection

The efficacy of molecular characterization in indigenous livestock and poultry breeds relies heavily on the informativeness of the selected genetic markers. Most current commercial chicken SNP arrays are designed based on commercial lines developed in Europe and the Americas. Consequently, applying these arrays to Chinese indigenous breeds often introduces ascertainment bias, leading to reduced polymorphism detection, skewed allele frequencies, and compromised accuracy in conservation evaluations(Geibel et al., 2021; Groenen et al., 2011; Wang et al., 2025).

To address this limitation, we developed a customized 5K liquid SNP panel specifically for the Hetian chicken utilizing a minor allele frequency (MAF)-based screening strategy. According to the polymorphism information content (PIC) theory, markers with an MAF approaching 0.5 provide the highest discriminatory power among individuals(Botstein et al., 1980). Given the capacity constraints of a low-density panel, rare variants (MAF < 0.05) were excluded. The retention of common, high-frequency loci improves the accuracy of kinship estimation and enhances sensitivity to exogenous introgression—a screening rationale consistent with findings from custom low-density panels designed for indigenous pigs(Gao et al., 2022; Shashkova et al., 2020; vonHoldt et al., 2011). Furthermore, while prolonged artificial selection has driven numerous loci to fixation in commercial lines, indigenous chickens typically harbor and maintain high levels of heterozygosity(Groeneveld et al., 2010).

Although high-density arrays are ideal for genome-wide association studies (GWAS), their prohibitive genotyping costs render them impractical for routine, population-wide monitoring in conservation programs. By strictly selecting highly polymorphic loci uniformly distributed across the genome, our 5K marker density strikes an optimal balance between cost-effectiveness and statistical power. This panel maximizes PIC and individual resolution, proving highly sufficient for accurately deciphering fine-scale inbreeding structures and identifying exotic gene introgression. Finally, our empirical validation—demonstrated by a >99.9% genotyping call rate and clear population clustering in principal component analysis—confirms that this 5K panel can robustly and reliably meet the practical demands of Hetian chicken conservation management.

Mitigating cryptic genetic introgression in germplasm conservation

Cryptic genetic introgression from exotic commercial breeds represents a primary bottleneck in the conservation of Hetian chickens. Although phenotypic monitoring indicates a suspected admixture rate of 8%–12% within certain nucleus flocks, traditional morphological evaluation fails to accurately identify these admixed individuals at an early stage(Groeneveld et al., 2010). Our custom 5K liquid target-capture SNP panel effectively circumvents these limitations. Sourced directly from the whole-genome resequencing data of Hetian chickens, the selected markers exclude conserved, low-polymorphism loci across breeds while retaining high-frequency variants specific to the Hetian population. Consequently, individuals harboring exogenous genomic segments will exhibit pronounced deviations in genetic distance (Liu et al., 2019).

Compared with commercial solid-state arrays, this liquid panel demonstrates superior polymorphism within indigenous flocks, thereby providing higher resolution for delineating breed-specific genetic architectures. Leveraging genotyping by targeted sequencing (GBTS) technology, the per-sample genotyping cost of our panel is drastically reduced to $10–$15. This is considerably lower than that of commercial arrays ($40–$50) and represents less than one-tenth the cost of whole-genome resequencing, all while maintaining a genotype concordance rate exceeding 99% (Liu et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2020). Such cost-effectiveness aligns perfectly with the economic model proposed by Meuwissen et al(Meuwissen et al., 2001). for implementing affordable genomic selection in small populations.

Furthermore, the liquid capture platform offers the distinct advantage of flexibly updating probe combinations to accommodate long-term germplasm monitoring(Zhao et al., 2025). To mitigate potential batch effects arising from probe iterations, we propose establishing a fixed core set of 3,000 highly informative SNPs. When coupled with genotype imputation via the GLIMPSE software, this strategy ensures technical adaptability while seamlessly harmonizing genotypic data across multiple experimental batches.

Evaluation and management of the genetic structure in hetian chicken conservation populations

Population genomic analysis of 307 Hetian chickens utilizing our custom 5K panel provides a high-resolution evaluation of current conservation efficacy. Both principal component analysis (PCA) and the neighbor-joining (NJ) phylogenetic tree revealed a highly uniform genetic background devoid of pronounced subpopulation stratification. This confirms that existing closed-flock conservation measures have effectively maintained the breed’s overall genetic integrity(X. Zhang et al., 2024).

However, in-depth analyses leveraging the genomic relationship matrix (G-matrix) and runs of homozygosity (ROH) revealed latent risks. Although the median inbreeding coefficient remains relatively low, significant familial clustering was observed among certain individuals. Furthermore, while the current effective population size (Ne) remains above the short-term survival threshold, it exhibits a gradual downward trajectory across recent generations(Tolone et al., 2023).

These findings imply that, despite the successful containment of genetic admixture, the disproportionate proliferation of a few dominant families risks driving the accumulation of cryptic inbreeding. Left unchecked, this could ultimately trigger inbreeding depression and deteriorate productive performance(Zhang et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2023). Because traditional pedigree records frequently underestimate true genetic relatedness, the high-precision genomic profiles generated by our panel are essential for directly informing and optimizing mating designs.

For future conservation management, we highly recommend integrating the Optimal Contribution Selection (OCS) strategy. By employing the array-derived G-matrix, breeders can maximize genetic diversity while rigorously constraining the rate of inbreeding. This approach will facilitate a crucial paradigm shift from traditional “empirical conservation” to genome-assisted “precision conservation” (Zhao et al., 2021).

Conclusions

In conclusion, based on whole-genome resequencing data from Hetian chicken conservation populations and a rigorous marker screening pipeline, we successfully identified 5,781 highly polymorphic SNPs that are uniformly distributed across the genome. These markers were subsequently utilized to develop a breed-specific 5K liquid SNP panel powered by GBTS technology. Exhibiting a high average genotype call rate of 99.9%, this customized panel provides a robust tool for implementing genomic-informed mating schemes and managing genetic purity within the conservation flocks. Ultimately, the successful development of this low-density liquid panel not only demonstrates the efficacy of GBTS technology in preserving indigenous poultry genetic resources, but also offers a cost-effective and replicable paradigm for the conservation of local livestock and poultry breeds in other developing countries.
