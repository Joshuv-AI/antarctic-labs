Generalized i i -boson model and boxed BUC plane partitions Thanks: *Corresponding author. Email: yanzw@imu.edu.cn (Z.W. Yan)
Shengyu Zhang, Denghui Li and Zhaowen Yan ∗

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to investigating the relation between the generalized i i -boson model and boxed BUC plane partitions. The representation of the generalized i i -boson algebra and the actions of the monodromy matrix operators on basis vectors have been studied. We also consider the actions of neutral fermion vertex operators on state vectors in terms of the neutral fermionic Fock space. With the help of the scalar product of the generalized i i -boson model, the generating function for boxed BUC plane partitions is derived which can be represented as the products of Schur Q Q -functions. Moreover, the generating function for BUC plane partitions with the double scaling limit is presented. Keywords : Quantum Inverse Scattering Method; quantum integrable models; generalized i i -boson model; generating functions of plane partitions Mathematics Subject Classifications (2000) : 17B80, 35Q55, 37K10

2.1.
Plane partitions

A (strict) partition
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots)
is (strict) decreasing non-negative integers sequence with the weight
|\mu|=\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}\mu_{i}

\mu

A pair of partitions

\displaystyle\ \mu_{1}\geq\nu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\nu_{2}\geq\ldots,

\displaystyle(\tilde{\chi}_{1})\succ(\tilde{\chi}_{2})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\tilde{\mu}^{1}\succ\tilde{\mu}^{2}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\tilde{\nu}^{1}\succ\tilde{\nu}^{2}.

(2.3)

Plane partition, also known as a
3

(3D)

\pi_{ij}

\displaystyle\pi_{ij}\geq\pi_{(i+1)j},\quad\pi_{ij}\geq\pi_{i(j+1)},\quad\lim_{i\to\infty}\pi_{ij}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\pi_{ij}=0,\quad\text{for}~i,j\geq 1.

(2.4)

Slicing diagonally yields a series of interlacing partitions

\displaystyle\emptyset=\pi_{-M}\prec\cdots\prec\pi_{-2}\prec\pi_{-1}\prec\pi_{0}\succ\pi_{1}\succ\pi_{2}\succ\cdots\succ\pi_{N}=\emptyset,

\displaystyle\pi_{i}=\begin{cases}(\pi_{1(i+1)},\pi_{2(i+2)},\pi_{3(i+3)},\ldots)&\quad\text{for}~i\geq 0,\\

(2.6)

2.2.
Neutral fermions and state vectors

Consider neutral fermions sets
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}
and
\{\bar{\phi}_{m}\}_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}
generated by the relations

\displaystyle\phi_{n}=\psi_{n}+(-1)^{n}\psi_{-n}^{*},\quad\bar{\phi}_{m}=\gamma_{m}+(-1)^{m}\gamma_{-m}^{*},

(2.10)

where charged fermions

satisfy

\displaystyle[\psi_{m},\psi_{n}]_{+}=[\psi_{m}^{*},\psi_{n}^{*}]_{+}=0,[\psi_{m},\psi_{n}^{*}]_{+}=\delta_{m+n,0},

(2.11)

\displaystyle[\gamma_{m},\gamma_{n}]_{+}=[\gamma_{m}^{*},\gamma_{n}^{*}]_{+}=0,[\gamma_{m},\gamma_{n}^{*}]_{+}=\delta_{m+n,0},

(2.12)

\displaystyle[\psi_{m},\gamma_{n}]=[\psi_{m},\gamma_{n}^{*}]=[\psi_{m}^{*},\gamma_{n}]=[\psi_{m}^{*},\gamma_{n}^{*}]=0,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\psi_{n}^{2}=\psi_{n}^{*2}=\gamma_{m}^{2}=\gamma_{m}^{*2}=0,

(2.14)

and

\displaystyle[A,B]=AB-BA,\quad[A,B]_{+}=AB+BA,\quad\delta_{m+n,0}=\begin{cases}1,&m+n=0,\\
0,&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2.15)

The algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
over

\mathbb{C}
is generated by
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}
and
\{\bar{\phi}_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}
satisfying

\displaystyle[\phi_{m},\phi_{n}]_{+}=[\bar{\phi}_{m},\bar{\phi}_{n}]_{+}=2(-1)^{m}\delta_{m+n,0},\quad[\phi_{m},\bar{\phi}_{n}]=0,\quad\phi_{m}^{2}=\bar{\phi}_{m}^{2}=\delta_{m,0}.

(2.16)

Introduce the neutral fermionic Fock space
\mathcal{\widetilde{F}}
and the dual Fock space
\mathcal{\widetilde{F}}^{*}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{\widetilde{F}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\cdot|0\rangle=\left\{a|0\rangle|a\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{\widetilde{F}}^{*}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\langle 0|\cdot\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}=\{\langle 0|a\mid a\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\},

(2.17)

where the vacuum state
|0\rangle
and the dual vacuum state
\langle 0|
are denoted as

\displaystyle\phi_{n}|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\bar{\phi}_{n}|0\rangle=0\quad\text{for }n<0,

(2.18)

\displaystyle\langle 0|\phi_{n}

\displaystyle=\langle 0|\bar{\phi}_{n}=0\quad\text{for }n>0.

(2.19)

Define the bilinear pairing
\mathcal{\widetilde{F}}^{*}\times\mathcal{\widetilde{F}}\longmapsto\mathbb{C}
as

\displaystyle(\langle 0|a,b|0\rangle)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\langle 0|a\cdot b|0\rangle=\langle ab\rangle,

(2.20)

with
\langle 0|0\rangle=1
.
The operators
\lambda_{m}^{{}^{\prime}}
and
\bar{\lambda}_{m}^{{}^{\prime}}
(
m\in\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{odd}}
) are expressed as

\displaystyle\lambda_{m}^{{}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}(-1)^{j}\phi_{j}\phi_{-j-m},\quad\bar{\lambda}_{m}^{{}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}(-1)^{1}\bar{\phi}_{j}\bar{\phi}_{-j-m}.

(2.21)

Then

\displaystyle[\lambda_{m}^{{}^{\prime}},\phi_{n}]=\phi_{n-m},\quad[\bar{\lambda}_{m}^{{}^{\prime}},\bar{\phi}_{n}]=\bar{\phi}_{n-m},\quad[\lambda_{m}^{{}^{\prime}},\lambda^{{}^{\prime}}_{n}]=[\bar{\lambda}_{m}^{{}^{\prime}},\bar{\lambda}^{{}^{\prime}}_{n}]=\frac{m}{2}\delta_{m+n,0},

(2.22)

and

\displaystyle\lambda^{{}^{\prime}}_{m}|0\rangle=\bar{\lambda}^{{}^{\prime}}_{m}|0\rangle=0\quad\text{if }m>0.

(2.23)

For convenience, set

\displaystyle\phi_{n}^{*}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\phi_{-n}=\psi_{n}^{*}+(-1)^{n}\psi_{-n},

(2.24)

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}_{m}^{*}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}\bar{\phi}_{-m}=\gamma_{m}^{*}+(-1)^{m}\gamma_{-m}.

(2.25)

The state vectors in
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
identified by
\tilde{\mu}^{j}=(\tilde{\mu}_{1}^{j},\tilde{\mu}_{2}^{j},\cdots,\tilde{\mu}_{2r_{j}}^{j})
are defined as

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{\mu}^{2},\tilde{\mu}^{1}|

\displaystyle=\langle 0|\bar{\phi}_{\tilde{\mu}^{2}_{2r_{2}}}^{*}\dots\bar{\phi}_{\tilde{\mu}^{2}_{1}}^{*}\phi_{\tilde{\mu}^{1}_{2r_{1}}}^{*}\dots\phi_{\tilde{\mu}^{1}_{1}}^{*},

(2.26)

\displaystyle|\tilde{\mu}^{1},\tilde{\mu}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\phi_{\tilde{\mu}^{1}_{1}}\dots\phi_{\tilde{\mu}^{1}_{2r_{1}}}\bar{\phi}_{\tilde{\mu}^{2}_{1}}\dots\bar{\phi}_{\tilde{\mu}^{2}_{2r_{2}}}|0\rangle,

(2.27)

where

\displaystyle l(\tilde{\mu}^{j})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2r_{j}-1,&\tilde{\mu}^{2}_{2r_{2}}=0,\\
2r_{j},&\tilde{\mu}^{2}_{2r_{2}}>0,\end{array}\right.\quad j=1,2.

Note that

\tilde{\mu}^{j}=(\tilde{\mu}_{1}^{j},\tilde{\mu}_{2}^{j},\cdots,\tilde{\mu}_{2r_{j}}^{1})
and

\tilde{\nu}^{j}=(\tilde{\nu}_{1}^{j},\tilde{\nu}_{2}^{j},\cdots,\tilde{\nu}_{2r_{j}}^{1})
,
then

\displaystyle\left(\langle\tilde{\mu}^{2},\tilde{\mu}^{1}|,|\tilde{\nu}^{1},\tilde{\nu}^{2}\rangle\right)=

\displaystyle\langle 0|\bar{\phi}_{\tilde{\mu}^{2}_{2r_{2}}}^{*}\dots\bar{\phi}_{\tilde{\mu}^{2}_{1}}^{*}\phi_{\tilde{\mu}^{1}_{2r_{1}}}^{*}\dots\phi_{\tilde{\mu}^{1}_{1}}^{*}\phi_{\tilde{\mu}^{1}_{1}}\dots\phi_{\tilde{\mu}^{1}_{2r_{1}}}\bar{\phi}_{\tilde{\mu}^{2}_{1}}\dots\bar{\phi}_{\tilde{\mu}^{2}_{2r_{2}}}|0\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{l(\tilde{\mu}^{1})}2^{l(\tilde{\mu}^{2})}\prod_{i=1}^{2r_{1}}\delta_{\tilde{\mu}_{i}^{1},\tilde{\nu}_{i}^{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{2r_{2}}\delta_{\tilde{\mu}_{j}^{2},\tilde{\nu}_{j}^{2}}=2^{l(\tilde{\mu}^{1})}2^{l(\tilde{\mu}^{2})}\delta_{\tilde{\mu}^{1},\tilde{\nu}^{1}}\delta_{\tilde{\mu}^{2},\tilde{\nu}^{2}},

(2.30)

where

\displaystyle\delta_{\tilde{\mu}^{j},\tilde{\nu}^{j}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\tilde{\mu}^{j}=\tilde{\nu}^{j},\\
0,&otherwise,\end{array}\right.\quad j=1,2.

3.1.
The generalized

-boson algebra and the space of states

Define the generalized

-boson algebra based on the generators
\{\varphi^{(j)},\varphi^{(j)\dagger},\mathcal{N}^{(j)}\}
satisfying

\displaystyle[\varphi^{(i)},\varphi^{(j)\dagger}]=\delta_{i,j}(-1)^{\mathcal{N}^{(i)}},\quad[\mathcal{N}^{(i)},\varphi^{(j)}]=-\delta_{i,j}\varphi^{(i)},\quad[\mathcal{N}^{(i)},\varphi^{(j)\dagger}]=\delta_{i,j}\varphi^{(i)\dagger},\mathrm{~for~}i,j=1,2.

(3.1)

Introduce

copies of the generalized

-boson algebra generated by
\{\varphi_{k}^{(j)},\varphi_{k}^{(j)\dagger},\mathcal{N}_{k}^{(j)}\}
,

Different copies and types of the generalized
i
-boson algebra are assumed to commute, giving rise to equations

\displaystyle[\varphi_{l_{i}}^{(i)},\varphi_{k_{j}}^{(j)\dagger}]

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\delta_{l_{i},k_{j}}(-1)^{\mathcal{N}_{l_{i}}^{(i)}},&i=j,\\
0,&i\neq j,\end{array}\right.\quad[\mathcal{N}_{l_{i}}^{(i)},\varphi_{k_{j}}^{(j)}]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\delta_{l_{i},k_{j}}\varphi_{l_{i}}^{(i)},&i=j\\
0,&i\neq j,\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle[\mathcal{N}_{l_{i}}^{(i)},\varphi_{k_{j}}^{(j)\dagger}]

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\delta_{l_{i},k_{j}}\varphi_{l_{i}}^{(i)\dagger},&i=j,\\
0,&i\neq j,\end{array}\right.\ \ \mathrm{~for~all~}i,j=1,2,\mathrm{~and~}0\leq l_{i},k_{j}\leq M_{j}.

Consider integral lattices composed of a pair of lattices

\displaystyle 0\leq n_{0}^{(j)},~0\leq n_{1}^{(j)},\cdots,n_{M_{j}}^{(j)}\leq 1,\mathrm{~for~all~}j=1,2\mathrm{~and~}0\leq i\leq M_{j}.

(3.8)

Define a space of states
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{V}^{(1)}\bigotimes\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(2)}

as the linear span of all lattice configurations and denote the basis vector in

as

\displaystyle\text{Basis}(\tilde{\mathcal{V}})=\left\{|\tilde{n}^{1}\rangle^{(1)}\bigotimes|\tilde{n}^{2}\rangle^{(2)}=\bigotimes_{i=0}^{M_{1}}|\tilde{n}^{1}_{i}\rangle_{i}^{(1)}\bigotimes\bigotimes_{j=0}^{M_{2}}|\tilde{n}^{2}_{j}\rangle_{j}^{(2)}\right\},

(3.9)

\displaystyle\text{Basis}(\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(j)})

\displaystyle=\left\{|\tilde{n}^{j}\rangle^{(j)}=|\tilde{n}_{0}^{j}\rangle_{0}^{(j)}\otimes\cdots\otimes|\tilde{n}_{M_{j}}^{j}\rangle_{M_{j}}^{(j)}=\bigotimes_{i=0}^{M_{j}}|\tilde{n}_{i}^{j}\rangle_{i}^{(j)}\right\},\quad j=1,2.

(3.10)

We write the number of particles as

The inner product between two basis vectors

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{I}}\big(|\tilde{m}^{1}\rangle^{(1)}\bigotimes|\tilde{m}^{2}\rangle^{(2)},|\tilde{n}^{1}\rangle^{(1)}\bigotimes|\tilde{n}^{2}\rangle^{(2)}\big)=\theta_{0}^{1}\theta_{0}^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{M_{j}}2^{-\tilde{m}_{i}^{j}}\delta_{\tilde{m}_{i}^{j},\tilde{n}_{i}^{j}},

The dual space of states

\displaystyle\text{Basis}(\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{\ast})=\left\{\prescript{(2)}{}{\langle\tilde{m}^{2}|}\bigotimes\prescript{(1)}{}{\langle\tilde{m}^{1}|}=\bigotimes_{j=0}^{M_{2}}\prescript{(2)}{j}{\langle\tilde{m}^{2}_{j}|}\bigotimes\bigotimes_{i=0}^{M_{1}}\prescript{(1)}{i}{\langle\tilde{m}^{1}_{i}|}\right\},

(3.14)

\displaystyle=\left\{\prescript{(j)}{}{\langle\tilde{m}^{j}|}=\bigotimes_{i=0}^{M_{j}}\prescript{(j)}{i}{\langle\tilde{m}^{j}_{i}|}\right\},\quad j=1,2,

Conclusions and discussions

This paper is concerned with the relation between generalized
i
-boson model and boxed BUC plane partitions.
With the help of maps

and
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}^{*}

Meanwhile, the generating function for boxed BUC plane partitions have been expressed as products of Schur

We also discuss the limiting forms of the scalar product of the generalized
i
-boson model and obtain the generating function for BUC plane partitions with the double scaling limit.
It is worth studying the relation between topological field theories and quantum integrable systems.
It should be pointed out that the correspondence
between the G/G gauged Wess-Zumino-Witten (WZW) model and phase model, as well as that between the G/G gauged WZW-matter model
and
q

It would be interesting to investigate the correspondence between
