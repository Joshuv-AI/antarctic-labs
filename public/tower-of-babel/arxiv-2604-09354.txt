Enriched coalgebras are sometimes comonadic
Oisín Flynn-Connolly

Abstract
We introduce an enriched notion of a coalgebra over an operad 𝒫 \mathcal{P} in a symmetric monoidal 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . When 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is semicartesian and 𝒫 \mathcal{P} is unital, we construct a 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -endofunctor on 𝒞 \mathcal{C} associated to 𝒫 \mathcal{P} and give conditions under which it is a 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -comonad with co-Eilenberg–Moore 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -category isomorphic to the 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -category of 𝒫 \mathcal{P} -coalgebras in 𝒞 . \mathcal{C}. In many cases, this permits computation of 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -categories of coalgebras. The key example is 𝒱 = 𝖳𝗈𝗉 \mathcal{V}=\mathsf{Top} and 𝒞 = ( 𝖳𝗈𝗉 ∗ , ∨ ) \mathcal{C}=({\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}},\vee) , where this construction recovers the comonadic description of C n C_{n} -coalgebras of Moreno-Fernández, Wierstra and the present author. We further recover one direction of Fox’s theorem.

Introduction

The standard theory of (co)algebras over an operad
[
Lei04
,
Fre09
,
LV12
]
assumes that both the operad and its (co)algebras live within the same symmetric monoidal category. However, mathematical structures have recently emerged that sit less well with this picture.

An example of this phenomenon is the notion of a coalgebra over the little
n
-cubes operad
C_{n}
introduced in
[
GTZ12
,
FCMFW23
]
, with related ideas appearing in
[
AK14
,
Mal25
]
, which is used to study structures on the higher Hochschild homology and characterise iterated suspensions.
The coalgebras over operads in question are pointed spaces
X
equipped with structure morphisms

\Delta_{r}:C_{n}(r)\times X\to X^{\vee r}.

This definition mixes the two distinct monoidal structures
\vee
and
\times
, and pointed and unpointed spaces. From a classical categorical perspective, this definition is quite odd as it combines types in a categorically non-standard way. However, this is completely natural from the perspective of
enriched category theory

[
Kel05
]
. In this paper, we develop this connection, with the goal of giving clean categorical foundation to the topic. This in particular applies to
[
FCMFW23
, Section 2]
and
[
FC24
, Chapter 6]
along with some of the literature on factorization homology
[
GTZ12
,
Gin15
]
. This paper has a target audience that may not be familiar with the particulars of enriched category theory, especially in a non-simplicial setting, so therefore we start with a brief review of it.

The first contribution of this paper is a general notion of an enriched coalgebra over an operad (Definition
24
) via coendomorphism operads. While the definition works in the setup of any symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-category, we only obtain a
\mathcal{V}
-category of such objects when
\mathcal{V}
is Cartesian. This is because the construction of hom-objects depends on the existence of diagonals. We phrase this as a theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a Cartesian monoidal category and
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-category. Then for any operad
\mathcal{P}
in
\mathcal{V}
, the enriched coalgebras of
\mathcal{P}
in
\mathcal{C}
form a
\mathcal{V}
-category.

The construction of a monad associated to an operad critically depends on the fact that coproducts distribute over tensor products. The situation for comonads associated to operads is more subtle as the dual of this statement generally fails, and one would not expect the comonad to be given by such a simple formula, or indeed the category of
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebras to be comonadic at all. Indeed, there are simple counter-examples to comonadicity in the absence of either certain limits or of well-behaved cotensors
1
1

1

as a very simple example, consider the discrete symmetric monoidal category
(\mathbb{N},+,0)
. The only cocommutative coalgebra is
\{0\}
. But the inclusion
\{0\}\to\mathbb{N}
does not have a right adjoint.
. This asymmetry is well-known to theoretical computer scientists, who have observed that final coalgebras can form monads as readily as comonads
[
GLdP01
]
, but is perhaps less well appreciated by topologists. Nonetheless, Anel
[
Ane14
]
, building on
[
Smi03
]
, has constructed comonads via recursive procedures, under certain technical assumptions, for a different notion of coalgebra in the setting of closed monoidal categories.

A central technical mechanism of
[
FCMFW23
]
was demonstrating that under the specific topological conditions of that paper, a very tractable comonadic structure emerges. In this paper, we distill the categorical framework underlying it and show that it can be viewed as a formal consequence of a much more general theory of how the tensor product on
\mathcal{V}
interacts with that on
\mathcal{C}
.

Given an operad
\mathcal{P}
in
\mathcal{V}
, the unary component
\mathcal{P}(1)
is naturally a monoid in
\mathcal{V}
. We define the
coaction
\mathcal{V}
-comonad
, which is a
\mathcal{V}
-comonad on
\mathcal{C}
induced by this monoid structure and the left (ie., the cotensor) action of
\mathcal{V}
on
\mathcal{C}
that looks very much like the classical coaction comonad on an ordinary category. Using
indexed limits
, we define a subcomonad
C_{\mathcal{P}}
of this that takes the higher arity operations of the operad into account. With this object we are able to prove the following theorem, which is the main result of our note.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a Cartesian monoidal category and
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal, monically projecting
\mathcal{V}
-category, complete and cotensored over
\mathcal{V}
. Further let
\mathcal{P}
be a unital operad in
\mathcal{V}.
Then the
\mathcal{V}
-category of counital
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebras in
\mathcal{C}
is the co-Eilenberg–Moore
\mathcal{V}
-category of a
\mathcal{V}
-comonad
C_{\mathcal{P}}
.

The key compatibility condition in the above theorem statement is
monically projecting
. While a precise definition will be given later in the paper, it is an abstraction of the fact that the wedge sum
\vee
is semicartesian and that the map
X\vee Y\hookrightarrow X\times Y
in pointed spaces is a monomorphism. The main idea of the proof is that the nullary operation in the operad along with the projection maps of the category, allow one to show that the multiplication is controlled by the unary component
\mathcal{P}(1)
. This theorem admits a strengthening, which states that if
\mathcal{C}
is isomorphically projecting, ie., if the map
X\otimes Y\hookrightarrow X\times Y
is an isomorphism, then the comonad is precisely the coaction
\mathcal{V}
-comonad induced by
\mathcal{P}(1).
We conclude the development of our theory by showing that if
\mathcal{C}
satisfies a strong version of the semicartesian property, then the computation of
C_{\mathcal{P}}
can be rendered quite simple.

We conclude by giving a few examples from topology, set theory, logic and theoretical computer science to which our theory applies. The simplest is small semicartesian categories, which are naturally
\mathsf{Set}
-enriched. Our theorem simply says that, if the category is monically projecting, the category of coalgebras over any
\mathsf{Set}
-operad is comonadic. In particular, Cartesian categories are always monically projecting, in this case meaning that
\mathcal{C}(c,X\times Y)\cong\mathcal{C}(c,X)\times\mathcal{C}(c,Y)
is an isomorphism, so in the case of commutative operad in
\mathsf{Set}
, the comonad in question can easily be computed to be the identity. It follows that the category of counital cocommutative coalgebras in any Cartesian category is equivalent to the original category, recovering one direction of a well-known result of Fox
[
Fox76
]
.

Finally, we have two appendices. The first briefly further reviews the background on symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-categories. The second consists of some routine verifications that certain categories are symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-categories. The main result is that the category of
\mathcal{P}
-algebras with categorical coproduct is such an example. The point is that it thus admits the construction of an appropriate
\infty
-coendomorphism operad. The results of this paper, and this section in particular, are intended to be used in forthcoming work to construct operations on the higher Hochschild (co)homology of
E_{\infty}
-algebras
[
FC26a
]
.

Although this paper’s results are primarily category-theoretical, they highlight the value of a homotopy theory for symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-categories in calculations related to iterated suspensions and noncommutative geometry. Currently, to the author’s knowledge, a fully satisfactory theory of this kind is lacking in both the model-categorical (see
[
GM20
, Section 5.4]
) and
\infty
-categorical settings. We would particularly be interested if such a theory allowed the interpretation of the relationship between the recognition principles for iterated suspensions and iterated loop spaces as some form of Koszul duality.

A definition of an algebra over an operad using enriched endomorphism operads is given in

A line of work developed in depth by Adámek, Milius, Moss and collaborators

,

The standard conditions in this literature, eg., accessibility and presentability, are rather different from the monic projection we use; the operadic and mixed-monoidal-structure setting we treat falls outside the scope of those theorems, though we suspect connections may be made in future, especially in light of the recursive constructions of the unpublished
[
Ane14
]

Anel constructs cofree

\mathcal{P}
-coalgebra, for a distinct notion of coalgebra defined without using coendomorphism operads, comonads
\mathcal{P}^{\vee}
for operads
\mathcal{P}
in symmetric monoidal
categories
\mathcal{V}
satisfying two technical conditions: the canonical map

[A,A^{\prime}]\otimes[B,B^{\prime}]\to[A\otimes B,A^{\prime}\otimes B^{\prime}]
is a monomorphism,
and
\otimes
commutes with countable intersections in each variable. Anel’s construction works by first computing a
lax comonad
and then constructing its comonadic coreflection
\mathcal{P}^{\vee}
via a transfinite
limit.

Our framework is complementary to this. In Anel’s setting, the operad and its coalgebras live in the same category
\mathcal{V}
.
In contrast, we treat operads
\mathcal{P}
in a Cartesian base
\mathcal{V}
and coalgebras in a separate
\mathcal{V}
-category
\mathcal{C}
, with its own monoidal structure
\otimes
. For example, the setting of
[
FCMFW23
]
, where the wedge sum which is not closed,
does not fit Anel’s framework.
The compatibility condition between the two monoidal structures in our framework is monic projection, a condition that has no analogue in Anel’s setting since he works with a
single monoidal structure. We compute our comonad
C_{\mathcal{P}}
very explicitly in a single step as a subcomonad of the
\mathcal{P}(1)
-coaction comonad, with no recursion required. In the special case
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{V}
with
\times=\otimes
both frameworks simultaneously apply, and our
C_{\mathcal{P}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\vee}
will agree as cofree
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebra comonads by the uniqueness of right adjoints to the forgetful functor.

Enriched category theory

In this section, we first revise the ideas from enriched category theory that we shall use in the sequel. We conclude with an extended discussion on
\mathcal{V}
-comonads.

2.1
Recollections on enriched category theory

We assume knowledge of basic category theory
[
Rie16
]
, but shall briefly recall the necessary elements of enriched category theory. For a comprehensive treatment, we refer the reader to
[
Kel05
]
.

In standard category theory, the collection of morphisms between two objects forms a set. In nature, mapping sets often carry additional structure capturing topological, simplicial, or algebraic data. To handle this, we replace hom-sets with
hom-objects
valued in a suitable base category
\mathcal{V}
. While in the most general case, one needs only a symmetric monoidal category (the monoidal structure is needed for the third and fourth axioms of Definition
3
), most of the theory developed in the literature requires
(\mathcal{V},\times,I_{\mathcal{V}})
to be a
(Bénabou) cosmos
: that is, a closed, symmetric monoidal category that is complete and cocomplete. Examples of such categories abound and include compactly generated, locally Hausdorff topological spaces with the Cartesian product, simplicial sets with the Cartesian product, metric spaces with the
l_{1}
-monoidal product and chain complexes with the standard tensor product.

\mathcal{V}
-categories:
With a sufficiently nice base category in hand, one can immediately define
\mathcal{V}
-categories.

Definition 3
.

Let
(\mathcal{V},\times,I_{\mathcal{V}})
be a symmetric monoidal category. A
\mathcal{V}
-category

\mathcal{C}
consists of:

1.

A collection of objects
\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
.

2.

For every pair of objects
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
, a
mapping object

\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\in\mathcal{V}
.

3.

For every triple of objects
X,Y,Z\in\mathcal{C}
, a
composition morphism
in
\mathcal{V}
:

c_{X,Y,Z}:\underline{\mathcal{C}}(Y,Z)\times\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\to\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Z).

4.

For every object
X\in\mathcal{C}
, an identity-assigning morphism in
\mathcal{V}
:

j_{X}:I_{\mathcal{V}}\to\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X).

Locally small categories are the same thing as
\mathsf{Set}
-enriched categories.

Example 4
.

Every ordinary category
\mathcal{C}
has an associated
free
\mathcal{V}
-category

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}}
. This has the same objects, but we have

\underline{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{V}}}(X,Y):=\coprod_{f\in\mathcal{C}(X,Y)}I_{\mathcal{V}}.

A
map

f\colon X\to Y
in
\mathcal{C}
is shorthand for a map
f\colon I_{\mathcal{V}}\to\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
in
\mathcal{V}
. Given a morphism
f:Y\to Z
, we have induced maps

f_{\ast}:\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\xrightarrow{\lambda^{-1}}I_{\mathcal{V}}\times\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\xrightarrow{f}\underline{\mathcal{C}}(Y,Z)\times\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\xrightarrow{\circ}\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Z)

where
\lambda:I_{\mathcal{V}}\times\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\to\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
is the left unitor. Similarly, there is a notion of precomposition
f^{\ast}
defined in terms of right unitors. It follows that we can manipulate morphisms as though they are morphisms in an ordinary category and every time we speak of a morphism in
\mathcal{C},
this should be implicitly understood.
It immediately follows that a
\mathcal{V}
-category
\mathcal{C}
has an
underlying category

\mathcal{C}_{0}
, which has the same objects and where the morphisms are defined as

\mathcal{C}_{0}\left(X,Y\right)\coloneqq\mathcal{V}_{0}(I_{\mathcal{V}},\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)).

Relatedly, we can also define an enriched notion of
monomorphism
. We say a map
f\colon X\to Y
in
\mathcal{C}
is an enriched monomorphism if, for every
W\in\mathcal{C}
, the morphism
f_{\ast}\colon\underline{\mathcal{C}}(W,X)\to\underline{\mathcal{C}}(W,Y)
is a monomorphism in
\mathcal{V}
.

Symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-categories:
We will now start building towards a notion of a symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-category. First, one has a notion of a
\mathcal{V}
-functor.

Definition 5
.

A
\mathcal{V}
-functor

F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
between two
\mathcal{V}
-categories assigns to each object
X\in\mathcal{C}
an object
F(X)\in\mathcal{D}
, and to each pair of objects
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
a morphism in
\mathcal{V}
:

F_{X,Y}:\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\to\underline{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),F(Y))

such that
F
is strictly compatible with the composition and identity morphisms of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
.

The collection of functors between two
\mathcal{V}
-categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
form a
\mathcal{V}
-category. The hom-object is defined by the end:

[\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}](F,G)\coloneqq\int_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\underline{\mathcal{D}}(F(c),G(c)).

Finally, there is an enriched notion of a natural transformation.

Definition 6
.

Let
F,G:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be two
\mathcal{V}
-functors. A
\mathcal{V}
-natural transformation

\alpha:F\Rightarrow G
consists of an
\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
-indexed family of morphisms in the base category
\mathcal{V}
from the monoidal unit:

\alpha_{X}:F(X)\to G(X)\quad\text{for every }X\in\mathcal{C}.

The maps appearing in the definitions above must satisfy various axioms, which are revised in Appendix
A
. For now, it suffices to note that we have defined a 2-category
\mathcal{V}\mathsf{Cat}
. The objects of
\mathcal{V}\mathsf{Cat}
are
\mathcal{V}
-categories, the 1-morphisms are
\mathcal{V}
-functors, and the 2-morphisms are
\mathcal{V}
-natural transformations. From the symmetric monoidal structure of
\mathcal{V}
, the category
\mathcal{V}\mathsf{Cat}
inherits a symmetric monoidal structure: so we may define the tensor product of
\mathcal{V}
-categories.

Definition 7
.

A
symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-category
is precisely a symmetric pseudomonoid in the symmetric monoidal 2-category
\mathcal{V}\mathsf{Cat}
. More intuitively, it consists of an object
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{V}\mathsf{Cat}
equipped with
\mathcal{V}
-functors:

\otimes:\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}\quad\text{and}\quad I:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{C}

where
\mathcal{I}
is the unit
\mathcal{V}
-category
2
2

2

ie., consisting of one object
\ast
such that
\underline{\mathcal{I}}(\ast,\ast)=I_{\mathcal{V}}
.
. It also has the data of invertible 2-morphisms in
\mathcal{V}\mathsf{Cat}
for associativity
\alpha
, left (and right) unitality
\lambda
, and symmetry
\tau
.

Example 8
.

Our running example throughout this text will be the symmetric monoidal category
({\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}},\vee)
of pointed topological spaces with wedge sum. This is a
\mathsf{Top}
-category with Cartesian products
\times
.

Because the monoidal product
\otimes
is, in particular, a
\mathcal{V}
-bifunctor, for any objects
A,B,C,D\in\mathcal{C}
, there is a morphism in
\mathcal{V}
that allows us to tensor mapping objects together:

T:\underline{\mathcal{C}}(A,C)\times\underline{\mathcal{C}}(B,D)\to\underline{\mathcal{C}}(A\otimes B,C\otimes D).

Note that, rather than denoting it by
\otimes_{(A,B),(C,D)}
, we have chosen to use
T
. We shall later slightly abuse notation further by also denoting the higher arity variants of
T
by
T
also. This morphism
T
will allow us to define the operadic composition maps for the coendomorphism operad strictly within the enrichment category
\mathcal{V}
.

We say that a symmetric monoidal category is
semicartesian
if the unit of the monoidal structure coincides with the terminal object in the category. For example
(\mathsf{Top},\times)
is semicartesian as the terminal object and the unit object are both the one point set
\{\ast\}
. The category
({\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}},\vee)
is semicartesian for the same reason.

In direct analogy, we say that a
\mathcal{V}
-category has a terminal object
1_{\mathcal{C}}
if
\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,1_{\mathcal{C}})\cong I_{\mathcal{V}}
for all
X\in\mathcal{C}.
We say a symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-category is
semicartesian
if the terminal object coincides with the unit object
3
3

3

ie., the object in the image of
I:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{C}
of the symmetric monoidal structure.

Oplax monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-functors:
The last basic notion of enriched category theory that we shall introduce is oplax monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-functors.

Definition 9
.

An
oplax monoidal
\mathcal{V}

\underline{F}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
between symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}

\mathcal{V}

1.

\nu\colon F(I_{\mathcal{C}})\to I_{\mathcal{D}}

2.

satisfying the enriched analogues of the usual coassociativity and counitality axioms. Importantly, it must also satisfy the identity

commutes.

Limits in enriched categories.
Enriched categories possess a more general theory of limits than ordinary ones. Namely, limits
F:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{C}
may be
indexed
by a functor
W:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{V}
, where
\mathcal{K}
is a
\mathcal{V}
-category. We use Kelly’s notation for this:
\{W,F\}
. This is defined by the universal property

\underline{\mathcal{C}}(c,\{W,F\})\cong[\mathcal{K},\mathcal{V}](W(-),\underline{\mathcal{C}}(c,F(-))).

The most basic example of this is the
cotensor

V\pitchfork X\in\mathcal{C}
of an object
X\in\mathcal{C}
by an object
V\in\mathcal{V}
is defined by the natural isomorphism in
\mathcal{V}
:

\underline{\mathcal{C}}(c,V\pitchfork X)\cong\underline{\mathcal{V}}(V,\underline{\mathcal{C}}(c,X)).

Example 10
.

In the running example, the cotensor of
M\in\mathsf{Top}
and
X\in{\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}}
is the mapping space
\underline{\mathsf{Top}}(M,X).
This contains a unique map that sends the whole domain to the basepoint of
X
. Thus it is pointed itself. It is easy to see that

Example: the coaction
\mathcal{V}
-comonad
Given any monoid in
\mathcal{V}
, the cotensor induces the
coaction
\mathcal{V}
-comonad
on
\mathcal{C}.

Proposition 13
.

Let
(M,m,\nu)
be a monoid in
\mathcal{V}
. Then there is an induced comonad on
\mathcal{C}
with underlying endofunctor defined up to isomorphism by

X\mapsto M\pitchfork X.

Proof.

First we show that one has a
\mathcal{V}
-functor:

\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\xrightarrow{M\pitchfork(-)_{X,Y}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}(M\pitchfork X,M\pitchfork Y).

To construct this, we first write

\underline{\mathcal{C}}(M\pitchfork X,M\pitchfork Y)\cong\underline{\mathcal{V}}(M,\underline{\mathcal{C}}(M\pitchfork X,Y))

It follows by hom-tensor adjunction that it suffices to give a map:

\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\times M\to\underline{\mathcal{C}}(M\pitchfork X,Y).

Finally, completing the construction is the following

\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\times M\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)}\times\nu_{X}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\times\underline{\mathcal{C}}(M\pitchfork X,X)\xrightarrow{c}\underline{\mathcal{C}}(M\pitchfork X,Y)

where
\nu_{X}\colon M\to\underline{\mathcal{C}}(M\pitchfork X,X)
is constructed as the following composite:

M\times I_{\mathcal{V}}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{M}\times j_{M\pitchfork X}}M\times\underline{\mathcal{C}}(M\pitchfork X,M\pitchfork X)\cong M\times\underline{\mathcal{V}}(M,\underline{\mathcal{C}}(M\pitchfork X,X))\xrightarrow{ev}\underline{\mathcal{C}}(M\pitchfork X,X)

where we use the evaluation map.

Next, we construct the structure maps of the comonad. The comultiplication is defined as the following morphism:

I_{\mathcal{V}}\xrightarrow{j_{M\pitchfork X}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}\left(M\pitchfork X,M\pitchfork X\right)\cong\underline{\mathcal{V}}\left(M,\underline{\mathcal{C}}\left(M\pitchfork X,X\right)\right)\xrightarrow{\underline{\mathcal{V}}\left(m,\underline{\mathcal{C}}\left(M\pitchfork X,X\right)\right)}\\
\underline{\mathcal{V}}\left(M\times M,\underline{\mathcal{C}}\left(M\pitchfork X,X\right)\right)\cong\underline{\mathcal{V}}\left(M,\underline{\mathcal{C}}\left(M\pitchfork X,M\pitchfork X\right)\right)\cong\\
\underline{\mathcal{C}}(M\pitchfork X,M\pitchfork\left(M\pitchfork X\right))

The counit is constructed as follows:

I_{\mathcal{V}}\xrightarrow{j_{M\pitchfork X}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}(M\pitchfork X,M\pitchfork X)\cong\underline{\mathcal{V}}(M,\underline{\mathcal{C}}(M\pitchfork X,X))\xrightarrow{\underline{\mathcal{V}}(\nu,\underline{\mathcal{C}}(M\pitchfork X,X))}\\
\underline{\mathcal{V}}(I_{\mathcal{V}},\underline{\mathcal{C}}(M\pitchfork X,X))\cong\underline{\mathcal{C}}(M\pitchfork X,X)

We omit the verification of the coassociativity and the counitality axioms, as it follows by routine diagram chase from the monoid axioms.
∎

3
Operads and algebras in symmetric monoidal categories

3.1
Recollections on operads

An operad encodes algebraic structures expressible via the tensor product. Most of the details in this section can be found in
[
Fre09
]
. Throughout, operads will be ordinary (i.e., unenriched) operads in
\mathcal{V}
.

Let
(\mathcal{V},\otimes,I_{\mathcal{V}})
be a symmetric monoidal ordinary category with small colimits. To define an operad, we first need to define the notion of a symmetric sequence.

Definition 14
.

A
symmetric sequence
in
\mathcal{V}
is a sequence of objects
A=\{A(n)\}_{n\geq 0}
in
\mathcal{V}
such that each
A(n)
is equipped with a right action of the symmetric group
\mathbb{S}_{n}
ie. there is a group homomorphism from
\mathbb{S}_{n}
to
\mathcal{V}(A(n),A(n)).

An operad is a symmetric sequence equipped with operations between the objects.

Definition 15
.

An
operad

\mathcal{P}
in
\mathcal{V}
consists of:

1.

A symmetric sequence
\{\mathcal{P}(n)\}_{n\geq 0}
in
\mathcal{V}
.

2.

A unit morphism
\eta:I_{\mathcal{V}}\to\mathcal{P}(1)
.

3.

For every integer
k\geq 1
and every sequence of integers
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\geq 0
, a composition morphism in
\mathcal{V}
:

\gamma:\mathcal{P}(k)\times\mathcal{P}(n_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{P}(n_{k})\to\mathcal{P}(n_{1}+\dots+n_{k}).

These data are required to satisfy the following three axioms:

Associativity

The following diagram must commute:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}(k)\times\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{P}(n_{i})\times\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\mathcal{P}(m_{i,j})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}(k)\times\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}m_{i,j}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}(\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{i})\times\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\mathcal{P}(m_{i,j})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}\left(\sum_{i,j}m_{i,j}\right)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}\times\prod\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma\times\operatorname{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}

Unitality

The unit morphism
\eta:I_{\mathcal{V}}\to\mathcal{P}(1)
acts as a two-sided identity for the composition
\gamma
. The following two diagrams must commute, where
\lambda
and
\rho
denote the standard left and right unitors of
\mathcal{V}
:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I_{\mathcal{V}}\times\mathcal{P}(n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}(1)\times\mathcal{P}(n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}(n)\par}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta\times\operatorname{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}(n)\times I_{\mathcal{V}}^{\times n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}(n)\times\mathcal{P}(1)^{\times n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}(n)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}\times\eta^{\times n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}

Equivariance

The composition
\gamma
is compatible with the actions of the symmetric groups. The group
\mathbb{S}_{k}
has a natural action on
\mathbb{S}_{n_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{S}_{n_{k}}
given by permuting individual blocks. Let
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{k}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{n_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{S}_{n_{k}}
, where
n=\sum n_{i}
, then we have a permutation
\sigma\ltimes\tau
, by dividing it into
k
blocks of with the
i^{th}
of length
n_{i}
and acting on it by
{\mathbb{S}}_{n_{i}}
, and permuting the blocks themselves by
{\mathbb{S}}_{k}
. Permuting the inputs before composition is equivalent to applying the corresponding block product permutation
\sigma\ltimes\tau\in S_{n}
, applied to the composed operation. The following diagram must commute:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}(k)\times\mathcal{P}(n_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{P}(n_{k})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}(k)\times\mathcal{P}(n_{\sigma^{-1}(1)})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{P}(n_{\sigma^{-1}(k)})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}(n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}(n)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma\times\tau_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\times\cdots\times\tau_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma\ltimes\tau}

Remark 16
.

If we remove the axioms about the symmetric group,
non-symmetric
operads can be defined in an arbitrary monoidal category.

Remark 17
.

Equivalently, Markl has shown that an operad can be defined via
partial composition
operations

\circ_{i}\colon\mathcal{P}(m)\times\mathcal{P}(n)\to\mathcal{P}(m+n-1)\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq m,

(1)

which, informally, represent putting an
n
-ary operation into the
i
-th slot of an
m
-ary operation. These partial compositions satisfy various axioms that can be worked easily out from our previous definition of an operad.
If
\mathcal{P}(0)=I_{\mathcal{V}}
, the operad is said to be
unital
. In this case, the
i^{th}
partial composition map
d_{i}\colon\mathcal{P}(m)\xrightarrow{\alpha}\mathcal{P}(m)\times I_{\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{P}(m)\times\mathcal{P}(0)\xrightarrow{\circ_{i}}\mathcal{P}(m-1)
induces the
i^{th}
canonical restriction operator.

Example 18
.

In any symmetric monoidal category
(\mathcal{V},\times,I_{\mathcal{V}})
we can define the
commutative operad

\mathsf{Com}(n)=I_{\mathcal{V}}.
The composition maps of the operad are given in the obvious way by the unitors of the symmetric monoidal category. The symmetric action is given by the obvious action on the factor
I_{\mathcal{V}}^{\times n}
of
{\mathbb{S}}_{n}
in

\mathcal{V}(I_{\mathcal{V}}^{\times n},I_{\mathcal{V}})\cong\mathcal{V}(I_{\mathcal{V}},I_{\mathcal{V}}).

We conclude with a short discussion about morphisms of operads. The easiest notion is the following: a
morphism of operads

\psi\colon\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{Q}
is a collection of maps
\psi_{n}\colon\mathcal{P}(n)\to\mathcal{Q}(n)
that preserves the identity in
\mathcal{P}(1)
, and is equivariant with respect to the group action and composition. This clearly defines a (ordinary) category of operads
\mathsf{Op}\left(\mathcal{V}\right)
over
\mathcal{V}
. We briefly remark that
\mathsf{Op}\left(\mathcal{V}\right)
can be made a
\mathcal{V}
-category as
\mathcal{V}
is complete and hom-objects may be constructed using appropriate equalisers - first one constructs morphisms of symmetric sequences and then one restricts to those maps that preserve composition.

3.2
Enriched coendomorphism operads

In
[
FCMFW23
]
, the authors show that one can define a
coalgebra over an operad
in the symmetric monoidal category of pointed topological spaces endowed with the wedge sum, using the
coendomorphism operad
. Via enriched category theory, this construction can be viewed as an instance of a more general construction.

Definition 19
.

Let
(\mathcal{V},\times,I_{\mathcal{V}})
be a symmetric monoidal category. Let
(\mathcal{C},\otimes,I_{\mathcal{C}})
be a symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-category. Given
X\in\mathcal{C}
, the coendomorphism operad
\mathsf{CoEnd}(X)
is an operad in
\mathcal{V}
with arity
r
component

\mathsf{CoEnd}(X)(r)\coloneqq\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X^{\otimes r}).

For
r=0
, set
\mathsf{CoEnd}(X)(0)=\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,I_{\mathcal{C}})
.

1.

As
\mathsf{CoEnd}(X)(1)=\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X)
, we may define the operadic identity to be the identity morphism
j_{X}\colon I_{\mathcal{V}}\to\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X)
.

2.

By the definition of a symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-category, there is an induced morphism
T\colon\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X^{\otimes n_{1}})\times\cdots\times\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X^{\otimes n_{r}})\to\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X^{\otimes r},X^{\otimes(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r})})
. The operadic composition map
\gamma
is then defined as the composite in
\mathcal{V}
given by

\gamma=c\circ(\operatorname{id}_{\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X^{\otimes r})}\times T),

where
c
is the enriched categorical composition.

3.

Given a permutation
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{k}
, the symmetry in the symmetric monoidal structure of the
\mathcal{V}
-category
\mathcal{C}
provides a canonical structural isomorphism
\sigma^{X}\colon X^{\otimes k}\xrightarrow{\sim}X^{\otimes k}
. The
\mathbb{S}_{k}
-action on the arity
k
component is defined as the
\mathcal{V}
-morphism
\sigma_{\ast}=\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,\sigma^{X})\colon\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X^{\otimes k})\to\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X^{\otimes k})
induced by enriched post-composition.

The coendomorphism operad
\mathsf{CoEnd}(X)
above depends both on the choice of enrichment
\mathcal{V}
and the choice of monoidal product on
\mathcal{C}
. We prove that this defines an operad in
\mathcal{V}.
This is a straightforward verification.

Proposition 20
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-category. The coendomorphism operad
\mathsf{CoEnd}(X)
is a symmetric operad in
\mathcal{V}
.

The proof is a straightforward direct verification of the three axioms.

Example 21
.

In
[
FCMFW23
]
, the coendomorphism operad is an example of this construction: the category
({\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}},\vee)
with wedge sum is enriched over the category
\mathsf{Top}
with Cartesian products
\times
. We have already noted in the introduction that enriched categories provide a more natural perspective on
[
FCMFW23
, Remark 2.1]
.

Proposition 22
.

The
\mathsf{CoEnd}(-)
-construction is functorial with respect to isomorphisms in
\mathcal{C}
and every oplax symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-functor
(F,\mu,\nu)
induces a natural transformation of functors

\tilde{F}\colon\mathsf{CoEnd}(-)\to\mathsf{CoEnd}(F(-)).

Proof.

We first show functoriality with respect to isomorphisms. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-category and let
f\colon X\xrightarrow{\sim}Y
be an isomorphism in
\mathcal{C}
with given inverse
f^{-1}
. For each arity
r\geq 0
, we define a map in
\mathcal{V}
:

\Phi_{f}(r)\colon\mathsf{CoEnd}(X)(r)\to\mathsf{CoEnd}(Y)(r)\qquad\underline{\mathcal{C}}(f^{-1},f^{\otimes r})(r)\colon\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X^{\otimes r})\to\underline{\mathcal{C}}(Y,Y^{\otimes r}).

This is compatible with the operadic composition
\gamma
, essentially as one has
(f^{-1})^{\otimes r}\cong(f^{\otimes r})^{-1}
cancel by the functoriality of the tensor product. Thus
\Phi_{f}
is an isomorphism of operads.

Next, we show that the construction commutes with oplax monoidal functors. Let
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be an oplax symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-functor between symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-categories. By definition,
F
comes equipped with natural structural morphisms
\mu_{A,B}\colon F(A\otimes B)\to F(A)\otimes F(B)
, which satisfy the obvious naturality conditions. By iterating these maps, we obtain a canonical structural map in
\mathcal{D}
for any arity
r
:

\left(\mu^{(r)}\right)_{\ast}\colon\underline{\mathcal{D}}(-,F(X^{\otimes r}))\to\underline{\mathcal{D}}(-,F(X)^{\otimes r}).

Applying the
\mathcal{V}
-functor
F
to the mapping objects of
\mathcal{C}
yields a canonical
\mathcal{V}
-morphism:

F_{X,X^{\otimes r}}\colon\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X^{\otimes r})\to\underline{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),F(X^{\otimes r})).

We define the induced map of operads
\Psi\colon\mathsf{CoEnd}(X)\to\mathsf{CoEnd}_{\mathcal{D}}(F(X))
in arity
r
by post-composing
F_{X,X^{\otimes r}}
with the iterated oplax structure map
\mu^{(r)}
:

\Psi(r)=\left(\mu^{(r)}\right)_{\ast}\circ F_{X,X^{\otimes r}}\colon\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X^{\otimes r})\to\underline{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),F(X)^{\otimes r}).

This defines the desired map of symmetric sequences.

We must also verify compatibility with the unit. This is easy as

I_{\mathcal{V}}\to\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X)\xrightarrow{F_{X,X}}\underline{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),F(X))

is the identity as
\mathcal{V}
-functors send identities to identities.
Compatibility with the equivariant action follows directly from the fact that the
F
is symmetric monoidal and therefore commutes with the symmetry maps.

To conclude, we must also verify compatibility with composition.
The following diagram commutes.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X^{\otimes r})\times\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X^{\otimes n_{1}})\times\\
\cdots\times\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X^{\otimes n_{r}})\end{subarray}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),F(X^{\otimes r}))\times\underline{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),F(X^{\otimes n_{1}}))\times\\
\cdots\times\underline{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),F(X^{\otimes n_{r}}))\end{subarray}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X^{\otimes r})\times\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X^{\otimes r},X^{\otimes n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),F(X)^{\otimes r})\times\underline{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),F(X)^{\otimes n_{1}})\times\\
\cdots\times\underline{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),F(X)^{\otimes n_{r}})\end{subarray}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,X^{\otimes n_{1}+\dots+n_{r}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),F(X)^{\otimes r})\times\\
\underline{\mathcal{D}}(F(X)^{\otimes r},F(X)^{\otimes n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}})\end{subarray}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),F(X^{\otimes n_{1}+\dots n_{r}}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),F(X)^{\otimes n_{1}+\dots n_{r}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}\times T}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mu)_{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\circ}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}\times T}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\circ}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mu)_{\ast}}

In the above diagram, where it is unambiguous, we have abbreviated some of the arrows. But the diagram commutes essentially by the bifunctoriality axiom of the oplax monoidal functor. Thus,
\Psi
is a well-defined morphism of operads in
\mathcal{V}
.
∎

Remark 23
.

Our definition recovers almost all of the coendomorphism operads appearing in the Related Work section of the paper. The only exception is the simplicial coendomorphism operad constructed in
[
FC26b
]
which uses the operation
\operatorname{Ex}^{\infty}(X\vee X)
, where
\operatorname{Ex}^{\infty}
is Kan’s fibrant replacement functor. This does not define a symmetric monoidal product on the nose as it is not strictly associative (but it does define one up to coherent homotopy).

We also remark in passing that the (co)endomorphism operad construction recovers many well-known examples of operads in the literature. For example, one can consider the subcategory of topological spaces with embeddings as maps. This is enriched over
\mathsf{Top}
and can be made monoidal by the coproduct
\sqcup.
Then the enriched endomorphism operad of
D^{n}
is equivalent to the framed little
n
-discs operad.

3.3
Enriched
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebras in a
\mathcal{V}
-category

Having defined the coendomorphism operad, we may then define a coalgebra in
\mathcal{C}
over an operad in
\mathcal{V}
.

Definition 24
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a unital operad in
\mathcal{V}
.
A
\mathcal{V}
-enriched
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebra is the data of an object
X\in\mathcal{C}
and a morphism of operads in
\mathcal{V}
:

\Phi\colon\mathcal{P}\to\mathsf{CoEnd}(X).

Remark 25
.

We define coalgebras over non-unital operads by also making
\mathsf{CoEnd}(X)
non-unital by forgetting the
\mathsf{CoEnd}(X)(0)
component of the operad.

Remark 26
.

A very important observation is that if
\mathcal{C}
is semicartesian, the coalgebras are canonically counital, because the mapping space
\underline{\mathcal{C}}(X,I_{\mathcal{C}})
is the terminal object in
\mathcal{V},
so there is only one choice of map
\mathcal{P}(0)\to\mathsf{CoEnd}(X)(0).

Example 27
.

Any locally small category is enriched over
\mathsf{Set}
. It follows that we may consider the action of
\mathsf{Set}
-operads like
\mathsf{Com}
and
\mathsf{Ass}
(see
[
LV12
]
for the relevant definitions) on any locally small symmetric monoidal category. If we consider vector spaces with the tensor product, this recovers the theory of associative and commutative coalgebras
[
LV12
]
.

.

When

Theorem 31
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be Cartesian and
\mathcal{P}
be an operad (not necessarily unital). Then, with definitions as above,
\mathcal{P}\mathsf{CoAlg}
is a
\mathcal{V}
-category.

The base category
\mathcal{V}
is complete, which ensure that the equaliser defining the mapping objects

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

defining

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathcal{P}\mathsf{CoAlg}}(Y,Z)\times\underline{\mathcal{P}\mathsf{CoAlg}}(X,Y)\times\mathcal{P}(r)}

The goal of this section is to give conditions under which the

\mathcal{P}

C_{\mathcal{P}}
associated to a unital operad
\mathcal{P}
in a symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-category
\mathcal{C}

With this setup, we are able to prove the following theorem, which is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 32

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a Cartesian monoidal category and
\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{V}

We define the comonad object via an indexed limit. We first establish the indexing category. This category contains the information about the arities, the symmetric group actions, and the operadic restriction operations, but not the composition of operations of arity greater than 0.

Definition 33

Let

\mathbf{n}\to\mathbf{n}.

2.

The restriction maps

the opposite of the category of finite sets and injective maps.

Remark 34

Mutatis mutandis

and consider only the order preserving maps. We believe that our results also extend to the operads with general groups of equivariance considered in

Appendix B
Examples of symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-categories

In this section, we give the details on our examples of monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-categories and explicitly describe the enrichment.

Proposition 53
.

The symmetric monoidal category
({\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}},\vee)
with wedge sum is enriched over the category
\mathsf{Top}
with Cartesian products
\times
.

Proof.

The category
\mathsf{Top}
is closed symmetric monoidal
[
Ste67
]
.

Now we may explicitly describe the enrichment of
{\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}}
over
\mathsf{Top}
. For any based spaces
\left(X,x_{0}\right)
and
\left(Y,y_{0}\right)
, the enriched hom-object is the space of basepoint-preserving maps
{\underline{{\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}}}}\left(X,Y\right)
, equipped with the subspace topology inherited from the unbased mapping space
\underline{\mathsf{Top}}\left(X,Y\right)
. Composition in
{\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}}
is simply the restriction of the continuous composition maps in
\mathsf{Top}
, making
{\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}}
a
\mathsf{Top}
-enriched category.

The wedge sum
\vee
is the categorical coproduct in the underlying category
\left({\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}}\right)_{0},
so it follows that it defines a symmetric monoidal structure.

Finally, we must show that the wedge sum
\vee
defines the structure of a symmetric monoidal
\mathcal{V}
-category. It suffices to show that
\vee
is an enriched coproduct, meaning it satisfies the universal property of the coproduct internally via a natural homeomorphism in
{\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}}
:

\vee_{(X,Y),(Z,Z^{\prime})}\colon{\underline{{\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}}}}\left(X,Z\right)\times{\underline{{\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}}}}\left(Y,Z^{\prime}\right)\to{\underline{{\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}}}}\left(X\vee Y,Z\vee Z^{\prime}\right)

This is the product of the inclusion of subspaces, quotiented by identifying basepoints. Therefore,
\left({\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}},\vee\right)
is a symmetric monoidal
\mathsf{Top}
-category. So it is continuous and clearly natural. The conclusion follows. The symmetric monoidal identities follow from the fact that
\vee
is the categorical coproduct in the (unenriched) category
{\mathsf{Top}_{\ast}}.

∎

Another important source of examples are when one has a simplicial enrichment. Given an algebraic operad
\mathcal{P}
(for precise details see
[
LV12
]
), the category of
\mathcal{P}
-algebras can be equipped with an simplicially enriched structure as follows.

Recollection 1
.

The commutative operad is the unit for the Hadamard product
\otimes_{H}

[
LV12
, Section 5.3.3]
. Let
X\in\mathsf{Com}\mathsf{Alg}
and
A,B\in\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}
, then by
[
LV12
, Proposition 5.3.4]
the tensor product
X\otimes A
is an algebra over
\mathsf{Com}\otimes_{H}\mathcal{P}\cong\mathcal{P}
. Explicitly, there is a natural isomorphism

\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}\left(A,X\otimes B\right)\cong\mathsf{Com}\otimes_{H}\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}\left(X\otimes A,X\otimes B\right)

We can establish the following.

Proposition 54
.

The symmetric monoidal category
(\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg},\ast)
, with categorical coproduct, is enriched over the category
\operatorname{sSet}
with Cartesian products
\times
.

Proof.

The category of simplicial sets equipped with Cartesian product
\times
is a Grothendieck topos and therefore Cartesian closed. We define the simplicial mapping space

\underline{\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}}(A,B)_{n}:=\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}(A,A_{PL}^{\ast}(\Delta^{n})\otimes B)

via Recollection
1
, where the simplicial structure is induced by the simplicial structure on
A_{PL}^{\ast}(\Delta^{n})
(where
A_{PL}^{\ast}(\Delta^{n})
denotes the polynomial differential forms functor after Sullivan).

To show that
\ast
defines an
enriched
symmetric monoidal structure, we must verify that it acts as an enriched bifunctor. Given
A,B,C,D\in\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}
, we construct a map of simplicial sets:

\Psi:\underline{\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}}(A,C)\times\underline{\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}}(B,D)\rightarrow\underline{\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}}(A\ast B,C\ast D)

We define
\Psi
level-wise. In degree
n
, let
f\in\underline{\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}}(A,C)_{n}
and
g\in\underline{\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}}(B,D)_{n}
. We have canonical inclusions
\iota_{C}:C\rightarrow C\ast D
and
\iota_{D}:D\rightarrow C\ast D
. Since tensoring with the commutative algebra
A_{PL}^{\ast}(\triangle^{n})
preserves
\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}
morphisms, these induce maps:

\text{id}\otimes\iota_{C}:A_{PL}^{\ast}(\triangle^{n})\otimes C\rightarrow A_{PL}^{\ast}(\triangle^{n})\otimes(C\ast D)

\text{id}\otimes\iota_{D}:A_{PL}^{\ast}(\triangle^{n})\otimes D\rightarrow A_{PL}^{\ast}(\triangle^{n})\otimes(C\ast D)

By post-composing, we obtain
\tilde{f}=(\text{id}\otimes\iota_{C})\circ f
and
\tilde{g}=(\text{id}\otimes\iota_{D})\circ g
. By the universal property of the categorical coproduct
A\ast B
, the maps
\tilde{f}
and
\tilde{g}
uniquely determine a single
\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}
morphism:

f\ast g:A\ast B\rightarrow A_{PL}^{\ast}(\triangle^{n})\otimes(C\ast D)

We define
\Psi_{n}(f,g):=f\ast g
. Compatibility with the simplicial structure is easily checked.

We now verify that
(\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg},\ast)
assembles into a symmetric monoidal
\operatorname{sSet}
-category. First, the assignment
\Psi
is functorial in each variable level-wise by the universal property of the coproduct, and the two functorialities commute because the coproduct is symmetric in its inputs; this is precisely the enriched bifunctor axiom. The unit isomorphism
I_{\mathcal{P}}\mathsf{Alg}\ast A\cong A
and the associator
(A\ast B)\ast C\cong A\ast(B\ast C)
are isomorphisms in the underlying category
\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}_{0}
; tensoring with
A_{PL}^{\ast}(\Delta^{n})
preserves these isomorphisms by Recollection
1
, so they lift level-wise to isomorphisms of simplicial mapping spaces. The symmetry
A\ast B\cong B\ast A
is an isomorphism in
\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}_{0}
and lifts level-wise by the same argument. In each case, the lifted maps are natural in
A,B,C
at every simplicial degree because they are induced by the universal property of the coproduct, which is natural; level-wise naturality in
\operatorname{sSet}
is enriched naturality. The monoidal coherence axioms hold level-wise because they hold in
\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}_{0}
and tensoring with
A_{PL}^{\ast}(\Delta^{n})
is a functor.
Therefore,
(\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg},\ast)
is a symmetric monoidal
\operatorname{sSet}
-category.
∎

This immediately allows us to define
\operatorname{sSet}
-enriched coalgebras in
\mathcal{P}\mathsf{Alg}.
Similarly, given a Koszul cooperad
\mathcal{P}
, the category
\mathcal{P}\mathsf{CoAlg}
of (non-enriched) coalgebras of
\mathcal{P}

\mathcal{P}^{\text{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\textexclamdown}}}.
One can therefore apply the above construction to the category
\mathcal{P}^{\text{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\textexclamdown}}}\mathsf{Alg}

\mathcal{E}

Proposition 55
.

The symmetric monoidal category
(\mathcal{E}\mathsf{CoAlg},\oplus)

\operatorname{sSet}

Proof.
