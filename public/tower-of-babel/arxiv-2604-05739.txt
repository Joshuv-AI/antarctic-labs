On Iwasawa theory of abelian varieties over ℤ p 2 \mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2} -extension with applications to Diophantine stability and integally Diophantine extensions
Meng Fai Lim

Abstract
We present certain results on the Iwasawa theory of an abelian variety with potentially good ordinary reduction at all primes above p p . These are then applied to study Diophantine stability and integally Diophantine extensions. Along the way, we also obtain some results pertaining to Mazur growth conjecture which refine previous results of Gajek-Leonard, Hatley, Kundu and Lei. Finally, we extend our investigation to the case of an elliptic curve with good supersingular reduction at the prime p p and make a similar analysis. Keywords and Phrases: Selmer groups, ℤ p 2 \mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2} -extension, Diophantine stability, integally Diophantine extensions Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 11G05, 11R23, 11S25.

Introduction

Throughout the paper,
p
will always denote a fixed odd prime. Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over a number field
F
which has potentially good ordinary reduction at all primes of
F
above
p
. A well-known conjecture of Mazur
[
30
]
predicts that the (
p
-primary) Selmer group of the said abelian variety over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
of
F
is cotorsion over the Iwasawa algebra of
\Gamma=\Gal(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)
. The works of Rubin
[
43
]
and Kato
[
16
]
provides many evidences towards this conjecture.

The aim of this paper is study certain consequences of this conjecture of Mazur. Denote by
X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
the Pontryagin dual of the Selmer group of
A
over
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
. Assuming Mazur conjecture conjecture, we may appeal to the structure theory of finitely generated torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
-module and Weierstrass preparation Theorem to define the characteristic polynomial of
X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
, which we write as
\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
. We shall identity
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
with the power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket U\rrbracket
, and under this identification, it makes sense to speak of
\mathrm{ord}_{U}\big(\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})\big)
which is the highest power of
U
dividing
\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
. We can now present our first main result.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.3
)
.

Suppose that
A
is an abelian variety defined over
F
which has potential good ordinary reduction at every prime of
F
above
p
. Let
F_{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
-extension of
F
which contains the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
. Suppose that
X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)\rrbracket
. Denote by
\mathfrak{M}(A/F_{\infty})
the set of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions
K_{\infty}
of
F
contained in
F_{\infty}
such that the Mordell-Weil rank of
A
is unbounded in
K_{\infty}
. Then the set
\mathfrak{M}(A/F_{\infty})
is finite with

\#\mathfrak{M}(A/F_{\infty})\leq\mathrm{ord}_{U}\left(\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}\big(X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})\big)\right).

When
A
is an elliptic curve, the above result has been established in
[
5
, Proposition 3.9]
, albeit under an extra technical assumption. Our result here not only generalizes this to an abelian variety but also remove the said extra assumption (see Remark
3.6
(1)). As a consequence, we may apply our theorem to refine some of the results of Gajek-Leonard-Hatley-Kundu-Lei
[
5
]
and Kundu-Lei
[
22
]
pertaining to the Mazur growth conjecture.

We next come to the study of Diophantine stability and integally Diophantine extensions. Here the notion of Diophantine stability is defined as in the sense of Mazur-Rubin
[
33
]
. On the other hand, the integrally Diophantine extensions are intimately related with the Hilbert’s 10th problem (see
[
32
]
; also see
[
6
,
21
,
24
,
42
]
and references therein for further works on this).
We will apply our theorem to show that, under the assumption of Mazur’s conjecture, there exist an abundance of cyclic extensions, where the abelian variety is Diophantine stable. Consequently, we then see that there is an abundance of cyclic extensions, which are integally Diophantine extensions. However, we must emphasize that while our results show the existence of many such extensions, whether pertaining to Diophantine stability or integrally Diophantine extensions, we are currently unable to ensure that they are specifically extensions of the base field
F
.
To provide a more precise description of our result in these aspects, we begin by letting
n
denote a given positive integer. Denote by
\mathfrak{D}_{n}(A/F)
the set consisting of pairs
(L_{1},L_{2})
satisfying the following:

(1)

L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are contained in a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
with
|L_{1}:L_{2}|=p^{n}
.

(2)

The abelian variety
A
is diophantine-stable for
L_{1}/L_{2}
.

We also denote by
\mathcal{I}_{n}(F)
the set which consists of pairs
(L_{1},L_{2})
satisfying the following:

(1)

L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are contained in a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
with
|L_{1}:L_{2}|=p^{n}
.

(2)

L_{1}/L_{2}
is integrally Diophantine.

Our results are then as follows.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

Let
F
be a number field with at least one complex prime.
Suppose that
A
is an abelian variety defined over
F
with potential good ordinary reduction at every prime above

. Assume that
X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)\rrbracket
. Then the set

\mathfrak{D}_{n}(A/F)
is uncountable for every integer

.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
5.5
)
.

Let
F
be a number field with at least one complex prime.
Suppose that there exist an abelian variety
A
defined over
F
which satisfies all of the following.

(i)

The abelian variety
A
has potential good ordinary reduction at every prime above
p
.

(ii)

X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)\rrbracket
.

(iii)

\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F)>0

Then, for every integer

, the set

\mathcal{I}_{n}(F)
is uncountable.

Combining our result with the deep works of Rubin and Kato, we therefore have the following unconditional result on the Diophantine stability of an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Corollary 1.4

(Corollary
5.2
)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}

.
Suppose that

Furthermore, by invoking deep results of Rubin, Kato, Bump-Friedberg-Hoffstein, Murty-Murty and Gross-Zagier, we have the following unconditional conclusion on the set

.

Corollary 1.5

(Corollary
5.6
)
.

Suppose that
F
is a finite abelian imaginary extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Then for every integer

, the set

\mathcal{I}_{n}(F)
is uncountable.

(Theorem

)

2
Algebraic preliminaries

In this section, we establish certain algebraic preliminaries
and notation that are necessary for the discussion in the paper. We always let
G
denote the group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
, and denote by
\Lambda
the Iwasawa algebra
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
. We fix a subgroup
H
of
G
such that
H\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}\cong G/H
. Fix a choice of topological generators
\sigma
and
\tau
of
G
such that
\tau
is a topological generator of
H
and
\sigma~(\mathrm{mod}~H)
is a topological generator of
\Gamma:=G/H
. We then identified
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
with the power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket U,W\rrbracket
under the correspondence
\sigma\mapsto U,\tau\mapsto W
. We will also frequently identify
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
with the power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket U\rrbracket
.

For each
(a,b)\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, we denote by
H_{a,b}
the subgroup of
G
topologically generated by

\sigma^{a}\tau^{b}
. In particular, one has
H=H_{0,1}
. The Iwasawa algebra
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket H_{a,b}\rrbracket
can then be identified as the power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket(U+1)^{a}(W+1)^{b}-1\rrbracket
. Moreover, we have
G/H_{a,b}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We then write
\Gamma_{a,b}=G/H_{a,b}
, and in particularly, write
\Gamma=\Gamma_{0,1}=G/H
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M
be a torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module. Set

\mathfrak{A}(M):=\{(a,b)\in P_{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\mid M_{H_{a,b}}~\mbox{is not torsion over}~\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G/H_{a,b}\rrbracket\}.

A commutative algebraic argument tells us that the set
\mathfrak{A}(M)
is finite (for instance, see
[
28
, Lemma 2.2]
). The aim of this section is to give an upper bound of the cardinality of this set which is the content of the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
M
be a torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module with the property that
M_{H}
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
. Then we have

\#\mathfrak{A}(M)\leq\mathrm{ord}_{U}(\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}M_{H}).

Note that
M_{H}=M/W
and
H_{1}(H,M)=M[W]
, where the latter is the submodule of
M
consisting of elements annihilated by
W
. We will make use of these identifications without further mention. The remainder of the section will be devoted to the proof of the preceding proposition. As a start, we record the following lemma which is taken from a monograph of Perrin-Riou
[
41
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
M
be a torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module with the property that
M_{H}
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
. Denote by
\pi
the natural projection map
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
. Then
H_{1}(H,M)
is also torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
, and we have

\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}(H_{1}(H,M))\cdot\pi\big(\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M)\big)=\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}(M_{H}).

In particular, if
H_{1}(H,M)
is pseudo-null as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
-module, we then have

\pi\big(\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M)\big)=\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}(M_{H}).

Proof.

See
[
41
, §I.1.3, Lemma 4]
. ∎

We can now give the proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

By definition, we have
(a,b)\in\mathfrak{A}(M)
if and only if
M_{H_{a,b}}
is not torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma_{a,b}\rrbracket
. The latter will force the prime element
f_{a,b}:=(U+1)^{a}(W+1)^{b}-1
to
lie in the support of
M
which in turn implies that
f_{a,b}
divides
\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M)
. Since the
f_{a,b}
’s are mutually coprime and
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
is a unique factorization domain, we have

\prod_{(a,b)\in\mathfrak{N}(M)}f_{a,b}~\Big|~\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M).

Applying the map
\pi
, we obtain the divisibility

\prod_{(a,b)\in\mathfrak{N}(M)}(U+1)^{a}-1~\Big|~\pi\big(\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}(M)\big).

Since
U~\Big|~(U+1)^{a}-1
, and taking Lemma
2.3
into account, we obtain

U^{\#\mathfrak{N}(M)}~\Big|~\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}(M_{H}).

The conclusion of the proposition is now immediate from this.
∎

3
Main theorem

Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over
F
which has potential good ordinary reduction at every prime of
F
above
p
. Let
S
be a finite set of primes of
F
which contains all the primes above
p
, the infinite primes and the primes of bad reduction of
A
. Denote by
F_{S}
the maximal algebraic extension of
F
which is unramified outside S. If
\mathcal{L}
is a (possibly infinite) extension of
F
contained in
F_{S}
, we write
G_{S}(\mathcal{L})=\Gal(F_{S}/\mathcal{L})
and denote by
S(\mathcal{L})
the set of primes of
\mathcal{L}
above
S
.

For each
v\in S
and a finite extension
L
of
F
, set

J_{v}(A/L)=\bigoplus_{w|v}H^{1}(L_{w},A)_{p^{\infty}}.

If
\mathcal{L}
is an infinite extension of
F
contained in
F_{S}
, we define

J_{v}(A/\mathcal{L})=\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}J_{v}(A/L),

where
L
runs through all the finite extensions of
F
contained in
\mathcal{L}
.

The classical (
p
-primary) Selmer group of
A
over
\mathcal{L}
is defined by

\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/\mathcal{L})=\ker\left(H^{1}(G_{S}(\mathcal{L}),A_{p^{\infty}})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S}J_{v}(A/\mathcal{L})\right).

The Pontryagin dual of
\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/\mathcal{L})
is then denoted
by
X(A/\mathcal{L})
.

Throughout our discussion, we shall frequently call upon the following conjecture of Mazur
[
30
]
.

Conjecture (Mazur).
Suppose that
A
is an abelian variety defined over
F
which has potential good ordinary reduction at every prime of
F
above
p
. Let
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
be the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
. Then
X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
is a torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)\rrbracket
-module.

Deep results of Kato
[
16
, Theorem 17.4]
and Rubin
[
43
, Theorem 4.4]
showed that the conjecture holds, whenever
A
is an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction and
F
is an abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Prior to these, Mazur established the validity of his conjecture under the assumption that
\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/F)
is finite. In his proof, Mazur made use of the following, which nowadays is coined as “Mazur control theorem”.

Theorem 3.1

(Mazur Control Theorem)
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over
F
which has potential good ordinary reduction at every prime of
F
above
p
. Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
and
\mathcal{F}_{n}
the intermediate subfield of
\mathcal{F}
with
|\mathcal{F}_{n}:F|=p^{n}
. Then the restriction map

\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/\mathcal{F}_{n})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/\mathcal{F})^{\Gal(\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{F}_{n})}

has finite kernel and cokernel which are bounded independent of
n
.

Proof.

See
[
8
, Proposition 3.7]
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over
F
which has potential good ordinary reduction at every prime of
F
above
p
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
and
\mathcal{F}_{n}
the intermediate subfield of
\mathcal{F}
with
|\mathcal{F}_{n}:F|=p^{n}
. Suppose that
X(A/\mathcal{F})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(\mathcal{F}/F)\rrbracket
. Then
n\to\infty
, we have

\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(\mathcal{F}_{n})=O(1).

Proof.

Since
X(A/\mathcal{F})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(\mathcal{F}/F)\rrbracket
, we have

\mathrm{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left(\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/\mathcal{F})^{\Gal(\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{F}_{n})}\right)=O(1).

By Theorem
3.1
, this in turn implies that

\mathrm{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\big(\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/\mathcal{F}_{n})\big)=O(1).

Since we always have
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}\big(A(\mathcal{F}_{n})\big)\leq\mathrm{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\big(\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/\mathcal{F}_{n})\big)
, the conclusion of the corollary follows.
∎

Let
F_{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
-extension of
F
which contains the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
.
Write
G=\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
. We identify the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
with the power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket U,W\rrbracket
such that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\infty}/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})\rrbracket\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket W\rrbracket
and
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)\rrbracket\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket U\rrbracket
. Under this identification, we write
H_{a,b}
for the subgroup of
G
generated by
(1+U)^{a}(1+W)^{b}-1
, and
\Gamma_{a,b}=G/H_{a,b}
. Denote by
F_{a,b}
the fixed field of
H_{a,b}
. We are interested in the following set

\mathfrak{M}(A/F_{\infty})=\big\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})~|~\mbox{the Mordell-Weil rank of $A$ in $F_{a,b}$ is unbounded}\big\}

The main theorem of the section is presented below, where we show that the set is finite and provides an upper bound for the size of this particular set.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over
F
which has potential good ordinary reduction at every prime of
F
above
p
. Let
F_{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
-extension of
F
which contains the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
. Suppose that
X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)\rrbracket
. Then we have

\#\mathfrak{M}(A/F_{\infty})\leq\mathrm{ord}_{U}\left(\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}\big(X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})\big)\right).

The remainder of the section will be devoted to the proof of Theorem
3.3
. As a start, we have the following general observation.

Lemma 3.4
.

For every
(a,b)\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, the restriction map

\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/F_{a,b})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/F_{\infty})^{H_{a,b}}

has kernel which is cofinitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Proof.

Indeed, the kernel of the restriction map is contained in
H^{1}(H_{a,b},A_{p^{\infty}}(F_{\infty}))
, and the latter is plainly cofinitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
∎

In the context of
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
, we have the following sharper result on the restriction map.

Lemma 3.5
.

The restriction map

\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/F_{\infty})^{H}

has finite kernel and cokernel.

Proof.

Consider the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\textstyle{H^{1}\big(G_{S}(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}),A_{p^{\infty}}\big)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h}
\textstyle{\displaystyle\bigoplus_{v\in S}J_{v}(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\oplus g_{v}}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/F_{\infty})^{H}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{1}\big(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A_{p^{\infty}}\big)^{H}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\left(\displaystyle\bigoplus_{v\in S}J_{v}(A/F_{\infty})\right)^{H}}

with exact rows. Via the snake lemma, it suffices to show that
\ker h
,
\ker g_{v}
and
\mathrm{coker}\,h
are finite. To begin with, we show that
h
is surjective with a finite kernel. Indeed, since
H\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the
restriction-inflation sequence tells us that the map
h
is surjective with kernel
H^{1}(H,A_{p^{\infty}}(F_{\infty}))
. On the other hand, we have

0=\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket H\rrbracket}\Big(A_{p^{\infty}}(F_{\infty})\Big)^{\vee}=\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\Big(\big(A_{p^{\infty}}(F_{\infty})\big)^{\vee}\Big)_{H}-\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\Big(A_{p^{\infty}}(F_{\infty})^{\vee}\Big)^{H}

where the second equality follows from
[
37
, Proposition 5.3.20]
. But observe that

\Big(A_{p^{\infty}}(F_{\infty})^{\vee}\Big)_{H}=\Big(A_{p^{\infty}}(F_{\infty})^{H}\Big)^{\vee}=\Big(A_{p^{\infty}}(F_{\mathrm{cyc}})\Big)^{\vee},

and the latter is finite by a theorem of Imai (
[
13
]
; also see
[
45
, Theorem 3.4]
). Hence
\Big(A_{p^{\infty}}(F_{\infty})^{\vee}\Big)^{H}
is finite. But this group is precisely

H^{1}(H,A_{p^{\infty}}(F_{\infty}))^{\vee}
, and so we have our claim.

It remains to show that
g_{v}
has finite kernel for every
v
. As a start, recall that for
\mathcal{L}\in\{F_{\mathrm{cyc}},F_{\infty}\}
, we have isomorphisms

J_{v}(E/\mathcal{L})\cong\begin{cases}\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}\bigoplus_{w|v}H^{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{w},\tilde{A}_{w,p^{\infty}}),&\mbox{if $v$ divides $p$},\\
\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}\bigoplus_{w|v}H^{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{w},A_{p^{\infty}}),&\mbox{if $v$ does not divide $p$},\end{cases}

where the direct limit is taken over all finite extensions
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
of
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
contained in
\mathcal{L}
(see
[
2
, Propositions 4.1, 4.7 and 4.8]
).

Taking these isomorphisms into account, it follows from the restriction-inflation sequence that we have
\ker g_{v}=\oplus_{w|v}H^{1}(H_{w},D(F_{\infty,w}))
, where here the sum runs over all the primes of
F_{c}
above
v
,
H_{w}
is the decomposition group of
w
in
H
and
D
denotes either
\tilde{A}_{w,p^{\infty}}
or
A_{p^{\infty}}
according as
v
divides
p
or not.
In particular, if
H_{w}=1
, then
H^{1}(H_{w},D(F_{\infty,w}))=0
. This is indeed the case when
v
does not divide
p
. It remains to consider primes
w
which divides
p
and that
H_{w}
is nontrivial. Since
H\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, it then follows that
H_{w}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We may then apply the same argument as in the preceding paragraph to conclude that
H^{1}(H_{w},\tilde{A}_{w,p^{\infty}})
is finite. The proof of the proposition is now complete.
∎

We are in position to prove Theorem
3.3
.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Taking the hypothesis that
X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
into account, it then follows from Lemma
3.5
that
X(A/F_{\infty})_{H}
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket

with

\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})=\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}X(A/F_{\infty})_{H}.

By
[
12
, Lemma 2.6]
,
X(A/F_{\infty})
is a torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module. Thus, we may invoke Proposition
2.2
to conclude that

\#\mathfrak{A}\big(X(A/F_{\infty})\big)\leq\mathrm{ord}_{U}\big(\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})\big).

On the other hand, taking Corollary
3.2
and Lemma
3.4
into account, we have the following inclusion

\mathfrak{M}\big(A/F_{\infty}\big)\subseteq\mathfrak{A}\big(X(A/F_{\infty})\big).

The conclusion of theorem then follows from combining this inclusion with the above inequality.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Retain the assumptions of Theorem

(a)

For

and

If

\mathfrak{S}(A^{*}/K_{\infty})\hookrightarrow H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty}/F,T^{*})\hookrightarrow\Ext^{0}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(K_{\infty}/F)\rrbracket}\big(H^{1}(G_{S}(K_{\infty}),A^{*}_{p^{\infty}})^{\vee},\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(K_{\infty}/F)\rrbracket\big).

We now verify statement (b). Taking (a) into account, we have the following diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\textstyle{H^{1}\big(G_{S}(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}),A_{p^{\infty}}\big)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

4

We now apply Theorem

]

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
with conductor
N
such that it has good ordinary reduction at
p
. Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field of
\mathbb{Q}
which satisfies all of the following.

(i)

The prime

, and every prime divisor of

.

(ii)

One has

is the largest

divisible only by primes that are split in
K
, and
N^{-}
is a squarefree product
of an even number of primes all of which are inert in

.

(iii)

The two primes of

From the discussion in
[
29
, Appendix A.1]
, we see that

is not torsion over

Remark 4.2

(b)

\displaystyle\rank_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket\Gal(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}/K)\rrbracket}X_{\mathfrak{p}^{\infty}}(\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{K}_{\infty})\!

\displaystyle\!\rank_{\mathcal{O}}X_{\mathfrak{p}^{\infty}}(\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{K}_{\infty})_{\Gal(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}/K)}-\rank_{\mathcal{O}}X_{\mathfrak{p}^{\infty}}(\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{K}_{\infty})^{\Gal(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}/K)}

5
Diophantine stability and integrally Diophantine extension

We now apply Theorem
3.3
to study the question of Diophantine stability and integrally Diophantine extensions. To begin with, we let
A
be an abelian variety defined over a number field over
F
, and
L
a finite extension of
F
. Following Mazur-Rubin
[
33
]
, we say that the abelian variety
A
is diophantine-stable for
L/F
if
A(L)=A(F)
.

Let
n
be a given positive integer. We will be concerned with the following set
\mathfrak{D}_{n}(A/F)
which consists of pair
(L_{1},L_{2})
satisfying the following:

(1)

L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are contained in a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
with
|L_{1}:L_{2}|=p^{n}
.

(2)

The abelian variety
A
is diophantine-stable for
L_{1}/L_{2}
.

We are now in position to state our result pertaining to Diophantine stability.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
F
be a number field with at least one complex prime.
Suppose that
A
is an abelian variety defined over
F
with potential good ordinary reduction at every prime above
p
. Suppose that
X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)\rrbracket
. For every integer
n\geq 1
, the set

\mathfrak{D}_{n}(A/F)
is uncountable.

Proof.

Let
F_{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
-extension of
F
which contains
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
. For each
(a,b)\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\setminus\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{A}/F_{\infty})
, the Mordell-Weil rank of
A
is bounded in
F_{a,b}
. On the other hand, a result of Wingberg
[
45
, Theorem 4.3]
tells us that there is at most finitely many
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\infty}
of
F
such that
A(K_{\infty})
has an infinite torsion group. (Remark: Wingberg result’s is stated for a simple abelian variety, but in general, an abelian variety
A
is isogenous to a finite product of simple abelian varieties, and so this finite number of simple abelian varieties will still give finitely many many
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\infty}
of
F
such that

has an infinite torsion group.) Hence we have uncountably many

such that the Mordell-Weil rank of

is bounded in

and the torsion subgroup of

is finite. It then follows from a classical argument (for instances, see
[

) that
A(F_{a,b})
is finitely generated for each of such

. Therefore, we can always find subextensions

in

which lies in
\mathfrak{D}_{n}(A/F)
.
∎

Combining the above with the results of Rubin and Kato, we have the following unconditional result.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at
p
.
Suppose that
F
is a finite abelian imaginary extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Then for every integer

, the set

\mathfrak{D}_{n}(E/F)
is uncountable.

We now come to the topic of integrally diophantine extensions, where we begin by recalling their definitions.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring with identity and let
n
be a given positive
integer. We say that a subset
\Sigma
of
R
is Diophantine in
R
if there exists a positive integer
m
and a polynomial
P(x,y_{1},...,y_{m})
with coefficients in
R
such that

a
is in
\Sigma
if and only if there exist elements
b_{1},...,b_{m}
of
R
for which

P(a,b_{1},...,b_{m})=0
.

Let
L_{1}/L_{2}
be an extension of number fields. If the ring of integers of
L_{2}
is Diophantine in the ring of integers of
L_{1}
, then
L_{1}/L_{2}
is said to be integrally Diophantine.

A conjecture of Denef and Lipshitz predicts that

is integrally diophantine for every number field

(see
[
3
]
), and the validity of this conjecture has been established by Koyman and Pagano (see
[
21
]
). It’s then natural ask whether the following will always hold.

Relative Denef-Lipshitz conjecture.

Every finite extension of number field
L/K
is integrally diophantine.

The following theorem of Shlapentokh
[
44
, Theorem 1.9]
(also see
[
34
, Theorem 3.1]
) is a fundamental tool for the study of these type of problems.

Theorem 5.4

(Shlapentokh)
.

Let
L_{2}/L_{1}
be an extension of number fields. Suppose that
there is an abelian abelian
A
defined over
L_{1}
such that
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(L_{2})=\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(L_{1})>0
. Then
L_{2}/L_{1}
is integrally Diophantine.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{n}(F)
be the set which consists of pair
(L_{1},L_{2})
satisfying the following:

(1)

L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are contained in a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
with
|L_{1}:L_{2}|=p^{n}
.

(2)

L_{1}/L_{2}
is integrally Diophantine.

Our result concerning integrally Diophantine extensions is as follows.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
F
be a number field with at least one complex prime.
Suppose that there exist an abelian variety
A
defined over
F
which satisfies all of the following.

(i)

The abelian variety
A
has potential good ordinary reduction at every prime above
p
.

(ii)

X(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)\rrbracket
.

(iii)

\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F)>0
.

Then, for every integer
n\geq 1
, the set

\mathcal{I}_{n}(F)
is uncountable.

Proof.

As seen in the proof of Theorem
5.1
, there exists uncountably many
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of
F
such that the Mordell-Weil rank of
A
is bounded. Therefore, for each such
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension, one can always find intermediate extension
L_{1}/L_{2}
with
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(L_{1})=\rank A(L_{2})
, and this common rank is necessarily greater than
0
by hypothesis (iii). Hence we may apply Shlapentokh’s theorem to conclude that
L_{1}/L_{2}
is integrally Diophantine.
∎

Unconditionally, we have the following.

Corollary 5.6
.

Suppose that
F
is a finite abelian imaginary extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Then for every integer
n\geq 1
, the set

\mathcal{I}_{n}(F)
is uncountable.

Proof.

By
[
8
, Proposition 5.4 and Remark after which]
, there exist an elliptic curve
E
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
having good ordinary reduction at
p
with
L(E/\mathbb{Q},1)\neq 0
. By appealing to the work of Bump-Friedberg-Hoffstein
[
1
]
or that of Murty-Murty
[
35
]
, we can find a quadratic twist
E^{(D)}
of
E
such that
L(E^{(D)}/\mathbb{Q},s)
has a simple zero at
s=1
. From the deep result of Gross-Zagier
[
10
]
, this in turn implies
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E^{(D)}(\mathbb{Q})=1
. In particular, we have
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E^{(D)}(F)>0
. On the other hand, a result of Kato
[

]
tells us that

is torsion over

are satisfied, and this gives the conclusion of the corollary.
∎

We end the section with another result which is useful in obtaining cases of uncountable

and
\mathcal{I}_{n}(L)
for non-abelian

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
F
be a number field with at least one complex prime.
Suppose that there exist an elliptic curve
E
defined over

which satisfies all of the following.

(i)

The elliptic curve

p
.

(ii)

It’s well-known that under assumptions of the proposition,

We give an example to illustrate Proposition

Some further remark on Theorem

We retain the notation and setting of Section

. In particular, we continue to identify

and is not an associate of

If one assumes the elementary factors of

for every

\mathrm{ord}_{U}\Big(\mathrm{char}\big(X(\mathcal{A}/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})\big)\Big)=\corank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\big(\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(\mathcal{A}/F)\big).

We note that the above assumption is plainly true if

.
(for instance, see
[
8
, Page 59, 1st paragraph]
or
[
27
, Proposition 4.5]
).
In fact, this assumption is a consequence of the following semi-simplicity conjecture of Greenberg
[
8
, Conjecture 1.12]
.

Conjecture 6.1

(Greenberg)
.

\beta_{j}=1
for every
j
.

Plainly, this conjecture of Greenberg is true if the Iwasawa
\lambda
-invariant is
1
. To the best knowledge of the author, there seems very little evidence in literature on Conjecture
6.1
. In
[
27
, Lemma 4.6]
, the author gives a simple criterion (which is far from being sufficient!) for verifying this conjecture.

Granted this conjecture of Greenberg, we have the following interesting (conjectural) observation.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over
F
with potential good ordinary reduction at every prime above
p
. Let
F_{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
-extension of

which contained the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension

. Suppose that

\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)\rrbracket
. If we assume that the semisimplicity conjecture of Greenberg holds, then we have

If we further assume that the

The above observation, though conjectural, is very interesting, as it is saying that the number of

-extensions in a

-extension at which

at the base field! It is also interesting to note that this upper bound is independent of the

-extension (as long as it contains

Definition 7.7

For

\mathfrak{Z}^{s,z}(E/F_{\infty})=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})~|~\mbox{$X^{s,z}(E/F_{a,b})$ is not torsion over $\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{a,b}/F)\rrbracket$.}\}

We now present the following signed analogue of Theorem
3.3
.

Theorem 7.8
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N
over
\mathbb{Q}
with good supersingular reduction at the prime
p
and
a_{p}=0
. Let
F
be an imaginary quadratic field of
\mathbb{Q}
at which all the prime divisors of
p
split completely in
F/\mathbb{Q}
. If
X^{s,z}(E/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
, then
X^{s,z}(E/F_{\infty})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
, and we have the following estimate

\#\mathfrak{Z}^{s,z}(E/F_{\infty})\leq\mathrm{ord}_{U}\big(\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}X^{s,z}(E/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})\big).

In particular, if
X^{s,z}(E/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
for every
s,z\in\{+,-\}
, we have

\#\mathfrak{M}(E/F_{\infty})\leq\sum_{s,z\in\{+,-\}}\mathrm{ord}_{U}\big(\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}X^{s,z}(E/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})\big).

Proof.

The proof proceeds quite similarly to that in Theorem
3.3
, and we give a sketch of it here. We begin noting that in view of Lemma
7.4
, for every
(a,b)\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, there is a restriction map

\mathrm{Sel}^{s,z}(E/F_{a,b})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sel}^{s,z}(E/F_{\infty})^{\Gal(F_{\infty}/F_{a,b})}

on the signed Selmer groups. Furthermore, this restriction map has kernel contained in
H^{1}(H_{a,b},E_{p^{\infty}}(F_{\infty}))
. Since
E_{p^{\infty}}(F_{\infty})=0
by
[
20
, Proposition 8.7]
, the restriction map is therefore an injection. Therefore, it follows that if
X^{s,z}(E/F_{a,b})
is not torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{a,b}/F)\rrbracket
, then so is
X^{s,z}(E/F_{\infty})_{H_{a,b}}
. Thus we have the inclusion

\mathfrak{Z}^{s,z}\big(E/F_{\infty}\big)\subseteq\mathfrak{A}\big(X^{s,z}(E/F_{\infty})\big).

On the other hand, in the case of
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
, the argument of the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 5.1]
shows that the restriction map

\mathrm{Sel}^{s,z}(E/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sel}^{s,z}(E/F_{\infty})^{\Gal(F_{\infty}/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})}

is bijective. Hence we may proceed similarly to that in Theorem
3.3
to obtain the estimate

\#\mathfrak{Z}^{s,z}(E/F_{\infty})\leq\mathrm{ord}_{U}\big(\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}X^{s,z}(E/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})\big).

On the other hand, it follows from
[
5
, Corollary 4.6]
that

\mathfrak{M}(E/F_{\infty})\subseteq\bigcup_{s,z\in\{+,-\}}\mathfrak{Z}^{s,z}(E/F_{\infty}).

Combining the above observations, we have the final estimate of the theorem.
∎

Remark 7.9
.

(1)

We note that this result refines
[
5
, Proposition A.10]
, where we remove the assumption that
X^{s,z}(E/F_{\infty})
is a sum of cyclic torsion modules. This assumption is required for the authors for proving

\pi\big(\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket}X^{s,z}(A/F_{\infty})\big)=\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}X^{s,z}(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}),

where
\pi:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
is the map induced by the natural projection
G\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma
. Although we do not require this, we mention quickly how this identity can actually be proven unconditionally. By the torsionness assumption of the signed Selmer groups, it follows from
[
25
, Propositions 4.6 and 4.12]
that we have the surjectivity of the defining sequence of the signed Selmer groups and the vanishing of
H^{2}(G_{S}(\mathcal{L}),E_{p^{\infty}})
for
\mathcal{L}=F_{\mathrm{cyc}},F_{\infty}
. Therefore, we may apply the argument of Proposition
3.7
to obtain the vanishing of
H^{1}(H,\mathrm{Sel}^{s,z}(E/F_{\infty}))
. The required identity then follows from this and Lemma
2.3
.

(2)

In
[
19
, Theorem 2.18]
, the authors obtain an upper bound in term of the sum of the
\lambda
-invariants of the signed Selmer groups. The bound presented here, and also that in
[
5
]
, is therefore an improvement of theirs.

We now apply Theorem
7.10
to the study of Diophantine stability for an elliptic curve with supersingular reduction at
p
. Indeed, it has been shown in
[
5
, Corollary 4.6]
that if
(a,b)\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
has the property that
X^{s,z}(E/F_{a,b})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{a,b}/F)\rrbracket
for every
s,z\in\{+,-\}
, then the Mordell-Weil ranks of
E
are bounded in
F_{a,b}/F
. Therefore, if we have that

X^{s,z}(E/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)\rrbracket
for every
s,z\in\{+,-\}
, then the set
\mathfrak{D}_{n}(E/F)
is uncountable for every integer
n\geq 1
. Instead of writing this down formally, we prefer to present the following unconditional result.

Theorem 7.10
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N
over
\mathbb{Q}
with good supersingular reduction at the prime
p
and
a_{p}=0
. Let
F
be an imaginary quadratic field of
\mathbb{Q}
such that
p
splits completely in
F/\mathbb{Q}
, and the primes of
F
above
p
are totally ramified in
F_{\mathrm{ac}}/F
. Suppose either of the following statement is valid.

(i)

\mathrm{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F)
is finite.

(ii)

Every prime divisor of
N
splits completely in
F/\mathbb{Q}
.

Then, for every integer
n\geq 1
, the set

\mathfrak{D}_{n}(E/F)
is uncountable.

Proof.

Under assumption (i), it has been proven in
[
5
, Proposition 4.7]
that
X^{s,z}(E/F_{a,b})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{a,b}/F)\rrbracket
for every
s,z\in\{+,-\}
and every
(a,b)\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. We will therefore left with establishing the theorem under assumption (ii). As seen before, the module
X^{s,s}(E/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)\rrbracket
by
[
26
, Proposition 8.4]
, and so Theorem
7.8
tells us that
\mathfrak{Z}^{s,s}(E/F_{\infty})
is finite for
s\in\{+,-\}
. It remains to show that
\mathfrak{Z}^{s,z}(E/F_{\infty})
is finite for
s\neq z
. For this, we will go from the anticyclotomic direction. Under assumption (ii), it is shown in
[
5
, Theorem A.13]
that
X^{s,z}(E/F_{ac})
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\mathrm{ac}}/F)\rrbracket
. We may then apply the argument in
[
25
, Theorem 5.1]
to show that the restriction map

\mathrm{Sel}^{s,z}(E/F_{\mathrm{ac}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sel}^{s,z}(E/F_{\infty})^{\Gal(F_{\infty}/F_{\mathrm{ac}})}

is an isomorphism, thanks to our ramification assumption on
p
and that every prime divisor of
N
splits in
F/\mathbb{Q}
. Indeed, these two assumptions ensure that the local result obtained in
[
25
, Proposition 3.8]
applies (also see Lemma
7.4
), and that every prime divisor of
N
in
F
are finitely decomposed in
F_{\mathrm{ac}}/F
which is required for
J_{v}(E/F_{\mathrm{ac}})
to be written as a finite sum (see Remark
3.6
). As a consequence, we can show that
\mathfrak{Z}^{s,z}(E/F_{\infty})
is finite with

\#\mathfrak{Z}^{s,z}(E/F_{\infty})\leq\mathrm{ord}_{W}\big(\mathrm{char}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{ac}/F)\rrbracket}X^{s,z}(E/F_{\mathrm{ac}})\big).

The conclusion of the theorem then follows.
∎

Remark 7.11
.
