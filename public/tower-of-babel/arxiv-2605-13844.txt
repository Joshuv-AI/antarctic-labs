Fields where torsion forms decompose
M. Archita, Karim Johannes Becher

Abstract
. Over a real field which is an extension of transcendence degree 1 1 of a hereditarily pythagorean base field, every quadratic form which is torsion decomposes into an orthogonal sum of 2 2 -dimensional torsion forms. This is obtained from a more general study of weakly isotropic forms over henselian valued fields and over function fields in one variable. Keywords: Real field, pythagorean field, sums of squares, quadratic form, hyperbolic, weakly isotropic, henselian valuation, local-global principle Classification (MSC 2020): 11E04, 11E81, 12D15, 12J10



1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a field of characteristic different from
2
.
By the Artin-Schreier Theorem
[
11
, Chap. VIII, Theorem 1.10]
,
K
can be endowed with a field ordering if and only if
-1
is not a sum of squares in
K
. In this case we call the field
K

real
, otherwise
nonreal
.

We denote by
\mathsf{W}K
the Witt ring of quadratic forms over
K
.
A regular quadratic form over
K
which corresponds to a torsion element of
\mathsf{W}K
is called a
torsion form
.
If
K
is nonreal, then
\mathsf{W}K
is a torsion group, and hence every regular quadratic form over
K
is torsion, and by diagonalization it decomposes into a sum of
1
-dimensional torsion forms. Assume that
K
is real.
In that case, Pfister’s Local-Global Principle
[
11
, Chap. VIII, Theorem 3.2]
characterizes torsion forms as those regular quadratic forms having signature zero at every field ordering of
K
.
In particular, torsion forms are even-dimensional.
Two-dimensional quadratic forms are called
binary forms
.
In
[
1
]
, J.K. Arason and A. Pfister called a quadratic form
strongly balanced
(
stark ausgeglichen
) if it is isometric to a sum of binary torsion forms.
They studied the problem over which real fields all torsion forms (
weakly balanced
/
schwach ausgeglichen
) are in fact strongly balanced.

In particular, they show that the quadratic form

\langle 1,Y,-(3+X^{2}+Y),-Y(1+X^{2}+3Y)\rangle

over
\mathbb{Q}(X,Y)
(the rational function field in two variables
X,Y
over
\mathbb{Q}
) is torsion but not strongly balanced.
See
2.7
for the more general statement on rational function fields in two variables.
No example of such a quadratic form seems to be known over a field that is not a rational function field in two variables over some real field.

In particular, one may ask over which base fields
K
every real field extension of transcendence degree
1
has the property that all torsion forms are strongly balanced.
When
K
is a real number field,
[
1
, Satz 5]
establishes this property for the rational function field
K(X)
, but it is still open whether the same holds for its finite extensions.

In this article, we settle this problem for a different class of base fields, confirming
[
2
, Conjecture 4.4]
by proving the following.

Theorem
.

Let
K
be a hereditarily pythagorean field. Let
F/K
be a field extension of transcendence degree
1
such that
F
is real.
Then every torsion form over
F
is strongly balanced.

Recall a field is called
pythagorean
if in it every sum of two squares is equal to a square,
and
hereditarily
pythagorean
if it is real and all its finite real field extensions are pythagorean.

The theorem will be established by
5.6
, which states a formulation that is equivalent in view of
2.5
.
We will use Bröcker’s valuation theoretic characterization of hereditarily pythagorean fields
[
8
, Prop. 3.5]
and derive our theorem from a more general relation for function fields in one variable over a henselian valued field.
Our proof follows the path of
[
4
]
, using the same methods. In particular, it relies on
[
4
, Theorem 4.4]
, a variant of Mehmeti’s local-global principle from
[
12
]
for quadratic forms over a function field in one variable with a henselian rank-
1
valued base field.
For the present purpose, however, we need an extension covering infinite-dimensional quadratic spaces, which is provided by
4.1
and of independent interest.

We will use basic facts and standard terminology from quadratic form theory and valuation theory, our standard references being
[
11
]
and
[
9
]
, respectively.

2.
Minimal weakly isotropic forms

By a
quadratic space over
K
, we mean a pair
\varphi=(V,q)
of a
K
-vector space
V

and a map
q:V\to K
such that

V\times V\to K,(x,y)\mapsto q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y)
is
K
-bilinear and
q(\lambda x)=\lambda^{2}x
for every
\lambda\in K
and every
x\in V
.
A
quadratic form
is a finite-dimensional quadratic space, and if the corresponding
K
-bilinear form is non-degenerate, it is said to be
regular
.

Let
\varphi=(V,q)
be a quadratic space over
K
.
We call
\varphi

isotropic
if
q(v)=0
for some
v\in V\smallsetminus\{0\}
, and
anisotropic
otherwise.
For a field extension
L/K
,
q
extends uniquely to a map
q_{L}:V_{L}\to L
on the
L
-vector space
V_{L}=V\otimes_{K}L
that makes
(V_{L},q_{L})
into a quadratic space over
L
, which we denote by
\varphi_{L}
.

Let
\varphi
be a regular quadratic form over
K
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
n\times\varphi
the
n
-fold orthogonal sum
\varphi\perp\ldots\perp\varphi
. We call
\varphi

weakly isotropic
if
n\times\varphi
is isotropic for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and we call
\varphi

minimal weakly isotropic
if it is weakly isotropic but contains no proper weakly isotropic subform over
K
.
We denote by
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)
the dimension of
\varphi
.
In
[
2
]
, the second author of the present article introduced the following field invariant:

w(K)=\mathsf{sup}\{\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)\mid\varphi\mbox{ minimal weakly isotropic form over }K\}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}\,.

We continue the study of this invariant.

Proposition 2.1
.

We have
w(K)=1
if and only if
K
is nonreal.

Proof.

There exists a
1
-dimensional weakly isotropic quadratic form over
K
if and only if
-1
is a sum of squares, that is, if and only if
K
is nonreal, and in that case, any
1
-dimensional quadratic form over
K
is weakly isotropic.
∎

For a quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
, we denote by
\mathsf{D}(\varphi)
the set of nonzero elements represented by
\varphi
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{K}^{\times}
, we write
\mathsf{D}\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle
instead of
\mathsf{D}(\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle)
.
We further denote by
{(\mathsf{\Sigma}K^{2})}^{\times}
the subgroup of
{K}^{\times}
consisting of the nonzero sums of squares in
K
.
A quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
is called
totally positive
if it is regular and
\mathsf{D}(\varphi)\subseteq{(\mathsf{\Sigma}K^{2})}^{\times}
.

The following statement generalizes
[
1
, Satz 1, Folgerung]
, which corresponds to the case where
r=2
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\geqslant 2
. Assume that, for every
t_{0},\dots,t_{r}\in{(\mathsf{\Sigma}K^{2})}^{\times}
, there exists a totally positive
r
-dimensional quadratic form
\rho
over
K
with
t_{0},\dots,t_{r}\in\mathsf{D}(\rho)
.
Then
w(K)\leqslant r
.

Proof.

In view of
2.1
we may assume that
K
is real.
Let
\varphi
be a weakly isotropic quadratic form over
K
with
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)>r
.
We need to show that
\varphi
is not minimal weakly isotropic.

Note that the hypothesis of the statement remains true when the value of
r
is increased.
Therefore we may assume that
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)=r+1
.
Let
a_{0},\dots,a_{r}\in{K}^{\times}
be such that
\varphi\simeq\langle a_{0},\dots,a_{r}\rangle
.
Since
\varphi
is weakly isotropic, there exist
t_{0},\dots,t_{r}\in{(\mathsf{\Sigma}K^{2})}^{\times}
such that
\langle a_{0}t_{0},\dots,a_{r}t_{r}\rangle
is isotropic.
By the hypothesis, there exists a totally positive
r
-dimensional quadratic form
\rho
over
K
such that
t_{0},\dots,t_{r}\in\mathsf{D}(\rho)
.
Let
s_{1},\dots,s_{r}\in{K}^{\times}
be such that
\rho\simeq\langle s_{1},\dots,s_{r}\rangle
.
Since
\rho
is totally positive, we have that
s_{1},\dots,s_{r}\in{(\mathsf{\Sigma}K^{2})}^{\times}
.
Since
\rho\otimes\varphi
is isotropic, there exist
b_{1},\dots,b_{r}\in\mathsf{D}(\varphi)
such that
\langle s_{1}b_{1},\dots,s_{r}b_{r}\rangle
is isotropic.
Then
\varphi
has an
r
-dimensional subform
\varphi^{\prime}
such
b_{1},\dots,b_{r}\in\mathsf{D}(\varphi^{\prime})
.
It follows that
\rho\otimes\varphi^{\prime}
is isotropic.
Since
\rho
is totally positive, we obtain that
\varphi^{\prime}
is weakly isotropic.
Therefore
\varphi
is not minimal weakly isotropic.
∎

We denote by
p(K)
the smallest
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that every sum of squares in
K
can be written as a sum of
n
squares, and we set
p(K)=\infty
if no such integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
exists.
This field invariant is called the
Pythagoras number of
K
.
Hence
K
is pythagorean if and only if
p(K)=1
.
We retrieve
[
2
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Corollary 2.3
.

If
K
is not real pythagorean, then
w(K)\leqslant p(K)
.

Proof.

We may assume that
1<p(K)<\infty
, and then apply
2.2
with
r=p(K)
and
\rho=r\times\langle 1\rangle
.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\pi
be a Pfister form over
K
.
Then
\pi
is strongly balanced
if and only if there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n<p(K)
such that
n\times\langle 1\rangle\perp\pi
is isotropic.

Proof.

Assume that
\pi
is isometric to an orthogonal sum of binary torsion forms.
In particular,
\pi
contains some binary torsion form
\beta
.
Let
a,d\in{K}^{\times}
be such that
\beta\simeq a\langle 1,-d\rangle
over
K
.
Then
a\in\mathsf{D}(\beta)\subseteq\mathsf{D}(\pi)
. Since
\pi
is a Pfister form, it follows that
a\pi\simeq\pi
, by
[
11
, Chap. X, Theorem 2.8]
.
Since
\langle 1,-d\rangle\simeq a\beta
, which is a subform of
a\pi\simeq\pi
,
we conclude that
\langle 1,-d\rangle
is a subform of
\pi
.
As
\beta
is torsion, we have
d\in{(\mathsf{\Sigma}K^{2})}^{\times}
.
Hence
d
is a sum of
n+1
squares in
K
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n<p(K)
.
Then the form
(n+1)\times\langle 1\rangle\perp\langle-d\rangle
over
K
is isotropic.
As
\langle 1,-d\rangle
is a subform of
\pi
, it follows that
n\times\langle 1\rangle\perp\pi
is isotropic.

Conversely, assume there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n<p(K)
such that
n\times\langle 1\rangle\perp\pi
is isotropic. Let
\pi^{\prime}
be the quadratic form over
K
such that
\pi\simeq\langle 1\rangle\perp\pi^{\prime}
.
Then
(n+1)\times\langle 1\rangle\perp\pi^{\prime}
is isotropic.
Therefore
-\pi^{\prime}
and
(n+1)\times\langle 1\rangle
represent some element
d\in{K}^{\times}
in common.
Then
d
is a sum of
n+1
squares in
K
and
\langle 1,-d\rangle
is a subform of
\pi
.
It follows that
\pi_{K(\sqrt{d})}
is isotropic, and hence hyperbolic, because
\pi
is a Pfister form. Using
[
11
, Chap. VII, Theorem 3.2]
, we obtain that
\pi\simeq\langle\!\langle d\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\psi
for some quadratic form
\psi
over
K
.
For
r=\mathsf{dim}(\psi)
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\in{K}^{\times}
with
\psi\simeq\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\rangle
, we obtain that

\pi\simeq\langle\!\langle d\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\psi\simeq\langle a_{1},-a_{1}d\rangle\perp\ldots\perp\langle a_{r},-a_{r}d\rangle\,,

and since
d
is a sum of squares, this shows that
\pi
is strongly balanced.
∎

Corollary 2.5

(Arason-Pfister)
.

We have
w(K)\leqslant 2
if and only if, for every torsion
2
-fold Pfister form
\pi
over
K
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n<p(K)
such that
n\times\langle 1\rangle\perp\pi
is isotropic.
Furthermore,
w(K)=2
if and only if
K
is real and every torsion form over
K
is strongly balanced.

Proof.

This follows from
[
1
, Satz 4]
together with
2.4
.
∎

In view of
2.5
, the example by Arason and Pfister mentioned in the introduction shows that
w(\mathbb{Q}(X,Y))\geqslant 3
.
It is plausible that there exist real fields with arbitrarily large (or infinite)
w
-invariant, but so far there is no confirmed example of a field
K
with
w(K)>3
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\mathsf{I}^{n}K
the
n
th power of the fundamental ideal
\mathsf{I}K
in
\mathsf{W}K
, the Witt ring of quadratic forms over
K
.
We further denote by
\mathsf{I}_{\mathsf{tors}}^{n}K
the torsion part of
\mathsf{I}^{n}K
.

Proposition 2.6
.

If
n\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
\mathsf{I}^{n+2}_{\mathsf{tors}}K=0
, then
w(K)\leqslant 2^{n}
.

Proof.

See
[
2
, Theorem 3.5]
.
∎

Note that
(\mathsf{I}_{\mathsf{tors}}K)^{n}\subseteq\mathsf{I}_{\mathsf{tors}}^{n}K
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
.

Theorem 2.7

(Arason-Pfister)
.

Let
k
be a real field with
(\mathsf{I}_{\mathsf{tors}}k)^{2}\neq 0
.
Then there exist
u_{1},u_{2}\in{(\mathsf{\Sigma}k^{2})}^{\times}
such that
u_{2}\notin\mathsf{D}\langle 1,u_{1}\rangle
. For any such elements
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
, the quadratic form
\langle\!\langle-Y,u_{1}+X^{2}+u_{2}Y\rangle\!\rangle
over
K=k(X,Y)
is torsion but not strongly balanced.
In particular
w(K)\geqslant 3
.

Proof.

Since
(\mathsf{I}_{\mathsf{tors}}k)^{2}\neq 0
, there exist

u,u^{\prime}\in{(\mathsf{\Sigma}k^{2})}^{\times}
such that
\langle 1,-u,-u^{\prime},uu^{\prime}\rangle
is anisotropic.
For
u_{1}=uu^{\prime}
and
u_{2}=u
, we obtain that
u_{1},u_{2}\in{(\mathsf{\Sigma}k^{2})}^{\times}
and
u_{2}\notin\mathsf{D}\langle 1,u_{1}\rangle
.
It follows by
[
1
, Satz 3]
that
there exists no
t\in{(\mathsf{\Sigma}k(X)^{2})}^{\times}
such that
X^{2}+u_{1}
and
u_{2}

are represented by the binary form
\langle 1,t\rangle
over
k(X)
.
Therefore
[
1
, Satz 2]
shows that
\pi
has the claimed properties.
∎

Example 2.8
.

Real fields
k
with
u_{1},u_{2}\in{(\mathsf{\Sigma}k^{2})}^{\times}
such that
u_{2}\notin\mathsf{D}\langle 1,u_{1}\rangle
are:

(i)

Any real field
k
with
p(k)\geqslant 3
. (Take
u_{1}=1
and
u_{2}\in{(\mathsf{\Sigma}k^{2})}^{\times}
not a sum of two squares in
k
.)

(ii)

Any real number field. (Special case of
(i)
, since
p(k)\in\{3,4\}
, by
[
11
, Chap. XI, Example 5.9 (2)]
.)

(iii)

The fields
\mathbb{R}(t_{1},t_{2})
,
\mathbb{R}(\!(t_{1})\!)(t_{2})
and
\mathbb{R}(\!(t_{1},t_{2})\!)
. (Take
u_{1}=t_{1}^{2}+t_{2}^{2}
and
u_{2}=t_{1}^{2}(t_{1}+1)^{2}+t_{2}^{2}(t_{1}-1)^{2}
; see
[
6
, Example 6.6]
.)

In these cases, we obtain for
K=k(X,Y)
that
w(K)\geqslant 3
, by
2.7
, and
furthermore
\mathsf{I}^{4}_{\mathsf{tors}}K\neq 0
and
p(K)\geqslant 5
.

We list a few crucial open questions in the study of the invariant
w
.

Question 2.9
.

Does there exist a field
K
with
w(K)\geqslant 3
and
p(K)\leqslant 4
?

Question 2.10
.

Does there exist a field
K
with
w(K)\geqslant 3
and
\mathsf{I}^{4}_{\mathsf{tors}}K=0
?

Question 2.11
.

Does there exists a field
K
with
w(K)>3
?

Question 2.12
.

Is
w(K)\leqslant 2
if
K/\mathbb{Q}
is an extension of transcendence degree
1
?

3.
Valuations and weakly isotropic forms

We now study the interplay between valuations and the
w
-invariant.

Consider a valuation
v
on
K
.
We denote by
vK
its value group, and we call
v

trivial
if
vK=0
.
We denote by
\mathcal{O}_{v}
the valuation ring of
v
and by
\mathfrak{m}_{v}
its maximal ideal.
We set
Kv=\mathcal{O}_{v}/\mathfrak{m}_{v}
and call it the
residue field of
v
.
For
a\in\mathcal{O}_{v}
we write
\overline{a}^{v}
for the residue
a+\mathfrak{m}_{v}
in
Kv
.

Assume that
\mathsf{char}(Kv)\neq 2
. A quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
is called
v
-unimodular
if
\varphi\simeq\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle
for
n=\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)
and certain
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{v}
.
In this case, the form
\langle\overline{a}^{v}_{1},\dots,\overline{a}_{n}^{v}\rangle
over
Kv
is determined up to isometry by
\varphi
, and it is called the
residue form of
\varphi
(with respect to
v
) and denoted by
\overline{\varphi}^{v}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

We have
w(K)\geqslant w(Kv)
.

Proof.

Consider an anisotropic, minimal weakly isotropic quadratic form
\phi
over
Kv
.
Set
n=\mathsf{dim}(\phi)
and choose
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{v}
such that

\phi\simeq\langle\overline{a}_{1}^{v},\dots,\overline{a}_{n}^{v}\rangle
.
Since
\phi
is minimal weakly isotropic, there exist
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{v}
which are sums of squares in
K
and such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\vphantom{t}a}^{v}_{i}\overline{t}^{v}_{i}=0
,
whereby
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}t_{i}\in\mathfrak{m}_{v}
.
Let
a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}t_{n}^{-1}a_{i}
.
Then
-\overline{a}^{v}=\overline{a}_{n}^{v}
in
Kv
.
By the choice of
a
, the quadratic form
\varphi=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1},-a\rangle
over
K
is weakly isotropic.
Furthermore,
\vartheta
is
v
-unimodular and
\phi\simeq\overline{\varphi}^{v}
.
We choose a weakly isotropic subform
\vartheta
of
\varphi
with
\mathsf{dim}(\vartheta)\leqslant w(K)
.
Since
\overline{\varphi}^{v}=\phi
, which is anisotropic, it follows that
\vartheta
is unimodular and
\overline{\vartheta}^{v}
is a weakly isotropic subform of
\phi
.
Since
\phi
was minimal weakly isotropic, we obtain that
\overline{\vartheta}^{v}\simeq\phi
and hence
\mathsf{dim}(\phi)=\mathsf{dim}(\vartheta)\leqslant w(K)
.
This argument shows that
w(Kv)\leqslant w(K)
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

For every algebraic field extension
L/K
, we have

w(L)\leqslant w(K(X))\,.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case of a finite extension
L=K[X]/(p)
where
p\in K[X]
is a monic irreducible polynomial.
Then
L
is the residue field of the
p
-adic valuation on
K(X)
.
Hence
w(K(X))\geqslant w(L)
, by
3.1
.
∎

The following is a variation of
[
2
, Prop. 5.5]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
v
be a henselian valuation on
K
.
Then
w(K)=w(Kv)
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a weakly isotropic form over
K
.
It follows by Durfee’s Theorem
[
5
, Theorem 3.11]
that there exists
c\in{K}^{\times}
and a
v
-unimodular quadratic form
\vartheta
over
K
such that
c\vartheta
is a subform of
\varphi
and
\overline{\vartheta}^{v}
is weakly isotropic.
Then
\overline{\vartheta}^{v}
contains a weakly isotropic subform of dimension at most
w(Kv)
. This subform is the residue form
\overline{\psi}^{v}
of a weakly isotropic form
\psi
over
K
.
Since
v
is henselian, it follows again from Durfee’s Theorem that
\psi
is a subform of
\vartheta
.
Then
c\psi
is a weakly isotropic subform of
\varphi
of dimension at most
w(Kv)
.
This argument shows that
w(K)\leqslant w(Kv)
.
The converse inequality is given by
3.1
.
∎

4.
Local-global principle for function fields in one variable

Mehmeti’s local-global principle from
[
12
]
for quadratic forms over function fields in one variable over a
complete rank-
1
valued base field can be extended to quadratic spaces that are not necessarily finite-dimensional.

Consider a valuation
v
on
K
.
We denote by
\mathsf{Conv}(vK)
the set of all proper convex subgroups of
vK
.
This set is totally ordered by inclusion.
We set
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)=|\mathsf{Conv}(vK)|\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
and call this the
rank of
v
.
We denote by
K^{v}
the henselization
(see
[
9
, Sect. 5.2]
) and by
\hat{K}^{v}
the completion with respect to
v
.
If
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)=1
, then
\hat{K}^{v}
is henselian with respect to the natural extension of
v
, by
[
9
, Theorem 1.3.1]
, whereby
K^{v}\subseteq\hat{K}^{v}
.
For a convex subgroup
\Delta
of
vK
,
composing
v
with the quotient map
vK\rightarrow vK/\Delta
defines a valuation

v_{1}:K\rightarrow(vK/\Delta)\cup\{\infty\}
with
\mathcal{O}_{v}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{v_{1}}
, also called a
coarsening of
v
.

We denote by
\mathcal{V}(K)
the set of equivalence classes
[\nu]
of valuations
\nu
on
K
.
For a class
\gamma\in\mathcal{V}(K)
of a valuation
\nu
, we set
\mathcal{O}_{\gamma}=\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
,
K\gamma=K\nu
and we denote by
K^{\gamma}
the henselization of
K
with respect to
\nu
.
We endow
\mathcal{V}(K)
with the constructible topology, defined as the coarsest topology such that the subsets
\{\gamma\in\mathcal{V}(K)\mid a\in\mathcal{O}_{\gamma}\}
are open and closed for all
a\in{K}^{\times}
.
This turns
\mathcal{V}(K)
into
a compact Hausdorff space; see
[
10
, Sect. 1]
.

A finitely generated field extension of transcendence degree
1
is called a
function field in one variable
.
Given a rank-
1
valuation
v
on
K
and a function field in one variable
F/K
,
we set

\mathcal{V}(F/v)=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{V}(F)\mid\mathcal{O}_{\gamma}\cap K=\mathcal{O}_{v}\}\,.

Note that
\mathcal{V}(F/v)
is closed in
\mathcal{V}(F)
, and hence it is a compact subspace.

The following is a variation of
[
3
, Cor. 7.2]
.
Recall that we assume that

\mathsf{char}(K)\neq 2\,.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
v
be a henselian valuation on
K
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)=1
and let
F/K
be a function field in one variable.
Let
\varphi
be an anisotropic quadratic space over
F
of dimension at least
3
. Then there exists a valuation
v^{\prime}
on
F
extending
v
such that
\varphi_{F^{v^{\prime}}}
is anisotropic.

Proof.

Assume first that
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)<\infty
.
Hence
\varphi
is a quadratic form over
K
.
By
[
4
, Theorem 4.4]
, there exists a valuation
\nu
of rank
1
on
F
such that
\varphi_{\hat{F}^{\nu}}
is anisotropic and such that
\mathcal{O}_{v}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
.
Since
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(\nu)=1
, we have
F^{\nu}\subseteq\hat{F}^{\nu}
.
Hence
\varphi_{F^{\nu}}
is anisotropic.
If
\mathcal{O}_{v}=\mathcal{O}_{\nu}\cap K
, then we may rescale
\nu
to achieve that
\nu|_{K}=v
, and then we take
v^{\prime}=\nu
.
Assume now that

\mathcal{O}_{v}\subsetneq\mathcal{O}_{\nu}\cap K
.
Since
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)=1
, it follows that
K\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
.
Then the residue field
F\nu
is a finite field extension of
K
.
Let
\nu^{*}
denote the natural extension of
\nu
to
F^{\nu}
.
Note that
(F^{\nu})\nu^{*}=F\nu
.
Since the valuation
v
on
K
is henselian and
F\nu/K
is a finite extension,
v
extends uniquely to a valuation
v^{\ast}
on
F\nu
, and this extension is again henselian with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v^{\ast})=\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)=1
.
We compose
\nu^{\ast}
with the valuation
v^{\ast}
defined on
(F^{\nu})\nu^{\ast}=F\nu
; see
[
9
, p. 45]
. This yields a rank-
2
valuation
\mu
on
F^{\nu}
extending
v
with

\mathcal{O}_{\mu}=\{x\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu^{\ast}}\mid\bar{x}^{\nu^{\ast}}\in\mathcal{O}_{v^{\ast}}\}
.
By
[
9
, Cor. 4.1.4]
, since
\nu^{\ast}
and
v^{\ast}
are henselian, so is
\mu
.
Therefore
F^{\nu}
is equal to the henselization
F^{v^{\prime}}
of the restriction
v^{\prime}=\mu|_{F}
.
Hence

\varphi_{F^{v^{\prime}}}
is anisotropic.

This proves the statement in the case where
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)<\infty
.
Consider now the general case. Write
\varphi=(V,q)
and set
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}(F/v)
.
We denote by
\mathcal{U}
the set of finite-dimensional
K
-subspaces of
V
.
With
U\in\mathcal{U}
, we associate the quadratic form
\varphi_{U}=(U,q|_{U})
over
F
and the set
\mathcal{V}_{U}=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{V}\mid(\varphi_{U})_{F^{\gamma}}\mbox{ isotropic}\}
.
For
U,U^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}
with
U\subseteq U^{\prime}
, we have that
\varphi_{U}
is a subform of
\varphi_{U^{\prime}}
, whereby
\mathcal{V}_{U}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{U^{\prime}}
.
For
U\in\mathcal{U}
, we have by
[
3
, Lemma 8.1]
that
\mathcal{V}_{U}
is open in
\mathcal{V}
.

Assume now that
\varphi_{F^{\gamma}}
is isotropic for every
\gamma\in\mathcal{V}(F/v)
.
Then for every
\gamma\in\mathcal{V}
there exists
U\in\mathcal{U}
such that
(\varphi_{U})_{F^{\gamma}}
is isotropic.
Hence
\mathcal{V}=\bigcup_{U\in\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{V}_{U}
.
Since
\mathcal{V}
is compact, there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}\in\mathcal{U}
such that
\mathcal{V}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{V}_{U_{i}}
.
For
U=U_{1}+\dots+U_{n}
, we obtain that
U\in\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{U}
.
Hence
(\varphi_{U})_{F^{\gamma}}
is isotropic for every
\gamma\in\mathcal{V}
.
Since
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi_{U})\geqslant 3
, the first part of the proof yields that
\varphi_{U}
is isotropic.
Consequently,
\varphi
is isotropic.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
v
be a henselian valuation on
K
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)=1
.
Let
F/K
be a function field in one variable.
Let
\varphi
be a quadratic space of dimension at least
3
over
F
. Then
\varphi
is isotropic if and only if
\varphi_{\hat{F}^{\nu}}
is isotropic for every rank-
1
valuation
\nu
on
K
with
\mathcal{O}_{v}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
.

Proof.

If
\varphi
is isotropic, then
\varphi_{\hat{F}^{\nu}}
is isotropic for every valuation
\nu
on
F
.
Assume now that
\varphi
is anisotropic over
F
.
By
4.1
, there exists
\gamma=[\eta]\in\mathcal{V}(F/v)
such that
\varphi_{F^{\gamma}}
is anisotropic.

Then
1\leqslant\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(\eta)\leqslant 2<\infty
, whereby
\eta F
has a maximal proper convex subgroup
\Delta
.
Let
\nu
denote the rank-
1
valuation on
F
obtained as the coarsening of
\eta
with value group
\eta F/\Delta
.
Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(\nu)=1
and
\mathcal{O}_{v}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\eta}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
.
Let
\eta^{\ast}
denote the natural extension of
\eta
to
F^{\eta}
.
Note that
\eta^{\ast}
is henselian and its value group is
\eta F
.
Its rank-
1
coarsening
\nu^{\ast}
with value group
\eta F/\Delta
extends
\nu

and, by
[
9
, Cor. 4.1.4]
, it is henselian on
F^{\eta}
.
Therefore
F^{\nu}\subseteq F^{\eta}
, and we conclude that
\varphi_{F^{\nu}}
is anisotropic.
Since
F^{\nu}
is dense in
\hat{F}^{\nu}
, it follows by
[
12
, Lemma 3.16]
that
\varphi_{\hat{F}^{\nu}}
is anisotropic.
∎

5.
Weakly isotropic forms over function fields in one variable

We are ready to study the
w
-invariant for function fields in one variable over a henselian valued field.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
v
be a henselian valuation on
K
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)=1
.
Let
F/K
be a function field in one variable and
\pi
a Pfister form over
F
.
Then
\pi
is strongly balanced if and only if
\pi_{F^{\gamma}}
is strongly balanced for every
\gamma\in\mathcal{V}(F/v)
.

Proof.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we set
\varphi_{n}=n\times\langle 1\rangle\perp\pi
.
Let
\varphi
be the quadratic space over
K
given as the direct limit of
(\varphi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
For any field extension
F^{\prime}/F
,
we have by
2.4
that
\pi_{F^{\prime}}
is strongly balanced if and only if
(\varphi_{n})_{F^{\prime}}
is isotropic for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which is if and only if
\varphi_{F^{\prime}}
is isotropic.
Now the conclusion follows by
4.1
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
v
be a henselian valuation on
K
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)=1
and let
F/K
be a function field in one variable.
Assume that
w(F^{\gamma})\leqslant 2
for every
\gamma\in\mathcal{V}(F/v)
.
Then
w(F)\leqslant 2
.

Proof.

Consider a torsion
2
-fold Pfister form
\pi
over
F
.
It follows by
2.5
from the hypothesis that
\pi_{F^{\gamma}}
is strongly balanced for every
\gamma\in\mathcal{V}(F/v)
.
Hence, according to
5.1
,
\pi
is strongly balanced over
F
.
In view of
2.5
, this argument shows that
w(F)\leqslant 2
.
∎

We set

\widetilde{w}(K)=\mathsf{sup}\{w(F)\mid F/K\mbox{ is a function field in one variable}\}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}\,.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
v
be a valuation on
K
with
\mathsf{char}(Kv)\neq 2
. Then

\widetilde{w}(K)\geqslant\widetilde{w}(Kv)\,.

Proof.

Consider a function field in one variable
F_{0}/Kv
. By
[
4
, Lemma 5.6]
, there exists a function field in one variable
F/K
and an extension
v
to a valuation
v^{\prime}
on
F
such that
Fv^{\prime}
is
Kv
-isomorphic to
F_{0}
.
By
3.1
, we obtain that
w(F)\geqslant w(Fv^{\prime})
.
Hence
w(F_{0})=w(Fv^{\prime})\leqslant w(F)\leqslant\widetilde{w}(K)
. Having this for all function fields in one variable
F_{0}/Kv
, we conclude that
\widetilde{w}(Kv)\leqslant\widetilde{w}(K)
.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
v
be a henselian valuation on
K
.
Assume that
\mathsf{char}(Kv)\neq 2
and
\widetilde{w}(Kv)\leqslant 2
.
Then

\widetilde{w}(K)=\widetilde{w}(Kv)\,.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\widetilde{w}(Kv)=1
. Then
Kv
is nonreal.
Since
v
is henselian, it follows that
K
is nonreal.
Hence every field extension
K^{\prime}/K
is nonreal, whereby
w(K^{\prime})=1
.
In particular
\widetilde{w}(K)=1=\widetilde{w}(Kv)
in this case.

We may now assume that
\widetilde{w}(Kv)=2
.
Then
w(E)=2
for some function field in one variable
E/Kv
, and in particular
E
is real, whereby
Kv
is real.
It follows that
K
is real.
Then
K(X)
is real, so we obtain that
\widetilde{w}(K)\geqslant w(K(X))\geqslant 2
.
It therefore remains to show that
\widetilde{w}(K)\leqslant 2
.

We first consider the case where
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)=1
.
Consider a function field in one variable
F/K
.
Recall that, f or
\gamma=[\nu]\in\mathcal{V}(F/v)
, we set
F\gamma=F\nu
and denote the henselisation

F^{\nu}
by
F^{\gamma}
.

Consider an arbitrary
\gamma\in\mathcal{V}(F/v)
. We claim that
w(F^{\gamma})\leqslant 2
.
Let
\nu
be a valuation on
F
with
\gamma=[\nu]
.
Assume first that
\nu|_{K}
is trivial. Then
F\gamma
is a finite extension of
K
.
As
v
is henselian, it extends uniquely to a valuation
v^{\prime}
on
F\gamma
.
Then
v^{\prime}
is henselian, so
w(F\gamma)=w((F\gamma)v^{\prime})
, by
3.3
.
Since
(F\gamma)v^{\prime}
is a finite field extension of
Kv
, we have
w((F\gamma)v^{\prime})\leqslant w(Kv(X))
, by
3.2
.
Since
Kv(X)/Kv
is a function field in one variable,
the hypothesis implies that
w(Kv(X))\leqslant 2
.
Therefore by
3.2
, we have
w(F\gamma)=w((F\gamma)v^{\prime})\leqslant w(Kv(X))\leqslant 2
.
Assume now that
\nu|_{K}
is nontrivial. Then
\nu|_{K}
is equivalent to
v
.
This implies that
F\gamma/Kv
is either a function field in one variable or an algebraic extension.
In view of
3.2
, we obtain in either case from the hypothesis on
Kv
that
w(F\gamma)\leqslant 2
.
By
3.3
, we thus have
w(F^{\gamma})=w(F\gamma)\leqslant 2
.

We have thus shown that
w(F^{\gamma})\leqslant 2

for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{V}(F/v)
. Since
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)=1
, we conclude by
5.2
that
w(F)\leqslant 2
.
This completes the proof that
\widetilde{w}(K)\leqslant 2
in the case where
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)=1
.

We will now extend this
to the situation where
n=\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)<\infty
.
We will prove by induction on
n
that
\widetilde{w}(K)\leqslant 2
.
If
n=0
, then
Kv=K
, and hence the statement is trivial.
Assume now that
1\leqslant n<\infty
.
We will view
v
as a composition of two valuations of rank
1
and rank
n-1
, respectively.
Since the value group
vK
is nontrivial and of finite rank, it has a unique maximal proper convex subgroup
\Delta
.
Let
v_{1}
denote the valuation on

K
which is the coarsening of
v
with value group
vK/\Delta
.
Then
v_{1}
is a rank-
1
valuation on
K
.
Furthermore
v
induces a valuation
\eta
on the residue field
K{v_{1}}
with value
group
\eta(K{v_{1}})=\Delta
, and with residue field
(K{v_{1}})\eta=Kv
. In particular,
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(\eta)=n-1
.
By
[
9
, Cor. 4.1.4]
the valuations
v_{1}
on
K
and
\eta
on
K{v_{1}}

are both henselian.
Since
(Kv_{1})\eta=Kv
,
\widetilde{w}(Kv)\leqslant 2
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(\eta)=n-1
, the induction hypothesis yields that
\widetilde{w}(Kv_{1})\leqslant 2
.
Since
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v_{1})=1
, the case which is already established then yields that

\widetilde{w}(K)\leqslant 2
.

We will now prove that
\widetilde{w}(K)\leqslant 2
without assuming that
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)<\infty
.
Consider a function field in one variable
F/K
.
We need to show that
w(F)\leqslant 2
.
To this aim, by
2.5
it suffices to show that every torsion
2
-fold Pfister form over
F
is strongly balanced.
Consider a torsion
2
-fold Pfister form
\pi
over
F
.
We choose a finite subset
S
of
F
such that
\pi
is defined as a
2
-fold Pfister form over
\mathbb{Q}(S)
and is a torsion form over
\mathbb{Q}(S)
, and further such that

F=K(S)
and
\mathbb{Q}(S)/\mathbb{Q}(S\cap K)
is of transcendence degree
1
.
We set
K_{0}=\mathbb{Q}(S\cap K)
.
Since the absolute transcendence degree of
K_{0}
is bounded by
|S|
, it follows by
[
9
, Theorem 3.4.3]
that
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v|_{K_{0}})\leqslant|S|+1<\infty
.
By
[
5
, Lemma 2.3]
, there exists a subfield
K_{1}
of
K
containing
K_{0}
such that
vK_{1}/vK_{0}
is torsion,
v|_{K_{1}}
is henselian, and
Kv/K_{1}v
is algebraic and purely inseparable.
Since
\mathsf{char}(Kv)=0
, the last simply means that
Kv=K_{1}v
.
Since
vK_{1}/vK_{0}
is torsion, we have bijection
\mathsf{Conv}(vK_{1})\to\mathsf{Conv}(vK_{0}),\Delta\mapsto\Delta\cap vK_{0}
, whereby
\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v|_{K_{1}})=\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v|_{K_{0}})<\infty
.
Using the finite-rank case which we already settled, we obtain that
\widetilde{w}(K)=\widetilde{w}(K_{1}v)=\widetilde{w}(Kv)=2
.
Let
F_{1}=K_{1}(S)
.
Then
F_{1}/K_{1}
is a function field in one variable and
\pi
is is defined over
F_{1}
and it is a torsion form over
F_{1}
.
Hence
w(F_{1})\leqslant\widetilde{w}(K_{1})=2
and we conclude that
\pi
contains a binary torsion form over
F_{1}
, and hence also over
F
.
Hence, in view of
2.4
,
\pi
is strongly balanced.
This finishes the proof.
∎

Question 5.5
.

Does
\widetilde{w}(K)=\widetilde{w}(Kv)
hold in general if
v
is a henselian valuation
v
on
K
with
\mathsf{char}(Kv)\neq 2
?

We turn to the case where
K
is hereditarily pythagorean.
By a result of E. Becker
[
7
, Chap. III, Theorem 4]
, this is equivalent to having that
K
is real and
p(K(X))=2
.
In particular, this implies that
w(K(X))=2
, by
2.3
.

Theorem 5.6
.

If
K
is hereditarily pythagorean, then
\widetilde{w}(K)=2
.

Proof.

By definition,
K
is real, so we have
\widetilde{w}(K)\geqslant w(K(X))\geqslant w(K)\geqslant 2
, and it remains to show that
\widetilde{w}(K)\leqslant 2
.

Since
K
is hereditarily pythagorean, by Bröcker’s Theorem
[
8
, Prop. 3.5]
, there exist a henselian valuation
v
such that the residue field
Kv
is hereditarily pythagorean and
|{(Kv)}^{\times}/{(Kv)}^{\times 2}|\leqslant 4
.
For a function field in one variable
E/Kv
, it follows by
[
4
, Theorem 6.6]
that
\mathsf{I}^{3}_{\mathsf{tors}}E=0
, and by
2.6
, this implies that
w(E)\leqslant 2
.
This argument shows that
\widetilde{w}(Kv)\leqslant 2
.
We conclude by
5.4
that
\widetilde{w}(K)\leqslant 2
.
∎

By
[
4
, Theorem 1.6]
, if
K
is hereditarily pythagorean, then
p(F)\leqslant 5
for every function field in one variable
F/K
, and examples where
p(F)=3
are known.
Hence
5.6
cannot be obtained via
2.3
.

Question 5.7
.

Let
K
be hereditarily pythagorean and
F/K
a function field in one variable.
Can
2.2
be applied to
F
with
r=2
?
Equivalently, for arbitrary
t_{1},t_{2}\in{(\mathsf{\Sigma}F^{2})}^{\times}
, does there exist
t\in{(\mathsf{\Sigma}F^{2})}^{\times}
with
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathsf{D}\langle 1,t\rangle
?
