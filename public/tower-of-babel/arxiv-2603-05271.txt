Introduction

Rank-1 lattice rules are a classical and powerful tool in quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) methods for high-dimensional problems. They have been developed extensively for numerical integration in periodic function spaces, with a mature theory covering existence, construction, randomization, and tractability; see, e.g.,
[
10
,
11
,
29
,
38
]
. In particular, for weighted Korobov spaces, component-by-component (CBC) constructions and their fast variants provide lattice rules that achieve optimal or near-optimal convergence rates under natural summability assumptions on the weights
[
7
,
8
,
12
,
24
,
32
,
39
]
. Randomized lattice rules further yield sharp root-mean-square error bounds and often improve robustness with respect to unknown problem structure
[
9
,
13
,
14
,
22
,
26
]
.

Beyond integration, lattice sampling has become an increasingly important paradigm for
approximation
and
reconstruction
of multivariate periodic functions. Early investigations demonstrated how lattice information can be used to recover trigonometric polynomials and to design efficient spectral algorithms
[
27
,
28
]
, see also
[
17
]
. More recent work established systematic lattice-based approximation schemes in periodic spaces with general weights together with fast CBC constructions for approximation settings
[
5
,
6
,
25
]
. Complementary strands of research analyze approximation based on single or multiple rank-1 lattices from a harmonic analysis viewpoint, emphasizing sampling stability and explicit discretizations for multivariate trigonometric polynomials
[
2
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
. The continuing interest in this topic is also reflected in recent advances on subsampled and structured lattices aimed at optimal
L_{2}
approximation error convergence
[
1
]
.

A key challenge in lattice-based approximation is to control aliasing and to obtain
high-probability
guarantees for randomized constructions with minimal overhead. In the wider randomized numerical analysis and QMC literature, the
median
and
median-of-means
principles have emerged as effective tools for turning average-case or second-moment bounds into sharp tail estimates without requiring precise knowledge of smoothness parameters. This idea goes back to robust estimation in Monte Carlo contexts
[
23
,
30
]
and has recently been used to construct smoothness-adaptive or nearly universal randomized QMC strategies for integration
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
33
,
36
,
37
]
. These developments suggest that median aggregation is a natural candidate for stabilizing lattice-based
approximation
algorithms as well.

The present note fits into this emerging line of research. Building on the
L_{2}
-approximation analysis of median lattice algorithms in Korobov spaces from
[

]
(see also
[
34
]
) and the recent multiple-shift lattice approximation framework of
[

, we investigate the worst-case
L_{p}
-approximation for the full range

. We consider a randomized median lattice algorithm that approximates Fourier coefficients on a weighted hyperbolic-cross-type index set by
R

L_{p}

\beta>0

,

.

2
Preliminaries and notation

Throughout, let
d\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\mathbb{N}
is the set of positive integers, be the dimension and let boldface symbols denote vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

For a multi-index
\bm{h}=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, its support is denoted by

\supp(\bm{h}):=\{\,j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\,:\,h_{j}\neq 0\,\}.

Let
\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}})_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}}
be a family of positive weights with
\gamma_{\emptyset}=1
. For a smoothness parameter
\alpha>0
, define the frequency weight

r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{h}):=\gamma_{\supp(\bm{h})}^{-1}\prod_{j\in\supp(\bm{h})}\left|h_{j}\right|^{2\alpha},\hskip 18.49988pt\bm{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

so that in particular
r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{0})=1
.

For
f\in L_{2}([0,1]^{d})
we use the Fourier coefficients

\widehat{f}(\bm{h})\;:=\;\int_{[0,1]^{d}}f(\bm{x})\,e^{-2\pi i\,\bm{h}\cdot\bm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x},\hskip 18.49988pt\bm{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

where
\bm{h}\cdot\bm{x}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}h_{j}x_{j}
.

The weighted periodic Korobov space
\mathcal{H}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
is the collection of all
f\in L_{2}([0,1]^{d})
with finite norm

\|f\|_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}^{2}:=\sum_{\bm{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left|\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right|^{2}r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{h}),

i.e.,

\mathcal{H}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}=\{f:[0,1]^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\,:\,\|f\|_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}<\infty\},

(1)

where the norm arises from the inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}\;:=\;\sum_{\bm{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\overline{\widehat{f}(\bm{h})}\,\widehat{g}(\bm{h})\,r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{h}),

under which
\mathcal{H}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
is a Hilbert space.

For
g:[0,1]^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
we use the standard Lebesgue norms

\|g\|_{L_{p}}:=\left(\int_{[0,1]^{d}}\left|g(\bm{x})\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\right)^{1/p},\hskip 18.49988pt\|g\|_{L_{\infty}}:=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\bm{x}\in[0,1]^{d}}\left|g(\bm{x})\right|.

An approximation algorithm
A
is a map
A:\mathcal{H}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}\to L_{p}([0,1]^{d})
(for
1\leq p\leq\infty
) that assigns to each
f
an approximation

A(f)
. The worst-case error of
A
over the unit ball of

\mathcal{H}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
is measured by

\mathrm{err}(\mathcal{H}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}},L_{p},A):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{H}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}\\
\left\|f\right\|_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\|f-A(f)\|_{L_{p}},\hskip 9.24994pt1\leq p\leq\infty.

In the following, we will frequently write
\{k{:}\ell\}
to denote the set
\{k,k+1,k+2,\ldots,\ell\}
for integers
k\leq\ell
.
The role of
\bm{\gamma}
is to modulate the importance of coordinate subsets (see
[
40
]
): frequencies supported on
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq\{1{:}d\}
are scaled by
\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{-1}
. In the present paper, we consider the most prominent class, namely product weights
\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}=\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\gamma_{j}
with
\gamma_{\emptyset}=1
, where
(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
is a non-increasing sequence of positive reals. We remark that we could also allow non-negative
\gamma_{j}
, but this would result in additional technical notation, which we avoid. The factor
\prod_{j\in\supp(\bm{h})}|h_{j}|^{2\alpha}
in the definition of
r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
, and thus also in the norm of
\mathcal{H}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
, penalizes higher frequencies according to the smoothness parameter
\alpha
; larger
\alpha
corresponds to stronger decay of Fourier coefficients in
\mathcal{H}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
.

Let us now outline the definition of the QMC rules we use in our median algorithm, namely rank-1 lattice rules. For simplicity, let us assume that
N
is a prime in the following (assuming that
N
is prime guarantees that all one-dimensional projections of a lattice point set are evenly distributed, which is an advantage. If we would allow composite
N
, we presumably could derive similar results, but would need more technical notation). Let
\bm{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})
be a
vector with each component in
\{1{:}(N-1)\}
. We can then define a
lattice point set
P_{N,\bm{z}}
with points
\bm{x}_{0},\ldots,\bm{x}_{N-1}
as follows. For
j\in\{1{:}d\}
and
k\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
, the
j
-th component of
\bm{x}_{k}
is given by

x_{k}^{(j)}:=\left\{\frac{kz_{j}}{N}\right\},

where
\{y\}=y-\lfloor y\rfloor
denotes the fractional part of a real number
y
. By using this definition for all
j\in\{1{:}d\}
and
k\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
, we obtain the full lattice point set
P_{N,\bm{z}}
. A QMC rule using
P_{N,\bm{z}}
is called a (rank-1) lattice rule. We further define the dual lattice of
P_{N,\bm{z}}
by

\displaystyle P_{N,\bm{z}}^{\perp}:=\{\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,:\,\bm{z}^{\top}\bm{\ell}\equiv 0{\;(\mathrm{mod}\;N)}\}.

(2)

Note that, for fixed
N
and
d
, a lattice rule is fully characterized by the choice of the
generating vector

\bm{z}
. Not all choices of
\bm{z}
yield lattice rules of sufficient quality as integration node sets. However, there are fast construction algorithms available that return good generating vectors for given
N
,
d
,
\alpha
, and
\bm{\gamma}
. We refer to the books
[
10
,
38
]
for overviews of the theory of lattice rules, and in particular to
[
10
, Chapters 3 and 4]
for constructions of good lattice rules. In the present paper, however, we will make a random choice of the generating vectors
\bm{z}
to obtain the lattice point sets used. Therefore, we need not be concerned with the search for good generating vectors here, which is a general computational advantage of median rules.

It is sometimes useful to introduce an additional random element when applying lattice rules. This is usually done by a
random shift
,
\Delta\in[0,1)^{d}
. Given
P_{N,\bm{z}}=\{\bm{x}_{0},\ldots,\bm{x}_{N-1}\}
and drawing
\bm{\Delta}
from a uniform distribution over
[0,1)^{d}
, the corresponding
randomly shifted lattice rule
applied to a function
g
is then

Q_{N,d,\bm{\Delta}}(g):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}g\left(\left\{\bm{x}_{k}+\bm{\Delta}\right\}\right),

i.e., all points of
P_{N,\bm{z}}
are shifted modulo one by the same
\bm{\Delta}
.

3
The median algorithm

For a real
L\geq 0
, we put

\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(L):=\left\{\bm{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,:\,r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{h})<L^{2\alpha}\right\}=\left\{\bm{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,:\,r_{1,\bm{\gamma}^{1/(2\alpha)}}(\bm{h})<L\right\}.

(3)

For an odd positive integer
R
, we define the median of
R
complex numbers
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{R}
as

\mathrm{median}_{r\in\{1{:}R\}}(Z_{r})=\mathrm{median}_{r\in\{1{:}R\}}\Re(Z_{r})+\mathtt{i}\cdot\mathrm{median}_{r\in\{1{:}R\}}\Im(Z_{r}),

(4)

where
\Re(Z_{r})
and
\Im(Z_{r})
denote the real and imaginary parts of
Z_{r}
, respectively.
In the following, we will also assume that
N
is an odd prime
number. Though we do not need this requirement for all results below, we sometimes do, and it is therefore easier to make this assumption from now on.

Algorithm 3.1
.

Let
\alpha>1/2
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\gamma_{j}\in(0,1]
for

j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. Given a parameter
\tau>0
, an odd number of repetitions
R>1
, and a prime number
N
, do the following.

1.

Define

P_{N,d}(\tau,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}):=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+2\gamma_{j}^{1/(2\alpha)}(1+\tau\log N)\right),

(5)

and

N_{2}:=\frac{N-1}{\exp(\tau^{-1})P_{N,d}(\tau,\alpha,\bm{\gamma})}.

(6)

2.

For

r
from
1
to
R
, do the following:

(a)

Randomly draw
\bm{z}_{r}
from the uniform distribution over the set
\{1{:}(N-1)\}^{d}
.

(b)

For all
\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
, set

\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}(\bm{h}):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}f\left(\left\{\frac{k\bm{z}_{r}}{N}\right\}\right)\exp\left(-2\pi\mathtt{i}\bm{h}\cdot\left(\frac{k\bm{z}_{r}}{N}\right)\right).

end for

3.

For all
\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
, define the aggregation

\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{\{1{:}R\}}}(\bm{h}):=\mathrm{median}_{r\in\{1{:}R\}}\Big(\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}(\bm{h})\Big),

where
\bm{z}_{\{1{:}R\}}
denotes the collections of
\bm{z}_{r}
for
r\in\{1{:}R\}
, respectively.
Define the final approximation by

(A_{N,\bm{z}_{\{1{:}R\}},\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}(f))(\bm{x}):=\sum_{\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{\{1{:}R\}}}(\bm{h})\exp(2\pi\mathtt{i}\bm{h}\cdot\bm{x}).

Remark 3.2
.

The parameter
\tau>0
does not affect the asymptotic convergence rates of Algorithm
3.1
to be established in Section
4
. For guidance on optimal tuning of
\tau
to improve finite-sample performance, see
[
35
, Remark 16]
.

Remark 3.3
.

A natural modification of Algorithm
3.1
is to incorporate additional independent uniform shifts
\bm{\Delta}_{1},\dots,\bm{\Delta}_{R}\in[0,1)^{d}
, and replace
\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{\{1{:}R\}}}(\bm{h})
by

\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{\{1{:}R\}},\bm{\Delta}_{\{1{:}R\}}}(\bm{h}):=\mathrm{median}_{r\in\{1{:}R\}}\Big(\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{r},\bm{\Delta}_{r}}(\bm{h})\Big),

for

\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{r},\bm{\Delta}_{r}}(\bm{h}):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}f\left(\left\{\frac{k\bm{z}_{r}}{N}+\bm{\Delta}_{r}\right\}\right)\exp\left(-2\pi\mathtt{i}\bm{h}\cdot\left(\frac{k\bm{z}_{r}}{N}+\bm{\Delta}_{r}\right)\right).

We will study Algorithm
3.1
for simplicity, but all error bounds stated in Section
4
also hold under the above change due to the common upper bounds

\left|\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}(\bm{h})-\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right|=\left|\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\widehat{f}({\bm{h}+\bm{\ell}})\right|\leq\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\left|\widehat{f}({\bm{h}+\bm{\ell}})\right|

and

\left|\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{r},\bm{\Delta}_{r}}(\bm{h})-\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right|=\left|\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\widehat{f}({\bm{h}+\bm{\ell}})\exp(2\pi\mathtt{i}\bm{\ell}\cdot\bm{\Delta}_{r})\right|\leq\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\left|\widehat{f}({\bm{h}+\bm{\ell}})\right|,

where the dual lattice
P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}
is given by (
2
).

When measured in the root-mean-squared error, which is the setting considered in
[
35
]
,
\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{r},\bm{\Delta}_{r}}(\bm{h})
enjoys a tighter error bound

\left(\int_{[0,1]^{d}}|\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{r},\bm{\Delta}_{r}}(\bm{h})-\widehat{f}(\bm{h})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\Delta}_{r}\right)^{1/2}=\left(\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in P_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}^{\perp}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{h}+\bm{\ell})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Therefore, we expect median lattice algorithms with the random shifts to perform better than those without the shifts, despite the two sharing the same worst-case error bound.

4.1
L_{\infty}
-approximation

The next lemma provides a bound on the size of the frequency set
\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
, which was shown in
[
35
, Corollary 6]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
N_{2}
defined as in (
6
) and
\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
as in (
3
),
we have

|\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})|\leq 1+\frac{N-1}{1+\tau\log N_{2}}.

For a given generating vector
\bm{z}
, we define the set
K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z})
of frequencies
\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
for which no frequency
\bm{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
is aliased with
\bm{h}
, i.e., we have
(\bm{h}-\bm{k})\cdot\bm{z}\not\equiv 0\pmod{N}
for all
\bm{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}\setminus\{\bm{h}\}
:

K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}):=\left\{\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})\,:\,(\bm{h}+P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}})\bigcap\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})=\{\bm{h}\}\right\}.

This set is crucial in the proof of our main result, since for “good” generating vectors
\bm{z}
we obtain “good” approximations of the Fourier coefficients for frequencies in
K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z})
. Roughly speaking, the proof strategy is to show that for each frequency
\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
, more than half of the
\bm{z}_{1},\ldots,\bm{z}_{R}
are such that
\bm{h}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})
.

Next, we show several auxiliary results.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
and
\bm{z}
chosen uniformly from
\{1{:}(N-1)\}^{d}
,

\Pr(\bm{h}\notin K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}))\leq\frac{1}{1+\tau\log N_{2}}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
, we have
|\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})|<N
. Consequently, the one-dimensional projection of
\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
onto any
j
-th coordinate, defined as

\proj_{j}(\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})):=\left\{h_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}\,:\,\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})\right\},

is contained in the set
\{-(N-1)/2,\ldots,0,\ldots,(N-1)/2\}
. Thus, for any distinct elements
\bm{h},\bm{h}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
, it follows that
h_{j}-h^{\prime}_{j}\not\equiv 0\pmod{N}
for at least one
j
. This implies that
\bm{h}\not\equiv\bm{h}^{\prime}\pmod{N}
, or equivalently,
N\nmid\bm{h}-\bm{h}^{\prime}
.

Now, for each
\bm{h}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
distinct from
\bm{h}
, the fact that
N\nmid\bm{h}-\bm{h}^{\prime}
leads to

\Pr\left(\bm{z}\cdot(\bm{h}^{\prime}-\bm{h})\equiv 0{\;(\mathrm{mod}\;N)}\right)\leq\frac{1}{N-1}.

Hence,
\Pr(\bm{h}^{\prime}-\bm{h}\in P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}})\leq 1/(N-1)
. By a union bound, we obtain

\Pr(\bm{h}\notin K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}))\leq\frac{|\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})|-1}{N-1}\leq\frac{1}{1+\tau\log N_{2}}.

Let

(7)

If

1-\varepsilon_{1}
,

\min_{\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\bm{1}\{\bm{h}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})\}\geq\lceil R/2\rceil,

where
\bm{1}
denotes the indicator function of a given set.

Proof.

For each
\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
, the probability that
\bm{h}
does not belong to at least
\lceil R/2\rceil
instances of

among

is bounded by

2^{R}\left(\frac{1}{1+\tau\log N_{2}}\right)^{\lceil R/2\rceil}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{4}{1+\tau\log N_{2}}\right)^{\lceil R/2\rceil}.

The conclusion follows from a union bound over
\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{R}\}\subseteq[0,\infty)
. For any
J\subseteq\{1{:}R\}
with
|J|\geq\lceil R/2\rceil
,

\mathrm{median}_{r\in\{1{:}R\}}x_{r}\leq\sum_{r\in J}x_{r}.

Proof.

Let
r^{*}\in\{1{:}R\}
be such that it satisfies
x_{r*}=\mathrm{median}_{r\in\{1{:}R\}}x_{r}
. If
r^{*}\in J
, the inequality is trivial. When
r^{*}\notin J
, we must have

x_{r*}\leq\max_{r\in J}x_{r}\leq\sum_{r\in J}x_{r}.

∎

We need the following lemma, whose proof can be found, for instance, in
[

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\alpha>1/2
and

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\backslash\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(M)}\frac{1}{r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{k})}\leq\frac{1}{(\gamma_{1}^{1/2}M^{2\alpha})^{(1/q-1)/(2\alpha)}}\frac{q}{1-q}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+2\gamma_{j}^{q}\zeta(2\alpha q)\right)^{1/q}.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\varepsilon_{1}

\mathrm{err}(\mathcal{H}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}},L_{\infty},A_{N,\bm{z}_{\{1{:}R\}},\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})})\leq(2R+1)\left(\frac{1}{(\gamma_{1}^{1/(4\alpha)}N_{2})^{(1/q-1)}}\frac{q}{1-q}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+2\gamma_{j}^{q}\zeta(2\alpha q)\right)^{1/q}\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

For

\|A_{N,\bm{z}_{\{1{:}R\}},\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}(f)-f\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq\sum_{\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}\left|\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{\{1{:}R\}}}(\bm{h})-\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right|+\sum_{\bm{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}\left|\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right|.

In the following we use that for a set of real numbers

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{\{1{:}R\}}}(\bm{h})-\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{median}_{r\in\{1{:}R\}}\left|\Re\left(\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}(\bm{h})-\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right)\right|+\mathrm{median}_{r\in\{1{:}R\}}\left|\Im\left(\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}(\bm{h})-\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\ \mathrm{median}_{r\in\{1{:}R\}}\left|\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}(\bm{h})-\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right|.

By Corollary
4.3

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}\left|\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{\{1{:}R\}}}(\bm{h})-\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right|

\displaystyle\|A_{N,\bm{z}_{\{1{:}R\}},\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}(f)-f\|_{L_{\infty}}

4.5
.

4.2
L_{2}
-approximation

For
\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we let

\rho_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z})=\min_{\bm{\ell}\in P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{\ell}).

The following result is shown in
[
3
, Eq. (3.9)]
. We state it as a lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\bm{z}\in\{1{:}(N-1)\}^{d}
. Let
\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\ldots)
with
\gamma_{1}\geq\gamma_{2}\geq\cdots
. Let
Q>1
,
\beta>1/2
, and
\rho_{\beta,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z})>1
. Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
2^{m-1}<M\leq 2^{m}
. Then

\sup_{\bm{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left|\bm{\ell}\in\bm{h}+P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}}:r_{\beta,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{\ell})\leq Q\right|\leq\frac{2^{m}}{m}\min\left\{\frac{(1+m/2)^{d}}{\rho_{\beta,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z})^{1/\beta}},\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+\gamma_{j}^{1/\beta}m)\right\}.

In the notation of the present paper, where
\beta
is replaced by
2\alpha
,
Q=L^{2\alpha}
, and hence
2^{m-1}<L\leq 2^{m}
, this reads

\sup_{\bm{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left|\bm{\ell}\in\bm{h}+P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}}:r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{\ell})\leq L^{2\alpha}\right|\leq\frac{2^{m}}{m}\min\left\{\frac{(1+m/2)^{d}}{\rho_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z})^{1/(2\alpha)}},\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+\gamma_{j}^{1/(2\alpha)}m)\right\}.

(8)

For
L_{\infty}
-approximation it was sufficient to show that for each
\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
, more than half of the generating vectors satisfy
\bm{h}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})
, which was shown in Corollary
4.3
. However, for
L_{2}
-approximation we need the additional property that for all generating vectors the figure of merit satisfies
\rho_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})\geq N_{2}^{2\alpha}
, which we show in the following lemma. Ensuring that the figure of merit is sufficiently large will allow us to improve the convergence rate of the
L_{2}
-approximation error by a factor of
N_{2}^{1/2}
compared to the
L_{\infty}
-approximation error.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let

\varepsilon_{2}:=\frac{|\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})|}{2}\left(\frac{8}{1+\tau\log N_{2}}\right)^{\lceil R/2\rceil}.

(9)

If
\varepsilon_{2}\leq 1
, then with probability at least
1-\varepsilon_{2}
,

\min_{\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\bm{1}\{\bm{h}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})\}\geq\lceil R/2\rceil

for

\mathcal{R}=\left\{r\in\{1{:}R\}\,:\,\rho_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})\geq N^{2\alpha}_{2}\right\}.

Proof.

For any
r\in\{1{:}R\}
, when
\bm{0}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})
, no
\bm{\ell}\in P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}
other than
\bm{0}
belong to
\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
, so

\rho_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})=\min_{\bm{\ell}\in P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{\ell})\geq N^{2\alpha}_{2}

and
r\in\mathcal{R}
. Consequently, for any
\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
,

\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\bm{1}\{\bm{h}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})\}\geq\sum_{r\in\{1:R\}}\bm{1}\{\bm{0}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})\text{ and }\bm{h}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})\}.

The probability that

\sum_{r\in\{1:R\}}\bm{1}\{\bm{0}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})\text{ and }\bm{h}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})\}<\lceil R/2\rceil

for at least one
\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
is equivalently the probability that

\sum_{r\in\{1:R\}}\bm{1}\{\bm{0}\notin K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})\text{ or }\bm{h}\notin K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})\}\geq\lceil R/2\rceil.

(10)

for at least one
\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
.
However, Lemma
4.2
implies for every
r\in\{1{:}R\}
and
\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
,

\Pr\left(\bm{0}\notin K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})\text{ or }\bm{h}\notin K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})\right)\leq\frac{2}{1+\tau\log N_{2}}.

Therefore, the probability that (
10
) holds for a given
\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
is bounded from above by

2^{R}\left(\frac{2}{1+\tau\log N_{2}}\right)^{\lceil R/2\rceil}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{8}{1+\tau\log N_{2}}\right)^{\lceil R/2\rceil}.

The conclusion follows after taking a union bound over
\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

With probability at least
1-\varepsilon_{2}
for
\varepsilon_{2}
given by (
9

\mathrm{err}(\mathcal{H}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}},L_{2},A_{N,\bm{z}_{\{1{:}R\}},\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})})\leq C_{d,\alpha}\frac{(1+\log_{2}N_{2})^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{N_{2}^{\alpha}},

where
C_{d,\alpha}>0
is a constant independent of
N
and
N_{2}
but dependent on
d
and
\alpha
.

Proof.

For
f\in\mathcal{H}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
, we have

\|A_{N,\bm{z}_{\{1{:}R\}},\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}(f)-f\|^{2}_{L_{2}}=\sum_{\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}\left|\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{\{1{:}R\}}}(\bm{h})-\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right|^{2}+\sum_{\bm{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}\left|\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right|^{2}.

(11)

The second term on the right hand side can be bounded by

\sum_{\bm{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}\left|\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{N^{2\alpha}_{2}}\sum_{\bm{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}\left|\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right|^{2}r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{h})\leq\frac{\|f\|^{2}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}}{N^{2\alpha}_{2}}.

For the first term, Lemma
4.8
together with Lemma
4.4
shows that with probability at least
1-\varepsilon_{2}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}\left|\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{\{1{:}R\}}}(\bm{h})-\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}\mathrm{median}_{r\in\{1{:}R\}}\left|\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}(\bm{h})-\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\sum_{\bm{h}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})}\left|\widehat{f}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}(\bm{h})-\widehat{f}({\bm{h}})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\sum_{\bm{h}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})}\left(\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\left|\widehat{f}({\bm{h}+\bm{\ell}})\right|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\sum_{\bm{h}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})}\left(\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\left|\widehat{f}({\bm{h}+\bm{\ell}})\right|^{2}r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{h}+\bm{\ell})\right)\left(\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\frac{1}{r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{h}+\bm{\ell})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2R\|f\|^{2}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}\sup_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\sup_{\bm{h}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\frac{1}{r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{h}+\bm{\ell})}.

Because
\bm{h}+\bm{\ell}\notin\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
for
\bm{h}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})
and
\bm{\ell}\in P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{h}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\frac{1}{r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{h}+\bm{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\bm{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{\bm{\ell}^{\prime}\in(\bm{h}+P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}})\setminus\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})}\frac{1}{r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{\ell}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2^{m}N_{2})^{2\alpha}}|(\bm{h}+P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}})\cap\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(2^{m+1}N_{2})|.

Letting
m_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
2^{m_{0}-1}<N_{2}\leq 2^{m_{0}}
, we apply (
8
) to each
r\in\mathcal{R}
and bound

\displaystyle\sup_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\sup_{\bm{h}\in K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in P^{\perp}_{N,\bm{z}_{r}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\frac{1}{r_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{h}+\bm{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2^{m}N_{2})^{2\alpha}}\frac{2^{m_{0}+m}}{m_{0}+m}\min\left\{\frac{(1+(m_{0}+m)/2)^{d}}{\rho_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})^{1/(2\alpha)}},\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+\gamma_{j}^{1/(2\alpha)}(m_{0}+m))\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{N_{2}^{2\alpha-1}}\sup_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{m(2\alpha-1)}}\frac{1}{m_{0}+m}\frac{(1+(m_{0}+m)/2)^{d}}{\rho_{2\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{z}_{r})^{1/(2\alpha)}}

(12)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{N_{2}^{2\alpha}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{m(2\alpha-1)}}\frac{(1+(m_{0}+m)/2)^{d}}{m_{0}+m}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{d,\alpha}\frac{(1+\log_{2}N_{2})^{d-1}}{N_{2}^{2\alpha}}

for a positive constant
C^{\prime}_{d,\alpha}
.
Here we used
1\leq m_{0}<1+\log_{2}N_{2}
and

\displaystyle m_{0}^{-d+1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{m(2\alpha-1)}}\frac{(1+(m_{0}+m)/2)^{d}}{m_{0}+m}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{m(2\alpha-1)}}\left(\frac{1}{m_{0}+m}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{m_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{m}{2m_{0}}\right)^{d-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3^{d}}{2^{d}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1+m)^{d-1}}{2^{m(2\alpha-1)}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3^{d}(d-1)!}{2^{d}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{m(2\alpha-1)}}\binom{m+d-1}{d-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{3^{d}(d-1)!}{2^{d}(1-2^{-2\alpha+1})^{d}}.

Our conclusion follows by putting the above bounds into (
11
).
∎

Remark 4.10
.

If we set the number of repetitions
R
to be proportional to
\log N
, the failure probabilities
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{2}
in Theorem
4.6
and Theorem
4.9
vanish at the rate

\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N}{(\log N)^{\log N}}\right).

This decay rate is super-polynomial; that is, it decays faster than any fixed power of
N^{-1}
. Consequently, the total number of function evaluations, which is of order
N\log N
, is asymptotically sufficient for the median lattice algorithm to achieve high reliability.

Furthermore, the median lattice algorithm can also be viewed as a variant of multiple lattice-based algorithms, as the generating vectors
\bm{z}_{r}
for which the median aggregation is attained can be different for each frequency
\bm{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
. While similar probabilistic strategies employing multiple lattices have been proposed in
[
4
,
18
,
20
]
, the median lattice approach offers a distinct advantage: it eliminates the need for an explicit verification step to detect aliasing terms across all frequencies in
\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(N_{2})
. Instead, the median operation ensures that the approximations of all Fourier coefficients are simultaneously free from aliasing issues with high probability, simplifying the implementation and reducing the computational overhead.

4.3
L_{p}
-approximation

We prove Theorem
1.1
in this section. Since the
L_{p}
-approximation error for
1\leq p\leq 2
is bounded by the
L_{2}
-approximation error and we can estimate
(1+\log_{2}N_{2})^{d-1}
by
C_{d,\beta}N^{\beta}
for any
\beta>0
, this part follows from Theorem
4.9
. The bound independent of the dimension for
p=\infty
follows directly from Theorem
4.6
.

It remains to prove the result for
2<p<\infty
. We use the inequality
\|g\|_{L_{p}}\leq\|g\|_{L_{2}}^{2/p}\|g\|_{L_{\infty}}^{1-2/p}
, which holds for
g\in L_{2}\cap L_{\infty}
. Since every function
f
in the
weighted periodic Korobov space
\mathcal{H}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
is bounded and continuous, we have
f\in L_{\infty}
. Further,
A(f)\in L_{\infty}
and so the norm inequality can be applied.

As above, we estimate
(1+\log_{2}N_{2})^{d-1}\leq C_{d,\beta}N^{\beta}
for any
\beta>0
. From Theorem
4.6
we get the error bound
\|f-A(f)\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq CN^{-\alpha+1/2+\beta}
, and from Theorem
4.9
we get the error bound
\|f-A(f)\|_{L_{2}}\leq CN^{-\alpha+\beta}
. Applying the norm interpolation inequality, we obtain the bound

\|f-A(f)\|_{L_{p}}\leq CN^{(-\alpha+\beta)2/p}N^{(-\alpha+1/2+\beta)(1-2/p)}=CN^{-\alpha+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}+\beta},

and hence

\mathrm{err}(\mathcal{H}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}},L_{p},A)\leq CN^{-\alpha+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}+\beta},\hskip 9.24994pt2\leq p\leq\infty.

Thus, the result follows.
