Duality of zero mean curvature surfaces in the Lorentzian Heisenberg group
Sai Rasmi Ranjan Mohanty, Priyank Vasu

Abstract
. We study a transformation surface associated with a zero mean curvature surface in the three-dimensional Heisenberg group with respect to two left-invariant semi-Riemannian metrics. We investigate the duality and prove that the transformation surface also has zero mean curvature. Furthermore, we derive the Sym formula for the dual surface in both metric cases.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 53C42, 53A10; Secondary 53C50.

1.
Introduction

In classical surface theory in Euclidean space, certain minimal surfaces admit a
nontrivial partner surface, called a
dual surface
, which is conformally related to the original surface and has parallel tangent planes.
A fundamental example is provided by the catenoid and the helicoid, which arise as a dual pair within a one-parameter family of associated surfaces.
Motivated by this, Christoffel in
[
2
]
classified surfaces admitting a dual surface; these are precisely the
isothermic surfaces
, and the corresponding dual is known as the Christoffel dual. The dual surface is unique up to homothety; furthermore, applying the duality transformation twice recovers the original surface. Dual surfaces in Euclidean space were studied by Kamberov et al.
[
5
]
; explicit representations of Christoffel duals in Euclidean and Minkowski spaces were obtained by Fujimori et al.
[
4
]
, and a dual surface theory for minimal surfaces in the 3-dimensional Heisenberg group with left-invariant Riemannian metric was given by Kobayashi in
[
8
]
.

In this paper, we investigate an analogue of dual surfaces in the three-dimensional
Heisenberg group
\mathrm{Nil}^{3}
which is identified with
\mathbb{R}^{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
equipped
with the group multiplication

(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\cdot(\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{x}_{2},\tilde{x}_{3})=\bigl(x_{1}+\tilde{x}_{1},\;x_{2}+\tilde{x}_{2},\;x_{3}+\tilde{x}_{3}+\tfrac{1}{2}(x_{1}\tilde{x}_{2}-\tilde{x}_{1}x_{2})\bigr),

and endowed with either of the two left-invariant semi-Riemannian metrics

ds_{\pm}^{2}=\pm dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}\mp\bigl(dx_{3}+\tfrac{1}{2}(x_{2}\,dx_{1}-x_{1}\,dx_{2})\bigr)^{2}.

We denote
\operatorname{Nil}_{1}^{3}:=\bigl(\operatorname{Nil}^{3},ds_{+}^{2}\bigr),\ \operatorname{Nil}_{t}^{3}:=\bigl(\operatorname{Nil}^{3},ds_{-}^{2}\bigr),

and refer to them as the
Lorentzian Heisenberg groups
. We introduce the notion of a dual surface associated with a zero mean
curvature surface in both
\operatorname{Nil}_{1}^{3}
and
\operatorname{Nil}_{t}^{3}
, and show that the dual surface also has zero mean curvature and applying the dual transformation twice gives the original surface.
The main idea of this article is motivated by the work of Kobayashi
[
8
]
.

A surface immersed in a Lorentzian manifold is called
spacelike
if the induced metric is Riemannian.
Spacelike zero mean curvature surfaces, also known as maximal surfaces, in

\operatorname{Nil}_{1}^{3}
have been studied extensively; see

[
1
,
3
,
9
]
.
Analogously, a surface immersed in a Lorentzian manifold is called
timelike
if the induced metric is Lorentzian.
Timelike zero mean curvature surfaces, or timelike minimal surfaces, in

\operatorname{Nil}_{t}^{3}
have been investigated in

[
6
,
3
,
7
]
.

This paper is divided into two parts. First,
in Section
2
, we study maximal surfaces in

\operatorname{Nil}_{1}^{3}
.
Subsection
2.1
recalls the spinor representation and the normal Gauss map of a maximal surface, and in Subsection
2.2
, we define the dual generating spinors for a given maximal surface and
establish the maximality and duality of the associated surface
(Theorem
2.3
).
In Subsection
2.3
, we derive the
Sym formula for the dual maximal surface via the logarithmic derivative of the extended frame of the given maximal surface with respect to the spectral parameter. In addition, we show that the generalized maximal surface and its dual preserve both the singularity set and the nature of the singularities.
Analogously, in the second part, in Section
3
,
we study timelike minimal surfaces in
\operatorname{Nil}_{t}^{3}
. In Subsection
3.1
, we begin by reviewing the
para-complex structure, the Gauss map, and the spinor representation of a timelike
minimal surface in
\operatorname{Nil}_{t}^{3}
.
In Subsection
3.2
, we define the dual generating
spinors and establish the main result of this section
(Theorem
3.4
).
Finally, in Subsection
3.3
, we derive the Sym formula for dual timelike minimal surfaces and conclude with
some examples.

2.
Dual maximal surfaces in
\operatorname{Nil}_{1}^{3}
and the Sym formula

2.1.
Preliminaries for maximal surfaces

In this subsection, we recall the necessary background on maximal surfaces in the Lorentzian Heisenberg group
\operatorname{Nil}_{1}^{3}
from
[
1
]
. In particular, we review the generating spinors associated with maximal surfaces and describe their normal Gauss maps.

Let
f\colon M\longrightarrow\mathrm{Nil}_{1}^{3}
be a conformal spacelike immersion of a Riemann surface
M
, equipped with a local conformal parameter
z=x+iy
in a simply connected domain
\mathbb{D}
in
M
, and an orthonormal basis of Lie algebra
\mathfrak{nil}_{1}^{3}
of
\mathrm{Nil}_{1}^{3}
is
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
. The left-invariant Maurer-Cartan form of
f
is given by

f^{-1}df=(f^{-1}f_{z})\,dz+(f^{-1}f_{\bar{z}})\,d\bar{z},

where

f^{-1}f_{z}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\varphi_{k}e_{k},\qquad f^{-1}f_{\bar{z}}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\overline{\varphi_{k}}\,e_{k}.

The conformality condition on
f
yields the relations

(2.1)

\varphi_{1}^{2}+\varphi_{2}^{2}-\varphi_{3}^{2}=0,\qquad|\varphi_{1}|^{2}+|\varphi_{2}|^{2}-|\varphi_{3}|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{u}\neq 0,

and hence the induced metric is given by
I=e^{u}|dz|^{2}
.

The system (
2.1
) can be parametrized using a pair of
generating spinors

\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
, through the expressions

(2.2)

\varphi_{1}=(\overline{\psi_{2}})^{2}-\psi_{1}^{2},\qquad\varphi_{2}=i\big((\overline{\psi_{2}})^{2}+\psi_{1}^{2}\big),\qquad\varphi_{3}=2i\psi_{1}\overline{\psi_{2}}.

Let
N
denote the positively oriented unit normal vector field along the immersion
f
and define the rescaled normal vector
L:=e^{u/2}N
. The
support function

h
is then defined by
h=\langle f^{-1}L,e_{3}\rangle.

In terms of the generating spinors
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
, we obtain the identities

(2.3)

e^{u}=4\big(|\psi_{1}|^{2}-|\psi_{2}|^{2}\big)^{2},\qquad h=2\big(|\psi_{1}|^{2}+|\psi_{2}|^{2}\big).

The immersion
f
is regular if and only if
|\psi_{1}|\neq|\psi_{2}|
. Furthermore, we set the Dirac potential by setting
e^{w/2}=U=V
, and define the Abresch-Rosenberg quadratic differential by
Q\,dz^{2}=4B\,dz^{2}
. Following
[
7
]
, these can be expressed as

(2.4)

e^{w/2}=U=V=\frac{H}{2}e^{u/2}i-\frac{1}{4}h,\qquad B=\frac{2iH+1}{2}\bigl(\psi_{1}\overline{\psi_{2}}_{z}-\overline{\psi_{2}}\,{\psi_{1}}_{z}\bigr)+2iH(\psi_{1}\overline{\psi_{2}})^{2},

where
H
is the mean curvature of the spacelike surface
f
.

The generating spinors
\tilde{\psi}=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
satisfy a
linear spinor system
of the form

(2.5)

\tilde{\psi}_{z}=\tilde{\psi}\,\widetilde{U},\qquad\tilde{\psi}_{\bar{z}}=\tilde{\psi}\,\widetilde{V},

where the coefficient matrices are given by

\widetilde{U}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}w_{z}-\frac{i}{2}H_{z}e^{-w/2+u/2}&-e^{w/2}\\[4.0pt]
Be^{-w/2}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\widetilde{V}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\overline{B}e^{-w/2}\\[4.0pt]
e^{w/2}&\frac{1}{2}w_{\bar{z}}-\frac{i}{2}H_{\bar{z}}e^{-w/2+u/2}\end{pmatrix}.

The second column of the first equation, together with the first column of the second equation, yields the nonlinear Dirac equations. In particular, letting
\mathfrak{D}
denote the Dirac operator for the conformal immersion
f
in
\operatorname{Nil_{1}^{3}}
,

(2.6)

\mathfrak{D}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{z}\psi_{2}+U\psi_{1}\\[4.0pt]
-\partial_{\bar{z}}\psi_{1}+V\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\end{pmatrix},\qquad U=V=e^{w/2}.

The left-translated unit normal vector
f^{-1}N
then takes values in the union of the two hyperbolic planes

\mathbb{H}_{+}^{2}\cup\mathbb{H}_{-}^{2}\subset\mathfrak{nil}_{1}^{3}
.
More precisely,
f^{-1}N
lies in
\mathbb{H}_{+}^{2}
when
|\psi_{1}|<|\psi_{2}|
, and in
\mathbb{H}_{-}^{2}
when
|\psi_{1}|>|\psi_{2}|
.
By composing
f^{-1}N
with the stereographic projection
\pi
from the south pole, we obtain the
normal Gauss map

g=\pi\circ(f^{-1}N)\colon M\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}\setminus\mathbb{S}^{1}.
In terms of the generating spinors, the Gauss map
g
and the normal vector
f^{-1}N
can be written as

(2.7)

g=\frac{\psi_{1}}{\overline{\psi_{2}}},\qquad f^{-1}N=\frac{1}{|g|^{2}-1}\bigl(2\,\Im(g)\,e_{1}-2\,\Re(g)\,e_{2}-(|g|^{2}+1)\,e_{3}\bigr).

We next recall the associated family of Maurer-Cartan forms
\alpha^{\lambda}
, defined for
\lambda\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
by

(2.8)

\alpha^{\lambda}=\widetilde{U}^{\lambda}\,dz+\widetilde{V}^{\lambda}\,d\bar{z}.

Here the matrices
\widetilde{U}_{\lambda}
and
\widetilde{V}_{\lambda}
are given by

(2.9)

\widetilde{U}^{\lambda}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{4}w_{z}-\frac{i}{2}H_{z}e^{-w/2+u/2}&-\lambda^{-1}e^{w/2}\\[6.0pt]
\lambda^{-1}Be^{-w/2}&-\frac{1}{4}w_{z}\end{pmatrix},\quad\widetilde{V}^{\lambda}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{4}w_{\bar{z}}&-\lambda\overline{B}e^{-w/2}\\[6.0pt]
\lambda e^{w/2}&\frac{1}{4}w_{\bar{z}}-\frac{i}{2}H_{\bar{z}}e^{-w/2+u/2}\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
\widetilde{U}^{\lambda}|_{\lambda=1}=\widetilde{U}
and

\widetilde{V}^{\lambda}|_{\lambda=1}=\widetilde{V}
in (
2.5
) after suitable gauge transformation applied to
\widetilde{U}
and
\widetilde{V}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
1
, Theorem 3.2]

Let
f:\mathbb{D}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Nil_{1}^{3}}
be a conformal spacelike immersion and
\alpha_{\lambda}

the
1
-form defined in (
2.8
) and
g
is the normal Gauss map. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

f
is a maximal surface.

(2)

d+\alpha^{\lambda}
is a family of flat connections on
\mathbb{D}\times\mathrm{SU}_{2}
.

(3)

The normal Gauss map
g
for
f
is a nowhere holomorphic harmonic map into the
2-sphere.

For more details on the maximal surface in
\mathrm{Nil_{1}^{3}}
, see
[
1
,
9
]
.

Next, starting from a given pair of generating spinors associated with a maximal surface in
\mathrm{Nil}_{1}^{3}
, we introduce a new pair of
dual generating spinors
.
We show that this dual pair satisfies the corresponding spinor system and therefore
determines a new maximal surface in
\mathrm{Nil}_{1}^{3}
.
Moreover, applying the same dual construction to the new surface recovers the original
surface, up to rigid motions.

2.2.
Dual maximal surface

Let
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
denote the generating spinors of a conformal maximal surface

f
in
\mathrm{Nil}_{1}^{3}
. We define the corresponding
dual generating spinors

\psi_{1}^{\ast}
and
\psi_{2}^{\ast}
by

(2.10)

\psi_{1}^{\ast}=\frac{4\sqrt{-B}}{h}\,\psi_{2},\qquad\psi_{2}^{\ast}=\frac{4\sqrt{-\overline{B}}}{h}\,\psi_{1},

where
B
is the Abresch-Rosenberg differential and
h
is the support function of
f
as
introduced in Subsection
2.1
.
In the case
B\equiv 0
, we have
\psi_{1}^{\ast}\equiv\psi_{2}^{\ast}\equiv 0
; therefore, we exclude such maximal surfaces. If
B\not\equiv 0
, then since
B
is holomorphic its zeros are isolated and correspond to
branch points of the dual surface, which will be discussed in
Theorem
2.3
.

The following lemma shows that the dual generating spinors satisfy a linear spinor system.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{D}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Nil}_{1}^{3}
be a conformal maximal surface with generating
spinors
(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
, and suppose that
B\not\equiv 0
.
Let
(\psi_{1}^{\ast},\psi_{2}^{\ast})
denote the associated dual generating spinors
defined in (
2.10
).
Then the vector of generating spinors
\tilde{\psi}^{\ast}=(\psi_{1}^{\ast},\psi_{2}^{\ast})
satisfies the following linear spinor system:

(2.11)

\tilde{\psi}^{\ast}_{z}=\tilde{\psi}^{\ast}\,\widetilde{U}^{\ast},\qquad\tilde{\psi}^{\ast}_{\bar{z}}=\tilde{\psi}^{\ast}\,\widetilde{V}^{\ast},

where

\widetilde{U}^{\ast}=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{1}{2}w^{\ast}_{z}&-e^{w^{\ast}/2}\\[3.0pt]
B^{\ast}e^{-w^{\ast}/2}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\widetilde{V}^{\ast}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\overline{B^{\ast}}e^{-w^{\ast}/2}\\[3.0pt]
e^{w^{\ast}/2}&\dfrac{1}{2}w^{\ast}_{\bar{z}}\end{pmatrix},

with

(2.12)

e^{w^{\ast}/2}=\frac{4|B|}{h},\qquad B^{\ast}=B.

Proof.

Using (
2.12
), and the relations

\frac{1}{2}w^{\ast}_{z}=\Bigl(\log{\frac{4\sqrt{-B}}{h}}\Bigr)_{z},\qquad\frac{hB}{4|B|}=B^{\ast}e^{-w^{\ast}/2},\qquad e^{w/2}=-h/4,

where
B(z)\neq 0
, we compute

\displaystyle\psi^{\ast}_{1z}

\displaystyle=\Bigl({\frac{4\sqrt{-B}}{h}}\Bigr)_{z}\psi_{2}+{\frac{4\sqrt{-B}}{h}}\;\psi_{2z}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\log{\frac{4\sqrt{-B}}{h}}\Bigr)_{z}\psi_{1}^{\ast}+\frac{hB}{4|B|}\psi_{2}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}w_{z}^{\ast}\psi_{1}^{\ast}+B^{\ast}e^{-w^{\ast}/2}\psi_{2}^{\ast}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\psi^{\ast}_{2z}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\frac{4\sqrt{-\overline{B}}}{h}\Bigr)_{z}\psi_{1}+\frac{4\sqrt{-\overline{B}}}{h}\;\psi_{1z}

\displaystyle=-\!(\log h)_{z}\frac{4\sqrt{-\overline{B}}}{h}\psi_{1}+\frac{4\sqrt{-\overline{B}}}{h}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}w_{z}\psi_{1}+Be^{-w/2}\psi_{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=-e^{w^{\ast}/2}\psi^{\ast}_{1}.

Furthermore, a similar computation for
\tilde{\psi}^{\ast}_{\bar{z}}
gives the second equation
in (
2.11
).
∎

Using the above lemma, we now state the main theorem for this section.

Theorem 2.3
.

Retain all the notation and the assumptions of Lemma
2.2
and the relation in (
2.12
), there exists a maximal immersion
f^{\ast}
in
\mathrm{Nil_{1}^{3}}
whose generating
spinors are
\psi_{1}^{\ast}
and
\psi_{2}^{\ast}
, with Dirac potential

U^{\ast}=V^{\ast}=e^{w^{\ast}/2}
, and whose left-translated unit normal equals

f^{-1}N
up to sign.
Moreover, the following hold:

(1)

By choosing the left-translated unit normal
N^{\ast}
so that
(f^{\ast})^{-1}N^{\ast}=f^{-1}N\subset\mathfrak{nil_{1}^{3}}
, the metric
e^{u^{\ast}}|dz|^{2}
, support function
h^{\ast}
, Abresch-Rosenberg differential
B^{\ast}dz^{2}
, and normal Gauss map
g^{\ast}
of
f^{\ast}
are respectively given by

(2.13)

e^{u^{\ast}}=\frac{16^{2}|B|^{2}}{h^{4}}\,e^{u},\qquad h^{\ast}=\frac{16|B|}{h},\qquad B^{\ast}=B,\qquad\text{and }\quad g^{\ast}={g}.

(2)

It satisfies the duality
f^{\ast\ast}=f
up to a rigid motion, and the zeros of
B
are branch points of the dual
f^{\ast}
.

The surface
f^{\ast}
will be called the
dual maximal surface
to
f
.

Proof.

By using Lemma
2.2
, the vector of generating spinors
\tilde{\psi}^{\ast}=(\psi_{1}^{\ast},\psi_{2}^{\ast})
defines a conformal immersion

f^{\ast}
in
\mathrm{Nil_{1}^{3}}
. The Dirac potential

U^{\ast}=V^{\ast}=e^{w^{\ast}/2}=\frac{H^{\ast}}{2}ie^{u^{\ast}/2}-\frac{1}{4}h^{\ast}=\frac{4|B|}{h}\in\mathbb{R}.

Thus
f^{\ast}
is maximal surface in
\operatorname{Nil_{1}^{3}}
.
Moreover, the tangent planes of
f
and
f^{\ast}
coincide, so their left-translated unit normals
f^{-1}N
and
(f^{\ast})^{-1}N^{\ast}
agree
up to sign.

(1) The metric follows from the definition of

\psi_{1}^{\ast}
and
\psi_{2}^{\ast}
, and
h^{\ast}
follows from the definition of support function of
f^{\ast}
. Since we choose
(f^{\ast})^{-1}N^{\ast}=f^{-1}N
, therefore
g^{\ast}=g
.

(2) The double-dual spinors are

\psi^{\ast\ast}_{1}={\frac{4\sqrt{-B^{\ast}}}{h^{\ast}}}\,\psi^{\ast}_{2}=\psi_{1},\qquad\psi^{\ast\ast}_{2}={\frac{4\sqrt{-B^{\ast}}}{h^{\ast}}}\,\psi^{\ast}_{1}=\psi_{2}.

Hence
(\psi^{\ast\ast}_{1},\psi^{\ast\ast}_{2})
defines the original surface
f
up to rigid
motion, and since
e^{u^{\ast}}=\frac{16^{2}|B|^{2}}{h^{4}}\,e^{u}
, it is clear that the zeros of
B
are branch points of
f^{\ast}
.
∎

In the following subsection, we present the Sym formula for dual maximal surfaces in

\mathrm{Nil}_{1}^{3}
. Our approach relies on the loop group method developed for the study
of maximal surfaces in
\mathrm{Nil}_{1}^{3}
. (see
[
1
]
)

2.3.
Sym formula for dual maximal surface

We begin by identifying the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{nil}_{1}^{3}
with
\mathfrak{su}_{2}
(Lie algebra of
\operatorname{SU}_{2}
) as
real vector spaces. In
\mathfrak{su}_{2}
, we fix the basis

(2.14)

E_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
-i&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad E_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad E_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}i&0\\
0&-i\end{pmatrix},

which forms an orthogonal basis of
\mathfrak{su}_{2}
.
Then a linear isomorphism
\Xi\colon\mathfrak{su}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{nil}_{1}^{3}
is given by
x_{1}E_{1}+x_{2}E_{2}+x_{3}E_{3}\longmapsto x_{1}e_{1}+x_{2}e_{2}+x_{3}e_{3}.
Define a smooth bijection
\Xi_{\mathrm{nil}}:\mathfrak{su}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Nil_{1}^{3}}
by

\Xi_{\mathrm{nil}}:=\exp\circ\Xi
;

\Xi_{\mathrm{nil}}(x_{1}E_{1}+x_{2}E_{2}+x_{3}E_{3})=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}).

Now by Theorem
2.1
, the associated family of
Maurer-Cartan forms
\alpha^{\lambda}
in (
2.8
) for maximal surfaces,
can be simplified as follows:

(2.15)

\alpha^{\lambda}=U^{\lambda}\,dz+V^{\lambda}\,d\bar{z},

with

(2.16)

U^{\lambda}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{4}w_{z}&-\lambda^{-1}e^{w/2}\\[6.0pt]
\lambda^{-1}Be^{-w/2}&-\frac{1}{4}w_{z}\end{pmatrix},\qquad V^{\lambda}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{4}w_{\bar{z}}&-\lambda\overline{B}e^{-w/2}\\[6.0pt]
\lambda e^{w/2}&\frac{1}{4}w_{\bar{z}}\end{pmatrix}.

Let
F
be the
corresponding
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
valued solution of the equation
F^{-1}dF=\alpha^{\lambda}

(\lambda\in\mathbb{S}^{1})
, where
\alpha^{\lambda}
is defined by (
2.15
). We call
F

the
extended frame of the maximal surface

f
. In particular,
F
can be represented as:

(2.17)

F(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\psi_{1}(\lambda)|^{2}+|\psi_{2}(\lambda)|^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}(\lambda)&\psi_{2}(\lambda)\\[6.0pt]
-\overline{\psi_{2}(\lambda)}&\overline{\psi_{1}(\lambda)}\end{pmatrix},

where
\psi_{1}(\lambda)
and
\psi_{2}(\lambda)
denote families of functions such that

\psi_{1}(\lambda)|_{\lambda=1}=\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}(\lambda)|_{\lambda=1}=\psi_{2}
are the generating spinors
of the maximal surface
f
.

We now prove the Sym formula.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
F
be the extended frame for some maximal surface
f
on
\mathbb{D}
with

B\not\equiv 0
in
\mathrm{Nil_{1}^{3}}
, and define
m_{\pm}
and
N_{m}
respectively by

m_{\pm}=\pm\,i\,\lambda(\partial_{\lambda}F)F^{-1}-N_{m},\qquad N_{m}=\tfrac{i}{2}\operatorname{Ad}(F)\sigma_{3},

where
\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}
. Moreover, define maps
f^{\lambda}_{\pm}:\mathbb{D}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Nil_{1}^{3}}
by
f^{\lambda}_{\pm}:=\Xi_{\mathrm{nil}}\circ\widehat{f}^{\lambda}_{\pm}

with

(2.18)

\widehat{f}^{\lambda}_{\pm}=\Bigl(m_{\pm}^{\mathrm{o}}-\tfrac{i}{2}\lambda(\partial_{\lambda}m_{\pm})^{\mathrm{d}}\Bigr)\Big|_{\lambda\in\mathbb{S}^{1}},

where superscripts “o” and “d” denote off-diagonal and diagonal parts, respectively.
Then, for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, the maps
f^{\lambda}_{\pm}
are maximal surfaces in
\mathrm{Nil_{1}^{3}}

and
N_{m}
is the normal Gauss map of
f^{\lambda}_{\pm}
. In particular,
f^{\lambda}_{-}|_{\lambda=1}

is the original maximal surface
f
up to a rigid motion and
f^{\lambda}_{+}|_{\lambda=1}
is the
dual maximal surface
f^{\ast}
of
f
. Moreover, for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
f^{\lambda}_{-}
and
f^{\lambda}_{+}
are dual to each other.

Proof.

It is proven that
f^{\lambda}_{-}|_{\lambda=1}
is a maximal surface in
\operatorname{Nil_{1}^{3}}
in
[
1
, Theorem 3.4]
.
We only show for
f^{\lambda}_{+}|_{\lambda=1}
, and both are dual to each other for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. From a direct calculation, we have

\partial_{z}m_{+}=\operatorname{Ad}(F)\Bigl(i\lambda\partial_{\lambda}U_{\lambda}-\frac{i}{2}[U^{\lambda},\sigma_{3}]\Bigr)=-2i\lambda^{-1}Be^{-w/2}\operatorname{Ad}(F)\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Using the generating spinors and the support function
h
, this becomes

\partial_{z}m_{+}=\frac{16i\lambda^{-1}B}{h^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{2}(\lambda)\overline{\psi_{1}(\lambda)}&-\psi_{2}(\lambda)^{2}\\[4.0pt]
\overline{\psi_{1}(\lambda)^{2}}&-\psi_{2}(\lambda)\overline{\psi_{1}(\lambda)}\end{pmatrix}.

From the dual
generating spinors as in (
2.10
),
we have

(2.19)

\partial_{z}m_{+}=\varphi_{1}^{+}(\lambda)E_{1}+\varphi_{2}^{+}(\lambda)E_{2}+i\,\varphi_{3}^{+}(\lambda)E_{3},

where

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}^{+}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}\bigl((\overline{\psi_{2}}^{\ast}(\lambda))^{2}-(\psi_{1}^{\ast}(\lambda))^{2}\bigr),

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}^{+}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=i\lambda^{-1}\bigl((\overline{\psi_{2}}^{\ast}(\lambda))^{2}+(\psi_{1}^{\ast}(\lambda))^{2}\bigr),

\displaystyle\varphi_{3}^{+}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=2i\lambda^{-1}\psi_{1}^{\ast}(\lambda)\overline{\psi_{2}}^{\ast}(\lambda).

A computation of
\partial_{z}(i\lambda\partial_{\lambda}m_{+})
combined with the diagonal part
analysis yields

(2.20)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\Bigl(-\tfrac{i}{2}\lambda(\partial_{\lambda}m_{+})\Bigr)^{d}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}(\partial_{z}m_{+})^{d}-\frac{1}{2}[m_{+}+N_{m},\,\partial_{z}m_{+}]^{d}

\displaystyle=i(\partial_{z}m_{+})^{d}-\frac{1}{2}[m_{+},\,\partial_{z}m_{+}]^{d}

\displaystyle=-\Bigl(\varphi_{3}^{+}(\lambda)-\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{1}^{+}(\lambda)\int\varphi_{2}^{+}(\lambda)\,dz+\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{2}^{+}(\lambda)\int\varphi_{1}^{+}(\lambda)\,dz\Bigr)E_{3}.

Combining (
2.19
) and (
2.20
) we obtain

\partial_{z}\widehat{f}^{\lambda}_{+}=\varphi_{1}^{+}(\lambda)E_{1}+\varphi_{2}^{+}(\lambda)E_{2}-\Bigl(\varphi_{3}^{+}(\lambda)-\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{1}^{+}(\lambda)\int\varphi_{2}^{+}(\lambda)\,dz+\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{2}^{+}(\lambda)\int\varphi_{1}^{+}(\lambda)\,dz\Bigr)E_{3}.

Using the identification (
2.14
) and the left translation by
(f^{\lambda}_{+})^{-1}
, i.e.

(f^{\lambda}_{+})^{-1}\partial_{z}f^{\lambda}_{+}=\varphi_{1}^{+}(\lambda)e_{1}+\varphi_{2}^{+}(\lambda)e_{2}+\varphi_{3}^{+}(\lambda)e_{3},

we see that
\psi_{1}^{\ast}(\lambda)
and
\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\lambda)
are spinors for
f^{\lambda}_{+}
for
each
\lambda\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. In particular, the function

-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(|\psi_{1}^{\ast}(\lambda)|^{2}+|\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\lambda)|^{2}\bigr)=e^{w^{\ast}/2}=\frac{4|B|}{h}

is independent of
\lambda
, so the mean curvature
H=0
. Moreover, the conformal factor of the induced metric of
f^{\lambda}_{+}
is given by

e^{u^{\ast}}=4\bigl(|\psi_{1}^{\ast}(\lambda)|^{2}-|\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\lambda)|^{2}\bigr)^{2}=\frac{16^{2}|B|^{2}}{h^{4}}\,e^{u}.

Thus, for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
with
B(z)\neq 0
,
the immersion
f^{\lambda}_{+}
defines a maximal surface in
\mathrm{Nil}_{1}^{3}
.

∎

Remark 2.5
.

It can be observed that replacing

Consider two hyperbolic paraboloids

and

2.4.
Singularities for maximal surfaces

It is natural to consider maximal surfaces in
\operatorname{Nil}_{1}^{3}
that admit
singularities.
As shown in
[
1
]
, the induced metric
I
of a maximal immersion

f
, expressed in terms of the normal Gauss map
g
, can be written as

I=4\bigl(|\psi_{1}|^{2}-|\psi_{2}|^{2}\bigr)^{2}|dz|^{2}=4\,|\bar{g}_{z}|^{2}\frac{(1-|g|^{2})^{2}}{(1+|g|^{2})^{4}}\,|dz|^{2}.

It follows that singularities occur precisely at points where either
|g|=1
or

g_{\bar{z}}=0
.
Moreover, points at which the Gauss map
g
is holomorphic are always degenerate
singularities.
Any maximal surface with singularities whose normal Gauss map

g\colon M\to\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
is nowhere holomorphic is referred to as a

generalized maximal surface
.
Also note that the generic singularities of generalized maximal surfaces are cuspidal edges,
swallowtails, and cuspidal cross caps; see
[
1
]
for details.

As in the preceding subsection, a generalized maximal surface can be constructed from generating spinors. From Theorem
2.3
, the maximal surface
f
and its dual
f^{\ast}
share
the same normal Gauss map (upon choosing the same left-translated unit normal) and the same Abresch-Rosenberg differential.
Consequently, analogous properties hold for generalized maximal surfaces. The correspondence between the generic singularities of a generalized maximal surface and those of its dual follows from Lemma 4.3 and Theorem 4.4 in
[
1
]
.
Hence, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
f
be a generalized maximal surface with nonvanishing Abresch-Rosenberg differential, and
f^{\ast}
be its dual surface. Then both surfaces
f
and
f^{\ast}
have the same singularity set and the same generic singularities.

3.
Dual Timelike minimal surfaces in
\operatorname{Nil_{3}}
and its Sym formula

In this section, we will discuss the dual surfaces of the timelike minimal surfaces in
\mathrm{Nil}_{t}^{3}
. For the sake of simplicity, we retain the same notations as in the previous section.

3.1.
Preliminaries for timelike minimal surfaces

In this subsection, we recall the necessary background on timelike minimal surfaces in the
Lorentzian Heisenberg group
\operatorname{Nil}_{t}^{3}
from
[
6
]
.
We begin with a brief review of para-complex numbers and then discuss the generating
spinors associated with timelike minimal surfaces, together with their normal Gauss maps.

The ring of para-complex numbers denoted by
\mathbb{C^{\prime}}
is defined as,

\mathbb{C^{\prime}}:=\left\{z=x+i^{\prime}y\mid x,y\in\mathbb{R},\,(i^{\prime})^{2}=1\right\}.

We define the real part, imaginary part, and para-conjugation by
\Re z=x,\Im z=y,\text{ and }\bar{z}=x-i^{\prime}y.

Also note that
z\bar{z}=0
does not imply that
z=0
.
A para-complex number
z=x+i^{\prime}y
has a square root in
\mathbb{C}^{\prime}
if and only if

x+y\geq 0,\,x-y\geq 0.

In particular,
i^{\prime}
has no para-complex square root.
Similarly,
z=e^{w}
for some
w\in\mathbb{C}^{\prime}
holds if and only if

x+y>0,\,x-y>0.

Let
M
be an orientable, connected
2
-manifold, and let
G
be a Lorentzian manifold.
A smooth immersion
f:M\longrightarrow G
is called
timelike
if the induced metric is Lorentzian. Locally,
M
admits para-complex coordinates
z=x+i^{\prime}y
such that
I=e^{u}\,dz\,d\bar{z}=e^{u}\big((dx)^{2}-(dy)^{2}\big),

where
z
is a conformal coordinate and
e^{u}
the conformal factor. Also, for a para-complex coordinate
z=x+yi^{\prime}
, we define

\partial_{z}:=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}+i^{\prime}\partial_{y}),\qquad\partial_{\bar{z}}:=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}-i^{\prime}\partial_{y}).

Let
f:M\longrightarrow\mathrm{Nil}_{t}^{3}
be a conformal immersion from a Lorentz surface
M
with a local
conformal coordinate
z=x+yi^{\prime}
in a simply connected domain
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}\subset M
, and orthonormal basis of Lie algebra
\mathfrak{nil}_{t}^{3}
of
\mathrm{Nil}_{t}^{3}
is
\{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3}\}
. The left-translated Maurer-Cartan form expands as

f^{-1}df=(f^{-1}f_{z})\,dz+(f^{-1}f_{\bar{z}})\,d\bar{z},\qquad f^{-1}f_{z}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\varphi_{k}\varepsilon_{k},\quad f^{-1}f_{\bar{z}}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\overline{\varphi_{k}}\varepsilon_{k}.

The conformality of
f
gives the algebraic relations

(3.1)

-\varphi_{1}^{2}+\varphi_{2}^{2}+\varphi_{3}^{2}=0,\qquad-\varphi_{1}\overline{\varphi_{1}}+\varphi_{2}\overline{\varphi_{2}}+\varphi_{3}\overline{\varphi_{3}}=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{u}\neq 0,

so the induced metric is
I=e^{u}dzd\overline{z}
.

From
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
, the system (
3.1
) can be parametrized using a pair of
generating spinors

\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
, through the expressions

(3.2)

\varphi_{1}=\epsilon\bigg((\overline{\psi_{2}})^{2}+\psi_{1}^{2}\bigg),\qquad\varphi_{2}=\epsilon i^{\prime}\bigg((\overline{\psi_{2}})^{2}-\psi_{1}^{2}\bigg),\qquad\varphi_{3}=2\psi_{1}\overline{\psi_{2}},

where
\epsilon=i^{\prime}
or
-i^{\prime}
.
Let
N
denote the positively oriented unit normal vector field along the immersion
f
and define the rescaled normal vector
L:=e^{u/2}N
. The
support function

h
is then defined by
h=\langle f^{-1}L,\varepsilon_{3}\rangle.

In terms of the generating spinors
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
, we obtain the identities

(3.3)

e^{u}=4\big(\psi_{1}\overline{\psi_{1}}+\psi_{2}\overline{\psi_{2}}\big)^{2},\qquad h=2\big(\psi_{2}\overline{\psi_{2}}-\psi_{1}\overline{\psi_{1}}\big).

Furthermore, we set the Dirac potential
U=V={\widetilde{\epsilon}}e^{w/2}
for some
\mathbb{C}^{\prime}
valued function
w
and
\widetilde{\epsilon}=\{\pm 1,\pm i^{\prime}\}
, and define the the Abresch-Rosenberg
differential
Q\,dz^{2}=4B\,dz^{2}
. Following
[
6
]
, these can be expressed as

(3.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\epsilon}e^{w/2}

\displaystyle=U=V=-\frac{H}{2}e^{u/2}+\frac{i^{\prime}}{4}h,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\frac{2H-i^{\prime}}{4}\bigg(2(\psi_{1}\overline{\psi_{2}}_{z}-\overline{\psi_{2}}{\psi_{1}}_{z}\bigr)-4i^{\prime}H(\psi_{1}\overline{\psi_{2}})^{2}\bigg)-\frac{\varphi_{3}^{2}}{2H-i^{\prime}}.

The generating spinors
\tilde{\psi}=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
satisfy a
linear spinor system
of the form

(3.5)

\tilde{\psi}_{z}=\tilde{\psi}\,\widetilde{U},\qquad\tilde{\psi}_{\bar{z}}=\tilde{\psi}\,\widetilde{V},

where

\widetilde{U}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}w_{z}+\frac{1}{2}H_{z}\widetilde{\epsilon}e^{-w/2+u/2}&-\widetilde{\epsilon}e^{w/2}\\[4.0pt]
B\widetilde{\epsilon}e^{-w/2}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\widetilde{V}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\overline{B}\widetilde{\epsilon}e^{-w/2}\\[4.0pt]
\widetilde{\epsilon}e^{w/2}&\frac{1}{2}w_{\bar{z}}+\frac{1}{2}H_{\bar{z}}\widetilde{\epsilon}e^{-w/2+u/2}\end{pmatrix}.

The second column of the first equation, when combined with the first column of the second equation, yields the nonlinear Dirac equations. In particular, letting
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
denote the Dirac operator for the conformal immersion in
\operatorname{Nil}_{t}^{3}
,

(3.6)

\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{z}\psi_{2}+U\psi_{1}\\[4.0pt]
-\partial_{\bar{z}}\psi_{1}+V\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\end{pmatrix},\qquad U=V=\widetilde{\epsilon}e^{w/2}.

Remark 3.1
.

In particular, if the mean curvature
is zero and the function
h
has positive values, then
\tilde{\epsilon}=i^{\prime}
in (
3.6
).

The left-translated unit normal
f^{-1}N
lies in the de Sitter two sphere
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{1}^{2}\subset\mathfrak{nil}_{t}^{3}
,
where

\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{1}^{2}=\{x_{1}\varepsilon_{1}+x_{2}\varepsilon_{2}+x_{3}\varepsilon_{3}\in\mathfrak{nil}_{t}^{3}|-x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{3}=1\}.

Now, by composing the stereographic projection
\pi_{\mathfrak{nil}_{t}^{3}}^{+}
from
(0,0,1)\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{1}^{2}\subset\mathfrak{nil}_{t}^{3}
to
\mathbb{C}^{\prime}
with the left-translated unit normal
f^{-1}N
gives the
normal Gauss map

g=\pi_{\mathfrak{nil}_{t}^{3}}^{+}\circ(f^{-1}N):M\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\prime}
. In terms of generating spinors,

(3.7)

g=i^{\prime}\frac{\overline{\psi_{1}}}{\psi_{2}},\qquad f^{-1}N=\frac{1}{1-g\overline{g}}\big(2\Re(g)\,\varepsilon_{1}+2\Im(g)\,\varepsilon_{2}-(1+g\overline{g})\,\varepsilon_{3}\big).

Next, we recall the family of Maurer-Cartan forms
\alpha^{\mu}
parameterized by
\mu\in\mathbb{S}^{1^{\prime}}:=\{e^{i^{\prime}t}|\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
as follows:

(3.8)

\alpha^{\mu}=\widetilde{U}^{\mu}\,dz+\widetilde{V}^{\mu}\,d\overline{z}

where

\widetilde{U}^{\mu}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{4}w_{z}+\frac{1}{2}H_{z}\widetilde{\epsilon}e^{-w/2+u/2}&-\mu^{-1}\widetilde{\epsilon}e^{w/2}\\[4.0pt]
\mu^{-1}B\widetilde{\epsilon}e^{-w/2}&-\frac{1}{4}w_{z}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\widetilde{V}^{\mu}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{4}w_{\bar{z}}&-\mu\overline{B}\widetilde{\epsilon}e^{-w/2}\\[4.0pt]
\mu\widetilde{\epsilon}e^{w/2}&\frac{1}{4}w_{\bar{z}}+\frac{1}{2}H_{\bar{z}}\widetilde{\epsilon}e^{-w/2+u/2}\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
\widetilde{U}^{\lambda}|_{\lambda=1}=\widetilde{U}
and

\widetilde{V}^{\lambda}|_{\lambda=1}=\widetilde{V}
in (
3.5
) after suitable gauge transformation applied to
\widetilde{U}
and
\widetilde{V}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
6
, Theorem 3.2]

Let
f
be a conformal timelike immersion from a simply connected domain
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{C}^{\prime}
into
\mathrm{Nil_{t}^{3}}
where Dirac potential
(\Re\,U)^{2}-(\Im\,U)^{2}\neq 0
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

f
is a timelike minimal surface.

(2)

The Dirac potential
\widetilde{\epsilon}e^{w/2}=U=V=-\frac{H}{2}e^{u/2}+\frac{i^{\prime}}{4}h
takes purely imaginary values.

(3)

d+\alpha^{\mu}
defines a family of flat connections on
\mathbb{D}\times\mathrm{SU}_{1,1}^{\prime}
.

From now on, we will assume that the function
h
takes positive values, that is, the image of the left-translated unit normal
f^{-1}N
lies in the lower half of the de Sitter two-sphere
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{1}^{2}
. For more details on timelike minimal surfaces in the Lorentzian Heisenberg group, see
[
6
,
7
]
.

Next, we introduce dual generating spinors for timelike minimal surfaces and show that
they determine dual timelike minimal surface in
\mathrm{Nil}_{t}^{3}
.

3.2.
Dual timelike minimal surface

We define the pair of dual generating spinors

\psi_{1}^{\ast}
and
\psi_{2}^{\ast}
as

(3.9)

\psi_{1}^{\ast}={\frac{4\sqrt{\epsilon\,B}}{h}}\;\psi_{2},\qquad\psi_{2}^{\ast}={\frac{4\sqrt{\overline{\epsilon}\,\overline{B}}}{h}}\;\psi_{1},

where
B
is the Abresch-Rosenberg differential and
h
is the support
function as in Subsection
3.1
. If
B\bar{B}\equiv 0
, the corresponding timelike minimal surface is called a null scroll. We exclude null scroll from our study (for details see
[
7
]
).

The following lemma shows that the dual generating spinors satisfy a linear system.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
be the generating spinors of a timelike minimal
surface
f
in
\mathrm{Nil}_{t}^{3}
, and let
\psi_{1}^{\ast}
and
\psi_{2}^{\ast}
be the dual
generating spinors defined in (
3.9
).
Assume that the Abresch-Rosenberg differential satisfies
B\bar{B}\not\equiv 0
.
Then the vector of generating spinors
\tilde{\psi}^{\ast}=(\psi_{1}^{\ast},\psi_{2}^{\ast})

satisfies the following linear spinor system:

(3.10)

\tilde{\psi}^{\ast}_{z}=\tilde{\psi}^{\ast}\,\widetilde{U}^{\ast},\qquad\tilde{\psi}^{\ast}_{\bar{z}}=\tilde{\psi}^{\ast}\,\widetilde{V}^{\ast},

where

\widetilde{U}^{\ast}=\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac{1}{2}w^{\ast}_{z}&i^{\prime}\epsilon e^{w^{\ast}/2}\\[3.0pt]
-i^{\prime}\epsilon B^{\ast}e^{-w^{\ast}/2}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\widetilde{V}^{\ast}=\begin{pmatrix}0&i^{\prime}\bar{\epsilon}\bar{B}^{\ast}e^{-w^{\ast}/2}\\[3.0pt]
i^{\prime}\bar{\epsilon}e^{w^{\ast}/2}&\tfrac{1}{2}w^{\ast}_{\bar{z}}\end{pmatrix},

with

(3.11)

e^{w^{\ast}/2}=\frac{4\sqrt{\epsilon\bar{\epsilon}B\overline{B}}}{h},\qquad B^{\ast}=B.

Proof.

Recall that for
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1,\pm i^{\prime}\}
, we have
1/\epsilon=\epsilon
.
From (
3.11
), and the relations

\frac{1}{2}w^{\ast}_{z}=\Bigl(\log{\frac{4\sqrt{\epsilon B}}{h}}\Bigr)_{z},\qquad\frac{hB}{4\sqrt{\epsilon\bar{\epsilon}B\bar{B}}}=B^{\ast}\ e^{-w^{\ast}/2},\qquad e^{w/2}=\frac{h}{4},

where
B(z)\overline{B(z)}\neq 0
, we compute

\displaystyle\psi^{\ast}_{1z}

\displaystyle=\Bigl({\frac{4\sqrt{\epsilon B}}{h}}\Bigr)_{z}\psi_{2}+{\frac{4\sqrt{\epsilon B}}{h}}\;\psi_{2z}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\log{\frac{4\sqrt{\epsilon B}}{h}}\Bigr)_{z}\psi_{1}^{\ast}-\frac{\epsilon hBi^{\prime}}{4\sqrt{\epsilon\bar{\epsilon}B\bar{B}}}\psi_{2}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}w_{z}^{\ast}\psi_{1}^{\ast}-i^{\prime}\epsilon B^{\ast}e^{-w^{\ast}/2}\psi_{2}^{\ast}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\psi^{\ast}_{2z}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\frac{4\sqrt{\bar{\epsilon}\bar{B}}}{h}\Bigr)_{z}\psi_{1}+\frac{4\sqrt{\bar{\epsilon}\bar{B}}}{h}\;\psi_{1z}

\displaystyle=-\!(\log h)_{z}\frac{4\sqrt{\bar{\epsilon}\bar{B}}}{h}\psi_{1}+\frac{4\sqrt{\bar{\epsilon}\bar{B}}}{h}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}w_{z}\psi_{1}+i^{\prime}Be^{-w/2}\psi_{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=i^{\prime}\epsilon e^{w^{\ast}/2}\psi^{\ast}_{1}.

A similar computation for
\tilde{\psi}^{\ast}_{\bar{z}}
gives the second equation
in (
3.10
).
∎

With the above lemma, we state the main theorem for this section.

Theorem 3.4
.

Retain all the notation and the assumptions of Lemma
3.3
and the relation in (
3.11
), there exists a conformal immersion
f^{\ast}
in
\mathrm{Nil}_{t}^{3}
whose generating
spinors are
\psi_{1}^{\ast}
and
\psi_{2}^{\ast}
, with Dirac potential

U^{\ast}=V^{\ast}=i^{\prime}e^{w^{\ast}/2}
, and whose left-translated unit normal equals

f^{-1}N
up to sign.
Moreover, the following hold:

(1)

By choosing the left-translated unit normal
N^{\ast}
so that

(f^{\ast})^{-1}N^{\ast}=f^{-1}N\subset\mathfrak{nil}_{t}^{3}
, the metric
e^{u^{\ast}}dzd\bar{z}
, support function
h^{\ast}
, Abresch-Rosenberg differential
B^{\ast}dz^{2}
, and normal Gauss map
g^{\ast}
of
f^{\ast}
are respectively given by

(3.12)

e^{u^{\ast}/2}=\frac{16\sqrt{\epsilon\bar{\epsilon}B\bar{B}}}{h^{2}}\,e^{u/2},\qquad h^{\ast}=\frac{16\sqrt{\epsilon\bar{\epsilon}B\bar{B}}}{h},\qquad B^{\ast}=B,\qquad\text{and}\quad g^{\ast}={g}.

(2)

The surface
f^{\ast}
is non-vertical and timelike minimal.

(3)

It satisfies the duality
f^{\ast\ast}=f
up to a rigid motion.

The surface
f^{\ast}
will be called the
dual timelike minimal surface
to
f
.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.3
,
\tilde{\psi}^{\ast}=(\psi_{1}^{\ast},\psi_{2}^{\ast})
defines a conformal immersion

f^{\ast}
in
\mathrm{Nil}_{t}^{3}
.
Since tangent planes of
f
and
f^{\ast}
coincide, their left-translated unit normals
f^{-1}N
and
(f^{\ast})^{-1}N^{\ast}
agree
up to sign.

(1) The metric in (
3.12
) is clear from the definition of

\psi_{1}^{\ast},\psi_{2}^{\ast}
and
h^{\ast}
follows from the definition of the support function of
f^{\ast}
. We choose such Gauss map
(f^{\ast})^{-1}N^{\ast}=f^{-1}N
that gives
g^{\ast}=g.

(2) From construction
f^{\ast}
is non-vertical and
H^{\ast}=0
follows from the Dirac potential

U^{\ast}=V^{\ast}=i^{\prime}e^{w^{\ast}/2}=-\frac{H^{\ast}}{2}e^{u^{\ast}/2}+\frac{i^{\prime}}{4}h^{\ast}=\frac{4i^{\prime}\sqrt{\epsilon\bar{\epsilon}B\overline{B}}}{h}.

Thus
f^{\ast}
is non-vertical timelike minimal surface in
\operatorname{Nil}_{t}^{3}
.

(3) The double-dual spinors are

\psi^{\ast\ast}_{1}={\frac{4\sqrt{\epsilon\,B^{\ast}}}{h^{\ast}}}\,\psi^{\ast}_{2}=\psi_{1},\qquad\psi^{\ast\ast}_{2}={\frac{4\sqrt{\bar{\epsilon}\,\overline{B}^{\ast}}}{h^{\ast}}}\,\psi^{\ast}_{1}=\psi_{2}.

Hence
(\psi^{\ast\ast}_{1},\psi^{\ast\ast}_{2})
defines the original surface
f
up to rigid
motion.
∎

Next, we present the Sym formula for dual timelike minimal surfaces in

\mathrm{Nil}_{t}^{3}
.

3.3.
The Sym formula for dual timelike minimal surface

We first identify the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{nil_{t}^{3}}
with
\mathfrak{su}_{1,1}^{\prime}
(Lie algebra of
\operatorname{SU}^{\prime}_{1,1}
)
as a real vector space. In
\mathfrak{su}_{1,1}^{\prime}
, choose the basis

(3.13)

\mathcal{E}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&-i^{\prime}\\
i^{\prime}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}i^{\prime}&0\\
0&-i^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}.

We can see that
\{\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2},\mathcal{E}_{3}\}
is an orthogonal basis of
\mathfrak{su}_{1,1}^{\prime}
. A linear isomorphism
\Xi_{t}:\mathfrak{su}_{1,1}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{nil}_{t}^{3}

is then given by

(3.14)

\mathfrak{su}_{1,1}^{\prime}\ni x_{1}\mathcal{E}_{1}+x_{2}\mathcal{E}_{2}+x_{3}\mathcal{E}_{3}\longmapsto x_{1}\varepsilon_{1}+x_{2}\varepsilon_{2}+x_{3}\varepsilon_{3}\in\mathfrak{nil}_{t}^{3}.

Define a smooth bijection
\Xi_{\mathrm{nil}_{t}}:\mathfrak{su}_{1,1}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Nil}_{t}^{3}
by

\Xi_{\mathrm{nil}_{t}}:=\exp\circ\Xi_{t}

\Xi_{\mathrm{nil}_{t}}(x_{1}\mathcal{E}_{1}+x_{2}\mathcal{E}_{2}+x_{3}\mathcal{E}_{3})=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}).

For timelike minimal surface with
h>0
, the Maurer-Cartan forms
\alpha^{\mu}
as in (
3.8
) rewrite as

(3.15)

\alpha^{\mu}={U}^{\mu}\,dz+{V}^{\mu}\,d\overline{z}

where

{U}^{\mu}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{4}w_{z}&-\mu^{-1}i^{\prime}e^{w/2}\\[4.0pt]
\mu^{-1}Bi^{\prime}e^{-w/2}&-\frac{1}{4}w_{z}\end{pmatrix},\qquad{V}^{\mu}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{4}w_{\bar{z}}&-\mu\overline{B}i^{\prime}e^{-w/2}\\[4.0pt]
\mu i^{\prime}e^{w/2}&\frac{1}{4}w_{\bar{z}}\end{pmatrix}.

Let
F
be the
corresponding
\mathrm{SU}_{1,1}
valued solution of the equation
F^{-1}dF=\alpha^{\mu}

(\mu\in\mathbb{S}^{1^{\prime}})
, where
\alpha^{\mu}
is defined by (
3.15
). Then
F
is called
the
extended frame of the timelike minimal surface

f
. Also,
F
can be represented as:

(3.16)

F(\mu)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\psi_{2}(\mu)\overline{\psi_{2}(\mu)}-\psi_{1}(\mu)\overline{\psi_{1}(\mu)}}}\begin{pmatrix}\overline{\psi_{2}(\mu)}&\overline{\psi_{1}(\mu)}\\[6.0pt]
\psi_{1}(\mu)&\psi_{2}(\mu)\end{pmatrix},

where
\psi_{1}(\mu)
and
\psi_{2}(\mu)
denote families of functions such that

\psi_{1}(\mu=1)=\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}(\mu=1)=\psi_{2}
, which are the generating spinors
of the surface
f
.

We now prove the Sym formula for the dual of the timelike minimal surfaces.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
F
be the extended frame for a timelike minimal surface
f
on
\mathbb{D}
with

B\not\equiv 0
and
\epsilon=i^{\prime}
. Define
m_{\pm}
and
N_{m}
respectively by

m_{\pm}=-i^{\prime}\,\mu(\partial_{\mu}F)F^{-1}\pm N_{m},\qquad N_{m}=\frac{i^{\prime}}{2}\operatorname{Ad}(F)\sigma_{3},

where
\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
Moreover, define maps
f^{\mu}_{\pm}:\mathbb{D}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Nil}_{t}^{3}
by
f^{\mu}_{\pm}:=\Xi_{\mathrm{nil_{t}}}\circ\widehat{f}^{\mu}_{\pm}

with

(3.17)

\widehat{f}^{\mu}_{\pm}=\Bigl(m_{\pm}^{\mathrm{o}}-\frac{i^{\prime}}{2}\mu(\partial_{\mu}m_{\pm})^{\mathrm{d}}\Bigr)\Big|_{\mu\in\mathbb{S}^{1}},

where superscripts “o” and “d” denote off-diagonal and diagonal parts, respectively.
Then, for each
\mu\in\mathbb{S}^{1^{\prime}}
, the maps
f^{\mu}_{\pm}
are timelike minimal surfaces in
\mathrm{Nil}_{t}^{3}

and
N_{m}
is the normal Gauss map of
f^{\mu}_{\pm}
. In particular,
f^{\mu}_{-}|_{\mu=1}

is the original timelike minimal surface
f
up to a rigid motion and
f^{\mu}_{+}|_{\mu=1}
is the
dual timelike minimal surface
f^{\ast}
of
f
. Moreover, for each
\mu\in\mathbb{S}^{1^{\prime}}
,
f^{\mu}_{-}
and
f^{\mu}_{+}
are dual to each other.

Proof.

From
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
, it is clear that
f^{\mu}_{-}|_{\mu=1}
is a timelike minimal surface in
\operatorname{Nil}_{t}^{3}
.
We only show for
f^{\mu}_{+}|_{\mu=1}
, and both are dual to each other for each
\mu\in\mathbb{S}^{1^{\prime}}
. From a direct calculation, we have

\partial_{z}m_{+}=\operatorname{Ad}(F)\Bigl(-i^{\prime}\mu\partial_{\mu}U^{\mu}+\frac{i^{\prime}}{2}[U^{\mu},\sigma_{3}]\Bigr)=2\mu^{-1}Be^{-w/2}\operatorname{Ad}(F)\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Using the generating spinors and the support function
h
, this becomes

\partial_{z}m_{+}=\frac{16\mu^{-1}B}{h^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{2}(\mu)\overline{\psi_{1}(\mu)}&-\overline{\psi_{1}(\mu)^{2}}\\[6.0pt]
\psi_{2}(\mu)^{2}&-\psi_{2}(\mu)\overline{\psi_{1}(\mu)}\end{pmatrix}.

From the dual
generating spinors (
3.9
),
we have

(3.18)

\partial_{z}m_{+}=\varphi_{1}^{+}(\mu)\mathcal{E}_{1}+\varphi_{2}^{+}(\lambda)\mathcal{E}_{2}+i\,\varphi_{3}^{+}(\mu)\mathcal{E}_{3},

where

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}^{+}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\mu^{-1}\epsilon\,i^{\prime}\,\bigl((\overline{\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\mu)})^{2}+(\psi_{1}^{\ast}(\mu))^{2}\bigr),

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}^{+}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\mu^{-1}\epsilon\,\bigl((\overline{\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\mu)})^{2}-(\psi_{1}^{\ast}(\mu))^{2}\bigr),

\displaystyle\varphi_{3}^{+}(\mu)

\displaystyle=2\mu^{-1}\epsilon\,\psi_{1}^{\ast}(\mu)\overline{\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\mu)}.

A computation of
\partial_{z}(i\mu\partial_{\mu}m_{+})
combined with the diagonal part
analysis yields

(3.19)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\Bigl(-\tfrac{i^{\prime}}{2}\mu(\partial_{\mu}m_{+})\Bigr)^{d}

\displaystyle=\frac{i^{\prime}}{2}(\partial_{z}m_{+})^{d}+\frac{1}{2}[m_{+}-N_{m},\,\partial_{z}m_{+}]^{d}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\varphi_{3}^{+}(\mu)-\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{1}^{+}(\mu)\int\varphi_{2}^{+}(\mu)\,dz+\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{2}^{+}(\mu)\int\varphi_{1}^{+}(\mu)\,dz\Bigr)\mathcal{E}_{3}.

Combining (
3.18
) and (
3.19
) we obtain

\partial_{z}\widehat{f}^{\mu}_{+}=\varphi_{1}^{+}(\mu)\mathcal{E}_{1}+\varphi_{2}^{+}(\mu)\mathcal{E}_{2}+\Bigl(\varphi_{3}^{+}(\mu)-\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{1}^{+}(\mu)\int\varphi_{2}^{+}(\mu)\,dz+\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{2}^{+}(\mu)\int\varphi_{1}^{+}(\mu)\,dz\Bigr)\mathcal{E}_{3}.

Using the identification (
3.14
) and the left translation by
(f^{\mu}_{+})^{-1}
, we obtain

(f^{\mu}_{+})^{-1}\partial_{z}f^{\mu}_{+}=\varphi_{1}^{+}(\mu)\varepsilon_{1}+\varphi_{2}^{+}(\mu)\varepsilon_{2}+\varphi_{3}^{+}(\mu)\varepsilon_{3}.

It is clear that
\psi_{1}^{\ast}(\mu)
and
\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\mu)
are spinors for
f^{\mu}_{+}
for
each
\mu\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. In particular, the function

\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\psi_{2}^{*}\,\overline{\psi_{2}^{*}}-\psi_{1}^{*}\,\overline{\psi_{1}^{*}}\bigg)=e^{w^{\ast}/2}=\frac{4\sqrt{\epsilon\bar{\epsilon}B\bar{B}}}{h}

does not depend on
\mu
and so the mean curvature
H=0
(
i^{\prime}e^{w^{*}/2}\in i^{\prime}\mathbb{C}^{\prime}
, for each
\mu
). Moreover, the conformal factor of the induced metric of
f^{\lambda}_{+}
is given by

e^{u^{\ast}}=4(\psi_{1}^{*}\,\overline{\psi_{1}^{*}}+\psi_{2}^{*}\,\overline{\psi_{2}^{*}})^{2}=\frac{16\sqrt{\epsilon\bar{\epsilon}B\bar{B}}}{h^{2}}\,e^{u},

which is degenerate only at
B(z)\overline{B(z)}=0
. Thus,
f^{\mu}_{+}
defines a timelike minimal surface in

\mathrm{Nil}_{t}^{3}
for each
\mu\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
where
B(z)\overline{B(z)}\neq 0.
This completes the proof.
∎

Example:
Consider two hyperbolic paraboloids
