Support is Search
Alexander V. Gheorghiu

Abstract
. Sandqvist’s base-extension semantics for intuitionistic propositional logic defines a support relation parametrised by atomic bases, with validity identified as support in every base. Sandqvist’s completeness theorem answers the global question: which formulae are valid? This paper addresses the local question: given a fixed base, what does support in that base correspond to? We show that support in a fixed base coincides with proof-search in a second-order hereditary Harrop logic program, via an encoding of formulae as logic-programming goals. This encoding proceeds by reading the semantic clauses in continuation-passing style, revealing that the universal quantifiers over base extensions and atoms appearing in those clauses are not domain-ranging quantifiers over a completed totality, but eigenvariables governed by a standard freshness discipline. Base-extension semantics thereby admits a fully constructive and computationally transparent interpretation: support is proof-search. The result complements Sandqvist’s global theorem with a local correspondence, vindicates the anti-realist foundations of the framework on its own terms, and opens the way for implementing the semantics in modelling tasks.

Introduction

Schroeder-Heister dubbed the programme concerned with grounding the meaning of logical constants not in denotational or model-theoretic notions, but in inferential or proof-theoretic behaviour ‘proof-theoretic semantics’ (P-tS)
[
30
]
. The programme, developed in its modern form by Prawitz
[
22
,
23
]
, Dummett
[
3
,
1
,
2
]
, and Schroeder-Heister
[
27
,
28
,
30
]
, takes as its point of departure the anti-realist thesis that meaning must be publicly manifestable and therefore tied to what a speaker can in principle demonstrate, rather than to potentially verification-transcendent truth conditions. On this view, the meaning of a sentence is nothing over and above the capacity to recognise proofs of it.

A particularly well-known realisation of this programme is the
base-extension semantics
(B-eS) for intuitionistic propositional logic due to Sandqvist
[
26
]
. The semantics takes as its primitive notion a
base

\mathcal{B}
— a collection of rules governing
atomic
sentences. This has an intuitive notion of derivability; we write
\vdash_{\mathcal{B}}p
to denote that an atom
p
is derivable in
\mathcal{B}
. The meaning of the logical constants is given by an inductively defined relation called
support
with clauses for each of the connectives.

For example, the clause for implication (
\to
) is:

\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi\to\psi\qquad\text{iff}\qquad\text{for every $\mathcal{C}\sqsupseteq\mathcal{B}$, if $\Vdash_{\mathcal{C}}\varphi$, then $\Vdash_{\mathcal{C}}\psi$,}

where
\mathcal{C}\sqsupseteq\mathcal{B}
means that
\mathcal{C}
extends
\mathcal{B}
(i.e. contains at least all the rules of
\mathcal{B}
). While this may be reminiscent of Kripke semantics for intuitionistic logic
[
12
]
, the analogy can be misleading. This is most clear in two place.

First, taking the standard clause for disjunction,

\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi\lor\psi\qquad\text{iff}\qquad\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi\text{ or }\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\psi,

does not yield intuitionistic logic (see Piecha et al.
[
16
,
21
,
20
]
), but rather general inquisitive logic (see Stafford
[
31
]
). To get intuitionistic logic, Sandqvist
[
26
]
instead adopts the following clause:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi\lor\psi&\text{iff}&\text{for every $\mathcal{C}\sqsupseteq\mathcal{B}$ and every atom $p$,}\\[4.0pt]
&&\text{if }\varphi\Vdash_{\mathcal{C}}p\text{ and }\psi\Vdash_{\mathcal{C}}p\text{, then }\Vdash_{\mathcal{C}}p.\end{array}

Secondly, what logic the semantics gives depends on precisely what is meant by
base
. Sandqvist
[
26
]
proved that

\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi\text{ for all $\mathcal{B}$}\qquad\text{iff}\qquad\varphi\textbf{ is intuitionistically valid}

when
\mathcal{B}
are higher-level atomic systems. But Sandqvist
[
25
]
, Gheorghiu
[
9
]
, and Stafford et al.
[
17
]
have each shown that

\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi\text{ for all $\mathcal{B}$}\qquad\text{iff}\qquad\varphi\textbf{ is classically valid}

when
\mathcal{B}
are first-level atomic systems. This change of logic comes without a change in the definition of the logical constants.

Observe that in both cases, soundness and completeness concern
global
support. That is, they characterise the formulae supported in
every
base
\mathcal{B}
. In this paper, we consider the
local
question:
what does
\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi
correspond to for a
fixed
base
\mathcal{B}
?

The local question has received some attention previously. In particular, it is clear that it cannot be characterized by
export
:

\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi\qquad\text{is not equivalent to}\qquad\mathcal{B}^{\ast}\vdash\varphi,

where
\mathcal{B}^{\ast}
is a natural representation of a base as a set of formulae. A telling counterexample involves disjunction: by the disjunction property,
\mathcal{B}^{\ast}\vdash A\lor B
is equivalent to
\mathcal{B}^{\ast}\vdash A
or
\mathcal{B}^{\ast}\vdash B
, but
\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi\lor\psi
need not imply
\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi
or
\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\psi
.

The challenge for understanding
\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi
is that the definition of support involves several quantification over bases and atoms and meta-level implications, as seen in the example clauses above. Read in a realist manner, with the meta-level ranging over a completed totality and no constructive content passed from
\varphi
to
\psi
, the semantics seemingly betrays its anti-realist foundations. Schroeder-Heister
[
29
]
has indeed been critical of some of these aspects of the semantics saying that despite its difference from model-theoretic semantics, proof-theoretic semantics still adheres to the ‘dogmas’ of standard semantics.

But what alternative reading available? This paper provides an answer to these questions by giving a fully computational account of
\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi
. We show that it corresponds to proof-search in a logic programming language. That is, we define an operational judgment relation
\vdash_{O}
and associate to each formula
\varphi
a formula
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
such that the following holds:

\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi\qquad\text{iff}\qquad\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket,

The universal quantifiers in the support judgment are governed by an eigenvariable discipline in the operational semantics of
\vdash_{0}
. Accordingly, the meaning of the support judgment is given by a proof-search procedure.
Support is search.

Contribution.

This paper contributes to the understanding of base-extension semantics the following:

•

Meta-theoretic commitments.
The clauses of the support relation need not be regarded as ranging over completed infinite sets — a realist presupposition at odds with the anti-realist foundations of the programme — but are given a constructive interpretation internal to the proof-search framework, vindicating the coherence of the framework.

•

Computational content.
We give an explicit procedure — executing
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
as a logic-programming goal against
\mathcal{B}
— that finds a witnessing uniform derivation whenever
\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi
holds; support is not merely effectively verifiable in principle but searchable in practice.

•

Local soundness and completeness.
For any fixed base
\mathcal{B}
,

\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi\qquad\mbox{iff}\qquad\mathcal{B}\vdash_{\mathcal{O}}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket

— complementing Sandqvist’s global result with a local correspondence that characterises exactly what support in a given base.

Organization.

The paper is organised as follows. Section
2

develops the framework of atomic systems and hereditary Harrop programs,
including the second-order extension. Section
3
recalls
Sandqvist’s base-extension semantics and establishes the relevant

5

2.
Atomic Systems (Generalized)

Higher-level Atomic Systems

Atomic systems are the foundation of base-extension semantics. They are often
presented within the framework of Gentzen-style natural deduction
[
32
]
.
The notion was made explicit by Prawitz
[
22
,
23
]
.

Definition 2.1

(First-level Atomic System)
.

A first-level
atomic system

\mathcal{S}
is a system of production rules governing atomic
propositions — rules of the form

\frac{A_{1}\quad\cdots\quad A_{n}}{C}

where
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
and
C
are all atomic propositions.

Derivability in
\mathcal{S}
— denoted
\vdash_{\mathcal{S}}
—is defined compositionally (i.e., in the standard way but without substitutions of atoms).

This basic format has been generalised to admit rules that
discharge hypotheses
—
a feature already present in natural deduction for logical constants, most perspicuously
in implication introduction and disjunction elimination. In its full generality, Piecha and Schroeder-Heister
[
18
,
19
]
allow the premisses of
an atomic rule may themselves be finite sets of rules, and this process may be iterated
to yield rules of arbitrary finite level.

Definition 2.2

(Higher-Level Atomic System)
.

A
higher-level atomic system
is a set of atomic rules each of level
at most
n
.

An
atomic rule of level
0
is an axiom

\frac{}{~~B~~}

where
B
is an atomic proposition. An
atomic rule of level
1
is
a production rule of the form

\frac{A_{1}\quad\cdots\quad A_{n}}{B}

where
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
and
B
are atomic propositions. More generally,
an
atomic rule of level
n+1
, for
n\geq 1
, takes the form

B\lx@proof@logical@and A_{1}[\Sigma_{1}]\ldots A_{k}[\Sigma_{k}]

where
B
and each
A_{i}
are atomic propositions and each
\Sigma_{i}
is a
finite set of atomic rules of level at most
n
.

A higher-level atomic rule of the above form is read as:
if, for each
i
, the atom
A_{i}
is derivable from the rules in
\Sigma_{i}
,
then
B
may be concluded, discharging the rules of
\Sigma_{i}
.

Given an atomic system
\mathcal{S}
, the derivability relation

\vdash_{\mathcal{S}}
is defined inductively in the usual way.

(A Third-Level Rule)
.

Consider the atomic system
\mathcal{S}
with single level-

This

Suppose

This rule witnesses the required condition for the first rule to be applied. Thus we may derive

.

Higher-level atomic systems play an essential
role in delivering intuitionistic propositional
logic: restricting to first-level
production rules yields a semantics that characterises classical rather than
intuitionistic logic
[
25
]
. The presence of higher-level rules is
thus what makes the intuitionistic character of the semantics possible.

While they offer a systematic way of organizing atomic systems, we prefer a more computational reading of higher-level rules to deliver the central result of this paper. To this end, we employ a computational representation of them inspired by Hallnäs and Schroeder-Heister
[
10
,
11
]
. They observed that systems of clauses can be read as defining a natural deduction system. We reverse this reading: rules in a natural deduction system can be read as clauses.

Rules-as-Clauses

The clauses we use are defined by the
hereditary Harrop formulae as developed by Miller
et al.
[
14
,
13
]
. This is used as a general proof-theoretic foundation of logic programming.

Definition 2.4

(Definite Formulae, Goals, and Programs)
.

The propositional fragment of the hereditary Harrop language is generated by the
following grammar, in which
A
ranges over atomic propositions,
D
denotes a

definite
(program) formula, and
G
denotes a
goal
formula:

D\;::=\;A\;\mid\;G\supset A\;\mid\;D\cap D\qquad\qquad G\;::=\;A\;\mid\;D\supset G\;\mid\;G\cap G\;\mid\;G\cup G

A set of definite formulae is a
program

P
. A
query
is a
sequent
P\longrightarrow G
with
G
a goal.

The operational semantics of the logic programming language is a judgment
P\vdash_{O}G
expressing that the program
P
resolves the goal

G
. It is based on the idea that the logical structure of the goal should drive a proof-search proceedure, with the program consulted
only when an atomic goal is reached.

Definition 2.5

(Operational Semantics)
.

The
operational semantics relation
\vdash_{O}
is defined as follows:

•

Fact
:
P\vdash_{O}A
if
A\in[P]
.

•

:
P\vdash_{O}A
if there exists
G\supset A\in[P]

such that
P\vdash_{O}G
.

•

And

P\vdash_{O}G_{1}\cap G_{2}

P\vdash_{O}G_{1}
and

P\vdash_{O}G_{2}
.

•

Or
:
P\vdash_{O}G_{1}\cup G_{2}

P\vdash_{O}G_{1}
or

P\vdash_{O}G_{2}
.

•

Augment

P\vdash_{O}D\supset G

P\cup\{D\}\vdash_{O}G
.

where
[-]
is defined inductively by:
P\subseteq[P]

D_{1}\in[P]
and

.

Gheorghiu and Pym
[

]
observed that the hierarchy of atomic systems above corresponds

\mathcal{S}

Example 2.6

(Atomic Rules as Clauses)
.

The second-level rule

Using the rule amounts

Let

Observe that this essentially reverse that standard way of reading derivability in a base. Rather than moving deductively by iteratively applying rules, we move

Infinite Systems

Atomic systems are often infinite. Indeed, permitting infinite atomic systems is
crucial to the completeness of
intuitionistic propositional logic with respect to support. However, not every
infinite atomic system need be admitted. This is not merely a technical observation, but one important to the anti-realist motivations for the semantics. From an anti-realist
standpoint one cannot appeal to arbitrary infinite sets as primitive: to do so
would be to presuppose a completed infinity whose existence outruns any
manifestable warrant.

Fortunately, one can restrict to using only infinite systems that are schematic in some way. For instance, one may wish to
include every instance of a rule of the form

(A\supset X)\cap(B\supset X)\supset X,

as
X
ranges over atoms.

So, what are the permissible schemas? We require only arbitrary substitutions of rules such as the above example. To express this, we introducing an
atomic

second-order quantifier
\forall
binding atomic variables
X
:

G\;::=\;\ldots\;\mid\;\forall X\,G\qquad\qquad D\;::=\;\ldots\;\mid\;\forall X\,D

The above schema then becomes the single clause

\forall X\bigl((A\supset X)\land(B\supset X)\supset X\bigr).

In so doing, we have moved from requiring infinite systems/programs to finite ones.

This use of the second-order quantifier is natural in the context of logic programming. It is already present in Miller
et al.
[
13
]
without the restriction to atoms.

Definition 2.7

(Second-Order Operational Rules)
.

The operational rules governing the second-order quantifier are:

•

Generic
:
P\vdash_{O}\forall X\,G
if
P\vdash_{O}G[A/X]
for
an atom
A
fresh in
P
and
G
.

•

Instance
:
P\vdash_{O}A
if there is
\forall X\,D\in P
such
that
P,D[B/X]\vdash_{O}A
for an atom
B\neq A
fresh in
P
.

This is the standard eigenvariable discipline, applied at the level of predicate
position.

Observe that the use of a fresh variable is crucial: if instead one required the condition to be
fulfilled for
every
atomic instantiation, the same infinitary problem would
recur. We therefore adopt the following standing assumption throughout.

We conclude by recording two standard properties, elementary to prove by induction,
which we shall use throughout.

Lemma 2.8

(Cut)
.

If
P\vdash_{O}D
and
Q,D\vdash_{O}G
, then
Q,P\vdash_{O}G
.

We write
\mathcal{Q}\sqsupseteq\mathcal{P}
to say that
\mathcal{Q}
is a program extending

\mathcal{P}
.

Lemma 2.9

(Monotonicity)
.

If
P\vdash_{O}G
and
P\sqsupseteq P^{\prime}
, then
P^{\prime}\vdash_{O}G
.

3.
Base-extension Semantics

We fix an infinite set
\mathrm{At}
of
atomic sentences
ranged over by
p,q,r,\ldots
. The sense in which the language is infinite is that of a
potential
infinity:
at any stage of a derivation, only finitely many witnesses have been introduced, but
no bound is placed on how many may be introduced in subsequent steps. This is
precisely the kind of infinity admissible from an anti-realist standpoint
— it does not presuppose any completed totality, but only the open-ended
availability of new resources as derivation proceeds.

The language of
IPL
is given by the grammar

\varphi,\psi,\chi\;::=\;A\in\mathrm{At}\;\mid\;\bot\;\mid\;\varphi\land\psi\;\mid\;\varphi\lor\psi\;\mid\;\varphi\to\psi.

We set
\lnot\varphi\coloneqq\varphi\to\bot
. The notation
\Gamma\vdash_{O}\varphi

denotes intuitionistic derivability of
\varphi
from
\Gamma
.

Base-extension semantics is grounded in atomic systems.
In Section
2
we generalized the concept of atomic systems using logic programming. This is the formalism with which we shall continue forward.

A

The base-extensions semantics is carried by a judgment relation called

\displaystyle\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi\text{ for all $\mathcal{B}$}\quad

This captures support locally, but at the cost of flattening
\varphi
entirely
— all logical structure is absorbed into the base

It is worth noting that Sandqvist’s construction of

has an
independent precursor in the work of Mints
[
15
]

translating formulae into clausal form. Mints’ goals were entirely

]
yet his clausal transformation and Sandqvist’s
base construction coincide up to renaming for any given formula. This
convergence, arrived at from opposite directions, suggests that the encoding
is a natural one.

On the one hand, this generalization of the flattening system indeed captures support locally. On the other, it trivializes the problem by removing all structure — it flattens
\varphi
. This paper addresses the local question in a way that makes the relevant structures explicit and direct. We encode formulae directly as logic-programming goals, revealing that the universal quantifiers in the semantic clauses are not domain-ranging but eigenvariable-governed.

4.
Soundness and Completeness (Generalized)

The clause for disjunction in Figure
1
has a distinctive character. This pattern is an instance of what functional programmers call
continuation-passing style
(CPS): rather than returning a result directly, a CPS function takes an extra argument — an explicit
continuation
— and “returns” by passing its computed result to that continuation. The definition of
\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi\lor\psi
is a CPS definition of the connective, with the two supports
\varphi\Vdash_{\mathcal{C}}p
and
\psi\Vdash_{\mathcal{C}}p
playing the role of continuations.

It is perhaps less clear that the other connectives have the same style, but Gheorghiu et al.
[
4
]
showed that conjunction can be understood in CPS:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi\land\psi&\mbox{iff}&\qquad\text{for any $\mathcal{C}\sqsupseteq\mathcal{B}$ and $p\in\textbf{At}$}\\
&&\qquad\text{if $\varphi,\psi\Vdash_{\mathcal{C}}p$, then $\varphi,\psi\Vdash_{\mathcal{C}}p$}\end{array}

This observation is useful as CPS is easy to express in logic programming. Therefore, we can systematically turn formulae into clauses using their definitions.

Definition 4.1

(Encoding)
.

To each formula
\varphi
, define a clause
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
as follows:

\displaystyle\llbracket p\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\;p

\displaystyle\llbracket\bot\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\;\forall X(X)

\displaystyle\llbracket\varphi\to\psi\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\;\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\supset\llbracket\psi\rrbracket

\displaystyle\llbracket\varphi\land\psi\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\;\forall X\,\big((\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\supset\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\supset X)\supset X\big)

\displaystyle\llbracket\varphi\lor\psi\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\;\forall X\,\big((\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\supset X)\supset(\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\supset X)\supset X\big)

Under the operational semantics for logic programming above, the
predicate variable
X
is always instantiated with atoms. Accordingly, the apparent
second-order quantification in the semantics for
\land
,
\lor
, and
\bot

collapses to ordinary execution.

The main result of this paper is that support is not merely
analogous
to logic-program
execution — it
is
logic-program execution:

\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{B}}\varphi\qquad\text{iff}\qquad\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket.

The proof of this involves moving smoothly moving formulae and their encodings in atomic systems. To this end, we require some technical results.

Definition 4.2

(Neutral Formula)
.

A logic programming formula
F
is
neutral
if it is both a definite clause and a goal formula.

There are many examples: all atoms are neutral. Indeed, by inspection on the definition of definite and goal formula, all
\cup
-free formulae are neutral. This observation enables the following:

Lemma 4.3
.

For any intuitionistic formula
\varphi
, its encoding
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
is neutral.

The useful property of neutral formulae is that it allows us to generalized basic properties of the operational provability relation:

Lemma 4.4
.

If
F
is a neutral formula, then
P,F\vdash_{O}F
.

Proof.

By induction on the structure of
F
.

Case
F=A
(atom).

Since
A\in[P,A]
, the result
P,A\vdash_{O}A
follows immediately by

Fact
.

Case
F=F_{1}\supset F_{2}
.

By
Augment
, it suffices to show
P,F_{1}\supset F_{2},F_{1}\vdash_{O}F_{2}
.
Since
(F_{1}\supset F_{2})\in[P,F_{1}\supset F_{2},G]
, by
Backchain
it
suffices to show
P,F_{1}\supset F_{2},F_{1}\vdash_{O}F_{1}
. Since
F
is neutral,
F_{1}

is a neutral formula of strictly smaller size, so the induction hypothesis
gives
P,F_{1}\supset F_{2},F_{1}\vdash_{O}F_{2}
, as required.

Case
F=F_{1}\cap F_{2}
.

Since
F
is neutral,
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are neutral. By
And
, it
suffices to show
P,F_{1}\cap F_{2}\vdash_{O}F_{i}
for
i=1,2
. By
definition of
[-]
,
F_{1}\cap F_{2}\in[P,F_{1}\cap F_{2}]
implies

F_{i}\in[P,F_{1}\cap F_{2}]
for each
i
. Hence
P,F_{1}\cap F_{2},F_{i}\vdash_{O}F_{i}
by the induction hypothesis (since
F_{i}
is neutral and
strictly smaller), and the result follows by monotonicity
(Lemma
2.9
).

Case
F=\forall X\,F^{\prime}
.

By
Generic
, taking a fresh atom
A
, it suffices to show

P,\forall X\,F^{\prime}\vdash_{O}F^{\prime}[A/X]
. Since
F
is neutral,
F^{\prime}[A/X]
is
a neutral formula of strictly smaller size (it is the result of substituting
an atom for a predicate variable in
F^{\prime}
, which is
\cup
-free). In
particular,
F^{\prime}[A/X]
is a definite clause. By
Augment
it
therefore suffices to show

P,\,\forall X\,F^{\prime},\,F^{\prime}[A/X]\;\vdash_{O}\;F^{\prime}[A/X],

which is exactly the induction hypothesis applied to the neutral formula

F^{\prime}[A/X]
.
∎

Lemma 4.5

(Substitution)
.

If
P\vdash_{O}G
and
X\notin\mathrm{FV}(P)
, then
P\vdash_{O}G[F/X]
for
any neutral clause
F
.

Proof.

By induction on the derivation of
P\vdash_{O}G
.

Case
Fact
:
G=A\in[P]
.

Since
X\notin\mathrm{FV}(P)
, we have
A[F/X]=A
and
[P]=[P][F/X]
,
so
A\in[P]
still holds. Hence
P\vdash_{O}A=A[F/X]
by
Fact
.

Case
Backchain
:
G=A
, with
G^{\prime}\supset A\in[P]

and
P\vdash_{O}G^{\prime}
.

Since
X\notin\mathrm{FV}(P)
, substitution does not affect
P
, so

G^{\prime}\supset A\in[P]
still holds after substitution, and
A[F/X]=A
.
By the induction hypothesis,
P\vdash_{O}G^{\prime}[F/X]
. By
Backchain
,

P\vdash_{O}A
.

Case
And
:
G=G_{1}\cap G_{2}
, with
P\vdash_{O}G_{i}

for
i=1,2
.

By the induction hypothesis,
P\vdash_{O}G_{i}[F/X]
for each
i
. Since

(G_{1}\cap G_{2})[F/X]=G_{1}[F/X]\cap G_{2}[F/X]
, the result follows by

And
.

Case
Or
:
G=G_{1}\cup G_{2}
, with
P\vdash_{O}G_{i}

for some
i
.

By the induction hypothesis,
P\vdash_{O}G_{i}[F/X]
. By
Or
,

P\vdash_{O}G_{1}[F/X]\cup G_{2}[F/X]=(G_{1}\cup G_{2})[F/X]
.

Case
Augment
:
G=D\supset G^{\prime}
, with

P,D\vdash_{O}G^{\prime}
.

By the induction hypothesis applied with
P,D
in place of
P
— noting
that
X\notin\mathrm{FV}(P)
and that any free occurrence of
X
in
D

is replaced — we obtain
P,D\vdash_{O}G^{\prime}[F/X]
. By
Augment
,

P\vdash_{O}D\supset G^{\prime}[F/X]=(D\supset G^{\prime})[F/X]
.

Case
Generic
:
G=\forall Y\,G^{\prime}
, with

P\vdash_{O}G^{\prime}[A/Y]
for a fresh atom
A
.

Renaming if necessary, assume
Y\neq X
. By the induction hypothesis,

P\vdash_{O}G^{\prime}[A/Y][F/X]
. Since
Y\neq X
and
A
is atomic (hence

A[F/X]=A
), we have
G^{\prime}[A/Y][F/X]=G^{\prime}[F/X][A/Y]
. By
Generic
,

P\vdash_{O}\forall Y\,G^{\prime}[F/X]=(\forall Y\,G^{\prime})[F/X]
.

Case
Instance
:
G=A
, with
\forall Y\,D\in P

and
P,D[B/Y]\vdash_{O}A
for a fresh atom
B\neq A
.

Since
X\notin\mathrm{FV}(P)
, the clause
\forall Y\,D
is unaffected,
and
A[F/X]=A
. By the induction hypothesis,
P,D[B/Y]\vdash_{O}A
.
By
Instance
,
P\vdash_{O}A
.

This completes the induction.
∎

These background results suffice to prove soundness and completeness.

Theorem 4.6

(Soundness and Completeness)
.

For any base
\mathcal{B}
and formula
\varphi
,

\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{B}}\varphi\qquad\text{iff}\qquad\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket.

Proof.

By simultaneous induction on the structure of
\varphi
, establishing both
directions together. Monotonicity of support under base extension
(Proposition
3.3
) and monotonicity of
\vdash_{O}
under program
extension (Lemma
2.9
) are used freely throughout.

Case
\varphi=p
:
Immediate from clause
(At)
of Figure
1
and the
definition
\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}p\;\coloneqq\;\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}p
.

Case
\varphi=\bot
.

(\Rightarrow)
:

Suppose
\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{B}}\bot
. By clause (
\bot
),

\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{B}}p
for every
p\in\mathrm{At}
, hence
\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}p

for every atom
p
. By
Generic
,
taking a fresh atom
x
, we have
\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}x
, so

\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}\forall x(x)=\llbracket\bot\rrbracket
.

(\Leftarrow)
:

Suppose
\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}\forall X(X)
. By

Generic
,
\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}p
for any atom
p
. Hence

\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{B}}p
for any
p
. Whence,

\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{B}}\bot
by clause (
\bot
).

Case
\varphi=\varphi_{1}\to\varphi_{2}
:

(\Rightarrow)
:

Suppose
\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{B}}(\varphi_{1}\to\varphi_{2})
. By
clause (
\to
),
\varphi_{1}\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{B}}\varphi_{2}
— that is, for every

\mathcal{C}\sqsupseteq\mathcal{B}
, if
\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{C}}\varphi_{1}
then

\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{C}}\varphi_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{B},\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket
.
By the
induction hypothesis
,
\mathcal{B}\cup\{\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket\}\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{2}\rrbracket
.
By
Augment
,
\mathcal{B}\cup\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket\supset\llbracket\varphi_{2}\rrbracket
. That is,
\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{1}\supset\varphi_{2}\rrbracket
, as required.

(\Leftarrow)
:

Suppose
\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{1}\to\varphi_{2}\rrbracket
. That is,
\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket\supset\llbracket\varphi_{2}\rrbracket
.
Let
\mathcal{C}\sqsupseteq\mathcal{B}
with
\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{C}}\varphi_{1}
. By the induction
hypothesis,
\mathcal{C}\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket
. By cut (Lemma
2.8
) on the hypothesis,

\mathcal{C}\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{2}\rrbracket
. By the induction
hypothesis,
\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{C}}\varphi_{2}
. Since
\mathcal{C}\sqsupseteq\mathcal{B}
was
arbitrary,
\varphi_{1}\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{B}}\varphi_{2}
— that is,
\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{B}}(\varphi_{1}\to\varphi_{2})
.

Case
\varphi=\varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{2}
:

(\Rightarrow)
:

Suppose
\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{B}}(\varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{2})
. By
clause (
\land
),
\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{B}}\varphi_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Hence
\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket
by the induction hypothesis. We must show

\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}\forall X\,\bigl((\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket\supset\llbracket\varphi_{2}\rrbracket\supset X)\supset X\bigr).

By
Generic
, take a fresh atom
A
and let
D\coloneqq\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket\supset\llbracket\varphi_{2}\rrbracket\supset A
. By
Augment
it
suffices to show
\mathcal{B},D\vdash_{O}A
. By
Backchain
on
D
it
suffices to show
\mathcal{B},D\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket
for
i=1,2
, which
follows from
\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket
.

(\Leftarrow)
:

Suppose
\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{2}\rrbracket
. By
Generic
,
\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}(\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket\supset\llbracket\psi_{2}\rrbracket\supset X)\supset X
for a fresh

. By substitution (Lemma
4.5
),
\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}(\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket\supset\llbracket\psi_{2}\rrbracket\supset\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket)\supset\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket
for

; thus,
\mathcal{B},(\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket\supset\llbracket\psi_{2}\rrbracket\supset\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket)\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket
for
i=1,2
.
Simultaneously, By Lemma
4.4
),
\mathcal{B},\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket,\llbracket\varphi_{2}\rrbracket\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket
for
i=1,2
. Hence, by
Argument

\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket\supset\llbracket\varphi_{2}\rrbracket\supset\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket
for
i=1,2
. The desired result follows from cut (Lemma
2.8
).

Case
\varphi=\varphi_{1}\lor\varphi_{2}
:

(\Rightarrow)
:

Suppose
\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{B}}(\varphi_{1}\lor\varphi_{2})
. We must
show

\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}\forall X\,\bigl((\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket\supset X)\supset(\llbracket\varphi_{2}\rrbracket\supset X)\supset X\bigr).

By
Generic
, take a fresh atom
A
and let
D_{i}\coloneqq\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket\supset A
. By

it suffices to show

\mathcal{B},D_{1},D_{2}\vdash_{O}A
. Set
\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\mathcal{B}\cup\{D_{1},D_{2}\}
. For each

, we claim
\varphi_{i}\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{C}}A

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\sqsupseteq\mathcal{C}
with
\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}\varphi_{i}
; by the induction

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket
; since

,

gives
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\vdash_{O}A
, hence

\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}A
by definition. So
\varphi_{i}\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{C}}A
for
i=1,2
. The hypothesis thus
yields

\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{C}}A
; that is,
\mathcal{C}\vdash_{O}A
by

(\Leftarrow)
:

Suppose
\mathcal{B}\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{1}\lor\varphi_{2}\rrbracket
. Let

\mathcal{C}\sqsupseteq\mathcal{B}
and

with
\varphi_{i}\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{C}}p
for

. By

to lift to

, we

with

and

and backchaining, it suffices to show
\mathcal{C}\vdash_{O}\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket
for each

, which is exactly the induction

\varphi_{i}\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{C}}p

\mathcal{C}\vdash_{O}p
, which is
\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{C}}p
by definition. Since
\mathcal{C}

and

were arbitrary,
\Vdash_{\!\mathcal{B}}(\varphi_{1}\lor\varphi_{2})
by

\lor
).

∎

5.
Discussion

The central result of this paper — Theorem
4.6
— identifies
support in a fixed base with uniform proof-search in a second-order hereditary
Harrop logic program. This resolves the local question left open by Sandqvist’s
global completeness theorem: given a base
\mathcal{B}
, the judgment

\Vdash_{\mathcal{B}}\varphi
corresponds to a uniform proof in a fragment of
intuitionistic logic with second-order quantifiers over atoms. The philosophical
payoff is immediate. The universal quantifiers over base extensions and atoms in
the semantic clauses do not range over a completed totality of inferential
contexts — a realist presupposition at odds with the anti-realist foundations
of the programme — but receive a constructive interpretation internal to the
proof-search framework. The coherence of base-extension semantics is thereby
vindicated on its own terms: the semantics is not merely anti-realist in
aspiration but in execution.

The result depends essentially on the presence of higher-level rules in the
atomic systems, and this dependence is itself illuminating. Sandqvist
[
25
]

showed that restricting to first-level production rules yields a semantics
characterising classical rather than intuitionistic logic. From the perspective
of the present paper, this corresponds precisely to replacing hereditary Harrop
formulae with Horn clauses — that is, to a logic-programming language without
hypothetical goals. The loss of higher-level rules forecloses the
continuation-passing encoding that drives the correspondence, and no obvious
repair is available. This is significant because the classical case is in many
respects the more pressing one: a central ambition of Dummett’s programme is to
justify classical logic on anti-realist grounds without prior commitment to
bivalence, and Sandqvist
[
24
,
25
]

explicitly situates his semantics within that ambition. A computationally
transparent, search-based account of support in the classical setting remains
missing, and we leave its development as an open problem.

Beyond the classical case, the approach suggests a broader research programme.
Base-extension semantics has been developed for a range of
logics
[
9
,
4
,
5
]
, and the semantic clauses in each case follow the
same pattern: the meaning of a connective is given by its elimination behaviour,
encoded as a universal quantification over base extensions. Wherever this pattern
holds, and wherever an appropriate analogue of higher-level atomic systems is
available, the present result suggests that support should again reduce to
proof-search in a suitable logic-programming language. Establishing this
systematically — identifying the right class of programs, the right encoding,
and the right notion of uniform proof for each logic — would place
proof-theoretic semantics on a uniform computational foundation. We leave this
investigation to future work.
