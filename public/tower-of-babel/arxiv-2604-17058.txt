Kramers-Kronig Relations and Causality in Non-Markovian Open Quantum Dynamics: Kernel, State, and Effective Kernel
Kejun Liu

Abstract
Kramers-Kronig (KK) relations have not, to the best of our knowledge, been systematically formulated for the central object of non-Markovian open quantum dynamics: the Nakajima-Zwanzig (NZ) memory kernel. We organize the analysis around three distinct objects – the memory kernel 𝒦 ~ ​ ( z ) \tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z) , the reduced-state Laplace transform σ ~ ​ ( z ) \tilde{\sigma}(z) , and the force-fit effective kernel 𝒦 ~ eff ​ ( z ) \tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{eff}}(z) – each with different sensitivity to initial system-bath correlations. Under an explicit real-axis spectral-representation hypothesis for the projected generator, with a coupling-weighted spectral density in L 1 ∩ L p L^{1}\cap L^{p} , 𝒦 ~ ​ ( z ) \tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z) belongs to the vector-valued Hardy space H + p ​ ( ℬ ) H^{p}_{+}(\mathcal{B}) and obeys the corresponding KK or subtracted KK relations. Three consequences follow: a CPTP-Hardy consistency criterion (uncancelled upper-half-plane kernel poles imply dynamics incompatible with uniformly bounded CPTP reduction), a passivity-analyticity compatibility statement via the Herglotz-Nevanlinna class for passive bosonic baths, and a finite-truncation Carleman diagnostic for Padé reconstructions. The reduced-state transform σ ~ ​ ( z ) \tilde{\sigma}(z) is analytic in the upper half-plane for any initial state, factorized or correlated; microscopic unitarity forces ‖ σ ⁡ ( t ) ‖ op ≤ 1 \|\sigma(t)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1 . In scalar channels, the force-fit effective kernel 𝒦 ~ eff ​ ( z ) = 𝒦 ~ ​ ( z ) + ℐ ~ ​ ( z ) ​ σ ~ ​ ( z ) − 1 \tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{eff}}(z)=\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)+\tilde{\mathcal{I}}(z)\tilde{\sigma}(z)^{-1} can develop upper-half-plane poles at simple zeros of σ ~ ​ ( z ) \tilde{\sigma}(z) . Passive bosonic baths occupy a robust regime where these zeros stay on the real axis, while near-resonant systems can enter a fragile regime in which coherence-channel zeros migrate into the upper half-plane – an intrinsic property of the channel symmetry, existing already for factorized initial states, with initial correlations controlling only whether the inhomogeneous numerator fails to cancel the pole. We verify the bath-correlator KK at Born order (a proxy check), the robust-regime self-consistency for the independent boson model, and – in the Jaynes-Cummings model, whose projected generator has real spectrum – the full operator-valued KK relation for the extracted 4 × 4 4\times 4 NZ memory kernel. The measured integrated relative L 2 L_{2} residual of 3.8 % 3.8\% sits at or below the circular FFT-Hilbert pipeline’s calibrated noise floor ( ≈ 5 % {\approx}5\% at N t = 4000 N_{t}{=}4000 on a known-analytic test function), so the data are consistent with exact matrix-valued KK within the protocol’s accuracy and do not certify a physical violation. In the Jaynes-Cummings model, a scalar force-fit extraction of the coherence-channel effective kernel gives a slight increase of the KK residual under initial correlation (ratio ≈ 1.17 \approx 1.17 at Laplace shift ε = 0.3 \varepsilon=0.3 , rising where finite-truncation leakage dominates) – a qualitative finite-truncation diagnostic rather than a quantitative prediction.



I
Introduction

Apparent acausality in reduced open-quantum-system dynamics does not, in general, signal a breakdown of physical causality.
When a modeller discards the inhomogeneous initial-correlation term and force-fits a homogeneous Volterra equation to correlated initial data, the resulting
effective
memory kernel can develop upper-half-plane poles.
In scalar channels, the pole mechanism is simple: an uncancelled simple zero of the reduced-state Laplace transform
\tilde{\sigma}(z)
becomes a pole of
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{eff}}(z)
through the quotient term
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}(z)\tilde{\sigma}(z)^{-1}
.
The reduced-state transform itself is never acausal, a consequence of microscopic unitarity:
\|\sigma(t)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
forces
\tilde{\sigma}(z)
to be analytic in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
for
any
initial state, factorized or correlated.
The exact memory kernel is also well-behaved when the projected dynamics admits a real-axis spectral representation with an
L^{p}
coupling-weighted density; in that case it belongs to the vector-valued Hardy space
H^{p}_{+}(\mathcal{B})
and obeys a rigorous Kramers-Kronig (KK) relation.
Only the force-fit kernel
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{eff}}(z)=\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)+\tilde{\mathcal{I}}(z)\tilde{\sigma}(z)^{-1}
can fail KK, and in scalar channels it does so at frequencies determined by uncancelled UHP zeros of
\tilde{\sigma}(z)
.
Recognising these three distinct analytic objects — the kernel, the state, and the force-fit effective kernel — localises the geometric origin of “acausality” in the complex plane and suggests a possible frequency-domain residual in spectroscopic reconstructions.

This mechanism is the open-quantum-system analogue of the coarse-graining constructions of Gavassino and collaborators for relativistic hydrodynamics and classical field theory

Mapping the problem onto the operator-valued Hardy space

II
Preliminaries

II.1
Nakajima-Zwanzig projection

We consider a composite Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{s}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{b}
with Hamiltonian
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{s}+\hat{H}_{b}+\hat{H}_{sb}
and total Liouvillian
\mathcal{L}\,\cdot\equiv[\hat{H},\,\cdot\,]
.
We note that
\mathcal{L}
is self-adjoint in the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product
\langle\!\langle A,B\rangle\!\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}[A^{\dagger}B]
: using cyclicity of trace,
\langle\!\langle\mathcal{L}A,B\rangle\!\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}[(HA{-}AH)^{\dagger}B]=\mathrm{Tr}[A^{\dagger}HB]-\mathrm{Tr}[A^{\dagger}BH]=\langle\!\langle A,\mathcal{L}B\rangle\!\rangle
.
This statement concerns
\mathcal{L}
itself.
The standard NZ projection used below is generally not an orthogonal Hilbert-Schmidt projection, and therefore
Q\mathcal{L}Q
is not automatically self-adjoint.
Whenever a real-axis spectral representation of
Q\mathcal{L}Q
is used, we state it explicitly as a spectral hypothesis.
We use
\mathcal{L}=[H,\cdot]
as a frequency Liouvillian for spectral statements; in Volterra equations the conventional factors of
-i
from Schrödinger-picture evolution are absorbed into the displayed dynamical generators and kernels.
The explicit Laplace convention
\tilde{f}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{izt}f(t)\,dt
fixes the signs in the Cauchy representations below.
Equivalently, one may start from the physical generator
\mathfrak{L}=-i\mathcal{L}
and translate back to the present convention by the corresponding factors of
-i
; no Hardy or KK statement depends on this bookkeeping choice.

Remark 1

(On the self-adjointness of
\mathcal{L}
)
.

The Liouvillian
\mathcal{L}=[\hat{H},\,\cdot\,]
is frequently described as “anti-Hermitian” or “skew-adjoint”; this is correct in the
\mathrm{Tr}[\rho\,\cdot\,]
pairing (which is a bilinear form, not an inner product), but not in the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product
\langle\!\langle A,B\rangle\!\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}[A^{\dagger}B]
we use here.
A direct computation with
H=\sigma_{z}
,
A=\sigma_{x}
,
B=\sigma_{y}
gives
\langle\!\langle A,\mathcal{L}B\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle\mathcal{L}A,B\rangle\!\rangle=-4i
, confirming
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{L}
.
For an orthogonal projection
Q
this would imply
Q\mathcal{L}Q
is self-adjoint on the range of
Q
.
For the physical partial-trace projection
P\rho=\mathrm{Tr}_{b}[\rho]\otimes\rho_{b}^{\mathrm{ref}}
, however,
P^{\dagger}=P
holds only in special cases such as
\rho_{b}^{\mathrm{ref}}\propto I_{b}
or after changing to a suitable weighted Mori inner product.
Thus the real-axis spectral measures used below should be read as an additional assumption on the chosen projection/inner product pair, not as a consequence of the standard NZ projection alone.

The projection superoperator
P\rho=\mathrm{Tr}_{b}[\rho]\otimes\rho_{b}^{\mathrm{ref}}
maps onto the relevant (system) sector; its complement is
Q=\mathbbm{1}-P
, satisfying
P^{2}=P
,
Q^{2}=Q
, and
PQ=0

31
;
41
;
38
.

With this convention, the exact reduced dynamics is represented by the GQME:

\frac{d}{dt}P\rho(t)=P\mathcal{L}P\rho(t)+\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\,\mathcal{K}(\tau)P\rho(t-\tau)+\mathcal{I}(t),

(1)

with memory kernel
\mathcal{K}(t)=P\mathcal{L}Q\,e^{iQ\mathcal{L}Qt}\,Q\mathcal{L}P
and inhomogeneous (initial-correlation) term
\mathcal{I}(t)=P\mathcal{L}Q\,e^{iQ\mathcal{L}Qt}\,Q\rho(0)
.
For factorized initial states
\rho(0)=\sigma(0)\otimes\rho_{b}^{\mathrm{eq}}
, the condition
Q\rho(0)=0
implies
\mathcal{I}(t)=0
and the GQME reduces to a homogeneous Volterra equation.

The tensorial MKCT extension
26
writes the same structure for the basis-correlation matrix
\bm{\mathcal{C}}(t)
:

\frac{d}{dt}\bm{\mathcal{C}}(t)=\bm{\Omega}_{1}\bm{\mathcal{C}}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\,\bm{\mathcal{K}}(\tau)\bm{\mathcal{C}}(t-\tau),

(2)

with moments
\bm{\Omega}_{n}=[((i\mathcal{L})^{n}\hat{\phi}_{i},\hat{\phi}_{j})]_{ij}
encoding the short-time Taylor coefficients.

II.2
Hardy spaces and the Nevanlinna class

For
0<p\leq\infty
, the Hardy space
H^{p}_{+}(\mathbb{H}_{+})
on the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}_{+}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Im}(z)>0\}
consists of analytic functions
F:\mathbb{H}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying

\|F\|_{H^{p}_{+}}\equiv\sup_{y>0}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|F(x+iy)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

(3)

For
p=\infty
the usual modification is understood, with the essential supremum replacing the
L^{p}
integral.
Functions in
H^{1}_{+}
have non-tangential boundary values a.e. on
\mathbb{R}
and satisfy the Cauchy integral formula on semicircular contours in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
.
The vector-valued Hardy space
H^{p}_{+}(\mathcal{B})
replaces
|F|
by the operator norm
\|F\|_{\mathrm{op}}
throughout; the Banach space
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{HS}}(\mathcal{H}_{s}))
is that of bounded superoperators on the system’s Hilbert-Schmidt Liouville space, equipped with the operator norm.
For finite-dimensional
\mathcal{H}_{s}
, the operator norm and the Hilbert-Schmidt norm are equivalent up to a dimension-dependent constant; all
H^{p}_{+}
statements are equivalent between the two choices in that case
23
;
8
.
Boundary values and principal-value integrals of
\mathcal{B}
-valued functions are understood in the Bochner sense
14
; by polarization, equivalent statements hold against arbitrary pairs of Hilbert-Schmidt test vectors.

The Herglotz-Nevanlinna class
\mathcal{N}
consists of functions
f:\mathbb{H}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
analytic in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
with
\operatorname{Im}[f(z)]\geq 0
.
Every such function admits the
Nevanlinna integral representation

18
:

f(z)=\alpha z+\beta+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{\lambda-z}-\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda^{2}}\right]d\mu(\lambda),

(4)

with
\alpha\geq 0
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\mu
a positive Borel measure satisfying
\int d\mu(\lambda)/(1+\lambda^{2})<\infty
.
Functions with
\operatorname{Im}[f(z)]\leq 0
(the
anti-Herglotz
class,
-\mathcal{N}
) are obtained by negating Eq. (
4
): the measure changes sign and
\alpha\leq 0
.
As shown in Theorem
7
, passive bosonic baths place
-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}
(not
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}
itself) in
\mathcal{N}
, so it is
-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}
that admits the representation as written.

III
Causality Manufacturing by Projection

Theorem 1

(Causality Transfer)
.

Assume the initial state is factorized,
\rho(0)=\sigma(0)\otimes\rho_{b}^{\mathrm{eq}}
.
Then the memory kernel satisfies
\operatorname{supp}\mathcal{K}\subseteq[0,\infty)
.
If, in addition, the projected generator admits a real-axis coupling-weighted spectral representation

\mathcal{K}(t)=-i\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\lambda t}w(\lambda)\,d\lambda,\qquad w\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{B}),

(5)

then the kernel decays:
\|\mathcal{K}(t)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.
Substituting Eq. (
5
) into the Laplace transform
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{izt}\mathcal{K}(t)\,dt
gives the Cauchy representation of Eq. (
6
) directly:
for
\operatorname{Im}z>0
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i(z-\lambda)t}\,dt=\frac{i}{z-\lambda},\qquad\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{w(\lambda)}{z-\lambda}\,d\lambda.

The
-i
in Eq. (
5
) and the
+i
from the Laplace integral cancel; the Cauchy representation follows without any redefinition of
w
.

Proof.

For factorized states,
Q\rho(0)=0
, so
\mathcal{I}(t)=0
and the GQME is a homogeneous Volterra equation with integration over
[0,t]
, enforcing causal support.

The decay of
\mathcal{K}(t)
is not a consequence of the norm of
e^{iQ\mathcal{L}Qt}
.
It follows from the assumed absolutely continuous spectral representation.
By the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma,
\|\mathcal{K}(t)\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|\int e^{-i\lambda t}w(\lambda)\,d\lambda\|_{\mathrm{op}}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
(the unimodular prefactor
-i
does not affect the norm).
∎

Remark 2

(Decay rate depends on analytic structure, not a spectral gap)
.

A compact-support or gap condition on
w(\lambda)
is insufficient for exponential decay of
\mathcal{K}(t)
: a support condition controls only polynomial decay.
Exponential decay
\|\mathcal{K}(t)\|\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}
requires
w(\lambda)
to admit an analytic continuation into a strip
|\operatorname{Im}\lambda|<\gamma
of the complex plane, so that the contour of the Fourier integral can be deformed.
This analytic-strip condition is satisfied by rational baths such as Drude-Lorentz (whose bath correlation has a finite set of Matsubara-like poles off the real axis), but not automatically by a generic spectral density with a gap on the real line.

Physical picture.

The bath correlator
C_{\mathrm{bath}}(t)=\langle B(t)B(0)\rangle_{\mathrm{eq}}
is non-causal: it is nonzero for both
t>0
and
t<0
, related by the KMS condition
C_{\mathrm{bath}}(t-i\beta)=C_{\mathrm{bath}}(-t)
.
By contrast, the memory kernel is causal.
Causality is
manufactured
by the projection: the partial trace eliminates the bath degrees of freedom that carry the non-causal (thermal/KMS) structure, leaving only the retarded environmental response as seen from the system.

Theorem 2

(Hardy Placement under a real-axis spectral hypothesis)
.

Let the system Liouville space be finite-dimensional.
Assume that, for the chosen projection/inner-product pair, the projected kernel has the Cauchy-transform representation

\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{w(\lambda)}{z-\lambda}\,d\lambda,\qquad z\in\mathbb{H}_{+},

(6)

with no singular real-axis part.
Assume further that there exists
p_{0}>1
such that
w\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{B})\cap L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{B})
.
For Ohmic, Drude-Lorentz, and Lorentzian baths
p_{0}
can be taken arbitrarily large in the finite-
p
sense; for sub-Ohmic
J\propto\omega^{s}e^{-\omega/\omega_{c}}
with
0<s<1
,
w(\lambda)\sim|\lambda|^{s-1}
near
0
forces
p_{0}<1/(1-s)
, so the allowable range is
p\in(1,\,1/(1-s))
and shrinks as
s\to 0
.
Then
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}
belongs to
H^{p}_{+}(\mathcal{B})
for every finite
p
with
1<p\leq p_{0}
.

Proof.

Since
w\in L^{1}\cap L^{p_{0}}
, interpolation gives
w\in L^{p}
for every
1<p\leq p_{0}
.
For each pair of Hilbert–Schmidt test vectors
\xi,\eta
, the scalar density

w_{\xi\eta}(\lambda)=\langle\!\langle\xi,w(\lambda)\eta\rangle\!\rangle
belongs to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for every finite
1<p\leq p_{0}
.
The classical scalar Cauchy transform is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
to the scalar Hardy space
H^{p}(\mathbb{H}_{+})
; equivalently, its non-tangential boundary value is

\operatorname{p.v.}\!\int\frac{w_{\xi\eta}(\lambda)}{\lambda-x}\,d\lambda

up to the standard sign convention, and the Hilbert transform is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for
1<p<\infty
.

For the vector-valued lift, the Hardy space
H^{p}_{+}(\mathcal{B})
with finite-dimensional
\mathcal{B}
is a Bochner space, and the Hilbert transform is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{B})
for
1<p<\infty
whenever
\mathcal{B}
is a UMD Banach space
9
;
3
;
20
.
Every finite-dimensional Banach space is UMD, so
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{HS}}(\mathcal{H}_{s}))
with
\dim\mathcal{H}_{s}<\infty
is UMD with constant depending only on
d_{s}
.
Accordingly, the vector-valued Cauchy transform

\mathcal{C}[w](z)\;=\;\int\frac{w(\lambda)}{z-\lambda}\,d\lambda,\qquad z\in\mathbb{H}_{+},

is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{B})
to
H^{p}_{+}(\mathcal{B})
by the Plemelj–Sokhotski + Hilbert-transform estimate, applied componentwise in any Hilbert–Schmidt matrix-unit basis of
\mathcal{B}
.
Equivalence of norms in the finite-dimensional basis then gives
\|\tilde{\mathcal{K}}\|_{H^{p}_{+}(\mathcal{B})}\leq C(d_{s},p)\,\|w\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{B})}
, hence
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}\in H^{p}_{+}(\mathcal{B})
for every
1<p\leq p_{0}
.
The finite-dimensional reduction step limits the theorem to finite-dimensional system Liouville space; an infinite-dimensional extension would require controlling the UMD constants uniformly in the dimension, which is not needed for the applications below.

The exclusion of
p=1
is an endpoint limitation of this general theorem: the Cauchy transform is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
to
H^{p}(\mathbb{H}_{+})
for
1<p<\infty
but the Hilbert transform is not bounded on all of
L^{1}
.
Thus a standard
H^{1}
KK theorem may still hold for a special model, but it requires a separate endpoint estimate rather than following from the present
L^{p_{0}}
hypothesis.
For threshold singularities or algebraic tails, the safer general statement is the
L^{p}
/principal-value or suitably subtracted form in Corollary
4
.
∎

Remark 3

(On the spectral hypothesis and its verification)

Remark 4

(Finite systems and Poincaré recurrence)
.

For a finite-dimensional bath (or any bath with discrete spectrum),
Q\mathcal{L}Q
has discrete eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{n}\}
and the spectral measure consists of
\delta
-peaks:
d\mu(\lambda)=\sum_{n}w_{n}\delta(\lambda-\lambda_{n})\,d\lambda
.
With the convention used in Eq. (
5
), the kernel becomes a sum of exponentials
\mathcal{K}(t)=-i\sum_{n}w_{n}e^{-i\lambda_{n}t}
, and the corresponding Cauchy transform
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)=\sum_{n}w_{n}/(z-\lambda_{n})
has real-axis poles at
z=\lambda_{n}
in agreement with Eq. (
6
).
Strictly, such a kernel is
not
in
H^{p}_{+}
because
\|(z-\lambda_{n})^{-1}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}
diverges.
However, the KK relations hold in a distributional (principal-value) sense, and the practical KK reconstruction remains well-defined away from the poles.
In any realistic experimental setting, the coupling to a macroscopic environment provides natural broadening (finite bath lifetime), which shifts the poles to
\operatorname{Im}(\lambda_{n})<0
and restores strict
H^{p}_{+}
membership.
The thermodynamic limit is the idealization where this broadening becomes infinitesimal.

IV
KK Reconstruction and New Results

IV.1
Kramers-Kronig relations

Theorem 3

(KK Reconstruction)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}(t)
be causal and absolutely integrable with
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}\in H^{1}_{+}(\mathcal{B})
.
Then the standard KK relations hold a.e.:

\tilde{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}(\omega)=\frac{1}{\pi}\mathcal{P}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!d\omega^{\prime}\,\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime\prime}(\omega^{\prime})}{\omega^{\prime}-\omega}.

(7)

Corollary 4

(Principal-value and subtracted KK beyond
H^{1}
)
.

If
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}\in H^{p}_{+}
for some finite
p>1
but
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}\notin H^{1}_{+}
, the KK relation still holds in the
L^{p}
boundary-value/principal-value sense.
If, in addition, a real frequency
\omega_{*}
is chosen where the boundary value is finite and the subtracted integral is convergent, the once-subtracted KK relation reads

\tilde{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}(\omega)-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}(\omega_{*})=\frac{\omega-\omega_{*}}{\pi}\mathcal{P}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!d\omega^{\prime}\,\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime\prime}(\omega^{\prime})}{(\omega^{\prime}-\omega_{*})(\omega^{\prime}-\omega)}.

(8)

The subtraction point must not be placed at a threshold singularity such as
\omega=0
for a sub-Ohmic density with
|\omega|^{s-1}
divergence.

It is not difficult to see that the Cauchy integral formula on semicircular contours in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
, combined with the Plemelj-Sokhotski boundary-value formula, gives the standard relations; see
22
;
37
;
24
;
34
for the scalar case.
The operator-valued generalization follows because the Cauchy kernel
(\omega^{\prime}-\omega)^{-1}
is scalar and the integral converges in operator norm.

IV.2
CPTP-Hardy consistency criterion

Theorem 5

(CPTP–Hardy consistency for uncancelled rational poles)
.

Let the system Liouville space be finite-dimensional (
\dim\mathcal{H}_{s}<\infty
) and let
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)
be a rational (Padé) approximation
2
to the memory kernel with an isolated simple pole at
z_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{+}
,
\operatorname{Im}(z_{0})>0
, with residue matrix
R
.
Write
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)=R/(z-z_{0})+\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(z)
and assume
z_{0}
is not an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}_{s}+\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(z_{0})
(the
generic
condition, Remark
6
).
Choose
r\in(0,\operatorname{Im}(z_{0}))
such that
z-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(z)
is invertible throughout the closed disc
\overline{D}(z_{0},r)
, and define

n(r)\;\equiv\;\min_{|z-z_{0}|=r}\bigl\|(z-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(z))^{-1}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{-1}\;>\;0.

(9)

(i) Local bound (Rouché).

If
\|R\|<r\,n(r)
for some
r\in(0,\operatorname{Im}(z_{0}))
, then the denominator polynomial for
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}(z)
has exactly
d
zeros (counted with multiplicity) in the open disc
D(z_{0},r)\subset\mathbb{H}_{+}
.
Any such zero that is not removed by the numerator/adjugate factor is an upper-half-plane propagator pole; in particular, this gives the CPTP obstruction under the generic local no-cancellation condition.

(ii) Generic global existence (Vieta).

If the rational denominator factors are coprime, the coefficient
q_{m-1}
of the reduced denominator is real (as in the Hermitian-symmetry case), the associated matrix polynomial has no denominator-root zeros that are cancelled by the prefactor
(z-z_{0})q(z)
, and no numerator/adjugate cancellation removes all UHP zeros, then
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}(z)
has at least one pole in the open upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}_{+}
.
Whenever either condition produces an uncancelled pole
\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{+}
,
\|\mathcal{G}(t)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq C\,e^{\operatorname{Im}(\zeta_{0})\,t}
along an unbounded sequence of times for some
C>0
, hence
\mathcal{G}(t)
cannot be a uniformly bounded CPTP family for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

Finite dimension.

With
\dim\mathcal{H}_{s}<\infty
, the effective Liouvillian
\mathcal{L}_{s}+\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)
acts on a finite-dimensional Liouville space; poles of
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}(z)=i(z-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z))^{-1}
are tracked through zeros of
G(z)\equiv\det(z-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z))
.
Write
A(z)=(z-z_{0})\bigl(z-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(z)\bigr)
, so that
(z-z_{0})\bigl[z-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)\bigr]=A(z)-R
.
Since only the zero-set of
\det(z-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z))
matters for propagator poles, it is convenient to work with the polynomial object
G(z)\equiv\det\bigl(A(z)-R\bigr)=(z-z_{0})^{d}\det\bigl(z-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)\bigr)
; zeros of
G
away from
z_{0}
coincide with propagator poles.

Part (i): Rouché bound on the circle
C_{r}=\{|z-z_{0}|=r\}
.

For every
z\in C_{r}
the Neumann series gives

\displaystyle\bigl\|\bigl[A(z)-R\bigr]^{-1}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\bigl\|A(z)^{-1}\bigl(I-RA(z)^{-1}\bigr)^{-1}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|A(z)^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{1-\|R\|\,\|A(z)^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}}

provided
\|R\|<\|A(z)^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{-1}
.
Now

\displaystyle\|A(z)^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=|z-z_{0}|\,\bigl\|(z-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(z))^{-1}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=r\,\bigl\|(z-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(z))^{-1}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{-1}\geq r\,n(r).

Hence, if
\|R\|<r\,n(r)
, then
\|R\|<\|A(z)^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{-1}
for every
z\in C_{r}
, and consequently
A(z)-R
is invertible on
C_{r}
; i.e.
G(z)\neq 0
on
C_{r}
.
Because
G(z)=\det(A(z)-R)
depends continuously on
R
, the map
s\mapsto G_{s}(z)=\det(A(z)-sR)
is a homotopy with no zeros on
C_{r}
for
s\in[0,1]
.
By the argument principle,
G_{1}(z)=G(z)
and
G_{0}(z)=\det(A(z))
have the same number of zeros inside
D(z_{0},r)
.
But
G_{0}(z)=(z-z_{0})^{d}\det(z-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(z))
with
d=\dim\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{HS}}(\mathcal{H}_{s})
.
By the closed-disc regularity assumption,
\det(z-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(z))\neq 0
on
\overline{D}(z_{0},r)
; therefore
G_{0}
has exactly
d
zeros inside
D(z_{0},r)
, all at
z_{0}
.
Thus
G(z)
also has exactly
d
denominator zeros inside
D(z_{0},r)\subset\mathbb{H}_{+}
.
They are propagator poles precisely for the zeros not cancelled by the numerator/adjugate factor in
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
.

Part (ii): Global existence via Vieta’s formula.

Since
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}
is rational, write
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(z)=P(z)/q(z)
with
\deg q=m
and leading coefficient
1
.
Multiplying
G(z)
by
q(z)^{d}
eliminates the denominator:

\begin{aligned} q(z)^{d}G(z)&=\det\!\Bigl[(z-z_{0})\bigl(zq(z)-q(z)\mathcal{L}_{s}-P(z)\bigr)-R\,q(z)\Bigr]\\
&\equiv\det M(z).\end{aligned}

M(z)
is a matrix polynomial of degree
m+2
; its determinant is a scalar polynomial of degree
d(m+2)
.
Expanding the leading terms,

M(z)=z^{m+2}I-(z_{0}+\mathcal{L}_{s}-q_{m-1}I)z^{m+1}+O(z^{m+1}),

where
q_{m-1}
is the coefficient of
z^{m-1}
in
q(z)
.
Vieta’s formula gives the sum of
all

d(m+2)
zeros of
\det M(z)
:

\sum_{j=1}^{d(m+2)}z_{j}=\operatorname{Tr}(z_{0}+\mathcal{L}_{s}-q_{m-1}I)=d\,z_{0}+\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{L}_{s})-d\,q_{m-1}.

(10)

We now impose the generic no-cancellation condition that all UHP zeros of
\det M(z)
which carry the positive imaginary-part budget below are propagator poles.
At a root
\alpha
of
q
the first term of
M
vanishes and
M(\alpha)=-(\alpha-z_{0})P(\alpha)
.
For a matrix-valued rational approximant, scalar coprimeness of the entries is not by itself enough to guarantee
\det P(\alpha)\neq 0
, so this nonvanishing is included in the denominator-root no-cancellation hypothesis of part (ii).
At
z=z_{0}
,
M(z_{0})=-R\,q(z_{0})
gives
\det M(z_{0})=(-1)^{d}\det(R)\,q(z_{0})^{d}
; if
R
is singular, the corresponding possible cancellation is again covered by the explicit no-cancellation hypothesis.
Under these coprime, denominator-root, and no-adjugate-cancellation assumptions, no relevant zero of
\det M
coincides with
z_{0}
or with a root of
q
, and the numerator factor
(z-z_{0})q(z)
in
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}(z)=i(z-z_{0})q(z)\,\mathrm{adj}(M(z))/\det M(z)
cancels none of them.

Two facts then close the argument.
(a)
\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{L}_{s})=0
identically.
In the Hilbert-Schmidt basis
\{E_{ij}=|i\rangle\langle j|\}
,
\langle\!\langle E_{ij},\mathcal{L}_{s}E_{ij}\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}=\langle i|H_{s}|i\rangle-\langle j|H_{s}|j\rangle
; summing over
i,j
gives
0
independent of
H_{s}
.
(b) The Padé denominator
q(z)
has real coefficients, since
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}
is the Laplace transform of a Hermitian-valued real-time kernel; in particular
q_{m-1}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Taking imaginary parts of Eq. (
10
),

\sum_{j=1}^{d(m+2)}\operatorname{Im}(z_{j})\;=\;d\,\operatorname{Im}(z_{0})\;>\;0,

(11)

so at least one propagator pole lies in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
.

Singular residues and nongeneric cancellations.

If
R
is singular, or if an adjugate numerator vanishes at a zero of
\det M
, some polynomial zeros can be removable singularities of
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
.
The local Rouché statement in part (i) still locates the denominator zeros, but pole conclusions require the same explicit no-cancellation check.
For the global Vieta statement we likewise keep the generic no-cancellation assumption rather than claiming a cancellation-free theorem in all degenerate cases.

Lower bound on
\|\mathcal{G}(t)\|
.

Bromwich inversion picks up the residue at any UHP zero
\zeta_{0}
:
\mathcal{G}(t)
contains a nonzero finite-rank term of the form
-i\Pi_{0}e^{-i\zeta_{0}t}
, up to possible polynomial factors if the pole is higher order.
Terms from other poles cannot make the family uniformly bounded for all
t
unless there is an exact cancellation of the whole unstable modal subspace; this is precisely the cancellation excluded in the theorem hypotheses.
Thus
\|\mathcal{G}(t)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
grows exponentially along an unbounded sequence of times.
Since finite-dimensional norms are equivalent, such growth also violates uniform boundedness in the trace norm.
Every CPTP map
\Lambda_{t}
on a finite-dimensional system is trace-norm contractive and therefore satisfies
\|\Lambda_{t}\|_{1\to 1}=1
.
The unstable pole is therefore incompatible with a CPTP reduced evolution family.
∎

Corollary 6

(Explicit perturbative bound)
.

With
N\equiv\|(z_{0}-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(z_{0}))^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
as before, choose
r=\operatorname{Im}(z_{0})/2
.
If
\tilde{K}_{\mathrm{reg}}
varies sufficiently little on the circle
|z-z_{0}|=\operatorname{Im}(z_{0})/2
that
n(\operatorname{Im}(z_{0})/2)\geq 1/(2N)
, then the hypothesis
\|R\|<\operatorname{Im}(z_{0})/(2N)
of the earlier perturbative statement is satisfied and conclusion (i) of Theorem
5
holds.

Remark 5

(Local vs. global information)
.

Part (i) gives stronger
local
information: it places exactly
d
denominator zeros inside a prescribed disc
D(z_{0},r)
, but requires the small-residue hypothesis
\|R\|<r\,n(r)
and a no-cancellation check before those zeros are called propagator poles.
Part (ii) is weaker (only one pole in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
, with no control on its location) and generic: it assumes the rational representation is reduced and that numerator/adjugate cancellations do not remove all UHP zeros of the polynomial pencil.
In the applications of this paper the residue comes from an
O(\epsilon)
correlation perturbation, so
\|R\|\ll r\,n(r)
and the local bound is both rigorous and informative.
The global result should be read as a useful algebraic diagnostic, not as a replacement for checking degeneracies in a concrete Padé reconstruction.

Remark 6

(Defining the
generic
condition)
.

In the finite-dimensional parameter space of
(\mathcal{L}_{s},\{\kappa_{n}\})
, where
\{\kappa_{n}\}
are the moments of
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}
, invertibility of
z_{0}-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(z_{0})
fails on the real-algebraic variety
\det[z_{0}-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(z_{0})]=0
.
This is a codimension-one (Zariski-closed, Lebesgue-measure-zero) subset.
“Generic” means the complement of this set, which is a dense open subset of parameter space; on this set the obstruction is structurally stable under small perturbations of
H_{sb}
.

Remark 7

(CPTP vs. signal-causality)
.

The theorem name is chosen deliberately.
An upper-half-plane kernel pole forces violation of the boundedness required of a CPTP reduced family, not the signal-propagation sense of causality (which would require super-luminal communication).
The bridge to macroscopic causality is this: CPTP is a necessary consequence of factorized unitary system-bath dynamics under partial trace, so an approximate homogeneous kernel whose propagator is unbounded cannot be that exact CPTP reduced family.
In that sense Theorem
5
is the open-quantum-system shadow of Gavassino’s observation that macroscopic acausality signals incompatibility with causal microscopic origin
17
, rather than a local super-luminal effect.

Remark 8

(Connection to PMKCT)
.

The PMKCT approach
27
uses spectral projection to remove unstable eigenvalues from the dynamics matrix.
Theorem
5
gives a consistency rationale for this practice: uncancelled upper-half-plane contributions would produce unbounded reduced propagators and therefore cannot represent a CPTP family.

IV.3
Passivity-analyticity link

Theorem 7

(Passivity-Analyticity compatibility)
.

Suppose
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)
is analytic in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
and satisfies the
operator dissipation condition

\operatorname{Im}\langle\!\langle\xi,\,\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)\,\xi\rangle\!\rangle\leq 0,\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{H}_{+},\ \forall\xi\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{HS}}(\mathcal{H}_{s}),

(12)

where the inner product is taken over the full Hilbert-Schmidt space
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{HS}}(\mathcal{H}_{s})
(not only on the cone of density matrices).
Assume in addition the spectral hypotheses of Theorem
2
, and assume the coupling-weighted spectral density is operator-positive,
w(\lambda)\geq 0
as a bounded operator on
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{HS}}(\mathcal{H}_{s})
for almost every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
(so that
w_{\xi}(\lambda)=\langle\!\langle\xi,w(\lambda)\xi\rangle\!\rangle\geq 0
for all
\xi
).
Then
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}
belongs to
H^{p}_{+}(\mathcal{B})
for every
p\in(1,p_{0}]
and obeys KK; condition (
12
) identifies this Hardy kernel as lying in the operator-valued anti-Herglotz/Nevanlinna subclass.
This compatibility applies in particular to passive bosonic baths at thermal equilibrium when their kernel representation satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
2
and the operator-positivity assumption.

Proof.

Since (
12
) is imposed on every
\xi\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{HS}}(\mathcal{H}_{s})
, polarization gives
\operatorname{Im}\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)\leq 0
as an operator on
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{HS}}(\mathcal{H}_{s})
for each
z\in\mathbb{H}_{+}
.
Equivalently,
-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}
is an operator-valued Herglotz (Nevanlinna) function on
\mathbb{H}_{+}
.
Under the spectral representation (
6
), a positive coupling-weighted density gives the scalar check

\operatorname{Im}\langle\!\langle\xi,\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)\xi\rangle\!\rangle=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{y}{(\lambda-x)^{2}+y^{2}}\,w_{\xi}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\leq 0,

(13)

where
w_{\xi}(\lambda)=\langle\!\langle\xi,w(\lambda)\xi\rangle\!\rangle\geq 0
and
z=x+iy
.
The Hardy membership and KK relations then follow from Theorem
2
; passivity supplies the Nevanlinna sign structure, not an independent replacement for the
L^{p}
spectral hypothesis.
∎

Remark 9

(Compatibility, not independence)
.

Theorem
7
should be read as a
compatibility
statement: under the spectral hypotheses of Theorem
2
, operator dissipativity singles out the Herglotz-Nevanlinna sub-class of Hardy kernels and certifies that no additional pathology occurs.
A fully independent proof of Hardy placement from passivity alone (without re-using the spectral representation of
Q\mathcal{L}Q
) would require an operator-valued Herglotz theorem bounded directly on the axis of observation; we view this as a sharper — but not essential — refinement.

This establishes the conditional chain:

\displaystyle\text{Hardy spectral hypothesis}+\text{Dissipation}

(14)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\text{Nevanlinna-compatible Hardy kernel}\Rightarrow\text{KK}.

Physical picture.

The condition (
12
) states that the kernel’s anti-Hermitian part is negative semidefinite on
\mathbb{H}_{+}
: the bath absorbs energy from the system at every frequency.
This is the standard situation for a passive bosonic bath at thermal equilibrium.
Theorem
7
is the open-quantum-system analogue of Gavassino’s stability-causality theorem
16
for this restricted class of baths: thermodynamic stability constrains causal dispersion in relativistic hydrodynamics; here dissipative passivity is compatible with the Hardy-class analyticity supplied by the spectral hypothesis.
The theorem does
not
apply to driven, squeezed, or gain media (Remark
10
).

The passivity criterion can be checked from a measured
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(\omega)
on the real axis, provided the spectral hypotheses of Theorem
2
are otherwise assumed or justified separately.

Remark 10

(When passivity fails)
.

Condition (
12
) fails when (i) the bath is out of equilibrium (driven/squeezed) and provides gain at some frequencies; (ii) the effective Hamiltonian is non-Hermitian (gain media,
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric systems); or (iii) the kernel is a phenomenological ansatz with unphysical amplification.
In these cases the kernel may still be analytic in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
but belong to a more general class requiring Blaschke-Nevanlinna decomposition with residue-corrected KK.
Extending the theorem to these regimes is the subject of ongoing work (see Sec.
IX
).

IV.4
Moment-based Carleman diagnostic

Theorem 8

(Moment-Based Carleman diagnostic; finite truncations)
.

Consider a finite total Hilbert-space truncation in which the projected moment sequence is generated by a bounded self-adjoint spectral problem satisfying the real-axis hypothesis of Theorem
2
.
Because the MKCT convention in Eq. (
2
) uses
(i\mathcal{L})^{n}
, the positive Hamburger moments are the sign-corrected even moments

\bm{M}_{2n}=(-1)^{n}\bm{\Omega}_{2n}.

Under the real-axis self-adjoint hypothesis,
\bm{M}_{2n}\geq 0
on the relevant Liouville-space sector, so the diagonal scalar moments
\langle\!\langle\xi,\bm{M}_{2n}\xi\rangle\!\rangle
are moments of positive scalar Hamburger measures.
If the scalar diagonal moment sequences obtained from the MKCT moments satisfy the Carleman condition, for example as certified by the sufficient operator bound

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\bm{\Omega}_{2n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{-1/(2n)}=\infty,

(15)

then the scalar Hamburger moment problem is determinate for the corresponding diagonal spectral measures, and off-diagonal matrix elements are fixed by polarization.
This is a uniqueness and stability diagnostic for Padé or MKCT reconstructions.
It does not by itself prove Hardy membership or convergence of every finite-order Padé sequence; those properties still require the
L^{p}
spectral-density assumptions of Theorem
2
or a separate Markov/Stieltjes Padé convergence theorem for the particular model.

Proof.

For a fixed unit vector
\phi
in the finite-dimensional Liouville space, the diagonal entries of
\bm{M}_{2n}=(-1)^{n}\bm{\Omega}_{2n}
are moments of a positive scalar spectral measure under the self-adjoint finite-truncation hypothesis.
The scalar Carleman theorem gives determinacy of that positive Hamburger moment problem
1
.
In a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, every off-diagonal matrix element is a linear combination of diagonal ones by the polarization identity.
Thus the off-diagonal entries are fixed once the diagonal measures are fixed.
For Padé convergence and Hardy membership one must still verify the standard positivity/support hypotheses of the relevant Markov-function convergence theorem and the
L^{p}
boundary-density condition of Theorem
2
.
In infinite dimension the promotion to a fully operator-valued determinacy statement requires additional operator-valued Hamburger theory
13
.
∎

For a finite total Hamiltonian truncation,
\|\bm{\Omega}_{2n}\|\leq C\|H\|^{2n}
, so
\|\bm{\Omega}_{2n}\|^{-1/(2n)}\geq(C\|H\|^{2})^{-1/2}
and the Carleman sum diverges.
This automatic conclusion does not apply to an infinite bosonic bath with unbounded total Hamiltonian.

Remark 11

(Asymptotic criterion vs. finite moments)
.

Condition (
15
) is an asymptotic (
n\to\infty
) statement, while practical MKCT pipelines compute only
\bm{\Omega}_{\leq N_{\max}}
with
N_{\max}
typically
\leq 20
.
Extrapolation from a finite partial sum to the infinite series is itself an inference, and can fail for baths whose moments grow marginally.
For the Drude-Lorentz model the moments grow polynomially; for Ohmic baths with
J(\omega)\propto\omega e^{-\omega/\omega_{c}}
the moments grow as
\sim(2n-1)!\omega_{c}^{2n}
, and (
15
) is a genuine constraint (see Appendix
D
).

Remark 12

(Froissart doublets)
.

When the Carleman condition is marginally violated (borderline moment growth), the Padé approximants may develop
Froissart doublets
: spurious pole-zero pairs that wander in the complex plane without converging.
These manifest as numerical noise in the kernel reconstruction and should not be confused with genuine Hardy-space violations.
The Carleman diagnostic helps separate moment-indeterminacy or truncation artifacts from instabilities that survive a separate Hardy/spectral check.

V
State Robustness Against Initial Correlations

The preceding sections treat the memory kernel
\mathcal{K}(t)
under the assumption that the initial state is factorized,
\rho(0)=\sigma(0)\otimes\rho_{b}^{\mathrm{eq}}
.
Realistic preparations — thermal equilibrium of the joint system, post-measurement conditional states, post-quench dynamics — are generally
correlated
.
One may ask whether such correlations destroy the causal analytic structure.

Here we show that the reduced-state Laplace transform is protected regardless of initial correlations. The memory kernel is a Hamiltonian-level object, but
\tilde{\sigma}(z)
also enters the dispersion analysis whenever one reconstructs an effective kernel from observed dynamics (Sec.
VI
). The following theorem guarantees that the state transform itself cannot be pushed out of
\mathbb{H}_{+}
.

Theorem 9

(State analyticity under arbitrary initial correlations)
.

Let
H
be a self-adjoint total Hamiltonian on
\mathcal{H}_{s}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{b}
and let
\rho_{0}
be any density operator on this joint space (factorized or correlated). By Stone’s theorem, the unitary group is strongly continuous; for trace-class
\rho_{0}
,
t\mapsto e^{-iHt}\rho_{0}e^{iHt}
is continuous in trace norm. The partial trace is trace-norm continuous, so
\sigma(t)=\mathrm{Tr}_{b}[e^{-iHt}\rho_{0}e^{iHt}]
is trace-norm continuous with
\|\sigma(t)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
for all
t\geq 0
. Its reduced-state Laplace transform

\tilde{\sigma}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{izt}\,\sigma(t)\,dt

is analytic in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
as a trace-class-valued function, with
\|\tilde{\sigma}(z)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1/\operatorname{Im}(z)
.

Proof.

Since
e^{-iHt}
is unitary and
\rho_{0}
is a valid density operator, the partial trace produces a valid reduced density operator:
\sigma(t)\geq 0
and
\mathrm{Tr}[\sigma(t)]=1
. For a positive operator with unit trace, the operator norm is bounded by the trace:
\|\sigma(t)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
. Trace-norm continuity of
\sigma(t)
ensures the Laplace integral is well-defined in trace norm. For
z=x+iy
with
y>0
:

\|\tilde{\sigma}(z)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-yt}\|\sigma(t)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\,dt\leq\frac{1}{y}.

The bound is uniform in
x
, so the integrand is dominated by an integrable function and
\tilde{\sigma}(z)
is analytic in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
by standard results (Morera or direct differentiation under the integral).
∎

Remark 13

(Kernel vs. reduced-state Laplace transform)
.

Theorem
9
concerns
\tilde{\sigma}(z)
, not the memory kernel.
Strictly,
\tilde{\sigma}(z)
is the Laplace transform of a
state trajectory
: it is a propagator map on the reduced initial state only when the initial state is factorized.
For correlated preparations the map from
\sigma(0)
to
\sigma(t)
is not well-defined without an assignment rule (and may be nonlinear or nonunique under such a rule), although
\sigma(t)
remains linear in the full initial state
\rho_{0}
.
The NZ memory kernel
\mathcal{K}(t)
, in contrast, depends on the Hamiltonian but not on the initial state; its Hardy membership (Theorem
2
) is a property of
H
alone.
The initial state enters the exact reduced equation through the inhomogeneous term
\mathcal{I}(t)=P\mathcal{L}Q\,e^{iQ\mathcal{L}Qt}\,Q\rho_{0}
, which modifies
\sigma(t)
but cannot push
\|\sigma(t)\|
above
1
.

V.0.1
Connection to Gavassino

Gavassino
17
showed that macroscopic acausal behaviour can be entirely encoded in initial conditions when information is discarded.
Theorem
9
shows that the quantum analogue is more restrictive:
\sigma(t)
inherits boundedness from microscopic unitarity, preventing acausal leaks into
\mathbb{H}_{+}
through the state transform itself.
The parallel is preserved at the effective kernel, as shown next.

A natural stress test: the
\sigma_{x}
coupling regime.

Theorem
9
is model-independent, but it should be stress-tested in models where intuition most strongly suggests apparent amplification or backflow.
A standard candidate is the spin-boson model with
\sigma_{x}
coupling, where populations and coherences mix nontrivially and information backflow
5
;
4
is known to occur.
The theorem does not bound a particular matrix element such as
|\sigma_{01}(t)|
by its initial value; Hamiltonian mixing can transfer population into coherence.
What it does bound is the operator norm of the full density matrix, and hence the Laplace-domain analyticity of
\tilde{\sigma}(z)
.

VI
Effective Memory Kernel with Initial Correlations

Theorem
9
shows that the reduced-state Laplace transform is analytically safe.
A separate question is what happens to the
memory kernel
when an experimentalist (or numerical extractor) force-fits the correlated dynamics into a homogeneous master equation, discarding the inhomogeneous term
\mathcal{I}(t)
.
This is the natural mathematical object for dispersion analysis, and it is not protected by the state-boundedness theorem.

VI.1
Force-fit and the effective kernel

Starting from the exact inhomogeneous GQME

\frac{d}{dt}\sigma(t)=\mathcal{L}_{s}\sigma(t)+(\mathcal{K}*\sigma)(t)+\mathcal{I}(t)

(16)

and demanding a purely homogeneous form
\dot{\sigma}=\mathcal{L}_{s}\sigma+\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{eff}}*\sigma
, Laplace transformation gives, in a scalar channel,

\boxed{\quad\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{eff}}(z)=\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)+\tilde{\mathcal{I}}(z)\,\tilde{\sigma}(z)^{-1},\quad}

(17)

where the inverse means division by the scalar channel transform.
For a full operator-valued reconstruction one should instead stack several linearly independent trajectories into a matrix
\tilde{S}(z)
and write
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{eff}}(z)=\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)+\tilde{I}(z)\tilde{S}(z)^{-1}
. This matrix form is well-defined only on the open set
\{z\in\mathbb{H}_{+}:\det\tilde{S}(z)\neq 0\}
; at an isolated determinant zero
\zeta
,
\tilde{S}(z)^{-1}=\mathrm{adj}\,\tilde{S}(z)/\det\tilde{S}(z)
makes
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{eff}}
meromorphic with a pole whose order is controlled by the vanishing order of
\det\tilde{S}
minus the vanishing order of
\tilde{I}(z)\,\mathrm{adj}\,\tilde{S}(z)
.
At zeros of the scalar denominator, or determinant zeros of
\tilde{S}(z)
with no adjugate cancellation, the effective kernel has poles; this is the mechanism studied below.
The effective kernel picks up a correction that is the quotient of the inhomogeneous term and the reduced-state transform in the scalar channel.

VI.2
Perturbative expansion

In such a scalar channel, parametrize the deviation from factorization by
\epsilon
(physically, some small coupling-related ratio such as
g/\omega_{c}
):

\rho_{0}(\epsilon)=\rho_{\mathrm{fact}}+\epsilon\,\delta\rho+\epsilon^{2}\,\rho^{(2)}+O(\epsilon^{3}).

Since
Q\rho_{\mathrm{fact}}=0
by definition,

\tilde{\mathcal{I}}(z;\epsilon)=\epsilon\,\tilde{\mathcal{I}}^{(1)}(z)+\epsilon^{2}\,\tilde{\mathcal{I}}^{(2)}(z)+O(\epsilon^{3}),

with
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}^{(n)}(z)
analytic in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
under the same projected spectral hypothesis used for
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}
. The Taylor expansion of Eq. (
17
) gives

\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{eff}}(z;\epsilon)=\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)+\epsilon\,\tilde{\mathcal{I}}^{(1)}(z)\,\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(z)^{-1}+O(\epsilon^{2}),

(18)

where
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(z)
is the factorized-state transform.
The omitted inverse correction starts at
O(\epsilon^{2})
because the inhomogeneous numerator itself is
O(\epsilon)
.

VI.3
The zero-of-
\tilde{\sigma}
mechanism

Both
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}^{(1)}(z)
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(z)
are analytic in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
. Their quotient is also analytic —
except at points where
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(z)=0
. A simple scalar zero
\zeta\in\mathbb{H}_{+}
of
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}
becomes a scalar pole of
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{eff}}
only when
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}^{(1)}(\zeta)\neq 0
.
Under this non-cancellation condition, Theorem
5
gives a CPTP-incompatibility diagnostic for the force-fit effective dynamics.
At the level of matrix-valued
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}
, the corresponding statement involves zeros of the determinant
\det\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(z)
and possible adjugate cancellations, and is best viewed as a conjecture at this stage; the scalar statement already suffices to define a channel-resolved KK diagnostic.

For standard passive bosonic baths (Ohmic, sub-Ohmic, Drude-Lorentz) with factorized initial state, scalar channels that admit a positive Stieltjes representation have no zeros in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
. In this
robust regime
, Eq. (
18
) preserves Hardy membership at order
\epsilon
and the modified KK relation reduces to the standard one to this order.

For systems with near-resonant structure (vacuum Rabi splittings, multi-peak spectral densities, band edges), channels with no such positivity constraint — in particular, coherence components
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}^{(eg)}(z)
— can admit zeros in the open upper half-plane.
These zeros are an intrinsic property of the channel symmetry, arising already for factorized initial states; initial correlations enter only through the inhomogeneous numerator
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}^{(1)}(\zeta)
, whose non-vanishing at a zero
\zeta
is the second independent condition required for a force-fit pole.
This is the natural setting for a
fragile regime
: two independent conditions must coincide — (i) a channel-symmetry zero of
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}
in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
(an intrinsic property of the coherence channel, already present for factorized states) and (ii) a non-vanishing inhomogeneous numerator
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}^{(1)}(\zeta)\neq 0
(requiring initial correlations). When both hold, a pole of
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{eff}}
in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
appears at order
\epsilon
, and Theorem
5
indicates CPTP incompatibility of the force-fit description.
Figure
1
identifies this zero-side precondition explicitly in a finite Jaynes-Cummings model: the coherence-channel transform
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}^{(eg)}(z)
for
|+\rangle\langle+|\otimes\rho_{b}^{\mathrm{th}}
carries a substantial fraction of zeros in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
(about
11\%
across the coupling scan, with
\max|\mathrm{Im}(z)|\approx 0.35\,\omega_{c}
), while the population channel keeps all its scanned zeros on the real axis within numerical precision.
Whether this zero-side crossing admits a winding-number classification as the coupling is varied is an open question (Problem
IX
).

Dependence on the Laplace regularization.

Because the JC kernel does not decay (Poincaré recurrence in the truncated Fock space), the KK check must be performed on the shifted line

VI.5
Modified KK relation

Applying the Cauchy-integral machinery of Theorem
3
to Eq. (
18
) gives, in the fragile regime with simple zeros
\{\zeta_{j}\}\subset\mathbb{H}_{+}
of
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}
,

\operatorname{Re}[\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{eff}}(\omega)]=\mathcal{H}\big[\operatorname{Im}\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{eff}}\big](\omega)+\epsilon\,\Delta(\omega;\{\zeta_{j}\})+O(\epsilon^{2}),

(19)

with the explicit correction

\Delta(\omega;\{\zeta_{j}\})=2\sum_{j}\operatorname{Re}\!\left[\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{I}}^{(1)}(\zeta_{j})/\tilde{\sigma}_{0}^{\prime}(\zeta_{j})}{\omega-\zeta_{j}}\right].

This follows from the Plemelj formula: a simple pole at
\zeta_{j}
with residue
R_{j}
in the UHP contributes
2\operatorname{Re}[R_{j}/(\omega-\zeta_{j})]
to the discrepancy between
\operatorname{Re}[f]
and
\mathcal{H}[\operatorname{Im}\,f]
on the real axis.
Each uncancelled
\zeta_{j}
contributes a Lorentzian term in
\Delta(\omega)
. The amplitude of each term sets the size of the channel-resolved force-fit KK residual.

VI.6
Analytic self-consistency for the robust regime (IBM)

For the independent boson model with Drude-Lorentz spectral density, the scalar decoherence function
\Gamma(t)
is monotone and its Laplace transform
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(z)
is a Stieltjes function of a positive measure on
\mathbb{R}
; hence
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}
has no zeros in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
and the robust-regime prediction
\Delta\equiv 0
at order
\epsilon
follows immediately.
Figure

checks this analytic consistency of our
O(\epsilon)
formula: the residual is at numerical roundoff because the perturbation is constructed to be Hilbert-paired by the formula itself. This is a self-consistency sanity check of Eq. (
18
), not an independent numerical extraction of
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{eff}}
from correlated IBM dynamics; such an extraction is inherently underdetermined from a single trajectory (the reconstruction problem has more unknowns than equations in the
4\times 4
operator-valued case), and we leave it as an open computational problem.
The Jaynes-Cummings coherence-channel extraction above has vacuum-Rabi near-zeros and therefore sits closer to the fragile regime; the spin-boson correlated-state diagnostic in Sec.

gives a separate time-domain illustration of how the discarded inhomogeneous term contaminates a force-fit kernel.

VII
Boundaries of Causality Preservation

The spin-boson model used in this section has a projected generator

The numerical demonstrations below are therefore restricted to (i) the Born-order bath-correlator KK as a proxy for the kernel-level check and (ii) finite-truncation force-fit diagnostics, rather than a non-perturbative Hardy verification of Theorem

VIII
Discussion

Our results adapt Gavassino’s causality-from-coarse-graining framework
16
;
15
;
17
to open quantum systems, in the negative/diagnostic form described in the Introduction.
Theorem
1
captures the “correct initialization preserves causality” part of that picture: the factorized initial state is the quantum analogue of Gavassino’s causal preparation.
Theorem
7
captures the stability-causality chain for passive bosonic baths in a conditional form: just as thermodynamic stability constrains causal dispersion in relativistic systems
16
, dissipative passivity is compatible with the Hardy-class analyticity and KK reconstruction supplied by the spectral hypothesis.
The correlated-state diagnostics in Sec.
VII
, together with the Jaynes-Cummings coherence-kernel extraction in Sec.
VI
, illustrate the “stadium wave” mechanism
17
: correlations in the joint state, combined with discarding the inhomogeneous term
\mathcal{I}(t)
, encode information that can masquerade as acausal behaviour of the force-fit effective kernel.
The principal numerical result is the operator-valued KK verification (Sec.
VII
): the measured
3.8\%
integrated relative
L_{2}
residual is at or below the
{\approx}5\%
circular-Hilbert noise floor calibrated on a known-analytic test function, so the extracted
4\times 4
NZ memory kernel is consistent with exact matrix-valued KK within the present protocol’s accuracy. Establishing a quantitative sub-baseline residual would require a calibrated non-circular Hilbert protocol, which we identify as an immediate open problem.
The scalar effective-kernel diagnostic, by contrast, is a suggestive but model-dependent signature whose quantitative residual ratio depends on the Laplace regularization and finite-truncation artifacts.

For computational methods (MKCT
26
, HEOM, process tensors
21
;
33
), the theorems provide three practical diagnostics.
(1)
Padé pole check
(Theorem
5
): if a reduced rational kernel reconstruction produces uncancelled upper-half-plane poles, the force-fit dynamics is generically incompatible with a uniformly bounded CPTP reduced family — regularize, check cancellations, or extend the moment sequence.
(2)
Passivity check
(Theorem
7
): test whether
\operatorname{Im}\langle\!\langle\xi,\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(\omega)\xi\rangle\!\rangle\leq 0
on the real axis, after the separate Hardy/spectral hypotheses have been justified.
(3)
Carleman check
(Theorem
8
): monitor the growth rate of MKCT moments in finite truncations; for infinite bosonic baths this remains a genuine asymptotic diagnostic.

Experimental outlook.

The modified KK residual
\Delta(\omega;\{\zeta_{j}\})
in Eq. (
19
) is a concrete frequency-domain signature of a force-fit reduced description; turning this into a direct experimental test requires closing the input-output gap between the force-fit kernel and the measured susceptibility.
Candidate THz platforms and the specific steps needed are discussed in Sec.
VIII.1
.

Extensions not covered here include: non-Hermitian effective Hamiltonians, where Blaschke-Nevanlinna decomposition provides residue-corrected KK
18
; thermal/Matsubara structure, where the KMS condition introduces additional imaginary-time constraints; and exceptional-point singularities, where branch cuts on multi-sheeted Riemann surfaces modify the dispersion structure.
The present paper identifies non-unitary microdynamics (
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric dimers, gain media, and related systems) as the boundary at which the framework may break down, and leaves the rigorous analysis of that regime open.
A closely related complementary thread on dynamical information leakage and internal causality breaking in reduced subsystem evolution has recently been explored
40
.

Confirming intuition to locate its boundary.

The present results confirm physicists’ intuition: factorized initial states preserve causality, microscopic unitarity protects
\tilde{\sigma}(z)
, and passive baths respect the standard dispersion relations.
This is not a weakness – it sharpens the question of where the framework fails.
By establishing that the straightforward causal regime is indeed robust, the analysis points to the boundary between unitary and non-unitary dynamics, where gain-induced poles and exceptional-point singularities can push zeros of
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(z)
into the upper half-plane.
The scope of this paper is foundational: it provides the language and diagnostics for a systematic search for causality-breaking phenomena, while the surprising predictions remain for future work (Problem
IX
).

A counter-intuitive robustness.

Theorem
9
reveals a structural feature that contradicts naive intuition: initial entanglement between system and bath does
not
destroy the upper-half-plane analyticity of the reduced-state transform
\tilde{\sigma}(z)
.
One might expect that discarding correlations and fitting a single-variable memory kernel to a multi-variable joint state would generically produce upper-half-plane singularities in the reduced-state transform itself; instead,
\tilde{\sigma}(z)
remains analytic in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
for
any
initial state.
The acausality observed in the force-fit effective kernels is an artifact of the reconstructed
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{eff}}
, not a breakdown of underlying causality.
In physical terms, the joint system-bath evolution never violates causality; it is only our stubborn insistence on describing it with a single-variable memory kernel that creates the illusion of acausality.
Even more subtly, Theorem
5
shows that, away from nongeneric cancellations, the propagation of a rational kernel pole into a propagator pole is tracked by an algebraic sum rule: the Vieta budget
\sum\operatorname{Im}(z_{j})=d\,\operatorname{Im}(z_{0})>0
must be carried by zeros of the associated polynomial pencil.
This gives a non-perturbative diagnostic complementary to the local implicit-function viewpoint, but it must be applied together with the explicit no-cancellation checks stated in the theorem.

VIII.1
The THz correlation fingerprint

The modified KK relation (
19
) suggests a possible experimental signature of initial correlations in the frequency domain.
Consider a strong-coupling system, such as a molecular crystal or a polaritonic cavity, interrogated by terahertz (THz) spectroscopy.
If the measured susceptibility can be modelled by the same force-fit reduced kernel, then the absorption spectrum and refractive response should inherit the Hilbert-pair structure of the causal kernel.

In the fragile regime, Eq. (
19
) predicts a Lorentzian residual
\Delta(\omega)
in the force-fit memory kernel.
Each uncancelled upper-half-plane zero
\zeta_{j}
of
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}
contributes a Lorentzian term centered at
\operatorname{Re}(\zeta_{j})
with half-width
\operatorname{Im}(\zeta_{j})
.
If this reduced-kernel description is the right effective response variable, the correction could appear as a “phantom resonance” — a feature in the reactive response without the usual dissipative counterpart — or as an anomalous line-shape asymmetry in 2D THz or 2D-IR echo spectroscopy.
A plausible platform is a vibrational polariton or cavity-QED system near vacuum Rabi splitting
\omega_{\mathrm{Rabi}}\sim 2g
in the THz regime.
Preparing a joint thermal state of system plus cavity, rather than a factorized product, and tuning
g
toward stronger coupling may move reduced-state zeros toward the upper half-plane.
Turning this into a direct experimental test requires deriving the measured susceptibility from the same force-fit object and is left as future work.

IX
Open Problems

The present framework suggests several directions.

Numerical extensions.

The Jaynes-Cummings verification in Sec.
VII
supports the matrix-valued KK relation for a model whose projected generator has real spectrum.
Extending this to the spin-boson model at moderate-to-strong coupling – where
Q\mathcal{L}Q
develops complex eigenvalues – requires either a modified Hilbert-pair condition on the shifted spectral contour or a direct extraction from HEOM/pseudomode data.
On the calibration side, the operator-valued KK residual reported in Sec.
VII
(integrated relative
L_{2}
of
3.8\%
) sits at or below the noise floor of a circular FFT-Hilbert pipeline (
\approx 5\%
at
N_{t}=4000
).
Quantifying genuine intrinsic residuals requires a calibrated protocol that uses a non-circular (aperiodic) Hilbert transform with endpoint tapering, extrapolates to the
N_{t}\to\infty
and
\omega_{\mathrm{max}}\to\infty
limits against a bank of analytically known test kernels, and reports channel-by-channel absolute
L_{2}
errors with error bars traceable to the tapering and extrapolation steps.

Topological classification.

Fig.
1
shows that the coherence channel of
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(z)
already hosts UHP zeros in a finite Jaynes-Cummings model.
What remains is a quantitative classification: as a coupling or detuning parameter is varied, how do the zeros move, and is the transition from robust to fragile labelled by a winding number of
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}(z)
along a closed contour in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
?
A perturbation theory in the initial-correlation strength
\epsilon
, combined with a direct reconstruction of
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{eff}}(z)
from correlated JC trajectories, would test whether a critical parameter
\gamma_{c}
exists at which the number of UHP zeros changes discontinuously, protected by a topological invariant – a “causality phase transition” connecting non-Hermitian physics, topological phases, and open-quantum-system theory.

Experimental contact.

The modified KK residual
\Delta(\omega)
of Eq. (
19
) is a diagnostic, not yet a prediction.
Bridging this gap requires an input-output derivation that relates the measured THz susceptibility to the force-fit kernel, a specific phantom-resonance prediction for a given correlated initial state, and a protocol that distinguishes the force-fit artifact from genuine drive/gain physics.
The author’s experimental background in THz spectroscopy provides a natural pathway.

Mathematical refinements.

Several open problems remain at the formal level.
The operator-valued Titchmarsh converse – if a memory kernel satisfies KK, must it be causal and Hardy? – has no known analogue of the scalar Titchmarsh theorem and may fail if operator-valued Hilbert transforms have nontrivial kernels.
The sharpness of the Carleman condition for the MKCT moment problem is unsettled: for unbounded Hamiltonians (bosonic baths in the UV), the condition may become a genuine constraint rather than an automatic diagnostic.
Multi-time KK relations, suggested by Breuer’s extension of the NZ formalism to multitime correlation functions
7
, would connect the present single-time framework to nonlinear response theory and two-dimensional spectroscopy.

Non-Hermitian regime.

Extension to structured baths (multi-peak spectral densities, band-gap environments) tests the framework where Padé reconstructions are most needed.
Non-equilibrium baths – driven, squeezed, gain media – form the natural frontier: gain-induced poles appear in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
, and the Blaschke product
B(z)=\prod_{k}(z-\bar{z}_{k})/(z-z_{k})
factors them out, yielding
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)=B(z)\,\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{passive}}(z)
where
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{passive}}
satisfies standard KK with explicit residue corrections.
The Blaschke winding number, counting UHP poles of the gain-corrected kernel, naturally labels the robust/fragile transition at the unitary/non-unitary boundary and connects the present framework to active photonic environments,
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric dimers, and Floquet-engineered baths.

Appendix A
Supplement to Theorem
5
: implicit-function and Vieta viewpoints

Theorem
5
gives two independent arguments for the propagation of a UHP kernel pole into a UHP propagator pole. Here we collect the two viewpoints for comparison.

Perturbative local viewpoint.

Write
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)=R/(z-z_{0})+\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(z)
and define
F(z)=(z-z_{0})(z-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(z))
.
If
A_{0}=z_{0}-\mathcal{L}_{s}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}(z_{0})
is invertible, the local denominator zeros are obtained from

\det[(z-z_{0})A_{0}-R+O((z-z_{0})^{2})]=0
.
When the small pencil
A_{0}^{-1}R
is diagonalizable, their first-order locations are

\zeta_{j}=z_{0}+\mu_{j}+O(\|R\|^{2}),\qquad\mu_{j}\in\operatorname{spec}(A_{0}^{-1}R),

(20)

so all
d
roots remain in the upper half-plane for sufficiently small
\|R\|
compared with
\operatorname{Im}z_{0}
.
Without this diagonalizability assumption the local root splitting can be non-analytic in
R
, but the Rouché count in Part (i) of the theorem still gives the robust statement needed here.

Vieta (global) viewpoint.

For the rational kernel
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(z)=R/(z-z_{0})+P(z)/q(z)
, the propagator poles satisfy
\det M(z)=0
with
M(z)=(z-z_{0})(zq(z)-q(z)\mathcal{L}_{s}-P(z))-R\,q(z)
.
The sum of all zeros of the scalar polynomial
\det M(z)
equals
d(z_{0}-q_{m-1})+\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{L}_{s})
.
When the rational representation is reduced and no numerator/adjugate cancellation removes the UHP zeros carrying this imaginary-part budget, the positive contribution
d\,\operatorname{Im}z_{0}
forces at least one propagator pole in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
.
This is the generic non-perturbative statement of Part (ii); degenerate Padé pencils should be checked directly.

No-cancellation anchor example (
d=2
,
m=0
).

To see that the no-cancellation hypotheses are non-vacuous, take the simplest non-trivial instance of Theorem
5
.
Fix a qubit system with
\mathcal{L}_{s}=\mathrm{diag}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
in the Hilbert–Schmidt basis (
\omega_{1,2}\in\mathbb{R}
), set
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}=0
(so
P\equiv 0
,
q\equiv 1
,
m=0
), and take a scalar residue
R=r\,I_{2}
with
r>0
at
z_{0}=i\gamma
,
\gamma>0
.
Then

\displaystyle M(z)

\displaystyle=(z-i\gamma)(zI-\mathcal{L}_{s})-rI,

\displaystyle\det M(z)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{2}\bigl[(z-i\gamma)(z-\omega_{k})-r\bigr].

and the four propagator-pole candidates are

z_{k}^{\pm}=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\omega_{k}+i\gamma\pm\sqrt{(\omega_{k}-i\gamma)^{2}+4r}\bigr),\qquad k=1,2.

Summing gives
\sum_{k,\pm}z_{k}^{\pm}=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+2i\gamma=\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{L}_{s})+d\,z_{0}
, consistent with Eq. (
10
) (with the convention that the
q_{m-1}
term is absent for constant
q
).
Each quadratic factor has discriminant with strictly positive imaginary part, so its two roots carry total imaginary part
\gamma
, giving
\sum_{k,\pm}\operatorname{Im}z_{k}^{\pm}=2\gamma=d\,\operatorname{Im}(z_{0})>0
; hence at least one root of each factor lies in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
, and the global Vieta inequality (
11
) is saturated by genuinely distinct propagator poles.
Here
R=rI_{2}
is manifestly non-singular,
q
has no real root to be cancelled, and
\mathrm{adj}(M(z))
is already diagonal and generically non-zero at the
z_{k}^{\pm}
, so the denominator-root and adjugate no-cancellation hypotheses hold automatically.
Perturbing this anchor by an
O(\epsilon)
generic
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{reg}}
preserves non-cancellation by continuity, giving a Lebesgue-positive-measure set of input data for which Theorem
5
(ii) applies without degeneracy.

Appendix B
Supplement to Theorem
7
: why passivity is only a compatibility condition

Passivity alone is not used here as an independent proof of Hardy membership.
The reason is simple: a Herglotz-Nevanlinna representation controls the sign of the imaginary part, but by itself it does not give the global
L^{p}
boundary density required in Theorem
2
.
Moreover, convergence of the Nevanlinna integral at one point does not imply that the representing measure has finite total mass.

Under the real-axis representation (
6
), however, the situation is clean.
For every Hilbert-Schmidt vector
\xi
,

\operatorname{Im}\langle\!\langle\xi,\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(x+iy)\xi\rangle\!\rangle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{y}{(\lambda-x)^{2}+y^{2}}\langle\!\langle\xi,w(\lambda)\xi\rangle\!\rangle\,d\lambda.

Thus positivity of the coupling-weighted density implies the anti-Herglotz sign condition (
12
).
The
H^{p}
bound and the KK relations are still supplied by the
L^{p}
assumption in Theorem
2
.

Appendix C
Numerical verification of the projected generator spectrum

This appendix reports the numerical procedure used to test whether the projected generator
Q\mathcal{L}Q
has purely real spectrum for the standard Nakajima–Zwanzig partial-trace projection.
The results justify the spectral hypothesis invoked in Theorem
2
for the Jaynes–Cummings model used in the main-text numerics, and identify a counterexample where the hypothesis fails.

C.1
Matrix representation of the Liouvillian and the NZ projection

For a finite-dimensional total Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{s}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{b}
with
\dim\mathcal{H}_{s}=d_{s}
and
\dim\mathcal{H}_{b}=d_{b}
, the Hamiltonian is a
d\times d
Hermitian matrix (
d=d_{s}d_{b}
).
The Liouvillian superoperator
\mathcal{L}\rho=[\hat{H},\rho]
is represented as a
d^{2}\times d^{2}
matrix in the vectorization (column-stacking) basis:

L_{\text{mat}}=\mathbbm{1}_{d}\otimes\hat{H}-\hat{H}^{T}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{d}.

(21)

Cyclicality of trace guarantees
L_{\text{mat}}^{\dagger}=L_{\text{mat}}
in the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product.

The standard NZ projection superoperator
P
acts as
(P\rho)_{\alpha n,\beta m}=\rho_{b,nm}^{\text{ref}}\sum_{k}\rho_{\alpha k,\beta k}
.
In the same vectorized basis its matrix elements are

P_{(\alpha n)(\beta m),(\alpha^{\prime}k)(\beta^{\prime}k^{\prime})}=\delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\delta_{\beta\beta^{\prime}}\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\,\rho_{b,nm}^{\text{ref}},

(22)

where Greek indices label system states (
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq d_{s}
) and Latin indices label bath states (
1\leq n,m,k\leq d_{b}
).
This projection satisfies
P^{2}=P
, but
P\neq P^{\dagger}
unless
\rho_{b}^{\text{ref}}\propto\mathbbm{1}_{d_{b}}
.
Its complement is
Q=\mathbbm{1}_{d^{2}}-P
.

The projected generator is then the
d^{2}\times d^{2}
matrix

Q\mathcal{L}Q\;\longleftrightarrow\;Q_{\text{mat}}\,L_{\text{mat}}\,Q_{\text{mat}}.

(23)

Because
Q_{\text{mat}}\neq Q_{\text{mat}}^{\dagger}
in general, this matrix need not be Hermitian even though
L_{\text{mat}}
is.

C.2
Algorithm

The numerical test proceeds as follows.

1.

Build
\hat{H}
for the chosen model and truncation.

2.

Construct
L_{\text{mat}}
via Eq. (
21
).

3.

Choose a bath reference state
\rho_{b}^{\text{ref}}
(vacuum, thermal, or other).

4.

Construct
P_{\text{mat}}
via Eq. (
22
) and verify
P_{\text{mat}}^{2}=P_{\text{mat}}
to machine precision.

5.

Form
Q_{\text{mat}}=\mathbbm{1}_{d^{2}}-P_{\text{mat}}
and compute
M=Q_{\text{mat}}L_{\text{mat}}Q_{\text{mat}}
.

6.

Verify non-orthogonality:
\|P_{\text{mat}}-P_{\text{mat}}^{\dagger}\|_{F}>10^{-10}
.

7.

Compute all eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{j}\}
of
M
using standard dense eigensolvers.

8.

Report
\max_{j}|\operatorname{Im}(\lambda_{j})|
.

A threshold of
10^{-10}
is used to distinguish genuine complex eigenvalues from machine-precision noise; for the matrices tested (
d^{2}\leq 42^{2}=1764
), dense eigensolvers give imaginary parts below
10^{-14}
when the spectrum is truly real.

Implications

These numerical tests support the conditional formulation adopted in the main text.
For the Jaynes–Cummings model — the setting of Fig.
2
and Sec.
VI
— the real-spectrum part of the spectral hypothesis is satisfied in the tested finite truncations; strict Hardy membership still requires the continuous-density assumption stated in Theorem
2
.
For the spin-boson model at moderate-to-strong coupling, the hypothesis fails, and Theorem
2
does not apply without additional structural assumptions.
This model-dependence underscores why the theorems are stated as
sufficient conditions
rather than universal consequences of unitarity alone.

Appendix D
Supplement to Theorem
8
: Carleman condition for standard baths

For a Drude-Lorentz bath
J(\omega)=2\lambda\gamma\omega/(\omega^{2}+\gamma^{2})
, the projected moments satisfy
\|\bm{\Omega}_{2n}\|\sim\gamma^{2n}
, so
\|\bm{\Omega}_{2n}\|^{-1/(2n)}\to 1/\gamma>0
and the Carleman series in (
15
) diverges linearly: the criterion is satisfied with margin.
For an Ohmic bath
J(\omega)\propto\omega e^{-\omega/\omega_{c}}
, the cumulants grow as
\mu_{2n}\sim(2n-1)!\,\omega_{c}^{2n}
, so
\|\bm{\Omega}_{2n}\|^{-1/(2n)}\sim 1/(\omega_{c}\,n)
(by Stirling’s approximation) and the partial sum
\sum_{n\leq N}\|\bm{\Omega}_{2n}\|^{-1/(2n)}\sim\ln(N)/\omega_{c}
grows without bound — the Carleman condition is marginally satisfied.
For a sub-Ohmic bath
J(\omega)\propto\omega^{s}e^{-\omega/\omega_{c}}
with
0<s<1
, moments grow as
\Gamma(2n+s)
, so the partial sum grows only as
\log N
.
The Carleman series is therefore
marginally
divergent in the asymptotic sense; in practical MKCT pipelines with
N_{\mathrm{max}}\lesssim 20
, distinguishing marginal divergence from genuine convergence requires a separate convergence study, and the criterion should be treated as a binding constraint rather than an automatic one.
In all three cases the finite-truncation Carleman diagnostic of Theorem
8
applies only after the total Hamiltonian has been reduced to a bounded finite matrix; operator-valued determinacy in infinite dimension would require the extension of
13
.
