Comparison of Venetoclax‐Based Treatments for Patients With Relapsed/Refractory Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia: A Single‐Center Real‐World Experience
Authors: Benjamin Heyman, Michael Choi, Thomas J. Kipps
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroductionThe optimal venetoclax‐based treatment for patients with relapsed/refractory (R/R) CLL is still unknown, especially for high‐risk patients.MethodsWe performed a retrospective analysis of 98 patients with R/R CLL treated with venetoclax‐based regimens. Patients received venetoclax alone or in combination with rituximab (VenR), obinutuzumab (VenO), or a BTKi (primarily ibrutinib).ResultsCombination groups achieved higher complete remission (CR) and uMRD rates: VenO (CR 68%, uMRD 86%), VenR (CR 63%, uMRD 82%), Ven + BTKi (CR 43%, uMRD 70%), versus monotherapy (CR 51%, uMRD 67%). At a median 52‐month follow‐up, median PFS was superior in combination arms (76.6 months) compared to monotherapy (58.9 months, p = 0.04).ConclusionThis analysis validates, in a real‐world academic cohort, that venetoclax‐based combination therapy, particularly with obinutuzumab or BTKi, yields higher rates of deep remission and superior PFS compared to monotherapy for R/R CLL.

Targeted therapies have transformed outcomes in chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), with venetoclax‐based regimens offering deep responses and durable remissions in relapsed/refractory (R/R) settings [1, 2]. However, optimal regimen choices—particularly among combinations with anti‐CD20 antibodies or BTK inhibitors—remain a subject of ongoing investigation, especially for heavily pre‐treated and high‐risk patients [3, 4]. This study aims to clarify real‐world outcomes with venetoclax monotherapy and commonly used combinations in a diverse institutional cohort, emphasizing MRD response and clinical durability.

Ninety‐eight patients with R/R CLL treated at UC San Diego between 2012 and 2024 with venetoclax‐based regimens were retrospectively included. Patients received venetoclax alone or in combination with rituximab (VenR), obinutuzumab (VenO), or a BTKi (primarily ibrutinib). Venetoclax was administered using a 5‐week ramp‐up to 400 mg daily; combination agents were dosed per established protocols.

Patients who received continuous venetoclax beyond 24 months were excluded from “end of treatment” MRD analyses. MRD was quantified in peripheral blood by flow cytometry (sensitivity 10− 4), at EOT and 12 months post‐EOT. Responses were evaluated by iwCLL criteria [5]. Progression‐free survival (PFS) was calculated from therapy initiation using the Kaplan–Meier method; subgroup comparisons used the log‐rank test [6]. Multivariable Cox models assessed the impact of cytogenetic risk and MRD status. Cases with missing key data points were excluded from relevant analyses. Institutional review board approval was obtained (HRPP#171884).

The median patient age was 68 (range, 46–89); most had advanced Rai stage, with a median of four prior therapies (range, 1–14) and 63% previously exposed to BTKi. High‐risk features included del(17p)/TP53 mutation in 24.7% and complex karyotype in 33%. (Table 1)

Patient disposition.

Treatment breakdown: venetoclax monotherapy (43.9%), VenR (19.4%), VenO (25.5%), and venetoclax + BTKi (11.2%). The median venetoclax duration was 16.3 months. At analysis, 14.3% were receiving ongoing therapy. Progression occurred in 39.2%, and 15.3% developed Richter's transformation. (Table 1)

Combination groups achieved higher complete remission (CR) and uMRD rates: VenO (CR 68%, uMRD 86%), VenR (CR 63%, uMRD 82%), Ven + BTKi (CR 43%, uMRD 70%), versus monotherapy (CR 51%, uMRD 67%). At a median 52‐month follow‐up, median PFS was superior in combination arms (76.6 months) compared to monotherapy (58.9 months, p = 0.04). Presence of del(17p)/TP53 mutation and detectable MRD at 12 months post‐EOT were each associated with significantly inferior PFS (median 39.7 vs. 118 months, and 42.8 months vs. not reached, respectively). This analysis validates, in a real‐world academic cohort, that venetoclax‐based combination therapy, particularly with obinutuzumab or BTKi, yields higher rates of deep remission and superior PFS compared to monotherapy for R/R CLL [7, 8, 9, 10]. Our population was notably high‐risk, with broad prior therapy exposure and a substantial proportion carrying adverse genetic features. The strong association between sustained uMRD and prolonged remission supports MRD as a valuable prognostic tool in practice.

Despite small subgroup sizes and retrospective design, our results align with established trial findings, confirming that high‐risk biology and lack of uMRD at end‐of‐treatment predict shorter remissions [11, 12]. These findings underscore the importance of individualized regimen selection and vigilant MRD monitoring post‐venetoclax. Finally, the observed effectiveness of shorter‐duration venetoclax plus obinutuzumab with lower toxicity risk has direct clinical application. Prospective studies are warranted to refine optimal strategies by disease risk and prior therapy.

Limitations include single‐institution and retrospective design, modest subgroup size—especially for venetoclax + BTKi—and possible selection bias. Outcome generalizability may be affected by real‐world patient heterogeneity.

Venetoclax‐based combinations, particularly with obinutuzumab or BTKi, provide superior depth and duration of response compared to monotherapy in R/R CLL in a real‐world population. MRD assessment should guide risk‐adapted monitoring and management.
