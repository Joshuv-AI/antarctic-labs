On Drinfeld’s representability theorem This work was supported by the Morningside Center of Mathematics, Chinese Academy of Sciences.
Arnaud Vanhaecke

Abstract
In the seventies, V. G. Drinfeld proved that a moduli problem of deformations by quasi-isogenies of certain p p -divisible groups with extra actions is representable by an explicit semi-stable model of the p p -adic symmetric space. This theorem, known as Drinfeld’s representability theorem , has been one of the cornerstones of geometric aspects in p p -adic Hodge theory. The purpose of these notes is twofold. On the one hand we give a new and more transparent proof of Drinfeld’s representability theorem; on the other hand, we give a detailed presentation of Drinfeld’s moduli space and the formal model of the p p -adic symmetric space.

The main issue is that critical indices don’t always exist over arbitrary objects in

The local existence of critical indices allows us to write:

{\rm{M}}_{k}^{\bar{\eta}}(R)\coloneqq\{(X,\rho)\in{\rm{M}}_{k}^{{\rm pf}}(R)\mid\text{$i\in{\symAMSb Z}/d$ is critical and $E^{\times}\cdot\eta_{X}^{i}=\bar{\eta}$ through \eqref{eq:intinclat}}\}.

Without more details, let us mention that this isomorphisms turns out to be

{\rm{O}}^{\prime}\subset E^{\prime}
be its integer ring. The strategy is to show that if the theorem is true for
{\rm{O}}
then it is true for
{\rm{O}}^{\prime}
, which is enough since it is true for
{\rm{O}}={\symAMSb Z}_{p}
by the references.

If
E^{\prime}/E
is unramified of degree
f
, then decomposing along the
E

as in (

), we get

W_{{\rm{O}}}(R)\otimes_{{\rm{O}}}{\rm{O}}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i\in{\symAMSb Z}/f}W_{{\rm{O}}^{\prime}}(R)_{i}.

Hence
M_{{\rm{O}}}(G)\otimes_{{\rm{O}}}{\rm{O}}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i\in{\symAMSb Z}/f}M_{i}
such that
F
is of degree
-1
and
V
is of degree
1
. Moreover,
V\colon M_{i}\rightarrow M_{i+1}
is an isomorphism if

by the strictness condition. Set
M_{{\rm{O}}^{\prime}}(G)\coloneqq M_{0}
and define

V^{\prime}\coloneqq V^{f},\quad F^{\prime}=V^{1-f}F.

It is clear that this defines an equivalence of categories. Finally,

M^{\prime}/V^{\prime}M^{\prime}=M/VM={\rm Lie}(G)

which concludes the proof in that case.

Suppose that
E^{\prime}/E
is totally ramified. The hypothesis that
R
is perfect will simplify the proof since
W_{{\rm{O}}^{\prime}}(R)\cong W_{{\rm{O}}}(R)\otimes_{{\rm{O}}}{\rm{O}}^{\prime}
such that
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}
and
\tau^{\prime}=\tfrac{\varpi^{\prime}}{\varpi}\tau
. Hence
M_{{\rm{O}}^{\prime}}(G)\coloneqq M_{{\rm{O}}}(G)
is naturally a
W_{{\rm{O}}^{\prime}}(R)
-module and

F^{\prime}\coloneqq\tfrac{\varpi^{\prime}}{\varpi}F,\quad V^{\prime}\coloneqq V,

which defines a
{\symAMSb E}_{{\rm{O}}^{\prime}}(R)
-module. Finally, since
M_{{\rm{O}}}(G)
is reduced,

M_{{\rm{O}}^{\prime}}(G)/V^{\prime}M_{{\rm{O}}^{\prime}}(G)=M_{{\rm{O}}}(G)/VM_{{\rm{O}}}(G).

Let us mention that another reference for formal
{\rm{O}}
-modules is
[
haz
, 26.3]
. Finally, let us mention that the theory of
{\rm{O}}
-displays in
[

]
is developed based on the proof we sketched.
∎

We recall the compatibility between Cartier theory and Dieudonné theory over an algebraically closed field

of characteristic
p
. If

In the case

\remaname

\the\smf@thm
.

Recall that if

In this paragraph we recall Grothendieck–Messing theory for

-modules as presented by Fargues
[

[

[

in the context of

Let
\Sigma\coloneqq\Spec({\rm{O}})
and
S
be a scheme over
\Sigma
. We endow

with its
{\rm{O}}
-divided power structure
coming from
\varpi\in{\rm{O}}

(

[

{\rm{O}}
-crystalline nilpotent site of

with respect to the
{\rm{O}}
-divided power structure. This site comes with a structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{S/\Sigma}
. We recall that a

\mathcal{O}_{S/\Sigma}
-modules
is a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
{\rm NCRIS}_{{\rm{O}}}(S/\Sigma)
such that for any map

(2.5)

is an isomorphism.

Let
G
be a
\varpi
-divisible
{\rm{O}}
-module on

. Then one can define a universel vector extension (
cf.

[

, Proposition B.3.3]
and see
[

, Proposition 3.16]

-modules)

(2.6)

which is an exact sequence of

sheaves in
{\rm{O}}
-modules over

. This exact sequence is functorial in

(

[

, Chapter IV, Proposition 1.15]
). We set

{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(G)_{S}\coloneqq{\rm Lie}(E_{{\rm{O}}}(G))

{\rm{O}}
-modules which defines a sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-modules. In particular, by taking the Lie algebra of the sequence (

), we get a map:

Recall that this map is surjective as a morphism of
fppf
-sheaves on

(
cf.

[

, Chapter IV, Proposition 1.22]
). The kernel of

is usually called

and we will say that

is induced by the Hodge filtration. The map

is functorial in both
S
and

. The main proposition is that
{\symAMSb D}(G)_{S}
extends to a crystal on
{\rm NCRIS}_{{\rm{O}}}(S/\Sigma)
:

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

The Lie algebra of
E_{{\rm{O}}}(G)
defines a crystal of
\mathcal{O}_{S/\Sigma}
-modules
{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(G)
, which we will call the

.

Proof.

The main idea is to associate, to every
(U,T,\delta)\in{\rm NCRIS}_{{\rm{O}}}(S/\Sigma)
and to any lift
G^{\prime}
to
T
of
{\left.\kern-1.2ptG\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{U}}
the sheaf

{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(G)_{(U\hookrightarrow T)}={\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(G^{\prime})_{T}

(2.7)

and to show that this defines a crystal. See
[
mes
, Chapter IV, §2]
for the case
{\rm{O}}={\symAMSb Z}_{p}
and
[
far
, Théorème B.6.1]
for the extension to
\varpi
-divisible
{\rm{O}}
-modules; compare with
[
acz
, Definition 3.24]
for the case of formal
{\rm{O}}
-modules through
{\rm{O}}
-displays.
∎

\remaname

\the\smf@thm
.

If
S=\Spec R
with
p\cdot R=0
and
R^{\prime}\rightarrow R
satisfies
R^{\prime}=\varprojlim_{n}R^{\prime}/p^{n}
and
R^{\prime}/p^{n}
is a finite
R
-algebra, we would like to evaluate
{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(G)
at
R^{\prime}
. We will consider

{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(G)_{(R^{\prime}\rightarrow R)}\coloneqq\varprojlim_{n}{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(G)_{(R^{\prime}/p^{n}\rightarrow R)},

which is consistent with
[
dej
]
(see also
[
scwe
, Remark 3.2.4]
).

Following
[

]
(where
{\rm{O}}={\symAMSb Z}_{p}
) the (contravariant) Dieudonné crystal can be defined as

{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}^{*}(G)\coloneqq{\mathcal{E}xt}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{S/\Sigma}}(G,\mathcal{O}_{S/\Sigma}).

This definition is not the classical one (for example Messing’s original book
[
mes
]
is used in
[
razi
]
and
[
scwe
]
) but it is more convenient for different reasons. First, it can be defined on the full crystalline site and not only the nilpotent one. Second, this definition makes sense for finite flat group schemes, and behaves well with respect to truncations for finite group schemes (which is also an advantage of working contravariantly). The functor
{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}^{*}(G)
can be related to
{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(G)
by duality (
i.e.
Cartier duality using a Lubin-Tate group).

Let
(S\hookrightarrow T)\in{\rm NCRIS}_{{\rm{O}}}(S/\Sigma)
. We define the following categories:

\bullet

\mathcal{D}
the category of pairs
(G,\mathcal{F})
where

is a
\varpi
-divisible
{\rm{O}}
-module over

and
\mathcal{F}\subset{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(G)_{(S\hookrightarrow T)}

{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(G)_{(S\hookrightarrow T)}
such that
\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{T}}\mathcal{O}_{S}=V_{{\rm{O}}}(G)
,

\bullet

\mathcal{C}
the category of
\varpi
-divisible
{\rm{O}}
-modules over

.

Recall that if

is a
\varpi
-divisible
{\rm{O}}
-module over

, then by definition (

)

{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(G^{\prime})_{T}={\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(G^{\prime}\times_{T}S)_{(S\hookrightarrow T)}.

We get the following theorem which is called the

[

, Chapter V, Theorem 1.6]
for the case

[

\theoname

\the\smf@thm
.

The functor

Finally, we need to compare the Dieudonné crystal with the classical Dieudonné module. The resulting corollary is the following (

[

and Remark

):

\coroname

\the\smf@thm

Let

-divisible

2.1.4
Classification of SFD over perfect rings

We extend the equivalence of Proposition
2.1.2
to special formal
{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}
-modules. For this, we first introduce special Cartier modules.

\definame

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
R\in{\rm Nilp}_{\breve{{\rm{O}}}}
. A
special Cartier
R
-module
is a pair
M=(M,\Pi)
where:

\bullet

M
is a
{\symAMSb Z}/d
-graded reduced Cartier
R
-module,
i.e.
a Cartier
R
-module endowed with a
{\symAMSb Z}/d
-grading

M=\bigoplus_{i\in{\symAMSb Z}/d}M_{i},

such that
\deg F=-1
,
\deg V=1
and
\deg[a]=0
for
a\in R
,

\bullet

\Pi
is a
{\symAMSb E}_{{\rm{O}}}(R)
-linear endomorphism
\Pi\colon M\rightarrow M
such that
\deg\Pi=1
and
\Pi^{d}=\varpi
,

such that for every
i\in{\symAMSb Z}/d
,
M_{i}/VM_{i-1}
is free of rank
1
over
R
.

Moreover, we call
i\in{\symAMSb Z}/d
a
critical index
if the map

\Pi_{i}\colon M_{i}/VM_{i-1}\rightarrow M_{i+1}/VM_{i},

induced by
\Pi
, is zero.

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
R
be a perfect
k
-algebra and let
M
be a special Cartier
R
-module. Then
\dim M=d
and
\heig_{{\rm{O}}}M
is a multiple of
d^{2}
.

Proof.

The claim on the dimension is clear since

\dim M=\rank_{R}M/VM=\sum_{i\in{\symAMSb Z}/d}\rank_{R}M_{i}/VM_{i-1}=d.

First, since
V
is injective and commutes with
\varpi
, we get that
V\colon M_{i}/\varpi M_{i}\rightarrow M_{i+1}/\varpi M_{i+1}
is injective, thus
r\coloneqq\rank_{R}M_{i}/\varpi M_{i}
is independent of
i
and
\rank_{R}M/\varpi M=dr
. Moreover, because
R
is perfect,
M
has no
\varpi
-torsion: thus
\Pi
is injective, and since

\rank_{R}M_{i}/\varpi M_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\rank_{R}\Pi^{j}M_{i-j}/\Pi^{j+1}M_{i-j-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\rank_{R}M_{i-j}/\Pi M_{i-j-1}.

Thus it remains to prove that
\rank_{R}M_{i}/\Pi M_{i-1}
is independent of
i
. Computing the rank of
M_{i}/\Pi VM_{i-2}
in two different ways, we get

\rank_{R}M_{i}/VM_{i-1}+\rank_{R}VM_{i-1}/\Pi VM_{i-2}=\rank_{R}M_{i}/\Pi M_{i-1}+\rank_{R}\Pi M_{i-1}/\Pi VM_{i-2},

and since

\begin{gathered}\rank_{R}M_{i}/VM_{i-1}=\rank_{R}\Pi M_{i-1}/\Pi VM_{i-2}=1,\\
\rank_{R}VM_{i-1}/\Pi VM_{i-2}=\rank_{R}M_{i-1}/\Pi M_{i-2},\end{gathered}

we get
\rank_{R}M_{i}/\Pi M_{i-1}=\rank_{R}M_{i-1}/\Pi M_{i-2}
.
∎

Let
R\in{\rm Nilp}_{\breve{{\rm{O}}}}
, and let
X
be a special formal
{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}
-module over
R
, we associate to
X
a special Cartier
R
-module. Let
M=M_{{\rm{O}}}(X)
be its associated reduced Cartier
R
-module. By functoriality,

is an
{\rm{O}}_{d}\otimes_{{\rm{O}}}{\rm{W}}_{{\rm{O}}}(R)
-module and by (

):

{\rm{O}}_{d}\otimes_{{\rm{O}}}{\rm{W}}_{{\rm{O}}}(R)\cong{\rm{W}}_{{\rm{O}}}(R_{d})\cong\prod_{i\in{\symAMSb Z}/d}{\rm{W}}_{{\rm{O}}}(R_{i}),

and we obtain a
{\symAMSb Z}/d
-grading

M=\bigoplus_{i\in{\symAMSb Z}/d}M_{i},\quad\text{where }M_{i}\coloneqq M\otimes_{{\rm{W}}_{{\rm{O}}}(R_{d})}{\rm{W}}_{{\rm{O}}}(R_{i}).

For this grading
\deg F=-1
,
\deg V=1
, and for
a\in R
,
\deg[a]=0
. Moreover, for
i\in{\symAMSb Z}/d
,

M_{i}/VM_{i-1}\cong{\rm Lie}(X)_{i},

which is locally free of rank
1
over
R
by the special condition. By functoriality,
\Pi\in{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}
defines and endomorphism
\Pi\colon M\rightarrow M
, such that
\Pi^{d}=\varpi
and
\deg\Pi=1
. From the grading and the endomorphism

{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}
on

. We thus obtain the following proposition:

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
R\in{\rm Nilp}_{\breve{{\rm{O}}}}
. The previous construction gives an equivalence of categories

\left\{\text{Special formal ${\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}$-modules over $R$}\right\}\xrightarrow{M_{{\rm{O}}}}\left\{\text{Special Cartier $R$-modules}\right\}.

Proof.

Following the proof of Proposition
2.1.2
in the unramified case, we see that the action of
{\rm{O}}_{d}
can be recovered from the grading and the action of
{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}
is then obtained from the action of
\Pi
. See
[
boca
, Chapitre II, section 2, Théorème]
for

, the proof adapts easily for arbitrary

.
∎

For the rest of this paragraph, we suppose
R=\kappa
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
.

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
M
be a special Cartier
\kappa
-module of height
d^{2}
. Then
M
admits a critical index and for every critical index
i\in{\symAMSb Z}/d
we get
\Pi M_{i}=VM_{i}
.

Proof.

Note that the situation is the same as in Lemma
2.1.1
and we get the existence of a critical index

by the same proof, which means that
\Pi M_{i}\subset VM_{i}
. Using Lemma
2.1.4
we see that
\dim_{\kappa}M_{i+1}/\Pi M_{i}=1

An essential construction of special Cartier modules is the following: let
M
be a special Cartier

and let

we know that
\Pi M_{i}=VM_{i}

which is a

is of slope

. Note that the association

\coroname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
\kappa
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
.

\bullet

There exists, up to isogeny, a unique special formal
{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}

.

\bullet

X
a special formal
{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}
-module of height

. This gives the uniqueness of

2.1.5
Grothendieck–Messing theory of SFD

As in the case of Cartier modules, the Dieudonné crystal of an SFD is endowed with an action of
{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}
, which can be translated into a grading and the action of a degree
1
endomorphism
\Pi
.
.

Let
\breve{\Sigma}=\Spec\breve{{\rm{O}}}
and
S
be a scheme over
\breve{\Sigma}
. Let
X
be a special formal
{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}
-module and let
{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(X)
be its Dieudonné crystal, which is endowed with an action of
{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}
. Note that
S\otimes_{{\rm{O}}}{\rm{O}}_{d}=\sqcup_{i\in{\symAMSb Z}/d}S_{i}
. Thus, for
\mathcal{O}_{S/\breve{\Sigma}}
, the structure sheaf of the crystalline site
{\rm NCRIS}_{{\rm{O}}}(S/\breve{\Sigma})
, we get

\mathcal{O}_{S/\breve{\Sigma}}\otimes_{{\rm{O}}}{\rm{O}}_{d}=\bigoplus_{i\in{\symAMSb Z}/d}\mathcal{O}_{S_{i}/\breve{\Sigma}}.

This defines a
{\symAMSb Z}/d
-grading
{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(X)=\bigoplus_{i\in{\symAMSb Z}/d}{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(X)_{i}
as before. Moreover,
\Pi\in{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}
defines a map
\Pi\colon{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(X)\rightarrow{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(X)
which is of degree
+1
and satisfies
\Pi^{d}=\varpi
. As before, the grading together with
\Pi
determines the action of
{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}
.

Since the map
h_{X}\colon{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(X)\rightarrow{\rm Lie}(X)
induced by the Hodge filtration, is functorial in
X
, it preserves the grading and we obtain

h_{X}=\sum_{i\in{\symAMSb Z}/d}h_{X,i}\colon\bigoplus_{i\in{\symAMSb Z}/d}{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(X)_{i}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i\in{\symAMSb Z}/d}{\rm Lie}(X)_{i}.

(2.8)

For any
i\in{\symAMSb Z}/d
,
h_{X,i+1}\circ\Pi_{i}=\Pi_{i}\circ h_{X,i}
.

Let us spell out the Grothendieck–Messing theorem in this case. We define

\bullet

\mathcal{D}_{{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}}
the category of pairs
(X,\mathcal{F})
where
X
is a special formal
{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}
-module over
S
and
\mathcal{F}\subset{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(X)_{(S\hookrightarrow T)}
is an
{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}
-stable direct factor of
{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(X)_{S\hookrightarrow T}
such that
\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{T}}\mathcal{O}_{S}=V_{{\rm{O}}}(X)
,

\bullet

\mathcal{C}_{{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}}
the category of special formal
{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}
-modules over
T
.

\coroname

\the\smf@thm
.

The functor
\mathcal{C}_{{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}}
defined on objects by

X^{\prime}(X^{\prime}\times_{T}S,V_{{\rm{O}}}(X^{\prime})\subset{\symAMSb D}_{{\rm{O}}}(X^{\prime})_{(S\hookrightarrow T)}),

defines an equivalence of categories.

We do not spell out the analogues of the previous propositions for special formal
{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}
-modules. Let us just note that from Proposition
2.1.4
and Corollary
2.1.3
, if
X
is a special formal module
{\rm{O}}_{{\rm{D}}}
