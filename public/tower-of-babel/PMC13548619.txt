Sex differences in peritoneal dialysis duration and management among Japanese patients: a nationwide registry analysis
Authors: Yusuke Watanabe, Koji Tomori, Tsutomu Inoue, Hirokazu Okada
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPeritoneal dialysis (PD) duration is limited worldwide by PD-related infections and difficulties in fluid and solute management. Sex-associated differences in PD duration among Japanese patients have not been well characterized. We examined sex-associated differences in clinical characteristics, PD duration, and PD-related outcomes using a nationwide Japanese registry.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed annual aggregated data of adult Japanese patients on PD extracted from the Japanese Society for Dialysis Therapy Renal Data Registry (2014–2023; cumulative 74,274 patients). Because the extract contained annual aggregated statistics rather than individual-level data, analyses were descriptive and based on within-year cross-sectional comparisons. We compared male and female patients regarding PD duration, fluid management, solute clearance, and PD-related infections, and performed diabetes-stratified analyses, a descriptive temporal trend analysis, and body-composition–corrected sensitivity analyses for fluid output indices.ResultsFemale patients consistently had longer mean PD duration than male patients in every year from 2014 to 2023; in 2023, the difference was 4.80 months (95% confidence interval, 3.10 to 6.50), and the 10-year sample size-weighted mean was 28.59 months in male versus 34.73 months in female patients. Female patients also had higher registry-reported total Kt/V (weighted mean, 1.90 vs. 1.76) and a higher total output (urine output + PD ultrafiltration volume) / body weight ratio, calculated as a ratio of annual means (24.0 vs. 21.5 mL/kg/day). The sex difference in PD duration persisted in both diabetic and non-diabetic strata.ConclusionIn this nationwide descriptive analysis, female patients on PD in Japan had longer registry-recorded PD duration and higher registry-reported indices of fluid and solute management than male patients, with differences persisting across diabetes strata. However, because the analyses were based on aggregated registry data with substantial, potentially non-random missingness and without patient-level adjustment, causal inference is not possible, and the findings should be interpreted strictly as registry-level descriptive associations rather than patient-level survival or technique-failure differences.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12882-026-05130-6.

Background

Currently, more than 300,000 patients with end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) are undergoing dialysis therapy in Japan. Of these patients, more than 90% are receiving hemodialysis (HD) and the others are receiving peritoneal dialysis (PD) [1, 2]. However, PD offers several advantages over HD for patients with ESKD, including more gradual and physiologic removal of fluid and solutes, preservation of residual renal function, and a better quality of life [3–6]. Nevertheless, many patients are compelled to discontinue PD within a few years of initiation because of complications such as PD-related infections, inadequate dialysis efficiency, and difficulties controlling the fluid volume. Therefore, these patients are often transitioned to HD or PD/HD hybrid therapy [7, 8].

The Peritoneal Dialysis Outcomes and Practice Patterns Study (PDOPPS) is a large, international, observational cohort study designed to characterize PD practices and outcomes, including time on therapy and transfer to other modalities. The PDOPPS provides valuable insights into real-world PD practices across participating countries. According to PDOPPS data collected from 7121 patients in Australia/New Zealand, Canada, Japan, Thailand, the United Kingdom, and the United States between 2014 and 2017, the overall median PD duration was 2.3 years (interquartile range [IQR], 1.1–4.4 years). During follow-up (median, 1.1 years; IQR, 0.6–1.7 years), 19% of patients transferred to HD or PD/HD hybrid therapy [9]. These results indicate that further extension of the PD duration is desirable in all countries.

The PDOPPS also identified several patient factors associated with an increased rate of transition from PD to HD or PD/HD hybrid therapy, including male sex, body mass index (BMI) ≥ 30 kg/m², diabetes, and psychiatric disorders [9]. In several observational studies conducted in different countries, female patients have shown equal or better PD technique survival and lower PD technique failure rates than male patients, and in those studies the time on PD tended to be shorter for male patients than for female patients [10–12]. A recent sex-specific analysis from the PDOPPS likewise reported that female patients had lower risks of mortality, transfer to HD, and peritonitis than male patients across multiple countries [13]. Subsequent contemporary registry-based studies have also continued to suggest sex-associated differences in PD discontinuation, including a recent Australian cohort study that observed a higher risk of all-cause and inadequate-dialysis-related PD discontinuation in male patients [14].

However, few studies have specifically examined sex differences in the PD duration among Japanese patients, and relationships between the direct causes of PD discontinuation, such as PD-related infections, inadequate solute clearance, and fluid management, and sex remain unclear. Therefore, this study used a nationwide registry to investigate sex-based differences in the clinical characteristics, PD duration, and complications of patients on PD in Japan.

Methods

This retrospective observational study included annual aggregated clinical information of adult Japanese patients (age 20 years or older) on PD obtained from the Japanese Society for Dialysis Therapy (JSDT) Renal Data Registry (JRDR) through the Web-based Analysis of Dialysis Data Archives (WADDA) system (version 2.1). Data were extracted for calendar years 2014 through 2023. Patients receiving PD/HD hybrid therapy were excluded. Details of the JRDR data collection system have been described previously [1]. The JSDT distributes annual questionnaires to all dialysis facilities in Japan, and the facility-level response rate has consistently exceeded 98% [2].

The WADDA system is an automated data extraction platform linked to the JRDR that enables users to generate customized datasets according to specific conditions [15]. Because the registry provides only annual aggregated statistics, it is not possible to distinguish incident from prevalent patients or to identify repeated measurements of the same individual across years. The facility-level response rate (> 98%) and the variable-level data completeness rate are conceptually distinct: the former represents the proportion of dialysis facilities that responded to the JSDT survey, whereas the latter reflects the proportion of completed data fields across the patient-level variables analyzed in this study. In the 2023 dataset, the variable-level data completeness rate was 64.2% overall (63.7% in male patients and 64.9% in female patients), calculated as the proportion of completed data fields across the 25 patient-level variables analyzed (Σ available / [25 × N], where N is the total number of patients in the WADDA extract). Variable-specific completeness rates differed substantially: demographic and primary-disease variables (age, primary kidney disease, and time on dialysis) were essentially complete (≥ 99.9%), and diabetes mellitus was reported as a recorded category for all patients but with 1821 patients (1105 male, 716 female; 23.1% of the 2023 cohort) classified as “unknown” diabetes status by the reporting facility, leaving 6067 patients (3951 male, 2116 female) with a known diabetes status. In contrast, peritoneal equilibration test results, Kt/V values, and other treatment-specific variables had lower completeness (typically 27–69%) because these measurements were performed at only a subset of facilities. The full variable-by-variable completeness profile for the 2023 dataset, including the handling of the “unknown” diabetes category, is provided in Supplementary Table S10; comparable variable-specific completeness patterns were observed in the 2014–2022 surveys.

Variables and definitions

Data regarding clinical variables such as age, sex, primary cause of ESKD, prevalence of diabetes mellitus, use of antihypertensive medications, smoking history, body weight (BW), height, and BMI were extracted. PD-related parameters included PD duration, 4-hour dialysate-to-plasma creatinine ratio determined by the peritoneal equilibration test (PET D/P Cr ratio), dialysis prescription (use of icodextrin), and fluid and solute removal indices (daily PD fluid volume, daily urine output, mean PD ultrafiltration volume per day, residual renal Kt/V, PD Kt/V, and total Kt/V). Data regarding total Kt/V were available for the years 2017 to 2023. Kt/V values were analyzed as registry-reported aggregated values; the JRDR/WADDA system does not enforce a single, prespecified estimation formula for the urea distribution volume (V) across institutions, so V was recorded as estimated by each participating facility, and the present dataset did not allow confirmation of the formula used. The use of icodextrin was analyzed as the registry-reported proportion of patients prescribed icodextrin at the time of the annual survey, and information on the specific clinical indication was not available. Information regarding the PD modality (use of an automated PD system and type of exchange procedure) and PD-related infections (number of peritonitis episodes per year, peritonitis rate, number of exit-site infections per year, and exit-site infection rate) were also collected. Data regarding PD-related infections were available for the years 2017, 2018, 2021, 2022, and 2023.

To evaluate fluid volume management at the aggregated registry level, the urine output per kg of BW, PD ultrafiltration volume per kg of BW, and their sum (total output / BW ratio [mL/kg/day]) were calculated from the corresponding annual mean values. Because the WADDA extract provides only group-level summary statistics rather than individual-patient measurements, these indices represent ratios of annual mean values (i.e., mean output / mean body weight), not the mean of individual patient-level ratios. The total output / BW ratio was therefore treated as a pragmatic exploratory index intended to summarize overall fluid output relative to body weight in patients on PD, rather than as a definitive patient-level physiological measure. To evaluate the sensitivity of this index to the choice of body-composition normalization variable, additional sensitivity analyses were performed in the 2023 cross-sectional dataset using body surface area (BSA) and Watson-estimated total body water (V) as alternative normalization variables. BSA was estimated using the DuBois formula: BSA (m²) = 0.007184 × W^0.425 × H^0.725. V was estimated using the Watson formula: male V (L) = 2.447 − 0.09156 × age + 0.1074 × H + 0.3362 × W; female V (L) = − 2.097 + 0.1069 × H + 0.2466 × W. These sensitivity analyses were also calculated as ratios of annual mean values.

Statistical analysis

The WADDA system provides only anonymized aggregated data. Therefore, we conducted a secondary descriptive analysis of annual aggregated registry data. Continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median and interquartile range (IQR), as appropriate. Sex comparisons within each calendar year were performed using Welch’s t test based on summary statistics (mean, SD, and sample size), and categorical variables were compared using the chi-squared test. Welch’s t test based on summary statistics is well established for comparing two independent groups when only group-level mean, SD, and sample size are available, and is appropriate for unequal variances and large samples [16]. Because the present registry extract provided annual aggregated statistics rather than longitudinal patient-level records, the within-year sex comparisons reported here were treated as independent cross-sectional comparisons within each calendar year; the inability to identify the same individual across years is therefore relevant to the longitudinal interpretation of trends across years rather than to the validity of the within-year between-sex comparisons themselves.

For principal continuous outcomes with available group-specific summary statistics, effect sizes are presented as female-male mean differences with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Sample size-weighted means were calculated as the sum of each annual mean multiplied by the corresponding annual sample size, divided by the sum of the annual sample sizes.

To evaluate the robustness of the observed sex differences against the unequal between-sex distribution of diabetes mellitus, additional stratified analyses according to diabetes status were performed for PD duration. The diabetic and non-diabetic strata were defined according to the WADDA-extracted diabetes status, and Welch’s t test was applied within each stratum for each calendar year (Supplementary Tables S2 and S3). Patients classified as having an “unknown” diabetes status by the reporting facility were excluded from the stratified analyses (in 2023, 1821 of 7888 patients [1105 male, 716 female] were classified as unknown).

To assess temporal trends in PD duration, we performed a descriptive trend analysis of annual mean PD duration separately in male and female patients using inverse-variance weighted linear regression, with calendar year as the independent variable and annual mean PD duration as the dependent variable. The weights were defined as the inverse of the variance of the annual mean (N / SD²).

As a single-year sensitivity analysis to minimize the influence of repeated patient counting across survey years, the principal sex comparisons were repeated using the 2023 cross-sectional dataset alone, with effect sizes and 95% CIs reported (Supplementary Table S5). Body-composition–corrected sensitivity analyses for fluid output indices were performed in the 2023 cross-sectional dataset using BSA and Watson-estimated V, as described in the Variables and Definitions section (Supplementary Table S9).

Because the present study used annual aggregated registry data rather than patient-level longitudinal follow-up, all analyses should be interpreted as descriptive, and not as patient-level survival analyses or causal inferences. P < 0.05 (two-tailed) was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using JMP version 19 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

Large language model–based AI assistants (Claude, Anthropic; and ChatGPT, OpenAI) were used to support language editing, grammar refinement, and improvement of manuscript structure. They were not used for data analysis, interpretation, or drawing conclusions. The authors reviewed, edited, and verified all content and take full responsibility for the accuracy and integrity of the manuscript.

The Medical Ethics Committee of the Japanese Society for Dialysis Therapy approved the use of de-identified aggregated registry data for secondary analyses, and the requirement for separate ethics approval for this specific protocol was waived.

Results

Mean PD duration of female and male patients

At the end of 2023, the mean PD durations were 29.19 ± 26.21 months in male patients (n = 3333 with available PD duration data) and 33.99 ± 32.35 months in female patients (n = 1909). The median PD durations were 22 months (IQR, 10 to 40) in male patients and 26 months (IQR, 11 to 46) in female patients (Table 1). Across 2014 to 2023, female patients consistently had longer mean PD duration than male patients in every year (Fig. 1). The annual female-male difference ranged from 4.28 to 8.01 months, and all yearly comparisons were statistically significant. In 2023, the female-male difference in mean PD duration was 4.80 months (95% CI, 3.10 to 6.50; p < 0.001). The sample size-weighted mean PD duration over the 10-year period was 28.59 months in male patients and 34.73 months in female patients. Median PD duration was also consistently longer in female patients throughout the study period.

In a descriptive inverse-variance weighted trend analysis of annual mean PD duration—interpreted as a registry-level secular trend rather than as individual-level change over time, because the same patients may contribute to multiple survey years—no significant temporal trend was observed in male patients (slope, − 0.12 months/year; 95% CI, − 0.44 to 0.20; p = 0.404), whereas a significant downward trend was observed in female patients (slope, − 0.55 months/year; 95% CI, − 0.93 to − 0.17; p = 0.011). When this trend analysis was repeated within the diabetes-stratified subgroups, the downward trend among female patients was driven primarily by the non-diabetic stratum (slope, − 0.64 months/year; 95% CI, − 1.09 to − 0.20; p = 0.010), whereas no significant temporal trend was observed in female patients in the diabetic stratum (slope, − 0.01; 95% CI, − 0.33 to 0.31; p = 0.941). The corresponding slopes among male patients were not significant in either stratum, although a borderline-significant downward trend was observed in non-diabetic male patients (slope, − 0.33; 95% CI, − 0.67 to 0.01; p = 0.058) (Supplementary Table S4).

Because diabetes mellitus was more prevalent in male patients, we performed additional stratified analyses according to diabetes status. In both strata, female patients consistently had longer mean PD duration than male patients in every year from 2014 to 2023. In the non-diabetic stratum, the female-male difference ranged from 4.16 to 9.02 months, and all yearly comparisons were statistically significant; the sample size-weighted mean PD duration was 30.73 months in male patients and 37.06 months in female patients. In the diabetic stratum, the female-male difference ranged from 2.75 to 6.19 months, and all yearly comparisons were also statistically significant; the corresponding sample size-weighted means were 26.54 and 30.33 months, respectively. Detailed year-by-year comparisons for the overall cohort and diabetes-stratified analyses are provided in Supplementary Tables S1–S3.

Male patients had a higher prevalence of diabetic nephropathy as the primary cause of ESKD and higher prevalences of diabetes mellitus, antihypertensive medication use, and current smoking during 2014 to 2023 (all p < 0.001) (Table 1). The mean BMI of male patients was slightly but significantly higher than that of female patients, whereas the prevalence of obesity (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m²) did not differ substantially by sex. The mean PET D/P Cr ratio in male patients was slightly higher than that in female patients during most years between 2014 and 2023, although no significant differences were observed in 2017 and 2023. In the 2023 single-year sensitivity analysis, PET D/P Cr ratio also remained similar between sexes (0.69 ± 0.14 in male patients vs. 0.68 ± 0.14 in female patients; female-male difference, − 0.01; 95% CI, − 0.021 to 0.001; p = 0.071).

Fluid volume management in female and male patients

Although the absolute urine output and PD ultrafiltration volumes were higher in male patients than in female patients, the body weight-normalized urine output and PD ultrafiltration indices were consistently higher in female patients across all study years. Consequently, the ratio of mean total output (urine output + PD ultrafiltration volume) to mean body weight was also consistently higher in female patients. Over the 10-year period, the sample size-weighted mean total output / body weight ratio was 21.5 mL/kg/day in male patients and 24.0 mL/kg/day in female patients (Fig. 2). It should be emphasized that this index was calculated as a ratio of annual mean values (i.e., mean total output / mean body weight) rather than as the mean of individual patient-level ratios; it is therefore a pragmatic registry-level summary measure rather than a definitive patient-level physiological index, and no inferential statistical test was applied to this aggregated ratio.

To evaluate whether the apparent sex difference in body weight-normalized fluid output was driven by sex-related differences in body composition, we performed body-composition–corrected sensitivity analyses in the 2023 cross-sectional dataset using BSA and Watson-estimated V as alternative normalization variables. The female-to-male ratio of total fluid output remained > 1.0 across all three normalization schemes (BW: 1.115; BSA: 1.042; V: 1.195), although the magnitude of the ratio varied across the normalization schemes (Supplementary Table S9). The direction of the sex difference was therefore consistent across normalization schemes, but its magnitude depended on the choice of normalization variable.

The proportion of male patients who used icodextrin solution was consistently higher than that of female patients throughout the study period. The annual difference in use ranged from approximately 2% to 6%, and most yearly comparisons were statistically significant; in the 2023 cross-sectional dataset, icodextrin was used by 53.0% of male patients and 48.3% of female patients (between-sex difference, − 4.70%; 95% CI, − 7.51% to − 1.89%; p = 0.001).

Solute clearance in female and male patients

Residual renal Kt/V was generally similar between sexes from 2017 to 2023. In contrast, PD Kt/V was consistently lower in male patients than in female patients, resulting in higher total Kt/V values in female patients (Fig. 3). The annual female-male difference in total Kt/V ranged from 0.09 to 0.17, and all yearly comparisons were statistically significant. The sample size-weighted mean total Kt/V over this period was 1.76 in male patients and 1.90 in female patients.

PD-related infections in female and male patients

Five nationwide surveys of PD-related infections were conducted between 2017 and 2023. The sample size-weighted mean annual incidence rate of peritonitis across these surveys was 0.23 episodes/patient-year in male patients and 0.19 episodes/patient-year in female patients, and the corresponding exit-site infection rates were 0.37 and 0.33 episodes/patient-year, respectively. Although both infection rates tended to be slightly higher in male patients, none of the yearly differences in the multi-year sample-size–weighted comparisons reached statistical significance. In the 2023 single-year sensitivity analysis, however, the peritonitis rate was significantly lower in female patients (0.19 ± 0.62 vs. 0.25 ± 0.87 episodes/patient-year; female-male difference, − 0.06; 95% CI, − 0.10 to − 0.02; p = 0.004), whereas the exit-site infection rate showed a similar but non-significant pattern (0.30 ± 0.93 vs. 0.35 ± 1.02 episodes/patient-year; female-male difference, − 0.05; 95% CI, − 0.11 to 0.005; p = 0.075). Infection-related variables were available only for selected years (2017, 2018, 2021, 2022, and 2023).

Discussion

This nationwide descriptive analysis of annual aggregated registry data revealed persistent sex-associated differences in PD-related outcomes in Japan. Across 10 years of national data, female patients had longer registry-recorded PD duration and higher registry-reported total Kt/V and total output / body weight ratios than male patients. Throughout this discussion, “PD duration” should be understood as a registry-level descriptive measure of the time on PD recorded at the annual survey rather than as a precise patient-level survival or technique-failure endpoint, because the aggregated registry extract does not support patient-level time-to-event analysis. Because the present analyses were based on annual aggregated data, these findings should be interpreted as descriptive associations rather than evidence of an independent causal effect of sex. Recent sex-specific analyses from the PDOPPS likewise showed that female patients had lower risks of mortality, transfer to HD, and peritonitis than male patients [13].

Several observational studies in different countries have reported that female patients have equal or better PD technique survival than male patients [9–12, 14]. Our findings are broadly consistent with those reports. More recently, an Australian cohort study evaluating all-cause and cause-specific PD discontinuation reported that male patients had a higher risk of all-cause PD discontinuation and inadequate dialysis-related discontinuation, whereas sex differences in infection-related discontinuation were less pronounced [14]. Importantly, additional stratified analyses according to diabetes status showed that female patients had longer PD duration than male patients in every study year in both the diabetic and non-diabetic strata. These results argue against the crude between-sex difference in diabetes prevalence as the sole explanation for the observed sex difference in PD duration; however, because we could not adjust for age, smoking, comorbidity burden, diabetes severity, or other patient-level factors, residual confounding and non-random missingness remain possible.

In addition to biological explanations, differences in care processes may also contribute to sex-associated differences in PD outcomes. A French registry study reported that women had lower risks of both transfer to HD and peritonitis than men, and mediation analyses suggested that nurse assistance may partly explain these differences [11]. Assisted PD, including nurse-supported care, is also practiced in Japan. In a nationwide Japanese survey, 173 of 475 responding PD facilities (36.4%) reported following patients who were unable to perform PD by themselves, and nurses accounted for 15.0% of the identified caregivers/supporters [17]. However, that survey was performed in 2017, and Japanese data directly examining sex differences in assisted PD utilization remain limited. In Japanese clinical practice, family caregivers and visiting nursing services are commonly involved in supporting older patients on PD, but these arrangements are not systematically captured in the JRDR/WADDA dataset. Thus, whether assisted PD is used differently in male and female patients in Japan remains uncertain. Although the present JRDR/WADDA dataset did not include sufficiently detailed information on nurse-assisted PD or third-party support, such factors may still be relevant when interpreting sex-associated differences in PD duration, particularly in older PD populations.

Regarding fluid management, previous studies have shown that male patients and patients with diabetes are more likely to have volume overload and that overhydration is associated with adverse outcomes in PD [18, 19]. Studies of peritoneal transport have also suggested that higher transporter status may contribute to reduced ultrafiltration efficiency [20, 21]. In the present study, male patients had slightly higher PET D/P Cr ratios and lower body weight-normalized ultrafiltration. Male patients also used icodextrin more frequently. However, this should be interpreted cautiously, because the registry data do not permit assessment of the specific clinical indication for icodextrin prescription, and a higher rate of icodextrin use may reflect prescribing patterns or local practice preferences rather than a clinical necessity for additional ultrafiltration.

The total output / body weight ratio proposed in this study should also be interpreted with caution. Because individual-patient data were not available, this index was calculated as a ratio of annual mean values rather than as the mean of individual patient-level ratios; therefore, formal between-sex statistical testing of this aggregated ratio is not appropriate, and the index should be regarded as a pragmatic descriptive summary of fluid output relative to body weight at the registry level rather than as a definitive patient-level physiological measure. Furthermore, normalization by body weight alone does not fully account for sex-related differences in body composition, total body water, or anthropometric variables. To evaluate the sensitivity of this index to the choice of normalization variable, we performed body-composition–corrected sensitivity analyses in the 2023 cross-sectional dataset using BSA and Watson-estimated V. The female-to-male ratio of total output remained > 1.0 across normalization by BW, BSA, and V, although the magnitude of the ratio varied across normalization schemes (Supplementary Table S9). These findings suggest that the directional sex difference in registry-level normalized fluid output in this population is consistent across multiple normalization variables, but the absolute magnitude of any sex difference is sensitive to the choice of normalization. The development of a sex- or body-composition–adjusted formula tailored to PD populations was beyond the scope of this aggregated-registry analysis but represents an important direction for future patient-level research, ideally validated against direct measures of volume status, such as bioimpedance-based parameters or natriuretic peptide levels.

Regarding solute clearance, previous studies have reported higher total Kt/V values in female patients on PD, largely because of differences in body size and urea distribution volume for a given dialysis prescription [22, 23]. Our registry-based findings are consistent with this pattern. We also observed a modest downward temporal trend in mean PD duration among female patients, whereas no significant trend was observed among male patients. The diabetes-stratified trend analysis indicated that this downward trend was driven primarily by the non-diabetic female stratum, whereas no temporal trend was observed in the diabetic female stratum. This pattern is consistent with the possibility that broader practice changes in Japan—such as evolving criteria for transition to HD or PD/HD hybrid therapy, increasing access to kidney transplantation, or changes in the case-mix of incident PD patients—have had a more pronounced influence on PD duration in non-diabetic female patients than in diabetic patients of either sex. Because the present analysis was based on annual aggregated values and may have included repeated observations of some patients across years, this trend should be interpreted cautiously and not as direct evidence of an individual-level shortening of PD duration.

A particularly important consideration is the substantial and potentially non-random missingness in the registry extract. In the 2023 dataset, overall variable-level completeness was 64.2%, and it was considerably lower for treatment-related variables—approximately two-thirds for PD duration, icodextrin use, ultrafiltration, and peritonitis rate, and only about one-quarter to one-third for the Kt/V indices (Supplementary Table S10); in addition, 23.1% of patients had an “unknown” diabetes status and were excluded from the stratified analyses. Because tests such as the peritoneal equilibration test and Kt/V are more likely to be measured and reported at selected centers or in particular patients, the recorded subset may not be representative of the entire Japanese PD population, and non-random missingness could bias the observed associations. We therefore interpret all results as descriptive findings within the subset of patients for whom each variable was recorded, rather than as estimates generalizable to the whole prevalent PD population. Although the direction of the sex difference was similar across variables with differing completeness, this does not address whether the recorded subset for any individual variable is representative and therefore does not exclude selection bias. Because the registry provided no patient-level data, neither multivariable adjustment nor formal evaluation or correction of the missing-data mechanism (e.g., multiple imputation) was possible.

We were also unable to evaluate potentially important contextual factors such as assisted PD, family support, or nurse-assisted PD. Similarly, the present registry extract did not allow us to identify the reasons for PD discontinuation in individual patients. In Japanese clinical practice, the most commonly recognized reasons for transition from PD to other modalities include PD-related infection, inadequate solute clearance, fluid management difficulties, encapsulating peritoneal sclerosis, social or caregiver-related factors, and transition to kidney transplantation; however, our aggregated data could not directly link the observed sex-associated differences in PD duration to specific reasons for discontinuation at the patient level.

This study had several limitations. First, the analyses relied on annual aggregated rather than individual-level data and therefore did not permit multivariable adjustment, patient-level time-to-event analysis, or formal technique-survival modeling; the present analyses describe sex-associated differences in registry-recorded PD duration and other registry-reported variables, not individual-level PD technique survival in the strict statistical sense. Second, incident and prevalent patients could not be distinguished, and repeated measurements of the same patient across survey years could not be identified; consequently, the temporal trend analysis reflects registry-level secular trends rather than individual-level changes over time. Third, Kt/V values were analyzed as registry-reported aggregated values, the method used to estimate V at individual facilities could not be verified, and total Kt/V data were available only from 2017 onward. Fourth, infection-related data were available only for selected years (2017, 2018, 2021, 2022, and 2023). Fifth, and most importantly, the variable-level data completeness rate was 64.2% in the 2023 dataset, with substantial variation across variables—ranging from essentially complete demographic data to only about one-quarter to one-third for the Kt/V indices (Supplementary Table S10). Because measurements such as the peritoneal equilibration test and Kt/V are more likely to be performed and reported at selected centers or in particular patient groups, the patients with available data may not be fully representative of the entire prevalent PD population, and the possibility that non-random missingness and selective reporting introduced selection bias cannot be excluded; the absence of patient-level data also precluded formal evaluation of the missing-data mechanism (e.g., by multiple imputation). The results should therefore be regarded as descriptive findings within the subset of patients for whom each variable was recorded. In addition, although diabetes mellitus was reported as a recorded category for all patients, 1821 of 7888 patients (23.1%) in the 2023 cohort were classified as having an “unknown” diabetes status by the reporting facility and were therefore excluded from the diabetes-stratified analyses; misclassification of diabetes status in the remaining cohort cannot be entirely excluded, although the consistency of the sex difference across both diabetic and non-diabetic strata argues against this being a major driver of the observed associations. Sixth, the body weight-normalized fluid output index was calculated as a ratio of annual mean values rather than as the mean of individual patient-level ratios, which limits its physiological interpretation, and the magnitude of the apparent sex difference depended on the choice of normalization variable in body-composition–corrected sensitivity analyses. Finally, the temporal trend analysis was based on annual aggregated values and should be interpreted as a registry-level secular trend rather than as longitudinal individual-level change.

Despite these limitations, the present study provides a long-term nationwide description of sex-associated differences in PD practice using a comprehensive Japanese registry. In conclusion, female patients on PD in Japan had longer registry-recorded PD duration and higher registry-reported indices of fluid and solute management than male patients. These differences persisted in both diabetic and non-diabetic strata, arguing against the crude between-sex difference in diabetes prevalence as the sole explanation, although residual confounding and non-random missingness remain possible. However, given the aggregated nature of the data, the substantial and potentially non-random missingness, and the absence of patient-level adjustment, causal inference is not possible, and these findings should be regarded strictly as registry-level descriptive associations rather than as patient-level survival or technique-failure differences. Further individual-level studies with more complete data are needed to confirm these observations and to clarify the mechanisms underlying these sex-associated differences.
