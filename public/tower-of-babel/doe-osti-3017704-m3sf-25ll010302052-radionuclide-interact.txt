1 INTRODUCTION
This progress report (Level 3 Milestone Number M3SF-25LL010302052) summarizes research
conducted at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) within the Crystalline Host Rock
Properties & Processes - LLNL Number SF-25LL01030205. Observed changes in radionuclide
sorption after bentonite/clay heating have implications for radionuclide diffusive transport through
engineered barriers. Recent research performed at Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL) has
provided key insights regarding the hydrothermal alteration behavior of bentonite backfill in the
presence of repository materials (steel, concrete, etc.). We are examining how this mineral alteration
affects retardation behavior of a suite of radionuclides of interest to repository performance
assessment. Sorption experiments and data analysis for 233U were initiated in FY24 following earlier
experiments performed on 243Am, 90Sr, 137Cs. In FY25, we completed the 233U study and initiated and
completed a study of 237Np sorption. Below, we summarize the results and potential impacts of
hydrothermal alteration on radionuclide retardation and assess the importance of this process to
radionuclide migration from a GHRDC. We also use statistical tools (i.e. PCA) to help us determine
the major drivers in affecting changes in measured Kd values induced by hydrothermal alteration.
These experiments also allow us to test the predictive ability of our component additivity approach to
surface complexation and ion exchange. Our guiding hypothesis is that a robust surface
complexation/ion exchange model and associated database can effectively predict changes in
radionuclide sorption behavior resulting from the hydrothermal alteration of mineralogy in a
repository near field. In November 2024 the paper “Selenium interaction with iron minerals:
Quantitative comparison of sorption and coprecipitation impacts on mobility” was published in
Applied Geochemistry. A short update of results to date is presented below.
In FY25, we also actively supported the DITUSC project, which is part of the EURADII initiative, as
associate partners. We are executing the Migration2025 conference and support associated the
NEA-TDB and Thermochimie workshops that will provide critical international engagements and
develop consensus and synergy in thermodynamics as it relates to supporting the US nuclear waste
repository program.

2 EFFECT OF BENTONITE HEATING ON RADIONUCLIDE SORPTION
A commonly considered idea for high-level nuclear waste disposal is to emplace steel waste
canisters in a geological repository with a bentonite/clay barrier between the canister and host rock
(Meunier et al., 1998; Pusch, 1979). Bentonite can be used to provide 1) a physical barrier to
prevent fluid seeping in from natural surroundings and interacting with the waste package and 2) a
chemical barrier by attenuating actinide migration if a release occurs.
High temperatures (100–200°C) near waste canisters resulting from radioactive decay may alter the
bentonite clay's ability to sorb contaminants. Temperature effects on the bentonite/clay barrier may
include changes to the clay's hydrological and mechanical properties, changes to pore water
chemical compositions, and changes to the clay and accessory mineral composition (Cuadros and
Linares, 1996; Wersin et al., 2007; Zheng et al., 2017). For these reasons the potential effects of
elevated temperature on the engineered barrier should be considered within reasonable margins.
In this study, we examine the capacity of hydrothermally altered clay samples (Table 1Table 1) to
sorb radionuclides (Table 2) in batch sorption experiments. The hydrothermally altered samples were
subjected to short-term (days to months) high temperature (200-300 °C) heating in the laboratory
(Table 1). Hydrothermal samples prepared at these higher temperatures were selected to simulate
extreme and unanticipated conditions of potential hydrothermal alteration, in order to assess the
maximum possible changes in Kd values that could result from such alteration - a bounding
scenario.

2.1 MATERIALS AND METHODS
The hydrothermally altered samples used in this study were provided by collaborators from LANL
(Table 1Table 1). All the samples received at LLNL were prepared for surface area analysis using
BET, but no further characterization was performed at LLNL as complete chemical and mineralogical
characterization of the selected samples was presented in previous publications (Caporuscio, 2014,
2015; Caporuscio, 2018; Caporuscio, 2019; Caporuscio, 2020; Cheshire et al., 2014; Mills et al.,
2023).
In this work, batch sorption experiments with hydrothermally altered barrier materials (Table 1) and
different radionuclides (Table 2) are performed at a pH range of 7-8 using either a low (NaCl 0.01M,
NaHCO3 0.01M) or high (NaCl 1M, NaHCO3 0.01M) ionic strength buffer solution as background
electrolyte. In both low and high ionic strength solutions, a 0.01M HEPES buffer is used to maintain
the pH in the desired range. Each sorption experiment is performed in triplicate using a solid to
solution ratio of 5 g/L. To monitor the effects of heating on the sorption properties of bentonite,
batch sorption experiments are also performed using control, unheated samples (Table1). The linear
distribution coefficient (Kd), was calculated as the ratio between the solid phase and aqueous phase

2.2 RESULTS

In FY25, we completed and analyzed data for 233U and 237Np sorption experiments, and results of our
findings are reported below. The overall goal of this work is to 1) monitor the changes in Kd values
between original, non-altered materials and hydrothermally altered samples; 2) determine the
factors that control changes in sorption capacity of radionuclides after hydrothermal alteration of
bentonite, host rock, and structural materials.
Sorption experiments with hydrothermally altered materials provided by LANL
In the figures below (1-3) the Kd values measured in sorption experiments using the hydrothermally
altered materials (EBS 5 though IEBS 6) are reported on the x axis while the Kd for the original, non-
hydrothermally altered materials (Wy, Na-Wy, OPC, Na-OPC, GG) are plotted on the y axis. For
samples that included mixtures of starting materials (Wy, OC, GG) in the hydrothermal experiments
(EBS 15, EBS 19, EBS 20, EBS28, IEBS 5, IEBS 6), we calculated the expected Kd values of the
unaltered substrate as weighted averages of Kd measured for the starting materials. In Figure 1, and
2, the dashed line represents a 1-to-1 line. In the figures, if a hydrothermally altered sample has a
Kd that is unchanged from the starting material, then its Kd will plot on (or around) the 1-to-1 line. If
hydrothermally altered samples have larger Kd values compared to their starting material, then they
will plot below the 1-to-1 line, whereas if Kd values are reduced during hydrothermal processing,
samples will plot above the 1-to-1 line.
Figure 1 shows the results for the 233U sorption experiments. Data shows that the measured Kd
values are overall lower for sorption experiments conducted in high ionic strength solution (triangles)
compared to those conducted in low ionic strengths (circles). In experiments conducted at either low
or high ionic strength (circles or triangles) most hydrothermally altered samples exhibit some
decrease in Kd after hydrothermal alteration (plot above the 1:1 line). In both high and low ionic
strength conditions, three samples display increased Kd values compared to the starting materials ,
plotting below the 1-to-1 line, including samples containing quartz (EBS 9), granodiorite (IEBS 5, IEBS
6) portland cement (IEBS 6).

2.3 SUMMARY OF RESULTS: 243AM, 233U, 237NP, 90SR, 137CS
Figure 3 displays the changes in sorption distribution coefficients (Kd) for five radionuclides: 243Am,
237Np,233U (actinides), and 90Sr, 137Cs (fission products), based on sorption experiments with

hydrothermally altered samples (EBS 5, EBS 9, EBS 10, EBS 12, EBS 15, EBS 19, EBS 20, EBS 28,
IEBS 5 and IEBS 6). Each radionuclide is represented by a separate horizontal bar chart, with sample
names shown on the vertical axis. The suffixes “-H” and “-L” near the sample names indicate
sorption solutions condition of high and low ionic strength, respectively. The horizontal axis features
a logarithmic scale from 0.01 to 100, centered at 1, where a value of 1 corresponds to no change in
Kd relative to the unaltered starting material. Blue bars indicate increased sorption, while red bars
represent decreased sorption.
Among the actinides, 243Am and 237Np generally display increased sorption after alteration, as
evidenced by predominantly blue bars, with a few samples showing increases greater than an order
of magnitude. 233U contrasts this trend, exhibiting mostly decreased sorption values, often falling
below 0.1, which indicates a significant reduction in sorption related to hydrothermal alteration of
the substrates. For the fission products, 90Sr shows minimal sorption changes, with bars largely
clustered near 1 and a mixture of small increases and decreases. 90Sr sorption appears to be
affected by ionic strength of the sorption solution, with sorption data from high ionic strength
solutions displaying relative increase in Kd values, in contrast to low ionic strength. 137Cs mainly
exhibits a decrease in sorption, with most changes remaining within one order of magnitude. Overall,
the actinide elements demonstrate more pronounced and variable sorption changes compared to
the fission products, highlighting the differential impact of hydrothermal alteration on radionuclide
sorption behavior.

2.4 STATISTICAL EXPLORATION OF SORPTION DATA
Principal component analysis (PCA) and partial least squares (PLS) regression were performed on the
entire dataset, as well as on each subset, including every combination of sorbing cation and ionic
strength. Variables analyzed included Kd, the ionic strength of the solution used in the sorption
experiments, the pH of the sorbing solution, the ionic strength of the solution used in hydrothermal
alteration experiments, the measured specific surface area of each solid sample, the temperature
and reaction time (in weeks) of the LANL hydrothermal alteration experiments, the abundance of 20
different minerals and phases identified by XRD in the reacted solid samples, and the initial and final
water-rock ratios of the hydrothermal alteration experiments. Analyses were conducted using Origin
Pro and/or Python’s statistics package. No substantial differences were found between results from
the two software platforms.
PCA was not pursued in depth because the dataset exhibited high collinearity among variables and
had relatively few data points compared to the number of variables, resulting in a lack of meaningful
relationships between Kd and experimental components (results not shown). In contrast, PLS
analyses were more informative. PLS regression, a supervised machine-learning approach, was used
to predict the dependent variable (Kd) from independent variables such as mineralogy, experimental
conditions of the LANL hydrothermal experiments, and conditions of the LLNL sorption experiments.
PLS regression reduces dimensionality by extracting important factors from the independent
variables and using them to predict Kd. The influence of each independent variable in the predictive
models is illustrated by the variable influence on projection (VIP) weighting term. Figure 4Figure 4
shows heatmaps of the VIP values for each independent variable across the different sorption data
subsets. Higher VIP values (shown in red) indicate variables that are more heavily weighted in the
predictive model for a given subset.
The heatmap reveals which variables are most likely to affect the changes in Kd observed in the
experiments. The presence of amorphous silica and K-feldspar strongly influences sorption changes
for 243Am, 237Np, 233U, and 90Sr. In contrast, 137Cs sorption is relatively unaffected by these variables
and is instead influenced by changes in mineralogy, particularly the presence of illite and
clinoptilolite.

2.5 OUTLOOK

We have performed a comprehensive set of sorption experiments using fifteen substrates, including
ten hydrothermally altered samples and five original, non-altered substrates, with three actinide
elements and two fission products. The overall goals of this work were to (1) monitor the changes in
Kd values between the original, non-altered materials and the hydrothermally altered samples, and
(2) determine the factors controlling changes in sorption capacity of radionuclides after hydrothermal
alteration of bentonite, host rock, and structural materials. The ultimate objective was to broadly
quantify the changes in Kd related to thermal effects and identify which radionuclides may be more
sensitive to these alterations, and provide guidance to transport/safety models.

August 20, 2025                                                                                      11

Our results reveal that changes in Kd for 137Cs, 90Sr, 243Am, and 237Np, in both high and low ionic
strength conditions, are within or less than an order of magnitude from the unaltered substrate. For
243Am and 237Np, we generally observe an increase in Kd. Our statistical analyses reveal that the

presence of amorphous material and other minor mineralogical changes (such as presence of illite
and zeolites) are responsible for shifts in Kd sorption values. For these four radionuclides,
hydrothermal alteration of the substrate does not significantly decrease sorption capacity. The
highest decrease in Kd is observed across the 233U experiments.
The findings of this work could be used to simulate the effects of hydrothermal alteration on specific
radionuclides, identify which geochemical parameters influence Kd changes, and ultimately provide
an estimate of the expected Kd variations that could be quantitatively included in in performance
assessment models. Importantly, our experiments suggest that hydrothermal alternation may affect
radionuclide Kds but the effect is limited to approximately one order of magnitude.

3 PUBLISHED PAPER: “SELENIUM INTERACTION WITH IRON MINERALS:
 QUANTITATIVE COMPARISON OF SORPTION AND COPRECIPITATION
 IMPACTS ON MOBILITY”
A paper titled “Selenium interaction with iron minerals: Quantitative comparison of sorption and
coprecipitation impacts on mobility” authored by Enrica Balboni, Solchan Han, and Mavrik Zavarin
was accepted in Applied Geochemistry in November 2024
(https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0883292724002993 ). Below, we summarize
the results of this research and its correlation to the performance of nuclear waste repositories.
Given the significance of selenium (Se) as a micronutrient, the radioactive nature of some of its
isotopes, and its affinity to iron (Fe) minerals, extensive research has been conducted on the
sorption mechanisms between Se and these minerals. We employed adsorption data sourced from
the L-SCIE database and coprecipitation data from available literature to achieve the following
objectives: i) establish coherence between adsorption and coprecipitation processes, ii)
quantitatively evaluate the importance of these processes in nuclear waste repository science, and
iii) propose a forward-looking approach for integrating coprecipitation into reactive transport models.
Our findings indicate that a correlation between Se adsorption and coprecipitation can be
established using the ߣ formalism (See Balboni et al., 2024 for detailed model description). Our
analysis confirms a stronger affinity of Se(IV) compared to Se(VI) for both adsorption and
coprecipitation processes with hydrous ferric oxide, goethite, hematite, and magnetite. Moreover, the
comparable log(λSe(IV)/ λSe(VI)) ratios derived from adsorption and coprecipitation experiments
suggest that these processes can be quantitatively compared and evaluated using our numerical
approach. Across all iron oxide phases examined, coprecipitation leads to significantly greater
immobilization of Se compared to adsorption. Specifically, for hydrous ferric oxide, hematite, and
goethite, coprecipitation is predicted to result in 100 to 1000 times more Se immobilization
compared to adsorption, irrespective of the Se oxidation state (Se(IV) or Se(VI)); notably stronger
immobilization potential via coprecipitation was observed for magnetite. The modeling approach and
quantitative analysis presented in this manuscript clearly highlight the importance of including
coprecipitation processes when simulating Se (and other elements) transport, particularly under
conditions where mineral compositions are transient or evolving with time. Neglecting coprecipitation
in models is likely to lead to significant overestimates of radionuclide migration in nuclear waste
repositories.

4 FY26 PROPOSED EFFORTS
LLNL recommendations for work packages for FY 26 are below and leverage work that has been
done previously to expediate siting a deep geologic repository.

Task Develop a data repository and online tool to investigate sorption behavior of radionuclides on
rocks and minerals relevant to both crystalline and shale repositories with specific focus on
relevance to US rock formations.
This work is in support of the US DOE efforts to develop conceptual models for disposition in high
temperature repositories.
 We propose to develop a web-accessible database of sorption data for minerals and rocks that are
likely to be present in a US hosted repository. The database will expand the LLNL’s longstanding
effort and expertise developing the L-SCIE mineral sorption database to include sorption to
representative US rock formations. It will also provide an online platform accessible to both scientists
and the public to understand the mobility of radionuclides that form the nuclear waste inventory. The
web platform will provide access to data through query and provide visual aids (graphs, etc.) to help
the user understand the implications to repository safety.
