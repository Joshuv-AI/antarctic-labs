Reflection Symmetry, APS Boundary Conditions, and Equivariant Spectral Flow on a Warped Cylinder
Taro Kimura, Sanchita Sharma

Abstract
We study reflection symmetry and Atiyah-Patodi-Singer (APS) boundary conditions for twisted Dirac operators on a finite warped cylinder. For a complex line twist with holonomy parameter A A , we show that the reflection lifts to a unitary symmetry of the twisted Dirac setting if and only if 2 ​ A ∈ ℤ 2A\in\mathbb{Z} . In the resulting reflection-compatible fixed-holonomy case, reflection pairs opposite shifted angular modes, and the paired APS blocks are unitarily equivalent. The reflection trace on the APS harmonic space localizes to the unique self-paired zero-mode sector. We then turn to parameter-dependent versions of the model. For fixed gauge-trivial holonomy, the family remains pointwise O ⁡ ( 2 ) O(2) -equivariant, and its spectral flow admits an R ​ O ​ ( O ⁡ ( 2 ) ) RO(O(2)) -valued decomposition. For genuinely varying holonomy, pointwise O ⁡ ( 2 ) O(2) -equivariance is lost along the path. The representation-ring-valued invariant is then replaced by a residual sign-level invariant: the mod-two parity of the APS crossing events.

For a line twist with holonomy parameter

[A]=[-A]\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad 2A\in\mathbb{Z}.

k\longmapsto k^{\vee}=-k-2A,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad m=k+A\longmapsto-m,

RO(O(2))

O(2)

The analysis is split into three cases. In the reflection-compatible fixed-holonomy line-twist case, the APS operator decomposes into reflection-paired Fourier blocks. As a consequence, in the invertible-boundary fixed-holonomy case, the ordinary APS index of the flat line-twist model vanishes. When the self-paired sector is present, the finite-dimensional kernel correction has to be kept separately. In either case, a symmetry-visible quantity remains, namely the trace of the lifted reflection on the APS harmonic space. This trace is supported in the self-paired sector

For moving families, there are again two distinct possibilities. In the line-twist model, if the holonomy is fixed and gauge-trivial, then after choosing the gauge

Main results.

We summarize the main results as follows.

(i)

The reflection
r:(t,\theta)\mapsto(t,-\theta)
lifts to a unitary symmetry of the twisted warped-cylinder Dirac setting if and only if

2A\in\mathbb{Z}.

(1.4)

(ii)

In the reflection-compatible fixed-holonomy case, reflection pairs the Fourier blocks by

k\leftrightarrow k^{\vee}=-k-2A,\qquad m\leftrightarrow-m.

(1.5)

The paired APS blocks are unitarily equivalent. In the invertible-boundary case, the ordinary APS index of the fixed-holonomy line-twist model with flat twisting connection vanishes; when the self-paired sector is present, the finite-dimensional kernel correction has to be kept separately. The reflection trace in the APS harmonic space is supported in the self-paired sector
m=0
.

(iii)

For gauge-trivial fixed-holonomy bulk perturbation families in the line-twist model, equivalently for
[A_{0}]=0
and after choosing the gauge
A_{0}=0
, the relevant invariant is the
RO(O(2))
-valued equivariant spectral flow. Away from the self-paired sector, every non-self-paired reflection orbit contributes an even amount to the ordinary spectral flow.

(iv)

For non-constant holonomy deformations in the original line-twist model, the remaining invariant is the mod-two crossing parity

\operatorname{sf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A),

(1.6)

namely, the parity of the crossing events
k+A(s_{*})=0
, equivalently, the parity of the one Fourier label changes its APS sign assignment.

Geometric and analytic setup

This section fixes the warped-cylinder Dirac model and the notation used throughout. The geometric and analytic setup is adapted from
[
11
]
.

2.1
Geometry, bundles, and function spaces

Let
M
be the finite warped cylinder

M=[0,T]\times S^{1},\qquad g=dt^{2}+f(t)^{2}\,d\theta^{2},

(2.1)

where
f\in C^{\infty}([0,T])
is strictly positive. Thus,
t\in[0,T]
is the longitudinal coordinate,
\theta
is the angular coordinate on
S^{1}
, and the metric
g
is the warped product line element
ds^{2}=dt^{2}+f(t)^{2}\,d\theta^{2}
, so that the circle fiber at
t
has radius
f(t)
.
Its boundary is

\partial M=Y_{0}\sqcup Y_{T},\qquad Y_{0}=\{0\}\times S^{1},\qquad Y_{T}=\{T\}\times S^{1}.

(2.2)

We use the orthonormal coframe

e^{1}=dt,\qquad e^{2}=f(t)\,d\theta,

(2.3)

with dual frame

e_{1}=\partial_{t},\qquad e_{2}=\frac{1}{f(t)}\partial_{\theta}.

(2.4)

We orient
M
by
dt\wedge d\theta
. With this convention, the inward unit normal is

N=\begin{cases}+\partial_{t},&\text{on }Y_{0},\\
-\partial_{t},&\text{on }Y_{T}.\end{cases}

(2.5)

Fix a spin structure on
M
, and let
S\to M
denote the corresponding complex spinor bundle. In dimension two,
S
has rank
2
and splits chirally as

S=S^{+}\oplus S^{-}.

(2.6)

Let
E\to M
be a Hermitian complex line bundle with a unitary connection

\nabla^{E}=d+iA\,d\theta,\qquad A\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.7)

Only the class

[A]\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}

(2.8)

is gauge invariant: if one changes the local trivialization of the line bundle by a unitary gauge transformation, then the parameter
A
may change by an integer. However, its class modulo
\mathbb{Z}
remains unchanged. Equivalently, different real numbers
A
and
A+n
, with
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, determine gauge-equivalent connections and hence the same holonomy around the circle. Thus, the twisted Dirac operator acts on sections of the twisted bundle,

S\otimes E\to M.

(2.9)

From this point onward, we suppress the twist
E
from the notation when no confusion can arise, and write
S
,
S^{\pm}
, and
S_{Y_{t_{0}}}
for the corresponding twisted bundles.

For any vector bundle
V\to M
, we write
\Gamma(V)
for the smooth sections of
V
. We use the standard Hilbert and Sobolev spaces

L^{2}(M;V),\qquad H^{1}(M;V),

(2.10)

defined using the Riemannian volume form of
g
and the Hermitian bundle metric. On each boundary component
Y_{t_{0}}
, where
t_{0}\in\{0,T\}
, we similarly write

L^{2}(Y_{t_{0}};V|_{Y_{t_{0}}}),\qquad H^{1}(Y_{t_{0}};V|_{Y_{t_{0}}}),\qquad H^{1/2}(Y_{t_{0}};V|_{Y_{t_{0}}})

(2.11)

for the corresponding boundary spaces, where
H^{1/2}
is the standard trace space. In particular, for the twisted spinor bundle
S
, the trace map takes the form

H^{1}(M;S)\longrightarrow H^{1/2}(Y_{t_{0}};S|_{Y_{t_{0}}}).

(2.12)

2.2
Clifford conventions and the twisted Dirac operator

Choose Pauli matrices
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}
, and set

\gamma_{1}=\sigma_{1},\qquad\gamma_{2}=\sigma_{2},\qquad\gamma_{3}=i\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}=-\sigma_{3}.

(2.13)

Thus the chirality decomposition (
2.6
) is the
\pm 1
-eigenspace decomposition of
\gamma_{3}
.

Using the tensor product connection
\nabla^{S\otimes E}=\nabla^{S}\otimes 1+1\otimes\nabla^{E}
, we define the twisted Dirac operator

D:\Gamma(S)\longrightarrow\Gamma(S).

(2.14)

In the orthonormal frame (
2.4
), one obtains

D=i\sigma_{1}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{2f(t)}\Bigr)+i\sigma_{2}\,\frac{1}{f(t)}\bigl(\partial_{\theta}+iA\bigr).

(2.15)

Equivalently,

D=i\begin{pmatrix}0&\partial_{t}+\dfrac{f^{\prime}(t)}{2f(t)}-\dfrac{i}{f(t)}(\partial_{\theta}+iA)\\
\partial_{t}+\dfrac{f^{\prime}(t)}{2f(t)}+\dfrac{i}{f(t)}(\partial_{\theta}+iA)&0\end{pmatrix}.

(2.16)

2.3
Fourier reduction and mode spaces

Since the metric and connection are
\theta
-independent, the operator
D
commutes with rotations in the
\theta
-variable and therefore preserves the Fourier-mode decomposition in the angular direction. Equivalently,
D
acts diagonally with respect to the Fourier basis, so the full operator splits into independent mode operators, one for each allowed angular mode. Depending on the chosen spin structure on
S^{1}
, the allowed Fourier modes are

\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{(periodic case)},\qquad\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{(anti-periodic case)}.

(2.17)

For
k\in\mathcal{K}
, we write

\psi(t,\theta)=e^{ik\theta}\binom{u(t)}{v(t)}.

(2.18)

Since
(\partial_{\theta}+iA)e^{ik\theta}=i(k+A)e^{ik\theta}
, it is convenient to introduce the shifted mode parameter

m=k+A.

(2.19)

For each mode
k
, the natural radial Hilbert and Sobolev spaces are

H_{k}=L^{2}([0,T],f(t)\,dt;\mathbb{C}^{2}),\qquad W_{k}=H^{1}([0,T],f(t)\,dt;\mathbb{C}^{2}).

(2.20)

In the
k
-th mode,
D
reduces to

D^{(k)}=i\begin{pmatrix}0&A^{+}\\
A^{-}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad A^{\pm}=\partial_{t}+\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{2f(t)}\pm\frac{m}{f(t)}.

(2.21)

Hence, the eigenvalue equation

D\psi=\lambda\psi,\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

(2.22)

becomes the modewise first-order system,
A^{+}v=-i\lambda u
,
A^{-}u=-i\lambda v
.
Equivalently,

v^{\prime}(t)+\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{2f(t)}v(t)+\frac{m}{f(t)}v(t)=-i\lambda u(t),\qquad u^{\prime}(t)+\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{2f(t)}u(t)-\frac{m}{f(t)}u(t)=-i\lambda v(t).

(2.23)

Eliminating one component yields a second-order scalar equation; in the present paper, the key point is that the boundary conditions and spectral data are organized entirely in terms of
m
.

2.4
Boundary operators and APS boundary conditions

Let
Y_{t_{0}}
be one of the boundary circles. The induced boundary spinor bundle is
S_{Y_{t_{0}}}=S|_{Y_{t_{0}}}
.
For the unit tangent vector
U=e_{2}|_{t=t_{0}}
, define the induced boundary Clifford action by

c(U)=-\,i\,\gamma(N)\gamma(U).

(2.24)

With the inward normal convention (
2.5
), this gives

c(U)=\begin{cases}\ \sigma_{3},&\text{on }Y_{0},\\
-\sigma_{3},&\text{on }Y_{T}.\end{cases}

(2.25)

The intrinsic tangential operator is

D_{t_{0}}=\frac{1}{f(t_{0})}(-i\partial_{\theta}+A),

(2.26)

and the self-adjoint boundary Dirac operators entering the APS projector are

B_{0}=\frac{1}{f(0)}\,\sigma_{3}\,(-i\partial_{\theta}+A),\qquad B_{T}=-\frac{1}{f(T)}\,\sigma_{3}\,(-i\partial_{\theta}+A).

(2.27)

On the
k
-th Fourier mode, these reduce to

B_{0}^{(k)}=\frac{m}{f(0)}\sigma_{3},\qquad B_{T}^{(k)}=-\frac{m}{f(T)}\sigma_{3}.

(2.28)

Let
P_{>0}(B_{t_{0}})
denote the orthogonal projection onto the strictly positive spectral subspace of
B_{t_{0}}
. For
m\neq 0
, the APS boundary condition is

P_{>0}(B_{0})\bigl(\psi|_{Y_{0}}\bigr)=0,\qquad P_{>0}(B_{T})\bigl(\psi|_{Y_{T}}\bigr)=0.

(2.29)

Since (
2.28
) is diagonal, this becomes, modewise,

m>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u(0)=0,\qquad v(T)=0,

(2.30a)

m<0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v(0)=0,\qquad u(T)=0.

(2.30b)

The global APS convention handles the kernel
m=0
stated in Remark
2.4
.

Accordingly, the APS domain of the mode operator
D^{k}
is

\Dom(D_{k,\text{APS}})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left\{\binom{u}{v}\in W_{k}:\ u(0)=0,\ v(T)=0\right\},&m>0,\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\left\{\binom{u}{v}\in W_{k}:\ v(0)=0,\ u(T)=0\right\},&m<0.\end{cases}

(2.31)

Remark 2.1

(APS convention and the self-paired sector)
.

Throughout the paper, the APS condition is understood in the following sense. On every
nonzero boundary mode, we impose the standard APS projection

P_{>0}(B_{t})=1_{(0,\infty)}(B_{t})
.

If
B_{t}
has a nontrivial kernel, then the APS boundary condition is understood to be completed
by a fixed self-adjoint kernel condition on
\ker B_{t}
. In the reflection-symmetric parts of the
paper, this kernel completion is assumed to be invariant under the boundary reflection, denoted later by
\mathcal{R}_{t}
.
Equivalently, the completed APS projector will still be denoted by
P_{t}^{\APS}
, and it is assumed
to define a self-adjoint Fredholm APS realization of the Dirac operator.

For
m\neq 0
, the boundary operators are invertible, so no kernel completion is needed. In
those sectors, the APS condition reduces exactly to

m>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u(0)=0,\qquad v(T)=0,

(2.32a)

and

m<0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v(0)=0,\qquad u(T)=0.

(2.32b)

Only the self-paired sector
m=0
depends on the auxiliary kernel completion. With this convention fixed, we continue to write
P_{t}^{APS}
and
D^{APS}
without explicitly
recording the auxiliary kernel completion in the notation.

3
O(2)
-compatible reflection lift: reflection symmetry and the holonomy obstruction

In this section, we analyze the symmetry of the twisted Dirac problem beyond the automatic

S^{1}
-rotation symmetry of the warped cylinder. Throughout,
D
denotes the twisted Dirac
operator from
Section 2
, acting on sections of the twisted spinor bundle
S
,
with twist class

[A]\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}.

(3.1)

The rotation symmetry is present for every holonomy class (
3.1
),
whereas the reflection symmetry is compatible with the twisting only when

2A\in\mathbb{Z}.

(3.2)

3.1
The rotation symmetry

Let

R_{\varphi}:M\longrightarrow M,\qquad(t,\theta)\mapsto(t,\theta+\varphi),\qquad\varphi\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z},

(3.3)

be the standard rotation of the angular variable. Since both the warped metric
g=dt^{2}+f(t)^{2}\,d\theta^{2}
and the connection form
iA\,d\theta

are independent of
\theta
, the operator
D
is invariant under this action.

We denote by

\rho(\varphi):\Gamma(S)\longrightarrow\Gamma(S)

(3.4)

the induced unitary action on sections, given in the chosen trivialization by pullback:

(\rho(\varphi)\psi)(t,\theta)=\psi(t,\theta+\varphi).

(3.5)

Then

\rho(\varphi)\,D\,\rho(\varphi)^{-1}=D.

(3.6)

Equivalently,
D
commutes with the circle action and therefore decomposes into Fourier modes, as in
Section 2
.

3.2
The reflection map and its effect on the angular operator

We now introduce the reflection

r:M\longrightarrow M,\qquad(t,\theta)\longmapsto(t,-\theta).

(3.7)

This map is an isometry of the warped metric, but it reverses the angular coordinate.

In the chosen trivialization, we write
r^{*}

for pullback on coefficient functions induced by
r
. Since
r
reverses
\theta
, one has

r^{*}\,(-i\partial_{\theta})\,(r^{*})^{-1}=+\,i\partial_{\theta}.

(3.8)

Thus, reflection reverses the angular momentum operator.

It is convenient to write the angular part of
D
using

T_{A}=-i\partial_{\theta}+A.

(3.9)

Then the explicit formula for the twisted Dirac operator from
Section 2
becomes

D=i\sigma_{1}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{2f(t)}\Bigr)-\frac{1}{f(t)}\,\sigma_{2}\,T_{A}.

(3.10)

Under reflection, one has

r^{*}\,T_{A}\,(r^{*})^{-1}=-\,T_{-A}.

(3.11)

Indeed, since
r(t,\theta)=(t,-\theta)
acts only on the angular variable, it reverses
\partial_{\theta}
but acts trivially on the constant scalar parameter
A
. Thus,

r^{*}\,T_{A}\,(r^{*})^{-1}=r^{*}(-i\partial_{\theta}+A)(r^{*})^{-1}=+i\partial_{\theta}+A=-(-i\partial_{\theta}-A)=-\,T_{-A}.

(3.12)

Under the reflection (
3.7
), the connection term
A\,d\theta
is sent to
-A\,d\theta
, so the holonomy class transforms as

[A]\longmapsto[-A]\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}.

(3.13)

Hence, reflection preserves the twisted conditions only when the reflected twist class is gauge-equivalent to the original one.

3.3
Gauge transformations and the arithmetic condition

For each integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define

g_{n}:S^{1}\longrightarrow U(1),\qquad g_{n}(\theta)=e^{in\theta}.

(3.14)

In the chosen trivialization of the line bundle, this acts on sections by multiplication by
e^{in\theta}
. Conjugating
T_{A}
by
g_{n}
gives

g_{n}\,T_{A}\,g_{n}^{-1}=T_{A-n}.

(3.15)

Indeed,

g_{n}(-i\partial_{\theta}+A)g_{n}^{-1}=-i\partial_{\theta}+(A-n).

(3.16)

Thus, the parameter
A
is defined only modulo integers, and the invariant datum is the class
[A]\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
.

Reflection is compatible with the twisting exactly when the original and reflected classes agree:

[A]=[-A]\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}.

(3.17)

This is equivalent to the condition

2A\in\mathbb{Z}.

(3.18)

Indeed, (
3.17
) means that
A-(-A)=2A
is an integer.

3.4
The spinor reflection and the lifted reflection operator

To lift the base reflection to the twisted spinor bundle, we first choose a matrix acting on the spinor factor. Let

U_{r}=\sigma_{1}.

(3.19)

Since the Clifford matrices were fixed in
Section 2
as (
2.13
), one computes

U_{r}\gamma_{1}U_{r}^{-1}=\gamma_{1},\qquad U_{r}\gamma_{2}U_{r}^{-1}=-\gamma_{2}.

(3.20)

Thus,
U_{r}
fixes the radial Clifford generator and reverses the angular one, exactly as required by the reflection
\theta\mapsto-\theta
.

Assume now that (
3.18
) holds. Then
-2A\in\mathbb{Z}
, so the gauge factor

g_{-2A}(\theta)=e^{-2iA\theta}

(3.21)

is single-valued on
S^{1}
. We define the lifted reflection operator on sections by

\mathcal{U}_{r}=U_{r}\,g_{-2A}\,r^{*}.

(3.22)

Here,
r^{*}
implements the base reflection,
U_{r}
supplies the spinor reflection, and
g_{-2A}
corrects the reflected twist
-A
back to
A
.

Remark 3.1

(Bundle-theoretic form of the reflection lift)
.

It is conceptually useful to separate the bundle-level lift from the induced operator on sections. Let
\pi:S\to M
denote the twisted spinor bundle projection, and let

\pi_{r}:r^{*}(S)\longrightarrow M

denote the projection of the pullback bundle under the reflection

Assuming (

), define the fiberwise bundle isomorphism

\Phi_{r}=U_{r}\,g_{-2A}:r^{*}(S)\longrightarrow S.

(3.24)

Then
\Phi_{r}
is the bundle-level reflection lift associated with

, covering the identity on

, in the sense that

\pi\circ\Phi_{r}=\pi_{r}.

(3.25)

Equivalently,
\Phi_{r}
fits into the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}

The corresponding operator in sections is

\mathcal{U}_{r}=\Gamma(\Phi_{r})\circ r^{*},

where

denotes the map induced by

Lemma 3.2

(Intertwining of the reflected twisted operator)
.

3.5
The holonomy obstruction to a reflection lift

We now state the precise obstruction theorem.

Theorem 3.4

(Holonomy obstruction to a reflection lift)
.

Let
D
be the twisted warped-cylinder Dirac operator with constant holonomy parameter
A
.
Then the reflection map

r:(t,\theta)\longmapsto(t,-\theta)

lifts to a unitary symmetry of the twisted Dirac problem if and only if

2A\in\mathbb{Z}.

(3.35)

Proof.

Assume first that (
3.35
) holds. Then the operator
\mathcal{U}_{r}
from
(
3.22
) is globally defined and unitary, and by Lemma
3.2

it satisfies

\mathcal{U}_{r}\,D\,\mathcal{U}_{r}^{-1}=D.

(3.36)

Thus, reflection lifts to a unitary symmetry of the twisted problem.

Conversely, suppose a unitary reflection lift exists. Then conjugation by that lift identifies the reflected twisted operator with the original one. In particular, the reflected connection class

must define the same twisted problem as the original class

4
Static fixed-holonomy APS structure

If reflection is required at every parameter value in the line-twist model, then the
holonomy must be constant. We therefore fix

A=A_{0},\qquad 2A_{0}\in\mathbb{Z},

(4.1)

and record the resulting static consequences: the pairing
m\leftrightarrow-m
, the
unitary equivalence of the paired APS blocks, and the vanishing of the boundary

\eta
-terms away from
m=0
. The genuinely moving
O(2)
-equivariant family is
treated later in
Section 6
, and non-equivariant holonomy deformations in

Section 7
.

4.1
Pointwise reflection compatibility forces constant holonomy

Proposition 4.1

(Pointwise reflection compatibility forces constant holonomy)
.

Let
A:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous. If
2A(s)\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
s\in[0,1]
, then
A
is constant.

Proof.

The image of
A
lies in the discrete set
\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. Since
[0,1]
is connected and

A
is continuous,
A([0,1])
must be connected, hence a singleton.
∎

4.2
The fixed-holonomy APS operator

Fix
A_{0}
as in (
4.1
). The twisted Dirac operator is

D_{A_{0}}=i\sigma_{1}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{2f(t)}\Bigr)+i\sigma_{2}\,\frac{1}{f(t)}(\partial_{\theta}+iA_{0}),

(4.2)

with boundary operators

B_{0,A_{0}}=\frac{1}{f(0)}\,\sigma_{3}(-i\partial_{\theta}+A_{0}),\qquad B_{T,A_{0}}=-\frac{1}{f(T)}\,\sigma_{3}(-i\partial_{\theta}+A_{0}).

(4.3)

For
t\in\{0,T\}
, let
P_{t,A_{0}}^{\APS}
denote the completed APS projector in the sense of
Remark
2.4
. Thus, on every nonzero boundary mode it agrees with

\mathbf{1}_{(0,\infty)}(B_{t,A_{0}})
, while in the boundary kernel it includes the fixed auxiliary self-adjoint kernel completion. With this convention, the APS domain is

\Dom(D_{A_{0}}^{\APS})=\Bigl\{\psi\in H^{1}(M;S):P_{0,A_{0}}^{\APS}(\psi|_{Y_{0}})=0,\ P_{T,A_{0}}^{\APS}(\psi|_{Y_{T}})=0\Bigr\}.

(4.4)

Setting
m=k+A_{0}
, the modewise boundary operators are

B_{0,A_{0}}^{(k)}=\frac{m}{f(0)}\sigma_{3},\qquad B_{T,A_{0}}^{(k)}=-\frac{m}{f(T)}\sigma_{3}.

(4.5)

For
m\neq 0
, the APS condition becomes

m>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u(0)=0,\qquad v(T)=0,

(4.6a)

m<0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v(0)=0,\qquad u(T)=0.

(4.6b)

The self-paired sector is
m=0
.

4.3
Reflection on boundary blocks

Because
2A_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
, the lifted reflection is

\mathcal{U}_{r}=U_{r}\,g_{-2A_{0}}\,r^{*}
, with boundary restriction

\mathcal{R}_{t}:L^{2}(Y_{t};S)\to L^{2}(Y_{t};S).

(4.7)

For each
k\in\mathcal{K}
, define

k^{\vee}=-k-2A_{0}.

(4.8)

Then

m^{\vee}=k^{\vee}+A_{0}=-m,

(4.9)

as discussed in
Section 3.7
.

Lemma 4.2

(Reflection exchanges the boundary labels)
.

For each
k\in\mathcal{K}
,

B_{0,A_{0}}^{(k^{\vee})}=-B_{0,A_{0}}^{(k)},\qquad B_{T,A_{0}}^{(k^{\vee})}=-B_{T,A_{0}}^{(k)}.

(4.10)

Proof.

Insert
m^{\vee}=-m
into

B_{0,A_{0}}^{(k)}=\frac{m}{f(0)}\sigma_{3},\qquad B_{T,A_{0}}^{(k)}=-\frac{m}{f(T)}\sigma_{3}.

(4.11)

∎

Lemma 4.3

(Reflection conjugates boundary blocks)
.

For each
k\in\mathcal{K}
and
t\in\{0,T\}
, one has

\mathcal{R}_{t}B^{(k)}_{t,A_{0}}\mathcal{R}_{t}^{-1}=B^{(k^{\vee})}_{t,A_{0}}.

(4.12)

Proof.

The boundary operators are

B^{(k)}_{0,A_{0}}=\frac{m}{f(0)}\sigma_{3},\qquad B^{(k)}_{T,A_{0}}=-\frac{m}{f(T)}\sigma_{3}.

(4.13)

The lifted reflection changes the Fourier label from
k
to
k^{\vee}=-k-2A_{0}
, so

m^{\vee}=-m.

(4.14)

On the spinor factor, the reflection matrix is
U_{r}=\sigma_{1}
, and

\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}\sigma_{1}=-\sigma_{3}.

(4.15)

Therefore, at
Y_{0}
,

\mathcal{R}_{0}B^{(k)}_{0,A_{0}}\mathcal{R}_{0}^{-1}=\frac{m}{f(0)}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}\sigma_{1}=-\frac{m}{f(0)}\sigma_{3}=\frac{m^{\vee}}{f(0)}\sigma_{3}=B^{(k^{\vee})}_{0,A_{0}}.

(4.16)

Similarly, at
Y_{T}
,

\mathcal{R}_{T}B^{(k)}_{T,A_{0}}\mathcal{R}_{T}^{-1}=-\frac{m}{f(T)}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}\sigma_{1}=\frac{m}{f(T)}\sigma_{3}=-\frac{m^{\vee}}{f(T)}\sigma_{3}=B^{(k^{\vee})}_{T,A_{0}}.

(4.17)

∎

Corollary 4.4

(Action on APS projections)
.

Assume
m\neq 0
. Then, for
t\in\{0,T\}
,

\mathcal{R}_{t}\,P_{>0}\!\bigl(B_{t,A_{0}}^{(k)}\bigr)\,\mathcal{R}_{t}^{-1}=P_{>0}\!\bigl(B_{t,A_{0}}^{(k^{\vee})}\bigr).

(4.18)

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
4.3
by functional calculus.
∎

4.4
Paired APS blocks

Let

L^{2}(M;S)=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathcal{H}_{k},\qquad\mathcal{H}_{k}\cong L^{2}([0,T],f(t)\,dt;\mathbb{C}^{2}),

(4.19)

be the Fourier decomposition. For
m\neq 0
, set
\mathcal{H}_{\{m,-m\}}=\mathcal{H}_{k}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{k^{\vee}}
. On this block, we have

D_{A_{0}}^{\APS}\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\{m,-m\}}}=\begin{pmatrix}D_{A_{0},m}^{\APS}&0\\
0&D_{A_{0},-m}^{\APS}\end{pmatrix}.

(4.20)

Proposition 4.5

(Unitary equivalence of paired APS blocks)
.

For each
m\neq 0
, the operators
D_{A_{0},m}^{\APS}
and
D_{A_{0},-m}^{\APS}
are unitarily equivalent. Consequently,

\Spec\bigl(D_{A_{0},m}^{\APS}\bigr)=\Spec\bigl(D_{A_{0},-m}^{\APS}\bigr)

(4.21)

with the same multiplicities.

Proof.

The lifted reflection
\mathcal{U}_{r}
maps the
k
-block unitarily to the
k^{\vee}
-block
and transports the APS condition by
Corollary
4.4
. Hence
\mathcal{U}_{r}\,D_{A_{0},m}^{\APS}\,\mathcal{U}_{r}^{-1}=D_{A_{0},-m}^{\APS}
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

The significance of Proposition
4.5
is structural: any later fixed-holonomy perturbation that preserves the same reflection symmetry inherits identical contributions from the paired sectors
m
and
-m
. Away from the self-paired sector
m=0
, spectral-flow contributions therefore occur in equal pairs.

4.5
Vanishing of boundary
\eta
-terms off the self-paired sector

For any self-adjoint operator
B
with discrete spectrum, we write

\eta(B,s)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Spec(B)\setminus\{0\}}\sign(\lambda)\,|\lambda|^{-s},\qquad\Re(s)\gg 0,

(4.22)

and denote by

\eta(B)=\eta(B,0)

(4.23)

its APS
\eta
-invariant, defined by meromorphic continuation to
s=0
. We also write

h(B)=\dim\ker(B),

(4.24)

and

\bar{\eta}(B)=\frac{\eta(B)+h(B)}{2}

(4.25)

for the reduced
\eta
-invariant.

For each Fourier mode
k\in\mathcal{K}
, the boundary operators are

B_{0,A_{0}}^{(k)}=\frac{m}{f(0)}\sigma_{3},\qquad B_{T,A_{0}}^{(k)}=-\frac{m}{f(T)}\sigma_{3},\qquad m=k+A_{0}.

(4.26)

If
m\neq 0
, then each boundary block is invertible and has spectrum symmetric about
0
:

\Spec\bigl(B_{0,A_{0}}^{(k)}\bigr)=\left\{\frac{m}{f(0)},-\frac{m}{f(0)}\right\},\qquad\Spec\bigl(B_{T,A_{0}}^{(k)}\bigr)=\left\{-\frac{m}{f(T)},\frac{m}{f(T)}\right\}.

(4.27)

Therefore,

\eta\!\bigl(B_{0,A_{0}}^{(k)}\bigr)=0,\qquad\eta\!\bigl(B_{T,A_{0}}^{(k)}\bigr)=0\qquad(m\neq 0).

(4.28)

Since the kernel is trivial for
m\neq 0
,

h\!\bigl(B_{0,A_{0}}^{(k)}\bigr)=0,\qquad h\!\bigl(B_{T,A_{0}}^{(k)}\bigr)=0\qquad(m\neq 0),

(4.29)

and hence also

\bar{\eta}\!\bigl(B_{0,A_{0}}^{(k)}\bigr)=0,\qquad\bar{\eta}\!\bigl(B_{T,A_{0}}^{(k)}\bigr)=0\qquad(m\neq 0).

(4.30)

Thus, every non-self-paired sector contributes trivially to the APS boundary correction on each boundary component separately. However, in the self-paired sector
m=0
, the boundary operator may have nontrivial kernel, and the reduced invariant

\bar{\eta}=(\eta+h)/2
need not vanish. Hence
m=0
is the unique sector in which a nontrivial boundary contribution can occur.

Remark 4.7
.

The self-paired sector
m=0
occurs exactly when
k=-A_{0}
. It is the unique sector fixed by the reflection pairing
m\leftrightarrow-m
, but in the present explicit model, the stronger statement holds that every nonzero boundary block already has vanishing
\eta
-invariant on its own because its spectrum is symmetric about
0
. The genuinely moving-family analysis of the self-paired sector is deferred to
Section 6
.

4.6
Ordinary APS index in the static line-twist case

We briefly record the ordinary chiral APS index associated with the fixed-holonomy
line-twist model. This is distinct from the reflection trace studied in
Section 5
: the latter is the natural symmetry-visible quantity for the orientation-reversing reflection,
whereas the ordinary APS index is the Fredholm index of the positive-chirality half of the
Dirac operator.

Let

D_{A_{0}}^{+}:\Gamma(S^{+})\longrightarrow\Gamma(S^{-})

(4.31)

denote the positive-chirality half of the twisted Dirac operator
D_{A_{0}}
, equipped with
the APS boundary condition induced by
\Dom(D_{A_{0}}^{\APS})
.

We write

B_{t,A_{0}}=\begin{pmatrix}B^{-}_{t,A_{0}}&0\\
0&B^{+}_{t,A_{0}}\end{pmatrix},\qquad t\in\{0,T\},

(4.32)

for the chiral decomposition of the full boundary operator on

S|_{Y_{t}}=S^{-}|_{Y_{t}}\oplus S^{+}|_{Y_{t}}
. Since
S^{-}
is the upper component and

S^{+}
is the lower component in our convention, the positive-chirality boundary operators
are given modewise by

B^{+}_{0,A_{0}}(k)=-\frac{m}{f(0)},\qquad B^{+}_{T,A_{0}}(k)=+\frac{m}{f(T)},\qquad m=k+A_{0}.

(4.33)

After identifying the two boundary circles by the angular coordinate, the endpoint
positive-chirality boundary operators satisfy the sign/scale relation

B^{+}_{T,A_{0}}=-\,c\,U\,B^{+}_{0,A_{0}}\,U^{-1},\qquad c=\frac{f(0)}{f(T)}>0,

(4.34)

with
U
unitary.

Proposition 4.8

(Vanishing of the ordinary APS index in the invertible fixed-holonomy case)
.

Assume
A=A_{0}
,
2A_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and

-A_{0}\notin\mathcal{K}.

(4.35)

Equivalently, the self-paired mode
m=0
is absent. Then

\ind\!\bigl(D_{A_{0},\APS}^{+}\bigr)=0.

(4.36)

Proof.

By the APS index theorem, specialized to the present flat line-twist model and written for the
positive-chirality boundary operators, the local index density has no interior contribution. Hence

\ind\!\bigl(D_{A_{0},\APS}^{+}\bigr)=-\bar{\eta}\!\left(B^{+}_{0,A_{0}}\right)-\bar{\eta}\!\left(B^{+}_{T,A_{0}}\right).

(4.37)

By (
4.34
), the endpoint positive-chirality boundary
operators satisfy

B^{+}_{T,A_{0}}=-\,c\,U\,B^{+}_{0,A_{0}}\,U^{-1},\qquad c=\frac{f(0)}{f(T)}>0.

(4.38)

Because
-A_{0}\notin\mathcal{K}
, the boundary operators are invertible. Therefore

\bar{\eta}(B)=\frac{\eta(B)}{2}.

(4.39)

Positive rescaling and unitary conjugation do not change the eta-invariant, while multiplication
by
-1
changes
\eta
to
-\eta
. Hence

\bar{\eta}\!\left(B^{+}_{T,A_{0}}\right)=-\bar{\eta}\!\left(B^{+}_{0,A_{0}}\right).

(4.40)

Therefore

\bar{\eta}\!\left(B^{+}_{0,A_{0}}\right)+\bar{\eta}\!\left(B^{+}_{T,A_{0}}\right)=0,

(4.41)

and the result follows.
∎

Remark 4.9

(Kernel correction)
.

If the self-paired sector is present, then the preceding reduced-
\eta
cancellation does not
hold in this simple form. Indeed, for a self-adjoint operator
B
,

\eta(-B)=-\eta(B),\qquad h(-B)=h(B),

(4.42)

where
h(B)=\dim\ker B
. Therefore

\bar{\eta}(-B)=-\bar{\eta}(B)+h(B).

(4.43)

Thus the reduced eta-invariant does not simply change sign in the presence of kernel. This is
why Proposition
4.8
is stated under the invertibility assumption

-A_{0}\notin\mathcal{K}
.

Remark 4.10
.

Thus, in the invertible fixed-holonomy line-twist model with flat twisting connection, the ordinary chiral APS index vanishes. When the self-paired sector is present, one must keep the finite-dimensional kernel correction described in Remark
4.6
.

5
Reflection trace for the APS operator with reflection symmetry

In this section, we study the part of the APS boundary problem that is visible to reflection symmetry.
Since the reflection element reverses orientation, the natural quantity is not a chiral equivariant
index, but rather the trace of the lifted reflection on the APS harmonic space

H^{\APS}=\ker(D^{\APS}).

(5.1)

Here
D^{\APS}
denotes the APS Dirac operator, so
H^{\APS}
is the finite-dimensional space of
APS-harmonic spinors, that is, solutions of

D^{\APS}\psi=0,\qquad\psi\in\Dom(D^{\APS}).

(5.2)

Using the Fourier decomposition and the reflection pairing from the previous sections, we obtain
the decomposition

\ker(D^{\APS})=\bigoplus_{\{k,k^{\vee}\}}\Bigl((\ker(D^{\APS})\cap\mathcal{H}_{k})\oplus(\ker(D^{\APS})\cap\mathcal{H}_{k^{\vee}})\Bigr)\oplus(\ker(D^{\APS})\cap\mathcal{H}_{-A_{0}}),

(5.3)

where the direct sum runs over all non-self-paired reflection orbits

\{k,k^{\vee}\},\qquad k^{\vee}=-k-2A_{0},\qquad k\neq-A_{0}.

(5.4)

The final summand
\ker(D^{\APS})\cap\mathcal{H}_{-A_{0}}
is the contribution of the unique self-paired Fourier block, when it occurs.

Accordingly, the reflection-sensitive quantity studied in this section is

\chi_{\text{ref}}(r)=\Tr_{H^{\APS}}\mathcal{U}_{r}.

(5.5)

The same Fourier-mode pairing as above will show that this reflection trace localizes to the
self-paired zero-mode sector.

5.1
Fixed twist and kernel convention

Assume throughout this section that

A=A_{0},\qquad 2A_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}.

(5.6)

Then the lifted reflection operator
\mathcal{U}_{r}
is globally defined. As in
Section 4
, its restriction to boundary data on
Y_{t}
for
t\in\{0,T\}
, is denoted by

\mathcal{R}_{t}:L^{2}(Y_{t};S)\longrightarrow L^{2}(Y_{t};S).

(5.7)

In the self-paired sector, we use the completed APS convention from
Remark
2.4
. When the self-paired mode
k=-A_{0}
occurs, the
corresponding boundary operator vanishes on that block. The auxiliary self-adjoint kernel
completion is assumed to be invariant under the boundary reflection
\mathcal{R}_{t}
. Equivalently,
the completed APS projector satisfies

\mathcal{R}_{t}\,P_{t}^{\APS}\,\mathcal{R}_{t}^{-1}=P_{t}^{\APS}

(5.8)

on the self-paired boundary block.

The APS operator used in this section is

D^{\APS}=D_{A_{0}}\big|_{\Dom(D^{\APS})},

(5.9)

with domain

\Dom(D^{\APS})=\Bigl\{\psi\in H^{1}(M;S):P_{0}^{\APS}\bigl(\psi|_{Y_{0}}\bigr)=0,\quad P_{T}^{\APS}\bigl(\psi|_{Y_{T}}\bigr)=0\Bigr\}.

(5.10)

5.2
Reflection preserves the APS boundary conditions

Proposition 5.1

(Reflection preserves APS boundary conditions)
.

Assume (
5.6
) and (
5.8
). Then

\mathcal{U}_{r}\bigl(\Dom(D^{\APS})\bigr)=\Dom(D^{\APS}),

(5.11)

and

\mathcal{U}_{r}\,D^{\APS}=D^{\APS}\,\mathcal{U}_{r}.

(5.12)

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
,

\mathcal{U}_{r}\,D\,\mathcal{U}_{r}^{-1}=D

(5.13)

on smooth sections, so it remains only to check preservation of the APS boundary condition.

For each nonzero mode
m=k+A_{0}\neq 0
, Proposition
3.8
gives the paired label,

is connected and contained in a discrete set. Therefore
A([0,1])
consists of a single
point, so
A
is constant on
[0,1]
.

The conclusion now follows immediately. Any non-constant holonomy deformation in the original
line-twist model cannot remain in the pointwise
O(2)
-equivariant case along the full path.
As a result, the
RO(O(2))
-valued spectral flow from
Section 6
is generally
unavailable for the entire deformation, and the remaining invariant is the

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued boundary crossing parity we introduce below.
∎

Remark 7.2

(Relation with
Section 6
)
.

Proposition
7.1
explains the dichotomy between the two moving-family cases treated in this paper.

In the gauge-trivial fixed-holonomy line-twist setting of
Section 6
, i.e.
[A_{0}]=0
and the gauge choice
A_{0}=0
, the ordinary spectral flow is even away from the self-paired sector, and the full invariant is the
RO(O(2))
-valued spectral flow; see Theorem
6.7
and Corollary
6.9
. However, a nontrivial holonomy deformation in the original line-twist model necessarily leaves that case. The representation-valued refinement is then lost, but the parity of the rank-one APS boundary crossing events survives. The present section isolates precisely this
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
datum.

7.1
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
crossing parity for holonomy deformations

Setup.

Fix the Fourier lattice

\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{(periodic spinors)},\qquad\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{Z}+\tfrac{1}{2}\quad\text{(anti-periodic spinors)}.

(7.4)

Let
A:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth holonomy path, and for
k\in\mathcal{K}
we define

m(s,k)=k+A(s).

(7.5)

The APS boundary data depend only on the sign of
m(s,k)
and can change only when

m(s,k)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k+A(s)=0.

(7.6)

For each
s\in[0,1]
, let

D_{s}^{\APS}

(7.7)

denote the APS operator corresponding to the holonomy value
A(s)
.

Assumptions.

Throughout this subsection, we assume:

k+A(0)\neq 0,\qquad k+A(1)\neq 0\qquad\text{for all }k\in\mathcal{K},

(7.8)

and whenever

k+A(s_{*})=0,

(7.9)

one has

A^{\prime}(s_{*})\neq 0.

(7.10)

These hypotheses ensure that crossings are isolated and simple.

Convention on spectral flow in this subsection.

Because the APS boundary condition changes at the crossing parameters
k+A(s_{*})=0
, the family
s\mapsto D_{s}^{\APS}
is in general only piecewise continuous as a family of self-adjoint Fredholm boundary problems. Therefore, throughout this subsection, the notation
\operatorname{sf}(D_{s}^{\APS})
refers to the
crossing spectral flow
of the holonomy family, specifically the signed sum of the local simple crossings contributions established for this warped-cylinder APS model in
[
11
]
.
Away from the crossing parameters, this agrees with the usual spectral flow on each
continuity interval.

Moreover, since
A([0,1])\subset\mathbb{R}
is compact, only finitely many
k\in\mathcal{K}
can satisfy
k+A(s)=0
for some

s\in[0,1]
. Under the transversality assumption
(
7.10
), all crossings are therefore finite in number and isolated.

To avoid confusion, this is not the ordinary spectral flow of a single continuous self-adjoint Fredholm path, but rather the boundary-crossing spectral flow defined by the modewise local crossing formula in
[
11
]
.

Proposition 7.3

(Crossing formula for holonomy deformations)
.

Assume (
7.8
) and
(
7.10
). Then the boundary crossing spectral flow of the APS holonomy family is

\operatorname{sf}\bigl(D_{s}^{\APS}\bigr)=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\ \sum_{s_{*}:\,k+A(s_{*})=0}\operatorname{sign}\bigl(A^{\prime}(s_{*})\bigr).

(7.11)

Equivalently, each transverse crossing contributes
\pm 1
, with sign
determined by
\operatorname{sign}(A^{\prime}(s_{*}))
.

Proof.

This is exactly the mode-by-mode boundary crossing formula established in

[
11
]
for the warped-cylinder APS holonomy family. After Fourier
reduction, a crossing is confined to a single mode
k
, in other words the one for
which
m(s,k)=k+A(s)
vanishes. Under the transversality hypothesis, that
crossing is simple, and its local contribution is

\operatorname{sign}(A^{\prime}(s_{*}))
. Summing the local contributions over all
crossings and all Fourier modes yields (
7.11
).
∎

Remark 7.4

(Modewise interpretation)
.

Proposition
7.3
is the mode-by-mode boundary crossing formula: in the present
holonomy-deformation case, spectral flow is determined entirely by signed crossings of the self-paired sector
k+A(s)=0
. When a full pointwise
O(2)
-equivariant structure is absent, this is
the correct replacement for an
RO(O(2))
-valued spectral-flow formula.

Unsigned boundary crossing count and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
reduction.

Define the total number of crossings by

N(A)=\#\bigl\{(s_{*},k)\in(0,1)\times\mathcal{K}:\ k+A(s_{*})=0\bigr\}.

(7.12)

Definition 7.5

(
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
crossing parity)
.

The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
boundary-parity invariant of the holonomy path
A
is

\operatorname{sf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)=\operatorname{sf}\bigl(D_{s}^{\APS}\bigr)\pmod{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

(7.13)

Equivalently,

\operatorname{sf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)=N(A)\pmod{2}.

(7.14)

Theorem 7.6

(Parity formula)
.

Under the standing assumptions,

\operatorname{sf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\#\bigl\{s_{*}\in(0,1):k+A(s_{*})=0\bigr\}\pmod{2}.

(7.15)

Hence
\operatorname{sf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)
is simply the parity of the total number of boundary crossings.

Proof.

Reduce the boundary crossing formula (
7.11
) modulo
2
. The signs

\operatorname{sign}(A^{\prime}(s_{*}))
disappear modulo
2
, and only the total number of
crossings remains.
∎

Proposition 7.7

(Local APS sign switch at a transverse boundary)
.

At a transverse crossing
k+A(s_{*})=0
, exactly one Fourier mode changes its
APS sign assignment, i.e. the mode
k
for which
m(s_{*},k)=0
.
Equivalently, in that mode, the boundary condition switches from the
m>0

form (
2.30a
) to the
m<0
form (
2.30b
), or vice
versa. Consequently,
\operatorname{sf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)
is the parity of
the number of simple APS sign-switch events along the path.

Proof.

Away from the boundary zeros, the APS boundary condition depends only on the sign of

m(s,k)=k+A(s)
. At a transverse zero of
m(s,k)
, exactly one Fourier
mode changes sign, and hence exactly one mode changes its APS boundary
assignment. Modulo
2
, counting such simple sign-switch events is exactly
the boundary crossing count of Theorem
7.6
.
∎

Corollary 7.8

(Monotone case)
.

If
A
is strictly monotone, then each equation
k+A(s)=0
has at most one solution, and

\operatorname{sf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)\equiv\#\bigl(\mathcal{K}\cap(-A(1),-A(0))\bigr)\pmod{2},

(7.16)

with the obvious interval modification if
A(1)<A(0)
.

Proof.

Under strict monotonicity, a boundary zero can be crossed at most once for each
k\in\mathcal{K}
. The
crossing parity is therefore obtained by counting which lattice points of
\mathcal{K}
lie
between the two endpoint values
-A(0)
and
-A(1)
.
∎

Remark 7.9

(Why endpoint data do not define an
RO(O(2))
-valued flow)
.

If the path
s\mapsto D_{s}^{\APS}
fails to be pointwise
O(2)
-equivariant for intermediate
s
, then the
RO(O(2))
-valued equivariant spectral flow is not defined for the full path, even if the endpoints admit a reflection lift. Nevertheless,

\operatorname{sf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)
is always defined under the standing assumptions and provides a robust invariant of the deformation.

7.2
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
crossing parity and sign-level determinant language

We now record a sign-level determinant/Pfaffian interpretation of the same parity invariant. We do not introduce a new invariant, and nor construct an actual determinant-line orientation transport for the present complex APS family. Rather, we interpret the same
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-class in a form closer to the determinant/Pfaffian sign language, in analogy with the real Fredholm setting.

Sign-level replacement in the non-
O(2)
-equivariant case.

For the complex APS family considered here, we do not introduce a determinant-line orientation transport as a primary invariant. The remaining sign-level invariant is instead the mod-two crossing parity from Definition
7.5
. In the present
holonomy-deformation model, this
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued quantity is the correct sign-level replacement for the representation-valued spectral flow available in the pointwise
O(2)
-equivariant case; compare the parity viewpoint for real Fredholm paths
[
25
]
and the determinant-line/orientation-transport formalism in the real setting
[
26
]
.

Warped cylinder

In the present holonomy-deformation model, crossings occur exactly when

k+A(s_{*})=0.

(7.17)

By the mode-by-mode boundary crossing analysis from

[
11
]
, a transverse crossing affects exactly one Fourier
mode, and exactly one boundary eigenvalue crosses zero. Equivalently, the associated APS family undergoes a simple one-dimensional kernel event.

Corollary 7.10

(Crossing parity as the remaining global sign)
.

If the endpoints admit a reflection lift, but the path is not pointwise

O(2)
-equivariant, then the corresponding sign-level invariant of the deformation is

\operatorname{sf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)
. Equivalently, it is the parity of the total number
of rank-one APS boundary crossing events.

Proof.

By Proposition
7.1
, a non-constant holonomy path in the line-twist
model cannot remain pointwise
O(2)
-equivariant, so the representation-valued invariant
of
Section 6
is unavailable along the full path. By Theorem
7.6
and Proposition
7.7
, the remaining sign-level data of the deformation is exactly the crossing parity

\operatorname{sf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)
, i.e., the parity of the simple APS
sign-switch events along the path.
∎

Example (periodic spin structure)

Assume
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{Z}
, and consider the path

A(s)=\frac{1}{2}+s,\qquad s\in[0,1].

(7.18)

Then the endpoint invertibility condition
(
7.8
) holds, and there is exactly one boundary crossing, at
s_{*}=\tfrac{1}{2}
for the mode
k=-1
. Hence,

N(A)=1,\qquad\operatorname{sf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)=1.

(7.19)

Thus, the path has odd crossing parity: exactly one simple APS sign-switch event
occurs along the deformation.

Remark 7.11

(Domain-wall interpretation of the mod-two parity)
.

The parity invariant
\operatorname{sf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)
admits a natural
conceptual interpretation in the light of the mod-two APS/domain-wall
correspondence. In the formulation of

[
27
]
, the mod-two APS index of a real Dirac problem on a manifold with boundary is reformulated on a
closed manifold by replacing the boundary with a sign-changing mass term, i.e.
by a domain wall. In that picture, the mod-two index is read as a fermionic sign
associated with the wall system, and the bulk and edge contributions are
naturally separated.

The present warped-cylinder holonomy deformation is not itself rewritten here as
a domain-wall fermion problem, so we do
not
claim a direct identification
theorem. Nevertheless, the role of

\operatorname{sf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)
is fully consistent with that
perspective: when pointwise
O(2)
-equivariance is lost and the full

RO(O(2))
-valued spectral flow of
Section 6
is unavailable, the deformation still carries a robust sign-level invariant, namely the parity
of the simple APS boundary crossing events. Thus,
\operatorname{sf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)
may
be viewed as the natural mod-two shadow of the more refined symmetry-resolved
spectral-flow data.

Pfaffian and domain-wall language.

For real skew-adjoint or Majorana-type formulations, one often packages the same
mod-two information as a Pfaffian sign: a simple eigenvalue crossing flips the
Pfaffian sign once. From this viewpoint, the parity class

\operatorname{sf}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(A)
may be regarded as the global fermionic
sign obstruction attached to the holonomy path. This interpretation is
compatible with the domain-wall reformulation of the mod-two APS index in

[
27
]
, where the
boundary problem is replaced by a sign-changing mass term on a closed manifold.
We emphasize again that in the present paper, this is an interpretive analogy,
not an additional equivalence theorem.

Appendix A
Numerical illustration of the single-mode APS spectrum

We briefly record the numerical procedure used to generate the single-mode spectrum plot in the
reflection-compatible fixed-holonomy case. In the sample computation, we take

T=3,\qquad\alpha=1,\qquad A_{0}=\frac{1}{2},\qquad k=1,

(A.1)

so that

m=k+A_{0}=\frac{3}{2}>0.

(A.2)

Thus, we are in a non-boundary-zero mode of the reflection-compatible sector

A=A_{0},\qquad 2A_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}.

(A.3)

We work with the warped factor

f(t)=e^{t}+\alpha e^{-t}=e^{t}+e^{-t},\qquad t\in[0,T]=[0,3].

(A.4)

Starting from the mode system

A^{+}v=-i\lambda u,\qquad A^{-}u=-i\lambda v,\qquad A^{\pm}=\partial_{t}+\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{2f(t)}\pm\frac{m}{f(t)},

(A.5)

it is convenient to write

p(t)=\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{2f(t)},\qquad q(t)=\frac{m}{f(t)}.

(A.6)

Then

A^{+}=\partial_{t}+p+q,\qquad A^{-}=\partial_{t}+p-q.

(A.7)

Applying
A^{+}
to the equation
A^{-}u=-i\lambda v
gives

A^{+}A^{-}u=-\lambda^{2}u,

(A.8)

while applying
A^{-}
to
A^{+}v=-i\lambda u
gives

A^{-}A^{+}v=-\lambda^{2}v.

(A.9)

Hence
u
and
v
each satisfy a scalar second-order equation. To remove the first derivative
term, we set

u(t)=f(t)^{-1/2}U(t),\qquad v(t)=f(t)^{-1/2}V(t).

(A.10)

A direct computation then yields the decoupled real equations

U^{\prime\prime}+\bigl(\lambda^{2}-q^{\prime}(t)-q(t)^{2}\bigr)U=0,

(A.11a)

and

V^{\prime\prime}+\bigl(\lambda^{2}+q^{\prime}(t)-q(t)^{2}\bigr)V=0.

(A.11b)

Equivalently, since
q(t)=m/f(t)
,

U^{\prime\prime}+\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{m\,f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)^{2}}-\frac{m^{2}}{f(t)^{2}}\right)U=0,

(A.12)

and

V^{\prime\prime}+\left(\lambda^{2}-\frac{m\,f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)^{2}}-\frac{m^{2}}{f(t)^{2}}\right)V=0.

(A.13)

The APS boundary condition becomes a scalar boundary-value problem, and it is useful to record both sign cases separately.

Case
m>0
.

In this case, the APS condition is

u(0)=0,\qquad v(T)=0.

(A.14)

Using

A^{-}u=-i\lambda v

(A.15)

at
t=T
, the condition
v(T)=0
is equivalent to

A^{-}u(T)=0.

(A.16)

Since

A^{-}\!\bigl(f^{-1/2}U\bigr)=f^{-1/2}(U^{\prime}-qU),

(A.17)

the scalar
U
-problem becomes

U(0)=0,\qquad U^{\prime}(T)-q(T)U(T)=0.

(A.18)

Thus, one may solve the
U
-equation with the normalized initial data

U(0)=0,\qquad U^{\prime}(0)=1,

(A.19)

and define the shooting function

S_{U}(\lambda)=U^{\prime}(T;\lambda)-q(T)U(T;\lambda).

(A.20)

Its zeros are exactly the APS eigenvalues of the chosen mode.

Equivalently, one may work with the
V
-equation. Using

A^{+}v=-i\lambda u

(A.21)

at
t=0
, the condition
u(0)=0
is equivalent to

A^{+}v(0)=0.

(A.22)

Since

A^{+}\!\bigl(f^{-1/2}V\bigr)=f^{-1/2}(V^{\prime}+qV),

(A.23)

the scalar
V
-problem becomes

V^{\prime}(0)+q(0)V(0)=0,\qquad V(T)=0.

(A.24)

Thus, one may instead solve the
V
-equation with the normalized initial data

V(0)=1,\qquad V^{\prime}(0)=-q(0),

(A.25)

and define

S_{V}(\lambda)=V(T;\lambda).

(A.26)

Again, its zeros are exactly the APS eigenvalues.

Case
m<0
.

In this case, the APS condition is

v(0)=0,\qquad u(T)=0.

(A.27)

Using

A^{-}u=-i\lambda v

(A.28)

at
t=0
, the condition
v(0)=0
is equivalent to

A^{-}u(0)=0.

(A.29)

Hence the scalar
U
-problem becomes

U^{\prime}(0)-q(0)U(0)=0,\qquad U(T)=0.

(A.30)

A convenient normalization is

U(0)=1,\qquad U^{\prime}(0)=q(0),

(A.31)

and the corresponding shooting function is

S_{U}(\lambda)=U(T;\lambda).

(A.32)

Equivalently, using

A^{+}v=-i\lambda u

(A.33)

at
t=T
, the condition
u(T)=0
is equivalent to

A^{+}v(T)=0,

(A.34)

so the scalar
V
-problem becomes

V(0)=0,\qquad V^{\prime}(T)+q(T)V(T)=0.

(A.35)

Thus, one may solve the
V
-equation with the normalized initial data

V(0)=0,\qquad V^{\prime}(0)=1,

(A.36)

and define

S_{V}(\lambda)=V^{\prime}(T;\lambda)+q(T)V(T;\lambda).

(A.37)

In either sign case, the zeros of the corresponding scalar shooting function are precisely the APS
eigenvalues of the chosen Fourier block. For the numerical plot in the main text, we use the sample
values

T=3,\qquad\alpha=1,\qquad A_{0}=\frac{1}{2},\qquad k=1,\qquad m=\frac{3}{2}>0,

(A.38)

so the displayed graph is the shooting function

S(\lambda)=S_{U}(\lambda)=U^{\prime}(T;\lambda)-q(T)U(T;\lambda)

(A.39)

associated with the
U
-equation.

is included only as a numerical illustration of the
single-block spectrum in the warped fixed-holonomy model; none of the proofs in the main text
depend on this computation.
