Attaining 19.12% solar cell efficiency for non-fused ring electron acceptors via multi-dimensional charge transport
Authors: Miao Li, Qianqian Zhu, Nan Wei, Yuanyuan Zhou, Guangliu Ran, Shuaishuai Shen, Zhiying Ma, Longfei Jia, Wenchao Zhao, Jiahe Zhang, Wenkai Zhang, Wenjun Zhang, Yuanyuan Zuo, Ruiping Qin, Xiaodan Tang, Congxin Xia, Jinsheng Song, Zhishan Bo, Yahui Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Non-fused ring electron acceptors (NFREAs) exhibit substantial commercial application potential. Nevertheless, their subpar electron transport performance results in slightly low power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of organic solar cells (OSCs). Herein, we put forward an innovative supramolecular side-chain-induced multi-dimensional charge transport strategy to exploit two NFREAs 3TT-Ph2 and 3TT-Ph4 with terminal phenyl/fluorinated phenyl side chains. In comparison to the unfunctionalized control molecule 3TT-2, both 3TT-Ph2 and 3TT-Ph4 form additional phenyl-acceptor (Ph-A) supramolecular interactions between the phenyl side groups and the cyanoindanone end groups. Meanwhile, these fluorinated terminal phenyl groups can induce the formation of multiple supramolecular interactions, including F···F, F···H and F···C. The synergistic effect of these supramolecular interactions promotes the tight and ordered stacking of molecules, facilitates the construction of multi-dimensional charge transport channel. Finally, D18:3TT-Ph4-based device achieved a record-breaking PCE of 19.12% and a fill factor of 80.65%. Our research pave an effective strategy for the molecular design of high-performance NFREAs.

Introduction

Organic solar cells (OSCs), representing a cutting-edge renewable energy technology, have garnered significant attention owing to their distinctive merits, including mechanical flexibility, lightweight, semi-transparency, and solution-processability1–5. Over the past decade, remarkable advancements have been achieved in active layer materials, particularly with the rapid evolution of fused ring electron acceptors (FREAs)6–10. These developments have propelled the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of single-junction OSCs from below 10% to surpass 20%11–14. Notably, FREAs exemplified by ITIC, Y6, and their derivatives demonstrate exceptional photovoltaic performance attributed to their rigid planar core architectures, which facilitate efficient charge transport and ordered molecular self-assembly15–20. However, the multi-fused-ring aromatic frameworks inherent to FREAs present formidable challenges, such as lengthy synthetic pathways and low yields, which severely impede their large-scale industrial production and commercial viability. In this context, non-fused ring electron acceptors (NFREAs), characterized by aromatic rings or small fused rings interconnected via rotatable C-C single bonds, have emerged as promising alternatives21–26. This design strategy circumvents the low-yield cyclization reactions associated with FREAs and enables direct modular coupling synthesis, offering advantages of reduced synthesis costs and enhanced molecular design flexibility27–32.

Despite these benefits, NFREAs still confront several critical issues: the rotatable C-C single bonds can induce main-chain distortion, disrupting the ordered molecular packing; concurrently, strong intermolecular interactions can induce extensive molecular aggregation, leading to deteriorated material solubility and less-than-ideal active layer morphologies33–37. To mitigate these problems, researchers often introduce sterically hindered side groups (e.g., 2,6-dialkoxybenzene, diarylamine) to enhance molecular solubility and regulate molecular conformations and aggregation38–44. Although these side groups can induce planar main-chain conformations and suppress excessive molecular aggregation, they introduce drawbacks: the large dihedral angles between the side groups and the main chain weaken electron coupling; additionally, excessively lateral bulky substituents can disrupt close molecular packing45,46. Both factors collectively hinder efficient charge transport. In stark contrast, fused ring systems, such as Y-series acceptors, leverage their unique banana-shaped molecular backbones and specific intermolecular interactions between end groups/end groups or end groups/cores to form three-dimensional (3D) molecular packing structures with multi-dimensional charge transport pathways47–52. This structural advantage enables them to achieve a PCE breakthrough exceeding 20%53–57. Consequently, addressing the charge transport bottlenecks of NFREAs and constructing efficient charge transport channels have become pivotal scientific challenges in the pursuit of high-performance NFREAs.

In this study, we propose an innovative molecular design strategy that involves introducing supramolecular interaction sites on the side groups of NFREAs. By inducing multiple non-covalent interactions, we can construct multi-dimensional charge transport channels and thus achieve efficient charge transport. We successfully synthesized two NFREAs, namely 3TT-Ph2 and 3TT-Ph4, and then systematically investigated the influence of terminal phenyl groups on molecular packing and physicochemical properties. Single-crystal analysis revealed that, compared with the non-functionalized 3TT-2, both 3TT-Ph2 and 3TT-Ph4 retained the traditional A-π/π-A main-chain packing mode. Moreover, they also exhibited additional Ph-A supramolecular interactions between the phenyl side-groups and the cyanoindanone end-groups. Notably, the fluorinated terminal phenyl groups can trigger the formation of multiple supramolecular interactions. These interactions include F···H/F···C non-covalent interactions between the terminal phenyl groups and alkyl side-chains, as well as F···F/F···H non-covalent interactions among the terminal phenyl groups. These synergistic supramolecular interactions facilitate the close and ordered packing of acceptor molecules, establish multi-dimensional charge transport channels, and significantly enhance charge transport properties. Moreover, when blended with the polymer donor D18, the D18:3TT-Ph4 blend film forms a nanofiber structure, exhibits fast exciton dissociation/diffusion capabilities, and demonstrates high-efficiency charge transport characteristics. Consequently, the D18:3TT-Ph4 device achieves a record-high PCE of 19.12% and a fill factor (FF) of 80.65%. This performance not only outperforms the highest reported value for NFREAs-based OSCs but also far surpasses that of 3TT-Ph2 (17.59%) and 3TT-2 (16.40%) devices. This study demonstrates that supramolecular side-group design is in favor of achieving close and ordered molecular packing and constructing multi-dimensional charge transport channels. It opens up an efficient avenue for the molecular design of high-performance NFREAs and holds significant scientific and practical value for the further advancement of OSCs.

Results

Material design and characterization

The synthetic pathways for the 3TT-series acceptors (3TT-2, 3TT-Ph2, and 3TT-Ph4) are delineated in Fig. 1a. The preparation commenced with the synthesis of precursor compound 1 according to previously reported procedures58. A Stille cross-coupling reaction was executed between compound 1 and tributyl(6-hexylthieno[3,2-b]thiophen-2-yl)stannane, yielding compound 2 with a 73% yield. Subsequent demethylation of compound 2 using BBr₃ furnished the tetrahydroxy intermediate 3 in 97% yield. Substitution reactions were performed with brominated alkanes to generate compounds 4a, 4b, and 4c, achieving yields ranging from 78% to 95%. These intermediates were then subjected to the Vilsmeier-Haack reaction, successfully yielding the dialdehyde derivatives 5a, 5b, and 5c with yields between 81% and 88%. Ultimately, the target acceptors 3TT-2, 3TT-Ph2, and 3TT-Ph4 were synthesized via Knoevenagel condensation between the dialdehyde precursors and 3-(1,1-dicyanomethylene)-5,6-difluoro-1-indanone (IC-2F), attaining yields of 87%, 86%, and 91%, respectively. All three acceptors demonstrated good solubility in prevalent organic solvents, including dichloromethane, chloroform, and o-dichlorobenzene. Single crystal analysis

The chemical structures of three acceptors (3TT-2, 3TT-Ph2, and 3TT-Ph4) were further verified by cultivating single crystals, which can also provide visualized and critical insights into molecular geometries, intermolecular stacking modes, and charge transport properties after the phenyl side-chain modification. Specifically, we employed the solvent diffusion method, utilizing chloroform as the good solvent and methanol as the poor solvent, and successfully obtained single crystals of these three acceptors. The relevant results are depicted in Fig. 2 and Fig. 3, and detailed crystallographic parameters are listed in Supplementary Table 1. As illustrated in Fig. 2, 3TT-2 and 3TT-Ph4 exhibit only one molecular conformation, whereas 3TT-Ph2 displays two conformations, designated as α and β, respectively. For all three acceptors, the three thiophene[3,2-b]thiophene (TT) units adopt a cis configuration. A certain torsion angle can be observed between the central TT unit and its adjacent TT units. Meanwhile, the 2,6-dialkoxyphenyl side groups adopt an orientation nearly perpendicular to the molecular backbone, with the torsion angles varying in the range of 66.19°–78.53°. Notably, these torsion angles are increased as the substituent transitions from a 3,5-bis(alkyloxy)phenyl group (in 3TT-2) to a 3,5-bis(ω-phenylalkyloxy)phenyl group (in 3TT-Ph2) and then to a 3,5-bis(ω-(3-fluorophenyl)alkyloxy)phenyl group (in 3TT-Ph4). This change is primarily attributed to the steric hindrance introduced by the 3,5-bis(ω-(3-fluorophenyl)alkyloxy)phenyl group. Additionally, the dihedral angles between the TT units and the IC-2F end groups are relatively small, all less than 4°, indicating that the end-group moieties possess good planarity. Further analysis of the single-crystal results revealed that all three acceptors exhibit an ordered two-dimensional (2D) layered arrangement, albeit with discernible variations in their packing modes. For acceptor 3TT-2, a single dominant A-π/π-A packing mode is observed between the IC-2F end group and the adjacent TT unit, characterized by a π-π stacking distance of 3.32 Å. In contrast, acceptors 3TT-Ph2 and 3TT-Ph4 exhibit altered packing modes. Specifically, both conformations of acceptor 3TT-Ph2 (3TT-Ph2-α and 3TT-Ph2-β) retain the A-π/π-A packing mode, yet exhibit slightly increased π-π stacking distances of 3.35 Å and 3.42 Å, respectively. In comparison, 3TT-Ph4 demonstrates more compact A-π/π-A packing mode, with smaller stacking distances of 3.27 Å and 3.42 Å, respectively, stemming from the different distortion of two IC-2F end groups. More importantly, in 3TT-Ph2 and 3TT-Ph4, we observe a novel intermolecular packing mode (designated as Ph-A) between the terminal phenyl side group and the IC-2F end group, with corresponding π-π stacking distances of 3.60 Å and 3.35 Å, respectively. Clearly, the introduction of a terminal phenyl group can induce Ph-A molecular interactions, thereby promoting intermolecular packing and facilitating the construction of new charge transport channels. Meanwhile, we employed the independent gradient model method (IGMH) based on Hirshfeld partitioning to verify the existence of Ph-A intermolecular interactions, with the results shown in Fig. 2d. In 3TT-Ph2 and 3TT-Ph4, the Ph-A interactions (green surfaces) can be visually observed, indicating the presence of supramolecular interactions between the terminal phenyl group and the IC-2F moiety in 3TT-Ph2 and 3TT-Ph4.

Additionally, as shown in Fig. 3, the introduction of a terminal 3-fluorophenyl group in 3TT-Ph4 can induce multiple non-covalent interactions, such as F···H and F···C non-covalent interactions between the fluorophenyl and alkyl side chains, as well as F···F and F···H non-covalent interactions between the two terminal fluorophenyl groups in adjacent molecules. These interactions not only enhance molecular stability but may also have a positive impact on charge transport properties. Meanwhile, from the crystal structure, we also obtained information on the lamellar distance (dlamellar). The dlamellar value of 3TT-Ph2 is 9.88 Å, significantly larger than that of 3TT-2 (9.52 Å), which may be ascribed to the steric hindrance of the terminal phenyl ring. Further, the dlamellar value of 3TT-Ph4 decreases to about 9.39 Å, primarily due to the formation of multiple supramolecular interactions upon the introduction of 3-fluorophenyl groups. Furthermore, as shown in Supplementary Fig. 2, S···F and O···H etc., non-covalent interactions are confirmed in 3TT-2, which is similar to 3TT-Ph4, facilitating the small layer distance as well. In summary, 3TT-Ph4 can form a tight molecular arrangement via multiple intermolecular non-covalent interactions, thereby establishing efficient charge transport pathways and contributing to high Jsc and PCE for OSCs.

To further evaluate the influence of terminal phenyl groups on charge transport properties, the electron transfer integrals (te) between adjacent molecules were calculated from the single-crystal stacking data of three acceptors by using density functional theory (DFT) calculations at the pw91pw91/6-31 g* level. As shown in Fig. 2, for 3TT-2, the te value is only 7.82 meV for A-π/π-A packing mode. In comparison, the significantly increased te values of 20.68 and 23.36 meV for 3TT-Ph2, 19.12 and 18.88 meV for 3TT-Ph4 can be obtained, indicating more efficient charge transport in 3TT-Ph2 and 3TT-Ph4 incorporated terminal phenyl groups. Meanwhile, extra electron transport channels are also observed in 3TT-Ph2 and 3TT-Ph4 via super-exchange electron coupling, where the electron hopping occurs between the IC-2F end groups of acceptors and embedded phenyl groups. The calculated te values are 0.27 and 0.46 meV for 3TT-Ph2 and 3TT-Ph4, respectively. Further, the phenyl groups also participated in the formation of 2D charge transport channels via Ph-A super-exchange electron coupling, providing extra charge transport channels. In one word, the tethered phenyl groups in 3TT-Ph2 and 3TT-Ph4 can induce the formation of multi-dimensional charge transport channels, thus promoting more efficient charge transport.

Device performance

We fabricated OSCs by using the conventional ITO/2PACz/active layer/PDINN/Ag structure to evaluate the photovoltaic performance of three NFREAs (3TT-2, 3TT-Ph2, and 3TT-Ph4). The classic polymer D18 was selected as the electron donor due to its complementary light absorption characteristics and well-matched energy levels with three acceptor materials. Throughout the device fabrication process, we systematically optimized key parameters including the donor/acceptor ratio, additives, and thermal annealing conditions. The detailed optimization process is provided in the Supporting Information. After optimization, the current density-voltage (J-V) curves and corresponding photovoltaic parameters of the devices are shown in Fig. 4 and Table 2. The device based on 3TT-2 delivered a moderate PCE of 16.40% with a Voc of 0.918 V, a Jsc of 23.61 mA/cm², and an FF of 75.64%; whereas the device based on 3TT-Ph2 gave a higher PCE of 17.59% with the enhanced Jsc of 24.59 mA/cm2 and FF of 78.21%. Further, the device based on 3TT-Ph4 set a record PCE of up to 19.12%, with a Voc of 0.902 V, a Jsc of 26.30 mA/cm², and an FF of 80.65%. As shown in Fig. 4c and Supplementary Table 6, this PCE and FF are among the highest values reported to date for OSCs based on NFREAs. The excellent performance of the D18:3TT-Ph4 device is mainly attributed to the significant improvements in Jsc and FF, which are due to the tight and ordered molecular packing and efficient charge transport. As shown in Fig. 4b, further evidence for the performance trends was provided by the external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra. All three devices exhibited a broad-spectrum photo-response from 300 nm to 900 nm, which is consistent with their absorption spectra. Among them, the device based on 3TT-Ph4 showed an extremely high EQE response, with a maximum value of 90%, indicating highly efficient charge generation, transport, and collection processes. To gain deeper insight into the exciton dissociation and charge collection behavior in devices incorporating the three acceptors, the dependence of photocurrent density (Jph) on the effective voltage (Veff) was measured, where Jph is calculated as Jph = JL − JD, with JL and JD representing the current densities under illumination and dark conditions, respectively. As illustrated in Fig. 4d, the exciton dissociation probabilities (Pdiss) of the device based on 3TT-2, 3TT-Ph2, and 3TT-Ph4 are determined to be 98.2%, 98.5%, and 99.1%, respectively. Compared with D18:3TT-2 and D18:3TT-Ph2, the D18:3TT-Ph4 device exhibits a high Pdiss value, indicating effective exciton dissociation and charge collection within the active layer. Meanwhile, the charge recombination dynamics were probed by examining the dependence of Jsc on light density (Plight). The relationship can be described by Jsc ∝ Plightα, where the exponential factor α serves as the bimolecular recombination parameter. As shown in Fig. 4e, the derived α values of three devices based on 3TT-2, 3TT-Ph2, and 3TT-Ph4 are 0.988, 0.990, and 0.996, respectively. The values approaching unity, particularly for 3TT-Ph4, suggest significantly suppressed bimolecular recombination.

Additionally, the transient photocurrent (TPC) and transient photovoltage (TPV) techniques were conducted to further elucidate the charge extraction and recombination characteristics. As illustrated in Fig. 4, the charge extraction time (τext) and photogenerated carrier lifetime (τpho) of three optimized devices are 0.29/1.26 µs for D18:3TT-2, 0.26/1.49 µs for D18:3TT-Ph2, and 0.18/2.28 µs for D18:3TT-Ph4, respectively. Evidently, the extended carrier lifetime (2.28 μs) coupled with a shortened extraction time (0.18 μs) observed in the D18:3TT-Ph4 device indicates markedly reduced charge recombination and efficient charge extraction. Meanwhile, the charge carrier mobility (μ) was also estimated via photon-induced charge-carrier extraction by linearly increasing voltage (Photo-CELIV) measurements, and the results are displayed in Fig. 4f. The μ values of optimized devices based on 3TT-2, 3TT-Ph2, and 3TT-Ph4 can be estimated to be 1.41×10−4, 1.80×10−4, and 2.24×10−4 cm2V-1s-1, indicating that efficient charge transport in the D18:3TT-Ph4 device. In one word, these comprehensive charge dynamic studies corroborate the superior Jsc and FF achieved in the 3TT-Ph4-based device for OSCs.

The hole and electron mobilities (μh and μe) for 3TT-2, 3TT-Ph2, and 3TT-Ph4-based blend films were determined using space-charge-limited-current (SCLC) measurements. Hole-only and electron-only devices were fabricated with the architectures ITO/PEDOT: PSS/active layer/MoO₃/Ag and ITO/ZnO/active layer/PDINN/Ag, respectively. The results are summarized in Supplementary Fig. 3 and Supplementary Table 7, and statistical histograms are depicted in Fig. 4i. The measured μh and μe values are 3.45×10⁻⁴/2.13×10⁻⁴ cm²V⁻¹s⁻¹ for D18:3TT-2 based devices, 4.14×10⁻⁴/2.85×10⁻⁴ cm²V⁻¹s⁻¹ for D18:3TT-Ph2 based devices, and 6.01×10⁻⁴/4.95×10⁻⁴ cm²V⁻¹s⁻¹ for D18:3TT-Ph4 based devices, yielding corresponding μh/μe ratio of 1.62, 1.45, and 1.20, respectively. Notably, the D18:3TT-Ph4 blend film exhibits high and more balanced charge transport properties, which contribute to the improved Jsc and FF for OSCs.

Films investigation

The atomic force microscopy (AFM) was employed to investigate the influence of the tethered phenyl side chain on the blend film morphology, and the results are shown in Fig. 5. The blend film based on 3TT-2 exhibits a rough surface morphology with the root mean square (RMS) roughness value of 1.36 nm. With the introduction of terminal phenyl groups, the D18:3TT-Ph2 blend film displays a smooth surface morphology, corresponding to the decreased RMS value of 1.09 nm. The incorporation of fluorine atoms can slightly increase the RMS value of the D18:3TT-Ph4 blend film (1.33 nm). Meanwhile, distinct interpenetrating fibrous network structures can be observed in D18:3TT-2 and D18:3TT-Ph4 blend films. AFM-IR, which combines conventional AFM and infrared spectroscopy, was utilized to further characterize the composition distribution in blend films. Here, 1580 cm⁻¹ was selected as the specific peak of the donor component, and 1342 cm⁻¹ as that of the acceptor component. In contrast, D18:3TT-Ph2 and D18:3TT-Ph4 blend films form relatively uniform morphologies. The 3TT-2, 3TT-Ph2, and 3TT-Ph4 blend films exhibit fiber sizes of 14.43, 12.24, and 13.65 nm, respectively. Charge carrier dynamic analysis

To elucidate the hole transfer dynamic process, we systematically investigated the femtosecond transient absorption (fs-TA) spectra for both neat and blend films of these three acceptors (3TT-2, 3TT-Ph2, and 3TT-Ph4). A pump wavelength of 800 nm was selected to exclusively excite the acceptor materials, thereby avoiding exciting the donor, and the corresponding results are presented in Fig. 7 and Supplementary Figs. 8-9. In the pristine acceptor films, two distinct ground-state bleaching (GSB) features are observed around 720 and 830 nm, respectively. These characteristic peaks discernibly remain in the corresponding blend film as well. Notably, a previously unobserved negative band emerges near 600 nm in the blend films, and this phenomenon can be assigned to the GSB signal of donor D18, considering that there are no such signals in pure acceptor films. This observation of novel GSB signals can provide direct evidence of hole transfer from the acceptor to D18. Of particular interest, the blend film based on 3TT-Ph4 exhibits a markedly intensified GSB signal at 600 nm compared to those with 3TT-2 and 3TT-Ph2, indicating a more efficient hole transfer process in the D18:3TT-Ph4 blend system.Fig. 7Charge carrier dynamic process.a contour plots of the time-resolved absorption difference spectra, b TA spectra at different delay times, c kinetic decay traces at selected wavelength and d singlet exciton decay dynamics with different excitation fluence.

Furthermore, the charge carrier dynamics in the blend films were probed by analyzing the decay kinetics of the donor GSB peak at 600 nm using a biexponential fitting function. The resulting time constants, τ1 and τ2, were determined to be 4.29 ± 1.24 ps/34.74 ± 3.68 ps for D18:3TT-2, 3.85 ± 1.16 ps/34.04 ± 2.88 ps for D18:3TT-Ph2, and 3.48 ± 0.50 ps/29.78 ± 1.46 ps for D18:3TT-Ph4, respectively. In this context, τ1 corresponds to the characteristic time for exciton dissociation at the donor/acceptor interface, while τ2 is associated with the timescale of photo-induced exciton diffusion within the domain. Notably, the D18:3TT-Ph4 blend exhibits markedly accelerated exciton dissociation and diffusion, which collectively contribute to efficient charge generation and collection. Meanwhile, the exciton diffusion lengths (LD) of three pure acceptor films were further investigated by applying the exciton-exciton annihilation (EEA) model and fs-TA spectra. As shown in Fig. 7d and Supplementary Table 8, the LD values can be calculated to be 25.49 nm for 3TT-2, 31.94 nm for 3TT-Ph2, and 35.49 nm for 3TT-Ph4, respectively. Obviously, 3TT-Ph4 possesses a long exciton diffusion distance to mitigate the exciton recombination, thereby facilitating high Jsc for OSCs.

Universality and economic scalability

To evaluate the universality of acceptor 3TT-Ph4, we fabricated binary OSCs using several representative polymer donors, including D18-Cl, PM6, and PBQx-TF. The corresponding J-V and EQE characteristics are presented in Fig. 8, with detailed parameters summarized in Supplementary Table 9. Notably, all the devices show PCEs exceeding 17% when paired with diverse polymer donors, demonstrating the excellent versatility of 3TT-Ph4. Discussion

In summary, we have successfully synthesized two NFERAs, namely 3TT-Ph2 and 3TT-Ph4, through the separate introduction of phenyl and 3-fluorophenyl groups at the terminal positions of the alkyl chains within the 2,5-dialkoxybenzene steric side-chains. The incorporation of 3-fluorophenyl groups, being capable of forming supramolecular interactions, enables regulation of molecular packing and charge-transport properties. In comparison with the control acceptor 3TT-2, which lacks terminal phenyl groups, 3TT-Ph2 and 3TT-Ph4 not only exhibit the conventional A-π/π-A packing but also form additional Ph-A stacking between the phenyl side-chains and the IC-2F end-groups. Of particular significance is the fact that the fluorine atom introduced onto the terminal phenyl group induces multiple non-covalent interactions (F···F, F···C, and F···H). These interactions serve to stabilize the molecular conformation of 3TT-Ph4, reduce the interlayer spacing, and consequently lead to the formation of a tightly packed molecular arrangement and multi-dimensional charge transport channels. When blended with the polymer donor D18, the blend film based on 3TT-Ph4 displays a well-defined nanofiber structure, along with ultra-rapid exciton dissociation and diffusion characteristics, as well as highly efficient charge transport capabilities. As a result, the device based on D18:3TT-Ph4 achieves a record-high PCE of 19.12%, accompanied by a Voc of 0.902 V, a Jsc of 26.30 mA/cm², and an exceptionally high FF of 80.65%, surpassing that of D18:3TT-2 (16.40%) and D18:3TT-Ph2 (17.59%). The PCE of 19.12% and the FF of 80.65% represent the highest values ever reported for NFERAs-based OSCs. This study offers an effective strategy for the construction of high-performance NFERAs with side-chain-induced multi-dimensional charge transport properties via supramolecular interactions.

Methods

Device fabrication and testing

OSCs were fabricated with a general configuration of ITO/2PACz/active layer/PDINN/Ag. The ITO-coated glass substrates were thoroughly cleaned with deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol successively, followed by drying at 150 °C for 10 minutes. The ITO glasses were subjected to plasma treatment for 25 min. Then, an ethanol solution of 2PACz with a concentration of 0.3 mg mL-1 was spin-cast onto the ITO surface at 5000 rpm for 30 s, and baked at 70oC for 2 minutes in the air. A mixture of D18 (4.5 mg/mL) and acceptors in CF/ o-xylene solution (with 4.5 mg mL−1 solid additive of DIB) was stirred at 100 °C for 30 min to ensure complete dissolution of solids, and then cooled to 70 °C for spin coating onto 2PACz modified glass substrates in the N2-filled glove box. After the film was completely dried, it was thermally annealed at 100 °C for 1 min. Afterwards, a methanolic solution of PDINN (1.5 mg mL-1) was poured on top of the active layer by rotating it at 3000 rpm. Finally, a 100 nm Ag layer was thermally deposited under vacuum pressure below 10-7 Torr. The effective area of each cell is 0.04 cm2. The J-V characteristics were recorded using an Enli Technology Ltd, Taiwan (SS-F5 3 A) under an AM 1.5 G AAA class solar simulator with an intensity of 100 mW cm-2 as the white light source, and the intensity was calibrated with a standard single crystal Si photovoltaic cell. The EQE measurements of OSCs were performed by the solar cell spectral response measurement system QE-R3011 (Enli Technology Ltd, Taiwan), which was calibrated by a monocrystalline silicon solar cell in advance.

General measurements

The 1H and 13C NMR spectra of these compounds were recorded on a Bruker AVANCE NEO 400 M spectrometer. Matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization-time of flight mass spectrum (MALDITOF-MS) was measured on a Bruker Autoflex III instrument. UV-vis-NIR absorption spectra were recorded with a UV-1900 pc spectrophotometer. The energy levels were measured by utilizing CHI 600E Electrochemical Analyzer via cyclic voltammetry methods. A conventional three-electrode system with a glass carbon disk coated with a sample film as the working electrode, Pt wire as the counter electrode, and Ag/AgCl as the reference electrode was tested in acetonitrile solution of tetrabutylammonium hexafluorophosphate (n-Bu4NPF6, 0.1 M) at a scanning rate of 100 mV s-1. The CV curves were recorded versus the potential of the working electrode, which was calibrated by the ferrocene-ferrocenium (Fc/Fc+) redox couple. The electron transfer integral was calculated using the energy splitting method and performed at the pw91pw91/6-31 g* level to accurately estimate the exchange-correlation interactions.

Single-crystal growth

A single crystal was prepared by the solution diffusion method. Acceptors (0.5 mg) were dissolved in CHCl3 (0.5 mL) in clean NMR tubes, then 1 mL of methanol was slowly layered on top of the CHCl3 solution. The NMR tubes were then sealed and left undisturbed for 10 days until the solution’s color faded.

Atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurements

AFM topography images and corresponding chemical IR imaging were acquired using a NanoIR3 (Bruker). We employed contact-mode NanoIR3 probes (model: PREX-nIR3, Bruker) with a resonance frequency of 13 ± 4 kHz and a spring constant of 0.07–0.4 N/m. The AFM-nano-IR technique involved coupling a pulsed tunable IR source with an AFM, focusing the light on the tip-sample contact area. Absorption of the pulsed light by the sample caused rapid heating and expansion, generating an impulse on the AFM cantilever and inducing oscillation. The oscillation amplitude of the cantilever was directly proportional to the IR absorption coefficient of the sample.

Femtosecond transient absorption measurements

For femtosecond transient absorption measurements, a Yb:KGW laser (1030 nm, 54 kHz, Light Conversion) is split into two fundamental light beams. One of the light beams is transferred to the optical parametric amplifier (Orpheus, Light Conversion) to generate a high-intensity pulse of a specific wavelength as the pump beam. At the same time, the other is focused on a 5 mm sapphire to generate low-intensity continuum light, employed as the probe beam. The pump and probe beams were spatially set at the magic angle (54.7°) and overlapped at the sample. The time delays between pump and probe beams were achieved using a delay stage monitor, and the transmitted probe light was collected using a charge-coupled device. For the film samples, the transient absorption experiment was done six times on several spots of the film for each sample, of which the average was taken. Upon completion, no sample degradation was detected.
