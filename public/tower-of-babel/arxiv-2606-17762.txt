Horizon-Uniform Sensitivity Certificates for Finite-Horizon Pontryagin Systems footnoteinfo
Pyuyi Chufeng Huang, Zikang Song, Xingshu Chen

Abstract
Finite-horizon optimal-control computations repeatedly solve two-point Pontryagin boundary value problems whose conditioning can deteriorate as the horizon grows. We give a verifiable data-level certificate under which it does not. Hyperbolicity of the reduced state–costate transition matrix, together with scaled stable–unstable boundary transversality, yields an endpoint-corrected Green inverse with horizon-independent constants; weighted contractions transfer this inverse to the nonlinear problem, so the original Pontryagin endpoint rows x 0 = x in x_{0}=x_{\rm in} and p T = r x ​ ( x T , y ) p_{T}=r_{x}(x_{T},y) carry a unique local stationary branch whose first-order expansion and Lipschitz constants are uniform in the horizon. Consequently the finite-horizon feedback map is horizon-uniformly Lipschitz, first-order expandable, and satisfies an exact shrinking-horizon consistency identity. Symplectic and Riccati criteria certify the hypotheses from matrix data: every stabilizable definite linear-quadratic system with invertible dynamics and a locally concave terminal Hessian at the reference qualifies. Reproducible computations illustrate both certificates.

Introduction

Finite-horizon optimal-control computations repeatedly solve two-point Pontryagin systems: shooting methods, continuation in boundary data, and shrinking-horizon model predictive control all depend on how the state–costate branch changes when the endpoints change
(
4
;
19
;
10
)
. The central issue is not solvability at a fixed horizon. A fixed-horizon implicit function theorem gives a local branch, but its constants may deteriorate with the horizon. This paper gives a verifiable certificate under which the linearized two-point Pontryagin boundary value problem has an inverse with constants independent of the horizon, and then proves that the nonlinear stationary branch inherits horizon-independent weighted first-order sensitivity estimates.

Main result (informal).

Let
F,\ell
be
C^{3}
and let
r
be
C^{3}
in
x
near an interior stationary triple. If the reduced two-point Pontryagin transition matrix at the reference is hyperbolic and its stable and unstable subspaces are transversal to the endpoint rows, then for every sufficiently large horizon and every small endpoint datum
(x_{\rm in},y)
the discrete Pontryagin boundary value problem

x_{0}=x_{\rm in},\qquad p_{T}=r_{x}(x_{T},y)

has a unique local stationary branch
(x_{t},u_{t},p_{t})_{t=0}^{T}
whose weighted first-order expansion and Lipschitz constants are independent of the horizon.

Here “transversal” is made precise in Section
3
as uniform
invertibility of the scaled boundary matrices
\widehat{\Gamma}_{N}
for all

N\geq T_{0}
; invertibility of the limiting matrix alone gives the conclusion for
all sufficiently large horizons.

The abstract inverse is certified directly from matrix data: hyperbolicity plus a scaled stable–unstable boundary transversality matrix gives the Green inverse, and the symplectic/Riccati structure of LQ data turns that transversality condition into finite-dimensional tests.

The contributions are as follows.

1.

We prove a discrete endpoint-corrected Green estimate for first-order two-point systems. Compared with standard dichotomy estimates
(
6
;
15
;
16
)
, the kernel keeps the two finite-horizon endpoint-image terms explicit because those terms are consumed by the nonlinear estimates.

2.

We combine the Green estimate with weighted contractions to obtain existence, uniqueness, Lipschitz dependence, and first-order expansions for nonlinear implicit boundary value problems with constants independent of the horizon.

3.

We apply the reconstruction theorem to the original Pontryagin endpoint rows, not only to artificial affine boundary rows; for stabilizable definite LQ data, any locally concave terminal Hessian
r_{xx}(x_{\infty},y_{\infty})\preceq 0
satisfies the resulting shifted graph transversality.

4.

We provide data-level certificates: symplectic transversality and a Riccati/LQ verification covering stabilizable definite LQ systems with invertible dynamics, including coupled noncommuting matrices.

5.

As a control-theoretic consequence, the first-control feedback map

\mathsf{K}_{T}
, with value
u_{0}
at
(x_{\rm in},y)
, inherits
horizon-uniform Lipschitz dependence and a horizon-uniform first-order
expansion, together with an exact shrinking-horizon consistency identity; the
induced one-step rollout map is locally Lipschitz with a constant independent of
the horizon (Corollary
5.5
).

Scope.

Throughout, we certify stationary Pontryagin branches. Finite-horizon optimality, dissipativity, and closed-loop conclusions require additional second-order or dissipativity arguments; those questions are outside the present local sensitivity certificate. A saddle-type stationary branch is still covered whenever its reduced transition matrix is hyperbolic and the endpoint rows satisfy scaled transversality.

The cited horizon-uniform sensitivity literature proves exponential decay and perturbation bounds for optimal solutions or KKT systems, typically using second-order sufficient conditions, constraint qualifications, or related strong regularity assumptions
(
21
;
14
;
17
;
11
)
. The distinction we stress is structural: our hypotheses are hyperbolicity of the reduced two-point transition matrix and scaled endpoint transversality, both finite-dimensional matrix conditions certified below from
(A,B,Q,R)
and the terminal Hessian, without imposing a second-order sufficient condition, a constraint qualification, or a turnpike assumption a priori. Our result addresses a complementary local object: stationary Pontryagin branches near a steady triple. The explicit Green kernel keeps the finite-horizon endpoint-image terms
e^{-\gamma(t+T-s)}
and
e^{-\gamma(T-t+s)}
, which are then matched by the two-sided weight
w_{T}(t)=e^{-\alpha t}+e^{-\alpha(T-t)}
in the nonlinear reconstruction. Thus the output is a certificate for well-conditioned shooting and continuation around a selected branch. Turnpike and dissipativity results
(
20
;
7
;
18
;
9
;
8
)
provide global optimality-oriented mechanisms; here the mechanism is local endpoint invertibility plus a weighted nonlinear reconstruction.

2.1
Pontryagin equations and sign convention

This section fixes the sign convention used later and records precisely which part of the Pontryagin system is treated as a boundary value problem. We use the maximization convention for (
2.2
) and the forward Hamiltonian (
2.3
); the costate paired with the step from

. Consequently

\displaystyle H_{x}(x,u,p_{+})

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}F(x,u)^{\top}p_{+}-\lambda\partial_{x}\ell(x,u),

\displaystyle H_{u}(x,u,p_{+})

\displaystyle=\partial_{u}F(x,u)^{\top}p_{+}-\lambda\partial_{u}\ell(x,u).

If a trajectory is locally optimal for (
2.2
), then one-sided variations of a single control value inside the convex set
U
give the discrete-time first-order conditions

\begin{gathered}x_{t+1}=F(x_{t},u_{t}),\qquad p_{t}=H_{x}(x_{t},u_{t},p_{t+1}),\\
p_{T}=\partial_{x}r(x_{T},y),\\
\langle H_{u}(x_{t},u_{t},p_{t+1}),v-u_{t}\rangle\leq 0,\\
0\leq t<T,\qquad v\in U.\end{gathered}

(2.10)

These are the standard discrete-time Pontryagin conditions
(
4
;
19
)
; the signs reflect maximization of terminal reward minus accumulated cost, and all formulas below use (
2.3
) and (
2.10
).

Since
u_{\infty}\in\operatorname{int}U
, all sufficiently small local branches generated below remain in the interior of
U
; on those branches the variational inequality in (
2.10
) is equivalent to
H_{u}(x_{t},u_{t},p_{t+1})=0
. Lemma
2.1
then eliminates

as
u_{t}=\nu(x_{t},p_{t+1})
, and the first two equations in (
2.10
) become exactly the reduced implicit equation (
2.6
). The remaining endpoint conditions, such as
x_{0}=x_{\rm in}
and
p_{T}=r_{x}(x_{T},y)
, are handled later as affine or smooth endpoint rows. Thus a stationary Pontryagin branch in this paper means a local branch of (
2.10
) after interior stationarity reduction.

3
Linear endpoint estimates

Throughout this section, constants denoted by
C
may change from line to line and they may depend on fixed norms, local
C^{2}
bounds, boundary-row norms, and the Green constants in Definition
3.1
. In parameterized statements, uniformity is asserted only after the parameter set is shrunk to a fixed compact neighborhood on which the local tubes, smoothness bounds, spectral gaps, transversality margins, and row-perturbation bounds are chosen once and for all.

The main finite-horizon input is a two-sided inverse estimate for the linearized boundary value problem. We use a form with endpoint-correction terms, because this is what is produced by finite-horizon boundary conditions and it is exactly what the nonlinear proof needs.

Definition 3.1

(Endpoint-corrected inverse property)

Let

The linear problem

\displaystyle L[h_{t},h_{t+1}]=f_{t},\qquad\mathsf{B}_{0}h_{0}+\mathsf{B}_{T}h_{T}

\displaystyle=b,

(3.1)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq t<T,

V^{-1}=\binom{W_{s}}{W_{u}}
. Set
\Pi_{s}=V_{s}W_{s}
and
\Pi_{u}=V_{u}W_{u}
. After absorbing the fixed basis and projector norms,
\|M^{k}\Pi_{s}\|\leq Ce^{-\gamma k}
and
\|(M|_{E_{u}})^{-k}\Pi_{u}\|\leq Ce^{-\gamma k}
for
k\geq 0
, where the inverse powers are taken only on
E_{u}
.

Every homogeneous solution has a unique representation

q_{t}=V_{s}D_{s}^{t}a+V_{u}D_{u}^{t-N}\eta
: indeed, write

q_{0}=V_{s}a+V_{u}c
and put
\eta=D_{u}^{N}c
. For such a solution,

\mathsf{B}_{0}q_{0}+\mathsf{B}_{T}q_{N}=\widehat{\Gamma}_{N}\binom{a}{\eta}
. Hence, for
homogeneous boundary datum
b
, scaled transversality gives

\|(a,\eta)\|\leq C\|b\|
. The dichotomy estimates then yield

\|q_{t}\|\leq C(e^{-\gamma t}+e^{-\gamma(N-t)})\|b\|.

This bound is uniform in
N
because the bases and the inverse bound for

\widehat{\Gamma}_{N}
are uniform.

For forcing, use the empty-sum convention and define

p_{t}=\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}M^{t-1-j}\Pi_{s}g_{j}-\sum_{j=t}^{N-1}(M|_{E_{u}})^{t-1-j}\Pi_{u}g_{j}.

In the second sum the exponent is negative; it means the corresponding inverse power of the automorphism
M|_{E_{u}}
.
Set
S_{t}
equal to the first sum and
U_{t}
equal to the whole signed second term in this display. Then
S_{t+1}-MS_{t}=\Pi_{s}g_{t}
and
U_{t+1}-MU_{t}=\Pi_{u}g_{t}
, so
p_{t+1}-Mp_{t}=g_{t}
. The dichotomy bounds give

\displaystyle\|p_{t}\|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}e^{-\gamma|t-j|}\|g_{j}\|,

\displaystyle\|p_{0}\|+\|p_{N}\|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(e^{-\gamma j}+e^{-\gamma(N-j)})\|g_{j}\|.

The formulas also cover the endpoints:

\displaystyle p_{0}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(M|_{E_{u}})^{-1-j}\Pi_{u}g_{j},

\displaystyle p_{N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}M^{N-1-j}\Pi_{s}g_{j}.

The boundary residual for arbitrary
b
is

r=b-\mathsf{B}_{0}p_{0}-\mathsf{B}_{T}p_{N}
, so

\|r\|\leq C\|b\|+C\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(e^{-\gamma j}+e^{-\gamma(N-j)})\|g_{j}\|.

Adding the homogeneous solution
q
with boundary datum
r
yields
h=p+q
. Its homogeneous correction obeys

\|q_{t}\|\leq C(e^{-\gamma t}+e^{-\gamma(N-t)})\|b\|+C\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}K_{t,j}\|g_{j}\|,

where

\displaystyle K_{t,j}={}

\displaystyle e^{-\gamma(t+j)}+e^{-\gamma(t+N-j)}+e^{-\gamma(N-t+j)}

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+e^{-\gamma(2N-t-j)}.

The first and fourth terms are bounded by
e^{-\gamma|t-j|}
, since
t+j\geq|t-j|
and
2N-t-j\geq|t-j|
. The two middle terms are exactly the endpoint-image kernels
e^{-\gamma(t+N-j)}
and
e^{-\gamma(N-t+j)}
in (
3.2
). Thus
h=p+q
exists for arbitrary
(g,b)
and satisfies (
3.3
), after the harmless replacement of
|t-1-j|
by
|t-j|
in the particular-solution constants. If
g=b=0
, the homogeneous boundary equation gives
\widehat{\Gamma}_{N}\binom{a}{\eta}=0
, hence
a=\eta=0
and the solution is zero; uniqueness follows by applying this to the difference of two solutions.

Finally,
\|\widehat{\Gamma}_{N}-\widehat{\Gamma}_{\infty}\|\leq Ce^{-\gamma N}
. If (
3.7
) is invertible, Neumann’s lemma gives uniform inverses for all sufficiently large
N
, and the finitely many remaining invertible matrices have a positive minimum least singular value. This proves the last assertion.

Remark 3.5

(Rates below the spectral gap)
.

For a fixed hyperbolic matrix, the displayed dichotomy bounds hold, after increasing
K
, for every rate below the spectral gap. This includes the case of nontrivial Jordan blocks.

The scaled boundary test is useful only if it survives small modeling errors. The next proposition shows this finite-horizon robustness under continuous perturbations of the dynamics and boundary rows.

Proposition 3.6

(Robustness of scaled transversality)
.

Let
M(\theta)
,
\mathsf{B}_{0}(\theta)
, and
\mathsf{B}_{T}(\theta)
depend continuously on
\theta
near
0
. Suppose
M(0)
is hyperbolic and the scaled matrices
\widehat{\Gamma}_{N}(0)
in (
3.6
) are uniformly invertible for all
N\geq T_{0}
. Then, using any locally continuous choice of stable and unstable bases, the matrices
\widehat{\Gamma}_{N}(\theta)
are uniformly invertible for all
N\geq T_{0}
after shrinking
\theta
to a small neighborhood. Hence Definition
3.1
holds with constants uniform in small
\theta
.

Proof 3.7
.

Let
P_{s}(\theta)
and
P_{u}(\theta)
be the Riesz spectral projectors around the parts of the spectrum inside and outside the unit circle. They depend continuously on
\theta
. With fixed bases
V_{s}(0),V_{u}(0)
, the matrices
V_{s}(\theta):=P_{s}(\theta)V_{s}(0)
and
V_{u}(\theta):=P_{u}(\theta)V_{u}(0)
remain bases after shrinking. Choose continuous left inverses
L_{s}(\theta)
and
L_{u}(\theta)
for these bases and set

\displaystyle D_{s}(\theta)

\displaystyle=L_{s}(\theta)M(\theta)V_{s}(\theta),

\displaystyle D_{u}(\theta)

\displaystyle=L_{u}(\theta)M(\theta)V_{u}(\theta).

Then
M(\theta)V_{s}(\theta)=V_{s}(\theta)D_{s}(\theta)
and similarly on
E_{u}(\theta)
, and the blocks are continuous. On a compact smaller neighborhood the spectra stay a positive distance from the unit circle. Hence for each parameter there are
k_{0}
and
\beta_{0}>0
with
\|D_{s}(\theta_{0})^{k_{0}}\|<e^{-2\beta_{0}k_{0}}
and
\|D_{u}(\theta_{0})^{-k_{0}}\|<e^{-2\beta_{0}k_{0}}
; by continuity these bounds persist on a neighborhood of
\theta_{0}
, submultiplicativity extends them to all powers, and a finite subcover gives
\beta>0
and
C
with

\|V_{s}(\theta)D_{s}(\theta)^{k}\|+\|V_{u}(\theta)D_{u}(\theta)^{-k}\|\leq Ce^{-\beta k},\qquad k\geq 0.

Uniform invertibility of
\widehat{\Gamma}_{N}(0)
implies that

\widehat{\Gamma}_{\infty}(0)=[\mathsf{B}_{0}(0)V_{s}(0)\ \mathsf{B}_{T}(0)V_{u}(0)]
is invertible. Indeed, the uniform bound gives
s_{\min}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{N}(0))\geq c_{0}>0
, the dichotomy bounds give
\widehat{\Gamma}_{N}(0)\to\widehat{\Gamma}_{\infty}(0)
, and continuity of singular values then gives
s_{\min}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{\infty}(0))\geq c_{0}
. Therefore
\widehat{\Gamma}_{\infty}(\theta)
remains invertible on a neighborhood
\Theta_{1}
, with
\sup_{\Theta_{1}}\|\widehat{\Gamma}_{\infty}(\theta)^{-1}\|\leq C_{\infty}
. The uniform dichotomy bounds imply

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{1}}\|\widehat{\Gamma}_{N}(\theta)-\widehat{\Gamma}_{\infty}(\theta)\|\leq Ce^{-\beta N}.

Choose
N_{*}
so large that
C_{\infty}Ce^{-\beta N}\leq 1/2
for
N\geq N_{*}
. Neumann’s lemma gives uniform inverses for
N\geq N_{*}
. For the finite set
T_{0}\leq N<N_{*}
, continuity and invertibility at
\theta=0
give a smaller neighborhood
\Theta_{2}\subset\Theta_{1}
on which the least singular values remain bounded below. Thus
\widehat{\Gamma}_{N}(\theta)
is uniformly invertible for every
N\geq T_{0}
and
\theta\in\Theta_{2}
, and Proposition
3.3
gives Definition
3.1
with constants uniform in
\theta
.

4
Nonlinear reconstruction in weighted spaces

The nonlinear fixed-point argument uses endpoint weights matched to the Green kernel. For
0<\alpha<\gamma/2
, set

w_{T}(t)=e^{-\alpha t}+e^{-\alpha(T-t)}.

(4.1)

For each horizon define the weighted space

\displaystyle X_{T}

\displaystyle=\{z=(z_{0},\ldots,z_{T}):\|z\|_{w,T}<\infty\},

\displaystyle\|z\|_{w,T}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\frac{\|z_{t}\|}{w_{T}(t)}.

All differentiability statements below concern the family of maps into these horizon-dependent spaces uniformly in
T
.

The convolution estimate controls quadratic interior residuals.

Lemma 4.1

(Weighted endpoint convolution)
.

If
0<\alpha<\gamma/2
, then there is a constant
C_{\rm conv}
, independent of
T
and
t
, such that

\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\kappa_{T}(t,s)w_{T}(s)^{2}\leq C_{\rm conv}w_{T}(t)^{2}.

Moreover
e^{-\gamma t}+e^{-\gamma(T-t)}\leq C_{\rm conv}w_{T}(t)^{2}
, with
\kappa_{T}
from (
3.2
).

Proof 4.2
.

Use
w_{T}(s)^{2}\leq 2e^{-2\alpha s}+2e^{-2\alpha(T-s)}
, which includes the cross term by
2ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}
. For the interior kernel, splitting at
s=t
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{s}e^{-\gamma|t-s|}e^{-2\alpha s}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s\leq t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}e^{-2\alpha s}

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+\sum_{s>t}e^{-\gamma(s-t)}e^{-2\alpha s}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-2\alpha t},

because the two ratios are
e^{-(\gamma-2\alpha)}
and
e^{-(\gamma+2\alpha)}
. The change
s\mapsto T-s
gives

\sum_{s}e^{-\gamma|t-s|}e^{-2\alpha(T-s)}\leq Ce^{-2\alpha(T-t)}.

For the endpoint kernel
e^{-\gamma(t+T-s)}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{s}e^{-\gamma(t+T-s)}e^{-2\alpha(T-s)}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\gamma t},

\displaystyle\sum_{s}e^{-\gamma(t+T-s)}e^{-2\alpha s}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}e^{-2\alpha T}.

Since
\gamma>2\alpha
,
e^{-\gamma t}\leq e^{-2\alpha t}\leq w_{T}(t)^{2}
, and the second bound is even smaller. For the other endpoint kernel,

\displaystyle\sum_{s}e^{-\gamma(T-t+s)}e^{-2\alpha s}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\gamma(T-t)},

\displaystyle\sum_{s}e^{-\gamma(T-t+s)}e^{-2\alpha(T-s)}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\gamma(T-t)}e^{-2\alpha T},

which are bounded by
Cw_{T}(t)^{2}
by the same use of
\gamma>2\alpha
; the displayed endpoint decay follows from the two endpoint comparisons, and combining the three kernel parts proves the estimate, including
t=0,T
.

The convolution estimate is paired with a standard quadratic residual bound for the nonlinear equation.

Lemma 4.3

(Quadratic residual from
C^{2}
smoothness)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be
C^{2}
near
(0,0)
, assume
\mathcal{F}(0,0)=0
, and let
L=D\mathcal{F}(0,0)
. Define

N(a,b)=\mathcal{F}(a,b)-L[a,b].

After shrinking the neighborhood, there is a constant
C_{N}
such that

\lVert N(a,b)\rVert\leq C_{N}(\lVert a\rVert+\lVert b\rVert)^{2}

and

\displaystyle\lVert N(a,b)-N(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})\rVert

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}(\lVert a\rVert+\lVert b\rVert+\lVert\tilde{a}\rVert+\lVert\tilde{b}\rVert)

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle\times(\lVert a-\tilde{a}\rVert+\lVert b-\tilde{b}\rVert).

Proof 4.4
.

Use the product norm
|(a,b)|=\|a\|+\|b\|
and choose a convex ball
\mathcal{N}
on which
\|D^{2}\mathcal{F}\|\leq M
. Taylor’s formula with integral remainder gives, for
z=(a,b)\in\mathcal{N}
,

N(z)=\int_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)D^{2}\mathcal{F}(\theta z)[z,z]\,d\theta,

so
\|N(a,b)\|\leq C_{N}(\|a\|+\|b\|)^{2}
. Also
DN(0)=0
and

DN(y)=\int_{0}^{1}D^{2}\mathcal{F}(\theta y)[y,\cdot]\,d\theta,\qquad\|DN(y)\|\leq M|y|.

Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus to
N
along the segment joining
(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})
and
(a,b)
, which stays in the convex ball
\mathcal{N}
, yields

\displaystyle\|N(a,b)-N(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}(\|a\|+\|b\|+\|\tilde{a}\|+\|\tilde{b}\|)

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle\times(\|a-\tilde{a}\|+\|b-\tilde{b}\|).

We now combine the inverse estimate, the convolution bound, and the residual estimate.

Theorem 4.5

(Affine endpoint reconstruction and first-order stability)
.

Assume
\mathcal{F}(0,0)=0
, assume
\mathcal{F}
is
C^{2}
and satisfies the residual estimates of Lemma

, and assume that the linearization

3.1

by (
4.1
). There are

and
\|\Delta\|\leq\delta

\mathcal{F}(z_{t},z_{t+1})=0,\qquad\mathsf{B}_{0}z_{0}+\mathsf{B}_{T}z_{T}=\Delta

(4.2)

\|z\|_{w,T}\leq\rho\|\Delta\|

\|z\|_{w,T}\leq r_{*}

, then, uniformly in
T

\displaystyle\|z_{t}(\Delta)\|

\displaystyle\leq Cw_{T}(t)\|\Delta\|+Cw_{T}(t)^{2}\|\Delta\|^{2},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|z_{t}(\Delta)-z_{t}^{\mathrm{lin}}(\Delta)\|

\displaystyle\leq Cw_{T}(t)^{2}\|\Delta\|^{2}.

(4.4)

For two admissible data
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}
, write

E_{t}(\Delta):=z_{t}(\Delta)-z_{t}^{\mathrm{lin}}(\Delta)
. Then

\displaystyle\|z_{t}(\Delta_{1})-z_{t}(\Delta_{2})\|

\displaystyle\leq Cw_{T}(t)\|\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}\|,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|E_{t}(\Delta_{1})-E_{t}(\Delta_{2})\|

\displaystyle\leq Cw_{T}(t)^{2}(\|\Delta_{1}\|+\|\Delta_{2}\|)

\displaystyle\quad{}\times\|\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}\|.

(4.6)

Equivalently, if
S_{T}(\Delta)=z(\Delta)
, then

\sup_{T\geq T_{0}}\frac{\|S_{T}(\Delta)-\mathcal{L}_{T}^{\mathrm{bd}}\Delta\|_{w,T}}{\|\Delta\|}\to 0\qquad(\Delta\to 0),

so the family is Fréchet differentiable at
0
uniformly in
T
, with derivative
\mathcal{L}_{T}^{\mathrm{bd}}
.

Proof 4.6
.

Work in
X_{T}
and write
\|\cdot\|_{w}=\|\cdot\|_{w,T}
inside this proof. From Definition
3.1
and Lemma
4.1
, if
\|f_{s}\|\leq M_{f}w_{T}(s)^{2}
, then
\|({\mathcal{L}}_{T}^{\rm for}f)_{t}\|\leq C_{1}M_{f}w_{T}(t)^{2}
and
\|({\mathcal{L}}_{T}^{\mathrm{bd}}b)_{t}\|\leq C_{1}w_{T}(t)\|b\|
, and the sharper boundary estimate
\|({\mathcal{L}}_{T}^{\mathrm{bd}}b)_{t}\|\leq C_{1}w_{T}(t)^{2}\|b\|
holds for any boundary datum

, because
e^{-\gamma t}+e^{-\gamma(T-t)}\leq Cw_{T}(t)^{2}
. This will be applied to quadratic endpoint residuals. The estimate
w_{T}(t+1)\leq e^{\alpha}w_{T}(t)
and Lemma
4.3
give, on every ball
\|z\|_{w},\|\tilde{z}\|_{w}\leq R
inside the
C^{2}
tube,

\displaystyle\|N(z_{t},z_{t+1})\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}R^{2}w_{T}(t)^{2},

\displaystyle\|N(z_{t},z_{t+1})-N(\tilde{z}_{t},\tilde{z}_{t+1})\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}Rw_{T}(t)^{2}\|z-\tilde{z}\|_{w}.

Since
w_{T}(t)\leq 2
, choose
r_{*}>0
so that every sequence with
\|z\|_{w,T}\leq 2r_{*}
has each point
z_{t}
in the fixed
C^{2}
tube; then every pair
(z_{t},z_{t+1})
lies in the product neighborhood where the residual estimates apply. Also require
C_{1}C_{2}r_{*}<1/4
. Then choose
\delta
so that
2C_{1}\delta<r_{*}
and
4C_{1}^{2}C_{2}\delta<1/2
. With
R=2C_{1}\|\Delta\|
, define
\Psi(z)=y
by

\displaystyle L[y_{t},y_{t+1}]

\displaystyle=-N(z_{t},z_{t+1}),

\displaystyle\mathsf{B}_{0}y_{0}+\mathsf{B}_{T}y_{T}

\displaystyle=\Delta.

For
\|z\|_{w}\leq R
and
\|\Delta\|\leq\delta
,

\displaystyle\|\Psi(z)\|_{w}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|\Delta\|+C_{1}C_{2}R^{2}\leq R,

\displaystyle\|\Psi(z)-\Psi(\tilde{z})\|_{w}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C_{2}R\|z-\tilde{z}\|_{w}<\tfrac{1}{2}\|z-\tilde{z}\|_{w}.

Banach’s theorem gives a unique fixed point in
\|z\|_{w}\leq R
. If
z
and
\tilde{z}
are any two solutions in
\|\cdot\|_{w}\leq r_{*}
, then their difference solves the same linear problem with zero boundary datum and forcing
-[N(z)-N(\tilde{z})]
; hence
\|z-\tilde{z}\|_{w}\leq C_{1}C_{2}r_{*}\|z-\tilde{z}\|_{w}
, so

.

Subtracting
z^{\mathrm{lin}}=\mathcal{L}_{T}^{\mathrm{bd}}\Delta
from the fixed point leaves zero boundary datum and forcing

. The convolution estimate gives (
4.4
); (
4.3
) follows by adding the boundary solution. For two data,

solves

\displaystyle L[\delta z_{t},\delta z_{t+1}]

\displaystyle=-[N(z_{1})-N(z_{2})]_{t},

\displaystyle\mathsf{B}_{0}\delta z_{0}+\mathsf{B}_{T}\delta z_{T}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}.

The residual Lipschitz bound gives an interior forcing bounded by

. The Green estimate and
Lemma
4.1
therefore give

\|\delta z\|_{w}\leq C\|\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}\|+C(\|\Delta_{1}\|+\|\Delta_{2}\|)\|\delta z\|_{w},

and the last term is absorbed by the choice of
\delta
. This proves
(
4.5
). Subtracting

leaves zero boundary datum and the same

in the

4.6
). Taking one datum equal

,
(

The affine boundary argument also covers smooth endpoint maps after their quadratic boundary residual is included in the fixed-point map.

(Nonlinear endpoint boundary data)
.

\displaystyle\|z_{t}(\Delta)\|

\displaystyle\leq Cw_{T}(t)\|\Delta\|+Cw_{T}(t)^{2}\|\Delta\|^{2},

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|z_{t}(\Delta_{1})-z_{t}(\Delta_{2})\|

\displaystyle\leq Cw_{T}(t)\|\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}\|,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\|E_{t}(\Delta_{1})-E_{t}(\Delta_{2})\|

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle\times\|\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}\|.

Proof 4.8

\displaystyle\|R_{B}(a,b)\|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|a\|+\|b\|)^{2},

\displaystyle\leq C\Sigma\,(\|a-\tilde{a}\|+\|b-\tilde{b}\|),

where

. Define
\Psi(z)=y
by

\displaystyle L[y_{t},y_{t+1}]

\displaystyle=-N(z_{t},z_{t+1}),

\displaystyle\mathsf{B}_{0}y_{0}+\mathsf{B}_{T}y_{T}

Since

, on

Let

\displaystyle L[r_{t},r_{t+1}]

\displaystyle\mathsf{B}_{0}r_{0}+\mathsf{B}_{T}r_{T}

\displaystyle\Delta_{B}

\displaystyle:=R_{B}(z_{1,0},z_{1,T})-R_{B}(z_{2,0},z_{2,T}),

\displaystyle\Delta_{N}(t)

\displaystyle:=N(z_{1,t},z_{1,t+1})-N(z_{2,t},z_{2,t+1})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\Delta_{1}\|+\|\Delta_{2}\|)\|\delta z\|_{w},

6
Data-level symplectic transversality and LQ data

The endpoint inverse property in Definition
3.1
is useful for control computations only when it can be verified from the data. This section gives such a verification for stationary LQ references. The key algebraic point is that the reduced LQ transition matrix is symplectic, independently of any optimality interpretation. For a hyperbolic symplectic matrix, the stable and unstable subspaces are Lagrangian; endpoint transversality then becomes a finite-dimensional graph condition on the state and costate blocks of these subspaces. We first record the symplectic structure, then turn the abstract scaled transversality test into Riccati graph tests, and finally identify a stabilizable definite LQ class where those tests hold.

Before verifying the abstract inverse property, we fix the Riccati sign convention used by the maximization Hamiltonian and then use the standard symplectic and Lagrangian subspace viewpoint for Riccati equations and Hamiltonian and symplectic pencils

Proof 6.4

9
Concluding remarks

The chain developed here—endpoint-corrected Green estimates from scaled transversality, horizon-uniform weighted contractions, and symplectic data-level certificates—gives a verifiable route to local stationary branches of finite-horizon Pontryagin systems with constants independent of the horizon and, through Corollary
5.5
, to a horizon-uniformly conditioned finite-horizon feedback map along that branch. This supports shooting, continuation, shrinking-horizon rollout, and finite-horizon sensitivity routines. The certificate framework developed here is used as the formal basis of a companion empirical study of prompt optimization for frozen sequence models
(
2
)
, where the present hypotheses are fitted and tested as falsifiable diagnostics. Four natural extensions remain:

(O1)

references on a face of a polyhedral control set, via a fixed-active-face reduction under strict complementarity;

(O2)

nonstationary references, via nonautonomous dichotomies with projector-based limits in place of (
3.7
);

(O3)

singular
L_{1}
, for which Definition
3.1
needs a data-level verification route of its own;

(O4)

closed-loop or optimality strengthening of Corollary
5.5
, where the certified local feedback would need to be coupled with second-order sufficient conditions, dissipativity assumptions, or another optimality mechanism before stability or performance claims can be made.

All quantitative hypotheses used here are checkable at the level of the matrix data, as Section
8
illustrates.
