The Gomory-Hu inequality and trees
Oleksiy Dovgoshey, Olga Rovenska

Abstract
Let G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) be a finite connected graph with vertex set V V and edge set E E , and let U ⁡ ( G ) U(G) be the set of all ultrametric spaces ( V , d l ) (V,d_{l}) generated by vertex labelings l : V → ℝ + l\colon V\to\mathbb{R}^{+} . We prove that the inequality | D ⁡ ( V ) | ≤ | E | + 1 |D(V)|\leq|E|+1 holds for all ( V , d l ) ∈ U ⁡ ( G ) (V,d_{l})\in U(G) , where D ⁡ ( V ) D(V) is the distance set of ( V , d l ) (V,d_{l}) . The necessary and sufficient conditions under which the above inequality turns to an equality are found. Moreover, we prove that each connected graph with non-negative vertex labeling generates a pseudoultrametric space and find some sufficient conditions under which this space is ultrametric.

Introduction

The theory of ultrametric spaces is closely connected with problems of applied mathematics, physics, linguistics, psychology, and computer science, see, for example,
Rammal et al. 1986
;
Murtagh et al. 2008
;
Lemin 2003
;
Murtagh 2004
;
Krötzsch 2006
;
Seda and Hitzler 2008
;
Priess-Crampe and Ribenboim 2000
.

By studying the flows in networks, R. Gomory and T. Hu
Gomory and Hu 1961
deduced a statement that can be formulated as follows: If
(X,d)
is a finite ultrametric space with
distance set

D(X):=\{d(x,y):x,y\in X\},

then the inequality

|D(X)|\leq|X|

(1)

holds.

We will call inequality (
1
) the
Gomory–Hu inequality
.

Definition 1.1
.

A finite ultrametric space
(X,d)
is said to be a
{\bf GH}
-space if the equality

|D(X)|=|X|

holds. In what follows we denote by
{\bf GH}
the class of all
{\bf GH}
-spaces.

Two descriptions of the class
{\bf GH}
were obtained in terms of the representing trees of finite ultrametric spaces and, respectively, so-called diametrical graphs of such spaces (see
Petrov and Dovgoshey 2014
, Theorem 2.3 and Theorem 3.1).
A criterion of
(X,d)\in{\bf GH}
in terms of weighted Hamiltonian cycles and weighted Hamiltonian paths are found in Theorem 2.5 of paper
Dovgoshey et al. 2015
. The number of non-isometric spaces
(X,d)\in{\bf GH}
are also calculated in
Dovgoshey et al. 2015
.

The finite ultrametric spaces
(X,d)
with
|X|\geq 3
for which every self-isometry
X\to X
has at least
|X|-2
fixed points give us an interesting example of
{\bf GH}
-spaces. (See Theorem 2.3 of
Petrov and Dovgoshey 2014
and Theorem 3.4 of
Dovgoshey et al. 2017
.)

At the present paper we
investigate the ultrametric spaces generated by
connected graphs
G
with given vertex labelings
l:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

The ultrametric spaces generated by labeled trees were introduced in
Dovgoshey 2020
. Some results describing the metric and topological properties of such spaces can be found in
Dovgoshey et al. 2025
;
Dovgoshey and Kostikov 2023
;
Dovgoshey and Küçükaslan 2022
;
Dovgoshey and Rovenska 2026a
;
Dovgoshey and Rovenska 2026b
;
Dovgoshey and Rovenska 2026c
;
Dovgoshey and Rovenska 2025
;
Dovgoshey and Vito 2025
.

The present paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we collect together some definitions and facts related to ultrametric spaces and graphs.

The main results of the paper are presented in Section
3
.
Theorem
3.3
, the first theorem of Section
3
, shows that every connected labeled graph
G
generates a pseudoultrametric on
V(G)
. Moreover, we show that this pseudoultrametric is an ultrametric if
G
is locally finite. The necessary and sufficient conditions under which an ultrametric space generated by finite labeled tree belongs to the class
\bf GH
are given in Theorem
3.8
.
Theorem
3.10
contains a partial generalization of Theorem
3.8
to the case of ultrametric spaces generated by arbitrary finite connected labeled graphs. In Proposition
3.12
we prove that every finite connected labeled graph
G(l)

generates a
\bf GH
-space for
some suitable vertex labeling
l
.
Example
3.13
describes the vertex labelings on complete finite graphs which generate
\bf GH
-spaces.

The final Section
4
contains new conjectures and some open problems.

2
Preliminaries

Recall some necessary definitions from the theory of metric spaces and the graph theory.

Let
X
be a non-empty set. An
ultrametric
on
X
is a function
d:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
\mathbb{R}^{+}:=[0,\infty)
, such that for all
x,y,z\in X
:

(i)

d(x,y)=d(y,x)
,

(ii)

d(x,y)=0\iff x=y
,

(iii)

d(x,y)\leq\max\{d(x,z),d(z,y)\}
.

Inequality
(iii)
is often called the
strong triangle inequality
.

If we replace condition
(ii)
by the weaker condition

d(x,x)=0,

then
d
is called a
pseudoultrametric
on
X
.

Just as in the case of an ultrametric space, we introduce the distance set
D(X)
of a pseudoultrametric space
(X,d)
as

D(X):=\{d(x,y):x,y\in X\}.

The following proposition directly follows from Theorem 14 in Chapter 4 of book
Kelley 1975
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a pseudoultrametric space. Then there exists
Y\subseteq X
such that the function

\rho:=d|_{Y\times Y}

is an ultrametric on
Y
and the equality

D(X)=D(Y)

holds, where
D(X)
and, respectively,
D(Y)
are the distance sets of the pseudoultrametric space
(X,d)
and the ultrametric space
(Y,\rho)
.

Let us turn now to the graph theory.
Recall that a
graph
is a pair
(V,E)
consisting of a non-empty set
V
and a (possibly empty) set
E
whose elements are unordered pairs of distinct points from
V
.
For a graph
G=(V,E)
, the sets
V=V(G)
and
E=E(G)
are called the
set of vertices
and the
set of edges
, respectively. If
x
and
y
are vertices of
G
and
\{x,y\}\in E(G)
, then we say that
x
and
y
are
adjacent
in
G
. A graph
G
is said to be
empty
if the equality
E(G)=\emptyset
holds. A graph
H
is called a
subgraph
of a graph
G
,
H\subseteq G
, if the inclusions

V(H)\subseteq V(G)\quad\text{and}\quad E(H)\subseteq E(G)

hold. A graph
G
is said to be
finite
if this graph contains a finite number of vertices,
|V(G)|<\infty
.

Let
v
be a vertex of a graph
G
. The cardinal number of the set

\{u\in V(G):\{u,v\}\in E(G)\}

is the
degree
of
v
. The degree of
v\in V(G)
will be denoted by
\delta_{G}(v)
,

\delta_{G}(v):=\left|\{u\in V(G):\{u,v\}\in E(G)\}\right|.

(2)

A graph
G
is called
locally finite
if the degree
\delta_{G}(v)
is finite,

\delta_{G}(v)<\infty,

for every
v\in V(G)
.

The next theorem is a special case of Theorem 1.2 of book
Bondy and Murty 2008
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
G
be a finite graph. Then the equality

2|E(G)|=\sum_{v\in V(G)}\delta_{G}(v)

holds.

A
path
is a graph
P=(V,E)
of the form

V=\{x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\},\quad E=\{\{x_{0},x_{1}\},\dots,\{x_{k-1},x_{k}\}\},

(3)

where all
x_{i}
are distinct and
k\geq 1
. If (
3
) holds, then we write
P=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{k})
and say that
P
is a path joining the vertices
x_{0}
and
x_{k}
.
A graph
G
is called
connected
if for any two distinct vertices
u,v\in V(G)
there exists a path
P\subseteq G
joining these vertices. In particular, any graph
G
with
|V(G)|=1
is connected.
A finite graph
C
is a
cycle
if
|V(C)|\geq 3
and there exists an enumeration
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}
of its vertices such that

\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in E(C)\iff(|i-j|=1\text{ or }|i-j|=n-1).

A connected graph without cycles is called a
tree
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
G
be a finite connected graph. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

G
is a tree.

(ii)

Any two distinct vertices of
G
are joined by unique path in
G
.

(iii)

The equality

|V(G)|=1+|E(G)|

holds.

See, for example, Theorem 1.5.2 and Corollary 1.5.3 in
Diestel 2017
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. Then there exists a tree
T\subseteq G
such that

V(T)=V(G).

For the proof see, for example, Proposition 14 in Chapter 1 of book
Serre 1980
.

Proposition
2.3
and Theorem
2.4
imply the following corollary.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
G
be a finite connected graph. If the inequality

1+|E(G)|\leq|V(G)|

(4)

holds, then
G
is a tree.

Proof.

Let inequality (
4
) hold. By Theorem
2.4
there exists a tree
T
such that

E(T)\subseteq E(G)\quad\text{and}\quad V(T)=V(G).

Consequently, we have the inequality

|E(T)|\leq|E(G)|

(5)

and the equality

|V(T)|=|V(G)|.

(6)

Inequality (
5
), Proposition
2.3
, and equality (
6
) imply

|V(G)|=|V(T)|=1+|E(T)|\leq 1+|E(G)|.

Thus the inequality

|V(G)|\leq 1+|E(G)|

holds. The last inequality and (
4
) imply the equality

|V(G)|=1+|E(G)|.

Consequently,
G
is a tree by Proposition
2.3
.
∎

A tree
T
may have a distinguished vertex
r
called the
root
; in this case
T
is called a
rooted tree
and we write
T=T(r)
.

If
T=T(r)
is a rooted tree,
v
is a vertex of
T
, such that
v\neq r
,
then the
level
of
v
is the number
\operatorname{lev}(v):=|E(P)|
where
P
is a path joining
v
with
r
.

Moreover, the root
r
of
T
has, by definition, the zero level
\operatorname{lev}(r):=0
.

A non-empty graph
G=(V,E)
together with a function
w:E\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is called a
weighted graph
, and
w
is called a
weight
. The weighted graphs will be denoted by
G(w)
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
G(w)
be a weighted graph. A weight
w
is said to be ultrametrizable (pseudoultrametrizable) if there exists an ultrametric (pseudoultrametric)
\rho_{w}:V(G)\times V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

w(\{x,y\})=\rho_{w}(x,y)

(7)

for each
\{x,y\}\in E(G)
.

The next theorem directly follows from Theorems 1–3 of paper
Dovgoshey and Petrov 2013
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
G(w)
be a weighted graph. The weight
w
is pseudoultrametrizable if and only if for any cycle
C\subseteq G
there exist at least two different edges
e_{1},e_{2}\in E(C)
such that

w(e_{1})=w(e_{2})=\max_{e\in E(C)}w(e).

(8)

If
G
is connected,
w
is pseudoultrametrizable, then the function
\rho_{w}:V(G)\times V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
defined for all
x,y\in V(G)
as

\rho_{w}(x,y):=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }x=y,\\[6.0pt]
\inf\limits_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{x,y}}\left(\max\limits_{e\in E(P)}w(e)\right),&\text{if }x\neq y,\end{cases}

(9)

where
\mathcal{P}_{x,y}
is the set of all paths joining
x
and
y
in
G
, is a pseudoultrametric on
V(G)
and equality (
7
) holds.

Remark 2.8
.

The pseudoultrametric defined by formula (
9
) can be treated as an ultrametric analogue of the shortest path pseudometric. In particular, Theorem
2.7
is an ultrametric modification of Proposition 2.1 from paper
Dovgoshey et al. 2013
.

Let us recall now the concept of a
labeled graph.

Definition 2.9
.

A labeled graph
G(l)
is a pair
(G,l)
, where
G
is a graph and
l
is a mapping defined on the vertex set
V(G)
.

We will consider
the labeled graphs

G(l)
with non-negative labelings
l\colon V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
only.

Let
G=G(l)
be a connected labeled graph. Similarly to
Dovgoshey 2020
, we define a mapping
d_{l}:V(G)\times V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
as

d_{l}(x,y):=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }x=y,\\
\inf\limits_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{x,y}}\left(\max\limits_{v\in V(P)}l(v)\right),&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(10)

where
\mathcal{P}_{x,y}
is the set of all paths joining
x
and
y
in
G
.

In what follows, we will say that a space
(X,d_{l})
is
generated by
labeled graph

G(l)
.

If a graph
G
is a tree, then, by Proposition
2.3
, for each two distinct
x,y\in V(G)
there is a unique path
P\in\mathcal{P}_{x,y}
.
So we can rewrite formula (
10
) in the form

d_{l}(x,y):=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }x=y,\\
\max\limits_{v\in V(P)}l(v),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(11)

The following result is a direct corollary of Proposition 3.2 from
Dovgoshey 2020
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
T=T(l)
be a labeled tree and let
d_{l}\colon V(T)\times V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be defined by (
11
). Then the function
d_{l}
is an ultrametric on
V(T)
if and only if the inequality

\max\{l(u),l(v)\}>0

holds for every
\{x,y\}\in V(T)
.

3
HG-spaces generated by labeled graphs

The following lemma allows us to move from the vertex labelings
l\colon V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
to the weights
w\colon E(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G=G(l)
be a connected labeled graph,
let
d_{l}:V(G)\times V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be generated by
G(l)
, and
let a weight
w:E(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be defined as

w(\{u,v\}):=\max\{l(u),l(v)\}

(12)

for each
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
. Then the equality

d_{l}(x,y)=\inf_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{x,y}}\left(\max_{e\in E(P)}w(e)\right)

(13)

holds for all different
x,y\in V(G)
, where
\mathcal{P}_{x,y}
is the set of all paths joining
x
and
y
in
G
.

Proof.

Let
x
and
y
be distinct vertices of
G
and let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{x,y}
. Then the vertices of
P
can be numbered such that

P=(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})

(14)

with
v_{0}=x
and
v_{k}=y
.
Using (
12
) and (
14
) we obtain the equalities

\max_{v\in V(P)}l(v)=\max_{0\leq i\leq k}l(v_{i})=\max_{0\leq i\leq k-1}\max\{l(v_{i}),l(v_{i+1})\}

=\max_{0\leq i\leq k-1}w(\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\})=\max_{e\in E(P)}w(e).

Thus the equality

\max_{v\in V(P)}l(v)=\max_{e\in E(P)}w(e)

(15)

holds for every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{x,y}
. Now equality (
13
) follows from (
15
).
∎

Equality (
13
) can be rewritten in a much more simple form if
\{x,y\}\in E(G)
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
G=G(l)
be a connected labeled graph with vertex labeling
l:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+},
and let
d_{l}:V(G)\times V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be generated by
G(l)
. Then the equality

d_{l}(x,y)=\max\{l(x),l(y)\}

(16)

holds for each edge
\{x,y\}\in E(G)
.

Proof.

Let
x,y
be distinct vertices of
G
.
It is clear that
x
and
y
are vertices of every path
P\in\mathcal{P}_{x,y}
.
Hence the inequality

\inf_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{x,y}}\left(\max_{v\in V(P)}l(v)\right)\geq\max\{l(x),l(y)\}

(17)

holds.
Suppose that
x
and
y
are adjacent in
G
. Then the two-point path
(x,y)
belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{x,y}
and, consequently, we have the inequality

\inf_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{x,y}}\left(\max_{v\in V(P)}l(v)\right)\leq\max\{l(x),l(y)\}.

(18)

Inequalities (
17
) and (
18
) imply the equality

\inf_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{x,y}}\left(\max_{v\in V(P)}l(v)\right)=\max\{l(x),l(y)\}.

(19)

Equality (
16
) follows from (
10
) and (
19
).
∎

The following theorem is a partial generalization of Theorem
2.10
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G=G(l)
be a connected labeled graph and let
d_{l}:V(G)\times V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

be generated by
G(l)
.
Then
d_{l}
is a pseudoultrametric on
V(G)
. Moreover, if
G
is locally finite, then
d_{l}
is an ultrametric if and only if the inequality

\max\{l(u),l(v)\}>0

(20)

holds for every edge
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
.

Proof.

Let us define a weight

w:E(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

as

w(\{u,v\}):=\max\{l(u),l(v)\}

(21)

for each
\{u,v\}\in E(G).

First we claim that the weight
w
is pseudoultrametrizable.

To prove this claim we will use Theorem
2.7
.

Let us consider an arbitrary cycle
C\subseteq G.

By Theorem
2.7
,
w
is pseudoultrametrizable if and only if we can find
different
e_{1},

e_{2}\in E(C)
such that

w(e_{1})=w(e_{2})=\max_{e\in E(C)}w(e).

(22)

Since
C
is a finite graph, there exists
v_{0}\in V(C)
such that

l(v_{0})=\max_{u\in V(C)}l(u).

(23)

It follows from the definition of the cycles that there are two distinct vertices
u_{1},u_{2}\in V(C)
such that
\{u_{1},v_{0}\}
and
\{u_{2},v_{0}\}
belong to
E(C)
. Equalities (
21
) and (
23
) imply that

w(\{u_{1},v_{0}\})=l(v_{0})=w(\{u_{2},v_{0}\}).

Consequently (
22
) holds with
e_{1}=\{u_{1},v_{0}\}
,
e_{2}=\{u_{2},v_{0}\}
.
Thus
w:E(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is pseudoultrametrizable by Theorem
2.7
.
Theorem
2.7
implies also that the mapping
\rho_{w}:V(G)\times V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

defined by formula (
9
) is a pseudoultrametric on
V(G)
.
Equality (
13
) implies the equality
d_{l}=\rho_{w}
. Thus
d_{l}
also is a pseudoultrametric on
V(G)
.

Let
G
be locally finite.
Suppose that
d_{l}
is an ultrametric on
V(G)
.
Let
\{u,v\}
be an edge of
G
,

\{u,v\}\in E(G).

Then inequality (
20
) follows from Corollary
3.2
and the definition of ultrametrics.

Suppose now that inequality (
20
) holds for all
\{u,v\}\in E(G).

We must show that
d_{l}
is an ultrametric on
V(G).

Suppose contrary that
d_{l}
is not an ultrametric.
Then, using the definitions of the ultrametric spaces and pseudoultrametric ones, we can find different
u^{*},v^{*}\in V(G)
such that

d_{l}(u^{*},v^{*})=0.

(24)

If the inequality

\max\{l(u^{*}),l(v^{*})\}>0

holds, then using (
10
) with
x=u^{*}
and
y=v^{*}
, we obtain

d_{l}(u^{*},v^{*})>0,

contrary to (
24
). Thus the equalities

l(u^{*})=0=l(v^{*})

(25)

hold.

Let
N(u^{*})
be the set of all vertices of
G
which are adjacent with
u^{*}
,

N(u^{*})=\{u\in V(G):\{u,u^{*}\}\in E(G)\}.

Recall that the degree
\delta_{G}(u^{*})
is the cardinal number of the set
N(u^{*})
,

\delta_{G}(u^{*})=|N(u^{*})|.

The graph
G
is locally finite. Consequently, the degree
\delta_{G}(u^{*})
is finite. Hence the set
N(u^{*})
is finite,

|N(u^{*})|<\infty.

(26)

Moreover, since (
20
) holds for all
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
, double equality (
25
) implies the inequality

l(u)>0

for each
u\in N(u^{*})
.
The last inequality and (
26
) give us the inequality

\inf_{u\in N(u^{*})}l(u)>0.

(27)

Now using (
27
) and formula (
10
) we can prove that

d_{l}(u^{*},v^{*})\geq\inf_{u\in N(u^{*})}l(u)>0,

contrary to (
24
).

Thus, if
G
is locally finite and (
20
) holds for each
\{u,v\}\in E(G),
then pseudoultrametric
d_{l}:V(G)\times V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is an ultrametric on
V(G)
as required.

The proof is completed.
∎

V(G):=\{x,y,z_{1},z_{2},\dots\,z_{n},z_{n+1},\ldots\},\quad E(G):=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\{x,z_{n}\}\cup\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\{y,z_{n}\}.

Let us define a labeling
l:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
as

l(x):=l(y):=0\quad\text{and}\quad l(z_{n}):=\frac{1}{n}

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
l
is a non-degenerate labeling, but
we have

d_{l}(x,y)=\inf_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{x,y}}\left(\max_{v\in V(P)}l(v)\right)=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\max\{l(x),l(z_{n}),l(y)\}

=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\max\left\{0,\frac{1}{n}\right\}=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n}=0.

Thus
d_{l}
is not an ultrametric.

Let us consider an example of a connected graph
H
that is not locally finite but generates an ultrametric
d_{l}
for each non-degenerate
l:V(H)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Example 3.7
.

Let
H
be the graph depicted in Figure
1
,

V(H):=\{y\}\cup\left(\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\{x_{n}\}\right),\quad E(H):=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\{x_{n},x_{n+1}\}\cup\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\{y,z_{n}\}.

Then
\delta_{H}(y)=|\mathbb{Z}|
holds but a simple modification of the proof of Theorem
3.3
shows that
d_{l}
is an ultrametric for each non-degenerate labeling
l:V(H)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

The following theorem gives some necessary and sufficient conditions under which an ultrametric space generated by finite labeled tree belongs to the class
{\bf GH}
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
T=T(l)
be a finite labeled tree with
|V(T)|\geq 2
, let the labeling
l:V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be non-degenerate, let

w(\{u,v\}):=\max\{l(u),l(v)\}

for each
\{u,v\}\in E(T)
, and let
d_{l}
be generated by
T(l)
.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

(V(T),d_{l})\in{\bf GH}
.

(ii)

The mapping
w:E(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is injective.

(iii)

The distance set
D(V(T))
of
(V(T),d_{l})
satisfies the equality

|D(V(T)|=|E(T)|+1.

(28)

(iv)

The distance set
D(V(T))
of
(V(T),d_{l})
satisfies the equality

2|D(V(T)|=2+\sum\limits_{v\in V(T)}\delta_{T}(v).

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Let
(V(T),d_{l})\in{\bf GH}
hold.

Let us consider the distance set
D(V(T))
of the ultrametric space
(V(T),d_{l})
,

D(V(T))=\{d_{l}(x,y):x,y\in V(T)\}.

Then the equality

|V(T)|=|D(V(T))|

holds by Definition
1.1
.

Since
l:\in V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is non-degenerate, Corollary
3.2
implies the inclusion

w(E(T))\subseteq D(V(T))\setminus\{0\}.

(29)

Moreover, using (
10
), we obtain
the converse inclusion

w(E(T))\supseteq D(V(T))\setminus\{0\}.

Thus the equality

w(E(T))=D(V(T))\setminus\{0\}

(30)

holds.

Since the tree
T
is finite, the mapping

w:E(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

is injective if and only if the equality

|w(E(T))|=|E(T)|

(31)

holds.

Equality (
30
) and the membership relation

(V(T),d_{l})\in{\bf GH}
imply the equality

|w(E(T))|=|V(T)|-1

(32)

by Definition
1.1
.

Since
T
is a tree we also have

|E(T)|=|V(T)|-1

(33)

by Proposition
2.3
. Equality (
31
) follows from (
32
) and (
33
). Statement
(ii)
is proved.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
.
Let
w:E(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be injective. Corollary
3.2
implies inclusion (
29
).
Consequently the inequality

|D(V(T))|\geq|E(T)|+1

holds.
Since
T
is a tree, the last inequality and Proposition
2.3
give us the inequality

|D(V(T))|\geq|V(T)|.

(34)

The Gomory–Hu inequality (
1
) with
(X,d)=(V(T),d_{l})
implies the inequality

|D(V(T))|\leq|V(T)|.

(35)

Hence the equality

|D(V(T))|=|V(T)|

(36)

holds. The last equality implies statement
(i)
by Definition
1.1
.

(i)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
. By Definition
1.1
, statement
(i)
holds if and only if we have equality (
36
). Proposition
2.3
implies that equalities (
28
) and (
36
) are equivalent. Thus the logical equivalence
(i)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
is valid.

(iii)\Leftrightarrow(iv)
. The validity of the equivalence

(iii)\Leftrightarrow(iv)
follows from Theorem
2.2
.

The proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
T
be a finite tree. Then there exists a non-degenerate labeling

l:V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

such that

(V(T),d_{l})\in{\bf GH},

(37)

and the equality

d_{l}(x,y)=\max\{l(x),l(y)\}

(38)

holds for any two distinct
x
,
y\in V(T)
.

Proof.

Let
r
be a fixed vertex of the tree
T
. Then there are a non-negative integer number
n
and disjoint non-empty subsets
V_{0},\ldots,V_{n}
of the vertex set
V(T)
such that

V(T)=\bigcup_{i=0}^{n}V_{i}

(39)

and the equality

\operatorname{lev}(v)=i

(40)

holds for every
v\in V_{i}
and each
i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
, where
\operatorname{lev}(v)
is the level of the vertex
v
in the rooted tree
T(r)
.

Let

l:V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

be an injective labeling and let the inequality

l(v)<l(u)

hold whenever
\operatorname{lev}(v)<\operatorname{lev}(u)
.
Then it is easy to see that the mapping
w:E(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,

w(\{u,v\}):=\max\{l(u),l(v)\}

for each
\{v,u\}\in E(T)
, also is injective.

Consequently (
37
) holds by Theorem
3.8
.

Let us prove equality (
38
).
Let
x
and
y
be two distinct vertices of
T
and let

P_{x}=(x,\dots,r),
and
P_{y}=(y,\dots,r)

be the paths joining
x
with
r
and, respectively,
y
with
r
. Let us consider the graph
P_{x}\cup P_{y}
,

V(P_{x}\cup P_{y}):=V(P_{x})\cup V(P_{y}),

(41)

E(P_{x}\cup P_{y}):=E(P_{x})\cup E(P_{y}).

(42)

Then the inequality

\operatorname{lev}(v)<\max\{\operatorname{lev}(x),\operatorname{lev}(y)\}

(43)

holds for each
v\in V(P_{x}\cup P_{y})\setminus\{x,y\}
.

Let
P_{T}
be the path joining
x
and
y
in
T
. Since the graph
P_{x}\cup P_{y}
is connected, there is a path
P^{*}
joining
x
and
y
in
P_{x}\cup P_{y}
,

P^{*}\subseteq P_{x}\cup P_{y}.

(44)

It follows from (
41
) and (
42
) that
P_{x}\cup P_{y}
is a subgraph of
T
,

P_{x}\cup P_{y}\subseteq T.

(45)

Now (
44
) and (
45
) imply the inclusion

P^{*}\subseteq T.

(46)

Since
T
is a tree, statement
(ii)
of Proposition
2.3
and inclusion (
46
) give us the equality

P^{*}=P_{T}.

The last equality and inequality (
43
) imply the inequality

\max_{v\in V(P_{T})}l(v)\leq\max\{l(x),l(y)\}.

(47)

Inequality (
47
) and equality (
8
) give us the inequality

d_{l}(x,y)\leq\max\{l(x),l(y)\}.

The converse inequality

d_{l}(x,y)\geq\max\{l(x),l(y)\}

follows directly from (
8
).
Equality (
38
) is proven.

The proof is completed.

∎

The following theorem gives us a partial generalization of Theorem
3.8
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
G
be a finite connected graph. Then, for each labeling

l:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we have the inequality

|D(V(G))|\leq|E(G)|+1,

(48)

where
D(V(G))
is the distance set of the pseudoultrametric space
(V(G),d_{l})
with
d_{l}:V(G)\times V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
generated by
G(l)
. Moreover,
the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

There is a labeling
l:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

|D(V(G))|=|E(G)|+1.

(49)

(ii)

The graph
G
is a tree.

Proof.

Let us prove inequality (
48
).

By Proposition
2.1
we can find
Y\subseteq V(G)
such that

\rho=d_{l}|_{Y\times Y}
is an ultrametric on
Y
and the equality

D(V(G))=D(Y)

(50)

holds,
where
D(Y)
is the distance set of
(Y,\rho)
. The set
Y
is a subset of
V(G)
, that implies the inequality

|Y|\leq|V(G)|.

The last inequality and condition
(ii)
of
Proposition
2.3
give us the inequality

|Y|\leq 1+|E(G)|.

(51)

Since the space
(Y,\rho)
is ultrametric, we have

|D(Y)|\leq|Y|

(52)

by the Gomory–Hu inequality. Now using (
50
)–(
52
), we obtain

|D(V(G))|=|D(Y)|\leq|Y|\leq 1+|E(G)|.

Inequality (
48
) follows.

Let us prove that statements
(i)
and
(ii)
are equivalent.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Suppose that there is a labeling

l:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

such that equality (
49
) holds for the distance set
D(V(G))
of the space
(V(G),d_{l})
.

We must show that
G
is a tree.
By Corollary
2.5
the graph
G
is a tree if the inequality

|V(G)|\geq 1+|E(G)|

(53)

holds.
By Theorem
2.4
we can find a tree
T
such that

V(T)=V(G)\quad\text{and}\quad E(T)\subseteq E(G).

(54)

Equalities (
49
) and (
50
) imply the equality

|D(Y)|=|E(G)|+1.

(55)

The space
(Y,\rho)
is an ultrametric space. Consequently, we have (
52
) by Gomory–Hu inequality. Inequality (
52
), equality (
55
) and the inclusion
Y\subseteq V(G)
give us

|E(G)|+1=|D(Y)|\leq|Y|\leq|V(G)|.

Consequently inequality (
53
) holds. Thus
G
is a tree as required.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
. Let
G
be a tree. Then, by Lemma
3.9
, there is a labeling

l:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

such that

(V(G),d_{l})\in{\bf GH},

(56)

where
d_{l}
is an ultrametric on
V(G)
generated by
G(l)
.

Formula (
56
) and statements
(i)
,
(iii)
of Theorem
3.8
with
T(l)=G(l)
imply equality (
49
) as required.

The proof is completed.
∎

and

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
G
be a finite connected graph.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

There is a labeling
l:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

2|D(V(G)|=2+\sum\limits_{v\in V(G)}\delta_{G}(v),

where
D(V(G))
is the distance set of the pseudoultrametric space
(V(G),d_{l})
with
d_{l}:V(G)\times V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
generated by

.

(ii)

The graph
G
is a tree.

The next proposition follows from Theorem
2.4
and Lemma
3.9
.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
G
be a finite connected graph. Then there exists a non-degenerate labeling

l:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

such that

(V(G),d_{l})\in{\bf GH},

(57)

where
d_{l}
is an ultrametric on
V(G)
generated by
G(l)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.4
there is a tree
T
such that

E(T)\subseteq E(G)
and
V(T)=V(G).

Using Lemma
3.9
we can find

l:V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

such that

(V(T),d_{l})\in{\bf GH},

(58)

where

d_{l}:V(T)\times V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

is an ultrametric on
V(T)
generated by labeled tree
T(l)
.

Let us define a labeling
l:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
as

l^{*}(v):=l(v)

for every
v\in V(G)
. We claim that the ultrametric
d_{l^{*}}:V(G)\times V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
generated by
G(l^{*})
coincides with the ultrametric
d_{l}:V(T)\times V(T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
generated by
T(l)
.

Let us consider an arbitrary pair of distinct
x,y\in V(G)
.
We must show that

d_{l}(x,y)=d_{l^{*}}(x,y).

(59)

Since
l^{*}(v)=l(v)
holds for each
v\in V(G)
, the inclusion
E(T)\subseteq E(G)
and formula (
10
) imply the inequality

d_{l}(x,y)\geq d_{l^{*}}(x,y).

Thus (
59
) holds if we have the converse inequality

d_{l}(x,y)\leq d_{l^{*}}(x,y).

(60)

Using equalities (
10
) and (
38
) we can rewrite (
60
) in the form

\max\{l(x),l(y)\}\leq\inf_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{x,y}}\left(\max_{v\in V(P)}l(v)\right),

(61)

where
\mathcal{P}_{x,y}
is the set of all paths joining
x
and
y
in
G
. Since
x
and
y
are vertices of each
P\in\mathcal{P}_{x,y}
, we obtain

\max_{v\in V(P)}l^{*}(v)\geq\max\{l^{*}(x),l^{*}(y)\}=\max\{l(x),l(y)\}

(62)

for every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{x,y}
.

Now (
61
) follows from (
62
). Thus (
60
) holds, that implies (
59
).
Formulas (
58
) and (
59
) imply (
57
).

The proof is completed.
∎

G
is called
complete

\{u,v\}\in E(G)

is valid for every two distinct

Example 3.13
.

Let
G=G(l)

(i)

(V(G),d_{l})\in{\bf GH}
holds, where
d_{l}
is an ultrametric on
V(G)
generated by
G(l)
.

(ii)

The set

l(V):=\{l(v):v\in V(G)\}

contains at least
|V(G)|-1
points,

|l(V)|\geq|V(G)|-1.

Conclusion — Expected Results

We believe that the following hypothesis is correct.

Conjecture 4.1
.

Let
i=1,2
and
T_{i}=T_{i}(l_{i})
be finite labeled trees with non-degenerate labelings
l_{i}:V(T_{i})\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
If
(V(T_{1}),d_{l_{1}})
and
(V(T_{2}),d_{l_{2}})
are
\bf GH
-spaces, then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The equality

D(V(T_{1}))=D(V(T_{2}))

holds, where
D(V(T_{1}))
and
D(V(T_{2}))
are the distance set of the space
(V(T_{1}),d_{l_{1}})
and
(V(T_{2}),d_{l_{2}})
respectively.

(ii)

The spaces
(V(T_{1}),d_{l_{1}})
and
(V(T_{2}),d_{l_{2}})
are isometric.

We note that the implication
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
is evidently true and the validity of
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
follows from the strong triangle inequality if
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
contain at most three vertices.

The following problem naturally arises.

Problem 4.2
.

Describe, up to isometry, all finite
\bf GH
-spaces generated by labeled trees.

It was shown in Theorem
3.3
that every locally finite connected tree with non-degenerate labeling generates an ultrametric space.

Conjecture 4.3
.

The following statements are equivalent for every connected graph
G
:

(i)

The ultrametric space
(V(G),d_{l})
is discrete for every non-degenerate labeling

l:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(ii)

The graph
G
is locally finite.

Recall that an ultrametric space
(X,d)
is called
discrete
if for every
p\in X
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

d(p,x)>\varepsilon

for every
x\in X\setminus\{p\}
.

We note that Conjecture
4.3
is valid if
G
is a tree (see Corollary 5 in
Dovgoshey and Küçükaslan 2022
).

In Example
3.7
we describe a connected graph
H
that is not locally finite but generates an ultrametric
for each non-degenerate labeling
l:V(H)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Problem 4.4
.

Describe, up to graph isomorphism, all connected graphs
G
which generate ultrametric spaces for each non-degenerate labeling
l:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Conjecture 4.5
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

There exists a non-degenerate labeling
l:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that the pseudoultrametric space
(V(G),d_{l})
is not ultrametric.

(ii)

There exist two distinct vertices
u,v\in V(G)
and an infinite sequence of cycles
C_{n}\subseteq G
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

u,v\in V(C_{n})

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\{u,v\}\notin E(G),

and the sets

V(C_{n_{1}})\setminus\{u,v\}
and
V(C_{n_{2}})\setminus\{u,v\}

are disjoint whenever
n_{1}\neq n_{2}
.

It should be noted here that arguing as in Example
3.6
one can easily prove the validity of the implication
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
of Conjecture
4.5
.
