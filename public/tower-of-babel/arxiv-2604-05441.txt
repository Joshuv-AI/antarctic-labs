The Stability of a Coupled Degenerate Wave System Under Boundary Control
SUN Ya-nan, ZHANG Qiong

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate a system composed of two degenerate wave equations which are connected at one point. By introducing some inequalities on the weighted spaces and employing the frequency domain method, we prove that the system is polynomially stable, which depends on the degree of the degeneracy.



Keywords

C_{0}

Introduction

In this paper, we investigate the stability of a system consisting of two coupled degenerate wave equations. Specifically, one string is defined on the interval
(0,1)
and degenerates at the point
x=0
, while the other string is defined on the interval
(-1,0)
and degenerates at the point
x=-1
. These two strings are connected at the common point
x=0
, and boundary control is only applied at the free endpoint of the right string (i.e.,
x=1
). The specific mathematical description is given as follows,

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-(a(x)u_{x})_{x}=0,&(x,t)\in(0,1)\times(0,\infty),\\
w_{tt}-(b(x)w_{x})_{x}=0,&(x,t)\in(-1,0)\times(0,\infty),\\
u(0,t)=w(0,t),&t\in(0,\infty),\\
\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}(au_{x})(x,t)=(bw_{x})(0,t),&t\in(0,\infty),\\
\gamma u(1,t)+u_{t}(1,t)+u_{x}(1,t)=0,&t\in(0,\infty),\\
w(-1,t)=0,&t\in(0,\infty),\;\mbox{if}\;\mu_{b}<1,\\
\lim_{x\rightarrow-1^{+}}(bw_{x})(x,t)=0,&t\in(0,\infty),\;\mbox{if}\;\mu_{b}\geq 1,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\;\;u_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x),&x\in(0,1),\\
w(x,0)=w_{0}(x),\;\;w_{t}(x,0)=w_{1}(x),&x\in(-1,0),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\gamma>0
,
a(x)=x^{\mu_{a}}
(with
0\leq\mu_{a}<1
) and
b(x)=(x+1)^{\mu_{b}}
(with
0\leq\mu_{b}<2
). The energy of (
1.1
) is defined as

\begin{split}E(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|\sqrt{a}u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|w_{t}\|_{L^{2}(-1,0)}^{2}+\|\sqrt{b}w_{x}\|_{L^{2}(-1,0)}^{2}+\gamma a(1)|u(1)|^{2}\right),\end{split}

and

\frac{d}{dt}E(t)=-a(1)|u_{t}(1)|^{2}.

Recently, the degenerate wave equations have received much attention. A common feature of these systems is that they can be naturally linked to differential operators with variable diffusion coefficients. While these operators are not uniformly elliptic, even though they are in general uniformly elliptic in compact subsets of the space domain, provided that these subsets are at a positive distance from the so-called zone of degeneracy. This degeneracy zone may occur either on a part of the boundary or on a sub-manifold of the space domain.

Alabau-Boussouira, Cannarsa and Leugering
[
6
]
studied controllability and observability issues for degenerate wave equations of the form

u_{tt}-(\alpha(x)u_{x})_{x}=0,\;\;(x,t)\in(0,1)\times(0,\infty),

(1.2)

where
\alpha
is positive on
(0,1)
but vanishes at zero. The degeneracy of (
1.2
) at
x=0
is measured by the parameter
\mu
defined by

\mu=\sup_{0<x\leq 1}\frac{x|\alpha^{\prime}(x)|}{\alpha(x)}.

One says that (
1.2
) degenerates weakly if
0\leq\mu<1
, strongly if
1\leq\mu<2
. They established observability inequalities for weakly and strongly degenerate equations respectively, which fails when
\mu\geq 2
. Then, the exact controllability of the weakly and strongly degenerate equations was proved by Hilbert uniqueness method. They also obtained the exponential stability of the system with linear boundary control. Bai and Chai
[
11
]
proved the exact controllability of the same system in domains with moving boundary, on which control acts.
Different from
[
6
]
, Gueye
[
5
]
concentrated on the degenerate wave equation with control acting on the degenerate boundary, and obtained the boundary exact controllability for
\mu<1
. Kogut, Kupenko and Leugering
[
12
]
focused on the case where the degenerate point is internal. They discussed how the defect at this damaged point affects the transmission conditions at the site.
Kogut et.al
[
7
]
discussed some issues related to a special class of weighted Sobolev spaces and derived some Poincare’s type inequalities for the weighted function
\alpha
. Some results will be listed in Section 2, which are helpful in proving our main result.

All the above works focus on a single system. For the coupled system, Kogut et al.
[
10
,
14
]
investigated the boundary observability and exact null controllability for weakly and strongly degenerate linear wave equation on a star-shaped planar network.
Salhi, Moumni and Tilioua
[
16
,
17
]
considered a system composed of two degenerate wave equations, which are coupled via velocity terms, with only one equation controlled by a boundary feedback. They proved the controllability and the exponential stability of this system by the multiplier method.
Furthermore, similar to the approach in this paper, Akil, Fragnelli and Issa
[
18
]
studied a coupled system of one degenerate and one non-degenerate string connected at endpoints: the left part is a wave equation degenerate at the left endpoint, and the right part is a non-degenerate wave equation. It is shown that exponential stability of the whole coupled system can be
achieved by applying control only at the right endpoint. Liu and Zhang
[
19
]
showed the stability of an elastic string system with local Kelvin-Voigt damping. They assumed that the damping coefficient has a singularity at the interface
of the damped and undamped regions and behaves like
x^{\mu}
near the interface. The polynomial or exponential stability, which depends on the parameter
\mu\in(0,1]
, is obtained.

Furthermore, the topic of degenerate parabolic equation has received a lot of attention of many authors.
For the null controllability of one-dimensional parabolic system, Cannarsa, Ferretti and Martinez
[
8
]
proved that, with respect to the “damaged” point, “one-side” control can only guarantee the controllability of weakly degenerate systems. For strongly degenerate systems, the degeneracy is so strong that “two-sides” control is necessary to ensure the system’s controllability. Moreover, there are many studies on the stability of a wave equation coupled with a degenerate parabolic equation. In
[
9
]
, Han, Wang and Wang proved that the semigroup corresponding to such a system has a decay rate of
O(t^{-\frac{3-\mu}{2(1-\mu)}})
as
t\to\infty
, where
\mu\in(0,1)
. However, it is not the optimal decay rate. Tebou
[
13
]
improved the decay rate and established a better decay rate over different intervals with respect to the degree of the degeneracy for
\mu\in(0,\frac{3}{4})
. Later, by analyzing the norm of the resolvent operator along the imaginary axis, Han, Song and Yu
[
15
]
enhanced the decay rate to
t^{-\frac{2-\mu}{1-\mu}}
, which is consistent with the optimal result obtained in
[
3
]
for the constant-diffusion coefficient equation (
\mu=0
).

\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}=o(1),\;\|\sqrt{a}u_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}=o(1).

(3.21)

Multiplying (
3.17d
) by
2(1+x)w_{n}^{\prime}
and integrating over
(-1,0)
and using lemma
2.3
again, we obtain

\begin{gathered}\|y_{n}\|_{L^{2}(-1,0)}^{2}+(1-\mu_{b})\|\sqrt{b}w_{n}^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(-1,0)}-b(0)|w_{n}^{\prime}(0)|^{2}-|y_{n}(0)|^{2}=o(1).\end{gathered}

(3.22)

It is clear that
a^{-1}\in L^{1}(0,1)
since
0\leq\mu_{a}<1
.Then, we deduce from (
3.17a
) that

\begin{split}|v_{n}(0)|\leq&|\lambda_{n}|\left|\int_{0}^{1}u_{n}^{\prime}dx\right|+|v_{n}(1)|\\
\leq&|\lambda_{n}|\left(\int_{0}^{1}{1\over a}dx\right)^{1\over 2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}a|u_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}dx\right)^{1\over 2}+|v_{n}(1)|\\
=&{\lambda_{n}\over\sqrt{1-\mu_{a}}}\|\sqrt{a}u_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+|v_{n}(1)|,\end{split}

(3.23)

by the transmission condition, (
3.18
), (
3.21
) and
|\lambda_{n}|<\eta
, which implies that

|y_{n}(0)|=|v_{n}(0)|\leq o(1).

(3.24)

Furthermore, since
b(0)=1
and using the transmission condition, it holds that

b(0)|w_{n}^{\prime}(0)|^{2}=|b(0)w_{n}^{\prime}(0)|^{2}=\left|\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}(au_{n}^{\prime})(x)\right|^{2}.

(3.25)

Then, it follows from
|\lambda_{n}|<\eta
, (
3.17b
), (
3.19
) and (
3.21
) that

\begin{split}\left|\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}(au_{n}^{\prime})(x)\right|\leq&\left|\int_{0}^{1}(au^{\prime})^{\prime}dx\right|+|a(1)u_{n}^{\prime}(1)|\\
\leq&|\lambda_{n}|\left|\int_{0}^{1}v_{n}dx\right|+|a(1)u_{n}^{\prime}(1)|\\
\leq&o(1).\end{split}

(3.26)

From (
3.22
)–(
3.26
), we have

\|y_{n}\|_{L^{2}(-1,0)}^{2}+(1-\mu_{b})\|\sqrt{b}w_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(-1,0)}^{2}=o(1).

(3.27)

Due to
0\leq\mu_{b}<1
, one can directly deduce from (
3.27
) that

\|y_{n}\|_{L^{2}(-1,0)}^{2}=o(1),\;\;\|\sqrt{b}w_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(-1,0)}^{2}=o(1),

which is a contradiction to
\|U_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}=1
.

Next, we shall show that
i\mathbb{R}\subset\rho(\mathcal{A}_{2})
by the same method. In this case, (
3.17c
) holds in
V_{b}^{1}(-1,0)
. Repeating the above arguments yields that (
3.27
) remains true. Then, multiplying (
3.17d
) by
w_{n}
and integrating over
(-1,0)
, we obtain

\|\sqrt{b}w_{n}^{\prime}\|^{2}-\|y_{n}\|^{2}+b(0)w_{n}(0)w_{n}^{\prime}(0)=o(1).

(3.28)

By (
3.17a
), (
3.23
), (
3.26
) and the transmission conditions, it holds that

|b(0)w_{n}(0)w_{n}^{\prime}(0)|\leq\left|\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}a(x)u_{n}^{\prime}(x)\right||u_{n}(0)|\leq\lambda_{n}^{-1}o(1).

Here
|u_{n}(0)|
is bounded. Indeed,

|u_{n}(0)|\leq|u_{n}(1)|+\int_{0}^{1}|u_{n}^{\prime}|dx\leq|u_{n}(1)|+C\|\sqrt{a}u_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq o(1).

(3.29)

Combining with (
3.27
) and (
3.29
), it implies that

(2-\mu_{b})\|\sqrt{b}w_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(-1,0)}^{2}=o(1).

Then,
\|U_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}=o(1)
, which leads to a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

[
4
]
Assume that
\mathcal{A}
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
e^{\mathcal{A}t}
of contraction on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and satisfies
i\mathbb{R}\subset\rho(\mathcal{A})
. Then,
e^{\mathcal{A}t}
is polynomially stable with order
\theta
, if and only if

\limsup_{|\omega|\rightarrow\infty}\omega^{-{1\over\theta}}\|(i\omega-\mathcal{A})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}<\infty

(3.30)

The proof of main result

In this section, we shall use the frequency domain method to prove that the system (
1.1
) is polynomially stable, which depends only on
\mu_{a}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

There is a constant
C>0
such that

\|T_{i}(t)U_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq t^{-{2-\mu_{a}\over 2}}\|U_{0}\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_{i})},\;\;\mbox{for each }U_{0}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_{i}),\;i=1,2.

(4.1)

Proof.

For clarity, we only provide the detailed argument for the case
i=1
. The case
i=2
follows analogously. By lemma
3.3
, it is sufficient to show that there exists a constant
r>0
such that

\inf_{\|U\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1,\;\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}\|\lambda_{n}^{\delta}(i\lambda_{n}-\mathcal{A}_{1})U_{n}\|\geq r,\qquad\forall\;U\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_{1}),

(4.2)

where
\delta={2\over 2-\mu_{a}}
. By contradiction, we suppose that (
4.2
) is not true. Then, there exists a sequence
\{\lambda_{n},U_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_{1})
with
\|U_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}=1
and
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\infty
, such that

\|\lambda_{n}^{\delta}(i\lambda_{n}-\mathcal{A}_{1})U_{n}\|=o(1),

(4.3)

which is equivalent to, as
n\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{\delta}(i\lambda_{n}u_{n}-v_{n})=o(1),\;\;

\displaystyle\mbox{in}\;V_{a}^{1}(0,1),

(4.4a)

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{\delta}(i\lambda_{n}v_{n}-(au_{n}^{\prime})^{\prime})=o(1),\;\;

\displaystyle\mbox{in}\;L^{2}(0,1),

(4.4b)

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{\delta}(i\lambda_{n}w_{n}-y_{n})=o(1),\;\;

\displaystyle\mbox{in}\;W_{b}^{1}(-1,0),

(4.4c)

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{\delta}(i\lambda_{n}y_{n}-(bw_{n}^{\prime})^{\prime})=o(1),\;\;

\displaystyle\mbox{in}\;L^{2}(-1,0).

(4.4d)

By dissipativeness of the operator
\mathcal{A}_{1}
, one has

\Re\langle(i\lambda_{n}-\mathcal{A}_{1})U_{n},U_{n}\rangle=-a(1)|v_{n}(1)|^{2}=\lambda_{n}^{-\delta}o(1).

Combining with (
4.4a
) and the boundary condition, it holds that

u_{n}(1)=\lambda_{n}^{-1-{\delta\over 2}}o(1),\;\;\left|u_{n}^{\prime}(1)\right|\leq\gamma|u_{n}(1)|+|v_{n}(1)|=\lambda_{n}^{-{\delta\over 2}}o(1).

(4.5)

In what follows, we shall prove that

\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)},\;\|\sqrt{a}u_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)},\;\|y_{n}\|_{L^{2}(-1,0)},\;\|\sqrt{b}w_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(-1,0)}=o(1),

which contradicts
\|U_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}=1
.

Taking
L^{2}(0,1)
inner product of (
4.4b
) with
2xu_{n}^{\prime}
, then using lemma
2.3
, one has

\begin{gathered}\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+(1-\mu_{a})\int_{0}^{1}a|u_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}dx-|v_{n}(1)|^{2}-a(1)|u_{n}^{\prime}(1)|^{2}=\lambda_{n}^{-\delta}o(1).\end{gathered}

From
0\leq\mu_{a}<1
and (
4.5
), it holds that

\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}=\lambda_{n}^{-{\delta\over 2}}o(1),\;\;\|\sqrt{a}u_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}=\lambda_{n}^{-{\delta\over 2}}o(1).

(4.6)

Multiplying (
4.4b
) and (
4.4d
) by
u_{n}
and
w_{n}
separately, adding the results yields

\begin{gathered}\|\sqrt{a}u_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|\sqrt{b}w_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(-1,0)}^{2}+\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}a(x)u_{n}^{\prime}(x)u_{n}(x)+\lim_{x\to-1^{+}}b(x)w_{n}^{\prime}(x)w_{n}(x)\\
-\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}-\|y_{n}\|_{L^{2}(-1,0)}^{2}-a(1)u_{n}^{\prime}(1)u_{n}(1)-b(0)w_{n}^{\prime}(0)w_{n}(0)=\lambda_{n}^{-\delta}o(1).\end{gathered}

(4.7)

Then, it follows from transmission condition, the boundary conditions and (
4.5
) that

\begin{gathered}\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}a(x)u_{n}^{\prime}(x)u_{n}(x)+\lim_{x\to-1^{+}}b(x)w_{n}^{\prime}(x)w_{n}(x)-a(1)u_{n}^{\prime}(1)u_{n}(1)-b(0)w_{n}^{\prime}(0)w_{n}(0)=\lambda_{n}^{-\delta}o(1).\end{gathered}

(4.8)

Thus, substituting (
4.6
) and (
4.8
) into (
4.7
), one has

\|\sqrt{b}w_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(-1,0)}^{2}-\|y_{n}\|_{L^{2}(-1,0)}^{2}=\lambda_{n}^{-\delta}o(1).

(4.9)

Taking
L^{2}(-1,0)
inner product of (
4.4c
) with
2(x+1)w_{n}^{\prime}
, and using lemma
2.3
and (
4.9
), we obtain

(2-\mu_{b})\|y_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(-1,0)}-|y_{n}(0)|^{2}-b(0)|w_{n}^{\prime}(0)|^{2}=\lambda_{n}^{-\delta}o(1).

(4.10)

We claim that
y_{n}(0)=o(1)
and
b(0)|w_{n}^{\prime}(0)|^{2}=o(1)
. Indeed, it holds that

y_{n}(0)=v_{n}(0),\;\;\;b(0)|w_{n}^{\prime}(0)|^{2}=\left|\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}a(0)u_{n}^{\prime}(0)\right|^{2}.

(4.11)

Thus, it is sufficient to prove that

\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}a(x)u_{n}^{\prime}(x)=o(1),\;\;v_{n}(0)=o(1).

Let
\xi_{n}=\lambda_{n}^{-\beta}
, where
\beta={2\over 2-\mu_{a}}>0
is a positive number. Then, there exists a sequence
\{\zeta_{n}\}
satisfying
\xi_{n}\leq\zeta_{n}\leq 2\xi_{n}
such that

|(au^{\prime})(\zeta_{n})|=\min_{\xi_{n}\leq x\leq 2\xi_{n}}|(au_{n}^{\prime})(x)|\leq\xi_{n}^{-1}\int_{\xi_{n}}^{2\xi_{n}}(au_{n}^{\prime})(x)dx.

(4.12)

where, by Holder’s inequality,

\begin{split}\int_{\xi_{n}}^{2\xi_{n}}(au_{n}^{\prime})(x)dx\leq\left(\int_{\xi_{n}}^{2\xi_{n}}adx\right)^{1\over 2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}a|u_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}dx\right)^{1\over 2}\leq C\xi_{n}^{1+\mu_{a}\over 2}\|\sqrt{a}u_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}.\end{split}

(4.13)

Due to (
4.6
), it is easy to obtain that

|(au_{n}^{\prime})(\zeta_{n})|\leq\lambda_{n}^{{\beta(1-\mu_{a})\over 2}-{\delta\over 2}}o(1).

(4.14)

Thus, it follows from (
4.4b
) that

\begin{split}\left|\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}a(x)u_{n}^{\prime}(x)\right|\leq&|(au_{n}^{\prime})(\zeta_{n})|+|\lambda_{n}|\left|\int_{0}^{\zeta_{n}}v_{n}dx\right|\\
\leq&|(au_{n}^{\prime})(\zeta_{n})|+\zeta_{n}^{1\over 2}\lambda_{n}\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\\
\leq&\lambda_{n}^{{\beta(1-\mu_{a})\over 2}-{\delta\over 2}}o(1)+\lambda_{n}^{1-{\beta\over 2}-{\delta\over 2}}o(1).\end{split}

(4.15)

On the other hand, there exists a sequence
\{\eta_{n}\}
satisfying
\xi_{n}\leq\eta_{n}\leq 2\xi_{n}
such that

\begin{split}|v_{n}(\eta_{n})|=\min_{\xi_{n}\leq x\leq 2\xi_{n}}|v_{n}(x)|\leq\xi_{n}^{-{1\over 2}}\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}=\lambda_{n}^{{\beta\over 2}-{\delta\over 2}}o(1).\end{split}

(4.16)

Notice that
a^{-1}\in L^{1}(0,1)
since
0\leq\mu_{a}<1
, one has

\begin{split}|v_{n}(0)|&\leq\left|\int_{0}^{\eta_{n}}v_{n}^{\prime}dx\right|+|v_{n}(\eta_{n})|\\
&\leq|\lambda_{n}|\left(\int_{0}^{\eta_{n}}{1\over a(x)}dx\right)^{1\over 2}\left(\int_{0}^{\eta_{n}}a(x)|u_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}dx\right)^{1\over 2}+|v_{n}(\eta_{n})|\\
&\leq\lambda_{n}^{1-{\beta(1-\mu_{a})\over 2}-{\delta\over 2}}o(1)+\lambda_{n}^{{\beta\over 2}-{\delta\over 2}}o(1).\end{split}

(4.17)

Substituting (
4.15
) and (
4.17
) into (
4.10
), it follows from
\beta=\delta={2\over 2-\mu_{a}}
that

(2-\mu_{b})\|y_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(-1,0)}\leq o(1).

(4.18)

Then, it is easy to check that
\|U_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}=o(1)
by (
4.6
) and (
4.18
).

For the case
i=2
, (
4.4c
) holds in the space
V_{b}^{1}(-1,0)
. Repeating the above arguments, we can conclude the same result. The proof is completed.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

It is obvious that the stability of the system depends only on the degeneracy of the right string and is independent of the degeneracy of the left string. On the other hand, we can only obtain the stability of the system in the case of
0\leq\mu_{a}<1
. Mathematically, this is determined by the fact that
a^{-1}\in L^{1}
is valid only when
0\leq\mu_{a}<1
. In practice, when the degeneracy is too strong, i.e.,
1\leq\mu_{a}<2
, the defect at the connection point prevents the boundary control from passing through this point to control the left string.
