Asymptotic Profiles and Non-Trivial Breathers in Kähler-Ricci Flow
Longteng Chen

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the relationship between the long-time behavior of solutions to the Kähler–Ricci flow on an asymptotically conical gradient Kähler–Ricci expander ( M , g , X ) (M,g,X) and the asymptotic behavior of their initial data at spatial infinity. After reviewing several fundamental concepts and basic results concerning the Kähler–Ricci flow, we explore the possibility that a global solution need not be asymptotically self-similar. More precisely, we demonstrate that the asymptotic profile of a solution may depend on the choice of time sequence tending to infinity. In particular, there exist solutions that are asymptotic to infinitely many distinct self-similar solutions along different time sequences. We further establish an explicit connection between this phenomenon and the asymptotic behavior of the initial data under a corresponding family of dilations. Moreover, we show that a single solution can exhibit genuinely different asymptotic behaviors along different sequences of times tending to infinity, as well as under different choices of time-dependent rescalings. Finally, we discuss the possibility of equipping the space of Kähler–Ricci flows on ( M , g , X ) (M,g,X) with a suitable topology such that the parabolic rescaling map of the Kähler–Ricci flows defines a dynamical system. We prove there are infinitely many non-soliton fixed points in this dynamical system, which are non-trivial Ricci breathers.



1.
Introduction

This paper investigates the long-time behavior of the Kähler–Ricci flow on noncompact manifolds. Let
M
be a complex manifold. A smooth family of Kähler metrics
(g(t))_{t\in(0,T)}
on
M
is said to solve the Kähler–Ricci flow equation if it satisfies the evolution equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g(t)=-\operatorname{Ric}(g(t)),\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T).

As a weakly parabolic equation, the Kähler–Ricci flow is expected to exhibit long-time behavior analogous to that of the parabolic equation. It is well-known that for a large number of linear and nonlinear equations the set of solutions is invariant under some group of space-time dilations. This phenomenon gives rise to the study of self-similar solutions, i.e. those solutions which are themselves invariant under the same group of dilations. A self-similar solution to the Kähler–Ricci flow is given by a Kähler–Ricci soliton. An
expanding
(gradient) Kähler–Ricci soliton consists of a triple
(M,g,X)
, where
(M,g)
is a Kähler manifold and
X
is a real holomorphic vector field (of gradient type,
X=\nabla^{g}f
) satisfying the soliton equation:

\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{X}g=\operatorname{Ric}(g)+g.

In the gradient case, this equation can be rewritten as

\operatorname{Hess}_{g}f=\operatorname{Ric}(g)+g.

Let
\omega
denote the Kähler form associated to
g
. Then the soliton equation is equivalently expressed at the level of
(1,1)
-forms as

i\partial\bar{\partial}f=\operatorname{Ric}(\omega)+\omega.

If
\Phi_{t}
denotes the flow of
-\frac{X}{2t}
for any
t>0
, then the
self-similar
solution
g(t)_{t>0}
can be defined as follows:

g(t):=t\Phi_{t}^{*}g.

This solution is self-similar because it is invariant under the following dilation group action.

Definition 1.1

(Dilation group for Kähler-Ricci flow)
.

Let
h(t)_{t\in(0,T)}
be a Kähler-Ricci flow on a Kähler-Ricci expander
(M,g,X)

\mathcal{D}(\lambda)h(t):=\lambda\Phi_{\lambda}^{*}\left(h(\frac{t}{\lambda})\right),\quad\textnormal{for all $t\in(0,\lambda T)$}.

Moreover, the family
\{\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\}_{\lambda>0}
forms a multiplicative group.

2.1.
Kähler cone

For us, the definition of a Riemannian cone will take the following form.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(S,g_{S})
be a closed Riemannian manifold. The
Riemannian cone

\mathcal{C}
with
link

S
is defined to be
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times S
with metric
g_{\mathcal{C}}=dr^{2}\oplus r^{2}g_{S}
up to isometry. The radius function
r
is then characterised
intrinsically as the distance from the apex in the metric completion.

The following is a simple computation.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(S,\,g_{S})
be a closed Riemannian manifold of real dimension
m
and let
(\mathcal{C},g_{\mathcal{C}})
be the Riemannian cone with link
S
and radial function
r
. Then the Ricci curvature
\operatorname{Ric}(g_{S})
of
g_{S}
and the Ricci curvature
\operatorname{Ric}(g_{\mathcal{C}})
of the cone metric
g_{\mathcal{C}}
over
(S,\,g_{S})
are related by

\operatorname{Ric}(g_{\mathcal{C}})=\operatorname{Ric}(g_{S})-(m-1)g_{S}.

In particular, the scalar curvatures
R_{g_{0}}
and
R_{g_{S}}
of
g_{0}
and
g_{S}
respectively are related by

R_{g_{\mathcal{C}}}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(R_{g_{S}}-m(m-1)\right).

We may further impose that a Riemannian cone is Kähler, as the next definition demonstrates.

Definition 2.3
.

A
Kähler cone
is a Riemannian cone
(\mathcal{C},g_{\mathcal{C}})
such that

is Kähler, together with a choice of

-parallel complex structure

. This will in fact often be unique up to sign. We then have a Kähler form
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)=g_{\mathcal{C}}(J_{\mathcal{C}}X,Y)

.

The vector field

is real holomorphic and

group of the link of the cone generates the holomorphic isometric action of a real torus on

that
fixes the apex of the cone. Furthermore, every Kähler cone is affine algebraic

15

3.
Estimation along Kähler-Ricci flow

In this section, we prove Theorem

. We consider the initial Kähler metric
g_{0}
on an asymptotically conical gradient Kähler-Ricci expander
(M,g,X)
that satisfies the following conditions:

Definition 3.1

(

.

We say that a Kähler metric

is

(i)

Its Kähler form can be written as

\omega_{0}=\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{0},

\psi_{0}

The bi-Lipschitz norm at infinity (see Definition

(ii)

Let

(iii)

The metric
g_{0}
is
JX-

Definition 3.2

(bi-Lipschitz norm at infinity)
.

For each

\operatorname{BL}_{x}(g,g_{0}):=\sup\{\sigma\geq 0\ |\ e^{-\sigma}g_{0}(x)\leq g(x)\leq e^{\sigma}g_{0}(x)\}.

Then we define bi-Lipschitz norm at infinity in the following way:

\operatorname{BL}_{\infty}(g,g_{0}):=\limsup_{d_{g}(p,x)\to\infty}\operatorname{BL}_{x}(g,g_{0}).

3.1.
Quantification of initial metric

Combining the metric equivalence with the

Proposition 3.3

Proof.

Let

The following proposition shows that if the initial data

Proposition 3.13
.

There exists an
a>0
, such that if
g_{0}
is
a-
close to

, then there exists a uniform constant
D>1
such that along the flow, we have

\frac{1}{D}f\leq f_{\psi}\leq Df

Proof.

Recall that
f_{\psi}
satisfies that following evolution equation (
[
9
, Lemma 4.4]
):

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi},X}\right)f_{\psi}=-f_{\psi}.

Here
\Delta_{\omega_{\psi},X}:=\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}+\frac{X}{2}
.

Let
A_{\pm}\geq 0
be defined as follows:

\begin{split}&A_{+}:=\inf\{K\geq 0\ |\ (\nabla^{g})^{2}f-Kg\leq 0\};\\
&A_{-}:=\inf\{K\geq 0\ |\ (\nabla^{g})^{2}f+Kg\geq 0\}.\end{split}

Since
(M,g,X)
is an asymptotically conical Ricci expander, the constants
A_{+},A_{-}
are well defined and nonnegative. On
M
, we have
-A_{-}g\leq(\nabla^{g})^{2}f\leq A_{+}g
. By soliton equation
\operatorname{Ric}(g)+g=(\nabla^{g})^{2}f
and the fact that
R_{\omega}+n
is strictly positive, we conclude that
A_{+}>0
.

Take
a>0
such that
A_{+}(e^{2a}-1)+A_{+}^{2}(e^{a}-1)<A_{+}
and
A_{-}(e^{a}-1)<1
. We only need to show on
\{r^{2}\leq\lambda\}\times[0,\log(T+1))
,

\frac{1}{D}f\leq f_{\psi}\leq Df,

holds for some uniform constant
D>1
.

First, we compute

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi},X}\right)(f-A_{-}\psi)&=-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}f-\frac{X}{2}\cdot f-A_{-}\dot{\psi}+A_{-}\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}\psi+A_{-}\frac{X}{2}\cdot\psi\\
&=-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}f-|\partial f|_{g}^{2}-A_{-}\dot{\psi}-A_{-}(\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{\psi}}\omega-n)+A_{-}f_{\psi}-A_{-}f\\
&\leq C_{0}-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}f-A_{-}\dot{\psi}-A_{-}(\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{\psi}}\omega-n)A_{-}f_{\psi}-(A_{-}+1)f.\end{split}

Here
C_{0}\geq 0
such that
|\partial f|_{g}^{2}\geq f-C_{0}
. Since
-A_{-}g\leq(\nabla^{g})^{2}f
, it follows that

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi},X}\right)(f-A_{-}\psi)&\leq C_{0}+A_{-}\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{\psi}}\omega-A_{-}\dot{\psi}-A_{-}(\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{\psi}}\omega-n)+A_{-}f_{\psi}-(A_{-}+1)f\\
&=A_{-}n+C_{0}-A_{-}\dot{\psi}+A_{-}f_{\psi}-(A_{-}+1)f\\
&=A_{-}n+C_{0}-A_{-}\dot{\psi}+A_{-}f_{\psi}-(A_{-}+1)(f-A_{-}\psi)-A_{-}(A_{-}+1)\psi.\end{split}

The Corollary
3.11
shows that
\dot{\psi}\geq-(e^{2a}-1)f_{\psi}-C
and
\psi\geq-(e^{a}-1)f_{\psi}-C
, then we have

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi},X}\right)(f-A_{-}\psi)&\leq C_{1}+C_{2}f_{\psi}-(A_{-}+1)(f-A_{-}\psi).\end{split}

Here
C_{1}=C_{0}+A_{-}n+A_{-}C+A_{-}^{2}C,\ C_{2}=(A_{-}+A_{-}(e^{2a}-1)+A_{-}(A_{-}+1)(e^{a}-1))
. Now we consider
u=f-A_{-}\psi-Bf_{\psi}
with
B>0
to be determined. We compute

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi},X}\right)u&\leq Bf_{\psi}+C_{1}+C_{2}f_{\psi}-(A_{-}+1)(f-A_{-}\psi)\\
&=Bf_{\psi}+C_{1}+C_{2}f_{\psi}-(A_{-}+1)(f-A_{-}\psi-Bf_{\psi})-(A_{-}+1)Bf_{\psi}\\
&=C_{1}+(C_{2}-A_{-}B)f_{\psi}-(A_{-}+1)u.\end{split}

One can take
B>>1
such that
C_{2}-A_{-}B<0
and on
\{r^{2}=\lambda\}\bigcup M\times\{0\}
,
u=f-A_{-}\psi-Bf_{\psi}\leq C_{3}
holds for some uniform constant
C_{3}
. Then it follows that
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi},X}\right)u\leq C_{1}-(A_{-}+1)u
, by maximum principle, we have on
\{r^{2}\leq\lambda\}\times[0,\log(t+1))
,

f-A_{-}\psi-Bf_{\psi}=u\leq C_{4},

holds for some uniform constant
C_{4}>0
. Since
A_{-}\psi\leq A_{-}(e^{a}-1)f+A_{-}C
and we have taken
a>0
such that
A_{-}(e^{a}-1)<1
, therefore

f\leq C_{4}+A_{-}(e^{a}-1)f+A_{-}C+Bf_{\psi}.

It follows that

f\leq\frac{1}{1-A_{-}(e^{a}-1)}\left(C_{4}+A_{-}C+Bf_{\psi}\right)\leq\frac{1}{1-A_{-}(e^{a}-1)}\left(\frac{C_{4}+A_{-}C}{\varepsilon}+B\right)f_{\psi}.

Here
\varepsilon=\inf_{M}f>0
.

Second, we compute

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}+\frac{X}{2}\right)(f+A_{+}\psi)&=-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}f+|\partial f|_{g}^{2}+A_{+}\dot{\psi}-A_{+}\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}\psi+A_{+}\frac{X}{2}\cdot\psi\\
&=-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}f+|\partial f|_{g}^{2}+A_{+}\dot{\psi}-A_{+}\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}\psi+A_{+}f_{\psi}-A_{+}f\\
&\geq-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}f+A_{+}\dot{\psi}-A_{+}\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}\psi+A_{+}f_{\psi}-A_{+}f.\end{split}

Since
(\nabla^{g})^{2}f\leq A_{+}g
, then we have

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}+\frac{X}{2}\right)(f+A_{+}\psi)&\geq-A_{+}\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{\psi}}\omega+A_{+}\dot{\psi}-A_{+}\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}\psi+A_{+}f_{\psi}-A_{+}f\\
&=-A_{+}n+A_{+}\dot{\psi}+A_{+}f_{\psi}-A_{+}f\\
&=-A_{+}n+A_{+}\dot{\psi}+A_{+}f_{\psi}-A_{+}(f+A_{+}\psi)+A_{+}^{2}\psi.\end{split}

The Corollary
3.11
shows that
\dot{\psi}\geq-(e^{2a}-1)f_{\psi}-C
and
\psi\geq-(e^{a}-1)f_{\psi}-C
, then we have

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}+\frac{X}{2}\right)(f+A_{+}\psi)&\geq-C_{5}+\sigma f_{\psi}-A_{+}(f+A_{+}\psi).\end{split}

Here
C_{5}=A_{+}n+A_{+}^{2}C+A_{+}C
,
\sigma=\left(A_{+}-A_{+}(e^{2a}-1)-A_{+}^{2}(e^{a}-1)\right)
. By the definition of
a
, constant
\sigma>0
. Now we consider
v=f+A_{+}\psi-\alpha f_{\psi}
with
\alpha>0
to be determined. We compute

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}+\frac{X}{2}\right)v&\geq-C_{5}+\sigma f_{\psi}-A_{+}(f+A_{+}\psi)-\alpha(X\cdot f_{\psi}-f_{\psi})\\
&\geq-C_{5}+\sigma f_{\psi}-A_{+}(f+A_{+}\psi)-\alpha|X|_{g_{\psi}}^{2}\\
&=-C_{5}+\sigma f_{\psi}-A_{+}(f+A_{+}\psi-\alpha f_{\psi})-A_{+}\alpha f_{\psi}-\alpha|X|_{g_{\psi}}^{2}.\end{split}

Corollary
3.11
tells us that
|X|_{g_{\psi}}^{2}\leq Cf_{\psi}+C
, it follows that

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi}}+\frac{X}{2}\right)v\geq-C_{5}-\alpha C+(\sigma-\alpha C-A_{+}\alpha)f_{\psi}-A_{+}v.\end{split}

One can take
\alpha<<1
such that
\sigma-\alpha C-A_{+}\alpha\geq 0
and
(1-A_{+}(e^{a}-1))-\alpha\geq 0
, therefore, on
\{r^{2}=\lambda\}\bigcup M\times\{0\}
,

holds for some uniform constant
C_{6}>0
. Then it follows that
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}-\Delta_{\omega_{\psi},X}\right)v\geq-C_{5}-\alpha C-A_{+}v
, by maximum principle, we have on
\{r^{2}\leq\lambda\}\times[0,\log(t+1))
,

f+A_{+}\psi-\alpha f_{\psi}=v\geq-C_{7},

holds for some uniform constant
C_{7}>0
. Then we have

f_{\psi}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(f+A_{+}\psi+C_{7}\right)\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(f+A(e^{a}-1)f+AC+C_{7}\right)\leq C_{8}f.

Here
C_{8}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(1+A_{+}(e^{a}-1)+\frac{C_{7}+A_{+}C}{\varepsilon}\right)
. Take
D=\max\{C_{8},\frac{1}{1-A_{-}(e^{a}-1)}\left(\frac{C_{4}+A_{-}C}{\varepsilon}+B\right)\}
, we get

\frac{1}{D}f\leq f_{\psi}\leq Df,

holds on
\{r^{2}\leq\lambda\}\times[0,\log(T+1))
.
∎

[

is a function that only depends on

C_{i}(t)
as the primitive function of

and define
\varphi_{i}(t)-C_{i}(t)
as the new
\varphi_{i}(t)
then we get

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi_{i}(t)-\log\frac{\omega_{\operatorname{bd},i}(t)^{n}}{\omega(t)^{n}}=0.

Let
\varphi:=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(t)-\log\frac{\omega_{\operatorname{bd},1}(t)^{n}}{\omega_{\operatorname{bd},2}(t)^{n}}=0.

The above equation implies that when
t
tends to 0, the function
\varphi(t)
converge to a smooth function
\varphi(0)
on
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{o\}
. In particular, since
(\omega_{\operatorname{bd},i}(t))_{t>0}
has the same initial condition, then
i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(0)=0
. Use the Liouville lemma in
[
11
]
, since
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M\geq 2
, we conclude that
\varphi(0)
is constant
c

\mathcal{C}\setminus\{o\}
. Define
\varphi-c
as the new
\varphi
then we get

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(t)-\log\frac{\omega_{\operatorname{bd},1}(t)^{n}}{\omega_{\operatorname{bd},2}(t)^{n}}=0,

and
\varphi(t)
converges to
0
locally smoothly on
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{o\}
as
t
tends to 0.

Observe that
(\omega_{\operatorname{bd},i})_{t>0}
is a bounded solution, therefore, there exists a uniform constant
C>0
such that for all
t>0

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(t)\right|\leq C.

Since
\varphi(0)=0
on
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{o\}

M\setminus E
on
M

|\varphi(t)|\leq Ct,

holds for all

For all
0<s<T<\infty

\Omega_{s,T}:=\{(x,t)\ |\ x\in M,t\in[s,T]\}

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{\omega_{\operatorname{bd},i}}\right)f_{\varphi,i}=0.

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{\omega_{\operatorname{bd},i}}\right)f_{\varphi,i}&=\Phi_{t}^{*}f-\frac{X}{2}\cdot\Phi_{t}^{*}f+\frac{X}{2}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi_{i}-\Delta_{\omega_{\operatorname{bd},i}}f_{\varphi,i}\\

Observe that, by the elementary inequality, we have

\Delta_{\omega_{\operatorname{bd},1}(t)}\varphi(t)\leq\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(t)\leq\Delta_{\omega_{\operatorname{bd},2}(t)}\varphi(t).

6.4.
Periodic points and Ricci breathers

Let
\omega_{1}\in(\mathcal{N}_{C}^{B},d,(\phi^{\tau})_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}})

notice that

\phi^{\tau}(\omega_{1})=\omega_{1}
for all
\tau\geq 0
if and only if
(M,\omega_{(}1),X)
is an expanding Kähler-Ricci soliton. It turns out that the fixed points of

are self-similar solutions to Kähler-Ricci flow, but the set of periodic points contains more elements.

Theorem 6.12
.

For each
\lambda>1
, on the AC gradient Kähler-Ricci expander
(M,g,X)
there are non-trivial Ricci breathers, which are immortal Kähler-Ricci flow with type III singularity, such that each
\tilde{\omega}(t)_{t>0}
of them satisfies

\lambda\Phi_{\lambda}^{*}\tilde{\omega}\left(\frac{t}{\lambda}\right)=\tilde{\omega}(t).

Here
\Phi_{t}
is the flow of
-\frac{r\partial_{r}}{2t}:=-\frac{X}{2t}
for
t>0
.

In particular,
(\tilde{\omega}(t))_{t>0}
is a periodic point of the dynamical system
(\mathcal{N}_{C}^{B},d,(\phi^{\tau})_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\lambda=e
, indeed, the following method works for any
\lambda>1
. For all smooth non-constant periodic function
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with period
1
on
\mathbb{R}
such that

\sup_{\mathbb{R}}|\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}\psi|\leq C_{k},

consider

\omega_{\operatorname{sing}}=\omega_{\mathcal{C}}+\varepsilon i\partial\bar{\partial}(r^{2}\psi(\log(r^{2}))).

We observe that

e\Phi_{e}^{*}\omega_{\operatorname{sing}}=\omega_{\operatorname{sing}}.

This 2-form satisfies the conditions in Corollary
D
if
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small. Thanks to Corollary
D
and Proposition
6.9
, there is a unique bounded solution to Kähler-Ricci flow
(\omega_{\operatorname{smooth}}(t))_{t>0}
with initial data
\omega_{\operatorname{sing}}
. Since
(e\Phi_{e}^{*}\omega_{\operatorname{smooth}}(t/e))_{t>0}
is also a bounded solution to Kähler-Ricci flow
(\omega_{\operatorname{smooth}}(t))_{t>0}
with initial data
\omega_{\operatorname{sing}}
, then we must have:

e\Phi_{e}^{*}\omega_{\operatorname{smooth}}(t/e)=\omega_{\operatorname{smooth}}(t).

Now we prove
\omega_{\operatorname{smooth}}(t)_{t>0}
is not a self-similar solution. Since this flow is only defined for positive time, if it is a self-similar solution, it must be determined by a Ricci expander.
Suppose that

g_{\operatorname{smooth}}(t)=g_{E}(t),

with
(M,g_{E},Y)
being a Kähler-Ricci expander (we are not assuming it is gradient) with
Y
being a real-holomorphic vector field. Recall the soliton equation:

g_{E}(t)+t\operatorname{Ric}(g_{E}(t))=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{Y}g_{E}(t),

and let
t
tend to 0, we get

g_{\operatorname{sing}}=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{Y}g_{\operatorname{sing}}.

(6.2)

Since

is

. Since
Y

. In this case,

\begin{split}0=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{Y}g_{\operatorname{sing}}-g_{\operatorname{sing}}&=i\partial\bar{\partial}(\frac{Y}{2}\cdot(r^{2}/2+\varepsilon r^{2}\psi(\log r^{2}))-r^{2}/2-\varepsilon r^{2}\psi(\log r^{2})):=i\partial\bar{\partial}\kappa.\end{split}

Since
\kappa
is

invariant and locally bounded, we claim that
\kappa\equiv 0
. Locally, on an open neighborhood

with

Remark 6.13
.

See
[
22
]
for cylindrical non-trivial Ricci breather.

In the above Theorem, we have proved the above solution to Kähler-Ricci flow is not a self-similar solution associated with a Kähler-Ricci expander. In fact, if the AC gradient Kähler-Ricci expander is one of FIK expanders, Cao’s expanders and Gaussian expander, then the Ricci breathers that we constructed are not self-similar solutions associated with Ricci expanders.

To see this, suppose that

g_{\operatorname{smooth}}(t)=g_{E}(t),

with
(M,g_{E},Y)
being a Ricci expander (we are not assuming it is gradient or Kähler). Recall the soliton equation:

g_{E}(t)+t\operatorname{Ric}(g_{E}(t))=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{Y}g_{E}(t),

and let
t
tend to 0, we get

g_{\operatorname{sing}}=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{Y}g_{\operatorname{sing}}.

(6.3)

Since
g_{\operatorname{sing}}
is
U(n)-
invariant, so is
g_{\operatorname{smooth}}(t)
by its construction. Therefore, we can always assume that
Y
is
U(n)-
invariant. In this case, there exists a smooth function
a
that only depends on
r
such that
Y=a(r)X
.
Let
X^{\flat}
be the flat of
X
with respect to metric
g_{\operatorname{sing}}
. Since
g_{\operatorname{sing}}
is
JX-
invariant, it follows that

X^{\flat}=d(\frac{r^{2}}{2}+\frac{X}{2}\cdot(\varepsilon r^{2}\psi(\log r^{2})):=d(\psi_{\operatorname{sing}}(r)).

The metric
g_{\operatorname{sing}}
is Kähler, the equation (
6.3
) implies that

0=(\mathcal{L}_{Y}g_{\operatorname{sing}})_{ij}=\partial_{i}a\partial_{j}\psi_{\operatorname{sing}}+\partial_{j}a\partial_{i}\psi_{\operatorname{sing}}=2\partial_{i}r\partial_{j}r\frac{da}{dr}\frac{d\psi_{\operatorname{sing}}}{dr}.

Therefore,

\frac{da}{dr}\frac{d\psi_{\operatorname{sing}}}{dr}=0,

which implies that
a\equiv constant
. In this case, we have

\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{Y}g_{\operatorname{sing}}=\frac{1}{2}a\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{\operatorname{sing}}=g_{\operatorname{sing}},

which
leads to the same contradiction as in the proof of Theorem
6.12
.
