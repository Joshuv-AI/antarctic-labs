Abstract
In heterogeneous OLG economies, age alone does not tell us who counts as young or old in a local welfare comparison. For such a comparison to have normative content, it must specify the represented margin, the private unit of account, the current information that identifies the relevant cell, and the common support over which claims are made. This paper develops such a discipline, separating raw resource weights from residual weights expressed in private value units and showing that imposed local discount labels are valid precisely when they are induced by a potential on the supported comparison graph, whose vertices are current cells and whose edges are admissible age comparisons. A calibrated audit of an OLG model then maps the frontier of certified claims, with support, units, wedges, and anchors determining which welfare signs are justified. Keywords. Social discounting; intergenerational welfare; overlapping generations; heterogeneous agents; welfare weights; common support; current-cell matching; graph rationalizability JEL Codes. D61, D63, D15, D52, E21, H43, C63

Introduction

Who is the young person in a local social discounting comparison? In a representative agent model, or in an overlapping generations (OLG) model in which age is the only state, the question hardly arises. In a heterogeneous life cycle economy it is central. Young agents differ in assets, earnings, constraints, health, continuation values, and private marginal values of current resources. Choosing one cell rather than another therefore changes the welfare object before any ethical view about age or time has been imposed.

Comparing by age alone has content only after that object is fixed. One may ask how the planner values an extra physical unit at two cells. One may instead express the unit in terms of private marginal value. Or one could ask, in physical resource units, whether age still carries social priority once the information that matters for the current margin, apart from age, and the private unit are held fixed.

The diagnostic I propose here targets the last object. Fix a represented margin, such as saving over one period, and fix the private unit. Record the information, apart from age, that prices that margin, together with the private marginal value of current resources. Young and old cells are compared only where both are supported. When that information and that value coincide, any remaining difference in the planner’s valuation is residual social priority measured in physical resources. Coarser comparisons can still answer other questions, but they acquire the residual interpretation only under this current cell discipline.

Given these admissible comparisons, the formal restriction is a potential condition on a graph whose vertices are supported cells at the current date and whose edges are comparisons from young to old cells that pass the bridge test. Edge labels combine the market compensation factor, the imposed social discount schedule, the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) wedge, and any declared bridge correction. A local schedule is rationalizable by positive residual multipliers if and only if parallel witnesses agree and all cycle sums vanish. Residual levels are then identified up to one multiplicative constant in each connected component. Although the graph lemma itself is standard, the novelty here lies in the economic construction that gives content to its vertices, labels, support, units, bridges, and anchors.

Because edge labels contain bridge corrections, those corrections must be declared before the graph is solved. In the baseline, the bridge is nonpaternalistic on the represented margin: given the current marginal weights, the planner respects the individual’s private ranking of the future leg. The KKT term records implementability rather than a further ethical view, while paternalism, survival, health, or other continuation concerns enter through the bridge if they give that leg a different ethical value. The theorem is conditional on the declared bridge, hence the audit treats bounded corrections as maintained alternatives rather than repairs after the graph is solved.

The fixed anchor result therefore reflects normalization across components and unequal exposure of the target cells, and it is fully consistent with the restriction that every admissible edge tilts toward younger agents.

The study’s contribution is therefore an interpretation discipline: it identifies when an age sign has residual-priority content, and when it is instead a consequence of the maintained support, unit, wedge, or anchor.

Environment

This section fixes the demographic environment, admissible perturbations, and attainable self-transfer correspondence used in the bridge.

3.4
Valuation systems

Social valuation is the directional derivative of the planner’s objective. Private valuation is summarized by the private envelope derivative

4
Making Local Welfare Weights Comparable

This section turns the remaining question of the previous section into a comparison rule. To that aim, herein I state the local derivative assumptions, fix the private scale used across cells, and define the fiber on which residual weights can be compared. A fiber requires equality of the current code for the margin and equality of the private envelope. Within such a fiber, a comparison in physical resources isolates residual social priority, whereas outside it, an age sign may also reflect the unit or the current state.

, then the raw ratio differs from the residual ratio by
\upsilon_{t}(x)/\upsilon_{t}(x^{\prime})
, violating residual isolation. If it merges distinct verified codes, it abandons the verified margin sufficiency of

. The exact fiber imposes precisely these two equalities and is unchanged by common rescaling.
∎

Remark 4.13

(Weaker comparability)
.

If the analyst has only partial cardinal comparability, the exact-fiber graph is indexed by that weaker unit system. All graph and interval statements remain conditional on the chosen unit.

Lemma 4.14

(Nonvacuity under a monotone matching state)
.

Suppose that for two ages and a common verified code
u
, the scalar resource state
b\mapsto\upsilon_{t}(a,b,u)
is continuous and strictly monotone on support intervals whose images overlap. Then every
v
in the overlap defines a nonempty cross-age exact fiber.

4.5
Local scope and conditional residual age neutrality

Definition 4.15

(Conditional residual age neutrality relative to a comparison system)
.

Given a comparison system
(M,\chi,\upsilon)
, the social criterion is
conditionally residual-age neutral
on a set of exact comparison tuples if

\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}})=\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})

for each tuple in the set. The neutrality statement is local to the represented margin, verified current map, private unit, and support.

Remark 4.16

(From exact comparisons to reported intervals)
.

Exact fiber tuples are the objects governed by the theorem. An
\varepsilon
band turns
equality into explicit mismatch bounds, and a coarser map adds a contamination radius

\kappa

numerical section reports intervals before any point summary.

Pairwise Decomposition under Current-Cell Matching

This section derives the pairwise algebra behind the local comparison. On exact fibers,
the current term reduces to a ratio of residual multipliers, while coarser rules also
carry variation in the private scale and approximate matches record that variation as
an explicit error. Longer horizons are treated first along a fixed chain. The graph
restrictions later on in

different chains with the same endpoints agree.

5.1
Comparison tuples

Now fix date
t
. Let
x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}}
and
x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}}
be current cells in the same comparison fiber, with
a^{\mathrm{o}}>a^{\mathrm{y}}

s=t+a^{\mathrm{o}}-a^{\mathrm{y}}

has age

Definition 5.1

(Comparison tuple)
.

A

is

\pi=(t,x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}},x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}},x_{s}^{\mathrm{o}})

such that (i)
x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}}
and

with

Definition 5.2

(Ordered and baseline bridge-verified tuples)

An

is a quadruple
(t,x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}},x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}},x_{s}^{\mathrm{o}})
satisfying the same age ordering, alignment, and continuation condition, without imposing exact fiber equality. It is
admissible approximate
when the maintained current codes agree and the stated private-envelope mismatch bound holds. It is

\mathfrak{B}(\pi)
introduced in

6.2
.

Remark 5.3

(Portfolio-valued future legs)
.

The displayed tuple uses a singleton continuation cell. For finite-support represented leg
L_{s}^{\pi}=\sum_{j}\ell_{j}\delta_{x_{sj}}
, wth survival and transition probabilities absorbed in
\ell_{j}
, write

\mathcal{V}_{s}^{\omega}(\pi):=\sum_{j}\ell_{j}\omega_{s}(x_{sj}),

and set
\mathcal{V}_{s}^{\omega}(\pi)=\omega_{s}(x_{s}^{\mathrm{o}})
in the singleton case. Corresponding

is the gross market compensation factor for that represented portfolio leg, and the one-step risk-free margin uses the same convention.

Definition 5.4

(Gross social discount ratio)
.

For
\pi=(t,x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}},x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}},x_{s}^{\mathrm{o}})
, or its portfolio-leg version, define

\mathcal{D}^{S}(\pi):=\frac{\omega_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}})}{\mathcal{V}_{s}^{\omega}(\pi)}.

(8)

It is the marginal number of represented future old-age leg units socially equivalent to one current old-age resource unit.

Under this convention,
\mathcal{D}^{S}(\pi)
is the gross social discount ratio for the represented future leg, so
\rho^{S}
is the corresponding net rate when
\mathcal{D}^{S}=1+\rho^{S}
. The factor
\mathcal{Q}(\pi)
is the gross market compensation factor for that same leg, and
\Lambda(\pi)
corrects for KKT implementability. The comparison is meaningfull only when the social and market ratios price the same represented leg in the same private unit.

5.2
Exact fiber decomposition

Proposition 5.5

(Exact-fiber pairwise decomposition)
.

For any comparison tuple
\pi
, define

\mathcal{M}(\pi):=\frac{\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}})}{\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})}

(9)

and

\mathcal{R}^{S}(\pi):=\frac{\omega_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})}{\mathcal{V}_{s}^{\omega}(\pi)}.

(10)

Then

\mathcal{D}^{S}(\pi)=\mathcal{M}(\pi)\mathcal{R}^{S}(\pi).

(11)

Proof.

Using
\omega_{t}=\eta_{t}\upsilon_{t}
,

\mathcal{D}^{S}(\pi)=\frac{\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}})}{\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})}\frac{\upsilon_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}})}{\upsilon_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})}\frac{\omega_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})}{\mathcal{V}_{s}^{\omega}(\pi)}.

The middle ratio is
1
on an exact fiber.
∎

Exact fiber matching turns the current term into a ratio of residual multipliers. The remaining term is the within-person intertemporal comparison along the younger individual’s continuation.

5.3
Coarser and approximate current matching

A coarser rule may hold fixed only the state attribute and allow different values of
\upsilon_{t}
. The identity remains exact, and the error bound comes from the maintained bound on the difference in
\log\upsilon_{t}
.

Proposition 5.6

(Current scale contamination under coarser matching)
.

Fix an ordered tuple
\bar{\pi}=(t,x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}},x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}},x_{s}^{\mathrm{o}})
with
a(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}})>a(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})

\chi_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}})=\chi_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})

\upsilon_{t}

\mathcal{D}^{S}(\bar{\pi})=\frac{\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}})}{\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})}\frac{\upsilon_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}})}{\upsilon_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})}\mathcal{R}^{S}(\bar{\pi}),

(12)

or equivalently

\log\frac{\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})}{\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}})}=\log\mathcal{R}^{S}(\bar{\pi})-\log\mathcal{D}^{S}(\bar{\pi})+\log\frac{\upsilon_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}})}{\upsilon_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})}.

(13)

Corollary 5.7

(Approximate matching bound)
.

Under

5.3

|\log\upsilon_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}})-\log\upsilon_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})|\leq\varepsilon,

then

\left|\log\frac{\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})}{\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}})}-\bigl(\log\mathcal{R}^{S}(\bar{\pi})-\log\mathcal{D}^{S}(\bar{\pi})\bigr)\right|\leq\varepsilon.

(14)

Equivalently,

\displaystyle\log\frac{\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})}{\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}})}

\displaystyle\geq\log\mathcal{R}^{S}(\bar{\pi})-\log\mathcal{D}^{S}(\bar{\pi})-\varepsilon,

(15)

\displaystyle\log\frac{\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})}{\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}})}

\displaystyle\leq\log\mathcal{R}^{S}(\bar{\pi})-\log\mathcal{D}^{S}(\bar{\pi})+\varepsilon.

(16)

When
\varepsilon=0
, the exact fiber identity obtains.

Proposition 5.8

(Ordering ambiguity under coarse matching)
.

For any
\gamma>0
and
\delta>\gamma

with common comparison attribute such that

(17)

Any exact fiber tuple with the same
\mathcal{R}^{S}
and
\mathcal{D}^{S}
satisfies
\log(\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})/\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}}))=\gamma>0
. Coarse matching can overturn the inferred age tilt.

5.4
Multiplication along a fixed exact fiber chain

For a chain of exact fiber comparison tuples

\mathcal{D}^{S}(\pi_{0:n-1}):=\prod_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{D}^{S}(\pi_{\ell}).

Corollary 5.9

(Blockwise multiplication on a fixed chain)
.

For any exact fiber comparison tuples

(19)

The product form also holds for portfolio-valued legs, with bounds multiplying across links and log errors adding.

6
The Represented Margin and Market Bridge

In this section, I fix the represented self-transfer margin
M
, state the benchmark represented-margin bridge, and record the KKT-consistent market factor used by the graph. All objects are local to the maintained margin, current map, and private unit.

Throughout this section, comparisons are per current living person. For the one-step saving margin, the represented perturbation removes one current resource unit from the young cell and, conditional on survival, delivers the represented continuation portfolio over next-period states. Survival probabilities enter the weights on that future leg,
\mathcal{Q}
prices the corresponding route, and
\Lambda
records the KKT correction. Alternative actuarial conventions define different represented margins and require rebuilding the graph.

6.1
The bridge domain and verified current maps

For cell
(t,x)
, let
P_{t,x}
be the benchmark continuation law. A
pathwise self-connected perturbation
on
M
removes one current resource unit at
(t,x)
and reallocates the compensating future leg only across that individual’s represented continuation cells, holding benchmark prices, policies, and constraints fixed. Let
D^{P}_{t,x}
and
D^{S,M}_{t,x}
be the private and represented social derivatives.

Assumption 6.1

(Represented domain pathwise derivatives)
.

On
M
, every
(t,x)
admits affine pathwise derivatives on admissible self-connected perturbations, with the null perturbation assigned value
0
. When the same perturbation is described either pathwise or as the induced cross-sectional perturbation, the two derivatives agree on their common domain.

Definition 6.2

(Nonpaternalism)
.

A criterion is
nonpaternalistic on
M
if, for any represented perturbations
\delta,\widetilde{\delta}
at
(t,x)
, equality of private derivatives implies equality of represented social derivatives, and a strict private ranking implies the same strict represented social ranking. This is the baseline case
\mathfrak{B}(\pi)=1
. Relative to the individual’s private ranking, a higher social value for the represented future leg corresponds to
\mathfrak{B}(\pi)<1
, and a lower social value corresponds to
\mathfrak{B}(\pi)>1
.

6.2
What the bridge assumes and what it leaves open

The bridge states how the represented continuation enters the planner’s valuation and is separate from the map defining current cells. Formally,
\mathfrak{B}:\Pi^{M}\to(0,\infty)
is a measurable primitive on the declared domain of tuples. It is fixed before the graph is solved, not chosen edge by edge to fit residuals. Three cases fix the convention.

(A)

Private-ranking benchmark.
If the planner uses the individual’s private ranking of the represented continuation, then
\mathfrak{B}(\pi)=1
and
\mathcal{R}^{S}(\pi)=\mathcal{Q}(\pi)\Lambda(\pi)
.

(B)

Paternalism about continuation.
If the planner values that continuation differently from the individual, then
\mathcal{R}^{S}(\pi)=\mathcal{Q}(\pi)\Lambda(\pi)\mathfrak{B}(\pi)
. A higher social value corresponds to
\mathfrak{B}(\pi)<1
and a lower social value to
\mathfrak{B}(\pi)>1
.

(C)

Survival, health, or other continuation weights.
Ethical weights on survival, health, or future states enter
\mathfrak{B}
, not
\chi
or
\upsilon
. They change the edge label, but not current support unless they change the represented margin itself.

Let
\widetilde{\Lambda}(\pi):=\Lambda(\pi)\mathfrak{B}(\pi)
. Positivity makes
\log\mathfrak{B}(\pi)
well defined. For an exact edge,

\log\frac{\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}})}{\eta_{t}(x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}})}=\log\mathcal{Q}(\pi)-\log\mathcal{D}^{S}(\pi)+\log\Lambda(\pi)+\log\mathfrak{B}(\pi).

(20)

Thus younger residual priority is certified whenever

\mathfrak{B}(\pi)>\frac{\mathcal{D}^{S}(\pi)}{\mathcal{Q}(\pi)\Lambda(\pi)},

with the coresponding
e^{\varepsilon}
adjustment under approximate matching. The baseline sets
\mathfrak{B}\equiv 1
. Parametric bridge corrections are primitives. When they are bounded rather than fixed, the bounds enter through explicit restrictions on edges or target corridors, and the audit reports how certification changes with the unit, wedge, bridge range, or support.

Consider now a finite-life household with state

, age, nonresource state, and scalar resources. At age

V_{a}(w,\zeta;m)=\max_{d\in D_{a}(\zeta,w)}\left\{u_{a}(c_{a}(d,w,\zeta)+m,\zeta)+\beta p_{a}\int V_{a+1}(W_{a}(d,w,\zeta,\zeta^{\prime}),\zeta^{\prime};0)P_{a}(\zeta,w,d,\mathrm{d}\zeta^{\prime})\right\}

(21)

g_{aj}(d,w,\zeta)\leq 0,\qquad j=1,\ldots,J_{a}.

(22)

Let

.

Assumption 6.3

(Convex age-structured household class)
.

For each
(a,\zeta)

A primitive certificate lists the represented leg, market price, and KKT loadings, then lets

Definition 6.5

(Verification protocol for the current map)

For margin

\chi_{a}^{M}(w,\zeta):=\Psi_{a}^{M}(\zeta),\qquad\Psi_{a}^{M}(\zeta)=\min\{\text{non-age primitives identifying }(L^{M},\mathcal{Q}^{M},\Xi^{M},\operatorname{supp}L^{M})\},

(23)

On maintained support, a cross-age current match requires

Every strict coarsening requires a separate sufficiency check, whereas any refinement remains sufficient and can only reduce the admissible match set.

Proposition 6.6

(One-asset saving-margin current map)

In the one-asset life-cycle problem of

The full rule also requires equality of

Remark 6.7

(Worked one-asset primitive certificate)
.

In the benchmark one-asset model,

6.3

Fix an ordered tuple
\pi=(t,x_{t}^{\mathrm{o}},x_{t}^{\mathrm{y}},x_{s}^{\mathrm{o}})
, or a finite-support portfolio version of its future leg. Let

Definition 7.3

(Exact fiber current comparison graph)

Proposition 7.4

and witness

Theorem 7.5

(Current-graph rationalizability of imposed local discount labels)
.

Fix a represented margin
M
, current map
\chi
, private unit
\upsilon
, and bridge domain. Construct the finite exact current-date comparison graph from that triplet. For each directed current edge

that satisfy two restrictions. First, all witness labels attached to the same current edge agree (parallel-witness consistency). Second, every closed walk in the underlying undirected graph has zero signed
\bar{g}

Relative residual-multiplier levels across disconnected components are not identified without additional anchors. If targets load differently across disconnected components, target comparisons are not identified without component anchors. Countable or measurable graphs are treated componentwise under integrable, path-independent sums.

Corollary 7.7

(Component normalization and recursion)

Fix a connected component

9
Conclusion

Local social discounting with rich heterogeneity begins by specifying who is being compared.
The current matching rule fixes the represented self-transfer margin, the information that prices that margin apart from age, and the private marginal value used as the common unit.
On supported exact matches, a comparison in physical resources isolates residual social priority.
The graph theorem then gives the discipline.
Imposed local discount labels can be represented by positive residual multipliers if and only if parallel witnesses agree and cycle sums vanish, with levels identified separately in each connected component.

The method is diagnostic and conditional.
A full policy ranking requires assumptions about missing cells, support, units, bridges, and anchors across components.
In heterogeneous life-cycle economies, comparisons by age alone can mix private marginal values, rely on unsupported matches, and conceal violations of the graph restrictions.
The applied recipe is therefore to derive the current map from the represented margin, state the private unit, build the supported graph, report mismatch and component diagnostics, compute intervals before point summaries, and rebuild the graph whenever the unit or the represented margin changes.

The quantitative exercise shows that these requirements bind.
The verified graph can change support and rematching enough to alter selected signs, while alternative units, raw Euler propagation, and richer state spaces can remove sign certification altogether.
When certification disappears, the result is not a numerical artifact but the frontier of the local social discounting claims that the maintained data, margin, unit, bridge, and support can justify.
