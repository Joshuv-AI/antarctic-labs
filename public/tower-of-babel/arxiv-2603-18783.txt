Index Estimates for CMC and Minimal Surfaces with Capillary Boundary
Luca Seemungal

Abstract
We prove that the index of a cmc surface with capillary boundary is bounded from above linearly by its genus, number of boundary components, and branching order, and also by some Willmore-type energy involving the area, mean curvature, contact angle, and ambient curvature. The main auxiliary theorem of more general interest is a comparison of the second variations of area and energy at a branched conformal map with boundary. In the appendix we derive the various second variation formulae for area, enclosed-volume, and wetting functionals away from critical points and for non-admissible variations, the purpose of which is to rather comprehensively fill a gap in the literature.

4.1
Sobolev inequality

We will find, in proving a Sobolev inequality for boundaried branched immersions
u:\Sigma\to M
, that we will need to extend the normal vector field
N
of
\partial M
some distance into the interior of
M
.
We will desire for this extension to have as small gradient as possible, so that our Sobolev inequality might be more optimal, and so we want to extend the normal a large distance into the interior of
M
.
On the other hand, if we extend too far, then we may find the extension to be ill-defined.
It is clear then that we need to consider a modification of the notion of
focal radius
, which we define below.

Definition 8
.

The
focal radius
of a manifold
M
with boundary is the largest radius
r>0
such that every point on the boundary is touched by an interior ball of that radius; i.e.,

\sup\left\{r>0\medspace|\medspace\forall y\in\partial M,\exists B(x,r)\subset M\medspace\text{s.t.}\medspace y\in\partial B(x,r)\right\}.

We can now extend
N
smoothly into
M
in the following way: as long as we stay within a distance of the focal radius of the boundary, we can extend
N
by parallelly transporting it along geodesic lines orthogonal to the boundary.
At the focal radius, we may encounter a non-uniqueness problem, which is resolved by making the vector field decay to zero as we approach the focal radius.
Naturally, our choice of decay factor will determine the constant in the Sobolev inequality.
In the following proposition we construct this extension.

Proposition 9
.

Suppose that
M
is a manifold with boundary, whose outward-pointing unit normal we denote by
N
, and whose inner focal radius we denote by
\rho
.
Then for every
\epsilon\in(0,\rho)
, there is a vector field
X_{\epsilon}\in\Gamma(M)
such that
\left|X_{\epsilon}\right|\leq 1
and
\nabla X_{\epsilon}\leq 1/(\rho-\epsilon)
.

Proof.

Since
0<\rho-\epsilon<\rho
, there is a smooth function
\phi_{\epsilon}:M\to\mathbb{R}
which is unity on
\partial M
, decreasing monotonically along geodesic lines emanating orthogonally from
\partial M
, zero on
M_{-(\rho-\epsilon)}:=\left\{x\in M:\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\limits(x,\partial M)<\rho-\epsilon\right\}
(the
(\rho-\epsilon)
-thinning of
M
), and moreover whose derivative is not steeper than
1/(\rho-\epsilon)
.
Indeed, for the very same reason, one can parallelly transport
N
into
M\setminus M_{-(\rho-\epsilon)}
along geodesic lines, emanating orthogonally from
\partial M
, obtaining a smooth vector field
\tilde{N}
on
M\setminus M_{-(\rho-\epsilon)}
.
Defining then

X_{\epsilon}=\begin{cases}\phi_{\epsilon}\tilde{N}&\text{on }M\setminus M_{-(\rho-\epsilon)}\\
0&\text{elsewhere}\end{cases}

yields the requisite vector field.

\int_{\partial\Sigma}\!\left|f\right|\leq\frac{\max\left|X\right|}{\sin\theta}\int_{\Sigma}\!\left|\nabla f\right|+\frac{2\max\left|\nabla X\right|}{\sin\theta}\int_{\Sigma}\!\left|f\right|+\frac{\max\left|X\right|}{\sin\theta}\int_{\Sigma}\!\left|fH\right|,

where

is a vector field on

such that on
\partial M

X=N
.
Combined with the above, this yields

\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\int_{\Sigma}\!f^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left(1+\frac{\max\left|X\right|}{\sin\theta}\right)\int_{\Sigma}\!\left|\nabla f\right|+\left(1+\frac{\max\left|X\right|}{\sin\theta}\right)\int_{\Sigma}\!\left|fH\right|+\frac{2\max\left|\nabla X\right|}{\sin\theta}\int_{\Sigma}\!\left|f\right|.

We may take specifically, for each

to be the vector field

obtained in Proposition
9

\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\int_{\Sigma}\!f^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\right)\int_{\Sigma}\!\left|\nabla f\right|+\left(1+\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\right)\int_{\Sigma}\!\left|fH\right|+\frac{2}{(\rho-\epsilon)\sin\theta}\int_{\Sigma}\!\left|f\right|,

+\mathop{\mathrm{div}}_{\Sigma}\left(\nabla_{s}s+\nabla_{\sigma}s+\nabla_{s}\sigma+\nabla_{\sigma}\sigma\right)^{\top}.

Above and throughout the following, we suppress the notation for summing over

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{E_{i}}\sigma\right)^{\perp}

\displaystyle=A(E_{i},\sigma)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Rm}}^{M}(s,E_{i},E_{i},s)

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla_{E_{i}}s,E_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle E_{i},\nabla_{E_{j}}s\right\rangle

Claim I.

\mathcal{B}=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}_{\Sigma}\left(\sigma\mathop{\mathrm{div}}_{\Sigma}\sigma-\nabla^{\top}_{\sigma}\sigma\right).

Claim II.

\mathcal{C}=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}_{\Sigma}\left(-2\left(\nabla_{\sigma}s\right)^{\top}-2\left\langle s,H\right\rangle\sigma\right)-\left\langle\nabla_{s}\sigma,H\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla^{\perp}_{\sigma}s,H\right\rangle

\mathop{\mathrm{Rm}}^{M}(\sigma,E_{i},E_{i},\sigma)=\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}^{\Sigma}(\sigma,\sigma)-\left\langle A(\sigma,\sigma),H\right\rangle+\left|A(E_{i},\sigma)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{div}}_{\Sigma}\left(\nabla^{\top}_{\sigma}\sigma\right)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{E_{i}}\left\langle\nabla_{\sigma}\sigma,E_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\nabla_{E_{i}}\left(\left\langle\sigma,E_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle\nabla^{\top}_{E_{j}}\sigma,E_{i}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla^{\top}_{E_{i}}\sigma,E_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle\nabla^{\top}_{E_{j}}\sigma,E_{i}\right\rangle+\left\langle\sigma,E_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle\nabla^{\top}_{E_{i}}\nabla^{\top}_{E_{j}}\sigma,E_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sigma,E_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle\nabla^{\top}_{E_{j}}\nabla^{\top}_{E_{i}}\sigma,E_{i}\right\rangle+\left\langle\sigma,E_{j}\right\rangle\mathop{\mathrm{Rm}}^{\Sigma}(E_{i},E_{j},\sigma,E_{i})+\left\langle\nabla^{\top}_{E_{i}}\sigma,E_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle\nabla^{\top}_{E_{j}}\sigma,E_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\sigma}\left\langle\nabla^{\top}_{E_{i}}\sigma,E_{i}\right\rangle+\mathop{\mathrm{Rm}}^{\Sigma}(E_{i},\sigma,\sigma,E_{i})+\left\langle\nabla^{\top}_{E_{i}}\sigma,E_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle\nabla^{\top}_{E_{j}}\sigma,E_{i}\right\rangle

\mathcal{B}=\nabla_{\sigma}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}_{\Sigma}\sigma\right)+\left(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}_{\Sigma}\sigma\right)^{2}-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}_{\Sigma}\left(\nabla^{\top}_{\sigma}\sigma\right);

applying the formula

Proof of Claim II.

Starting with the Codazzi–Mainardi equation, we proceed with the following computation

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Rm}}^{M}(s,E_{i},E_{i},\sigma)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{Rm}}^{M}(\sigma,E_{i},E_{i},s)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(\nabla_{\sigma}A\right)(E_{i},E_{i}),s\right\rangle-\left\langle\left(\nabla_{E_{i}}A\right)(\sigma,E_{i}),s\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla_{\sigma}H,s\right\rangle-2\left\langle A\left(\nabla^{\top}_{\sigma}E_{i},E_{i}\right),s\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla_{E_{i}}A(\sigma,E_{i}),s\right\rangle+\left\langle A\left(\nabla^{\top}_{E_{i}}\sigma,E_{i}\right),s\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad-\nabla_{E_{i}}\left\langle A(\sigma,E_{i}),s\right\rangle+\left\langle A(\sigma,E_{i}),\nabla_{E_{i}}s\right\rangle+\left\langle A\left(\nabla^{\top}_{E_{i}}\sigma,E_{i}\right),s\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\sigma}\left\langle H,s\right\rangle-\left\langle H,\nabla^{\perp}_{\sigma}s\right\rangle+\nabla_{E_{i}}\left\langle E_{i},\nabla_{\sigma}s\right\rangle+\left\langle A(\sigma,E_{i}),\nabla_{E_{i}}s\right\rangle+\left\langle A\left(\nabla^{\top}_{E_{i}}\sigma,E_{i}\right),s\right\rangle;

of course, the third term after the fourth equality vanishes.
Combining this with the expression for
\mathcal{C}
above, we have after some cancellations

\mathcal{C}=-2\nabla_{E_{i}}\left\langle E_{i},\nabla_{\sigma}s\right\rangle-2\left\langle s,H\right\rangle\mathop{\mathrm{div}}_{\Sigma}\sigma-2\nabla_{\sigma}\left\langle H,s\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla_{s}\sigma,H\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla^{\perp}_{\sigma}s,H\right\rangle.

Expanding the claimed expression for

reveals that it is equal to the above, which gives the claim, and hence the lemma.

.

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\!\Phi(t)^{*}\iota_{\psi}\omega

We begin with the useful formula

For the second derivative, notice first that (again by the useful formula (
