Classification of Rational Functions of Degree Three over Finite Fields
Xiang-dong Hou, Siyu Peng, Yongyu Qiang, Shujun Zhao

Abstract
. We study rational functions over finite fields under PGL-equivalence. We say that f , g ∈ 𝔽 q ​ ( X ) f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X) are equivalent if there exist ψ , ϕ ∈ 𝔽 q ​ ( X ) \psi,\phi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X) of degree one such that g = ψ ∘ f ∘ ϕ g=\psi\circ f\circ\phi . Most properties of rational functions over finite fields as they appear in theory and applications are preserved under this equivalence. In a recent work, Mattarei and Pizzato classified rational functions of degree three over finite fields in even characteristic. In the present paper, we classify all rational functions of degree three over finite fields in odd characteristic. Our approach is based on careful analyses of the value frequencies and the ramification points of the degree three rational functions. The completion of our classification also relies on an explicit formula for the number of equivalence classes of degree three rational functions over finite fields recently obtained by the first author.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denote the finite field with
q
elements. For a nonconstant rational function
f=g/h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
g,h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
and
\gcd(g,h)=1
, its degree is
\deg f=\max\{\deg g,\deg h\}=[\mathbb{F}_{q}(X):\mathbb{F}_{q}(f)]
. We treat the projective linear group
\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{F}_{q})
as
\{\phi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X):\deg\phi=1\}
equipped with composition.
Elements of
\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{F}_{q})
are of the form
(aX+b)/(cX+d)
, where
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
ad-bc\neq 0
. Two nonconstant rational functions
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
are said to be
PGL-equivalent
, or simply,
equivalent
, denoted as
f\sim g
, if
g=\psi\circ f\circ\phi
for some
\psi,\phi\in\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{F}_{q})
. It is easy to see that
f\sim g
if and only if an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
restricts to an isomorphism
\mathbb{F}_{q}(f)\to\mathbb{F}_{q}(g)
; if
g=\psi\circ f\circ\phi
,
\psi,\phi\in\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{F}_{q})
, the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
is given by
X\mapsto\phi(X)
. Therefore, for the two elements
\psi,\phi\in\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{F}_{q})
that define the equivalence
f\sim g
,
\phi
is essential and
\psi
is passive, that is, given
g
and
f\circ\phi
, it is easy to determine the unique
\psi\in\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{F}_{q})
, if it exists, such that
g=\psi\circ f\circ\phi
.

The properties of a rational function
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
are very much encoded in the attributes of the function field extension
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(f)
, and vice versa. Qualities and quantities pertaining to the function field extension
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(f)
are preserved under the PGL action on
f
. For example, the number of ramification points and their types in this extension are PGL-invariants of the rational function
f
.

The (two-sided) action by
\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{F}_{q})
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
is quite natural, and the PGL classification of rational functions over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a fundamental question. However, for a long time, this question has not received much attention. Historically, the study of finite fields put a lot emphasis on polynomials and the interest in rational functions was limited. The situation began to change recently when people realized the important role played by rational functions in the theory and applications of finite fields. It was discovered that one can use rational functions to construct high degree irreducible polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. This construction was explored in a series works by several authors
[
1
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
18
]
. For equivalent rational functions, the constructions are essentially the same. Another active topic in finite fields is permutation polynomials which are polynomials that permute the finite field. Such polynomials have extensive applications in combinatorics and cryptography. Rational functions in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
induce functions from the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\mathbb{F}_{q}\cup\{\infty\}
to itself. Rational functions that permute the projective line not only generalize permutation polynomials over finite fields but also facilitate new constructions of permutation polynomials. Permutation rational functions of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
come in equivalence classes, and such equivalence classes have been determined for permutation rational functions of degree
\leq 4
.
There is a rapidly growing volume of literature on permutation polynomials and permutation rational functions over finite fields; we list a few works
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
19
,
20
]
, and more references can be found therein.

All degree one rational functions over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are equivalent. The classification of degree two rational functions over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is also trivial
[
9
]
: there are precisely two equivalence classes which are represented by
X^{2}
and
X^{2}+X
for even
q
and by
X^{2}
and
(X^{2}+b)/X
, where
b
is a nonsquare of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, for odd
q
. The case of degree three is difficult. Very recently, Mattarei and Pizzato
[
13
]
classified degree three rational functions over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for even
q
with an approach based on analysis of ramification points. For odd
q
, they gave an upper bound for the number of equivalence classes of degree three rational function. Let
\mathfrak{N}(q,n)
denote the number of equivalence classes of rational functions of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The results of
[
13
]
give that

\mathfrak{N}(q,3)\begin{cases}=2(q+1)&\text{if $q$ is even and a square},\cr=2q&\text{if $q$ is even and a nonsquare},\cr\leq 4q&\text{if $q$ is odd}.\end{cases}

An explicit formula of
\mathfrak{N}(q,n)
for all
q
and
n
has been found in
[
9
]
. For
n=3
, we have

(1.1)

\mathfrak{N}(q,3)=\begin{cases}2(q+1)&\text{if}\ q\equiv 1,4\pmod{6},\cr 2q&\text{if}\ q\equiv 2,5\pmod{6},\cr 2q+1&\text{if}\ q\equiv 3\pmod{6}.\end{cases}

The main objective of the present paper is to provide a complete classification of degree three rational functions over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with odd
q
. Our strategy is to find a collection of
\mathfrak{N}(q,3)
degree 3 rational functions which are pairwise nonequivalent. Here is a brief description of our approach. We divide degree 3 rational functions
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
into three classes, viewing them as functions from
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
to itself.

(I)

|f^{-1}(\alpha)|=1
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

(II)

|f^{-1}(\alpha)|=2
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

(III)

The remaining case:
|f^{-1}(\alpha)|=3
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, but
|f^{-1}(\beta)|\neq 2
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Clearly, functions in different classes are nonequivalent.

Classification for Class I is known
[
4
]
. For functions in Class II, we are able to bring them to canonical forms and show that the canonical forms are pairwise nonequivalent. Let
\mathfrak{N}_{\rm I}(q,3)
,
\mathfrak{N}_{\rm II}(q,3)
and
\mathfrak{N}_{\rm III}(q,3)
denote the number of equivalence classes in Classes I, II, and III, respectively. We know that

(1.2)

\mathfrak{N}_{\rm I}(q,3)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ q\not\equiv 3\pmod{6},\cr 2&\text{if}\ q\equiv 3\pmod{6},\end{cases}

and

(1.3)

\mathfrak{N}_{\rm II}(q,3)=\begin{cases}q+2&\text{if}\ q\equiv 1\pmod{3},\cr q&\text{if}\ q\not\equiv 1\pmod{3}.\end{cases}

Combining (
1.1
) with (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) gives

(1.4)

\mathfrak{N}_{\rm III}(q,3)=q-1.

To classify functions in Class III, we first assume that
p:=\text{\rm char\,}\mathbb{F}_{q}\geq 5
. We find a set of functions in Class III and show that the functions in the set are pairwise nonequivalent and that the set has cardinality
\geq q-1
. This turns out to be quite difficult. To establish pairwise nonequivalence, we construct some variants based the types of the ramification points of the functions. The difficulty that we encounter here is the fact that degree 3 rational functions over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in odd characteristic can have up to four ramification points while in even characteristic, they can have at most two ramification points. To prove that the set of functions has cardinality
\geq q-1
, we use the Hasse-Weil bound on the number of rational points on absolutely irreducible algebraic curves over finite fields. For
p=3
, the same approach with a few modifications also produces the classification for Class III.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we recall the known classification for Class I. In Section 3, we state and prove the classification for Class II. In preparation for the classification for Class III, two unrelated topics, ramification points of rational functions over finite fields and the cross ratio, are discussed in Sections 4 and 5 separately. Section 6 is devoted to the classification for Class III with
p\geq 5
. Here, Class III is divided into several subclasses according to the types of the ramification points of the rational functions. Class III with
p=3
is resolved in Section 7. We construct several few invariants in Class III which allow us to separate at least
q-1
equivalence classes. Since
\mathfrak{M}_{\text{III}}(q,3)=q-1
, we conclude, a priori, that these invariants completely determine the equivalence classes in Class III. One wonders if it is possible to prove this conclusion directly, i.e., without using the knowledge of
\mathfrak{N}_{\text{III}}(q,3)
. The answer is “almost”, and explore this alternative in Section 8. As concrete examples, the classification of degree 3 rational functions over
\mathbb{F}_{3^{3}}
and over
\mathbb{F}_{5^{2}}
are exhibited in Section 9. Our approach also works for the classification of degree 3 rational functions over finite fields in even characteristic. Appendix A provides a comparison between the result in
[
13
]
and ours. Appendix B contains some simple Mathematica codes for readers to verify the computational results in the paper.

The notion of equivalence extends to nonconstant rational functions over an arbitrary field
\mathbb{F}
. An element of
\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{F})
is represented either by an invertible matrix
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\cr c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right]
or by a degree one rational function
(aX+bY)/(cX+d)
. For
f(X)=P(X)/Q(X)\in\mathbb{F}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F}
, where
P,Q\in\mathbb{F}[X]
,
\gcd(P,Q)=1
, define

\mathcal{S}(f)=\langle P,Q\rangle_{\mathbb{F}},

where
\langle\ \rangle_{\mathbb{F}}
denotes the
\mathbb{F}
-span. For
f,g\in\mathbb{F}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F}
, it is clear that
f\sim g
if and only if
\mathcal{S}(g)=\mathcal{S}(f\circ\phi)
for some
\phi\in\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{F})
. In fact,
\mathcal{S}(g)=\mathcal{S}(f\circ\phi)
means that
g=\psi\circ f\circ\phi
for some
\psi\in\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{F})
. Note that when
\phi
is given,
\psi
is unique since
f\circ\phi
is transcendental over
\mathbb{F}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

In Class II, the equivalence classes are represented by

\frac{X^{3}+t}{X},\ t\in\mathcal{C}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{X^{3}+X^{2}+t}{X},\ t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}.

The number of equivalence classes in Class II is

\begin{cases}q+2&\text{if}\ q\equiv 1\pmod{3},\cr q&\text{if}\ q\not\equiv 1\pmod{3}.\end{cases}

.

(ii)

.

(iii)

Even Characteristic

Our approach also works in characteristic 2. Classes I and II do not depend on the characteristic. We will see that equivalence classes in Class III are determined by the invariant
\theta
. We assume that
q
is even throughout this section.

Proposition A.1
.

(i)

f_{0,t}
belongs to Case II.

(ii)

For
s\neq 0
,
f_{s,t}
belongs to Case II if and only if
\text{\rm Tr}_{q/2}(t/s^{2})=0
and
(s,t)\neq(1,1)
, where
\text{\rm Tr}_{q/2}
is the trace from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Proof.

(i)
In this case,

X^{4}-2X^{3}-sX^{2}-2tX+t=X^{4}+t=(X+a)^{4},

where
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
is such that
a^{4}=t
. By Theorem
6.1
, it suffices to show that
a^{3}\neq t
. If
a^{3}=t
, then
t=1
, whence
1+s+t=0
, which is a contradiction.

(ii).
It is well known that
X^{4}+sX^{2}+t
has a root in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
\text{Tr}_{q/2}(t/s^{2})=0
. Moreover,
X^{4}+sX^{2}+t
has a root
x
with
x^{3}\neq t
if and only if
(X^{3}+t)^{2}\not\equiv 0\pmod{X^{4}+sX^{2}+t}
. We have

(X^{3}+t)^{2}\equiv(s^{2}+t)X^{2}+t(s+t)\pmod{X^{4}+sX^{2}+t}.

It follows that
(X^{3}+t)^{2}\not\equiv 0\pmod{X^{4}+sX^{2}+t}
if and only if
(s,t)\neq(1,1)
. Now statement (ii) follows from Theorem
6.1
.
∎

Theorem A.2
.

For each
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}
, there exist
s,t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
t(1+s+t)\neq 0
and
\text{Tr}_{q/2}(t/s^{2})=1
such that

\theta(s,t)=b
.

The proof of Theorem
A.2
uses the following result on exponential sums over elliptic curves:

Theorem A.3
.

[
10
, Corollary 1]

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
\mathbb{F}_{q}(E)
be its function field and
E(\mathbb{F}_{q})
be the set of rational points on
E
. Let
\chi
be a nontrivial additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(E)
with
\deg f>0
. Then

\Bigl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in E(\mathbb{F}_{q})\cr f(P)\neq\infty\end{subarray}}\chi(f(P))\Bigr|\leq 2(\deg f)q^{1/2}.

Proof of Theorem
A.2
.

Let

g(X,Y)=X^{3}-bY(1+X+Y)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X,Y].

We have

\frac{\partial g}{\partial X}=X^{2}-bY,\quad\frac{\partial g}{\partial Y}=b(1+X).

Since
b\neq 0,1
, it is easy to see that the system

\begin{cases}g(x,y)=0,\vskip 3.99994pt\cr\displaystyle\frac{\partial g}{\partial X}(x,y)=0,\vskip 3.99994pt\cr\displaystyle\frac{\partial g}{\partial Y}(x,y)=0\end{cases}

has not solution. Therefore, the curve defined by the degree 3 polynomial
g
is nonsingular and hence is an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which is denoted by
E
.

Let
N_{0},N_{1}
denote the number of points
(s,t)\in E(\mathbb{F}_{q})
with
\trace_{q/2}(t/s^{2})=0,1
, respectively. Then
N_{0}+N_{1}+1=|E(\mathbb{F}_{q})|
, where
1
counts for the point of
E
at infinity. By the Hasse-Weil bound,
|E(\mathbb{F}_{q})|\geq q+1-2q^{1/2}
. Let
f=Y/X^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(E)
and let
\chi(z)=(-1)^{\trace_{q/2}(z)}
be the canonical additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Set

S=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in E(\mathbb{F}_{q})\cr f(P)\neq\infty\end{subarray}}\chi(f(P))=N_{0}-N_{1}.

By Theorem
A.3
,
|S|\leq 4q^{1/2}
. Hence we have

N_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(|E(\mathbb{F}_{q})|-1-S)\geq\frac{1}{2}(q-2q^{1/2}-4q^{1/2})=\frac{1}{2}q-3q^{1/2},

which is positive when
q\geq 2^{6}
. For
q\leq 2^{5}
, we also have
N_{1}>0
via computer verification.
∎

Remark A.4
.

There is a slightly different prof of Theorem
A.2
without using Theorem
A.3
. Choose
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
\trace_{q/2}(\epsilon)=1
, and show that the equation
s^{3}/t(1+s+t)=b
has a solution
(s,t)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
with

for some
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The equation

,

One can show that

.

Remark A.5
.

The proofs of Theorem

in Theorem
