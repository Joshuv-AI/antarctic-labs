Logarithmic spectral correspondence for V V –twisted Higgs bundles on punctured curves
Pradip Kumar

Abstract
. Let X X be a smooth projective complex curve, P ⊂ X P\subset X a reduced effective divisor, and X 0 = X ∖ P X^{0}=X\setminus P . We study logarithmic V V -twisted Higgs bundles arising from a logarithmic Hecke compactification of a rank-two bundle on X 0 X^{0} . We show that a pair of induced logarithmic line-twisted fields lifts uniquely exactly under explicit local Hecke conditions, and that the lift is integrable precisely when the fields commute. Fixing the compactified spectral curve Y Y , we classify such Higgs bundles by pairs ( F , ϑ ) (F,\,\vartheta) , where F F is a rank-one torsion-free sheaf on Y Y and ϑ \vartheta satisfies a marked spectral condition on a finite subscheme Z ⊂ Y Z\subset Y . This gives a logarithmic extension of the compact rank-two spectral correspondence of [ ABK ] to the punctured case. On the line-bundle locus, the moduli stack is canonically equivalent to Pic d ⁡ ( Y ) × A Z . \operatorname{Pic}^{d}(Y)\times A_{Z}.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective complex curve over
\mathbb{C}
, let
P\subset X
be a reduced effective divisor, and set

X_{0}:=X\setminus P.

Fix a rank-two holomorphic vector bundle
V_{0}
on
X_{0}
. A
V_{0}
-twisted Higgs field on a holomorphic vector bundle
E_{0}
over
X_{0}
is a section

\theta_{0}\in H^{0}\bigl(X_{0},\operatorname{End}(E_{0})\otimes V_{0}\bigr)

satisfying

\theta_{0}\wedge\theta_{0}=0.

The purpose of this paper is to develop a spectral correspondence for such twisted Higgs fields in the case where the twisting bundle is defined only on the punctured curve
X_{0}
. Our approach is based on a logarithmic compactification of
V_{0}
across the punctures
P
, followed by a rank-two Hecke description of the compactified twisting bundle. This produces a framework in which the punctured problem can be studied on the compact curve
X
, while keeping track of the additional data contributed by the punctures.

This problem lies at the intersection of several existing directions. In
[
GGN
]
, the authors study
V
-twisted Higgs bundles for a fixed holomorphic vector bundle
V
on a compact projective curve. In
[
KSZ
]
, Kydonakis–Sun–Zhao establish a Beauville–Narasimhan–Ramanan type correspondence for twisted Higgs
V
-bundles and apply it to parabolic
\mathrm{Sp}(2n,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs moduli; in that setting, the marked structure is built directly into the Higgs bundle, and the spectral side naturally leads to parabolic, orbifold, or root-stack geometry. On the other hand, in
[
ABK
]
, the compact rank-two case is analyzed under a Hecke presentation of the twisting bundle by two line bundles, and the twisted Higgs field is reconstructed from a pair of line-twisted fields satisfying finitely many compatibility conditions.

The present work may be viewed as a logarithmic extension of the rank-two Hecke picture developed in
[
ABK
]
. Starting from a rank-two bundle
V_{0}
on the punctured curve
X_{0}
, we first choose a logarithmic compactification across
P
. Once this compactification is fixed, the problem fits naturally into the general compact framework of
[
GGN
]
, but with new puncture-dependent features that do not appear in the compact case. Our main point is that these puncture contributions can still be incorporated without enlarging the ambient spectral category: the spectral side remains in the classical compactified Beauville–Narasimhan–Ramanan framework of rank-one torsion-free sheaves on an ordinary compactified spectral curve. The extra logarithmic information is instead recorded by finitely many scheme-theoretic conditions on a finite marked subscheme lying over the punctures and the Hecke points. In this sense, the paper is complementary to
[
KSZ
]
: rather than passing to parabolic or stack-theoretic spectral objects, we retain the usual compactified spectral curve and encode the puncture behaviour through explicit marked conditions.

A further feature of the logarithmic setting is that it produces genuinely new enhancement data on the spectral side. This has no direct analogue in
[
ABK
]
. In particular, after establishing the logarithmic extension of the rank-two Hecke correspondence, we isolate an additional puncture-dependent parameter space determined by a finite marked spectral scheme. This leads to the affine scheme
A_{Z}
, which governs the enhancement data on the line-bundle locus.

Our starting point is the existence of logarithmic Hecke compactifications. By Proposition
2.2
, every rank-two holomorphic bundle
V_{0}
on
X_{0}
admits a logarithmic Hecke presentation: there exist line bundles
S,L
on
X
, a reduced effective divisor
D\subset X_{0}
, quotient maps

q_{x}:(S\oplus L)_{x}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{x}\qquad(x\in D),

and lines
\ell_{p}\subset(S\oplus L)_{p}
for
p\in P
, giving an exact sequence

(1.1)

0\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow W(P)\longrightarrow T_{\mathrm{tot}}\longrightarrow 0,\qquad W:=S\oplus L,

with
V|_{X_{0}}\cong V_{0}
and
T_{\mathrm{tot}}
supported on

D_{\mathrm{tot}}:=D\cup P.

Thus the compactified problem is determined by the logarithmic Hecke data

(S,L,D,\{q_{x}\},\{\ell_{p}\}).

Once such a presentation is fixed, every section

\theta\in H^{0}(X,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes V)

induces a pair of logarithmic line-twisted fields

\Theta\in H^{0}(X,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes S(P)),\qquad\Theta^{\prime}\in H^{0}(X,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes L(P)).

Our first main result shows that this reduction is exact.

Theorem
3.2
.

A pair
(\Theta,\Theta^{\prime})
arises from a unique section

\theta\in H^{0}(X,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes V)

if and only if explicit local logarithmic Hecke constraints hold at every point of
D_{\mathrm{tot}}
. Under these conditions, the lift
\theta
is a
V
-twisted Higgs field if and only if
\Theta
and
\Theta^{\prime}
commute.

The spectral theory is built from the first field
\Theta
. Its compactified spectral curve

\varphi:\overline{Y}\longrightarrow X

carries, via the compactified Beauville–Narasimhan–Ramanan correspondence, a rank-one torsion-free sheaf
\mathcal{F}
such that
E\simeq\varphi_{*}\mathcal{F}
. When
\Theta^{\prime}
commutes with
\Theta
, it corresponds to an
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}
-linear morphism

\vartheta:\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\otimes\varphi^{*}L(P).

The logarithmic Hecke constraints are then encoded on the finite marked spectral scheme

Z:=\varphi^{-1}(D_{\mathrm{tot}})\subset\overline{Y}.
This leads to the intrinsic form of the logarithmic spectral correspondence.

Theorem
4.8
.

Assume that
\overline{Y}
is integral. Then logarithmic
V
-twisted Higgs bundles whose associated logarithmic
S
-twisted field has compactified spectral curve
\overline{Y}
are naturally classified by pairs
(\mathcal{F},\vartheta)
, where
\mathcal{F}
is a rank-one torsion-free sheaf on
\overline{Y}
and

\vartheta:\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\otimes\varphi^{*}L(P)

satisfies an intrinsic marked spectral condition on
Z
.

Under additional hypotheses, this intrinsic condition becomes completely explicit.

Theorem
4.10
.

If
Z
is reduced, if the slope maps determined by the logarithmic Hecke data are defined at all points of
D_{\mathrm{tot}}
, and if
\mathcal{F}
is locally free along
Z
, then the intrinsic marked spectral condition is equivalent to a finite system of scalar equations on the fibers over
Z
.

The final part of the paper studies the line-bundle locus of the logarithmic spectral correspondence. In this case the admissible enhancement data
(\mathcal{F},\vartheta)
are controlled by a fixed affine scheme depending only on the marked spectral data and not on the chosen line bundle. More precisely, in Definition
5.1
we introduce the
affine enhancement scheme

A_{Z}:=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}\times_{\underline{H^{0}(Z,\,\varphi^{*}L|_{Z})}}\underline{H^{0}\bigl(\overline{Y},\,\varphi^{*}L(P)\bigr)},

which depends only on
(\overline{Y},Z,b_{Z})
. If

\mathfrak{H}^{\log,\mathrm{lb}}_{\overline{Y},d}

denotes the category fibered in groupoids of logarithmic spectral data
(\mathcal{F},\vartheta)
with
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(\overline{Y})
, then we prove:

Theorem
5.6
.

For each degree
d
, there is a canonical equivalence of categories fibered in groupoids

\mathfrak{H}^{\log,\mathrm{lb}}_{\overline{Y},d}\;\simeq\;\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(\overline{Y})\times A_{Z}.

Moreover, by Corollary
5.7
, this upgrades to a canonical equivalence of algebraic stacks.

Therefore, on the line-bundle locus, the logarithmic enhancement problem separates cleanly into two independent choices: a line bundle on the compactified spectral curve and a point of the affine enhancement scheme
A_{Z}
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the logarithmic Hecke compactification data and proves the existence of logarithmic presentations of rank-two bundles on the punctured curve. Section
3
proves Theorem
3.2
. Section
4
develops the spectral correspondence, first in the intrinsic marked form of Theorem
4.8
, and then in the scalar form of Theorem
4.10
. Section
5
studies the line-bundle locus and proves the product decomposition of Theorem
5.6
.

Throughout the paper, all curves are defined over
\mathbb{C}
, all divisors are reduced effective, and all sheaves are coherent unless stated otherwise.

2.
Logarithmic Hecke compactification data and induced fields

Let
X
be a smooth projective complex curve, and let

P\,=\,\sum_{j\,=\,1}^{m}p_{j}

be a reduced effective divisor
(m\geq 1)
. Set

X_{0}\,:=\,X\setminus P
and
j:X_{0}\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow}X,

and fix a holomorphic vector bundle
V_{0}
of rank
2
on
X_{0}
.

For any holomorphic vector bundle
M
on
X
, write

M(P)\,:=\,M\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(P).

Since
P
is reduced, the quotient
M(P)/M
gives principal parts of meromorphic sections with at most simple poles along
P
. Equivalently, there is a natural short exact sequence

(2.1)

0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow M(P)\xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{pp}_{P}^{M}\ }\bigoplus_{p\in P}M_{p}\longrightarrow 0,

where
M_{p}\,:=\,M\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\kappa(p)
denotes the fiber of
M
at
p
. For each
p\in P
, we write

\beta_{p}^{M}:M(P)_{p}\longrightarrow M_{p}

for the corresponding fiber map. In a local coordinate
z
centered at
p
, and for a local frame
e_{1},\dots,e_{r}
of
M
, the map
\beta_{p}^{M}
is given by

\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{r}\left(\frac{a_{i,-1}}{z}+a_{i,0}+a_{i,1}z+\cdots\right)e_{i}\longmapsto\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{r}a_{i,-1}\,e_{i}(p).

2.1.
Logarithmic Hecke presentations

Fix line bundles
S
and
L
on
X
, and set

W\,:=\,S\oplus L.

Let

D\,=\,\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{\ell}x_{i}

be a reduced effective divisor contained in
X_{0}
, so that
D\cap P\,=\,\varnothing
. For each
x\in D
, choose a nonzero one-dimensional quotient

q_{x}:W_{x}\,=\,(S_{x}\oplus L_{x})\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow}\mathbb{C}_{x},

and define

T_{D}\,:=\,\bigoplus_{x\in D}\mathbb{C}_{x}.

For each puncture
p\in P
, choose a one-dimensional subspace

\ell_{p}\subset W_{p}\,=\,(S\oplus L)_{p},

and set

Q_{p}\,:=\,W_{p}/\ell_{p}.

Applying (
2.1
) to
M\,=\,W
gives a natural surjection

\operatorname{pp}_{P}^{W}:W(P)\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow}\bigoplus_{p\in P}W_{p}.

The data
\{q_{x}\}_{x\in D}
and
\{\ell_{p}\}_{p\in P}
determine a torsion sheaf

T_{\mathrm{tot}}\,:=\,T_{D}\oplus\bigoplus_{p\in P}Q_{p}

supported on

D_{\mathrm{tot}}\,:=\,D\cup P,

and a surjective morphism

\xi:W(P)\longrightarrow T_{\mathrm{tot}}.

At a point
x\in D
, the stalk map
\xi_{x}:W(P)_{x}\,\longrightarrow\,(T_{\mathrm{tot}})_{x}
is the chosen quotient
q_{x}
, using the canonical identification
W(P)_{x}\,\simeq\,W_{x}
. At a puncture
p\in P
, the stalk map
\xi_{p}
is the composition

W(P)_{p}\xrightarrow{\ \beta_{p}^{W}\ }W_{p}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow}Q_{p}.

Definition 2.1

(Logarithmic Hecke presentation)
.

A
logarithmic Hecke presentation
of
V_{0}
is a choice of data
S,L,D,\{q_{x}\}_{x\in D},\{\ell_{p}\}_{p\in P}
as above together with an exact sequence

(2.2)

0\longrightarrow V\xrightarrow{\ \Phi\ }W(P)\xrightarrow{\ \xi\ }T_{\mathrm{tot}}\longrightarrow 0,

such that
V|_{X_{0}}\,\cong\,V_{0}
.

Because
X
is a smooth curve and
T_{\mathrm{tot}}
is a torsion sheaf, the kernel
V\,=\,\ker(\xi)
is torsion-free, hence locally free. Since
W(P)
has rank
2
, so does
V
. Its degree is

\operatorname{deg}(V)\,=\,\operatorname{deg}\bigl(W(P)\bigr)-\operatorname{length}(T_{\mathrm{tot}})\,=\,\operatorname{deg}(S)+\operatorname{deg}(L)+|P|-|D|.

2.2.
Existence and dependence on auxiliary data

We next address the basic existence question: given
V_{0}
, can one always realize it by a logarithmic Hecke presentation? The answer is yes.

Proposition 2.2
.

Every rank-two holomorphic vector bundle
V_{0}
on
X_{0}
admits a logarithmic Hecke presentation.

Proof.

Since
X_{0}
is an open Riemann surface, it is Stein. By the Oka–Grauert principle, every rank-two holomorphic vector bundle on
X_{0}
is holomorphically trivial. Choose a trivialization

\tau:V_{0}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}^{\oplus 2}.

Set

S\,=\,\,\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and
L\,=\,\,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-P),

so that

W(P)\,=\,(S\oplus L)(P)\,=\,\,\mathcal{O}_{X}(P)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}.

Take
D\,=\,\varnothing
. For each
p\in P
, define

\ell_{p}\,=\,\{0\}\oplus L_{p}\subset S_{p}\oplus L_{p}\,=\,W_{p}.

Then
Q_{p}\,=\,W_{p}/\ell_{p}\,\simeq\,S_{p}
, and the induced map

\xi:\mathcal{O}_{X}(P)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{p\in P}Q_{p}

is obtained by taking principal parts in the first summand and zero in the second summand. Under the identification
Q_{p}\,\simeq\,S_{p}
, this is exactly

\xi\,=\,\bigoplus_{p\in P}\beta_{p}^{S}.

We claim that
\ker(\xi)\,=\,\,\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Indeed, near a point
p\in P
, a local section of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(P)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}
has the form

\left(\frac{a_{-1}}{z}+a_{0}+a_{1}z+\cdots,\;b_{0}+b_{1}z+\cdots\right),

and
\xi
gives only the coefficient
a_{-1}
. Thus the kernel condition is precisely that the first component be holomorphic at
p
. Since the second component is already holomorphic, the kernel is
\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

Therefore (
2.2
) is exact with
V\,=\,\ker(\xi)\,\cong\,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 2}
. Restricting to
X_{0}
and using
\tau
, we obtain

V|_{X_{0}}\,\cong\,\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}^{\oplus 2}\xrightarrow[\sim]{\tau^{-1}}V_{0}.

Hence
V_{0}
admits a logarithmic Hecke presentation.
∎

Proposition
2.2
shows that existence is not restrictive in the holomorphic category. The real issue is non-canonicity: the same bundle
V_{0}
may admit many different logarithmic Hecke presentations, depending on the auxiliary choices of
S
,
L
,
D
,
\{q_{x}\}
, and
\{\ell_{p}\}
.

From this point on, we fix one logarithmic Hecke data:
(S,\,L,\,D,\,\{q_{x}\}_{x\in D},\,\{\ell_{p}\}_{p\in P})
and presentation

0\longrightarrow V\xrightarrow{\ \Phi\ }W(P)\xrightarrow{\ \xi\ }T_{\mathrm{tot}}\longrightarrow 0,\qquad W\,=\,S\oplus L.

2.3.
Slope maps and induced logarithmic fields

Let

p_{1}:W(P)\,\longrightarrow\,S(P),\qquad p_{2}:W(P)\,\longrightarrow\,L(P)

be the natural projections. Composing with
\Phi
gives bundle morphisms

(2.3)

\widehat{\rho}_{1}\,:=\,p_{1}\circ\Phi:V\longrightarrow S(P),\qquad\widehat{\rho}_{2}\,:=\,p_{2}\circ\Phi:V\longrightarrow L(P).

For each
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
, let

\xi_{a}:W_{a}\,=\,S_{a}\oplus L_{a}\longrightarrow(T_{\mathrm{tot}})_{a}

be the fiber map induced by
\xi
. Thus
\xi_{x}\,=\,q_{x}
for
x\in D
, while
\xi_{p}:W_{p}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow}Q_{p}
is the quotient map for
p\in P
. Since
(T_{\mathrm{tot}})_{a}
is one-dimensional for every
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
, we can consider the component maps

(2.4)

\xi_{1,a}:S_{a}\longrightarrow(T_{\mathrm{tot}})_{a},\qquad\xi_{2,a}:L_{a}\longrightarrow(T_{\mathrm{tot}})_{a},

obtained by restricting
\xi_{a}
to the two summands.

Assume that both maps in (
2.4
) are nonzero. Since the source and target are then one-dimensional, they are isomorphisms, and there is a unique isomorphism

(2.5)

\rho^{a}:S_{a}\longrightarrow L_{a}

such that

\xi_{2,a}\circ\rho^{a}\,=\,-\,\xi_{1,a}.

Equivalently,

\rho^{a}\,=\,-\,\xi_{2,a}^{-1}\circ\xi_{1,a},

and
\ker(\xi_{a})\subset S_{a}\oplus L_{a}
is the graph of
\rho^{a}
. We call
\rho^{a}
the
slope map
at
a
.

At a point
x\in D
, this is the same fiberwise slope datum that appears in the ordinary Hecke presentation of
[
ABK
]
. At a puncture
p\in P
, one has
\ker(\xi_{p})\,=\,\ell_{p}
, so the slope map
\rho^{p}
is defined exactly when
\ell_{p}
is transverse to both summands
S_{p}
and
L_{p}
, or equivalently when

\ell_{p}\cap S_{p}\,=\,\ell_{p}\cap L_{p}\,=\,0.

Now let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle on
X
. A
V
-twisted Higgs field
on
E
is a section

\theta\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes V\bigr)

satisfying

(2.6)

\theta\wedge\theta\,=\,0\qquad\text{in}\qquad H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes\wedge^{2}V\bigr).

Definition 2.3

(Logarithmic line–twisted field)
.

Let
M
be a line bundle on
X
, and let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle on
X
. A
logarithmic
M
–twisted field
on
E
is a section of

H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes M(P)\bigr).

Given
\theta\in H^{0}(X,\,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes V)
, composition with (
2.3
) produces logarithmic line–twisted fields

(2.7)

\Theta\,:=\,(\mathrm{id}_{\operatorname{End}(E)}\otimes\widehat{\rho}_{1})(\theta)\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes S(P)\bigr),

(2.8)

\Theta^{\prime}\,:=\,(\mathrm{id}_{\operatorname{End}(E)}\otimes\widehat{\rho}_{2})(\theta)\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes L(P)\bigr).

If
E_{0}\,:=\,E|_{X_{0}}
, then the restrictions of
\Theta
and
\Theta^{\prime}
to
X_{0}
are holomorphic sections of
\operatorname{End}(E_{0})\otimes S|_{X_{0}}
and
\operatorname{End}(E_{0})\otimes L|_{X_{0}}
, respectively.

The integrability of
\theta
imposes a commutativity relation between the induced fields.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\theta\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes V\bigr)
satisfy (
2.6
), and let
\Theta,\,\Theta^{\prime}
be defined by (
2.7
) and (
2.8
). Then

(2.9)

(\Theta^{\prime}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{S(P)})\circ\Theta\,=\,(\Theta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{L(P)})\circ\Theta^{\prime}

as meromorphic homomorphisms

E\longrightarrow E\otimes S(P)\otimes L(P).

Proof.

Set

X^{\circ}\,:=\,X\setminus D_{\mathrm{tot}}.

Since
T_{\mathrm{tot}}
is supported on
D_{\mathrm{tot}}
, the sequence (
2.2
) restricts over
X^{\circ}
to an isomorphism

\Phi|_{X^{\circ}}:V|_{X^{\circ}}\xrightarrow{\sim}W(P)|_{X^{\circ}}\,\cong\,W|_{X^{\circ}}\,\cong\,S|_{X^{\circ}}\oplus L|_{X^{\circ}}.

With respect to this splitting, the section
\theta|_{X^{\circ}}
decomposes as

\theta|_{X^{\circ}}\,=\,\Theta|_{X^{\circ}}+\Theta^{\prime}|_{X^{\circ}},

with
\Theta|_{X^{\circ}}\in H^{0}(X^{\circ},\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes S)
and

\Theta^{\prime}|_{X^{\circ}}\in H^{0}(X^{\circ},\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes L)
.

Under the canonical identification
\wedge^{2}(S\oplus L)\,\cong\,S\otimes L
, the relation
\theta\wedge\theta\,=\,0
becomes

\Theta\wedge\Theta^{\prime}+\Theta^{\prime}\wedge\Theta\,=\,0\qquad\text{on }X^{\circ}.

Locally, choose frames
s
of
S
and
\ell
of
L
, and write

\Theta\,=\,A\otimes s,\qquad\Theta^{\prime}\,=\,B\otimes\ell

with
A,B\in\operatorname{End}(E)
. Then

\theta\wedge\theta\,=\,(AB-BA)\otimes(s\wedge\ell),

so
\theta\wedge\theta\,=\,0
is equivalent to
AB\,=\,BA
. This is precisely the identity

(\Theta^{\prime}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{S})\circ\Theta\,=\,(\Theta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{L})\circ\Theta^{\prime}

on
X^{\circ}
. Both sides extend to meromorphic homomorphisms

E\longrightarrow E\otimes S(P)\otimes L(P),

and they agree on the dense open subset
X^{\circ}
. Hence they agree on all of
X
.
∎

3.
Reconstruction from logarithmic line–twisted fields

We continue with the fixed logarithmic Hecke presentation

0\longrightarrow V\xrightarrow{\ \Phi\ }W(P)\xrightarrow{\ \xi\ }T_{\mathrm{tot}}\longrightarrow 0,\qquad W\,=\,S\oplus L,

and write
D_{\mathrm{tot}}\,:=\,D\cup P
. This section solves the reconstruction problem: given a pair of logarithmic line–twisted fields, determine when it comes from a section of
\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes V
and when the resulting lift is integrable.

For each
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
and each line bundle
M\in\{S,L\}
, define a linear map

\beta_{a}^{M}:M(P)_{a}\longrightarrow M_{a}

by the rule

•

if
a\in D
, then
a\notin P
and
M(P)_{a}\,\simeq\,M_{a}
canonically; in this case
\beta_{a}^{M}
is this identification;

•

if
a\,=\,p\in P
, then
\beta_{p}^{M}
is the principal-part map from (
2.1
).

Thus
\beta_{a}^{M}
is an evaluation map at points of
D
and a residue map at points of
P
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle on
X
, and let

\Theta\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes S(P)\bigr),\qquad\Theta^{\prime}\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes L(P)\bigr)

be logarithmic line–twisted fields. For each
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
, we write

\Theta_{a}:E_{a}\,\longrightarrow\,E_{a}\otimes S(P)_{a},\qquad\Theta^{\prime}_{a}:E_{a}\,\longrightarrow\,E_{a}\otimes L(P)_{a}

for their fiber values.

Definition 3.1

(Local logarithmic Hecke constraint)
.

For each
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
, define

\mathsf{C}_{1,a}(\Theta)\,:=\,(\mathrm{id}_{E_{a}}\otimes\xi_{1,a})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{E_{a}}\otimes\beta_{a}^{S})\circ\Theta_{a},

\mathsf{C}_{2,a}(\Theta^{\prime})\,:=\,(\mathrm{id}_{E_{a}}\otimes\xi_{2,a})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{E_{a}}\otimes\beta_{a}^{L})\circ\Theta^{\prime}_{a},

and

\mathsf{C}_{a}(\Theta,\,\Theta^{\prime})\,:=\,\mathsf{C}_{1,a}(\Theta)+\mathsf{C}_{2,a}(\Theta^{\prime})\in\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(E_{a},E_{a}\otimes(T_{\mathrm{tot}})_{a}\bigr).

We say that
(\Theta,\,\Theta^{\prime})
satisfies the
local logarithmic Hecke constraint at
a
if

(3.1)

\mathsf{C}_{a}(\Theta,\,\Theta^{\prime})\,=\,0.

At a puncture, this condition has a concrete residue interpretation. If
p\in P
, then
\beta_{p}^{S}
and
\beta_{p}^{L}
are residue maps, so we may identify

(\mathrm{id}_{E_{p}}\otimes\beta_{p}^{S})\Theta_{p}\,=\,\operatorname{Res}_{p}(\Theta),\qquad(\mathrm{id}_{E_{p}}\otimes\beta_{p}^{L})\Theta^{\prime}_{p}\,=\,\operatorname{Res}_{p}(\Theta^{\prime}).

Since
\xi_{p}:W_{p}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow}Q_{p}\,=\,W_{p}/\ell_{p}
has kernel
\ell_{p}
, the condition (
3.1
) at
p
is equivalent to the statement that the residue pair

\bigl(\operatorname{Res}_{p}(\Theta),\operatorname{Res}_{p}(\Theta^{\prime})\bigr)\in\operatorname{End}(E_{p})\otimes W_{p}

lies in the distinguished line

\operatorname{End}(E_{p})\otimes\ell_{p}\subset\operatorname{End}(E_{p})\otimes W_{p}.

The next theorem is the basic reconstruction statement.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle on
X
, and let

\Theta\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes S(P)\bigr),\qquad\Theta^{\prime}\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes L(P)\bigr)

be a pair of logarithmic line–twisted fields.

(a)

(lifting)
There exists a unique section

\theta\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes V\bigr)

inducing
(\Theta,\,\Theta^{\prime})
via (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) if and only if the local logarithmic Hecke constraints (
3.1
) hold for every
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
.

(b)

(integrability)
Under the equivalent conditions in
(a)
, the lift
\theta
is a
V
-twisted Higgs field, that is,
\theta\wedge\theta\,=\,0
, if and only if the commutativity relation (
2.9
) holds.

Proof.

For part (a), tensor the exact sequence (
2.2
) with
\operatorname{End}(E)
. Since
\operatorname{End}(E)
is locally free, we obtain a short exact sequence of coherent sheaves

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes V\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes W(P)\xrightarrow{\ \Psi\ }\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes T_{\mathrm{tot}}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\Psi\,:=\,\mathrm{id}_{\operatorname{End}(E)}\otimes\xi
. Under the decomposition

\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes W(P)\,\cong\,\bigl(\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes S(P)\bigr)\oplus\bigl(\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes L(P)\bigr),

set

s\,:=\,\Theta\oplus\Theta^{\prime}\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes W(P)\bigr).

By left exactness of global sections, a lift

\theta\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes V\bigr)

with image
s
exists if and only if
s\in\ker H^{0}(\Psi)
, that is, if and only if

\Psi(s)\,=\,0\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes T_{\mathrm{tot}}\bigr).

Such a lift is unique because the map on global sections induced by
\Phi
is injective.

Since
T_{\mathrm{tot}}
is supported on the finite set
D_{\mathrm{tot}}
, one has a canonical decomposition

H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes T_{\mathrm{tot}}\bigr)\,=\,\bigoplus_{a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}}\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(E_{a},E_{a}\otimes(T_{\mathrm{tot}})_{a}\bigr).

Under this identification, the
a
-component of
\Psi(s)
is exactly

\mathsf{C}_{1,a}(\Theta)+\mathsf{C}_{2,a}(\Theta^{\prime})\,=\,\mathsf{C}_{a}(\Theta,\,\Theta^{\prime}).

Therefore
\Psi(s)\,=\,0
if and only if (
3.1
) holds for every
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
. This proves (a).

For part (b), assume that the equivalent conditions in (a) hold, and let
\theta
be the unique lift. Set

X^{\circ}\,:=\,X\setminus D_{\mathrm{tot}}.

Since
T_{\mathrm{tot}}|_{X^{\circ}}\,=\,0
, the sequence (
2.2
) restricts over
X^{\circ}
to an isomorphism

\Phi|_{X^{\circ}}:V|_{X^{\circ}}\xrightarrow{\sim}W(P)|_{X^{\circ}}\,\cong\,W|_{X^{\circ}}\,\cong\,S|_{X^{\circ}}\oplus L|_{X^{\circ}}.

Under this identification,
\theta|_{X^{\circ}}
corresponds to

\Theta|_{X^{\circ}}\oplus\Theta^{\prime}|_{X^{\circ}}.

By the local computation used in the proof of Lemma
2.4
, the vanishing of
\theta\wedge\theta
on
X^{\circ}
is equivalent to the commutativity relation (
2.9
) on
X^{\circ}
.

Now
\theta\wedge\theta
is a holomorphic section of

\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes\wedge^{2}V,

and the difference of the two sides of (
2.9
) is a holomorphic section of

\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(E,E\otimes S(P)\otimes L(P)\bigr).

A holomorphic section on the smooth irreducible curve
X
vanishes identically if and only if it vanishes on the dense open subset
X^{\circ}
. Hence
\theta\wedge\theta\,=\,0
on
X
if and only if (
2.9
) holds on
X
. This proves (b).
∎

The local constraint admits a more explicit form when the slope map is defined. Assume that for some
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
both maps in (
2.4
) are nonzero, so that the slope map

\rho^{a}:S_{a}\longrightarrow L_{a}

is defined by (
2.5
). Since
\xi_{2,a}
is then an isomorphism and

\rho^{a}\,=\,-\,\xi_{2,a}^{-1}\circ\xi_{1,a},

the condition (
3.1
) is equivalent to

(3.2)

(\mathrm{id}_{E_{a}}\otimes\beta_{a}^{L})\circ\Theta^{\prime}_{a}\,=\,(\mathrm{id}_{E_{a}}\otimes\rho^{a})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{E_{a}}\otimes\beta_{a}^{S})\circ\Theta_{a}

in
\operatorname{Hom}(E_{a},\,E_{a}\otimes L_{a})
. This pointwise form is the version used in Section
4
.

4.
Logarithmic compactified spectral correspondence

Fix the logarithmic Hecke presentation

0\longrightarrow V\xrightarrow{\ \Phi\ }W(P)\xrightarrow{\ \xi\ }T_{\mathrm{tot}}\longrightarrow 0,\qquad W=S\oplus L,

from Section
2
, and let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle on
X
.
Let

\Theta\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes S(P)\bigr),\qquad\Theta^{\prime}\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes L(P)\bigr)

be logarithmic line–twisted fields.

4.1.
The first field and its compactified spectral curve

Set

X_{0}\,:=\,X\setminus P,\qquad\Theta_{0}\,:=\,\Theta|_{X_{0}}\in H^{0}\bigl(X_{0},\,\operatorname{End}(E|_{X_{0}})\otimes S|_{X_{0}}\bigr).

Let

\pi_{0}:\operatorname{Tot}\bigl(S|_{X_{0}}\bigr)\longrightarrow X_{0}

be the bundle projection, and let
t_{0}
be the tautological section of
\pi_{0}^{*}S
. The spectral curve of
\Theta_{0}
is the closed subscheme

Y_{0}\subset\operatorname{Tot}\bigl(S|_{X_{0}}\bigr)

defined by

\det\bigl(t_{0}-\pi_{0}^{*}\Theta_{0}\bigr)=0.

Next let

\pi:\operatorname{Tot}\bigl(S(P)\bigr)\longrightarrow X

be the total space of
S(P)
, with tautological section

t\in H^{0}\bigl(\operatorname{Tot}(S(P)),\,\pi^{*}S(P)\bigr).

We define the compactified spectral curve of
\Theta
to be the closed subscheme

Y\subset\operatorname{Tot}\bigl(S(P)\bigr)

cut out by

(4.1)

\det\bigl(t-\pi^{*}\Theta\bigr)=0.

Since the defining equation is monic in the fiber coordinate,
Y\longrightarrow X
is finite of degree
\operatorname{rk}(E)
, and

Y\times_{X}X_{0}=Y_{0}.

Let

q:\mathbb{P}_{S}\,:=\,\mathbb{P}\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus S(P)^{-1}\bigr)\longrightarrow X

be the projective completion of
\operatorname{Tot}(S(P))
, and let
\Sigma_{\infty}\subset\mathbb{P}_{S}
be the infinity section. Write

\overline{Y}\subset\mathbb{P}_{S}

for the scheme-theoretic closure of
Y_{0}
. The homogenization of (
4.1
) is again monic in the affine fiber coordinate, hence does not vanish along
\Sigma_{\infty}
. Therefore

\overline{Y}\cap\Sigma_{\infty}=\varnothing,

so
\overline{Y}
is contained in the affine chart
\operatorname{Tot}(S(P))
and coincides there with
Y
. We write

\varphi\,:=\,q|_{\overline{Y}}=\pi|_{Y}:\overline{Y}\longrightarrow X.

Assume from now on that
\overline{Y}
is integral. The ordinary compactified Beauville–Narasimhan–Ramanan correspondence for the line-bundle twist
S(P)
gives a bijection between

(a)

pairs
(E,\,\Theta)
whose compactified spectral curve is
\overline{Y}
, and

(b)

rank-one torsion-free sheaves
\mathcal{F}
on
\overline{Y}
,

such that

E\,\cong\,\varphi_{*}\mathcal{F},

and under this identification
\Theta
is induced by multiplication by the tautological section
t
.

Definition 4.1
.

Assume that
\overline{Y}
is integral. A
compactified spectral sheaf
for
(E,\,\Theta)
is a rank-one torsion-free sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
\overline{Y}
such that
E\,\cong\,\varphi_{*}\mathcal{F}
and
\Theta
is induced by multiplication by
t
.

The second field is encoded spectrally by
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}
-linear endomorphisms of
\mathcal{F}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
\overline{Y}
is integral, and let
\mathcal{F}
be the compactified spectral sheaf of
(E,\,\Theta)
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

the fields
\Theta
and
\Theta^{\prime}
satisfy the commutativity relation (
2.9
);

(ii)

under the identification
E=\varphi_{*}\mathcal{F}
, the field
\Theta^{\prime}
is induced by an
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}
-linear morphism

\vartheta:\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\otimes\varphi^{*}L(P).

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is finite, adjunction and the projection formula give canonical identifications

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}}\!\bigl(\mathcal{F},\,\mathcal{F}\otimes\varphi^{*}L(P)\bigr)\,\cong\,\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\!\bigl(\varphi_{*}\mathcal{F},\,\varphi_{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes L(P)\bigr)\,\cong\,H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes L(P)\bigr).

Under these identifications, an
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}
-linear morphism
\vartheta
corresponds to an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear morphism

\Theta^{\prime}:E\longrightarrow E\otimes L(P)

which commutes with the
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}
-action on
\varphi_{*}\mathcal{F}
. Since
\overline{Y}\subset\operatorname{Tot}(S(P))
is generated over
X
by the tautological section
t
, this is equivalent to commuting with multiplication by
t
, i.e. with
\Theta
. This is precisely (
2.9
).
∎

4.2.
The marked spectral scheme and the intrinsic condition

The punctured problem is controlled not only by
\overline{Y}
, but also by its finite subscheme above the marked divisor.

Definition 4.3
.

Let

Z\,:=\,\overline{Y}\times_{X}D_{\mathrm{tot}},\qquad Z_{D}\,:=\,\overline{Y}\times_{X}D,\qquad Z_{P}\,:=\,\overline{Y}\times_{X}P.

Then
Z
is a finite scheme over
D_{\mathrm{tot}}
, and since
D\cap P=\varnothing
,

Z=Z_{D}\sqcup Z_{P}.

We call
Z
the
marked spectral scheme
attached to
\Theta
.

Let

\psi:Z\longrightarrow D_{\mathrm{tot}}

be the structure morphism. Pulling back
\xi
along
\psi
gives a surjective morphism

(4.2)

\xi_{Z}:\varphi^{*}W(P)|_{Z}=\psi^{*}\bigl(W(P)|_{D_{\mathrm{tot}}}\bigr)\longrightarrow\psi^{*}T_{\mathrm{tot}}=\varphi^{*}T_{\mathrm{tot}}|_{Z}.

For
M\in\{S,L,W\}
, let

\beta_{Z}^{M}:\varphi^{*}M(P)|_{Z}\longrightarrow\varphi^{*}M|_{Z}

be the morphism obtained componentwise from the maps
\beta_{a}^{M}
of Section
3
: it is the identity over
Z_{D}
and the principal-part map over
Z_{P}
. By construction,
\xi_{Z}
factors as

(4.3)

\varphi^{*}W(P)|_{Z}\xrightarrow{\ \beta_{Z}^{W}\ }\varphi^{*}W|_{Z}\xrightarrow{\ \overline{\xi}_{Z}\ }\varphi^{*}T_{\mathrm{tot}}|_{Z},

where
\overline{\xi}_{Z}
is induced by the fiber maps

\xi_{a}:S_{a}\oplus L_{a}\longrightarrow(T_{\mathrm{tot}})_{a},\qquad a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}.

Now assume that
\overline{Y}
is integral, let
\mathcal{F}
be the compactified spectral sheaf of
(E,\,\Theta)
, and suppose that
\Theta^{\prime}
satisfies (
2.9
). Let

\vartheta:\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\otimes\varphi^{*}L(P)

be the corresponding
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}
-linear morphism from Lemma
4.2
. Together with multiplication by the tautological section,

m_{t}:\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\otimes\varphi^{*}S(P),

it defines an
\mathcal{O}_{Z}
-linear morphism

(4.4)

\Gamma_{Z}(\vartheta)\,:=\,(m_{t},\,\vartheta)|_{Z}:\mathcal{F}|_{Z}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}|_{Z}\otimes\varphi^{*}W(P)|_{Z}.

Definition 4.4
.

We say that
\vartheta
satisfies the
marked spectral condition
if

(4.5)

(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}|_{Z}}\otimes\xi_{Z})\circ\Gamma_{Z}(\vartheta)=0

as a morphism

\mathcal{F}|_{Z}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}|_{Z}\otimes\varphi^{*}T_{\mathrm{tot}}|_{Z}.

This is the intrinsic spectral form of the local logarithmic Hecke constraints.

Proposition 4.5
.

Assume that
\overline{Y}
is integral. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the compactified spectral sheaf of
(E,\,\Theta)
, and let

\vartheta:\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\otimes\varphi^{*}L(P)

be the
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}
-linear morphism corresponding to
\Theta^{\prime}
via Lemma
4.2
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

the pair
(\Theta,\,\Theta^{\prime})
satisfies the local logarithmic Hecke constraints (
3.1
) for every
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
;

(ii)

\vartheta
satisfies the marked spectral condition (
4.5
).

Proof.

For each
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
, set

Z_{a}\,:=\,\overline{Y}\times_{X}\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(a)=\varphi^{-1}(a)\subset Z.

Since
\varphi
is finite, base change for coherent sheaves gives canonical identifications

E_{a}=(\varphi_{*}\mathcal{F})\otimes\kappa(a)\,\cong\,H^{0}\bigl(Z_{a},\,\mathcal{F}|_{Z_{a}}\bigr),

E_{a}\otimes W(P)_{a}\,\cong\,H^{0}\bigl(Z_{a},\,\mathcal{F}|_{Z_{a}}\otimes\varphi^{*}W(P)|_{Z_{a}}\bigr),

E_{a}\otimes(T_{\mathrm{tot}})_{a}\,\cong\,H^{0}\bigl(Z_{a},\,\mathcal{F}|_{Z_{a}}\otimes\varphi^{*}T_{\mathrm{tot}}|_{Z_{a}}\bigr).

Under these identifications, multiplication by
t
induces
\Theta_{a}
, the morphism
\vartheta
induces
\Theta^{\prime}_{a}
, and

\bigl((\mathrm{id}\otimes\xi_{Z})\circ\Gamma_{Z}(\vartheta)\bigr)|_{Z_{a}}

induces the map

\mathsf{C}_{a}(\Theta,\,\Theta^{\prime}):E_{a}\longrightarrow E_{a}\otimes(T_{\mathrm{tot}})_{a}

from Definition
3.1
.

Since
Z_{a}
is finite, it is affine. A morphism of quasi-coherent sheaves on an affine scheme is zero if and only if the induced map on global sections is zero. Therefore

\mathsf{C}_{a}(\Theta,\,\Theta^{\prime})=0\iff\bigl((\mathrm{id}\otimes\xi_{Z})\circ\Gamma_{Z}(\vartheta)\bigr)|_{Z_{a}}=0.

Finally,
Z=\coprod_{a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}}Z_{a}
, so vanishing on all
Z_{a}
is equivalent to (
4.5
).
∎

4.3.
Residual tautological data and the graph form

Assume now that the slope map

\rho^{a}:S_{a}\longrightarrow L_{a}

is defined for every
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
; equivalently,
\ker(\xi_{a})\subset S_{a}\oplus L_{a}
is the graph of
\rho^{a}
for every marked point. Pulling back the collection
\{\rho^{a}\}_{a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}}
along
\psi:Z\longrightarrow D_{\mathrm{tot}}
gives a morphism

\rho_{Z}:\varphi^{*}S|_{Z}\longrightarrow\varphi^{*}L|_{Z}.

Definition 4.6
.

The
residual tautological section
is defined as

\lambda_{Z}\,:=\,\beta_{Z}^{S}\bigl(t|_{Z}\bigr)\in H^{0}\bigl(Z,\,\varphi^{*}S|_{Z}\bigr).

We set

b_{Z}\,:=\,\rho_{Z}(\lambda_{Z})\in H^{0}\bigl(Z,\,\varphi^{*}L|_{Z}\bigr).

If

\vartheta:\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\otimes\varphi^{*}L(P)

is
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}
-linear, define

(4.6)

\vartheta_{Z}\,:=\,(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}|_{Z}}\otimes\beta_{Z}^{L})\circ\vartheta|_{Z}:\mathcal{F}|_{Z}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}|_{Z}\otimes\varphi^{*}L|_{Z}.

Since
b_{Z}
is a section of
\varphi^{*}L|_{Z}
, multiplication by
b_{Z}
defines an
\mathcal{O}_{Z}
-linear morphism

m_{b_{Z}}:\mathcal{F}|_{Z}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}|_{Z}\otimes\varphi^{*}L|_{Z}.

Proposition 4.7
.

Assume that
\overline{Y}
is integral and that the slope maps
\rho^{a}
are defined for all
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
. Then the marked spectral condition (
4.5
) is equivalent to the equality

(4.7)

\vartheta_{Z}=m_{b_{Z}}

as morphisms

\mathcal{F}|_{Z}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}|_{Z}\otimes\varphi^{*}L|_{Z}.

Proof.

Using the factorization (
4.3
), condition (
4.5
) is equivalent to

(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}|_{Z}}\otimes\overline{\xi}_{Z})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}|_{Z}}\otimes\beta_{Z}^{W})\circ\Gamma_{Z}(\vartheta)=0.

By definition of
\Gamma_{Z}(\vartheta)
, of
\beta_{Z}^{S}
, and of
\vartheta_{Z}
, this may be rewritten as

(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}|_{Z}}\otimes\overline{\xi}_{Z})\circ(m_{\lambda_{Z}},\,\vartheta_{Z})=0.

Restricting to
Z_{a}
, the kernel of
\overline{\xi}_{Z}|_{Z_{a}}
is

\mathcal{F}|_{Z_{a}}\otimes\ker(\xi_{a})=\mathcal{F}|_{Z_{a}}\otimes\ell_{a}.

Since
\ell_{a}\subset S_{a}\oplus L_{a}
is the graph of
\rho^{a}
, the preceding vanishing condition on
Z_{a}
is equivalent to

\vartheta_{Z}|_{Z_{a}}=m_{\rho^{a}(\lambda_{Z}|_{Z_{a}})}.

By definition of
b_{Z}
, this is exactly

\vartheta_{Z}|_{Z_{a}}=m_{b_{Z}}|_{Z_{a}}.

Since
Z=\coprod_{a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}}Z_{a}
, the claim follows.
∎

4.4.
The logarithmic spectral correspondence

We can now state the spectral classification in its intrinsic form.

Theorem 4.8
.

Assume that
\overline{Y}
is integral. Then there is a natural bijection between the isomorphism classes of:

(I)

logarithmic
V
-twisted Higgs bundles

(E,\theta),\qquad\theta\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes V\bigr),\qquad\theta\wedge\theta=0,

such that the associated logarithmic
S
-twisted field
\Theta
has compactified spectral curve
\overline{Y}
;

(II)

pairs
(\mathcal{F},\,\vartheta)
, where
\mathcal{F}
is a rank-one torsion-free sheaf on
\overline{Y}
and

\vartheta:\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\otimes\varphi^{*}L(P)

is an
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}
-linear morphism satisfying the marked spectral condition (
4.5
).

Under the correspondence,

E=\varphi_{*}\mathcal{F},

the field
\Theta
is induced by multiplication by
t
, the field
\Theta^{\prime}
is induced by
\vartheta
, and
\theta
is the unique logarithmic
V
-twisted Higgs field lifting
(\Theta,\,\Theta^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
(E,\theta)
be an object of (I), and let
(\Theta,\,\Theta^{\prime})
be the induced logarithmic line–twisted fields. Since
\overline{Y}
is the compactified spectral curve of
\Theta
, the compactified BNR correspondence gives a rank-one torsion-free sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
\overline{Y}
such that

E\,\cong\,\varphi_{*}\mathcal{F},

with
\Theta
induced by multiplication by
t
. Because
\theta\wedge\theta=0
, Theorem
3.2
(b) implies (
2.9
); hence Lemma
4.2
gives an
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}
-linear morphism

\vartheta:\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\otimes\varphi^{*}L(P)

inducing
\Theta^{\prime}
. Finally, Theorem
3.2
(a) and Proposition
4.5
show that
\vartheta
satisfies (
4.5
). Thus
(\mathcal{F},\,\vartheta)
is of type (II).

Conversely, let
(\mathcal{F},\,\vartheta)
be an object of (II), and set

E\,:=\,\varphi_{*}\mathcal{F}.

Multiplication by
t
defines a logarithmic
S
-twisted field

\Theta:E\longrightarrow E\otimes S(P),

and
\vartheta
defines a logarithmic
L
-twisted field

\Theta^{\prime}:E\longrightarrow E\otimes L(P).

By construction,
\Theta^{\prime}
commutes with
\Theta
, so (
2.9
) holds. By Proposition
4.5
, the pair
(\Theta,\,\Theta^{\prime})
satisfies the local logarithmic Hecke constraints. Hence Theorem
3.2
produces a unique section

\theta\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes V\bigr)

lifting
(\Theta,\,\Theta^{\prime})
, and this lift is integrable. This gives an object of (I). The two constructions are inverse to one another.
∎

4.5.
Reduced fibers and the scalar form of the condition

The intrinsic condition on
Z
is the natural one. For the line-bundle locus studied in Section
5
, we also need its pointwise form under the additional hypotheses used there.

For
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
and a closed point
y\in\varphi^{-1}(a)
, define

\lambda_{a}(y)\,:=\,\beta_{a}^{S}\bigl(t(y)\bigr)\in S_{a}.

If
a=x\in D
, this is simply the value of the tautological section at
y
. If
a=p\in P
, it is the residual value of the tautological coordinate at
y
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Assume
\overline{Y}
is integral,
Z=\varphi^{-1}(D_{\mathrm{tot}})
is reduced, the slope maps
\rho^{a}
are defined for all
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
, and
\mathcal{F}
is locally free at every point of
Z
. Let

\vartheta:\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\otimes\varphi^{*}L(P)

be an
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}
-linear morphism. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\vartheta
satisfies the marked spectral condition (
4.5
);

(ii)

for every
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
and every closed point
y\in\varphi^{-1}(a)
, if

\mu_{y}\in L(P)_{a}

denotes the unique element corresponding to the induced map

\vartheta(y):\mathcal{F}(y)\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(y)\otimes L(P)_{a}

under the canonical identification

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}\bigl(\mathcal{F}(y),\,\mathcal{F}(y)\otimes L(P)_{a}\bigr)\,\cong\,L(P)_{a},

then

(4.8)

\beta_{a}^{L}(\mu_{y})=\rho^{a}\bigl(\lambda_{a}(y)\bigr)\in L_{a}.

Proof.

Because
Z
is reduced and finite over
X
, it is the disjoint union of its closed points. Since
\mathcal{F}
is locally free at every point of
Z
, each
\mathcal{F}(y)
is one-dimensional. By Proposition
4.7
, condition (i) is equivalent to

\vartheta_{Z}=m_{b_{Z}}.

Restricting to the component
y\in Z
, the left-hand side is multiplication by

\beta_{a}^{L}(\mu_{y})\in L_{a},\qquad a=\varphi(y),

while the right-hand side is multiplication by

b_{Z}(y)=\rho^{a}\bigl(\lambda_{a}(y)\bigr)\in L_{a}.

Therefore
\vartheta_{Z}=m_{b_{Z}}
is equivalent to (
4.8
) for every closed point
y\in Z
.
∎

We now record the pointwise version of the correspondence used in Section
5
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Assume that
\overline{Y}
is integral, that
Z=\varphi^{-1}(D_{\mathrm{tot}})
is reduced, and that the slope maps
\rho^{a}
are defined for all
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
. Then there is a natural bijection between the isomorphism classes of:

(I)

logarithmic
V
-twisted Higgs bundles

(E,\theta),\qquad\theta\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes V\bigr),\qquad\theta\wedge\theta=0,

such that the associated logarithmic
S
-twisted field
\Theta
has compactified spectral curve
\overline{Y}
, and the associated compactified spectral sheaf is locally free at every point of
Z
;

(II)

pairs
(\mathcal{F},\,\vartheta)
, where
\mathcal{F}
is a rank-one torsion-free sheaf on
\overline{Y}
, locally free at every point of
Z
, and

\vartheta:\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\otimes\varphi^{*}L(P)

is an
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}
-linear morphism satisfying (
4.8
) for every
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
and every closed point
y\in\varphi^{-1}(a)
.

Under the correspondence,

E=\varphi_{*}\mathcal{F},

the field
\Theta
is induced by multiplication by
t
, the field
\Theta^{\prime}
is induced by
\vartheta
, and
\theta
is the unique logarithmic
V
-twisted Higgs field lifting
(\Theta,\,\Theta^{\prime})
.

Proof.

This is immediate from Theorem
4.8
and Proposition
4.9
.
∎

Theorem
4.8
is the intrinsic spectral statement. The extra hypotheses in Proposition
4.9
and Theorem
4.10
are needed only to pass from the scheme-theoretic condition on
Z
to the pointwise scalar equations used later.

5.
Enhanced logarithmic Hitchin fibers on the line-bundle locus

Throughout this section we assume the hypotheses of Theorem
4.10
. Thus
\overline{Y}
is an integral compactified spectral curve, the marked spectral scheme

Z\,:=\,\varphi^{-1}(D_{\mathrm{tot}})

is reduced, and the slope maps
\rho^{a}
are defined for all
a\in D_{\mathrm{tot}}
. In this situation, Proposition
4.7
identifies the marked condition with the equality

(5.1)

\vartheta_{Z}=m_{b_{Z}}.

We now restrict to the line-bundle locus. The point of this section is that, once
(\overline{Y},Z,b_{Z})
is fixed, the admissible enhancement data are controlled by a single affine scheme independent of the chosen line bundle.

Set

M\,:=\,\varphi^{*}L(P),\qquad M_{Z}\,:=\,\varphi^{*}L|_{Z},\qquad V\,:=\,H^{0}(\overline{Y},M),\qquad V_{Z}\,:=\,H^{0}(Z,M_{Z}).

Restriction to
Z
, followed by the residual map
\beta_{Z}^{L}
, gives a linear map

(5.2)

\operatorname{evres}_{Z}:V\longrightarrow V_{Z},\qquad s\longmapsto\beta_{Z}^{L}(s|_{Z}).

Recall from Definition
4.6
that

b_{Z}\in V_{Z}

is the distinguished marked section.

5.1.
The fixed affine enhancement scheme

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a line bundle on
\overline{Y}
. An
enhancement
of
\mathcal{N}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}
-linear morphism

\vartheta:\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}\otimes M.

Since
\mathcal{N}
is invertible, there is a canonical identification

(5.3)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}}\!\bigl(\mathcal{N},\,\mathcal{N}\otimes M\bigr)\,\cong\,H^{0}\!\bigl(\overline{Y},M\bigr)=V.

Thus every enhancement of
\mathcal{N}
is given by multiplication by a unique section
s\in V
.

If
W
is a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space, write

\underline{W}\,:=\,\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}(W^{\vee})\bigr)

for the associated affine space. The map (
5.2
) induces a morphism of affine schemes

\underline{\operatorname{evres}}_{Z}:\underline{V}\longrightarrow\underline{V_{Z}}.

Definition 5.1
.

The
affine enhancement scheme
is

(5.4)

A_{Z}\,:=\,\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}\times_{\underline{V_{Z}}}\underline{V},

where
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}\longrightarrow\underline{V_{Z}}
is the
\mathbb{C}
-point corresponding to
b_{Z}
.

Equivalently,
A_{Z}
is the scheme-theoretic fiber of
\underline{\operatorname{evres}}_{Z}
over
b_{Z}
. In particular,
A_{Z}
depends only on
(\overline{Y},Z,b_{Z})
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For every
\mathbb{C}
-scheme
T
, there is a natural identification

A_{Z}(T)\,\cong\,\Bigl\{s\in V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Gamma(T,\,\mathcal{O}_{T})\;\Bigm|\;(\operatorname{evres}_{Z}\otimes 1)(s)=b_{Z}\otimes 1\Bigr\}.

Proof.

For any finite-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
W
, morphisms
T\longrightarrow\underline{W}
are the same as global sections of
W\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}_{T}
, i.e.

\operatorname{Hom}(T,\,\underline{W})\,\cong\,W\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Gamma(T,\,\mathcal{O}_{T}).

Applying this to
\underline{V}
and
\underline{V_{Z}}
, and then using the fiber-product description (
5.4
), gives the result.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a line bundle on
\overline{Y}
. Under the identification (
5.3
), the admissible enhancements

\vartheta:\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}\otimes M

are naturally identified with
A_{Z}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

Let
\vartheta
correspond to
s_{\vartheta}\in V
. Since
\mathcal{N}|_{Z}
is invertible, there is a canonical identification

\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{Z}}\!\bigl(\mathcal{N}|_{Z},\,\mathcal{N}|_{Z}\otimes M_{Z}\bigr)\,\cong\,M_{Z}.

Under this identification, the morphism

\vartheta_{Z}=(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{N}|_{Z}}\otimes\beta_{Z}^{L})\circ\vartheta|_{Z}

corresponds exactly to
\operatorname{evres}_{Z}(s_{\vartheta})\in V_{Z}
. Therefore

\vartheta_{Z}=m_{b_{Z}}\iff\operatorname{evres}_{Z}(s_{\vartheta})=b_{Z}.

By (
5.1
), the left-hand side is precisely the admissibility condition. Hence admissible enhancements are exactly the
\mathbb{C}
-points of
A_{Z}
.
∎

The next lemma identifies the translation space of
A_{Z}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
i_{Z}:Z\hookrightarrow\overline{Y}
be the closed immersion, and set

Z_{D}\,:=\,\varphi^{-1}(D),\qquad Z_{P}\,:=\,\varphi^{-1}(P).

Then there is a natural short exact sequence of coherent sheaves on
\overline{Y}

(5.5)

0\longrightarrow\varphi^{*}L(-Z_{D})\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{\ \eta_{Z}\ }{i_{Z}}_{*}(M_{Z})\longrightarrow 0,

whose induced map on global sections is
\operatorname{evres}_{Z}
. Consequently,

\ker(\operatorname{evres}_{Z})\,\cong\,H^{0}\bigl(\overline{Y},\,\varphi^{*}L(-Z_{D})\bigr).

In particular, if
A_{Z}\neq\varnothing
, then
A_{Z}
is a torsor under the additive group attached to
H^{0}(\overline{Y},\,\varphi^{*}L(-Z_{D}))
.

Proof.

Because
Z
is reduced and finite over the curve
X
, the schemes
Z_{D}
and
Z_{P}
are reduced effective divisors on the integral curve
\overline{Y}
, they are disjoint, and

Z=Z_{D}\sqcup Z_{P}.

Since
P
is reduced, one has

\varphi^{*}P=Z_{P},\qquad\text{hence}\qquad M=\varphi^{*}L(Z_{P}).

For any reduced effective divisor
E
on a curve and any line bundle
N
, there is an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow N(E)\longrightarrow{i_{E}}_{*}(N|_{E})\longrightarrow 0.

Applying this first with
N=\varphi^{*}L
and
E=Z_{P}
, and then with
N=\varphi^{*}L
and
E=Z_{D}
, one obtains

0\longrightarrow\varphi^{*}L\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow{i_{Z_{P}}}_{*}(\varphi^{*}L|_{Z_{P}})\longrightarrow 0

and

0\longrightarrow\varphi^{*}L(-Z_{D})\longrightarrow\varphi^{*}L\longrightarrow{i_{Z_{D}}}_{*}(\varphi^{*}L|_{Z_{D}})\longrightarrow 0.

Since
Z_{D}\cap Z_{P}=\varnothing
, these quotient maps combine to a surjection

\eta_{Z}:M\longrightarrow{i_{Z}}_{*}(M_{Z})\,\cong\,{i_{Z_{D}}}_{*}(\varphi^{*}L|_{Z_{D}})\oplus{i_{Z_{P}}}_{*}(\varphi^{*}L|_{Z_{P}}).

Its kernel consists exactly of those sections of
M
with zero principal part along
Z_{P}
and vanishing restriction along
Z_{D}
, namely
\varphi^{*}L(-Z_{D})
. This proves (
5.5
). On global sections,
\eta_{Z}
is precisely the map
\operatorname{evres}_{Z}
.

The final statement is immediate: every nonempty fiber of a linear map of affine spaces is a torsor under its kernel.
∎

5.2.
Families and functoriality

Let

p_{Y}:\overline{Y}\times T\longrightarrow\overline{Y},\qquad p_{T}:\overline{Y}\times T\longrightarrow T

be the projections.

Define
\mathfrak{H}^{\log,\,\mathrm{lb}}_{\overline{Y},d}
to be the category fibered in groupoids over
\mathbb{C}
-schemes whose objects over a scheme
T
are pairs
(\mathcal{N},\,\vartheta)
such that:

(i)

\mathcal{N}
is a line bundle on
\overline{Y}\times T
whose restriction to every geometric fiber of
p_{T}
has degree
d
;

(ii)

\vartheta:\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}\otimes p_{Y}^{*}M
is an
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}\times T}
-linear morphism whose restriction to every geometric fiber is admissible.

Morphisms are isomorphisms of line bundles intertwining the enhancement morphisms.

Let
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(\overline{Y})
denote the Picard stack of degree-
d
line bundles on
\overline{Y}
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
T
be a
\mathbb{C}
-scheme, and let
\mathcal{N}
be a line bundle on
\overline{Y}\times T
. Then:

(a)

relative enhancements

\vartheta:\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}\otimes p_{Y}^{*}M

are canonically equivalent to morphisms

s_{\vartheta}:T\longrightarrow\underline{V};

(b)

under this correspondence,
\vartheta
is admissible on every geometric fiber if and only if
s_{\vartheta}
factors through the closed subscheme

A_{Z}\hookrightarrow\underline{V}.

Both constructions are functorial in
T
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{N}
is invertible,

\mathcal{H}om\!\bigl(\mathcal{N},\,\mathcal{N}\otimes p_{Y}^{*}M\bigr)\,\cong\,p_{Y}^{*}M.

Because
\overline{Y}
is proper over
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
, flat base change gives

p_{T*}(p_{Y}^{*}M)\,\cong\,H^{0}(\overline{Y},M)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}_{T}=V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}_{T}.

Therefore

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}\times T}}\!\bigl(\mathcal{N},\,\mathcal{N}\otimes p_{Y}^{*}M\bigr)\,\cong\,\Gamma(T,V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}_{T})\,\cong\,\operatorname{Hom}(T,\,\underline{V}),

which proves (a).

Now let
s_{\vartheta}:T\longrightarrow\underline{V}
be the morphism corresponding to
\vartheta
. By Lemma
5.2
, the factorization of
s_{\vartheta}
through
A_{Z}
is equivalent to the equality

(\operatorname{evres}_{Z}\otimes 1)(s_{\vartheta})=b_{Z}\otimes 1

in
V_{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Gamma(T,\,\mathcal{O}_{T})
. Pulling back to a geometric point
t\longrightarrow T
, this becomes precisely the condition that the fiber enhancement
\vartheta_{t}
satisfy

(\vartheta_{t})_{Z}=m_{b_{Z}}.

By (
5.1
), that is the admissibility condition on the fiber over
t
. Hence (b) follows.
∎

The key point is that the admissibility condition is cut out by the fixed closed subscheme
A_{Z}\subset\underline{V}
, independently of the chosen line bundle.

Theorem 5.6
.

Viewing
A_{Z}
as a category fibered in groupoids through its functor of points, there is a canonical equivalence of categories fibered in groupoids

(5.6)

\mathfrak{H}^{\log,\,\mathrm{lb}}_{\overline{Y},d}\;\simeq\;\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(\overline{Y})\times A_{Z}.

Under this equivalence, the forgetful morphism

\mathfrak{H}^{\log,\,\mathrm{lb}}_{\overline{Y},d}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(\overline{Y}),\qquad(\mathcal{N},\,\vartheta)\longmapsto\mathcal{N},

identifies with the projection to the first factor.

Proof.

For a
T
-point
(\mathcal{N},\,\vartheta)
of
\mathfrak{H}^{\log,\,\mathrm{lb}}_{\overline{Y},d}
, let

s_{\vartheta}:T\longrightarrow\underline{V}

be the morphism corresponding to
\vartheta
under Proposition
5.5
(a). By Proposition
5.5
(b), the fiberwise admissibility of
\vartheta
is equivalent to the factorization of
s_{\vartheta}
through
A_{Z}
. Hence we obtain a functor

\mathfrak{F}:\mathfrak{H}^{\log,\,\mathrm{lb}}_{\overline{Y},d}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(\overline{Y})\times A_{Z},\qquad(\mathcal{N},\,\vartheta)\longmapsto(\mathcal{N},s_{\vartheta}).

Conversely, given a
T
-point
(\mathcal{N},s)
of
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(\overline{Y})\times A_{Z}
, compose
s
with the closed immersion
A_{Z}\hookrightarrow\underline{V}
. Proposition
5.5
(a) then gives a unique relative enhancement

\vartheta_{s}:\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}\otimes p_{Y}^{*}M,

and Proposition
5.5
(b) shows that
\vartheta_{s}
is admissible on every geometric fiber. Thus
(\mathcal{N},\,\vartheta_{s})
defines an object of
\mathfrak{H}^{\log,\,\mathrm{lb}}_{\overline{Y},d}(T)
. This gives a functor

\mathfrak{G}:\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(\overline{Y})\times A_{Z}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{H}^{\log,\,\mathrm{lb}}_{\overline{Y},d}.

The constructions
\mathfrak{F}
and
\mathfrak{G}
are functorial in
T
, and they are inverse to one another by construction. This proves (
5.6
). The final compatibility with the forgetful morphism is immediate.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

The category fibered in groupoids
\mathfrak{H}^{\log,\,\mathrm{lb}}_{\overline{Y},d}
is an algebraic stack. Moreover, the equivalence (
5.6
) upgrades canonically to an equivalence of algebraic stacks

\mathfrak{H}^{\log,\,\mathrm{lb}}_{\overline{Y},d}\;\simeq\;\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(\overline{Y})\times A_{Z}.

Proof.

The Picard stack
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(\overline{Y})
is algebraic, and
A_{Z}
is an affine
\mathbb{C}
-scheme. Hence
\mathbf{Pic}^{d}(\overline{Y})\times A_{Z}
is an algebraic stack. The claim follows from Theorem
5.6
.
∎
