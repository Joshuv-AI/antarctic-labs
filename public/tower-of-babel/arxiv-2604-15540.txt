Accessible Quantum Correlations Under Complexity Constraints
Álvaro Yángüez, Noam Avidan, Jan Kochanowski, Thomas A. Hahn

Abstract
Quantum systems may contain underlying correlations which are inaccessible to computationally bounded observers. We capture this distinction through a framework that analyses bipartite states only using efficiently implementable quantum channels. This leads to a complexity-constrained max-divergence and a corresponding computational min-entropy. The latter quantity recovers the standard operational meaning of the conditional min-entropy: in the fully quantum case, it quantifies the largest overlap with a maximally entangled state attainable via efficient operations on the conditional subsystem. For classical-quantum states, it further reduces to the optimal guessing probability of a computationally bounded observer with access to side information. Lastly, in the absence of side information, the computational min-entropy simplifies to a computational notion of the operator norm. We then establish strong separations between the information-theoretic and complexity-constrained notions of min-entropy. For pure states, there exist highly entangled families of states with extremal min-entropy whose efficiently accessible entanglement in terms of computational min-entropy is exponentially suppressed. For mixed states, the separation is even sharper: the information-theoretic conditional min-entropy can be highly negative while the complexity-constrained quantity remains nearly maximal. Overall, our results demonstrate that computational constraints can fundamentally limit the quantum correlations that are observable in practice.



I
Efficiently accessible quantum correlations

Quantum theory permits extraordinarily rich correlations, yet only a restricted subset can be realized in practice. As quantum systems grow, the spaces of states, measurements, and transformations expand far more rapidly than the operations available under realistic spatial and temporal constraints. This gap is especially consequential when correlations are viewed as a resource: in the fully quantum setting, it limits the entanglement which can be distilled by feasible operations, while in the classical-quantum setting it limits the utility of quantum side information for predicting classical information
Konig et al. 2009
. The physically relevant question is therefore not only which correlations a state contains, but which of them can be turned into observable consequences by feasible operations.

This mismatch becomes especially pronounced in large many-body systems. In quantum many-body physics, for example, observation and control are typically limited to local measurements, low-complexity dynamics, and variational ansätze. Among the approaches shaped by these restrictions are tensor-network methods
Eisert et al. 2010
;
Schollwöck 2011
;
Orús 2014
. In such regimes, complexity is not merely a practical overhead: it shapes which non-classical features can actually be detected, manipulated, or certified at scale
Bouland et al. 2024
;
Feng and Ippoliti 2025
;
Bansal et al. 2025
. Related questions also arise in high-energy physics, where entanglement is central to the AdS/CFT correspondence
Ryu and Takayanagi 2006
, yet may not by itself determine the full dual spacetime description
Ryu and Takayanagi 2006
;
Susskind 2016
;
Arnon-Friedman et al. 2023
;
Cheng et al. 2025
. Taken together, these settings suggest that, in large quantum systems, the central question is not simply how much entanglement or side information a state contains in principle, but how much of it remains detectable, certifiable, or exploitable under efficiently implementable operations, as represented in
.

Motivated by this distinction, we develop a channel-based framework for quantum information under computational restrictions and use it to identify the part of quantum correlations that remains operationally accessible to efficient observers. Comparing bipartite states only through efficiently implementable quantum channels induces a restricted order relation on quantum states, from which natural complexity-constrained versions of the max-divergence and the min-entropy emerge. Importantly, the latter retains the operational interpretation of the conditional min-entropy
Konig et al. 2009
: in the fully quantum setting, it quantifies the largest overlap with a maximally entangled state attainable by an efficient channel acting on the conditioning system. For classical-quantum states, it reduces to the optimal probability of inferring a classical variable from quantum side information using efficient measurements. When the conditioning system is trivial, it reduces to a single-system entropy determined by a computational analogue of the operator norm. In this way, our framework unifies recently defined computational conditional entropies
Avidan and Arnon 2026
;
Avidan et al. 2025
with the efficient-measurement formalism of
Yángüez et al. 2026
. It extends the latter from measurements to general quantum channels, recovers the former in both the fully quantum and classical-quantum settings, and includes the binary efficient-effect model of
Yángüez et al. 2026
as a special case. Within this same framework, we then prove strong separations between information-theoretic and computationally accessible correlations.

These separations already appear if one simply considers pure states. For polynomially many copies, an adaptation of universal entanglement concentration
Matsumoto and Hayashi 2007
shows that feasible one-shot operations can still certify a nontrivial logarithmic amount of entanglement, and our converse bound shows that this scaling is essentially optimal. Thus, even pure states that are information-theoretically close to a maximally entangled state can retain only a logarithmic amount of entanglement within efficient reach. When considering mixed states, the same techniques yield an even stronger and nearly maximal separation. There exist families of states whose information-theoretic conditional min-entropy remains highly negative, while the complexity-constrained conditional min-entropy is, up to negligible corrections, as large as the information-theoretic min-entropy of an uncorrelated, maximally mixed state. Computational complexity constraints can therefore induce an almost maximal separation between information-theoretic and computational notions of conditional min-entropy: entanglement and side information may be extensively present in principle, yet become nearly operationally inaccessible to any efficient observer.

Recent works have shown that computational restrictions can substantially reshape familiar notions from quantum information theory. In entanglement theory, one-shot computational entanglement cost and distillable entanglement have been introduced under efficient LOCC constraints
Arnon-Friedman et al. 2023
, and recent asymptotic results for pure states have shown that these constraints can lead to large separations between information-theoretic and computational notions of entanglement manipulation
Leone et al. 2025
.

In parallel, efficient-measurement frameworks have shown how distinguishability, information measures, and resource quantification change when the admissible tests themselves are computationally restricted
Yángüez et al. 2026
;
Meyer et al. 2025
;
Meyer et al. 2026
. The present work is close in spirit to recent proposals for quantum computational entropies, incorporating computational constraints to the operational meaning of entropy
Avidan and Arnon 2026
;
Avidan et al. 2025
;
Hsiao et al. 2007
, as opposed to computational smoothing based on computational indistinguishability from high entropy states
Håstad et al. 1999
;
Chen et al. 2017
.
Our approach is the following: by extending the efficient-measurement perspective of
Yángüez et al. 2026
to general quantum channels, we obtain a channel-based framework from which a complexity-constrained conditional min-entropy arises naturally through a computational divergence. This recovers the previously proposed computational conditional min-entropy from
Avidan and Arnon 2026
;
Avidan et al. 2025
, thus cementing our computational divergence as a foundational and operationally meaningful quantity. Related ideas also appear in complexity-constrained thermodynamics, where one-shot entropic quantities govern feasible implementations of thermodynamic tasks
Yunger Halpern et al. 2022
;
Munson et al. 2025
;
Meyer et al. 2025
.

We now turn to the channel-based formulation that underpins our main results.

I.1
Complexity-Constrained Quantum Operations

Quantum information theory typically treats all theoretically feasible physical transformations equally, independent of the underlying computational complexity
Tomamichel 2016
;
Khatri and Wilde 2024
.
This standard assumption leads to a clean operational interpretation of quantum correlations and entropies (see, e.g.,
Brandão and Datta 2011
;
Konig et al. 2009
;
Datta 2009
), but it ignores basic physical constraints: not every admissible transformation is efficiently implementable. Once computational complexity is treated as a physical constraint, the relevant observer is no longer characterized by the full set of quantum channels, but by a restricted class of efficiently realizable operations.

To make the notion of a feasible observer precise, we fix a finite (typically universal) gate set
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
and a polynomial circuit-size bound
p(n)
. We then consider the set of quantum channels (CPTP maps) which can be implemented by circuits of size at most
p(n)
. Similar to, e.g.,
Avidan and Arnon 2026
;
Avidan et al. 2025
;
Yángüez et al. 2026
;
Meyer et al. 2025
, we consider the following operations to be free: initializing ancillary registers in a fixed basis state
\ket{0}
, discarding subsystems, and measuring subsystems in the computational basis. The corresponding implementation cost is given by the gate complexity
C(T)
of the quantum channel
T
.

We describe this set in the Heisenberg picture and, from now on, restrict ourselves to bipartite systems for the remainder of the paper. Thus, given a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
, the allowed transformation acts only on the subsystem
B
, while
A
is kept as a reference system. We highlight that, for typical practical settings, the size of both subsystems implicitly scale as a function of
n
. For an isomorphic copy of the reference system
A\cong A^{\prime}
, the corresponding Choi representation
Choi 1975
;
Jamiołkowski 1972
represents the dual map
T^{*}
acting from
A^{\prime}
to
B
by its Choi operator

\displaystyle J(T^{*})\coloneq d_{A}(\mathrm{Id}_{A}\otimes T^{*})\bigl(\outerproduct{\Omega_{AA'}}{\Omega_{AA'}}\bigr)\,,

where
|\Omega_{AA^{\prime}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{A}}}\sum_{x\in[d_{A}]}\ket{x_{A}x_{A'}}
denotes a maximally entangled state on
\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{A^{\prime}}_{n}
and
d_{A}
the dimension of the system
A
, as represented in
. The dual channels
T^{*}
(CPU channels) and thus the admissible set of Choi operators are implicitly constrained by the gate complexity underpinning the set of efficient quantum channels
T

Definition I.1
.

Given a finite, discrete gate set

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}\coloneq\left\{J(T^{*})\;:\;C(T)\leq p(n)\right\}\,.

Moreover, we denote the smallest cone containing

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}\coloneq\operatorname{cone}\,\left(\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}\right)\,.

Under the informational-completeness condition stated in

Jenčová 2013

;

;
Yángüez et al. 2026

Structural properties of this construction are given in

Definition I.2
.

For positive semi-definite operators

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B}(\rho_{AB}\|\sigma_{AB})\coloneq\inf\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\;:\;\rho_{AB}\leq_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}}2^{\lambda}\sigma_{AB}\right\}\,.

II
Computational min-entropy

Motivated by the information-theoretic definition of the min-entropy, we define a computational analogue to it in

\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B}
.
We show below that it retains the standard operational interpretation of the conditional min-entropy from
Konig et al. 2009
, except that admissible channels are now required to be efficiently implementable.

Definition II.1
.

For a bipartite quantum state
\rho_{AB}
, the
computational conditional min-entropy
of
A
given
B
is defined by

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A|B)_{\rho}\coloneq-\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma_{B}\geq 0\\
\Tr[\sigma_{B}]=1\end{subarray}}\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B}\!\left(\rho_{AB}\,\middle\|\,\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}\right)\,.

Since the computational max-divergence is independent of the choice of
\sigma_{B}
(

II.1
The Fully Quantum Setting

This subsection is dedicated to our first main result, namely that our proposed computational min-entropy admits the same operational interpretation as its information-theoretic analogue
Konig et al. 2009
. In particular, we recover the computational min-entropy defined in
Avidan et al. 2025

\ket{\Omega_{AA'}}
and any state achievable by applying an efficiently implementable channel to the subsystem

\rho_{AB}

.

For every bipartite quantum state

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 1.53334pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=-\log\!\left(d_{A}\max_{T}F_{T}(\rho_{AB})\right),

where

\displaystyle F_{T}(\rho_{AB})\coloneq\bra{\Omega_{AA'}}(\mathrm{Id}_{A}\otimes T)(\rho_{AB})\ket{\Omega_{AA'}}

and the maximum is taken over efficiently implementable quantum channels
T:B\to A^{\prime}

II.2
The Classical-Quantum Setting

Let us now consider classical-quantum (cq-) states, i.e., states of the form

\displaystyle\rho_{XB}=\sum_{x}p_{x}\outerproduct{x}{x}_{X}\otimes\rho_{B}^{x}\,.

For this class of states, the closed-form expression for the computational conditional min-entropy can be further simplified. In particular, its resulting operational meaning can be expressed in terms of

II.3
The Non-Conditional Setting

The present framework also admits a natural non-conditional reduction. Here, one simply appends a trivial, one-dimensional conditioning register
B
, i.e., one defines the state

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{AB}\coloneq\rho_{A}\otimes\outerproduct{1}{1}_{B}\,

and sets

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A)_{\rho}\coloneq\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A|B)_{\hat{\rho}}\,.

In this way, the computational conditional min-entropy reduces to a genuine single-system entropy. Moreover, as we describe below, when the conditioning system is trivial, the set of admissible quantum channels reduces to an optimization over efficiently preparable quantum states, and the entropy reduces to a complexity-constrained analogue of the operator norm.

Lemma II.4
.

For every quantum state
\rho_{A}
, its computational min-entropy can be expressed as

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 1.53334pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=-\log\sup_{\tau_{A}\in\mathcal{S}^{p(n)}_{n,A}}\Tr\!\left[\rho_{A}\tau_{A}^{\,\mathsf{T}}\right]

\displaystyle\eqcolon-\log\|\rho_{A}\|_{\infty,\text{comp}}\,,

where
\mathcal{S}^{p(n)}_{n,A}
denotes the set of efficiently preparable states on
A
.

A proof of this result can be found in
. Thus, even without side information, computational power constrains this entropy at a foundational level: it is determined not by the largest overlap with an arbitrary state, but by the largest overlap with a state that can be prepared efficiently. Lastly, in the absence of computational constraints, the derived expression reduces to the ordinary non-conditional min-entropy
H_{\min}(A)_{\rho}=-\log\|\rho_{A}\|_{\infty}

(
Bhatia 1997

III
Computationally Hidden Quantum Correlations

As discussed in the previous section, the operational interpretation of
\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A|B)
is directly related to the entanglement that can be accessed from system
B
under computational constraints.
H_{\min}(A|B)
captures the same phenomenon in the information-theoretic setting. However, the gap between the computational and information-theoretic min-entropy can be significant; see also
.

Following similar arguments to
Leone et al. 2025
, we show that, for pure states, efficient operations guarantee access to a nontrivial amount of entanglement, i.e., the computational min-entropy is negative. Our converse bound shows that the resulting logarithmic scaling is essentially optimal. For mixed states, the effect can be much stronger, as there exist states whose information-theoretic conditional min-entropy is highly negative while their computational conditional min-entropy is nearly maximal. These results also strongly improve and extend on the previous computational vs. informational min-entropy gap from
(
Avidan et al. 2025
, Lemma 33)
, which only considers fixed circuit depth (i.e.,
p(n)=c
) and analyzes specific one-shot single-copy states rather than the poly-many copy regime.

III.1
Pure States

We begin by considering pure states. Our first separation result is obtained by adapting the state-agnostic concentration protocol of
Matsumoto and Hayashi 2007

;

Theorem III.1
.

Let
\{|\psi_{AB,n}\rangle\}_{n}
be a family of bipartite

\displaystyle H_{\min}(A)_{\rho}\geq\log k\,,

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 1.53334pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A^{k}|B^{k})_{|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k}}\leq-\Omega(k\log k)\,.

A sharper version, retaining the explicit dependence on

The natural question is whether the logarithmic scaling above is merely an artifact of the concentration protocol used in the proof of

There exist highly entangled pure states whose information-theoretic conditional min-entropy nearly saturates the quantum bound, while their computational conditional min-entropy stays much closer to zero.

Theorem III.2
.

Let

\displaystyle m(n_{A})=\omega(\log n_{A})\,,

\displaystyle H_{\min}(A^{k}|B^{k})_{|\psi_{n}\rangle^{\otimes k}}

III.2
Mixed States

We now turn to mixed states, where mixing can hide correlations even more effectively than in the pure-state setting. The construction is based on the generalized Hilbert–Schmidt ensemble, obtained by tracing out
m
qubits from a Haar-random
(n+m)
-qubit pure state
Bansal et al. 2025
;
Collins and Nechita 2015
;
Nechita 2007
;
Zyczkowski et al. 2011
;
Hall 1998
. For these states, the informational conditional min-entropy remains highly negative, while the computational conditional min-entropy becomes almost maximal.

Theorem III.3
.

Fix an
A|B
bipartition with
n_{A}=n_{B}=\frac{n}{2}
, let
m:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
satisfy

\displaystyle m(n_{A})=\omega(\log n_{A})\,,

and let
k\in\mathsf{poly}(n)
. Then there exists a family of
n
-qubit mixed states

\{\rho_{n}\}_{n}
such that

\displaystyle H_{\min}(A^{k}|B^{k})_{\rho_{n}^{\otimes k}}

\displaystyle\leq-k\bigl(n_{A}-m(n_{A})\bigr)+\mathsf{negl}(n)\,,

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 1.53334pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A^{k}|B^{k})_{\rho_{n}^{\otimes k}}

\displaystyle\geq k\,n_{A}-\mathsf{negl}(n)\,.

The mixed-state construction is proven in
. This separation is qualitatively stronger than the gap achieved in the pure-state setting. There, computational constraints are sufficient to certify at least some of the contained entanglement. For mixed states, by contrast, they render those correlations essentially inaccessible. In this sense, a state that is highly correlated can nevertheless appear, to an efficient observer, as if it were almost maximally uncorrelated.

IV
Discussion and open problems

Computational complexity can reshape the scaling of operationally accessible quantum correlations. We capture this phenomenon by showing large gaps between
\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A|B)
and
H_{\min}(A|B)
. This distinction already appears when considering pure states, where one-shot processing of polynomially many copies leaves only a logarithmic amount of entanglement within efficient reach in the worst case. For mixed states, this effect is sharper: we construct an ensemble with highly negative information-theoretic conditional min-entropy but a complexity-constrained conditional min-entropy that is, up to negligible corrections, as large as that of an uncorrelated maximally mixed state. In this regime, computational complexity can render an extensive amount of entanglement or quantum side information inaccessible for any efficient observer.

An important open problem is to understand how explicit and how physically natural this phenomenon can be made. Our strongest mixed-state separation is obtained from the generalized Hilbert–Schmidt ensemble
Hayden et al. 2006
;
Collins and Nechita 2015
;
Hall 1998
;
Zyczkowski et al. 2011
. Since this ensemble is not efficiently constructable
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
;
Goldreich 2008
, a natural next step is to replace this construction with explicit efficient state families, ideally under standard cryptographic assumptions
Bouland et al. 2024
or related notions of quantum pseudorandomness
Brakerski and Shmueli 2020
. Equally important is to determine whether comparable separations arise for physically motivated families, such as states generated by shallow local dynamics that are pseudorandom to efficient observers
Bostanci et al. 2025a
. Resolving these questions would show whether almost-maximal separations between information-theoretic and operationally accessible side information are exceptional consequences of randomness, or a robust feature of complexity-constrained quantum many-body systems.

Moreover, in this work, we unify two previous, separately established frameworks in computational quantum information theory
Yángüez et al. 2026
;
Avidan and Arnon 2026
;
Avidan et al. 2025
by constructing a computational max-divergence which recovers the operationally meaningful computational min-entropy from
Avidan and Arnon 2026
;
Avidan et al. 2025

(

, Lemma 32)

holds an uncorrelated ancillary system.
.

Our work suggests viewing complexity-constrained quantum information not only as a collection of restricted tasks, but as a modification of the order structure and operational primitives from which entropic quantities arise. From this perspective, several research directions appear natural. An immediate question that arises is whether our approach can be extended
to more general Rényi-divergences
Tomamichel 2016
and whether they admit equally sharp operational interpretations. Moreover, it is not yet clear how these entropies should be smoothed. Whereas computational notions of smoothing allow one to more naturally study standard cryptographic objects such as pseudo-random
generators
Chen et al. 2017
, information-theoretic smoothing yields desirable chain rules
Avidan and Arnon 2026
;
Avidan et al. 2025
. Finding a middle-ground would thus be of practical interest.
Lastly, it is not yet fully understood how these computational divergences and entropies can generally be adapted to settings where one considers other notions of the computational complexity underpinning quantum states and channels; see, e.g.,
Bostanci et al. 2025b
.

A.1
Notation

In this work we assume all Hilbert spaces to be finite dimensional. Hilbert spaces will be denoted by
\mathcal{H}
. When we consider
n
qudits of local dimension
d
, we write
\mathcal{H}_{n}\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
. Given a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, we denote the set of bounded operators acting on it as
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and its subset of positive semi-definite operators by
\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H})
. An operator
\rho\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H})
is called a
quantum state
if we have
\Tr\left[\rho\right]=1
. The set of quantum states on
\mathcal{H}
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
. The Hilbert-Schmidt inner product on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is given by
\langle a,b\rangle\coloneqq\Tr[a^{*}b]
, where
∗
denotes the Hilbert-Schmidt adjoint. The partial trace is the unique map
\tr_{B}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A})
such that

\Tr[\tr_{B}[X]Y]=\Tr[X(Y_{A}\otimes\mathds{1}_{B})]\,.

We denote the identity operator on
\mathcal{H}
as
\mathds{1}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
while the identity channel is the map

\mathrm{Id}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\;,

such that
\mathrm{Id}(X)=X
for all
X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. For two operators
\rho,\sigma\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H})
we write
\rho\ll\sigma
if
\ker{\sigma}\subseteq\ker{\rho}
, where
\ker{\tau}\coloneqq\{\ket{v}\,:\,\tau\ket{v}=0\}
.

In Landau notation, given two functions
f(n)
and
g(n)
, we write
f(n)=o(g(n))
if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f(n)/g(n)=0
. In the same way,
f(n)=\omega(g(n))
if
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f(n)/g(n)=\infty
.
f(n)=O(g(n))
if there exist two constant
C,N>0
such that
|f(n)|\leq C|g(n)|
for all
n\geq N
. Similarly,
f(n)=\Omega(g(n))
if there exist two constant
C,N>0
such that
|f(n)|\geq C|g(n)|
for all
n\geq N
. Lastly,
f(n)=\Theta(g(n))
if both
f(n)=O(g(n))
and
f(n)=\Omega(g(n))
. A function
f(n)
is
\mathsf{negl}(n)
, i.e., negligible, if, for every fixed
c
,
f(n)=o(1/n^{c})
.

With
\log
we denote the logarithm to base 2 and with
\ln
the logarithm to base
e
.
For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
.
For a set
S
, its affine hull is given by

\operatorname{aff}(S)\coloneq\Bigl\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}x_{i}:\ x_{i}\in S,\ \lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}=1\Bigr\}\;.

Lastly, its relative interior is
\operatorname{ri}(S)\coloneq\{x\in S:\exists\varepsilon>0\text{ such that }B(x,\varepsilon)\cap\operatorname{aff}(S)\subseteq S\}
, where
B(x,\varepsilon)
is an open ball of radius
\varepsilon
around
x
.

A.2
Information Theoretic Concepts

We begin by introducing the class of linear maps acting on quantum states that will be used throughout this paper and their adjoints.

Definition A.1

(Dual map)
.

Given a bounded linear map
T:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
, there exists a unique linear map
T^{*}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A})
defined as the dual map, such that
\forall X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B}),\;\forall\rho\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A})
:

\Tr[T(\rho)X]=\Tr[\rho T^{*}(X)]\,.

(A.1)

Moreover, the linear maps that represent physically admissible transformations are
quantum channels
, i.e., completely positive and trace-preserving (CPTP) maps in the Schrödinger picture. Their adjoints are completely positive and
unital
(CPU) maps, i.e. they satisfy

\displaystyle T^{*}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}})=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}}.

Throughout this work we will frequently use the Choi-Jamiołkowski representation of quantum channels.

Definition A.2

(Choi-Jamiołkowski representation
Choi 1975
,
Jamiołkowski 1972
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n},\mathcal{H}^{A^{\prime}}_{n}
be finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces with
\dim\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}=d_{A}
, and let
\mathcal{H}_{A}\cong\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime}}
. Let
T:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime}})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
be a CPTP map. For such a
T
, its Choi-Jamiołkowski operator representation on
AB
is given by

\displaystyle J(T)\coloneq d_{A}(\mathrm{Id}_{A}\otimes T)(\outerproduct{\Omega_{AA'}}{\Omega_{AA'}})\,,

where
\ket{\Omega_{AA'}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{A}}}\sum_{x\in[d_{A}]}\ket{x}_{A}\otimes\ket{x}_{A^{\prime}}
.

Having fixed our notation for quantum channels, we next specialize to channels arising from quantum measurements, which are conveniently described in terms of POVMs.

Definition A.3

(POVM)
.

A
positive operator-valued measure
(POVM) is given by a tuple of positive semi-definite operators
M=(E_{1},\dots,E_{m})
(for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
) such that
{\sum_{k=1}^{m}E_{k}=\mathds{1}}
. Moreover, if
{E_{i}E_{j}=\delta_{ij}E_{i}}
for all
i,j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, we call this a
projection-valued measure
(PVM).
The operators
E_{i}
of a POVM are called
effect operators
or
POVM elements
.

Theorem A.4

(Naimark’s Dilation Theorem in Finite Dimensions; see, e.g., Theorem 2.5.1 of
Holevo 2011
)
.

Any POVM
M=(E_{i})_{i=1}^{K}
on
\mathcal{H}
can be implemented via an isometry and a projective measurement on ancilla systems. In particular, there exists an isometry
V:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{K}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{K}
denotes a Hilbert space of dimension
K
, such that

\displaystyle E_{i}=V^{*}\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes\outerproduct{i}{i}_{\mathcal{H}_{K}}\right)V=T^{*}(\outerproduct{i}{i})\quad\forall i\in[K]\,.

(A.2)

Here
T^{*}(X)\coloneq V^{*}\left(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes X\right)V
for
X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{K})
is a completely positive unital (CPU) map. Note that one may choose
V=U\ket{0}
, where
U:\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{K}\to\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{K}
is a unitary and
\ket{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{K}
.

Moreover, one can associate to any
m
-outcome POVM
M=(E_{1},...,E_{m})
a channel that maps quantum states to classical distributions over
\{1,\dots,m\}
, via

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{M}\,:\;\rho\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Tr[\rho E_{i}]\,\outerproduct{i}{i}\,.

(A.3)

We will sometimes drop the subscript
M
, when the POVM to which this measurement map corresponds is clear from context.

To quantify how well states can be distinguished we will use a number of standard distance and divergence measures. We begin with fidelity and relative entropy, and then introduce the conditional min-entropy, which will play a central role in our paper.

Definition A.5

(Fidelity)
.

Given two quantum states
\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
, the
fidelity
between
\rho
,
\sigma
is given by:

\displaystyle F(\rho,\sigma)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\norm{\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\sigma}}_{1}^{2}\;.

Definition A.6

(Max-divergence
Datta 2009
)
.

Given any two positive semi-definite operators
\rho,\sigma\in\text{Pos}(\mathcal{H})
with
\Tr\left[\rho\right]>0
and
\rho\ll\sigma
, the
max-divergence
between
\rho
,
\sigma
is given by:

\displaystyle D_{\text{max}}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\log\inf\{\lambda\geq 0\,|\,\rho\leq\lambda\sigma\}\;.

(A.4)

Definition A.7

(Conditional Min-Entropy
Konig et al. 2009
)
.

Let
\rho_{AB}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
be a bipartite quantum state. The
min-entropy
of
A
conditioned on
B
is given by,

\displaystyle H_{\text{min}}(A|B)_{\rho}\coloneq-\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma_{B}\geq 0\\
\Tr[\sigma_{B}]=1\end{subarray}}D_{\text{max}}(\rho_{AB}\|\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})\,.

Moreover, as shown in
Konig et al. 2009
, the conditional min-entropy admits the following operational interpretation:

\displaystyle H_{\text{min}}(A|B)_{\rho}=-\log d_{A}\max_{T}F\left((\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes T)(\rho_{AB}),|\Omega_{AA^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\Omega_{AA^{\prime}}|\right)\,,

where the maximization is over channels
T:B\to A^{\prime}
, and
|\Omega_{AA^{\prime}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{A}}}\sum_{x\in[d_{A}]}\ket{x}_{A}\otimes\ket{x}_{A^{\prime}}
. Thus, the conditional min-entropy is a one-shot measure that quantifies how well one can recover a maximally entangled state on
AA^{\prime}
from
\rho_{AB}
by acting locally on
B
. For states with quantum correlations,
H_{\text{min}}(A|B)_{\rho}
can be negative, reaching
-\log d_{A}
for a maximally entangled state.

In the case of the input state being a classical-quantum state, i.e.,
\rho_{XB}=\sum_{x}p_{x}|x\rangle\langle x|\otimes\rho_{B}^{x}
, the operational meaning of the conditional min-entropy is the following:

\displaystyle H_{\text{min}}(X|B)_{\rho}=-\log\max_{\{E_{B}^{x}\}_{x}}\sum_{x}p_{x}\Tr[E^{x}_{B}\rho^{x}_{B}]\,,

where
\{E_{B}^{x}\}_{x}
is a POVM on
B
. Then, in this case, the conditional min-entropy quantifies the predictability of the classical register
X
given access to
B
.

One can define other relative entropies or divergence from an operational perspective. In this case, we will also work with the hypothesis testing relative entropy.

Definition A.8

(
\varepsilon
-hypothesis testing relative entropy
Khatri and Wilde 2024
)
.

Given any two positive semidefinite operators
\rho,\sigma\in\text{Pos}(\mathcal{H})
with
\Tr\left[\rho\right]>0
and
\rho\ll\sigma
, and
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, its corresponding
\varepsilon
-hypothesis testing relative entropy
is given by

\displaystyle D_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho\|\sigma)\coloneq-\log\inf_{E}\{\Tr[E\sigma]:0\leq E\leq\mathds{1}\;,\Tr[(\mathds{1}-E)\rho]\leq\varepsilon\}\,.

(A.5)

This entropy can equivalently be expressed as

D_{H}^{1-\varepsilon}(\rho||\sigma)\coloneq-\log\inf_{E}\{\Tr[E\sigma]:0\leq E\leq\mathds{1}\;,\Tr[E\rho]\geq\varepsilon\}\;.

(A.6)

A.3
Cone Theory

In this section we summarize some basic notions of cone theory. For a deeper understanding, the reader may refer to
Bushell 1973a
,
Bushell 1973b
,
Eveson 1995
,
Reeb et al. 2011
,
George and Chitambar 2024
.

Definition A.9

(Convex Cone)
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a finite-dimensional real vector space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
A
convex cone

\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{V}
is a subset that satisfies

\displaystyle a,b\in\mathcal{C}\implies\lambda a+\mu b\in\mathcal{C}\quad\forall\lambda,\mu\geq 0\,.

Moreover, for any
K\subset\mathcal{V}
, we denote by
\mathsf{cone}(K)\coloneqq\mathsf{conv}(\bigcup_{\lambda\geq 0}\lambda K)
the smallest (convex) cone that contains
K
.

A cone is
closed
if it is closed in the norm topology induced by the inner product on
\mathcal{V}
and, hence, it contains its boundary. Following
Reeb et al. 2011
,
\mathcal{C}
is
pointed
if
\mathcal{C}\cap(-\mathcal{C})=\{0\}
, where

\displaystyle a\in-\mathcal{C}\iff-a\in\mathcal{C}\,.

Moreover, a cone is
solid
if
\mathsf{span}(\mathcal{C})=\mathcal{V}
. In finite dimensions this is equivalent to
\mathsf{ri}(\mathcal{C})\neq\emptyset
.

Definition A.10

(Proper Cone)
.

A convex, closed, pointed, and solid cone is called a
proper cone
.

One can also assign to each cone a dual cone.

Definition A.11

(Dual Cone)
.

Given a cone
\mathcal{C}
, its
dual cone

\mathcal{C}^{*}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{*}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathcal{V}^{*}|\langle x,c\rangle\geq 0,\ \forall c\in\mathcal{C}\}\,.

Therefore, if
\mathcal{C}_{1}\subset\mathcal{C}_{2}
, then
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{2}\subset\mathcal{C}_{1}^{*}
. We additionally note that the dual cone is also closed and convex. Moreover, if
\mathcal{C}
is a proper cone, then so is
\mathcal{C}^{*}
. Lastly, in finite-dimensional vector spaces, closed convex cones satisfy that the corresponding dual cone is also its pre-dual cone, i.e.
(\mathcal{C}^{*})^{*}=\mathcal{C}
.
Given a real vector space
\mathcal{V}
and a proper cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{V}
, one can define a natural partial order on
\mathcal{V}
.

Definition A.12

(Partial Order)
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a finite-dimensional real vector space and
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{V}
be a proper cone. We say that
a\geq_{\mathcal{C}}b
if and only if
a-b\in\mathcal{C}
.

In the following, we take
\mathcal{V}=\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H})
. This vector space is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}
, where
d=\dim(\mathcal{H})
.
A canonical example of a proper cone in quantum information theory is the cone of
positive semidefinite
operators in
\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H})\coloneqq\{a\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H})\,|\,a\geq 0\}\,.

(A.7)

This cone is self-dual in
\mathcal{V}
and we denote its induced partial order by
\geq
. This particular partial order is relevant for the definition of the max-divergence
Datta 2009
,
Reeb et al. 2011
, which can be written as

\displaystyle D_{\text{max}}(a\|b)

\displaystyle=\log\inf\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}|a\leq\lambda b\}

\displaystyle=\log\sup_{v\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H})}\frac{\langle v,a\rangle}{\langle v,b\rangle}\,.

In the optimization above,
v
should be understood as an effect operator in the pre-dual of the cone of
\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H})
, in which
a,b
live, rather than that cone itself. Since
\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H})
is self-dual with respect to the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product, these cones coincide here. This distinction will matter for general cones in the coming sections.

If one considers other proper cones inside of
\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H})
, then one can use
this relation between the max-divergence and the conic structure of
\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H})
to derive a more general notion of max-divergences.

Definition A.13

(
\mathcal{C}
-Max-divergence
Reeb et al. 2011
,
George and Chitambar 2024
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a proper cone. For any
a,b\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}
, the
\mathcal{C}
-max-divergence
between
a,b
is given by

\displaystyle D^{\mathcal{C}}_{\text{max}}(a\|b)\coloneqq\log\inf\{\lambda\geq 0\,|\,a\leq_{\mathcal{C}}\lambda b\}\,.

(A.8)

If no
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
exists such that
\lambda b-a\in\mathcal{C}
, i.e. the infimum is over an empty set, then we set
D^{\mathcal{C}}_{\text{max}}(a\|b)=\infty
.

Moreover, there exists a dual formulation of the
\mathcal{C}
-max-divergence.

Lemma A.14

(Dual Expression
Reeb et al. 2011
,
George and Chitambar 2024
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a proper cone. Then

\displaystyle D^{\mathcal{C}}_{\textup{max}}(a\|b)\coloneqq\log\inf\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}|a\leq_{\mathcal{C}}\lambda b\}=\log\sup_{v\in\mathcal{C}^{*}}\frac{\langle v,a\rangle}{\langle v,b\rangle}\,.

(A.9)

In order to exploit the duality between cones, it is convenient to use tools from
conic
(and in particular
semidefinite
) programming
Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004
. Let
\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
\mathcal{V}_{2}
be finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces equipped with inner products
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{1}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{2}
. Generalizing
, given a linear map
\Phi:\mathcal{V}_{1}\to\mathcal{V}_{2}
, its (Hilbert-space) adjoint
\Phi^{*}:\mathcal{V}_{2}\to\mathcal{V}_{1}
is the unique linear map satisfying

\displaystyle\langle\Phi(v_{1}),v_{2}\rangle_{2}=\langle v_{1},\Phi^{*}(v_{2})\rangle_{1}\qquad\forall\;v_{1}\in\mathcal{V}_{1},\ \forall\;v_{2}\in\mathcal{V}_{2}\,.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{2}
be proper cones, and let
c\in\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
b\in\mathcal{V}_{2}
. A standard conic program (the
primal
problem) and its associated
dual
problem are given by

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mathrm{primal}}\;\coloneq\;\inf_{x\in\mathcal{V}_{1}}\ \langle c,x\rangle_{1}\quad\text{such that}\quad\Phi(x)-b\in\mathcal{C}_{2},\quad x\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\,,

(A.10)

and

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mathrm{dual}}\;\coloneq\;\sup_{y\in\mathcal{V}_{2}}\ \langle b,y\rangle_{2}\quad\text{such that}\quad c-\Phi^{*}(y)\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{*},\quad y\in\mathcal{C}_{2}^{*}\,,

(A.11)

where
\mathcal{C}_{1}^{*}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}^{*}
denote the dual cones with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{1}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{2}
, respectively. When
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
are positive semidefinite cones, these become
semidefinite programs
.

If the primal and dual problems are feasible, then
weak duality
holds: for every primal-feasible

x\in\mathcal{V}_{1}
and dual-feasible
y\in\mathcal{V}_{2}
one has

\displaystyle\langle c,x\rangle_{1}\;\geq\;\langle b,y\rangle_{2},

and in particular
\gamma^{\mathrm{primal}}\geq\gamma^{\mathrm{dual}}
.

Moreover, under a standard constraint qualification (Slater’s interiority condition) there is no duality
gap. One convenient conic formulation is the following.

Theorem A.15

(Slater’s interiority condition)
.

Assume that (
A.10
) is feasible and that there exists a
strictly feasible
point

\bar{x}\in\mathsf{ri}\,(\mathcal{C}_{1})
such that
\Phi(\bar{x})-b\in\mathsf{ri}\,(\mathcal{C}_{2})
.
If
\gamma^{\mathrm{primal}}
is finite, then strong duality holds, i.e.,

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mathrm{primal}}=\gamma^{\mathrm{dual}},

and the dual optimum is attained.

A.4
Representation Theory

Representation theory provides a natural framework for exploiting symmetries in composite quantum systems, in particular when dealing with many identical copies. A key instance is Schur-Weyl duality, which describes the commuting actions of the unitary group and the symmetric group on tensor powers
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}
, yielding a canonical decomposition into irreducible sectors and the associated Schur basis. These tools underpin several standard primitives in quantum information theory—such as spectrum estimation, permutation-invariant tomography, and entanglement concentration—and they will be used throughout this work. Let us briefly recall the notions and notation we need. For a more detailed treatment, we refer the reader to
Weyl 1939
,
Fulton and Harris 1991
.

Given a group
G
, a (unitary) representation of
G
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is a homomorphism
R:G\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
, i.e., a family of unitaries
\{R_{g}\}_{g\in G}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
satisfying
R_{e}=\mathds{1}
and
R_{gh}=R_{g}R_{h}
for all
g,h\in G
. A subspace
K\subseteq\mathcal{H}
is called
G
-invariant (or simply
invariant
) if
R_{g}K\subseteq K
for all
g\in G
. In this case, the restricted maps
\{R_{g}|_{K}\}_{g\in G}
form a
subrepresentation
. The representation is
irreducible
if its only invariant subspaces are
\{0\}
and
\mathcal{H}
. These irreducible representations of a group
G
play a fundamental role in representation theory.

In particular, every finite-dimensional unitary representation of a compact group is
completely reducible
, i.e., it decomposes as a direct sum of irreducible representations (or
irreps
), possibly with multiplicities. To relate two representations
R:G\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{1})
and
S:G\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
, we introduce
intertwiners
: a linear map
T:\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
is an intertwiner if
TR_{g}=S_{g}T
for all
g\in G
. Schur’s lemma characterizes intertwiners between irreducible representations.

Lemma A.16

(Schur’s Lemma
Fulton and Harris 1991
)
.

Let
R:G\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{1})
and
S:G\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
be irreducible representations, and let
T:\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
be an intertwiner, i.e.
TR_{g}=S_{g}T
for all
g\in G
. Then:

•

If
R
and
S
are not isomorphic, then
T=0
.

•

If
R
and
S
are isomorphic, then
T
is an isomorphism; in particular, if
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{H}_{2}
and
R=S
, then
T=\alpha\,\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
.

Let us now focus on two the groups that we will use in this work: the unitary group
\mathcal{U}(d)
acting
collectively
on all copies, and the symmetric group
S_{k}
permuting the
k
copies
Weyl 1939
,
Fulton and Harris 1991
. Schur–Weyl duality describes the joint representation of these commuting actions on the tensor-power space
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes k}
. Let
d=\dim(\mathbb{C}^{d})
.

The
unitary group
on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(d)\coloneqq\{U\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\,:\,U^{*}U=\mathds{1}\}\,,

and it acts on
k
copies via the tensor-power representation
U\mapsto U^{\otimes k}
on
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes k}
.

The
symmetric group

S_{k}
is the group of permutations of
[k]\coloneqq\{1,\dots,k\}
under composition. It acts on
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes k}
through the permutation unitaries
\{R_{\pi}\}_{\pi\in S_{k}}
defined by

Theorem B.5

Let

Definition B.6

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{J}^{G}_{n,A|B}\right|\leq\sum_{m=0}^{G}\left(|\mathcal{G}|(n+\ell G)^{\ell}\right)^{m}\leq 2^{\,c\,G\log(n+G)}

(B.10)

for some constant
c>0
depending only on
\mathcal{G}
and
\ell
.
In particular, if
G=p(n)
is polynomial, then there exists a polynomial
r(n)
such that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}\right|\leq 2^{r(n)}.

(B.11)

Proof.

By
, every element of
\mathbf{J}^{G}_{n,A|B}
is the Choi operator of a CPU map

T^{*}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{A^{\prime}}_{n})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})
implemented by a unitary circuit
U
of size at most
G
,
possibly using ancillary registers.

Although ancillary registers are unrestricted in principle, only those on which at least one gate acts
are relevant. Hence every such circuit is equivalent to one in which all ancillas on which no gate acts
have been removed. Moreover, the remaining ancillas may be relabelled according to the first gate in
which they appear. Since each gate acts on at most
\ell
registers, a circuit with at most
G
gates
uses at most
\ell G
active ancilla registers. Therefore every such circuit admits a canonical
description on at most

\displaystyle N\coloneq n+\ell G

(B.12)

registers.

For each of the
m\leq G
gate positions, one chooses:

1.

the gate in
\mathcal{G}
, giving at most
|\mathcal{G}|
possibilities;

2.

the ordered tuple of registers on which it acts, giving at most
N^{\ell}
possibilities.

Hence the number of exact circuit descriptions of length
m
is at most

\displaystyle\left(|\mathcal{G}|\,N^{\ell}\right)^{m}=\left(|\mathcal{G}|(n+\ell G)^{\ell}\right)^{m}.

(B.13)

Summing over all
m\leq G
gives

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{J}^{G}_{n,A|B}\right|\leq\sum_{m=0}^{G}\left(|\mathcal{G}|(n+\ell G)^{\ell}\right)^{m}.

(B.14)

Since distinct Choi operators are fewer than distinct circuit descriptions, this proves finiteness.

Finally, because
\ell
and
|\mathcal{G}|
are constants,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{G}\left(|\mathcal{G}|(n+\ell G)^{\ell}\right)^{m}\leq(G+1)\left(|\mathcal{G}|(n+\ell G)^{\ell}\right)^{G}\leq 2^{\,c\,G\log(n+G)}

(B.15)

for some constant
c>0
. Taking
G=p(n)
yields the claim.
∎

Therefore, for every polynomial
p(n)
, the family

\{\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
consists of at most
2^{r(n)}
Choi operators for some polynomial
r(n)
. In particular, for each fixed
n
, the set
\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}
is finite. There is a direct connection between this family and the family of efficiently generated effect operators
\{\mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{eff}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
defined in
Yángüez et al. 2026
.

Lemma B.12
.

Given a gate set
\mathcal{G}
, let
\mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{eff}}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})
denote the set of efficiently generated binary effect operators from
[
Yángüez et al. 2026
, Definition 3.8]
, i.e. those
F_{B}
for which the binary POVM
\{F_{B},\mathds{1}_{B}-F_{B}\}
is implementable by a circuit of size
q(n)
.
Let
\mathcal{H}^{A^{\prime}}_{n}\cong\mathbb{C}^{2}
with computational basis
\{\ket{0},\ket{1}\}
. Then, for every
F_{B}\in\mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{eff}}_{n}
there exists a CPU map
T^{*}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{A^{\prime}}_{n})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})
with
C(T^{*})\leq q(n)
and Choi operator
J_{AB}\in\mathbf{J}^{q(n)}_{n,A|B}
such that

\displaystyle F_{B}=T^{*}(\outerproduct{0}{0}_{A^{\prime}})=\tr_{A}\!\left[J_{AB}(\outerproduct{0}{0}_{A}\otimes\mathds{1}_{B})\right]=\bra{0}_{A}J_{AB}\ket{0}_{A}\,.

Similarly,
\mathds{1}_{B}-F_{B}=T^{*}(\outerproduct{1}{1}_{A^{\prime}})
. In particular,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{eff}}_{n}\subseteq\left\{\,\bra{i}_{A}J_{AB}\ket{i}_{A}\ \Big|\ J_{AB}\in\mathbf{J}^{q(n)}_{n,A|B},\ i\in\{0,1\}\right\}.

The same inclusion holds more generally for any choice of
\dim(\mathcal{H}^{A^{\prime}}_{n})\geq 2
.

Proof.

Fix
F_{B}\in\mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{eff}}_{n}
. By definition, there exists a circuit of size
q(n)
implementing the binary POVM
\{F_{B},\mathds{1}_{B}-F_{B}\}
. By
, we may assume this circuit can be described in the following way

\displaystyle F_{B}=\bra{0}_{A^{\prime}}\,U^{*}(\mathds{1}_{B}\otimes\outerproduct{0}{0}_{A^{\prime}})U\,\ket{0}_{A^{\prime}}\,.

Define
T^{*}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{A^{\prime}}_{n})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})
by

\displaystyle T^{*}(X)\coloneq\bra{0}_{A^{\prime}}\,U^{*}(\mathds{1}_{B}\otimes X)U\,\ket{0}_{A^{\prime}}\qquad\forall\,X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{A^{\prime}}_{n})\,.

Then
T^{*}
is completely positive and unital, and it satisfies
T^{*}(\outerproduct{0}{0}_{A^{\prime}})=F_{B}
and
T^{*}(\outerproduct{1}{1}_{A^{\prime}})=\mathds{1}_{B}-F_{B}
. Moreover,
C(T^{*})\leq q(n)
since it is implemented by the same circuit. The rest of the proof follows from
Equation

B.3
.
∎

Then, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the set of efficient measurements
\mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{eff}}_{n}
implementable with
p(n)
gates is included (as a representation of its corresponding quantum channel) in the set of complexity constrained Choi operators
\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}
.

Similarly to
Yángüez et al. 2026
, one can define the family of cones generated by the family of sets
\{\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. In contrast to the measurement setting considered there, these cones are not meant to capture efficient measurements on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n})
. Rather, they capture the operator representations of efficiently implementable quantum channels
T:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{A^{\prime}}_{n})
.

Definition B.13

(Family of the cones of efficiently generated Choi operators )
.

Given a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n}
, the cone of efficiently generated Choi operators is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}=\mathsf{cone}(\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B})=\bigcup_{\lambda\geq 0}\lambda\cdot\mathsf{conv}\big(\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}\big)\,.

(B.16)

where
\mathsf{conv}\big(\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}\big)
is the convex hull of the set
\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}
.

Lemma B.14

(Normalization of Choi operators)
.

For any CPU map
T^{*}
, the Choi operator satisfies
J(T^{*})\geq 0
and
\tr_{A}[J(T^{*})]=\mathds{1}_{B}
.
Consequently every
F_{AB}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}
obeys

\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]=\lambda\,\mathds{1}_{B}
for some
\lambda\geq 0
.

Proof.

Complete positivity gives
J(T^{*})\geq 0
, and

\tr_{A}[J(T^{*})]=d_{A}\,T^{*}(\tr_{A}[\outerproduct{\Omega_{AA^\prime}}{\Omega_{AA^\prime}}])=T^{*}(\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}})=\mathds{1}_{B}
.
The trace condition extends by linearity and the conic closure.
∎

Throughout this paper we will work with two normalized bases of the same cone
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}
. The first one is the
channel-normalized
slice,

\displaystyle\mathsf{conv}\big(\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}\big)\coloneq\left\{F_{AB}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}\,:\,\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]=\mathds{1}_{B}\right\},

which corresponds to the convex hull of efficiently generated Choi operators. The second one is the
effect-normalized
slice,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{eff}}_{n,A|B}\coloneq\left\{F_{AB}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}\,:\,\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]=\mathds{1}_{B}/d_{A}\right\},

which arises from the normalized Choi effect operators. Nevertheless, as discussed in
, these two normalizations differ only by a positive scalar rescaling, and hence generate the same proper cone. Which normalization is preferable depends on the application.

Lemma B.15
.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}\;\coloneq\;\left\{X\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})\;:\;\tr_{A}[X]\in\mathbb{R}\cdot\mathds{1}_{B}\right\}

be the subspace spanned by Hermitian operators whose partial trace is proportional to the identity. If

\displaystyle\mathsf{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\,\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}\,\}\;=\;\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then the family of cones
\{\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a family of proper cones with respect to the subspaces
\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}
, i.e., each
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}
is closed, convex, pointed, and solid in
\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}
.

Proof.

Convexity is clear from the definition. By
, the set
\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}
is finite for every
n
. Hence
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}=\mathsf{cone}(\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B})
is a finitely generated cone, and therefore it is closed. Pointedness follows since every generator
J(T^{*})
is positive semidefinite, since
T^{*}
are all completely positive, and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})
, hence
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}\cap(-\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B})=\{0\}
. Moreover, for CPU maps one has
\tr_{A_{n}}J(T^{*})=\mathds{1}_{B_{n}}
, so
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}
.

Lastly, solidness holds in
\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}
as the assumption implies

\displaystyle\mathsf{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}\right)=\mathsf{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}\}=\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}\,,

(using
\mathsf{span}(\mathsf{cone}(\mathsf{conv}(S)))=\mathsf{span}(S)
for nonempty
S
). Hence, by the standard full-dimensionality argument (cf.
[
Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004
, Section 2.1.3]
),
\mathrm{ri}_{\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B})\neq\emptyset
, where
\mathrm{ri}
denotes the relative interior.
∎

The solidness condition

\displaystyle\mathsf{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}\right)=\mathsf{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}\}=\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}\,,

can be seen as an informational completeness condition at the level of Choi representations: every Choi operator associated with a CPU map (efficient or not) lies in
\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}
and can be spanned by Choi operators of efficiently implementable CPU maps, i.e.,

\displaystyle\forall X\in\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}\;\exists\,c_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\,J^{i}_{AB}\in\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}\ \text{s.t.}\ X=\sum_{i}c_{i}\,J^{i}_{AB}\,.

Example B.16

(An explicit example of a set of informationally complete quantum channels)
.

In order to show the feasibility of the informational completeness assumption under complexity constraints, let us provide an example. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and set of symbols
\mathcal{S}_{n}\coloneq\{0,1,+,+i\}^{n}
, where its corresponding quantum states are defined as
|+\rangle\coloneq 1/\sqrt{2}(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)
and
|+i\rangle\coloneq 1/\sqrt{2}(|0\rangle+i|1\rangle)
. Then, let us define the product states on
\mathcal{H}^{A\prime}_{n}\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
by

\displaystyle\sigma_{s}\coloneq\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}|s_{k}\rangle\!\langle s_{k}|\,,

and set
\sigma_{0^{n}}\coloneq|0\rangle\langle 0|^{\otimes n}
. For each Pauli string
Q\in\{\pm\mathds{1},\pm X,\pm Y,\pm Z\}^{\otimes n}
, let us now define the binary POVM
\{E_{Q},\mathds{1}-E_{Q}\}
with

\displaystyle E_{Q}\coloneq\frac{\mathds{1}+Q}{2}\,.

Assuming that the POVMs
\{\{E_{Q},\mathds{1}-E_{Q}\}\}_{Q}
and the states
\{\sigma_{s}\}_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}
are efficiently implementable, then the measurement map
T_{s,Q}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n}^{B})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n}^{A^{\prime}})
defined by

\displaystyle T_{s,Q}(\rho)\coloneq\Tr[E_{Q}\rho]\sigma_{s}+\Tr[(\mathds{1}-E_{Q})\rho]\sigma_{0^{n}}

is also efficiently implementable for every
s\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
and
Q
.

Note that this linear map is nothing but a measure-and-prepare channel, where the set of measurements is the tomographically complete set of Pauli measurements, and the prepared states are products of Pauli eigenstates. One can easily verify that it is a CPTP map and, thus, its dual, a CPU map. Its corresponding Choi operators can be written as

\displaystyle J(T_{s,Q}^{*})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{s}^{\,\mathsf{T}}\otimes E_{Q}+\sigma_{0^{n}}^{\,\mathsf{T}}\otimes(\mathds{1}-E_{Q})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0^{n}}\otimes\mathds{1}+(\sigma_{s}-\sigma_{0^{n}})^{\,\mathsf{T}}\otimes E_{Q}\,,

where
{\mathsf{T}}
denotes transpose in the computational basis. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathsf{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{J(T_{s,Q}^{*}):s,Q\}=\mathbb{R}\cdot(\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\mathds{1}_{B})\oplus\mathrm{Herm}_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{A_{n}})\otimes\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}_{B_{n}})=\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}\,.

Let us now define the dual subspace of the channel subspace
\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}
. Following
Jenčová 2013
,
Jenčová 2014
, the algebraic dual
\mathcal{V}^{*}_{n,A|B}
can be identified with a quotient of
\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})
via the Hilbert-Schmidt product. Let us define the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\perp}_{n,A|B}\coloneq\left\{Y\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})\,|\,\Tr[YX]=0\;\forall X\in\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}\right\}\,.

Let
\pi:\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})\rightarrow\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})/\mathcal{V}^{\perp}_{n,A|B}
, be the quotient map
\pi(Y)=Y+\mathcal{V}^{\perp}_{n,A|B}
. Then
\pi
induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{V}^{*}_{n,A|B}\cong\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})/\mathcal{V}^{\perp}_{n,A|B}
, where the duality between
\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}
and
\mathcal{V}^{*}_{n,A|B}
is given by

\displaystyle\langle\pi(Y),X\rangle=\Tr[YX],\qquad Y\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n}),\,X\in\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}\,.

In particular, two operators
X
and
X^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})
define the same functional on the effect subspace
\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}
if

\displaystyle X-X^{\prime}=\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes Z,\qquad\Tr[Z]=0\,.

Definition B.17

(Dual cone of quantum states)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}
be the cone of efficiently generated Choi operators. Its dual cone is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}\coloneq\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}\,*}_{n,A|B}=\left\{\pi(Y)\,|\,Y\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n}),\,\Tr[YX]\geq 0\;\forall X\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}\,\right\}\,,

(B.17)

where
\pi(Y)=Y+\mathcal{V}^{\perp}_{n,A|B}
is the quotient representation of the operator
Y\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})
.

Then, the dual cone contains (the image of) every quantum state: if
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})
, then
\pi(\rho_{AB})\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}
for every
\rho_{AB}\geq 0
, since
\Tr[\rho_{AB}F_{AB}]\geq 0
for all
\rho_{AB}\geq 0
and all
F_{AB}\geq 0
. Thus, whenever
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}
is a proper cone with respect to
\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}
, then
\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}
is also proper with respect to
\mathcal{V}^{*}_{n,A|B}
.

Under the informational-completeness condition,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}
is a proper cone, and hence we can define a partial order on
\mathcal{V}^{*}_{n,A|B}
, via

\displaystyle\pi(\rho_{AB})\leq_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}}\pi(\sigma_{AB})\,\iff\Tr[X\rho_{AB}]\leq\Tr[X\sigma_{AB}]\;\forall X\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}\,.

(B.18)

In a slight abuse of notation, we will often omit the quotient map
\pi(\cdot)
and write
\rho_{AB}\leq_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}}\sigma_{AB}
to mean
\pi(\rho_{AB})\leq_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}}\pi(\sigma_{AB})
.

Appendix C
Computational Conditional Min-Entropy

In this section, we first introduce the
computational max-divergence
, defined with respect to the cone of efficiently implementable Choi operators, and study its basic properties as well as its relation to other operationally meaningful quantities, such as a corresponding computational hypothesis-testing divergence. We then show, in
, that the computational conditional min-entropy admits the same operational interpretation as in the informational setting: it characterizes the largest fidelity with a maximally entangled state achievable by a recovery channel acting on
B
. Next, in
, we consider the special case of bipartite classical-quantum states and prove that the corresponding operational quantity is the maximal guessing probability under efficiently implementable measurements. Finally, in
, we turn to the non-conditional case and derive a single-letter expression mirroring the informational one.

In
Yángüez et al. 2026
, the computational max-divergence is induced by the cone generated by efficiently implementable binary measurements. Here, we consider the analogous max-divergence induced by efficiently implementable completely positive unital (CPU) maps, represented as operators through the Choi-Jamiołkowski isomorphism. This viewpoint strictly generalizes the measurement-based approach of
Yángüez et al. 2026
: by
, every complexity-constrained effect operator from
Yángüez et al. 2026
arises from an efficiently implementable CPU map. Moreover, in
we show that the max-divergence of
Yángüez et al. 2026
is recovered as a special case of the construction introduced below.

Definition C.1

(Computational Max-Divergence)
.

Given a family of proper cones
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}
and two positive semidefinite operators
\rho_{AB},\sigma_{AB}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})
, we denote its corresponding computational max-divergence by:

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B}(\rho_{AB}\|\sigma_{AB})\coloneqq\log\inf\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,\rho_{AB}\leq_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}}\lambda\sigma_{AB}\}=\log\sup_{F\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}}\frac{\Tr[\rho_{AB}F_{AB}]}{\Tr[\sigma_{AB}F_{AB}]}\;.

(C.1)

If there does not exists any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\rho_{AB}\leq_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}}\lambda\sigma_{AB}
, then
\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B}(\rho_{AB}\|\sigma_{AB})=\infty
.

Note that
\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B}
is defined using the partial order from Eq.(
B.18
). Compared to
Yángüez et al. 2026
, the order here is induced by the cone generated by efficiently implementable CPU maps (via their Choi operators), rather than directly by efficiently implementable effect operators on
\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n}
.

Similarly to
Yángüez et al. 2026
, let us assume that the polynomial defining the family of sets of complexity-constrained Choi operators
\{\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is strictly super additive, i.e.,
p(n+m)\geq p(n)+p(m)+1
. Then, the following lemma holds.

Lemma C.2

(Super-additivity of the
\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B}
)
.

The computational max-divergence
\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 1.53334pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B}(\rho_{AB}\|\sigma_{AB})
is super-additive, i.e., for any
\rho_{AB}=\rho_{AB}^{1}\otimes\rho_{AB}^{2}
and
\sigma_{AB}=\sigma_{AB}^{1}\otimes\sigma_{AB}^{2}
such that
\rho^{i}_{AB},\sigma^{i}_{AB}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})
,

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 1.53334pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B,\,p(n_{1}+n_{2})}(\rho_{AB}\|\sigma_{AB})\geq\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 1.53334pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B,\,p(n_{1})}(\rho^{1}_{AB}\|\sigma^{1}_{AB})+\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 1.53334pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B,\,p(n_{2})}(\rho^{2}_{AB}\|\sigma^{2}_{AB})\,.

Proof.

The proof follows by arguments similar to those in
[
Yángüez et al. 2026
, Lemma 4.3]
.
∎

Moreover, the structure of the cone of Choi operators allows us to prove the following lemma

Lemma C.3

(
\sigma_{B}
independence of the
\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B}(\rho\|\mathds{1}\otimes\sigma_{B})
)
.

Given a quantum state
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n}
, the computational max divergence satisfies

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 1.53334pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B}(\rho_{AB}\|\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})=\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 1.53334pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B}(\rho_{AB}\|\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\tau_{B})

for any quantum states
\sigma_{B},\tau_{B}\in\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n}
that satisfy
\rho_{AB}\ll\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B},\ \rho_{AB}\ll\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\tau_{B}
.

Proof.

Let us recall that the computational max-divergence can be expressed as

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B}(\rho_{AB}\|\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})=\log\sup_{F_{AB}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}}\frac{\Tr[\rho_{AB}F_{AB}]}{\Tr[(\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})F_{AB}]}\,.

Every non-zero
F_{AB}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}
satisfies
\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]=\lambda(F_{AB})\,\mathds{1}_{B}
for some
\lambda(F_{AB})>0
. Hence, for
\Tr[\sigma_{B}]=1
,

\displaystyle\Tr[(\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})F_{AB}]=\Tr\!\left[\sigma_{B}\,\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]\right]=\lambda(F_{AB})\,\Tr[\sigma_{B}]=\lambda(F_{AB})\,,

which is independent of
\sigma_{B}
. Therefore, each ratio in the supremum is independent of
\sigma_{B}
. Replacing
\sigma_{B}

This special structure of the Choi cone also allows us to identify the computational max divergence with a hypothesis-testing quantity.

Definition C.4

(Computational hypothesis-testing Choi divergence)
.

Fix

\Tr[E\rho_{AB}]\geq\eta\end{subarray}}\frac{\Tr[E\sigma_{AB}]}{\Tr[E\rho_{AB}]}\,.

(C.2)

If the feasible set is empty, we set the corresponding divergence to

Unlike the computational hypothesis-testing relative entropies introduced in

Lemma C.5

(Equivalence of hypothesis-testing and max divergence)
.

Let
\rho_{AB}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})
be a state, and let

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 1.53334pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{H}^{p(n),\eta}(\rho_{AB}\|\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})=\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 1.53334pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B,\,p(n)}(\rho_{AB}\|\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})

Therefore, for every feasible

It follows that

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{H}^{p(n),\eta}(\rho_{AB}\|\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})

\displaystyle=-\log\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}E_{AB}\in\mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{eff}}_{n,A|B}\\
\Tr[E_{AB}\rho_{AB}]\geq\eta\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d_{A}\,\Tr[E_{AB}\rho_{AB}]}

\displaystyle=\log\!\left(d_{A}\,\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}E_{AB}\in\mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{eff}}_{n,A|B}\\
\Tr[E_{AB}\rho_{AB}]\geq\eta\end{subarray}}\Tr[E_{AB}\rho_{AB}]\right)\,.

(C.7)

Since

(C.8)

By the Choi-cone dual formulation of the computational max-divergence, equivalently
by

(C.9)

which proves the claim.

C.1
The Operational Meaning of the Computational Min-Entropy

Let us now prove that, in the complexity-constrained setting,
\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A|B)_{\rho}
admits an operational interpretation analogous to the standard (information-theoretic) conditional min-entropy
Konig et al. 2009
. In particular, we recover the corresponding notion considered in
Avidan et al. 2025
. Let us first prove the following lemma.

Lemma C.10
.

Let
\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}
and
\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n}
be finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. Let
\{\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the family of Choi operators of CPU maps generated by at most
p(n)
given a gate-set
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}
its corresponding dual cone. Then, for any
\rho_{AB}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})
and
\sigma_{B}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})

\displaystyle\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma_{B}\geq 0\\
\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}\geq_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}}\rho_{AB}\end{subarray}}\Tr[\sigma_{B}]=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}F_{AB}\geq_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}}0\\
\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]\leq\mathds{1}_{B}\end{subarray}}\Tr[\rho_{AB}F_{AB}]\,.

(C.11)

Proof.

The argument is inspired by the proof of
[
Konig et al. 2009
, Lemma 4]
, but its implementation in our setting requires several nontrivial modifications in order to accommodate the complexity constraints.

Let us first exploit the special structure of the cone of efficiently generated Choi operators to reduce the problem to a dual program.

For each efficiently generated Choi operator
F_{AB}
we have that
\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]=\mathds{1}_{B}
. Therefore, for the cone of efficient Choi operators
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}
define in
, any element
F_{AB}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}
can be written as

\displaystyle F_{AB}=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}J^{(j)}_{AB},\qquad\alpha_{j}\geq 0\,,

with
J^{(j)}_{AB}
a (channel-normalized) Choi operator, i.e.,
\tr_{A}[J^{(j)}_{AB}]=\mathds{1}_{B}
. In consequence, it also satisfies

\displaystyle\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]=\left(\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}\right)\mathds{1}_{B}=:t\,\mathds{1}_{B}

for some scalar
t\geq 0
. If
t=0
, then
F_{AB}=0
and the objective is zero.
For
0<t\leq 1
, define the rescaled operator

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{AB}\coloneq\frac{1}{t}F_{AB}\,.

Since
\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}
is a cone, then
\widehat{F}_{AB}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}
, and
\tr_{A}[\widehat{F}_{AB}]=\mathds{1}_{B}
. Moreover, for any state
\rho_{AB}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\Tr[\rho_{AB}\widehat{F}_{AB}]=\frac{1}{t}\,\Tr[\rho_{AB}F_{AB}]\geq\Tr[\rho_{AB}F_{AB}]\,.

Thus, since the supremum in the r.h.s. of Eq.(
C.11
) is attained on elements with
\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]=\mathds{1}_{B}
, we can extend the maximization to all operators
F_{AB}
such that
\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]\leq\mathds{1}_{B}
. Therefore, we will prove the following claim:

\displaystyle\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma_{B}\geq 0\\
\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}\geq_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}}\rho_{AB}\end{subarray}}\Tr[\sigma_{B}]=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}F_{AB}\geq_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}}0\\
\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]\leq\mathds{1}_{B}\end{subarray}}\Tr[\rho_{AB}F_{AB}]\,.

(C.12)

We formulate Eq. (
C.12
) as a conic program using the tools introduced in
on the spaces

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{1}\coloneq\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}_{B}^{n})\,,\qquad\mathcal{V}_{2}\coloneq\mathcal{V}^{*}_{n,A|B}\,,

with cones

\displaystyle C_{1}\coloneq\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H}_{B}^{n})\,,\qquad C_{2}\coloneq C^{S,\mathrm{eff}}_{n,A|B}\,,

and identifying the elements of Eq.(
A.10
) and Eq.(
A.11

and

, and the linear map

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathcal{V}_{1}\to\mathcal{V}_{2},\qquad\Phi(\sigma_{B})\coloneq\pi(\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})\,.

Then the primal problem is exactly

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathrm{primal}}=\inf_{\sigma_{B}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}_{B}^{n})}\left\{\Tr[\sigma_{B}]:\ \sigma_{B}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H}_{B}^{n}),\Phi(\sigma_{B})-\pi(\rho_{AB})\in C^{S,\mathrm{eff}}_{n,A|B}\right\}.

(C.13)

Since the pairing between
\mathcal{V}^{*}_{n,A|B}
and
\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}

\displaystyle\langle\pi(Y),F_{AB}\rangle=\Tr[YF_{AB}],\qquad Y\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}_{A}^{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}^{n}),\ \ F_{AB}\in\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}\,,

the adjoint map
\Phi^{*}:\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}\to\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}_{B}^{n})

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}(F_{AB})=\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]\,,

because

\displaystyle\langle\Phi(\sigma_{B}),F_{AB}\rangle=\Tr[(\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})F_{AB}]=\Tr[\sigma_{B}\,\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]]\,.

Hence, the dual problem is given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathrm{dual}}=\sup_{F_{AB}\in\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}}\left\{\Tr[\rho_{AB}F_{AB}]:\ F_{AB}\in C^{E,\mathrm{eff}}_{n,A|B},\ \mathds{1}_{B}-\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H}_{B}^{n})\right\}\,,

which can be simplified as,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathrm{dual}}=\sup_{F_{AB}\in C^{E,\mathrm{eff}}_{n,A|B}}\left\{\Tr[\rho_{AB}F_{AB}]:\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]\leq\mathds{1}_{B}\right\}\,.

(C.14)

Therefore, by weak duality for feasible primal-dual conic programs,

\displaystyle\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma_{B}\geq 0\\
\pi(\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})-\pi(\rho_{AB})\in C^{S,\mathrm{eff}}_{n,A|B}\end{subarray}}\Tr[\sigma_{B}]\ \geq\ \max_{\begin{subarray}{c}F_{AB}\in C^{E,\mathrm{eff}}_{n,A|B}\\
\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]\leq\mathds{1}_{B}\end{subarray}}\Tr[\rho_{AB}F_{AB}]\,.

(C.15)

To prove equality, it remains to show that there is no duality gap. For this, we verify Slater’s condition for the primal problem. Since
C^{E,\mathrm{eff}}_{n,A|B}
is a proper cone in
\mathcal{V}_{n,A|B}
, its dual cone
C^{S,\mathrm{eff}}_{n,A|B}
is a proper cone in
\mathcal{V}^{*}_{n,A|B}
. Thus it suffices to find
\sigma_{B}\in\mathsf{ri}(\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H}_{B}^{n}))
such that

\displaystyle\Phi(\sigma_{B})-\pi(\rho_{AB})=\pi(\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}-\rho_{AB})\in\mathsf{ri}(C^{S,\mathrm{eff}}_{n,A|B})\,.

By taking
\sigma_{B}\coloneq 2\lambda_{\max}(\rho_{AB})\,\mathds{1}_{B}

\sigma_{B}\in\mathsf{ri}(\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H}_{B}^{n}))
. Moreover, for every

F_{AB}\in C^{E,\mathrm{eff}}_{n,A|B}\setminus\{0\}

\displaystyle\left\langle\pi(\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}-\rho_{AB}),F_{AB}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\Tr[(\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}-\rho_{AB})F_{AB}]

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{\max}(\rho_{AB})\Tr[F_{AB}]-\Tr[\rho_{AB}F_{AB}]

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{\max}(\rho_{AB})\Tr[F_{AB}]>0\,,

where the second to last inequality is Hölder inequality and the last strict inequality follows since
\Tr[E]=\|E\|_{1}>0
as by assumption
F_{AB}\neq 0
and
\rho_{AB}
is a finite dimensional normalized quantum state, hence
\lambda_{\max}(\rho_{AB})>0

\displaystyle\pi(\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}-\rho_{AB})\in\mathsf{ri}(C^{S,\mathrm{eff}}_{n,A|B})\,.

Therefore, Slater’s condition holds, and the primal and dual optimal values coincide.

∎

Let us now prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem C.11
.

Given a quantum state
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n}
, its computational min-entropy can be expressed as

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 1.53334pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A|B)_{\rho}=-\log\accentset{\text{\tiny\hskip-0.51114pt\raisebox{0.10764pt}{c}}}{q}_{\mathrm{corr}}(A|B)_{\rho}\,,

(C.16)

where

\displaystyle\accentset{\text{\tiny\hskip-0.51114pt\raisebox{0.10764pt}{c}}}{q}_{\mathrm{corr}}(A|B)_{\rho}\coloneq d_{A}\cdot\max_{T}F\left((\mathrm{Id}_{A}\otimes T)(\rho_{AB}),\outerproduct{\Omega_{AA'}}{\Omega_{AA'}}\right)

(C.17)

is the maximal fidelity with a maximally entangled state achievable by channel
T:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{A^{\prime}}_{n})
, with
\mathcal{H}^{A^{\prime}}_{n}\cong\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}
and
\ket{\Omega_{AA'}}\coloneq\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{A}}}\sum_{x\in[d_{A}]}\ket{x}_{A}\otimes\ket{x}_{A^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

By definition of the computational max-divergence,

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=-\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma_{B}\geq 0\\
\Tr[\sigma_{B}]=1\end{subarray}}\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{D}_{\mathrm{max}}^{A|B}\left(\rho_{AB}\,\big\|\,\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}\right)

\displaystyle=-\log\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma_{B}\geq 0\\
\Tr[\sigma_{B}]=1\end{subarray}}\inf\left\{\lambda\geq 0:\rho_{AB}\leq_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}}\lambda(\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})\right\}.

Introduce the unnormalised variable
\tilde{\sigma}_{B}\coloneq\lambda\sigma_{B}\geq 0
.
Then the order constraint can be written as

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}\leq_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}}\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\tilde{\sigma}_{B}\iff\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\tilde{\sigma}_{B}-\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B},

and
\Tr[\tilde{\sigma}_{B}]=\lambda
. Hence

\displaystyle 2^{-\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A|B)_{\rho}}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tilde{\sigma}_{B}\geq 0}\left\{\Tr[\tilde{\sigma}_{B}]:\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\tilde{\sigma}_{B}\geq_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}}\rho_{AB}\right\}\;.

This is exactly the primal conic program of

\displaystyle 2^{-\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A|B)_{\rho}}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}F_{AB}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}\\
\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]=\mathds{1}_{B}\end{subarray}}\Tr[\rho_{AB}F_{AB}]\;.

(C.18)

Let us now prove the following equivalence:

\displaystyle\log\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}F_{AB}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_{n,A|B}\\
\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]=\mathds{1}_{B}\end{subarray}}\Tr[\rho_{AB}F_{AB}]=\log\left(d_{A}\cdot\max_{T}F\left((\mathrm{Id}_{A}\otimes T)(\rho_{AB}),\outerproduct{\Omega_{AA'}}{\Omega_{AA'}}\right)\right)

By construction,
F_{AB}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{A}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})
is a nonnegative operator such that
\tr_{A}[F_{AB}]=\mathds{1}_{B}
. Then, due to the Choi-Jamiołkowski isomorphism, it corresponds to the Choi operator of a CPU map

. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Tr[\rho_{AB}F_{AB}]

\displaystyle=d_{A}\Tr\left[\rho_{AB}(\mathrm{Id}_{A}\otimes T^{*})\outerproduct{\Omega_{AA'}}{\Omega_{AA'}}\right]

\displaystyle=d_{A}\Tr\left[(\mathrm{Id}_{A}\otimes T)(\rho_{AB})(\outerproduct{\Omega_{AA'}}{\Omega_{AA'}})\right]

\displaystyle=d_{A}\bra{\Omega_{AA'}}(\mathrm{Id}_{A}\otimes T)(\rho_{AB})\ket{\Omega_{AA'}}

\displaystyle=d_{A}F\left((\mathrm{Id}_{A}\otimes T)(\rho_{AB}),\outerproduct{\Omega_{AA'}}{\Omega_{AA'}}\right)\,,

where the first equality follows from duality, with no complexity overhead as proven in

taking into account that
\ket{\Omega_{AA'}}
is a pure state.

Then, since the maximization is done for a linear function w.r.t. a convex set, it suffices to take the extremal point of the convex set, i.e.,
\mathbf{J}^{p(n)}_{n,A|B}
. Lastly, due to the Choi-Jamiołkowski isomorphism to

C.3
Measurement Channels for CQ–States

For classical-quantum states, we show that restricting to efficient Choi operations preserves the classical-quantum structure. Let

Equivalently, one might see the cone

C.5
Non-conditional Computational Min-Entropy

We now consider the case in which there is no side information. Following

It is important to distinguish this construction from the flagged c-q reduction of

In the latter, one adjoins a classical flag on the first register and obtains a formulation in terms of efficient POVMs. In the present case, however, the conditioning register is one-dimensional, and the corresponding cone is generated by efficient state-preparation maps. Definition C.14

(Non-conditional computational min-entropy)

Let

Corollary C.16

(Operational norm in the non-conditional setting)

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\,\mathsf{T},p(n)}_{n,A}\coloneqq\left\{\tau_{A}^{\,\mathsf{T}}\;:\;\tau_{A}\in\mathcal{S}^{p(n)}_{n,A}\right\}\,,\qquad\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{T}}}_{n,A}\coloneqq\mathsf{cone}\!\left(\mathcal{S}^{\,\mathsf{T},p(n)}_{n,A}\right)\subseteq\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}_{n}^{A})\,,

\displaystyle\|X_{A}\|_{\infty,\mathrm{comp}}\coloneqq\sup_{\omega_{A}\in\mathcal{S}^{\,\mathsf{T},p(n)}_{n,A}}\left|\Tr[X_{A}\omega_{A}]\right|\,.

Then
\|\cdot\|_{\infty,\mathrm{comp}}
is a seminorm on
\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}_{n}^{A})
. If further

\displaystyle\mathsf{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\!\left(\mathcal{S}^{\,\mathsf{T},p(n)}_{n,A}\right)=\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}_{n}^{A})\,,

then

is a norm. In this case,

\displaystyle\|X_{A}\|_{\infty,\mathrm{comp}}=\inf\Bigl\{t\geq 0\;:\;-t\,\mathds{1}_{A}\leq_{(\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{T}}}_{n,A})^{*}}X_{A}\leq_{(\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{T}}}_{n,A})^{*}}t\,\mathds{1}_{A}\Bigr\}\,.

For

this is given by

\displaystyle\|X_{A}\|_{\infty,\mathrm{comp}}=\sup_{\tau_{A}\in\mathcal{S}^{p(n)}_{n,A}}\Tr[X_{A}\tau_{A}^{\,\mathsf{T}}]\,.

Proof.

Let us first prove that
\|\cdot\|_{\infty,\mathrm{comp}}
defines a seminorm. For every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and

\displaystyle\|\alpha X_{A}\|_{\infty,\mathrm{comp}}=\sup_{\omega_{A}\in\mathcal{S}^{\,\mathsf{T},p(n)}_{n,A}}\left|\Tr[\alpha X_{A}\omega_{A}]\right|=|\alpha|\,\|X_{A}\|_{\infty,\mathrm{comp}}\,,

\displaystyle\|X_{A}+Y_{A}\|_{\infty,\mathrm{comp}}

If

\displaystyle\Tr[(t\,\mathds{1}_{A}\pm X_{A})\bar{\eta}_{A}]\geq 0\qquad\forall\,\bar{\eta}_{A}\in\mathsf{conv}\!\left(\mathcal{S}^{\,\mathsf{T},p(n)}_{n,A}\right)\;,

or, equivalently,

Indeed, one implication is immediate since

If

\displaystyle\Tr\sqrt{\rho_{A}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{A}}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(\rho_{A})}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{d_{A}}\,\frac{1}{d_{A}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{A}}\sqrt{x_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{d_{A}}\int\sqrt{x}\,d\mu_{M}(x)\,.

Then, as proven by
[
Nechita 2007
, Theorem 5]
, the empirical spectral distribution given by
\mu_{M}
converges weakly almost surely to the Marchenko-Pastur distribution
\mu_{MP}

\displaystyle\mu_{MP}=\max\{1-c,0\}\delta_{0}+\frac{\sqrt{(x-a)(b-x)}}{2\pi x}\mathds{1}_{[a,b]}(x)dx\,,

(D.13)

where
a=(\sqrt{c}-1)^{2}
and
b=(\sqrt{c}+1)^{2}
and
c\in(0,\infty)
. More specifically, by taking
c=\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{B}/d_{A}=1
, the empirical distribution given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{M}\coloneq\frac{1}{d_{A}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{A}}\delta_{y_{i}}\,,

with
y_{i}=c\cdot x_{i}
weakly converges to

\displaystyle\mu_{MP}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{4-x}{x}}\mathds{1}_{[0,4]}(x)dx\,.

Since weak convergence implies convergence of integrals only against bounded continuous test functions (Portmanteau theorem, e.g.
[
Billingsley 1999
, Theorem 2.1]
), it cannot be applied directly to
x\mapsto\sqrt{x}
, which is unbounded on
[0,\infty)
. We therefore first use
[
Nechita 2007
, Theorem 6]
to control the largest eigenvalue, and hence to obtain an almost-sure bound on the support of
\mu_{M}
. In particular,

\displaystyle\max_{i}x_{i}=d_{A}\lambda_{\max}(\rho_{A})\xrightarrow{d_{A}\to\infty}4\,,

since
c=1
. Hence, for every
\varepsilon>0
, almost surely there exists
d_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
d_{A}\geq d_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{supp}(\mu_{M})\subset[0,4+\varepsilon]\,.

Let
g_{\varepsilon}
be a continuous, bounded real function such that

\displaystyle g_{\varepsilon}(x)=\sqrt{x}\qquad\text{for all }x\in[0,4+\varepsilon]\,.

Then, for all
d_{A}\geq d_{0}
,

\displaystyle\int\sqrt{x}\,d\mu_{M}(x)=\int g_{\varepsilon}(x)\,d\mu_{M}(x)\,.

By weak convergence of
\mu_{M}
to
\mu_{MP}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int g_{\varepsilon}(x)\,d\mu_{M}(x)\xrightarrow[d_{A}\to\infty]{a.s.}\int g_{\varepsilon}(x)\,d\mu_{MP}(x)\,.

Since
\mathsf{supp}(\mu_{MP})=[0,4]
, we also have

\displaystyle\int g_{\varepsilon}(x)\,d\mu_{MP}(x)=\int\sqrt{x}\,d\mu_{MP}(x)\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int\sqrt{x}d{\mu_{M}}(x)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{d_{A}\to\infty}\int\sqrt{x}d{\mu_{MP}}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{4}\sqrt{4-x}dx=\frac{8}{3\pi}\,.

Threfore, almost surely it holds that,

\displaystyle\Tr\sqrt{\rho_{A}}=\left(\frac{8}{3\pi}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{d_{A}}\,,

and hence,

\displaystyle H_{\text{min}}(A|B)_{|\psi\rangle}

\displaystyle=-\log d_{A}-2\log\left(\frac{8}{3\pi}\right)+o(1)

\displaystyle=-n_{A}+O(1)\,,

almost surely, since
d_{A}=2^{n_{A}}
.
Lastly, since in the informational case the conditional min-entropy is additive, one has that

\displaystyle H_{\text{min}}(A^{k}|B^{k})_{|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k}}=k\cdot H_{\text{min}}(A|B)_{|\psi\rangle}\,.

∎

Let us now bound the case in which the state is given by the Haar-subsystem state ensemble.

Lemma D.4

(Min-entropy of a Haar-subsystem random state)
.

Let

. For a state

drawn from the Haar-subsystem random ensemble

of Eq. (
D.1
), one has, almost surely,

H_{\min}(A^{k}|B^{k})_{|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k}}\geq-k\,m(n_{A})\,.

(D.14)

Proof.

Similarly to the proof of

\displaystyle H_{\text{min}}(A|B)_{|\psi\rangle}=-2\log\left(\Tr\sqrt{\rho_{A}}\right)\,,

where

Let us now prove the main theorem of this section. We characterize the computational min-entropy for a Haar random state (see Eq.(
D.1
)) and compare it with the information-theoretic one. This yields a large separation between the two entropies. The separation holds unconditionally when one is restricted to polynomially many copies and polynomially generated quantum channels.

Theorem D.5

(Lower bound on the computational min-entropy for pure states)
.

Fix an
A|B
bipartition with

and let
k\in\mathsf{poly}(n)
be the number of copies of a given state. Then, there exists a family of bipartite pure states

such that

\displaystyle H_{\min}(A^{k}|B^{k})_{|\psi_{n}\rangle^{\otimes k}}

\displaystyle=-k\,(n_{A}-O(1))\,,

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 1.53334pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A^{k}|B^{k})_{|\psi_{n}\rangle^{\otimes k}}

\displaystyle\geq-k\,m(n_{A})\,.

Proof.

Let
A\cong B
, where
A_{k}
denotes the space
A^{\otimes k}
and
B_{k}
denotes
B^{\otimes k}
, such that

and
d=d_{A}\cdot d_{B}=d_{A}^{2}
. Then,
T:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{B_{k}}_{n})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{A^{\prime}_{k}}_{n})
acts on

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A^{k}|B^{k})_{|\psi_{n}\rangle^{\otimes k}}

\displaystyle=-\log\left(\ d_{A}^{k}\cdot\max_{T}\bra{\Omega_{A_kA'_k}}(\mathrm{Id}_{A_{k}}\otimes T)(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|^{\otimes k})\ket{\Omega_{A_kA'_k}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\log\left(\ d_{A}^{k}\cdot\max_{T}F_{T}(|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k})\right)\,,

where
T:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{B_{k}}_{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{A^{\prime}_{k}}_{n})
is maximized w.r.t. the set of polynomially implementable quantum channels and
F_{T}(|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k})\coloneq\bra{\Omega_{A_kA'_k}}(\mathrm{Id}_{A_{k}}\otimes T)(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|^{\otimes k})\ket{\Omega_{A_kA'_k}}
.

Note that

corresponds to the following observable: given any efficient
T
, let us define a (non-uniform) quantum poly time (
\mathsf{QPT}
) distinguisher

that, on input

|\psi\rangle

and measures the two-outcome POVM

Then

\displaystyle\Pr[\mathcal{D}_{T}((|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k})=1]=F_{T}((|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k})\,.

Let us now calculate the difference in the expected value of the observable

for the following ensembles

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{Haar}}=\left\{|\psi_{AB}\rangle\,,\qquad|\psi_{AB}\rangle\sim\mathsf{Haar}(n)\right\}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{m}=\left\{U_{\pi}|0\rangle^{n-m}|\phi_{m}\rangle\,:\,U_{\pi}\in P\,,|\phi_{m}\rangle\sim\mathsf{Haar}(m)\right\}\,,

where
\mathcal{E}_{m}
is a Haar-subsystem state ensemble, where

is the set of unitaries implementing a random permutation on
n
-bit strings
\pi:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}^{n}

.

Let us now bound the difference between the expected values,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{Haar}}}F_{T}(|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k})-\mathbb{E}_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{m}}F_{T}(|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k})\right|

\displaystyle\qquad=\left|\Tr\left[M_{T}\left(\mathbb{E}_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{Haar}}}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|^{\otimes k})-\mathbb{E}_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{m}}\left(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|^{\otimes k}\right)\right)\right]\right|

with
M_{T}\coloneq(\mathrm{Id}_{A_{k}}\otimes T^{*})(|\Omega_{AA^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\Omega_{AA^{\prime}}|)
, where the Haar average of

can be described as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{Haar}}}\left[|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|^{\otimes k}\right]=\frac{\Pi^{(n,k)}_{\mathsf{sym}}}{\tr\left(\Pi^{(n,k)}_{\mathsf{sym}}\right)}=\frac{\Pi^{(n,k)}_{\mathsf{sym}}}{\binom{d+k-1}{k}}\,,

where

is the projector onto the symmetric subspace (see for example

, a sample can be described equivalently as follows: pick a subset

of cardinality

uniformly at random, and then draw

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{|\phi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{m}}\left[|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|^{\otimes k}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{S:|S|=d_{S}}\left[\frac{\Pi_{\mathsf{Sym}}^{(S,k)}}{\Tr(\Pi_\mathsf{Sym}^{(S,k)})}\right]\,.

Moreover, let us fix a subset of basis labels

\displaystyle(a,b)\in S\iff|a\rangle_{A}\otimes|b\rangle_{B}\in\mathcal{H}_{S}\,,

S_{B}\subset[d_{B}]
of

\displaystyle S_{B}\coloneq\left\{b\in[d_{B}]\,:\,\exists a\in[d_{A}]\;\text{s.t.}\;(a,b)\in S\right\},\quad\Pi_{S_{B}}\coloneq\sum_{b\in S_{B}}|b\rangle\langle b|\,.

Then,

\displaystyle\Pi_{S}=\sum_{(a,b)\in S}|ab\rangle\langle ab|\leq\sum_{b\in S_{B}}\sum_{a\in[d_{A}]}|ab\rangle\langle ab|=\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\Pi_{S_{B}}\,.

Moreover, since

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathsf{Sym}}^{(S,k)}\leq\mathds{1}_{A_{k}}\otimes\Pi^{\otimes k}_{S_{B}}\,.

with respect to the Haar-random ensemble, we first use the fact that it is computationally indistinguishable from the Haar-subsystem random enemble to estimate a bound
\delta

holds with overwhelming probability for computationally bounded obsevers via Markov’s inequality.

Let us first evaluate the asymptotic behavior of the expectation values of the Haar-random ensemble introduced in Eq. (
D.1
).

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{Haar}}}[F_{T}(|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k})]

\displaystyle=\frac{k!}{d^{k}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{k^{2}}{d}\right)\right)\Tr\left[M_{T}\Pi^{(n,k)}_{\mathsf{sym}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{k!}{d^{k}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{k^{2}}{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{k!}{d^{k}}\right)\,.

Similarly, the expected value of
F_{T}
for the Haar-susbsytem ensemble is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{m}}[F_{T}(|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k})]

\displaystyle=\frac{k!}{d_{S}^{k}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{k^{2}}{d_{S}}\right)\right)\mathbb{E}_{S:|S|=d_{S}}\Tr\left[M_{T}\Pi^{(S,k)}_{\mathsf{sym}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{k!}{d_{S}^{k}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{k^{2}}{d_{S}}\right)\right)\mathbb{E}_{S:|S|=d_{S}}\Tr\left[M_{T}\left(\mathds{1}_{A_{k}}\otimes\Pi^{\otimes k}_{S_{B}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{k!}{d_{A}^{k}d_{S}^{k}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{k^{2}}{d_{S}}\right)\right)\mathbb{E}_{S:|S|=d_{S}}\Tr\left[\Pi^{\otimes k}_{S_{B}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{k!}{d_{A}^{k}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{k^{2}}{d_{S}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{k!}{d_{A}^{k}}\right)\,,

where in the third line we use the fact that
\Pi_{\mathsf{Sym}}^{(S,k)}\leq\mathds{1}_{A_{k}}\otimes\Pi^{\otimes k}_{S_{B}}

\tr_{A_{k}}[M_{T}]=\frac{\mathds{1}_{B_{k}}}{d_{A}^{k}}

Using the triangle inequality and the above asymptomatic inequalities, we can see that:

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{Haar}}}F_{T}(|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k})-\mathbb{E}_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{m}}F_{T}(|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k})\right|=O\left(\frac{k!}{d_{A}^{k}}\right)\,.

Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{Haar}}}F_{T}(|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k})=\mathbb{E}_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{m}}F_{T}(|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k})+O\left(\frac{k!}{d_{A}^{k}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{m}}F_{T}(|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k})=\frac{k!}{d_{A}^{k}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{k^{2}}{d_{S}}\right)\right)\,.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{Haar}}}F_{T}(|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k})

One can calculate the Haar moment bound for the

\displaystyle\Pr_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{Haar}}}\left[F_{T}\!\left(|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k}\right)\geq\delta\right]

\displaystyle=\Pr_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{Haar}}}\left[\left(F_{T}\!\left(|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k}\right)\right)^{t}\geq\delta^{t}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{|\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{Haar}}}\left[\left(F_{T}\!\left(|\psi\rangle^{\otimes k}\right)\right)^{t}\right]}{\delta^{t}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(kt)!}{2^{kt(n_{A}+\,m(n_{A}))}}\left(1+O\!\left(\frac{(kt)^{2}}{d}\right)\right).

Let us now fix a polynomial circuit-size bound

\displaystyle|\mathsf{QPT}_{\leq p}|\leq 2^{r(n)}.

Since

Definition D.6

(Generalized Hilbert-Schmidt ensemble (GHSE)

Proof.

Similarly to the proof of

A\cong B

d_{A_{k}}=d^{k}_{A}=2^{k\cdot n_{A}}
and
d_{n}=d_{A}\cdot d_{B}=d^{2}_{A}
. Then,
T:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{B_{k}}_{n})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{A^{\prime}_{k}}_{n})
acts on
k
copies. Given the definition of the computational min-entropy,

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A^{k}|B^{k})_{\rho^{\otimes k}}

\displaystyle=-\log\left(\ d_{A}^{k}\cdot\max_{T}\bra{\Omega_{A_kA'_k}}(\mathrm{Id}_{A_{k}}\otimes T)(\rho^{\otimes k})\ket{\Omega_{A_kA'_k}}\right)

(D.20)

\displaystyle=-\log\left(\ d_{A}^{k}\cdot\max_{T}F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k})\right)\;,

(D.21)

where
T:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{B_{k}}_{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{A^{\prime}_{k}}_{n})
is maximized over the set of polynomially implementable quantum channels and
F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k})\coloneq\bra{\Omega_{A_kA'_k}}(\mathrm{Id}_{A_{k}}\otimes T)(\rho^{\otimes k})\ket{\Omega_{A_kA'_k}}
.

Note that
F_{T}(\cdot)
corresponds to the following observable: given any efficient
T
, let us define a (non-uniform) QPT distinguisher
\mathcal{D}_{T}
that, on input
k
copies of an unknown mixed state
\rho
, applies
(\mathrm{Id}\otimes T)
and measures the two-outcome POVM
\{\ket{\Omega}\!\bra{\Omega},\,\mathds{1}-\ket{\Omega}\!\bra{\Omega}\}
on
AA^{\prime}
, outputting
1
if and only if the outcome is
\ket{\Omega}
.
Then

\displaystyle\Pr[\mathcal{D}_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k})=1]=F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k})\,.

Let us now bound the difference between the expected values of
F_{T}
for the Haar random and Haar subsystem ensembles introduced in Eq. (
D.1
). As stated in
[
Bansal et al. 2025
, Theorem D.1]
, the
k
-th moment of the GHSE ensemble can be constructed as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{E}_{\rho\sim\eta_{n,m}}}(\rho^{\otimes k})=\mathbb{E}_{\psi\in\mathsf{Haar}(d_{n}d_{m})}[\tr_{m}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)^{\otimes k}]=\frac{(d_{n}d_{m}-1)!}{(d_{n}d_{m}+k-1)!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}d_{m}^{\text{cycles}(\pi)}\hat{\pi}_{n}\,,

(D.22)

where
S_{k}
is the symmetric group of degree
k
, and
\hat{\pi}_{n}
denotes the permutation unitary acting on
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{\otimes k}
. The function
\text{cycles}(\pi)
denotes the number of disjoint cycles in the cycle decomposition of
\pi\in S_{k}
, counting fixed points as cycles of length
1
. In particular,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in S_{k}\\
\pi\neq\mathds{1}\end{subarray}}d_{m}^{\text{cycles}(\pi)}=\frac{(d_{m}+k-1)!}{(d_{m}-1)!}-d_{m}^{k}.

(D.23)

Using
M_{T}\coloneq(\mathrm{Id}_{A_{k}}\otimes T^{*})(|\Omega_{A_{k}A^{\prime}_{k}}\rangle\langle\Omega_{A_{k}A^{\prime}_{k}}|)
, we find that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\eta_{n,m}}F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k})-F_{T}\left(\frac{\mathds{1}_{n}^{\otimes k}}{d_{n}^{k}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\quad=\left|\Tr\left[M_{T}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{E}_{\rho\sim\eta_{n,m}}}(\rho^{\otimes k})-\frac{\mathds{1}_{n}^{\otimes k}}{d_{n}^{k}}\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\quad=\left|\Tr\left[M_{T}\left(\frac{1}{d_{n}^{k}}\left(-\frac{k(k-1)}{2d_{n}d_{m}}+O\left(\frac{k^{4}}{d_{n}^{2}d_{m}^{2}}\right)\right)\mathds{1}_{n}^{\otimes k}\right.\right.\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\,\left.\left.+\frac{1}{(d_{n}d_{m})^{k}}\left(1-\frac{k(k-1)}{2d_{n}d_{m}}+O\left(\frac{k^{4}}{d_{n}^{2}d_{m}^{2}}\right)\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in S_{k}\\
\pi\neq\mathds{1}\end{subarray}}d_{m}^{\text{cycles}(\pi)}\hat{\pi}_{n}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{d_{n}^{k}}\left(\frac{k(k-1)}{2d_{n}d_{m}}+O\left(\frac{k^{4}}{d_{n}^{2}d_{m}^{2}}\right)\right)\Tr(M_T)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\,+\frac{1}{(d_{n}d_{m})^{k}}\left(1+\frac{k(k-1)}{2d_{n}d_{m}}+O\left(\frac{k^{4}}{d_{n}^{2}d_{m}^{2}}\right)\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in S_{k}\\
\pi\neq\mathds{1}\end{subarray}}d_{m}^{\text{cycles}(\pi)}|\Tr(M_T \hat{\pi}_n)|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{d_{n}^{k}}\left(\frac{k(k-1)}{2d_{n}d_{m}}+O\left(\frac{k^{4}}{d_{n}^{2}d_{m}^{2}}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{(d_{n}d_{m})^{k}}\left(1+\frac{k(k-1)}{2d_{n}d_{m}}+O\left(\frac{k^{4}}{d_{n}^{2}d_{m}^{2}}\right)\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in S_{k}\\
\pi\neq\mathds{1}\end{subarray}}d_{m}^{\text{cycles}(\pi)}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{d_{n}^{k}}\left(\frac{k(k-1)}{2d_{n}d_{m}}+O\left(\frac{k^{4}}{d_{n}^{2}d_{m}^{2}}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{(d_{n}d_{m})^{k}}\left(1+\frac{k(k-1)}{2d_{n}d_{m}}+O\left(\frac{k^{4}}{d_{n}^{2}d_{m}^{2}}\right)\right)\left(\frac{(d_{m}+k-1)!}{(d_{m}-1)!}-d_{m}^{k}\right)\,.

The second equality follows from splitting the sum over
S_{k}
into the identity permutation and the sum over non-identity permutations, as in
[
Bansal et al. 2025
, Theorem D.1]
. The first inequality follows from applying the triangle inequality and the second one from the fact that
|\Tr(M_T \hat{\pi}_n)|\leq\Tr(M_T)=1
. The last equality follows by using Eq. (
D.23
). Finally, since

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(d_{m}+k-1)!}{(d_{m}-1)!}-d_{m}^{k}\right)=d_{m}^{k}\left(\frac{k(k-1)}{2d_{m}}+O\left(\frac{k^{4}}{d_{m}^{2}}\right)\right)\,,

it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\eta_{n,m}}F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k})-F_{T}\left(\frac{\mathds{1}^{\otimes k}}{d^{k}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{d_{n}^{k}}\left(\frac{k(k-1)}{2d_{n}d_{m}}+O\left(\frac{k^{4}}{d_{n}^{2}d_{m}^{2}}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{d_{n}^{k}}\left(1+\frac{k(k-1)}{2d_{n}d_{m}}+O\left(\frac{k^{4}}{d_{n}^{2}d_{m}^{2}}\right)\right)\left(\frac{k(k-1)}{2d_{m}}+O\left(\frac{k^{4}}{d_{m}^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{d_{n}^{k}}\left(\frac{k(k-1)}{2d_{n}d_{m}}+\frac{k(k-1)}{2d_{m}}\right)+O\left(\frac{k^{4}}{d_{n}^{k}d_{m}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=O\left(\frac{k^{2}}{d_{n}^{k}d_{m}}\right)\,.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\eta_{n,m}}F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k})\leq F_{T}\left(\frac{\mathds{1}^{\otimes k}}{d_{n}^{k}}\right)+O\left(\frac{k^{2}}{d_{n}^{k}d_{m}}\right)\,.

On the other hand, by using the defintion of the (informational) min-entropy, it follows that for any efficient CPTP map
T

F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k})\ \leq\ \frac{2^{-H_{\min}(A^{k}|B^{k})_{\rho^{\otimes k}}}}{d_{A}^{k}}.

(D.24)

Let us now evalaute the conditional min-entropy for
\rho=\frac{\mathds{1}_{AB}}{d_{A}d_{B}}
with
d_{n}=d_{A}\cdot d_{B}
. Then, it directly follows that

\displaystyle H_{\min}(A|B)_{\frac{\mathds{1}_{AB}}{d_{A}d_{B}}}=\log d_{A}\,.

By the additivity of the conditional min-entropy one has that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\eta_{n,m}}F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{d_{n}^{k}}+O\left(\frac{k^{2}}{d_{n}^{k}d_{m}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\varepsilon}{d_{n}^{k}}\,,

for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
satisfying
\varepsilon\geq Ck^{2}/d_{m}
for some constant
C\geq 0
.

Let us now prove that there exists a state in the GHSE ensemble that satisfies this bound with high probability. To do so, we define the
t
-th moment of the distribution

\displaystyle(F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k}))^{t}=\Tr[M_{T}\rho^{\otimes k}]^{t}=\Tr\left(M_{T}^{\otimes t}\rho^{\otimes kt}\right)

By Markov’s inequality, one has that for any moment
t\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\Pr_{\rho\sim\eta_{n,m}}\left[F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k})\geq\delta\right]=\Pr_{\rho\sim\eta_{n,m}}\left[(F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k}))^{t}\geq\delta^{t}\right]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\eta_{n,m}}(F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k}))^{t}}{\delta^{t}}\,.

Let us first calculate
\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\eta_{n,m}}(F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k}))^{t}
. Taking Eq.(
D.22
), one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\eta_{n,m}}(F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k}))^{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{(d_{n}d_{m}-1)!}{(d_{n}d_{m}+kt-1)!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{kt}}d_{m}^{\text{cycles}(\pi)}\Tr(M_T^{\otimes t}\hat{\pi}_n)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(d_{n}d_{m}-1)!}{(d_{n}d_{m}+kt-1)!}\frac{(d_{m}+kt-1)!}{(d_{m}-1)!}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{d_{n}^{kt}}\exp\left(\frac{kt(kt-1)}{2d_{m}}\right)\,.

Then, by taking
\delta=(1+\varepsilon)/d^{k}_{n}
, one has that for any moment
t\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\Pr_{\rho\sim\eta_{n,m}}\left[F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k})\geq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)}{d^{k}_{n}}\right]\leq\frac{1}{(1+\varepsilon)^{t}}\cdot\exp\left(\frac{kt(kt-1)}{2d_{m}}\right)\leq\exp\left(\frac{k^{2}t^{2}}{2d_{m}}-t\log(1+\varepsilon)\right)\,.

Fixing
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and taking

\displaystyle t\coloneq\left\lfloor\frac{d_{m}}{k^{2}}\log(1+\varepsilon)\right\rfloor\geq 1\,,

one gets

\displaystyle\Pr_{\rho\sim\eta_{n,m}}\left[F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k})\geq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)}{d^{k}_{n}}\right]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{d_{m}}{2k^{2}}\log(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\right)\,.

Let us now fix a polynomial circuit-size bound
p(n)
and let
\mathsf{QPT}_{\leq p}
denote the set of channels implementable by circuits of size at most
p(n)
. By
, there exists a polynomial
r(n)
such that
|\mathsf{QPT}_{\leq p}|\leq 2^{r(n)}
.
Therefore, by taking the union bound

\displaystyle\Pr_{\rho\sim\eta_{n,m}}\left[\exists T\in\mathsf{QPT}_{\leq p}\,:\;F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k})\geq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)}{d^{k}_{n}}\right]\leq 2^{r(n)}\exp\left(-\frac{d_{m}}{2k^{2}}\log(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\right)\,.

Let us now fix the variable
\varepsilon
such that

\displaystyle\varepsilon(n)\coloneq 2k(n)\sqrt{\frac{2(r(n)+n)\ln 2}{d_{m(n)}}}\,.

Since
m(n)=\omega(\log n)
and
k(n),r(n)=\mathsf{poly}(n)
,
\varepsilon
satisfies for a sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
that
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and
\varepsilon\geq Ck^{2}/d_{m}
. Moreover, using the standard bound
\log(1+\varepsilon)\geq\varepsilon/2
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d_{m}}{2k^{2}}\log(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\geq\frac{d_{m}\varepsilon^{2}}{8k^{2}}=(r(n)+n)\ln 2\,.

Hence,

\displaystyle\Pr_{\rho\sim\eta_{n,m}}\left[\forall T\in\mathsf{QPT}_{\leq p}\,:\;F_{T}(\rho^{\otimes k})\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)}{d^{k}_{n}}\right]\geq 1-2^{-n}\,.

Therefore, for
k=\mathsf{poly}(n)
there exists a state
\tilde{\rho}\in\eta_{n,m}
such that for every polynomially implementable quantum channel
\mathsf{QPT}_{\leq p}\ni T:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{B}_{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{A^{\prime}}_{n})
with at most
p(n)
gates,

\displaystyle F_{T}(\tilde{\rho}^{\otimes k})\leq\frac{1+\varepsilon}{d_{n}^{k}}\,.

Finally, by the definition of the computational min-entropy,

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A^{k}|B^{k})_{\tilde{\rho}^{\otimes k}}

\displaystyle\geq k\cdot n_{A}-\log(1+\varepsilon)\geq k\cdot n_{A}-\mathsf{negl}(n)\,.

Let us now calculate the (informational) conditional min-entropy of a state sampled from the GHSE. Since
H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}\leq H(A|B)_{\rho}
and
H(A|B)_{\rho}=H(AB)_{\rho}-H(B)_{\rho}
, where
H(\cdot)
is the von-Neumann entropy (see
[
Tomamichel 2016
, Definition 5.1]
), it follows that

\displaystyle H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}\leq H(M)_{\ket{\psi}}-H(B)_{\ket{\psi}}\,,

where we have used the fact that
H(M)_{\ket{\psi}}=H(AB)_{\ket{\psi}}
for the bipartite state
|\psi\rangle\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}d_{B}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{m}}
as defined in
. Then, we used the fact that the von Neumann entropy is upper bounded by the rank, obtaining

\displaystyle H(M)_{\ket{\psi}}\leq\log d_{m}=m

almost surely. On the other hand, as proven in
[
Hayden et al. 2006
, Lemma II.4]
, since
d_{m}=\omega(\log n)

\displaystyle H(B)_{\ket{\psi}}\geq n_{A}-\mathsf{negl}(n)

almost surely as well.
Then, since the conditional min-entropy is additive and
k=\mathsf{poly}(n)
,

\displaystyle H_{\text{min}}(A^{k}|B^{k})_{\rho^{\otimes k}}\leq-k\cdot\left(n_{A}-m\right)+\mathsf{negl}(n)\,.

∎

Then, given
k=\mathsf{poly}(n)
copies, there exists a family of mixed states
\{\rho_{n,m}\}_{n}
whose computational conditional min-entropy is asymptotically maximal:

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A^{k}|B^{k})_{\rho_{n,m}^{\otimes k}}\geq k\cdot n_{A}-\mathsf{negl}(n),

while its information-theoretic conditional min-entropy remains highly negative:

\displaystyle H_{\text{min}}(A^{k}|B^{k})_{\rho_{n,m}^{\otimes k}}\leq-k\cdot\bigl(n_{A}-m(n_{A})\bigr)+\mathsf{negl}(n).

By subadditivity of
\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A|B)
and additivity of the ordinary conditional min-entropy, this yields the corresponding per-copy bounds

\displaystyle\overset{\smash{\text{\tiny\hskip 2.0417pt\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{c}}}}{H}_{\mathrm{min}}(A|B)_{\rho_{n,m}}\geq n_{A}-\mathsf{negl}(n),\qquad H_{\text{min}}(A|B)_{\rho_{n,m}}\leq-\bigl(n_{A}-m(n_{A})\bigr)+\mathsf{negl}(n).

Thus, in the mixed-state regime, the computational conditional min-entropy is nearly maximal even though the information-theoretic conditional min-entropy remains highly negative, yielding an almost maximal separation between operationally accessible and information-theoretic side information.
