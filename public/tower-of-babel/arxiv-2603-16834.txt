Bohr phenomenon for analytic and harmonic mappings on shifted disks
Vasudevarao Allu, Raju Biswas and Rajib Mandal

Abstract
. The primary objective of this paper is to establish several sharp results concerning the Bohr inequality, the refined Bohr inequality, and the improved Bohr inequality for the classes of analytic functions and harmonic mappings defined on the shifted disks Ω γ = { z ∈ ℂ : | z + γ 1 − γ | < 1 1 − γ } for γ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) . \displaystyle\Omega_{\gamma}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right|<\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\right\}\quad\text{for}\quad\gamma\in[0,1).

Let
\mathcal{B}(\Omega)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}(\Omega):f(\Omega)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{D}}\right\}

[
28

\mathcal{B}(\Omega)
is defined to be the number
B_{\Omega}\in(0,1)
such

\displaystyle B_{\Omega}=\sup\left\{\rho\in(0,1):M_{f}(\rho)\leq 1\;\text{for}\;f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega),z\in\mathbb{D}\right\},

where
M_{f}(\rho)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|\rho^{n}

is the majorant series corresponding to the analytic functions

Theorem A
.

(The Classical Bohr Theorem) If

M_{f}(\rho)\leq 1
for

. The number

is best possible.

It should be noted that for

, where

To check this, we consider the function
\varphi_{r}(z)=(r-z)/(1-rz)

M_{\varphi_{r}}(\rho)>1
if, and only if,
\rho>1/(1+2r)
.
This shows that
1/3
is optimal for

.

In 2010, Fournier and Ruscheweyh
[
28
]
extended the concept of Bohr’s inequality as follows:

Theorem B
.

[
28
, Theorem 1]
For
\gamma\in[0,1)
, let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}z^{n}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|\rho^{n}\leq 1\;\;\text{ for}\;\;\rho\leq\rho_{\gamma}:=\frac{1+\gamma}{3+\gamma}.

Also
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|\rho_{\gamma}^{n}=1
holds for a function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
if, and only if,
f(z)=c
with
|c|=1
.

In
2021
, Evdoridis
et al.
[
27
]
obtained an improved version of Theorem B as follows:

Theorem C
.

[
27
, Theorem 1]
For
\gamma\in[0,1)
, let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}z^{n}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|\rho^{n}+\frac{8}{9}\left(\frac{S_{\rho(1-\gamma)}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\;\;\text{ for}\;\;\rho\leq\rho_{\gamma}:=\frac{1+\gamma}{3+\gamma},

where the area of the image of the disk
\mathbb{D}(0;\rho)
under the mapping
f
is denoted by
S_{\rho}
. Also, the inequality result is strict unless
f
is a constant function. The numbers
8/9
and
(1+\gamma)/(3+\gamma)
cannot be replaced by a larger value.

For an analytic function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}z^{n}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we write

\displaystyle\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{\rho}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}\rho^{2n},\quad\text{where}\quad f_{0}(z)=f(z)-f(0).

Theorem D

[

For

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|\rho^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|\alpha_{0}|}+\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\right)||f_{0}||_{\rho}\leq 1\;\;\text{ for}\;\;\rho\leq\rho_{0}:=\rho_{\gamma}:=\frac{1+\gamma}{3+\gamma},

and the number
\rho_{0}
cannot be improved.

Let
f=u+iv
be a complex-valued function in a simply connected domain
\Omega
.
If
f
satisfies the Laplace equation
\Delta f=4f_{z\overline{z}}=0
, then it is harmonic in
\Omega
,
i.e.
,
u
and
v
are real harmonic in
\Omega
.
It should be noted that the canonical representation of every harmonic mapping
f
is
f=h+\overline{g}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic in
\Omega
. This representation is unique
up to an additive constant (see
[
25
]
).
The inverse function theorem, as well as a result established by Lewy
[
39
]
, indicates that a harmonic function
f
is locally univalent within the domain
\Omega
if, and only if,
the Jacobian of f, defined by
J_{f}(z)=|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}
, retains a non-zero value in
\Omega
.
A locally univalent function
f
is sense-preserving when
J_{f}(z)>0
in
\Omega
. Thus, a harmonic mapping
f
is locally univalent and sense-preserving
in
\Omega
if, and only if,
J_{f}(z)>0
in
\Omega
.
This is equivalent to the conditions that
h^{\prime}\not=0
in
\Omega
and the dilatation
\omega_{f}:=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
of
f
has the property that
|\omega_{f}|<1
in
\Omega
(see
[
22
,
25
,
39
]
).

A locally univalent and sense-preserving harmonic mapping
f=h+\overline{g}
on a domain
\Omega
is said to be a
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping if it satisfies the condition
|\omega_{f}(z)|\leq k<1
for all
z\in\Omega
, where
K=(1+k)/(1-k)\geq 1
(see
[
32
,
45
]
).
It is evident that the limit of
k
approaching
1
correlates with the scenario where
K
is approaching
+\infty
.

[

2.
Main results

In the shifted disk
\Omega_{\gamma}
, Evdoridis
et al.

[
27
]
have recently studied sharply improved versions of the Bohr inequality for analytic and harmonic mappings,
with its restriction to the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. Subsequently, Ahamed
et al.

[
6
]
have established a number of sharply improved and refined versions of Bohr’s inequality for analytic functions in
\Omega_{\gamma}
, with the restriction to the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.

In this paper, we define the Bohr radius for the class
\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
, motivated by Fournier and Ruscheweyh
[
28
]
. It is defined as the number

B_{\Omega_{\gamma}}\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle B_{\Omega_{\gamma}}=\sup\left\{\rho\in(0,1):M_{f}(\rho)\leq 1\;\text{for}\;f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma}),z\in\Omega_{\gamma}\right\},

where
M_{f}(\rho)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|\left(\rho/(1-\gamma)\right)^{n}
with
|\gamma+(1-\gamma)z|=\rho
, is the majorant series associated with the analytic functions
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
. It is well known that
B_{\mathbb{D}}=1/3
when
\Omega_{\gamma}=\mathbb{D}
.

For the class of analytic and harmonic mapping in
\Omega_{\gamma}
, we obtain sharply Bohr radius, refined Bohr radius, and improved Bohr radius in this paper, without its restriction to the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.

The following are key lemmas of this paper and will be used to prove the main results.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
48
]
For
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left|f^{(n)}(\alpha)\right|}{n!}\leq\frac{1-|f(\alpha)|^{2}}{(1-|\alpha|)^{n-1}(1-|\alpha|^{2})}\;\;\text{for each}\;\;n\geq 1\;\;\text{and}\;\;\alpha\in\mathbb{D}.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
27
]
Suppose that
h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
are two analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|g’(z)|\leq k|h’(z)|
in
\mathbb{D}
and for some
k\in[0,1)
with
|h(z)|\leq 1
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|b_{n}|^{2}\rho^{2n}\leq k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}\rho^{2n}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|^{2}\rho^{n}\leq k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\rho^{n}\;\;\text{for}\;\;|z|=\rho<1.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}
are two analytic functions in
\Omega_{\gamma}
such that
|g’(z)|\leq k|h’(z)|
in
\Omega_{\gamma}
and for some
k\in[0,1)
with
|h(z)|\leq 1
. Then, for
\left|(1-\gamma)z+\gamma\right|=\rho<1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\frac{|b_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}\rho^{2n}\leq k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\frac{|a_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}\rho^{2n}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|b_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}\rho^{n}\leq k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}\rho^{n}.

Proof.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega_{\gamma}
be a function defined by
\Phi(z)=(z-\gamma)/(1-\gamma)
. Since
h,g:\Omega_{\gamma}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, so
the composition
\varphi_{1}=h\circ\Phi
and
\varphi_{2}=g\circ\Phi
are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
.
Since
z\in\Omega_{\gamma}
, so we write
z=(\xi-\gamma)/(1-\gamma)
for
\xi\in\mathbb{D}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(\xi)=h\left(\frac{\xi-\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\xi^{n}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\varphi_{2}(\xi)=g\left(\frac{\xi-\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\xi^{n}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for}\;\;\xi\in\mathbb{D}.

Since

\displaystyle\left|g’\left(\frac{\xi-\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)\right|\leq k\left|h’\left(\frac{\xi-\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)\right|\;\text{for}\;\xi\in\mathbb{D},

we have

|\varphi_{2}’(\xi)|\leq k|\varphi_{1}’(\xi)|
for
\xi\in\mathbb{D}
. In light of Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\frac{|b_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}\rho^{2n}\leq k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\frac{|a_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}\rho^{2n}\;\text{and}\;\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|b_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}\rho^{n}\leq k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}\rho^{n}

for
|\xi|=\rho<1
,
i.e.
,
|\gamma+(1-\gamma)z|=\rho<1
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f
be analytic in
\Omega_{\gamma}
, bounded by
1
with the series expansion
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}
in
\Omega_{\gamma}
. Then,
|a_{n}|\leq(1-\gamma)^{n}(1-|a_{0}|^{2})
for
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega_{\gamma}
be a function defined by
\Phi(z)=(z-\gamma)/(1-\gamma)
. Since
f:\Omega_{\gamma}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
is analytic, so
the composition
g=f\circ\Phi
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
. Thus,

\displaystyle g(z)=f\left(\frac{z-\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}z^{n}

with
g(0)=a_{0}
and

\displaystyle a_{n}=\frac{g^{(n)}(0)}{n!}(1-\gamma)^{n}.

In view of Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle|a_{n}|\leq(1-\gamma)^{n}\left(1-|g(0)|^{2}\right)=(1-\gamma)^{n}\left(1-|a_{0}|^{2}\right).

∎

For the class of analytic functions
\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
, we obtain a sharp refined version of Bohr inequality as follows:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f
be an analytic function in
\Omega_{\gamma}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
on
\Omega_{\gamma}
. If
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}
in
\Omega_{\gamma}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\rho^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}\rho^{2n}\leq 1

(2.1)

for
\left|(1-\gamma)z+\gamma\right|=\rho\leq\rho_{0}=1/3
. The number
\rho_{0}
is the best possible.

Note that in Theorem
2.1
we obtain
\rho_{0}=1/3
, which is same as the classical Bohr radius
1/3
.

In the following, we obtain a sharp Bohr inequality for harmonic mapping on the shifted disk
\Omega_{\gamma}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be harmonic mapping in
\Omega_{\gamma}
with
|h(z)|\leq 1
on
\Omega_{\gamma}
. If
h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}
in
\Omega_{\gamma}
and
|g’(z)|\leq k|h’(z)|
for some
k\in[0,1)
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\rho^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|b_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\rho^{n}\leq 1\;\text{for}\;\left|(1-\gamma)z+\gamma\right|=\rho\leq\rho_{0}=1/(2k+3).

The number
\rho_{0}
is the best possible.

Letting
k\to 1
in Theorem
2.2
, we obtain the following sharp harmonic analogues of the classical Bohr inequality on the shifted disk
\Omega_{\gamma}
.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be harmonic mapping in
\Omega_{\gamma}
with
|h(z)|\leq 1
on
\Omega_{\gamma}
. If
h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}
,
g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}
in
\Omega_{\gamma}
and

is sense-preserving in

, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\rho^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|b_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\rho^{n}\leq 1\;\text{for}\;\left|(1-\gamma)z+\gamma\right|=\rho\leq\rho_{0}=1/5.

The number
\rho_{0}
is the best possible.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be harmonic mapping in
\Omega_{\gamma}
with
|h(z)|\leq 1
on
\Omega_{\gamma}
. If
h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}
,
g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}
in
\Omega_{\gamma}
and
|g’(z)|\leq k|h’(z)|
for some

, then

\rho>\rho_{0}=1/(2k+3)
, we have

\displaystyle F_{3}(a,\rho)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle F_{3}(a,\rho_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1+a)(1+k)}{3+2k-a}+\frac{(2k+4)^{2}(2k+2)^{2}}{8(2k+3)^{2}}\frac{(1-a)(1+a)^{2}(3+2k)^{2}}{(3+2k+a)^{2}(3+2k-a)^{2}}-1.

It is evident that
F_{3}(a,\rho_{0})\to 0
as
a\to 1
. Hence,
1+(1-a)F_{3}(a,\rho)>1
for
\rho>\rho_{0}
. This shows that
\rho_{0}
is the best possible. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Let
h(z)
be analytic on
\Omega_{\gamma}
with
|h(z)|\leq 1
. In view of Lemma
2.4
, we have
|a_{n}|\leq(1-\gamma)^{n}(1-|a_{0}|^{2})
for
n\geq 1
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\rho^{n}\leq|a_{0}|+\frac{(1-|a_{0}|^{2})\rho}{1-\rho}.

(3.8)

By a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
2.2
, Lemma
2.3
and
|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq k|h^{\prime}(z)|
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|b_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\rho^{n}\leq\frac{k(1-|a_{0}|^{2})\rho}{(1-\rho)}\;\;\text{for}\;\left|z(1-\gamma)+\gamma\right|=\rho.

(3.9)

In view of Lemma
2.3
and the condition
|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq k|h^{\prime}(z)|
gives that for
\left|(1-\gamma)z+\gamma\right|=\rho
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\frac{|b_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}\rho^{2n}\leq k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\frac{|a_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}\rho^{2n}

(3.10)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\frac{|b_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\rho^{2n}\leq\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\frac{|b_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}\rho^{2n}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\rho^{2n}\right)^{1/2}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\frac{|a_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}\rho^{2n}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{\rho^{2}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\leq\frac{k\left(1-|a_{0}|^{2}\right)\rho^{2}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}.

It is well known that, for analytic functions
\varphi_{1}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
\varphi_{2}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
with
\phi(z)=\varphi_{1}(z)+\overline{\varphi_{2}(z)}
, the
area
S_{\rho}
of the image
\phi\left(|z|<\rho\right)
(see
[
25
, Chapter 7]
) is given by

\displaystyle\frac{S_{\rho}}{\pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|z|<\rho\end{subarray}}\left(|\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|\varphi_{2}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\right)dxdy=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left(|a_{n}|^{2}-|b_{n}|^{2}\right)\rho^{2n}.

Since
z\in\Omega_{\gamma}
, so we write
z=(\xi-\gamma)/(1-\gamma)
for
\xi=\xi_{1}+i\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{D}
and the Jacobian
J_{f}
of
f
is given by

\displaystyle J_{f}\left(\frac{\xi-\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)=\left|h^{\prime}\left(\frac{\xi-\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)\right|^{2}-\left|g^{\prime}\left(\frac{\xi-\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)\right|^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle h\left(\frac{\xi-\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\xi^{n}\;\text{and}\;g\left(\frac{\xi-\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\xi^{n}\;\text{for}\;\xi\in\mathbb{D}.

Thus, the area of the image
f\left(|\xi|<\rho\right)
,
i.e.
,
f\left(|z+\gamma/(1-\gamma)|<\rho/(1-\gamma)\right)
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{S_{\rho}^{\gamma}(f)}{\pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\xi|<\rho\end{subarray}}\left(\left|h^{\prime}\left(\frac{\xi-\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)\right|^{2}-\left|g^{\prime}\left(\frac{\xi-\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)\right|^{2}\right)d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

(3.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left(\frac{|a_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}-\frac{|b_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}\right)\rho^{2n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left(\frac{|a_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}+\frac{|b_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\right)\left(\frac{|a_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}-\frac{|b_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\right)\rho^{2n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\frac{|a_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\left(\frac{|a_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}+\frac{|b_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\right)\rho^{2n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|a_{0}|^{2})^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\left(1+k\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\rho^{2n}=\frac{(1+k)(1-|a_{0}|^{2})^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\frac{\rho^{2}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}.

Let
|a_{0}|=a\in[0,1]
. From (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and (
3.12
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\rho^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|\rho^{n}+K\left(\frac{S_{\rho}^{\gamma}(f)}{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle\leq a+(1+k)\frac{(1-a^{2})\rho}{1-\rho}+K\frac{(1+k)(1-a^{2})^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\frac{\rho^{2}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}\leq 1+\Phi(\rho),

where

\displaystyle\Phi(\rho)=(1+k)\frac{(1-a^{2})\rho}{1-\rho}+K\frac{(1+k)(1-a^{2})^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\frac{\rho^{2}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}-(1-a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-a^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{2K(1+k)\left(1-a^{2}\right)}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\frac{\rho^{2}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}+\left(\frac{2(1+k)\rho}{1-\rho}-1\right)-\frac{2}{1+a}\right).

Note that

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(\rho)=(1+k)\frac{(1-a^{2})}{(1-\rho)^{2}}+K\frac{(1+k)\left(1-a^{2}\right)^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\frac{2\rho(1+\rho^{2})}{(1-\rho^{2})^{3}}>0.

Therefore,
\Phi(\rho)
is an increasing function and
\Phi(\rho)\leq\Phi(\rho_{0})
for
\rho\leq\rho_{0}=1/(2k+3)
.
To prove that
\Phi(\rho)\leq 0
, it suffices to show that
\Phi(\rho_{0})\leq 0
for all
a\leq 1
. Now

\displaystyle\Phi(\rho_{0})=\frac{1-a^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{2K(1+k)\left(1-a^{2}\right)}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\frac{(2k+3)^{2}}{(2k+4)^{2}(2k+2)^{2}}-\frac{2}{(1+a)}\right).

Let

\displaystyle F(x)=1+\frac{2K(1+k)(2k+3)^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}(2k+4)^{2}(2k+2)^{2}}\left(1-x^{2}\right)-\frac{2}{1+x}\quad\text{for}\;x\in[0,1].

It is enough to show that
F(x)\leq 0
for
x\in[0,1]
, so that
\Phi(\rho_{0})\leq 0
. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle F(0)=\frac{2K(1+k)(2k+3)^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}(2k+4)^{2}(2k+2)^{2}}-1\;\text{and}\;\lim_{x\to 1^{-}}F(x)=0.

By differentiating
F(x)
with respect to
x
, we have

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{4K(1+k)(2k+3)^{2}x}{(1-\gamma)^{2}(2k+4)^{2}(2k+2)^{2}}+\frac{2}{(1+x)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{(1+x)^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2K(1+k)(2k+3)^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}(2k+4)^{2}(2k+2)^{2}}x(1+x)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{(1+x)^{2}}\left(1-\frac{8K(1+k)(2k+3)^{2}}{(2k+4)^{2}(2k+2)^{2}}\right)\geq 0\;\text{if}\;K\leq\frac{(2k+4)^{2}(2k+2)^{2}}{8(1+k)(2k+3)^{2}}.

Therefore,
F(x)
is a monotonically increasing function of
x\in[0,1]
if
K\leq(2k+4)^{2}(2k+2)^{2}/(8(1+k)(2k+3)^{2})
. Hence
F(x)\leq 0
for
x\in[0,1]
.

To prove the sharpness of the result, we consider
f_{4}(z)=h_{4}(z)+\overline{g_{4}(z)}
in
\Omega_{\gamma}
, where
h_{4}=\psi\circ\Phi_{4}
with
\Phi_{4}:\Omega_{\gamma}\to\mathbb{D}
defined by
\Phi_{4}(z)=\gamma+(1-\gamma)z
and
\psi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
defined by
\psi(z)=(a-z)/(1-az)
for
a\in(0,1)
and
\gamma\in[0,1)
. Thus

\displaystyle h_{4}(z)=\psi\left(\gamma+(1-\gamma)z\right)=\frac{a-(1-\gamma)\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)}{1-a(1-\gamma)\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)}

\displaystyle=A_{0}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}\;\text{for}\;z\in\Omega_{\gamma},

where
A_{0}=a
,
A_{n}=a^{n-1}(1-a^{2})(1-\gamma)^{n}
and
g_{4}(z)=-k\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}
.
Therefore

\displaystyle S:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\rho^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|b_{n}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\rho^{n}+\frac{(2k+4)^{2}(2k+2)^{2}}{8(2k+3)^{2}}\frac{S_{\rho}^{\gamma}(h)}{\pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{0}+\frac{(1-a^{2})(1+k)}{a}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(a\rho\right)^{n}

\displaystyle+\frac{(2k+4)^{2}(2k+2)^{2}}{8(2k+3)^{2}}\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left(\frac{|A_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}-\frac{k^{2}|A_{n}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2n}}\right)\rho^{2n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+(1-a)F_{4}(\rho),

where

\displaystyle F_{4}(\rho)=\frac{(1+a)(1+k)\rho}{1-a\rho}+\frac{(2k+4)^{2}(2k+2)^{2}(1-k^{2})}{8(2k+3)^{2}(1-\gamma)^{2}}\frac{(1-a)(1+a)^{2}\rho^{2}}{(1-a^{2}\rho^{2})^{2}}-1.

Then, we have

\displaystyle F_{4}^{\prime}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1+a)(1+k)}{(1-a\rho)^{2}}+\frac{(2k+4)^{2}(2k+2)^{2}(1-k^{2})}{8(2k+3)^{2}(1-\gamma)^{2}}\frac{2(1-a)(1+a)^{2}\rho(1+a^{2}\rho^{2})}{(1-a^{2}\rho^{2})^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1+a)(1+k)}{(1-a\rho)^{2}}+\frac{(2k+4)^{2}(2k+2)^{2}(1-k^{2})(1-a)(1+a)^{2}\rho(1+a^{2}\rho^{2})}{4(2k+3)^{2}(1-\gamma)^{2}(1-a^{2}\rho^{2})^{3}}>0

for
\rho\in(0,1)
. Therefore
F_{4}(\rho)
is a strictly increasing function of
\rho
in
(0,1)
. Thus, for
\rho>\rho_{0}=1/(2k+3)
, we have

\displaystyle F_{4}(\rho)>F_{4}(\rho_{0})=\frac{(1+a)(1+k)}{3+2k-a}+\frac{(2k+4)^{2}(2k+2)^{2}(1-k^{2})(1-a)(1+a)^{2}}{8(1-\gamma)^{2}(3+2k+a)^{2}(3+2k-a)^{2}}-1.

Clearly,

. Hence,

. This shows that
\rho_{0}
