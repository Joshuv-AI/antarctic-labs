Scalar curvature bounds for 3D continuous metrics through the Inverse Mean Curvature Flow
Mattia Fogagnolo, Giorgio Gatti, Alessandra Pluda

Abstract
. We propose a notion of scalar curvature lower bounds in a three-dimensional Riemannian manifold endowed with a 𝒞 0 \mathscr{C}^{0} metric based on the monotonicity of the Hawking mass along the inverse mean curvature flow. We present a stability theorem for continuous Riemannian metrics with nonnegative scalar curvature in such IMCF sense.



MSC (2020):
53E10, 53C21, 53C23.

Keywords
: scalar curvature, inverse mean curvature flow, Hawking mass, monotonicity formula.

1.
Introduction and main result

Under the inverse mean curvature flow, a family of hypersurfaces evolves with a speed inversely proportional to their mean curvature. Its weak formulation involves the level sets of a locally Lipschitz solution
u
to a boundary value problem. For a bounded domain
E
within a complete noncompact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
,
u
must satisfy:

(IMCF)

{\left\{\kern-3.0pt\begin{array}[]{@{\ }rcl@{\hspace{1cm}}l}\div\left(\frac{\nabla u}{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\nabla u$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\nabla u\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\nabla u$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\nabla u$}}\right\rvert$}}}}\right)&=&{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\nabla u$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\nabla u\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\nabla u$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\nabla u$}}\right\rvert$}}}\hfil\hskip 28.45274pt&\text{on $M\smallsetminus E$}\\
u&=&0\hfil\hskip 28.45274pt&\text{on $\partial E$}\\
u&\to&+\infty\hfil\hskip 28.45274pt&\text{as $d(x,E)\to+\infty$}.\end{array}\right.}

Solutions to
eq.

IMCF
are understood in the nonstandard variational sense introduced and comprehensively developed by Huisken and Ilmanen
[
18
]
. With the aim of proving the Riemannian Penrose inequality, they show that, in topologically simple
3
-manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature, the Hawking mass

(1.1)

\mathfrak{m}_{H}(\Sigma)=\sqrt{\frac{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\Sigma$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\Sigma\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Sigma$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Sigma$}}\right\rvert$}}}}{16\pi}}\left(1-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\H^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)

is monotone nondecreasing. This note stems from observing that the converse holds:

in dimension
3
, the monotonicity of the Hawking mass implies nonnegative scalar curvature.

Consequently, nonnegative scalar curvature can be characterized purely via the IMCF - a formulation that makes sense even for merely continuous Riemannian metrics.
In fact, one can consistently say (see
Theorem

3.1
and
Definition

3.3
) that a noncompact manifold endowed with a continuous Riemannian metric admitting a weak solution to (
IMCF
) has nonnegative scalar curvature (in the sense of IMCF) if, for all
t\geq 0
, the following holds

(1.2)

32\pi\left(e^{t/2}-1\right)-\int_{\{u\leq t\}}e^{\frac{u}{2}}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\nabla u$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\nabla u\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\nabla u$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\nabla u$}}\right\rvert$}}}^{3}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\geq t\left(16\pi-\int_{\partial E}\H^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right).

Since a
\mathscr{C}^{0}
metric is not enough to define the mean curvature classically, the mean curvature
\H
of the initial hypersurface
\partial E
is understood in the variational sense of
Definition

2.6
.

In this note, we present a definition of nonnegative scalar curvature in the IMCF sense and give the main arguments for its
\mathscr{C}^{0}
stability.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a smooth, oriented, noncompact
3
-manifold without boundary and with
H_{2}(M)=\{0\}
. Let
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of complete
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-Riemannian metrics on
M
and assume that each
(M,g_{j})
satisfies a global Euclidean isoperimetric inequality
eq.

2.1
with a constant
C_{\mathrm{iso}}
independent of
j
and has nonnegative scalar curvature in the sense of IMCF. Let
g_{j}\to g
as
j\to\infty
in
\mathscr{C}^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M)
. Then,
(M,g)
has nonnegative scalar curvature in the sense of IMCF as well.

For sequences of smooth metrics, a
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-stability result was first established using entirely different techniques by Gromov
[
17
]
(see also
[
20
]
), and refined through a completely different Ricci flow argument by Bamler
[
5
]
. Burkhardt-Guim
[
11
,
12
]
devised a Ricci-flow related notion of scalar curvature bounds for
\mathscr{C}^{0}

Defining and understanding scalar curvature bounds in a non-smooth setting may also be of interest outside of Riemannian metric geometry. In fact, since nonnegative scalar curvature arises when constraining the Dominant Energy Condition of a Lorentzian spacetime to an initial Riemannian dataset, the ideas put forth here are naturally tied to the very active field of non-smooth spacetime geometry, where
\mathscr{C}^{0}
regularity plays a crucial role (see the very recent works
[
25
,
9
]
and references therein).

We point out that this note has a partly expository nature. We focus here on the core geometric ideas and on the main steps of the proofs, deferring the many technical details to
[
15
]
which will contain a comprehensive study of the IMCF for
\mathscr{C}^{0}
metrics. We emphasize that

can be improved with a localized statement, thereby completely removing the global topological and the isoperimetric assumptions on

. The latter are, in fact, added only to deal with the more familiar global IMCFs
[

]
. The localization can be obtained by replacing the global IMCF with a suitable local version, inspired by
[

(see also
[

Moreover, we decided to focus only on nonnegative scalar curvature to have simpler statements and computations, but the extension to more general lower bounds is easily achieved. All of these improvements will be part of a future installment of this work.

Finally, we must mention the very recent developments by Mazurowski-Yao
[
23
,
24
]
, which recover and improve upon Gromov’s theorem
[
17
]
in dimension
3
through harmonic functions (see in this direction the very inspirational
[

]
) and

[
19

, which strengthens the approach of Bamler
[

to recover the results of

We hope that working on and possibly unifying the various perspectives currently arising in this context will lead to a deeper understanding of scalar curvature bounds in low regularity.

Setting and tools

We consider

to be a smooth, oriented and noncompact

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial F\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial F$}}\right\rvert$}}}^{3/2}\geq\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{iso}}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert F\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}}

When we write
\,\mathrm{d}\sigma
and

\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}

Given a finite perimeter set

2.1.
Weak variational IMCF

We will work in
M

\mathscr{C}^{0}
-Riemannian metric

The following is the class of initial data we are working with.

Definition 2.1

(
2

.

Let

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial E\setminus\partial^{\ast}E)=0.

Definition 2.2

Given a bounded open set

(2.3)

\displaystyle J_{u}^{K}(v)={\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle Dv$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert Dv\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle Dv$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle Dv$}}\right\rvert$}}}(K^{1}\cup\partial^{*}K)+\int_{K^{1}\cup\partial^{*}K}v\,\mathrm{d}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle Du$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert Du\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle Du$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle Du$}}\right\rvert$}}}

such that

a.e. outside

, subject to:

(2.4)

{\left\{\kern-3.0pt\begin{array}[]{@{\ }rcl@{\hspace{1cm}}l}u&=&0\hfil\hskip 28.45274pt&\text{a.e. in $E$},\\
\mathrm{tr}_{\partial E}u&=&0,\hfil\hskip 28.45274pt&\\

By

(2.5)

\lim_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(p)\cap M\setminus\overline{E}}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle u$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert u\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle u$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle u$}}\right\rvert$}}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=0\quad\text{for $\mathcal{H}^{2}$-a.e. }p\in\partial E.

E_{t}=\mathrm{int}\{u\leq t\}\quad\mathrm{for}\,t\geq 0.

Under this definition, one can show, as in

Remark 2.3
.

When
u
happens to be Lipschitz, it can be checked that the above notion coincides with that of Huisken-Ilmanen
[
18
]
. In particular, when the ambient metric is smooth and the initial set

is

is a Huisken-Ilmanen’s weak Lipschitz solution. Details on this will be included in

The first two conditions in (

in

is prescribed to be the initial datum. Similarly, the last condition ensures that the solution is proper, analogous to requiring that

For the whole duration of the note, we will refer to a proper weak variational solution to IMCF simply as weak IMCF, or weak solution to (
IMCF
)

The existence of a proper weak variational solution to the IMCF is ensured whenever the ambient metric

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
M
be a smooth noncompact Riemannian
3
-manifold with a global Euclidean isoperimetric inequality
eq.

2.1
and let
g
be a complete
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-Riemannian metric on
M
. Then, for any given bounded open set
E\subset M
with finite perimeter and
2
-dimensional boundary, there exists a unique proper weak variational solution to the IMCF of
E
.

To guide the reader, we divide the proof in steps.

Step 1: approximation.

We first consider a sequence of smooth regularizing metrics
g_{j}
such that
g_{j}\to g

globally
uniformly in
\mathscr{C}^{0}

[
13
, Proposition 4.9]
. The global uniformity in particular implies that there exists an isoperimetric constant
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{iso}}
common to all
g_{j}
’s and
g
.

Then,
E
having
2
-dimensional boundary allows us to apply
[
27
, Theorem 1.1]
to find a sequence
E_{j}
of equibounded open sets with smooth boundary which converge to
E
as sets of finite perimeter and satisfy
E\subset E_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
.
For each
g_{j}
, we can apply
[
30
, Theorem 1.2]
or
[
7
]
and obtain a proper, Lipschitz IMCF
u_{j}
starting from
E_{j}
.

Step 2:
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-estimates.

We first crucially observe that the
u_{j}
’s naturally come with a uniform bounded oscillation estimate. Indeed, by
[
8
, Theorem 2.8]
, we can realize
u_{j}
as the local uniform limit as
p\to 1^{+}
of
u_{j}^{p}=-(p-1)\log(w_{j}^{p})
, where
w_{j}^{p}
is
p
-harmonic with respect to the metric
g_{j}
. Then, the Harnack inequality of
[
26
]
applies to
w_{j}^{p}
, translating into

(2.7)

u^{p}_{j}(x)\leq u^{p}_{j}(y)+\mathrm{C}_{K}

for any
K\Subset M\setminus\overline{E}_{j}
, with
\mathrm{C}_{K}
independent of
j
and
p
. The constant
\mathrm{C}_{K}
can be checked to depend on
\mathscr{C}^{0}
quantities only.
We thus get

(2.8)

u_{j}(x)\leq u_{j}(y)+\mathrm{C}_{K}

for any
K\Subset M\setminus\overline{E}_{j}
, with
\mathrm{C}_{K}
independent of
j
.

The validity of (
2.8
) implies that if the sequence
u_{j}
were not locally uniformly bounded, the level sets
\{u_{j}<t\}
, for
t>0
would collapse to
E^{*}
as
j\to\infty
.

We first show that this cannot happen for an auxiliary IMCF built as follows. Let
o\in E
,
R>0
small enough such that
\overline{B}_{2R}(o)
is a closed disk satisfying
\overline{B}_{2R}(o)\subset E^{1}
. Let
\tilde{g}
be a continuous metric that coincides with
g
in
M\setminus B_{2R}(o)
and with the flat metric
\delta
in
B_{R}(o)
. Let
\tilde{g}_{j}
be uniform smooth approximations of
\tilde{g}
that coincide with
g_{j}
in
M\setminus B_{2R}(o)
and with
\delta
in
B_{R/2}(o)
. If
R
is small enough
[
32
, Lemma 2.6.3]
ensures that for
\rho\leq R/4

\mathbb{B}_{\rho}(o)
are strictly outward minimizing with positive mean curvature for all
\tilde{g}_{j}
’s. In particular, the weak IMCFs
\tilde{u}_{j}
issuing from
\mathbb{B}_{R/8}(o)
are all smooth and given by
u_{j}(x)=f({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert x\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}})
, for
R/8\leq{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert x\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}}\leq R/4
, which implies that

cannot happen. By construction of
\tilde{g}_{j}
, the functions

are actually

-IMCF outside

.

Step 3:
BV
-estimates and existence.

Once uniform

-estimates on

are available, the weak formulation itself, together with the (locally) uniform convergence of the metrics, implies

-compactness for

. Up to subsequence, we can take the limit

and verify that

is a proper weak variational solution to IMCF. To show the global properness of

, we crucially use the fact that
(M,g)
has a global isoperimetric inequality. In fact, it follows from the global uniform convergence

that a global Euclidean isoperimetric inequality holds on all

with isoperimetric constant independent of

[
30

to see that the

are uniformly proper and obtain that

Step 4: uniqueness.
The uniqueness follows from a nontrivial generalization of
[
18
, Uniqueness Theorem 2.2]
. In the original proof, the continuity of two compared solutions

is required to show that if

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle Du$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert Du\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle Du$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle Du$}}\right\rvert$}}}(\{u>v\})={\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle Dv$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert Dv\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle Dv$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle Dv$}}\right\rvert$}}}(\{u>v\})=0

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\{u>v\}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\{u>v\}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\{u>v\}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\{u>v\}$}}\right\rvert$}}}=0

BV
functions.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

The principle that the weak formulation of
Definition

2.2
together with uniform
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-estimates implies
BV
-compactness is not new, although it has never been used in a
\mathscr{C}^{0}
setting before. It was, in fact, used in the classical Euclidean setting
[
21
,
22
]

and the in the anisotropic setting
[
14
]
, which serve as important inspirations for this part of the proof. The attainment of
u=0
on
\partial E
in the trace sense is also inspired by these papers; we have slightly improved in this direction by relaxing their assumption of Lipschitz initial boundary to requiring
eq.

2.2

On the other hand, the argument presented in

Step 2
seems to be novel, and genuinely motivated by the lack of both barriers at infinity and local curvature estimates in general
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-regular metrics.
In fact, in the aforementioned works, these estimates are derived using explicit barrier functions that we cannot provide in our context. Where such barriers are not available, as in nonflat Riemannian settings, local bounds on the Ricci curvature are employed
[
18
,
30
,
7
]
. Again, this is of course harmless in any smooth ambient, but not available in general
\mathscr{C}^{0}
contexts.

2.2.
Variational mean curvature

Definition 2.6
.

We say that an open bounded set

,

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial F\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial F$}}\right\rvert$}}}-\int_{F}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu

.

In a smooth ambient space, the class of

Let

For

be the mean curvature of

at

. Observe that the function

is continuous. Define

By the direct method in the Calculus of Variations there exists a minimizer

where

\partial E_{t}
, that by the weak formulation of

.
The quantity

3.1

with

1.1

Indeed, the evolution equation for the area yields
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E_{t}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}}=\ee^{t}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E^{\ast}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E^{\ast}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E^{\ast}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E^{\ast}$}}\right\rvert$}}}
, where

is the strictly outward minimizing hull of

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth, complete, oriented, noncompact Riemannian
3
-manifold with
H_{2}(M)=\{0\}
. Assume that a global Euclidean isoperimetric inequality
eq.

2.1
holds.
Then, the following are equivalent:

(i)

(M,g)
has nonnegative scalar curvature;

(ii)

for any bounded
E\subset M
with connected boundary and continuous variational mean curvature, we have

(3.2)

\int_{0}^{T}m_{H}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\geq T\left(16\pi-\int_{\partial E}\H^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right),

where
\H
is the weak (distributional) mean curvature of
\partial E
.

Remark 3.2
.

In the setting of condition
(ii)
, the regularity theory for prescribed mean curvature sets implies that, if
\varphi
is a variational mean curvature for
\partial E
, then
\H=\varphi_{|\partial E}
.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Let
E\subset M
be a bounded set with connected boundary and continuous variational mean curvature. By classical regularity theory,
\partial E
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{1,\alpha}\cap W^{2,p}
for any
0<\alpha<1
and
1<p<+\infty
, with continuous distributional mean curvature
\H
(see e.g.
[
33
, Theorem 2.2]
for an account). Geroch monotonicity for the weak IMCF
[
18
, Geroch Monotonicity Formula 5.8]
then yields

m_{H}(t)\geq m_{H}(0)

for any

. Notice that for this we need the connectedness of the level sets, which is granted by the topological assumption
H_{2}(M)=\{0\}
, see
[
10
, Proposition 1]
or
[
6
, Proposition 2.5]
. Since
E_{0}=E^{*}
is the strictly outward minimizing hull of
E
, we also have
\int_{\partial E_{0}}\H^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq\int_{\partial E}\H^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma
(from, e.g.
[
18
, (1.15)]
), and so

(3.3)

m_{H}(t)\geq m_{H}(0)\geq\left(16\pi-\int_{\partial E}\H^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)

for any

. Integrating over

provides (
3.2
).

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
. Let

. We aim to show that
\mathrm{Scal}(p)\geq 0
. For small enough radii

,

is smooth, strictly mean-convex and strictly outward minimizing (see
[

, Lemma 2.6.3]
). Furthermore, by

has continuous variational mean curvature. Let then

and take

IMCF
with initial datum

. Observe that the right hand side of (

) coincides in this case with

. By

,

, and in particular so is

for

, with

small enough. By (

), we have
