Balanced and pluriclosed metrics on real semisimple Lie groups
Joseph Kwong

Abstract
. We characterise the existence of balanced and pluriclosed metrics on compact quotients of real semisimple Lie groups equipped with regular complex structures, in terms of Vogan diagrams. Consequently, such complex manifolds cannot simultaneously admit a balanced metric and a pluriclosed metric. Along the way, we revisit and correct the classification of regular complex structures on real semisimple Lie groups.



1.
Introduction

A
Hermitian metric
on a complex manifold
(M^{2n},J)
is a Riemannian metric
g
on
M
such that
J^{*}g=g
. A Hermitian metric
g
is
balanced
if the
2
-form
\omega:=g(J\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies

d\omega^{n-1}=0,
and
pluriclosed
if
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=0
.
We say that a complex manifold is
balanced
/
pluriclosed
if it admits a balanced/pluriclosed metric, respectively. Every Kähler manifold is both balanced and pluriclosed. In fact, a Hermitian metric is Kähler if and only if it is simultaneously balanced and pluriclosed; see

[
1
]
or
[
25
]
. The
Fino–Vezzoni conjecture
predicts the converse at the manifold level:

Conjecture 1.1

(Fino–Vezzoni
[
13
]
)
.

A compact complex manifold admitting both a balanced metric and a (possibly distinct) pluriclosed metric also admits a Kähler metric.

The main aim of this article is to study the conjecture on Lie group quotients. Let
G
be a Lie group, let
\Gamma
be a cocompact lattice (i.e. a discrete subgroup such that
\Gamma\backslash G
is compact), and let
J
be a left-invariant complex structure on
G
. Then
J
descends to a complex structure on
\Gamma\backslash G
, and we obtain a compact complex manifold
(\Gamma\backslash G,J)
.
The Fino–Vezzoni conjecture is known to hold for
(\Gamma\backslash G,J)
when
J
is a bi-invariant complex structure
[
30
]
, when
G
is a nilpotent Lie group
[
14
]
, and for other classes of solvable Lie groups
[
13
,
24
,
11
,
7
,
15
,
12
]
.
Our main result verifies the conjecture when
G
is any semisimple Lie group and
J
is any
regular
left-invariant complex structure:

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a real semisimple Lie group, let
J
be a regular complex structure on
G
, and let
\Gamma
be a cocompact lattice of
G
. The compact complex manifold
(\Gamma\backslash G,J)
cannot admit both a balanced metric and a pluriclosed metric.

Recall that every semisimple Lie group
G
admits a cocompact lattice
[
5
]
.
A left-invariant complex structure
J
on
G
is called
regular
if it is
also right-invariant with respect to a Cartan subgroup of
G

[
26
]
.

Theorem
A
has already been proved when
G
is compact semisimple: in this case,
(G,J)
does not admit any balanced metrics
[
9
, Corollary 5.1]
. Moreover, the left-invariant complex structures admitting pluriclosed metrics on compact
G
are classified by Lauret and Montedoro in
[
20
]
.
For each even-dimensional non-compact simple
G
of
inner type
(i.e. there is a Cartan involution of the Lie algebra of
G
which is an inner automorphism), Giusti and Podestà
[
16
]
construct a family of regular complex structures
J
for which
(\Gamma\backslash G,J)
admits balanced metrics but no pluriclosed metrics.

1.1.
Refined statements

Using a well-known symmetrisation argument (see Proposition
2.2
), Theorem
A
follows from Theorems
B
and
C
, which completely characterise the existence of balanced and pluriclosed metrics on regular complex structures using
Vogan diagrams
. We now introduce some notation and definitions in order to state these technical results. We refer the reader to §
3
for the background on real semisimple Lie algebras and Vogan diagrams.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
denote the Lie algebra of the semisimple Lie group
G
, and let
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
denote its complexification. A left-invariant complex structure
J
on
G
is determined by the
\operatorname{i}
-eigenspace
\mathfrak{q}
of
J:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
(see §
2.1
). We say that
J
is a
regular complex structure
if
J\circ\operatorname{ad}(H)=\operatorname{ad}(H)\circ J
for all
H\in\mathfrak{h}
for some Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
(see §
4.1
). In this case, we say that
J
is
\mathfrak{h}
-regular
. Every regular complex structure on
G
can be constructed by the following algorithm, which is a corrected version of a procedure in
[
26
]
(see Algorithm
4.5
):

(i)

Choose a maximally compact Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. The complex conjugation map of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
induces an automorphism
\sigma
of the root system
\Phi
of
(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}})
.

(ii)

Choose a set of positive roots
\Phi^{+}
of
\Phi
such that
\sigma\Phi^{+}=-\Phi^{+}
.

(iii)

Choose a subset
\Delta_{0}
of the simple roots of
\Phi^{+}
such that no vertex of
\Delta_{0}
is adjacent to a vertex of
-\sigma\Delta_{0}
in the Dynkin diagram determined by
\Phi^{+}
.

(iv)

Choose a complex subspace
\mathfrak{l}
of
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\mathfrak{l}\oplus\sigma\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
and
[\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{-\alpha}]\subseteq\mathfrak{l}
for all
\alpha\in\Delta_{0}
.

Set
\mathcal{R}_{0}:=\mathbb{R}\Delta_{0}\cap\Phi
, and
\mathcal{R}:=\mathcal{R}_{0}\sqcup\Phi^{+}\backslash(\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}\sqcup(-\sigma\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}))
. Then

\mathfrak{q}:=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}}\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}

is the
\operatorname{i}
-eigenspace of a regular complex structure
J=J(\Phi^{+},\Delta_{0},\mathfrak{l})
, where
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}
denotes the root space of
\alpha\in\Phi
.

A
Vogan diagram
is a triple
\mathcal{V}=(\mathcal{D},\theta,\mathcal{P})
, where
\mathcal{D}
is a Dynkin diagram,
\theta
is an involutive automorphism of
\mathcal{D}
whose two-cycles are denoted by bidirectional arrows, and
\mathcal{P}
is a subset of the fixed points of
\theta
, which we paint.

Fix a maximally compact Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\Phi
denote the roots of
(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}})
. Let
J=J(\Phi^{+},\Delta_{0},\mathfrak{l})
be an
\mathfrak{h}
-regular complex structure on
G
. The choice of positive roots
\Phi^{+}
determines a Vogan diagram
\mathcal{V}
(see Algorithm
3.10
).

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
G
be an even-dimensional semisimple Lie group, and let
\Gamma
be a cocompact lattice.

(i)

There exists a regular
J

(\Gamma\backslash G,J)

(ii)

(iii)

If
\mathfrak{g}

(\Gamma\backslash G,J)

\mathfrak{g}

is

§

2.
Invariant Hermitian structures on Lie groups

2.1.
Invariant complex structures on Lie groups

Let
G
be a real connected Lie group. A complex structure
J
on
G
is called
left-invariant
if the left translation maps
L_{a}:G\rightarrow G
given by
L_{a}(b)=ab
are all holomorphic with respect to
J
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
denote the Lie algebra of
G
, i.e. the left-invariant vector fields on
G
. If
J
is a left-invariant complex structure on
G
, then
JX\in\mathfrak{g}
for all
X\in\mathfrak{g}
. Thus,
J
is determined by a linear map
J:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\hat{\operatorname{i}}\mathfrak{g}
denote the complexification of
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
\sigma:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
be the complex conjugation map
\sigma:X+\hat{\operatorname{i}}Y\mapsto X-\hat{\operatorname{i}}Y
for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Complexifying
J
, we obtain a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map
J:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Consider the
\operatorname{i}
-eigenspace of
J:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
, which we denote by
\mathfrak{q}
. The
-\operatorname{i}
-eigenspace is
\sigma\mathfrak{q}
, and we have
\mathfrak{q}\oplus\sigma\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Moreover, the integrability of
J
implies that
\mathfrak{q}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Conversely, every subalgebra
\mathfrak{q}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying
\mathfrak{q}\oplus\sigma\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
arises in this manner. In summary, we have a bijection

(1)

\left\{\begin{matrix}\text{Left-invariant complex }\\
\text{structures $J$ on $G$}\end{matrix}\right\}\longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{matrix}\text{Subalgebras $\mathfrak{q}$ of $\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}$ }\\
\text{such that $\mathfrak{q}\oplus\sigma\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}$}\end{matrix}\right\}.

Example 2.1
.

A complex structure on
G
is called
bi-invariant
if both the left and right translation maps are holomorphic.
If
J
is bi-invariant, then we can form a complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}
by equipping
\mathfrak{g}
with
\operatorname{i}\cdot X:=JX
.
Conversely, if
\mathfrak{s}
is a complex Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{g}
is the underlying real Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{s}
, then
J:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
,
X\mapsto\operatorname{i}X
is a bi-invariant complex structure on
G
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{s}
is a complex Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{g}
is the underlying real Lie algebra. The following is an isomorphism of complex Lie algebras:

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{s}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{s}},\qquad X+\hat{\operatorname{i}}Y\mapsto(X+\operatorname{i}Y,X-\operatorname{i}Y),\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Here,
\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
is the complex Lie algebra with underlying real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and scalar multiplication
\lambda\cdot X:=\overline{\lambda}X
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, where
\overline{\lambda}X
denotes scalar multiplication in
\mathfrak{s}
. We identify
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathfrak{s}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
via the isomorphism above. Under this identification, complex conjugation
\sigma
is given by
\sigma:(X,Y)\mapsto(Y,X)
for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Now, let
J:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be the bi-invariant complex structure on
G
given by
JX=\operatorname{i}X
. The complexification
J:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
is given by
J(X,Y)=(\operatorname{i}X,\operatorname{i}Y)
.
Thus, the
\pm\operatorname{i}
-eigenspaces of
J
are
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus 0
and
\sigma\mathfrak{q}=0\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
, respectively.

We say that two left-invariant complex structures
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
on
G
are
equivalent
if there exists a Lie algebra automorphism
f:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
such that
f\circ J_{1}=J_{2}\circ f
. If
\mathfrak{q}_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
denotes the
\operatorname{i}
-eigenspace of
J_{i}:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
, then
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
are equivalent if and only if there exists a (real) Lie algebra automorphism
f:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
such that
f\mathfrak{q}_{1}=\mathfrak{q}_{2}
.

Symmetrising Hermitian metrics on
\Gamma\backslash G

The following result is well-known to experts, and is stated and proved under additional assumptions in

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a Lie group, and let
\Gamma
be a cocompact lattice. Let
J
be a left-invariant complex structure on
G
, and let
J
denote the induced complex structure on
\Gamma\backslash G
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

(\Gamma\backslash G,J)
admits a Kähler/balanced/pluriclosed metric.

(ii)

(G,J)
admits a left-invariant Kähler/balanced/pluriclosed metric.

Proof.

Let
\pi:G\rightarrow M=\Gamma\backslash G
denote the natural projection map. For each
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, there exists a unique smooth vector field
X^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
which is
\pi
-related to
X
.
Since
G
admits a cocompact lattice,
G
is unimodular
[
23
, Lemma 6.2]
; by definition, this means that there exists a nowhere vanishing bi-invariant volume form
\mu
on
G
. Since
\mu
is left-invariant, it descends to a nowhere-vanishing form
\mu^{\prime}
on
M
.
For each
k
, define

S:\Omega^{k}(M)\rightarrow\Lambda^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*},\qquad(S\alpha)(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}):=\int_{M}\alpha(X_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,X_{k}^{\prime})\,\mu^{\prime},

where
\Omega^{k}(M)
denotes the smooth
k
-forms
\alpha
on
M
, and
\Lambda^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
denotes the left-invariant
k
-forms on
G
.
The map
S
satisfies the following properties:

(a)

S
commutes with the exterior derivative
d
.

(b)

Extending
S
to complex
k
-forms in the obvious way,
S
sends
(p,q)
-forms to
(p,q)
-forms.

(c)

S
commutes with the Dolbeault operators
\partial
and
\overline{\partial}
.

(d)

S
sends positive
(p,p)
-forms to positive
(p,p)
-forms.

We show (a) only; the other properties are easy to show.
Let
\alpha
be a
k
-form on
M
, and let
X_{0},\ldots,X_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}
. We find

\displaystyle(dS\alpha)(X_{0},\ldots,X_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}(S\alpha)([X_{i},X_{j}],X_{0},\ldots,\widehat{X_{i}},\ldots,\widehat{X_{j}},\ldots,X_{k})

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\alpha([X_{i}^{\prime},X_{j}^{\prime}],X_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,\widehat{X_{i}^{\prime}},\ldots,\widehat{X_{j}^{\prime}},\ldots,X_{k}^{\prime})\,\mu^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}(d\alpha)(X_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,X_{k}^{\prime})\,\mu^{\prime}-\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\int_{M}X^{\prime}_{i}\alpha(X_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,\widehat{X_{i}^{\prime}},\ldots,X^{\prime}_{k})\,\mu^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}(d\alpha)(X_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,X_{k}^{\prime})\,\mu^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(Sd\alpha)(X_{0},\ldots,X_{k}),

where a hat denotes the omission of that element. The second last equality holds from the following fact:
if
X\in\mathfrak{g}
and
f:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function, then

(2)

\int_{M}X^{\prime}(f)\,\mu^{\prime}=0.

Let us prove Equation (
2
). By Cartan’s magic formula, we find that

\int_{M}\mathcal{L}_{X^{\prime}}(f\mu^{\prime})=\int_{M}(d\circ\iota_{X^{\prime}})(f\mu^{\prime})+\int_{M}(\iota_{X^{\prime}}\circ d)(f\mu^{\prime})=0,

where
\mathcal{L}
is the Lie derivative, and
\iota
denotes interior product. The last equality follows from Stokes’ theorem and that
f\mu^{\prime}
is a top-degree form. The product rule implies that

0=\int_{M}\mathcal{L}_{X^{\prime}}(f\mu^{\prime})=\int_{M}X^{\prime}(f)\,\mu^{\prime}+\int_{M}f\,\mathcal{L}_{X^{\prime}}\mu^{\prime}=\int_{M}X^{\prime}(f)\,\mu^{\prime}.

The last equality follows because
\mathcal{L}_{X^{\prime}}\mu^{\prime}=0
: the flow of
X\in\mathfrak{g}
is given by the family of right translations
t\mapsto R_{\exp(tX)}
, and
\mu
is right-invariant, so
\pi^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{X^{\prime}}\mu^{\prime})=\mathcal{L}_{X}\mu=0
.

Now, let
\omega^{\prime}
be a positive
(1,1)
-form on
(M,J)
.
By (a) and (c), if
\omega^{\prime}
is Kähler or pluriclosed, respectively, then so is
\omega:=S\omega^{\prime}
.
Next, suppose
d(\omega^{\prime})^{n-1}=0
. By
[
22
, §4]
, the map
\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{n-1}
is a bijection (on any complex manifold) between positive
(1,1)
-forms and positive
(n-1,n-1)
-forms. Thus,
\Omega^{\prime}:=(\omega^{\prime})^{n-1}
is a positive
(n-1,n-1)
-form with
d\Omega^{\prime}=0
. By (d) and (a),
\Omega:=S\Omega^{\prime}
is a left-invariant positive
(n-1,n-1)
-form on
(G,J)
with
d\Omega=0
. There exists a unique positive
(1,1)
-form
\omega
such that
\omega^{n-1}=\Omega
. Uniqueness of
\omega
implies that
\omega
is also left-invariant.
∎

2.3.
Symmetrising invariant Hermitian metrics on
G

Let
G
be a Lie group and let
T
be a connected Lie subgroup of
G
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{t}
be the Lie algebras of
G
and
T
, respectively. The following are equivalent for a left-invariant complex structure
J
on
G
:

(i)

J
is
right
T
-invariant
, i.e. the diffeomorphism
R_{a}:G\rightarrow G
given by
R_{a}(b):=ba
is holomorphic for any
a\in T
.

(ii)

J:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is
\operatorname{Ad}_{G}(T)
-invariant
, i.e.
\operatorname{Ad}(a)\circ J=J\circ\operatorname{Ad}(a)
for all
a\in T
.

(iii)

J:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is
\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{t})
-invariant
, i.e.
\operatorname{ad}(X)\circ J=J\circ\operatorname{ad}(X)
for all
X\in\mathfrak{t}
.

(iv)

The
\operatorname{i}
-eigenspace
\mathfrak{q}
of
J:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
is
\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}})
-invariant
, i.e.
[\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{q}]\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
.

The following are equivalent for a left-invariant covariant tensor field
\alpha
on
G
:

(i)

\alpha
is
right
T
-invariant
, i.e.
(R_{a})^{*}\alpha=\alpha
for all
a\in T
.

(ii)

\alpha:\mathfrak{g}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
\operatorname{Ad}_{G}(T)
-invariant, i.e.
\operatorname{Ad}(a)^{*}\alpha=\alpha
for all
a\in T
.

(iii)

\alpha
is
\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{t})
-invariant, i.e.

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha(Y_{1},\ldots,[X,Y_{i}],\ldots,Y_{k})=0

for all
X\in\mathfrak{t}
and
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
G
be a Lie group, and let
T
be a Lie subgroup of
G
such that
\operatorname{Ad}_{G}(T)=\{\operatorname{Ad}(a):\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}:a\in T\}
is compact. Let
J
be a left-invariant complex structure on
G
which is also right
T
-invariant. The following are equivalent:

(i)

(G,J)
admits a left-invariant Kähler/pluriclosed/balanced metric.

(ii)

(G,J)
admits a left-invariant right
T
-invariant Kähler/pluriclosed/balanced metric.

Proof.

Let
\nu
be a right-invariant nowhere vanishing volume form on
\widehat{T}:=\operatorname{Ad}_{G}(T)
. For each
k
, define

S:\Lambda^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\Lambda^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*},\qquad(S\alpha)(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}):=\int_{\widehat{T}}(\varphi^{*}\alpha)(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})\;\nu(\varphi).

This map satisfies the following properties:

(a)

S\alpha
is right
T
-invariant for any
\alpha\in\Lambda^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

(b)

S
commutes with the exterior derivative
d
.

(c)

Extending
S
to complex forms in the obvious way,
S
sends
(p,q)
-forms to
(p,q)
-forms.

(d)

S
commutes with the Dolbeault operators
\partial
and
\overline{\partial}
.

(e)

S
sends positive
(p,p)
-forms to positive
(p,p)
-forms.

We show (a) only; (b), (c), (d) and (e) are straightforward. Fix
\alpha\in\Lambda^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}
. Define
f:\widehat{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by
f(\varphi)=(\varphi^{*}\alpha)(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})
. Given
\psi\in\widehat{T}
, we find

\displaystyle(\psi^{*}S\alpha)(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})

\displaystyle=(S\alpha)(\psi X_{1},\ldots,\psi X_{k})

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{T}}(\varphi\circ\psi)^{*}\alpha(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})\;\nu(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{T}}(f\circ R_{\psi})\;\nu=\int_{\widehat{T}}(R_{\psi^{-1}})^{*}((f\circ R_{\psi})\;\nu)

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{T}}(f\circ R_{\psi}\circ R_{\psi^{-1}})\;(R_{\psi^{-1}})^{*}\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{T}}f\;\nu=(S\alpha)(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}),

where
R_{\psi}:\widehat{T}\rightarrow\widehat{T}
is given by
\varphi\mapsto\varphi\circ\psi
.

The rest of the argument is similar to the proof of Proposition
2.2
.
∎

3.
Real semisimple Lie algebras and Vogan diagrams

3.1.
Cartan subalgebras of a real semisimple Lie algebra

Throughout this section, let
\mathfrak{g}
denote a real semisimple Lie algebra. The complexification
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a complex semisimple Lie algebra. A
Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
is a subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
such that
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\Phi
denote the roots of
(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}})
, and let
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}
denote the root space of
\alpha\in\Phi
. Let
\sigma:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
denote the complex conjugation map
X+\hat{\operatorname{i}}Y\mapsto X-\hat{\operatorname{i}}Y
for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Since
\sigma\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
, we obtain a root system automorphism
\sigma:\Phi\rightarrow\Phi
given by
(\sigma\alpha)(H)=\overline{\alpha(\sigma H)}
for all
H\in\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Moreover,
\sigma:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
permutes the root spaces via
\sigma\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\sigma\alpha}
. We say that
\alpha\in\Phi
is
real
if
\sigma\alpha=\alpha
,
imaginary
if
\sigma\alpha=-\alpha
, and
complex
if
\alpha
is neither real nor imaginary.

Now, let
B
denote the Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Fix a root
\alpha\in\Phi
, and let
E_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}
be a non-zero root vector. Then
\alpha
is imaginary if and only if
B(E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha})\neq 0
. We say that
\alpha
is
compact
if
B(E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha})<0
, and
non-compact
if
B(E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha})>0
.

A
Cartan involution
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra automorphism
\theta:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
such that
\theta^{2}=\operatorname{Id}
and
B(X,\theta X)<0
for all non-zero
X\in\mathfrak{g}
. If
\theta
is a Cartan involution, then
B_{\theta}:=-B(\cdot,\theta\cdot)
is a positive-definite symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{g}
, and we have a vector space decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
, called the
Cartan decomposition
, where
\mathfrak{k}
and
\mathfrak{p}
are the
+1
and
-1
eigenspaces of
\theta
, respectively. Observe that
B
is negative-definite on
\mathfrak{k}
and positive-definite on
\mathfrak{p}
. These subspaces satisfy the following relations:

(3)

[\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{k}]\subseteq\mathfrak{k},\qquad[\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{p}]\subseteq\mathfrak{p},\qquad[\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]\subseteq\mathfrak{k}.

If
\mathfrak{h}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, then there exists a (not necessarily unique) Cartan involution
\theta
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\theta\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}

[
19
, Proposition 6.59]
, and we have a decomposition

\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a},\qquad\text{where}\qquad\mathfrak{t}=\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{k},\quad\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{p}.

Such a Cartan involution permutes the roots via
(\theta\alpha)(H)=\alpha(\theta^{-1}H)
for all
H\in\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
and the root spaces via
\theta\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\theta\alpha}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
\theta
be a Cartan involution with
\theta\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}
.

(i)

The subspaces
\mathfrak{t}
and
\mathfrak{a}
defined above can be written as

(4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{t}&=\{H\in\mathfrak{h}:\alpha(H)\text{ is imaginary for all $\alpha\in\Phi$}\},\\
\mathfrak{a}&=\{H\in\mathfrak{h}:\alpha(H)\text{ is real for all $\alpha\in\Phi$}\}.\end{split}

In particular,
\mathfrak{t}
and
\mathfrak{a}
are independent of the choice of Cartan involution
\theta
.

(ii)

View
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
as subsets of
(\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}})^{*}
in the obvious manner. Then the real and imaginary parts of
\alpha\in\Phi
with respect to
\sigma
are

\operatorname{Re}(\alpha)=\alpha|_{\mathfrak{a}},\qquad\operatorname{i}\operatorname{Im}(\alpha)=\alpha|_{\mathfrak{t}}.

Moreover, we have
\mathbb{R}\Phi=\operatorname{i}\mathfrak{t}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{*}
.

(iii)

\theta\alpha=-\sigma\alpha
for all
\alpha\in\Phi
.

(iv)

Let
\alpha\in\Phi
. Then
\alpha
is compact if and only if
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{k}^{\mathbb{C}}
, and
\alpha
is non-compact if and only if
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

To show (i), fix
H\in\mathfrak{h}
, and consider
\operatorname{ad}(H):\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
. If
H\in\mathfrak{t}
, then
\operatorname{ad}(H)
is skew-symmetric with respect to the inner product
B_{\theta}
, so
\alpha(H)
is imaginary for all
\alpha\in\Phi
in this case. Analogously, if
H\in\mathfrak{a}
, then
\operatorname{ad}(H)
is symmetric with respect to
B_{\theta}
, so
\alpha(H)
is real for all
\alpha\in\Phi
in this case. The opposite inclusions follow because
\mathbb{C}\Phi=(\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}})^{*}.

The first part of (ii) follows from (i) and the formulas
2\operatorname{Re}(\alpha)=\alpha+\sigma\alpha
and
2\operatorname{i}\operatorname{Im}(\alpha)=\alpha-\sigma\alpha
. In particular, it follows that
\mathbb{R}\Phi\subseteq\operatorname{i}\mathfrak{t}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{*}
. The opposite inclusion holds because
\mathbb{R}\Phi
is a real vector space of dimension
\dim\mathfrak{h}=\dim(\operatorname{i}\mathfrak{t}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{*})
. Part (iii) follows from (ii), because
\theta=\operatorname{Id}
on
\operatorname{i}\mathfrak{t}^{*}
and
\theta=-\operatorname{Id}
on
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
.

Finally, let us show (iv). First, by (iii), we find that
\theta\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\theta\alpha}=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{-\sigma\alpha}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}
is one-dimensional, it follows that
\alpha
is imaginary if and only if
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{k}^{\mathbb{C}}
or
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Now, suppose
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{k}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Fix a non-zero
Z=X+\hat{\operatorname{i}}Y\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}
, where
X,Y\in\mathfrak{k}
. Then
B(Z,\sigma Z)=B(X,X)+B(Y,Y)<0
, so
\alpha
is compact. Analogously, if
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{C}}
, then
\alpha
is non-compact.
∎

Thanks to Proposition
3.1
Part (iv) and the relations (
3
), we immediately have the following:

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\alpha,\beta
be imaginary roots of
(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}})
, and suppose
\alpha+\beta
is a root. Then
\alpha+\beta
is imaginary, and the compactness of
\alpha+\beta
is determined by the following rules:

(5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\text{compact}+\text{compact}&=\text{compact},\\
\text{compact}+\text{non-compact}&=\text{non-compact},\\
\text{non-compact}+\text{non-compact}&=\text{compact}.\end{split}

Example 3.3

(Compact type)
.

We say that a real semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is
compact
if
\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie algebra of a compact Lie group. A real semisimple Lie algebra is compact if and only if its Killing form
B
is negative-definite
[
19
, Chapter IV.4]
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is compact. The Cartan subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
are precisely the maximal abelian subspaces of
\mathfrak{g}
. Up to automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}
, there is a unique Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Since
B
is negative-definite,
-B(\cdot,\sigma\cdot):\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\times\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a (positive-definite) Hermitian inner product on
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Thus, if
\alpha
is a root of
(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}})
and
E_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}
is a non-zero root vector, then
B(E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha})<0
, so every root of
(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}})
is imaginary and compact.

Example 3.4

(Inner type)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a real semisimple Lie algebra, and fix a Cartan involution
\theta
of
\mathfrak{g}
. We say that
\mathfrak{g}
is
inner
if
\theta
is an inner automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
. For example, if
\mathfrak{g}
is compact, then
\mathfrak{g}
is also inner, because the identity map
\operatorname{Id}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a Cartan involution. A real semisimple
\mathfrak{g}
is inner if and only if there exists a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{k}

[
19
, Chapter VI Problems 10 and 11]
. In this case,
\mathfrak{a}=0
in the decomposition
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
defined in (
4
), so all roots are imaginary.

Example 3.5

(Complex type)
.

We say that a real semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is
complex
if
\mathfrak{g}
is the underlying real Lie algebra of a complex semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}
. From Example
2.1
, recall that we have an isomorphism
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathfrak{s}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
, where
\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
is the complex Lie algebra obtained by replacing scalar multiplication in
\mathfrak{s}
with
\lambda\cdot X=\overline{\lambda}X
, and
\mathfrak{g}
embeds into
\mathfrak{s}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
via
X\mapsto(X,X)
.

The Cartan subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
are precisely the Cartan subalgebras of
\mathfrak{s}
viewed as real subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
. Thus,
\mathfrak{g}
has a unique Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
, up to automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}
. Under the identification
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathfrak{s}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
, we may write
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{h}}
. The roots of
(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}})
are
\Phi=\Phi_{1}\sqcup\Phi_{2}
, where
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
are the roots of
(\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{h})
and
(\overline{\mathfrak{s}},\overline{\mathfrak{h}})
, respectively. The induced automorphism
\sigma:\Phi\rightarrow\Phi
swaps the two factors
\Phi_{1}\leftrightarrow\Phi_{2}
. In particular, all roots are complex.

3.2.
Maximally compact Cartan subalgebras

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
\Phi
denote the roots of
(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}})
. We say that
\mathfrak{h}
is
maximally compact
if
\sigma:\Phi\rightarrow\Phi
has no fixed points, i.e. there are no real roots.

Remark 3.6
.

Every real semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a maximally compact Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
. If
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
is another maximally compact Cartan subalgebra, then there exists an inner automorphism
f
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}=f\mathfrak{h}

[
19
, Propositions 6.59, 6.60 and 6.61]
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, and write
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
as in (
4
). The following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathfrak{h}
is maximally compact.

(ii)

\dim\mathfrak{t}
is maximal among all Cartan subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
.

(iii)

If
\theta
is a Cartan involution of
\mathfrak{g}
with
\theta\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}
, then
\mathfrak{t}
is a maximal abelian subspace of
\mathfrak{k}=\text{Fix}(\theta)
.

(iv)

There exists a set of positive roots
\Phi^{+}
such that
\sigma\Phi^{+}=-\Phi^{+}
.

Proof.

The equivalence of (i), (ii) and (iii) follows from
[
19
, Propositions 6.60 and 6.70]
. Let us show that (i) and (iv) are equivalent. If
\mathfrak{h}
is not maximally compact, then there is a root
\alpha\in\Phi
such that
\sigma\alpha=\alpha
. Thus, any set of positive roots
\Phi^{+}
with
\sigma\Phi^{+}=-\Phi^{+}
must satisfy
\emptyset\neq(\sigma\Phi^{+})\cap\Phi^{+}=(-\Phi^{+})\cap\Phi^{+}=\emptyset
, a contradiction.

Conversely, suppose
\mathfrak{h}
is maximally compact. Let
y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}
be a basis for
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, and let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}
be a basis for
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
. Since
\Phi\subseteq\operatorname{i}\mathfrak{t}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, we can write every root as

\alpha=a_{1}\operatorname{i}y_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}\operatorname{i}y_{k}+a_{k+1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k+\ell}x_{\ell},

where
a_{i}
are real numbers. Since
x_{i},y_{j}:\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are real, we find that

\sigma\alpha=-a_{1}\operatorname{i}y_{1}-\cdots-a_{k}\operatorname{i}y_{k}+a_{k+1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k+\ell}x_{\ell}.

Let
i(\alpha)
be the first index where
a_{i(\alpha)}\neq 0
. Let
\Phi^{+}
be the set of roots
\alpha
such that
a_{i(\alpha)}(\alpha)>0
. It is easy to see that
\Phi^{+}
is a set of positive roots. Since
\mathfrak{h}
is maximally compact, at least one of
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
is non-zero for any root
\alpha
. Thus,
\sigma\Phi^{+}=-\Phi^{+}
.
∎

Example 3.8
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}
is inner and
\mathfrak{h}
is maximally compact. By Example
3.4
and Proposition
3.7
(ii), it follows that
\mathfrak{a}=0
, so
\sigma=-\operatorname{Id}
on
\Phi
. Consequently, any set of positive roots
\Phi^{+}\subseteq\Phi
satisfies
\sigma\Phi^{+}=-\Phi^{+}
.

3.3.
Vogan diagrams

A
Vogan diagram
is a triple
\mathcal{V}=(\mathcal{D},\theta,\mathcal{P})
, where
\mathcal{D}
is a (not necessarily connected) Dynkin diagram,
\theta:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
is a graph automorphism of order
1
or
2
, and
\mathcal{P}
is a subset of the vertices of
\mathcal{D}
fixed by
\theta
. Pictorially, we denote the two-cycles of
\theta
with a bidirectional arrow, and paint the vertices in
\mathcal{P}
.

Example 3.9
.

Consider
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{1}\sqcup\mathcal{D}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
are two copies of the same Dynkin diagram. Let
\theta:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
be the map that swaps
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
. Then
\mathcal{V}=(\mathcal{D},\theta,\emptyset)
is a Vogan diagram.

Algorithm 3.10
.

We now explain how Vogan diagrams arise from a real semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Fix a maximally compact Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
\Phi

There exist root vectors

B(E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha})=1,\qquad[E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha}]=H_{\alpha},\qquad\sigma E_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}E_{\sigma\alpha}&\text{if $\alpha$ is complex}\\

Let

Proposition 3.21
.

A left-invariant Riemannian metric
g
on
G
is right
T
-invariant if and only if

(i)

g(\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha})=0
for each
\alpha\in\Phi
, and

(ii)

g(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\beta})\neq 0
implies
\beta\in\{-\alpha,\sigma\alpha\}
for any
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi
.

Proof.

Suppose
g
is right
T
-invariant. This is equivalent to
g
being
\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{t})
-invariant, i.e.
g(\operatorname{ad}(H)\cdot,\cdot)+g(\cdot,\operatorname{ad}(H)\cdot)=0
for all
H\in\mathfrak{t}
(see §
2.3
). To show (i), fix
H^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
and
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}
for some root
\alpha\in\Phi
. Since
\mathfrak{h}
is maximally compact, there exists
H\in\mathfrak{t}
such that
\alpha(H)\neq 0
(Proposition
3.1
). We find

g(H^{\prime},X)=\frac{1}{\alpha(H)}g(H^{\prime},[H,X])=\frac{1}{\alpha(H)}g([H^{\prime},H],X)=0.

To show (ii), fix
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}
and
Y\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\beta}
for roots
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi
, and suppose that
g(X,Y)\neq 0
. By
[
29
, page 5]
, the imaginary part of a root determines the real part of a root, up to a sign. Thus,
\beta\in\{-\alpha,\sigma\alpha\}
if and only if
\operatorname{i}\operatorname{Im}(\alpha+\beta)=\alpha|_{\mathfrak{t}}+\beta|_{\mathfrak{t}}=0
. Fix
H\in\mathfrak{t}
, and let us show that
\alpha(H)+\beta(H)=0
. If
\alpha(H)=\beta(H)=0
, then we are done. Without loss of generality, assume that
\alpha(H)\neq 0
. Then

g(X,Y)=\frac{1}{\alpha(H)}g([H,X],Y)=-\frac{1}{\alpha(H)}g(X,[H,Y])=-\frac{\beta(H)}{\alpha(H)}g(X,Y),

so rearranging gives
\alpha(H)+\beta(H)=0
.

Conversely, suppose (i) and (ii) hold. Fix
H\in\mathfrak{t}
,
H^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
,
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}
, and
Y\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\beta}
for roots
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi
. Then

\displaystyle g([H,H^{\prime}],X)+g(H^{\prime},[H,X])=0+\alpha(H)g(H^{\prime},X)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle g([H,X],Y)+g(X,[H,Y])=(\alpha+\beta)(H)g(X,Y)

\displaystyle=0,

as desired.

such that
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{f}(\mathcal{R},\mathfrak{l})
.

Remark 4.4
.

Observe that a subspace
\mathfrak{q}\leq\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
is the
\operatorname{i}
-eigenspace of an
\mathfrak{h}
-regular complex structure
J:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
on
G
if and only if
\mathfrak{q}
is an
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
-regular subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying
\mathfrak{q}\oplus\sigma\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
.
Now, let
\mathfrak{q}
denote a
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
-regular subalgebra

\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{f}(\mathcal{R},\mathfrak{l})=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}}\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha},

where
\mathcal{R}
is a closed subset of roots and
\mathfrak{l}\leq\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
contains
H_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}\cap(-\mathcal{R})
. Then
\mathfrak{q}\oplus\sigma\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
if and only if

\mathcal{R}\sqcup\sigma\mathcal{R}=\Phi\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{l}\oplus\sigma\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}.

We say that two subdiagrams of a Dynkin diagram are
disconnected
if they are disjoint and no vertex of one subdiagram shares an edge with a vertex in the other subdiagram.

Algorithm 4.5
.

(i)

Choose a maximally compact Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

(ii)

Choose a set of positive roots
\Phi^{+}\subseteq\Phi
such that
\sigma\Phi^{+}=-\Phi^{+}
. Let
\Delta
denote the simple roots determined by
\Phi^{+}
. Recall that
-\sigma
permutes the simple roots.

(iii)

Choose a (possibly empty) set of simple roots
\Delta_{0}\subseteq\Delta
such that the subdiagrams of
\Delta_{0}
and
-\sigma\Delta_{0}
are disconnected in the Dynkin diagram of
\Delta
.

(iv)

Choose a complex subspace
\mathfrak{l}\leq\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
such that
H_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{l}
for each
\alpha\in\Delta_{0}
, and
\mathfrak{l}\oplus\sigma\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
G
be a real semisimple Lie group, and let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.

(A)

Suppose
\mathfrak{h}
is maximally compact. Let
(\Phi^{+},\Delta_{0},\mathfrak{l})
be chosen as in Algorithm
4.5
. Set
\mathcal{R}_{0}:=\mathbb{C}\Delta_{0}\cap\Phi
,
\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}:=\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\Phi^{+}
, and
\mathcal{R}=(-\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+})\sqcup\Phi^{+}\backslash(-\sigma\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}).
Then

\mathfrak{q}:=\mathfrak{f}(\mathcal{R},\mathfrak{l})=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}}\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}

is an
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
-regular subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying
\mathfrak{q}\oplus\sigma\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
, and the corresponding left-invariant complex structure
J=J(\Phi^{+},\Delta_{0},\mathfrak{l})
is
\mathfrak{h}
-regular.

(B)

Conversely, suppose
J
is an
\mathfrak{h}
-regular complex structure on
G
. Then
\mathfrak{h}
is maximally compact, and
J=J(\Phi^{+},\Delta_{0},\mathfrak{l})
for some choice of
(\Phi^{+},\Delta_{0},\mathfrak{l})
described in Algorithm
4.5
.

We give a proof of Proposition
4.6
in §
4.2
.

Remark 4.7
.

We give a few remarks on the choices made in Algorithm
4.5
. In particular, we explain why any even-dimensional real semisimple
G
admits a regular complex structure.

(i)

By Remark
3.6
, a maximally compact
\mathfrak{h}
can always be chosen, and every regular complex structure on
G
is equivalent to one which is
\mathfrak{h}
-regular with respect to a fixed maximally compact Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
.

(ii)

By Proposition
3.7
, a
\Phi^{+}
as in Algorithm
4.5
can always be chosen. By Corollary
3.13
, the choice of
\Phi^{+}
in Algorithm
4.5
is equivalent to a choice of Vogan diagram for
\mathfrak{g}
, up to equivalence of left-invariant complex structures on
G
. In particular, if
\mathfrak{g}
has a unique Vogan diagram, then the choice of
\Phi^{+}
does not matter (e.g. if
\mathfrak{g}
is compact or complex, see Proposition
3.14
).

(iii)

The roots
\Delta_{0}
chosen as in Algorithm
4.5
must be complex, i.e. not fixed points of
-\sigma:\Phi\rightarrow\Phi
.

(iv)

Suppose
\mathfrak{h}
,
\Phi^{+}
and
\Delta_{0}
are chosen as in Algorithm
4.5
.
Assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is even-dimensional. Then
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}+2|\Phi^{+}|
is even, so
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}=2m
for some integer
m
. By identifying
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathbb{C}^{2m}
via an isomorphism
\mathfrak{h}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2m}
,
Lemma
4.12
implies that we can always choose
\mathfrak{l}
as in Algorithm
4.5
, and the set of all such
\mathfrak{l}
forms a smooth manifold
\mathcal{M}
of real dimension

\dim\mathcal{M}=\dim(\mathfrak{h})\left(\frac{1}{2}\dim(\mathfrak{h})-|\Delta_{0}|\right).

Example 4.8
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is inner, then any set of positive roots
\Phi^{+}
satisfies
\sigma\Phi^{+}=-\Phi^{+}
(Example
3.8
), and the only choice of
\Delta_{0}
is
\Delta_{0}=\emptyset
, because all roots are imaginary (Proposition
3.14
). Thus, the
\mathfrak{h}
-regular complex structures on
G
are precisely

\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha},

where
\Phi^{+}
is any set of positive roots, and
\mathfrak{l}\leq\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
is any subspace with
\mathfrak{l}\oplus\sigma\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
.

Example 4.9
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}
is complex, i.e.
\mathfrak{g}
is the underlying real Lie algebra of a complex semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}
. From Example
2.1
, we have a bi-invariant complex structure
J:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
given by
JX:=\operatorname{i}X
, where
\operatorname{i}X
is scalar multiplication in
\mathfrak{s}
.
By Proposition
3.14
,
\mathfrak{g}
has a unique Vogan diagram: the underlying Dynkin diagram is
\mathcal{D}_{1}\sqcup\mathcal{D}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{D}_{i}
is the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{s}
. The involution
-\sigma
swaps the copies
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
(see Proposition
3.14
).
Choose
\Delta_{0}
to be the simple roots corresponding to the first copy
\mathcal{D}_{1}
. Then
-\sigma\Delta_{0}
corresponds to
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, so
\Delta_{0}
and
-\sigma\Delta_{0}
are disconnected. In this case, the only choice of
\mathfrak{l}
is
\mathfrak{l}=\mathbb{C}\{H_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathcal{D}_{1}\}
, and the corresponding
\mathfrak{h}
-regular complex structure is the bi-invariant complex structure
J
on
G
.

Remark 4.10
.

The author believes that there are a couple of minor errors in

Lemma 4.12
.

Fix
m\geq 1
, and let
\sigma
denote the standard complex conjugation map of
\mathbb{C}^{2m}
. Let
\operatorname{Gr}_{m}(\mathbb{C}^{2m})
denote the Grassmannian of
m
-dimensional complex subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{2m}
. Fix an
r
-dimensional complex subspace
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2m}
such that
U\cap\sigma U=0
. Then

\mathcal{M}=\left\{V\in\operatorname{Gr}_{m}(\mathbb{C}^{2m}):U\subseteq V\text{ and }\mathcal{V}\oplus\sigma V=\mathbb{C}^{2m}\right\}

is an embedded submanifold of
\operatorname{Gr}_{m}(\mathbb{C}^{2m})
with
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{M}=2m(m-r).

Proof.

Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{2m}
denote the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2m}
. For each
m
-element subset
I
of
\{1,\ldots,2m\}
, let
V_{I}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{e_{i}:i\in I\}
,
I^{c}=\{1,\ldots,2m\}\backslash I
,

\mathcal{U}_{I}:=\left\{V\in\operatorname{Gr}_{m}(\mathbb{C}^{2m}):V\cap V_{I^{c}}=0\right\},

F_{I}:\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{I},V_{I^{c}})\rightarrow\mathcal{U}_{I},\qquad F_{I}(X):=\{v+Xv:v\in V_{I}\}.

Each
\mathcal{U}_{I}
is an open subset of
\operatorname{Gr}_{m}(\mathbb{C}^{2m})
, each
F_{I}:\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{I},V_{I^{c}})\rightarrow\mathcal{U}_{I}
is a diffeomorphism, and the sets
\mathcal{U}_{I}
cover
\operatorname{Gr}_{m}(\mathbb{C}^{2m})
.

Now, define

\mathcal{C}:=\left\{V\in\operatorname{Gr}_{m}(\mathbb{C}^{2m}):V\oplus\sigma V=\mathbb{C}^{2m}\right\}.

Let us show that
\mathcal{C}
is an open subset of
\operatorname{Gr}_{m}(\mathbb{C}^{2m})
. It suffices to show that
F_{I}^{-1}(\mathcal{C})
is open for all
I
. Choose an identification
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{I},V_{I^{c}})\cong M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
via the basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{2m}
. A straightforward computation shows that

F_{I}^{-1}(\mathcal{C})=\left\{X\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C}):\det(X-\overline{X})\neq 0\right\},

which is open.

Next, let

\mathcal{N}:=\left\{V\in\operatorname{Gr}_{m}(\mathbb{C}^{2m}):U\subseteq V\right\}.

Let us show that
\mathcal{N}
is an embedded submanifold of
\operatorname{Gr}_{m}(\mathbb{C}^{2m})
of dimension
2m(m-r)
. It suffices to show that
F_{I}^{-1}(\mathcal{N})
is either empty or an embedded submanifold of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{I},V_{I^{c}})
of dimension
2m(m-r)
for each
I
. Fix
I
, and suppose
F_{I}^{-1}(\mathcal{N})
is not empty, so that there exists
V\in\operatorname{Gr}_{m}(\mathbb{C}^{2m})
with
U\subseteq\mathcal{V}
and
V\cap\mathcal{V}_{I^{c}}=0
. In particular,
U\cap V_{I^{c}}=0
, so
\pi_{I}|_{U}:U\rightarrow V_{I}
is injective, where
\pi_{I}:\mathbb{C}^{2m}\rightarrow V_{I}
is the projection map with respect to
\mathbb{C}^{2m}=V_{I}\oplus V_{I^{c}}
. Set
U_{0}:=\pi_{I}(U)
, and let
L:U_{0}\rightarrow V_{I^{c}}
be the composition

L:U_{0}\xrightarrow{(\pi_{I}|_{U})^{-1}}U\xhookrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}^{2m}\xrightarrow{\pi_{I^{c}}}V_{I^{c}}.

A straightforward computation shows that

F^{-1}_{I}(\mathcal{N})=\left\{X\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{I},V_{I^{c}}):X|_{U_{0}}=L\right\},

which is an embedded submanifold of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{I},V_{I^{c}})
of dimension
2m(m-r)
.

We are done if we show that
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{N}
is non-empty. Let
E
be the orthogonal complement of
U\oplus\sigma U
with respect to the standard Hermitian inner product of
\mathbb{C}^{2m}
. Then
\sigma E=E
and
E
is even-dimensional, so there exists a complex subspace
W\subseteq E
with
W\oplus\sigma W=E
. Then
U\oplus W\in\mathcal{M}
.
∎

4.2.
Proof of Proposition
4.6

Let us begin by showing (A). In the notation of Proposition
4.6
, it suffices to show the following (see Remark
4.4
):

(A1)

\mathcal{R}
is a closed subset of
\Phi
.

(A2)

\mathcal{R}\sqcup\sigma\mathcal{R}=\Phi
.

(A3)

H_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{l}
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}\cap(-\mathcal{R})
.

Let us first show that
\mathcal{R}
is a closed subset of
\Phi
.
For each root
\alpha\in\Phi
, let us write
\alpha
in terms of the simple roots, i.e.

\alpha=\sum_{\varphi\in\Delta}c_{\varphi}(\alpha)\varphi

for some integers
c_{\varphi}(\alpha)
. Recall that the
c_{\varphi}(\alpha)
are either all non-negative or all non-positive, and
\alpha
is a positive root if and only if
c_{\varphi}(\alpha)\geq 0
for all
\varphi\in\Delta
. The
support of
\alpha
is

\operatorname{supp}(\alpha):=\left\{\varphi\in\Delta:c_{\varphi}(\alpha)\neq 0\right\}.

The subdiagram of the Dynkin diagram of
\Delta
corresponding to
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\subseteq\Delta
is always connected
[
6
, page 179, Corollary 3]
. Observe that
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
if and only if
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\subseteq\Delta_{0}
. Define
\mathcal{R}_{1}:=\mathcal{R}\backslash\mathcal{R}_{0}=\Phi^{+}\backslash(\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}\sqcup-\sigma\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+})
, and

\Delta_{1}:=\Delta\backslash(\Delta_{0}\sqcup-\sigma\Delta_{0})
. Note that
\Delta_{1}
does not necessarily generate
\mathcal{R}_{1}
. Let us show the following statement:

(7)

Let
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
be a positive root. Then
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{1}
if and only if
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\cap\Delta_{1}\neq\emptyset
.

First, suppose that
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{1}
. For the sake of contradiction, suppose that
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\cap\Delta_{1}=\emptyset
. Then
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\subseteq\Delta_{0}\sqcup-\sigma\Delta_{0}
. Recall that, by assumption,
\Delta_{0}
and
-\sigma\Delta_{0}
are disconnected in the Dynkin diagram of
\Delta
. Since
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)
is connected, either
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\subseteq\Delta_{0}
or
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\subseteq-\sigma\Delta_{0}
. Therefore,
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\sqcup\sigma\mathcal{R}_{0}
, which contradicts
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{1}
.
Conversely, suppose that
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\cap\Delta_{1}\neq\emptyset
. Then
\alpha
does not belong to
\mathcal{R}_{0}\sqcup\sigma\mathcal{R}_{0}
, so
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{1}
.

Now, to show that
\mathcal{R}
is closed, fix
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{R}
, and suppose that
\alpha+\beta
is a root. If
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
, then clearly
\alpha+\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
.
Suppose
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{1}
. By (
7
), there exists
\varphi\in\Delta_{1}
such that
c_{\varphi}(\alpha)\geq 1
. If
\beta
is positive, then
c_{\varphi}(\alpha+\beta)\geq c_{\varphi}(\alpha)\geq 1
. If
\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
, then
c_{\varphi}(\beta)=0
, so
c_{\varphi}(\alpha+\beta)=c_{\varphi}(\alpha)\geq 1
. In either case, it follows that
\alpha+\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{1}
, thanks to (
7
).

Next, let us show that
\mathcal{R}\sqcup\sigma\mathcal{R}=\Phi
. Observe that

\mathcal{R}\cap\sigma\mathcal{R}=(\mathcal{R}_{1}\cap\sigma\mathcal{R}_{1})\sqcup(\mathcal{R}_{1}\cap\sigma\mathcal{R}_{0})\sqcup(\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\sigma\mathcal{R}_{1})\sqcup(\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\sigma\mathcal{R}_{0}).

Since
\Delta_{0}\sqcup(-\sigma\Delta_{0})
is a linearly independent subset of
(\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}})^{*}
, it follows that
\mathbb{C}\Delta_{0}\cap\mathbb{C}(-\sigma\Delta_{0})=0
, so
\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\sigma\mathcal{R}_{0}=\emptyset
.
Since
\mathcal{R}_{1}=\Phi^{+}\backslash(\mathcal{R}_{0}\sqcup\sigma\mathcal{R}_{0})
, we have
\sigma\mathcal{R}_{1}=-\mathcal{R}_{1}
. By definition, we have
\emptyset=\mathcal{R}_{1}\cap\sigma\mathcal{R}_{1}=\mathcal{R}_{1}\cap\sigma\mathcal{R}_{0}=\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\sigma\mathcal{R}_{1}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{R}\cap\sigma\mathcal{R}=\emptyset
. The union
\mathcal{R}\sqcup\sigma\mathcal{R}=\Phi
follows because
|\mathcal{R}|=\frac{1}{2}|\Phi|.

Finally, let us show that
H_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{l}
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}\cap(-\mathcal{R})
; it suffices to show that
\mathcal{R}_{0}=\mathcal{R}\cap(-\mathcal{R})
. It is obvious that
\mathcal{R}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{R}\cap(-\mathcal{R})
. For the opposite inclusion, suppose
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}\cap(-\mathcal{R})
. Since
\sigma\mathcal{R}_{1}=-\mathcal{R}_{1}
, (A2) implies that
(-\mathcal{R}_{1})\cap\mathcal{R}=\emptyset
. Thus,
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
. This completes the proof of (A).

Let us show (B). Using the notation of Proposition
4.6
Part (B), set

\mathcal{R}_{0}:=\mathcal{R}\cap(-\mathcal{R})
and
\mathcal{R}_{1}:=\mathcal{R}\backslash\mathcal{R}_{0}.
It suffices to show the following three facts:

(B1)

Let
\mathcal{R}^{+}_{0}
be a set of positive roots for
\mathcal{R}_{0}
. Then

\Phi^{+}:=\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}\sqcup-\sigma\mathcal{R}^{+}_{0}\sqcup\mathcal{R}_{1}

is a set of positive roots for
\Phi
such that
\sigma\Phi^{+}=-\Phi^{+}
.

(B2)

If
\Delta_{0}
denotes the simple roots of
\mathcal{R}^{+}_{0}
and
\Delta
denotes the simple roots of
\Phi^{+}
, then
\Delta_{0}\subseteq\Delta
.

(B3)

The subdiagrams corresponding to
\Delta_{0}
and
-\sigma\Delta_{0}
are disconnected in the Dynkin diagram of
\Delta
.

Indeed, suppose that (B1), (B2) and (B3) hold. (B1) implies that
\sigma:\Phi\rightarrow\Phi
has no fixed points. Thus,
\mathfrak{h}
is maximally compact, since there are no real roots. Parts (B1), (B2) and (B3) imply that
(\Phi^{+},\Delta_{0},\mathfrak{l})
satisfy the conditions described in Algorithm
4.5
. Finally, observe that
\mathcal{R}_{0}=\mathbb{C}\Delta_{0}\cap\Phi
,
\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}=\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\Phi^{+}
, and
\mathcal{R}=(-\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+})\sqcup\Phi^{+}\backslash(-\sigma\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}),
so

is one of the subalgebras constructed in Part (A).

(i)

\sigma\mathcal{R}_{1}=-\mathcal{R}_{1}
.

(ii)

If
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
and
\beta\in\sigma\mathcal{R}_{0}
, then
\alpha+\beta
is not a root.

To show (i), observe that

. Therefore, it follows that

, so

, since these two sets have the same cardinality. To show (ii), fix

,

, and for the sake of contradiction, suppose
\alpha+\beta
is a root. Without loss of generality, suppose that

. Since
\mathcal{R}
is closed,
\beta=(\alpha+\beta)-\alpha\in\mathcal{R}
, a contradiction. Now, to show (B1), fix a set of positive roots
\mathcal{R}^{+}_{0}
of
\mathcal{R}_{0}
, and define
\Phi^{+}
as in (B1). First, let us show that
\Phi^{+}
is a closed subset. Fix
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi^{+}
, and suppose
\alpha+\beta
is a root.

(1)

If
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{R}^{+}_{0}
, then
\alpha+\beta\in\mathcal{R}^{+}_{0}
, since
\mathcal{R}_{0}
is a closed subset and
\mathcal{R}^{+}_{0}
is a set of positive roots for
\mathcal{R}_{0}
. Thus, if
\alpha,\beta\in-\sigma\mathcal{R}^{+}_{0}
, then
\alpha+\beta\in-\sigma\mathcal{R}^{+}_{0}
.

(2)

If
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{1}
and
\beta\in\mathcal{R}
, then Remark

implies that
\alpha+\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{1}
.

(3)

If
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{1}
and
\beta\in-\sigma\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}
, then (i) implies that
-\sigma\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{1}

-\sigma\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{0}

-\sigma(\alpha+\beta)\in\mathcal{R}_{1}

\alpha+\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{1}

(4)

Fact (ii) implies that the case
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}

In all cases, we find that
\alpha+\beta\in\Phi^{+}
. It is easy to see that
\Phi^{+}\sqcup(-\Phi^{+})=\Phi
. The equality
\sigma\Phi^{+}=-\Phi^{+}
follows from (i).

To show (B2), fix
\gamma\in\Delta_{0}
. For the sake of contradiction, suppose
\gamma
is not simple in
\Phi^{+}
, i.e.
\gamma=\alpha+\beta
for some
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi^{+}

\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}
, but this contradicts the simplicity of
\gamma
in
\mathcal{R}^{+}_{0}
.

Finally, let us show (B3). Since
\mathcal{R}\cap\sigma\mathcal{R}=\emptyset

\Delta_{0}\cap(-\sigma\Delta_{0})=\emptyset

\alpha\in\Delta_{0}

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle<0
. Thus,
\alpha+\beta
is a root by
[

. However, (ii) tells us that
\alpha+\beta
is not a root.

In this section, let

be a real semisimple Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a maximally compact Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
\Phi
denote the roots of
(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}})
. Let
\mathfrak{t}
be as in (
4
), and let
T
be the connected subgroup of
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}
. Let
J=J(\Phi^{+},\Delta_{0},\mathfrak{l})
be an
\mathfrak{h}
-regular complex structure (see Proposition
4.6
).

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
B
. Observe that
J
is right
T
-invariant (see §
2.3
). Thus, by symmetrisation (Proposition
2.3
and Lemma
3.20
), it suffices to consider the balanced equation for left-invariant right
T
-invariant Hermitian metrics on
(G,J)
.

5.1.
Left-invariant right
T
-invariant balanced metrics

By Lemma
3.19
, there exist non-zero root vectors
E_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}
such that

(8)

[E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha}]=H_{\alpha},\qquad\sigma E_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}E_{\sigma\alpha}&\text{if $\alpha$ is complex, }\\
-s_{\alpha}E_{-\alpha}&\text{if $\alpha$ is imaginary},\end{cases}

where
H_{\alpha}
is the dual of
\alpha
with respect to the Killing form
B
of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
restricted to
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
, and

s_{\alpha}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\alpha$ is compact,}\\
-1&\text{if $\alpha$ is non-compact.}\end{cases}

Let
\varepsilon^{\alpha}:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
denote the dual of
E_{\alpha}
with respect to
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}
.

By definition of a regular complex structure, the
\operatorname{i}
-eigenspace of
J=J(\Phi^{+},\Delta_{0},\mathfrak{l})
is

\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}}\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha},\qquad\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{0}\sqcup\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{Im}}\sqcup\mathcal{R}_{2},

where
\mathcal{R}_{0}=\mathbb{R}\Delta_{0}\cap\Phi
,
\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{Im}}
are the imaginary positive roots, and
\mathcal{R}_{2}
is the set of complex roots in
\Phi^{+}\backslash(\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}\sqcup(-\sigma\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}))
. Using Proposition
3.21
, it is easy to see the following:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
g
be a left-invariant right
T
-invariant Hermitian metric on
(G,J)
, and let
\omega:=g(J\cdot,\cdot)
be the corresponding positive
(1,1)
-form. Then

\omega=\omega|_{\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}}+\operatorname{i}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}}\lambda_{\alpha}\varepsilon^{\alpha}\wedge\sigma\varepsilon^{\alpha}+\operatorname{i}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{2}}\mu_{\alpha}\varepsilon^{\alpha}\wedge\varepsilon^{-\alpha},

where

(i)

\omega|_{h\times\mathfrak{h}}
is a positive
(1,1)
-form on
(\mathfrak{h},J|_{\mathfrak{h}})
,

(ii)

\lambda_{\alpha}=g(E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha})
is a positive real number for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}
, and

(iii)

\mu_{\alpha}=g(E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha})
for
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{2}
are complex numbers satisfying
\mu_{-\sigma\alpha}=\overline{\mu_{\alpha}}
and
\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{-\sigma\alpha}-|\mu_{\alpha}|^{2}>0.

Conversely, any such
\omega
defines a left-invariant right
T
-invariant Hermitian metric on
(G,J)
.

Decompose
\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{Im}}=\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{c}}\sqcup\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{nc}}
, where
\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{c}}
and
\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{nc}}
are the compact and non-compact positive roots, respectively. Observe that
-\sigma\mathcal{R}_{2}=\mathcal{R}_{2}
, so
-\sigma
partitions
\mathcal{R}_{2}
into two-element orbits, and we can choose a subset
\mathcal{R}_{3}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{2}
such that
\mathcal{R}_{3}\sqcup(-\sigma\mathcal{R}_{3})=\mathcal{R}_{2}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Using the notation of Lemma
5.1
, a left-invariant right
T
-invariant Hermitian metric
g
on
(G,J)
is balanced if and only if

(9)

\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{c}}}\frac{\alpha}{\lambda_{\alpha}}-\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{nc}}}\frac{\alpha}{\lambda_{\alpha}}+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{2}}\frac{\mu_{-\sigma\alpha}\alpha}{D_{\alpha}}=0,

where
D_{\alpha}:=\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{-\sigma\alpha}-|\mu_{\alpha}|^{2}>0
.

Proof.

Let
\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{n}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{q}
with respect to the Hermitian inner product
g(\cdot,\sigma\cdot):\mathfrak{q}\times\mathfrak{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. By
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
,
g
is balanced if and only if

\sum[V_{i},\sigma V_{i}]=0.

By the Gram-Schmidt process, an orthonormal basis
\{V_{i}\}
for
\mathfrak{q}
is given by

\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{r}\}\sqcup\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\alpha}}}E_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{0}\sqcup\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{Im}}\right\}\sqcup\left\{Z_{\alpha}^{(1)},Z_{\alpha}^{(2)}:\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{3}\right\},

where
H_{1},\ldots,H_{r}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathfrak{l}
, and

Z_{\alpha}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\alpha}}}E_{\alpha},\qquad Z_{\alpha}^{(2)}=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{\alpha}}}{\sqrt{D_{\alpha}}}\left(E_{-\sigma\alpha}-\frac{\overline{\mu_{\alpha}}}{\lambda_{\alpha}}E_{\alpha}\right).

We find

\displaystyle\sum[V_{i},\sigma V_{i}]

\displaystyle=\sum\underbrace{[H_{i},\sigma H_{i}]}_{=0}+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{0}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\alpha}}\underbrace{[E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha}]}_{=0}+\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{Im}}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\alpha}}[E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha}]+

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{3}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\alpha}}\underbrace{[E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha}]}_{=0}+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{3}}[Z^{(2)}_{\alpha},\sigma Z^{(2)}_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{Im}}}\frac{s_{\alpha}}{\lambda_{\alpha}}H_{\alpha}-\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{3}}\left(\mu_{-\sigma\alpha}H_{\alpha}+\mu_{\alpha}H_{-\sigma\alpha}\right).

The first equality holds because
\alpha+\sigma\alpha
is real for any root
\alpha
, so
\alpha+\sigma\alpha
cannot be a root since
\mathfrak{h}
is maximally compact. In particular,
[\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\sigma\alpha}]=0
for complex
\alpha
. The second equality follows by the properties of the root vectors
E_{\alpha}
(
8
).
Transferring the above to
(\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}})^{*}
via the Killing form gives Equation (
9
).
∎

Observe that Equation (
9
) does not depend on the choice of
\mathfrak{l}
nor
g|_{\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}}
. Therefore, the left-invariant balanced condition on
(G,J)
is determined by
(\mathcal{V},\Delta_{0})
, where
\mathcal{V}
is the Vogan diagram determined by
\Phi^{+}
.

5.2.
Balanced pairs
(\mathcal{V},\Delta_{0})

Let
\mathcal{V}
be an abstract Vogan diagram. By Proposition
3.12
,
\mathcal{V}
determines a root system
\Phi
, a complex conjugation map
\sigma:\Phi\rightarrow\Phi
, a decomposition of the roots into complex, compact and non-compact, and a set of positive roots
\Phi^{+}
such that
\sigma\Phi^{+}=-\Phi^{+}
.

Let
\Delta_{0}
be a subset of the vertices of
\mathcal{V}
such that
\Delta_{0}
and
-\sigma\Delta_{0}
are disconnected in the underlying Dynkin diagram of
\mathcal{V}
.
Let
\mathcal{R}_{0}:=\mathbb{R}\Delta_{0}\cap\Phi
, and let
\mathcal{R}_{2}
be the complex roots in
\Phi^{+}\backslash(\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}\sqcup(-\sigma\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}))
.

Definition 5.3
.

We say that the pair
(\mathcal{V},\Delta_{0})
is
balanced
if there exists a solution to Equation (
9
) for some
\lambda_{\alpha}>0
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}
and
\mu_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{2}
satisfying
\mu_{-\sigma\alpha}=\overline{\mu_{\alpha}}
and
D_{\alpha}:=\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{-\sigma\alpha}-|\mu_{\alpha}|^{2}>0
.

Let
G
be a real semisimple Lie group, let
J=J(\Phi^{+},\Delta_{0},\mathfrak{l})
be a regular complex structure, and let
\mathcal{V}
be the Vogan diagram determined by
\Phi^{+}
. Decompose
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}^{(1)}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\mathcal{V}^{(s)}
, where
\mathcal{V}^{(i)}
are the connected components of
\mathcal{V}
, and let
\Delta_{0}=\Delta_{0}^{(1)}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\Delta_{0}^{(s)}
be the corresponding decomposition of
\Delta_{0}
.
By Proposition
5.2
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

(G,J)
admits a left-invariant balanced metric.

(ii)

The pair
(\mathcal{V},\Delta_{0})
is balanced.

(iii)

Each
(\mathcal{V}^{(i)},\Delta_{0}^{(i)})
is balanced for
i=1,\ldots,s
.

Thus, Theorem
B
is proved if we show the following:

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be connected, and let
\Delta_{0}
be as above. Then
(\mathcal{V},\Delta_{0})
is balanced if and only if
\mathcal{V}
is not listed in Table
1
.

We dedicate the rest of this section to proving Proposition
5.4
. Henceforth, assume that
\mathcal{V}
is a connected Vogan diagram. Recall that
\mathcal{V}
is either inner, complex or is listed in Table
3
.

Lemma 5.5
.

If
\mathcal{V}
is connected but not inner, then
(\mathcal{V},\Delta_{0})
is balanced for any choice of
\Delta_{0}
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{V}
is complex, then all roots are complex, so
\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{c}}=\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{nc}}=\emptyset
. Setting
\lambda_{\alpha}>0
to be arbitrary and
\mu_{\alpha}=0
for
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{2}
gives a solution to Equation (
9
).

Next, suppose
\mathcal{V}
is listed in Table
3
. If we choose
\mu_{\alpha}
to be real, we can rewrite Equation (
9
) as

\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{c}}}\frac{\alpha}{\lambda_{\alpha}}-\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{nc}}}\frac{\alpha}{\lambda_{\alpha}}+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{2}}\frac{\mu_{\alpha}}{2D_{\alpha}}(\alpha-\sigma\alpha)=0.

Observe that any real number can be written as
\mu_{\alpha}/2D_{\alpha}
for some choice of
\mu_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha},\lambda_{-\sigma\alpha}
. Thus, it suffices to show that

(10)

\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{Im}}}\frac{s_{\alpha}\alpha}{\lambda_{\alpha}}\in V,\qquad V=\mathbb{R}\left\{\alpha-\sigma\alpha:\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{2}\right\}.

for some choice of
\lambda_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}_{\operatorname{Im}}
.

3
, we give a solution to (
10
).

\alpha
is compact, then
\sum c_{i}(\alpha)y_{i}\geq 0
, and if
\alpha
is non-compact, then
\sum c_{i}(\alpha)y_{i}\leq 0
. In particular, if
\alpha_{k}
is compact then
y_{k}\geq 0
, and if
\alpha_{k}
is non-compact then
y_{k}\leq 0
.

Lemma 5.8
.

Suppose
\mathcal{V}
has at least one painted vertex. Suppose
y\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
satisfies
Ay\geq 0
and
Ay\neq 0
. Then there exists
k=1,\ldots,r
such that
y_{k}<0
.

Proof.

Since
Ay\neq 0
, we know that
y\neq 0
. Therefore, there exists some
i_{1}=1,\ldots,r
such that
y_{i_{1}}\neq 0
. If
y_{i_{1}}<0
, we are done. Suppose
y_{i_{1}}>0
. In particular,
\alpha_{i_{1}}
is compact by Remark
5.7
. Choose a path in the Dynkin diagram
\alpha_{i_{1}},\alpha_{i_{2}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{\ell}}
such that
\alpha_{i_{\ell}}
is non-compact, but
\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{\ell-1}}
are compact. By Corollary
3.2
and Lemma
3.17
,
\alpha_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\alpha_{i_{\ell-1}}
is a compact positive root and
\alpha_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\alpha_{i_{\ell}}
is a non-compact positive root. Thus, Remark
5.7
implies that

\underbrace{y_{i_{1}}+\cdots+y_{i_{\ell-1}}}_{>0}+y_{i_{\ell}}\leq 0,

so
y_{i_{\ell}}<0
, as desired.
∎

We are now ready to finish the proof of Proposition
5.4
. Again, assume that
\mathcal{V}
is connected and inner.

First, suppose
\mathcal{V}
has no painted vertices. Then all roots are compact, so
s_{\alpha}=1
for all
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
. Thus,
y=(1,\ldots,1)^{\top}
satisfies
Ay\geq 0
and
Ay\neq 0
, so
(\mathcal{V},\emptyset)
is not balanced by Lemma
5.6
.

Next, suppose
\mathcal{V}
has exactly one painted vertex
\alpha_{k}
, and
\mathcal{V}
is listed in Table
1
. By Lemma
3.16
, it follows that
c_{k}(\alpha)\in\{0,1\}
for all
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
. Since
\alpha_{k}
is the only non-compact simple root,
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
is compact if and only if
c_{k}(\alpha)=0
. Let
y=(0,\ldots,0,-1,0,\ldots,0)^{\top}
, where the
-1
is in the
k
th spot. For each
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
, the
\alpha
th entry of
Ay
is

s_{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}(\alpha)y_{i}=-s_{\alpha}c_{k}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $c_{k}(\alpha)=0$,}\\
1&\text{if $c_{k}(\alpha)=1$.}\end{cases}

Thus,
Ay\geq 0
and
Ay\neq 0
, so
(\mathcal{V},\emptyset)
is not balanced.

Now, suppose that
\mathcal{V}
has exactly one painted vertex
\alpha_{k}
, but
\mathcal{V}
is not listed in Table
1
. For the sake of contradiction, suppose that
(\mathcal{V},\emptyset)
is not balanced. By Lemma
5.6
, there exists

such that
Ay\geq 0
and

. By Lemma
3.16
, there exists a non-compact positive root
\alpha
such that
\alpha_{k}+\alpha
is a root. Since

5.8
and Remark
5.7
imply that
y_{k}<0
. Since
\alpha_{k}+\alpha

5.7
implies that

0\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}(\alpha_{k}+\alpha)y_{i}=\underbrace{y_{k}}_{<0}+\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}(\alpha)y_{i}}_{\leq 0}<0,

a contradiction.

Finally, suppose
\mathcal{V}
has at least

and
Ay\neq 0

, there exists

6.
The pluriclosed condition

In this section, let
G
be a real semisimple Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a maximally compact Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
\Phi
be the roots of
(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}})
. Let
J=J(\Phi^{+},\Delta_{0},\mathfrak{l})
be a regular complex structure on
G
(see Proposition
4.6
). Let
\mathfrak{t}\leq\mathfrak{h}
be the subspace defined in (
4
), and let
T
denote the connected subgroup of
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}
.

Let
g
denote a left-invariant right
T
-invariant Hermitian metric on
(G,J)
, and let
\omega:=g(J\cdot,\cdot)
be the corresponding positive
(1,1)
-form. Let
d^{c}
be the operator on forms given by
d^{c}:=J\circ d\circ J
, where
J
acts on forms via pullback. In particular,
d^{c}\omega=d\omega(J\cdot,J\cdot,J\cdot)
. We have
dd^{c}\omega=-2\operatorname{i}\partial\overline{\partial}\omega
, so
\omega
is pluriclosed if and only if
dd^{c}\omega=0
.
The following proves Part (a) of Theorem
C
:

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}
is not inner. Then
(G,J)
admits no left-invariant pluriclosed metrics.

Proof.

Thanks to symmetrisation (Proposition
2.3
), it suffices to show that there are no left-invariant right
T
-invariant pluriclosed metrics on
(G,J)
. For the sake of contradiction, suppose
g
is such a metric, and
\omega
is the corresponding positive
(1,1)
-form. Since
\mathfrak{g}
is not inner, there exists a complex root
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\cap\mathcal{R}
(Proposition
3.14
). Fix a non-zero root vector
E_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}
, and let
H\in\mathfrak{l}
. Then

\displaystyle dd^{c}\omega(H,\sigma H,E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=-d^{c}\omega([H,\sigma H],E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha})+d^{c}\omega([H,E_{\alpha}],\sigma H,\sigma E_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\quad-d^{c}\omega([H,\sigma E_{\alpha}],\sigma H,E_{\alpha})-d^{c}\omega([\sigma H,E_{\alpha}],H,\sigma E_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\quad+d^{c}\omega([\sigma H,\sigma E_{\alpha}],H,E_{\alpha})-d^{c}\omega([E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha}],H,\sigma H)

\displaystyle=-(\alpha+\sigma\alpha)(H)d^{c}\omega(\sigma H,E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\quad+(\alpha+\sigma\alpha)(\sigma H)d^{c}\omega(H,E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{i}(\alpha+\sigma\alpha)(H)d\omega(\sigma H,E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\quad+\operatorname{i}(\alpha+\sigma\alpha)(\sigma H)d\omega(H,E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha}).

Note that
[E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha}]=0
, because
\alpha+\sigma\alpha
is non-zero and not a root, since
\mathfrak{h}
is maximally compact.
Now, given
H^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
, we find

\displaystyle(d\omega)(H^{\prime},E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=-\omega([H^{\prime},E_{\alpha}],\sigma E_{\alpha})+\omega([H^{\prime},\sigma E_{\alpha}],E_{\alpha})-\omega([E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha}],H^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-(\alpha+\sigma\alpha)(H^{\prime})\omega(E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{i}(\alpha+\sigma\alpha)(H^{\prime})g(E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle dd^{c}\omega(H,\sigma H,E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=2(\alpha+\sigma\alpha)(H)(\alpha+\sigma\alpha)(\sigma H)g(E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=2|(\alpha+\sigma\alpha)(H)|^{2}g(E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha}),

where the last equality holds because
(\alpha+\sigma\alpha)(\sigma H)=\overline{(\alpha+\sigma\alpha)(H)}
. Since
\alpha+\sigma\alpha
is non-zero, there exists
H\in\mathfrak{l}
such that
(\alpha+\sigma\alpha)(H)\neq 0
. In particular, the computation above shows that
dd^{c}\omega(H,\sigma H,E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha})\neq 0
, so
\omega
is not pluriclosed.
∎

It remains to consider the inner case, so assume from now on that
g
is inner. Let
g
be a left-invariant right
T
-invariant Hermitian metric on
(G,J)
, and let
\omega=g(J\cdot,\cdot)
be the corresponding positive
(1,1)
-form. Let
E_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}
be root vectors chosen as in Lemma
3.19
, and let
y_{\alpha}:=\omega(E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha})
for all
\alpha\in\Phi
.

Decompose
\mathfrak{g}
into its simple factors,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{s}
, let
B_{i}
denote the Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
and let
\Phi=\Phi_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\Phi_{s}
be the corresponding decomposition of the root system.
Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
denote the simple roots of
\Phi^{+}
. For each
\alpha\in\Phi
, let
c_{k}(\alpha)
be the integers given by
\alpha=\sum_{k=1}^{r}c_{k}(\alpha)\alpha_{k}
. Let
\mathcal{V}
denote the Vogan diagram induced by
\Phi^{+}
. Let
\theta
be the unique Cartan involution of
\mathfrak{g}
with
\theta\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}
, and set
\theta_{i}:=\theta|_{\mathfrak{g}_{i}}:\mathfrak{g}_{i}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{i}
.

With a slight modification to the proof,
[
20
, Theorem 3.7]
(the compact case) generalises to the following result:

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}
is inner, and
g
is left-invariant and right
T
-invariant. The following are equivalent:

(a)

g
is pluriclosed.

(b)

There exist positive real numbers
\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{s}
such that
g|_{\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}}=-(\sum_{i=1}^{s}\kappa_{i}B_{i})|_{\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}}
, and for any
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi^{+}_{i}
such that
\alpha+\beta
is a root, we have

(11)

\operatorname{i}y_{\alpha+\beta}=\operatorname{i}y_{\alpha}+\operatorname{i}y_{\beta}-\kappa_{i}.

Let
\lambda_{\alpha}=g(E_{\alpha},\sigma E_{\alpha})
, and let
s_{\alpha}=1
if
\alpha
is compact and
s_{\alpha}=-1
if
\alpha
is non-compact. By the choice of
E_{\alpha}
in Lemma
3.19
, for
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
, we find

\operatorname{i}y_{\alpha}=\operatorname{i}\omega(E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha})=-g(E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha})=s_{\alpha}g(E_{\alpha},-s_{\alpha}E_{-\alpha})=s_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}.

Therefore, Equation (
11
) is equivalent to

(12)

s_{\alpha+\beta}\lambda_{\alpha+\beta}=s_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}+s_{\beta}\lambda_{\beta}-\kappa_{i}.

Proof of Theorem
C
Part (b).

First, let us show (i) implies (ii). Suppose
(G,J)
admits a left-invariant pluriclosed metric
g
. Now, by symmetrising (Proposition
2.3
), we may assume that
g
is right
T
-invariant, so
g

6.2
(b). Since

and

-(\sum\kappa_{i}B_{i})|_{\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}}
is compatible with
J|_{\mathfrak{h}}
. Next, for the sake of contradiction, suppose that there is a connected component of
\mathcal{V}
which is not listed in Table
1

3.18

, a contradiction.

Next, let us show that (ii) implies (iii). By (ii), we may choose

such that
-(\sum\kappa_{i}B_{i})|_{\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}}
is
J|_{\mathfrak{h}}
-invariant. Set
g:=-\sum_{i}\kappa_{i}B_{i}(\cdot,\theta_{i}\cdot)
. Then

3.21

g(\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{l})=0

Therefore, by Proposition

is listed in Table

\beta

.
∎
