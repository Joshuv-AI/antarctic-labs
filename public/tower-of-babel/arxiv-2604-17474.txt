Integrated Sensing, User Location and Orientation Estimation in RIS-Assisted Dynamic Rich Scattering Environment
Anum Umer, Ivo Müürsepp, Muhammad Mahtab Alam

Abstract
This paper investigates an uplink user equipment (UE) location and orientation estimation problem in an indoor rich-scattering environment (RSE) for a multiple-input-multiple-output (MIMO) narrowband reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS) -assisted communication system. The localization problem in RSE is challenging as the uplink pilot signal undergoes multiple interactions with the RIS and dynamic scattering objects (SOs) . This paper proposes an approach where base station (BS) adaptively senses the environment with the help of RIS . Based on this sensing, it sequentially designs RIS configuration, BS beamforming and UE beamforming vectors, using the sequence of pilot transmissions from the UE to the BS , with an objective of progressively focusing them onto the UE . Towards this end, we train a bidirectional long-short term memory (biLSTM) network based controller to capture the temporal dependencies between measurements to first adaptively sense the RSE and then design RIS , BS and UE beamforming vectors to localize the UE . We evaluate the proposed approach under various RSE conditions such as various distributed RIS installations, varying number of randomly moving SOs and sensing RIS elements. Simulation results illustrate that it effectively enables adaptive sensing to achieve low localization error with robustness in various RSEs .



I
Introduction

Achieving reliable localization in
rich-scattering environments (RSEs)
dominated by dense multipath and rapid changes has become increasingly important as many modern applications depend on precise and resilient positioning
[
25
,
4
]
. Conventional approaches often fail in such settings, especially indoors, where infrastructure constraints and rich scattering further complicate signal behavior. The need is amplified in emerging 6G networks that span microwave through terahertz and optical bands, where indoor environments exhibit strong scattering
[
39
]
. The
user equipment (UE)
localization becomes challenging in narrowband indoor environments with rich scattering multipath propagation, where the
UE
pilot signal is reflected by surrounding objects and reaches the
base station (BS)
through multiple paths, after several reflections. As a result, these overlapping propagation components with different amplitudes and phases are hard to distinguish from one another.

This paper explores the role of
reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS)
as an affordable substitute for large antenna arrays for localizing the
UE
in
RSEs
. The
RIS
use numerous passive low power elements to tune reflected wave phases and regulate the propagation environment
[
5
,
20
,
18
]
. These characteristics enable them to act as intelligent anchors or enhance existing systems to mitigate interference, and directional issues to support the localization requirements for autonomous platforms, robotics, immersive applications, smart manufacturing, and traffic management
[
25
,
23
,
4
,
15
,
34
]
.
It is viewed as a key enabler for 6G localization because it can maintain a dependable reflected link when the direct path is weak or obstructed and can introduce additional anchor points without the need for extra
Radio Frequency (RF)
chains
[
4
,
34
]
. Under dynamic
RSE
conditions, repeated interactions with objects and RIS elements create composite reflections with diverse
angle-of-arrival (AoA)
and polarizations
[
7
,
27
,
26
]
, leading to nonlinear behavior far from free or quasi free space models
[
34
]
. In these settings, RIS supply controlled perturbations, whereas moving objects or humans, referred to as
scattering objects (SOs)
, introduce uncontrolled, time varying effects, yielding a nonlinear system shaped jointly by RIS configurations and environmental evolution
[
28
]
. It has been proven in
[
36
]
that with adapative sensing accurate
UE
localization can be achieved using large antenna arrays at the
UE
and
BS
, with the
RIS
acting as a virtual anchor in
RSEs
.

In this work we consider the uplink localization problem in
RSEs
in which a
UE
repeatedly transmits the known pilot signals and
BS
determines the location and orientation of the
UE
via received pilots. Since the wireless channel depends on the
UE
location, considered scenario can be viewed as an inverse mapping problem, where the goal is to infer the
UE
location from the channel realizations, which are obtained by estimating the received pilot signals. The received signal at the
BS
is reflected off multiple
RIS
and the scattering environment. The main idea is to exploit the multiple paths such that the
BS
uses the received measurements and senses the environment via the dedicated sensing RIS elements and adaptively reconfigures the
RIS
,
BS
and
UE
beamforming vectors over multiple time slots, with an objective of localizing the
UE
with maximum accuracy. The adaptive design approach is investigated in diverse multipath
RSE
scenarios and the robustness and applicability of the approach across varying
RSEs
conditions is evaluated.

I-A

Related Work

Prior studies on
UE
localization assisted by
RIS
can be broadly classified into model based and learning based approaches
[
34
]
. Model based methods optimize the
RIS
configuration through analytical formulations, typically by minimizing the
Cramér-Rao lower bound (CRLB)
of the
UE
position
[
21
,
6
,
38
,
8
,
16
]
, providing useful theoretical performance bounds. These approaches rely on explicit relationships between the
UE
location and measurements such as
time-of-arrival (ToA)
or
AoA
to construct the
Fisher Information Matrix (FIM)
. However, such formulations become impractical in multipath dominated
RSE
, where reliable path separation and association are difficult, particularly in narrowband indoor scenarios with unknown
SOs
. Extensions including Bayesian
FIM
and robust
Bayesian Cramér-Rao lower bound (BCRLB)
incorporate prior uncertainty in channel or geometric parameters
[
12
]
, and have enabled adaptive
RIS
beamforming schemes exploiting location uncertainty
[
33
]
. Nevertheless, these methods often neglect scattering effects, limiting performance in dynamic environments. Recent works instead exploit multipath structure for improved estimation, including Bayesian multi target trajectory tracking
[
44
]
, cooperative
RIS
assisted 3D positioning with low complexity estimator
[
3
]
, and tensor based integrated sensing and communication framework supporting joint multi user communication and near and far field localization
[
22
]
.

In contrast, model free localization techniques have gained increasing attention with advances in machine learning. Fingerprinting based method maps received signal measurements under multiple
RIS
configurations to
UE
locations
[
23
]
, while deep reinforcement learning has been applied to optimize
RIS
configurations for improved localization accuracy
[
37
]
. Despite their effectiveness, these approaches typically rely on fixed or predefined configurations and therefore do not fully exploit adaptive
RIS
reconfiguration. Adaptive sensing has recently emerged as a powerful paradigm across wireless applications, including initial beam alignment in millimeter wave systems
[
32
,
30
,
13
]
, precoder and combiner design for reciprocal
MIMO
channels
[
14
]
,
RIS
assisted beam tracking
[
11
]
, and localization in single path and multipath environments as well as with adaptive
RIS
codebooks
[
42
,
19
,
43
]
. These works commonly employ
recurrent neural network (RNN)
based architectures that iteratively adapt beamforming using previously observed pilot signals, progressively refining sensing toward a desired objective. However, the resulting models are generally not directly generalizable to complex
RSEs

[
36
]
. Recent studies address this limitation through learning driven approaches designed for
RSEs
, including joint fingerprinting and feature extraction networks for 3D localization
[
40
]
, self adaptive
RIS
optimization via learned surrogate models
[
29
]
, RNN based codebook driven joint
RIS
phase and position estimation
[
35
]
, and adaptive sensing strategies that iteratively refine
RIS
configurations using pilot feedback
[
36
]
. Building on
[
36
]
, this paper investigates adaptive
BS
and
UE
beamforming design in
MIMO

RIS
-assisted
RSEs
with randomly moving
SOs
, targeting joint
UE
location and orientation estimation. The proposed approach is evaluated under previously unexplored scenarios, including distributed
RIS
deployments, varying numbers of mobile scatterers, and variable sensing elements, and is analytically benchmarked against conventional
BCRLB
based optimization methods.

I-B

Main Contributions

In this paper we develop an adaptive learning based localization approach for distributed
RIS
assisted
MIMO
networks operating in
RSEs
, where both the propagation medium and network geometry are dynamic due to randomly moving
SOs
. We propose a learning based approach to jointly design
RIS
configurations as well as
BS
and
UE
beamforming vectors with the objective of simultaneously estimating
UE
position and orientation. Unlike conventional free-space or sparsely scattered localization methods that rely on
AoA
extraction or geometric ray tracing, dynamic
RSEs
exhibit dense and highly coupled multipath propagation, where localization information is embedded in complex spatio temporal scattering patterns. To address this challenge, we exploit sequential learning to capture long range dependencies in the received pilot measurements and environmental sensing measurements, enabling the network to learn the evolving relationship between scattering dynamics,
RIS
configurations, and localization performance. The proposed approach employs a
bidirectional long-short term memory (biLSTM)
based architecture to model temporal correlations induced by dynamic scattering, while adaptively refining
RIS
configuration,
BS
and
UE
beamforming vectors to progressively enhance localization accuracy. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows.

1.

In a distributed
RIS
-assisted uplink
MIMO
network operating in dynamic
RSE
, we study an adaptive sensing based localization approach for jointly and adaptively designing
RIS
configuration vectors,
BS
beamforming vectors, and
UE
beamforming vectors based on past measurements at
BS
and sensed environmental scattering. The proposed approach enables simultaneous estimation of
UE
position and orientation while accounting for scattering from randomly moving
SOs
.

2.

We provide analytical benchmarking of the proposed learning based adaptive sensing approach using
BCRLB
based modeling and optimization approach. This analysis quantifies the performance gap between the proposed data driven approach and conventional estimation theoretic limits in
RSEs
.

3.

We conduct a comprehensive performance evaluation of the proposed approach under a variety of
RSEs
conditions, including different deployment configurations of distributed RIS arrays, varying numbers of randomly moving
SOs
, and different numbers of sensing
RIS
elements. This study provides insights into the impact of environmental complexity and sensing capability on scattering estimation, localization performance and the robustness of proposed approach.

4.

The proposed framework yields interpretable results, demonstrating that the joint sequential design of RIS configurations, and
BS
and
UE
beamforming vectors progressively enhances the quality of sensed multipath information and improves
UE
localization and orientation estimation accuracy in
RSE
.

Simulation results confirm that the proposed approach performs effectively in various
RSEs
and consistently outperforms adaptive schemes that do not explicitly account for scattering dynamics as well as the theoretical benchmark.

. The simulation results are presented in Section

System Model and Problem Formulation

]
. The estimated location of

are represented by the vector
\mathbf{p}={[\mathbf{p_{1}},\mathbf{p_{2}},...,\mathbf{p_{M}}]}\in\mathbb{R}^{2M}

II-B

End-to-End Channel Modeling

We assume the channel is constant over a coherence interval spanning multiple time frames, with small-scale
RSE
perturbations, such as slight
SOs
movements or multipath changes, modeled as discrete-time structured fast-fading to capture temporal correlations and enhance scattering and
UE
localization. We consider an indoor
RSE
with low
UE
motion with coherence interval exceeding the sensing interval. Unlike the linear
BS
–
RIS
–
UE
cascade in free space,
RSE
involves complex multipath with multiple RIS elements and dynamic
SOs
, rendering the channel highly nonlinear and analytically intractable
[
2
]
. We employ a physics-based end-to-end channel for
RIS
-parameterized
RSE

[
7
]
, modeling wireless entities as configurable dipole assemblies. This approach realistically captures scattering effects while adhering to fundamental electromagnetic principles, including space-time causality, frequency dispersion, RIS phase-amplitude coupling, mutual coupling, and long-range correlations.

As shown in Figure
1
, the end-to-end channel models
SOs
, walls,
RIS
, and transceivers as dipole arrays. The total number of dipoles is
N=N_{\text{BS}}+N_{\text{UE}}+N_{\text{E}}+N_{\text{RIS}}
. Let
\textbf{H}_{\text{BS}-\text{U}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{UE}}\times N_{\text{BS}}}
denote the stochastic channel frequency response at
U
be given by

\mathbf{H}_{\text{BS}-\text{U}}=\mathbf{V}\left[(N_{\text{BS}}+1):(N_{\text{BS}}+N_{\text{U}}),1:N_{\text{BS}}\right],

(1)

where

\mathbf{V}\triangleq\text{diag}\left(\left[\alpha^{-1}_{1},\alpha^{-1}_{2},\dots,\alpha^{-1}_{N}\right]\right)\mathbf{W}^{-1},

(2)

The
i^{th}
diagonal element of
N\times N
complex-valued matrix
\mathbf{W}
represents the inverse polarizability
\alpha^{-1}_{i}(f)
of the
i^{th}
dipole. The polarizability
\alpha_{i}(f)
of the
i
th dipole is given by

\alpha_{i}(f)=\frac{\chi^{2}_{i}}{4\pi^{2}f^{2}_{\text{res},i}-4\pi^{2}f^{2}+j(\gamma^{R}_{i}+2\pi f\Gamma^{L}_{i})},

(3)

where
\chi^{2}_{i}
scales the amplitude of
\alpha_{i}(f)
,
f_{\text{res},i}
is the resonance frequency,
\gamma^{R}_{i}
captures radiation damping and,
\Gamma^{L}_{i}\geq 0
represents absorptive damping.

The off-diagonal entry
(i,j)
of
\mathbf{W}
is
-G_{ij}(f)
, representing the negative of two-dimensional free-space Green’s function between dipoles at positions
\mathbf{r}_{i}
and
\mathbf{r}_{j}
is given by

G_{ij}(\mathbf{r}_{i},\mathbf{r}_{j},f)\triangleq-j\frac{k^{2}}{4\epsilon\delta}\text{H}^{(2)}_{0}\left(k|r_{i}-r_{j}|\right).

(4)

where
\text{H}^{(2)}_{0}(\cdot)
is Hankel function of the second kind.

Pilot Transmission and Reception

Assuming a narrowband system model, when a localization request is initiated the
UE
transmits a sequence of
T
known uplink pilot symbols, with beamforming
\mathbf{\omega_{\text{UE}}}^{(t)}
, to the
BS
over
T
time frames. Let
\textbf{x}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{U}\times 1}
denote the pilot sent at the
t
-th frame. At the
BS
, the received signal from the
UE
is combined using a beamforming vector
\mathbf{\omega_{\text{BS}}}^{(t)}
. The effective signal received by the
BS
,

\mathbf{y}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{U}\times 1}
,
consisting of contributions from the direct transmission, if present,
as well as multipath components resulting from reflections off the
RIS
and the surrounding scattering environment, is given by

\mathbf{y}^{(t)}=(\omega_{\rm{BS}}^{(t)})^{\top}\textbf{H}_{\text{BS}-\text{U}}^{(t)}(\omega_{\rm{UE}}^{(t)})^{\top}\mathbf{k}^{(t)}\textbf{x}^{(t)}+\textbf{w}^{(t)},\quad t\in\mathbb{Z}

(5)

where
\textbf{w}^{(t)}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^{2})
is additive Gaussian noise. The received pilot signal is the function of
UE
and
BS
beamforming vectors as well as the
RIS
configuration at the
t
-th time stage.

In addition, the
BS
uses
S_{\text{RIS}}

RIS
elements to sense the environment and estimate the location of scattering objects
\mathbf{p}^{\rm{(t)}}
at
t
-th frame by measuring the channel
\mathbf{H}_{\text{BS}-\text{S}}\in\mathbb{C}^{S_{\text{RIS}}\times N_{\text{BS}}}
. The observations are given by

\mathbf{y}_{s}^{(t)}=\mathbf{H}_{\text{BS}-\text{S}}^{(t)}\mathbf{x}^{(t)}+\mathbf{w}^{(t)},\quad t\in\mathbb{Z}.

(6)

The goal of this paper is to analyse various
RSEs
with adaptive joint optimization of the
BS
beamforming vector,
UE
beamforming vector and and
RIS
configurations to improve the
UE
location and orientation estimate in addition to estimation of scattering in the environment.

II-D

Problem Formulation

The main objective in this work is to estimate the unknown
UE
location
\mathbf{u}
and orientation
\alpha
from
T
received observations
[\mathbf{y}^{(t)}]_{t=0}^{T-1}
, given known
BS
and
RIS
locations and a predicted
SOs
location at
t
-th time frame. The
SOs
state
\mathbf{p}
is inferred from
RIS
sensing signals
[\mathbf{y}_{s}^{(\tau)}]_{\tau=0}^{t}
at
S_{\text{RIS}}
elements to model scattering in the
RSE
. In this setting,
RIS
provides directional information by acting as virtual anchors through its dense reflective elements. We, therefore, propose to design the
RIS
configuration,
BS
beamforming vector and
UE
beamforming vector over
t
time stages to improve the
UE
location and orientation estimates at
T
.

The field observed at any spatial point in
RSE
is formed by the superposition of multiple propagation signals, with fluctuations caused by dynamic
SOs
whose behavior depends on their position
\mathbf{p}^{(t)}
. Define the mapping
G^{(t)}(\cdot):\mathbb{C}^{t+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{2M}
from the received pilot signals at
S_{\text{RIS}}
to estimated location of
SOs
for time frame
t
as

\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{(t)}=G^{(t)}([\textbf{y}_{s}^{(\tau)}]_{\tau=0}^{t}]),

(7)

where
\tau
measures previous time steps up to the current time
t
.

Block diagram of the proposed
UE
localization approach with adaptive sensing in RIS-assisted
RSE
.

Define the mapping
F^{(t)}:\mathbb{C}^{t+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{2M}\to\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{BS}}\times 1}\times\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{UE}}\times 1}\times\mathbb{C}^{1\times N_{\text{RIS}}}
from the previously received pilot signals at
BS
and mapping in (
7
) to jointly design the next
RIS
configuration,
BS
beamforming vector and
UE
beamforming vector for the
(t+1)
-th stage as

[\omega_{\rm{BS}}^{(t+1)},\omega_{\rm{UE}}^{(t+1)},\textbf{k}^{(t+1)}]=F^{(t)}\left([\textbf{y}^{(\tau)}]_{\tau=0}^{t},\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{(t)}\right).

(8)

Define the mapping
Q:\mathbb{C}^{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{2M}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
from all pilot signals received in
T
time frames and estimated scattering to the final
UE
location
\hat{\mathbf{u}}
and orientation estimate
\hat{\alpha}
as

[\hat{\mathbf{u}},\hat{\alpha}]=Q\left([\textbf{y}^{(t)}]_{t=0}^{T-1},\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{(t)}\right).

(9)

The ultimate objective of
RIS
aided localization is to minimize the localization error based on the received pilot signals. This objective can be expressed as an optimization problem that seeks to minimize the localization error, and is formulated as

\displaystyle\min_{\{G^{(t)}(\cdot),F^{(t)}(\cdot)\}_{t=0}^{T-1},Q(\cdot)}

\displaystyle\bigg[\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\mathbf{u}\|^{2}\right]},\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\|\hat{\alpha}-\alpha\|^{2}\right]}\bigg]

(10a)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}\quad\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{(t)}

\displaystyle=G^{(t)}([\textbf{y}_{s}^{(\tau)}]_{\tau=0}^{t}),

(10b)

\displaystyle[\omega_{\rm{BS}}^{(t+1)},\omega_{\rm{UE}}^{(t+1)},\textbf{k}^{(t+1)}]

\displaystyle=F^{(t)}\left([\textbf{y}^{(\tau)}]_{\tau=0}^{t},\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{(t)}\right),

(10c)

\displaystyle[\hat{\mathbf{u}},\hat{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=Q\left([\textbf{y}(t)]_{t=0}^{T-1},\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{(t)}\right),

(10d)

\displaystyle\left|\left[\mathbf{k}^{(t+1)}\right]_{n}\right|

\displaystyle=1,\quad\forall n\in N_{RIS},t,

(10e)

\displaystyle\left|\left[\mathbf{\omega_{\rm{BS}}}^{(t+1)}\right]_{n}\right|

\displaystyle=1,\quad\forall n\in N_{\rm{BS}},t,

(10f)

\displaystyle\left|\left[\mathbf{\omega_{\rm{UE}}}^{(t+1)}\right]_{n}\right|

\displaystyle=1,\quad\forall n\in N_{\rm{BS}},t.

(10g)

The analytical solution to the problem in (
10
) is difficult since it involves complex mappings for the estimation of
UE
location and orientation alongside scattering estimation, with joint optimized design of
RIS
configuration,
BS
and
UE
beamforming vectors. A common approach is to address the problem in a simpler yet greedy manner by reducing the estimation
RMSE
at every time step. However, evaluating the exact objective in (
10
) requires high dimensional integration, which is difficult to compute numerically. As a result, earlier studies
[
12
]
suggest
BCRLB
as a surrogate metric to be minimized at each time step. This approach has been used as a benchmark for comparison with the proposed
biLSTM
based data driven approach.
We employ a
biLSTM
network to perform the mapping in (
7
) to perform scattering estimation and for mapping
F^{(t)}(.)
, to construct the
RIS
configuration,
BS
and
UE
beamforming vectors. The same network is used to adaptively select the set of next
RIS
configuration vector,
BS
beamforming vector as well as the
UE
beamforming vector for subsequent measurement, thus, solving the problem stated in (
10
). Our learning based approach can achieve substantially better performance than
BCRLB
based design, as demonstrated by the simulation results in Section
V
.

III
Proposed Joint User Location and Orientation Estimation with Environment Sensing Solution

The
UE
localization in
RSE
with
RIS
has two main stages: sensing the environment to gain context and determining optimal
BS
and
UE
beamforming vector, and
RIS
configurations for
UE
localization. Sensing estimates the
SOs
state from field measurements, guiding beamforming and configuration design for accurate localization.
We propose a learning based adaptive approach that jointly designs the
RIS
configuration and the
BS
and
UE
beamforming to solve the localization problem in (
10
). It involves offline training specific to the
RSE
and online inference during deployment. At each time step
t
, the
RIS
configuration
\mathbf{k}^{(t+1)}
, the
BS
beamforming
\mathbf{\omega}_{\mathrm{BS}}^{(t+1)}
, and the
UE
beamforming
\mathbf{\omega}_{\mathrm{UE}}^{(t+1)}
vectors are chosen based on the received signal measurements history up to
t
,
[\mathbf{y}^{(\tau)}]_{\tau=0}^{t}
and the current
SOs
estimate
\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{(t)}
. Since the number of measurements increases over time, a neural network is used to compress past observations into a fixed dimensional representation that preserves the information needed for efficient adaptation.

We use a
biLSTM
network to model temporal correlations and encode sequential measurements efficiently. Instead of adapting the
RIS
configurations and beamforming vectors only from
BS
pilots measurements, the proposed two stage framework consists of scattering estimation
biLSTM
, which estimates the time varying
SOs
positions from sensing measurements at
S_{\text{RIS}}
to characterize the
RSE
, and

UE
localization
biLSTM
, which designs the

IV
Analytical Modeling of UE localization with Environment Sensing in RSE

Analytical modeling of the
UE
localization problem with scattering estimation in
RSE
is a valuable benchmark. The proposed learning driven approach addresses
UE
location and orientation estimation problem in (
10
) by estimating scattering in
RSE
in (
7
) and jointly optimizing the
RIS
configuration,
BS
and
UE
beamforming vectors mapping
\{F^{(t)}(\cdot)\}
in (
8
) over
t
time frames, with the goal of reducing localization error. Computational complexity of an analytical optimization of this multistage design would grow as the number of time frames increases. For this reason, analytical modeling of this formulation would need stage by stage greedy design of
F^{(t)}(\cdot)
. In addition, evaluating the exact
RMSE
is analytically intractable, thus, such modeling approach would rely on optimizing a tractable lower bound on
RMSE
instead.

In this section, we outline an analytical modeling approach for
UE
localization in
RSE
. The approach is devised on similar lines as in
[
12
,
42
]
. Rather than learning a direct mapping from observations to
RIS
configuration, the mapping function in
F^{(t)}(\cdot)
is decomposed into two consecutive mappings: first from the accumulated observations to the
BCRLB
, and then from that bound to the
RIS
configuration vector. At every time step, the posterior distribution of the UE location is refined using previously received pilots. This updated posterior is then used to recompute the conditional BCRLB. The
RIS
configuration vector is finally selected so as to minimize this conditional
BCRLB
.

IV-A

The unknown
UE

\mathbf{u}_{e}=\begin{bmatrix}x_{u}\quad y_{u}\quad\alpha\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(20)

To solve this optimization problem, we employ the projected gradient descent method described in
[
10
]
. Additionally, the posterior distribution
f\left(\bm{\theta}\mid[\mathbf{y}^{(\tau)}]_{\tau=0}^{t},[\mathbf{k}^{(\tau)}]_{\tau=0}^{t}\right)
is computed recursively

\displaystyle f\left(\bm{\theta}\mid[y^{(\tau)}]_{\tau=1}^{t},[y_{s}^{(\tau)}]_{\tau=1}^{t},[\mathbf{k}^{(\tau)}]_{\tau=1}^{t}\right)=

\displaystyle L\cdot f\left(y^{(t)}\mid\bm{\theta},\mathbf{k}^{(t)}\right)\,f\left(\bm{\theta}\mid[y^{(\tau)}]_{\tau=0}^{t-1},[y_{s}^{(\tau)}]_{\tau=1}^{t},[\mathbf{k}^{(\tau)}]_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\right),

(32a)

where
L
is the normalization constant.

The adaptive
BCRLB
algorithm is summarized in Algorithm
2
. For the

\bm{\theta}

. The incremental matrix
\mathbf{J}_{D}^{(t)}
follows from the squared norm of these gradients and the Schur complement and conditional

VI
Conclusion

This paper presents a learning-based
UE

RIS
-assisted communication network operating in
RSE
to sequentially design the
RIS

RIS

RIS
