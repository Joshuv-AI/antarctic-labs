A canonicity criterion for toric varieties and the classification of canonical 4-simplices
Marco Ghirlanda

Abstract
. Based on the Reid–Shepherd-Barron–Tai criterion for canonical and terminal quotient singularities, we characterize canonicity and terminality of a toric variety in terms of its local class group actions. Specializing it to the Picard number one setting, we arrive at a classification algorithm for canonical and terminal fake weighted projective spaces in any dimension. In dimension four it gives, up to isomorphism, 710450 canonical fake weighted projective spaces. We take a look at the corresponding Calabi–Yau hypersurfaces, compute the Fine interior of the associated canonical simplices, and discuss the results.

Introduction

A
canonical polytope
is a lattice polytope with the origin as the only interior lattice point. These polytopes play a

There are extensive classifications, see
[

In this article, we develop a new approach.
The idea is to use the Reid–Shepherd-Barron–Tai criterion for canonicity
of quotient singularities
[
MS84
]
.
Given an
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective toric variety
X
,
we look at Cox’s quotient construction
X=\hat{X}/H
, where

\hat{X}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r}
is an open toric

H
is a quasitorus acting diagonally on

.
For the toric fixed points

, let

be the (finite) subgroup having the local class group
\Cl(X,x_{i})

.

Let

-factorial projective toric variety. Then

is canonical
if and only if
\mathrm{age}(h)\geq 1
for all

.

We apply this result to the
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective toric varieties

2.
Characterizing canonicity and terminality

The main result of this section is a criterion for canonicity and terminality of simplicial toric varieties in terms of their local class groups.

We assume the reader is familiar with the basic concepts
of toric geometry
[
CoxLittleSchenck
]
.
Let us fix the setting and the notation for the section.
By
\Sigma
we denote a simplicial fan in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
by
v_{1},\dots,v_{r}
the primitive generators of its rays.
The
generator matrix
of
\Sigma
is the
n\times r
matrix

P=\begin{bmatrix}v_{1}&\dots&v_{r}\end{bmatrix}.

We assume that
P
is of rank
n
. An associated
degree matrix

Q
is obtained as follows. Consider
K=\mathbb{Z}^{r}/\im(P^{T})
and write

K=\mathbb{Z}^{k}\times\Gamma
with

\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}/\mu_{1}\mathbb{Z}\times\dots\times\mathbb{Z}/\mu_{s}\mathbb{Z},\quad\mu_{s}\geq 2,\quad\mu_{s}\mid\mu_{s-1},\dots,\mu_{2}\mid\mu_{1}.

Then the
i
-th column of
Q
is the image
\omega_{i}\in K
of
e_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}

under the canonical projection
\mathbb{Z}^{r}\to K
:

Q=\begin{bmatrix}\omega_{1}&\dots&\omega_{r}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}w_{1}&\dots&w_{r}\\
\eta_{1}&\dots&\eta_{r}\end{bmatrix},\quad w_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k},\ \eta_{i}\in\Gamma.

Note that the degree matrix
Q
is
almost free
, that means
that any
r-1
of its columns generate
K
.

Let
X
be the toric variety associated with
\Sigma
.
Then
\Cl(X)\cong K
.
We recall Cox’s quotient construction
[
Cox95
]
.
Denote by
C(\mu)
the group of
\mu
-th roots of unity
and consider the characteristic quasitorus, having
K

as its character group:

H=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k}\times C(\mu_{1})\times\dots\times C(\mu_{s}).

Taking the columns
\omega_{i}=(w_{i},\eta_{i})
of the degree
matrix
Q
as weights, we obtain a diagonal action of
H

on
\mathbb{C}^{r}
; explicitly, the elements
h=(t,\zeta)\in H

act via

(t,\zeta)\cdot z=(t^{w_{1}}\zeta^{\eta_{1}}z_{1},\dots,t^{w_{r}}\zeta^{\eta_{r}}z_{r}),\quad t^{w_{i}}=t_{1}^{w_{1i}}\cdots t_{k}^{w_{ki}},\quad\zeta^{\eta_{i}}=\zeta_{1}^{\eta_{1i}}\cdots\zeta_{s}^{\eta_{si}}.

Cox’s quotient construction presents
X
as the quotient of the

H
-action on the open toric subvariety of
\hat{X}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r}

given by the fan

\hat{\Sigma}=\{\hat{\sigma};\ \sigma\in\Sigma\},\qquad\hat{\sigma}=\cone(e_{i};\ v_{i}\in\sigma),

where the quotient map
p\colon\hat{X}\rightarrow X
arises from the
homomorphism
P\colon\mathbb{Z}^{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
As we assume
\Sigma
to be simplicial, the quotient is geometric, i.e.,
the
p
-fibers are exactly the
H
-orbits.
Note that for the affine toric charts we have

p^{-1}(X(\sigma))=\hat{X}(\hat{\sigma}).

Example 2.1
.

Let
X
be the toric surface arising from the (unique) complete simplicial fan
with generator matrix

P=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&-2\\
0&3&-3\end{bmatrix}.

Then the divisor class group of
X
is
\Cl(X)=K=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
, and a choice
for an associated degree matrix is

Q=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1\\
\bar{0}&\bar{1}&\bar{2}\end{bmatrix}.

The
characteristic quasitorus
is
H=\mathbb{C}^{*}\times C(3)
.
An element
(t,\zeta)\in H
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
as follows

(t,\zeta)\cdot(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=(tz_{1},t\zeta z_{2},t\zeta^{2}z_{3}).

Furthermore,
\hat{X}=\mathbb{C}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
and thus
X=(\mathbb{C}^{3}\setminus\{0\})/H
.
Taking first the quotient by the connected component
\mathbb{C}^{*}
, we see

X\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}/C(3)
.

Construction 2.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a simplicial fan in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with generator
matrix
P
.
Consider the associated toric variety
X
with Cox’s quotient
presentation
p\colon\hat{X}\to X
.
For any
n
-dimensional
\sigma\in\Sigma
,
the
affine slice
at the distinguished point

\hat{x}_{\hat{\sigma}}\in\hat{X}
is

Y_{\sigma}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{r};\ x_{i}=1\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,r\text{ with }\ v_{i}\not\in\sigma\}\subseteq\hat{X}.

Note that
Y_{\sigma}
is an
n
-dimensional
affine subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{r}
.
For the characteristic quasitorus
H
and the

stabilizer

H_{\sigma}:=\Stab_{H}(Y_{\sigma})\subseteq H

of
Y_{\sigma}\subseteq\hat{X}
, we have

H\cdot Y_{\sigma}=\hat{X}(\hat{\sigma}),\qquad X(\sigma)=\hat{X}(\hat{\sigma})/H=Y_{\sigma}/H_{\sigma}.

Moreover, the stabilizer
H_{\sigma}
of
Y_{\sigma}

equals the isotropy group
H_{\hat{x}_{\hat{\sigma}}}

and thus its character group is given by

\mathbb{X}(H_{\sigma})=\Cl(X,x_{\sigma}),

where
\Cl(X,x_{\sigma})
is the local class group of the distinguished
point
x_{\sigma}\in X
, that means the group of Weil divisors
modulo those being principal near
x_{\sigma}
. In particular,
as
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial,
H_{\sigma}
is finite.

Lemma 2.3
.

Consider a simplicial fan
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with generator
matrix
P
and associated degree matrix
Q
.
For any
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-parts
w_{j}
of
the columns
\omega_{j}
of
Q
with
v_{j}\not\in\sigma

generate
\mathbb{Q}^{k}
as a vector space.

Proof.

Note that
v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}
in
\mathbb{Q}^{n}
and
w_{1},\ldots,w_{r}
in
\mathbb{Q}^{k}

are Gale dual vector configurations.
In particular, linearly independent families of
v_{i}

correspond to generating systems of
w_{j}
.
∎

Proof of Construction
2.2
.

We show
H\cdot Y_{\sigma}=\hat{X}(\hat{\sigma})
.
Let
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq r
be the
indices with
v_{i_{j}}\not\in\sigma
.
Then
\hat{X}(\hat{\sigma})
consists precisely of
the points
x\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
with
x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}}

non-zero.
Thus
H\cdot Y_{\sigma}\subseteq\hat{X}(\hat{\sigma})
.
For the reverse inclusion, let
x\in\hat{X}(\hat{\sigma})

be given.
Consider the
k\times k
matrix

W_{\sigma}=[w_{i_{1}},\ldots,w_{i_{k}}]^{T}
.
Lemma
2.3
ensures that
W_{\sigma}
is of
rank
k
.
Thus, the homomorphism

\varphi\colon\mathbb{T}^{k}\to\mathbb{T}^{k}
associated
with
W_{\sigma}
is surjective
and we find
t\in\mathbb{T}^{k}
with

\varphi(t)=(x_{i_{1}}^{-1},\ldots,x_{i_{k}}^{-1})
.
For
h=(t,1)\in H
, this means

h\cdot x\in Y_{\sigma}
, showing
x\in H\cdot Y_{\sigma}
.

The fact that
X(\sigma)
equals
Y_{\sigma}/H_{\sigma}

is then obvious.
Moreover,
H_{\sigma}=H_{\hat{x}_{\hat{\sigma}}}
is
clear, because
\hat{x}_{\hat{\sigma}}\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
is the
point with coordinates at
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}
equal
to one and all others zero.
Finally, we obtain
\mathbb{X}(H_{\sigma})=\Cl(X,x_{\sigma})

by applying
[
ADHL
]
*Proposition 1.6.5.2 to the special
case of our toric variety
X
.
∎

Construction 2.4
.

Consider a simplicial fan
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with generator
matrix
P
and associated degree matrix
Q
.
Let
\sigma\in\Sigma
be an
n
-dimensional cone
and let
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq r
be the
indices with
v_{i_{j}}\not\in\sigma
. These give
us matrices:

W_{\sigma}\ :=\ \begin{bmatrix}w_{i_{1}}\ldots w_{i_{k}}\end{bmatrix}^{T},\qquad E_{\sigma}\ :=\ \begin{bmatrix}\eta_{i_{1}}\ldots\eta_{i_{k}}\end{bmatrix}^{T}.

where
\omega_{i}=(w_{i},\eta_{i})\in K=\mathbb{Z}^{k}\times\Gamma
is the
i
-th column
of
Q
. For any
\eta\in\Gamma
, we write
\eta=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{s})

with
0\leq e_{l}<\mu_{l}
and set
b(\eta)=(e_{1}/\mu_{1},\ldots,e_{s}/\mu_{s})
.
Then we obtain a monomorphism

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Phi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{k}/\mathrm{im}(W_{\sigma})\times\Gamma&\to&\mathbb{Q}^{k}/\mathbb{Z}^{k}\times\mathbb{Q}^{s}/\mathbb{Z}^{s},\\[5.0pt]
(c,\eta)&\mapsto&\left(W_{\sigma}^{-1}\cdot(c-E_{\sigma}\cdot b(\eta)),\,b(\eta)\right).\end{array}

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
, the matrix
W_{\sigma}
has rank
k
,
hence it is invertible over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Furthermore, replacing
c
with
c+W_{\sigma}u
, where
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
,
does not change the class of the first component in
\mathbb{Q}^{k}/\mathbb{Z}^{k}
.
Hence
\Phi
is well defined. Clearly
\Phi
is a homomorphism.
Finally, suppose
\Phi(c,\eta)=0
. Then
\eta=0
, and,
choosing a representative
c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
of
c
, we infer

c=0
from

W_{\sigma}^{-1}\cdot c^{\prime}=W_{\sigma}^{-1}\cdot\left(c^{\prime}-E_{\sigma}\cdot b(\eta)\right)=0\in\mathbb{Q}^{k}/\mathbb{Z}^{k}.

∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Consider the setting of Construction
2.4
.
With the monomorphism
\Phi
we obtain an isomorphism of groups

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathrm{im}(\Phi)&\to&H_{\sigma},\\[5.0pt]
(a,b)&\mapsto&h(a,b):=(\exp(2\pi ia_{1}),\ldots,\exp(2\pi ia_{k}),\,\exp(2\pi ib_{1}),\ldots,\exp(2\pi ib_{s})).\end{array}

Denote by
\alpha_{j}(a,b)
the fractional parts of
\omega_{m_{j}}\cdot(a,b)
,
where
1\leq m_{1}<\ldots<m_{n}\leq r
are the indices with

.
Then the eigenvalues of

acting on

\exp(2\pi i\alpha_{1}(a,b)),\ldots,\exp(2\pi i\alpha_{n}(a,b)).

We begin with a short preparation.
Write the transposed degree matrix as
Q^{T}=[W,E]
, where
W

(resp.
E
) has the upper
k
(resp. lower

) rows of

as

\mathbb{Q}_{\mu}^{s}=\mathbb{Q}_{\mu_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{Q}_{\mu_{s}}\subset\mathbb{Q}^{s}

(b_{1}/\mu_{1},\dots,b_{s}/\mu_{s})

. Consider now the homomorphism

\Psi\colon\mathbb{Q}^{k}/\mathbb{Z}^{k}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\mu}^{s}/\mathbb{Z}^{s}\ \to\ \mathbb{Q}^{r}/\mathbb{Z}^{r},\qquad(a,b)\ \mapsto\ Q^{T}\cdot(a,b)=W\cdot a+E\cdot b.

Then

tells us how the elements

of

\mathbb{C}^{r}
.
Any such element can be written uniquely as

h\ =\ (t,\zeta)\ =\ \left(\exp(2\pi ia_{1}),\ldots,\exp(2\pi ia_{k}),\exp(2\pi ib_{1}),\ldots,\exp(2\pi ib_{s})\right),

where
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in\mathbb{Q}^{k}/\mathbb{Z}^{k}
and
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{s})\in\mathbb{Q}_{\mu}^{s}/\mathbb{Z}^{s}
.
With the rows

of the transpose

h\cdot z\ =\ (t^{w_{1}}\zeta^{\eta_{1}}z_{1},\ldots,t^{w_{r}}\zeta^{\eta_{r}}z_{r})\ =\ \left(\exp(2\pi i\mathfrak{a}_{1})z_{1},\ldots,\exp(2\pi i\mathfrak{a}_{r})z_{r}\right).

Now, by Construction
2.2
, the group
H_{\sigma}\subseteq H

consists precisely of the finite order elements of

having eigenvalue

1
at the coordinates

v_{l}\not\in\sigma
.
Using the definitions of
\Phi
and

, we see that any

.
Obviously,
\Phi
is injective.
For surjectivity, let

be given.
Write

and
\zeta_{l}=\exp(2\pi ib_{l})

with

and

We claim

Here comes the main result of this section:

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
X
be the toric variety arising from a simplicial fan
\Sigma

in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and let
\sigma\in\Sigma
be an
n
-dimensional cone.
Consider the affine slice
Y_{\sigma}
and the action of its stabilizer

H_{\sigma}
from Construction
2.2
. Then

(1)

x_{\sigma}\in X
is canonical if and only if
\age(h)\geq 1
for all
1\neq h\in H_{\sigma}
,

(2)

x_{\sigma}\in X
is terminal if and only if
\age(h)>1
for all
1\neq h\in H_{\sigma}
.

Proof.

Construction
2.2
tells us that the affine toric
chart
X(\sigma)
hosting the distinguished point
x_{\sigma}

is isomorphic to the quotient
Y_{\sigma}/H_{\sigma}
, with the slice

Y_{\sigma}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}
and the finite abelian group
H_{\sigma}
.
By Proposition
2.5
, the eigenvalues of
h\in H_{\sigma}

acting on
Y_{\sigma}
are given as

\exp(2\pi i\alpha_{1}(a,b)),\dots,\exp(2\pi i\alpha_{n}(a,b)),

where the
\alpha_{l}(a,b)
can be extracted from the degree matrix
and a suitable presentation of
h\in H_{\sigma}
.
In particular we have

\age(h)=\alpha_{1}(a,b)+\dots+\alpha_{n}(a,b).

The idea is to apply the Reid–Shepherd-Barron–Tai criterion
[
MS84
]
,
which directly yields the assertion, provided that no

1\neq h\in H_{\sigma}
is a quasi-reflection on
Y_{\sigma}
.
Let us verify the latter.
Any
h\in H_{\sigma}\subseteq H
acts diagonally on the whole

\mathbb{C}^{r}
and its fixed point set is a coordinate subspace
V^{h}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r}
.
Moreover, from
h\in H_{\sigma}
and the definition of
H_{\sigma}

we infer

\hat{x}_{\hat{\sigma}}\in V(z_{l};\ v_{l}\in\sigma)\subseteq V^{h}.

Now assume that
h
is a quasi-reflection on
Y_{\sigma}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Then the fixed point set
Y_{\sigma}^{h}\subseteq Y_{\sigma}

is an affine subspace of dimension
n-1
and by the
definition of
Y_{\sigma}
, we have

Y_{\sigma}^{h}\subseteq Y_{\sigma}=\hat{x}_{\hat{\sigma}}+V(z_{l};\ v_{l}\not\in\sigma).

Thus,
Y_{\sigma}^{h}-\hat{x}_{\hat{\sigma}}
is a vector subspace
of dimension
n-1
in
V(z_{l};\ v_{l}\not\in\sigma)
consisting
of
h
-fixed points.
Consequently, the fixed point set
V^{h}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r}
of
h
is
a coordinate subspace of dimension

3.
Classifying fake weighted projective spaces

We focus on the case that our toric variety
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, projective and
of Picard number one. These varieties are called
fake weighted projective spaces

(fwps), and each of them is Fano, i.e., the anticanonical divisor is ample.
The main result of the section is Algorithm
3.10
, which
classifies the canonical fwps in a given dimension.

Setting 3.1
.

For an fwps
X
of dimension
n
, our notation and shape of the

n\times(n+1)
generator matrix and the degree matrix throughout
the section are:

P=\begin{bmatrix}v_{0}&\dots&v_{n}\end{bmatrix},\qquad Q=\begin{bmatrix}w_{0}&\dots&w_{n}\\
\eta_{0}&\dots&\eta_{n}\end{bmatrix},\quad w_{0}\leq\ldots\leq w_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>}0,\ \eta_{i}\in\Gamma,

where
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}/\mu_{1}\mathbb{Z}\times\dots\times\mathbb{Z}/\mu_{s}\mathbb{Z}
with
\mu_{s}\geq 2
and

\mu_{s}\mid\mu_{s-1},\dots,\mu_{2}\mid\mu_{1}
.
The fan
\Sigma
of
X
has the maximal cones
\sigma_{i}=\cone(v_{j};\ j\neq i)
,
where
i=0,\dots,n
.

We specialize Theorem
2.7
to fwps and, as it turns out to be
useful for computational purposes, formulate the characterizing conditions
for canonicity and terminality entirely in terms of integers.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional fwps.
For
0\leq i\leq n
,
0\leq c\leq w_{i}-1
and
b=(b_{1}/\mu_{1},\dots,b_{s}/\mu_{s})

with
0\leq b_{\ell}\leq\mu_{\ell}-1
such that
(c,b)\neq(0,0)
let

R_{ij}(c,b)
denote the smallest non-negative integer with

R_{ij}(c,b)=\mu_{1}\bigl(w_{j}c+(w_{i}\eta_{j}-w_{j}\eta_{i})\cdot b\bigr)\mod\mu_{1}w_{i}.

Then
X
is canonical if and only if
\Sigma_{j\neq i}R_{ij}(c,b)\geq\mu_{1}w_{i}

for all
i,c,b
as above
and
X
is terminal if and only if
\Sigma_{j\neq i}R_{ij}(c,b)>\mu_{1}w_{i}

for all
i,c,b
as above.

Proof.

First note that for the maximal cone
\sigma_{i}=\cone(v_{j};\ j\neq i)
,
the associated matrices
W_{\sigma_{i}}
and
E_{\sigma_{i}}
from
Construction
2.4
are just the integer
w_{i}

and the column vector
\eta_{i}
.
Moreover, Construction
2.4
and Proposition
2.5
,
uniquely represent the elements of
H_{\sigma_{i}}
as
h=h(a,b)
with

a=\frac{1}{w_{i}}(c-\eta_{i}\cdot b)\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}

and
c
,
b
as in the assertion.
For
j\neq i
, Proposition
2.5
shows that
the corresponding eigenvalue of
h=h(a,b)
on
Y_{\sigma_{i}}
has
fractional part

A_{ij}(a,b)\coloneqq\left\{w_{j}a+\eta_{j}\cdot b\right\}=\left\{\tfrac{w_{j}}{w_{i}}c+\tfrac{1}{w_{i}}(w_{i}\eta_{j}-w_{j}\eta_{i})\cdot b\right\}.

We conclude
\mathrm{age}(h)=\Sigma_{j\neq i}A_{ij}(a,b)
.
Moreover,
R_{ij}(c,b)=\mu_{1}w_{i}A_{ij}(a,b)
holds.
Together with Theorem
2.7
, this yields the
assertion.
∎

The following observation gives us in particular easy-to-compute
necessary criteria for canonicity and terminality for a degree matrix
by applying Theorem
3.2
to submatrices obtained
by removing rows.
By a
canonical (terminal) degree matrix
we mean the degree
matrix of a canonical (terminal) fwps.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

be a canonical (terminal) degree matrix and
Q^{\prime}
be the
submatrix obtained from

by deleting some of the lower
s
rows.
Then

is a canonical (terminal) degree matrix.

Proof.

Let
Q^{\prime}
arise from

by deleting the rows number
k_{1},\dots,k_{t}

from the lower
s
ones.
Then the characterizing conditions for

from Theorem

are
precisely the characterizing conditions for

with

b_{k_{1}}=\dots=b_{k_{t}}=0
.
∎

We call the first row
w=(w_{0},\ldots,w_{n})
of a degree matrix

the
weight vector
of
Q
or of the associated fwps
X
.
By Proposition
3.3
, if
X
is canonical
(terminal), then the weight vector defines a canonical (terminal)
weighted projective space.

Reminder 3.4
.

Let
X
be a canonical fwps with weight vector
w=(w_{0},\ldots,w_{n})
and
denote by
s_{i}
the
i
-th Sylvester number. Then
[
AKN
]
*Theorem 2.7 and

[
Kas1
]
*Corollary 2.11 provide us with the following bounds:

w_{0}+\ldots+w_{n}\ \leq\ (s_{n+1}-1)^{n},\qquad\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{s}\ \leq\ \frac{(w_{0}+\ldots+w_{n})^{n-1}}{w_{1}\cdots w_{n}}.

The first bound gives a finite search space for the canonical weight
vectors.
One can further restrict by upper bounds on the ratios
w_{i}/(w_{0}+\ldots+w_{n})
,
see
[
Kas1
]
*Theorem 3.5. This way, Kasprzyk classified all canonical
weighted projective spaces in dimension four, see
[
Kas2
]
.

Let
w=(w_{0},\dots,w_{n})
be a weight vector. By a

\mu
-torsion vector for
w
we mean an

\eta=(\eta_{0},\dots,\eta_{n})\in(\mathbb{Z}/\mu\mathbb{Z})^{n+1}

such that the
2\times(n+1)
matrix
Q
with first
row
w
and second row
\eta
is a degree matrix.
In this situation, we call
\eta

canonical (terminal)
if
Q
is so.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
w=(w_{0},\ldots,w_{n})
be a weight vector.

(1)

Two
\mu
-torsion vectors
\eta,\eta^{\prime}
for
w
are
row-equivalent

if there is an automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/\mu\mathbb{Z}
sending
(w_{i},\eta_{i})

to
(w_{i},\eta^{\prime}_{i})
for
i=0,\dots,n
.

(2)

A
\mu
-torsion vector
\eta
is
row-minimal
if it is minimal w.r.t.
the lexicographical order among all
\mu
-torsion vectors for
w
being
row-equivalent to
\eta
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
w=(w_{0},\dots,w_{n})
be a weight vector and

\eta=(\eta_{0},\dots,\eta_{n})
a
\mu
-torsion vector for
w
.
For
i=0,\ldots,n
set

g_{-1}=\mu,\quad g_{i}=\gcd(\mu,w_{0},\dots,w_{i}),\quad d_{i}\coloneqq\dfrac{\mu g_{i}}{g_{i-1}}.

Any
\kappa\in(\mathbb{Z}/\mu\mathbb{Z})^{*}
admits a unique
\mu
-torsion
vector
\eta(\kappa)
for
w
row-equivalent to
\kappa\eta
with

0\leq\eta(\kappa)_{i}<d_{i}
for
i=0,\dots,n
.
The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

The
\mu
-torsion vector
\eta
is row-minimal.

(2)

We have
0\leq\eta_{i}<d_{i}
for
i=0,\dots,n
and
\eta\leq\eta(\kappa)
for all
\kappa\in(\mathbb{Z}/\mu\mathbb{Z})^{*}
.

Proof.

By
[
Ghi
]
*Theorem 2.1, the
\mu
-torsion vectors row-equivalent to

\kappa\eta
are exactly the vectors
\kappa\eta+mw
with
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We fix
\kappa\in(\mathbb{Z}/\mu\mathbb{Z})^{*}
and normalize the coordinates successively via the choice of
m
.
Assume that the first
i-1
coordinates have already been normalized. Replacing
m
by

m+t\,\mu/g_{i-1}
leaves the first
i-1
coordinates unchanged and changes the
i
-th coordinate by
multiples of
(\mu/g_{i-1})w_{i}
. As

\gcd\!\left(\mu,\frac{\mu}{g_{i-1}}w_{i}\right)=\frac{\mu g_{i}}{g_{i-1}}=d_{i},

there is a choice of
t
with
0\leq\kappa\eta_{i}+(m+\mu/g_{i-1})w_{i}<d_{i}
. By induction on
i
, this gives a unique
normalized representative
\eta(\kappa)
.

Every row-equivalence class contains exactly one such normalized
representative. Therefore
\eta
is row-minimal if and only if

\eta
itself is normalized, i.e.
0\leq\eta_{i}<d_{i}
for all
i
,
and is lexicographically minimal among the representatives

\eta(\kappa)
, that is,
\eta\leq\eta(\kappa)
for all

\kappa\in(\mathbb{Z}/\mu\mathbb{Z})^{*}
.
∎

We extend the notions of row-minimality and minimality to degree
matrices
Q
with columns in
\mathbb{Z}\times\Gamma
as in
Setting
3.1
.

Definition 3.7
.

Consider degree matrices
Q=[\omega_{0},\ldots,\omega_{n}]

and
Q^{\prime}=[\omega_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,\omega_{n}^{\prime}]
with columns in
\mathbb{Z}\times\Gamma
.

(1)

Q,Q^{\prime}
are
row-equivalent
if there is an automorphism

\varphi
of
\mathbb{Z}\times\Gamma
such that
\omega_{i}^{\prime}=\varphi(\omega_{i})

for
i=0,\ldots,n
.

(2)

Q,Q^{\prime}
are
equivalent
if they are row-equivalent up to column permutations fixing their (common) weight vector.

(3)

Q
is
row-minimal
(
minimal
) if, w.r.t. the row-wise
lexicographical order,
Q\leq Q^{\prime}
holds for all
Q^{\prime}
row-equivalent
(equivalent) to
Q
.

Remark 3.8
.

Two degree matrices are equivalent if and only if the associated
fwps are isomorphic to each other.

Remark 3.9
.

Theorem 2.1 of
[
Ghi
]
provides an explicit set of generators
for the automorphism group of any finitely generated abelian group.
In the case
\mathbb{Z}\times\Gamma
, this allows an efficient encoding
of its automorphism group.

We are now ready to state our classification algorithm for canonical (terminal) fwps.
The idea is to start with a canonical (terminal) weight vector and to adjoin
torsion rows iteratively, retaining only those extensions that preserve
canonicity (terminality). At each stage, it is enough to test the torsion rows
that survived the previous one.
We denote by
Q_{\eta}
the stack matrix obtained from
Q
by adjoining
\eta
as
the last row.

Algorithm 3.10
.

Input
: a positive integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Initialization

(1)

Compute the set
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
of all canonical (terminal) weight
vectors of length
n+1
using Reminder
3.4
.

(2)

For each
w\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
, compute the set
M(w)
of all pairs

(\eta,\mu)
where
\eta
is a row-minimal canonical (terminal)

\mu
-torsion vector for
w
, using Lemma
3.6

and Reminder
3.4
.

Recursion

(1)

For all
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{i-1}
and all
(\eta,\mu)\in M(Q)
, compute
the set
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
of all minimal matrices of the form
Q_{\eta}
.

(2)

For all
Q_{\eta}\in\mathcal{Q}_{i}
, compute the set
M(Q_{\eta})
of
all pairs
(\eta^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})\in M(Q)
such that
\mu^{\prime}\mid\mu
, the bound
of Reminder
3.4
is respected, and the matrix
(Q_{\eta})_{\eta^{\prime}}

is almost-free, canonical (terminal), and row-minimal.

(3)

Terminate the algorithm if
M(Q_{\eta})
is empty for all
Q_{\eta}\in\mathcal{Q}_{i}

or if
i=n-1
.

Output:
The set
\bigcup_{i}\mathcal{Q}_{i}
of all minimal
canonical (terminal) degree matrices for
n
-dimensional fwps.

Remark 3.11
.

To isolate the row-minimal (minimal) matrices in a set
\mathcal{Q}
we
implement a sieve method.
We iteratively select a matrix in
\mathcal{Q}
and compute its entire
equivalence class.
We then identify the row-minimal (minimal) representative
Q
, add it
to the target set
\mathcal{Q^{\prime}}
, and discard from
\mathcal{Q}
all
matrices row-equivalent (equivalent) to
Q
.
This process is repeated until
\mathcal{Q}
is empty.

Proof of Algorithm
3.10
.

Every matrix output by Algorithm
3.10
is minimal
and canonical (terminal).
Conversely, let
Q
be a minimal canonical (terminal) degree matrix
with
s+1
rows.
By almost freeness, we have
s\leq n-1
. Write
Q_{i}
for the submatrix
consisting of the first
i+1
rows of
Q
. Since
Q
is minimal,
Q_{i}
is also minimal. Hence, by Proposition
3.3
and induction on
i
,
we have
Q_{i}\in\mathcal{Q}_{i}
for all
i
. In particular,

Q=Q_{s}\in\mathcal{Q}_{s}
.
∎

4.
Fine interiors

We determine the Fine interiors of all four-dimensional
canonical lattice polytopes.
First recall that the Fine interior of an
n
-dimensional
lattice polytope
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^{n}
, as introduced
in
[
Fin
]
, is the following

F(\Delta)\coloneqq\bigcap_{0\neq v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\left\{x\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}\;\middle|\;\langle x,v\rangle\geq\ord_{\Delta}(v)+1\right\},\qquad\ord_{\Delta}(v)\coloneqq\min_{x\in\Delta}\langle x,v\rangle.

The Fine interior is a polytope satisfying

\Delta^{\circ}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}\subseteq F(\Delta)\subsetneq\Delta
.
Whereas in dimensions one and two the Fine interior equals
the convex hull over
\Delta^{\circ}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, this is no
longer true in higher dimensions, where
F(\Delta)
has
rational vertices in general.

The Fine interior plays an important role in the birational
geometry of toric hypersurfaces.
Take any
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^{n}

be a
\Delta

polynomial in
n
-variables in the sense of
[

Following Batyrev

*Definition 2.15, a

of

is a projective normal
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein canonical variety

birational to

the canonical divisor is base point free.

By
[
Bat17
]
*Theorem 2.18, the hypersurface

admits a

is non-empty.
Now, let

admit a canonical model.

[
