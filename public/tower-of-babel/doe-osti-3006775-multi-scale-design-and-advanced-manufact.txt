Executive Summary of CRADA Work:
As part of the Cyclotron Road program, METAseismic sought to investigate a multi-scale
approach, both in terms of manufacturing and design, for seismic metamaterials with
applications in the energy sector. Specifically, this project explored the feasibility of using
nanofabrication to enable the incorporation of 3D nanostructured components into a
mechanical metamaterial. These components were explored to increase the seismic resilience

of our infrastructure to earthquakes, with the final aim of providing customized levels of
protection for its components. This was envisioned as the base of a multi-scale design approach
that consisted of using a multiplicity of metamaterial architectures ranging from the nano- to
the meso-scale to address the stringent seismic design objectives of electric-power equipment.

Summary of Research Results:
The research team explored a multi-scale approach, both in terms of manufacturing and design,
to improve the seismic resilience of energy infrastructure components. This project investigated
the feasibility of nano-fabrication methods such as holographic lithography and electroplating
to enable the fabrication of 3D multi-scale mechanical metamaterials. The key technical risks
associated with this approach were related to the difficulty of integrating nano-scale
components into a meso-scale metamaterial architecture and of achieving cost-effectiveness for
large-volume production. The technical performance of such multi-scale metamaterial was
measured in terms of mitigation of seismic-induced acceleration and displacement. The project
team explored nanostructures capable of meeting design goals for energy infrastructure
components applications and tested the feasibility for large volume fabrication for energy
infrastructure components applications.
