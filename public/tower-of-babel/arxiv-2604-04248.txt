Bures–Kuratowski Metrics and Simplicial Complexes for Completely Bounded Maps Thanks: The research of R.F. and S.P. was partially funded by NSERC Discovery Grants.
Remus Floricel, Sarah Plosker, Avner Sadikov

Abstract
. Let A A be a unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebra and H H a Hilbert space. The cone CP ⁡ ( A , B ⁡ ( H ) ) \mathrm{CP}(A,B(H)) of completely positive maps carries the Bures metric β \beta , closely related to the cb-norm. We introduce a family of Bures–Kuratowski (BK) metrics on CB ⁡ ( A , B ⁡ ( H ) ) \mathrm{CB}(A,B(H)) that extend β \beta exactly on CP ⁡ ( A , B ⁡ ( H ) ) \mathrm{CP}(A,B(H)) . The construction combines a Kuratowski embedding of the Bures cone, based at an anchor θ ∈ CP ⁡ ( A , B ⁡ ( H ) ) \theta\in\mathrm{CP}(A,B(H)) , with a regular-representation Hausdorff coordinate arising from universal regular models. Each BK metric admits an ℓ p \ell^{p} -wedge decomposition, splitting CB ⁡ ( A , B ⁡ ( H ) ) \mathrm{CB}(A,B(H)) into the Bures cone and a non-CP component attached at θ \theta . We then study Vietoris–Rips and Čech complexes of BK metric spaces. The wedge formula yields explicit criteria for mixed simplices, a join-type description of the mixed Rips complex, and ball-intersection criteria for mixed Čech simplices. For finite point clouds, this makes the mixed simplicial geometry computable from the two component metrics and reveals new homological features arising from the interaction between the CP and non-CP sectors.

1.
Introduction

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and
H
a Hilbert space. We consider

\mathcal{X}:=\mathrm{CB}(A,B(H)),\qquad\mathcal{C}:=\mathrm{CP}(A,B(H))\subseteq\mathcal{X},

the spaces of completely bounded and completely positive maps, respectively; see
[
13
,
14
]
for a comprehensive treatment of the subject.

The cone
\mathcal{C}
carries a natural metric geometry given by the Bures
distance
\beta
, defined through common Stinespring representations

[
10
]
. This metric is quantitatively linked to the cb-norm by the
Kretschmann–Schlingemann–Werner inequalities
[
10
]
, and therefore provides a
canonical geometric structure on the completely positive cone. In particular,
when
H=\mathbb{C}
, it reduces to the classical Bures distance on positive functionals
[
4
]
.

A basic question is whether this geometry extends in a meaningful way from

\mathcal{C}
to the full space
\mathcal{X}
. A direct extension compatible with
the cb-norm is too much to expect globally. Instead, the point of view of this
paper is that the geometry of
\mathcal{X}
should be understood relative
to the CP cone: one should preserve the Bures metric exactly on
\mathcal{C}
,
while organizing the non-CP maps as a separate geometric sector attached to the
cone in a controlled way. At a conceptual level, the BK framework links the theory of completely bounded
maps in operator algebras, through the induced metric geometry, to simplicial
constructions in algebraic topology.

Our construction uses regular representation theory for completely bounded maps.
By a theorem of Bhat–Mallick–Sumesh
[
1
]
, every completely bounded
map admits a factorization through a regular homomorphism. Universal regular
models for the completely contractive ball, introduced in Definition
2.5
, then allow us to define an intrinsic
Hausdorff-type metric
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}
on
\mathcal{X}
, obtained by
comparing implementers in universal regular models and taking a normalized
supremum over all such models (see Definition
3.6
). This metric provides the non-CP coordinate in
our construction.

We then define a family of Bures–Kuratowski metrics
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}

on
\mathcal{X}
, depending on an anchor
\theta\in\mathcal{C}
, a scale
parameter
\lambda>0
, an exponent
p\in[1,\infty]
, and a parameter

\alpha\in(0,1]
; see Definition
3.11
. These metrics agree exactly with the Bures distance on

\mathcal{C}
(Theorem
3.12
), and combine two pieces of information: a Kuratowski embedding
[
11
]
of
the Bures cone based at
\theta
, and a collapsed regular coordinate that
measures the non-CP sector. The resulting metric is intentionally asymmetric
with respect to the anchor: non-CP maps can approach the CP cone only at the
distinguished point
\theta
.

A central structural result is that every BK metric admits a canonical

\ell^{p}
-wedge decomposition

(\mathcal{X},\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha})\cong(\mathcal{C},\beta,\theta)\ \vee_{p}\ (\mathcal{Y},d_{\mathrm{reg}},\ast),

where
\mathcal{Y}
is obtained from the non-CP locus by adjoining a basepoint; see Theorem
5.5
.
Thus the space of completely bounded maps splits into two metric pieces: the
classical Bures geometry on the CP cone and a purely non-CP metric space
attached at the anchor. This decomposition clarifies several basic phenomena:
the CP cone is closed and the non-CP region accumulates on the cone only at the
anchor (Proposition
5.6
).

The paper also studies the resulting family of BK metrics as a geometric bundle
over the Bures cone. We show that changing
p
,
\lambda
, or
\alpha
does not
change the induced topology, whereas changing the anchor generally does (Proposition
4.3
). In
fact, the assignment

\theta\longmapsto\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}

defines an isometric embedding of
(\mathcal{C},\beta)
into the space of all
metrics on
\mathcal{X}
equipped with the uniform distance (Proposition
4.6
). In this sense, the
Bures cone parametrizes a canonical family of metric structures on the ambient
space of completely bounded maps.

The final section turns from metric geometry to simplicial constructions. Once a
BK space is identified as an
\ell^{p}
-wedge, it becomes natural to ask how
Vietoris–Rips and Čech complexes detect the interaction between the CP and
non-CP sectors. For Vietoris–Rips complexes, the wedge structure yields a
complete and explicit description of mixed simplices: they are governed by the
two radial functions measuring distance to the glued basepoints, leading to a
join-type decomposition of the mixed part of
\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{X},d_{\theta})

(Theorem
6.5
). In the case
p=\infty
, this description becomes
exact on finite point clouds, where the mixed simplices form a genuine simplicial
join of sublevel-set complexes from the two components.

For Čech complexes, the situation is more delicate. Mixed simplices are no
longer determined purely by pairwise radial bounds; instead, they reduce to
ball-intersection problems within one component subject to a radial constraint
imposed by the other (Proposition
6.24
). This leads to
a natural distinction between intrinsic and ambient Čech complexes in the BK
setting: ambient complexes can detect the glued basepoint and become
contractible at relatively small scales, while intrinsic complexes retain the
combinatorial geometry of the point cloud. In particular, for finite BK clouds,
the ambient Čech complex exhibits a “cone effect” driven by the basepoint,
which can collapse homology earlier than in the corresponding Vietoris–Rips
filtration. These results show that the wedge geometry produces new and
explicitly computable interactions between the CP and non-CP sectors at the
level of simplicial topology.

2.
Background: Bures distance and universal regular models

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and
H
a Hilbert space.

Definition 2.1

(Bures distance on
\mathrm{CP}(A,B(H))

[
10
, Def. 1]
)
.

Let
\phi,\psi\in\mathrm{CP}(A,B(H))
.
For a representation
\pi:A\to B(K)
, denote by
\mathscr{S}_{\pi}(\phi)
the set of all operators
V:H\to K

such that
(\pi,V,K)
is a Stinespring representation of
\phi
, i.e.
\phi(a)=V^{*}\pi(a)V
.
Define

\beta_{\pi}(\phi,\psi):=\inf\{\|V-W\|:\ V\in\mathscr{S}_{\pi}(\phi),\ W\in\mathscr{S}_{\pi}(\psi)\},

(with the convention
\beta_{\pi}(\phi,\psi)=\infty
if one of the sets is empty), and define the Bures distance by

\beta(\phi,\psi):=\inf_{\pi}\beta_{\pi}(\phi,\psi),

the infimum taken over all representations
\pi
of
A
.

It is well known that
(\mathcal{C},\beta)
is a metric space, and that
\beta
coincides with the classical Bures distance when

H=\mathbb{C}
. Moreover,
\beta
satisfies the KSW comparison inequalities (see
[
10
, Th. 1]
), namely

(1)

\frac{\|\phi-\psi\|_{cb}}{\|\phi\|_{cb}^{1/2}+\|\psi\|_{cb}^{1/2}}\ \leq\ \beta(\phi,\psi)\ \leq\ \|\phi-\psi\|_{cb}^{1/2},\qquad(\phi,\,\psi\in\mathcal{C}).

Thus
\beta
and the cb-norm induce the same topology on
\mathrm{CP}(A,B(H))
, and are quantitatively comparable via (
1
).

Definition 2.2

(cf.
[
1
, Def. 2.7]
)
.

Let
A
and
B
be unital
C^{*}
-algebras.
A (multiplicative) homomorphism
\tau:A\to B
is called regular if

\tau(u)^{*}\tau(u)=\tau(1)^{*}\tau(1)\quad\text{and}\quad\tau(u)\tau(u)^{*}=\tau(1)\tau(1)^{*}\qquad\forall\,u\in\mathcal{U}(A).

Theorem 2.3

(Regular representation theorem
[
1
, Thm. 3.2]
)
.

For every
\phi\in\mathrm{CB}(A,B(H))
there exist a Hilbert space
K
, a regular homomorphism

\tau:A\to B(K)
, and an operator
W\in B(H,K)
such that

\phi(a)=W^{*}\tau(a)W\qquad\forall a\in A.

Remark 2.4
.

A completely positive map may admit regular representations with
non-
*
-homomorphic
\tau
. Indeed, let
\phi\in\mathrm{CP}(A,B(H))
be nonzero, and choose a Stinespring
representation
\phi(a)=V^{*}\pi(a)V

with
\pi:A\to B(K)
a
*
-homomorphism.
Set
\widetilde{K}:=K\oplus K
and define

\widetilde{\tau}(a):=\begin{bmatrix}\pi(a)&\pi(a)\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad\widetilde{W}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\binom{V}{V}.

Then
\widetilde{\tau}
is a regular homomorphism,
\phi(a)=\widetilde{W}^{*}\,\widetilde{\tau}(a)\,\widetilde{W}

(a\in A),

but
\widetilde{\tau}
is not a
*
-homomorphism since

\widetilde{\tau}(1)\neq\widetilde{\tau}(1)^{*}.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{X}_{1}:=\mathrm{CC}(A,B(H))
be the space of completely contractive linear maps from
A
to
B(H)
,
A pair
(K,\tau)
, consisting of a Hilbert space
K
and a regular homomorphism

\tau:A\to B(K)
, is called a universal regular model for
\mathrm{CC}(A,B(H))
if for every

\psi\in\mathrm{CC}(A,B(H))
there exists an isometry
V_{\psi}\in B(H,K)
such that

\psi(a)=V_{\psi}^{*}\tau(a)V_{\psi}\qquad(a\in A).

Let
\mathfrak{U}
denote the collection of all universal regular models
(K,\tau)
for
\mathrm{CC}(A,B(H))
.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
1
, Thm. 3.5]
)
.

There exists a universal regular model for
\mathrm{CC}(A,B(H))
.

Remark 2.7
.

Universal regular models for
\mathrm{CC}(A,B(H))
are not unique up to unitary equivalence,
even under a direct analogue of the minimal Stinespring cyclicity condition.
One can call
(K,\tau)
minimal if
K=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{\tau(a)Vh,\tau(a)^{*}Vh:\ a\in A,\ h\in H,\ V\in\mathcal{V}\},

where
\mathcal{V}
consists of all isometries
V\in B(H,K)
implementing maps in
\mathrm{CC}(A,B(H))
.

Non-uniqueness already appears for
(A,H)=(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})
.
For
a\geq 3
define
\tau_{a}(\lambda)=\lambda P_{a}
on
K=\mathbb{C}^{2}
, where

P_{a}=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then
(\mathbb{C}^{2},\tau_{a})
is a minimal universal regular model for
\mathrm{CC}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})
, but
(\mathbb{C}^{2},\tau_{a})
and
(\mathbb{C}^{2},\tau_{b})
are not unitarily equivalent for
a\neq b
since

\sigma(P_{a}^{*}P_{a})=\{0,1+a^{2}\}\neq\{0,1+b^{2}\}.

Thus minimality alone does not yield unitary uniqueness of universal regular models.

3.
Bures–Kuratowski metrics

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and
H
a Hilbert space. For each
(K,\tau)\in\mathfrak{U}
set
T_{\tau}:=\tau(1)\in B(K)
. For
\phi\in\mathcal{X}
,
\phi\neq 0
, define

\mathscr{R}_{\tau}(\phi):=\left\{\|\phi\|_{cb}^{1/2}\,V:\ V\in B(H,K)\text{ is an isometry and }\widehat{\phi}(a)=V^{*}\tau(a)V\ \forall a\in A\right\},

where
\widehat{\phi}:=\phi/\|\phi\|_{cb}\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
. We also set
\mathscr{R}_{\tau}(\phi):=\{0\}
and
\widehat{\phi}:=0
if
\phi=0
. We then consider the linear map
\Gamma_{\tau}:B(H,K)\to B\bigl(H,K\oplus K\bigr)
defined by

\Gamma_{\tau}(W):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}W\\
T_{\tau}W\end{bmatrix},

and set
\mathscr{F}_{\tau}(\phi):=\Gamma_{\tau}(\mathscr{R}_{\tau}(\phi))
. By Theorem
2.6
, the sets
\mathscr{R}_{\tau}(\phi)
and
\mathscr{F}_{\tau}(\phi)
are nonempty.

We begin with basic norm-topological properties of the model-dependent implementer sets.

Lemma 3.1
.

Fix
(K,\tau)\in\mathfrak{U}
and
\phi\in\mathcal{X}
. Then the set
\mathscr{F}_{\tau}(\phi)
is norm-bounded, and norm-closed in

B\bigl(H,K\oplus K\bigr)
.

Proof.

Boundedness holds because every
W\in\mathscr{R}_{\tau}(\phi)
has
\|W\|=\sqrt{\|\phi\|_{cb}}
and

\|\Gamma_{\tau}(W)\|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl(\|W\|+\|T_{\tau}W\|\bigr)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1+\|T_{\tau}\|)\sqrt{\|\phi\|_{cb}}.

For closedness,
assume
\phi\neq 0
.
Let
\Gamma_{\tau}(W_{n})\in\mathscr{F}_{\tau}(\phi)
and assume
\Gamma_{\tau}(W_{n})\to X
in operator norm.
Reading the first coordinate shows
W_{n}\to W
in operator norm, hence
X=\Gamma_{\tau}(W)
.
Write
W_{n}=\sqrt{\|\phi\|_{cb}}V_{n}
with
V_{n}
isometries implementing
\widehat{\phi}
.
Then
V_{n}\to V:=W/\sqrt{\|\phi\|_{cb}}
in norm, so
V^{*}V=\lim V_{n}^{*}V_{n}=I
and

V^{*}\tau(a)V=\lim V_{n}^{*}\tau(a)V_{n}=\widehat{\phi}(a)
. Thus
W\in\mathscr{R}_{\tau}(\phi)
and
X\in\mathscr{F}_{\tau}(\phi)
.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

Fix
(K,\tau)\in\mathfrak{U}
.
Let
d_{H}
denote the Hausdorff distance on the collection of nonempty closed bounded subsets of
the normed space
B\bigl(H,K\oplus K\bigr)
.
Define

\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}(\phi,\psi):=d_{H}\bigl(\mathscr{F}_{\tau}(\phi),\mathscr{F}_{\tau}(\psi)\bigr),\qquad\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{X}.

Each fixed-model Hausdorff coordinate induces a metric on
\mathcal{X}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For each
(K,\tau)\in\mathfrak{U}
, the function
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}
is a metric on
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Symmetry and the triangle inequality follow from the corresponding properties of the Hausdorff distance.
For separation, suppose
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}(\phi,\psi)=0
. Then
\mathscr{F}_{\tau}(\phi)=\mathscr{F}_{\tau}(\psi)
.
Pick
W\in\mathscr{R}_{\tau}(\phi)
, so
\Gamma_{\tau}(W)\in\mathscr{F}_{\tau}(\phi)=\mathscr{F}_{\tau}(\psi)
.
Thus
\Gamma_{\tau}(W)=\Gamma_{\tau}(W^{\prime})
for some
W^{\prime}\in\mathscr{R}_{\tau}(\psi)
.
Since
\Gamma_{\tau}
remembers
W
in its first coordinate, it is injective, so
W=W^{\prime}
.
Finally,
\phi(a)=W^{*}\tau(a)W=\psi(a)
for all
a\in A
, hence
\phi=\psi
.
∎

The following example shows that a fixed universal model need not recover the Bures metric on the CP cone.

Example 3.4
.

In general, for a fixed universal regular model
(K,\tau)
, the coordinate
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}
need not agree with
\beta
on
\mathcal{C}
.
Take
(A,H)=(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})
and fix
a\geq 3
. Let
(\mathbb{C}^{2},\tau_{a})
be the universal regular model from
Remark
2.7
, and for
c\geq 0
define
\phi_{c}(\lambda)=c\lambda
.

For
c_{1},c_{2}\geq 0
,

(2)

\beta(\phi_{c_{1}},\phi_{c_{2}})=|\sqrt{c_{1}}-\sqrt{c_{2}}|,

since
\phi_{c}(\lambda)=V_{c}^{*}\lambda V_{c}
with
V_{c}=\sqrt{c}
, and the reverse inequality follows from

\|V\|^{2}=\phi_{c}(1)=c
for any Stinespring implementer
V
.

Let
S:=\mathscr{F}_{\tau_{a}}(\phi_{1})
. Since
\mathscr{R}_{\tau_{a}}(\phi_{c})=\sqrt{c}\,\mathscr{R}_{\tau_{a}}(\phi_{1})
and
\Gamma_{\tau_{a}}
is linear,

\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau_{a}}(\phi_{c_{1}},\phi_{c_{2}})=|\sqrt{c_{1}}-\sqrt{c_{2}}|\sup_{x\in S}\|x\|.

For
v_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+a^{2}}}\binom{1}{a},

we have
v_{0}^{*}P_{a}v_{0}=1
, so
\Gamma_{\tau_{a}}(v_{0})\in S
, and
\|\Gamma_{\tau_{a}}(v_{0})\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\|v_{0}\|^{2}+\|P_{a}v_{0}\|^{2}\bigr)=1+\frac{a^{2}}{2}.

Hence

\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau_{a}}(\phi_{c_{1}},\phi_{c_{2}})\geq\sqrt{1+\frac{a^{2}}{2}}\,|\sqrt{c_{1}}-\sqrt{c_{2}}|.

Thus
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau_{a}}
can differ from
\beta
on
\mathrm{CP}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})
.

The model-dependent radius admits quantitative bounds in terms of the cb-norm.

Lemma 3.5
.

Fix
(K,\tau)\in\mathfrak{U}
and let

c_{+}(\tau):=(\sqrt{1+\|T_{\tau}\|^{2}})/\sqrt{2}.

Then for every
\phi\in\mathcal{X}
,

{\sqrt{2}}^{-1}\,\sqrt{\|\phi\|_{cb}}\ \leq\ \delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}(\phi,0)\ \leq\ c_{+}(\tau)\,\sqrt{\|\phi\|_{cb}}.

Proof.

If
\phi=0
the claim is trivial. Assume
\phi\neq 0
and take
W\in\mathscr{R}_{\tau}(\phi)
, so
\|W\|=\sqrt{\|\phi\|_{cb}}
.
Then

\|\Gamma_{\tau}(W)\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\|W^{*}W+W^{*}T^{*}_{\tau}T_{\tau}W\|.

For the lower bound,
\|W^{*}W+W^{*}T^{*}_{\tau}T_{\tau}W\|\geq\|W^{*}W\|=\|W\|^{2}=\|\phi\|_{cb}
.
For the upper bound,
W^{*}T^{*}_{\tau}T_{\tau}W\leq\|T_{\tau}\|^{2}W^{*}W
implies

\|W^{*}W+W^{*}T^{*}_{\tau}T_{\tau}W\|\leq(1+\|T_{\tau}\|^{2})\|W^{*}W\|=(1+\|T_{\tau}\|^{2})\|\phi\|_{cb}
.
Taking suprema over
W\in\mathscr{R}_{\tau}(\phi)
yields the bounds.
∎

Definition 3.6
.

Define

\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi):=\sup_{(K,\tau)\in\mathfrak{U}}\ \overline{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}(\phi,\psi),

for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{X},
where
\overline{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}:=\frac{1}{c_{+}(\tau)}\,\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}
and
c_{+}(\tau)
is as in Lemma
3.5
.

The intrinsic regular coordinate defines a metric compatible with cb-scaling.

Lemma 3.7
.

The function
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}
is a metric on
\mathcal{X}
. Moreover, for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{X}
,
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)\leq\|\phi\|_{cb}^{1/2}+\|\psi\|_{cb}^{1/2},
and in particular
\rho(\phi):=\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,0)\leq\|\phi\|_{cb}^{1/2}.

Proof.

Each
\overline{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}
is a metric by Lemma
3.3
.
A pointwise supremum of metrics is again a metric: the triangle inequality follows by taking suprema, and if
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)=0
then in particular
\overline{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}(\phi,\psi)=0
for any fixed model
(K,\tau)
, so
\phi=\psi
.

For the bound, fix
(K,\tau)\in\mathfrak{U}
and use the Hausdorff triangle inequality:

\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}(\phi,\psi)\leq d_{H}(\mathscr{F}_{\tau}(\phi),\{0\})+d_{H}(\mathscr{F}_{\tau}(\psi),\{0\}).

By the upper bound in Lemma
3.5
, one has

\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}(\phi,0)\leq c_{+}(\tau)\|\phi\|_{cb}^{1/2}
and similarly for
\psi
.
Dividing by
c_{+}(\tau)
gives

\overline{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}(\phi,\psi)\leq\|\phi\|_{cb}^{1/2}+\|\psi\|_{cb}^{1/2}.

Taking the supremum over
(K,\tau)\in\mathfrak{U}
yields the claimed bound for
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}
, and the radius estimate is the special case
\psi=0
.
∎

This leads to the following Kuratowski-type gluing construction.

Definition 3.8
.

Fix a CP map
\theta\in\mathcal{C}
and define
\kappa_{\theta}:\mathcal{C}\to\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{C})
by

\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)(\eta):=\beta(\phi,\eta)-\beta(\theta,\eta)\qquad(\eta\in\mathcal{C}).

Extend
\kappa_{\theta}
to all of
\mathcal{X}
by setting
\kappa_{\theta}(\phi):=0
whenever
\phi\notin\mathcal{C}
. This discontinuous extension is intentional and drives the wedge phenomenon.

The Kuratowski embedding realizes the Bures metric exactly inside
\ell^{\infty}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

For all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}
one has
\|\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)-\kappa_{\theta}(\psi)\|_{\infty}=\beta(\phi,\psi),
and

\|\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)\|_{\infty}=\beta(\phi,\theta).

Proof.

This is the standard Kuratowski embedding:
|\beta(\phi,\eta)-\beta(\psi,\eta)|\leq\beta(\phi,\psi)
for all
\eta
,
with equality at
\eta=\psi
. The second identity is the special case
\psi=\theta
.
∎

Definition 3.10
.

Define

\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi):=\begin{cases}0,&\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C},\\
\rho(\phi),&\phi\notin\mathcal{C},\ \psi\in\mathcal{C},\\
\rho(\psi),&\phi\in\mathcal{C},\ \psi\notin\mathcal{C},\\
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi),&\phi,\psi\notin\mathcal{C},\end{cases}

where
\rho(\cdot)
is as in Lemma
3.7
.

We notice that the function
\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}
satisfies symmetry and the triangle inequality, but it is a pseudometric:
it vanishes on
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}
by construction.

Definition 3.11

(Bures–Kuratowski family)
.

Fix parameters
\theta\in\mathcal{C},

\lambda>0,

p\in[1,\infty],
and
\alpha\in(0,1].

Define the Bures–Kuratowski metric
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}
on
\mathcal{X}
by

\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi):=\Bigl\|\bigl(\|\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)-\kappa_{\theta}(\psi)\|_{\infty},\ \lambda\,(\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi))^{\alpha}\bigr)\Bigr\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

The BK construction yields a genuine extension of the Bures metric.

Theorem 3.12
.

For every choice of parameters
(\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha)
, the function
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}
is a metric on
\mathcal{X}
.
Moreover, for
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}
one has
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi)=\beta(\phi,\psi).

Proof.

Both coordinates
d_{1}(\phi,\psi):=\|\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)-\kappa_{\theta}(\psi)\|_{\infty}
and

d_{2}(\phi,\psi):=(\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi))^{\alpha}

satisfy the triangle inequality (for
d_{2}
this uses that
\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}
is a pseudometric and
t\mapsto t^{\alpha}
is subadditive for
\alpha\in(0,1]
).
Hence any
\ell^{p}
-combination satisfies the triangle inequality.

If
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi)=0
then
d_{1}(\phi,\psi)=0
and
d_{2}(\phi,\psi)=0
.
If
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}
, then
d_{1}(\phi,\psi)=\beta(\phi,\psi)
by Lemma
3.9
, hence
\phi=\psi
.
If
\phi,\psi\notin\mathcal{C}
, then
d_{2}(\phi,\psi)=\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)^{\alpha}=0
forces
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)=0
, hence
\phi=\psi
.
If exactly one of
\phi,\psi
lies in
\mathcal{C}
, then
d_{2}(\phi,\psi)=\rho(\text{non-CP map})^{\alpha}>0
(since
\rho(\eta)=0
iff
\eta=0\in\mathcal{C}
), a contradiction.
Thus
\phi=\psi
, and
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}
is a metric.

Finally, if
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}
then
\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)=0
and

\|\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)-\kappa_{\theta}(\psi)\|_{\infty}=\beta(\phi,\psi)
, proving CP-reduction.
∎

The next two propositions show that no BK metric admits a global KSW-type upper bound on
\mathcal{X}
, while a KSW-type lower bound holds on the non-CP sector.

Proposition 3.13
.

Fix
\theta\in\mathcal{C}
,
\lambda>0
,
p\in[1,\infty]
, and
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
Then there does not exist a function
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
f(t)\to 0
as
t\downarrow 0

such that

\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi)\leq f\bigl(\|\phi-\psi\|_{cb}\bigr)\qquad\forall\,\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{X}.

In particular, there is no constant
C>0
such that
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi)\leq C\,\|\phi-\psi\|_{cb}^{1/2}

for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Let
\omega
be a state on
A
, and define the unital completely positive map
\eta(a):=\omega(a)I_{H}
,
a\in A
.
Choose
\phi:=0
if
\theta\neq 0
, and
\phi:=\eta
if
\theta=0
. Then
\phi\in\mathcal{C}
and
\phi\neq\theta
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\psi_{n}(a):=\phi(a)+\frac{i}{n}\,\eta(a)\qquad(a\in A).

Since
\phi(1)
is selfadjoint and
\psi_{n}(1)
is not, one has
\psi_{n}\notin\mathcal{C}
for every
n
.
Moreover,
\|\psi_{n}-\phi\|_{cb}=\frac{1}{n}\,\|\eta\|_{cb}=\frac{1}{n},

because
\eta
is unital completely positive.

On the other hand,
\kappa_{\theta}(\psi_{n})=0
by definition, while
\phi\in\mathcal{C}
gives
\|\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)\|_{\infty}=\beta(\phi,\theta)

by Lemma
3.9
. Therefore

\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi_{n})=\Bigl\|\bigl(\|\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)\|_{\infty},\,\lambda\rho(\psi_{n})^{\alpha}\bigr)\Bigr\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\geq\|\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)\|_{\infty}=\beta(\phi,\theta)>0.

Hence any function
f
as above would satisfy

f\Bigl(\frac{1}{n}\Bigr)\geq\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi_{n})\geq\beta(\phi,\theta)>0\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

contradicting
f(t)\to 0
as
t\downarrow 0
.
The final statement follows by taking
f(t)=Ct^{1/2}
.
∎

Proposition 3.14
.

Fix
(K,\tau)\in\mathfrak{U}
.
Then for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{X}
,

\|\phi-\psi\|_{cb}\leq\sqrt{2}\,\|\tau\|_{cb}\,c_{+}(\tau)\,\bigl(\|\phi\|_{cb}^{1/2}+\|\psi\|_{cb}^{1/2}\bigr)\,\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi).

Consequently, for every
\theta\in\mathcal{C}
,
\lambda>0
,
p\in[1,\infty]
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
, and all

\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C}
,

\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi)\geq\lambda\left(\frac{\|\phi-\psi\|_{cb}}{\sqrt{2}\,\|\tau\|_{cb}\,c_{+}(\tau)\,\bigl(\|\phi\|_{cb}^{1/2}+\|\psi\|_{cb}^{1/2}\bigr)}\right)^{\alpha}.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. By definition of the Hausdorff distance
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}
,
for any
X\in\mathscr{F}_{\tau}(\phi)
there exists
Y\in\mathscr{F}_{\tau}(\psi)
such that

\|X-Y\|\leq\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}(\phi,\psi)+\varepsilon.

Choose
W\in\mathscr{R}_{\tau}(\phi)
with
X=\Gamma_{\tau}(W)
, and pick
W^{\prime}\in\mathscr{R}_{\tau}(\psi)
with

Y=\Gamma_{\tau}(W^{\prime})
satisfying the bound. Since
\Gamma_{\tau}
remembers
W
in its first coordinate,

\|W-W^{\prime}\|\leq\sqrt{2}\,\|\Gamma_{\tau}(W)-\Gamma_{\tau}(W^{\prime})\|\leq\sqrt{2}\bigl(\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}(\phi,\psi)+\varepsilon\bigr).

Now
\phi(a)=W^{*}\tau(a)W
and
\psi(a)=W^{\prime*}\tau(a)W^{\prime}
for all
a\in A
, hence for any
n
and any
x\in M_{n}(A)
,

\|\phi^{(n)}(x)-\psi^{(n)}(x)\|=\|(W-W^{\prime})^{*}\tau^{(n)}(x)W+W^{\prime*}\tau^{(n)}(x)(W-W^{\prime})\|\leq\|\tau\|_{cb}\,\|x\|\,(\|W\|+\|W^{\prime}\|)\,\|W-W^{\prime}\|.

Taking suprema over
n
and
x
yields

\|\phi-\psi\|_{cb}\leq\|\tau\|_{cb}\,(\|W\|+\|W^{\prime}\|)\,\|W-W^{\prime}\|.

By definition of
\mathscr{R}_{\tau}(\cdot)
,
\|W\|=\|\phi\|_{cb}^{1/2}
and
\|W^{\prime}\|=\|\psi\|_{cb}^{1/2}
.
Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
gives

\|\phi-\psi\|_{cb}\leq\sqrt{2}\,\|\tau\|_{cb}\,\bigl(\|\phi\|_{cb}^{1/2}+\|\psi\|_{cb}^{1/2}\bigr)\,\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}(\phi,\psi).

Since

\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)=\sup_{(L,\sigma)\in\mathfrak{U}}\frac{1}{c_{+}(\sigma)}\,\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\sigma}(\phi,\psi)\geq\frac{1}{c_{+}(\tau)}\,\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}(\phi,\psi),

it follows that
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\tau}(\phi,\psi)\leq c_{+}(\tau)\,\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi),

and therefore

\|\phi-\psi\|_{cb}\leq\sqrt{2}\,\|\tau\|_{cb}\,c_{+}(\tau)\,\bigl(\|\phi\|_{cb}^{1/2}+\|\psi\|_{cb}^{1/2}\bigr)\,\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi).

This proves the first inequality.

If now
\phi,\psi\notin\mathcal{C}
, then by definition of the BK metric,

\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi)=\bigl\|(0,\lambda\,\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)^{\alpha})\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\lambda\,\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)^{\alpha}.

Hence

\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)=\lambda^{-1/\alpha}\bigl(\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi)\bigr)^{1/\alpha},

and substituting this into the first inequality yields the stated estimate on

\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C}
.
∎

4.
The Bures–Kuratowski metric bundle

With notation as in the previous section, we first show that the BK topology is independent of the choice of
\ell^{p}
-norm and scaling parameter.

Proposition 4.1
.

Fix
\theta\in\mathcal{C}
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. For any
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
\lambda,\mu>0
,
the metrics
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}
and
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\mu,q,\alpha}
are bi-Lipschitz equivalent.
In particular, they induce the same topology on
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Since all norms on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
are equivalent, there exists a constant
C_{p,q}\geq 1
such that

C_{p,q}^{-1}\|(a,b)\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq\|(a,b)\|_{\ell^{q}}\leq C_{p,q}\|(a,b)\|_{\ell^{p}}\qquad\text{for all }(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Also, for every
\lambda,\mu>0
,

\min\{1,\mu/\lambda\}\,\|(a,\lambda b)\|_{\ell^{q}}\leq\|(a,\mu b)\|_{\ell^{q}}\leq\max\{1,\mu/\lambda\}\,\|(a,\lambda b)\|_{\ell^{q}}

for all
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Now apply these inequalities with
a=\|\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)-\kappa_{\theta}(\psi)\|_{\infty},

b=\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)^{\alpha}.

Then, for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{X}
,

C_{p,q}^{-1}\min\{1,\mu/\lambda\}\,\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi)\leq\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\mu,q,\alpha}(\phi,\psi)\leq C_{p,q}\max\{1,\mu/\lambda\}\,\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi),

and therefore the two metrics are bi-Lipschitz equivalent.
∎

The exponent parameter does not affect the induced topology.

Proposition 4.2
.

Fix
\theta\in\mathcal{C}
,
\lambda>0
, and
p\in[1,\infty]
. If
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in(0,1]
, then

\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}
and
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha^{\prime}}
induce the same topology on
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Write
d_{\alpha}(\phi,\psi):=\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi),

d_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\phi,\psi):=\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha^{\prime}}(\phi,\psi),
for simplicity,
and set
u(\phi,\psi):=\|\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)-\kappa_{\theta}(\psi)\|_{\infty},
and

v(\phi,\psi):=\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi).

Then
d_{\alpha}(\phi,\psi)=\bigl\|\bigl(u(\phi,\psi),\,\lambda\,v(\phi,\psi)^{\alpha}\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}},
and similarly
d_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\phi,\psi)=\bigl\|\bigl(u(\phi,\psi),\,\lambda\,v(\phi,\psi)^{\alpha^{\prime}}\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}.

It suffices to show that the identity map
\mathrm{id}:(\mathcal{X},d_{\alpha})\to(\mathcal{X},d_{\alpha^{\prime}})

is continuous.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
and define

\omega_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}(t):=\Bigl\|\bigl(t,\lambda^{\,1-\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha}t^{\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha}\bigr)\Bigr\|_{\ell^{p}},

for every
t\geq 0.

Since
t\mapsto t^{\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha}
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
and vanishes at
0
,
we have
\omega_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}(t)\to 0

as
t\downarrow 0
.
Choose
\delta>0
such that
\omega_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}(\delta)<\varepsilon,
and let
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{X}
satisfy
d_{\alpha}(\phi,\psi)<\delta
.
Since each coordinate of a vector is bounded above by its
\ell^{p}
-norm, we obtain
u(\phi,\psi)<\delta,
and
\lambda\,v(\phi,\psi)^{\alpha}<\delta.

Hence
v(\phi,\psi)<(\delta/\lambda)^{1/\alpha},

and therefore

\lambda\,v(\phi,\psi)^{\alpha^{\prime}}<\lambda\Bigl(\frac{\delta}{\lambda}\Bigr)^{\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha}=\lambda^{\,1-\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha}\delta^{\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha}.

Using that the
\ell^{p}
-norm is monotone in each coordinate on
[0,\infty)^{2}
, we get

d_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\phi,\psi)=\Bigl\|\bigl(u(\phi,\psi),\,\lambda\,v(\phi,\psi)^{\alpha^{\prime}}\bigr)\Bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq\Bigl\|\bigl(\delta,\lambda^{\,1-\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha}\delta^{\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha}\bigr)\Bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}=\omega_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}(\delta)<\varepsilon.

Thus
B_{d_{\alpha}}(\phi,\delta)\subseteq B_{d_{\alpha^{\prime}}}(\phi,\varepsilon)
for all
\phi\in\mathcal{X}
,
so
\mathrm{id}:(\mathcal{X},d_{\alpha})\to(\mathcal{X},d_{\alpha^{\prime}})
is continuous. By symmetry, the same argument with
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
interchanged completes the proof.
∎

We next show that distinct anchors give rise to inequivalent metric topologies.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
with
\theta_{1}\neq\theta_{2}
. Fix
\lambda>0
,
p\in[1,\infty]
, and
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
Then the metrics
\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{1},\lambda,p,\alpha}
and
\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{2},\lambda,p,\alpha}
induce different topologies on
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Choose any state
\omega
on
A
and define
\psi_{n}:A\to B(H)
by

\psi_{n}(a):=\frac{i}{n}\,\omega(a)\,I_{H}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
\|\psi_{n}\|_{cb}=1/n\to 0
, and
\psi_{n}\notin\mathcal{C}
for all
n
since
\psi_{n}(1)=\frac{i}{n}I_{H}
is not positive.

For the BK-metric defined at
\theta_{1}
:

\kappa_{\theta_{1}}(\theta_{1})=0
and
\kappa_{\theta_{1}}(\psi_{n})=0
, so the Kuratowski term is
0
and

\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\theta_{1},\psi_{n})=\rho(\psi_{n})\leq\|\psi_{n}\|_{cb}^{1/2}\to 0

by Lemma

\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{1},\lambda,p,\alpha}(\theta_{1},\psi_{n})\to 0

For the BK-metric defined at
\theta_{2}
:

\kappa_{\theta_{2}}(\psi_{n})=0
and

\|\kappa_{\theta_{2}}(\theta_{1})-\kappa_{\theta_{2}}(\psi_{n})\|_{\infty}=\|\kappa_{\theta_{2}}(\theta_{1})\|_{\infty}=\beta(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})>0,

so
\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{2},\lambda,p,\alpha}(\theta_{1},\psi_{n})\geq\beta(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
for all

.
Thus
\psi_{n}
converges to
\theta_{1}
in one topology but not the other.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Fix
\theta\in\mathcal{C}
.
Then the metric topology on
\mathcal{X}
induced by
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}
does not coincide with

Proof.

Choose any
\phi\in\mathcal{C}
with
\phi\neq\theta
; notice that
\beta(\phi,\theta)>0
. Let
\omega
be any state on
A
, and define a sequence
\psi_{n}\in\mathcal{X}
as in the proof of Proposition
3.13
, i.e.

\psi_{n}(a):=\phi(a)+\frac{i}{n}\,\omega(a)\,I_{H}\qquad(a\in A).

Then
\|\psi_{n}-\phi\|_{\mathrm{cb}}=1/n\to 0
, so
\psi_{n}\to\phi
in cb-norm.
However, each
\psi_{n}\notin\mathcal{C}
because

Since

for all

∎

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\mathrm{Met}(\mathcal{X})
denote the collection of all metrics on
\mathcal{X}
.
For
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathrm{Met}(\mathcal{X})
define the extended pseudo-distance

D_{\infty}(d_{1},d_{2}):=\sup_{\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{X}}\bigl|d_{1}(\phi,\psi)-d_{2}(\phi,\psi)\bigr|\in[0,\infty].

The BK construction embeds the CP cone isometrically into the space of metrics.

Proposition 4.6
.

Fix
\lambda>0
,
p\in[1,\infty]
, and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Then the map
\Theta:(\mathcal{C},\beta)\to(\mathrm{Met}(\mathcal{X}),D_{\infty})
,
\Theta(\theta):=\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}
, is an isometric embedding.

Proof.

For simplicity, denote
\beta^{BK}_{\theta}:=\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha},
for all
\theta\in\mathcal{C}
. Let
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
be fixed. We show that

D_{\infty}\bigl(\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{1}},\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{2}}\bigr)=\beta(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
.

(“
\leq
”).
It is enough to show that

(3)

\bigl|\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{1}}(\phi,\psi)-\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{2}}(\phi,\psi)\bigr|\leq\beta(\theta_{1},\theta_{2}),\qquad(\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{X}).

Fix
\phi
and
\psi
in
\mathcal{X}
.
If
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}
, then
\widetilde{\delta}_{\rm reg}(\phi,\psi)=0
and CP-reduction gives

\beta^{BK}_{\theta}(\phi,\psi)=\beta(\phi,\psi)
for every
\theta
, so the difference is
0
.

If
\phi,\psi\notin\mathcal{C}
, then
\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)=\kappa_{\theta}(\psi)=0
for every
\theta
, hence

\beta^{BK}_{\theta}(\phi,\psi)=\lambda(\delta_{\rm reg}(\phi,\psi))^{\alpha}
is independent of
\theta
and the difference is again
0
.

It remains to treat the cross case. By symmetry assume
\phi\in\mathcal{C}
and
\psi\notin\mathcal{C}
.
Then
\kappa_{\theta}(\psi)=0
and
\|\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)\|_{\infty}=\beta(\phi,\theta)
, for all
\theta\in\mathcal{C}
, by Lemma
3.9
.
Also
\widetilde{\delta}_{\rm reg}(\phi,\psi)=\rho(\psi)
is independent of
\theta
.
Set
c:=\lambda(\rho(\psi))^{\alpha}\geq 0
. Then

\beta^{BK}_{\theta}(\phi,\psi)=\|(\beta(\phi,\theta),\,c)\|_{\ell^{p}}=g_{c}\bigl(\beta(\phi,\theta)\bigr).

Since the function
[0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto\|(t,c)\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\in[0,\infty)

is
1
-Lipschitz for any

, we have

\bigl|\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{1}}(\phi,\psi)-\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{2}}(\phi,\psi)\bigr|\leq\bigl|\beta(\phi,\theta_{1})-\beta(\phi,\theta_{2})\bigr|\leq\beta(\theta_{1},\theta_{2}),

which is (
3
).

(“
\geq
”)
Let
\varepsilon>0
.
Set
\phi:=\theta_{1}\in\mathcal{C}
and choose
\psi_{n}\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C}

4.3

as

3.7

Hence for
n
large enough,

\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{2}}(\phi,\psi_{n})-\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{1}}(\phi,\psi_{n})\geq\beta(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})-\varepsilon
,
so
D_{\infty}(\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{1}},\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{2}})\geq\beta(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})-\varepsilon
.

.
∎

Different anchors produce uniformly close metric structures on compact subsets.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
and set
\varepsilon:=\beta(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
.
Then:

(i)

For every subset
E\subseteq\mathcal{X}
, the identity map
\mathrm{id}_{E}:(E,\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{1}})\to(E,\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{2}})

is a
(1,\varepsilon)
-quasi-isometry, i.e. for all
x,y\in E
,

\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{1}}(x,y)-\varepsilon\ \leq\ \beta^{BK}_{\theta_{2}}(x,y)\ \leq\ \beta^{BK}_{\theta_{1}}(x,y)+\varepsilon.

(ii)

If
E\subseteq\mathcal{X}
is compact with respect to both metrics
\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{1}}
and
\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{2}}
,
then the Gromov–Hausdorff distance satisfies

d_{GH}\bigl((E,\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{1}}),\,(E,\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{2}})\bigr)\ \leq\ \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Proof.

Write
d_{1}:=\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{1}},

d_{2}:=\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{2}},
for simplicity.

(i) is (
3
) restricted to
E\times E
.

(ii) Assume that
E
is compact with respect to both
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
. Consider the diagonal correspondence
\Delta_{E}:=\{(x,x):x\in E\}\subseteq E\times E.

Its projections onto the two factors are both equal to
E
, so
\Delta_{E}
is indeed a correspondence between
(E,d_{1})
and
(E,d_{2})
.

Recall that for compact metric spaces
X
and
Y
,
d_{GH}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{R}\operatorname{dis}(R),

where the infimum is taken over all correspondences
R\subseteq X\times Y
and

\operatorname{dis}(R):=\sup\Bigl\{\bigl|d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})-d_{Y}(y,y^{\prime})\bigr|:(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in R\Bigr\};

see
[
3
, Theorem 7.3.25]
. In particular,

d_{GH}(X,Y)\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{dis}(R)

for every correspondence
R
. Applying this to
X=(E,d_{1})
,
Y=(E,d_{2})
, and
R=\Delta_{E}
, we obtain

d_{GH}\bigl((E,d_{1}),(E,d_{2})\bigr)\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{dis}(\Delta_{E}).

Now, if
(x,x),(y,y)\in\Delta_{E}
, then
\bigl|d_{1}(x,y)-d_{2}(x,y)\bigr|\leq\varepsilon

by part (i). Hence
\operatorname{dis}(\Delta_{E})=\sup_{x,y\in E}\bigl|d_{1}(x,y)-d_{2}(x,y)\bigr|\leq\varepsilon.

Therefore

d_{GH}\bigl((E,\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{1}}),\,(E,\beta^{BK}_{\theta_{2}})\bigr)=d_{GH}\bigl((E,d_{1}),(E,d_{2})\bigr)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

This proves (ii).
∎

5.
An
\ell^{p}
wedge decomposition for BK metrics

Fix
\lambda>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
, and
p\in[1,\infty]
. We begin by isolating the non-CP component and equipping it with a natural metric structure.

Definition 5.1

(Collapsed non-CP space)
.

Let
\mathcal{Y}:=(\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C})\ \sqcup\ \{\ast\}

be the disjoint union of the non-CP region with a distinguished basepoint
\ast
.
Define a metric
d_{\mathrm{reg}}
on
\mathcal{Y}
by

d_{\mathrm{reg}}(x,y):=\begin{cases}\lambda\bigl(\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(x,y)\bigr)^{\alpha},&x,y\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C},\\[2.84526pt]
\lambda\bigl(\rho(x)\bigr)^{\alpha},&x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C},\ y=\ast,\\[2.84526pt]
\lambda\bigl(\rho(y)\bigr)^{\alpha},&y\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C},\ x=\ast,\\[2.84526pt]
0,&x=y=\ast\end{cases}

where
\rho(\psi):=\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\psi,0)\in(0,\infty)
is as in Lemma
3.7
.

The following lemma shows that the collapsed non-CP component is indeed a metric space.

Lemma 5.2
.

The function
d_{\mathrm{reg}}
is a metric on
\mathcal{Y}
.

Proof.

Symmetry and nonnegativity are clear.
If
x,y,z\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C}
, then
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}
is a metric and
t\mapsto t^{\alpha}
is subadditive, hence

d_{\mathrm{reg}}(x,z)=\lambda\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(x,z)^{\alpha}\leq\lambda(\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(x,y)+\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(y,z))^{\alpha}\leq d_{\mathrm{reg}}(x,y)+d_{\mathrm{reg}}(y,z).

For a triangle involving the basepoint, note that
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(x,y)\leq\rho(x)+\rho(y)
, hence

d_{\mathrm{reg}}(x,y)=\lambda\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(x,y)^{\alpha}\leq\lambda(\rho(x)+\rho(y))^{\alpha}\leq\lambda\rho(x)^{\alpha}+\lambda\rho(y)^{\alpha}=d_{\mathrm{reg}}(x,\ast)+d_{\mathrm{reg}}(\ast,y).

Separation follows because
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}
is a metric and
\rho(x)=0
iff
x=0\in\mathcal{C}
.
∎

We henceforth regard
(\mathcal{Y},d_{\mathrm{reg}})
as a pointed metric space with basepoint
\ast
. We now introduce the
\ell^{p}
wedge construction for pointed metric spaces, a natural
\ell^{p}
-analogue of the usual gluing metric on a wedge sum; compare
[
2
, Definition 5.23 and Lemma 5.24]
for the standard gluing metric and
[
5
, §5.1 and Proposition 5.5]
for the corresponding
\ell^{p}
construction.

Definition 5.3

(
\ell^{p}
metric wedge)
.

Let
(M_{1},d_{1},m_{0})
and
(M_{2},d_{2},n_{0})
be pointed metric spaces, and fix
p\in[1,\infty]
.
The
\ell^{p}
metric wedge
M_{1}\vee_{p}M_{2}
is the quotient set of
M_{1}\sqcup M_{2}
obtained by identifying

m_{0}\sim n_{0}
, equipped with the function
d_{\vee,p}
defined by

d_{\vee,p}(x,y):=\begin{cases}d_{1}(x,y),&x,y\in M_{1},\\[2.84526pt]
d_{2}(x,y),&x,y\in M_{2},\\[2.84526pt]
\left\lVert\bigl(d_{1}(x,m_{0}),\,d_{2}(y,n_{0})\bigr)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}},&x\in M_{1},\ y\in M_{2},\\[2.84526pt]
\left\lVert\bigl(d_{1}(y,m_{0}),\,d_{2}(x,n_{0})\bigr)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}},&x\in M_{2},\ y\in M_{1}.\end{cases}

The
\ell^{p}
wedge construction defines a metric on the glued space.

Lemma 5.4
.

For every
p\in[1,\infty]
, the function
d_{\vee,p}
is a metric on
M_{1}\vee_{p}M_{2}
.

Proof.

Symmetry and nonnegativity are clear. Separation follows because:
(i) if
x,y
lie in the same component, separation holds since
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are metrics;
(ii) if
x\in M_{1}
and
y\in M_{2}
, then

d_{\vee,p}(x,y)=\left\lVert(d_{1}(x,m_{0}),d_{2}(y,n_{0}))\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}=0

forces
d_{1}(x,m_{0})=0
and
d_{2}(y,n_{0})=0
, hence
x=m_{0}
and
y=n_{0}
, i.e. they represent the same glued point.

For the triangle inequality, fix
x,y,z\in M_{1}\vee_{p}M_{2}
and consider cases.

Case 1:
x,y,z
all lie in
M_{1}
(or all in
M_{2}
). Then the triangle inequality holds by that of
d_{1}
(or
d_{2}
).

Case 2:
x,y\in M_{1}
and
z\in M_{2}
.
Then
d_{\vee,p}(x,z)=\left\lVert(d_{1}(x,m_{0}),\,d_{2}(z,n_{0}))\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}.

By the triangle inequality in
M_{1}
, we have

(d_{1}(x,m_{0}),\,d_{2}(z,n_{0}))\leq(d_{1}(x,y)+d_{1}(y,m_{0}),\,d_{2}(z,n_{0}))=(d_{1}(x,y),0)+(d_{1}(y,m_{0}),d_{2}(z,n_{0}))

componentwise, and therefore (using Minkowski on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
)

d_{\vee,p}(x,z)\leq\left\lVert(d_{1}(x,y),0)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}+\left\lVert(d_{1}(y,m_{0}),d_{2}(z,n_{0}))\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}=d_{1}(x,y)+d_{\vee,p}(y,z)=d_{\vee,p}(x,y)+d_{\vee,p}(y,z).

Case 3:
x\in M_{1}
and
y,z\in M_{2}
. This is symmetric to Case 2.

Case 4:
x\in M_{1}
,
y\in M_{2}
,
z\in M_{1}
.
Then

d_{\vee,p}(x,z)=d_{1}(x,z)\leq d_{1}(x,m_{0})+d_{1}(m_{0},z)=\left\lVert(d_{1}(x,m_{0}),0)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}+\left\lVert(d_{1}(z,m_{0}),0)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}.

Also, by definition,
d_{\vee,p}(x,y)=\left\lVert(d_{1}(x,m_{0}),d_{2}(y,n_{0}))\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\geq\left\lVert(d_{1}(x,m_{0}),0)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}=d_{1}(x,m_{0}),

and similarly
d_{\vee,p}(y,z)\geq d_{1}(z,m_{0})
. Therefore

d_{\vee,p}(x,z)\leq d_{1}(x,m_{0})+d_{1}(z,m_{0})\leq d_{\vee,p}(x,y)+d_{\vee,p}(y,z).

All remaining configurations reduce to these by symmetry. Thus
d_{\vee,p}
is a metric.
∎

The following theorem shows that BK metric spaces admit a canonical
\ell^{p}
wedge decomposition.

Theorem 5.5
.

Fix
\theta\in\mathcal{C}
,
\lambda>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
, and
p\in[1,\infty]
. Then the BK metric space
(\mathcal{X},\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha})
is isometric to the
\ell^{p}
wedge
\mathcal{C}\vee_{p}\mathcal{Y}
of the pointed metric spaces
(\mathcal{C},\beta,\theta)

and
(\mathcal{Y},d_{\mathrm{reg}},\ast)
,
via the identification
J:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{C}\vee_{p}\mathcal{Y}
that is the identity on
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and on
\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
. Equivalently, for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{X}
,

\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi)=\begin{cases}\beta(\phi,\psi),&\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C},\\[2.84526pt]
d_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)=\lambda\bigl(\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)\bigr)^{\alpha},&\phi,\psi\notin\mathcal{C},\\[2.84526pt]
\left\lVert\bigl(\beta(\phi,\theta),\ d_{\mathrm{reg}}(\psi,\ast)\bigr)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}=\left\lVert\bigl(\beta(\phi,\theta),\ \lambda\rho(\psi)^{\alpha}\bigr)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}},&\phi\in\mathcal{C},\ \psi\notin\mathcal{C},\\[2.84526pt]
\left\lVert\bigl(\beta(\psi,\theta),\ d_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\ast)\bigr)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}=\left\lVert\bigl(\beta(\psi,\theta),\ \lambda\rho(\phi)^{\alpha}\bigr)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}},&\psi\in\mathcal{C},\ \phi\notin\mathcal{C}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We verify that the distance formulas on
\mathcal{X}
match those on the wedge.

If
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}
, then by construction
\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)=0
, and by Lemma
3.9

\left\lVert\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)-\kappa_{\theta}(\psi)\right\rVert_{\infty}=\beta(\phi,\psi).

Hence

\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi)=\left\lVert(\beta(\phi,\psi),0)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}=\beta(\phi,\psi),

which agrees with the wedge metric on the
\mathcal{C}
component.

If
\phi,\psi\notin\mathcal{C}
, then
\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)=\kappa_{\theta}(\psi)=0
and

\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)=\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)
, so

\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi)=\left\lVert(0,\lambda\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)^{\alpha})\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}=\lambda\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)^{\alpha}=d_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi),

which matches the wedge metric on the
\mathcal{Y}
component.

In the cross case, assume
\phi\in\mathcal{C}
and
\psi\notin\mathcal{C}
.
Then
\kappa_{\theta}(\psi)=0
, so
\left\lVert\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)-\kappa_{\theta}(\psi)\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert\kappa_{\theta}(\phi)\right\rVert_{\infty}=\beta(\phi,\theta),

and
\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\phi,\psi)=\rho(\psi)
by definition of the collapse.
Thus

\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi)=\left\lVert\bigl(\beta(\phi,\theta),\ \lambda\rho(\psi)^{\alpha}\bigr)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}.

On the wedge side,
J(\phi)\in\mathcal{C}
and
J(\psi)\in\mathcal{Y}
, so by Definition
5.3
,

d_{\vee,p}\bigl(J(\phi),J(\psi)\bigr)=\left\lVert\bigl(\beta(\phi,\theta),\ d_{\mathrm{reg}}(\psi,\ast)\bigr)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}=\left\lVert\bigl(\beta(\phi,\theta),\ \lambda\rho(\psi)^{\alpha}\bigr)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}},

which is exactly the same value. The remaining cross case is symmetric.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Fix
\theta\in\mathcal{C}
.
Then:

(i)

For every
\phi\in\mathcal{C}
,
\operatorname{dist}_{\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}}(\phi,\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C})=\beta(\phi,\theta)
.

(ii)

\overline{\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C}}^{\,\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}}\cap\mathcal{C}=\{\theta\}
.

(iii)

\mathcal{C}
is
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}
-closed in
\mathcal{X}
, and
\overline{\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C}}^{\,\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}}=(\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C})\ \cup\ \{\theta\}
.

Proof.

(i) If
\psi\notin\mathcal{C}
, then by Theorem
5.5
,
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi)=\left\lVert(\beta(\phi,\theta),\lambda\rho(\psi)^{\alpha})\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\geq\beta(\phi,\theta).

Thus
\operatorname{dist}(\phi,\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C})\geq\beta(\phi,\theta)
.

Conversely, pick any nonzero state
\omega
on
A
and define
\psi_{n}(a):=\frac{i}{n}\,\omega(a)\,I_{H}
, as in the proof of Proposition
4.3
.
Then
\psi_{n}\notin\mathcal{C}
and

\rho(\psi_{n})\leq\|\psi_{n}\|_{cb}^{1/2}\to 0
(Lemma
3.7
).
Hence

\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi,\psi_{n})=\left\lVert(\beta(\phi,\theta),\lambda\rho(\psi_{n})^{\alpha})\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\longrightarrow\beta(\phi,\theta),

so
\operatorname{dist}(\phi,\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C})\leq\beta(\phi,\theta)
and equality holds.

(ii) A point
\phi\in\mathcal{C}
lies in
\overline{\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C}}^{\,\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}}
iff

\operatorname{dist}_{\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}}(\phi,\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C})=0
.
By (i) this is equivalent to
\beta(\phi,\theta)=0
, i.e.
\phi=\theta
.

(iii) Let
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
and suppose that
\phi_{n}\to\psi
in
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}.

We claim that
\psi\in\mathcal{C}
. Indeed, if
\psi\notin\mathcal{C}
, then by Theorem
5.5
, for every
n
,

\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi_{n},\psi)=\Bigl\|\bigl(\beta(\phi_{n},\theta),\,\lambda\,\rho(\psi)^{\alpha}\bigr)\Bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}.

Since
\psi\notin\mathcal{C}
, we have
\psi\neq 0
, because
0\in\mathcal{C}
, and hence
\rho(\psi)=\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\psi,0)>0.

Therefore
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\phi_{n},\psi)\geq\lambda\,\rho(\psi)^{\alpha}>0
for all
n
,
which contradicts
\phi_{n}\to\psi
in
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}
.
Thus necessarily
\psi\in\mathcal{C}
, proving that
\mathcal{C}
is closed.

Next we prove
\overline{\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C}}^{\,\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}}=(\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C})\cup\{\theta\}.

The inclusion “
\subseteq
” follows immediately from part (ii).
For the reverse inclusion, it is enough to show that
\theta
belongs to the closure of

\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C}
. For this, let
\psi_{n}(a):=\frac{i}{n}\,\omega(a)\,I_{H}
be as in the proof of part (i). Apply the cross-case formula from Theorem
5.5
with
\phi=\theta\in\mathcal{C}
:

\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\theta,\psi_{n})=\Bigl\|\bigl(\beta(\theta,\theta),\,\lambda\,\rho(\psi_{n})^{\alpha}\bigr)\Bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}=\lambda\,\rho(\psi_{n})^{\alpha}\leq\lambda\,n^{-\alpha/2}.

Hence
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}(\theta,\psi_{n})\to 0,
i.e.,
so
\psi_{n}\to\theta
in
\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}
.
Therefore
\theta\in\overline{\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C}}^{\,\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}}.

∎

6.
Vietoris–Rips and Čech complexes for BK metric spaces

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and
H
a Hilbert space. Fix
\lambda>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
, and
p\in[1,\infty]
, and set
d_{\theta}:=\beta^{BK}_{\theta,\lambda,p,\alpha}
, for simplicity. By the
\ell^{p}
wedge decomposition (Theorem
5.5
), we identify

(\mathcal{X},d_{\theta})\ \cong\ (\mathcal{C},\beta,\theta)\ \vee_{p}\ (\mathcal{Y},d_{\mathrm{reg}},\ast),

so that: (i) on
\mathcal{C}
we have
d_{\theta}=\beta
; (ii)
on
\mathcal{Y}^{\circ}:=\mathcal{Y}\setminus\{\ast\}\cong\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C}
we have

d_{\theta}=d_{\mathrm{reg}}=\lambda\,\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}^{\alpha}
; and
(iii) for
x\in\mathcal{C}
and
y\in\mathcal{Y}
,

(4)

d_{\theta}(x,y)=\bigl\|\bigl(\beta(x,\theta),\ d_{\mathrm{reg}}(y,\ast)\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}.

It is convenient to introduce the radial functions
r_{\mathcal{C}}(x):=\beta(x,\theta)
for all
x\in\mathcal{C}
, and
r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y):=d_{\mathrm{reg}}(y,\ast)
for all
y\in\mathcal{Y}.

With this notation, (
4
) becomes

d_{\theta}(x,y)=\bigl\|\bigl(r_{\mathcal{C}}(x),r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}},
for all
x\in\mathcal{C},\ y\in\mathcal{Y}.

6.1.
Vietoris–Rips complexes

We now study the Vietoris–Rips complexes associated to the BK metric.

Definition 6.1

(cf.
[
9
]
[
8
]
)
.

Let
(Z,d)
be a metric space and
t\geq 0
.
The Vietoris–Rips complex
\mathrm{VR}_{t}(Z,d)
is the abstract simplicial complex with vertex set
Z

such that a finite subset
\sigma\subseteq Z
spans a simplex iff
d(z,z^{\prime})\leq t
for all
z,z^{\prime}\in\sigma.

Equivalently,
\sigma
is a simplex iff
\mathrm{diam}(\sigma)\leq t
.

We begin by characterizing cross edges in terms of the radial coordinates.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{C}
and
y\in\mathcal{Y}^{\circ}
. Then
\{x,y\}
is an edge of
\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{X},d_{\theta})
iff
\bigl\|\bigl(r_{\mathcal{C}}(x),r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq\ t.

Proof.

This follows directly from the cross-distance formula (
4
).
∎

We next characterize mixed simplices in terms of a maximal radial constraint.

Proposition 6.3
.

Fix
t\geq 0
and let
\sigma\subseteq\mathcal{C}
and
\tau\subseteq\mathcal{Y}^{\circ}
be finite, nonempty subsets.
Set

A:=\max_{x\in\sigma}r_{\mathcal{C}}(x),\qquad B:=\max_{y\in\tau}r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y).

Then
\sigma\cup\tau
is a simplex of
\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{X},d_{\theta})
if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\sigma
is a simplex of
\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{C},\beta)
;

(2)

\tau
is a simplex of
\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{Y}^{\circ},d_{\mathrm{reg}})
;

(3)

\bigl\|\bigl(A,B\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq t
.

Proof.

If
\sigma\cup\tau
is a simplex in
\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{X},d_{\theta})
, then in particular all pairwise distances
between vertices in
\sigma
are
\leq t
, which is exactly (1) since
d_{\theta}|_{\mathcal{C}}=\beta
.
Similarly all pairwise distances between vertices in
\tau
are
\leq t
, giving (2) since
d_{\theta}|_{\mathcal{Y}}=d_{\mathrm{reg}}
.

For the cross pairs,
\sigma\cup\tau
being a simplex means that for all
x\in\sigma
and
y\in\tau
,
d_{\theta}(x,y)=\bigl\|\bigl(r_{\mathcal{C}}(x),r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq t.

Since
\bigl\|\bigl(\cdot,\cdot\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}
is increasing in each variable, this is equivalent to the single inequality

\Bigl\|\Bigl(\max_{x\in\sigma}r_{\mathcal{C}}(x),\ \max_{y\in\tau}r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)\Bigr)\Bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq t,

i.e.
\bigl\|\bigl(A,B\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq t
. (Necessity uses the particular pair
(x_{0},y_{0})
where the maxima are attained.)

Conversely, if (1) and (2) hold and
\bigl\|\bigl(A,B\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq t
, then for each cross pair
(x,y)
we have

r_{\mathcal{C}}(x)\leq A
and
r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)\leq B
, hence

d_{\theta}(x,y)=\bigl\|\bigl(r_{\mathcal{C}}(x),r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq\bigl\|\bigl(A,B\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq t.

Thus all pairwise distances in
\sigma\cup\tau
are
\leq t
, so it is a simplex.
∎

To describe the mixed part more structurally, we introduce radial sublevel sets and a join-type construction.

Definition 6.4
.

(i) For
u\geq 0
define the radial sublevel sets

\mathcal{C}^{\leq u}:=\{x\in\mathcal{C}:\ r_{\mathcal{C}}(x)\leq u\},\qquad\mathcal{Y}^{\leq u}:=\{y\in\mathcal{Y}^{\circ}:\ r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)\leq u\}.

(ii) Given a simplicial complex
K
on vertex set
V(K)
and a subset
W\subseteq V(K)
, we write

K|_{W}
for the induced subcomplex on
W
.
In particular, we set

\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{C}^{\leq u}):=\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{C},\beta)\big|_{\mathcal{C}^{\leq u}},\qquad\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{Y}^{\leq v}):=\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{Y}^{\circ},d_{\mathrm{reg}})\big|_{\mathcal{Y}^{\leq v}}.

(iii) Let
K,L
be simplicial complexes on disjoint vertex sets.
Define their mixed join to be the collection

K\ \star\ L:=\bigl\{\sigma\cup\tau:\ \sigma\in K,\ \tau\in L,\ \sigma\neq\varnothing,\ \tau\neq\varnothing\bigr\}.

(Equivalently,
K\star L
is the subcollection of simplices of the usual join
K*L
that meet both factors.)

The following theorem identifies the mixed simplices as a union of constrained joins.

Theorem 6.5
.

For each
t\geq 0
, the set of simplices of
\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{X},d_{\theta})
that meet both

\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{Y}^{\circ}
is exactly

\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\geq 0\\
\left\lVert(u,v)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\leq t\end{subarray}}\Bigl(\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{C}^{\leq u})\ \star\ \mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{Y}^{\leq v})\Bigr).

In particular, when
p=\infty
one has the global decomposition

(5)

\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{X},d_{\theta})=\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{C},\beta)\ \cup\ \mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{Y}^{\circ},d_{\mathrm{reg}})\ \cup\ \Bigl(\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{C}^{\leq t})\star\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{Y}^{\leq t})\Bigr).

Proof.

Fix
t\geq 0
, and write
\mathrm{VR}_{t}:=\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{X},d_{\theta})
, for simplicity.

(
\subseteq
) Let
\eta\in\mathrm{VR}_{t}
be a simplex that meets both
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{Y}^{\circ}
.
Set
\sigma:=\eta\cap\mathcal{C}
and
\tau:=\eta\cap\mathcal{Y}^{\circ}
, so
\sigma,\tau
are finite and nonempty and

\eta=\sigma\cup\tau
(disjoint union of vertex sets).
Define

u:=\max_{x\in\sigma}r_{\mathcal{C}}(x),\qquad v:=\max_{y\in\tau}r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y).

Since
\eta
is a simplex, all pairwise distances in
\eta
are
\leq t
.
In particular,
d_{\theta}|_{\mathcal{C}}=\beta
implies
\sigma\in\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{C},\beta)
,
and
d_{\theta}|_{\mathcal{Y}^{\circ}}=d_{\mathrm{reg}}
implies
\tau\in\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{Y}^{\circ},d_{\mathrm{reg}})
.
Moreover, for any cross pair
(x,y)\in\sigma\times\tau
,

d_{\theta}(x,y)=\left\lVert\bigl(r_{\mathcal{C}}(x),r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)\bigr)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\leq t.

By monotonicity of
\ell^{p}
-norm in each coordinate, this yields
\left\lVert(u,v)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\leq t
.
Also, by definition of
u
and
v
, we have
\sigma\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\leq u}
and
\tau\subseteq\mathcal{Y}^{\leq v}
,
hence
\sigma\in\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{C}^{\leq u})
and
\tau\in\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{Y}^{\leq v})
.
Therefore
\eta=\sigma\cup\tau\in\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{C}^{\leq u})\star\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{Y}^{\leq v})

for some
(u,v)
with
\left\lVert(u,v)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\leq t
, proving the containment.

(
\supseteq
) Fix
u,v\geq 0
with
\left\lVert(u,v)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\leq t
and let

\eta=\sigma\cup\tau\in\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{C}^{\leq u})\star\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{Y}^{\leq v})
.
Then
\sigma,\tau
are nonempty,
\sigma\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\leq u}
,
\tau\subseteq\mathcal{Y}^{\leq v}
,

\sigma\in\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{C},\beta)
, and
\tau\in\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{Y}^{\circ},d_{\mathrm{reg}})
.
Hence all within-
\mathcal{C}
and within-
\mathcal{Y}^{\circ}
distances inside
\eta
are
\leq t
.

For a cross pair
x\in\sigma
,
y\in\tau
, we have
r_{\mathcal{C}}(x)\leq u
and
r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)\leq v
, so

d_{\theta}(x,y)=\left\lVert\bigl(r_{\mathcal{C}}(x),r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)\bigr)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\leq\left\lVert(u,v)\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\leq t.

Thus all pairwise distances in
\eta
are
\leq t
, i.e.
\eta\in\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{X},d_{\theta})
,
and by construction
\eta
meets both sides. This proves equality of mixed parts.

Finally, assume
p=\infty
. Then
\left\lVert(u,v)\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq t
iff
u\leq t
and
v\leq t
.
Hence the mixed part of
\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{X},d_{\theta})
is exactly

\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{C}^{\leq t})\star\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{Y}^{\leq t})
.
Since the full complex
\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{X},d_{\theta})
consists of:
(i) simplices entirely in
\mathcal{C}
, i.e.
\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{C},\beta)
;
(ii) simplices entirely in
\mathcal{Y}^{\circ}
, i.e.
\mathrm{VR}_{t}(\mathcal{Y}^{\circ},d_{\mathrm{reg}})
;
and (iii) mixed simplices,
we obtain (
5
).
∎

Remark 6.6
.

The cross edges (and hence all mixed simplices) are governed purely by the two scalar functions

r_{\mathcal{C}}
and
r_{\mathcal{Y}}
.

6.2.
Čech complexes

Let
(Z,d)
be a metric space and let
S\subseteq Z
.
In the literature, the Čech complex is most commonly understood as the nerve of a cover by metric balls in an ambient space, typically with
Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where intersections are taken in the ambient space; see
[
7
,
12
,
6
]
.
In contrast, for a general metric space
(Z,d)
one may also consider an intrinsic Čech complex, where balls are taken in
Z
itself and intersections are required to lie in
Z
.
To our knowledge, this distinction is not systematically emphasized in the literature, but it plays a crucial role in wedge-type metric spaces such as the BK construction.

In the BK wedge setting, this distinction is essential: ambient Čech complexes can detect the glued basepoint and become contractible at relatively small scales, while intrinsic Čech complexes retain the combinatorial geometry of the cloud.
We now make these two constructions precise.

Definition 6.21
.

Let
(Z,d)
be a metric space and
t\geq 0
.

(i) The intrinsic Čech complex
\check{C}_{t}(Z,d)
is the nerve of the cover of
Z
by
(closed) metric balls
\{\overline{B}_{Z}(z,t)\}_{z\in Z}
.
Equivalently, a finite subset
\sigma\subseteq Z
is a simplex if and only if
\bigcap_{z\in\sigma}\overline{B}_{Z}(z,t)\neq\varnothing.

(ii) The ambient Čech complex of a cloud
S
in
Z
is the abstract simplicial complex
\check{C}^{\mathrm{amb}}_{t}(S\subseteq Z,d)

with vertex set
S
such that a finite subset
\sigma\subseteq S
is a simplex if and only if
\bigcap_{s\in\sigma}\overline{B}_{Z}(s,t)\neq\varnothing.

When the ambient space is clear, we simply write
\check{C}^{\mathrm{amb}}_{t}(S,d)
.

Conventions 6.22
.

(i) (convention for finite clouds.)
We reserve
\check{C}_{t}(Z,d)
for the intrinsic Čech complex of the metric space
Z
itself.
For a finite cloud
S\subseteq Z
, the ambient complex

\check{C}^{\mathrm{amb}}_{t}(S\subseteq Z,d)
allows witnesses anywhere in
Z
,
whereas the intrinsic complex
\check{C}_{t}(S,d|_{S})
allows only witnesses in
S
.
Hence

\check{C}_{t}(S,d|_{S})\ \subseteq\ \check{C}^{\mathrm{amb}}_{t}(S\subseteq Z,d).

All basepoint-driven contractibility statements for finite BK clouds below are ambient statements.

(ii) (radius-
t
convention.) Some authors use the “

” convention (balls of radius
t/2
) so that
\check{C}_{t}

approximates the union of
t/2
-balls.
All statements below use the radius-

convention.

For every metric space
(Z,d)
and every cloud

, one has

\mathrm{VR}_{t}(S,d|_{S})\ \subseteq\ \check{C}^{\mathrm{amb}}_{t}(S\subseteq Z,d)\ \subseteq\ \mathrm{VR}_{2t}(S,d|_{S}).

Indeed, the usual proof of
\mathrm{VR}_{t}\subseteq\check{C}_{t}\subseteq\mathrm{VR}_{2t}
works verbatim in the

\mathrm{VR}_{t}(Z,d)\ \subseteq\ \check{C}_{t}(Z,d)\ \subseteq\ \mathrm{VR}_{2t}(Z,d).

Metric balls in the wedge admit an explicit componentwise description.

Lemma 6.23

d_{\theta}(z,y_{j})=\bigl\|\bigl(r_{\mathcal{C}}(z),r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y_{j})\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}
and monotonicity in the second coordinate, the inequalities
d_{\theta}(z,y_{j})\leq t
for all
j

are equivalent to
\bigl\|\bigl(r_{\mathcal{C}}(z),B\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq t.
This is exactly condition (C).

Conversely, if (C) holds, then for each
j
we have
r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y_{j})\leq B
, hence

d_{\theta}(z,y_{j})=\bigl\|\bigl(r_{\mathcal{C}}(z),r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y_{j})\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq\bigl\|\bigl(r_{\mathcal{C}}(z),B\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq t,

and also
d_{\theta}(z,x_{i})=\beta(z,x_{i})\leq t
for all
i
.
Thus
z
lies in all ambient balls.
The proof for (Y) is symmetric, using Lemma
6.23
and monotonicity
in the first coordinate.
∎

The preceding criterion can be reformulated more explicitly using elementary properties of
\ell^{p}
norms.

Observation 6.25
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
, and fix
t\geq 0
.
Then for
a,b\geq 0
,
\bigl\|\bigl(a,b\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq t
if and only if

a\leq t\ \text{ and }\ b\leq(t^{p}-a^{p})^{1/p}

and equivalently
\bigl\|\bigl(a,b\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq t

if and only if

b\leq t\ \text{ and }\ a\leq(t^{p}-b^{p})^{1/p}.

For
p=\infty
one has

\bigl\|\bigl(a,b\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq t
if and only if

a\leq t\ \text{ and }\ b\leq t.

Using these equivalences, condition (C) above can be rewritten as follows:

(i) If
1\leq p<\infty
, then (C) is equivalent to

B\leq t\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\overline{B}_{\mathcal{C}}(x_{i},t)\right)\cap\overline{B}_{\mathcal{C}}\!\Bigl(\theta,(t^{p}-B^{p})^{1/p}\Bigr)\neq\varnothing.

(ii) If
p=\infty
, then (C) is equivalent to

B\leq t\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\overline{B}_{\mathcal{C}}(x_{i},t)\right)\cap\overline{B}_{\mathcal{C}}(\theta,t)\neq\varnothing.

Similarly, condition (Y) is equivalent to the following:

(iii) If
1\leq p<\infty
, then (Y) is equivalent to

A\leq t\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{n}\overline{B}_{\mathcal{Y}}(y_{j},t)\right)\cap\overline{B}_{\mathcal{Y}}\!\Bigl(\ast,(t^{p}-A^{p})^{1/p}\Bigr)\neq\varnothing.

(iv) If
p=\infty
, then (Y) is equivalent to

A\leq t\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{n}\overline{B}_{\mathcal{Y}}(y_{j},t)\right)\cap\overline{B}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\ast,t)\neq\varnothing.

We now deduce a simple sufficient condition: vertices with sufficiently small radii automatically form mixed Čech simplices via the basepoint.

Corollary 6.26
.

Fix
t\geq 0
and let
\sigma\subseteq\mathcal{C}
and
\tau\subseteq\mathcal{Y}^{\circ}

\max_{x\in\sigma}r_{\mathcal{C}}(x)\leq t

\max_{y\in\tau}r_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)\leq t,

\sigma\cup\tau

\check{C}_{t}(\mathcal{X},d_{\theta})

\sigma\cup\tau\subseteq S

\sigma\cup\tau

\check{C}^{\mathrm{amb}}_{t}(S\subseteq\mathcal{X},d_{\theta})

Proof.

Under the stated assumptions, the glued basepoint

t

Hence the common intersection of the ambient balls is nonempty.
The final statement is immediate, since the same witness point
\theta\sim\ast\in\mathcal{X}

∎

Mixed BK clouds yield contractible ambient Čech complexes at moderate scales.

Example 6.32

(Ambient Čech contractibility on the mixed cloud)
.

Let
S:=\{x_{0},x_{4},y_{+},y_{-}\}\subset\mathrm{CB}(M_{n},M_{m})
be as in Example
6.18
.
If
t\geq\max\{1,r_{-},r_{+}\},

then the ambient Čech complex

\check{C}^{\mathrm{amb}}_{t}(S\subseteq\mathcal{X},d_{\theta})

is the full simplex on the four vertices
S
, hence contractible.

Indeed, since
\theta=x_{1}
is the anchor, one has

d_{\theta}(x_{0},\theta)=\beta(x_{0},\theta)=1,\qquad d_{\theta}(x_{4},\theta)=\beta(x_{4},\theta)=1.

Also,

d_{\theta}(y_{-},\theta)=r_{-}.

Therefore
\theta\in\overline{B}_{\mathcal{X}}(s,t)

.
So the four ambient balls have nonempty common intersection, and the ambient Čech complex is the full simplex.

Remark 6.33
.
