Higher-Order Normality and No-Gap Conditions in Impulsive Control with L 1 L^{1} -Control Topology Note: This research is partially supported by INdAM-GNAMPA Project 2024, CUP E53C2300167000, and by PRIN 2022, Prot. 2022238YY5, CUP C53D23002370006. Journal: Systems and Control Letters
Monica Motta, Michele Palladino, Franco Rampazzo

Abstract
In optimal control, extending the class of admissible controls is a common strategy to guarantee the existence of optimal solutions. However, such extensions may introduce a gap between the infimum of the original problem and the minimum of the extended one, especially in the presence of endpoint constraints. Since Warga’s seminal work, normality of first-order necessary conditions for extended minimizers has been recognized as a sufficient condition to avoid this phenomenon, though it is far from being necessary. In this paper, we consider impulsive extensions of control-affine systems with unbounded controls. We establish that a notion of higher-order normality , based on iterated Lie brackets of the system’s vector fields, suffices to prevent an infimum gap. The key novelty of this manuscript consists in showing that this holds under a local topology defined by the L 1 L^{1} -distance between controls, rather than the more common L ∞ L^{\infty} -distance between trajectories. Among the reasons that motivate the interest in this issue, let us mention that a counterexample by R. B. Vinter shows that for a different extension –based on convexification of the velocity set– a local extended minimizer that is normal with respect to the L 1 L^{1} -norm of the controls may still exhibit a gap. Our method relies on set-separation techniques. Such an approach makes it possible to derive higher-order conditions and to exploit the corresponding notion of higher-order normality.

Introduction

When an optimal control problem fails to have a local minimizer, a common strategy consists of:

i)

embedding the optimal control problem of interest –which, from now on we will refer to as the
original optimal control problem
– in a larger one –which we will refer to as the
extended optimal control
– that possesses a local minimizer,

ii)

approximating the minimizer of the extended optimal control problem with solutions of the original optimal control problem.

Such a procedure can be successfully carried out as long as an “infimum gap" does not occur, that is as long as the infimum value of the original optimal control problem coincides with the minimum value of the extended optimal control problem. Therefore, it is of interest to provide conditions guaranteeing that the optimal control problem under consideration does not have an infimum gap.

Some early results by J.Warga have inspired the idea that, under certain hypothesis, a general criterion for the absence of an infimum gap can be stated as follows:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\textit{If a set of necessary conditions holds true in normal form},\\
\textit{then the infimum gap does not occur."}\end{array}

(1)

The expression “in normal form" here means that the corresponding cost multiplier is different from zero for each set of multipliers. As a simple consequence of statement (
1
), any optimal control problem without end-point constraints and state constraints does not have an infimum gap.

In fact, it turns out that the validity of statement (
1
) heavily depends on several aspects, such as:

i)

the type of dynamics appearing in the optimal control problem (e.g., multifunctions, nonlinear control differential equations, control-affine nonlinear differential equations, etc.);

ii)

the type of extension one is carrying out on the original optimal control problem (relaxation, impulsive extension, etc.);

iii)

the topology that one considers in the notion of local minimizer (
L^{\infty}
,
W^{1,1}
, etc.);

iv)

the type of necessary conditions taken into account.

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\tilde{\rm d}(\bar{z}_{j},\bar{z})=|\bar{S}_{j}-\bar{S}|+\int_{0}^{\bar{S}}\left[|\bar{w}^{0}_{j}(s)-\bar{w}^{0}(s)|+|\bar{w}_{j}(s)-\bar{w}(s)|\right]\,ds\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{\bar{S}}^{\bar{S}_{j}}\left[\bar{w}_{j}^{0}(s)+|\bar{w}_{j}(s)|\right]\,ds\\
\displaystyle\quad\quad=|S_{j}-\tilde{S}|+\int_{0}^{\bar{S}}\left[|w^{0}_{j}(\sigma_{j}(s))-\tilde{w}^{0}(\sigma(s))|+|w_{j}(\sigma_{j}(s))-\tilde{w}(\sigma_{j}(s))|\right]\frac{d\sigma_{j}}{ds}(s)\,ds\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{\bar{S}}^{\bar{S}_{j}}\left[w_{j}^{0}(\tilde{S}+(s-\bar{S}))+|w_{j}(\tilde{S}+(s-\bar{S}))|\right]\,ds\leq\tilde{\rm d}(z_{j},\tilde{z})<\frac{1}{j}.\end{array}

Again, this contradicts the existence at
\bar{z}
of a local infimum gap w.r.t.
(X^{\mathcal{S}}_{\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{+}},\tilde{\rm d})
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

At a feasible canonical extended process
(\bar{S},\bar{w}^{0},\bar{w},\bar{y}^{0},\bar{y},\bar{\beta})\in X^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{W}}
there is a local infimum gap w.r.t.
(X^{\mathcal{S}}_{\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{+}},\tilde{\rm d})
,
if and only if there is a local infimum gap w.r.t.

(X^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{W}_{+}},{\rm d})
, i.e. among canonical processes only.

Proof.

Ler
\bar{z}:=(\bar{S},\bar{w}^{0},\bar{w},\bar{y}^{0},\bar{y},\bar{\beta})
be a feasible canonical extended process.
If at
\bar{z}
there is a local infimum gap w.r.t. feasible processes in
(X^{\mathcal{S}}_{\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{+}},\tilde{\rm d})
, by definition there are some
r>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that for any feasible embedded strict-sense process
z:=(S,w^{0},w,y^{0},y,\beta)\in X^{\mathcal{S}}_{\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{+}}
such that
\tilde{\rm d}(z,\bar{z})<r
, we have

\Psi(\bar{y}^{0}(\bar{S}),\bar{y}(\bar{S}))\leq\Psi(y^{0}(S),y(S))-\varepsilon.

(18)

Now, for any canonical feasible embedded strict-sense process
z
with
{\rm d}(z,\bar{z})<\frac{r}{2}
, we have
\tilde{\rm d}(z,\bar{z})\leq 2{\rm d}(z,\bar{z})<r
. Therefore (
18
) holds true, and at
\bar{z}
there is also a local infimum gap w.r.t. to
feasible canonical processes in
(X^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{W}_{+}},{\rm d})
.

Conversely, assume now that at
\bar{z}
there is a local infimum gap w.r.t. to feasible canonical processes in
(X^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{W}_{+}},{\rm d})
only, but not w.r.t. the larger set
(X^{\mathcal{S}}_{\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{+}},\tilde{\rm d})
. Since
\bar{z}
is a canonical process, then there exists a sequence of feasible (possibly non-canonical) embedded strict-sense processes
z_{j}:=(S_{j},w^{0}_{j},w_{j},y^{0}_{j},y_{j},\beta_{j})\in X^{\mathcal{S}}_{\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{+}}
, such that

\tilde{\rm d}(z_{j},\bar{z})<\frac{1}{j}\quad\text{for any $j\in\mathbb{N}$, \ $j\geq 1$.}

For any
j\geq 1
, let us associate with
z_{j}
its canonical representative
z^{c}_{j}:=(S^{c}_{j},w^{0^{c}}_{j},w_{j},^{c},y^{0^{c}}_{j},y_{j}^{c},\beta_{j}^{c})
. Namely, let us take
\sigma_{j}:[0,S_{j}]\to[0,S_{j}^{c}]
defined as

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\sigma_{j}(s):=y^{0}_{j}(s)+\beta_{j}(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\left[w^{0}_{j}(s^{\prime})+|w_{j}(s^{\prime})|\right]\,ds^{\prime}\ \ \text{for all $s\in[0,S_{j}]$,}\\
\displaystyle S_{j}^{c}:=\sigma_{j}(S_{j})=y^{0}_{j}(S_{j})+\beta_{j}(S_{j}),\end{array}

and consider the canonical process
z^{c}_{j}\in X^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{W}_{+}}
satisfying

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle(w^{0}_{j},w_{j})(s)=(w^{0^{c}}_{j},w_{j}^{c})(\sigma_{j}(s))\cdot\frac{d\sigma_{j}}{ds}(s)\ \ \text{for a.e. $s\in[0,S_{j}]$},\\[4.30554pt]
(y^{0}_{j},y_{j},\beta_{j})(s)=(y^{0^{c}}_{j},y_{j}^{c},\beta_{j}^{c})(\sigma_{j}(s))\ \ \text{for all $s\in[0,S_{j}]$}.\end{array}

If we extend
\sigma_{j}
to
\mathbb{R}_{+}
by setting

\sigma_{j}(s):=\begin{cases}\sigma_{j}(s)\ \ &\text{if $s\in[0,S_{j}]$},\\
S_{j}^{c}+(s-S_{j})\ \ &\text{if $s\geq S_{j}$},\end{cases}

we have that the sequence
(\sigma_{j})_{j}
is an
{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}
-approximation of the identity function
id
. Indeed, recalling that
\bar{w}^{0}+|\bar{w}|=1
a.e. (because
\bar{z}
is canonical), we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left|\frac{d\sigma_{j}}{ds}(s)-1\right|\,ds=\int_{0}^{S_{j}\land\bar{S}}\left|w^{0}_{j}(s)+|w_{j}(s)|-\bar{w}^{0}(s)-|\bar{w}(s)|\right|\,ds+\\
\ \ \quad\qquad+\begin{cases}0\ \ &\text{if $S_{j}\leq\bar{S}$}\\
\int_{\bar{S}}^{S_{j}}\left|w^{0}_{j}(s)+|w_{j}(s)|\right|\,ds+|S_{j}-\bar{S}|\ \ &\text{if $S_{j}>\bar{S}$}\end{cases}\ \leq\tilde{\rm d}(z_{j},\bar{z})<\frac{1}{j}.\end{array}

It follows that
\|\sigma_{j}-id\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq\frac{1}{j}
. Let us now estimate
{\rm d}(z^{c}_{j},\bar{z})
. As in the proof of Lemma
4.2
, possibly passing to a subsequence, we may assume that either

(a)
S^{c}_{j}\leq\bar{S}
for every
j
or  (b)

S^{c}_{j}>\bar{S}
for every
j
.

Case
(a). We have

\displaystyle{\rm d}(z^{c}_{j},\bar{z})=|S^{c}_{j}-\bar{S}|+\int_{0}^{S^{c}_{j}}\left[|w^{0^{c}}_{j}(s)-\bar{w}^{0}(s)|+|w_{j}^{c}(s)-\bar{w}(s)|\right]\,ds,

(19)

where

\displaystyle|S^{c}_{j}-\bar{S}|\leq|\sigma_{j}(S_{j})-S_{j}|+|S_{j}-\bar{S}|<\frac{1}{j}+\frac{1}{j}=\frac{2}{j},

and, using the time-change
s=\sigma_{j}(s^{\prime})
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{S^{c}_{j}}\left[|w^{0^{c}}_{j}(s)-\bar{w}^{0}(s)|+|w_{j}^{c}(s)-\bar{w}(s)|\right]\,ds\\[4.30554pt]
\quad\quad\qquad\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{S_{j}}\left[|w^{0^{c}}_{j}(\sigma_{j}(s))-\bar{w}^{0}(\sigma_{j}(s))|+|w_{j}^{c}(\sigma_{j}(s))-\bar{w}(\sigma_{j}(s))|\right]\frac{d\sigma_{j}}{ds}(s)\,ds\\[4.30554pt]
\quad\quad\qquad\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{S_{j}}\left[\left|w^{0}_{j}(s)-\bar{w}^{0}(\sigma_{j}(s))\frac{d\sigma_{j}}{ds}(s)\right|+\left|w_{j}(s)-\bar{w}(\sigma_{j}(s))\frac{d\sigma_{j}}{ds}(s)\right|\right]\,ds.\end{array}

(20)

Now, recalling how
\sigma_{j}
approximates the identity and that
|\bar{w}^{0}|\leq 1
a.e., we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{S_{j}}\left|w^{0}_{j}(s)-\bar{w}^{0}(\sigma_{j}(s))\frac{d\sigma_{j}}{ds}(s)\right|\,ds\\[4.30554pt]
\quad\qquad\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{S_{j}}\left[|w^{0}_{j}(s)-\bar{w}^{0}(s)|+|\bar{w}^{0}(s)-\bar{w}^{0}(\sigma_{j}(s))|\right]\,ds\\[4.30554pt]
\quad\qquad\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{S_{j}}\bar{w}^{0}(\sigma_{j}(s))\left|1-\frac{d\sigma_{j}}{ds}(s)\right|\,ds\leq\frac{1}{j}+\int_{0}^{S_{j}}|\bar{w}^{0}(s)-\bar{w}^{0}(\sigma_{j}(s))|\,ds+\frac{1}{j}.\end{array}

By standard density results, for every
j\geq 1
there is a continuous function
\bar{w}^{0}_{j}\in C([0,\bar{S}+2])
, which we can assume verifying
|\bar{w}^{0}_{j}(s)|\leq 2
for all
s
, such that

\|\bar{w}^{0}-\bar{w}^{0}_{j}\|_{L^{1}(0,\bar{S}+2)}<\frac{1}{j}.

Since
\bar{w}^{0}_{j}
is uniformly continuous on the compact interval
[0,\bar{S}+2]
and
\sigma_{j}\to\mathrm{id}
uniformly in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
(so,
\sigma_{j}(\bar{S}+1)<\bar{S}+1+\frac{1}{j}\leq\bar{S}+2
), there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
j\geq N
,

\int_{0}^{\bar{S}+1}|\bar{w}^{0}_{j}(\sigma_{j}(s))-\bar{w}^{0}_{j}(s)|\,ds<\frac{1}{j}.

Recalling that
S_{j}<\bar{S}+\frac{1}{j}\leq\bar{S}+1
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{S_{j}}|\bar{w}^{0}(\sigma_{j}(s))-\bar{w}^{0}(s)|\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{S_{j}}|\bar{w}^{0}(\sigma_{j}(s))-\bar{w}^{0}_{j}(\sigma_{j}(s))|\,ds\\
\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{S_{j}}|\bar{w}^{0}_{j}(\sigma_{j}(s))-\bar{w}^{0}_{j}(s)|\,ds+\int_{0}^{S_{j}}|\bar{w}^{0}_{j}(s)-\bar{w}^{0}(s)|\,ds\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad<\int_{0}^{S_{j}}|\bar{w}^{0}(\sigma_{j}(s))-\bar{w}^{0}_{j}(\sigma_{j}(s))|\,ds+\frac{2}{j}.\end{array}

To estimate
\int_{0}^{S_{j}}|\bar{w}^{0}(\sigma_{j}(s))-\bar{w}^{0}_{j}(\sigma_{j}(s))|\,ds
, we make a change of variables using the fact that each
\sigma_{j}
is bi-Lipschitz. Let
s^{\prime}=\sigma_{j}(s)
, then
s=\sigma_{j}^{-1}(s^{\prime})
, and we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{S_{j}}|\bar{w}^{0}(\sigma_{j}(s))-\bar{w}^{0}_{j}(\sigma_{j}(s))|\,ds=\int_{0}^{\sigma_{j}(S_{j})}|\bar{w}^{0}(s^{\prime})-\bar{w}^{0}_{j}(s^{\prime})|\frac{d\sigma_{j}^{-1}}{ds^{\prime}}\,ds^{\prime}\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\int_{0}^{\bar{S}+2}|\bar{w}^{0}(s^{\prime})-\bar{w}^{0}_{j}(s^{\prime})|\frac{d\sigma_{j}^{-1}}{ds^{\prime}}\,ds^{\prime}\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\|\bar{w}^{0}-\bar{w}^{0}_{j}\|_{L^{1}(0,\bar{S}+2)}+3\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left|\frac{d\sigma_{j}^{-1}}{ds^{\prime}}(s^{\prime})-1\right|\,ds^{\prime}<\frac{4}{j},\end{array}

since, using the time-change
s=\sigma_{j}^{-1}(s^{\prime})
, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left|\frac{d\sigma_{j}^{-1}}{ds^{\prime}}(s^{\prime})-1\right|\,ds^{\prime}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left|\frac{1}{\frac{d\sigma_{j}}{ds}(\sigma_{j}^{-1}(s^{\prime}))}-1\right|\,ds^{\prime}\\
\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left|\frac{d\sigma_{j}}{ds}(\sigma_{j}^{-1}(s^{\prime}))-1\right|\frac{d\sigma_{j}^{-1}}{ds^{\prime}}(s^{\prime})\,ds^{\prime}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left|\frac{d\sigma_{j}}{ds}(s)-1\right|\,ds<\frac{1}{j}.\end{array}

Analogous calculations imply that

\int_{0}^{S_{j}}\left|w_{j}(s)-\bar{w}(\sigma_{j}(s))\frac{d\sigma_{j}}{ds}(s)\right|\,ds<\frac{4}{j}

Hence,
\displaystyle{\rm d}(z^{c}_{j},\bar{z})<\frac{10}{j}
, in contradiction with the hypothesis that at
\bar{z}
there is a local infimum gap w.r.t. canonical embedded strict-sense processes. Hence, in view of (
19
), (
20
), the proof in case (a) is concluded.

In case (b), the proof follows from that of case (a), since

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\rm d}(z^{c}_{j},\bar{z})=|S^{c}_{j}-\bar{S}|+\int_{0}^{\bar{S}}\left[|w^{0^{c}}_{j}(s)-\bar{w}^{0}(s)|+|w_{j}^{c}(s)-\bar{w}(s)|\right]\,ds\\
\displaystyle\ \ \quad\qquad\leq|S^{c}_{j}-\bar{S}|+\int_{0}^{S^{c}_{j}}\left[|w^{0^{c}}_{j}(s)-\bar{w}^{0}(s)|+|w_{j}^{c}(s)-\bar{w}(s)|\right]\,ds.\end{array}

∎

4.3
Conclusion of the proof of Theorem
3.1

The three lemmas of the previous subsection justify the fact that it is not restrictive to consider a local infimum gap only at canonical processes and solely among canonical processes. As a main consequence one has that reference canonical extended process
\bar{z}=(\bar{S},\bar{w}^{0},\bar{w},\bar{y}^{0},\bar{y},\bar{\beta})
exhibits a local infimum gap even among the non-canonical processes in
(X^{\mathcal{S}}_{\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{+}},\tilde{\rm d})
.

For every
r>0
, let us now define the following sets:

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{R^{\prime}}^{r}_{\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{+}}:=\Big\{(y^{0},y,\beta)(S):\ z=(S,w^{0},w,y^{0},y,\beta)\in X_{\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{+}},\ \tilde{\rm d}\left(z,\bar{z}\right)<r\Big\},\\[4.30554pt]
\mathcal{R^{\prime}}_{\tilde{\mathcal{W}}}^{r}:=\Big\{(y^{0},y,\beta)(S):\ z=(S,w^{0},w,y^{0},y,\beta)\in X_{\tilde{\mathcal{W}}},\ \tilde{\rm d}\left(z,\bar{z}\right)<r\Big\},\end{array}

as the
reachable set
and the
extended reachable set
(of radius
r
), respectively.

From this point onward, the proof proceeds in a manner entirely analogous to the proof of
[
16
, Theorem 3.1]
, and is therefore omitted. In particular, we note that the needle variations and bracket-like variations introduced in
[
16
]
yield approximations of the reference control
(\bar{w}^{0},\bar{w})
that converge to it in the control distance
\tilde{{\rm d}}
. Thanks to this property, they allow for the construction of a
QDQ
approximating cone to
{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}^{r}_{\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{+}}
at
(\bar{y}^{0}(\bar{S}),\bar{y}(\bar{S}))
.
