On the meagerness of the set of irregular bundles on Hopf surfaces
Edoardo Ballico and Elizabeth Gasparim

Abstract
. Let ℳ r , c \mathcal{M}_{r,c} denote the moduli space of stable bundles with rank r r and second Chern class c > 0 c>0 on a Hopf surface. We prove that the subset of ℳ r , c \mathcal{M}_{r,c} formed by irregular bundles is meager.



Motivation and main result

Our interest in vector bundles on Hopf surfaces is motivated
by open questions about the moduli spaces of instantons on
S^{3}\times S^{1}

described by Edward Witten. As a corollary of our result, we answer in the affirmative
the question of connectedness (posed in
[
W
, Sec. 2.1.4]
)
of the moduli of
SU(2)
instantons.

Let
\pi\colon X\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a classical Hopf surface.
Hence
X
is elliptically fibered and diffeomorphic to
S^{3}\times S^{1}
.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{r,c}
denote the moduli space of stable bundles
with rank
r
and second Chern class
c>0
on
X
.
Our main result here is:

Theorem
.

Let
\cal{U}
be a connected component of
\cal{M}_{r,c}
. Then the set of all
E\in\cal{U}
which are not regular, i.e. either with jumps or irregular, is meager in
\cal{U}

(and hence the set of all
E\in\cal{U}
which are regular is dense in every open subset of
\cal{U}
.

A caveat and some basic concepts

Before proving the main theorem, it is worth pointing out why it can not be argued
simply by analogy with the algebraic case. Using the concepts of
filtrable and unstable bundles, we give
2
illustrative examples of new phenomena
appearing in the non-Kähler setup.

Example 1
.

It is well know that bundles on projective surfaces are filtrable;
e.g. if
E
is a rank
2
bundle on a a smooth projective surface then
then it may be written as an extension

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }L\xrightarrow{\ \ }E\xrightarrow{\ \ }F\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

of a rank
1
sheaf by a line bundle, see
[
Fr
, Ch. 2, Prop. 5]
.

However,
[
BG2
, Thm. 3.9]
proves that filtrable bundles on a classical
Hopf surface
X
must have jumps.
Therefore, the dense open set of
\mathcal{M}_{r,c}
formed by stable bundles without jumps
consists solely of nonfiltrable bundles.

Example 2
.

On
[
BGRS
, App. A]
it is
shown that the moduli stack of rank
2
vector bundles on a projective variety of general type
has entire components formed by uniquely of unstable bundles, with some such components
having dimension strictly larger than that of the moduli space of stable bundles.
With this in mind, a priori one might have expected that there also existed large components
of the moduli of bundles on the Hopf surface which are formed only of irregular bundles.
It is the purpose of this note to prove this is not the case.

We now establish some notation and basic definitions.
Fix a Gauduchon metric on the classical Hopf
X
. Stability will always be with respect to this metric.
For each pair of integers
r\geq 2
and
c>0
, let
\cal{M}_{r,c}
denote the moduli space of all
stable rank
r
vector bundles
E
on
X
such that
c_{2}(E)=c
and
\det(E)\cong\cal{O}_{X}
.
It is known that
\cal{M}_{r,c}
is smooth and of dimension
2rc
.

We now recall a couple of elementary concepts from topology. Let
M
be a topological space.
The subspace
A\subset M
is called nowhere dense in
M
if
\mbox{int}_{M}(\overline{A})=\emptyset
,
that is, if the closure of
A
contains no open subset of
M
.
The set
A
is called meager if it is a countable union
A=\cup A_{n}
of nowhere dense subsets
A_{n}\subset M
.

Let
\pi\colon X\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the elliptic fibration of the Hopf surface. For each
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
set
T_{x}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\pi^{-1}(x)
.
Denote by
T
be the elliptic curve such that
\pi
is a principal
T
-bundle, hence
T_{x}\cong T
for all
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Take
E\in\cal{M}_{r,c}
. We say that
E
has a jump if there is
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that
E_{|T_{x}}
is not semistable.

Let
F
be a rank
r
semistable vector bundle on
T
such that
\det(F)\cong\cal{O}_{T}
.
Recall from
[
FMW
, Def. 1.12]
that
F
is said to be regular
if its indecomposable factors are all different i.e. associated to pairwise distinct elements of
\mathrm{Pic}(T)
. Otherwise
F
is said to be irregular on
T
. Regular bundles present good behaviour inside the moduli spaces, because
they have the smallest possible amount of automorphisms.
A bundles
E
on
X
is said to be irregular if there exists an
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that
E|_{T_{x}}
is irregular.
Otherwise,
E
is said to be regular on
X
.

The meagerness of the irregular locus

Lemma 3
.

Let
F
be a rank
r\geq 2
vector bundle on an elliptic curve
T
such that
\det(F)\cong\cal{O}_{T}
. By Atiyah’s classification
F
is a direct sum of semistable indecomposable ones,
h^{0}(T,\operatorname{End}(F))\geq r
and equality holds if
F
is regular. If
F
is not regular, then
h^{0}(T,\operatorname{End}(F))\geq r+2
.

Proof.

For the first statement is proved in
[
FMW
, Lem. 1.13]
. Now assume that
F
is not regular. In particular,
F
is decomposable. Write
F=F_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus F_{s}
with each
F_{i}
indecomposable. Let
r_{i}
be the rank of
F_{i}
. Set
d_{i}:=\deg(F_{i})
.

First, assume that
F
is not semistable. Since
\det(F)
has degree
0
, there is
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
such that
d_{i}>0
. Since
d_{1}+\cdots+d_{s}=0
, there is an index
j
such that
d_{j}<0
. Let
e
(resp.
f
) be the number of factors of
F
with positive (resp. negative) degree. We have
e>0
,
f>0
and
e+f\leq s
.
Permuting the factors of
F
we may assume
d_{a}\geq d_{b}
for all
a\geq b
. For all
a,b\in\{1,\dots,s\}
the vector bundle
\operatorname{Hom}(F_{b},F_{a})
is semistable of rank
r_{a}r_{b}
and of degree
d_{a}r_{b}-d_{b}r_{a}
. Fix
a,b\in\{1,\dots,s\}
such that
d_{a}>d_{b}
. Since
\operatorname{Hom}(F_{b},F_{a})
is semistable of rank
r_{a}r_{b}
and of degree
d_{a}r_{b}-d_{b}r_{a}>0
, we have
h^{0}(T,\operatorname{Hom}(F_{b},F_{a}))=d_{a}r_{b}-d_{b}r_{a}
. Write
F=F[-1]\oplus F[0]\oplus F[1]
with
F[0]
the direct sum of the degree
0
factors of
F
, if any, and
F[-1]
the direct sum of the negative degree factors and
F[1]
the sum of the positive degree factors of
F
. Set
a_{i}:=\mathrm{rank}(F[i])
. We have
a_{0}+a_{1}+a_{-1}=r
,
a_{0}\geq 0
,
a_{1}\geq 1
and
a_{2}\geq 1
. Since each factor of
F[1]
has degree
\geq 1
and each factor of
F[-1]
has degree
\leq-1
, we have
h^{0}(T,\operatorname{Hom}(F[-1],F[1])\geq a_{1}+a_{-1}
. We also have
h^{0}(T,\operatorname{Hom}(F[-1],F[0])\geq a_{0}
. Since
H^{0}(T,\operatorname{Hom}(F,F))
contains also
H^{0}(T,\operatorname{Hom}(F[-1],F[-1])
and
H^{0}(T,\operatorname{Hom}(F[1],F[1])
and these vector spaces are non-zero (containing the multiplication by constants), we get
h^{0}(T,\operatorname{Hom}(F,F))\geq a_{1}+a_{0}+a_{2}+2=r+2
.

Now assume that
F
is semistable. Hence (with the notation of
[
FMW
, §1]
)
F_{i}\cong I_{r_{i}}(\lambda_{i})
for some
\lambda_{i}\in T^{\ast}
. By
[
FMW
, Lemma 1.13]
we have
h^{0}(T,\operatorname{End}(F_{i}))=r_{i}
. Hence
h^{0}(T,\operatorname{End}(F))=r+\sum_{i\neq j}h^{0}(T,\operatorname{Hom}(F_{i},F_{j}))
(in the sum we take all
i<j
and all
i>j
).
Since
F
is irregular, there are
i,j
such that
i\neq j
and
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{j}
. Hence
h^{0}(T,\operatorname{Hom}(F_{i},F_{j}))\geq 1
and
h^{0}(T,\operatorname{Hom}(F_{j},F_{i}))\geq 1
.
We conclude that
h^{0}(T,\operatorname{End}(F))\geq r+2
.
∎

Theorem 4
.

Let
\cal{U}
be a connected component of
\cal{M}_{r,c}
. Then the set of all
E\in\cal{U}
which are not regular, i.e. either with jumps or irregular, is meager in
\cal{U}

(and hence the set of all
E\in\cal{U}
which are regular is dense in every open subset of
\cal{U}
.

Proof.

Let
A
be the set of all
E\in\cal{U}
which are not regular, i.e. such that there is
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
for which
E_{|F_{x}}
is not regular. For each
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, let
A_{x}
denote the set of all
E\in\cal{U}
such that
E_{|T_{x}}
is not regular.
Since
\dim\mathbb{P}^{1}=1
, it is sufficient to prove that each
A_{x}
is locally, around each of its points,
a closed analytic subset of
\cal{U}
of codimension at least

2
. Fix
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
E\in A_{x}
. Set
F:=E_{|T_{x}}
. Consider the exact sequence

(1)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{End}(E)(-T_{x})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{End}(E)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{End}(F)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0\text{.}

Duality gives

h^{2}(X,\operatorname{End}(E)(-T_{x}))=h^{0}(X,\operatorname{End}(E)(+T_{x})\otimes\omega_{X})=h^{0}(X,\operatorname{End}(E)(-T_{x})).

Since
E
is simple, each element of
H^{0}(\operatorname{End}(E))
is induced by the multiplication by a constant. Therefore,
h^{0}(X,\operatorname{End}(E)(-T_{x}))=0
.

Thus, the linear map
\rho\colon H^{1}(X,\operatorname{End}(E))\xrightarrow{\ \ }H^{1}(T_{x},\operatorname{End}(F))
is surjective, and an open neighborhood
of
0\in H^{1}(X,\operatorname{End}(E))
is biholomorphic to an open neighborhood of
E
in
\cal{M}_{r,c}
. An open neighborhood of
0
in
H^{1}(T_{x},\operatorname{End}(F))
is a versal deformation space for
F
. Since
\rho
is surjective, Lemma
3
gives that

A_{x}
has locally at
E
at least codimension
2
in
\cal{U}
.
∎

The following corollary follows implicitly from
[
BH
]
and is discussed in further detail in
[
Ga
]
,
where it is proved under the assumption that each component of the moduli
\mathcal{M}_{2,c}

contains a stable bundle. Our Theorem
4
shows that such an assumption is a truism.

Corollary 5
.

Take
r=2
. The complex manifold
\cal{M}_{2,c}
is connected.

Proof.

By Theorem
4
it is sufficient to prove that the set of all regular
E\in\cal{M}_{c,2}
it is connected.
Recall that to each
E\in\cal{M}_{2,c}
there corresponds a spectral curve
C_{E}
, which
when
E
is regular is a smooth and connected curve of genus
2n-1

[
BG1
, Thm. 1]
.
To each
E\in\cal{M}_{2,c}
there also corresponds a graph
G_{E}
, and each graph is associated to a unique spectral curve.

The set of all graphs associated to at least one regular bundle is an irreducible Zariski open subset of
\mathbb{P}^{2n+1}
.
Furthermore, for each spectral curve
C_{E}
associated to a regular bundle, all bundles
F

having
C_{E}=C_{F}
are regular
[
BG1
, Lem. 1]
.
Moreover, the set of such bundles (i.e. the fibre of the graph map
G^{-1}(E)
) is
parametrized, by a smooth and connected projective variety, the Jacobian of the smooth genus
2n-1
curve
C_{E}
.
∎
