Safety and efficacy of microsurgical decompression for 6 arachnoid bands
Authors: Igor Baroni Cardoso, Lara Mensato Döring, Anna Caroline Ulson da Costa, Lucas Cesar Werneck, Emannuel Lucas Machado da Silva, Joel Sanabria, José Lucas Malosti Teodoro Rodrigues, Afonso Henrique de Aragão, Leonardo Gilmone Ruschel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Dorsal spinal arachnoid bands are a rare cause of focal spinal cord compression and thoracic myelopathy that alter cerebrospinal fluid dynamics and may lead to progressive neurological deficits and syringomyelia. Nevertheless, the clinical outcomes of surgical treatment remain incompletely characterized. Here, we evaluated the safety and clinical outcomes of 6 microsurgical decompressions for dorsal thoracic arachnoid bands in a single-center cohort.Methods: Six patients underwent surgery for dorsal thoracicspinal arachnoid bands with/without syringomyelia (2021–2025) at a single center. Demographic, diagnostic, and surgical reports and outcomes were assessed. Surgical decompressions utilized microsurgical techniques and intraoperative neurophysiological monitoring.Results: Six patients averaged 49.3 years of age, and four were female. T6 was the most involved thoracic level. Surgery included mostly hemilaminectomies addressing 6 arachnoid bands and 4 syrinxes. Postoperatively, 5 patients improved, while one remained unchanged. Only one postoperative adverse event occurred: pneumonia. Postoperative magnetic resonance studies documented complete arachnoid band/syrinx resolution.Conclusion: Microsurgical decompression of dorsal spinal arachnoid bands is both safe and effective.

INTRODUCTION

Spinal arachnoid webs/dorsal arachnoid bands are rare, typically thoracic lesions that cause myelopathy due to focal spinal cord indentation, disruption of cerebrospinal fluid flow, and/ or syrinx formation. On magnetic resonance (MR) studies, the classically show the “scalpel sign”.[8] Surgical removal of these arachnoid membranes/bands is indicated to relieve progressive myelopathy.[3] Here, we reviewed 6 cases of arachnoid bands/4 syrinxes, all of whom successfully underwent surgical decompression.

METHODS

This retrospective single-center case series involves 6 patients who underwent thoracic decompressions for spinal arachnoid bands/4 syrinx formation (2021–2025). Surgical approaches utilized intraoperative neural monitoring and were localized to the site of the individual lesions. The authors utilized medical records to assess clinical, radiographic, and surgical data and define postoperative outcomes.

RESULTS

Illustrative case

A 50-year-old male presented with a 1-year history of progressive lower-limb weakness and paresthesias, and 6 months of thoracic back pain. His neurological examination revealed fasciculations in the anterior thigh region, hyperreflexia, and a wide-based gait, but normal motor/sensory function. The thoracic MR showed a “scalpel sign” and a dorsal T6-T7 arachnoid web producing posterior spinal cord compression and a hyperintense intramedullary signal on the T2-weighted study [Figure 1]. Through a unilateral T6–T7 hemilaminectomy, under microscope visualization and neurophysiological monitoring, the unilateral ligamentum flavum was removed. Once the dura was opened, the thickened arachnoid membrane/band was found to be causing posterior compression of the spinal cord; it was readily removed [Figure 2]. Postoperatively, the patient experienced complete resolution of preoperative symptoms and was discharged on postoperative day 1.

Summary of all 6 cases

Six patients (4 female, 2 male), averaging 49.3 years (range 28–68) of age, presented with preoperative symptoms that included: back/neck pain (50%), gait instability (16.7%), sensory/motor deficits (33.3%), and syringomyelia (66.6%). Arachnoid bands were predominantly thoracic (83.3%), with T6 being the most common level (n = 2). Other levels included C7, T4, T5, and T10. Surgical techniques consisted of hemilaminectomy (n = 4), laminoplasty (n = 1), and laminectomy (n = 1). Post-operative symptomatic improvement was achieved in 83.3% (n = 5) of patients, while one remained stable (16.7%); no clinical deterioration was observed. Radiological follow-up confirmed complete resolution of the arachnoid band in all cases. The only recorded complication was one case of postoperative pneumonia (16.7%) [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

In the largest single-institution series reported to date, Carr et al.[2] analyzed 41 surgically treated patients with predominant thoracic spine involvement (i.e., mostly at the T6 level).

Most reports diagnose arachnoid bands predominantly in the fifth to sixth decades of life,[2,4,6] suggesting a slowly progressive pathophysiological process or delayed clinical recognition.

Although conventional laminectomy remains widely employed, there is currently an increasing interest in less invasive microsurgical posterior approaches. Carr et al.[2] reported six hemilaminectomies, while Nisson et al.[6] described five hemilaminectomies; hemilaminectomy was the approach successfully utilized in our cohort [Table 2].

CONCLUSION

Microsurgical decompression for resection of symptomatic arachnoid bands is a safe and effective technique, typically resulting in significant neurological improvement of preoperative myelopathy.
