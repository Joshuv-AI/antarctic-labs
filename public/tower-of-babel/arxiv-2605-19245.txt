Entangling gate performance and fidelity limits with neutral atom Förster resonances
Sam A. Norrell, Yufei Shen, Mark Saffman, Matthew Otten

Abstract
Neutral-atom entangling gates are commonly analyzed with a single effective Rydberg-pair state, but near Förster resonances the pair manifold contains resonantly coupled interaction channels that change both the control landscape and the achievable fidelity. We develop a two-eigenstate model for this regime and show that when allowing for coupling to both pair states in the resonance, the gate fidelity is bounded by ℱ ≤ 1 − ( π / 2 ) / ( V ​ τ R ) \mathcal{F}\leq 1-(\pi/2)/(V\tau_{R}) , for interaction strength V V and Rydberg lifetime τ R \tau_{R} . We construct a gate protocol that saturates this bound in the large-Rabi-frequency limit, improving the existing fidelity limit by approximately 40 % 40\% . We also evaluate common gate protocols near Förster resonances and find that retaining the exchange dynamics increases predicted fidelities by up to two orders of magnitude over earlier treatments.

A key challenge in pushing fidelity further is understanding and controlling the two fundamental error channels: decay from Rydberg states during the gate, and coherent leakage into off-target states in the Rydberg manifold due to finite blockade
Saffman and Walker 2005
;
Doultsinos and Petrosyan 2025
;
Jandura and Pupillo 2022
;
Jandura et al. 2023
;
Pagano et al. 2022
;
Mohan et al. 2023
;
Cole et al. 2026
;
Petrosyan et al. 2017

To model these error sources, most analyses adopt a single-channel interaction model in which an effective state
\ket{rr}
describes and condenses all Rydberg interactions

;
Jandura and Pupillo 2022
;
Jaksch et al. 2000
;
Shi 2018

In this letter, we develop a systematic treatment of
two-state
pair physics and translate the resulting mechanism into concrete pulse sequences for entangling gates.
Two-state models are of particular relevance, as resonant dipole-dipole interactions between Rydberg atoms (so-called Förster resonances) result in symmetric, bifurcated interaction channels.
Förster resonances are popular state choices in quantum processors as they increase gate fidelities at longer interaction range due to
1/r^{3}
scaling, compared to the
1/r^{6}
Van der Waals scaling of induced dipole interactions

;
Ryabtsev et al. 2010
;
Nipper et al. 2012
;
Ravets et al. 2015
;
Beterov and Saffman 2015
;
Beterov et al. 2016
;
Browaeys et al. 2016
;
Anand et al. 2024
;
Giudici et al. 2025
;
Palm et al. 2026
.
Whereas Förster resonances increase atom-atom interaction magnitude
V
, we compare such resonances to single-channel models with the same effective energy, thereby isolating the effects of the increased dimensionality alone.

In Förster resonances, the two equally spaced eigenstates combine under optical drive into a laser-

and a laser-

;
Mostaan et al. 2026
;
Keating et al. 2015

The dark state is not directly driven but is coupled to the bright state via the pair interaction. This bright / dark interaction gives rise to a cancellation mechanism that can strongly suppress coherent gate errors. We investigate the dynamics between the bright and dark states, and demonstrate the importance of including this interaction when evaluating gate performance for Förster resonant states.

Atomic Models
–
We consider a pair of atoms –
A
and
B
– in Rydberg states
\ket{a}
,
\ket{b}
, respectively, separated by
\textbf{r}=r\hat{n}
(as in Fig.

The atoms interact via a dipole-dipole interaction, summed over many Rydberg states
\ket{k},\ket{l},\ket{m},\ket{n}
,

H_{\rm dd}(\textbf{r})=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}r^{3}}\sum_{k,l,m,n}\ket{k}_{AA}\hskip-1.9919pt\bra{l}\otimes\ket{m}_{BB}\hskip-1.9919pt\bra{n}\times\\
\left[\textbf{d}_{kl,A}\cdot\textbf{d}_{mn,B}-3\left(\textbf{d}_{kl,A}\cdot\hat{n}\right)\left(\textbf{d}_{mn,B}\cdot\hat{n}\right)\right],

In standard treatments, all interaction channels
i
are condensed into a single effective state
\ket{rr}
, which is shifted by an amount
V\equiv\left(\sum_{i}\frac{o_{i}}{V_{i}^{2}}\right)^{-1/2}

Walker and Saffman 2008
.
We refer to such a model as the “one-eigenstate” model.
In the limit of a perfect Förster resonance,
H_{\rm dd}
has exactly two eigenstates,
\ket{e_1}
and
\ket{e_2}
, shifted symmetrically by energies
V_{1}=-V_{2}
from the non-interacting target state energy. We refer to this model as the “two-eigenstate” model. Of note, the one-eigenstate model applied to a Förster resonance condenses the two channels to
\ket{rr}
such that
|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=V
, but as we show below, this operation does not preserve state dynamics crucial to gate fidelity calculations.

A Förster resonance requires two additional nearby states
\ket{\alpha}_{A},\ket{\beta}_{B}
with energy spacings
\delta_{A}=E_{\alpha}-E_{a}
and
\delta_{B}=E_{\beta}-E_{b}
satisfying
\delta_{A}+\delta_{B}=0
. In the resonance, a virtual photon process causes coherent population exchange
\ket{ab}\leftrightarrow\ket{\alpha\beta}
. Figure
1
(b-c) shows a Förster resonance in both the single-atom and pair-state frames, along with the population transfer between
\ket{ab}
and
\ket{\alpha\beta}
. The remaining panels summarize our core findings. Here, and in all subsequent figures,
\tau_{R}=150\ \mu\rm{s}
, corresponding to
n\approx 70
for rubidium or cesium
ns
states at
300
K.

For atom
X\in\{A,B\}
, consider the distinct Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{q}^{X}
and
\mathcal{H}_{R}^{X}
, describing the qubit and Rydberg manifolds respectively. We define the
rank
of any pulse sequence as the maximum of
\{\textsf{rank}(H_{\rm drive}^{A}),\textsf{rank}(H_{\rm drive}^{B})\}
, where
H_{\rm drive}^{X}:\mathcal{H}_{q}^{X}\mapsto\mathcal{H}_{R}^{X}
.
Typically, neutral-atom Rydberg gates use rank-one pulses. Rank-two pulses have been proposed, enabling fast or long-range gates
Bergonzoni et al. 2026
;
Ildefonso et al. 2025
.

Within the Rydberg manifold of a Förster resonance, the non-zero elements of
H_{\rm dd}
require both atoms to change states, as
H_{\rm dd}
only contains products of single-atom operators. This results in the interaction Hamiltonian
(\hbar=1)
, in the basis
\{\ket{ab},\ket{\alpha\beta},\ket{a\beta},\ket{\alpha b}\}
of

H_{\rm int}=\begin{pmatrix}0&V&0&0\\
V^{*}&0&0&0\\
0&0&\delta_{B}&W\\
0&0&W^{*}&\delta_{A}\end{pmatrix},

(2)

where
V
and
W
are calculated per Eq. (
1
). For atoms aligned along a shared quantization axis,
V=W
. We assume this geometry henceforth. In the rotating frame, and by making the rotating wave approximation (RWA), the Hamiltonian reduces to

H_{\rm int}=V\left(\ket{ab}\bra{\alpha\beta}+\ket{\alpha\beta}\bra{ab}\right).

(3)

The validity of RWA mandates
V/|\delta_{X}|\ll 1
. For Rydberg level spacings of order GHz and long-range Förster resonances of order MHz, this condition is easily satisfied.

Existing Fidelity Bound
– Following existing gate fidelity analysis frameworks
Doultsinos et al. 2025
;
Wesenberg et al. 2007
, we define for any two-atom state
\ket{\psi}
the Rydberg population
P_{r}=\bra{\psi}\hat{\Pi}_{r}\ket{\psi}
, where
\hat{\Pi}_{r}
is the projector onto all configurations in which at least one atom occupies a Rydberg state.
For a gate of duration
T\ll\tau_{R}=1/\Gamma
, the decay error is

\varepsilon_{\rm decay}=\Gamma\int_{0}^{T}dt\,P_{r}(t)=\Gamma T_{R},

(4)

where
T_{R}
is the integrated Rydberg population time. Minimizing gate errors due to finite Rydberg lifetime is equivalent to minimization of
T_{R}
.

The minimum integrated Rydberg time to generate complete entanglement is measured by the entropy
S=-\log_{2}(c_{1}^{2})
, where
c_{1}
is the largest Schmidt coefficient of the atom pair.
It can be shown that
Doultsinos et al. 2025

T_{R}\geq\int_{0}^{1}ds\,G(s),\quad G(s)=\min_{\psi:\,S(\psi)=s}\frac{P_{r}(\psi)}{|\dot{S}(\psi)|},

(5)

where
G
is minimized over all two-atom states with minimum entropy
s
. The coefficient

\eta\equiv V\int_{0}^{1}ds\,G(s)

(6)

is therefore the figure-of-merit: gates that prepare a maximally entangled state satisfy
1-\mathcal{F}\geq\frac{\eta}{V\tau_{R}}
. It can be shown that for any rank-one pulse sequence

\eta\geq 1+\frac{\pi}{2},

(7)

and this bound is tight
Doultsinos et al. 2025
.

Rank-Two Gate Fidelity Bound
–

We consider now a gate protocol which directly addresses each of the single-atom states of a Förster resonance,
\ket{a},\ket{b},\ket{\alpha},\ket{\beta}
. In this model, the fundamental gate limit is set by

\eta\geq\frac{\pi}{2}.

(8)

The full proof of this bound is presented in the Supplemental Material
Nor
. This value is approximately 40% lower than the value calculated from single-eigenstate models.
We show in the proof that even two-eigenstate models with rank-one access are bounded by
\eta\geq 1+\frac{\pi}{2}
. Therefore, the increase in fidelity can be directly attributed to the increase in the rank access of the pulse.

To saturate the equality of this bound, we consider a
\pi-\rm{gap}-\pi
sequence, with crossed rank-two
\pi
pulses of Rabi frequency
\Omega
on atoms
A
and
B
(shown in Fig.
1
(e)):

Drive on
A
:

\displaystyle\ket{0}_{A}\leftrightarrow\ket{\alpha}_{A},\ \ket{1}_{A}\leftrightarrow\ket{a}_{A}

Drive on
B
:

\displaystyle\ket{0}_{B}\leftrightarrow\ket{b}_{B},\ \ket{1}_{B}\leftrightarrow\ket{\beta}_{B}.

This gate is analogous to other Rydberg interaction gate protocols
Jaksch et al. 2000
;
Chew et al. 2022
;
Petrosyan et al. 2017
, but extended to rank-two drive. All subsequent analysis is performed in the rotating frame, so Eq. (
3
) is the relevant interaction. We address the three steps of the pulse individually.

(i) Excitation (
\pi
Pulse 1)
The Rydberg population for each atom is given by

p_{r}(t)=\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\Omega^{2}+V^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\Omega^{2}+V^{2}}}{2}t\right).

(9)

The off-resonant cross-terms,
\ket{0}\to\ket{a}
for instance, are forbidden by selection rules, as
\ket{a}
and
\ket{\alpha}
are assumed to have different parities
(-1)^{\ell}
with
\ell
the orbital angular momentum. The total Rydberg population during the excitation pulse is

P_{r}(t)=p_{r}^{A}(t)+p_{r}^{B}(t)=2p_{r}(t).

(10)

Setting
t_{\pi}=\pi/\Omega
,

\eta_{\rm excitation}=V\int_{0}^{t_{\pi}}dt\,P_{r}(t)=\pi\frac{V}{\Omega}+\mathcal{O}\left[\left(\frac{V}{\Omega}\right)^{3}\right].

(11)

There is an additional error term, namely the incomplete population transfer, which scales as
\left(V/\Omega\right)^{2}
. Assuming
\Omega\gg V
, this error is sub-leading and the following map is realized:

\displaystyle\ket{00}\to-\ket{a\beta},\quad\ket{01}\to-\ket{ab},

\displaystyle\ket{10}\to-\ket{\alpha\beta},\quad\ket{11}\to-\ket{\alpha b}.

(ii) Interaction (“gap”)

The states
\ket{a\beta}
and
\ket{\alpha b}
are eigenstates of
H_{\rm int}
and are thus stationary in the rotating frame. The states
\ket{ab}
and
\ket{\alpha\beta}
oscillate under the Förster coupling. Explicitly, for
t_{\rm gap}=\pi/(4V)
:

\displaystyle-\ket{ab}

\displaystyle\to\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(-\ket{ab}+i\ket{\alpha\beta}\right),

\displaystyle-\ket{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\to\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(-\ket{\alpha\beta}+i\ket{ab}\right),

\displaystyle-\ket{a\beta}

\displaystyle\to-\ket{a\beta},

\displaystyle-\ket{\alpha b}

\displaystyle\to-\ket{\alpha b}.

The total Rydberg population is
P_{r}(t)=2
for all four inputs throughout the gap, so

\eta_{\rm gap}=V\int_{0}^{t_{\rm gap}}dt\,P_{r}(t)=V\cdot 2\cdot\frac{\pi}{4V}=\frac{\pi}{2}.

(12)

(iii) Return (
\pi
Pulse 2)

The system is brought back to the qubit manifold via the same
\pi
pulse as in the excitation step, so the calculation of
\eta
is identical. This protocol realizes a
\sqrt{i\sf{SWAP}}^{\dagger}
gate, which is maximally entangling.

Summing the three contributions to
\eta
,

\eta=\eta_{\rm excitation}+\eta_{\rm gap}+\eta_{\rm return}=\frac{\pi}{2}+2\pi\frac{V}{\Omega}+\mathcal{O}\left[\left(\frac{V}{\Omega}\right)^{3}\right],

(13)

so that in the limit
\Omega\gg V
,
\eta\to\pi/2
.

Concrete examples of the performance of this pulse sequence are shown in Fig.

8
), with complete saturation in the limit
\Omega\to\infty
. Finite Rabi frequency causes incomplete Rydberg transfer, leading to gate infidelity above the bound. This error channel can be mitigated by pulse sequences robust to off-resonant drive, resulting in higher gate performance at lower Rabi frequency.

Increased Gate Fidelity with Förster Resonances
–
Owing to the experimental challenges in realizing rank-two access to the Rydberg manifold, we now consider rank-one implementations, where population is only driven to
\ket{a}
and
\ket{b}
.

We analyze the following gate protocols: Adiabatic Rapid Passage (ARP)
Saffman et al. 2020
,
\pi-2\pi-\pi

Jaksch et al. 2000
, and Time Optimal (TO)
Jandura and Pupillo 2022
. We calculate gate fidelities for a Förster resonance using both one- and two-eigenstate models at Rabi frequency
\Omega_{\rm max}=2\pi\times 10
MHz, with results presented in Fig.
3
. Simulated gate fidelities differ by up to two orders of magnitude between the two atomic models. Imperfect Förster resonances, where
o_{1}\approx o_{2}
yet
o_{1}\neq o_{2}
still exhibit exchange dynamics and therefore provide increased gate fidelities over one-eigenstate models. An analysis of imperfect resonances is presented in
Nor
.

To explain the increase in gate fidelity in the two-eigenstate model, we consider the
\pi-2\pi-\pi
gate in the
V>\Omega
limit.
In the one-eigenstate model, the
2\pi
pulse off-resonantly drives
\ket{a1}\mapsto\ket{ab}
, due to finite detuning
V
. This produces a residual linear AC-Stark phase on
\ket{a1}
of order
\Omega/V
, the dominant error source compared to the population leakage of order
\left(\Omega/V\right)^{2}
.
In the two-eigenstate model,
\ket{ab}
is not a stationary state; the Förster coupling
V
continuously mixes
\ket{ab}
and
\ket{\alpha\beta}
. There is therefore identical phase cancellation due to dark-state structure
Petrosyan et al. 2017
, and the remaining error contributions are to state amplitudes of order
\left(\Omega/V\right)^{2}
. Full calculations of the presented scalings are given in
Nor
.

Optimization of TO gates under one- or two-eigenstate models yields identical gate performance in the blockade regime (Fig.
3
(c)), which we attribute to the ability of the pulse to adapt to different models by changing optimization weights.
Despite this, gates numerically optimized under the one-eigenstate model perform significantly worse when applied to the two-eigenstate model. This discrepancy emphasizes the importance of including all dominant interaction channels within the Rydberg manifold during gate design and optimization. Simplified one-eigenstate descriptions fail to accurately predict gate performance for Rydberg pair states with multiple interaction channels. Furthermore, due to the presence of local minima in the phase landscape, it is unlikely experimental optimizations of pulse waveforms will converge to maximum fidelity when starting from pulses optimized under an atomic model that does not match the dimensionality of the pair interaction space.

Conclusion
–
We have analyzed Rydberg gate design through the lens of two-eigenstate pair physics of Förster resonances. The main results are as follows.

(i) The exchange interaction between manifolds within Förster resonances can strongly attenuate entangling gate errors. This mechanism is not predicted by models typically used in gate analyses. Atomic models that include this interaction are therefore required to provide accurate gate fidelity calculations.

(ii) The two-eigenstate model presented here provides a fundamental gate limit of
\eta\geq\frac{\pi}{2}
when allowing for rank-two access to the pair state Hilbert space. We provide exact pulse sequences that saturate this bound.

We note that for rank-one gate pulses, no additional hardware is required to realize enhanced gate fidelities other than addressing levels that are Förster resonant. Further, the increased gate fidelity is most pronounced in the low
V
regime, thereby improving long-range gates which is a key challenge in realizing error-correction codes for neutral atoms.

Define

C_{jk}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x}}\sum_{i>1}c_{i}\,u_{i,j}\,v_{i,k},\qquad|n\rangle=\sum_{j,k}C_{jk}|jk\rangle.

Each product state
|jk\rangle
is an eigenvector of
\Pi
with eigenvalue
0
,
1
, or
2
, and the eigenvalue equals
2
exactly when
j\in\{a,\alpha\}
and
k\in\{b,\beta\}
. Therefore

P_{n}=\langle n|\Pi|n\rangle\geq 2\bigl(|C_{ab}|^{2}+|C_{\alpha\beta}|^{2}\bigr)=2\bigl(|\langle ab|n\rangle|^{2}+|\langle\alpha\beta|n\rangle|^{2}\bigr).

Combining we obtain

\bigl|\langle uv|H_{\mathrm{int}}|n\rangle\bigr|\leq V\,\frac{P_{1}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{P_{n}}{2}}.

(SM.13)

If
P_{1}=0
or
P_{n}=0
, then (
SM.13
) gives
\langle uv|H_{\mathrm{int}}|n\rangle=0
, hence
\dot{S}_{\min}(\psi)=0
by (
SM.7
), and the desired lower bound is trivial. We therefore assume
P_{1}>0
and
P_{n}>0
.

Combining (
SM.8
), (
SM.13
), and (
SM.6
),

\frac{P(\psi)}{|\dot{S}_{\min}(\psi)|}\geq\frac{\ln 2}{V}\sqrt{\frac{x}{1-x}}\;\frac{xP_{1}+(1-x)P_{n}}{P_{1}\sqrt{P_{n}/2}}.

(SM.14)

Now set
t=\sqrt{P_{n}/2}\in(0,1]
. Then

\frac{xP_{1}+(1-x)P_{n}}{P_{1}\sqrt{P_{n}/2}}=\frac{x}{t}+\frac{2(1-x)}{P_{1}}\,t\geq\frac{x}{t}+(1-x)t,

because
P_{1}\leq 2
. Finally,

\frac{x}{t}+(1-x)t-1=\frac{(1-t)\bigl(x-(1-x)t\bigr)}{t}\geq 0,

(SM.15)

since
0<t\leq 1
and
x\geq 1/2
. Therefore

\frac{xP_{1}+(1-x)P_{n}}{P_{1}\sqrt{P_{n}/2}}\geq 1,

which implies from (
SM.14
) that

\frac{P(\psi)}{|\dot{S}_{\min}(\psi)|}\geq\frac{\ln 2}{V}\sqrt{\frac{x}{1-x}}.

(SM.16)

This lower bound is saturated by

|\psi_{s}\rangle=\sqrt{x}\,|ab\rangle+i\,\operatorname{sgn}(V)\sqrt{1-x}\,|\alpha\beta\rangle,

for which
P_{1}=P_{n}=2
and
|\langle uv|H_{\mathrm{int}}|n\rangle|=V
.
Hence

G_{\mathrm{full}}(s)\equiv\min_{\psi_{s}}\frac{P(\psi_{s})}{|\dot{S}_{\min}(\psi_{s})|}=\frac{\ln 2}{V}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{s}-1}}.

(SM.17)

The proof above is for
0<s<1
; the value at
s=1
follows by continuity from below and is attained by the wavefunction
\ket{\psi}=(|ab\rangle+i\,\operatorname{sgn}(V)|\alpha\beta\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
.

Integrating
G_{\mathrm{full}}(s)
over the interval
s\in[0,1]
yields

\eta_{\mathrm{full}}=V\int_{0}^{1}G_{\mathrm{full}}(s)\,ds=\frac{\pi}{2}.

(SM.18)

The proof presented above relies on rank-two coupling to the Rydberg manifold. A two-eigenstate model with only rank-one coupling would not be able to map the entanglement generated from the exchange interaction back to the qubit manifold. Therefore, the increased fidelity bound depends on the
rank
of the pulse sequence, not the dimensionality of the atomic model. Since the rank of the pulse must be no greater than the dimensionality of the atomic model, though, higher-dimensional models are required to accurately evaluate pulses of large rank.

SM.2
Physical Realization

SM.2.1
Rank-Two Pulses

Since the single-atom target states
\ket{a}
and
\ket{b}
are spectrally isolated from the exchange states
\ket{\alpha}
and
\ket{\beta}
, rank-two access to the Rydberg pair state manifold will require one laser per single-atom state. In symmetric structures, where both atoms are the same species,
\ket{a}
and
\ket{b}
are identical states, so they share an excitation laser.
Functionally, to realize rank-two control, one would have to add lasers that address
\ket{\alpha}
and
\ket{\beta}
in addition to the existing lasers that excite
\ket{a}
and
\ket{b}
.
This is most-easily achieved by coupling to Rydberg states of different values of
\ell
, for instance
np
and
ns
states, where, for the case of an
s
orbital ground state, the
p
state is coupled via a one-photon excitation and two-photon excitation to the
s
state.

SM.2.2
Förster Resonances and Interaction Strength

Here, we consider the needed interaction strength
V
to achieve the gate fidelities presented in the main text.
Since we have identified pulses nearly saturating the gate fidelity bound, we calculate interaction strength as given by the fundamental gate fidelity bound, shown in Fig.
SM.1
.
From this figure, we can see that in order to generate entanglement with fidelity
\mathcal{F}>0.999
, an interaction strength of at least 2-5 MHz is required, depending on the lifetime of the specific state addressed.

Interaction energy required to generate entanglement at a given fidelity.

Förster resonances, known for their favorable scaling properties, can provide interaction strengths of this magnitude at impressively large atom separation distances. The resonance between Cs atoms
\ket{64s_{1/2}}\otimes\ket{68s_{1/2}}
, for example, provides 2.88 MHz at 11
\ \mu\rm{m}
, thereby providing the possibility of an entangling gate of up to 4 atoms away in a tightly spaced lattice at moderate values of
n
.

Additional resonances at higher principal quantum number could be used to increase this spacing or afford higher gate fidelity at the same spacing. Further, higher
n
states provide longer Rydberg lifetimes, thereby further increasing the fundamental fidelity limit.

For rank-two pulses, this distance can be extended as the fundamental limit is approximately 40 percent lower than for rank-one pulses. For the resonance considered above, this translates to an atom separation distance of
\sim 13\ \ \mu\rm{m}
, following
1/r^{3}
scaling of resonant dipole-dipole interactions.

SM.3
Existing Gate Protocols with Förster Resonances

Here, we provide details on the gate pulses used to calculate gate fidelities using gate pulses commonly used in neutral atom quantum processors.

SM.3.1
\bf\pi-2\pi-\pi

For the original
\pi-2\pi-\pi
Rydberg gate
Jaksch et al. 2000
we assume degree-6 super-Gaussian pulse profiles for each pulse. Explicitly, the functional form of each pulse
i
for this gate is

\Omega_{i}(t;t_{0},\tau)=\Omega_{\rm max}e^{-((t-t_{0})/\tau)^{6}},

(SM.19)

for
\Omega_{c}(t)
and
\Omega_{t}(t)
defined as the Rabi rates on the control and target qubits, respectively. The three pulses are timed such that pulse overlap is
0.1\%
between the
\pi
and
2\pi
tails and the same percentage sets the start and end times for the gate.

SM.3.2
Adiabatic Rapid Passage (ARP)

The parameters that describe the Rabi rate (
\Omega
) and detuning (
\Delta
) of this pulse are
Saffman et al. 2020

\Omega(t)=\Omega_{\rm max}\,\frac{e^{-(t-t_{0})^{4}/\tau^{4}}-a}{1-a},\qquad a=\exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{t_{0}}{\tau}\right)^{4}\right],

(SM.20)

\Delta_{r}(t)=-\Delta_{r}\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}\,t\right),\qquad t\in[0,T].

(SM.21)

SM.3.3
Time Optimal (TO)

TO gates
Jandura and Pupillo 2022
have a pulse profile with uniform Rabi frequency and a time-varying phase. For this phase, parameterize the phase with an assumed functional form

\Phi(t)=A\cos\left[\frac{\omega}{\Omega}\left(t-\frac{T}{2}\right)-\phi\right]+\frac{\delta}{\Omega}t,

(SM.22)

which approximates the optimal phase function for a total gate time
T
within
0.2\%
of the optimum
Evered et al. 2023
.
Via gradient descent methods, one can tune the parameters
\delta,A,\omega,\phi,T
to optimize gate fidelity. We set the total gate time to
T=7.612/\Omega
. For our analysis we perform a two-step optimization. The first step uses SciPy’s L-BFGS-B minimizer, which uses numerical gradients to walk to a local minimum. Due to the structure of the landscape, this optimizer can get stuck in local minima. To resolve this, stage 2 initializes the optimizer from different starting points, keeping the best result seen. We combine fully random initializations with initializations drawn from a Gaussian distribution around the best-found gate parameters. This does not guarantee full convergence to global maxima, but we heuristically find that the gates found by this protocol result in parameters that match the expected behavior of TO gates.

SM.4
Gate Error Scaling

Here, we use the standard rank-one
\pi-2\pi-\pi
gate as an example to show the reduced errors when considering the two-eigenstate model.

SM.4.1
One-Eigenstate Model

After the first
\pi
pulse, the map

\ket{11}\mapsto\ket{a1}
is realized.
The
2\pi
pulse causes off-resonant excitation to the doubly-populated Rydberg state
\ket{ab}
, due to the fact that
\ket{ab}
has finite detuning
V
. The drive Hamiltonian for this
2\pi
pulse, in the basis of
\{\ket{a1},\ket{ab}\}
is

H_{2\pi}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\Omega/2\\
\Omega/2&V\end{pmatrix}.

(SM.23)

Perturbation theory at second order then gives the energy shift on
\ket{a1}
from this drive:

\Delta E_{\ket{a1}}=\frac{|\bra{a1}H_{2\pi}\ket{ab}|^{2}}{E_{\ket{a1}}-E_{\ket{ab}}}=\frac{\left(\Omega/2\right)^{2}}{0-V}=-\frac{\Omega^{2}}{4V}

(SM.24)

This energy shift causes a phase

\phi=-\Delta E_{\ket{a1}}\times t_{2\pi}=\frac{\Omega^{2}}{4V}\times\frac{2\pi}{\Omega}=\frac{\pi\Omega}{2V}.

(SM.25)

This phase limits gate fidelity and scales as
\frac{\Omega}{V}
.

SM.4.2
Two-Eigenstate Model

In the two-eigenstate model, and in the basis
\{\ket{a1},\ket{ab},\ket{\alpha\beta}\}
, the rank-one drive Hamiltonian for the
2\pi
pulse is

H_{2\pi}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\Omega/2&0\\
\Omega/2&0&V\\
0&V&0\end{pmatrix}.

(SM.26)

The Hamiltonian has eigenvalues
\{0,\pm\Lambda\}
with
\Lambda=\sqrt{V^{2}+\Omega^{2}/4}
, and (non-normalized) eigenvectors

\displaystyle\ket{\phi_0}

\displaystyle=\ket{a1}+-\epsilon\ket{\alpha\beta},

(SM.27)

\displaystyle\ket{\phi_\pm}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\ket{a1}\pm\tfrac{\Lambda}{V}\ket{ab}+\ket{\alpha\beta},

(SM.28)

where
\epsilon\equiv\Omega/(2V)
. Decomposing the initial state
\ket{\psi(0)}=\ket{a1}
into this eigenbasis, evolving under
H_{2\pi}
for time
t
, and projecting back onto
\ket{a1}
yields

\langle a1|\psi(t)\rangle=\frac{1+\epsilon^{2}\cos(\Lambda t)}{1+\epsilon^{2}}.

(SM.29)

This quantity is real at all times, with an amplitude modulation at frequency
\Lambda\approx V
with magnitude
\epsilon^{2}\approx\Omega^{2}/(4V^{2})
. The coherent error for the two-eigenstate model therefore scales quadratically in
\Omega/V
, compared to the linear scaling for the one-eigenstate model, thereby suppressing errors.

o_{1}=1-o_{2}

o_{1}=1/2

o_{1}=o_{2}=1/2
and evaluated with a scan of
