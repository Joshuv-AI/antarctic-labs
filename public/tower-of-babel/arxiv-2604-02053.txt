Terbium-149 PET/CT: First Quantitative Imaging with a Clinical Long-Axial Field-of-View Scanner Journal: EANM Innovation
Lorenzo Mercolli, Pascal V. Grundler, Anzhelika N. Moiseeva, Lars Eggimann, Saverio Braccini, Nicholas P. van der Meulen

Abstract
Introduction: Terbium-149 ( Tb 149 {}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb} ) is a promising radionuclide for targeted α \alpha therapy that has a non-zero branching ratio (BR) for positron decay. However, its relatively low positron branching fraction and multiple prompt γ \gamma emissions may challenge quantitative imaging. This study evaluates, for the first time, the imaging performance and quantitative accuracy of Tb 149 {}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb} using a clinical long axial field-of-view (LAFOV) PET/CT system. Methods: Quantitative accuracy of Tb 149 {}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb} was assessed with a NEMA IEC body phantom, which was filled with about 45 MBq 45\text{\,}\mathrm{MBq} Tb 149 {}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb} and a sphere-to-background ration of 10:1. The phantom was scanned for 20 min 20\text{\,}\mathrm{min} and shorter scan times and lower activities were simulated. Recovery coefficients, coefficient of variation, and lung residual error were evaluated for different reconstruction settings and compared to the EARL standard 2 for F 18 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 18$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{F} . Results: High-quality PET images of Tb 149 {}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb} were obtained, even with a simulated total activity of 4.5 MBq 4.5\text{\,}\mathrm{MBq} . The 20 min 20\text{\,}\mathrm{min} and full activity scan yielded a mean recovery coefficient R ​ C mean RC_{\textit{mean}} of 0.55 0.55 , 0.69 0.69 , 0.73 0.73 , 0.76 0.76 , 0.79 0.79 , and 0.81 0.81 for the six phantom spheres. Despite the low count statistics, the coefficient of variation stays mostly below 15 % 15\,\% . Relative scatter correction combined with prompt γ \gamma modeling provided robust quantification. Conclusion: Tb 149 {}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb} can be imaged using a commercial LAFOV PET/CT with a quantitative accuracy comparable wo the EARL standard 2 for F 18 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 18$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{F} . These findings demonstrate the feasibility of PET-based treatment verification and dosimetry for targeted α \alpha therapy with Tb 149 {}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb} .

Introduction

{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 68$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}
- and
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 177$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Lu}
–labeled prostate-specific membrane antigen ligands and DOTA-conjugated peptides have achieved a landmark success as theranostic agents and have become a cornerstone modern nuclear medicine. However, chelation is different for these diagnostic and therapeutic radionuclides and the post-treatment imaging of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 177$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Lu}
relies on single-photon emission computed tomography (SPECT). Terbium has emerged as a promising rare earth with isotopes that are suitable for both diagnostic and therapeutic applications
[

,

. Some of the terbium isotopes have already found their way into clinics (see Refs.
[

,

The combination of

emission with conversion and Auger electrons of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 161$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
increases linear energy transfer (LET) compared to
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 177$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Lu}
, to the patients’ benefit. The use of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
could further extend this approach by adding
\alpha

[

,

,

,
15
,
23
,
37

). However,
\alpha
emitting radionuclides, like
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 223$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ra}
or
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 225$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ac}
, are notoriously difficult to image (see e.g. Refs.
[
30
,
35
,
38
]
) and rely on SPECT. SPECT is in fact the standard imaging modality for post-treatment verification and image-based dosimetry, but it suffers from limitations in sensitivity, spatial resolution and quantitative accuracy. In addition to the
\alpha
decay channel,
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
has a positron branching ratio (BR) of
7.12\pm 0.24\%
(see Tab.

{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
therefore offers the ability to perform post
-
therapy control and dosimetry with positron emission tomography (PET), which represents a potential game changer in nuclear medicine. In comparison with
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 90$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Y}
, the only therapeutic radionuclide imaged with PET in clinical routine,
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}

[
14

39
,
25

Unlike SPECT, PET provides superior sensitivity, resolution, and quantitative accuracy. This would allow clinicians to assess on- and off
-
target uptake and estimate patient
-
specific radiation doses with unprecedented reliability. By integrating PET into a post-therapy workflow, post
-
therapy imaging would evolve from a qualitative check into a powerful quantitative tool.

In this study, we image
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}

{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 18$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{F}
or
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 68$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}

Materials and methods

A tantalum target was irradiated with a
1.4\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}
proton beam and mass separated at the ISOLDE facility (project IS688) of CERN (Geneva, Switzerland). Mass 149 isobars and molecular pseudo-isobars were implanted into zinc-coated foils and the desired radionuclide chemically separated at the Paul Scherrer Institute (PSI, Villigen-PSI, Switzerland) to obtain a high-purity product (
>99.7\%
radionuclidic purity
41\text{\,}\min
after the end of separation), as described in Ref.
[
15
]
. A glass vial with about
186.3\text{\,}\mathrm{MBq}

{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
was sent to the Department of Nuclear Medicine at Inselspital (Bern, Switzerland) where the data acquisition was performed with a Biograph Vision Quadra (Siemens Healthineers, Knoxville TN, USA), henceforth referred to as Quadra. There is no preset for acquiring
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
data on Quadra. Hence, the scans were performed in the
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 44$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Sc}
setting, which requires a correction for the positron fraction as well as for the decay factor in the post-processing of the images. All image reconstructions were performed off-line using the prototype software e7-tools (Siemens Healthineers, Knoxville TN, USA).

The dose calibrator in the Department of Nuclear Medicine (VDC-405/VIK-202, Comecer, Italy) was cross-calibrated with the one at PSI. However, the rather complex decay chain of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
and the out-of-equilibrium activity distribution of the daughter radionuclides make an activity measurement of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
with a dose calibrator challenging. We therefore opted to measure
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
activities by sampling the phantom compartments and measure the samples with a high-purity germanium gamma spectrometer (HPGe).

We prepared capillaries and a NEMA IEC body phantom (Data Spectrum Corporation) for the assessment of the imaging capabilities of Quadra with
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
. Following the NEMA NU 2-2018 protocol, the capillaries were measured at the center and at one eighth of the axial field-of-view (FOV) and at
1\text{\,}\mathrm{cm}10\text{\,}\mathrm{cm}20\text{\,}\mathrm{cm}
in the transverse plane. The images of the capillaries were reconstructed with a filtered backprojection algorithm and
880\times 880
matrix size. The reconstruction includes time-of-flight (TOF) but no attenuation (AC), scatter nor point-spread-function (PSF) corrections were applied. The spatial resolution of Quadra with
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
is reported according to the NEMA NU 2-2018 standard in terms of full width at half maximum (FWHM) and full width at tenth maximum (FWTM).

Reconstructions of the NEMA phantom data were performed with an ordered subset expectation maximization (OSEM) algorithm including TOF, attenuation and scatter correction, as well as PSF recovery. The matrix size was
440\times 440
and a
2\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}
Gaussian FWHM post-reconstruction filter was applied. We compared different scan times, number of iterations and scatter correction models (absolute vs. relative, see Ref.
[
4
]
). In the case of absolute scatter correction, no p
\gamma
correction is computed. Since the activity of
\alpha
emitters, like
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 225$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ra}
or
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 225$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ac}
, that are administered to patients is typically limited to a few
\text{\,}\mathrm{MBq}
(though these radionuclides have a significantly longer half-life compared to
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
), we simulated a
20\text{\,}\min
-scan with a low activity by downsampling the list mode data to one tenth of the acquired raw data. This brings the total activity in the NEMA phantom to about
4.5\text{\,}\mathrm{MBq}
, which is in the order of administered activities of
\alpha
emitters used in clinical routine. The resulting reconstructions are marked as ‘
20\text{\,}\min
trim’.

The positron’s kinetic energy from the
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
decay is significantly higher than for
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 18$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{F}
, which leads to an increased PSF and should therefore be corrected. Using the FLUKA particle transport toolkit
[
6
,
1
,
5
,
13
]
with a modified MGDRAW routine, we simulated the location of positron annihilation from a point source of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
in water. The resulting kernel density is used to correct for the PR in the NEMA phantom images. The top left panel of Fig.
1
shows the PR in water of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
in comparison with other radionuclides used in clinical routine.
Instead of using custom PR correction projectors for the OSEM algorithm, as was done e.g. in Ref.
[
20
]
for
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 124$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{I}
, we applied a Richardson–Lucy deconvolution to the reconstructed images. This allowed us to correct the PSF for the PR without compromising the scanner dependent contribution to the PSF
[
31
]
and without the need of a custom implementation of the OSEM reconstruction algorithm. As is common in clinical reconstruction protocols, we applied
2\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}
Gauss filter after the deconvolution with the simulated PR kernel.

1
. In order to disentangle the limitations of the p
\gamma
correction and the CT distortions, we constructed a synthetic CT by registering the undistorted phantom from the central scan to the off-axis image (see bottom right panel of Fig
1
). Reconstruction with this synthetic AC CT are labeled as ‘mod. CT’.

is computed by placing a spherical VOI with
12\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}
diameter such that it yields the hightest mean activity concentration. The recovery coefficients for each sphere
i
are therefore defined as

RC_{max}^{(i)}\;\doteq\;\frac{A_{max}^{(i)}}{A_{s}}\;,\qquad RC_{mean}^{(i)}\;\doteq\;\frac{A_{mean}^{(i)}}{A_{s}}\;,\qquad RC_{peak}^{(i)}\;\doteq\;\frac{A_{peak}^{(i)}}{A_{s}}\;,

(1)

where
A_{s}
is the
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
activity concentration reported in Tab.
2
.

For the coefficient of variation (COV), we define three quadratic region-of-interest (ROI) in three distinct slices in the phantom’s background compartment according to Ref.
[
21
,
18
]
. For each ROI
\mathrm{COV}_{j}

j=1,...6
is computed by taking the standard deviation of all pixel values and dividing it by the mean
\bar{A}_{j}
. The COV is the average across all nine
\mathrm{COV}_{i}

\mathrm{COV}\;\doteq\;\frac{1}{9}\sum_{j=1}^{9}\mathrm{COV}_{j}\;=\;\frac{1}{9}\sum_{i=j}^{9}\frac{\sigma_{j}}{\bar{A}_{j}}\cdot 100\;.

(2)

This is the inverse of the signal-to-noise ratio.
\mathrm{COV}\leq 15\%
is compliant with the EARL limit for image noise.

Finally, we consider the average residual lung error by dividing the mean activity concentration in a cylindrical VOI (
120\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}
length,
30\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}
diameter) placed at the center of the lung insert of the phantom by the average activity concentration
A_{bkg}=1/9\sum\bar{A}_{j}
in the background ROIs.

4
Discussion

Firstly, we wish to emphasize the transformative potential of imaging an
\alpha
-emitting radionuclide for therapeutic applications with PET resulting in a high qualitative image quality shown in Fig.
2
. While imaging of
\alpha
emitters like
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 223$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ra}
or
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 225$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ac}
is hampered by the challenging decay radiation (low photon energies and/or BRs) and the limited quantitative abilities of SPECT (see e.g.
[
30
,
35
,
38
]
,
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
promises to bring all the advantages of PET to therapies
\alpha
. Our study shows that therapy control and dosimetry of small lesions for
\alpha
therapies are not wishful thinking anymore, as the images Fig.
2
illustrate.

Fig.
2
visualizes how even small lesions, i.e. the smallest sphere with
\emptyset\,$10\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}$
, can be imaged with a patient-friendly scan time of
20\text{\,}\min
and the trimmed data. This can be attributed to the high sensitivity of the Quadra. In comparison to the phantom study with
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 90$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Y}
, i.e. the only therapeutic radionuclide imaged with PET/CT in clinical routine
[
39
]
, it is immediately clear that even at the qualitative level the image quality of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
greatly exceeds
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 90$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Y}
(despite the longer scan time and higher activity). Obviously, this is due to the much smaller positron BR of
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 90$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Y}
. If we dare to compare with
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 177$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Lu}
imaging, even high-sensitivity ring shaped CZT SPECT/CT struggle to visualize the smallest sphere of the NEMA phantom (see Ref.
[
41
]
). It is therefore noteworthy that we can dare to compare the image quality and quantitative accuracy of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
in conjunction with a LAFOV PET/CT with
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 18$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{F}
on SAFOV PET/CT systems.

Fig.
3
and Tab.
4
demonstrate overall high quantitative accuracy. Despite being at the lower end of or slightly below the EARL standard 2 limits for
RC_{mean}
and
RC_{max}
, this is remarkable considering the case of the trimmed raw data. The HPGe activity measurement can be assumed to be very accurate
[
12
]
. Possibly, the larger uncertainty of a dose calibrator measurement would increase the overlap of the
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
recoveries with the EARL limits.
Obviously, the increased
\mathrm{COV}
of the trimmed raw data impacts mostly
RC_{max}
, while leaving only minor differences in
RC_{mean}
and
RC_{peak}
. As expected for a LAFOV PET/CT (see e.g. Refs.
[
36
,
33
,
32
]
), the recover coefficients for the smaller spheres show a relatively higher accuracy, i.e. the curves in Fig.
3
are somewhat flatter than the EARL limits. This highlights again the remarkable prospect of imaging small lesions while enabling accurate dosimetry in a future clinical implementation.

Comparing the top and center rows of Fig.
3
, it is clear that the impact of the post-reconstruction PR correction on the recovery coefficients is marginal. In clinical routine, there is typically no isotope-specific PR correction for
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 68$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}
, i.e. the
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 18$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{F}
PSF correction is applied.
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
has a similar PR in water as
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 68$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}
(see top left panel of Fig.
1
), hence one could also simply neglect the relatively large PR. However, Ref.
[
20
]
showed a significant improvement in quantitative accuracy for
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 124$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{I}
, which also as a similar PR as
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
and
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 68$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ga}
, with a dedicated PR correction. Likely, we are unable to see a similar improvement because our
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
-specific PR correction operates only in image-space, which represents a gross simplification compared to the custom projector used in Ref.
[
20
]
’s OSEM implementation. Despite having a PR well shorter than
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 82$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Rb}
(see e.g. Ref.
[
22
]
), it would be interesting to investigate how much a custom OSEM implementation with
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
-specific projectors would improve the recovery coefficients, in particular given the low statistics environment of our
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
measurements compared to the study of Ref.
[
20
]
. We defer exploration of this aspect to future work.

Given the fast convergence of the recovery coefficients and the rapidly increasing
\mathrm{COV}
with the number of iterations, we deem three iterations as the optimal value. This is merely one iteration less than the typical protocol used in clinical routine for
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 18$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{F}
and accounts for the limited count statistics in our data set.

Despite the quantitative accuracy discussed in the previous paragraphs, there are two main challenges with
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
. Quadra’s sensitivity cannot fully compensate the relatively low positron BR of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
, leading to limited count statistics. The noise quickly rises above the
15\,\%
threshold for
\mathrm{COV}
and the lung error even for few iterations, as seen in Fig.
4
. Nevertheless, our study shows that with a LAFOV PET/CT even activities as low as
4.5\text{\,}\mathrm{MBq}
of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
can be imaged with a reasonable quantitative accuracy. The low positron BR of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
inhibits a similar image quality as presented in Ref.
[
34
]
for a few
\text{\,}\mathrm{MBq}
of
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 18$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{F}
. If in a future clinical implementation of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
the dosage should be as low as for
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 223$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ra}
or
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 225$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ac}
, we suggest to extend the scan time beyond
20\text{\,}\min
.
We speculate that a SAFOV PET/CT would require significantly longer scan times, maybe even longer than for a
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 177$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Lu}
SPECT on a dual head camera, in order to reach an acceptable noise level.
In terms of clinically used radionuclides that face similar challenges for count statistics,
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
can probably be best compared to
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 124$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{I}
(see positron BR in Tab.
1
). However, considering the very long scan time on a SAFOV PET/CT system of Ref.
[
20
]
, it is apparent that the increased sensitivity of the LAFOV PET/CT can efficiently compensate for the slightly lower positron BR of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
.

The second challenge is the large number of p
\gamma
, in particular above the
511\text{\,}\mathrm{keV}
energy window. Considering the left column in Fig.
5
, the substantial number of observed events observed in the sinograms’ tail region, i.e. outside the NEMA phantom, demonstrates the high prevalence of random events generated by p
\gamma
. However, the linear combination of the (relative) scatter contribution and the smoothed randoms
[
11
]
(dashed line in Fig.
5
) is an adequate correction model for p
\gamma
and scatter events in the case of a centered phantom. In the case of an off-axis phantom, the limits of this correction model become evident. In particular in the bottom center panel of Fig.
5
, the dash-dotted line cannot account for full the emission outside of the phantom on the detector-proximal side. The rise in the emission sinogram towards the detector ring cannot be represented by uncorrelated events, i.e. smoothed randoms, nor scatter kinetmatics. Possibly, also increased pile-up of p
\gamma
below the
511\text{\,}\mathrm{keV}
energy window leads to an underestimation of the total correction of the emission sinogram.

The absolute scatter correction algorithm clearly fails to account for p
\gamma
contributions in the emission sinogram (see left and center panels in Fig.
5
). Neither the decreasing emission tails close to the phantom walls or the flat emission background closer to the detector rings are appropriately modeled. In terms of quantification, absolute scatter correction seems to be slightly better as a significantly lower number of events is subtracted from the emission sinogram. However, given the large number events outside the source region shown in the tails of Fig.
5
we strongly discourage using an absolute scatter correction for
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
.

Interestingly, the distorted AC CT does not significantly affect the quantitative accuracy (see bottom row of Fig.
3
). In the right column of Fig.
5
, the difference between the sinograms with distorted and the modified CT sinograms is marginal. Likely, this is due to the fact that the mask used for the fitting of the relative scatter and p
\gamma
contribution lies entirely on the detector-distal side of the phantom.

5
Conclusions

In this study we demonstrated for the first time that
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
can be imaged with a commercial LAFOV PET/CT. Despite the low positron BR and a high number prompt
\gamma
, it is possible to image
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
with a quantitative accuracy that is comparable to
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 18$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{F}
. Accurate dosimetry and post-treatment verification should be feasible even for small lesions in a future clinical translation of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
. Our findings strengthen the case of
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 149$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Tb}
as a promising radionuclide for targeted
\alpha
therapy and may open new avenues for its application in theranostics.
