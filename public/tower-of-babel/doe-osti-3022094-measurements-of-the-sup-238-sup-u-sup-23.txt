Summary of Results
A quasi-monoenergetic neutron beam was used to measure the neutron-induced fission cross-
section ratios for 238U(n,f)/235U(n,f) (Table 1) and 239Pu(n,f)/235U(n,f) (Table 2). These results are
plotted in comparison with data from the fissionTPC and ENDF/B-VIII.0 in Figs.1-3. For each cross-
section ratio, the total, systematic, and statistical uncertainties are listed, along with the total
beamtime required.

A dual-fission ionization chamber loaded with a pair of actinide-plated foils (one for each isotope in
the ratio) was irradiated with pulsed neutron beams at the Triangle Universities Nuclear Laboratory
10 MV FN Tandem Van de Graaff accelerator. The neutron beams were produced via the 3H(p,n)
reaction (En = 1.8 MeV) and the 2H(d,n) reaction (En = 4.6 MeV and 6.4 MeV).

The orientation of the fission chamber was flipped halfway through the measurement to cancel out
the geometry effects of the diverging neutron beam. For the data presented in this summary, the
upstream-to-downstream effects could be suitably reproduced by a combination of MCNP
simulations of the neutron beam production and GEANT4 modeling of the kinematic boost and
fission chamber efficiency. The atom number ratio for each pair of actinide foils was characterized
with a dedicated alpha spectrometer (Silano et al., 2024).

Since the uranium foils contain about 15 times the actinide mass as the 239Pu foils, we were more
easily able to perform measurements at additional neutron beam energies. Data was also taken at
En = 1.8 MeV for 239Pu(n,f)/235U(n,f), but the upstream-to-downstream effects could not be
reproduced by our neutron beam model. This discrepancy indicates there is some systematic
effect for this particular neutron production target (the 3H(p,n) reaction) that we are still working to
resolve.

We have also validated our technique by measuring a cross section ratio of 1 for 239Pu(n,f)/239Pu(n,f)
at 6.4 MeV, using the 2H(d,n) reaction (Table 3). This pair of 239Pu foils have an atom number ratio of
3.01  0.02.
