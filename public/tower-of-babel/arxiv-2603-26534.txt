Blowup analysis of a Camassa-Holm type equation with time-varying dissipation
Yonghui Zhou, Xiaowan Li, Shuguan Ji

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the local well-posedness, wave breaking, blow-up rate for a Camassa-Holm type equation with time-dependent weak dissipation. Firstly, we obtain the local well-posedness of solutions by using Kato’s theory. Secondly, by using energy estimates, characteristic methods, and comparison principles, we derive two blowup criteria involving both pointwise gradient conditions and mixed amplitude-gradient conditions, and prove the blowup rate is universally − 2 -2 . Our results extend wave breaking analysis to physically relevant variable dissipation regimes.

Introduction

An intriguing phenomenon in oceanography is the occurrence of waves whose wavelengths significantly exceed the water depth. To model the propagation of such long waves in shallow water, various mathematical frameworks have been developed. Among the most prominent are the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation and the Camassa–Holm (CH) equation
Camassa1993

m_{t}+2mu_{x}+m_{x}u=0,

(1.1)

where
u(t,x)
represents the free surface elevation. The CH equation is a nonlinear dispersive wave equation describing unidirectional irrotational shallow water waves over a flat bed.

The CH equation possesses several remarkable properties. First, it admits peakon solutions–solitary waves with a peaked crest–which have been proven to be stable
Constantin2000
. Second, it exhibits wave breaking phenomena
Constantin1998
; that is, smooth solutions can develop singularities in finite time in the form of wave breaking, where the solution remains bounded while its spatial derivative becomes unbounded. Furthermore, the CH equation is an infinite-dimensional integrable Hamiltonian system
Fokas1981
and can be interpreted as a re-expression of geodesic flow on the diffeomorphism group
Constantin2000
.

Over the past three decades, the CH equation and its generalizations have been extensively studied regarding local well-posedness, global existence of strong solutions, persistence properties, wave breaking mechanisms, peakon dynamics, and global conservative weak solutions (see, e.g.,
Constantin19981
,
Constantin1998
,
Constantin2000
,
Bressan2007
,
Brandolese2012
,
Brandolese20141
,
Brandolese20142
,
Constantin20004
,
Constantin2009
,
Mustafa20072
,
Freire1
,

,

,

,
Ji2022
,

,
Coclite20052
,
Zhou2022
,
Zhou2024
,
Zhou2026
and references therein).

In realistic coastal and estuarine environments, however, dissipative effects–such as bottom friction, viscous drag, or permeable boundaries—often play a significant role and cannot be neglected. To incorporate such energy loss, dissipative mechanisms have been introduced into these models. Early studies primarily focused on constant-coefficient dissipation for simplicity. For instance, Ott and Sudan
Ott1970
examined the modification of the KdV equation due to dissipation and its effect on solitary waves. Ghidaglia
Ghidaglia1988
investigated the long-time behavior of solutions to a weakly dissipative KdV equation as a finite-dimensional dynamical system. In 2009, Wu and Yin
Wu2009
analyzed global existence and wave breaking for the weakly dissipative Camassa–Holm equation

u_{t}-u_{txx}+3uu_{x}+\lambda(u-u_{xx})=uu_{xxx}+2u_{x}u_{xx},

(1.2)

where
\lambda\geq 0
is a dissipative parameter. Freire et al.
Freire1
studied local well-posedness and wave breaking for a weakly dissipative Camassa-Holm type equation with quadratic and cubic nonlinearities.

Recently, growing attention has been directed toward models with time-dependent dissipative coefficients, as they more accurately capture the effects of dynamically varying environmental factors-such as tidal oscillations, fluctuating wind stress, and seasonal hydrological changes. Such models not only enhance the physical realism of shallow water wave descriptions but also provide a more flexible mathematical framework for analyzing how variable external forcing influences wave stability, propagation, and breaking phenomena.
Inspired by these advancements, this work investigates the following shallow water wave equation incorporating a time-varying dissipation term

u_{t}-u_{txx}+3u^{2}u_{x}+\lambda(t)(u-u_{xx})=uu_{xxx}+2u_{x}u_{xx},

(1.3)

where
\lambda(t)
is a continuous function.

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{-\infty}^{\xi(t)}u(t,y)u_{x}(t,y)dy-\int_{\xi(t)}^{+\infty}u(t,y)u_{x}(t,y)dy\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\xi(t)}(u^{2}(t,y)+u_{x}^{2}(t,y))dy+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\xi(t)}^{+\infty}(u^{2}(t,y)+u_{x}^{2}(t,y))dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{1}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{1}^{2},

thus we get
|u(t,x)|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{1}.
Then we have

|h(u)|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\|u_{0}\|^{3}_{1}+\frac{3}{4}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{1}\ \ \text{and}\ \ |P\ast h(u)|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\|u_{0}\|^{3}_{1}+\frac{3}{4}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{1}.

Therefore, (

\displaystyle m^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq-\lambda(t)m(t)-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}(t)+K

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(m+\lambda(t)+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}(t)+2K})(m+\lambda(t)-\sqrt{\lambda^{2}(t)+2K}),\ a.e.\ on\ (0,T),

(3.15)

where

By (

m(0)<-\delta-\sqrt{\delta^{2}+2K},

thereby
m^{\prime}(0)<0

m(t)
with respect to
t

, there is
m^{\prime}(t)<0
. Therefore

m(t)<-\delta-\sqrt{\delta^{2}+2K}.

For
m(t)<0

\displaystyle m^{\prime}(t)\leq-\delta m(t)-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}(t)+K,\ \ a.e.\ on\ (0,T).

(3.16)

Let

satisfy

\displaystyle\omega^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=-\delta\omega(t)-\frac{1}{2}\omega^{2}(t)+K

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(\omega+\delta+\sqrt{\delta^{2}+2K})(\omega+\delta-\sqrt{\delta^{2}+2K}),\ a.e.\ on\ (0,T),

(3.17)

By the comparison principle, we have
m(t)\leq\omega(t)
. Under the given initial condition,
\omega(t)

must also blow up in finite time.

For the comparison equation (

\omega(0)<-\delta-\sqrt{\delta^{2}+2K},

thereby
\omega^{\prime}(0)<0

\omega(t)

Then, by solving the above inequality, we obtain

\frac{\omega(0)+\delta+\sqrt{\delta^{2}+2K}}{\omega(0)+\delta-\sqrt{\delta^{2}+2K}}e^{\sqrt{\delta^{2}+2K}t}-1\leq\frac{2\sqrt{\delta^{2}+2K}}{\omega(t)+\delta-\sqrt{\delta^{2}+2K}}\leq 0.

Due to

Theorem 3.4
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
3.3
. If
T_{1}^{\ast}
is finite, then

\liminf_{t\rightarrow T_{1}^{\ast}}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u_{x}(t,x)(T_{1}^{\ast}-t)=-2.

Proof.

The argument of proof is divided into six steps.

Step 1. Key differential inequality.

From (
3.14
), we obtain that
m(t)
satisfies

\displaystyle m^{\prime}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\big(m(t)+\lambda(t)\big)^{2}=H(t),

(3.18)

where

H(t)=u^{2}-P\ast\left(u^{2}+\frac{u_{x}^{2}}{2}\right)-P\ast h(u)+h(u)+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}(t).

It is easy check that
H(t)
is uniformly bounded:
|H(t)|\leq K+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}:=K_{1}
for all
t\in[0,T_{1}^{*})
.

Step 2. Introduction of auxiliary variables.

Let

z(t)=m(t)+\lambda(t).

Then
z(t)\to-\infty
as
t\to T_{1}^{*}
. By the continuity of
\lambda(t)
, the limit
\bar{\lambda}=\lim_{t\to T_{1}^{*}}\lambda(t)
exists. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

|\lambda(t)-\bar{\lambda}|<\varepsilon,\text{ for }T_{1}^{*}-t<\delta.

Define
\theta(t)=m(t)+\bar{\lambda}
. Then
\theta(t)\to-\infty
, and
z(t)=\theta(t)+(\lambda(t)-\bar{\lambda})
.

Step 3. Estimating the difference
z^{2}(t)-\theta^{2}(t)
.

We compute

|z^{2}(t)-\theta^{2}(t)|=|2\theta(t)(\lambda(t)-\bar{\lambda})+(\lambda(t)-\bar{\lambda})^{2}|\leq 2\varepsilon|\theta(t)|+\varepsilon^{2}.

(3.19)

Step 4. Reformulating the equation for
\theta(t)
.

From (
3.18
) and
m^{\prime}(t)=\theta^{\prime}(t)
, we have

\theta^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}z^{2}(t)+H(t).

Using (
3.19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}(t)+\varepsilon|\theta(t)|+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}+K_{1},

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}(t)-\varepsilon|\theta(t)|-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}-K_{1}.

(3.20)

Since
\theta(t)<0
, let
\phi(t)=-\theta(t)>0
. Then
\phi(t)\to+\infty
and (
3.20
) becomes

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2}(t)-\varepsilon\phi(t)-K_{2},

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2}(t)+\varepsilon\phi(t)+K_{2},

(3.21)

where
K_{2}=K_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}
.

Step 5. Precise blow-up rate for
\phi(t)
.

Dividing (
3.21
) by
\phi^{2}(t)
gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{\phi(t)}-\frac{K_{2}}{\phi^{2}(t)}\leq\frac{\phi^{\prime}(t)}{\phi^{2}(t)}\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\phi(t)}+\frac{K_{2}}{\phi^{2}(t)}.

(3.22)

Since
\phi(t)\to+\infty
, for any
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
, there exists
t_{2}\in[t_{1},T_{1}^{*})
such that for
t\in[t_{2},T_{1}^{*})
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq\frac{\phi^{\prime}(t)}{\phi^{2}(t)}\leq\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon^{\prime}.

(3.23)

Note that
\frac{\phi^{\prime}(t)}{\phi^{2}(t)}=-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{\phi(t)}\right)
. Integrating (
3.23
) with respect to
t
from
t
to
T_{1}^{*}
yields

\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}\right)(T_{1}^{*}-t)\leq\frac{1}{\phi(t)}\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon^{\prime}\right)(T_{1}^{*}-t).

Hence,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to T_{1}^{*}}\phi(t)(T_{1}^{*}-t)=2.

(3.24)

Step 6. Returning to
m(t)
.

Since
\phi(t)=-m(t)-\bar{\lambda}
, we have

\displaystyle-m(t)-\bar{\lambda}\sim\frac{2}{T_{1}^{*}-t},

which implies

\displaystyle m(t)(T_{1}^{*}-t)\sim-2-\bar{\lambda}(T_{1}^{*}-t)\to-2.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to T_{1}^{*}}m(t)(T_{1}^{*}-t)=-2,

i.e.,

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to T_{1}^{*}}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u_{x}(t)\cdot(T_{1}^{*}-t)=-2.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
u_{0}(x)\in H^{s}\ (s>\frac{3}{2})

u(t,q(t,x))

\displaystyle u^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle P_{+}\ast\left(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}+h(u)\right)-P_{-}\ast\left(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}+h(u)\right)-\lambda(t)u,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}-P_{+}\ast\left(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}+h(u)\right)-P_{-}\ast\left(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}+h(u)\right)+u^{2}

\displaystyle+h(u)-\lambda(t)u_{x}.

Define the two convolution operators

P_{+}\ast f(x)=\frac{1}{2}e^{-x}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{y}f(y)dy,\ \ P_{-}\ast f(x)=\frac{1}{2}e^{x}\int^{+\infty}_{x}e^{-y}f(y)dy,

From (

\displaystyle u^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle u_{t}+uu_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{x}P\ast\left(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}+h(u)\right)-\lambda(t)u

\displaystyle P_{+}\ast\left(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}+h(u)\right)-P_{-}\ast\left(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}+h(u)\right)-\lambda(t)u,

\displaystyle u_{x}^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}-P\ast\left(u^{2}+\frac{u_{x}^{2}}{2}+h(u)\right)+u^{2}+h(u)-\lambda(t)u_{x}

\displaystyle+h(u)-\lambda(t)u_{x}.

Theorem 3.5
.

\lambda(t)\leq\delta

(3.26)

Proof.

Let
T
be the maximal existence time of the unique solution
u(t,x)
guaranteed by Theorem

.

In order to obtain the blowup result, we define two

differentiable functions
\Phi(t)
and
\Psi(t)

\Phi(t)=(u-u_{x})(t,q(t,x_{1})),\ \ \ \Psi(t)=(u+u_{x})(t,q(t,x_{1})).

Then we have

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle u^{\prime}(t)-u^{\prime}_{x}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}+2P_{+}\ast\left(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}+h(u)\right)-u^{2}-h(u)-\lambda(t)(u-u_{x}).

(3.27)

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle u^{\prime}(t)+u^{\prime}_{x}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}-2P_{-}\ast\left(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}+h(u)\right)+u^{2}+h(u)-\lambda(t)(u+u_{x}).

(3.28)

Note that

\displaystyle P_{+}\ast\left(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}e^{-x}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{\xi}\left(u^{2}+u_{\xi}^{2}\right)d\xi\geq e^{-x}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{\xi}uu_{\xi}d\xi=\frac{1}{2}u^{2}-P_{+}\ast u^{2},

P_{+}\ast\left(2u^{2}+u_{x}^{2}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}u^{2},\ \ \text{i.e.,}\ \ P_{+}\ast\left(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}\right)\geq\frac{1}{4}u^{2}.

Similarly,

P_{-}\ast\left(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}\right)\geq\frac{1}{4}u^{2},

|h(u)|,\ \ |P_{+}\ast h(u)|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\|u_{0}\|_{1}^{3}+\frac{3}{4}\|u_{0}\|_{1}^{2}.

Therefore, (

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(t)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+2P_{+}\ast h(u)-h(u)-\lambda(t)(u-u_{x})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Phi\Psi-\lambda(t)\Phi-K

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Phi(\Psi+2\lambda(t))-K,

(3.29)

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime}(t)\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}-2P_{-}\ast h(u)+h(u)-\lambda(t)\Psi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Phi\Psi-\lambda(t)\Psi+K

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Psi(\Phi+2\lambda(t))+K.

(3.30)

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\Phi(\Psi+2\delta)-K,\ \ \bar{\Psi}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\Psi(\Phi+2\delta)+K,

\bar{\Phi}(0)>\delta+\sqrt{\delta^{2}+2K}>0,\ \ \bar{\Phi}^{\prime}(0)>0,

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)=

Remark 3.1
.

Theorem
3.3
is a pure slope criterion, while Theorem
3.5
is a mixed criterion that also accounts for the initial amplitude. The latter is generally more restrictive but provides additional geometric and quantitative information about the blowup. Both results illustrate how sufficiently large negative slope can lead to wave breaking, even in the presence of weak dissipation.

Remark 3.2
.

Theorems

While both results yield the same asymptotic rate
