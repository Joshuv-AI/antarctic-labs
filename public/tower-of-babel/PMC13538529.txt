Memristive nano-neuromorphic spiking neural network with self-adaptive continual and predictive learning for edge gesture recognition
Authors: Raju Vidap, Ranjith Kumar Nadialli, Vinod Kumar Teriveedhi, K Suresh Babu, Suresh K Pulluru, Aele Manohar, Suresh Thondapu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGesture recognition refers to a surface electromyography based method of gesture recognition becoming an essential element of edge computing systems to aid human-machine interface, wearable interfaces, and assistive technology systems. The latest advancements in deep learning have shown encouraging accuracy and are still constrained by frame-based processing, offline learning, excessive energy usage, and the incapacity to adjust to the constantly changing patterns of gestures. These limitations limit the use in edge devices with limited resources. To address them, SCaP-SNN (Self-adaptive Continuous and Predictive Spiking Neural Network) is presented as a memristive nano-neuromorphic model of adaptive and power efficient gesture recognition in the edge. Self-adaptive learning is described as dynamically modulating synaptic updates according to the variance of the signal, continual learning is used to retain previous knowledge about gestures while receiving incremental updates, and predictive learning can predict future gestures for a short period of time based on temporal spike dependence. The main innovation of this study is the design of a unified memristive spiking neural network incorporating self-adaptive learning, continual learning, and predictive intelligence for efficient gesture recognition at the edge of the network. The presented approach combines the spiking neural networks with memristive synapses modeling in order to support event-based computation, self-adaptive plasticity, lifelong learning without disastrous forgetting, and predictive inference. The online synaptic adaptation and consolidation processes allow to stabilize learning of new gestures without loss of the earlier learnt information The experimental results show a classification accuracy of 96.8%, F1-score of 0.965, low forgetting rate of 2.1% and a prediction accuracy of 91.4%. Power consumption analysis shows that the mean power consumption is 28 microjoules per inference, which is suitable in being used in low-power edges.Keywords: Edge Intelligence, Continual Learning, Surface Electromyography, Spiking Neural Networks, Predictive LearningSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Neuroscience

Introduction

The interaction between human and machine has been developing at a very high rate due to the rising need of having user-friendly, real-time gesture recognition system in wearable electronics, prosthetics, and assistive technologies1,2. Surface electromyography (sEMG) as one of the sensing modalities has become a promising bio-signal used to decode fine-grained gestures of the hands because it is directly related to muscle activity3. The traditional gesture-recognition pipelines are mostly based on the convolutional and recurrent neural networks, which are highly accurate, but require offline training, massive labelled data as well as cloud computing4,5. These are resources-intensive in nature, which results in high-energy consumption and latency, and thus cannot be used in edge devices6,7. In recent developments in neuromorphic computing, spiking neural networks (SNNs) have been proposed as a biologically plausible alternative providing an event-driven process of information consumption that is much better energy efficient8. Simultaneously, memristive nano-devices have been considered synaptic elements because of their non-volatility, analog storage of weight, and scalability9. The neuromorphic gesture-recognition systems have not been able to cope with the changing user-specific muscle patterns, even though most of these systems are not dynamic after training10. The lack of interrelation here between the biological adaptability and the artificial learning processes underscores the fact that intelligent edge systems that are able to learn continuously and independently are necessary11.

Recent studies have tried to bring on-chip learning and plasticity to the neuromorphic systems, but these solutions tend to deal with single issues but not offer a learning system11. Current models of SNNs generally use spike-timing-dependent plasticity, or supervised spike-based learning, which fail to be stable when subjected to non-stationary streams of data12. Also, the issue of the continuous learning in such systems is scarcely studied past the narrow scope of a sequence of tasks, which causes disastrous forgetting in case of adding new gestures13. The predictive learning, which allows one to predict how things will happen in the future, is mostly not present in the edge neuromorphic gesture-recognition research14. Hardwarewise, numerous studies consider perfect synaptic behavior dynamics without considering the natural nonlinearity and variability of memristive devices15. To overcome these constraints, this study suggests a Self-adaptive Continual and Predictive Spiking Neural Network (SCaP-SNN) which more closely couples the algorithmic learning policies with the memristive nano-neuromorphic constraints. In contrast to the current methods, SCaP-SNN allows the online adaptation, retention of knowledge, and short-horizon prediction to take place at the edge itself. Combining self-adaptive plasticity, the continuous learning and predictive intelligence in one memristive SNN architecture, the research study is a step toward the present state of the art in low-power, intelligent gesture-recognition systems.

Problem statement

Even though remarkable advances have been made in gesture recognition and neuromorphic computing, there are still several inherent limitations to the current research16,17. To begin with, the majority of gesture-recognition models are based on the paradigms of static training where data distributions are fixed and therefore are not applicable in the real-world, where sEMG signals change with time because of muscle fatigue, electrode motion and user adaptation18,19. Second, the existing SNN-based techniques do not have sturdy continual learning algorithms, and hence the forgetting of a new gesture or session is devastating as new gestures or sessions are loaded in the same order20. Third, predictive learning, which is important in assistive and interactive systems to enable anticipatory control is not well studied in edge neuromorphic architectures21. Moreover, the hardware-algorithm mismatch is overlooked in many other previous works since the ideal synaptic performance is assumed, which restricts the practical attention of the memristive implementations. Most of the existing solutions tend to focus on adaptability, constant learning and energy efficiency as independent goals resulting in fractured designs, which cannot be quickly scaled to edge deployment. As a result, a definite lack of an integrated neuromorphic system with self-adaptation, lifelong learning, and temporal prediction with realistic hardware constraints is obvious. It is necessary to address these gaps so that can create intelligent, low-power, and resilient gesture-recognition systems that can be applied to the actual edges.

Self-adaptive memristive spiking learning framework

The suggested methodology presents SCaP-SNN as a memristive nano-neuromorphic spiking neural network intended to be used in edge-based gesture recognition tasks through adaptation. To guarantee low-latency compatibility of edges, multichannel sEMG signals are first recorded and normalized by means of fast filtering. An event-based temporal encoding scheme divides the preprocessed signals into temporal windows and codes them into spike trains at a minimum energy usage. A memristive SNN having leaky integrate-and-fire neurons with synaptic weights represented by memristive conductance states is then fed with these spike trains. The self-adaptive plasticity mechanism that controls learning is a dynamical mechanism of modulating the synaptic updates depending on neural activity and stability constraints. To facilitate lifelong learning a selective consolidation of valuable synapses during contiguous learning, avoiding disastrous forgetting during incremental gesture acquisition, occurs. There is also a predictive learning module that utilizes the temporal spike correlations to predict future pattern of gestures. All the learning processes are conducted online and this allows low-power and hardware-conscious autonomous intelligence at the edge.

Data collection

The proposed SCaP-SNN framework is tested on the publicly available surface electromyography dataset, the FORS-EMG dataset available on the Kaggle31. The data set is a collection of multichannel sEMG recordings of various subjects that are performing a set of varied hand gestures under controlled experimental conditions. It includes thousands of segmented gesture samples recorded on repeated tries and allows to give a strong analysis of inter-subject and intra-subject variability. The dataset format is scalable to larger gesture vocabularies, and allows the assessment of inter-subject adaptability and the long-term learning performance under different signal conditions. These signals are also encoded into a high-tempor resolution which is very appropriate in spike-based encoding as well as temporal learning. It has the benefit of being evaluated on online, continuous, and predictive learning scenarios, which are necessary when it comes to edge neuromorphic systems. The proposed model takes as its input multichannel sEMG signal , where C is the number of EMG channels and T is the length of the signal in time. The samples are segmented time-series data, and further transformed into the spike-ready inputs of the spiking neural architecture. The dataset is split in 70% training set and 30% testing set to make sure that the performance of the model and analysis of the generalization can be evaluated and analyzed fairly as the classes of gestures are represented equally.

Data preprocessing

Spike-based neuromorphic system learning requires proper preprocessing of surface electromyography (sEMG) signals. Raw sEMG signals are by nature not only rough but also non-stationary and electrode position dependent, prone to muscle fatigue, and motion artifact. In order to guarantee sound learning in the proposed SCaP-SNN architecture, a multi-stage preprocessing pipeline is utilized in order to improve signal quality, normalize inter-session variability and preserve temporal patterns of muscle activation. All of the preprocessing stages are intended to be computationally lean and compatible with edge deployment and spike encoding and memristive synaptic learning. This pipeline is systematic, enhances stability in classification, increases the rate of convergence and it facilitates continuous and predictive learning that is viable under realistic operating conditions.

Bandpass filtering

The bandpass filter is used to block motion artifacts in low frequency, and electrical noise in high frequency, that is part of sEMG recordings. The signals of muscle activity are usually in a certain frequency band and separating this area improves the information of gestures. The digital bandpass filter is used to retain the components that are physiologically relevant, and the phase distortion is as small as possible. This measure enhances the quality of the signals and standardizes the encoding of spikes on the downstream level. The filtering functionality is small in size and computationally inexpensive, which makes it applicable to edge deployment at real-time. Signal rectification

Rectification transforms the bipolar sEMG signal into unipolar forms to allow uniform interpretation of the intensity of muscle activation. As positive and negative signal amplitudes are both associated with contraction of muscles, rectification allows all the activity to add value to signal magnitude. Full-wave rectification is more desirable because it does not lose the full signal energy. In this change, temporal envelope detecting is improved and spike generating thresholds are stabilized. Self-adaptive continual predictive memristive spiking framework

Self-adaptive learning controls synaptic updates depending on the signal variance. Continual learning retains the previous knowledge gained from the model using importance-based consolidation. Predictive learning is able to forecast future gestures within a short period of time based on temporal spikes. The suggested methodology makes use of a memristive spiking neural model that integrates adaptive spike coding, temporal membrane integration, self-regulated synaptic plasticity, and predictive decoding to obtain powerful sEMG gesture recognition. Adaptive thresholds are used to convert normalized biosignals into sparse spike trains without loss of temporal fidelity and with reduced redundant activity. Conductance-based computation and non-volatile storage of memory is possible with memristive synapses. Learning processes continuously maintain the already gained knowledge, whereas predictive decoding of temporal changes foresees the change of gesture to lower latencies. The energy-efficient event-driven inference, cross-subject domain adaptation, neural stability control, and uncertainty-sensitive decision modeling, are all aspects that guarantee reliable, adaptive and low-power edge deployment to dynamic real-world biosignal conditions. An idealized conductance-based model of membrane neuromorphic learning that models non-volatility, analog switching, and incremental updates is implemented as memristive synapses, allowing neuromorphic learning behavior to be controlled under simulation conditions. The SCaP-SNN is proposed to have four key components: (i) input encoding layer converting normalized sEMG signals to spike trains, (ii) memristive spiking neural network comprising of leaky integrate-and-fire neurons, (iii) self-adaptive and continual learning module with stability factor and importance factor , and (iv) predictive The implemented architecture and learning flow is well defined in this structured design. The architecture aims at generalization across the subjects with adaptive learning and alignment of domain and is robust to circumstances other than those imposed by the controlled datasets and learn-free enlargement of classes of gestures, making gesture classes scalable and flexible in real-world applications. Previous studies have focused mostly on developing memristive spiking neural networks mainly for static classification or offline learning. In contrast, the SCaP-SNN proposed in this study incorporates four different strategies into one unified model, including memristive synaptic computation for efficient hardware implementation, spike temporal dynamics for efficient event-driven processing, continual learning to solve catastrophic forgetting problems, and predictive coding to conduct short-term gesture predictions.

Memristive synaptic computation mechanism design

The weights of the synapses are achieved with the implementation of memristive conductance states, which allow storage and parallel calculation in analog. Current is output by each presynaptic spike in proportion to conductance, meaning that it is possible to have continuous representation of weights instead of discrete updates. The property improves resolution of pattern to fine-grained gesture differences. Learned knowledge can be retained without energy consumption, a requirement that is necessary in wearable edge devices; this is guaranteed by non-volatile conductance. Training includes the variability of hardware, which is resilient to device flaws. The memristor model is based on the assumption of limited linear change in conductance with slow switching behavior, modeling realistic device behavior without taking much account of the behavior of actual devices, but providing stable and repeatable simulation of synaptic updates. Memristive synapses enable computation and memory to coexist on the same physical element, thus reducing latency and enhancing efficiency, and the architecture can be used in real-time neuromorphic inference with very stringent power requirements. Self- adaptive synaptic plasticity regulation

The adaptive plasticity rule controls learning, in which weight changes are modified in response to the consistency of neural activity. When there are more changes in signals, the magnitude of update is minimized in order to avoid unstable learning. This rule makes certain that training is constant in the case of non-stationary biosignal properties. This mechanism resembles the biological homeostasis which helps to keep the balance of firing in the neurons. Consequently, the network is able to change to accommodate new gesture patterns without causing a destabilization of the existing ones. Predictive temporal gesture decoding strategy

The predictive block makes short-term future temporal spike sequence prediction and forecasting of future gestures through the use of spike time correlation between spike timing at present (t) and the previous time (t − 1). The final stage forecasts future gestures based on spatio-temporal spikes. The SNN makes a prediction explicitly of future gestures from temporal spike dependencies. Predictive decoding enhances responsiveness of the assistive and interactive devices. The population of neurons in the output integrates the evidence of the spikes and gives out an estimate of a gesture in a probabilistic way. Anticipation decreases latency and increases response time to control. Temporal prediction further averages classification outputs as well to avoid sudden changes in outputs due to the noise of the transient signal. Result and discussion

The Experimental findings confirm the usefulness of the suggested SCaP-SNN framework in the efficient, adaptive, and energy-efficient sEMG-based gesture recognition at the edge. The resulting performance indicates the capability of the spike-based temporal encoding to maintain discriminative patterns of muscle activation with the outcome of reduction in the unnecessary processing. The experiments of continual learning provide the evidence of the sustainable knowledge retention in the sequential acquisition of gestures, which means effective catastrophic forgetting mitigation. Predictive decoding also increases responsiveness through predicting gesture changes, which increases temporal consistency. The neuromorphic design is more flexible and efficient than current frame-based designs, making it an ideal option in the implementation of long-term implementation in complex real-world human-machine interaction systems.

Experimental outcome

The Experimental analysis performed on the FORS-EMG data shows that the suggested SCaP-SNN model can be used to provide strong results in gesture recognition in the case of real-world edge limitations. Balanced precision–recall behaviour and high classification effectiveness prove the existence of reliable discrimination in several classes of gestures. Continual learning capability is demonstrated via a task-incremental experiment where new gesture classes are incrementally introduced to the model, while performance retention is tested after each increment. The forgetting rate in incremental learning is low and this confirms self-adaptive synaptic regulation in maintaining the pattern that has been learned before. The results of predictive inference show better continuity in time and less decision latency. Also, the low energy per-inference consumption indicates the potential of event-driven computation and memristive synaptic modeling, and this fact confirms the appropriateness of the framework to a low-powered, continuous, edge-based biosignal processing application.

Discussion

The Experimental outcomes prove that the suggested SCaP-SNN is capable of achieving the goals of adaptive, energy-efficient, and continuous gesture recognition on the edge. The recognition performance is high, which demonstrates that spike-based temporal encoding retains discriminative sEMG properties and minimizes the unnecessary computation. These sequential stages of learning with a low forgetting rate proves that self-adaptive syngaptic consolidation mechanism is effective in alleviating catastrophic forgetting, which is one of the limitations of the traditional deep learning methods. The findings substantiate the hypothesis that the suggested continuing learning approach is an effective means to avoid catastrophic forgetting and the facilitation of incremental acquisition of knowledge in dynamical gesture settings. The results indicate the effectiveness of continuous learning through the retention of stable performance through successive updates to the tasks, preventing the occurrence of catastrophic forgetting in non-stationary gesture streams. The findings of predictive learning also indicate that temporal spike dynamics makes early prediction of gesture transition, which is especially useful to proactive human-machine interaction. The energy analysis indicates that the event-based processing and memristive synaptic modeling is a cost-effective way to inference, which supports its appropriateness to long-term deployment of edges. For energy comparison purposes, event-based computing together with the use of memristive synapses be used for the estimation. The proposed framework has a high level of adaptability and learning stability when compared to existing frame-based CNN, RNN, and transformer models when presented with realistic edge constraints. These results are consistent with other recent literature on neuromorphic computing, which puts more emphasis on event-based learning and hardware-aware design, but extends an earlier literature that has independently considered continual learning and predictive learning in one spiking architecture. An implicit ablation case shows that adding gestures without adaptive learning results in an increase in forgetting, but the framework proposed does not affect performance, which confirms the defense of catastrophic forgetting. Though it is evaluated using the FORS-EMG dataset, its variability on multi-subjects allows generalization and future research involve cross-dataset validation in different real world settings. The study involves testing the model without predictive learning, without continual learning, without self-adaptation mechanisms, as well as threshold changes, to prove the role played by each individual factor. The proposed framework is inherently scalable, as adaptive and continual learning mechanisms enable efficient integration of new gestures and support robust multi-user adaptation over extended operational periods.

Conclusion and future work

This study has tackled the issue of attaining adaptive and low-power long-term gesture recognition of edge intelligence applications on the surface electromyography signal. The current methods are mostly frame-based deep learning models which are not online adaptable and are also very energy-consuming. In order to address these shortcomings, a memristive nano-neuromorphic model, called SCaP-SNN, was proposed that incorporates self-adaptive, continual, and predictive learning into a spiking neural network design. The suggested solution allows processing events, stable online learning, and future gesture anticipation without disastrous forgetting. Experimental analysis on a realistic sEMG dataset showed that the framework can be highly recognized and the energy consumption and learning stability can be maintained under edge constraint. The findings confirm that incorporating memristive synaptic dynamics along with spike-based computation, offers a promise of pathway through which intelligent and lifelong edge gesture recognition can be achieved. In general, this work provides a scalable and practical neuromorphic solution that advances the status of edge-based human-machines interaction by considering in one aspect adaptability, prediction, and power efficiency. It can be noted that the novelty of the approach involves the incorporation of all three aspects: memristive synaptic hardware constraints, stability during continual learning, and predictive intelligence based on spikes into a single architecture for SNNs, unlike other models where these factors do not co-exist.

Future studies will proceed with the expansion of the framework to larger and more heterogeneous sEMG samples to further test the applicability of the findings to other users, other sessions and other complexities of gestures. Multi-modal sensory input (i.e. inertial signal, or touch) can also make it more robust in dynamic conditions. Physical neuromorphic or memristive validation Hardware-level validation Hardware-level validation on physical systems should be an important step toward real-world application. Also the investigations on alternative spike encoding approaches and adaptive neuron modeling may provide an additional enhancement of predictive accuracy and learning efficiency. The framework can also be furthered to the associated applications of the prosthetic control, rehabilitation monitoring, and collaborative robotics. These guidelines will create a stronger practical effect of the suggested methodology and will contribute to the further development of neuromorphic continual learning studies. Simulation-based current results are based on an idealized memristive model, owing to the lack of large-scale hardware; future work aims at validation on physical memristive arrays and in-memory computing platforms to deploy it in the real world. This study relies on an ideal memristive synapse and does not consider issues like device variability, limited endurance, and susceptibility to noise, all of which continue to pose challenges for any realistic hardware realization of the system. While the system has shown positive outcomes, there still exist challenges like variability in the devices, endurance limits, and noise.
