Dirichlet problem for Lane–Emden type equations with several sublinear terms

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

We study the Dirichlet problem for
the Lane–Emden type equation

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}u=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}u^{q_{i}}+\sigma_{0},\quad u\geq 0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\liminf\limits_{x\to y}u(x)=f(y),&y\in\partial^{\infty}\Omega,\end{cases}

involving several sublinear growth terms where

0<q_{i}<1
(
i=1,\dots,m
),
and the coefficients
\sigma_{i}
(
i=0,1,\dots,m
)
are nonnegative locally integrable functions,
or more generally
nonnegative locally finite Borel measures on
a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 2)
.
The set of all such measures will be denoted by
\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
.

The operator

\mathcal{L}u=-\operatorname{div}(\mathcal{A}\nabla u)

with
\mathcal{A}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

being a real
n\times n
symmetric matrix-valued measurable function on
\Omega

is assumed to satisfy
the uniform ellipticity condition, that is,
there exists a positive constant
\beta\geq 1
such that

(1.2)

\beta^{-1}|\xi|^{2}\leq\mathcal{A}(x)\xi\cdot\xi\leq\beta|\xi|^{2},

for almost every
x\in\Omega
and for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
When
\mathcal{A}(x)=I_{n}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix,

\mathcal{L}=-\Delta
the classical Laplace operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The domain
\Omega

is assumed to be
\mathcal{A}
-regular (or simply, regular)
for the Dirichlet problem (cf.
[
14
]
)
and possess a positive Green function
G_{\mathcal{L}}

associated with
\mathcal{L}

(see e.g.
[
18
,
20
]
).
Also,
f\in C^{+}(\partial^{\infty}\Omega)
;
the set of all nonnegative continuous functions on the boundary of
\Omega
,
denoted by
\partial^{\infty}\Omega
,
in the one point compactification
\mathbb{R}^{n}\cup\{\infty\}
.
Therefore,

\infty
belongs to
\partial^{\infty}\Omega
if and only if
\Omega
is unbounded.

We will understand a
solution

u
to (
1.1
)
as a nonnegative function on
\Omega

belonging to
L_{\rm loc}^{q_{i}}(\Omega,\sigma_{i})

for all
i=1,\dots,m
,
which satisfies the integral equation

(1.3)

u=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})+{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{0}+H_{f}\quad\text{in }\Omega,

where
H_{f}
is the Perron solution (cf.
[
14
]
)
of the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}h=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow y}h(x)=f(y),&y\in\partial^{\infty}\Omega.\end{cases}

Here the potential
{\bf{G}}\sigma
of
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)

associated with a positive lower semicontinuous function

G:\Omega\times\Omega\to(0,+\infty]

is defined by

(1.4)

{\bf{G}}\sigma(x)=\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)\,d\sigma(y),\quad x\in\Omega.

Note that

{\bf{G}}\sigma
is again lower semicontinuous on
\Omega

(see
[
6
]
).
We call such
G
a
kernel
.
When
G=G_{\mathcal{L}}
,
we use
{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma
to denote
the corresponding Green potential of
\sigma
.

In addition, we will also investigate the following sublinear nonlocal problem

(1.5)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}u^{q_{i}}+\sigma_{0},\quad u\geq 0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\liminf\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}u(x)=0,\end{cases}

where
q_{i}
and
\sigma_{i}
are as above and

(-\Delta)^{\alpha}
, with
0<\alpha<n/2
,
is the fractional Laplace operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In the same spirit as above, (
1.5
) will be understood in the integral sense

(1.6)

u=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{I}_{2\alpha}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})+\mathbf{I}_{2\alpha}\sigma_{0}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

where we always implicitly assume

u\in L_{\rm loc}^{q_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\sigma_{i})

for all
i=1,\dots,m
so that each

u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The Riesz potential
\mathbf{I}_{2\alpha}\sigma
of

\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

with order
0<\alpha<n/2
is given by
[
1
]

\mathbf{I}_{2\alpha}\sigma(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}k_{2\alpha}(x,y)\,d\sigma(y)=c(n,\alpha)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma(B(x,r))}{r^{n-2\alpha}}\frac{dr}{r},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
k_{2\alpha}(x,y)=|x-y|^{2\alpha-n}
is the Riesz kernel and

B(x,r)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-y|<r\}

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
.
In particular, when
\alpha=1
,
\mathbf{I}_{2}\sigma
becomes the classical Newtonian potential.
Note that
\liminf\limits_{x\to\infty}\ \mathbf{I}_{2\alpha}\sigma(x)=0
whenever
\mathbf{I}_{2\alpha}\sigma\not\equiv+\infty

(see
[
19
,
25
]
).

1.2.
Main Goal

This work aims to develop a method for simultaneously establishing necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence and uniqueness of positive bounded solutions to the Dirichlet problems (
1.1
) and (
1.5
).
Our framework further yields sharp two-sided pointwise estimates for such solutions.
Moreover, we obtain an analogous result for positive continuous solutions to similar problems of the type (
1.1
).

1.3.
State of the Art

One primary motivation for studying
the Lane–Emden type equation (
1.1
) with sublinear terms
stems from its connection to the porous medium equation (PME).
In particular, by setting

\mathcal{L}=-\Delta
,
\sigma_{0}=0
,
m=1
,
q_{1}=q

and letting
\sigma_{1}=\sigma(x)

be a nonnegative measurable function, (
1.1
) reduces to

(1.7)

-\Delta u=\sigma(x)u^{q}\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Such equations are intimately linked to the analysis of the associated PME

\sigma(x)U_{t}=\Delta(U^{Q})
in
\Omega\times(0,\infty)
,
where
Q=1/q>1
(see
[
29
]
).

Brezis and Kamin’s seminal work
[
7
]
established
the existence and uniqueness of a positive bounded solution
u

to (
1.7
)
with zero boundary values in the case

\sigma(x)\in L_{\rm loc}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 3
).
They also proved
the sharp two-sided pointwise estimates

(1.8)

c^{-1}(\mathbf{I}_{2}\sigma)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}\leq u\leq c\,\mathbf{I}_{2}\sigma\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

See also the work of Mabrouk
[
10
]
for a treatment of the existence of a bounded solution to (
1.7
) in an arbitrary domain
\Omega
.

We would like to refer the reader to
[
3
,
9
]
for recent related results concerning the existence and pointwise behaviors of bounded solutions to a more general class of sublinear problems of the form
(
1.7
).

For the nonlinear counterparts of (
1.7
), a characterization of positive
p
-superharmonic (or equivalently locally renormalized) solutions to the corresponding
p
-Laplace equations in the sub-natural growth case
0<q<p-1
, satisfying the Brezis–Kamin type estimates in terms of the nonlinear Wolff potentials, have been established in the work of Verbitsky
[
30
]

(see also
[
8
]
).

We seize this opportunity to mention the work
[
17
]
which investigates the existence and bilateral pointwise estimates of positive continuous solutions to (
1.1
) in the case

\mathcal{L}=-\Delta
and
m=1
.
Furthermore, the uniqueness of such solutions is established
under certain integrability conditions of the coefficient
\sigma_{1}
.
For further developments in this direction, see

[
4
,
5
,
16
,
21
,
22
]
.

In recent years, we have seen significant progress in the study of several classes of solutions to problems of the type (
1.1
) and (
1.5
). For instance, in
[
27
]
, the last-named author and Verbitsky established necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of positive finite generalized energy solutions to (
1.1
) with zero boundary values in the case
m=1

(see also
[
28
]
for a related result).
Subsequently, Verbitsky
[
31
]
investigated the existence and uniqueness of general positive solutions to an exterior fractional Laplace problem for
m=1
. More recently, May and the last-named author
[
23
]
provided sufficient conditions for the existence of positive solutions with zero boundary values to (
1.5
) in Lebesgue spaces when
m=1
, and later
[
24
]
extended the results to the case
m\geq 1
in the more refined framework of Lorentz spaces.

1.4.
Main Result

Throughout this paper, we suppose that

•

\{q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subset(0,1)

is nonincreasing, and

•

\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i=0}^{m}\subset\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
satisfies

\sigma_{i}\not\equiv 0
for all
i=1,\dots,m
.

We say that a positive solution
u
to (
1.1
)
is
minimal

if
u\leq\tilde{u}
in
\Omega

for any nontrivial supersolution
\tilde{u}
to (
1.1
).

We now state our main result as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a regular domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 2)

with a positive Green function
G_{\mathcal{L}}

and let
f\in C^{+}(\partial^{\infty}\Omega)
.
Then there exists a positive bounded solution to (
1.1
)
if and only if

(1.9)

\text{${\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{i}$ is bounded on $\Omega$ for all $i=0,1,\dots,m$}.

In this case, there exists a minimal one,
and also all positive bounded solutions
u
to (
1.1
)
satisfy the two-sided pointwise estimates

(1.10)

c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen1}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}({\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{0}+H_{f}\leq u\leq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:ub-gen1}}^{q_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{i}+{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{0}+H_{f}\quad\text{in }\Omega,

where
c_{1}:=(1-q_{1})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{1}}}
and

c_{2}:=\max\left\{1,\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}\|{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}+\|f\|_{\infty}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-q_{1}}}\right\}.

If, in addition
\Omega
is a bounded uniform domain or
a uniform cone (when
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta
and
n\geq 3
),
then the positive bounded solution is unique.

See
[
13
,
15
]

for the definitions of uniform domains and uniform cones.

Remark 1.2
.

It is worth pointing out that
the statement of Theorem
1.1
remains valid
when
f\equiv 0
,
for arbitrary (not necessarily regular) domains
\Omega

with a positive Green function
G_{\mathcal{L}}
.
As noted above, such results were obtained in
[
7
]

using a different method from ours,
in the case of
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
,

\mathcal{L}=-\Delta
,
\sigma_{0}=0
(homogeneous case),
and a single sublinear term
m=1
with
nonnegative
\sigma_{1}\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In our approach to Theorem
1.1
presented in Section
2
,
we investigate positive bounded solutions to
the corresponding integral equation of the form (
1.3
)
in a more general setting

(1.11)

u=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H,\quad u\geq 0\quad\text{in }\Omega,

where
0<q_{i}<1
,
\sigma_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
,

H
is a nonnegative bounded function on an arbitrary domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
and the kernel

G(x,y)
is mildly assumed to satisfy
the
weak maximum principle
(WMP),
also known as the boundedness principle.
That is, there exists a constant
b\geq 1
such that

{\bf{G}}\sigma\leq b\text{ on }\Omega\quad\text{whenever $\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)$ satisfies ${\bf{G}}\sigma\leq 1$ on $\operatorname{supp}(\sigma)$}.

Here
\operatorname{supp}(\sigma)
denotes the support of
\sigma
.
In the case
b=1
,
the kernel
G
is said to satisfy the

strong (Frostman’s) maximum principle
(SMP).
We note that the Green function
G_{\mathcal{L}}

on
\Omega

associated with a uniformly elliptic operator
\mathcal{L}

satisfies the SMP (see
[
18
,
20
]
).
Also, the WMP is satisfied by radially nonincreasing kernels on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(see
[
1
, Section 2.6]
).
In particular, the Riesz kernel

k_{2\alpha}(x,y)=|x-y|^{2\alpha-n}
with
0<\alpha<n/2

satisfies the WMP
with
b=2^{n-2\alpha}

(see
[
19
, Theorem 1.5]
).

The robustness of our method in the nonlocal setting yields the following result for (
1.5
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
0<\alpha<n/2
, where
n\geq 2
.
Then there exists a unique positive bounded solution
u

to (
1.5
) if and only if

(1.12)

\text{$\mathbf{I}_{2\alpha}\sigma_{i}$ is bounded on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ for all $i=0,1,\dots,m$}.

Moreover,
u
satisfies the two-sided pointwise estimates

(1.13)

c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen3}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\mathbf{I}_{2\alpha}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+\mathbf{I}_{2\alpha}\sigma_{0}\leq u\leq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:ub-gen3}}^{q_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{I}_{2\alpha}\sigma_{i}+\mathbf{I}_{2\alpha}\sigma_{0}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

where

c_{3}:=(1-q_{1})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{1}}}2^{\frac{-q_{1}(n-2\alpha)}{(1-q_{1})^{2}}}

and

c_{4}:=\max\left\{1,\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}\|\mathbf{I}_{2\alpha}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-q_{1}}}\right\}.

The Kato-type condition stated below was introduced in
[
16
,
17
]
in the classical setting where
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta
.
We may naturally extend it as follows.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain with a positive Green function
G_{\mathcal{L}}
.
We say that
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
satisfies the
G_{\mathcal{L}}
-Kato condition

if

(1.14)

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\left(\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\Omega\cap B(x,r)}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,d\sigma(y)\right)=0,

and

(1.15)

\lim_{r\to 0+}\left(\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\Omega\setminus B(0,1/r)}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,d\sigma(y)\right)=0\quad\text{(when $\Omega$ is unbounded)}.

By leveraging characterizations of the
G_{\mathcal{L}}
-Kato condition (see Section
3
),
together with Theorem
1.1
,
we obtain the following generalization of the recent finding in
[
17
]
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a regular domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 2)

with a positive Green function
G_{\mathcal{L}}

and let
f\in C^{+}(\partial^{\infty}\Omega)
.
Then there exists a (minimal) positive solution
u\in C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty})
to

(1.16)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}u=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}u^{q_{i}}+\sigma_{0},\quad u\geq 0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow y}u(x)=f(y),&y\in\partial^{\infty}\Omega,\end{cases}

if and only if
\sigma_{i}
satisfies
the
G_{\mathcal{L}}
-Kato condition
for all
i=0,1,\dots,m
.
Moreover,
u
satisfies (
1.10
).
If, in addition
\Omega
is a bounded uniform domain or
a uniform cone (when
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta
and
n\geq 3
),
then the positive continuous solution is unique.

2.
Bounded Solutions to Sublinear Problems

We begin this section by collecting essential ingredients for our analysis.
In this section, unless otherwise stated,
we denote by
\Omega
an arbitrary domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 1
).

The first lemma provides an iterated pointwise estimate for potentials associated with WMP kernels.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

Suppose
G
is a kernel on
\Omega\times\Omega

satisfying the
WMP
with constant
b
.
Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
and
s\geq 1
.
Then

({\bf{G}}\sigma)^{s}\leq sb^{s-1}\,{\bf{G}}\big(({\bf{G}}\sigma)^{s-1}d\sigma\big)\quad\text{in }\Omega.

The next lemma establishes pointwise lower bounds
for supersolutions of sublinear integral equations involving WMP kernels.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
12
]
)
.

Suppose
G
is a kernel on
\Omega\times\Omega

satisfying the
WMP
with constant
b
.
Let
0<q<1
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
.
If
u\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\sigma)

is a nontrivial nonnegative function satisfying

(2.1)

u\geq{\bf{G}}(u^{q}d\sigma)\quad\text{in }\Omega,

then

u\geq(1-q)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}b^{\frac{-q}{1-q}}\left({\bf{G}}\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}\quad\text{in }\Omega.

As in
[
31
, (3.6)]
, the above two lemmas yield the following.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
31
]
)
.

Under the same assumption as in Lemma
2.2
,
we have

{\bf{G}}(u^{q}d\sigma)\geq(1-q)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}b^{\frac{-q}{1-q}}({\bf{G}}\sigma)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}\quad\text{in }\Omega,

whenever
u\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\sigma)

is a nontrivial nonnegative function satisfying (
2.1
).

A kernel
G
on
\Omega\times\Omega

is said to be
quasi-symmetric
(QS)
if there exists a constant
a\geq 1
such that

a^{-1}G(y,x)\leq G(x,y)\leq aG(y,x),\quad\forall x,y\in\Omega.

In the case
a=1
,
G
is said to be
symmetric
.

We say that a symmetric kernel
G
on
\Omega\times\Omega

is
quasi-metric
(QM) if

d(x,y):=1/G(x,y)
satisfies the quasi-triangle inequality

d(x,y)\leq h\,[d(x,z)+d(z,y)],\quad\forall x,y,z\in\Omega,

with quasi-metric constant
h
.
Without loss of generality,
we may assume that
d
is nontrivial, i.e.,

d(x,y)\neq 0
for some
x,y\in\Omega
,
and thus
h\geq 1/2
.
It was shown in
[
31
, Lemma 2.1]
(see also
[
26
, Lemma 3.5]
)
that a QM kernel with quasi-metric constant
h

satisfies the WMP with constant
b=2h
.
On
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
the Riesz kernel of order
0<\alpha<n/2

is QM (see
[
31
]
).

Given a kernel
G
on
\Omega\times\Omega
,
we define

B_{G}(x,r)=\left\{y\in\Omega:G(x,y)>\frac{1}{r}\right\}\quad\text{for }x\in\Omega\text{ and }r>0.

Let
B:=B_{G}(x,r)
,
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
and
0<q<1
.
By
\varkappa(B)
,
we denote the least constant
c
of
the following localized version of the weighted norm
inequalities of
(1,q)
-type
[
26
]
:

(2.2)

\|{\bf{G}}\nu\|_{L^{q}(\Omega,\,\sigma_{B})}\leq c\|\nu\|,\quad\forall\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega),

where
\sigma_{B}
is the restriction of
\sigma
to
B
.
Then the intrinsic nonlinear potential
{\bf{K}}\sigma
,
introduced in
[
31
]
, is defined by

{\bf{K}}\sigma(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{[\varkappa(B_{G}(x,r))]^{\frac{q}{1-q}}}{r^{2}}\,dr,\quad x\in\Omega.

The next lemma provides an upper pointwise estimate for potentials associated with QM kernels.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
31
]
)
.

Suppose
G
is a
QM
kernel on
\Omega\times\Omega

with quasi-metric constant
h
.
Let
\nu,\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
and
0<q<1
.
Then

{\bf{G}}\big(({\bf{G}}\nu)^{q}d\sigma\big)\leq(2h)^{q}({\bf{G}}\nu)^{q}\left[{\bf{G}}\sigma+({\bf{K}}\sigma)^{1-q}\right]\quad\text{in }\Omega.

When the WMP kernel
G
in Lemma
2.2

is symmetric (or more generally, QS),
one can refine the lower estimates for solutions to (
2.1
)
in terms of the intrinsic potential
{\bf{K}}\sigma
.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
31
, Lemma 3.2 and (3.6)]
)
.

Suppose

G
is a symmetric kernel on
\Omega\times\Omega

satisfying the
WMP
with constant
b
.
Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
and
0<q<1
.
If
u\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\sigma)

is a nontrivial nonnegative function
satisfying (
2.1
), then

(2.3)

u\geq{\bf{G}}(u^{q}d\sigma)\geq(1-q)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}b^{\frac{-q}{1-q}}{\bf{K}}\sigma\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Remark 2.6
.

In Lemma
2.5
,
if the kernel
G
is only QS
with quasi-symmetry constant
a
,
then (
2.3
) remains valid
with a constant
c=c(q,b,a)
in place of

(1-q)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}b^{\frac{-q}{1-q}}
.
Indeed, the symmetrized kernel
G^{s}(x,y):=G(x,y)+G(y,x)

is symmetric and satisfies

(1+a^{-1})G(x,y)\leq G^{s}(x,y)\leq(1+a)G(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in\Omega.

Our key theorems in this section discuss the existence, uniqueness, and pointwise behavior of bounded solutions to (
1.11
) under various settings of the corresponding kernel
G
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
G
be a kernel on
\Omega\times\Omega
and
let
H
be a nonnegative bounded function on
\Omega
.

(i)

Suppose
G
satisfies the
WMP
with constant

.
Then the following hold:

(a)

If
{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}
is bounded on
\Omega
for all

,
then there exists a minimal positive bounded solution to (
1.11
).

(b)

If (
1.11
) admits a nontrivial bounded supersolution,
then
{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}
is bounded on
\Omega
for all
i=0,1,\dots,m
.

(c)

All nontrivial bounded solutions
u
to (
1.11
)
enjoy the Brezis–Kamin type estimates

(2.4)

c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H\leq u\leq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:ub-gen2}}^{q_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H\quad\text{in }\Omega,

where

c_{5}:=(1-q_{1})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{1}}}b^{\frac{-q_{1}}{(1-q_{1})^{2}}}

and

c_{6}:=\max\left\{1,\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}\|{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}+\|H\|_{\infty}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-q_{1}}}\right\}.

In particular,
\|u\|_{\infty}\leq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:ub-gen2}}
.

(d)

If, in addition
G
is symmetric,
then any nontrivial supersolution
u
to (
1.11
)
enjoys the lower pointwise estimate

u\geq\frac{c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}}{4}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left[({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{K}}\sigma_{i}+{\bf{G}}(H^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\right]+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}\right)+H\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Moreover, this estimate still holds
with the constant
c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}/4
replaced by

c=c(q_{1},b,a)
,
when
G
is only
QS
with quasi-symmetry constant
a
.

(ii)

Suppose
G
is
QM
with quasi-metric constant
h
.
Then the following hold:

(e)

Any subsolution
u
to (
1.11
)
satisfies the upper pointwise estimate

u\leq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:ub-gennn}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left[({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{K}}\sigma_{i}+{\bf{G}}(H^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\right]+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}\right)+H\quad\text{in }\Omega,

where
c_{7}:=2(8mh)^{\frac{q_{1}}{1-q_{1}}}
.

(f)

If (
1.11
) admits a minimal positive bounded solution,
then it is the only positive bounded solution to (
1.11
).

In arguments below, we often use the inequalities

(2.5)

c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}\leq(1-q_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}b^{\frac{-q_{i}}{(1-q_{i})^{2}}}\leq(1-q_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}b^{\frac{-q_{i}}{1-q_{i}}},\quad i=1,\dots,m,

which follow by
0<q_{i}\leq q_{1}<1\leq b
.

Proof of Theorem
2.7
: Part
(i)(a)
.

Define a sequence of functions

\{u_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
on
\Omega
by

u_{0}=c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H\quad\text{and}\quad u_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}(u_{j-1}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H\quad\text{for }j\in\mathbb{N}.

Then

u_{0}\geq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}>0

on
\Omega
by
\sigma_{i}\not\equiv 0
and
G>0
.
Applying Lemma
2.1
with
s=\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}

and (
2.5
) yields that

(2.6)

\begin{split}u_{1}&=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}(u_{0}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H\\
&\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}^{q_{i}}{\bf{G}}\big(({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{q_{i}}{1-q_{i}}}d\sigma_{i}\big)+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H\\
&\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}^{q_{i}}(1-q_{i})b^{\frac{-q_{i}}{1-q_{i}}}({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H\\
&\geq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H\\
&=u_{0}.\end{split}

Moreover, by assumptions,

\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+\|{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|H\|_{\infty}<+\infty,

and thus

\|u_{1}\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{q_{i}}\|{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}+\|{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|H\|_{\infty}<+\infty.

Suppose there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that

u_{0}\leq u_{1}\leq\cdots\leq u_{j}
on
\Omega

with
\|u_{j}\|_{\infty}<+\infty
. Then

u_{j+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}(u_{j}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}(u_{j-1}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H=u_{j},

and again by assumptions,

\|u_{j+1}\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|u_{j}\|_{\infty}^{q_{i}}\|{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}+\|{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|H\|_{\infty}<+\infty.

Hence, by induction,

\{u_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}

is a nondecreasing sequence of positive bounded functions in
\Omega
.
In addition, since

0<q_{i}\leq q_{1}<1
and

0<\|u_{j-1}\|_{\infty}\leq\|u_{j}\|_{\infty}<+\infty
,
we have

(2.7)

\begin{split}\|u_{j}\|_{\infty}^{1-q_{1}}&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\|u_{j-1}\|_{\infty}^{q_{i}}}{\|u_{j}\|_{\infty}^{q_{1}}}\|{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}+\frac{\|{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}\|_{\infty}}{\|u_{j}\|_{\infty}^{q_{1}}}+\frac{\|H\|_{\infty}}{\|u_{j}\|_{\infty}^{q_{1}}}\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\|u_{j}\|_{\infty}^{q_{i}}}{\|u_{j}\|_{\infty}^{q_{1}}}\|{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}+\frac{\|{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}\|_{\infty}}{\|u_{j}\|_{\infty}^{q_{1}}}+\frac{\|H\|_{\infty}}{\|u_{j}\|_{\infty}^{q_{1}}}\\
&\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m}\|{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}+\|H\|_{\infty},\end{split}

where the last inequality is valid whenever
\|u_{j}\|_{\infty}>1
.
Therefore

(2.8)

\|u_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq\max\left\{1,\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}\|{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}+\|H\|_{\infty}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-q_{1}}}\right\}=c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:ub-gen2}}.

By the monotone convergence theorem,
the pointwise limit
u=\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}u_{j}

is a positive bounded solution to (
1.11
).
Minimality of the solution
u
follows from its construction.
In fact, if
\tilde{u}
is a nontrivial supersolution to (
1.11
),
then
\tilde{u}\geq{\bf{G}}(\tilde{u}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})
,
and so

{\bf{G}}(\tilde{u}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\geq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}

by Lemma
2.3
and (
2.5
).
Therefore

\tilde{u}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}(\tilde{u}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H\geq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H=u_{0}.

The definition of
u_{j}
and induction yield that

u_{j}\leq\tilde{u}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Hence,

u\leq\tilde{u}
in
\Omega
.
∎

2.7
: Part
(i)(b)
.

Let

be a nontrivial bounded supersolution to (
1.11
).
Then
u\geq{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}
.
Also, since

.
These imply that

must be bounded on
\Omega

for all

.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.7
: Part
(i)(c)
.

Let
u
be any nontrivial bounded solution to (
1.11
).
By a similar argument to (
2.7
)–(
2.8
)
replaced

by
u
,
we get
\|u\|_{\infty}\leq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:ub-gen2}}
.
Therefore

u\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|u\|_{\infty}^{q_{i}}{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H\leq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:ub-gen2}}^{q_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H.

and (

) to obtain

2.4
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.7
: Part
(i)(d)
.

Let
u
be any nontrivial supersolution to (
1.11
).
For
i=1,\dots,m
,
we set
v_{i}:={\bf{G}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})
.
Note that
v_{i}\leq v_{i}+H\leq u
in
\Omega
.
Since
v_{i}\geq{\bf{G}}(v_{i}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})
,
we derive from Lemmas
2.3
and
2.5

and (
2.5
) that

{\bf{G}}(v_{i}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\geq\frac{c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}}{2}\left(({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{K}}\sigma_{i}\right)\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Therefore, by using the elementary inequality

(a+b)^{q}\geq\frac{1}{2}(a^{q}+b^{q})\quad\text{for }a,b\geq 0\text{ and }0<q<1,

we deduce

\displaystyle{\bf{G}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})

\displaystyle\geq{\bf{G}}\big((v_{i}+H)^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\big)\geq\frac{1}{2}\big[{\bf{G}}(v_{i}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})+{\bf{G}}(H^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\big]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}}{4}\left[({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{K}}\sigma_{i}+{\bf{G}}(H^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\right].

Therefore

\begin{split}u&\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H\\
&\geq\frac{c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}}{4}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left[({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{K}}\sigma_{i}+{\bf{G}}(H^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\right]+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}\right)+H.\end{split}

As discussed in Remark
2.6
,
this estimate remains valid
even when

is QS,
by changing the constant
c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}/4
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.7
: Part
(ii)(e)
.

Let
u
be any subsolution to (
1.11
) and let

d\nu:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}+d\sigma_{0}.

Then
u\leq{\bf{G}}\nu+H
in
\Omega
, and

d\nu\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}({\bf{G}}\nu+H)^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}+d\sigma_{0}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}({\bf{G}}\nu)^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}H^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}+d\sigma_{0}

on Borel subsets of
\Omega
.
Therefore

(2.9)

{\bf{G}}\nu\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}\big(({\bf{G}}\nu)^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\big)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}(H^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}.

Appealing to Lemma
2.4
, followed by the inequality

(a+b)^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(a^{p}+b^{p})\quad\text{for }a,b\geq 0\text{ and }p>1,

we estimate

\displaystyle{\bf{G}}\big(({\bf{G}}\nu)^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\big)

\displaystyle\leq(2h)^{q_{i}}({\bf{G}}\nu)^{q_{i}}\left[{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}+({\bf{K}}\sigma_{i})^{1-q_{i}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(2h)^{q_{i}}({\bf{G}}\nu)^{q_{i}}2^{q_{i}}\left[({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{K}}\sigma_{i}\right]^{1-q_{i}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{2m}\Big)^{q_{i}}({\bf{G}}\nu)^{q_{i}}\cdot(2m)^{q_{i}}(4h)^{q_{i}}\left[({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{K}}\sigma_{i}\right]^{1-q_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q_{i}}{2m}{\bf{G}}\nu+(1-q_{i})(8mh)^{\frac{q_{i}}{1-q_{i}}}\left[({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{K}}\sigma_{i}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2m}{\bf{G}}\nu+(8mh)^{\frac{q_{1}}{1-q_{1}}}\left[({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{K}}\sigma_{i}\right],

where we used Young’s inequality in the third inequality and,

0<q_{i}\leq q_{1}<1
and
h\geq 1/2
in the last inequality.
Substituting this into (
2.9
) and moving
{\bf{G}}\nu
to the left side,
we obtain

{\bf{G}}\nu\leq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:ub-gennn}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left[({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{K}}\sigma_{i}+{\bf{G}}(H^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\right]+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}\right).

Hence

u\leq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:ub-gennn}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left[({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{K}}\sigma_{i}+{\bf{G}}(H^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\right]+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}\right)+H,

as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.7
: Part
(ii)(f)
.

Let
u
and
\tilde{u}
be positive bounded solutions to (
1.11
)
such that
u
is minimal.
It suffices to show that
\tilde{u}\leq u
in
\Omega
.
By parts
(i)(d)
and
(ii)(e)
,
we see that

\begin{split}\tilde{u}&\leq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:ub-gennn}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left[({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{K}}\sigma_{i}+{\bf{G}}(H^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\right]+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}\right)+H\\
&\leq\kappa\left\{\frac{c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}}{4}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left[({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{K}}\sigma_{i}+{\bf{G}}(H^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\right]+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}\right)+H\right\}\\
&\leq\kappa u,\end{split}

where
\kappa=4c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:ub-gennn}}/c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}\geq 1
.
Therefore

\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}(\tilde{u}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\kappa^{q_{i}}{\bf{G}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\leq\kappa^{q_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}),

and thus

\tilde{u}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}(\tilde{u}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H\leq\kappa^{q_{1}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H\right]=\kappa^{q_{1}}u.

\tilde{u}\leq\kappa^{q_{1}^{j}}u,\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
0<q_{1}<1
,
letting
j\rightarrow\infty
yields that
\tilde{u}\leq u
in
\Omega
.
This proves the claim.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

We can establish
the uniqueness of a positive bounded solution to (
1.11
)
without relying on its minimality among supersolutions.
Indeed, let
u
and
\tilde{u}

be positive bounded solutions to (
1.11
).
Arguing as in the proof of Theorem
2.7

Part
(ii)(f)
above,
we find a constant
\kappa\geq 1
such that

\kappa^{-q_{1}^{j}}u\leq\tilde{u}\leq\kappa^{q_{1}^{j}}u,\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore
\tilde{u}=u
.
We refer the reader to
[
31
]
,
where a similar argument was employed to prove
the uniqueness of positive solutions to (
1.11
)
in the case
m=1
.

The QM assumption
imposed in Theorem
2.7

(ii)

is quite restrictive,
since
it fails for the Green function
G_{\mathcal{L}}
on a ball and,
more generally, on any bounded uniform domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
To address these limitations
while preserving the matching bilateral pointwise bounds
(and thus the uniqueness result),
we explore a broader class of the so-called quasi-metrically modifiable kernels.

A kernel
G:\Omega\times\Omega\to(0,+\infty]

is called
quasi-metrically modifiable
(QMM)
with a positive function
\mathfrak{m}\in C(\Omega)

if the kernel
\tilde{G}
defined by

(2.10)

\tilde{G}(x,y)=\frac{G(x,y)}{\mathfrak{m}(x)\mathfrak{m}(y)},\quad x,y\in\Omega,

is QM.
We call
\mathfrak{m}
a
modifier
of
G
.

Examples of QMM kernels are presented in
[
2
,
11
,
13
]
.
In particular,
the Green functions associated with the Laplacian and the fractional Laplacian
of order
0<\alpha<n/2

are QMM for bounded domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

satisfying the boundary Harnack principle,
such as Lipschitz domains,
or more generally NTA (non-tangentially accessible) domains,
or uniform domains.
Also, the second named author
[
15
]
established
the generalized
3G
-inequality on a uniform cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
,
which guarantees that

G_{-\Delta}
is QMM with

\mathfrak{m}
being
the Martin kernel on
\Omega
with pole at
\infty
.

We now observe that (
1.11
)
is equivalent to the following integral equation in terms of
the modified kernel
\tilde{G}
with modifier
\mathfrak{m}
:

(2.11)

v=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{{\bf{G}}}(v^{q_{i}}d\tilde{\sigma}_{i})+\tilde{{\bf{G}}}\tilde{\sigma}_{0}+\tilde{H}\quad\text{in }\Omega,

where

(2.12)

v:=\frac{u}{\mathfrak{m}},\quad d\tilde{\sigma}_{i}:=\mathfrak{m}^{1+q_{i}}d\sigma_{i},\quad d\tilde{\sigma}_{0}:=\mathfrak{m}\,d\sigma_{0},\quad\tilde{H}:=\frac{H}{\mathfrak{m}}.

In the same spirit as above,
the corresponding modified intrinsic potential

\tilde{\mathbf{K}}\tilde{\sigma}_{i}

is defined by

\tilde{{\bf{K}}}\tilde{\sigma}_{i}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{[\tilde{\varkappa}(B_{\tilde{G}}(x,r))]^{\frac{q_{i}}{1-q_{i}}}}{r^{2}}\,dr,\quad x\in\Omega,

where for
B=B_{\tilde{G}}(x,r)
,

\tilde{\varkappa}(B)
is
the least constant satisfying

\|\tilde{{\bf{G}}}\nu\|_{L^{q_{i}}(\Omega,\,(\tilde{\sigma}_{i})_{B})}\leq\tilde{\varkappa}(B)\|\nu\|,\quad\forall\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega).

The following theorem provides
matching lower and upper pointwise bounds
for bounded solutions to (
1.11
)
in the case of QMM kernels
G
.

) holds for the subsolution
u
.
Moreover, if (
1.11
) admits a positive bounded solution,
then it must be unique in view of Remark
2.8
.
∎

Let us now prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
,
where
\Omega
is assumed to be regular due to the boundary condition.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Applying Theorem
2.7

(i)(a)
,
(i)(b)
with

G=G_{\mathcal{L}}

and

H=H_{f}
,
we see that
(
1.9
) is valid if and only if
there exists a (minimal) positive bounded solution to (
1.1
).
Moreover, Theorem
2.7

(i)(c)
implies (
1.10
).
The uniqueness follows from Theorem
2.9

when
\Omega
is a bounded uniform domain or
a uniform cone.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

This follows from Theorem
2.7

with

\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

G(x,y)=k_{2\alpha}(x,y)=|x-y|^{2\alpha-n}

and
H\equiv 0
.
∎

We conclude this section with the following observation.

Remark 2.10
.

In the case
m=\infty
(infinitely many sublinear terms),
the analysis of (
1.11
) becomes more delicate.
Nevertheless,
our arguments yield that a sufficient condition for
the existence of a (minimal) positive bounded solution to (
1.11
) is

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\|{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}<+\infty.

Moreover, such a solution satisfies the pointwise bounds

c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:lb-gen2}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}({\bf{G}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H\leq u\leq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:ub-gen3inf}}^{q_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}+{\bf{G}}\sigma_{0}+H\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Here,
\{q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset(0,1)
is nonincreasing
and

c_{8}:=\max\left\{1,\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\|{\bf{G}}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}+\|H\|_{\infty}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-q_{1}}}\right\}.

Also, analogous results for
(
1.1
) and (
1.5
) with
m=\infty

can be obtained as well.

3.
Continuous Solutions to (
1.16
)

We begin this section with the following properties of
the
G_{\mathcal{L}}
-Kato condition (Definition
1.4
),
which can be derived by modifying
the proofs of
[
17
, Lemmas 2.1–2.3]
.

For
z=\infty
and
r>0
,
we use the notation

B(z,r)=(\mathbb{R}^{n}\cup\{\infty\})\setminus\overline{B(0,1/r)}

and

\int_{\Omega\cap B(z,r)}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,d\sigma(y)=\int_{\Omega\setminus\overline{B(0,1/r)}}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,d\sigma(y).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
.
Then the following are equivalent.

(a)

\sigma
satisfies the
G_{\mathcal{L}}
-Kato condition.

(b)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0+}\left(\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\Omega\cap B(z,r)}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,d\sigma(y)\right)=0

for any
z\in\overline{\Omega}^{\infty}
.

(c)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0+}\left(\sup_{z\in\overline{\Omega}^{\infty}}\left(\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\Omega\cap B(z,r)}G_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\,d\sigma(y)\right)\right)=0
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
satisfies the
G_{\mathcal{L}}
-Kato condition,
then
{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma
is bounded on
\Omega
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
and
C>0
.
Then the following are equivalent.

(a)

\sigma
satisfies the
G_{\mathcal{L}}
-Kato condition.

(b)

{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{E}\in C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty})

for any Borel subset
E
of
\Omega
,
where
\sigma_{E}
is the restriction of
\sigma
to
E
.

(c)

{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma\in C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty})
.

(d)

The family

\{{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}(\psi\,d\sigma):\psi\in\Psi\}

is equicontinuous on
\overline{\Omega}^{\infty}
,
where

\Psi:=\left\{\psi:0\leq\psi\leq C\text{ $\sigma$-a.e. on $\Omega$}\right\}.

Moreover, in this case,

{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}(\psi\,d\sigma)

vanishes continuously on
\partial^{\infty}\Omega

for each
\psi\in\Psi
.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

For the necessity part, let

u\in C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty})

be a positive solution to (
1.16
).
We write

{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{0}=u-\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})-H_{f}.

Since the right-hand side is
upper semicontinuous on
\overline{\Omega}^{\infty}
,
we have

{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{0}\in C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty})
.
Similarly,

{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\in C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty})

for all
i=1,\dots,m
.
Hence, by Lemma
3.3
,
the measures
\sigma_{0}
and
u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}

satisfy the
G_{\mathcal{L}}
-Kato condition.
It remains to show that
\sigma_{i}
itself satisfies
the
G_{\mathcal{L}}
-Kato condition for all
i=1,\dots,m
.
To this end, let
z\in\overline{\Omega}^{\infty}
,
r>0
and define

\omega_{i}^{(r)}:=(\sigma_{i})_{\Omega\cap B(z,r)}
.
Then
u\geq{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\geq{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}(u^{q_{i}}d\omega_{i}^{(r)})
in
\Omega
.
Appealing to Lemma
2.3
with
b=1
,
we have

\sup_{\Omega}{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}(u^{q_{i}}d\omega_{i}^{(r)})\geq(1-q_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}\sup_{\Omega}({\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\omega_{i}^{(r)})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}.

Since
u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}
satisfies the
G_{\mathcal{L}}
-Kato condition,
Lemma
3.1
gives

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\left(\sup_{x\in\Omega}{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}(u^{q_{i}}d\omega_{i}^{(r)})(x)\right)=0.

Therefore

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\sup_{x\in\Omega}{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\omega^{(r)}_{i}(x)\right)=0.

Applying Lemma
3.1
again, we conclude that

\sigma_{i}
satisfies the
G_{\mathcal{L}}
-Kato condition.

For the sufficiency part,
suppose each
\sigma_{i}
satisfies the
G_{\mathcal{L}}
-Kato condition.
According to Lemma
3.2
,

{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{i}
is bounded on
\Omega
.
By Theorem
1.1
,
there exists a minimal positive bounded solution
u
to (
1.1
),
enjoying (
1.10
).
Moreover,

{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}),{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{0}\in C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty})

by Lemma
3.3
.
Thus
u\in C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty})
by (
1.3
).
Hence
u
is a desired solution to (
1.16
).

The uniqueness follows from Theorem
1.1
,
since continuous solutions are bounded on
\Omega
.
∎

We conclude this paper
by presenting an alternative construction of
a continuous solution to (
1.16
),
employing Schauder’s fixed point theorem,
which does not rely on the use of Theorem
1.1
.

In the rest, we suppose that

\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
satisfy the
G_{\mathcal{L}}
-Kato condition.
Note that

{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{i}
is bounded on
\Omega

by Lemma
3.2
.
Moreover,
H_{f}\in C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty})

since
\Omega
is regular and
f\in C^{+}(\partial^{\infty}\Omega)
,
and
\|H_{f}\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}

by the maximum principle.
Define

v_{0}(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(1-q_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{i}(x)^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{0}(x)+H_{f}(x),\quad x\in\Omega,

and

c_{9}:=\max\left\{1,\|v_{0}\|_{\infty},\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}\|{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}+\|f\|_{\infty}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-q_{1}}}\right\}.

Consider the class of functions

\mathcal{P}:=\left\{v\in C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty}):v_{0}(x)\leq v(x)\leq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:cor}}\text{ for }x\in\Omega\right\}.

Then
\mathcal{P}
is a nonempty closed bounded convex subset of
C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty})
.
Define a linear operator
T_{f}
on
\mathcal{P}
by

T_{f}(v)(x):=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}(v^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})(x)+{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{0}(x)+H_{f}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\[8.00003pt]
f(x),&x\in\partial^{\infty}\Omega.\end{cases}

We shall show that
T_{f}:\mathcal{P}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}
is a compact operator.

Lemma 3.4
.

T_{f}(\mathcal{P})\subset\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

Let
v\in\mathcal{P}
.
Then

T_{f}(v)\in C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty})

by Lemma
3.3
and
H_{f}\in C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty})
.
Since
c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:cor}}\geq 1
and
0<q_{i}\leq q_{1}
, we have

\begin{split}\|T_{f}(v)\|_{\infty}&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|v\|_{\infty}^{q_{i}}\|{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}+\|{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|f\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:cor}}^{q_{1}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}+\|{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|f\|_{\infty}\right)\\
&\leq c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:cor}}^{q_{1}}\cdot c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:cor}}^{1-q_{1}}=c_{\scriptsize\ref{c:cor}}.\end{split}

Also, since

v\geq v_{0}\geq(1-q_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}({\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}
in
\Omega
,
it follows from Lemma
2.1
that in
\Omega
,

\begin{split}T_{f}(v)&\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}(1-q_{i})^{\frac{q_{i}}{1-q_{i}}}{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\big(({\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{q_{i}}{1-q_{i}}}d\sigma_{i}\big)+{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{0}+H_{f}\\
&\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}(1-q_{i})^{\frac{q_{i}}{1-q_{i}}}(1-q_{i})({\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}+{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{0}+H_{f}\\
&=v_{0}.\end{split}

Thus
T_{f}(v)\in\mathcal{P}
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

T_{f}(\mathcal{P})
is relatively compact in
C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty})
.

Proof.

Since
\sigma_{i}
satisfies the
G_{\mathcal{L}}
-Kato condition
and
H_{f}\in C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty})
,
it follows from Lemma
3.3
that

T_{f}(\mathcal{P})
is equicontinuous on
\overline{\Omega}^{\infty}
.
By Lemma
3.4
,

T_{f}(\mathcal{P})
is uniformly bounded on
\overline{\Omega}^{\infty}
.
Therefore, by Arzelà–Ascoli’s theorem,

T_{f}(\mathcal{P})
is relatively compact in
C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty})
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

T_{f}
is continuous on
\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

Let
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathcal{P}
satisfy
\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\infty}<1
.
Then, in
\Omega
,

\displaystyle|T_{f}(v_{1})-T_{f}(v_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}(|v_{1}^{q_{i}}-v_{2}^{q_{i}}|d\sigma_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}(|v_{1}-v_{2}|^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|^{q_{i}}_{\infty}\|{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}.

Since
0<q_{m}\leq q_{i}
, we have

\|T_{f}(v_{1})-T_{f}(v_{2})\|_{\infty}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|{\bf{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{i}\|_{\infty}\right)\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\infty}^{q_{m}}.

This implies the continuity of
T_{f}
on
\mathcal{P}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
(Sufficiency Part).

The above discussions enable us to employ
Schauder’s fixed point theorem
[
32
]

to establish the existence of

u\in\mathcal{P}
such that
T_{f}(u)=u
on
\overline{\Omega}^{\infty}
.
Clearly,
this
u
is a positive solution in

C(\overline{\Omega}^{\infty})
to (
1.16
).
∎
