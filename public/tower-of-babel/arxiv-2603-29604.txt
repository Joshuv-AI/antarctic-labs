On the application of the SCD semismooth* Newton method to solving Stokes problem with stick-slip boundary conditions
V. Arzt, P. Beremlijski, H. Gfrerer, J.V. Outrata

Abstract
The paper deals with the 3D Stokes problem with Navier-Tresca stick-slip boundary conditions. A weak formulation of this problem leads to a variational inequality of the second kind, coupled with an equality constraint. This problem is then approximated using the mixed finite element method, yielding a generalized equation, to the numerical solution of which we implement a variant of the SCD semismooth* Newton method. This includes also a globalization technique ensuring convergence for arbitrary starting points. Numerical experiments demonstrate the effeciency of this approach.



1
Introduction

To model the flow of incompressible fluids, one often makes use of the Stokes equations, which are a simplification of the Navier-Stokes equations. It accurately characterizes low-speed, non-turbulent flows, i.e., flows with low Reynolds numbers.

The standard kinematic boundary condition for flow problems is represented by a no-slip condition. This condition states that the flow velocity at the boundary created by a solid, impermeable wall is zero. However, in many real-world scenarios, a non-zero flow velocity can be observed at the boundary (see
[
25
]
and
[
5
]
). To address these situations, boundary conditions that allow for slip at the boundary have been introduced. At this boundary, there may be nodes where sticking occurs (i.e., they are in a stick state) and nodes where slipping occurs (i.e., they are in a slip state). These so-called stick-slip boundary conditions can be described either by a smooth function (the Navier boundary condition, see
[
21
]
) or by a nonsmooth function (the Tresca and Navier-Tresca boundary conditions, see
[
27
,
26
,
14
,
13
]
). The Tresca and Navier-Tresca conditions are more efficient in describing problems where slip has a threshold character. When the Stokes problem is formulated with the Tresca or Navier-Tresca condition in a weak formulation, it leads to an inequality-type problem. It is known that a solution to the Stokes problem with either of these boundary conditions exists and is unique (see
[
13
]
).

The Stokes problem with stick-slip boundary conditions is typically addressed by transforming the respective nonsmooth variational inequality into a dual problem. This dual problem is solved using methods such as the active set method, the path-following interior point method, or the semismooth Newton method. These approaches leverage Lagrange multipliers to enforce constraints including impermeability and incompressibility, and employ the preconditioned conjugate gradient method for linear systems, often with specialized diagonal preconditioners (see
[
19
,
12
]
). Each method ultimately solves a system of equations derived from smoothing the original system. For numerical approximation, P1-bubble/P1 elements are frequently used (see
[
1
,
18
]
). Their advantage is a lower degree of freedom than, for example, P2-P1 elements.

In this paper we employ to the numerical solution of the Stokes problem with the Navier-Tresca stick-slip boundary conditions a different approach, based on the recently developed SCD (Subspace Containing Derivative) semismooth* Newton method. In the sequel, this method will be denoted by the acronym SSSN. To achieve this goal, we will proceed in a similar way as employed in the papers
[
7
]
and
[
6
]
in case of different variational problems. Note that, unlike the methods mentioned in the previous paragraph, this technique does not require any substantial reformulation/smoothing of the original nonsmooth problem. The SSSN method is a powerful numerical tool for solving a class of generalized equations (systems with both single- and multi-valued parts), including various types of variational inequalities. It has been developed by Gfrerer and Outrata in
[
9
]
and it improves an earlier version of it (
[
8
]
) by using the SCD property of the multi-valued part of the respective generalized equation (GE). Thanks to this modification, one can construct a suitable approximation (linearization) of the considered GE in an efficient way. Similarly to the classical semismooth Newton methods, the SSSN method achieves locally superlinear convergence.
To use the described method, it is necessary just to rewrite the Stokes problem with stick-slip boundary conditions in form of a GE.

The outline of the paper is as follows. Section 2 is devoted to the Stokes problem with the Navier-Tresca stick-slip boundary condition. In its first part, we focus on the continuous formulation of this problem. Next, we present a discrete formulation based on the mixed finite element method and, finally, optimality conditions are derived. In Section 3, we describe our main tool, the SSSN method.
Among other things, this requires to introduce the so-called SCD mappings and some notions from the respective theory. Thereafter, we present the two fundamental steps of the SSSN method: The approximation step and the Newton step. Section 4 focuses on applying the SSSN method to the considered problem and includes all implementation details. Section 5 is devoted to test examples showing the efficiency of the presented approach.

2
Stokes problem

In this section, the classical formulation of the Stokes problem with the Navier-Tresca stick-slip boundary condition is presented. Thereafter, the weak formulation and the respective discretized problem are derived. Finally, optimality conditions are stated.

2.1
Continuous and two-field weak formulation of the problem

Consider a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\dabR^{3}
with a sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. The boundary is divided into three non-empty disjoint parts such that
\partial\Omega=\overline{\gamma}_{D}\cup\overline{\gamma}_{N}\cup\overline{\gamma}_{S}
. On this domain, the steady flow of a viscous incompressible Newtonian fluid is modeled by the Stokes system with the Dirichlet and the Neumann boundary conditions on
\gamma_{D}
,
\gamma_{N}
, respectively, and with the impermeability and nonlinear (Navier-Tresca) stick-slip boundary conditions on
\gamma_{S}
.
The classical formulation reads as follows:

Find a velocity field
{u}:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\dabR^{3}
and a pressure field
p:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\dabR
such that

\begin{array}[]{rcll}-2\nu\nabla\cdot\nabla_{S}\,{u}+\nabla p&=&{f}&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot{u}&=&0&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
{u}&=&{u}_{D}&\mbox{on }\gamma_{D},\\
{\sigma}&=&{\sigma}_{N}&\mbox{on }\gamma_{N},\\
u_{n}&=&0&\mbox{on }\gamma_{S},\\
||{\sigma}_{t}+\kappa{u}_{t}||&\leq&g&\mbox{on }\gamma_{S},\\
{\sigma}_{t}\cdot{u}_{t}+g||{u}_{t}||+\kappa{u}_{t}\cdot{u}_{t}&=&0&\mbox{on }\gamma_{S},\end{array}

(1)

where
\nu>0
is the kinematic viscosity,
\nabla_{S}\,u=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\mathsf{T}})\in\dabR^{3}\times\dabR^{3}
is the symmetric gradient,
{f}:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\dabR^{3}
are volume forces acting on the fluid and
{\sigma}:\partial\Omega\rightarrow\dabR^{3}
is a stress defined as:

{\sigma}=(2\nu\,\nabla_{S}\,{u}-pI){n}\quad\mbox{on }\partial\Omega.

(2)

The second equation in (
1
) guarantees the incompressibility condition. The mappings
{u}_{D}:\overline{\gamma}_{D}\rightarrow\dabR^{3}
and
{\sigma}_{N}:\overline{\gamma}_{N}\rightarrow\dabR^{3}
specify the given Dirichlet and Neumann boundary data. The adhesive function is denoted by
\kappa:\gamma_{S}\rightarrow\dabR^{+}
, and the threshold slip bound function is represented by
g:\gamma_{S}\rightarrow\dabR^{+}
. The unit outward normal vector, denoted by
{n}={n}({x})\in\dabR^{3}
, along with two unit tangential vectors
{t}_{1}={t}_{1}({x})\in\dabR^{3}
and
{t}_{2}={t}_{2}({x})\in\dabR^{3}
, are chosen such that the triplet
\{{n},{t}_{1},{t}_{2}\}
forms an orthonormal basis at
{x}\in\partial\Omega
.

The values of the normal and tangential velocity
{u}
along
\partial\Omega
are given by
u_{n}={u}\cdot{n}
and
{u}_{t}=\left({u}\cdot{t}_{1},{u}\cdot{t}_{2}\right)
, respectively. Analogously,
{\sigma}_{n}
,
{\sigma}_{t}
are defined.

The classical solution of the problem (
1
) is represented by the functions
{u}\in\left(C^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{3}
and
p\in C^{1}(\Omega)
, which possess a continuous extension to the boundary
\partial\Omega
, such that all relations in (
1
) are satisfied after the substitution of
{u}
and
p
.

The weak formulation of the problem (
1
) requires the following forms:

\displaystyle a_{0}:\left(H^{1}(\Omega)\right)^{3}\times\left(H^{1}(\Omega)\right)^{3}\rightarrow\dabR,\quad

\displaystyle a_{0}({w},{v})=2\nu\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{S}\,{w}:\nabla_{S}\,{v}\,\mathrm{d}x,

(3)

\displaystyle a_{\kappa}:\left(H^{1}(\Omega)\right)^{3}\times\left(H^{1}(\Omega)\right)^{3}\rightarrow\dabR,\quad

\displaystyle a_{\kappa}({w},{v})=a_{0}({w},{v})+\kappa\int_{\gamma_{S}}{w}_{t}\cdot{v}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}s,

(4)

\displaystyle b:L^{2}(\Omega)\times\left(H^{1}(\Omega)\right)^{3}\rightarrow\dabR,

\displaystyle b(q,{w})=-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}q(\nabla\cdot{w})\,\mathrm{d}x,

(5)

\displaystyle l:\left(H^{1}(\Omega)\right)^{3}\rightarrow\dabR,

\displaystyle l({v})=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{f}\cdot{v}+\int_{\gamma_{N}}{\sigma}_{N}\cdot{v}\,\mathrm{d}s,

(6)

\displaystyle j:\left(H^{1}(\Omega)\right)^{3}\rightarrow\dabR,\quad

\displaystyle j({v})=\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{S}}g||{v}_{t}||\,\mathrm{d}s.

(7)

Furthermore, we define the velocity solution set:

V_{{u}_{D}}=\left\{{v}\in\left(H^{1}(\Omega)\right)^{3}:{v}={u}_{D}\ \mbox{ on }\ \gamma_{D},\ v_{n}=0\ \mbox{ on }\ \gamma_{S}\right\}.

We denote by
V_{0}
the space
V_{u_{D}}
with
u_{D}=0
on
\gamma_{D}
.
The weak formulation of problem (
1
) attains the following form:

\left.\begin{aligned} &\text{Find}({u},p)\in V_{{u}_{D}}\times L^{2}(\Omega)\ \text{such that for all}\ ({v},q)\in V_{0}\times L^{2}(\Omega)\!\ \\
&a_{\kappa}({u},{v}-{u})+b(p,{v}-{u})+j({v})-j({u})\geq l({v}-{u}),\\
&b(q,{u})=0.\end{aligned}\right\}

(8)

It is thus a variational inequality of the second kind coupled with an equality constraint that guarantees the fulfillment of the second equation in (
1
).

The existence and uniqueness of a weak solution follow from the next theorem (
[
12
]
). The weak formulation described in the paper is equivalent to (
8
).

Theorem 1
.

Let
f\in(L^{2}(\Omega))^{3},\ \sigma_{N}\in\left(L^{2}(\gamma_{N})\right)^{3}
, and
g,\kappa\in L^{\infty}(\gamma_{S})
,
g\geq 0
,
\kappa\geq 0
. Then the solution
(u,p)
to (
8
) exists, and the velocity component
u
is unique. If
\gamma_{N}\neq\emptyset
, then the pressure component
p
is unique as well, and it is defined up to an additive constant.

2.2
Mixed finite element method

We approximate problem (
8
) using the mixed finite element method
[
4
]
with the P1-bubble/P1 finite element pair (also called MINI) introduced by Arnold, Brezzi, and Fortin
[
1
]
. This pair is known for satisfying the
\inf
-
\sup
stability Babuška-Brezzi condition
[
11
,
23
]
, good approximation property, and a small degree of freedom. Matrices and vectors will be assembled using vectorized codes proposed by J. Koko in
[
16
]
and their improved versions from
[
18
]
.

Let
\mathcal{T}^{h}
be a regular partition of
\overline{\Omega}
and consider its segment
T\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
. By
h>0
, we denote the largest diameter of
T\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
.
N(\Omega)=\{{x}_{1},\dots,{x}_{n_{p}}\}
is the set of all nodes of
\mathcal{T}^{h}
belonging to
\overline{\Omega}
, with the cardinality (size)
n_{p}=|N(\Omega)|
. Let us introduce integers
n_{d}=|N(\gamma_{D})|
,
n_{s}=|N(\gamma_{S})|
, where
N(\gamma_{D})=\{{x}_{i}\in N(\Omega):{x}_{i}\in\overline{\gamma}_{D}\}
, and
N(\gamma_{S})=\{{x}_{i}\in N(\Omega):{x}_{i}\in\overline{\gamma}_{S}\setminus\overline{\gamma}_{D}\}
, respectively. Finally,
n_{u}=|N(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\overline{\gamma}_{D})|
, and
n_{t}
is the number of all
T\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
.

The piecewise linear Courant basis functions
\phi_{i}:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\dabR
, are associated with each vertex of
T\in\mathcal{T}^{h}
such that
\phi_{i}({x}_{j})=\delta_{ij}
,
1\leq i,j\leq n_{p}
. These functions generate the space

W^{h}=\left\{v^{h}\in C(\overline{\Omega}):v^{h}_{|_{T}}\in P^{1}(T)\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}^{h}\right\},\quad\dim W^{h}=n_{p}.

The bubble functions
\phi_{b}^{T}:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\dabR
, which are non-zero only in one
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, are defined as follows:

\phi_{b}^{T}=4^{4}\prod_{j=1}^{4}\phi_{i(j)},

where
\{{x}_{i(1)},{x}_{i(2)},{x}_{i(3)},{x}_{i(4)}\}
are the vertices of
T
. These functions generate the space

B^{h}=\left\{v^{h}\in C(\overline{\Omega}):\ v^{h}_{|T}=c_{T}\phi_{b}^{T},\ c_{T}\in\dabR\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}^{h}\right\},\quad\dim B^{h}=n_{t}.

With the above-introduced two spaces, we associate the following spaces:

\displaystyle V^{h}

\displaystyle=\left(W^{h}\oplus B^{h}\right)^{3},

\displaystyle V^{h}_{{u}_{D}}

\displaystyle=\left\{{v}^{h}\in V^{h}:{v}^{h}({x}_{i})={u}_{D}({x}_{i})\quad\forall{x}_{i}\in N(\gamma_{D}),\ v^{h}_{n}({x}_{i})=0\quad\forall{x}_{i}\in N(\gamma_{S})\right\},

\displaystyle V^{h}_{0}

\displaystyle=V^{h}_{{u}_{D}}\ \text{with}\ {u}_{D}=0,\quad\dim V^{h}_{{u}_{D}}=\dim V^{h}_{0}=3(n_{u}+n_{t}).

The mixed finite element approximation of the problem (
8
) reads now as follows:

\left.\begin{aligned} &\text{Find }({u}^{h},p^{h})\in V^{h}_{{u}_{D}}\times W^{h}\ \text{such that for all}\ ({v}^{h},q^{h})\in V^{h}_{0}\times W^{h}\!\ \\
&a_{\kappa}({u}^{h},{v}^{h}-{u}^{h})+b(p^{h},{v}^{h}-{u}^{h})+j({v}^{h})-j({u}^{h})\geq l({v}^{h}-{u}^{h}),\\
&b(q^{h},{u}^{h})=0.\end{aligned}\right\}

(9)

2.3
Algebraic formulation

The vectorized assembly functions of the finite element matrices and vectors representing the forms
a_{\kappa}
,
b
, and
l
are described in
[
18
,
17
]
. Let us note that during matrix construction, bubble components at the element level are effectively eliminated.

We introduce first the algebraic vectors

{u}=\begin{pmatrix}{u}^{h}({x}_{1})\\
\vdots\\
{u}^{h}({x}_{n_{u}})\end{pmatrix}\in\dabR^{3n_{u}}\text{, and}\quad{p}=\begin{pmatrix}p^{h}({x}_{1})\\
\vdots\\
p^{h}({x}_{n_{p}})\end{pmatrix}\in\dabR^{n_{p}}

of the nodal velocities and pressure components, and the index set
\mathcal{N}:=\{1,2,\ldots,n_{s}\}
, which is the index set of the nodes lying on
\overline{\gamma}_{S}\setminus\overline{\gamma}_{D}
. The index set of the remaining nodes is denoted by
\mathcal{I}:=\{n_{s}+1,\ldots,n_{u}\}
.

Problem (
9
) can be written as searching a solution
({u},{p})\in\dabR^{3n_{u}}\times\dabR^{n_{p}}
of the generalized equation (GE)

0\in F({u},{p})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{\kappa}&B^{\mathsf{T}}\\
B&-E\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{u}\\
{p}\end{array}\right)-\left(\begin{array}[]{c}b\\
c\end{array}\right)+\partial q({u},{p}),

(10)

with

q({u},{p})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}}\left(g_{i}||T^{i}{u}^{i}||+\delta_{S^{i}}({u}^{i})\right),\quad S^{i}:=\left\{{v}\in\dabR^{3}:N^{i}{v}=0\right\},

where the indicator function
\delta_{S}
is given by

\delta_{S}({x})=\left\{\begin{aligned} 0\quad\text{if }{x}\in S,\cr\infty\quad\text{if }{x}\notin S,\end{aligned}\right.

and ensures thus the impermeability condition.

The matrix
A_{\kappa}\in\dabR^{3n_{u}\times 3n_{u}}
is nonsingular, symmetric and positive definite and
B\in\dabR^{n_{p}\times 3n_{u}}
has the full row-rank. Further, the matrix
E\in\dabR^{n_{p}\times n_{p}}
and the vector
c\in\dabR^{n_{p}}
are products of the bubble compoments elimination. Moreover,
E
is symmetric and positive semidefinite.

The matrices
T^{i}
and
N^{i}
are
2\times 3
and
1\times 3
matrices, respectively, containing the two tangents and the normal to the boundary at the i-th node. Further,
g_{i}=\mu({x}_{i})g({x}_{i})
, where
\mu({x}_{i})
is the measure of the element. Note that the
3\times 3
matrix

Q^{i}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}T^{i}\\
N^{i}\end{array}\right)

must be orthogonal. Note that due to
[
13
]
the generalized equation GE (
10
) has a unique solution.

3
SCD semismooth Newton* method

After substituting
{p}=-\hat{p}
, problem (
10
) can be written as finding a solution
({u},\hat{p})
of the GE

0\in F({u},\hat{p})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{\kappa}&-B^{\mathsf{T}}\\
B&E\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{u}\\
\hat{p}\end{array}\right)-\left(\begin{array}[]{c}b\\
c\end{array}\right)+\partial q({u},\hat{p}).

(11)

By setting
x:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u\\
\hat{p}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
m:=n_{u}+n_{p}
and

H(x):=\begin{pmatrix}A_{\kappa}&-B^{\mathsf{T}}\\
B&E\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
\hat{p}\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}b\\
c\end{pmatrix},

the GE (
11
) attains the form

0\in F(x)=H(x)+\partial q(x)

(12)

with an affine linear operator
H:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and a proper convex lower semicontinuous (lsc) function
q:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
. Since for all
x_{i}=(u_{i},\hat{p}_{i})
,
i=1,2
, we have

\langle H(x_{1})-H(x_{2}),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle=\langle A_{\kappa}(u_{1}-u_{2}),u_{1}-u_{2}\rangle+\langle E(\hat{p}_{1}-\hat{p}_{2}),\hat{p}_{1}-\hat{p}_{2}\rangle\geq 0,

mapping
H
is maximally monotone.

In order to solve problems of this type one may apply a globally convergent algorithm based on the SCD semismooth
∗
Newton method as presented in the recent paper by Gfrerer, Outrata and Valdman
[
7
]
. Let us briefly describe this method, which is based on the SC (subspace containing) derivative introduced in
[
9
]
. In what follows, we will extensively work with
m
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Note that for two
m\times m
matrices
A,B
the subspace

{\rm rge\;}(A,B):=\{(Ap,Bp)\,\big|\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}

has dimension
m
if and only if the
2m\times m
matrix
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A\\
B\end{smallmatrix}\right)
has full column rank
m
.

If a single-valued mapping
G:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is Fréchet differentiable at a point
\bar{x}
, the tangent cone to the graph of
G
at
(\bar{x},G(\bar{x}))
is the
m
-dimensional subspace
{\rm rge\;}(I,\nabla G(\bar{x}))
. This observation is the motivation for the following definition.

Definition 1
.

The set-valued mapping
G:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
is called
graphically smooth of dimension
m
at
(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,G
, if the tangent cone
T_{\mbox{\rm gph}\,G}(x,y)
is an
m
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Furthermore,
{\cal O}_{G}
denotes the set of all points
(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,G
such that
G
is graphically smooth of dimension
m
at
(x,y)
.

Next, we consider the metric space
{\cal Z}_{m}
of all
m
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, equipped with the metric

d_{{\cal Z}}(L_{1},L_{2}):=\|P_{1}-P_{2}\|\,,

where
P_{i}
,
i=1,2
denote the orthogonal projections onto the subspaces
L_{i}
.

Given a subspace
L\in{\cal Z}_{m}
, we define its adjoint subspace
L^{*}\in{\cal Z}_{m}
by

L^{*}=\{(v^{*},u^{*})\,\big|\,(u^{*},-v^{*})\in L^{\perp}\}.

Note that
(L^{*})^{*}=L
and
d_{\cal Z}(L_{1},L_{2})=d_{\cal Z}(L_{1}^{*},L_{2}^{*})
.

Next consider the definition of
subspace containing (SC) derivatives
.

Definition 2
.

Let
G:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a set-valued mapping. Then:

1.

The
subspace containing (SC) derivative

{\cal S}G:\mbox{\rm gph}\,G\rightrightarrows{\cal Z}_{m}
of
G
is defined by

\displaystyle{\cal S}G(x,y):=\{L\in{\cal Z}_{m}\,\big|\,\exists\,(x_{k},y_{k})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{{{\cal O}_{G}}}(x,y):\,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}d_{\cal Z}\big(L,T_{\mbox{\rm gph}\,G}(x_{k},y_{k})\big)

\displaystyle=0\}.

2.

The
adjoint SC derivative

{\cal S}^{*}G:\mbox{\rm gph}\,G\rightrightarrows{\cal Z}_{m}
of
G
is given by

{\cal S}^{*}G(x,y)=\{L^{*}\,\big|\,L\in{\cal S}G(x,y)\}.

3.

The mapping
G
is said to have the
subspace containing derivative (SCD) property at

(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,G
, if there holds
{\cal S}G(x,y)\neq\emptyset
. Furthermore, we say that
G
has the SCD property
around

(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,G
, if there is a neighborhood
W
of
(x,y)
such that
G
has the SCD property at every
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,G\cap W
. Finally, we call
G
an
SCD mapping
, if
G
has the SCD property at every point of its graph.

The SC derivative may be considered as an extension of the so-called
B-Jacobian (B-differential)
from single-valued to set-valued mappings. Given a mapping
H:D\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
open, let
{\cal O}_{H}
denote the set of all points belonging to
D
where
H
is Fréchet  differentiable. Then the B-Jacobian at a point
x\in D
is given by

\overline{\nabla}H(x):=\{A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}\,\big|\,\exists x_{k}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{{\cal O}_{H}}x:\ A=\lim_{k\to\infty}\nabla H(x_{k})\}.

It follows from
[
9
, Lemma 3.11]
that

{\cal S}H(x,H(x))\supset\{{\rm rge\;}(I,A)\,\big|\,A\in\overline{\nabla}H(x)\}\,,\quad\forall\,x\in D\,,

and equality holds for all
x
such that
H
is Lipschitz continuous near
x
.

The set-valued mappings we are dealing with in this paper, are subdifferentials of convex lsc functions and for such mappings the SC derivative has a special structure. Further, the subspaces contained in the SC derivative offer a certain particularly useful basis representation.

Definition 3
.

We denote by
{\cal Z}_{m}^{P,W}
the collection of all subspaces
L\in{\cal Z}_{m}
such that there are symmetric
m\times m
matrices
P
and
W
with the following properties:

(i)

L={\rm rge\;}(P,W)
,

(ii)

P^{2}=P
, i.e.,
P
represents the orthogonal projection onto some subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

iii)

W(I-P)=I-P
.

Note that for symmetric matrices
W,P
fulfilling conditions (ii) and (iii) of the above definition we have

\displaystyle(I-P)W=\big(W(I-P)\big)^{T}=(I-P)^{T}=I-P=W(I-P),

(13)

\displaystyle PW=WP=PWP,

(14)

\displaystyle W=WP+W(I-P)=PWP+(I-P).

(15)

By
[
10
, Lemma 3.3]
, subspaces

are self adjoint, i.e.,
L=L^{*}
. Since lsc convex functions are both prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous (
[
24
, Chapter 8]
), we may combine
[
9
, Corollary 3.2]
and
[
10
, Proposition 3.6]
to obtain the following statement.

Proposition 2
.

For every lsc proper convex function
q:R^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
the subdifferential mapping
\partial q
is an SCD mapping and for every
(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial q
there holds
{\cal S}(\partial q)(x,x^{*})={\cal S}^{*}(\partial q)(x,x^{*})\subset{\cal Z}_{m}^{P,W}
.

In what follows, we denote for a convex lsc function
q:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and a pair
(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial q
by
{\cal M}_{P,W}\partial q(x,x^{*})
the collection of all symmetric
m\times m
matrices
P
and
W
fulfilling conditions (ii) and (iii) of Definition
3
and such that
{\rm rge\;}(P,W)\in{\cal S}(\partial q)(x,x^{*})
. By convexity of
q
,
W
is positive semidefinite for every
(P,W)\in{\cal M}_{P,W}\partial q(x,x^{*})
. Further note that for twice differentiable functions
q
we have
{\cal M}_{P,W}\partial q(x,\nabla q(x))=\{(I,\nabla^{2}q(x))\}
.

Finally we need the definition of SCD semismoothness.

Definition 4

(
[
9
, Definition 5.1]
)
.

We say that
G:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
SCD semismooth
∗
at

(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,G
if
G
has the SCD property around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and
for every
\epsilon>0
there is some
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle|\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y^{*},y-\bar{y}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\|(x,y)-(\bar{x},\bar{y})\|\|(x^{*},y^{*})\|

(16)

holds for all
(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,G\cap{\cal B}_{\delta}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and all
(y^{*},x^{*})\in L^{*}
,
L^{*}\in{\cal S}^{*}G(x,y)
.

It can be easily shown that
G
is SCD semismooth
∗
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if and only if

\lim_{(x,y)\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mbox{\rm gph}\,G}(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\frac{\sup\{{\rm dist}((\bar{x}-x,\bar{y}-y),L)\,\big|\,L\in{\cal S}G(x,y)\}}{\|(\bar{x}-x,\bar{y}-y)\|}=0,

(17)

cf.
[
10
, Proposition 2.12]
.

Many mappings appearing in applications are SCD semismooth
∗
. For instance, if
\mbox{\rm gph}\,G
is a closed subanalytic set then it follows from a result of Jourani
[
15
]
, that
G
is SCD semismooth
∗
at every point belonging to its graph.

Let us now briefly describe the SCD semismooth
∗
Newton method for solving problem (
12
), where we formulate the method for continuously differentiable mappings
H
. Let
\bar{x}
denote a solution to (
12
) and assume that
\partial q
is SCD semismooth
∗
at
(\bar{x},-H(\bar{x}))\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial q
. Consider an iterate
{x}^{(k)}
close to
\bar{x}
. In a first step, the so-called
approximation step
, we compute a pair
({z}^{(k)},{z^{*}}^{(k)})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial q
by

{z}^{(k)}:={\rm prox}_{\lambda q}({x}^{(k)}-\lambda H({x}^{(k)})),\quad{z^{*}}^{(k)}:=\frac{{x}^{(k)}-{z}^{(k)}}{\lambda}-H({x}^{(k)}),

where
\lambda>0
is a fixed parameter and

{\rm prox}_{\varphi}(x):=\arg\min_{z}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\|z-x\|^{2}+\varphi(z)\right\}

denotes the
proximal mapping
of an lsc function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. Since
q
is convex lsc,
\partial(\lambda q)
is maximally monotone and thus both
{\rm prox}_{\lambda q}
and
Q:=Id-{\rm prox}_{\lambda q}
are single-valued and nonexpansive by Minty’s theorem, cf.

[
24
, Theorem 12.15]
. Note that the computation of
{z}^{(k)}
amounts exactly to one step of the well-known
forward-backward splitting method
, see, e.g.,
[
2
]
. Together with the identities
\bar{x}={\rm prox}_{\lambda q}(\bar{x}-\lambda H(\bar{x}))
,
H(\bar{x})=-Q(\bar{x}-\lambda H(\bar{x}))/\lambda
and
{z^{*}}^{(k)}=Q({x}^{(k)}-\lambda H({x}^{(k)}))/\lambda
and
we obtain the bounds

\|{z}^{(k)}-\bar{x}\|\leq\|{x}^{(k)}-\lambda H({x}^{(k)})-(\bar{x}-\lambda H(\bar{x}))\|\leq(1+\lambda l)\|{x}^{(k)}-\bar{x}\|

and

\displaystyle\|{z^{*}}^{(k)}+H(\bar{x})\|

\displaystyle=\|Q({x}^{(k)}-\lambda H({x}^{(k)}))-Q(\bar{x}-\lambda H(\bar{x}))\|/\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\|{x}^{(k)}-\lambda H({x}^{(k)})-(\bar{x}-\lambda H(\bar{x}))\|/\lambda\leq(\frac{1}{\lambda}+l)\|{x}^{(k)}-\bar{x}\|,

where
l
denotes the Lipschitz constant of
H
in some ball around
\bar{x}
containing the point
{x}^{(k)}
.
Next we choose an arbitrary pair

({P}^{(k)},{W}^{(k)})\in{\cal M}_{P,W}\partial q({z}^{(k)},{z^{*}}^{(k)}).

Then, by taking into account (
17
), there is some
{p}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

\displaystyle\|(\bar{x}-{z}^{(k)}-{P}^{(k)}{p}^{(k)},-H(\bar{x})-{z^{*}}^{(k)}-{W}^{(k)}{p}^{(k)})\|

(18)

\displaystyle={\rm dist}((\bar{x}-{z}^{(k)},-H(\bar{x})-{z^{*}}^{(k)}),{\rm rge\;}({P}^{(k)},{W}^{(k)}))

\displaystyle=o(\|({z}^{(k)}-\bar{x},{z^{*}}^{(k)}+H(\bar{x}))\|)=o(\|{x}^{(k)}-\bar{x}\|).

By differentiability of
H
we have

H({x}^{(k)})-H(\bar{x})+\nabla H({x}^{(k)})(\bar{x}-{x}^{(k)})=o(\|{x}^{(k)}-\bar{x}\|)

and, since we can derive from (
18
) the relationships
\bar{x}-{x}^{(k)}={z}^{(k)}-{x}^{(k)}+{P}^{(k)}{p}^{(k)}+o(\|{x}^{(k)}-\bar{x}\|)
and

-H(\bar{x})={z^{*}}^{(k)}+{W}^{(k)}{p}^{(k)}+o(\|{x}^{(k)}-\bar{x}\|)
, the equation

H({x}^{(k)})+{z^{*}}^{(k)}+{W}^{(k)}{p}^{(k)}+\nabla H({x}^{(k)})({z}^{(k)}-{x}^{(k)}+{P}^{(k)}{p}^{(k)})=o(\|{x}^{(k)}-\bar{x}\|)

follows. By multiplying with
{P}^{(k)}
and by using (
14
) and the fact that
{P}^{(k)}
represents an orthogonal projection, we obtain that

\displaystyle\big({P}^{(k)}\nabla H({x}^{(k)}){P}^{(k)}+{W}^{(k)}\big){P}^{(k)}{p}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=-{P}^{(k)}\big(H({x}^{(k)})+\nabla H({x}^{(k)})({z}^{(k)}-{x}^{(k)})+{z^{*}}^{(k)}\big)+o(\|{x}^{(k)}-\bar{x}\|)

\displaystyle=-{P}^{(k)}\big(\nabla H({x}^{(k)})-I/\lambda\big)({z}^{(k)}-{x}^{(k)})+o(\|{x}^{(k)}-\bar{x}\|).

Thus, if the matrix
{P}^{(k)}\nabla H({x}^{(k)}){P}^{(k)}+{W}^{(k)}
is invertible and the norm of its inverse is uniformly bounded for
{x}^{(k)}
close to
\bar{x}
, it follows that the solution
{\triangle z}^{(k)}
of the system

\big({P}^{(k)}\nabla H({x}^{(k)}){P}^{(k)}+{W}^{(k)}\big)\triangle z=-{P}^{(k)}\big(\nabla H({x}^{(k)})-I/\lambda\big)({z}^{(k)}-{x}^{(k)})

(19)

satisfies

\triangle{z}^{(k)}={P}^{(k)}{p}^{(k)}+o(\|{x}^{(k)}-\bar{x}\|)=\bar{x}-{z}^{(k)}+o(\|{x}^{(k)}-\bar{x}\|).

By setting
{x}^{(k+1)}:={z}^{(k)}+\triangle{z}^{(k)}
, we obtain finally that

\|{x}^{(k+1)}-\bar{x}\|=o(\|{x}^{(k)}-\bar{x}\|).

Remark 1
.

By using the properties of
{P}^{(k)}
and
{W}^{(k)}
, it is easy to see that the difference
{\triangle x}^{(k)}:={x}^{(k+1)}-{x}^{(k)}={z}^{(k)}-{x}^{(k)}+{\triangle z}^{(k)}
is a solution of the linear system

\big({P}^{(k)}\nabla H({x}^{(k)})+{W}^{(k)}\big)\triangle x=({W}^{(k)}+{P}^{(k)}/\lambda)({z}^{(k)}-{x}^{(k)}).

(20)

This formula corresponds to the computation of the Newton direction in
[
7
]
, where also other basis representations of subspaces
{L}^{(k)}\in{\cal S}(\partial q)({z}^{(k)},{z^{*}}^{(k)})
are considered.

The following theorem is implied by
[
7

:

Theorem 3
.

Assume that
\bar{x}\in F^{-1}(0)
is a solution to the GE (
12

\partial q

∗

.

In order to globalize the SCD semismooth
∗
Newton method, we use a hybrid technique with a globally convergent method for finding zeros of the sum of two maximally monotone operators, e.g., the well-known Douglas-Rachford method, cf.
[
20

. In our case, one iterate of the Douglas-Rachford method can be written down in the form

Given the iterate

, we perform a line-search along the direction
{\triangle x}^{(k)}
given either by the solution of (
20
) or by the formula

,
where

We refer to

\partial q_{i}(u)=\begin{cases}\emptyset&\mbox{if $N^{i}u\not=0$,}\\

In what follows we frequently use the following properties of

T^{i}{T^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}=I_{2},\ N^{i}{N^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}=1,\ T^{i}{N^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}=0_{2},\ {T^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}T^{i}+{N^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}N^{i}=I_{3}.

Further, the symmetric

Lemma 4
.

For every
\lambda>0
and every
u\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
there holds

{\rm prox}_{\lambda q_{i}}(u)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if $\|T^{i}u\|\leq\lambda g_{i}$,}\\
\Big(1-\frac{\lambda g_{i}}{\|T^{i}u\|}\Big){T^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}T^{i}u&\mbox{else.}\end{cases}

Proof.

By taking into account the relationship
S^{i}=\ker N^{i}={\rm rge\;}{T^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}
, we obtain that
{\rm prox}_{\lambda q_{i}}(u)={T^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}z
with

\displaystyle z\in\mathop{\rm arg\,min\,}\left\{\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|{T^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}z-u\|+g_{i}\|T^{i}{T^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}z\|\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\rm arg\,min\,}\left\{\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|z-T^{i}u\|+g_{i}\|z\|\right\}

\displaystyle={\rm prox}_{\lambda g_{i}\|\cdot\|}(T^{i}u),

where we have used the equality

. By using the well-known formulas for the proximal mapping of the Euclidean norm, see, e.g.

3

∎

In what follows we partition

\displaystyle=\{(u,u^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial q_{i}\,\big|\,u=0,\ \|T^{i}u^{*}\|=g_{i}\}.

Let

\displaystyle T_{\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial q_{i}}(u,u^{*})

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(v,\frac{g_{i}}{\|T^{i}u\|}{T^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}\Big(I_{2}-\frac{(T^{i}u)(T^{i}u)^{\mathsf{T}}}{\|T^{i}u\|^{2}}\Big)T^{i}v+{N^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}w\right)\,\Big|\,N^{i}v=0,w\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

and by taking into account that the orthogonal projections onto

{T^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}T^{i}
and

{N^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}N^{i}

\displaystyle T_{\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial q_{i}}(u,u^{*})

\displaystyle={\rm rge\;}\Big({T^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}T^{i},\frac{g_{i}}{\|T^{i}u\|}{T^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}\Big(I_{2}-\frac{(T^{i}u)(T^{i}u)^{\mathsf{T}}}{\|T^{i}u\|^{2}}\Big)T^{i}+{N^{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}N^{i}\Big)\in{\cal Z}_{d}

Remark 2

.

Next, let
\bar{x}=(\bar{u},\bar{p})
denote the solution to (
11
) and we have to show that
P\nabla H(\bar{x})P+W
is nonsingular for every pair
(P,W)\in{\cal M}_{P,W}\partial q(\bar{x})
. To this aim we partition the matrices
A_{\kappa}
and
B
into

A_{\kappa}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{\mathcal{N}\mathcal{N}}&A_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{N}}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
A_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{N}}&A_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}\end{pmatrix},\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}B_{\mathcal{N}}&B_{\mathcal{I}}\end{pmatrix},

where
A_{\mathcal{N}\mathcal{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3n_{s}\times 3n_{s}}
,
A_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3(n_{u}-n_{s})\times 3n_{s}}
,
A_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3(n_{u}-n_{s})\times 3(n_{u}-n_{s})}
,
B_{\mathcal{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{p}\times 3n_{s}}
and
B_{\mathcal{I}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{p}\times 3(n_{u}-n_{s})}
. Now consider any
(P,W)\in{\cal M}_{P,W}\partial q(\bar{x})
and its representation (
22
). It follows that

P\nabla H(\bar{x})P+W=\begin{pmatrix}P_{\mathcal{N}}A_{\mathcal{N}\mathcal{N}}P_{\mathcal{N}}+W_{\mathcal{N}}&P_{\mathcal{N}}A_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{N}}^{\mathsf{T}}&-P_{\mathcal{N}}B_{\mathcal{N}}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
A_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{N}}P_{\mathcal{N}}&A_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}&-B_{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
B_{\mathcal{N}}P_{\mathcal{N}}&B_{\mathcal{I}}&E\end{pmatrix}.

Consider now the blocks

\hat{A}:=\begin{pmatrix}P_{\mathcal{N}}A_{\mathcal{N}\mathcal{N}}P_{\mathcal{N}}+W_{\mathcal{N}}&P_{\mathcal{N}}A_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{N}}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
A_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{N}}P_{\mathcal{N}}&A_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}\end{pmatrix},\quad\hat{B}:=\begin{pmatrix}B_{\mathcal{N}}P_{\mathcal{N}}&B_{\mathcal{I}}\end{pmatrix}.

We first claim that
\hat{A}
is positive definite. Assume on the contrary that there is a vector
z\not=0
satisfying
z^{\mathsf{T}}\hat{A}z\leq 0
. By setting

z:=\begin{pmatrix}z_{\mathcal{N}}\\
z_{\mathcal{I}}\end{pmatrix},\ \hat{z}:=\begin{pmatrix}P_{\mathcal{N}}z_{\mathcal{N}}\\
z_{\mathcal{I}}\end{pmatrix}

we obtain that

0\geq z^{\mathsf{T}}\hat{A}z=\hat{z}^{\mathsf{T}}A_{\kappa}\hat{z}+z_{\mathcal{N}}^{\mathsf{T}}W_{\mathcal{N}}z_{\mathcal{N}}.

Thus, since
A_{\kappa}
is positive definite and
W_{\mathcal{N}}
is symmetric positive semidefinite, it follows that
z_{\mathcal{I}}=0
,
P_{\mathcal{N}}z_{\mathcal{N}}=0
and
W_{\mathcal{N}}z_{\mathcal{N}}=0
. Hence
z_{\mathcal{N}}=(I-P_{\mathcal{N}})z_{\mathcal{N}}
and, by using (
13
), we obtain that

0=W_{\mathcal{N}}z_{\mathcal{N}}=W_{\mathcal{N}}(I-P_{\mathcal{N}})z_{\mathcal{N}}=(I-P_{\mathcal{N}})z_{\mathcal{N}}=z_{\mathcal{N}}

and consequently
z=0
, a contradiction. This implies that
\hat{A}
is positive definite and it follows that the matrix

P\nabla H(\bar{x})P+W=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{A}&-\hat{B}^{T}\\
\hat{B}&E\end{pmatrix}

is nonsingular if and only if

\hat{B}\hat{A}^{-1}\hat{B}^{\mathsf{T}}+E
is nonsingular. Since
E
is symmetric and positive semidefinite, this is in turn equivalent to the condition

\hat{B}^{\mathsf{T}}s=0,\ Es=0\ \Rightarrow\ s=0.

(25)

Now consider the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}A_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}&-B_{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
B_{\mathcal{I}}&E\end{pmatrix}

(26)

corresponding to the discretized Stokes problem with no-slip boundary conditions which has a unique solution. Hence, the matrix (
26
) is nonsingular and our considerations above imply the condition
B_{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{T}}s=0,Es=0\Rightarrow s=0
. From this we can immediately deduce that (
25
) holds true and therefore the matrix
P\nabla H(\bar{x})P+W
is nonsingular.

We may conclude that all the conditions of Theorem
3
are fulfilled and therefore the SCD semismooth
∗
Newton method converges locally superlinearly to the solution of the Stokes problem with stick-slip boundary conditions.

5
Numerical experiments

The following section shows two examples where the SSSN method has been tested. In the first one, the convergence rate is examined on a simple unit cube region as a function of the mesh size and the settings of the stick-slip condition parameters. In the second example, the behavior of the method for various slip condition parameters is monitored on a more complex Cerebral Aneurysm region.

In our implementation we solved the linear system (

Conclusion

We proposed an algorithm for the numerical solution of the 3D Stokes problem with Navier-Tresca stick-slip boundary conditions based on the recently developed SCD (Subspace Containing Derivative) semismooth* Newton method (SSSN method). This algorithm can also be used to solve the Navier-Stokes problem and the unsteady Navier-Stokes problem after a relatively simple modification of the implementation.

The suggested method has been compared by the authors with the semismooth Newton method presented in
[
12
]
. We used this method for the same examples presented in the last section of the paper. Based on numerical experiments, we report that the SSSN method yields slightly better convergence rates than the semismooth Newton method (
[
12
]
). Further, this method does not require to cast the problem in form of an equation.
