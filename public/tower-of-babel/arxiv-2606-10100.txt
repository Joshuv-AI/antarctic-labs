Static Spherically Symmetric Chaplygin and Polytropic Fluid Solutions in Teleparallel F ⁡ ( T ) F(T) Gravity
A. Landry

Abstract
We investigate static, spherically symmetric (SS) spacetimes in covariant teleparallel F ⁡ ( T ) F(T) gravity sourced by nonlinear Chaplygin and polytropic fluids. Using the covariant coframe/spin-connection (CSC) formalism, we derive the corresponding field equations and conservation laws governing admissible matter distributions and nonlinear torsion sectors. A general reconstruction procedure is developed, allowing the systematic determination of teleparallel F ⁡ ( T ) F(T) models for arbitrary coframe ansätze and fluid equations of state. Focusing on power-law configurations, we obtain several classes of reconstructed solution branches, including constant-radius, compact-object-like, black-hole-like (BH-like), and wormhole-like (WH-like) branches. The Chaplygin sector naturally generates effective dark-energy and exotic-matter regimes capable of supporting traversable wormhole geometries, while the polytropic sector provides physically relevant models for stellar interiors and compact objects. We discuss the associated horizon and throat structures, torsion singularities, energy conditions, and stability properties of the reconstructed branches. The resulting geometries are organized within the Coley–Landry invariant classification framework, highlighting the role of nonlinear torsion corrections in shaping the solution space. Overall, this work provides a unified covariant framework for the construction and interpretation of compact objects, effective cosmological sectors, and regular-core strong-field geometries beyond General Relativity.



I
Introduction

The teleparallel formulation of gravity provides an alternative geometrical description of the gravitational interaction in which torsion, rather than curvature, plays the fundamental role. In this framework, gravity is described by a tetrad or coframe field together with a flat spin connection, leading to the Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (TEGR), which is dynamically equivalent to Einstein’s theory
Aldrovandi2013
;
Hayashi1979
;
Maluf2013
;
Arcos2004
. The torsion scalar
T
replaces the Ricci scalar
R
as the gravitational Lagrangian, thereby providing a geometrically distinct but dynamically equivalent formulation of standard General Relativity in the TEGR limit.

A natural extension of TEGR is obtained by promoting the torsion scalar
to an arbitrary function
F(T)
, giving rise to modified teleparallel gravity

Ferraro2007
;
Linder2010
;
Bengochea2009
. Beyond standard
F(T)

models, several broader classes of modified teleparallel theories have
also been proposed and investigated in recent years

Bahamonde2019
;
Bahamonde2023b
. These theories have attracted considerable attention as viable geometric extensions of General Relativity, particularly in cosmology, where nonlinear torsion corrections may account for the accelerated expansion of the Universe and mimic dynamical dark-energy behaviour
Bahamonde2023
;
Cai2016
;
Capozziello2011
;
Dent2011
;
Copeland2006
. Observational constraints, cosmological perturbations, and large-scale structure formation in
F(T)
gravity have also been widely investigated
Wu2010
;
Li2011
;
Izumi2013
;
Nesseris2013
.

The physical consistency of
F(T)
gravity requires a covariant formulation. Early non-covariant approaches suffered from local Lorentz invariance problems, since the choice of tetrad could introduce spurious frame-dependent constraints. This issue is resolved by the covariant coframe/spin-connection (CSC) formalism, in which the tetrad
h^{a}{}_{\mu}
and the flat spin connection
\omega^{a}{}_{b\mu}
are treated together as the fundamental geometrical structure
Krssak2019
;
Krssak2015
;
Golovnev2021
;
Bahamonde2023b
. This framework consistently separates inertial and gravitational effects and provides the natural setting for constructing physically admissible solutions in nonlinear teleparallel gravity.

Beyond cosmology, the construction of exact static and spherically symmetric (SS) solutions is an important problem in
F(T)
gravity. Unlike General Relativity, where Birkhoff’s theorem strongly restricts the SS solution space, modified teleparallel theories admit a richer set of inequivalent branches. Static SS geometries, anisotropic matter distributions, relativistic stars, and compact-object configurations have been investigated in several contexts
DeBenedictis2022
;
Daouda2012
;
Wang2011
;
Boehmer2011
. Recent work has established covariant reconstruction procedures for
static spherically symmetric teleparallel geometries sourced by
perfect fluids and scalar fields, together with invariant
classification schemes for the resulting solution spaces

Landry2024_fluid
;
Landry2025_scalar
;
Landry2024_spherical
;
Landry2026_electro
;
roberthudsonSSpaper
.
A central objective in modified teleparallel gravity is therefore the
reconstruction of admissible functions
F(T)
from prescribed matter
sources and geometrical ansätze. The present work extends this program to nonlinear Chaplygin and polytropic matter sectors.

An additional tool for organizing these solutions is the invariant classification program for teleparallel geometries. Inspired by Cartan equivalence methods, this approach uses torsion invariants and Cartan scalars to distinguish inequivalent teleparallel spacetimes in a coordinate- and frame-independent manner
Coley2020
;
McNutt2023
;
Coley2024
;
Olver1995
. Such methods are particularly relevant in nonlinear
F(T)
gravity, where different choices of
F(T)
may generate distinct invariant branches even when the metric ansatz has the same symmetry structure. This invariant point of view has already proved useful in the classification of static SS and generalized teleparallel de Sitter geometries
Landry2024_spherical
;
TdSpaper
.

In the present work, we focus on nonlinear matter sources described by Chaplygin and polytropic fluids. Chaplygin-type fluids were introduced as effective dark-energy and unified dark-matter–dark-energy candidates, and they naturally generate negative pressure through equations of state of the form
p=-A/\rho^{\alpha}

Kamenshchik2001
;
Bento2002
;
Bilic2002
;
Benaoum2002
. These properties make them especially relevant for dark-energy-like sectors, regularized compact objects, and wormhole geometries. In particular, Chaplygin fluids have been used to construct traversable wormhole configurations and generalized exotic-matter sources
Lobo2006
;
Kuhfittig2009
;
MorrisThorne1988
;
Visser1995
;
Lobo2017
. Among the most intriguing strong-field applications are traversable
wormhole geometries, which provide a natural testing ground for the
interplay between exotic matter sources and effective torsion
contributions. Chaplygin fluids are particularly attractive in this
context because their negative-pressure equation of state can
naturally support NEC-violating sectors.

Polytropic fluids, on the other hand, provide a standard and physically motivated description of stellar interiors, relativistic fluid spheres, and compact astrophysical objects. Their equation of state,
p=K\rho^{\Gamma}
, has long been used in the modeling of self-gravitating fluid configurations and compact stars
Tooper1964
;
Tooper1965
;
Chandrasekhar1939
. Extensions involving anisotropic polytropes and modified gravity sources further enrich the possible compact-object phenomenology
Herrera2013
;
Harko2011
. Thus, Chaplygin and polytropic fluids offer complementary physical regimes: the former naturally probes exotic or dark-energy-like behaviour, while the latter is suited to regular matter-supported compact configurations.

Consequently, the solutions obtained in this work may be relevant not
only for theoretical investigations of modified gravity, but also for
effective descriptions of compact stars, stellar interiors, regular
cores, and dark-energy-inspired compact configurations.

While perfect-fluid, scalar-field, and other matter sectors have
already been investigated within static spherically symmetric
teleparallel gravity, comparatively little attention has been devoted
to nonlinear fluid equations of state such as Chaplygin and polytropic
models in a fully covariant CSC framework. In particular, the interplay
between nonlinear matter sectors, torsion reconstruction procedures,
and invariant classification schemes remains largely unexplored.

Despite substantial progress on static SS teleparallel solutions, a systematic covariant treatment of Chaplygin and polytropic fluids in nonlinear
F(T)
gravity remains incomplete. In particular, the role of nonlinear fluid equations of state in shaping reconstructed teleparallel sectors, torsion singularities, horizon structures, energy conditions, and invariant classifications has not yet been fully clarified. Moreover, wormhole-like configurations in
F(T)
gravity raise the important question of whether the null-energy-condition violation required at the throat must be carried directly by the physical matter source, or whether it can be shifted partly or entirely to the effective torsion sector

Studying both within a common teleparallel framework allows one to
investigate how different matter sectors influence the reconstruction
and classification of admissible teleparallel
F(T)
models.

Motivated by these considerations, the goal of this work is to develop a covariant analysis of static SS solutions in teleparallel
F(T)
gravity sourced by Chaplygin and polytropic fluids. We adopt the CSC formalism to ensure local Lorentz covariance, derive the corresponding field equations and conservation laws, and construct a reconstruction procedure for admissible nonlinear
F(T)
models. Particular attention is devoted to constant-radius, compact-object-like, black-hole-like, and wormhole-like branches, as well as to their torsion invariants, energy conditions, and stability properties. From a broader perspective, the reconstructed solution space may also be interpreted within the framework of dynamical systems and invariant classification techniques, which provide complementary tools for understanding the global structure of nonlinear teleparallel models

Coley2020

McNutt2023
;
Coley2024

II
Teleparallel Field Equations and Geometry

II.1
Covariant Field Equations with Nonlinear Fluids

We consider the covariant formulation of teleparallel
F(T)
gravity
coupled to a nonlinear fluid source. The matter sector is described by
a fluid Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{m}
:

S=\int d^{4}x\,h\left[\frac{1}{2\kappa}F(T)+\mathcal{L}_{m}\right],

(1)

where
h=\det(h^{a}{}_{\mu})
and
\kappa=8\pi
. For perfect and nonlinear fluid sources, different choices of the
matter Lagrangian may be adopted, such as

L_{m}=-\rho
or
L_{m}=p
. Since the present analysis relies only on the
effective energy-momentum tensor and the conservation law,
the results are independent of the particular fluid Lagrangian
representation. The use of different fluid Lagrangians in modified gravity theories has been discussed extensively in the literature, particularly in the context of non-minimal matter couplings and effective fluid descriptions
Harko2011
.

The symmetric and antisymmetric field equations are

\displaystyle\kappa\Theta_{(ab)}=

\displaystyle F_{T}\mathring{G}_{ab}+F_{TT}S^{\mu}{}_{(ab)}\partial_{\mu}T+\frac{g_{ab}}{2}\left[F-TF_{T}\right],

(2)

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle F_{TT}S^{\mu}{}_{[ab]}\partial_{\mu}T.

(3)

In the TEGR limit
F(T)=T+\beta
, one has
F_{T}=1
and

F_{TT}=0
, and the field equations reduce to the Einstein equations
with an effective cosmological constant
\Lambda_{\rm eff}=-\beta/2
.

The nonlinear fluid source is allowed to be either isotropic or
anisotropic. Its energy-momentum tensor is written as

\Theta^{\mu}{}_{\nu}=\mathrm{diag}\left[-\rho(r),p_{r}(r),p_{t}(r),p_{t}(r)\right].

(4)

The isotropic limit

p_{r}=p_{t}=p
is recovered as a particular case and
will be adopted throughout most of the present work.

Compared with the perfect-fluid sector studied in Ref.
Landry2024_fluid
,
the nonlinear equations of state considered here introduce additional
density-dependent couplings. In contrast with field-theoretic matter models

Landry2025_scalar
;
Landry2026_electro
, the matter dynamics is governed entirely by
the conservation law and the chosen equation of state.

III
Conservation Law Solutions

III.1
Nonlinear Fluid Conservation Law

The matter conservation equation is

\mathring{\nabla}_{\mu}\Theta^{\mu}{}_{\nu}=0.

(10)

For a static spherically symmetric anisotropic fluid, the radial component gives

p_{r}^{\prime}+\frac{A_{1}^{\prime}}{A_{1}}\left(\rho+p_{r}\right)+2\frac{A_{3}^{\prime}}{A_{3}}\left(p_{r}-p_{t}\right)=0.

(11)

For an isotropic nonlinear fluid,
p_{r}=p_{t}=p
, this reduces to

p^{\prime}+\frac{A_{1}^{\prime}}{A_{1}}\left(\rho+p\right)=0.

(12)

When the equation of state is invertible,
\rho=\rho(p)
, Eq. (
12
)
may be formally integrated as

A_{1}(r)=A_{10}\exp\left[-\int^{p}\frac{d\bar{p}}{\rho(\bar{p})+\bar{p}}\right].

(13)

Equation (
12
) provides the master conservation law governing all
nonlinear-fluid sectors considered below. Once an equation of state

p=p(\rho)
is specified, it yields a first-order differential equation
for the density profile. This equation provides the matter-sector constraint replacing the source-specific conservation laws used in other teleparallel models
Landry2024_fluid
.

III.2
Chaplygin Fluid Sector

The generalized Chaplygin equation of state is

p=-\frac{A}{\rho^{\alpha}},\qquad A>0,\qquad 0\leq\alpha\leq 1.

(14)

A related extension is the modified Chaplygin gas,

p=B\rho-A/\rho^{\alpha}
, which interpolates between a barotropic
fluid contribution and a Chaplygin-type negative-pressure sector
Benaoum2002
. The standard Chaplygin gas corresponds to
\alpha=1
,
while
\alpha=0
yields a constant negative-pressure sector.
In the high-density regime
\rho^{\alpha+1}\gg A
, the pressure
contribution becomes negligible compared with the density,

|p|/\rho\ll 1
. Near
\rho^{\alpha+1}\simeq A
it behaves approximately as an effective cosmological-constant sector. This behaviour explains why Chaplygin-type fluids have often been considered as unified dark-matter/dark-energy candidates in cosmology
Kamenshchik2001
;
Bento2002
;
Bilic2002
;
Benaoum2002
.

For an isotropic Chaplygin fluid, the conservation law becomes

-\alpha A\rho^{-\alpha-1}\rho^{\prime}+\frac{A_{1}^{\prime}}{A_{1}}\left(\rho-\frac{A}{\rho^{\alpha}}\right)=0.

(15)

Equivalently,

\rho^{\prime}=\frac{A_{1}^{\prime}}{A_{1}}\frac{\rho\left(\rho^{\alpha+1}-A\right)}{\alpha A}.

(16)

The adiabatic sound speed is

c_{s}^{2}=\frac{dp}{d\rho}=\frac{\alpha A}{\rho^{\alpha+1}}.

(17)

For
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
, positivity of the sound speed is automatic.
The causality condition
c_{s}^{2}\leq 1
imposes additional restrictions
on the admissible density range.

The radial null energy condition is

\rho+p_{r}=\rho-\frac{A}{\rho^{\alpha}}.

(18)

Thus the Chaplygin fluid violates or saturates the radial NEC whenever

\rho^{\alpha+1}\leq A.

(19)

Consequently, low-density regions naturally favour NEC violation,
whereas sufficiently dense configurations behave more like ordinary
matter sources. This behaviour makes Chaplygin fluids particularly
attractive for connecting compact objects, dark-energy-like sectors,
and wormhole-like geometries.

III.3
Polytropic Fluid Sector

The polytropic equation of state is

p=K\rho^{\Gamma},\qquad\Gamma=1+\frac{1}{n_{p}},

(20)

where
K
is the polytropic constant and
n_{p}
is the polytropic index. Typical astrophysical applications involve
n_{p}\approx 1
–
5
,
covering a wide range of stellar-interior and compact-object models. Such polytropic models provide the basis of many classical and relativistic
stellar-structure calculations ranging from white dwarfs to neutron-star
configurations
Tooper1964
;
Tooper1965
;
Chandrasekhar1939
;
Herrera2013
. Common astrophysical values include
n_{p}=1.5
for non-relativistic
degenerate matter,
n_{p}=3
for relativistic degenerate matter, and

n_{p}\simeq 5
for diffuse stellar envelopes.

For an isotropic polytropic fluid, the conservation law gives

K\Gamma\rho^{\Gamma-1}\rho^{\prime}+\frac{A_{1}^{\prime}}{A_{1}}\left(\rho+K\rho^{\Gamma}\right)=0.

(21)

Therefore,

\rho^{\prime}=-\frac{A_{1}^{\prime}}{A_{1}}\frac{\rho+K\rho^{\Gamma}}{K\Gamma\rho^{\Gamma-1}}.

(22)

For a barotropic polytropic fluid, the specific enthalpy contribution is

h(\rho)=\int\frac{dp}{\rho}=\frac{K\Gamma}{\Gamma-1}\rho^{\Gamma-1}.

(23)

This expression corresponds to the barotropic enthalpy contribution
commonly used in stellar-structure models; a fully relativistic
definition may instead involve
\int dp/(\rho+p)

Tooper1964
;
Tooper1965
;
Chandrasekhar1939
. This quantity frequently appears in relativistic stellar structure and stability analyses.

For
K>0
and
\rho>0
, one has

\rho+p=\rho+K\rho^{\Gamma}>0,

(24)

so the polytropic branch is naturally associated with compact-object and
stellar-interior configurations rather than exotic matter.

The adiabatic sound speed is

c_{s}^{2}=\frac{dp}{d\rho}=K\Gamma\rho^{\Gamma-1}.

(25)

Physical configurations generally require
c_{s}^{2}>0
and, in relativistic
units,
c_{s}^{2}\leq 1
.

The Chaplygin and polytropic equations of state may be viewed as
representative examples of exotic and ordinary matter sectors,
respectively. Together they provide a convenient testing ground
for investigating how nonlinear matter sources affect torsion
reconstruction, invariant classification, and the existence of
compact-object and wormhole-like solutions in teleparallel

F(T)

IV.2
Reconstruction in the Constant-Radius Sector

Using the inversion relation

r^{-(b+1)}=\frac{T-T_{0}}{T_{1}},

(34)

T

equations then lead to the reconstruction equation

(T-T_{0})F_{TT}+\gamma(a,b)F_{T}=\Lambda_{\rm fluid}(T),

(35)

where

\gamma(a,b)=\frac{b+1-2a}{b+1}.

(36)

A necessary consistency requirement is that the fluid source

\Lambda_{\rm fluid}(T)
be expressible solely as a function
of
T

density profile, the conservation law, and the chosen coframe
ansatz.

The source function
\Lambda_{\rm fluid}(T)
is determined by combining
the selected equation of state with the conservation law and the
chosen radial ansatz. For the Chaplygin case,

p=-\frac{A}{\rho^{\alpha}},

whereas for the polytropic case,

p=K\rho^{\Gamma}.

(38)

The formal reconstruction solution is

F(T)=\int^{T}d\bar{T}\,(\bar{T}-T_{0})^{-\gamma}\left[C_{1}+\int^{\bar{T}}d\tilde{T}\,(\tilde{T}-T_{0})^{\gamma-1}\Lambda_{\rm fluid}(\tilde{T})\right]+C_{2}.

and

parametrize the homogeneous reconstruction sector and possible effective cosmological-constant
contributions. Different choices of the coframe ansatz or fluid profile may lead to different reconstructed functions

, illustrating the non-uniqueness of the inverse reconstruction problem. Reconstruction procedures of this type are common in modified teleparallel gravity and provide a useful bridge between prescribed matter sectors and admissible torsion functions

IV.3
TEGR Consistency Checks

An important consistency requirement for any reconstructed teleparallel model is the recovery of the Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (TEGR). In this limit,

F(T)=T+\beta,

(40)

with

F_{T}=1,\qquad F_{TT}=0.

(41)

The nonlinear torsion contributions therefore vanish and the field equations reduce to those of General Relativity with an effective cosmological constant

\Lambda_{\rm eff}=-\frac{\beta}{2},

(42)

written in the covariant coframe/spin-connection formalism

Aldrovandi2013
;
Maluf2013
;
Krssak2015
.

Substituting
F_{TT}=0
into Eq. (
35
) yields the TEGR compatibility condition

\Lambda_{\rm fluid}(T)=0,

(43)

which becomes an algebraic compatibility condition relating the fluid sector and the effective cosmological constant contribution.

Consequently, both the constant-radius and areal-radius branches continuously recover the corresponding Einstein-sector solutions. The TEGR limit therefore provides a useful consistency check on the reconstruction procedure, with all deviations from General Relativity encoded in the nonlinear torsion corrections generated by
F_{TT}\neq 0

Aldrovandi2013
;
Maluf2013
;
Krssak2015

VI
Discussion and Conclusion

We have reformulated the static spherically symmetric teleparallel
F(T)

framework by coupling covariant teleparallel
F(T)
gravity to nonlinear
Chaplygin and polytropic fluids. The Chaplygin branch naturally supports dark-energy-like, wormhole-like, and regular-core configurations, while the polytropic branch is better suited to compact-object and stellar-interior models.

The reconstruction strategy follows the same covariant CSC logic used
in previous static spherically symmetric teleparallel models
Krssak2019
;
Krssak2015
;
Landry2024_spherical
;
Landry2024_fluid
;
Landry2025_scalar
;
Landry2026_electro
: once a
CSC pair, a coframe ansatz, and an equation of state are specified,
the reduced field equations determine admissible functions
F(T)
. The main difference is that the matter density is dynamically determined by the nonlinear fluid conservation equation together with the selected equation of state.

From a physical viewpoint, the Chaplygin branch naturally favors
dark-energy-like and wormhole-supporting configurations, whereas the
polytropic branch reproduces the behaviour expected for ordinary compact
objects and stellar interiors. The two sectors therefore probe
complementary regions of the teleparallel solution space.

Geometrically, the reconstructed solutions demonstrate how nonlinear
torsion corrections may significantly alter the invariant structure of
static spherically symmetric spacetimes while preserving covariance under
local Lorentz transformations. These results show that nonlinear fluid equations of state provide a sensitive probe of the reconstructed torsion sector in covariant teleparallel gravity.

The present framework provides a starting point for a systematic classification of nonlinear-fluid compact objects and wormhole-like geometries in covariant teleparallel gravity. Future work may include exact numerical integrations, anisotropic extensions, dynamical collapse models, stability analyses beyond the leading scalar-torsion approximation, and the investigation of analogous nonlinear-fluid sectors in more general teleparallel theories such as
F(T,B)
gravity and related modified torsion frameworks
Bahamonde2019
;
Bahamonde2023b
.
