1.0 Summary
 Multiple land-atmosphere coupling processes influence the spatial distribution of boundary-layer energy,
 moisture, aerosol, or cloud condensation nuclei (CCN) properties. Accurately quantifying the spatial and
 temporal distributions of CCN concentrations is essential for investigating aerosol-cloud interactions and
 reducing uncertainties in aerosol radiative forcing estimates of Earth's system.

 In this U.S. Department of Energy Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM) User Facility field
 campaign, we deployed CCN and aerosol guest instruments at the Bankhead National Forest (BNF) S20
 and S30 sites from June to August 2025. We deployed one CCN instrument and one condensation particle
 counter at each supplementary site (Figure 1). Our measurements aimed to evaluate the impact of the
 spatial and temporal variability of CCN and aerosol properties influenced by local production and
 transport in the BNF region in northern Alabama. The objectives of this campaign were:

 Objective 1: Investigate aerosol properties' spatial and temporal distribution from the M1, S20, and S30
 sites.

 Objective 2: Investigate the spatial and temporal variability in CCN concentrations from the M1, S20,
 and S30 sites.

 Objective 3: Investigate the closure between CCN measurements from the M1, S20, and S30 sites and
 theoretically calculated CCN concentrations using ArcticShark uncrewed aerial system aerosol
 measurements.

 These individual processes will be analyzed holistically to elucidate the important factors controlling the
 spatial and temporal variability in the BNF region.

2.0 Results
 We deployed CCN guest instruments at the S20 and S30 sites. These CCN instruments provided CCN
 concentrations at a supersaturation range (0.1 to 1%). At S20, we deployed a dual-column CCN
 instrument. Here, colA was measuring CCN properties of ambient particles, whereas colB was measuring
 the CCN properties of heat-treated particles. At S30, we deployed a single-column CCN instrument.
 Hourly CCN concentrations from these two supplementary sites were compared against those from the
 M1 site.

 Figure 2 shows the time series of CCN anomalies for the entire campaign. The CCN concentrations
 corresponding to 0.4% supersaturation are used.

 Figure 2 shows daily CCN anomalies from June through August 2025 for M1, S20, and S30. All three
 time series exhibit pronounced synoptic-scale variability with alternating positive and negative anomalies
 throughout the summer. M1 displays the largest amplitude fluctuations, including an extreme positive
 excursion in early June (>1500) and additional peaks approaching ~800–900 in late August. S20 closely
 follows the temporal evolution of M1, capturing the timing and phase of major events but with slightly
 reduced amplitudes, indicating strong correspondence with modest reduction of extremes. In contrast, S30
 exhibits substantially lower variance and smoother behavior, systematically underestimating peak
 magnitudes while retaining the overall temporal structure. Overall, variability decreases from M1 to S20
 to S30, with S20 providing the closest representation of M1 variability and S30 showing pronounced
 attenuation of high-amplitude CCN anomaly events.

 The results indicate strong inter-site temporal correlation implying that large-scale meteorology or the
 regional background is dominating during the summer period. The consistent alignment of peaks and
 troughs across all three time series indicates that large-scale drivers (e.g., synoptic forcing) are shared,
 while differences arise primarily in how strongly each site experiences the aerosol conditions.
