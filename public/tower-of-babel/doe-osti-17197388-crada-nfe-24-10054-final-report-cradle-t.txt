 ABSTRACT

Hearth Labs’ core technology has been commercialized as Q Sense which is currently available as a beta
product. Q Sense generates 3D thermal point clouds which are then analyzed to audit the performance of
existing buildings, improve control systems setpoints and design effective retrofit projects. Integrating the
ORNL-developed Automatic point Cloud Building Envelope Segmentation (Auto-CuBES) algorithm into
Q Sense can improve the geometric analysis of thermal point clouds.

 1. INTRODUCTION

Over 50% of buildings in the US were constructed before the introduction of energy codes. Overclad
panel retrofits, in which a new envelope is constructed over the existing building, are a promising solution
since they minimize occupant disruption and shorten construction time at the job site. In order to design
the retrofit panel layout, modern retrofit construction practices use 3D point cloud data of the building
envelope to obtain the as-built dimensions. However, manual segmentation by a trained professional is
required to identify and measure window openings, door openings, and other architectural features,
making the use of 3D point clouds labor-intensive. The technology developed at Oak Ridge National
Laboratory called Automatic point Cloud Building Envelope Segmentation (Auto-CuBES) algorithm can
significantly reduce the time spent during point cloud segmentation. The Auto-CuBES algorithm inputs a
3D point cloud generated by commonly available surveying equipment and outputs a wire-frame model of
the building envelope. Unsupervised machine learning methods were used to identify facades, windows,
and doors while minimizing the number of calibration parameters. With a scan resolution of 3 mm, the
resulting window dimensions showed a mean absolute error of 4.2 mm compared to manual laser
measurements.

The Auto-CubES can be combined with HearthLabs’ technology to automate the accurate calculation of
surface areas for interior buildings features in order to inform heat transfer properties and detect leaks.

 2. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY

There are two primary components of the analysis: heat transfer and geometry. The existing geometry
analysis and point cloud segmentation is the weakest part of the Q Sense technology and has a substantial
impact on (1) the accuracy and detail of our analysis, (2) the types of analysis we can provide to
customers, and (3) the degree to which the analysis process can be automated.

The proposed goal was to integrate the AutoCubes technology developed by ORNL into Q Sense to
improve the geometric analysis of our thermal point clouds. The AutoCubes technology was developed to
analyze building geometry using conventional point clouds and should be directly applicable to Q Sense
thermal point clouds. Currently, we can segment point clouds and classify different features and objects.
However, the AutoCubes algorithm has the potential to accelerate the segmentation of the point cloud
data and enable the identification of detailed features (such as window frames) in order to improve the
accuracy of Q Sense’s results and enable more advanced analyses and use cases.
