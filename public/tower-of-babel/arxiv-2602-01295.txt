Best-of-Both-Worlds for Heavy-Tailed Markov Decision Processes
Yu Chen, Yuhao Liu, Jiatai Huang, Yihan Du, Longbo Huang

Abstract
We investigate episodic Markov Decision Processes with heavy-tailed feedback (HTMDPs). Existing approaches for HTMDPs are conservative in stochastic environments and lack adaptivity in adversarial regimes. In this work, we propose algorithms HT-FTRL-OM and HT-FTRL-UOB for HTMDPs that achieve Best-of-Both-Worlds (BoBW) guarantees: instance-independent regret in adversarial environments and logarithmic instance-dependent regret in self-bounding (including the stochastic case) environments. For the known transition setting, HT-FTRL-OM applies the Follow-The-Regularized-Leader (FTRL) framework over occupancy measures with novel skipping loss estimators, achieving a 𝒪 ~ ​ ( T 1 / α ) \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{1/\alpha}) regret bound in adversarial regimes and a 𝒪 ⁡ ( log ⁡ T ) \mathcal{O}(\log T) regret in stochastic regimes. Building upon this framework, we develop a novel algorithm HT-FTRL-UOB to tackle the more challenging unknown-transition setting. This algorithm employs a pessimistic skipping loss estimator and achieves a 𝒪 ~ ​ ( T 1 / α + T ) \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{1/\alpha}+\sqrt{T}) regret in adversarial regimes and a 𝒪 ⁡ ( log 2 ⁡ ( T ) ) \mathcal{O}(\log^{2}(T)) regret in stochastic regimes. Our analysis overcomes key barriers through several technical insights, including a local control mechanism for heavy-tailed shifted losses, a new suboptimal-mass propagation principle, and a novel regret decomposition that isolates transition uncertainty from heavy-tailed estimation errors and skipping bias.

Introduction

Markov Decision Process (MDP) is a fundamental framework for modeling sequential decision-making problems, underpinning modern advancements in Reinforcement Learning (RL), operations research, and control systems. Standard RL theory often relies on the assumption that feedback signals (losses or rewards) are bounded or sub-Gaussian, enabling the use of classical concentration inequalities. While convenient for theoretical analysis, this assumption can be violated in real-world scenarios where the feedback is inherently heavy-tailed. In domains such as network traffic routing
(
20
)
, financial asset pricing
(
2
;
1
)
, and image signal processing
(
8
)
, agents frequently encounter extreme events or outliers with non-negligible probability, underscoring the importance of developing robust RL algorithms to handle MDPs with heavy-tailed losses.

Beyond modeling the tail behavior of loss distributions, robust algorithm design must also account for the underlying nature of the environment. This line of research distinguishes between distinct environment types, typically categorized into stochastic and adversarial regimes. In the stochastic regime, losses are generated from stationary distributions, while in the adversarial regime, losses may be arbitrarily chosen by an adversary and may vary over time. Since the nature of the environment is often unknown
a priori
, a desirable property is
Best-of-Both-Worlds
(BoBW), as proposed by
(
4
)
for the multi-armed bandit (MAB) problem. Ideally, without knowing the regime in advance, a BoBW algorithm should simultaneously achieve an optimal instance-dependent regret in stochastic environments (typically logarithmic in
T
) and an instance-independent regret in adversarial environments.

In this paper, we investigate the
Markov Decision Processes with Heavy-Tailed losses
(HTMDPs) under various environmental conditions, including stochastic and adversarial regimes. Unlike standard settings with bounded feedback, the loss
\ell
that occurs in HTMDPs may be unbounded and can take negative values, and we only assume a bounded
\alpha
-moment (
\alpha\in(1,2]
), i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[|\ell|^{\alpha}]\leq\sigma^{\alpha}
for some
\sigma>0
.
Although there is a large body of work studying HTMDPs in tabular and function approximation settings
(
35
;
26
;
34
;
10
;
11
;
19
)
, existing results almost exclusively focus on stochastic environments and primarily provide instance-independent regret guarantees, without providing instance-dependent regret bounds of logarithmic order in the number of episodes
T
(i.e.,
{\mathcal{O}}(\log T)
). Consequently, it remains a crucial problem to design BoBW algorithms for HTMDPs which, without knowing the regime
a priori
, simultaneously attain nearly minimax-optimal instance-independent regret in adversarial regimes and logarithmic instance-dependent regret in stochastic regimes.

Challenges.

Tackling HTMDPs in both adversarial and stochastic environments presents notable obstacles.

i)
Existing BoBW algorithms for MDPs critically depend on concentration arguments under boundedness or sub-Gaussian assumptions to control stability terms and second-order regret components, but these tools break down under heavy-tailed feedback.

ii)
Existing algorithms for HTMDPs are largely developed for stationary stochastic environments and their analyses exploit such structure to control heavy-tailed estimation errors. These guarantees do not extend to adversarially varying losses, and thus cannot be directly used when the regime is unknown
a priori
.

iii)
Unlike bandits, MDPs introduce long-horizon dependence through state transitions, causing heavy-tailed noise to accumulate in value estimation. Moreover, the state-action structure implies huge challenges in the optimal-action elimination procedure, which is the crucial step for achieving a logarithmic stochastic regret bound (see
Section

4.2
).
This leads to the following challenging and important open question:

Can BoBW performance be achieved under HTMDPs?

3.1
Episodic MDPs with Heavy-Tailed Losses

We consider a tabular and episodic finite-horizon Markov decision process (MDP), denoted by
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{S}}\cup\{s_{H+1}\},{\mathcal{A}},{\mathbb{P}},H,T,\{\bm{\nu}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T})
. Here
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
are the state space and action space, respectively.
{\mathbb{P}}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to\Delta({\mathcal{S}}\cup\{s_{H+1}\})
is the transition function where
s_{H+1}
represents the terminal state.
H
is the horizon length.
T
is the number of episodes.
\bm{\nu}_{t}=\{\nu_{t}(s,a)\}_{s\in{\mathcal{S}},a\in{\mathcal{A}}}
is the loss distribution for each episode
t\in[T]
.
We use
S
and
A
to represent the numbers of states and actions, respectively.

Without loss of generality, we assume that the state space
{\mathcal{S}}
can be partitioned into
H
disjoint layers
{\mathcal{S}}=\bigsqcup_{h=1}^{H}{\mathcal{S}}_{h}
, where
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}=\{s_{1}\}
consists of only a fixed initial state. The transition can only happen between adjacent layers, i.e., for any action
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
,
{\mathbb{P}}[\cdot|s_{h},a]
is supported on
{\mathcal{S}}_{h+1}
for all
s_{h}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h}
. Moreover,
{\mathbb{P}}[s_{H+1}\mid s_{H},a]=1
for all
s_{H}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{H}
.

The learner interacts with the MDP for
T
episodes.
In the beginning of each episode
t\in[T]
, the environment chooses a loss distribution
\nu_{t}(s,a)
for each state-action pair
(s,a)
.
1
1

1

\nu_{t}(s,a)
can be fixed in stochastic regimes, or time-varying and history-dependent in adversarial regimes.

In each episode
t
, the learner chooses a policy
\pi_{t}:{\mathcal{S}}\to\Delta({\mathcal{A}})
.
The interaction proceeds as follows: starting in an initial state
s_{1}^{t}=s_{1}
, for each step
h\in[H]
, the learner observes state
s_{h}^{t}
, samples action
a_{h}^{t}\sim\pi_{t}(\cdot|s_{h}^{t})
, observes loss
\ell_{t}(s_{h}^{t},a_{h}^{t})\sim\nu_{t}(s_{h}^{t},a_{h}^{t})
, and the state transitions to
s_{h+1}^{t}\sim{\mathbb{P}}[\cdot|s_{h}^{t},a_{h}^{t}]
.

Heavy-Tailed Losses.
We consider the heavy-tailed loss distributions whose variance may be unbounded. Following previous works
(
3
;
35
)
, we assume that the loss distributions have a bounded
\alpha
-moment.
Specifically, there exist constants
\alpha\in(1,2]
and
\sigma>0
such that for all
t\in[T]
and
(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}
,

\mathbb{E}_{\ell\sim\nu_{t}(s,a)}[|\ell|^{\alpha}]\leq\sigma^{\alpha}.

This assumption covers both the finite variance case (
\alpha=2
) and infinite variance case (
1<\alpha<2
). We note that we
do not
need further assumptions on the loss distribution, which allows the loss to be negative.

Regret Metric.
We first define the value and Q-value functions for policy
\pi
and transition kernel
{\mathbb{P}}
. Given loss functions
\bm{\ell}=\{\ell(s,a)\}_{s\in{\mathcal{S}},a\in{\mathcal{A}}}
, for any step
h\in[H]
and
(s_{h},a)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h}\times{\mathcal{A}}
, we define

\displaystyle Q^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}(s_{h},a;\bm{\ell})

\displaystyle:=\!\ell(s_{h},a)\!+\!\!\!\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h+1}}{\mathbb{P}}[s^{\prime}|s_{h},a]V^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}(s^{\prime};\bm{\ell}),

\displaystyle V^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}(s_{h};\bm{\ell})

\displaystyle:=\!\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\pi(a|s_{h})Q^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}(s_{h},a;\bm{\ell}),

with
Q^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}(s_{H+1},a;\bm{\ell}):=0
and
V^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}(s_{H+1};\bm{\ell}):=0
. Then,
\mathbb{E}[V^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi_{t}}(s_{1};\bm{\ell}_{t})]
represents the expected loss of policy
\pi_{t}
in episode
t
, where the expectation is taken over the randomness of the loss distributions
\bm{\ell}_{t}\sim\bm{\nu}_{t}
.
The performance of the learner is measured by the regret against a fixed deterministic benchmark policy
{\mathring{\pi}}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}_{T}({\mathring{\pi}})=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(V^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi_{t}}(s_{1};\bm{\ell}_{t})-V^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s_{1};\bm{\ell}_{t})\right)\right].

We choose
{\mathring{\pi}}
to be the optimal deterministic policy that minimizes the expected cumulative loss
\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}V^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s_{1};\bm{\ell}_{t})\right]
.

Occupancy Measure.
We further introduce the state-action occupancy measure
\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}
, given transition kernel
{\mathbb{P}}
and policy
\pi
. In step
h\in[H]
, we denote the probability of visiting state-action pair
(s_{h},a)
with policy
\pi
under
{\mathbb{P}}
as
\rho^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}(s_{h},a)
for any
s_{h}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h}
and
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Therefore, we can write
J_{t}^{\mathbb{P}}(\pi)=\mathbb{E}[\sum_{s\in{\mathcal{S}},a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\rho^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}(s,a)\ell_{t}(s,a)]
.
Moreover, for any fixed
{\mathbb{P}}
, we define the occupancy measure set
{\mathcal{Q}}({\mathbb{P}})
as a convex polytope defined as:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}({\mathbb{P}}):=\left\{\bm{\rho}\geq 0:\exists\pi\ \ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \ \bm{\rho}=\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}\right\}.

(1)

For each feasible occupancy measure
\bm{\rho}\in{\mathcal{Q}}({\mathbb{P}})
, we denote
\rho(s)=\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\rho(s,a)
. Then it corresponds to a unique policy
\pi
under
{\mathbb{P}}
, which can be derived by
\pi(a|s)=\rho(s,a)/\sum_{a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}\rho(s,a^{\prime})
.

3.2
Adversarial and Self-Bounding Regimes.

In this section, we introduce the regimes considered in this paper. Note that our algorithms do not have any prior knowledge of regime types.

Adversarial Regime.

In the adversarial setting, the environment may choose
\bm{\nu}_{t}
adaptively: for each episode
t
, the loss distributions
\bm{\nu}_{t}
are allowed to be
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable.

Self-Bounding Regime.

To model benign (e.g., stochastic) or nearly benign environments, we adopt the following self-bounding condition, which is standard in the BoBW literature
(
16
;
37
;
33
)
.

Definition 3.1

(Self-Bounding Constraint)
.

An environment satisfies the
({\mathring{\pi}},\Delta,C_{\mathrm{sb}})
-self-bounding constraint if there exists a gap function
\Delta:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
\Delta(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))=0
for all
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
and a constant
C_{\mathrm{sb}}\geq 0
, such that the regret against
{\mathring{\pi}}
is lower bounded by the cumulative expected gap:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}_{T}({\mathring{\pi}})\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s\in{\mathcal{S}},a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\rho^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi_{t}}(s,a)\Delta(s,a)\right]\!-\!C_{\mathrm{sb}}.

Intuitively, the above inequality states that up to an additive constant, the regret must be paid for sampling suboptimal state-action pairs, quantified by the gap function
\Delta
.
The (i.i.d.) stochastic regime, where
\bm{\nu}_{t}\equiv\bm{\nu}
and losses are drawn independently across episodes, satisfies this condition with
C_{\mathrm{sb}}=0
by taking
{\mathring{\pi}}
as an optimal policy.
Moreover, the stochastic regime with adversarial corruptions (with total corruption budget
C_{\mathrm{corr}}
) also falls into this framework
(
24
)
, and one can take
C_{\mathrm{sb}}={\mathcal{O}}(C_{\mathrm{corr}})
with the same gap function.

4.1
Algorithm and Results with Known Transition

We introduce algorithm
HT-FTRL-OM
(
Algorithm

1
) for HTMDPs with known transitions. Our approach is built upon the FTRL framework over the polytope
{\mathcal{Q}}({\mathbb{P}})
equipped with a
1/\alpha
-Tsallis entropy regularizer. Crucially, we design a novel skipping loss estimator tailored for occupancy measures. While skipping techniques have been explored in heavy-tailed bandits
(
9
;
33
)
, our design marks the first successful adaptation of these mechanisms to HTMDPs, enabling robust estimation in the presence of state-transition structures.

Algorithm 1

HT-FTRL-OM

0:

Heavy-tail parameters
\alpha
and
\sigma
, skipping threshold constant
C>0
, and learning rate constant
\beta>0
.

1:

for

t=1,2,\dots,T

do

2:

Compute occupancy distribution

{\bm{x}}_{t}\leftarrow\argmin_{{\bm{x}}\in{\mathcal{Q}}({\mathbb{P}})}\left\langle{\bm{x}},\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}\left(\widetilde{\bm{\ell}}_{t^{\prime}}^{{\text{skip}}}-{\bm{b}}_{t^{\prime}}^{\text{skip}}\right)\right\rangle+\Psi_{t}({\bm{x}}),

(2)

where
{\mathcal{Q}}({\mathbb{P}})
is the occupancy measure defined in
Equation

1
and
\Psi_{t}({\bm{x}})=-\frac{1}{\eta_{t}}\sum_{(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}}x(s,a)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
is the
1/\alpha
-Tsallis entropy regularizer with inverse learning rate
\eta_{t}:=\beta/(\sigma\cdot t^{1/\alpha})
.

3:

Derive policy
\pi_{t}(a|s)\leftarrow x_{t}(s,a)/\sum_{a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}x_{t}(s,a^{\prime})
.

4:

Roll out episode

using
\pi_{t}
, and observe trajectory

\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s,a)\leftarrow\frac{{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s,a)}{{x_{t}(s,a)}}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{t}[(s,a)].

(3)

Here

4.2
Challenges and Core Technical Insights

Our results are accomplished by integrating the FTRL framework over occupancy measures and self-bounding techniques
(
23
;
36
;
15
)
with the loss tuning technique in heavy-tailed bandits with Tsallis entropy regularizer
(
9
;
33
)
.
However, extending these methods to heavy-tailed MDPs faces significant challenges due to the interaction between the state-action structure of occupancy measures and the heavy-tailed nature of the losses. Below we provide a roadmap of our analysis and highlight the core technical insights.

Following the standard FTRL analysis, we decompose the regret into three terms: (i) a
stability term
that captures the stability of the FTRL update, (ii) a
penalty term
that arises from the time-varying regularizer
\Psi_{t}
, and (iii) a
skipping error
that accounts for the bias introduced by loss truncation.

The central challenge is to bound these three terms while achieving both the adversarial rate
{\mathcal{O}}(T^{1/\alpha})
and self-bounding rate
{\mathcal{O}}(\log T)
. To address these challenges, we develop two core technical contributions: (1) a novel
heavy-tailed loss shifting
analysis for bounding the stability term under heavy-tailed noise, and (2) a
suboptimal mass propagation
lemma for bounding the penalty and skipping terms by restricting to suboptimal actions only.

Heavy-Tailed Loss Shifting.

Loss shifting is a powerful technique proposed by
16
which considers a shifted loss function

\displaystyle\ell^{\text{shift}}_{t}(s,a):=Q^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi_{t}}(s,a;\bm{\ell})-V^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi_{t}}(s;\bm{\ell}),

instead of the original loss
\ell_{t}(s,a)
as an invariant transformation to maintain the FTRL update rule:
\argmin_{{\bm{x}}\in{\mathcal{Q}}({\mathbb{P}})}\left\langle{\bm{x}},\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}{\bm{\ell}}_{t^{\prime}}^{{\text{shift}}}\right\rangle+\Psi_{t}({\bm{x}})
is exactly the same as
\argmin_{{\bm{x}}\in{\mathcal{Q}}({\mathbb{P}})}\left\langle{\bm{x}},\sum_{t^{\prime}<t}{\bm{\ell}}_{t^{\prime}}\right\rangle+\Psi_{t}({\bm{x}})
.
This shifting enables lower variance control over the shifted loss functions and simplifies the analysis of the FTRL update over occupancy measures by controlling the second-moment of the shifted loss to a probability term locally (e.g.,
(1-\pi_{t}(a|s))/x_{t}(s,a)
in
16
).

However, standard analyses for shifted losses
(
16
;
12
)
heavily rely on the properties of bounded losses to establish the pointwise and second-moment control of losses, which fails under heavy-tailed noise. Moreover, simply combining the skipping loss tuning with the loss shifting technique
(
9
)
does not solve the problem. Since each skipping loss
\ell^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s,a)
is bounded by
\tau_{t}(s,a)={\mathcal{O}}(t^{1/\alpha}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha})
, the shifted skipping loss, which sums up the skipping loss in “future” steps, introduces significant difficulties in producing the pointwise uniform bound and second-moment control in local state-action pairs
(s,a)
.

Our novel analysis presents a new technique in deriving a
local
control of the shifted loss
\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{shift}}_{t}(s,a)
, which is defined as the advantage function of skipping loss
\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s,a)
:

\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{shift}}_{t}(s,a):={Q}^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi_{t}}(s,a;\widetilde{\bm{\ell}}^{\text{skip}}_{t})-{V}^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi_{t}}(s;\widetilde{\bm{\ell}}^{\text{skip}}_{t}).

To be specific, we decompose the shifted loss into a centered loss term and a differential value function term. Then, by careful calculation of the reaching probability, we can derive the pointwise uniform bound:

Lemma 4.2

(Pointwise Uniform Bound of Shifted Loss)
.

For any
(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}
, we have

|\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{shift}}_{t}(s,a)|\leq\mathrm{poly}(H,S,A)\sigma t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}x_{t}(s,a)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}
.

Further employing the decomposition trick in
(
16
)
, we present the second-moment control:

Lemma 4.3

(Second Moment Bound of Shifted Loss)
.

For any
(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{shift}}_{t}(s,a)^{2}\right]\leq\mathrm{poly}(H,S,A)(1-\pi_{t}(a|s))\sigma^{2}t^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}x_{t}(s,a)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-2}
.

We discuss in detail and prove the above two lemmas in Appendix
B.2
(see
Lemmas

B.2
and
B.3
).

Suboptimal Mass Propagation.

A major approach towards BoBW guarantees is “excluding the optimal action” from the summation of regret terms. To be specific, we want to reform the total regret as the summation of some contributions on suboptimal actions, thereby leading to an instance-dependent bound of
\log(T)
order.

In MABs, this is straightforward as the optimal policy is a Dirac distribution, which is concentrated on the single optimal action.
However, it becomes difficult in MDPs when we consider the occupancy measure. Different from the case in MABs, the structure of polytope
{\mathcal{Q}}({\mathbb{P}})
is more complex due to the state-action structure. Specifically, for each
h\in[H]
, the occupancy measure must satisfy
\sum_{s_{h}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h}}\rho(s_{h})=1
and
\sum_{s_{h}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h},a\in{\mathcal{A}}}{\mathbb{P}}[s_{h+1}|s_{h},a]\rho(s_{h},a)=\rho(s_{h+1})
for any
s_{h+1}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h+1}
.
Therefore, the reference policy’s occupancy measure is distributed across the state space, which prevents us from simply excluding a single optimal action as in the MAB case.

In previous works focusing on achieving BoBW with bounded losses
(
16
;
12
)
, their analysis relies on the special structure of the
1/2
-Tsallis entropy regularizer in analyzing the penalty term (arising from the time-varying regularizer) and the
(1-\pi_{t}(a|s))
term in the second-moment control of the shifted loss. However, for heavy-tailed losses, we consider the
1/\alpha
-Tsallis entropy regularizer and involve additional skipping error terms as the payload of skipping loss tuning, which poses significant challenges on analysis.

We address this via a
suboptimal mass propagation
analysis. We show that the total occupancy mass of the reference policy at any layer is upper-bounded by the cumulative mass of suboptimal actions in previous layers.

Lemma 4.4

(Suboptimal Mass Propagation)
.

Let
\pi
be any policy and
\pi^{\dagger}
be a deterministic policy. Let
\rho^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}(s)=\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\rho^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}(s,a)
and
\rho^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi^{\dagger}}(s)=\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\rho^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi^{\dagger}}(s,a)
be the state marginals. Then,

\sum_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}(\rho^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}(s)-\rho^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi^{\dagger}}(s))_{+}\leq H\!\!\sum_{(s,a):a\neq\pi^{\dagger}(s)}\rho^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}(s,a).

This result allows us to bound the penalty terms and the skipping errors via the summation over suboptimal actions, which enables us to achieve an instance-dependent
O(\log T)
rate in the self-bounding regime. See
Lemma

B.8
for a formal proof.

Technical Innovations.
To analyze the regret bound for
Algorithm

2
, we derive an innovative regret decomposition scheme for HTMDPs with unknown transitions. Our novel pessimistic estimator allows us to decompose the regret into four terms: transition error, estimation error, skipping error, and pessimism error. This decomposition is different from previous works with similar algorithm framework
(
15
;
16
;
12
)
, since the heavy-tailed losses and skipping tuning technique considered in heavy-tailed settings introduce extra skipping error terms. Controlling this term requires careful design of the transition kernel and UOB deviation term. To describe our innovative regret decomposition scheme for HTMDPs with unknown transitions, we first introduce the auxiliary loss function
\bm{\ell}_{t}^{\text{pess}}
:

\ell_{t}^{\text{pess}}(s,a):=\ell_{t}(s,a)-D\cdot B_{i}(s,a).

(10)

This term is crucial for regret decomposition, since we need to carefully arrange the decomposition order of the extra operations we did in Line 8 of
Algorithm

2
to construct the pessimistic estimator
\widehat{\ell}_{t}(s,a)
. Specifically, we have the following regret decomposition:

\mathrm{Reg}_{T}({\mathring{\pi}})=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}V^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi_{t}}(s_{1};\bm{\ell}_{t})-V^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s_{1};\bm{\ell}_{t})\right]=\textsc{TransErr}+\textsc{EstReg}+\textsc{SkipErr}+\textsc{PessErr},

where we denote these four terms as:

TransErr

\displaystyle=\!\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\sum_{t=1}^{T}{V}^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi_{t}}(s_{1};\bm{\ell}_{t})\!-\!V^{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{i(t)},\pi_{t}}(s_{1};\bm{\ell}^{\text{pess}}_{t})\Bigg],

EstReg

\displaystyle=\!\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\sum_{t=1}^{T}V^{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{i(t)},\pi_{t}}(s_{1};\widehat{\bm{\ell}}_{t})\!-\!V^{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{i(t)},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s_{1};\widehat{\bm{\ell}}_{t})\Bigg],

SkipErr

\displaystyle=\!\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\sum_{t=1}^{T}V^{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{i(t)},\pi_{t}}(s_{1};\bm{\ell}^{\text{pess}}_{t}\!-\!\widehat{\bm{\ell}}_{t})-V^{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{i(t)},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s_{1};\bm{\ell}^{\text{pess}}_{t}-\widehat{\bm{\ell}}_{t})\Bigg],

PessErr

\displaystyle=\!\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\sum_{t=1}^{T}V^{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{i(t)},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s_{1};\bm{\ell}^{\text{pess}}_{t})\!-\!V^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s_{1};{\bm{\ell}}_{t})\Bigg].

This decomposition order is crucial, since we can handle
EstReg
by the similar methods we conducted for the known transition case, and handle
PessErr
by the Bernstein-type concentration argument (given in
15
). Therefore, we can focus on bounding the
TransErr
and
SkipErr
terms. The
TransErr
term can be solved by applying similar techniques introduced by
16
. We highlight the procedure of bounding the
TransErr
term here.

We first note that the FTRL result
{\bm{x}}_{t}
is the occupancy measure
\rho^{\widehat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{i(t)},\pi_{t}}
, which does not match the true occupancy measure
\rho^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi_{t}}
in the unknown transition case. Therefore, we can only handle the skipping error term

by incorporating the skipping bonus to compensate for the skipping bias under the empirical model
\widehat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{i(t)}
given the definition of

in
Equation

8

Algorithm

is weighted by the optimistic importance sampling factor
u_{t}(s,a)

in the known transition case. Thus, we should involve the difference on UOB term when bounding the deviation between the "true" loss
\bm{\ell}_{t}
and estimated loss
\widehat{\bm{\ell}}_{t}

With high probability, we have

\displaystyle\textup{{SkipErr}}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathcal{O}}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sigma t^{1/\alpha-1}\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{(s,a):a\neq{\mathring{\pi}}(s)}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\ \ +\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathcal{O}}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{(s,a)}\sigma|u_{t}(s,a)-\rho^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi_{t}}(s,a)|\right)\right].

Here the first term is the desired form that excludes the reference action term
{\mathring{\pi}}(s)

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we present the first Best-of-Both-Worlds (BoBW) framework for episodic Markov decision processes with heavy-tailed feedback (HTMDPs). For settings with known transitions, we propose
HT-FTRL-OM
, an algorithm leveraging the FTRL framework with
1/\alpha
-Tsallis entropy regularization and a novel skipping loss estimator. By introducing innovative techniques such as local control for heavy-tailed shifted losses and suboptimal mass propagation, we establish that
HT-FTRL-OM
achieves nearly minimax-optimal instance-independent regret
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{1/\alpha})
in adversarial regimes and logarithmic instance-dependent regret
\mathcal{O}(\log T)
in self-bounding environments.

We further extend our results to the unknown transition setting with
HT-FTRL-UOB
, which incorporates a pessimistic estimator and upper occupancy bounds. Through a novel regret decomposition that isolates transition uncertainty from heavy-tailed estimation errors, we prove that this algorithm attains a regret bound of
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{1/\alpha}+\sqrt{T})
in adversarial environments and
\mathcal{O}(\log^{2}T)
in stochastic ones. Our work bridges the gap between heavy-tailed RL and adaptive algorithm design. Future directions include tightening the bounds with respect to
S,A
and
H
factors and extending these guarantees to the function approximation setting.

Appendix A
Related Works

A.1
Heavy-Tailed Feedback in Online Decision

A.1.1
Heavy-Tailed Multi-Armed Bandits

The study of heavy-tailed feedback in online learning dates back to stochastic bandits, where the payoff distribution may only admit a bounded
\alpha
-moment (
\alpha\in(1,2]
) and classical sub-Gaussian concentration no longer applies.

3
pioneered the use of truncated mean estimators in UCB-style algorithms for heavy-tailed stochastic MABs.

25
extended robust estimation ideas to heavy-tailed
linear
bandits and established sublinear regret without requiring boundedness.
For stochastic linear bandits,
28
and
31
provided (near-)optimal regret rates under heavy-tailed payoffs via carefully designed median-of-means/dynamic truncation estimators.
More recently,
29
developed a Huber-regression based approach with one-pass updates for heavy-tailed linear bandits, further illustrating the broad utility of robust estimation in heavy-tailed online decision-making.

22
studied Lipschitz bandits with heavy-tailed rewards and derived optimal regret rates under moment conditions.
Beyond bandits,
27
investigated Bayesian optimization under heavy-tailed payoffs by combining truncation with GP-UCB style exploration.
Overall, these works highlight that robust mean estimation is indispensable under heavy tails and typically yields worst-case regret scaling as a polynomial in
T
(e.g.,
T^{1/\alpha}
), in sharp contrast to the
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}(\sqrt{T})
rates under light tails.
At a technical level, our skipping loss estimator can be viewed as an occupancy-adaptive form of truncation, but it is embedded into FTRL over occupancy measures and is tailored to control stability/second-order terms that arise in BoBW analyses for MDPs.

A.1.2
Heavy-Tailed Markov Decision Processes

For episodic RL with heavy-tailed rewards,
35
established no-regret guarantees for tabular MDPs with heavy-tailed feedback and proposed robust variants of optimistic algorithms, achieving near-optimal
instance-independent
rates under moment assumptions (and matching lower bounds up to logarithmic factors in several regimes).

26
also studied heavy-tailed RL from the perspective of robust estimation and proposed a penalized robust estimator framework.
Moving beyond tabular models,
10
studied heavy-tailed RL with (linear) function approximation and derived minimax-optimal regret bounds together with instance-dependent guarantees;
19
further developed variance-aware algorithms for linear MDPs under heavy-tailed rewards.
These
instance-dependent
guarantees are typically expressed in terms of problem-dependent quantities such as conditional variances or instance measures, and their dependence on the number of episodes remains polynomial (e.g., scaling like
T^{1/\alpha}
in heavy-tailed regimes), rather than logarithmic in
T
.
Moreover, existing HTMDP results almost exclusively focus on (stationary) stochastic environments and do not address best-of-both-worlds adaptivity when the regime is unknown
a priori
.
Orthogonal to online regret minimization, another line of work studies robustness in
offline
RL under heavy-tailed rewards or data corruption
(
34
;
32
)
, and privacy-constrained heavy-tailed RL
(
30
)
.
In contrast, our goal is to achieve best-of-both-worlds guarantees for HTMDPs, i.e., nearly minimax-optimal instance-independent regret in adversarial regimes while simultaneously attaining
logarithmic
instance-dependent regret in self-bounding (including stochastic) regimes (e.g.,
{\mathcal{O}}(\log T)
for known transitions and
{\mathcal{O}}(\log^{2}T)
for unknown transitions in our results).

A.2
Best-of-Both-Worlds (BoBW) Algorithms for MDPs

The best-of-both-worlds goal in online episodic MDPs is to design a single algorithm that achieves near-minimax regret in the worst case (e.g.,
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}(\sqrt{T})
against adversarial losses) while simultaneously enjoying much smaller regret in benign regimes (e.g., polylogarithmic regret under stochastic/self-bounding losses), without knowing the regime
a priori
.
A unifying backbone behind the first BoBW results for episodic MDPs is to cast learning as online optimization over
occupancy measures
and apply FTRL with carefully chosen regularization.
In particular, for tabular MDPs with
bounded
losses and bandit feedback,
18
gave the first BoBW result for the known-transition setting by running FTRL over occupancy measures with a carefully designed hybrid regularizer, achieving
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}(\sqrt{T})
regret in the adversarial regime while attaining
{\mathcal{O}}(\log T)
regret when losses are i.i.d. stochastic (and more generally, graceful degradation under corrupted stochastic losses).

16
extended this line to the unknown-transition setting, again within the occupancy-measure FTRL paradigm, introducing a loss-shifting analysis and a key argument that controls transition-estimation error by the regret itself in stochastic regimes, leading to
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}(\sqrt{T})
adversarial regret and
{\mathcal{O}}(\log^{2}T)
stochastic regret.

Going beyond fixed dynamics,
17
studied online RL when both losses and transitions may be adversarial; since fully adversarial transitions make no-regret learning impossible in general, they obtain guarantees that scale with a transition-adversariality/corruption measure, thereby interpolating between learnable and impossible regimes within an online-learning framework for MDPs.
With more restrictive feedback,
12
studied episodic MDPs with
aggregate
bandit feedback (only the episode-level cumulative loss is observed) and developed BoBW adaptivity between stochastic and adversarial loss regimes under this limited observation model, highlighting additional connections to partial-feedback/online-learning tools beyond the standard step-wise bandit setting.

Complementary to the occupancy-measure viewpoint,
7
provided BoBW guarantees for policy-optimization style algorithms in tabular MDPs by combining entropy-type regularization, exploration bonuses, and learning-rate schedules, achieving
\sqrt{T}

Finally, while not BoBW,
21
sharpened the worst-case baseline by studying adversarial
linear
MDPs with bandit feedback and proving improved (rate-optimal for an inefficient algorithm) regret bounds.
At a broader methodological level,
13
established nearly optimal BoBW algorithms for online learning with feedback graphs, which provides a general toolkit for partial-feedback settings that also inform subsequent developments in online RL.

All the above BoBW-MDP results crucially rely on bounded (light-tailed) losses and do not address robustness to heavy-tailed feedback, which is the focus of our work.

A.3
BoBW Algorithms with Heavy-Tailed Feedback

Best-of-both-worlds learning under heavy-tailed feedback has been studied primarily in bandit models.
For heavy-tailed multi-armed bandits (MABs) where losses/rewards may be unbounded but admit only a bounded
\alpha
-moment,
9
introduced the first BoBW algorithms that simultaneously achieve minimax-optimal polynomial regret in adversarial environments (scaling as
T^{1/\alpha}
up to factors depending on the number of arms and logs) and logarithmic (gap-dependent) regret in stochastic environments, with additional adaptivity to unknown tail parameters.
Recently,
6
further strengthened this line by developing BoBW guarantees for adversarial bandits with heavy-tailed losses under mild moment assumptions, providing high-probability bounds and relaxing technical distributional conditions used in earlier work.
On the parameter-free front (where the tail parameter
\alpha,\sigma
are unknown),
5
proposed
uniINF
, a BoBW algorithm for heavy-tailed MABs that do not require prior knowledge of tail parameters and achieves near-optimal regret in both stochastic and adversarial regimes via adaptive learning-rate and robust loss-control mechanisms.
Beyond classical MABs,
33
extended BoBW methodology to heavy-tailed
linear
bandits and broader adversarial heavy-tailed bandit problems via an FTRL-based framework with data-dependent stability–penalty matching. They also achieve BoBW without any further nonnegativity assumption on loss distribution.

In contrast to these bandit works, our setting involves episodic MDPs where feedback is coupled across time through state visitation and transition dynamics.
This coupling introduces additional stability/second-order challenges (e.g., through occupancy-measure constraints) that do not arise in bandits, and necessitates a robust loss estimator that can be embedded into BoBW analyses for MDPs.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}_{T}({\mathring{\pi}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}V_{t}^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi_{t}}(s_{1})-V_{t}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s_{1})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\langle\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi_{t}}-\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}},\bm{\ell}_{t}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}},{\bm{\ell}}^{\text{skip}}_{t}-{\bm{b}}_{t}^{\text{skip}}\right\rangle\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}},\bm{\ell}_{t}-{\bm{\ell}}^{\text{skip}}_{t}+{\bm{b}}_{t}^{\text{skip}}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}},\widetilde{\bm{\ell}}^{\text{skip}}_{t}-{\bm{b}}_{t}^{\text{skip}}\right\rangle\right]}_{\text{FTRL Regret}}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}},\bm{\ell}_{t}-{\bm{\ell}}^{\text{skip}}_{t}+{\bm{b}}_{t}^{\text{skip}}\right\rangle\right]}_{\text{Skipping Error}},

where the last equality holds since
\widetilde{\bm{\ell}}^{\text{skip}}_{t}
is the importance sampling over occupancies measure of
\bm{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}

Denote the Bregman Divergence

D_{\Psi}({\bm{y}},{\bm{x}})=\Psi({\bm{y}})-\Psi({\bm{x}})-\left\langle\nabla\Psi({\bm{x}}),{\bm{y}}-{\bm{x}}\right\rangle.

(18)

We further decompose the FTRL Regret via standard analysis for FTRL, using the shifted loss:

\displaystyle\quad\text{FTRL Regret}=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}},\widetilde{\bm{\ell}}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}-{\bm{b}}_{t}^{\text{skip}}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t+1},\widetilde{\bm{\ell}}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}-{\bm{b}}_{t}^{\text{skip}}\right\rangle-D_{\Psi_{t}}({\bm{x}}_{t+1},{\bm{x}}_{t})\right]}_{\text{Stability Term}}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\Psi_{t}(\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}})-\Psi_{t-1}(\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}})\right)-\left(\Psi_{t}({\bm{x}}_{t})-\Psi_{t-1}({\bm{x}}_{t})\right)\right]}_{\text{Penalty Term}}

\texttt{stab}_{t}

\texttt{skip}_{t}

\displaystyle\texttt{stab}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t+1},\widetilde{\bm{\ell}}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}-{\bm{b}}_{t}^{\text{skip}}\right\rangle-D_{\Psi_{t}}({\bm{x}}_{t+1},{\bm{x}}_{t}),

\displaystyle\texttt{pena}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\left(\Psi_{t}(\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}})-\Psi_{t-1}(\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}})\right)-\left(\Psi_{t}({\bm{x}}_{t})-\Psi_{t-1}({\bm{x}}_{t})\right),

\displaystyle\texttt{skip}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}},\bm{\ell}_{t}-{\bm{\ell}}^{\text{skip}}_{t}+{\bm{b}}_{t}^{\text{skip}}\right\rangle.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}_{T}({\mathring{\pi}})

B.2
Loss Shifting Technique

Inspired by
16
, we introduce the shifted loss technique to simplify the analysis.

We first consider the skipping value function and Q function defined as:

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{t}(s_{H+1}):=0,

\displaystyle\forall s_{H+1}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{H+1},

(19)

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}_{t}(s_{h},a):=\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s_{h},a)+\sum_{s_{h+1}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h+1}}{\mathbb{P}}_{h}[s_{h+1}\mid s_{h},a]\widetilde{V}_{t}(s_{h+1}),

\displaystyle\forall s_{h}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h},a\in{\mathcal{A}},

(20)

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{t}(s_{h}):=\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\pi_{t}(a\mid s_{h})\widetilde{Q}_{t}(s_{h},a),

\displaystyle\forall s_{h}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h}.

(21)

We define the shifted loss as:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\ell}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}(s_{h},a)

\displaystyle:=\widetilde{Q}_{t}(s_{h},a)-\widetilde{V}_{t}(s_{h})

(22)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s_{h},a)+\sum_{s_{h+1}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h+1}}({\mathbb{P}}_{h}[s_{h+1}\mid s_{h},a]\widetilde{V}_{t}(s_{h+1}))-\widetilde{V}_{t}(s_{h}).

We can further write the shifted value function as the recursive definition:

\widetilde{V}_{t,h}(s)=\sum_{a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}\pi_{t}(a^{\prime}|s)\left(\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s,a^{\prime})+\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h+1}}{\mathbb{P}}[s^{\prime}|s,a^{\prime}]\widetilde{V}_{t,h+1}(s^{\prime})\right),\quad\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h}

(23)

Following the loss-shifting trick from
16
, we observe that the shifted loss
\widetilde{\bm{\ell}}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}
(defined in
Equation

22
) satisfies:

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}},\widetilde{\bm{\ell}}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}\right\rangle\middle|{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}},\widetilde{\bm{\ell}}^{\text{skip}}_{t}\right\rangle\middle|{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right].

(24)

This is because the shifting function telescopes: by the performance difference lemma, the additional value function terms cancel out when taking the inner product with the occupancy difference
{\bm{x}}_{t}-\bm{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}
. Therefore, for the purpose of bounding the FTRL regret, we can equivalently analyze using the shifted loss
\widetilde{\bm{\ell}}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}
, which has the advantage that its variance is concentrated on suboptimal actions.

Another key property is that the shifted loss is concentrated on a subset of actions excluding the reference policy. To be specific, for the reference action
\mathring{a}={\mathring{\pi}}(s)
, the shifted loss satisfies
\widetilde{\ell}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}(s,\mathring{a})\approx 0
when
\pi_{t}\approx{\mathring{\pi}}
, as the terms approximately cancel by the performance difference argument.

Different from
16
where they consider
\ell_{t}\in[0,1]
, involving loss-shifting techniques faces significant challenges in handling the heavy-tailed loss.

Our novel analysis presents a new technique in bounding the first and second order moments of the shifted loss, which is crucial for the analysis of FTRL with Tsallis entropy regularizer.

Lemma B.2

(The Uniform Bound on the Shifted Loss)
.

We can bound the shifted loss

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\ell}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}(s,a)|\leq\left(1+HSA(1+A^{1-1/\alpha})\right)\cdot C\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha-1}.

Proof.

Decomposition of the shifted loss.

Denote

\bar{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s):=\sum_{a^{\prime}}\pi_{t}(a^{\prime}|s)\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s,a^{\prime}),

(25)

as the policy-weighted loss at state
s

(26)

as the policy-weighted value function at state
s
.
Then, we have the decomposition of shifted loss
\widetilde{\ell}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}(s,a)

\widetilde{\ell}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}(s,a)=\underbrace{\left(\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s,a)-\bar{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s)\right)}_{=:\widetilde{\ell}^{\mathrm{center}}_{t}(s,a)}+\underbrace{\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}}{\mathbb{P}}[s^{\prime}|s,a]\widetilde{V}_{t,h+1}(s^{\prime})-\bar{V}_{t,h+1}(s)\right)}_{=:\Delta V_{t}(s,a)},

Equipped with this decomposition, we can bound the first order moments of the shifted loss by the first order moments of the center loss and the value function difference, separately.

In the following argument, we fix
(s,a)

(s,a^{\prime})

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\ell}^{\mathrm{center}}_{t}(s,a)|

\displaystyle=|\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s,a)-\pi_{t}(a|s)\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s,a)|

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\ell}^{\mathrm{center}}_{t}(s,a)|

\displaystyle=|\pi_{t}(a^{\prime}|s)\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s,a^{\prime})|

by:

\displaystyle|\Delta V_{t}(s,a)|

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha}\sum_{h^{\prime}=h+1}^{H}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h^{\prime}},a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}|\omega_{t}^{h\to h^{\prime}}(s,a;s^{\prime},a^{\prime})|\cdot x_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})^{1/\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\leq HSA(1+A^{1-1/\alpha})\cdot C\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha-1}.

Therefore, we can bound the first order moments of the shifted loss by:

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\ell}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}(s,a)|

\displaystyle\leq|\widetilde{\ell}^{\mathrm{center}}_{t}(s,a)|+|\Delta V_{t}(s,a)|

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha}\cdot x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha-1}+HSA(1+A^{1-1/\alpha})\cdot C\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\leq(1+HSA(1+A^{1-1/\alpha}))\cdot C\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha}\cdot x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha-1}.

∎

We highlight that this decomposition is crucial for the analysis of heavy-tailed shifted loss
\widetilde{\ell}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}(s,a)
. This technique could be of independent interest.

Now we analyze the second-order moments of the shifted loss. Following the analysis in
16
, we have the lemma:

Lemma B.3

(Second-order Moments of Shifted Loss)
.

We can bound the second-order moments of the shifted loss by

\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{\ell}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}(s,a)^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}]\leq 2H^{2}(1-\pi_{t}(a|s))\cdot C^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{2}t^{2/\alpha-1}x_{t}(s,a)^{2/\alpha-2}.

(29)

Proof.

We utilize the definition of the shifted loss in
Equation

22
. We have
\widetilde{\ell}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}(s,a)=\widetilde{Q}_{t}(s,a)-\widetilde{V}_{t}(s)=(1-\pi_{t}(a|s))\widetilde{Q}_{t}(s,a)-\sum_{b\neq a}\pi_{t}(b|s)\widetilde{Q}_{t}(s,b)
.

Inspired by
16
, we first bound the second-order moments of the shifted Q function.

Fixed state-action pair
(s,a)
, and denote
h
to be the index of the layer that
(s,a)
is at, i.e.,
s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h}
. Define
q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,a)
is the probability of visiting
(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})
after taking action
a
from
s
and following the policy
\pi_{t}
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{Q}_{t}(s,a)^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{h^{\prime}\geq h}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h^{\prime}},a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,a)\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\right)^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq H\cdot\sum_{h^{\prime}\geq h}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h^{\prime}},a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,a)^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})^{2}\cdot\mathbbm{1}[(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\text{ is visited}]\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle=H\cdot\sum_{h^{\prime}\geq h}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h^{\prime}},a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}\frac{q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,a)^{2}}{x_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})^{2}\cdot\mathbbm{1}[(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\text{ is visited}]\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq H\cdot\sum_{h^{\prime}\geq h}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h^{\prime}},a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}\frac{q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,a)^{2}}{x_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})}\mathbb{E}\left[|{\ell}_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})|^{\alpha}\tau_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})^{2-\alpha}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right],

where the first inequality is due to Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and
\mathbbm{1}[(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\text{ is visited}]\cdot\mathbbm{1}[(s^{\prime\prime},a^{\prime\prime})\text{ is visited}]=0
if
(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\neq(s^{\prime\prime},a^{\prime\prime})
. Notice that we can bound the second-order moments of the skipped loss by:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|{\ell}_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})|^{\alpha}\tau_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})^{2-\alpha}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]\leq C^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{2}t^{2/\alpha-1}x_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})^{2/\alpha-1}.

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{Q}_{t}(s,a)^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]\leq H\cdot C^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{2}t^{2/\alpha-1}\sum_{h^{\prime}\geq h}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h^{\prime}},a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,a)^{2}x_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})^{2/\alpha-2}.

Notice that

\displaystyle x_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})=\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h},a^{\prime\prime}}x_{t}(s^{\prime\prime},a^{\prime\prime})\cdot q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s^{\prime\prime},a^{\prime\prime})\geq x_{t}(s,a)q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,a),

and
u\mapsto u^{2/\alpha-2}
is decreasing on
u\in[0,1]
since
2/\alpha-2\in[-1,0)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{Q}_{t}(s,a)^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq H\cdot C^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{2}t^{2/\alpha-1}\sum_{h^{\prime}\geq h}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h^{\prime}},a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,a)^{2/\alpha}x_{t}(s,a)^{2/\alpha-2}

\displaystyle\leq H\cdot C^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{2}t^{2/\alpha-1}x_{t}(s,a)^{2/\alpha-2}.

For the second-order moments of the shifted value function, we have

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{b\neq a}\pi_{t}(b|s)\widetilde{Q}_{t}(s,b)\right)^{2}\middle|{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{h^{\prime}\geq h}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h^{\prime}},a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}\left(\sum_{b\neq a}\pi_{t}(b|s)q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,b)\right)\cdot\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\right)^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq H\cdot\sum_{h^{\prime}\geq h}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h^{\prime}},a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}\left(\sum_{b\neq a}\pi_{t}(b|s)q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,b)\right)^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})^{2}\cdot\mathbbm{1}[(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\text{ is visited}]\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right],

where this inequality holds because
\mathbbm{1}[(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\text{ is visited}]\cdot\mathbbm{1}[(s^{\prime\prime},a^{\prime\prime})\text{ is visited}]=0
if
(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\neq(s^{\prime\prime},a^{\prime\prime})
. Consider the term
\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})^{2}\cdot\mathbbm{1}[(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\text{ is visited}]\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})^{2}\cdot\mathbbm{1}[(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\text{ is visited}]\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]\leq x_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})^{-1}\cdot C^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{2}t^{2/\alpha-1}x_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})^{2/\alpha-1}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{b\neq a}\pi_{t}(b|s)\widetilde{Q}_{t}(s,b)\right)^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq H\cdot C^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{2}t^{2/\alpha-1}\sum_{h^{\prime}\geq h}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h^{\prime}},a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}\left(\sum_{b\neq a}\pi_{t}(b|s)q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,b)\right)^{2}x_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})^{2/\alpha-2}

By the decomposition of
x_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{h^{\prime}\geq h}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h^{\prime}},a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}\left(\sum_{b\neq a}\pi_{t}(b|s)q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,b)\right)^{2}x_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})^{2/\alpha-2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{h^{\prime}\geq h}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h^{\prime}},a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}\left(\sum_{b\neq a}\pi_{t}(b|s)q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,b)\right)^{2}\cdot\left(\sum_{b\neq a}x_{t}(s,b)q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,b)\right)^{2/\alpha-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x_{t}(s)}\sum_{h^{\prime}\geq h}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h^{\prime}},a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}\left(\sum_{b\neq a}x_{t}(s,b)q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,b)\right)^{2/\alpha-1}\cdot\left(\sum_{b\neq a}\pi_{t}(b|s)q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,b)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{x_{t}(s)}\sum_{h^{\prime}\geq h}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h^{\prime}},a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}\left(\sum_{b\neq a}x_{t}(s,b)\right)^{2/\alpha-1}\cdot\left(\sum_{b\neq a}\pi_{t}(b|s)q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,b)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{x_{t}(s)}\sum_{h^{\prime}\geq h}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h^{\prime}},a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}(x_{t}(s))^{2/\alpha-1}\cdot\left(\sum_{b\neq a}\pi_{t}(b|s)q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,b)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h^{\prime}\geq h}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h^{\prime}},a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}x_{t}(s)^{2/\alpha-2}\cdot\left(\sum_{b\neq a}\pi_{t}(b|s)q_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}|s,b)\right)

\displaystyle\leq H\cdot x_{t}(s,a)^{2/\alpha-2}\cdot\sum_{b\neq a}\pi_{t}(b|s)

\displaystyle=H\cdot(1-\pi_{t}(a|s))x_{t}(s,a)^{2/\alpha-2}.

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{b\neq a}\pi_{t}(b|s)\widetilde{Q}_{t}(s,b)\right)^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]\leq(1-\pi_{t}(a|s))\cdot H^{2}C^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{2}t^{2/\alpha-1}x_{t}(s,a)^{2/\alpha-2}.

This induced the bound on the second-order moments of the shifted loss:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{\ell}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}(s,a)^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[(1-\pi_{t}(a|s))^{2}\widetilde{Q}_{t}(s,a)^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{b\neq a}\pi_{t}(b|s)\widetilde{Q}_{t}(s,b)\right)^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2H^{2}(1-\pi_{t}(a|s))\cdot C^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{2}t^{2/\alpha-1}x_{t}(s,a)^{2/\alpha-2}.

∎

B.3
Bounding Stability Term

To begin with, we first state the local stability of Tsallis entropy, which is a famous result for
\alpha^{\prime}
-Tsallis entropy, introduced in
14
. We rewrite it as follows for completeness.

Lemma B.4

(Local stability of Tsallis entropy)
.

Consider the
\alpha^{\prime}
-Tsallis entropy
\psi({\bm{x}})=-\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}\sum_{i}(x_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}}-x_{i})
defined on the simplex, with
\alpha^{\prime}\in[1/2,1)
.
Let
{\bm{x}}
be a point in the simplex and
{\bm{g}}
be the vector of the gradient of
\psi(\cdot)
.
Let
\tilde{{\bm{y}}}
be the unconstrained update defined by
\nabla\psi(\tilde{{\bm{y}}})=\nabla\psi({\bm{x}})-{\bm{g}}
.
If the gradient is small enough such that
|g_{i}|\leq\frac{1-\alpha^{\prime}}{4}x_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}-1}
for all indices
i
, then

\langle{\bm{g}},{\bm{x}}-\tilde{{\bm{y}}}\rangle-D_{\psi}(\tilde{{\bm{y}}},{\bm{x}})\leq\frac{2}{1-\alpha^{\prime}}\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2-\alpha^{\prime}}g_{i}^{2}.

(30)

Proof.

Since
\nabla\psi(\cdot)_{i}
is the map
u\mapsto\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}-u^{\alpha^{\prime}-1}
, the optimality condition
\nabla\psi(\tilde{{\bm{y}}})_{i}=\nabla\psi({\bm{x}})_{i}-g_{i}
implies
\tilde{y}_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}-1}=x_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}-1}+g_{i}
.
The condition
|g_{i}|\leq\frac{1-\alpha^{\prime}}{4}x_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}-1}
implies

\left(1-\frac{1-\alpha^{\prime}}{4}\right)x_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}-1}\leq\tilde{y}_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}-1}\leq\left(1+\frac{1-\alpha^{\prime}}{4}\right)x_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}-1}.

Since
\alpha^{\prime}<1
, the function
u\mapsto u^{\alpha^{\prime}-1}
is strictly decreasing. Using elementary inequalities for the power function with exponents in
(-\infty,-2]
(as implied by
\alpha^{\prime}\in[1/2,1)
), one can verify that the multiplicative bounds above imply
|\tilde{y}_{i}-x_{i}|\leq\frac{1}{2}x_{i}
.

By Taylor’s theorem,
D_{\psi}(\tilde{{\bm{y}}},{\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{{\bm{y}}}-{\bm{x}})^{\top}\nabla^{2}\psi({\bm{z}})(\tilde{{\bm{y}}}-{\bm{x}})
for some
{\bm{z}}
on the segment connecting
{\bm{x}}
and
\tilde{{\bm{y}}}
.
Since
|\tilde{y}_{i}-x_{i}|\leq\frac{1}{2}x_{i}
, we have
z_{i}\leq\frac{3}{2}x_{i}
. The Hessian is diagonal with entries
\nabla^{2}\psi({\bm{z}})_{ii}=(1-\alpha^{\prime})z_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}-2}
. Since
\alpha^{\prime}-2<0
, we have
z_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}-2}\geq(\frac{3}{2})^{\alpha^{\prime}-2}x_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}-2}\geq\frac{1}{2}x_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}-2}
.
Thus,

D_{\psi}(\tilde{{\bm{y}}},{\bm{x}})\geq\frac{1-\alpha^{\prime}}{4}\sum_{i}x_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}-2}(\tilde{y}_{i}-x_{i})^{2}.

Applying Young’s inequality
ab\leq\frac{a^{2}}{2\lambda}+\frac{\lambda b^{2}}{2}
with
\lambda_{i}=\frac{1-\alpha^{\prime}}{2}x_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}-2}
to the term
\langle{\bm{g}},{\bm{x}}-\tilde{{\bm{y}}}\rangle
yields
Equation

30
.
∎

Equipped with the local stability of Tsallis entropy, we now bound the stability term via the following lemma.

Lemma B.5
.

The stability term is bounded as

\texttt{stab}_{t}\leq\frac{4\alpha^{2}}{\alpha-1}\eta_{t}\sum_{(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}}x_{t}(s,a)^{2-1/\alpha}\left((\widetilde{\ell}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}(s,a))^{2}+(b_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,a))^{2}\right).

Proof.

Recall the regularizer
\Psi_{t}({\bm{x}})=-\frac{1}{\eta_{t}}\sum_{(s,a)}x(s,a)^{1/\alpha}
where
\alpha\in(1,2]
.

Define
\alpha^{\prime}:=1/\alpha\in[1/2,1)
. The standard
\alpha^{\prime}
-Tsallis entropy is
\psi({\bm{x}})=-\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}\sum_{(s,a)}(x(s,a)^{\alpha^{\prime}}-x(s,a))
.
We have
\Psi_{t}({\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{\alpha\eta_{t}}\psi({\bm{x}})+\text{linear terms}
, which implies

D_{\Psi_{t}}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}})=\frac{1}{\alpha\eta_{t}}D_{\psi}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}})=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\eta_{t}}D_{\psi}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}).

We apply
Lemma

B.4
with
{\bm{x}}={\bm{x}}_{t}
and the scaled loss gradient
{\bm{g}}=\alpha\eta_{t}(\widetilde{\bm{\ell}}_{t}^{{\text{shift}}}-{\bm{b}}_{t}^{\text{skip}})
.
First, we verify the condition
|g(s,a)|\leq\frac{1-\alpha^{\prime}}{4}x_{t}(s,a)^{\alpha^{\prime}-1}
.

Here we use the uniform bound on the shifted loss
Lemma

4.2
and the definition of the skipping bonus
{\bm{b}}_{t}^{\text{skip}}
:

\displaystyle|g(s,a)|

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\eta_{t}\cdot\left(|\widetilde{\ell}_{t}^{\text{shift}}(s,a)|+|b_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,a)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\eta_{t}\cdot(1+HSA(1+A^{1-1/\alpha}))\cdot C\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha}\cdot x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha-1}+\alpha\eta_{t}\cdot C^{1-\alpha}\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha-1}\cdot x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha-1}

\displaystyle=\alpha\cdot\beta\cdot(C+HSA(1+A^{1-1/\alpha})C+C^{1-\alpha}/t)\cdot x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha-1},

Since we set
C=\left(\frac{(1+HSA+HSA^{2-1/\alpha})}{\alpha-1}\right)^{-1/\alpha}
in
Equation

16
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\beta\cdot(C+HSA(1+A^{1-1/\alpha})C+C^{1-\alpha}/t)

\displaystyle\leq\beta\cdot\left(\alpha(\alpha-1)^{1/\alpha-1}\cdot R^{1-1/\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha-1}{4\alpha^{2}},

by the definition of
\beta
in
Equation

17
.
Therefore, we get
|g(s,a)|\leq\frac{1-\alpha^{\prime}}{4}x_{t}(s,a)^{\alpha^{\prime}-1}
.

The FTRL stability term is
\texttt{stab}_{t}=\langle\widetilde{\bm{\ell}}_{t}^{{\text{shift}}}-{\bm{b}}_{t}^{\text{skip}},{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t+1}\rangle-D_{\Psi_{t}}({\bm{x}}_{t+1},{\bm{x}}_{t})
.
Using the relation
D_{\Psi_{t}}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\eta_{t}}D_{\psi}
, we write

\texttt{stab}_{t}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\eta_{t}}\left[\left\langle\frac{\eta_{t}}{\alpha^{\prime}}(\widetilde{\bm{\ell}}_{t}^{{\text{shift}}}-{\bm{b}}_{t}^{\text{skip}}),{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t+1}\right\rangle-D_{\psi}({\bm{x}}_{t+1},{\bm{x}}_{t})\right].

Let
F({\bm{y}}):=\langle{\bm{g}},{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}\rangle-D_{\psi}({\bm{y}},{\bm{x}}_{t})
. The quantity in the brackets is
F({\bm{x}}_{t+1})
. If
\tilde{{\bm{y}}}
(the unconstrained update) maximizes
F
, we have
F({\bm{x}}_{t+1})\leq F(\tilde{{\bm{y}}})
.
Applying
Lemma

B.4
:

\texttt{stab}_{t}\leq\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\eta_{t}}\cdot\frac{2}{1-\alpha^{\prime}}\sum_{(s,a)}x_{t}(s,a)^{2-\alpha^{\prime}}\frac{\eta_{t}^{2}}{\alpha^{\prime 2}}\left(\widetilde{\ell}_{t}^{{\text{shift}}}(s,a)-b_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,a)\right)^{2}.

Simplifying with
\alpha^{\prime}=1/\alpha
and
\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}(1-\alpha^{\prime})}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\alpha-1}
. Notice that
(x-y)^{2}\leq 2x^{2}+2y^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\texttt{stab}_{t}\leq\frac{4\alpha^{2}}{\alpha-1}\eta_{t}\sum_{(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}}x_{t}(s,a)^{2-1/\alpha}\left((\widetilde{\ell}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}(s,a))^{2}+(b_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,a))^{2}\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

In the following statement, we separately control the second moment of
\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{shift}}_{t}(s,a)
and
b_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,a)
weighted by
x_{t}(s,a)^{2-1/\alpha}
and finally derive the control on the expectations of
\texttt{stab}_{t}
. The key challenge is to provide the summation over
(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}
without the reference action
{\mathring{\pi}}(s)
. We denote

\displaystyle\texttt{loss}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\eta_{t}\sum_{(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}}x_{t}(s,a)^{2-1/\alpha}\cdot\left(\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{shift}}_{t}(s,a)\right)^{2},

(31)

\displaystyle\texttt{bonus}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\eta_{t}\sum_{(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}}x_{t}(s,a)^{2-1/\alpha}\left(b_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,a)\right)^{2}.

(32)

B.3.1
Control on the Second Moment of the Shifted Loss

Lemma B.6
.

For the shifted loss part of the stability term
\texttt{stab}_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\texttt{loss}_{t}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]\leq 8H\beta C^{2-\alpha}\cdot\sigma t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}.

Proof.

We analyze the stability term with the shifted loss
\widetilde{\ell}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}
. The key insight is that the shifted loss has a special structure that eliminates the contribution from reference actions. We apply the bounds for second-order moment of shifted loss in
Lemma

4.3
and get

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\eta_{t}\cdot\sum_{(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}}x_{t}(s,a)^{2-1/\alpha}(\widetilde{\ell}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}(s,a))^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\eta_{t}\cdot\sum_{(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}}x_{t}(s,a)^{2-1/\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\widetilde{\ell}^{\text{shift}}_{t}(s,a)\right)^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 4H\cdot\eta_{t}\cdot C^{2-\alpha}\cdot\sigma^{2}t^{2/\alpha-1}\cdot\sum_{(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}}x_{t}(s,a)^{2-1/\alpha}\cdot(1-\pi_{t}(a|s))x_{t}(s,a)^{2/\alpha-2}

\displaystyle\leq 4H\cdot\beta C^{2-\alpha}\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha-1}\cdot\sum_{(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}}(1-\pi_{t}(a|s))x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}.

We note that for fixed
s
and
\mathring{a}={\mathring{\pi}}(s)
,

\displaystyle(1-\pi_{t}(\mathring{a}|s))x_{t}(s,\mathring{a})^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a\neq\mathring{a}}\pi_{t}(a|s)x_{t}(s)^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a\neq\mathring{a}}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}\cdot\pi_{t}(a|s)^{1-1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a\neq\mathring{a}}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}.

Therefore, we get the result

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\eta_{t}\cdot\sum_{(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}}x_{t}(s,a)^{2-1/\alpha}(\widetilde{\ell}^{{\text{shift}}}_{t}(s,a))^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]\leq 8H\cdot\beta C^{2-\alpha}\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha-1}\cdot\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}.

∎

B.3.2
Control on the Second Moment of the Skipping Bonus

Lemma B.7
.

For the skipping bonus part of the stability term
\texttt{stab}_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\texttt{bonus}_{t}\right]\leq 2SA^{1-1/\alpha}\beta C^{2-2\alpha}\cdot\sigma\cdot\log(T)

Proof.

Recall the definition of the skipping bonus:

b_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,a)=C^{1-\alpha}\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha-1}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha-1}.

Substituting this into the definition of
\texttt{bonus}_{t}
:

\displaystyle\texttt{bonus}_{t}

\displaystyle=C^{2-2\alpha}\cdot\eta_{t}\cdot\sigma^{2}t^{2/\alpha-2}\sum_{(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\beta C^{2-2\alpha}\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha-2}\cdot\sum_{h=1}^{H}(S_{h}A)^{1-1/\alpha},

\displaystyle\leq\beta C^{2-2\alpha}\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha-2}\cdot SA^{1-1/\alpha}.

The first inequality is obtained by observing that the sum reaches its maximum when
x_{t}
is uniform in each layer. Notice that
1/\alpha-2\in[-3/2,-1)
. Therefore, this is the higher-order term. We can bound it by
O(\log(T))
:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\texttt{bonus}_{t}

\displaystyle\leq SA^{1-1/\alpha}\cdot\beta C^{2-2\alpha}\cdot\sigma\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{1/\alpha-2}

\displaystyle\leq 2SA^{1-1/\alpha}\cdot\beta C^{2-2\alpha}\cdot\sigma\log(T).

∎

B.4
Bounding Penalty Term

To begin with this section, we need to analyze the occupancy difference layer by layer.
Following Lemma 20 in
16
, we derive the following lemma.

Lemma B.8

(Suboptimal Mass Propagation)
.

Let
\pi
be any policy and
\pi^{\dagger}
be a deterministic policy. Let
q^{\pi}(s,a)
and
q^{\pi^{\dagger}}(s,a)
be the state-action occupancy measures, and
q^{\pi}(s)=\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}q^{\pi}(s,a)
and
q^{\pi^{\dagger}}(s)=\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}q^{\pi^{\dagger}}(s,a)
be the state marginals. Then,

\sum_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}(q^{\pi}(s)-q^{\pi^{\dagger}}(s))_{+}\leq H\cdot\sum_{(s,a):a\neq\pi^{\dagger}(s)}q^{\pi}(s,a).

(33)

Proof.

Define
f(s):=(q^{\pi}(s)-q^{\pi^{\dagger}}(s))_{+}
. Since
\pi^{\dagger}
is deterministic,
q^{\pi^{\dagger}}(s,a)=0
for all
a\neq\pi^{\dagger}(s)
.
For any state
s
at layer
h>0
, we decompose the state occupancy based on the previous layer:

\displaystyle q^{\pi}(s)-q^{\pi^{\dagger}}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h-1}}\sum_{a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}{\mathbb{P}}[s|s^{\prime},a^{\prime}]q^{\pi}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})-\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h-1}}{\mathbb{P}}[s|s^{\prime},\pi^{\dagger}(s^{\prime})]q^{\pi^{\dagger}}(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h-1}}{\mathbb{P}}[s|s^{\prime},\pi^{\dagger}(s^{\prime})](q^{\pi}(s^{\prime})-q^{\pi^{\dagger}}(s^{\prime}))+\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h-1}}\sum_{a^{\prime}\neq\pi^{\dagger}(s^{\prime})}{\mathbb{P}}[s|s^{\prime},a^{\prime}]q^{\pi}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}).

Here, we used the substitution
q^{\pi}(s^{\prime},\pi^{\dagger}(s^{\prime}))=q^{\pi}(s^{\prime})-\sum_{a^{\prime}\neq\pi^{\dagger}(s^{\prime})}q^{\pi}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})
, and
q^{\pi}(s^{\prime},\pi^{\dagger}(s^{\prime}))\leq q^{\pi}(s^{\prime})
.
Taking the positive part
f(s)=(\cdot)_{+}
and using
(a+b)_{+}\leq a_{+}+b_{+}
, we get the recursion:

f(s)\leq\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h-1}}{\mathbb{P}}[s|s^{\prime},\pi^{\dagger}(s^{\prime})]f(s^{\prime})+\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h-1}}\sum_{a^{\prime}\neq\pi^{\dagger}(s^{\prime})}{\mathbb{P}}[s|s^{\prime},a^{\prime}]q^{\pi}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}).

(34)

This recursion implies that the occupancy difference propagates from layer
h-1
or is generated by suboptimal actions (relative to
\pi^{\dagger}
).

Summing over all states
s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h}
at layer
h
:

\displaystyle\sum_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h}}f(s)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h-1}}f(s^{\prime})\underbrace{\sum_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h}}{\mathbb{P}}[s|s^{\prime},\pi^{\dagger}(s^{\prime})]}_{=1}+\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h-1}}\sum_{a^{\prime}\neq\pi^{\dagger}(s^{\prime})}q^{\pi}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\underbrace{\sum_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h}}{\mathbb{P}}[s|s^{\prime},a^{\prime}]}_{=1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h-1}}f(s^{\prime})+\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h-1}}\sum_{a^{\prime}\neq\pi^{\dagger}(s^{\prime})}q^{\pi}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}).

Unrolling this recurrence from
h=1
to
H
(with
f(s_{1})=0
), we obtain:

\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h}}f(s)\leq\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{k}}\sum_{a^{\prime}\neq\pi^{\dagger}(s^{\prime})}q^{\pi}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\right)\leq H\sum_{(s,a):a\neq\pi^{\dagger}(s)}q^{\pi}(s,a).

∎

Lemma B.9
.

The penalty term
\texttt{pena}_{t}
can be bounded as following:

\displaystyle\texttt{pena}_{t}\leq\left(2+2H^{1/\alpha}S^{1-1/\alpha}\right)\beta\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha-1}\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}.

Proof.

We follow a recursive analysis similar to
16
(Lemma 20). Recall the penalty term from the FTRL decomposition:

\texttt{pena}_{t}=\left(\Psi_{t}({\bm{\rho}}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}})-\Psi_{t-1}({\bm{\rho}}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}})\right)-\left(\Psi_{t}({\bm{x}}_{t})-\Psi_{t-1}({\bm{x}}_{t})\right).

(35)

Recall the Tsallis-type regularizer
\Psi_{t}({\bm{x}})=-\eta_{t}^{-1}\sum_{(s,a)}x(s,a)^{1/\alpha}
with
\eta_{t}^{-1}=\sigma t^{1/\alpha}
. The increment in the inverse learning rate is:

\eta_{t}^{-1}-\eta_{t-1}^{-1}=\beta\cdot\sigma\left(t^{1/\alpha}-(t-1)^{1/\alpha}\right)\leq\beta\cdot\frac{2\sigma}{\alpha}t^{1/\alpha-1},

(36)

where the inequality uses the mean value theorem:
t^{1/\alpha}-(t-1)^{1/\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}(t-1)^{1/\alpha-1}\leq 2\cdot\frac{1}{\alpha}t^{1/\alpha-1}
for
t\geq 2
.

For any occupancy measure
{\bm{q}}
, we have:

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}({\bm{q}})-\Psi_{t-1}({\bm{q}})

\displaystyle=-(\eta_{t}^{-1}-\eta_{t-1}^{-1})\sum_{(s,a)}q(s,a)^{1/\alpha}.

Thus, substituting into
Equation

35
:

\displaystyle\texttt{pena}_{t}

\displaystyle=(\eta_{t}^{-1}-\eta_{t-1}^{-1})\left(\sum_{(s,a)}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}-\sum_{(s,a)}{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}\right).

(37)

Since
{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s,a)=0
for
a\neq{\mathring{\pi}}(s)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{(s,a)}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}-\sum_{(s,a)}{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}=\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}+\sum_{s}\left(x_{t}(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))^{1/\alpha}-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s)^{1/\alpha}\right).

(38)

The first sum is exactly the suboptimal term we want. The key challenge is to bound the second sum by suboptimal terms. We use the concavity of
u\mapsto u^{1/\alpha}
and the layer structure of the MDP.

For each state
s
at layer
h
, define
x_{t}(s):=\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}x_{t}(s,a)
as the state marginal under
x_{t}
. We have the recursive relation:

x_{t}(s)=\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h-1}}\sum_{a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}{\mathbb{P}}[s|s^{\prime},a^{\prime}]x_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}).

The second term in the decomposition is
\sum_{s}(x_{t}(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))^{1/\alpha}-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s)^{1/\alpha})
. Consider the set of states where
x_{t}(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))\geq{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s)
(otherwise the term is negative).
Using the concavity of
u\mapsto u^{1/\alpha}
, we have:

\displaystyle x_{t}(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))^{1/\alpha}-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s)^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq(x_{t}(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))-\rho^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s))_{+}^{1/\alpha}

Summing over all states and applying Hölder’s inequality with
p=\alpha,q=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{s}(x_{t}(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))^{1/\alpha}-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s)^{1/\alpha})_{+}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s}(x_{t}(s)-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s))_{+}^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{s}(x_{t}(s)-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s))_{+}\right)^{1/\alpha}\left(\sum_{s}1\right)^{1-1/\alpha}.

Using
Lemma

4.4
with
q^{\pi}=x_{t}
and
q^{\pi^{\dagger}}={\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}
, we have

RHS

\displaystyle\leq\left(H\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)\right)^{1/\alpha}S^{1-1/\alpha}

\displaystyle=H^{1/\alpha}S^{1-1/\alpha}\left(\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)\right)^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq H^{1/\alpha}S^{1-1/\alpha}\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha},

where the last step uses the inequality
(\sum z_{i})^{1/\alpha}\leq\sum z_{i}^{1/\alpha}
for
\alpha\geq 1
.

Substituting this back into
Equation

38
:

\displaystyle\sum_{(s,a)}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}-\sum_{(s,a)}{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}+H^{1/\alpha}S^{1-1/\alpha}\cdot\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+H^{1/\alpha}S^{1-1/\alpha}\right)\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}.

Finally, multiplying by the learning rate increment
\eta_{t}^{-1}-\eta_{t-1}^{-1}\leq\beta\cdot\frac{2\sigma}{\alpha}t^{1/\alpha-1}
gives the result:

\displaystyle\texttt{pena}_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\beta\cdot\frac{2+2H^{1/\alpha}S^{1-1/\alpha}}{\alpha}\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha-1}\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2+2H^{1/\alpha}S^{1-1/\alpha}\right)\beta\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha-1}\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}.

∎

B.5
Bound the Skipping Term

Lemma B.10
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\texttt{skip}_{t}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]\leq 2C^{1-\alpha}(1+H^{\alpha}S^{1-\alpha})\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha-1}\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}.

Proof.

We follow a recursive layer-by-layer analysis similar to
Lemma

B.9
. The skipping error is
\ell_{t}(s,a)-{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s,a)=\ell_{t}(s,a)\mathbbm{1}[|\ell_{t}(s,a)|>\tau_{t}(s,a)]
. We denote this term as
\Delta^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s,a)
.
For any
(s,a)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\Delta_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,a)|\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}]

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}[|\ell_{t}(s,a)|\cdot\mathbbm{1}[|\ell_{t}(s,a)|>\tau_{t}(s,a)]\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}]

(39)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[|\ell_{t}(s,a)|^{\alpha}\cdot\tau_{t}(s,a)^{1-\alpha}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}]

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{\alpha}\cdot C^{1-\alpha}\sigma^{1-\alpha}t^{1/\alpha-1}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\leq C^{1-\alpha}\cdot\sigma\cdot t^{1/\alpha-1}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha-1}

We decompose the skipping error into contributions from suboptimal actions and the optimal action. Since
{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s,a)\neq 0\iff a={\mathring{\pi}}(s)
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{(s,a)}(x_{t}(s,a)-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s,a))\cdot\left(\Delta_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,a)+b^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s,a)\right)\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{(s,a)}|x_{t}(s,a)-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s,a)|\cdot|\Delta_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,a)|\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{(s,a)}(x_{t}(s,a)-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s,a))\cdot b_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,a)\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)\cdot\mathbb{E}[|\Delta_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,a)|\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}]+\sum_{s}|x_{t}(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s)|\cdot\mathbb{E}[|\Delta_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))|\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)\cdot b_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,a)+\sum_{s}(x_{t}(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s))\cdot b_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s)).

Notice that by definition, we have

\displaystyle b_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,a)=C^{1-\alpha}\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha-1}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha-1},

we further have

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{(s,a)}(x_{t}(s,a)-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s,a))\cdot\left(\Delta_{t}^{\text{skip}}(s,a)+b^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s,a)\right)\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)\cdot(C^{1-\alpha}\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha-1}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha-1})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{s}|x_{t}(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s)|\cdot(C^{1-\alpha}\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha-1}x_{t}(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))^{1/\alpha-1})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{s}(x_{t}(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s))\cdot(C^{1-\alpha}\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha-1}x_{t}(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))^{1/\alpha-1})

\displaystyle=2C^{1-\alpha}\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha-1}\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\quad+2C^{1-\alpha}\cdot\sigma t^{1/\alpha-1}\sum_{s}\left(x_{t}(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s)\right)_{+}\cdot(x_{t}(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))^{1/\alpha-1})

For the optimal action
\mathring{a}={\mathring{\pi}}(s)
, we only need to bound the terms where
x_{t}(s,\mathring{a})>{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s)
(as negative terms vanished). In this regime,
x_{t}(s,\mathring{a})>{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{s}(x_{t}(s,\mathring{a})-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s))_{+}\cdot x_{t}(s,\mathring{a})^{1/\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s}(x_{t}(s)-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s))_{+}^{1/\alpha}\cdot(x_{t}(s,\mathring{a})-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s))_{+}^{1-1/\alpha}x_{t}^{1/\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s}(x_{t}(s)-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s))_{+}^{1/\alpha}\cdot\left(\frac{(x_{t}(s,\mathring{a})-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s))_{+}}{x_{t}(s,\mathring{a})}\right)^{1-1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s}(x_{t}(s)-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s))_{+}^{1/\alpha}.

Using the same argument as in
Lemma

B.9
(the Hölder inequality with
Lemma

4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{s}(x_{t}(s)-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s))_{+}^{1/\alpha}\leq H^{\alpha}S^{1-\alpha}\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}.

Summing up the bounds, we final get the result:

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{(s,a)}(x_{t}(s,a)-{\rho}^{{\mathbb{P}},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s,a))\cdot(\ell_{t}(s,a)-{\ell}^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s,a)+b^{\text{skip}}_{t}(s,a))\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2C^{1-\alpha}(1+H^{\alpha}S^{1-\alpha})\sigma t^{1/\alpha-1}\sum_{(s,a)\neq(s,{\mathring{\pi}}(s))}x_{t}(s,a)^{1/\alpha}.

∎

Self-Bounding Bound.

C.1

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}{V}^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi_{t}}(s_{1};\bm{\ell}_{t})-V^{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{i(t)},\pi_{t}}(s_{1};\bm{\ell}^{\text{pess}}_{t})\right]}_{\textsc{TransErr}}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}V^{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{i(t)},\pi_{t}}(s_{1};\widehat{\bm{\ell}}_{t})-V^{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{i(t)},{\mathring{\pi}}}(s_{1};\widehat{\bm{\ell}}_{t})\right]}_{\textsc{EstReg}}

Lemma C.4

(Pessimistic Value Function)

Under event
{\mathcal{E}}

We prove this by induction on the horizon

•

Base case:

•

Induction step: Suppose the lemma holds for

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}[Q^{\widehat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{i(t)},\pi}(s_{h},a;{\bm{\ell}}^{\text{pess}}_{t})\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[{\ell}_{t}(s_{h},a)-D\cdot B_{i}(s_{h},a)\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}]+\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h+1}}\widehat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{i(t)}[s^{\prime}|s_{h},a]\mathbb{E}[V^{\widehat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{i(t)},\pi}(s^{\prime};{\bm{\ell}}^{\text{pess}}_{t})\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}]

\displaystyle\quad-D\cdot B_{i}(s_{h},a)+\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h+1}}\left(\widehat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{i(t)}[s^{\prime}|s_{h},a]-{\mathbb{P}}[s^{\prime}|s_{h},a]\right)\mathbb{E}[V^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}(s^{\prime};{\bm{\ell}}_{t})\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\ell_{t}(s_{h},a)\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}\right]+\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h+1}}{\mathbb{P}}[s^{\prime}|s_{h},a]\mathbb{E}[V^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}(s^{\prime};{\bm{\ell}}_{t})\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}]

where the first inequality holds by induction hypothesis, and the second inequality holds since
\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h+1}}\left(\widehat{{\mathbb{P}}}_{i(t)}[s^{\prime}|s_{h},a]-{\mathbb{P}}[s^{\prime}|s_{h},a]\right)\mathbb{E}[V^{{\mathbb{P}},\pi}(s^{\prime};{\bm{\ell}}_{t})\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}]-D\cdot B_{i}(s_{h},a)\leq 0

and

Lemma C.6
