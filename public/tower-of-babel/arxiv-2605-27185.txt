Integrable equations with negative evolution numbers.
A. K. Pogrebkov

Abstract
Two integrable systems are constructed in a ( 2 + 1 ) (2+1) -dimensional space. Each of these systems involves two evolutions with negative numbers.



1
Introduction

It is well known that the times of hierarchies
(2+1)
-dimensional integrable systems can be conveniently introduced by means of commutators, see
[
1
]
. Say in the case of the Davey–Stewartson (DS) hierarchy,
[
2
]
, we have two sets ot times:

B_{t_{m}}=[A^{m},B],\qquad B_{x_{m}}=i[\sigma A^{m},B],

(1.1)

where

\sigma^{2}=1,\qquad[\sigma,A]=0,\qquad\sigma B+B\sigma=0.

(1.2)

Here
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,
m\geq 1
,
A
and
B
are arbitrary elements of an associative algebra
\mathcal{A}
with unity
I
. We can consider elements
A
,
B
, and
\sigma
as
2\times 2
matrices,
A
to be proportional to the unity matrix
I
,
B
to be the off-diagonal matrix and
\sigma=\sigma_{3}
, the Pauli matrix. These relations are not mutually independent. For instance, for any
A
and
B
we have identities

[A^{2m},B]=\sigma[A^{m},[\sigma A^{m},B]],

(1.3)

see
[
3
]
. Commutator identities of this type lead due to (
1.1
) to linear differential equations for
B
. Thus for
m=1
we get

B_{t_{2}}+i\sigma B_{t_{1}x_{1}}=0,

(1.4)

i.e., a version of the linearized DS equation (the lowest one in the hierarchy). Similar relations exist for the higher values of
m
in (
1.1
), see
[
3
]

In order to raise a linear differential equation to a nonlinear integrable one, we must select one of the variables
t_{m}
,
x_{m}
as the
leading
, in a sense that all other derivatives will be given as differential operators with respect to this one. If we choose
t_{1}
as leading then by means of (

) for

AB=(\partial_{t_{1}}+z)B,\qquad BA=zB,

(1.5)

where we took into account that operator

. Thus by (

B_{t_{m}}=[(z+\partial_{t_{1}})^{m}-z^{m}]B,\qquad B_{x_{m}}=i\sigma[(z+\partial_{t_{1}})^{m}+z^{m}]B.

(1.6)

So we see that all evolutions, with the exception of the leading one, are expressed through the latter. Moreover, the effect of all these evolutions depends on the parameter
z
, introduced as an ambiguity in the construction of
A
by (
1.1
) for

[
4

In

]

[

, several other times from (

) and

. The symbol for the composition of two operators is defined as

(FG)(t,x,z)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int dp\int dy\,F(t,x,z+ip)e^{ip(t_{1}-y_{1})}G(y,x,z),

(1.7)

if the integral is well defined in the sense of distributions. Here we denote
t=\{t_{1},t_{2},\ldots\}
,
x=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots\}
,

and
\ldots
denote some finite subsets of times. Symbol of the unity operator

A=z,

(1.8)

plays the special role, indeed. Due to (

) we have for any pseudo-differential operator

(A^{m}F)(t,x,z)=(z+\partial_{t_{1}})^{m}F(t,x,z),\quad(FA^{m})(t,x,z)=z^{m}F(t,x,z),

(1.9)

denotes the

1.7

(AF)(t,x,z)=F_{t_{1}}(t,x,z)+zF(t,x,z)

(FA)(t,x,z)=zF(t,x,z)

F_{t_{1}}=[A,F].

(1.10)

Thus due to (

On this set of operators we introduce

2
System with two evolutions with negative numbers.

2.1
Commutator identities and linear equations.

Let us consider two versions of 2-dimensional Toda chain defined by the commutator identities

\displaystyle[A,[A^{-1},B]]+ABA^{-1}-2B+A^{-1}BA=0,

(2.1)

\displaystyle[A,[\sigma A^{-1},B]]-\sigma ABA^{-1}+\sigma A^{-1}BA=0.

(2.2)

Thus we extend equalities (
1.1
) to
m=-1
, i.e., we set

B_{t_{-1}}=[A^{-1},B],\qquad B_{x_{-1}}=i[\sigma A^{-1},B],

(2.3)

where
\sigma
is defined in (
1.2
). Moreover,
these identities suggest introduction of the discrete “time”
n\in\mathbb{Z}
in
B
. So that now symbol of the pseudo-differential operator
F\in\mathcal{A}
depends on variables
t_{1}
,
t_{-1}
,
x_{-1}
,
n
, and
z
. We also denote shift of the discrete variable
n
as

F^{(1)}(t_{1},t_{-1},x_{-1},n,z)=F(t_{1},t_{-1},x_{-1},n+1,z).

(2.4)

We see (cf.
[
9
]
) that evolution with the negative number leads to introduction of discrete variable. Evolutions of such variables are given as commutators also, but in the group sense, i.e., by similarity transformations. We introduce evolution of the Scattering Data with respect to the discrete variable by means of equalities

B^{(1)}=ABA^{-1},\qquad B^{(-1)}=A^{-1}BA,

(2.5)

so that (
2.1
), (
2.2
) prove that
B
obeys linear differential-difference equations

\displaystyle B_{t_{1}t_{-1}}+B^{(1)}-2B+B^{(-1)}=0,

(2.6)

\displaystyle B_{t_{1}x_{-1}}-i\sigma B+i\sigma B^{(-1)}=0,

(2.7)

where we use (
1.10
).

Eqs. (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) are linearized versions of the Toda chain. Every of them involves only one time with negative number. Thus we have to exclude either commutator
[A,B]
, or shift
ABA^{-1}
. So we have a new commutator identity

[A,[\sigma A^{-1},[\sigma A^{-1},B]]-[A^{-1},[A^{-1},B]]]+4[A^{-1},B]=0,

(2.8)

that due to (
1.10
) and (
2.3
) gives the linear differential equation

\bigl(B_{x_{-1}x_{-1}}+B_{t_{-1}t_{-1}}\bigr)_{t_{1}}-4B_{t_{-1}}=0,

(2.9)

with independent variables
t_{1}
,
t_{-1}
, and
x_{-1}
, where the last two have negative numbers.

On the other hand we can exclude commutator
[A,B]
by means of the similarity transformation (
2.5
). In this way we arrive to the commutator identity

[A^{-1},ABA^{-1}]+\sigma[\sigma A^{-1},ABA^{-1}]+[A^{-1},B]-\sigma[\sigma A^{-1},B]=0,

(2.10)

and definitions (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) show that operator
B
obeys linear differential-difference equation

B^{(1)}_{t_{-1}}-i\sigma B^{(1)}_{x_{-1}}+B_{t_{-1}}+i\sigma B_{x_{-1}}=0.

(2.11)

Thus we have one discrete independent variable
n
and two evolutions
t_{-1}
and
x_{-1}
with negative numbers. It is necessary to understand if equation (
2.11
) admit nonlinearization to the integrable one. For this purpose we have to consider a special version of pseudo-differential operators.

2.2
Pseudo-differential operators.

Following Introduction, such operators denoted as
F
,
G
, etc., are defined by the corresponding symbols
F(t_{1},t_{-1},x_{-1},n,z)
,
G(t_{1},t_{-1},x_{-1},n,z)
, etc., where
z\in\mathbb{C}
is an arbitrary complex parameter. Thanks to (
1.7
) variable
t_{1}
is the leading time variable. Symbol of composition of two pseudo-differential operators is given by means of a pointwise product of their symbols with respect to variables
t_{-1}
,
x_{-1}
, and
n
. The symbol of the unity operator
I
is the unity
2\times 2
matrix, the symbol of the operator
A
is given in (
1.8
). In the following, we consider this space as space of tempered distributions in the arguments of their symbols.

Action of operations (
1.9
) are more transparent in terms of the Fourier transform with respect to

:

\widetilde{F}(p,z)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int dt_{1}\,e^{-ipt_{1}}F(t_{1},z)

(2.12)

(we omit other variables for convenience), so that (
1.7
) takes the form

\widetilde{(FG)}(p,z)=\int dp^{\prime}\widetilde{F}(p-p^{\prime},z+ip^{\prime})\widetilde{G}(p^{\prime},z),

(2.13)

where we omit inessential variables.
Correspondingly, by (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}(p,z)=z\delta(p),

(2.14)

\displaystyle\widetilde{(A^{-1}F)}(p,t_{-1},x_{-1},n,z)=\dfrac{\widetilde{F}(p,t_{-1},x_{-1},n,z)}{z+ip},

(2.15)

\displaystyle\widetilde{(FA^{-1})}(t_{1},t_{-1},x_{-1},n,z)=\dfrac{\widetilde{F}(p,t_{-1},x_{-1},n,z)}{z}.

(2.16)

In terms of the Fourier transform (
2.8
) relations (
2.3
), (
2.5
), and (
1.10
) sound as

\displaystyle\widetilde{B_{t_{-1}}}=\biggl(\dfrac{1}{z+ip}-\dfrac{1}{z}\biggr)\widetilde{B},\qquad\widetilde{B_{x_{-1}}}=i\sigma\biggl(\dfrac{1}{z+ip}+\dfrac{1}{z}\biggr)\widetilde{B},

(2.17)

\displaystyle\widetilde{B^{(1)}}=\dfrac{z+ip}{z}\widetilde{B},\qquad\widetilde{B^{(-1)}}=\dfrac{z}{z+ip}\widetilde{B},\qquad\widetilde{B_{t_{1}}}=ip\widetilde{B}.

(2.18)

Equations (
1.6
) and (
2.5
) allow us to find a representation for the general form of the symbol of operator
B
:

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle(t_{1},t_{-1},x_{-1},n,z)=\int dp\,f(p,z)\biggl(\dfrac{z+ip}{z}\biggr)^{n}\times

\displaystyle\times\exp\Bigl(ipt_{1}+((z+ip)^{-1}-z^{-1})t_{-1}+i\sigma((z+ip)^{-1}+z^{-1})x_{-1}\Bigr),

(2.19)

where

is an off-diagonal
2\times 2
matrix with elements that are arbitrary functions of their variables that guarantees the convergence of the integral and the boundedness of the limits when
t_{1}
tends to infinity. There are two ways to achieve this. The first one is given by choice

. Then (
2.19
) takes the form

\displaystyle B(t_{1},t_{-1},

\displaystyle x_{-1},n,z)=g(z)\biggl(\dfrac{\overline{z}}{z}\biggr)^{n}\times

\displaystyle\times\exp\biggl((\overline{z}-z)t_{1}+\biggl(\dfrac{1}{\overline{z}}-\dfrac{1}{z}\biggr)t_{-1}+i\sigma\biggl(\dfrac{1}{\overline{z}}+\dfrac{1}{z}\biggr)x_{-1}\biggr),

(2.20)

where
g(z)
inherits properties of matrix

above.
For another way to impose condition of boundedness on the integral in (
2.19
) see
[
3
]
.

2.3
Evolutions on the dressing operator.

We define Dressing Procedure by means of the
\overline{\partial}
-problem (
1.14

1.15

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}K_{t_{1}}=K_{t_{1}}B+K[A,B],

(2.21)

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}K_{t_{-1}}=K_{t_{-1}}B+K[A^{-1},B],

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}K_{x_{-1}}=K_{x_{-1}}B+iK[\sigma A^{-1},B],

Notice that (

\overline{\partial}(K_{t_{-1}}A)=K_{t_{-1}}BA+KA^{-1}BA-KB
, where in the l.h.s. we used (
1.13
). Using in the last term (
1.14
), we get

\overline{\partial}(K_{t_{-1}}A+K)=(K_{t_{-1}}A+K)A^{-1}BA
, or thanks to (

\overline{\partial}(K_{t_{-1}}A+K)=(K_{t_{-1}}A+K)B^{(-1)}
. Now shift with respect to the discrete variable

\overline{\partial}(K^{(1)}_{t_{-1}}A+K^{(1)})=(K^{(1)}_{t_{-1}}A+K^{(1)})B
, i.e., combination

\displaystyle K(t_{1},t_{-1},x_{-1},n,z)

\displaystyle=I+\dfrac{u(t_{1},t_{-1},x_{-1},n)}{z}+

\displaystyle+\dfrac{v(t_{1},t_{-1},x_{-1},n)}{z^{2}}+o(z^{-2}),\qquad z\to\infty,

where

K^{(1)}_{t_{-1}}A+K^{(1)}=V_{0}K.

(2.26)

Now, taking “normal ordering” (

\displaystyle K^{(1)}_{t_{-1}}A+K^{(1)}=(I+u^{(1)}_{t_{-1}})K,

\displaystyle K^{(1)}_{x_{-1}}A+iK^{(1)}\sigma=(i\sigma+u^{(1)}_{x_{-1}})K,

\displaystyle K^{(1)}A=KA+K_{t_{1}}+(u^{(1)}-u)K,

3
Jost solutions and compatibility conditions.

The derivation of the compatibility conditions can be simplified by introducing the Jost solution instead of the dressing operator:

\displaystyle\varphi(t_{1},t_{-1},x_{-1},n,z)

\displaystyle=K(t_{1},t_{-1},x_{-1},n,z){z}^{n}\exp\biggl(zt_{1}+\dfrac{t_{-1}}{z}+i\sigma\dfrac{x_{-1}}{z}\biggr).

On the other hand we can check directly compatibility of (

4
Different choices of leading evolutions.

4.1
Leading time

We start with a set of pseudo-differential operators

\displaystyle(F

\displaystyle G)(t_{-1},x_{-1},n,z)=

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int dp\int dy\,F\biggl(t_{-1},x_{-1},n,\dfrac{z}{1+ipz}\biggr)e^{ip(t_{-1}-y)}G(y,x_{-1},n,z),

5
Conclusion.

We have constructed two integrable systems, each of which has two independent variables with negative time numbers. In this case, the system with independent variables
