Interference-induced state engineering and Hamiltonian control for noisy collective-spin metrology
Le Bin Ho, Vu Xuan Tung Duong, Nozomu Takahashi, Hiroaki Matsueda

Abstract
Interference provides a fundamental mechanism for generating and manipulating entanglement in many-body quantum systems. Here, we develop an interference framework in which the nonlinear dynamics of collective spin- 1 2 \tfrac{1}{2} ensembles are mapped onto phase accumulation and self-interference in phase space, providing a direct and physically transparent description of entanglement formation. Within this framework, one-axis twisting produces Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) states, while two-axis twisting generates multi-component GHZ superpositions relevant for multiparameter quantum metrology. Building on this interference-based description, we analyze metrological performance under realistic Markovian noise, including local and collective emission, pumping, and dephasing, and examine the role of Hamiltonian control based on linear and nonlinear interactions. We show that while control can enhance single-parameter sensitivity in a noise-dependent regime, the achievable precision in multiparameter estimation is fundamentally constrained. These results establish interference as a unifying principle linking nonlinear dynamics, entanglement generation, and metrological performance, and reveal intrinsic limitations of multiparameter quantum sensing. Our framework provides broadly applicable insight into the design of robust quantum-enhanced measurement protocols in noisy many-body systems.



I
Introduction

Quantum metrology exploits nonclassical resources, such as entanglement and squeezing, to enhance measurement precision beyond what is achievable using independent subsystems
Giovannetti et al. 2006
;
Degen et al. 2017
. For a probe consisting of
N
independent subsystems, the achievable precision is bounded by the standard quantum limit (SQL), scaling as
1/\sqrt{N}
. By introducing quantum correlations among the subsystems, this bound can be surpassed and, in ideal conditions, the precision can approach the Heisenberg limit with scaling
1/N
. Such quantum-enhanced sensitivities have enabled substantial improvements in precision technologies including atomic clocks
Derevianko and Katori 2011
;
Ludlow et al. 2015
;
Pedrozo-Peñafiel et al. 2020
;
Robinson et al. 2024
, magnetometers
Taylor et al. 2008
;
Nguyen et al. 2024
;
Schmitt et al. 2017
, gravitational-wave detectors
Schnabel et al. 2010
;
Liu and Gong 2020
;
Jia et al. 2024
;
Tobar et al. 2024
, dark-matter detection
Zaheer et al. 2025
;
Jiang et al. 2024
;
Santoso and Ho 2025
;
Fukuda et al. 2025
, and quantum-enhanced imaging
Vernière and Defienne 2024
;
Xie et al. 2025
;
Pearce et al. 2026
.

Collective spin-
\frac{1}{2}
ensembles provide an attractive and experimentally relevant platform for quantum metrology due to their high controllability and naturally collective dynamics
Chase and Geremia 2008
;
Schleier-Smith et al. 2010
;
Qin et al. 2021
;
Poggi and Muñoz-Arias 2024
;
Viet et al. 2023
;
Zheng et al. 2022
;
Kaubruegger et al. 2019
. The coherent spin state (CSS) serves as a classical reference probe but is restricted to SQL performance
Ma et al. 2011
. Quantum-enhanced metrology therefore requires generating nonclassical correlations, typically via nonlinear collective interactions that produce squeezed or highly entangled many-body states
Ma et al. 2011
.

Nonlinear collective interactions provide a powerful mechanism for generating nonclassical collective-spin states for quantum-enhanced sensing. A paradigmatic example is one-axis twisting (OAT)
Wineland et al. 1992
;
Kitagawa and Ueda 1993
, which can generate strong spin squeezing
Płodzień et al. 2022
;
Shen and Duan 2013
;
Carrasco et al. 2022
;
Auccaise and de Castro 2025
and, at specific evolution times, produce maximally entangled Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) states with optimal noiseless sensitivity
Zhang et al. 2024
;
Xia and Twamley 2016
. To achieve faster and more symmetric entanglement generation, two-axis twisting (TAT), has also been proposed and can produce more complex GHZ superpositions relevant for multiparameter metrology
Ho 2024
. Beyond state preparation, nonlinear collective dynamics further enable interaction-based quantum metrology, where entanglement generation, parameter encoding, and echo-type readout can be interleaved within a single evolution, yielding improved robustness against detection noise and dephasing
Macrì et al. 2016
;
Haine and Hope 2020
;
Koppenhöfer and Clerk 2023
;
Schulte et al. 2020
;
Davis et al. 2016
;
Nolan et al. 2017
. Related strategies have been explored across OAT, TAT, and twist-and-turn (TNT) dynamics
Haine 2018
;
Mirkhalaf et al. 2018
;
Anders et al. 2018
and supported by experiments demonstrating substantial sensing enhancement in collective spin systems
Chalopin et al. 2018
, reinforcing the perspective that Hamiltonian engineering can enable robust quantum metrology beyond maximally entangled probes
Braun et al. 2018
;
Ho 2024
.

Although the emergence of GHZ and multi-component GHZ structures under nonlinear dynamics is well established, the underlying physical mechanism is often not made explicit. Here, we develop an interference-based framework for collective-spin dynamics. We show that nonlinear evolution can be understood as phase accumulation across collective-spin amplitudes, leading to constructive and destructive interference in phase space. Within this picture, GHZ and multi-component GHZ states emerge naturally as interference patterns at specific evolution times. This approach provides a direct and general link between nonlinear dynamics, entanglement generation, and metrological resources.

In realistic sensing platforms, decoherence remains a fundamental limitation. Spin ensembles are subject to both local and collective noise processes
Shammah et al. 2018
, including emission, pumping, and dephasing, which degrade quantum coherence and suppress the quantum Fisher information (QFI), thereby imposing an optimal interrogation time beyond which the estimation precision rapidly deteriorates. A broad range of control strategies has been developed to counteract these effects. Optimization-based approaches directly target metrological performance by maximizing the QFI under open-system dynamics, using variational formulations
Yang et al. 2022
;
Zuñiga Castro et al. 2024
, adaptive feedback
Pang and Jordan 2017
;
Lin et al. 2021
,
optimal control
Titum et al. 2021
;
Gardner et al. 2025
, and hybrid quantum-classical algorithms to co-design state preparation, parameter encoding, and measurement
Yang et al. 2021
;
Meyer et al. 2021
;
Le et al. 2023
;
MacLellan et al. 2024
. At the physical implementation level, Hamiltonian engineering and time-dependent modulation reshape the effective system-environment coupling where dynamical decoupling and related pulse sequences average out unwanted interactions, suppress dephasing, and stabilize QFI scaling
Zhou et al. 2023
;
Sekatski et al. 2016
;
Ji and Hu 2021
;
Lahcen et al. 2025
. In addition, interaction-based echo protocols exploit controlled nonlinear dynamics to reverse phase diffusion and partially recover metrological gain even in the presence of dissipation
Riedel et al. 2010
;
Nolan et al. 2017
. Together, these developments establish quantum control as a central tool for extending the useful sensing window and preserving quantum-enhanced precision in noisy many-body systems.

In this work, we develop an interference-based framework for collective-spin dynamics and employ it to assess quantum metrology under realistic decoherence. We begin by analyzing state formation under the OAT and TAT dynamics, showing that the emergence of GHZ and multi-component GHZ states can be understood as self-interference patterns in phase space. Building on this perspective, we evaluate their metrological performance in the presence of local and collective noise by computing the QFI and the corresponding metrological gain. We then investigate Hamiltonian control protocols, designed to reshape the coherent dynamics at the Hamiltonian level, based on linear and nonlinear interactions to determine how control modifies the noise-interference interplay. Finally, we extend the analysis to three-parameter magnetic-field estimation and characterize the attainable precision using the weighted quantum Cramér-Rao bound. This unified treatment clarifies the roles of interference, decoherence, and Hamiltonian control, and identifies the regimes in which collective control can, and cannot, enhance metrological performance in noisy spin ensembles.

The remainder of this work is organized as follows. In Sec.

, we present the interference-based framework and analyze the generation of GHZ and multi-component GHZ states under nonlinear collective-spin dynamics. Sec.
III
investigates single-parameter metrology with GHZ probes in the presence of realistic noise. In Sec.
IV
, we examine control-assisted metrology, focusing on Hamiltonian engineering and its impact on noise resilience. Sec.
V
extends the analysis to multiparameter magnetic-field estimation using multi-component GHZ probes. Finally, Sec.
VI
summarizes the main results and outlines future directions.

II
Interference-based generation of GHZ and multi-GHZ states

II.1
Spin ensemble

We consider an ensemble of
N
spin-
\frac{1}{2}
particles with the joint Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes N}
. The collective behavior of the ensemble is captured by the total spin operators

J_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{\mu}^{(i)},\ \mu\in\{x,y,z\},

where
\sigma_{\mu}^{(i)}
is the Pauli matrix acting on the
i
th spin. These operators generate a collective representation of the rotation group
\mathrm{SU}(2)
on
\mathcal{H}
and satisfy the angular-momentum algebra

[J_{\mu},J_{\nu}]=i\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\pi}\,J_{\pi},

with
\epsilon_{\mu\nu\pi}
denotes the Levi-Civita symbol. The representation
(\tfrac{1}{2})^{\otimes N}
can be decomposed into irreducible subspaces labeled by the total spin quantum number
j
,

\displaystyle(\tfrac{1}{2})^{\otimes N}=\bigoplus_{j=\{0\text{ or }1/2\}}^{N/2}\,\mathcal{H}_{j}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{j}},

(1)

where
\mathcal{H}_{j}
is the spin-
j
subspace of dimension
2j+1
, and
d_{j}
is its multiplicity
Sakurai and Napolitano 2010
.
The standard basis in each sector is given by
the Dicke state
|j,m\rangle
, which is the eigenstate of
J^{2}
and
J_{z}
,

\displaystyle J^{2}|j,m\rangle=j(j+1)|j,m\rangle,\ J_{z}|j,m\rangle=m|j,m\rangle,

(2)

with
m=-j,-j+1,\dots,j
. The largest sector
j=N/2
corresponds to the fully symmetric subspace, which is invariant under particle permutations and has the reduced dimension
N+1

French and Rickles 2003
.

In many collective-spin platforms, the dynamics preserve permutation symmetry
Sun et al. 2008
;
Hansenne et al. 2022
;
Novo et al. 2013
;
Barnes et al. 2022
;
Ho et al. 2019
and therefore remain in the symmetric sector
j=N/2
. In the presence of decoherence or imperfections that break permutation symmetry, the system can populate sectors with total spin
j<N/2

Ho et al. 2020
, and the dynamics are no longer confined to the symmetric manifold. In the fully symmetric sector
j=N/2
, these states admit the explicit form

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{N}{2},m\Big\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{N}{\frac{N}{2}+m}}}\sum_{\text{permutations}}|\uparrow\cdots\uparrow\downarrow\cdots\downarrow\rangle,

(3)

where
\frac{N}{2}+m
spins are in
|\!\!\uparrow\rangle
and
\frac{N}{2}-m
spins are in
|\!\!\downarrow\rangle
.

Important examples include the coherent spin state (CSS),

\Big|\frac{N}{2},\frac{N}{2}\Big\rangle=|\!\!\uparrow\uparrow\cdots\uparrow\rangle,

and the GHZ state,

|\psi_{{\rm GHZ}_{z}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\Big|\frac{N}{2},\frac{N}{2}\Big\rangle+\Big|\frac{N}{2},-\frac{N}{2}\Big\rangle\right).

Nonlinear collective interactions enable the generation of highly nonclassical states within the symmetric spin manifold. In particular, nonlinear dynamics such as OAT, TAT, and TNT can produce strong squeezing and multipartite entanglement, providing versatile resources for both single-parameter and multiparameter quantum metrology
Ho 2024
.

II.2
GHZ state formation via OAT

To generate a GHZ state, we apply the OAT evolution along the
x
axis to an ensemble initialized in the ground state of
H_{0}=\omega J_{z}\;,\omega>0
, namely
|N/2,-N/2\rangle
.
The dynamics are governed by the OAT Hamiltonian

H_{\text{OAT}}=\chi J_{x}^{2},

where
\chi
denotes the nonlinear interaction strength. The corresponding time-evolution operator is

U_{\text{OAT}}(t)=\exp(-i\chi tJ_{x}^{2}).

Starting from the coherent state, the OAT generates spin squeezing by imprinting the nonlinear phases between Dicke components. As
\chi t
increases, strong correlations and interference patterns emerge in phase space. Figure
1
(a) shows the Husimi distributions for different squeezing angles
\chi t
, illustrating the emergence of these interference patterns.
At
\chi t=\pi/2
, the interference is maximal and the state becomes the GHZ state

\displaystyle|\psi_{{\rm GHZ}_{z}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Big(|N/2,N/2\rangle+|N/2,-N/2\rangle\Big),

(4)

which is a coherent superposition of the two extremal Dicke states along the
z
axis.

II.3
Multi-GHZ state formation via TAT

TAT generates squeezing via nonlinear twisting about two orthogonal axes. Starting from the GHZ state
|\psi_{{\rm GHZ}_{z}}\rangle
, the system evolves under

H_{\rm TAT}=\chi(J_{x}^{2}-J_{y}^{2}).

This evolution deforms the two extremal Dicke components in opposite directions, so that the Husimi distribution spreads from the
\pm z
poles toward the
\pm x
and
\pm y
axes
Ho 2024
, as shown in Fig.
1
(b).

At the appropriate twisting time, squeezing and self-interference produce a coherent superposition of GHZ components aligned along different axes. The resulting multi-GHZ state can be written as

\displaystyle|\psi_{\text{mGHZ}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\Big(\big|\psi_{{\rm GHZ}_{x}}\big\rangle+\big|\psi_{{\rm GHZ}_{y}}\big\rangle+\big|\psi_{{\rm GHZ}_{z}}\big\rangle\Big),

(5)

where
\mathcal{N}
is a normalization constant and
|\psi_{{\rm GHZ}_{\mu}}\rangle
denotes a GHZ state polarized along the
\mu
axis. This evolution is illustrated in Fig.
1
(b). Notably,
|\psi_{\text{mGHZ}}\rangle
has been shown to be an optimal resource for multiparameter estimation
Ho 2024
.

III
Single-parameter metrology with GHZ probes under noise

III.1
Metrology scheme for sensing an external field

We consider a standard quantum metrology protocol where an ensemble of
N
two-level particles is used to estimate an external field. The probe-field coupling is described by

H(\phi)=\phi J_{z},

where
\phi
is the unknown parameter to be estimated (e.g., a magnetic-field amplitude). During the sensing time, the probe accumulates a
\phi
-dependent phase, which is then inferred from an appropriate measurement.

We initialize the probe in the GHZ state
\rho_{0}=|\psi_{{\rm GHZ}_{z}}\rangle\langle\psi_{{\rm GHZ}_{z}}|
, which can be prepared via OAT evolution. Here, we assume that the OAT preparation occurs on timescales shorter than the dominant dissipation rates, allowing us to neglect decoherence during state generation and attribute dissipation effects solely to the sensing process.

During the sensing time
t
, the probe state evolves according to the master equation

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{\phi}(t)}{dt}=-i\big[H(\phi),\rho_{\phi}(t)\big]+\mathcal{L}\!\left[\rho_{\phi}(t)\right],

(6)

where
\mathcal{L}
is the Lindblad generator describing decoherence. The estimation precision is characterized by the quantum Fisher information (QFI),

\displaystyle Q(t)=2\sum_{k,l}\frac{\big|\langle k|\partial_{\phi}\rho_{\phi}(t)|l\rangle\big|^{2}}{\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{l}},

(7)

where
\rho_{\phi}(t)=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}|k\rangle\langle k|
is the spectral decomposition and the sum runs over all pairs satisfying
\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{l}\neq 0
. The QFI determines the quantum Cramér-Rao bound,

\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\phi})\geq\frac{1}{\nu Q(t)},

where
\nu
is the number of independent repetitions.

III.2
The effect of noise

In practical quantum metrology, the probe is inevitably affected by noise. We consider both local noise and collective noise processes arising from a shared environment, which induce correlated emission, pumping, and dephasing
Shammah et al. 2018
. These effects degrade quantum coherence and reduce the achievable estimation precision. The probe state evolution explicitly gives

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{\phi}(t)}{dt}=-i\Big[H(\phi),\rho_{\phi}(t)\Big]+\sum_{n=1}^{N}

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}\mathcal{L}_{j}[\rho_{\phi}(t)]

\displaystyle+\sum_{j}\Gamma_{j}\mathcal{L}_{j}[\rho_{\phi}(t)],

(8)

where
\gamma_{j}
and
\Gamma_{j}
denote the dissipation rates of local and collective noise channels, respectively. Each Lindblad term takes the standard form

\mathcal{L}_{j}[\rho]=L_{j}\rho L_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\Big\{L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j},\rho\Big\},

with
L_{j}
is the jump operator. The local and collective noise types considered in this work are summarized in Table
1
.

IV
Control-assisted metrology via Hamiltonian engineering

Noise reduces the coherence of the probe state, which directly lowers the QFI and degrades the estimation precision. To mitigate this decoherence and enhance metrological sensitivity, quantum control can be applied during the sensing dynamics to stabilize the probe evolution and enhance the useful quantum correlations.

To mitigate noise-induced decoherence, we introduce a control Hamiltonian during the sensing dynamics. The total Hamiltonian is written as

\displaystyle H_{\text{total}}(\phi)=H(\phi)+H_{\text{ctrl}},

(9)

where
H_{\text{ctrl}}
is an externally applied control term used to protect coherence and enhance the QFI.

Here, we consider several control Hamiltonians relevant for noisy spin sensing, including a linear interaction
H_{\text{ctrl}}=\chi J_{x}
and a nonlinear interaction
H_{\text{ctrl}}=\chi J_{x}^{2}
, where
\chi
is the control strength.
These controls are natural choices for collective spin ensembles, since
J_{x}
corresponds to a global driving field, while
J_{x}^{2}
represents a simple nonlinear interaction capable of generating squeezing and entanglement (i.e., OAT).
Including these controls, the probe state will be governed by the Lindblad master equation in Eq. (
III.2
), with
H(\phi)
replaced by
H_{\rm total}(\phi)
. By tuning
\chi
, the control can partially protect coherence and generate noise-resilient correlations, leading to an enhanced QFI.

\chi\gtrsim 0.3
. By contrast, the nonlinear control provides only a modest improvement and saturates at a smaller value.

These results demonstrate that the optimal control strategy depends crucially on the dominant collective noise mechanism. Under the collective emission, applying control does not improve the metrological performance and can even reduce it at large
\chi
, whereas under the collective dephasing, the linear control provides a robust enhancement and yields the largest integrated gain.

These results can be explained as follows. Under the collective emission, the relaxation is governed by collective jump operators, and strong control competes with this correlated dissipation, reducing the overall gain and causing
\mathcal{I}
to decrease with
\chi
.
Under the collective dephasing, the dominant effect is the correlated phase noise generated by
J_{z}
. Here, the linear control efficiently counteracts the dephasing-induced loss of coherence and produces a broad enhancement of
G(t)
, while the nonlinear control yields only a modest improvement.

V
Multiparameter sensing with multi-GHZ probes

We consider a multiparameter quantum metrology protocol where an ensemble of
N
spin-
\frac{1}{2}
particles is used to estimate a three-dimensional magnetic field
\bm{\phi}=(\phi_{x},\phi_{y},\phi_{z})
. The field couples to the collective spin via

\displaystyle H(\bm{\phi})=\bm{\phi}\cdot\mathbf{J}=\phi_{x}J_{x}+\phi_{y}J_{y}+\phi_{z}J_{z},

(10)

where
\mathbf{J}=(J_{x},J_{y},J_{z})
denotes the total spin operator. During the sensing time
t
, the probe accumulates field-dependent phases that encode the three components of
\bm{\phi}
.

Starting from the initial multi-GHZ state
\rho_{0}
in Eq. (
5
), the system evolution in the presence of decoherence is governed by the Lindblad master equation

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{\bm{\phi}}(t)}{dt}=-i\big[H(\bm{\phi}),\rho_{\bm{\phi}}(t)\big]+\mathcal{L}\left[\rho_{\bm{\phi}}(t)\right],

(11)

where
\mathcal{L}
denotes the Lindblad generator.

The achievable precision is characterized by the quantum Fisher information matrix (QFIM)
\mathbf{Q}
. Using the spectral decomposition, i.e.,

\rho_{\bm{\phi}}(t)=\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}\,|\lambda_{n}\rangle\langle\lambda_{n}|,

the QFIM elements are given by

\displaystyle\big[\mathbf{Q}\big]_{\mu\nu}=2\sum_{n,m}\frac{\langle\lambda_{n}|\partial_{\phi_{\mu}}\rho_{\bm{\phi}}(t)|\lambda_{m}\rangle\langle\lambda_{m}|\partial_{\phi_{\nu}}\rho_{\bm{\phi}}(t)|\lambda_{n}\rangle}{\lambda_{n}+\lambda_{m}},

(12)

where the sum runs over all eigenpairs with
\lambda_{n}+\lambda_{m}>0
and
\mu,\nu\in\{x,y,z\}
.

For any unbiased estimator
\hat{\bm{\phi}}
, the covariance matrix satisfies the multiparameter quantum Cramér–Rao bound (QCRB),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{\bm{\phi}})\succeq\frac{1}{M}\mathbf{Q}^{-1},

(13)

where
M
is the number of independent repetitions. To quantify the overall estimation performance, we use the weighted cost

\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\mathbf{W}\,\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{\bm{\phi}})\right),

which obeys the weighted QCRB

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\geq\frac{1}{M}\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\mathbf{W}\,\mathbf{Q}^{-1}\right),

(14)

with
\mathbf{W}\succeq 0
a weight matrix. In general, this bound may not be saturable due to measurement incompatibility among different parameters. A sufficient condition for asymptotic compatibility is

\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\rho_{\bm{\phi}}(t)\,[L_{\mu},L_{\nu}]\right)=0,

where the symmetric logarithmic derivatives
L_{\mu}
are defined by

\partial_{\phi_{\mu}}\rho_{\bm{\phi}}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\big(L_{\mu}\rho_{\bm{\phi}}(t)+\rho_{\bm{\phi}}(t)L_{\mu}\big).

In the following, we study the impact of noise on the weighted QCRB.

V.2
Quantum control on the weighted QCRB under noise

We next explore whether adding a simple control Hamiltonian can further improve the sensing performance under noise. Since our probe is a collective spin ensemble, a natural choice is to consider nonlinear twisting dynamics, which are well known to generate spin squeezing and multipartite entanglement useful for quantum-enhanced metrology. As a representative example, we apply a TAT control Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle H_{\rm ctrl}=H_{\rm TAT}=\chi\left(J_{x}^{2}+J_{z}^{2}\right),

(15)

where
\chi
denotes the control strength. Figure

shows the weighted QCRB,
\mathrm{Tr}[\mathbf{Q}^{-1}]

t
for both local and collective noise channels, including emission and dephasing for each case.

As shown in Fig.
7
, the TAT control does not lead to a systematic improvement over the uncontrolled dynamics. In most parameter regimes, the bound remains nearly unchanged, and in some cases it becomes slightly larger at long times. This suggests that, for the present three-parameter estimation task, a straightforward application of twisting-based control is not sufficient to enhance the achievable precision under realistic noise.

This outcome is expected because multiparameter quantum sensing generally requires control fields that are tailored to the specific estimation geometry. In particular, the control must simultaneously amplify the relevant QFIM components while mitigating noise effects, which cannot be guaranteed by a fixed nonlinear interaction such as
H_{\rm TAT}
. A systematic design of optimized control protocols, including time-dependent and variationally optimized controls, remains an interesting direction for future studies.

VI
Conclusion

In this work, we developed an interference-based description of collective spin-
\frac{1}{2}
dynamics and used it to elucidate the formation of metrologically useful entangled states under nonlinear twisting interactions. Within this framework, the OAT and TAT naturally generate GHZ and multi-component GHZ structures through nonlinear phase accumulation and self-interference inside the Dicke manifold, providing a transparent physical interpretation of state formation.

We then examined how realistic local and collective noise processes modify these interference patterns and impose a finite optimal sensing time. By evaluating the quantum Fisher information and the corresponding metrological gain, we showed that decoherence progressively suppresses the interference features responsible for enhanced sensitivity. In the single-parameter setting, Hamiltonian-level control can partially mitigate this degradation, with performance strongly dependent on the dominant noise channel. Nonlinear control proves more effective in the presence of emission-type noise, whereas linear driving offers greater robustness under dephasing. In contrast, for three-parameter magnetic-field estimation, fixed twisting-type control does not yield a systematic improvement of the weighted quantum Cramér-Rao bound, indicating that multiparameter enhancement requires more specialized control strategies.
Our results establish a unified connection between nonlinear interference, decoherence, and Hamiltonian control in collective-spin metrology, and provide quantitative benchmarks for realistic sensing platforms.

Future work may explore tailored control protocols optimized directly for multiparameter precision under open-system dynamics, and extensions to non-Markovian noise.

Appendix A
Dynamic of spin system under noise.

We consider a single spin-
\tfrac{1}{2}
particle coupled to a noisy environment.
The system’s density matrix
\rho(t)
evolves according to the Lindblad master equation:

\dot{\rho}(t)=-i[H,\rho(t)]+\sum_{k}\mathcal{D}[L_{k}]\rho(t),

(A.1)

where
H
is the system Hamiltonian,
L_{k}
are the Lindblad operators describing various decoherence channels, and the dissipator is defined as

\mathcal{D}[L_{k}]\rho=L_{k}\rho L_{k}^{\dagger}-\tfrac{1}{2}{L_{k}^{\dagger}L_{k},\rho}.

(A.2)

We analyze below the characteristic dynamics under several physically relevant types of noise: amplitude damping, excitation (pumping), and dephasing.

For a single spin in an external field, the Hamiltonian is typically

H(\phi)=\phi\sigma_{z}.

(A.3)

In the absence of noise, the Bloch vector
\vec{r}(t)
rotates uniformly around the
z
-axis:

\displaystyle\rho(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-iHt}\rho(0)e^{iHt},

(A.4)

\displaystyle\vec{r}(t)

\displaystyle=(r_{x}\cos\omega_{0}t-r_{y}\sin\omega_{0}t,,r_{x}\sin\omega_{0}t+r_{y}\cos\omega_{0}t,,r_{z}).

(A.5)

Thus, the population remains constant while the phase coherence oscillates periodically.

A.1
Emission noise

Emission corresponds to spontaneous relaxation from the excited state
|1\rangle
to the ground state
|0\rangle
, with Lindblad operator

L=\sqrt{\gamma_{-}}\sigma_{-}

(A.6)

where
\sigma_{-}=|0\rangle\langle 1|
and
\gamma_{-}
is the decay rate.

The master equation becomes

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}=-i[H(\phi),\rho]+\gamma_{-}\Big(\sigma_{-}\rho\sigma_{+}-\tfrac{1}{2}\{\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-},\rho\}\Big).

(A.7)

In Bloch-vector form
\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}(I+\vec{r}\cdot\vec{\sigma})
, the dynamics are

\displaystyle\dot{r}_{x}=-\frac{\gamma_{-}}{2}r_{x}-\phi r_{y},\qquad\dot{r}_{y}=\phi r_{x}-\frac{\gamma_{-}}{2}r_{y},\qquad\dot{r}_{z}=-\gamma_{-}(r_{z}+1).

(A.8)

The steady state is
\vec{r}_{\text{ss}}=(0,0,-1)
, corresponding to the ground state
|0\rangle
.
Amplitude damping thus reduces excited-state population and coherence simultaneously, leading to irreversible energy loss.
See Fig.
8
for the state evolution.

Figure 8:
Bloch-sphere trajectories illustrating the time evolution of a single spin subject to various noise types, comparing the cases without control
(\chi=0)
and with control
(\chi=0.2)
.

A.2
Pumping

The inverse process, thermal pumping or excitation, drives the system toward
|1\rangle
.
The corresponding Lindblad operator is

L=\sqrt{\gamma_{+}}\sigma_{+},

(A.9)

with
\sigma_{+}=|1\rangle\langle 0|
.
The Bloch dynamics are

\displaystyle\dot{r}_{x}=-\frac{\gamma_{+}}{2}r_{x}-\phi r_{y},\qquad\dot{r}_{y}=\phi r_{x}-\frac{\gamma_{+}}{2}r_{y},\qquad\dot{r}_{z}=\gamma_{+}(1-r_{z}),

(A.10)

yielding the steady state
\vec{r}_{\text{ss}}=(0,0,+1)
.
This process can model incoherent excitation due to an external pump field or a thermal bath at high temperature.
See Fig.
8
for the state evolution.

A.3
Dephasing

Dephasing destroys coherence without changing population.
The corresponding Lindblad operator is

L=\sqrt{\gamma_{\phi}}\sigma_{z},

(A.11)

where
\gamma_{\phi}
is the dephasing rate.
The evolution equations are

\displaystyle\dot{r}_{x}=-2\gamma_{\phi}r_{x}-\phi r_{y},\qquad\dot{r}_{y}=\phi r_{x}-2\gamma_{\phi}r_{y},\qquad\dot{r}_{z}=0.

(A.12)

The steady state retains its population but loses phase information
(r_{x},r_{y}\to 0)
.
In other words, the density matrix becomes diagonal in the energy basis, representing a classical mixture of
|0\rangle
and
|1\rangle
.
See Fig.
8
for the state evolution.

A.4
Combined effects

In realistic systems, all three processes may coexist

\dot{\rho}=-i[H,\rho]+\gamma_{-}\mathcal{D}[\sigma_{-}]\rho+\gamma_{+}\mathcal{D}[\sigma_{+}]\rho+\gamma_{\phi}\mathcal{D}[\sigma_{z}]\rho.

(A.13)

This equation describes the most general Markovian noise affecting a single spin.
The steady state corresponds to a thermal equilibrium distribution,

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{ss}}=\frac{1}{2}\big(I+r_{z}^{\text{(ss)}}\sigma_{z}\big),\quad r_{z}^{\text{(ss)}}=\frac{\gamma_{+}-\gamma_{-}}{\gamma_{+}+\gamma_{-}}.

(A.14)

The emission noise dominates at low temperature
(\gamma_{+}\ll\gamma_{-})
, while pumping dominates at high temperature
(\gamma_{+}\gg\gamma_{-})
.
Dephasing accelerates the decay of off-diagonal elements, reducing quantum coherence and thus suppressing the QFI and metrological advantage.
