## What Is This?

*De Magorum Daemonomania Libri IV* (Four Books on the Demon Mania of the Magicians) is one of the most influential pro-persecution treatises in European history. Written by the French jurist Jean Bodin and first published in 1580 (Latin 1581), it argued that witches were real, that they made pacts with demons, and that they must be executed for the good of society.

## Who Was Jean Bodin?

Bodin (1530–1596) was one of the most important legal and political theorists of the Renaissance. He served as a lawyer, magistrate, and advisor to the French king. His *Six Books of the Commonwealth* (1576) established him as a foundational thinker in political philosophy. His work on witchcraft represented the extreme end of the pro-persecution spectrum.

## Key Arguments

- **Denial of witchcraft is heresy** — To say witches don't exist is to contradict scripture and the laws of every Christian nation.
- **Witches must be executed** — Capital punishment is the only appropriate response to witchcraft, both for the crime itself and to prevent the devil's influence from spreading.
- **Witchcraft is a real, tangible threat** — Bodin claimed to have documented cases and used legal reasoning to prove the reality of maleficia (harmful magic).
- **Legal procedure should be streamlined** — He argued against overly technical legal defenses in witchcraft cases, advocating for expedience.

## Bodin vs. Weyer

Bodin explicitly targeted Johann Weyer's *De Lamiis* as the most dangerous skeptical work of the era. Where Weyer argued witches were mentally ill, Bodin insisted they were apostates who chose to serve Satan. The Bodin-Weyer debate became the defining intellectual conflict of the witch trial era.

## Why It Matters## Why It Matters

Bodin is essential reading because he represents the most sophisticated, rigorous case for witchcraft persecution. Unlike firebrand preachers, Bodin was a trained jurist who built a careful legal argument. Understanding his position is essential to understanding why so many educated people accepted witch trials as legitimate.
