Stochastic integration with respect to a Lévy basis
Markus Riedle

Abstract
We develop a stochastic integration theory for predictable integrands with respect to a Lévy basis. Our approach is based on decoupling inequalities for tangent sequences and reduces the construction of the stochastic integral essentially to the deterministic integration theory for infinitely divisible random measures developed by Rajput and Rosiński. We characterise the corresponding class of integrable predictable processes in terms of the semimartingale characteristics associated with the driving random measure and show that the resulting space of integrands possesses a natural Musielak–Orlicz type structure equipped with an F F -norm. Furthermore, we establish continuity properties of the integral operator and a stochastic version of Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem.

Lévy basis, infinitely divisible random measure, stochastic integration, decoupling inequality, tangent sequence, random measure.

1
Introduction

Stochastic partial differential equations can be studied either as infinite-dimensional stochastic evolution equations or within a space-time random field framework. In the latter approach, the driving noise is naturally described by random measures, and a suitable stochastic integration theory with respect to such measures becomes fundamental.

In the Gaussian setting, covariance structures are sufficient to characterise the law of the driving noise and lead naturally to Gaussian random fields and space-time white noise. In the non-Gaussian setting, however, covariance information is no longer sufficient to determine the distribution of the noise, and the random field approach naturally leads to a description of the driving noise in terms of random measures. Consequently, the study of stochastic partial differential equations driven by non-Gaussian noise requires a suitable stochastic integration theory with respect to random measures.

The general theory of stochastic integration with respect to random measures was developed by Bichteler and Jacod
[
4
]
following a stochastic Daniell approach to integration; see also the monograph by Bichteler
[
3
]
. This approach is mathematically elegant and conceptually complete, but the resulting definition of the stochastic integral is rather implicit and often technically difficult to apply in concrete situations. This difficulty was addressed by Chong and Klüppelberg
[
7
]
, who, building on the work of Bichteler and Jacod, characterised the space of predictable integrands in terms of semimartingale characteristics associated with the underlying random measure. Their theory has already been successfully applied to stochastic partial differential equations driven by non-Gaussian noise; see, for example, Balan and Jiménez
[
2
]
and Chong, Dalang, and Humeau
[
6
]
.

The purpose of the present article is to develop an alternative approach for stochastic integration with respect to a Lévy basis based on explicit topological and functional analytic structures. The central idea of our approach is that stochastic integration for predictable integrands can essentially be reduced to the deterministic integration theory by means of decoupling inequalities for tangent sequences. More precisely, once the stochastic integral is constructed for deterministic integrands, decoupling allows one to separate the randomness of the integrand from that of the integrator and thereby extend the integral to predictable integrands. In this way, the integration theory for predictable processes is largely reduced to the deterministic case.

In this article, we develop a stochastic integration theory for predictable integrands with respect to a Lévy basis and characterise the corresponding largest space of admissible integrable processes in terms of the semimartingale characteristics associated with the driving noise. The resulting space of integrands is shown to possess a natural Musielak–Orlicz type structure equipped with an
F
-norm, while the corresponding stochastic integral process is studied in an Émery-type topology. In addition, we establish continuity properties of the integral operator together with a stochastic version of Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem. One advantage of the present approach is that the underlying topological structures are explicit and based on standard functional analytic objects. This makes the stochastic integral technically easier to handle and facilitates the derivation of further structural properties.

The decoupling approach to stochastic integration originated independently in the 1980s in two different contexts. McConnell
[
13
]
developed decoupling methods for stochastic integration in infinite-dimensional spaces, whereas Kwapień and Woyczyński
[
11
]
used decoupling techniques to characterise admissible integrands for semimartingales. McConnell’s approach was later systematically developed by van Neerven, Veraar, and Weis and led to powerful results for stochastic evolution equations driven by cylindrical Brownian motion; see the survey
[
18
]
. The second line of development was further extended and presented in detail in the monograph by Kwapień and Woyczyński
[
12
]
.

The article is intended to be largely self-contained and contains detailed arguments throughout. For this reason, we also revisit the deterministic integration theory of Rajput and Rosiński
[
16
]
, although our presentation contains several modifications inspired by arguments from
[
12
]
. The treatment of predictable integrands follows ideas from our joint work with Bodó
[
5
]
, from which several arguments and aspects of the presentation are adapted.

Infinitely divisible random measures

In this section, we introduce random measures with infinitely divisible distributions. These objects are the foundation to define a Lévy-type noise in space and time. They will play a central role in the construction of stochastic models discussed in the next section.

A
\delta
-ring
{\mathcal{R}}
is a family of subsets of a non-empty set
R
with
\emptyset\in{\mathcal{R}}
and which is closed under finite unions, closed under relative complementation, and closed under countable intersections. We say it includes a partition if there exists
an increasing sequence
(R_{k})_{k\in\N}
of sets
R_{k}
in
{\mathcal{R}}
with
R=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}R_{k}
.
Any
\delta
-ring with
R\in{\mathcal{R}}
is a
\sigma
-algebra.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{R}}
be a
\delta
-ring with a partition on a non-empty set
R
.

(a)

A random measure on
{\mathcal{R}}
is a map
\Theta\colon{\mathcal{R}}\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
satisfying:

\Theta\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Theta(A_{k})\qquad\text{ $P$-a.s. }

for any sequence
(A_{k})_{k\in\N}\subseteq{\mathcal{R}}
of disjoint sets with
\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k}\in{\mathcal{R}}
.

(b)

A random measure
\Theta\colon{\mathcal{R}}\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
on
{\mathcal{R}}
is called independently scattered if

\Theta(A_{1}),\dots,\Theta(A_{n})
are independent for every disjoint sets
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in{\mathcal{R}}
.

(c)

An independently scattered random measure
\Theta\colon{\mathcal{R}}\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
is called
infinitely divisible (resp. Poisson) if
\Theta(A)
is infinitely divisible (resp. Poisson) for each
A\in{\mathcal{R}}
;

In the following, we will typically omit the qualifier independently scattered and simply refer to infinitely divisible random measures, with the understanding that all infinitely divisible random measures considered are independently scattered.

The infinite divisibility property also guarantees that a random measure admits a Lévy–Khintchine representation. To state it, we fix throughout this work the truncation function

\displaystyle\tau\colon\R\to\R,\qquad\tau(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\alpha,&\text{if }\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert\leqslant 1,\\[1.99997pt]
\tfrac{\alpha}{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert},&\text{if }\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert>1.\end{cases}

The following Lévy–Khintchine formula is shown in
[
16
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\Theta\colon{\mathcal{R}}\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
be an infinitely divisible random measure. Then, for every
A\in{\mathcal{R}}
, the characteristic function of
\Theta(A)
is given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\Theta(A)}(\beta)=\exp\left(i\beta a_{0}(A)-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta^{2}q_{0}(A)+\int_{\R}\big(e^{i\beta\alpha}-1-i\beta\tau(\alpha)\big)\,\lambda_{0}(A,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)\right),

for every
\beta\in\R
, where
a_{0}\colon\sigma({\mathcal{R}})\to\R
is a signed measure,

q_{0}\colon\sigma({\mathcal{R}})\to[0,\infty)
is a measure, and

\lambda_{0}\colon\sigma({\mathcal{R}})\otimes\Borel(\R)\to[0,\infty]
is a
\sigma
-finite measure.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Theta\colon{\mathcal{R}}\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
be an infinitely divisible random measure
with characteristic function as in Theorem
2.2
. We define the characteristics of
\Theta
to be the triplet
[a_{0},q_{0},\lambda_{0}]
and its control measure as

\chi\colon\sigma({\mathcal{R}})\to[0,\infty],\qquad\chi(A)=\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert\!(A)+q_{0}(A)+\int_{\R}\big(\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{2}\wedge 1\big)\,\lambda_{0}(A,{\mathrm{d}}\beta),

where
\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert
denotes the total variation of the signed measure
a_{0}
.

3
Deterministic integrands

Let
\Theta
be an infinitely divisible random measure on a
\delta
-ring
{\mathcal{R}}
with characteristics
[a_{0},q_{0},\lambda_{0}]
and control measure
\chi
. To develop the integration theory, it is useful to consider the Radon-Nikodym derivatives of the components of the characteristic triplet. Given that
a_{0}
and
q_{0}
are absolutely continuous with respect to the control measure
\chi
, their densities exist and are defined by

\displaystyle a(r):=\frac{da_{0}}{d\chi}(r),\qquad q(r):=\frac{dq_{0}}{d\chi}(r).

(1)

A disintegration theorem for bimeasures (
[
14
, Th. 2.4]
and localising) guarantees that there exists a kernel
\kappa\colon R\times\Borel(\R)\to[0,\infty]
satisfying

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}({\mathrm{d}}r,{\mathrm{d}}\beta)=\kappa(r,{\mathrm{d}}\beta)\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}r).

(2)

The image of an infinitely divisible random variable under a linear map is again infinitely divisible with known transformation of its characteristics, see
[
17
, Pro. 11.10]
.
Thus, for fixed
A\in{\mathcal{R}}
and
\alpha\in\R
, the random variable
\alpha\Theta(A)
is infinitely divisible with characteristics
[a_{A,\alpha},q_{A,\alpha},\lambda_{A,\alpha}]
satisfying

\displaystyle a_{A,\alpha}=\alpha a_{0}(A)+\int_{\R}\big(\tau(\alpha\beta)-\alpha\tau(\beta)\big)\lambda_{0}(A,{\mathrm{d}}\beta),\qquad q_{A,\alpha}=\alpha^{2}q_{0}(A)

\displaystyle\lambda_{A,\alpha}=\lambda_{0}(A,\cdot)\circ m_{\alpha}^{-1}\qquad\text{on }\Borel(\R\setminus\{0\}),

where
m_{\alpha}\colon\R\to\R
is defined by
m_{\alpha}(\beta)=\alpha\beta
.
Applying the Radon-Nikodym derivatives (
1
) and disintegration (
2
), and letting

\displaystyle a_{\Theta}(r,\alpha):=\alpha a(r)+\int_{\R}\big(\tau(\alpha\beta)-\alpha\tau(\beta)\big)\,\kappa(r,{\mathrm{d}}\beta),

(3)

we can rewrite these characteristics as

\displaystyle a_{A,\alpha}=\int_{A}a_{\Theta}(r,\alpha)\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}r),\qquad\quad q_{A,\alpha}=\int_{A}\alpha^{2}q(r)\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}r)

(4)

\displaystyle\int_{\R}\big(\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{2}\wedge 1\big)\,\lambda_{A,\alpha}({\mathrm{d}}\beta)=\int_{A}\int_{\R}\big(\left\lvert\alpha\beta\right\rvert^{2}\wedge 1\big)\,\kappa(r,{\mathrm{d}}\beta)\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}r).

(5)

Motivated by these representations of the characteristics of
\alpha\Theta(A)
, we define, for
r\in R
and
\alpha\in\R
,

\displaystyle\zeta_{\Theta}(r,\alpha)

\displaystyle=\int_{\R}\left(\left\lvert\alpha\beta\right\rvert^{2}\wedge 1\right)\kappa(r,{\mathrm{d}}\beta)+\alpha^{2}q(r),

(6)

\displaystyle\eta_{\Theta}(r,\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leqslant 1}\left\lvert a_{\Theta}(r,\alpha\beta)\right\rvert.

(7)

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Theta\colon{\mathcal{R}}\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
be an infinitely divisible random measure with control measure
\chi
.
Define the space

{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta}:=\left\{f\in L^{0}_{\chi}(R,\sigma({\mathcal{R}});\R):\iota_{\Theta}(f)<\infty\right\},

where

\iota_{\Theta}(f):=\int_{R}\big(\zeta_{\Theta}(r,\left\lvert f(r)\right\rvert)+\eta_{\Theta}(r,\left\lvert f(r)\right\rvert)\big)\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}r).

Example 3.2
.

Let
\Theta
be a Poisson random measure. From the characteristic function of the Poisson distribution, it follows that the characteristics of
\Theta(A)
is
[a_{0}(A),0,a_{0}(A)\delta_{1}]
, where
a_{0}(A):=E[\Theta(A)]
for all
A\in{\mathcal{R}}
. Theorem
2.2
guarantees that
a_{0}
extends to a measure on
\sigma({\mathcal{R}})
.

The control measure of
\Theta
is given by
\chi(A)=2a_{0}(A)
, so that
a(r)=\tfrac{1}{2}
for all
r\in R
and
\kappa(r,{\mathrm{d}}\beta)=\tfrac{1}{2}\delta_{1}({\mathrm{d}}\beta)
. Consequently, we obtain
a_{\Theta}(r,\alpha)=\tfrac{1}{2}\tau(\alpha)
for each
r\in R
and
\alpha\in\R
. Hence, for any measurable function

, we obtain

\iota_{\Theta}(f)=\int_{R}\left(\big(\left\lvert f(r)\right\rvert^{2}\wedge 1\big)+\big(\left\lvert f(r)\right\rvert\wedge 1\big)\right)\,a_{0}({\mathrm{d}}r).

Hence, it follows that
f\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta}
if and only if

\displaystyle\int_{R}\left(\left\lvert f(r)\right\rvert\wedge 1\right)\,a_{0}({\mathrm{d}}r)<\infty.

.

Let
\Theta

\displaystyle\lim_{k,\ell\to\infty}\sup_{\gamma\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm det}^{1}}E\left[\left\lvert\int_{R}\gamma(r)\big(f_{k}(r)-f_{\ell}(r)\big)\,\Theta({\mathrm{d}}r)\right\rvert\wedge 1\right]=0.

In this case, the stochastic integral of the deterministic function
f
is defined by

I_{\Theta}(f):=\int f\,{\mathrm{d}}\Theta:=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{R}f_{k}(r)\,\Theta({\mathrm{d}}r)\quad\text{in}\;L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R).

The convergence in Part (b) guarantees that
(\int_{R}(f_{k}-f_{\ell})\,\Theta)_{k,\ell\in\N}
is a Cauchy sequence
L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
, and thus the limit is well-defined. Standard arguments show that the limit does not depend on the approximating sequence
(f_{k})
.

The following result exactly characterises the space of deterministic functions integrable with respect to a given infinitely divisible random measure
\Theta
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\Theta\colon{\mathcal{R}}\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
be an infinitely divisible random measure.
Then the space of measurable functions
f\colon(R,\sigma({\mathcal{R}}))\to(\R,\Borel(\R))
integrable with respect to
\Theta
coincides with the space
{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta}
.

We need a few results for the proof of Theorem
3.7
, which we will present in the following.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\Theta\colon{\mathcal{R}}\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
be an infinitely divisible random measure with control measure
\chi
. For

defined in (

satisfies

\int_{R}\sup_{\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leqslant 1}a_{\Theta}(r,\beta f(r))\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}r)=\sup_{\gamma\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm det}^{1}}\int_{R}a_{\Theta}(r,\gamma(r)f(r))\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}r).

Proof.

Since the function
\alpha\mapsto a_{\Theta}(r,\alpha)
is continuous for each fixed
r\in R
, we can define the function

\displaystyle\pi\colon R\times\R\to[-1,1],\qquad\pi(r,\alpha)=\min\{\beta\in[-1,1]:a_{\Theta}(r,\alpha\beta)=\sup_{\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert\leqslant 1}a_{\Theta}(r,\alpha\gamma)\}.

To show that
\pi
is measurable with respect to
\sigma({\mathcal{R}})\otimes\Borel(\R)
, define, for fixed
x\in[-1,1]
, the function

M_{x}\colon R\times\R\to\R,\qquad M_{x}(r,\alpha)=\sup_{-1\leqslant\gamma\leqslant x}a_{\Theta}(r,\alpha\gamma).

The function
M_{x}
is measurable with respect to
\sigma({\mathcal{R}})\otimes\Borel(\R)
, since
a_{\Theta}\colon R\times\R\to\R
is measurable in the first argument and continuous in the second argument. Since, for
x\in[-1,1]
,

\{(r,\alpha)\in R\times\R:\,\pi(r,\alpha)\leqslant x\}=\{(r,\alpha)\in R\times\R:M_{x}(r,\alpha)=\sup_{\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert\leqslant 1}a_{\Theta}(r,\alpha\gamma)\},

we obtain that
\pi
is measurable with respect to
\sigma({\mathcal{R}})\otimes\Borel(\R)
. Since the definition of
\pi
shows

\int_{R}\sup_{\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leqslant 1}a_{\Theta}(r,\beta f(r))\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}r)=\int_{R}a_{\Theta}(r,\pi(r,f(r))f(r))\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}r),

the proof is completed by approximating the measurable function

by simple functions in
{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm det}^{1}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
L
be a real-valued Lévy process with characteristics
(b,q,\lambda)
with respect to the truncation function
\tau
, and let
(\pi_{n})_{n\in\mathbbm{N}}
be a normal sequence of partitions
\pi_{n}=(p_{k,n})_{k=0,\dots,m_{n}}
of

(a)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{m_{n}}E\left[\tau(d_{k,n})\right]=(t-s)b
;

(b)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{m_{n}}E\left[\tau^{2}(d_{k,n})\right]=(t-s)\left(\int_{\R}\left(\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{2}\wedge 1\right)\,\lambda({\rm d}\beta)+q\right)

Proof.

Define
e(r):=E[\tau(L(r))]
for
r\geqslant 0
. Let
A
denote the generator of
L
. For every

continuous, bounded, and locally twice differentiable in

Applying
A

\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{r}E[\tau(L(r))]=A\tau(0)=b.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be independent real-valued random variables. Then

E\left[\left\lvert\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}\right\rvert\wedge 1\right]\leqslant\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}E\left[\tau^{2}(X_{k})\right]+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}E[{\tau}(X_{k})]\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\!\!\!\!\!+\sum_{k=1}^{n}E\left[{\tau}^{2}(X_{k})\right].

Proof.

Independence of the random variables implies

E[\tau(X_{k})\tau(X_{\ell})]=E[\tau(X_{k})]E[\tau(X_{\ell})]\qquad\text{for all }k\neq\ell.

It follows that

\displaystyle E\left[\left\lvert\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tau(X_{k})\right\rvert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}E\left[\tau^{2}(X_{k})\right]+\sum_{k\neq\ell}E[\tau(X_{k})]E[\tau(X_{\ell})]

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{n}E\left[\tau^{2}(X_{k})\right]+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}E[\tau(X_{k})]\right)^{2}.

Applying this inequality together with the Cauchy–Schwarz and Markov inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle E\left[\left\lvert\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}\right\rvert\wedge 1\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant E\left[\left\lvert\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\tau}(X_{k})\right\rvert\wedge 1\right]+P\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}\neq\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\tau}(X_{k})\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(E\left[\left\lvert\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\tau}(X_{k})\right\rvert^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}+P\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}\{\left\lvert X_{k}\right\rvert>1\}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}E\left[\tau^{2}(X_{k})\right]+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}E[{\tau}(X_{k})]\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}E\left[\tau^{2}(X_{k})\right],

which completes the proof.
∎

4
Tangent sequences and decoupling inequalities

The technique of constructing decoupled tangent sequences is a powerful tool to derive strong results on a sequence of possibly dependent random variables. In this section, we briefly recall the fundamental definition and result, see e.g. de la Peña and Giné
[
8
]
and Kwapień and Woyczyński
[
12
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\big(\Omega,\mathcal{F}\!,P,(\mathcal{F}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbbm{N}}_{0}}\big)
be
a filtered probability space and
(X_{k})_{k\in\mathbbm{N}}
an adapted sequence of real-valued random variables. If
\big(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},P^{\prime},(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbbm{N}}_{0}}\big)
is another filtered probability space, then a sequence
(Y_{k})_{k\in\mathbbm{N}}
of real-valued random variables defined on
\big(\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\prime},P\otimes P^{\prime},(\mathcal{F}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbbm{N}}_{0}}\big)
is said to be a decoupled tangent sequence to
(X_{k})_{k\in\mathbbm{N}}
if

(a)

(Y_{k}(\omega,\cdot))_{k\in\mathbbm{N}}
is a sequence of independent random variables on
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},P^{\prime})
for each
\omega\in\Omega
;

(b)

the sequences
(X_{k})_{k\in\mathbbm{N}}
and
(Y_{k})_{k\in\mathbbm{N}}
satisfy for each
k\in{\mathbbm{N}}
that

\mathcal{L}(X_{k}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{k-1})=\mathcal{L}(Y_{k}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{k-1})\quad P\otimes P^{\prime}\!-\!\text{almost surely. }

In Definition
4.1
and throughout, we identify a random variable
X\colon\Omega\to\R
with its canonical extension to the product space
\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime}
, defined by
X(\omega,\omega^{\prime}):=X(\omega)
for all
\omega\in\Omega
and
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}
.

A typical example arising from integration with respect to a process with independent increments is the following:

Example 4.2
.

Let
(\xi_{k})_{k\in\N}
be a sequence of independent, real-valued random variables and set
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}:=\sigma(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k})
.
Let
(\xi_{k}^{\prime})_{k\in\N}
be an independent copy of
(\xi_{k})_{k\in\N}
defined on a second probability space
(\Omega^{\prime},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime},P^{\prime})
and set
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}^{\prime}:=\sigma(\xi_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\xi_{k}^{\prime})
.
Let
(\psi_{k})_{k\in\N}
be a sequence of
{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}
-measurable, real-valued random variables and set
X_{k}:=\psi_{k}\xi_{k}
.
Consider the product space as in Definition
4.1
and define
Y_{k}:=\psi_{k}\xi_{k}^{\prime}
.
Then
(Y_{k})_{k\in\N}
is a decoupled tangent sequence to
(X_{k})_{k\in\N}
. Indeed, for each fixed
\omega\in\Omega
, the sequence
(Y_{k}(\omega,\cdot))_{k\in\N}
is independent on
(\Omega^{\prime},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime},P^{\prime})
. Moreover, for every bounded Borel measurable function
f\colon\R\to\R
, since
\psi_{k}
is
{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}^{\prime}
-measurable and
\xi_{k}
is independent of
{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}^{\prime}
, we have

E\big[f(X_{k})\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}^{\prime}\big]=E\big[f(\psi_{k}\xi_{k})\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}^{\prime}\big]=\int_{\R}f(\psi_{k}\beta)\,\mathcal{L}(\xi_{k})({\mathrm{d}}\beta),

and similarly

E\big[f(Y_{k})\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}^{\prime}\big]=E\big[f(\psi_{k}\xi_{k}^{\prime})\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}^{\prime}\big]=\int_{\R}f(\psi_{k}\beta)\,\mathcal{L}(\xi_{k}^{\prime})({\mathrm{d}}\beta).

Since
\xi_{k}^{\prime}
is an independent copy of
\xi_{k}
, we have
\mathcal{L}(\xi_{k}^{\prime})=\mathcal{L}(\xi_{k})
, which verifies Condition (b) in Definition
4.1
.

In the following theorem and subsequently, we write
E_{P}
and
E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}
to indicate the underlying probability measure with respect to which the expectation is taken.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\big(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,(\mathcal{F}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbbm{N}}_{0}}\big)
and
\big(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},P^{\prime},(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbbm{N}}_{0}}\big)
be filtered probability spaces.
Then there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that, for every
n\in\N
, every adapted sequence

(X_{k})_{k\in\N}
of real random variables on
\big(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,(\mathcal{F}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbbm{N}}_{0}}\big)
with corresponding decoupled tangent sequence
(Y_{k})_{k\in\N}
on
\big(\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\prime},P\otimes P^{\prime},(\mathcal{F}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbbm{N}}_{0}}\big)
satisfy:

(a)

\displaystyle E_{P}\left[\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}\right|\wedge 1\right]\leq c_{1}E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\left[\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}Y_{k}\right|\wedge 1\right]
;

(b)

\displaystyle E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\left[\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}Y_{k}\right|\wedge 1\right]\leq c_{2}\sup_{\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n}\in\{\pm 1\}}E_{P}\left[\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{k}X_{k}\right|\wedge 1\right].

Proof.

See
[
12
, Prop. 5.7.1.(ii)]
and
[
12
, Prop. 5.7.2]
.
∎

5
Integral for predictable integrands

In this section, we introduce a stochastic integral for random integrands with respect to an infinitely divisible random measure. To this end, it is necessary to distinguish a temporal component and to work within a filtration-based framework.
Accordingly, we endow the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
with a filtration
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
.
Throughout this section, let
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},P^{\prime})
be a copy of
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
, and let
(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
denote the corresponding filtration. Product spaces are formed with respect to these copies.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
denote the predictable
\sigma
-algebra on
\Omega\times[0,T]
for some
T>0
, and let
{\mathcal{O}}\subseteq\R^{d}
be a Borel set. A stochastic process

\Xi\colon\Omega\times[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}\to\R

is called predictable if it is measurable with respect to
{\mathcal{P}}\otimes\Borel({\mathcal{O}})
.

For
D\in\Borel(\R^{n})
, let
\Borel_{b}(D)
denote the
\delta
-ring on
D
of all sets
A\in\Borel(\R^{n})
such that
A\cap D
has finite Lebesgue measure.
We extend the notion of an infinitely divisible random measure to include a temporal domain as follows:

Definition 5.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{O}}
be in
\Borel(\R^{d})
.
An infinitely divisible random measure
\Theta\colon\Borel_{b}(\Rp\times{\mathcal{O}})\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
is called a Lévy basis if:

(a)

for every
A\in\Borel_{b}(\Rp\times{\mathcal{O}})
for which there exists
t\geqslant 0
such that
A\subseteq[0,t]\times{\mathcal{O}}
,
the random variable
\Theta(A)
is
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
-measurable;

(b)

for every
A\in\Borel_{b}(\Rp\times{\mathcal{O}})
for which there exists
t\geqslant 0
such that
A\subseteq(t,\infty)\times{\mathcal{O}}
,
the random variable
\Theta(A)
is independent of
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
;

(c)

\Theta(\{t\}\times[-n,n]^{d}\cap{\mathcal{O}})=0

P
-a.s. for all
t\geqslant 0
and
n\in\N
.

Conditions (a) and (b) in Definition
5.1
correspond to the usual conditions of adaptedness and independent increments. Condition (c) ensures that the noise has no fixed times of discontinuity.

Throughout, we fix a finite time horizon
T>0
. Although the filtration and the Lévy basis are defined on
[0,\infty)
, all predictable integrands and stochastic integrals are considered on
[0,T]
.
As in the case of deterministic integrands, we begin by introducing two classes of functions on which our definition of the stochastic integral will be based.

Definition 5.2
.

(a)

A real-valued predictable step process
\Xi\colon\Omega\times[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}\to\R
is said to be of the form

\displaystyle\Xi(\omega,t,r)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\xi_{k}(\omega)\mathbbm{1}_{(t_{k},t_{k+1}]}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}(r),

(15)

where
0=t_{0}<\cdots<t_{n}=T
,
\xi_{k}
is an
{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{k}}
-measurable real-valued random variable, and

A_{k}\in\Borel_{b}({\mathcal{O}})
for
k=0,\dots,n-1
. The space of all real-valued predictable step processes is denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}
.

(b)

The subspace of all real-valued predictable step processes
\Gamma
satisfying

\displaystyle\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega}\sup_{(t,r)\in[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\left\lvert\Gamma(\omega,t,r)\right\rvert\leq 1

is denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}
.

Let
\Xi\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}
be of the form (
15
).
The stochastic integral of
\Xi
with respect to the Lévy basis
\Theta
is defined by
the real-valued random variable

\displaystyle I(\Xi):=\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Xi\,{\mathrm{d}}\Theta:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\xi_{k}\Theta((t_{k},t_{k+1}]\times A_{k}).

(16)

This definition is independent of the chosen representation by finite additivity of
\Theta
and the independence assumptions.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\Theta\colon\Borel_{b}(\Rp\times{\mathcal{O}})\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
be a Lévy basis with control measure
\chi
.
A predictable process
\Xi\colon\Omega\times[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}\to\R
is called integrable with respect to
\Theta
if there exists a sequence
(\Xi_{k})_{k\in\mathbbm{N}}
of processes in
\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}
such that

(a)

(\Xi_{k})_{k\in\mathbbm{N}}
converges
P\otimes\chi
-almost everywhere to
\Xi
;

(b)

\displaystyle\lim_{k,\ell\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma(\Xi_{k}-\Xi_{\ell})\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]=0.

In this case, the stochastic integral of
\Xi
is defined by

I_{\Theta}(\Xi):=\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Xi\;{\rm d}\Theta=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Xi_{k}\;{\rm d}\Theta\quad\text{in}\;L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R).

By choosing
\Gamma\equiv 1
on the support of
\Xi_{k}-\Xi_{\ell}
in Part (b) of Definition
5.3
, we obtain that
\big(\int\Xi_{k}\,{\rm d}\Theta\big)_{k\in\N}
is a Cauchy sequence in probability, and hence converges in
L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
. Moreover, it can be shown by standard arguments that the limit does not depend on the choice of the approximating sequence
(\Xi_{k})_{k\in\N}
.

For an integrable process
\Xi\colon\Omega\times[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}\to\R
, the stochastic integral process
\big(\int_{(0,t]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Xi\,{\mathrm{d}}\Theta:\,t\in[0,T]\big)
is defined by

\int_{(0,t]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Xi\,{\mathrm{d}}\Theta:=\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\1_{(0,t]}\Xi\,{\mathrm{d}}\Theta.

It follows immediately from Definition
5.3
that the integral process is well defined. Moreover, Lemma
5.4
below implies that the integral process has càdlàg paths.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\Theta\colon\Borel_{b}(\Rp\times{\mathcal{O}})\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
be a Lévy basis and
\Xi\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}
. Then, for every
c>0
,

\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}^{1}}P\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lvert\int_{(0,t]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi\,{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\geqslant c\right)\leqslant\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}^{1}}P\left(\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi\,{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\geqslant c\right).

Proof.

Fix
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}^{1}
. Refining partitions if necessary, write

\Gamma\Xi=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\eta_{i}\,\1_{(t_{i},t_{i+1}]}\1_{A_{i}},

where
0=t_{0}<\cdots<t_{n}=T
,
\eta_{i}
is
{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{i}}
-measurable, and disjoint sets
A_{i}\in\Borel_{b}({\mathcal{O}})
. Set

Z_{i}:=\eta_{i}\Theta((t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times A_{i})
and

S_{k}:=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}Z_{i}
for
k=1,\dots,n
, with
S_{0}:=0
.
For
c>0
, define

B_{i}:=\left\{\sup_{k=0,\dots,i}\left\lvert S_{k}\right\rvert<c\right\}
for
i=0,\dots,n-1
.
Then
B_{i}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{i}}
. Hence

\widetilde{\Gamma}:=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Gamma\,\1_{B_{i}}\1_{(t_{i},t_{i+1}]}\1_{A_{i}}

belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}^{1}
. If
\rho:=\inf\{k=1,\dots,n:\left\lvert S_{k}\right\rvert\geqslant c\}
, then on
\{\rho<\infty\}
we have

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\1_{B_{i}}Z_{i}=S_{\rho}.

Consequently,

\left\{\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lvert\int_{(0,t]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi\,{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\geqslant c\right\}\subseteq\left\{\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\widetilde{\Gamma}\Xi\,{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\geqslant c\right\}.

Taking probabilities and the supremum over
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}^{1}
completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

A random measure is a mapping

X\colon\Borel_{b}([0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}})\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)

which satisfies Part (a) of Definition
2.1
for
{\mathcal{R}}=\Borel_{b}([0,\infty)\times{\mathcal{O}})
and Part (a) of Definition
5.1
. For such a random measure
X
and every
\Gamma\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm prd}^{1}
, the stochastic integral can be defined by the same finite-sum
construction as in (
16
). This yields the associated integral process

(\Gamma\cdot X)_{t}:=\int_{(0,t]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\,{\mathrm{d}}X=\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\1_{(0,t]}(s)\Gamma(s,r)\,X({\mathrm{d}}s,{\mathrm{d}}r),\qquad t\in(0,T].

For random measures
X
and
Y
, define

d(X,Y):=\sup_{\Gamma\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm prd}^{1}}E\left[\sup_{t\in(0,T]}\left|(\Gamma\cdot(X-Y))_{t}\right|\wedge 1\right].

This functional can be viewed as a space-time analogue of the Émery metric on the space of real-valued semimartingales introduced in
[
9
]
. Indeed, the random measures are indexed by subsets of
[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}
, while the bounded predictable space-time processes in
{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm prd}^{1}
play the role of bounded predictable integrands in the classical Émery topology.

Now let
\Theta\colon\Borel_{b}([0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}})\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)

be a Lévy basis and let
\Xi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm prd}
. Associated with the integral
I(\Xi)
defined in (
16
) is the random measure

I(\Xi)(B):=\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\1_{B}\Xi\,{\mathrm{d}}\Theta,\qquad B\in\Borel_{b}([0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}).

Its existence will follow from Theorem
6.1
below.
For every
\Gamma\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm prd}^{1}
, we have

(\Gamma\cdot I(\Xi))_{t}=\int_{(0,t]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma(s,r)\Xi(s,r)\,\Theta({\mathrm{d}}s,{\mathrm{d}}r),\qquad t\in[0,T].

Consequently, for all
\Xi_{1},\Xi_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm prd}
,

d\big(I(\Xi_{1}),I(\Xi_{2})\big)=\sup_{\Gamma\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm prd}^{1}}E\left[\sup_{t\in(0,T]}\left|\int_{(0,t]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\big(\Xi_{1}-\Xi_{2}\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}\Theta\right|\wedge 1\right].

Hence, by Lemma
5.4
, the convergence required in Part (b) of Definition
5.3
is precisely convergence with respect to the Émery-type metric
d
on the space of random measures.

Our approach to stochastic integration relies on decoupling inequalities. The following result will be fundamental in what follows.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\Theta\colon\Borel_{b}(\Rp\times{\mathcal{O}})\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
be a Lévy basis, let

0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=T
be a partition of
[0,T]
, let
\psi_{k}
be an
{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{k-1}}
-measurable real-valued random variable, and let
A_{k}\in\Borel_{b}({\mathcal{O}})
for
k=1,\dots,n
. Define

\widetilde{\Theta}\colon\Borel_{b}(\Rp\times{\mathcal{O}})\to L^{0}_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}(\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime};\R),\qquad\widetilde{\Theta}(D)(\omega,\omega^{\prime}):=\Theta(D)(\omega^{\prime}).

Then
\widetilde{\Theta}
is a Lévy basis, and the sequence

\Big(\psi_{k}\widetilde{\Theta}((t_{k-1},t_{k}]\times A_{k})\Big)_{k=1,\dots,n}

defined on

\big(\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\prime},P\otimes P^{\prime},(\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{t_{k}})_{k=0,\dots,n}\big)

is a decoupled tangent sequence to the sequence

\Big(\psi_{k}\Theta((t_{k-1},t_{k}]\times A_{k})\Big)_{k=1,\dots,n}

defined on
\big(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,(\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}})_{k=0,\dots,n}\big)
.

Proof.

Since, for every finite family
D_{1},\dots,D_{m}\in\Borel_{b}(\Rp\times{\mathcal{O}})
, the random vectors

(\widetilde{\Theta}(D_{1}),\dots,\widetilde{\Theta}(D_{m}))
and
(\Theta(D_{1}),\dots,\Theta(D_{m}))

have the same distribution, it follows that
\widetilde{\Theta}
is an independently scattered infinitely divisible random measure. Moreover, the defining properties of a Lévy basis are inherited from
\Theta
by construction.

Now set

\xi_{k}:=\Theta((t_{k-1},t_{k}]\times A_{k}),\qquad\xi_{k}^{\prime}:=\widetilde{\Theta}((t_{k-1},t_{k}]\times A_{k}),\qquad k=1,\dots,n.

Since
\Theta
is independently scattered, the sequence
(\xi_{k})_{k=1,\dots,n}
consists of independent random variables. Moreover,
(\xi_{k}^{\prime})_{k=1,\dots,n}
is an independent copy of
(\xi_{k})_{k=1,\dots,n}
.
Since
\psi_{k}
is
{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{k-1}}
-measurable for each
k=1,\dots,n
, Example
4.2
shows that

\big(\psi_{k}\xi_{k}^{\prime}\big)_{k=1,\dots,n}=\Big(\psi_{k}\widetilde{\Theta}((t_{k-1},t_{k}]\times A_{k})\Big)_{k=1,\dots,n}

is a decoupled tangent sequence to

\big(\psi_{k}\xi_{k}\big)_{k=1,\dots,n}=\Big(\psi_{k}\Theta((t_{k-1},t_{k}]\times A_{k})\Big)_{k=1,\dots,n}.\qed

6
Characterisation of predictable integrands

For a given Lévy basis
\Theta\colon\Borel_{b}(\Rp\times{\mathcal{O}})\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
, let
({\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\Theta})
be the topological vector space of deterministic integrands, which is separable by Theorem
3.5
. We define
L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta})
as the space of equivalence classes of Borel-measurable mappings
\Xi:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta}
, endowed with the
F
-norm

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Xi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}:=E\left[\left\lVert\Xi\right\rVert_{\Theta}\wedge 1\right]\quad\text{for }\Xi\in L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta}).

With this
F
-norm,
L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta})
is complete.

The main result of this section is the following characterisation of the space of predictable integrands which are integrable with respect to a given Lévy basis.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\Theta\colon\Borel_{b}(\Rp\times{\mathcal{O}})\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
be a Lévy basis. A predictable process

\Xi\colon\Omega\times[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}\to\R
is integrable with respect to
\Theta
if and only if the mapping

\omega\mapsto\Xi(\omega,\cdot,\cdot)
belongs to
L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta})
.

This section is devoted to the proof of this result. The first result we require is standard and provides a suitable approximation of predictable integrands.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\Theta\colon\Borel_{b}(\R_{+}\times{\mathcal{O}})\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
be a Lévy basis with control measure
\chi
.
For every predictable representative
\Xi
with
\omega\mapsto\Xi(\omega,\cdot)\in L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta})
, there exists a sequence

(\Xi_{k})_{k\in\N}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}
such that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Xi_{k}-\Xi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}\to 0
and
\Xi_{k}\to\Xi

P\otimes\chi
-almost everywhere on
\Omega\times[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}
.

Proof.

The
\sigma
-algebra
{\mathcal{P}}\otimes\Borel({\mathcal{O}})
on
\Omega\times[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}
is generated by the
\pi
-system

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle:=\big\{A\times(s,t]\times B:A\in{\mathcal{F}}_{s},\,0\leqslant s<t\leqslant T,\,B\in\Borel_{b}({\mathcal{O}})\big\}

\displaystyle\qquad\cup\big\{A\times\{0\}\times B:A\in{\mathcal{F}}_{0},\,B\in\Borel_{b}({\mathcal{O}})\big\}.

Let
H
be the class of all bounded, non-negative predictable processes

Y\colon\Omega\times[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}\to\Rp
for which there exists a sequence

(\Xi_{k})_{k\in\N}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}
such that

0\leqslant\Xi_{k}\uparrow Y

P\otimes\chi
-almost everywhere.
Then
H
contains the indicators of all sets in
\mathcal{C}
, is closed under
positive linear combinations, and is closed under bounded increasing limits.
Hence, by the functional monotone class theorem,
H
contains every bounded,
non-negative
{\mathcal{P}}\otimes\Borel({\mathcal{O}})
-measurable function.

Let first
\Xi
be bounded. Applying the previous paragraph to
\Xi^{+}
and

\Xi^{-}
yields sequences
(\Xi_{k}^{+})
and
(\Xi_{k}^{-})
in
\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}

such that
0\leqslant\Xi_{k}^{\pm}\uparrow\Xi^{\pm}

P\otimes\chi
-almost everywhere.
Define
\Xi_{k}:=\Xi_{k}^{+}-\Xi_{k}^{-}
. Then
\Xi_{k}\to\Xi

P\otimes\chi
-almost everywhere and

|\Xi_{k}-\Xi|\leqslant 2|\Xi|
.
Since

\alpha\mapsto\zeta_{\Theta}(t,x,\alpha)+\eta_{\Theta}(t,x,\alpha)
is
continuous and non-decreasing on
\R_{+}
for every
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}
and
\iota_{\Theta}
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition (
9
), Lebesgue’s dominated convergence
theorem and Fubini’s theorem imply
\iota_{\Theta}(\Xi_{k}-\Xi)\to 0

P
-almost surely. By Theorem
3.5
,

\left\lVert\Xi_{k}-\Xi\right\rVert_{\Theta}\to 0

P
-almost surely, and hence in probability, which proves the claim for bounded
\Xi
.

For a general predictable
\Xi\in L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta})
, define

\Xi^{(n)}:=\Xi\1_{\{|\Xi|\leqslant n\}}
for
n\in\N
.
Then each
\Xi^{(n)}
is bounded and predictable,

\Xi^{(n)}\to\Xi

P\otimes\chi
-almost everywhere,
and
|\Xi^{(n)}-\Xi|\leqslant|\Xi|
.
As above, dominated convergence yields
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Xi^{(n)}-\Xi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}\to 0
.
For every
n\in\N
, choose

(\Xi_{n,m})_{m\in\N}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}
such that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Xi_{n,m}-\Xi^{(n)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}\to 0
and

\Xi_{n,m}\to\Xi^{(n)}

P\otimes\chi
-almost everywhere.
A standard diagonal argument yields a sequence in

\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}
converging to
\Xi
both in

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}
and

P\otimes\chi
-almost everywhere.
∎

The following result is the analogue for predictable integrands of Proposition
3.11
for deterministic integrands. In the next section, we will strengthen this result and obtain a strong continuity property of the integral operator.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\Theta\colon\Borel_{b}(\Rp\times{\mathcal{O}})\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
be a Lévy basis and
(\Xi_{k})_{k\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
a sequence in
\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}
. Then the following are
equivalent:

(a)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}E[\left\lVert\Xi_{k}\right\rVert_{\Theta}\wedge 1]=0
;

(b)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\displaystyle\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]=0
.

Proof.

To prove (a)
\Rightarrow
(b), let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed. Proposition
3.11
enables us to choose
\delta>0
such that for every
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm det}
we have
the implication:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{\Theta}\leqslant\delta\;\Rightarrow\;\sup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{\rm det}}P\left(\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\gamma\psi\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert>\varepsilon\right)\leqslant\varepsilon.

(17)

Choose
k_{0}\in{\mathbbm{N}}
such that the set

A_{k}:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\left\lVert\Xi_{k}(\omega)\right\rVert_{\Theta}\leqslant\delta\right\}

satisfies
P(A_{k})\geq 1-\varepsilon
for all
k\geqslant k_{0}
. By recalling the definition of
\widetilde{\Theta}
and
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},P^{\prime})
from Proposition
5.6
, implication
(
17
) implies for all
\omega\in A_{k}
and
k\geq k_{0}
that

\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}P^{\prime}\left(\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}:\left\lvert\left(\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma(\omega)\Xi_{k}(\omega)\;{\rm d}\widetilde{\Theta}(\omega,\cdot)\right)(\omega^{\prime})\right\rvert>\varepsilon\right)\leq\varepsilon.

Fubini’s theorem implies for all
k\geq k_{0}
and
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}
that

\displaystyle(P\otimes P^{\prime})\left((\omega,\omega^{\prime})\in\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime}\colon\left\lvert\left(\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}\;{\rm d}\widetilde{\Theta}\right)(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\right\rvert>\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}P^{\prime}\left(\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}:\left\lvert\left(\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma(\omega)\Xi_{k}(\omega)\;{\rm d}\widetilde{\Theta}(\omega,\cdot)\right)(\omega^{\prime})\right\rvert>\varepsilon\right)\,P({\rm d}\omega)\leq 2\varepsilon

As
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we obtain

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}^{1}}E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}\;{\rm d}\widetilde{\Theta}\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]=0.

(18)

For each
k\in\mathbbm{N}
and
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}
the integrand
\Gamma\Xi_{k}
lies in
{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm prd}
and has a representation of the form

\displaystyle\Gamma\Xi_{k}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n_{k}-1}\zeta_{\ell}^{k}\xi_{\ell}^{k}\mathbbm{1}_{(t_{\ell}^{k},t_{\ell+1}^{k}]}\1_{A_{\ell}^{k}},

(19)

where
0=t_{0}^{k}<\cdots<t_{n_{k}}^{k}=T
,
\zeta_{\ell}^{k}\xi_{\ell}^{k}
is an
\mathcal{F}_{t_{\ell}^{k}}
-measurable real-valued random variable, and
A_{\ell}^{k}\in\Borel_{b}({\mathcal{O}})
for each
\ell=0,...,n_{k}-1
and
k\in\N
.
Proposition
5.6
guarantees for each
k\in\mathbbm{N}
that the sequence

\Big(\zeta_{\ell}^{k}\xi_{\ell}^{k}\Theta\big((t_{\ell}^{k},t_{\ell+1}^{k}]\times A_{\ell}^{k})\big)\Big)_{\ell=0,...,n_{k}-1}

has the decoupled tangent sequence

\Big(\zeta_{\ell}^{k}\xi_{\ell}^{k}\widetilde{\Theta}\big((t_{\ell}^{k},t_{\ell+1}^{k}]\times A_{\ell}^{k})\big)\Big)_{\ell=0,...,n_{k}-1}.

We conclude from Theorem
4.3
that there exists a constant
c>0
such that, for all
k\in\mathbbm{N}
and
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}
, we have

\displaystyle E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle=E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lvert\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{k}-1}\zeta_{\ell}^{k}\xi_{\ell}^{k}\Theta((t_{\ell}^{k},t_{\ell+1}^{k}]\times A_{\ell}^{k}))\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq cE_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lvert\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{k}-1}\zeta_{\ell}^{k}\xi_{\ell}^{k}\widetilde{\Theta}((t_{\ell}^{k},t_{\ell+1}^{k}]\times A_{\ell}^{k})\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle=cE_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}\;{\rm d}\widetilde{\Theta}\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg].

We conclude from (
18
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E_{P}\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]=0,

which shows (b).

For establishing (b)
\Rightarrow
(a), we assume that
\Xi_{k}
is of the form

\displaystyle\Xi_{k}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n_{k}-1}\xi_{\ell}^{k}\mathbbm{1}_{(t_{\ell}^{k},t_{\ell+1}^{k}]}\1_{A_{\ell}^{k}},

where
0=t_{0}^{k}\leq\cdots<t_{n_{k}}^{k}=T
,
\xi_{\ell}^{k}
is an
\mathcal{F}_{t_{\ell}^{k}}
-measurable real-valued random variable, and
A_{\ell}^{k}\in\Borel_{b}({\mathcal{O}})
for
\ell=0,\dots,n_{k}-1
and
k\in\N
. For every choice of signs
(\varepsilon_{\ell})_{\ell=0}^{n_{k}-1}\in\{\pm 1\}^{n_{k}}
, the process

\Gamma_{\varepsilon}:=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n_{k}-1}\varepsilon_{\ell}\1_{(t_{\ell}^{k},t_{\ell+1}^{k}]}\1_{A_{\ell}^{k}}

belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}^{1}
. Hence, Theorem
4.3
implies that there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Xi_{k}\;{\rm d}\widetilde{\Theta}\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle=E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lvert\sum_{\ell=0}^{n_{k}-1}\xi_{\ell}^{k}\widetilde{\Theta}((t_{\ell}^{k},t_{\ell+1}^{k}]\times A_{\ell}^{k})\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant c\max_{\varepsilon_{\ell}\in\{\pm 1\}}E_{P}\Bigg[\left\lvert\sum_{\ell=0}^{n_{k}-1}\varepsilon_{\ell}\xi_{\ell}^{k}\Theta((t_{\ell}^{k},t_{\ell+1}^{k}]\times A_{\ell}^{k})\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant c\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E_{P}\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg].

(20)

The hypothesis in Part (b) implies that
(\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Xi_{k}\,{\mathrm{d}}\widetilde{\Theta})_{k\in\N}
converges to
0
in
probability. It follows that for every subsequence of
(\Xi_{k})_{k\in\mathbbm{N}}
, there exists a further subsequence for which this convergence is
P\otimes P^{\prime}
-a.s. For ease of notation, we denote this sub-subsequence by
(\Xi_{k})_{k\in\N}
. Thus, there exists a set
N\subseteq\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime}
with
(P\otimes P^{\prime})(N)=0
satisfying

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Bigg(\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Xi_{k}\;{\rm d}\widetilde{\Theta}\Bigg)(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=0\quad\text{for each}\;(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\in N^{c}.

Define the section of the set
N
for each
\omega\in\Omega
by

N_{\omega}=\Bigg\{\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}\colon\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left(\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Xi_{k}(\omega)\;{\rm d}\widetilde{\Theta}(\omega,\cdot)\right)(\omega^{\prime})\neq 0\Bigg\},

where we note that since
\Xi_{k}
are step processes, it holds that

\Bigg(\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Xi_{k}\;{\rm d}\widetilde{\Theta}\Bigg)(\omega,\cdot)=\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Xi_{k}(\omega)\;{\rm d}\widetilde{\Theta}(\omega,\cdot)\qquad\text{for all }\omega\in\Omega.

Fubini’s theorem implies
0=(P\otimes P^{\prime})(N)=\int_{\Omega}P^{\prime}(N_{\omega}){\rm d}P(\omega)
, from which it follows that there exists
\Omega_{1}\subseteq\Omega
with
P(\Omega_{1})=1
such that
P^{\prime}(N_{\omega})=0
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
. In other words, for each fixed
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
, the real-valued random variables

\widetilde{I}(\Xi_{k}(\omega)):=\widetilde{I}(\Xi_{k}(\omega,\cdot,\cdot)):=\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Xi_{k}(\omega,t,x)\;\widetilde{\Theta}(\omega,\cdot,{\mathrm{d}}t,{\mathrm{d}}x)

converges
P^{\prime}
-a.s. to
0
as
k\to\infty
on
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},P^{\prime})
. Since
\widetilde{I}(\Xi_{k}(\omega))
is the sum of independent infinitely divisible random variables, it is also infinitely divisible. Using the notations introduced in (
4
) and (
5
), its characteristics is given as

\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{n_{k}-1}a_{D_{\ell}^{k},\xi_{\ell}^{k}(\omega)},\sum_{\ell=0}^{n_{k}-1}q_{D_{\ell}^{k},\xi_{\ell}^{k}(\omega)},\sum_{\ell=0}^{n_{k}-1}\lambda_{D_{\ell}^{k},\xi_{\ell}^{k}(\omega)}\right),

where
D_{\ell}^{k}:=(t_{\ell}^{k},t_{\ell+1}^{k}]\times A_{\ell}^{k}
.
Since
\widetilde{I}(\Xi_{k}(\omega))\to 0
in
L_{P^{\prime}}^{0}(\Omega^{\prime};\R)
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
, it follows from
Remark VII.2.10 in
[
10
]
as in (
12
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\zeta_{\widetilde{\Theta}(\omega,\cdot)}(t,x,\Xi_{k}(\omega,t,x))\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}t,{\mathrm{d}}x)

\displaystyle=0.

Since
\widetilde{\Theta}(\omega,\cdot)
has the same characteristics as
\Theta
for each
\omega\in\Omega
, we obtain for all
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\zeta_{\Theta}(t,x,\Xi_{k}(\omega,t,x))\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}t,{\mathrm{d}}x)

\displaystyle=0.

As
P(\Omega_{1})=1
and
(\Xi_{k})_{k\in\N}
was assumed to be a subsequence of an arbitrary subsequence, it follows, for all
\varepsilon>0
, and the original sequence
(\Xi_{k})_{k\in\N}
that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}P\left(\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\zeta_{\Theta}\big(t,x,\Xi_{k}(\cdot,t,x)\big)\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}t,{\mathrm{d}}x)>\varepsilon\right)=0.

(21)

To finish the proof, it remains to show that for all
\varepsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}P\left(\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\eta_{\Theta}\big(t,x,\left\lvert\Xi_{k}(\cdot,t,x)\right\rvert\big)\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}t,{\mathrm{d}}x)>\varepsilon\right)=0.

(22)

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be fixed.
By
[
17
, Pro. 11.10]
, or equivalently by the continuity criterion for characteristics of infinitely divisible laws, there exists
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon)
such that every infinitely divisible random variable
Z
with first characteristic
a_{Z}
satisfies

P(|Z|>\sqrt{\delta})<\sqrt{\delta}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|a_{Z}|<\varepsilon.

Applying this to the stochastic integral, we obtain for all
f\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta}
that

\displaystyle P\left(\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}f\,{\mathrm{d}}\Theta\right\rvert>\sqrt{\delta}\right)<\sqrt{\delta}\implies\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}a_{\Theta}(t,x,f(t,x))\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}t,{\mathrm{d}}x)\right\rvert<\varepsilon.

(23)

It follows from Equation (
20
) that there exists an
N\in\mathbbm{N}
such that for all
k\geq N
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}(P\otimes P^{\prime})\left(\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}\,{\mathrm{d}}\widetilde{\Theta}\right\rvert>\sqrt{\delta}\right)<\delta.

(24)

Chebyshev’s inequality, Fubini’s theorem and Equation (
24
) imply for all
k\geq N
and
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}
that

\displaystyle P\left(\omega\in\Omega:P^{\prime}\left(\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}:\left\lvert\left(\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma(\omega)\Xi_{k}(\omega)\;{\rm d}\widetilde{\Theta}(\omega,\cdot)\right)(\omega^{\prime})\right\rvert>\sqrt{\delta}\right)<\sqrt{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta}}\int_{\Omega}P^{\prime}\left(\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}:\left\lvert\left(\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma(\omega)\Xi_{k}(\omega)\;{\rm d}\widetilde{\Theta}(\omega,\cdot)\right)(\omega^{\prime})\right\rvert>\sqrt{\delta}\right)\,{\rm d}P(\omega)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta}}(P\otimes P^{\prime})\left((\omega,\omega^{\prime})\in\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime}:\left\lvert\left(\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}\;{\rm d}\widetilde{\Theta}\right)(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\right\rvert>\sqrt{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sqrt{\delta}.

(25)

Equations (
23
) and (
6
) show for all
k\geq N
and
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}
that

\displaystyle P\left(\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}a_{\Theta}\big(t,x,\Gamma(t,x)\Xi_{k}(\cdot,t,x)\big)\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}t,{\mathrm{d}}x)\right\rvert<\varepsilon\right)\geq 1-\sqrt{\delta},

or equivalently, for all
k\geq N
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}P\left(\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}a_{\Theta}\big(t,x,\Gamma(t,x)\Xi_{k}(\cdot,t,x)\big)\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}t,{\mathrm{d}}x)\right\rvert\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq\sqrt{\delta}.

The above inequality, combined with an approximation argument using functions in
\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}
shows that for any predictable real-valued process
\Lambda\colon\Omega\times[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}\to\R
uniformly bounded by 1 it holds that, for
k\geq N
,

\displaystyle P\left(\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}a_{\Theta}\big(t,x,\Lambda(\cdot,t,x)\Xi_{k}(\cdot,t,x)\big)\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}t,{\mathrm{d}}x)\right\rvert\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq\sqrt{\delta}.

(26)

By applying this to
\Lambda(\omega,t,x)=\pi(t,x,\Xi_{k}(\omega,t,x))
, where
\pi
is defined in the proof of Lemma
3.8
, it follows that

\displaystyle P\left(\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\sup_{\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leqslant 1}a_{\Theta}\big(t,x,\beta\Xi_{k}(\cdot,t,x)\big)\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}t,{\mathrm{d}}x)\right\rvert\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq\sqrt{\delta}.

Observing that
\sup_{\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leqslant 1}a_{\Theta}(t,x,\beta\alpha)=\eta_{\Theta}(t,x,\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert)
since
\beta\mapsto a_{\Theta}(t,x,\beta)
is an odd function shows (
22
). Together with (
21
), this proves that
\iota_{\Theta}(\Xi_{k})
converges to zero in probability, and hence Part (a) follows from Theorem
3.5
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Let
\Xi
be a predictable process integrable with respect to
\Theta
. Then there exists a sequence
(\Xi_{k})_{k\in\mathbbm{N}}
of elements of
\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}
converging
P\otimes\chi
-a.e. to
\Xi
and satisfying

\displaystyle\lim_{k,\ell\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma(\Xi_{k}-\Xi_{\ell})\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]=0.

Proposition
6.3
implies that
\lim_{k,\ell\rightarrow\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Xi_{k}-\Xi_{\ell}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}=0
. Completeness of the metric space
(L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta}),{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta})
and the fact that
(\Xi_{k})_{k\in\mathbbm{N}}
converges
P\otimes\chi
-a.e. to
\Xi
together yield that the sequence
(\Xi_{k})_{k\in\mathbbm{N}}
has a limit in
L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta})
and that this limit necessarily coincides with
\Xi
. Thus
\Xi\in L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta})
.

To establish the reverse inclusion, let
\Xi
be a predictable process in the space
L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta})
. By Lemma
6.2
, there exists a sequence
(\Xi_{k})_{k\in\mathbbm{N}}
of elements of
\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}
converging to
\Xi
in
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}
and
P\otimes\chi
-a.e. Proposition
6.3
implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{k,\ell\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma(\Xi_{k}-\Xi_{\ell})\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]=0.

Thus,

.

7
Limit theorems

We begin this section by generalising Proposition
6.3
from step processes to arbitrary sequences of integrable predictable processes. In this way, we obtain not only a powerful limit theorem, but also continuity of the integral operator.

Corollary 7.1
.

Let
\Theta\colon\Borel_{b}(\Rp\times{\mathcal{O}})\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
be a Lévy basis and
(\Xi_{k})_{k\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
a sequence of
\Theta
-integrable predictable processes. Then the following are
equivalent:

(a)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}E[\left\lVert\Xi_{k}\right\rVert_{\Theta}\wedge 1]=0
;

(b)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\displaystyle\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]=0
.

Proof.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b): Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed. Proposition
6.3
implies that there exists a
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that for all
\Xi\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}
we have the implication:

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Xi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}\leqslant\delta(\varepsilon)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\displaystyle\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]\leqslant\varepsilon.

(27)

Since
\lim_{k\to\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Xi_{k}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}=0
, there exists a
k_{0}\in\mathbbm{N}
such that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Xi_{k}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}<\tfrac{\delta(\varepsilon)}{2}
for all
k\geq k_{0}
. Furthermore, Lemma
6.2
guarantees for each
k\in\mathbbm{N}
the existence of a sequence
(\Xi_{k}^{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbbm{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}
converging to
\Xi_{k}
in
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}
and
P\otimes\chi
-a.e. Consequently, for each
k\in\mathbbm{N}
we can find
\ell_{0}(k,\varepsilon)\in\mathbbm{N}
such that for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}(k,\varepsilon)
we have

{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\Xi_{k}^{\ell}-\Xi_{k}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}_{\Theta}\leqslant\tfrac{\delta(\varepsilon)}{2}.

Therefore, for each
k\geq k_{0}
and
\ell\geq\ell_{0}(k,\varepsilon)
,

{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\Xi_{k}^{\ell}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}_{\Theta}\leq{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\Xi_{k}^{\ell}-\Xi_{k}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}_{\Theta}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Xi_{k}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}\leqslant\delta(\varepsilon),

which implies by (
27
) that

\displaystyle\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}^{\ell}\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]\leqslant\varepsilon.

(28)

Fix
k\geq k_{0}
. Since the stochastic integral is independent of the approximating sequence and

\Xi_{k}^{\ell}\to\Xi_{k}
in
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}
and
P\otimes\chi
-a.e., it follows from (
28
) that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}E\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}^{\ell}\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}^{\ell}\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(a): Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed. Proposition
6.3
implies that there exists a
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that for all
\Xi\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}
we have the implication

\displaystyle\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]\leqslant\delta(\varepsilon)\quad\Rightarrow\quad{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Xi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}\leqslant\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}.

(29)

By assumption, there exists a
k_{0}\in\mathbbm{N}
such that for all
k\geq k_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]\leqslant\tfrac{\delta(\varepsilon)}{4}.

(30)

Lemma
6.2
guarantees that for each
k\in\mathbbm{N}
there exists a sequence
(\Xi_{k}^{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbbm{N}}
in
\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}
converging to
\Xi_{k}
in
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}
and
P\otimes\chi
-a.e. Consequently, for each
k\in\mathbbm{N}
we can find
\ell_{0}(k,\varepsilon)\in\mathbbm{N}
such that for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}(k,\varepsilon)
we have

{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\Xi_{k}^{\ell}-\Xi_{k}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}_{\Theta}\leqslant\varepsilon/2.

(31)

Since for each
k\in\mathbbm{N}
we have
\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Xi_{k}^{\ell}-\Xi_{k}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}=0
, the implication
(a)\Rightarrow(b)
already proved above implies that for each
k\in\mathbbm{N}
there exists an
\ell_{1}(k,\varepsilon)\in\mathbbm{N}
such that for all
\ell\geq\ell_{1}(k,\varepsilon)
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma(\Xi_{k}-\Xi_{k}^{\ell})\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]\leqslant\tfrac{\delta(\varepsilon)}{2}.

(32)

By combining (
30
) and (
32
), we obtain for all
k\geq k_{0}
and
\ell\geq\max\{\ell_{0}(k,\varepsilon),\ell_{1}(k,\varepsilon)\}
that

\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi_{k}^{\ell}\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]\leqslant\delta(\varepsilon),

which implies by (
29
) and (
31
) that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Xi_{k}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}\leq{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\Xi_{k}-\Xi_{k}^{\ell}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}_{\Theta}+{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\Xi_{k}^{\ell}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}_{\Theta}\leqslant\varepsilon.

As
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, this concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary
7.1
, together with Lemma
5.4
, shows that the integral operator
I_{\Theta}
is continuous from
L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta})
equipped with the
F
-norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}
into the space of càdlàg processes equipped with the Emery-type topology induced by the seminorm

\Xi\mapsto\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm prd}^{1}}E\Bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{(0,t]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma\Xi\,{\rm d}\Theta\Big|\wedge 1\Bigg].

We finish this section with a stochastic dominated convergence theorem.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
\Theta\colon\Borel_{b}(\Rp\times{\mathcal{O}})\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega;\R)
be a Lévy basis and
(\Xi_{k})_{k\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
a sequence of predictable processes
\Xi_{k}\colon\Omega\times[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}\to\R
satisfying:

1.

(\Xi_{k})_{k\in\mathbbm{N}}
converges
P\otimes\chi
-a.e. to a predictable process
\Xi
;

2.

there exists a predictable,
\Theta
-integrable process
\Upsilon
such that, for all
k\in\mathbbm{N}
,

(\zeta_{\Theta}+\eta_{\Theta})(t,x,\left\lvert\Xi_{k}(\omega,t,x)\right\rvert)\leq(\zeta_{\Theta}+\eta_{\Theta})(t,x,\left\lvert\Upsilon(\omega,t,x)\right\rvert),

for
P\otimes\chi
-a.a.
(\omega,t,x)\in\Omega\times[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}
.

Then
\Xi
is
\Theta
-integrable and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm prd}}^{1}}E\Bigg[\left\lvert\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Gamma(\Xi_{k}-\Xi)\;{\rm d}\Theta\right\rvert\wedge 1\Bigg]=0.

Proof.

By assumption, there exists a set
N\subseteq\Omega\times[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}
with
P\otimes\chi(N)=0
such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Xi_{k}(\omega,t,x)=\Xi(\omega,t,x)

and

(\zeta_{\Theta}+\eta_{\Theta})(t,x,\left\lvert\Xi_{k}(\omega,t,x)\right\rvert)\leq(\zeta_{\Theta}+\eta_{\Theta})(t,x,\left\lvert\Upsilon(\omega,t,x)\right\rvert)

for all
(\omega,t,x)\in N^{c}
and
k\in\mathbbm{N}
. Fubini’s theorem implies that there exists a set
\Omega_{1}\subseteq\Omega
with
P(\Omega_{1})=1
such that for each
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
we have

(\zeta_{\Theta}+\eta_{\Theta})(t,x,\left\lvert\Xi_{k}(\omega,t,x)\right\rvert)\leq(\zeta_{\Theta}+\eta_{\Theta})(t,x,\left\lvert\Upsilon(\omega,t,x)\right\rvert)

and

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Xi_{k}(\omega,t,x)=\Xi(\omega,t,x)

for
\chi
-a.a.
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}
. Monotonicity of
\zeta_{\Theta}+\eta_{\Theta}
and the
\Delta_{2}
-condition (
9
) yield

(\zeta_{\Theta}+\eta_{\Theta})(t,x,\left\lvert\Xi_{k}(\omega,t,x)-\Xi_{\ell}(\omega,t,x)\right\rvert)\leq 5\,(\zeta_{\Theta}+\eta_{\Theta})(t,x,\left\lvert\Upsilon(\omega,t,x)\right\rvert).

Furthermore, Theorem
6.1
guarantees that there exists a set
\Omega_{2}\subseteq\Omega
with
P(\Omega_{2})=1
such that

\iota_{\Theta}(\Upsilon(\omega,\cdot))<\infty\qquad\text{for all $\omega\in\Omega_{2}$.}

Since
\zeta_{\Theta}
and
\eta_{\Theta}
are continuous, another application of Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem implies that for all
\omega\in\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{k,\ell\rightarrow\infty}\iota_{\Theta}(\Xi_{k}(\omega,\cdot)-\Xi_{\ell}(\omega,\cdot))

\displaystyle\qquad=\lim_{k,\ell\rightarrow\infty}\Bigg(\int_{(0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}}\Big(\zeta_{\Theta}(t,x,\left\lvert\Xi_{k}(\omega,t,x)-\Xi_{\ell}(\omega,t,x)\right\rvert)\Big.\Bigg.

\displaystyle\Bigg.\Big.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\eta_{\Theta}(t,x,\left\lvert\Xi_{k}(\omega,t,x)-\Xi_{\ell}(\omega,t,x)\right\rvert)\Big)\,\chi({\mathrm{d}}t,{\mathrm{d}}x)\Bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad=0.

Hence, for each
\omega\in\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}
, the sequence
(\Xi_{k}(\omega))_{k\in\mathbbm{N}}
is Cauchy in
({\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\Theta})
. Since
\Xi_{k}(\omega)\rightarrow\Xi(\omega)
for
\chi
-a.a.
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathcal{O}}
, it follows that
\Xi(\omega)\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\Theta}
. As
P(\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2})=1
, Theorem
6.1
implies that
\Xi
is
\Theta
-integrable. Another application of Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem yields
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Xi_{k}-\Xi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\Theta}=0
, and the assertion now follows from Corollary
7.1
.
∎
