 Background
V2G AC is a type of grid interconnection by mobile battery that transforms an EV into a
DER with the potential to stabilize the grid and lower costs for electricity.
UL 1741SC is a critical new standard to enable grid interconnection of V2G AC-capable
vehicles, by defining requirements for V2G AC-intended EVSEs as a gatekeeper between
the grid and the EV to support grid safety, such as through redundant voltage trip
(namely oversight).
EVSE manufacturers need a clear set of conformance test cases and criteria by which to
evaluate their equipment and confirm the compliance with UL 1741SC, so that the
EVSEs performs as intended for the grid safety.
Hence, this project aims to identify necessary conformance tests and find gaps by
performing the tests identified.

 Findings from EVSE
 Responses
• Overall, the EVSE performed well – the partner already has a product for V2G DC.
• Suggestions for UL 1741SC
 ◦ Clearer definition and testing for detecting unauthorized export; at what level for
 what duration is the power regarded as export?
 ◦ Clarify options for returning operation from oversight. The EVSE occasionally
 stayed idle after the oversight.
 ◦ 60 ms window is too tight in longer trip. It needs to be relaxed for longer clearing
 time setup.
• Future considerations for UL 1741SC
 ◦ Evaluation with other EVSE vendors – partner’s EVSE performed as intended
 because they have IEEE 1547 experience; can the standard be exercised in the
     same way w/ less knowledgeable vendor?                          National Laboratory of the Rockies | 24

 Findings from Performing
 Test
• Conformance test procedures developed were exercisable.
• Suggestions for UL 1741SC
 ◦ EV emulators for UL 1741SC don’t commercially exist, and the spec needs details.
 ◦ Oversight pass/fail criteria are different from UL 1741SB. Can UL 1741SB tool
 detect EVSE’s trip, not the EV’s? Do we need the EV emulator to start exporting at
 the desired timing? Can a stopwatch be used for Enter Service test?
• Challenges faced/pitfalls found for the EV emulator buildout:
 ◦ Pure single-phase inverter is preferred, because the use of three-phase inverter as
 a single-phase inverter tripped GFCIs.
 ◦ The inverter couldn’t keep exporting AC voltage below 20 Vrms, hence limiting the
 ability to evaluate zero voltage ride-through/trip.

 Oversight Window Proposal:
 Approach
The following needs to be considered;
1. Minimum RT requirements and clearing time which can never be exceeded.
 + IEEE 1547 4.7 needs to be considered.
2. EV always trips first (assuming SAE J3072 is updated to harmonize) …
 Oversight window of EVSE ensures that EVs can trip first and then EVSE trips.
3. Clock tolerance: ≤ 50 ppm (from a known clock vendor’s line-up) …
 Overall tolerance at 1000 s is +/-50 ms.
4. Simple to implement

                                                                         National Laboratory of the Rockies | 28

 Oversight Window Proposal
• New window (criteria rather than implementation):
 ◦ Clearing time ≤ 100 s; oversight window = 60 ms
 ◦ Clearing time > 100 s; oversight window = 500 ms
• 500 ms should be sufficient to cover the tolerance (+/- 50 ms at 1000 s).
• At 100 s, the tolerance is +/- 5 ms, hence 60 ms window should be doable.
• The minimum may-trip duration in clearing time > 100 s is 1 s. (OF1/UF1’s default)
  Hence, 500 ms was chosen to equally split the buffer. The value could be larger.
