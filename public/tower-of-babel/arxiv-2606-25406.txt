Abstract
We establish a family of linear relations for the coefficients of the Tutte polynomial of a semimatroid. We also introduce ranked central sets, a mild generalization of semimatroids, and prove that the same identities hold in this broader setting. The proof relies on the specialization x = z z − 1 x=\frac{z}{z-1} , y = z y=z along the hyperbola ( x − 1 ) ​ ( y − 1 ) = 1 (x-1)(y-1)=1 ; under this specialization, the edge subgraph expansion over central sets reduces to the f f -polynomial of the central set complex. As applications, we obtain explicit formulas for Tutte polynomials of affine hyperplane arrangements and for balanced Tutte polynomials of biased graphs, whose central sets are central subarrangements and balanced edge sets, respectively. Our results recover the generalized Brylawski’s identities for Tutte polynomials of ranked sets, matroids and graphs. Keywords: Tutte polynomial, linear relation, semimatroid, ranked central set, affine hyperplane arrangement, biased graph Mathematics Subject Classifications: 05C31, 05B35, 52C35

Linear relations for Tutte polynomials of semimatroids

1
Introduction

The study of graph polynomials originated with the chromatic polynomial, which was introduced by Birkhoff
[
3
]
in 1912 for planar graphs in connection with the four-color problem, and later generalized to arbitrary graphs by Whitney via edge subgraph expansions
[
17
,
18
]
. Tutte’s two-variable polynomial unified several graph polynomial invariants, including the chromatic polynomial and the flow polynomial, and has since become a central object in the study of graphs, matroids, hyperplane arrangements, and semimatroids. For a finite graph
G=(V,E)
, the
Tutte polynomial
admits the following edge subgraph expansion:

T_{G}(x,y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E}(x-1)^{r(E)-r(A)}(y-1)^{|A|-r(A)},

where
r
denotes the rank function of
G
. By Tutte’s original definition
[
14
]
, the coefficient of
x^{i}y^{j}
in
T_{G}(x,y)
counts the number of spanning forests with internal activity
i
and external activity
j
, with respect to a fixed total ordering of the edges. This interpretation, now known as the
basis activities expansion
, shows that all coefficients of the Tutte polynomial are non-negative integers, and was subsequently generalized to matroids by Crapo
[
6
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to study generalized Brylawski’s identities for Tutte polynomials. Brylawski
[
4
]
showed that a family of linear relations holds among the coefficients of the Tutte polynomial not only for graphs, but also for all matroids. More specifically, write the Tutte polynomial
T_{M}(x,y)
of a matroid
M=(E,r)
as
T_{M}(x,y)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}t_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}
. Brylawski’s identity states that, for any integer
0\leq k<|E|
,

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}(-1)^{j}t_{ij}=0.

In 2015, Gordon
[
8
]
extended this result by proving that the same relation holds for a much weaker notion of rank function
r:2^{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, which only requires

r(A)\leq\mbox{\rm min}\{r(E),|A|\}\quad\forall A\subseteq E,

without the submodularity and monotonicity assumptions that hold for matroids. Moreover, Gordon also obtained a new identity for the case
k=|E|
:

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}(-1)^{j}t_{ij}=(-1)^{|E|-r(E)}.

Most recently, Beke, Csáji, Csikvári and Pituk
[
2
]
gave a short proof of this result and extended the work of Brylawski and Gordon to all integers
k\geq 0
:

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}(-1)^{j}t_{ij}=(-1)^{|E|-r(E)}\binom{k-r(E)}{k-|E|},

(1.1)

where the binomial coefficient
\binom{k-r(E)}{k-|E|}
is interpreted as
0
when
k<|E|
. These relations are called the
generalized Brylawski identities
. The key observation in their proof is that one can simplify
T_{M}(x,y)
by substituting
x=\frac{z}{z-1}
and
y=z
into the hyperbola relation
(x-1)(y-1)=1
. Consequently,

(z-1)^{r(E)}T_{M}\Big(\frac{z}{z-1},z\Big)=z^{|E|}.

Comparing coefficients of
z^{k}
and taking appropriate linear combinations of the resulting equations yields the generalized Brylawski’s identities. This idea is also a key ingredient in obtaining our desired results.

Motivated by the earlier work, our main goal is to generalize the identities of Brylawski, Gordon and Beke et al. to Tutte polynomials of semimatroids. Ardila
[
1
]
extended the Tutte polynomial to semimatroids, which are abstract structures capturing the dependence properties of affine hyperplane arrangements and generalizing matroids. Semimatroids were introduced by Wachs and Walker
[
16
]
in terms of the “geometric lattice” of closed sets, later developed in terms of subsets of a finite set by Ardila
[
1
]
, and also discovered independently by Kawahara
[
9
]
.

Let
E
be a finite set. A nonempty collection
\mathcal{C}
of subsets of
E
is called a
simplicial complex
if whenever
A\in\mathcal{C}
, every subset of
A
also belongs to
\mathcal{C}
. A
semimatroid
is a triple
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
consisting of a finite set
E
, a nonempty simplicial complex
\mathcal{C}
on
E
, and a function
r_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, satisfying the following five properties:

(R1)

If
A\in\mathcal{C}
, then
r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)\leq|A|
.

(R2)

If
A,B\in\mathcal{C}
and
A\subseteq B
, then
r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)\leq r_{\mathcal{C}}(B)
.

(R3)

If
A,B\in\mathcal{C}
and
A\cup B\in\mathcal{C}
, then

r_{\mathcal{C}}(A\cap B)+r_{\mathcal{C}}(A\cup B)\leq r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)+r_{\mathcal{C}}(B).

(SR4)

If
A,B\in\mathcal{C}
and
r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)=r_{\mathcal{C}}(A\cap B)
, then
A\cup B\in\mathcal{C}
.

(SR5)

If
A,B\in\mathcal{C}
and
r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)<r_{\mathcal{C}}(B)
, then
A\cup e\in\mathcal{C}
for some
e\in B-A
.

Every member in
\mathcal{C}
is referred to as a
central set
of the semimatroid
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
.
E
,
\mathcal{C}
and
r_{\mathcal{C}}
are called the
ground set
, the
collection of central sets
and the
rank function
of
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
, respectively. In addition, all the maximal sets in
\mathcal{C}
have the same rank, which is denoted by
r(\mathcal{C})
and called the
rank
of
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
. In particular, when
\mathcal{C}=2^{E}
, the axioms (SR4) and (SR5) hold trivially, and the remaining three axioms (R1)–(R3) coincide with the rank function axioms of matroids. Hence,
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
reduces to a matroid in this case. For more information about matroids, we refer the reader to Oxley’s book
[
12
]
.

The
Tutte polynomial
of a semimatroid
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
, introduced by Ardila
[
1
, Definition 8.1]
, is defined as

T_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y):=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{C}}(x-1)^{r(\mathcal{C})-r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)}(y-1)^{|A|-r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)}.

Ardila further showed that
T_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)
satisfies a deletion-contraction relation
[
1
, Proposition 8.2]
and admits a basis activities expansion
[
1
, Theorem 9.5]
. In particular,
T_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)
can be written in the form
T_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)=\sum_{i\geq 0,\,j\geq 0}t_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}
, where each coefficient
t_{ij}
is a non-negative integer counting bases with internal activity
i
and external activity
j
. We now present our linear relations for Tutte polynomials of semimatroids.

Theorem 1.1

(Generalized Brylawski Identities for Semimatroids)
.

Let
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
be a semimatroid, and
T_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}t_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}
. Then, for all integers
k\geq 0
,

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}(-1)^{j}t_{ij}=\sum_{n=0}^{|E|}(-1)^{r(\mathcal{C})+n}f_{n}\binom{k+n-r(\mathcal{C})}{k},

where
f_{n}:=\#\{X\in\mathcal{C}:|X|=n\}
.

When
\mathcal{C}=2^{E}
, the triple
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
is a matroid. In this case, we have
f_{n}=\binom{|E|}{n}
and
r(\mathcal{C})=r(E)
, and hence

\sum_{n=0}^{|E|}(-1)^{r(\mathcal{C})+n}f_{n}\binom{k+n-r(\mathcal{C})}{k}=(-1)^{|E|-r(E)}\binom{k-r(E)}{k-|E|}.

(1.2)

Indeed,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{|E|}(-1)^{r(E)+n}\binom{|E|}{n}\binom{k+n-r(E)}{k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r(E)}[z^{k}](1+z)^{k-r(E)}\sum_{n=0}^{|E|}\binom{|E|}{n}(-(1+z))^{n}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r(E)}[z^{k}](1+z)^{k-r(E)}(-z)^{|E|}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|E|-r(E)}\binom{k-r(E)}{k-|E|}.

Thus, the generalized Brylawski identities for Tutte polynomials of matroids, given in (
1.1
), can be recovered as a special case of
Theorem

1.1
.

A further purpose of this paper is to generalize these linear relations to ranked central sets, following the spirit of Gordon’s approach to isolate the key properties of semimatroids. This gives rise to a natural rank function generalization of semimatroids, and extends Gordon’s original notion of ranked sets by allowing any simplicial subcomplex of the power set, rather than requiring the full power set itself.

A
ranked central set
is a triple
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
consisting of a finite set
E
, a nonempty simplicial complex
\mathcal{C}
on
E
, and a function
r_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, satisfying the following properties:

(R0)

r_{\mathcal{C}}(\emptyset)=0
.

(R1)

r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)\leq|A|
for any
A\in\mathcal{C}
.

The members in
\mathcal{C}
are called the central sets of the ranked central set
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
, the function
r_{\mathcal{C}}
is called its
rank function
, and

r(\mathcal{C}):=\mbox{\rm max}\{r_{\mathcal{C}}(A):A\in\mathcal{C}\}

is called its
rank
.

Moreover, we define the
generalized Tutte polynomial

T_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)
of the ranked central set
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
using the rank function, as follows:

T_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y):=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{C}}(x-1)^{r(\mathcal{C})-r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)}(y-1)^{|A|-r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)}.

The assumption (R1) and the definition of
r(\mathcal{C})
guarantee that
T_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)
is a two-variable polynomial. Thus, we can write
T_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)
in the form
T_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}t_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}
. Unlike the usual Tutte polynomial, its coefficients
t_{ij}
need not be non-negative. For instance, let
E=\{a,b\}
,
\mathcal{C}=\{\emptyset,\{a\},\{b\}\}
, and define
r_{\mathcal{C}}(\emptyset)=r_{\mathcal{C}}(\{a\})=0
and
r_{\mathcal{C}}(\{b\})=1
. Then
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
is a ranked central set of rank
r(\mathcal{C})=1
, and its generalized Tutte polynomial is

T_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)=(x-1)+(x-1)(y-1)+1=xy-y+1.

Thus
t_{01}=-1
. The following theorem extends the generalized Brylawski identities given in
Theorem

1.1
to the generalized Tutte polynomials of ranked central sets.

Theorem 1.2

(Generalized Brylawski Identities for Ranked Central Sets)
.

Let
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
be a ranked central set, and
T_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)=\sum_{i\geq 0,\,j\geq 0}t_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}
. Then, for all integers
k\geq 0
,

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}(-1)^{j}t_{ij}=\sum_{n=0}^{|E|}(-1)^{r(\mathcal{C})+n}f_{n}\binom{k+n-r(\mathcal{C})}{k},

where
f_{n}:=\#\{X\in\mathcal{C}:|X|=n\}
.

It is worth noting that when
\mathcal{C}=2^{E}
and
r(\mathcal{C})=r(E)
, the triple
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
is a ranked set in the sense of Gordon, and the equality in (
1.2
) still holds. Therefore, the generalized Brylawski identities for Tutte polynomials of ranked sets, given in (
1.1
), can be recovered as a special case of
Theorem

1.2
. In addition, a semimatroid is also a ranked central set, and hence we can directly obtain
Theorem

1.1
from
Theorem

1.2
.

The paper is organized as follows. In
Section 2
, we prove
Theorem

1.2
, deduce
Theorem

1.1
, and examine some basic properties of the coefficients
t_{ij}
. In
Section 3
, we apply
Theorem

1.1
to affine hyperplane arrangements and biased graphs.

Theorem

1.2
, deduce
Theorem

, and then examine some basic questions associated with the coefficients
t_{ij}
.

2.1
Proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

In this subsection, we prove
Theorem

1.2
. Since every semimatroid is a ranked central set,
Theorem

1.1
follows immediately. Let
a,b
and
m
be integers. Throughout this section we use the convention

\binom{a}{b}=\frac{a(a-1)\cdots(a-b+1)}{b!}\quad\text{for }b\geq 0,\qquad\binom{a}{b}=0\quad\text{for }b<0.

We shall also use the Chu–Vandermonde identity
[
15
,
23
]
in the form

\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\binom{a}{m-l}\binom{b}{l}=\binom{a+b}{m}.

(2.1)

Let
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
be a ranked central set. We consider the polynomial
F_{\mathcal{C}}(t)
as the ordinary generating function for the central sets of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to cardinality. Equivalently, it is the
f
-polynomial of the simplicial complex
\mathcal{C}
, where
f_{n}
denotes the number of central sets (faces) of size
n
:

F_{\mathcal{C}}(t):=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{C}}t^{|A|}=\sum_{n=0}^{|E|}f_{n}t^{n}.

Notice that this polynomial depends only on the underlying simplicial complex
\mathcal{C}
, and not on the rank function
r_{\mathcal{C}}
. Below we simplify the generalized Tutte polynomial
T_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)
using
F_{\mathcal{C}}(t)
and the method of Beke et al.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every ranked central set
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
, we have

(z-1)^{r(\mathcal{C})}T_{\mathcal{C}}\Big(\frac{z}{z-1},z\Big)=F_{\mathcal{C}}(z-1).

Proof.

Let us consider a new variable
z
, and plug in
x=\frac{z}{z-1}
and
y=z
. Then the contribution of a central set
A\in\mathcal{C}
to
T_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\frac{z}{z-1},z\right)
is

(z-1)^{-r(\mathcal{C})+r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)}(z-1)^{|A|-r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)}=(z-1)^{|A|-r(\mathcal{C})}.

Multiplying by
(z-1)^{r(\mathcal{C})}
and summing over all
A\in\mathcal{C}
gives
Section

2.1
.
∎

We now proceed to verify
Theorem

1.2
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

Write
r=r(\mathcal{C})
for brevity. Substituting
x=\frac{z}{z-1}
and
y=z
into
T_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}t_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}
, and applying
Section

2.1
, we obtain

\sum_{i,j\geq 0}t_{ij}z^{i+j}(z-1)^{r-i}=F_{\mathcal{C}}(z-1).

(2.2)

Since
0\leq r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)\leq\mbox{\rm min}\{r,|A|\}
for any
A\in\mathcal{C}
, we have
t_{ij}=0
whenever
i>r
. Therefore, both sides in (
2.2
) are polynomials in
z
. Thus, comparing the coefficients of
z^{l}
in both sides of (
2.2
) gives

\sum_{i,j\geq 0}t_{ij}(-1)^{r-l+j}\binom{r-i}{l-i-j}=[z^{l}]F_{\mathcal{C}}(z-1).

(2.3)

Multiplying (
2.3
) by
(-1)^{l}\binom{k-r}{k-l}
and summing over
l=0,\ldots,k
, the left-hand side in (
2.2
) becomes

\sum_{i,j\geq 0}t_{ij}(-1)^{r+j}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k-r}{k-l}\binom{r-i}{l-i-j}.

By (
2.1
), we have

\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k-r}{k-l}\binom{r-i}{l-i-j}=\binom{k-i}{k-i-j}.

This term is zero unless
0\leq i\leq k
and
0\leq j\leq k-i
; in that range it is equal to
\binom{k-i}{j}
. Hence, the transformed left-hand side in (
2.2
) is

(-1)^{r}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}(-1)^{j}t_{ij}.

For the right-hand side in (
2.2
), since
[z^{l}]F_{\mathcal{C}}(z-1)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{C}}(-1)^{|A|-l}\binom{|A|}{l}
, we derive

\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{l}\binom{k-r}{k-l}\big([z^{l}]F_{\mathcal{C}}(z-1)\big)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{C}}(-1)^{|A|}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k-r}{k-l}\binom{|A|}{l}.

Applying (
2.1
) again yields

\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k-r}{k-l}\binom{|A|}{l}=\binom{k+|A|-r}{k}.

Therefore,

\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{l}\binom{k-r}{k-l}\big([z^{l}]F_{\mathcal{C}}(z-1)\big)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{C}}(-1)^{|A|}\binom{k+|A|-r}{k}.

Multiplying by
(-1)^{r}
, we get

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}(-1)^{j}t_{ij}=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{C}}(-1)^{r+|A|}\binom{k+|A|-r}{k}.

Grouping the central sets according to their cardinalities gives

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}(-1)^{j}t_{ij}=\sum_{n=0}^{|E|}(-1)^{r+n}f_{n}\binom{k+n-r}{k},

which completes the proof.
∎

2.2
Further consequences

In this subsection, we restrict our attention to some basic properties of the coefficients
t_{ij}
. It is natural to ask whether the sum
\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}(-1)^{j}t_{ij}
given in
Theorem

1.2
is constant for all sufficiently large integers
k
. The following corollary answers this question.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
be a ranked central set and set
r=r(\mathcal{C})
. Then, for all
k\geq r
, if
f_{n}=0
for all integers
r<n\leq|E|
, then the sum
\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}(-1)^{j}t_{ij}
is constant and equal to
f_{r}
.

Proof.

We first look at the right-hand side
T_{\mathcal{C}}(k):=\sum_{n=0}^{|E|}(-1)^{r+n}f_{n}\binom{k+n-r}{k}
of
Theorem

1.2
. If
k\geq r
and
n<r
, then
0\leq k+n-r<k
, and hence
\binom{k+n-r}{k}=0
. Thus all terms with
n<r
disappear for
k\geq r
. For
n\geq r
, applying the identity
\binom{k+n-r}{k}=\binom{k+n-r}{n-r}
, we derive that for all
k\geq r
,

T_{\mathcal{C}}(k)=\sum_{n=r}^{|E|}(-1)^{r+n}f_{n}\binom{k+n-r}{n-r}.

Thus,
T_{\mathcal{C}}(k)
is a polynomial in
k
. Let
\rho:=\mbox{\rm max}\{n:f_{n}\neq 0\}
. The term with
n=\rho
is
(-1)^{r+\rho}f_{\rho}\binom{k+\rho-r}{\rho-r}
.
If
\rho>r
, then this term has degree
\rho-r
and leading coefficient
(-1)^{r+\rho}\frac{f_{\rho}}{(\rho-r)!}\neq 0
. All other terms have smaller degree. Hence the polynomial
T_{\mathcal{C}}(k)
has degree
\rho-r
. It follows that the polynomial
T_{\mathcal{C}}(k)
is constant exactly when
\rho=r
, equivalently when
f_{n}=0
for all
n>r
. In that case only the term
n=r
remains, and the constant is
f_{r}\binom{k}{0}=f_{r}
. Applying
Theorem

1.2
, the corollary holds.
∎

Let
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
be a semimatroid. For any
A\in\mathcal{C}
, its
closure
is

{\rm cl}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)=\big\{e\in E:A\cup\{e\}\in\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}}(A\cup\{e\})=r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)\big\}.

A central set
A\in\mathcal{C}
is
independent
if
r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)=|A|
, and is called a
spanning set
if
r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)=r(\mathcal{C})
. A maximal independent set is referred to as a
basis
. Some evaluations of semimatroid Tutte polynomials have combinatorial interpretations as follows:

•

T_{\mathcal{C}}(1,1)
is the number of bases.

•

T_{\mathcal{C}}(2,1)
is the number of independent sets.

•

T_{\mathcal{C}}(1,2)
is the number of spanning sets.

•

T_{\mathcal{C}}(2,2)
is the number of central sets.

Note that matroids and semimatroids are examples of ranked central sets. As a byproduct of
Section

2.2
, we obtain the following result:

•

If every central set in a semimatroid
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
is independent, then for all
k\geq r
, the sum
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}(-1)^{j}t_{ij}
is constant and equal to
T_{\mathcal{C}}(1,1)
.

•

If
M=U_{r,r}
is the uniform matroid of size
r
and rank
r
, then for all
k\geq r
, the sum
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}(-1)^{j}t_{ij}
is constant and equal to
T_{\mathcal{C}}(1,1)
.

We conclude this section with some combinatorial interpretations for the coefficient
t_{00}
. The
characteristic polynomial

\chi_{\mathcal{C}}(t)
of a semimatroid
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
is a special case of the Tutte polynomial
T_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)
, defined by

\chi_{\mathcal{C}}(t):=(-1)^{r(\mathcal{C})}T_{\mathcal{C}}(1-t,0)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{C}}(-1)^{|A|}t^{r(\mathcal{C})-r_{\mathcal{C}}(A)}.

According to
Theorem

1.1
, the first identity is

t_{00}=\sum_{n=0}^{|E|}(-1)^{r(\mathcal{C})+n}f_{n}.

Comparing the expressions of
t_{00}
and
\chi_{\mathcal{C}}(t)
, we immediately deduce

t_{00}=(-1)^{r(\mathcal{C})}\chi_{\mathcal{C}}(1).

Additionally, the coefficient
t_{00}
is closely related to an important combinatorial invariant, Crapo’s beta invariant. For a matroid
M=(E,r)
, the
Crapo’s beta invariant

\beta(M)
was introduced by Crapo
[
5
]
and is given by

\beta(M):=(-1)^{r(E)}\sum_{A\subseteq E}(-1)^{|A|}r(A).

It can also be computed from the
characteristic polynomial

\chi_{M}(t)
of
M
, defined as

\chi_{M}(t):=\sum_{A\subseteq E}(-1)^{|A|}t^{r(E)-r(A)},

and the relation between them is

\beta(M)=(-1)^{r(E)+1}\left.\frac{d\chi_{M}(t)}{dt}\right|_{t=1}=(-1)^{r(E)+1}\chi^{\prime}_{M}(1).

For further details on Crapo’s beta invariant, we refer the reader to
[
11
]
.

In the study of semimatroids, there are many equivalent ways to define a semimatroid. One can define a semimatroid in terms of a matroid
\tilde{N}
on the ground set
E\sqcup\{p\}
with a distinguished element
p
. The pair
(\tilde{N},p)
is called a
pointed matroid
. Ardila
[
1
, Theorem 5.4]
showed that an arbitrary pointed matroid
(\tilde{N},p)
on
E\sqcup\{p\}
defines a unique semimatroid
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
such that

\mathcal{C}=\big\{A\subseteq E\mid p\notin\operatorname{cl}_{\tilde{N}}(A)\big\}

and
r_{\mathcal{C}}
is the restriction of
r_{\tilde{N}}
to
\mathcal{C}
. This correspondence was implicit in the work of Wachs and Walker
[
16
]
. Based on this result, Ardila
[
1
, Proposition 8.7]
further obtained a close relation between the characteristic polynomials of the semimatroid and the pointed matroid:

\chi_{\tilde{N}}(t)=(t-1)\chi_{\mathcal{C}}(t).

Furthermore, a routine calculation gives

t_{00}=\beta(\tilde{N}).

Summarizing the above arguments, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
(\tilde{N},p)
be a pointed matroid, and
(E,\mathcal{C},r_{\mathcal{C}})
be its corresponding semimatroid. Then

t_{00}=(-1)^{r(\mathcal{C})}\chi_{\mathcal{C}}(1)=\beta(\tilde{N}).

3
Applications to hyperplane arrangements and biased graphs

In this section, we apply our results to two concrete semimatroid models: affine hyperplane arrangements and biased graphs. We also record the resulting interpretations of the coefficient
t_{00}
.

3.1
Application to affine hyperplane arrangements

An
affine hyperplane arrangement

\mathcal{A}
is a finite set of affine hyperplanes in a
d
-dimensional vector space
V
over a field
\mathbb{F}
. A subarrangement
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
is called
central
if
\bigcap_{H\in\mathcal{B}}H\neq\emptyset
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}
denote the collection of central subarrangements of
\mathcal{A}
. For any
\mathcal{B}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}
, define

r_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B}):={\rm codim}\bigcap_{H\in\mathcal{B}}H=d-\dim\bigcap_{H\in\mathcal{B}}H.

As noted in
[
1
, Proposition 2.2]
, the triple
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}},r_{\mathcal{A}})
forms a semimatroid, where
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}
is the corresponding collection of central sets and
r_{\mathcal{A}}
is the corresponding rank function. Its
Tutte polynomial
is given by

T_{\mathcal{A}}(x,y):=\sum_{\mathcal{B}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}}(x-1)^{r(\mathcal{A})-r_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})}(y-1)^{|\mathcal{B}|-r_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})},

where
r(\mathcal{A})=r(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}})
. The
characteristic polynomial

\chi_{\mathcal{A}}(t)
of
\mathcal{A}
is a special case of
T_{\mathcal{A}}(x,y)
, defined as

\chi_{\mathcal{A}}(t):=\sum_{\mathcal{B}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}}(-1)^{|\mathcal{B}|}t^{\dim\bigcap_{H\in\mathcal{B}}H}=(-1)^{r(\mathcal{A})}t^{d-r(\mathcal{A})}T_{\mathcal{A}}(1-t,0).

Specializing
Theorem

1.1
to affine hyperplane arrangements gives the following corollary.

Corollary 3.1

(Generalized Brylawski Identities for Affine Hyperplane Arrangements)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an affine hyperplane arrangement, and
T_{\mathcal{A}}(x,y)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}t_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}
. Then, for all integers
k\geq 0
,

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}(-1)^{j}t_{ij}=\sum_{n=0}^{|\mathcal{A}|}(-1)^{r(\mathcal{A})+n}c_{n}\binom{k+n-r(\mathcal{A})}{k},

where
c_{n}:=\#\{\mathcal{B}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}:|\mathcal{B}|=n\}
.

Furthermore, when
\mathcal{A}
is central, every subarrangement is central. Thus
c_{n}=\binom{|\mathcal{A}|}{n}
, and
Section

3.1
reduces to the generalized Brylawski identity for the central hyperplane arrangement:

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}(-1)^{j}t_{ij}=(-1)^{|\mathcal{A}|-r(\mathcal{A})}\binom{k-r(\mathcal{A})}{k-|\mathcal{A}|}.

We are now ready to present a small example to illustrate
Section

3.1
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the affine hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
consisting of the four lines

H_{1}:x=0,\quad H_{2}:y=0,\quad H_{3}:x+y=0,\quad H_{4}:x=1,

which is depicted as follows:
H_{2}:y=0
H_{1}:x=0
H_{4}:x=1
H_{3}:x+y=0

Then
r(\mathcal{A})=2
, and the central subarrangements are the empty subarrangement, the four one-element subarrangements, all two-element subarrangements except
\{H_{1},H_{4}\}
, and the unique three-element subarrangement
\{H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}\}
. Hence

c_{0}=1,\quad c_{1}=4,\quad c_{2}=5,\quad c_{3}=1,\quad c_{n}=0\;(n\geq 4).

Then the Tutte polynomial
T_{\mathcal{A}}(x,y)
is

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{A}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=(x-1)^{2}+4(x-1)+5+(y-1)

\displaystyle=x^{2}+2x+y+1.

Thus, the nonzero coefficients are

t_{20}=1,\qquad t_{10}=2,\qquad t_{01}=1,\qquad t_{00}=1.

Section

3.1
gives that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}k=0:&t_{00}=1&=1-4+5-1,\\[5.69054pt]
k=1:&t_{00}-t_{01}+t_{10}=2&=\binom{-1}{1}-4\binom{0}{1}+5\binom{1}{1}-\binom{2}{1},\\[5.69054pt]
k=2:&t_{00}-2t_{01}+t_{10}+t_{20}=2&=\binom{0}{2}-4\binom{1}{2}+5\binom{2}{2}-\binom{3}{2}.\end{array}

More generally, for every
k\geq 2
, both sides are equal to
4-k
.

For
k=0
, the first identity takes the form

t_{00}=\sum_{n=0}^{|\mathcal{A}|}(-1)^{r(\mathcal{A})+n}c_{n}

Thus, we have

t_{00}=(-1)^{r(\mathcal{A})}\chi_{\mathcal{A}}(1).

When
\mathcal{A}
is a real affine hyperplane arrangement, Zaslavsky’s region-counting formula
[
19
]
states that
(-1)^{r(\mathcal{A})}\chi_{\mathcal{A}}(1)
is the number of relatively bounded regions of
\mathcal{A}
. Consequently,
t_{00}
is the number of relatively bounded regions of
\mathcal{A}
.

As in the semimatroid setting, coning is a basic construction in the study of hyperplane arrangements that relates affine and central arrangements. Suppose an affine hyperplane
H
in
\mathbb{F}^{d}
is defined by
H:a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+a_{d}x_{d}=b
. We then define a linear hyperplane
cH
in
\mathbb{F}^{d+1}
by
cH:a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+a_{d}x_{d}-bx_{d+1}=0
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be an affine hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{F}^{d}
. The
cone

c\mathcal{A}
of
\mathcal{A}
is the central hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{F}^{d+1}
consisting of all
cH
for
H\in\mathcal{A}
, together with the additional hyperplane
K_{0}:x_{d+1}=0
. The corresponding characteristic polynomials satisfy

\chi_{c\mathcal{A}}(t)=(t-1)\chi_{\mathcal{A}}(t),

which can be found in
[
10
, Proposition 2.51]
and
[
13
]
. The normal vectors of the linear hyperplanes in
c\mathcal{A}
naturally determine an
\mathbb{F}
-vector matroid
M_{c\mathcal{A}}
. In this context, the semimatroid determined by the pointed matroid
(M_{c\mathcal{A}},K_{0})
corresponds precisely to the semimatroid
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}},r_{\mathcal{A}})
arising from the affine hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}
. Thus, as a direct consequence of
Theorem

2.3
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an affine hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{F}^{d}
. Then

t_{00}=(-1)^{r(\mathcal{A})}\chi_{\mathcal{A}}(1)=\beta(M_{c\mathcal{A}}).

When
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
,
t_{00}
is the number of relatively bounded regions of
\mathcal{A}
.

3.2
Application to biased graphs

In this subsection, we specialize
Theorem

1.1
to biased graphs. Biased graphs provide another natural semimatroid model; they were introduced by Zaslavsky
[
20
,
21
]
as an abstraction of gain graphs that retains their combinatorial structure. Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. If
G^{\prime}
is a subgraph containing no edge
e\in E
, we denote by
G^{\prime}+e
the subgraph obtained from
G^{\prime}
by adding
e
. For
u,v\in V
, a
uv
-path
is a path from
u
to
v
. A closed path is called a
cycle
, and its edge set is called a
circle
. A
theta graph
is the union of three internally disjoint open paths with the same two endpoints. A
biased graph

\Omega=(G,\mathcal{B})
consists of a graph
G
and a class
\mathcal{B}
of circles in
G
satisfying the following
theta property
: if two circles of a theta graph belong to
\mathcal{B}
, then so does the third. We call
G
the
underlying graph
of
\Omega
, and
\mathcal{B}
the set of
balanced circles
of
\Omega
. An edge set is
balanced
if every circle contained in it is balanced. Moreover, for any
A\subseteq E
, we say that the spanning subgraph
(V,A)
is
balanced
if
A
itself is balanced. In particular, the cycle corresponding to a balanced circle is called a
balanced cycle
. We denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}
the collection of balanced edge sets of
\Omega
. It is clear that if
A\subseteq E
is balanced, then every subset of
A
is balanced as well. Thus,
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}
is a simplicial complex. The
rank function

r_{\Omega}
of
\Omega
is given by

r_{\Omega}(A):=|V|-c(A),\quad\forall\,A\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega},

where
c(A)
is the number of connected components of the spanning subgraph
(V,A)
. In fact,
r_{\Omega}
is the usual rank function
r_{G}
of the underlying graph
G
restricted to balanced edge sets. Thus, the rank
r(\Omega)
of
\Omega
is at most the rank
r_{G}(E)
of
G
. Since every spanning forest of
G
is balanced and has rank
|V|-c(E)=r_{G}(E)
, we obtain
r(\Omega)=|V|-c(E)
.

In 1995, Zaslavsky
[
22
]
further generalized the classical chromatic and Tutte polynomials of graphs to biased graphs, and proposed the balanced chromatic polynomial and the balanced Tutte polynomial. The
balanced chromatic polynomial

\chi_{\Omega}^{b}(t)
of
\Omega
is defined by

\chi_{\Omega}^{b}(t):=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}(-1)^{|A|}t^{r(\Omega)-r_{\Omega}(A)},

and the
balanced Tutte polynomial

T_{\Omega}^{b}(x,y)
of
\Omega
is given by

T_{\Omega}^{b}(x,y):=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}(x-1)^{r(\Omega)-r_{\Omega}(A)}(y-1)^{|A|-r_{\Omega}(A)}.

Clearly,
\chi_{\Omega}^{b}(t)=(-1)^{r(\Omega)}T_{\Omega}^{b}(1-t,0)
, and these polynomials reduce to the corresponding chromatic and Tutte polynomials when
\mathcal{B}
is the set of all circles of
G
.

Work of Forge and Zaslavsky
[
7
]
implies that a gain graph yields a semimatroid when its balanced edge sets are taken as central sets, and hence suggests that the triple
(E,\mathcal{C}_{\Omega},r_{\Omega})
should be a semimatroid. Since we have not found a detailed proof of this claim in the existing literature, we give a self-contained proof below. We first establish the following lemma, which characterizes how balance is preserved under the theta construction.

With
Section

3.2
, we now proceed to show that the triple
(E,\mathcal{C}_{\Omega},r_{\Omega})
is a semimatroid.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\Omega=(G,\mathcal{B})
be a biased graph with the underlying graph
G=(V,E)
. Then the triple
(E,\mathcal{C}_{\Omega},r_{\Omega})
is a semimatroid.

Proof.

Note that the pair
(E,r_{G})
defines the cycle matroid of
G
, and
r_{\Omega}
is the restriction of the rank function
r_{G}
to the balanced edge sets. Therefore, the function
r_{\Omega}
automatically satisfies the axioms (R1)–(R3) by
[
12
, Theorem 1.3.2]
.

We now verify (SR4). Let
A,B\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}
satisfy
r_{\Omega}(A)=r_{\Omega}(A\cap B)
. Since
r_{G}(X)=|V|-c(X)
for all
X\subseteq E
and
r_{\Omega}(X)=r_{G}(X)
for all
X\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}
, we immediately obtain
c(A)=c(A\cap B)
. Together with the inclusion relation
A\cap B\subseteq A
, this implies that the spanning subgraphs
(V,A)
and
(V,A\cap B)
induce the same connected vertex partition of
V
, where each block is the vertex set of a connected component of
(V,A)
. Consequently, for every edge
e=uv\in A\setminus B
, there exists a
uv
-path
P_{e}
in
(V,A\cap B)
. Write
A\setminus B=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}
and set
X_{0}=B
,
X_{i}=B\sqcup\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{i}\}
for
i=1,\ldots k
. We prove by induction that each
X_{i}
is balanced. The base case
X_{0}=B
is clear. Suppose
X_{i-1}
is balanced. As
P_{e_{i}}
is a path in
(V,A\cap B)
and
A\cap B\subseteq X_{i-1}
, the path
P_{e_{i}}
is contained in
(V,X_{i-1})
, and
P_{e_{i}}+e_{i}
forms a cycle of
(V,A)
. Since
A
itself is balanced,
P_{e_{i}}+e_{i}
is also balanced. It follows from
Section

3.2
that
X_{i}
is balanced. Thus,
X_{k}=A\cup B
is balanced, which completes the proof of (SR4).

For (SR5), suppose
A,B\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}
satisfy
r_{\Omega}(A)<r_{\Omega}(B)
. We claim that some edge
e\in B\setminus A
joins two different connected components of
(V,A)
. Otherwise,
(V,A)
and
(V,A\sqcup(B\setminus A))=(V,A\cup B)
have the same number of connected components, and hence
r_{G}(A\cup B)=r_{G}(A)
. Since
B\subseteq A\cup B
, the monotonicity of
r_{G}
gives
r_{\Omega}(B)=r_{G}(B)\leq r_{G}(A\cup B)=r_{G}(A)=r_{\Omega}(A)
, which contradicts
r_{\Omega}(A)<r_{\Omega}(B)
. Now choose such an edge
e
. By the choice of
e
, it connects two different connected components of
(V,A)
, so
(V,A\sqcup\{e\})
has no new cycles compared with
(V,A)
. Consequently, every circle in
A\sqcup\{e\}
is already a circle of
A
, which is balanced by the assumption that
A\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}
. Hence
A\sqcup\{e\}
is also balanced, i.e.,
A\sqcup\{e\}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}
, which completes the proof of (SR5).
∎

Following
Section

3.2
, the specialization of
Theorem

1.1
to biased graphs is the next result.

Corollary 3.6

(Generalized Brylawski Identities for Biased Graphs)
.

Let
\Omega=(G,\mathcal{B})
be a biased graph with the underlying graph
G=(V,E)
, and
T_{\Omega}^{b}(x,y)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}t_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}
. Then, for all integers
k\geq 0
,

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}(-1)^{j}t_{ij}=\sum_{n=0}^{|E|}(-1)^{|V|-c(E)+n}b_{n}\binom{k+n-|V|+c(E)}{k},

where
b_{n}:=\#\{A\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}:|A|=n\}
.

Signed graphs and gain graphs form important subclasses of biased graphs. As a direct consequence,
Section

3.2
holds for their balanced Tutte polynomials. Finally, if every circle of
G
is balanced, then
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}=2^{E}
, and
T_{\Omega}^{b}(x,y)
is the ordinary Tutte polynomial
T_{G}(x,y)
of
G
. In this case,
b_{n}=\binom{|E|}{n}
. Following the same reasoning as in (
1.2
),
Section

3.2
simplifies to the generalized Brylawski identity for graphs:

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}(-1)^{j}t_{ij}=(-1)^{|E|-|V|+c(E)}\binom{k-|V|+c(E)}{k-|E|},

which was first established in
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
.
