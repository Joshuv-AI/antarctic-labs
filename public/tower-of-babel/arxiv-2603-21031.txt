1
Introduction

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure with compact support on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We say that
\mu
is a
spectral measure
if there exists a countable subset
\Lambda
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}:=\{e^{2\pi i\langle\lambda,x\rangle}\mid\lambda\in\Lambda\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
forms an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\mu)
.
In this case, we call
\Lambda
a
spectrum
for
\mu
. If a spectral measure
\mu
is the normalized Lebesgue measure restricted to a Lebesgue measurable set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then we say
\Omega
is a
spectral set
.

It is well known in classical Fourier analysis that the unit cube
[0,1]^{n}
is a spectral set with spectrum
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
The study of spectral sets is closely related to the Fuglede conjecture
[
17
]
, which states that

\Omega
is a spectral set if and only if
\Omega
tiles
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by translations.

In his seminal paper, Fuglede showed that certain planar domains, such as triangles and disks, are not spectral.
The conjecture was later disproved in both directions for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
by Tao
[
35
]
, and by Kolountzakis and Matolcsi
[
23
,
32
]
.
At present, the conjecture remains open in dimensions
n=1
and
n=2
.
Although Fuglede’s conjecture fails in higher dimensions, positive results are known in several special cases
[
38
,
15
,
20
,
33
]
and the references therein.
In particular, Lev and Matolcsi
[
28
]
proved that Fuglede’s conjecture holds for all convex domains.
Moreover, they proved that every spectral set admits a
weak tile
, which is a measure-theoretic relaxation of the classical notion of translation tile.
This result indicates that spectrality remains closely connected to geometric tiling properties.

While spectral sets have been extensively studied, a complete classification of spectral measures is substantially difficult. It was shown in
[
18
]
that any spectral measure
\mu
must be of pure type, namely,
\mu
is discrete with finite support, singularly continuous or absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure. In the absolutely continuous case, it is necessary
[
13
]
that
\mu
coincides with the Lebesgue measure on some measurable set
\Omega
.
Therefore, the study of absolutely continuous spectral measures reduces to the original Fuglede conjecture. This reduction motivates a separate investigation of singular spectral measures.
Accordingly, the primary focus of this paper is the spectrality of singular measures, with a further exploration of the spectral and tiling properties of the associated self-similar sets.

1.1
Spectrality of self-similar measure with product-form like digit sets

In 1998, Jorgensen and Pedersen
[
21
]
discovered the first non-atomic singular spectral measure. They showed that the middle-fourth Cantor measure is a spectral measure.
This result led to a systematic method for constructing fractal spectral measures.
Let us recall the standard definitions in fractal geometric
[
14
]
.
Let
\{\phi_{d}(x)\}_{d\in D}
be an
iterated function system
(IFS) defined by

\phi_{d}(x)=\rho(x+d),

where
x\in\mathbb{R},0<\rho<1
and
D\subset\mathbb{R}
is a finite set.
The
self-similar measure
is the unique probability measure
\mu:=\mu_{\rho,D}
satisfying

\mu=\frac{1}{\#D}\sum_{d\in D}\mu\circ\phi_{d}^{-1},

(1.1)

where
\#D
is the cardinality of the digit set
D
. Such a measure
\mu_{\rho,D}
is supported on the
self-similar set
(or
attractor
)
T(\rho,D)
, which is the unique nonempty compact set satisfying

T(\rho,D)=\bigcup_{d\in D}\phi_{d}(T(\rho,D)).

Moreover,
T(\rho,D)
can be expressed by the following radix expansion

\displaystyle T(\rho,D)=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\rho^{j}d:d\in D\ {\rm for\ all}\ j\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

(1.2)

Following the work of Jorgensen and Pedersen
[
21
]
, Hu and Lau
[
19
]
studied infinite orthogonal sets of exponential functions for the Bernoulli convolution measures
\mu_{\rho,\{0,1\}}
with
0<\rho<1
.
Dai
[
8
]
gave a complete description of the spectrality of
\mu_{\rho,\{0,1\}}
. Later, Dai, He and Lau extended
\mu_{\rho,\{0,1\}}
to the
N
-Bernoulli convolution measures
\mu_{\rho,D_{N}}
, where
D_{N}=\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}
is a consecutive digit set for any positive integer
N\geq 2
. They
[
7
]
proved that
\mu_{\rho,D_{N}}
is a spectral measure if and only if
\rho^{-1}=p
is an integer and
N
divides
p
.
Indeed, a key observation is that the spectrality of
\mu_{\rho,D_{N}}
can be reformulated within the setting of Hadamard triples. In some sense, the spectrality of the measure is closely to the existence of the Hadamard triple.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
R\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
be an expanding matrix
1
1

1

R\in GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is an expanding matrix means that all the eigenvalues of
R
are larger than one in module.
and
D,L\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be two finite digit sets with the same cardinality,
i.e.,\#D=\#L
. We say that
(R,D,L)
is a Hadamard triple (or
(R,D)
is an admissible pair) if the matrix

\displaystyle H=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\#D}}\left(e^{2\pi i\langle M^{-1}d,l\rangle}\right)_{d\in D,l\in L}

is unitary, i.e.,
H^{\ast}H=I_{n}
, where
H^{\ast}
means the transposed conjugate of
H
and
I_{n}
denotes the
n\times n
identity matrix.

Jorgensen and Pedersen
[
21
]
proved that if
(R,D,L)
is a Hadamard triple, then

\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda(R,L)}
admits an infinite orthogonal system in
L^{2}(\mu_{R^{-1},D})
, where

\Lambda(R,L)=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}R^{*j}l_{j}:k\geq 1,l_{j}\in L\right\}.

Dutkay and Jorgensen
[
10
,
11
]
conjectured that
\mu_{R^{-1},D}
is a spectral measure whenever
(R,D)
is admissible.
This conjecture was first proved on
\mathbb{R}
by Łaba and Wang
[
37
]
.
In higher dimensions, the situation is more complex.
Partial results were obtained under additional assumptions in
[
10
,
34
]
.
This conjecture was finally solved by Dutkay, Haussermann and Lai
[
9
]
.
Although Hadamard triples are sufficient to generate spectral self-similar measures, not all spectral self-similar measures can be generated by Hardamard triples.

Example 1.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
R=4
and
D=\{0,1,8,9\}
. Then
\mu_{4^{-1},D}
coincides with the Lebesgue measure restricted to
T=[0,1]\cup[2,3]
, and
T
is spectral with a spectrum
\mathbb{Z}+\{0,1/4\}
. However, since the elements of
D
are not distinct modulo 4, there does not exist any
L
such that
(R,D,L)
forms a Hadamard triple.

In this example, the digit set

D=\{0,1,8,9\}

can be written in a product-form as

D=\{0,1\}\oplus 2^{3}\{0,1\}.

This representation places
D
within a class of product-form digit sets. For convenience, in what follows, we use symbol
D_{n}
to denote the consecutive digit set
\{0,1,\cdots,n-1\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, where
\mathbb{N}^{*}
means the set of positive integers. In this setting, Liu et al.
[
31
]
considered the spectrality of the measures
\mu_{\rho,D}
generated by
\rho^{-1}=N^{q}
and the product-form digit set

\displaystyle D=D_{N}\oplus N^{p}D_{N},

(1.3)

where integers
q,p\geq 1
and
N\geq 2
.
They proved that
\mu_{\rho,D}
is a spectral measure if and only if
q
does not divide
p
. Wu
[
36
]
studied the spectrality of a class of self-similar measures generated by the alternating contractive ratios IFSs
\{\phi_{d}(x)=(-\rho)^{d}(x+d)\}_{d\in D_{2N}}
.
A key observation in the proof of
[
36
, Proposition 4.2]
is that the spectrality of the self-similar measure for the digit set
D_{2N}
reduces to that of
\mu_{\rho,D}
defined by (
1.1
),
where
0<\rho<1
and

D=D_{N}\oplus\left(N-\frac{1+\rho}{2}\right)D_{2}.

In other words, the spectrality problem for such self-similar measures reduces to the case of measures associated with product-form digit sets.
It is also worth noting that An and Lai
[
2
]
introduced a more general class of product-form digit sets constructed from finitely many Hadamard triples, and proved that the associated product-form self-similar measures are spectral.

Motivated by the existed results related to product-form digit set, in this paper, we consider a class of self-similar measures generated by product-form like digit set, which encompass both Example
1.1
and the digit sets presented in (
1.3
).
Specifically, we consider digit sets of the form

D=D_{N}\oplus mD_{L},

(1.4)

where
N,m,L\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
with
N,L\geq 2
.
The aim of this paper is to characterize the arithmetic conditions on the parameters
m
,
N
, and
L
under which the associated self-similar measure
\mu_{\rho,D}
is spectral.
When
N=1
or
L=1
, the spectrality of
\mu_{\rho,D}
follows directly from
[
7
]
. Therefore, throughout this paper we restrict our attention to the case
N,L\geq 2
.
Our main result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mu_{\rho,D}
be a self-similar measure defined by (
1.1
), where

0<\rho<1
and
D
is given by (
1.4
).
Then
\mu_{\rho,D}
is a spectral measure if and only if
\rho^{-1}=p\in\mathbb{N}

satisfies

N\mid p,\quad L\mid p,\quad{\rm{and}}\quad N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})},

where

d=\max\left\{i:\gcd\left(\frac{mL}{\gcd(mL,p^{i})},L\right)\neq 1,i\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

(1.5)

Strategy of the proof and remarks
.

(1)

The main difficulty is to relate the parameters
N
,
L
, and
m
, which are independent. This leads to a complicated relation among the zero sets of the Fourier transforms of
\mu_{\rho,D}
. To avoid this difficulty in the proof, we use an equivalent definition of
d
in (
1.5
), which is given in (
3.4
). The original definition in (
1.5
) is convenient for concrete applications (e.g., Examples 1.3 and 1.4), while the new one in (
3.4
) better reflects the essential relations among the parameters.

(2)

For the necessity part, traditional methods for proving the relation
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
are limited. We instead use a proof by contradiction, inspired by
[
1
]
. Assuming
N\nmid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
, we show that any orthogonal set
\Lambda
for
\mu_{\rho,D}
must be incomplete.
More precisely, we construct a nonzero function

f\in L^{2}(\mu_{\rho,D})
such that

\langle f,e_{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{\rho,D})}=0\quad\text{for all }\lambda\in\Lambda,

where
e_{\lambda}=e^{2\pi i\langle\lambda,x\rangle}
. The detailed proof is given in Section
3
.

(3)

We finally remark that the conditions
N\mid p
,
L\mid p
, and

N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
imply
NL\mid p
, and hence

p\geq\#D

(see Remark
3.1
).
In most cases, the condition
p\geq\#D
indicates that the associated
self-similar measure is singular.
When
p<\#D
, the situation becomes more complicated due to overlaps
among the sets
T+d
,
d\in D
.
The special case
p=\#D
will be studied further in connection with
self-similar tiles and spectral sets in Section
4
.

To gain some insight into our results, we present two examples below.

Example 1.3
.

Let
\mu_{\rho,D}
be the self-similar measure defined by (
1.1
), where

\rho^{-1}=72
and
D=D_{12}\oplus mD_{4}
with some
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Then
\mu_{\rho,D}
is not a spectral measure for any
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.

Proof.

Suppose, to the contrary, that
\mu_{\rho,D}
is a spectral measure for some

m=2^{\tau}m^{\prime}
with
\tau\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
m^{\prime}\in 2\mathbb{Z}+1
. Let
d
be defined by (
1.5
). Then we have

\gcd\left(\frac{2^{\tau+2}m^{\prime}}{\gcd(2^{\tau+2}m^{\prime},2^{3(d+1)}3^{2(d+1)})},4\right)=1,

which yields that
\tau\leq 3d+1
.
According to Theorem
1.2
, it is necessary that

4\mid\frac{2^{\tau}m^{\prime}}{\gcd(2^{\tau}m^{\prime},2^{3d}3^{2d})}.

This condition implies
\tau-3d\geq 2
, which contradicts the fact that

\tau\leq 3d+1
. Therefore, there exists no

m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that
\mu_{\rho,D}
is a spectral measure.
∎

Example 1.4
.

Let
\mu_{\rho,D}
be the self-similar measure defined by (
1.1
), where

\rho^{-1}=144
and
D=D_{12}\oplus mD_{4}
with some
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Then
\mu_{\rho,D}
is a
spectral measure if and only if

m=2^{\tau_{1}}3^{\tau_{2}}m^{\prime}
satisfies

\tau_{1}=4k+2\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{2}\geq 2k+1

for some nonnegative integer
k
and
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
with
\gcd(m^{\prime},6)=1
.

Proof.

Note that
p:=\rho^{-1}=2^{4}\cdot 3^{2}

and
N:=12=2^{2}\cdot 3
. By using Theorem
1.2
, it follows that
\mu_{p^{-1},D}
is a spectral measure if and only if
12\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
, where
d
is defined by (
1.5
).
Then the condition
12\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
gives that

\tau_{1}-4d\geq 2\quad{\rm{and}}\quad\tau_{2}-2d\geq 1.

(1.6)

On the other hand, the definition of
d
in (
1.5
) forces that
\tau_{1}+2\leq 4(d+1)
. Combining this and (
1.6
) gives that
\tau_{1}=4d+2
and
\tau_{2}\geq 2d+1
.
∎

1.2
Fuglede conjecture on self-similar sets with product-form like digit set

In this section, we investigate the spectral and tiling properties of self-similar sets generated by the product-form like digit sets, which is closely connected to the one-dimensional Fuglede conjecture.
Recall that if
\rho^{-1}=\#D
and the attractor
T(\rho,D)
defined by (
1.2
) has nonempty interior, then
T(\rho,D)
tiles
\mathbb{R}
by translations
[
25
]
and is called a
self-similar tile
. In the context of the Fuglede conjecture, it is thus natural to ask whether such a self-similar tile is also a spectral set. This problem is at the heart of the “tile
\Rightarrow
spectral set” direction of the Fuglede conjecture in
\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, the spectrality of self-similar (and more generally, self-affine) tiles has been extensively studied in the literature.

In dimension one, Kenyon
[
22
]
studied tile digit sets using number-theoretic methods.
Coven and Meyerowitz
[
6
]
introduced the (T1) and (T2) conditions for the spectrality of self-similar tiles. Lagarias and Wang
[
25
,
24
]
introduced product-form and modulo product-form digit sets. Lai et al.
[
27
]
and Fu et al.
[
16
]
studied the spectrality of the corresponding self-similar tiles.
In higher dimensions, An and Lau
[
3
]
studied self-affine tiles generated by the expanding matrix
pI_{2}
, where
p
is prime. More recently, Li and Rao
[
29
]
extended several characterization results to self-affine tiles in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and obtained certain spectral properties of self-similar tiles in
\mathbb{R}
. Chen et al.
[
5
]
further proved that the generalized Sierpiski-type set is a spectral set if and only if it is a self-affine tile.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.2
, we obtain the following result on tiling and spectral properties for the self-similar set generated by product-form like digit sets.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
T(\rho,D)
be a self-similar set defined by (
1.1
), where
\rho^{-1}=LN
and
D
is given by (
1.4
).
Then
T(\rho,D)
is a spectral set if and only if
T(\rho,D)
is a translation tile of
\mathbb{R}
.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows.
In Section
2
, we provide some basic definitions and preliminaries concerning self-similar measures.
In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.2
, which establishes a necessary and sufficient condition for the spectrality of
\mu_{\rho,D}
.
Finally, in Section
4
, we study the tiling and spectral properties of the self-similar set
T(\rho,D)
and present the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some basic definitions and useful conclusions related to self-similar measures. Assume that
\mu
is a probability measure with compact support on
\mathbb{R}
. The Fourier transform of
\mu
is defined as

\hat{\mu}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{2\pi i\langle\xi,x\rangle}d\mu(x),~~~\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

It is widely known that the self-similar measure
\mu_{\rho,D}
can be represented as an infinite convolution of discrete measures

\displaystyle\mu_{\rho,D}=\delta_{\rho D}\ast\delta_{\rho^{2}D}\ast\delta_{\rho^{3}D}\ast\cdots

(2.1)

where
\delta_{D}=\frac{1}{\#D}\sum_{d\in D}\delta_{d}
,
\delta_{d}
is the Dirac measure at the point
d\in D
. By direct calculations, the Fourier transform of the self-similar measure
\mu_{\rho,D}
in (
2.1
) is as follows:

\hat{\mu}_{\rho,D}(\xi)=\prod^{\infty}_{n=1}\hat{\delta}_{\rho^{n}D}(\xi)=\prod^{\infty}_{n=1}m_{D}(\rho^{n}\xi),

where
m_{D}(\cdot)
is the mask polynomial of the digit set
D
, i.e.,

\displaystyle m_{D}(x)=\frac{1}{\#D}\sum_{d\in D}e^{2\pi i\langle d,x\rangle},x\in\mathbb{R}.

We denote
Z(f)=\{x:f(x)=0\}
as the zero set of function
f
, then it is easy to know

\displaystyle Z(\hat{\mu}_{\rho,D})=\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=1}\rho^{-n}Z(m_{D}).

(2.2)

Notice that the properties of spectra are invariant under translation. Without loss of generality, we always assume that
0\in\Lambda
.
For any
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}\in\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
, the orthogonality condition shows that

\langle e^{2\pi i\langle\lambda_{1},x\rangle},e^{2\pi i\langle\lambda_{2},x\rangle}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{\rho,D})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{2\pi i(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})x}d\mu_{\rho,D}=\hat{\mu}_{\rho,D}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})=0.

Consequently, for a countable set
0\in\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
, the family of exponential functions
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}
forms an orthogonal set of
L^{2}(\mu_{\rho,D})
if and only if

\displaystyle\Lambda\subset(\Lambda-\Lambda)\backslash\{0\}\subset Z(\hat{\mu}_{\rho,D}).

(2.3)

Jorgensen and Pedersen
[
21
]
provided the following criterion for a countable set
\Lambda
to be an orthogonal set or a spectrum for the measure
\mu
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
21
]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure with compact support on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
Q_{\mu,\Lambda}(\cdot)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|\hat{\mu}(\cdot+\lambda)|^{2}
for a countable set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

(i)

\Lambda
is an orthogonal set of
\mu
if and only if
Q_{\mu,\Lambda}(x)\leq 1
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

(ii)

\Lambda
is a spectrum of
\mu
if and only if
Q_{\mu,\Lambda}(x)\equiv 1
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Based on Theorem
2.1
, Dai et al.
[
7
]
gave the following criterion for the non-spectrality of a measure.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\mu=\mu_{1}\ast\mu_{2}
be the convolution of two probability measures
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
, neither of which is a Dirac measure. Suppose that
\Lambda
is an orthogonal set of
\mu_{1}
, then
\Lambda
is also an orthogonal set of
\mu
, but it cannot be a spectrum of
\mu
.

The following consequence follows directly from Theorem
2.2
and provides a convenient criterion for detecting non-spectral self-similar measures.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
\mu_{\rho,D}
be a self-similar measure defined by (
1.1
). Assume that the digit set
D
admits a decomposition

D=D_{(1)}\oplus D_{(2)}\oplus\cdots\oplus D_{(n)}.

If there exist distinct indices
1\leq j_{1}\neq j_{2}\leq n
and integers
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 1
such that

Z(\hat{\delta}_{\rho^{m_{1}}D_{(j_{1})}})\subset Z(\hat{\delta}_{\rho^{m_{2}}D_{(j_{2})}}),

then Theorem
2.2
implies that
\mu_{\rho,D}
is not a spectral measure.

Theorem 2.3

Strichartz

introduced the notion of Hadamard triple (Definition

), which plays an important role in the study of spectral measures. The following lemma provides a concrete criterion for verifying whether a given triple forms a Hadamard triple.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
R\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
be an expanding matrix, and let
B,L\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be two finite digit sets with the same cardinality. Then
(R,B,L)
is a Hadamard triple if and only if
m_{B}(R^{*-1}(l_{1}-l_{2}))=0
for any
l_{1}\neq l_{2}\in L
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
. Define the integral periodic zero set of the Fourier transform
\hat{\mu}
by

\mathcal{Z}(\mu)=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:\hat{\mu}(\xi+k)=0~~{\rm{for~all}}~k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

In general, it is difficult to determine whether the set
\mathcal{Z}(\mu)
is empty. Consider the infinite convolution

\displaystyle\nu:=\delta_{R_{1}^{-1}B_{1}}*\delta_{(R_{1}R_{2})^{-1}B_{2}}*\cdots*\delta_{(R_{1}R_{2}\cdots R_{k})^{-1}B_{k}}*\cdots

(2.4)

generated by a sequence of admissible pairs
{(R_{k},B_{k})}_{k=1}^{\infty}
. Li et al.
[
30
]
derived a sufficient condition for
\mathcal{Z}(\nu)=\emptyset
when
(R_{k},B_{k})
is selected from a finite collection of admissible pairs.
When
(R_{k},B_{k})=(R,B)
for all

2.4
) reduces to the self-similar measure

\mu_{R^{-1},B}
given by (
2.1
).

Proposition 2.5

(
[
30
]
)
.

Let the sequence of admissible pairs
\{(R_{k},B_{k})\}^{\infty}_{k=1}
be chosen from a finite set of admissible pairs in

be defined by (
2.4
). If

\gcd\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{k=n}\left(B_{k}-B_{k}\right)\right)=1

.

Hadamard triples also yield a large class of self-affine spectral measures. In particular, it has been shown that if a self-affine measure

Theorem 2.6

Let

The similarity transformation implies that both spectrality and translational tiling are preserved under the action of matrix operators, the proof of which can be found in

Theorem 2.7

This section is devoted to studying the spectrality of self-similar measures associated with product-form like digit sets given by (

1.2
by establishing the necessity and sufficiency separately in Theorems

Recall that
\mu_{\rho,D}
is a self-similar measure generated by
0<\rho<1
and

D=D_{N}\oplus mD_{L},

where
N,L,m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
with
N,L\geq 2
.
We now give an equivalent description of
d
defined in (
1.5
)
in terms of the prime factorization of

Let

L=L_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}L_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{\alpha_{\kappa}}

(3.1)

be the prime factorization of

, where
L_{i}
are distinct primes greater than two, and

\alpha_{i}\geq 1
for

Notice that the spectrality of
\mu_{\rho,D}

(see Proposition
3.1
) imposes a constraint on the parameter

, namely
L\mid p
with
p:=\rho^{-1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. This allows us to also write

m=L_{1}^{\tau_{1}}L_{2}^{\tau_{2}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{\tau_{\kappa}}m^{\prime},\qquad p=L_{1}^{l_{1}}L_{2}^{l_{2}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{l_{\kappa}}p^{\prime},

(3.2)

where
\tau_{i}\geq 0
,

and

\gcd(L_{i},m^{\prime})=\gcd(L_{i},p^{\prime})=1~\text{for all }1\leq i\leq\kappa.

Therefore,
for each
i
, there exist unique integers
d_{i}\geq 0

and
0\leq r_{i}<l_{i}
such that

\tau_{i}+\alpha_{i}-1=d_{i}l_{i}+r_{i}.

(3.3)

Thus
d
defined in (
1.5
) can be equivalently expressed as

d=\max_{1\leq i\leq\kappa}d_{i}.

(3.4)

3.1
The necessity.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mu_{\rho,D}
be a self-similar measure defined by (
1.1
), where

0<\rho<1
and
D
is given by (
1.4
).
If
\mu_{\rho,D}
is a spectral measure, then
\rho^{-1}
is a positive integer that is divisible by both
N
and
L
.

Proof.

It can be checked that
Z(m_{D})
is contained in a lattice set. Hence, by Theorem
2.3
, the hypothesis that
\mu_{\rho,D}
is a spectral measure implies
\rho^{-1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Assume that
\rho^{-1}=p
, then we will show that
N
and
L
must be factors of
p
.

First, we prove that
N\mid p
. Suppose for contradiction that this is not the case, and let

d=\gcd(N,p)
. Since
1\leq d<N
, there exist positive integers
p^{\prime}
and
N^{\prime}\geq 2
such that
p=dp^{\prime}
,
N=dN^{\prime}
and
\gcd(p^{\prime},N^{\prime})=1
.
At this time,
D
can be discomposed into

D=D_{d}\oplus dD_{N^{\prime}}\oplus mD_{L},

which implies that the self-similar measure
\mu_{\rho,D}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mu_{\rho,D}=

\displaystyle\delta_{\rho D}\ast\delta_{\rho^{2}D}\ast\delta_{\rho^{3}D}\ast\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\delta_{\rho D_{N}}*\delta_{\rho mD_{L}})*(\delta_{\rho^{2}D_{d}}\ast\delta_{\rho^{2}dD_{N^{\prime}}}\ast\delta_{\rho^{2}mD_{L}})\ast\delta_{\rho^{3}D}\cdots.

By using
\gcd(p^{\prime},N^{\prime})=1
, we can obtain

\displaystyle Z(\hat{\delta}_{\rho^{2}dD_{N^{\prime}}})

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{2}}{dN^{\prime}}\big(\mathbb{Z}\setminus N^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{pp^{\prime}}{N^{\prime}}\big(\mathbb{Z}\setminus N^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}\big)

\displaystyle\subset\frac{p}{N^{\prime}}\big(\mathbb{Z}\setminus N^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}\big)

\displaystyle\subset\frac{p}{N}\big(\mathbb{Z}\setminus N\mathbb{Z}\big)=Z(\hat{\delta}_{\rho D_{N}}).

It follows from Remark
2.1
that
\mu_{\rho,D}
is not a spectral measure. Hence we have
N\mid p
.

The conclusion
L\mid p
can be proved by a similar argument, and hence we omit the details for brevity.

∎

To prove the necessity of Theorem
1.2
, we also need some new notation, defined as follows.
Let
\sigma=\gcd(m,p^{d})
. We can then write
m=\sigma\tilde{m}
and
p^{d}=\sigma\tilde{p}
for some integers
\tilde{m}
and
\tilde{p}
satisfying
\gcd(\tilde{m},\tilde{p})=1
. Now, define

\tilde{d}=\gcd(N,\tilde{m}).

Then
N=\tilde{d}N_{1}
and
\tilde{m}=\tilde{d}m_{1}
for some
N_{1},m_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\gcd(N_{1},m_{1})=1
.
Consequently,
N
and
m
can be expressed as

\displaystyle N=\tilde{d}N_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad m=\sigma\tilde{d}m_{1}.

(3.5)

Without loss of generality, we assume that
d_{1}=d=\max\{d_{i}:1\leq i\leq\kappa\}
. Notice that
d_{1}
depends on
\alpha_{1}
,
\tau_{1}
and
l_{1}
by (
3.3
). Thus, by factoring out
L_{1}
from the prime factorization of
L
, we write
L=L_{1}L^{\prime}
, where
L^{\prime}=L_{1}^{\alpha_{1}-1}L_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{\alpha_{\kappa}}
. Then the digit set
D
decomposes as

D=D_{N}\oplus mD_{L}=\left(D_{\tilde{d}}\oplus\tilde{d}D_{N_{1}}\right)\oplus m\left(D_{L^{\prime}}\oplus L^{\prime}D_{L_{1}}\right).

(3.6)

It is clear to see that if
N_{1}=1
, then
D_{\tilde{d}}\oplus\tilde{d}D_{N_{1}}=D_{N}
. Hence, we shall restrict our attention to
N_{1}>1
.
Combining (
3.6
) with formulas (
2.1
) and (

), we define the following three subsets related to the zero set

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle:=Z(\hat{\delta}_{\rho\tilde{d}D_{N_{1}}})=p\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus N_{1}\mathbb{Z}}{\tilde{d}N_{1}},

(3.7)

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle:=Z(\hat{\delta}_{\rho^{d+1}mL^{\prime}D_{L_{1}}})=p^{d+1}\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus L_{1}\mathbb{Z}}{Lm}=p\frac{\tilde{p}(\mathbb{Z}\setminus L_{1}\mathbb{Z})}{L\tilde{d}m_{1}},

(3.8)

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle:=Z(\widehat{\mu^{\prime}})=\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{d}p^{j}\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus L\mathbb{Z}}{mL}\right)\cup\left(p^{d+1}\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus L^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}}{L^{\prime}m}\right),

(3.9)

where
\mu^{\prime}:=\delta_{\rho mD_{L}}*\delta_{\rho^{2}mD_{L}}*\cdots*\delta_{\rho^{d}mD_{L}}*\delta_{\rho^{d+1}mD_{L^{\prime}}}
.

With the above preparations, we now prove the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mu_{p^{-1},D}
be a self-similar measure defined by (
1.1
), where
p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
D
is given by (
1.4
). Suppose that
L\mid p
,
N\nmid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
and
0\in\Lambda
is a bi-zero set of
\mu_{p^{-1},D}
. Then

\Lambda\cap(R_{1}\setminus(S_{1}\cup S_{2}))=\emptyset\quad{\rm{or}}\quad\Lambda\cap(S_{1}\setminus(R_{1}\cup S_{2}))=\emptyset,

where
R_{1},S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are defined in (
3.7
), (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), respectively.

Proof.

Under the assumption
N\nmid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
, the value
N_{1}
defined in (
3.5
) is greater than one.
Suppose, for contradiction, that the conclusion does not hold.
Then by (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), there exist
a_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus N_{1}\mathbb{Z}
and
a_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus L_{1}\mathbb{Z}
, along with elements

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\Lambda
, such that

\lambda_{1}=p\frac{a_{1}}{\tilde{d}N_{1}}\in\Lambda\cap\left(R_{1}\setminus(S_{1}\cup S_{2})\right)

(3.10)

and

\lambda_{2}=p^{d+1}\frac{a_{2}}{Lm}=p\frac{\tilde{p}a_{2}}{L\tilde{d}m_{1}}\in\Lambda\cap\left(S_{1}\setminus(R_{1}\cup S_{2})\right).

(3.11)

Since
\Lambda
is an orthogonal set, we have
\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\in Z(\hat{\mu}_{p^{-1},D})
by (
2.3
). Then,

\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}
must satisfy either

\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}p^{j}\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus N\mathbb{Z}}{N}\quad{\rm{or}}\quad\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}p^{j}\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus L\mathbb{Z}}{Lm}.

Case 1:

\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}=p^{j_{1}}\frac{a_{3}}{N}
for some integers
j_{1}\geq 1
and
a_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus N\mathbb{Z}
.

Since
N=\tilde{d}N_{1}
, we obtain

p\frac{a_{1}}{\tilde{d}N_{1}}-p\frac{\tilde{p}a_{2}}{L\tilde{d}m_{1}}=p^{j_{1}}\frac{a_{3}}{\tilde{d}N_{1}}.

Multiplying both sides by
N_{1}Lm_{1}
leads to

Lm_{1}(a_{1}-p^{j_{1}-1}a_{3})=N_{1}\tilde{p}a_{2}.

Hence
Lm_{1}\mid N_{1}\tilde{p}a_{2}
. Furthermore, we can obtain
\frac{N_{1}\tilde{p}a_{2}}{Lm_{1}}\notin N_{1}\mathbb{Z}.

Indeed, if
\frac{N_{1}\tilde{p}a_{2}}{Lm_{1}}=N_{1}v
for some
v\in\mathbb{Z}
, then

\tilde{p}a_{2}=Lm_{1}v.

(3.12)

Note that

\tilde{p}=\frac{p^{d}}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}=:L_{1}^{\sigma_{1}}p^{\prime\prime},

where
\sigma_{1}=dl_{1}-\min\{\tau_{1},dl_{1}\}\geq 0
and
p^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfies
\gcd(p^{\prime\prime},L_{1})=1
. Combining
d_{1}=d
and (
3.3
), one may obtain that

\tau_{1}+\alpha_{1}-1=dl_{1}+r_{1}\quad(0\leq r_{1}<l_{1}).

This shows that
0\leq\sigma_{1}\leq\alpha_{1}-1
. By (
3.12
) and
L=L_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}L_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{\alpha_{\kappa}}
, we have

\displaystyle p^{\prime\prime}a_{2}=L_{1}^{\alpha_{1}-\sigma_{1}}L_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{\alpha_{\kappa}}m_{1}v.

(3.13)

Since
\gcd(p^{\prime\prime}a_{2},L_{1})=1
and
\alpha_{1}-\sigma_{1}\geq 1
, the equation (
3.13
) leads to a contradiction.
Therefore, we get
\frac{N_{1}\tilde{p}a_{2}}{Lm_{1}}\notin N_{1}\mathbb{Z}
and

\lambda_{2}=p\frac{\tilde{p}a_{2}}{L\tilde{d}m_{1}}=p\cdot\frac{1}{\tilde{d}N_{1}}\cdot\frac{N_{1}\tilde{p}a_{2}}{Lm_{1}}\in p\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus N_{1}\mathbb{Z}}{\tilde{d}N_{1}}=R_{1},

which contradicts the condition
\lambda_{2}\in\Lambda\cap\left(S_{1}\setminus(R_{1}\cup S_{2})\right)
.

Case 2:

\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}=p^{j_{2}}\frac{a_{4}}{Lm}
for some integers
j_{2}\geq 1
and
a_{4}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus L\mathbb{Z}
.

Substituting (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) into the expression for
\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}
gives

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}=p\frac{a_{1}}{\tilde{d}N_{1}}-p^{d+1}\frac{a_{2}}{Lm}=p^{j_{2}}\frac{a_{4}}{Lm}.

(3.14)

We first show that
j_{2}\geq d+1
. Suppose, to the contrary, that
j_{2}<d+1
.
From (
3.14
),
a_{4}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus L\mathbb{Z}
, and
L\mid p
, we can obtain that

\lambda_{1}=p^{j_{2}}\frac{a_{4}+p^{d+1-j_{2}}a_{2}}{Lm}\in p^{j_{2}}\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus L\mathbb{Z}}{Lm}\subset S_{2}.

This contradicts the assumption
\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda\cap\left(R_{1}\setminus(S_{1}\cup S_{2})\right)
. Hence
j_{2}\geq d+1
.

From (
3.14
), along with
m=\sigma\tilde{d}m_{1}
and
p^{d}=\sigma\tilde{p}
, we further obtain

Lm_{1}a_{1}=N_{1}\tilde{p}\left(a_{2}+p^{j_{2}-d-1}a_{4}\right).

Indeed, this implies

\displaystyle a_{2}+p^{j_{2}-d-1}a_{4}=\frac{Lm_{1}a_{1}}{N_{1}\tilde{p}}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus L\mathbb{Z}.

(3.15)

Otherwise, we would have
\frac{Lm_{1}a_{1}}{N_{1}\tilde{p}}=Lz_{0}

for some
z_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and hence

m_{1}a_{1}=N_{1}\tilde{p}z_{0}\in N_{1}\mathbb{Z},

which contradicts the assumptions that
\gcd(m_{1},N_{1})=1
and

a_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus N_{1}\mathbb{Z}
.
Thus, it follows from (
3.15
) that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=p\frac{a_{1}}{\tilde{d}N_{1}}=p\frac{\tilde{p}\big(a_{2}+p^{\,j_{2}-d-1}a_{4}\big)}{\tilde{d}m_{1}L}\in p^{d+1}\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus L\mathbb{Z}}{mL}\subset S_{1}\cup S_{2},

which again contradicts the condition
\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda\cap\left(R_{1}\setminus(S_{1}\cup S_{2})\right)
.
Therefore, both cases lead to a contradiction and we complete the proof.

∎

Now we continue proving the necessity of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mu_{\rho,D}
be a self-similar measure defined by (
1.1
), where
0<\rho<1
and
D
is given by (
1.4
). If
\mu_{\rho,D}
is a spectral measure, then
\rho^{-1}=p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
with
N\mid p
,
L\mid p
and
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
, where
d\in\mathbb{N}
is defined as (
3.4
).

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
, it follows that
\rho^{-1}=p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
with
N\mid p
and
L\mid p
. It therefore remains to show that
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
. We argue by contradiction. Assume, to the contrary, that
N\nmid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
. Let
0\in\Lambda
be a spectrum of
\mu_{\rho,D}
. A contradiction will be obtained by constructing a nonzero function

F\in L^{2}(\mu_{\rho,D})
satisfying

\langle F,e_{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{\rho,D})}=0
for all
e_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}
.

Recall that
R_{1},S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are defined by (
3.7
), (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) respectively.
Then by Proposition
3.2
, we have

\Lambda\cap(R_{1}\setminus(S_{1}\cup S_{2}))=\emptyset\quad{\rm{or}}\quad\Lambda\cap(S_{1}\setminus(R_{1}\cup S_{2}))=\emptyset.

In view of the decomposition of
D
in (
3.6
), we treat the two possibilities separately.

Case 1:
\Lambda\cap(R_{1}\setminus(S_{1}\cup S_{2}))=\emptyset
.

Note that the self-similar measure
\mu_{\rho,D}
is supported on
T(p^{-1},D)
, which satisfies the equation

T(p^{-1},D)=p^{-1}D+\sum_{k\geq 2}p^{-k}D=:p^{-1}\tilde{d}D_{N_{1}}+\tilde{T}_{1}

with
\tilde{T}_{1}:=p^{-1}D_{\tilde{d}}+p^{-1}mD_{L}+\sum_{k\geq 2}p^{-k}D
from the decomposition
D=\tilde{d}D_{N_{1}}\oplus D_{\tilde{d}}\oplus mD_{L}
. The measure
\mu_{\rho,D}
also admits the convolution representation

\mu_{\rho,D}=\delta_{p^{-1}\tilde{d}D_{N_{1}}}\ast\mu_{1},\quad\mu_{1}:=\delta_{p^{-1}D_{\tilde{d}}}\ast\delta_{p^{-1}mD_{L}}\ast(\ast^{\infty}_{j=2}\delta_{p^{-j}D}).

It is easy to see that
\tilde{T}_{1}
is the support of
\mu_{1}
.

The assumption
N\nmid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
implies that
N_{1}>1
. Define

f(x)=\chi_{p^{-1}\{0\}}(x)-\chi_{p^{-1}\{\tilde{d}\}}(x)\quad{\rm{and}}\quad F(x)=f\ast\chi_{\tilde{T}_{1}}(x),

where
\chi_{B}
denotes the indicator function of the set
B
.
Clearly,
f\in L^{2}(\delta_{p^{-1}\tilde{d}D_{N_{1}}})
and

F(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\chi_{\tilde{T}_{1}}(x-y)dy=\chi_{\tilde{T}_{1}}(x)-\chi_{\tilde{T}_{1}}(x-p^{-1}\tilde{d})\in L^{2}(\mu_{\rho,D})

since

\int|F|^{2}d\mu_{\rho,D}\leq\int 1d\mu_{\rho,D}=1<\infty.

Utilizing the properties of convolution and the Fourier transform, we obtain that for any
\lambda\in\Lambda
we have

\displaystyle\langle F,e_{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{\rho,D})}=

\displaystyle\langle f\ast\chi_{\tilde{T}_{1}},e_{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{\rho,D})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{N_{1}}(1-e^{-2\pi i\frac{\tilde{d}}{p}\lambda})\cdot\hat{\chi}_{\tilde{T}_{1}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{N_{1}}(1-e^{-2\pi i\frac{\tilde{d}}{p}\lambda})\cdot\hat{\delta}_{D_{\tilde{d}}}(\frac{\lambda}{p})\cdot\hat{\delta}_{D_{L}}(\frac{m\lambda}{p})\cdot\prod_{k\geq 2}\hat{\delta}_{D}(p^{-k}\lambda).

For any nonzero
\lambda\in\Lambda
, the condition
\Lambda\cap(R_{1}\setminus(S_{1}\cup S_{2}))=\emptyset
implies

\hat{\delta}_{D_{\tilde{d}}}(\frac{\lambda}{p})\cdot\hat{\delta}_{D_{L}}(\frac{m\lambda}{p})\cdot\prod_{k\geq 2}\hat{\delta}_{D}(p^{-k}\lambda)=0.

If
\lambda=0
, then
1-e^{-2\pi i\frac{\tilde{d}}{p}\lambda}=0
.
Hence we have
\langle F,e_{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{\rho,D})}=0
for each
e_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}
. However,
F
is not identically zero, which contradicts the completeness of
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}
. We therefore conclude that
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
in Case 1.

Case 2:
\Lambda\cap(S_{1}\setminus(R_{1}\cup S_{2}))=\emptyset
.

Similar to the discussion in the previous case, the self-similar set
T(p^{-1},D)
can be written as

T(p^{-1},D)=p^{-(d+1)}D+\sum_{k\geq 1,k\neq d+1}p^{-k}D=:p^{-(d+1)}mL^{\prime}D_{L_{1}}+\tilde{T}_{2},

where
\tilde{T}_{2}:=p^{-(d+1)}D_{N}+p^{-(d+1)}mD_{L^{\prime}}+\sum_{k\geq 1,k\neq d+1}p^{-k}D
.
The measure
\mu_{\rho,D}
can be expressed as

\mu_{\rho,D}=\delta_{p^{-(d+1)}mL^{\prime}D_{L_{1}}}\ast\mu_{2},

where
\mu_{2}:=\delta_{p^{-(d+1)}D_{N}}\ast\delta_{p^{-(d+1)}mD_{L^{\prime}}}\ast(\ast^{\infty}_{j=1,j\neq d+1}\delta_{p^{-j}D})
is supported on
\tilde{T}_{2}
.
Since
L_{1}>1
, we define

g(x)=\chi_{p^{-(d+1)}\{0\}}(x)-\chi_{p^{-(d+1)}\{mL^{\prime}\}}(x)\quad{\rm{and}}\quad G(x)=g\ast\chi_{\tilde{T}_{2}}(x).

One can easily check that
g\in L^{2}(\delta_{p^{-(d+1)}mL^{\prime}D_{L_{1}}})
and
G\in L^{2}(\mu_{\rho,D})
. An analogous calculation yields that

\langle G,e_{\lambda}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{\rho,D})}=0
for any
e_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}
. Since
G\not\equiv 0
, we obtain a contradiction, which implies that
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
.
∎

3.2
The sufficiency.

In this subsection, we prove the sufficiency of Theorem
1.2
.
Under the assumption that
\rho^{-1}=p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
with
L
dividing
p
, the numbers
L
,
m
, and
p
admit prime factorizations as given in equations (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), which satisfy (
3.3
) and (
3.4
).
Together with the condition that
N
divides
\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
, this implies the following crucial observation.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose that
L\mid p
,
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
and (
3.1
)-(
3.4
) hold. If
L_{i_{0}}\mid N
for some
1\leq i_{0}\leq\kappa
, then
d_{i_{0}}=d=\max\{d_{i}:1\leq i\leq\kappa\}
.

Proof.

Since
L\mid p
, it is clear that
l_{i}\geq\alpha_{i}\geq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq\kappa
. Suppose that there exists some
1\leq i_{0}\leq\kappa
such that
L_{i_{0}}\mid N
and
d_{i_{0}}<d
.
It follows from (
3.3
) and
0\leq r_{i_{0}}<l_{i_{0}}
that

\tau_{i_{0}}\leq\tau_{i_{0}}+\alpha_{i_{0}}-1=d_{i_{0}}l_{i_{0}}+r_{i_{0}}<dl_{i_{0}},

which implies that

\gcd\left(L_{{i_{0}}},\frac{m}{\gcd\left(m,p^{d}\right)}\right)=1.

This contradicts the hypotheses that
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
and
L_{i_{0}}\mid N
. Hence, the proposition holds.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mu_{\rho,D}
be a self-similar measure defined by (
1.1
), where
0<\rho<1
and
D
is given by (
1.4
). Suppose that
\rho^{-1}=p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
with
N,L\mid p
and
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
, where
d\in\mathbb{N}
is defined as (
3.4
). Then
\mu_{\rho,D}
is a spectral measure.

Proof.

Recall the factorizations of
L
,
m
and
p
in (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), which satisfy (
3.3
) and (
3.4
).
Consider the factorization
N=L_{1}^{s_{1}}L_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{s_{\kappa}}N^{\prime}
, where
s_{i}\geq 0
and
\gcd(L_{i},N^{\prime})=1
for
1\leq i\leq\kappa
. Without loss of generality, after relabeling, we may arrange that
s_{i}=0
for
1\leq i\leq b
and
s_{i}\geq 1
for
b+1\leq i\leq\kappa
. In other words,

N=L_{b+1}^{s_{b+1}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{s_{\kappa}}N^{\prime}.

(3.16)

Write
\tilde{r}_{i}:=\tau_{i}-dl_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq\kappa
. For
1\leq i\leq b
, by
d_{i}\leq d
and (
3.3
), we have

\tilde{r}_{i}\leq r_{i}-\alpha_{i}+1.

(3.17)

For
b+1\leq i\leq\kappa
, it follows from Proposition
3.4
and (
3.3
) that

\tau_{i}+\alpha_{i}-1=d_{i}l_{i}+r_{i}=dl_{i}+\tilde{r}_{i}+\alpha_{i}-1.

Combining with
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
, one may obtain that

s_{i}\leq\tilde{r}_{i}\leq\tilde{r}_{i}+\alpha_{i}-1=r_{i}<l_{i},\quad b+1\leq i\leq\kappa.

(3.18)

Let
\frac{m}{p^{d}}=\frac{\tilde{m}}{\tilde{p}}
with
\gcd(\tilde{m},\tilde{p})=1
. Then, (
3.17
) and (
3.18
), together with
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
, imply that there exists some integer
m_{0}
with
\gcd(m_{0},L)=1
such that

\tilde{m}=L_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}\cdots L_{b}^{\gamma_{b}}L_{b+1}^{\tilde{r}_{b+1}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{\tilde{r}_{\kappa}}N^{\prime}m_{0}=:\bar{L}N^{\prime}m_{0},

where
\gamma_{i}\in\{0,\tilde{r}_{i}\}
for
1\leq i\leq b
. Here,

\bar{L}:=L_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}\cdots L_{b}^{\gamma_{b}}L_{b+1}^{\tilde{r}_{b+1}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{\tilde{r}_{\kappa}}

satisfies
\bar{L}L\mid p
since
\gamma_{i}+\alpha_{i}\leq l_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq b
and
\tilde{r}_{i}+\alpha_{i}\leq l_{i}
for
b+1\leq i\leq\kappa
. Moreover,
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
also implies that
\gcd(\tilde{p},N)=1
.

By Theorem
2.7
, it suffices to show that
\mu_{p^{-1},\tilde{p}D}
is a spectral measure. In the following, we construct a spectrum for
\mu_{p^{-1},\tilde{p}D}
. In the case
d\geq 1
, observe that
\mu_{p^{-1},\tilde{p}D}
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\mu_{p^{-1},\tilde{p}D}

\displaystyle=\mu_{p^{-1},m\tilde{p}D_{L}}*\mu_{p^{-1},\tilde{p}D_{N}}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\delta_{p^{-1}m\tilde{p}D_{L}}*\cdots*\delta_{p^{-d}m\tilde{p}D_{L}}\Bigr)*\Bigl(*_{j\geq 1}\delta_{p^{-j}(p^{-d}m\tilde{p}D_{L}\oplus\tilde{p}D_{N})}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=:\delta_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}*\mu_{p^{-1},\mathcal{D}_{2}},

where
\mathcal{D}_{1}=p^{-1}m\tilde{p}D_{L}\oplus\cdots\oplus p^{-d}m\tilde{p}D_{L}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}=p^{-d}m\tilde{p}D_{L}\oplus\tilde{p}D_{N}
.
The spectra of
\delta_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
and
\mu_{p^{-1},\mathcal{D}_{2}}
are constructed separately below.

Let

\Lambda_{1}=\left\{\sum^{d-1}_{k=0}\frac{\eta_{k}}{\tilde{m}p^{k}L}\mid\eta_{k}\in\left\{0,1,\cdots,L-1\right\}\right\},

then a direct verification shows that
(\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{1})\setminus\{0\}\subset Z(\hat{\delta}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}})
and
\#\Lambda_{1}=L^{d}
. Hence
\Lambda_{1}
is a spectrum of
\delta_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
.

We then verify that the measure
\mu_{p^{-1},\mathcal{D}_{2}}
admits an integer spectrum. Note that

\mathcal{D}_{2}=\tilde{m}D_{L}\oplus\tilde{p}D_{N}=\bar{L}N^{\prime}m_{0}D_{L}\oplus\tilde{p}D_{N}

with
\gcd(m_{0},L)=\gcd(\tilde{p},N)=1
. Define

\mathcal{L}:=\frac{1}{\bar{L}N^{\prime}}\left\{0,\frac{1}{L},\cdots,\frac{L-1}{L}\right\}\oplus\left\{0,\frac{1}{N},\cdots,\frac{N-1}{N}\right\}.

A simple calculation yields that
(\mathcal{L}-\mathcal{L})\setminus\{0\}\subset Z(m_{\mathcal{D}_{2}})
by using (
3.18
). Moreover, it follows from
N\mid p
,
\bar{L}L\mid p
and
\gcd(N^{\prime},L)=1
that
p\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{Z}
. Thus,
(p,\mathcal{D}_{2})
is an admissible pair by Lemma
2.4
, and consequently
\mu_{p^{-1},\mathcal{D}_{2}}
is a spectral measure
[
9
]
. Since

\gcd(\tilde{p},\tilde{m})=1
,
we have
\gcd(\mathcal{D}_{2}-\mathcal{D}_{2})=1
, which shows that
\mathcal{Z}(\mu_{p^{-1},\mathcal{D}_{2}})=\emptyset
by Proposition
2.5
. Therefore, Theorem
2.6
guarantees that

\mu_{p^{-1},\mathcal{D}_{2}}
is a spectral measure possessing a spectrum
\Lambda_{2}\subset\mathbb{Z}
.

Notice that
\hat{\delta}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
is integer-periodic since

\mathcal{D}_{1}=\tilde{m}(D_{L}\oplus pD_{L}\oplus\cdots\oplus p^{d-1}D_{L})
is an integer digit set. Now we set

\Lambda=\Lambda_{1}\oplus\Lambda_{2}.

Recall that
\mu_{p^{-1},\tilde{p}D}=\delta_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}\ast\mu_{p^{-1},\mathcal{D}_{2}}
. Then it follows that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

Q_{\Lambda}(t)=\sum_{\eta\in\Lambda}|\hat{\mu}_{p^{-1},\tilde{p}D}(t+\eta)|^{2}=\sum_{\eta\in\Lambda}|\hat{\delta}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}(t+\eta)|^{2}\cdot|\hat{\mu}_{p^{-1},\mathcal{D}_{2}}(t+\eta)|^{2}.

Combining
\Lambda_{2}\subset\mathbb{Z}
, the integral periodicity of
\hat{\delta}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
and Theorem
2.1
, one may obtain that

\displaystyle Q_{\Lambda}(t)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda_{1}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{2}}|\hat{\delta}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}(t+\gamma+\lambda)|^{2}\cdot|\hat{\mu}_{p^{-1},\mathcal{D}_{2}}(t+\gamma+\lambda)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda_{1}}|\hat{\delta}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}(t+\gamma)|^{2}\cdot\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{2}}|\hat{\mu}_{p^{-1},\mathcal{D}_{2}}(t+\gamma+\lambda)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda_{1}}|\hat{\delta}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}(t+\gamma)|^{2}\equiv 1

holds for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Applying Theorem
2.1
again, we conclude that

\Lambda
is a spectrum of
\mu_{p^{-1},\tilde{p}D}
.
In the remaining case
d=0
,
\Lambda_{2}
is a spectrum of
\mu_{p^{-1},\tilde{p}D}
.
The proof is completed.

∎

Remark 3.1
.

In the end of this section, we remark that the conditions
N\mid p
,
L\mid p
and
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
imply
NL\mid p
. In fact, using the factorizations of
L,m,p
and
N
in (
3.1
)-(
3.4
) and (
3.16
), the conditions
N\mid p
and
L\mid p
tell us that
L_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots L_{b}^{\alpha_{b}}N^{\prime}\mid p
.
Moreover, (
3.18
) yields
s_{i}+\alpha_{i}\leq l_{i}
for all
b+1\leq i\leq\kappa
, which ensures
L_{b+1}^{\alpha_{b+1}+s_{b+1}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{\alpha_{\kappa}+s_{\kappa}}\mid p
. Hence we have
NL\mid p
.

4
Spectrality of self-similar tiles

In Section
3
, we have obtained a complete characterization of the spectrality for self-similar measures
\mu_{\rho,D}
associated with product-form digit sets
D
defined by (
1.4
). By Remark
3.1
, we know that
p=\rho^{-1}\geq\#D
if
\mu_{\rho,D}
is a spectral measure.
In this section, we turn to the tiling properties of the associated self-similar sets
T(\rho,D)
, with the focus on the case

\rho^{-1}=\#D=NL

and on digit sets of the form
D=D_{N}\oplus mD_{L}
, where
N,m,L\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
with
N,L\geq 2
. For our study, we recall a fundamental tiling criterion for self-similar tiles. This characterization was first established in one dimension by Kenyon
[
22
]
and later extended to higher dimensions by Lagarias and Wang
[
26
]
.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
26
]
)
.

For an expanding matrix
R\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and a finite digit set
B\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with
|\det(R)|=\#B
, the following three statements are equivalent:

(i)

T(R,B)
has positive Lebesgue measure;

(ii)

For each
k\geq 1
, the set
\sum^{k-1}_{i=0}R^{i}B
contains
(\#B)^{k}
distinct elements;

(iii)

For each
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\backslash\{\bf{0}\}
, there exists an integer
k\geq 1
such that
m_{B}((R^{\ast})^{-k}\nu)=0.

For convenience, we summarize the decomposition process for
L
,
m
,
N
and
p=\rho^{-1}
below.
Let
L=L_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}L_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{\alpha_{\kappa}}
with
\alpha_{i}\geq 1
for
1\leq i\leq\kappa
be the prime factorization. Write

N=L_{b+1}^{s_{b+1}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{s_{\kappa}}N^{\prime}\quad{\rm{and}}\quad m=L_{1}^{\tau_{1}}L_{2}^{\tau_{2}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{\tau_{\kappa}}m^{\prime}

(4.1)

for some
0\leq b<\kappa
, where
s_{i}\geq 1
,
\tau_{i}\geq 0
and
\gcd(L_{i},N^{\prime})=\gcd(L_{i},m^{\prime})=1
for each
i
. The condition
p=LN
yields the factorization

p=L_{1}^{l_{1}}L_{2}^{l_{2}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{l_{\kappa}}N^{\prime}

(4.2)

with exponents satisfying

l_{i}=\alpha_{i}~~~~(1\leq i\leq b)\quad{\rm{and}}\quad l_{i}=\alpha_{i}+s_{i}~~~~(b+1\leq i\leq\kappa).

(4.3)

For the sake of clarity, we separate the proof of Theorem
1.5
into two propositions (
4.2
and
4.3
), which collectively establish the theorem.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
T(\rho,D)
be a self-similar set defined by (
1.1
), where
\rho^{-1}=NL
and
D
is given by (
1.4
).
If
T(\rho,D)
is a translation tile, then
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,(NL)^{d})}
with
d
given by (
3.4
) and
T(\rho,D)
is a spectral set.

Proof.

Suppose that
T(\rho,D)
is a translation tile. By Theorem
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\subset Z(\hat{\mu}_{\rho,D})=\bigcup^{\infty}_{k=1}(NL)^{k}Z(m_{D}).

(4.4)

Notice that

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}\cap Z(\hat{\mu}_{\rho,D})=

\displaystyle~\mathbb{Z}\cap\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=1}(NL)^{n}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus N\mathbb{Z}}{N}\cup\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus L\mathbb{Z}}{Lm}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\mathbb{Z}\cap\left\{\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=1}(NL)^{n}\frac{(L\mathbb{Z}\setminus NL\mathbb{Z})}{NL}\right)\cup\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=1}(NL)^{n}\frac{(\mathbb{Z}\setminus L\mathbb{Z})}{Lm}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\left\{\mathbb{Z}\cap\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=0}(NL)^{n}(L\mathbb{Z}\setminus NL\mathbb{Z})\right)\right\}\cup\left\{\mathbb{Z}\cap\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=1}(NL)^{n}\frac{(\mathbb{Z}\setminus L\mathbb{Z})}{Lm}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle~Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}.

By (
3.1
)-(
3.4
) and (
4.1
)-(
4.3
)，
without loss of generality, we assume that
d=d_{\kappa}
. Combining (
3.3
) and
l_{\kappa}=\alpha_{\kappa}+s_{\kappa}
, we have

\displaystyle\tau_{\kappa}+\alpha_{\kappa}-1=d(\alpha_{\kappa}+s_{\kappa})+r_{\kappa}\quad(0\leq r_{\kappa}<\alpha_{\kappa}+s_{\kappa}).

Let
\sigma=\gcd(m,(NL)^{d})
. Then we can write
m=\sigma\tilde{m}
and
(NL)^{d}=\sigma\tilde{p}
for some integers
\tilde{m}
and
\tilde{p}
.
Note that

d(\alpha_{\kappa}+s_{\kappa})-\tau_{\kappa}=\alpha_{\kappa}-1-r_{\kappa}\leq\alpha_{\kappa}-1.

It follows that
L_{\kappa}^{\alpha_{\kappa}}\nmid\tilde{p}
and consequently
\tilde{p}\notin Z_{1}
. By (
4.4
), we then have
\tilde{p}\in Z_{2}
; that is, there exist an integer
n_{0}\geq 1
and some
a\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus L\mathbb{Z}
such that

(NL)^{n_{0}}\frac{a}{mL}=\tilde{p}
, hence

(NL)^{n_{0}}a=mL\tilde{p}=(NL)^{d}L\tilde{m}.

Since
a\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus L\mathbb{Z}
, the above equation shows that
n_{0}\geq d+1
. Hence,
N\mid\tilde{m}
, i.e.,

N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,(NL)^{d})}
and
T(\rho,D)
is a spectral set by Theorem
1.2
. This complete the proof.
∎

Using Theorem
1.2
, we can also obtain the converse that spectrality implies translational tiling for the specific class of self-similar sets under consideration.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
T(\rho,D)
be a self-similar set defined by (
1.1
), where
\rho^{-1}=NL
and
D
is given by (
1.4
). If
T(\rho,D)
is a spectral set, then
T(\rho,D)
is a translation tile.

Proof.

Write
p=\rho^{-1}=NL
. By Theorem
1.2
,
T(\rho,D)
is a spectral set implies that
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
with
d
given by (
3.4
). By using Theorem
2.7
, it suffices to show that
T(p^{-1},\tilde{p}D)
is a translation tile if
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
, where

\tilde{p}:=L_{1}^{d\alpha_{1}-\tau_{1}}L_{2}^{d\alpha_{2}-\tau_{2}}\cdots L_{b}^{d\alpha_{b}-\tau_{b}}.

Binding together (i) and (iii) in Theorem
4.1
, we need to show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\subset Z(\hat{\mu}_{\rho,\tilde{p}D})=\bigcup^{\infty}_{k=1}p^{k}Z(m_{\tilde{p}D}).

(4.5)

Under the assumption that
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
, Proposition
3.4
gives
d_{i}=d
for all
b+1\leq i\leq\kappa
. We further establish the following results:

\displaystyle\tau_{i}-d\alpha_{i}\leq 0~~~~(1\leq i\leq b)\quad{\rm{and}}\quad\tau_{i}-dl_{i}=s_{i}~~~~(b+1\leq i\leq\kappa).

(4.6)

For
1\leq i\leq b
, by using
l_{i}=\alpha_{i}
and
d_{i}\leq d
, it follows that

\tau_{i}+\alpha_{i}-1=d_{i}l_{i}+r_{i}\leq d\alpha_{i}+r_{i},\quad 0\leq r_{i}<\alpha_{i},

and we thus have

\tau_{i}-d\alpha_{i}\leq r_{i}-(\alpha_{i}-1)\leq 0
.
For
b+1\leq i\leq\kappa
, the assumption
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,p^{d})}
together with
d_{i}=d
gives

\tau_{i}-dl_{i}=r_{i}-(\alpha_{i}-1)\geq s_{i},

i.e.,

r_{i}+1\geq\alpha_{i}+s_{i}=l_{i}
. Combining with
0\leq r_{i}<l_{i}
, we must have
r_{i}+1=l_{i}
. This implies that

\tau_{i}-dl_{i}=r_{i}-(\alpha_{i}-1)=l_{i}-\alpha_{i}=s_{i}
. We complete the proof of (
4.6
).

Recall that
m=L_{1}^{\tau_{1}}L_{2}^{\tau_{2}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{\tau_{\kappa}}m^{\prime}
and
N=L_{b+1}^{s_{b+1}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{s_{\kappa}}N^{\prime}
. Let
m^{\prime}=N^{\prime t}m^{\prime\prime}
with
N^{\prime}\nmid m^{\prime\prime}
for some
t\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, then it follows from
N\mid\frac{m}{\gcd(m,(NL)^{d})}
and
\gcd(L,N^{\prime})=1
that
t-d\geq 1
. Applying the results in (
4.6
) gives that

\displaystyle\frac{p^{d}}{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{1}^{d\alpha_{1}-\tau_{1}}\cdots L_{b}^{d\alpha_{b}-\tau_{b}}\,N^{\prime d}}{L_{b+1}^{\tau_{b+1}-dl_{b+1}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{\tau_{\kappa}-dl_{\kappa}}\,m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{p}}{L_{b+1}^{s_{b+1}}\cdots L_{\kappa}^{s_{\kappa}}\,N^{\prime\,t-d}m^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{p}}{N\,N^{\prime\,t-d-1}m^{\prime\prime}},

where
\gcd(N^{\prime t-d-1}m^{\prime\prime},L)=1
. Combining with
\gcd(\tilde{p},N)=1
, a direct calculation yields that

\displaystyle Z(\hat{\mu}_{\rho,\tilde{p}D})=

\displaystyle\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=1}p^{n}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus N\mathbb{Z}}{\tilde{p}N}\cup\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus L\mathbb{Z}}{\tilde{p}Lm}\right)

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=1}p^{n}\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus N\mathbb{Z}}{N}\right)\cup\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=d+1}p^{n}\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus L\mathbb{Z}}{\tilde{p}Lm}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=1}p^{n}\frac{L\mathbb{Z}\setminus LN\mathbb{Z}}{LN}\right)\cup\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=1}p^{n}\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus L\mathbb{Z}}{LNN^{\prime t-d-1}m^{\prime\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=1}p^{n}\frac{L\mathbb{Z}\setminus LN\mathbb{Z}}{LN}\right)\cup\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=1}p^{n}\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus L\mathbb{Z}}{LN}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=1}p^{n}\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus p\mathbb{Z}}{p}=\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}.

Consequently, (
4.5
) holds, and hence
T(p^{-1},\tilde{p}D)
is a translation tile.
∎

Combining the Propositions
4.2
and
4.3
leads to the equivalence between spectrality and translational tiling for the self-similar set
T(\rho,D)
. Consequently, the Fuglede’s conjecture holds for these one-dimensional product-form like sets, as stated in Theorem
1.5
.
