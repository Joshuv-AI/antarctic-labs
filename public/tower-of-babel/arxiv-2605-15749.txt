Constraints on primordial black holes from the first part of LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA fourth observing run
M. Andrés-Carcasona, A.J. Iovino, E. Vallejo-Pagès, V. Vaskonen, H. Veermäe, M. Martínez, Ll. M. Mir

Abstract
We analyze PBH populations using state-of-the-art modeling of PBH binaries, deriving the strongest bounds on PBH abundance in the 0.6 ​ – ​ 100 ​ M ⊙ 0.6–100M_{\odot} range from LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA O4a data and demonstrating sensitivity in the 10 − 4 ​ – ​ 10 4 ​ M ⊙ 10^{-4}–10^{4}M_{\odot} range. The constraints are dominated by resolvable PBH mergers, while the associated gravitational wave background provides complementary but weaker limits. Allowing PBHs to account for a subset of the cataloged events slightly relaxes these bounds. However, a joint fit with astrophysical black holes shows no compelling evidence for a PBH contribution.



Introduction –
The first part of the fourth LIGO-Virgo-Kagra (LVK) observing run (O4a)
40
unveiled 85 new gravitational wave (GW) signals from compact binaries with a false alarm rate of less than one per year
59
. Combined with the results of the first three observation runs (O1-O3)
9
;
13
;
20
, LVK has detected a total of 161 compact binaries in the O1-O4a integrated observation period. Population studies of this catalog have revealed interesting features in the black hole (BH) mass distribution, including evidence for multiple peaks above a power-law-like continuum
58
.

These findings challenge astrophysical scenarios of BH binary formation. Multiple channels, such as isolated binary evolution and dynamical assembly in dense stellar environments
69
;
101
, have been proposed, each contributing differently to the astrophysical black hole (ABH) binary population and imprinting distinct mass, spin, and redshift demographics. Accurate modeling of these formation channels requires detailed treatments of stellar evolution, supernova physics, binary interactions, and cluster dynamics, leading to substantial uncertainties in theoretical predictions. Therefore, the current approach in population studies is to parametrize the binary population.

In this paper, we consider the possibility that some features of the observed binary BH population may not be fully explained by the ABH channels but instead originate from a population of primordial BHs (PBHs). Such objects, hypothesized to have formed in the early Universe from the collapse of large density fluctuations
42
, would constitute a fundamentally different BH population. If present, PBHs could imprint characteristic signatures on the merger rate, mass spectrum, spin distribution, and redshift evolution of binary BHs, potentially accounting for features that are difficult to reconcile with ABH scenarios alone.

Previous analyzes of the LVK catalog have not identified statistically significant evidence for a PBH component in the observed binary population
61
;
60
;
100
;
55
;
48
;
28
. Instead, the absence of a clear PBH signal has been translated into stringent upper limits on the abundance of PBHs in the solar mass range
61
;
28
, typically expressed as the fraction of dark matter (DM) that they can constitute.

In our earlier work
28
, we derived constraints on the abundance of PBHs using the LVK catalog up to the third observing run. Beyond adding the new data from O4a, the present analysis extends the scope of
28
in several ways: (i) by introducing a new way of estimating the model agnostic constraint, (ii) by incorporating contributions from galactic binaries
84
, and (iii) by deriving the constraints implied by the non-observation of a stochastic GW background (SGWB)

;
86
;

;

;

;

Methods –

The GW signatures of BH binaries stem from their merger rate. For the most common PBH scenarios, the merger rate can be estimated analytically if the PBH mass function is not too wide and the PBHs are not initially formed in clusters.
1
1

1

These assumptions are relatively mild, as strong initial clustering is difficult to achieve in critical collapse scenarios
46
, and the PBH constraints tend to prefer narrow mass functions
43
;
41
.

We consider the early two- and three-body channels for PBH binary formation, as summarized in
87
;
1
. We omit PBH binary formation in the late universe, which gives a subdominant contribution to the merger rate.

The shape of the mass function of the PBH population depends on the model of PBH formation and, e.g., in critical collapse scenarios, can possess additional features due to the softening of the equation of state during the QCD phase transition, which enhances the PBH abundance around a solar mass
62
;
39
;
56
;
51
;
80
;
53
. However, as shown in
28
, the constraints on the fraction of DM in PBHs,
f_{\rm PBH}\equiv\Omega_{\rm PBH}/\Omega_{\rm DM}
, are primarily dependent on the average PBH mass
\langle m\rangle
. To account for variations in the width of the mass function, we consider log-normal mass functions

\psi\propto\exp\left[-\frac{\ln^{2}(m/m_{c})}{2\sigma^{2}}\right]\,,

(1)

where
m_{c}
and
\sigma^{2}
denote the mode and the width of the distribution. It is a good approximation of wider peaks in the mass spectrum and is predicted by several PBH formation mechanisms
41
. We vary the mode
m_{c}
across the LVK-sensitive range (sub-solar to stellar masses), where the means of these distributions are given by
\langle m\rangle=m_{c}e^{-\sigma^{2}/2}
. The limiting case
\sigma\to 0
gives the monochromatic mass function,
\psi\propto\delta(m-\langle m\rangle)
.

Although there has been some speculation about the potential primordial origin of some more extreme GW events

47

Deciding whether an event stems from astrophysics is important not only for the identification of candidate PBHs, but also for imposing reliable constraints on PBHs. The constraints inevitably get weaker if any of the observed GW events might have a primordial origin. In the following, when deriving constraints on the abundance of PBHs, the modeling of the ABH population is approached in three different ways:

1.

In the
astrophysics only
case, all the observed events originate from ABH binaries and no PBH are assumed to have been observed. In this case, the constraint on
f_{\rm PBH}
is derived from the non-observation of any excess of events. The 95% CL upper bound on
f_{\rm PBH}
corresponds to the region where
N(\Lambda)<3
.

2.

In the
astrophysically agnostic
case, any of the observed events could be primordial. We therefore set limits without modeling the ABH population, using two complementary procedures: (i) taking random subsets of the catalog and assuming that they are PBHs, as done in Refs.
61
;
28

1

3.

In the
astrophysically informed
case, we model all observed events from a merger rate that combines contributions from both PBHs and ABHs. For BH binaries formed through astrophysical channels, a reliable estimate of the merger rate based on first principles is missing, which is why we adopt the phenomenological power-law + 2 peaks (PL+2P) parameterization

;
1
;
4
;
14

Results –

The constraints from the non-observation of resolvable PBH binaries in the astrophysics only case and from the non-observation of a SGWB are shown in Fig.
1
by the solid lines for the monochromatic and log-normal mass functions. The bounds on
f_{\rm PBH}
from the non-observation of resolvable PBH binaries exceed those from the non-observation of a SGWB from unresolvable PBH binaries by about three orders of magnitude. The O4a data slightly improves the O3 constraint from resolvable binaries (shown in black for the monochromatic case) providing the tightest constraints in the mass region
0.5-90~M_{\odot}
, for which the upper bounds in
f_{\rm PBH}
vary between
10^{-2}
and
10^{-4}
, respectively. For broader mass functions, the constraint extends to higher masses and becomes weaker around
\langle m\rangle\sim 100\,M_{\odot}
. Qualitatively, this result aligns with the general trend in the PBH constraints for extended mass functions, as reported in
43
;
41
. However, the method of
43
cannot be applied directly here, as PBHs of different masses do not contribute independently.

Although our analysis of galactic subsolar mass binaries does not yield a new constraint on the PBH abundance, it allows us to identify a sensitivity region where a detection would have been expected given the current LVK sensitivity.
Indeed, under our assumptions, observing PBH binaries would be consistent at 95% CL with PBH models when
f_{\rm PBH}\gtrsim 0.1
around the mass range
\langle m\rangle\in(10^{-4}M_{\odot},10^{-3}M_{\odot})
. This sensitivity floor is shown by the dashed lines in Fig.
1
, and the region above it corresponds to
f_{\rm PBH}
values for which the expected number of observed events is
N(\Lambda)>0.05
. For galactic binaries, this region of the parameter space is, however, excluded by EROS and OGLE microlensing bounds.

Allowing for the possibility that a subset of the catalog is primordial, Fig.
2
compares two agnostic procedures. The procedure based on random subsets, constructed with
10^{5}
combinations, yields conservative limits by construction, and the constraint converges as more combinations are sampled
1
. On the other hand, the subset–marginalized likelihood
1
produces comparable constraints while smoothly down–weighting events that are disfavored by the PBH model. Both methods show that when some of the events are allowed to be primordial, the constraints relax in the region
2~M_{\odot}<\langle m\rangle<20~M_{\odot}
. Increasing the width
\sigma
does not qualitatively alter the behavior, but it relaxes the constraints on the PBH abundance. For a
\sigma=1.5
, these lower to
f_{\rm PBH}\sim 10^{-1}
, while for
\sigma=1.0
reduce to
f_{\rm PBH}\sim 10^{-2}
and for
\sigma=0.6
to
f_{\rm PBH}\sim 10^{-3}
. Since these agnostic constraints themselves do not rely on any ABH rate model, they provide robust, model-independent bounds on
f_{\rm PBH}
. While wider mass functions seem to allow for a larger fraction of PBHs, these models are ruled out by other experiments, such as CMB constraints

;
83
.

When we model the ABH population with the PL+2P parameterization and add a PBH component with a log–normal mass function with
\sigma=0.6

m\gtrsim 7~M_{\odot}

1

\ln\mathcal{B}\simeq 30.5
, indicating very strong evidence towards the PBH+ABH hypothesis under the models being employed. Upon closer inspection of the individual likelihoods of the events

\approx 7~M_{\odot}
.
To avoid overinterpreting this phenomenological flexibility, we fix
m_{\min}=5\,M_{\odot}
and omit GW200210_092254, whose lighter component might be a neutron star, in our fiducial inference. By only changing this, the Bayes factor no longer provides evidence for a PBH contribution, yielding a log-Bayes factor of
\ln\mathcal{B}\simeq 1.7

f_{\rm PBH}
as a function of
\langle m\rangle
. As seen from Fig.

Moreover, the mixed ABH+PBH inferences are highly sensitive to seemingly modest analysis choices. This behavior reflects the limited identifiability between a flexible parametrization of the ABH population and a subdominant PBH population. Consequently, apparent evidence for PBHs can be prior- or selection-driven rather than data-driven. We therefore treat the agnostic bounds as the most robust baseline.

Comparison with previous analyzes –

Recently, Ref.
8
derived an upper limit of
f_{\rm PBH}\lesssim\mathcal{O}(0.1)
in the
0.4-1\,M_{\odot}
mass interval for monochromatic PBH mass functions. These bounds are weaker by approximately an order of magnitude than those presented in this work. A direct comparison is shown in Fig.
3
. The discrepancy can be mostly attributed to differences adopted in the underlying PBH binary formation model: the analysis of Ref.
8
employed a simplified suppression factor for the two-body formation channel and did not include the three-body channel, which is expected to dominate for abundances
f_{\rm PBH}\gtrsim 0.04
, where the two-body channel becomes strongly suppressed. As shown in Fig.
3
, these effects account for most of the difference between the results. The remaining discrepancy is relatively minor and likely arises from differences in the analysis methodology. The analysis in Ref.
8
estimated the sensitive hypervolume using injection campaigns and false-alarm-rate-based detection criteria, whereas our analysis relies on representative detector noise curves and a fixed SNR threshold. However, as seen in Fig.
3
, the comparison between the gray dashed and black solid curves leads to only an
\mathcal{O}(10\%)
difference in the resulting constraint on
f_{\rm PBH}
.

The bounds from SGWBs produced by PBH binaries in Ref.

Finally, searches for planetary-mass binaries of Ref.

Conclusions –

Using LVK data up to the O4a run, we have derived updated constraints on the PBH abundance from both the observed population of binary mergers and the non-observation of a SGWB. The limits from individually resolvable binaries provide the most stringent constraints to date for monochromatic mass functions in the range
0.6~M_{\odot}\lesssim\langle m\rangle\lesssim 100~M_{\odot}
. For broader mass functions, the constraints weaken slightly near
\langle m\rangle\lesssim 1~M_{\odot}
and
\sim 100~M_{\odot}
, but extend to cover masses up to
\langle m\rangle\sim 10^{3}~M_{\odot}
The SGWB non-detection yields roughly three orders of magnitude weaker bounds that are more sensitive to the mass function width than the resolvable binary limits.

We have also explored a possible population of galactic subsolar binaries, finding that the current LVK sensitivity does not yet constrain such systems. Nevertheless, we have identified a sensitivity floor in the mass range
10^{-4}~M_{\odot}\lesssim\langle m\rangle\lesssim 10^{-3}~M_{\odot}
, where LVK could probe PBH fractions down to
f_{\rm PBH}\approx 0.2
. Overall, we found that LVK can probe PBH scenarios in the mass range
10^{-4}-10^{4}M_{\odot}
.

To derive PBH limits that are agnostic about the ABH population, we have introduced two independent, data-driven methods based on the observed binary sample. These methods yield consistent results, modestly relaxing the constraints in the mass range
2~M_{\odot}<\langle m\rangle<20~M_{\odot}
while providing robust, ABH model independent bounds.

Finally, we have performed joint fits to the ABH and PBH populations and found that the inferred PBH contribution depends sensitively on the choices made in the analysis. Allowing the ABH low mass cutoff to vary freely and retaining all events can spuriously favor a PBH component (
\ln B\simeq 30
), while fixing
m_{\min}=5~M_{\odot}
and excluding the low mass gap outlier removes any significant preference (
\ln B\simeq 1.7
). Overall, the LVK data show no compelling evidence for a subdominant PBH population. Our bounds are robust to astrophysical-population assumptions and represent the most stringent constraints to date.

II
Agnostic treatment of ABHs

The most conservative constraints on the PBH abundance are obtained without restricting which subset of the observed events is of primordial origin. Such constraints can be derived by systematically selecting different subsets of the observed events and fitting each subset with a PBH population model. The resulting upper bound on
f_{\rm PBH}
is then taken from the subset that yields the weakest (i.e., least stringent) constraint within the class of models considered. In this way, the inferred constraint is completely independent of any modeling of the remaining, non-primordial events, which are typically described using a phenomenological, astrophysically motivated parametrization.

However, since the number of possible subsets that can be constructed from a given set of events increases exponentially with the total number of events, performing dedicated PBH fits for all such subsets is computationally intractable. Consequently, it is necessary to employ an optimized or approximate strategy to explore the space of subsets. A specific approach to this optimization will be presented below.

As a starting point, we will first consider a decomposition of the likelihood (
2
) in case there are two distinct channels of BH binary formation. We will denote these channels A and B, respectively (e.g., A=PBH and B=ABH). A decomposition of the merger rate
{\rm d}\mathcal{R}={\rm d}\mathcal{R}_{A}+{\rm d}\mathcal{R}_{B}
implies that the hyperprior can be split as

\pi(\theta|\Lambda)=\pi_{A}(\theta|\Lambda_{A})+\pi_{B}(\theta|\Lambda_{B})\,,

(S6)

where
\Lambda=\Lambda_{A}\cup\Lambda_{B}
. The decomposition of everything else then follows from Eq. (
S6
): we also have
N(\Lambda)=N_{A}(\Lambda_{A})+N_{B}(\Lambda_{B})
and the likelihood (
2
) can be recast as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\{d\}|\Lambda)

\displaystyle\propto e^{-N_{A}(\Lambda)-N_{B}(\Lambda)}\prod_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{obs}}}(\ell_{i,A}+\ell_{i,B})

(S7)

\displaystyle=\sum_{S_{A}\subset S}\mathcal{L}(\{d\}_{S_{A}}|\Lambda_{A})\mathcal{L}(\{d\}_{S\setminus S_{A}}|\Lambda_{B})\,,

where the sum runs over all subset
S_{A}
of the set of events
S
and we defined the per-event marginal likelihood in the model
M\in\{A,B\}

\ell_{i,M}=\int\mathcal{L}(d_{i}|\theta)\pi_{M}(\theta|\Lambda){\rm d}\theta\,.

(S8)

and we defined the channel
M
likelihood for a subset of
S_{M}\subset S
events
\{d\}_{S_{M}}\equiv\{d_{i}:i\in S_{M}\}
as

\mathcal{L}(\{d\}_{S_{M}}|\Lambda_{M})\equiv e^{-N_{M}(\Lambda)}\prod_{i\in S_{M}}l_{i,M}\,.

(S9)

The reformulation in Eq. (
S7
) makes it explicit how the likelihood distributes all events between models
A
and
B
: it considers all possible ways for dividing the events between different channels. Since testing all of them is computationally unfeasible we tackle this problem with two approaches.

The first is to test only some of the subsets chosen purely at random. This method has already been used in Refs.
61
;
28
to quote agnostic limits to the PBH population. The more combinations are tested, the more likely it is to add one that is less constraining than the rest at a given mass-region. For a sufficiently large number of subsamples tested, the result reaches a plateau, and we can consider that to be a reasonable approximation of the actual agnostic limits. Fig.
S1
, shows the constraint obtained for different values of the subsamples,
N
, added. Since this procedure is cumulative, the results for a larger subsample can only be equal or less tightening than the previous ones. This implies that this method produces conservative limits.

Figure S1:

, for an increasing number of combinations.

The second method is to slightly modify the likelihood so that some events are automatically discarded. The modified log-likelihood reads as

\ln\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}=-N(\Lambda)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{obs}}}\ln\left(\alpha+\ell_{i}(\Lambda)\right)\,.

(S10)

This modified version of the likelihood presented in Eq. (
2
) allows us to set a threshold that accounts for some events only, governed by the value of
\alpha
. In the limiting case where
\alpha\to 0
, the original likelihood is recovered, which would correspond to the case where all detected events are considered primordial. On the other hand, if
\alpha\to\infty
, then this term becomes bigger than the likelihood of the event, making the sum over the events simply a constant. Therefore,
\ln\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\propto-N(\Lambda)
which becomes the likelihood in which all the events are considered astrophysical. In the range
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
we can obtain a set of constraints between these two limiting cases. The role of
\alpha
is to introduce a soft floor to the contribution of each event. To see this, note that the score with respect to any hyperparameter component
\Lambda_{j}
is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\ln\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}}{\partial\Lambda_{j}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial N}{\partial\Lambda_{j}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm obs}}\frac{1}{\alpha+\ell_{i}(\Lambda)}\frac{\partial\ell_{i}(\Lambda)}{\partial\Lambda_{j}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial N}{\partial\Lambda_{j}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm obs}}w_{i}(\alpha,\Lambda)\frac{\partial\ln\ell_{i}(\Lambda)}{\partial\Lambda_{j}}\,,

(S11)

where the per–event weight is

w_{i}(\alpha,\Lambda)\equiv\frac{\ell_{i}(\Lambda)}{\alpha+\ell_{i}(\Lambda)}\in[0,1]\,.

(S12)

Thus, events for which the PBH population predicts large
\ell_{i}
(relative to
\alpha
) are effectively kept with a weight
\approx 1
, whereas events with small
\ell_{i}\ll\alpha
are smoothly down–weighted (
w_{i}\approx 0
). Equivalently, this modified likelihood implements a continuous relaxation of a hard selection of the events, with the two limiting cases already discussed. It is useful to define the effective number of PBH–like events

N_{\rm eff}(\alpha,\Lambda)\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm obs}}w_{i}(\alpha,\Lambda)\in[0,N_{\rm obs}]\,,

(S13)

which provides a direct intuition for how much of the catalog the model is using. As
\alpha
increases,
N_{\rm eff}
decreases monotonically from
N_{\rm obs}
to
0
. The construction of this likelihood can be viewed as a robust, ABH–agnostic mixture where each event arises either from the PBH population with marginal
\ell_{i}(\Lambda)
or from any other channel with an unknown per–event marginal likelihood that we approximate by an event–independent scale,
\alpha
.

Although the random subsampling method can be interpreted as a hard gate where an event contributes
\ln\ell_{i}
or nothing, this other likelihood replaces that step function by some smooth weights, yielding a differentiable function with stable optimization and sampling, while preserving the same limiting behaviors. In practice, we observe a similar behavior of constraints because events that are inconsistent with the PBH population are automatically down–weighted, and those well–described by
\Lambda
contribute almost as in the standard hierarchical likelihood.

The connection to the random–subset method is exact under a simple agnostic mixture. Let binary selectors
s_{i}\in\{0,1\}
indicate whether the event
i
is treated as primordial, and suppose that the ones with a non–PBH origin contribute some constant
C>0
at the product level. In this case, the likelihood becomes

\mathcal{L}(\Lambda|s)=e^{-N(\Lambda)}\prod_{i=1}^{N_{\rm obs}}\left[\ell_{i}(\Lambda)\right]^{s_{i}}C^{1-s_{i}}\,.

(S14)

If
s_{i}\sim{\rm Bernoulli}(\pi)
i.i.d., corresponding to
p(s)=\prod_{i}\pi^{s_{i}}(1-\pi)^{1-s_{i}}
, we can marginalize over all subsets to obtain the mixed likelihood as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm mix}(\Lambda\mid\pi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}p(s)\mathcal{L}(\Lambda|s,C)

(S15)

\displaystyle=e^{-N(\Lambda)}\prod_{i=1}^{N_{\rm obs}}\big(\pi\,\ell_{i}(\Lambda)+(1-\pi)\,C\big)\,,

with the sum in the first row running over all possible assignments of
\{0,1\}
for
s_{i}
.
Up to an irrelevant constant, this is identical to
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
after reparametrization

\alpha\;=\;\frac{1-\pi}{\pi}\,C,\qquad w_{i}(\alpha,\Lambda)=\frac{\pi\,\ell_{i}}{\pi\,\ell_{i}+(1-\pi)C}.

(S16)

Hence, the likelihood in Eq.
S10
is the closed form of random subset marginalization for this mixture. We therefore refer to this likelihood as the subset marginalized likelihood (SML) used in the main text.

Under this mixture, the posterior probability that an event is primordial is simply

\mathbb{P}(s_{i}=1|\Lambda,\pi)=\frac{\pi\,\ell_{i}}{\pi\,\ell_{i}+(1-\pi)C}=w_{i}(\alpha,\Lambda)\,,

(S17)

which can be linked to the effective number of PBH events as

N_{\rm eff}(\alpha,\Lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm obs}}w_{i}(\alpha,\Lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm obs}}\mathbb{P}(s_{i}=1|\Lambda,\pi)\,,

(S18)

allowing to interpret
N_{\rm eff}
as the posterior of the expected number of PBHs.

A practical difference arises when one averages log–likelihoods over random subsets. By Jensen’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\ln\mathcal{L}(\Lambda\mid\mathbf{s})\right]\;\leq\;\ln\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathcal{L}(\Lambda\mid\mathbf{s})\right]=\ln\mathcal{L}_{\rm mix}(\Lambda\mid\pi,C),

(S19)

so log–averaging subsets produces a downward bias in
\ln\mathcal{L}
(i.e., more conservative posteriors) relative to the closed form. Regarding the effect on the bounds, consider a PBH parameter
\Lambda_{j}
, from Eq. (
S11
) one sees that introducing
\alpha>0
uniformly attenuates the informative event terms by the
w_{i}\in(0,1)
while leaving the Poisson penalty
-\partial N/\partial\Lambda_{j}
unchanged. Therefore, the posterior mass shifts toward regions favored by the count term, typically yielding higher (more conservative) upper limits on
\Lambda_{j}
unless many events have
\ell_{i}\gg\alpha
.

Bias enters if the mixture is misspecified. If the agnostic contribution is heterogeneous,
\alpha=\alpha_{i}
, but we fit a single
\alpha
, the fitted log–likelihood differs by

\Delta\ell(\Lambda)=\sum_{i}\Big[\ln(\alpha+\ell_{i})-\ln(\alpha_{i}+\ell_{i})\Big].

(S20)

For small
\delta\alpha_{i}=\alpha-\alpha_{i}
,

\Delta\ell(\Lambda)\approx\sum_{i}\frac{\delta\alpha_{i}}{\alpha_{i}+\ell_{i}}-\sum_{i}\frac{\delta\alpha_{i}}{\alpha_{i}+\ell_{i}}\,\frac{\partial\ln\ell_{i}}{\partial\Lambda}^{\!\top}\delta\Lambda,

(S21)

and a standard
M
–estimation expansion gives the leading hyperparameter bias

\displaystyle\delta\Lambda

\displaystyle\approx\mathcal{I}(\Lambda)^{-1}\sum_{i}\frac{\delta\alpha_{i}}{(\alpha_{i}+\ell_{i})^{2}}\,\frac{\partial\ell_{i}}{\partial\Lambda}\,,

(S22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle\equiv-\,\frac{\partial^{2}\ln\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}}{\partial\Lambda\,\partial\Lambda^{\!\top}}\,.

Overestimating
\alpha
for events in regions where
\partial\ell_{i}/\partial\Lambda
points toward larger PBH abundance pushes the estimate toward smaller abundances (weaker
\ell_{i}
), i.e., towards stronger down–weighting of PBH–like regions and consequently yielding weaker limits.
