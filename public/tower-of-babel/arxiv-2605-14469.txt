Geodesic currents of coarse negative curvature
Meenakshy Jyothis, Dídac Martínez-Granado

Abstract
. Strong hyperbolicity is a coarse notion of negative curvature, stronger than Gromov hyperbolicity, that includes all CAT ⁡ ( − k ) \CAT(-k) metrics for k > 0 k>0 and allows the use of dynamical techniques available in negative curvature, such as thermodynamical formalism. We prove that the subset of geodesic currents whose dual pseudometric is strongly hyperbolic is dense in the space of geodesic currents. The proof combines an elementary finite-cover argument with a characterization of strong hyperbolicity in terms of boundary data for pseudometrics dual to geodesic currents. In contrast, we show that currents arising from non-positively curved metrics on the surface are not dense. As a consequence, we construct infinitely many pairwise non-roughly-isometric invariant strongly hyperbolic geodesic metrics on the universal cover of the surface which are not CAT ⁡ ( 0 ) \CAT(0) . Finally, we establish correlation counting results for the associated length spectra.



1.
Introduction

Geodesic currents, introduced by Bonahon, provide a unifying framework for studying surface geometry, dynamics, and topology.
They simultaneously generalize closed geodesics, measured laminations
[
Bon86
,
Bon88
]
, and a wide range of geometric structures on surfaces, including hyperbolic and non-positively curved Riemannian metrics
[
HP97
,
CFF92
,
BL17a
,
Con18
]
.

Throughout this paper, we assume that
S
is a closed orientable surface of genus at least two, and let X denote
S
equipped with a hyperbolic structure. Every geodesic current
\mu
on a surface
X
determines a dual invariant (pseudo-)metric
d_{\mu}
on the universal cover
\widetilde{X}
, introduced in
[
BIPP21a
]
and further developed in
[
RMG23
]
(see also
[
CR25a
]
). These pseudometrics are always Gromov hyperbolic, and their marked length spectra can be recovered via intersection numbers. In fact, when the currents are filling, they induce points in
\mathcal{D}(\Gamma)
, the space of classes of Gromov hyperbolic pseudometrics on
\pi_{1}(S)
quasi-isometric to a word metric (
[
Fur02
,
OR22
,
RMG23
,
CT25
,
CMGR26
]
). This gives a natural embedding from projective filling currents
\PCf(S)
into
\mathcal{D}(\Gamma)
(Proposition
2.17
, see also
[
CR25a
, Proposition 6.12]
).

On the one hand, we show that geodesic currents associated to metrics of sharp negative or non-positive curvature—such as geodesic currents arising from non-positively curved Riemannian or locally
\CAT(0)
metrics on
S
—have strong algebraic constraints in their marked length spectrum and thus fail to be dense in the space of geodesic currents.

On the other hand, we identify a dense subset of geodesic currents whose associated pseudometrics exhibit a coarse form of negative curvature: the class of
strongly hyperbolic
pseudometrics. Introduced in
[
NS16
]
, strong hyperbolicity is a coarse analogue of negative curvature in the following sense:

(1)

it implies Gromov hyperbolicity
[
NS16
, Theorem 4.2]
;

(2)

negatively curved Riemannian metrics, as well as
\CAT(-k)
metrics for
k>0
, are strongly hyperbolic
[
NS16
, Theorem 5.1]
;

(3)

strongly hyperbolic metrics induce Hölder potentials on
T^{1}X

[
CMGR26
, Example 4.12]
, allowing the use of sharp dynamical tools (e.g. thermodynamic formalism), much as in the setting of negatively curved Riemannian metrics.

We exploit the third property above to derive counting results for pairs of strongly hyperbolic currents by applying classical dynamical counting theorems (Theorem
H
).

Finally, combining both the density of the strongly hyperbolic family of currents and the non-density of
\CAT(0)
currents, we construct a dense subset
\mathcal{F}
of geodesic currents whose associated pseudometrics
d_{\mu}
are, in fact,
\pi_{1}(X)
-invariant
geodesic metrics
on
\widetilde{X}
that are strongly hyperbolic but
not

\CAT(0)
(Corollary
5.15
).

1.1.
Density of strongly hyperbolic geodesic currents

Suppose
X
is a closed hyperbolic surface and
let
\pi\colon Y\to X
be a finite-degree cover of
X
. We define the
transfer map
associated to
\pi
and denoted by
\Pi_{Y}:\mathcal{C}(Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(X)
as

\Pi_{Y}(\mu)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(g_{i})_{*}\mu

where
\mu\in\mathcal{C}(Y)
, and
g_{i}
is a transversal of
\pi_{1}(Y)\leq\pi_{1}(X)
.
We define the following subspace of geodesic currents

(1.1)

\mathbb{R}\Teich^{\infty}(S)\coloneqq\left\{\Pi_{Y}\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\mathcal{L}_{Y_{i}}\right):\;k\in\mathbb{N},\;\lambda_{i}\geq 0,\;Y_{i}\in\Teich(Y),Y\to X\text{ finite cover}\right\}.

i.e. the subspace of the image under
\Pi_{Y}
of non-negative linear combinations of hyperbolic Liouville currents on all finite covers of
S
.
We prove the following density result.

Theorem A
.

The subspace of geodesic currents
\mathbb{R}\Teich^{\infty}(X)
is dense in
\mathcal{C}(X)
.

The relevance of
\mathbb{R}^{\infty}\Teich(X)
is that it consists of geodesic currents whose dual pseudometrics are strongly hyperbolic metrics.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, a metric space
X
is said
\varepsilon
-
strongly hyperbolic
if for every points
x,y,z,w\in X
, we have

(1.2)

e^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(d(x,z)+d(y,w))}\leq e^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(d(x,y)+d(z,w))}+e^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(d(x,w)+d(y,z))}.

We say that a geodesic current
\mu
is
strongly hyperbolic
if its dual pseudometric
d_{\mu}
on
\widetilde{X}
is strongly hyperbolic.
While the metric
d_{\mu}
depends on the choice of universal cover, its marked length spectrum does not (see Section
2.5
), and strong hyperbolicity is in fact an invariant of this spectrum (Corollary
3.16
).

Theorem B
.

\mathbb{R}\Teich^{\infty}(S)
consists of strongly hyperbolic geodesic currents.

See Theorem
6.7
for the proofs
A
and
B
. As an immediate corollary of both, we have the following.

Corollary C
.

The subspace of strongly hyperbolic currents is dense in the space of geodesic currents of
S
.

This result can be compared of recent work of Cantrell, Reyes, and the second author
[
CMGR26
, Theorem C]
, which shows that Green metrics associated to admissible random walks—also strongly hyperbolic—are dense in the space
\mathcal{D}(\Gamma)
for any non-elementary hyperbolic group.
By constrast, our Theorem
A
provides a family of metrics approximating any geodesic current entirely
within
the space of geodesic currents. Our result does not follow from
[
CMGR26
, Theorem C]
, since it is not known whether such Green metrics arise from geodesic currentse
[
MGT26
, Question 11.8]
(and, indeed, this is likely false).

The proof of Theorem
A
proceeds by an explicit geometric construction using finite covers.
Given a closed curve
\gamma
on
X
, Scott’s LERF theorem for surface subgroups
[
Sco78a
]
provides a finite cover in which
\gamma
lifts to a union of simple closed geodesics.
We approximate each simple geodesic by weighted Liouville currents, show that such combinations satisfy strong hyperbolicity, and then push forward to the original surface
X
via the transfer map on currents. The above result uses a characterization of strong hyperbolicity in the setting of geodesic currents (see Theorem
3.1
).

Observe that, by Equation
1.2
, if
d
is
\varepsilon
-strongly hyperbolic, then
a\cdot d
is
\varepsilon/a
-strongly hyperbolic for every
a>0
. We will typically work with the representative in the projective class normalized to have entropy 1, i.e.
\hat{d}\coloneqq h(d)d
.

One consequence of Theorem
3.1
is that strong hyperbolicity for geodesic currents can be detected from boundary data. In particular, given a box
B=I\times J\subset G(S)
of bi-infinite geodesics of
\widetilde{X}
(for some bounded intervals
I,J
of
S^{1}
), let
B^{\perp}=I^{c}\times J^{c}
denote the box of geodesics determined by the complementary intervals defining
B
.
Theorem
3.1
implies, in particular, that among geodesic currents with entropy 1 (Eq. (
2.11
)), the
1
-strongly hyperbolic ones are characterized by the inequalities

1\leq e^{-\mu(B)}+e^{-\mu(B^{\perp})}

for every box of geodesics
B\subset G(S)

In fact, the Teichmüller space is exactly the locus where all these inequalities become equalities, by
[
Bon88
, Theorem 13]
.

A qualitative version of strong hyperbolicity, still sharper than Gromov hyerbolicity, is that of
strong bolicity
(see Section
4
). It has played a key technical role in relevant rigidity conjectures relating geometric group theory with harmonic analysis and operator algebras (
[
MY02
]
).

We show that filling geodesic currents whose pseudometric is non-atomic are exactly the strongly bolic ones.

Theorem D
.

Let
\mu
be a filling geodesic current.
Then
\mu
is non-atomic if and only if
d_{\mu}
is strongly bolic.

See Corollary
4.3
for the proof of the above statement. In fact, we prove a slightly stronger result (Theorem
4.2
).

1.2.
\CAT(0)
-geodesic currents are not dense and examples of strongly hyperbolic metrics that are not
\CAT(0)

We next show that the
\CAT(0)
condition imposes strong algebraic constraints on the marked length spectrum, preventing density, and allowing us to construct examples of strongly hyperbolic metrics which are not
\CAT(0)
.

A metric space
X
is called
Ptolemaic
if for all
x,y,z,w\in X
,

d(x,z)d(y,w)\leq d(x,y)d(z,w)+d(x,w)d(y,z).

Examples include lifts of negatively curved Riemannian or locally
\CAT(0)
Riemannian metrics on
S
.

We show that any
\pi_{1}(S)
-invariant Ptolemaic length metric on
\widetilde{S}
satisfies a countable family of homogeneous quadratic inequalities on its marked length spectrum:

Theorem E
.

Let
\rho
be a Ptolemaic metric on
S
which is also a length metric.
For any
a,b\in\pi_{1}(S)
with crossing axes, and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\ell_{\rho}(a^{n})\,\ell_{\rho}(b^{n})\leq\frac{\ell_{\rho}(a^{n}b^{n})^{2}}{4}+\frac{\ell_{\rho}(a^{n}b^{-n})^{2}}{4}.

See Theorems
5.3
and
5.5
below.
These inequalities provide a strong rigidity constraint.
We then show that intersection functionals associated to non-simple curves violate these relations, and deduce:

Corollary F
.

The Liouville currents induced by Ptolemaic metrics on
S
(in particular, locally
\CAT(0)
or negatively curved Riemannian metrics) are not dense in the space of geodesic currents.

See Corollary
5.14
below. This contrasts with
[
Ham99
, Lemma 2.8]
, where density is asserted for currents arising from negatively curved metrics. Corollary
F
shows that the statement is not correct as stated and that the proof contains a gap. See the discussion following Corollary
5.14
for further details. Nevertheless, the statement of
[
Ham99
, Lemma 2.8]
was one of the inspirations for Theorem
A
and Corollary
C
.

Finally, our methods allow us to construct infinitely many
distinct rough isometry classes
of strongly hyperbolic geodesic metrics which are not
\CAT(0)
.

Corollary G
.

There exists a subset
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathbb{R}\Teich^{\infty}(S)
which is dense in the space of geodesic currents such that, for every
\mu\in\mathcal{F}
, the associated
\pi_{1}(S)
-invariant pseudometric
d_{\mu}
on
\widetilde{S}
is a strongly hyperbolic geodesic metric but not Ptolemaic, and hence not
\CAT(0)
.

See Corollary
5.15
below.

1.3.
A correlation counting result

The same techniques as in
[
Sha98
]
and
[
DM23
]
,
ultimately relying on a theorem of Lalley
[
Lal87
]
,
yield a correlation counting theorem for strongly hyperbolic currents.

Given two strongly hyperbolic currents
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
, their
Manhattan curve

\mathcal{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
(Section
7.2.4
) is the boundary of the set

\left\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\sum_{[\gamma]\in[\Gamma]}e^{-a\ell_{\mu_{1}}([\gamma])-b\ell_{\mu_{2}}([\gamma])}<\infty\right\}.

The
renormalized length
spectrum of a geodesic current
\mu
is

L_{\mu}\coloneqq h(\mu)\ell_{\mu},

where
h(\mu)
denotes the topological
entropy
of the geodesic current, i.e., the exponential growth rate of curves with respect to
\ell_{\mu}
(Definition
2.11
).
For any pair of strongly hyperbolic geodesic currents
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
, the Manhattan curve
\mathcal{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is real analytic (
[
CT25
, Theorem 4.16]
and paragraph after its proof) and, whenever
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
have distinct renormalized length spectra, its corresponding abscissa function is a strictly convex curve (
[
CR25b
, Proposition 2.3]
).

Theorem H
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
be strongly hyperbolic currents with distinct renormalized length spectra. Then there exist constants

C=C(\varepsilon,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad M=M(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\in(0,1)

such that

(1.3)

\#\Big\{[\gamma]\in[\Gamma]:L_{\mu_{1}}([\gamma])\in(x,x+h(\mu_{1})\varepsilon),\ L_{\mu_{2}}([\gamma])\in(x,x+h(\mu_{2})\varepsilon)\Big\}\sim C\frac{e^{Mx}}{x^{3/2}}.

Moreover,

(1.4)

M(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\frac{a}{h(\mu_{1})}+\frac{b}{h(\mu_{2})},

where
(a,b)\in\mathcal{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is the unique point at which the tangent line is parallel to the line joining
(h(\mu_{1}),0)
and
(0,h(\mu_{2}))
.

The counting statement is proved in Theorem
7.14
, while the characterization of
M(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
in terms of the Manhattan curve is established in Theorem
7.18
.

As an immediate consequence of Theorems
H
,

B
, and
A
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary I
.

The correlation counting result of Theorem
H

holds for pairs of geodesic currents belonging to a dense subset of the space of geodesic currents.

2.
Background

2.1.
Gromov hyperbolicity

We say
d\colon X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
is a
pseudometric
on
X
if, for every
x,y,z\in X
, it satisfies
d(x,x)=0
,
d(x,y)\geq 0
,
d(x,y)=d(y,x)
and the
triangular inequality

d(x,z)\leq d(x,y)+d(y,z)
, but does not necessarily satisfy the condition that
d(x,y)=0
implies
x=y
.
We say that a pseudometric
d
on
X
is
\delta
-Gromov hyperbolic
if there exist
\delta>0
so that for every four tuple of points
x,y,z,w\in X
,

(2.1)

d(x,z)+d(y,w)\leq\max\{d(x,y)+d(z,w),d(x,w)+d(y,z)\}+2\delta.

This condition can be equivalently rewritten as

(x|y)_{w}\geq\min\{(x|z)_{w},(z|y)_{w}\}-\delta,

where
(x|y)_{w}
denotes the
Gromov product
of
x,y\in X
based at
w\in X
, and is defined as

(x|y)_{w}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left(d(x,w)+d(y,w)-d(x,y)\right).

Sometimes we will simply say
X
is a
hyperbolic space
.
Hyperbolic spaces can be compactified using their ideal
Gromov boundary
\partial X
consisting of equivalence classes of
divergent sequences
. A sequence of elements
(x_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
diverges if
(x_{n}|x_{m})_{o}
for some
o\in X
, as
\min\{n,m\}
diverges to infinity.
Two divergent sequences
(x_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
(y_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
are
equivalent
if
(x_{n}|y_{m})_{o}
diverges as
\min\{n,m\}
, tends to infinity.

The Gromov product extends to points in
X\cup\partial X
, however this extension need not be continuous. The extension is defined as follows. Let

(\xi|\eta)_{o}=\sup\liminf_{n\to\infty}(x_{n}|y_{n})_{o}\colon\xi=[(x_{n})],\eta=[(y_{n})]\},

for
\xi,\eta\in X\cup\partial X
, where if
\xi,\eta
is in
X
, then we take
(x_{n})
as the constant sequence
x_{n}=\xi
for all
n\geq 0
.

We say a (pseudo)-metric space
X
is
\alpha
-roughly geodesic
if there exist a uniform constant
\alpha>0
so that for every two points
x,y\in X
, there exist a finite sequence of points
(x_{i})_{i=0,\cdots,n}
in
X
so that
x_{0}=x
and
x_{n}=y
satisfying

i-j-\alpha\leq d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq i-j+\alpha

for every
i\geq j
.
We say
X
is
roughly geodesic
if it is
\alpha
-roughly geodesic for some
\alpha
.

We say that a finitely generated group
\Gamma
is
Gromov hyperbolic
if some/any of its Cayley graphs with the edge-metric is a Gromov hyperbolic space.
We say a Gromov hyperbolic group is
non-elementary
if
\partial\Gamma
has at least 3 points (in which case it has infinitely many, in fact).

Given a pseudometric
d
in
\Gamma
, we define the
stable length
of
g\in\Gamma
, as follows.

\ell_{d}(g)\coloneqq\lim_{n}\frac{d(o,g^{n})}{n},

where
o
denotes the identity element of
\Gamma
. This is a conjugacy class invariant.

2.2.
Strongly hyperbolicity and bolicity

Following Nica–Špakula
[
NS16
]
, we define the following.

Definition 2.2

(
\varepsilon
-strongly hyperbolic)
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, a metric space
X
is said
\varepsilon
-strongly hyperbolic if for every points
x,y,z,w\in X
, we have

(2.3)

e^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(d(x,z)+d(y,w))}\leq e^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(d(x,y)+d(z,w))}+e^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(d(x,w)+d(y,z))}.

The notion of strong hyperbolicity implies Gromov hyperbolicity (
[
NS16
, Theorem 4.2]
and Proposition
2.5
below).

There is also the following equivalent definition, introduced by Nica–Špakula.

Definition 2.4

(
(A_{0},B_{0},C_{0})
-strongly hyperbolic)
.

Given positive constants
A_{0},B_{0}
and
C_{0}
, a metric space
X
is said
(A_{0},B_{0},C_{0})
-strongly hyperbolic if it is hyperbolic (according to Equation
2.1
) and if for every points
x,y,z,w\in X
, we have that, if

d(x,z)+d(y,w)-d(x,y)-d(z,w)>A\geq A_{0},

then

d(x,z)+d(y,w)-d(x,w)-d(y,z)<B_{0}e^{-C_{0}\cdot A}.

In
[
Nv16
, Lemma 6.2]
, Nica–Špakula prove the following.

Proposition 2.5
.

(1)

If
X
is
\varepsilon
-strongly hyperbolic, then:

(a)

X
is
\frac{\log(2)}{\varepsilon}
-Gromov hyperbolic;

(b)

X
is
(A_{0}(\varepsilon),\,4/\varepsilon,\,\varepsilon/2)
-strongly hyperbolic,
where
A_{0}(\varepsilon)
is given implicitly.

(2)

If
X
is
(A_{0},B_{0},C_{0})
-strongly hyperbolic with Gromov hyperbolicity constant
\delta
, then
X
is
\varepsilon
-strongly hyperbolic, where

\varepsilon\coloneqq\min\left\{\frac{1}{2\delta},\frac{2\log(2)}{A_{0}}\right\}.

Remark 2.6
.

Observe that if
X
is
\varepsilon
-strongly hyperbolic, then it is also
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-strongly hyperbolic for every
\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq\varepsilon
.
It thus makes sense to consider the supremum
\varepsilon^{*}
of the constants
\varepsilon
of
\varepsilon
-strong hyperbolicity for a given metric space
X
. Note that, by the above, if
\delta^{*}
is the infimum of the constants
\delta
of
\delta
-hyperbolicity of
X
(Equation
2.1
), then

\varepsilon^{*}\leq\frac{1}{2\delta^{*}}
.

There is a related notion, weaker than that of strong hyperbolicity, namely,
strong bolicity
, originally defined by Lafforgue
[
Laf02
]
. In the context of roughly geodesic spaces, strong bolicity can be formulated as follows
[
NS16
, p. 960]
; this is the definition we will use.

Definition 2.7
.

We say a that a roughly geodesic space
X
is
strongly bolic
if
X
satisfies

d(x,z)+d(y,w)-d(x,w)-d(y,z)\to 0

as

d(x,z)+d(y,w)-d(x,y)-d(z,w)\to\infty

for every 4-tuple of points
x,y,z,w\in X
.

From this definition, the following is straightforward (c.f.
[
NS16
, Theorem 1.1]
).

Lemma 2.8
.

If
X
is strongly hyperbolic and roughly geodesic, then
X
is strongly bolic.

2.3.
Space of (pseudo-)metrics

For the following, we consider the references
[
Fur02
,
OR22
,
OR23
]
.
For
\Gamma
a non-elementary hyperbolic group, let
D(\Gamma)
denote the space of all Gromov hyperbolic, left-invariant pseudometrics on
\Gamma
that are quasi-isometric (q.i) to a word-metric. We consider pseudometrics up to rough similarity: two pseudometrics
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
on
\Gamma
are said to be equivalent if there exist constants
A,K>0
such that

|K\cdot d_{1}-d_{2}|<A.

When
K=1
, we say
d_{1},d_{2}
are
roughly isometric
.
We define
\mathcal{D}(\Gamma)\coloneqq D(\Gamma)/\sim
, where
\sim
denotes the equivalence relation of rough similarity.
For any pseudometric
d\in D(\Gamma)
, and
g,h\in\Gamma
,
one has
d(x,y)=d(y^{-1}x,o)\geq\ell_{d}(y^{-1}x)
.
Since
\ell_{d}(y^{-1}x)>0
whenever
y^{-1}x
is non-torsion, the
subgroup
\{x\in\Gamma\mid d(o,x)=0\}
is torsion, and hence
finite. In particular, if
\Gamma
is torsion-free (such as when

\Gamma=\pi_{1}(S)
), then every pseudometric on
D_{\Gamma}
is an actual metric
[
OR23
, Remark 4.1.3]
. An important invariant of
d\in D(\Gamma)
is the
entropy
of
d
, the
positive
real number given by

(2.9)

h(d)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log|\{g\in\Gamma:\ell_{d}(g)<n\}|}{n}.

Examples of elements in
\mathcal{D}(\Gamma)
include equivalence classes of word-metrics on
\Gamma
induced by finite, symmetric generating sets, as well as pullback of metrics arising from geometric (i.e., proper and cocompact) actions on Gromov hyperbolic, roughly geodesic spaces. We will see below that the pseudometrics associated to geodesic currents also provide examples of elements in
\mathcal{D}(\Gamma)
.

The space
\mathcal{D}(\Gamma)
can be equipped with a natural symmetric metric, defined as follows.

(2.10)

\Delta([d_{1}],[d_{2}])\coloneqq\log\left(\sup_{[g]\in[\Gamma^{\prime}]}\frac{\ell_{d_{2}}([g])}{\ell_{d_{1}}([g])}\sup_{[g]\in[\Gamma^{\prime}]}\frac{\ell_{d_{1}}([g])}{\ell_{d_{2}}([g])}\right),

where
\Gamma^{\prime}
denotes the set of loxodromic elements of
\Gamma
.

2.4.
Geodesic currents

Let
S
be a closed, connected, orientable hyperbolic surface. Fix a hyperbolic metric
X
on
S
once and for all. The universal cover
\widetilde{X}
of
X
is isometric to the Poincaré disk with the standard hyperbolic metric. Let
\mathcal{G}(\widetilde{X})
denote the space of unparametrized bi-infinite geodesics of
\widetilde{X}
. It is known that
\mathcal{G}(\widetilde{X})
is homeomorphic to
(S^{1}\times S^{1}-\Delta)/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. A
geodesic current
is a locally finite, positive,
\pi_{1}(X)
-invariant Borel measure on
\mathcal{G}(\widetilde{X})
. We will let
\mathcal{C}(X)
denote the space of all geodesic currents on
X
. Examples of such objects are geodesic currents
\delta_{c}
arising from a close geodesic
c
in
X
: precisely,
\delta_{c}
is obtained as the Dirac measure on the full lift of the closed geodesic to
\widetilde{X}
. Furthermore, every choice of hyperbolic metric
Y
on
S
induces a geodesic current
\mathcal{L}_{Y}
, called the
(hyperbolic) Liouville current
via the hyperbolic cross-ratio on the boundary of at infinity of
\widetilde{Y}
.
Precisely, for every point in Teichmüller space represented by a marked hyperbolic surface
\mathcal{Y}\coloneqq[(Y,f)]
, where
f\colon X\to Y
is a quasi-conformal homeomorphism, we obtain an equivariant quasiconformal homeomorphism

\widetilde{f}\colon\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{Y}.

There is a
\pi_{1}(Y)
-invariant measure
L_{Y}
on
\mathcal{G}(\widetilde{Y})
(the space of geodesics of
\widetilde{Y})
), given by the logarithm of the hyperbolic cross ratio on
\partial\widetilde{Y}
.
We can push
L_{Y}
forward to
\mathcal{G}(\widetilde{X})
via
f^{-1}
, obtaining:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{Y}}\coloneqq(f^{-1})_{*}L_{Y}.

Bonahon showed the map
\mathcal{Y}\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{Y}}
gives a proper embedding of
\mathcal{T}(X)
into
\mathcal{C}(X)
. In fact, the embedding is into
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}(X)\coloneqq(\mathcal{C}(X)-\{0\})/\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, the
projectivized space of geodesic currents
.
Many other metric structures induce geodesic currents. See, for example,
[
Ota90
,
CFF92
,
HP97
,
BL18
,
Erl19
]
.

The space
\cal C(X)
of geodesic currents is a convex cone in an infinite dimensional vector space. Bonahon
[
Bon86
]
extended the geometric intersection pairing on closed curves to the space of geodesic currents. That is, he showed that there exists a non-negative, symmetric, bilinear and continuous pairing

i\colon\cal C(X)\times\cal C(X)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

such that
i(\delta_{c},\delta_{d})
equals the geometric intersection number of the closed geodesics
c
and
d
.

Given a geodesic current
\nu
, we can use the intersection number to define its length spectrum
\ell_{\nu}\colon[\Gamma]\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
as
\ell_{\nu}([g])=i(\nu,\delta_{[g]})
. The
systole
of
\nu
is then
\rm{sys}(\nu):=\inf_{[g]\in[\Gamma]}\ell_{\nu}([g])
.

[
BIPP21a
, Corollary 1.5]
shows that
\rm{sys}\colon\cal C(S)\to[0,\infty)
is a continuous function.

A geodesic current
\nu
is
filling
if for every
\mu\neq 0
, we have
i(\nu,\mu)>0
. We denote the space of filling geodesic currents by
\cal C_{f}(X)
, and its projectivization by
\mathbb{P}\cal C_{f}(X)
.
We define the
entropy
of a filling geodesic current
\nu
by

(2.11)

h(\nu)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\{[g]\in[\Gamma]\mid\ell_{\nu}([g])<n\}|.

Examples of filling currents are hyperbolic Liouville currents, or currents associated to filling closed curves.
Examples of non-filling currents are
measured laminations
, i.e. geodesic currents
\alpha
satisfying
i(\alpha,\alpha)=0

[
Bon88
, Proposition 14]
.
We will denote the space of measured laminations by
\mathcal{ML}(S)
.

2.5.
Dual pseudometric of a geodesic current

Let
Y
be an arbitrary hyperbolic structure on the surface
S
.
Any geodesic current induces a pseudometric

on

as follows.

Definition 2.12

(pseudometric)
.

Given

d_{\mu}^{Y}(x,y)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\mu(G[x,y))+\frac{1}{2}\mu(G(x,y]),

where, in general, the set
G(I)
, for

a geodesic segment in
\widetilde{Y}

G[x,y))
is the set of bi-infinite geodesics intersecting the geodesic segment

transversely.

This pseudometric on

.

Given a hyperbolic structure

on

(b)

The stable length of

is equal to the
intersection number

of

(2.14)

\ell_{d_{\mu}^{Y}}([g])=i(\mu,[g]).

(c)

d_{\mu}^{Y}
is

in the sense of
Equation (
2.1
).

(d)

If
\mu
is a filling geodesic current, then

is

equivariantly quasi-isometric
to the hyperbolic metric
\widetilde{X}

and hence also quasi-isometric to any word metric on

[

, Theorem C]
and also

[

, Theorem 1.11]

(e)

Proof.

Item

[

.
Item
2.13 (b)

[

Item

[

, Theorem A]
(c.f.
[

, Proposition 6.12]

Item

∎

Note that from

).

We can also see the class of a filling geodesic current
\mu
as a point in
\mathcal{D}(\Gamma)
somewhat canonically. Given
\mathcal{Y}\coloneqq[(Y,f)]\in\Teich(S)

f_{\#}\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}(Y)

.

For every

(d_{\mu}^{\mathcal{Y}})_{\Gamma}(g,h)\coloneqq d_{f_{*}(\mu)}^{Y}(f_{\#}(g)w,f_{\#}(h)w).

From Proposition

\ell_{d_{\mu}^{\mathcal{Y}}}(g)=i(\mu,g)

.
In particular,
[(d_{\mu}^{Y_{1}})_{\Gamma}]\in\mathcal{D}(\Gamma)
, and the rough isometry class of
[(d_{\mu}^{Y_{1}})_{\Gamma}]
and
[(d_{\mu}^{Y_{2}})_{\Gamma}]
is the same for every
Y_{1},Y_{2}\in\Teich(S)

[
RMG23
]
,
[
CR25a
]
.

Notation 2.16
.

Since the stable length of this metric does not depend on underlying

, or the choice of basepoint

, we will take
Y=X
and the marking to be the identity.
In particular, we will sometimes omit the hyperbolic metric and write
d_{\mu}
to refer to the pseudometric

on

.

The following was first implicitly observed in
[
Sap23
]
, but, for completeness, we provide an explicit proof here (for related results see
[

, Proposition 6.12]
and
[
RMG23
, Theorem F]
).

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{f}(S)
denote the space of projective filling currents equipped with the weak
∗
-topology, and let
\mathcal{D}(\Gamma)
be equipped with the topology induced by the metric
\Delta
.
Then the map
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{f}(S)\to\mathcal{D}(\Gamma),[\mu]\mapsto[d_{\mu}]
is an embedding.

Proof.

The injectivity follows by a result of Otal
[
Ota90
, Théorème 1]
. The continuity of the map and its inverse follows from the equivalence of the weak
∗
-topology and the subspace topology induced by the distance
\Delta
on
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{f}(S)
(Equation (
2.10
)). This in turn follows from the fact that
\Delta
is continuous with respect to the weak
∗
topology. Indeed, the suprema involved in the definition of
\Delta
can be written as maxima of continuous functions indexed over the
compact
space of projective geodesic currents
[
Bon88
, Corollary 5]
. Indeed the function of type
\Cf(S)\times\Cf(S)\times\PC(S)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},[\mu])\mapsto\frac{\ell_{d_{\mu_{2}}}(\mu)}{\ell_{d_{\mu_{1}}}(\mu)}

is continuous.
This follows by Proposition
2.13 (b)
, from the fact that
i(\cdot,\cdot)
is a continuous, bilinear function on the space of geodesic currents
[
Bon86
, Proposition 4.5]
, and
i(\mu_{i},\cdot)>0
since
\mu_{i}
are filling for
i=1,2
.
∎

2.6.
From strongly hyperbolic to Hölder cocycles

Let
d
be a strongly hyperbolic metric on a non-elementary Gromov hyperbolic group
\Gamma
and fix
w\in\Gamma
.
The associated
Busemann cocycle
is

\beta_{w}(x,\xi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}d(x,\xi_{n})-d(w,\xi_{n}),

where
\xi=[(\xi_{n})]\in\partial\Gamma

The following properties are known.

Lemma 2.18
.

If
d
is strongly hyperbolic, then:

(1)

the limit defining
\beta_{w}
exists and satisfies

\beta_{w}(x,\xi)=d(w,x)-2(x|\xi)_{w};

and

(2.19)

\beta_{w}(xy,\xi)=\beta_{w}(y,x^{-1}\xi)+\beta_{w}(xw,\xi).

(2)

for every
w,x\in\Gamma
, the map

\xi\mapsto\beta_{w}(x,\xi)

is Hölder continuous with the same Hölder constant.

Proof.

For the first item see the discussion after
[
CT24
, Definition 2.1]
; for the second one, see
[
CMGR26
, Example 4.12]
.
∎

A
Hölder cocycle
is a map
c\colon\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

c(gh,\xi)=c(g,h\xi)+c(h,\xi)

for every
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
, and every
g,h\in\Gamma
, and such that there exists a uniform
\alpha\in(0,1)
so that for every
g\in\Gamma
, the functions
c(g,\cdot)\colon\partial\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
is
\alpha
-Hölder with respect to any visual metric on
\partial\Gamma
.

The Busemann cocycle
\beta
associated to a strongly hyperbolic metric
d
induces a Hölder cocycle.

Lemma 2.20
.

Given a strongly hyperbolic metric
d
with Busemann cocycle
\beta
, the map

c_{d}(g,\xi)\coloneqq\beta_{0}(g^{-1},\xi)

is a Hölder cocycle.

Proof.

We have

c_{d}(gh,\xi)=\beta_{o}(h^{-1}g^{-1},\xi)=\beta_{o}(g^{-1},h\xi)+\beta_{o}(h^{-1},\xi)=c_{d}(g,h\xi)+c_{d}(h,\xi)

where we used the first item of Lemma
2.18
.
Therefore,
c_{d}
satisfies the cocycle relation. Finally, the second item of
2.18
finishes the proof.
∎

3.
Equivalent definitions of strong hyperbolicity for currents

In this section, we will consider five notions of strong hyperbolicity and show that for pseudometrics dual to geodesic currents, all of these conditions are equivalent. We abbreviate strongly hyperbolic by
s.h.
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mu
be a filling geodesic current on
X
.
The following statements are equivalent:

\begin{array}[]{ll}(1)~\emph{$\varepsilon$-s.h.\ of$d_\mu^X$}&(5)~\emph{$(A_0,B_0,C_0)$-s.h.\ of$d_\mu^X$}\\[3.00003pt]
(2)~\emph{$\varepsilon$-s.h.\ withtransversals}&(6)~\emph{$(A_0,B_0,C_0)$-s.h.\ withtransversals}\\[3.00003pt]
(3)~\emph{$\varepsilon$-s.h.\ withboxes}&(7)~\emph{$(A_0,B_0,C_0)$-s.h.\ withboxes}\\[3.00003pt]
(4)~\emph{$\varepsilon$-s.h.\ withcrossingpairs}&(8)~\emph{$(A_0,B_0,C_0)$-s.h.\ withcrossingpairs}\end{array}

The conditions in the first line are the two equivalent characterizations of strong hyperbolicity by Nica–Špakula
[
NS16
]
applied to the pseudometric
d_{\mu}^{X}
(Proposition
2.5
).

The conditions in the second line are, in some sense, a restatement of the definition of
\varepsilon
-strong hyperbolicity and
(A_{0},B_{0},C_{0})
-strong hyperbolicity for pseudometrics dual to geodesic currents (Lemma
3.5
,
3.6
,
3.7
). They feature
(double) transversals
, involving four points in the interior
\widetilde{X}
. The conditions in the third line use boxes of geodesics, and hence only depend on boundary data (Lemma
3.8
,
3.9
). Finally, the conditions in the fourth line are expressed in terms of marked length spectrum and show, in particular, that for pseudometrics arising from geodesic currents, strong hyperbolicity is an invariant of the rough isometry class among pseudometrics dual to geodesic currents (Lemma
3.12
, Remark
3.15
).

We begin by introducing the notion of double transversals. The proof of
theorem

3.1
is then obtained through a sequence of results ranging from
lemma

3.3
to
lemma

3.12
.

Definition 3.2

(double transversals)
.

Given two geodesic segments
I,J
in
\widetilde{X}
, let
G(I,J)
denote the set of geodesics intersecting both
I
and

transversely.
Specifically, consider four points
x,y,w,z
in
\widetilde{X}
. We define the

G_{x,y,z,w}\coloneqq G([w,x),[y,z))
.
In general, because of atoms, one has to be careful about including interval endpoints. However, we will show that a filling strongly hyperbolic current
\mu
has no atoms (Corollary
4.4
), and hence, in the setting we will be mostly interested about, the specific choice of endpoints will be irrelevant.

In what follows, we use a setup similar to that of
[
MGT26

Compare Figure

Figure 3.1.

Lemma 3.8

1\leq e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\mu(G)}+e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\mu(G^{\perp})}.

For the other direction, suppose there exists a transversal

4.

We say a geodesic current

We will use the following decomposition theorem for geodesic currents, stated in a form tailored to our purposes.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
BIPP21a
, Theorem 1.2(1)]
(c.f.
[

, Proposition A]

Let
\mu

(1.1)

\mu\;=\;\nu\;+\;\lambda,

where, for a finite indexing sets

•

\nu\coloneqq\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}\mu_{v}
, and each
\mu_{v}

projects to a subsurface

\Sigma_{v}^{\circ}

•

\lambda\coloneqq\sum_{c\in\mathcal{E}_{\mu}}\lambda_{c}\,\delta_{c}

We now prove the following.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mu
be a geodesic current decomposing as

where
\lambda
is the simple multi-curve corresponding to the special geodesics in the decomposition of
\mu

\mu

Proof.

Use Theorem

to write

\mu=\nu+\lambda

\lambda
is the simple multi-curve consisting of the special (simple closed) geodesics of

\nu
has no special geodesics in its support.

We first prove that if

is strongly bolic, then
\nu
is non-atomic.

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
\nu
has an atom. Let
\gamma
be an atomic geodesic in the support of
\nu

be the primitive element stabilizing
\gamma
, with endpoints
b^{+},b^{-}\in\partial\widetilde{X}

\gamma
is not special, it intersects some other geodesic in the support of
\nu

For each

Since the union is disjoint and

\nu(B_{n})=(2n+1)\nu(B)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\infty.

Now choose points

We now prove the converse. Assume that

\mu=\nu+\lambda,

\nu

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that

For each

Write

6.
Strongly hyperbolic currents are dense

In this section we prove that strongly hyperbolic currents are dense in
\mathcal{C}(X)
. To prove this, we will consider a regular finite sheeted covering map
\pi\colon Y\to X
and an associated transfer map
\Pi:\mathcal{C}(Y)\rightarrow C(X)
. Recall that a covering map is said to be
regular
if the action of its deck group is transitive (equivalently, by the Galois correspondence, the corresponding subgroup is normal).

The transfer map is a natural map to consider for any finite-degree covering of hyperbolic surfaces.

Definition 6.1
.

Suppose
X,Y
are hyperbolic surfaces.
Let
\pi\colon Y\to X
be a finite-degree covering map of degree
n
. We define the
transfer map
associated to
\pi
and denoted by
\Pi:\mathcal{C}(Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(X)
as

\Pi(\mu)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(g_{i})_{*}\mu

where
\mu\in\mathcal{C}(Y)
and the set
\{g_{1},g_{2}\dots,g_{n}\}
is a
transversal
of
\pi_{1}(Y)\leq\pi_{1}(X)
, i.e., a set of elements in
\pi_{1}(Y)
so that
\pi_{1}(X)
can be partitioned into left cosets as as
\cup_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}\pi_{1}(X)
. For any box
B
in
G(\widetilde{X})
,
(g_{i})_{*}(\mu)(B)=\mu((g_{i})^{-1}(B))
. We choose
g_{1}
to be the identity element in
\pi_{1}(X)
. Note that the definition of
\Pi
depends on the choice of transversal, even though it is not included in the notation.

Consider a quasiconformal diffeomorphism
f\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
between conformally hyperbolic Riemann surfaces,
and lift it to a map
\widetilde{f}\colon\widetilde{X_{1}}\to\widetilde{X_{2}}
between their universal covers.
The Beurling-Ahlfors Theorem
[
Hub06
, Corollary 4.9.4]
says that
\widetilde{f}
has a continuous extension to the compactification obtained by adding the boundary at infinity

\widetilde{f}\colon\widetilde{X_{1}}\cup\partial\widetilde{X_{1}}\to\widetilde{X_{2}}\cup\partial\widetilde{X_{2}}
.
We say a box of geodesics
B\subset G(\widetilde{X_{1}})
is
symmetric
if
L_{\widetilde{X_{1}}}(B)=\log(2)
.
A homeomorphism
\widetilde{f}\colon\partial\widetilde{X_{1}}\to\partial\widetilde{X_{2}}
is
quasi-symmetric
if the supremum

M(\widetilde{f})\coloneqq\sup_{B\text{ symmetric}}\frac{L_{X}(\widetilde{f}(B))}{\log(2)},

as
B
ranges over all symmetric boxes
B\subset G(\widetilde{X_{1}})
is finite.
We let
M(f)
denote the
quasi-symmetric constant of
f
. See
[
BŠ21
, Section 3.3]
for details (in particular, the above definition is equivalent to other usual definitions of quasi-symmetry).

Proposition 6.2

(
[
BŠ21
, Proposition 15]
)
.

If a homeomorphism
f\colon\partial\widetilde{X_{1}}\to\partial\widetilde{X_{2}}
is quasisymmetric, there exists a homeomorphism
\omega\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
depending only on the quasisymmetric constant of
f
so that

L_{X_{2}}(f(B))\leq\omega(L_{X_{1}}(B))

for every box of geodesics
B
.

By the proof of
[
BŠ21

, there exists an increasing homeomorphism
\eta\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, and
\omega(x)\coloneqq\eta^{-1}(M\cdot\eta(x))
, where

is a constant depending only on the quasisymmetric constant of
f
.
In fact, using estimates on elliptic integrals, the behaviour of
\omega
can be estimated explicitly, as we show in Appendix

Theorem 6.3
.

Given a closed hyperbolic surface
X
, any finite positive linear combination of hyperbolic Liouville currents

a_{1}\mathcal{L}_{Y_{1}}+\cdots+a_{n}\mathcal{L}_{Y_{n}}

is strongly hyperbolic.

Proof.

Let
f_{i}\colon X\to Y_{i}
be the marking for
Y_{i}
, and
\widetilde{f_{i}}\colon\partial\widetilde{X}\to\partial\widetilde{Y_{i}}
its equivariant quasisymmetric lift to the boundary. Recall that we view the Liouville current

of

as a current
\mathcal{L}_{Y_{i}}
on
X
by setting

\mathcal{L}_{Y_{i}}(B)\coloneqq(\widetilde{f_{i}}^{-1})_{*}L_{Y_{i}}(B)=L_{Y_{i}}(\widetilde{f_{i}}(B)).

For

\widetilde{f_{kj}}\coloneqq\widetilde{f_{j}}\circ\widetilde{f_{k}}^{-1}\colon Y_{k}\to Y_{j}.

Since
\widetilde{f_{kj}}
is a composition of quasisymmetric homeomorphisms, it is quasisymmetric. Therefore, by Proposition
6.2
, there exists an increasing homeomorphism
\omega_{kj}\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that

\omega_{kj}^{-1}\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{Y_{j}}(B)\bigr)\leq\mathcal{L}_{Y_{k}}(B)\leq\omega_{kj}\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{Y_{j}}(B)\bigr)

for every box
B
.

Moreover, by Corollary
B.4
, for each pair
(k,j)
there exists a constant
M_{kj}\geq 1
(depending on the quasisymmetric constant of
f_{kj}
) such that

\omega_{kj}(t)\leq M_{kj}t\qquad\text{for every }t\geq 1.

Let

M\coloneqq\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n}M_{kj}.

Then

\omega_{kj}(t)\leq Mt\qquad\text{for every }t\geq 1

and every
j,k
.

Now set

\mu\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\mathcal{L}_{Y_{i}}.

Suppose that

A\leq\mu(B)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\mathcal{L}_{Y_{i}}(B).

Then there exists some index
k

\frac{A}{na_{k}}\leq\mathcal{L}_{Y_{k}}(B).

Fix

. Using the comparison inequality above, we get

\frac{A}{na_{k}}\leq\mathcal{L}_{Y_{k}}(B)\leq\omega_{kj}\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{Y_{j}}(B)\bigr),

Since

\omega_{kj}^{-1}(t)\geq\frac{t}{M}.

\omega_{kj}^{-1}\!\left(\frac{A}{na_{k}}\right)\geq\frac{A}{Mna_{k}}\geq\frac{A}{Mna_{\max}}.

Hence, for every
j

\mathcal{L}_{Y_{j}}(B)\geq\frac{A}{Mna_{\max}}.

Now let

Thus

7.1.1.
Translation flows

Let

\partial^{2}\Gamma=\{(x,y)\in\partial\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma:x\neq y\},\qquad\varphi_{t}(x,y,s)=(x,y,s-t).

Theorem 7.1

(Sambarino
[

]
)
.

If

is a topologically mixing, metric Anosov flow, admitting a symbolic coding with Hölder roof functions and Hölder conjugate to a Hölder reparametrization of the geodesic flow of a negatively curved surface.

In particular, the translation flow admits Livšic theory, equilibrium states, and the thermodynamic formalism.

Fix a Hölder cocycle

Let

A flow

Consider strongly hyperbolic currents

Recall that the renormalized length spectrum of a strongly hyperbolic current

If is a filling geodesic current

7.3.
Beyond strongly hyperbolic currents

Theorem
7.14
applies to strongly hyperbolic currents because, in this setting, the corresponding Busemann cocycles are Hölder and the associated translation flows admit the thermodynamic formalism needed for the Lalley–Sharp theorem. Nevertheless, the statement of the counting problem only involves the renormalized length spectra

L_{\mu}=h(\mu)\ell_{\mu}.

This suggests asking whether an analogous result should hold for arbitrary filling currents.

There is some evidence for this formulation. The entropy function

h:\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{fill}}(S)\to\mathbb{R}

is continuous, and the Manhattan-curve expression for the correlation exponent extends continuously to pairs of non-proportional projective filling currents. More precisely, since Manhattan curves of points in

are always

(
[
CT25
, Theorem 1.1]
) one can define

\overline{M}(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{a}{h(\alpha)}+\frac{b}{h(\beta)},

where
(a,b)
is the point on the generalized Manhattan curve whose tangent line is parallel to the line joining

(h(\alpha),0)\qquad\text{and}\qquad(0,h(\beta)).

With this definition, and using the strict convexity of
\overline{M}
, one can check it is continuous on

\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{fill}}(S)\times\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{fill}}(S)-\Delta.

This motivates the following question.

Question 7.19
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
be two non-proportional filling geodesic currents. Do there exist constants

C=C(\varepsilon,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad M=M(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})>0

such that, as

,

\#\Big\{[\gamma]\in[\Gamma]\;\Big|\;L_{\mu_{1}}([\gamma])\in\bigl(x,x+h(\mu_{1})\varepsilon\bigr),\ L_{\mu_{2}}([\gamma])\in\bigl(x,x+h(\mu_{2})\varepsilon\bigr)\Big\}\sim C\frac{e^{Mx}}{x^{3/2}}?

By the above discussion, the main obstruction to extending Theorem

is not the definition of the exponent

(since it extends continuously to all filling geodesic currents), but guaranteeing the convergence of the asymptotic and the existence of the constant

. Indeed,

is governed by the second derivative of the pressure, equivalently by the curvature of the Manhattan curve. For general filling currents, it is not clear whether the corresponding Manhattan curve is

.

Question 7.20
.

Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be a pair of filling geodesic currents. Is the Manhattan curve abscissa

a

function? Is it analytic?

Some evidence supporting this comes from the fact that Manhattan curves are analytic for pairs of distinct strongly hyperbolic currents
[

\gamma
is filling, then the pseudometric

has the same stable lengths as the geometric action of

on the dual Sageev complex

]

Appendix A
Signed geodesic currents

A
signed geodesic current
on
S
is a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon signed measure on the space of geodesics
G(\widetilde{X})
.
Equivalently,

\mathcal{C}^{\pm}(S)=\{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}:\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{C}(S)\}.

By uniqueness of the Jordan decomposition, if

\alpha=\alpha^{+}-\alpha^{-}

is the decomposition of a signed current into mutually singular positive Radon measures, then
\alpha^{\pm}
are
\Gamma
-invariant. In particular,
\alpha^{\pm}\in\mathcal{C}(S)
.
The associated total variation measure

|\alpha|=\alpha^{+}+\alpha^{-}

is therefore also a geodesic current.

Bonahon’s intersection form extends uniquely by bilinearity to a symmetric bilinear form

i:\mathcal{C}^{\pm}(S)\times\mathcal{C}^{\pm}(S)\to\mathbb{R}.

Moreover, if
\gamma
is a closed curve and
[\gamma)
is a fundamental domain for an axis of
\gamma
in
\widetilde{X}
, then we can extend the intersection number on geodesic currents to signed currents linearly as

i(\alpha,\gamma)=\alpha(G_{[\gamma)}),

where
G_{[\gamma)}
denotes the set of geodesics intersecting
[\gamma)
.

A.1.
Extension of Otal rigidity

The following extends Otal’s injectivity of marked length spectrum from geodesic currents (
[
Ota90
, Théorème 2]
) to signed currents.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{C}^{\pm}(S)
.
If

i(\alpha,\gamma)=i(\beta,\gamma)

for every closed curve
\gamma
, then
\alpha=\beta
.

Proof.

Write

\alpha=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2},\qquad\beta=\nu_{1}-\nu_{2},

with
\mu_{i},\nu_{i}\in\mathcal{C}(S)
.

Otal’s argument
[
Ota90
]
shows that for every box of geodesics

B=(a,b)\times(c,d)\subset\mathcal{G}(\widetilde{X})

(where
a,b,c,d
are endpoints of lifts of a dense orbit in the unit tangent bundle of
S
),
and for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist closed curves

\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3},\gamma_{4}

such that for every geodesic current
\mu
,

\left|\mu(B)-\frac{i(\mu,\gamma_{1})+i(\mu,\gamma_{2})-i(\mu,\gamma_{3})-i(\mu,\gamma_{4})}{2}\right|<\varepsilon.

Since the expression above is linear in
\mu
, the same approximation formula holds for signed currents. Therefore,

\left|\alpha(B)-\frac{i(\alpha,\gamma_{1})+i(\alpha,\gamma_{2})-i(\alpha,\gamma_{3})-i(\alpha,\gamma_{4})}{2}\right|<\varepsilon,

and similarly for
\beta
.

Since

i(\alpha,\gamma)=i(\beta,\gamma)

for every closed curve
\gamma
, we obtain

|\alpha(B)-\beta(B)|<2\varepsilon.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
gives

\alpha(B)=\beta(B)

for every such box
B
.
Since these boxes have endpoints dense in
S^{1}
, they generate the topology of
\mathcal{G}(\widetilde{X})
, and thus the measures
\alpha
and
\beta
coincide.
∎

A.2.
Integral signed currents

We say that a signed current is a
signed weighted multicurve
if it is a finite linear combination

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\gamma_{i},

where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma_{i}
are closed curves.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\pm}(S)
.
If

i(\alpha,\gamma)\in\mathbb{Z}

for every closed curve
\gamma
, then
\alpha
is an signed weighted multicurve.

Proof.

Suppose that
\alpha
is not a signed weighted multicurve.
Then the total variation geodesic current
|\alpha|
is not purely atomic.
Hence there exists a box
B
such that

0<|\alpha|(B)<\frac{1}{4}.

In particular,

|\alpha(B)|<\frac{1}{4}.

By Proposition
A.1
, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exist closed curves

\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3},\gamma_{4}

such that

\left|\alpha(B)-\frac{i(\alpha,\gamma_{1})+i(\alpha,\gamma_{2})-i(\alpha,\gamma_{3})-i(\alpha,\gamma_{4})}{2}\right|<\varepsilon.

Since each intersection number is integral,

\frac{i(\alpha,\gamma_{1})+i(\alpha,\gamma_{2})-i(\alpha,\gamma_{3})-i(\alpha,\gamma_{4})}{2}\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}.

Thus
\alpha(B)
can be approximated arbitrarily well by half-integers.
Since

|\alpha(B)|<\frac{1}{4},

the only possible such half-integer is
0
.
Hence

\alpha(B)=0,

contradicting the choice of
B
.
Therefore
|\alpha|
is purely atomic, and so
\alpha
is a signed weighted multicurve.
∎

Remark A.3
.

The proof of the proposition shows that
\alpha
is a half-integral signed multi-curve, although we will not use this. Indeed, the argument implies that
|\alpha|
and the components of its Hahn decomposition are geodesic currents whose box measures are half-integers. By
[
BIPP21b
, Proposition 4.12]
, twice each of these currents is an integral multi-curve, and hence the currents themselves are half-integral multi-curves. Therefore
\alpha
is a half-integral signed multi-curve.

Appendix B
Elliptic integrals and the modulus of a box

In this section we express the conformal modulus of a box in terms of complete elliptic integrals and derive the two asymptotic regimes that will be used later.

B.1.
Background on elliptic integrals

For
k\in(0,1)
, the
complete elliptic integral of the first kind
is defined by

K(k)=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{1-k^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}},

and we write

k^{\prime}=\sqrt{1-k^{2}}

for the
complementary modulus
.

We will use the following two classical facts.

Proposition B.1

(Estimates for
K
)
.

There exist absolute constants
c,C>0
such that:

(1)

if
k
stays in a compact subset of
(0,1)
, then

c\leq K(k)\leq C;

(2)

as
k\to 1
, equivalently
k^{\prime}\to 0
,

c\,\log\frac{4}{k^{\prime}}\leq K(k)\leq C\,\log\frac{4}{k^{\prime}}.

Proof.

These estimates are classical; see
[
NIS25
, §19.12]
. In particular, the second statement follows from the asymptotic expansion

K(k)=\log\frac{4}{k^{\prime}}+O\bigl((k^{\prime})^{2}\log(1/k^{\prime})\bigr).\qed

We will also use
Carlson’s symmetric elliptic integral

R_{F}(x,y,z)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{(t+x)(t+y)(t+z)}},

which satisfies

(B.2)

K(k)=R_{F}(0,1-k^{2},1)

by
[
NIS25
, §19.25]
.

Finally, we will use the following rectangular conformal map. If
x_{1}>x_{2}>x_{3}
are real numbers, then

z(p)=R_{F}(p-x_{1},p-x_{2},p-x_{3})

maps the upper half-plane conformally onto a rectangle, whose horizontal and vertical side lengths are

W=R_{F}(0,x_{1}-x_{2},x_{1}-x_{3}),\qquad H=R_{F}(0,x_{1}-x_{3},x_{2}-x_{3}),

respectively. In particular, the conformal modulus of this rectangle is

\mu=\frac{W}{H}.

See
[
NIS25
, §19.32]
.

B.2.
Normalization of a box

Let
Q=[a,b]\times[c,d]
be a box, where
a,b,c,d\in\partial\widetilde{X}
are in positive cyclic order, and let

t=L_{X}(Q)>0

be its Liouville mass. Identifying
\partial\widetilde{X}
with
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
and using Möbius invariance, we may assume that

(a,b,c,d)=(\infty,1,\lambda,0)

for some
\lambda\in(0,1)
.

In this normalization,

L_{X}(Q)=\log\frac{(c-a)(d-b)}{(b-a)(d-c)}=\log\frac{1}{1-\lambda},

and therefore

e^{t}=\frac{1}{1-\lambda},\qquad\text{so}\qquad\lambda=1-e^{-t}.

Thus the conformal modulus of
Q
depends only on the parameter
t\in(0,\infty)
.

B.3.
Expression of the modulus in terms of
K

Apply the rectangular conformal map above with

x_{1}=1,\qquad x_{2}=\lambda,\qquad x_{3}=0.

Then

z(p)=R_{F}(p-1,p-\lambda,p)

maps the upper half-plane conformally onto a rectangle. Its side lengths are

W=R_{F}(0,1-\lambda,1),\qquad H=R_{F}(0,1,\lambda)=R_{F}(0,\lambda,1),

so its conformal modulus is

\mu(Q)=\frac{R_{F}(0,1-\lambda,1)}{R_{F}(0,\lambda,1)}.

Now set

k=\sqrt{\lambda},\qquad k^{\prime}=\sqrt{1-\lambda}.

Using Equation
B.2
, we obtain

R_{F}(0,1-\lambda,1)=K(\sqrt{\lambda})=K(k),

and, by symmetry of
R_{F}
,

R_{F}(0,\lambda,1)=K(\sqrt{1-\lambda})=K(k^{\prime}).

Hence

\mu(Q)=\frac{K(k)}{K(k^{\prime})}.

Since
\lambda=1-e^{-t}
, this yields

k=\sqrt{1-e^{-t}},\qquad k^{\prime}=e^{-t/2},

and therefore

\eta(t):=\mu(Q)=\frac{K(\sqrt{1-e^{-t}})}{K(e^{-t/2})}.

B.4.
The two regimes for
\eta

We now derive the estimates we will need.
The notation
f(t)\asymp g(t)
will be used in the relevant
asymptotic regime. More precisely, as
t\to 0
, it means that there
exist constants
c,C,t_{0}>0
such that

cg(t)\leq f(t)\leq Cg(t)\qquad\text{for }0<t<t_{0},

and as
t\to\infty
, it means that the same estimate holds for

t>t_{0}
.

Proposition B.3
.

The function
\eta:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
satisfies

\eta(t)\asymp\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{\log(16/t)},&0<t\leq 1,\\[5.16663pt]
t,&t\geq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Assume first that
0<t\leq 1
. Then

k=\sqrt{1-e^{-t}}\to 0\qquad\text{as }t\to 0.

Since
K
extends continuously to
0
and
K(0)=\pi/2
, we have

K(k)\asymp 1.

On the other hand,

k^{\prime}=e^{-t/2}\to 1\qquad\text{as }t\to 0,

and the complementary modulus of
k^{\prime}
is

\sqrt{1-(k^{\prime})^{2}}=\sqrt{1-e^{-t}}=k.

Therefore Proposition
B.1
(2) gives

K(k^{\prime})\asymp\log\frac{4}{k}.

Since

1-e^{-t}\asymp t\qquad(0<t\leq 1),

we have

k=\sqrt{1-e^{-t}}\asymp\sqrt{t}.

Hence

\log\frac{4}{k}\asymp\log\frac{16}{t}.

Combining these estimates yields

\eta(t)=\frac{K(k)}{K(k^{\prime})}\asymp\frac{1}{\log(16/t)}.

Assume now that
t\geq 1
. Then

0<k^{\prime}=e^{-t/2}\leq e^{-1/2}.

Since
K
extends continuously to
0
and is positive on

[0,e^{-1/2}]
, we have

K(k^{\prime})\asymp 1.

At the same time,

k=\sqrt{1-e^{-t}}\to 1\qquad\text{as }t\to\infty,

and its complementary modulus is precisely

\sqrt{1-k^{2}}=k^{\prime}=e^{-t/2}.

Thus Proposition
B.1
(2) implies

K(k)\asymp\log\frac{4}{k^{\prime}}=\log 4+\frac{t}{2}\asymp t.

Therefore

\eta(t)=\frac{K(k)}{K(k^{\prime})}\asymp t.

This proves the result.
∎

B.5.
The distortion function
\omega

The function
\eta
is strictly increasing and maps
(0,\infty)

onto
(0,\infty)
. Hence
\eta^{-1}
is well-defined.
Let
M
be any real number so that
M\geq 1
and define

\omega(t)\coloneqq\eta^{-1}(M\,\eta(t)).

In the applications,
M
will be the quasi-symmetric distortion constant.

Corollary B.4
.

The function
\omega:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
satisfies

\omega(t)\asymp\begin{cases}t^{1/M},&0<t\leq 1,\\[5.16663pt]
t,&t\geq 1,\end{cases}

where the implicit constants depend on
M
.

Proof.

Let
s=\omega(t)
. By definition,

\eta(s)=M\,\eta(t).

Assume first that
0<t\leq 1
. For
t
sufficiently small, the estimate

\eta(t)\asymp\frac{1}{\log(16/t)}

implies that
\eta(t)
is small. Since
M
is fixed,
\eta(s)=M\eta(t)

is also small, and hence
s
lies in the small regime as well. Therefore

\frac{1}{\log(16/s)}\asymp\eta(s)=M\eta(t)\asymp\frac{M}{\log(16/t)}.

Thus

\log(16/s)\asymp\frac{1}{M}\log(16/t).

Exponentiating gives

s\asymp t^{1/M}.

After enlarging the implicit constants, the same estimate holds for all

0<t\leq 1
. Hence

\omega(t)\asymp t^{1/M}.

Assume next that
t\geq 1
. Then

\eta(t)\asymp t,

so

\eta(s)=M\eta(t)\asymp t.

In particular, for
t
sufficiently large,
s
lies in the large regime.
Using
\eta(s)\asymp s
, we obtain

s\asymp t.

Again enlarging the implicit constants if necessary, this holds for all

t\geq 1
. Therefore

\omega(t)\asymp t.

This proves the claim.
∎
