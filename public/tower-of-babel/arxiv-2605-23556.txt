Is Dimensionality a Barrier for Retrieval Models?
Kiril Bangachev, Guy Bresler, Jonathan Kogan, Yury Polyanskiy

Abstract
Why does the low dimensionality of representations, typically d ≈ 1000 d\approx 1000 , not prevent modern embedding-based retrieval models from scaling to billions, or even trillions, of data points? To answer this question, we study maximal-margin embeddings in the following retrieval model, classically studied in communication complexity 52 and more recently in embedding-based retrieval 70 . Let A ∈ { 0 , 1 } N × n A\in\{0,1\}^{N\times n} be a matrix indicating whether each of N N queries is relevant to each of n n documents. We are interested in the largest margin m > 0 , m>0, denoted by 𝗆 𝗋𝖽 ​ ( d , A ) , \mathsf{m}^{\mathsf{rd}}(d,A), for which there exist unit norm embeddings of the queries and documents { U j } j = 1 N , { V i } i = 1 n \{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n} with the following property. ⟨ U j , V i ⟩ ≥ m \langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle\geq m whenever A j ​ i = 1 A_{ji}=1 and ⟨ U j , V i ⟩ ≤ − m \langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle\leq-m otherwise. A large margin is a key proxy for representation quality: it controls both robustness to perturbations and compositional generalization across queries. Our main theorem establishes that the best possible margin without a restriction on the dimension, 𝗆 𝗋𝖽 ​ ( + ∞ , A ) , \mathsf{m}^{\mathsf{rd}}(+\infty,A), can be nearly achieved in dimension d = O ⁡ ( 𝗆 𝗋𝖽 ​ ( + ∞ , A ) − 2 ​ log ⁡ n ) d=O(\mathsf{m}^{\mathsf{rd}}(+\infty,A)^{-2}\log n) which improves a theorem of 11 . Together with a matching lower bound in Theorem 1.5, we conclude that when A ∈ { 0 , 1 } ( n k ) × n A\in\{0,1\}^{\binom{n}{k}\times n} is the matrix containing all possible k k -sparse rows once, dimension d = O ⁡ ( k ​ log ⁡ ( n / k ) ) d=O(k\log(n/k)) is necessary and sufficient for the maximal possible margin 𝗆 𝗋𝖽 ( + ∞ , A ) = Θ ( k − 1 / 2 ) \mathsf{m}^{\mathsf{rd}}(+\infty,A)=\Theta(k^{-1/2}) in this setting. This fully resolves the setup of 70 . We also give several constructions for large margins when d = o ⁡ ( k ​ log ⁡ ( n / k ) ) . d=o(k\log(n/k)). Our proofs are based on connections between this problem and the literature on compressed sensing and the restricted isometry property. Finally, we empirically test the InfoNCE and sigmoid losses for producing large margin embeddings and demonstrate a clear advantage of the sigmoid loss. Code is available at github/TopK .

Background and Motivation

Modern information retrieval systems rely on

Depending on the modality, a variety of training paradigms and architectures exist for representing data as vectors: for text, popular choices include transformer-based architectures trained with contrastive or self-supervised objectives over query-passage pairs such as SBERT, DPR, E5, GTE, BGE, EmbeddingGemma

Once data is represented compactly in vector form (typically

enables low-memory caching of representations and fast algorithmic primitives (such as nearest neighbor search). Both speed and low-cost storage are integral to high-performance AI. However, compactness of representations is not enough on its own.
For the design of accurate, reliable, and precise AI systems, it is also necessary that embeddings be of
high-quality

That

and

are at odds is no surprise. Low dimension (rank) limits the geometric configurations available to item representations. What should be surprising instead is that models based on low-dimensional representations still achieve excellent performance on retrieval from huge datasets.
For instance, DPR embeds data in
d=768
dimensions and achieves remarkable performance when retrieving from a pool of
\approx 2\times 10^{7}
passages

. This raises the question:

Q: How can embedding models be so successful despite their low dimensionality?

We approach this question theoretically by analyzing the top-
k

70

the margin
– we show a surprising low-rank saturation result (formally stated in Theorem
1.4

Informal Main Result: The highest possible quality embeddings are already achieved in a dimension growing only logarithmically with the data size.

Hence, embedding-based retrieval models do not require a high dimension for optimal performance. This main result opens the door to
further theoretical results related to precise dimension recommendations for encoders and linking different notions of “quality” of representations. To obtain these results, we connect the model of

1.2
Mathematical Model and Problem Formulation

Basic Retrieval Model.
In the model of
70
, an incidence matrix
A\in\{0,1\}^{N\times n}
encodes the relevance relationship between
N
queries and
n
objects. A query
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}=[N]
is relevant to object

i\in[n]
if and only if
A_{ji}=1.
This abstraction captures a variety of practical settings, for example when
[n]
corresponds to a corpus of passages and
[N]
to a set of queries
39
, or when
[N]
corresponds to the images in a dataset and
[n]
to their classes
57
.

The results of
70
focus primarily on the case where
N=\binom{n}{k}
and
A=S_{n,k}\in\{0,1\}^{N\times n}
contains every possible
k
-sparse row exactly once. In this setting, for every group of
k
objects, there is a query to which exactly those
k
objects are relevant.
Many of our results apply more broadly to
matrices
A
for which all rows are distinct and
k
-sparse (so that only some groups of
k
documents can be retrieved, which is more realistic). Typically, we will think about the quantity
k
as a large constant or slowly growing with
n
, even though our results apply for any
k
unless otherwise stated. We note that
7
analyzes the case when
k=1
,
S_{n,k}=I
.

Corresponding to
A
, models
f_{\theta}
and
g_{\phi}
produce embeddings, respectively
\{f_{\theta}(O_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}=\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of the objects
\{O_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
and
\{g_{\phi}(Q_{j})\}_{j=1}^{N}=\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
of the queries
\{Q_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}
. These embeddings are assumed to be unit norm throughout the paper, which is without loss of generality as only cosine similarities between them will be considered.

The goal of the representations is to enable retrieval of objects relevant to a given query. One property that ensures this is if there exists some
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
such that:

\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle>\tau\text{ if }A_{ji}=1\text{ and }\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle<\tau\text{ if }A_{ji}=0.

(1)

Indeed, this would imply that the documents relevant to query
j
are those whose embeddings have cosine similarity with
U_{j}
exceeding
\tau
. These documents can be retrieved by the standard procedure of finding the nearest neighbors of
U_{j}
and filtering out those with cosine similarity less than
\tau.

Retrieval Margin.
While property (
1
) guarantees correct retrieval, it is too brittle to be useful in practice. A minor modification
O^{\prime}_{i}
of object
O_{i},
for example flipping few pixels, may lead to an incorrect retrieval by some query
Q_{j},
i.e.

\langle f_{\theta}(O_{i}),g_{\phi}(Q_{j})\rangle<\tau<\langle f_{\theta}(O^{\prime}_{i}),g_{\phi}(Q_{j})\rangle.
To capture the fact that strong models should succeed on retrieval even when data is slightly perturbed, we introduce the
margin
of a model. Borrowing the terminology of
7
, we make the following definition.

Definition 1
.

Given an incidence matrix
A\in\{0,1\}^{N\times n},
we say that the vectors
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
are a margin-
m
, relative-bias-
\tau
embedding of
A
for
m\geq 0
if

\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle\geq\tau+m\text{ if }A_{ji}=1\text{ and }\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle\leq\tau-m\text{ if }A_{ji}=0.

(2)

The main question that we will study is bounding the maximal possible margin of the embedding. We note that for the rest of the paper, we will only focus on the case of
\tau=0
for simplicity of exposition. Following
70
, this is without loss of generality up to increasing the dimension by 1 and multiplying the margin by a small absolute constant. See Appendix
A
for completeness.

Problem 1
.

For a given
A\in\{0,1\}^{N\times n},
find the optimal restricted-dimension margin

\mathsf{m}^{rd}(d,A)\coloneqq\sup\{m\;:\;\exists\text{ margin-}m,\text{ relative-bias-}0\text{ embeddings of }A\text{ in dimension }d\}.

Whenever there does not exist a margin-
m
embedding of
A
for any
m\geq 0,
we set
\mathsf{m}^{rd}(d,A)=-\infty.

The quantity without the dimension restriction, i.e.
\mathsf{m}^{rd}(+\infty,A)\coloneqq\sup_{d}\mathsf{m}^{rd}(d,A)
is known as the
margin complexity
in the communication complexity literature
59
.

1.4
Results

We highlight a gap in the literature. There is no positive result which guarantees simultaneously
large margin
(quality) and a
small dimensionality
(compactness) of representations.

Part 1: Main Result.

Our main result directly addresses this gap.

Theorem 1.4

(Main Result)
.

Let
A\in\{0,1\}^{N\times n}.

For every
\epsilon>0,
there exists some
C_{\epsilon}=O(\epsilon^{-4})
depending only on
\epsilon
such that

\mathsf{m}^{rd}(C_{\epsilon}\;\times\;\mathsf{m}^{rd}(+\infty,A)^{-2}\;\times\;\log(\min(n,N))\;,\;A)\geq(1-\epsilon)\mathsf{m}^{rd}(+\infty,A).

We interpret this result as follows: one can obtain nearly the optimal margin
\mathsf{m}^{rd}(+\infty,A)
(i.e., nearly the same as in dimension
d=\infty
) in dimension
O(\mathsf{m}^{rd}(+\infty,A)^{-2}\;\times\;\log(\min(n,N))).
For concreteness, recall that if each row of
A
is
k
-sparse, then
\mathsf{m}^{rd}(+\infty,A)\geq\frac{1-o_{k}(1)}{2\sqrt{k}}.
In particular, an optimal margin for any
k
-sparse matrix can be obtained in dimension
O(k\log n)
!

It turns out that one can recover that
\mathsf{m}^{rd}(Ck\log n,S_{n,k})=\Omega(1/\sqrt{k})
for a large enough
C
from results on compressed sensing
15
. However, it seems like the approach is limited to
margin
\Theta(1/\sqrt{k})
and dimension
\Theta(k\log n)
and falls short of our Theorem
1.4
. Theorem
1.4
goes further and implies, for example, that for
k
-sparse
A
with
\mathsf{m}^{rd}(+\infty,A)=\omega_{k}(k^{-1/2}),
maximal margin can be achieved in an even smaller dimension
o_{k}(k\log n).

See Appendix
E
.

We note that Theorem
1.4
is a substantial strengthening of the result from
11
that states that
\mathsf{sr}(A)=O(\mathsf{m}^{rd}(+\infty,A)^{-2}\log(nN)).
The result of
11
is for sign rank, so it only states that a positive margin embedding exists, without preserving the margin. More importantly, our result has
\log(\min(n,N))
dependence instead of

\log(nN)=\Theta(\log(\max(n,N))).
Instead of our
O(k\log(n)),

11
achieves dimension
O(k^{2}\log(n))
for margin
\Theta(k^{-1/2})
for
S_{n,k}.

Both the proof of our Theorem
1.4
, presented in Section
2
, and the result of
11
use the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma (Theorem
2.1
) to perform dimensionality reduction. Concretely, the result
\mathsf{sr}(A)=O(\mathsf{m}^{rd}(+\infty,A)^{-2}\log(nN))
proceeds by taking embeddings
\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
attaining the maximal margin
\mathsf{m}^{rd}(+\infty,A)

and applying JL on the
Nn
pairs
(U_{j},V_{i})
so that inner products are preserved up to an additive error smaller than the margin
\mathsf{m}^{rd}(+\infty,A).

The novelty in our proof is that we apply JL so that
only the norms
of (certain convex combinations of the)
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
(when
n\leq N
) are approximately preserved. Then, we use the following non-constructive certificate for the existence of the desired
N
vectors corresponding to queries instead of explicitly constructing them. The proof of Lemma
1
is simple and delayed to Appendix
A

Lemma 1
.

Given are vectors
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{D},
a sign vector
\sigma\in\{\pm 1\}^{n},
and some
m>0.
Then,

(\Longrightarrow)

Suppose that there exists a unit vector
U
with the following property. Whenever
\sigma_{i}=1,
it holds that
\langle U,V_{i}\rangle\geq m
and whenever
\sigma_{i}=-1,
it holds that
\langle U,V_{i}\rangle\leq-m.
Then, for every
x\in\mathsf{conv}(\{\sigma_{i}V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}),
it holds that
\|x\|_{2}\geq m.

(\Longleftarrow)

Conversely, suppose that
m=\min\big\{\|x\|_{2}\;:\;x\in\mathsf{conv}(\{\sigma_{i}V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n})\big\}.
Then, there exists a unit vector
U
with the following property. Whenever
\sigma_{i}=1,
it holds that
\langle U,V_{i}\rangle\geq m
and whenever
\sigma_{i}=-1,
it holds that
\langle U,V_{i}\rangle\leq-m.

We show that this property is satisfied by the (convex combinations of)
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
after the JL-projection via Maurey’s Approximation Lemma (Theorem

). The idea for using Maurey’s approximation lemma originates from the literature on compressed sensing

Part 2: Impossibility Results.
We next prove that the dimension bound in Theorem
1.4
is tight for the
S_{n,k}
model. In the maximal margin regime
m=\Theta(1/\sqrt{k})
, Theorem
1.3
of
70
gives the lower bound
d=\Omega(k\log(n/k)/\log(k))
while Theorem
1.4
gives an upper bound of
d=O(k\log(n))
. There is still a

gap between these upper and lower bounds. We close this gap with the following theorem, which we prove in Section

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
n\geq 3k
and

\mathsf{m}^{rd}(d,S_{n,k})>0.

For some universal constant
C>0,

d\geq C\frac{k\log(n/k)}{\log(1+2(\mathsf{m}^{rd}(d,S_{n,k})\sqrt{k})^{-1})}.

We conclude that the minimal dimension required to obtain margin
m=\Theta(1/\sqrt{k})
is exactly
\Theta(k\log(n/k)),
thus fully resolving the
S_{n,k}
case
70
. Theorem
1.5
can be adapted to general

-sparse

but the resulting bound is more complex. See Appendix
F
.

We briefly compare the proof of our Theorem
1.5
with the proof of the weaker
d=\Omega(k\log(n/k)/\log(1/m))
lower bound of
70

70

are a margin

then the vectors
\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
form a radius-

Our proof also relies on a volume argument. However, instead of directly applying it on the query embeddings
\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
or document embeddings

we pursue an idea akin to the proof of Theorem
1.4
. We non-constructively show the existence of a collection of vectors which also has size
\exp(\Omega(k\log(n/k)))
but is a packing with a much higher radius of
\Omega(m\sqrt{k}).
This collection is formed by linear combinations of the vectors
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and again crucially relies on Lemma

We prove a separate result which establishes a smooth trade-off between dimension and margin. It is based on self-Khatri-Rao lifts

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose that

r\coloneqq\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{8d-7}-1}{2}\rfloor=\sqrt{2d}+O(1).
Then,

\mathsf{m}^{rd}(d,A)\geq\frac{\Delta}{2(\sqrt{k}+1)-\Delta}

where
\Delta=\Theta\Big(\frac{r-k}{r}\times\Big(\frac{1}{(n-k)N}\Big)^{\frac{2}{r-k}}\Big).

The proof is in Section
4
. We illustrate the bound with the following cases when
N=\binom{n}{k},A=S_{n,k}.

1.

If
d\longrightarrow+\infty,
as
r=\sqrt{2d}(1+o_{d}(1)),
it follows that
\Delta\longrightarrow 1.
We recover the fact
\mathsf{m}^{rd}(d,S_{n,k})\geq\frac{1+o_{k}(1)}{2\sqrt{k}}.
Note, however, that this construction requires
d=\Omega(k^{2}\log^{2}n)
for margin
\Theta(1/\sqrt{k})
even though we know that dimension
\Theta(k\log n)
is sufficient.

2.

If
d=\frac{k^{2}}{2}(1+\alpha)^{2}
for some
\alpha>0,
we get

\mathsf{m}^{rd}(\frac{k^{2}}{2}(1+\alpha)^{2},S_{n,k})\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}n^{-\frac{2(k+1)}{\alpha k}}.
Thus, even for growing

we obtain any inverse polynomial margin in dimension
\Theta(k^{2}).

We need the following two classical results to prove Theorem

Theorem 2.1

(Johnson-Lindenstrauss Lemma
37
)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{D}
is a set of
n

\epsilon>0.
Then, there exists a matrix

(Maurey’s Lemma,
53
)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{Y}=\mathsf{conv}(\{Y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n})
for some points
\{Y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
of norm at most 1. Let
T\in\mathbb{N}
and let
x
be any point inside
\mathcal{Y}.
Then, there exist (not necessarily different) indices

such that

\|x-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=1}^{T}Y_{i_{j}}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Without loss of generality, suppose that
n\leq N.
Otherwise, we can argue about
A^{T}
instead of
A.

Suppose that there exist unit vectors
\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
and
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of some dimension
D
such that
(-1)^{A_{ji}+1}\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle\geq m
for every
j\in[N],i\in[n].
By Lemma
1
, for every
j\in[N]
and

x\in\mathcal{X}_{j}\coloneqq\mathsf{conv}(\{(-1)^{A_{ji}+1}V_{i}\}),
it holds that
\|x\|_{2}\geq m.
In particular, for some
T
that we will choose later,
\|x\|_{2}\geq m
holds for every
x
in the following set:

\mathcal{Z}_{T}\coloneqq\Big\{\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\ell=1}^{T}(-1)^{A_{j,i_{\ell}}+1}V_{i_{\ell}}\;:\;j\in[N],i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{T}\in[n]\Big\}.

Now, observe that
|\mathcal{Z}_{T}|\leq(2n)^{T}.
Indeed, this holds, since there are
n^{T}
choices for
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{T}\in[n]
and at most
2^{T}
choices for the signs
(-1)^{A_{j,i_{\ell}}+1}.

We will apply the dimensionality reduction Theorem
2.1
to
\mathcal{Z_{T}}.
Let
T=\frac{128}{m^{2}\epsilon^{2}}
so that
T\log n=\Theta(\epsilon^{-2}m^{-2}\log n),

as desired.
By Theorem
2.1
, there exists some
\Pi\in\mathbb{R}^{O(\epsilon^{-4}m^{-2}\log n)\times D}
such that for every
v\in\mathcal{Z}_{T},
it holds that

(1-\epsilon/2)^{2}\|v\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|\Pi v\|^{2}_{2}\leq\|v\|^{2}_{2}.

Define
\widetilde{V_{i}}\coloneqq\Pi V_{i}.
For every
i,
at least one of
V_{i}
and
-V_{i}
belongs to
\mathcal{Z}_{T}
(take
i=i_{1}=\cdots=i_{T}
). Hence,
1-\epsilon/2\leq\|\widetilde{V}_{i}\|_{2}\leq 1.
To obtain the final
V
-vectors, we will simply normalize.

Before that, we demonstrate the existence of vectors
\widetilde{U}_{j}
for
\{\widetilde{V}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}

via Lemma
1
. Concretely, take some
j\in[N]
and consider
\widetilde{X_{j}}=\mathsf{conv}(\{(-1)^{A_{ji}+1}\widetilde{V_{i}}\}).
Let
\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{X_{j}}.
It is enough to show that
\|\widetilde{x}\|_{2}\geq(1-\epsilon)m.
To this end, we use the JL Lemma on
\mathcal{Z}_{T}
and Theorem
2.2
. Namely, by
2.2
, there exist some indices
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{T}
such that

\Big\|\widetilde{x}-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\ell=1}^{T}(-1)^{A_{j,i_{\ell}}+1}\widetilde{V_{i_{\ell}}}\Big\|_{2}\leq T^{-1/2}\leq m\epsilon/2.

On the other hand, by the guarantees of Theorem
2.1
,

\Big\|\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\ell=1}^{T}(-1)^{A_{j,i_{\ell}}+1}\widetilde{V_{i_{\ell}}}\Big\|_{2}=\Big\|\Pi\Big(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\ell=1}^{T}(-1)^{A_{j,i_{\ell}}+1}V_{i_{\ell}}\Big)\Big\|_{2}\geq(1-\epsilon/2)\Big\|\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\ell=1}^{T}(-1)^{A_{j,i_{\ell}}+1}V_{i_{\ell}}\Big\|_{2}.

Finally, as
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\ell=1}^{T}(-1)^{A_{j,i_{\ell}}+1}V_{i_{\ell}}\in\mathcal{X}_{j},
we know that
\|\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\ell=1}^{T}(-1)^{A_{j,i_{\ell}}+1}V_{i_{\ell}}\|_{2}\geq m.

Hence,

\|\widetilde{x}\|_{2}\geq\|\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\ell=1}^{T}(-1)^{A_{j,i_{\ell}}+1}\widetilde{V_{i_{\ell}}}\|_{2}-\|\widetilde{x}-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\ell=1}^{T}(-1)^{A_{j,i_{\ell}}+1}\widetilde{V_{i_{\ell}}}\|_{2}\geq m(1-\epsilon/2)-m\epsilon/2=m(1-\epsilon).

Applying Lemma

, there exist unit vectors
\{\widetilde{U}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
in dimension

such that

(-1)^{A_{ji}+1}\langle\widetilde{V_{i}},\widetilde{U_{j}}\rangle\geq m(1-\epsilon)\quad\forall i,j.
Clearly, this property remains true even if we replace
\{\widetilde{V_{i}}\}^{n}_{i=1}
by
\{\widetilde{V_{i}}/\|\widetilde{V_{i}}\|_{2}\}^{n}_{i=1}
as
\|\widetilde{V_{i}}\|_{2}\leq 1\quad\forall i.
∎

Impossibility Results: Proof of Theorem
1.5

We need the following technical ingredients toward constructing large sphere packings.

Lemma 2

(
27
)
.

Let
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
with
s<n
. Then there exists a family
\mathcal{F}
of subsets of
[n]
with the following properties. (1) Every set in
\mathcal{F}
consists of exactly
s
elements. (2) For all
T,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
with
T\neq T^{\prime}
,
|T\cap T^{\prime}|<\frac{s}{2}.
(3)
|\mathcal{F}|\geq(\frac{n}{4s})^{\frac{s}{2}}.

Lemma 3
.

Suppose that
n\geq 3k.
Let
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
,
\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
be any dimension-
d
margin-
m
embedding of
S_{n,k}.
Then, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\|x\|_{0}\leq k
, it holds that

\|Vx\|_{2}\geq m\|x\|_{1}.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Lemma
1
. By homogeneity, it is enough to prove the fact when
\|x\|_{1}=1.
Now, take some
j
such that
(-1)^{A_{ji}+1}
and
x_{i}
have the same sign whenever
x_{i}\neq 0.
Then,
Vx\in\mathsf{conv}(\{(-1)^{A_{ji}+1}V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n})
and the result follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
V
be as in Lemma
3
. We first show that
m\sqrt{k}\leq 1.
We already know even the sharper inequality
m\leq\frac{1+o_{k}(1)}{2\sqrt{k}}

from Corollary
1
but will nevertheless give a proof as the proof technique will be useful later on.
Fix any set
T\subset[n]
with
|T|=k
, and let
\epsilon
be a uniformly random vector in
\{\pm 1\}^{T}.
Define
x=\sum_{i\in T}\epsilon_{i}e_{i}
. By Lemma
3
, we have

\|Vx\|_{2}=\|\sum_{i\in T}\epsilon_{i}V_{i}\|_{2}\geq m\|x\|_{1}=mk.

On the other hand,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\|\sum_{i\in T}\epsilon_{i}V_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{i\in T}\|V_{i}\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,\ell\in T\\
i\neq\ell\end{subarray}}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{\ell}\langle V_{i},V_{\ell}\rangle\Big]=\sum_{i\in T}\|V_{i}\|_{2}^{2}=k,

where we used that
\epsilon_{i}
and
\epsilon_{\ell}
are independently chosen from
\pm 1
for
i\neq\ell
. Thus, there exists an
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{T}
such that
\|\sum_{i\in T}\epsilon_{i}V_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\leq k
. Combining with
\|\sum_{i\in T}\epsilon_{i}V_{i}\|_{2}\geq mk
, we get
m^{2}k^{2}\leq k
, and therefore
m\sqrt{k}\leq 1
. Assume
k\geq 8
as otherwise the theorem holds after decreasing
C
.

Now, we will construct the large sphere packing. Set
s\coloneqq\lfloor\frac{k}{8}\rfloor
. By Lemma
2
there exists a set
\mathcal{F}\subset\binom{[n]}{s}
such that for any
T\neq T^{\prime}
with
T,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
,
|T\cap T^{\prime}|<\frac{s}{2}
and
|\mathcal{F}|\geq(\frac{n}{4s})^{\frac{s}{2}}
.

For a
T\in\mathcal{F}
, define the sign vector
\sigma^{T}\in\{\pm 1\}^{T}
such that
\|\sum_{i\in T}\sigma_{i}^{T}V_{i}\|_{2}
is minimized. Denote
y_{T}\coloneqq\sum_{i\in T}\sigma_{i}^{T}V_{i}.

By the same argument as above,
\|y_{T}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\sum_{i\in T}\sigma_{i}^{T}V_{i}\|^{2}_{2}\leq s.
In other words,
y_{T}\in B_{d}(0,\sqrt{s})
for all
T\in\mathcal{F}
where
B_{d}(0,r)
is the
L_{2}
ball of radius
r
centered at 0.

Define
x_{T}\coloneqq\sum_{i\in T}\sigma_{i}^{T}e_{i}
so that
Vx_{T}=y_{T}
. Consider any
T,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
with
T\neq T^{\prime}
. Since
|T\cap T^{\prime}|<\frac{s}{2}
and
s\leq\frac{k}{8}
, it follows that
\|x_{T}-x_{T^{\prime}}\|_{0}\leq k
. Hence, by Lemma
3
we get

\|V(x_{T}-x_{T^{\prime}})\|_{2}=\|Vx_{T}-Vx_{T^{\prime}}\|_{2}\geq m\|x_{T}-x_{T^{\prime}}\|_{1}.

Moreover, as every coordinate of
x_{T},x_{T^{\prime}}
is in
\{0,\pm 1\},

\|x_{T}-x_{T^{\prime}}\|_{1}\geq|T\triangle T^{\prime}|=|T|+|T^{\prime}|-2|T\cap T^{\prime}|>2s-s=s.

Combining the last two inequalities, we obtain

\|y_{T}-y_{T^{\prime}}\|_{2}=\|V(x_{T}-x_{T^{\prime}})\|_{2}=\|Vx_{T}-Vx_{T^{\prime}}\|_{2}\geq m\|x_{T}-x_{T^{\prime}}\|_{1}\geq ms.

Thus, the vectors
\{y_{T}\}_{T\in\mathcal{F}}
are pairwise
ms
separated in the Euclidean ball
B_{d}(0,\sqrt{s})
. So, the vectors
\tilde{y}_{T}\coloneqq\frac{y_{T}}{\sqrt{s}}
are pairwise
m\sqrt{s}
separated in the ball
B_{d}(0,1)
. By a standard volume bound (for example
(
66
, Corollary 4.2.11)
), it follows that

|\mathcal{F}|\leq(1+\frac{2}{m\sqrt{s}})^{d}.
Using
|\mathcal{F}|\geq(\frac{n}{4s})^{\frac{s}{2}},

(\frac{n}{4s})^{\frac{s}{2}}\leq|\mathcal{F}|\leq(1+\frac{2}{m\sqrt{s}})^{d}\implies d\log(1+\frac{2}{m\sqrt{s}})\geq\frac{s}{2}\log{\frac{n}{4s}}.

By compactness, there exists an embedding attaining margin
\mathsf{m}^{rd}(d,S_{n,k})
. Taking
m=\mathsf{m}^{rd}(d,S_{n,k})
in the preceding argument and using
s=\Theta(k)
, the conclusion follows.
∎

Construction: Proof of Theorem
1.6

The construction will be based on lifts of random spherical vectors. To this end, we will need the following standard facts about the distribution of random spherical vectors.

Fact 1

(
29
)
.

Let
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})
be a uniformly random vector on
\mathbb{S}^{r-1}.
Then, for any
1\leq s\leq r,
the distribution of
z_{1}^{2}+\ldots+z_{s}^{2}
is the distribution
\mathsf{Beta}(s/2,(r-s)/2)
with density

f_{s/2,(r-s)/2}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{r}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{s}{2})\Gamma(\frac{r-s}{2})}x^{\frac{s}{2}-1}(1-x)^{\frac{r-s}{2}-1}.

An immediate corollary is the following tail estimate which we prove in Appendix
B
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
X\sim\mathsf{Beta}(s/2,(r-s)/2)
for some
2\leq s\leq r-2
and
\delta\in(0,1].
Then,

\mathbb{P}[X\leq\delta]\leq\frac{\Gamma(\frac{r}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{s}{2})\Gamma(\frac{r-s}{2})}\delta^{s/2}.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
r=\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{8d-7}-1}{2}\rfloor
so that
d\geq\frac{r(r+1)}{2}+1
and
r\geq k+2.
Define the map
\mathsf{vec}:\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{r(r+1)/2}
as the vector
\mathsf{vec}(M)
with entries
(M_{ii})_{i=1}^{r}
and
(\sqrt{2}M_{ij})_{i<j}.
We use the fact that for two symmetric matrices
M_{1},M_{2},
it holds that
\langle\mathsf{vec}(M_{1}),\mathsf{vec}(M_{2})\rangle=\langle M_{1},M_{2}\rangle_{F}.

Now, we can get to the actual construction. Let
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}
be i.i.d. vectors uniformly drawn from
\mathbb{S}^{r-1}.
Let
\widetilde{V}_{i}=\mathsf{vec}(a_{i}a_{i}^{T}).
Let
P_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
be the symmetric matrix projecting onto
\mathsf{span}(\{a_{i}\;:\;A_{ji}=1\}).
Let
\widetilde{U}_{j}=\mathsf{vec}(P_{j}).
Note that for every
i,j,

\|\widetilde{V}_{i}\|_{2}=\|a_{i}a_{i}^{T}\|_{F}=1
and

\|\widetilde{U}_{j}\|_{2}=\|P_{j}\|_{F}=\sqrt{k}
as it is a projection matrix on a subspace of dimension
k.
The actual
U_{j},V_{i}
that we will construct are simple linear transformations of
\widetilde{U_{j}},\widetilde{V_{i}}.
First, however, we analyze the inner products between the constructed
\widetilde{U}_{j}
and
\widetilde{V}_{i}.

If
A_{ji}=1,
then
\langle\widetilde{V}_{i},\widetilde{U}_{j}\rangle=\langle P_{j},a_{i}a_{i}^{T}\rangle=\langle P_{j}a_{i},a_{i}\rangle=\langle a_{i},a_{i}\rangle=1
as
a_{i}\in\mathsf{span}(\{a_{i}\;:\;A_{ji}=1\}).

If
A_{ji}=0,

\langle\widetilde{V}_{i},\widetilde{U}_{j}\rangle=\langle P_{j},a_{i}a_{i}^{T}\rangle=\langle P_{j}a_{i},a_{i}\rangle=\langle P_{j}a_{i},P_{j}a_{i}\rangle=\|P_{j}a_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\sim\mathsf{Beta}(k/2,(r-k)/2)
by Fact
1
since
P_{j}
is a projection to a
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{r}
independent of
a_{i}.
Therefore,
1-\|P_{j}a_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\sim\mathsf{Beta}((r-k)/2,k/2).

Now, let
\Delta=\Bigg(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{k}{2})\Gamma(\frac{r-k}{2})}{2N(n-k)\Gamma(\frac{r}{2})}\Bigg)^{\frac{2}{r-k}}.
By Proposition
1
,

\mathbb{P}[\langle\widetilde{V}_{i},\widetilde{U}_{j}\rangle\geq 1-\Delta]=\mathbb{P}[1-\|P_{j}a_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\Delta]\leq\frac{\Gamma(\frac{r}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{k}{2})\Gamma(\frac{r-k}{2})}\Delta^{\frac{r-k}{2}}=\frac{1}{2N(n-k)}.

By union bound, it follows that there exists a realization of
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}
such that

\langle\widetilde{V}_{i},\widetilde{U}_{j}\rangle\leq 1-\Delta
for every
j,i
such that
A_{ji}=0.

The final construction in dimension
\frac{r(r+1)}{2}+1\leq d
is formed by setting
\beta=\sqrt{\frac{2\sqrt{k}}{2(\sqrt{k}+1)-\Delta}}
and

\displaystyle V_{i}=(\beta\widetilde{V}_{i},\sqrt{1-\beta^{2}}),U_{j}=(\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{k}}\widetilde{U}_{j},-\sqrt{1-\beta^{2}}).

As
\|\widetilde{V}_{i}\|_{2}=1,\|\widetilde{U}_{j}\|_{2}=\sqrt{k},
it follows that
\|U_{j}\|_{2}=1,\|V_{i}\|=1.
Using
\langle\widetilde{V}_{i},\widetilde{U}_{j}\rangle=1,
we can check that whenever
A_{ji}=1,

\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle=\frac{\Delta}{2(\sqrt{k}+1)-\Delta}.
Whenever
A_{ji}=0,
using that

\langle\widetilde{V}_{i},\widetilde{U}_{j}\rangle\leq 1-\Delta,
we conclude that

\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle\leq-\frac{\Delta}{2(\sqrt{k}+1)-\Delta}.

All that is left to show is the asymptotics of
\Delta.
This is a standard corollary of Stirling’s approximation which we prove for completeness in Appendix
B
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Suppose that

are real numbers. Then,

\Bigg(\frac{\Gamma(x)\Gamma(y)}{\Gamma(x+y)}\Bigg)^{1/y}=\Theta\Big(\frac{y}{x+y}\Big).

Proof.

Hence

\left(\frac{\Gamma(x)\Gamma(y)}{\Gamma(x+y)}\right)^{1/y}=\Theta\left(\frac{x^{x-\frac{1}{2}}y^{y-\frac{1}{2}}}{(x+y)^{x+y-\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{1/y}=\frac{y}{x+y}\left(1+\frac{y}{x}\right)^{-x/y}\left(\frac{x+y}{xy}\right)^{1/(2y)}.

x_{i}\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle\geq m|x_{i}|
. Since
\|U_{j}\|_{2}=1
, it follows that

\|Vx\|_{2}=\Big\|\sum_{i\in S}x_{i}V_{i}\Big\|_{2}\geq\Big\langle U_{j},\sum_{i\in S}x_{i}V_{i}\Big\rangle\geq m.\qed

Then we can use a nearly identical proof as in Theorem
1.5
.

Theorem F.1

(Lower bound for
k
-sparse matrices)
.

Let
A\in\{0,1\}^{N\times n}
be such that every row of
A
has exactly
k
ones, and let

\mathcal{C}_{A}\coloneqq\bigl\{\{i\in[n]:A_{ji}=1\}:j\in[N]\bigr\}\subseteq 2^{[n]}

denote the family of row supports. Suppose
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
form a margin-
m
embedding of
A
with
m>0
. Fix
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
and set
s=\lfloor\alpha k\rfloor
. If
s\geq 1
, then

d\geq\frac{\log|\mathcal{F}_{s}(A)|}{\log\!\left(1+\frac{2}{m\sqrt{s}}\right)},

where
\mathcal{F}_{s}(A)\subseteq\binom{[n]}{s}
is a family such that for all distinct
T,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{s}(A)
,
|T\cap T^{\prime}|<\frac{s}{2}
and
T\cup T^{\prime}
is shattered by
\mathcal{C}_{A}
.

Proof of Theorem
F.1
.

Let
V
be as in Lemma
5
. Set
s=\lfloor\alpha k\rfloor
. By definition, there exists a family
\mathcal{F}_{s}(A)\subseteq\binom{[n]}{s}
such that for any distinct
T,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{s}(A)
, we have
|T\cap T^{\prime}|<\frac{s}{2}
and
T\cup T^{\prime}
is shattered by
\mathcal{C}_{A}
.

For each
T\in\mathcal{F}_{s}(A)
, define the sign vector
\sigma^{T}\in\{\pm 1\}^{T}
so that
\left\|\sum_{i\in T}\sigma_{i}^{T}V_{i}\right\|_{2}
is minimized, and set
y_{T}\coloneqq\sum_{i\in T}\sigma_{i}^{T}V_{i}
.

To bound
\|y_{T}\|_{2}
, let
\epsilon
be uniformly random in
\{\pm 1\}^{T}
. Then

\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{i\in T}\epsilon_{i}V_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\in T}\|V_{i}\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,\ell\in T\\
i\neq\ell\end{subarray}}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{\ell}\langle V_{i},V_{\ell}\rangle\right]=\sum_{i\in T}\|V_{i}\|_{2}^{2}=s,

since the cross terms vanish in expectation. Because
\sigma^{T}
minimizes the norm, it follows that

\|y_{T}\|_{2}^{2}=\Bigl\|\sum_{i\in T}\sigma_{i}^{T}V_{i}\Bigr\|_{2}^{2}\leq s.

Hence
y_{T}\in B_{d}(0,\sqrt{s})
for all
T\in\mathcal{F}_{s}(A)
.

Now define
x_{T}\coloneqq\sum_{i\in T}\sigma_{i}^{T}e_{i}
so that
Vx_{T}=y_{T}
. Consider distinct
T,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{s}(A)
. Since
\operatorname{supp}(x_{T}-x_{T^{\prime}})\subseteq T\cup T^{\prime}
and
T\cup T^{\prime}
is shattered by
\mathcal{C}_{A}
, Lemma
5
gives

\|V(x_{T}-x_{T^{\prime}})\|_{2}\geq m\|x_{T}-x_{T^{\prime}}\|_{1}.

Moreover, since every coordinate of
x_{T}
and
x_{T^{\prime}}
lies in
\{0,\pm 1\}
,

\|x_{T}-x_{T^{\prime}}\|_{1}\geq|T\triangle T^{\prime}|=|T|+|T^{\prime}|-2|T\cap T^{\prime}|>2s-s=s.

Therefore,

\|y_{T}-y_{T^{\prime}}\|_{2}=\|Vx_{T}-Vx_{T^{\prime}}\|_{2}=\|V(x_{T}-x_{T^{\prime}})\|_{2}\geq m\|x_{T}-x_{T^{\prime}}\|_{1}\geq ms.

Thus, the vectors
\{y_{T}\}_{T\in\mathcal{F}_{s}(A)}
are pairwise
ms
-separated in the ball
B_{d}(0,\sqrt{s})
. When we rescale by
\tilde{y}_{T}\coloneqq y_{T}/\sqrt{s}
, we obtain a collection of points in
B_{d}(0,1)
that are pairwise
m\sqrt{s}
-separated. By the standard volume bound (for example
[
66
, Corollary 4.2.11]
),

|\mathcal{F}_{s}(A)|\leq\left(1+\frac{2}{m\sqrt{s}}\right)^{d}.

So, it immediately follows that when
s=\lfloor\alpha k\rfloor
, we have

d\geq\frac{\log|\mathcal{F}_{\lfloor\alpha k\rfloor}(A)|}{\log\left(1+\frac{2}{m\sqrt{\lfloor\alpha k\rfloor}}\right)}

which concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4

(Compositional Generalization and Margin)
.

Suppose
\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
,
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
form a margin-
m
embedding in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of
S_{n,k}
with
0<m<1
. Then for all
T\subset[n]
with
k\leq|T|\leq\min(k+\frac{2km}{1-m},n-1)
, there exists an
h\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all
i\in T,

it holds that

\langle h,V_{i}\rangle\geq c
while for all
j\not\in T,

\langle h,V_{j}\rangle\leq c.

Proof of Proposition
4
.

For a set
S\subseteq[n]
with
|S|=k
, let
U_{S}
denote the vector such that
\langle U_{S},V_{i}\rangle\geq m
for all
i\in S
and
\langle U_{S},V_{j}\rangle\leq-m
for all
j\notin S
. Define

h_{T}\coloneqq\sum_{S\subseteq T,\ |S|=k}U_{S}.

Then

\displaystyle\langle h_{T},V_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum_{S\subseteq T,\ i\in S,\ |S|=k}U_{S}+\sum_{S\subseteq T,\ i\notin S,\ |S|=k}U_{S},V_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\subseteq T,\ i\in S,\ |S|=k}\langle U_{S},V_{i}\rangle+\sum_{S\subseteq T,\ i\notin S,\ |S|=k}\langle U_{S},V_{i}\rangle.

Notice that if
i\in S
, then by definition
\langle U_{S},V_{i}\rangle\geq m
. If
i\notin S
, then since
U_{S}
and
V_{i}
have unit norm, we have
\langle U_{S},V_{i}\rangle\geq-1
.

Now fix any
i\in T
. Then there are exactly

\binom{|T|-1}{k-1}
sets of size
k
containing
i
and contained in
T
. Also, there are exactly
\binom{|T|}{k}-\binom{|T|-1}{k-1}
sets of size
k
contained in
T
that do not contain
i
.

So it follows that

\displaystyle\langle h_{T},V_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\subseteq T,\ i\in S,\ |S|=k}\langle U_{S},V_{i}\rangle+\sum_{S\subseteq T,\ i\notin S,\ |S|=k}\langle U_{S},V_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq m\binom{|T|-1}{k-1}+(-1)\left(\binom{|T|}{k}-\binom{|T|-1}{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=(1+m)\binom{|T|-1}{k-1}-\binom{|T|}{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+m)k}{|T|}\binom{|T|}{k}-\binom{|T|}{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+m)k-|T|}{|T|}\binom{|T|}{k}.

Furthermore, if
i\notin T
, then
\langle U_{S},V_{i}\rangle\leq-m
for all
S\subseteq T
with
|S|=k
. Since there are
\binom{|T|}{k}
subsets of size
k
contained in
T
, we get

\langle h_{T},V_{i}\rangle=\sum_{S\subseteq T,\ |S|=k}\langle U_{S},V_{i}\rangle\leq-m\binom{|T|}{k}.

We also know that, since
m<1
,

\displaystyle k+\frac{2km}{1-m}\geq|T|

\displaystyle\implies(1-m)k+2mk\geq|T|(1-m)

\displaystyle\implies(1+m)k-|T|\geq-m|T|

\displaystyle\implies\frac{(1+m)k-|T|}{|T|}\geq-m.

Therefore, for every
i\in T
and
j\notin T
, we have

\langle h_{T},V_{i}\rangle\geq\frac{(1+m)k-|T|}{|T|}\binom{|T|}{k}\geq-m\binom{|T|}{k}\geq\langle h_{T},V_{j}\rangle.

Therefore, there exists a real constant
c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\langle h_{T},V_{i}\rangle\geq c^{\prime}\geq\langle h_{T},V_{j}\rangle\qquad\text{for all }i\in T\text{ and }j\notin T.

Notice that
\|h_{T}\|\neq 0
because, since
|T|<n
, for some
i\notin T
we have
\langle V_{i},h_{T}\rangle\leq-m\binom{|T|}{k}<0
. So, if we set
h\coloneqq h_{T}/\|h_{T}\|
, it follows that there exists an
h\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
c=c^{\prime}/\|h_{T}\|\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\langle h,V_{i}\rangle\geq c\qquad\forall i\in T,\quad\text{and}\quad\langle h,V_{j}\rangle\leq c\qquad\forall j\notin T.\qed

We note that even a small margin already suffices to force downward generalization. Indeed, once
m\geq\frac{k-1}{2n-k-1}
, the following proposition implies that every nonempty subset with
1\leq|T|\leq k
is retrievable. This is related to the notion of a
k
-neighborly polytope
[
32
]
: if
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
forms a
k
-neighborly polytope, then every subset of at most
k
vertices forms a face and is therefore exposed by a supporting hyperplane. Thus, every such subset can be separated from the remaining vertices and hence can be retrieved.

Proposition 5

(Downward Generalization and Margin)
.

Suppose
\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
,
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
form a margin-
m
embedding in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of
S_{n,k}
with
0<m<1
. Then for all
T\subset[n]
with
\max\{1,k-\frac{2m(n-k)}{1-m}\}\leq|T|\leq k
, there exists an
h\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all
i\in T,

it holds that

\langle h,V_{i}\rangle\geq c
while for all
j\not\in T,

\langle h,V_{j}\rangle\leq c.

Proof of Proposition
5
.

The case
|T|=k
is immediate by taking
h=U_{T}
and
c=0
, so assume
|T|<k
.

We use the same notation as in the proof of Proposition
4
. Define

h_{T}\coloneqq\sum_{S\supset T,\ |S|=k}U_{S}.

Then

\displaystyle\langle h_{T},V_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum_{S\supset T,\ i\in S,\ |S|=k}U_{S}+\sum_{S\supset T,\ i\notin S,\ |S|=k}U_{S},V_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\supset T,\ i\in S,\ |S|=k}\langle U_{S},V_{i}\rangle+\sum_{S\supset T,\ i\notin S,\ |S|=k}\langle U_{S},V_{i}\rangle.

Again, if
i\in S
, then by definition
\langle U_{S},V_{i}\rangle\geq m
.

Now fix any
i\in T
. Then there are exactly

\binom{n-|T|}{k-|T|}
sets of size
k
containing
T
, and so

\langle h_{T},V_{i}\rangle\geq m\binom{n-|T|}{k-|T|}.

For
j\notin T
, the number of sets of size
k
containing
T
and
j
is
\binom{n-|T|-1}{k-|T|-1}
, while the number of sets of size
k
containing
T
but not
j
is
\binom{n-|T|-1}{k-|T|}
. Hence, using
\langle U_{S},V_{j}\rangle\leq 1
when
j\in S
and
\langle U_{S},V_{j}\rangle\leq-m
when
j\notin S
, we have

\langle h_{T},V_{j}\rangle\leq\binom{n-|T|-1}{k-|T|-1}-m\binom{n-|T|-1}{k-|T|}.

Thus it suffices to show

\binom{n-|T|-1}{k-|T|-1}-m\binom{n-|T|-1}{k-|T|}\leq m\binom{n-|T|}{k-|T|}.

Since

\binom{n-|T|}{k-|T|}=\binom{n-|T|-1}{k-|T|-1}+\binom{n-|T|-1}{k-|T|},

this inequality is equivalent to

m\geq\frac{\binom{n-|T|-1}{k-|T|-1}}{\binom{n-|T|-1}{k-|T|-1}+2\binom{n-|T|-1}{k-|T|}}=\frac{k-|T|}{k-|T|+2(n-k)}\iff|T|\geq k-\frac{2m(n-k)}{1-m}.

Therefore, for every
i\in T
and
j\notin T
,

\langle h_{T},V_{i}\rangle\geq c^{\prime}\coloneqq m\binom{n-|T|}{k-|T|}\geq\langle h_{T},V_{j}\rangle.

Since
c^{\prime}>0
, we have
h_{T}\neq 0
. Taking
h=\frac{h_{T}}{\|h_{T}\|}
and
c=\frac{c^{\prime}}{\|h_{T}\|}
completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 6

(Robustness and Margin)
.

Suppose that the object space
\mathcal{O}
and the query space
\mathcal{Q}
are metric spaces with metrics
d_{\mathcal{O}}
and
d_{\mathcal{Q}},
respectively. Suppose that the corresponding encoders
f_{\theta}
and
g_{\phi}
have relative-bias-
\tau
and margin
m
on the datasets
\{Q_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{Q}
and
\{O_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\mathcal{O}
with relevance matrix
A\in\{0,1\}^{N\times n}
. If
f_{\theta}
and
g_{\phi}
are both
L
-Lipschitz for some
L>0
, then for every
j\in[N]
, every perturbation
Q_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}
satisfying

d_{\mathcal{Q}}(Q_{j}^{\prime},Q_{j})<\frac{m}{L}

preserves perfect retrieval. Namely,

\langle g_{\phi}(Q_{j}^{\prime}),f_{\theta}(O_{i})\rangle>\tau\quad\text{ if }A_{ji}=1
and

\langle g_{\phi}(Q_{j}^{\prime}),f_{\theta}(O_{i})\rangle<\tau\quad\text{if }A_{ji}=0.
Likewise, the same holds for small perturbations of the objects.

Proof of Proposition
6
.

Since
d_{\mathcal{Q}}(Q_{j}^{\prime},Q_{j})<\frac{m}{L}
and
g_{\phi}
is
L
-Lipschitz, we have

\|g_{\phi}(Q_{j}^{\prime})-g_{\phi}(Q_{j})\|\leq L\,d_{\mathcal{Q}}(Q_{j}^{\prime},Q_{j})<m.

Now fix
i\in[n]
. If
A_{ji}=1
, then since
f_{\theta}(O_{i})
has norm
1
, we have

\bigl|\langle g_{\phi}(Q_{j}^{\prime})-g_{\phi}(Q_{j}),\,f_{\theta}(O_{i})\rangle\bigr|\leq\|g_{\phi}(Q_{j}^{\prime})-g_{\phi}(Q_{j})\|\,\|f_{\theta}(O_{i})\|<m.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle g_{\phi}(Q_{j}^{\prime}),f_{\theta}(O_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle g_{\phi}(Q_{j}^{\prime})-g_{\phi}(Q_{j}),f_{\theta}(O_{i})\rangle+\langle g_{\phi}(Q_{j}),f_{\theta}(O_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle>-m+(\tau+m)=\tau.

Similarly, if
A_{ji}=0
, then

\displaystyle\langle g_{\phi}(Q_{j}^{\prime}),f_{\theta}(O_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle g_{\phi}(Q_{j}^{\prime})-g_{\phi}(Q_{j}),f_{\theta}(O_{i})\rangle+\langle g_{\phi}(Q_{j}),f_{\theta}(O_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle<m+(\tau-m)=\tau.\qed

Proposition 7

(Maximal-Margin Embeddings and Sigmoid Loss)
.

Consider the sigmoid loss with inverse-temperature
t>0
and bias
b
over embeddings
\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{sigmoid}}(\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n};t,b)\coloneqq\sum_{j\in[N],i\in[n]}\log\Big(1+\exp\big((-1)^{A_{ji}}(t\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle-b)\big)\Big).

Then

1.

If
\mathcal{L}_{\text{sigmoid}}(\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n};t,b)<\log 2,
then there exists
m>0
such that
\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a margin-
m,
relative-bias-
\frac{b}{t}
embedding of
A
.

2.

If
\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
and
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
form a margin-
m_{*}
, relative-bias-
\tau
embedding of
A
where

m_{*}\coloneqq\min\left\{\min_{A_{ji}=1}\left(\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle-\tau\right),\min_{A_{ji}=0}\bigl(\tau-\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle\bigr)\right\}>0.

Then

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\mathcal{L}_{\text{sigmoid}}(\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n};T,T\tau)=-m_{*}.

Proof of Proposition
7
.

We begin with part (1).
Suppose that
\mathcal{L}_{\text{sigmoid}}(\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n};t,b)<\log 2
. For each fixed pair
(j,i)
, the term
\log\Big(1+\exp\big((-1)^{A_{ji}}(t\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle-b)\big)\Big)
is nonnegative. So, it follows that for every fixed pair
(j,i),

\log\Bigl(1+\exp\bigl((-1)^{A_{ji}}(t\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle-b)\bigr)\Bigr)<\log 2.

Since the function
x\mapsto\log(1+e^{x})
is strictly increasing, we obtain

(-1)^{A_{ji}}(t\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle-b)<0.

If
A_{ji}=1
, then
-(t\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle-b)<0
, so
t\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle-b>0.
If
A_{ji}=0
, then
t\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle-b<0.

Since there are finitely many pairs, set

m\coloneqq\min\left\{\min_{A_{ji}=1}\left(\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle-\frac{b}{t}\right),\min_{A_{ji}=0}\left(\frac{b}{t}-\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle\right)\right\}>0.

Then
\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
form a margin-
m
, relative-bias-
\frac{b}{t}
embedding of
A
.

We now prove part 2. For each pair
(j,i)\in[N]\times[n]
, define

\gamma_{ji}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle-\tau,&A_{ji}=1,\\[4.0pt]
\tau-\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle,&A_{ji}=0.\end{cases}

Then
\gamma_{ji}>0
for all
(j,i)
, and by definition
m_{*}=\min_{j,i}\gamma_{ji}
.

If
A_{ji}=1
, then

(-1)^{A_{ji}}\bigl(T\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle-T\tau\bigr)=-\bigl(T\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle-T\tau\bigr)=-T(\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle-\tau)=-T\gamma_{ji}.

If
A_{ji}=0
, then

(-1)^{A_{ji}}\bigl(T\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle-T\tau\bigr)=T\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle-T\tau=-T(\tau-\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle)=-T\gamma_{ji}.

Therefore,

\mathcal{L}_{\text{sigmoid}}(\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n};T,T\tau)=\sum_{j\in[N],\,i\in[n]}\log\!\bigl(1+e^{-T\gamma_{ji}}\bigr).

Since
\gamma_{ji}\geq m_{*}
for all
(i,j)
, we obtain the upper bound

\mathcal{L}_{\text{sigmoid}}(\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n};T,T\tau)\leq Nn\log(1+e^{-Tm_{*}})\leq Nne^{-Tm_{*}},

where we used
\log(1+x)\leq x
for all
x\geq 0
. For the lower bound, choose
(j_{0},i_{0})
such that
\gamma_{j_{0}i_{0}}=m_{*}
. Then
\mathcal{L}_{\text{sigmoid}}(\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n};T,T\tau)\geq\log(1+e^{-Tm_{*}})
. Also, for
x\in[0,1]
,
\log(1+x)\geq\frac{x}{2}
. Hence for all sufficiently large
T
(so that
e^{-Tm_{*}}\leq 1
),
\log(1+e^{-Tm_{*}})\geq\frac{1}{2}e^{-Tm_{*}}
. Thus for all sufficiently large
T
,

\frac{1}{2}e^{-Tm_{*}}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\text{sigmoid}}(\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n};T,T\tau)\leq Nne^{-Tm_{*}}.

Taking logarithms gives

-Tm_{*}-\log 2\leq\log\mathcal{L}_{\text{sigmoid}}(\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n};T,T\tau)\leq-Tm_{*}+\log(Nn).

Dividing by
T
and letting
T\to\infty
, we conclude that

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\mathcal{L}_{\text{sigmoid}}(\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n};T,T\tau)=-m_{*}\qed

Proposition 8

(Large Margins and Quantization)
.

Suppose that
\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
,
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a margin-
m
embedding of
A
. Then there exists a codebook
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
with size
|\mathcal{C}|\leq\left(1+\frac{8}{m}\right)^{d}
such that the quantized embeddings
\tilde{U}_{j}\in\arg\min_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\|x-U_{j}\|
and
\tilde{V}_{i}\in\arg\min_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\|x-V_{i}\|
form a margin-
\frac{m}{2}

Proof.

There exists a
\frac{m}{4}
-net
\mathcal{C}
of size
|\mathcal{C}|\leq\left(1+\frac{2}{\frac{m}{4}}\right)^{d}=\left(1+\frac{8}{m}\right)^{d}

[
66
, Corollary 4.2.11]
. For any
j\in[N]
and
i\in[n]
, we have

|\langle\tilde{U}_{j},\tilde{V}_{i}\rangle-\langle U_{j},V_{i}\rangle|=|\langle\tilde{U}_{j}-U_{j},\tilde{V}_{i}\rangle+\langle U_{j},\tilde{V}_{i}-V_{i}\rangle|

because

. Therefore, the vectors
\{\tilde{U}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
,
\{\tilde{V}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
form a margin-
\frac{m}{2}
embedding of
A.

∎

(5)

1
, except that there is a different relative bias
\tau_{j}
corresponding to each query. Again, they show that the loss converges to 0 as
T\longrightarrow+\infty.

We note, however, that when
A
has rows of sparsity more than 1, the loss cannot converge to 0. In fact, for some embeddings of
A
satisfying the conditions in Definition
1
, it is possible that
\mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}}
diverges as
T\longrightarrow+\infty.

Proposition 9
.

Suppose that
A\in\{0,1\}^{N\times n}
is such that
A_{ji_{1}}=A_{ji_{2}}=1
and
\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is an embedding of
A
such that
\langle U_{j},V_{i_{1}}\rangle\neq\langle U_{j},V_{i_{2}}\rangle.
Then,

\lim_{T\longrightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}}(\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n};T)=+\infty.

Proof.

First note that each summand in (
5
) is non-negative. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}}(\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n};T)

\displaystyle\geq\log\Big(\frac{\sum_{\ell}\exp(T\langle U_{j},V_{\ell}\rangle)}{\exp(T\langle U_{j},V_{i_{1}}\rangle)}\Big)+\log\Big(\frac{\sum_{\ell}\exp(T\langle U_{j},V_{\ell}\rangle)}{\exp(T\langle U_{j},V_{i_{2}}\rangle)}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\log\frac{\exp(T\langle U_{j},V_{i_{1}}\rangle)+\exp(T\langle U_{j},V_{i_{2}}\rangle)}{\exp(T\langle U_{j},V_{i_{1}}\rangle)}+\log\frac{\exp(T\langle U_{j},V_{i_{1}}\rangle)+\exp(T\langle U_{j},V_{i_{2}}\rangle)}{\exp(T\langle U_{j},V_{i_{2}}\rangle)}

\displaystyle\geq\log\frac{\exp(T\langle U_{j},V_{i_{1}}\rangle)+\exp(T\langle U_{j},V_{i_{2}}\rangle)}{\min(\exp(T\langle U_{j},V_{i_{1}}\rangle),\exp(T\langle U_{j},V_{i_{2}}\rangle))}

\displaystyle=\log\bigg(1+\exp\Big(T|\langle U_{j},V_{i_{1}}\rangle-\langle U_{j},V_{i_{2}}\rangle|\Big)\bigg)

Taking
T\longrightarrow+\infty
shows that

\mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}}(\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n};T)\longrightarrow+\infty.

∎
