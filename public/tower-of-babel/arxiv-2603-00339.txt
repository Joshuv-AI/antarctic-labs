Laplace problem with an exponential nonlinear boundary condition
Jamel Benameur, Chokri Elhechmi and Gmar Benhenda

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish a new result for the Laplace problem with exponential Robin boundary conditions posed on the unit disk in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} . More precisely, we prove the existence and uniqueness of a solution under suitable smallness assumptions on the boundary data. Our approach relies on an iterative method combined with periodic Sobolev embedding results.

35-XX, 35A01, 35A02, 46E36, 46F05

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study an elliptic problem posed on the open unit disc
\Omega=D(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with an exponential boundary condition prescribed on a part of the boundary:

(S)\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\Delta u=0,&\;{\rm in}&\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\,u=0,&\;{\rm on}&\Gamma_{D},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=\phi(x)&\;{\rm on}&\;\Gamma_{N},\\
\vskip 8.53581pt\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}+\varphi(x)\displaystyle\left({e^{\alpha u}-e^{-(1-\alpha)u}}\right)=g(x)&\;{\rm on}&\;\Gamma_{R},\end{array}\right.

Here, the boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega=S(0,1)
is decomposed into three open subsets
\Gamma_{D}
,
\Gamma_{N}
, and
\Gamma_{R}
, modulo a negligible set, satisfying

(H1)\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Gamma=\overline{\Gamma_{D}}\cup\overline{\Gamma_{N}}\cup\overline{\Gamma_{R}},\\
\Gamma_{D}\cap\Gamma_{N}=\Gamma_{D}\cap\Gamma_{R}=\Gamma_{N}\cap\Gamma_{R}=\emptyset,\\
\sigma(\Gamma_{D})>0,\;\sigma(\Gamma_{N})>0,\;\sigma(\Gamma_{R})>0,\end{array}\right.

with
\sigma
is the superficial measure on
\partial\Omega
.

The data of the problem satisfy

\phi\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{N}),\quad\phi\not\equiv 0,\quad g\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{R}),\quad\varphi\in C^{0}(\overline{\Gamma_{R}})\text{ and }\alpha\in(0,1).

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we introduce the function
f_{\alpha}
defined by

f_{\alpha}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\frac{e^{\alpha x}-e^{-(1-\alpha)r}}{r}&\mbox{ if }r\neq 0,\\
1&\mbox{ if }r=0,\end{array}\right.

then, problem
(S)
can be rewritten as

(S)\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\Delta u=0,&\;{\rm in}&\;\Omega,\\
\displaystyle u=0,&\;{\rm on}&\;\Gamma_{D},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=\phi(x)&\;{\rm on}&\;\Gamma_{N},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}+\varphi(x)f_{\alpha}(u)u=g(x)&\;{\rm on}&\;\Gamma_{R}.\end{array}\right.

Remark 1.1
.

The function
f_{\alpha}
satisfies the following properties:

(P1)

\forall\,r\in\mathbb{R}:\;\;f_{\alpha}(r)>0
.

(P2)

\forall\,r\in\mathbb{R}:\;\;0\leq f_{\alpha}(r)r^{2}\leq 2\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\delta^{k}}{k!}|r|^{k+1}
,
where

\delta=\max(\alpha,1-\alpha)
.

(P3)

\forall\,r\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}:\;\;\leq f_{\alpha}(r)\leq 2\delta(1+\delta|r|e^{\delta|r|})
.

The proof of properties
(P1)
and
(P2)
are obtained by direct calculation, while the proof of
(P3)
is given in
Appendix A
.

A first approximation of problem
(S)
is obtained by replacing the nonlinear boundary condition on
\Gamma_{R}
with the linear Robin condition

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}+\varphi(x)u=g(x)\;{\rm on}\;\Gamma_{R},

This linear case has been studied by several authors. In particular, S. Chaabane
et al.
(
[
8
,
9
]
) established existence and uniqueness results by applying the Lax-Milgram theorem to the variational formulation associated with the problem. In addition, numerical results were provided for both the direct problem and the inverse problem of identifying the Robin coefficient.

In the case where the boundary condition on
\Gamma_{R}
is quadratic, specifically:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}+\varphi(x)u+\psi(x)u^{2}=g(x)\;{\rm on}\;\Gamma_{R}.

J. Benameur
et al.

[
5
]
established the existence of solutions in an appropriate Hilbert space
V
. Uniqueness was proved within a closed ball of
V
. These results were obtained under suitable assumptions on the functions
\varphi
and
\psi
. The proofs rely on an iterative method based on the construction of a sequence of linear boundary value problems,
which converges to the solution of the original problem with the quadratic nonlinear boundary condition.

A generalization of this study was recently proposed by C. Elhechmi
[
6
]
. The author investigated an elliptic partial differential equation subject to a polynomial Robin boundary condition, established existence and uniqueness results for the associated solutions, and developed an iterative scheme for their construction.

In this work, we investigate problem
(S)
, characterized by an exponential-type boundary condition on
\Gamma_{R}
. To establish existence and uniqueness results for solutions to problem
(S)
, we employ an iterative scheme by constructing a sequence of linear problems
(S_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
designed to approximate
(S)
. We prove that each linearized problem
(S_{k})
admits a unique solution
u_{k}
, and that the sequence
(u_{k})
converges to the unique solution of the original nonlinear problem
(S)
.

The analysis relies on the application of suitable Sobolev embedding results in the periodic setting, together with the development of specific technical estimates. It should be noted that the uniqueness result is local, established within a specific closed ball of the Hilbert space
V
, the construction of which is detailed in the following section.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present some preliminary results and state the main result of this work. In Section
3
, we define the sequence of linear problems
(S_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and prove the existence and uniqueness of a solution to each problem
(S_{k})
. In Section
4
, we provide a rigorous analysis of the convergence of the sequence of solutions
(u_{k})
to a limit
u
within the Hilbert space
V
. In Sections
5
and
6
, we establish the existence and uniqueness of a solution to problem
(S)
, respectively. Finally, in Section
7
, we derive an explicit estimation of the Sobolev embedding constants relevant to our periodic setting.

2.
Preliminaries and main result

The linear operator is defined and continuous by

\tau_{0}:H^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\partial\Omega),\;u\mapsto u_{/\partial\Omega}.

Precisely,
\tau_{0}
is continuous from
(H^{1}(\Omega),\|.\|_{H^{1}})
to
(L^{2}(\partial\Omega),\|.\|_{L^{2}})
. The image of the operator
\tau_{0}
is noted

H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
. This space is endowed by the following norm

\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}}=\inf\{\|u\|_{H^{1}}:\;u\in H^{1}(\Omega);\;u_{/\partial\Omega}=\varphi\}.

Then, we can define the following operator

\tilde{\tau}_{0}:(H^{1}(\Omega),\|.\|_{H^{1}})\rightarrow(H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega),\|.\|_{H^{1/2}}),\;u\mapsto u_{/\partial\Omega}.

Clearly,
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
is continuous and
\|\tilde{\tau}_{0}\|\leq 1
. By using this definition of the space
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
, we obtain the following embeddings. Precisely, we have the following classical result.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
11
]
) Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a Lipschitzian domain. Then, for
p\in[2,+\infty)
we have

H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\partial\Omega).

Precisely, there is a constant

C_{\Omega,p}>0
, depending only on
p
and domain
\Omega
, such that

(1)

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega,p}\|u\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)},\;\forall u\in H^{1}(\Omega).

and

(2)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega,p}\|f\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},\;\forall f\in H^{1}(\Omega).

In the present study, we extend the right-hand side of equation (
1
) to the fractional Sobolev space
H^{s}(\Gamma)
for
s\in(0,1)
, (
\Gamma
is nonempty subset of
S(0,1)
), and derive a refined estimate for the constant
C_{\Omega,p}
. To obtain precise inequalities with explicit constants, we provide upper bounds for the embedding constant in the specific case where the domain is the unit disk,
\Omega=D(0,1)
.

Specifically, we establish the following result:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
2\leq p<\infty
. The embedding
H^{1/2}(S(0,1))\hookrightarrow L^{p}(S(0,1))
is continuous. In particular, for all
f\in H^{1/2}(S(0,1))
, we have:

(3)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(S(0,1))}\leq\lambda_{p}\|f\|_{H^{1/2}(S(0,1))},

where the embedding constant
\lambda_{p}
is given by:

(4)

\lambda_{p}=\left(C_{1}\frac{p}{p-2}C_{2}^{p-2}p^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\forall p>2,

and
C_{1},C_{2}
denote some universal constants.

Remark 2.1
.

(1)

If
p\geq 3
, we can take

\lambda_{p}=\left(\mathcal{C}^{p-2}p^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
\mathcal{C}=2\max(C_{1},C_{2})
.

(2)

A rigorous proof of Proposition
2.1
is provided in
Appendix C
.

(3)

The proof of Proposition
2.1
is done in two step:

(a)

First step: We prove
H^{s}_{2\pi}\hookrightarrow L^{p}_{2\pi}
, with
\frac{1}{p}+s=\frac{1}{2}
.

(b)

Second step: We use
\|.\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}}\leq\|.\|_{H^{1/2}_{2\pi}},\;\forall 0<s<1/2
.

Now, we define the following Sobolev space
V
adapted to our case. Let
V
the space defined by

V=\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)\;{\rm such\,that}\;v=0\;{\rm on}\;\Gamma_{D}\}.

The space
V
is a Hilbert space when endowed with the inner product

\langle u,v\rangle_{V}=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u.\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x,

and the associated norm

\|u\|_{V}=\left(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Let
\tau
denote the trace operator defined by

\begin{array}[]{llll}\tau&:V&\rightarrow&H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})\\
&u&\mapsto&u_{|\Gamma_{R}}.\end{array}

The linear operator
\tau
is continuous when the space
H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})
is equipped by the norm
\|.\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{R})}
, (
2\leq p<\infty
). Also, we define the constant

\beta_{p}=\inf\big\{C>0;\;\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{R})}\leq C\|u\|_{V},\,\,\,\forall u\in V\big\}\in(0,\infty).

Remark 2.2
.

Proposition
2.1
plays a crucial role in the analysis of the nonlinear exponential boundary condition on
\Gamma_{R}
. Indeed, the embedding

H^{1/2}\bigl(S(0,1)\bigr)\hookrightarrow L^{p}\bigl(S(0,1)\bigr),\qquad 2\leq p<\infty,

together with the continuity of the trace operator

\tau:V\to H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})
, implies that for every
u\in V
and every

p\geq 2
,

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{R})}\leq\beta_{p}\,\|u\|_{V},

where the constant
\beta_{p}
can be explicitly controlled in terms of
\lambda_{p}
.

This estimate allows us to control polynomial growth terms on the boundary. More importantly, it provides a fundamental tool for handling the exponential nonlinearity appearing in the Robin boundary condition:

\varphi(x)\left(e^{\alpha u}-e^{-(1-\alpha)u}\right).

Dependence of the constant
\beta_{p}
on
\lambda_{p}
:
Recall that the constant
\beta_{p}
is defined by

\beta_{p}=\inf\left\{C>0\;:\;\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|u\|_{V},\ \forall u\in V\right\}.

The continuity of the operators

\tau:V\longrightarrow H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R}),\;u\longmapsto u_{/\Gamma_{R}},

and

H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Gamma_{R}),\qquad 2\leq p<\infty,

implies that, for all
p>2

(5)

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{R})}\leq\lambda_{p}\|u\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})}\leq\lambda_{p}\|\tau\|\|u\|_{V}.

Therefore, the constant
\beta_{p}
satisfies the estimate

(6)

\beta_{p}\leq\lambda_{p}\mathcal{T},

where:

•

\mathcal{T}=\|\tau\|
denotes the norm of the trace operator

\tau:V\to H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{R})
.

By the definition
\|.\|_{H^{1/2}}
, we get
\|\mathcal{T}\|\leq 1
, which implies
\beta_{p}\leq\lambda_{p}
.

•

\lambda_{p}
is the constant given explicitly in Proposition
2.1
.

Before stating the main result of this paper, it is necessary to provide some definitions and conditions:

\bullet

M_{0}=\beta_{2}(\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})})
.

\bullet

We denote by
\Phi_{ad}
the set of admissible coefficient:

(7)

\Phi_{ad}=\left\{h\in C^{0}(\overline{\Gamma_{R}});\;\;0\leq h\leq{\xi}\Lambda\right\}.

where the parameter
\xi\in(0,1)
, and

(8)

\Lambda=\bigg[\beta_{3}^{3}M_{0}+\beta_{4}^{4}M_{0}^{2}+(CM_{0})^{3}R(CM_{0})\bigg]^{-1}.

Note that the constant
C
, and the entire function
R
will be defined in the proof of Lemma
4.1
.

We are now in a position to state the main result of this article.

Theorem 2.1
.

Problem
(S)
admits a solution
u\in V
. Moreover, this solution is unique in the closed ball
\overline{B_{V}(0,M_{0})}=\{v\in V\,:\,\|v\|_{V}\leq M_{0}\}
.

3.
Study of approximation system

To approximate the solution of the nonlinear problem
(S)
, we define the following iterative scheme. For each
k\geq 1
, let
(S_{k})
be the linear problem defined by:

(S_{k})\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\Delta u=0,&{\rm in}&\;\Omega,\\
\displaystyle u=0,&{\rm on}&\;\Gamma_{D},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=\phi(x)&{\rm on}&\;\Gamma_{N},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}+\varphi(x)f_{\alpha}(u_{k-1})u=g(x)&{\rm on}&\;\Gamma_{R}.\end{array}\right.

with the initial iteration
u_{0}=0
.

Referring to
[
9
]
, we have the following result

Lemma 3.1
.

For
k=1
, the linear problem
(S_{1})
admits a unique solution
u_{1}\in V
. Furthermore, this solution is bounded in the
V
-norm by
M_{0}
:

(9)

\|u_{1}\|_{V}\leq M_{0},

where
M_{0}=\beta_{2}\left(\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})}\right)
.

In what follows, we state the main result of this section. Precisely, in this proposition we establish the well-posed-ness of the iterative sequence and the uniform bounded-ness of its solutions.

Proposition 3.1
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the linear problem
(S_{k})
admits a unique solution
u_{k}\in V
. Furthermore, the sequence
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
V
, satisfying:

(10)

\|u_{k}\|_{V}\leq M_{0},\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},

where
M_{0}
is the constant defined in Lemma
3.1
.

Proof.
The proof relies on the variational formulation of problem
(S_{k})
and the application of the Lax-Milgram Theorem.

The problem
(S_{k})
is formally equivalent to the following variational problem:

(\tilde{S}_{k})\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\mbox{Find }u\in V\mbox{ such that}\\
\\
B_{\alpha,k}(u,v)=L(v)\,\,\forall v\in V,\end{array}\right.

where

B_{\alpha,k}(u,v)=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma_{R}}\varphi(x)f_{\alpha}(u_{k-1})uv\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,

and

L(v)=\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\phi v\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\int_{\Gamma_{R}}gv\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

To establish the existence of a unique solution to this variational problem, we apply the Lax-Milgram theorem. This requires verifying that the bilinear form
B_{\alpha,k}
is both continuous and coercive on
V\times V
, and that the linear form
L
is continuous on
V
.

Coercivity of
B_{\alpha,k}
:
For
u\in V
, we have

\begin{array}[]{llll}B_{\alpha,k}(u,u)&=&\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma_{R}}\varphi(x)f_{\alpha}(u_{k-1})u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\\
&=&\|u\|_{V}^{2}+\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{R}}\varphi(x)f_{\alpha}(u_{k-1})u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.\end{array}

Since the positivity of the functions
\varphi
and
f_{\alpha}
, we get

B_{\alpha,k}(u,u)\geq\|u\|_{V}^{2}.

Continuity of
B_{\alpha,k}
:
Let
(u,v)\in V^{2}
.

By applying the triangle inequality and the
L^{\infty}
bound on
\varphi
, we have:

\begin{array}[]{lllllllll}|B_{\alpha,k}(u,v)|&\leq&\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\Gamma_{R}})}\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{R}}f_{\alpha}(u_{k-1})|u||v|\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.\end{array}

Using property
(P3)
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lllllllll}|B_{\alpha,k}(u,v)|&\leq&\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{V}+2\delta\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\Gamma_{R}})}\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{R}}(1+\delta|u_{k-1}|e^{\delta|u_{k-1}|})|u||v|\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\\
&\leq&\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{V}+2\delta\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\Gamma_{R}})}\bigg[\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})}+\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{R}}\delta|u_{k-1}||u||v|e^{\delta|u_{k-1}|}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\bigg].\\
\end{array}

Applying Hölder’s inequality to the integral term yields:

\begin{array}[]{lllllllll}|B_{\alpha,k}(u,v)|&\leq&\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{V}+2\delta\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\Gamma_{R}})}\bigg[\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})}+\\
&&\|u\|_{L^{3}(\Gamma_{R})}\|v\|_{L^{3}(\Gamma_{R})}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{R}}(\delta|u_{k-1}|)^{3}e^{3\delta|u_{k-1}|}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\bigg)^{1/3}\bigg].\\
\end{array}

Let

I_{k}=\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{R}}|u_{k-1}|^{3}e^{3\delta|u_{k-1}|}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

Using the power series expansion of the exponential function, we have

\begin{array}[]{lllllllll}I_{k}&=&\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{R}}|u_{k-1}|^{3}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(3\delta)^{n}}{n!}|u_{k-1}|^{n}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\\
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Gamma_{R}}\frac{(3\delta)^{n}}{n!}|u_{k-1}|^{3+n}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\\
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(3\delta)^{n}}{n!}\|u_{k-1}\|_{L^{3+n}(\Gamma_{R})}^{3+n}.\end{array}

Using the inequalities (
5
,
6
) and the uniform bound
\|u_{k-1}\|_{V}\leq M_{0}
, we obtain

\|u_{k-1}\|_{L^{3+n}(\Gamma_{R})}^{3+n}\leq\lambda_{3+n}^{3+n}{\mathcal{T}}^{3+n}\|u_{k-1}\|_{V}^{3+n}\leq\lambda_{3+n}^{3+n}{\mathcal{T}}^{3+n}M_{0}^{3+n}.

By using the fact
\mathcal{T}\leq 1
, we get

\begin{array}[]{llllll}I_{k}&\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(3\delta)^{n}}{n!}\lambda_{3+n}^{3+n}M_{0}^{3+n}.\end{array}

By using the explicit definition of
\lambda_{3+n}
, we get

I_{k}\leq(CM_{0})^{3}\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}(3\delta CM_{0})^{n}a_{n},

where

a_{n}=\displaystyle\frac{(n+3)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}{n!}=\displaystyle\frac{(n+3)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}{n^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}\;\displaystyle\frac{n^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}{n!}=\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{3}{n}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\;\displaystyle\frac{n^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}{n!}.

To analyze the convergence of the series, we use the approximation
\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{3}{n}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\thicksim e^{\frac{3}{2}}
, and the Stirling’s formula:
n!\sim n^{n+1/2}e^{-n}(2\pi)^{1/2}
implies that

a_{n}\sim\displaystyle\frac{e^{n}(2\pi)^{-1/2}}{n^{n/2}}.

Let
d_{0}=\max\Big(a_{0},\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{n}}{\frac{e^{n}(2\pi)^{-1/2}}{n^{n/2}}}\Big)\in(0,\infty)
. Clearly, the series
\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{e^{n}(2\pi)^{-1/2}}{n^{n/2}}(3\delta CM_{0})^{n}
converges, which implies that

\begin{array}[]{llll}I_{k}&\leq d_{0}(CM_{0})^{3}\Big[1+\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}(3\delta CM_{0})^{n}\frac{e^{n}(2\pi)^{-1/2}}{n^{n/2}}\Big]:=r_{0}<\infty.\end{array}

Finally

\begin{array}[]{lcl}|B_{\alpha,k}(u,v)|&\leq&\left[1+2\delta\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\Gamma_{R}})}\left(\beta_{2}^{2}+\beta_{3}^{2}I_{k}^{1/3}\right)\right]\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{V}\\
&\leq&\left[1+2\delta\varepsilon_{0}\left(\beta_{2}^{2}+\beta_{3}^{2}r_{0}^{1/3}\right)\right]\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{V},\end{array}

This establishes the continuity of the bilinear form
B_{\alpha,k}
on
V\times V
.

Continuity of
L
:
The continuity of the linear functional
L
follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the continuity of the trace operator.

Precisely, for
u\in V
, we have

\begin{array}[]{llll}|L(v)|&=&|\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\phi v\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\int_{\Gamma_{R}}gv\,\mathrm{d}\sigma|\\
&\leq&\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})}\\
&\leq&\beta_{2}\left(\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})}\right)\|v\|_{V}.\end{array}

This confirms that
L
is a bounded linear functional on
V
.

Conclusion:
According to the Lax-Milgram Theorem, problem
(\tilde{S}_{k})
has a unique solution
u_{k}\in V
. Which implies that problem
(S_{k})
admits a unique solution
u_{k}\in V
for each
k\geq 1
.

It remains to be establish the uniform bound-ness of the sequence
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

For this, let
k\geq 1
. We have

B_{\alpha,k}(u_{k},u_{k})=L(u_{k}),

then

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma_{R}}\varphi(x)f_{\alpha}(u_{k-1})u_{k}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma=\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\phi u_{k}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\int_{\Gamma_{R}}gu_{k}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.\end{array}

Since the positivity of functions
f_{\alpha}
and
\varphi
, we get

\|\nabla u_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\beta_{2}(\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{R})})\|u_{k}\|_{V}

then

\|u_{k}\|_{V}\leq M_{0}.

4.
Convergence result

The purpose of this section is to prove that the sequence of solutions
(u_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of problems
(S_{k})_{k\geq 1}
converges to a solution of the original problem
(S)
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
u_{k}
denote the unique solution of the problem

(S_{k})\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}\displaystyle\Delta u=0,&{\rm in}&\;\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\,u=0,&{\rm on}&\;\Gamma_{D},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=\phi(x)&{\rm on}&\;\Gamma_{N},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}+\varphi(x)f_{\alpha}(u_{k-1})u=g(x)&{\rm on}&\;\Gamma_{R}.\end{array}\right.

with initial iteration
u_{0}=0
.

Let
w_{k}:=u_{k+1}-u_{k}
, where
u_{k+1}
and
u_{k}
are respectively the solutions of problems
(S_{k+1})
and
(S_{k})
.

We obtain that
w_{k}
solves

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}\displaystyle\Delta w_{k}&=&0,&{\rm in}&\;\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\,w_{k}&=&0,&{\rm on}&\;\Gamma_{D},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial w_{k}}{\partial n}&=&0&{\rm on}&\;\Gamma_{N},\\
\vskip 8.53581pt\displaystyle\frac{\partial w_{k}}{\partial n}&+&\varphi(x)\big[f_{\alpha}(u_{k})u_{k+1}-f_{\alpha}(u_{k-1})u_{k}\big]=0&{\rm on}&\;\Gamma_{R}.\end{array}\right.

Using Green formula’s, we obtain

5.

In this section, we prove that the limit

.

For
s\geq 0
, the periodic Sobolev space of index
s
is defined by

H^{s}_{2\pi}=\{f\in L^{2}_{2\pi}:\;\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|k|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(k)|^{2}<\infty\}.

H^{s}_{2\pi}
is equipped by the inner product

\langle f/g\rangle_{H^{s}_{2\pi}}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|k|^{2})^{s}\widehat{f}(k)\overline{\widehat{g}(k)}

and the associated norm

\|f\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}}=\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|k|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(k)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Proposition 7.1
.

We have

(1)

For
s\geq 0
:
(H^{s}_{2\pi},\langle./.\rangle_{H^{s}_{2\pi}})
is an Hilbert space.

(2)

For
s_{2}\geq s_{1}\geq 0
, we have
H^{s_{2}}_{2\pi}\hookrightarrow H^{s_{1}}_{2\pi}
. Precisely, we have

\|f\|_{H^{s_{1}}_{2\pi}}\leq\|f\|_{H^{s_{2}}_{2\pi}},\;\forall f\in H^{s_{2}}_{2\pi}.

Now, we give the main result statement for this section.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
2<p<\infty
and
s\in(0,1/2)
such that
\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}+\frac{s}{1}=\frac{1}{2}
,
then
H^{s}_{2\pi}\hookrightarrow L^{p}_{2\pi}.

Precisely, we have

(27)

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{2\pi}}\leq 2{\tilde{R}}_{p}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}},

with

(28)

{\tilde{R}}_{p}^{p}=C_{1}\frac{p}{p-2}C_{2}^{p-2}p^{\frac{p-2}{2}},

and
C_{1},C_{2}
are universal constants.

Remark 7.2
.

Combining this result with Remark
2.1
we get: for
p\in(2,\infty)

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{2\pi}}\leq\lambda_{p}\|f\|_{H^{1/2}_{2\pi}},\;\forall f\in H^{1/2}_{2\pi},

with
\lambda_{p}=2{\tilde{R}}_{p}
.

Proof of Proposition
7.2
.
This proof is inspired by the classical case proof in (
[
1
]
). Our method of study requires us to distinguish three cases:

\bullet

First case:
Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{2\pi}
such that
\widehat{f}(0)=0
.

We have

2\pi\|f\|_{L^{p}_{2\pi}}^{p}=p\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-1}\lambda_{1}(\{x\in[0,2\pi]:\;|f(x)|>t\})dt.

We will decompose
f
in such a way as to reveal norms of type
L^{2}
. To do this, let us split
f
into ”low” and ”high” frequencies by setting, for
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

f=f_{1,N}+f_{2,N}

with

\begin{array}[]{lll}f_{1,N}&=&\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big({\bf 1}_{\{|k|<N\}}\widehat{f}(k)\big)\\
\\
f_{2,N}&=&\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big({\bf 1}_{\{|k|\geq N\}}\widehat{f}(k)\big).\\
\end{array}

Since the support of the Fourier transform of
f_{1,N}
is finite, the function
f_{1,N}
is bounded and more precisely, we have

-
If
N=0
, then
f_{1,N}=0

-
If
N\geq 1
, we can write

\begin{array}[]{lll}2\pi\|f_{1,N}\|_{L^{\infty}_{2\pi}}&\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{k=-(N-1)}^{N-1}|\widehat{f}(k)|\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{k=-(N-1)}^{N-1}(1+k^{2})^{-s/2}(1+k^{2})^{s/2}|\widehat{f}(k)|\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{k=-(N-1)}^{N-1}(1+k^{2})^{-s}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{k=-(N-1)}^{N-1}(1+k^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(k)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{k=-(N-1)}^{N-1}(1+k^{2})^{-s}\Big)^{1/2}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}}.\end{array}

Let
S_{N}=\displaystyle\sum_{k=-(N-1)}^{N-1}(1+k^{2})^{-s}\leq\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(1+k^{2})^{-s}.

For
k\geq 0
, we have

1+k^{2}=\displaystyle 1+\frac{1}{2}k^{2}+\frac{1}{2}k^{2}\geq\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{4}k^{2}+\frac{1}{2}k\geq\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}(1+k)^{2},

so

(1+k^{2})^{-s}\leq 4^{s}(1+k)^{-2s}.

Hence, for
N\geq 1
, we get

S_{N}\leq\displaystyle 4^{s}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(1+k)^{-2s}\leq\displaystyle 4^{s}\sum_{k=1}^{N}k^{-2s}\leq\displaystyle 4\sum_{k=1}^{N}k^{-2s}.

By Lemma
7.1
, we get

S_{N}\leq 8\frac{N^{1-2s}}{1-2s}.

Then, in both cases
N=0
and
N\geq 1
we get

(29)

2\pi\|f_{1,N}\|_{L^{\infty}_{2\pi}}\leq 2\sqrt{2}(1-2s)^{-1/2}N^{\frac{1}{2}-s}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}}.

By applying the triangular inequality, we have, for any integer
N\geq 0
,

\{|f|>t\}\subset\{|f_{1,N}|>t/2\}\cup\{|f_{2,N}|>t/2\}.

Using the inequality (
29
) above, if we choose
N=N_{t}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with

N_{t}\leq\Big(\frac{t(1-2s)^{1/2}}{4C\|f\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}}}\Big)^{p}<N_{t}+1

we obtain

\|f_{1,N_{t}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{2\pi}}\leq\frac{t}{4}\Longrightarrow\lambda_{1}\big(\{|f_{1,N_{t}}|>t/2\}\big)=0.

Therefore, we can deduce that

(30)

2\pi\|f\|_{L^{p}_{2\pi}}^{p}\leq p\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-1}\lambda_{1}(\{2|f_{2,N_{t}}|>t\})dt.

It is well known that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\lambda_{1}\Big(\{|f_{2,N_{t}}|>t/2\}\Big)&=&\displaystyle\int_{\{|f_{2,N_{t}}|>t/2\}}1\;dx\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\int_{\{|f_{2,N_{t}}|>t/2\}}\frac{4|f_{2,N_{t}}(x)|^{2}}{t^{2}}dx\\
&\leq&\displaystyle 4\frac{\|f_{2,N_{t}}\|_{L^{2}_{2\pi}}^{2}}{t^{2}}.\end{array}

By the equation (
30
), we obtain

(31)

2\pi\|f\|_{L^{p}_{2\pi}}^{p}\leq 4p\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-3}\|f_{2,N_{t}}\|_{L^{2}_{2\pi}}^{2}dt

It is well known that the Fourier transform is (up to a constant) an isometry from
L^{2}_{2\pi}
to
l^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
; we therefore have

\|f_{2,N_{t}}\|_{L^{2}_{2\pi}}^{2}=\sum_{|k|\geq N_{t}}|\widehat{f}(k)|^{2}.

Then by applying inequality (
31
), we get

2\pi\|f\|_{L^{p}_{2\pi}}^{p}\leq 4p\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-3}{\bf 1}_{\{(t,k);\;|k|\geq N_{t}\}}(t,k)|\widehat{f}(k)|^{2}dt.

By using the definition of
N_{t}
, we have, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
,

|k|\geq N_{t}\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{t(1-2s)^{1/2}}{4C\|f\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}}}\Big)^{p}\Longrightarrow t\leq\alpha_{k}=\frac{4C\|f\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}}}{(1-2s)^{1/2}}2^{1/p}|k|^{1/p}.

By applying Fubini’s theorem, we obtain(
C=\sqrt{2}/\pi
)

\begin{array}[]{lll}2\pi\|f\|_{L^{p}_{2\pi}}^{p}&\leq&4p\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\alpha_{k}}t^{p-3}dt\Big)|\widehat{f}(k)|^{2}\\
&\leq&4p\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\frac{{\alpha_{k}}^{p-2}}{p-2}|\widehat{f}(k)|^{2}\\
&\leq&4\displaystyle\frac{p}{p-2}2^{(p-2)/p}\big(\frac{4C}{(1-2s)^{1/2}}\big)^{p-2}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}}^{p-2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}|k|^{\frac{p-2}{p}}|\widehat{f}(k)|^{2}\\
&\leq&4\displaystyle\frac{p}{p-2}2^{(p-2)/p}\big(\frac{4C}{(1-2s)^{1/2}}\big)^{p-2}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}}^{p}.\end{array}

Finally, using
1-2s=\frac{2}{p}
we get

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{2\pi}}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}r_{p}^{p}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}}^{p},

with

r_{p}=\Big(4\frac{p}{p-2}2^{\frac{p-2}{p}}(4C)^{p-2}(\frac{p}{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\Big)^{1/p}.

Using the elementary inequalities, for all
p\geq 3
,

\frac{p}{p-2}\leq 3\;\;{\rm and}\;\;2^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\leq 2,

we get

r_{p}\leq R_{p}=\Big(24\times(4/\pi)^{p-2}p^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\Big)^{1/p}.

Then, we obtain

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{2\pi}}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}R_{p}^{p}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}}^{p}=\tilde{R}_{p}^{p}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}}^{p},

with
\tilde{R}_{p}=\Big(3\times(4/\pi)^{p-1}p^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\Big)^{1/p}.

\bullet

Second case:
Let
f\in C_{2\pi}^{\infty}
be any element of
C_{2\pi}^{\infty}
.

Put
g=f-\widehat{f}(0)
. By the first case we obtain

\|g\|_{L^{p}_{2\pi}}^{p}\leq\tilde{R}_{p}^{p}\|g\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}}^{p}.

Then, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{2\pi}}&=&\|g+\widehat{f}(0)\|_{L^{p}_{2\pi}}\\
&\leq&\|g\|_{L^{p}_{2\pi}}+|\widehat{f}(0)|\\
&\leq&\tilde{R}_{p}\|g\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}}+|\widehat{f}(0)|\\
&\leq&2\tilde{R}_{p}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}}.\end{array}

Which prove the required inequality with the constant
2\tilde{R}_{p}
.

\bullet

Third case:
Let
f\in H^{s}_{2\pi}
.

Using the extension theorem for a continuous linear map defined on a dense subspace and the second case treated with
\overline{C^{\infty}_{2\pi}}=H_{2\pi}^{s}
, we obtain
H^{s}_{2\pi}\hookrightarrow L^{p}_{2\pi}
and

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{2\pi}}\leq 2\tilde{R}_{p}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{2\pi}},\;\forall f\in H^{s}_{2\pi}.

Which complete the proof of Proposition
7.2
.
