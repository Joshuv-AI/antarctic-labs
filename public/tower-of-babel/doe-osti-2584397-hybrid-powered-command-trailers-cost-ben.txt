1 Introduction
U.S. Forest Service (USFS) wildfire base camps (also referred to as incident command posts, or
ICPs) use portable power for electrical needs, including heating, cooling, powering devices,
cooking, heating water for showering, and more. The “main street” of ICPs typically includes
yurts and/or trailers, from which logistics staff work during the incident. These yurts and trailers
include heating and cooling systems, computer equipment, lighting, and other plug loads. They
are conventionally powered by portable diesel generators. Hybrid portable power systems
consisting of a combination of solar photovoltaics (PV), battery energy storage systems (BESS),
and/or backup diesel generators have been used in recent years and were piloted by the National
Technology and Development Program (NTDP) on incidents in fall 2024 as part of NTDP’s
Portable Power Project.

A cost-benefit analysis tool was developed as part of this NTDP project as a tool for USFS
contracting officers and NTDP engineers to analyze hybrid portable power systems for trailers in
base camps. In addition, a separate Incident Energy Systems Model was developed, estimating
loads and power outputs for hybrid systems powering yurts as well as trailers, resulting in an
Excel tool and associated report, “Incident Energy Systems Model: User Guide and Examples”
(Powell 2025). Although the Excel tools are not currently publicly available, the insights gained
from their development and initial use, as described in these reports, could be applied to other
scenarios involving portable power systems.

2 Overview
Hybrid portable power systems can reduce fuel costs compared to conventional diesel generator-
only systems. Because the USFS directly pays for fuel and fuel delivery at ICPs, hybrid power
systems have the potential to save USFS operating costs.

Command trailers powered by hybrid portable power systems have already been deployed on
ICPs, and vendors report the systems result in fuel cost savings. The National Renewable Energy
Laboratory (NREL) created a Hybrid Powered Command Trailers Cost-Benefit Analysis Excel-
based tool to estimate the cost differences for command trailers powered by a variety of energy
sources and to validate this claim. It includes changeable input assumptions and specifically
includes two scenarios, both compared with traditional command trailers powered only by diesel
generators:

 1. Hybrid rooftop solar system: Efficient, well-insulated command trailers powered by
 rooftop solar PV, an indoor BESS (including inverters), and a backup diesel generator.
 2. Hybrid ground-mount solar system: Standard command trailers powered by an
 exterior, temporary ground-mount solar PV array and mobile trailer with rooftop PV,
 BESS system (including inverters), and backup diesel generator.
The tool focuses on fuel costs (direct and delivery) as daily rental rates vary by vendor, region,
length of deployment, and year.

3 User Guide
There are two primary tabs in the tool, one for each scenario (“Hybrid Rooftop” and “Hybrid
Ground-Mount”). User inputs are displayed in orange cells (column C), calculations in white
cells (column C), subtotals in grey cells (column C), and the outputs—or results—containing
fuel savings (gallons and costs) are displayed in green cells, in columns C and E. Calculations,
subtotals, and results automatically update when inputs are changed. Inputs include the number
of trailers, number of backup generators, fuel consumed per hour, operating hours, and cost of
fuel. Non-orange cells containing calculations could be locked in a future version of the tool to
prevent accidental updates. The tool also contains a “Gen Fuel Consumption” sheet, which has
the assumed gallons per hour of fuel consumed by different sizes of generators. These
assumptions could be changed within this sheet.

4 Scenarios
The cost-benefit analysis tool includes two scenarios: (1) command trailers powered by a hybrid
rooftop solar system and (2) command trailers powered by a hybrid ground-mount solar system.

4.1 Hybrid Rooftop Solar Command Trailers
The Incident Energy Systems Model (Powell 2025) estimates that hybrid command trailers
powered by rooftop solar + BESS + diesel generators do not require diesel generator use in the
evaluated location over the full “typical meteorological year” (TMY) day. A TMY day includes
weather data from an actual historical day in the median (“judged most typical by the TMY
algorithm,” Wilcox and Marion 2008) month within the last approximately 20 years. This
matches NTDP’s pilot results, where hybrid command trailers stationed on the Lake Fire ICP in
July 2024 provided uninterrupted power without using their back-up diesel generators.
According to an interview with a vendor that has provided these solar command trailers for
multiple fire incidents during at least two seasons, their backup diesel generator serving three to
five command trailers ran for 26 hours in total over 60+ assigned days. This averages
approximately 6 minutes of generator use a day. For the cost-benefit analysis, we conservatively
assumed 1 hour of generator use per day.

For the baseline case, command trailers were assumed to each be powered by one 25-kW diesel
generator. Generators were assumed to run 24 hours per day, consuming 1 gallon of diesel fuel
per hour, on average. Actual fuel consumption varies based on load conditions, among other
factors, but this average matches the field knowledge of vendors and USFS logistics staff and
was validated using generator specification sheets.

For Scenario 1, with the assumption of $5.72/gal for diesel including delivery ($5.00/gal for
diesel and $0.72/gal for delivery, estimated from incidents with available unpublished invoice
data), six solar command trailers save approximately $800/day in fuel, which translates to more
than $11,000 during a 2-week deployment. The cost per gallon of diesel notably impacts the
estimated cost savings and can be changed by the user of the tool. Rooftop solar command
trailers may have higher rental fees than traditional command trailers powered by diesel
generators. However, this could pay back in fuel savings, particularly during longer
deployments. In other words, six solar command trailers could have a rental fee up to
approximately $11,000 higher than six conventional command trailers (with power) over a 2-
week deployment and still save the USFS money.

4.2 Hybrid Ground-Mount Solar System
For this scenario, Scenario 2, we considered a piloted system that included a temporary ground-
mounted solar PV array, a mobile trailer with rooftop PV and BESS inside the trailer, and a
backup diesel generator. This hybrid portable power system served six command trailers. During
the pilot in Payson, Arizona, 4 hours of diesel generator use in 1 day was observed. We used this
value as the assumed number of generator operating hours in the efficient (study) case. The
baseline case was the same as in Scenario 1—command trailers each fully powered by a 25-kW
diesel generator.

We analyzed six command trailers for this cost comparison. (Six was the number of command
trailers powered by a large ground-mount solar PV and hybrid system during the pilot.) This
scenario demonstrates slightly lower fuel cost savings as compared to Scenario 1 because the
diesel generator ran 3 hours longer per day.

5 Conclusion
Command trailers powered by hybrid power systems have the potential to save the USFS money
on fuel costs. These savings could make up for the higher rental fees from hybrid portable power
systems. Hybrid power systems have additional benefits beyond cost savings, including
reliability and resilience benefits, because the trailers are not as dependent on fuel delivery and
can continue providing power in the event fuel is not delivered to the ICP. In addition, solar PV
and BESS systems provide additional contingency if fossil fuel generators experience
mechanical failures that require service or replacement. Solar PV and BESS also operate silently,
a characteristic logistics staff favorably noted during the NTDP pilot, and their use leads to
reduced carbon monoxide and particulate matter exposure.

The accuracy of hybrid portable power system cost savings depends on user inputs. To evaluate
different scenarios, input assumptions could be changed and results reevaluated using the cost-
benefit analysis tool.
