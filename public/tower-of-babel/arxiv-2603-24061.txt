Conserved quantities and ensemble measure for Martyna–Tobias–Klein barostats with restricted cell degrees of freedom Preprint: perspective
Kohei Shinohara

Abstract
We derive the conserved energy-like quantity and ensemble measure for Martyna–Tobias–Klein (MTK) barostats in which only a restricted subset of the cell degrees of freedom are active. In the standard fully anisotropic MTK formulation the number of barostat degrees of freedom is d 2 d^{2} , where d d is the spatial dimension. When only n c n_{c} axes of the cell matrix are allowed to fluctuate, the conserved energy-like quantity retains the same functional form but with d 2 d^{2} replaced by n c n_{c} in every term that counts barostat degrees of freedom. The derivation builds on the generalized Liouville framework for non-Hamiltonian systems and the existing MTK integration machinery. We verify that this quantity is exactly conserved, show that the resulting dynamics samples the isothermal–isobaric ensemble restricted to the submanifold of cell shapes in which inactive components are held fixed, and provide a complete Liouville-operator-based integration scheme for the masked MTK variant.



I
Introduction

The Martyna–Tobias–Klein (MTK) equations of motion
3
extend the Nosé–Hoover chain (NHC) thermostat
2
;
9
to constant-pressure molecular dynamics by coupling the simulation cell to a barostat variable.
When combined with a measure-preserving, time-reversible integrator derived from the Liouville-operator formalism
6
, the MTK scheme provides a robust route to sampling the isothermal–isobaric (
NPT
) ensemble.

In many practical applications, one does not wish to allow the full cell tensor to fluctuate.
For example, slab geometries call for pressure control only along the surface normal (
NP_{z}T
ensemble), while uniaxial-stress simulations constrain the lateral dimensions.
Codes such as LAMMPS
5
already support such restricted-axis ensembles, following the Shinoda formulation
4
.
The original MTK paper
3
derives the conserved energy-like quantity for the isotropic and fully anisotropic (
d^{2}
degrees of freedom) cases, but does not treat the intermediate case in which a subset of cell axes is active.
To the best of my knowledge, no compact published derivation exists for the corresponding reduced-degree-of-freedom conserved energy-like quantity and ensemble measure.

Two restrictions apply to the present formulation.
First, we address only diagonal cell-length fluctuations; the extension to off-diagonal tilt factors is left for future work.
Second, each active cell axis must be orthogonal to all other axes (Sec.
III
), which excludes certain non-orthogonal cell shapes from partial barostatting.
This orthogonality condition is automatically satisfied for orthorhombic cells.
For a hexagonal cell, the unique six-fold axis is orthogonal to the basal plane and may be barostatted alone, but simultaneous control of both in-plane basis vectors requires reformulation in an orthohexagonal supercell because they are not orthogonal.
A fully triclinic cell cannot satisfy the condition at all.

This short note fills the derivation gap identified above: we recall the generalized Liouville framework for non-Hamiltonian systems (Sec.
II
), derive the equations of motion, the conserved energy-like quantity, and the expected ensemble for the masked MTK barostat (Sec.
III
), and provide the Liouville-operator-based integration scheme for the masked variant (Appendix
D
).
For completeness, Appendices

II
Non-Hamiltonian systems

The MTK equations of motion are non-Hamiltonian: the thermostat and barostat couplings break the symplectic structure.
Their statistical-mechanical validity rests on the generalized Liouville framework for non-Hamiltonian systems
7
;
8
;
6
;
9
, which we now summarize.

We consider a dynamical system

\displaystyle\dot{{\bm{x}}}=\bm{\xi}({\bm{x}})

(1)

for
n
-dimensional phase-space variables
{\bm{x}}
.
We write an initial condition
{\bm{x}}_{0}
and the corresponding trajectory
{\bm{x}}_{t}:={\bm{x}}({\bm{x}}_{0},t)
at time
t
.
The Liouville operator

\displaystyle i\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bm{\xi}({\bm{x}})\cdot\nabla_{{\bm{x}}},

(2)

gives the formal solution
{\bm{x}}_{t}=e^{i\mathcal{L}t}\,{\bm{x}}_{0}
.
Factorizing
e^{i\mathcal{L}\Delta t}
via symmetric Trotter splittings yields time-reversible, measure-preserving integrators
6
.

The phase-space compressibility of the dynamical system is defined as

\displaystyle\kappa({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}\cdot\dot{{\bm{x}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\xi_{i}({\bm{x}})}{\partial x_{i}}.

(3)

The phase-space volume elements
d{\bm{x}}_{0}
and
d{\bm{x}}_{t}
are related by the phase-space compressibility as

\displaystyle d{\bm{x}}_{t}=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa({\bm{x}}_{s})ds\right)d{\bm{x}}_{0}.

(4)

The measure
e^{-w(t)}d{\bm{x}}_{t}
is conserved along the trajectory, where
w(t)
is an indefinite time integral of
\kappa({\bm{x}})
along the trajectory,

\displaystyle w(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int^{t}\kappa({\bm{x}}_{s})ds.

(5)

Note that we drop the dependence of
w(t)
on the initial condition
{\bm{x}}_{0}
when it is clear from context.

We suppose that the system has
m
independent conserved quantities
\Lambda_{k}({\bm{x}})\,(k=1,\dots,m)
.
For the initial condition
{\bm{x}}_{0}
, we write the values as
C_{k}=\Lambda_{k}({\bm{x}}_{t})
.
The microcanonical partition function of this system with conserved quantities
\{C_{k}\}
is defined as

\displaystyle\Omega(\{C_{k}\})=\int d{\bm{x}}\,e^{-w(t)}\prod_{k=1}^{m}\delta(\Lambda_{k}({\bm{x}})-C_{k}).

(6)

Because the measure
e^{-w}d{\bm{x}}
is conserved along the trajectory, we can sample the microcanonical distribution by running a single trajectory and accumulating time averages with the assumption of ergodicity.

III
Masked MTK barostat

Throughout this section,
d
denotes the spatial dimension,
N
the number of particles, and
N_{f}=dN
the number of physical degrees of freedom (in the absence of constraints).
We write
{\bm{r}}_{i}
,
{\bm{p}}_{i}
,
m_{i}
, and
{\bm{F}}_{i}
for the position, momentum, mass, and force on particle
i
, and
{\bm{h}}
for the
d\times d
cell matrix whose rows are the basis vectors.

We consider the case where only
n_{c}
of the
d
cell axes are allowed to fluctuate, i.e. the barostat momentum
{\mathbf{p}_{g}}
is restricted to have at most
n_{c}
nonzero diagonal entries while the cell matrix
{\bm{h}}
itself may have arbitrary shape.
Label the first
n_{c}
axes as active and the remaining
d-n_{c}
axes as inactive, and let
{\bm{e}}_{k}
be the unit vector along the
k
-th cartesian axis associated with the cell matrix
{\bm{h}}
, not a fixed cartesian unit vector.
We denote the length of the
k
-th axis as
\lambda_{k}
,

\displaystyle{\bm{h}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}{\bm{e}}_{1}^{\top}\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{d}{\bm{e}}_{d}^{\top}\end{pmatrix}.

(7)

The masked barostat momentum is restricted to the subspace spanned by the active axes:

\displaystyle{\mathbf{p}_{g}}=\sum_{c=1}^{n_{c}}p_{c}\,{\bm{e}}_{c}{\bm{e}}_{c}^{\top},

(8)

where
p_{c}
is the scalar momentum conjugate to axis
c
.
We assume the active axes are orthogonal to all other axes,

\displaystyle{\bm{e}}_{c}^{\top}{\bm{e}}_{k}=0,\quad c=1,\dots,n_{c},\;k\neq c.

(9)

This condition is automatically satisfied for orthorhombic cells.
For non-orthogonal cells it restricts which axes may be active: in a hexagonal cell the unique six-fold axis is orthogonal to the basal plane and may be barostatted alone, but activating both in-plane hexagonal basis vectors would violate orthogonality because they are not orthogonal; one must instead adopt an orthohexagonal supercell.
A fully triclinic cell, in which no pair of axes is in general orthogonal, falls outside the scope of this formulation entirely.

III.1
Equations of motion

To make the masked formulation explicit, we first write the physical Hamiltonian and instantaneous internal stress tensor:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}({\bm{r}},{\bm{p}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{{\bm{p}}_{i}^{2}}{2m_{i}}+U({\bm{r}}),

(10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{\mathrm{int}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\det{\bm{h}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\frac{{\bm{p}}_{i}{\bm{p}}_{i}^{\top}}{m_{i}}+{\bm{F}}_{i}{\bm{r}}_{i}^{\top}\right],

(11)

where we assume the potential energy
U
depends only on the particle positions
{\bm{r}}
and not on the cell
{\bm{h}}
.
The thermostat chain variables are
(\eta_{j},p_{\eta_{j}})
and the barostat chain variables are
(\xi_{j},p_{\xi_{j}})
for
j=1,\dots,M
.
Their driving forces are

\displaystyle G_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{{\bm{p}}_{i}^{2}}{m_{i}}-N_{f}kT,

(12a)

\displaystyle G_{j}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{p_{\eta_{j-1}}^{2}}{Q_{j-1}}-kT\quad(j=2,\dots,M),

(12b)

\displaystyle G^{\prime}_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{c=1}^{n_{c}}\frac{p_{c}^{2}}{W_{g}}-n_{c}kT,

(12c)

\displaystyle G^{\prime}_{j}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{p_{\xi_{j-1}}^{2}}{Q^{\prime}_{j-1}}-kT\quad(j=2,\dots,M).

(12d)

The thermostat and barostat mass parameters are
9
;
2
;
3

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle=N_{f}kT\tau^{2},

(13a)

\displaystyle Q_{j}

\displaystyle=kT\tau^{2}\quad(j=2,\dots,M),

(13b)

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}_{1}

\displaystyle=n_{c}kT\tau^{2},

(13c)

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}_{j}

\displaystyle=kT\tau^{2}\quad(j=2,\dots,M),

(13d)

\displaystyle W_{g}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{f}+d}{d}\,kT\tau^{2},

(13e)

where
\tau
is the characteristic thermostat/barostat time scale and
Q^{\prime}_{1}
replaces
d^{2}
with
n_{c}
to match the reduced barostat degrees of freedom.
The barostat mass
W_{g}
retains its fully anisotropic form rather than being re-derived for the restricted case; this is a pragmatic choice that preserves compatibility with existing implementations.
The masked MTK equations of motion are then

\displaystyle\dot{{\bm{r}}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\bm{p}}_{i}}{m_{i}}+\frac{{\mathbf{p}_{g}}}{W_{g}}{\bm{r}}_{i},

(14a)

\displaystyle\dot{{\bm{p}}}_{i}

\displaystyle={\bm{F}}_{i}-\left({\mathbf{p}_{g}}+\frac{\mathrm{Tr}[{\mathbf{p}_{g}}]}{N_{f}}{\bm{I}}\right)\frac{{\bm{p}}_{i}}{W_{g}}-\frac{p_{\eta_{1}}}{Q_{1}}{\bm{p}}_{i},

(14b)

\displaystyle\dot{{\bm{h}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\bm{h}}{\mathbf{p}_{g}}}{W_{g}},

(14c)

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{c}

\displaystyle=\det[{\bm{h}}]\cdot{\bm{e}}_{c}^{\top}(\mathbf{P}^{\mathrm{int}}-P{\bm{I}})\,{\bm{e}}_{c}+\frac{1}{N_{f}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{{\bm{p}}_{i}^{2}}{m_{i}}-\frac{p_{\xi_{1}}}{Q^{\prime}_{1}}\,p_{c}\quad(c=1,\dots,n_{c}),

(14d)

and the thermostat/barostat NHC chains obey

\displaystyle\dot{\eta}_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{\eta_{j}}}{Q_{j}}\quad(j=1,\dots,M),

(15a)

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{\eta_{j}}

\displaystyle=G_{j}-\frac{p_{\eta_{j+1}}}{Q_{j+1}}p_{\eta_{j}}\quad(j=1,\dots,M-1),

(15b)

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{\eta_{M}}

\displaystyle=G_{M},

(15c)

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{\xi_{j}}}{Q^{\prime}_{j}}\quad(j=1,\dots,M),

(15d)

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{\xi_{j}}

\displaystyle=G^{\prime}_{j}-\frac{p_{\xi_{j+1}}}{Q^{\prime}_{j+1}}p_{\xi_{j}}\quad(j=1,\dots,M-1),

(15e)

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{\xi_{M}}

\displaystyle=G^{\prime}_{M}.

(15f)

Here
P
is the target pressure,
{\bm{I}}
is the
d\times d
identity matrix, and only the
n_{c}
active cell components fluctuate.
The orthogonality condition [Eq. (
9
)] ensures that
{\bm{e}}_{c}^{\top}(\mathbf{P}^{\mathrm{int}}-P{\bm{I}})\,{\bm{e}}_{c}
extracts a single diagonal element of the stress tensor, giving a well-defined scalar pressure along axis
c
.

Although we write as if
{\bm{h}}
is fully anisotropic in Eq. (
14
), the masked form of
{\mathbf{p}_{g}}
ensures that only the
n_{c}
active cell lengths fluctuate,

\displaystyle\dot{\lambda}_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{c}p_{c}}{W_{g}}\quad(c=1,\dots,n_{c}).

(16)

III.2
Conserved energy-like quantity

The conserved energy-like quantity for the masked MTK barostat is

\displaystyle H^{\prime}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathcal{H}({\bm{r}},{\bm{p}})+\sum_{c=1}^{n_{c}}\frac{p_{c}^{2}}{2W_{g}}+P\det[{\bm{h}}]+\sum_{j=1}^{M}\left(\frac{p_{\eta_{j}}^{2}}{2Q_{j}}+\frac{p_{\xi_{j}}^{2}}{2Q^{\prime}_{j}}\right)+N_{f}kT\eta_{1}+n_{c}kT\xi_{1}+kT(\eta_{c}+\xi_{c}),

(17)

where
\eta_{c}\coloneqq\sum_{j=2}^{M}\eta_{j}
and
\xi_{c}\coloneqq\sum_{j=2}^{M}\xi_{j}
.
Comparing with the fully anisotropic conserved energy-like quantity [Eq. (
62
)], the only structural differences are:

1.

The barostat kinetic energy sums over the
n_{c}
active components
p_{c}
instead of the full
d\times d
matrix
{\mathbf{p}_{g}}
.

2.

The coupling to the first barostat chain variable is
n_{c}kT\xi_{1}
instead of
d^{2}kT\xi_{1}
.

We now verify that
\dot{H}^{\prime}=0
by computing the time derivative and using the equations of motion.

(I) Physical Hamiltonian, barostat kinetic energy, and
PV
term.

Using
{\bm{F}}_{i}=-\partial U/\partial{\bm{r}}_{i}
and the stress tensor identity [Eq. (
11
)], the time derivative of the physical Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{H}}=-\frac{\mathrm{Tr}[{\mathbf{p}_{g}}]}{N_{f}W_{g}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{{\bm{p}}_{i}^{2}}{m_{i}}-\frac{p_{\eta_{1}}}{Q_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{{\bm{p}}_{i}^{2}}{m_{i}}-\frac{\det[{\bm{h}}]}{W_{g}}\mathrm{Tr}\!\left[{\mathbf{p}_{g}}\mathbf{P}^{\mathrm{int}}\right],

(18)

where we use
{\mathbf{p}_{g}}^{\top}={\mathbf{p}_{g}}
.

The barostat kinetic energy evolves as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{c=1}^{n_{c}}\frac{p_{c}^{2}}{2W_{g}}=\frac{\det[{\bm{h}}]}{W_{g}}\mathrm{Tr}\!\left[{\mathbf{p}_{g}}\mathbf{P}^{\mathrm{int}}\right]-\frac{P\det[{\bm{h}}]}{W_{g}}\mathrm{Tr}[{\mathbf{p}_{g}}]+\frac{\mathrm{Tr}[{\mathbf{p}_{g}}]}{N_{f}W_{g}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{{\bm{p}}_{i}^{2}}{m_{i}}-\frac{p_{\xi_{1}}}{Q^{\prime}_{1}}\sum_{c=1}^{n_{c}}\frac{p_{c}^{2}}{W_{g}}.

(19)

The
PV
term gives

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(P\det[{\bm{h}}]\bigr)=P\det[{\bm{h}}]\,\mathrm{Tr}[{\bm{h}}^{-1}\dot{{\bm{h}}}]=\frac{P\det[{\bm{h}}]}{W_{g}}\mathrm{Tr}[{\mathbf{p}_{g}}].

(20)

Summing Eqs. (
18
)–(
20
), all stress-tensor and
PV
terms cancel, leaving

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{H}}+\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\sum_{c=1}^{n_{c}}\frac{p_{c}^{2}}{2W_{g}}\right)+P\frac{d}{dt}\det[{\bm{h}}]=-\frac{p_{\eta_{1}}}{Q_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{{\bm{p}}_{i}^{2}}{m_{i}}-\frac{p_{\xi_{1}}}{Q^{\prime}_{1}}\sum_{c=1}^{n_{c}}\frac{p_{c}^{2}}{W_{g}}.

(21)

(II) Thermostat chain.

The time derivative of the thermostat chain terms in
H^{\prime}
is

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{p_{\eta_{j}}^{2}}{2Q_{j}}+N_{f}kT\eta_{1}+kT\eta_{c}\right]=+\frac{p_{\eta_{1}}}{Q_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{{\bm{p}}_{i}^{2}}{m_{i}}.

(22)

(III) Barostat chain.

Similarly, the barostat chain contribution is

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{p_{\xi_{j}}^{2}}{2Q^{\prime}_{j}}+n_{c}kT\xi_{1}+kT\xi_{c}\right]=+\frac{p_{\xi_{1}}}{Q^{\prime}_{1}}\sum_{c=1}^{n_{c}}\frac{p_{c}^{2}}{W_{g}}.

(23)

Summing Eqs. (
21
)–(
23
), all terms cancel and
\dot{H}^{\prime}=0
.

III.3
Phase-space compressibility and metric factor

The extended phase space comprises the variables
{\bm{x}}=(\{{\bm{r}}_{i},{\bm{p}}_{i}\},\{\lambda_{c}\},\{p_{c}\},\{\eta_{j},p_{\eta_{j}}\},\{\xi_{j},p_{\xi_{j}}\})
.
We compute the compressibility
\kappa=\nabla_{{\bm{x}}}\cdot\dot{{\bm{x}}}
by summing the diagonal contributions from each group of variables:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}\frac{\partial\dot{r}_{i\alpha}}{\partial r_{i\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{N\,\mathrm{Tr}[{\mathbf{p}_{g}}]}{W_{g}},

(24a)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}\frac{\partial\dot{p}_{i\alpha}}{\partial p_{i\alpha}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(N+1)\mathrm{Tr}[{\mathbf{p}_{g}}]}{W_{g}}-N_{f}\frac{p_{\eta_{1}}}{Q_{1}},

(24b)

\displaystyle\sum_{c=1}^{n_{c}}\frac{\partial\dot{\lambda}_{c}}{\partial\lambda_{c}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}[{\mathbf{p}_{g}}]}{W_{g}},

(24c)

\displaystyle\sum_{c=1}^{n_{c}}\frac{\partial\dot{p}_{c}}{\partial p_{c}}

\displaystyle=-n_{c}\frac{p_{\xi_{1}}}{Q^{\prime}_{1}},

(24d)

where Eq. (
24b
) uses
N_{f}=dN
.
The NHC chains contribute

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=2}^{M}\frac{p_{\eta_{j}}}{Q_{j}}-\sum_{j=2}^{M}\frac{p_{\xi_{j}}}{Q^{\prime}_{j}}.

(25)

Collecting all terms and using
p_{\eta_{j}}/Q_{j}=\dot{\eta}_{j}
,
p_{\xi_{j}}/Q^{\prime}_{j}=\dot{\xi}_{j}
:

\displaystyle\kappa=-\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(N_{f}\eta_{1}+n_{c}\xi_{1}+\eta_{c}+\xi_{c}\right).

(26)

Since
\kappa=\dot{w}
, the metric factor is

\displaystyle e^{-w}=\exp\left(N_{f}\eta_{1}+n_{c}\xi_{1}+\eta_{c}+\xi_{c}\right).

(27)

Geometrically, the masked barostat momentum
{\mathbf{p}_{g}}
[Eq. (
8
)] lives in the
n_{c}
-dimensional linear subspace spanned by
\{{\bm{e}}_{c}{\bm{e}}_{c}^{\top}\}
, so the first barostat-chain entropic term (
n_{c}\xi_{1}
) and the metric factor [Eq. (
27
)] each count
n_{c}
degrees of freedom rather than the
d^{2}
of the fully anisotropic case.

III.4
Isothermal–isobaric ensemble

The microcanonical partition function is

\displaystyle\Omega(E^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int d{\bm{x}}\,e^{-w}\delta(H^{\prime}({\bm{x}})-E^{\prime})

(28)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n_{c}kT}\left(\prod_{c=1}^{n_{c}}\sqrt{2\pi kTW_{g}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{M}\sqrt{2\pi kTQ_{j}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{M}\sqrt{2\pi kTQ^{\prime}_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\int d{\bm{r}}d{\bm{p}}d\lambda^{n_{c}}d\eta^{M}d\xi^{M-1}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{kT}\left(\mathcal{H}({\bm{r}},{\bm{p}})+P\det[{\bm{h}}]-E^{\prime}\right)\right).

(29)

In passing from the first to the second line, the barostat momenta
p_{c}
(
c=1,\dots,n_{c}
), the thermostat momenta
p_{\eta_{j}}
,
p_{\xi_{j}}
, and the thermostat positions
\eta_{j}
(
j\geq 2
),
\xi_{j}
(
j\geq 2
) are integrated out as Gaussian integrals, producing the square-root prefactors.
The
\eta_{1}
integral enforces the energy-shell constraint via
\delta(H^{\prime}-E^{\prime})
, and because the conserved energy-like quantity
H^{\prime}
[Eq. (
17
)] contains the term
n_{c}kT\xi_{1}
, integrating
\xi_{1}
over the delta function yields the prefactor
1/(n_{c}kT)
—directly analogous to the
1/(d^{2}kT)
factor in the fully anisotropic case.
Only the
n_{c}
active cell lengths
\lambda_{c}
appear as dynamical variables in
H^{\prime}
; the inactive cell lengths
\lambda_{k}
(
k=n_{c}+1,\dots,d
) satisfy
\dot{\lambda}_{k}=0
and are absent from the conserved energy-like quantity, so they contribute no integration measure.
These inactive axes are externally fixed parameters determined by the initial condition, not sampled variables; they enter
\det[{\bm{h}}]
only as constant multiplicative factors.

Because the prefactors are independent of the real dynamical variables
{\bm{r}}
,
{\bm{p}}
, and
\lambda_{c}
, the microcanonical partition function is proportional to the isothermal–isobaric partition function,

\displaystyle\Omega(E^{\prime})

\displaystyle\propto\int d{\bm{r}}d{\bm{p}}d\lambda^{n_{c}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{kT}\left(\mathcal{H}({\bm{r}},{\bm{p}})+P\det[{\bm{h}}]\right)\right)=:\Delta(N,P,T).

(30)

Thus, the masked MTK equations sample the isothermal–isobaric ensemble
(N,P,T)
restricted to the submanifold in which the
d-n_{c}
inactive cell axes are held fixed.

III.5
Isothermal–isobaric ensemble without external forces

When there is no external field
\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bm{F}}_{i}=\mathbf{0}
, the total momentum
{\bm{P}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bm{p}}_{i}
obeys

\displaystyle\dot{{\bm{P}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{W_{g}}{\mathbf{p}_{g}}{\bm{P}}-\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tr}[{\mathbf{p}_{g}}]}{W_{g}}+\frac{p_{\eta_{1}}}{Q_{1}}\right){\bm{P}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\log[{\bm{h}}]+\frac{\log\det[{\bm{h}}]}{N_{f}}{\bm{I}}+\eta_{1}{\bm{I}}\right){\bm{P}}.

(31)

Thus, the additional momentum-like quantity

\displaystyle{\bm{K}}({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\bm{h}}{\bm{P}}(\det[{\bm{h}}])^{1/N_{f}}e^{\eta_{1}}

(32)

is also conserved.
Because
{\bm{K}}
is a constant of motion, the trajectory is confined to a fixed-
{\bm{K}}
submanifold of the extended phase space.
To ensure that the dynamics still samples the correct isothermal–isobaric ensemble under this additional constraint, we must verify that the microcanonical partition function
\Omega(E^{\prime},{\bm{K}}^{\prime})
remains proportional to
\Delta(N,P,T)
.
We write the values as
{\bm{K}}^{\prime}={\bm{K}}({\bm{x}})
.

When
{\bm{K}}^{\prime}\neq\bm{0}
, the microcanonical partition function is

\displaystyle\Omega(E^{\prime},{\bm{K}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int d{\bm{x}}\,e^{-w}\delta(H^{\prime}({\bm{x}})-E^{\prime})\delta^{(d)}([{\bm{h}}{\bm{P}}](\det[{\bm{h}}])^{1/N_{f}}e^{\eta_{1}}-{\bm{K}}^{\prime})

(33)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n_{c}kT}\left(\prod_{c=1}^{n_{c}}\sqrt{2\pi kTW_{g}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{M}\sqrt{2\pi kTQ_{j}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{M}\sqrt{2\pi kTQ^{\prime}_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\int d{\bm{r}}d{\bm{p}}d\lambda^{n_{c}}d\eta^{M}d\xi^{M-1}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{kT}\left(\mathcal{H}({\bm{r}},{\bm{p}})+P\det[{\bm{h}}]-E^{\prime}\right)\right)\delta^{(d)}\left({\bm{h}}{\bm{P}}(\det[{\bm{h}}])^{1/N_{f}}e^{\eta_{1}}-{\bm{K}}^{\prime}\right).

(34)

The first-to-second-line step performs the same Gaussian integrations as in the
{\bm{K}}^{\prime}
-unconstrained case: the barostat momenta
p_{c}
, thermostat momenta
p_{\eta_{j}}
,
p_{\xi_{j}}
, and the
\xi_{1}
and
\eta_{1}
integrals produce the square-root prefactors and
1/(n_{c}kT)
, while the delta function
\delta^{(d)}({\bm{K}}-{\bm{K}}^{\prime})
passes through unchanged because none of the integrated-out variables appear in
{\bm{K}}
.
In the third line, the remaining thermostat positions
\eta_{j}
(
j\geq 2
) and
\xi_{j}
(
j\geq 2

\displaystyle\Omega(E^{\prime},{\bm{K}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\propto\int d{\bm{r}}d{\bm{p}}d\lambda^{n_{c}}d\eta_{1}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{kT}\left(\mathcal{H}({\bm{r}},{\bm{p}})+P\det[{\bm{h}}]\right)\right)\delta^{(d)}\left({\bm{h}}{\bm{P}}(\det[{\bm{h}}])^{1/N_{f}}e^{\eta_{1}}-{\bm{K}}^{\prime}\right)

When

\displaystyle\propto\Omega(E^{\prime})\propto\Delta(N,P,T).

Thus, in either case, the masked MTK equations sample the isothermal–isobaric ensemble
(N,P,T)
restricted to the submanifold in which the inactive cell axes are held fixed.

IV
Summary

We have derived the conserved energy-like quantity for MTK barostats with a restricted subset of
n_{c}
active cell degrees of freedom [Eq. (
17
)] and verified that it is exactly conserved (Sec.
III.2
).
Using the generalized Liouville framework, we computed the phase-space compressibility and metric factor for the restricted system [Eqs. (
26
)–(
27
)] and showed that the resulting dynamics samples the isothermal–isobaric ensemble restricted to the submanifold of cell shapes with inactive axes held fixed (Sec.
III.4
).
The key structural difference from the fully anisotropic case is that
d^{2}
is replaced by
n_{c}
in every term that counts barostat degrees of freedom—the barostat NHC driving force, the NHC mass parameter, the conserved energy-like quantity, and the metric factor.
This result provides a compact reference for verifying energy conservation and ensemble correctness in restricted-axis isobaric simulations.
The orthogonality assumption on the active axes [Eq. (
9
)] limits the method to cell shapes where each active axis is perpendicular to all others; non-orthogonal active subspaces such as hexagonal in-plane pairs require an orthohexagonal redefinition, while fully triclinic cells are excluded altogether.
We also provide a complete Liouville-operator-based integration scheme for the restricted MTK barostat (Appendix
D
), constructed from the same operator-splitting approach used for the isotropic and fully anisotropic cases summarized for completeness in Appendices
B
–
C
.
