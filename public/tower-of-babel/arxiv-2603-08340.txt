The Complexity of Extending Storylines with Minimum Local Crossing Number Thanks: This work started at the Bertinoro Workshop on Graph Drawing BWGD 2025.
Alexander Dobler, Siddharth Gupta, Philipp Kindermann, Fabrizio Montecchiani, Martin Nöllenburg

Abstract
Storyline layouts visualize temporal interactions by drawing each character as an x x -monotone curve and enforcing that the participants of every meeting form a contiguous vertical group. We study a drawing extension variant in which a layout of a sub-storyline is fixed and has to be extended by inserting missing characters while preserving all meeting constraints. We minimize the local crossing number χ \chi , i.e., the maximum number of crossings along any single character. We prove that the problem is 𝖶 \mathsf{W} [1]-hard parameterized by the number k k of inserted characters plus the maximum number σ \sigma of active characters, in 𝖷𝖯 \mathsf{XP} parameterized by σ \sigma and in 𝖥𝖯𝖳 \mathsf{FPT} parameterized by σ + χ \sigma+\chi .

Introduction

Storyline layouts (or
storylines
for short)
are visualizations of temporal interaction patterns of a set of actors.
They have been initially proposed to show the plots of novels and movies
[
19
]
, but are more widely applicable for temporal co-authorship networks
[
11
,
13
]
, collaboration in software development
[
17
]
, or rolling stock scheduling
[
7
]
.
A storyline depicts time on the x-axis, shows a set of characters as x-monotone curves, and interactions (or
meetings
) as vertical character groupings at the corresponding point in time (see
Fig.

1
).
Combinatorially, the primary degree of freedom in drawing a storyline is to determine valid vertical character orderings, where the characters in each meeting must form a contiguous interval, at all of the discrete meeting times.
The predominant optimization goal in storylines is crossing minimization
[
16
,
12
,
8
]
, yet other aspects, such as wiggle and whitespace minimization, have also been considered
[
7
,
20
,
18
]
.
While most existing approaches target the minimization of the total number of crossings, we are more interested in minimizing the maximum local number of crossings per character.
This is motivated by the aim to balance the required crossings (and consequently the visual quality) among the different characters and is related to the established concept of
k
-planarity and
k
-plane graph drawings, in which each edge may have at most
k
crossings
[
14
,
6
]
.

In this paper, we study local crossing minimization in storylines as a
drawing extension problem

[
1
,
2
,
3
,
5
,
10
,
9
]
, i.e., we are given a (partial) storyline layout as well as a set of missing characters. The task is to optimally insert the missing characters into the partial layout with respect to the local crossing number of both the old and the new characters. We call this problem
Local StoryLine Extension
(
LSLE
). We take a parameterized complexity perspective and present two main results: Firstly, we show
\mathsf{W}
[1]-hardness of
LSLE
, parameterized by the number
k
of new characters and the maximum number
\sigma
of characters per time instant, even if there are just two meetings with only new characters. Secondly, we present a dynamic program that yields an
\mathsf{XP}
-algorithm parameterized by
\sigma
and an
\mathsf{FPT}
-algorithm parameterized by
\sigma+\chi
.

Preliminaries.

A
storyline
instance
\mathcal{S}=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{M})
consists of a set
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},C_{2},\dots,C_{n}\}
of
n

characters
and a set
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{1},M_{2},\dots,M_{m}\}
of
m

meetings
.
Each meeting
M_{i}\in\mathcal{M}
is a triple
\langle\mathcal{C}(M_{i}),b_{i},e_{i}\rangle
, where
\mathcal{C}(M_{i})\subseteq\mathcal{C}
is a subset of characters,
b_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
is the
begin-time
of
M_{i}
and
e_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
is the
end-time
of
M_{i}
(thus,
b_{i}\leq e_{i}
).
We say that a meeting
M_{i}
is
active
at any time
t
with
b_{i}\leq t\leq e_{i}
, and that each character
C_{j}\in\mathcal{C}(M_{i})

participates
in
M_{i}
.
A common assumption is that a character cannot participate in two distinct meetings at the same time instant.
For each character
C_{j}\in\mathcal{C}
we define its
lifespan

L_{j}=[c_{j},f_{j}]
as the interval between the begin-time
b_{i}
of the first meeting
M_{i}
that
C_{j}
participates in and the end-time
e_{l}
of the last meeting
M_{l}
it participates in.
For any time instant in its lifespan, we say a character is
active
.
Let
A_{t}
be the set of active characters at time
t
and let
\sigma_{t}=|A_{t}|
be their number.
By convention, we set to
1
the minimum
c_{j}
over all characters
C_{j}
, and we denote by
\tau
the largest
f_{j}
over all characters
C_{j}
(i.e.,
\mathcal{S}
is defined over
\tau
time instants).

In a
storyline layout

\Gamma
(see
Fig.

1
), each character
C_{j}
is represented as an x-monotone curve from time instant
c_{j}
to time instant
f_{j}
.
For each time instant
t\in[\tau]=\{1,2,\dots,\tau\}
the active character curves in
A_{t}
are ordered vertically as a permutation
\pi_{t}
.
Two basic combinatorial properties must be satisfied:

(i)

For each meeting
M_{i}
, the curves representing the characters in
\mathcal{C}(M_{i})
are
consecutive
in the permutations
\pi_{j}
for all time instants
j\in[b_{i},e_{i}]
, i.e., they form a contiguous group between
b_{i}
and
e_{i}
;

(ii)

for meetings
M_{i}
with a duration
e_{i}-b_{i}>0
the characters
\mathcal{C}(M_{i})
cannot be crossed while
M_{i}
is active.

For any two consecutive time instants
i
and
i+1
, two character curves
cross
if and only if they have a different relative order in
\pi_{i}
and
\pi_{i+1}
. The
local crossing number
of a storyline layout is the maximum number of crossings along any single character curve.

We are interested in extension problems. Let
\mathcal{S}=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{M})
be a storyline instance. A
sub-storyline of
\mathcal{S}
is a storyline instance
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{C},\mathcal{M^{\prime}})
such that if a meeting in
\mathcal{S}
contains at least one character of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, then such a meeting exists also in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
(even if incomplete). More formally, for each meeting
M_{i}=\langle\mathcal{C}(M_{i}),b_{i},e_{i}\rangle\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\mathcal{C}(M_{i})\cap\mathcal{C^{\prime}}\neq\emptyset
, there is a meeting
M^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
such that
M^{\prime}_{i}=\langle\mathcal{C}(M_{i})\cap\mathcal{C^{\prime}},b_{i},e_{i}\rangle
. For a storyline layout
\Gamma
of
\mathcal{S}
we denote the induced
sub-storyline layout

\Gamma[\mathcal{S}^{\prime}]
as the layout
\Gamma
restricted to the characters and meetings in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. We focus on the following decision problem:

Local StoryLine Extension (LSLE)

Input:
A storyline instance
\mathcal{S}=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{M})
, an integer
\chi
, a storyline layout
\Gamma^{\prime}
of a sub-storyline
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{C},\mathcal{M^{\prime}})
with local crossing number at most
\chi

Question:
Does there exist a storyline layout
\Gamma
of
\mathcal{S}
such that
\Gamma[\mathcal{S}^{\prime}]=\Gamma^{\prime}
with local crossing number at most
\chi
?

We define the following parameters relevant to quantify the complexity of
LSLE
.

•

n=|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|
, number of characters in the sub-storyline
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

•

k=|\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|
, number of new characters to be inserted

•

\tau
, number of time instants of
\mathcal{S}

•

\mu=|\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathcal{M}^{\prime}|
, number of meetings with only new characters in
\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\prime}

•

\sigma
, maximum number of active characters at any time instant

Hardness of
Local StoryLine Extension

Theorem 2.1
.

LSLE
parameterized by
k+\sigma
is
\mathsf{W}
[1]-hard, even for
\mu=2
.

In order to prove
Theorem

2.1
, we exploit a reduction from the
Unary Bin Packing
problem. Here, we are given a set
I=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\}
of positive integers encoded in unary, and two integers
B
and
K
. The task is to
decide whether
I
can be partitioned into
K
disjoint subsets (bins) such that the integers in each bin sum up to at most
B
. This problem has been proven to be W[1]-hard parameterized by the number of bins
K

[
15
]
. It is not difficult to see that the same W[1]-hardness holds for
Exact Unary Bin Packing
(
EUBP
for short), where the sum of the integers in each bin must be exactly
B
(see, e.g.,
[
4
]
). A necessary condition for an instance of
EUBP
to admit a solution is that
\sum_{j=0}^{n}x_{j}=K\cdot B
.

Overview.
The idea is to transform an instance
\langle I,K,B\rangle
of
EUBP
into an instance
\langle\mathcal{S},\chi,\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\rangle
of
LSLE
as follows. The full storyline instance
\mathcal{S}
and its sub-storyline
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
differ by exactly
k=K
characters. In order to extend the layout
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, the
k
new characters are forced to go through specific regions in
\Gamma^{\prime}
in which they collect a controlled number of crossings. In particular, some special regions will force the new characters to collect a number of crossings that corresponds to an integer of
I
. Crucially, the structure of
\Gamma^{\prime}
is such that, if a valid extension
\Gamma
exists, then the number of crossings collected by a single character over all special regions is exactly
B
. Moreover, no two characters can go through the same regions, which implies a partition of the integers (encoded by crossings) into bins (encoded by characters) such that the integers in each bin sum up to precisely
B
.
We now proceed by describing the gadgets used to construct the instance
\langle\mathcal{S},\chi,\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\rangle
of
LSLE
. These gadgets should be intended as parts of
\Gamma^{\prime}
(and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
) that will be eventually assembled together.

The following two properties easily follow from the described construction.

Property 1

Every character of

Based on

Property 2
.

In any solution of

, a channel can be traversed by at most one character.

Proof 2.2
.

Consider a
c

\chi_{c}=\chi-c
times in its
\chi_{c}
-saturator gadget. Since any character traversing

Construction of
\mathcal{S}
.
It remains to construct
\mathcal{S}
from

, which implies introducing
k

, and the corresponding meetings. In particular, these new characters all meet together among them and with both

and
B

, and again at the time instant just before
M_{r}
. Finally, we fix the values of
\Delta
and
\chi
as
\Delta=2
and
\chi=B+\Delta\cdot B\cdot(K-1)
.

Reduction.
The gadgets are now arranged in such a way that each new character
N_{i}
corresponds to one bin. Each new character must be routed through some sparse and some dense channels and the capacities are designed in such a way that a solution with local crossing number
\chi
exists if and only if the collected sparse channels add up to the bin capacity
B
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that the instance
\langle I,K,B\rangle
of
EUBP
admits a solution
\ell
. Then, the corresponding instance
\langle\mathcal{S},\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\rangle
of
LSLE
admits a solution
\Gamma
.

Proof 2.4
.

In order to construct
\Gamma
from
\Gamma^{\prime}
, we must draw the curves corresponding to the
k
new characters. In particular, we associate
N_{i}
to the
i
-th subset of the solution
\ell
as follows. If the integer
x_{j}
belongs to this subset, then
N_{i}
is routed through the sparse channel of
X_{j}
, otherwise to one of the dense channels of
X_{j}
. This can be done such that no two new characters cross each other, since we have
k-1
dense channels above and below the sparse channel of every column. See
Fig.

6

To establish the validity of this solution, we shall verify that each character is crossed at most
\chi
times. Recall that no channel is traversed by more than one character due to
2
. The only existing characters being crossed by the new characters are the central characters of the traversed channels. Consider one such central character: it is crossed by exactly one new character by construction, and hence in total it receives
\chi_{c}+c=\chi-c+c=\chi
crossings, as desired. On the other hand, each new character receives:

•

B
crossings over all the traversed sparse channels, because the integers of any subset of
\ell
sum up to exactly
B
;

•

\Delta(\sum_{j=0}^{n}x_{j}-B)
crossings over all the traversed dense channels, as
\sum_{j=0}^{n}x_{j}-B
is the sum of the integers that are not part of the subset. On the other hand, we noted that
\sum_{j=0}^{n}x_{j}=k\cdot B
, hence
\Delta(\sum_{j=0}^{n}x_{j}-B)=\Delta\cdot B\cdot(K-1)
.

Overall,
B+\Delta\cdot B\cdot(K-1)=\chi
, as desired.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that the instance
\langle\mathcal{S},\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\rangle
of
LSLE
admits a solution
\Gamma
. Then, the corresponding instance
\langle I,K,B\rangle
of
EUBP
admits a solution
\ell
.

Proof 2.6
.

Consider the solution
\Gamma
and the routing of the new characters
N_{1}
,
N_{2}
,
\dots
,
N_{k}
. By
1
, the only characters with fewer than
\chi
crossings in
\Gamma^{\prime}
are the central characters of the channel gadgets, and the non-leading characters of the saturator gadgets. One easily observes that crossings of new characters with non-leading characters of the saturator gadgets can always be avoided and hence, by focusing on a crossing-minimal solution, we shall assume that
\Gamma
does not contain such crossings.

Also, by
2
, we know that any channel is traversed by at most one new character. For any new character
N
, let
B^{\prime}=B-\delta
be the sum of the capacities of the sparse channels traversed by
N
. Then, overall,
N
is crossed
B^{\prime}+\Delta\cdot(\sum_{j=0}^{n}x_{j}-B^{\prime})
times, which can be written as
B-\delta+\Delta\cdot(\sum_{j=0}^{n}x_{j}-B+\delta)=B-\delta+\Delta\cdot\delta+\Delta(\sum_{j=0}^{n}x_{j}-B)=\delta(\Delta-1)+B+\Delta(\sum_{j=0}^{n}x_{j}-B)=\delta(\Delta-1)+B+\Delta\cdot B\cdot(K-1)=\delta(\Delta-1)+\chi
.

We claim that
\delta=0
, that is,
B^{\prime}=B
. If this is the case, a solution
\ell
for

can be constructed by mapping the
i
-th subset of
I
to the integers corresponding to the capacities of the sparse channels traversed by
N_{i}
.

Suppose first, for a contradiction, that
\delta>0
, that is,
B^{\prime}<B
. Then, since
\Delta>1
,
N
is crossed more than
\chi
times, which contradicts the fact that
\Gamma
is a valid solution of
LSLE
.

Suppose now, still for a contradiction, that
\delta<0
, that is,
B^{\prime}>B
. Since
\sum_{j=0}^{n}x_{j}=K\cdot B
, if

traverses a set of sparse channels whose capacities sum up to

, then there is a different new character

which traverses a set of sparse channels whose capacities sum up to

. Then we can apply the above argument to show that

is crossed more than

\Gamma
is a valid solution of

.

The next lemma concludes the proof by discussing the size of the constructed instance and its parameters.

Lemma 2.7
.

The constructed storyline instance

is such that: (1)

3
An
\mathsf{XP}
- and
\mathsf{FPT}
-algorithm for
Local StoryLine Extension

In this section, we present an algorithm for
Local StoryLine Extension
that shows membership in
\mathsf{XP}
if parameterized by
\sigma
and in
\mathsf{FPT}
if parameterized by
\sigma+\chi
.

Let
\langle\mathcal{S},\chi,\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\rangle
be an instance of
LSLE
. We use dynamic programming.

Figure 7
:
Illustration for the dynamic program. New characters are drawn dash-dotted.

The DP states.

Consider the discrete time instants
t=1,\ldots,\tau
. Let
O_{t}=A_{t}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be the set of
existing
active characters at time
t
and let
N_{t}=A_{t}\setminus O_{t}
be the set of
new
active characters at
t
.
In
\Gamma^{\prime}
, the characters in
O_{t}
appear in some fixed vertical order
\pi_{t}
,
and partition the vertical line at time
t
into
|O_{t}|+1\leq\sigma_{t}+1\leq\sigma+1

slots
; see
Fig.

7
.

A
placement
of the new characters at time
t
must specify which slot each new character occupies, as well as the relative order of the new characters within the same slot.
Accordingly, we define a placement
P_{t}
as a pair
(s_{t},\prec_{t})
, where
s_{t}\colon N_{t}\to\{0,1,\ldots,|O_{t}|\}
assigns each new character to a slot, and
\prec_{t}
is a permutation of
N_{t}
that aligns with the slot assignment, i.e., every character in slot
i
comes before every character in slot
j>i
. From
(s_{t},\prec_{t})
, we can obtain a single total order
\rho_{t}
of
A_{t}
for all characters at time
t
.

We process the time instants from left to right. We define a DP state at time
t
as a pair
(P_{t},u_{t})
, where

(i)

P_{t}
is a placement of the new characters at time
t
, and

(ii)

u_{t}
is a vector that assigns to every character
C\in O_{t}
the number
u_{t}(C)
of crossings involving
C
that occur strictly before time
t
.

The state
(P_{t},u_{t})
is

if there exists a partial extension of
\Gamma^{\prime}
up to time
t
that realizes the placement
P_{t}
at time
t
, respects all meeting contiguity constraints for the meetings up to time
t

C\in A_{t}

Filling the table.

At time
t=1
, we enumerate all placements
P_{1}
of
N_{1}
that are consistent with the meeting constraints, and set
u_{1}
to the all-zero vector. At the final time
t=\tau
, we accept the instance if there exists any feasible state
(P_{\tau},u_{\tau})
.

We now describe how to move from time
t-1
to time
t
. Consider a feasible state
(P_{t-1},u_{t-1})
. We enumerate all possible placements
P_{t}
of the active new characters
N_{t}
that

(i)

satisfy all meeting constraints at time
t
, and

(ii)

do not have any character cross an ongoing meeting between time
t-1
and
t
that it does not participate in.

For every character
C\in A_{t-1}\cap A_{t}
, we can compute the number
cr_{t}(C)
of crossings involving
C
that occur in the open strip
(t-1,t)
from
(P_{t-1},P_{t})
: this is exactly the number of characters whose relative vertical order with
C

We define the updated budget vector
u_{t}(C)
for every character

C\in A_{t-1}

u_{t}(C)=u_{t-1}(C)+cr_{t}(C)

C

t

u_{t}(C)=0
. If
u_{t}(C)\leq\chi
for every
C\in A_{t}

(P_{t},u_{t})
as a feasible state. All such transitions generate the set of feasible DP states at time

Theorem 3.1
.

LSLE
parameterized by
\sigma

\tau\cdot(\sigma+\chi)^{O(\sigma)}

Proof 3.2
.

We prove by induction on
t
that a pair
(P_{t},b_{t})
is marked feasible by the DP if and only if there exists a partial extension of
\Gamma^{\prime}
up to time
t
that realizes placement
P_{t}
, budgets
b_{t}
and respects all constraints.
The base case
t=0
holds by construction. For the inductive step, assume that the claim holds for time
t-1
. The DP marks
(P_{t},b_{t})
as feasible, then it comes from some feasible
(P_{t-1},b_{t-1}
) and a transition that respects the meetings constraints at
t
and updates budgets exactly by the crossings induced in strip
(t-1,t)
. Concatenating the corresponding partial drawing up to
t-1
with the strip
(t-1,t)
yields a valid partial drawing up to
t
with the stated placement and budgets.

Conversely, any valid partial drawing up to
t
restricts to a valid partial drawing up to
t-1
for some
(P_{t-1},b_{t-1})
. Comparing the two consecutive time orders determines exactly the increments
cr_{t}(C)
, hence the DP includes the corresponding transition and marks
(P_{t},b_{t})
as feasible. This establishes the invariant, and for
t=\tau
it yields the equivalence between acceptance and existence of an extension with local crossing number at most
\chi
.

It remains to bound the running time in terms of
\sigma
,
\chi
and
\tau
. Fix a time instant
t
.

The number of possible slot assignments at
t
is at most
(|O_{t}|+1)^{|N_{t}|}\leq(\sigma+1)^{\sigma}
. For each slot assignment, the order of the
|N_{t}|
new characters can be chosen in at most
|N_{t}|!\leq\sigma!
ways. Thus, the number of possible placements satisfies

|\{P_{t}\}|\leq(|O_{t}|+1)^{|N_{t}|}\cdot|N_{t}|!\leq(\sigma+1)^{\sigma}\cdot\sigma!\in\sigma^{O(\sigma)}.

For the budget vector, each entry is an integer in
\{0,\ldots,\chi\}
. Hence, for a fixed placement, the number of possible budget vectors is at most
(\chi+1)^{|N_{t}|}\leq(\chi+1)^{O(\sigma)}
. Therefore, the total number of states at time
t
is bounded by

\sigma^{O(\sigma)}\cdot(\chi+1)^{O(\sigma)}=(\sigma+\chi)^{O(\sigma)}.

For each state at time
t-1
, we enumerate all placements at time
t
, i.e., at most
\sigma^{O(\sigma)}
candidates. For a state
(P_{t},u_{t})
, we can compute the number of crossings in the strip
(t-1,t)
in
O(\sigma^{2})
time by counting. As there can be at most
\sigma
ongoing meetings, we can check in
O(\sigma^{2})
time that the meeting constraints are satisfied at time
t
and that no character crosses an ongoing meeting between time
t-1
and
t
that it does not participate in.
Thus, we need
(\sigma+\chi)^{O(\sigma)}\cdot\sigma^{O(\sigma)}\cdot O(\sigma^{2})
time to find all feasible DP states at time
t
. Consequently, the total running time over all
\tau
layers is

\tau\cdot(\sigma+\chi)^{O(\sigma)}\cdot\sigma^{O(\sigma)}\cdot O(\sigma^{2})=\tau\cdot(\sigma+\chi)^{O(\sigma)}.

4
Conclusions

This paper introduced two new aspects in storyline optimization, namely extending partial layouts and local crossing minimization, and presented a
\mathsf{W}
[1]-hardness result, as well as an
\mathsf{XP}
/
\mathsf{FPT}
-algorithm. Our plans for future work include storyline extension with respect to the global crossing number as well as complexity questions for further parameters.
