Entropy Concentration and Universal Typicality for Weakly Almost i.i.d. Quantum Sources
Nilanjana Datta

Abstract
Weakly almost i.i.d. quantum sources are sequences of multipartite states whose fixed-size marginals converge, on average, to tensor powers of a reference state, while allowing arbitrary global correlations and entanglement. We establish two concentration principles for such sources: a noncommutative weak law of large numbers for empirical observables, and a universal entropy-concentration principle showing asymptotic concentration on subspaces of exponential dimension governed by the von Neumann entropy of the reference state. These concentration principles provide a unified and conceptually transparent approach to several information-theoretic applications beyond the i.i.d. setting, including direct proofs of universal compression within classes of weakly almost i.i.d. sources sharing a common reference state, asymmetric quantum hypothesis-testing bounds, concentration results for macroscopic observables in quantum many-body systems including generalized Gibbs ensembles and for repeated local measurement statistics, as well as bounds on smooth- and spectral entropy quantities.



1
Introduction

Classical and quantum Shannon theories are traditionally formulated under the assumption that resources are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) across many uses. However, exact i.i.d. structure is often unrealistic in the presence of correlations, adversarial behaviour, or experimental imperfections, and may in practice be impossible to verify operationally. This has motivated several approaches to information theory beyond the i.i.d. paradigm, most notably the information-spectrum framework introduced by Han and Verdú
[
1
]
and the smooth-entropy formalism
[
2
]
of one-shot information theory, both of which avoid exact tensor-power assumptions altogether (see also
[
3
]
).

A complementary line of work investigates situations in which the i.i.d. assumption fails only mildly, with the aim of understanding which information-theoretic properties survive under controlled correlations or defects. This has led to several notions of “almost i.i.d.” behaviour. Early progress in this direction was made by Renner, who introduced the notion of “almost power” states in quantum cryptography
[
4
]
. Closely related ideas later appeared in the work of Brandão and Plenio on the generalized quantum Stein’s lemma
[
5
]
, and more recently in the work of Lami
[
6
]
. More recent works, including
[
7
,
8
]
, have compared different notions of approximate i.i.d. structure and clarified the relations between them.
These works make clear that “almost i.i.d.” is not a single well-defined notion. Different definitions capture different operational and physical aspects of approximate independence, and they form a genuine hierarchy of conditions. Consequently, understanding robustness beyond the i.i.d. regime requires determining which operational properties persist under which forms of approximate independence (see e.g.
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
).

In this paper we study this question for
weakly almost i.i.d.
quantum sources, introduced in
[
8
]
. Roughly speaking, a sequence

\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{n})_{n}

of quantum states is weakly almost i.i.d. along a reference state
\rho
if, for every fixed
k
, the average
k
-site marginal of
\rho_{n}
converges in trace norm to
\rho^{\otimes k}
. Among the currently studied notions of approximate i.i.d. structure, the weakly almost i.i.d. condition is essentially the broadest one (see e.g. Figure 1 of
[
8
]
), requiring only asymptotic local consistency with an i.i.d. model while allowing arbitrary global correlations and multipartite entanglement. In particular, weakly almost i.i.d. sources may be globally pure and highly entangled even when the reference state is maximally mixed.

The main contribution of this paper is the identification of two concentration principles for weakly almost i.i.d. sources. The first is a noncommutative weak law of large numbers, showing spectral concentration of empirical averages of local observables around their expectations with respect to the reference state. The second is a universal entropy-concentration principle, establishing asymptotic concentration on subspaces whose exponential dimension is governed by the von Neumann entropy of the reference state.

These principles provide a unified and conceptually transparent framework for several applications beyond the i.i.d. setting. These include direct proofs of universal quantum data compression within classes of weakly almost i.i.d. sources sharing a common reference state, asymmetric quantum hypothesis-testing bounds, and bounds on smooth and spectral entropy quantities. In several cases, the resulting proofs are significantly more direct than previous approaches
[
9
]
, with the relevant operational statements emerging naturally from the underlying concentration principles. We also derive concentration results for macroscopic observables in quantum many-body systems, including generalized Gibbs ensembles, and for repeated local measurement statistics.

2
Weakly almost i.i.d. sources

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space. Let
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
denote the algebra of linear operators acting on
\mathcal{H}
, and let
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})\subset\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
denote the set of quantum states (or density operators), i.e. positive semidefinite operators of unit trace on
\mathcal{H}
. A
pure state
is a rank-one projection of the form
\ket{\psi}\!\bra{\psi}
, where
\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}
is a unit vector.

The identity operator on
\mathcal{H}
is denoted by
\mathds{1}
, while
\mathds{1}_{n}
denotes the identity operator of
{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes n}
. An operator
\Pi\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is called an
orthogonal projection
if
\Pi=\Pi^{\dagger}=\Pi^{2}
. The range of
\Pi
is denoted by

{\rm{Ran}}\,(\Pi):=\{\Pi\ket{\psi}:\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}\}.

For a positive semidefinite operator
X\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, its support is defined as

\supp(X):={\rm{Ran}}\,(\{X>0\}),

where
\{X>0\}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto the span of all eigenvectors of
X
corresponding to strictly positive eigenvalues. For an operator
A\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, we denote by
A^{(i)}
the operator in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n})
given by
A^{(i)}:=\mathds{1}^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes A\otimes\mathds{1}^{\otimes(n-i)}
.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, the Schatten
p
-norm of an operator
X\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is defined by

\|X\|_{p}:=\bigl(\Tr|X|^{p}\bigr)^{1/p},

where

|X|:=\sqrt{X^{\dagger}X}.

The Schatten
\infty
-norm is the operator norm

\|X\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{\|\psi\|=1}\|X\psi\|.

In particular,

\|X\|_{1}=\Tr|X|

is the trace norm, and

\|X\|_{2}=\sqrt{\Tr(X^{\dagger}X)}

is the Hilbert–Schmidt norm.
For a self-adjoint operator
Q=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\ket{\psi_{i}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{i}}
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote the spectral projections by

\{Q>r\}:=\sum_{\lambda_{i}>r}\ket{\psi_{i}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{i}}

and

\{Q\leq r\}:=\sum_{\lambda_{i}\leq r}\ket{\psi_{i}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{i}}.

The von Neumann entropy of a state
\rho\in{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{H})
is given by
S(\rho):=-\Tr\rho\log\rho
. Here and henceforth, all logarithms are taken to base
2
, and so
\exp(a)
and
e^{a}
are both understood to denote
2^{a}
.

A quantum channel

\pazocal E:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K}),

where
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
are finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, is a linear completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) map. We denote the identity channel by
{\rm id}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
.

For a state
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
and a quantum channel
\pazocal C:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K})
, the
entanglement fidelity
of
\Lambda
with respect to
\rho
is defined as

\displaystyle F_{e}(\rho,\pazocal C):=\bra{\psi_{\rho}}({\rm id}\otimes\pazocal C)\bigl(\ket{\psi_{\rho}}\!\bra{\psi_{\rho}}\bigr)\ket{\psi_{\rho}},

(1)

where
\ket{\psi_{\rho}}\in\mathcal{H}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{H}
is any purification of
\rho
, i.e.
\Tr_{R}(\ket{\psi_{\rho}}\!\bra{\psi_{\rho}})=\rho,

with
\mathcal{H}_{R}
denoting the Hilbert space of a purifying reference system
R
. It can be shown that
F_{e}(\rho,\pazocal C)
is independent of the choice of purification. If
\pazocal C
has Kraus operators
\{E_{k}\}_{k}
, then (see e.g.
[
11
]
)

\displaystyle F_{e}(\rho,\pazocal C)=\sum_{k}|\Tr(\rho E_{k})|^{2}.

(2)

We now introduce the central notion of this paper.

Definition 1

((Weakly almost i.i.d. source))
.

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
. A sequence

\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{n})_{n}
, with

\rho_{n}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n})
, is called a

weakly almost i.i.d. quantum source along
\rho
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq[n]\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}\bigl\|(\rho_{n})_{I}-\rho^{\otimes k}\bigr\|_{1}=0\quad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}_{+},

(3)

where the expectation is uniform over all subsets

I\subseteq[n]
of cardinality
k
.

Condition (
3
) imposes only asymptotic agreement of fixed-size marginals, on average, with those of the tensor-power state
\rho^{\otimes n}
; it does not require the global state
\rho_{n}
itself to be close to
\rho^{\otimes n}
in trace norm. Consequently, weakly almost i.i.d. sources may still exhibit substantial long-range correlations and multipartite entanglement.

Example: Haar-random pure states

The following example illustrates that weakly almost i.i.d. sources may consist of globally pure states exhibiting strong multipartite entanglement and long-range correlations.

Let

\rho_{n}=\ket{\psi_{n}}\!\bra{\psi_{n}}
, where

\ket{\psi_{n}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
is Haar-random and
\mathcal{H}\simeq{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
, with

d\geq 2
. Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and consider
n\geq k
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{I}:=\bigotimes_{i\in I}\mathcal{H}_{i}\simeq\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}
, and let

\psi_{I}:=\Tr_{I^{c}}\ket{\psi_{n}}\!\bra{\psi_{n}}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{I})

be the reduced state associated with
I
.
By unitary invariance of the Haar measure and permutation symmetry of the tensor factors, the distribution of the reduced state
\psi_{I}
depends only on
|I|=k
. Fix such a subset
I\subseteq[n]
, with
|I|=k
. Identifying

\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{d^{k}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d^{n-k}},

the well-known formula for the expected purity of a Haar-random reduced state (see, e.g.,
[
12
]
) yields

\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{n}}\Tr(\psi_{I}^{2})=\frac{d^{k}+d^{n-k}}{d^{n}+1}.

(4)

Hence, for fixed
k
,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{n}}\operatorname{Tr}(\psi_{I}^{2})\to\frac{1}{d^{k}}\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty,

(5)

which is precisely the purity of the maximally mixed state on

\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}
.

For an operator
X
acting on a
D
-dimensional Hilbert space, we use

\|X\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{D}\,\|X\|_{2}
. Then Jensen’s inequality, together with

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\psi_{I}-\frac{\mathds{1}_{k}}{d^{k}}\right)^{2}=\operatorname{Tr}(\psi_{I}^{2})-\frac{1}{d^{k}},

(6)

yields

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{n}}\left\|\psi_{I}-\frac{\mathds{1}_{k}}{d^{k}}\right\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{d^{k}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{n}}\Tr(\psi_{I}^{2})-\frac{1}{d^{k}}\right)}.

(7)

Substituting (
4
) into (
7
) yields

\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{n}}\left\|\psi_{I}-\frac{\mathds{1}_{k}}{d^{k}}\right\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{d^{k}\left(\frac{d^{k}+d^{n-k}}{d^{n}+1}-\frac{1}{d^{k}}\right)},

(8)

which converges exponentially fast to zero as
n\to\infty
for fixed
k
. Therefore, for fixed
k
,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathbb{E}_{\psi_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq[n]\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}\left\|(\rho_{n})_{I}-\left(\frac{\mathds{1}}{d}\right)^{\otimes k}\right\|_{1}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(9)

Moreover, the exponential decay in (
8
), together with Markov’s inequality and the first Borel–Cantelli lemma (e.g.
[
13
]
), implies that, with probability one,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq[n]\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}\left\|(\rho_{n})_{I}-\left(\frac{\mathds{1}}{d}\right)^{\otimes k}\right\|_{1}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty,

(10)

for every fixed
k
.

Thus almost every Haar-random pure-state sequence is weakly almost i.i.d. along
\mathds{1}/d
, even though Haar-random pure states are typically highly entangled
[
14
]
; see Appendix
Appendix A: Existence of weakly almost i.i.d. pure-state sequences
for details of the above.

3
The key lemmas

We now prove the two basic concentration lemmas on which the subsequent applications rest.

Lemma 2

((Noncommutative weakly almost i.i.d. law of large numbers))
.

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
, where
\mathcal{H}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{d}
with
d<\infty
. Let

\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, with

\rho_{n}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n})
, be weakly almost i.i.d. along
\rho
.
Let
A\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be self-adjoint, define

\mu:=\Tr(\rho A)
and

\overline{A}_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}A^{(i)}
, where

A^{(i)}:=\mathds{1}^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes A\otimes\mathds{1}^{\otimes(n-i)}
.
Then
\Tr(\rho_{n}\overline{A}_{n})\to\mu
and

\Tr[\rho_{n}(\overline{A}_{n}-\mu\mathds{1}_{n})^{2}]\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Consequently, for every
\delta>0
,

\Tr\!\left[\rho_{n}\left\{|\overline{A}_{n}-\mu\mathds{1}_{n}|>\delta\right\}\right]\to 0\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty.

(11)

Remark
.

The lemma shows that empirical averages of one-site observables exhibit asymptotic spectral concentration around their expectation values with respect to the reference state
\rho
. It is the noncommutative analogue of convergence in probability in the classical weak law of large numbers.

Proof.

Set
M:=\|A\|_{\infty}
. Since
A^{(i)}
acts only on the
i
-th tensor factor,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr(\rho_{n}\overline{A}_{n})-\mu=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Tr\!\bigl[((\rho_{n})_{i}-\rho)A\bigr],

(12)

and hence

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\bigl|\Tr(\rho_{n}\overline{A}_{n})-\mu\bigr|\leq M\,\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|(\rho_{n})_{i}-\rho\|_{1}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty,

(13)

by the weakly almost i.i.d. assumption with
k=1
.

For the second moment, write

\overline{A}_{n}^{2}=n^{-2}\sum_{i}(A^{(i)})^{2}+2n^{-2}\sum_{i<j}A^{(i)}A^{(j)}
.
The contribution of the first sum to
\Tr(\rho_{n}\overline{A}_{n}^{2})
is bounded in absolute value by

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl|\Tr[\rho_{n}(A^{(i)})^{2}]\bigr|\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|A\|_{\infty}^{2}=\frac{M^{2}}{n},

(14)

and therefore vanishes as
n\to\infty
.

For the off-diagonal part,

\Tr(\rho_{n}A^{(i)}A^{(j)})=\Tr((\rho_{n})_{ij}(A\otimes A))
, whence

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\frac{2}{n^{2}}\sum_{i<j}\Tr(\rho_{n}A^{(i)}A^{(j)})=\frac{n(n-1)}{n^{2}}\mu^{2}+E_{n},

(15)

where the error term
E_{n}
satisfies

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt|E_{n}|\leq\frac{n(n-1)}{n^{2}}M^{2}\left[\frac{1}{\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{i<j}\|(\rho_{n})_{ij}-\rho^{\otimes 2}\|_{1}\right].

(16)

The bracketed average tends to zero by the assumption with
k=2
, so

\Tr(\rho_{n}\overline{A}_{n}^{2})\to\mu^{2}
as
n\to\infty
. This, together with the first-moment convergence
gives
\Tr[\rho_{n}(\overline{A}_{n}-\mu\mathds{1}_{n})^{2}]\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Finally, by functional calculus,

(\overline{A}_{n}-\mu\mathds{1}_{n})^{2}\geq\delta^{2}\{|\overline{A}_{n}-\mu\mathds{1}_{n}|>\delta\}
.
Taking the trace against
\rho_{n}
gives the noncommutative Chebyshev bound

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr\!\left[\rho_{n}\{|\overline{A}_{n}-\mu\mathds{1}_{n}|>\delta\}\right]\leq\delta^{-2}\Tr[\rho_{n}(\overline{A}_{n}-\mu\mathds{1}_{n})^{2}],

(17)

and the right-hand side tends to zero in the limit
n\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma 3

((Universal typical projector))
.

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
, with
\dim\mathcal{H}=d<\infty
, and let

\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{n})_{n}
, with
\rho_{n}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n})
, be weakly almost i.i.d. along
\rho
.
Fix
q\in(0,1)
, set

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\sigma_{q}:=(1-q)\rho+q\mathds{1}/d,

(18)

A_{q}:=-\log\sigma_{q}
, and

h_{q}:=\Tr(\rho A_{q})
.
For
\delta>0
, define the spectral projector

\Pi_{n}:=\left\{-\frac{1}{n}\log\sigma_{q}^{\otimes n}\leq h_{q}+\delta\right\}.

(19)

Then
\Tr(\Pi_{n}\rho_{n})\to 1
as
n\to\infty
, while

\Tr\Pi_{n}\leq e^{n(h_{q}+\delta)}
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Moreover,

h_{q}\to S(\rho)
as
q\to 0
.

Remark
.

This lemma is the basic entropy-concentration result underlying the paper. It shows that every weakly almost i.i.d. source along
\rho
, despite possibly exhibiting strong long-range correlations, is asymptotically supported on subspaces whose dimensions grow asymptotically no faster than
e^{nS(\rho)}
.

Proof.

Since
\sigma_{q}
is full rank,
A_{q}=-\log\sigma_{q}
is bounded. Then

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\overline{A}_{q,n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{q}^{(i)}=-\frac{1}{n}\log\sigma_{q}^{\otimes n},

(20)

and therefore

\Pi_{n}=\{\overline{A}_{q,n}\leq h_{q}+\delta\}
.

Applying Lemma
2
to
A_{q}
gives for every
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr\!\left[\rho_{n}\{|\overline{A}_{q,n}-h_{q}\mathds{1}_{n}|>\delta\}\right]\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(21)

Since

\{\overline{A}_{q,n}>h_{q}+\delta\}\subseteq\{|\overline{A}_{q,n}-h_{q}\mathds{1}_{n}|>\delta\}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr[\rho_{n}(\mathds{1}_{n}-\Pi_{n})]\to 0\quad\text{ as }\quad n\to\infty,

(22)

and hence
\Tr(\Pi_{n}\rho_{n})\to 1
in this limit.

For the rank bound, observe that, by definition of
\Pi_{n}
, the spectrum of

\overline{A}_{q,n}
on
\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{n}
is bounded above by
h_{q}+\delta
. Hence, by functional calculus,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Pi_{n}e^{-n\overline{A}_{q,n}}\Pi_{n}\geq e^{-n(h_{q}+\delta)}\Pi_{n}.

(23)

Since
e^{-n\overline{A}_{q,n}}=\sigma_{q}^{\otimes n}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt1\geq\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{q}^{\otimes n}\Pi_{n})=\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_{n}e^{-n\overline{A}_{q,n}}\Pi_{n})\geq e^{-n(h_{q}+\delta)}\operatorname{Tr}\Pi_{n}.

(24)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr\Pi_{n}\leq e^{n(h_{q}+\delta)}\quad\text{for all}\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(25)

Finally,
\sigma_{q}
commutes with
\rho
. If

\rho=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\ket{i}\!\!\bra{i}
denotes the eigenvalue decomposition of
\rho
, then

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pth_{q}=-\sum_{\lambda_{i}>0}\lambda_{i}\log\!\left((1-q)\lambda_{i}+\frac{q}{d}\right),

(26)

and letting
q\to 0
gives

h_{q}\to-\sum_{\lambda_{i}>0}\lambda_{i}\log\lambda_{i}=S(\rho)
.
∎

4
Applications of the Concentration Lemmas

4.1
Universal quantum data compression for weakly almost i.i.d. sources

The universal entropy-concentration principle of Lemma
3
yields the universal quantum data compression result stated in Theorem
6
below. In contrast to the approach of
[
9
]
, where this result was derived indirectly through a reduction to hypothesis testing within a broader robustness framework, the present proof shows that compression follows directly from entropy concentration via the universal typical-projector construction.

4.1.1
Individual versus universal optimal rates

We first distinguish between compression schemes tailored to a single source and
universal
schemes that work uniformly over all weakly almost i.i.d. sources along the same reference state.

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
, with
\dim\mathcal{H}<\infty
, and let
\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
denote the class of all weakly almost i.i.d. quantum sources along
\rho
.

Definition 4
.

(Optimal rate for an individual weakly almost i.i.d. source along
\rho
)

Let
\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{n})_{n}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
, with
\rho_{n}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n})
.
A number
R\geq 0
is said to be achievable for the source

\bm{\rho}
if there exist Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{K}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
and
encoding (compression) and decoding (decompression) channels

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\pazocal E_{n}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n})\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K}_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\pazocal D_{n}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K}_{n})\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}),

(27)

such that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\dim\mathcal{K}_{n}\leq R,

(28)

and

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptF_{e}\!\left(\rho_{n},\pazocal D_{n}\circ\pazocal E_{n}\right)\longrightarrow 1\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(29)

The optimal compression rate of the source

\bm{\rho}
is defined by

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptR_{\mathrm{opt}}(\bm{\rho}):=\inf\left\{R:R\text{ is achievable for }\bm{\rho}\right\}.

(30)

Definition 5
.

(Optimal universal compression rate for
\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
)

A number
R\geq 0
is said to be a universal achievable rate of data compression for
\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
if there exist sequences of finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{K}_{n}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes n}
, and encoding and decoding (CPTP) maps

\pazocal E_{n}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n})\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K}_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\pazocal D_{n}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K}_{n})\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}),

depending only on
R
and the reference state
\rho
, but not on the individual source,
such that for every
{\bm{\omega}}=(\omega_{n})_{n}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\dim\mathcal{K}_{n}\leq R\quad\text{and}\quad F_{e}(\omega_{n},\pazocal D_{n}\circ\pazocal E_{n})\to 1\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(31)

Any such compression scheme
({\mathcal{E}_{n}},{\mathcal{D}_{n}})_{n}
is said to be a universally reliable compression scheme for
\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}.
The optimal universal compression rate for
\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
is given by

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptR_{\rm opt}^{\rm univ}(\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}):=\inf\{R\,:\,R\text{\, is a universal achievable rate for\,}{\mathfrak{S}}_{\rho}\}

(32)

By definition,

R_{\rm opt}^{\rm univ}(\mathfrak{S}_{\rho})\geq\sup_{\bm{\omega}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}}R_{\rm opt}(\bm{\omega})
,
and the inequality may be strict.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
, with
\dim\mathcal{H}<\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptR_{\rm opt}^{\rm univ}(\mathfrak{S}_{\rho})=S(\rho).

(33)

Proof.

Fix
R>S(\rho)
. By Lemma
3
, there exist
q\in(0,1)
,
\delta>0
, and spectral projectors

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Pi_{n}=\{-\frac{1}{n}\log\sigma_{q}^{\otimes n}\leq h_{q}+\delta\},

(34)

where
\sigma_{q}=(1-q)\rho+q\mathds{1}/d
, such that
for every
\bm{\omega}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr(\Pi_{n}\omega_{n})\to 1\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty,

(35)

Define

\mathcal{K}_{n}

\pazocal C_{n}:=\pazocal D_{n}\circ\pazocal E_{n}

has a Kraus representation with

, and for every

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptF_{e}(\omega_{n},\pazocal C_{n})\geq|\Tr(\omega_{n}\Pi_{n})|^{2}.

Conversely, note that the i.i.d. source

∎

The preceding theorem concerns universal compression over the entire class

into a one-dimensional Hilbert space. Indeed, let
\ket{0}

\pazocal E_{n}(\omega):=\Tr(\omega)\ket{0}\!\bra{0}

\pazocal D_{n}(\ket{0}\!\bra{0}):=\rho_{n}

1

1

More precisely, letting

4.2
Asymmetric binary hypothesis testing

We now apply Lemmas
2
and
3
to asymmetric binary quantum hypothesis testing. The resulting theorem (Theorem
7
) may be viewed as a robustness version of the direct part of quantum Stein’s lemma
[
17
,
18
]
: although the null hypothesis is only weakly almost i.i.d., the optimal universal type-II error exponent (i.e. the quantum Stein exponent) remains unchanged.

Theorem
7
also illustrates the strength of the entropy-concentration framework developed in Section
3
. The same robustness result for quantum hypothesis testing was previously obtained in
[
9
]
, but through a substantially more involved analysis embedded within a broader operational robustness framework. In contrast, the present proof is direct and conceptually transparent: the desired tests arise immediately from the concentration properties established in Lemmas
2
and
3
. In particular, the resulting tests are universal, depending only on the reference states
\rho
and
\sigma
, and not on the individual weakly almost i.i.d. source.

As in the previous section, let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
, with
\dim\mathcal{H}<\infty
, and let
\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
denote the class of all weakly almost i.i.d. quantum sources
{\bm{\rho}}=(\rho_{n})_{n}
along
\rho
.
We consider the following binary hypothesis testing problem:

•

Null hypothesis

H_{0}:
the source belongs to the class
\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
.

•

Alternative hypothesis

H_{1}:
the source is an i.i.d. source
\sigma^{\otimes n}
,
where
\sigma\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{H}})
.

If
0\leq T_{n}\leq{\mathds{1}}_{n}
defines the binary POVM used to distinguish between these two hypotheses, then for any
{\bm{\rho}}=(\rho_{n})_{n}\in{\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}}
, the probabilities of type-I and type-II errors are, respectively, given by

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\alpha_{n}:=\Tr[({\mathds{1}}_{n}-T_{n})\rho_{n}]\quad;\quad\beta_{n}:=\Tr[T_{n}\sigma^{\otimes n}]

(41)

In the asymmetric setting, one minimizes the probability of type-II error (
\beta_{n}
) under the constraint that the probability of type-I error (
\alpha_{n}
) is below some fixed threshold
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
The quantity of interest is the optimal asymptotic type-II error exponent (also called the quantum Stein exponent), which can be expressed in terms of the hypothesis testing relative entropy as follows:

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n}),

(42)

where for any two states
\tau,\omega
on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptD_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\tau\|\omega):=-\log\inf\left\{\Tr(T\omega):0\leq T\leq\mathds{1},\ \Tr[(\mathds{1}-T)\tau]\leq\varepsilon\right\}.

(43)

The celebrated quantum Stein’s lemma
[
18
,
19
]
states that if the null hypothesis is also i.i.d. (say, given by
\rho^{\otimes n}
) and
{\rm supp}\,\rho\subseteq{\rm supp}\,\sigma
, then the quantum Stein exponent given by (
42
) is equal to the Umegaki relative entropy
[
20
]
:

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptD(\rho\|\sigma):=\Tr[\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)]

(44)

Theorem 7
.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
, with
\dim\mathcal{H}<\infty
and
\sigma>0
, and let
\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{n})_{n}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
. Then, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})\geq D(\rho\|\sigma).

(45)

More precisely, for every
R<D(\rho\|\sigma)
, there exists a sequence of tests

(T_{n})_{n}
, depending only on
\rho
and
\sigma
, such that, for every

\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{n})_{n}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr(T_{n}\rho_{n})\to 1\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty\quad\text{while}\quad\Tr(T_{n}\sigma^{\otimes n})\leq e^{-nR}\,\,\text{for all}\,\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(46)

Proof.

Set

A:=-\log\sigma

and

a:=\Tr(\rho A)=\Tr[\rho(-\log\sigma)].

For
\delta>0
, define the spectral projector

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptQ_{n}:=\left\{-\frac{1}{n}\log\sigma^{\otimes n}\geq a-\delta\right\}=\left\{\overline{A}_{n}\geq a-\delta\right\},

(47)

where

\overline{A}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}A^{(i)}.
Thus
Q_{n}
is precisely the spectral projector associated with the
empirical observable appearing in
Lemma
2
. Hence

\Tr(Q_{n}\rho_{n})\to 1

as
n\to\infty
.
Intuitively,
Q_{n}
projects onto the spectral region in which

\sigma^{\otimes n}
is exponentially suppressed at rate
approximately
a
.

Next, fix
q\in(0,1)
. By
Lemma
3
, there exists a sequence of
projections
P_{n}
such that

\Tr(P_{n}\rho_{n})\to 1

as
n\to\infty
, while

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr P_{n}\leq e^{n(h_{q}+\delta)}\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

(48)

where

h_{q}:=\Tr[\rho(-\log\rho_{q})]

and

\rho_{q}:=(1-q)\rho+q\frac{\mathds{1}}{d}.

The projections
P_{n}
provide entropy concentration: they asymptotically capture all of the mass of
\rho_{n}
, while their dimensions grow at most exponentially at rate
h_{q}
.

We now combine the spectral concentration information encoded in
Q_{n}
with the entropy-concentration information encoded in
P_{n}
. Define

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptT_{n}:=Q_{n}P_{n}Q_{n}.

(49)

Since
P_{n}
and
Q_{n}
are projections,

0\leq T_{n}\leq\mathds{1}_{n},

and hence

\{T_{n},\mathds{1}_{n}-T_{n}\}

defines a valid binary POVM. Since

\Tr(Q_{n}\rho_{n})\to 1

as
n\to\infty
, the gentle operator lemma
(see e.g. Lemma 9.4.2 of
[
21
]
) implies

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\|Q_{n}\rho_{n}Q_{n}-\rho_{n}\|_{1}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(50)

Moreover,

\Tr(T_{n}\rho_{n})=\Tr(P_{n}Q_{n}\rho_{n}Q_{n})=\Tr(P_{n}\rho_{n})+\Tr\!\left[P_{n}(Q_{n}\rho_{n}Q_{n}-\rho_{n})\right].

Since
\|P_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\left|\Tr\!\left[P_{n}(Q_{n}\rho_{n}Q_{n}-\rho_{n})\right]\right|\leq\|Q_{n}\rho_{n}Q_{n}-\rho_{n}\|_{1}.

(51)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr(T_{n}\rho_{n})\geq\Tr(P_{n}\rho_{n})-\|Q_{n}\rho_{n}Q_{n}-\rho_{n}\|_{1}.

(52)

Since

\Tr(P_{n}\rho_{n})\to 1

and

\|Q_{n}\rho_{n}Q_{n}-\rho_{n}\|_{1}\to 0,

it follows that

\Tr(T_{n}\rho_{n})\to 1

as
n\to\infty
. Hence, the probability of type-I error for the sequence of binary POVMs defined by
(T_{n})_{n}
approaches
0
, asymptotically.

Next, we analyze the asymptotic behaviour of the corresponding probability of type-II error. Note that
Q_{n}
commutes with
\sigma^{\otimes n}
. Moreover, on

{\rm{Ran}}\,Q_{n}
,

-\frac{1}{n}\log\sigma^{\otimes n}\geq a-\delta.

Hence, by functional calculus,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptQ_{n}\sigma^{\otimes n}Q_{n}\leq e^{-n(a-\delta)}Q_{n}.

(53)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr(T_{n}\sigma^{\otimes n})=\Tr(Q_{n}P_{n}Q_{n}\sigma^{\otimes n})\leq e^{-n(a-\delta)}\Tr(P_{n})\leq e^{-n(a-h_{q}-2\delta)}.

(54)

Finally,

a-h_{q}\to\Tr[\rho(-\log\sigma)]-S(\rho)=D(\rho\|\sigma)

as
q\to 0
. Hence, choosing
q
and
\delta
sufficiently small
yields

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr(T_{n}\sigma^{\otimes n})\leq e^{-nR}\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

(55)

for any
R<D(\rho\|\sigma)
.

Since

\Tr(T_{n}\rho_{n})\to 1,

for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, the POVM

\{T_{n},\mathds{1}_{n}-T_{n}\}

is feasible in the definition of

D_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})
for all sufficiently
large
n
. Hence

D_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})\geq nR

for all sufficiently large
n
. Dividing by
n
, taking the limit
inferior, and then letting

R\uparrow D(\rho\|\sigma)

proves (
45
).
∎

Remark
.

The theorem is optimal in the universal setting. Indeed, the i.i.d. source

(\rho^{\otimes n})_{n}
belongs to
\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
, so any universal testing strategy
must in particular work for the genuinely i.i.d. null hypothesis. The strong converse part of
quantum Stein’s lemma therefore rules out any universal exponent larger than

D(\rho\|\sigma)
. Thus, in the universal setting, the quantum Stein exponent is the same as in
the i.i.d. case.

Remark
.

By contrast, an individual-source converse fails in general. Take

\rho=\sigma=\mathds{1}/d
, so that
D(\rho\|\sigma)=0
, and let

\rho_{n}=\ket{\psi_{n}}\!\bra{\psi_{n}}
, where

\ket{\psi_{n}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
is Haar-random and

d:=\dim\mathcal{H}
. As shown in Section
2
, with probability one the sequence
(\rho_{n})_{n}
is weakly almost i.i.d. along
\mathds{1}/d
. Choosing the source-dependent test
T_{n}:=\rho_{n}
, we have

\Tr(T_{n}\rho_{n})=1
and
\Tr(T_{n}\sigma^{\otimes n})=\frac{1}{d^{n}}
, and hence for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1),

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})\geq\log d>D(\rho\|\sigma).

(56)

Thus no individual-source converse of the form

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})\leq D(\rho\|\sigma)

can hold for arbitrary weakly almost i.i.d. null hypotheses.

Remark
.

Note that our proof of Theorem
7
is tailored to the setting in which the weakly almost i.i.d. source appears in the null hypothesis. Indeed, Lemma
3
provides universal projectors onto subspaces capturing asymptotically all of the weight of every weakly almost i.i.d. source along a fixed reference state, which is precisely what is needed to ensure asymptotically vanishing type-I error.

If instead the null hypothesis is i.i.d. and the alternative hypothesis is weakly almost i.i.d., one would need upper bounds on quantities of the form
\Tr(T_{n}\sigma_{n})
, where
(\sigma_{n})_{n}
is weakly almost i.i.d. and the tests
(T_{n})_{n}
are designed to accept the i.i.d. null hypothesis. Our concentration lemmas do not yield such bounds, since the weakly almost i.i.d. structure constrains only fixed-size marginals while permitting arbitrary long-range correlations in the global alternative states.

4.3
Many-body concentration and generalized Gibbs ensembles

We next apply Lemma
2
to quantum many-body systems. Here

\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
is interpreted as the Hilbert space of an
n
-body system with local
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and a weakly almost i.i.d. source

\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{n})_{n}
describes a sequence of correlated many-body states whose fixed-size
local marginals asymptotically agree, on average, with those of the product state

\rho^{\otimes n}
. The following theorem shows that, despite possible long-range
correlations and multipartite entanglement, empirical averages of finitely many commuting
one-site observables concentrate around the values predicted by the reference state
\rho\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{H}})
.

Theorem 8

((Thermodynamic typicality))
.

Let
\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{n})_{n}
, with
\rho_{n}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n})
, be weakly almost i.i.d. along
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
. Let

A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be mutually commuting self-adjoint observables, and define

a_{j}:=\Tr(\rho A_{j})
and

\overline{A}_{j,n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{j}^{(i)},
where

A_{j}^{(i)}=\mathds{1}^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes A_{j}\otimes\mathds{1}^{\otimes(n-i)}
.
For
\delta>0
, let

P_{j,n}:=\{|\overline{A}_{j,n}-a_{j}\mathds{1}_{n}|\leq\delta\}
.
Then

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr\!\left[\rho_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m}P_{j,n}\right]\to 1\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(57)

Equivalently, for every fixed
\delta>0
, the probability that at least one empirical observable
\overline{A}_{j,n}
takes a value outside the interval
[a_{j}-\delta,a_{j}+\delta]
tends to zero as
n\to\infty
.

The theorem says that, for any finite family of commuting one-site observables, the corresponding empirical observables concentrate around the values predicted by the reference state
\rho
, even for highly correlated many-body states which are weakly almost i.i.d. along
\rho
. Hence, weakly almost i.i.d. many-body states are thermodynamically indistinguishable from the corresponding i.i.d. state
\rho^{\otimes n}
at the level of finitely many empirical observables, despite possibly containing arbitrary long-range correlations and multipartite entanglement.

Remark
.

In a spin-
\frac{1}{2}
system with local Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
, one may take

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptA_{1}=\sigma_{z},\,\,A_{2}=\frac{\mathds{1}+\sigma_{z}}{2}=|\uparrow\rangle\!\langle\uparrow|.

(58)

The corresponding empirical observables describe respectively the average magnetization per site in the
z
-direction and the density of spin-up sites.

For a spin-
1
lattice, with local Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{3}
, one may instead take

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptA_{1}=S_{z},\,\,A_{2}=S_{z}^{2},

(59)

where
S_{z}
denotes the usual spin-
z
operator. The associated empirical observables correspond respectively to the magnetization density and quadrupole density. Theorem
8
then implies that these empirical observables concentrate around the values predicted by the reference state
\rho
, even though the underlying many-body states need not be close to the i.i.d. state
\rho^{\otimes n}
.

Proof.

Since the observables
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
commute, so do the empirical observables
\overline{A}_{1,n},\ldots,

\overline{A}_{m,n}
and their spectral projections
P_{1,n},\ldots,P_{m,n}
. From Lemma
2
we have that, for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\},

\Tr[\rho_{n}(\mathds{1}_{n}-P_{j,n})]\to 0

as
n\to\infty
. Using the operator inequality

\mathds{1}_{n}-\prod_{j=1}^{m}P_{j,n}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\mathds{1}_{n}-P_{j,n})
, which follows by simultaneous diagonalization of the commuting projections
P_{j,n}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt1-\Tr\!\left[\rho_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m}P_{j,n}\right]\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Tr[\rho_{n}(\mathds{1}_{n}-P_{j,n})]\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(60)

∎

Integrable quantum many-body systems possess, in addition to the Hamiltonian, an extensive family of mutually commuting conserved quantities

Q_{1},Q_{2},\ldots

that strongly constrain their dynamics, preventing conventional thermalization to the Gibbs state
2
2

2

By contrast, for generic non-integrable systems, equilibrium behaviour is often expected to be governed by the Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis (ETH) (see e.g.
[
22
,
23
]
), according to which local observables thermalize to ordinary Gibbs states determined solely by the energy density.
. Typical examples include quantum spin chains such as the XXZ model, whose conserved quantities contain the Hamiltonian, total magnetization, and higher-order multi-site observables. As a consequence of these additional conservation laws, such systems generally equilibrate not to ordinary Gibbs states, but rather to generalized Gibbs ensembles (GGEs) (see e.g.
[
24
]
) of the form

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\gamma_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\frac{\exp\!\left(-\lambda_{0}H-\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}Q_{j}\right)}{\Tr\!\left[\exp\!\left(-\lambda_{0}H-\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}Q_{j}\right)\right]},

(61)

where
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})
is a collection of fixed real parameters
3
3

3

The parameters
\lambda_{j}
arise as Lagrange multipliers enforcing fixed expectation values of the conserved quantities
H,Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{m}
in the maximum-entropy principle underlying generalized Gibbs ensembles. In particular,
\lambda_{0}
plays the role of the inverse temperature associated with the Hamiltonian
H
, while the remaining parameters may be interpreted as generalized chemical potentials corresponding to the additional conserved quantities.
.

We now specialize to the setting in which the conserved quantities

Q_{1},Q_{2},\ldots
are one-site observables, and consider quantum sources

\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{n})_{n}
that are weakly almost i.i.d. along a fixed GGE state

\gamma_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
.
The following corollary shows that such sources exhibit the same thermodynamic concentration behaviour, at the level of empirical observables, as the corresponding i.i.d. tensor-power source

(\gamma_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{\otimes n})_{n}.

Corollary 9

((GGE typicality))
.

Let
\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{n})_{n}
be weakly almost i.i.d. along
\gamma_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
. Then, for every
\delta>0
, the empirical observables

\overline{H}_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}H^{(i)}\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{Q}_{\ell,n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}Q_{\ell}^{(i)}

simultaneously concentrate around their GGE expectation values:

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr\!\left[\rho_{n}\left\{|\overline{H}_{n}-\Tr(\gamma_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}H)\mathds{1}_{n}|\leq\delta\right\}\prod_{\ell=1}^{m}\left\{|\overline{Q}_{\ell,n}-\Tr(\gamma_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}Q_{\ell})\mathds{1}_{n}|\leq\delta\right\}\right]\to 1\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(62)

Proof.

Apply Theorem
8
with reference state
\rho=\gamma_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and commuting observables
H,Q_{1},\ldots,

\ldots,Q_{m}
.
∎

Thus, even in the presence of arbitrary long-range correlations and multipartite
entanglement, weakly almost i.i.d. states along a GGE reproduce the same
macroscopic equilibrium behaviour as the corresponding tensor-power GGE state.

4.4
Measurement statistics of weakly almost i.i.d. sources

We next show that weakly almost i.i.d. structure is reflected directly in the statistics of local measurements. In particular, empirical outcome frequencies obtained from repeated local measurements concentrate around the probabilities predicted by the reference state, just as in the genuinely i.i.d. setting.

Theorem 10

((Concentration of empirical measurement frequencies))
.

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
, with
\dim\mathcal{H}<\infty
, and let

\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{n})_{n}
, with

\rho_{n}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n})
, be weakly almost i.i.d. along
\rho
.
Let
\mathsf{M}=\{M_{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
be a POVM on
\mathcal{H}
, where
\mathcal{X}
is finite, and define

p(x):=\Tr(\rho M_{x})
.
Measuring
\mathsf{M}^{\otimes n}
on
\rho_{n}
induces a probability distribution
p_{n}
on
\mathcal{X}^{n}
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{n}
denote probabilities with respect to
p_{n}
. If
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\sim p_{n}
, define the empirical frequencies

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\hat{p}_{n}(x):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{i}=x\}},\qquad x\in\mathcal{X}.

(63)

Then, for every
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt{\mathbb{P}}_{n}\!\left(\max_{x\in\mathcal{X}}|\hat{p}_{n}(x)-p(x)|>\delta\right)\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(64)

Remark
.

Thus, although weakly almost i.i.d. sources may exhibit strong global correlations, the empirical statistics produced by repeated local measurements with the POVM
\mathsf{M}
are asymptotically indistinguishable from those obtained by independently measuring the reference state
\rho
with the same POVM. Equivalently, weakly almost i.i.d. structure is operationally stable under any fixed local measurement: the empirical measurement statistics obey the same asymptotic law of large numbers as genuinely independent samples. In particular, such correlations are asymptotically invisible to standard repeated local-measurement and tomography protocols based on empirical outcome frequencies.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}
denote the CPTP measurement map associated with the POVM
M
4

4

That is, for any

4.5
Smooth zero-Rényi entropy bounds

Smooth entropy quantities (see e.g.
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
) play a central role in one-shot quantum information theory,
where they characterize operational tasks such as data compression, randomness extraction,
and hypothesis testing in the non-asymptotic regime. In the asymptotic i.i.d. setting,
smooth entropies converge to the von Neumann entropy and related Shannon-theoretic
quantities. We now show that the universal entropy-concentration principle of
Lemma
3
directly yields an asymptotic upper bound on the smooth zero-Rényi entropy for weakly almost i.i.d. sources.

Lemma 11

(Smooth zero-Rényi entropy bound)
.

Let
\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{n})_{n}
be weakly almost i.i.d. along a state

\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
, where
\dim\mathcal{H}<\infty
.
For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, define

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptH_{0}^{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n}):=\inf\left\{\log(\Tr P):\ P\text{ is a projection and }\Tr(P\rho_{n})\geq 1-\varepsilon\right\}.

(71)

Then

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{0}^{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n})\leq S(\rho).

(72)

Remark
.

The quantity
H_{0}^{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n})
measures the logarithmic dimension of the
smallest subspace capturing at least
1-\varepsilon
of the mass of
\rho_{n}
. It therefore has a direct operational interpretation in one-shot quantum information theory, where it characterizes the size of approximate compression subspaces required for one-shot quantum compression of the state
\rho_{n}
.
Lemma
11
shows that every weakly almost i.i.d. source
asymptotically concentrates on subspaces whose exponential dimension is governed asymptotically by the entropy of the reference state
\rho
,
just as in the genuinely i.i.d. setting.

For genuinely i.i.d. sources, the quantum asymptotic equipartition property (AEP)
establishes that suitably normalized smooth entropy quantities converge to the von
Neumann entropy in the asymptotic limit; see e.g.
[
28
]
. By contrast,
Lemma
11
applies to the substantially broader class of
weakly almost i.i.d. sources, which may exhibit arbitrary long-range correlations and
entanglement. In this setting one cannot expect asymptotic equality in general:
globally pure weakly almost i.i.d. sources along the maximally mixed state provide
counterexamples. The lemma should therefore be viewed as an entropy-concentration
upper bound rather than a full asymptotic equipartition property.

Proof.

Fix
R>S(\rho)
. Since
h_{q}\to S(\rho)
as
q\to 0
,
we may choose
q\in(0,1)
and
\delta>0
such that

h_{q}+\delta<R
,
where

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\sigma_{q}:=(1-q)\rho+q\frac{\mathds{1}}{d},\qquad h_{q}:=-\Tr(\rho\log\sigma_{q}).

(73)

For these choices of
q
and
\delta
, Lemma
3

provides projectors

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Pi_{n}=\left\{-\frac{1}{n}\log\sigma_{q}^{\otimes n}\leq h_{q}+\delta\right\}

(74)

satisfying

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr(\Pi_{n}\rho_{n})\to 1\qquad\text{as}\qquad n\to\infty,

(75)

together with the rank bound

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr\Pi_{n}\leq e^{n(h_{q}+\delta)}<e^{nR}

(76)

for every sufficiently large
n
.

Now fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Since

\Tr(\Pi_{n}\rho_{n})\to 1,
as
n\to\infty
,
there exists
n_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr(\Pi_{n}\rho_{n})\geq 1-\varepsilon\qquad\forall\,n\geq n_{0}.

(77)

Hence, for all sufficiently large
n
, the projector
\Pi_{n}
is admissible in the definition
of
H_{0}^{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptH_{0}^{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n})\leq\log\Tr\Pi_{n}<nR

(78)

for all sufficiently large
n
. Dividing by
n
and taking the limit superior yields

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{0}^{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n})\leq R.

(79)

Since
R>S(\rho)
was arbitrary, the result follows.
∎

4.6
Spectral entropy rates and entropy concentration

The information-spectrum approach, initiated in the classical setting by Han and Verdú
and subsequently extended to quantum information theory by Hayashi and Nagaoka
[
29
]
(see also, e.g.
[
30
]
and references therein),
provides a powerful framework for studying information-theoretic tasks beyond the
i.i.d. setting. Rather than relying on asymptotic equipartition properties or tensor-product
structure, the information-spectrum method applies to completely general sequences of
states and channels, possibly exhibiting arbitrary correlations and non-stationary behaviour.

Within this framework, spectral entropy rates play a central role. In particular, the
spectral sup-entropy rate admits an operational interpretation as the optimal asymptotic
quantum compression rate for arbitrary quantum sources
[
31
]
. Thus spectral entropy rates generalize the role
played by the von Neumann entropy in Schumacher compression to fully non-i.i.d. settings.

We now show that the universal concentration projectors of Lemma
3

directly yield bounds on spectral entropy quantities for weakly almost i.i.d. sources.
The key observation is that the projectors produced by Lemma
3

already satisfy the asymptotic concentration and dimension-growth conditions appearing
in the projector characterization of the spectral sup-entropy rate.

We use the definition of the spectral sup-entropy rate introduced in
[
32
]
, which, as shown in that paper, is equivalent to the definition given in
[
29
]
.

Definition 12

((Spectral sup-entropy rate))
.

Let
\bm{\omega}=(\omega_{n})_{n}
, with

\omega_{n}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n})
.
For
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, define

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptA_{n}(\gamma):=\omega_{n}-e^{-n\gamma}\mathds{1}_{n},

(80)

where
\mathds{1}_{n}
denotes the identity operator on
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
.
The spectral sup-entropy rate of
\bm{\omega}
is

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\overline{S}(\bm{\omega}):=\inf\left\{\gamma:\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Tr\!\left[\{A_{n}(\gamma)\geq 0\}A_{n}(\gamma)\right]=1\right\}.

(81)

The spectral sup-entropy rate admits the following projector characterization. We include its proof for completeness.

Proposition 13

((Projector characterization of the spectral sup-entropy rate))
.

For every sequence
\bm{\omega}=(\omega_{n})_{n}
,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\overline{S}(\bm{\omega})=\inf\left\{R:\exists\ \text{projections }P_{n}\text{ such that }\Tr(P_{n}\omega_{n})\to 1\text{\,as }n\to\infty\,;\,\,\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\Tr P_{n}\leq R\right\}.

(82)

Proof.

Let
R
be such that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Tr[\{\omega_{n}\geq e^{-nR}\mathds{1}_{n}\}(\omega_{n}-e^{-nR}\mathds{1}_{n})]=1.

Define
P_{n}:=\{\omega_{n}\geq e^{-nR}\mathds{1}_{n}\}
.
By the definition of
\overline{S}(\bm{\omega})
,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Tr\!\left[P_{n}(\omega_{n}-e^{-nR}\mathds{1}_{n})\right]=1.

(83)

Since

\Tr\!\left[P_{n}(\omega_{n}-e^{-nR}\mathds{1}_{n})\right]\leq\Tr(P_{n}\omega_{n})\leq 1

for every
n
, it follows that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr\!\left[P_{n}(\omega_{n}-e^{-nR}\mathds{1}_{n})\right]\to 1\quad\text{as }\quad n\to\infty.

(84)

As

\Tr(P_{n}\omega_{n})\geq\Tr\!\left[P_{n}(\omega_{n}-e^{-nR}\mathds{1}_{n})\right]

and
\Tr(P_{n}\omega_{n})\leq 1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr(P_{n}\omega_{n})\to 1\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(85)

Moreover, since every eigenvalue of
\omega_{n}
on
{\rm{Ran}}\,P_{n}
is at least

e^{-nR}
,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt1\geq\Tr(P_{n}\omega_{n})\geq e^{-nR}\Tr P_{n},

(86)

and hence
\Tr P_{n}\leq e^{nR}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Conversely, suppose that
P_{n}
are projections satisfying

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr(P_{n}\omega_{n})\to 1\,\,\text{as}\,\,n\to\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\Tr P_{n}\leq R.

(87)

Fix
\eta>0
. Then
\Tr P_{n}\leq e^{n(R+\eta)}
for all sufficiently large
n
. Define

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptQ_{n}:=\{\omega_{n}\geq e^{-n(R+2\eta)}\mathds{1}_{n}\}.

(88)

Since
Q_{n}
is the positive spectral projection of

\omega_{n}-e^{-n(R+2\eta)}\mathds{1}_{n},

the Ky Fan maximum principle (see e.g.
[
33
]
) gives

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr\!\left[Q_{n}(\omega_{n}-e^{-n(R+2\eta)}\mathds{1}_{n})\right]\geq\Tr\!\left[P_{n}(\omega_{n}-e^{-n(R+2\eta)}\mathds{1}_{n})\right].

(89)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr\!\left[Q_{n}(\omega_{n}-e^{-n(R+2\eta)}\mathds{1}_{n})\right]\geq\Tr(P_{n}\omega_{n})-e^{-n(R+2\eta)}\Tr P_{n}\geq\Tr(P_{n}\omega_{n})-e^{-n\eta}.

(90)

The right-hand side of (
90
) tends to
1
as
n\to\infty
. Since the left-hand side is bounded above by

\Tr(Q_{n}\omega_{n})\leq 1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\Tr\!\left[Q_{n}(\omega_{n}-e^{-n(R+2\eta)}\mathds{1}_{n})\right]\to 1\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(91)

By the definition of the spectral sup-entropy rate, this implies

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\overline{S}(\bm{\omega})\leq R+2\eta.

(92)

Letting
\eta\to 0
gives the claim.

Let
\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{n})_{n}

Fix

5
Conclusions and open questions

The results of this paper show that several characteristic features of the i.i.d. regime persist under the weakest of the currently studied forms of approximate i.i.d. structure. We identified two fundamental concentration principles for weakly almost i.i.d. quantum sources: a noncommutative weak law of large numbers for empirical observables, and a universal entropy-concentration principle yielding asymptotic concentration on subspaces whose exponential dimension is governed by the von Neumann entropy of the reference state. Together, these principles provide a unified and conceptually transparent framework for a range of information-theoretic applications beyond the tensor-power setting, including universal quantum data compression within classes of weakly almost i.i.d. sources sharing a common reference state, asymmetric quantum hypothesis testing, concentration results for quantum many-body systems and generalized Gibbs ensembles, concentration of repeated local measurement statistics, as well as bounds on smooth and spectral entropy quantities. In several cases, the resulting proofs are substantially more direct than the previous robustness-based approach of [9], with the relevant operational statements emerging naturally from the underlying concentration principles.

A number of natural open questions remain. One important direction is to
extend the present framework to more general quantum information-theoretic
tasks, such as quantum communication, entanglement distillation, dense
coding, and decoupling-based protocols for weakly almost i.i.d. sources.
It would also be interesting to investigate possible second-order and
moderate-deviation refinements of the present results. On the many-body
side, an important problem is to extend the thermodynamic concentration
results beyond one-site observables to more general local or quasi-local
conserved quantities.
