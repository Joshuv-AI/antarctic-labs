PrismQuant: Rate–Distortion-Optimal Vector Quantization for Gaussian-Mixture Sources
Bumsu Park, Chanho Park, Youngmok Park, Namyoon Lee

Abstract
For a Gaussian source under mean-squared error (MSE), classical transform coding is rate–distortion (RD) optimal: the Karhunen–Loève transform (KLT) diagonalizes the covariance, reverse waterfilling allocates the bits, and scalar quantization closes the loop. This elegant story breaks down for multimodal sources, where no single covariance can capture heterogeneous local geometries and the RD function loses its closed form. We revisit this problem through Gaussian-mixture sources and develop a constructive RD theory for them. Our key finding is that mixture structure costs only a component label . Conditioned on the active mixture component, each branch is Gaussian; the challenge is allocating bits across heterogeneous branches. We prove that the genie-aided conditional RD function is governed by a single global reverse-waterfilling level shared across all components and eigenmodes. Building on this result, we introduce PrismQuant , which transmits the component label losslessly and encodes the residual using the component-matched KLT followed by scalar quantization, achieving a rate within H ⁡ ( C ) / n H(C)/n bits per source dimension of the converse with a vanishing asymptotic gap. We further develop a practical implementation based on EM-driven Gaussian-mixture learning, component-adaptive KLTs, and entropy-constrained scalar quantization (ECSQ). Experiments on synthetic Gaussian mixtures show that PrismQuant closely approaches the theoretical RD bound, while experiments on real-world channel-state-information (CSI) data demonstrate competitive or superior performance compared with transformer-based learned codecs at more than one order of magnitude smaller model size.

Setting.

We focus on the most analytically tractable and operationally meaningful class of multimodal sources:
Gaussian mixtures
. We use
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
to denote the random source vector and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
a generic realization. The source distribution is

\displaystyle p_{X}(x)=\sum_{c=1}^{K}\pi_{c}\,\mathcal{N}(x;\,\mu_{c},R_{c}),

(1)

where

K

X\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{c},R_{c})
with mean
\mu_{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

R_{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

far less restrictive than it first appears

arbitrary

The CSI compression as a motivating application.

Compression of high-dimensional multimodal sources is a fundamental challenge in modern data-driven systems. A particularly important example arises in frequency-division-duplex (FDD) massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) orthogonal-frequency-division-multiplexing (OFDM) wireless systems, where accurate channel-state information (CSI) feedback is critical for reliable communication
[
6
,
7
]
. Since CSI is inherently high-dimensional due to large antenna arrays and many subcarriers, compression is unavoidable under strict bandwidth constraints. While CSI under fixed propagation conditions is well approximated by a correlated Gaussian model
[
8
]
, realistic CSI aggregated across users and environments exhibits strongly multimodal structure, naturally motivating Gaussian-mixture modeling
[
9
]
. In this regime, single-covariance KLT coding becomes fundamentally mismatched, whereas modern learned CSI codecs
[
10
,
11
]
often rely on large black-box neural architectures.
PrismQuant
addresses this gap through an explicit constructive RD theory together with a low-complexity and interpretable coding framework.

Contributions.

(i) First explicit constructive RD characterization for Gaussian-mixture sources.

We extend the explicit, constructive RD theory, previously known only for single-covariance Gaussian sources, to general Gaussian mixtures via a converse-achievability
sandwich
. The resulting converse–achievability gap is exactly the amortized label entropy
H(C)/n
and vanishes asymptotically whenever the mixture order satisfies
K=2^{o(n)}
. By Gaussian-mixture universality, the result further lifts to arbitrary multimodal sources through EM-based mixture fitting. Conditional source coding
[
2
,
3
]
provides a general theoretical framework, but obtaining an explicit RD characterization together with a constructive code for multimodal sources has remained elusive.
Why has this been hard?
The difficulty stems from the fact that the unconditional Gaussian-mixture density admits no tractable entropy characterization; even sharp bounds and closed-form approximations for mixture entropy are themselves nontrivial
[
12
]
. Our key idea is to augment the source with the latent component label, converting the non-Gaussian entropy into a sum of Gaussian entropies plus an exact label cost
H(C)
. This yields a separable convex optimization whose Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions induce a single global reverse-waterfilling level shared across all mixture components.

(ii) PrismQuant codec.
We propose
PrismQuant
, a practical two-stage lossless–lossy coding framework for Gaussian-mixture sources that attains the achievability bound under correct label detection. The codec first learns the underlying Gaussian-mixture distribution via expectation–maximization (EM), estimates the component label through maximum-a-posteriori (MAP) inference, and transmits the label losslessly. Conditioned on the detected component,
PrismQuant
then applies the component-matched KLT followed by entropy-constrained scalar quantization (ECSQ) and entropy coding across the resulting eigenmodes. The entire pipeline admits closed-form operations at every stage and yields a fully practical bitstream-level implementation with low complexity and strong interpretability.

(iii) One global reverse-waterfilling level.

Both the converse and the achievability are governed by a
single global
reverse-waterfilling level
\mu^{\star}
shared across the eigenmodes of
all
mixture components. The Gaussian-mixture RD problem therefore collapses onto the same one-dial allocation structure as classical single-covariance Gaussian transform coding (TC), despite the presence of heterogeneous eigenbases. This is fundamentally different from prior classified TC methods such as WUTC
[
13
]
, which use independent per-component water levels
\mu_{c}^{\star}
and therefore cannot perform globally optimal rate allocation with respect to the full mixture prior. In contrast, the shared water level in
PrismQuant
induces more practical cross-component eigenmode pruning and yields the genie-aided conditional RD characterization up to the amortized label cost
H(C)/n
.

(iv) Validation on synthetic mixtures and real-world CSI data.

On synthetic Gaussian-mixture sources, we sweep the mixture order
K
at fixed source dimension
n
and show that
PrismQuant
with MAP-estimated component labels closely tracks both the genie-aided oracle and the theoretical RD bound across varying
K
, while single-covariance TC exhibits an increasingly severe mismatch gap as multimodality grows. On the real-world DeepMIMO dataset
[
14
]
,
PrismQuant
substantially improves over classical TC and achieves competitive reconstruction performance against learned CSI compression schemes including CsiNet
[
10
]
and Swin-NTC
[
11
]
, while requiring one to two orders of magnitude smaller model size.

Classified vector quantization and weighted universal transform coding (WUTC).

Adaptive multiple transform (AMT) in standardized video codecs.

A standardized, hand-designed instance of the library-of-transforms idea is the

(AMT)—also called Multiple Transform Selection (MTS)—adopted in HEVC and VVC. The concept was first introduced by Han, Saxena, and Rose

PrismQuant

Gaussian-Mixture Source and Universality

Problem setup.

We consider an independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random vector source
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
with
X_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
drawn from the real Gaussian mixture (
1
);

x_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes a generic realization. A blocklength-

f_{m}:(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}\to\{1,\ldots,2^{mnR}\}
and
g_{m}:\{1,\ldots,2^{mnR}\}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}

is the infimum of all achievable rates at distortion
D

[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

Why Gaussian mixtures?

Many real-world sources are well modeled as locally Gaussian within a homogeneous regime, but globally non-stationary across regimes (e.g., channel responses across propagation geometries
[
32
,
33
]
, image patches across textures
[
34
,
35
]
, semantic features across classes
[
28
,
36
]
, and speech or acoustic features across speakers
[
37
]

1
below, the model is universal: any continuous integrable density admits an arbitrarily accurate Gaussian-mixture surrogate.

Lemma 1

(Universality of Gaussian mixtures
[
5
]
)
.

For any continuous integrable density
p
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
K
, weights
\{\pi_{c}\}_{c=1}^{K}
, means
\{\mu_{c}\}_{c=1}^{K}
, and covariances
\{\Sigma_{c}\}_{c=1}^{K}
such that
p_{K}(x)=\sum_{c}\pi_{c}\mathcal{N}(x;\mu_{c},\Sigma_{c})
satisfies
\int|p-p_{K}|\,dx<\varepsilon
.

Composing Lemma
1
with
PrismQuant
gives a constructive codec for an
arbitrary
continuous multimodal source: learn the parameters of Gaussian mixture distribution by EM and feed it to the encoder. The explicit RD theory below is stated for the finite mixture
(
1
), but its operational reach is the entire class of continuous multimodal densities.

Lemma 2

(Entropy decomposition)
.

Let
C\in[K]
have prior
\{\pi_{c}\}_{c=1}^{K}
and let
\left.X\right|\{C=c\}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{c},R_{c})
. Then
h(X)=h(\left.X\right|C)+I(X;C)
and

\displaystyle h(\left.X\right|C)\leq h(X)\leq h(\left.X\right|C)+H(C),\quad h(\left.X\right|C)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{c=1}^{K}\pi_{c}\log_{2}\bigl[(2\pi e)^{n}\det R_{c}\bigr],

(2)

where the conditional differential entropy depends on
\{R_{c}\}_{c=1}^{K}
only (component means
\{\mu_{c}\}_{c=1}^{K}
contribute zero entropy because differential entropy is translation-invariant).

The lower endpoint
h(\left.X\right|C)
is the genie-aided benchmark in which the active component is known for free; the upper endpoint corresponds to (1)
lossless label coding
and (2)
component-matched lossy transform coding
. This is the organizing principle of
PrismQuant
: the discrete, multimodal part of the source is absorbed into a low-rate label stream, leaving a textbook Gaussian transform-coding problem within the selected branch. The non-oracle price is exactly the normalized label cost
H(C)/n
. A complementary view of the same decomposition, including sharp bounds and closed-form approximations of the unconditional mixture entropy
h(X)
in terms of pairwise component overlaps, is developed in
Lee 2026
, providing a route from raw mixture parameters

4
The PrismQuant Codec

PrismQuant
is a practical hybrid lossless–lossy coding framework for the Gaussian-mixture source (
1
). The mixture parameters
\theta=\{(\pi_{c},\mu_{c},R_{c})\}_{c=1}^{K}
are learned offline (e.g., EM on training samples) and stored as a shared probabilistic dictionary at both the encoder and decoder; only the component label and the coded transform coefficients are transmitted online. The entire pipeline admits closed-form operations at every stage and yields a fully practical bitstream-level implementation.

Encoder.

Let
R_{\rm tot}
denote the total budget in bits/dim.
(1)
Component label estimation:
the encoder selects the most likely mixture component using MAP estimation, i.e.,
\hat{c}(x)=\mathop{\mathrm{arg\,max}}_{c\in[K]}\pi_{c}\,\mathcal{N}(x;\mu_{c},R_{c}).

(2)
Lossless label coding:
the estimated label
\hat{c}
is entropy coded; the normalized label rate is
R_{\rm lbl}=H(C)/n
.
(3)
Mean centering and component KLT:
the encoder forms the centered residual
s=U_{\hat{c}}^{\top}(x-\mu_{\hat{c}})
, where
R_{\hat{c}}=U_{\hat{c}}\Lambda_{\hat{c}}U_{\hat{c}}^{\top}
is the spectral decomposition. The transform coefficients are independent and
s_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\lambda_{\hat{c},i}),\,\forall i\in[n]
.
(4)
Global reverse-waterfilling:
given the residual budget
R_{\rm q}=R_{\rm tot}-R_{\rm lbl}
, a single water level
\mu\geq 0
allocates per-eigenmode rate and distortion across
all
components by

\displaystyle d_{c,i}^{\star}(\mu)=\min\{\lambda_{c,i},\,\mu\},\qquad r_{c,i}^{\star}(\mu)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\log_{2}(\lambda_{c,i}/\mu)\bigr]_{+},

(3)

chosen so that
R_{\rm q}=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{c}\pi_{c}\sum_{i}r_{c,i}^{\star}(\mu)
(see Theorem
1
).
(5)
Scalar quantization and entropy coding:
active coefficients (
\lambda_{\hat{c},i}>\mu
) are quantized at the allocated rate using ECSQ
[
38
]
, while inactive coefficients are set to zero.
(6)
Reconstruction:
the decoder reconstructs
\hat{x}=\mu_{\hat{c}}+U_{\hat{c}}\hat{s}
. The overall encoding and decoding pipeline of
PrismQuant
is illustrated in Figure
1
.

Cross-component pruning.

Because the same global water level
\mu
thresholds the eigenvalues of
every
component, eigenmodes satisfying
\lambda_{c,i}\leq\mu
need not be stored, transmitted, or processed during runtime. Consequently,
PrismQuant
retains only the active eigenmodes that receive nonzero rate allocation under reverse waterfilling, significantly reducing both the effective model footprint and the coding complexity. Classical Gaussian reverse waterfilling performs pruning only within a single fixed eigenbasis; in contrast, the multimodal setting induces pruning jointly across heterogeneous component-dependent eigenbases under a
single
global allocation parameter. The resulting runtime memory and per-vector encode/decode complexity therefore scale as
\Theta(nL_{c}(\mu))
, where
L_{c}(\mu)=|\{i:\lambda_{c,i}>\mu\}|
denotes the number of active eigenmodes in component
c
.

For a multimodal source, the textbook intuition is one component-specific water level per component plus another to balance the component budgets. Theorem

MAP label error in high dimension.

Practical
PrismQuant
replaces the oracle label with a hard MAP decision
\hat{C}(x)=\mathop{\mathrm{arg\,max}}_{c}\pi_{c}\mathcal{N}(x;\mu_{c},R_{c})

P_{e}^{\rm MAP}\leq\sum_{c<j}\sqrt{\pi_{c}\pi_{j}}\,\rho_{c,j}^{(n)}

\rho_{c,j}^{(n)}=\int\sqrt{f_{c}f_{j}}\,dx

7
Conclusion

We presented
PrismQuant
, a constructive transform-coding framework for Gaussian-mixture sources. By augmenting the source with the latent component label, we derived an explicit converse–achievability sandwich with gap
H(C)/n
and showed that the entire multimodal RD problem is governed by a single global reverse-waterfilling level shared across all components and eigenmodes. We further developed a fully practical coding pipeline based on EM-driven Gaussian-mixture learning, MAP component inference, component-matched KLTs, and ECSQ. Experiments on synthetic Gaussian mixtures demonstrated that
PrismQuant
closely approaches the theoretical RD bound, while experiments on the DeepMIMO CSI dataset showed competitive or superior performance compared with transformer-based learned codecs at two orders of magnitude smaller model size and complexity. Extending the experimental validation to broader multimodal source classes remains an important direction for future work.

Appendix A
Proof of Theorem
1
(single global water level)

The conditional benchmark (
4
) is a separable convex
program over
\{D_{c}\}
subject to a single linear constraint. Form the
Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\{D_{c}\},\nu,\{\eta_{c}\})=\sum_{c=1}^{K}\pi_{c}R_{c}(D_{c})+\nu\Bigl(\sum_{c=1}^{K}\pi_{c}D_{c}-D\Bigr)-\sum_{c=1}^{K}\eta_{c}D_{c},

(11)

with
\nu\geq 0
and
\eta_{c}\geq 0
. KKT conditions: primal feasibility

D_{c}\geq 0
,
\sum_{c}\pi_{c}D_{c}\leq D
; dual feasibility; complementarity

\nu(\sum_{c}\pi_{c}D_{c}-D)=0
,
\eta_{c}D_{c}=0
; stationarity

\displaystyle\pi_{c}\frac{dR_{c}}{dD_{c}}+\nu\pi_{c}-\eta_{c}=0,\qquad c\in[K].

(12)

For the Gaussian RD function on a fixed component
c
, on any interval
where the active set
A_{c}(\mu_{c})=\{i:\lambda_{c,i}>\mu_{c}\}
is constant,

\displaystyle R_{c}(\mu_{c})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in A_{c}(\mu_{c})}\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}\!\frac{\lambda_{c,i}}{\mu_{c}},\quad D_{c}(\mu_{c})=\frac{1}{n}\Bigl(\sum_{i\not\in A_{c}(\mu_{c})}\lambda_{c,i}+|A_{c}(\mu_{c})|\mu_{c}\Bigr),

(13)

so

\displaystyle\frac{dR_{c}}{dD_{c}}=\frac{dR_{c}/d\mu_{c}}{dD_{c}/d\mu_{c}}=-\frac{1}{\mu_{c}\ln 2}.

(14)

For any active component (
D_{c}>0
, hence
\eta_{c}=0
), stationarity
collapses to
\pi_{c}\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{\mu_{c}\ln 2}+\nu\bigr)=0
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mu_{c}=\frac{1}{\nu\ln 2}\;\triangleq\;\mu,

(15)

which is independent of
c
. Hence a
single
water level
\mu

applies across all components and all eigenmodes. Substituting back,

\displaystyle R_{\rm cond}(D)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{c}\pi_{c}\sum_{i}\frac{1}{2}\Bigl[\log_{2}\!\frac{\lambda_{c,i}}{\mu}\Bigr]_{+},\quad D=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{c}\pi_{c}\sum_{i}\min\{\lambda_{c,i},\mu\}.

(16)

Monotonicity is immediate:
\min\{\lambda_{c,i},\mu\}
is nondecreasing
in
\mu
, so
D
is nondecreasing; for
\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}
the active set

A_{c}(\mu_{2})\subseteq A_{c}(\mu_{1})
and each summand decreases in
\mu
,
so
R_{\rm cond}
is nonincreasing.

The interpretation is that the multimodal allocation collapses onto
the same single-Gaussian reverse-waterfilling rule applied to the

pooled
eigenmodes weighted by
\pi_{c}
: a mode
(c,i)
is active
iff
\lambda_{c,i}>\mu
, regardless of
\pi_{c}
, while the prior enters
only through the expected-rate sum.

Appendix B
Proof of Theorem
2
(genie-aided converse)

Consider any admissible test channel
p(\hat{x}\mid x)
with

\mathbb{E}\|x-\hat{x}\|_{2}^{2}\leq nD
. Since the latent state is generated first
and the reproduction depends on
C
only through
x
,
C-x-\hat{x}
is
a Markov chain. Then

\displaystyle I(x;\hat{x})

\displaystyle=I(x,C;\hat{x})-I(C;\hat{x}\mid x)=I(C;\hat{x})+I(x;\hat{x}\mid C)

(17)

\displaystyle\geq I(x;\hat{x}\mid C)=\sum_{c=1}^{K}\pi_{c}I(x;\hat{x}\mid C{=}c).

(18)

For each branch let
D_{c}\triangleq\mathbb{E}[\|x-\hat{x}\|^{2}\mid C{=}c]/n
. By
the definition of the per-branch Gaussian RD function,

\tfrac{1}{n}I(x;\hat{x}\mid C{=}c)\geq R_{c}(D_{c})
, so

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}I(x;\hat{x})\;\geq\;\sum_{c=1}^{K}\pi_{c}R_{c}(D_{c}).

(19)

Since
\sum_{c}\pi_{c}D_{c}\leq D
, the right-hand side is at least

R_{\rm cond}(D)
. Taking the infimum over admissible test channels
gives
R^{\star}(D)\geq R_{\rm cond}(D)
.

Appendix C
Proof of Theorem
3
(label-aware achievability)

Fix
D\geq 0
and
\epsilon>0
. By the definition of
R_{\rm cond}(D)
,
choose
\{D_{c}\}
with
\sum_{c}\pi_{c}D_{c}\leq D
and

\sum_{c}\pi_{c}R_{c}(D_{c})\leq R_{\rm cond}(D)+\epsilon/2
. For each branch

c
, choose a conditional test channel
q_{c}(\hat{x}\mid x)
with

\mathbb{E}[\|x-\hat{x}\|^{2}\mid C{=}c]/n\leq D_{c}
and

\tfrac{1}{n}I(x;\hat{x}\mid C{=}c)\leq R_{c}(D_{c})+\epsilon/2
. Define the
augmented source
Y=(C,x)
with reproduction
\hat{Y}=(\hat{C},\hat{x})

via

\displaystyle q(\hat{c},\hat{x}\mid c,x)=\mathbf{1}\{\hat{c}=c\}\,q_{c}(\hat{x}\mid x).

(20)

The label is reproduced losslessly and the source by a branch-matched
channel; the total distortion is

\mathbb{E}\|x-\hat{x}\|^{2}/n\leq\sum_{c}\pi_{c}D_{c}\leq D
. The mutual information
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}I(Y;\hat{Y})

\displaystyle=\frac{H(C)}{n}+\frac{1}{n}I(x;\hat{x}\mid C)

(21)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{H(C)}{n}+\sum_{c}\pi_{c}R_{c}(D_{c})+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\leq\frac{H(C)}{n}+R_{\rm cond}(D)+\epsilon.

(22)

Apply Shannon’s lossy source-coding theorem to the augmented source

Y=(C,x)
: for every sufficiently large blocklength there exists a
code with rate at most the right-hand side and expected distortion at
most
D+\epsilon
. Re-normalizing per source dimension gives

R_{\rm PQ}(D)\leq R_{\rm cond}(D)+H(C)/n+\epsilon
; letting

\epsilon\downarrow 0
proves the claim.

Appendix D
EM update for the Gaussian-mixture dictionary

The dictionary
\{(\pi_{c},\mu_{c},R_{c})\}_{c=1}^{K}
is learned offline by
EM on a training set
\{x^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{N_{\rm tr}}
of source
vectors (real for Section
3
; complex for the CSI
application of Section
6
, with
\mathcal{N}
replaced by

\mathcal{CN}
in what follows). The E-step computes responsibilities

\displaystyle r_{j,c}=\Pr(C=c\mid x^{(j)})=\frac{\pi_{c}\,\mathcal{N}(x^{(j)};\mu_{c},R_{c})}{\sum_{d=1}^{K}\pi_{d}\,\mathcal{N}(x^{(j)};\mu_{d},R_{d})},

(23)

and the M-step updates the prior, mean, and covariance by weighted
moments:

\displaystyle\pi_{c}^{\rm new}=\frac{1}{N_{\rm tr}}\sum_{j}r_{j,c},\quad\mu_{c}^{\rm new}=\frac{\sum_{j}r_{j,c}\,x^{(j)}}{\sum_{j}r_{j,c}},\quad R_{c}^{\rm new}=\frac{\sum_{j}r_{j,c}\,(x^{(j)}-\mu_{c}^{\rm new})(x^{(j)}-\mu_{c}^{\rm new})^{\rm H}}{\sum_{j}r_{j,c}}.

(24)

The KLT
U_{c}
and eigenvalues
\Lambda_{c}
follow from the spectral
decomposition
R_{c}^{\rm new}=U_{c}\Lambda_{c}U_{c}^{\rm H}
. Per-mode
Lloyd–Max scalar quantizers can be precomputed once
\Lambda_{c}
is
known. For zero-mean sources (e.g., proper-complex CSI), the M-step
mean update collapses to
\mu_{c}^{\rm new}=0
.

Numerical stability of complex EM.

For high-dimensional CSI, the empirical second-moment
R_{c}^{\rm new}

can be ill-conditioned at small
K
effective sample sizes
(
\sum_{j}r_{j,c}\ll n
). We add a Tikhonov regularizer

R_{c}^{\rm new}\!\leftarrow\!R_{c}^{\rm new}+\lambda I
with

\lambda{=}10^{-4}\,\mathrm{tr}(R_{c}^{\rm new})/n
, guaranteeing strict
positive definiteness; degenerate components (priors below

10^{-4}
) are pruned and replaced by a split of the largest
component. Across
5
random restarts on COST2100 the largest
across-restart NMSE deviation is
<0.1
dB at every measured rate.

Appendix E
MAP label error: closed-form bound for Gaussian components

For Gaussian components, the pairwise Bhattacharyya overlap satisfies

\rho_{c,j}^{(n)}=\exp(-B_{c,j}^{(n)})
with

\displaystyle B_{c,j}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{8}(\mu_{c}-\mu_{j})^{\rm H}\!\Bigl(\frac{R_{c}+R_{j}}{2}\Bigr)^{\!-1}\!(\mu_{c}-\mu_{j})+\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\det\!\bigl(\tfrac{R_{c}+R_{j}}{2}\bigr)}{\sqrt{\det R_{c}\,\det R_{j}}}.

(25)

The same Bhattacharyya overlap drives the closed-form bounds and
approximations of mixture entropy in
Lee 2026
, where
analogous expressions are derived for Gaussian, factorized Laplacian,
uniform, and hybrid mixture families; the bound below is the
Gaussian specialization used in the present paper.
When
\mu_{c}\neq\mu_{j}
, both the Mahalanobis term and the determinant
term contribute; for proper-complex CSI with
\mu_{c}=0
only the
determinant term remains. The standard Bhattacharyya union bound

\displaystyle P_{e}^{\rm MAP}\leq\sum_{c<j}\sqrt{\pi_{c}\pi_{j}}\,e^{-B_{c,j}^{(n)}}\leq\frac{K-1}{2}\,e^{-\beta n}

(26)

holds whenever
B_{c,j}^{(n)}/n\geq\beta>0
for all
c\neq j
, by
Cauchy–Schwarz on the prior square roots. Hence MAP errors decay
exponentially in
n
.

F.2
Why a single global water level is information-theoretically optimal

Let
\{(\pi_{c},R_{c},U_{c},\Lambda_{c}=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{c,1},\ldots,\lambda_{c,n}))\}_{c=1}^{K}

be the true Gaussian-mixture parameters. Conditioning on the label

C
, the genie-aided per-dim rate–distortion characterization is a

joint
convex program in the per-mode distortions

\{d_{c,i}\geq 0\}
:

\displaystyle\min_{\{d_{c,i}\}}\;\frac{1}{n}\sum_{c=1}^{K}\pi_{c}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{c,i}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\frac{1}{n}\sum_{c=1}^{K}\pi_{c}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}\bigl[\log_{2}\!\tfrac{\lambda_{c,i}}{d_{c,i}}\bigr]_{+}\leq R.

(27)

(A) PrismQuant: one constraint, one Lagrange multiplier.

Forming the Lagrangian with multiplier
\nu\geq 0
,

\mathcal{L}(\{d_{c,i}\},\nu)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{c,i}\pi_{c}\!\left[d_{c,i}+\nu\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\log_{2}\!\tfrac{\lambda_{c,i}}{d_{c,i}}\bigr]_{+}\right]-\nu R,

the KKT stationarity for any active mode is

\pi_{c}\!\left[1-\frac{\nu}{2d_{c,i}\ln 2}\right]=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_{c,i}^{\star}\;=\;\frac{\nu}{2\ln 2}\;\triangleq\;\mu^{\star}\quad\text{for all}\;(c,i),

and the active set is
\mathcal{A}_{c}(\mu^{\star})=\{i:\lambda_{c,i}>\mu^{\star}\}
.
The prior
\pi_{c}

cancels
from both sides of the
stationarity condition: every extra bit on mode
(c,i)
is paid and
rewarded with the same probability
\pi_{c}
. The rate constraint is the

only
coupling between modes, hence a
single
multiplier

\nu
, hence a
single
global water level
\mu^{\star}

(Theorem
1
).

(B) WUTC:
K
extra constraints,
K
extra multipliers.

WUTC
[
13
]
uses a per-class bit budget
R_{q}
for every
class: the bits spent inside class
c
cannot be shared with any other
class. Equivalently, WUTC adds
K-1

extra
equality constraints
to (
27
),

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}\bigl[\log_{2}\!\tfrac{\lambda_{c,i}}{d_{c,i}}\bigr]_{+}\;=\;R_{q},\qquad c=1,\ldots,K,

so the Lagrangian acquires
K
independent multipliers

\{\nu_{c}\}_{c=1}^{K}
and the KKT condition becomes

d_{c,i}^{\rm WUTC}=\nu_{c}/(2\ln 2)\triangleq\mu_{c}^{\star}
, a

per-class
water level that ignores the eigenvalue spectrum of
every other class.

(C) Strict dominance via constraint-set inclusion.

The feasible set of WUTC is a proper subset of that of

PrismQuant
(more constraints
\Rightarrow
smaller feasible
set), so by elementary convex analysis,

D_{\rm PQ}(R)\;\leq\;D_{\rm WUTC}(R)\qquad\text{for every }R\geq 0,

with equality
iff
the per-class allocation produced by WUTC
happens to satisfy
\mu_{1}^{\star}=\cdots=\mu_{K}^{\star}=\mu^{\star}
,
which occurs only in the degenerate symmetric case

\Lambda_{1}=\cdots=\Lambda_{K}
(all classes have identical
eigenvalue spectrum). For
any
non-degenerate mixture the
inequality is strict. The gap is concentrated on classes whose
eigenvalues are an outlier of the joint distribution: bits stuck in a
narrow-spectrum class with
\lambda_{c,i}\leq\mu^{\star}
would have
been more useful in a wide-spectrum class, but WUTC cannot move them.

(D) Information-theoretic interpretation.

The same statement reads as a converse argument:

PrismQuant
attains the genie-aided RD function

R_{\rm cond}(D)
up to
H(C)/n
(Theorem
3
), so by
construction it is sandwiched between the unconditional RD function

R_{X}(D)
and
R_{X}(D)+H(C)/n
. WUTC, by contrast, is not sandwiched:
its operating point lies strictly above
R_{\rm cond}(D)+H(C)/n
at
every non-degenerate mixture. Single-cov TC has an even larger gap
since it does not even use the label, paying the additional

mixture-mismatch
cost

\mathrm{KL}\!\bigl(\sum_{c}\pi_{c}\mathcal{N}(\mu_{c},R_{c})\;\big\|\;\mathcal{N}(\hat{\mu},\hat{\Sigma})\bigr)
.

F.3
Adaptive Multiple Transform (AMT/MTS): the standardized counterpart

The library-of-transforms principle also appears in the HEVC and VVC
video-coding standards under the name
adaptive multiple
transform
(AMT), later renamed multiple transform selection (MTS).
The AMT concept was first proposed by
Han et al. 2010
,
Han et al. 2012
as a joint optimization of spatial
prediction and block transform;
Saxena and Fernandes 2013
extended
it into a mode-dependent DCT/DST scheme,
Zhao et al. 2016

consolidated the design as enhanced multiple transform, and

Bross et al. 2021
documents its current form in VVC. The library is a
small set of
fixed trigonometric transforms
(DCT-II together
with DST-VII and DCT-VIII variants), the branch is chosen by an
encoder-side rate–distortion search and signaled with a few
context-coded bits, and the bit allocation is governed by a
per-block quantization parameter from the standard.

AMT thus sits next to WUTC in the design space: both transmit a
branch index and apply a per-branch transform plus scalar
quantization, but AMT trades content-adaptivity for standardization,
fast trigonometric implementations, and hardware friendliness. It
uses neither a learned library nor a probabilistic source model, and
its rate–distortion behavior is documented only operationally, with
no analogue of the strict-dominance argument of §(C).
PrismQuant

differs from AMT in the same three respects that distinguish it from
WUTC:
(i)
the library is derived from a Gaussian-mixture source
model instead of being hand-designed;
(ii)
the branch is chosen
by a Bayes-optimal MAP classifier instead of by an RD search; and

(iii)
the bit allocation is summarized by a single global water
level with a matching closed-form converse, instead of by per-block
quantization parameters without an information-theoretic gap
analysis.

F.5
Take-away

The structural advantage of
PrismQuant
is
information-theoretic, not algorithmic. WUTC and
PrismQuant

share the same encoder–decoder skeleton, but WUTC’s per-class bit
budget is a
strictly tighter
constraint than
PrismQuant
’s
single total-rate constraint, and tighter constraints produce weakly
larger optimal distortion. AMT/MTS faces the same structural gap from
the opposite direction: its transform library and quantizer are fixed
and standardized rather than data-driven, so it cannot match either
the per-class adaptivity of WUTC or the cross-class joint allocation
of
PrismQuant
. Single-covariance TC inherits both losses. The
only way to recover the slack is to merge all per-class budgets into a
single global one over a data-driven transform library—which is
exactly what
PrismQuant
does, and what
Theorem
1
proves to be optimal.
