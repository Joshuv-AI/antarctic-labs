A Distributed SOS Program For Local Stability Analysis of Polynomial PDEs in the PIE Representation
Carl R. Richardson Declan S. Jagt Matthew M. Peet Antonis Papachristodoulou

Abstract
It has recently been shown that the evolution of a state, described by a Partial Differential Equation (PDE), can be more conveniently represented as the evolution of the state’s highest spatial derivative (the “fundamental state”), which lies in L 2 L_{2} and has no boundary conditions (BCs) or continuity constraints. For linear PDEs, this yields a Partial Integral Equation (PIE) parametrized by Partial Integral (PI) operators mapping the fundamental state to the PDE state. In this paper, we show that for polynomial PDEs, the dynamics of the fundamental state can instead be compactly expressed as a distributed polynomial in the fundamental state, parametrized by a new tensor algebra of PI operators acting on the tensor product of the fundamental state. We further define a SOS parametrization of the distributed polynomial and use this to construct a distributed SOS program, for testing local stability of polynomial PDEs.

SOS Polynomials on a Distributed State

Inspired by SOSTOOLS
[
19
]
, which builds on SOS polynomials for the analysis of polynomial ODEs, we introduce the notion of a distributed SOS polynomial. This class allows us to enforce positivity of distributed polynomials by definition and through equality constraints. In the subsequent section, we employ distributed SOS polynomials as LFs and formulate an SOS program to certify exponential stability of the equilibrium solution,
u\equiv 0
, on an
L_{2}
-ball of radius
r
.

Definition 9
.

For a given domain
\Omega:=[a,b]
, let the vector of degree
\bar{d}\in\mathbb{N}
monomials in
s,\theta\in\Omega
be denoted by
\mathcal{U}_{\bar{d}}(s,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{\mu(\bar{d})}[s,\theta]
, where
\mu(\bar{d}):=\frac{1}{2}(\bar{d}+1)(\bar{d}+2)
. Define

\displaystyle(\hat{\mathcal{U}}x)(s)

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}\int_{a}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{\bar{d}}(s,\theta)x(\theta)\,d\theta\\
\int_{s}^{b}\mathcal{U}_{\bar{d}}(s,\theta)x(\theta)\,d\theta\end{bmatrix}\in\Pi_{2}^{2\mu(\bar{d})}

We say that
q:L_{2}[\Omega]\to\mathbb{R}
is a distributed SOS polynomial in
x
, of degree
2d\in\mathbb{N}
, if there exists
\bar{d}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\bar{Q}\succeq 0
of appropriate dimension such that

\hskip-5.69054ptq(x):=\left\langle\mathcal{Z}_{d}(x),\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{Z}_{d}(x)\right\rangle_{L_{2}}

(15)

with
\mathcal{Q}
defined in (
16
). In which case, we write
q\in\Sigma_{2d}
.

It should be observed that
\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{\otimes i}:L_{2}[\Omega^{i}]\to L_{2}^{(2\mu(\bar{d}))^{i}}[\Omega]
is a T-PI operator; as a result, each matrix
\bar{Q}_{ij}
has dimension
{(2\mu(\bar{d}))^{i}\times(2\mu(\bar{d}))^{j}}
. Furthermore, the distributed SOS polynomial can be expressed as a sum of quadratic forms

q(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left\langle x^{\otimes i},\mathcal{Q}_{ij}x^{\otimes j}\right\rangle_{L_{2}}\quad\mathcal{Q}_{ij}:L_{2}[\Omega^{j}]\to L_{2}[\Omega^{i}]

By parametrizing distributed SOS polynomials in a quadratic manner, as in Def.
9

satisfies

for all

. However, since the quadratic representation of polynomials is not unique, it must be converted to the linear form for enforcing equality constraints. The remainder of this section shows how to perform this conversion, thereby also verifying that elements of
\Sigma_{2d}
are indeed distributed polynomials as per Def.
3
. The next definition shows how the individual quadratic terms can be evaluated by defining the “vectorization” of a distributed SOS polynomial.

Definition 10

For a given domain
\Omega:=[a,b]
and operator

\displaystyle\textnormal{vec}(\mathcal{Q})

It should be observed that

. Combining Def.
10

Lemma 6

For a given domain
\Omega:=[a,b]
and any

Proof.

By Def.
10
and expanding the inner product, for any

\displaystyle\left\langle z,\mathcal{Q}y\right\rangle_{L_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{L}z,\bar{Q}\circ\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{R}y\right\rangle_{L_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=1}^{m^{i}}\sum_{q=1}^{n^{j}}\bar{Q}_{pq}\int_{\Omega}((\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{L})_{p}z)(s)((\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{R})_{q}y)(s)ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=1}^{m^{i}}\sum_{q=1}^{n^{j}}\bar{Q}_{pq}\int_{\Omega}\left(\big((\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{L})_{p}\otimes(\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{R})_{q}\big)[z\otimes y]\right)(s)ds

For a given domain

VII
Distributed SOS Program For Local Stability Analysis of Polynomial PDEs

In the previous sections, we have shown how polynomial PIEs and semi-algebraic sets can be represented as distributed polynomials. Furthermore, we have also parameterized a class of nonnegative distributed polynomials, as distributed SOS polynomials. Using these results, we now show how local stability of the equilibrium
u\equiv 0
for a PDE as in (
2
) can be verified in the associated PIE representation (
13
), by solving a distributed SOS program. Specifically, to verify stability, we look for a distributed SOS polynomial LF,
V(v)
, which decays towards zero on a given semi-algebraic region. We test this decay by verifying negativity of the Lie derivative, defined as follows.

Definition 11
.

For a given polynomial PIE defined by
\{\mathcal{T},\mathcal{C}\}
and distributed polynomial,
V(v)
, define the Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T},\mathcal{C}}V(v)
such that
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T},\mathcal{C}}V(v(t))=\frac{d}{dt}V(v(t))
for any classical solution
v(t)
to the PIE.

Choosing
V\in\Sigma_{2d}
, the Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T},\mathcal{C}}V(v)
along the polynomial PIE in (
13
) will be a distributed polynomial as well. In order to evaluate this derivative, we assume
V
to be of the form
V(v)=\hat{V}(\mathcal{T}v)=\hat{V}(u)
, where
\hat{V}(u)
is then a LF for the PDE. For simplicity, the following Lemma provides an explicit expression for the Lie derivative of a degree 2 distributed SOS LF. Of course, this can also be derived for arbitrary degree
d\in\mathbb{N}
, but the proof is significantly more cumbersome.

Lemma 8
.

For a given polynomial PIE defined by
\{\mathcal{T},\mathcal{C}\}
, let
\mathcal{C}:=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{C}_{1}&\cdots&\mathcal{C}_{d^{\prime}}\end{bmatrix}
. For a self-adjoint 3-PI operator
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}^{*}
, let
V(v):=\left\langle\mathcal{T}v,\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}v\right\rangle_{L_{2}}

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T},\mathcal{C}}V(v)=2\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}}\textnormal{vec}\left(\mathcal{C}_{i}\otimes(\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{T})\right)[v^{\otimes i+1}]

Proof.

Let
v(t)
be an arbitrary classical solution to the PIE defined by
\{\mathcal{T},\mathcal{C}\}
. Then, by Lemma
4
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}V(v(t))

\displaystyle=2\left\langle\mathcal{T}v_{t}(t),\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}v(t)\right\rangle_{L_{2}}

\displaystyle=2\left\langle\mathcal{C}\mathcal{Z}_{d}(v(t)),\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}v(t)\right\rangle_{L_{2}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}}\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{C}_{i}v(t,\cdot)^{\otimes i})(s)(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}v(t,\cdot))(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathcal{C}_{i}\otimes(\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{T})\right)[v(t,\cdot)^{\otimes i+1}](s)\,ds

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}}\textnormal{vec}(\mathcal{C}_{i}\otimes(\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{T}))v(t)^{\otimes i+1}

∎

∎

Given that the Lie derivative of a distributed polynomial is again a distributed polynomial, negativity of this derivative can be tested by searching for a distributed SOS polynomial,
W\in\Sigma_{2d}

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T},\mathcal{C}}V=-W
. In this manner, existence of a LF certifying stability on the

\lVert u(t)\rVert_{L_{2}}\leq Me^{-\lambda t}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L_{2}}\hskip 2.84526pt\>\>\forall\>t\geq 0

\displaystyle V(v)\geq\epsilon^{2}\textnormal{vec}(\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathcal{T})v^{\otimes 2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T},\mathcal{C}}V(v)\leq-2\lambda V(v)-p_{2}(v)g(v)

IX
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed the distributed polynomial representation. This compact and flexible representation can model the PIE, associated with the polynomial PDE, when parametrized by Tensor-PI operators. When parametrized by Functional-PI operators, it can represent distributed semi-algebraic sets and distributed SOS LFs. To enforce positivity of the distributed SOS LF and negativity of its derivative, a distributed SOS polynomial was also defined. Leveraging these representations, a distributed SOS program was constructed for testing local stability over the
L_{2}
-ball of radius
r
, as demonstrated on the Fisher Equation. Whilst currently limited to scalar and 1D PDEs, the results of this paper may be extended to coupled PDEs in higher dimensions.
