Notational analysis of men’s curling at the 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics: comparing China with medal-winning teams
Authors: Pengfei Zhang, Wanning Huang, Qiang Li, Xiuyun Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study investigates the technical and tactical performance of the Chinese men’s curling team in comparison with medal-winning teams during the 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics, based on notational analysis of 34 matches. Guided by three research questions, the frequency and types of delivery and takeout techniques were first analysed, revealing that the Chinese team relied heavily on offensive shots such as draw and hit-and-rolls. In contrast, medal-winning teams adopted more balanced strategies, showing greater variability across both offensive and defensive shot types. Second, inter-team comparisons using Spearman correlation and chi-square tests showed that medalists demonstrated significantly higher tactical diversity and superior success rates in key shot types. Third, binary logistic regression was employed to evaluate the predictive value of specific tactical indicators. Promotion takeout success was found to be a significant positive predictor of match outcome (B = 1.150, p = 0.005), with a model classification accuracy of 85.7% and an AUC of 0.91. Conversely, sweeping success was negatively associated with winning (B = -0.200, p = 0.004), and its corresponding model achieved 76.2% accuracy and an AUC of 0.88. These findings suggest that sweeping may function as a compensatory tactic in high-pressure contexts. By combining descriptive, comparative, and predictive approaches, this study establishes a comprehensive framework for assessing elite curling performance and offers practical insights for coaching and strategic decision-making.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-026-01825-3.

Introduction

Curling is a precision-focused and strategy-driven winter sport that necessitates flawless synergy between tactical strategy and technical performance. Commonly known as “snooker on ice”, the sport requires precise stone delivery, effective sweeping techniques, immediate decision-making, situational awareness, as well as psychological fortitude [1, 2]. Following its incorporation into the Winter Olympics, curling has gained significant global attention and has emerged as a critical focus of performance analysis within sports science [2, 3].

Despite its relatively late entry into the international curling arena, China has made substantial investments in the sports over the past decade, including the creation of national training centres, the hiring of international of coaches, and the implementation of technology-assisted performance analysis. These endeavours have yielded favorable outcomes: the fourth-place finish at the 2014 Winter Olympics in Sochi and a second-place standing for the Chinese men’s team in the Curling World Cup Grand Final. Nevertheless, the men’s team still struggles to match the consistency and tactical versatility of traditional powerhouses such as Canada, Sweden, and Great Britain. This performance gap emphasises the need for systematic data-informed assessment to support the continued development of men’s curling in China.

In current curling research, scholars [4, 5] have adopted a variety of methodologies to investigate athletes’ shot performance and related physiological characteristics. For example, the BODPOD is used to assess body composition, including body fat percentage and lean mass, while the SPORTKAT 300 is employed to evaluate static and dynamic balance ability. A detailed discussion of these approaches is presented in the literature review (Sect. 2.2). However, systematic comparisons of technical and tactical execution between emerging teams like China and top-performing international teams remain limited. In particular, there are limited number of studies that have used notational analysis to assess the impact of specific technique indicators and tactical strategies contribute to match results, particularly in the Olympic-level competition background.

To address this research gap, the present study applies notational analysis (NA) to the men’s curling event at the 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics. It focuses on identifying the technical and tactical performance patterns of the Chinese team in comparison to medal-winning teams, with particular attention to success rates and the predictive effects of key indicators on match outcomes. Based on video-based data and statistical modelling, the following two objectives are addressed: to quantify differences in the frequency, type, and effectiveness of technical and tactical indicators between the Chinese team and top-performing teams (i.e., Sweden, Great Britain and Canada); to identify which specific indicators are most predictive of match results and to generate both theoretical insights and practical implications for performance improvement in Chinese men’s curling.

Literature background

This section reviews recent literature on the use of notational analysis in sports performance research, with particular focus on its application to team sports. In addition to research that explicitly employs notational analysis, it also considers research that adopts alternative methodological approaches to examine performance indicators in curling. The goal is to identify a gap in which systematic notational analysis remains underutilised in curling studies. To ensure that the review reflects the most recent scholarly discussions, only literature published within the last five years was considered.

Notational analysis in team sports

Notational Analysis (NA), originally proposed by Hughes [6], is a systematic method used for the structural analysis of sports performance. According to Hughes, the advantages of this method lie in its ability to objectively record, qualify, and evaluate technical and tactical indicators during actual gameplay. This enables researchers and practitioners to identify performance patterns, assess efficiency, and inform coaching strategies. Due to these practical advantages, many independent studies [7–10] have adopted notational analysis across a variety of sporting contexts, ranging from individual sports (e.g., table tennis, and street skateboarding) to team sports (e.g., ice hockey and football).

For example [9], perform a notational observation analysis of 192 matches in Swedish top ice hockey league, recording 4,886 goal-scoring opportunities (GSOs). Using logistic regression, they determine that tactical and situational variables (i.e., shot location, game time and player positioning) significantly affected the livelihood of scoring. These findings highlight the critical role of notational analysis in identifying performance patterns and outcomes in elite team sports. Expanding upon this methodology [10], integrate OpenPose, an open-source AI tool for real-time posture identification, with a football-specific notational analysis (OSPAF) to examine penalty kick scenarios in elite European leagues from 2017 to 2020. Their findings illustrate that the goalkeeper’s body gesture was highly correlated with his strategy choice, and the model accuracy increased to 97% after incorporating predictive variables. In contrast, the penalty taker’s body angle was not significantly associated with shooting strategy.

Alternative methods of studying curling

Although notational analysis has not yet been applied to curling in existing literature, several scholars examine the sport using alternative research methods. Recent curling research increasingly focuses on individual athletic attributes, using experimental methodologies to evaluate the effect of physiological and cognitive elements on technical and tactical performance. For instance [4], employ BODPOD and SPORTKAT 300 to assess the body composition and balance capabilities in elite players. Their findings indicate that balance significantly affects shot accuracy, highlighting the importance of physical stability in curling performance. In a related study [11], adopt experimental methods to evaluate the effects of life dynamics training on the rapid decision-making abilities of female curling players. Using Shapiro-Wilk test and independent-sample T-tests, they find that the training significantly improves both decision-making efficiency and throwing performance, reflecting the value of physiological interventions in optimising technical and tactical indicators. Building on this physiological perspective [5], adopt cognitive neuroscience methods by integrating E-Prime 3.0 (a software platform for designing psychology and neuroscience experiments) and Quick-20 (a 23-channel EEG device for recoding brain activity) to explore the relationship between time perception and throwing accuracy. Their results show that duration judgment ability is positively correlated with performance outcomes, suggesting that temporal cognition is also a critical factor in technical and tactical execution.

Research gaps and questions

A review of the above literature shows that notational analysis has been widely applied across various sports, including both individual and team disciplines. However, its application in winter sports remains limited, and to date, no study has systematically employed this method in curling. Existing studies primarily focus on physiological or cognitive factors at the individual level, lacking a systematic analysis of the structural relationship between key technical and tactical indicators and match results. This gap is particularly evident in studies on the Chinese men’s curling team, where performance modelling based on actual game data and comparison with medal-winning teams remains absent.

To address these research gaps, the present study applies notational analysis to systematically encode match data from the men’s curling event at the 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics. It aims to compare key tactical indicators between the Chinese team and medal-winning teams and to assess the predictive validity of these indicators in relation to game outcomes. On this basis, the following research questions (RQs) are systematically examined:

RQ1

What are the most frequently used delivery and takeout techniques by the Chinese men’s curling team?

RQ2

What are the significant differences in key technical and tactical indicators between the Chinese team and medal-winning teams?

RQ3

How do key tactical indicators predict match results, and are there differences in these predictive patterns between the Chinese team and medal-winning teams?

Section 3 presents the data sources and methodological procedures adopted in this study, followed by a detailed presentation of the empirical findings in Sect. 4. These findings are then critically discussed in relation to existing literature and practical implications in the discussion section (Sect. 5).

Data and methods

Sample

Compared with other international competitions, such as the World Curling Championships and the Pan Continental Curling Championships, the Winter Olympics represent the highest level of competition in curling. The tournament features top national teams and reflects highly structured, stable, and strategically refined technical-tactical execution. Therefore, Olympic data provide a valuable basis for examining elite-level performance patterns. In addition, the participation of the Chinese men’s team in a home Olympics offers a rare opportunity to assess its performance against the world’s strongest teams under high-pressure competitive conditions.

In the present study, 34 matches from the men’s curling event at the 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics were included, covering all the matches involving the Chinese team and the three medal-winning teams, namely Canada, Great Britain, and Sweden, from the round-robin stage to the final. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) official matches from the men’s curling event at the 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics; (2) matches involving the Chinese team or one of the three medal-winning teams; and (3) complete publicly available match footage and official shot records. Practice matches, non-Olympic matches, or incomplete recordings were excluded. Technical and tactical data were obtained from the official website of the World Curling Federation (WCF) [12] and its authorised broadcasting platform (YouTube). These sources provide reliable, complete, and systematically recorded match data that are well suited to coding and quantitative notational analysis.

Coding system and procedure

Coding system

To perform the notation analysis, a customised coding system was developed based on the official competition rules of the WCF and expert consultations with certified international curling referees. The system categorised each throw according to three core dimensions: throwing type, rotation direction, and shot outcome.

As shown in Table 1, throwing types were grouped into three broad categories: deliveries (e.g., draw, guard, freeze), takeouts (e.g., hit and stay, double takeout, promotion takeout), and strategies (e.g., key shot, steal, hammer). All delivery and takeout actions, except for strategic indicators, were further classified by rotation direction as either clockwise or counterclockwise.

Classification of technical and tactical indicators in men’s curling

To improve methodological transparency, several core indicators are briefly defined here. A draw refers to a shot intended to stop in the house or another designated scoring area. A guard refers to a stone placed in front of the house to protect a scoring stone. A freeze refers to a controlled shot designed to stop adjacent to another stone. A promotion takeout refers to a takeout in which one stone is promoted into another to remove it. A hit and roll refers to a takeout followed by controlled movement of the shooter stone into another tactically advantageous position. Further details on the definitions and classifications of all indicators are provided in the Supplementary Materials (see Table A1).

Shot outcomes were categorised as Success, Neutral, or Failure. A successful shot was defined as one in which the stone landed in the intended position and fulfilled the planned tactical purpose. A neutral shot did not reach the exact intended position but did not disrupt the subsequent tactical sequence and still retained its strategic value. A failed shot negatively affected subsequent technical or tactical execution. This coding system enabled fine-grained analysis of each throwing event and provided the basis for subsequent descriptive, comparative, and predictive analyses.

Coding procedure

All match videos were obtained from the official WCF YouTube channel, which provides full public access to complete match footage. Using Dartfish, a professional sports performance analysis software, all throwing actions were annotated frame by frame according to the coding framework described above. For each shot, three core variables were recorded: throwing type (see Table 1), rotation direction (clockwise and counterclockwise) and shot outcome (Success, Neutral, and Failure).

For coding and descriptive analyses, the shot was treated as the basic unit of observation. For comparative analyses, shot-level data were aggregated by team, position, and rotation direction as appropriate. For predictive modelling, shot-level indicators were further aggregated to the match level before match outcome was analysed.

The full official sample was coded by the principal coder using a standardised coding protocol and recording sheet. To assess the objectivity and reproducibility of the coding process, an external nationally certified curling coach was invited to independently code the same men’s curling match using the established coding framework. Before formal coding, the external coder received two days of targeted training covering the operational definitions of the observation indicators, classification criteria for technical actions, rotation-direction judgement rules, shot-outcome recording rules, and coding-sheet completion requirements. After training, the principal coder and the external coder independently coded the same match under non-communicating conditions, with no discussion, negotiation, or cross-revision during the coding process.

Inter-rater reliability was assessed in IBM SPSS Statistics 25.0 using Cohen’s kappa, and the corresponding inferential statistics are reported in Table 2. The resulting Cohen’s κ coefficient was 0.81, with a standard error of 0.071, reaching statistical significance (p < 0.001, N = 42), indicating a high level of agreement between coders. These results suggest that the coding framework and annotation procedure demonstrated satisfactory objectivity and reproducibility.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted in IBM SPSS Statistics 25.0 with statistical significance set at p < 0.05. The analytical procedure followed a progressive structure of “description → comparison → prediction” in order to address the three research questions.

To address RQ1, descriptive statistics (mean and standard deviation: Mean ± SD) were used to summarise the frequency and distribution of delivery and takeout techniques. These analyses provided an overview of the technical-tactical profiles of the Chinese men’s team and the medal-winning teams.

To address RQ2, Spearman correlation analysis and chi-square tests were performed. Spearman correlation was selected because several indicators were non-normally distributed and the analysis focused on monotonic associations among key tactical variables. Chi-square tests were used to examine differences in the distributions of categorical tactical variables, including deliveries, takeouts, and strategic indicators. Before interpreting the chi-square results, expected cell frequencies were examined to ensure the appropriateness of the test. Overall, these two methods captured both the associations among key indicators and the distributions of categorical tactical variables.

To address RQ3, binary logistic regression was used to evaluate the predictive value of technical and tactical indicators for match outcome. Match outcome (0 = loss, 1 = win) served as the dependent variable, and aggregated performance indicators, including delivery success rate, takeout success rate, and strategy success rate, were entered as independent variables. The model was estimated using maximum likelihood estimation. Prior to interpretation, multicollinearity among predictors was examined, and model performance was evaluated using classification accuracy and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves with corresponding area under the curve (AUC) values. Given the relatively limited sample size, the predictive results were interpreted cautiously in view of the potential risk of overfitting. Findings

Addressing RQ1: Frequency of delivery and takeout techniques

To examine the central tendency and variability of the key technical and tactical indicators, descriptive statistics were calculated in IBM SPSS Statistics 25.0. The number of throws for each technique (e.g., draw, promotion takeout) was analysed, and mean ± standard deviation values were reported for each variable. The full descriptive statistics are provided in the Supplementary Materials (see Table A2), while Fig. 1 presents a visual summary of the dominant delivery and takeout techniques across teams and base directions. As shown in Fig. 1, technique preferences varied across teams, positions, and base directions, with draw and hit and roll emerging as the dominant tactical choices in most contexts.

For the lead position in the clockwise direction, Canada (draw: 3.18 ± 3.64) and Great Britain (draw: 3.36 ± 2.71) most frequently employed draw shots. Front shots ranked second for both teams (Canada: 2.55 ± 1.81; Great Britain: 2.27 ± 2.10), suggesting a tactical preference for front-zone placement to establish early control. China showed relatively balanced usage between draw (1.78 ± 1.73) and guard (1.89 ± 1.73), reflecting a more diversified approach. In contrast, Sweden adopted a less aggressive pattern, with lower frequencies for draw (1.09 ± 1.14) and guard (1.27 ± 1.95). In the counterclockwise direction, Sweden (draw: 6.73 ± 5.03) and China (draw: 5.11 ± 3.62) recorded the highest draw frequencies, far exceeding those of other teams. This usage of front shots was also elevated (Sweden: 3.64 ± 2.36; China: 4.33 ± 3.18), indicating a strategic focus on building layered defensive setups. Canada (draw: 2.36 ± 1.95) and Great Britain (draw: 3.64 ± 2.86) ranked lower in draw frequency under this direction.

At the second position, China ranked first in the use of hit and roll, with frequencies of 2.11 ± 2.37 in the clockwise direction and 3.67 ± 2.69 in the counterclockwise direction, indicating a dominant attacking tendency. Canada also showed an increase in hit and roll usage, rising from 1.91 ± 2.00 (clockwise) to 2.91 ± 2.89 (counterclockwise). This increase suggests that Canada adopted a more aggressive or precision-oriented strategy in the counterclockwise direction, using the second player to execute more hit and roll shots as a means of gaining positional advantage. Sweden showed a modest increase from 1.82 ± 2.25 to 2.36 ± 1.79, with a more focused tactical application in this position. Great Britain demonstrated a relatively balanced use of hit and roll (1.91 ± 2.02 clockwise; 2.00 ± 2.46 counterclockwise), and also frequently employed hit and away (2.09 ± 2.47), suggesting an emphasis on clearing the house.

In the Vice-Skip (aka. The third) position, all four teams generally adopted hit and roll as the primary tactical option. In the clockwise direction, China recorded the highest usage frequency (2.44 ± 2.51), indicating a preference for proactive clearing strategies. Canada (1.82 ± 2.19), Great Britain (1.73 ± 2.24), and Sweden (1.55 ± 1.81) employed it less frequently, suggesting a more restrained tactical deployment. In the counterclockwise direction, hit and roll frequency increased across all teams. China led again with the highest value (3.67 ± 3.21), reflecting a reinforced offensive strategy. Canada also showed a marked increase (2.91 ± 2.70), followed by Sweden (2.36 ± 2.31) and Great Britain (2.00 ± 2.09). These upward shifts imply an overall tendency to adopt more aggressive or precision-targeted shots from this base direction. Overall, the increased reliance on hit and roll in the counterclockwise direction highlights teams’ efforts to strengthen house control and optimise scoring potential during this phase of play.

In the Skip position, China adopted a high-frequency dual strategy, with both draw and hit and roll occurring 2.78 times on average in the clockwise direction (draw: 2.78 ± 2.38; hit and roll: 2.78 ± 3.13). In the counterclockwise direction, draw frequency remained at 2.78 ± 2.58, while hit and roll declined to 1.78 ± 1.83; however, both techniques remained central to the Skip’s tactical repertoire. Canada’s use of hit and roll in the counterclockwise direction increased significantly to 3.00 ± 2.20, showing a more aggressive closing strategy. Great Britain maintained a relatively balanced pattern, with hit and roll frequency reaching 2.36 ± 2.10 in the counterclockwise direction. Sweden’s draw frequency in the clockwise direction was the highest among the teams (3.73 ± 2.57), and while its counterclockwise draw frequency declined to 1.55 ± 1.81, the team still retained a dual approach by combining it with hit and roll (2.18 ± 1.79).

Addressing RQ2: Comparative differences in technical and tactical indicators

To build on the frequency-based on observations presented in Sect. 4.1, this section addresses RQ2 by statistically comparing the Chinese men’s curling team with medal-winning teams in terms of key technical and tactical indicators. While Sect. 4.1 identified each team’s most frequently used techniques, this section further examines whether these observed patterns reflect statistically significant differences. Spearman’s rank correlation analysis is applied to explore structural similarities or divergences in indicator usage, while Chi-square tests are used to assess differences in categorical tactical preferences.

Specifically, Spearman’s rank correlation analysis was first performed (“Analyze > Correlate > Bivariate”) to examine relationships among key technical and tactical variables (e.g., draw, guard, hit and roll), with the non-parametric correlation coefficient (ρ) and associated p-values calculated to identify potential synergies. Subsequently, Chi-square tests (“Analyze > Descriptive Statistics > Crosstabs”) were used to evaluate whether the use of specific tactics varied significantly with categorical variables such as throwing category, direction, and outcome. These inferential methods complement the descriptive statistics in Sect. 4.1 by providing deeper insights into between-team differences and inter-variable relationships. As shown in Table 3, Spearman correlation analysis revealed a statistically significant positive correlation between the Chinese team and Great Britain in key shot failure at the Lead position in the counterclockwise direction (r = 0.756, p = 0.018). This indicates that both teams encountered similar challenges or lower execution success rates when performing key shots in this specific direction.

In the Second position, China and Sweden showed a significant negative correlation in clockwise hit-and-roll success (r = -0.736, p = 0.024), reflecting diverging strategies in similar game situations. However, both teams experienced high failure rates in clockwise key shot, as indicated by a strong positive correlation (r = 0.992, p < 0.01), suggesting shared execution challenges. Moreover, the two teams demonstrated a positive correlation in counterclockwise neutral hit-and-roll throws (r = 0.722, p = 0.028), pointing to a degree of convergence in control-based tactics.

In the Vice-Skip position, the Chinese team showed a positive correlation with Great Britain in clockwise hit-and-roll success (r = 0.667, p = 0.050), indicating similar execution tendencies in this tactic. Conversely, a significant negative correlation with Sweden in key-shot success (r = -0.805, p = 0.009) suggests a contrasting tactical approaches or performance outcomes in executing scoring-critical shots in the same direction.

In the Skip position, the Chinese team showed a significant positive correlation with Great Britain in clockwise hit-and-roll success (r = 0.707, p = 0.033), suggesting similar tactical execution in creating scoring opportunities. However, the two teams exhibited a negative correlation in key-shot neutral outcomes (r = -0.756, p = 0.018), indicating that China favoured clear results (success or failure), whereas Great Britain tended to retain more neutral outcomes – possibly reflecting differences in risk-taking strategies. In comparison with Canada, China showed strong negative correlations in both counterclockwise double takeout neutral (r = -0.791, p = 0.011) and counterclockwise key-shot neutral (r = -0.919, p = 0.000), the latter being the strongest in this analysis. This suggests fundamentally divergent approaches between the two teams in high-stakes end-game scenarios. The Spearman correlation analysis revealed both the similarities and differences in the relationships among key performance indicators (e.g., draw, hit and roll), including directional variables such as clockwise and counterclockwise shot outcomes. However, this analysis did not capture whether the distribution of tactical choices significantly varied across teams. Thus, a chi-square test was performed to further assess inter-team differences in the distribution of technical and tactical indicators. The results are presented in Table 4.

In the Skip position, inter-team performance differences remain prominent. China and Great Britain exhibited had the largest disparity in the success rate of counterclockwise hit and stay shots (χ²(6, 9) = 14.000, p = 0.030). China also differed significantly from Canada in the distribution of hit-and-roll neutral outcomes (χ²(2, 9) = 9.000, p = 0.011) and double takeout neutral performance (χ²(1, 9) = 5.625, p = 0.018). These results indicate that teams adopt systematically different strategies in shot selection and outcome tolerance during the final stage of play.

Addressing RQ3: Predictive value of tactical indicators for match outcomes

The previous sections systematically explored the use of delivery and takeout techniques (RQ1) and examined inter-team differences in key technical and tactical indicators (RQ2). These analyses revealed the most frequently adopted strategies and notable variations in tactical execution across teams. Building on these findings, RQ3 examines whether such tactical indicators can statistically predict match results. This section therefore transitions from descriptive and comparative methods to a predictive modelling framework. Specifically, logistic regression is applied to assess how different techniques (e.g., draw as well as hit and roll) influence the probability of winning or losing a match. This approach establishes a direct link between tactical execution and competitive success, providing a comprehensive understanding of how individual shot decisions translate into overall performance.

Binary logistic regression was conducted in IBM SPSS Statistics 25.0 (Analysis > Regression > Binary Logistic Regression), with match outcome as the dependent variable (win = 1, lose = 0), and key tactical indicators (e.g., draw, hit and roll, sweeping) as independent variables. The regression coefficient (β) indicates the direction and magnitude of each variable’s impact on match outcome: a positive β suggests a higher likelihood of winning, while a negative β implies the opposite. Statistical significance was assessed using p-values, with p < 0.05 considered significant.

To evaluate model performance, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated. Higher AUC values indicate stronger discriminatory ability. The empirical findings, including significant predictors and model performance statistics, are presented in Tables 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10; Figs. 2 and 3. Among the tactical indicators, promotion takeout success emerged as a key variable and is discussed first below (See Tables 5, 6 and 7; Fig. 2).

Discussion

This study systematically addressed three research questions by examining the technical and tactical performance of the Chinese men’s curling team at the 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics in comparison with the medal-winning teams. First, descriptive statistics were used to identify the most frequently adopted delivery and takeout techniques across different shot positions and directions (RQ1), thereby highlighting each team’s overall strategic preferences. Next, Spearman correlation analysis and chi-square tests were conducted to examine inter-team similarities and differences in key tactical indicators (RQ2), revealing statistically significant variation in shot execution and performance patterns. Building on these findings, binary logistic regression was applied to evaluate the predictive value of selected tactical variables (RQ3). The results showed that promotion takeout success was positively associated with winning outcomes, whereas sweeping success was unexpectedly negatively associated with match victory. This multi-layered analytical approach provides a comprehensive empirical basis for understanding how specific tactical decisions influence competitive results and highlights systematic disparities between the Chinese team and the medal-winning teams.

Rather than simply extending previous notational analysis studies, this study offers a novel perspective by integrating performance indicators into a predictive framework. The use of logistic regression enabled the contribution of each variable to match outcomes to be quantified, thereby providing a more rigorous and data-driven approach to evaluating tactical effectiveness. While previous curling studies have tended to focus on descriptive summaries or post-game observations, the present research identifies which technical and tactical executions are most strongly associated with winning. One particularly noteworthy finding was the negative association between sweeping success and match outcome. Although sweeping is typically regarded as a supportive skill that enhances shot precision, a higher frequency of successful sweeping may reflect compensatory responses in less favourable situations. For example, sweeping may be used more intensively when delivery quality is suboptimal or when teams are under increased pressure. This result highlights the importance of contextual interpretation and suggests that sweeping performance should not be evaluated in isolation.

The findings also offer several theoretical and practical implications. First, the strong association between promotion takeout success and winning underscores the critical role of advanced takeout techniques in elite-level curling, particularly in decisive stages of competition. Second, the relatively greater tactical diversity shown by the medal-winning teams suggests that successful performance depends not only on execution quality but also on the flexibility to adapt technical choices across positions and shot directions. In contrast, the Chinese team’s comparatively narrower tactical profile may indicate a need for more diversified tactical preparation. From a coaching perspective, these results highlight the importance of strengthening advanced takeout training, improving tactical adaptability, and prioritising shot planning and execution under pressure rather than relying on reactive adjustments during play.

However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, although the shot was treated as the basic observational unit for coding purposes, curling is inherently sequential in nature. Shot selection and outcome are shaped by the immediately preceding shot, score differential, and the broader tactical context within each end. This operational assumption may therefore simplify the interdependence embedded in shot-by-shot decision-making. Future studies could apply Markov chain models or multilevel modelling to better capture the sequential structure of curling performance. Second, an important contextual factor not explicitly modelled in the present study is hammer status. In curling, possession of the hammer strongly influences whether teams adopt offensive or defensive tactical choices, and its absence from the present analysis may partly explain some of the disparities observed between the Chinese team and the medal-winning teams. Third, the sample was limited to matches from a single Olympic tournament, which restricts the generalisability of the findings. Although the ROC and AUC results indicated good discriminatory performance, the possibility of overfitting cannot be fully excluded given the relatively limited sample size. Accordingly, these predictive findings should be interpreted with caution until they can be validated using larger samples from multiple competitions.

Despite these limitations, the present study provides a useful foundation for future curling research by demonstrating how technical and tactical indicators can be quantitatively linked to match outcomes. Future research could expand the dataset to include multiple international tournaments and different competition formats, such as women’s and mixed doubles curling. Methodologically, incorporating sequential models, multilevel approaches, or machine learning techniques may help capture the temporal and interactive nature of in-game decision-making more effectively. In addition, integrating contextual variables such as hammer status, score situation, and end-specific tactical context may improve the explanatory power of predictive models. Combining quantitative indicators with qualitative evidence, such as player interviews or coaching commentary, may also provide deeper insight into the strategic logic of elite curling performance.

Conclusion

This study applied notational analysis to evaluate the technical and tactical performance of the Chinese men’s curling team at the 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics. By comparing the Chinese team with the medal-winning teams, the study addressed three research questions concerning usage patterns, inter-team differences, and the predictive value of key performance indicators. The findings showed that the Chinese team adopted a relatively narrower tactical profile, whereas the medal-winning teams displayed greater tactical diversity and flexibility. Promotion takeout success positively predicted match outcomes, while sweeping success showed a negative association, suggesting that it may function more often as a reactive adjustment in less favourable situations. By introducing predictive modelling into curling performance analysis, this study contributes a novel framework for understanding how specific technical and tactical variables are associated with competitive success. Future research should build on these findings by using larger datasets, incorporating sequential and multilevel modelling approaches, and including key contextual factors such as hammer status to better capture the complexities of elite curling decision-making.
