Introduction
At Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory in the National Ignition Facility Optics
Group, we use the systems engineering approach for project management and as a
design tool. Systems engineering is used in a graded approach to design and project
management that is based on risk, informing how much rigor to apply. The tools and
techniques from systems engineering offer a framework to organize projects with
everyone speaking the same language to provide consistent and repeatable project
success that satisfies the stakeholders’ needs and meets the mission. The classes have
provided a framework with tools for communicating system design, requirements,
verification and validation, and an operational context.

The Georgetown systems engineering courses have bolstered my understanding and
provided another viewpoint on systems engineering, providing a broader understanding
of the principles. I gained the most from doing the projects and applying the course
material then receiving feedback from the professor.

What did you take away from the course?

These courses have reinforced the need to succinctly communicate technical
information for system success. This ensures that the entire team can evaluate the
design and function of the system. The importance of having a full set of requirements
that completely define the system that can be verified and validated through inspection,
analysis, testing and/or demonstration. These requirements also serve as a contract
with the stakeholders defining success of the project.

The last course in the series has provided tools to tie verification and validation directly
to the requirements. The lessons we learned map well to our documentation and
approach to verification and validation, further reinforcing the ideas and concepts.

What was interesting?

The most interesting tools in systems engineering are the visual ways of showing
different parts of systems engineering and organizing thoughts. There are a variety of
different ways to visually break down a system and communicate the inputs/outputs,
display concepts, show failure modes and risk and they each have value in presenting
ideas and concepts. These also provide a way to reduce the risk that aspects of the
system are overlooked or missed.

What do you look forward to applying from the course?

I look forward to connecting the principles of systems engineering to concurrent
engineering and design. The two concepts/methods have similar content, and it would
be interesting to compare/contrast the two. A brief look at the AI generated definitions
and comparisons shows some conflicting conclusions whether they are similar or not
and there are a few articles discussing the topic. I am always looking for better ways to
communicate technical information and to organize a project. Systems engineering
provides that framework while also being adaptable and general enough to apply to
different types of systems and projects.

What did you take away that you can use?

Taking stakeholder expectations and turning them into requirements is going to be an
important skill as we develop new equipment and systems. We deal with stakeholders
from a variety of different backgrounds and experiences, and taking their input then
distilling them into requirements is something that can be difficult, these classes have
provided helpful discussion in that process.

Conclusions

Systems engineering has become an important tool in project management and system
design. It provides a holistic picture of the entire lifecycle of a system ensuring success
through risk mitigation, clear requirements and expectations, requirement verification
and validation and while also considering operations and maintenance. These tools
have made me a better engineer and improved my technical communication with
management and our engineering team. Working through the course projects, given an
opportunity to apply the skills with real time feedback has made the material more
digestible and made a lasting impact. Being able to apply the course material directly to
my work at LLNL has also made drawing a connection between theory and application
easier.
