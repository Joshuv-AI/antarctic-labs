Temporal Berry Phase and the emergence of Bose-Glass-Analog Phase in a Clean U(1) Superfluid
Ryuichi Shindou, Pengwei Zhao, Xiaonuo Fang

Abstract
A U(1) nonlinear sigma model (NLSM) with a one-dimensional temporal Berry phase term describes the critical theory of phase-fluctuation-driven superfluid (SF) transitions. We clarify that the temporal Berry phase leads to space-time anisotropic interference in vortex proliferation, resulting in a quasi-disordered phase characterized by short-range spatial order but persistent temporal phase coherence. This phase shares the essential SF phase correlation properties of the Bose Glass phase known from disordered boson systems, suggesting a unified topological origin for the emergence of the glassy phase in phase-fluctuation-driven superfluid transitions.

. To determine the scaling dimension
y_{\chi}
of the Berry phase parameter
\chi

\chi
around the saddle-fixed point
(t_{*},x_{*})
on the non-magnetic
[\chi=0]

\displaystyle\frac{d\chi}{d\ln b}=\bigg\{(D-1)-16\pi^{2}e^{2}\chi A^{2}\Big(\frac{a_{0}}{2}\Big)^{D-2}\frac{1}{2^{D}}\frac{\pi^{\frac{D}{2}}}{\Gamma\big(\frac{D+2}{2}\big)}\bigg\}\!\ \chi,

(III.16)

where we used

\displaystyle\int_{S_{D-1}}d^{D-1}{\bm{e}}_{0}\cos\theta^{2}=\frac{1}{D-1}\int_{S_{D-1}}d^{D-1}{\bm{e}}_{0}\sin\theta^{2}=\frac{\pi^{\frac{D}{2}}}{\Gamma\big(\frac{D+2}{2}\big)}.

(III.17)

With Eq. (
III.6
), this becomes,

\displaystyle\frac{d\chi}{d\ln b}=

\displaystyle\Big\{(D-1)-\frac{4\sqrt{\pi}}{D-1}\frac{\big(\frac{\pi}{2}\big)^{D}}{\Gamma\big(\frac{D+2}{2}\big)\Gamma\big(\frac{D+1}{2}\big)}\!\ e^{2}\!\ \exp[t]\Big\}\chi.

(III.18)

Eqs. (

of the Berry phase parameter

is always 1, being independent from the spatial dimension

\displaystyle\frac{d\chi}{d\ln b}=\Big\{(D-1)-(D-2)\Big\}\chi=\chi\,\ \,\ @\,\ (e,t,\chi)=(e_{*},t_{*},0).

(III.19)

y_{\chi}=1
at the fixed point
(e,t,\chi)=(e_{*},t_{*},0)
originates from the absence of the renormalization factor to the electric charge
e
. To see this point, let us first include
\chi
into
\partial_{\mu}u_{\mu\tau}
, and rewrite it into a form of an external magnetic field
\chi_{h}
in the Maxwell action. Namely, with
\chi_{h}\equiv\chi/e

\partial_{\mu}u_{\nu\mu}+\chi_{h}\delta_{\nu\tau}\rightarrow\partial_{\mu}u_{\nu\mu}

\displaystyle\frac{ie}{2}\int d^{D}{\bm{x}}\Big\{u_{\mu\nu}({\bm{x}})v_{\mu\nu}({\bm{x}})+2\chi_{h}b_{\tau}({\bm{x}})\Big\}

\displaystyle=ie\int d^{D}{\bm{x}}\Big\{\big(\partial_{\mu}u_{\nu\mu}({\bm{x}})\big)b_{\nu}({\bm{x}})+\chi_{h}b_{\tau}({\bm{x}})\Big\}

\displaystyle\rightarrow ie\int d^{D}{\bm{x}}\big(\partial_{\mu}u_{\nu\mu}({\bm{x}})\big)b_{\nu}({\bm{x}}),

(III.20)

while the transformation gives rise to the magnetic field term in the Maxwell action,

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{\tau}}{2}\int d^{D}{\bm{x}}\big(\partial_{\lambda}u_{\nu\lambda}\big)\big(\partial_{\mu}u_{\nu\mu}\big)\rightarrow\frac{\gamma_{\tau}}{2}\int d^{D}{\bm{x}}\Big\{\big(\partial_{\lambda}u_{\nu\lambda}\big)\big(\partial_{\mu}u_{\nu\mu}\big)-2\chi_{h}\partial_{\lambda}u_{\tau\lambda}\Big\}.

(III.21)

Thereby, the partition function can be generally rewritten into another form as well:

\displaystyle Z_{\rm v}=

\displaystyle\int{\cal D}{\bm{u}}\,\ \exp\bigg[-\frac{1}{2}\int_{|\partial u|<\Lambda|u|}d^{D}{\bm{x}}\!\ \Big\{(\partial_{\mu}u_{\mu\nu})(\partial_{\lambda}u_{\lambda\nu})+2\chi_{h}(\partial_{\lambda}u_{\lambda\tau})\Big\}\bigg]

\displaystyle\Bigg\{1+\sum^{\infty}_{N=1}\frac{1}{N!}\prod^{N}_{j=1}\bigg(\int{\cal D}^{D}{\bm{X}}_{j}\int_{|{\bm{Y}}_{j}|>a_{0}}{\cal D}^{D}{\bm{Y}}_{j}\int{\cal D}{\sf g}_{j}({\bm{s}})\,\ \exp\big[E_{j}\big]\bigg)

\displaystyle\exp\Big[-\frac{i}{2}e\int_{|\partial u|<\Lambda|u|}d^{D}{\bm{x}}\,\ u_{\mu\nu}({\bm{x}})v_{\mu\nu}({\bm{x}})\Big]\Bigg\}.

(III.22)

The partition function undergoes an ordered-disordered phase transition. When

[the space-time isotropic case], the universal criticality of the phase transition is characterized by the saddle-fixed point at
(e,t)=(e_{*},t_{*})
with

\displaystyle\chi_{h}\rightarrow\chi^{\prime}_{h}=b^{D-1}Z^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{u}b^{-\frac{D-2}{2}}\chi_{h},

(III.23)

with

\displaystyle u_{\cdots}(\bm{x})\rightarrow u^{\prime}_{\cdots}({\bm{x}}^{\prime})=Z^{\frac{1}{2}}_{u}b^{\frac{D-2}{2}}u_{\cdots}({\bm{x}}).

(III.24)

Notice that

at the saddle fixed point. This is because the integration of the short-distance degrees of freedom at
\chi_{h}=0
yields only the renormalization to the electromagnetic constitutive constant and vortex fugacity, while keeping intact the electric charge

\displaystyle Z_{\rm v}=

\displaystyle\int{\cal D}{\bm{u}}\,\ \exp\bigg[-\frac{1}{2}\int_{|\partial u|<\Lambda|u|}d^{D}{\bm{x}}\!\ (\partial_{\mu}u_{\mu\nu})(\partial_{\lambda}u_{\lambda\nu})\bigg]\,\ \Bigg\{1+\sum^{\infty}_{N=1}\frac{1}{N!}

\displaystyle\prod^{N}_{j=1}\bigg(\int{\cal D}^{D}{\bm{X}}_{j}\int_{|{\bm{Y}}_{j}|>a_{0}}{\cal D}^{D}{\bm{Y}}_{j}\int{\cal D}{\sf g}_{j}({\bm{s}})\,\ \exp\big[t\int_{\partial\Gamma_{j}}d^{D-2}{\bm{s}}\sqrt{\det\hat{\bf g}({\bm{s}})}\big]\bigg)

\displaystyle\exp\Big[-\frac{i}{2}e\int_{|\partial u|<\Lambda|u|}d^{D}{\bm{x}}\,\ u_{\mu\nu}({\bm{x}})v_{\mu\nu}({\bm{x}})\Big]\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int{\cal D}{\bm{u}}\,\ \exp\bigg[-\frac{1}{2}\int_{|\partial u|<\Lambda b^{-1}|u|}d^{D}{\bm{x}}\!\ (1+\delta\gamma)(\partial_{\mu}u_{\mu\nu})(\partial_{\lambda}u_{\lambda\nu})\bigg]\,\ \Bigg\{1+\sum^{\infty}_{N=1}\frac{1}{N!}

\displaystyle\prod^{N}_{j=1}\bigg(\int{\cal D}^{D}{\bm{X}}_{j}\int_{|{\bm{Y}}_{j}|>a_{0}b}{\cal D}^{D}{\bm{Y}}_{j}\int{\cal D}{\sf g}_{j}({\bm{s}})\,\ \exp\big[(t+\delta t)\int_{\partial\Gamma_{j}}d^{D-2}{\bm{s}}\sqrt{\det\hat{\bf g}({\bm{s}})}\big]\bigg)

\displaystyle\exp\Big[-\frac{i}{2}e\int_{|\partial u|<\Lambda b^{-1}|u|}d^{D}{\bm{x}}\,\ u_{\mu\nu}({\bm{x}})v_{\mu\nu}({\bm{x}})\Big]\Bigg\}.

(III.25)

As a result, the scaling property of the electric charge

\displaystyle e\rightarrow e^{\prime}=\frac{b^{\frac{D-2}{2}}}{\sqrt{Z_{u}}}e,

(III.26)

with

\displaystyle{Z_{u}}_{|e^{2}=e^{2}_{*},t=t_{*}}=b^{D-2}.

(III.27)

Then, from Eq. (
III.23

\chi_{h}
around the saddle fixed point must always be one,

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}_{h}=b\chi_{h}.

(III.28)

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}\equiv\chi^{\prime}_{h}e^{\prime}=b\chi_{h}e=b\chi.

\displaystyle\exp[-E_{\rm Coul}(C,\gamma_{\tau})]\equiv

\displaystyle\int D{\sf a}({\bm{x}})\exp\bigg[-{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\frac{1}{2}}\int d^{3}{\bm{x}}\!\ (\nabla\times{\sf a})^{T}\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\gamma_{\tau}&0\\
0&{\bf 1}_{2\times 2}\\
\end{array}\right)\cdot(\nabla\times{\sf a})-2\pi ie\oint_{C}d{\bm{l}}\cdot{\sf a}({\bm{l}})\bigg],

with
{\bm{x}}\equiv(\tau,{\bm{r}})

\nabla\equiv(\nabla_{\tau},\nabla_{\bm{r}})

{\bm{l}}\equiv(l_{\tau},{\bm{l}}_{\bm{r}})

{\sf a}\equiv({\sf a}_{\tau},{\sf a}_{\bm{r}})

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}=\tau,\,\ {\bm{r}}^{\prime}={\bm{r}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma_{\tau}}},\,\ {\sf a}^{\prime}_{\tau}={\sf a}_{\tau},\,\ {\sf a}^{\prime}_{\bm{r}}=\sqrt{\gamma_{\tau}}{\sf a}_{\bm{r}},

\displaystyle\nabla^{\prime}_{\tau}=\nabla_{\tau},\,\ \nabla^{\prime}_{\bm{r}}=\sqrt{\gamma_{\tau}}\nabla_{\bm{r}},\,\ l^{\prime}_{\tau}=l_{\tau},\,\ {\bm{l}}^{\prime}_{\bm{r}}={\bm{l}}_{\bm{r}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma_{\tau}}},

\gamma_{\tau}\neq 1

\gamma_{\tau}=1

\displaystyle\int D{\sf a}({\bm{x}})\exp\bigg[-{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\frac{1}{2}}\int d^{3}{\bm{x}}(\nabla\times{\sf a})^{T}\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\gamma_{\tau}&0\\

\end{array}\right)\cdot(\nabla\times{\sf a})-2\pi ie\oint_{C}d{\bm{l}}\cdot{\sf a}({\bm{l}})\bigg].

\displaystyle={\rm const}.\int D{\sf a}^{\prime}({\bm{x}}^{\prime})\exp\bigg[-{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\frac{1}{2}}\int d^{3}{\bm{x}}^{\prime}(\nabla^{\prime}\times{\sf a}^{\prime})^{T}\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\

The scale transformation deforms

C^{\prime}

\displaystyle E_{\rm Coul}(C,\gamma_{\tau})=E_{\rm Coul}(C^{\prime},\gamma_{\tau}=1)+{\rm const}.

(III.122)

|\log a_{0}|

\displaystyle E_{\rm Coul}(C^{\prime},\gamma_{\tau}=1)=\frac{\pi e^{2}}{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}2}}L(C^{\prime})|\log a_{0}|+\cdots.

(III.123)

{\bm{r}}

with a finite projection in
{\bm{r}}

without any projection in
{\bm{r}}

L(C^{\prime}_{1})\gg L(C^{\prime}_{2})

{\bm{r}}

\tau

IV.1

In the isotropic and general

\gamma_{\tau}=1

) and calculate
F_{\tau}({\bm{x}})=F_{\bm{r}}({\bm{x}})
with a soft ultraviolet (UV) cutoff for the momentum integral,

\displaystyle F({\bm{x}})\equiv

\displaystyle F_{\tau}({\bm{x}})=F_{\bm{r}}({\bm{x}})=\Lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}\bigg[\int\frac{d^{D}{\bm{k}}}{(2\pi)^{D}}\frac{e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{x}}}}{k^{2}}e^{-\Lambda^{-1}k}\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi^{\frac{D}{2}}}{\Lambda}\bigg[\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{dk}{(2\pi)^{D}}k^{D-2}e^{-\Lambda^{-1}k}\!\ _{0}\tilde{F}_{1}\big(\frac{D}{2},-\frac{k^{2}x^{2}}{4}\big)\bigg],

(IV.1)

where
{}_{0}\tilde{F}_{1}(a,b)

[

]
, we further calculate the
\Delta{\bm{s}}
-integral in Eqs. (
II.78
,
II.79
) for

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\int d^{D-2}\Delta{\bm{s}}\,\ F\Big(\sum^{D-2}_{a=1}\Delta s_{a}\frac{\partial{\bm{R}}_{j}({\bm{s}})}{\partial s_{a}}\Big)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det\hat{\bf g}_{j}}}\int d^{D-2}{\bm{\xi}}\,\ F({\bm{\xi}}).

(IV.2)

In the first line, the
(D-2)
-dimensional vector
\Delta{\bm{s}}
is transformed into a
(D-2)
-dimensional vector
{\bm{\xi}}
associated with the first fundamental form
\hat{\bf g}_{j}({\bm{s}})
,

\displaystyle\hat{\bf g}_{j,ab}({\bm{s}})\equiv\sum_{\mu=\tau,1,\cdots,D-1}\frac{\partial{\bm{R}}_{j,\mu}}{\partial s_{a}}\frac{\partial{\bm{R}}_{j,\mu}}{\partial s_{b}}

(IV.3)

(a,b=1,2,\cdots,D-2)
. Firstly, this
(D-2)\times(D-2)

\eta_{a}

(a=1,\cdots,D-2)
. Then,
\Delta{\bm{s}}
is linearly transformed into a
(D-2)

(IV.5)

IV.2
Derivation of Eqs. (
III.68
,
III.69
)

As we discussed in Section
II.2
, the short-range part of the Coulomb interaction gives rise to a fugacity renormalization. In the general
D
case, the fugacity renormalization is given by a rather complicated function of the temporal and spatial components of the metric tensor,
\hat{\bf g}_{\tau}
and
\hat{\bf g}_{\bm{r}}
[see Eqs. (
II.80
)]. In
D=3
, however, the components reduce to scalar quantities, and the function reduces to a simpler form. To see this, let us start from the gradient expansion of the short-range part of the 3D Coulomb interaction within the same vortex loop
\partial\Gamma_{j}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\delta E_{j}

\displaystyle=\ln b\frac{e^{2}}{2}\sum_{\sigma=\tau,1,2}\int d^{3}{\bm{R}}_{1}\int d^{3}{\bm{R}}_{2}F_{\sigma}(|{\bm{R}}_{1}-{\bm{R}}_{2}|)J_{\sigma}({\bm{R}}_{1})J_{\sigma}({\bm{R}}_{2}),

\displaystyle=2\pi^{2}e^{2}\ln b\sum_{\sigma=\tau,1,2}\sum_{j}\int_{\partial\Gamma_{j}}ds_{1}\int_{\partial\Gamma_{j}}ds_{2}\,\ F_{\sigma}(|{\bm{R}}_{j}(s_{1})-{\bm{R}}_{j}(s_{2})|)\frac{\partial R_{j,\sigma}(s_{1})}{ds_{1}}\frac{\partial R_{j,\sigma}(s_{2})}{ds_{2}},

\displaystyle=2\pi^{2}e^{2}\ln b\sum_{\sigma=\tau,1,2}\sum_{j}\int_{\partial\Gamma_{j}}ds\Big(\frac{\partial R_{j,\sigma}(s)}{ds}\Big)^{2}\int d\Delta s\,\ F_{\sigma}\Big(\frac{\partial R_{j,\sigma}(s)}{ds}\Delta s\Big)+\cdots.

(IV.7)

Here we used,

\displaystyle J_{\sigma}({\bm{R}})

\displaystyle=2\pi\sum_{j}\int_{\partial\Gamma_{j}}ds\frac{\partial R_{j,\sigma}(s)}{ds}\delta^{3}({\bm{R}}-{\bm{R}}_{j}(s)),

\displaystyle\ln b\!\ F_{\sigma}({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\int_{b^{-1}\Lambda<|{\bm{k}}|<\Lambda}\frac{d^{3}{\bm{k}}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{e^{i{\bm{q}}{\bm{r}}-i\omega\tau}}{k^{4}}\begin{cases}{\bm{q}}^{2}+(2-\gamma^{-1}_{\tau})\omega^{2}&\ \ \ {\rm for}\,\ \sigma=\tau,\\
\gamma^{-1}_{\tau}{\bm{q}}^{2}+\omega^{2}&\ \ \ {\rm for}\,\ \sigma=1,2.\\
\end{cases}

(IV.8)

The short-range part of the interaction
F_{\sigma}({\bm{x}})

(\sigma=\tau,1,2)
may be calculated with a soft UV cutoff with the cylindrical symmetry,

\displaystyle F_{\tau}({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=-\Lambda^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda^{-1}}\int\frac{d^{3}{\bm{k}}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{i{\bm{q}}{\bm{r}}-i\omega\tau}e^{-\Lambda^{-1}|{\bm{q}}|}\,\ \frac{|{\bm{q}}|^{2}+(2-\gamma^{-1}_{\tau})\omega^{2}}{k^{4}},

(IV.9)

\displaystyle F_{\bm{r}}({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle\equiv F_{1}({\bm{x}})=F_{2}({\bm{x}})=-\Lambda^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda^{-1}}\int\frac{d^{3}{\bm{k}}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{i{\bm{q}}{\bm{r}}-i\omega\tau}e^{-\Lambda^{-1}|{\bm{q}}|}\,\ \frac{\gamma^{-1}_{\tau}|{\bm{q}}|^{2}+\omega^{2}}{k^{4}},

(IV.10)

with
{\bm{x}}=(\tau,{\bm{r}})
and
{\bm{k}}=(\omega,{\bm{q}})
. As
F_{\sigma}\big(\frac{\partial{\bm{R}}}{\partial s}\Delta s\big)
comes with
(\partial_{s}{\bm{R}}_{\sigma})^{2}
in Eq. (
IV.7
), it is natural to estimate the
\Delta s
-integral of
F_{\tau}\big(\frac{\partial{\bm{R}}}{\partial s}\Delta s\big)
using a geometry of
\frac{\partial{\bm{R}}}{\partial s}\Delta s=\Delta s(1,0,0)
, and the
\Delta s
-integral of
F_{\bm{r}}\big(\frac{\partial{\bm{R}}}{\partial s}\Delta s\big)
by the other geometry:
\frac{\partial{\bm{R}}}{\partial s}\Delta s=\Delta s(0,\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
. With this simplification, the one-dimensional
\Delta s
-integrals in Eqs. (
II.78
,
II.79
) can be readily calculated, giving reasonable estimates of
f_{\tau}
and
f_{\bm{r}}
in the
D=3
case,

\displaystyle f_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\int d\Delta s\,\ F_{\tau}\big(\Delta s(1,0,0)\big)=-\Lambda^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda^{-1}}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bm{q}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{|{\bm{q}}|^{2}}e^{-\Lambda^{-1}|{\bm{q}}|}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{-1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{\infty}_{0}d|{\bm{q}}|e^{-\Lambda^{-1}|{\bm{q}}|}=\frac{1}{2\pi},

(IV.11)

\displaystyle f_{\bm{r}}

\displaystyle=\int d\Delta s\,\ F_{\bm{r}}\big(\Delta s(0,1,0)\big)

\displaystyle=-\Lambda^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda^{-1}}\int d\Delta s\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{qdq}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int^{2\pi}_{0}d\theta\,\ \frac{1}{(\omega^{2}+q^{2})^{2}}e^{-\Lambda^{-1}q}e^{-iq|\Delta s|\cos\theta}\,\ \big(\gamma^{-1}_{\tau}q^{2}+\omega^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=-\Lambda^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda^{-1}}\int d\Delta s\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{qdq}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int^{2\pi}_{0}d\theta\,\ \frac{\gamma^{-1}_{\tau}+1}{4q}e^{-\Lambda^{-1}q}e^{-iq|\Delta s|\cos\theta}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{-1}\int d\Delta s\int^{2\pi}_{0}d\theta\,\ \frac{1}{(\Lambda^{-1}+i\Delta s\cos\theta)^{2}}\frac{\gamma^{-1}_{\tau}+1}{4}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{-1}\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}d\Delta s\frac{\Lambda^{-1}}{(\Lambda^{-2}+|\Delta s|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{\gamma^{-1}_{\tau}+1}{4}\frac{1}{2\pi}=\frac{\gamma^{-1}_{\tau}+1}{4\pi}.

(IV.12)

Eqs. (
IV.11
,
IV.12
) give Eqs. (
III.68
,
III.69
).
