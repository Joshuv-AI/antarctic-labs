Project Overview
Are we alone? REDWOODS is an exoplanet imaging adaptive optics (AO) technology
demonstration instrument at Lick Observatory aimed at developing the technology to help
answer this question. The developed technology aims to enable similar technology for future
larger observatories to image planets around other stars that ultimately could harbor life on
their surface. However, current state-of-the-art exoplanet imaging adaptive optics technologies
are insufficient to achieve this goal if placed on such larger telescopes today, and if so would be
~100x less sensitive than needed. The REDWOODS instrument aims to bridge this 100x gap with
new second stage AO wavefront sensing and control technologies, including (1) a Fast
Atmospheric Self-coherent camera (SCC) Technique (FAST) sensor, equipped with a broadband
Wynne corrector mode, (2) a fully reflective high spectral bandwidth Pyramid wavefront sensor
mode, and (3) multi-wavefront sensor control software. The project successfully validated all
three of these technologies on-sky.

Mission Impact
LLNL’s Space Science Institute (SSI) emphasizes the development of AO and exoplanet imaging
technologies, such as the ones in this proposal, in its Strategic Plan for development of and
integration with future large ground- and space-based observatories. LLNL’s Space Science and
Security Program (SSSP) is also in contributing to such future large flagship space science
missions, which this project helps enable. AO technologies similar to those in this proposal are
also used for national security in areas of interest to SSSP for space domain awareness
applications and the Directed Energy Technologies programs.
