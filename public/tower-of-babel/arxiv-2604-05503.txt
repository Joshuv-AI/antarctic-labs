Exact solution of three-point functions in critical loop models
Morris Ang, Gefei Cai, Jesper Lykke Jacobsen, Rongvoram Nivesvivat, Paul Roux, Xin Sun, Baojun Wu

Abstract
We propose an exact formula for three-point functions on the sphere in critical loop models with primary fields V ( r , s ) V_{(r,s)} characterized by 2 ​ r 2r legs and a parameter s s that describes diagonal fields for r = 0 r=0 and the momentum of legs for r > 0 r>0 . We demonstrate its validity in three ways: a transfer-matrix study of the lattice model, the conformal bootstrap method for 4-point functions, and a probabilistic method based on conformal loop ensemble and Liouville quantum gravity. This work provides a crucial missing piece for solving critical loop models and reveals a deep unity between three fundamental approaches to 2D statistical physics: transfer matrix, conformal field theory, and probability theory.



Introduction.

Two-dimensional (2D) critical loop models occupy a unique position in theoretical physics. They describe the universal properties of many statistical systems, from the
Q
-state Potts model to the O(
n
) model
Nienhuis 1982
;
Di Francesco et al. 1987
, while simultaneously providing a geometric framework for conformal field theory (CFT)
Belavin et al. 1984
;
Francesco et al. 2012
and serving as a central object of study in probability theory through Schramm-Loewner Evolution (SLE) and Conformal Loop Ensembles (CLE)
Miller et al. 2021
;
Miller et al. 2022
. Understanding these models in full has been a long-standing goal, with significant progress made on critical exponents
Nienhuis 1982
;
Cardy 1992
;
Nolin et al. 2025
and partition functions
Di Francesco et al. 1987
;
Cardy 2006
;
Dubail et al. 2009
.

A complete solution requires knowledge of the correlation functions. On the sphere, one-point functions vanish, and two-point functions are determined by critical exponents, up to field normalizations.
The next outstanding object
for critical loop models is then the sphere three-point function of primary fields of the form

V_{(r,s)}
. The parameter
r\geq 0
is half the number of open loop segments (legs) inserted by
V_{(r,s)}
, while
s
gives their momentum or, for
r=0
, modifies the fugacity of loops that wind around the insertion point
Grans-Samuelsson et al. 2023
;
Grans-Samuelsson et al. 2022
.

In this Letter, we report a general exact formula (
3
) for the three-point functions of these primary fields in critical loop models, which was proposed in
Jacobsen et al. 2025
and inspired by
Ang et al. 2024a
;
Nivesvivat et al. 2024
.
Previously known results
Dotsenko and Fateev 1984
;
Delfino and Viti 2010
;
Ikhlef et al. 2016
concern diagonal operators, and have the form of the imaginary DOZZ formula
Schomerus 2003
;
Zamolodchikov 2005
;
Kostov and Petkova 2007
;
Kupiainen et al. 2020
that is crucial to Liouville theory and related CFTs.
Expressed using the Barnes double gamma function, our formula (
3
) goes significantly beyond the imaginary DOZZ formula and captures much more geometric observables. In particular, we obtain the three-point function for the two-leg operator, which encompasses the probability that three points are on the same loop. In the rest of the Letter, we present our result and describe the three complementary methods coming from transfer matrix, CFT, and probability theory.

Critical loop models.

The CFT of critical loop models is parametrized by a coupling constant
\beta^{2}

such that
{\rm Re}\,\beta^{2}>0
.
We take generic values of
n
and
Q=n^{2}
; our main interest is the interval
\beta^{2}\in[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
corresponding to
0\leq n\leq 2

The torus partition function can be computed
Di Francesco et al. 1987
from the Coulomb gas (CG) method
Nienhuis 1982
. It determines the central charge

c=13-6\beta^{2}-6\beta^{-2}\,,\qquad n=-2\cos(\pi\beta^{2})\,,

(1)

of the underlying O(
n
) or
Q

Q=n^{2}

It also determines the

with left and right conformal dimensions

\Delta_{(r,s)}=P_{(r,s)}^{2}-P_{(1,1)}^{2}\,,\quad P_{(r,s)}=\frac{1}{2}(r\beta-s\beta^{-1})\,.

(2)

The parameters take values
r\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
, subject to the constraint that the conformal spin
rs\in\mathbb{Z}

Nivesvivat et al. 2024

and

modifies the weight of loops that surround it, replacing

Three-point functions.

Global conformal invariance determines the coordinate dependence of two- or three-point correlation functions
Belavin et al. 1984
.
In particular, using the short-hands
i=(r_{i},s_{i})
and
z_{ij}=z_{i}-z_{j}
, the three-point function
\left\langle V_{1}(z_{1})V_{2}(z_{2})V_{3}(z_{3})\right\rangle
on the sphere reads

\frac{C_{123}}{|z_{12}|^{2(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-\Delta_{3})}|z_{13}|^{2(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{3}-\Delta_{2})}|z_{23}|^{2(\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}-\Delta_{1})}}\,,

where it remains to determine the structure constant

C_{123}=\hskip-11.38092pt\prod_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3}=\pm}\hskip-11.38092pt\Gamma_{\beta}^{-1}\big(\tfrac{\beta+\beta^{-1}}{2}+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\big|\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}r_{i}\big|+\tfrac{\beta^{-1}}{2}\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}s_{i}\big)\,.

(3)

The Barnes double gamma function

Ang et al. 2025

Eq. (

Nivesvivat et al. 2024

It is defined for all parameters satisfying the constraints

r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3}\in\mathbb{N}\,,\qquad r_{i}s_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

(4)

which guarantee that
\left\langle V_{1}(z_{1})V_{2}(z_{2})V_{3}(z_{3})\right\rangle
is non-zero and single-valued
Nivesvivat et al. 2024

The three-point functions of degenerate fields and diagonal fields

were previously known, from the CG
Dotsenko and Fateev 1984

Delfino and Viti 2010

Ikhlef et al. 2016

;
Kupiainen et al. 2020

Kostov and Petkova 2007

A key insight from the lattice construction is that this structure constant has a direct combinatorial interpretation

Grans-Samuelsson et al. 2022

(r_{i},s_{i})

(3,3,2)

(5)

The last two examples show that self-contractions are allowed only if there is an operator inside the enclosure. The first of (

On the sphere with punctures at

The probabilistic method.

The third approach to critical loop models comes from probability theory, where the continuum limit is constructed directly as a Conformal Loop Ensemble (CLE)
Camia and Newman 2006
;
Sheffield 2009
. This construction depends on a parameter
\kappa\in(8/3,8)

related to parameters in (
1
) by
\kappa=4\beta^{-2}
.
CLE
κ
with parameter
\kappa
is a conformally invariant random collection of loops, each of which behaves as an SLE
κ
curve
Schramm 2000
.

In this framework, correlation functions correspond to probabilities of geometric events involving loops. For example, the three-point function

\langle V_{(1,0)}V_{(1,0)}V_{(1,0)}\rangle
is proportional to the probability that a single CLE loop passes through three points. Although this event has probability zero, the three-point function can be understood as a scaling limit of the probability that a loop passes through three small disks centered at the three points. The normalized ratio taken as in (
6
) is independent of the scaling factor. By
Smirnov 2001
;
Camia and Newman 2006
;
Garban et al. 2013
, the Bernoulli site percolation on the triangular lattice converges to CLE
6
in a rather strong sense. Hence,
for the dense O
(1)
model on the hexagonal lattice, the normalized ratio (
6
) for
\langle V_{(1,0)}V_{(1,0)}V_{(1,0)}\rangle
defined by CLE
6
agrees with the continuum limit of the lattice counterpart.

The key to the probabilistic approach is the coupling of SLE/CLE and random surfaces
Duplantier and Sheffield 2011
in Liouville quantum gravity (LQG) pioneered by Sheffield
Sheffield 2016
. First, LQG surfaces coupled with CLE inherit a rich integrable structure from random planar maps decorated with the O
(n)
loop model,
as demonstrated in
Duplantier et al. 2021
;
Miller et al. 2021
;
Miller et al. 2022
. Second, Liouville CFT, the field theory that describes LQG, is itself integrable.
In particular, the three-point function on the sphere is given by the DOZZ formula
Kupiainen et al. 2020
;
Dorn and Otto 1994
;
Zamolodchikov and Zamolodchikov 1996
.
The overarching strategy of combining insights from both sources of solvability was initiated in
Ang et al. 2024b
and has since been successfully applied to several problems
Nolin et al. 2023
;
Ang et al. 2024c
;
Ang et al. 2022
.

We demonstrate this approach using the derivation of
\langle V_{(1,0)}V_{(1,0)}V_{(1,0)}\rangle
in the dilute O(
n
) model.
Let
b\in(0,1)
,
Q=b+b^{-1}
, and
c_{\mathrm{L}}=1+6Q^{2}
be the coupling parameter, the background charge, and the central charge of the Liouville theory.
In the aforementioned coupling, the central charge for the Liouville CFT governing the LQG surfaces and the central charge
c_{\beta}
for the loop model as in (
1
) are related by
c_{\rm L}+c_{\beta}=26
. Here 26 is the dimension of the critical bosonic string
Polyakov 1981
, and the loop model acts as a conformal matter field
David 1988
;
Distler and Kawai 1989
. When
c_{\mathrm{L}}=26
, the LQG surface describes the continuum limit of
uniformly sampled random planar maps, which models pure 2D gravity. In general, LQG surfaces with
c_{\mathrm{L}}=26-c_{\beta}
describe the continuum limit of random planar maps decorated with the O
(n)
loop model.
Let
\mathrm{QD}_{0,3}
be the LQG disk with three marked boundary points,
conformally parameterized by the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.
Then the law of the conformal factor
\phi
is given by the Liouville CFT on
\mathbb{D}
with
an insertion at the three marked points. The insertions are of the form
e^{b\phi}
thanks to
the Knizhnik-Polyakov-Zamolodchikov (KPZ) relation
Knizhnik et al. 1988
;
Duplantier and Sheffield 2011
.

Now we glue two independent copies of the CLE-decorated
\mathrm{QD}_{0,3}
along their boundary. The resulting object is an LQG sphere decorated with CLE that has a loop passing through three marked points. See Figure
2
for an illustration.

Figure 2:
Illustration for the derivation of
\langle V_{(1,0)}V_{(1,0)}V_{(1,0)}\rangle
.

The conformal factor
\phi
describing the LQG sphere is given by Liouville theory on the sphere with three insertions of the form
e^{\alpha\phi}
. By the KPZ relation,
\alpha
satisfies

2d_{\mathrm{L}}(\alpha)+\Delta_{(1,0)}=2\,,

where

d_{\mathrm{L}}(\alpha)=\frac{\alpha}{2}(Q-\frac{\alpha}{2})
is the scaling dimension for the
e^{\alpha\phi}
-insertion, and

2-\Delta_{(1,0)}=1+\frac{\kappa}{8}
is the fractal dimension of an SLE
κ
curve
Beffara 2008
. This yields
\alpha=b
.
Writing
\langle e^{\alpha_{1}\phi}e^{\alpha_{2}\phi}e^{\alpha_{3}\phi}\rangle
as the three-point function of Liouville theory on the sphere,
the partition function of the right-hand side of Figure
2
is then
\langle V_{(1,0)}V_{(1,0)}V_{(1,0)}\rangle\times\langle e^{b\phi}e^{b\phi}e^{b\phi}\rangle
. On the other hand, this quantity can be computed from
\mathrm{QD}_{0,3}
.
Using the relation between the imaginary DOZZ formula at
c_{\beta}
and the DOZZ formula at
c_{\rm L}

Schomerus 2003
;
Zamolodchikov 2005
;
Kostov and Petkova 2007
, we arrive at an exact expression for
\langle V_{(1,0)}V_{(1,0)}V_{(1,0)}\rangle
, which agrees with (
3
) and (
6
). See
(
Ang et al. 2024a
, Theorem 1.6)
. This approach was also used in
Ang et al. 2024a
to derive the three-point function of diagonal operators
V_{(0,s)}
, which recovers the results from
Delfino and Viti 2010
;
Ikhlef et al. 2016
. Furthermore, the approach was used in
Cai et al. 2025
to derive an exact formula for the three-point function for the spin cluster in the
Q
-Potts model, which matches the numerical result from
Delfino et al. 2013
.

Conclusions.

Using three different methods, we gave evidence for the expression (
3
) for the three-point structure constants. This subsumes previous results for degenerate
Dotsenko and Fateev 1984
and diagonal
Delfino and Viti 2010
;
Ikhlef et al. 2016
operators, and shows that correlation functions of legged operators
V_{(r,s)}
with
r>0
in critical loop models require a framework beyond the CG and imaginary Liouville approaches. Several directions remain open.
First, although we could extract three-point structure constants from
solutions to (
10
), the four-point constants
d^{(x)}_{V}
are not in general the product of two three-point constants. It was noticed in

Nivesvivat et al. 2024
that there is in most cases an extra factor, a rational
function of the loop weights
n
and
w_{i}
. Solving the four-point functions requires bringing this factor under analytical control. Second, in rational CFT the
C_{123}
play the dual role of structure constants and operator-product expansion (OPE) coefficients. Here we lack a full understanding of fusion of
V_{(r,s)}
operators. Third, tackling other geometries than the sphere requires further control of conformal boundary conditions and bulk-boundary fusion rules. We plan to come back to those issues in future work.
