q q -Numerical Ranges and Spectral Sets
Ryan O’Loughlin, Jyoti Rani

Abstract
We study spectral constants for convex domains Ω \Omega containing the spectrum of an operator. We extend the Crouzeix–Palencia framework by obtaining bounds depending on a parameter γ \gamma and relating these bounds to geometric properties of Ω \Omega and the numerical range W ⁡ ( A ) W(A) . We generalise the proof that the numerical range is a 1 + 2 1+\sqrt{2} -spectral set to scaled q q -numerical ranges. We also propose a generalisation of Crouzeix’s Conjecture in the context of q q -numerical ranges.



Keywords:
numerical range, spectral set, Crouzeix conjecture,
q
-numerical range, spectral constants

Introduction

Let
A
be a bounded operator on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be a bounded connected set such that the spectrum is contained in the closure of
\Omega
. If for all
f\in{\cal A}(\Omega)
(holomorphic functions on the interior of
\Omega
which are continuous on the boundary of
\Omega
) we have

\|f(A)\|\leq c_{\Omega}\sup_{z\in\Omega}|f(z)|

then
\Omega
is said to be a
c_{\Omega}
-spectral set
. In this case we call
c_{\Omega}
the
spectral constant
.

One of the most important results in operator theory is von Neumann’s inequality which states that when
\|A\|\leq 1
the unit disk is a 1-spectral set. Crouzeix’s Conjecture claims that the numerical range,
W(A)=\{\langle Ax,x\rangle:\|x\|=1\}
is a 2-spectral set. Thus far, the best known spectral constant for the numerical range is
1+\sqrt{2}

[
4
,
20
]
. Crouzeix’s Conjecture has gained a lot of traction from the Functional and Complex Analysis communities
[
17
,
6
,
5
,
10
,
1
,
8
,
13
,
2
,
7
,
11
,
14
,
4
,
2
,
15
]
. The main result of this paper provides spectral constant bounds for the scaled

q
-numerical range
\Omega_{q}=W_{q}(A)/q
. These bounds depend on the parameter

q
and on a quantity measuring the non-normality of
A
. When
q=1
the result recovers the

1+\sqrt{2}
spectral constant bound for the numerical range obtained in

[
4
]
.

In Section
2
we extend previous results in
[
2
,

]
, and deduce a spectral constant for
\Omega
which depends on a parameter

coming from a positivity criterion on the double layer potential. In Section

we obtain explicit spectral constant bounds for operators

only dependent on the geometry of
\Omega
and

. In Section

-numerical range,

\Omega_{q}=\frac{W_{q}(A)}{q},\quad W_{q}(A)=\{\langle Ax,y\rangle:\|x\|=\|y\|=1,\,\langle x,y\rangle=q\},

is a complex number such that

2
Spectral Constants

Recall
{\cal A}(\Omega)
denotes the holomorphic functions on the interior of
\Omega
which are continuos on the boundary of
\Omega
. For any function
f\in{\cal A}(\Omega)
, we define
f(A)
via the Cauchy integral formula as

f(A)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Omega}(\sigma I-A)^{-1}f(\sigma)\,d\sigma.

If
f(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}z^{i}
is a polynomial with
a_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
, then the functional calculus above becomes
f(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}A^{i}
. If
|\partial\Omega|=L
and
\sigma:[0,L]\to\partial\Omega
is an arc length parametrisation of
\partial\Omega
, then

f(A)=\int_{0}^{L}\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(s)}{2\pi i}R(\sigma(s),A)f(\sigma(s))\,ds,

where
R(\sigma,A)
is the resolvent,
(\sigma I-A)^{-1}
.

A key idea in
[
4
]
was to look at

g(A):=\int_{0}^{L}\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(s)}{2\pi i}R(\sigma(s),A)\overline{f(\sigma(s))}\,ds,

(1)

which in the scalar case reduces to the Cauchy transform of
\overline{f}
.
Crouzeix and Palencia analysed the operator

f(A)+g(A)^{*}=\int_{0}^{L}\mu(\sigma(s),A)f(\sigma(s))\,ds,

where the Hermitian operator
\mu(\sigma(s),A)
is

\mu(\sigma(s),A)=\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(s)}{2\pi i}R(\sigma(s),A)+\left[\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(s)}{2\pi i}R(\sigma(s),A)\right]^{*}.

(2)

They showed that
\|f(A)+g(A)^{*}\|\leq 2
, and then consequently showed that the numerical range was a
1+\sqrt{2}
-spectral set (see also
[
20
]
for an alternative shorter proof). In
[
2
]
, the authors studied regions
\Omega

that do not necessarily contain
W(A)
. They defined

M(\sigma,A):=\mu(\sigma,A)-\lambda_{\min}(\mu(\sigma,A))I,

(3)

where
\lambda_{\min}(\mu(\sigma,A))
is the infimum of the spectrum of

\mu(\sigma,A)
at the point
\sigma
on
\partial\Omega
. Thus, by definition,

M(\sigma,A)
is positive semidefinite on
\partial\Omega
.

We introduce the notation

\gamma(f):=-\int_{0}^{L}\lambda_{\min}(\mu(\sigma(s),A))f(\sigma(s))\,ds,\quad|\partial\Omega|=L.

(4)

Using the same method of proof as in
[
4
]
, they proved the following lemma. We provide an alternate proof.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a region with smooth boundary containing the spectrum of
A
in its interior.
For
f\in{\cal A}(\Omega)
with
\|f\|_{\Omega}=1
, let

S:=f(A)+g(A)^{*}+\gamma(f)I.

(5)

Then
\|S\|\leq 2+\gamma(1)
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi(h)=\int_{0}^{L}h(s)M(\sigma(s),A)ds
be a map from continuous function on
\partial\Omega
to the space of bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}
. Then since
M
is positive semidefinite,
\Phi
is a positive map between unital
C^{*}
algebras and thus by
[
18
, Chapter 2]

\|\Phi\|=\Phi(1)=2+\gamma(1)
. So for
f\in{\cal A}(\Omega)
with
\|f\|_{\Omega}\leq 1
, we have
\|S\|=\|\Phi(f)\|\leq 2+\gamma(1)
.
∎

In
[
2
]
, the authors then used this lemma to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\Omega
be bounded, convex domain with smooth boundary containing the spectrum of
A

in its interior. Then

\Omega
is a
1+\frac{\gamma(1)}{2}+\sqrt{2+\gamma(1)+\gamma(1)^{2}/4+m}
-spectral set where

m=\int_{0}^{L}|\lambda_{\min}(\mu(\sigma(s),A))|\,ds.

(6)

Theorem 2 in
[
3
]
sharpens this spectral constant to

1+\frac{\gamma(1)}{2}+\sqrt{\left(1+\frac{\gamma(1)}{2}\right)^{2}+c_{1}+\hat{\gamma}},\quad\hat{\gamma}:=\max\{|\gamma(f)|\,:|f|\leq 1\text{ in }\Omega\}

where
c_{1}=\sup\{\|g\|_{\infty,\Omega}:\|f\|_{\infty,\Omega}\leq 1\}
and
g
is the Cauchy transform of
\overline{f}
. We sharpen this result with the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

1.

Let
\Omega
be bounded, convex domain with smooth boundary containing the spectrum of
A

in its interior. Then

\Omega
is a

1+\frac{\gamma(1)}{2}+\sqrt{\left(1+\frac{\gamma(1)}{2}\right)^{2}+a(\Omega)}

(7)

-spectral set, where
a(\Omega)=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)}\{\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}\|g-\lambda\|_{\infty,\Omega}:\|f\|_{\infty,\Omega}\leq 1\}
, where
g
is the Cauchy transform of
\overline{f}
.

2.

There is a bounded, convex
\Omega
with smooth boundary containing the spectrum of
A
and a
f\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
such that
\|f(A)\|=\left(1+\frac{\gamma(1)}{2}+\sqrt{\left(1+\frac{\gamma(1)}{2}\right)^{2}+a(\Omega)}\right)\|f\|_{\Omega}
.

The second statement in the theorem above shows that spectral constant
1+\frac{\gamma(1)}{2}+\sqrt{\left(1+\frac{\gamma(1)}{2}\right)^{2}+a(\Omega)}
is in some sense optimal. In
[
14
]
the quantity
a(\Omega)
was studied to sharpen the
1+\sqrt{2}
spectral constant for the numerical range by an indefinite amount. Before proving Theorem
2.3
we state a result, which will be instrumental in the proof of Theorem
2.3
.

In the following theorem a function
\hat{f}\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
maximising
\|f(A)\|
among all holomorphic functions
f
on
\Omega
with
|f|\leq 1
on
\Omega
is called extremal for the pair
(A,\Omega)
. A
x\in\mathcal{H}
is said to be an associated extremal vector if
\|\hat{f}(A)\|\|x\|=\|\hat{f}(A)x\|
. By construction, if
\|\hat{f}(A)\|\leq c_{\Omega}
, then
\Omega
is a
c_{\Omega}
-spectral set.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\hat{f}
be extremal for
(A,\Omega)
, and let
x
be an associated extremal unit vector.
Then there exists a unique Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\partial\Omega
such that

\langle h(A)x,x\rangle=\int_{\partial\Omega}h\,d\mu\quad\text{ for all }h\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega).

(8)

If, further,
\|\hat{f}(A)\|>1
, then

\int_{\partial\Omega}\hat{f}d\mu=0.

(9)

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

1.

Let
\hat{f}
be an extremal function with associated extremal unit vector
x
and let
g
be the Cauchy transform of
\overline{\hat{f}}
. Then by Lemma
2.1
we have

\displaystyle\|\hat{f}(A)\|^{2}+\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}\left(\Re\int_{\partial\Omega}\hat{f}(g-\lambda)d\mu\right)

(10)

\displaystyle=\|\hat{f}(A)\|^{2}+\Re\int_{\partial\Omega}\hat{f}gd\mu\quad\text{by \eqref{E:rep}}

(11)

\displaystyle\leq|\|\hat{f}(A)\|^{2}+|\langle x,\hat{f}g(A)x\rangle|\quad\text{by \eqref{1st}}

(12)

\displaystyle=|\langle Sx,\hat{f}(A)x\rangle|\quad\text{by Lemma \ref{mainlemma} and \eqref{E:rep}}

(13)

\displaystyle\leq(2+\gamma(1))\|\hat{f}(A)\|\quad\text{by Lemma \ref{mainlemma}}.

(14)

Labelling
r=\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}\left(\Re\int_{\partial\Omega}\hat{f}g-\lambda)d\mu\right)
algebraically manipulating the above yields

\|\hat{f}(A)\|\leq 1+\frac{\gamma(1)}{2}+\sqrt{\left(1+\frac{\gamma(1)}{2}\right)^{2}-r}.

Since
\mu
is a probability measure and
\|\hat{f}\|_{\Omega}\leq 1
it follows that
|r|\leq a(\Omega)
, and thus statement
(i)
follows.

2.

This statement follows by setting
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
,
\Omega=W(A)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq 1\}
and
f(z)=z
. In this case, a direct computation yields
\lambda_{\min}(\mu(\sigma(s),A))=0
and
g=\overline{f(0)}
, and thus
a(\Omega)=0
.

∎

Remark 2.5
.

Note that when
\Omega=W(A)
we recover the spectral constant obtained in
[
14
]
.

3
Geometric Bounds for Spectral Constants

From Theorem
2.3
we see the spectral constant for
\Omega
depends on the parameter
\gamma(1)
(where
\gamma
is defined in (
4
)). In this section we estimate the parameter
\gamma(1)
to obtain spectral constants dependent on the geometry of
\Omega
and
W(A)
. The following two lemmas are needed for the proof of Theorem
3.3
.

The function,

h_{\Omega}(e^{i\theta}):=\sup_{p\in\Omega}\Re(pe^{-i\theta})

is called the support function of
\Omega
.

Lemma 3.1
.

\int_{0}^{2\pi}h_{\Omega}(e^{i\theta})d\theta=|\partial\Omega|.

Proof.

The projection of
\Omega
on
e^{i\theta}\mathbb{R}
has length
h_{\Omega}(e^{i\theta})+h_{\Omega}(-e^{i\theta})
, so Cauchy’s surface area formula (see, for example Theorem 5.2.1 in
[
21
]
) yields

\int_{0}^{2\pi}h_{\Omega}(e^{i\theta})d\theta=|\partial\Omega|.

(15)

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

1.

Let
W(A)\subseteq\Omega
and suppose
W(A)
has non-empty interior and
v
be the unit normal at
\sigma\in\partial\Omega
. Then

\frac{1}{\pi}r(\sigma)\left(h_{\Omega}(v)-h_{W(A)}(v)\right)\leq\lambda_{\min}(\mu(\sigma,A)),

(16)

where
r(\sigma)=\inf_{\|y\|=1}\|(\sigma-A)^{-1}y\|^{2}
is the square of the minimum modulus of the resolvent.

2.

Let
w_{\Omega}(A)=\sup_{z\in\Omega}(|z|)
and
w_{W(A)}=\sup_{z\in W(A)}(|z|)
. Then

\left(\frac{1}{w_{\Omega}(A)+2w_{W(A)}}\right)^{2}\leq r(\sigma).

Proof.

1.

Let
\|y\|=1
and
y=(\sigma-A)x
. As
\mu(\sigma(s),A)
is a selfadjoint matrix, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}(\mu(\sigma(s),A))

(17)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\|y\|=1}\langle\mu(\sigma(s),A)y,y\rangle

(18)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\|y\|=1}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\|x\|^{2}\Re\left(\left(\sigma-\left\langle A\frac{x}{\|x\|},\frac{x}{\|x\|}\right)\right\rangle\right)v^{*}\right)

(19)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\pi}r(\sigma)\inf_{p\in W(A)}\Re((\sigma-p)v^{*})

(20)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}r(\sigma)\left(\Re(\sigma v^{*})-h_{W(A)}(v)\right)

(21)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}r(\sigma)\left(h_{\Omega}(v)-h_{W(A)}(v)\right),

(22)

where
\Re(\sigma v^{*})=h_{\Omega}(v)
because convexity of
\Omega
and the supporting half plane condition yields
\Re(zv^{*})\leq\Re(\sigma v^{*})
for all
z\in\Omega
.

2.

Observe for each
y
of unit norm, we have
y=(\sigma I-A)(\sigma I-A)^{-1}y
, so the sub-multiplicative property of norms gives

\frac{1}{|\sigma|+\|A\|}\leq\frac{1}{\|(\sigma I-A)\|}\leq\|(\sigma I-A)^{-1}y\|.

The result follows since
\|A\|\leq 2w_{W(A)}
.

∎

We now present the main result of this section which provides a bound for
\gamma(1)
only in terms of the geometry of
\Omega
and
W(A)
. In the following theorem
w_{\Omega}(A)=\sup_{z\in\Omega}(|z|)
and
w_{W(A)}=\sup_{z\in W(A)}(|z|)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
W(A)
have non-empty interior. Then for
\gamma
as given in (
4
),

\gamma(1)\leq-\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{w_{\Omega}(A)+2w_{W(A)}}\right)^{2}\min_{\sigma\in\partial\Omega}\rho(\sigma)\left(|\partial\Omega|-|\partial W(A)|\right),

where
\rho(\sigma)
is the radius of curvature at a point
\sigma\in\partial\Omega
.

Proof.

The previous lemma gives

\gamma(1)\leq-\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{w_{\Omega}(A)+2w_{W(A)}}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{L}\left(h_{\Omega}(v)-h_{W(A)}(v)\right)ds.

Write
v(s)=e^{i\theta(s)}
, then Frenet formulas imply
\kappa(s)=|v^{\prime}(s)|=\theta^{\prime}(s)
where
\kappa
is the (unsigned) curvature, so
ds=\rho(\theta)d\theta
where
\rho(\theta)=\frac{1}{k(\theta)}
is the radius of curvature. Thus

\gamma(1)\leq-\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{w_{\Omega}(A)+2w_{W(A)}}\right)^{2}\min_{\sigma\in\partial\Omega}\rho(\sigma)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(h_{\Omega}(e^{i\theta})-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})\right)d\theta.

Now the result follows from Lemma
3.1
.
∎

There are several proofs in the literature (see for example
[
4
,
16
]
) showing that if
\Omega
is a disc with
W(A)\subseteq\Omega
, then
\Omega
is a
2
-spectral set for
A
. The result above, combined with Theorem
2.3
, yields an explicit improvement in the case when
W(A)\subsetneq\Omega
. It provides a computable constant
K<2
, depending only on the geometry of
W(A)
and
\Omega
, such that
\Omega
is a
K
-spectral set for
A
.

4
Scaled q-Numerical Range as a Spectral Set

In this section for
A\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
such that
A\neq\lambda I
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
we consider the scaled
q
-numerical range
\Omega_{q}:=\frac{W_{q}(A)}{q}
for
0<|q|<1
as a spectral set. In general
W_{q}(A)
cannot be a spectral set since there are matrices with eigenvalues lying outside
W_{q}(A)
. By contrast,
W(A)\subseteq\Omega_{q}

[
9
, p. 381]
, and since
W(A)
is a spectral set
\Omega_{q}
will also be a spectral set for
A
. Note that
W_{q}(A)
(and hence
\Omega_{q}
) is convex
[
9
, p. 381]
and the spectrum of
A
lies in the interior of
\Omega_{q}

[
12
, Theorem 2.10]
. Throughout this section, in order to apply the results of previous sections, (where it was assumed
\Omega
had smooth boundary) we further assume
A
is such that
\Omega_{q}
has smooth boundary, this holds for example when
\mathcal{H}
is finite dimensional
[
19
]
.

By applying similar arguments to those used in

[
12
, Theorem 2.5]

in the operator setting, it follows that

\Omega_{q_{1}}\subseteq\Omega_{q_{2}}

whenever
0<|q_{2}|\leq|q_{1}|<1
, it is natural to expect the corresponding spectral constant for
\Omega_{q}
to decrease as
|q|
decreases.
Furthermore any nontrivial bound must depend not only on
q
but also on a quantitative measure of the non-normality of
A
. When
\mathcal{H}
is finite dimensional, the term

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sup_{x\in M_{\theta}}\sqrt{\|Ax\|^{2}-|\langle Ax,x\rangle|^{2}}\,d\theta

where
M_{\theta}=\arg\max_{\|x\|=1}\Re\langle e^{-i\theta}Ax,x\rangle
plays this role. However
M_{\theta}
may be empty when
\mathcal{H}
is infinite dimensional, so we consider the set of approximate maximisers
M_{\theta,\varepsilon}
in the theorem below. Moreover, the furthermore part identifies this integral as a variational quantity, describing the initial rate of change of
|\partial\Omega_{q}|
as
|q|
varies. The following theorem generalises the result that the numerical range is a

1+\sqrt{2}
-spectral set
[
4
]
by providing a bound for the spectral constant of
the scaled
q
-numerical range
\Omega_{q}
. The bound depends on
q
and on a
quantity measuring the non-normality of
A
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
0<|q|<1
, let
\Omega_{q}=\frac{W_{q}(A)}{q}
have smooth boundary and let
W(A)
have non-empty interior. For
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
and
\varepsilon>0
, define

M_{\theta,\varepsilon}:=\left\{x\in H:\|x\|=1,\ \Re\langle e^{-i\theta}Ax,x\rangle\geq h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})-\varepsilon\right\},

and set

m(\theta):=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sup_{x\in M_{\theta,\varepsilon}}\sqrt{\|Ax\|^{2}-|\langle Ax,x\rangle|^{2}}.

Then

\gamma(1)\leq-C\min_{\sigma\in\partial\Omega_{q}}\rho(\sigma)\frac{\sqrt{1-|q|^{2}}}{|q|}\int_{0}^{2\pi}m(\theta)\,d\theta,

where

C=\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{w_{\Omega_{q}}(A)+2w_{W(A)}}\right)^{2}.

Furthermore setting
t\in(0,\infty)
such that
|q|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}
and writing
\Omega(t)=\Omega_{q(t)}
we have

\int_{0}^{2\pi}m(\theta)=\frac{d}{dt}_{|_{t=0+}}\left|\partial\Omega(t)\right|.

Proof.

First observe that
\Omega_{q}=\Omega_{|q|}
so it suffices to prove the theorem for
\Omega_{|q|}
. Furthermore, in light of the previous theorem and Lemma
3.1
it suffices to prove that

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(h_{\Omega_{|q|}}(e^{i\theta})-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})\right)ds\geq\frac{\sqrt{1-|q|^{2}}}{|q|}\int_{0}^{2\pi}m(\theta).

Fix
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
and
x\in\mathcal{H}
with
\|x\|=1
. Then any
y\in\mathcal{H}
with
\|y\|=1
and
\langle x,y\rangle=|q|
can be written as

y=|q|x+\sqrt{1-|q|^{2}}\,z
with
z\perp x
and
\|z\|=1
. Hence

\displaystyle\Re\big(e^{-i\theta}\langle Ax,y\rangle\big)=|q|\,\Re\langle e^{-i\theta}Ax,x\rangle+\sqrt{1-|q|^{2}}\,\Re\langle e^{-i\theta}Ax,z\rangle

(23)

\displaystyle\leq|q|\,\Re\langle e^{-i\theta}Ax,x\rangle+\sqrt{1-|q|^{2}}\,\|P_{x^{\perp}}(e^{-i\theta}Ax)\|,

(24)

where
P_{x^{\perp}}
is the projection onto the orthogonal complement of the span of
x
. Clearly equality is attained by choosing
z
in the direction of
P_{x^{\perp}}(e^{-i\theta}Ax)
and so

h_{W_{|q|}(A)}(e^{i\theta})=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\Big(|q|\,\Re\langle e^{-i\theta}Ax,x\rangle+\sqrt{1-|q|^{2}}\,\|P_{x^{\perp}}(e^{-i\theta}Ax)\|\Big),

which means

h_{\Omega_{|q|}}(e^{i\theta})=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\Big(\Re\langle e^{-i\theta}Ax,x\rangle+\frac{\sqrt{1-|q|^{2}}}{|q|}\,\|P_{x^{\perp}}(e^{-i\theta}Ax)\|\Big).

(25)

For every
\varepsilon>0
, restricting the supremum to
x\in M_{\theta,\varepsilon}
gives

h_{\Omega_{|q|}}(e^{i\theta})\geq\sup_{x\in M_{\theta,\varepsilon}}\left(\Re\langle e^{-i\theta}Ax,x\rangle+\frac{\sqrt{1-|q|^{2}}}{|q|}\|P_{x^{\perp}}(e^{-i\theta}Ax)\|\right).

Hence

\displaystyle h_{\Omega_{|q|}}(e^{i\theta})-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})

(26)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{x\in M_{\theta,\varepsilon}}\left(\Re\langle e^{-i\theta}Ax,x\rangle-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})+\frac{\sqrt{1-|q|^{2}}}{|q|}\|P_{x^{\perp}}(e^{-i\theta}Ax)\|\right).

(27)

Since

\Re\langle e^{-i\theta}Ax,x\rangle\geq h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})-\varepsilon\qquad\text{for }x\in M_{\theta,\varepsilon},

it follows that

h_{\Omega_{|q|}}(e^{i\theta})-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})\geq-\varepsilon+\frac{\sqrt{1-|q|^{2}}}{|q|}\sup_{x\in M_{\theta,\varepsilon}}\|P_{x^{\perp}}(e^{-i\theta}Ax)\|.

Using

\|P_{x^{\perp}}(e^{-i\theta}Ax)\|^{2}=\|e^{-i\theta}Ax\|^{2}-|\langle e^{-i\theta}Ax,x\rangle|^{2}=\|Ax\|^{2}-|\langle Ax,x\rangle|^{2},

we obtain

h_{\Omega_{|q|}}(e^{i\theta})-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})\geq-\varepsilon+\frac{\sqrt{1-|q|^{2}}}{|q|}\sup_{x\in M_{\theta,\varepsilon}}\sqrt{\|Ax\|^{2}-|\langle Ax,x\rangle|^{2}}.

Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
yields

h_{\Omega_{|q|}}(e^{i\theta})-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})\geq\frac{\sqrt{1-|q|^{2}}}{|q|}\,m(\theta).

Integrating over
[0,2\pi]
, it follows that

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bigl(h_{\Omega_{|q|}}(e^{i\theta})-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})\bigr)\,d\theta\geq\frac{\sqrt{1-|q|^{2}}}{|q|}\int_{0}^{2\pi}m(\theta)\,d\theta.

The desired bound for
\gamma(1)
now follows from Theorem
3.3
and Lemma
3.1
.

We now prove the furthermore statement. From (
25
), noting that
t=\frac{\sqrt{1-|q|^{2}}}{|q|}
,
we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{h_{\Omega(t)}(e^{i\theta})-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})}{t}

(28)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\left(\frac{\Re\langle e^{-i\theta}Ax,x\rangle-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})}{t}+\|P_{x^{\perp}}(e^{-i\theta}Ax)\|\right).

(29)

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. If
x\notin M_{\theta,\varepsilon}
, then
\Re\langle e^{-i\theta}Ax,x\rangle-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})<-\varepsilon
,
and hence

\frac{\Re\langle e^{-i\theta}Ax,x\rangle-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})}{t}+\|P_{x^{\perp}}(e^{-i\theta}Ax)\|<-\frac{\varepsilon}{t}+\|A\|.

Thus, for sufficiently small
t
, the supremum may be restricted to vectors

x\in M_{\theta,\varepsilon}
, and so

\frac{h_{\Omega(t)}(e^{i\theta})-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})}{t}\leq\sup_{x\in M_{\theta,\varepsilon}}\|P_{x^{\perp}}(e^{-i\theta}Ax)\|.

Taking
\limsup
as
t\downarrow 0
gives

\limsup_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{h_{\Omega(t)}(e^{i\theta})-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})}{t}\leq\sup_{x\in M_{\theta,\varepsilon}}\|P_{x^{\perp}}(e^{-i\theta}Ax)\|.

Since this holds for every
\varepsilon>0
, it follows that

\limsup_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{h_{\Omega(t)}(e^{i\theta})-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})}{t}\leq m(\theta).

(30)

Let
\delta>0
. If
x\in M_{\theta,\delta t}
, then
\Re\langle e^{-i\theta}Ax,x\rangle-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})\geq-\delta t
and so

\frac{h_{\Omega(t)}(e^{i\theta})-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})}{t}\geq-\delta+\|P_{x^{\perp}}(e^{-i\theta}Ax)\|.

Taking the supremum over
x\in M_{\theta,\delta t}
yields

\frac{h_{\Omega(t)}(e^{i\theta})-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})}{t}\geq-\delta+\sup_{x\in M_{\theta,\delta t}}\|P_{x^{\perp}}(e^{-i\theta}Ax)\|.

Taking
\liminf
as
t\downarrow 0
, we obtain

\liminf_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{h_{\Omega(t)}(e^{i\theta})-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})}{t}\geq-\delta+m(\theta).

Since
\delta>0
is arbitrary, it follows that

\liminf_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{h_{\Omega(t)}(e^{i\theta})-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})}{t}\geq m(\theta).

(31)

Combining (
30
) and (
31
) gives

\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0+}h_{\Omega(t)}(e^{i\theta})=m(\theta).

Observe that

\|P_{x^{\perp}}(e^{-i\theta}Ax)\|^{2}=\|e^{-i\theta}Ax\|^{2}-|\langle e^{-i\theta}Ax,x\rangle|^{2}=\|Ax\|^{2}-|\langle Ax,x\rangle|^{2},

so

0\leq\frac{h_{\Omega(t)}(e^{i\theta})-h_{W(A)}(e^{i\theta})}{t}\leq\sup_{\|x\|=1}\|P_{x^{\perp}}(e^{-i\theta}Ax)\|\leq\|A\|.

Thus the dominated convergence theorem allows us to integrate the derivative identity,
and by Lemma
3.1
we obtain

\int_{0}^{2\pi}m(\theta)\,d\theta=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0+}|\partial\Omega(t)|.

∎

We remark that when
\mathcal{H}
is finite dimensional, the
m(\theta)
in the theorem above can be replaced with
\sup_{x\in M_{\theta}}\sqrt{\|Ax\|^{2}-|\langle Ax,x\rangle|^{2}}
. Furthermore when
q=1
the set
\Omega_{q}
reduces to the numerical range
W(A)
and the
result recovers the
1+\sqrt{2}
spectral constant bound.
