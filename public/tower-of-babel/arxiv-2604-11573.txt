Asymptotic-Preserving and Well-Balanced Linearly Implicit IMEX Schemes for the Anelastic Limit of the Isentropic Euler Equations with Gravity Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Marco Artiano, Hendrik Ranocha, Saurav Samantaray

Abstract
We consider the compressible Euler system with anelastic scaling, modeling isentropic flows under the influence of gravity. In the zero-Mach-number limit, the solution of the compressible Euler system converges to a variable density anelastic incompressible limit system. In this work, we present the design and analysis of a class of higher-order linearly implicit IMEX Runge-Kutta schemes that are asymptotic preserving, i.e., they respect the transitory nature of the governing equations in the limit. The presence of gravitational potential warrants the incorporation of the well-balancing property. The scheme is developed as a novel combination of a penalization of a linear steady state, a finite-volume balance-preserving reconstruction, and a source term discretization preserving steady states. The penalization plays a crucial role in obtaining a linearly implicit scheme, and well-balanced flux-source discretization ensures accuracy in very low Mach number regimes. Some results of numerical case studies are presented to corroborate the theoretical assertions.

Introduction

Numerical approximation of the compressible Euler equations, owing to the multi-scale nature of the flow dynamics they model, is a challenging task. These equations model flows with velocities ranging from supersonic to low-speed (atmospheric) flows.
In this work, we consider the isentropic Euler equations with periodic or reflecting boundary conditions and source terms describing the effect of a gravitational potential, i.e.,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle=0,

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho\mathbf{u})+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u})+\frac{\nabla p}{\varepsilon^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\rho\nabla\phi}{\varepsilon^{2}},

(2)

where
\rho(t,\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}
is the fluid density,
\mathbf{q}(t,\mathbf{x})=\rho\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the momentum,
p(t,\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}
is the pressure,
\phi(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}
is a known gravitational potential,
t
denotes time, and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the spatial variable in
d
dimensions.
The parameter
\varepsilon
is a non-dimensional scaled Mach number describing the ratio of a reference fluid velocity to that of a reference sound velocity, i.e.,

\varepsilon=\frac{u_{\mathrm{ref}}}{\sqrt{p^{\prime}(\rho_{\mathrm{ref}})}}.

The above system is closed by considering an isentropic equation of state (EOS), which links the pressure and the density variables by the following power law:

p=P(\rho)=\rho^{\gamma},

(3)

with the isentropic constant
\gamma>1
.
The
\varepsilon^{2}
denominator of the source term in (
2
) corresponds to a particular choice of scaling called anelastic scaling
[
18
,
26
,
29
]
.

The homogeneous counterpart of the above system has been extensively studied in the literature, e.g.,
[
25
]
.
In
[
17
]
the authors have investigated the homogeneous system with so-called well-prepared and ill-prepared initial conditions using the framework of dissipative measure-valued solutions to conclude that the solutions of the compressible system converge to smooth solutions of the incompressible Euler system when the Mach number goes to zero.

A formal derivation of the anelastic model for the adiabatic pressure law is carried out in
[
26
]
and for an isentropic pressure law in
[
3
]
.
Following
[
26
]
, formally, to obtain the limit system for the compressible Euler system (
1
)–(
2
) when
\varepsilon\to 0
, we begin by assuming that the dependent variables admit the three-term ansatz

f(t,\mathbf{x})=f_{(0)}(t,\mathbf{x})+\varepsilon f_{(1)}(t,\mathbf{x})+\varepsilon^{2}f_{(2)}(t,\mathbf{x})+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}).

(4)

Plugging the above into the Euler system (
1
)–(
2
) and first balancing the
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
terms in the momentum equation (
2
) yields the hydrostatic balance at zeroth order:

\nabla p_{(0)}=-\rho_{(0)}\nabla\phi.

(5)

Making the anelastic assumption that the leading-order density
\rho_{(0)}
is temporally constant, so that any temporal variation in density occurs only at higher orders of
\varepsilon
, we can obtain the anelastic density for the limit system as

\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}:=\rho_{(0)}(\mathbf{x})=\left(C_{0}-\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\phi(\mathbf{x})\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}},

(6)

where
C_{0}
is a constant of integration.
Similarly, a balance between
p_{(1)}
and
\rho_{(1)}
like (
5
) can be obtained when
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-1})
terms are considered from the momentum balance (
2
). So, both the zeroth-order and the first-order densities
\rho_{(0)}
and
\rho_{(1)}
, respectively, exhibit the same dynamics.
Thus, we absorb one into the other and get the final multi-scale expansion for the density:

\rho(t,\mathbf{x})=\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}(\mathbf{x})+\varepsilon^{2}\rho_{(2)}(t,\mathbf{x}).

(7)

Considering the
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{0})
terms from the Euler system (
1
)–(
2
) we obtain the anelastic limit system:

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left(\rho_{(0)}\mathbf{u}_{(0)}\right)

\displaystyle=0,

(8)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\rho_{(0)}\mathbf{u}_{(0)}\right)+\nabla\cdot\left(\rho_{(0)}\mathbf{u}_{(0)}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{(0)}\right)+\nabla p_{(2)}

\displaystyle=-\rho_{(2)}\nabla\phi.

(9)

The asymptotic expansion for the pressure using the equation of state
p=P(\rho)
gives

p_{(0)}+\varepsilon^{2}p_{(2)}=P\left(\rho_{(0)}+\varepsilon^{2}\rho_{(2)}\right)=P(\rho_{(0)})+\varepsilon^{2}\rho_{(2)}P^{\prime}(\rho_{(0)}).

We close the anelastic limit system (
8
)–(
9
) by comparing the
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})
terms in the above expansion to obtain the closure relation

p_{(2)}=\rho_{(2)}P^{\prime}(\rho_{(0)}).

(10)

Note that, as explored in
[
17
]
, the convergence of the solution of the compressible system (
1
)–(
2
) to the anelastic limit system (
8
)–(
9
) can be obtained for both well-prepared and ill-prepared initial data; this notion will be defined precisely later.

Explicit time discretizations are plagued by prohibitively restrictive CFL conditions since the stability constraints are directly proportional to
\varepsilon
in the low-Mach regime.
For
\varepsilon\approx 0
these schemes, while adhering to the stability requirements, become almost non-evolving in time due to rather small time steps.
Moreover, explicit schemes also suffer from inaccuracies in the low Mach number regime due to their inability to filter out acoustic waves
[
14
]
. The framework of asymptotic-preserving (AP) schemes, initially introduced by Jin
[
22
]
, takes into account the transitional behavior of the governing equations for
\varepsilon\to 0
.
These schemes transform into approximations of the limit system while remaining stable with their stability requirements being independent of the singular perturbation parameter
\varepsilon
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Semi-implicit time-stepping schemes provide an efficacious framework for designing AP schemes
[
13
,
16
,
12
,
28
]
. The literature for semi-implicit schemes for the Euler equations is quite abundant
[
4
,
13
,
7
,
31
,
28
,
15
]
.
However, mere satisfaction of the AP property will not enable the scheme to perform well in the weakly asymptotic regime because of the gravitational source term.
As most of the flows under study in the low Mach regimes are perturbations of a steady state, it is absolutely critical for a numerical scheme to not amplify small perturbations to steady states.
This is usually achieved by obtaining discretizations which exactly preserve certain steady states, i.e., are well-balanced (WB)
[
10
,
5
]
. For example, the temporal semi-discretization

\displaystyle\frac{\rho^{n+1}-\rho^{n}}{\Delta t}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf{q}^{n+1}=0,

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{q}^{n+1}-\mathbf{q}^{n}}{\Delta t}+\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\mathbf{q}^{n}\otimes\mathbf{q}^{n}}{\rho^{n}}\right)+\frac{\nabla p(\rho^{n+1})}{\varepsilon^{2}}=-\frac{\rho^{n+1}\nabla\phi}{\varepsilon^{2}},

(12)

presented in
[
3
]
is formally asymptotic preserving.

\varepsilon=10^{-3}
.
In contrast, the WB scheme still damps the initial perturbation as expected.
This showcases the need for well-balancing while deriving a spatial discretization.

In
[
33
]
the authors have presented an AP-WB scheme for Euler equations with gravity and an energy equation using the Suliciu relaxation method.
In
[
5
]
the authors have developed AP-WB schemes using IMEX methods for the perturbations of variables.
We tread a different path to design an AP-WB scheme: we combine a penalization approach
[
16
]
, hydrostatic reconstruction of steady-state variables, and prudent source discretization
[
10
]
.
The penalization relies on the derivation of a linearized balance against the non-linear balance equation (
5
), but essentially having the same solution as (
6
).
This allows us to be linearly implicit, hence avoiding expensive Newton solvers.
The source pressure balance obtained following
[
10
]
and a novel discretization of the linear balance yields an AP-WB first-order scheme in time.
Higher orders in time are achieved by using the additive IMEX framework
[
30
]
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is dedicated to the development and AP analysis of the first-order in time semi-discrete scheme, including the derivation of the linear balance and the penalized momentum equation.
In Section
3
, higher-order extensions are obtained using additive IMEX Runge-Kutta (RK) schemes.
Spatial discretization of the time-semi-discrete scheme, in particular, a hydrostatic reconstruction and appropriate source-term discretization combined with a second-order finite volume (FV) discretization, thus yielding an implementable fully-discrete scheme, is presented in Section
4
.
Moreover, it is proved that the fully discrete scheme is well-balanced. In Section
5
, results of numerical case studies are presented to corroborate the AP and WB property of the designed penalized IMEX-RK schemes.
Finally, we close the paper in Section
6
with some conclusions and future prospects.

2
First-order time discretization and its analysis

To be asymptotic preserving in the limit of the Mach number going to zero, i.e.,
\varepsilon\to 0
, we follow the seminal works of
[
13
,
22
,
20
,
16
,
15
,
7
,
5
]
. The idea is to introduce some amount of implicitness to the numerical scheme judiciously so that the resulting scheme is able to transition to a consistent discretization of the limit system. To achieve this, the requirement is to identify terms (specifically flux terms) which are to be treated implicitly. The problem is that all the flux terms in the momentum equation are non-linear and, therefore, will pose challenges in the form of iterative Newton solvers when treated implicitly. The next design feature is that the first-order scheme must allow a straightforward higher-order extension using the classical additive IMEX-RK framework.

This section is organized as follows. We begin with some necessary definitions, pivotal to the analysis of the schemes to be designed.
Following that we recognize a few terms that are introduced to the momentum balance equation to aid in developing the time discretization (without the need of Newton solvers); similar developments have been reported in
[
16
]
for kinetic systems and for fluid systems in
[
12
,
31
]
.
This leads to the presentation of the design of the first-order time semi-discrete scheme.
Finally, the section ends with AP analysis of the developed scheme.

2.1
Well-prepared and ill-prepared initial data

Definition 1
(cf.
[
17
]
).

A tuple
(\rho,\mathbf{u})
of solutions to the compressible Euler equations (
1
)–(
2
) is said to be well-prepared if the following decompositions hold:

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\rho_{(0)}+\varepsilon^{2}\rho_{(2)}

(13)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{u}_{(0)}+\varepsilon\mathbf{u}_{(1)}

(14)

where:

\rho_{(0)}=\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\nabla\cdot(\rho_{(0)}\mathbf{u}_{(0)})=0.

(15)

Remark 2
.

It has to be noted that although well-preparedness of the initial data is a sufficient condition for the solution of the compressible system to converge to the solution of the incompressible counterpart, it is not absolutely necessary.

Definition 3
(cf.
[
17
]
).

A tuple
(\rho,\mathbf{u})
of solutions to the compressible Euler equations (
1
)–(
2
) is said to be ill-prepared if the decompositions (
13
)–(
14
) hold, but we only have that

||\rho_{(0)}||_{L^{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad||\nabla\cdot(\rho_{(0)}u_{(0)})||_{L^{2}}\quad\mbox{are bounded}

instead of (
15
).

Remark 4
.

In
[
17
]
the authors have shown that in spite of the ill-preparedness of the initial data for the isentropic Euler equation without gravitational source term, still the compressible solution at least locally converges to its incompressible counterpart in the limit of
\varepsilon\to 0
. Therefore, it would be highly desirable for a numerical scheme to perform a similar transitory act.

2.2
Equilibrium reformulation

The gradient of the equilibrium solution (
6
) is

\displaystyle\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}=-\frac{1}{\gamma}\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(2-\gamma)}\nabla\phi.

(16)

Note that when
\varepsilon\to 0
,
\rho\to\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}
. Using this and (
16
), we get

\displaystyle\nabla\rho+\frac{1}{\gamma}\rho^{(2-\gamma)}\nabla\phi\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.53581pt\varepsilon\to 0\hskip 8.53581pt}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\rho^{(2-\gamma)}_{\mathrm{eq}}\nabla\phi=0.

Assuming that
\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}>0
, which is a fair assumption since it is the density as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla\rho+\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\rho}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(\gamma-1)}}\nabla\phi\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.53581pt\varepsilon\to 0\hskip 8.53581pt}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(2-\gamma)}\nabla\phi=0.

The following equality can be obtained and used in the above expressions to simplify them:

\frac{\nabla\phi}{\gamma\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(\gamma-1)}}=-\frac{\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}.

Note that
\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}
is the unique solution to the non-linear problem (
5
) as well as for the linear problem

\nabla\rho-\frac{\rho}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}=0.

(17)

This linear balance (
17
) which has the same solution
\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}
as the non-linear balance (
5
) up to a constant multiple
K
(to be fixed to 1 by boundary conditions) allows us to maintain the non-linear balance by just maintaining the linear one.
The idea is to utilize the linear hydrostatic balance (
17
) to obtain a stiff-non-stiff splitting leading to an IMEX scheme.
To this end, we follow
[
31
]
and reformulate the momentum balance equation (
2
) by adding and subtracting the balanced terms in (
17
) to obtain the momentum equation

\partial_{t}(\rho\mathbf{u})+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u})+\frac{\nabla p}{\varepsilon^{2}}+\frac{\rho\nabla\phi}{\varepsilon^{2}}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\nabla\rho-\frac{\rho}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}\right)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\nabla\rho-\frac{\rho}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}\right)=0.

(18)

Remark 5
.

The addition and subtraction of terms has two fundamental aims.
The first and obvious one is to get linear terms associated to the non-linear balance (
5
) (to provide a choice of linear terms for implicit discretization).
Secondly, discretizing the newly introduced cancelling terms at two different time levels (one explicit and the other implicit) introduces a numerical error. This penalizing error contributes toward stabilizing the scheme to tackle smaller values of Mach numbers. This in turn leads to the asymptotic preserving property for the time discretization.

2.3
First-Order Time Discretization

The first-order accurate semi-implicit time discretization is obtained by treating the mass flux fully implicitly and making a judicious choice of splitting for the momentum fluxes and source terms.

Definition 6
.

The first-order time semi-discrete scheme for the Euler equations (
1
)–(
2
) updates the solution
(\rho^{n},\mathbf{q}^{n})
at time
t^{n}
to
(\rho^{n+1},\mathbf{q}^{n+1})
at time
t^{n+1}=t^{n}+\Delta t
semi-implicitly as

\displaystyle\frac{\rho^{n+1}-\rho^{n}}{\Delta t}+

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{q}^{n+1}=0,

(19)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{q}^{n+1}-\mathbf{q}^{n}}{\Delta t}+

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\mathbf{q}^{n}\otimes\mathbf{q}^{n}}{\rho^{n}}\right)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\nabla p^{n}+\rho^{n}\nabla\phi\right)-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\nabla\rho^{n}-\frac{\rho^{n}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}\right)+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\nabla\rho^{n+1}-\frac{\rho^{n+1}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}\right)=0.

(20)

Following the approach of
[
13
]
we obtain a reformulation of the above scheme. The numerical solution computed is the solution of this reformulation rather than the scheme in Definition
6
. To this end, the momentum update (
20
) can be written as

\mathbf{q}^{n+1}=\hat{\mathbf{q}}^{n+1}-\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\nabla\rho^{n+1}-\frac{\rho^{n+1}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}\right),

(21)

where
\hat{\mathbf{q}}^{n+1}
is the part of
\mathbf{q}^{n+1}
that can be updated explicitly, i.e.,

\hat{\mathbf{q}}^{n+1}:=\mathbf{q}^{n}-\Delta t\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\mathbf{q}^{n}\otimes\mathbf{q}^{n}}{\rho^{n}}\right)-\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\nabla p^{n}+\rho^{n}\nabla\phi\right)+\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\nabla\rho^{n}-\frac{\rho^{n}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}\right).

(22)

Substituting the expression for
\mathbf{q}^{n+1}
from (
21
) in the mass update (
19
) we get the following elliptic problem for
\rho^{n+1}
:

\rho^{n+1}=\rho^{n}-\Delta t\nabla\cdot\left(\hat{\mathbf{q}}^{n+1}-\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\nabla\rho^{n+1}-\frac{\rho^{n+1}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}\right)\right).

(23)

Definition 7
.

The first-order reformulated time semi-discrete scheme for the Euler equations (
1
)–(
2
) updates the solution
(\rho^{n},\mathbf{q}^{n})
at time
t^{n}
to
(\rho^{n+1},\mathbf{q}^{n+1})
at time
t^{n+1}
by first evaluating
\hat{\mathbf{q}}^{n+1}
via (
22
), followed by solving the ensuing linear elliptic problem, to update
\rho^{n+1}
,

-\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Delta\rho^{n+1}+\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\rho^{n+1}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}\right)+\rho^{n+1}=\rho^{n}-\Delta t\nabla\cdot\hat{\mathbf{q}}^{n+1}.

(24)

Once
\rho^{n+1}
is obtained, the momentum
\mathbf{q}^{n+1}
is computed explicitly via (
21
).

Algorithm 1

First-Order Scheme:
(\rho^{n},\mathbf{q}^{n})\to(\rho^{n+1},\mathbf{q}^{n+1})

1:

Compute
\hat{\mathbf{q}}^{n+1}
explicitly using (
22
)

2:

Solve the elliptic problem (
24
) for
\rho^{n+1}

3:

Update
\mathbf{q}^{n+1}
explicitly using (
21
)

Proposition 8
.

The first-order time semi-discrete scheme in Definition
6
and the reformulated one in Definition
7
are equivalent.

Remark 9
.

This reformulation technique was introduced in
[
13
]
. It renders an equivalent scheme which is at least twice (depending on spatial dimensions) as efficient as the original semi-implicit scheme since only one matrix inversion is required to solve the elliptic problem for the density
\rho^{n+1}
, avoiding the need to take
\mathbf{q}^{n+1}
as an unknown for the implicit scheme and thus leading to a much smaller system matrix.

2.4
Asymptotic Consistency

The AP property
[
23
]
of a numerical scheme is linked to two facets associated with any numerical discretization. The first is consistency: the ability to yield a consistent discretization of the limit system in the limit of the singular perturbation parameter going to zero, called the asymptotic consistency. The second one is stability: the ability of the scheme to remain stable independent of the singular perturbation parameter, called asymptotic stability. Here we study the consistency of the first-order time semi-discrete scheme for
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proposition 10
.

Suppose that
(\rho^{n},\mathbf{u}^{n})
at time
t^{n}
are well-prepared, i.e., they admit the decomposition in Definition
1
. Then, if the solution
(\rho^{n+1},\mathbf{u}^{n+1})
defined by the scheme in Definition
6
admits the multi-scale decomposition (
13
)–(
14
), it is well-prepared as well. Further, the time semi-discrete scheme in Definition
6
transforms to a consistent discretization of the incompressible limit system, in other words, the first-order time semi-discretization is weakly asymptotically consistent.

Proof.

We start by stating the assumption of the multi-scale decomposition for
(\rho^{n+1},\mathbf{u}^{n+1})
, i.e.,

\rho^{n+1}=\rho_{(0)}^{n+1}+\varepsilon^{2}\rho_{(2)}^{n+1},\qquad\mathbf{u}^{n+1}=\mathbf{u}_{(0)}^{n+1}+\varepsilon\mathbf{u}_{(1)}^{n+1}.

(25)

Plugging this into the momentum update (
20
), the
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
terms yield

\left(\nabla p_{(0)}^{n}+\rho_{(0)}^{n}\nabla\phi\right)-\left(\nabla\rho^{n}_{(0)}-\frac{\rho^{n}_{(0)}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}\right)+\left(\nabla\rho^{n+1}_{(0)}-\frac{\rho^{n+1}_{(0)}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}\right)=0.

(26)

Since
\rho_{(0)}^{n}
is well-prepared, i.e.,
\rho_{(0)}^{n}
satisfies (
5
), the expression in the first bracket above is zero.
Similarly, the terms in the second bracket also vanish since
\rho^{n}
is well-prepared, i.e.,
\rho_{(0)}^{n}=\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}
and by the equality in (
16
), the linearized hydrostatic balance.
Therefore, the above equation boils down to

\nabla\rho^{n+1}_{(0)}-\frac{\rho^{n+1}_{(0)}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}=0,

which can be solved for
\rho^{n+1}_{(0)}
uniquely up to a constant
K
by imposing appropriate (periodic or no-flux) boundary conditions to obtain

\rho^{n+1}_{(0)}=K\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}.

(27)

Proceeding further by considering the
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{0})
terms in the mass update (
19
) we get

\frac{\rho_{(0)}^{n+1}-\rho_{(0)}^{n}}{\Delta t}=-\nabla\cdot(\rho_{(0)}^{n+1}\mathbf{u}^{n+1}_{(0)}).

(28)

Integrating over a domain
\Omega
, using Gauss’ divergence theorem and imposing periodic or no-flux boundary conditions, the right-hand-side integral vanishes, yielding

\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{\Omega}K\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}-\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}=0.

Thus,
K=1
or
\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}=0
; since the second is in general not true we conclude that
K=1
, which gives that the density
\rho^{n+1}
is well-prepared i.e.
\rho^{n+1}_{(0)}=\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}
.

Now using the above in the
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{0})
terms of the mass update (
28
) we obtain the asymptotic divergence condition (
8
) for time
t^{n+1}
as

\nabla\cdot(\rho_{(0)}^{n+1}\mathbf{u}_{(0)}^{n+1})=0.

(29)

Finally, considering
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{0})
terms of the momentum update (
20
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{q}^{n+1}_{(0)}-\mathbf{q}^{n}_{(0)}}{\Delta t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho_{(0)}^{n}\mathbf{u}_{(0)}^{n}\otimes\mathbf{u}^{n}_{(0)})

(30)

\displaystyle+(\nabla p_{(2)}^{n}+\rho_{(2)}^{n}\nabla\phi)-\nabla(\rho_{(2)}^{n}-\rho_{(2)}^{n+1})

\displaystyle+\frac{(\rho_{(2)}^{n}-\rho_{(2)}^{n+1})}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}=0.

This is a consistent discretization of the limit equation (
9
), where
\nabla p_{(2)}^{n}
is the incompressible pressure term and
\rho_{(2)}^{n}\nabla\phi
is the source term for the incompressible limit system. The extra terms, i.e.,
\nabla(\rho_{(2)}^{n}-\rho_{(2)}^{n+1})
and
\frac{\rho_{(2)}^{n}-\rho_{(2)}^{n+1}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}
are, respectively, first-order discretizations of
\nabla(\rho_{(2)}-\rho_{(2)})
and
\frac{(\rho_{(2)}-\rho_{(2)})}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}
, which are essentially zero terms (up to the order of the time integration scheme).

Remark 11
.

The above proof for asymptotic consistency requires well-prepared data
(\rho^{n},\mathbf{u}^{n})
at time
t^{n}
. This is called weak asymptotic consistency
[
31
]
.

Suppose the initial condition is not well-prepared (Definition
1
).
Now, considering
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
terms in (
20
) yields (
26
).
The lack of well-preparedness implies

\nabla p_{(0)}^{n}+\rho_{(0)}^{n}\nabla\phi\neq 0,\qquad\nabla\rho^{n}_{(0)}-\frac{\rho^{n}_{(0)}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}\neq 0.

As a consequence, we do not have the well-preparedness for
\rho^{n+1}
.
The inability of the first-order scheme to furnish a well-prepared density hinders the extraction of the divergence-free condition for the limiting velocity.
Hence, the first-order time semi-discrete scheme in Definition
6
is not strongly asymptotic preserving.

Remark 12
.

For strongly asymptotic consistency, type-A IMEX-RK schemes

,
24
]
have to be considered, as discussed in the next section.

This class of additively-split IMEX-RK schemes provides an uncomplicated, robust, and efficient base to design schemes that are uniformly stable with respect to fast-scale dynamics.
In the recent past, they have been extensively used

3.1
Additively split IMEX-RK methods

We restrict ourselves to a specific subclass of IMEX-RK schemes; namely, we rely on diagonally implicit (DIRK) schemes. This is a typical choice in many different contexts where computational cost is one of the main concerns. In general, an
s
-stage IMEX-RK scheme is characterized by the two
s\times s
lower triangular matrices
\tilde{A}=(\tilde{a}_{i,j})
, and
A=(a_{i,j})
, the coefficients
\tilde{c}=(\tilde{c}_{1},\tilde{c}_{2},\ldots,\tilde{c}_{s})
and
c=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{s})
, and the weights
\tilde{\omega}=(\tilde{\omega}_{1},\tilde{\omega}_{2},\ldots,\tilde{\omega}_{s})
and
\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\ldots,\omega_{s})
.
Here, for the DIRK class, the entries of
\tilde{A}
and
A
satisfy the conditions
\tilde{a}_{i,j}=0
for
j\geq i
, and
a_{i,j}=0
for
j>i
. To introduce the method, let us consider the following general additively split stiff system of ODEs:

y^{\prime}=f(t,y)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}g(t,y),

(31)

where
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
is called the stiffness parameter. The functions
f
and
g
are known as, respectively, the non-stiff part and the stiff part of the system (
31
) (see, e.g.
[
21
,
9
]
, for a comprehensive treatment of such systems). Let
y^{n}
be a solution of (
31
) at time
t^{n}
and let
\Delta t
denote a fixed time step. An
s
-stage IMEX-RK scheme updates
y^{n}
to
y^{n+1}
through the computation of
s
intermediate stage updates:

Y^{(i)}=y^{n}+\Delta t\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i-1}\tilde{a}_{i,j}f(t^{n}+\tilde{c}_{j}\Delta t,Y^{(j)})+\Delta t\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}a_{i,j}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}g(t^{n}+c_{j}\Delta t,Y^{(j)}),\ 1\leq i\leq s,

(32)

followed by the final update:

y^{n+1}=y^{n}+\Delta t\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\tilde{\omega}_{i}f(t^{n}+\tilde{c}_{i}\Delta t,Y^{(i)})+\Delta t\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\omega_{i}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}g(t^{n}+c_{i}\Delta t,Y^{(i)}),

(33)

Typically IMEX-RK schemes are represented by a pair of Butcher tableaux

\begin{array}[]{c|c}\tilde{c}&\tilde{A}\\
\hline\cr&\tilde{\omega}^{T}\end{array}\qquad\begin{array}[]{c|c}c&A\\
\hline\cr&\omega^{T}\end{array}

(34)

The values of the RK coefficients of the Butcher tableaux in (
34
) for a particular IMEX-RK scheme satisfy the particular order conditions; see
[
30
]
.

We consider the following two subcategories of IMEX-RK schemes, namely type-A and type-CK, which are defined below following
[
6
,
16
,
24
]
.

Definition 13
.

An IMEX-RK scheme is said to be of

•

type-A, if the matrix
A
is invertible;

•

type-CK, if the matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times s},\ s\geq 2
,
can be written as

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
\alpha&A_{s-1}\end{pmatrix},

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{s-1}
and
A_{s-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{s-1\times s-1}
is invertible.

Another property which is crucial to obtain the AP property for non-linear problems is that the IMEX-RK scheme is globally stiffly accurate
[
8
]
.

Definition 14
.

An IMEX-RK scheme with the Butcher tableaux given in
(
34
) is said to be globally stiffly
accurate (GSA), if

\tilde{a}_{s,j}=\tilde{\omega}_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad a_{s,j}=\omega_{j}\qquad\text{for all }j=1,\ldots,s.

(35)

This property is enough to guarantee that the solution obtained from the last stage of the IMEX-RK method coincides with the numerical solution
y^{n+1}=Y^{(s)}
.

3.3
Strong asymptotic consistency

As stated in Section
2
, the first-order scheme relies on well-prepared initial data to be asymptotically consistent. This deficiency or rather strict requirement on the initial data can be relaxed for type-A schemes. To this end we show that for type-A schemes (e.g. DP-A(1,2,1) in (
44
)), asymptotic consistency of the general IMEX-RK scheme can be obtained after two time steps, even for ill-prepared initial data (Definition
3
).

Proposition 21
.

Suppose
(\rho^{n},\mathbf{u}^{n})
, the datum at time
t^{n}
is ill-prepared (Definition
3
). Let the solution
(\rho^{n+1},\mathbf{u}^{n+1})
at time
t^{n+1}
admit the multiscale decomposition (
4
) for the
s
-stage
IMEX-RK scheme in Definition
15
with type-A tableaux. Then, the solution after two time steps, i.e.,
(\rho^{n+2},\mathbf{u}^{n+2})
is well-prepared and the scheme reduces to a consistent discretization of the anelastic limit system (
8
)–(
9
).

Proof.

We divide the proof into two parts: first the iteration
n\to n+1
and next the update
n+1\to n+2
. We then apply induction on the number of intermediate stages
k
to each iteration.

First time-step
t^{n}\to t^{n+1}

k=1
:
Equating terms associated with
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
in the momentum update (
37
), we get,

a_{1,1}\left(\nabla\rho^{1}_{(0)}-\frac{\rho^{1}_{(0)}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}\right)=0\quad(\mbox{type-A}\implies a_{1,1}\neq 0).

Therefore, the density
\rho^{1}_{(0)}
becomes well-prepared, but since the density at the previous time level
\rho_{(0)}^{n}
is not well-prepared, we don’t have the divergence-free condition for
\mathbf{q}_{(0)}^{1}
.

k>1
: Let us assume that for the stages
k=1,\ldots,s-1
the density is well-prepared. Now, equating like powers of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
terms in the momentum update for the
s^{th}
stage and considering the well-preparedness for
\rho^{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,s-1
we get:

a_{s,s}\left(\nabla\rho^{s}_{(0)}-\frac{\rho^{s}_{(0)}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}\right)=0\quad(\mbox{type-A}\implies a_{s,s}\neq 0).

Since the scheme is GSA we have
\rho^{n+1}_{(0)}=\rho^{s}_{(0)}=\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}
. But, we don’t have the divergence-free condition for
\mathbf{q}^{n+1}
.

Second time-step
t^{n+1}\to t^{n+2}

As obtained for the last iteration, analogously the intermediate densities are well-prepared, i.e.,

\nabla\rho^{k}_{(0)}-\frac{\rho^{k}_{(0)}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}=0,\quad\mbox{for }k=1,\ldots s.

k=1
:
Considering the
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{0})
terms in the mass update (
36
)

\frac{\rho_{(0)}^{1}-\rho_{(0)}^{n+1}}{\Delta t}=-a_{1,1}\nabla\cdot(\rho_{(0)}^{1}\mathbf{u}_{(0)}^{1}).

Since
\rho^{n+1}
is well-prepared
\rho_{(0)}^{1}=\rho_{(0)}^{n+1}
, and
a_{1,1}\neq 0
(type-A), consequently we get

\nabla\cdot(\rho_{(0)}^{1}\mathbf{u}_{(0)}^{1})=0.

k>1
:
Let us assume that for the stages
k=1,\dots,s-1
the following conditions hold true

\rho_{(0)}^{k}=\rho_{\mathrm{eq}},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\nabla\cdot(\rho_{(0)}^{k}\mathbf{u}_{(0)}^{k})=0.

Now, the
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{0})
terms in the mass update (
36
) for
k=s
give:

\frac{\rho_{(0)}^{s}-\rho_{(0)}^{n+1}}{\Delta t}=-a_{s,s}\nabla\cdot(\rho_{(0)}^{s}\mathbf{u}_{(0)}^{s}),\quad\mbox{as}\ \nabla\cdot(\rho_{(0)}^{k}\mathbf{u}_{(0)}^{k})=0,k=1,\ldots s-1.

Using the same arguments as above and the fact that for a type-A scheme
a_{s,s}\neq 0
we get the divergence condition
\nabla\cdot(\rho_{(0)}^{s}\mathbf{u}_{(0)}^{s})=0
.

The above proof fails for type-CK schemes in general as

4
Space-time fully discrete scheme and its analysis

To obtain an implementable numerical scheme the spatial derivatives of the higher-order time semi-discrete scheme (Definition
15
) need to be approximated. As understood from the scheme presented in the Introduction (Section
1
), much caution needs to be exercised to maintain the hydrostatic balance (
5
) also for non-zero
\varepsilon
. In this section we present a second-order well-balanced FV discretization of the AP time semi-discrete scheme (Definition

).

To this end we consider the following 1D form of the Euler equations (
1
)–(
2

\partial_{t}U+\partial_{x_{1}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
F_{2}(U)\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\frac{S(U)}{\varepsilon^{2}}\end{pmatrix}-\partial_{x_{1}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\frac{G_{2}(U)}{\varepsilon^{2}}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\frac{\tilde{S}(U)}{\varepsilon^{2}}\end{pmatrix}+\partial_{x_{1}}\begin{pmatrix}G_{1}(U)\\
\frac{G_{2}(U)}{\varepsilon^{2}}\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\frac{\tilde{S}(U)}{\varepsilon^{2}}\end{pmatrix}=0,

(45)

\displaystyle U:=(\rho,q)^{T},\ \

\displaystyle F_{2}(U):=\frac{q^{2}}{\rho}+\frac{p}{\varepsilon^{2}},\ S(U):=\rho\partial_{x_{1}}\phi,\ \tilde{S}(U):=\frac{\rho}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\partial_{x_{1}}\rho_{\mathrm{eq}},

\displaystyle G_{1}(U):=q\mbox{ and }\ G_{2}(U):=\rho.

Note that due to the addition and subtraction of the linear balance to the momentum two different types of sources are required to be discretized:

1.

S(U)
: termed the gravitational source, and

2.

\tilde{S}(U)
: termed the penalization source.

Here, we specify the scheme in 1D; it can be extended dimension by dimension to higher dimensions. Let
\Omega
be the closed interval
[a,b]
, discretized via Cartesian mesh cells of lengths
\Delta x_{1}
in
x_{1}
-direction.
For notational convenience, we introduce the finite difference operators
\delta
and
\mu

\delta_{x_{1}}\omega_{i}=\omega_{i+\frac{1}{2}}-\omega_{i-\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\mu_{x_{1}}\omega_{i}=(\omega_{i+\frac{1}{2}}+\omega_{i-\frac{1}{2}})/2.

(46)

4.1
Gravitational source term discretization

Following the approach introduced in
[
11
]
, we first rewrite the gravitational source term
S(U)
in (
45

\rho\partial_{x_{1}}\phi=-pe^{-H(x_{1})}\partial_{x}e^{H(x_{1})},\;\;\mbox{where}\;H(x_{1})=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\log\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\left(\beta-\phi(x_{1})\right)\right).

(47)

Here,
\beta=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\gamma-1}+\phi
.
As a consequence of the above formulation, the gravitational source term on a finite volume cell

is approximated as

S(U_{i})\approx\mathcal{S}_{i}:=-p_{i}e^{-H_{i}}\left(\frac{e^{H_{i+1/2}}-e^{H_{i-1/2}}}{\Delta x_{1}}\right),\ \ \mbox{where}\ \ H_{i+1/2}=H(x_{1,i+1/2}),

(48)

and

Lemma 23
.

The source term discretization (

\rho_{i}\partial_{x}\phi(x_{i})

Proof.

The expression in (
48

\frac{(\gamma-1)}{\gamma}p_{i}e^{-H_{i}}\left(\frac{e^{H_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}-e^{H_{i-\frac{1}{2}}}}{\Delta x_{1}}\right)=\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}p_{i}^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\left(\frac{(\beta-\phi_{i+\frac{1}{2}})^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}-(\beta-\phi_{i-\frac{1}{2}})^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}{\Delta x_{1}}\right),

(49)

where
\phi_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=\phi(x_{i+\frac{1}{2}})
. Now, plugging in the Taylor series expansion around

for each of the terms in the above expression, we get

\frac{(\beta-\phi_{i+\frac{1}{2}})^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}-(\beta-\phi_{i-\frac{1}{2}})^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}{\Delta x_{1}}=-\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}(\beta-\phi_{i})^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\partial_{x_{1}}\phi_{i}+\mathcal{O}(\Delta x_{1}^{2}).

Substituting the above expression in (

-\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}p_{i}e^{-H_{i}}\left(\frac{e^{H_{i+\frac{1}{2}}}-e^{H_{i-\frac{1}{2}}}}{\Delta x_{1}}\right)=\rho_{i}\partial_{x}\phi_{i}+\mathcal{O}(\Delta x_{1}^{2}).

x_{1}
-direction are

\alpha^{\ell}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}:=2\max\left(\left|q_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{\ell,-}\big/\rho_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{\ell,-}\right|,\left|q_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{\ell,+}\big/\rho_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{\ell,+}\right|\right).

(60)

The momentum flux
F_{2}
is approximated by a Rusanov-type scheme whereas the mass flux is approximated using central differences.
The eigenvalues of the Jacobian of the flux which is approximated by a Rusanov-type flux are
\lambda_{1}=0
,
\lambda_{2}=2\frac{q}{\rho},
and the CFL condition at time
t^{n}
is given by

\Delta t\max_{i}\left(\left|\lambda_{2,i}\right|\big/\Delta x_{1}\right)=\nu,

(61)

where
\nu<1
is the given CFL number; see
[
19
]
for details about the choice of the CFL conditions.

Remark 26
.

The reconstruction is first carried out for the hydrostatic variable
pe^{-H}
and is then transformed to obtain the reconstruction of
\rho
; see Section
4.2
.

4.4
Analysis of the fully-discrete scheme

Lemma 27
.

The discretization of the source term to gradient pair
\partial_{x_{1}}(\frac{(q^{\ell})^{2}}{\rho^{\ell}}+\frac{p^{\ell}}{\varepsilon^{2}})+\rho^{\ell}\frac{\partial_{x_{1}}\phi}{\varepsilon^{2}}
in Definition
25
is well-balanced, i.e., if
(\rho^{\ell}_{i},u^{\ell}_{i})=(\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i},0)
, then the discretization
\nu_{x_{1}}\delta_{x_{1}}\mathcal{F}^{\ell}_{2,i}+\Delta t\mathcal{S}^{\ell}_{i}/\varepsilon^{2}=0
.

Proof.

Consider the discretization of the momentum flux
\partial_{x_{1}}(\frac{q^{2}}{\rho}+\frac{p}{\varepsilon^{2}})
. First, note that the reconstruction preserves the equilibrium state achieved by the source gradient pair
\partial_{x_{1}}p^{n}+\rho^{n}\partial_{x_{1}}\phi
.
Since
p_{i}e^{-H_{i}}=\text{const.}
,
the reconstruction of the pressure term on the left can be written as

\left(\omega^{L}_{i+1/2}\right)_{1}=p_{i}e^{-H_{i}},

(62)

as the diffusion term in the flux
\alpha_{2,i+\frac{1}{2}}(q_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n,+}-q_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n,-})=0
.
Note that this holds for any linear reconstruction. The pressure is therefore

p_{i+1/2}^{-}=e^{H_{i+1/2}}p_{i}e^{-H_{i}}=p_{i}e^{H_{i+1/2}-H_{i}}=p_{i+1/2}.

(63)

The same applies for the right interface and for
i-1/2
, thus we end up with

\frac{1}{\Delta x_{1}}\left(\frac{p^{+}_{i+1/2}+p^{-}_{i+1/2}}{2}-\frac{p^{+}_{i-1/2}+p^{-}_{i-1/2}}{2}\right)=\frac{p_{i+1/2}-p_{i-1/2}}{\Delta x_{1}}.

(64)

For the second term, i.e., the discretization of the source term, we notice that

-p_{i}e^{-H_{i}}\left(\frac{e^{H_{i+1/2}}-e^{H_{i-1/2}}}{\Delta x_{1}}\right)=\frac{p_{i+1/2}-p_{i-1/2}}{\Delta x_{1}}.

(65)

Thus, the discrete source gradient pair is well-balanced.

Lemma 28
.

The discretization of the source term-gradient pair
\partial_{x_{1}}\rho^{\ell}-\frac{\rho^{\ell}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}\partial_{x_{1}}\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}
in Definition
25
is well-balanced, i.e., if
(\rho^{\ell}_{i},u^{\ell}_{i})=(\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i},0)
then the discretization
\nu_{x_{1}}\delta_{x_{1}}\mathcal{G}_{2,i}^{\ell}-\Delta t\tilde{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\ell}}=0
.

Proof.

From (
58
) we have the term
\partial_{x_{1}}\rho^{\ell}
is discretized as:

\nu_{x_{1}}\delta_{x_{1}}\mathcal{G}_{2,i}^{\ell}=\nu_{x_{1}}\frac{(\rho^{\ell}_{i+1}+\rho^{\ell}_{i})-(\rho^{\ell}_{i}+\rho^{\ell}_{i-1})}{2}=\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x_{1}}\frac{(\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i+1}-\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i-1})}{2}.

(66)

Now, looking at
\Delta t\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{\ell}_{i}
from (
59
) we get

\displaystyle\Delta t\mathcal{\tilde{S}}_{i}^{\ell}=\Delta t\frac{\rho^{\ell}_{i}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i}}\left(\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i+1}-\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i-1}}{2\Delta x_{1}}\right)=\Delta t\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i+1}-\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i-1}}{2\Delta x_{1}}.

(67)

Combining (
66
) and (
67
) we get

\nu_{x_{1}}\delta_{x_{1}}\mathcal{G}_{2,i}^{\ell}-\Delta t\mathcal{\tilde{S}}_{i}^{\ell}=0
.

Lemma 29
.

The discrete elliptic problem (
56
) for initial data in equilibrium, i.e.,
(\rho^{n}_{i},u^{n}_{i})=(\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i},0)
, and for Dirichlet (
\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}
), periodic, or Neumann extrapolation boundary conditions, has the unique solution
\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i}
.

Proof.

We prove the result for the first stage, i.e.,
k=1
. For the other stages, the result follows by induction and well-balancedness of the flux-source discretizations.

Since
u^{n}_{i}=0
and
\rho^{n}_{i}=\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i}
, Lemmas
27
and
28
yield
\hat{q}^{1}_{i}=0
. Hence, the elliptic problem (
56
) becomes

\displaystyle-\frac{\nu_{x_{1}}^{2}a_{1,1}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\delta_{x_{1}}\left(\delta_{x_{1}}\rho_{i}^{1}\right)+\frac{\nu_{x_{1}}\Delta ta_{1,1}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\delta_{x_{1}}\tilde{\mathcal{S}}^{1}_{i}+\rho^{1}_{i}=\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle-\frac{\nu_{x_{1}}^{2}a_{1,1}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\delta_{x_{1}}\left(\delta_{x_{1}}\rho_{i}^{1}\right)+\frac{\nu_{x_{1}}^{2}a_{1,1}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\delta_{x_{1}}\left(\frac{\rho_{i}^{1}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i}}\delta_{x_{1}}\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i}\right)+\rho^{1}_{i}=\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i}.

If
\rho^{1}_{i}=\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i}
, then

-\frac{\nu_{x_{1}}^{2}a_{1,1}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\delta_{x_{1}}\left(\delta_{x_{1}}\rho_{i}^{1}\right)+\frac{\nu_{x_{1}}^{2}a_{1,1}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\delta_{x_{1}}\left(\frac{\rho_{i}^{1}}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i}}\delta_{x_{1}}\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i}\right)=0.

Thus,
\rho^{1}_{i}=\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i}
solves the linear system above.
Imposing boundary conditions yields the uniqueness of the solution.

Proposition 30
.

The fully-discrete numerical scheme in Definition
25
is well-balanced, i.e., if
(\rho^{n}_{i},u^{n}_{i})=(\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i},0)\implies(\rho^{n+1}_{i},u^{n+1}_{i})=(\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i},0)
.

Proof.

Lemmas
27
and
28
ensure that the flux and source term discretizations maintain the steady state at every intermediate stage, i.e.,

u^{k}_{i}=0
for
k=1,\ldots s
.
Moreover, the uniqueness of
\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i}
as a solution to the linear elliptic problem (
56
) ensures that

\rho^{k}_{i}=\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i}
, for
k=1,\ldots,s
.
Since we consider GSA IMEX-RK schemes we have

\rho^{n+1}_{i}=\rho^{s}_{i}=\rho_{\mathrm{eq},i}
and
u^{n+1}_{i}=0
.

6
Conclusions

In this paper we have designed, analyzed, and tested a higher-order asymptotic-preserving well-balanced scheme for the anelastic limit of the isentropic Euler equations with gravity (
1
)–(
2
).
The design leverages the derivation of an equivalent steady-state flux-source pair (
17
) to the non-linear pair (
5
).
This allows us to penalize the Euler system (
1
)–(
2
) with a linear flux-source pair leading to the first-order time semi-discrete AP scheme.
The simple structure of the first-order time semi-discrete AP scheme enables usage of the additive IMEX-RK framework
[
34
,
30
]
to obtain high-order time semi-discretizations.
As demonstrated in the introduction, the AP property is not enough for a scheme to maintain accuracy in the low Mach regime for balance laws such as the Euler system considered here.
The combination of a balance-preserving reconstruction, judicious source-term discretization and a finite-volume approach using Rusanov and central fluxes leads to an AP well-balanced fully-discrete scheme.
Numerical case studies confirm the uniform performance of the designed scheme with respect to the Mach number.

This novel approach of penalizing the governing equations using a linear equilibrium to obtain a linearly implicit scheme serves as a stepping stone to design linearly implicit additive IMEX-RK schemes for more general equations of state in the low Mach number regime.
In this case, for example, if potential temperature variations are to be considered, it will be crucial to understand how to obtain a linear, balance- and asymptotic-preserving relation.
Indeed, this is a work in progress.
