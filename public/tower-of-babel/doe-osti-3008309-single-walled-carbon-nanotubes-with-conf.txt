Executive Summary
The goal of this project is to synthesize and characterize a new non-metal electro-catalyst for
oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) for fuel cell applications. The intended catalyst is a composite
material composed of sulfur chains encapsulated in narrow diameter single-walled carbon
nanotubes (S@SWNTs). S@SWNTs were successfully synthesized through sulfur vapor infusion
method, and validated with Raman spectroscopy. However, the electrochemical analysis on the
ORR catalytic activity of S@SWNTs indicated that it had low ORR catalytic activity. Our
theoretical study based on density functional theory (DFT) revealed that the poor oxygen
adsorption (low binding energy) on the surface of S@SWNTs was the bottleneck of the entire
catalytic reaction.
The focus of the project was subsequently pivoted to the development of a new non-noble metal
ORR catalyst that could provide durability in acidic electrolyte. The hypothesis was to encapsulate
small iron (Fe) clusters in SWNTs (Fe@SWNTs) can provide ORR electro-catalytic activity and
long durability in acidic environment. DFT-based computational studies were carried out to
explore the feasibility of the Fe@SWNTs catalyst. The theoretical study indicated that
Fe@SWNTs indeed could catalyze the ORR with lower theoretical overpotential than platinum
(Pt). However, its weaker bonding energy with oxygen was the bottleneck of the overall reaction.
On the other hand, Fe clusters (composed of 7 Fe atoms) encapsulated in nitrogen-doped SWNTs
(Fe7@N4WSNTs) showed proper oxygen adsorption by the Fe cluster and low theoretical
overpotential of ORR. Comparing to the Fe single atom catalyst on N-doped SWNTs (Fe-
N4SWNTs), which is one the best non-noble metal ORR catalysts reported in the literature, the
Fe7@N4WSNTs showed lower overpotential and better resistance to acidic environment. Fe
encapsulated N-doped SWNTs were synthesized with ferrocene as the Fe precursors through vapor
infusion method, and experimental validate is underway.
This study theoretically demonstrated the feasibility of a new type of non-noble metal electro-
catalyst for ORR that could have high catalytic activity and long durability.

Background:
Electrochemical oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) is the cathodic reaction in proton-exchange
membrane fuel cells (PEMFCs). ORR requires catalysts to improve the reaction kinetics to reduce
the ORR overpotential, thus achieving high operation voltage. The state-of-the-art ORR catalysts
are based on Platinum (Pt) due to Pt’s high electrocatalytic activity and its excellent durability in
the acidic environment at the cathode of PEMFCs. The use of Pt catalysts significantly increases
the cost of PEMFCs thus preventing the wide deployment of these technologies. Platinum group
metals (PGM)-free ORR catalysts for PEMFCs has been under development for the last two
decades. The PGM-free ORR catalysts that demonstrate the highest catalytic activities can be
categorized as metal-nitrogen-carbon (M-N-C) complex composed of transition metal (iron, cobalt,
and manganese) coordinated with nitrogen atoms doped in a carbon framework. [1] To date, the
most active M-N-C catalysts are iron (Fe) coordinated by N (FeN4) dispersed in a carbon
framework developed by Wu and coworkers. [2] However, the most significant challenge for this
type of M-N-C catalysts is their inferior long-term stability under practical PEMFC operating
conditions compared to PGM catalysts. The research focus on M-N-C catalysts has recently shifted
from improving activity to improving stability by investigating the mechanism of degradation. It
is the current understanding that the degradation of catalytic activity of M-N-C complex is mainly
caused by two reasons: (1) During the PEMFC operation, carbon is inevitably oxidized by oxygen
(O2) and/or hydrogen peroxide (H2O2, a byproduct from incomplete ORR), particularly carbon
with low graphitic structure (i.e., carbon atoms connected to the nitrogen coordinating the metal
center). [3,4] As a result of carbon oxidization, metal centers are not coordinated by nitrogen atoms
anymore and agglomerate. (2) Demetallization is another main cause of degradation of M-N-C
catalysts. Recently studies reveal that the fast decrease of initial high catalytic activity of Fe-N-C
catalyst is due to the transformation from FeN4 sites to Fe oxides during the long-term ORR
process. [5]
Tremendous efforts have been devoted to increase the stability of the M-N-C catalysts, but the
structure of the N-coordinated metal centers is their intrinsic disadvantage. In this project, we
explored classes of new non-PGM catalysts composed of elements including sulfur (S), selenium
(Se), and iron (Fe) encapsulated in single-walled carbon nanotubes. The nanometer size tubular
structure of SWNTs provides an ideal enclosure to encapsulate a variety of atomic or molecular
substances Over past two decades, considerable efforts in this field were devoted to the
encapsulation of fullerenes, [6-9] organic molecular dopants, [10] metal and metal oxide catalytic
nanoparticles. [11,12] Elements including tellurium, phosphorus, and iodine have also been
encapsulated in SWNTs. [13-15] Synthesis and characterization of these materials span a challenging
broad range of physiochemical parameters, all further influenced by external factors including
various confinement environments, diffusion processes, which remain poorly understood.
Project Objectives:
The original goal of this project was to synthesize and characterize the proposed S@SWNTs and
Se@SWNTs ORR catalysts, understand their fundamental electronic structures and the
relationship to their catalytic activity, and evaluate the ORR activity and durability in MEAs for
PEMFCs. The objectives were (1) Synthesis of the proposed S@SWNTs and Se@SWNT
materials constructed with SWNTs with the narrow diameters ranging between 0.8 and 1.1 nm. (2)
Fundamental understanding of the relationship between electronic structure and catalytic activity
using spectroscopic, microscopic, and theoretical studies. (3) Investigating the catalytic properties
of S@SWNTs and Se@SWNTs via electrochemical analysis combined with spectroscopic

We studied the ORR catalytic activity of S@SWNTs with Rotating Disk Electrode (RDE)
experiments with optimized S@SWNTs electrode preparation. In a typical electrode preparation
process, 7.6 mg of the S@SWNTs powder or commercial Pt/C catalyst powder (20 wt.% of Pt,
Premetek) was mixed in 7.6 mL of water with 7.6 mg of sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS), 2.4 mL of
ethanol, and 40 µL of 5 wt.% Nafion suspension (Ion Power). Then, high power sonication was
conducted for a total of 20 minutes of sonication time (1s pulse 4s rest). 10 µL of the final
dispersion was drop-coated on a spinning glassy carbon RDE at 400 RPM followed by drying in
vacuum oven at 80°C overnight. Figure 5 displays the linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) curves of
these two catalysts on RDE in 0.5 M H2SO4 at 10mV/s at different RPM with Ag/AgCl reference
electrode.

It is clear that the overpotential of S@SWNTs was high compared to the Pt/C electrode prepared
with the same process and same mass loading. The experimental high overpotential is contradictive
to the result from our computational study. Therefore, we studied the effect of the impurity in the
SWNTs sample on the ORR catalytic activity. The results from energy dispersive spectroscopy
(EDS) combined with scanning electron microscopy clearly indicated that there was a considerable
amount of Fe in the S@SWNTs sample: the mass content of Fe from EDS analysis is
approximately 6.9 wt.%, which is consistent with the elemental analysis with inductively coupled

 7

plasma optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES). It is clear that the Fe content is the residue after
the SWNTs purification process. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern of the S@SWNTs sample
(Figure 6a) revealed that the Fe content in the S@SWNTs was iron disulfide (FeS2), which was
formed from the sulfurization of Fe during the sulfur vapor infusion process. FeS2 has been known
to have electrocatalytic activity towards ORR. To understand the effect of the FeS2 impurity on
the catalytic activity of S@SWNTs, we purchased super-pure SWNTs (SPSWNTs), in which the
Fe impurity was removed by the vendor (NanoIntegris). Both the EDS and ICP-OES analyses
indicated that the Fe content in the SPSWNTs was only about 1.3 wt.%. S@SPSWNTs was
synthesized with the same sulfur vapor infusion process. LSV measurement on RDE was
performed to compare S@SWNTs and S@SPSWNTs as the catalysts, and pristine SPSWNTs was
used as the baseline. Figure 6b shows the corresponding LSV curves. It is clear that the
S@SWNTs (with FeS2 impurity) demonstrated some ORR catalytic activity consistent with what
we previously observed. On the other hand, the S@SPSWNTs shows no ORR catalytic activity,
which is consistent with the pure SPSWNTs sample. Therefore, we conclude that the previously
demonstrated ORR catalytic activity of S@SWNTs is from the FeS2 impurity.
Our theoretical study also revealed the reason for the inconsistency between the calculated and
experimental measured ORR catalytic activity of S@SWNTs: In the calculation displayed in
Figure 4 and Table 2, the affinity of O2 and S@SWNTs, i.e., adsorption energy of O2, was not
considered. The adsorption of O2 on S@SWNT was calculated: Figure 7 shows our DFT
calculation on O2 adsorption on S@SWNTs (7,7). Our calculation shows negligible electron
transfer from O2 to S@SWNTs that indicates the bottleneck of the S@SWNTs catalyzed ORR is
the poor adsorption of oxygen.

Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions:
In conclusion, we investigated the effect of an encapsulated Fe7 cluster on the ORR catalytic
activity of pristine and N-doped SWNTs with different chiralities. The optimized structure of
Fe7@SWNT shows that the Fe7 cluster maintains its D5h structure inside the carbon nanotube,
while in Fe7@N4SWNT, the Fe7 cluster dissociates into a Fe6 cluster and a single Fe atom, the
latter of which forms the Fe-N4 moiety similar to Fe-N4 SAC. Due to the lack of positively charged
adsorption sites, Fe7@SWNTs has an unfavored O2 adsorption, the prerequisite step of ORR.
Fe7@N4SWNTs, on the other hand, have strong O2 adsorption on the N-coordinated Fe atom,
comparable to that of the Fe-N4SWNT. The incorporation of Fe7 leads to electron accumulation

 11

on both pristine and N-doped nanotube surfaces, which destabilizes the ORR intermediates.
Overall, moderate intermediate adsorption and high O2 affinity caused by Fe7 cluster incorporation
collectively lead to the enhanced ORR catalytic activity of Fe7@N4SWNTs. Lastly, the
encapsulation of a Fe7 cluster significantly improved the conductivity of the material, especially
the (13,0) Fe7@N4SWNT, which reverts the semiconducting Fe-N4SWNT to metallic via the
presence of 6 additional Fe atoms. Our strategy leverages the advantages of N doping, Fe
coordination modulation, and transition metal clusters to provide a solution to the utilization of
non-precious metal catalysts with high catalytic activity and stability. Based on our findings, future
work may explore other metal or alloy clusters, alternative defect structures beyond pyridinic N₄
for cluster anchoring, and an experimental assessment of the stability of partially exposed metal
clusters under acidic conditions.
Path Forward:
We will synthesize Fe clusters encapsulated N-doped SWNTs and experimentally characterize
their ORR catalytic activity. We will also synthesize and characterize molybdenum cluster N-
doped SWNTs ORR catalysts as an alternative.
