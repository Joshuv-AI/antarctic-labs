Prediction of college student psychological state based on deep learning framework combining the improved Whale Optimization Algorithm and LSTM
Authors: Xiaohan Sun, Hanhui Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In intelligent education, accurate prediction of college students’ psychological states is crucial for teaching quality improvement and mental health interventions. Psychological state data with temporal dependency and nonlinearity limits traditional machine learning, while Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) is sensitive to hyperparameters and prone to premature convergence. To solve these problems, this paper proposes a CNWOA-LSTM framework combining an improved Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) with LSTM: chaotic initialization is introduced to enhance population diversity and global search ability, and a niche operator is added to alleviate premature convergence; the improved WOA optimizes key LSTM hyperparameters (hidden units, learning rate, dropout rate, batch size). Experiments are conducted on a public kaggle dataset of college students’ behavioral features, learning interaction data and psychological assessment indicators. CNWOA-LSTM is compared with traditional machine learning models (SVM, Random Forest, GBDT), classical deep learning models (CNN, Transformer, GRU, BiLSTM), and meta-heuristic optimized LSTM models (DE-LSTM, HHO-LSTM, MPA-LSTM, AOA-LSTM, RSA-LSTM). Results show CNWOA-LSTM achieves 93.64% accuracy, 9.33% and 6.63% higher than original LSTM and standard WOA-LSTM respectively, and 5.21%–7.34% higher than other meta-heuristic optimized LSTM models, verifying its effectiveness for psychological state prediction.

Introduction

In recent years, deep learning has emerged as a powerful tool in the field of psychological state prediction for college students, bringing revolutionary changes to the accurate perception and dynamic monitoring of students’ mental conditions. Unlike traditional shallow machine learning methods, deep learning algorithms—such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)1, and attention-based models—possess strong capabilities in feature automatic extraction and complex temporal dependency modeling2–5. These advantages enable them to effectively process multi-modal data related to college students’ psychological states, including behavioral logs, physiological signals, and textual expressions, thereby overcoming the limitations of traditional assessment methods such as subjectivity and low temporal resolution. By capturing subtle changes in students’ emotional, cognitive, and motivational states in real time, deep learning not only improves the accuracy and timeliness of psychological state prediction but also provides scientific support for the formulation of personalized and precision-oriented educational guidance strategies, laying a solid technical foundation for the digital transformation of Ideological and Political Education (IPE) in colleges and universities6,7.

With the rapid advancement of information technology, higher education is undergoing a profound transformation toward digitalization and intelligence. In this process, Ideological and Political Education (IPE) has been increasingly emphasized as a fundamental approach to cultivating students’ values, moral awareness, and social responsibility8–11. As an essential component of classroom ecology, students’ psychological states play a decisive role in shaping learning outcomes. Psychological engagement, emotional responses, and cognitive acceptance not only reflect students’ learning effectiveness but also influence the internalization of ideological and political concepts12–14. Therefore, accurately perceiving and predicting students’ psychological states has become a crucial prerequisite for implementing student-centered and precision-oriented IPE teaching15–18.

However, the psychological states of college students in classroom environments are characterized by strong dynamics, nonlinearity, and concealment. Traditional assessment approaches, such as post-class questionnaires or occasional teacher observations, are often limited by subjectivity and temporal delay, making it difficult to capture real-time emotional fluctuations during instruction19–22. To overcome these shortcomings, data-driven methods based on artificial intelligence have been increasingly introduced into educational research. By leveraging students’ behavioral logs, learning interactions, and physiological signals, intelligent models can uncover latent psychological patterns that are difficult to observe directly23–26.

Among various deep learning models, the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network has demonstrated remarkable performance in modeling sequential and time-dependent data. Its gating mechanism effectively alleviates the vanishing gradient problem and enables the capture of long-term dependencies in time-series educational data, such as learning trajectories and emotional evolution27–30. Consequently, LSTM has been widely applied to emotion recognition, engagement analysis, and psychological state prediction in educational contexts. Nevertheless, the predictive performance of LSTM is highly dependent on the configuration of its hyperparameters, including the number of hidden units, learning rate, dropout rate, and batch size31–34. Improper parameter settings may lead to slow convergence, overfitting, or suboptimal prediction accuracy.

To address the challenge of hyperparameter optimization, meta-heuristic algorithms have attracted considerable attention due to their gradient-free nature and strong global search capability. As a classic sequential deep learning model, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) has outstanding advantages in mining time-series psychological data features and is widely adopted for temporal state prediction tasks. Algorithms such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO), and Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) have been successfully applied to optimize neural network structures and training parameters35–38. Among them, WOA, inspired by the bubble-net hunting strategy of humpback whales, exhibits a simple structure and efficient convergence behavior39–42. In recent years, numerous improved variants of WOA have been proposed to optimize its search mechanism and solve practical optimization bottlenecks43,44. However, when dealing with high-dimensional and complex optimization problems such as LSTM hyperparameter tuning, the standard WOA still suffers from inherent limitations, including insufficient population diversity and premature convergence in later iterations45–48. These issues restrict its ability to explore the global optimum effectively.

Motivated by these observations, this paper proposes a novel hybrid framework named CNWOA-LSTM, which integrates an improved Whale Optimization Algorithm with an LSTM network for college students’ psychological state prediction in IPE classrooms. Specifically, two enhancement strategies are introduced into the standard WOA. First, a chaotic initialization operator is employed to replace random population initialization. By exploiting the ergodicity and randomness of chaotic mapping, the initial whale population is more uniformly distributed in the search space, thereby strengthening global exploration capability in the early optimization stage49–52. Second, a niche operator is incorporated to preserve population diversity during evolution. By dividing individuals into multiple subpopulations based on fitness similarity, the niche mechanism effectively suppresses domination by elite individuals and mitigates premature convergence, enabling the algorithm to explore multiple promising regions simultaneously53–56. The improved CNWOA is then used to automatically optimize key LSTM hyperparameters, leading to enhanced predictive performance and robustness57–66.

In order to comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed method, a publicly available IPE classroom psychological state dataset67 was used, including behavioral observations, learning interaction records, and physiological response characteristics. As shown in 1, the proposed CNWOA-LSTM model is systematically compared with traditional machine learning models (SVM, RF, and GBDT), representative deep learning models (CNN, Transformer, GRU, and BiLSTM), and LSTM models optimized by other advanced meta-heuristic algorithms, such as DE, HHO, MPA, AOA, and RSA. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method consistently outperforms baseline models in terms of Accuracy, F1-score, and AUC, highlighting its effectiveness and practical value for intelligent psychological state perception in IPE teaching scenarios.

Psychological state analysis in IPE

In Ideological and Political Education (IPE), students’ psychological states are widely regarded as an essential indicator of learning effectiveness and value acceptance. Early studies primarily relied on traditional assessment methods, including psychological self-report scales (e.g., SCL-90) and qualitative classroom observations conducted by instructors68,69. Although these approaches provide intuitive insights into students’ mental conditions, they are inherently constrained by subjectivity, delayed feedback, and limited temporal resolution, which makes them unsuitable for capturing rapid psychological fluctuations during classroom instruction19.

With the development of educational data mining and learning analytics, researchers have gradually shifted toward quantitative and intelligent approaches. Machine learning techniques such as Support Vector Machines (SVM) and Random Forests (RF) have been applied to classify students’ emotional or psychological states based on behavioral and physiological features8,70. While these methods improve objectivity compared with traditional assessments, they generally rely on handcrafted features and static assumptions, limiting their ability to model complex temporal dependencies in IPE classroom environments.

Deep learning for sequential psychological modeling

To overcome the limitations of shallow learning models, deep learning methods have been increasingly adopted for analyzing sequential educational data. Among them, the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network has demonstrated significant advantages due to its gated recurrent structure, which enables effective modeling of long-term temporal dependencies while alleviating the vanishing gradient problem27,28. LSTM has been widely used in emotion recognition, engagement analysis, and psychological state prediction tasks, particularly in scenarios involving continuous behavioral logs and physiological signals.

Recent studies have further enhanced LSTM-based frameworks by integrating Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) or attention mechanisms to extract spatial–temporal features from multimodal educational data, leading to notable improvements in prediction performance71,72,77. Despite these advances, LSTM-based models remain highly sensitive to hyperparameter configurations, such as the number of hidden units, learning rate, batch size, and dropout rate31. Exhaustive tuning strategies, including grid search and random search, are computationally expensive and often ineffective in high-dimensional parameter spaces78.

Meta-heuristic optimization for LSTM models

Meta-heuristic optimization algorithms have emerged as effective tools for automating hyperparameter tuning in deep learning models. Inspired by natural phenomena, swarm intelligence algorithms such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)79,80, Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO), and Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) have been successfully applied to optimize neural network parameters73–75. Among these methods, WOA has attracted particular attention due to its simple structure, few control parameters, and efficient convergence behavior.

However, existing studies indicate that the standard WOA still suffers from several inherent limitations when applied to complex, high-dimensional optimization problems. Random population initialization may lead to uneven coverage of the search space, while population diversity tends to decrease rapidly in later iterations, increasing the risk of premature convergence49,81. These issues restrict the algorithm’s ability to consistently identify high-quality solutions when optimizing LSTM hyperparameters.

To address these challenges, various enhancement strategies have been proposed. Chaotic mapping techniques have been widely adopted to improve population initialization by exploiting their ergodic and stochastic properties, thereby enhancing global exploration capability76. In addition, niche-based mechanisms have been introduced to preserve population diversity by encouraging parallel exploration of multiple promising regions in the solution space, effectively mitigating premature convergence82. Although these strategies have shown effectiveness in other optimization contexts, their application to WOA-based LSTM optimization for psychological state prediction in IPE remains limited.

Work motivation

The motivation of this study stems from the need to develop a robust and intelligent framework that bridges advanced deep learning techniques with the practical requirements of Ideological and Political Education. Although LSTM networks are well suited for modeling the temporal dynamics of students’ psychological states, their performance in real-world IPE classrooms is hindered by the difficulty of hyperparameter optimization and limited robustness across different teaching contexts.

Moreover, while the Whale Optimization Algorithm provides a promising meta-heuristic optimization tool, its standard form is constrained by random initialization and insufficient population diversity during evolution. These limitations motivate the development of an enhanced optimization strategy that can better balance global exploration and local exploitation.

To this end, this paper proposes a novel CNWOA-LSTM framework, which integrates an improved Whale Optimization Algorithm with an LSTM network for psychological state prediction. Two targeted enhancement mechanisms are introduced: (1) Chaotic Initialization Operator, which replaces random initialization to generate a uniformly distributed initial population and improve early-stage global search efficiency; (2) Niche Operator, which dynamically maintains population diversity by partitioning individuals into subpopulations based on fitness similarity, thereby alleviating premature convergence.

By combining these algorithmic improvements with the temporal modeling capability of LSTM, the proposed CNWOA-LSTM framework aims to provide an accurate, robust, and automated solution for predicting college students’ psychological states, supporting intelligent and data-driven decision-making in IPE classrooms.

It is shaped by external triggers (e.g., IPE teaching content, ambient environment) and internal factors (e.g., cognitive load, physical condition), requiring a time-series-based modeling approach. To accurately assess students’ mood states during IPE activities, we frame this problem as a supervised sequence classification task.

Chaotic Niche Whale Optimization Algorithm (CNWOA)

Although LSTM networks possess strong temporal modeling capabilities, their predictive performance is highly sensitive to hyperparameter settings, such as the number of hidden units, learning rate, dropout ratio, and batch size. To automatically identify optimal hyperparameter configurations, this study proposes an improved Whale Optimization Algorithm, termed Chaotic Niche Whale Optimization Algorithm (CNWOA).

Standard Whale Optimization Algorithm

The Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) is a population-based meta-heuristic inspired by the bubble-net hunting behavior of humpback whales. Each whale represents a candidate solution (position vector) \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{X}_i = [X_{i,1}, \ldots , X_{i,d}]$$\end{document} in a d-dimensional search space. Let \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{X}^*(t)$$\end{document} denote the best solution found up to iteration t. WOA alternates between exploitation (encircling prey and spiral bubble-net attack) and exploration (random search), controlled by adaptive coefficients.

CNWOA: Chaotic initialization and Niche-based diversity preservation

Although standard WOA is effective, it may suffer from insufficient initial diversity (due to random initialization) and premature convergence in later iterations. To mitigate these issues, we propose CNWOA by incorporating: (i) chaotic initialization to enhance early global exploration, and (ii) a niche operator to maintain diversity throughout the search process.

(i) Chaotic initialization. We adopt the Logistic chaotic map to generate an ergodic sequence:12\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} z_{k+1}=\mu z_k(1-z_k), \quad \mu =4, \end{aligned}$$\end{document}and map it to the search bounds:13\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} X_{i,j}(0)=lb_j + z_{i,j}(ub_j-lb_j). \end{aligned}$$\end{document}

Parameter sensitivity analysis of CNWOA

To address concerns about reproducibility and verify the robustness of the proposed CNWOA, we conducted a systematic sensitivity analysis on its core control parameters. We tested the performance of CNWOA-LSTM under different parameter configurations within reasonable ranges, with the results showing that the model maintains stable performance across a wide range of parameter settings. Specifically: (1) For population size N, values between 30 and 70 yield similar prediction accuracy (fluctuation < 0.8%), with N=50 achieving the optimal balance between search capability and computational efficiency; (2) Maximum iterations \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_{max}=100$$\end{document} is sufficient for CNWOA to converge stably, and further increasing iterations does not bring significant performance improvement; (3) Niche radius \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma _{share}$$\end{document} has a moderate impact on performance, with values between 0.3 and 0.7 maintaining high accuracy, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma _{share}=0.5$$\end{document} achieving the best balance between population diversity preservation and convergence speed; (4) The spiral constant b has minimal impact on the optimization results, and the standard value b=1 is sufficient for this task. Overall, the performance fluctuation of CNWOA-LSTM is less than 1.2% across all tested parameter configurations, demonstrating that the parameter settings adopted in this study are robust and the experimental results are highly reproducible.

Dataset description

To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed CNWOA-LSTM framework, experiments were conducted on a public psychological state dataset67 for validation. This dataset is specifically constructed for ideological and political education (IPE) scenarios, containing 1000 student samples. It integrates multi-source information closely related to students’ in-class states, including physiological responses (e.g., HRV, GSR, EEG power bands), a single environmental variable (ambient noise), behavioral records (focus duration), demographic attributes (age, gender, educational level, study major), task metadata (IPE activity types), and psychological context (cognitive load). This multi-dimensional composition supports comprehensive and fine-grained modeling of students’ mood states during IPE sessions.

There are a total of 17 columns in this dataset that are valid input features. The target variable is the Mood State label, which is categorized into four classes based on actual annotations in the dataset: Happy, Neutral, Sad, and Anxious. These categories accurately reflect typical emotional responses in IPE classrooms—for example, Happy corresponds to positive engagement during group discussions, Neutral to stable attention during theoretical lectures, Anxious to tension during in-class exams, and Sad to low mood when facing difficult learning tasks.

Raw multimodal classroom data inevitably contain noise, scale inconsistencies, and heterogeneous formats. To ensure model robustness and stable convergence, a standardized preprocessing pipeline was employed, including data cleaning, feature transformation, temporal sequence construction, and rigorous model regularization and validation strategies tailored to the characteristics of psychological datasets.

All records were first examined for missing or abnormal values. Due to the controlled acquisition process of the public dataset, no large-scale data imputation was required. Complex composite features were decomposed into independent variables when necessary. Dataset partitioning

The generated sequence samples were stratified and split into training, validation, and testing sets with a ratio of 70:10:20. Stratified sampling ensures consistent class distribution across subsets, thereby preventing performance bias caused by class imbalance.

Performance evaluation metrics

To comprehensively evaluate the multi-class prediction performance of the proposed CNWOA-LSTM framework, we employ four widely used classification metrics: Accuracy (Acc), Precision (Pre), Recall (Rec), and F1-score (F1). In addition, the Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC) is reported to assess the overall discrimination capability. In the IPE context, a reliable model should not only achieve high overall accuracy but also maintain a balanced trade-off between false alarms and missed detections when distinguishing psychologically at-risk states (e.g., Anxious, Stressed, Depressed) from Normal patterns. The metric formulations are defined as follows:18\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} Accuracy = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + TN + FP + FN}, \end{aligned}$$\end{document}19\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} Precision = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}, \quad Recall = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}, \end{aligned}$$\end{document}20\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} F1\text {-}score = 2 \times \frac{Precision \times Recall}{Precision + Recall}, \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where TP, TN, FP, and FN denote true positives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives, respectively. For the multi-class setting, we adopt a macro-averaging strategy, i.e., metrics are first computed per class and then averaged across all classes, ensuring that minority psychological states are not overwhelmed by majority-class dominance.

Compared with standard hyperparameter optimization methods, CNWOA-LSTM outperforms grid search, random search, and Bayesian optimization by 8.44%, 7.89%, and 7.09% respectively in terms of accuracy. Grid search suffers from exponential computational complexity with the increase of hyperparameter dimensions, making it impractical for high-dimensional LSTM hyperparameter optimization. Random search improves efficiency but lacks directional guidance, often requiring a large number of iterations to find satisfactory solutions. Bayesian optimization performs better than the above two methods but tends to converge prematurely to local optima in complex non-convex search spaces, which is common in deep learning hyperparameter tuning tasks.

Furthermore, when compared against classic meta-heuristic optimization algorithms including PSO and GA, CNWOA-LSTM still maintains a significant performance advantage (6.94% and 7.19% higher in accuracy respectively). PSO is prone to premature convergence due to its rapid information sharing mechanism, while GA has slow convergence speed and high computational cost in high-dimensional spaces. In contrast, the proposed CNWOA combines the advantages of chaotic initialization and niche diversity preservation, achieving a better balance between global exploration and local exploitation.

Compared with deep learning baselines, CNWOA-LSTM surpasses Transformer and BiLSTM by a large margin. This observation suggests that in small-to-medium scale educational datasets, where data collection is constrained by privacy and deployment cost, simply adopting highly complex architectures may not yield proportional performance benefits. In contrast, an LSTM model equipped with an effective hyperparameter optimizer can better exploit the temporal dependencies and multimodal correlations that characterize students’ psychological dynamics in IPE classrooms.

Furthermore, when compared against other state-of-the-art meta-heuristic optimized LSTMs (DE/HHO/MPA/AOA/RSA), CNWOA-LSTM maintains a clear advantage (5.21%–7.34% higher in accuracy), confirming the superiority of the proposed optimization strategy. This improvement is attributed to the synergy of (i) chaotic initialization, which provides a high-quality and diverse starting population, and (ii) niche-based diversity preservation, which prevents the optimization process from collapsing prematurely around local optima in later stages.

Training loss convergence comparison over 100 epochs. All models were trained under identical experimental conditions with the same early stopping mechanism.

The proposed CNWOA-LSTM exhibits the fastest and smoothest convergence trend among all compared models, reaching a substantially lower loss region early in training and maintaining excellent stability throughout the entire training process. Quantitatively, CNWOA-LSTM achieves convergence (defined as the point where the training loss stops decreasing significantly) at approximately 35 epochs, which is nearly 25 epochs earlier than the original LSTM (converges at 60 epochs) and 10-15 epochs earlier than other meta-heuristic optimized LSTM variants (converge at 45-55 epochs).

The original LSTM and CNN converge more slowly and stabilize at relatively higher loss values, reflecting suboptimal learning dynamics under default hyperparameter configurations. Their loss curves show a gradual decline without obvious inflection points, indicating that the models are struggling to find effective parameter updates in the high-dimensional search space. Meta-heuristic optimized models such as RSA-LSTM and DE-LSTM accelerate convergence compared to the original LSTM, but their curves show mild to moderate oscillations in the middle and late training stages (with loss fluctuations of approximately 0.02-0.03). These oscillations suggest that these optimization algorithms occasionally get trapped in local optima or experience detours in the search process, leading to unstable training dynamics.

In contrast, CNWOA-LSTM yields a smooth and rapid loss reduction with minimal fluctuations after convergence (loss fluctuation < 0.005). This exceptional convergence stability can be directly attributed to the synergistic effect of the two core improvement mechanisms in CNWOA: 1. Chaotic initialization provides a uniformly distributed initial population across the entire search space, avoiding the optimization process starting from poor local regions and ensuring a high-quality starting point for hyperparameter search. 2. Niche-based diversity preservation maintains population diversity throughout the entire iteration process, effectively preventing the algorithm from converging prematurely to local optima and ensuring continuous exploration of better hyperparameter configurations.

These results clearly demonstrate that the integration of CNWOA with LSTM not only does not lead to unstable convergence behavior but actually significantly improves both the convergence speed and stability compared to standard LSTM and other meta-heuristic optimized LSTM frameworks. The smooth and rapid convergence trajectory indicates that CNWOA provides high-quality hyperparameter configurations that optimize the entire training process, making the proposed framework more reliable and efficient for practical deployment in IPE classroom scenarios.

Discussion

The experimental results consistently demonstrate that CNWOA-LSTM provides a robust and effective solution for multi-class psychological state prediction in IPE classrooms. The superiority of the proposed framework is mainly attributed to the synergy between (i) LSTM’s capability to capture temporal dependencies in multimodal classroom signals and (ii) CNWOA’s enhanced global optimization behavior for hyperparameter tuning. Compared with complex architectures such as Transformers, the optimized LSTM shows stronger data efficiency and lower computational overhead, which is more compatible with real-world educational environments where data availability and training resources are often limited.

Moreover, ablation and convergence analyses confirm that chaotic initialization and niche-based diversity preservation play complementary roles: chaotic mapping improves the exploration quality at the beginning, while the niche operator mitigates premature convergence and sustains exploration in later iterations. These properties enable CNWOA to search the hyperparameter landscape more effectively and to generate configurations that improve both prediction accuracy and training stability. In practical IPE applications, such reliability is crucial for supporting timely pedagogical interventions and early-warning mechanisms, ultimately contributing to student-centered intelligent teaching and mental health support.

From the perspective of practical educational application, this research also possesses prominent application value and practical significance in multiple scenarios. Firstly, this prediction framework can realize the early detection of college students’ potential psychological problems, timely identify negative emotions, learning burnout and abnormal psychological tendencies hidden in daily learning, so as to achieve early intervention and early guidance. Secondly, the established prediction model can be seamlessly integrated into the existing smart campus management systems, realizing real-time collection, intelligent analysis and unified management of students’ classroom state data, and promoting the intelligent upgrading of campus education management. Thirdly, the quantitative prediction results can provide intuitive, scientific and reliable data-driven decision support for school psychological counselors and front-line educators, helping them formulate targeted psychological counseling plans and personalized teaching strategies, and further improve the overall quality of campus mental health education and ideological and political education.

Regarding the generalization capability across different student populations and datasets, we acknowledge that this is an important aspect requiring further discussion. Although the proposed framework achieves stable and excellent performance on the adopted public dataset, students from different grades, majors, regional backgrounds and educational institutions may exhibit distinct psychological characteristics, emotional expression patterns and behavioral habits, which may lead to certain performance fluctuations when the model is directly migrated. Nevertheless, the comprehensive regularization strategies (dropout, L2 weight decay, early stopping) and 5-fold stratified cross-validation adopted in this study have effectively enhanced the model’s generalization ability to a certain extent. In future research, we will collect multi-source heterogeneous data from multiple universities and different student groups to conduct cross-dataset and cross-population validation, and further optimize the model through transfer learning techniques to improve its adaptability to diverse educational scenarios.

Challenges and limitations

Although the proposed CNWOA-LSTM framework achieves satisfactory performance in student psychological state prediction within classroom IPE scenarios, this research still faces several inevitable challenges and inherent limitations in practical popularization and large-scale application. First of all, there exist prominent data privacy risks and ethical constraints in actual deployment. The student behavioral data, emotional features and psychological state information adopted in this study all belong to highly sensitive personal educational and mental health data. In the process of data collection, transmission, storage and model training, improper data management may easily lead to personal privacy leakage. Meanwhile, relevant educational ethics norms and informed consent mechanisms need to be strictly followed when acquiring student-related psychological information, which brings certain restrictions to data acquisition and model practical deployment. Secondly, the proposed hybrid model still suffers from insufficient model interpretability. The integrated LSTM network combined with meta-heuristic optimization belongs to a typical black-box intelligent prediction architecture. It can output accurate psychological state prediction results, but cannot clearly present the internal logical reasoning process and key feature contribution basis of judgment results. The lack of transparent interpretation mechanism restricts the further recognition and promotion of the model in formal educational psychological counseling work. Thirdly, the model exhibits limited generalization ability across diverse student populations. The experimental data in this paper are mainly collected from specific student groups with fixed educational backgrounds. There are obvious differences in learning habits, emotional expression modes and psychological characteristics among students of different ages, majors, educational backgrounds and regional environments. The current model cannot well adapt to heterogeneous student groups, and it is difficult to maintain stable and reliable prediction performance when directly migrated to different educational environments and student crowds.

Conclusion

In this study, a novel psychological state prediction framework for Ideological and Political Education (IPE) scenarios, termed CNWOA-LSTM, was proposed. The framework integrates a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network with an improved Whale Optimization Algorithm enhanced by chaotic initialization and a niche-based diversity preservation mechanism. By addressing the sensitivity of LSTM performance to hyperparameter settings and the premature convergence issue of conventional meta-heuristic algorithms, the proposed method provides an effective solution for modeling complex and dynamic psychological state sequences in educational environments.

Extensive experiments were conducted on a public dataset consisting of behavioral features, learning interaction data, and psychological assessment indicators. Comparative evaluations against traditional machine learning models, classical deep learning architectures, and LSTM variants optimized by recent meta-heuristic algorithms demonstrate that CNWOA-LSTM consistently achieves superior performance in terms of Accuracy, F1-score, and AUC. In particular, the proposed framework attains an accuracy of 93.64%, significantly outperforming the original LSTM, standard WOA-LSTM, and other state-of-the-art optimization-based baselines. Convergence analysis and ablation studies further verify that chaotic initialization enhances global exploration in the early optimization stage, while the niche operator effectively maintains population diversity and suppresses premature convergence during later iterations.

From a practical perspective, the proposed CNWOA-LSTM framework exhibits favorable computational efficiency and robustness, making it suitable for real-world deployment in intelligent IPE classrooms. By enabling accurate and timely perception of students’ psychological states, the model can provide meaningful decision support for instructors, facilitating adaptive teaching strategies and targeted educational interventions. Beyond the IPE domain, the proposed optimization framework is general and can be extended to other time-series prediction tasks that require reliable hyperparameter tuning under complex and multimodal data conditions.

Future work will focus on several directions. First, incorporating richer multimodal signals, such as fine-grained facial expression dynamics and speech prosody features, may further enhance psychological state representation. Second, online and incremental learning mechanisms will be explored to enable continuous model adaptation as new classroom data become available. Finally, integrating explainable artificial intelligence techniques will be investigated to improve the interpretability of prediction results, thereby increasing user trust and facilitating ethical deployment in educational settings.
