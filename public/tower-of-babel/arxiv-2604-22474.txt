A ∞ A_{\infty} -invariance of oscillatory norms, and Schatten characterisations of commutators Thanks: The author was supported by the Research Council of Finland, projects 364208 and 371637.
Tuomas Hytönen

Abstract
. Schatten class properties of commutators [ b , T ] [b,T] of pointwise multipliers b b and singular integral operators T T have been characterised in a variety of settings. An abstract framework, covering many of these results as special cases, was proposed by the author [arXiv:2411.02613]. However, recent results about commutators of the concrete Bessel–Riesz transforms by Fan–Li–Sukochev–Zanin [arXiv:2411.14928] are beyond this abstract setting. In this work, we present an extension of the framework of [arXiv:2411.02613], introducing two measures μ \mu and ν \nu that are A ∞ A_{\infty} -equivalent to each other. The commutators act on a given space L 2 ​ ( μ ) L^{2}(\mu) , but the characterising function space norms of the multiplier b b are taken with respect to another measure ν \nu . In this way, assumptions like Ahlfors regularity and Poincaré inequality on the original measure μ \mu may be relaxed, as long as there is an A ∞ A_{\infty} -equivalent measure ν \nu that satisfies these assumptions. In the Bessel example, the original μ \mu fails to be Ahlfors regular, but ν \nu is simply the Lebesgue measure. Within this framework, the Schatten norm characterisations of commutators of the Bessel–Riesz transforms at the critical-index by Fan–Li–Sukochev–Zanin [op cit.] are recovered by a completely different argument, replacing non-commutative techniques by real-variable harmonic analysis and hardly using any specifics of the Bessel setting. As a by-product, we also obtain a simpler characterisation in the non-critical case, replacing an ad hoc Besov space of Fan–Lacey–Li–Xiong [J. Funct. Anal. 2026] by a classical Besov space.

Introduction

Different mapping properties of commutators

[b,T]:f\mapsto bT(f)-T(bf)

of pointwise multipliers
b

play a role in various areas of mathematics, like factorisation of function spaces
[
2
]
and compensated compactness in nonlinear PDE
[
1
]
. Specifically, questions of quantitative compactness of
[b,T]
, measured in terms of Schatten–Lorentz

norms (Definition

), arise in connection with the quantised calculus of Connes
[
4
, Chapter 4]
and are particularly relevant for the critical parameters
(p,q)=(d,\infty)
, where
d
is an underlying dimension
[
5
,
11
,
12
,
25
]
.

Over the past few years, classical characterisations
[
18
,
27
]
of commutator Schatten properties have been extended to various new settings like weighted Euclidean spaces
[
13
]
,
Heisenberg groups
[
7
,
9
]
, more general Carnot groups
[
24
]
,
and the so-called Bessel setting
[

]
. Besides its intrinsic interest, the last-mentioned case serves as a useful model for a general theory, featuring a concrete example of a doubling metric measure space with different upper and lower dimensions, thus failing to be Ahlfors regular (see Definitions
2.2
and
2.4
).

A unified framework covering many of these previous results as special cases was provided in
[
15
]
; however, within three weeks (in terms of the original arXiv dates in November 2024), it was challenged by
[
10
]
with a characterisation for the concrete Bessel–Riesz transforms in the important critical-index case, which was not covered by the abstract assumptions of
[
15
]
. The goal of this paper is to place these bounds of
[
10
]
into a further general framework, a natural extension of
[
15
]
. This leads to a new proof of these bounds, quite transparent in our opinion, and offers a new explanation of the underlying phenomenon in a broader context. At the same time, the scope of the bounds of
[
10
]
is significantly expanded from the concrete Bessel setting to a wide class of operators and spaces.

We should note that, besides the uniform bounds of
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
, the same paper also proves related spectral asymptotics in
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
, and these latter remain beyond the scope of our approach. However, to obtain these asymptotics for all relevant functions rather than just test functions, the uniform bounds of
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
are used in the proof of the asymptotics in
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]

[
10

, not only offer an alternative (simpler, in our opinion) approach to this major component of the proof of
[
10

, but they should similarly serve as a step towards the proof of spectral asymptotics in new situations. In prospective new cases where spectral asymptotics for commutators are hoped for, the specifics of the situation may still be needed for the eventual asymptotic considerations, while the uniform non-asymptotic bounds will be available from the general theory presented here.

To discuss our contributions in more detail, we recall that the following norm equivalence (first due to
[
27
]
in the Euclidean context, and recently extended in
[
15
]
) holds in great generality:

\|[b,T]\|_{S^{p,q}(L^{2}(\mu))}\sim\|b\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}(\mu)},

(1.1)

where

is the usual Schatten–Lorentz space of operators on

(Definition

) and

\|f\|_{X}:=\infty

\|f\|_{X}\sim\|g\|_{Y}

\|f\|_{X}\sim\|g\|_{Y}

While the equivalence (

\mu

(p,q)
, an identification of the oscillation norm with a suitable Sobolev-type norm (see Definition
2.6
),

\|b\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{d,\infty}(\mu)}\sim\|b\|_{\dot{M}^{1,d}(\mu)},

was established in
[

,
17
]
for Ahlfors

-regular measures

(Definition

). This Ahlfors regularity fails for the Bessel measure

\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)

\|[b,T]\|_{S^{d,\infty}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1},m_{\lambda}))}\sim\|b\|_{\dot{W}^{1,d}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})},\qquad d=n+1.

Here, the Sobolev space
\dot{W}^{1,d}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})
on the right is the classical one with respect to the unweighted Lebesgue measure, not with respect to the Bessel measure

appearing on the left. The Lebesgue measure on the half-space

is evidently Ahlfors regular; hence, there is still some hidden Ahlfors-regularity in the setting of
[

[

,
17
]

In this paper, we provide an additional ingredient that is needed to bring the Schatten bounds of

]

1.2 Proposition
.

Let
\mu,\nu
be two doubling measures on a metric space
(X,\rho)
such that they satisfy the
A_{\infty}
relation

\mu(E)\leq\varepsilon\mu(B)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\nu(E)\leq(1-\delta)\nu(B)

(1.3)

for some
\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1)

E\subset B
is a measurable subset of a metric ball
B
.

Then for all
p\in(0,\infty)
and
q\in(0,\infty]

3.2
satisfy

\|b\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}(\mu)}\sim\|b\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}(\nu)}

for all measurable functions

, with implied constants independent of

This can be thought of as an extension of a classical

-invariance result of
[
26

\operatorname{BMO}(\mu)

\operatorname{Osc}^{\infty,\infty}(\mu)

1.2
is based on similar ideas, but seems to be new, to our knowledge. But perhaps the main novelty here is using Proposition
1.2
in combination with (
1.1
) and the characterisations of
\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}(\nu)
from
[
15
,
17
]
to obtain the following extension of
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
, where we characterise Schatten norms of the commutator
[b,T]
on
L^{2}(\mu)
in terms of function space norms with another measure
\nu
. The usefulness of this change of measure becomes apparent in the application to the Bessel setting further below.

1.4 Theorem
.

Let
d\in[1,\infty)
, let
(X,\rho)
be a metric space, and suppose that

(i)

\mu
and
\nu
are doubling measures on
(X,\rho)
that satisfy the
A_{\infty}
relation (
1.3
);

(ii)

every ball
B(x,R)
in
X
contains at most
C(R/r)^{d}
points

with
\rho(x_{i},x_{j})\geq r
, uniformly in
x\in X
and
0<r\leq R<\infty
; and

(iii)

T\in\mathscr{L}(L^{2}(\mu))
is a singular integral operator

Tf(x)=\int_{X}K(x,y)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y),\qquad x\notin\operatorname{supp}f,

with non-degenerate standard kernel

of Hölder exponent
\eta>(1-\frac{d}{2})_{+}
(Definition
2.3
), where
x_{+}:=\max\{x,0\}

Then the following conclusions are valid for all functions

:

(0)

If
p\in(d,\infty)
, then

\|[b,T]\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}(\mu))}\sim\|b\|_{\dot{B}^{p}(\nu)}:=\Big(\iint_{X\times X}\frac{|b(x)-b(y)|^{p}}{\nu(B(x,\rho(x,y)))^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(1.5)

(1)

If
p\in(d,\infty)
and
(X,\rho,\nu)
is Ahlfors

-regular, then

\|[b,T]\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}(\mu))}\sim\|b\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,p}(\nu)}:=\Big(\iint_{X\times X}\frac{|b(x)-b(y)|^{p}}{\rho(x,y)^{2d}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(2)

If
d>1

(X,\rho,\nu)
has lower dimension

-Poincaré inequality (Definition

), then

[b,T]\in S^{p}(L^{2}(\mu))\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad b=\text{constant a.e.},\quad\text{for all }p\in(0,d].

(1.6)

(3)

If
d>1
and
(X,\rho,\nu)
is Ahlfors
d
-regular and supports the
(1,d_{-})
-Poincaré inequality (Definition

) for some

\|[b,T]\|_{S^{d,\infty}(L^{2}(\mu))}\sim\|b\|_{\dot{M}^{1,d}(\nu)},

where
\dot{M}^{1,d}(\nu)
is the Hajłasz–Sobolev space (Definition

).

(4)

If
w\in A_{2}(\mu)
(Definition

), then all claims above remain valid with the

space on the left replaced by the weighted space

When

[

(

(

2.
Basic definitions

For the convenience of reference, we collect here some basic definitions that were already used in the Introduction.
While these definitions are needed to understand the statements of some of our results, we note that most of them will be used only indirectly in this paper, in the sense that these notions appear as input or output of some results that we quote from elsewhere, but hardly in any hands-on computations.

2.1 Definition
.

Let
H
is a Hilbert space and
A,F\in\mathscr{L}(H)
be a bounded linear operators on
H
.
We write
\operatorname{rank}F\leq n
if there are elements
e_{k},f_{k}\in H
such that

F(h)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(h,e_{k})f_{k}

for all
h\in H
. The
n
th approximation number of
A
is defined by

a_{n}(A):=\inf\{\|A-F\|:F\in\mathscr{L}(H),\operatorname{rank}F\leq n\},

where
n\in\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
. For
p\in(0,\infty)
and
q\in(0,\infty]
, the Schatten–Lorentz
S^{p,q}(H)
(quasi-)norm and space are defined by

\begin{split}\|A\|_{S^{p,q}(H)}&:=\|(a_{n}(A))_{n=0}^{\infty}\|_{\ell^{p,q}},\\
S^{p,q}(H)&:=\{A\in\mathscr{L}(H):\|A\|_{S^{p,q}(H)}<\infty\}.\end{split}

We abbreviate
S^{p}(H):=S^{p,p}(H)
.

2.2 Definition
.

A metric measure space is a triplet
(X,\rho,\mu)
, where
X
is a set,
\rho
is a metric on
X
, and
\mu
is a Borel measure on
X
. The space is called doubling if

\mu(B(x,2R))\lesssim\mu(B(x,R))

uniformly in
x\in X
and
R>0
.

2.3 Definition
.

A function
K:\{(x,y)\in X\times X:x\neq y\}\to\mathbb{C}
is called a standard kernel of Hölder exponent
\eta\in(0,1]
on
(X,\rho,\mu)
if

|K(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\mu(B(x,\rho(x,y))}

and, whenever
\rho(x,x^{\prime})\ll\rho(x,y)
,

|K(x,y)-K(x^{\prime},y)|+|K(y,x)-K(y,x^{\prime})|\lesssim\frac{1}{\mu(B(x,\rho(x,y))}\Big(\frac{\rho(x,x^{\prime})}{\rho(x,y)}\Big)^{\eta}.

We say that such kernel is non-degenerate if for every
x\in X
and
r(0,\infty)
, there is a point
y\in X
with
\rho(x,y)\sim r
such that

|K(x,y)|+|K(y,x)|\gtrsim\frac{1}{\mu(B(x,\rho(x,y))}.

2.4 Definition
.

For
0\leq d\leq D<\infty
, we say that a metric measure space
(X,\rho,\mu)
has lower dimension
d
and upper dimension
D
if

\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{d}\lesssim\frac{\mu(B(x,R))}{\mu(B(x,r))}\lesssim\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{D}

uniformly for all
x\in X
and all
0<r\leq R\lesssim\operatorname{diam}(X)
.
The space is called Ahlfors
d
-regular, if it has both upper and lower dimension
d
.

2.5 Definition
.

We say that a space
(X,\rho,\mu)
satisfies the
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality if there are constant
\lambda,c_{P}>0
such that, for every Lipschitz function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
and every ball
B(x,R)
in
X
,

\fint_{B(x,R)}|f-\langle f\rangle_{B(x,R)}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq c_{P}\Big(\fint_{B(x,\lambda R)}\operatorname{lip}f(x)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where

\operatorname{lip}f(x):=\liminf_{t\to 0}\sup_{\rho(x,y)\leq t}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{t}.

2.6 Definition
.

For a measurable function
f
on
(X,\rho,\mu)
, we say that a measurable
h:X\to[0,\infty]
is a Hajłasz upper gradient of
f
if

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\rho(x,y)(h(x)+h(y))\qquad\text{for $\mu$-a.e. }x,y\in X.

For
p\in(0,\infty]
, the Hajłasz–Sobolev norm of
f
is

\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mu)}:=\inf\Big\{\|h\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}:h\text{ is a Haj\l{}asz upper gradient of }f\Big\}.

2.7 Definition
.

On a doubling metric measure space
(X,\rho,\mu)
, a measurable function
w:X\to[0,\infty]
is called an
A_{2}(\mu)
weights, denoted by
w\in A_{2}(\mu)
if

[w]_{A_{2}(\mu)}:=\sup_{B}\Big(\fint_{B}w\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)\Big(\fint_{B}\frac{1}{w}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)

is finite, where the supremum is taken over all metric balls
B
in
X
.

3.
Proof of Proposition
1.2
on the
A_{\infty}
invariance

We begin by introducing the oscillatory spaces
\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}(\mu)
featuring in Proposition
1.2
and some variants needed in its proof.

For
r\in(0,\infty)
, a set
E
of finite positive
\mu
-measure and a measurable function
f
, let

\operatorname{osc}_{r,\mu}(f;E):=\inf_{c}\Big(\fint_{E}|f-c|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

Note that this is well defined (but possibly
\infty
) for all measurable
f
. It is finite if and only if
1_{E}f\in L^{r}(\mu)
. If
r\in[1,\infty)
, and
1_{E}f\in L^{r}(\mu)
, then

\operatorname{osc}_{r,\mu}(f;E)\sim\Big(\fint_{E}|f-\langle f\rangle_{E}^{\mu}|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}},\quad\langle f\rangle_{E}^{\mu}:=\fint_{E}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

(3.1)

We fix a system
\mathscr{D}
of dyadic cubes on
(X,\rho)
in the sense of
[
16
]
. For each dyadic cube
Q\in\mathscr{D}
with “centre”
z_{Q}
and “side-length”
\ell(Q)
, let
B_{Q}:=B(z_{Q},c\ell(Q))
be a “concentric” ball with a fixed large expansion factor
c
. Then we define:

3.2 Definition
.

With the notion as above, for all measurable functions
f
and exponents
p,r\in(0,\infty)
and
q\in(0,\infty]
, we define

\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{r}(\mu)}:=\Big\|\Big\{\operatorname{osc}_{r,\mu}(f;B_{Q})\Big\}_{Q\in\mathscr{D}}\Big\|_{\ell^{p,q}}

and

\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}(\mu)}:=\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{1}(\mu)}.

Using (
3.1
), one sees the equivalence of this definition with a slightly different version appearing in
[
15
]
.

We also consider a finite collection of adjacent dyadic systems
\mathscr{D}^{m}
,
m=1,\ldots,M
, with the following additional property; these exist by
[
16
, Theorem 4.1]
:

\forall\ B=B(x,t)\ \exists m\in\{1,\ldots,M\},\ \exists\ Q\in\mathscr{D}^{m}:\quad B\subset Q,\quad\operatorname{diam}(Q)\lesssim r.

(3.3)

For each of these
\mathscr{D}^{m}
, we consider the dyadic oscillation norm

\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{r}(\mathscr{D}^{m},\mu)}:=\Big\|\Big\{\operatorname{osc}_{r,\mu}(f;Q)\Big\}_{Q\in\mathscr{D}^{m}}\Big\|_{\ell^{p,q}}.

3.4 Lemma
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space and
p,r\in(0,\infty)
and
q\in(0,\infty]
.
For all measurable functions
f
we have the following equivalence with implicit constants independent of
f
:

\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{r}(\mu)}\sim\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{r}(\mathscr{D}^{m},\mu)}

Proof.

\lesssim
: For every
Q\in\mathscr{D}
, we apply (
3.3
) to
B_{Q}
to find a
Q^{m}\in\mathscr{D}^{m}
with
B_{Q}\subset Q^{m}
and
\operatorname{diam}(Q^{m})\lesssim\ell(Q)
; hence
\mu(Q^{m})\lesssim\mu(Q)\sim\mu(B_{Q})
.
It follows that
\operatorname{osc}_{r,\mu}(f,B_{Q})\lesssim\operatorname{osc}_{r,\mu}(f,Q^{m})
. For
t^{\prime}\neq t
, let
Q^{t^{\prime}}:=\varnothing
and
\operatorname{osc}_{r,\mu}(f,\varnothing):=0
.

Under the correspondence
Q\mapsto Q^{m}=R
, we claim that each
R\in\mathscr{D}^{m}
may have at most boundedly many preimages. Indeed, these
Q
satisfy
\ell(Q)\sim\ell(R)
, so they belong to boundedly many generations only. Within each fixed generation, these
Q
are pairwise disjoint, contained in
R
, and their measure
\mu(Q)\gtrsim\mu(R)
is at least a fixed fraction of
\mu(R)
. Hence, there can be only boundedly many such
Q
in each generation, and then only boundedly many in the boundedly many generations altogether. It thus follows that

\begin{split}\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{r}(\mu)}&=\Big\|\Big\{\operatorname{osc}_{r,\mu}(f;B_{Q})\Big\}_{Q\in\mathscr{D}}\Big\|_{\ell^{p,q}}\lesssim\sum_{m=1}^{M}\Big\|\Big\{\operatorname{osc}_{r,\mu}(f;Q^{m})\Big\}_{Q\in\mathscr{D}}\Big\|_{\ell^{p,q}}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{m=1}^{M}\Big\|\Big\{\operatorname{osc}_{r,\mu}(f;R)\Big\}_{R\in\mathscr{D}^{m}}\Big\|_{\ell^{p,q}}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{r}(\mathscr{D}^{m},\mu)}.\end{split}

\gtrsim
: It suffices to consider a fixed
m
. For each
R\in\mathscr{D}^{m}
, there is
\hat{R}\in\mathscr{D}
of the same side-length such that
z_{R}\in Q
. Then
R\subset B_{\hat{R}}
when expansion factor defining the latter ball is fixed large enough, but also
\mu(B_{\hat{R}})\lesssim\mu(R)
. Hence
\operatorname{osc}_{r,\mu}(f;R)\lesssim\operatorname{osc}_{r,\mu}(f,B_{\hat{R}})
.

Reasoning as before, under the correspondence
R\mapsto\hat{R}=Q
, each
Q\in\mathscr{D}^{m}
may have at most boundedly many preimages. Hence

\begin{split}\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{r}(\mathscr{D}^{m},\mu)}&=\Big\|\Big\{\operatorname{osc}_{r,\mu}(f;R)\Big\}_{R\in\mathscr{D}^{m}}\Big\|_{\ell^{p,q}}\lesssim\Big\|\Big\{\operatorname{osc}_{r,\mu}(f;B_{\hat{R}})\Big\}_{R\in\mathscr{D}^{m}}\Big\|_{\ell^{p,q}}\\
&\lesssim\Big\|\Big\{\operatorname{osc}_{r,\mu}(f;B_{Q})\Big\}_{Q\in\mathscr{D}}\Big\|_{\ell^{p,q}}=\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{r}(\mu)}.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

3.5 Lemma
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space and
p,r,s\in(0,\infty)
and
q\in(0,\infty]
.
For all measurable functions
f
we have the following equivalence with implicit constants independent of
f
:

\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{r}(\mathscr{D}^{m},\mu)}\sim\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{s}(\mathscr{D}^{m},\mu)}

and

\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{r}(\mu)}\sim\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{s}(\mu)}.

Proof.

The first claim is
[
15
, Proposition 11.3]
. The second one follows by combining the first claim with Lemma
3.4
.
∎

In the Euclidean space, there are many equivalent versions of the
A_{\infty}
condition (
1.3
). Not all of these equivalences remain valid in greater generality (see
[
20
]
), but the following result is enough for our purposes:

3.6 Proposition

(
[
20
]
, Corollary 4.19)
.

Let
\mu,\nu
be doubling measures on a metric space
(X,\rho)
. Then
\mu,\nu
satisfy the
A_{\infty}
relation if and only if
\,\mathrm{d}\nu=w\,\mathrm{d}\mu
, where the density
w
satisfies the following reverse Hölder inequality for some
t>1
and all metric balls
B
:

\Big(\fint_{B}w^{t}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{t}}\lesssim\fint_{B}w\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\frac{\nu(B)}{\mu(B)}.

Equipped with these tools, we are ready to give:

Proof of Proposition
1.2
.

Let
p,r\in(0,\infty)
and
q\in(0,\infty]
.
Let
B
be an arbitrary metric ball, and let
w
and
t>1
be as in Proposition
3.6
. Then

\begin{split}\operatorname{osc}_{r,\nu}(f;B)&=\inf_{c}\Big(\frac{1}{\nu(B)}\int_{B}|f-c|^{r}w\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
&=\inf_{c}\Big(\frac{\mu(B)}{\nu(B)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\Big(\fint_{B}|f-c|^{r}w\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
&\leq\inf_{c}\Big(\frac{\mu(B)}{\nu(B)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\Big(\fint_{B}|f-c|^{rt^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{rt^{\prime}}}\Big(\fint_{B}w^{t}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{tr}}\\
&\lesssim\inf_{c}\Big(\frac{\mu(B)}{\nu(B)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\Big(\fint_{B}|f-c|^{rt^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{rt^{\prime}}}\Big(\frac{\nu(B)}{\mu(B)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\quad\text{by Proposition \ref{prop:KK}}\\
&=\operatorname{osc}_{rt^{\prime},\mu}(f;B).\end{split}

Using this with
B=B_{Q}
for each
Q\in\mathscr{D}
and taking the
\ell^{p,q}
norm over
\mathscr{D}
, we obtain

\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{r}(\nu)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{rt^{\prime}}(\mu)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{r}(\mu)}

using Lemma
3.5
with
s=rt^{\prime}
in the last step.

By taking complements, it is immediate to see that the assumed
A_{\infty}
relation (
1.3
) is symmetric in
\mu
and
\nu
. Thus we also obtain

\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{r}(\mu)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{r}(\nu)}.

A combination of the two bounds shows that

\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{r}(\mu)}\sim\|f\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}_{r}(\nu)},

and taking
r=1
completes the proof.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.4
on general commutators

The special case
\mu=\nu
of Theorem
1.4
is contained in
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
. The proof of Theorem
1.4
as stated is a combination of Proposition
1.2
and main ingredients of the proof of
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
from
[
15
,
17
]
, as we will now detail.

In the notation of
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
, assumption (
ii
) of Theorem
1.4
says that
\Delta=d
. Combined with assumption (
iii
) of Theorem
1.4
, it follows that

\frac{\eta}{\Delta}+\frac{1}{2}>\frac{1}{d}(1-\frac{d}{2})+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{d},\qquad\Big(\frac{\eta}{\Delta}+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{-1}<d

Hence, the index
p(\eta)
of
[
15
, (1.9)]
satisfies

p(\eta):=\max\Big\{1,\Big(\frac{\eta}{\Delta}+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{-1}\Big\}\quad\begin{cases}<d,&\text{if }d>1,\\
=1,&\text{if }d=1.\end{cases}

(4.1)

For all
p\in(p(\eta),\infty)\subset(1,\infty)
and
q\in[1,\infty]
, we obtain

\begin{split}\|[b,T]\|_{S^{p,q}(L^{2}(\mu))}&\sim\|b\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}(\mu)}\quad\text{by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Hyt:Sp}{}{}, Proposition 1.26]}}\\
&\sim\|b\|_{\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}(\nu)}\quad\text{by Proposition \ref{prop:Osc(mu)=Osc(nu)}}.\\
&\sim\|b\|_{\dot{B}^{p}(\nu)}\quad\text{by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Hyt:Sp}{}{}, Proposition 11.5]}, if }p=q.\end{split}

(4.2)

(
0
): By (
4.1
), we have
p(\eta)\leq d
for all
d\in[1,\infty)
.
Hence, we can apply (
4.2
) with
p=q\in(d,\infty)\subseteq(p(\eta),\infty)
to conclude that

\|[b,T]\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}(\mu))}\sim\|b\|_{\dot{B}^{p}(\nu)}.

(
1
):
If
\nu
is Ahlfors
d
-regular, it is immediate that

\|b\|_{\dot{B}^{p}(\nu)}\sim\|b\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,p}(\nu)}.

(
2
): The direction “
\Leftarrow
” is obvious. Turning to “
\Rightarrow
”, if
[b,T]\in S^{p}(L^{2}(\mu))
for some

, then
[b,T]\in S^{d}(L^{2}(\mu))
by the standard containments between the
S^{p}
spaces. Noting that we now assume

, it follows from (

) that

, and hence

\|[b,T]\|_{S^{d}(L^{2}(\mu)}\sim\|b\|_{\dot{B}^{d}(\nu)}\quad\text{by \eqref{eq:Spq-Osc-pq} with }p=q=d\in(p(\eta),\infty).

Under the assumptions of lower dimension

and
(1,d)
-Poincaré inequality made in part (

),

, Theorem 1.5(2)]
says that

(

): We recall from

where

5.
Proof of Corollary
1.8
in the Bessel setting

The proof consists of simply verifying the assumptions of the general Theorem
1.4
in the special case under consideration. Let
d:=n+1\in[1,\infty)
and
(X,\rho):=(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1},|x-y|)
.

(
i
): The Lebesgue measure
\,\mathrm{d}\nu:=\,\mathrm{d}x
is evidently doubling, and it is easy to check this for the Bessel measure
\,\mathrm{d}\mu:=dm_{\lambda}:=x_{n+1}^{2\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}x
as well. Also, for the power weight
w=x_{n+1}^{2\lambda}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
, one easily checks that
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})
as soon as
p>2\lambda+1
. In particular, we have
w\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})
, and one of the many equivalent characterisations of this is (
1.3
).

(
ii
): It is easy and well known that the Euclidean half-space
(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1},|x-y|)
satisfies this geometric property with
d=n+1
.

(
iii
): The Bessel–Riesz transforms are standard kernels of (the best possible) Hölder exponent
\eta=1
on
(X,\rho,\mu)=(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1},|x-y|,m_{\lambda})
; this follows from
[
8
, Lemma 2.5]
. The non-degeneracy of these kernels is verified in
[
8
, Lemma 2.6]
. Note that
(1-\frac{d}{2})_{+}=(1-\frac{n+1}{2})_{+}\in\{0,\frac{1}{2}\}
, where both values are strictly less than
\eta=1
, as required.

Since
\,\mathrm{d}m_{\lambda}=x_{n+1}^{2\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}x
and the weight
x_{n+1}^{2\lambda}
is locally bounded, it follows that
b\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})\subset L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(m_{\lambda})=L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mu)
. Thus all common assumptions of Theorem
1.4
are verified, and we proceed to the assumptions in the different cases. Note that these case-wise assumptions only involve the easy space
(X,\rho,\nu)=(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1},|x-y|,\,\mathrm{d}x)
with the Lebesgue measure.

Depending on the case,
(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1},|x-y|,\,\mathrm{d}x)
should be either Ahlfors
d
-regular or have lower dimension
d
, which is weaker. This regularity is evident for the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
with
d=n+1
. In the last two cases of Theorem
1.4
, we need
d=n+1>1
, which is fine, since we also assume
n\geq 1
in the corresponding cases of Corollary
1.8
. Finally, in these cases where
d=n+1>1
, the space
(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1},|x-y|,\,\mathrm{d}x)
should have either the
(1,d)
-Poincaré inequality or the stronger
(1,d_{-})
-Poincaré inequality for some
d_{-}\in[1,d)
. But it is classical that
(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1},|x-y|,\,\mathrm{d}x)
satisfies even the best possible
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality.

Thus all assumptions of Theorem
1.4
are verified, and hence its conclusions are valid. In cases (
1
) and (
2
), it is immediate that they reduce to the corresponding claims (
1
) and (
2
) of Corollary
1.8
. In case (
3
), it suffices to observe that

\|b\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})}\sim\|b\|_{\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})},\qquad p\in(1,\infty],

by
[
14
, Theorem 1]
and take
p=n+1
, recalling that
n+1>1
under the assumptions of this case. (Strictly speaking,
[
14
, Theorem 1]
is stated for the full space or a bounded domain in place of the half space
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
, but it is noted immediately below that the theorem “can be stated in much more general form”, and indeed the case of a half space easily follows along the same lines.)

This completes the proof of Corollary
1.8
.∎

6.
Final comments

The motivation for this investigation came from
[
10
]
, which showed that the abstract framework of
[
15
]
was not broad enough to capture interesting and relevant results in the concrete Bessel setting. By extending the said framework via the use of two different measures, we have managed to view (
1.9
), originally obtained as
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
, as an instance of general real-variable theory in abstract spaces. Having completed the proof, let us briefly compare our approach with that of
[
10
]
.

In
[
10
]
, two proofs of the upper bound
\lesssim
in (
1.9
) are given. The first approach, given in
[
10
, Sections 3–5]
is based on the following main ingredients:

(a)

the corresponding result for the classical Riesz transforms
R_{k}
from
[
25
]
;

(b)

explicit formulas relating the kernels of
R_{\lambda,j}
and
R_{k}
; and

(c)

recent deep results on Schur multipliers from
[
3
]
to deduce Schatten bounds for the operators from the obtained pointwise relations of their kernels.

The second approach, in
[
10
, Sections 6]
, only applies to
n\geq 2
. It depends on:

(d)

an explicit computation of certain
S^{2}
(Hilbert–Schmidt) norms by the Fourier–Bessel transform and Fredholm’s trace formula;

(e)

abstract Cwikel estimates (generalisations of the classical
[
6
]
) from
[
23
]
to lift the
p=2
bounds to
p\in(2,\infty)
; and

(f)

explicit computations with the Leibniz rule to pass over some derivatives from the Bessel–Riesz transforms
\partial_{j}(-\Delta_{\lambda})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
to the multiplier
f
.

In contrast to these, our approach was built on:

(g)

a characterisation of Schatten bounds of
[b,T]
in terms of the
\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}(\mu)
norms for general singular integrals
T
over abstract spaces
(X,\rho,\mu)
, from
[
15
,
27
]
;

(h)

verification of the kernel bounds of the general theory for
R_{\lambda,j}
, from
[
8
]
;

(i)

identification of
\operatorname{Osc}^{d,\infty}(\nu)
with
\dot{M}^{1,d}(\nu)
for “nice”
\nu
, from
[
15
,
17
]
; and

(j)

coincidence of
\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}(\mu)
and
\operatorname{Osc}^{p,q}(\nu)
for
A_{\infty}
-equivalent measures
\mu,\nu
.

In summary, both approaches of
[
10
]
use some deep tools of non-commutative analysis (either the Schur multiplier theorem of
[
3
]
or the abstract Cwikel estimates of
[
23
]
), and the specifics of the Bessel setting are somewhat present throughout the argument. In contrast, our approach is based on real-variable harmonic analysis and the use of the specifics of the Bessel setting is essentially restricted to showing that this setting fits into the general theory in (
h
).

Of course, proving any results about the Schatten
S^{p,q}
spaces, also known as non-commutative
\ell^{p,q}
spaces, non-commutative analysis cannot be completely avoided, and indeed some results about the
S^{p,q}
spaces like interpolation and duality are hidden in (
g
). Nevertheless, these are arguably lighter (and certainly more classical) aspects of the non-commutative theory than the tools used in
[
10
]
. Besides the new results of this work, providing a common extension of
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
, we think that our approach makes the existing
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
more approachable to harmonic analysts without a non-commutative background.
