Enhancing complaint locations prediction with image-space embedding representations and customized large language models
Authors: Theng-Jia Law, Choo-Yee Ting, Hu Ng, Hui-Ngo Goh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Predicting where public complaints are likely to occur is essential for proactive urban management, yet remains challenging due to the heterogeneous structured nature of urban data. Although large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong performance in complaint analytics, existing approaches rely on transforming structured data into text, limiting their ability to capture spatial relationships and cross-feature interactions. To address this limitation, this paper proposed an image-space embedding framework that transformed structured data into multi-channel, image-like representations and integrated them with customized LLMs for complaint location prediction. 48,103 complaints were collected from Seberang Perai, Malaysia with complaint information, points of interest, weather conditions, population density, and socioeconomic indicator. These features are embedded into a unified image-space representation and the existing LLMs are customized by replacing their token embedding layers with convolutional neural network architecture for direct operation in image space. The framework is compared against existing text-based LLM pipelines under both partial and full fine-tuning. Experimental results demonstrated that combining LaBSE embeddings with customized CerebrasGPT-590M, achieved the highest accuracy (74.2%), Cohen’s kappa (71.8%), and F1-score (65.6%) while requiring significantly less training time (19.531 minutes) than fully fine-tuned baseline LLMs. Furthermore, strong performance gains obtained with customized vision-based models confirmed the effectiveness of the proposed image-space representations. Overall, the proposed framework provides an efficient solution for complaint location prediction and advances the integration of structured data with LLMs in image space.

Introduction

Public complaints are formal reports submitted by residents to government or municipal agencies to express dissatisfaction or request services1–7. Complaints related to infrastructure, public safety, environmental conditions, healthcare, and administrative processes often signal underlying problems within urban systems6–9. Predicting where complaints are likely to occur next is therefore essential for proactive urban management, rather than a purely technical task. It allows authorities to move from reactive responses to preventive actions, including targeted inspections, timely maintenance, and more effective allocation of public resources10,11. This capability is especially critical in dense urban areas, where delayed responses can lead to wider disruptions, increased public dissatisfaction, and greater impact on vulnerable communities5,7,12. However, complaint location prediction remains difficult due to the complex interaction of many factors, such as historical complaint patterns, nearby points of interest, weather conditions, socioeconomic differences, population density, and temporal variation13–23. These challenges highlight the need for predictive frameworks that can integrate diverse structured data while preserving spatial relationships and contextual dependencies.

Large language models (LLMs) have recently shown strong generalization performance in public complaint prediction tasks. They are effective at capturing complex patterns, modeling long-range dependencies, and transferring knowledge across related problems2,3,24–41. Their success with large and heterogeneous datasets has motivated efforts to extend their use beyond unstructured text. However, most existing applications in complaint analytics focus on textual complaint descriptions or represent structured data as simple text sequences. This limits the ability of LLMs to capture the spatial structure and relational information that are inherent in urban tabular data.

To address these challenges, the main contribution of this work is introducing an embedding-based image-space transformation framework combined with a customized LLM for complaint location prediction. In this work, the image-space transformation refers to a structured and grid-based embedding representation in which non-visual and heterogeneous urban data are embedded and arranged into an image-like form. The proposed framework converts structured tabular data into image-like embedding representations that are suitable for spatial learning and LLM adaptation. Structured inputs, including complaint records, points of interest (POI), weather variables, relative wealth indices (RWI), and population density, are mapped into a unified embedding space that preserves spatial proximity, contextual similarity, and feature interactions. Unlike existing row-wise embeddings, this transformation organizes data into a spatially meaningful layout, allowing models to benefit from inductive biases commonly used in image-based learning. Based on the transformed representations, a customized large language model is designed to perform predictive analytics directly in image space. Instead of using LLMs as generic sequence models, the architecture and input representations are modified to capture spatial and temporal patterns as well as heterogeneous dependencies in urban complaint data. This design enables LLMs to act as powerful spatial reasoning models while maintaining strong generalization performance. The proposed framework is applied to the task of predicting complaint locations, with the goal of identifying where complaints are most likely to occur next across space and time.

Finally, Section "Conclusion" concludes this work and discusses limitations and directions for future research.

Literature review

Recent advances in complaint management increasingly leverage large language models (LLMs) to analyze large-scale complaint data and support proactive, early-stage interventions2,3,24–41. Evidence suggests that LLMs are already embedded in real-world complaint processes; for example, approximately 18% of financial consumer complaints were found to involve LLM assistance by late 202424. In parallel, operational systems using LLMs for complaint analysis have emerged. An LLM-driven complaint management platform for the electric utility sector demonstrates the feasibility of using LLMs to classify, prioritize, and route complaints with minimal human intervention25. These systems highlight the ability of modern LLMs to process unstructured complaint text at scale while maintaining efficiency and effectiveness. In healthcare settings, LLM-generated responses to patient complaints were rated as significantly more appropriate and empathetic than responses written by hospital staff26, underscoring the potential of LLMs to improve service quality and patient satisfaction. Collectively, these studies establish LLMs as practical and effective components of contemporary complaint workflows, often outperforming existing rule-based or non-LLM ML approaches25,26.

Beyond system-level deployment, substantial attention has been given to the use of LLMs for complaint classification tasks2,3,27–41. A notable example is a zero-shot LLM framework for civic grievance systems that routes multimodal complaints into semantic categories, estimates urgency using sentiment cues, and flags abusive submissions through toxicity detection27. In a complementary study, a comprehensive evaluation of 14 LLMs is conducted on zero-shot classification of financial consumer complaints from the consumer financial protection bureau (CFPB) complaints2, reporting that many models achieved accuracy exceeding 70% without task-specific fine-tuning. Similarly, Koh et al.28 applied LLMs to classify primary-care patient complaints using a standardized healthcare taxonomy. Together, these findings suggest that pretrained LLMs possess strong general knowledge for distinguishing nuanced complaint types, although fine-tuning or domain adaptation is often required to further improve predictive performance2,27,28.

Moving beyond text-centric approaches, a growing body of research emphasizes the importance of incorporating rich urban contextual data into complaint prediction models. Complaints are increasingly recognized as products of broader urban dynamics rather than isolated reporting behaviors. Points of interest (POIs), including fitness facilities, retail stores, restaurants, and offices, are commonly used as proxies for human activity intensity and functional land use13–18. Empirical studies consistently show that areas with higher POI density or diversity tend to generate more complaints related to noise, traffic, sanitation, and public order, reflecting increased population presence and activity throughout the day13–15. Incorporating POI features into predictive models has been shown to reveal latent relationships between urban functions and complaint patterns that are not captured by complaint histories alone13,14.

Environmental and weather conditions also play a significant role in shaping complaint occurrence, particularly for infrastructure, environmental, and public health issues. A global analysis integrating long-term temperature trends with demographic data found that cities experiencing greater warming exhibited the fastest growth in rat sighting complaints19. Similarly, studies of 311 service requests related to flooding have incorporated rainfall and weather-station data using Poisson and random forest models, demonstrating that extreme weather events can trigger sharp increases in infrastructure-related complaints20. Despite these insights, the systematic integration of weather dynamics into complaint prediction models remains relatively limited21.

Population density and socioeconomic context further contribute to the spatial heterogeneity of complaint reporting. Numerous studies report that noise and traffic complaints cluster in densely populated urban districts13,14, while global analyses of rat sightings reveal a strong positive association between population density and complaint trends19. However, this relationship is often non-linear. In New York City, for example, complaint density does not increase proportionally with population or building density, and moderate reductions in density can lead to fewer complaints22. In parallel, socioeconomic indicators such as relative wealth indices (RWI) are increasingly used to account for disparities in reporting behavior and access to public services. Prior work suggests that lower-income communities may experience substantial issues but report fewer complaints due to reduced civic engagement, leading to systematic underestimation of local problems13,22,23.

Despite these advances, several important research gaps remain. While existing studies incorporate POIs, weather variables, population density, or socioeconomic indicators into complaint models, few provide unified frameworks that jointly integrate these features with complaint histories while preserving spatial structure and cross-feature interactions21. Consequently, higher-order dependencies among heterogeneous urban signals remain insufficiently explored. Although tabular-to-image transformation methods have demonstrated strong performance gains by embedding each record into image-like representations42–44, the potential of image-space embedding representation for urban complaint analytics, particularly involving high-dimensional spatial, temporal, environmental, and socioeconomic features, remains underexamined. Moreover, most existing approaches focus on two-dimensional image representations, leaving more flexible N-dimensional embedding spaces largely unexplored.

At the same time, current LLM-based complaint analytics predominantly rely on unstructured text or serialized tabular inputs, which limits their ability to exploit the spatial and structural richness of urban data21. There is limited research on customizing LLM architectures or input representations to directly operate on image-like embeddings derived from structured urban features. As a result, the full potential of LLMs for spatial complaint location prediction has yet to be realized. Addressing these challenges calls for a unified framework that integrates rich urban contextual data, transforms heterogeneous tabular features into expressive image-like embeddings, and adapts LLMs to perform effectively over these representations for accurate and generalizable complaint location prediction.

Method

Figure 1 illustrates the proposed framework for complaint location prediction, which integrates image-space embedding representations of structured urban data with customized LLMs. Motivated by the limitations of text-based LLM pipelines in capturing spatial structure and cross-feature interactions, the proposed method transforms heterogeneous tabular inputs into multi-channel, image-like embeddings that preserve spatial proximity, contextual similarity, and semantic grouping. This representation enables both language-based and vision-based architectures to exploit spatial inductive biases that are absent in conventional serialized text representations.

As shown in Fig. 1, each complaint record is processed through a unified transformation pipeline. Structured feature groups including complaints information, POI, weather information, population density, and RWI are independently embedded using pretrained embedding models, reshaped into balanced two-dimensional matrices, and stacked along a channel dimension. This design preserves intra-group structure while maintaining clear inter-group separation, yielding a compact yet expressive image-space representation suitable for downstream spatial learning. Building on this representation, the framework supports two complementary modeling strategies. First, pretrained LLMs are customized by replacing their original token embedding layers with the proposed convolutional neural network architecture, allowing the models to operate directly in image space before leveraging their contextual modeling capacity for prediction. Second, established vision architectures are applied to the same image-space embeddings with minimal modification. This dual-path design enables a clear assessment of whether performance gains arise from improved representation rather than from model-specific capacity alone. Image-space embedding representations of structured data

This subsection introduces the transformation of structured data embeddings generated by LLMs into image-space representations. Each structured record is first converted into a textual representation by concatenating feature–value pairs, ensuring that heterogeneous features are consistently encoded in a format suitable for LLM processing. The resulting text is then embedded by the LLM into a one-dimensional vector. Given that LLM embeddings are inherently one-dimensional vectors, a dedicated reshaping strategy is subsequently required to convert these vectors into two-dimensional matrices that are compatible with image-based learning architectures, such as convolutional neural networks (CNN), while preserving structural balance.

Embedding models with 768 dimensions are reshaped into 24 × 32 matrices, whereas 1024-dimensional embeddings naturally yield square 32 × 32 matrices. The consistent outcomes across models demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed aspect ratio estimation and enable a standardized conversion of heterogeneous embeddings into image-like forms. Building on this reshaping scheme, the final input to the customized LLMs is organized as a four-dimensional tensor of shape (N, 5, a, b) where N denotes to the total number of complaint records. The channel dimension aggregates five feature groups, as detailed in Table 1: (i) complaint information, including date, district, and first- and second-level complaint categories; (ii) POI variables; (iii) weather variables; (iv) population density variables; and (v) RWI. Each feature group is independently embedded and reshaped into an a × b matrix using the estimated aspect ratio, and subsequently stacked along the channel dimension as illustrated in Fig. 1. This transformation does not assume the existence of natural image-like geometric structures. Instead, the reshaping strategy provides a structured embedding arrangement that facilitates localized activation aggregation and hierarchical interaction learning across semantically grouped feature representations. The proposed organization preserves feature-group separation while enabling convolutional architectures to capture correlated activation patterns and contextual dependencies within the projected embedding space.

Large language model customization and evaluation

This subsection describes the customization of LLMs and the evaluation process used to assess the proposed image-space embedding framework. In this study, the prediction task is defined as identifying the most likely DUN constituency immediately after complaint submission using only the information available at submission time by adapting both language and vision-based architectures. The available inputs consist of complaint metadata, including Date, first-level and second-level complaint categories, and the raw textual address provided by complainants. Although address information is submitted together with each complaint, these addresses are frequently incomplete, inconsistent, ambiguous, abbreviated, or noisy, which reduces the reliability of direct geocoding and administrative routing. Consequently, the proposed framework is designed to infer the most probable complaint region prior to reliable location standardization and validation. The location-derived contextual variables, including POI distributions, weather conditions, population density, and RWI, are not extracted from manually verified ground-truth DUN labels or finalized complaint coordinates. Instead, they are automatically derived from the raw submitted address through an approximate preprocessing and geocoding pipeline that may itself contain uncertainty due to imperfect address quality. The proposed customization is applied to a diverse set of pretrained LLMs with varying parameter scales and architectural designs, including Qwen3-0.6B, CerebrasGPT-590M, Smol2-360M, Pythia-410M, and Bloom-560M. Within the proposed framework, the original embedding layers of these LLMs are replaced by a custom convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture, as illustrated in Fig. 1. The CNN architecture comprises a sequence of two-dimensional convolutional layers followed by batch normalization, rectified linear unit (ReLU) activation, max pooling, and linear layers equipped with layer normalization and Gaussian error linear unit (GeLU) activation. This architecture is specifically designed to capture hierarchical spatial patterns from the image-space embeddings derived from structured data.

Table 4 summarizes the tensor dimensionality flow throughout the customized architectures. The input representation is organized as a five-channel tensor of dimension (N, 5, a, b). For the customized LLM framework, the CNN first extracts hierarchical feature representations and subsequently projects them into a vector representation of dimension (N, 32768). The projected features are then reshaped into transformer-compatible token sequences of dimension (N, 32, D), where 32 denotes the generated sequence length and D corresponds to the hidden dimension of the pretrained transformer backbone. These generated token embeddings are directly forwarded into the pretrained transformer encoder layers without modifying the internal self-attention or feed-forward architectures. Consequently, although the original textual token embedding interface is replaced, the pretrained LLMs continue to contribute through contextual representation learning and long-range dependency modeling over the generated spatial-contextual token sequences. The resulting transformer representations are subsequently passed to a task-specific classification head that performs multiclass prediction over 22 DUNs, as summarized in Table 2.

Beyond LLMs, several pretrained vision models are incorporated to examine the generality and effectiveness of the proposed image-space representations, including ResNet50, EfficientNetV2-M, and ConvNeXt-Large. To accommodate the proposed five-channel image-space embeddings, the original input stems of the pretrained vision models are modified from three-channel inputs to customized five-channel convolutional layers. The modified convolutional stems generate intermediate feature maps of dimension (N, I, a, b), where I denotes the intermediate feature channels, before being processed by the remaining pretrained backbone layers to produce compact feature embeddings of dimension (N, O) where O denotes the output size of the existing final layer of the vision model. Except for the modified input stems, all remaining pretrained layers preserve their original pretrained initialization and are subsequently fine-tuned on the proposed complaint prediction task. The final feature representations are then passed to task-specific classification heads aligned with the 22-class prediction setting. All customized LLMs and vision models are trained under a full fine-tuning setting with all layers unfrozen, enabling end-to-end optimization. This strategy allows both the feature extractors and classification layers to jointly learn representations that are optimally aligned with the structured multi-channel image-space inputs.

To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed framework, its performance is compared against a baseline pipeline in which structured data are serialized into textual representations and directly fed into the same LLM backbones, without applying the image-space embedding transformation. Two baseline training configurations are considered: (i) full fine-tuning except the token embedding layer, in which all model parameters are updated while the token embedding layer remains frozen, and (ii) partial fine-tuning, where only the classification layer is unfrozen while the remaining layers are kept fixed. In addition to the baseline comparisons, the proposed framework is further evaluated through ablation studies and error analysis to investigate the contribution of different feature groups and model architectures to DUN prediction performance. The ablation study systematically removes specific contextual feature groups, including location-derived attributes, POI, weather, population density, and RWI, while preserving the remaining inputs to quantify the contribution of each component. The evaluation further compares three categories of models: (i) machine learning (ML) models, including logistic regression (LR), Gaussian Naive Bayes (GNB), Random Forest (RF), Adaptive Boosting (AdaBoost), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), light gradient boosting machine (LGBM) and categorical boosting (CatBoost) trained directly on structured tabular features without embeddings, (ii) customized LLMs integrated with image-space embeddings, and (iii) vision-based models operating on the same image-space representations. For consistency and fairness, the highest-performing ML model under each feature configuration is reported together with the best-performing customized LLM and vision-based architectures identified during preliminary experiments. Model performance is evaluated using a comprehensive set of metrics, including accuracy, Cohen’s kappa, precision, recall, F1-score, and the area under the curve (AUC). To further assess model effectiveness under class imbalance, an error analysis is conducted using class-wise true positive rate (TPR), true negative rate (TNR), false positive rate (FPR), and false negative rate (FNR). Results and discussions

This section evaluates the proposed image-space embedding framework from both computational and predictive perspectives. Table 7 presents the training cost of the proposed framework, which combines image-space embeddings with customized LLMs and vision models under full fine-tuning. For the customized LLMs, the number of trainable parameters increases moderately compared to the baseline full fine-tuning setting due to the introduction of the convolutional encoder, with parameter counts ranging from approximately 336 million to 564 million depending on the backbone. Despite this increase, the observed training times are dramatically reduced, typically ranging from 12 to 50 minutes across different embedding models and LLM backbones. This substantial reduction indicates that the image-space embedding representation, together with the convolutional front-end, leads to more efficient optimization and faster convergence than directly fine-tuning textual LLM pipelines.

A similar trend is observed for the pretrained vision models. ResNet50 and EfficientNetV2-M exhibit relatively low parameter counts and complete training within a few minutes, while ConvNeXt-Large, with a significantly larger number of parameters, requires longer training time but remains substantially more efficient than fully fine-tuned LLM baselines. Across all embedding models, the training times of vision-based architectures remain consistently low, demonstrating the computational advantages of leveraging CNN backbones for image-space structured data.

Taken together, the results in Tables 6 and 7 highlight a key advantage of the proposed framework. It achieves full fine-tuning with substantially reduced computational cost. By transforming structured data into image-space embeddings and coupling them with customized LLMs or vision models, the framework enables efficient end-to-end training while avoiding the prohibitive training times associated with existing text-based LLM fine-tuning. These findings suggest that image-space representations provide a practical and scalable alternative for modeling high-dimensional structured data in real-world settings.

The vision-based models further demonstrate the generality of the proposed image-space embeddings. ConvNeXt-large consistently achieves the strongest performance among the vision architectures, with accuracy reaching up to 74.5% and high Cohen’s kappa and F1-scores, while ResNet50 and EfficientNetV2-M also deliver competitive results depending on the embedding model. These findings confirm that the image-space representations are not only effective for customized LLMs but also readily transferable to existing convolutional backbones.

In addition to predictive effectiveness, a clear relationship emerges between computational cost and model performance by jointly examining the training time reported in Tables 6 and 7 alongside the predictive results in Tables 8 and 9. Despite introducing a large number of trainable parameters through full fine-tuning and customized embedding layers, the proposed framework consistently requires less overall training time than the baseline LLM pipelines. This counterintuitive outcome can be attributed to the more structured and information-dense image-space representations, which facilitate faster convergence and more stable optimization compared to directly learning from serialized textual representations of structured data.

A more fine-grained analysis of the proposed framework further reveals that the embedding layers account for a larger proportion of the training time than the subsequent convolutional layers. This observation is expected, as the embedding transformation serves as the primary interface between high-dimensional structured inputs and the downstream models, requiring more intensive computation to construct semantically and spatially meaningful representations. In contrast, once the image-space embeddings are formed, the convolutional and classification layers operate on well-organized spatial features, enabling more efficient learning and reducing the marginal cost of additional model capacity. This division of computational effort underscores the central role of the embedding stage in driving both performance gains and training efficiency.

When considering the best-performing configurations, customized LLMs equipped with image-space embeddings emerge as the most effective and computationally balanced solutions compared to baseline LLMs. Across embedding models, combinations such as LaBSE with CerebrasGPT-590M achieved the highest accuracy (74.2%), Cohen’s kappa (71.8%), and F1-scores (65.6%) while maintaining favorable training times (19.531 minutes). These results suggest that the proposed customization enables LLMs to leverage their contextual modeling strengths more effectively when operating on structured data encoded in a spatial format, thereby maximizing performance without incurring prohibitive computational overhead.

Following closely, vision models integrated with the same image-space embeddings also demonstrate strong performance with comparatively lower computational cost. In particular, ConvNeXt-Large consistently delivers the best results among the vision architectures, achieving performance levels comparable to, and in some cases approaching, those of the customized LLMs. This highlights the versatility of the proposed image-space embeddings, as they allow both language-centric and vision-centric models to exploit the same structured representations efficiently. Taken together, these findings confirm that the proposed framework not only improves predictive accuracy but also offers a favorable trade-off between computational cost and performance, making it well-suited for scalable and resource-aware complaint prediction systems.

The proposed configuration, which preserves the original embedding ordering and exploits the two-dimensional image-space representation through CNN-based learning, achieved the best overall performance across all evaluation metrics, including accuracy (74.2%), Cohen’s Kappa (71.8%), precision (69.8%), recall (67.8%), F1-score (65.6%), and AUC (96.3%). Although the improvement over the flattened MLP representation appears moderate, the consistent gains across all metrics indicate that the proposed arrangement enables the model to capture additional discriminative information beyond what is obtainable from purely vector-based learning. More importantly, the randomized embedding ordering experiments provide critical evidence regarding the role of spatial organization. When the embedding positions were randomly permuted while retaining the same CNN architecture, the performance decreased substantially, with accuracy dropping from 74.2% to 70.6% and F1-score decreasing from 65.6% to 60.2%. This degradation demonstrates that the CNN does not simply benefit from arbitrary convolutional processing over reshaped embeddings. Instead, the effectiveness of the convolutional operations depends on the consistency of neighboring embedding dimensions within the proposed arrangement. If the improvement were solely attributable to the CNN inductive bias, the randomized CNN configuration would be expected to exhibit performance comparable to the ordered CNN configuration. However, the notable decline confirms that preserving embedding positional consistency contributes meaningfully to discriminative feature extraction.

Furthermore, the relatively smaller performance variation observed between the ordered and randomized MLP configurations suggests that the embedding ordering primarily benefits architectures that exploit local neighborhood interactions. Unlike CNNs, MLPs process features globally without assuming locality, making them less sensitive to positional rearrangement. This observation further supports the hypothesis that the proposed image-space transformation provides a structured topological organization that enhances local dependency learning rather than introducing arbitrary spatial artifacts. It is important to clarify that the proposed reshaping strategy does not claim to create a physically meaningful spatial structure equivalent to natural images. Instead, the transformation serves as a consistent topological mapping mechanism that enables convolutional filters to learn stable local dependency patterns among embedding dimensions. The experimental findings therefore suggest that the performance gain emerges from the interaction between structured embedding organization and localized convolutional feature extraction rather than from the CNN architecture alone.

The Grad-CAM heatmaps reveal continuous discriminative patterns with stronger regional concentration, indicating that the CNN successfully learns stable local dependency structures from the organized embedding representation. The emergence of structured activation continuity suggests that neighboring embedding dimensions contain correlated semantic information that becomes exploitable when positional consistency is preserved throughout training.

After randomly permuting the embedding positions, the Grad-CAM activations become fragmented and spatially inconsistent, with reduced regional coherence and weaker discriminative localization. Instead, the structured embedding arrangement facilitates the emergence of learnable local dependency patterns that can be effectively exploited through convolutional operations. The Grad-CAM results therefore provide qualitative evidence supporting the quantitative findings from the ablation study, confirming that the proposed image-space transformation contributes meaningfully to feature representation learning beyond the standalone effect of CNN-based classification.

Conclusion

This paper proposes an image-space embedding framework for complaint location prediction that enables LLMs to effectively learn from heterogeneous structured urban data. Departing from existing text-based LLM approaches, the proposed method transforms multi-source features including complaint information, POI, weather conditions, population density, and RWI into multi-channel, image-like representations that preserve spatial structure and cross-feature relationships. This representation-level redesign allows models to exploit spatial inductive biases that are inaccessible to serialized textual inputs. Extensive experiments on real-world data demonstrate that the proposed framework consistently outperforms baseline LLM approaches across all evaluation metrics while substantially reducing training time under full fine-tuning. Importantly, strong and competitive results obtained with purely vision-based architectures confirm that the performance gains are driven primarily by the quality of the image-space representations rather than by LLM capacity alone. The customization of LLMs via convolutional embedding layers further shows that language models can be effectively adapted to operate directly in image space. Overall, this study provides the first comprehensive demonstration that image-space embedding representations offer a principled, efficient, and generalizable foundation for integrating structured urban data with LLMs. While this study employs image-like matrices with dimensions selected to be as close as possible, future investigations may examine the generalizability of the proposed framework under varying matrix dimensions (a, b). Moreover, future work may explore parameter-efficient adaptation strategies to further reduce computational overhead in large-scale deployments, as well as explainable artificial intelligence techniques to improve the interpretability of customized LLMs and to quantify feature importance within image-space representations.
