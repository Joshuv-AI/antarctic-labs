Systematic hybrid feature selection using optimal filter classifier pairing and parallel evaluation
Authors: Deepak Kumar Rakesh, Shashi Kant Tiwari, Nallakaruppan Kailasanathan, Abhinav Pathak, Aniket S Nagane
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThere is extensive literature on feature selection algorithms, which are typically categorized into filter, wrapper, and embedded methods. Each category has its own merits and limitations. As a result, researchers have increasingly focused on developing hybrid methods that combine the strengths of these approaches. However, the selection of algorithms in hybrid methods is often made without adequate justification, which limits their applicability and overall effectiveness. We propose a Systematic Hybrid Feature Selection framework with Optimal Filter-Classifier Pairing and Parallel Evaluation (SHFS-OFCP-PE), designed to provide a filter-agnostic and classifier-independent hybrid feature selection strategy. The hybridization process emphasizes selecting features with minimal redundancy and maximum relevance to class labels. It also accounts for improvements in model performance when new features are added. Additionally, we present a parallel framework based on a master-slave architecture to enhance scalability. We conduct extensive experiments on twelve hybrid combinations and compare the results with those of traditional and state-of-the-art methods. The findings confirm the superiority of the proposed hybrid methods across multiple performance metrics and are further validated through a post hoc Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) test.Keywords: Hybrid feature selection, Filter, Wrapper, ClassificationSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Feature selection (FS) algorithms are the backbone of many real-world applications such as image processing1, big data2, text mining3, intrusion detection4, and other systems5. FS improves data quality by selecting a relevant subset of features, thereby reducing computational time and enhancing the performance and interpretability of machine learning (ML) models6. FS algorithms are broadly categorized into filter, wrapper, and embedded methods. Filter methods are computationally efficient and scalable. However, they often ignore feature dependencies and may not align well with specific learning algorithms. In contrast, wrapper methods consider feature interactions and typically yield higher accuracy, but they are computationally expensive and prone to overfitting. Embedded methods are more integrated. Thus, they also struggle with high-dimensional data due to complexity. Hence, filters are useful in high-dimensional settings because of their lower computational time7,8. It is worth mentioning that filters alone can be unstable as they rely on heuristic criteria. To tackle these limitations, hybrid approaches that combine filter and wrapper methods have received growing interest. These approaches aim to harness the complementary strengths of both paradigms, enabling more robust, scalable, and accurate FS methods across a wide range of applications.

Many hybrid methods have been proposed9–12. The author in9 proposed two methods based on information gain (IG) with genetic algorithm (GA) and principal component analysis (PCA) separately. However, they work efficiently on different metrics but suffer from high time complexity, especially for IG-GA. Agarwal et al.10 proposed a hybrid method to select an optimal subset of features for reviews of movies and products. Though their approach can reduce the size of features, the performance of classification was not acceptable. Aghaeipoor and Javidi11 have proposed an mRMR based hybrid method for regression problems. However, most of the proposed methods are based on the particular filter method. Therefore, the demerits of the individual methods are inherited in their hybridization process. Tiwari and Chaturvedi13 proposed an information-theory (IT) hybrid method. This assertion is supported by Bommert et al.7, in which the authors experimented with 22 filter methods and found that none of them is clearly superior to the others. Therefore, a hybridized method developed on a particular filter can not be superior to all other hybrids, and hence the applicability of the method is restricted. Feature selection is a fundamental preprocessing technique in machine learning that aims to identify the most relevant and non-redundant features for predictive modeling. By reducing dimensionality, feature selection improves classification performance, enhances interpretability, mitigates overfitting, and decreases computational cost. The importance of feature selection has been extensively discussed in foundational works such as14, and15, as well as comprehensive surveys including16 and17. In parallel, hybrid physical-deep learning modeling and multichannel feature fusion frameworks demonstrate the effectiveness of integrating complementary information sources while preserving interpretability and computational efficiency18–25.

Recent advances in large-scale optimization and intelligent feature selection highlight the growing importance of robust metaheuristic and hybrid frameworks26. For example, Chen et al.27 proposed a particle swarm optimization (PSO) framework with modified global and local search exemplars to enhance large-scale optimization performance, demonstrating improved convergence stability and solution diversity. Similarly, Tu et al.28 introduced a constrained multi-objective evolutionary algorithm based on a dynamic clustering strategy to effectively balance exploration and exploitation in complex search spaces. In applied intelligent systems, Lin and Lin29 utilized multilayer perceptron neural networks for engagement prediction in mechanical systems, illustrating the broader applicability of learning-based optimization techniques. These studies emphasize the increasing demand for scalable, stable, and adaptive hybrid feature selection mechanisms, particularly in high-dimensional classification problems.

Despite these advancements, existing hybrid feature selection approaches still face several challenges, including premature convergence in metaheuristic search, computational inefficiency in high-dimensional settings, insufficient stability analysis, and limited cross-domain generalization. Many recent models primarily focus on improving classification accuracy while overlooking the robustness and consistency of the selected feature subsets. Furthermore, several hybrid methods remain tightly coupled with specific filters, classifiers, or optimization strategies, thereby restricting their general applicability.

Therefore, there remains a need for a generalized and systematic hybrid feature selection framework that integrates statistical relevance evaluation, redundancy control, and adaptive optimization while ensuring scalability, robustness, and consistent performance across heterogeneous classification problems. Formally, the problem addressed in this work is to design a hybrid feature selection mechanism that (i) systematically determines optimal filter-classifier pairings, (ii) minimizes redundancy among selected features, (iii) maximizes relevance with respect to class labels, and (iv) maintains computational efficiency across diverse datasets.

Research gap

Feature Selection (FS) aims to identify informative and non-redundant features that improve predictive performance while reducing dimensionality and computational complexity. Existing approaches can be broadly categorized based on their evaluation mechanisms and search strategies.

Traditional feature selection methods

Many filter, wrapper, and hybrid methods have been proposed in the literature. Filter methods evaluate features independently of any predictive model using statistical, distance-based, or information-theoretic criteria such as ANOVA, Fisher score, ReliefF, and Mutual Information (MI)7,30. These methods are computationally efficient and particularly effective for high-dimensional datasets14. However, filters typically ignore feature interactions and classifier dependency, which may result in suboptimal predictive performance. Moreover, many filter methods do not explicitly model redundancy among selected features. Wrapper methods evaluate subsets using classifier performance as the selection criterion12. While they often produce higher predictive accuracy by accounting for feature interactions, they are computationally expensive and classifier-dependent. Embedded approaches partially address this limitation by incorporating feature selection within model training, yet they remain tied to specific learning architectures and lack flexibility across heterogeneous classifiers.

Metaheuristic and optimization-driven hybrid frameworks

To balance computational efficiency and predictive accuracy, hybrid methods combine filter ranking with wrapper evaluation. Several studies have proposed hybrid strategies based on specific filters such as MI, Fisher, or ReliefF31–33. Metaheuristic optimization algorithms, including PSO and GA-based variants, have been widely adopted to enhance subset search performance34,35. Although these approaches effectively explore large search spaces, they often introduce increased computational overhead, parameter sensitivity, and convergence instability36.

Recent intelligent optimization frameworks further demonstrate the integration of metaheuristics with deep learning architectures. For instance, Logapriya et al.37 proposed Opposition Learning-based Red Panda Optimization for feature selection within a deep learning recommendation framework. Jasmine et al.38 introduced a hybrid deep gated neural model combined with robust feature selection for environmental prediction. Optimization-based adaptive mechanisms have also been applied in large-scale communication and security systems, including energy-aware 6G IoMT clustering39, cooperative caching in 5G networks40, and optimized cloud security frameworks41. While these studies demonstrate the effectiveness of hybrid optimization strategies, most remain domain-specific and tightly coupled with particular optimization algorithms or model architectures. Their feature selection mechanisms are often not statistically grounded in redundancy verification and lack generalizable hybrid integration across multiple classifiers.

To address these challenges, we propose a generalized hybrid feature selection framework that (i) statistically verifies dependency between candidate features and class labels, (ii) explicitly models redundancy among selected features, (iii) incorporates wrapper-based performance without being restricted to a specific classifier, and (iv) guides the selection of the most effective filter-classifier combination within a unified and adaptable structure.

Despite extensive research, existing hybrid feature selection methods lack a systematic mechanism to evaluate and identify optimal filter-classifier pairings across datasets with varying characteristics. Most approaches are tied to specific filters or optimization strategies, limiting their generalizability and reproducibility. This limitation motivates the proposed framework. Unlike many domain-specific optimization-driven methods, the proposed framework is filter-agnostic, classifier-independent, and designed for robust applicability across diverse classification problems.

Methodology

In this section, we propose a Systematic Hybrid Feature Selection framework with Optimal Filter-classifier Pairing and Parallel Evaluation (SHFS-OFCP-PE). The framework is designed to provide a filter-agnostic and classifier-independent hybrid feature selection strategy while ensuring statistical redundancy control and scalable evaluation. Feature selection (FS) is suitable for a dataset if it produces stable performance with reasonable computation time. Hybrid FS methods are one of the approaches that justify using the FS method by harnessing the strength of traditional FS methods. Therefore, our proposed framework generates the ranking of features based on filter methods. This ranking gives information about the dependency of features with the class labels. In addition to this, we find a correlation between every pair of features. This helps avoid selecting features that are highly correlated with those already chosen. Next, the framework applies a greedy-based sequential search algorithm to find the optimal subset of features in coordination with the classifier. It is worth mentioning that although greedy forward selection does not guarantee global optimality in an NP-hard problem such as feature selection, exhaustive search becomes computationally infeasible in practice42. Greedy strategies are therefore widely adopted due to their strong balance between computational efficiency and empirical performance14. Furthermore, the proposed framework mitigates the risk of poor local optima by integrating relevance, redundancy, and wrapper performance within a unified objective function in an iterative manner. The iterative classifier feedback further reduces myopic decisions by reassessing the predictive contribution at each step. Empirically, the stability and competitive performance observed across datasets indicate that the greedy strategy does not significantly degrade solution quality. Further, our framework searches for the best performing hybrid combination based on different filters and classifiers used.

Figure 1 illustrates the architecture of the proposed hybrid feature selection framework, where n filter methods and m classifiers are emlpoyed in the hybridization process. In this framework, each dataset is first processed through all n filters to generate feature ranking based on various statistical or information-theoretic criteria. These rankings reflect the relevance of each feature with respect to the target class labels. Subsequently, each of the m classifiers is applied to evaluate and select the optimal subset of features based on their classification performance, guided by the rankings produced in the filtering phase. Since every filter is paired with each classifier, the framework explores a total of mxn distinct filter-classifier combinations. For each such combination, it allows the framework to identify the most effective hybrid configurations, ensuring that the selected features are not only relevant but also enhance the performance of the corresponding machine learning model. The hybridization process of the proposed framework effectively integrates filter and wrapper approach based on relevancy, redundancy, and predictive performance of the classifiers.

In the proposed framework, feature redundancy is estimated using Pearson correlation due to its computational efficiency and scalability in high-dimensional settings, where redundancy must be repeatedly computed during greedy forward selection. Pearson correlation has been widely used in filter-based feature selection methods, including correlation-based feature selection (CFS), due to its simplicity and effectiveness in reducing multicollinearity43.

Although Pearson correlation primarily captures linear dependencies, the wrapper performance component incorporated in the objective function provides implicit modeling of complex and potentially non-linear feature interactions through classifier feedback. As discussed in hybrid feature selection literature14, wrapper methods can compensate for limitations of filter criteria by directly optimizing predictive performance.

Alternative redundancy measures such as Mutual Information-based criteria, Maximal Information Coefficient (MIC), and distance correlation have been proposed to capture nonlinear dependencies15. While these measures may better characterize complex feature relationships, they typically introduce higher computational overhead, which may limit scalability when embedded within iterative hybrid feature selection frameworks.

Parallel computation

The serial version of the hybrid method, as described in the previous section, requires O(nm Γ F) time. Here, we present a parallel computing model of the proposed framework and the corresponding algorithm, which runs in O(Γ F) time only. Fig. 3 demonstrates the parallel computing model based on master-slave architecture. For ease of understanding, both the serial and parallel methods are shown side by side in this figure. While the serial employs one computing node that sequentially generates the feature subset for each hybrid combination, the Parallel method adopts the same hybridization process as that of serial computing. However, it distributes the working of different hybrid combinations over master-slave nodes. The master nodes are created corresponding to filter methods to generate feature ranking. The slave nodes perform the wrapper process in-coordination with the classifier. Further, each master-slave combination performs the complete hybridization process to generate the feature subset in parallel with each other. Unlike existing hybrid feature selection methods, which are often designed around fixed filter-classifier combinations, the proposed framework is filter- and classifier-independent. It systematically evaluates multiple filter-classifier pairs to identify the optimal hybrid combination for a given dataset, making it highly adaptive and generalizable. Additionally, it incorporates relevance, redundancy, and model performance into a unified scoring function and introduces serial and parallel computing architectures to improve scalability. These features make our framework adaptable across diverse datasets and problem domains, and thus represent a significant advancement over prior work.

Optimal filter-classifier pairing and parallel evaluation

Traditional hybrid feature selection methods typically rely on predefined filter-classifier combinations, which may introduce bias toward specific learning models or evaluation criteria. In contrast, the proposed framework systematically evaluates all possible filter-classifier pairs within a unified structure. Each pair is assessed using the hybrid objective function that integrates feature relevance, redundancy control, and classifier performance. The optimal combination is determined based on aggregated cross-validation performance, ensuring a data-driven and statistically justified pairing strategy. This systematic pairing enhances generalizability by avoiding dependence on a specific filter or classifier. Unlike metaheuristic-based hybrid approaches that embed a single optimization strategy, the proposed method explores multiple hybrid configurations in a controlled and comparable manner.

To address computational overhead, the evaluation of independent filter-classifier combinations is executed in parallel using a master–slave architecture. Each slave process computes the hybrid objective for a specific pair, while the master node aggregates results and identifies the optimal configuration. Since each hybrid combination is independent, parallelization significantly reduces practical runtime without altering theoretical complexity. This design improves scalability, particularly for high-dimensional datasets where multiple combinations must be evaluated. Overall, the proposed pairing and parallel evaluation strategy ensures systematic exploration, improved robustness, and computational efficiency.

Computational complexity and theoretical analysis

For a dataset with n samples and d features, classical filter methods typically require computations, as they evaluate each feature independently with respect to the class labels. Wrapper methods incur higher complexity, generally , where k denotes the selected subset size and C represents the classifier training cost. Metaheuristic-based feature selection approaches (e.g., PSO or GA) further increase computational burden due to population-based iterative search mechanisms, often resulting in complexity, where T and P denote the number of iterations and population size, respectively. The proposed SHFS-OFCP-PE framework adopts a deterministic greedy forward selection strategy combined with statistical redundancy verification, yielding scoring complexity per classifier without requiring stochastic population updates. Although multiple filter-classifier pairings are evaluated, the parallel evaluation strategy distributes these computations across processing units, thereby reducing practical runtime while preserving theoretical efficiency. This design achieves a controlled trade-off between computational cost and selection robustness. The computational efficiency of the proposed framework arises from two factors: (i) filter-based ranking reduces the search space, and (ii) parallel evaluation enables simultaneous processing of multiple filter-classifier combinations. As a result, the effective number of sequential classifier evaluations is reduced, leading to improved scalability compared to conventional wrapper-based approaches.

In addition to computational complexity, several theoretical aspects of the proposed framework are noteworthy. First, with respect to the curse of dimensionality, high-dimensional datasets often degrade model performance and increase computational cost. The proposed framework mitigates this issue through filter-based ranking, which reduces the effective feature space before classification, thereby improving scalability and generalization. Second, the optimal subset search problem is addressed using a greedy forward selection strategy. While exhaustive search guarantees optimality with exponential complexity, the proposed approach provides a near-optimal solution with significantly reduced computational overhead, making it suitable for large-scale applications. Third, in comparison to traditional feature selection paradigms, filter methods are computationally efficient but lack interaction with classifiers, wrapper methods offer improved accuracy at the expense of high computational cost, and embedded methods are model-dependent. The proposed hybrid framework effectively combines the strengths of these approaches by integrating filter-based ranking with classifier-based validation in a systematic and flexible manner. Finally, redundancy handling is achieved through statistical verification and stability analysis, which reduce the selection of highly correlated or redundant features. This leads to improved robustness and consistency of the selected feature subsets across different datasets. Overall, the proposed framework provides a balanced trade-off between computational efficiency, scalability, and selection performance.

To robustly evaluate the proposed feature selection (FS) framework, we selected a diverse set of datasets that vary in number of features (dimensionality), number of classes, number of instances, class imbalance ratio, and data types (discrete and continuous). For datasets containing continuous attributes, discretization was applied as a standard preprocessing step to ensure stable entropy-based feature scoring and compatibility with information-theoretic filters. Prior studies have shown that supervised entropy-based discretization can improve the stability of mutual information estimation and enhance classification performance, particularly for probabilistic classifiers such as Naïve Bayes48. However, discretization may also influence statistical measures such as ANOVA by altering variance structure and potentially introducing a bias–variance trade-off. Furthermore, recent work on feature transformation and stochastic selection mechanisms highlights that preprocessing decisions can directly affect model robustness and generalization49. In this study, discretization is treated uniformly as preprocessing rather than as a comparative experimental factor, as the primary objective is to evaluate the proposed hybrid feature selection framework. The class imbalance ratio is calculated as the number of instances in the most frequent class divided by the number of instances in the least frequent class. This diversity ensures a comprehensive assessment of the robustness and scalability of the proposed method. For example, Breast_cancer is a low-dimensional binary classification dataset, while Lung_discrete and Colon are high-dimensional, small-sample medical datasets typical in bioinformatics. WarpPIE10P, with 2420 features and 10 classes, evaluates performance in multi-class, high-dimensional settings. Waveform and Spambase represent moderate-dimensional, continuous datasets for signal and text classification tasks. Mice Protein and Lung offer biomedical data with varied class distributions and feature counts. Finally, the ORL dataset presents a high-dimensional image recognition task with 40 classes. Collectively, these datasets address key challenges in FS, such as high dimensionality, class imbalance, and domain variability, thereby enabling a thorough and generalizable validation of our approach.

To analyze the behavior of the hybrid methods in detail, we selected two representative datasets (Breast_cancer and Waveform) and conducted experiments using three different FS thresholds. For the high-dimensional datasets, a fixed threshold was used. Missing values, such as those in the Mice Protein dataset, were imputed using the mean of the corresponding column. Continuous features were discretized into five equal-width bins, and nominal features were encoded as integers. Finally, we applied the 10-fold cross-validation to evaluate the performance of the final model. Herein, for each outer fold, feature selection and model training were performed strictly within the training partition, and performance was computed on the corresponding validation fold. Final performance metrics represent the average across all outer folds. Statistical comparisons were performed on aggregated performance measures rather than individual fold-level results. Although cross-validation introduces partial dependence between folds due to overlapping training data, this evaluation protocol follows established best practices in machine learning research50. In the next section, we discuss the diversity study of the filter methods used.

Diversity study

In order to show that the feature ranking produced by different filter methods are significantly different, we conducted a diversity study on the four filters based on Spearman’s rank and Kendall’s rank correlation coefficient on two datasets Scadi and Waveform. The diversity analysis is chosen to reflect contrasting characteristics in dimensionality and class distribution. The purpose of this analysis is to illustrate the behavioral tendencies of the proposed hybrid framework rather than to establish universal statistical generalization across all datasets. Since the diversity measure depends primarily on the interaction between feature selection mechanisms and classifier behavior, similar trends are expected for datasets with comparable structural properties.

The results are shown in Tables 4 and 5 respectively in which the ρ values are in the range of [-1, 1], where 1 indicates that the corresponding filter methods are equal. It can be observed that the ρ values are far away from 1 for two different filter methods, but equals to 1 when the two same filters are compared. This can be seen in the diagonals of the tables. This simple experiment shows that the filter methods are chosen in this study generate different feature rankings, thereby ensuring enough diversity. Similar experiments have been done in earlier work51 but with different filters.

Results and discussion

Benchmark implementation and verification

To ensure a fair and reproducible comparison, all baseline feature selection methods, ANOVA14, Fisher Score52, ReliefF53, Mutual Information (MI)54, JMIM55, and DCSF56 were implemented using standard Scikit-learn library functions. For all comparative experiments, identical preprocessing steps, discretization strategy, cross-validation protocol, feature selection thresholds, and classifier parameter settings were maintained across methods. This controlled experimental setup ensures that observed performance differences arise from the feature selection mechanisms themselves rather than from inconsistencies in implementation or evaluation. Furthermore, each computational table explicitly specifies the corresponding comparison group, ensuring clarity in the evaluation of baseline, hybrid, and state-of-the-art methods.

Comparative performance against baseline filters

First, we performed experiments on two representational datasets (Breast cancer and Waveform) with three different feature threshold values, i.e., 10% , 25% , and 50% of the original set of features. The thresholds (10%, 25%, and 50%) are selected to evaluate the robustness of the proposed framework across varying subset sizes rather than to determine a single optimal cutoff. Reporting performance under multiple selection ratios enables analysis of stability and generalization behavior. While adaptive thresholding approaches based on data complexity measures as used in57 may provide a principled mechanism for selecting γ, the present study focuses on evaluating the hybrid feature integration mechanism independently of a specific subset-size optimization strategy. We compare the resulted accuracy of basic filter methods (i.e., Anova, Fisher, ReliefF, and Mutual information) as well as their hybrid combinations with three classifiers (i.e., NB, k-NN, and SVM) referred as HybridA, HybridF, HybridR, and HybridMI. The comparisons are shown shown in Table 6. For different threshold values, the results of the best performing ML models are shown in bold. It can be observed from the table that the hybrid combinations outperform the basic filter methods on most of the cases for both the datasets. However, the HybridMI performs best among all the hybrid methods on almost all threshold values.

We propose hybrid methods to harness the strength of filter and wrapper methods. Hence, it is crucial to explicitly compare the time complexity of our method with the existing basic methods. For this, we choose a wrapper SVM-RFE and embedded Tree-based FS method on the datasets with moderate dimensions. We choose these datasets to make the comparison fair as wrapper and embedded methods suffer from high time complexity on high-dimensional datasets58. Table 7 manifests the computational time of SVM-RFE, Tree-based FS, and HybridMI with SVM on two datasets, spambase and Mice protein. Table 7 shows the accuracy of the datasets with minimum features. The last column of the table refers to the execution time comparison concerning the number of wins in terms of execution time. It can be observed from the table that HybridMI wins 38 number of times as compared to SVM-RFE and Tree-based FS, whose wins are 27 and 35 respectively on Spambase datasets. In the case of Mice protein datasets, HybridMI wins 43 number of times compared to SVM-RFE (25), and Tree-based FS (32). It is found that the hybrid method generate better average accuracy with less number of features and low computational time.

High-dimensional datasets with three classifiers

Next, we discuss the results on high-dimensional datasets. We compare the hybrid methods with basic filters (Anova, Fisher, ReliefF, and MI) as well as two recently proposed MI-based FS methods: JMIM55 and DCSF56. We choose the MI-based methods for comparison because HybridMI has demonstrated superior performance over other hybrid approaches, as discussed earlier. We choose to select the first 30 features (Γ) to calculate the average accuracy and the highest accuracy. Earlier literature59 has used a similar threshold value. The reasons are as follows. The threshold should not be too small or large. The smaller threshold will have fewer feature alternatives and hence diversity of selected features can be compromised. At the same time, a larger threshold value will increase the computational time of the model.

We statistically verified the significance of experimental results by means of hypothesis testing. For this purpose, we used a statistical method named ANOVA which compare the differences between the means of two or more groups. Based on the outcomes of ANOVA, it decides that a null hypothesis can be rejected or not. The null hypothesis states that the means of the methods are same. To test the null hypothesis, we take 30 samples for each method at a significance level of α = 0.05. The null hypothesis () is rejected based on two conditions from the ANOVA test: (i) the p-value (significance value) must be lower than the significance level, and (ii) the F-statistic must be greater than the F-critical value. Table 12 shows that both conditions are satisfied with respect to the accuracy of the methods. Therefore, we reject the null hypothesis, which means there is a statistically significance difference in accuracy among the methods.

The ANOVA tells us that at least one method performs differently, however, it fails to identify which specific methods differ. To find that out, we conduct a Fisher Least Significant Difference (LSD) post hoc test. This test compares the average accuracy between each pair of methods. If the difference between two methods is larger than a certain threshold, that is LSD, we conclude that their performance is significantly different. Comparison with state-of-the-art technique

Table 18 presents a comparison of state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods65,66 proposed in 2024. For consistency, the SOTA methods were implemented using an SVM classifier and compared against the results generated by the proposed framework, also using SVM. From the table, it can be observed that the method in66 outperforms others on the LDI dataset. It shows comparable performance to HybridMI on the COL and LUN datasets, and to HybridA on the WAR dataset. However, it is outperformed by HybridMI on the ORL dataset. It is important to note that the method in66 is a metaheuristic-based optimization feature selection technique, which makes it computationally expensive. Additionally, it is classifier-dependent and, therefore, lacks generalization capability. In contrast, the proposed framework guides the selection of the best hybrid combination by identifying which filter and classifier will perform best for a given dataset.

Execution time comparison

The execution time comparison between the proposed framework (in both serial and parallel modes) and state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods is shown in Table 19. The execution times were recorded on a Windows 10 system with a 64-bit processor: Intel i7-10750H CPU @ 2.60GHz, 2592 MHz, 6 cores, 24 GB RAM, and a 4 GB GPU. It is worth mentioning that the aforesaid configuration is specifically used to compare the execution time as shown in Table 19. The runtime comparisons are valid only within experiments conducted on the same hardware environment. As observed in the table, the parallel framework demonstrates lower execution time compared to the serial framework on the WAR, LUN, and ORL datasets. However, for the LDI and COL datasets, the parallel version shows higher execution time. This is primarily due to the overhead associated with initializing and managing parallel processes, such as data copying and process synchronization, which outweigh the performance benefits in lightweight computations as compared to serial. Further, when compared with SOTA methods65,66, the proposed parallel framework is only outperformed by the method in65 on smaller datasets. In contrast, it outperforms the method in66 across all five datasets.

Theoretical analysis of parallel efficiency: The practical speedup achieved by the parallel implementation is influenced by system-level overhead, including communication and synchronization costs associated with the master–slave architecture. According to Amdahl’s Law67, the maximum achievable speedup is limited by the non-parallelizable fraction of the algorithm. For smaller datasets, where computational workload per worker is limited, overhead may dominate, resulting in marginal or negative speedup. However, as dataset dimensionality and feature evaluation complexity increase, the parallelizable fraction grows, leading to improved acceleration. This behavior aligns with Gustafson’s Law68, which indicates that speedup scales with problem size. Thus, the break-even point is primarily governed by feature dimensionality, subset size (τ), and classifier training cost.

Limitations and scope for future work

While the proposed hybrid feature selection framework demonstrates strong improvements over traditional and recent methods, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the framework has been evaluated only on single-label classification tasks, which limits its applicability to more complex real-world scenarios such as multi-label or multi-output learning. In addition, although cross-validation is a standard evaluation strategy, the folds are not strictly independent, and therefore some statistical assumptions may not be fully satisfied. From an optimization perspective, the use of greedy forward selection provides computational efficiency for the NP-hard feature selection problem; however, it does not guarantee globally optimal feature subsets. More advanced search strategies, such as metaheuristic or submodular optimization methods, may further improve solution quality.

From a modeling standpoint, the scoring function relies on fixed weights for relevance, redundancy, and classifier performance. Although sensitivity analysis indicates robustness, adaptive or learnable weighting mechanisms could improve generalization across diverse datasets. Furthermore, redundancy estimation is currently based on linear correlation, which may not capture complex nonlinear dependencies among features. Incorporating nonlinear dependency measures would enhance the representational capability of the framework. Additionally, the feature subset size is determined using predefined thresholds rather than adaptive, data-driven criteria.

From a scalability perspective, despite the use of parallel evaluation, the framework may still incur computational overhead for smaller datasets and may face challenges when applied to extremely high-dimensional data (e.g., large-scale omics datasets). Advanced acceleration strategies, such as GPU-based or distributed implementations, could address these limitations. Finally, the framework evaluates filter-classifier combinations exhaustively rather than learning to predict optimal combinations for a given dataset. Future work may explore meta-learning approaches to recommend effective combinations, thereby improving scalability, adaptability, and practical deployment.

Conclusion

This article has proposed a general framework for the hybrid feature selection method, which is independent of a particular filter or classifier method. In order to reduce time complexity, we have also presented a parallel architecture of the proposed framework. We conducted diversity test on the filters and performed experiments with 12 combinations of hybrid methods and compared them with traditional as well as the two state-of-the-art methods. We have found that the hybrid methods outperform others with respect to accuracy, AUC, and feature stability. Specifically, we have shown that the MI-based hybrid method exhibits 24.66% and 2.04% a maximum and a minimum improvement respectively for high dimensional datasets in terms of average accuracy. Although MI-based hybrid methods show strong performance in several datasets, the proposed framework provides a systematic mechanism to identify optimal filter-classifier combinations across diverse data characteristics, ensuring general applicability rather than reliance on a single method. The results confirm that the proposed framework effectively adapts to diverse feature characteristics and task objectives by selecting optimal feature–classifier pairings across datasets. Finally, as the research finds MI based hybrid method as the best model, our future effort will be made to work on the same model for multi-label datasets incorporating label-specific information shared by the features.
