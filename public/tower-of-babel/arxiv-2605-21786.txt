Existence of solutions for a model of the Earth’s magnetic field Thanks: F.W. and T.S. were supported in part by NSF DMS-2438083, J.B. was supported in part by NSF DMS-2108633 and NSF DMS-2510949
Jacob Bedrossian, Tom Schang, Franziska Weber

Abstract
. We study a physically realistic, whole-core mathematical model of the dynamics in the Earth’s core and we prove existence of Leray-Hopf type weak solutions to the model. Our model combines Magneto-Hydrodynamic equations in the liquid outer core with solid physics for the electrically conducting inner core, and treats everything exterior to the core as a perfect insulator governed by Maxwell’s equations. We prove existence of weak solutions using Galerkin approximations. In order to control the nonlinearities, we must define an appropriate function space for the magnetic field and prove a Biot-Savart type result. The main new difficulty here is properly setting up the functional framework to simultaneously deal with the fluid structure interaction with the inner core and the magnetic transmission problem, with both the perfectly conducting inner core and the perfectly insulating mantle/exterior.

1.
Introduction

In 1919, Larmor proposed a model, which has developed into the geodynamo model, to explain how the Earth generates its magnetic field
[
19
]
. According to the geodynamo model, heat is slowly released from Earth’s solid inner core to the liquid outer core. This heating creates a convection effect which, coupled with the Coriolis force from rotation of the Earth, produces helical currents in the liquid outer core. Because the outer core is composed of electrically conducting molten metals, the helical currents act as solenoids and create the magnetic field that we experience. While the dominant theory is that the convection due to buoyant forces related to temperature and composition of the fluid is the primary driving force, researchers are also exploring other mechanisms, such as tidal forces and precession
[
18
]
.

More recently, researchers have simulated the geodynamo model numerically
[
13
,
26
,
12
]
. Their experiments yielded results that support the geodynamo model – the total magnetic energy is experimentally observed to sustain over time. Their experiments are also consistent with the observed phenomenon of
geomagnetic reversals
, which is that the Earth’s magnetic field flips direction at random times on the scale of hundreds of thousands of years
[
23
,
24
,
8
]
. Some recent simulations have focused on the effect of turbulence in the geodynamo
[
28
]
.

However, to our knowledge, there is no rigorous mathematical proof that the systems being simulated are mathematically well-defined in that they admit solutions. In particular, we do not know whether the numerical schemes from previous simulations converge to a solution of the system of equations they purport to solve. In this paper, we address this research gap by rigorously defining a system of equations that models the earth’s magnetic field and the dynamics of the molten metals in the core, and proving existence of Leray-Hopf-type weak solutions to this system of equations for all times. We focus on weak solutions as we are interested in the long-time dynamics in non-perturbative regimes where it will not be possible to prove the existence of strong solutions beyond a short time.

1.3.
Related Work

Various aspects of the problem at hand have already been studied. In the realm of fluid-structure interaction, weak solutions have been shown to exist for a rigid body immersed in a viscous incompressible fluid, up to the time of first contact between the solid and the fluid boundary
[
9
]
. We use a similar notion of weak solutions as in that work. The motion of rigid bodies submerged in incompressible fluids has also been studied by: Ortega, Rosier, and Takahashi
[
25
]
, where global in time existence of classical solutions was shown for a ball emerged in a perfect incompressible fluid in 2D; by Ducomet and Nečasová
[
10
]
, where the motion of rigid bodies in incompressible and compressible viscous
fluids under the action of gravitational forces was studied; and by Bălilescu, San Martín and Takahashi
[
3
]
, where the existence of weak solutions for a model of “a rigid body and a viscous incompressible fluid using a boundary condition based on Coulomb’s law” was shown. The resistive
MHD
equations have also been well-studied, such as in the works of: Duvaut and Lions
[
11
]
where the existence and uniqueness of weak and strong solutions was studied; Sermange and Temam
[
32
]
where the long time behavior of solutions was studied; and Cheskidov and Dai
[
6
]
where regularity criteria for solutions are studied, to mention a few.

A similar system to ours is analyzed in
[
4
]
. That work considers a rigid insulating body immersed in a viscous, incompressible, electrically conducting fluid, with perfect conductor boundary conditions on the boundary between the fluid and the exterior. For this system, the authors prove existence of weak solutions up to the time of first contact between the solid and the fluid boundary. This result has also been extended to the case of multiple rigid bodies immersed in a compressible fluid
[
30
,
31
]
. A rigorous derivation of these systems and boundary conditions can be found in
[
29
]
. In contrast, we consider a setting in which the rigid body is conductive and the region external to the fluid is a perfect insulator which leads to a different problem for the unbounded region. We also consider a rotating but not translating rigid body since the translation is not relevant for the dynamics in the core of the earth. We do, however, allow for discontinuous, piecewise constant physical parameters instead of strictly constant ones as in
[
4
]
due to the inner core, outer core, and the mantle having different physical properties. In addition, the proofs in these previous works appear more complicated and involve both Rothe’s method and Brinkman penalization, whereas we achieve our results using only a spatial discretization.

The problem studied here falls into the wider area of electromagnetic transmission problems. A variety of magnetic transmission problems relevant for different physical scenarios have been studied in the literature. A review can be found in
[
20
]
, where the authors distinguish three basic types found in
[
16
,
15
]
: (a) the
perfect conductor
problem, where a fluid is confined to a finite vessel with perfectly conducting walls, resulting in an initial-boundary value problem, and (b) the
matching
problem, where a bounded region of electrically conducting fluid is coupled to another region via jump conditions. A typical problem of this type is a situation with a “bounded region of conducting fluid surrounded by an insulator of infinite extent.” There are also (c)
mixed
problems, where two regions are coupled via jump conditions and are contained themselves in a larger region with perfectly conducting walls. For example, a “conducting fluid and a current carrying solid conductor embedded in a region of insulating material bounded by perfectly conducting walls” would fall into this category. The problem we are considering in this work has aspects of types (b) and (c), as it concerns two conducting regions coupled via jump conditions and surrounded by an infinite insulator. Problems of type (b) and (c) were first studied in
[
16
,
17
,
33
]
.
More recent progress has been made both on the theoretical (e.g.,
[
22
]
), as well as the numerical side (e.g.,
[
21
,
14
]
). In
[
22
]
, the authors investigate higher regularity for vector fields with piecewise regular curl and/or divergence, which are precisely the sort of vector fields encountered in matching problems. In
[
14
]
, the authors define a boundary element method for treating matching problems numerically without having to extend the magnetic field to an insulator of infinite extent.

In our work here, we combine for the first time aspects of magnetic transmission problems of type (b) and (c) such as the matching boundary conditions and the infinite insulating exterior, with rigid body dynamics and the resistive
MHD
equations. To do so, we first define a suitable notion of weak solutions inspired by
[
9
]
that avoids the boundary integral of the stress tensor in (
1.2a
) which may otherwise not be regular enough for weak solutions. Then we use a Galerkin approach to set up finite dimensional approximate systems for (
1.1
)-(
1.5
) that satisfy an energy balance yielding a priori estimates. The main challenge here is to define a suitable Hilbert space for the approximation of the magnetic field
B
leading to the correct transmission conditions. This requires an appropriate Biot-Savart law with refraction. Then we pass to the limit in the approximating system using the Aubin-Lions lemma and conclude that the limit is a weak solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.5
).

We combine the fluid velocity

\mathbb{U}^{1}=\left\{(u,\omega)\in H^{1}_{\tdiv}({\Omega_{o}})\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:u\big|_{\partial{\Omega_{c}}}=0,u\big|_{\partial{\Omega_{i}}}=\omega\times x\right\}

\mathbb{U}^{0}=\overline{\mathbb{U}^{1}}^{L^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}.

Combining both variables is important since it will enable a good energy estimate for the approximating solutions.
We define the inner product in
\mathbb{U}^{0}
to be

\langle(u,\omega),(v,\alpha)\rangle_{\mathbb{U}^{0}}=\rho\langle u,v\rangle_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}+J\omega\cdot\alpha

\langle(u,\omega),(v,\alpha)\rangle_{\mathbb{U}^{1}}=\langle(u,\omega),(v,\alpha)\rangle_{\mathbb{U}^{0}}+2\rho\nu a^{S}(u,v)

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(u,\omega)\in\mathbb{U}^{1}
. Then
\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}({\Omega_{o}})}\sim\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}+\lVert D^{S}(u)\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}

\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}({\Omega_{o}})}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}+\lVert D^{S}(u)\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}.

More strongly,
\lVert D^{S}(u)\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}\sim\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}
, which implies

\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}({\Omega_{o}})}\lesssim\lVert D^{S}(u)\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}.

Proof.

The weaker statement is Korn’s inequality (e.g., Theorem 2.1 in
[
7
]

For the stronger statement, consider

on the entire core

, where we extend to
{\Omega_{i}}

\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}({\Omega_{o}})}\leq\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}({\Omega_{c}})}\lesssim\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{c}})}\lesssim\lVert D^{S}(u)\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{c}})}=\lVert D^{S}(u)\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}.

The last equality comes from the fact that

Let

Lemma 2.3

The space

Proof.

with smooth boundary and connected exterior
\Omega_{+}
, and
\mu
be piecewise constant on
\Omega_{-}
and
\Omega_{+}
.

2.1.1.
Beppo-Levi space

Define the space
\dot{H}^{1}(\Omega_{+})
to be the completion of compactly supported smooth functions in
\overline{\Omega}_{+}
under the homogeneous
\dot{H}^{1}
norm, i.e. under the norm

\lVert\phi\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(\Omega_{+})}=\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{+})}.

It is known that in
\dot{H}^{1}(\Omega_{+})
this norm is equivalent to the norm

\left(\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{+})}^{2}+\lVert(1+|x|^{2})^{-1/2}\phi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{+})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(e.g., Proposition 2.10.10 in
[
27
]
) and this space is called the
Beppo-Levi
space. For functions in
\dot{H}^{1}(\Omega_{+})
, observe that on compact subsets
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega_{+}
, the
L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})
norm is controlled by the
\dot{H}^{1}(\Omega_{+})
norm.

2.1.2.
Refraction problem

The refraction problem arises when we have elliptic-type equations in divergence form with a piecewise constant coefficient. What is important to us is that solutions are piecewise-regular up to the boundary.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{-}
be non-empty connected compact domains in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
\Gamma=\overline{\Omega}_{+}\cap\overline{\Omega}_{-}
is a smooth codimension-1 surface, and
S=\partial(\overline{\Omega}_{+}\cup\overline{\Omega}_{-})
is also a smooth codimension-1 surface, and such that
\Gamma\cap S
is empty. Let
\mu=\mu_{\pm}
on
\Omega_{\pm}
where
\mu_{+},\mu_{-}
are positive constants. Consider the refraction problem

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\tdiv(\mu\nabla\phi)=f&\Omega_{\pm}\\
\llbracket\phi\rrbracket=0&\Gamma\\
\llbracket\mu\nabla\phi\cdot n\rrbracket=0&\Gamma\\
\phi=0&S\end{cases}.

Suppose
f\in H^{s}(\Omega_{+})\times H^{s}(\Omega_{-})
for
s>-1
. Then there is a unique weak solution
\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{+}\cup\Gamma\cup\Omega_{-})
to
eq.

2.1
. Moreover,
\phi\in H^{s+2}(\Omega_{+})\times H^{s+2}(\Omega_{-})
and it satisfies the estimate

\lVert\phi\rVert_{H^{s+2}(\Omega_{+})\times H^{s+2}(\Omega_{-})}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{H^{s}(\Omega_{+})\times H^{s}(\Omega_{-})}.

This is exactly Theorem 1.1 in
[
2
]
. A similar result for the specific case where
f\in L^{2}
can be found with proof as Lemma 1 in
[
16
]
.

2.1.3.
Biot-Savart law

We define the Newton potential in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

G(x)=\frac{-1}{4\pi|x|}

and the operator of convolution with the Newton potential
\mathcal{L}_{0}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
by

\mathcal{L}_{0}\phi(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}G(x-y)\phi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Lemma 2.5
.

Given
h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, the vector field
f=-\tcurl\mathcal{L}_{0}h
is the unique solution in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
of

\begin{cases}\tdiv f=0&\mathbb{R}^{3}\\
\tcurl f=h&\mathbb{R}^{3}\end{cases}

and is bounded as a map
L^{2}_{\tdiv}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Moreover, if
h
has compact support, then
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

This is Lemma 2.2 (b) in
[
20
]
.

2.1.4.
Biot-Savart with refraction.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
B\in\mathbb{B}^{1}
. Then

(2.2)

\lVert B\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}({\Omega_{i}})\times\dot{H}^{1}({\Omega_{o}})\times\dot{H}^{1}({\Omega_{e}})}\lesssim\lVert\tcurl\mu^{-1}B\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{c}})}.

Proof.

We will follow the technique of Theorem 2.7 in
[
20
]
. Our strategy will be to show that for any
h\in L^{2}_{\tdiv}({\Omega_{c}})
there exists a unique
f\in\mathbb{B}^{1}
such that
\tcurl(\mu^{-1}f)=h
, and that this
f
satisfies the desired estimate. To put it in different words, we pretend that we only know
\tcurl(\mu^{-1}B)=h
, and we construct a function
f
such that
\tcurl(\mu^{-1}f)=h
and such that the desired inequality is satisfied. Finally, we prove that this
f
is unique, so that it is in fact
B
and therefore
B
satisfies the desired inequality.

Extending
h
to be zero outside
{\Omega_{c}}
, we define
f_{1}:=-\tcurl\mathcal{L}_{0}h
. By
Section

2.1.3
, we know
f_{1}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(in fact,
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
because
h
has compact
support) and

\tdiv f_{1}=0,\qquad\tcurl f_{1}=h

as functions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Moreover,

\lVert f_{1}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\lVert h\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

and thus, taking the trace,

f_{1}\cdot n\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
where
\Gamma
is any smooth codimension 1 surface.
If we now set
f=\mu f_{1}
, then we find that

\tcurl(\mu^{-1}f)=\tcurl f_{1}=h

which fulfills some of our requirements, but on the other hand

\tdiv f\not\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

because

\llbracket f\cdot n\rrbracket_{\partial{\Omega_{i}},\partial{\Omega_{c}}}=\llbracket\mu f_{1}\cdot n\rrbracket

which is in general nonzero, because
\llbracket f_{1}\cdot n\rrbracket
is zero and the trace of
f_{1}\cdot n
is in general nonzero. This means that we need to correct this error in the jump condition. We look for a decomposition
f=\mu(f_{1}+f_{2})
.
In particular, we need to find
f_{2}
solving

\begin{cases}\tcurl f_{2}=0&\mathbb{R}^{3}\\
\tdiv f_{2}=0&{\Omega_{i}},{\Omega_{o}},{\Omega_{e}}\\
\llbracket\mu f_{2}\cdot n\rrbracket=-\llbracket\mu f_{1}\cdot n\rrbracket&\partial{\Omega_{i}},\partial{\Omega_{c}}\,.\end{cases}

Instead of solving this directly, we look for a solution
f_{2}=\nabla\phi
where
\phi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfies
\nabla^{2}\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and solves

\begin{cases}\tdiv(\mu\nabla\phi)=0&{\Omega_{i}},{\Omega_{o}},{\Omega_{e}}\\
\llbracket\mu\nabla\phi\cdot n\rrbracket=-\llbracket\mu f_{1}\cdot n\rrbracket&\partial{\Omega_{i}},\partial{\Omega_{c}}\,.\end{cases}

Observe that the right hand side of the jump condition
\llbracket\mu f_{1}\cdot n\rrbracket
is in the space
H^{1/2}(\partial{\Omega_{i}})
and
H^{1/2}(\partial{\Omega_{o}})
. This means that we can find a bounded, compactly supported, piecewise
H^{2}
extension of this jump condition.
In particular, we can choose
g_{o}\in H^{2}({\Omega_{i}})\times H^{2}({\Omega_{o}})\times H^{2}({\Omega_{e}})
with
\supp g_{o}\subseteq R_{o}-\delta\leq|x|\leq R_{o}
(with
\delta<R_{o}-R_{i}
) such that
\llbracket\mu\nabla g_{o}\cdot n\rrbracket_{\partial{\Omega_{c}}}=-\llbracket\mu f_{1}\cdot n\rrbracket_{\partial{\Omega_{c}}}
.
Similarly, we can choose
g_{i}\in H^{2}({\Omega_{i}})\times H^{2}({\Omega_{o}})\times H^{2}({\Omega_{e}})
with
\supp g_{i}\subseteq\overline{{\Omega_{i}}}
such that
\llbracket\mu\nabla g_{i}\cdot n\rrbracket_{\partial{\Omega_{i}}}=-\llbracket\mu f_{1}\cdot n\rrbracket_{\partial{\Omega_{i}}}
. We can now define
\phi=\widetilde{\phi}+g_{i}+g_{o}
and define a problem for
\widetilde{\phi}
:

\begin{cases}\tdiv(\mu\nabla\widetilde{\phi})=-\tdiv(\mu\nabla g_{i})-\tdiv(\mu\nabla g_{o})&{\Omega_{i}},{\Omega_{o}},{\Omega_{e}}\\
\llbracket\mu\nabla\widetilde{\phi}\cdot n\rrbracket=0&\partial{\Omega_{i}},\partial{\Omega_{o}}\,.\end{cases}

Applying Lax-Milgram to the variational formulation in the space
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\widetilde{\phi}\cdot\nabla\psi\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\tdiv(\mu\nabla g_{i})+\tdiv(\mu\nabla g_{o})\right)\psi\,\mathrm{d}x

yields a solution
\widetilde{\phi}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfying

\lVert\nabla\widetilde{\phi}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\lVert g_{i}\rVert_{H^{2}({\Omega_{i}})}+\lVert g_{o}\rVert_{H^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}.

However, we also require
\widetilde{\phi}\in\dot{H}^{2}({\Omega_{i}})\times\dot{H}^{2}({\Omega_{o}})\times\dot{H}^{2}({\Omega_{e}})
. In order to show that our solution has this higher piecewise regularity, we will localize to three different regions.

(1)

We localize near infinity. Take
\chi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\chi=1
for
r\geq R_{o}+2
,
\chi=0
for
r\leq R_{o}+1
, and with bounded derivative (say
\left\lvert\chi^{\prime}\right\rvert\leq C
). Since on this region,
\mu=\mu_{e}
is constant, by considering the Poisson equation solved by
\chi(|x|)\widetilde{\phi}

\Delta(\chi(|x|)\widetilde{\phi})=\widetilde{\phi}\Delta\chi(|x|)+2\nabla\chi(|x|)\cdot\nabla\widetilde{\phi}+\chi(|x|)\Delta\widetilde{\phi}

we get that

\lVert\widetilde{\phi}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{2}(\{|x|\geq R_{o}+2\})}\lesssim\lVert\nabla\widetilde{\phi}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\lVert\widetilde{\phi}\rVert_{L^{2}(\{|x|\in\supp\Delta\chi\})}\lesssim\lVert\widetilde{\phi}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(2)

We localize near
\partial{\Omega_{c}}
. This time take
\chi=1
on
[(R_{o}+R_{i})/2,R_{o}+2]
and
\chi=0
for
r\geq R_{o}+3
and
r\leq(R_{o}+3R_{i})/4
. We can then apply the existence and regularity result
Section

2.1.2
for the refraction problem

\begin{cases}\tdiv(\mu\nabla(\chi(|x|)\widetilde{\phi})\,)=\mu\widetilde{\phi}\Delta\chi+2\mu\nabla\widetilde{\phi}\cdot\nabla\chi+\chi\tdiv(\mu\nabla\widetilde{\phi})&{\Omega_{e}}\cap\{|x|\leq R_{0}+3\},{\Omega_{c}}\\
\llbracket\chi(|x|)\widetilde{\phi}\rrbracket=0&\partial{\Omega_{c}}\\
\llbracket\mu\nabla(\chi(|x|)\widetilde{\phi})\cdot n\rrbracket=0&\partial{\Omega_{c}}\\
\chi(|x|)\widetilde{\phi}=0&|x|=R_{0}+3\end{cases}

to conclude

(2.3)

\left(\lVert\widetilde{\phi}\rVert^{2}_{\dot{H}^{2}({\Omega_{e}}\cap\{|x|\leq R_{o}+2\})}+\lVert\widetilde{\phi}\rVert^{2}_{\dot{H}^{2}({\Omega_{o}}\cap\{|x|\geq(R_{o}+R_{i})/2\})}\right)^{1/2}\\
\lesssim\lVert\nabla\widetilde{\phi}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\lVert\widetilde{\phi}\rVert_{L^{2}(\{|x|\leq R_{o}+3\})}+\lVert g_{o}\rVert_{H^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}.

(3)

We localize near
\partial{\Omega_{i}}
. This time, take
\chi=1
for
r<(R_{i}+R_{o})/2
and
\chi=0
for
r\geq(R_{i}+3R_{o})/4
. We apply
Section

2.1.2
again to conclude

\left(\lVert\widetilde{\phi}\rVert^{2}_{\dot{H}^{2}({\Omega_{o}}\cap\{|x|<(R_{o}+R_{i})/2\})}+\lVert\widetilde{\phi}\rVert^{2}_{\dot{H}^{2}({\Omega_{i}})}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\lVert\nabla\widetilde{\phi}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\lVert\widetilde{\phi}\rVert_{L^{2}(\{|x|\leq(R_{o}+R_{i})/2\}}+\lVert g_{i}\rVert_{H^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}.

Putting these local estimates together, we conclude that
\widetilde{\phi}\in\dot{H}^{2}({\Omega_{i}})\times\dot{H}^{2}({\Omega_{o}})\times\dot{H}^{2}({\Omega_{e}})
and satisfies the estimate

\lVert\widetilde{\phi}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\lVert\widetilde{\phi}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{2}({\Omega_{i}})\times\dot{H}^{2}({\Omega_{o}})\times\dot{H}^{2}({\Omega_{e}})}\lesssim\lVert g_{i}\rVert_{H^{2}({\Omega_{i}})}+\lVert g_{o}\rVert_{H^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}\lesssim\lVert f_{1}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Thus

\lVert f_{2}\rVert_{H^{1}({\Omega_{i}})\times H^{1}({\Omega_{o}})\times H^{1}({\Omega_{e}})}\lesssim\lVert f_{1}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

and therefore

\lVert f\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}({\Omega_{i}})\times\dot{H}^{1}({\Omega_{o}})\times\dot{H}^{1}({\Omega_{e}})}\lesssim\lVert f_{1}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\lVert f_{2}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}({\Omega_{i}})\times\dot{H}^{1}({\Omega_{o}})\times\dot{H}^{1}({\Omega_{e}})}\lesssim\lVert h\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

It is easy to verify that
f
solves
\tdiv f=0
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\tcurl(\mu^{-1}f)=h
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

It remains to show uniqueness. This follows from the fact that we can replace the original control
\lVert f_{1}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\lesssim\lVert h\rVert_{L^{2}}
with
\lVert f_{1}\rVert_{H^{1}}\lesssim\lVert h\rVert_{L^{2}}
due to the compact support of
h
in
{\Omega_{c}}
(
Section

2.1.3
).
In particular, if
f
and
g
are two functions in
\mathbb{B}^{1}
solving
\tcurl(\mu^{-1}f)=\tcurl(\mu^{-1}g)=h
, then
f-g
satisfies

\lVert f-g\rVert_{H^{1}({\Omega_{i}})\times H^{1}({\Omega_{o}})\times H^{1}({\Omega_{e}})}\lesssim 0\implies f-g=0.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Lemma 2.2.

We apply
Section

2.1.4
and deduce that

\lVert B\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}({\Omega_{i}})\times\dot{H}^{1}({\Omega_{o}})\times\dot{H}^{1}({\Omega_{e}})}\lesssim\lVert\tcurl\mu^{-1}B\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\lVert\tcurl\mu^{-1}B\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{c}})}.

The desired inequality follows.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

We have in fact shown (for
B\in\mathbb{B}^{1}
) the stronger statement

\lVert\tcurl(\mu^{-1}B)\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\sim\lVert B\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{1}}.

3.
Weak solutions

In this section we define Leray-Hopf-type weak solutions for the geodynamo problem, and we prove existence of such solutions (given suitable assumptions on initial conditions, boundary conditions, and forcing terms).

We begin by deriving weak forms of each equation in order to motivate our definition of weak solutions and to highlight the role of the electric field in the exterior. Assuming there exists a piecewise smooth solution
B,(u,\omega),\theta
to the geodynamo problem, we test
\partial_{t}B
against
\mu^{-1}A
where
A
is a piecewise smooth test function
A
satisfying the same transmission,
\tdiv
-free, and
\tcurl
-free conditions as
B
.

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{t}B\cdot\mu^{-1}A\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{{\Omega_{o}}}\operatorname{curl}(u\times B)\cdot\mu^{-1}A\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{{\Omega_{i}}}\operatorname{curl}(u\times B)\cdot\mu^{-1}A\,\mathrm{d}x\\
=\int_{{\Omega_{e}}}-\operatorname{curl}E\cdot\mu^{-1}A\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{{\Omega_{o}}}\operatorname{curl}(\sigma^{-1}\operatorname{curl}\mu^{-1}B)\cdot\mu^{-1}A\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{{\Omega_{i}}}\operatorname{curl}(\sigma^{-1}\operatorname{curl}\mu^{-1}B)\cdot\mu^{-1}A\,\mathrm{d}x.

We now perform some integration by parts explicitly. First, for the frozen-in flux terms

-\int_{{\Omega_{o}}}\operatorname{curl}(u\times B)\cdot\mu^{-1}A\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{{\Omega_{i}}}\operatorname{curl}(u\times B)\cdot\mu^{-1}A\,\mathrm{d}x\\
=\int_{\partial{\Omega_{o}}}\left[(u\times B)\times\mu^{-1}A\right]\cdot n\,\mathrm{d}S+\int_{\partial{\Omega_{i}}}\left[(u\times B)\times\mu^{-1}A\right]\cdot n\,\mathrm{d}S\\
-\int_{{\Omega_{o}}}\operatorname{curl}\mu^{-1}A\cdot(u\times B)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{{\Omega_{i}}}\operatorname{curl}\mu^{-1}A\cdot(u\times B)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
u=0
on
\partial{\Omega_{c}}
, the boundary integral on
\partial{\Omega_{c}}
disappears. Similarly, we can re-write the remaining boundary integral

\int_{\partial{\Omega_{i}}}[(u\times B)\times\mu^{-1}A]\cdot n\,\mathrm{d}S=\int_{\partial{\Omega_{i}}}(\mu_{i}^{-1}A_{i}\cdot u_{i})(B_{i}\cdot n)-(\mu_{o}^{-1}A_{o}\cdot u_{o})(B_{o}\cdot n)\,\mathrm{d}S

and apply the transmission conditions
eqs.

1.4c
,
1.4d
and
1.4a
(using in addition the fact that
u
is tangential on
\partial{\Omega_{i}}
) to conclude that this term also vanishes.

Continuing with the magnetic diffusion terms,

-\int_{{\Omega_{o}}}\operatorname{curl}(\sigma^{-1}\operatorname{curl}\mu^{-1}B)\cdot\mu^{-1}A\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{{\Omega_{i}}}\operatorname{curl}(\sigma^{-1}\operatorname{curl}\mu^{-1}B)\cdot\mu^{-1}A\,\mathrm{d}x\\
=-\int_{\partial{\Omega_{o}}}(\sigma^{-1}\operatorname{curl}\mu^{-1}B\times\mu^{-1}A)\cdot n\,\mathrm{d}S-\int_{\partial{\Omega_{i}}}(\sigma^{-1}\operatorname{curl}\mu^{-1}B\times\mu^{-1}A)\cdot n\,\mathrm{d}S\\
-\int_{{\Omega_{o}}}\frac{1}{\sigma}\operatorname{curl}\mu^{-1}B\cdot\operatorname{curl}\mu^{-1}A\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{{\Omega_{i}}}\frac{1}{\sigma}\operatorname{curl}\mu^{-1}B\cdot\operatorname{curl}\mu^{-1}A\,\mathrm{d}x.

Again, we apply
eqs.

1.4d
and
1.4e
combined with the fact that
\sigma^{-1}\tcurl\mu^{-1}B\times n
is tangential to deduce that the boundary integrals on
\partial{\Omega_{i}}
cancel. Finally, we account for the term involving the electric field.

\int_{{\Omega_{e}}}\operatorname{curl}E\cdot\mu^{-1}A\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\partial{\Omega_{e}}}(E\times\mu^{-1}A)\cdot n\,\mathrm{d}S+\int_{{\Omega_{e}}}E\cdot\operatorname{curl}\mu^{-1}A\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\partial{\Omega_{e}}}-(E\times n)\cdot\mu^{-1}A\,\mathrm{d}S.

We have used that
\tcurl\mu^{-1}A=0
on
{\Omega_{e}}
. Using
eqs.

1.5d
and
1.5e
, the remaining boundary integral on
\partial{\Omega_{e}}
cancels with the previous boundary integral on
\partial{\Omega_{c}}
. All together, this yields

(3.1)

\left\langle\partial_{t}B,A\right\rangle_{\mathbb{B}^{0}}+d(B,A)=\left\langle u\times B,\tcurl\mu^{-1}A\right\rangle_{{\Omega_{c}}}

which will be the equation used to define the weak solution for the magnetic field. Observe that in the same way pressure is eliminated from the Navier-Stokes equations after testing against a divergence-free field, so is the electric field eliminated when testing against a field
A
that is curl-free on
{\Omega_{e}}
.

Testing
\partial_{t}u
with a
\rho v
where
v
is smooth and satisfying the same divergence-free constraint and boundary conditions as
u
(except with
v=\alpha\times x
on
\partial{\Omega_{i}}
) produces

\langle\partial_{t}u,\rho v\rangle+\langle u\cdot\nabla u,\rho v\rangle+\langle\nabla p,\rho v\rangle=\langle\nu\Delta u,\rho v\rangle+\langle\rho^{-1}\mu^{-1}\tcurl B\times B,\rho v\rangle+\langle\theta g\widehat{x},\rho v\rangle-\langle Le_{3}\times u,\rho v\rangle.

All inner products above are in
{\Omega_{o}}
.
After integrating by parts and applying boundary conditions this becomes

(3.2)

\langle\partial_{t}u,\rho v\rangle_{\Omega_{o}}-\rho b(u,v,u)+\rho\nu a(u,v)=-\mu_{o}^{-1}b(B,v,B)+\langle\theta g\widehat{x},v\rangle_{\Omega_{o}}-\langle Le_{3}\times u,\rho v\rangle_{\Omega_{o}}\\
+\int_{\partial{\Omega_{o}}}\mu_{o}^{-1}(B\cdot v)(B\cdot n)\,\mathrm{d}S+\rho\nu\int_{\partial{\Omega_{o}}}v\cdot(\nabla u)n\,\mathrm{d}S

where
b(u,v,w)=\int_{\Omega_{o}}[(u\cdot\nabla)v]\cdot w\,\mathrm{d}x
is the standard trilinear form associated with the Navier-Stokes equations.
In order to remove the surface integrals, we test the equation for
\omega
with
\alpha
. We use that
u=\omega\times\widehat{x}
and
v=\alpha\times\widehat{x}
on
\partial{\Omega_{i}}
.

J\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\omega\cdot\alpha=-(JLe_{3}\times\omega)\cdot\alpha+\int_{\partial{\Omega_{i}}}(2\rho\nu D^{s}(u)+\mu_{o}^{-1}B_{o}\otimes B_{o})n\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}S.

Integration by parts and application of boundary conditions leads to the following.

(3.3)

J\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\omega\cdot\alpha=-(JLe_{3}\times\omega)\cdot\alpha\\
+\int_{\partial{\Omega_{i}}}\mu_{0}^{-1}(B\cdot n)(B\cdot v)\,\mathrm{d}S+\rho\nu\int_{\partial{\Omega_{i}}}v\cdot(\nabla u)n\,\mathrm{d}S-\rho\nu\int_{\Omega_{o}}\nabla v:\nabla u^{T}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Combining
eqs.

3.3
and
3.2
, canceling matching terms, and using that
2a^{S}(u,v)=\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle+\langle\nabla u,\nabla^{T}v\rangle
leads to an equation without boundary integrals.

(3.4)

\langle\partial_{t}(u,\omega),(v,\alpha)\rangle_{\mathbb{U}^{0}}-\rho b(u,v,u)+2\rho\nu a^{S}(u,v)\\
=-\mu_{o}^{-1}b(B,v,B)+\langle\theta g\widehat{x},\rho v\rangle_{\Omega_{o}}-\langle Le_{3}\times u,\rho v\rangle_{{\Omega_{o}}}-(JLe_{3}\times\omega)\cdot\alpha.

Testing
\partial_{t}\tau

against a smooth test function
\varphi
is relatively straightforward. After integrating by parts and applying boundary conditions the equation is

(3.5)

\langle\partial_{t}\tau,\varphi\rangle-b(u,\varphi,\tau)+\kappa a(\tau,\varphi)=-b(u,\theta^{c},\varphi)-\kappa a(\theta^{c},\varphi)-\kappa\langle f_{b},\varphi\rangle_{\partial{\Omega_{i}}}+\langle f,\varphi\rangle.

In order to derive a formal energy identity, one sums
eqs.

3.1
,
3.4
and
3.5
with
A=B
,
(v,\alpha)=(\omega,u)
and
\varphi=\tau
. The resulting identity is found after carefully canceling terms and using boundary conditions. These detailed cancellations will be shown in the analysis of the Galerkin approximating solutions later in this section.

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\left(\lVert\mu^{-1/2}B\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\lVert\rho^{1/2}u\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{2}+J|\omega|^{2}+\lVert\tau\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{2}\right)\\
+\left(\lVert\sigma^{-1/2}\tcurl\mu^{-1}B\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{c}})}^{2}+2\rho\nu\lVert D^{S}(u)\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{2}+\kappa\lVert\nabla\tau\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{2}\right)\\
=\langle\theta^{c}g\widehat{x},\rho u\rangle_{\Omega_{o}}+\langle\tau g\widehat{x},\rho u\rangle_{\Omega_{o}}-b(u,\theta^{c},\tau)-\kappa a(\theta^{c},\tau)\\
-\kappa\langle f_{b},\tau\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\partial{\Omega_{i}}),H^{1/2}(\partial{\Omega_{i}})}+\langle f,\tau\rangle_{(\mathbb{T}^{1})^{\prime},\mathbb{T}^{1}}.

This
a-priori
energy identity is indicative that the system will be amenable to Galerkin analysis, since (after further estimates) it is clear there will be at most exponential growth in time of the magnetic energy, kinetic energy, and temperature energy, as well as control over derivatives of the solution due to magnetic dissipation, viscosity in the momentum, and dissipation in the buoyancy.
We are now prepared to rigorously define a weak solution.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
B_{0}\in\mathbb{B}^{0}
,
(u_{0},\omega_{0})\in\mathbb{U}^{0}
,
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{T}^{0}
. A weak solution to the
eqs.

1.1
,
1.2
,
1.3
,
1.4
and
1.5
is a a triple
(B,(u,\omega),\tau)\in L^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{B}^{1}\times\mathbb{U}^{1}\times\mathbb{T}^{1})\cap L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{B}^{0}\times\mathbb{U}^{0}\times\mathbb{T}^{0})
such that
for any test triple

3.1.
Existence

In this section, we prove the following rigorous version of
Theorem

.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose

,
(u_{0},\omega_{0})\in\mathbb{U}^{0}
,
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{T}^{0}
,

,

Our overall strategy will be to use Galerkin approximation. We will first find a sequence of approximate solutions to finite dimensional approximating problems, and then we will show that there exists a limit of that sequence satisfying

3
. The functional framework is designed to be amenable to Galerkin approximation, in that an energy inequality will be satisfied at the level of the approximations, and in that relevant embeddings of function spaces will be available to deduce convergence to a true solution. In particular, we will need to use the Korn and Biot-Savart-type inequalities (

2
and
2
) to deal with the nonlinear terms. We will also need to use the Aubin-Lions lemma to show strong convergence on a subsequence of the approximations in

3.1.1.
Galerkin approximation

Because

\mathbb{T}^{1}
are separable Hilbert spaces, they each have a countable orthogonal basis, which we enumerate as

be the spans of the first

to be defined as orthogonal projection in the appropriate

for

as

(3.10b)

(3.10c)

are chosen such that the following equations are satisfied

(3.11b)

3.1.2.
Galerkin energy

The energy of an approximate solution
(B_{m},(u,\omega)_{m},\tau_{m})
is given by testing against
(\mu^{-1}B_{m},\rho(u,\omega)_{m},\tau_{m})
according to

eqs.

3.11a
,
3.11b
and
3.11c
and summing them all together.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\lVert\mu^{-1/2}B_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\lVert\sigma^{-1/2}\tcurl\mu^{-1}B_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\lVert\rho^{1/2}u_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{2}+J\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}|\omega_{m}|^{2}+2\rho\nu a^{S}(u_{m},u_{m})+\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\lVert\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{2}+\kappa a(\tau_{m},\tau_{m})

\displaystyle\hskip 34.14322pt=\underbrace{\langle u_{m}\times B_{m},\tcurl\mu^{-1}B_{m}\rangle_{\Omega_{c}}}_{\text{(c)}}+\underbrace{\rho b(u_{m},u_{m},u_{m})}_{\text{(a)}}-\underbrace{\mu_{o}^{-1}b(B_{m},u_{m},B_{m})}_{\text{(c)}}

\displaystyle\hskip 45.52458pt+\langle\theta_{m}g\widehat{x},\rho u_{m}\rangle_{\Omega_{o}}-\underbrace{\langle Le_{3}\times u_{m},\rho u_{m}\rangle_{\Omega_{o}}}_{\text{(b)}}-\underbrace{J(Le_{3}\times\omega_{m})\cdot(\omega_{m})}_{\text{(b)}}+\underbrace{b(u_{m},\tau_{m},\tau_{m})}_{\text{(a)}}

\displaystyle\hskip 45.52458pt-b(u_{m},\theta^{c},\tau_{m})-\kappa a(\theta^{c},\tau_{m})-\kappa\langle f_{b},\tau_{m}\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\partial{\Omega_{i}}),H^{1/2}(\partial{\Omega_{i}})}+\langle f,\tau_{m}\rangle_{(\mathbb{T}^{1})^{\prime},\mathbb{T}^{1}}.

We make the following simplifications.

•

We use that
b(u_{m},\cdot,\cdot)
is antisymmetric in the last two arguments since
u_{m}\in H^{1}_{\tdiv}
,
u_{m}=0
on
\partial{\Omega_{c}}
, and
u_{m}\cdot n=0
on
\partial{\Omega_{i}}
. This eliminates the terms labeled (a).

•

We use that
\langle a\times b,a\rangle=0
to eliminate terms labeled (b).

•

The last simplification is to show
\langle u_{m}\times B_{m},\tcurl\mu^{-1}B_{m}\rangle_{\Omega_{c}}
and
\mu^{-1}b(B_{m},u_{m},B_{m})
(labeled (c) ) cancel each other out. We first integrate by parts (and use the boundary conditions from the definition of
\mathbb{B}^{1}
) to deduce

\langle u_{m}\times B_{m},\tcurl\mu^{-1}B_{m}\rangle_{\Omega_{c}}=\langle\tcurl(u_{m}\times B_{m}),\mu^{-1}B_{m}\rangle_{\Omega_{i}}+\langle\tcurl(u_{m}\times B_{m}),\mu^{-1}B_{m}\rangle_{\Omega_{o}}.

Next we expand

\tcurl(u_{m}\times B_{m})=u_{m}\tdiv B_{m}-B_{m}\tdiv u_{m}+(B_{m}\cdot\nabla)u_{m}-(u_{m}\cdot\nabla)B_{m}.

The first two terms vanish due to incompressibility. The last also vanishes when we consider the
L^{2}
inner product over
{\Omega_{o}}
or
{\Omega_{i}}
with
\mu^{-1}B_{m}
due to antisymmetry. We are left with

\langle u_{m}\times B_{m},\tcurl\mu^{-1}B_{m}\rangle_{\Omega_{c}}=\mu_{o}^{-1}\left\langle(B_{m}\cdot\nabla)u_{m},B_{m}\right\rangle_{\Omega_{o}}+\mu_{i}^{-1}\left\langle(B_{m}\cdot\nabla)u_{m},B_{m}\right\rangle_{\Omega_{i}}.

At this point we must show that
\left\langle(B_{m}\cdot\nabla)u_{m},B_{m}\right\rangle_{\Omega_{i}}=0
. To do so, we use that
u_{m}=\omega_{m}\times x
on
{\Omega_{i}}
:

\int_{\Omega_{i}}(B_{m}\cdot\nabla)\left(\omega\times x\right)\cdot B_{m}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega_{i}}\left(\omega\times B_{m}\right)\cdot B_{m}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

We now have the following energy expression.

(3.12)

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\left(\lVert\mu^{-1/2}B_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\rho\lVert u_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{2}+J|\omega_{m}|^{2}+\lVert\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{2}\right)\\
+\lVert\sigma^{-1}\tcurl\mu^{-1}B_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{c}})}^{2}+2\rho\nu\lVert D^{S}(u_{m})\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{2}+\kappa\lVert\nabla\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{2}\\
=\langle\theta^{c}g\widehat{x},\rho u_{m}\rangle_{\Omega_{o}}+\langle\tau_{m}g\widehat{x},\rho u_{m}\rangle_{\Omega_{o}}-b(u_{m},\theta^{c},\tau_{m})\\
-\kappa a(\theta^{c},\tau_{m})-\kappa\langle f_{b},\tau_{m}\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\partial{\Omega_{i}}),H^{1/2}(\partial{\Omega_{i}})}+\langle f,\tau_{m}\rangle_{(\mathbb{T}^{1})^{\prime},\mathbb{T}^{1}}.

Finally we need to estimate the right hand side of
eq.

3.12
. Recall that
\theta^{c}
is an extension into
{\Omega_{o}}
of the boundary conditions
\theta=\theta_{b}
on
\partial{\Omega_{c}}
and
\nabla\theta\cdot n=0
on
\partial{\Omega_{i}}
. In particular, we can pick this extension to satisfy
\lVert\nabla\theta^{c}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}\lesssim\lVert\theta_{b}\rVert_{H^{1/2}(\partial{\Omega_{o}})}

•

|\langle\theta^{c}g\widehat{x},\rho u_{m}\rangle_{\Omega_{o}}|\lesssim\lVert\rho u_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}\lVert\theta^{c}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}\lVert g\widehat{x}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{o}})}\lesssim_{\rho,g,\theta_{b}}\lVert u_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}
.

•

|\langle\tau_{m}g\widehat{x},\rho u_{m}\rangle_{\Omega_{o}}|\lesssim\lVert\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}\lVert\rho u_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}\lVert g\widehat{x}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{o}})}\lesssim_{\rho,g}\lVert\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}\lVert u_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}

•

Using Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality,

|b(u_{m},\theta^{c},\tau_{m})|\leq\lVert u_{m}\rVert_{L^{4}({\Omega_{o}})}\lVert\nabla\theta^{c}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}\lVert\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{4}({\Omega_{o}})}\\
\lesssim_{\theta_{b}}\lVert u_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{1-d/4}\lVert\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{1-d/4}\lVert\nabla u_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{d/4}\lVert\nabla\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{d/4}.

•

|\kappa a(\theta^{c},\tau_{m})|\lesssim\kappa\lVert\nabla\theta^{c}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}\lVert\nabla\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}\lesssim_{\kappa,\theta_{b}}\lVert\nabla\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}

•

Using a trace inequality for
\tau_{m}

|\kappa\langle f_{b},\tau_{m}\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\partial{\Omega_{i}}),H^{1/2}(\partial{\Omega_{i}})}|\lesssim\kappa\lVert f_{b}\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\partial{\Omega_{i}})}\lVert\tau_{m}\rVert_{H^{1/2}(\partial{{\Omega_{i}}})}\lesssim_{\kappa,f_{b}}\lVert\nabla\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}.

•

|\langle f,\tau_{m}\rangle_{(\mathbb{T}^{1})^{\prime},\mathbb{T}^{1}}|\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{(\mathbb{T}^{1})^{\prime}}\lVert\tau_{m}\rVert_{H^{1}({\Omega_{o}})}\lesssim_{f}\lVert\nabla\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}
.

Using these estimates, it follows that

(3.13)

\frac{d}{dt}F_{m}+G_{m}\lesssim_{\rho,\kappa,g,\theta_{b},f_{b},f}\lVert u_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}+\lVert\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}\lVert u_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}+\lVert\nabla\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}+\lVert\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}\\
+\lVert u_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{1-d/4}\lVert\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{1-d/4}\lVert\nabla u_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{d/4}\lVert\nabla\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{d/4}

where
F_{m}
and
G_{m}
are defined as the corresponding quantities in
eq.

3.12

F_{m}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\lVert\mu^{-1/2}B_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\rho\lVert u_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{2}+J|\omega_{m}|^{2}+\lVert\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{2}\right),

G_{m}=\lVert\sigma^{-1}\tcurl\mu^{-1}B_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{c}})}^{2}+2\rho\nu\lVert D^{S}(u_{m})\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{2}+\kappa\lVert\nabla\tau_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}^{2}.

By applying Young’s inequality and

by
\chi(t)
. Then, for any

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}B_{m},\chi\mu^{-1}A_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}+\chi\langle\sigma^{-1}\tcurl\mu^{-1}B_{m},\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\rangle_{\Omega_{c}}-\chi\langle u_{m}\times B_{m},\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\rangle_{\Omega_{c}}\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

Integrating by parts in time yields

\underbrace{\langle B_{m}(0),\chi(0)\mu^{-1}A_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}_{(\text{a})}-\int_{0}^{T}\chi^{\prime}\underbrace{\langle B_{m},\mu^{-1}A_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}_{(\text{b})}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
+\int_{0}^{T}\chi\underbrace{\langle\sigma^{-1}\tcurl\mu^{-1}B_{m},\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\rangle_{\Omega_{c}}}_{(\text{c})}-\chi\underbrace{\langle u_{m}\times B_{m},\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\rangle_{\Omega_{c}}}_{(\text{d})}\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

As we send

, it is clear that the linear terms (a), (b), and (c) converge to

\langle B_{0},\chi(0)\mu^{-1}A_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}-\int_{0}^{T}\chi^{\prime}\langle B,\mu^{-1}A_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}+\chi\langle\sigma^{-1}\tcurl\mu^{-1}B,\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\rangle_{\Omega_{c}}\,\mathrm{d}t

due to the weak convergence of
B_{m}
and
\tcurl\mu^{-1}B_{m}
in
L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
.

For term (d), we require convergence of
u_{m}
or
B_{m}
in
L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
(along a further subsequence, again without relabeling).

Lemma 3.5

(restate=lem:compactness)
.

The sequence
\{u_{m}\}
(where
u_{m}
in this case are extended to
{\Omega_{i}}
according to
eq.

1.2b
) converges strongly in
L^{2}_{t}L^{2}({\Omega_{c}})
. Similarly, the sequence
\{B_{m}\}
converges strongly in
L^{2}_{t}L^{2}({\Omega_{c}})
.

The proof follows standard techniques using fractional derivatives and fractional Aubin-Lions lemma. For this reason, the proof is included in the appendix (
Section

A.1
).

We compute

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{c}}u_{m}\times B_{m}\cdot\chi\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\\
\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{c}}(u_{m}-u)\times B_{m}\cdot\chi\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{c}}(\chi\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\times u)\cdot B_{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Observe that
\chi\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\times u\in L^{2}_{t}(\mathbb{B}^{1})^{\prime}
by picking some
\psi\in L^{2}_{t}\mathbb{B}^{1}
and estimating

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{c}}(\chi\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\times u)\cdot\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\chi\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}(H^{1}({\Omega_{i}})\times H^{1}({\Omega_{o}}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}\mathbb{B}^{1}}

where we used the embedding
Section

2
.
Due to the weak convergence
B_{m}\xrightharpoonup[L^{2}_{t}\mathbb{B}^{1}]{}B
we have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{c}}(\chi\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\times u)\cdot B_{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\to\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{c}}(\chi\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\times u)\cdot B\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

On the other hand, we can estimate

\left\lvert\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{c}}(u_{m}-u)\times B_{m}\cdot\chi\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right\rvert\leq\int_{0}^{T}\lVert u_{m}-u\rVert_{L^{4}({\Omega_{c}})}\lVert B_{m}\rVert_{L^{4}({\Omega_{c}})}\lVert\chi\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{c}})}\,\mathrm{d}t

to which we can apply Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality and strong convergence of
u_{m}
in
L^{2}_{t}L^{2}({\Omega_{c}})
to deduce

\int_{0}^{T}\lVert u_{m}-u\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{c}})}^{1-d/4}\lVert u-u_{m}\rVert_{H^{1}({\Omega_{c}})}^{d/4}\lVert B_{m}\rVert_{H^{1}({\Omega_{i}})\times H^{1}({\Omega_{o}})}\lVert\chi\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Omega_{c}})}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
\leq\lVert u_{m}-u\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{1-d/4}\lVert u-u_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}^{d/4}\lVert B_{m}\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}\mathbb{B}^{1}}\lVert\chi\tcurl\mu^{-1}A_{j}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}({\Omega_{c}})}\lesssim\lVert u_{m}-u\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{1-d/4}\to 0.

We can immediately generalize to the case where
A
is a finite linear combination of
A_{j}

\langle B,\chi(0)\mu^{-1}A\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}-\int_{0}^{T}{\langle B,\chi^{\prime}A\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}+\chi\langle\sigma^{-1}\tcurl\mu^{-1}B,\tcurl\mu^{-1}A\rangle_{\Omega_{c}}-\chi{\langle u\times B,\tcurl\mu^{-1}A\rangle_{\Omega_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}t=0

and then general

follows from density and boundedness of each term above as a functional on
\mathbb{B}^{1}
. This completes the proof that
eq.

3.6a
is satisfied by
(B,(u,\omega),\tau)
for any test function in
\mathcal{C}^{1}_{0}([0,T);\mathbb{B}^{1})
.

Next, let us check that

3.6b
is satisfied by our limit. This time we multiply

3.11b
with
\chi(t)
for arbitrary

.

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}u_{m},\chi\rho v_{j}\rangle_{\Omega_{o}}+\chi J\frac{d}{dt}\omega_{m}\cdot\alpha_{j}+\chi\rho b(u_{m},u_{m},v_{j})+2\rho\nu a^{S}(u_{m},v_{j})\,\mathrm{d}t\\

As before, we integrate the time derivative by parts. All the linear terms converge using weak convergence of

and

3.6b

(B,(u,\omega),\tau)
.

Finally, we test

Remark 3.6
.

Although

Lemma 3.8
.

Let

3.8

Proof.

3.6a

(3.17)

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\langle B,\mu^{-1}A\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\langle B,\mu^{-1}A\rangle_{(\mathbb{B}^{1})^{\prime},\mathbb{B}^{1}}=\langle f^{B},A\rangle_{(\mathbb{B}^{1})^{\prime},\mathbb{B}^{1}}

(in the distributional sense in time) for any

. We define

by

where
\mathcal{N}:H^{1}({\Omega_{c}})\times\mathbb{B}^{1}\to(\mathbb{B}^{1})^{\prime}

\langle\mathcal{N}(u,B),A\rangle=\int_{\Omega_{c}}(u\times B)(\tcurl\mu^{-1}A)\,\mathrm{d}x

\|\mathcal{N}(B,u)\|_{(\mathbb{B}^{1})^{\prime}}=\sup_{\|A\|_{\mathbb{B}^{1}}=1}\left\lvert\int_{{\Omega_{c}}}(u\times B)\cdot\operatorname{curl}\mu^{-1}A\,\mathrm{d}x\right\rvert\\

We use control of
u,B
in
L^{2}_{t}H^{1}({\Omega_{c}})
to deduce

\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\mathcal{N}(B,u)\rVert_{(\mathbb{B}^{1})^{\prime}}+\lVert\mathcal{D}B\rVert_{(\mathbb{B}^{1})^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

By Lemma 1.1 in
[
34
, Chapter 3]
,
this means that
B
is continuous with values in
(\mathbb{B}^{1})^{\prime}
. Therefore it makes sense to test
eq.

3.17
with some
\chi\in\mathcal{D}([0,T))
such that

=-\int_{0}^{T}\langle f^{B},A\rangle_{(\mathbb{B}^{1})^{\prime},\mathbb{B}^{1}}\chi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

3.6a

for every

Now we detail the proof for

. We repackage the velocity equation

3.6b

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\langle(u,\omega),(v,\alpha)\rangle_{\mathbb{U}^{0}}=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\langle(u,\omega),(v,\alpha)\rangle_{(\mathbb{U}^{1})^{\prime},\mathbb{U}^{1}}=\langle f^{u},(v,\alpha)\rangle_{(\mathbb{U}^{1})^{\prime},\mathbb{U}^{1}}

by

\langle f^{u},(v,\alpha)\rangle_{(\mathbb{U}^{1})^{\prime},\mathbb{U}^{1}}=\langle\rho\theta g\widehat{x}-\rho Le_{3}\times u,v\rangle_{L^{2}({\Omega_{o}})}-JL(e_{3}\times\omega)\cdot\alpha\\

Here,

To verify that

(3.22)

\lVert{f}^{u}\rVert_{(\mathbb{U}^{1})^{\prime}}=\sup_{\lVert(v,\alpha)\rVert_{\mathbb{U}^{1}}=1}\left\lvert\langle{f}^{u},(v,\alpha)\rangle_{(\mathbb{U}^{1})^{\prime},\mathbb{U}^{1}}\right\rvert\\
