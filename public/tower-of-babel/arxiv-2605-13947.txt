Conformal defects and Goldstone bosons in Anti-de Sitter space Preprint: APS/123-QED
Lorenzo Bianchi, Elia de Sabbata, Marco Meineri

Abstract
We study local quantum field theories in Anti-de Sitter (AdS) space, with boundary conditions that break some of the bulk isometries. Specifically, we focus on conformal defects and we prove that their spectrum supports a displacement operator of protected dimension, despite the non-local nature of the conformal theory living at the boundary of AdS. If the defect breaks a global symmetry, a tilt operator is also present. The existence of a displacement was conjectured in 21 for Wilson loops in Yang-Mills theories in AdS. Our proof is valid in general and applies, in particular, to defects in long-range models, as we discuss in various examples. In the bulk, the modes sourced by the protected operators have Compton wavelength of order of the AdS radius: they constitute the AdS analogue of the Goldstone bosons for the spontaneous breaking of the corresponding symmetries.

III
Examples

One can verify the presence of the displacement in many examples. The case of a Wilson loop
W
in a gauge theory is treated in detail in

\partial

The double-trace interface described in

IV
Conclusions

Let us first comment on the implications of our result for the physics of confinement in AdS. On one hand, the existence of the displacement operator puts the study of string excitations in AdS via the corresponding Ward identities on solid ground. On the other hand, our proof shows that the displacement is generic, and in this sense it is not an order parameter for confinement. Any QFT in AdS with a local defect on the boundary supports this excitation, including, for instance, Abelian gauge theories. Only screened defects
1
—
i.e.
defects invisible to local bulk and boundary operators—have no displacement operator. This implies, for instance, that string breaking in gauge theories with appropriate matter is realized as screening in the boundary CFT. One way to interpret the generic presence of the displacement is that the flux tube always exists at the scale of the curvature. This suggests that better order parameters might be hidden in the spacetime dependence of expectation values or in the scattering amplitudes of the Goldstone bosons, which should exhibit two dimensional dynamics for a confining theory up to scales parametrically larger than the AdS scale.

It is perhaps useful to comment explicitly on the codimension one case. An interface on
\partial
AdS which is not attached to a brane of course obeys the theorem. Such a defect can never be factorizing: the displacement which obeys (
9
) with the unique bulk stress tensor appears in the boundary OPE of operators with a vev on both sides of the interface, whose connected correlator, therefore, does not vanish. Vice versa, boundary conditions are always associated with explicit breaking of the bulk isometries, typically via an end-of-the-world brane
32
;
48
. At the other extreme, an interface which can be tuned to be topological can only exist in a theory with a marginal operator, or extend in the bulk non-trivially
33

4
4

4

The double-trace interface described in section
III

41
becomes topological only at the BF bound. In this case, the argument in
33
correctly predicts the existence of a marginal operator which does not need to be exactly marginal.
.

Turning our attention away from AdS, the proof presented here, with minimal modifications, shows the existence of a displacement operator on conformal defects embedded in other conformal defects in generic CFTs. Under the same assumptions as reference
33
, this implies that continuous families of defects in a fixed CFT are generated by marginal defect operators, unless the defect is attached to (topological) surfaces. In the latter case, the interface between two defects in the same family can extend in the bulk as a (non-topological) defect on the topological surface: this happens, for instance, in the case of a monodromy defect
6
;
34
.

Our proof applies to defects in long-range CFTs reached in the IR from a local relevant deformation of Generalized Free Fields
19
;
45
;
4
. Indeed, these theories are equivalent to free fields in AdS with boundary couplings. One may ask whether protected operators associated to broken symmetries exist for defects in non-local CFTs without a local higher-dimensional embedding. Intuitively, these operators reflect the ability to couple the defect to the appropriate background fields in a local way,
i.e.
such that their variation inserts the corresponding local operator in the path integral
39
;
7
. The role of the higher-dimensional theory with its conserved currents and its local DOE is to give a sharp criterion for this possibility.

Finally, the fact that the displacement multiplet alone accounts for the breaking of all relevant isometries is the rigorous AdS counterpart to the inverse Higgs constraint in flat space
49
;
42
;
30
;
37
;
50
. It would be interesting to explore whether, in the flat space limit, our construction can provide an equally rigorous proof of the existence of Goldstone modes that propagate in
p+1
dimensions, without assuming the presence of a
p+1-
dimensional classical surface as in the standard CCWZ treatment
13
;
2
.

VI
Convergence of the DOE of
Q_{\xi}

In this section, we show that the correlator
\braket{\phi\,Q_{\xi}}
can be computed by integrating the DOE of the stress tensor term by term. While the DOE converges absolutely in the region of integration, this is not sufficient in order to exchange the
\rho
-expansion and the
\theta
-integral: a sufficient condition is met if the integral of the series of absolute values converges (Fubini-Tonelli theorem). More directly, we will use the dominated convergence theorem to bound the partial sums. The integral along the parallel directions
x_{a}
is harmless and we discuss it last.

The required property can be shown to hold by means of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Since the details of the
SO(d)
representation of
T_{\mu\nu}
are irrelevant to the argument and clutter notation, we will replace the stress tensor with a bulk scalar operator
\Phi
. Hence, consider the following correlator and its DOE:

\begin{split}&\braket{\phi(w^{\prime})\,\Phi(x,\hat{y},\rho,\theta)}=\sum_{\hat{O}_{n}}\mathcal{N}_{\hat{\Delta}_{n}}(\theta)\rho^{\hat{\Delta}_{n}}\braket{\phi(w^{\prime})\,\hat{O}_{n}(x,\hat{y})}\,,\\
&\hat{O}_{n}(x,\hat{y})\equiv\hat{y}^{i_{1}}\dots\hat{y}^{i_{s_{n}}}\hat{O}_{n,i_{1}\dots i_{s_{n}}}(x)\,,\end{split}

(14)

where
\rho<1,

\hat{O}_{n}
are defect scaling operators and
\phi
is a spectator that lies at
\rho^{\prime}>1
. With an abuse of notation, the argument of
\Phi
is written in the coordinate system (
4
) while
w^{\prime}
denotes Poincaré coordinates as in the main text. As usual, the terms in the
\rho
-expansion are in one-to-one correspondence with coefficients in the expansion of the state
\ket{\Phi(w)}
on the surface at
\rho=1
in a complete orthonormal set. The partial sums are, therefore, all bounded by the product of the norms as follows

\begin{split}&\left|S_{N}\right|\equiv\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{O}_{n}\\
n<N\end{subarray}}\mathcal{N}_{\hat{\Delta}_{n}}(\theta)\rho^{\hat{\Delta}_{n}}\braket{\phi(w^{\prime})\,\hat{O}_{n}(x,\hat{y})}\Big|\\
&\leq\left(\braket{\Phi(x,\hat{y},1/\rho,\theta)\Phi(x,\hat{y},\rho,\theta)}\braket{\phi(w^{\prime})\phi(\mathcal{I}w^{\prime})}\right)^{1/2}~,\end{split}

(15)

where
\mathcal{I}w^{\prime}
is the result of an inversion centered at the point
w_{0}=(x^{a},y^{i}=0,z=0)
,
i.e.

(\mathcal{I}w^{\prime})^{\mu}=(w^{\prime\mu}-w_{0}^{\mu})/(w^{\prime}-w_{0})^{2}+w_{0}^{\mu}.
In particular,
(\mathcal{I}w^{\prime}-w_{0})^{2}=(\rho^{\prime})^{-2}
lies inside the unit sphere. Since the right hand side of (

N
independent, the hypotheses of the dominated convergence theorem are satisfied if the two-point function
\braket{\Phi(x,\hat{y},1/\rho,\theta)\Phi(x,\hat{y},\rho,\theta)}
is integrable in
\theta
with the appropriate measure.

One then notices that the small
\theta

\braket{\Phi(x,\hat{y},1/\rho,\theta)\Phi(x,\hat{y},\rho,\theta)}\sim\theta^{2\Delta}~,

(16)

\Delta
is the scaling dimension of the leading boundary operator in the BOE of
\Phi

15

14

40
, when
\Delta<d
). Convergence of the integral over the transverse sphere
S^{q-1}
is obvious.

Regarding the integral over the directions parallel to the defect, we notice that the dependence of
\braket{\phi(w^{\prime})\Phi(w)}
on the AdS
p+1

\zeta=\frac{\rho\rho^{\prime}}{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}+\rho^{2}+\rho^{\prime}{}^{2}}~.

(17)

Therefore, the

\begin{split}&\left|\int d^{p}x\braket{\phi\,\Phi}-\int d^{p}x\,S_{N}\right|\leq\\
&\leq K(\theta,\hat{y})\rho^{\hat{\Delta}_{N}}\left|\int d^{p}x\left(x^{2}+\rho^{\prime}{}^{2}\right)^{-(\hat{\Delta}_{N}+\kappa)/2}\right|\\
&=K^{\prime}(\theta,\hat{y})\,\frac{(\rho^{\prime})^{p-\hat{\Delta}_{N}-\kappa}}{(\hat{\Delta}_{N}+\kappa)^{p/2}}\left(1+\text{O}\big(\hat{\Delta}_{N}^{-1}\big)\right)\overset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0~.\end{split}

(18)

Here,

are positive. To obtain the inequality, we used that the DOE converges absolutely to bound the tail by the leading term
\propto\rho^{\Delta_{N}}

\rho
and
(x-x^{\prime})

1>\rho_{0}>\rho.
The presence of the positive constant

Of course, one should still check that each term integrates to a finite value, and this fails if there are defect operators of dimension

\rho

have

5

is at the unitarity bound.
, so their leading contribution to the series is

\mathbb{R}^{p}

\theta

IX
AdS conserved current and the tilt operator

Consider a QFT in AdS with a global symmetry associated to the conserved current
J^{\mu}(w)
. As we have done for the displacement, we will show the existence of a protected tilt operator on a conformal defect which breaks the global symmetry at the AdS boundary. For simplicity of notation, we consider a
U(1)
subgroup of the symmetry group. When the CFT on
\partial AdS
contains a conserved current, it sources a gauge field in the AdS interior. We assume this is not the case and that the symmetry is non-locally realized on the boundary,
i.e.
it is generated by a charge operator that extends inside AdS. Technically, this assumption translates in the absence of a scalar operator in the BOE of the current
J^{\mu}
away from the defect. Indeed,
a scalar operator in the BOE of an AdS conserved current is allowed only when the scalar has dimension
\Delta_{\mathcal{O}}=d
and this implies a non-vanishing flux of the current through the AdS boundary. Assuming this does not happen, the
U(1)
symmetry is only broken at the location of the defect.

As in the main text, we will use the Poincaré coordinate system described below equation (
2

4

\begin{split}&J_{\theta}=\rho\Big(\cos\theta J_{z}-\sin\theta\,\hat{y}^{i}J_{i}\Big)\,,\\
&J_{\rho}=\sin\theta J_{z}+\cos\theta\,\hat{y}^{i}J_{i}\,,\\
&J_{\alpha}=\rho\cos\theta\partial_{\alpha}\hat{y}^{i}J_{i}\,,\end{split}

(25)

and

is not affected by the change of coordinates. We consider the scalar contribution to the DOE of the current in the coordinates (
4

\begin{split}&J_{a}\sim\mathcal{N}_{1}(\theta)\bigg(\frac{\rho^{\hat{\Delta}_{\hat{O}}}}{\hat{\Delta}_{\hat{O}}}\partial_{a}\hat{O}(x)+\ \dots\bigg)\,,\\

&J_{\theta}\sim\mathcal{N}_{2}(\theta)\bigg(\rho^{\hat{\Delta}_{\hat{O}}}\hat{O}(x)+\dots\bigg)\,.\end{split}

\mathcal{N}_{1}(\theta)

\begin{split}&J_{z}\sim\mathcal{N}_{z}(\theta)\rho^{\hat{\Delta}_{\hat{O}}-1}\hat{O}(x)+\dots\,,\\

(30)

\hat{D}_{i}

{\mathcal{N}}
is a constant computable in perturbation theory.
Classically, the dimension of this operator is
n\Delta_{\phi}+m\Delta_{\hat{\psi}}=p+1-\varepsilon
, but it receives an anomalous dimension that exactly cancels the
\varepsilon
contribution.
This is due to a factorization property that holds to all orders in perturbation theory:

\langle\tilde{D}_{i}(x)\tilde{D}_{j}(x^{\prime})\rangle=\langle\partial_{i}\hat{\phi}(x)\partial_{j}\hat{\phi}(x^{\prime})\rangle\,\langle\hat{\phi}^{n-1}\hat{\psi}^{m}(x)\hat{\phi}^{n-1}\hat{\psi}^{m}(x^{\prime})\rangle\,.

(39)

The simple reason for this factorization is that all the fields in the interaction term in (
34
) do not carry any
SO(q)
index.
Any diagram in which
\partial_{i}\hat{\phi}(x)
is connected to the interaction through a free propagator
G_{0}(u)
vanishes, since
\partial_{i}G_{0}(x,y)\big|_{y=0}=0
.
Therefore, in any non-vanishing diagram,
\partial_{i}\hat{\phi}(x)
and
\partial_{j}\hat{\phi}(x^{\prime})
must be connected by a free propagator.

For the same reason, the operator
\partial_{i}\hat{\phi}(x)
turns out to be protected with dimension
\hat{\Delta}_{\partial_{i}\hat{\phi}}=\Delta_{\phi}+1
, as its two-point function only has the tree-level diagram.
Finally, the operator
\hat{\phi}^{n-1}\hat{\psi}^{m}(x)
also has protected dimension
\hat{\Delta}_{\hat{\phi}^{n-1}\hat{\psi}^{m}}=p-\Delta_{\phi}
, as is argued in
4
both from the equation of motion and from the defect-channel decomposition of the bulk two-point function
\langle\phi(u)\phi(v)\rangle
.
Then (
39
) implies
\hat{\Delta}_{\hat{D}}=\hat{\Delta}_{\partial_{i}\hat{\phi}}+\hat{\Delta}_{\hat{\phi}^{n-1}\hat{\psi}^{m}}=p+1
.

In this example, one can also derive the modified Ward identity for the stress tensor explicitly.
The boundary field
\phi(u)
is the leading operator in the BOE of the AdS field
\Phi(u,z),

\Phi(u,z)\sim c_{\phi}\ z^{\Delta_{\phi}}\phi(u)+\ldots\,.

(40)

If we regulate the theory by cutting off AdS at
z=\eta>0
, the equation of motion for
\Phi(u,z)
is

\sqrt{g}\big(\Box_{\text{A}dS}-M^{2}\big)\Phi(x,y,z)=\frac{\delta S^{\eta}_{\text{defect}}}{\delta\Phi(x,y,z)}

(41)

where
S^{\eta}_{\text{defect}}
is the second term in (
34
), evaluated on the cutoff surface.
The stress tensor is

T_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial_{\nu}\Phi-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}((\partial\Phi)^{2}+M^{2}\Phi^{2})\,,

(42)

and by using (
41
) we find

\sqrt{g}\hskip 0.5pt\nabla^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\nu}\Phi\frac{\delta S^{\eta}_{\text{defect}}}{\delta\Phi(x,y,z)}~.

(43)

Since the theory is free, using (
41
) and (
40
) we get

\lim_{\eta\to 0}\Big(\nabla_{i}\Phi\frac{\delta S^{\eta}_{\text{defect}}}{\delta\Phi(x,y,z)}\Big)=\frac{nh}{2}\hat{\phi}^{n-1}\partial_{i}\hat{\phi}\,\hat{\psi}^{m}(x)\,\delta(z)\delta^{q}(y)\,,

(44)

so that, at the fixed point,

\sqrt{g}\hskip 0.5pt\nabla^{\mu}T_{\mu i}=\frac{nh_{*}}{2}\tilde{D}_{i}(x)\delta(z)\delta^{q}(y)\,,\

(45)

and therefore
\mathcal{C}_{\hat{D}}={\mathcal{N}}(nh_{*}/2)^{2}
.
As a sanity check, the same value can also be obtained from a CFT computation via

\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\hat{D}}}=\frac{\int d^{p}x^{\prime}\langle\phi^{2}(x,y)\hat{D}_{i}(x^{\prime})\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}}{\partial_{i}\langle\phi^{2}(x,y)\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}}\,.

(46)

The one-point function in the denominator has already been computed in
4
.
For the bulk-to-defect two-point function in the numerator, there is a factorization property analogous to (
39
) that holds at all orders in perturbation theory and reads

\langle\phi^{2}(x,y)\tilde{D}_{i}(x^{\prime})\rangle=\langle\phi(x,y)\partial_{i}\hat{\phi}(x^{\prime})\rangle\langle\phi(x,y)\hat{\phi}^{n-1}\hat{\psi}^{m}(x^{\prime})\rangle\,,

(47)

and both correlators can be evaluated exactly (up to the determination of
h_{*}
) using the same techniques as those used for
\langle\phi^{2}(x,y)\rangle
in
4
.
The result is once again
\mathcal{C}_{\hat{D}}={\mathcal{N}}(nh_{*}/2)^{2}
.

X.2
A defect without a displacement operator

There is another way to construct a non-trivial defect in GFF in flat space: one can again start from the GFF action

S=\int d^{d}u\,\tfrac{1}{2}\phi S^{d}_{2\Delta_{\phi}}\phi\,,

(55)

and then add a
p
-dimensional conformal defect by turning on a nonzero one-point function as follows

\langle\phi(x,y)\rangle=\frac{a_{\phi}}{|y|^{\Delta_{\phi}}}\,.

(56)

Since the theory is quadratic, all correlators are defined through Wick contractions of (
56
) and the free bulk propagator.
This can also be achieved by adding to the action an external source coupled to
\phi
:

S_{\text{source}}=h\int d^{p}xd^{q}y\,\frac{\phi(x,y)}{|y|^{d-\Delta_{\phi}}}\,,

(57)

where
h\propto a_{\phi}

8
8

8

The proportionality constant diverges for
\Delta_{\phi}=p
. In that case, the one-point function (
56
) is produced by the defect
\int d^{p}x\hskip 0.5pt\phi(x)
, which is the (free limit of) the pinning field defect and has a displacement operator.
.
Although this term is not localized on the defect, it still preserves the defect symmetry group.
In this example, the defect spectrum is trivial: there are no anomalous dimensions, so all defect operators have dimension
\Delta_{\mathcal{O}}=m\Delta_{\phi}+n
, with
m,n
integers.
In particular, for generic
\Delta_{\phi}
, there is no displacement operator, as there are no operators with dimension
p+1
.
In this simple model, the absence of a displacement operator is due to the fact that the defect interaction in (
57
), although preserving the defect symmetry group, is not truly localized on the defect but rather spread throughout space.

A natural question is what the AdS counterpart of this example is, and why the argument given in the main text cannot be applied to infer the existence of a protected displacement operator.
It turns out that there are two inequivalent AdS realizations of this boundary non-local defect CFT. They fail to satisfy the assumptions of the theorem for two different reasons. In one case, the stress tensor is not conserved everywhere in the bulk, while in the other the BOE is modified away from the defect, leading to a non-topological charge
Q_{\xi}
.
For both examples, the starting point is the AdS dual of GFF (
36
).

Second realization.
