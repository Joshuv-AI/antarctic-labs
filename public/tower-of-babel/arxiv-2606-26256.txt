The Colored Hofstadter Butterfly as a Many-Body Quantum Hall Phase Diagram
Giovanna Marcelli, Tadahiro Miyao, Domenico Monaco, Stefan Teufel, Marius Wesle

Abstract
We prove that the colored Hofstadter butterfly has a many-body interpretation for a broad class of weakly interacting lattice fermion systems. Starting from a spectral gap of a Hofstadter-like one-particle Hamiltonian at arbitrary magnetic flux b b , we construct an open region in the three-dimensional parameter space ( b , μ , λ ) (b,\mu,\lambda) of magnetic field, chemical potential, and interaction strength on which the infinite-volume interacting system has locally unique gapped ground states. The construction combines quasi-adiabatic continuation in the interaction strength with denominator-independent magnetic perturbation estimates, and therefore covers both commensurate and incommensurate fluxes, where no finite magnetic unit cell exists. On connected uniformly gapped regions meeting the non-interacting plane λ = 0 \lambda=0 , we prove a many-body gap-labeling theorem: the Hall conductivity appearing in the macroscopic Ohm’s law is constant and quantized, satisfying 2 ​ π ​ σ H ∈ ℤ 2\pi\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}\in\mathbb{Z} . Thus the integer colors of the non-interacting Hofstadter butterfly persist as Hall-conductivity labels of interacting quantum Hall phases.

Introduction

The colored Hofstadter butterfly is the paradigmatic phase diagram of the integer quantum Hall effect for non-interacting lattice electrons with one-body Hamiltonian
\mathfrak{h}^{b}
in a magnetic field
b
at chemical potential
\mu
. The phases are the connected components of

\{(b,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\;|\;\mu\notin\sigma(\mathfrak{h}^{b})\},

which are open sets as the map
b\mapsto\sigma(\mathfrak{h}^{b})
is continuous in the Hausdorff metric.
The color of such a component encodes an integer label
2\pi\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}
, which for commensurate magnetic fields
b\in 2\pi\mathbb{Q}
can be computed as the Chern number of a complex vector bundle. The physical meaning of

\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}
is the Hall conductivity within that phase. The study of these and more general models for non-interacting electrons has led to deep and beautiful mathematics, drawing from spectral analysis, topology, non-commutative geometry, number theory, and dynamical systems theory.
A longstanding and in parts still open problem is whether this labeled phase diagram retains a many-body meaning.
This is far from obvious as interactions destroy large parts of the structures basic to the analysis in the non-interacting case.
The question is whether the gapped regions nevertheless persist for interacting systems, and whether their integer labels still give the Hall conductivity appearing in a macroscopic Ohm’s law.

We prove that the answer is positive for a broad class of Hofstadter-like lattice fermion systems on the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with short-range interactions. More precisely, for every
(b_{0},\mu_{0})
with
\mu_{0}\notin\sigma(\mathfrak{h}^{b_{0}})
, we construct an open neighborhood in the three-dimensional parameter space
(b,\mu,\lambda)
of magnetic field, chemical potential, and interaction strength on which the interacting system has locally unique gapped ground states. On connected uniformly gapped regions meeting the non-interacting plane
\lambda=0
, the macroscopic Hall conductivity is constant and satisfies
2\pi\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

A central difficulty, and one of the technical novelties of the paper, is that the magnetic field is treated as a real parameter. The fractal structure of the Hofstadter butterfly stems from the interplay between two spatial scales and the relevant dimensionless parameter is the magnetic flux per unit cell in units of the flux quantum, namely
b/(2\pi)
. If
b/(2\pi)=p/q\in\mathbb{Q}
, the system is periodic with respect to the sublattice
\mathbb{Z}\times q\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, while if
b/(2\pi)\notin\mathbb{Q}
, no finite magnetic unit cell exists. As a consequence, for
b/(2\pi)\notin\mathbb{Q}
no exact periodizations of the model on finite tori exist. To overcome this difficulty, we work directly in the infinite-volume CAR algebra
\mathcal{A}
and obtain estimates that are uniform in the denominators of rational approximants, allowing us to pass to arbitrary fluxes.

2.1
Interacting Hofstadter-like Hamiltonians

We consider periodic, infinitely extended systems of interacting fermions in two spatial dimensions, subject to a transverse magnetic field of strength
b
.
A key feature of our approach is that the notion of periodicity we impose is compatible with arbitrary magnetic field strengths
b\in\mathbb{R}
.
The Hamiltonians are defined as the sum of a non-interacting part, to which the magnetic field is coupled through Peierls phases, and an interacting part, which is compatible with the magnetic field; for example standard density-density interactions.

To define the non-interacting Hamiltonians
\mathfrak{h}^{b}
acting on the one-body space
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{r})
, let first

h:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{r})
satisfy
h(-z)=h(z)^{*}
for all
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and have exponential decay, i.e., there exist
c,C>0
such that

\forall z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\,:\;\|h(z)\|\leq C\,\mathrm{e}^{-c\|z\|}\,.

Throughout, for
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
the norm
\|z\|
denotes the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm and for linear maps the operator norm.
For any
b\in\mathbb{R}
, the operator
\mathfrak{h}^{b}
is obtained by multiplying the translation-invariant hopping terms by a Peierls phase:

\mathfrak{h}^{b}(x,y)_{ij}:=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{x_{2}+y_{2}}{2}b(x_{1}-y_{1})}h(x-y)_{ij}\,.

Here we define the operator
\mathfrak{h}^{b}
in terms of its summation kernel given by

\mathfrak{h}^{b}(x,y)_{ij}=\langle e_{x}\otimes v_{i},\mathfrak{h}^{b}\,e_{y}\otimes v_{j}\rangle

(3)

with respect to the canonical orthonormal bases
\{e_{x}\,|\,x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}
of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
and
\{v_{i}\,|\,i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\}
of
\mathbb{C}^{r}
. Notice how, if one sets
r=1
and

h(z)\equiv h_{\mathrm{Hofstadter}}(z)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }|z|=1,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

one recovers as
\mathfrak{h}^{b}\equiv\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{Hofstadter}}^{b}
the usual Hofstadter Hamiltonian, namely the discrete magnetic Laplacian on the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}

[
32
]
.

The (dual) magnetic translations are defined for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
as

t^{b}_{\gamma}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{r})\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{r})\,,\quad(t^{b}_{\gamma}f)(x):=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}bx_{1}\gamma_{2}}f(x+\gamma)\,,

with inverse

\big(t^{b}_{\gamma}\big)^{-1}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{r})\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{r})\,,\quad\big[\big(t^{b}_{\gamma}\big)^{-1}f\big](x):=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}b(x_{1}-\gamma_{1})\gamma_{2}}f(x-\gamma)\,.

Then,
\mathfrak{h}^{b}
is
t^{b}
-invariant, i.e.

\big(t_{\gamma}^{b}\big)^{-1}\mathfrak{h}^{b}t_{\gamma}^{b}=\mathfrak{h}^{b}\quad\text{for all }\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\,.

(4)

Magnetic translations in general yield merely a projective representation of the abelian group
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, as

t_{\gamma}^{b}\,t_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{b}=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}b\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}^{\prime}}t_{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}^{b}=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}b(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}^{\prime}-\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{2})}t_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{b}\,t_{\gamma}^{b}\,,\quad\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

Only in the case where
b=2\pi\tfrac{p}{q}\in 2\pi\mathbb{Q}
is commensurate to

, magnetic translations provide a true unitary representation of the sublattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}:=\mathbb{Z}\times(q\mathbb{Z})\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and operators which commute with them are amenable to being studied via the Bloch–Floquet representation (compare Appendix
B.1
). In view of these remarks, later at times we will have to distinguish between “commensurate” and “incommensurate” magnetic fields

when studying spectral properties of magnetic Hamiltonians. At any rate, the results we present hold for any
b\in\mathbb{R}

It will be convenient to write Hamiltonians and other operators (one-body and many-body) as sums of local terms.
For
\mathfrak{h}^{b}
, and similarly for other one-body operators, define
R_{x}:=\{(y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}\,|\,x_{j}=\lfloor\frac{y_{j}+z_{j}+1}{2}\rfloor\,,\;j\in\{1,2\}\}
and

to be the operator with kernel

In short, every “hopping from

We view the above

We are interested in the gapped phases of

, which we define for the purpose of this paper as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

A
uniformly gapped region
for the family
(H_{(b,\mu,\lambda)})_{(b,\mu,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}
is an open connected non-empty set
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
together with a family of states
(\rho_{p})_{{p}\in\mathcal{P}}
such that:

(i)

There exists
g>0
such that for all
p\in\mathcal{P}
the state

\rho_{p}
is a locally unique gapped ground state for
H_{p}
with gap
g
.

(ii)

For any
A\in\mathcal{A}
the map

\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{C}\,,\quad(b,\mu,\lambda)\mapsto\rho_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}(A)

is continuous.

Still at an informal level, our results are as follows.

1.

We first show in Theorem
2.2
that every point of a non-interacting gapped region has a neighborhood that extends to a uniformly gapped region of the interacting model in the following sense: For each
\mu_{0}\notin\sigma(\mathfrak{h}^{b_{0}})
, we find
\delta,\lambda_{0}>0
such that
\mathcal{P}_{\delta,\lambda_{0}}:=B_{\delta}(b_{0},\mu_{0})\times(-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0})
, together
with an explicitly constructed family of states
(\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)})_{(b,\mu,\lambda)\in\mathcal{P}_{\delta,\lambda_{0}}}
,
is a uniformly gapped region for
H_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
. The proof is based on results on the stability of the spectral gap of free fermion systems
[
21
,
28
,
23
]
, but the extension to infinite systems with possibly incommensurate
b
requires additional arguments in view of the above-mentioned lack of periodicity.

2.

Having established the existence of gapped phases for our weakly interacting generalized Hofstadter Hamiltonians, our main new result is a “gap labeling” theorem: we show that the Hall conductivity, defined by the many-body version of the double commutator formula derived in
[
47
]
, is constant and quantized, with
2\pi\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}\in\mathbb{Z}
, in the uniformly gapped regions of
H_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
around
\lambda=0
. This is the content of Theorem
2.3
, which gives rise to an extended many-body phase diagram “colored” like in Figure
1
.

A key technical ingredient in our proof is the construction of a locally generated automorphism
\alpha_{(b,(0,\lambda))}
of
\mathcal{A}
that maps the unique gapped ground state of the non-interacting system
H_{(b,\mu,0)}
to the gapped ground state constructed below for the weakly interacting system
H_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
. This automorphism is in principle well known: in the case of the standard adiabatic theorem of quantum mechanics formulated for self-adjoint operators on Hilbert spaces it is given by the unitary parallel transport map in the vector bundle defined by the ground state projections
\lambda\mapsto P_{\lambda}
. In the many-body setting one speaks of automorphic equivalence of gapped ground states, see
[
8
]
for finite systems and
[
36
,
10
]
for infinitely extended systems. In
[
36
,
10
]
, however, one starts with a sufficiently regular family of gapped ground states and shows that they are connected by locally generated automorphisms. Here we will turn the logic around: we define the states through parallel transport and show that they are indeed gapped ground states.

To this end let us introduce the generator of this so-called quasi-adiabatic evolution to be the
B_{\infty}
-interaction
K^{(b,\lambda),g}
with
K^{(b,\lambda),g}_{0}
given by

K^{(b,\lambda),g}_{0}:=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}s\,W_{g}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}s\mathcal{L}_{H_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}}}v^{b}\,,

(6)

where
W_{g}
is a rapidly decaying function depending on a gap parameter
g>0
, defined as in Appendix
A.3
; note that
g
will be fixed in the proofs. The interaction

K^{(b,\lambda),g}
generates the strongly continuous cocycle of automorphisms

(\lambda_{0},\lambda)\mapsto\alpha_{(b,(\lambda_{0},\lambda))}
, the quasi-adiabatic evolution, satisfying

\partial_{\lambda}\,\alpha_{(b,(\lambda_{0},\lambda))}=\alpha_{(b,(\lambda_{0},\lambda))}\circ\mathrm{i}\mathcal{L}_{K^{(b,\lambda),g}}\,.

Note that
K^{(b,\lambda),g}
and thus also
\alpha_{(b,(\lambda_{0},\lambda))}
do not depend on
\mu
, since
v^{b}
is by assumption gauge-invariant and thus commutes with the number operator. For better readability we also not make explicit the
g
-dependence of
\alpha_{(b,(\lambda_{0},\lambda))}
.

Assume that
\mu\notin\sigma(\mathfrak{h}^{b})
and let
P_{(-\infty,\mu)}^{\mathfrak{h}^{b}}:=\chi_{(-\infty,\mu)}(\mathfrak{h}^{b})
denote the corresponding Fermi projection below the spectral gap around
\mu
.
Then the quasi-free state
\omega_{(b,\mu,0)}
, characterized uniquely by its two-point function

\omega_{(b,\mu,0)}(a^{*}_{x,i}a_{y,j})=P_{(-\infty,\mu)}^{\mathfrak{h}^{b}}(x,y)_{ij}
according to Wick’s theorem, is the unique infinite-volume ground state of
H_{(b,\mu,0)}
. Here
P_{(-\infty,\mu)}^{\mathfrak{h}^{b}}(x,y)_{ij}
are the matrix elements of
P_{(-\infty,\mu)}^{\mathfrak{h}^{b}}
with respect to the canonical basis of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{r})
defined as in (
3
).
Given
g>0
, we define for
(b,\mu,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
the state

\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}\coloneq\omega_{(b,\mu,0)}\circ\alpha_{(b,(0,\lambda))}\,.

(7)

Notice that
\omega_{(b,\mu,0)}
and
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
do not depend on
\mu
as long as
\mu
lies within the same gap of the one-particle Hamiltonian
\mathfrak{h}^{b}
. Still we keep
\mu
as a label of the state to indicate that the gapped phase in which
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
lies does depend on
\mu
.

We can now formulate our first main result.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(b_{0},\mu_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be such that
\mu_{0}\notin\sigma(\mathfrak{h}^{b_{0}})
. Then there exist
\delta,\lambda_{0},g_{*}>0
such that

\mathcal{P}_{\delta,\lambda_{0}}:=B_{\delta}(b_{0},\mu_{0})\times(-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0})

together with the family of states

(\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)})_{(b,\mu,\lambda)\in\mathcal{P}_{\delta,\lambda_{0}}}\,,

defined as in (
7
) with respect to
g=g_{*}
,
form a uniformly gapped region with gap
g_{*}
for
(H_{(b,\mu,\lambda)})_{(b,\mu,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}
. Furthermore, for all
(b,\mu,\lambda)\in\mathcal{P}_{\delta,\lambda_{0}}
the state
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
is
T^{b}
-invariant and
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}=\omega_{(b,\mu_{0},\lambda)}
.

The proof of Theorem
2.2
is presented in Section
3
.

The second theorem concerns the Hall current response of the states (
7
) to external electric fields. It has been shown in
[
47
]
that, when the system modeled by
H_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
is initially in the gapped ground state
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
and is then perturbed by an electric field of intensity
\varepsilon
in the direction of
x_{1}
, then the transverse current in the direction of
x_{2}
flowing in the corresponding non-equilibrium almost-stationary state (NEASS)
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}^{\varepsilon}:=\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}\circ\beta^{\varepsilon}=\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}\circ\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathcal{L}_{S^{\varepsilon}}}
is

j_{2}^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\,\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{\infty})\,.

Here

\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}:=\overline{\omega}_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}\left(\mathrm{i}\left[X_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}(b,\mu,\lambda)},X_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}(b,\mu,\lambda)}\right]\right)

(8)

is the Hall conductivity and
\overline{\omega}_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}(\cdot)
denotes the expectation-per-unit-volume in the state
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
(compare (
29
)). The off-diagonal part of the position operator
X_{j}:=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}x_{j}\,n_{x}
,
j\in\{1,2\}
, with respect to the state
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
is

X_{j}^{\mathrm{OD}(b,\mu,\lambda)}:=\mathrm{i}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}s\,W_{g}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}s\mathcal{L}_{H_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}}}\,\mathcal{L}_{X_{j}}H_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}\,.

(9)

See Appendix
A.3
for details.
For details on how to construct the NEASS

and under what conditions it provides the full response of a gapped system to a perturbation, see
[
43
,
31
,
9
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
M_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open connected set such that

\mu\notin\sigma(\mathfrak{h}^{b})
for all
(b,\mu)\in M_{0}
, i.e. part of a gapped phase of the non-interacting model. Assume that
(H_{(b,\mu,\lambda)})
admits a uniformly gapped region with gap
g>0
of the form
\left(\mathcal{P}_{M_{0}},\big(\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}\big)_{(b,\mu,\lambda)\in\mathcal{P}_{M_{0}}}\right)
, where

\mathcal{P}_{M_{0}}\subset M_{0}\times\mathbb{R}
is open, contains
M_{0}\times\{0\}
, and for each
(b,\mu,\lambda)\in\mathcal{P}_{M_{0}}
contains also the line segment
[(b,\mu,0),(b,\mu,\lambda)]
, and where
\big(\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}\big)_{(b,\mu,\lambda)\in\mathcal{P}_{M_{0}}}
is the family of states defined in (
7
) with respect to
g
.
Then the map

\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}\colon\mathcal{P}_{M_{0}}\to\mathbb{R},\quad(b,\mu,\lambda)\mapsto\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}

is constant and equal to the integer-quantized value of the label
\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}_{M_{0}}
of the non-interacting phase
M_{0}
, i.e.

\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}\equiv\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}_{M_{0}}\in\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\,\mathbb{Z}\,.

The proof of Theorem
2.3
is presented in Section
4
.

Note that Theorem
2.3
covers not only weak interactions, that is small
|\lambda|
, but all interactions
for which
\mu
effectively still lies in a gap between different Bloch–Landau levels. This is exactly the physical range for which integer quantization of Hall conductance is expected.

Remark 2.4
.

For fixed magnetic field
b
, the states
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
obtained by varying
\lambda
are connected by the quasi-adiabatic cocycle generated by the bounded local interaction
K^{(b,\lambda),g}
in (
6
).
The situation is different when one varies the magnetic field. Formally, for a differentiable path
b\mapsto H_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
, quasi-adiabatic perturbation theory would suggest a generator of the form

\partial_{b}\,\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}=\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}\circ i\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{K}^{(b,\lambda),g}},

with

\widetilde{K}^{(b,\lambda),g}_{x}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}s\,W_{g}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{is\mathcal{L}_{H_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}}}\partial_{b}H_{(b,\mu,\lambda),x}.

(10)

In the magnetic models considered here, however, the local derivative

\partial_{b}H_{(b,\mu,\lambda),x}
grows linearly in the spatial coordinate, for instance like
|x_{2}|
in the gauge used above. Thus (
10
) does not define a bounded interaction in the usual sense.
There is a conceptual obstruction: within phases labeled by a non-zero Hall conductivity, the charge density

\overline{\omega}_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}(N)
changes along a path in
b
, and the corresponding states cannot be connected by a locally generated automorphism produced by bounded gauge-invariant interactions, since such automorphisms preserve the charge density per unit volume.

One can envisage different approaches to retain a notion of automorphic equivalence for magnetic gapped phases.
One could try to stack
\omega_{(b_{1},\mu,\lambda)}
and
\omega_{(b_{2},\mu,\lambda)}
with short-range entangled states and then deform the enlarged states using bounded gauge-invariant generators on the enlarged system along a path that departs from the family of states
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
. Alternatively one could look for bounded but not gauge-invariant generators. Both approaches have, to our knowledge, not been explored yet for magnetic phases.

A third approach is to enlarge the class of automorphisms that facilitate automorphic equivalence within gapped ground state phases by including those generated by interactions with linear growth in space such as (
10
). In
[
42
]
it is shown that such spatially growing interactions generate automorphisms of
\mathcal{A}
, providing a possible framework for automorphic equivalence within magnetic gapped phases. We will explore this approach in a future work.

3
Proof of Theorem
2.2

Before we begin with the proof of Theorem
2.2
let us first compare the present section with the proof of Proposition 3.4 in
[
47
]
. There it was shown that for all
(b_{0},\mu_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\mu_{0}\notin\sigma(\mathfrak{h}^{b_{0}})
there exists
\delta>0
such that, for every
(b,\mu,\lambda)\in B_{\delta}(b_{0},\mu_{0},0)

has a locally unique gapped ground state. We extend this result to Hofstadter-like models of the type presented in Section
2.1
, and complement the argument by proving two crucial ingredients needed to show that the Hall conductivity remains constant within gapped phases of these models: namely, that the explicitly constructed state
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
as defined in (
7
) provides such a ground state, and that the map
B_{\delta}(b_{0},\mu_{0},0)\to S(\mathcal{A}),\,(b,\mu,\lambda)\mapsto\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
is continuous in the weak* topology,
where
S(\mathcal{A})
denotes the set of all states on
\mathcal{A}
.

Concerning this last point, as was observed after (
7
), the states
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
are constant along lines of fixed
b
and
\lambda
when
\mu\notin\sigma(\mathfrak{h}^{b})
(i.e. they depend on
\mu
in a constant way, and
a fortiori
continuously). Moreover, continuity in
\lambda

\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
follows at once from strong continuity of the family of automorphisms

, compare Section
3.3
. Furthermore, the states
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
are
T^{b}
-invariant by construction:
\omega_{(b,\mu,0)}
is
T^{b}
-invariant and
\alpha_{(b,(0,\lambda))}
is generated by the
T^{b}
-invariant interaction
K^{(b,\lambda),g}
.

Proving that the states
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
are locally unique gapped ground states for

and that they depend continuously on

are the most delicate issues. As mentioned previously, the spectral properties of magnetic Hamiltonians crucially depend on whether the magnetic field strength

is commensurate or not to
2\pi
. Thus, our proof is structured into three parts:

1.

We first treat a set of commensurate magnetic fields in Section
3.1
. For
b
in the set

Q:=\{2\pi\,\tfrac{p}{q}\,|\,p\in\mathbb{Z}\,,\;q\in\{12n+1|n\in\mathbb{N}\}\}\subset 2\pi\mathbb{Q}\,,

which is dense in
\mathbb{R}
, we consider periodic finite-volume approximations of the system, obtained as restrictions to an increasing sequence of boxes
\Lambda_{k_{n}}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We show that
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
can be obtained as the limit of finite-volume ground states of the form
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}^{(n)}=\omega_{(b,\mu,0)}^{(n)}\circ\alpha_{(b,(0,\lambda))}^{(n)}
, by showing that both
\omega_{(b,\mu,0)}^{(n)}
and
\alpha_{(b,(0,\lambda))}^{(n)}
converge to the appropriate infinite-volume objects in (
7
). Thus, by Lemma
3.1
,
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
is a locally unique gapped ground state of
H_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
.

2.

For
b\in\mathbb{R}\setminus Q
, we consider sequences
(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in

such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}b_{n}=b
, and show in Section
3.2
that
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
can be obtained as the limit of
\omega_{(b_{n},\mu,0)}\circ\alpha_{(b_{n},(0,\lambda))}
, by again showing that
\omega_{(b_{n},\mu,0)}
and
\alpha_{(b_{n},(0,\lambda))}
converge to
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
and
\alpha_{(b,(0,\lambda))}
respectively. Then the same Lemma
3.1
allows us to conclude that also here
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
is a locally unique gapped ground state of
H_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
.

3.

The third and last part of the proof concludes the weak*-continuity of the map
B_{\delta}(b_{0},\mu_{0},0)\to S(\mathcal{A}),\,(b,\mu,\lambda)\mapsto\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
using the convergence properties with respect to the magnetic field established in the second part.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
47
, Lemma G.1]
)
.

Let either
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}=\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{k_{n}}}
for some strictly increasing sequence of boxes
\Lambda_{k_{n}}
, i.e.
k:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
,
n\mapsto k_{n}
, is strictly increasing, or
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}=\mathcal{A}
for all

.
Let
(\mathcal{L}_{H_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of derivations, each
\mathcal{L}_{H_{n}}
acting on
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
, and
(\omega_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of states on
\mathcal{A}
such that for some

and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all

,

\omega_{n}(A^{*}\mathcal{L}_{H_{n}}A)\geq g\left(\omega_{n}(A^{*}A)-\left|\omega_{n}(A)\right|^{2}\right)\,.

(11)

If there exist a derivation
\mathcal{L}_{H}
and a state

.

3.1
A dense set of commensurate magnetic fields

In this first part of the proof we will show the following statement: For all
(b_{0},\mu_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\mu_{0}\notin\sigma(\mathfrak{h}^{b_{0}})
there exist
\delta,g_{*},\lambda_{0}>0
such that for all
(b,\mu)\in B_{\delta}(b_{0},\mu_{0})
with
b\in Q
and all
\lambda\in(-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0})
the state
\omega_{(b,\mu,\lambda)}
, defined as in (

, is a locally unique gapped ground state of

with gap

.

Let
(b_{0},\mu_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\mu_{0}\notin\sigma(\mathfrak{h}^{b_{0}})
.
Since the map

is continuous with respect to the Hausdorff metric, following for instance from gauge-covariant magnetic perturbation theory and the exponential decay of

Now let

with

. This means that

for some

and some

Thus, magnetic translations with respect to vectors

in the sublattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}:=\mathbb{Z}\times(q\mathbb{Z})\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}

)) is an integer multiple of

for all

and

. This allows us to apply Bloch–Floquet theory to
\mathfrak{h}^{b}
with respect to the sublattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}
in order to relate the spectrum of
\mathfrak{h}^{b}
to the spectra of its restrictions
\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{b}
to suitable finite boxes
\Lambda_{k_{n}}=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\,|\,\lVert x\rVert_{\infty}\leq k_{n}\}
. See also Appendix
B.1
for details.

Next we define the periodic finite-volume restrictions of the one- and many-body Hamiltonians.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

Let

resp.

resp. on

More precisely, for

t^{b}|_{{\Lambda_{k_{n}}},\gamma}:\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{k_{n}},\mathbb{C}^{r})\to\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{k_{n}},\mathbb{C}^{r})\,,\quad(t^{b}|_{{\Lambda_{k_{n}}},\gamma}f)(y):=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}by_{1}\gamma_{2}}f([y+\gamma]_{\Lambda_{k_{n}}})

and

T^{b}|_{{\Lambda_{k_{n}}},\gamma}
as the unique automorphism of
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{k_{n}}}
such that

\displaystyle T^{b}|_{{\Lambda_{k_{n}}},\gamma}(a_{y,j})=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}by_{1}\gamma_{2}}a_{[y+\gamma]_{\Lambda_{k_{n}}},j}\quad\forall\,y\in\Lambda_{k_{n}},\,j\in\{1,\dots r\},

\Lambda_{k_{n}}

The finite volume Hamiltonian
\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{b}\colon\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{k_{n}},\mathbb{C}^{r})\to\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{k_{n}},\mathbb{C}^{r})

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{b}(x,y)\coloneq\sum_{z\in(L_{n}\mathbb{Z})^{2}}\mathfrak{h}^{b}(x,y+z)\,.

In order to express this as a sum of

-translates

R_{n,0}:=\left\{(y,z)\in\Lambda_{k_{n}/2}\times\Lambda_{k_{n}/2}\,|\,\lfloor\tfrac{y_{j}+z_{j}+1}{2}\rfloor=0\,,\;j\in\{1,2\}\right\}

\mathfrak{h}^{b}_{n,0}(y,z):=\delta_{(y,z)\in R_{n,0}}\mathfrak{h}^{b}_{n}(y,z)\,.

Then
\mathfrak{h}^{b}_{n}=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{k_{n}}}(t^{b}|_{\Lambda_{k_{n}},x})^{-1}\mathfrak{h}^{b}_{n,0}t^{b}|_{\Lambda_{k_{n}},x}

and the restriction of

\displaystyle H_{(b,\mu,\lambda),n}\coloneq\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda_{k_{n}}}T^{b}|_{\Lambda_{k_{n}},\gamma}\left(\mathrm{d}\Gamma(\mathfrak{h}^{b}_{n,0})-\mu n_{0}+\lambda\,\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k_{n}}}(v^{b})\right)=:\mathrm{d}\Gamma(\mathfrak{h}^{b}_{n}-\mu\mathbf{1})+\lambda\,V^{b}_{n}\,.

These restricted Hamiltonians are

resp.

-invariant and by construction it holds for all

that

, see Appendix

for details.

We will use the fact that the dynamics generated by these restricted Hamiltonians approximates the one in infinite-volume in an appropriate sense, which is a consequence of the following general lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K\subseteq 3\,\mathbb{N}_{0}
be some infinite index set,
T

be its restriction to the box

\displaystyle T|_{\Lambda_{k},\gamma}(a_{y,j})=T_{([y+\gamma]_{\Lambda_{k}}-y)}(a_{y,j})\quad\forall\,y\in\Lambda_{k},\,j\in\{1,\dots r\}.

Further, let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an interval,
(\varPhi^{t})_{t\in I}
a continuous family of
T

B_{\infty}
-interactions and for each

\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}_{(b,\mu,0)}=\overline{\omega}_{(b,\mu,0)}\left(\mathrm{i}\,\left[X_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}(b,\mu,0)},X_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}(b,\mu,0)}\right]\right)

is constant and quantized on
M_{0}
.
This will follow immediately once we show that the many-body formula for
\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}
agrees, for non-interacting systems, with the one-body formula in terms of Fermi projections.
This is the content of the following proposition, which we formulate in greater generality also for non-periodic systems. In that case we use the Hall conductance defined in terms of so-called switch-functions instead of position operators. Compare
[
TW2025
]
and references therein.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
h\in\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{r}))
be self-adjoint and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\sigma(h)
such that
g:=\mathrm{dist}(\mu,\sigma(h))>0
. Let
P:=\chi_{(-\infty,\mu)}(h)
. Assume that the kernel
h(x,y)
of
h
has uniform exponential decay, i.e.,

\exists c,C>0\;\forall x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\quad\|h(x,y)\|\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-c\|x-y\|}\,.

(21)

(i)

Then
H:=\mathrm{d}\Gamma(h-\mu)\in B_{\exp}
and the unique quasi-free state
\omega
with
\omega(a^{*}_{x,j}a_{y,i})=P(x,y)_{ji}
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
j,i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
is the unique ground state of
\mathcal{L}_{H}
and has a gap of size at least
g
.

(ii)

Let
\lambda_{j}(x):=\chi_{x_{j}\geq 0}(x)
,
j\in\{1,2\}
, denote the one-body switch-functions, which define corresponding multiplication operators
\lambda_{j}
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{r})
, and
\Lambda_{j}:=\mathrm{d}\Gamma(\lambda_{j})
their many-body versions. Then

\mathrm{i}\,\omega([\Lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}},\Lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}}])=\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{tr}(P[\lambda_{2}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}},\lambda_{1}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}}])\in\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\mathbb{Z}\,,

where
\lambda_{j}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}}:=P\lambda_{j}P^{\perp}+P^{\perp}\lambda_{j}P
.

(iii)

If
H
is periodic with respect to some translation
T
, then also

\overline{\omega}([X_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}},X_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}}])=\omega([\Lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}},\Lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}}])\,.

Before proving the proposition, we conclude the proof of Theorem
2.3
: Proposition
4.1
shows that for all
(b,\mu)\in M_{0}

\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}_{(b,\mu,0)}=\mathrm{i}\,\operatorname{tr}\bigl(P^{\mathfrak{h}^{b}}_{(-\infty,\mu)}[\lambda_{2}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}(b,\mu)},\lambda_{1}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}(b,\mu)}]\bigr)

with
\lambda_{j}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}(b,\mu)}:=P^{\mathfrak{h}^{b}}_{(-\infty,\mu)}\lambda_{j}(P^{\mathfrak{h}^{b}}_{(-\infty,\mu)})^{\perp}+(P^{\mathfrak{h}^{b}}_{(-\infty,\mu)})^{\perp}\lambda_{j}P^{\mathfrak{h}^{b}}_{(-\infty,\mu)}
.
That this expression for
\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}_{(b,\mu,0)}
is continuous in
(b,\mu)\in M_{0}
can be proved with the methods of magnetic perturbation theory, in view of the uniform gap: compare
[
19
, Sec. 2.1.2]
. Since it takes values in the discrete set
\frac{1}{2\pi}\mathbb{Z}
, it is locally constant. Because
M_{0}
is connected,
(b,\mu)\mapsto\sigma^{\mathrm{H}}_{(b,\mu,0)}
is constant on
M_{0}
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Most of the statements are well known or follow from well known facts about second quantization. The simple argument for (i) is given for example in
[
47
]
. (iii) is established in
[
TW2025
]
. In (ii) the inclusion
\mathrm{tr}(P[\lambda_{2}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}},\lambda_{1}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}}])\in\tfrac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\mathbb{Z}
is also well known, see e.g.
[
5
,
40
,
11
,
1
,
19
,
17
]
.

The only statement in need of a proof is the equality of the many-body and the one-body double-commutator formulas, i.e. that

\omega([\Lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}},\Lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}}])=\mathrm{tr}(P[\lambda_{2}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}},\lambda_{1}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}}])
.
To this end note that, for self-adjoint one-body operators
a\in\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{r}))
,
it holds that
A:=\mathrm{d}\Gamma(a)
is in
\mathcal{A}
iff
a
is trace class (see, e.g.,
[
35
]
), and in that case we have

\omega(A)=\mathrm{tr}(Pa)\,.

Moreover,

\displaystyle(\Lambda_{j}^{\mathrm{OD}})_{x}

\displaystyle\coloneq

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}s\,W_{g}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}s\mathcal{L}_{H}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\Lambda_{j}}\,H_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\Gamma\left(\mathrm{i}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}s\,W_{g}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}sh}\,[\lambda_{j},h_{x}]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}sh}\right)\eqcolon\mathrm{d}\Gamma((\lambda_{j}^{\mathrm{OD}})_{x})

and
it was shown in
[
TW2025
]
that the sums in the following computation are absolutely convergent in
\mathcal{A}
:

\displaystyle[\Lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}},\Lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}}]

\displaystyle\coloneq

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}[(\Lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}})_{x},(\Lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}})_{y}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathrm{d}\Gamma([(\lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}})_{x},(\lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}})_{y}])=\mathrm{d}\Gamma([\lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}},\lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}}])

with

\lambda_{j}^{\mathrm{OD}}:=\mathrm{i}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}s\,W_{g}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}sh}\,[\lambda_{j},h]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}sh}\,.

(22)

Now we are left to prove that with

\lambda_{j}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}}:=P\lambda_{j}P^{\perp}+P^{\perp}\lambda_{j}P

it holds that

\mathrm{tr}(P[\lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}},\lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}}])=\mathrm{tr}(P[\lambda_{2}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}},\lambda_{1}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}}])\,.

But
since

\mathrm{tr}(P[\lambda_{2}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}},\lambda_{1}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}}])=\mathrm{tr}(P(\lambda_{2}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}}\lambda_{1}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}}-\lambda_{1}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}}\lambda_{2}^{\widetilde{\mathrm{OD}}})P)=\mathrm{tr}(P\lambda_{2}P^{\perp}\lambda_{1}P-P\lambda_{1}P^{\perp}\lambda_{2}P)\,,

this follows once we can show that

P\lambda_{j}^{\mathrm{OD}}P^{\perp}=P\lambda_{j}P^{\perp}\,,\quad P^{\perp}\lambda_{j}^{\mathrm{OD}}P=P^{\perp}\lambda_{j}P\,,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathrm{tr}([P\lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}}P,P\lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}}P])=0\,.

This was shown many times for finite dimensional Hilbert spaces and finite rank projections
P
. But it is also easy to see in general: by construction and assumption on
\mu
, there exist closed sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}
with dist
(A,B)>2g
such that
P:=\chi_{A}(h)
and
P^{\perp}=\chi_{B}(h)
.
Then, using the spectral decomposition
h=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\,\mathrm{d}P_{u}
, we find

\displaystyle P\lambda_{j}^{\mathrm{OD}}P^{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}P\int W_{g}(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}th}[h,\lambda_{j}]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}th}\,\mathrm{d}tP^{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\int W_{g}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(u-v)}(u-v)P\mathrm{d}P_{u}\lambda_{j}\mathrm{d}P_{v}\,\mathrm{d}tP^{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\sqrt{2\pi}\int_{A}\int_{B}\hat{W}_{g}(u-v)(u-v)\mathrm{d}P_{u}P\lambda_{j}P^{\perp}\mathrm{d}P_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A}\int_{B}\mathrm{d}P_{u}P\lambda_{j}P^{\perp}\mathrm{d}P_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P\lambda_{j}P^{\perp}\,,

and by taking the adjoint also

P^{\perp}\lambda_{j}^{\mathrm{OD}}P=P^{\perp}\lambda_{j}P
.

The following two lemmas show that
P\lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}}P\lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}}P
is trace class, which implies that

\mathrm{tr}([P\lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}}P,P\lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}}P])=0\,,

and thus concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
h
,
P
, and
\lambda_{j}
be as in Proposition
4.1

and define
\lambda_{j}^{\mathrm{OD}}
as in (
22
).
Then, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
C_{N}<\infty
such that

\bigl\|(P\lambda_{j}^{\mathrm{OD}}P)(x,y)\bigr\|\leq C_{N}\langle\|x-y\|\rangle^{-N}\langle|x_{j}|\rangle^{-N}\langle|y_{j}|\rangle^{-N}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Here
\langle r\rangle:=1+r
, and

\|\cdot\|
denotes the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Proof.

For
j\in\{1,2\}
the kernel of

C_{j}:=[\lambda_{j},h]
is

C_{j}(x,y)=\bigl(\lambda_{j}(x)-\lambda_{j}(y)\bigr)h(x,y).

Since
\lambda_{j}
is a half-space switch,
C_{j}(x,y)
can be nonzero only
if
x_{j}
and
y_{j}
lie on opposite sides of the switch. Hence, whenever

C_{j}(x,y)\neq 0
,

|x_{j}|+|y_{j}|\leq\|x-y\|\,.

Combining this support property with the exponential decay of
h
, we find
that, for every
L\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a constant
C_{L}<\infty
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}

\|C_{j}(x,y)\|\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-c/2\|x-y\|}\mathrm{e}^{-c/2(|x_{j}|+|y_{j}|)}\leq C_{L}\langle\|x-y\|\rangle^{-L}\langle|x_{j}|\rangle^{-L}\langle|y_{j}|\rangle^{-L}\,.

(23)

Next we use the following one-body Lieb–Robinson type bound: For any
0<\alpha<c
, where
c
is the exponent from (
21
), set

c_{\alpha}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\|h(x,y)\|\mathrm{e}^{\alpha\|x-y\|_{\infty}}.

then, by bounding each term in the exponential series separately, one finds

\bigl\|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}th}(x,y)\bigr\|\leq\min\left\{1,\mathrm{e}^{c_{\alpha}|t|-\alpha\|x-y\|_{\infty}}\right\}\,.

(24)

Estimating (
24
) in the two regimes
\|x-y\|_{\infty}\leq(1+2c_{\alpha}/\alpha)\langle|t|\rangle
and

\|x-y\|_{\infty}>(1+2c_{\alpha}/\alpha)\langle|t|\rangle
, one finds that
for every
M\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
Q_{M}<\infty
such that, for
all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\bigl\|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}th}(x,y)\bigr\|\leq Q_{M}\langle|t|\rangle^{M}\langle\|x-y\|\rangle^{-M}\,.

(25)

Let

C_{j}(t):=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}th}C_{j}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}th}\,,

fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
, choose
R>N+2
, and then choose
M\geq R+N
. Using
(
25
) and
(
23
) with exponent
R
, we obtain

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\|C_{j}(t)(x,y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\bigl\|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}th}(x,u)\bigr\|\|C_{j}(u,v)\|\bigl\|\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}th}(v,y)\bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{R}Q_{M}^{2}\langle|t|\rangle^{2M}\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\langle\|x-u\|\rangle^{-M}\langle\|u-v\|\rangle^{-R}\langle\|v-y\|\rangle^{-M}\langle|u_{j}|\rangle^{-R}\langle|v_{j}|\rangle^{-R}.

By the reverse triangle inequality,

1+|x_{j}|\leq 1+|u_{j}|+\|x-u\|\leq(1+|u_{j}|)(1+\|x-u\|),

and therefore the Peetre type inequalities

\langle|u_{j}|\rangle^{-N}\leq\langle\|x-u\|\rangle^{N}\langle|x_{j}|\rangle^{-N}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\langle|v_{j}|\rangle^{-N}\leq\langle\|v-y\|\rangle^{N}\langle|y_{j}|\rangle^{-N}

hold.
Using these inequalities and dropping the remaining negative powers that are all smaller or equal to one, we get

\|C_{j}(t)(x,y)\|\leq C_{R}Q_{M}^{2}\langle|t|\rangle^{2M}\langle|x_{j}|\rangle^{-N}\langle|y_{j}|\rangle^{-N}\!\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\!\langle\|x-u\|\rangle^{-R}\langle\|u-v\|\rangle^{-R}\langle\|v-y\|\rangle^{-R}\,.

Since
R>2
, the standard convolution estimate on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}

gives

\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\langle\|x-u\|\rangle^{-R}\langle\|u-v\|\rangle^{-R}\langle\|v-y\|\rangle^{-R}\leq\widetilde{C}_{R}\langle\|x-y\|\rangle^{-R}

and thus with
R>N
also

\|C_{j}(t)(x,y)\|\leq C_{N}\langle|t|\rangle^{2M}\langle\|x-y\|\rangle^{-N}\langle|x_{j}|\rangle^{-N}\langle|y_{j}|\rangle^{-N}\,.

(26)

Since
W_{g}
is rapidly decaying, the function

\langle|t|\rangle^{2M}W_{g}(t)
is integrable. Therefore, after
absorbing the
t
-integral into the constant, we obtain

\|\lambda_{j}^{\mathrm{OD}}(x,y)\|\leq C_{N}\langle\|x-y\|\rangle^{-N}\langle|x_{j}|\rangle^{-N}\langle|y_{j}|\rangle^{-N}.

(27)

Finally, by the spectral gap and exponential localization of
h
, the Fermi
projection has exponentially decaying kernel, and hence for every

M\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
C_{M}>0
such that

\|P(x,y)\|\leq C_{M}\langle\|x-y\|\rangle^{-M}.

Using

(P\lambda_{j}^{\mathrm{OD}}P)(x,y)=\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}P(x,u)\lambda_{j}^{\mathrm{OD}}(u,v)P(v,y),

and applying the same Peetre and convolution argument as above, with
(
27
) in place of
(
23
), we obtain, after redefining
C_{N}
,

\bigl\|(P\lambda_{j}^{\mathrm{OD}}P)(x,y)\bigr\|\leq C_{N}\langle\|x-y\|\rangle^{-N}\langle|x_{j}|\rangle^{-N}\langle|y_{j}|\rangle^{-N}\,.\qed

Lemma 4.3
.

With the notation of Lemma
4.2
, the operator

(P\lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}}P)(P\lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}}P)=P\lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}}P\lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}}P

and thus also its adjoint

(P\lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}}P)(P\lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}}P)=P\lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{OD}}P\lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{OD}}P

is trace class.

Proof.

For
j=1,2
set

D_{j}:=P\lambda_{j}^{\mathrm{OD}}P
.
By Lemma
4.2
, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
there is

C_{N}<\infty
such that

\|D_{1}(x,z)\|\leq C_{N}\langle\|x-z\|\rangle^{-N}\langle|x_{1}|\rangle^{-N}\langle|z_{1}|\rangle^{-N}

and

\|D_{2}(z,y)\|\leq C_{N}\langle\|z-y\|\rangle^{-N}\langle|z_{2}|\rangle^{-N}\langle|y_{2}|\rangle^{-N}\,.

Choose
N>2
. Then

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\|(D_{1}D_{2})(x,y)\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}^{2}\sum_{x,y,z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\langle\|x-z\|\rangle^{-N}\langle|x_{1}|\rangle^{-N}\langle|z_{1}|\rangle^{-N}\langle\|z-y\|\rangle^{-N}\langle|z_{2}|\rangle^{-N}\langle|y_{2}|\rangle^{-N}\,.

For fixed
z
, since
N>2
,

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\langle\|x-z\|\rangle^{-N}\langle|x_{1}|\rangle^{-N}\leq\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\langle\|x-z\|\rangle^{-N}\leq C_{N}^{\prime}\,,

uniformly in
z
. Similarly,

\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\langle\|z-y\|\rangle^{-N}\langle|y_{2}|\rangle^{-N}\leq C_{N}^{\prime}\,,

uniformly in
z
. Therefore

\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\|(D_{1}D_{2})(x,y)\|\leq(C_{N}^{\prime})^{2}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\langle|z_{1}|\rangle^{-N}\langle|z_{2}|\rangle^{-N}<\infty\,.

Thus
D_{1}D_{2}
has an absolutely summable matrix kernel. Since the fiber
dimension is finite, this implies that
D_{1}D_{2}
is trace class.
∎

Appendix A
Basic notions on fermionic lattice systems

We basically use the standard formalism for describing infinitely extended systems of interacting lattice fermions in the thermodynamic limit, as explained for example in
[
14
,
13
]
, together with more specific results for fermionic systems from
[
2
]
.
However, it turns out that our analysis requires a number of additional technical results, mostly concerning periodic interactions and position operators, which were not available in the previous literature.
The proofs of the corresponding lemmas and propositions are collected in
[
47
, Appendix B]
.

A.1
(Quasi-)local fermionic interactions

The anti-symmetric (or fermionic) Fock space over the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}):=\bigoplus_{N=0}^{\infty}\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r})^{\wedge N}\penalty\ ,

where
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r})^{\wedge N}
denotes the
N
-fold anti-symmetric tensor product of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r})
, with the convention
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r})^{\wedge 0}:=\mathbb{C}
.
We use
a^{*}_{x,i}
and
a_{x,i}
, for
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
, to denote the fermionic creation and annihilation operators associated to the standard basis of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r})
; recall that they satisfy the canonical anti-commutation relations (CAR).
The number operator at site
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is defined by

\displaystyle n_{x}:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a^{*}_{x,i}a_{x,i}\,.

The algebra of all bounded operators on
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r})
is denoted by
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}))
.
For each
M\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
let
\mathcal{A}_{M}
be the C*-subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}))
generated by

\displaystyle\{a^{*}_{x,i}\penalty\ |\penalty\ x\in M,\penalty\ i\in\{1,\dots,r\}\}\penalty\ .

The C*-algebra
\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is the CAR-algebra, which we also call the quasi-local algebra. We define
P_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}):=\{M\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\penalty\ |\penalty\ |M|<\infty\}
and call

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}:=\bigcup_{M\in P_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\mathcal{A}_{M}\subseteq\mathcal{A}

the local algebra, which is dense in
\mathcal{A}
with respect to the norm topology. Consequently, an operator in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}))
is called quasi-local if it lies in
\mathcal{A}
and local if it lies in
\mathcal{A}_{0}
.
For each
\varphi\in\mathbb{R}
there is a unique
*
-automorphism
g_{\varphi}
of
\mathcal{A}
, such that

\displaystyle g_{\varphi}(a^{*}_{x,i})=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}\,a^{*}_{x,i},\quad\text{for all}\penalty\ \penalty\ x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\penalty\ i\in\{1,\dots,r\}\penalty\ .

One defines the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{N}:=\{A\in\mathcal{A}\penalty\ |\penalty\ g_{\varphi}(A)=A\;\forall\varphi\in\mathbb{R}\}

and calls
\mathcal{A}^{N}
the gauge-invariant sub-algebra of
\mathcal{A}
. It is the closure of the set of all local observables that commute with the number operator, i.e. all local observables
A\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
that satisfy
A\in\mathcal{A}_{M}\Rightarrow[A,N_{M}]:=[A,\sum_{x\in M}n_{x}]=0
. Its part in
M\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{N}_{M}:=\mathcal{A}^{N}\cap\mathcal{A}_{M}
.
For disjoint regions
M_{1},M_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
M_{1}\cap M_{2}=\emptyset
, operators
A\in\mathcal{A}_{M_{1}}^{N}
and
B\in\mathcal{A}_{M_{2}}
commute:
[A,B]=0
.

In order to define quantitative notions of localization for quasi-local operators, one makes use of the fact that one can localize operators to given regions by means of the fermionic conditional expectation.
To this end first note that
\mathcal{A}
has a unique state
\omega^{\mathrm{tr}}
(commonly referred to as a tracial state) that satisfies

\displaystyle\omega^{\mathrm{tr}}(AB)=\omega^{\mathrm{tr}}(BA)

for all
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
(e.g.
[
2
, Definition 4.1, Remark 2]
).

Proposition A.1

(
[
2
, Theorem 4.7]
,
[
47
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

For each
M\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
there exists a unique linear map

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{M}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{M}\,,

called the conditional expectation with respect to
\omega^{\mathrm{tr}}
, such that

\forall A\in\mathcal{A}\;\;\forall B\in\mathcal{A}_{M}\,:\quad\omega^{\mathrm{tr}}(AB)=\omega^{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathbb{E}_{M}(A)B)\,.

(28)

It is unital, positive and has the properties

\displaystyle\forall M\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\;\;\forall A,C\in\mathcal{A}_{M}\;\;\forall B\in\mathcal{A}\,:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{M}\lparen A\,B\,C\rparen=A\,\mathbb{E}_{M}(B)\,C

\displaystyle\forall M_{1},M_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{M_{1}}\circ\mathbb{E}_{M_{2}}=\mathbb{E}_{M_{1}\cap M_{2}}\,

\displaystyle\forall M\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{M}\mathcal{A}^{N}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{N}

\displaystyle\forall M\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\;\;\forall A\in\mathcal{A}\,:

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{E}_{M}(A)\rVert\leq\lVert A\rVert\,.

With the help of
\mathbb{E}
we can define subspaces of
\mathcal{A}^{N}
that contain operators with well-defined decay properties (cf.
[
36
]
for similar definitions for quantum spin systems).

Definition A.2
.

For
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
A\in\mathcal{A}
let

\displaystyle\lVert A\rVert_{\nu}:=\lVert A\rVert+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}(\lVert A-\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k}}A\rVert(1+k)^{\nu})

where
\Lambda_{k}=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,|\,\lVert x\rVert_{\infty}\leq k\}
is the box with side-length
2k+1
around
0
(
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty}
is the maximum norm on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
).
We denote the set of all
A\in\mathcal{A}
with finite
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\nu}
by
\mathcal{A}_{\nu}
and also define
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}:=\bigcap_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}
. We will sometimes refer to these norms as
decay norms
. We also use the notation
\mathcal{A}_{\nu}^{N}:=\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\cap\mathcal{A}^{N}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}^{N}:=\mathcal{A}_{\infty}\cap\mathcal{A}^{N}
. Finally, we say that
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\exp}
iff
A\in\mathcal{A}
and there is a
a>0
such that
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}(\lVert A-\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k}}A\rVert\mathrm{e}^{ak})<\infty
.

The relevant physical dynamics on
\mathcal{A}
is generated by densely defined derivations, which in turn are constructed from so-called interactions.
An
interaction
is a map
\varPhi:P_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\to\mathcal{A}^{N}
, such that
\varPhi(\emptyset)=0
and for all
M\in P_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
it holds that
\varPhi(M)\in\mathcal{A}_{M}

For two interactions
\varPhi
and
\varPsi
their commutator is given by

\displaystyle[\varPhi,\varPsi]:P_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\to\mathcal{A}^{N}\,,\qquad M\mapsto[\varPhi,\varPsi](M)\;:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M_{1},M_{2}\subseteq M\\
M_{1}\cup M_{2}=M\end{subarray}}[\varPhi(M_{1}),\varPsi(M_{2})]\,.

The map

satisfies the definition of an interaction except for the last requirement, which is not always satisfied. All the commutators of interactions appearing in the following will however be interactions (see Lemma
A.4
and Proposition
A.11

Interactions define derivations on the algebra in the following way.
For an interaction
\varPhi
let

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi}^{\circ}:\mathcal{A}_{0}\to\mathcal{A},\quad A\mapsto\sum_{M\in P_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}[\varPhi(M),A]\,.

It follows from

Some basic interactions that will appear frequently in the following are the number operator

and the position operators

for

, which are non-vanishing only on one-element sets. They are defined by

and

for

In the operator-algebraic framework, states are described as positive linear functionals

of norm

. Given an interaction
\varPhi

\displaystyle\forall A\in D(\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi}):\quad\omega(A^{*}\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi}A)\geq 0\,.

Here, for a given linear operator

\mathcal{A}
,

For a state
\omega

\mathcal{A}
and an interaction
\varPhi

\overline{\omega}(\varPhi):=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{k}|}\sum_{M\subseteq\Lambda_{k}}\omega(\varPhi(M))\,,

(29)

whenever the limit exists and call it the per-volume expectation value of

Definition A.3
.

Let
\varPhi

A cocycle of automorphisms is a family

of automorphisms of

A.2
Periodic interactions

From now on we will specialise to periodic interactions and periodic states for which the existence of the per-volume expectation is easy to see.

Definition A.6
.

We denote by
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{A})
the group of automorphisms on
\mathcal{A}
.
A
translation
is a map
T:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{A})
that assigns to each possible shift vector
\gamma
an automorphism
T_{\gamma}
and satisfies the following properties:

(i)

For all
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
M\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
it holds that
T_{\gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{M})=\mathcal{A}_{M+\gamma}
.

(ii)

For all
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, it holds that
T_{\gamma}n_{x}=n_{x+\gamma}
.

Remark A.7
.

Note that the definition of a translation does
not
require it be a group homomorphism. As a consequence,
the magnetic translations defined in (
5
) are translations in the above sense for
all

b\in\mathbb{R}
.
Translations do however satisfy a homomorphism property when acting on certain elements like the local number operators or terms of interactions that are periodic with respect to the translation.

The two properties of the definition guarantee that translations are compatible with the conditional expectation and gauge automorphisms respectively:

Lemma A.8
.

Let
T
be a translation and
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. It holds that

\displaystyle\forall M\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon

\displaystyle\quad T_{\gamma}\,\mathbb{E}_{M}=\mathbb{E}_{M+\gamma}\,T_{\gamma}\,,

\displaystyle\forall\varphi\in\mathbb{R}\colon

\displaystyle\quad g_{\varphi}\,T_{\gamma}=T_{\gamma}\,g_{\varphi}\,.

Let
T
be a translation. We say that a state
\omega
is
T
-periodic if

\displaystyle\forall\,\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\,A\in\mathcal{A}:\quad\omega(T_{\gamma}A)=\omega(A)\,.

An interaction
\varPhi
is called
T
-periodic if

\displaystyle\forall\,\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\,M\in P_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}):\quad T_{\gamma}\varPhi(M)=\varPhi(M+\gamma)\,.

In order to define a standard representation of a periodic interaction in terms of a quasi-local observable, we introduce the following notions.
We say
M_{1},M_{2}\in P_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
have the same shape if there is a
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that
M_{1}=M_{2}+\gamma
. For each
M\in P_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we define

as the vector that shifts the center of mass of
M
closest to
0
. More precisely it is defined as the unique element of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\,M+s(M)}\frac{x}{\lvert M\rvert}\in{\big(}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,,\,\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\big]}^{d}\,.

(30)

We call
M+s(M)
the standard representative of
M
at
0
and denote the set of all standard representatives at
0
by
R_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.
We call an interaction
\varPhi
absolutely summable at
0
if

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\in R_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\end{subarray}}\varPhi(M)

converges absolutely. In that case we denote the limit by
\varPhi_{0}
.

The following construction shows that, under an additional compatibility condition, adding up all the translates of a quasi-local observable yields a
T
-invariant interaction.

Definition A.9
.

Let
T
be a translation. Then

is called
T
-compatible
if
T_{\gamma}\,T_{\tilde{\gamma}}\,A=T_{\gamma+\tilde{\gamma}}\,A
for all
\gamma,\tilde{\gamma}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

.

Let
T
be a translation and

be self-adjoint and

\varPhi_{A}^{T}\colon P_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\to\mathcal{A}^{N},\;M\mapsto\varPhi_{A}^{T}(M)
with

\displaystyle\varPhi_{A}^{T}(M)\coloneq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}T_{\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{0}}A&\mbox{if $M=\Lambda_{0}+\gamma$ for some $\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}$}\\
T_{\gamma}(\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k}}A-\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k-1}}A)&\mbox{if $M=\Lambda_{k}+\gamma$ for some $\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}$ and $k\in\mathbb{N}$}\\

defines a
T
-periodic

-interaction with

. Conversely, if
\varPhi
is a

-periodic

-interaction, then it is absolutely summable at

and
\varPhi_{0}
lies in

Let

and

A.3
The off-diagonal map

We need to introduce a further technical ingredient, namely the map that selects the off-diagonal part of an operator or of an interaction with respect to a ground state
\omega_{0}
, as well as the quasi-local inverse of the Liouvillian associated to the Hamiltonian. The former map was introduced in
[
27
,
8
]
for finite gapped systems and extended in
[
36
]
to infinite systems with a gap in the bulk. Here we further generalize it by composing it with automorphisms of the algebra

.
The definition of both objects involves the choice of a function

W_{g}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
having the following properties for some

:

(i)

is odd;

(ii)

the Fourier transform
\widehat{W}_{g}
of

satisfies
\widehat{W_{g}}(k)=\frac{-\mathrm{i}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,k}
for

;

(iii)

\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\bigl(\lvert s\rvert^{n}\lvert W_{g}(s)\rvert\bigr)<\infty
for all

.

It is easy to see that such a function exists for any

, and an explicit example with additional properties is given in
[
8
, Lemma 2.6]
(see also
[
8
, Lemma 2.3 and Equation 2.12]
).

.

Let

be a translation,

-periodic interaction,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\varPsi)_{*}\coloneq-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}s\,W_{g}(s)\int_{0}^{s}\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}u\mathcal{L}_{H}}\,\mathrm{i}\,\mathcal{L}_{\varPsi}\,H_{0}\,,

defined via Bochner integrals in
\mathcal{A}

are in

Definition A.13
.

Given the assumptions of Lemma

Appendix B
Some technical tools

We collect here a number of results that are instrumental to the proofs of the main results in the paper.

B.1
Basics on non-interacting periodic magnetic systems

We review here the Bloch–Floquet decomposition of operators commuting with a unitary representation of a lattice of (magnetic) translations, as well as their finite-volume approximations with periodic boundary conditions (the so-called supercell restrictions: cf. e.g.
[
24
, Sec. 4]
).

The non-interacting Hamiltonians
\mathfrak{h}^{b}
acting on the one-body space
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{r})
were defined in Section
2.1
.
We check (
4
):

\displaystyle((t^{b}_{\gamma})^{-1}\mathfrak{h}^{b}t^{b}_{\gamma}f)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y^{\prime}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}b(x_{1}-\gamma_{1})\gamma_{2}}\mathfrak{h}^{b}(x-\gamma,y^{\prime})\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}by_{1}^{\prime}\gamma_{2}}f(y^{\prime}+\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y=y^{\prime}+\gamma}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}b(x_{1}-\gamma_{1})\gamma_{2}}\mathfrak{h}^{b}(x-\gamma,y-\gamma)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}b(y_{1}-\gamma_{1})\gamma_{2}}f(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}bx_{1}\gamma_{2}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{x_{2}+y_{2}-2\gamma_{2}}{2}b(x_{1}-y_{1})}h(x-y)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}by_{1}\gamma_{2}}f(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{x_{2}+y_{2}}{2}b(x_{1}-y_{1})}h(x-y)f(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{h}^{b}f)(x)\,.

For
b=2\pi\frac{p}{q}\in Q

magnetic translations by vectors in the sublattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}:=\mathbb{Z}\times(q\mathbb{Z})\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
are actually standard translations:

(t^{b}_{\gamma}f)(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}bx_{1}\gamma_{2}}f(x+\gamma)=f(x+\gamma)=(t^{0}_{\gamma}f)(x)\quad\forall\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}\,,

as the argument of the magnetic phase is an integer multiple of
2\pi
. This allows us to use the Bloch–Floquet transformation with respect to this sublattice in order to diagonalize
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}
-periodic operators.

To define it, we decompose
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
as
x=x_{q}+[x]_{q}
with
x_{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}
and
[x]_{q}\in\{(0,0),\ldots,(0,q-1)\}
, and define
I_{q}:=\{0,\ldots,q-1\}
.
This allows us to identify

\iota_{q}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{r})\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}\times I_{q},\mathbb{C}^{r})\,,\quad f\mapsto(\iota_{q}f)(x,j):=f(x+(0,j))

and map this space to functions on the magnetic Brillouin zone
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q}:=[0,2\pi)\times[0,2\pi/q)
equipped with normalized Lebesgue measure
\mathrm{d}\mu_{q}:=\frac{q\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}
:

\mathcal{F}_{q}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{r})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q}\times I_{q},\mathrm{d}\mu_{q}\times\nu;\mathbb{C}^{r})\,,\quad f\mapsto\hat{f}_{j}(k):=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}kx}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k_{2}j}f(x+(0,j))\,,

with inverse

\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{q}:L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q}\times I_{q},\mathrm{d}\mu_{q}\times\nu;\mathbb{C}^{r})\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{r})\,,\quad f\mapsto\check{f}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}kx}f_{[x]_{q}}(k)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{q}(k)\,.

Let
A\in\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{r}))
be any bounded operator that is translation invariant with respect to translations in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}
, i.e., such that its kernel satisfies

A(x+\gamma,y+\gamma)=A(x,y)\quad\forall\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}\,,\;x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\,,

and is thus of the form

A(x_{q}+[x]_{q},y_{q}+[y]_{q})=A(x_{q}-y_{q}+[x]_{q},[y]_{q})=:a_{[x]_{q},[y]_{q}}(x_{q}-y_{q})\,.

Such operators are matrix-valued multiplication operators in the Bloch–Floquet representation:

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt(\mathcal{F}_{q}\,A\,\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{q}\hat{f})_{j}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}kx}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k_{2}j}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}A(x+(0,j),y)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}ty}\hat{f}_{[y]_{q}}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{q}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}kx}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k_{2}j}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}}\sum_{l\in I_{q}}\underbrace{A(x+(0,j),y+(0,l))}_{\eqcolon a_{jl}(x-y)}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(y+(0,l))}\hat{f}_{l}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{q}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q}}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}}\underbrace{\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}x(k-t)}}_{=\delta(k-t)}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(k_{2}j-t_{2}l)}\sum_{l\in I_{q}}a_{jl}(z)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tz}\hat{f}_{l}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{q}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in I_{q}}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}}a_{jl}(z)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k(z+(0,j-l))}\hat{f}_{l}(k)

\displaystyle\eqcolon\sum_{l\in I_{q}}\hat{a}_{jl}(k)\,\hat{f}_{l}(k)\,.

In particular, this applies to our Hamiltonian, for which we have

\mathfrak{h}^{b}(x+(0,j),y+(0,l))=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{x_{2}+y_{2}+j+l}{2}b(x_{1}-y_{1})}h(x-y+(0,j-l))=:h^{b}_{jl}(x-y)

and thus

(\mathcal{F}_{q}\mathfrak{h}^{b}\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{q}\hat{f})(k)=\hat{h}^{b}(k)\,\hat{f}(k)\quad\mbox{with}\quad\hat{h}^{b}_{jl}(k):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}}h^{b}_{jl}(z)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k(z+(0,j-l))}\,.

Exponential decay of
h(z)
implies that the above Bloch–Floquet fibers
\hat{h}^{b}_{jl}(k)
depend analytically on
k\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q}
, i.e. they can be extended to a complex-analytic function defined on a strip around the “real axis” in the space of complex quasi-momenta. This analyticity is inherited from the fibers of the Fermi projection
P^{\mathfrak{h}^{b}}_{(-\infty,\mu)}
via the Riesz formula, provided
\mu
lies in a spectral gap of
\mathfrak{h}^{b}
; in turn, this yields that the integral kernel
P^{\mathfrak{h}^{b}}_{(-\infty,\mu)}(x,y)
decays exponentially away from the diagonal, compare (
15
).

Next we discuss the finite volume restrictions
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}
-periodic operators to boxes
\Lambda_{k_{n}}
that are commensurate with the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}
-periodicity: let

k_{n}:=\frac{1}{2}((2n+1)q-1)\in\mathbb{N}
and
L:=(2n+1)q
.
We decompose
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
as
x=x^{n}+[x]^{n}
with
x^{n}\in(L\mathbb{Z})^{2}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}
and
[x]^{n}\in\Lambda_{k_{n}}
and define the restriction of a
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}
-periodic operator
A
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{r})
to
\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{k_{n}},\mathbb{C}^{r})
to be the operator
A_{n}
with kernel

A_{n}(x,y):=\sum_{z\in(L\mathbb{Z})^{2}}A(x,y+z)\,.

The (dual) magnetic translations on
\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{k_{n}},\mathbb{C}^{r})
are defined by

t^{b}_{n,\gamma}:\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{k_{n}},\mathbb{C}^{r})\to\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{k_{n}},\mathbb{C}^{r})\,,\quad f\mapsto(t^{b}_{n,\gamma}f)(x):=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}bx_{1}\gamma_{2}}f([x+\gamma]^{n})

with inverse

(t^{b}_{n,\gamma})^{-1}:\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{k_{n}},\mathbb{C}^{r})\to\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{k_{n}},\mathbb{C}^{r})\,,\quad f\mapsto((t^{b}_{n,\gamma})^{-1}f)(x)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}b(x_{1}-\gamma_{1})\gamma_{2}}f([x-\gamma]^{n})\,.

Now the magnetic Bloch–Floquet transform maps to the Brillouin zone
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}:=\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}I_{L}\right)\times\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}I_{L/q}\right)
, where, as above,
I_{m}:=\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
:

\mathcal{F}_{q,n}:\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{k_{n}};\mathbb{C}^{r})\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}\times I_{q};\mathbb{C}^{r})\,,\quad f\mapsto\hat{f}_{j}(k):=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}\cap\Lambda_{k_{n}}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}kx}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k_{2}j}f([x+(0,j)]^{n})\,,

with inverse

\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{q,n}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}\times I_{q};\mathbb{C}^{r})\to\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{k_{n}};\mathbb{C}^{r})\,,\quad f\mapsto\check{f}(x):=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}|}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}kx}f_{[x]_{q}}(k)\,.

For
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}
and
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
we find that

\displaystyle A_{n}([x+(0,j)]^{n},y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in(L\mathbb{Z})^{2}}A([x+(0,j)]^{n},y+z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in(L\mathbb{Z})^{2}}A([x+(0,j)]^{n}+(x+(0,j))^{n},y+z+(x+(0,j))^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in(L\mathbb{Z})^{2}}A(x+(0,j),y+z+(x+(0,j))^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{w\in(L\mathbb{Z})^{2}}A(x+(0,j),y+w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{w\in(L\mathbb{Z})^{2}}A(x-y_{q}-w+(0,j),[y]_{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{w\in(L\mathbb{Z})^{2}}a_{j[y]_{q}}(x-y_{q}-w)

and thus

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt(\mathcal{F}_{q,n}A_{n}\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{q,n}\hat{f})(k,j)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}|}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}\cap\Lambda_{k_{n}}}\!\!\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}kx}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k_{2}j}\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{k_{n}}}A_{n}([x+(0,j)]^{n},y)\sum_{t\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}ty}\hat{f}_{[y]_{q}}(t)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}|}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}\cap\Lambda_{k_{n}}}\!\!\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}kx}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k_{2}j}\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{k_{n}}}\sum_{w\in(L\mathbb{Z})^{2}}a_{j[y]_{q}}(x-y_{q}-w)\sum_{t\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}ty}\hat{f}_{[y]_{q}}(t)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}|}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}\cap\Lambda_{k_{n}}}\!\!\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}kx}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k_{2}j}\!\!\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}\cap\Lambda_{k_{n}}}\sum_{l\in I_{q}}\sum_{w\in(L\mathbb{Z})^{2}}a_{jl}(x-y-w)\sum_{t\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(y+(0,l))}\hat{f}_{l}(t)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}|}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}\cap\Lambda_{k_{n}}}\!\!\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}kx}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k_{2}j}\!\!\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}\cap\Lambda_{k_{n}}}\sum_{l\in I_{q}}\sum_{w\in(L\mathbb{Z})^{2}}a_{jl}(x-(y+w))\sum_{t\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(y+w+(0,l))}\hat{f}_{l}(t)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}|}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}\cap\Lambda_{k_{n}}}\!\!\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}kx}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k_{2}j}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}}\sum_{l\in I_{q}}a_{jl}(x-y)\sum_{t\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t(y+(0,l))}\hat{f}_{l}(t)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}|}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}}\underbrace{\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}\cap\Lambda_{k_{n}}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}x(k-t)}}_{=|\mathbb{T}^{2}_{q,n}|\delta(k-t)}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(k_{2}j-t_{2}l)}\sum_{l\in I_{q}}a_{jl}(z)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tz}\hat{f}_{l}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in I_{q}}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}}a_{jl}(z)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k(z+(0,j-l))}\hat{f}_{l}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in I_{q}}\hat{a}_{jl}(k)\,\hat{f}_{l}(k)\,.

So the restriction acts fiber-wise exactly as the infinite volume operator.

For self-adjoint
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{q}
-periodic operators
A
that implies, in particular, that the restriction
(f(A))_{n}
of any bounded Borel function
f
of
A
to
\Lambda_{k_{n}}
equals
f(A_{n})
. In particular,
we have (

Proof of Lemma

We define the non-periodic finite volume approximation of
\varPhi^{t}
as the interaction given by

\displaystyle\varPhi^{t,k,\mathrm{np}}(M)\coloneq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}T_{\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{0}}\varPhi^{t,k}_{0}&\mbox{if $M=\Lambda_{0}+\gamma$ for some $\gamma\in\Lambda_{k}$}\\
T_{\gamma}(\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{l}}\varPhi^{t,k}_{0}-\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{l-1}}\varPhi^{t,k}_{0})&\mbox{if $M=\Lambda_{l}+\gamma$ for some $\gamma\in\Lambda_{k}$ and $l\in\mathbb{N}$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

and denote its generated cocycle of automorphisms by
(\alpha_{s,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}})_{s,t\in I}
. Similarly we define a coarse grained version of
\varPhi^{t}
by

\displaystyle\varPhi^{t,\mathrm{cg}}(M)\coloneq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}T_{\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{0}}\varPhi^{t}_{0}&\mbox{if $M=\Lambda_{0}+\gamma$ for some $\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}$}\\
T_{\gamma}(\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{l}}\varPhi^{t}_{0}-\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{l-1}}\varPhi^{t}_{0})&\mbox{if $M=\Lambda_{l}+\gamma$ for some $\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}$ and $l\in\mathbb{N}$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

and note that it induces the same derivation on
\mathcal{A}
as
\varPhi^{t}
and therefore generates the same cocycle of automorphisms
(\alpha_{s,t})_{s,t\in I}
. A straightforward computation shows that for
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there is a constant
c_{\nu}
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we have
\lVert\varPhi^{t,k,\mathrm{np}}\rVert_{\nu}\leq c_{\nu}\,\lVert\varPhi_{0}^{t,k}\rVert_{\nu+4}
and
\lVert\varPhi^{t,\mathrm{cg}}\rVert_{\nu}\leq c_{\nu}\,\lVert\varPhi_{0}^{t}\rVert_{\nu+4}
, which gives us a bound for the interaction norms that is uniform in
k
.
Now by
[
10
, Lemma 2.10]
, we have that for each
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there exists an increasing function
f_{\nu}\colon[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
that grows at most polynomially at infinity and satisfies

\displaystyle\lVert\alpha_{s,t}\,A\rVert_{\nu}\leq f_{\nu}(\lvert t-s\rvert)\,\lVert A\rVert_{\nu}

and

\displaystyle\lVert\alpha^{k,\mathrm{np}}_{s,t}\,A\rVert_{\nu}\leq f_{\nu}(\lvert t-s\rvert)\,\lVert A\rVert_{\nu}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
s,t\in I
and
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
s,t\in I
and
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
. By the locality of
(\alpha_{u,v})_{u,v\in I}
we find

\lVert\alpha_{s,t}(A-\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k}}A)\rVert_{\nu}\leq f_{\nu}(|s-t|)\lVert A-\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k}}A\rVert_{\nu}\,\leq\,2\,f_{\nu}(|s-t|)\frac{\lVert A\rVert_{\nu+1}}{(1+k)}

which shows that we can assume
A=\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k}}A
for the rest of the proof.

We now proceed by adding and subtracting the non-periodic approximation in the expression and bounding the two resulting terms separately:

\displaystyle\lVert\alpha_{s,t}\,A-\alpha_{s,t}^{k}\,A\rVert_{\nu}\leq\lVert\alpha_{s,t}\,A-\alpha_{s,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A\rVert_{\nu}+\lVert\alpha_{s,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A-\alpha_{s,t}^{k}\,A\rVert_{\nu}.

In the second term
\alpha_{s,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A-\alpha_{s,t}^{k}\,A
lies in
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{2k}}
and therefore

\displaystyle\lVert\alpha_{s,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A-\alpha_{s,t}^{k}\,A\rVert_{\nu}\leq 3\,(1+2k)^{\nu}\,\lVert\alpha_{s,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A-\alpha_{s,t}^{k}\,A\rVert.

Now the fundamental theorem of calculus implies

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\lVert\alpha_{s,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A-\alpha_{s,t}^{k}\,A\rVert_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq 3\,(1+2k)^{\nu}\int_{s\land t}^{s\lor t}\mathrm{d}u\,\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda_{k}}\lVert[(T_{\gamma}-T_{\gamma}|_{\Lambda_{k}})\,\varPhi_{0}^{u,k},\,\alpha_{u,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A]\rVert

\displaystyle\leq 3\,(1+2k)^{\nu}\int_{s\land t}^{s\lor t}\mathrm{d}u\,\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda_{k}}\lVert[(T_{\gamma}-T_{\gamma}|_{\Lambda_{k}})\,\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k/3}}\,\varPhi_{0}^{u,k},\,\alpha_{u,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A]\rVert

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+3\,(1+2k)^{\nu}\int_{s\land t}^{s\lor t}\mathrm{d}u\,\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda_{k}}\lVert[(T_{\gamma}-T_{\gamma}|_{\Lambda_{k}})\,(1-\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k/3}})\,\varPhi_{0}^{u,k},\,\alpha_{u,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A]\rVert

\displaystyle\eqcolon S_{1}+S_{2}

For
S_{1}
we find

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 3\,(1+2k)^{\nu}\int_{s\land t}^{s\lor t}\mathrm{d}u\,\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda_{2k/3}}\lVert[(T_{\gamma}-T_{\gamma}|_{\Lambda_{k}})\,\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k/3}}\,\varPhi_{0}^{u,k},\,\alpha_{u,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A]\rVert

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+3\,(1+2k)^{\nu}\int_{s\land t}^{s\lor t}\mathrm{d}u\,\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda_{k}\setminus\Lambda_{2k/3}}\lVert[(T_{\gamma}-T_{\gamma}|_{\Lambda_{k}})\,\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k/3}}\,\varPhi_{0}^{u,k},\,\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k/3}}\,\alpha_{u,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A]\rVert

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+3\,(1+2k)^{\nu}\int_{s\land t}^{s\lor t}\mathrm{d}u\,\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda_{k}\setminus\Lambda_{2k/3}}\lVert[(T_{\gamma}-T_{\gamma}|_{\Lambda_{k}})\,\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k/3}}\,\varPhi_{0}^{u,k},\,(1-\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k/3}})\,\alpha_{u,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A]\rVert,

where the first term vanishes, since the two translations act identically and the second term vanishes, since the operators in the commutator are supported on disjoint sets with
\varPhi_{0}^{u,k}\in\mathcal{A}^{N}
. For the remaining part we find

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 3\,(1+2k)^{\nu}\int_{s\land t}^{s\lor t}\mathrm{d}u\,\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda_{k}\setminus\Lambda_{2k/3}}4\,\lVert\varPhi_{0}^{u,k}\rVert\,\lVert(1-\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k/3}})\,\alpha_{u,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A\rVert

\displaystyle\leq 3\,(1+2k)^{\nu}\int_{s\land t}^{s\lor t}\mathrm{d}u\,\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda_{k}\setminus\Lambda_{2k/3}}4\,\lVert\varPhi_{0}^{u,k}\rVert\,\frac{\lVert\alpha_{u,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A\rVert_{\nu+3}}{(1+k/3)^{\nu+3}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{12\,(1+2k)^{\nu+2}}{(1+k/3)^{\nu+3}}\,\lvert t-s\rvert\,\sup_{u\in[s\land t,s\lor t]}\lVert\varPhi_{0}^{u,k}\rVert\,f_{\nu+3}(\lvert t-u\rvert)\,\lVert A\rVert_{\nu+3},

which is a bound of the required form. Similarly,
S_{2}
can be bounded as follows

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 12\,(1+2k)^{\nu+2}\int_{s\land t}^{s\lor t}\mathrm{d}u\,\lVert(1-\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{k/3}})\,\varPhi_{0}^{u,k}\rVert\,\lVert A\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{12\,(1+2k)^{\nu+2}}{(1+k/3)^{\nu+3}}\,\lvert t-s\rvert\,\sup_{u\in[s\land t,s\lor t]}\lVert\varPhi_{0}^{u,k}\rVert_{\nu+3}\,\lVert A\rVert.

It remains to show that
\lVert\alpha_{s,t}\,A-\alpha_{s,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A\rVert_{\nu}
admits the same kind of bound. Again, the fundamental theorem of calculus yields

\displaystyle\lVert\alpha_{s,t}\,A-\alpha_{s,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A\rVert_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{s\land t}^{s\lor t}\mathrm{d}u\,\lVert\alpha_{s,u}\,(\mathrm{i}\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{u}}-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{u,k,\mathrm{np}}})\,\alpha_{u,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A\rVert_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{s\land t}^{s\lor t}\mathrm{d}u\,f_{\nu}(\lvert u-s\rvert)\,\lVert(\mathrm{i}\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{u}}-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{u,k,\mathrm{np}}})\,\alpha_{u,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A\rVert_{\nu}.

(32)

Next we write the difference of the generators as a sum, which we then split into two parts:

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\lVert(\mathrm{i}\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{u}}-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{u,k,\mathrm{np}}})\,\alpha_{u,t}^{k,\mathrm{np}}\,A\rVert_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\Lambda_{k}}\lVert[T_{\gamma}\,\varPhi_{0}^{u},\,\alpha^{k,\mathrm{np}}_{u,t}\,A]\rVert_{\nu}+\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda_{k}}\lVert[T_{\gamma}\,(\varPhi_{0}^{u}-\varPhi^{u,k}_{0}),\,\alpha^{k,\mathrm{np}}_{u,t}\,A]\rVert_{\nu}

(33)

\displaystyle\eqcolon S_{3}+S_{4}.

We again estimate the two terms separately, beginning with

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\Lambda_{k}}4^{\nu+7}\,\frac{\lVert\varPhi^{u}_{0}\rVert_{\nu+4}\,\lVert\alpha^{k,\mathrm{np}}_{u,t}\,A\rVert_{\nu+4}}{(1+\lVert\gamma\rVert)^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{\nu+7}\,\frac{f_{\nu+4}(\lvert t-u\rvert)\,\lVert A\rVert_{\nu+4}}{1+k}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{\sup_{v\in I}\lVert\varPhi^{v}_{0}\rVert_{\nu+4}}{(1+\lVert\gamma\rVert)^{3}}.

Next

\displaystyle S_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda_{k}}4^{\nu+3}\,\frac{\lVert\varPhi^{u}_{0}-\varPhi^{u,k}_{0}\rVert_{\nu+3}\,\lVert\alpha^{k,\mathrm{np}}_{u,t}\,A\rVert_{\nu+3}}{(1+\lVert\gamma\rVert)^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq f_{\nu+3}(\lvert t-u\rvert)\,\lVert A\rVert_{\nu+3}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}4^{\nu+3}\,\frac{\sup_{v\in I}\lVert\varPhi^{v}_{0}-\varPhi^{v,k}_{0}\rVert_{\nu+3}}{(1+\lVert\gamma\rVert)^{3}},

where the sum goes to
0
as
k\to\infty
due to dominated convergence. After estimating the integral in (
32
) with a supremum, this gives us the desired bound on
\lVert\alpha_{s,t}\,A-\alpha_{s,t}^{k}\,A\rVert_{\nu}
.

To show the bound for
\lVert(\mathrm{i}\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{t}}-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{t,k}})\,A\rVert_{\nu}
we also insert the non-periodic approximation

\displaystyle\lVert(\mathrm{i}\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{t}}-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{t,k}})\,A\rVert_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert(\mathrm{i}\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{t}}-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{t,k,\mathrm{np}}})\,A\rVert_{\nu}+\lVert(\mathrm{i}\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{t,k,\mathrm{np}}}-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{t,k}})\,A\rVert_{\nu}

and follow the same steps as above with
s=t
.
∎

As in the proof of Lemma

which generates the same cocycle of automorphisms as

and satisfies

for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with some constant
c_{\nu}>0
that is independent of
n
.
Due to
[
10

, this implies that for each
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there exists an increasing function
f_{\nu}\colon[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
that grows at most polynomially at infinity and satisfies

\displaystyle\lVert\alpha_{(s,t),n}\,A\rVert_{\nu}\leq f_{\nu}(\lvert t-s\rvert)\,\lVert A\rVert_{\nu}

for all
s,t\in I
,
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
and

. Now let
n,\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
s,t\in I
and
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
. By the fundamental theorem of calculus we find

\displaystyle\hskip-10.00002pt\lVert\alpha_{(s,t),n}\,A-\alpha_{(s,t),\infty}\,A\rVert_{\nu}

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sup_{u\in I}\,\lVert\varPhi^{u,n}_{0}-(T_{\gamma}^{n})^{-1}\,T_{\gamma}^{\infty}\,\varPhi^{u,\infty}_{0}\rVert_{\nu+3}

\varPhi_{0}^{u,n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\rightarrow}}\varPhi_{0}^{u,\infty}
in
\mathcal{A}_{\nu+3}

and the fact that
\lVert(T_{\gamma}^{n})^{-1}\,T_{\gamma}^{\infty}\,\varPhi^{u,\infty}_{0}\rVert_{\nu}=\lVert\varPhi^{u,\infty}_{0}\rVert_{\nu}
. The latter statement follows from the fact that the automorphism
(T_{\gamma}^{n})^{-1}\,T_{\gamma}^{\infty}
does not change the support of operators, which allows us to conclude
\mathbb{E}_{M}\,(T_{\gamma}^{n})^{-1}\,T_{\gamma}^{\infty}=(T_{\gamma}^{n})^{-1}\,T_{\gamma}^{\infty}\,\mathbb{E}_{M}
for all
M\in P_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})\,
from the uniqueness in Proposition
A.1
.

The bound

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I}\sup_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\lVert\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{t,n}}A-\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{t}}A\rVert_{\nu}}{\lVert A\rVert_{\nu+3}}\leq 4^{\nu+6}\,\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{\sup_{u\in I}\lVert\varPhi^{u,n}_{0}-(T_{\gamma}^{n})^{-1}\,T_{\gamma}^{\infty}\,\varPhi^{u,\infty}_{0}\rVert_{\nu+3}}{(1+\lVert\gamma\rVert)^{3}},

which can be obtained by following the same steps and together the two bounds imply the claimed statements.
∎

B.2.3
Proof of Lemma
3.4

Since automorphisms on the CAR algebra are determined by their action on the annihilation operators it suffices to show the identity only for them. Let
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,r\}

\displaystyle(T^{b}_{\gamma})^{-1}\,T_{\gamma}^{b_{0}}\,a_{y,j}

\displaystyle=(T^{b}_{\gamma})^{-1}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}b_{0}y_{1}\gamma_{2}}\,a_{y+\gamma,j}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}b_{0}y_{1}\gamma_{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}b(y+\gamma-\gamma)_{1}\gamma_{2}}\,a_{y,j}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(b-b_{0})y_{1}\gamma_{2}}\,a_{y,j}.

For the right-hand side one can now compare with the above value

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\left\|\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(b-b_{0})\gamma_{2}\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(b-b_{0})y_{1}\gamma_{2}}\right)a_{y,j}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{b\land b_{0}}^{b\lor b_{0}}\mathrm{d}s\,\left\|\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(s-b_{0})\gamma_{2}\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}}\,(-\mathrm{i}\,\gamma_{2}\,\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}-\mathrm{i}\,y_{1}\,\gamma_{2})\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(b-s)y_{1}\gamma_{2}}\,a_{y,j}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\lvert b-b_{0}\rvert\,\lvert\gamma_{2}\rvert\,\lVert\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}\,a_{y,j}+y_{1}\,a_{y,j}\rVert=0.

To show the bound, we first note that, since
X_{1}
is an on-site interaction, its generated group of automorphisms
s\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}s\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}}
does not change the support of operators. Hence, for all
M\in P_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
it holds that
\mathbb{E}_{M}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}s\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}s\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}}\,\mathbb{E}_{M}
, and therefore, for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
, one finds
\lVert\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}s\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}}\,A\rVert_{\nu}=\lVert A\rVert_{\nu}
. Now a simple Duhamel argument yields

\displaystyle\lVert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(b-b_{0})\gamma_{2}\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}}\,A-A\rVert_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{b\land b_{0}}^{b\lor b_{0}}\mathrm{d}s\,\lVert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(s-b_{0})\gamma_{2}\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}}\,(-\mathrm{i})\,\gamma_{2}\,\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}\,A\rVert_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\lvert b-b_{0}\rvert\,\lvert\gamma_{2}\rvert\,\lVert\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}\,A\rVert_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\lvert b-b_{0}\rvert\,\lvert\gamma_{2}\rvert\,\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}4^{7+\nu}\,\frac{\lvert x_{1}\rvert\,\lVert n_{0}\rVert_{\nu+4}\,\lVert A\rVert_{\nu+4}}{(1+\lVert x\rVert)^{4}},

where we used
[
47
, Lemma A.1]
in the last step.
