Prioritizing Nursing Care Improvements in Chronic Disease Outpatients: A Cross-Sectional Study Based on SERVQUAL and Kano Models
Authors: Na Liu, Wen Wang, Yuanyuan Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveWe aimed to analyze the requirement attributes and service quality gap characteristics in nursing care for patients with chronic diseases based on the Service Quality (SERVQUAL) model and Kano model, and to create a two-dimensional framework for priority analysis.MethodsA cross-sectional survey was conducted among chronic disease outpatients in a tertiary hospital. A modified Kano-SERVQUAL questionnaire was used to assess nursing service expectations, perceptions, and requirement attributes. SERVQUAL Gap scores, Kano classifications, Better and Worse coefficients, and the comprehensive improvement index (CII) were calculated. A Kano-SERVQUAL priority matrix and Kano-SERVQUAL Sensitivity Prioritization Model (KSSPM) were constructed, and subgroup analysis by specialty was performed.ResultsA total of 490 patients were included. Perception scores were lower than expectations in all SERVQUAL dimensions. Gap scores for tangibles, responsiveness, and empathy were significantly smaller in special outpatient clinics than in general outpatient clinics (P<0.05). Nursing safety assurance, information communication clarity, and privacy protection were classified as must-be requirements, whereas continuous follow-up, personalized health guidance, and psychological support were attractive requirements. Their Better coefficients were 0.76, 0.72, and 0.68, respectively, and they were identified as key improvement priorities in the Kano-SERVQUAL matrix and KSSPM. Oncology patients showed higher Better coefficients for these priority items (P<0.05).ConclusionOutpatient nursing care does not fully meet expectations. Continuous follow-up, personalized guidance, and psychological support are key priorities for improvement. The integrated SERVQUAL-Kano model may support resource allocation and refined nursing management.

Introduction

As the prevalence of chronic diseases continues to rise, outpatient clinics have gradually become an important setting for long-term follow-up and continuous nursing care. Outpatient nursing plays a key role in disease monitoring, health education, self-management support, and treatment adherence, and its quality directly affects patients’ healthcare experience, disease management outcomes, and medical resource use.1 Recent evidence also suggests that nurse-led interventions can improve medication adherence and health-related outcomes in patients with chronic diseases or multimorbidity, further highlighting the need to optimize outpatient nursing services.2 Therefore, systematically assessing and improving outpatient nursing care has become an important issue in chronic disease management.

The Service Quality (SERVQUAL) model has been widely used to evaluate healthcare service quality. Based on the perception-expectation gap, it identifies dimensions in which perceived care falls short of patient expectations and provides a quantitative basis for quality improvement.3 In medical settings, SERVQUAL has been applied to outpatient services, inpatient care, emergency management, and community health services, and its validity has been confirmed in different cultural and institutional contexts.4 Relevant research has shown that SERVQUAL can systematically reflect patients’ evaluations of tangibles, reliability, responsiveness, assurance, and empathy, thereby providing practical guidance for service process optimization and environmental improvement.5 However, SERVQUAL mainly focuses on the size of service gaps, while patient expectations may vary according to previous healthcare experience, disease status, and service context. Therefore, SERVQUAL alone may not fully explain which service attributes are most likely to improve satisfaction or prevent dissatisfaction.

The Kano model, based on the theory of satisfaction formation, classifies service attributes into must-be, one-dimensional, and attractive requirements, and emphasizes the nonlinear and asymmetric effects of different service attributes on satisfaction.6 Must-be requirements mainly prevent dissatisfaction, one-dimensional requirements show a relatively linear relationship with satisfaction, and attractive requirements can substantially enhance satisfaction when fulfilled, even if they are not explicitly expected.7 In recent years, the Kano model has been increasingly applied in medical and nursing care to identify high-value service items and differentiated service strategies.8 In patient-centered care, attractive and one-dimensional requirements often involve experiential dimensions such as communication quality, individualized support, and emotional care.9 Integrating SERVQUAL with the Kano model may therefore help identify not only where service gaps exist, but also which nursing service items have greater potential to improve satisfaction or prevent dissatisfaction.

In the context of Chinese tertiary hospitals, special outpatient clinics usually refer to appointment-based outpatient services with relatively individualized consultation arrangements, more flexible service processes, and enhanced patient support, whereas general outpatient clinics mainly provide routine outpatient diagnosis and follow-up services through standard registration and consultation procedures. Besides, different outpatient service models may vary in treatment process, resource allocation, and service focus, and patients’ expectations and requirements for nursing care may also differ.10 However, quantitative evidence regarding the nursing requirement characteristics of patients with chronic diseases and corresponding differences in improvement priorities across different outpatient settings remains limited.

Based on the above background and research gaps, the present study recruited chronic disease outpatients and constructed a Kano-SERVQUAL two-dimensional priority framework. Furthermore, by incorporating the comprehensive improvement index (CII), we proposed a Kano-SERVQUAL Sensitivity Prioritization Model (KSSPM), which visually combines service gaps, satisfaction-enhancing potential, and improvement priority in one decision framework. This model may help nursing managers identify services that are both insufficient and highly valuable to patients. Through comparison between special and general outpatient clinics, this study aims to provide evidence for more targeted allocation and refined management of outpatient nursing resources.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Subjects

This cross-sectional survey study was conducted in June 2025 in the outpatient clinics of a tertiary hospital, and a questionnaire survey was completed on chronic disease patients who received follow-up management during their consecutive visits, covering Departments of Cardiology, Urology, Nephrology, Oncology and Dermatology. Subjects were from special outpatient clinics (namely outpatient clinics providing appointment-based and personalized services) and general outpatient clinics. Finally, a total of 490 patients were enrolled, including 238 from special outpatient clinics and 252 from general outpatient clinics. The present study was approved by the Second Hospital of Tianjin Medical University Ethics Committee (updated approval No. KY2026K260 (due to a recent hospital ethics approval numbering system update, the ethics approval number is replaced to adhere to the latest numbering system [former approval no: TSHOTMU202402001]). All subjects signed the written informed consent form, and the survey was performed in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants joined the study voluntarily, completed the questionnaire anonymously, and their data were kept confidential.

The following inclusion criteria were adopted: (1) patients with an age of ≥18 years old, (2) those who had been diagnosed with chronic diseases by specialists and followed up for ≥3 months, and (3) those able to communicate and independently complete questionnaires. The executed exclusion criteria were listed below: (1) patients with cognitive dysfunction or severe mental diseases, (2) those in emergency or with unstable condition, or (3) those offering incomplete questionnaires or questionnaires with obvious logical contradictions.

Sample Size Estimation

The sample size was estimated according to the commonly used rule for questionnaire-based studies, which recommends that the sample size should be at least 5–10 times the number of questionnaire items.11 Considering the number of questionnaire items and the estimated proportion of invalid questionnaires, the minimum required sample size was set at 300 patients. Finally, totally 490 patients were recruited in the present study, meeting the requirements for statistical analysis.

Survey Tools

The survey tool used in the present study was built based on the SERVQUAL scale and the Kano model. The SERVQUAL scale, proposed by Parasuraman et al, serves as a tool for assessing the gap between expectations for services and actual perceptions, covering five dimensions: tangibles, reliability, responsiveness, assurance, and empathy.12 In the present study, the original items in the scale were adaptably adjusted based on the setting of outpatient nursing care for chronic diseases. A 5-point Likert scale was employed to measure patients’ scores on expectations and perceptions for nursing care, with 1 point indicating “strongly disagreed” and 5 points denoting “strongly agreed”. Higher scores indicating higher expectations or better perceived service quality. The Gap score was calculated as perception score minus expectation score. A negative Gap score indicated that perceived service quality was lower than patient expectations.

The Kano section was designed according to the Kano model and used paired functional and dysfunctional questions to assess patients’ requirement attributes for each nursing care item.13 Respondents evaluated each item under two scenarios, namely “service provided” and “service not provided”. Based on the Kano evaluation table, each item was classified as attractive (A), one-dimensional (O), must-be (M), indifferent (I), reverse (R), or questionable (Q). A five-level response scale was used, including “I like it”, “I expect it”, “I am neutral”, “I can tolerate it”, and “I dislike it”. The category with the highest proportion was regarded as the dominant attribute of that item.

Through a systematic literature review on questionnaire items, an initial framework was developed, followed by two rounds of expert consultation and content revision by nursing management experts and specialist nurses. The initial item pool was designed to cover key components of chronic disease outpatient nursing care, including basic safety assurance, information communication, privacy protection, waiting process guidance, nurse response, continuous follow-up, individualized health guidance, and psychological support. These items were selected because they reflected both basic service requirements and experience-oriented nursing needs in chronic disease outpatient care. During expert consultation, items with overlapping meanings, low clinical relevance, or limited feasibility were revised or removed. Finally, eight nursing service requirement items were retained for the formal questionnaire.

Testing of Reliability and Validity

Before the formal survey, the modified Kano-SERVQUAL questionnaire was developed and evaluated through a structured process. First, an initial item pool was generated based on the theoretical framework of the SERVQUAL scale and the Kano model, combined with a literature review on chronic disease outpatient nursing care and the practical characteristics of follow-up management in special outpatient clinics. The initial items covered key aspects of chronic disease outpatient nursing services, including nursing safety assurance, information communication, privacy protection, waiting process guidance, nurse response, continuous follow-up, individualized health guidance, and psychological support.

Second, the draft questionnaire was reviewed by a panel of seven experts, including three nursing management experts, three specialist nurses from chronic disease-related outpatient departments, and one expert in nursing education and questionnaire methodology. Two rounds of expert consultation were conducted to assess the clinical relevance, clarity, comprehensiveness, and feasibility of each item. Items with overlapping meanings, insufficient relevance to outpatient nursing practice, or limited feasibility were revised or removed according to expert feedback. After expert consultation, eight nursing service requirement items were retained in the formal questionnaire. The item-level content validity index ranged from 0.86 to 1.00, and the scale-level content validity index was 0.94, indicating good content validity.

A pilot test was then conducted among 50 chronic disease outpatients before the formal survey. The pilot participants were not included in the final analysis. The results showed that the questionnaire was easy to understand and can be completed within approximately 8–12 minutes. Internal consistency reliability was assessed using Cronbach’s α coefficient. The overall Cronbach’s α coefficient of the modified Kano-SERVQUAL questionnaire was 0.912. The Cronbach’s α coefficients of the expectation and perception sections were 0.893 and 0.905, respectively. The Cronbach’s α coefficients of the five SERVQUAL dimensions ranged from 0.812 to 0.876, indicating acceptable to good internal consistency.

Construct validity was assessed using the Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin test and Bartlett’s test of sphericity. The KMO value was 0.887, and Bartlett’s test of sphericity was statistically significant, χ2=1246.37, df=120, P<0.001, indicating that the data were suitable for factor analysis and subsequent validity evaluation. Exploratory factor analysis showed that the extracted factors were generally consistent with the theoretical dimensions of SERVQUAL, and the cumulative variance contribution rate was 71.42%. These results supported the reliability and validity of the modified Kano-SERVQUAL questionnaire for assessing nursing service requirements among chronic disease outpatients.

Data Collection

The questionnaire (in print and digital versions) was distributed by uniformly trained investigators in outpatient waiting areas or immediately after the consultation. Investigators explained the purpose of the survey, provided on-site guidance on the completion of the questionnaire, and reviewed for the completeness of the questionnaire to minimize missing data or logical inconsistencies. Sample collection was accomplished through consecutive convenience sampling.

SERVQUAL P-E Gap Score Analysis

The patients’ scores on expectations and perceptions for nursing care were calculated separately, and the Gap score (P-E) was used to indicate the gap in service quality. A negative Gap score denoted that the current level of service fell below patient expectations.

Kano Model-Based Requirement Attribute Analysis

The functional and dysfunctional responses to nursing care items were coded based on the Kano evaluation table to classify requirements into different attribute categories. On this basis, the Better coefficient (reflecting the potential of service improvement to enhance satisfaction) and the Worse coefficient (reflecting the risk of dissatisfaction caused by service deficiencies) were computed. The Better and Worse coefficients were calculated as follows: Better = (A + O)/(A + O + M + I), and Worse = −(O + M)/(A + O + M + I). Reverse and questionable responses were not included in the calculation of Better and Worse coefficients.

Construction of Kano-SERVQUAL Two-Dimensional Priority Matrix

A two-dimensional priority matrix was built based on the Better coefficient from the Kano model and the SERVQUAL Gap score, with the Better coefficient as the vertical axis and the Gap score as the horizontal axis. With the median Better coefficient and the mean Gap score as cutoff points, nursing care items were divided into critical improvement zone, advantage maintenance zone, potential enhancement zone and low-priority zone to identify priorities for nursing care improvement.

Construction of KSSPM

Based on the aforementioned analysis, a KSSPM was created, with the Better coefficient as the vertical axis and the Gap score as the horizontal axis. In addition, the CII (CII = Better coefficient × |Gap score|) was incorporated to denote the size of bubbles, and the bubble diagram offered an intuitive representation of the priorities of nursing care items.

Subgroup Analysis of Differences by Specialties

To explore the differences in nursing requirements of patients from different specialties in special outpatient clinics, one-way analysis of variance was carried out with the Better coefficient as the indicator. Patients from Departments of Cardiology, Urology, Nephrology, Oncology and Dermatology were compared for differences in diverse nursing care items.

Statistical Analysis

SPSS 26.0 software was adopted for statistical analysis of all data. The format of mean ± standard deviation was employed to describe continuous variables, which were subjected to the Shapiro–Wilk test for normality before statistical analysis and found to be normally distributed. Their between-group comparisons were completed via the independent sample t-test. Enumeration data were expressed as frequencies and percentages and compared through the chi-square test between groups. Multi-group comparisons were performed using one-way ANOVA, followed by Tukey’s honestly significant difference (HSD) test for post-hoc pairwise comparisons when the overall ANOVA result was statistically significant. All statistical tests were two-sided, with P<0.05 indicating a statistically significant difference.

Priorities of Nursing Requirements and Three-Level Intervention Framework Outcomes for Patients with Chronic Diseases from the Department of Oncology

In the subgroup analysis by specialties, patients from the Department of Oncology displayed high Better coefficients in psychological support nursing, personalized health guidance and continuous follow-up services. Post-hoc analysis using Tukey’s HSD test further showed that oncology patients had significantly higher Better coefficients for continuous follow-up services than all other specialty subgroups. For personalized health guidance, oncology patients had significantly higher Better coefficients than patients from the Departments of Urology, Nephrology, and Dermatology. For psychological support nursing, oncology patients had significantly higher Better coefficients than patients from the Department of Cardiology. To quantify the priorities for improving nursing care in the Department of Oncology, the Oncology Priority Index (OPI), calculated as (OPI = Better_{oncology} × |Gap|), was obtained from Kano-SERVQUAL two-dimensional analysis, thus comprehensively reflecting the combined effect of nursing care requirement responsiveness and service gap. It was uncovered that continuous follow-up services (OPI=0.336), personalized health guidance (OPI=0.296) and psychological support nursing (OPI=0.284) ranked the highest, implying a large service gap and a high potential for satisfaction enhancement in these three items in the Department of Oncology. In contrast, the OPI values of basic assurance items nursing safety assurance, information communication clarity and privacy protection measures were relatively small, suggesting limited priority for improvement.

Based on the above ranking results, the high-priority items were integrated to develop a three-level nursing pathway structure for the Department of Oncology, encompassing supportive communication and personalized health guidance at the individual interaction level, strengthened follow-up connection at the process coordination level, and long-term disease management and continuous nursing liaison at the continuous nursing level. In general, they demonstrate the comprehensive requirement characteristics concerning emotional support and continuous management.

Discussion

In the present study, the Kano-SERVQUAL two-dimensional model was used to analyze nursing care requirements and improvement priorities among patients with chronic diseases in special and general outpatient clinics. Overall, negative SERVQUAL Gap scores indicated that current outpatient nursing care did not fully meet patient expectations. By combining service gaps with Kano requirement attributes, continuous follow-up services, personalized health guidance, and psychological support nursing were identified as priority items. Rather than relying on service gaps alone, this integrated framework allowed nursing care needs to be interpreted from both unmet expectations and satisfaction-enhancing potential, providing a more nuanced perspective for outpatient nursing improvement.

In the SERVQUAL evaluation, the score on perceptions was found to be lower than that on expectations in the five dimensions of tangibles, reliability, responsiveness, assurance and empathy in patients with chronic disease in both special outpatient clinics and general outpatient clinics, resulting in negatively distributed Gap scores. This denotes that current outpatient nursing care as a whole fails to fully reach the expected level of patients, consistent with the findings of previous research on the quality of outpatient services for chronic diseases,14 reflecting that in the context of long-term disease management, patients hold relatively high holistic expectations regarding nursing care. Further, the Gap scores were compared between the two groups, and the results revealed that patients in special outpatient clinics had significantly lower absolute Gap scores in the dimensions of tangibles, responsiveness and empathy than those in general outpatient clinics, indicating that physical environmental conditions, service response efficiency and humanistic care provided in special outpatient clinics are closer to patient expectations. This difference may be ascribed to the fact that appointment-based management is generally implemented, treatment process is relatively simplified, and the allocation of nursing human resources is relatively sufficient in special outpatient clinics, allowing nursing staff to allocate more time to communication and tailored patient support.15 However, education level differed significantly between the two groups, and this factor may have influenced how patients formed expectations and interpreted perceived nursing service quality. Patients with higher educational levels may have greater health literacy and more explicit expectations regarding communication, service efficiency, and individualized support. Therefore, the observed differences in SERVQUAL Gap scores between special outpatient clinics and general outpatient clinics should be interpreted with consideration of this potential confounding factor. By contrast, the difference in the Gap scores of reliability and assurance between the two groups did not reach statistical significance, denoting that patients from both special outpatient clinics and general outpatient clinics have relatively consistent basic perceptions of professionalism and safety of nursing care.

Furthermore, the attributes of outpatient nursing care items for chronic diseases were classified based on the Kano model, and it was found that different nursing care items exhibited obvious hierarchical characteristics in requirement attributes. Nursing safety assurance, information communication clarity, and privacy protection measures were primarily classified as must-be requirements, denoting that patients generally regard them as the basic prerequisites of nursing care: Their absence will significantly lead to dissatisfaction, but satisfaction will not necessarily have an elevation once they are fully met.16 This also aligns with the theoretical essence of the Kano model on “preventing dissatisfaction rather than creating satisfaction”.17 In contrast, continuous follow-up services, personalized health guidance, and psychological support nursing were mainly classified as attractive requirements in the present study, with high Better coefficients, implying their greater potential in enhancing patient satisfaction. The Better and Worse coefficients in the present study were generally comparable with previous Kano-based medical and nursing studies. Yao et al6 reported Better coefficients of 0.57–0.66 and Worse coefficients of −0.22 to −0.35 for attractive nursing service demands among emergency observation patients. Similarly, Cao et al8 reported attractive nursing service items with Better coefficients of approximately 0.49–0.74 and Worse coefficients of approximately −0.09 to −0.44. In the present study, continuous follow-up services, personalized health guidance and psychological support nursing showed Better coefficients of 0.76, 0.72 and 0.68, respectively, suggesting relatively strong satisfaction-enhancing potential.

From the perspective of characteristics of chronic disease nursing, patients often face such challenges as protracted disease course, repeated treatment cycles, and high disease uncertainty, and thus exhibit an increasing reliance on continuous follow-up and personalized health guidance.18 Continuous follow-up services contribute to strengthening the transitional care between outpatient clinic and community settings and enhancing patients’ understanding and implementation of disease management plans, while personalized health guidance can render targeted support based on patients’ specific condition, cognitive levels, and lifestyles, thereby enhancing self-management ability.19 Moreover, patients with chronic diseases often experience varying degrees of anxiety, depression, and concerns about prognosis, and psychological support nursing plays a vital role in alleviating negative emotions and enhancing treatment confidence.20 Notably, nursing safety assurance and information communication clarity were primarily classified as must-be requirements in the present study, but they had relatively high Worse coefficients, signifying that the absence of such services will significantly raise the risk of patient dissatisfaction. This result underscores that it remains essential to maintain basic assurance services at a stable level while optimizing attractive requirements, so as to prevent adverse impacts on overall satisfaction.

Moreover, in the present study, the SERVQUAL and Kano models were integrated to create the Kano-SERVQUAL two-dimensional priority matrix and KSSPM, which were utilized to comprehensively assess nursing care items from the two dimensions of “service gap size” and “potential for satisfaction enhancement”. The results of two-dimensional analysis showed that continuous follow-up services, personalized health guidance and psychological support nursing were simultaneously located in the high Better-large-negative Gap zone, denoting that a large gap exists between current service levels and patient expectations of the above items, and these items may represent promising targets for improving patient experience. This result further corroborates the classification of attractive requirements in the preceding Kano model-based attribute analysis, suggesting that continuous and emotion-focused nursing care possesses both “urgency” and “high value” in the chronic disease outpatient setting. On this basis, subgroup analysis by specialties was further conducted on patients from special outpatient clinics in the present study, and it was uncovered that patients from different specialties had significantly different Better coefficients in continuous follow-up services, personalized health guidance, and psychological support nursing, with those from the Department of Oncology having higher Better coefficients in the above items, possibly attributed to the characteristics of substantial disease burden, prolonged disease course, complicated treatment process, and high psychological distress.21 The KSSPM visualized the CII in the form of bubbles of varying sizes, making the priority distribution of different nursing care items more intuitive, may help nursing managers identify potential high-priority targets and provide a practical reference for more targeted resource allocation.22

The present study has several limitations. First, this was a single-center cross-sectional survey, and participants were recruited using a non-random sampling method from the same tertiary hospital, which may introduce selection bias and limit the generalizability of the findings. In addition, the eight nursing care items were developed in the context of chronic disease outpatient care in a tertiary hospital; therefore, their applicability to community hospitals, primary care institutions, or other healthcare systems requires further validation. Second, the data were mainly collected through patient self-reported questionnaires and may be affected by social desirability and recall bias. Third, some specialty subgroup sample sizes were relatively small, so the subgroup findings should be interpreted with caution. Finally, because of the cross-sectional design, this study identified nursing care priorities but did not verify the actual effectiveness of Kano-SERVQUAL-guided interventions. Multicenter longitudinal or interventional studies are required to validate the effectiveness of nursing optimization strategies guided by the two-dimensional priority ranking.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the optimization of outpatient nursing care for chronic diseases should shift from a sole focus on service gaps to simultaneous consideration of improvement gains and patient experiential value. The results of the present study suggest that continuous follow-up services, personalized health guidance, and psychological support nursing may be important priorities for improvement in the current outpatient setting, and that patients from different specialties may show different levels of sensitivity to these high-value nursing services. Priority ranking based on the integrated Kano-SERVQUAL two-dimensional model may provide a useful reference for the rational allocation and refined management of outpatient nursing resources. Meanwhile, basic requirements such as nursing safety assurance and privacy protection should remain consistently maintained, as these fundamental elements are essential for preventing patient dissatisfaction. Further multicenter longitudinal or interventional studies are needed to validate the practical effectiveness of this prioritization framework.
