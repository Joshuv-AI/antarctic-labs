Tuning A Rotating Black Hole Spectrum with Dark Matter Halo: Quasibound States, Scalar Cloud, Black Hole Bomb and Superradiant Scattering
David Senjaya

Abstract
We investigate the spectral dynamics of a rotating black hole embedded in a Dehnen ( 1 , 4 , γ ) (1,4,\gamma) dark matter halo, where quasibound states and superradiant scattering jointly characterize the physical response of the system. Starting from an exact Schwarzschild–Dehnen solution, we construct its rotating counterpart via the Newman–Janis algorithm, yielding a consistent axisymmetric geometry that incorporates the influence of a structured halo. The Dehnen profile, through its inner slope parameter γ \gamma , introduces a controlled deformation of both the near-horizon and asymptotic regions of the spacetime. Using the analytical asymptotic matching method, we derive the quasibound-state spectrum and show that the real part of the frequency retains a hydrogen-like structure, but is systematically shifted by the halo through the effective mass scale ρ 0 ​ r 0 3 / ( γ − 3 ) \rho_{0}r_{0}^{3}/(\gamma-3) . In particular, denser, more extended, and more cuspy halos enhance the binding energy, lower the critical mass required for the onset of instability, and typically suppress the growth rate of the black hole bomb. In the scattering sector, we obtain an analytic expression for the superradiant amplification factor and find that the same halo properties that strengthen binding effects also tend to narrow the superradiant window. These results demonstrate that quasibound states and superradiant scattering are complementary manifestations of a unified spectral structure, with the Dehnen halo acting as an environmental tuner that imprints its properties directly onto both the resonance spectrum and the energy-extraction channels of the rotating black hole.

Introduction

Black holes in realistic astrophysical environments are not isolated systems, but are instead embedded within complex structures composed of baryonic matter, plasma, and dark matter. Observational evidence strongly supports the existence of supermassive black holes at the centers of galaxies
Rees 1984
;
Kormendy and Richstone 1995
, while galactic systems themselves are known to reside inside extended dark matter halos
Bertone and Tait 2018
. It is therefore clear that the idealized description of black holes as vacuum solutions must be refined by incorporating the gravitational influence of surrounding matter distributions, especially when addressing dynamical processes sensitive to the global spacetime structure.

Among the most significant phenomena in rotating black hole physics are quasibound states (QBSs) and superradiant scattering, both arising from the interaction between bosonic fields and curved spacetime. Quasibound states correspond to long-lived oscillatory configurations localized outside the event horizon, which appear when the effective potential develops a confining structure capable of trapping perturbations
Senjaya 2024a
;
Senjaya 2024b
;
Senjaya 2026a
;
Senjaya 2026b
. Their complex frequencies encode both oscillation and decay rates, thereby providing a sensitive probe of the underlying geometry. We note that in rotating spacetimes these modes become particularly relevant, as they are directly connected to superradiant energy extraction and may signal the onset of instabilities.

Superradiance is a wave amplification mechanism in which an incident bosonic field scatters off a rotating horizon and emerges with increased amplitude, extracting rotational energy from the black hole when the superradiant condition is satisfied
Senjaya and Ponglertsakul 2025
. The results show that this process plays a central role in black hole bomb scenarios, ultralight boson phenomenology, and stability analyses of rotating compact objects. The amplification factor provides a direct quantitative measure of this effect and therefore constitutes a key observable in scattering theory.

Since both quasibound spectra and superradiant amplification depend sensitively on the structure of the effective potential, they provide a natural framework for probing environmental effects. In particular, it is clear that the presence of a surrounding dark matter halo can deform the gravitational potential, modify the trapping structure of scalar perturbations, shift resonant frequencies, and alter the efficiency of superradiant scattering. Understanding these modifications is therefore essential for establishing how realistic astrophysical environments influence the dynamical response of rotating black holes.

Motivated by these considerations, considerable attention has been devoted to constructing black hole solutions embedded in dark matter halos, with the aim of capturing realistic astrophysical environments. It is found that different halo profiles induce distinct modifications of the spacetime geometry and its associated observables. For instance, black hole shadows, geodesic motion, and wave propagation have been analyzed in Hernquist-type geometries

Very recently, black hole spacetimes surrounded by dark matter halos have been proposed in several contexts. However, it is clear that not all such constructions are derived as exact solutions of the Einstein field equations, which may lead to inconsistencies between the metric and the underlying matter distribution. In this work, we address this issue by constructing a self-consistent Schwarzschild–Dehnen solution obtained directly from the Einstein equations, ensuring that the prescribed dark matter profile is faithfully reproduced.

Building on this exact static configuration, we generate its rotating counterpart via the Newman–Janis algorithm and investigate the dynamics of massive scalar perturbations in the resulting spacetime. Our primary objective is to determine how the Dehnen
(1,4,\gamma)
dark matter halo modifies the quasibound state spectrum and superradiant scattering properties of the rotating black hole.

{}_{1}F_{1}

In this work, it is shown that the presence of the Dehnen halo modifies the effective potential, leading to measurable shifts in the resonance spectrum of quasibound states and altering the efficiency of superradiant amplification. In this sense, the results demonstrate that quasibound modes and superradiant scattering are complementary aspects of a unified spectral structure, encoding how rotating black holes respond to their dark matter environment.

2
Constructing Schwarzschild–Dehnen Black Hole

To construct a self-consistent black hole solution embedded in a Dehnen dark matter environment, we consider a static and spherically symmetric spacetime sourced by a central compact object surrounded by a
(1,4,\gamma)
halo distribution. The most general metric ansatz respecting these symmetries can be written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2}\right),

(3)

where the metric function
f(r)
encodes the combined gravitational influence of the black hole and the surrounding dark matter halo.

To model the halo contribution, we assume that the matter source is described by an anisotropic fluid energy–momentum tensor of the form

T^{\mu}_{\ \nu}=\text{diag}\left[-\rho_{\rm DM}(r),\,p_{r}(r),\,p_{t}(r),\,p_{t}(r)\right],

(4)

where
\rho_{\rm DM}(r)
denotes the dark matter energy density, while
p_{r}(r)
and
p_{t}(r)
correspond to the radial and tangential pressures, respectively. Such anisotropic configurations naturally arise in extended halo models, where the internal stresses need not be isotropic.

Substituting the metric ansatz (
3
) into Einstein’s field equations yields the nonvanishing components

•

Temporal component

G_{tt}=-\frac{f(r)}{r^{2}}\left[rf^{\prime}(r)+f(r)-1\right]=8\pi f(r)\rho_{\rm DM}(r),

(5)

•

Radial component

G_{rr}=\frac{1}{r^{2}f(r)}\left[rf^{\prime}(r)+f(r)-1\right]=8\pi\frac{p_{r}(r)}{f(r)},

(6)

•

Angular components

G_{\theta\theta}=\frac{G_{\phi\phi}}{\sin^{2}\theta}=\frac{r}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{f^{\prime}(r)}{2}=8\pi r^{2}p_{t}(r).

(7)

Here, primes denote differentiation with respect to the radial coordinate. The first two equations imply the relation

p_{r}(r)=-\rho_{\rm DM}(r),

(8)

which shows that the radial pressure compensates the energy density contribution along the radial direction.

The temporal field equation can be rewritten in the compact form

\frac{d}{dr}\bigl[r(1-f(r))\bigr]=-8\pi r^{2}\rho_{\rm DM}(r),

(9)

which directly determines the metric once the density profile is specified. Integrating and requiring the correct Schwarzschild limit, we obtain

f(r)=1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}-\frac{8\pi}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\rho_{\rm DM}(r^{\prime})\,r^{\prime 2}\,dr^{\prime},

(10)

where
r_{s}=2M
is the Schwarzschild radius. This expression clearly separates the vacuum contribution from the cumulative mass of the dark matter halo.

For the Dehnen
(1,4,\gamma)
profile, the density is given by

\rho_{\rm DM}(r)=\rho_{0}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{-\gamma}\left(1+\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{\gamma-4},

(11)

where
\rho_{0}
and
r_{0}
denote the characteristic density and scale radius.

Substituting this profile into the integral expression and performing the integration analytically, we obtain

f(r)=1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}+\frac{8\pi\rho_{0}r_{0}^{3}}{\gamma-3}\,r^{2-\gamma}(r+r_{0})^{\gamma-3},

(12)

where
r_{s}=2M
.

In the limiting case
\rho_{0}=0
, the dark matter contribution vanishes and the metric reduces to the standard Schwarzschild solution, as expected.

3
Rotating the Black Hole

Astrophysical black holes are expected to possess angular momentum, making rotation an essential ingredient in any realistic spacetime description. To extend the static Schwarzschild–Dehnen solution into a rotating geometry, we employ the Newman–Janis algorithm (NJA)
Newman and Janis 1965
;
Newman et al. 1965
, which provides a systematic procedure to generate stationary and axisymmetric spacetimes from static spherical seeds through a complex deformation of coordinates. Originally developed to obtain the Kerr solution from Schwarzschild
Drake and Turolla 1997
, this method has proven to be remarkably effective across a wide range of gravitational models
Brauer et al. 2015
;
Lombardo 2004
;
Kim 2025
;
Abbas et al. 2024
;
Alexeyev et al. 2025
;
Jafarzade et al. 2025
;
Fazzini 2025
;
Li et al. 2025
;
Fathi and Sekhmani 2025
;
Zahid et al. 2025
;
Raza et al. 2025
.

We start from the general static, spherically symmetric line element

ds^{2}=-F(r)\,dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{G(r)}+H(r)\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2}\right),

(13)

which, for the Schwarzschild–Dehnen configuration, reduces to

F(r)=G(r)=f(r),\qquad H(r)=r^{2}.

(14)

As a first step, the metric is rewritten in advanced Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates via

dt=du+\frac{dr}{\sqrt{F(r)G(r)}},

(15)

which removes coordinate singularities at the horizon and allows for a regular null formulation of the geometry.

The inverse metric is then expressed using the Newman–Penrose null tetrad basis

g^{\mu\nu}=-l^{\mu}n^{\nu}-l^{\nu}n^{\mu}+m^{\mu}\bar{m}^{\nu}+m^{\nu}\bar{m}^{\mu},

(16)

with tetrads chosen as

\displaystyle l^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\delta_{r}^{\mu},

(17)

\displaystyle n^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\delta_{u}^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}F(r)\delta_{r}^{\mu},

\displaystyle m^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2H(r)}}\left(\delta_{\theta}^{\mu}+\frac{i}{\sin\theta}\delta_{\phi}^{\mu}\right),

where
\bar{m}^{\mu}
denotes the complex conjugate. These vectors satisfy the standard null normalization and orthogonality conditions.

Rotation is introduced through the complex transformation

u\rightarrow u-ia\cos\theta,\qquad r\rightarrow r+ia\cos\theta,

(18)

where
a
represents the specific angular momentum of the black hole. This step effectively couples the radial and angular sectors of the geometry, generating frame-dragging effects.

To ensure a consistent extension, we follow the refined prescription of Refs.
Azreg-Aïnou 2014
;
Yang and Miao 2023
, promoting the metric functions according to

F(r),\,G(r),\,H(r)\;\longrightarrow\;A(r,\theta),\,B(r,\theta),\,\rho^{2},

(19)

with

\rho^{2}=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta.

(20)

After reconstructing the metric from the transformed tetrads, the line element in Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates takes the form

ds^{2}=-\mathcal{F}\,du^{2}-2\,du\,dr+2(\mathcal{F}-1)a\sin^{2}\theta\,du\,d\phi\\
+2a\sin^{2}\theta\,dr\,d\phi+\rho^{2}\,d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\left[\rho^{2}+(2-\mathcal{F})a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]d\phi^{2},

(21)

where
\mathcal{F}(r,\theta)
encodes the deformation induced by rotation.

To bring the metric into Boyer–Lindquist form, we perform the coordinate transformation

du=dt-\frac{r^{2}+a^{2}}{\Delta}\,dr,\qquad d\phi=d\varphi-\frac{a}{\Delta}\,dr,

(22)

and impose the conditions
g_{tr}=g_{r\varphi}=0
, which eliminate mixed radial terms. This requirement uniquely fixes the lapse function to

\mathcal{F}=\frac{\Delta-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}.

(23)

The remaining metric functions are determined by enforcing the correct limiting behaviors. In particular, the solution must reduce to the static Schwarzschild–Dehnen geometry as
a\to 0
, and to the Kerr spacetime in the absence of dark matter. This leads to

\Delta(r)=r^{2}f(r)+a^{2},\qquad\mathcal{F}(r,\theta)=1-\frac{r^{2}\bigl(1-f(r)\bigr)}{\rho^{2}}.

(24)

It is worth noting that the Dehnen halo modifies
\Delta(r)
through the function
f(r)
, thereby altering the horizon structure and ergoregion. In particular, increasing the inner slope parameter
\gamma
enhances the central concentration of dark matter, which in turn produces stronger deviations from the Kerr geometry near the horizon.

Combining all elements, the rotating Schwarzschild–Dehnen solution can be written as

ds^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{\xi(r)}{\rho^{2}}\right)dt^{2}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\Delta}dr^{2}+\rho^{2}d\theta^{2}\\
-2\frac{\xi(r)}{\rho^{2}}a\sin^{2}\theta\,dt\,d\varphi+\frac{\Sigma(r,\theta)}{\rho^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta\,d\varphi^{2},

(25)

where

\displaystyle\xi(r)=r^{2}\bigl(1-f(r)\bigr),

(26)

\displaystyle\Delta(r)=r^{2}+a^{2}-\xi(r),

(27)

\displaystyle\Sigma(r,\theta)=(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-\Delta a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta.

(28)

This metric represents the Kerr black hole embedded in a Dehnen
(1,4,\gamma)
dark matter halo and serves as the background for analyzing scalar perturbations, quasibound states, and superradiant scattering. In the limit
\rho_{0}\to 0
, one recovers
f(r)=1-2M/r
, yielding

\Delta=r^{2}-2Mr+a^{2},

(29)

so that the spacetime reduces exactly to the Kerr solution, as expected.

4
Relativistic Wave Dynamics

We now turn to the propagation of scalar fields in the rotating Schwarzschild–Dehnen background, with the aim of uncovering the resonant structure of the spacetime. The analysis begins with the inverse metric, which encodes the dynamical couplings between temporal, radial, and angular sectors. Writing the covariant metric in matrix form,

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}-\dfrac{\Delta-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}&0&0&-\dfrac{a\sin^{2}\theta\,(r^{2}+a^{2}-\Delta)}{\rho^{2}}\\
0&\dfrac{\rho^{2}}{\Delta}&0&0\\
0&0&\rho^{2}&0\\
-\dfrac{a\sin^{2}\theta\,(r^{2}+a^{2}-\Delta)}{\rho^{2}}&0&0&\dfrac{\sin^{2}\theta\left[(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-\Delta a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]}{\rho^{2}}\end{array}\right),

one obtains the inverse metric

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}-\dfrac{(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-\Delta a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}\Delta}&0&0&-\dfrac{a(r^{2}+a^{2}-\Delta)}{\rho^{2}\Delta}\\
0&\dfrac{\Delta}{\rho^{2}}&0&0\\
0&0&\dfrac{1}{\rho^{2}}&0\\
-\dfrac{a(r^{2}+a^{2}-\Delta)}{\rho^{2}\Delta}&0&0&\dfrac{\Delta-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}\Delta\sin^{2}\theta}\end{array}\right),

together with the determinant

\sqrt{-g}=\rho^{2}\sin\theta.

(38)

We consider a massive scalar field evolving on this background. Its dynamics are governed by the covariant Klein–Gordon equation

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}\psi-m^{2}\psi=0,

(39)

which, in explicit form, becomes

\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\psi\right)-m^{2}\psi=0.

(40)

Substituting the metric components into Eq. (
40
), the wave equation reduces to

-\frac{(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-\Delta a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\Delta\rho^{2}}\,\partial_{t}^{2}\psi-\frac{2a(r^{2}+a^{2}-\Delta)}{\Delta\rho^{2}}\,\partial_{t}\partial_{\phi}\psi\\
+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\partial_{r}\left(\Delta\partial_{r}\psi\right)+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}\sin\theta}\partial_{\theta}\left(\sin\theta\partial_{\theta}\psi\right)\\
+\frac{\Delta-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\Delta\rho^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\,\partial_{\phi}^{2}\psi-m^{2}\psi=0.

(41)

Although the structure of Eq. (
41
) suggests a nontrivial coupling between radial and angular variables through
\rho^{2}
, the separability inherited from the Kerr geometry is preserved. This is a nontrivial result: despite the presence of the Dehnen halo, the underlying symmetry structure remains intact, allowing for a complete separation of variables.

We therefore adopt the standard mode decomposition

\psi(t,r,\theta,\phi)=e^{-i\omega t+im_{\ell}\phi}\,R(r)\,\Theta(\theta),

(42)

where
\omega
is the frequency and
m_{\ell}
the azimuthal number.

Substituting into Eq. (
41
) and separating variables, one finds

\frac{1}{R}\frac{d}{dr}\left(\Delta\frac{dR}{dr}\right)+\frac{1}{\Theta\sin\theta}\frac{d}{d\theta}\left(\sin\theta\frac{d\Theta}{d\theta}\right)\\
+\frac{(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}\omega^{2}-2am_{\ell}\omega(r^{2}+a^{2}-\Delta)+a^{2}m_{\ell}^{2}}{\Delta}\\
-a^{2}\omega^{2}\sin^{2}\theta-\frac{m_{\ell}^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}-m^{2}r^{2}-a^{2}m^{2}\cos^{2}\theta=0.

(43)

Rearranging terms, the equation splits into purely radial and angular parts,

\frac{1}{R}\frac{d}{dr}\left(\Delta\frac{dR}{dr}\right)+\frac{(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}\omega^{2}-2am_{\ell}\omega(r^{2}+a^{2}-\Delta)+a^{2}m_{\ell}^{2}}{\Delta}-m^{2}r^{2}\\
=-\left[\frac{1}{\Theta\sin\theta}\frac{d}{d\theta}\left(\sin\theta\frac{d\Theta}{d\theta}\right)+a^{2}(\omega^{2}-m^{2})\cos^{2}\theta-\frac{m_{\ell}^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}\right].

(44)

Since each side depends on a different variable, both must equal a separation constant
\lambda_{\ell}^{m_{\ell}}
.

The angular equation becomes

\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{d}{d\theta}\left(\sin\theta\frac{d\Theta}{d\theta}\right)+\left[a^{2}(\omega^{2}-m^{2})\cos^{2}\theta-\frac{m_{\ell}^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}+\lambda_{\ell}^{m_{\ell}}\right]\Theta=0,

(45)

with solutions given by spheroidal harmonics.

The radial equation, which encodes the essential physics of trapping and amplification, reads

\frac{d}{dr}\left(\Delta\frac{dR}{dr}\right)+\left[\frac{\big((r^{2}+a^{2})\omega-am_{\ell}\big)^{2}}{\Delta}-m^{2}r^{2}-\lambda_{\ell}^{m_{\ell}}\right]R=0.

(46)

It is evident that the function
\Delta(r)
, now modified by the Dehnen halo through
f(r)
, directly controls the effective potential governing scalar propagation. In particular, increasing the inner slope parameter
\gamma
enhances the central density, which in turn distorts the potential well and shifts the resonance structure of the quasistationary states.

Finally, the full scalar field can be expressed as

\psi(t,r,\theta,\phi)=e^{-i\omega t}R(r)\,S_{\ell}^{m_{\ell}}(\theta,\phi),

(47)

where

S_{\ell}^{m_{\ell}}(\theta,\phi)=e^{im_{\ell}\phi}\Theta(\theta).

(48)

4.1
Quasibound State

In this section, we analyze the quasibound states associated with the resonant scalar modes of the spacetime. Quasibound states correspond to localized solutions of the radial equation that are confined outside the black hole by an effective potential barrier, while slowly leaking through the horizon due to absorption. These modes are characterized by complex frequencies, where the real part determines the oscillation frequency and the imaginary part encodes the decay or growth rate of the mode. Physically, they represent long-lived resonances that probe both the near-horizon region and the asymptotic structure of the spacetime.

The defining boundary conditions for quasibound states are as follows:

•

Purely ingoing waves at the event horizon, ensuring that no radiation escapes from within the black hole,

•

Exponentially decaying behavior at spatial infinity, guaranteeing that the solution remains localized and normalizable.

To extract analytic information about the resonant spectrum encoded in Eq. (
46
), we employ the analytical asymptotic matching (AAM) method. This approach is particularly effective in the regime

mM\ll 1,\qquad|\omega|M\ll 1,

(49)

where the scalar mass and frequency are both small and of comparable order. We further assume slow rotation,

ma\ll 1,\qquad|\omega|a\ll 1.

(50)

Under these conditions, the angular sector reduces to the spherical harmonic limit, yielding

\lambda_{\ell}^{m_{\ell}}\approx\ell(\ell+1),\qquad\ell=0,1,2,\dots

(51)

In this regime, the resonant scalar modes exhibit a hydrogenic structure at leading order, with corrections arising from rotation and horizon absorption.

4.1.1
Far-region solution

The asymptotic matching method proceeds by solving the radial equation in two distinct regions—the far region and the near-horizon region—and then consistently matching the solutions in their overlap.

We first consider the far region defined by

r\gg M,

(52)

which also implies
r\gg a
. In this limit, the radial equation simplifies to

r^{2}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^{2}\frac{dR}{dr}\right)+\left[(\omega^{2}-m^{2})r^{4}-\lambda_{\ell}^{m_{\ell}}r^{2}+m^{2}r^{2}\xi(r)\right]R=0.

(53)

Using the asymptotic expansion of the metric function,

\lim_{r\to\infty}f(r)\approx 1+\frac{A}{r},\qquad A=-r_{s}+\frac{8\pi\rho_{0}r_{0}^{3}}{\gamma-3},

(54)

we obtain

\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}(rR)+\left[\omega^{2}-m^{2}-\frac{Am^{2}}{r}-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}\right]rR=0.

(55)

Introducing the dimensionless variables

k^{2}=m^{2}-\omega^{2},\qquad\nu=-\frac{Am^{2}}{2k},\qquad x=2kr,

(56)

the radial equation can be rewritten as

\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}(xR)+\left(-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\nu}{x}-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{x^{2}}\right)xR=0,

(57)

which takes the standard form of the confluent hypergeometric equation (see Appendix
B
).

Imposing the physical boundary conditions of regularity at the horizon and exponential decay at spatial infinity, the far-region solution is given by

R_{\infty}(x)=C_{1}\,x^{\ell}e^{-x/2}\,{}_{1}F_{1}(\ell+1-\nu,\,2\ell+2,\,x),

(58)

where
C_{1}
is a normalization constant. This solution describes the asymptotic tail of the quasibound scalar configuration, where gravitational confinement balances the exponential decay at large distances.

In the special case where

-(\ell+1-\nu)\equiv n_{r},

(59)

with
n_{r}
a non-negative integer, Eq. (
58
) reduces to the familiar hydrogen-like bound-state wavefunction, with
n_{r}
playing the role of the radial quantum number. In the present setup, however, the mode frequency
\omega
generally possesses a small imaginary component induced by the interaction with the rotating black hole. As a consequence, the parameter
\nu
also becomes slightly complex and may be written as

\nu=\ell+1+n_{r}+\delta\nu,\qquad|\delta\nu|\ll 1.

(60)

Accordingly, the far-region solution assumes the form

R_{\infty}(x)=C_{1}\,x^{\ell}e^{-x/2}\,{}_{1}F_{1}(-n_{r}-\delta\nu,\,2\ell+2,\,x).

(61)

To perform the matching procedure later on, we now determine the asymptotic behavior of Eq. (
61
) in the near-horizon regime. The overlap region between the near- and far-zone solutions is defined by

We now turn to the analysis of the near-horizon region. Expanding the metric functions around the event horizon

4.2
Superradiant Scattering

While quasibound states describe the trapped, discrete sector of the spectrum, scattering modes probe the open channel through which waves interact with the rotating geometry. In this regime, the black hole behaves as an energy amplifier: incident modes can extract rotational energy and return with enhanced amplitude. This phenomenon, known as superradiant scattering, provides a direct probe of horizon physics and its modification by the surrounding dark matter halo.

In contrast to quasibound states, scattering modes correspond to unbound configurations satisfying

\omega>m,

(101)

with
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
. Our objective is to determine how the Dehnen halo modifies the amplification process. To this end, we again employ the analytical asymptotic matching (AAM) method in the low-frequency regime.

4.2.1

In the asymptotic region
r\gg M

Its general solution is

(104)

In the matching region (

), this reduces to

(105)

while at spatial infinity (

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=B_{1}\frac{\Gamma(2\ell+2)}{\Gamma(\ell+1-i\Lambda)}+B_{2}\frac{\Gamma(-2\ell)}{\Gamma(-\ell-i\Lambda)},

(107)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=B_{1}\frac{\Gamma(2\ell+2)}{\Gamma(\ell+1+i\Lambda)}+B_{2}\frac{\Gamma(-2\ell)}{\Gamma(-\ell+i\Lambda)}.

(108)

4.2.2
Near-horizon solution

Near the event horizon, the physical boundary condition selects purely ingoing modes. The solution retains the form

\omega\xi(r_{H})-am_{\ell}<0,

\xi(r_{H})

Summary

We have explored the dynamics of scalar fields in a rotating black hole spacetime dressed by a King dark matter halo, focusing on how quasibound states and superradiant scattering intertwine to form the full spectral response of the system. Starting from a consistent rotating geometry constructed via the Newman–Janis algorithm, we formulated the Klein–Gordon equation and employed the analytical asymptotic matching (AAM) method to bridge the near-horizon and asymptotic regions. This approach provides a unified analytic framework in which both trapped modes and scattering processes emerge naturally from the same underlying structure.

In the quasibound regime, we derived an explicit analytical expression for the complex frequency spectrum, clearly separating its physical origin. The real part is governed by the asymptotic far-region behavior and retains a hydrogen-like structure, now shifted by the presence of the dark matter halo through the parameter

A=r_{s}+\frac{8\pi\rho_{0}r_{0}^{3}}{\gamma-3},

(118)

which acts as an effective mass scale for the halo contribution. Increasing either
\rho_{0}
or
r_{0}
deepens the effective potential and shifts the energy levels downward, leading to more tightly bound scalar clouds. The parameter
\gamma
, which controls the inner slope of the density profile, further modulates this effect: larger
\gamma
corresponds to cuspier and more centrally concentrated halos, enhancing the binding and producing more pronounced deviations from the vacuum spectrum, especially as
\gamma\to 3
. In contrast, smaller
\gamma
yields softer, more diffuse halos with weaker spectral shifts.

The imaginary part of the frequency arises entirely from the near-horizon dynamics, encoding the exchange of energy with the rotating black hole. This contribution depends on the quantity
\xi(r_{H})
, which is also modified by the halo parameters. As a consequence, the black hole bomb condition is deformed through the halo-dependent geometry, shifting the threshold for instability and the formation of scalar clouds. In particular, increasing
\rho_{0}
or
r_{0}
, or considering more cuspy profiles (larger
\gamma
), reduces the critical mass required for instability and typically suppresses the growth rate.

In the scattering sector, we obtained an analytical expression for the superradiant amplification factor
Z
by explicitly matching ingoing and outgoing modes at infinity. The dark matter halo modifies the effective potential felt by the scalar field, leading to changes in both the phase evolution and the efficiency of amplification. The superradiant condition is correspondingly altered to
\omega\xi(r_{H})-am_{\ell}<0

\xi(r_{H})

\rho_{0}
,
r_{0}

\gamma
shift the cutoff frequency and typically narrow the superradiant window, with denser, larger, and more cuspy halos reducing the efficiency of amplification relative to the Kerr case.

Taken together, quasibound states and superradiant scattering are not separate phenomena, but two complementary facets of the same spectral structure. The far region shapes the resonant black hole ringing by scalar fields, while the near-horizon region controls their growth, decay, and amplification. The presence of the Dehnen halo acts as an environmental tuner, with
\rho_{0}
and
r_{0}

Normal Form

A second–order ordinary differential equation can be cast into a normal (Schrödinger-like) form by eliminating the first–derivative term. This representation is particularly useful for qualitative and asymptotic analysis

(119)

(120)

term

. Substituting into Eq. (

(122)

\frac{d^{2}Y}{dx^{2}}=-Q(x)Y.

(123)

This representation provides immediate qualitative insight. If
