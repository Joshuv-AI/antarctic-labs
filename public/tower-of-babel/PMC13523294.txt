Distinguishing Ki67 stratification expression in ER-positive/HER2-negative breast cancers: comparison of advanced MRI diffusion models
Authors: Zhongqi Kang, Siyao Du, Li Zhao, Ruimeng Zhao, Si Gao, Yanni Zhang, Jiaping Yu, Xiaofei Liu, Yueluan Jiang, Yan Wang, Lina Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeIdentifying the stratification expression of Ki67 is crucial for directing clinical treatment strategies in ER+/HER2- breast cancers. This diagnostic study investigated the value of first-order features extracted from conventional DWI, diffusion kurtosis imaging (DKI), fractional order calculus (FROC), and continuous-time random walk (CTRW) in discriminating Ki67 expression of ER+/HER2- invasive ductal breast cancer.Materials and methodsThis retrospective study included 121 patients who underwent DWI, DKI, FROC and CTRW and were pathologically categorized into the low (≤ 5%, n = 30), medium (> 5% to < 30%, n = 41), and high Ki67 expression group (≥ 30%, n = 50). Sixty-three diffusion parameters were computed and subsequently compared across different groups. The area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) was used to quantify diagnostic efficacy. Multivariate logistic regression and bootstrap (1,000 samples) analyses were used to establish and evaluate, respectively, the optimal model to identify Ki67 expression.ResultsTwenty-three features showed statistically significant differences among the low, medium and high expression groups (all p values < 0.05). Further multivariable logistic regression analysis for discriminating the low Ki67 and non-low expression group showed that the FROC model constructed by D10%, µ90% and µSkewness had optimal diagnostic efficacy (AUC = 0.858; 95% confidence interval, 0.783–0.915), which was significantly better than that of DWI model (AUC = 0.677; p = 0.008). The validation model showed good accuracy (AUC = 0.850; 95%CI, 0.774–0.916).ConclusionsThe FROC model could help identify the low Ki67 expression (≤ 5%) and prevent unnecessary chemotherapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-026-02338-8.Keywords: Breast neoplasms, Magnetic resonance imaging, Diffusion-weighted MRI

Background

Breast cancer is the most common cancer in women, and incidence rates are increasing worldwide [1]. Within its diverse subtypes, the estrogen receptor-positive/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (ER+/HER2-) phenotype is the most prevalent, representing approximately 70% of all breast cancers [2]. In ER+/HER2- breast cancer, Ki67 expression is a critical biomarker for guiding treatment strategies and predicting patient outcomes. The Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology Breast Cancer 2024 (CSCO BC 2024) and the International Ki67 in Breast Cancer Working Group (IKWG) emphasize the use of Ki67 as a key determinant in clinical decision-making. Specifically, when Ki67 is ≤ 5% or ≥ 30%, these cut-offs can effectively inform decisions regarding the necessity for adjuvant chemotherapy and aid in prognostic prediction [3]. Importantly, this affordable approach reduces reliance on expensive gene expression assays, optimizing personalized therapy and avoiding unnecessary chemotherapy. Although biopsy remains the gold standard for Ki67 assessment, sampling limitations and tumor heterogeneity may affect accuracy [4, 5]. Consequently, noninvasive imaging biomarkers for accurate Ki67 assessment offer profound clinical implications for personalized treatment strategies.

Diffusion-weighted MRI (DWI) is a conventional tool for identifying pathologic prognostic factors and predicting therapy responses in breast cancer [6–8]. Ki67 expression can be noninvasively stratified (cut-offs 14% or 20%) through quantification of the apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) derived from Gaussian-based DWI model [9–13]. Such translational role arises from increased cellular density in high Ki67-expressing tumors, which restricts water diffusion. Systematic review and meta-analysis [14, 15] have demonstrated that diffusional kurtosis imaging (DKI) [16], as a non-Gaussian model, outperforms DWI in diagnostic performance. DKI extends conventional DWI by characterizing non-Gaussian water molecule diffusion behavior to probe microstructural heterogeneity. While, the value of diffusion-related parameters remains controversial due to inconsistent Ki67 thresholds and confounding effects of ER/HER2 expression across subtypes [17].

Recently proposed fractional-order calculus (FROC) [18] and continuous-time random walk (CTRW) [19, 20]models have been preliminarily investigated in breast tumors. In contrast to the non-Gaussian DKI model, the FROC model characterizes spatial heterogeneity through anomalous diffusion governed by fractional derivative orders (β) and spatial displacement scales (µ) [18]. The CTRW model captures temporal heterogeneity via probability distributions of molecular jump lengths (β) and waiting times (α) [21]. These heterogeneity-related parameters contribute to distinguish benign and malignant breast tumors, evaluate prognostic factors and subtypes [22–24]. However, the comparative performance of DKI, FROC, and CTRW in distinguishing Ki67 thresholds (5% and 30%) in the single ER+/HER2- subtype remains undetermined.

This study aims to determine whether advanced diffusion models can noninvasively identify clinically relevant Ki67 expression thresholds to guide chemotherapy decisions in ER+/HER2- breast cancer.

Materials and methods

Study patients

The Ethics Committee of our hospital approved this retrospective study. All participants signed informed consent. This retrospective study included 121 surgical pathology confirmed breast invasive ductal carcinoma preoperative patients between May 2020 and August 2023. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (i) all patients underwent multi-b value diffusion-weighted MR imaging before surgery; (ii) histopathological and immunohistochemistry (IHC) confirmation of ER + and HER2 − breast invasive ductal carcinoma; (iii) patients with T1 ~ T2, N0 ~ N1 stages. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (i) neoadjuvant chemotherapy before MRI examination; (ii) lesions smaller than 1.0 cm; (iii) non-mass enhancement lesions; (iv) poor DWI signal quality; (v) without IHC or fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) results regarding HER2 expression. Finally, 121 patients were included in our study. The flow chart of patient selection for this study is shown in Fig. 1.

Pathological evaluation

All lesion specimens were obtained surgically. HER2 and ER expression status was analyzed in the tumor specimens in accordance with the 2010 American Society of Clinical Oncology/College of American Pathologists (ASCO/CAP) histopathological consensus guidelines [25, 26]. HER2 expression status was evaluated by IHC analysis of post-surgical pathological specimens. IHC score of 0 or 1 + was considered as negative HER2 expression, and a score of 3 + was considered as positive HER2 expression. FISH analysis was performed for samples with an IHC score of 2 + to confirm the HER2 status. The cut-off value for ER was 1% (negative, ≤ 1%; positive, > 1%). Ki67 assessment was performed by a breast pathologist through visual quantification of positively stained tumor nuclei. The entire section was first examined under a low-power field to evaluate the distribution uniformity of positively stained cells. For uniformly distributed positive cells, the Ki67 index was calculated by averaging counts from ≥ 3 randomly selected high-power fields. For unevenly distributed positive cells, ≥ 3 high-power fields encompassing the most proliferative hotspots were evaluated [27]. According to IKWG recommendations [3], Ki67 thresholds of ≤ 5% and ≥ 30% were applied for therapeutic decision-making. Therefore, patients were defined as low expression group (Ki67 ≤ 5%), medium expression group (Ki67 > 5% to < 30%) and high expression group (Ki67 ≥ 30%).

MRI data acquisition

All MRI acquisitions were performed within a week before surgery using a 3T MRI scanner (MAGNETOM Skyra, Siemens Healthcare, Erlangen, Germany) with a dedicated 18-channel phased-array breast coil. The breast MRI protocol included axial fat-suppressed T2-weighted turbo spin-echo (TSE) imaging, axial T1-weighted images, DWI, and dynamic contrast-enhanced (DCE) sequences. Before contrast agent injection, the multi-b value axial DWI was executed using single-shot echo planar imaging with spectral adiabatic inversion recovery for fat suppression. The detailed scan parameters were as follows: Diffusion-sensitizing gradients were applied sequentially along the three orthogonal directions and images were obtained at 12 b values of 0, 20, 40, 100, 150, 200, 400, 800, 1200, 1600, 2000 and 2400s/mm2; repetition time/echo time = 3300/84ms; flip angle = 90–180 degrees; field of view = 400 × 325 mm2; matrix = 192 × 154; slice thickness = 4.0 mm; number of slices = 36; bandwidth = 1736 Hz; acquisition time = 4 min 18 s; generalized auto-calibrating partially parallel acquisition (GRAPPA) with acceleration factor = 3.

Quantitative Measurements of four diffusion models

The three-dimensional regions of interest (ROIs) segmentation of the whole tumor on the DWI images were conducted by two readers with 3 years and 9 years of experience in breast MRI respectively, using the BoDiLab software rooted in DIPY (https://dipy.org/). The ROI segmentation process was composed of four steps: (i) Data loading: the original DWI images were loaded into the software; (ii) ROIs drawing: ROIs were manually drawn along the tumor margins (excluding the necrotic regions) on DWI images with a b-value of 800 mm2/sec, referencing DCE images obtained 140s after injection of the contrast medium; (iii) Segmentation: The contour of ROIs in the DWI images (b = 800 s/mm2) was copied onto the same locations on the corresponding parametric maps; (iv) Histogram analysis: the whole tumor histogram analysis on the multi-parametric maps was automatically calculated with the corresponding algorithm. The histogram features extracted from each parametric map of all diffusion models are the same, including mean, median, kurtosis, entropy, skewness, 10th percentile, and 90th percentile. They were represented as subscripts. For example, the mean, median, and 10th percentile metrics of D value were presented as DMean, DMedian, and D10%, respectively.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed with SPSS 23.0 software (IBM, Chicago, IL, USA), MedCalc version 15.0 software (MedCalc Software bvba, Ostend, Belgium) and R (version 4.3.2, Vienna, Austria). The intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) were calculated and used for assessing the inter-observer reliabilities in measuring those nine diffusion parameters. ICC more than 0.9 and between 0.75 and 0.9 indicated excellent and good performance, respectively. Data distribution was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test for normality. Continuous variables with normal distribution are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and analyzed using independent t-tests or ANOVA. Non-normally distributed data are reported as median with interquartile ranges (IQRs) and compared via Mann-Whitney U tests or Kruskal-Wallis test (with Bonferroni correction). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages, and were compared using chi-square test. Spearman rank correlation analysis was used to assess the correlation between the diffusion metrics and Ki67 index. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was utilized to establish each diffusion model by combining statistically significant histogram features of diffusion parameters. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted, and the area under the curves (AUCs) were obtained to quantify the discriminant ability. The combination model for identifying the Ki67 expression was evaluated by bootstrap (1,000 samples) and ROC curves. The ROC curves were calculated and presented with two-sided 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The AUCs were compared using the DeLong test. A p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Among them, 30 patients (mean age ± standard deviation, 52.3 years ± 11.6) defined as low expression group, 41 patients (mean age ± standard deviation, 47.5 years ± 7.7) defined as medium expression group, and 50 patients (mean age ± standard deviation, 50.1 years ± 10.2) defined as high expression group. The histologic grade (p = 0.001) showed statistical differences between the low Ki67 expression and high expression groups as well as the high expression and medium expression groups. There were no significant differences in the patient age, menopausal status, tumor size and nodal status (p > 0.05) among the low Ki67 expression group, medium expression group and high expression groups.

For discriminating the low Ki67 and non-low expression group (medium and high expression groups), the AUC of features from DWI ranged from 0.555 to 0.677, DKI ranged from 0.608 to 0.710 and FROC ranged from 0.548 to 0.792 (Table S3). Delong test showed no significant difference between FROC_µSkewness and DWI_ADC10% in terms of AUC (0.792 vs. 0.677, z = 1.780, p = 0.075).

For discriminating the high and non-high Ki67 (low and medium expression groups) expression group, the AUC of features from DWI ranged from 0.515 to 0.622, DKI ranged from 0.530 to 0.585 and FROC ranged from 0.512 to 0.638 (Table S4). The FROC_DSkewness was significantly higher in the high Ki67 expression group (median [IQR]: 0.426 [-0.372, 1.199]) compared to the non-high expression group (median [IQR]: 0.024 [-0.588, 0.512]; Mann-Whitney U test, p = 0.002). Delong test showed no significant difference between FROC_DSkewness and DWI_ ADCSkewness in terms of AUC (0.638 vs. 0.622, z = 0.098, p = 0.539).

Correlation analysis of diffusion features with Ki67 index

The results indicated that DWI_ADC (kurtosis), DKI_D (kurtosis), DKI_K (median), FROC_D (kurtosis, skewness), FROC_β (skewness), FROC_µ (90th percentile, kurtosis, skewness, mean), CTRW_D (kurtosis) and CTRW_α (90th percentile) were positively correlated with the Ki67 expression in ER+/HER2- breast cancer (r = 0.122–0.395, p < 0.05). Conversely, DWI_ADC (skewness), DKI_K (skewness), FROC_β (median) and CTRW_D (90th percentile) were negatively correlated with Ki67 expression (r = -0.278 – -0.169, p < 0.05). Among these diffusion metrics, FROC_µKurtosis had the highest correlation with Ki67 expression (r = 0.395, p = 0.001). For DWI model, ADCSkewness exhibited the highest correlation with Ki67 expression (r = -0.278, p = 0.002). For DKI model, DKI_DKurtosis had the highest correlation with Ki67 expression (r = 0.226, p = 0.013). For CTRW model, CTRW_DKurtosis demonstrated the highest correlation with Ki67 expression (r = 0.198, p = 0.013), as detailed in Table S5.

Logistic regression analyses for differentiating Ki67 expression

A total of 23 features were examined using univariate logistic regression analysis and 17 features were found to be significantly associated with discrimination between the low Ki67 expression group and non-low expression group (p = 0.001 to p = 0.044, Table S6), while 6 features were found to be significantly associated with discrimination between the high Ki67 expression group and non-high expression group (p = 0.002 to p = 0.049, Table S8).

In the stepwise multivariate logistic regression analysis on the histogram features of single parametric maps (ADC, DKI_D, DKI_K and FROC_µ) for discriminating low Ki67 expression group and non-low expression group, the combination of Kurtosis and Median features derived from DKI_D resulted in a higher AUC value of 0.736 (95%CI, 0.648–0.812) (Table S7), which was higher than the AUC of DWI_ADC10% (AUC = 0.677; 95%CI, [0.585–0.759]) but lower than the AUC of FROC_µSkewness (AUC = 0.792; 95%CI, [0.709–0.860]). However, the Delong test indicated that this difference in AUC values was not statistically significant (p > 0.05). The results of the univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis on the histogram features of each diffusion parameter are presented in Supplementary File (Tables S6 and S7).

In the stepwise multivariate logistic regression analysis on the histogram features of single parametric maps (ADC, DKI_D, DKI_K and FROC_µ) for discriminating high Ki67 expression group and non-high expression group, the Skewness features derived from DWI_ADC achieved a AUC value of 0.622 (95%CI, 0.529–0.708), which was lower than the AUC of FROC_DSkewness (AUC = 0.638; 95%CI, [0.546–0.724]). The Delong test indicated that the difference in AUC values was not statistically significant (p > 0.05). Nevertheless, the multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that neither the features from DKI_D, DKI_K nor FROC_µ exhibited significant independent diagnostic capability. The details are shown in Supplementary File (Tables S8 and S9).

Diagnostic performance and validation of diffusion model

For discriminating low Ki67 expression group and non-low expression group, the AUC value of DKI model was 0.806 (95%CI, 0.724–0.872) when combining the independent features (DMean, KEntropy and DKurtosis). Discussion

Our study utilized histogram features derived from the DKI, FROC and CTRW diffusion models and conventional DWI model to assess the Ki67 expression with the threshold recommended by the 2024 CSCO and IKWG in ER+/HER2- invasive ductal breast cancer. For discriminating low (Ki67 ≤ 5%) and non-low (Ki67 > 5%) expression groups, FROC_µSkewness demonstrated the strongest discriminative performance (AUC = 0.792) among all diffusion features. The FROC model incorporating µ90%, D10% and µSkewness achieved optimal diagnostic power (AUC = 0.858), significantly outperforming the conventional DWI model (AUC = 0.677, p = 0.008). While the advanced diffusion models did not show surprising performance in distinguishing Ki67 ≤ 30% and > 30% group. These findings suggest the FROC model better identifies low Ki67 (≤ 5%) ER+/HER2- breast cancers, which typically have favorable prognosis and reduced chemotherapy needs.

A higher expression of Ki67 indicates an increased density of cells, which restricts the movement of water molecules and consequently leading to a reduction in ADC [33]. Previous studies reported that breast cancer with high Ki67 levels exhibited lower diffusion-related parameters, including ADC values [13, 33, 34], DKI-derived D [34–36] and CTRW-derived D [37]. These observations correspond to the biological basis that highly proliferative tumors demonstrate increased cellularity and disrupted tissue architecture, restricting water diffusion. However, other studies [38–40] highlighted the challenges of accurately diagnosing Ki67 expression using single diffusion-related parameters. Previous multicenter studies [41, 42] reported weak correlations between conventional ADC values and Ki67 expression (r = -0.202 to -0.126). Our study found no significant correlation between ADC (Mean and Median) and Ki67 expression (p > 0.05). Additionally, analysis based on the histogram features revealed a weak but significant negative association between ADCSkewness and Ki67 (r = -0.278, p = 0.002). This finding suggest that heterogeneity parameters derived from advanced diffusion models may offer superior characterization of Ki67 expression. In our results, specifically, the FROC model outperformed DWI (AUC: 0.858 vs. 0.677; p = 0.008) in stratifying low Ki67 (≤ 5%) by combining spatial heterogeneity (µ) and diffusion displacement (D) parameters.

Compared to mean values, histogram-derived metrics (median, skewness, kurtosis, percentiles) better capture tumor heterogeneity. Previous studies reported limited diagnostic performance of DKI for Ki67 stratification at the 20% cutoff (AUC < 0.70) [43, 44]. Our DKI model incorporating heterogeneity histogram features demonstrated superior discrimination of low Ki67 expression (≤ 5%), achieving an AUC of 0.806 (95% CI: 0.724–0.872). The CTRW model can provide insights into the spatial and temporal heterogeneity of water molecule diffusion within a voxel [31, 32]. In previous studies, CTRW-derived metrics have shown potential as imaging biomarkers for distinguishing malignant or benign breast lesions [18–20]. Qin et al. and He et al. reported significant differences in CTRW-derived parameters, including α (10%, mean and skewness) and β (mean, skewness) between high (≥ 20%) and low (< 20%) Ki67 expression [20, 23], whereas others found no such associations [45, 46]. Our study found that these parameters lacked significant discriminatory power at either the 5% or 30% cutoff. This discrepancy may stem from differences in Ki67 cutoff thresholds and our exclusive focus on ER+/HER2- subtypes. The FROC diffusion model has yielded a novel set of parameters that enables the assessment of spatial diffusion heterogeneity (β) and spatial diffusion parameter (µ) within tumor tissues. Some studies have demonstrated that the FROC model holds potential for differentiating between benign and malignant breast lesions [18, 19]. Previous studies have observed higher FROC_ µ values in breast malignant tumors [47] and HER2 overexpression subtypes [48]. The increased FROC_µ values indicate more restricted water diffusion in non-low Ki67 expression group, as µ exhibited a negative correlation with water molecule displacement distance [49]. This trend corresponded with our research findings that FROC_µMedian was higher whereas FROC_µSkewness was lower in the non-low Ki67 group relative to the low expression group. Since skewness reflects the asymmetry of the histogram distribution, this finding may indicate greater complexity of water molecule diffusion in breast cancers with high Ki67 expression. Further ROC analyses of the single feature showed that the FROC_µSkewness had the better performance (AUC = 0.792) in discriminating the low Ki67 expression group and non-low expression group and the FROC model yielded the highest AUC of 0.858 (95%CI, 0.783–0.915), which significantly outperformed DWI model (AUC = 0.677, p = 0.008). The discriminative performance of FROC-derived features remains suboptimal for Ki67 stratification at the 30% threshold. This limitation reflects ​​the difficulty of​​ current diffusion models ​​in​​ differentiating medium (6–29%) from high (≥ 30%) Ki67 expression groups. The IKWG recognized medium Ki67 expression (6–29%) remains a pathologically indeterminate range due to residual analytical variability persisting after assay standardization [50].

This study has several limitations. Firstly, this single-center retrospective study had a limited sample size. While internal validation was performed, future studies with larger multicenter cohorts are needed for external validation to confirm the diffusion model’s diagnostic value. Secondly, small tumors with a size less than 1.0 cm were excluded due to spatial resolution limitations of high b-value DWI. This particularly reduced the low Ki67 group sample size (typically smaller tumors), potentially impacting statistical power. Improved resolution and inclusion of low Ki67 cases could address this limitation. Thirdly, non-mass lesions were excluded due to poorly defined margins on diffusion maps, preventing accurate ROI delineation. Given the known ADC differences between mass and non-mass lesions, future large-scale studies should specifically evaluate non-mass enhancements. Finally, we focused on first-order, whole-tumor features, which may incompletely characterize tumor heterogeneity. Systematic evaluation of higher-order features is needed to optimize diffusion models’ diagnostic performance.

Conclusions

The diffusion features derived from DWI_ADC, DKI_D, DKI_K, FROC_D, FROC_µ, and CTRW_D were associated with Ki67 expression in ER+/HER2- invasive ductal breast cancer. The µSkewness from FROC was the best diffusion feature in discriminating the low Ki67 expression group and non-low expression group. The FROC model provide valuable information that aided in identifying low Ki67 expression, thereby preventing unnecessary chemotherapy in patients with ER+/HER2- breast cancer.
