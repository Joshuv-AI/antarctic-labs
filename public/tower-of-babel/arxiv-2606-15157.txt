PolyKV: Heterogeneous Retention and Allocation for KV Cache Compression
Chao Fei, Panos Kalnis

Abstract
KV cache compression is essential for reducing the memory cost of long-context large language model inference. Existing approaches, however, typically apply a single compression policy and a uniform cache budget across all transformer layers. This uniform design ignores the fact that different layers can play different roles during prefill and decoding, and may therefore require different eviction strategies and cache capacities. We present PolyKV , a layer-wise KV cache optimization framework that considers design space with method selection and budget allocation. PolyKV routes each layer to a suitable KV compression policy based on layer-level signals, while assigning non-uniform budgets under a fixed total budget. This formulation enables heterogeneous compositions of existing KV cache methods. Experiments on LLaMA-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B show that, under the same 512-token average KV budget, PolyKV recovers 54.5% and 25.7% of the LongBench performance gap between the strongest single-policy baseline and FullKV, respectively. Across 128–1024 budget sweep, PolyKV consistently improves over the strongest baseline by 1.7%–6.4%, corresponding to 40.0%–54.5% recovery of the FullKV gap.

Introduction

Long-context LLM inference is becoming increasingly important with the rise of agentic applications, retrieval-augmented generation, and other workloads that require models to process extended contexts.
However, longer contexts substantially increase the size of the KV cache, creating a major memory bottleneck during inference.
The growing KV cache can exhaust HBM capacity, reduce achievable batch size, and slow down token generation.

KV cache eviction mitigates this bottleneck by reducing cache memory usage while preserving long context quality.
Existing methods have introduced a range of effective eviction patterns.
Heavy hitter and persistence based methods retain tokens that receive large or stable attention mass
(
Zhang et al. 2023
;
Liu et al. 2023
)
.
Attention sink methods preserve sink tokens together with recent tokens
(
Xiao et al. 2024b
)
.
Prompt observation methods identify salient prompt tokens before generation
(
Li et al. 2024
)
.
Together, these approaches show that cache eviction pattern is a key factor in the trade off between quality and memory usage during long context inference.
However, canonical KV eviction methods primarily define useful eviction patterns, and are often instantiated as model-wide strategies.
This uniform treatment overlooks the heterogeneity of transformer layers, implicitly assuming that different layers should follow the same eviction rule and receive comparable cache capacity.

Transformer layers are not homogeneous computation blocks.
Prior work has shown that layers and heads differ in their attention patterns, represented abstractions, contextual roles, and sensitivity to pruning
(
Tenney et al. 2019
;
Jawahar et al. 2019
;
Clark et al. 2019
;
Voita et al. 2019
;
Michel et al. 2019
)
.
This heterogeneity suggests that a global KV eviction rule is a coarse abstraction.
Global policy imposes the same eviction bias and comparable cache capacity across all layers.
We therefore examine KV eviction as a per layer design space.
This design space exposes two coupled choices.
The first is what to evict, since different layers may prefer different eviction patterns.
The second is how much to retain, since layers differ in their sensitivity to KV cache reduction.
Recent adaptive KV methods have begun to reveal the value of non uniform eviction
(
Ge et al. 2024
;
Cai et al. 2024
;
Feng et al. 2025
;
Zhou et al. 2025
;
Shen et al. 2025
;
Tang et al. 2025
;
Xiao et al. 2024a
;
Fu et al. 2025
)
.
However, they explore only part of the design space rather than treating eviction pattern and cache capacity as coupled layer-wise design choices.

Together, these observations motivate treating eviction pattern and cache capacity as coupled layer-wise design choices.

We present
PolyKV
, a framework for designing and evaluating layer-wise KV cache eviction strategies.
PolyKV makes the two design dimensions explicit by allowing each layer to be assigned both a eviction pattern and a cache budget.
Through a modular interface, PolyKV integrates widely used eviction patterns and supports fine granularity control over both eviction pattern and cache capacity.
This unified framework enables us to systematically construct and evaluate eviction strategies that align both dimensions with the characteristics of each layer.

To instantiate this framework without exhaustively searching the full design space, PolyKV uses offline calibration on a small calibration set.
It collects two categories of layer-specific signals.
Eviction signals characterize which contextual patterns a layer relies on, guiding the selection of eviction pattern.
Cache-sensitivity signals estimate how strongly a layer is affected by KV reduction, guiding the allocation of cache capacity.
PolyKV converts these signals into a fixed heterogeneous eviction strategy across layers.
At deployment time, inference follows this calibrated strategy rather than performing online policy search, making layer-aware KV eviction practical while keeping the runtime procedure static.

We conduct comprehensive experiments to prove that the layer-wise design space exposed by PolyKV can translate into better quality.
Under fixed total KV budgets, we compare PolyKV with representative KV cache eviction baselines on LLaMA-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B across LongBench and RULER.
On LongBench, PolyKV improves over the strongest single-policy baseline for both model families under the same 512-token average KV budget, recovering 54.5% and 25.7% of the compression induced gap to FullKV on LLaMA-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B, respectively.
A budget sweep on LLaMA-3.1-8B further shows consistent gains from 128 to 1024 average KV tokens, where PolyKV improves over the strongest baseline by 1.7%–6.4%.

Our contributions are threefold:

•

We reformulate KV cache eviction as a layer-wise design space. This space couples two choices about what to retain and how much KV capacity each layer receives under a shared memory constraint.

•

We present
PolyKV
, a framework that operationalizes this design space by composing eviction patterns and cache budgets at layer granularity. PolyKV integrates multiple eviction primitives through a unified interface and uses offline calibration signals to guide layer-wise strategy construction.

•

We show empirically that layer-wise heterogeneous strategies improves quality under fixed memory budgets.
On LongBench, PolyKV recovers 54.5% and 25.7% of the gap between the strongest single-policy baseline and FullKV on LLaMA-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B, respectively, under the same 512-token average KV budget.
Across 128–1024 budget sweep on LLaMA-3.1-8B, PolyKV consistently improves over the strongest baseline by 1.7%–6.4%.

Related Work

2.1
KV Cache Eviction and Transformer Heterogeneity

KV cache eviction reduces inference memory by retaining only a subset of historical key and value states.
Existing methods can be understood as distinct token eviction patterns, each driven by a different eviction pattern.
Cumulative attention methods score historical tokens using accumulated attention scores and protect a recent window
(
Zhang et al. 2023
;
Zhang et al. 2024
;
Wan et al. 2025
)
.
Recent query window methods instead score historical tokens using attention from the most recent query window, optionally applying pooling or smoothing, and retain the top
k
historical tokens together with recent tokens
(
Li et al. 2024
;
Cai et al. 2024
)
.
Persistence based methods identify tokens that repeatedly exceed an attention threshold across recent query steps, thereby favoring tokens whose importance remains stable over time
(
Liu et al. 2023
)
.
StreamingLLM represents another eviction pattern. Rather than relying on an attention based salience score, it preserves fixed initial sink tokens and the most recent tokens according to their positions
(
Xiao et al. 2024b
)
.
TOVA uses attention from the current last query token to select the global top
k
cached tokens, making eviction depend on the immediate decoding state
(
Oren et al. 2024
)
.

These methods rely on different eviction patterns, yet they all apply the same strategy to every layer in the model.
This uniform treatment is coarse because transformer blocks are heterogeneous.
Prior analyses show that LLM layers differ in represented abstractions, attention patterns, contextual roles, and sensitivity to pruning
(
Tenney et al. 2019
;
Jawahar et al. 2019
;
Clark et al. 2019
;
Voita et al. 2019
;
Michel et al. 2019
)
.
For KV cache eviction, this suggests that different layers may prefer different eviction biases and different eviction cache budgets.
Layer heterogeneity therefore turns eviction into two related design questions.
The first is which eviction pattern each layer should use, and the second is how much cache budget each layer should receive.

2.2
Adaptive and Non-uniform KV Cache Eviction

Adaptive and non-uniform KV cache eviction methods, which challenge the assumption that all layers should have the same cache budget.
Some methods allocate different cache budgets across model depths, assigning descending or heterogeneous budgets to different layers
(
Ge et al. 2024
;
Cai et al. 2024
;
Feng et al. 2025
;
Fu et al. 2025
)
.
Other methods adapt cache budgets using heuristic metrics derived from the prompt, task, or generation time statistics
(
Zhou et al. 2025
;
Shen et al. 2025
;
Tang et al. 2025
;
Xiao et al. 2024a
)
.
Together, these works show that a uniform cache budget is often suboptimal and that tailored budgets can improve performance.

These methods explored the importance of heterogeneous budgets, however their design spaces are still limited.
Many adaptive methods primarily adjust how much cache budget is assigned, while ignoring which eviction pattern should be assigned to each layer.
PolyKV takes a complementary approach by expanding the design space along two dimensions, eviction pattern and cache budget.
It aims to identify a better strategy for each layer under the same overall cache budget.
This allows different layers to follow different eviction rules and receive different cache budgets, better matching the heterogeneous characteristics of each layers.

3.1
Layer-wise KV Eviction Formulation

Consider a model with
L
transformer layers and two phases
\Phi=\{\mathrm{pre},\mathrm{dec}\}
, corresponding to prefill and decoding.
At generation step
t
, layer
l
maintains a KV cache.
PolyKV represents a phase-aware layer-wise eviction strategy as

\Pi=\left\{\left(\{r_{l,\phi}\}_{\phi\in\Phi},\{b_{l,\phi}\}_{\phi\in\Phi},\right)\right\}_{l=1}^{L},

where
r_{l,\phi}\in\mathcal{R}
is the eviction pattern assigned to layer
l
during phase
\phi
, and
b_{l,\phi}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
is the budget assigned to layer
l
.

The total KV budget is constrained by

\sum_{l=1}^{L}b_{l,\phi}=B_{\phi},\qquad b_{l,\phi}\geq b_{\min}.

(1)

where
B
matches the total budget used by the corresponding baseline and
b_{\min}
is the minimal budget size for each layer.
Equivalently, if all layers are compared under an average budget
\bar{b}
, then
B=L\bar{b}
.
Here,
b_{l}
denotes the cache budget for layer
l
.

At generation step
t
, the actual cache size of layer
l
is at most
\min(t,b_{l})
.
FullKV is treated as a special eviction pattern whose budget equals the current full context length.
Therefore, any layer assigned FullKV is not exempt from the budget accounting; its full KV cost is included in the total budget.

In conventional model-wide eviction methods, all layers have the same eviction pattern and the same cache budget.
Adaptive eviction methods have different cache budgets, but they still follow a common eviction pattern.
PolyKV enlarges the design space by allowing both the eviction pattern and the cache budget to vary across layers.

To instantiate this design space, we incorporate a set of representative KV eviction baselines as eviction patterns within the PolyKV framework.
The goal of PolyKV is to explore whether existing eviction patterns can be used more effectively when they are assigned in a layer-wise manner with different cache budgets.

3.2
Offline Calibration

The design space defined above is too large for exhaustive enumeration.
With
|\mathcal{R}|
candidate patterns,
L
layers, and two phases, the number of possible eviction assignments is
|\mathcal{R}|^{2L}
even before considering budget allocation.
PolyKV therefore uses offline calibration signals to construct a fixed strategy directly, rather than performing online policy search or exhaustive configuration search.

Given a small calibration set
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}
, PolyKV first runs fullKV inference to obtain reference activations.
It then profiles baselines with the same token prefixes and records activations for comparison.
Calibration estimates two layer-specific signals:

•

eviction preference signal
, which measures which eviction parttern best preserves each layer’s fullKV computation.

•

Cache budget sensitivity signal
, which measures how strongly each layer is affected as its KV budget is reduced.

The calibration set is disjoint from evaluation data.
The resulting strategy is fixed for a model and target budget, and is reused during deployment without online policy search.

Calibration metrics.

For eviction preference, PolyKV uses these cosine similarity and PPL sensitivity as layer-wise signals.
For each candidate eviction pattern, cosine similarity measures how closely a layer-wise perturbed run matches the FullKV reference representation:

C_{l,\phi,r}=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}}\left[\cos\left(h^{\mathrm{full}}_{l,\phi}(x),h^{(l,\phi,r)}_{l,\phi}(x)\right)\right],

(2)

where
h^{\mathrm{full}}_{l,\phi}(x)
denotes the layer-
l
activation under FullKV during phase
\phi
,
and
h^{(l,\phi,r)}_{l,\phi}(x)
denotes the corresponding activation when only layer
l
in phase
\phi
uses eviction pattern
r
while all other layer-phase pairs remain FullKV.
A large value indicates better preservation.

PPL sensitivity measures the perplexity degradation caused by assigning a candidate eviction pattern to a single layer-phase pair:

\Delta\mathrm{PPL}_{l,\phi,r}=\mathrm{PPL}_{l,\phi,r}-\mathrm{PPL}_{\mathrm{full}}.

(3)

Here,
\mathrm{PPL}_{l,\phi,r}
is computed by applying eviction pattern
r
only to layer
l
during phase
\phi
, while keeping all other layers and the other phase under FullKV.
For this metric, PolyKV prefers the pattern with the smallest degradation.

For cache budget sensitivity, PolyKV uses entropy and PPL sensitivity.
The entropy signal is computed from the layer-wise attention distribution:

H_{l}=-\sum_{i}p_{l,i}\log p_{l,i},

(4)

where
p_{l,i}
denotes the attention probability assigned to key position
i
at layer
l
, averaged over calibration examples, tokens, and heads.
Higher entropy indicates more diffuse attention and therefore a bigger budget is required.

PPL sensitivity aggregates the worst per-layer degradation across patterns:

S^{\mathrm{ppl}}_{l}=\max_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\Delta\mathrm{PPL}_{l,r}.

(5)

Layers with larger
S^{\mathrm{ppl}}_{l}
are more sensitive and are assigned with larger budgets.

Given a sensitivity signal
S_{l}
, PolyKV allocates per-layer budgets under the global budget constraint in Eq. equation
1
.
A proportional allocation is used:

\tilde{b}_{l}=b_{\min}+(B-Lb_{\min})\frac{S_{l}}{\sum_{j=1}^{L}S_{j}},

(6)

Given a selected eviction preference signal
g
and a selected cache budget sensitivity signal
m
, Algorithm
1
constructs the final phase-aware layer-wise strategy.
Here,
\operatorname{Best}_{g}
selects the candidate pattern according to the ordering induced by signal
g
.
For example, cosine similarity is ranked in descending order, while PPL degradation is ranked in ascending order.
The operator
\operatorname{Round}_{B}
converts continuous budgets into integer budgets and enforces
\sum_{l=1}^{L}b_{l,\phi}=B
for each phase
\phi
.

3.3
PolyKV Implementation

We implement PolyKV as a hook-based framework that makes baseline methods easy to integrate and extend.
Our survey of existing methods shows that, compared with standard FullKV inference, most methods inject additional logic at a few common points in the generation pipeline, as illustrated in Figure
2
(b).
PolyKV exposes these points as programmable hooks.

The offline calibration stage produces eviction strategies.
As shown in Figure
2
(a), PolyKV supports YAML-based configurations, which makes large-scale experiments practical and reproducible.
During inference, PolyKV loads the calibrated strategy and attaches the corresponding logic to the model generation process through lightweight hooks.

Each eviction pattern is implemented as a policy composed of several hook handlers.
If a policy does not implement a particular hook, PolyKV falls back to the original model execution path.
This design decouples eviction logic from model specific code and allows different eviction methods to be incorporated under a unified interface.
Adding a new KV method only requires implementing its hook handlers and registering it as a policy, without modifying the runtime or existing methods.

4
Evaluation

We evaluate PolyKV under fixed total KV cache budgets, comparing against representative KV cache eviction baselines.
For all methods, the total cache budget is equivalent.

4.1
Experimental Setup

We evaluate PolyKV on LLaMA-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B.
Experiments are conducted on two long-context benchmarks, LongBench and RULER.
LongBench evaluates realistic long-context understanding across multiple task families, while RULER provides controlled stress tests for long-context retrieval and tracking.

We instantiate the candidate eviction set in PolyKV with representative KV cache compression methods, including H2O, TOVA, CAM, PyramidKV, SnapKV, D2O, CAKE, ScissorHands, and StreamingLLM.

4.3
Robustness across Budgets

We sweep the average KV budget from 128 to 1024 tokens per layer to evaluate PolyKV under severe to moderate compression regimes.
Across all tested budgets, PolyKV consistently outperforms the strongest baseline.
Notably, the strongest baseline is not fixed across budgets.
TOVA performs best under 128-token setting, while CAKE becomes stronger at larger budgets.
The advantage of PolyKV is largest under tighter budgets and gradually narrows as the budget increases, which is expected because all compressed methods move closer to the FullKV and the remaining recoverable gap becomes smaller.

A more direct indication of cache efficiency comes from cross-budget comparison.
Using only half the cache budget, PolyKV outperforms the best 512-token baseline by up to
0.49
points at a 256-token budget, and the best 1024-token baseline by up to
0.22
points at a 512-token budget.
These results indicate that PolyKV can reach a quality level that a global eviction rule requires substantially more cache to approach.

The sweep also helps separate the effects of the two layer-wise design choices.
The main performance gain comes from assigning different eviction rules to different layers.
Budget allocation has a more conditional effect.
Under the smallest budgets, uniform allocation is more reliable, likely because every layer needs a minimum amount of cache to avoid severe information loss.
Under this budget, redistributing an already limited budget can make some layers too constrained.
Once the average budget reaches 512 tokens or above, heterogeneous allocation begins to outperform uniform allocation, and the advantage remains consistent at larger budgets.
This suggests that budget heterogeneity is most useful when there is sufficient capacity to reallocate without starving sensitive layers.
Overall, the results show that heterogeneous eviction provides the primary gain, while heterogeneous budget allocation further improves performance when the cache budget is less restrictive.

The degradation on Qwen, especially in NIAH retrieval, suggests that a heterogeneous layer-wise policy can still discard tokens that appear low-salience during calibration but become decisive at evaluation time.

5
Conclusion

This paper revisits KV cache eviction from the perspective of transformer layer-wise heterogeneity, showing that a single global eviction pattern is a coarse abstraction for long context inference.
We introduced PolyKV, a phase-aware layer-wise framework that jointly assigns eviction pattern and KV cache budgets under a fixed total memory constraint.
PolyKV uses offline calibration to estimate each layer’s eviction pattern preference and cache sensitivity, enabling static heterogeneous compositions of existing KV eviction methods.
Experiments on LLaMA-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B show that, on LongBench under the same 512-token average budget, PolyKV improves over the strongest single-policy baselines and recovers 54.5% and 25.7% of the gap to FullKV, respectively.
Across a 128–1024 budget sweep on LLaMA-3.1-8B, PolyKV consistently outperforms the best baseline, with the largest gains under tighter cache budgets.
Ablations indicate that layer-wise retention routing, particularly during prefill, is the main source of improvement, while non-uniform budget allocation provides additional but more model- and budget-dependent benefits.
At the same time, weaker results on exact retrieval-style RULER tasks and code-like workloads indicate that current calibration signals do not always preserve sparse but decisive evidence tokens.
Overall, PolyKV suggests that KV cache eviction should be treated as a compositional, layer-aware optimization problem rather than as a single global retention policy, opening a path toward more reliable long-context eviction strategies.
