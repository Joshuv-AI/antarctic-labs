Translational landscape of cytokine-induced killer cell therapy: an analysis based on the Informa database
Authors: Yanni Zhang, Han Sun, Ye Yuan, Dehua Liu, Jinhan Qiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study maps the global clinical trial landscape of cytokine-induced killer (CIK) cell therapy across malignancies and selected immune-related disorders, based on 199 interventional trials retrieved from the Informa database up to February 2026. Analysis reveals a rapidly expanding field dominated by early-phase studies, with China leading clinical translation and academic institutions as primary sponsors. Oncology trials are most frequent, particularly in lung cancer, leukemia, and hepatocellular carcinoma, while combinatorial strategies such as dendritic cell-CIK therapy or checkpoint inhibitor combinations are increasingly explored. Adjuvant and maintenance settings predominate over neoadjuvant applications, and emerging studies indicate potential extension into autoimmune and inflammatory diseases. These findings underscore the translational potential of CIK therapy, highlight evolving combination strategies, and reveal opportunities to optimize patient stratification, standardize cell preparation, and incorporate biomarker-guided approaches. Collectively, this landscape analysis highlights key trends shaping the future clinical development of CIK-based immunotherapy and informs strategic design considerations for advancing its integration into modern oncology and immunomodulatory treatments.

Cytokine-induced killer (CIK) cells constitute a heterogeneous population of effector T lymphocytes generated through in vitro induction and expansion of peripheral blood cells with multiple cytokines. CIK cells were originally described by Schmidt-Wolf et al in 1991 using an SCID mouse/human lymphoma model, in which ex vivo expanded cytokine-induced effector cells demonstrated potent antitumor activity.1 These cells combine antigen-specific recognition of T lymphocytes with non-major histocompatibility complex-restricted cytotoxicity characteristic of natural killer (NK) cells. The principal effector subset, CD3+CD56+ cells, exhibits strong proliferative capacity, relative ease of ex vivo expansion, and a favorable safety profile with limited toxicity, supporting their potential as an accessible cellular immunotherapy platform. Unlike conventional chemotherapy or radiotherapy, which directly eliminate tumor cells, CIK cells exert sustained antitumor effects by enhancing host immune surveillance. This mechanism provides a rationale for their application in eliminating minimal residual disease and reducing tumor recurrence, key objectives in translational oncology.2

With advances in cellular therapy technologies and the increasing significance of immunotherapy, CIK cells have emerged as a promising therapeutic option in various malignancies and immune-related disorders. However, despite their potential, the global clinical development landscape and application priorities of CIK cell therapy remain poorly characterized, especially in terms of their translational progress and therapeutic strategies. This gap is critical, as understanding the trends in clinical trials will allow for more effective integration of CIK therapy into mainstream oncology treatments. Current literature has focused on individual studies or isolated trial reports, but there is a lack of comprehensive, landscape-level analyses that integrate these findings and outline the real-world challenges and future directions for this therapy. Previous registry-based efforts, including the international registry on CIK cells reported by Zhang and colleagues, have provided important clinical evidence and standardized reporting experience for CIK cell-based immunotherapy. Nevertheless, an updated, database-driven, landscape-level analysis integrating recent global clinical trial activity, translational progress, and therapeutic strategies remains needed.3 To address this gap, this study provides a translational landscape analysis of clinical trials involving CIK cell therapy from 2004 to 2025, with the aim to highlight global trends, identify emerging therapeutic strategies, and outline the key areas that need further investigation. By doing so, we can offer insight into the clinical development and translational potential of CIK cell therapy, which could significantly inform future clinical and regulatory strategies.

Data for this analysis were retrieved from the Informa database (https://pharma.id.informa.com/) with a cut-off date of February 2026. This platform is a subscription-based clinical research intelligence resource and is generally accessible through institutional or licensed access rather than as a fully open public database. The search strategy included the keywords “cytokine-induced killer cells,” “CIK cells,” and related terms, with Medical Subject Headings (MeSH)-based expansion to capture synonymous and related expressions, aiming to identify all interventional clinical trials of CIK cells across oncology and selected immune-related disorders. After initial retrieval, duplicate entries and overlapping trials were removed. Eligibility criteria were clearly defined: interventional trials involving human subjects, reporting clinical outcomes or translational endpoints, with available trial status (completed, ongoing, or planned); observational or preclinical studies and trials lacking sufficient methodological details were excluded. Data extraction and trial selection were conducted independently by two researchers, with discrepancies resolved by consensus, and manual cross-checking of trial registries, publications, and other sources was performed to verify trial characteristics. For trials with multiple arms, indications, or targets, an inclusive counting strategy was applied. This analysis was prespecified as a landscape assessment rather than a meta-analysis; no risk-of-bias evaluation was performed. Limitations of the database, including incomplete reporting and potential delays in trial updates, are acknowledged. Following screening and eligibility assessment, 199 clinical trials were included for analysis.

Temporal trends demonstrated a marked increase in the number of CIK cell trials after 2010, peaking around 2015, and subsequently stabilizing (figure 1A), suggesting a transition from early exploratory growth to a more mature phase of clinical development. Phase distribution analysis revealed that most studies were Phase I/II or Phase II, indicating that the field remains largely early-stage, with limited late-phase confirmatory trials. Nevertheless, 11 Phase III trials and 11 Phase IV trials were identified among the 199 included studies, suggesting that a subset of CIK cell-based interventions have progressed into late-stage clinical evaluation and post-marketing or real-world clinical assessment. Geographically, trials were predominantly conducted in China (154/199), followed by the USA (17/199), highlighting China’s leading role in clinical translation (figure 1B). This regional concentration likely reflects differences in regulatory frameworks, funding support, and translational pathways. Regarding trial status, the majority have been completed, while a significant portion remains ongoing or planned, demonstrating both a substantial evidence base and sustained research activity (figure 1C). Sponsor analysis showed that academic institutions were the main drivers of CIK clinical research (167/199), whereas industry participation was limited, revealing a gap between academic innovation and large-scale commercialization (figure 1D).

In terms of therapeutic strategies, CIK monotherapy was most commonly applied, followed by dendritic cell (DC)-CIK and other DC-based combination approaches (figure 1E), reflecting ongoing efforts to enhance efficacy through combinatorial immune strategies. Oncology accounted for the vast majority of trials (193/199), with lung cancer, leukemia, and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) being the most frequently studied (figure 1F), while emerging studies in autoimmune and inflammatory diseases suggest expanding applications beyond oncology. Within perioperative settings, adjuvant therapy (27 trials) and maintenance or consolidation therapy (17 trials) predominated, whereas neoadjuvant applications were rare (2 trials), indicating that CIK therapy is primarily used as an immune-enhancing adjunct following primary treatment. Overall, the global CIK clinical trial landscape is characterized by early-phase predominance, regional concentration in China, academic leadership, diversification of therapeutic strategies, and increasing exploration of combinatorial approaches. When integrated into a comprehensive visual framework (figure 1A–F), these data illustrate the field’s developmental trajectory, key drivers, core therapeutic directions, and areas for future translational focus.

The rapid development of CIK cell therapy has been fueled by key clinical advancements and evolving therapeutic strategies. The marked increase in trials post-2010, particularly after the success of early studies, aligns with broader trends in cellular immunotherapy. One example is a study evaluating the long-term effects of CIK therapy as an adjuvant treatment following curative therapy for HCC. The study, conducted by multiple medical institutions in Korea, showed that CIK therapy significantly reduced recurrence or mortality risk by 28% in patients with early HCC, with cancer-specific survival improving by approximately 51% compared with the control group.4 Similarly, a meta-analysis of 16 randomized controlled trials involving 1197 patients with lung cancer revealed that CIK therapy significantly improved objective response rate, disease control rate, and overall survival, with DC-CIK combined immunotherapy showing 93.3% progression-free survival at 1 year, compared with 79.3% in the control group.5 This growing body of evidence underscores the potential of CIK therapy as a highly promising treatment, particularly in solid tumors such as liver and lung cancer, and the feasibility of combining it with immune checkpoint inhibitors like programmed cell death protein-1 (PD-1) antibodies to enhance treatment outcomes.68 The integration of modified CIK cells with tumor stem cell membrane microparticles further elevates their tumor-targeting potential, as seen in patients with relapsed T/NK-cell lymphomas, where two of three patients achieved durable remission.9

Despite these promising results, several challenges remain that must be addressed for broader clinical implementation. Heterogeneity in clinical trial design, variability in CIK cell preparation protocols, and the lack of large-scale randomized controlled trials impede definitive efficacy assessment. Key clinical bottlenecks include the need for standardized biomarkers for patient stratification, toxicity concerns, and overcoming immune suppression within the tumor microenvironment (TME). The efficacy of combinatorial approaches, such as the integration of CIK therapy with checkpoint inhibitors or next-generation cell constructs, will likely be enhanced if these challenges are met. Emerging studies suggest that optimizing the immune landscape—through better understanding of TME modulation, cell persistence, and more effective delivery mechanisms—could pave the way for future advancements in CIK therapy.10 Moreover, regulatory frameworks and the gap between academic innovation and large-scale commercialization remain crucial hurdles that require increased collaboration between academic institutions and the biopharmaceutical industry. Moving forward, standardized, multicenter, and biomarker-driven trials are necessary to consolidate clinical evidence and further refine patient stratification, ultimately improving the clinical application and integration of CIK therapy in modern oncological practice.
