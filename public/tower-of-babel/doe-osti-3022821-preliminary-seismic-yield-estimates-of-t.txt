Abstract

 A series of large damaging explosions involving ﬁreworks storage occurred near Esparto,
Yolo County California on July 1, 2025. Three explosions were located and reported by the
University of California Berkeley Seismology Laboratory (UCB/BSL) and the United States
Geological Survey (USGS). Analysis of local distance (< 30 km) seismic recordings of the ﬁrst
blast indicates that there were actually three explosions with later blasts delayed by about 3
and 30 seconds. We measured the ﬁrst arriving P-wave amplitudes on four of these events with
good signal-to-noise ra<os. Yields were es<mated using the model of Ford et al. (2024)
assuming surface emplacement. Results indicate that the es<mated explosive yields are
consistent between the events in the range 1500 to 4700 kg trinitrotoluene (TNT) equivalent.
Es<mate yields may inform forensic analysis of this tragedy.

Summary and Conclusions

 Seismic waveforms from the July 1, 2025 explosions near Esparto, California show clear
evidence of impulsive, energe<c events. The air-blasts are especially prominent on the seismic

records and they are much larger than the seismic amplitudes. While three explosion events
were reported by the Northern California Seismic Network, analysis of the ﬁrst explosion reveals
two subsequent blasts delayed by 3 and 30 seconds from the reported event.

 Measurements of peak P-wave displacements from four of the explosions were used to
es<mate yields. Results indicate yields of in the range 1500-4700 kg TNT equivalent (Table 1).
Standard devia<ons are high, up to 50%, due to the variability in the measurements. This may
be due to measurement error for these extremely small displacements (1-100 nanometers),
likely at the threshold of detec<on above background noise. Systema<c errors likely also
contribute to the scamer in yield es<mates. Site eﬀects due to local geology around seismic
sta<ons can systema<cally bias amplitudes. Note that the rela<ve P-wave amplitudes of events
at a given sta<on tend to be rela<vely constant. This suggests that calibra<on of site eﬀects
could reduce uncertain<es in yield es<mates.

 This preliminary analysis indicates that the explosions in Esparto on July 1 2025 were
large, consistent with the tragic loss seven lives and damage at the site. Addi<onal analysis
using other methods may help reﬁne yield es<mates. In par<cular, analysis of air-blast or
infrasound may constrain yield. These atmospheric overpressure signals may have been
observed at longer ranges with bemer signal-to-noise ra<os than the seismic ground mo<ons.
Modeling of the seismic surface waves (Rg) with a local wavespeed model may constrain the
seismic moment and yield. Comparisons of seismic signals with other surface explosions of
known yield in dry alluvium geology could help constrain the yields of these blasts.
