Abstract
The objective of this article is to propose an interdisciplinary interpretive matrix that articulates philosophy of
mathematics, cognitive psychiatry, and recent neuroscientific discoveries (2021-2026) on embodied numerical
cognition, in order to bridge the gap between the formal validity of mathematics and its cognitive constitution. The
philosophy of mathematics traditionally focuses on the justification of arithmetic truths and the ontological status of
mathematical objects, often neglecting the fundamental question of how concrete plurality transforms into numerical
concepts in the human mind. This integrative review articulates classical and contemporary literature (2021-2026) on
numerical cognition, integrating perspectives from cognitive psychiatry, philosophy of mathematics, and neuroscience.
We argue that logic and mathematics formalize structures already constituted by corporeal, temporal, perceptual, motor,
communicative, and reflective processes. Drawing on recent evidence regarding embodied cognition, finger gnosis, and
the development of counting, we propose a sequential interpretive matrix: biological discrimination, iterative praxia,
reflexivity, communication, conceptualization, and logical formalization. This interdisciplinary clinical hypothesis aims
to bridge the gap between the formal validity of mathematics and its cognitive constitution, with implications for
understanding numerical processing disorders such as acalculia and Gerstmann syndrome.

1. Introduction
The philosophy of mathematics has traditionally concentrated its efforts on justifying arithmetic truths, the nature of
mathematical objects, and the universality of mathematical propositions (Frege, 1884). However, this approach frequently
neglects a foundational question: the psychogenetic process by which concrete plurality in the world becomes, for the human
mind, the abstract concept of number. The starting point of this investigation lies in the fundamental distinction between the
existence of quantitative differences in the environment and the psychological, praxic, and communicative constitution of
number.
The capacity to discriminate quantities does not equate to the possession of the concept of number. An organism may
react differently to two or three stimuli, or groups may be paired one-to-one, without this demonstrating voluntary understanding
of counting or the operation of succession (Sixtus et al., 2023). The central thesis we defend is that logic and mathematics
formalize structures already constituted by corporeal, temporal, perceptual, motor, communicative, and reflective processes.
Mathematical form, in its precision and analytical power, should not be confused with the genesis of the processes it formalizes.
In this sense, logic captures the anatomy of structure, while cognitive psychiatry seeks to reconstruct its physiology.
The objective of this article is to propose an interdisciplinary interpretive matrix that articulates philosophy of mathematics,
cognitive psychiatry, and recent neuroscientific discoveries (2021-2026) on embodied numerical cognition, in order to bridge
the gap between the formal validity of mathematics and its cognitive constitution. We present this formulation as an
interdisciplinary clinical and theoretical hypothesis, intended to guide future empirical investigations and deepen the
understanding of numerical processing disorders such as acalculia and Gerstmann syndrome.

2. Methodology: Integrative Review Strategy
This integrative theoretical review synthesizes literature from cognitive psychiatry, philosophy of mathematics, and
neuroscience. Our methodology follows established frameworks for integrative reviews, aiming to build a novel conceptual
model rather than systematically cataloging all empirical findings (Risemberg et al., 2026; Ferreira, 2021; Snyder, 2019;
Whittemore & Knafl, 2005).

2.1 Search strategy and selection criteria
We conducted narrative literature searches across PubMed, Scopus, and PsycINFO databases. The primary search
period covered 2021-2026 to capture contemporary evidence, supplemented by foundational texts (e.g., Frege, Piaget, Vygotsky)
necessary for theoretical continuity. Search terms included combinations of: ("numerical cognition" OR "number sense") AND
("embodied cognition" OR "finger gnosis" OR "sensorimotor numerosity" OR "subitization").

Inclusion criteria prioritized:
1. Empirical studies (behavioral and neuroimaging) demonstrating sensorimotor contributions to numerical processing.
2. Theoretical works addressing the psychogenesis of mathematical concepts.
3. Clinical reports detailing numerical deficits (e.g., Gerstmann syndrome, dyscalculia).
Exclusion criteria comprised: duplicate records retrieved across databases; incomplete texts, preliminary notes, and conference
abstracts; preprints lacking peer review; and editorials or commentaries without original data or a substantive theoretical
contribution.
After application of these criteria, 23 (twenty-three) primary sources were retained for the interpretive synthesis
presented in Section 3, as follows: Frege (1884); Moeller et al. (2012); Dehaene (2011); Carotenuto (2026); Skagenholt et al.
(2025); “Electrophysiological Signatures” (2022); Tucker and Johnson (2022); Carey (2009); Spelke and Kinzler (2007); Gelman
and Gallistel (1978); Wynn (1992); Anobile et al. (2026); Fischer and Brugger (2011); Soylu et al. (2018); Fischer et al. (2022);
“Gerstmann Syndrome Deconstructed” (2021); Dehaene and Cohen (1995); Andres et al. (2012); “Numerical Cognition after
Brain Injury” (2023); Kougioumtzis (2026); Vygotsky (1978); Piaget (1952); and Geary (2026).

2.2 Synthesis and framework construction
The selected literature was synthesized to construct the proposed six-stage interpretive matrix. Rather than a metaanalytic aggregation of effect sizes, our synthesis identifies mechanistic transitions between developmental stages. We
acknowledge the risk of narrative selection bias inherent in integrative reviews; therefore, our proposed matrix is presented as a
testable clinical hypothesis rather than a definitive developmental taxonomy.

3. Results and Discussion
3.1 Critique of logicism and the necessity of psychogenesis
Gottlob Frege's project of founding arithmetic represents one of the most rigorous attempts to derive arithmetic laws from
pure logical principles (Frege, 1884). By defining number in terms of one-to-one correspondence (equinumerosity) and rejecting
psychologism, Frege sought to guarantee the objectivity and universality of mathematics, divorcing it from private intuitions or
contingent mental processes.
Although logicism established solid foundations for the philosophy of language and formal logic, its radical exclusion of
psychogenesis creates a significant explanatory limitation. One-to-one correspondence can be experienced in practice (for
example, associating each person with a chair) without being interpreted numerically (Moeller et al., 2012). Bijectivity serves to
verify numerical equality after the consolidation of the concept of number, but it does not elucidate the cognitive process by
which the mind acquires such a concept.
Logic and cognitive psychiatry thus address different but complementary questions. Frege asks what number is
objectively and how arithmetic truths are justified; cognitive psychiatry asks how the human subject becomes capable of

3.2 From biological discrimination to subitization
Nature provides abundant examples of quantitative discrimination without the presence of a thematized numerical
concept. Animals adjust behaviors based on magnitude differences, choosing the larger food source or evaluating risks in
confrontations (Dehaene, 2011). The capacity to discriminate two from three does not equate to the possession of the numbers
two and three. An organism may respond differently to two configurations without understanding successor relationships, stable
cardinality, serial order, or addition. This distinction is critical because it prevents a conceptual leap: automatic magnitude
discrimination, while foundational, remains fundamentally different from voluntary, iterative, and serial counting. The
chimpanzee that flees when outnumbered demonstrates quantitative discrimination, not numerical comprehension.
Recent empirical studies demonstrate that young children's transition from subitization to conceptual understanding of
number involves specific perceptuomotor transformations. Carotenuto's (2026) microgenetic analysis of a preschooler's
interaction with multi-touch technology reveals how finger gnosis and control develop in real-time as children recognize partwhole relationships. This suggests a 'counting without counting' phenomenon, in which perceptuomotor engagement with
quantity precedes and scaffolds explicit numerical understanding.
In humans, small quantities (typically up to four items) can be apprehended globally and with precision without explicit
counting, a phenomenon known as subitization (Skagenholt et al., 2025). Recent neurophysiological studies have demonstrated
that subitization possesses distinct electrophysiological signatures compared to estimation of larger quantities, suggesting
specialized parallel visual processing mechanisms ("Electrophysiological Signatures," 2022).
Subitization provides a quantitative gestalt, but does not in itself constitute the concept of number. The formal concept
of "three" requires recognition of quantity invariance regardless of format (three sounds, three objects, three fingers), insertion
of this value into an ordered series, understanding the successor relationship (two plus one), and the notion of cardinality (Tucker
& Johnson, 2022). Our framework intersects with foundational neurocognitive models of development. While Carey and Spelke's
core knowledge accounts posit innate systems for approximate magnitudes and precise small-number tracking (Carey, 2009;
Spelke & Kinzler, 2007), our matrix emphasizes the praxic transition between these core systems and exact symbolic numbers.
Similarly, while Gelman and Gallistel's (1978) counting principles outline the logical requirements for counting (e.g., stable
order, one-to-one correspondence), our model focuses on the embodied mechanisms that allow children to fulfill these principles.
The progression we describe aligns with Wynn's (1992) "knower-levels," but we argue that the transition from a "subset-knower"
to a "cardinal-principle-knower" is heavily mediated by the iterative praxia of the body. The transition from automatic magnitude
discrimination to numerical conceptualization requires a specific cognitive operation: the process itself must become, at least
partially, an object of monitoring. What was automatic begins to observe itself. Three dots arranged in a line and three dots
arranged in a column present different spatial gestalts to perception. For the non-numerized mind, spatiality dominates over
cardinality. The subject must learn to treat qualitatively different configurations as equivalent under one aspect — their quantity
— without losing the discrimination that prevents counting the same element twice. This abstraction is not passive reception; it
is an active operation that transforms spatial perception into numerical cognition. When comparing two dots with three dots, the
cognitive system simultaneously captures an equality and a difference. The units share the same perceptual-spatial quality, yet

Recent discoveries in psychophysics and animal neurophysiology reveal the critical role of sensorimotor processes in

have been shown to be tuned to the number of self-generated actions; a parallel pattern extends to human cognition, where
precision in estimating numerosity based on self-generated actions explains up to 30% of the variance in mental calculation
ability in specific cohorts — reportedly three to fifteen times superior to spatial and temporal visual numerosity (Anobile et al.,
2026). However, these claims require further replication across diverse samples and tasks to ensure they are not overgeneralized,
and must be interpreted alongside controls for general motor ability and working memory.
This robust predictive relationship suggests that the sensorimotor number system functions not merely as a perceptual
mechanism but as a foundational system for mathematical development. By bridging automatic magnitude discrimination and
deliberate numerical reasoning, sensorimotor processes provide the cognitive substrate upon which formal numerical concepts
are constructed.

3.3 Embodied cognition: praxia, gesture, and finger gnosis
The numerical series rests upon a more general structure of life itself: to proceed, complete, repeat, anticipate, and
transcend the present moment. The 'next' is initially a form of vital action before it becomes formalized as the abstract relation
of successor. Counting is not an arbitrary cultural invention imposed upon a passive mind; it emerges from fundamental patterns
of living organisms — patterns of continuation, completion, and anticipation. The development of numerical cognition is
intrinsically linked to corporality and motor action. Contemporary literature (2021-2026) has consolidated the perspective of
"embodied cognition" applied to mathematics, demonstrating that physical interactions with the world profoundly shape abstract
thinking (Fischer & Brugger, 2011; Sixtus et al., 2023).
The psychogenetic trajectory frequently evolves from pointing to counting. Initially, the finger links the body to the
object in an indicative manner. Subsequently, the finger itself substitutes for the object, becoming a unit marker and fundamental
operational support (Soylu et al., 2018). The praxia of finger counting requires complex coordination of vision, somesthesia,
movement, spatial working memory, and selective attention.
Recent studies confirm that fine motor skills and finger gnosis (the capacity to perceive and distinguish one's own
fingers without visual control) are significant predictors of numerical competencies in preschool children (Fischer et al., 2022).
The classical clinical association observed in Gerstmann Syndrome, which groups finger agnosia, acalculia, right-left
disorientation, and agraphia due to left angular gyrus lesions, corroborates the overlap of neural networks responsible for finger
representation and calculation ("Gerstmann Syndrome Deconstructed," 2021). This embodied perspective provides a
developmental precursor to Dehaene and Cohen's (1995) Triple-Code Model. Before quantities are represented as visual Arabic
digits, verbal words, or an abstract analog magnitude, they are processed as sensorimotor actions. The persistence of spatialnumerical associations, such as the SNARC effect (Spatial-Numerical Association of Response Codes), where smaller numbers
are associated with leftward responses and larger numbers with rightward responses, provides enduring evidence of how spatialmotor mapping permanently structures the mental number line (Dehaene et al., 1993). Furthermore, meta-analytic and training
evidence nuances these causal interpretations. While studies demonstrate robust correlations between finger gnosis and
arithmetic (Noël, 2005), training interventions yield mixed results, suggesting that finger representation may scaffold early
arithmetic but is not strictly deterministic for all calculation profiles (Andres et al., 2012). The development of finger gnosis and
motor control represents a critical juncture in numerical development. Carotenuto (2026) documented how children's

The use of fingers introduces temporality, rhythm, and directionality into static spatial collections. Iteration is not mere
repetition of the same result. The operation is repeated, but a new term is produced. The mechanism remains constant; the
quantity changes. This combination of operational identity and novelty of result constitutes the cognitive nucleus of the successor
function. When a child repeats a rhythmic gesture — clap, clap, clap — each repetition produces a new counting term. The same
motor program generates different numerical outcomes. The fundamental mechanism involves a precise coordination between
functional identity and positional difference. To count three ducks, each duck must be different from the others as an occurrence
and equivalent to the others as a counting unit. If all were merely equal, individuation would be impossible; if all were merely
different, no common unit would exist. Number emerges from this coordination ("Numerical Cognition after Brain Injury,"
2023). Recent theoretical work validates these developmental mechanisms. Contemporary research on embodied cognition in
STEM education identifies gesture-based representation and perceptual-spatial structuring as primary learning mechanisms
(Kougioumtzis, 2026).
These mechanisms yield moderate positive effects on learning performance specifically when they substitute rather than
add to existing cognitive demands — a principle with direct implications for numerical development. This distinction clarifies
why the transition from qualitative perception to quantitative abstraction requires progressive neutralization of spatial distractors.
When embodied activities align functionally with learning objectives, they facilitate abstraction; when they add extraneous
cognitive load, they impede it.
3.4 Communication as objectification of number
Quantity differs fundamentally from sensible qualities. When we say 'the ducks are black, slow, short, and three,'
grammar places all these expressions as predicates. Yet 'three' is not a sensible quality of each individual duck. It is a property
of the plurality taken as a totality. Cognitively, it is a relational-operational property — it exists only in the relationship between
units and in the operation that produces their totality. This distinction clarifies why numerical concepts require a different
cognitive mechanism than perceptual qualities. The transition from private quantitative perception to an objective mathematical
concept is mediated by the necessity of communication. When quantity must be conveyed to another individual, the subject is
compelled to externalize perception, highlighting the quantitative property and stabilizing it in a public and reproducible sign
(Vygotsky, 1978). Communication does not arbitrarily create quantity — three objects are objectively real. However,
communication compels the subject to isolate the quantitative property, stabilize it, and convert it into a sign. The property
acquires objective conceptual existence only when it can be presented to another person, recognized by them, and reproduced
across different material supports. I may represent three by drawing three points in sand, pointing three times, striking three
times, raising three fingers, or opening a closed hand to show three units. Each medium is different; the quantity remains
invariant.
Intersubjectivity demands constancy. The numerical sign must remain recognizable regardless of the material support
used to represent it. This communicative requirement transforms quantity into a public invariance. This objectification process
is highly sensitive to cultural and linguistic variability. Cross-cultural studies with indigenous populations, such as the Pirahã
and Mundurukú, demonstrate that without a culturally transmitted exact numeral lexicon, exact large-quantity calculation does
not spontaneously develop, despite intact approximate number systems (Gordon, 2004; Pica et al., 2004). Furthermore,

This compression is not arbitrary; it reflects the actual developmental progression from embodied action to symbolic
representation. The symbol succeeds precisely because it encodes a procedure that the reader has already internalized through
sensorimotor experience.

3.5.1 Clinical and educational implications
The proposed interpretive matrix carries significant implications for clinical neuropsychology and educational practice.
Understanding numerical cognition as a developmental achievement grounded in embodied processes suggests that deficits in
finger gnosis, motor coordination, or spatial processing may underlie calculation difficulties, as observed in Gerstmann syndrome
and developmental dyscalculia.
We must emphasize the heterogeneity of developmental trajectories. Not all dyscalculia profiles are sensorimotor-led;
deficits may arise independently in phonological processing, visuospatial working memory, or domain-general executive
functions. Therefore, we propose tailored intervention decision trees rather than a one-size-fits-all approach. Ethical and practical
considerations demand that embodied interventions remain accessible and avoid pathologizing cultural counting practices or
individual differences in motor ability.
Furthermore, the proposed sequence is not strictly unidirectional. Current evidence suggests reciprocal influences: while
sensorimotor numerosity scaffolds early symbol acquisition, the acquisition of exact symbolic systems retroactively alters
perceptual salience and magnitude representations (symbol grounding).

3.6 Towards an evidence-backed framework: experimental assessment
To transition from a theoretical thesis to an evidence-backed framework, we propose specific experimental pathways to
validate the interpretive matrix. We recommend pre-registered longitudinal studies utilizing multi-method batteries to capture
the transitions between stages.

3.6.1 Candidate tasks and neural markers
For Stage 1 (Biological Discrimination), candidate tasks include subitization slope analyses and Weber fraction
assessments to index the precision of the Approximate Number System (ANS). Stage 2 (Iterative Praxia) requires action-based
numerosity tasks (e.g., self-generated taps) and precise finger gnosis evaluations (localization and differentiation). Stage 3
(Reflexivity) can be indexed by cross-modal matching tasks and visuospatial working memory batteries. Stage 4 and 5
(Communication and Conceptualization) should be evaluated through symbol mapping tasks, Give-N paradigms, and
standardized arithmetic benchmarks.
Neurophysiologically, future studies must situate neural claims within established parietal distinctions. We hypothesize
differential involvement of the Intraparietal Sulcus (IPS) for quantity representation versus the angular gyrus for overlearned
arithmetic facts. High-density, cross-subject neurophysiology (e.g., EEG or fMRI) is required to link task-specific dynamics
(e.g., frontal control during reflexivity versus parietal number coding during conceptualization) to the proposed stages.

3.6.2 Predicted longitudinal relations
We predict specific longitudinal relations, such as cross-lagged effects of early finger gnosis on later exact calculation,
even when controlling for socioeconomic status (SES), language ability, and domain-general executive functions. Furthermore,
mixed-effects modeling and mediation analyses are necessary to test whether sensorimotor numerosity mediates the relationship
between general motor skills and formal arithmetic achievement.

4. Conclusions and Future Perspectives
The present integrative review argues that the dichotomy between the formal validity of mathematics and its
psychological genesis can be overcome through an interdisciplinary approach. Contemporary cognitive psychiatry and
neuroscience provide substantial evidence that numerical processing is a developmental achievement grounded in sensorimotor
systems, particularly in corporeal representation and finger gnosis.
Formal logic and pure mathematics represent the final and refined stage of a lengthy process of embodiment, abstraction,
and communication of regularities in the physical world. Understanding the "physiology" of this process does not diminish the
importance of the logical "anatomy" described by authors such as Frege, but complements it, offering a holistic vision of human
cognition.

This review is subject to important limitations. As a narrative and integrative synthesis rather than a systematic or metaanalytic review, its source selection is not exhaustive and may be subject to selection bias. The proposed six-stage matrix remains
an unvalidated theoretical hypothesis rather than an empirically confirmed developmental sequence, and key supporting findings
— particularly the sensorimotor-arithmetic correlations reported by Anobile et al. (2026) — still require independent replication
across diverse populations, ages, and tasks before they can be considered robust.
For future research, we recommend rigorous empirical investigation of the transitions between the stages proposed in
our interpretive matrix. Longitudinal studies combining assessments of fine motor skills, functional neuroimaging, and
mathematical performance in clinical and neurotypical populations will be fundamental to validate and refine this model.
Additionally, application of this framework in developing therapeutic interventions for acalculia and optimizing elementary
mathematics curricula represents a promising field for integration of basic science and clinical practice.
