Abstract: When molecules are placed between closely spaced mirrors, they interact strongly with
the photons that are trapped between them, generating new quantum states which are no longer
exclusively material nor photonic alone, but rather, coherent superpositions of both. These hybrid
states are known as molecular polaritons, given that they arise from the strong interaction between
the electric field of light and the electrical polarization of the molecules. Recently, experimental
advances in nano‐ and microfabrication of molecular polariton architectures have successfully
demonstrated their feasibility to control the rate and outcome of a certain class of chemical
reactions in condensed phases. Importantly, these reactions proceed in strongly dissipative
environments such as liquid solvents and lossy mirrors that allow for photons to escape from their
confinement. The purpose of this research is to formulate quantum mechanical theories and
computational tools that can elucidate the origin of these intriguing phenomena and simultaneously
predict capabilities that this new generation of molecular materials affords. Attention is placed on
harnessing polaritons to carry out photophysics and photochemistry that challenge currently
existing paradigms, such as the optimization of energy conversion processes in organic solar cell
or light‐emitting devices, or unconventional phenomena such as long‐range excitation energy
transfer, remote control of chemical reactions, and a new quantum mechanical regime of chemical
reactivity due to wavefunction overlaps amongst a large number of molecular polaritons (Bose
condensation). This research explores a frontier of Chemistry and Physics where electrons,
vibrations, and photons interact strongly with each other to generate emergent behavior that can
be creatively exploited to address contemporary challenges in Basic Energy Sciences.

Introduction

known as molecular polaritons, given that they arise from the strong interaction between the

be creatively exploited to address contemporary challenges in Basic Energy Sciences.
Objectives of the Proposal

 1. Develop a computational methodology to study the quantum dynamics and linear optical
 spectroscopy of MP systems in the presence of static and dynamic disorder.
 2. Explore a class of emergent photophysical processes afforded by MPs, namely singlet-
 fission (SF) and photoluminescence (PL) recovery.
 3. Explore the possibility of remote control of energy transfer and chemical reactions
 mediated by confined electromagnetic modes.
 4. Harness polariton condensation to obtain highly selective chemical reactions.
Successfully Meeting the Objective of the Project: We (1) developed new understanding of the
delocalization properties of molecular eigenstates in a cavity, showed a completely new
phenomenon in the linear transmission of light through dielectric media that showcases quantum
fluctuations of a confined cavity mode, and explored new nanophotonic setups where to carry out
polariton architectures; (2) developed the theory of vibrational polariton mediated nonradiative
relaxation, and together with experimentalists, showed polariton modified vibrational dynamics in
𝐹𝑒(𝐶𝑂)5 solutions at room temperature, and demonstrated enhanced photoluminescence from
triplet-triplet annihilation processes in a cavity; (3) developed the notion of remote control of
chemistry whereby catalyst and substrate are connected via a cavity photon, explored an

Summary

A thorough theoretical and computational investigation of polariton mediated photophysics and
photochemistry of molecular polaritons was conducted in strong connection with state-of-the-art
experiments in the field. Our research has shown that a careful understanding of all the dissipative
and coherent processes involved in these hybrid light-matter systems is crucial to elucidate their
potential for applications in various frontiers of energy sciences, including catalysis and
optoelectronic engineering. We have unambiguously shown that polaritons can mediate long-range
interactions that are not present in the bare molecular materials, affect intramolecular processes,
and even drive new chemistry with unusual concepts such as vibrational condensates. Importantly,
we have identified conditions under which classical optics fails to explain polariton phenomena
and which necessitate the explicit quantum mechanical treatment of the electromagnetic modes;
this finding opens doors to the study of interesting quantum fluctuations in situations that were
previously thought to be fully captured by classical electromagnetism.
