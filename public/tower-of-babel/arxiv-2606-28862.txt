HKVLM: Faithful Reasoning Grounding by Binding Language Queries to a Frozen Detector
Bo Ma

Abstract
Many visual requests—“the object to open this bottle”, “the person not wearing a helmet”—require reasoning, not just category matching. Pure open-vocabulary detectors need an explicit phrase; vision-language models (VLMs) can reason yet “see but mis-speak”, attending to the right region but returning the wrong box or label. We argue this is a binding failure: in coordinate-as-text VLMs localization passes through the autoregressive head, coupling it to language generation; in two-stage pipelines the model’s intent is squeezed through a single class string. We present HKVLM, which removes localization from the language path. A frozen, language-aligned detector emits class-agnostic region proposals; a frozen language model encodes reasoning instructions as referential query embeddings; a lightweight alignment hook binds queries to regions by contrastive retrieval and bipartite assignment in a shared embedding space. A perception-grounded faithfulness veto forbids naming an object that no region supports. Only the hook is trained, targeting small-data cold-start settings where monolithic VLM tuning struggles. We formalize a say-vs-see decomposition separating localization error (SeeErr) from binding error (SayErr), and evaluate on RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg and POPE. With frozen Grounding DINO and Qwen2.5-VL, training only the hook lifts grounding accuracy by 50 50 – 90 × 90\times over untrained cross-space matching; the faithfulness veto raises POPE accuracy from near-chance ( 0.50 0.50 ) to 0.66 0.66 – 0.76 0.76 and reduces hallucination from ∼ 0.99 {\sim}0.99 to 0.23 0.23 – 0.43 0.43 , with gains from 200 200 expressions. Increasing proposals from M = 50 M{=}50 to M = 300 M{=}300 improves grounding by 19 19 – 24 % 24\% without retraining, confirming that residual error is perceptual (SeeErr) rather than binding (SayErr).

Introduction

Practitioners who adapt small open-source VLMs for object detection report a
stubborn pattern: out of the box the models localize poorly, and modest
fine-tuning—for example low-rank adaptation on a thousand images—barely
moves the needle. The models clearly attend to the right pixels yet still
return wrong labels or imprecise boxes. We call this the
see-but-mis-speak

gap.

The gap matters most for
reasoning grounding
: requests phrased as intent
or world knowledge rather than a category name—“something to cut paper with”,
“the unoccupied chair”, “the person not wearing a helmet”—where a pure
detector has no phrase to match and the system must both reason and localize.
This is the regime we target. Closed-set detection, where specialized detectors
already excel and language reasoning adds little, is not our battleground.

A common explanation blames the visual encoder and proposes a stronger one.
Encoder upgrades help only marginally in practice, which points elsewhere. In
a VLM the encoder output is consumed by the language model exactly as text is:
keys and values are read according to a query, and the quality of that query
is not guaranteed. When the box itself is produced by the autoregressive
decoder—as a sequence of coordinate tokens
(
20
;
4
;
32
)
or
quantized coordinate symbols
(
2
;
10
)
—the geometry of the
object is forced through the same brittle channel that produces words. A single
bad query token can override a correct percept. The localization signal and the
language signal are entangled.

We take the position that faithful grounding requires
three
separable
competencies:
localization competence
(place a tight box on an
object),
referential faithfulness
(name only what is actually there),
and
binding
(connect the user’s intent to the correct region).
Coordinate-as-text designs entangle all three inside one autoregressive head.
Two-stage reasoning pipelines
(
21
;
12
)
separate localization but
re-couple through a lossy text interface: the model’s intent is compressed into
a class string handed to an external detector, discarding instance-specific and
relational cues. Recent decoupled designs recast box regression as retrieval
over proposals
(
9
;
17
)
, a real step forward, but selection still
happens inside the autoregressive language model via index tokens and there is
no explicit objective that ties language semantics to region geometry or that
penalizes ungrounded assertions.

We propose HKVLM, which keeps localization entirely outside the language path.
A frozen, language-aligned open-vocabulary detector

(
16
;
13
)
produces class-agnostic, high-recall region proposals
together with vision-language-fused region embeddings. The language model reads
the image and the reasoning instruction and emits one or more
referential
query embeddings
that carry its world knowledge. A small trained module—the
alignment hook
—projects
queries and regions into a shared space and binds them by contrastive retrieval
and bipartite assignment, reusing the set-prediction machinery of DETR

(
3
)
. Categories and answers are produced in language, but every box
comes from the detector and every emitted category must be justified by an
aligned region whose embedding agrees. This last requirement is a

perception-grounded faithfulness
constraint, and it is motivated by
evidence that grounding alone does not automatically reduce hallucination

(
6
)
.

Freezing the heavy components has a practical payoff. Only the hook and a thin
projection are trained, so the cold-start regime that defeats parameter-efficient
tuning of a monolithic VLM becomes tractable: the localization stack already
works, and learning is confined to the binding.

Our contributions are:

1.

A diagnostic framing that separates localization competence, referential
faithfulness, and binding, and a
say-vs-see
decomposition that measures
localization error and naming-given-region error independently
(Section
3
).

2.

HKVLM, a grounding framework in which a frozen detector owns localization
and an LLM’s referential queries are bound to region embeddings by shared-space
alignment instead of autoregressive coordinate or index tokens
(Section
4
).

3.

A perception-grounded faithfulness objective that suppresses categories no
region supports, and a frozen-stack training recipe targeted at small-data,
cold-start adaptation (Sections
4
).

4.

An evaluation protocol—datasets, baselines, metrics, ablations, and
hypotheses—centered on reasoning and referring grounding
(RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg) and object hallucination
(Section
5
), together with
real
image-level results
(Section
6
) that validate the binding and faithfulness claims at
matched perception with a frozen Grounding DINO and a frozen Qwen2.5-VL.

We are explicit about scope. Section
6
reports real results on
RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg and POPE that confirm the three hypotheses at matched
perception: the trained hook binds
50
–
90\times
better than untrained
cross-space matching, the faithfulness veto converts grounding into hallucination
resistance, and the hook learns from a few hundred expressions. These are
mechanism and ablation claims at a fixed, frozen detector, not a state-of-the-art
absolute-localization claim: absolute [email protected] is capped by proposal recall under
a generic detection prompt. Section
7
adds a controlled embedding-space
study that isolates the binding step further (perception assumed perfect).

3
Problem Formulation

Given an image
I
and a free-form request
q
(a category name, a referring
expression, or an instruction), the system must return a set of
category–box pairs
\{(c_{i},b_{i})\}
that are both
accurate
(tight,
correctly labeled) and
faithful
(no
c_{i}
without supporting evidence in

I
).

We separate three competencies.
Localization competence
is the ability
of a perception module to produce a region set
\mathcal{R}=\{r_{1},\dots,r_{M}\}
,
with boxes
\{b_{j}\}
and embeddings
\{e_{j}\}
, that covers the true objects; we
measure it by proposal recall (the fraction of true objects covered by the top
M
proposals).
Binding
is the map
from the request
q
to the correct subset of
\mathcal{R}
.
Referential
faithfulness
is the property that every emitted category is supported by a bound
region.

Say-vs-see decomposition.

A grounding error can arise because the right region was never proposed
(
see
error) or because the right region was available but mis-bound or
mis-named (
say
error). Let
\mathcal{R}^{\star}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
be the
proposals that overlap a ground-truth object above an IoU threshold. We define

\displaystyle\mathrm{SeeErr}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{|\{g:\exists\,r\in\mathcal{R},\,\mathrm{IoU}(r,g)\geq\tau\}|}{|\mathcal{G}|},

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{SayErr}

\displaystyle=\Pr\big[\hat{c}\neq c^{\star}\,\big|\,\text{correct region}\in\mathcal{R}^{\star}\big],

(2)

where
\mathcal{G}
is the set of ground-truth objects,
\tau
an IoU threshold,

c^{\star}
the true label and
\hat{c}
the predicted one.
\mathrm{SeeErr}

isolates perception;
\mathrm{SayErr}
isolates language-binding given that
perception succeeded. Coordinate-as-text VLMs conflate the two; HKVLM is designed
to drive
\mathrm{SayErr}
down without inflating
\mathrm{SeeErr}
, because
perception is frozen and strong.

Method: HKVLM

HKVLM has four parts: a frozen perception module, a language module that emits
referential queries, the trained alignment hook, and a faithfulness objective.

4.1
Frozen perception module

The perception module is a frozen, pre-trained
language-aligned

open-vocabulary detector. We use Grounding DINO
(
16
)
(GLIP

(
13
)
is an alternative) and take its class-agnostic, high-recall set of

M
proposals
\mathcal{R}=\{(b_{j},e_{j})\}_{j=1}^{M}
, where
b_{j}
is a box and

e_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the detector’s vision-language-fused region feature for
that proposal. The choice is deliberate: because these region features already
live in a space that has been aligned to text during grounded pre-training,
binding them to the language model’s query embeddings is a short bridge rather
than a cross-modal leap. A purely self-supervised backbone (DINOv2/DINOv3

(
19
;
27
)
), whose features carry no language semantics, is evaluated
only as an ablation and is expected to make binding harder. The module is frozen,
so it carries all localization competence and box quality never depends on
language decoding; operating class-agnostically maximizes recall and decouples
“what is here” from “which one is meant.”

4.2
Referential query embeddings

The language model receives
I
and
q
and produces
K
referential query
embeddings
\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
, one per entity the request implies. We read these
from dedicated query slots appended to the prompt, so the model’s reasoning and
world knowledge shape
u_{k}
while the language model itself can remain frozen or
be adapted with low-rank updates. Crucially
u_{k}
is a continuous vector, not a
coordinate or an index token; it preserves more of the intent than a class
string.

4.3
Alignment hook: binding by retrieval and assignment

Two light projections map regions and queries into a shared space,

\tilde{e}_{j}=f_{\theta}(e_{j})
and
\tilde{u}_{k}=g_{\phi}(u_{k})
. The affinity
between query
k
and region
j
is
s_{kj}=\langle\tilde{u}_{k},\tilde{e}_{j}\rangle/\kappa
with temperature
\kappa
. Binding combines two
objectives.

Contrastive retrieval
pulls each query toward its matched region(s) and
pushes it from the rest:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ret}}=-\sum_{k}\log\frac{\sum_{j\in P(k)}\exp(s_{kj})}{\sum_{j=1}^{M}\exp(s_{kj})},

(3)

where
P(k)
is the set of positive regions for query
k
.

Bipartite assignment
resolves which proposal each query claims, reusing
the Hungarian matching of set prediction
(
3
)
. We find the assignment

\sigma
minimizing
\sum_{k}\mathcal{C}(u_{k},r_{\sigma(k)})
with a cost that
combines the negative affinity
-s_{k\sigma(k)}
and a box term against the
ground-truth box; the matched pairs supervise
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ret}}
and a
light box-refinement residual on
b_{\sigma(k)}
. Only
\theta,\phi
(and
optionally LoRA in the language model) are learned.

4.4
Perception-grounded faithfulness

A query may correspond to no object. We attach a verification head
v(\cdot)

that, for the best-matched region of each query, predicts whether the bound pair
is genuinely supported, supervised by whether a true object exists:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{faith}}=\sum_{k}\mathrm{BCE}\big(v(\tilde{u}_{k},\tilde{e}_{\sigma(k)}),\,y_{k}\big),

(4)

with
y_{k}=1
when query
k
has a real referent. At inference, a category is
emitted only when its bound region passes the verifier and the affinity exceeds
a threshold; otherwise the model abstains. This is the perceptual veto: the
language model cannot assert an object that perception does not support, directly
targeting object hallucination. The full objective is

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ret}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{b}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{box}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{f}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{faith}}
.

4.5
Cold-start training

Because perception and (optionally) the language model are frozen, the trainable
footprint is the two projections, the box residual, and the verifier—a small
fraction of a monolithic VLM. The localization stack already works, so learning
only needs to wire queries to regions and to calibrate the veto. This is the
regime in which adapting a monolithic VLM by low-rank tuning on a thousand
images tends to fail, and it is where we expect the frozen-stack design to pay
off; the data-fraction study in Section
5
is built to test this.

Experimental Design and Evaluation Protocol

We specify the study that tests HKVLM’s claims. The protocol below defines
tasks, baselines, metrics, ablations, and hypotheses; Section
6

reports the subset we have run on real images at matched perception.

Tasks and datasets.

The primary task is reasoning and referring grounding: return the box for a
free-form, often knowledge-dependent request. We use RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg

(
31
)
over COCO images
(
15
)
, with RefCOCOg emphasized for its
longer, more compositional expressions, and a reasoning-grounding setting with
implicit queries (paraphrased into intent/world-knowledge form). Faithfulness is measured on POPE
(
14
)

(and CHAIR
(
25
)
for generated descriptions). Open-vocabulary
generalization to unseen categories (e.g., long-tail classes
(
7
)
) is a
secondary, zero-shot check rather than the main result.

Baselines.

The closest competitor is the class-string reasoning pipeline DetGPT

(
21
)
(LLM names a phrase, an open-vocabulary detector localizes);
coordinate-as-text VLMs on referring
(
4
;
2
;
32
)
; the
decoupled-retrieval model ChatRex
(
9
)
; and frozen-LMM grounding

(
29
)
. For fairness, HKVLM, DetGPT, and ChatRex are paired with the

same
frozen Grounding DINO so differences reflect the binding interface,
not the detector.

Metrics.

Grounding accuracy (the top-ranked region is counted correct when its overlap
with the true referent reaches IoU
0.5
) on RefCOCO/+/g and the reasoning split; POPE accuracy/F1 under random, popular, and adversarial
sampling; CHAIR
s
/CHAIR
i
; our
\mathrm{SeeErr}
and
\mathrm{SayErr}
;
proposal recall; and efficiency (trainable parameters, latency).

Ablations.

(i) Replace the alignment hook with an AR-index variant (to isolate shared-space
alignment vs. index retrieval) and with a class-string variant (to isolate the
continuous query, i.e., a DetGPT-style interface). (ii) Remove

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{faith}}
and the veto (to measure faithfulness on
POPE-adversarial and SayErr). (iii) Swap the frozen perception from the
language-aligned detector
(
16
)
to a self-supervised backbone

(
27
)
(to confirm the language-alignment design choice). (iv) Freeze the
language model vs. LoRA. (v) Vary

(recall vs. precision).

Hypotheses.

H1: on reasoning/referring grounding, HKVLM attains higher grounding accuracy and lower

\mathrm{SayErr}
than the class-string pipeline and coordinate-as-text VLMs at
matched perception. H2: the faithfulness veto improves POPE (especially
adversarial) with negligible recall loss. H3: training only the hook reaches
strong grounding from a few hundred to a few thousand expressions, beating
low-rank tuning of a monolithic VLM at equal data.

Data-efficiency study.

Train at increasing numbers of referring expressions (e.g.,
0.5
k,
1
k,
5
k,
full) and compare HKVLM (hook only) against low-rank tuning of a monolithic VLM at
equal budget, reporting grounding accuracy versus data size.

Reproducibility.

All perception and language components are public; the trainable hook is small
and fully specified. We will release training and evaluation code and configs,
and complete the AAAI Reproducibility Checklist; seeds and component versions
will be fixed and reported.

To complement the real-image results of Section

simulation that removes pixel perception from the picture. Each scene provides

images of the same instance latent under two different linear maps, reproducing
the modality gap the alignment hook must bridge. Because the correct region is
always present for positive queries,
\mathrm{SeeErr}=0
by construction and every

8
Discussion, Limitations, and Broader Impact

The clearest limitation is visible directly in our numbers: grounding accuracy is
upper-bounded by frozen-proposal recall, so the absolute accuracy of
{\sim}0.26

(Table
2
,
M{=}300
) reflects residual
\mathrm{SeeErr}
under
a generic detection prompt—HKVLM cannot bind to an object the detector never
proposed. Increasing
M
from
50
to
300
improves grounding by
19
–
24\%

without any hook retraining, confirming
\mathrm{SeeErr}
as the dominant
remaining error; recall-oriented, expression-conditioned proposing is the natural
next step. We also observe that a larger proposal pool raises the false-positive
rate of the veto (hallucination increases from
{\leq}0.14
at
M{=}50
to

0.23
–
0.43
at
M{=}300
at the same threshold), indicating that the
faithfulness threshold
\gamma_{v}
should be jointly optimized with
M
in future
work. Our evidence is further confined to two ablation references sharing the frozen
perception; head-to-head comparison with external systems (DetGPT, ChatRex,
coordinate-as-text VLMs) and low-rank VLM tuning at equal data budget are
reserved for future work. The design also assumes region embeddings carry enough semantic signal
for shared-space binding; for very fine-grained or relational requests a single
query embedding may be insufficient, suggesting multi-query or compositional
extensions. Masks are a natural extension by attaching a promptable segmenter

(
11
)
to bound boxes. Finally, faithful open-vocabulary detection can be
misused for surveillance; the same veto that suppresses hallucination should be
paired with deployment controls and dataset governance.

9
Conclusion

We reframed the see-but-mis-speak problem of grounding VLMs as a binding
failure and proposed HKVLM, which gives localization to a frozen detector and
re-binds it to language by shared-space alignment with a perception-grounded
faithfulness veto, rather than by autoregressive coordinate or index tokens.
On RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg and POPE, at matched frozen perception, training
only the hook lifts grounding accuracy by
50
–
90\times
over untrained
cross-space matching, the faithfulness veto raises POPE from near-chance to

0.66
–
0.76
while substantially reducing object hallucination, and the hook
reaches useful accuracy from
200
training expressions. Increasing the proposal
budget from
M{=}50
to
M{=}300
improves grounding by
19
–
24\%
without
hook retraining, pinpointing residual
\mathrm{SeeErr}
as the dominant remaining
error and validating the say-vs-see decomposition as a diagnostic tool.
