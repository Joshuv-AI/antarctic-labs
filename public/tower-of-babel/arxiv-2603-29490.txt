Flatness-based control of a Timoshenko beam
Simon Schmidt, Nicole Gehring, Abdurrahman Irscheid

Abstract
The paper presents an approach to flatness-based control design for hyperbolic multi-input systems, building upon the hyperbolic controller form (HCF). The transformation into HCF yields a simplified system representation that considerably facilitates the design of state feedback controllers for trajectory tracking. The proposed concept is demonstrated for a Timoshenko beam and validated through numerical simulations, demonstrating trajectory tracking and closed-loop stability.



I
Introduction

The flatness-based control of nonlinear ordinary differential equations (ODEs) is based on the parametrization of all system variables by a flat output
[
5
]
.
The parametrization of the system’s input allows for the derivation of a system description in controller canonical form or Brunovsky normal form, state representations that significantly simplify the design of feedforward and state feedback (tracking) controllers.
This approach also extends to infinite-dimensional systems.
For hyperbolic single-input (SI) systems with dynamic boundary conditions, a flatness-based control concept is introduced in
[
19
]
.
Its central element is the transformation of the system into hyperbolic controller form (HCF), a special state representation in which the system dynamics are represented by a single boundary-actuated transport equation
[
14
]
, analogous to the chain of integrators in the finite-dimensional case.
In HCF, the control design becomes straightforward, and the resulting control law can be readily expressed as feedback of the original system state through the corresponding, invertible state transformation.

Generalizing the flatness-based control design from SI to multi-input (MI) systems poses additional challenges, similar to the ODE case.
In the latter, the controller form is no longer unique
[
7
]
as it may be represented by integrator chains of different length.
Moreover, it may involve time derivatives of the input, rendering it a generalized state representation
[
13
]
.
In this case, a purely static state feedback is generally insufficient for trajectory tracking.
Similar considerations arise for hyperbolic MI systems, as discussed in
[
16
]
for boundary-actuated systems without in-domain coupling.

This paper extends the flatness-based control design from

16
]
to hyperbolic MI systems with additional in-domain coupling.
The proposed concept relies on the transformation into HCF, a system representation that is advantageous for feedback design (see Figure

This is somewhat similar to the well-known

[

The main challenge of the HCF-based method lies in identifying a suitable candidate for the HCF state.
As the flat output of systems with multiple inputs is not unique, the same applies to the HCF state
[
16
]

Moreover, the HCF is no longer a state representation in the classical sense, in general, as it involves input predictions.
Therefore, a non-static feedback of the HCF state is derived for trajectory tracking.
It is shown that the transformation into HCF is invertible, allowing to express the control law in terms of the original system state.

The flatness-based control concept is illustrated for a Timoshenko beam
[

In the studied setup, the beam is clamped at one end and actuated at the opposite end by a boundary force and torque serving as the control inputs (see Figure

This is a prototypical hyperbolic MI system, for which numerous control strategies exist in the literature, including direct boundary assignment
[

]

]

III
Flatness-based parametrization

To derive a flatness-based parametrization for the Timoshenko beam, essentially, its solution is required.
To simplify this task, a series of transformations is applied in order to map the system into a form that is advantageous for solving the coupled PDEs (
2

IV
Hyperbolic Controller Form (HCF)

To obtain the HCF for the Timoshenko beam based on the input parametrization (
17
), we define the HCF state

\displaystyle\eta_{1}(\tau,t)

\displaystyle=y_{1}(t+\tau),\quad\tau\in[0,2\tau_{1}]

(18a)

\displaystyle\eta_{2}(\tau,t)

\displaystyle=y_{2}(t+\tau),\quad\tau\in[0,2\tau_{2}]

(18b)

in view of (
16
) and (
17
).
Then, the components
\eta_{i}(\tau,t)

, satisfy homogeneous
transport equations
\partial_{t}\eta_{i}(\tau,t)=\partial_{\tau}\eta_{i}(\tau,t)
with normalized, negative transport velocities on domains
\tau\in[0,2\tau_{i})

The respective outputs constitute the flat output components, i.e.,
\eta_{i}(0,t)=y_{i}(t)

The BCs of the HCF are obtained from the input parametrization (
17

can be replaced by the HCF state in (

However, for values

with prediction amplitudes

Instead, applying a forward time shift to (
17a

Hence, using the substitution

\displaystyle y_{1}(t+\tau)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\eta_{1}(\tau,t),&\tau\in[0,2\tau_{1}]\\
-\bar{u}_{1}(t+\tau-2\tau_{1}),&\tau>2\tau_{1}\end{cases}

(19a)

\displaystyle y_{2}(t+\tau)

\displaystyle=\eta_{2}(\tau,t),\qquad\qquad\qquad\tau\in[0,2\tau_{2}]

(19b)

in (
17

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta_{1}(\tau,t)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\tau}\eta_{1}(\tau,t),\quad\quad\tau\in[0,2\tau_{1})

(20a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta_{2}(\tau,t)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\tau}\eta_{2}(\tau,t),\quad\quad\tau\in[0,2\tau_{2})

(20b)

\displaystyle\eta_{1}(2\tau_{1},t)

\displaystyle=-\bar{u}_{1}(t)

(20c)

\displaystyle\eta_{2}(2\tau_{2},t)

\displaystyle=-\bar{u}_{2}(t)+\textstyle\int_{\tau_{1}}^{2\tau_{1}}\bar{a}_{0}^{-}(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}-\tau)\eta_{1}(\tau,t)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\textstyle\int_{0}^{\Delta\tau+\tau_{2}}\tilde{a}_{0}(\tau)\bar{u}_{1}(t+\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

To design a tracking controller that stabilizes the transition of the Timoshenko beam, we assume given reference trajectories

p_{i}(t),&t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{T}\\

\boldsymbol{y}(t)

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{2}(t)

To this end, we define the tracking errors

VII
Concluding remarks

This paper presents a flatness-based control design approach for hyperbolic systems that employs the HCF, a special state representation that greatly simplifies the design of stabilizing feedback laws.
The concept is demonstrated for the classical Timoshenko beam, but can be extended to general hyperbolic systems.
Since the HCF derived here constitutes a generalized state representation, a particular point of future research is whether a classical state representation free of input predictions can be obtained through an alternative choice of the HCF state.
