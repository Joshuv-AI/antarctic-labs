A nonconforming method for a generalized Darcy–Forchheimer model
Michele Botti, Lorenzo Mascotto, Marialetizia Mosconi

Abstract
We analyze a dual mixed nonconforming discretization of a generalized Darcy–Forchheimer model. Compared to the analogous scheme proposed in [ 23 ] , we consider general, i.e., nonquadratic, Forchheimer nonlinearities; we admit mixed, inhomogeneous boundary conditions; we allow for more general, i.e., with lower Lebesgue regularity, permeability tensors; we construct general-order schemes; we prove convergence to the exact solution under low regularity assumptions, based on novel Sobolev-trace inequalities for broken spaces; we derive error estimates of general-order assuming extra regularity of the exact solution and data; we present numerical results assessing the performance of the proposed schemes for different types of nonlinearity and nonlinear solvers. AMS subject classification : 76S05; 65N12; 65N30. Keywords : generalized Darcy–Forchheimer law; Sobolev-trace inequalities; Crouzeix-Raviart elements; high-order schemes.

Introduction

State-of-the-art.

The Darcy–Forchheimer’s law models the fluid flow
in porous media
[
21
]
.
It is a nonlinear extension of Darcy’s law
and provides a more accurate description
of high-velocity flow behaviours.
Numerical discretizations of
Darcy–Forchheimer equations pose extra theoretical and computational
challenges compared to their linear counterpart;
as such, they have received increasing attention
over the last three decades.
In
[
19
]
, existence and uniqueness
are established for a semi-discrete formulation
of the standard (quadratic) Darcy–Forchheimer model.
Well-posedness of the fully discrete scheme and corresponding error estimates
are provided in
[
26
]
; see also
[
28
]
;
more general nonlinearities are considered in
[
24
]
.
In these works, the problem is formulated in the standard mixed setting,
and Raviart-Thomas/piecewise-polynomial pairs are employed.
A different approach is undertaken in
[
23
]
,
where a lowest order mixed dual formulation
based on piecewise-constant/lowest order Crouzeix-Raviart (CR in what follows)
pairs is introduced:
convergence to the exact solution
of discrete solution sequences
under mesh refinements is proved
and the analysis of a nonlinear solver is also provided.
Numerical results for this scheme
are presented in
[
25
]
;
see also
[
30
]

for results concerning the standard mixed formulation.
We also mention
[
35
]
, where, compared to
[
23
]
,
additional stabilization in the normal direction for the fluxes are considered,
following ideas from
[
14
]
for the linear case.
More recent contributions include

[
36
,
29
,
2
,
17
,
16
,
34
,
31
,
37
,
4
,
3
,

]
.

Goals of the paper.

The main objective of this work is to design a general-order
dual mixed formulation for a generalized Darcy–Forchheimer problem.
The starting point is the work
[
23
]

of Girault and Wheeler,
which we extend along several directions.

•

We consider more general nonlinearities, replacing
the quadratic Darcy–Forchheimer nonlinearity with an
(\alpha-2)
-type law,
which boils down to the classical one when
\alpha=3
;
see problem (
7
) below.

•

We admit inhomogeneous, mixed boundary conditions.

•

We allow for permeability tensors
\underline{{\mathbf{K}}}

with lower Lebesgue regularity; cf. (
9
) below.

•

We introduce a general-order scheme
employing piecewise-polynomial spaces of order
p-1
for the fluxes
and Crouzeix-Raviart spaces of order
p
for the potentials.

•

We prove convergence under low-regularity assumptions
on the solution and data,
based on novel Sobolev-trace inequalities in broken spaces,
with constants independent of the type of discretization spaces
(in particular, independent of the polynomial degree in our case).

•

We derive general-order error estimates
under additional regularity assumptions on the solution and data.

•

We present numerical experiments assessing the performance of the scheme for different values of the nonlinearity parameter
\alpha

and for different nonlinear solvers.

The use of high-order schemes for nonlinear problems
may appear counterintuitive, as solutions
can exhibit singular behavior even for regular data and smooth domains;
our motivation is the prospective development of
hp
-adaptive strategies,
which are particularly well suited for
the efficient discretization of such problems.
As in
[
23
]
, we adopt a dual mixed formulation
and discretize the potential with nonconforming
(Crouzeix-Raviart) elements.
Using nonconforming methods in the discretization of more complex
nonlinear problems is important, as standard conforming discretization
might not converge to the exact solution; cf.
[
5
]
.

(3)

We have the following trace theorem

The trace operator is surjective

\W^{1,p}(X)\cap\L^{p}_{0}(\Omega).\end{cases}

Inequality (

The model problem

Given

,

,

\begin{cases}\nablabold\pop+\frac{\mu}{\rho}\underline{\mathbf{K}}^{-1}\mathbf{u}+\frac{\beta}{\rho}{\left|{\mathbf{u}}\right|}^{{\alpha-2}}\mathbf{u}=0&\text{ in }\Omega\\

If

,

With the notation as in (

8
Conclusions

In this work, we presented and analyzed a general-order
dual mixed nonconforming discretization
for a generalized Darcy–Forchheimer problem.
Our approach extends the framework in
[
23
]
,
along different directions:

•

(
\alpha
-2)-type Forchheimer nonlinearities
are allowed,
moving beyond the standard quadratic model;

•

the proposed method accommodates mixed,
inhomogeneous boundary conditions,
and permits permeability tensors with lower Lebesgue regularity;

•

we developed general-order schemes
based on piecewise-polynomial spaces of order
k-1
for the fluxes
paired with Crouzeix-Raviart elements of order
k
for the potentials;

•

based on novel Sobolev-trace inequalities
for broken spaces with constants
independent of the polynomial degree,
we established convergence to the exact solution
under low-regularity assumptions;

•

we further derived general-order error estimates
assuming additional regularity of the exact solution and data.

The theoretical findings were supported by numerical experiments
that assessed the performance of two iterative schemes
for different nonlinearity parameters
\alpha
,
orders
k
, and parameters of the model.
While a standard fixed point scheme does not converge
for large nonlinearity parameters
\alpha
,
a relaxed version may reach convergence
by suitably tuning the relaxation parameter.

The framework introduced in this work
lays the essential mathematical groundwork for our future objective:
the prospective development of
hp
-adaptive strategies
to efficiently discretize complex generalized Darcy–Forchheimer models.

Properties of the nonlinear operator

This section is devoted with showing three properties of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\cdot)
:

1.

A.1
;

;

.

A.1
Monotonicity

Here,
we show that
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\cdot)
is monotone.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\mathbf{y}
be in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}
.
Then,
recalling that
\lambda_{\text{min}}
is the smallest eigenvalue of
\underline{{\mathbf{K}}}^{-1}
,
the following estimate holds true:

\frac{\mu}{\rho}\lambda_{\text{min}}{\left\|{\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbf{w}+\mathbf{y})-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{y}))\cdot(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{v})\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}\qquad\qquad\forall\ \mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}.

Proof.

We define
\mathcal{J}:\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
as

\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{v}):=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mu}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}(\underline{\mathbf{K}}^{-1})\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{\beta}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{v}|^{\alpha}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}\qquad\qquad\forall\ \mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega).

The first Gateaux derivative
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\mathbf{v}):\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega)\to\mathbf{L}^{{\alpha^{\prime}}}(\Omega)
reads

\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\mathbf{v}),\mathbf{w}\rangle=\frac{\mu}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}(\underline{\mathbf{K}}^{-1})\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{w}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}+\frac{\beta}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{v}|^{{\alpha-2}}\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{w}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}\qquad\qquad\forall\ \mathbf{v},\,\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega).

(100)

The second Gateaux derivative
\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{w}:\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega)\to\mathbf{L}^{{\alpha^{\prime}}}(\Omega)
reads

\begin{split}\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w}\rangle&=\frac{\mu}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}(\underline{\mathbf{K}}^{-1})\mathbf{z}\cdot\mathbf{w}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\frac{\beta}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}(\alpha-2)|\mathbf{v}|^{\alpha-4}(\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{z})(\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{w})+|\mathbf{v}|^{\alpha-2}\mathbf{z}\cdot\mathbf{w}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}\hskip 9.24994pt\forall\ \mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{0},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}\in\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega).\end{split}

(101)

with

\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{0})\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}\rangle=\frac{\mu}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}(\underline{\mathbf{K}}^{-1})\mathbf{z}\cdot\mathbf{w}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}\qquad\forall\ \mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}\in\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega).

Let
\mathbf{y}
be fixed in
\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
,
and
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{w}
be in
\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
.
Define
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}:=\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{y}

and
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}:=\mathbf{w}+\mathbf{y}
;

\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}-\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}
coincides with
\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{v}
.
Hence,
we use the fundamental theorem of calculus and get

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}))&\cdot(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{v})\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}=\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})\cdot(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}-\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}))\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}\\
&\overset{\eqref{Acal},\eqref{Jcal-prime}}{=}\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}),\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}-\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}+\theta(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}-\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}))(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}-\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}),\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}-\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\rangle\,{\rm{d}}{\theta}.\end{split}

The integral on the right-hand side
is well-defined for all
\alpha>2
since

|\mathbf{v}|^{\alpha-4}(\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{z})(\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{w})\leq|\mathbf{v}|^{\alpha-2}|\mathbf{z}||\mathbf{w}|.

For all
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{w}
in
\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega)

\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}\rangle=\frac{\mu}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}(\underline{\mathbf{K}}^{-1}\mathbf{w})\cdot\mathbf{w}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}+\frac{\beta}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}({\alpha-2})|\mathbf{v}|^{\alpha-4}|\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{w}|^{2}+|\mathbf{v}|^{\alpha-2}|\mathbf{w}|\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}\geq\frac{\mu}{\rho}\lambda_{\text{min}}{\left\|{\mathbf{w}}\right\|}^{2}_{\L^{2}(\Omega)}.

In particular,
we deduce

\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}))\cdot(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{v})\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}\geq\frac{\mu}{\rho}\lambda_{\text{min}}{\left\|{\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}-\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}}\right\|}_{\L^{2}(\Omega)}=\frac{\mu}{\rho}\lambda_{\text{min}}{\left\|{\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{\L^{2}(\Omega)}.

∎

A.2
Coercivity

Here,
we show that
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\cdot)
is coercive.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
\mathbf{y}
be in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}
.
We have

\lim_{{\left\|{\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega)}\to+\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{{\left\|{\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega)}}\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{y})\cdot\mathbf{v}\Big)=+\infty.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{w}
be in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}
;
define
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}:=\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{y}
.
The definition of
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}

yields

\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})\cdot\mathbf{v}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})\cdot\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}-\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})\cdot\mathbf{y}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}\overset{\eqref{Acal},\eqref{Jcal-prime}}{=}\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}),\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\rangle-\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})\cdot\mathbf{y}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}.

(102)

We manipulate the two terms
on the right-hand side separately.
As for the former,
we have

\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}),\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\rangle=\frac{\mu}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}(\underline{\mathbf{K}}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})\cdot\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}+\frac{\beta}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}|\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}|^{\alpha+2}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}\geq\frac{\mu}{\rho}\lambda_{\text{min}}{\left\|{\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}}\right\|}^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{\beta}{\rho}{\left\|{\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}}\right\|}^{\alpha}_{\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega)}.

(103)

As for the latter,
Hölder’s inequality twice
entails

\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})\cdot\mathbf{y}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}\leq\frac{\mu}{\rho}{\left\|{\underline{\mathbf{K}}^{-1}}\right\|}_{\underline{\mathbf{L}}^{\frac{\alpha}{{\alpha-2}}}(\Omega)}{\left\|{\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}}\right\|}_{\L^{\alpha}(\Omega)}{\left\|{\mathbf{y}}\right\|}_{\L^{\alpha}(\Omega)}+\frac{\beta}{\rho}{\left\|{\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}}\right\|}^{{\alpha-1}}_{\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega)}{\left\|{\mathbf{y}}\right\|}_{\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega)}.

(104)

Inserting (
103
) and (
104
)
in (
102

A.3
Hemi-continuity

Here,
we show that the mapping
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\cdot)
is hemi-continuous.

Proposition A.3
.

Given
\mathbf{y}
,
\mathbf{v}
, and
\mathbf{w}
in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}
,
the mapping

t\to\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{v}+t\,\mathbf{w})\cdot\mathbf{w}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}

is continuous from
\mathbb{R}
into
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Given
\mathbf{y}
,
\mathbf{v}
,
and
\mathbf{w}
in
\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
,
and defining
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}:=\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{y}
,
for all
t
and
t_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}
,
the fundamental theorem of calculus gives

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}+t_{0}\mathbf{v})-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}+t\mathbf{v}))\cdot\mathbf{v}\,{\rm{d}}\mathbf{x}&\overset{\eqref{Acal},\eqref{Jcal-prime}}{=}\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}+t_{0}\mathbf{v})-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}+t\mathbf{v}),\mathbf{v}\rangle\\
&=-(t-t_{0})\int_{0}^{1}\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}-t\mathbf{v}-\theta(t-t_{0})\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}\rangle\,{\rm{d}}{\theta}.\end{split}

Hölder’s inequality in (
101
),
the fact that

\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}-t\mathbf{v}-\theta(t-t_{0})\mathbf{v}

belongs to
\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
,
and the fact that

\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{w}:\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}(\Omega)\to\mathbf{L}^{{\alpha^{\prime}}}(\Omega)

yield that the right-hand side of the above display
tends to zero as
t
goes to
t_{0}
.
The assertion follows.
∎
