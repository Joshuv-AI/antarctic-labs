On periodic homotopy and homology equivalences of spaces
Shaul Barkan, Gijs Heuts, Yuqing Shi

Abstract
There are at least two ways to approach the homotopy theory of spaces ‘at chromatic height n n ’: one may localize with respect to T ⁡ ( n ) T(n) -homology or with respect to v n v_{n} -periodic homotopy groups. It was already observed by Bousfield that these two options yield rather different results. We build on his work to prove precise comparison results between the two notions. A crucial concept is a more robust notion of T ⁡ ( n ) T(n) -equivalence that we call ‘parametric T ⁡ ( n ) T(n) -equivalence’: this is a map of spaces that induces an equivalence on ∞ \infty -categories of local systems valued in T ⁡ ( n ) T(n) -local spectra. Our results are sharpest in the case of infinite loop spaces, where amongst other things we prove a T ⁡ ( n ) T(n) -local version of a result of Kuhn on the Morava K K -theory of the Whitehead tower. As a corollary of our results we also produce a formula for the L n f \Loc_{n}^{f} -localization of an infinite loop space Ω ∞ E \operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E of a spectrum satisfying L n − 1 f ​ E ≃ 0 \Loc_{n-1}^{f}E\simeq 0 .

Introduction

The chromatic philosophy is the central organizing principle of stable homotopy theory. One studies the
\infty
-category of spectra by decomposing it into ‘monochromatic’ pieces. This can either mean localizing with respect to the prime fields
K(n)
, the Morava
K
-theories, or localizing with respect to the telescopes
T(n)
, recording the
v_{n}
-periodic homotopy groups of spectra. The two options are closely related, but have recently been shown to differ when
n\geq 2

[
11
]
.

In this paper we are concerned with unstable homotopy theory, i.e., the homotopy theory of spaces. As in the stable case one can decompose the
\infty
-category of spaces into
periodic
or
monochromatic
pieces, but there is potential ambiguity in what one means by this precisely. There are at least the following two possibilities to consider:

(1)

The localization of the
\infty
-category of pointed spaces
\mathscr{S}_{*}
with respect to
v_{n}
-periodic homotopy groups, denoted
\mathscr{S}_{v_{n}}
. We will review the
\infty
-category
\mathscr{S}_{v_{n}}
in Section
3
. This localization can be viewed as a generalization of rational homotopy theory to heights
n>0
. The
\infty
-category
\mathscr{S}_{v_{n}}
admits a description in terms of Lie algebras analogous to Quillen’s results on rational homotopy theory
[
18
]
.

(2)

The localization of the
\infty
-category of spaces
\mathscr{S}
with respect to
T(n)
-homology isomorphisms (or
K(n)
-homology isomorphisms). This is an example of Bousfield localization with respect to a homology theory
[
3
]
.

The first perspective emphasizes homotopy groups, the second focuses on homology. For the
\infty
-category of spectra,
v_{n}
-periodic homotopy and
T(n)
-homology groups are the same thing, so the distinction above does not play a role in stable homotopy theory.
Also, in the case
n=0
the theory of Serre classes shows that for spaces rational homotopy equivalences and rational homology isomorphisms agree, at least in the simply-connected case, so that (1) and (2) are almost identical. However, in the cases
n>0
, the localizations (1) and (2) behave
very
differently for the category of spaces. Let us illustrate this by some examples:

(a)

A
v_{n}
-periodic equivalence of pointed spaces
f\colon X\to Y
need not be a
T(n)
-homology isomorphism. For example, any truncated space
X=\tau_{\leq k}X
has vanishing
v_{n}
-periodic homotopy groups, but need not have vanishing
T(n)
-homology. Indeed, the
T(n)
-homology of Eilenberg–MacLane spaces
K(\mathbb{F}_{p},k)
is nontrivial for
k\leq n
by the calculations of Ravenel–Wilson
[
26
]
.

(b)

There exist more sophisticated examples of
v_{n}
-periodic equivalences that are not
T(n)
-homology isomorphisms and which cannot be avoided by imposing connectivity hypotheses. Indeed, consider the space
X=BU
. Then
X
has vanishing
v_{2}
-periodic homotopy groups, but it does not have vanishing
T(2)
-homology. One can show that any highly connected cover of
BU
will have the same features. (See
1.4
for more on this phenomenon.)

(c)

In the converse direction, a
T(n)
-homology isomorphism need not be a
v_{n}
-periodic equivalence. For example, Langsetmo–Stanley
[
24
]
construct modifications of the Adams map
\alpha\colon S^{k+2(p-1)}/p\to S^{k}/p
that retain the property of it being a
T(1)
-homology isomorphism, but fail to be
v_{1}
-periodic equivalences.

In his extensive work on homotopical and homological localizations, Bousfield gives many results on the relation between the notion of
v_{n}
-periodic equivalences and
T(n)
-homology isomorphisms
[
5
,
6
,
8
]
. These play an important role in recent developments in unstable periodic homotopy theory
[
18
]
and in the study of telescopically localized algebraic
K
-theory
[
23
]
. In this paper we further clarify the relation between localizations (1) and (2) above.

Main results

The starting point of our work is the following: rather than focusing on maps
f
inducing isomorphisms on
T(n)
-homology, it turns out to be more robust to consider maps giving
T(n)
-isomorphisms ‘with local coefficients’, in the following sense. We call a map
f\colon X\to Y
of spaces a
parametric
T(n)
-equivalence
if it induces an equivalence of
\infty
-categories

f^{*}\colon\mathrm{Fun}(Y,\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)})\to\mathrm{Fun}(X,\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)}).

In other words, we want
f
to identify the
\infty
-categories of parametrized
T(n)
-local spectra over
X
and over
Y
, hence the term. A parametric
T(n)
-equivalence is in particular a
T(n)
-homology isomorphism; we will discuss the relation between the two notions in more detail in
Section

2
.

The main thread of this paper will be to compare the three notions of
v_{n}
-periodic equivalence,
T(n)
-equivalence, and parametric
T(n)
-equivalence. Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.3
are general comparison results that are sharpenings of Bousfield’s existing work on the topic. The main novelty here is
1.6
, which gives a rather sharp answer for infinite loop spaces. Its proof involves not only our first set of results, but also a collection of results on
T(n)
-local
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings that might be of independent interest.

Note that when we speak of
T(n)
, we have implicitly already chosen a prime number
p
. To state our results we adopt the following somewhat nonstandard convention: we say a space
X
is
weakly
p
-local
if for any basepoint
x\in X
, the homotopy groups
\pi_{n}(X,x)
are
p
-local abelian groups for
n\geq 2
. (We are
not
assuming anything about
\pi_{0}
and
\pi_{1}
, nor any type of nilpotence condition.) Similarly, we call
X

weakly
p^{\infty}
-torsion
if the homotopy groups
\pi_{n}(X,x)
are
p^{\infty}
-torsion (meaning every element is annihilated by some power of

) for
n\geq 2
. In other words,
X
is weakly

-torsion if it is weakly
p
-local and its simply connected cover has trivial rationalization.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose

is a weakly

-local space and

a natural number. Then

is a parametric

-equivalence for every

if and only if

is
(n+1)
-connected and has vanishing
v_{i}
-periodic homotopy groups for every
i\leq n
.

Of course it is desirable to upgrade
1.1
from an absolute statement about

to a relative statement about a map

. First of all we build on Bousfield’s work
[
5
,
6
]
to prove the following two results:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
f\colon X\to Y
is a parametric

-equivalence of weakly
p^{\infty}
-torsion spaces. Then the truncation
\tau_{\leq n+1}f
is an equivalence and

is a
v_{n}
-periodic equivalence at every basepoint.

For the next statement, recall that a map

is said to be
k
-connected if its fiber (over any basepoint) is
(k-1)
-connected, or equivalently if
f
induces isomorphisms on
\pi_{i}
for
i<k
and a surjection on
\pi_{k}
, taken at any basepoint.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
f\colon X\to Y
is an
(n+2)
-connected map of spaces and that

is a

-periodic equivalence at every basepoint, for all
i\leq n
. Then

is a parametric
T(i)
-equivalence for every
i\leq n
.

Remark 1.4
.

One important difference between
1.2
and
1.3
is that the first focuses on a single height
n
, while the second concerns all heights
i\leq n
. This is necessary. Indeed, the statement of
1.3

cannot
be improved to a statement of the following kind:

(*)

An
(n+2)
-connected map
f\colon X\to Y
of spaces that is a

-periodic equivalence at every basepoint is also a parametric
T(n)
-equivalence.

Indeed, there exist highly connected spaces

with trivial

-periodic homotopy groups, but nontrivial

-homology (see example (b) earlier in this introduction or

below for details). In particular, for such

is also not a parametric

-equivalence.

Remark 1.5
.

Note that
1.3
offers a partial converse to
1.2
. However, the first assumes

to be
(n+2)
-connected whereas the second gives a statement about
\tau_{\leq n+1}f
, which is slightly weaker. One might hope that this is just a limitation of our proof technique and optimistically propose a statement of the following kind, for a fixed natural number

:

(**)

A map
f\colon X\to Y
of weakly

-torsion spaces is a parametric

-equivalence for every

if and only if
\tau_{\leq n+1}f
is an equivalence and

is a

-periodic equivalence for every

.

The ‘only if’ follows from

and the ‘if’ would be a strengthening of

. However, (**)
does not hold

that the map

is not a parametric

-equivalence. (Here

-periodic equivalence for any

. A different attempt to better align the statements of

and

would be to ask if the conclusion of

can be strengthened to say that

is in fact

-connected. This cannot work either, since

is a parametric

A more promising avenue to avoid the counterexample of

-periodic homotopy groups vanish for

. In that case we do get the following much sharper statement in the context of infinite loop spaces (but arbitrary maps between them):

.

Let

Suppose

The role of periodic homology equivalences of spaces

The fact that
1.3
does not admit a version at a single height
n
(cf.
1.4
) can be seen as a consequence of the following result of Bousfield
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
:

Theorem 1.9

(Bousfield)
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a
K(n)
-equivalence of spaces. Then
f
is also a
K(i)
-equivalence for
0<i<n
.

This statement contrasts starkly with stable homotopy theory: for a map
f
of
spectra
, the statement above is well-known in case
X
and
Y
are assumed to be finite, but completely false in general. The fact that it holds for arbitrary spaces suggests that localizing at
K(n)
-homology (or at
T(n)
-homology) should
not
be thought of as making the homotopy theory of spaces ‘monochromatic’: indeed, it still records information about all heights
i<n
(except 0) and is therefore perhaps best considered as a ‘transchromatic’ localization.

Remark 1.10
.

A statement like
1.9
does not hold for periodic homotopy groups, again illustrating the difference between periodic homotopy and periodic homology. Indeed, if
X
is a pointed space, then the nullification map
\eta\colon X\to\mathbf{P}_{V_{n}}X
with respect to a type
n
suspension
V_{n}
(see
Section

3.2
) kills the
v_{n}
-periodic homotopy groups but is an isomorphism on
v_{i}
-periodic homotopy groups for
i<n
. Observe that the map
\eta
is also a
T(i)
-homology isomorphism for
i<n
, simply because
V_{n}
is
T(i)
-acyclic. In particular, if one starts with a highly-connected space
X
with nonvanishing
K(i)
-homology, this yields an example of a highly connected space
\mathbf{P}_{V_{n}}X
with trivial
v_{n}
-periodic homotopy groups, but nonvanishing
K(i)
-homology, hence nonvanishing
K(n)
-homology by
1.9
, hence nonvanishing
T(n)
-homology. Such a space was used in
1.4
above to contradict the statement (*).

Of course these considerations naturally lead to the following question:

Question 2.
Does Bousfield’s
1.9
hold with
K
replaced by
T
? More precisely, is a
T(n)
-equivalence of spaces also a
T(i)
-equivalence for
0<i<n
?

Bousfield’s proof of
1.9
does not readily generalize to the telescopic case, so a new approach would be needed. If the answer to this question is affirmative, then we would have the following strengthenings of
1.1
and
1.2
:

Theorem 1.11
.

Suppose that the answer to Question 2 is yes. Let
X
be a weakly
p
-local space with vanishing rational homology and let
n\geq 1
be a natural number. Then
X\to*
is a parametric
T(n)
-equivalence if and only if
X
is
(n+1)
-connected and has vanishing
v_{i}
-periodic homotopy groups for every
i\leq n
.

Theorem 1.12
.

Suppose that the answer to Question 2 is yes. If
f\colon X\to Y
is a parametric
T(n)
-equivalence of weakly
p^{\infty}
-torsion spaces, then the truncation
\tau_{\leq n+1}f
is an equivalence and
f
is a
v_{i}
-periodic equivalence at every basepoint for any
i\leq n
.

This stronger version of
1.2
would strengthen the parallel with
1.3
. Indeed, the two results would be each other’s converse except for the appearance of
\tau_{\leq n+1}f
in one and an
(n+2)
-connected map

in the other.

The aim of this section is to develop the notion of

of spaces, for a homology theory

of spaces. We will investigate some basic properties, as well as the relation to ordinary

2.1
Parametric homology equivalences

Let
E
be a spectrum. As usual, we call a map
f\colon X\to Y
of spaces (resp. of spectra) an
E
-equivalence
if
E\otimes\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}f
(resp.

) is an equivalence of spectra. Bousfield has proved that the localization of spectra at the
E
-equivalences exists
[
9
]
. To be precise, a spectrum
Z
is
E
-local
if for any
E
-equivalence of spectra
f\colon X\to Y
, the induced map
\mathrm{Map}(Y,Z)\to\mathrm{Map}(X,Z)
is an equivalence of spaces. (Alternatively, one can require
\mathrm{Map}(A,Z)\cong 0
for every
E
-acyclic spectrum

.) We will write
\mathrm{Sp}_{E}
for the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Sp}
on the
E
-local spectra. Then the inclusion
\mathrm{Sp}_{E}\to\mathrm{Sp}
admits a left adjoint
\Loc_{E}\colon\mathrm{Sp}\to\mathrm{Sp}_{E}
with the property that for any spectrum

, the unit map
X\to\Loc_{E}X
is an
E
-equivalence. It follows from this that the functor
\Loc_{E}
exhibits
\mathrm{Sp}_{E}
as the localization of
\mathrm{Sp}
at the
E
-equivalences.

Definition 2.1
.

A map
f\colon X\to Y
of spaces is a

-equivalence
if the induced functor

f^{*}\colon\mathrm{Fun}(Y,\mathrm{Sp}_{E})\rightarrow\mathrm{Fun}(X,\mathrm{Sp}_{E})

is an equivalence of

is an equivalence of

∎

The case of connected

Lemma 2.4

By

for the basepoint of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Sp}_{E}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(X,\mathrm{Sp}_{E})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x^{*}}

is monadic; indeed, the right adjoint

∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Suppose

. Then if

is a parametric

-equivalence, the truncation

is an equivalence of spaces.

Proof.

2.3
implies that
\pi_{0}f
is a bijection. For any choice of basepoint

of

,

implies that the resulting map

∎

-local space. Writing

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Loc_{E}}

Proposition 2.6

(1)

A connected space

(2)

(3)

.

(1). It is straightforward to see that a connected space

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}_{*}(Y,Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}_{*}(X,Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}Y,\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}Z)}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

(3). It suffices to check that for every

∎

2.2
Parametric
T(n)
-equivalences

In the previous section we introduced the notion of parametric
E
-equivalence. In this paper we will mainly be interested in the case

. Let us first briefly analyze the special case

, corresponding to rational homology, and then proceed to higher heights.

Proposition 2.7
.

A map
f\colon X\to Y
is a parametric
\mathbb{Q}
-equivalence if and only if
\tau_{\leq 1}f
is an equivalence and for any choice of basepoint
x\in X
and
k\geq 2
, the map
f
induces an isomorphism

\pi_{k}(X,x)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_{k}(Y,f(x))\otimes\mathbb{Q}.

Remark 2.8
.

Relatedly, it follows from
2.6
that a space
X
is parametrically
\mathbb{Q}
-local if and only if at every basepoint
x\in X
, the homotopy groups
\pi_{k}(X,x)
are rational vector spaces for any

.

Proof.

Suppose that
f
is a parametric
\mathbb{Q}
-equivalence. Then
\tau_{\leq 1}f
is an equivalence by
2.5
. Moreover, for any choice of basepoint in
X
the induced map
\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}f\colon\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X\to\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}Y
is a rational homology equivalence by
2.4
, hence also a rational homotopy equivalence by the Hurewicz theorem. (Here we rely on the fact that any loop space is simple, i.e., has an abelian fundamental group that acts trivially on higher homotopy groups.) For the converse, suppose that
f
induces an equivalence
\tau_{\leq 1}f
and isomorphisms on rational homotopy groups in dimensions
\geq 2
. By
2.3
we may work one connected component at a time and thus reduce to the case that
X
and

are connected. Consider the diagram of fiber sequences

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{>0}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{>0}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}Y.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

The left-most map is a rational homotopy equivalence of simple spaces and hence a rational homology equivalence. It follows that the middle map is a rational homology equivalence as well, which suffices by

.
∎

For the remainder of this section we fix a choice of prime number
p
and height

. We will now record several results that are specific to the

-local setting, in particular on the behavior of parametric

-localization on truncated spaces. Most of these results follow directly from a combination of results of Bousfield
[
8
]
with the work of Carmeli–Schlank–Yanovski
[

]
.

Recall that an abelian group

is said to be

p
-complete
if it is
p
-complete when considered as an object of the derived
\infty
-category
\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{Z})
. Equivalently,

is derived

-complete if and only if
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p},A)=0
and the canonical homomorphism

is an isomorphism. (The map is induced by the short exact sequence
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}{[1/p]}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.) This is the condition that Bousfield–Kan refer to as being

-complete
.

Proposition 2.9
.

(1)

For
A
an abelian group and
k\geq n+2
, the Eilenberg–MacLane space
K(A,k)
is
T(n)
-acyclic. If
A
is torsion, then
K(A,n+1)
is
T(n)
-acyclic as well.

(2)

A truncated
H
-space
X
is
T(n)
-local if and only if
\pi_{k}(X,x)
is derived
p
-complete for any
x\in X
and
1\leq k\leq n+1
, torsionfree for
k=n+1
, and vanishes for
k>n+1
.

Proof.

(1). It is proved in
[
14
, Theorem E]
that the space
K(\mathbb{Z}/p,n+1)
is
T(n)
-acyclic. Then it follows easily by induction that for any finite abelian group
B
, the space
K(B,n+1)
is also
T(n)
-acyclic. Finally, a general torsion abelian group
A
is a filtered colimit of finite abelian groups, so that
K(A,n+1)
is
T(n)
-acyclic as well. Since
K(\mathbb{Z}_{p},n+2)
is the
p
-completion of

, it is also
T(n)
-acyclic. The same inductive argument as before now shows that

is
T(n)
-acyclic for any abelian group. The same is then true of the higher Eilenberg–MacLane spaces

with

since they can be built out of

via finite products and colimits.

(2). Bousfield
[
4
, Theorem 7.2]
proves that an
H
-space as in the statement is

-local, thus in particular
T(n)
-local. Conversely, if

is a truncated

-space that is

-local, then in particular it is
p
-complete. Furthermore, if
\widehat{\tau}_{\leq n+1}X
denotes the ‘modified Postnikov section’ of

(cf.
[
4
, Section 7.1]
) which is obtained from the usual truncation
\tau_{\leq n+1}X
by replacing

with its maximal torsionfree derived

-complete quotient, then the map

is a
T(n)
-equivalence by (1); indeed, its fiber is
T(n)
-acyclic. Furthermore,
\widehat{\tau}_{\leq n+1}X
is
T(n)
-local by the first half of the argument and therefore

is an equivalence.
∎

From
2.9
we can immediately deduce the following:

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a map between
H
-spaces and assume that the homotopy groups
\pi_{k}X
and
\pi_{k}Y
are
p^{\infty}
-torsion for

. If
f
is a
T(n)
-equivalence, then
\tau_{\leq n}f
is an equivalence.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may suppose that
X
and
Y
are connected. For any connected nilpotent space

we will write
\mathrm{tors}_{p}Z
for its
p^{\infty}
-torsion part, defined as the fiber of the rationalization map

. We note that for a connected

-space

, its

-torsion part

is

-truncated if and only if

is
(n+1)
-truncated and has torsionfree
\pi_{n+1}
. By

(2), this is equivalent to the statement that

is

-local.

Since
X
and

have

-torsion homotopy groups, it follows that for any

-space

we have an equivalence
\mathrm{Map}_{*}(X,\mathrm{tors}_{p}Z)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Map}_{*}(X,Z_{p}^{\wedge})
and similarly for

. In particular, the comments in the preceding paragraph combined with the hypothesis that

is a

-equivalence show that if

is any

-truncated

-space with

-torsion homotopy groups, then

is an equivalence. By the Yoneda lemma and the fact that

, it follows that the map

is an equivalence.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

We expect a version of

to hold true without the

-space hypothesis. Since we do not need it, we will not pursue this version here.

Corollary 2.12
.

Let

be a parametric

-equivalence between weakly

-torsion spaces. Then

is an equivalence.

Proof.

As usual we may work one connected component at a time and assume that

and

are connected. Picking a basepoint of

we find that

is a

-equivalence between spaces satisfying the hypotheses of

and the conclusion follows.
∎

2.3

-equivalences

This section serves to clarify the relation between our notion of parametric

-equivalence and Bousfield’s notion of virtual

-equivalence

, as well as to recall several useful properties of virtual

-equivalences that will be needed later.

Definition 2.13
.

A map

of pointed spaces is a

-equivalence
if the map

\tau_{>i}\Loc_{T(n)}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X)\to\tau_{>i}\Loc_{T(n)}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}Y)

is an equivalence for

.

In
[

, Theorem 11.10]
, Bousfield proves that the notion of virtual

-equivalence admits the following stronger characterization:

Theorem 2.14

(Bousfield)
.

Let

be a map of pointed spaces. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The map
f
is a virtual
T(n)
-equivalence.

(2)

For any
k\geq 1
and
i\geq n+2
, the map
\tau_{>i}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{k}X)\to\tau_{>i}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{k}Y)
is a
T(n)
-equivalence.

(3)

There exist
k\geq 1
and
i\geq n+2
so that
\tau_{>i}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{k}X)\to\tau_{>i}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{k}Y)
is a
T(n)
-equivalence.

We need several further properties, also proved by Bousfield on the way to establishing

.

Proposition 2.15

(
[
6
, Theorem 11.3]
)
.

A
T(n)
-equivalence of
H
-spaces is a virtual
T(n)
-equivalence.

The following is
[
6
, Theorem 11.4]
, but is also a formal consequence of
2.14
:

Proposition 2.16
.

Consider a map between fiber sequences of pointed spaces:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\prime}.}

If two out of the three vertical maps are virtual

-equivalences, then so is the third.

Finally we discuss the relation between parametric and virtual
T(n)
-equivalences. We have the following simple observation:

Lemma 2.17
.

A parametric
T(n)
-equivalence
f\colon X\to Y
of pointed spaces is also a virtual
T(n)
-equivalence.

Proof.

According to
2.4
the map
\Loc_{T(n)}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}f)
is an equivalence. Hence it is in particular an equivalence in high degrees.
∎

This statement admits the following converse:

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
f
be a virtual
T(n)
-equivalence of pointed connected spaces.

(1)

If the fiber
\mathrm{fib}(f)
is
n
-connected and
\pi_{n+1}(\mathrm{fib}(f))
is torsion, then
f
is a
T(n)
-equivalence.

(2)

If the fiber
\mathrm{fib}(f)
is
(n+1)
-connected and
\pi_{n+2}(\mathrm{fib}(f))
is torsion, then
f
is a parametric
T(n)
-equivalence.

Proof.

The fiber
\mathrm{fib}(f)
is virtually
T(n)
-acyclic by
2.16
. By
2.14
the space
\tau_{>n+2}\mathrm{fib}(f)
is then
T(n)
-acyclic, so that the map
\mathrm{fib}(f)\to\tau_{\leq n+2}\mathrm{fib}(f)
is a
T(n)
-equivalence. The space
\tau_{\leq n+2}\mathrm{fib}(f)
can only have nontrivial homotopy groups in degrees

and

, of which the first is torsion by hypothesis. It is therefore
T(n)
-acyclic as a consequence of
2.9
(1). We conclude that
\mathrm{fib}(f)
is
T(n)
-acylic and

is a
T(n)
-equivalence. For (2), we note that it suffices to prove that
\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}f
is a
T(n)
-equivalence. We start with the observation that also
\mathrm{fib}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}f)
is virtually
T(n)
-acyclic by

. Then
\mathrm{fib}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}f)\to\tau_{\leq n+2}(\mathrm{fib}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}f))=\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}\tau_{\leq n+3}(\mathrm{fib}(f))

-equivalence and the conclusion follows analogously.
∎

Periodic homotopy groups and localization of spaces

We will begin this section by recalling the definition of

-periodic homotopy groups and the construction of the telescopic functors of Bousfield and Kuhn. Then we review the relation between periodic homotopy groups and the theory of
nullification
with respect to a given space

developed by Bousfield
[

]
and Farjoun
[

]
. In particular, we discuss the (unstable) Bousfield classes of spaces. Apart from our synthesis we claim little originality here; all of the relevant ingredients were essentially already established by Bousfield
[

,
8
]
, but we need to put them in a form that facilitates our work in later sections.

3.1
Periodic homotopy groups

As always we assume fixed a prime number
p
. Let
V_{n}
be a finite pointed space of type

, equipped with a

self-map

. We will take

, so that also

. Then for any pointed space

we may consider the

of

defined by

. In fact these homotopy groups arise as the homotopy groups of a spectrum
[

]
. This spectrum is formed from the spaces

by using the ‘transition maps’

\mathrm{Map}_{*}(V_{n},X)\xrightarrow{v^{*}}\mathrm{Map}_{*}(\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{d}V_{n},X)\simeq\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{d}\mathrm{Map}_{*}(V_{n},X).

More precisely, Bousfield and Kuhn’s telescopic functors arise as follows:

Definition 3.1
.

The
telescopic functor

\Phi_{V_{n}}\colon\mathscr{S}_{*}\to\mathrm{Sp}
associated to
V
is defined by

\Phi_{V_{n}}(X):=\operatornamewithlimits{colim}\left(\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}\mathrm{Map}_{*}(V_{n},X)\xrightarrow{v^{*}}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty-d}\mathrm{Map}_{*}(V_{n},X)\xrightarrow{v^{*}}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty-2d}\mathrm{Map}_{*}(V_{n},X)\xrightarrow{v^{*}}\cdots\right).

Remark 3.2
.

One easily verifies that the groups
\pi_{*}\Phi_{V_{n}}(X)
are indeed isomorphic to the
v
-periodic homotopy groups of
X
as defined above. Moreover, as the notation suggests, the functor
\Phi_{V_{n}}
depends only on
V_{n}
and not on the choice of self-map
v
. This is a consequence of the asymptotic uniqueness of
v_{n}
self-maps
[
20
, Corollary 3.7]
.

Definition 3.3
.

A map
f
of pointed spaces is a
v_{n}
-periodic equivalence
if
\Phi_{V_{n}}(f)
is an equivalence.

The above is equivalent to demanding that
f
induce an isomorphism on
v
-periodic homotopy groups. It is a consequence of the thick subcategory theorem
[
20
]
that the notion of
v_{n}
-periodic equivalence only depends on
n
and not on the choice of
V_{n}
or of the self-map
v
.

Remark 3.4
.

The
Bousfield–Kuhn functor

\Phi_{n}\colon\mathscr{S}_{*}\to\mathrm{Sp}
removes the dependence on
V
(see
[
21
]
for a detailed discussion). It is a functor satisfying the following two properties:

(1)

There is a natural equivalence
V_{n}^{\vee}\otimes\Phi_{n}\simeq\Phi_{V_{n}}
, where
V_{n}^{\vee}
denotes the Spanier–Whitehead dual of
V
.

(2)

The composite
\mathrm{Sp}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}}\mathscr{S}_{*}\xrightarrow{\Phi_{n}}\mathrm{Sp}
is equivalent to the
T(n)
-localization functor.

In fact
\Phi_{n}
is completely characterized by property (1), if that equivalence is also required to be natural in
V_{n}
. The homotopy groups of
\Phi_{n}
can be interpreted as the ‘absolute’
v_{n}
-periodic homotopy groups, removing the dependence on a choice of ‘coefficients’
V_{n}
. In this paper we will not need
\Phi_{n}
itself and only use the telescopic functors
\Phi_{V_{n}}
.

Let us recall some basic properties of
\Phi_{V_{n}}
for later use.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any pointed space
X
, the spectrum
\Phi_{V_{n}}(X)
is
T(n)
-local.

The functor
\Phi_{V_{n}}
preserves fiber sequences. In particular, a map of pointed spaces is a
v_{n}
-periodic equivalence if and only if
\Phi_{V_{n}}
annihilates its fiber.

For a pointed space
X
, the
i
-connected cover
\tau_{>i}X\to X
is a
v_{n}
-periodic equivalence for any
i\geq 0
.

The canonical map
\mathrm{Map}_{*}(V_{n},X)\to\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}\Phi_{V_{n}}(X)
is a
v_{n}
-periodic equivalence.

Proof.

Item (1) is
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
, item (2) is straightforward from the construction of the telescopic functor, and (3) follows from the combination of (2) and the observation that the
v
-periodic homotopy groups of any truncated space must vanish. To establish (4) we should argue that
\Phi_{V_{n}}\mathrm{Map}_{*}(V_{n},X)\to\Phi_{V_{n}}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}\Phi_{V_{n}}(X)
is an equivalence. Since
\Phi_{V_{n}}
commutes with finite limits, it also commutes with the cotensoring of
\mathscr{S}_{*}
and
\mathrm{Sp}
by finite spaces, and therefore
\Phi_{V_{n}}\mathrm{Map}_{*}(V_{n},X)\simeq V_{n}^{\vee}\otimes\Phi_{V_{n}}(X)
. By
3.4
we can also identify

\Phi_{V_{n}}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}\Phi_{V_{n}}(X)\simeq V_{n}^{\vee}\otimes\Phi_{n}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}\Phi_{V_{n}}(X)\simeq V_{n}^{\vee}\otimes\Phi_{V_{n}}(X)

and under these identifications it is straightforward to verify that the map of (4) becomes the identity.
∎

The following will be a convenient criterion:

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a map of pointed spaces and suppose that

\mathrm{Map}_{*}(V_{n},f)\colon\mathrm{Map}_{*}(V_{n},X)\to\mathrm{Map}_{*}(V_{n},Y)

is a
T(n)
-equivalence. Then
f
is a
v_{n}
-periodic equivalence.

Proof.

Since
\Phi_{V_{n}}
is constructed as a colimit of desuspensions of the functor
\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}\mathrm{Map}_{*}(V_{n},-)
, it follows that
\Phi_{V_{n}}(f)
is a
T(n)
-equivalence of spectra. But
\Phi_{V_{n}}(X)
and
\Phi_{V_{n}}(Y)
are
T(n)
-local by
3.5
(1), so that
\Phi_{V_{n}}(f)
is an equivalence.
∎

3.2
Localization and nullification of spaces

In this section we briefly review Bousfield and Farjoun’s work
[
5
,
15
]
on the localization
\Loc_{f}
of the
\infty
-category
\mathscr{S}
of spaces with respect to a fixed map
f\colon V\to W
. In the special case of a map
f\colon V\to*
, this localization is usually referred to as the
nullification
with respect to the space
V
and denoted
\mathbf{P}_{V}
. The main technical results we will need are Bousfield’s
3.18
and its implications for the determination of the Bousfield classes of spaces.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
f\colon V\to W
be a map of spaces.

(1)

A space
X
is
\Loc_{f}
-local
if
f^{*}\colon\mathrm{Map}(W,X)\to\mathrm{Map}(V,X)
is an equivalence.

(2)

A map
g\colon A\to B
is an
\Loc_{f}
-equivalence
if for any
\Loc_{f}
-local space
X
, the map
g^{*}\colon\mathrm{Map}(B,X)\to\mathrm{Map}(A,X)
is an equivalence.

(3)

A map
i\colon C\to D
is
f
-cellular
if it lies in the weakly saturated class generated by
f
, i.e., if it can be obtained from
f
via pushouts, transfinite compositions, and retracts.

In the absolute case of a map
f\colon V\to*
, the terminology we use is as follows: we say
\mathbf{P}_{V}
-local
instead of
\Loc_{f}
-local,
\mathbf{P}_{V}
-equivalence
instead of
\Loc_{f}
-equivalence
and
V
-cellular
instead of
f
-cellular.

Remark 3.8
.

It is common to say
f
-local
for what we call
\Loc_{f}
-local and
V
-null
or
V
-periodic
for what we call
\mathbf{P}_{V}
-local. We stick with the terminology introduced above to limit the amount of necessary terminology and notation and to avoid confusion with the kernel of the functor
\mathbf{P}_{V}
.

We collect some of the basic existence results of the theory of localizations in the following (see e.g.
[
15
]
).

Proposition 3.9
.

(1)

Any space
X
admits an
f
-cellular map
X\to\Loc_{f}X
so that
\Loc_{f}X
is an
\Loc_{f}
-local space.

(2)

Any
f
-cellular map is an
\Loc_{f}
-equivalence.

(3)

The inclusion of the full subcategory
\Loc_{f}\mathscr{S}
of
\Loc_{f}
-local pointed spaces into all of
\mathscr{S}
admits a left adjoint, which we will denote
\Loc_{f}
.

Proof.

Item (1) is proved using the small object argument. Item (2) follows immediately from the observation that the
\Loc_{f}
-equivalences form a weakly saturated class. Item (3) follows from the observation that for any
X\in\mathscr{S}
, the functor

\mathrm{Map}(X,-)\colon\Loc_{f}\mathscr{S}\to\mathscr{S}

is corepresentable, namely by a space
\Loc_{f}X
built as in item (1).
∎

Example 3.10
.

Localization with respect to a homology theory
E
is an example of
\Loc_{f}
-localization with respect to a suitably chosen map
f

[
6
, Section 2.5]
. Indeed,
f
can be taken to be the disjoint union of ‘all’
E
-equivalences; to be more precise, one considers
E
-equivalences between
\kappa
-small spaces, with
\kappa
the cardinality of
\pi_{*}E
.

Let us observe that parametric
E
-localization is essentially also an example of
\Loc_{f}
-localization:

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
E
be a spectrum and let
f\colon A\to B
be a map of pointed spaces so that
\Loc_{f}=\Loc_{E}
(see
Example

3.10
). Then a connected pointed space
X
is
\Loc_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}f}
-local if and only if it is parametrically
E
-local.

Proof.

It is immediate from the definitions that
X
is
\Loc_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}f}
-local if and only if
\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X
is
\Loc_{f}
-local. The result now follows from
2.6
(1).
∎

We will need several results on the interplay between suspensions and localizations, of which the first is the following (see
[
16
]
,
[
5
, Section 3]
):

Theorem 3.12

(Bousfield, Farjoun)
.

Let
f\colon V\to W
be a map of pointed spaces. Then there is a natural equivalence of functors
\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}\Loc_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}f}\simeq\Loc_{f}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}
on the
\infty
-category
\mathscr{S}_{*}
of pointed spaces.

It will be useful to record the following observation on the interplay between
f
-localization and
E
-equivalences:

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
E
be a spectrum and
f\colon V\to W
an
E
-equivalence of pointed spaces. For a map
g\colon X\to Y
of pointed spaces we have:

(1)

The map
g
is an
E
-equivalence if and only if
\Loc_{f}(g)
is an
E
-equivalence.

(2)

If
X
and
Y
are connected, then
g
is a parametric
E
-equivalence if and only if
\Loc_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}f}(g)
is a parametric
E
-equivalence.

Proof.

For (1) it suffices to show that the maps
X\to\Loc_{f}X
and
Y\to\Loc_{f}Y
are
E
-equivalences. These maps are
f
-cellular by
3.9
and
f
-cellular maps are
E
-equivalences by the hypothesis that
f
is an
E
-equivalence. For (2), we rely on
2.4
and the fact that
\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}\Loc_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}f}X\simeq\Loc_{f}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X
(
3.12
) to reduce to showing that
\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X\to\Loc_{f}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X
is an
E
-equivalence (and similarly for
Y
). This follows as in the first part of the proof.
∎

For the remainder of this section we focus on the case of localization with respect to a map
f\colon V\to*
, in which case the corresponding localization functor is denoted
\mathbf{P}_{V}
.

Definition 3.14
.

The
Bousfield class
of a pointed space
V
, denoted by
\langle V\rangle
, is the class of all pointed spaces
X
for which
\mathbf{P}_{V}(X)\simeq 0
. These Bousfield classes are partially ordered by inclusion: we write
\langle V\rangle\geq\langle W\rangle
if
\mathbf{P}_{W}(X)\simeq 0
implies
\mathbf{P}_{V}(X)\simeq 0
.

Remark 3.15
.

There are many alternative ways to characterize the relation
\langle V\rangle\geq\langle W\rangle
, of which we highlight the following:

(1)

Every
\mathbf{P}_{V}
-local space is also
\mathbf{P}_{W}
-local.

(2)

\mathbf{P}_{V}(W)\simeq 0
.

3.12
implies the following elementary relations:

Corollary 3.16
.

Let
V
and
W
be pointed spaces. Then we have the following:

(1)

If
W
is connected, then
\langle\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V\rangle\geq\langle W\rangle
if and only if
\langle V\rangle\geq\langle\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}W\rangle
.

(2)

If
V
is connected, then
\langle\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}V\rangle\geq\langle V\rangle
.

(3)

\langle V\rangle\geq\langle\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V\rangle
.

Proof.

For (1), first note that
\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V}W
is still connected. Now
3.12
implies that
\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V}W\simeq 0
if and only if
\mathbf{P}_{V}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}W)\simeq 0
. For (2), we apply item (1) to see that the inequality is equivalent to
\langle\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}V\rangle\geq\langle\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}V\rangle
. For (3), note that
\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V
is connected and apply (1) to see that the inequality is equivalent to
\langle\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V\rangle\geq\langle\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V\rangle
.
∎

Remark 3.17
.

Note that
3.16
in particular implies
\langle\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}K(\mathbb{Z}/p,n)\rangle\geq\langle K(\mathbb{Z}/p,n+1)\rangle
.

The following was proved by Bousfield
[
5
, Theorems 9.10, 9.11]
and is a crucial tool in analyzing the Bousfield classes of spaces:

Theorem 3.18

(Bousfield)
.

Let
n>0
be a natural number and let
X
be a pointed space for which
\widetilde{H}_{*}(X)
consists of
p^{\infty}
-torsion,
\widetilde{H}_{i}(X)=0
for
i<n
and
\widetilde{H}_{n}(X)\neq 0
. Then for each
k\geq 1
there is an equality of Bousfield classes

\langle\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}X\rangle=\langle\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{k}X\rangle\vee\langle K(A,n+1)\rangle,

where
A=\mathbb{Z}/p
if
H_{n}(X;\mathbb{Z}/p)\neq 0
and
A=\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
if
H_{n}(X;\mathbb{Z}/p)=0
.

We explicitly record the following simple consequence for later use:

Corollary 3.19
.

Let
X
be as in
3.18
. Then for each
k\geq 1
we have

\langle\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}X\rangle\leq\langle\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{k}X\rangle\vee\langle K(\mathbb{Z}/p,n+1)\rangle.

Proof.

Considering the two possible cases in
3.18
, it is clear that it suffices to prove
\langle K(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}_{(p)},n+1)\rangle\leq\langle K(\mathbb{Z}/p,n+1)\rangle
. The class of abelian groups
A
for which
\mathbf{P}_{K(\mathbb{Z}/p,n+1)}(K(A,n+1))\simeq 0
is closed under extensions and directed colimits (cf.
[
5
, Lemma 7.4]
and its proof). This class contains
\mathbb{Z}/p
by definition, hence it also contains
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}
and
\varinjlim_{k}\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\cong\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
.
∎

Combining
3.18
with the Hopkins–Smith thick subcategory theorem, Bousfield easily deduces the following classification of finite suspensions
[
5
, Theorem 9.15]
:

Corollary 3.20
.

Let
V
and
W
be finite
p
-local spaces of type
>0
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\langle\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V\rangle\geq\langle\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W\rangle
,

(2)

\mathrm{type}(V)\leq\mathrm{type}(W)
and
\mathrm{conn}(\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V)\leq\mathrm{conn}(\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W)
.

Closely related to
3.12
and
3.18
, Bousfield proves that the localization functors
\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V}
preserve finite limits up to ‘low-dimensional error terms’. We finish this section by giving a precise statement to be used later. First of all, observe that for pointed spaces
A
and
X
, the mapping space
\mathrm{Map}_{*}(A,\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V}X)
is again
\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V}
-local. Hence we find a natural map

\eta\colon\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V}\mathrm{Map}_{*}(A,X)\to\mathrm{Map}_{*}(A,\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V}X).

Theorem 3.21

(Bousfield,
[
5
, Theorem 8.3]
)
.

Suppose
A
is a finite pointed space and suppose that
V
is a space for which
\widetilde{H}_{*}(V)
consists of
p^{\infty}
-torsion,
\widetilde{H}_{i}(V)=0
for
i<n
, and
H_{n}(V;\mathbb{Z}/p)\neq 0
. Then the fiber
F
of
\eta
over any point of
\mathrm{Map}_{*}(A,\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V}X)
is
n
-truncated.

3.3
The
v_{n}
-periodization of spaces

In this section we review the relation between
v_{n}
-periodic homotopy groups and the nullification
\mathbf{P}_{V_{n+1}}
with respect to a finite space
V_{n+1}
of type
n+1
. For a chosen such space
V_{n+1}
, we will write
W_{n+1}:=V_{n+1}\vee K(\mathbb{Z}/p,n+1)
. (It is not difficult to deduce from
3.18
and
3.20
that the Bousfield class of
W_{n+1}
does not depend on the choice of
V_{n+1}
, although we will not need this.) Bousfield proved, roughly speaking, that a map of highly connected pointed spaces is a
\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}
-equivalence if and only if it is a
v_{i}
-periodic equivalence for every
i\leq n
(see
[
5
, Theorem 11.10]
). The precise version that we need is captured by the following two results:

Theorem 3.22
.

Let
V_{n+1}
be a finite pointed space of type
n+1
and
W_{n+1}=V_{n+1}\vee K(\mathbb{Z}/p,n+1)
. Then for any pointed space
X
we have:

(1)

The map
X\to\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}X
is a
v_{i}
-periodic equivalence for every
i\leq n
.

(2)

If
X
is an
H
-space, then
X\to\mathbf{P}_{W_{n+1}}X
is a
v_{i}
-periodic equivalence for every
i\leq n
.

In the opposite direction, we have the following:

Theorem 3.23
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a map of
p
-local pointed spaces that is a
v_{i}
-periodic equivalence for every
i\leq n
and let
W_{n+1}
be as in
3.22
. If
f
is
(n+2)
-connected, then
f
is a
\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}
-equivalence.

To prepare for the proofs, we first recall the relation between nullification with respect to
V_{n+1}
and
W_{n+1}
.

Lemma 3.24
.

There exists
j\geq 1
so that for any pointed space
X
, the natural map

\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V_{n+1}}X\to\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}X

has
(n+j)
-truncated fiber. In particular, the map induces an equivalence on
(n+j+1)
-connected covers.

Proof.

It follows from
[
5
, Theorem 9.12]
that there is some
j\geq 1
so that
\langle\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{j}W_{n+1}\rangle=\langle\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V_{n+1}\rangle
. (Alternatively, this can be deduced directly from
3.18
.) The fiber of the natural map

\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{j}W_{n+1}}X\to\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}X

is
(n+j)
-truncated by
[
5
, Theorem 7.2]
or
[
16
]
.
∎

Proof of
3.22
.

We begin with item (1). Fix a finite pointed space
V_{i}
of type
i
with a
v_{i}
self-map
v\colon\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{d}V_{i}\to V_{i}
. We aim to show that
\Phi_{V_{i}}(X)\to\Phi_{V_{i}}(\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}X)
is an equivalence of spectra. The functor
\Phi_{V_{i}}
is insensitive to passing to highly connected covers, so invoking
3.24
we may replace
\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}X
with
\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V_{n+1}}X
. Since
\Phi_{V_{i}}(X)
and
\Phi_{V_{i}}(\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V_{n+1}}X)
are periodic, it will suffice to check that the associated map of infinite loop spaces is an equivalence, which by construction is the top horizontal map in the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{colim}_{k}\mathrm{Map}_{*}(\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{kd}V_{i},X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{colim}_{k}\mathrm{Map}_{*}(\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{kd}V_{i},\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V_{n+1}}X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V_{n+1}}\mathrm{colim}_{k}\mathrm{Map}_{*}(\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{kd}V_{i},X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{colim}_{k}\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V_{n+1}}\mathrm{Map}_{*}(\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{kd}V_{i},X).}

The bottom horizontal equivalence arises from the fact that
\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V_{n+1}}
preserves filtered colimits, since
V_{n+1}
is finite. The space on the upper left, which is
\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}\Phi_{V_{i}}X
, is

-local by
3.5
. It is therefore also
\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V_{n+1}}
-local, since
V_{n+1}
is
T(i)
-acyclic, so that the left vertical map is an equivalence. Finally, the vertical map on the right is an equivalence in high degrees by
3.21
. It follows that the top horizontal map is an equivalence in high degrees, but then it is in fact an equivalence since the spaces involved have periodic homotopy groups.

Now suppose that
X
is an
H
-space. Then it is a retract of
\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}X
, so for item (2) it will suffice to show that
\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}X\to\mathbf{P}_{W_{n+1}}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}X)
is a
v_{i}
-periodic equivalence for
i\leq n
. According to
3.12
we may identify this map with the image under
\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}
of the map
\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}X\to\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}X
, which is a
v_{i}
-periodic equivalence by the first part of the proof.
∎

Proof of
3.23
.

Write
F
for the fiber of
f
at an arbitrary basepoint
y
of
Y
. By hypothesis this is an
(n+1)
-connected space with vanishing
v_{i}
-periodic homotopy groups for
i\leq n
. It will suffice to show that
\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}F
is contractible. Observe that the space
\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}F
is still
(n+1)
-connected (because
\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}
is) and has vanishing
v_{i}
-periodic homotopy groups by
3.22
(1). Fix a sequence of finite pointed spaces
U_{i}
, with
0\leq i\leq n+1
, such that:

(1)

U_{0}
is a sphere
S^{k}
,

(2)

each
U_{i}
admits a
v_{i}
self-map
w_{i}\colon\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{d_{i}}U_{i}\to U_{i}
,

(3)

U_{i}=\mathrm{cof}(w_{i-1})
for
1\leq i\leq n+1
.

Such a sequence exists as a consequence of the Hopkins–Smith periodicity theorem. Now set
V_{i}:=\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{n-i+1}U_{i}
. We will prove by a finite downward induction on
i
that the space
\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}F
is
\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V_{i}}
-local. The base case
i=n+1
follows from the fact that

(where we may use

and the resulting fiber sequence

\mathrm{Map}_{*}(\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V_{i},\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}F)\xrightarrow{w_{i}^{*}}\mathrm{Map}_{*}(\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{d_{i}+1}V_{i},\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}F)\to\mathrm{Map}_{*}(\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V_{i+1},\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}F).

The right-hand term is contractible by the inductive hypothesis, so the first map is an equivalence. It follows that the natural map

\mathrm{Map}_{*}(\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V_{i},\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}F)\to\mathrm{colim}_{k}\mathrm{Map}_{*}(\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{1+kd_{i}}V_{i},\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}F)=\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}\Phi_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}V_{i}}(\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}F)

is an equivalence. The right-hand side is contractible because

has vanishing

-periodic homotopy groups. Hence the left-hand side is contractible and
\mathbf{P}_{\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}W_{n+1}}F
is

-local, as desired. Setting

is

-local, meaning it is

-truncated. At the same time, this space is

-torsion (since its

-periodic homotopy groups vanish), and

∎

5.1
Nilcompletion of

-local commutative rings

We begin by reviewing some general facts on the completion of commutative ring spectra. We will then specialize to the

-local context and consider the behavior of completion on two types of rings, namely free

-local commutative algebras and the
T(n)
-localized suspension spectra of Eilenberg–MacLane spaces.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a stable presentably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category and write
\mathrm{CAlg}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathscr{C})
for the
\infty
-category of augmented commutative algebras in
\mathscr{C}
(where the augmentation is to the unit
\mathbf{1}
of
\mathscr{C}
). Then there is an adjoint pair of functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CAlg}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathscr{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{cot}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{triv}}

where the right adjoint sends an object
X
of
\mathscr{C}
to the trivial square-zero extension
\mathbf{1}\oplus X
and the left adjoint takes the
cotangent fiber
of an augmented commutative algebra (also often referred to as

). Moreover, this adjunction exhibits
\mathscr{C}
as the stabilization of
\mathrm{CAlg}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathscr{C})

[
19
, Proposition 6.3]
. From this perspective, the functor
\mathrm{triv}
is analogous to
\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}
and the functor
\mathrm{cot}
to
\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}
.

In fact, to every

one can naturally associate a tower of augmented commutative algebras

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\vdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{3}R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{2}R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{1}R=\mathrm{triv}(\mathrm{cot}(R)).}

This is the nilcompletion tower considered in
[

, Section 9]
or alternatively the Goodwillie tower of the identity on the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{CAlg}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathscr{C})
(cf.
[

, Section 3]
). (This same tower also arises from the adic filtration of commutative algebras considered in
[

, Section 5.1]
.) We refer to the map

as the

of

. This nilcompletion is an analog in higher algebra of the completion of an ordinary augmented commutative ring at its augmentation ideal.

We will need a few basic properties of the nilcompletion tower. In the following, we write

\mathrm{Sym}(X)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}X^{\otimes k}_{h\Sigma_{k}}

for the free augmented commutative algebra on an object
X\in\mathscr{C}
. The functor
\mathrm{Sym}
can be constructed as the left adjoint of the augmentation ideal functor

\mathfrak{m}\colon\mathrm{CAlg}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathscr{C})\to\mathscr{C}\colon(R\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}\mathbf{1})\mapsto\mathrm{fib}(\varepsilon).

For the proof of the following proposition, item (1) can be found in
[
19
, Theorem 9.2]
or
[
22
, Theorem 3.10]
, whereas item (2) is immediate from the construction of

in
[

]
or stated explicitly as
[

, Example 3.14]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

(1)

The layers of the nilcompletion tower are given by

\mathrm{fib}(t_{k}R\to t_{k-1}R)\cong\mathrm{cot}(R)^{\otimes k}_{h\Sigma_{k}}.

More succinctly, the associated graded of the nilcompletion tower of
R
is the free commutative algebra on the cotangent fiber
\mathrm{cot}(R)
.

(2)

The nilcompletion tower of
\mathrm{Sym}(X)
can be identified with the system of natural projection maps

\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}X^{\otimes m}_{h\Sigma_{m}}\to\bigoplus_{m\leq k}X^{\otimes m}_{h\Sigma_{m}}

as
k
ranges from 1 to
\infty
. In particular, the nilcompletion
\lim_{k}t_{k}\mathrm{Sym}(X)
can be identified with the infinite product
\prod_{m}X^{\otimes m}_{h\Sigma_{m}}
.

We will now specialize to the case
\mathscr{C}=\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)}
. For a free commutative algebra
\mathrm{Sym}(X)
we use the shorthand
\widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}(X):=\lim_{k}t_{k}\mathrm{Sym}(X)
for its nilcompletion.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
be a
T(n)
-local spectrum. Then the homomorphism

T(n)_{*}\mathrm{Sym}(X)\to T(n)_{*}\widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}(X)

induced by nilcompletion is injective.

Proof.

The spectrum
T(n)
is the telescope of a
v_{n}
-self map on a finite spectrum
V
; in fact,
T(n)=L_{T(n)}V
. For a
T(n)
-local spectrum
Y
, we can therefore interpret the groups
T(n)_{*}Y
as the homotopy groups of the mapping spectrum
\mathrm{map}(L_{T(n)}V^{\vee},Y)
. Since
L_{T(n)}V^{\vee}
is a compact object of
\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)}
and
\mathrm{Sym}(X)
is an infinite direct sum of terms
X^{\otimes k}_{h\Sigma_{k}}
in
\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)}
, any map
f\colon L_{T(n)}V^{\vee}\to\mathrm{Sym}(X)
factors through a finite sum. In particular, if the composition of
f
with the map
\mathrm{Sym}(X)\to\widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}(X)
to the infinite product is null, then
f
itself must have been null.
∎

The preceding lemma shows that the nilcompletion of a free commutative algebra does not really lose any information. Quite the opposite is true for commutative algebras of the following kind:

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
A
be a torsion abelian group and consider the augmented commutative algebra

. Then
\mathrm{cot}(R)\cong 0
and hence the augmentation
R\to\mathbb{S}_{T(n)}
exhibits the unit as the nilcompletion of
R
.

Proof.

Since the functor
\mathrm{cot}
preserves colimits and its codomain is stable, it suffices to argue that the suspension of
R
is trivial (i.e., equivalent to the zero object
\mathbb{S}_{T(n)}
) in the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{CAlg}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)})
. This suspension is precisely the bar construction

\mathbb{S}_{T(n)}\otimes_{R}\mathbb{S}_{T(n)}\cong L_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}BK(A,n)=L_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}K(A,n+1).

The space

is
T(n)
-acyclic by
2.9
, giving the conclusion.
∎

R[e^{-1}]\to\mathbb{S}_{T(n)}
is an equivalence.

Proof.

Start with the standard identification of stable
\infty
-categories

\mathrm{LMod}_{R}(\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)})\simeq\mathrm{Fun}(K(A,n+1),\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)}).

Write
t\colon K(A,n+1)\to*
for the unique such map. Then there is a triple of adjoint functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(K(A,n+1),\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t^{*}}

with left adjoints written above the corresponding right adjoints. The functor
t_{!}
computes the colimit over
K(A,n+1)
, the functor
t_{*}
computes the limit. We claim that
t^{*}
is fully faithful. Indeed, for any
X\in\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)}
the counit map
t_{!}t^{*}(X)\to X
is an equivalence, as can be seen by identifying this map with
X\otimes K(A,n+1)_{+}\to X
and observing that the space
K(A,n+1)
is
T(n)
-acyclic by
2.9
. It now follows that the three adjoints above form part of a recollement (in the sense of
[
25
, Section A.8.1]
), assembling the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Fun}(K(A,n+1),\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)})
out of
\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)}
and its right orthogonal complement
\mathrm{ker}(t_{*})
. Ambidexterity in the
T(n)
-local category provides an equivalence
t_{!}\cong t_{*}
, forcing this recollement to be
split
(see
[
12
, Proposition 4.1.4]
): that is, we find an equivalence of
\infty
-categories

\mathrm{Fun}(K(A,n+1),\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)})\simeq\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)}\times\mathrm{ker}(t_{*}).

This splitting of the category induces a splitting of the unit object of
\mathrm{Fun}(K(A,n+1),\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)})
. The endomorphism ring of this unit is precisely
R
; we take
e
to be the idempotent corresponding to the factor
\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)}
. The desired properties are clear.
∎

The following is the crux of our proof of
5.5
:

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
A
be a torsion abelian group, write
R:=\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}K(A,n)
, and let
X
be a
T(n)
-local spectrum. Then the mapping space
\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)})}(R,\mathrm{Sym}(X))
is contractible. In particular, every map
R\to\mathrm{Sym}(X)
factors canonically over the augmentation
R\to\mathbb{S}_{T(n)}
.

Proof.

Writing
A
as a filtered colimit of finite groups, we reduce to the case where
A
is finite. Consider a map of augmented commutative algebras

. Together with the nilcompletions of these rings, this yields the following commutative square:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Sym}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{S}_{T(n)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}(X).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Here we have applied
5.3
to identify the nilcompletion of
R
. Without loss of generality we can arrange that the telescope
T(n)
is a ring spectrum (e.g. by inverting a
v_{n}
self-map on the endomorphism spectrum of a type
n

\overline{e}\in T(n)_{0}R
for the image of the idempotent

under the Hurewicz map. It follows from

and the square above that the image of

in

is 0. By

, the image of

is therefore 0 as well. It follows that the localization
\mathrm{Sym}(X)[(1-f(e))^{-1}]
vanishes. Since

∎

Proof of
5.5
.

We begin with the critical case
m=n+1
. Our aim is to show that for a spectrum
E
with vanishing
v_{i}
-periodic homotopy groups for
i<n
, the map
\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E\to\tau_{>n}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E
is a
T(n)
-equivalence. Consider the principal fiber sequence

K(\pi_{n+1}E,n)\to\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E\to\tau_{>n}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E.

Set
R:=\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}K(\pi_{n+1}E,n)
. We note that
\pi_{n+1}E
is torsion, since we have assumed the rational homotopy groups of
E
to vanish. The two maps above are infinite loop maps, so in particular we obtain a morphism of commutative
T(n)
-local rings
R\to\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E
. Furthermore, the fiber sequence gives an equivalence

\mathbb{S}_{T(n)}\otimes_{R}\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E\xrightarrow{\cong}\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E.

A result of Kuhn
[
22
, Theorem 2.10, Theorem 2.12]
implies that there is an equivalence of
T(n)
-local commutative rings

\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E\cong\mathrm{Sym}(\Loc_{T(n)}E).

5.7
therefore shows that the ring map
R\to\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E
factors over the augmentation and we obtain equivalences

\mathbb{S}_{T(n)}\otimes_{R}\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E\cong(\mathbb{S}_{T(n)}\otimes_{R}\mathbb{S}_{T(n)})\otimes\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E\cong\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E.

Here we have used
\mathbb{S}_{T(n)}\otimes_{R}\mathbb{S}_{T(n)}\cong\mathbb{S}_{T(n)}
, which we already showed in the proof of
5.3
. It follows that the map
\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E\to\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E
is an equivalence, completing the proof of the case
m=n+1
.

The cases

can now be handled by an easy inductive argument. Indeed, consider the map
\tau_{>m}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E\to\tau_{>m-1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E
. Its fiber is
K(\pi_{m}E,m-1)
. Since
m-1>n
and
\pi_{m}E
is torsion, this space is
T(n)
-acyclic by
2.9
and the map is a
T(n)
-equivalence.
∎

5.3
On the

-localization of infinite loop spaces

In this section we prove

and
1.8
.

.

Let

and

-local spectra with

and let

be a map of pointed spaces (but not necessarily a loop map). If

is a

-equivalence, then

implies that
\tau_{\leq n}f
is an equivalence and

is a

implies that also

is an equivalence.

Conversely, assume that
\tau_{\leq n}f
is an equivalence and

-periodic equivalence at any basepoint. Consider the diagram of fiber sequences

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{>n}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E}

.

. The corollary follows if we prove that

5.4
Generalizations

In the introduction we raised the following question. Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a map of spaces with vanishing
v_{i}
-periodic homotopy groups for
i<n
. If
\tau_{\leq n+1}f
is an equivalence and
f
is a
v_{n}
-periodic equivalence, then does it follow that
f
is a parametric
T(n)
-equivalence? In this section we indicate how some of the techniques we have used for infinite loop spaces might be adapted to approach this.

In fact, the proof of
1.6
would go through essentially unchanged if we could show that for a pointed space
X
with vanishing
v_{i}
-periodic homotopy groups for
i<n
, the map
\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X\to\tau_{>n}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X
is a
T(n)
-equivalence (cf.
5.5
). Consider the fiber sequence of loop maps

K(\pi_{n+2}X,n)\to\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X\to\tau_{>n}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X.

This sequence in particular yields a map of augmented
T(n)
-local
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-rings

R:=\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}K(\pi_{n+2}X,n)\xrightarrow{f}\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X

and an equivalence

\mathbb{S}_{T(n)}\otimes_{R}\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X.

If
f
factors over the augmentation
R\to\Loc_{T(n)}\mathbb{S}
, then the argument in the proof of
5.5
goes through to show that
\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X\to\tau_{>n}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X
is a
T(n)
-equivalence.

The
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Alg}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)})
of augmented
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebras also admits a nilcompletion tower (see
[
19
, Section 9]
): for any augmented
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-ring
R
there is a tower
\{t_{k}X\}_{k\geq 1}
under
R
of which the associated graded is the free
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebra on the
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-cotangent fiber (or
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-indecomposables)
\mathrm{cot}_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}(R)
of
R
. According to
[
17
, Remark 2.14]
, this object agrees up to a shift with the bar construction:
\mathrm{cot}_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}(R)\simeq\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{-1}\mathrm{Bar}(R)
. In particular, we obtain the following analog of
5.3
:

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
A
be a torsion abelian group and consider the augmented
T(n)
-local
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebra
R:=\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}K(A,n)
. Then the augmentation
R\to\mathbb{S}_{T(n)}
exhibits the unit as the nilcompletion (in the sense of
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebras) of
R
.

To imitate the argument we gave in the case of infinite loop spaces, we would now need to know that the
T(n)
-homology of
\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X
injects into the
T(n)
-homology of its nilcompletion. For an infinite loop space
X
, we identified the
T(n)
-localization of this suspension spectrum with a free commutative algebra. In the case of a general loop space the situation is not so straightforward, but a useful observation is that the result only depends on the
v_{n}
-periodic homotopy type of
X
. In fact, in
5.9
we will identify
\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X
with the universal enveloping algebra of a spectral Lie algebra naturally associated with
X
.

In the following we use that the values of the Bousfield–Kuhn functor
\Phi_{n}(X)
naturally come equipped with the structure of a spectral Lie algebra
[
18
]
. Moreover, the stabilization of the
\infty
-category of spectral Lie algebras yields a left adjoint functor that we denote

\mathrm{CE}\colon\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)})\to\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)},

since it is a version of the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex appropriate to this context. We write
\mathrm{CE}_{+}
for the functor
\mathbb{S}_{T(n)}\oplus\mathrm{CE}
. The relevance of this functor is that (analogously to
\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}
) it admits a symmetric monoidal structure with respect to the cartesian product on
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)})
and the smash product on
\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)}
. In particular, it sends the group object
\Phi_{n}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X)
in
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)})
to an
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebra in
\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)}
.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
X
be a pointed space with vanishing
v_{i}
-periodic homotopy groups for
i<n
. Then there is a natural equivalence of
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebras

\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X\simeq\mathrm{CE}_{+}(\Phi_{n}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X)).

Proof.

This statement is a fairly direct consequence of the work in
[
18
]
, but not explicitly stated there. Let us recall the relevant ingredients. Fix a finite type
n+1
suspension
V_{n+1}
. Let
d
be the smallest natural number so that
\pi_{d}V_{n+1}\neq 0
and write
\mathscr{L}_{n}^{f}
for the full subcategory of
\mathbf{P}_{V_{n+1}}\mathscr{S}_{*}
consisting of
d
-connected spaces. (By
[
18
, Theorem 3.7]
, the
\infty
-category
\mathscr{L}_{n}^{f}
is precisely the localization of the
\infty
-category of
d
-connected spaces at the maps inducing
v_{i}
-periodic equivalences for
i\leq n
.) Write
\mathscr{M}_{n}^{f}
for the full subcategory of
\mathscr{L}_{n}^{f}
on spaces with trivial
v_{i}
-periodic homotopy groups for
i<n
. (According to
[
18
, Section 3.2]
, the
\infty
-category
\mathscr{M}_{n}^{f}
is equivalent to
\mathscr{S}_{v_{n}}
.) By
[
18
, Proposition 3.19]
, the functor
\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}\colon\mathscr{M}_{n}^{f}\to\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)}
exhibits
\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)}
as the stabilization of
\mathscr{M}_{n}^{f}
.
[
18
, Theorem 2.6]
shows that the Bousfield–Kuhn functor gives an equivalence of
\infty
-categories
\Phi_{n}\colon\mathscr{M}_{n}^{f}\to\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)})
. The Chevalley–Eilenberg functor was defined as the stabilization of
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)})
, so that we arrive at a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{M}_{n}^{f}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)}.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{Sp}_{T(n)})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CE}_{+}}

Note that we have added basepoints both on
\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}
and
\mathrm{CE}
here. Now, let
X
be as in the statement of the proposition. Then the map
\tau_{>d}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X\to\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X
is a

-equivalence by the same straightforward induction on the Postnikov tower used in the proof of
5.5
. The space
\mathbf{P}_{V_{n+1}}\tau_{>d}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X
lies in the subcategory
\mathscr{M}_{n}^{f}
(where we use
[
18
, Proposition 3.6]
to see that this space is still

-connected). We conclude that there is a string of equivalences

\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>d}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X\simeq\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\mathbf{P}_{V_{n+1}}\tau_{>d}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X\simeq\mathrm{CE}_{+}(\Phi_{n}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X)),

where we have used the commutativity of the preceding diagram for the second equivalence and
3.13
(1) for the first equivalence.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

According to
[
1
, Theorem 1.8]
, the
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebra
\mathrm{CE}_{+}(\Phi_{n}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X))
can also be interpreted as the universal enveloping algebra of the spectral Lie algebra
\Phi_{n}(X)
.

Putting the ingredients together, we get the following extension of
5.5
:

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
X
be a pointed space with vanishing
v_{i}
-periodic homotopy groups for
i<n
. If the nilcompletion map (in the sense of
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebras) of
\mathrm{CE}_{+}(\Phi_{n}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X))
induces an injection on
T(n)
-homology, then the map
\tau_{>n+1}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X\to\tau_{>n}\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X
is a
T(n)
-equivalence of spaces.

To conclude, let us briefly point out two examples where
5.11
applies. If
X=\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}^{\infty}E
is an infinite loop space, then the
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-nilcompletion and
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-nilcompletion of
\Loc_{T(n)}\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{\infty}_{+}\tau_{>n+1}X\simeq\mathrm{Sym}(L_{T(n)}E)
are related as in the following diagram:

\mathrm{Sym}(\Loc_{T(n)}E)\to\mathrm{Sym}(\Loc_{T(n)}E)^{\wedge}_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}\to\mathrm{Sym}(\Loc_{T(n)}E)^{\wedge}_{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}.

The first map is the
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-nilcompletion, the composite of the two is the
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-nilcompletion. The composite is therefore injective on
T(n)
-homology, as observed in the proof of
5.5
, and hence the first map is injective on
T(n)
-homology as well. A second example is the case where
X
is a pointed space for which spectral Lie algebra
\Phi_{n}(X)
is free. (This happens, for example, when there exists a type
n
complex
V_{n}
with
v_{n}
self-map so that a highly connected cover of
X
is
T(n)
-equivalent to
\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{2}V
, cf.
[
18
, Theorem 2.13]
.) In that case its universal enveloping algebra is a free
T(n)
-local
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebra:

\mathrm{CE}_{+}(\Phi_{n}(\operatorname{\Omega}\mathopen{}X))\cong\Loc_{T(n)}\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(\operatorname{\Sigma}\mathopen{}^{-1}\mathrm{CE}(\Phi_{n}(X)))^{\otimes k}.

The
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-nilcompletion of this algebra is the natural map from direct sum to product, which is injective on
T(n)
-homology as in the proof of
5.2
.
