PHAROS: PROMETHIUM HIGH-PRESSURE AUTOMATED
RECOVERY WITH OPTIMIZED SEPARATION
PI: Benjamin Roach

Motivation and Impact
Promethium-147 is a valuable low-energy beta emitter with key applications in advanced technologies,
including nuclear batteries, luminescent devices, and defense-related microelectronics. Despite its
importance, domestic availability of 147Pm is limited, and its isolation from fission product waste streams
is technically challenging because of its low concentration and chemical similarity to neighboring
lanthanides—particularly Nd and Sm. The PHAROS project—Promethium High-Pressure Automated
Recovery with Optimized Separation—addresses these challenges by developing an integrated, automated
high-pressure ion chromatography (HPIC) platform for efficient, loss-free recovery of high-purity 147Pm.
The system combines a custom high-capacity HPIC column, advanced pumps, and a loss-free automated
sample handling unit. This closed-loop setup also minimizes cross contamination and waste generation,
enabling robust and reproducible separation of 147Pm at the milligram scale. In FY 2025, PHAROS
focused on optimizing HPIC methods for resolving Nd and Sm, testing multiple resin types and eluents,
and then employing 147Pm tracer materials to validate efficient Pm isolation. A number of high-capacity
(~500 mg) columns were tested using developed protocols and surrogate feeds. By incorporating
innovations such as coupling HPIC with ICP–mass spectrometry (MS) for real-time monitoring, the
resultant eluant and resin combination showed high-resolution separation of Pm, with optimized Nd–Pm,
Pm–Sm, and Pm–Cm delta values of up to 20 min, laying the groundwork for batch test implementation.

Methods
The HPIC–ICP-MS method combines a Thermo Scientific Dionex ICS-5000+ HPIC system with a
Thermo Scientific iCAP RQ quadrupole ICP-MS. Chromatographic separations used 10–200 µL
polyether ether ketone (PEEK) injection loops, an IonPac CG5A guard column (50 × 4 mm inner
diameter), and an IonPac CS5A analytical column (250 × 4 mm inner diameter, 9 µm particle substrate,
40 microequivalents per column). The eluent flow rates were 1.0–2.0 mL/min. Eluents were prepared
from ultrapure water (18.2 MΩ·cm) and trace-metal-grade reagents, including HCl (2.0 M) and oxalic
acid (150 mM, pH 4.6), buffered to a final pH (4.7–4.8) with NH4OH. The coupled HPIC–ICP-MS
system is housed in a radiological fume hood to safely analyze highly radioactive samples (up to 500 mR
on contact).

The mid-capacity resin columns (2.1 mm I.D. PEEK housings, VICI AG International) were loaded with
specific resins (or mixtures thereof) N,N,N’,N’-tetra-n-octyldiglycolamide, DGA Resin, Normal; or
N,N,N’,N’-tetra-2-ethylhexyldiglycolamide, DGA Resin, Branched; MP1, and AG50 resins (50–100 µm
particle size; Eichrom Technologies and Bio-Rad Laboratories) and preconditioned with sequential rinses
of ultrapure water, 0.5 M HNO₃, and 0.1 M HCl to remove contaminants and equilibrate the resin volume,
this was repeated until the column body was at capacity and the pressure stabilized. Typical loadings were
5–10 ug/ analyte, with pressure maintained below 2000 psi and retention times reproducible within ~2%
RSD. Columns were assembled with low-dead-volume fittings (PEEK, VICI AG International) to
minimize band broadening, and eluents were introduced to the ICP-MS through a high-throughput
nebulizer (PEEK, <2 mL/min flow) and quartz cyclonic spray chamber. When necessary, PEEK
backpressure loops were employed to increase the backpressure in the column body, resulting in
improved signal stability and elution time reproducibility. The iCAP RQ was tuned daily for sensitivity
(>5 × 105 cps/ppb at 115In), oxide ratios <3% (CeO⁺/Ce⁺), and doubly charged ions <2% (Ba²⁺/Ba⁺).
Calibration used multi-element standards (High Purity Standards) prepared in matched matrices, with
periodic blanks and resin-only controls verifying low carryover and consistent retention.

Results
The PHAROS team successfully installed and operationalized a nonradioactive ICP-MS and HPIC
system, fully training key staff. Analytical columns using novel and existing resins such as N,N,N’,N’-
tetra-n-octyldiglycolamide (DGA Resin, Normal) or N,N,N’,N’-tetra-2-ethylhexyldiglycolamide (DGA
Resin, Branched) were developed, fabricated, and tested, demonstrating effective lanthanide separations.
Key achievements included developing a simplified, isocratic elution profile using a single eluent (oxalic
acid); validating elution times of 147Pm and 248Cm tracers using a radiation-capable analogous system with
the developed isocratic oxalic acid gradient; and isolating 147Pm with greater than 99% recovery with
chemical purity exceeding 99.95%. A larger-capacity HPIC column was fabricated, tested at higher flow

This simplified, glovebox-compatible separation protocol significantly improved baseline resolution
between lanthanides. Challenges such as precipitation during initial shake tests with buffered oxalic acid
and difficulties in particle size determination methods have led to investigations into alternative organic
acids and improved sizing techniques. Next steps include implementing the pilot system in a radiological
fume hood, preliminarily testing a loss-free automated injection system, and coupling it with the higher-
capacity columns for fraction collection.
