Oscillon Formation in Palatini Modified Gravity Theories
and Sukanta Panda

Abstract
We investigate the formation of spatially localized, oscillatory in time and non topological solitonic, quasi-stable energy configurations, Oscillons, which are formed at the end of Inflationary epoch, during the preheating phase and decay over long periods of time. Oscillons have been previously studied in literature in the regime of General Relativity using Metric Formalism. In this paper we look for formation of these energy lumps by modifying the gravity part of the Einstein Hilbert Action, considering a non minimal coupling of the scalar field with Ricci Scalar, R R , and working in an alternative formulation of General Relativity known as Palatini Formalism . The potential we consider is of polynomial form. We demonstrate numerically, using Cosmo ℒ \mathcal{L} attice , that the equation governing the dynamics of the Inflaton scalar field give oscillatory and decaying solutions as it is expected in the case of Oscillons, with power spectrum governing the growth of perturbations of k k modes. The equation of state reveals an extended period of Oscillon domination in the early universe. Along with this, the Primordial Gravitational Wave spectrum due to asymmetric distribution of these energy configurations have also been studied. We observe that these generate Ultra-High Frequency regime Gravitational Waves, which lie in the range of the planned future detectors and experiments.

The Model

2.1
Action

We formulate a Palatini inflation model in
f(R,\Phi)
theory where
\Phi
is the Inflaton scalar field and
R
is the curvature scalar
[
28
,
66
]
. As of now we don’t define the explicit form for
f(R,\Phi)
and keep it general, but it is a non linear function of
R
.
We start with a general action in Jordan frame of the form :

S=\int\sqrt{-g}[\frac{1}{2}f(R,\Phi)-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial_{\nu}\Phi-V(\Phi)]d^{4}x+S_{m}(g_{\mu\nu},\psi).

(2.1)

where
g_{\mu\nu}
is the metric and
g
is its determinant,
R
is the Ricci Scalar defined as
R=g^{\alpha\beta}R_{\alpha\beta}(\Gamma)
in Palatini formalism. The matter action
S_{m}
consists of the other matter components of the universe.

The non linearity of
f(R,\Phi)
makes the gravitational equations of motion complicated to simplify it we introduce an auxiliary field
\phi
to linearize the action in R and this and brings us to the Scalar-Tensor theory which modifies the action as follows:

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{2}f(\phi,\Phi)+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(\phi,\Phi)(R-\phi)-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial_{\nu}\Phi-V(\Phi)\right]

(2.2)

where
f^{\prime}(\phi,\Phi)=\frac{\partial f(\phi,\Phi)}{\partial\phi}
.

As the auxiliary field,
\phi
, wont generate any kinetic terms so it would also not produce any equations of motion, instead it would give a constraint equation if we vary the action with respect to
\phi
:

\delta\mathbf{S}=0\implies\phi=R\quad\text{for}\hskip 14.22636pt\frac{\partial^{2}f(\phi,\Phi)}{\partial\phi^{2}}\neq 0

(2.3)

Using this in (
2.2
) we recover our original action (
2.1
). Rearranging (
2.2
) gives :

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(\phi,\Phi)R-W(\phi,\Phi)-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial_{\nu}\Phi-V(\Phi)\right]

(2.4)

where
W(\phi,\Phi)=\frac{1}{2}\phi f^{\prime}(\phi,\Phi)-\frac{1}{2}f(\phi,\Phi)
. We emphasize on the epoch where the coupling between the inflaton field and matter (radiation) is weak
[
32
]
. So we can effectively ignore the matter action in (
2.1
).
In this case, a single scalar field
\Phi
, that is non-minimally coupled to gravity is described by the action (
2.4
). The Weyl (conformal) transformation
[
69
]
is used to produce a minimally coupled scalar field in the Einstein frame. The transformation can be expressed as follows and is dependent on both
\Phi
and
\phi

g_{\mu\nu}\xrightarrow{}f^{\prime}(\phi,\Phi)g_{\mu\nu}

(2.5)

Applying this transformation the action obtained in the Einstein frame can be written as :

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{2}R-\frac{1}{2f^{\prime}(\phi,\Phi)}\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial^{\mu}\Phi-\frac{W(\phi,\Phi)+V(\Phi)}{f^{\prime}(\phi,\Phi)^{2}}\right]

(2.6)

Let us define a new potential :

\hat{V}(\phi,\Phi)=\frac{W(\phi,\Phi)+V(\Phi)}{f^{\prime}(\phi,\Phi)^{2}}

(2.7)

Here we consider our coupling function to be
f(R,\Phi)=G(\Phi)(R+\alpha R^{2})
. Accordingly the potential gets modified as:

\hat{V}=\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(\phi,\Phi)^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{8\alpha G(\Phi)}[f^{\prime}(\phi,\Phi)-G(\Phi)]^{2}+V(\Phi)\right]

(2.8)

Now varying the action equation (
2.6
) with respect to
\phi
and equating to
0
we get the new constraint equation to be:

f^{\prime}(\phi,\Phi)=\frac{8\alpha V(\Phi)+G(\Phi)}{1-2\alpha\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial^{\mu}\Phi}

(2.9)

Substituting equation (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) into the action equation (
2.6
), we get the new action to be

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+\frac{X}{2(8\alpha V+G)}+X^{2}\frac{2\alpha}{(8\alpha V+G)}-\frac{V}{G(8\alpha V+G)}\right]

(2.10)

where
X=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial^{\mu}\Phi
. The potential is taken to be
V(\Phi)=\frac{m^{2}\Phi^{2}}{2}-\frac{\lambda\Phi^{4}}{4}+\frac{\sigma\Phi^{6}}{6m^{2}}
(the reason for this particular choice is described in section
4
and the coupling function is
G(\Phi)=1+\zeta\Phi^{2}
.

The values of the parameters are fixed as :
m=5\times 10^{-6}M_{pl}
,
\lambda=1\times 10^{-7}
,
\sigma=1\times 10^{-14}
,
\alpha=0.005
,
\zeta=50000

This is the final action in Einstein frame which we will work with in the rest of the paper. Notice that the
R^{2}
terms have been translated into the quartic kinetic term
X^{2}
. The last term is the effective potential in Einstein frame
U(\Phi)
. We arrive at non-canonical quartic kinetic terms by moving from the modified gravity action in the Jordan frame to the Einstein frame via a Weyl transformation.
The time of hyperkination
[
87
]
, which can happen immediately following inflation and before to kination, is caused by quartic kinetic terms, a distinctive characteristic of
R^{2}
model.

We can canonicalize the action by introducing a field redefinition given by :

\frac{d\chi}{d\Phi}=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\alpha V(\Phi)+G(\Phi)}}

(2.11)

Hence we obtain the more familiar Canonical form of action as :

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+X+2\alpha\left[8\alpha V(\chi)+G(\chi)\right]\frac{X^{2}}{2}-U(\chi)\right]

(2.12)

where

(2.13)

\displaystyle U(\chi)

\displaystyle=\frac{V(\chi)}{G(\chi)\big[(8\alpha V(\chi)+G(\chi))\big]}

(2.14)

We will mostly use the Non-Canonical Action (
2.10
) unless otherwise stated.

2.2
Energy-Momentum Tensor

We can write the action (
2.10
) as :

\textbf{S}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}\right]

(2.15)

where the
\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}
is the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}=-\frac{\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial^{\mu}\Phi}{2(8\alpha V+G)}+\frac{\alpha(\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial^{\mu}\Phi)^{2}}{2(8\alpha V+G)}-\frac{V}{G(8\alpha V+G)}

(2.16)

We consider the flat FLRW metric :

g_{\mu\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0&0\\
0&a(t)^{2}&0&0\\
0&0&a(t)^{2}&0\\
0&0&0&a(t)^{2}\end{pmatrix}

By principle of least action,
\delta\textbf{S}=0
, we get

\frac{-1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}R+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta R}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}=\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}-\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}

(2.17)

The RHS of (
2.17
) is, by definition, Energy Momentum (Stress-Energy) Tensor
T_{\mu\nu}
. which now takes the form as follows :

T_{\mu\nu}=-2\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}+g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}

(2.18)

Substituting the Lagrangian and performing the differentiation we get :

T_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1+4\alpha X}{8\alpha V+G}\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial_{\nu}\Phi+g_{\mu\nu}\left[\frac{X}{(8\alpha V+G)}+X^{2}\frac{2\alpha}{(8\alpha V+G)}-\frac{V}{G(8\alpha V+G)}\right]

(2.19)

Now to compute the pressure and Energy density we consider the perfect fluid ansatz for which the Energy-Momentum tensor is given by :

T_{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+pg_{\mu\nu}

(2.20)

The invariant square of the 4-velocity of a perfect fluid is normalized to -1,
u_{\mu}u^{\mu}=-1
. For a scalar field acting as a perfect fluid, the 4-velocity is normalized by the gradient of the field :

u_{\mu}=\frac{\partial_{\mu}\Phi}{\sqrt{2X}}

(2.21)

In our metric signature for a time dependent cosmological background,
\Phi=\Phi(t)
, the kinetic term
X=-\frac{1}{2}g^{00}\dot{\Phi}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\Phi}^{2}
is strictly positive, ensuring the term in the square root is real and positive. Hence we get :

u_{\mu}u_{\nu}=\frac{\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial_{\nu}\Phi}{2X}

(2.22)

Mapping the terms of (
2.19
) and (
2.20
) we obtain :

p=\frac{X}{8\alpha V+G}+X^{2}\frac{2\alpha}{8\alpha V+G}-\frac{V}{G(8\alpha V+G)}

(2.23)

and

\rho+p=\frac{8\alpha X^{2}+2X}{8\alpha V+G}

(2.24)

Extracting the Energy Density

\rho=\frac{6\alpha X^{2}+X}{2(8\alpha V+G)}+\frac{V}{G(8\alpha V+G)}

(2.25)

The Cosmological Equation of State (EOS) parameter is defined as :

\ \mathcal{\omega}=\frac{p}{\rho}\implies\mathcal{\omega}=\frac{2\alpha GX^{2}+GX-V}{6\alpha GX^{2}+GX+V}

(2.26)

For a homogeneous scalar field :

\mathcal{\omega}=\frac{\alpha G\dot{\Phi}^{4}+G\dot{\Phi}^{2}-2V}{3\alpha G\dot{\Phi}^{4}+G\dot{\Phi}^{2}+2V}

(2.27)

This parameter will transition between the following different regimes depending upon the form of the potential and coupling as well as the value of the scalar field. Broadly they can be described as follows :

•

Vaccum Energy/Slow Roll limit (
\dot{\Phi}^{2}\xrightarrow{}0
)
: The kinetic energy of the field is negligible compared to its potential.
\omega\approx-1
. This mimics the nature of Cosmological Constant, driving accelerated Cosmic expansion.

•

Canonical Kinetic Domination (
\alpha\xrightarrow{}0
and
\dot{\Phi}^{2}>>V
)
:
\omega\approx 1
. This corresponds to a stiff fluid where the speed of sound is equal to speed of light.

•

Non Canonical/High Energy Limit (
\alpha\dot{\Phi}^{4}>>\dot{\Phi}^{2}
)
: This is the ultra kinetic region where the
X^{2}
term dominates and governs the dynamics. Here
\omega\approx\frac{1}{3}
. In this limit the scalar field behaves like a radiation fluid.

3
Linear Stability Analysis and Floquet Theory

Tachyonic resonance plays an important role in the growth of perturbations during Inflationary preheating leading to the necessity of investigating the instability bands in the momentum space of comoving
k
modes and to study their trajectory as they pass through these instability bands. Floquet Theory can be efficiently used to perform a linear stability analysis of the Inflaton fluctuations
\delta\chi_{k}
. We identify the instability bands by applying the Floquet Theory. The detailed mathematical approach to Floquet theory can be found in the Appendices.

3.1
Linear Perturbation Theory for Fluctuations

The Klein-Gordon equation, ignoring the
X^{2}
term in the action (reason argued later in section
4
), for the canonical field
\chi
, in Einstein Frame, governing the full Non-linear dynamics of the background homogeneous field and the fluctuations is given by :

\ddot{\chi}+3H\dot{\chi}-\frac{1}{a^{2}}\nabla^{2}\chi+\frac{dU}{d\chi}=0

(3.1)

We can split the field as
\chi(t,x)=\chi_{0}(t)+\delta\chi(t,x)
. The homogeneous background will satisfy the spatially averaged equation :

\ddot{\chi}_{0}+3H\dot{\chi}_{0}+\frac{dU}{d\chi}|_{\chi_{0}}=0

(3.2)

and the perturbations upto linear order in
\delta\chi
satisfy :

\delta\ddot{\chi}+3H\delta\dot{\chi}-\frac{\nabla^{2}\delta\chi}{a^{2}}+\frac{d^{2}U}{d\chi^{2}}|_{\chi_{0}}\delta\chi=0

(3.3)

This is the linearized perturbation equation in real space.

Now performing Fourier decomposition and transforming to Momentum space using :

\delta\chi(t,x)=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta\chi_{k}(t)e^{i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{x}}

(3.4)

We obtain the
k
space version of (
3.3
) :

\delta\ddot{\chi}_{k}+3H\delta\dot{\chi}_{k}+\left[\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+m^{2}_{eff}(t)\right]\delta\chi_{k}=0

(3.5)

where the time dependent
Effective Mass Squared
term is given by :

m^{2}_{eff}(t)=\frac{d^{2}U}{d\chi^{2}}|_{\chi_{0}(t)}

(3.6)

Initial Conditions (Bunch Davies Vaccum)
: The vaccum fluctuations that seed the subsequent instability takes this form -

\delta\chi_{k}(0)=\frac{1}{a\sqrt{2k}}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\delta\dot{\chi}_{k}(0)=\frac{-ik}{a}\delta\chi_{k}(0)

(3.7)

Flat Space limit
: For our values of model parameters we have
\frac{H_{end}}{m}\approx 8.96\times 10^{-4}<<1
so we are almost in the Minkowski space so setting
a=1
and neglecting the Hubble friction,
3H
, term, the equation (
3.5
) simplified to :

\delta\ddot{\chi}_{k}+\omega^{2}_{k}(t)\delta\chi_{k}=0

(3.8)

with
\omega^{2}_{k}(t)=k^{2}+\frac{d^{2}U}{d\chi^{2}}|_{\chi_{0}(t)}
where we have neglected metric perturbations and this type of equation is known as
Hill’s Differential Equation

[
76
]
, which according to Floquet’s Theorem have solutions of the form :

\delta\chi_{k}(t)=e^{\mu_{k}}\mathcal{P}_{(+)}(t)+e^{-\mu_{k}}\mathcal{P}_{(-)}(t)

(3.9)

\mu_{k}
in the exponents are called Floquet exponents and they govern how the
k
modes vary with time. The functions
\mathcal{P}_{(\pm)}(t)
are functions with period same as that of the oscillation of the background field
\chi_{0}(t)
. There are effectively three regimes which can be identified based on the nature of Real part of
\mu_{k}
,
\mathcal{R}(\mu_{k})
:

•

\mathcal{R}(\mu_{k})>0
: Exponential growth, Unstable.

•

\mathcal{R}(\mu_{k})=0
,
\mathcal{I}m(\mu_{k})\neq 0
: Oscillatory, Stable

•

\mathcal{R}(\mu_{k})=0
,
\mathcal{I}m(\mu_{k})=0
: Secular Growth, Resonance boundary

Effective Mass Squared
m^{2}_{eff}
: We make use of the chain rule to find
\frac{d^{2}U}{d\chi^{2}}
, which ultimately gives :

m^{2}_{eff}=G(\Phi)\frac{d^{2}U}{d\Phi^{2}}+\frac{G^{\prime}(\Phi)}{2}\frac{dU}{d\Phi}

(3.10)

Tachyonic Region
: The instability occurs when
m^{2}_{eff}<0
. From equation (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) we can imply that (which also follows from
[
84
]
), the maximum unstable wavenumber at a given instant is :

k_{tach}=\sqrt{|m^{2}_{eff}(\chi_{0}(t))|}

(3.11)

Only modes with
k<k_{tach}
are unstable at that instant.

Background Solutions and Oscillation Period
: In Minkowski limit (
3.2
) reduces to that of a Simple Harmonic Oscillator, and its first integral gives the Energy Conservation equation :

\frac{1}{2}\dot{\chi}^{2}_{0}+U(\chi_{0})=E=\text{Constant}

(3.12)

Now we assume the end of inflation and start of simulation as
t=0
, so at this moment

\chi_{0}(t=0)=\chi_{end}
and
\dot{\chi}_{0}(t=0)=0
.

The second initial condition can be justified by the following reasoning : Think of this system analogous to that of pendulum where
\chi_{end}
represents the value at the point of maximum displacement of the bob (Inflaton in our case). So when we release the pendulum at
t=0
, its initial velocity is zero.
So the initial total energy of the system is
E=U(\chi_{end})
and this remains constant throughout as there is no damping term. So we can write (
3.12
) as

\frac{1}{2}\dot{\chi}^{2}_{0}+U(\chi_{0})=U(\chi_{end})

(3.13)

so the background solutions at any general time
t
are given by :

\dot{\chi}_{0}=\pm\sqrt{2\left[U(\chi_{end})-U(\chi_{0})\right]}

(3.14)

For symmetric potential as in our case the time period of oscillation
T
(complete period from positive to negative end) is obtained by solving the integral :

T(\chi_{end})=4\int_{0}^{\chi_{end}}\frac{d\chi}{\sqrt{2\left[U(\chi_{end})-U(\chi_{0})\right]}}

(3.15)

3.2
Floquet Theory

We can now solve (
3.8
) by writing it as a system of coupled first order differential equation in matrix form :

\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}\delta\chi_{k}\\
\delta\pi_{k}\end{pmatrix}=\mathbf{U}(t)\begin{pmatrix}\delta\chi_{k}\\
\delta\pi_{k}\end{pmatrix},

(3.16)

where

\delta\pi_{k}\equiv\dot{\delta\chi}_{k},

(3.17)

and

\mathbf{U}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-k^{2}-m_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}(t)&0\end{pmatrix}.

(3.18)

Now we define two orthogonal initial conditions :

\{\delta\chi_{k}^{(1)}(0)=1,\hskip 2.84544pt\delta\pi_{k}^{(1)}(0)=0\}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{and}\hskip 8.5359pt\{\delta\chi_{k}^{(2)}(0)=0,\hskip 2.84544pt\delta\pi_{k}^{(2)}(0)=1\}

(3.19)

To solve this system we evolve it from
t=0
to
T
using a method known as
Matrix Monodromy Method
. In this section we only provide the final expressions for the Floquet exponents

Significant scalar density perturbations, as those linked to the oscillon number density fluctuations, can generate tensor perturbations, specifically gravitational waves, at the second order in cosmic perturbation theory.

Three potential scenarios exist for the generation of Gravitational Waves in the early universe dominated by Oscillons: formation, a stable oscillon phase, and decay followed by the thermalization of the universe. In General Relativity, a spherically symmetric object cannot produce gravitational waves, as it lacks a time-varying quadrupole moment

Summary and Discussion

In this paper we have established a detailed framework to study Oscillons that are spatially localized, oscillatory in time , pseudo-stable excitations (energy configurations) of the inflaton scalar field formed during preheating stage of universe, just after inflation.We considered a modified gravity model in Palatini formalism of General Relativity, with a non minimal coupling of the scalar field with gravity, with the coupling function of the form

f(R,\Phi)
. We consider an oscillatory, polynomial (sextic) potential with an attractive non-linear term which suffices the condition required for the formation of Oscillons. Subsequently we introduced the strategy of Stability Analysis and
Floquet theory to observe which momentum modes actually grow owing to tachyonic instability and eventually give rise to Oscillons. We implemented the numerical technique of
3+1
Classical Lattice Simulations using
Cosmo
\mathcal{L}
attice
. It was observed that when the fluctuations become comparable to the mean field values, the Inflaton fragments and looses its oscillatory nature with the increase in Gradient energy being one of the reasons along with Tachyonic instability. For our particular choice of model parameters, the Power spectrum reveals that low momentum modes are excited at early times and a sharp enhancement peak is observed for
k\lesssim 0.8m
. The cycle averaged value of the equation of state,
\bar{\omega}
, after rendering close to
0
at initial times, stabilizes to a constant value throughout the remaining time of simulation.

Additionally, we have demonstrated that these structures can generate a substantial gravitational wave signal, offering a potential observational avenue for this inflationary model.We conclude that the amplitude is commensurable with the sensitivity of current GWs experiments but the frequency is
\sim O(GHz)
(Ultra-High frequency regime) far away from those of the present day detectors. But the characteristic Strain and Frequency lies well within the range of the proposed future Gravitational Wave Detectors.

This work constitutes a preliminary effort toward understanding the Oscillon dynamics in preheating scenarios; nonetheless, there are some elements that our analysis did not adequately address, along with approximations utilized, which require mention :

1.

Oscillon Lifetime : Knowing the lifespan of Oscillons is crucial for accurately assessing the inflationary observables to be compared with empirical data. Furthermore, Oscillons may produce gravitational waves upon collapse, with extended lives correlating to increased probabilities of detecting gravitational waves within the frequency ranges of existing and next gravitational wave studies. Furthermore, if sufficiently stable, oscillons may ultimately cluster over extended time periods
[
11
]
.

2.

Gravitational Effects and Interactions : Given that Oscillons are well-localized entities with significant overdensities, it is reasonable to question if these quasi-spherical formations could plausibly collapse into Primordial black holes
[
58
]
, a scenario not considered in our simulations, which entirely neglect metric perturbation. Our methodology models the late-time behavior of the system by considering the Oscillons as isolated entities, hence disregarding any possible interactions that can induce deformations or subtle alterations in the energy profile.

3.

Towards Reheating Mechanism : A minimal amount of radiation is necessary post-inflation to reheat the universe. Our model not inherently result in radiation domination from the depletion of energy density of the scalar field to levels below that of radiation. To facilitate reheating, we can offer a framework guided by
[
63
]
and followed by
[
66
]
. Sub-dominant vacuum fluctuations grow significantly post-inflation, resulting in particle production. The particles created at the completion of inflation alters the Lagrangian by incorporating interaction terms between the scalar field and the particles. These interaction factors are additionally accountable for the disintegration of the scalar field into those particles. This decay reduces the energy density of the field, facilitating the necessary radiation domination for effective reheating.

Matrix Monodromy Method

This method
[
52
]
is standard technique implemented while solving Hill’s Differential Equation in matrix form as given in equation (
3.16
). It is the
2\times 2
linear system with coefficient matrix being periodic,
U(t+T)=U(t)
. The matrix
U(t)
has two notable properties :

•

tr(U)=0

•

U
is real

A general solution of the matrix system can be proposed by the following ansatz :

\delta(t)=M(t)\delta(0)

(A.1)

where
M(t)
is the fundamental matrix solution often called the
propagator matrix
satisfying the following :

\dot{M}(t)=U(t)M(t)\hskip 11.38092pt\text{with}\hskip 11.38092ptM(0)=1

(A.2)

The propagator carries all the information about the evolution of any initial state.
By Liouvilles Theorem, we have the determinant of
M(t)
conserved as
tr(U)=0
from which we obtain :

\frac{d|M(t)|}{dt}=tr(U)\cdot|M(t)|=0

(A.3)

giving the constraint equation :

det\hskip 0.56905ptM(t)=det\hskip 0.85358ptM(0)=det\hskip 0.85358ptI=1\hskip 11.38092pt\forall\hskip 1.42262ptt

(A.4)

This implies the two eigen values are always reciprocals of each other.

The
Monodromy Matrix
is the propagator evaluated at exactly one full period :

\textbf{M}\equiv\textbf{M}(T)=\begin{pmatrix}M_{11}&M_{12}\\
M_{21}&M_{22}\end{pmatrix}

(A.5)

with
det\hskip 0.85358ptM=M_{11}M_{22}-M_{12}M_{21}=1

Due to periodicity, after
n
full periods we get :

\textbf{M}(nT)=\textbf{M}(T)^{n}=\textbf{M}^{n}

(A.6)

Using the initial conditions (
3.19
) we can construct the full Monodromy matrix as :

M=\begin{pmatrix}\delta\chi_{k}^{(1)}(T)&\delta\chi_{k}^{(2)}(T)\\
\delta\pi_{k}^{(1)}(T)&\delta\pi_{k}^{(2)}(T)\end{pmatrix}

(A.7)

Simultaneously integrating both the solutions as a
4\times 4
system :

\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}\delta\chi^{(1)}\\
\delta\pi^{(1)}\\
\delta\chi^{(2)}\\
\delta\pi^{(2)}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&0\\
-\omega_{k}^{2}&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&1\\
0&0&-\omega_{k}^{2}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\delta\chi^{(1)}\\
\delta\pi^{(1)}\\
\delta\chi^{(2)}\\
\delta\pi^{(2)}\end{pmatrix}.

(A.8)

Floquet Multipliers
: Eigen values of
M
. Floquet theorem states that if
\delta(0)
is an eigen vector of
M
with eigenvalue
\lambda_{m}
, then :

\boldsymbol{\delta}(nT)=\mathbf{M}^{n}\boldsymbol{\delta}(0)=\lambda^{n}\boldsymbol{\delta}(0)

(A.9)

The eigenvalues
\lambda_{m\pm}
are called
Floquet multipliers
with charactersitic equation as :

\lambda_{m}^{2}-(M_{11}+M_{22})\,\lambda_{m}+1=0

(A.10)

The solutions are:

\lambda_{m\pm}=\frac{M_{11}+M_{22}}{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{M_{11}+M_{22}}{2}\right)^{2}-1}

(A.11)

The Floquet exponent can be extracted as :

\mu_{k}=\frac{1}{T}ln(\lambda_{m})

(A.12)

Taking the real part, we obtain

\mathcal{R}(\mu_{k}^{\pm})=\frac{1}{T}\ln\left|\frac{M_{11}+M_{22}}{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{M_{11}+M_{22}}{2}\right)^{2}-1}\right|.

(A.13)

In terms of the solution components, this becomes

\mathcal{R}(\mu_{k}^{\pm})=\frac{1}{T}\ln\left|\frac{\delta\chi_{k}^{(1)}(T)+\delta\pi_{k}^{(2)}(T)}{2}\pm\sqrt{\frac{\left[\delta\chi_{k}^{(1)}(T)-\delta\pi_{k}^{(2)}(T)\right]^{2}}{4}+\delta\chi_{k}^{(2)}(T)\,\delta\pi_{k}^{(1)}(T)}\right|.

(A.14)
