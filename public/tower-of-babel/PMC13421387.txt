FeVO4/reduced graphene oxide: a novel robust peroxidase nanozyme. Application to fabricate an electrochemical glucose biosensor
Authors: Tuan K. Nguyen, Anh H. Duong, Luyen T. Tran, B. Piro, Chinh D. Huynh, Hoang V. Tran
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Herein, a FeVO4/reduced graphene oxide (FeVO4/rGO) nanocomposite was synthesized via a surfactant-assisted hydrothermal method and applied as a bifunctional platform for non-enzymatic H2O2 sensing and substrate-competitive enzymatic glucose detection. In this synthesis, sodium dodecylbenzene sulfonate (SDBS) acted as a structure-directing agent to facilitate the uniform distribution of FeVO4 nanocrystals on the rGO network, thereby minimizing restacking and increasing the electroactive surface area. As a peroxidase-mimicking nanozyme, the FeVO4/rGO composite enabled the sensitive detection of H2O2via a hydroxyl radical-mediated electro-reduction mechanism. Michaelis–Menten kinetic analysis yielded a low Michaelis constant (Km) of 0.176 mM and Imax of 52.66 µA, indicating high substrate affinity and catalytic efficiency. Based on this activity, the H2O2 sensor achieved a low limit of detection (LOD) of 0.15 µM, and high sensitivity (2560 µA mM−1 cm−2). Furthermore, a highly efficient cathodic glucose biosensor was fabricated by immobilizing glucose oxidase (GOx) onto the FeVO4/rGO's surface. This bio-hybrid platform demonstrated high performance, achieving a linear concentration range from 0.05 to 1.0 mM with a remarkably low LOD of 0.06 µM and a sensitivity of 89.08 µA mM−1 cm−2. The glucose sensor displayed stability and selectivity against common interferents, and their practical applicability was validated in human urine samples. These results indicate that FeVO4/rGO is a potential material for the development of clinical diagnostic devices.

Introduction

Rapid and accurate quantification of glucose and hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) is of paramount importance in clinical diagnostics, serving as critical biomarkers for metabolic disorders and oxidative stress-related pathologies. While glucose monitoring is essential for glycemic control in diabetes mellitus, H2O2 acts not only as a byproduct of oxidase-based enzymatic reactions but also as a vital signaling molecule in cellular physiology.1–4 Consequently, the development of bifunctional sensing platforms capable of detecting both targets with high sensitivity and reliability remains a significant pursuit in bio-electrochemistry. Conventionally, glucose biosensors rely on a dual-enzyme system involving glucose oxidase (GOx) and horseradish peroxidase (HRP) to detect the generated H2O2.5–8 However, natural enzymes serve under physiological constraints, suffering from instability in harsh environments, high production costs, and complex purification requirements.9 This has triggered a shift toward nanozyme–nanomaterial-based enzyme mimics that offer superior stability and cost-effectiveness.10–12 Therefore, there is a critical need for advanced nanomaterials that can synergize intrinsic peroxidase-like activity for direct H2O2 sensing with efficient electron transport during glucose oxidization and sensing. Among these, iron vanadate (FeVO4), an n-type semiconductor, exhibits intrinsic peroxidase-mimicking activity attributed to the functional mimicry of its active Fe(III)/Fe(II) redox centers to the heme prosthetic group of natural peroxidases.13,14 This mixed-valence nature enables FeVO4 to functionally mimic the electron transfer capabilities of natural peroxidases. Despite these advantages, the electrocatalytic efficacy of pristine FeVO4 is often hindered by its intrinsically low electrical conductivity and the tendency to agglomerate, which imposes a high kinetic barrier on electron transfer kinetics.15 Coupling FeVO4 with reduced graphene oxide (rGO) is an effective strategy to enhance charge transport.16–19 In addition, S. Alwarappan20–22 reported that, using the rGO modified electrode can provide an anchoring surface for glucose oxidase (GOx) enzyme immobilization via noncovalent π–π stacking, which is formed via the interaction between the dimeric protein of the GOx with the hexagonal cells (carbon rings) on the graphene surface. However, a persistent challenge in rGO-based composites is the irreversible restacking of graphene sheets via π–π interactions, which reduces the effective electroactive surface area. To address this, interface engineering utilizing surfactants is a critical strategy. In this context, sodium dodecylbenzene sulfonate (SDBS) functions as a bifunctional structure-directing agent: it not only effectively exfoliates graphene layers to prevent restacking but also acts as an interfacial linker to direct the uniform nucleation of metal oxide precursors23–25 Beyond its intrinsic peroxidase-like activity, the high specific surface area and excellent conductivity of the FeVO4/rGO nanocomposite render it a favorable scaffold for enzyme immobilization, establishing effective electrical communication between the deeply buried active centers and the electrode surface, which is essential for driving a highly sensitive substrate-competitive biosensing mechanism.

Herein, we report a facile surface modification strategy to synthesize a hierarchical FeVO4/rGO nanocomposite via a surfactant-assisted hydrothermal method. Leveraging the optimized interface engineered by SDBS, this material was systematically investigated as a versatile bifunctional scaffold for two electrochemical applications: (1) a robust non-enzymatic sensor for H2O2 detection driven by intrinsic peroxidase-mimicking activity, and (2) a sensitive cathodic glucose biosensor featuring a substrate-modulated signal-off response driven by efficient interfacial bio-electrical communication. Here, chitosan (CS) was employed as a biocompatible matrix to immobilize the FeVO4/rGO material, preserve GOx's activity and facilitate glucose sensing. Ultimately, this work establishes the FeVO4/rGO nanocomposite as a versatile interface, bridging intrinsic electrocatalysis and enzymatic bioelectronics through rational material design.

Experimental

Materials and instrumentations

Materials and reagents

Potassium hexacyanoferrate(ii) trihydrate (K4Fe(CN)6·3H2O, ≥99.9 wt%), acetate buffer (ABS), ferric(iii) nitrate nonahydrate (Fe(NO3)3·9H2O), ≥99.95 wt%), ammonium vanadate (NH4VO3, ≥99.0 wt%), sodium dodecylbenzene sulfonate (SDBS), d-(+)-glucose (C6H12O6, ≥99.5 wt%), l-ascorbic acid (C6H8O6, ≥99.0 wt%), dopamine hydrochloride (C8H11NO2·HCl, ≥98.0 wt%), urea (CH4N2O, ≥99.0 wt%), lactose monohydrate (C12H22O11·H2O, ≥99.5 wt%), phosphate buffered saline tablets (PBS), and glucose oxidase (GOx) were purchased from Sigma Aldrich. Graphene oxide (GO) was synthesized in our laboratory using Hummers' method as previously reported.26 Chitosan (degree of deacetylation ≥ 90.0%), sodium hydroxide (NaOH, ≥96.0 wt%), ethanol (C2H5OH, ≥99.95 v/v%), hydrogen peroxidase (H2O2), 30 wt%) were purchased from Xilong Scientific Co., Ltd.

Instrumentations and methods

The crystalline structure and functional groups of the synthesized materials were characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD) (PANalytical X'Pert PRO, Cu Kα radiation (λ = 0.154 nm)) and Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy (Shimadzu IRAffinity-1S, KBr pellet, 400–4000 cm−1), respectively. Surface morphology and elemental composition were examined using a Hitachi S-4800 FE-SEM integrated with an EMAX ENERGY EDS system, while the internal nanoscale architecture was elucidated via high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM, JEOL JEM-2100). The physical surface area and mesoporous characteristics, including pore volume and pore diameter, were evaluated using a Micromeritics Gemini VII 2390 analyzer. Optical properties were recorded on an Agilent 8453 UV-vis spectrophotometer (200–1200 nm). Electrochemical measurements were conducted on a DY2200 workstation (Digi-Ivy, USA) using a standard three-electrode configuration: a modified GCE working electrode (GCE, Ø = 3 mm), a Pt plate counter electrode, and an Ag/AgCl (3 M KCl) reference electrode. For H2O2 detection, cyclic voltammetry (CV) and differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) were recorded in potential windows of −0.9 to 0.15 V, while chronoamperometric responses were monitored at −0.55 V. Conversely, glucose sensing employed CV scans between −1.2 and 0 V, while amperometry responses were monitored at −0.5 V. The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was performed on a CHI760C workstation to assess the interfacial charge-transfer properties of the modified electrodes in 0.1 M KCl containing 5.0 mM [Fe(CN)6]3−/4− with frequency from 100 kHz to 0.1 Hz.

Synthesis of FeVO4/rGO peroxidase nanozyme

The FeVO4/rGO peroxidase nanozyme was synthesized via a surfactant-assisted one-step hydrothermal method. In a typical procedure, an aqueous GO suspension (0.3 mg mL−1) was first ultrasonicated to obtain a homogeneous dispersion, followed by the addition of sodium dodecylbenzene sulfonate (SDBS). Subsequently, 1 mmol of Fe(NO3)3·9H2O was dissolved into the mixture. Concurrently, a stoichiometric NH4VO3 solution (1 mmol in 60 mL deionized water) was added dropwise to the suspension under magnetic stirring. The pH of the mixture was then adjusted to 4.0 using 0.5 M NaOH. After being agitated for an additional 15 minutes, the resulting homogeneous suspension was transferred into a Teflon-lined stainless-steel autoclave and heated at 180 °C for 20 hours. The synthesized precipitates were recovered by centrifugation, thoroughly washed with DI water/ethanol, and oven dried. The synthesis process is summarized in Scheme 1A.

Evaluation of peroxidase activity of the FeVO4/rGO peroxidase nanozyme

The intrinsic catalytic activity was quantitatively assessed via a colorimetric assay using 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) as the chromogenic substrate, monitored by UV-vis spectrophotometry. In a typical assay performed at 40 °C, the reaction mixture comprised 950 µL of 0.1 M ABS (pH 5.0), 20 µL of FeVO4/rGO dispersion (1 mg mL−1), 2 µL of TMB (5 mg mL−1), and 5 µL of H2O2.

Fabrication of the biosensing platform

The fabrication procedure comprised two main steps: (i) modification of GCE with FeVO4/rGO for H2O2 sensing, and (ii) immobilization of GOx/chitosan for glucose biosensing. Before surface modification, the GCE working electrode was mirror-polished with 0.05 µM alumina (α-Al2O3) slurry and ultrasonically cleaned. A catalyst suspension was prepared by dispersing FeVO4/rGO powder (6.0 mg mL−1) and chitosan (1.0 mg mL−1) (50 : 1, v/v) in water, under ultrasonication. Subsequently, 5 µL of this suspension was drop-cast onto the pretreated GCE and air-dried. Finally, to construct the glucose biosensor, 5 µL of a bio-composite mixture containing GOx (30.0 mg mL−1) (120 µg) and chitosan (1.0 mg mL−1) (4 : 1 ratio, v/v) was cast onto the modified surface. This step completed the fabrication of the GOx/FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE bio-architecture. The processes for fabrication of electrochemical H2O2 sensor and electrochemical glucose biosensor are summarized in Scheme 1B. Then the obtained FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE and GOx/FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE were electrochemically activated via continuous CV cycling in 0.1 M buffer until a stable steady-state response was attained for H2O2 and glucose sensing, respectively.

Glucose detection in a real sample

Three human urine specimens (which were centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 min to remove particulates) from volunteers were supported by a local hospital. To minimize matrix interferences and fit the sensor's linear range, the supernatants were 85-fold diluted with 0.1 M PBS (pH 7.4). Electrochemical analyses were subsequently performed using the standard addition method via chronoamperometry.

Results and discussions

Characterization of as synthesized FeVO4/rGO nanozyme

The crystallographic structure of the synthesized materials was investigated by XRD (Fig. 1A). The pristine FeVO4 (curve a) exhibits distinct diffraction peaks at 2θ value of 25.24°, 33.97°, 40.29°, 42.90°, 45.92°, 53.06°, 60.91°, 64.07°, corresponding to the (101), (002), (021), (112), (121), (202), (004), (023) planes of orthorhombic FeVO4 phase (JCPDS card no. 00-015-0294), respectively. For the FeVO4/rGO nanocomposite (curve b), the characteristic peaks of FeVO4 are well-preserved, confirming the successful formation of the heterostructure. The absence of secondary phases confirms the high purity of the synthesized nanocomposite. The calculated values revealed a significant reduction in crystallite size from 17.45 nm (pristine FeVO4) to 8.58 nm (FeVO4/rGO), consistent with the results from TEM and FE-SEM images.

Fig. 1C exhibits the UV-vis spectrum of the FeVO4/rGO in the range of 200–800 nm. The calculated band gap of Eg = 1.98 eV is notably narrower than that of pristine FeVO4 (Eg = 2.05–2.5 eV), indicative of strong interfacial coupling.16 This reduced energy barrier may facilitate charge transport, which could contribute to enhance the material's electrocatalytic performance. The internal nanoscale architecture and spatial distribution of the materials were explicitly validated by TEM image (Fig. 1D). The micrographs confirm that the FeVO4 nanoparticles (with particle size around 10–20 nm) exhibit a quasi-spherical morphology and are uniformly anchored across the ultrathin rGO matrix. Here, the rGO sheets serve as both a conductive backbone and a physical spacer, mitigating aggregation via steric hindrance. This unique 2D porous framework probably provides a substantial specific surface area, thereby expediting mass transport and amplifying the population of electrochemically active sites for sensing applications.

To elucidate the structure–activity relationship, N2 adsorption–desorption measurements were performed (Fig. 1E). Both pristine FeVO4 and the FeVO4/rGO nanocomposite exhibit type IV isotherms with H3-type hysteresis loops, which are characteristic of mesoporous structures. Notably, the nanocomposite displays a broader hysteresis loop extended to lower relative pressures, indicating the formation of an interconnected mesoporous network. The Barrett–Joyner–Halenda (BJH) analysis (Fig. 1F) reveals a narrow pore size distribution concentrated between 5 and 20 nm for both samples. As summarized in Table 1, the FeVO4/rGO nanocomposite possesses a specific BET surface area of 75.44 m2 g−1 which is significantly higher than that of pristine FeVO4 (62.91 m2 g−1).

Structure properties of the FeVO4 and FeVO4/rGO materials

The FE-SEM image reveals a stark contrast between pristine FeVO4 and FeVO4/rGO composite, in which, the as synthesized FeVO4/rGO nanocomposite (Fig. 1G) exhibits a highly dispersed morphology with a drastic reduction in the estimated average particle size of 32.44 nm (Fig. SI.1C). The pristine FeVO4 (Fig. SI.1A) appears as deformed quasi-spherical nanoparticles characterized by severe agglomeration and a large average particle size of approximately 124 nm (Fig. SI.1B). Regarding the elemental constitution, EDX mapping (Fig. 1H) confirms the exclusive presence of O, Fe, and V (Fig. 1H, inserted figures), with no detectable impurities. Quantitative analysis yields an atomic ratio for Fe : V : O of approximately 16 : 16 : 68, aligning well with the theoretical stoichiometry of the FeVO4 phase.

In agreement with the TEM and FE-SEM observations, as well as XRD, FT-IR, EDX and EDX mapping substantiates that the rGO network acts as an effective two-dimensional physical barrier, sterically restricting the severe spatial agglomeration and grain growth of the FeVO4 nanocrystals. This highly dispersed particulate architecture maximizes the population of exposed active sites on the heterostructure surface, which is highly advantageous for supporting peroxidase-mimicking catalytic activity, charge exchange, improved conductivity, and thus enhancing performance for subsequent electrochemical sensing applications.

Electrochemical behavior of FeVO4/rGO nanozyme modified glassy carbon electrode (FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE)

CV was employed to probe the electron transfer characteristics of the modified electrodes in 0.1 M KCl containing 1 mM [Fe(CN)6]3−/4−, as presented in Fig. 2A. The CV curve of FeVO4/rGO exhibited the highest redox peak intensities compared to the other materials. The peak current increased in the following sequence: GCE < rGO/CS@GCE < FeVO4/CS@GCE < FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE. This confirms the successful modification of the materials on the electrode surface. The superior charge transfer behavior observed after surface modification may be attributed to the greater conductivity and the increase in the electroactive surface area of the modified electrodes, thus enhancing the electrocatalytic activity.35 Besides, to elucidate the interfacial charge transfer properties, EIS measurements were performed in a 0.1 M KCl solution containing 5.0 mM [Fe(CN)6]3−/4−. As shown in Fig. 2B, SI.5 and Table SI.1, the Nyquist plots exhibit a substantial decreasing trend in the charge transfer resistance (Rct): bare GCE (4730 Ω) > rGO/CS@GCE (1300 Ω) > FeVO4/CS@GCE (474 Ω) > FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE (165 Ω). This minimized interfacial resistance and maximized exchange current density perfectly corroborate the enlarged electroactive surface area (ECSA) observed in the CV results (Fig. 2C and D). Consequently, the interconnected mesoporous architecture of the nanocomposite, as confirmed by the BET analysis (Fig. 1E and F), successfully exposes abundant active sites and provides unimpeded electron-conduction pathways via the rGO network, creating a highly conducive microenvironment for sensing applications.

The influence of scan rate on the electrochemical response of the FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE was further analyzed (Fig. 2C). Fig. 2D exhibits a linear relationship of both oxidation (Ipa) and reduction (Ipc) currents versus the square root of the scan rate, with the corresponding calibration plots as follows: Ipa (µA) = 475.4ν1/2 + 15.77 (R2 = 0.999) and Ipc (µA) = −282.9ν1/2 − 22.12 (R2 = 0.9997). In contrast, the linear fit of peak currents against the scan rate (ν) (Fig. SI.2) yielded inferior correlation coefficients, effectively excluding a surface-controlled adsorption mechanism. These results indicate that the electrochemical process occurring on the modified electrode surface is controlled by a diffusion process. The ECSA was estimated using the Randles–Sevcik for a quasi-reversible process36 (eqn (2)):2Ip = (3.01 × 105)n3/2α1/2AD1/2Cν1/2where n = 1 represents the number of electrons transferred, Ipa is the peak current, A is the electroactive surface area (cm2), assuming a standard charge transfer coefficient α = 0.5, D is the diffusion coefficient of K4[Fe(CN)6] (D = (6.7 ± 0.02) × 10−6 cm2 s−1), ν is the scan rate, and C is the bulk concentration of K4[Fe(CN)6] (1 × 10−6 mol cm−3). Based on this equation, the ECSA for bare GCE, rGO/CS@GCE, FeVO4/CS@GCE, and FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE was calculated to be 0.065, 0.129, 0.258, and 0.87 cm2, respectively. Notably, the FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE exhibited the highest active area, being approximately 3-fold higher than FeVO4/CS@GCE and 13.4-fold higher than the bare GCE. This substantial enhancement is attributed to the synergistic role of the rGO matrix, which not only prevents the agglomeration of FeVO4 nanoparticles but also establishes a highly conductive 2D network, thereby maximizing the density of accessible active sites for enhanced electrocatalytic H2O2 and glucose sensing.

To elucidate the electrochemical reaction mechanism, the CV response of FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE toward 0.5 mM H2O2 in 0.1 M ABS (pH 5.0) was investigated various scan rates (5, 10, 20, 50, 200, and 500 mV s−1) (Fig. 2E). Based on the figures, linear correlation was observed between the cathodic peak current (Ipc) and the square root of the scan rate ν1/2, by the equation: Ipc (µA) = −104.04 ν1/2 + 4.075 (R2 = 0.999) (Fig. 2F). This linearity confirms that the electrocatalytic reduction of H2O2 on the nanocomposite surface is a diffusion-controlled process, facilitated by the rapid electron transfer at the electrode–electrolyte interface.

The superior electrocatalytic performance of the sensor is intrinsically linked to the material's peroxidase-mimicking activity first evaluated via the colorimetric TMB assay (Fig. 3A). While control groups displayed negligible absorption, the FeVO4/rGO + TMB + H2O2 system exhibited a distinct characteristic absorbance peak at 652 nm. This result confirms that the nanocomposite efficiently catalyzes the decomposition of H2O2, generating a high flux of reactive oxygen species (ROS), specifically hydroxyl radicals ˙OH driven by the redox cycling of active Fe3+/Fe2+ and V5+/V4+ couples within the FeVO4 lattice and the highly conductive rGO network.37 To directly verify the generation of these ˙OH radicals, a radical scavenging assay was performed using isopropanol (IPA) as a highly specific ˙OH scavenger (Fig. 3A). Upon introducing 200 mM of IPA into the reaction mixture, the characteristic absorbance peak of oxidized TMB at 652 nm was almost completely quenched. This provides direct experimental evidence that ˙OH is indeed the primary reactive intermediate dictating the peroxidase-mimicking catalytic pathway. This robust generation of ˙OH serves as a critical predictor for the electrochemical sensing efficiency described below.

Subsequently, the electrocatalytic activity toward H2O2 reduction was assessed via CV in 0.1 M ABS containing 0.5 mM H2O2 (Fig. 3B). The unmodified GCE and rGO/CS@GCE displayed only weak reduction responses positioned at a high cathodic potential of approximately −0.64 V. In contrast, the incorporation of FeVO4 induced a distinct positive shift in the reduction potential to −0.58 V, indicating improved catalytic properties. Most notably, the FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE demonstrated superior performance, achieving the highest peak current of −51.54 µA at a significantly lower potential of −0.52 V. This catalytic enhancement is quantitatively summarized by the current change (ΔI) analysis (Fig. 3B and SI.3A–E) which follows the ascending order: GCE bare (3.24 µA) < rGO/CS@GCE (4.17 µA) < FeVO4/CS@GCE (20.37 µA) < FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE (37.60 µA). The remarkable enhancement in the FeVO4/rGO architecture is ascribed to the synergistic integration of morphological and electronic effects. Morphologically, SDBS acts as a critical linker to ensure uniform FeVO4 anchoring while preventing rGO restacking, thereby maximizing the ECSA and facilitating substrate diffusion. Thermodynamically, the robust redox synergy between Fe3+/Fe2+ and V5+/V4+ significantly lowers the activation energy for H2O2 decomposition, clearly evidenced by the cathodic potential shift (∼120 mV) observed at the heterostructured interface. These reduced centers catalyze the heterolytic cleavage of H2O2 to generate high-flux ˙OH (eqn (5) and (6), which subsequently undergoes immediate electro-reduction to yield an amplified cathodic current (eqn (7)).

Differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) was employed to minimize capacitive background current and further evaluate the sensitivity. As shown in Fig. 3C, the FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE displayed a well-defined reduction peak centered at −0.70 V. Upon successive additions of H2O2, the cathodic peak current increased linearly with the analyte concentration (Fig. 3D). Based on the linear regression analysis (N = 5), the sensitivity was determined to be 2560 ± 83 µA mM−1 cm−2. The limit of detection (LOD) was calculated to be 0.15 µM (S/N = 3). To gain deeper insights into the intrinsic catalytic efficiency and substrate affinity underlying the superior electrochemical performance observed above, the steady-state kinetics of the FeVO4/rGO nanozyme toward H2O2 were quantitatively analyzed. The calculated apparent Michaelis–Menten constant (Km) can be obtained using the electrochemical version of the Lineweaver–Burk equation52 (eqn (8)):8where I denote the reduction peak current, while C represents the concentration of the analyte. The parameters Km and Imax are critical metrics of enzyme kinetics, a lower Km signifies a higher affinity of the enzyme (nanozyme) for its substrate, whereas a higher Imax value indicates superior catalytic throughput.53 From the intercept and slope of the Lineweaver–Burk plot (Fig. 3E), the values of Km and Imax were calculated to be 0.176 mM and 52.66 µA, respectively. Notably, this obtained Km value is substantially lower than those reported for native horseradish peroxidase (HRP) (2.33, 167 mM) and other metal-oxide nanozymes48,49 summarized in Table 2. These results underscore the robust intrinsic peroxidase-mimicking activity of the FeVO4/rGO nanocomposite, validating its advantage over natural enzymes in overcoming kinetic limitations.

The anti-interference capability of the FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE was rigorously evaluated via amperometry technique. The sensor exhibited a sharp current response to H2O2 in a solution containing various potential interferents, whereas the signals from these potential interferents including inorganic ions (Fe2+, Na+, NO2−, Cu2+), saccharides (glucose, lactose, saccharose), and common electroactive biomolecules (ascorbic acid, dopamine, uric acid, lactic acid, urea, caffeine) at 25-fold excess concentrations were negligible (Fig. 3F). This high specificity is attributed to the catalytic capability of the nanocomposite, which lowers the H2O2 reduction potential to −0.55 V. Operating at such a low potential effectively avoids the oxidation of electroactive biomolecules (ascorbic acid, dopamine, uric acid, etc.), as they typically require more positive potentials to interfere. The practical reliability of the sensor was assessed through reproducibility and long-term stability tests. The reproducibility was examined by recording the CV responses of four independently fabricated electrodes under identical conditions (Fig. SI.4A). The sensor demonstrated excellent reproducibility, evidenced by a low relative standard deviation (RSD) of 2.69%. Additionally, long-term stability was evaluated over a 21 days storage period at ambient temperature (Fig. SI.4B). Remarkably, the sensor retained approximately 97.43% of its initial current response. This enduring stability is ostensibly attributed to the strong synergistic interaction and robust interfacial adhesion between the FeVO4/rGO nanocomposite and the electrode surface, which prevents material leaching during prolonged operation.

Electrochemical glucose detection based on GOx/FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE biosensor

Initially, the bio-functionality of the rGO/FeVO4/GOx system was validated via a colorimetric assay (Fig. 4A), where absorbance at a wavelength of 652 nm was observed. This optical signature confirms that the immobilization of GOx onto rGO/FeVO4 preserves the native catalytic conformation of GOx, preventing denaturation or steric deactivation. Such superior biocompatibility validates the nanocomposite as a robust anchoring scaffold for the biosensing interface.

Real sample application

The practical utility of the GOx/FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE biosensor was evaluated by quantifying glucose levels in human urine samples representing severe and moderate diabetic profiles (Fig. 5A–D). To fit the linear calibration range and minimize matrix effects, the specimens were pre-diluted with 0.1 M PBS (pH 7.4) prior to amperometric analysis via the standard addition method. As summarized in Table SI.2, the calculated glucose concentrations (17.15 and 5.61 mM) exhibited excellent correlation with hospital reference data (17.00 and 5.50 mM, respectively). Statistical validation from triplicate measurements (N = 3) demonstrated high precision (RSD ≤ 3.1%) and satisfactory recovery rates (97.8–106.6%), confirming the platform's reliability for clinical diagnostics.

Conclusions

In summary, a hierarchical FeVO4/rGO nanocomposite was successfully engineered via a facile surfactant-assisted one-step hydrothermal strategy. Using FeVO4/rGO nanocomposite to modify the glassy carbon electrode (FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE), a robust peroxidase-mimicking nanozyme for direct H2O2 reduction was demonstrated with a low Michaelis–Menten constant (Km) of 0.176 mM and an ultralow limit of detection (LOD) of 0.15 µM. By immobilization of GOx onto FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE, an electrochemical biosensor (GOx/FeVO4/rGO/CS@GCE) for glucose sensing was served with a superior sensitivity of 89.08 µA mM−1 cm−2 in a linear concentration range from 0.05 to 1.0 mM with a LOD of 0.06 µM. Both sensors demonstrated superior analytical performance, characterized by low detection limits, and excellent stability in complex biological matrices. Furthermore, the practical applicability of the developed biosensor was demonstrated through glucose determination in real human urine samples, yielding satisfactory recovery rates consistent with standard clinical measurements. These findings establish FeVO4/rGO as a versatile and promising candidate for advanced clinical diagnostics and environmental monitoring.
