Low-regularity global well-posedness theory for the generalized Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation on ℝ × 𝕋 \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T} and polynomial growth of higher Sobolev norms
Jakob Nowicki-Koth

Abstract
. We address the Cauchy problem for the k k -generalized Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation ( k k -gZK) posed on ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} and on ℝ × 𝕋 \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T} . By applying established and recently developed linear and bilinear Strichartz-type estimates within the framework of the I I -method, we obtain the following results: • The Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation is globally well-posed in H s ​ ( ℝ × 𝕋 ) H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}) for every s > 11 13 s>\frac{11}{13} . • The modified Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation is globally well-posed in H s ​ ( ℝ 2 ) H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) for every s > 2 3 s>\frac{2}{3} and in H s ​ ( ℝ × 𝕋 ) H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}) for every s > 36 49 s>\frac{36}{49} . Moreover, we show that the H s ​ ( ℝ × 𝕋 ) H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}) -norm of smooth global real-valued solutions of k k -gZK grows at most polynomially in time for every k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 .



1.
Introduction

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and
X\in\{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T},\mathbb{R}^{2}\}
, we consider the Cauchy problem

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}u=\pm\partial_{x}\left(u^{k+1}\right),\quad u(t=0)=u_{0}\in H^{s}(X)

(CP
k,X
)

for the
k
-generalized Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation (
k
-gZK), where
u=u(t,x,y)
is a real-valued function. As the name suggests, the
k
-gZK equation is a generalization of the Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation (ZK), which was originally introduced by Zakharov and Kuznetsov
[
39
]
to model the propagation of ion-acoustic waves in a magnetized plasma.

In the non-periodic setting, the well-posedness theory of
k
-gZK has attracted significant interest for more than two decades and continues to do so today. We provide an overview of the chronological development of well-posedness results for the modified equation (
k=2
), which is of particular interest here:

Biagioni and Linares
[
2
]
first established local and global well-posedness in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, with the global result being valid for
H^{1}_{xy}
-data of sufficiently small
L^{2}_{xy}
-norm. Linares and Pastor
[
24
,
25
]
were later the first to go below the
H^{1}_{xy}
level, proving local well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
s>\frac{3}{4}
and global well-posedness for
s>\frac{53}{63}
under an additional smallness assumption on the
L^{2}_{xy}
-norm of the initial data in the focusing case (i.e., the case where a negative sign appears before the nonlinearity).
Afterwards, the local result was improved by Ribaud and Vento
[
34
]
to the threshold
s>\frac{1}{4}
by exploiting the interplay between the Kato smoothing effect and a local-in-time
L^{2}_{x}L_{yT}^{\infty}
maximal function estimate due to Faminskii
[
12
]
.
Subsequently, Bhattacharya, Farah and Roudenko
[
1
]
improved the global well-posedness result to
s>\frac{3}{4}
(again under a similar smallness assumption in the focusing case) by means of the
I
-method, and this was followed by Kinoshita
[
22
]
, who further refined the local theory by establishing local well-posedness at the level of
s=\frac{1}{4}
. Moreover, in the same work he proved that the data-to-solution map fails to be
C^{3}
for
s<\frac{1}{4}
, thereby highlighting the critical nature of the threshold
s=\frac{1}{4}
in the perturbative sense. Most recently, Correia and Kinoshita
[
10
]
complemented the global well-posedness result of Bhattacharya, Farah, and Roudenko by establishing global well-posedness at the scaling-critical regularity
s_{c}=0
for small initial data. Their result is proved for initial data in function spaces that scale like
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and are specifically tailored to interact well with a
L^{4}_{txy}
-based smoothing estimate for the symmetrized equation. At this point, it is important to note that their argument applies only to non-quantifiably small initial data. Consequently, the question of global well-posedness for arbitrary initial data in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
s\leq\frac{3}{4}
remains open.

For further results concerning
k
-gZK in the non-periodic setting, we refer the reader to
[
2
,
12
,
13
,
18
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
29
,
36
]
in two spatial dimensions, to
[
16
,
17
,
20
,
21
,
27
,
29
,
35
]
in three spatial dimensions, and to
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
in dimensions
n\geq 4
. We emphasize that these lists are by no means exhaustive.

In contrast to the abundance of results in the non-periodic setting, the semiperiodic case has only begun to receive increased attention in recent years.

For the original equation (
k=1
), Linares, Pastor, and Saut
[
26
]
initiated the study by proving local well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
for all
s>\frac{3}{2}
. By developing a bilinear smoothing estimate for widely separated frequencies, Molinet and Pilod
[
29
]
later established global well-posedness for all
s\geq 1
, and Osawa
[
32
]
then further improved the underlying local result to
s>\frac{9}{10}
. Shortly thereafter, Osawa and Takaoka
[
33
]
proved global well-posedness for all
s>\frac{29}{31}
by employing the
I
-method, and most recently Cao-Labora
[
5
]
lowered the local threshold to
s>\frac{3}{4}
(
s>\frac{1}{2}
under an additional low frequency condition), showing in the same work that these results are optimal up to the endpoint, in the sense that the associated data-to-solution map fails to be
C^{2}
for
s<\frac{3}{4}
and
s<\frac{1}{2}
, respectively.

For all higher-order nonlinearities (
k\geq 2
), Farah and Molinet
[
14
]
established local well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
for all
s>1-
(and
s>\frac{5}{6}
for the modified equation), and also proved global well-posedness for all
s\geq 1
under certain smallness assumptions on the
L^{2}_{xy}
- or
H^{1}_{xy}
-norm of the initial data. By developing new linear and bilinear Strichartz-type estimates, the local well-posedness results were recently improved to
s>\frac{11}{24}
(
k=2
),
s>\frac{1}{2}
(
k=3
), and
s>1-\frac{16}{9k}
(
k\geq 4
)
[
30
,
31
]
, while the question of optimality remains open for all
k\geq 2
.

A common feature of most of the global well-posedness results mentioned above is the use of the
I
-method developed by Colliander, Keel, Staffilani, Takaoka, and Tao (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
9
]
). The core idea of this method is to regularize solutions corresponding to low-regularity data by means of a smoothing operator, thereby lifting them to the regularity level of a conserved quantity, and to establish an almost conservation law for the resulting modified energy. The existence of suitable conservation laws is therefore essential, and for
k
-gZK, we have the conservation of the mass

M[u](t)\coloneqq\int_{X}(u(t,x,y))^{2}\ \mathrm{d}(x,y)

at the
L^{2}_{xy}
level, while the conservation of the energy

E_{\pm}[u](t)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}\lvert\nabla u(t,x,y)\rvert_{2}^{2}\pm\frac{2}{k+2}(u(t,x,y))^{k+2}\ \mathrm{d}(x,y)

holds at the level of
H^{1}_{xy}
.

The present article is essentially divided into two parts. In the first part, we revisit the
I
-method described above:

The global well-posedness results established in
[
33
]
and
[
1
]
are based on Strichartz estimates that are suited to obtaining local well-posedness in
H^{s}_{xy}
for
s>\frac{9}{10}
(ZK on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
) and
s>\frac{1}{2}
(modified ZK on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
). Since, within the local theory, it has been possible to push these thresholds down to
s>\frac{3}{4}

[
5
]
(ZK on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
),
s\geq\frac{1}{4}

[
22
]
(modified ZK on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
), and more recently to
s>\frac{11}{24}

[
31
]
(modified ZK on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
), it is natural to expect that the global results for ZK on
{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}
and modified ZK on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
can be improved, and that a global result below
H^{1}_{xy}
may be achieved for modified ZK on
{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}
.
To this end, in the case of mZK on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we will make use of a linear
L^{4}_{txy}
-estimate which allows for a gain of
\frac{1}{4}
of a derivative in certain frequency regimes (see
[
29
]
), whereas for the treatment of ZK and modified ZK on
{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}
, we will make use of the Strichartz-type estimates recently developed in
[
31
]
.
More precisely, in the semiperiodic setting, a bilinear refinement of a linear
L^{4}_{txy}
-estimate (both obtained in
[
31
]
) will be decisive for the strength of the global results, and the targeted activation of this bilinear smoothing estimate within the framework of the
I
-method will account for a substantial part of our efforts in this article.

The precise statements of our global well-posedness results are given in

Theorem 1.1
.

The Cauchy problem
\mathrm{(CP}_{1,\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\mathrm{)}
is globally well-posed for every
s>\frac{11}{13}
. That is, for every
s>\frac{11}{13}
, every
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
, and every prescribed lifespan
T>0
, there exists a unique solution

u\in X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+}^{T}

of
\mathrm{(CP}_{1,\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\mathrm{)}
. This solution belongs to
C([-T,T],H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}))
, and for every
T^{\prime}\in(0,T)
, there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}\subseteq H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
of
u_{0}
such that the data-to-solution map

S:H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})\supseteq\mathcal{U}\rightarrow X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+}^{T^{\prime}},\quad v_{0}\mapsto S(v_{0})\coloneqq v

is smooth. Moreover, if
s\leq 1
, we have the following bound on the growth of the
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
-norm of
u
:

\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim(1+T)^{\frac{4(1-s^{2})}{13s-11}+}.

(1)

and

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X\in\{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T},\mathbb{R}^{2}\}
and define

s(X)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{36}{49},&\text{if}\quad X=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T},\\
\frac{2}{3},&\text{if}\quad X=\mathbb{R}^{2}.\end{cases}

Then the Cauchy problem
\mathrm{(CP}_{2,X}\mathrm{)}
is globally well-posed for all regularities
s>s(X)
. More precisely, let
s>s(X)
and
u_{0}\in H^{s}(X)
be given. In the focusing case, assume in addition that

\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(X)}<\lVert Q_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where
Q_{2}
denotes the unique positive radial solution of

\Delta_{xy}Q_{2}-Q_{2}+Q_{2}^{3}=0.

Then, for every prescribed lifespan
T>0
, there exists a unique solution

u\in X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+}^{T}

of
\mathrm{(CP}_{2,X}\mathrm{)}
. This solution belongs to
C([-T,T],H^{s}(X))
, and for every
T^{\prime}\in(0,T)
, there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}\subseteq H^{s}(X)
of
u_{0}
such that the data-to-solution map

S:H^{s}(X)\supseteq\mathcal{U}\rightarrow X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+}^{T^{\prime}},\quad v_{0}\mapsto S(v_{0})\coloneqq v

is smooth.
Moreover, if
s\leq 1
, the solutions satisfy the following growth bounds for their respective
H^{s}(X)
-norms:

\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{H^{s}(X)}\lesssim\begin{cases}(1+T)^{\frac{12s(1-s)}{49s-36}+},&\text{if}\quad X=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T},\\
(1+T)^{\frac{2s(1-s)}{3(3s-2)}+},&\text{if}\quad X=\mathbb{R}^{2}.\end{cases}

(2)

In the second part of the article, we change perspective and consider global solutions in the semiperiodic setting corresponding to initial data of higher regularity
s\gg 1
. Since the global existence of the solution essentially relies on the uniform boundedness of the
H^{1}_{xy}
-norm, it is natural to ask how the
H^{s}_{xy}
-norm of the solution behaves for
s\gg 1
. It turns out that, for all
k\geq 1
, the associated
H^{s}_{xy}
-norm grows at most polynomially in time, and this is made precise in

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\in 2\mathbb{N}
,

\alpha(k,s)\coloneqq\begin{cases}4(s-1),&\text{if}\quad k=1,\\
s-1,&\text{if}\quad k\geq 2,\end{cases}

and
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
. Assume in addition that
u_{0}
satisfies the smallness assumption specified in Theorem 1.2 of
[
14
]
. Then the
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
-norm of the associated unique global solution
u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}))\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}))\cap\left(\bigcap_{T>0}X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+}^{T}\right)
to
\mathrm{(CP}_{k,\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\mathrm{)}
grows at most polynomially in time. More precisely, for every
\alpha>\alpha(k,s)
, there exists a constant
C=C(k,\alpha,\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{H^{1}})>0
such that

\lVert u(t)\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\leq C(1+\lvert t\rvert)^{\alpha}(1+\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})})

(3)

holds for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Our considerations on the growth of higher
H^{s}_{xy}
-norms for
k
-gZK in the semiperiodic setting were inspired by an analogous result of Côte and Valet
[
11
]
for the original ZK equation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and the proof of Theorem
1.3
will proceed as follows: Following the ideas of Bourgain
[
3
,
4
]
and Staffilani
[
37
]
, we fix
t\in\mathbb{R}
and derive integral representations for differences of the form

\lVert u(t)\rVert_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}-\lVert u(0)\rVert_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}.

With the aid of the most recent local well-posedness results from
[
5
]
and
[
31
]
, we then estimate the resulting integral expressions to obtain

\lVert u(t)\rVert_{H^{s}}\leq\lVert u(0)\rVert_{H^{s}}+C\lVert u(0)\rVert_{H^{s}}^{1-\varepsilon},

and a Grönwall-type iteration argument will ultimately provide polynomial growth bounds depending on
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.

At this point, we state without proof that the same approach allows one to establish polynomial growth bounds in the non-periodic setting for all higher-oder nonlinearities as well. The general arguments presented in Section 5 also apply on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and one only needs to prove an analogue of Proposition
5.9
, which can be accomplished by exploiting the interplay between the Kato smoothing estimate, Faminskii’s
[
12
]

L_{x}^{2}L_{yT}^{\infty}
maximal function estimate, and the
L_{x}^{4}L_{yT}^{\infty}
maximal function estimate proved in
[
24
]
. Proceeding as described, one can achieve any exponent
\alpha>\frac{4(s-1)}{5}
in the corresponding growth estimate for all
k\geq 2
, which, as one would expect, is better than
\alpha>s-1
in the semiperiodic case.

2.
Preliminaries

We begin by introducing some notational conventions and defining the function spaces that will be used in this article.

2.1.
Notation

For
x=(x_{1},...,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, its Euclidean norm will be denoted by
\lvert x\rvert_{2}^{2}\coloneqq x_{1}^{2}+...+x_{n}^{2}
, so that the Japanese brackets take the form
\langle x\rangle\coloneqq(1+\lvert x\rvert_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
If
a\in\mathbb{R}
is an arbitrary real number, we denote its absolute value by
\lvert a\rvert
, and we use the notation
a+
to denote a number slightly larger than
a
, and
a-
to denote a number slightly smaller than
a
.
Within this notation, we also permit
\infty-
, to denote a very large, yet still finite, positive real number.
Given two real numbers
a
and
b
, we denote their maximum by
a\lor b
and their minimum by
a\land b
, for brevity.
Furthermore, if
a
and
b
are positive, we will often write
a\lesssim b
to indicate that there exists some constant
c>0
such that
a\leq cb
.
If the constant
c
in this inequality can be chosen particularly close to
0
, we write
a\ll b
to express that
b
is much larger than
a
.
Moreover, if both
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
hold, we will use the notation
a\sim b
.
In addition, for later use in Section 5, we denote the length of a multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
by
\lvert\alpha\rvert=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
, and we use

D^{\alpha}\coloneqq\frac{\partial^{\alpha_{1}}\partial^{\alpha_{2}}}{\partial x^{\alpha_{1}}\partial y^{\alpha_{2}}}

to simplify the notation for derivatives.
For a parameter
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, we define
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\lambda\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\lambda\mathbb{Z})
and also allow
\lambda=\infty
, in which case we set
\mathbb{T}_{\infty}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}
to maintain uniform notation.
If
f:\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
g:\mathbb{Z}/\lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are admissible functions, we define the Fourier transform of a
2\pi\lambda
-periodic function by

\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}f(q)\coloneqq\int_{-\pi\lambda}^{\pi\lambda}e^{-ixq}f(x)\ \mathrm{d}x,\quad q\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda,

and the corresponding inverse transform by

\mathcal{F}^{-1,\lambda}g(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda}e^{ixq}g(q),\quad x\in\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}.

In the special case
\lambda=\infty
, we choose the constants to match those in the periodic setting and define

\mathcal{F}^{\infty}f(\xi)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix\xi}f(x)\ \mathrm{d}x,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R},

as well as

\mathcal{F}^{-1,\infty}f(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix\xi}f(\xi)\ \mathrm{d}\xi,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

As we will primarily be working with functions
f:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
g:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}/\lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, we introduce the following shorthand notation for the mixed Fourier transform and its inverse, respectively:

\widehat{\!f}^{\lambda}(\tau,\xi,q)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{-\pi\lambda}^{\pi\lambda}e^{-i(t\tau+x\xi+yq)}f(t,x,y)\ \mathrm{d}(t,x,y),\quad(\tau,\xi,q)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}/\lambda,

and

\widecheck{\!g}^{\lambda}(t,x,y)\coloneqq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda}e^{i(t\tau+x\xi+yq)}g(\tau,\xi,q)\ \mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi),\quad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda},

with the obvious modifications in the case
\lambda=\infty
.
When employing partial Fourier transforms, we indicate this by using subscripts, as in
\mathcal{F}_{x}
,
\mathcal{F}_{ty}^{\lambda}
, or
\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda}
.
The parameter
\lambda
is included in the notation whenever
\lambda\notin\{1,\infty\}
, and omitted otherwise when clear from the context.
With the (parametrized) Fourier transform in hand, we define the Riesz and Bessel potential operators of order
-s
, where
s
is an arbitrary real number:

I^{s}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{-1,\lambda}\lvert(\xi,q)\rvert_{2}^{s}\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda},\qquad J^{s}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{-1,\lambda}\langle(\xi,q)\rangle^{s}\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda}.

As we will often apply these operators with respect to just one spatial variable, we will include subscripts when necessary to avoid confusion.
In addition to that, we make use of the Fourier transform to introduce the following family of unitary operators, associated with the linear part of
k
-gZK:

e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{-1,\lambda}e^{it\xi(\xi^{2}+q^{2})}\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

As usual, we denote the occurring phase function by
\varphi(\xi,q)\coloneqq\xi(\xi^{2}+q^{2})
, and use it to define the resonance functions
R_{\text{ZK}}
and
R_{\text{mZK}}
, corresponding to the original and modified ZK equations, respectively:

R_{\text{ZK}}(\xi_{1},q_{1},\xi,q)\coloneqq\varphi(\xi,q)-\varphi(\xi_{1},q_{1})-\varphi(\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1}),

R_{\text{mZK}}(\xi_{1},q_{1},\xi_{2},q_{2},\xi,q)\coloneqq\varphi(\xi,q)-\varphi(\xi_{1},q_{1})-\varphi(\xi_{2},q_{2})-\varphi(\xi-\xi_{1}-\xi_{2},q-q_{1}-q_{2}).

Lastly, we fix the notation
\lvert(\xi,q)\rvert^{2}\coloneqq 3\xi^{2}+q^{2}
, as this dilated quantity arises naturally from differentiating
R_{\text{ZK}}
in the context of a bilinear smoothing estimate, which will be used frequently throughout this article.

2.2.
Function spaces

Let
\lambda\in(0,\infty]
and
X\in\{\mathbb{T}_{\lambda},\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\}
.
For each
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we equip the Sobolev space
H^{s}(X)
with the norm

\lVert f\rVert_{H^{s}(X)}\coloneqq\lVert J^{s}f\rVert_{L^{2}(X)},

while we adopt the convention

\lVert f\rVert_{\dot{H}^{s}(X)}\coloneqq\lVert I^{s}f\rVert_{L^{2}(X)}

for its homogeneous variant.
To reduce notational overhead, we omit the underlying domain in the notation or use subscripts, as in
H_{x}^{s}=H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
H_{\lambda}^{s}=H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})
, or
H_{y,\lambda}^{s}=H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})
.
Moreover, in Section 5, we will dispense entirely with the parameter
\lambda=1
in the notation and simply write
H^{s}
for
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
and
\dot{H}^{s}
for
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
.
For
\lambda\neq\infty
, the convolution theorem and Parseval’s identity take the forms

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda}(fg)(\xi,q)

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda}f\ast\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda}g)(\xi,q)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda}f(\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1})\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda}g(\xi_{1},q_{1})\ \mathrm{d}\xi_{1}

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{-\pi\lambda}^{\pi\lambda}f(x,y)\overline{g(x,y)}\ \mathrm{d}(x,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda}f(\xi,q)\overline{\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda}g(\xi,q)}\ \mathrm{d}\xi,

respectively, allowing us to express the Sobolev norms in terms of Fourier transforms, as in

\lVert f\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})}^{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda}\langle(\xi,q)\rangle^{2s}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda}f(\xi,q)\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\xi.

Now let
T>0
and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
. We equip the mixed
L^{p}L^{q}
spaces in time and space with norms defined, for instance, by

\lVert f\rVert_{L_{t}^{p}L_{xy,\lambda}^{q}}\coloneqq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lVert f(t,\cdot,\cdot)\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})}^{p}\ \mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and

\lVert f\rVert_{L_{Tx}^{p}L_{y,\lambda}^{q}}\coloneqq\left(\int_{[-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}}\lVert f(t,x,\cdot)\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})}^{p}\ \mathrm{d}(t,x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

with the usual modifications in the cases
p=\infty
or
q=\infty
.
We want to emphasize that this list of examples in not intended to be exhaustive, but merely to clarify the notation we adopt for such mixed-norm spaces.
Moreover, in analogy with the notation for the Fourier transform, we often omit the parameter
\lambda
in the notation of the norm whenever
\lambda\in\{1,\infty\}
, provided there is no possibility of confusion.
To conclude this section, we introduce solution spaces that are specifically adapted to the phase function
\varphi
:
Given
s,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, we endow the rescaled semiperiodic Bourgain space
X_{s,b,\lambda}
with the norm

\lVert f\rVert_{X_{s,b,\lambda}}^{2}\coloneqq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda}\langle(\xi,q)\rangle^{2s}\langle\tau-\varphi(\xi,q)\rangle^{2b}\lvert\widehat{\!f}^{\lambda}(\tau,\xi,q)\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi),

while in the non-periodic case, we define the norm via

\lVert f\rVert_{X_{s,b,\infty}}^{2}\coloneqq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle(\xi,\eta)\rangle^{2s}\langle\tau-\varphi(\xi,\eta)\rangle^{2b}\lvert\widehat{\!f}^{\infty}(\tau,\xi,\eta)\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi,\eta).

For any
T>0
, we further define
X_{s,b,\lambda}^{T}
to be the space of all restrictions of
X_{s,b,\lambda}
-functions to
[-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
, and equip it with the norm

\lVert f\rVert_{X_{s,b,\lambda}^{T}}\coloneqq\inf\Big\{\lVert\widetilde{f}\rVert_{X_{s,b,\lambda}}\ \Big|\ \widetilde{f}|_{[-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}=f\ \text{and}\ \widetilde{f}\in X_{s,b,\lambda}\Big\}.

Provided there is no risk of ambiguity, we will also suppress the parameter
\lambda
in the notation for
X_{s,b,\lambda}
and the associated time-restricted spaces in the special cases
\lambda\in\{1,\infty\}
.

3.
Rescaled Strichartz-type estimates

As a final preparatory step, we revisit several well-established linear and bilinear estimates of Strichartz type and reformulate them within the framework of the
\lambda
-dependent function spaces introduced above. In doing so, we follow the approach of Osawa and Takaoka
[
33
]
in spirit, and our starting point is a slightly refined version of a bilinear smoothing estimate, originally due to Molinet and Pilod (see Proposition 3.6 in
[

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
b_{1},b_{2}>\frac{1}{2}
be arbitrary. For
\lambda\in[1,\infty]
, we define the bilinear convolution operator
MP_{\lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)
via its Fourier transform by

\displaystyle\widehat{\!MP_{\lambda}(u,v)}^{\lambda}\left(\tau,\xi,\begin{cases}q,&\text{if}\ \lambda\neq\infty\\
\eta,&\text{if}\ \lambda=\infty\end{cases}\right)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\lambda}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda\\
\ast\end{subarray}}\lvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert^{2}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{\!u}^{\lambda}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},q_{1})\widehat{\!v}^{\lambda}(\tau_{2},\xi_{2},q_{2})\ \mathrm{d}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1}),&\text{if}\ \lambda\neq\infty\\
\displaystyle\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbb{R}^{3}\\
\ast\end{subarray}}\lvert\lvert(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{2},\eta_{2})\rvert^{2}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{\!u}^{\infty}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\eta_{1})\widehat{\!v}^{\infty}(\tau_{2},\xi_{2},\eta_{2})\ \mathrm{d}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\eta_{1}),&\text{if}\ \lambda=\infty\end{cases}

where
\ast
refers to the convolution constraint
(\tau,\xi,q)=(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2},\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},q_{1}+q_{2})
, with an analogous formulation for the real Fourier variables
\eta
,
\eta_{1}
, and
\eta_{2}
, in place of
q
,
q_{1}
, and
q_{2}
.
Then, the estimate

\lVert MP_{\lambda}(u,v)\rVert_{L^{2}_{txy,\lambda}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,b_{1},b_{2}}\lVert J_{y}^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}u\rVert_{X_{0,b_{1},\lambda}}\lVert v\rVert_{X_{0,b_{2},\lambda}}

(4)

holds for all
u,v
with
J_{y}^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}u\in X_{0,b_{1},\lambda}
and
v\in X_{0,b_{2},\lambda}
. Moreover, the implicit constant is independent of the parameter
\lambda
.

Proof.

For
i\in\{1,2\}
and arbitrary
\varepsilon_{i}>0
, we write
b_{i}=\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon_{i}
, and fix
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
.
An application of Parseval’s identity, followed by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and a subsequent application of Fubini’s theorem, yields the estimate

\lVert MP_{\lambda}(u,v)\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}\lesssim\left(\sup_{(\tau,\xi,q)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}/\lambda}\frac{I_{\tau,\xi,q,\lambda}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert J_{y}^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}u\rVert_{X_{0,b_{1},\lambda}}\lVert v\rVert_{X_{0,b_{2},\lambda}}

with

I_{\tau,\xi,q,\lambda}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda\\
\ast\end{subarray}}\langle q_{1}\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon}\lvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert^{2}\rvert\prod_{i=1}^{2}\langle\tau_{i}-\varphi(\xi_{i},q_{i})\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon_{i}}\ \mathrm{d}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1}).

It therefore suffices to show that there exists some constant
C=C(\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})>0
such that

\frac{I_{\tau,\xi,q,\lambda}}{\lambda}\leq C

holds uniformly with respect to
\tau
,
\xi
,
q
, and
\lambda
.
We proceed from the innermost to the outermost integration, beginning with the integration with respect to
\tau_{1}
:
A direct application of Lemma 4.2 in
[
15
]
yields the bound

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\tau_{1}-\varphi(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon_{1}}\langle\tau-\tau_{1}-\varphi(\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1})\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon_{2}}\ \mathrm{d}\tau_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle\tau-\varphi(\xi_{1},q_{1})-\varphi(\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1})\rangle^{-1-2(\varepsilon_{1}\land\varepsilon_{2})},

and consequently

\displaystyle\frac{I_{\tau,\xi,q,\lambda}}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda}\langle q_{1}\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1})\rvert^{2}\rvert

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdot\langle\tau-\varphi(\xi_{1},q_{1})-\varphi(\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1})\rangle^{-1-2(\varepsilon_{1}\land\varepsilon_{2})}\ \mathrm{d}\xi_{1}.

The integration with respect to
\xi_{1}
can be handled by substituting

\widetilde{\xi_{1}}=\tau-\varphi(\xi_{1},q_{1})-\varphi(\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1})\curvearrowright"\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\xi_{1}}=(\lvert(\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2})\ \mathrm{d}\xi_{1}",

which results in the cancellation of the Fourier multiplier appearing in the definition of the operator
MP_{\lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)
.
We thus obtain

\displaystyle\frac{I_{\tau,\xi,q,\lambda}}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda}\langle q_{1}\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\widetilde{\xi_{1}}\rangle^{-1-2(\varepsilon_{1}\land\varepsilon_{2})}\ \mathrm{d}\widetilde{\xi_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{\widetilde{q_{1}}\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\frac{\widetilde{q_{1}}}{\lambda}\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon}

and an integral comparison leads us to

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\frac{y}{\lambda}\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon}\ \mathrm{d}y\right)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(1+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\widetilde{y}\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon}\ \mathrm{d}\widetilde{y}\right)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}1,

where, in the final step, we made use of the restriction
\lambda\geq 1
to ensure the uniformity of the estimate with respect to
\lambda
.
This concludes the proof for
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
. The case
\lambda=\infty
can be treated in a completely analogous manner, with the parameter
\lambda
not appearing in the estimates at all, and no final comparison with an integral being required.
∎

\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{8}}u\rVert_{L_{tx}^{4}L_{y,\lambda}^{2}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{3}{8}+,\lambda}}

4.
global well-posedness below
H^{1}

4.1.
The
I
-method: setup and general arguments

This subsection is devoted to setting up the
I
-method and some associated preliminary considerations that can be carried out simultaneously for both the ZK and mZK equations.
We begin by outlining the approach in its most basic form, as developed by Colliander, Keel, Staffilani, Takaoka, and Tao
[
7
]
. This blueprint will serve as a guiding framework throughout the remainder of Section 4:

Since we do not have direct access to the (conserved) energy
E_{\pm}[u](t)
for rough local solutions
u(t)\in H^{s}
with
s<1
, we introduce a smoothing operator
I_{N}
, which acts like the identity on low frequencies
\lesssim N
while damping high frequencies
\gg N
.
For
I_{N}u(t)
, the modified energy
E_{\pm}[I_{N}u](t)
is then well-defined, and although it is not conserved in general, we will be able to show that its increment over each time step remains sufficiently small to allow for repeated iteration of the local theory, enabling us to reach any prescribed lifespan
T>0
.
We thus aim to establish global existence based on an almost conservation law for the modified energy.

The quality of the global result obtained through the method essentially depends on the degree of nonlinear smoothing provided by the (currently) best known thresholds for local well-posedness, while the computational effort associated with the method is closely linked to the question of whether the scaling symmetry of
k
-gZK can be meaningfully exploited or not - more on this will follow later.

For
\lambda\in[1,\infty]
,
N\gg 1
, and
s<1
, let
m:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{>0}
be a smooth, radially decreasing function with

m(\xi,\eta)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if}\ \lvert(\xi,\eta)\rvert\leq N\\
\frac{\lvert(\xi,\eta)\rvert^{s-1}}{N^{s-1}},&\text{if}\ \lvert(\xi,\eta)\rvert\geq 2N.\end{cases}

Then, the previously introduced smoothing operator
I_{N}
is defined by

I_{N}f\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{-1,\lambda}m(\xi,q)\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda}f,

and a straightforward computation yields

\lVert I_{N}f\rVert_{H_{\lambda}^{1}}\lesssim N^{1-s}\lVert f\rVert_{H_{\lambda}^{s}},

which shows that
I_{N}:H^{s}_{\lambda}\rightarrow H^{1}_{\lambda}
indeed lives up to its designation as a smoothing operator.
At this point, we note the following drawback of the
I_{N}
operator: As the cutoff point
\sim N
moves farther from the origin, the constant appearing in the continuity estimate grows without bound, which would lead to technical difficulties at a later stage in the argument. To circumvent these issues, we now make use of the scaling symmetry of
k
-gZK.

For
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
s<1
, and
\widetilde{X}\in\{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda},\mathbb{R}^{2}\}
, we consider the Cauchy problem

\partial_{t}u_{\lambda}+\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}u_{\lambda}=\pm\partial_{x}\left(u_{\lambda}^{k+1}\right),\quad u_{\lambda}(t=0)=u_{0,\lambda}\in H^{s}(\widetilde{X}),

(
\mathrm{CP}_{k,\widetilde{X}}
)

where solutions
u
of
\mathrm{(CP}_{k,X}\mathrm{)}
on
[-T,T]
correspond one-to-one, via the transformation

u\mapsto u_{\lambda},\quad u_{\lambda}(t,x,y)\coloneqq\lambda^{-\frac{2}{k}}u(\frac{t}{\lambda^{3}},\frac{x}{\lambda},\frac{y}{\lambda}),

to solutions
u_{\lambda}
of
\mathrm{(CP}_{k,\widetilde{X}}\mathrm{)}
on the interval
[-\lambda^{3}T,\lambda^{3}T]
. The precise advantage of passing to this rescaled problem is the subject of the following

Lemma 4.1
.

For
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq s<1
, and
u_{0}\in H^{s}_{1}
, we define
u_{0,\lambda}(x,y)\coloneqq\lambda^{-\frac{2}{k}}u_{0}(\frac{x}{\lambda},\frac{y}{\lambda})
. Then, the estimates

\lVert I_{N}u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\lambda}}\lesssim\lambda^{1-\frac{2}{k}}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}_{1}}

(27)

and

\lVert\nabla I_{N}u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\lambda}}\lesssim N^{1-s}\lambda^{1-\frac{2}{k}-s}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{1}}

(28)

hold.

Proof.

We begin by computing the Fourier transform of
u_{0,\lambda}
. The substitution

(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})=\left(\frac{x}{\lambda},\frac{y}{\lambda}\right)\curvearrowright"\mathrm{d}(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathrm{d}(x,y)"

yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda}u_{0,\lambda}(\xi,q)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-\frac{2}{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{-\pi\lambda}^{\pi\lambda}e^{-i(\xi x+qy)}u_{0}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda},\frac{y}{\lambda}\right)\ \mathrm{d}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2-\frac{2}{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-i(\lambda\xi\widetilde{x}+\lambda q\widetilde{y})}u_{0}(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})\ \mathrm{d}(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2-\frac{2}{k}}\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{1}u_{0}(\lambda\xi,\lambda q),

which allows us to conclude

\displaystyle\lVert I_{N}u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\lambda}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\lambda}}^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda}u_{0,\lambda}(\xi,q)\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\lambda^{3-\frac{4}{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{1}u_{0}(\lambda\xi,\lambda q)\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2-\frac{4}{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{\widetilde{q}\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{1}u_{0}(\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{q})\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\widetilde{\xi}\sim\lambda^{2-\frac{4}{k}}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}_{1}}^{2},

where in the penultimate step, we made the substitution
(\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{q})=(\lambda\xi,\lambda q)
. This completes the proof of (
27
).
To prove (
28
), we invoke Parseval’s identity, taking into account the definition of
I_{N}
, and subsequently insert the expression we previously computed for the Fourier transform of
u_{0,\lambda}
. This leads us to

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla I_{N}u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\lambda}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda}\chi_{\{\lvert(\xi,q)\rvert\leq 2N\}}\lvert(\xi,q)\rvert^{2}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda}u_{0,\lambda}(\xi,q)\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\chi_{\{\lvert(\xi,q)\rvert>2N\}}N^{2-2s}\lvert(\xi,q)\rvert^{2s-2}\lvert(\xi,q)\rvert^{2}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda}u_{0,\lambda}(\xi,q)\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda}N^{2-2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda}\lvert(\xi,q)\rvert^{2s}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{\lambda}u_{0,\lambda}(\xi,q)\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\lambda^{3-\frac{4}{k}}N^{2-2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda}\lvert(\xi,q)\rvert^{2s}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{1}u_{0}(\lambda\xi,\lambda q)\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2-\frac{4}{k}-2s}N^{2-2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{\widetilde{q}\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert(\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{q})\rvert^{2s}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{1}u_{0}(\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{q})\rvert^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\widetilde{\xi}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{2-\frac{4}{k}-2s}N^{2-2s}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}_{1}}^{2},

and taking the square root on both sides of the inequality finally yields (
28
).
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

, and set
u_{0,\lambda}(x,y)\coloneqq\lambda^{-\frac{2}{k}}u_{0}(\frac{x}{\lambda},\frac{y}{\lambda})
. Then, the estimate

\lVert u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{L^{k+2}_{\lambda}}^{k+2}\leq C_{k,\mathbb{R}}\lVert u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\lambda}}^{2}\left(\lVert\nabla u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\lambda}}^{2}+C_{k,\mathbb{T}}\lambda^{-2}\lVert u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\lambda}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}

(29)

holds true. In this context,
C_{k,\mathbb{T}}
is a universal positive constant, while
C_{k,\mathbb{R}}
represents the sharp constant for the inequality

\lVert f\rVert_{L^{k+2}_{\infty}}\leq C_{k,\mathbb{R}}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}_{\infty}}^{2}\lVert\nabla f\rVert_{L^{2}_{\infty}}^{k},\quad f\in H^{1}_{\infty}.

(30)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\lVert u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{L_{\lambda}^{k+2}}^{k+2}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-2-\frac{4}{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{-\pi\lambda}^{\pi\lambda}\Big\lvert u_{0}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda},\frac{y}{\lambda}\right)\Big\rvert^{k+2}\ \mathrm{d}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-\frac{4}{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\lvert u_{0}(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})\rvert^{k+2}\ \mathrm{d}(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})=\lambda^{-\frac{4}{k}}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L_{1}^{k+2}}^{k+2},

and at this point we can employ the estimate developed by Farah and Molinet (see Lemma 1.1 in
[
14
]
) to further obtain

\leq\lambda^{-\frac{4}{k}}C_{k,\mathbb{R}}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L_{1}^{2}}^{2}(\lVert\nabla u_{0}\rVert_{L_{1}^{2}}^{2}+C_{k,\mathbb{T}}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L_{1}^{2}}^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}.

Now, observing that

\lVert u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{L_{\lambda}^{2}}^{2}=\lambda^{2-\frac{4}{k}}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L_{1}^{2}}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\lVert\nabla u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{L_{\lambda}^{2}}^{2}=\lambda^{-\frac{4}{k}}\lVert\nabla u_{0}\rVert_{L_{1}^{2}}^{2},

we conclude that

\lVert u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{L_{\lambda}^{k+2}}^{k+2}\leq C_{k,\mathbb{R}}\lVert u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\lambda}}^{2}\left(\lVert\nabla u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\lambda}}^{2}+C_{k,\mathbb{T}}\lambda^{-2}\lVert u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\lambda}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}},

which is exactly what we wanted to show.
∎

u_{\lambda}=u_{\lambda}(t,x,y)
denote the solution of
\mathrm{(CP}_{k,\widetilde{X}}\mathrm{)}
on
\widetilde{X}
with initial data
u_{0,\lambda}\in H^{s}(\widetilde{X})
, where
s<1
is such that the solution exists.
Then, for every
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
for which the solution exists, it holds that

\displaystyle E_{\pm}[I_{N}u_{\lambda}](\delta)-E_{\pm}[I_{N}u_{\lambda}](0)=

\displaystyle\mp\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{\widetilde{X}}\Delta I_{N}u_{\lambda}\partial_{x}\left(I_{N}(u_{\lambda}^{k+1})-(I_{N}u_{\lambda})^{k+1}\right)\ \mathrm{d}(x,y)\mathrm{d}t

(34)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{\widetilde{X}}I_{N}(u_{\lambda}^{k+1})\partial_{x}\left(I_{N}(u_{\lambda}^{k+1})-(I_{N}u_{\lambda})^{k+1}\right)\ \mathrm{d}(x,y)\mathrm{d}t.

Proof.

For the following calculations, we assume that
u_{\lambda}
is smooth for each fixed
t\in[-\lvert\delta\rvert,\lvert\delta\rvert]
and rapidly decreasing in the non-periodic directions of propagation. The general case then follows by approximation, taking into account the continuous dependence of the solution on the initial data.

An application of the fundamental theorem of calculus yields

E_{\pm}[I_{N}u_{\lambda}](\delta)-E_{\pm}[I_{N}u_{\lambda}](0)=\int_{0}^{\delta}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{\pm}[I_{N}u_{\lambda}](t)\ \mathrm{d}t,

(35)

where

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{\pm}[I_{N}u_{\lambda}](t)=\int_{\widetilde{X}}\langle\nabla I_{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}u_{\lambda},\nabla I_{N}u_{\lambda}\rangle_{2}\pm(I_{N}u_{\lambda})^{k+1}I_{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}u_{\lambda}\ \mathrm{d}(x,y),

and an integration by parts applied to the first term gives

=\int_{\widetilde{X}}\left(\pm(I_{N}u_{\lambda})^{k+1}-\Delta I_{N}u_{\lambda}\right)I_{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}u_{\lambda}\ \mathrm{d}(x,y).

Using that
u_{\lambda}
solves
\mathrm{(CP}_{k,\widetilde{X}}\mathrm{)}
, we further obtain

\displaystyle=\int_{\widetilde{X}}\left(\Delta I_{N}u_{\lambda}\mp(I_{N}u_{\lambda})^{k+1}\right)\partial_{x}\left(\Delta I_{N}u_{\lambda}\mp I_{N}(u_{\lambda}^{k+1})\right)\ \mathrm{d}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\widetilde{X}}\partial_{x}\Delta I_{N}u_{\lambda}\Delta I_{N}u_{\lambda}\ \mathrm{d}(x,y)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\int_{\widetilde{X}}\mp(I_{N}u_{\lambda})^{k+1}\partial_{x}\Delta I_{N}u_{\lambda}\mp\partial_{x}I_{N}(u_{\lambda}^{k+1})\Delta I_{N}u_{\lambda}\ \mathrm{d}(x,y)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\int_{\widetilde{X}}(I_{N}u_{\lambda})^{k+1}\partial_{x}I_{N}(u_{\lambda}^{k+1})\ \mathrm{d}(x,y)

\displaystyle\eqqcolon(I)+(II)+(III),

and these three integrals are now to be treated individually.
For the first integral, the fundamental theorem of calculus immediately gives

(I)=\int_{\widetilde{X}}\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}(\Delta I_{N}u_{\lambda})^{2}\ \mathrm{d}(x,y)=0,

and the second integral takes the form

(II)=\mp\int_{\widetilde{X}}\Delta I_{N}u_{\lambda}\partial_{x}\left(I_{N}(u_{\lambda}^{k+1})-(I_{N}u_{\lambda})^{k+1}\right)\ \mathrm{d}(x,y)

after an integration by parts.
For the third integral, another integration by parts, followed by the addition of a harmless zero term, yields

\displaystyle(III)

\displaystyle=\int_{\widetilde{X}}I_{N}(u_{\lambda}^{k+1})\partial_{x}\left(I_{N}(u_{\lambda}^{k+1})-(I_{N}u_{\lambda})^{k+1}\right)\ \mathrm{d}(x,y)

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\frac{1}{2}\int_{\widetilde{X}}\partial_{x}\left(I_{N}(u_{\lambda}^{k+1})\right)^{2}\ \mathrm{d}(x,y),

and the last term vanishes yet again by the fundamental theorem of calculus.
Inserting these partial results into (
35
) finally completes the proof.

Remark 4.6

The proof of Lemma

\mathrm{(CP}_{k,\widetilde{X}}\mathrm{)}
is locally well-posed for each

at least for some

. On

With this, we now have all the necessary general tools at our disposal and can proceed to the equation-specific arguments.

4.2.
Improved GWP for ZK on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}

We start by presenting an alternative proof of the bilinear

-estimate by Cao-Labora (see the proof of Theorem 1.3 in

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
. Then, for every
s>\frac{3}{4}

\varepsilon>0
such that

\lVert\partial_{x}(u_{1}u_{2})\rVert_{X_{s,-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lesssim\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}

(36)

holds for all
u_{1},u_{2}\in X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}

Proof.

By the definition of the
X_{s,b,\lambda}
-norms, we may, without loss of generality, assume that
\widehat{\!u_{i}}^{\lambda}\geq 0

\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\geq\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert

(\xi_{i},q_{i})
denotes the wave number associated with the factor
u_{i}
, and we use
\ast
to indicate the underlying convolution constraint
(\tau_{0},\xi_{0},q_{0})=(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2},\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},q_{1}+q_{2})
.
Furthermore, by duality and an application of Parseval’s identity, it suffices to verify

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{q_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda\\
\ast\end{subarray}}\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{s}\widehat{\!f}^{\lambda}(\tau_{0},\xi_{0},q_{0})

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdot\prod_{i=1}^{2}\widehat{\!u_{i}}^{\lambda}(\tau_{i},\xi_{i},q_{i})\ \mathrm{d}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1})\mathrm{d}(\tau_{0},\xi_{0})

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}

for every

with
\widehat{\!f}^{\lambda}\geq 0
and
\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\leq 1

(i)
\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\gg\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert

\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{s}\lesssim\lvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert^{2}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle^{s},

I_{f}\lesssim\lVert MP_{\lambda}(J^{s}u_{1},u_{2})\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}\lVert f\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}.

By using (
4

\lesssim\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{\frac{1}{2}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}},

and this works for every

(ii)
\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert

(ii.1)
\lvert\lvert(\xi_{i},q_{i})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{j},q_{j})\rvert^{2}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert^{\frac{3}{2}}

(i,j)\in\{(0,1),(0,2),(1,2)\}

In the case

\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{s}\lesssim\lvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert^{2}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle^{s}\langle(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rangle^{\frac{1}{4}},

which leads to
s>\frac{3}{4}
instead of
s>\frac{1}{2}
. For symmetry reasons, it remains only to consider the case
(i,j)=(0,1)
. By assumption (ii), it follows that

\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{s}\lesssim\lvert\lvert(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle^{s-}\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}-}\langle(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rangle^{\frac{3}{4}+},

and an application of Plancherel’s theorem, followed by Hölder’s inequality, then gives

I_{f}\lesssim\lVert MP_{\lambda}(J^{s-}\widetilde{u}_{1},J^{-\frac{1}{2}-}f)\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}\lVert J^{\frac{3}{4}+}u_{2}\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}},

with

. For the first factor, we can now employ (
5

\lesssim\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{\frac{3}{4}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}},

and the last inequality works for every
s>\frac{3}{4}
, provided that

is chosen sufficiently small.

(ii.2)
\lvert\lvert(\xi_{i},q_{i})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{j},q_{j})\rvert^{2}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert^{\frac{3}{2}}
for all tuples
(i,j)\in\{(0,1),(0,2),(1,2)\}

(ii.2.1)
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lesssim 1

which, after an application of Plancherel’s theorem and Hölder’s inequality, leads us to

I_{f}\lesssim\lVert J^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{1}J^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{2}\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}\lVert f\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}\leq\lVert J^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{1}\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{4}}\lVert J^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{2}\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{4}}\lVert f\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}.

For the first two factors, we can now invoke (
6

\lesssim\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{\frac{s}{2}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{\frac{s}{2}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}},

and this works for all

(ii.2.2)
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\gg 1
:

(ii.2.2.1)
\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{2}\rvert
or
\lvert\xi_{2}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert

1\ll\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert=\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\rvert
and
1\ll\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lesssim\lvert\xi_{0}+(-\xi_{1})\rvert

\displaystyle\lvert 3\xi_{0}^{2}-q_{0}^{2}\rvert

\displaystyle\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert,

(37)

\displaystyle\text{or}\quad\lvert 3\xi_{2}^{2}-q_{2}^{2}\rvert

\displaystyle\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{2}\rvert

holds, the bilinear refinement of the linear

-estimate (
6

. We illustrate this by working out the first case; the second case follows analogously to the first, taking into account the pointwise estimate

\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{s}\lesssim\lvert\xi_{0}+(-\xi_{1})\rvert^{\frac{1}{4}}\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{0-}\langle(-\xi_{1},-q_{1})\rangle^{\frac{3}{4}+}\langle(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rangle^{s}

Thus, let us assume that

and undoing Plancherel, followed by Hölder’s inequality, yields

I_{f}\lesssim\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}P_{\lambda}^{1}(J^{\frac{s}{2}+\frac{3}{8}}u_{1}J^{\frac{s}{2}+\frac{3}{8}}u_{2})\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}\lVert f\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}.

For the first factor, we can now invoke (
7
) in the case
\alpha=1
to further obtain

\lesssim\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{\frac{s}{2}+\frac{3}{8}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{\frac{s}{2}+\frac{3}{8}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}},

with the last estimate being valid for all

and

.
If, on the other hand, "

(ii.2.2.2)
\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert\sim\lvert\xi_{2}\rvert

First, we note that the assumption

and undoing Plancherel, followed by Hölder’s inequality gives

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert J^{\frac{s}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+}u_{1}J^{\frac{s}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+}u_{2}\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}}

An application of (
6

\displaystyle...

For the first factor, we now use (

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{-\frac{1}{2}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}

Using Proposition

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
. Then
\mathrm{(CP}_{1,\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}\mathrm{)}
is locally well-posed in

I_{N}^{-1}H^{1}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\{f\in H^{s}_{\lambda}\ |\ \lVert I_{N}f\rVert_{H^{1}_{\lambda}}<\infty\}
for every
s\in(\frac{3}{4},1)
. Moreover, for the lifespan
\delta>0
of the solution
u_{\lambda}
with initial data
u_{0,\lambda}
, one has

\delta\gtrsim\lVert I_{N}u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{H^{1}_{\lambda}}^{-\gamma}

for some
\gamma>0
, and the estimate

\lVert I_{N}u_{\lambda}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+,\lambda}^{\delta}}\lesssim\lVert I_{N}u_{0,\lambda}\rVert_{H^{1}_{\lambda}}

(38)

holds.

Proof.

It is well known that, for any given
s\in(\frac{3}{4},1)
, it suffices to find
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
such that the bilinear estimate

\lVert\partial_{x}I_{N}(u_{1}u_{2})\rVert_{X_{1,-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lesssim\lVert I_{N}u_{1}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert I_{N}u_{2}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}

(39)

holds for all functions
u_{1},u_{2}
with
I_{N}u_{1},I_{N}u_{2}\in X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}
. To this end, we write

\displaystyle\lVert\partial_{x}I_{N}(u_{1}u_{2})\rVert_{X_{1,-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon,\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\partial_{x}(I_{N}u_{1}I_{N}u_{2})\rVert_{X_{1,-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon,\lambda}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\lVert\partial_{x}(I_{N}(u_{1}u_{2})-I_{N}u_{1}I_{N}u_{2})\rVert_{X_{1,-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon,\lambda}},

and for the first term, Proposition
4.7
immediately gives

\lVert\partial_{x}(I_{N}u_{1}I_{N}u_{2})\rVert_{X_{1,-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lesssim\lVert I_{N}u_{1}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert I_{N}u_{2}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}

for all
s<1
, provided
\varepsilon>0
is chosen sufficiently small. We are therefore left to consider the second term. By duality and an application of Parseval’s identity, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\partial_{x}(I_{N}(u_{1}u_{2})-I_{N}u_{1}I_{N}u_{2})\rVert_{X_{1,-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\sim

\displaystyle\sup_{\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\leq 1}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{q_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda\\
\ast\end{subarray}}\xi_{0}\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle\frac{m(\xi_{0},q_{0})-m(\xi_{1},q_{1})m(\xi_{2},q_{2})}{m(\xi_{1},q_{1})m(\xi_{2},q_{2})}\right.

\displaystyle\cdot\left.\hat{\!f}^{\lambda}(\tau_{0},\xi_{0},q_{0})\prod_{i=1}^{2}\widehat{\!I_{N}u_{i}}^{\lambda}(\tau_{i},\xi_{i},q_{i})\ \mathrm{d}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1})\mathrm{d}(\tau_{0},\xi_{0})\right|\eqqcolon\sup_{\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\leq 1}I_{f}

with the convolution constraint
(\tau_{0},\xi_{0},q_{0})=(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2},\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},q_{1}+q_{2})
, and we now proceed to a detailed case-by-case analysis: Due to the definition of the
X_{s,b,\lambda}
-spaces, we may assume without loss of generality that
\widehat{\!f}^{\lambda},\widehat{\!I_{N}u_{1}}^{\lambda},\widehat{\!I_{N}u_{1}}^{\lambda}\geq 0
, and, for symmetry reasons, we can further restrict our attention to the frequency configuration
\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\geq\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert
.

(i)
\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\ll N
:

Since

m(\xi_{0},q_{0})-m(\xi_{1},q_{1})m(\xi_{2},q_{2})=1-1\cdot 1=0

in this case, it follows that

I_{f}=0,

and thus nothing more needs to be done.

(ii)
\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\gtrsim N
:

(ii.1)
\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\gg\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert
:

(ii.1.1)
\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert\ll N
:

In this situation, the mean value theorem yields

\displaystyle Diff_{\text{ZK}_{1}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\lvert m(\xi_{0},q_{0})-m(\xi_{1},q_{1})m(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert}{m(\xi_{1},q_{1})m(\xi_{2},q_{2})}=\frac{\lvert m(\xi_{0},q_{0})-m(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert}{m(\xi_{1},q_{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{-1}\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert,

which allows us to pass pointwise to

\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle Diff_{\text{ZK}_{1}}\lesssim\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{-\frac{1}{2}+}\lvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert^{2}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle\langle(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}-}.

If we also take
\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{-\frac{1}{2}+}\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}+}
into account, then after undoing Plancherel and applying Hölder’s inequality we obtain

I_{f}\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}+}\lVert MP_{\lambda}(J^{1}I_{N}u_{1},J^{\frac{1}{2}-}I_{N}u_{2})\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}\lVert f\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}},

and a subsequent application of (
4
) to the first norm finally leads to

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}+}\lVert I_{N}u_{1}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert I_{N}u_{2}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert f\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}+}\lVert I_{N}u_{1}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert I_{N}u_{2}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}.

In the last step, we made use of
\lVert f\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}
, which is justified as long as
0\leq\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon
is satisfied.

(ii.1.2)
\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert\gtrsim N
:

Due to the active assumption (ii.1), we still have
\lvert(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert
, which allows us to conclude

Diff_{\text{ZK}_{1}}\lesssim\frac{1}{m(\xi_{2},q_{2})}\sim N^{s-1}\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert^{1-s}.

\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\rangle Diff_{\text{mZK}_{1}}\lesssim N^{3s-3}\lvert(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})\rvert^{\frac{3}{2}-3s+}\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\rangle^{\frac{1}{4}+}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\langle(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\rangle^{\frac{3}{4}-},

N^{3s-3}\lvert(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})\rvert^{\frac{3}{2}-3s+}\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}
(
s>\frac{1}{2}
), this allows us to infer the bound

I_{f}\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}\lVert I_{x}^{1}(J^{\frac{3}{4}-}I_{N}u_{1}J^{\frac{3}{4}-}I_{N}u_{2}J^{\frac{3}{4}-}I_{N}u_{3})\rVert_{X_{\frac{1}{4}+,-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon,\infty}}.

For sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, we may now apply the trilinear local well-posedness estimate (
47
) to further deduce

...\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\lVert I_{N}u_{i}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\infty}},

which is exactly what we wanted to show.

Thus, in the non-periodic setting, the case-by-case analysis is complete and results in the commutator estimate

\lVert\partial_{x}(I_{N}(u_{1}u_{2}u_{3})-I_{N}u_{1}I_{N}u_{2}I_{N}u_{3})\rVert_{X_{1,-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon,\infty}}\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\lVert I_{N}u_{i}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\infty}}.

(50)

In view of the preceding discussion, this proves (
49
) for
\lambda=\infty
.
We now address the remaining subcases arising in the semiperiodic setting:

(ii.1.2.2.1)
{}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}

\max_{i=1}^{2}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert\ll\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert
:

In this situation, taking assumption (ii.1) into account, we obtain

\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert,\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert,\lvert\xi_{2}\rvert,\lvert\xi_{3}\rvert\ll\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert.

Together with (ii.1.2) and (ii.1.2.2), we can thus conclude that

\lvert q_{0}\rvert\sim\lvert q_{1}\rvert\sim\lvert q_{2}\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\quad\text{with}\quad\lvert\lvert q_{i}\rvert-\lvert q_{j}\rvert\rvert\ll\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\quad\text{for all}\quad i,j\in\{0,1,2\},

which is incompatible with
\lvert q_{3}\rvert\ll\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert
and the convolution constraint
q_{0}=q_{1}+q_{2}+q_{3}
. Hence, this case is excluded.

(ii.1.2.2.2)
{}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}

\max_{i=1}^{2}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert
:

(ii.1.2.2.2.1)
{}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}

\min_{i=1}^{2}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert\ll\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert
:

We first fix, for the remainder of the argument, the bound

Diff_{\text{mZK}_{1}}\lesssim N^{s-1}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{1-s}\frac{1}{m(\xi_{3},q_{3})}.

Without loss of generality, let
\max_{i=1}^{2}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert=\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert
. On the one hand, assumption (ii.1.2.2) then immediately implies

\lvert q_{2}\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\quad\text{with}\quad\lvert\lvert q_{2}\rvert-\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\rvert\ll\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert,

(51)

and on the other hand, we also have the pointwise estimates
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lesssim\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\rvert
and
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lesssim\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}\rvert
at our disposal, which pave the way for an application of (
7
):
If one of the relations

\lvert 3(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})^{2}-(q_{1}+q_{2})^{2}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\rvert

or

\lvert 3(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})^{2}-(q_{1}+q_{3})^{2}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}\rvert

holds - say, the former - then we may pass pointwise to

\displaystyle\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle Diff_{\text{mZK}_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{s-1}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{-\frac{1}{4}-s+}\frac{\langle(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+}}{m(\xi_{3},q_{3})}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdot\lvert\lvert(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rvert^{2}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{0-}\langle(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}-}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdot\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\rvert^{\frac{1}{4}}\langle(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle^{1-}\langle(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rangle^{1-}.

Taking into account that
N^{s-1}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{-\frac{1}{4}-s+}\lesssim N^{-\frac{5}{4}+}
for
s>-\frac{1}{4}
and

\frac{\langle(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+}}{m(\xi_{3},q_{3})}\lesssim 1
for
s>\frac{1}{2}
, it follows, after undoing Plancherel and applying Hölder’s inequality, that

I_{f}\lesssim N^{-\frac{5}{4}+}\lVert MP_{\lambda}(J^{0-}f,J^{\frac{1}{2}-}I_{N}\widetilde{u}_{3})\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}P_{\lambda}^{1}(J^{1-}I_{N}u_{1}J^{1-}I_{N}u_{2})\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}},

and the first factor can be further estimated using (
5
), while for the second we may appeal to (
7
). This then leads us to

...\lesssim N^{-\frac{5}{4}+}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\lVert I_{N}u_{i}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}},

and this argument closes provided
\varepsilon>0
is chosen sufficiently small.
The case
\lvert 3(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})^{2}-(q_{1}+q_{3})^{2}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}\rvert
is treated analogously and yields the same conclusion, with (
12
) applied to the pair
(f,\widetilde{u}_{2})
(note that
\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}\rvert=\lvert\xi_{0}+(-\xi_{2})\rvert
) and (
4
) applied to
(u_{1},u_{3})
.
It remains to show that the case

\displaystyle\lvert 3(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})^{2}-(q_{1}+q_{2})^{2}\rvert

\displaystyle\ll\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\rvert

(52)

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\lvert 3(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})^{2}-(q_{1}+q_{3})^{2}\rvert

\displaystyle\ll\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}\rvert.

cannot occur: From the relations in (
51
) and (
52
), and additionally taking into account

\lvert\xi_{2}\rvert,\lvert\xi_{3}\rvert,\lvert q_{3}\rvert\ll\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert
and
\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert
, it can be concluded that
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
must have opposite signs, with

\lvert\sqrt{3}\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert-\lvert q_{1}\rvert\rvert\ll\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert 2q_{1}+q_{2}\rvert\ll\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert.

However, this would imply

\lvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert^{2}\rvert\sim\lvert q_{1}\rvert^{2}\sim\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2},

which contradicts the active assumption (ii.1.2.2). Hence, this subcase is settled.

(ii.1.2.2.2.2)
{}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}

\min_{i=1}^{2}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert
:

(ii.1.2.2.2.2.1)
{}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}

\lvert\xi_{3}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert
:

In this case, we estimate the quantity
Diff_{\text{mZK}_{1}}
by

Diff_{\text{mZK}_{1}}\lesssim N^{2s-2}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2-2s}.

Since
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lesssim\lvert\xi_{3}\rvert
, we can shift almost half a derivative from the product onto the low-frequency factor
u_{3}
, which allows us to infer the pointwise bound

\displaystyle\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle Diff_{\text{mZK}_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{2s-2}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}-2s+}\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{0-}\langle(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rangle^{1-}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdot\lvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rvert^{2}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle\langle(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}-}.

Now, since we have
N^{2s-2}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}-2s+}\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}
for
s>\frac{1}{4}
, an application of Parseval’s identity, followed by Hölder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}\lVert MP_{\lambda}(J^{1}I_{N}u_{1},J^{\frac{1}{2}-}I_{N}u_{3})\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}\lVert J^{0-}fJ^{1-}I_{N}\widetilde{u}_{2}\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}\lVert MP_{\lambda}(J^{1}I_{N}u_{1},J^{\frac{1}{2}-}I_{N}u_{3})\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}\lVert J^{0-}f\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{4-}}\lVert J^{1-}I_{N}\widetilde{u}_{2}\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{4+}},

and the first factor can now be estimated by means of (
4
), while the second and third factors are controlled by (
11
) and (
10
), respectively.
Altogether, this gives

...\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\lVert I_{N}u_{i}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}},

as long as
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
is chosen sufficiently small.

(ii.1.2.2.2.2.2)
{}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}

\lvert\xi_{3}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert
:

We again fix the estimate

Diff_{\text{mZK}_{1}}\lesssim N^{2s-2}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2-2s}

and now make use of the resonance function
R_{\text{mZK}}
:
A straightforward computation shows that

R_{\text{mZK}}=-6(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3})+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\xi_{i}(\lvert(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{i},q_{i})\rvert^{2}),

and taking into account
\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\rvert\geq\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert-\lvert\xi_{3}\rvert\geq\frac{1}{2}\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert
,
\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}\rvert,\lvert\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert
, the active assumptions (ii.1.2) and (ii.1.2.2), and the fact that
\lvert\xi_{3}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert
, the inverse triangle inequality implies

\lvert R_{\text{mZK}}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2},

and hence also

\max_{i=0}^{3}\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle\coloneqq\max_{i=0}^{3}\langle\tau_{i}-\varphi(\xi_{i},q_{i})\rangle\gtrsim\lvert R_{\text{mZK}}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}.

We are now led to distinguish between two cases.
In the case
\max_{i=0}^{3}\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle=\langle\sigma_{3}\rangle
, we obtain the pointwise bound

\displaystyle\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle Diff_{\text{mZK}_{1}}\langle\sigma_{3}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{2s-2}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}-2s+}\langle(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rangle^{0-}\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{0-}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdot\langle(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle\langle(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rangle^{1-},

so that an application of Plancherel’s theorem and Hölder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}\lVert I_{N}u_{3}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert J^{1}I_{N}\widetilde{u}_{1}J^{1-}I_{N}\widetilde{u}_{2}J^{0-}f\rVert_{L_{t}^{2}L_{xy,\lambda}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}\lVert I_{N}u_{3}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert J^{1}I_{N}\widetilde{u}_{1}\rVert_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{xy,\lambda}^{2}}\lVert J^{1-}I_{N}\widetilde{u}_{2}\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{4+}}\lVert J^{0-}f\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{4-}}

(
N^{2s-2}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}-2s+}\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}
for
s>\frac{1}{4}
). By employing the Sobolev embedding theorem in the
t
-variable for the second factor and applying estimates (
10
) and (
11
) to the third and fourth factors, respectively, we further obtain

...\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\lVert I_{N}u_{i}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}},

which holds for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
. It thus remains to consider the case
\max_{i=0}^{3}\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle\neq\langle\sigma_{3}\rangle
, for instance
\max_{i=0}^{3}\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle=\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle
(the other cases are treated analogously): In this situation, we can infer the pointwise bound

\displaystyle\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle Diff_{\text{mZK}_{1}}\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{2s-2}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}-2s+}\langle(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rangle^{0-}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdot\langle(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle^{1-}\langle(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rangle^{1-},

which, taking into account that
N^{2s-2}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}-2s+}\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}-}
for
s>\frac{1}{4}
, leads us to

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert J^{1-}I_{N}u_{1}J^{1-}I_{N}u_{2}J^{0-}I_{N}u_{3}\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lVert J^{1-}I_{N}u_{1}\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{4}}\lVert J^{1-}I_{N}u_{2}\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{4}}\lVert J^{0-}I_{N}u_{3}\rVert_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{xy,\lambda}^{\infty}}.

We then apply the Sobolev embedding theorem in the
x,y
-variables to the low-frequency factor
u_{3}
, while the remaining two factors can be handled using estimate (
6
). This ultimately yields

...\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\lVert I_{N}u_{i}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}},

which again works for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, thereby completing the discussion of this subcase as well.

(ii.2)
{}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}

\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rvert
:

(ii.2.1)
{}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}

\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\gg\lvert(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rvert
:

In this situation, we write

Diff_{\text{mZK}_{1}}\lesssim N^{3s-3}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{3-3s},

which allows us to deduce the pointwise bound

\displaystyle\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle Diff_{\text{mZK}_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{3s-3}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{\frac{3}{2}-3s+}\langle(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rangle^{1-}\langle(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rangle^{1-}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdot\lvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rvert^{2}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{0-}\langle(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}-}.

Taking into account that
N^{3s-3}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{\frac{3}{2}-3s+}\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}
holds for
s>\frac{1}{2}
, it follows - after undoing Plancherel and applying Hölder’s inequality - that

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}\lVert MP_{\lambda}(J^{0-}f,J^{\frac{1}{2}-}I_{N}\widetilde{u}_{1})\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}\lVert J^{1-}I_{N}u_{2}J^{1-}I_{N}u_{3}\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}\lVert MP_{\lambda}(J^{0-}f,J^{\frac{1}{2}-}I_{N}\widetilde{u}_{1})\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{2}}\lVert J^{1-}I_{N}u_{2}\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{4}}\lVert J^{1-}I_{N}u_{3}\rVert_{L_{txy,\lambda}^{4}},

and the first factor can now be treated using (
5
), while the second and fourth factors can be handled using (
6
). Altogether, this yields

...\lesssim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\lVert I_{N}u_{i}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}},

which holds provided that
\varepsilon>0
is chosen sufficiently small.

(ii.2.2)
{}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}

\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rvert
:

In this final subcase, we are in the regime

\lvert(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rvert\gtrsim N,

which implies

Diff_{\text{mZK}_{1}}\lesssim N^{2s-2}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2-2s}.

As a consequence, we obtain the pointwise bound

\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle Diff_{\text{mZK}_{1}}\lesssim N^{2s-2}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{\frac{11}{12}-2s+}\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{\frac{11}{24}+}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\langle(\xi_{i},q_{i})\rangle^{\frac{13}{24}-},

and since
N^{2s-2}\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{\frac{11}{12}-2s+}\lesssim N^{-\frac{13}{12}+}
holds for
s>\frac{11}{24}
, undoing Plancherel and appealing to duality yields

I_{f}\lesssim N^{-\frac{13}{12}+}\lVert I_{x}^{1}(J^{\frac{13}{24}-}I_{N}u_{1}J^{\frac{13}{24}-}I_{N}u_{2}J^{\frac{13}{24}-}I_{N}u_{3})\rVert_{X_{\frac{11}{24}+,-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon,\lambda}}.

For this remaining term, we may now invoke the trilinear estimate (
45
) for some small number
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, to further obtain

...\lesssim N^{-\frac{13}{12}+}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\lVert I_{N}u_{i}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}},

wherein we harmlessly absorbed the resulting loss of
\lambda^{\widetilde{\varepsilon}}\lesssim N^{100\widetilde{\varepsilon}}\sim N^{0+}
into the factor
N^{-\frac{13}{12}+}
.

Gathering together all intermediate bounds in the semiperiodic setting, we conclude that

\lVert\partial_{x}(I_{N}(u_{1}u_{2}u_{3})-I_{N}u_{1}I_{N}u_{2}I_{N}u_{3})\rVert_{X_{1,-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon,\lambda}}\lesssim N^{-\frac{13}{12}+}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\lVert I_{N}u_{i}\rVert_{X_{1,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\lambda}},

(53)

which, combined with the preliminary discussion preceding this case-by-case analysis, yields the desired estimate (
49
).

5.1.

We devote this section to the proof of Theorem

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
K_{1}>0
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, and let
(a_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a sequence of nonnegative real numbers with

a_{m+1}\leq a_{m}+K_{1}\left(1+a_{m}^{1-\varepsilon}\right)\qquad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Then for every
d>\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
, there exists a constant
K_{2}=K_{2}(K_{1},d)
, such that

a_{m+1}\leq K_{2}(1+m)^{d}(1+a_{0})\qquad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

holds.

For the remainder of this section, let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\in 2\mathbb{N}
, and let
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
be a (small) real-valued initial datum. Moreover, we denote the associated unique global real-valued solution of
\mathrm{(CP}_{k,\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\mathrm{)}
by
u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}))\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}))\cap\left(\bigcap_{T>0}X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+}^{T}\right)
, and we note that the existence of such a solution was established by Molinet and Pilod in the case
k=1
(see Theorem 1.3 in
[
29
]
), while the cases
k\geq 2
were treated by Farah and Molinet (see Theorem 1.2 in
[
14
]
- in general, one has to impose a smallness condition on the
L^{2}
- or
H^{1}
-norm of the initial data).

We now outline the strategy of the proof: Let
\delta_{k}>0
denote the lifespan of the solution
u
obtained after a single iteration of the fixed-point argument. For
t\in[0,\frac{\delta_{k}}{2}]
, we consider differences

\lVert u(t)\rVert_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}-\lVert u(0)\rVert_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2},

and derive an estimate of the form

\lVert u(t)\rVert_{H^{s}}\leq\lVert u(0)\rVert_{H^{s}}+C\lVert u(0)\rVert_{H^{s}}^{1-\varepsilon},

by strongly relying on the Strichartz-type estimates collected in Section 3 and on the continuous dependence of the solution on the initial data. Having accomplished this, Theorem
1.3
can then be proved with the help of Lemma
5.1
in the context of a simple iteration argument.

We start with

Lemma 5.2
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\geq 2}
and
t\in[0,\frac{\delta_{k}}{2}]
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lVert u(t)\rVert_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}-\lVert u(0)\rVert_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\mp\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\lvert\alpha_{i}\rvert\leq s\\
\lvert\alpha_{1}\rvert+...+\lvert\alpha_{k+1}\rvert=s+1\end{subarray}}C(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k+1})\int_{0}^{t}\langle\prod_{i=1}^{k+1}D^{\alpha_{i}}u(t^{\prime}),I^{s}u(t^{\prime})\rangle_{L^{2}_{xy}}\ dt^{\prime}.

Proof.

Since
u
is a real-valued solution of
k
-gZK, we have that

\displaystyle\lVert u(t)\rVert_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}-\lVert u(0)\rVert_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\prime}}\lVert u(t^{\prime})\rVert_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}\ \mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\prime}}\langle I^{s}u(t^{\prime}),I^{s}u(t^{\prime})\rangle_{L_{xy}^{2}}\ \mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{t}\langle I^{s}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t^{\prime}}\left(t^{\prime}\right),I^{s}u(t^{\prime})\rangle_{L_{xy}^{2}}\ \mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{-2\int_{0}^{t}\langle I^{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Delta u(t^{\prime}),I^{s}u(t^{\prime})\rangle_{L_{xy}^{2}}\ \mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}_{\eqqcolon\ (I)}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \underbrace{\mp 2\int_{0}^{t}\langle I^{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(u^{k+1}\right)(t^{\prime}),I^{s}u(t^{\prime})\rangle_{L_{xy}^{2}}\ \mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}_{\eqqcolon\ (II)}.

Integration by parts at the
L^{2}_{xy}
level then shows that
(I)
vanishes, and to further treat
(II)
, we can write

By interpolation, the restriction
s\in 2\mathbb{N}
in Theorem
1.3
can be relaxed, provided the initial data are sufficiently regular. This simple argument is the content of the following

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\alpha(k,s)\coloneqq\begin{cases}4(s-1),&\text{if}\quad k=1,\\
s-1,&\text{if}\quad k\geq 2,\end{cases}

s\in(1,2m)
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
u_{0}\in H^{2m}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
, and let
u
be the associated global solution of
\mathrm{(CP}_{k,\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\mathrm{)}
, as stated in Theorem
1.3
. Then, for every
\alpha>\alpha(k,s)
, there exists a constant
C=C(k,\alpha,\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{H^{1}})>0
such that

\lVert u(t)\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim C(1+\lvert t\rvert)^{\alpha}(1+\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{H^{2m}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})})

holds true for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

We consider the case
k=1
. The cases
k\geq 2
can be handled analogously.
Given
s\in(1,2m)
, there exists
\theta\in(0,1)
such that

(1-\theta)+\theta 2m=s\Leftrightarrow\theta=\frac{s-1}{2m-1}.

Now, applying Theorem
1.3
at the regularity level

and taking into account that
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{H^{1}}<\infty
and
\frac{s-1}{2m-1}\leq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert u(t)\rVert_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u(t)\rVert_{H^{1}}^{1-\theta}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{H^{2m}}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u(t)\rVert_{H^{1}}^{1-\theta}(1+\lvert t\rvert)^{\theta(4(2m-1)+\varepsilon)}(1+\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{H^{2m}})^{\theta}

for all
