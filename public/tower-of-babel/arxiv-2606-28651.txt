The Thickness of Infinite Sidon Sets
Kevin O’Bryant

Abstract
Let γ ≥ 1 \gamma\geq 1 . A set 𝒜 \mathcal{A} of nonnegative integers is a Sidon set if for each d > 0 d>0 there is at most one pair ( a , b ) ∈ 𝒜 × 𝒜 (a,b)\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A} with d = a − b d=a-b . If there are at most γ \gamma pairs, then 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is a γ \gamma -Golomb ruler. We prove that if 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is a γ \gamma -Golomb ruler, then lim inf n → ∞ | 𝒜 ∩ [ 0 , n ) | n / log ⁡ n ≤ 2 log ⁡ 2 ​ γ . \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\mathcal{A}\cap[0,n)|}{\sqrt{n/\log n}}\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{\log 2}}\sqrt{\gamma}. There is a γ \gamma -Golomb ruler 𝒢 \mathcal{G} with lim sup n → ∞ | 𝒢 ∩ [ 0 , n ) | n ≥ 1 2 ​ γ . \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\mathcal{G}\cap[0,n)|}{\sqrt{n}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\gamma}.



1
Introduction

Let
\gamma\geq 1
. A set
\mathcal{A}
of nonnegative integers is a
\gamma
-Golomb ruler if each
d>0
has at most
\gamma
pairs
(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}
with
d=a-b
. With
\gamma=1
, these are known as Sidon sets in combinatorics, Golomb rulers in recreational math, Babcock sets in electrical engineering, and
B_{2}
sequences in number theory. See the author’s comprehensive annotated bibliography
[
4
]
for hundreds of citations.

Our main result is giving the constant in the following theorem. Erdős
[
6
]
proved finiteness for Sidon sets, and Cilleruelo
[
2
]
proved
8\sqrt{7}\approx 21.2
, and suggests bringing the constant down to
4
as an exercise. We bring the constant down to
2/\sqrt{\log 2}\approx 2.4
. The extension from Sidon sets to
\gamma
-Golomb rulers is new, but easy.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\gamma
be a positive integer.
If
\mathcal{A}
is a
\gamma
-Golomb ruler, then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{A(n)}{\sqrt{n/\log(n)}}\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{\log 2}}\,\sqrt{\gamma}.

There is a counterpart to Theorem
1
that also originates with Erdős
[
6
]
, who gave the constant
\frac{1}{2}
. This was improved by Krückeberg to
2^{-1/2}
. We extend this result to
\gamma
-Golomb rulers.

Theorem 2
.

Every
\gamma
-Golomb ruler
\mathcal{A}
has

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\mathcal{A}\cap[0,n)|}{\sqrt{n}}\leq\sqrt{\gamma}.

There is a
\gamma
-Golomb ruler
\mathcal{G}
with

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\mathcal{G}\cap[0,n)|}{\sqrt{n}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\sqrt{\gamma}.

Naturally, one asks if these results are close to best possible. Ruzsa
[
5
]
gives a beautiful construction of a Sidon set with
A(n)\sim n^{-1+\sqrt{2}}
, and this remains the record for an infinite set. Lemmas
3
and
4
below give upper and lower bounds on the size of
\gamma
-Golomb rulers in
[0,N)
.

3
The Proof of Theorem
1

The general structure of the proof is the same as Erdős’s for Sidon sets: we break the
\gamma
-Golomb ruler
\mathcal{A}
into blocks of length
N
, and will eventually take
N\to\infty
. We consider the energy

E\coloneqq\sum_{\ell}\left|\mathcal{A}\cap[(\ell-1)N,\ell N)\right|^{2}

and get an upper bound from the
\gamma
-Golomb property and a lower bound from Cauchy’s Inequality.

We believe that we have fully optimized this argument. Nevertheless, after the proof, we discuss some alternative approaches and make some guesses about where improvements could originate.

Proof.

Let
N
be a positive integer (we will take it to
\infty
) and set

\displaystyle A(n)

\displaystyle\coloneqq|\mathcal{A}\cap[0,n)|

\displaystyle\psi(n)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\log(en)

(1)

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{N}{\psi(N)}

(2)

\displaystyle\tau_{N}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf_{n\geq N}A(n)\sqrt{\frac{\psi(n)}{n}}.

(3)

We introduce the “
e
” in
\psi
so that
\psi:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
, avoiding some division-by-zero pedantism.
As
N
grows,
\tau_{N}
increases, and the content of Theorem
1
is that
\tau_{N}\leq 2/\sqrt{\log 2}.

We consider

F_{\ell}^{(t)}\coloneqq\left|\mathcal{A}\cap[\,t+(\ell-1)N,\ t+\ell N)\right|,

where
0\leq t<N
and
1\leq\ell\leq M
.
Any particular unordered pair
a<b
in
\binom{\mathcal{A}}{2}
with difference
d=b-a
(where
1\leq d<N
) is usually counted in

F_{\ell}^{(t)}
for
N-d
different values of
t
, but will lie in fewer if
b<N
, so that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\binom{F_{\ell}^{(t)}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\binom{F_{\ell}^{(t)}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{a,b\}\subseteq\mathcal{A}\\
1\leq a-b<N\end{subarray}}\bigl(N-(a-b)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\sum_{d=1}^{N-1}(N-d)

\displaystyle=\gamma\,\tfrac{N(N-1)}{2}.

(4)

Fix an offset
T
attaining at most the average, and define

F_{\ell}\coloneqq F_{\ell}^{(T)}.

The proof now proceeds by finding an upper and lower bound on the energy

E\coloneqq\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}F_{\ell}^{2}.

From Line (
4
) we have

\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\binom{F_{\ell}}{2}\leq\frac{\gamma(N-1)}{2}.

(5)

But
F_{\ell}^{2}=2\binom{F_{\ell}}{2}+F_{\ell}
and

\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}F_{\ell}=A(T+MN)-A(T),

so that the inequality in Line (
5
) gives

E=2\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\binom{F_{\ell}}{2}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}F_{\ell}\leq\gamma(N-1)+A(T+MN)-A(T).

Since
\mathcal{A}\cap[T,T+MN)
is a
\gamma
-Golomb ruler, and
MN\leq N^{2}/\log eN
, Lemma
3
gives

A(T+MN)-A(T)\leq 3\sqrt{\gamma MN}=o(N).

Hence, we have the upper bound on
E
:

E\leq\gamma N+o(N).

(6)

The lower bound on
E
uses Cauchy’s Inequality with the weights

w_{\ell}\coloneqq\begin{cases}(\ell\,\psi(\ell N))^{-1/2}&1\leq\ell\leq M;\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

By Cauchy’s inequality,

E\coloneqq\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}F_{\ell}^{2}\geq\frac{\bigl(\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}w_{\ell}F_{\ell}\bigr)^{2}}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}w_{\ell}^{2}}.

(7)

We need an upper bound on the denominator
{\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}w_{\ell}^{2}}
, and a lower bound on the numerator
{\bigl(\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}w_{\ell}F_{\ell}\bigr)^{2}}
.

Claim 5
.

\displaystyle{\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}w_{\ell}^{2}}\leq\log 2+o(1)
.

As
x\mapsto\bigl(x\,\psi(xN)\bigr)^{-1}
is decreasing,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}w_{\ell}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\frac{1}{\ell\,\psi(\ell N)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\psi(N)}+\int_{1}^{M}\frac{dx}{x\,\psi(xN)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\psi(N)}+\log\frac{\psi(MN)}{\psi(N)}.

Now, by routine calculus,
\log(e\frac{x}{\log(ex)}\cdot x)/\log(ex)
increases to
2
while
1/\psi(x)
decreases to
0
, so that

\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}w_{\ell}^{2}\leq o(1)+\log 2,

(8)

as claimed in Claim
5
.

Claim 6
.

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}w_{\ell}F_{\ell}\bigg)^{2}\geq\tau_{N}^{2}\left(\frac{\log 2}{2}\right)^{2}N+o(N).

To ease the notation visually, set
a_{i}=A(T+iN)
, and note that
F_{\ell}=a_{\ell}-a_{\ell-1}
. Also, set
M^{\prime}=\mbox{$\left\lfloor M\right\rfloor$}
. Rearranging the summation gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{M^{\prime}}w_{\ell}F_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{M^{\prime}}w_{\ell}(a_{\ell}-a_{\ell-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{M^{\prime}-1}a_{\ell}\,(w_{\ell}-w_{\ell+1})+w_{M^{\prime}}a_{M^{\prime}}-w_{1}a_{0}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\ell=1}^{M^{\prime}-1}a_{\ell}\,(w_{\ell}-w_{\ell+1})-w_{1}a_{0}.

Now, using Lemma
3
we have

w_{1}a_{0}=\frac{A(T)}{\sqrt{\log(eN)}}\leq\frac{3\sqrt{\gamma T}}{\sqrt{\log(eN)}}=o(\sqrt{N})

For
1\leq\ell\leq M^{\prime}-1
we have

T+\ell N\geq\ell N\geq N
, so the definition of
\tau_{N}
on Line (
3
) yields

a_{\ell}\geq\tau_{N}\sqrt{\ell N/\psi(\ell N)}
. Writing

\beta_{\ell}\coloneqq\sqrt{\ell N/\psi(\ell N)}

and using
w_{\ell}-w_{\ell+1}\geq 0
,

\sum_{\ell=1}^{M^{\prime}}w_{\ell}F_{\ell}\geq\tau_{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M^{\prime}-1}\beta_{\ell}(w_{\ell}-w_{\ell+1})-o(\sqrt{N}).

By rearranging the summation again, with
\beta_{0}\coloneqq 0
,

\sum_{\ell=1}^{M^{\prime}-1}\beta_{\ell}(w_{\ell}-w_{\ell+1})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{M^{\prime}-1}w_{\ell}(\beta_{\ell}-\beta_{\ell-1})-w_{{M^{\prime}}}\beta_{M^{\prime}-1}.

Since the
\beta
sequence is positive and strictly increasing and the
w
sequence is positive, we have

0<w_{M^{\prime}}\beta_{M^{\prime}-1}\leq w_{M^{\prime}}\beta_{M^{\prime}}=\frac{\sqrt{M^{\prime}}}{\psi(M^{\prime}N)}=o(\sqrt{N}).

At this point, we have

\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}w_{\ell}F_{\ell}\geq o(\sqrt{N})+\tau_{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M^{\prime}}w_{\ell}(\beta_{\ell}-\beta_{\ell-1}),

where
w_{\ell}
and
\beta_{\ell}
are explicit sequences with easily handled properties. Set
w(x)=(x\log(exN))^{-1/2}
and
\beta(x)=(xN/\log(exN))^{1/2}
, and we find that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{M^{\prime}}w_{\ell}(\beta_{\ell}-\beta_{\ell-1})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{1}^{M^{\prime}-1}w(x)\beta^{\prime}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2}\left(\log\left(\frac{\psi((M^{\prime}-1)N)}{\psi(N)}\right)-\frac{\log(M^{\prime}-1)}{\psi(N)\psi((M^{\prime}-1)N)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2}\left(\log(2+o(1))-o(1)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\log 2}{2}\sqrt{N}+o(\sqrt{N}).

Thus,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}w_{\ell}F_{\ell}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(o(\sqrt{N})+\tau_{N}\left(\frac{\log 2}{2}\sqrt{N}+o(\sqrt{N})\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\tau_{N}^{2}\frac{\log^{2}2}{4}N+o(N).

Thus, Claim
6
is verified.

Using Claim
5
and Claim
6
in Inequality (
7
), we get a lower bound on the energy:

E\geq\frac{\tau_{N}^{2}\bigl(\tfrac{\log 2}{2}\bigr)^{2}N\,(1+o(1))}{(\log 2)(1+o(1))}=\tau_{N}^{2}\,\frac{\log 2}{4}\,N+o(N).

(9)

Compare the upper bound on Line (
6
), with
N\to\infty
, and we have

\tau_{N}^{2}\frac{\log 2}{4}\leq\gamma,

exactly the inequality stated in Theorem
1
.
∎

3.1
Nonrigorous thoughts about the proof

The author has given each of the suggestions below serious thought and effort and has received no benefit from them, but is not convinced that no benefit is possible.

One only needs to take
N
through a subsequence to
\infty
, and this freedom plays no role in the proof. Perhaps some
N
allow for an improvement to Inequality (
5
); perhaps it is possible to also average over some values of
N
.

The upper bound in Inequality (
5
) is unchanged if one sums
\ell
to
\infty
. That is,
M
plays no role here. This suggests that there is a cleaner way to handle the infinite set
\mathcal{A}
instead of as a series of truncations
\mathcal{A}\cap[0,MN)=\mathcal{A}\cap[0,N^{2}/\log(eN))
. To this end, note that if we define energy as

E_{b}\coloneqq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\binom{F_{\ell}}{2},

then
E_{b}
is finite, and in fact the upper bound on
E_{b}
is
\gamma(N-1)/2
, and is a few lines easier to prove than the upper bound on
E
. The lower bound, however, seems not to benefit at all and the argument demands some truncation anyway.

The transition to
F_{\ell}
in the argument is (up to normalization) that of taking the conditional expectation of the indicator function of
\mathcal{A}
relative to the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\{[T+iN,T+(i+1)N):i\geq 0\}
. Perhaps there is some reverse-martingale behind the scenes, and the current work is merely the first step of that martingale.

Related to the last suggestion, there are powerful entropy inequalities that may be relevant, with entropy taking the role played by energy.

The use of Cauchy’s Inequality (
7
) is optimal only if
F_{\ell}\approx c\cdot w_{\ell}
for some constant
c
. The author knows no reason why
F_{\ell}
would decay this smoothly. For example, if
F_{\ell}
decreases consistently, then the set

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{N}\coloneqq(T+MN-\mathcal{A})\cap[0,MN)

is a
\gamma
-Golomb ruler contained in
[0,MN)
whose
F_{\ell}
sequence is consistently increasing, and
A(T+MN)-A(T)=\widetilde{A}_{N}(MN)
. However, the
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{N}
set does not have the same lower bound on its infimum, and so it is unclear how to use this to advantage.

4
Proof of Theorem
2

This proof closely follows that given in Halberstam & Roth
[
3
]
, modified to allow
\gamma>1
.

The first sentence of Theorem
2
follows immediately from Lemma
3
. For the second sentence, we need to construct an infinite
\gamma
-Golomb ruler, which we will do by taking a union of finite rulers, discarding a negligible number of elements at each stage. The next lemma addresses the basic situation of combining two sets.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\gamma\geq 1
, and let
V_{1}\leq V_{2}<W_{1}\leq W_{2}
be four nonnegative integers with

W_{1}-V_{2}>\max(V_{2}-V_{1},W_{2}-W_{1}).

Let
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W}
be
\gamma
-Golomb rulers contained in
[V_{1},V_{2}]
,
[W_{1},W_{2}]
, respectively.
Then there is a subset
\mathcal{W}^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{W}
with

|\mathcal{W}^{*}|\geq|\mathcal{W}|-\gamma\,\binom{|\mathcal{V}|}{2}

such that
\mathcal{V}\cup\mathcal{W}^{*}
is a
\gamma
-Golomb ruler.

Proof.

Classify the ordered pairs
(a,b)\in(\mathcal{V}\cup\mathcal{W})^{2}
with
a<b
by the their coordinates:

type
VV
:

a,b\in\mathcal{V}
, and so
d=b-a\leq V_{2}-V_{1}<W_{1}-V_{2}
;

type
VW
:

a\in\mathcal{V},b\in\mathcal{W}
, and so
d=b-a\geq W_{1}-V_{2}
;

type
WW
:

a,b\in\mathcal{W}
, and so
d=b-a\leq W_{2}-W_{1}<W_{1}-V_{2}
.

The differences
d\in\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{V}
can arise from at most
\gamma
type
VV
pairs. Set
WW_{d}
to be the left endpoint of each type
WW
pair with difference
d
, and note that
|WW_{d}|\leq\gamma
, and

R\coloneqq\bigcup_{d\in\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{V}}WW_{d},\qquad\left|R\right|\leq\gamma\cdot|(\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{V})\cap\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}|\leq\gamma\binom{|\mathcal{V}|}{2}.

Set
\mathcal{W}^{*}\coloneqq\mathcal{W}\setminus R
. Further, note now that each
d\in\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{V}
has
d\not\in\mathcal{W}^{*}-\mathcal{W}^{*}
and
d\not\in\mathcal{W}^{*}-\mathcal{V}
because
V_{2}-V_{1}<W_{1}-V_{2}
. Thus,
d
has at most
\gamma
representations as a difference of elements in
\mathcal{V}\cup\mathcal{W}^{*}
.

Any difference
d
that is less than
W_{1}-V_{2}
can only arise from type
VV
and type
WW
, and so can only arise from at most
\gamma
pairs of
\mathcal{V}\cup\mathcal{W}^{*}
.

It remains to consider differences
d\geq W_{1}-V_{2}
. These can only arise from type
VW
pairs. But if

d=w-v=w^{\prime}-v^{\prime},\qquad w>w^{\prime},v<v^{\prime},\qquad w,w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}^{*},v,v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V},

then,
w-w^{\prime}=v-v^{\prime}
. But from the above construction, no difference in
\mathcal{V}-\mathcal{V}
is in
\mathcal{W}^{*}-\mathcal{W}^{*}
. Thus, no such difference occurs more than once.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
q\geq 3
be a prime power with
q\equiv 1\pmod{\gamma}
. Set
q_{1}=q
and
q_{i+1}=q_{i}^{3}
, so that each
q_{i}
is a prime power with
q_{i}\equiv 1\pmod{\gamma}
. Set

m_{i}=\frac{q_{i}^{2}-1}{\gamma}.

By Lemma
4
, we can choose a
\gamma
-Golomb ruler
\mathcal{B}_{i}\subseteq[q_{i}+m_{i},q_{i}+2m_{i})
with
|\mathcal{B}_{i}|=q_{i}
and, so that

\frac{|\mathcal{B}_{i}|}{\sqrt{m_{i}}}=\frac{q_{i}}{\sqrt{(q_{i}^{2}-1)/\gamma}}\longrightarrow\sqrt{\gamma}.

We build
\mathcal{G}
as an increasing union
\mathcal{G}=\bigcup_{i}\mathcal{G}_{i}
, with
\mathcal{G}_{i}
to be defined inductively.
Set
\mathcal{G}_{1}=\mathcal{B}_{1}\subseteq[q_{1}+m_{1},q_{1}+2m_{1})
. For
i\geq 1
,
suppose
\mathcal{G}_{i}
has been constructed and is a
\gamma
-Golomb ruler
contained in
[0,q_{i}+2m_{i})
. Put

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}_{i}

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle[0,q_{i}+2m_{i}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}_{i+1}

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle[q_{i+1}+m_{i+1},q_{i+1}+2m_{i+1}).

With

V_{1}=0,V_{2}=q_{i}+2m_{i},W_{1}=q_{i+1}+m_{i+1},W_{2}=q_{i+1}+2m_{i+1},

The inequalities of Lemma
7
hold as

\displaystyle W_{1}-V_{2}

\displaystyle=q_{i+1}+m_{i+1}-q_{i}-2m_{i}=q_{i}^{3}+\frac{q_{i}^{6}-1}{\gamma}-q_{i}-2\frac{q_{i}^{2}-1}{\gamma}

\displaystyle V_{2}-V_{1}

\displaystyle=q_{i}+2m_{i}=q_{i}+2\frac{q_{i}^{2}-1}{\gamma}

\displaystyle W_{2}-W_{1}

\displaystyle=m_{i+1}=\frac{q_{i}^{6}-1}{\gamma},

and so (using
q_{i}\geq q\geq 3
and
\gamma\geq 1
)

\displaystyle(W_{1}-V_{2})-(V_{2}-V_{1})

\displaystyle=q_{i}^{3}+\frac{q_{i}^{6}-1}{\gamma}-2q_{i}-4\frac{q_{i}^{2}-1}{\gamma}

\displaystyle\geq q_{i}^{3}+q_{i}^{6}-1-2q_{i}-4q_{i}^{2}-4

\displaystyle>0

and

\displaystyle(W_{1}-V_{2})-(W_{2}-W_{1})

\displaystyle=q_{i}^{3}+\frac{q_{i}^{6}-1}{\gamma}-q_{i}-2\frac{q_{i}^{2}-1}{\gamma}-\frac{q_{i}^{6}-1}{\gamma}

\displaystyle=q_{i}^{3}-q_{i}-2\frac{q_{i}^{2}-1}{\gamma}

\displaystyle\geq q_{i}^{3}-q_{i}-2(q_{i}^{2}-1)

\displaystyle=(q_{i}-2)(q_{i}-1)(q_{i}+1)

\displaystyle>0.

Apply Lemma
7
to obtain
\mathcal{W}^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{W}
with

\mathcal{V}\cup\mathcal{W}^{*}
a
\gamma
-Golomb ruler, and set

\mathcal{G}_{i+1}\coloneqq\mathcal{G}_{i}\cup\mathcal{W}^{*}\subseteq[0,\,q_{i+1}+2m_{i+1}).

Each
\mathcal{G}_{i+1}
is a
\gamma
-Golomb ruler, and the union
\mathcal{G}
is a

\gamma
-Golomb ruler because any violating configuration involves finitely many elements and hence lies in some
\mathcal{G}_{i+1}
.

We now consider the size of
\mathcal{G}_{i+1}
. Clearly
\mathcal{G}_{i+1}=\mathcal{G}_{i}\cup W^{*}
, so that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{i+1}|

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{G}_{i}|+|\mathcal{B}_{i+1}|-|\mathcal{B}_{i+1}\setminus\mathcal{W}^{*}|

\displaystyle\geq q_{i+1}-\gamma\binom{|\mathcal{V}|}{2}

\displaystyle=q_{i}^{3}-\gamma\binom{|\mathcal{G}_{i}|}{2}.

Since
\mathcal{G}_{i}
is a
\gamma
-Golomb ruler contained in
[0,q_{i}+2m_{i})
, we know that
|\mathcal{G}_{i}|\leq 3\sqrt{q_{i}+2m_{i}}
, so that

\gamma\binom{|\mathcal{G}_{i}|}{2}=O(q_{i}^{2}).

Thus,
|\mathcal{G}_{i+1}|\geq q_{i}^{3}-O(q_{i}^{2}).

We now show that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{|\mathcal{G}\cap[0,q_{i+1}+2m_{i+1})|}{\sqrt{q_{i+1}+2m_{i+1}}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{G}\cap[0,q_{i+1}+2m_{i+1})|}{\sqrt{q_{i+1}+2m_{i+1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathcal{G}_{i+1}|}{\sqrt{q_{i}^{3}+2(q_{i}^{6}-1)/\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{i}^{3}-O(q_{i}^{2})}{\sqrt{2/\gamma}\,q_{i}^{3}+O(1)}

\displaystyle\to\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{\sqrt{2}}.

This proves the Theorem.
∎
