Cartan subalgebras in self-similar graph C ∗ C^{*} -algebras
Dawn Archey, Anna Duwenig, Shanshan Hua, Kathryn McCormick, Rachael Norton, Dilian Yang

Abstract
. For a self-similar graph ( G , E ) (G,E) , we find a distinguished subgroupoid of the associated path groupoid 𝒢 G , E \mathcal{G}_{G,E} – the symmetric cycline subgroupoid 𝒮 sym \mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}} . If the acting group G G is abelian, we show that 𝒮 sym \mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}} is open, abelian, and normal. For G = ℤ G=\mathbb{Z} , we describe the dual bundle 𝒮 ^ sym \widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}} of 𝒮 sym \mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}} which can be used to provide a different groupoid model for the self-similar graph C ∗ C^{*} -algebra 𝒪 ℤ , E ≅ C r ∗ ​ ( 𝒢 ℤ , E ) \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Z},E}\cong C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Z},E}) . For a large class of self-similar graphs ( ℤ , E ) (\mathbb{Z},E) , we further prove that 𝒮 sym \mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}} is maximal among open abelian subgroupoids of Iso ⁡ ( 𝒢 ℤ , E ) ∘ \operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Z},E})^{\circ} and closed in 𝒢 ℤ , E \mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Z},E} , so that it gives rise to a Cartan subalgebra of 𝒪 ℤ , E \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Z},E} . This result seems new even for genuine actions. Our proofs heavily rely on careful studies of dynamical behaviours of cycline triples of ( ℤ , E ) (\mathbb{Z},E) and on a dynamical-flavour classification for the vertices of E E . Some results hold in more general settings and may be of independent interest.



1.
Introduction

Self-similar graph
C^{*}
-algebras
\mathcal{O}_{G,E}
were introduced by Exel and Pardo in
[
10
]
as a unifying framework encompassing both Katsura algebras
\mathcal{O}_{A,B}
(
[
11
]
) and Nekrashevych algebras arising from self-similar groups (
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
).
A self-similar graph is a pair
(G,E)
consisting of a discrete countable group
G
and a directed graph
E
such that
G
acts on
E
by graph automorphisms from the left, while
E
‘acts’ on
G
from the right through the
restriction map

(g,\mu)\mapsto g|_{\mu}
, in such a way that for all
g\in G

and finite paths
\mu,\nu
in
E
,
the formula

g\cdot(\mu\nu)=(g\cdot\mu)(g|_{\mu}\cdot\nu)

holds. Under the additional assumptions that
G
is amenable and the action is

pseudo-free
(see Definition
2.9
), the
self-similar graph
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{O}_{G,E}
admits a natural groupoid model
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
, meaning that
\mathcal{O}_{G,E}\cong C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})\cong C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})
(
[
10
, Theorem 9.6, Corollary 10.18]
); the groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
is called the
self-similar path groupoid
. This places these algebras within the broader framework of étale groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras. Such constructions have since been extended to settings such as topological graphs (
[
2
]
) and higher-rank graphs (
[
12
,
14
]
).

Self-similar graph
C^{*}
-algebras provide a rich source of examples, bridging combinatorial, dynamical, and operator-algebraic perspectives. A special case is
where
E
‘acts’ trivially on
G
meaning that
g|_{\mu}=g
for all
\mu\in E
; we then
say that
G
acts on
E

genuinely
(rather than self-similarly). Here, the algebra
\mathcal{O}_{G,E}
is just the crossed product
C^{*}(E)\rtimes G
, where the action is induced by the given action of
G
on
E
.

Another fundamental example of self-similar graph
C^{*}
-algebras are the so-called
Katsura algebras
\mathcal{O}_{A,B}
, associated to pairs
A,B
of matrices. These all fall into the special case when
G=\mathbb{Z}
, and they play an important role in the classification program: every UCT Kirchberg algebra (i.e., any
C^{*}
-algebra in the UCT class that is simple, separable, nuclear, purely infinite) can be realized as a Katsura algebra (
[
11
]
), and hence admits a groupoid model. This perspective is central to Katsura’s result that actions of cyclic groups on
K
-theory can be lifted to genuine actions on UCT Kirchberg algebras
[
11
, Theorem 3.5]
.

Given the breadth of examples and applications of self-similar graph
C^{*}
-algebras, substantial effort has been devoted to understanding their structural properties (see, for example,
[
6
,
10
,
12
,
14
,
28
]
). In
[
12
]
, the authors (including the last author of the present paper) study the KMS-states of self-similar
k
-graph
C^{*}
-algebras. Under hypotheses ensuring well-behaved periodicity of the underlying self-similar
k
-graph (formulated via a notion called
local
faithfulness
), they identify a Cartan subalgebra arising from the interior of the isotropy subgroupoid of the associated self-similar path groupoid model. Recall that a
Cartan subalgebra
of a
C^{*}
-algebra is a maximal abelian subalgebra that is regular and admits a faithful conditional expectation onto it. The Cartan subalgebra identified in
[
12
]
plays a central role in describing equilibrium states: the KMS-simplex is affinely isomorphic to a distinguished compact convex subset of the tracial state space of the Cartan subalgebra.

More broadly, Cartan subalgebras have attracted considerable attention in recent years (see
[
7
,
8
,
22
]
and references therein) due to their deep connections with the classification program (
[
1
,
15
,
27
]
), rigidity phenomena (
[
17
]
), and the structure of intermediate subalgebras (
[
3
,
4
]
). In particular, the presence of a Cartan subalgebra realizes the ambient
C^{*}
-algebra as the reduced twisted groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra of an effective groupoid (
[
23
,
26
]
).

Cartan subalgebras are far from unique, even when one restricts to a fixed spectrum (
[
16
,
17
,
26
]
, etc.). On the other hand, the existence is not guaranteed in general, though it is often more accessible in settings where a non-effective groupoid model is available (
[
5
,
9
]
).
Motivated by these considerations, it is natural to investigate Cartan subalgebras of self-similar graph algebras
\mathcal{O}_{G,E}
. Our main goal is to identify, for a broad class of such
C^{*}
-algebras, a subgroupoid
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
such that
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{S})
is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathcal{O}_{G,E}
. Note that asking for
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{S})
to be a
C^{*}
-subalgebra
forces
\mathcal{S}
to be open, and commutativity of
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{S})

is equivalent to the open
\mathcal{S}
being an abelian subgroupoid of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
,
the interior of the isotropy in
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
.

One of the most natural subgroupoids to consider in any Hausdorff étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G})^{\circ}
itself.
It is shown in
[
5
]
that,
provided
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G})^{\circ}
is abelian and closed, the associated subalgebra is a Cartan subalgebra of
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})
. Closedness
of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
can be characterized in terms of a dichotomy of dynamical properties of infinite paths in
E
, namely Condition
(.1)
in Theorem
A
below; see Subsection
5.2
for definitions and proofs. Moreover, in
the special case that
(G,E)
is a genuine action and
G
is abelian, it turns out that

\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
is always abelian
(Corollary
2.14
). In this setting, we
thus obtain the following natural and tractable characterization of when
C^{*}_{r}(\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ})
is a Cartan subalgebra of
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})\cong C^{*}(E)\rtimes G
.

Theorem A

(Theorem
5.3
)
.

Let
E
be a source-free, finite directed graph and let
G
be an abelian group. If
(G,E)
is a genuine action, then the following are equivalent:

(
A
.1)

For any
x\in E^{\infty}
and
g\in G
,
x
is either
g
-generic or
g
-rare.

(
A
.2)

C^{*}_{r}(\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ})
is a Cartan subalgebra in

C^{*}(E)\rtimes G
.

To the best knowledge of the authors, this characterization of Cartan subalgebras in crossed products is new. Beyond the cases where
Condition
(.1)
is satisfied, it remains unclear how to identify Cartan subalgebras in
C^{*}(E)\rtimes G
.

For an arbitrary self-similar graph
(G,E)
, the open subgroupoid
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
is not necessarily abelian, especially when
(G,E)
is not assumed to be locally faithful.
This is in contrast to the setting of
[
12
]
. In order to identify a Cartan subalgebra of
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})
, we need to instead find maximal abelian open subgroupoids in
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
. To better understand the structure of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
, we classify vertices in terms of their dynamical properties (Corollary
5.9
) and systematically study
cycline triples
of
(G,E)
, that is, triples
(\alpha,g,\beta)
for which the associated open compact bisections
\mathcal{Z}(\alpha,g,\beta)
are contained in
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
. These bisections collectively form an open cover of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
, when
(G,E)
is pseudo-free (see the discussion following
Definition
2.12
).
We let
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
denote the union of
\mathcal{Z}(\alpha,g,\alpha)
ranging over all symmetric cycline triples
(\alpha,g,\alpha)
. This leads to the following concrete description of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
, which encapsulates the major original insights of the paper.

Theorem B

(Structure of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
; Proposition
3.3
, Corollary
5.10
)
.

Let
(G,E)
be a pseudo-free self-similar graph, where
E
is a source-free, finite directed graph and
G
is a discrete countable group with an element of infinite order. Then
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
is an open abelian normal subgroupoid of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
and

(1.1)

\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}=\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}\bigsqcup\left(\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\alpha,g,\beta)\text{ non-symmetric}\\
\text{cycline triple}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{Z}(\alpha,g,\beta)\right).

Moreover,
\mathcal{Z}(\alpha,g,\beta)
is a singleton for every non-symmetric cycline triple.

Theorem
B

provides a natural candidate
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
for obtaining a Cartan subalgebra. Note further that
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
is maximal abelian in
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
precisely when all singletons in
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
arising from non-symmetric cycline triples fail to commute with
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
.

We illustrate the efficacy of the concrete description of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
in the case
G=\mathbb{Z}
, where sufficient conditions for maximal abelianness of
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
in
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Z},E})^{\circ}
are obtained in Section
5
. Closedness of
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
in
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
is shown to be characterized again by condition
(.1)
. Combining these ingredients, an application of
[
9
, Corollary 4.5]
leads to the main result on Cartan subalgebras of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Z},E}
.

Theorem C

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

Let
(\mathbb{Z},E)
be a pseudo-free self-similar graph, where
E
is a source-free, finite directed graph. Assume additionally that the following conditions hold:

(
C
.1)

For any
x\in E^{\infty}
and
g\in\mathbb{Z}
,
x
is either
g
-generic or
g
-rare.

(
C
.2)

For any cycle
C
in
E
with no entrance, if
p
is the minimal positive integer such that
a^{p}
fixes a vertex on
C
, we have
a^{p}|_{C}\neq a^{\pm p}
.

Then
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}})
is a Cartan subalgebra of
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Z},E})
.

Condition
(.2)
may appear

ad hoc
; indeed, it arises from explicit computations in the special case of
(\mathbb{Z},E)
ensuring that
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
is maximal abelian (see Lemmas
5.15
and
5.16
). Although the assumptions in Theorem
C
seem restrictive, they cover a substantially larger class than those treated in the existing literature; see for example Example
5.21
.
In fact, Theorem
C
should be regarded as a concrete application of the more structural result, Theorem
B
. This same general strategy also allows for case-by-case analyses tailored to specific examples,
illustrated for instance in Appendix
B
when Condition
(.2)

in Theorem
C
is relaxed.

Another consequence of Theorem
B
is the existence of ‘easier models’ for
\mathcal{O}_{G,E}
: The ‘standard’ groupoid model
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
for
\mathcal{O}_{G,E}
that we mentioned earlier
is considerably more complicated than those associated naturally to, say,
the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(E)
of the directed graph
E
. In graph groupoids, elements encode shift equivalence between pairs of infinite paths together with an integer lag. By contrast, elements of
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}

keep track not only of the lag between infinite paths but also the self-similarity of the action of
G
. This amounts to the lag-group in
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
being vastly more complicated than the integers: it is a semi-direct product of
\mathbb{Z}
acting on a quotient
space of functions
\mathbb{N}\to G
(more details can be found in Section
2.2
). Hence, it is of interest to find other, hopefully more tractable, groupoid models for
\mathcal{O}_{G,E}
.

Our approach to constructing such ‘easier models’ is to leverage a result of
Renault’s (
[
25
, Theorem 5.5(ii)]
, quoted as Theorem
4.7
below), which is related to his Weyl groupoid construction (
[
26
]
) via
[
7
]
. The idea is that the reduced

C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})
of a groupoid can be realized as the twisted reduced
C^{*}
-algebra of a transformation groupoid
(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{S})\ltimes\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
, where
\mathcal{S}
is a normal abelian subgroupoid of
\mathcal{G}
that is sufficiently well-behaved topologically, and
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
denotes its dual groupoid, naturally acted upon by
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{S}
. In our setting,
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
provides a good candidate for
\mathcal{S}
, given the nice properties established in Theorem
B
. As illustrated by examples in Appendix
A
, the resulting new transformation groupoid
(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}})\ltimes\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}
is significantly more tractable than
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Z},E}
, capturing only the essential features of the underlying self-similar graph
(\mathbb{Z},E)
.

Structure of the paper

The preliminaries section,
Section
2
,
recalls the basic definitions of self-similar graph
C^{*}
-algebras and groupoid models. In Subsection
2.3
, we review cycline triples and refine the notion of (non)local faithfulness for our purposes. The section concludes with two corollaries identifying candidates for abelian subgroupoids in the genuine action case and the general setting (Corollaries
2.14
and
2.16
).

In Section
3
, we show that
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
is an open, abelian, normal subgroupoid of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
when
G
is abelian (Proposition
3.3
), establishing the first part of Theorem
B
. In the case
G=\mathbb{Z}
, the dual bundle
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}
is described explicitly in Section
4
(Theorem
4.4
).
The second part of Section
4
uses this description to give a more concrete account of the action of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Z},E}/\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
on
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}
.

Section
5
contains the main results of the paper. In Subsection
5.1
, we analyze cycline triples at four types of vertices with distinct dynamical behaviors (Corollary
5.9
). This leads to Corollary
5.10

and
establishes the second part of Theorem
B
. A detailed study of commutativity of elements in compact open bisections associated to cycline triples yields sufficient conditions for
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
to be maximal among open abelian subgroupoids of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
(Theorem
5.4
). Subsection
5.2
characterizes the closedness of subgroupoids
\mathcal{S}
of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
containing
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
(Theorem
5.20
).
Combined
with Corollary
2.14
, one direct consequence is the equivalent characterization of when
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
gives a Cartan subalgebra for genuine actions (Theorem
A
). Finally, in Subsection
5.3
, we prove Theorem
C
by assembling the necessary ingredients to apply
[
9
, Corollary 4.5]
, including Theorems
B
,

5.4
,

5.20

and Lemma
5.23
.

The paper concludes with two appendices. In Appendix
A
, we compute the transformation groupoids
(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Z},E}/\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}})\ltimes\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}
introduced in Section

(G,E)
. In Appendix

, we identify an alternative subgroupoid
\mathcal{S}_{\text{alt}}
of

Notation and
standing assumptions

We let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of all non-negative integers, and write
\mathbb{N}^{+}

G
is always a discrete countable group. Its identity is denoted by

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Self-similar graphs

A directed graph
E=(E^{0},E^{1},r,s)
consists of a set of vertices,
E^{0}
, a set of directed edges,
E^{1}
, and the maps
r,s\colon E^{1}\to E^{0}
, which specify the range and the source of each edge, respectively.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, we use
E^{n}
to denote directed paths in
E
with length
n
. We also use
|\mu|
to denote the length of a finite path
\mu
. We use
E^{*}\vcentcolon=\cup_{n\geq 0}E^{n}
to denote the set of all finite directed paths.
For
v\in E^{0}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
,
vE^{n}
(respectively
E^{n}v
) is the set of all paths of length
n
with range
v
(respectively source
v
). We also use
vE^{*}
and
E^{*}v
to denote all finite paths with range and source
v
respectively.

So, by definition,
E^{\infty}
is the set of all infinite paths of
E
.
If
x=e_{1}e_{2}\dots\in E^{\infty}
, for natural numbers
m<n
, take

x(m,n)\coloneqq e_{m+1}\dots e_{n}
, and define
x(n,n)\coloneqq r(e_{n+1})
.
For
p\in\mathbb{N}
, define the left shift map
\sigma^{p}:E^{\infty}\to E^{\infty}
by
\sigma^{p}(x)\coloneqq e_{p+1}e_{p+2}\dots\in E^{\infty}
.
For
\mu\in E^{*}
, we denote
\mathcal{Z}(\mu)\vcentcolon=\{\mu x:\>x\in E^{\infty},s(\mu)=r(x)\}
. We can give
E^{\infty}
the topology generated by
\mathcal{Z}(\mu)
, and since
\mathcal{Z}(\mu)
will be compact this will make
E^{\infty}
into a second-countable locally compact Hausdorff space.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a graph
E
, we call a bijective map
E^{*}\to E^{*}
an
automorphism
of the finite path space
E^{*}
if it maps
E^{n}
to
E^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
(i.e., the map preserves lengths of finite paths) and if it commutes with the source and range maps. We use
\operatorname{Aut}(E^{*})
to denote the set of all such automorphisms.

For a (discrete) group
G
, we say that
G
acts on
E
if there is a group homomorphism
\varphi\colon G\to\operatorname{Aut}(E^{*})
.
1
1

1

This is a slight abuse of terminology; see Remark
2.4
.
We will usually just write
g\cdot\mu
for the action of
g\in G
on
\mu\in E^{*}
, with no reference to the map
\varphi
. We call the action
genuine
if it is entirely determined by the action of
G
on
E
in the sense that the equality

g\cdot(e_{0}\dots e_{n})=(g\cdot e_{0})\dots(g\cdot e_{n})

holds for all
g\in G
and all finite paths
e_{0}\dots e_{n}\in E^{*}
.

We first give the definition of self-similar graphs in terms of group actions on finite paths, where self-similarity
explicitly appears in Condition
(5)
of Definition
2.2
. An alternative way of defining self-similar actions, more in the spirit of
[
10
]
, is to extend the action self-similarly from that on edges (see Remark
2.4
for a detailed exposition).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a (discrete) group. We call action of
G
on
E

self-similar
if there is a restriction map
G\times E^{*}\to G,\ (g,\mu)\mapsto g|_{\mu}
, such that the following conditions hold.

(1)

(gh)|_{\mu}=g|_{h\cdot\mu}h|_{\mu}
for all
g,h\in G,\mu\in E^{*}
;

(2)

g|_{v}=g
for all
g\in G,v\in E^{0}
;

(3)

1_{G}|_{\mu}=1_{G}
for all
\mu\in E^{*}
;

(4)

g|_{\mu\nu}=g|_{\mu}|_{\nu}
for all
g\in G,\mu,\nu\in E^{*}
with
s(\mu)=r(\nu)
;

(5)

g\cdot(\mu\nu)=(g\cdot\mu)(g|_{\mu}\cdot\nu)
for all
g\in G,\mu,\nu\in E^{*}
with
s(\mu)=r(\nu)
.

In this case, we call
E
a
self-similar graph over
G
, or
we say that
(G,E)
is a
self-similar graph
.

Example 2.3
.

A genuine action of
G
on
E
in the sense of Definition
2.1
is self-similar with the restriction map

g|_{\mu}:=g
for all
g\in G
and all
\mu\in E^{*}
.

Remark 2.4
.

The data of a self-similar action in our sense is already contained in the data on the level of the graph
E
instead of its finite path space
E^{*}
. To be more precise, suppose we only know how
G
acts on the set of edges and on the set of vertices (still preserving ranges and sources), and we only know how to restrict elements of
G
by vertices and edges. Suppose further that this restriction map satisfies the following axioms:

(1’)

(gh)|_{e}=g|_{h\cdot e}h|_{e}
for all
g,h\in G
and
e\in E^{1}
;

(2’)

g|_{v}=g
for all
g\in G
and
v\in E^{0}
;

(3’)

1_{G}|_{e}=1_{G}
for all
e\in E^{1}
;

(4’)

g|_{e}\cdot s(e)=g\cdot s(e)
for all
g\in G
and
e\in E^{1}
.

Then there is a
natural
way of extending the action and restriction maps to action and restriction maps on the level of
E^{*}
which fall into the scope of Definition
2.2
, namely by inductively defining

g\cdot(e_{0}e_{1}\dots e_{n})\vcentcolon=(g\cdot e_{0})\,g|_{e_{0}}\cdot(e_{1}\dots e_{n})\quad\text{ and }\quad g|_{e_{0}e_{1}\dots e_{n}}\vcentcolon=(g|_{e_{0}})|_{e_{1}}\dots|_{e_{n}}.

And again, only if the restriction map satisfies
g|_{\mu}=g
for all

\mu\in E^{1}
and all
g\in G
does the induced action
G\to\operatorname{Aut}(E^{*})
become a genuine action in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

A direct consequence of Definition
2.1
is that loops are mapped to loops by group actions, as they respect
r,s
. This and some additional easy facts are recorded in Lemma
2.5
below; they will be useful in Section
5.1
and Appendix
B
for instance.

Recall that a path
C=\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{k}\in E^{k}
is called a
cycle
if
r(C)=s(C)
, and that a cycle
C
is said to
have no entrance
if
e\in E^{1}
with
r(e)=r(\mu_{i})
for some
1\leq i\leq k
, then
e=\mu_{i}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
G
acts on

E
,
and let
C
be a cycle in
E
.
Then for any fixed
h\in G
, the following hold.

(1)

h\cdot C
is a cycle of the same length as
C
.

(2)

h\cdot C
has no entrance if and only if
C
has no entrance.

(3)

Suppose the action of
G
is self-similar. If
C
has no entrance, then
h\cdot C^{\infty}=h|_{C^{k}}\cdot C^{\infty}=(h\cdot C)^{\infty}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Fix
h\in G
. Let
C
be a cycle of length
n
and define
C^{\prime}:=h\cdot C
.

(1)

Since

r(h\cdot C)=h\cdot r(C)=h\cdot s(C)=s(h\cdot C)
,
we see that

C^{\prime}
is a cycle of length at most
n
. If
|C^{\prime}|=m<n
, then
C(0,0)=(h^{-1}\cdot C^{\prime})(0,0)=h^{-1}\cdot C^{\prime}(0,0)=h^{-1}\cdot C^{\prime}(m,m)=(h^{-1}\cdot C^{\prime})(m,m)=C(m,m)
, which is a contradiction. Thus
|C^{\prime}|=n
.

(2)

Now assume
C
has no entrance, and assume for a contradiction that
C^{\prime}
does have an entrance, say

e^{\prime}
. Then
r(e^{\prime})\in C^{\prime}
, so
r(h^{-1}\cdot e^{\prime})=h^{-1}\cdot r(e^{\prime})\in C
. This implies that
h^{-1}\cdot e^{\prime}
is an edge in
C
, since
C
has no entrance. But then
h\cdot(h^{-1}\cdot e^{\prime})=e^{\prime}
must be an edge in
C^{\prime}
since
h\cdot C=C^{\prime}
, a contradiction. Thus
C^{\prime}
has no entrance.
By symmetry, we deduce that
C^{\prime}
has no entrance if and only if
C
has no entrance.

(3)

If
C^{\prime}
has no entrance, then
|v^{\prime}E^{\infty}|=1
for all
v^{\prime}\in C^{\prime}
.
Since
h\cdot C=C^{\prime}
, then by self-similarity,
h\cdot C^{\infty}=(h\cdot C)(h|_{C}\cdot C^{\infty})=C^{\prime}(h|_{C}\cdot C^{\infty})
. Since
s(C^{\prime})
has only one incoming path, namely
(C^{\prime})^{\infty}
, then we must have
h|_{C}\cdot C^{\infty}=(C^{\prime})^{\infty}
. Therefore
h\cdot C^{\infty}=(C^{\prime})^{\infty}
. Finally, since
h\cdot C^{\infty}=(C^{\prime})^{\infty}
, then
(h\cdot C^{k})(h|_{C^{k}}\cdot C^{\infty})=(C^{\prime})^{k}(C^{\prime})^{\infty}
. Since paths
h\cdot C^{k}
and
(C^{\prime})^{k}
have the same length, then they are equal. So we must have
h|_{C^{k}}\cdot C^{\infty}=(C^{\prime})^{\infty}
.
∎

Note from
[
14
, Section 3]

that a self-similar action
(G,E)
defined above induces a canonical action of
G
on the infinite path space
E^{\infty}
: for any
g\in G
and infinite path
x
, we take

(2.1)

\displaystyle(g\cdot x)(p,q)

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=g|_{x(0,p)}\cdot x(p,q)\quad\text{for all }0\leq p<q\in\mathbb{N},

so that
g\cdot x\in E^{\infty}
. Moreover, for any
g\in G
and
x\in E^{\infty}
, a map
g|_{x}\colon\mathbb{N}\to G
is induced by the restriction in the following way:

(2.2)

\displaystyle g|_{x}(p)

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=g|_{x(0,p)}\quad\text{for all }p\in\mathbb{N}.

The map records how the group element
g
changes when traversing through
x
according to the restriction map. Note that our definition of
g|_{x}
leads to the formula

(gh)|_{x}=g|_{h\cdot x}h|_{x}\quad\text{ for any }g,h\in G,\text{ and }x\in E^{\infty},

by standard computations. This formula for infinite paths is consistent with Definition
2.2

(1)
for finite paths.
We include the following lemma, which will be used repeatedly in Sections
4
and
5
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
h\in G
and
x\in E^{\infty}
are such that
h\cdot x=x
. Then for
n\leq p\in\mathbb{N}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(h|_{x(0,p)})^{\ell}=(h|_{x(0,n)})^{\ell}|_{x(n,p)}
. In particular,

[h|_{x}]^{\ell}=[h^{\ell}|_{x}].

Proof.

Follows from an easy induction on
|\ell|
, with the second claim following from the case
n=0
.
∎

We now recall the notion of self-similar graph
C^{*}
-algebras introduced by Exel and Pardo in
[
10
, Definition 3.2]
(see also
[
10
, Definition 3.1]

or
[
24
]

for the definition of a Cuntz-Krieger family
\{s_{\mu}\}_{\mu\in E^{*}}
for a graph
E
).

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(G,E)
be a self-similar graph. The
self-similar graph
C^{*}
-algebra

\mathcal{O}_{G,E}

is defined to be the universal unital
C^{*}
-algebra generated by a family of unitaries
\{u_{g}\}_{g\in G}
and
a Cuntz-Krieger family
\{s_{\mu}\}_{\mu\in E^{*}}
satisfying

(i)

u_{gh}=u_{g}u_{h}
for all
g
and
h\in G
;

(ii)

u_{g}s_{\mu}=s_{g\cdot\mu}u_{g|_{\mu}}
for all
g\in G
and
\mu\in E^{*}
.

Remark 2.8
.

When the self-similar action is genuine in the sense of Definition
2.1
, Condition
(ii)
of Definition
2.7
becomes
u_{g}s_{\mu}u_{g}^{*}=s_{g\cdot\mu}
for all
g\in G
and
\mu\in E^{*}
, which is the defining relation for the crossed product
C^{*}(E)\rtimes G
. In other words,
\mathcal{O}_{G,E}\cong C^{*}(E)\rtimes G
in the special case of genuine actions.

2.2.
The groupoid of a self-similar graph

A
groupoid
is a small category in which every element has an inverse. Given a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, we let
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
denote the unit space of
\mathcal{G}
,
s\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
the source map
s(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}^{-1}\mathfrak{g}
,
r\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
the range map
r(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{g}^{-1}
, and
\mathcal{G}^{(2)}\subseteq\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}
the set of composable pairs.
For a unit
x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, we will write
\mathcal{G}_{x}\vcentcolon=s^{-1}(x)
,
\mathcal{G}^{x}\vcentcolon=r^{-1}(x)
, and
\mathcal{G}(x)\vcentcolon=\mathcal{G}_{x}\cap\mathcal{G}^{x}

will denote the isotropy group at
x
.
The isotropy subgroupoid of a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
will be denoted
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G})\vcentcolon=\{\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{G}:s(\mathfrak{g})=r(\mathfrak{g})\}=\cup_{x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\mathcal{G}(x)
.

A
topological groupoid
is a groupoid that is also a topological space, in which inversion, the range map, and the source map are continuous, and multiplication is continuous with respect to the relative topology on
\mathcal{G}^{(2)}\subseteq\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}
. A
bisection

U\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is a subset where
r|_{U}
and
s|_{U}
are injective.
A locally compact Hausdorff groupoid
is called
ample
when there is a basis consisting of compact open bisections.

Given a self-similar graph
(G,E)
,
we follow the notation for
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
as
in
[
14
, Definition 5.2]

for the case
k=1

(also refer to

[
10
, Theorem 8.19]
, where
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
was originally defined).
To encode the self-similarity,
we replace the ‘usual’ lag group that is the integers in a graph-groupoid (and
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
in a
k
-graph groupoid) with a group that includes
information about how the restriction map interacts with infinite paths
via a semi-direct product by a right shift map. To this end, we consider the group
C(\mathbb{N},G)

of functions
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to G
under pointwise multiplication. Define the tail equivalence relation by
f\sim g
exactly when there exists some
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f(k)=g(k)
for all
k\geq N
. We then get a well-defined quotient group
Q(\mathbb{N},G)=C(\mathbb{N},G)/\sim
, and we write
[f]
for the equivalence class of
f
in
Q(\mathbb{N},G)
. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
f\in C(\mathbb{N},G)
, and
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we can define the right translation

\mathcal{T}_{n}(f)(p)\vcentcolon=\begin{cases}f(p-n)&\text{if }p-n\geq 0,\\
1_{G}&\text{if }p-n<0.\end{cases}

The induced map
\mathcal{T}_{n}\colon Q(\mathbb{N},G)\to Q(\mathbb{N},G)
is a group automorphism and the map
\mathcal{T}\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\operatorname{Aut}(Q(\mathbb{N},G))
given by
n\mapsto\mathcal{T}_{n}
is a homomorphism. We can then form the semidirect product
Q(\mathbb{N},G)\rtimes_{\mathcal{T}}\mathbb{Z}
.

Now we define our groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{G,E}\vcentcolon=\left\{(x;[f],p-q;y):\begin{matrix}\sigma^{p}(x)=f(p)\cdot\sigma^{q}(y)\\
f(p+n)=f(p)|_{\sigma^{q}(y)}(n)\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\end{matrix}\right\},

or equivalently, as
explained in
[
14
, Remark 5.3 (ii)]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{G,E}=\left\{(\mu(g\cdot x);\mathcal{T}_{|\mu|}([g|_{x}]),|\mu|-|\nu|;\nu x):\begin{matrix}g\in G,\mu,\nu\in E^{*}\\
s(\mu)=g\cdot s(\nu),x\in s(\nu)E^{\infty}\end{matrix}\right\}.

The operations of the groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
are given as follows:

(2.3)

\displaystyle s(x;[f],p-q;y)

\displaystyle=(y;[1_{G}],0;y),

(2.4)

\displaystyle r(x;[f],p-q;y)

\displaystyle=(x;[1_{G}],0;x),

(2.5)

\displaystyle(x;[f],p-q;y)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(y;\mathcal{T}_{q-p}([f]^{-1}),q-p;x),

(2.6)

\displaystyle(x;[f],p-q;y)(y;[g],n-m;z)

\displaystyle=(x;[f]\mathcal{T}_{p-q}([g]),p-q+n-m;z).

Given
g\in G
,
\mu,\nu\in E^{*}
with
s(\mu)=g\cdot s(\nu)
, we define the

cylinder sets
by

\mathcal{Z}(\mu,g,\nu)\vcentcolon=\big\{(\mu(g\cdot x);\mathcal{T}_{|\mu|}([g|_{x}]),|\mu|-|\nu|;\nu x):\>x\in s(\nu)E^{\infty}\big\}.

We equip
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
with the topology generated by the cylinder sets which makes it a second-countable topological groupoid.
Under the following assumption of
pseudo-freeness
, the cylinder sets
\mathcal{Z}(\mu,g,\nu)
form a basis for the topology
and
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
is ample and Hausdorff.

Definition 2.9
.

A self-similar graph
(G,E)
is

pseudo-free
when the following holds:
if
g\in G
and
\mu\in E^{*}
satisfy
g\cdot\mu=\mu
and
g|_{\mu}=1_{G}
, then
g=1_{G}
.

We again illustrate in the case where
G
acts on
E
by a genuine action.

Remark 2.10
.

For a genuine action,
(G,E)
is necessarily pseudo-free, since
g|_{\mu}=1_{G}
implies
g=g|_{\mu}=1_{G}
for any
g\in G
and
\mu\in E^{*}
.

In this setting,
the self-similar groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
can be identified with an action groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{E}\rtimes G
associated to a canonical action of
G
on the path groupoid

(2.7)

\mathcal{G}_{E}=\{(\mu x,|\mu|-|\nu|,\nu x):s(\mu)=s(\nu),x\in s(\nu)E^{\infty}\},

which provides an alternative proof to Remark
2.8
. Indeed,
G
acts on
\mathcal{G}_{E}

by

\displaystyle g\cdot(\mu x,|\mu|-|\nu|,\nu x):=

\displaystyle\big((g\cdot\mu)(g\cdot x),|g\cdot\mu|-|g\cdot\nu|,(g\cdot\nu)(g\cdot x)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(g\cdot(\mu x),|\mu|-|\nu|,g\cdot(\nu x)\big),

for any
g\in G
,
\mu,\nu\in E^{*}
with
s(\mu)=s(\nu)
and
x\in s(\nu)E^{\infty}
.
Recall that,
as a topological space, the action groupoid is the Cartesian product
\mathcal{G}_{E}\times G
. We let the groupoid’s unit space be

(\mathcal{G}_{E}\rtimes G)^{(0)}=\mathcal{G}_{E}^{(0)}\times\{1_{G}\}\cong E^{\infty}
, and define
source and range maps

(2.8)

r(\gamma,g)=r(\gamma)\quad\text{and}\quad s(\gamma,g)=s(g^{-1}\cdot\gamma)

for
g\in G,\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{E}
,
and multiplication

(2.9)

(\gamma,g)(\zeta,h)=(\gamma(g\cdot\zeta),gh)\quad\text{whenever }s(\gamma,g)=r(\zeta,h).

An isomorphism
\mathcal{G}_{E}\rtimes G\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
is given by

(2.10)

\big((\mu x,|\mu|-|\nu|,\nu x),g\big)\mapsto\big(\mu x;[g^{-1}],|\mu|-|\nu|;(g^{-1}\cdot\nu)(g^{-1}\cdot x)\big),

where,
by a slight abuse of notation,

[g^{-1}]
denotes the equivalence class of the constant function with value
g^{-1}
.

Pseudo-freeness plays a crucial role in the study of self-similar graph
C^{*}
-algebras.
Under the assumption of pseudo-freeness, the cylinder sets
\mathcal{Z}(\mu,g,\nu)
form a basis for a topology, under which
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
is a second-countable Hausdorff ample groupoid.
This is first established through a topological groupoid isomorphism
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}\cong\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tight}}(\mathcal{S}_{G,E})
, where
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tight}}(\mathcal{S}_{G,E})
is the tight groupoid associated to the inverse semigroup coming from
(G,E)

(
[
10
, Theorem 8.19, Proposition 9.4]
); see also the more direct argument in
[
14
, Theorem 5.8]
. Moreover, in
[
10
]
the
C^{*}
-algebra isomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{G,E}\cong C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})
is obtained by first identifying
\mathcal{O}_{G,E}
with
C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tight}}(\mathcal{S}_{G,E}))
, and then assuming pseudo-freeness and using the groupoid isomorphism
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}\cong\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tight}}(\mathcal{S}_{G,E})
.

While non-pseudo-free self-similar actions have also been studied (see, for instance,
[
6
]
), this approach involves
non-Hausdorff groupoids and inverse semigroup techniques. In the present work, we restrict our attention to the pseudo-free setting, thereby working with Hausdorff groupoids admitting a basis of cylinder sets. To clarify the role of pseudo-freeness in relation to the existing literature, we indicate explicitly each instance in which this assumption is used and how it enters our arguments.

Given a self-similar pseudo-free action
(G,E)
,
if
G
is amenable, then
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
is amenable.
In particular, its full
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})
and reduced
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})
coincide, and thus isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{G,E}
.

We end this subsection with the following standard lemmas for later use, which follows essentially from the argument of
[
14
, Theorem 5.8]
. We include a quick proof for completeness.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
(G,E)
be a pseudo-free self-similar graph, and let

\mathfrak{g}=\bigl(\mu(g\cdot x);\mathcal{T}_{|\mu|}([g|_{x}]),|\mu|-|\nu|;\nu x\bigr)\in\mathcal{G}_{G,E}.

Then every
neighborhood of
\mathfrak{g}
contains a neighborhood of the form

\mathcal{Z}\bigl(\mu(g\cdot x)(0,n),g|_{x(0,n)},\nu x(0,n)\bigr)

for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Since
(G,E)
is pseudo-free, it follows that the cylinder sets form a basis of the topology. Thus, any neighborhood of
\mathfrak{g}
contains a set of the form
\mathcal{Z}(\alpha,h,\beta)
that, in turn, contains
\mathfrak{g}
. Note that for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(2.11)

\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{Z}\bigl(\alpha(h\cdot y(0,m)),h|_{y(0,m)},\beta y(0,m)\bigr)\subseteq\mathcal{Z}(\alpha,h,\beta).

Thus, by replacing
(\alpha,h,\beta)
with the triple
(\alpha(h\cdot y(0,m)),h|_{y(0,m)},\beta y(0,m))
, we can without loss of generality assume that
n\coloneqq|\alpha|-|\mu|>0
.

It follows from
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{Z}(\alpha,h,\beta)
that

(2.12)

\mathfrak{g}=\bigl(\alpha(h\cdot y);\mathcal{T}_{|\alpha|}([h|_{y}]),|\alpha|-|\beta|;\beta y\bigr)\quad\text{ for some }y\in s(\beta)E^{\infty}.

Comparing the coordinates of the two different representations of
\mathfrak{g}
, we conclude that
\mu(g\cdot x)=\alpha(h\cdot y)
,
|\mu|-|\nu|=|\alpha|-|\beta|
, and
\nu x=\beta y
. As
n>0
, it follows from the first equality that
\mu(g\cdot x(0,n))=\alpha
and
g|_{x(0,n)}\cdot\sigma^{n}(x)=h\cdot y
. The second equality implies
|\beta|-|\nu|=n
which, combined with the third equality, yields
\nu x(0,n)=\beta
and
\sigma^{n}(x)=y
.
In particular,
h\cdot y=g|_{x(0,n)}\cdot y
.

Lastly, comparing the two different forms of
\mathfrak{g}
implies that
\mathcal{T}_{|\mu|}([g|_{x}])=\mathcal{T}_{|\alpha|}([h|_{y}])
. The left-hand side can be reformulated as follows by applying
[
14
, Lemma 5.1]
:

(2.13)

\mathcal{T}_{|\mu|}([g|_{x}])=\mathcal{T}_{|\mu|}([g|_{x(0,n)\sigma^{n}(x)}])=\mathcal{T}_{|\mu|+n}([g|_{x(0,n)}|_{\sigma^{n}(x)}])=\mathcal{T}_{|\alpha|}([g|_{x(0,n)}|_{y}]).

Since this equals
\mathcal{T}_{|\alpha|}([h|_{y}])
, we conclude
[g|_{x(0,n)}|_{y}]=[h|_{y}]
. By pseudo-freeness, we can invoke
[
14
, Corollary 5.6]
, which implies that
g|_{x(0,n)}=h
. All in all, we have shown that

(\alpha,h,\beta)=\bigl(\mu(g\cdot x(0,n)),g|_{x(0,n)},\nu x(0,n)\bigr),

which finishes the claim.
∎

2.3.
Cycline triples and local faithfulness

We first recall the notion of cycline triples, which is introduced in
[
12
, Section 3]
, to study
periodicity
of
(G,E)
.

Definition 2.12
.

Given a self-similar graph
(G,E)
, finite paths
\mu,\nu\in E^{*}
and
g\in G
, we say that
(\mu,g,\nu)
is a
cycline triple at

s(\nu)
if

s(\mu)=g\cdot s(\nu)
and we have

\mu(g\cdot x)=\nu x
for all
x\in s(\nu)E^{\infty}
.
A cycline triple
is called
symmetric
if it is of the form
(\mu,g,\mu)

and
trivial
if it is of the form
(\mu,1_{G},\mu)
.

Note that a cylinder set
\mathcal{Z}(\mu,g,\nu)
is contained in
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})
if and only if
(\mu,g,\nu)
is a cycline triple. When
(G,E)
is pseudo-free, cylinder sets form a basis of the topology of
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
, which implies that

(2.14)

\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\mu,g,\nu)\\
\text{cycline}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{Z}(\mu,g,\nu).

This fact can also be found in
[
12
, Lemma 5.2]
.

As noted in the Introduction, our main goal is to identify an open abelian subgroupoid of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
that yields a Cartan subalgebra of
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})
. This requires understanding commutativity within
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
. The relevant criterion is given in the following lemma, whose proof can be extracted from the first part of
[
12
, Lemma 5.3]
in the
1
-graph case.

Lemma 2.13
.

If
(\mu,g,\nu)
and
(\alpha,h,\beta)
are cycline triples, then all elements belonging to the cylinder sets

\mathcal{Z}(\mu,g,\nu)
and
\mathcal{Z}(\alpha,h,\beta)
commute if and only if for every
x\in s(\nu)E^{\infty}
and
y\in s(\beta)E^{\infty}
, we have

(2.15)

\big[g|_{x(0,|\alpha|)}|_{\sigma^{|\alpha|}(x)}\,h|_{y(0,|\nu|)}|_{\sigma^{|\nu|}(y)}\big]=\big[h|_{y(0,|\mu|)}|_{\sigma^{|\mu|}(y)}g|_{x(0,|\beta|)}|_{\sigma^{|\beta|}(x)}\big].

An easy observation is that (
2.15
) always holds in the genuine action case.

Corollary 2.14
.

Let
G
be an abelian group and let
(G,E)
be a genuine action in the sense of Definition
2.1
. Then the subgroupoid
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
of
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
is abelian.

In general, identifying maximal abelian subgroupoids of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
is more intricate. Under the additional assumption that
(G,E)
is
locally faithful
, it is shown in
[
12
, Lemma 5.3]
that the equality (
2.15
) is always satisfied, thus implying that
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
is abelian. In this setting,
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
gives rise to a Cartan subalgebra when

\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
is closed (for instance when
(G,E)
is strongly connected by
[
12
, Proposition 5.5]
), as an application of
[
5
, Corollary 4.5]
.

Now we turn to the notion of local faithfulness, introduced in
[
12
]
. Recall that a self-similar graph
(G,E)
is
locally faithful
if for every vertex
v
in
E
, whenever
g\in G
is such that
g\cdot\mu=\mu
for all
\mu\in vE^{*}
, then
g=1_{G}
. As we will see later,
in the
1
-graph case,
local faithfulness forces all cycline triples at every vertex to be trivial (Corollary
5.9
),

directly from Lemma

Definition 2.15

Let
(G,E)
be a self-similar graph. A vertex

is

for all

,
then
g=1_{G}
.
Otherwise, the vertex

is called

Note that

is locally faithful if and only if every

is locally faithful. When both locally faithful and non-locally faithful vertices are present,

is not necessarily abelian, and it is not clear how to identify maximal abelian subgroupoids of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
. This motivates us to consider a special abelian subset
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}\subseteq\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
, consisting of cylinder sets corresponding to all symmetric cycline triples. Its abelianness follows from the following direct corollary of Lemma

Corollary 2.16

Let
(G,E)
be a self-similar graph with

abelian.
Given two symmetric cycline triples
(\mu,g,\mu)
and
(\alpha,h,\alpha)
,
all groupoid elements in
\mathcal{Z}(\mu,g,\mu)
and
\mathcal{Z}(\alpha,h,\alpha)
commute.

In Section
3
, we show that
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
is a normal subgroupoid and thus serves as a candidates for yielding a Cartan subalgebra.

While the proofs in the rest of this paper do not make explicit use of the following lemma, we record it here since it came in handy repeatedly when developing intuition about self-similar graphs and constructing (counter)examples.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
(G,E)
be a pseudo-free self-similar graph, and assume that
\mu\in E^{*}
. If
r(\mu)
is not locally faithful, then neither is

.

Proof.

By assumption, there exists
g\in G\setminus\{1_{G}\}
such that
g\cdot\alpha=\alpha
for all
\alpha\in r(\mu)E^{*}
. Since
(G,E)
is pseudo-free and

, this implies that

(2.16)

\mu\beta=g\cdot(\mu\beta)\quad\text{and}\quad g|_{\mu\beta}\neq 1_{G}\quad\text{ for any }\beta\in s(\mu)E^{*}.

As

\mu\beta\overset{\eqref{eq:fix but nontrivial}}{=}(g\cdot\mu)(g|_{\mu}\cdot\beta)=\mu(g|_{\mu}\cdot\beta),\text{ so }\beta=g|_{\mu}\cdot\beta,\quad\text{and}\quad g|_{\mu}\neq 1_{G}.

This shows that

, so that

is not locally faithful.
∎

h_{n}^{p}|_{x(n,\ell)}=(h_{n}|_{x(n,\ell)})^{p}=h_{\ell}^{pk_{n,\ell}}\quad\text{ and }\quad h_{m}^{q}|_{x(m,\ell)}=h_{\ell}^{qk_{m,\ell}},

so that

\bigl(h_{n}^{p}|_{x(n,\ell)}\,h_{m}^{q}|_{x(m,\ell)}\bigr)|_{\sigma^{\ell}(x)}=h_{\ell}^{pk_{n,\ell}+qk_{m,\ell}}|_{\sigma^{\ell}(x)}.

Another application of Lemma
2.6
then proves

(4.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{n}([h_{n}|_{\sigma^{n}(x)}])^{p}\mathcal{T}_{m}([h_{m}|_{\sigma^{m}(x)}])^{q}=\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\left(\left[h_{\ell}|_{\sigma^{\ell}(x)}\right]\right)^{pk_{n,\ell}+qk_{m,\ell}},

which was the claim.
∎

Out of the numbers
k_{n}
, we will now construct an inverse system
(X_{i},f_{i,j})
of topological groups which, as long as the self-similar graph is pseudo-free, is isomorphic to the fibre
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}(x)
.

If
h_{i}=1_{G}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
X_{i}(x)\vcentcolon=X_{i}\vcentcolon=\{1\}
; otherwise, there exists a minimal
N\in\mathbb{N}
with
h_{i}\neq 1_{G}
. We let

X_{i}(x)\vcentcolon=X_{i}\vcentcolon=\begin{cases}\{1\}&\text{ if }i<N\\
\mathbb{T}&\text{ if }i\geq N\end{cases},

and for
i\leq j
,

f_{i,j}\colon X_{j}\to X_{i},\quad z\mapsto z^{k_{i,j}}.

Note that this is well-defined, for if
i<N\leq j
, then
k_{i,j}=0
by (
4.6
), so that
z^{k_{i,j}}=1\in X_{i}
.
As
((X_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}},(f_{i,j})_{i\leq j\in\mathbb{N}})
is an inverse system of topological groups, we may let

(4.15)

X(x)\vcentcolon=X\vcentcolon=\varprojlim_{i}\,(X_{i},f_{i,j})

denote its inverse limit, and let
\pi_{i}\colon X\to X_{i}
be the natural projections satisfying
\pi_{i}=f_{i,j}\circ\pi_{j}
.
Recall that
X(x)
is equipped with the coarsest topology that makes all maps

continuous.

We can finally describe the fibre
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}(x)
of the dual group bundle
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}
:

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(\mathbb{Z},E)
be a self-similar graph, and fix any infinite path
x\in E^{\infty}
.
There exists an injective continuous homomorphism

\Omega\colon\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}(x)\to X(x)=\varprojlim_{i}\,(X_{i}(x),f_{i,j})\text{ uniquely determined by }(\pi_{n}\circ\Omega)(\kappa)=\kappa(\mathfrak{s}_{n})

for
\kappa\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}(x)
, where
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
is at (
4.7
).

If
(\mathbb{Z},E)
is pseudo-free, then
\Omega

is an isomorphism of topological groups whose inverse maps an infinite sequence
\vec{z}\in X
to the continuous homomorphism

\Omega^{-1}(\vec{z})\colon\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}(x)\to\mathbb{T}

that is uniquely determined by

(4.16)

\Omega^{-1}(\vec{z})\colon\quad\mathfrak{s}_{n}\mapsto\pi_{n}(\vec{z})\quad\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Note that we are using that the elements
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
generate
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}(x)
(Proposition

), so that
\Omega
is indeed uniquely determined by the given formula. To prove Theorem
4.4
, let us first check that the map

exists.

Lemma 4.5

(Existence of
\Omega
)
.

The continuous maps

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}^{*}_{i}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}(x)

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{T},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}^{*}_{i}(\kappa)

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\kappa(\mathfrak{s}_{i}),

satisfy
\mathfrak{s}^{*}_{i}=f_{i,j}\circ\mathfrak{s}^{*}_{j}
for all natural numbers
i\leq j
. Moreover, there exists a unique continuous group homomorphism
\Omega\colon\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}(x)\to X(x)

such that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}(x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X(x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{j}(x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{i}(x)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{s}^{*}_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{s}^{*}_{j}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{j}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{i,j}}

commutes for all
i\leq j
, where as before
X(x)
is the inverse limit as defined at (
4.15
).

Proof.

First, we point out that
\mathfrak{s}^{*}_{i}
is indeed continuous by definition of the topology on
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}(x)
.
Next note that the diagram makes sense: If
j\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
X_{j}\vcentcolon=X_{j}(x)
is
\{1\}
and not
\mathbb{T}
, then we must have that
h_{j}=1_{G}
. This implies
\mathfrak{s}_{j}=1_{\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}(x)}
and thus
\mathfrak{s}^{*}_{j}(\kappa)=1\in X_{j}
.

We have

\displaystyle f_{i,i+1}\circ\mathfrak{s}^{*}_{i+1}(\kappa)

\displaystyle=f_{i,i+1}\circ\kappa(\mathfrak{s}_{i+1})=\kappa(\mathfrak{s}_{i+1})^{k_{i}}

\displaystyle\overset{(\dagger)}{=}\kappa\left(\mathfrak{s}_{i+1}^{k_{i}}\right)\overset{(\ddagger)}{=}\kappa(\mathfrak{s}_{i})=\mathfrak{s}^{*}_{i}(\kappa)

where
(\dagger)
follows since
\kappa
is a homomorphism and
(\ddagger)
from
Lemma
4.3
.
The general claim for arbitrary
i\leq j
now follows from the fact that
f_{i,j}=f_{i,k}\circ f_{k,j}
for all
i\leq k\leq j
. The universal property of
\varprojlim_{i\in\mathbb{N}}(X_{i},f_{i})

then implies the existence and uniqueness of the continuous map
\Omega
.
∎

We need one more intermediate lemma, which gives the reason why we assumed pseudo-freeness in the second half of Theorem
4.4
.

Lemma 4.6

(Cascading down)
.

Suppose that
(\mathbb{Z},E)
is pseudo-free, and let
m\leq n\in\mathbb{N}
and
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(i)

If
h_{m+1}=1_{G}
, then
h_{i}=1_{G}
for all
0\leq i\leq m
also.

(ii)

If
\mathfrak{s}_{n}^{q}=\mathfrak{s}_{m}^{p}
in
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}(x)
,
then either
h_{n}=1_{G}
or
q=pk_{m,n}
.

Proof.

(i)
Lemma
4.1
implies that
h_{m}|_{x(m,m+1)}
is a power of
h_{m+1}
and hence equal to
h_{m+1}=1_{G}
.
Since
h_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{x(m,m)}
, we have

h_{m}\cdot x(m,m+1)=x(m,m+1)
, and so by pseudo-freeness, we must have
h_{m}=1_{G}
. Iteratively, we see that
h_{i}=1_{G}
for all
0\leq i\leq m
.

(ii)

Using Lemma
4.3
repeatedly, we get

(4.17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{n}([h_{n}|_{\sigma^{n}(x)}])^{q}=\mathcal{T}_{m}([h_{m}|_{\sigma^{m}(x)}])^{p}\overset{\ref{it:powers go in:relationship of generators of ssym}}{=}\left(\mathcal{T}_{n}[h_{n}|_{\sigma^{n}(x)}]^{k_{m,n}}\right)^{p},

or in other words,

[\mathrm{const}_{1_{G}}]=\mathcal{T}_{n}([h_{n}|_{\sigma^{n}(x)}])^{pk_{m,n}-q}.

Let
s\vcentcolon=pk_{m,n}-q
. The above means that, for large enough
r\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq n}
, we have

1_{G}=(h_{n}|_{\sigma^{n}(x)})^{s}(r-n)\overset{\ref{it:powers go in}}{=}h_{n}^{s}|_{\sigma^{n}(x)}(r-n)=h_{n}^{s}|_{x(n,r)}.

Now,
h_{n}^{s}\in\langle h_{n}\rangle=\mathbb{Z}_{x(n,n)}
, so
h_{n}^{s}\cdot\sigma^{n}(x)=\sigma^{n}(x)
. From this, it follows
that
h_{n}^{s}\cdot x(n,r)=x(n,r)
. Combined with
1_{G}=h_{n}^{s}|_{x(n,r)}
and pseudo-freeness, we deduce that
h_{n}^{s}=1_{G}
. Since
G=\mathbb{Z}
is an integral domain, we must either have
h_{n}=1_{G}
or
pk_{m,n}-q=s=0
, which proves the claim.
∎

We are ready to prove Theorem
4.4
.

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

The continuous homomorphism
\Omega
exists by Lemma
4.5
. Moreover, since every element of
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}(x)
can be written as
\mathfrak{s}_{n}^{k}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
by Proposition
4.2
, it is clear that
\Omega
is injective. It remains to show that, if
(\mathbb{Z},E)
is pseudo-free, then
\Omega
has a continuous inverse with the formula as claimed in the statement of the theorem.

First, let us check that
\Omega^{-1}(\vec{z})
is well-defined. If
i\leq j
,
then

(4.18)

\displaystyle z_{i}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\pi_{i}(\vec{z})=\bigl(f_{i,j}\circ\pi_{j}\bigr)(\vec{z})=z_{j}^{k_{i,j}}.

Now, if
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
and
m\leq n\in\mathbb{N}
are such that

\mathfrak{s}_{m}^{p}=\mathfrak{s}_{n}^{q}
, then by Lemma
4.6
(ii)
, there are two scenarios:
Either
h_{n}=1_{G}
, or
q=pk_{m,n}
. In the first case, it follows by definition of
X
and by Lemma

(i)
that
X_{m}=X_{n}=\{1\}
; thus,
z_{m}^{p}=1=z_{n}^{q}
. In the second case, we get

This proves that the assignment
\kappa\vcentcolon=\Omega^{-1}(\vec{z})\colon\mathfrak{s}_{n}^{q}\mapsto z_{n}^{q}
is unambiguous. Next, we show that
\kappa
is an element of
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}(x)
: for any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, any
\ell\geq m,n
, and any
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle\kappa(\mathfrak{s}_{m}^{p}\,\mathfrak{s}_{n}^{q})

\displaystyle=z_{\ell}^{pk_{m,\ell}+qk_{n,\ell}}

by Proposition
4.2
and
by def’n of
\kappa

\displaystyle=z_{m}^{p}\,z_{n}^{q}

by Equation (

) (twice)

\displaystyle=\kappa(\mathfrak{s}_{m}^{p})\kappa(\mathfrak{s}_{n}^{q})

\displaystyle\text{by def'n of $\kappa$}.

We further have that
\kappa(\mathfrak{s}_{m}^{0})=z_{m}^{0}=1
, so that
\kappa(1_{\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}})=1
, which finishes the proof that
\kappa
is a homomorphism. Since it is furthermore
\mathbb{T}
-valued and continuous (as
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}(x)
is discrete from the groupoid being étale), we have shown that
\kappa=\Omega^{-1}(\vec{z})
is an element of
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}(x)
.

To verify that
\vec{z}\mapsto\Omega^{-1}(\vec{z})
is continuous, let
(\vec{z}_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
be a net in

that converges to
\vec{z}
. To see that
\kappa_{\lambda}\vcentcolon=\Omega^{-1}(\vec{z}_{\lambda})
converges to
\kappa\vcentcolon=\Omega^{-1}(\vec{z})
, we must show

\kappa_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{t})\to\kappa(\mathfrak{t})
for all
\mathfrak{t}
in
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}(x)
. (Here, we have made use of the fact that
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}(x)
is discrete, as explained earlier.)
If we write
\mathfrak{t}=\mathfrak{s}_{n}^{p}
for some

and some

, which by continuity of

converges to

Lastly, note that the maps
\Omega
and

are indeed mutually inverse:
\bigl(\Omega\circ\Omega^{-1}\bigr)(\vec{z})
is the infinite sequence whose

\displaystyle(\pi_{n}\circ\Omega)\bigl(\Omega^{-1}(\vec{z})\bigr)=\Omega^{-1}(\vec{z})\bigl(\mathfrak{s}_{n}\bigr)=z_{n},

meaning it is the infinite sequence

.
Likewise,

\displaystyle(\Omega^{-1}\circ\Omega)(\kappa)=\bigl(\mathfrak{s}_{n}\mapsto\pi_{n}(\Omega(\kappa))=\kappa(\mathfrak{s}_{n})\bigr)=\kappa.

.
∎

4.2.
Towards a new groupoid model: The action

We have shown in Proposition

that, given any self-similar graph
(G,E)
, the abelian groupoid
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
is open and normal in
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
and has full unit space. This allows us to invoke the following theorem by Renault, where we write
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}
for the class of an element
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{G}
in the quotient
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{S}
:

Theorem 4.7

(
[
25
, Cor. A.8]
and special case of
[
25
, Theorem 5.5(ii)]
)
.

Let

\mathcal{G}
be a second countable, locally compact, Hausdorff,
étale groupoid.
Suppose that

\mathcal{S}
is
an open,
abelian, normal subgroupoid with
\mathcal{S}^{(0)}=\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
.
Then the quotient groupoid
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{S}
is locally compact (not necessarily Hausdorff), and

C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})
is isomorphic to a
twisted
reduced groupoid

-algebra of the action groupoid
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{S}\ltimes\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
,
where
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{S}
acts on the topological space
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
by

(4.19)

\displaystyle(\dot{\mathfrak{g}}\mathchoice{\displaystyle\mathbin{\smalltriangleright}}{\textstyle\mathbin{\smalltriangleright}}{\scriptstyle\mathbin{\smalltriangleright}}{\scriptscriptstyle\mathbin{\smalltriangleright}}\kappa)(\mathfrak{s})=\kappa(\mathfrak{g}^{-1}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{g})

for
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{G}_{y}^{x}
,
\kappa\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}}(y)
,
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathcal{S}(x)
.

Remark 4.8
.

(1)

The groupoid
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{S}\ltimes\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
can be seen as an improvement over the groupoid
\mathcal{G}
since one has, effectively, moved an abelian part of the dynamics (i.e.,
\mathcal{S}
) into the purely topological, spatial part of the groupoid (represented by the new unit space,
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
). However, this comes at the price of (potentially) needing to deal with a twisted
C^{*}
-algebra rather than the untwisted
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{G})
, and with a potentially non-Hausdorff groupoid.

(2)

The quotient
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{S}
is Hausdorff if and only if
\mathcal{S}
is closed in
\mathcal{G}
. Since many people prefer to work with Hausdorff groupoids, it is therefore reasonable to wonder when
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
is closed in
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
. Provided that the group
G
of the self-similar graph
(G,E)
has an element of infinite order, we identify in Theorem
5.20
of Section
5.2
which conditions on
E
are necessary and sufficient for
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
to be closed.

(3)

The properties that
\mathcal{S}^{(0)}=\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, that
\mathcal{S}
is open, and that
\mathcal{G}
is locally compact étale can be used to show that the quotient map
q\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{S}
is a surjective continuous open groupoid homomorphism which, in turn, implies that the action of
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{S}
on
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
is continuous.

We will now focus on the situation that
G=\mathbb{Z}
and that we are given a self-similar graph
(\mathbb{Z},E)
that is pseudo-free, so that Theorem
4.4
identifies the dual of the subgroup
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}(x)
of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Z},E}
. We will now describe the action
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Z},E}\curvearrowright\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}
as defined at (
4.19
) more explicitly, to see the advantage of the new groupoid model in Theorem
4.7
; the main take-away is that the new groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Z},E}/\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}\ltimes\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}
is much easier to understand than the original groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Z},E}
.

The first thing we point out is that the action of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Z},E}/\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
on
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}
can be easily computed—no matter the choice of
E
, the action of
\mathbb{Z}
on
E
, or the restriction ‘of
E
on
\mathbb{Z}
.’ In fact,
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Z},E}/\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}\curvearrowright\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{sym}}
really only depends on the copy of
\mathbb{Z}
in

Q(\mathbb{N},\mathbb{Z})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
in a way that we will now make precise.

Fix
x\in E^{\infty}
, and let
\mathfrak{s}_{x,n}\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}(x)
be as defined at (

), where we added the index

to

and an arbitrary element

and

By definition of the semi-direct product, we have

and so since

a standard computation shows

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\mathcal{T}_{m}([g|_{\sigma^{\ell}(y)}]),m-\ell)^{-1}\,(\mathcal{T}_{n}([h_{n}|_{\sigma^{n}(x)}]),0)\,(\mathcal{T}_{m}([g|_{\sigma^{\ell}(y)}]),m-\ell)\\

Let

\mathfrak{g}\mathchoice{\displaystyle\mathbin{\smalltriangleright}}{\textstyle\mathbin{\smalltriangleright}}{\scriptstyle\mathbin{\smalltriangleright}}{\scriptscriptstyle\mathbin{\smalltriangleright}}\kappa\colon\mathfrak{s}\mapsto\kappa(\mathfrak{g}^{-1}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{g})

\left(x;[f],k;y\right)\mathchoice{\displaystyle\mathbin{\smalltriangleright}}{\textstyle\mathbin{\smalltriangleright}}{\scriptstyle\mathbin{\smalltriangleright}}{\scriptscriptstyle\mathbin{\smalltriangleright}}\kappa\colon\quad\left(x;\mathcal{T}_{n}([h|_{\sigma^{n}(x)}]),0;x\right)\mapsto\kappa\left(y;\mathcal{T}_{n-k}([h|_{\sigma^{n}(x)}]),0;y\right).

This shows that the

Let

Since

\quad for all big enough $\ell$.
}

Lemma 5.16

.

Since

(III)

\displaystyle a^{p}|_{x(0,|\nu|+\ell)}

∎

We conclude this subsection with the proof that
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
is maximal among open and abelian subgroupoids of

, under the assumption that

.

Proof of Theorem
5.4
.

We have seen in Proposition

that

is an open and abelian subgroupoid of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Z},E})^{\circ}
. It remains to show that

is maximal: By Corollary
5.10
and Lemma

,
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Z},E})^{\circ}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}

consists exactly of Type
(II)
and Type
(III)

, by Lemmas

and

, Type
(II)

respectively Type

in particular.
Thus, any subset of

larger than

fails to be abelian.
∎

In cases where

or

, elements of Type

or Type

may commute with elements of

. The resulting computations become significantly more technical and largely ad hoc, although the underlying proof strategy remains essentially the same as that presented in this section. For this reason, we do not pursue these cases in greater generality. A discussion of maximal abelian subgroupoids in the case

is included in Appendix
B
for interested readers.

5.2.
Closedness of
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}

In this Subsection, we characterize closedness in
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
for subgroupoids
\mathcal{S}
of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
containing
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
. In particular, we provide equivalent conditions for closedness of both
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
and
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
, which are necessary for proving corresponding results for Cartan subalgebras (Theorems
5.1
,
5.3
, and Appendix
B.1
).

Given any subgroupoid
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
, we will write
\mathcal{S}^{c}\vcentcolon=\mathcal{G}_{G,E}\setminus\mathcal{S}
for its complement in
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
.
Note that if
\mathcal{S}
is further a subgroupoid of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})
, we have the following:

(5.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\text{ is closed in }\mathcal{G}_{G,E}\iff\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})\subseteq\mathcal{S}\sqcup(\mathcal{S}^{c})^{\circ}.

This follows since
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})
is closed in the Hausdorff groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
(as explained in Section
2.2
, Hausdorffness follows from pseudo-freeness of the self-similar action).

In the rest of this section, we translate the abstract groupoid dichotomy on the right hand side of the equivalence
(
5.16
)
into concrete dynamical properties of the underlying self-similar
graph (Theorem
5.20
).
This perspective naturally leads to the following notions of
g
-generic and
g
-rare infinite paths. The notion of
g
-generic infinite paths can be traced back at least to the work of Nekrashevych (see
[
19
, Section 4]
for instance, where an infinite path
x
is said to be
generic with respect to
g\in G
). In contrast, the notion of
g
-rare appears to be new, at least to the best of the authors’ knowledge, and is intended to capture essentially the opposite dynamical
phenomenon of
g
-genericity.

Definition 5.17
.

Let
(G,E)
be a self-similar graph, and let
x\in E^{\infty}
. For any
g\in G
, we say that

•

x
is
g
-generic
if either
g\cdot x\neq x
, or
g
pointwise fixes a neighborhood of
x
;

•

x
is
g
-rare
if
g\cdot x=x
, and there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
g
does not pointwise fix any neighborhood of any
z\in x(0,N)E^{\infty}
.

Remark 5.18
.

Unpacking the definition of
g
-rarity, one sees that
x
is
g
-rare if and only if there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
with the following property:
if
y\in x(N,N)E^{\infty}
satisfies
g|_{x(0,N)}\cdot y=y
, then for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the element
g|_{x(0,N)}|_{y(0,m)}
does not fix all of
y(m,m)E^{\infty}
.

The connection between the abstract groupoid condition in (
5.16
) and these dynamical characterizations is made precise in the following lemma. Note that the pseudo-freeness assumption will be used throughout the remainder of the argument for closedness, as we make essential use of Lemma
2.11
in what follows.

Lemma 5.19
.

Let
(G,E)
be a pseudo-free self-similar graph. Take an infinite path
x
and some
g\in G
such that
g\cdot x=x
, and a finite path
\mu
such that
s(\mu)=x(0,0)
. Let
\mathfrak{g}=\bigl(\mu x;\mathcal{T}_{|\mu|}([g|_{x}]),0;\mu x\bigr)
be an element of
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

x
is
g
-generic;

(2)

\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
;

(3)

\mathfrak{g}\in\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
;

(4)

\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{S}
for any subgroupoid
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}\subseteq\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
.

Likewise, the following are equivalent:

(i)

x
is
g
-rare;

(ii)

\mathfrak{g}\in(\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}^{c})^{\circ}
;

(iii)

\mathfrak{g}\in\bigl(\bigl(\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}\bigr)^{c}\bigr)^{\circ}
;

(iv)

\mathfrak{g}\in(\mathcal{S}^{c})^{\circ}
for any subgroupoid

\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}\subseteq\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
;

Proof.

The implications

(2)
\implies
(3)
and
(iii)
\implies
(ii)
follow from the fact that

, and the equivalences
(1)
+
(2)
\iff
(4)
and
(i)
+
(ii)
\iff
(iv)
are straightforward. It remains to

(3)
\implies
(1)
\implies
(2)
and
(ii)
\implies
(i)
\implies
(iii)
.

(3)

\implies

(1)
: Assume that
\mathfrak{g}\in\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
.
Since
(G,E)
is pseudo-free, by Lemma
2.11
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(5.17)

\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{Z}\bigl(\mu(g\cdot x(0,n)),g|_{x(0,n)},\mu x(0,n)\bigr)\subseteq\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}.

By (
2.14
), this
implies that

fixes any

.
Note that our assumption

implies
g\cdot x(0,n)=x(0,n)
. Combining this with

yields
that
g

\mathcal{Z}(x(0,n))
of

, so that

is
g
-generic.

(1)

\implies

(2)
: If
x
is
g
-generic, then since we assumed

,

\mathcal{Z}(x(0,n))
for some

. This means that for any

, we have

,

which implies

This means that

is a symmetric cycline triple.
Since

is in the associated cylinder set by construction, our definition of

implies
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
, as needed.

(ii)

\implies

(i)
:
We will show the contrapositive, so suppose
that

is not
g

of

intersects

non-trivially. Since

is pseudo-free, again by Lemma

, there exists

such that

\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{Z}\bigl(\mu x(0,n),g|_{x(0,n)},\mu x(0,n)\bigr)\subseteq U.

Since

such that

and

.

(i)
\implies
(iii)
: Suppose that

, the group element

.

.

∎

Combining the equivalence in (

) and Lemma

, we characterize closedness of any subgroupoid

sitting between

and

.

Theorem 5.20
.

Let
(G,E)
be a pseudo-free self-similar graph, where
G
is
a group containing
an element of infinite order, and
E
has finitely many vertices and no sources.
The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

For
every
g\in G
and every
x\in E^{\infty}
,
the path
x
is either
g
-generic or
g
-rare.

(2)

Every subgroupoid
\mathcal{S}

with

\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}\subseteq\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}

is closed in
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
.

(3)

The subgroupoid
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}
is closed in
\mathcal{G}_{G,E}
.

Proof.

As noted in (
5.16
),
(2)
holds if and only if
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})\subseteq\mathcal{S}\sqcup(\mathcal{S}^{c})^{\circ}
for any subgroupoid
\mathcal{S}
with
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}\subseteq\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
.
Note further that
(2)
\implies
(3)
is obvious.

(3)

\implies
(1)
: For any infinite path
x
and
g\in G
such that
g
fixes
x
, and a finite path
\mu
such that
s(\mu)=x(0,0)
, the isotropy element
\mathfrak{g}=\bigl(\mu x;\mathcal{T}_{|\mu|}([g|_{x}]),0;\mu x\bigr)
is contained in
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}\sqcup(\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}^{c})^{\circ}
by (

). By Lemma

, this implies

.

(1)
\implies
(2)
: Fix a subgroupoid
\mathcal{S}
with
\mathcal{S}_{\text{sym}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}\subseteq\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
. By (

), it suffices to show that
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})\subseteq\mathcal{S}\cup(\mathcal{S}^{c})^{\circ}
. Fix an element of
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})
, say

(5.22)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\bigl(\mu(g\cdot x);\mathcal{T}_{|\mu|}([g|_{x}]),|\mu|-|\nu|;\nu x\bigr),\text{ where $\mu(g\cdot x)=\nu x$}.

If
|\mu|=|\nu|
, then

\mu(g\cdot x)=\nu x

and

,
so Lemma
5.19

is either in
\mathcal{S}
or in

, as needed.

Now assume that
|\mu|\neq|\nu|
. We first consider the case that
\mathfrak{g}\in\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ}
. Since
(G,E)
is pseudo-free, by Lemma
2.11
, there exists

such that

(5.23)

\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{Z}\bigl(\mu(g\cdot x(0,n)),g|_{x(0,n)},\nu x(0,n)\bigr)\subseteq\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal{G}_{G,E})^{\circ},

which implies that
\bigl(\mu(g\cdot x(0,n)),g|_{x(0,n)},\nu x(0,n)\bigr)
is a cycline triple at

and is non-symmetric as

. By Corollary

Remark 5.22
.
