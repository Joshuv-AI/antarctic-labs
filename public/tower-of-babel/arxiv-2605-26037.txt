Peak-Then-Collapse and the Four Interface Channels of Knowledge-Graph Tool Use
Tianda Sun, Dimitar Kazakov

Abstract
We test the standard RLVR tool-use recipe — GRPO on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct — on a deliberately minimal knowledge-graph tool API: four Freebase navigation verbs over Complex WebQuestions. Under a self-verifiable retrieval reward, the policy’s tool-grounded answer rate climbs from 3.8 % 3.8\% to 9.6 % 9.6\% over 250 steps, then collapses to 0 % 0\% within a single 50-step window — a peak-then-collapse pattern replicated across four seeds. Across seven reward designs, we find four recurring failure modes: adding denser or more targeted proxy rewards shifts the failure mode rather than eliminating it. We argue that a key difference from Python interpreters, web search, and JSON APIs is interface feedback: their failures often leak natural-language signal the model saw in pretraining. A Python traceback names the failing line; an empty Freebase result [] does not. Stripping away that surface exposes a degradation regime that same-family reward redesigns do not fix. A direct oracle ablation rules out relation selection: injecting gold relations at every retrieval call lifts exact-match accuracy by only + 0.20 +0.20 pp, and 95.4 % 95.4\% of retrieval-dependent errors are retrieval-composition failures rather than answer-extraction failures. As a mitigation, one-iteration self-distillation reaches 40.0 % 40.0\% EM at 7B and is capacity-invariant: doubling capacity to 14B improves EM by only 0.25 0.25 pp, and initialization barely matters — the ceiling appears interface-bound within the 7B–14B range tested.

Introduction

Large language models increasingly act as
agents

(

(

Python tool use leaks natural-language signal the model saw in pretraining — a traceback names the failing line — whereas this KG interface leaks almost none. We ask whether the standard RLVR-tool recipe still converges once that surface is removed.

5
A Signal-Theoretic Account of Tool-Interface Difficulty

The four failure modes characterised in §
4.3
share a common substrate: each is a degradation of the gradient signal GRPO needs to converge on a tool-grounded policy. We propose this substrate has structure — four interface-level channels along which a tool API supplies (or fails to supply) usable RL signal (Figure
4
) — yielding four channel-level claims. These channels are a
descriptive organising hypothesis
, not a causally-validated decomposition: §
5.3
directly intervenes on one (the relation oracle); isolating the rest is future work.

•

(opaque compositional state).
Multi-hop KG reasoning carries entity IDs across turns; these IDs are semantically opaque and any paraphrasing breaks the chain.

•

L_{\mathrm{prior}}
(no pretraining prior).
Zero pretraining examples of Freebase schema navigation, versus extensive Python / search / JSON-API exposure.

6
Conclusion

Within a deliberately controlled scope (one model family, one task, one RL algorithm), seven GRPO-trained reward designs on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct + CWQ converge into four interface-level failure modes (
L_{\mathrm{sig/lang/comp/prior}}
); an oracle ablation rules out relation selection as the EM bottleneck, locating the residual gap upstream of answer extraction (the specific channel is unresolved). A one-iteration self-distillation recipe reaches
40.0\%
EM at 7B (self-distill), matched by 14B; richer concurrent pipelines exceed this via interface enrichments — advances need interface-aware training, not larger models or denser proxy rewards. Methodologically, our 50-step cadence caught the peak-then-collapse mode; step-resolved instrumentation is essential for process-reward RL. Open: whether a non-gameable self-verifiable signal exists; how far interface enrichments push the ceiling is the richer-pipeline line’s domain, where our channel attribution interprets their gains.

Limitations

Our empirical scope is bounded in six ways.

(i) Model family.
All main-body results are Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, consistent with concurrent KGQA-RL evaluation norm (GraphWalker, Graph-RFT, StepSearch, ToolRL, Search-R1 all evaluate on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct). Cross-family replication on Llama-3.1-8B-Base (App.
F
) reaches comparable EM but exhibits a parametric-memory mode-collapse distinct from Qwen’s peak-then-collapse, indicating model-family-specific channel weights.

(ii) Model scale.
The R-toolverbs·KL-14B replication (Table
2
: EM=40.24%, CvT=6.40%; Table
8
: hard-partition CvT 4.61%, within noise of 7B self-distill’s 4.19%) is positive evidence that the
\sim
40% ceiling on this reward family is interface-bound, not capacity-bound. The capacity-invariance argument (§
5.2
, P2) anchors on the 7B vs 14B comparison;
\geq
32B replication is left to follow-up.

(iii) Search-R1 reimplementation.
Our R-binary-SR variant is a faithful re-implementation of Search-R1’s recipe (verl + binary-EM outcome-only reward + Search-R1 hyperparameters) on our pipeline (Appendix
A
); we label it a reimplementation rather than a reproduction.

(iv) Benchmark scope.
Main-body training is on CWQ only. KGQAGen-10k appears as a cross-benchmark Oracle control (§
4.6
) but we do not retrain or evaluate our trained policy on it. We make no claims about web-search-style RAG or QA over non-KG textual corpora.

(v) Closed-model schema-unfamiliarity control.
GPT-4o (§
4.6
; App.
D
) is a schema-unfamiliarity control bracketing the
L_{\mathrm{lang}}
channel, not a head-to-head baseline: on a 200-question sub-eval,
\sim 78\%
of GPT-4o trajectories self-describe as “unable to retrieve X” — direct evidence that schema unfamiliarity, not reasoning capability, is the binding constraint at frontier closed-model scale.

(vi) Evaluation protocol.
Per-step monitoring (50-step cadence) and final reporting both use the full
3{,}531
-question CWQ test set; specific checkpoint labels (e.g., R-selfV peak at step 250) are therefore test-set-informed selections. We frame this as a controlled diagnostic study rather than a leaderboard claim — the peak-then-collapse
signature
replicates across four independent seeds with peak in step 200–250 and full collapse by step 300 in every seed (§
4.3
), reducing single-checkpoint cherry-picking risk; and the comparative claims that anchor the framework (capacity invariance, oracle
+0.20
pp,
95.4\%
retrieval-composition) are robust to a
\pm 50
-step window. A held-out dev/test re-selection (§
3.5
; App.
B.10
) confirms the reported checkpoints are not test-set artefacts: the dev peak is step 250 and all ten checkpoints fall within
\pm 1.85
pp, so full-set reporting is retained for comparability.

Appendix C
SFT corpora

Rule-based shared SFT corpus (5,000 trajectories).

The shared SFT initialisation used by every reward variant is constructed from the CWQ training split as follows. (i) For each question, the gold triple chain is extracted from the WebQuestionsSP annotation. (ii)
<think>
content is filled in from one of three fixed templates with variable substitution (entities and relations drawn from the gold chain). (iii)
<search>
calls are constructed directly from the gold chain, with the tool response obtained by executing the query against the Freebase sub-graph — so every call returns by construction a response that contains the next entity on the gold path. (iv) The final
<answer>
is the gold answer.
No LLM teacher is used anywhere in this pipeline.

Enhanced variant (6,000 trajectories).

An enhanced variant
sft_trajectories_enhanced.jsonl
uses the same pipeline with additional diversity in the
<think>
template and richer tool-call ordering. Both corpora are used interchangeably for the shared SFT base; results are not sensitive to which is used.

Self-distillation corpus (14,082 trajectories).

For init-from-iterate and self-distill (§
4.5
) we roll out the R-toolverbs·KL@400 checkpoint (HPC code: E5b-stab) greedily on the full
27{,}639
-question CWQ training split, then filter to trajectories satisfying
simultaneously

•

strict EM
=1

•

\geq 1
productive tool call (in the I-Self sense — non-empty response and
\geq 1

<answer>

•

valid
<answer>
envelope format.

Yield:
14{,}082

Methodological positioning.

Most
2025

agentic-RL papers use LLM teachers (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen-72B, GPT-4) for SFT data generation, introducing teacher-model bias confounds
(
