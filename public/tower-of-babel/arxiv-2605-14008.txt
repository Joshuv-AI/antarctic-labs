Predictive Inference via Kernel Density Estimates
Torey Hilbert

Abstract
Kernel density estimation is a widely used nonparametric approach to estimate an unknown distribution. Recent work in Bayesian predictive inference has considered stochastic processes formed by specifying the predictive distribution for the next data point given all observed data such that the resulting predictive distributions converge weakly almost surely. We study two kernel based prediction rules: the classic kernel density estimator, and a recursive version previously introduced for online problems. We show that both processes converge weakly almost surely, which opens the door for new Bayesian interpretations of kernel density estimation. Surprisingly, the process based on the classic kernel density estimates converges to a compactly supported measure, while the recursive version converges to a non-compactly supported measure.



1
Introduction

Recent work on Bayesian predictive inference has considered modeling data by specifying a collection of prediction rules for
X_{n+1}|X_{1:n}
, which when supplemented with a distribution over
X_{1}
gives a joint distribution over an infinite sequence of data
X_{1:\infty}
.
Denoting the (random) predictive measures by
P_{n}(B)=\mathbb{P}(X_{n+1}\in B|X_{1:n})
for Borel sets
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if the sequence of predictive measures
P_{n}
almost surely converges weakly to some (random) probability measure
P
, then the conditional limiting predictive measure
P|X_{1:n}
is a posterior distribution for a Bayesian analysis

(
7
;
6
;
8
)
.
The recent Bayesian predictive inference literature has largely focused on predictive distributions satisfying the conditionally identically distributed (CID) property
(
3
;
6
;
8
)
, or on predictive distributions that are “almost” a CID sequence as in
1
.

Part of the appeal of the Bayesian predictive inference framework, however, is the idea that the analyst could use any predictive distributions that they feel are “natural”.
When handed a non-parametric prediction problem, the first approach for many statisticians is to use the empirical distribution, leading to Bayesian bootstrap methods
(
5
;
12
;
10
)
. These methods are generalized in the works on measure-valued Pólya sequences
(
2
;
13
;
4
)
. A second natural approach for many statisticians is to smooth the empirical distribution, for example with a kernel density estimator
(
14
)
.

Let
K
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
h_{n}>0
be a sequence of bandwidths. For each
n
and Borel set
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we consider the predictive rule

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X_{n+1}\in B|X_{1:n})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K\bigg(\frac{B-X_{i}}{h_{n}}\bigg).

(1)

Here we use the notation
(B-X_{i})/{h_{n}}=\{(b-X_{i})/h_{n}:b\in B\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
To fully specify the model we will arbitrarily set
X_{1}=0
. Denote the sequence of random predictive measures by
P_{n}(B)=\mathbb{P}(X_{n+1}\in B|X_{1:n})
.
For example, if
K=N(0,1)
on
\mathbb{R}
, this is equivalent to using the probability density function

\displaystyle f(x|X_{1:n})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{h_{n}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{x-X_{i}}{h_{n}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg),

the classic kernel density estimator (KDE).

In this work, we show that for modestly heavy-tailed kernels
K
and bandwidths
h_{n}
that decay at order
n^{-\delta}
for some
\delta>0
, the sequence of predictive distributions defined by the KDE process above converges weakly almost surely to a limiting random measure
P
. Furthermore, with additional moment assumptions, the limiting random measure
P
is supported on a random compact set. While the almost sure weak convergence of
P_{n}
is expected, the fact that the limiting measure
P
is almost surely compactly supported was surprising to us.

While our primary interest is model
1
, we also consider a recursive kernel estimator introduced by
16
.
For standard implementations, the KDE process and this alternative recursive kernel process behave qualitatively differently, since the limiting measure of the recursive kernel process almost surely has non-compact support. For the recursive kernel estimator,
1
find that for very rapidly decaying bandwidths, such as
h_{n}=e^{-n}
, the sequence of predictive measures converges weakly almost surely for some kernels
K
. However, their approach does not work for
h_{n}
satisfying
nh_{n}\to\infty
, which is a well-known condition for frequentist point-wise consistency of the KDE
(
14
)
. We show, under much weaker conditions, that the recursive kernel estimator also converges weakly almost surely.

2
Kernel Density Process

Another way to write the same sequence of variables in model
1
is as follows:

\displaystyle Y_{1:\infty}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}K,

\displaystyle M_{n}

\displaystyle\overset{ind}{\sim}\text{Unif}(\{1,\ldots,n\}),

\displaystyle X_{n+1}

\displaystyle=X_{M_{n}}+h_{n}Y_{n}.

In contrast to
X_{1:\infty}
, we also consider a different process
\tilde{X}_{1:\infty}
defined by recursive kernel estimates, as in
16
;
1
. We will use a tilde to differentiate objects connected to
X_{1:\infty}
vs
\tilde{X}_{1:\infty}
. We define the recursive kernel process by

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{n}(B)=\mathbb{P}(\tilde{X}_{n+1}\in B|\tilde{X}_{1:n})=\bigg(1-\frac{1}{n}\bigg)\tilde{P}_{n-1}(B)+\frac{1}{n}K\bigg(\frac{B-\tilde{X}_{n}}{h_{n}}\bigg).

(2)

Again we arbitrarily select
\tilde{X}_{1}=0
to start the sequence. The key difference between
X_{1:\infty}
and
\tilde{X}_{1:\infty}
is that the former model uses the same bandwidth
h_{n}
around all points and recomputes the KDE at every step, while the latter model never updates the bandwidth for an already observed point in the sequence. Subjectively, we prefer the classic KDE approach defining the sequence
X_{1:\infty}
, but, as the two models have qualitatively different asymptotic behavior, we analyze both. Another way to write the recursive process is

\displaystyle Y_{1:\infty}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}K,

\displaystyle M_{n}

\displaystyle\overset{ind}{\sim}\text{Unif}(\{1,\ldots,n\}),

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\tilde{X}_{M_{n}}+h_{M_{n}}Y_{n}.

The following two results show that both the classic KDE predictive rule and the recursive predictive rule are asymptotically exchangeable, and thus can be used in the Bayesian predictive inference paradigm laid out by
8
.

Theorem 1

(Weak convergence for KDE process)
.

Suppose
0<h_{n}\leq Cn^{-\delta}
for some
C,\delta>0
, and suppose
\mathbb{E}(\|Y_{1}\|)<\infty
. Then
\mathbb{P}(P_{n}\overset{weak}{\to}P)=1
for some random probability measure
P
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Additionally, if
\mathbb{E}(\|Y_{1}\|^{1/c})<\infty
for some
0<c<\delta
, then
P
is almost surely supported on a (random) compact set
V
, and
\mathbb{P}(\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|X_{n}\|<\infty)=1
.

Theorem 2

(Weak convergence for recursive process)
.

Suppose
0<h_{n}\leq Cn^{-\delta}
for some
C,\delta>0
, and suppose
\mathbb{E}(\|Y_{1}\|)<\infty
. Then
\mathbb{P}(\tilde{P_{n}}\overset{weak}{\to}\tilde{P})=1
for some random probability measure
\tilde{P}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

A common choice for densities on
\mathbb{R}
is
h_{n}=O(n^{-1/5})
, which in a frequentist setting minimizes asymptotic mean integrated square error under regularity conditions e.g.,
11
;
14
.
Our theorems establish that, for any choice of kernel
K
with finite first moment, both the KDE process
P_{n}
and the recursive process
\tilde{P}_{n}
will converge weakly almost surely.
Furthermore, if
K
has finite sixth moments then
P
almost surely has compact support.
For densities on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, typically we use
h_{n}=O(n^{-1/(d+4)})
, so that if
\|Y_{1}\|
has
d+4
moments then
P
has compact support. Lastly, when
K
has exponential tails,
P
has compact support for any
\delta>0
.

Typically one would use a kernel
K
with
\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]=0
, but to demonstrate a critical difference between the KDE process and the recursive kernel process, we will consider an artificial example with
K([0,\infty))=1
that is substantially more straightforward mathematically.
Let
K=|N(0,1)|
, the absolute value of a
N(0,1)
random variable, and set
h_{n}=n^{-\delta}
.
Immediately, we see that
\tilde{P}_{n}
will always include a
\frac{1}{n}|N(0,h_{1}^{2})|
term coming from
X_{1}=0
, so by the Borel-Cantelli lemma with
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}1/n=\infty
, we have
\mathbb{P}(\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\tilde{X}_{n}\|=\infty)=1
, in contrast to
\mathbb{P}(\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|X_{n}\|<\infty)=1
.
Fix
M
and suppose
\tilde{X}_{n}>M
for some
n
. Let
\tilde{p}_{m}=\frac{1}{m}|\{j\geq n:\tilde{X}_{j}\text{ is a descendant of }\tilde{X}_{n}\}|
. Using a Polya urn scheme, we get
\tilde{p}_{m}\overset{a.s.}{\to}\tilde{p}\sim\text{Beta}(1,n-1)
. Then

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{m}(\{x:|x|>M\})\geq\tilde{p}_{m}\not\to 0.

But for any
M>0
, almost surely
\tilde{X}_{n}>M
for some
n
. Thus the limiting measure
\tilde{P}
is not compactly supported. The same result holds for
K=N(0,1)
, albeit with more tedious details since the descendants of
\tilde{X}_{n}
are no longer strictly greater than
\tilde{X}_{n}
.

3
Proofs

3.1
Notation

First we consider the non-negative processes on
\mathbb{R}
given by defining
W_{n}=\|Y_{n}\|
and

\displaystyle U_{n+1}=U_{M_{n}}+h_{n}W_{n},\quad\tilde{U}_{n+1}=\tilde{U}_{M_{n}}+h_{M_{n}}W_{n},

where
U_{1}=0
and
\tilde{U}_{1}=0
.
Notice that
\|X_{n}\|\leq U_{n}
and
\|\tilde{X}_{n}\|\leq\tilde{U}_{n}
. Throughout we will let
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\sigma(\{X_{k},\tilde{X}_{k}:k\leq n\})
be the sigma-field generated by data up to step
n
.

3.2
Tightness for the KDE process

Define
J_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[U_{n+1}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[U_{M_{n}}+h_{n}W_{n}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}+h_{n}\mathbb{E}[W_{1}],

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[J_{n+1}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{n+1}J_{n}+\frac{1}{n+1}\mathbb{E}[U_{n+1}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]=J_{n}+c_{n},

where
c_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}h_{n}\mathbb{E}[W_{1}]
. Notice that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}<\infty
. Defining the sequence of non-negative variables
S_{n}=J_{n}+\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}c_{k}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[S_{n+1}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]=J_{n}+c_{n}+\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}c_{k}=S_{n},

so that
S_{n}
is a non-negative martingale. Then
S_{n}\overset{a.s.}{\to}S
, and
\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}c_{k}\to 0
, so we get the almost sure convergence
J_{n}\overset{a.s.}{\to}S
.
Fix
\epsilon>0
, and let
N
be such that for all
n\geq N
,
|J_{n}-S|<\epsilon
and
h_{n}\mathbb{E}[W_{1}]<\epsilon
. Defining the predictive measures
Q_{n}(B)=\mathbb{P}(U_{n+1}\in B|\mathcal{F}_{n})
,

\displaystyle Q_{n}((S/\epsilon+1,\infty))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{J_{n}+h_{n}\mathbb{E}[W_{1}]}{S/\epsilon+1}\leq\epsilon\Big(\frac{S+\epsilon}{S+\epsilon}+\frac{\epsilon}{S+\epsilon}\Big)\leq 2\epsilon.

But since
\|X_{n}\|\leq U_{n}
, this implies
\limsup_{n}P_{n}(\{x:\|x\|>S/\epsilon+1\})\leq 2\epsilon
, showing that
\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is almost surely a tight collection of measures.

3.3
Tightness for the recursive process

Similarly, we define
\tilde{J}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{U}_{i}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\tilde{U}_{n+1}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\tilde{U}_{M_{n}}+h_{M_{n}}W_{n}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{U}_{i}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}h_{i}\mathbb{E}[W_{1}],

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\tilde{J}_{n+1}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{n+1}\tilde{J}_{n}+\frac{1}{n+1}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{U}_{n+1}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]=\tilde{J}_{n}+c_{n},

where
c_{n}=\frac{1}{n(n+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}h_{i}\mathbb{E}[W_{1}]
. Notice that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}<\infty
. Defining the sequence of non-negative variables
\tilde{S}_{n}=\tilde{J}_{n}+\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}c_{k}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\tilde{S}_{n+1}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]=\tilde{J}_{n}+c_{n}+\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}c_{k}=\tilde{S}_{n},

so that
\tilde{S}_{n}
is a non-negative martingale. Then
\tilde{S}_{n}\overset{a.s.}{\to}\tilde{S}
, and
\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}c_{k}\to 0
, so we get the almost sure convergence
\tilde{J}_{n}\overset{a.s.}{\to}\tilde{S}
.
Fix
\epsilon>0
, and let
N
be such that for all
n\geq N
,
|\tilde{J}_{n}-\tilde{S}|<\epsilon
and
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}h_{k}\mathbb{E}[W_{1}]<\epsilon
. Defining the predictive measures
\tilde{Q}_{n}(B)=\mathbb{P}(\tilde{U}_{n+1}\in B|\mathcal{F}_{n})
,

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{n}((\tilde{S}/\epsilon+1,\infty))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\tilde{J}_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}h_{k}\mathbb{E}[W_{1}]}{\tilde{S}/\epsilon+1}\leq\epsilon\Big(\frac{\tilde{S}+\epsilon}{\tilde{S}+\epsilon}+\frac{\epsilon}{\tilde{S}+\epsilon}\Big)\leq 2\epsilon.

But since
\|\tilde{X}_{n}\|\leq\tilde{U}_{n}
, this implies
\limsup_{n}\tilde{P}_{n}(\{x:\|x\|>\tilde{S}/\epsilon+1\})\leq 2\epsilon
, showing that
\{\tilde{P}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is almost surely a tight collection of measures.

3.4
Convergence of KDE characteristic functions

Let
\phi_{n}(t)
be the characteristic function of
P_{n}
, that is
\mathbb{E}[\exp(it^{T}X_{n+1})|\mathcal{F}_{n}]
. Let
\phi_{K}(t)
be the characteristic function of
K
. Then

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\exp(it^{T}X_{k})\phi_{K}(h_{n}t),

so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\phi_{n+1}(t)|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\exp(it^{T}X_{k})\phi_{K}(h_{n+1}t)+\frac{1}{n+1}\mathbb{E}[\exp(it^{T}X_{n+1}|\mathcal{F}_{n})]\phi_{K}(h_{n+1}t)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{n+1}\frac{\phi_{K}(h_{n+1}t)}{\phi_{K}(h_{n}t)}\phi_{n}(t)+\frac{1}{n+1}\phi_{n}(t)\phi_{K}(h_{n+1}t)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{\phi_{K}(h_{n}t)}{n+1}+\frac{n}{n+1}\bigg)\frac{\phi_{K}(h_{n+1}t)}{\phi_{K}(h_{n}t)}\phi_{n}(t).

Notice, there exists
N
such that for all
n\geq N
,
\phi_{K}(h_{n}t)\neq 0
by the continuity of
\phi_{K}
at
t=0
. Define

\displaystyle a_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi_{K}(h_{n}t)}{n+1}+\frac{n}{n+1},

\displaystyle b_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=a_{n}(t)\frac{\phi_{K}(h_{n+1}t)}{\phi_{K}(h_{n}t)},

\displaystyle c_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\Big(\prod_{k=n}^{\infty}b_{k}(t)\Big)=\Big(\prod_{k=n}^{\infty}a_{k}(t)\Big)\frac{1}{\phi_{K}(h_{n}t)},

where in Lemma
1
we show that
\prod_{k=n}^{\infty}a_{k}(t)\neq 0
. Then
\mathbb{E}[\phi_{n+1}(t)|\mathcal{F}_{n}]=b_{n}(t)\phi_{n}(t)
.
Define the martingale
S_{n}(t)=c_{n}(t)\phi_{n}(t)
, starting at step
N
to avoid dividing by
0
, by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[S_{n+1}(t)|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\Big(\prod_{k=n+1}^{\infty}b_{k}(t)\Big)\mathbb{E}[\phi_{n+1}(t)|\mathcal{F}_{n}]=\Big(\prod_{k=n+1}^{\infty}b_{k}(t)\Big)b_{n}(t)\phi_{n}(t)=S_{n}(t).

Since
\sup_{n\geq N}|c_{n}|<\infty
, we have
|S_{n}(t)|\leq\sup_{n\geq N}|c_{n}|<\infty
, so
S_{n}(t)
is a bounded martingale. Thus
S_{n}(t)\overset{a.s.}{\to}S(t)
, so that
\phi_{n}(t)\overset{a.s.}{\to}S(t)
because
c_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\geq N
and
\lim_{n}c_{n}(t)=1
.

We have already shown
\{P_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
almost surely is tight, and now the sequence of associated characteristic functions converges almost surely to
S(t)
; thus
P_{n}\overset{weak}{\to}P
almost surely for some (random) measure
P
.

3.5
Convergence of recursive process characteristic functions

Let
\tilde{\phi}_{n}(t)
be the characteristic function of
\tilde{P}_{n}
, that is
\mathbb{E}[\exp(it^{T}\tilde{X}_{n+1})|\mathcal{F}_{n}]
. Then

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\exp(it^{T}\tilde{X}_{k})\phi_{K}(h_{k}t),

so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\phi}_{n+1}(t)|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\exp(it^{T}\tilde{X}_{k})\phi_{K}(h_{k}t)+\frac{1}{n+1}\mathbb{E}[\exp(it^{T}\tilde{X}_{n+1}|\mathcal{F}_{n})]\phi_{K}(h_{n+1}t)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{n+1}\phi_{n}(t)+\frac{1}{n+1}\tilde{\phi}_{n}(t)\phi_{K}(h_{n+1}t).

Notice that there exists
N
such that for all
n\geq N
,
\phi_{K}(h_{n}t)\neq 0
by the continuity of
\phi_{K}
at
t=0
. Define

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi_{K}(h_{n+1}t)}{n+1}+\frac{n}{n+1},

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=n}^{\infty}\tilde{a}_{k}(t),

where in Lemma
1
we show that
\prod_{k=n}^{\infty}a_{k}(t)\neq 0
. Then
\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\phi}_{n+1}(t)|\mathcal{F}_{n}]=\tilde{a}_{n}(t)\tilde{\phi}_{n}(t)
.
Define the martingale
\tilde{S}_{n}(t)=\tilde{c}_{n}(t)\tilde{\phi}_{n}(t)
, starting at step
N
to avoid dividing by
0
, by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\tilde{S}_{n+1}(t)|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\prod_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\tilde{a}_{k}(t)\Bigg)\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\phi}_{n+1}(t)|\mathcal{F}_{n}]=\Bigg(\prod_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\tilde{a}_{k}(t)\Bigg)\tilde{a}_{n}(t)\tilde{\phi}_{n}(t)=\tilde{S}_{n}(t).

Since
|\tilde{c}_{n}|\leq 1
, we have
|\tilde{S}_{n}(t)|\leq 1
, so
\tilde{S}_{n}(t)
is a bounded martingale. Thus
\tilde{S}_{n}(t)\overset{a.s.}{\to}\tilde{S}(t)
, so that
\tilde{\phi}_{n}(t)\overset{a.s.}{\to}\tilde{S}(t)
because
\tilde{c}_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\geq N
and
\lim_{n}\tilde{c}_{n}(t)=1
.

We have already shown
\{\tilde{P}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
almost surely is tight, and now the sequence of associated characteristic functions converges almost surely to
\tilde{S}(t)
; thus
\tilde{P}_{n}\overset{weak}{\to}\tilde{P}
almost surely for some (random) measure
\tilde{P}
.

3.6
Compact support for limiting measure of KDE process

Assume that
\mathbb{E}(\|Y_{1}\|^{1/c})<\infty
for some
0<c<\delta
.
Let
L_{n,j}
be the number of descendants of
X_{j}
during the steps
\{n+1,n+2,\ldots,2n\}
; for example, at step
n+1
, we might have
M_{n+1}=j
, and then at step
n+2
we might have
M_{n+2}=n+1
, so that there are two descendants of
X_{j}
, thus
L_{n,j}=2
. Then
L_{n,j}
can be modeled using a Pólya urn, namely as an urn that starts with 1 black ball and
n-1
red balls, and
L_{n,j}
is the number of black balls drawn after
n
steps.
Then using the beta-binomial form for the Pólya urn, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(L_{n,j}=k)

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{k}\frac{B(k+1,2n-k-1)}{B(1,n-1)}

\displaystyle=(n-1)\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(n-i)}{\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(2n-1-i)}

\displaystyle\leq(n-1)(2/3)^{k},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(L_{n,j}\geq k)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r=k}^{n}(n-1)(2/3)^{r}\leq 3(n-1)(2/3)^{k}.

Then calling
L_{n}=\max_{j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}L_{n,j}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(L_{n}\geq k)\leq 3n(n-1)(2/3)^{k}.

Now let us partition
\mathbb{N}
into sets
J_{m}=\{j:2^{m}+1\leq j\leq 2^{m+1}\}
, starting with
J_{0}
. Pick some
r\in(c,\delta)
. We consider the events

\displaystyle A_{m}=\{L_{2^{m}}\geq 10m\},\quad B_{m}=\Big\{\max_{j\in J_{m}}\|Y_{j}\|\geq 2^{rm}\Big\}.

We aim to show that
A_{m}
and
B_{m}
happen only finitely many times.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{m})

\displaystyle\leq 3\cdot 2^{m}(2^{m}-1)(2/3)^{10m}\leq 3\cdot\exp(-m(10\log(3/2)-\log 4)),

and thus
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(A_{m})<\infty
. Next, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\max_{j\in J_{m}}\|Y_{j}\|\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\mathbb{E}\sum_{j\in J_{m}}^{n}\|Y_{j}\|^{1/c}\bigg)^{c}\leq 2^{cm}\mathbb{E}(|Y_{i}|^{1/c})^{c},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{j\in J_{m}}\|Y_{j}\|\geq 2^{rm}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\Big[\max_{j\in J_{m}}\|Y_{j}\|\Big]}{2^{rm}}\leq C_{1}2^{-m(r-c)},

so that
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(B_{m})<\infty
.
Thus there exists a random variable
N
such that for all
m\geq N
, neither
A_{m}
nor
B_{m}
occur. Then by tracing the genealogy of
X_{n}
through each set of steps given by
J_{m}
,

\displaystyle\|X_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}L_{2^{m}}h_{2^{m}}\max_{j\in J_{m}}\|Y_{j}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{N}L_{2^{m}}h_{2^{m}}\max_{j\in J_{m}}\|Y_{j}\|+\sum_{m=N}^{\infty}h_{2^{m}}10m\cdot 2^{rm}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{N}L_{2^{m}}h_{2^{m}}\max_{j\in J_{m}}\|Y_{j}\|+\sum_{m=N}^{\infty}10mC(2^{m})^{-(\delta-r)}=T<\infty,

where we have defined the random variable
T
in the last line. Then
\mathbb{P}(\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|X_{n}\|<\infty)=\mathbb{P}(\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|X_{n}\|\leq T)=1
. If
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|X_{n}\|<\infty
then

\displaystyle P_{m}(\{x:\|x\|>\sup_{n}\|X_{n}\|+\epsilon\})\leq\mathbb{P}(h_{m}\|Y_{m}\|>\epsilon)\to 0.

4
Discussion

Kernel density estimation is an appealing approach in part due to its simplicity, and thus substantial work has gone into understanding whether it can be interpreted in a Bayesian sense, for example
15
. Similar to the analysis of Newton’s algorithm by
7
, we can loosely interpret the random limiting measures
P
and
\tilde{P}
as draws from a prior distribution over measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
associated with the predictive rule.
When
K=N(0,1)
and
h_{n}=Cn^{-1/5}
, the support of

V

This holds even when using a heavier-tailed kernel. For example, Theorem

t_{7}

Appendix 1

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
h_{n}\leq Cn^{-\delta}
and
W\sim K
has
\mathbb{E}[\|W\|]<\infty
. There exists
N
such that for all
n\geq N
,

\displaystyle\prod_{k=n}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{\phi_{K}(h_{k}t)}{k+1}+\frac{k}{k+1}\bigg)

\displaystyle\neq 0.

(3)

Proof.

Fix
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Taylor expanding
\phi_{K}
, we get
\phi_{K}(h_{k}t)=1+ih_{k}t^{T}\mathbb{E}[W]+R_{k}
, where
|R_{k}|\leq 2\big(h_{k}\|t\|\big)\mathbb{E}[\|W\|]
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\bigg|\frac{\phi_{K}(h_{k}t)}{k+1}+\frac{k}{k+1}-1\bigg|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k+1}\Big|ih_{k}t^{T}\mathbb{E}[W]+R_{k}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{h_{k}\|t\|}{k+1}\Big(\|\mathbb{E}[W]\|+2\mathbb{E}[\|W\|]\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}C_{2}k^{-(1+\delta)}<\infty.

Using the continuity of
\phi_{K}
, for
k\geq n\geq N
we have
\phi_{K}(h_{k+1}t)\neq-1
, and the triangle inequality gives us
\frac{\phi_{K}(h_{k+1}t)}{k+1}+\frac{k}{k+1}\neq 0
, so the convergence of the above sum implies the convergence of (
3
) to a nonzero value.
∎
