Rapid construction and analysis of cascading events for disaster investigation and assessment
Authors: Zhikun Zhao, Yueqin Zhu, Jian Li, Wenlong Han, Wei Hua, Yingfei Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Between the occurrence of a disaster and the initiation of formal investigation and assessment, the overall evolution of the disaster often remain unclear, which hinders the timely progress of investigative work. To address this challenge, this study proposes a framework for constructing disaster event chains from limited post-disaster information. The framework first defines event patterns based on disaster system theory, then employs the unified information extraction model to automatically extract event chain nodes and their relationships from news texts. DBSCAN clustering is further applied to achieve semantic normalization and entity standardization, enabling the reconstruction of the disaster event chain. Finally, a fuzzy comprehensive evaluation is conducted to assess the effectiveness of the proposed framework. The results demonstrate that the framework can provide valuable initial analytical insights and auxiliary support for disaster investigation efforts.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-59180-9.

Introduction

In recent years, global climate change has led to an increased frequency of extreme weather events, significantly enhancing the complexity and coupling characteristics of disaster systems1. Disaster events seldom occur in isolation; rather, they typically evolve in the form of cascading events wherein one disaster triggers one or more secondary or derivative disasters, forming complex cascade effects2. This chain-like characteristic not only amplifies disaster losses but also poses severe challenges to post-disaster investigation and assessment efforts. Consequently, how to extract lessons from complex disaster processes and systematically optimize disaster prevention and mitigation work has become a critical issue in the field of public safety3,4.

Disaster investigations requires reconstructing the sequence of disaster events, conducting detailed investigations and analyses of key incidents, identifying the causes, and drawing lessons from the incident. From the practice of the "7·20" extreme rainstorm disaster investigation in Zhengzhou to the successive promulgation of the Interim Measures for Natural Disaster Investigation and Assessment in 2025, these developments signify that China’s natural disaster investigation work has entered a phase of institutionalization and normalization5. The new measures explicitly state that the primary task of disaster investigation is to precisely reconstruct the disaster process to ascertain causes, identify hidden dangers, and evaluate response effectiveness. However, in the early stages of disaster investigation, investigators face the constraints of limited time and data. Therefore, how to utilize limited, publicly available data to rapidly and precisely reconstruct the disaster evolution pathway in the early stages of disaster investigation is a pressing problem to be solved.

To address this challenge, various approaches have been explored. Traditional analysis of cascading events has primarily focused on macro level modeling and simulation for specific scenarios. For instance, methods such as Bayesian networks6 or system dynamics7 have been used to simulate the cascade impacts of disasters like droughts and typhoons, or energy transfer theory8 has been applied to conduct accident evolution analysis for specific facilities (e.g., underground utility corridors). However, these studies rely heavily on a priori knowledge and structured parameters. These approaches are complex and difficult to adapt to the requirements of rapid response and process reconstruction in the early post-disaster stage.

In the immediate aftermath of a disaster, news media represent one of the most efficient and reliable public data sources for acquiring disaster information. Compared to social media, news reports possess higher accuracy in event depiction9 and contain rich information regarding disaster causes, evolutionary processes, and consequences10. This provides a high value data foundation for rapidly reconstructing the cascading events. Some studies have attempted to utilize text mining techniques to extract disaster information from news texts. Sakahira and Hiroi constructed a cascade disaster network for earthquakes by mining information from newspaper articles11; Pita Costa et al. also validated that news data can effectively capture the dynamics of extreme events such as floods12. Although these works confirm the feasibility of using news data, they often rely on traditional Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques or substantial manual intervention. They exhibit deficiencies in the automation, precision, and generalization capabilities required for complex text information extraction, thus failing to achieve the intelligent construction of cascading events.

In summary, existing research primarily focuses on the theoretical analysis of cascading events evolution or high fidelity simulations of localized scenarios. Against the backdrop of institutionalized and normalized disaster investigation and assessment, there is an urgent need for a method capable of rapidly, automatically, and precisely constructing disaster evolution networks from public news data. To address these gaps, the present study focuses on rainstorm-flood disasters, proposing a news-text-driven intelligent framework for cascading events construction and analysis. The primary objectives of this study include: 1. Defining the entity and relation types for rainstorm-flood disaster events based on disaster system theory to construct a schema layer; 2. Applying a Universal Information Extraction (UIE) model to automatically construct a disaster evolution network from unstructured news texts; 3. Applying DBSCAN to achieve semantic alignment, thereby constructing a directed network of cascading events tailored to the specific disaster scenario. Finally, an indicator system is established and combined with fuzzy comprehensive evaluation (FCE) to assess the effectiveness of the proposed framework in disaster‑investigation applications.

Study area and data process

Study area

The core research object of this study is the rainstorm-flood disaster. Given that the evolutionary pathways and key nodes of cascading events are profoundly constrained by regional geographical conditions and socio economic patterns, this study selects two scenarios with distinct characteristics for systematic comparison: the Plains-Urban type and the Mountainous-Suburban type.

Scenario 1: plains-urban type (Zhengzhou, Henan "7·20" disaster)

Zhengzhou City is situated in the southern part of the North China Plain, characterized by an overall low-lying topography. Its core built up area constitutes a megalopolis with relatively simple terrain, yet it features a high concentration of population and economic elements. The critical infrastructure in this area—such as subway systems, underground tunnels, and high-rise buildings—is dense and deeply coupled. From July 17 to 23, 2021, Zhengzhou experienced a historically rare extreme rainstorm. The resultant severe flooding caused major casualties and property damage, serving as a typical representative of the plains-urban cascading events.

Scenario 2: mountainous-suburban type (Miyun, Beijing "25·7" disaster)

Miyun District, Beijing, is located in the Yanshan Mountains. Its topography is dominated by mountains and hills, featuring complex geological conditions and representing a typical mountainous terrain. Compared to the core area of Zhengzhou, its population and economic elements are relatively dispersed, exhibiting a lower degree of coupling. In July 2025, Miyun District was struck by an extreme rainstorm that rapidly triggered a series of secondary disasters, including floods, landslides, and debris flows. Multiple villages were affected, resulting in major casualties and property damage. This event is a representative case of a mountainous-suburban cascading events.

Data processing

To ensure the authoritativeness and factual accuracy of disaster event descriptions, this study selected China News Service (https://www.chinanews.com.cn) as the sole data source. A thematic web crawler was employed, using core keywords such as "Zhengzhou 7·20 Rainstorm" and "Miyun 25·7 Rainstorm".

The data collection period was constrained to the interval between the occurrence of the initial triggering event and the formal commencement of the disaster investigation work. For Scenario 1, the initial triggering event occurred on 17 July 2021, and the official disaster investigation team was established on 2 August 2021. Accordingly, the temporal scope of Scenario 1 was defined as 17 July to 2 August 2021. For Scenario 2, the rainstorm took place on 27 July 2025. Although no explicit establishment date of an investigation team is available, publicly accessible information suggests that the investigation work commenced in early August. Therefore, the temporal window for Scenario 2 was set from 27 July to 1 August 2025.

To prevent information leakage during model training and to ensure the independence between the training and test sets, this study implemented a hierarchical cleaning and semantic deduplication workflow for the raw corpus. First, duplicate news articles were removed at the article level by eliminating reports with identical titles or fully repeated content. Second, article‑level semantic similarity was computed using text embeddings, and highly similar articles were filtered out. Third, the remaining articles were segmented into paragraphs, and paragraph semantic similarity was calculated again to remove duplicate or near duplicate segments both within and across articles, resulting in text units that balance semantic completeness and appropriate length.

A total of 173 valid news articles for Scenario 1 and 122 for Scenario 2 were collected through web crawling. After multi‑stage cleaning and semantic deduplication, paragraph‑level text segments were used as the basic annotation units for information extraction. Following the cascading event schema defined in "Cascading events construction based on disaster system theory" section and the annotation guidelines provided in Appendix A, entity and relation annotations were performed on the Doccano platform.

To ensure annotation quality, a multi‑annotator workflow with consistency checking was adopted. Two experts in emergency management, independently completed the initial annotation, and inter‑annotator agreement was assessed using Cohen’s Kappa coefficient. The resulting Kappa score of 0.845 indicates a high level of agreement. Samples with annotation discrepancies were further reviewed and corrected through a second round of adjudication to ensure accuracy and reliability.

In total, 884 entity annotations and 268 relation annotations were obtained. To ensure rigorous model evaluation, the annotated paragraphs were randomly divided into training, validation, and test sets in an 8:1:1 ratio.

Methodology

The objective of this section is to develop a comprehensive research methodology for extracting cascading events from textual information and reconstructing disaster evolution, thereby providing a general framework for subsequent, detailed scientific disaster investigation. First, based on disaster system theory, the entities and relations of the cascading events are defined to clarify the extraction targets. Subsequently, a Universal Information Extraction (UIE) model is employed to identify and extract the key elements and relationships of the cascading events from unstructured news text corpora. Then, DBSCAN is applied to achieve semantic alignment, thereby constructing a directed network of cascading events tailored to the specific disaster scenario. Finally, an indicator system is established and combined with fuzzy comprehensive evaluation (FCE) to assess the effectiveness of the proposed framework in disaster‑investigation applications.

Cascading events construction based on disaster system theory

To intelligently extract event chains from unstructured news texts, it is first necessary to define a clear and standardized schema layer. This layer specifies the key elements (entities) and the relationships between these elements that the model must identify from the text. This study selects Disaster System Theory as its theoretical core, as it provides a mature analytical framework for structurally complex disasters.

The foundational concepts of Disaster System Theory can be traced to the research on floods by geographer Gilbert F. White13, which later evolved into a comprehensive theoretical system14. The central tenet of this theory redefines a disaster not as a mere natural phenomenon, but as the result of the coupling and interaction of three core systems: (1) The Hazard System, referring to natural events that have the potential to cause harm to human society; (2) The Exposure System, which includes people, property, and infrastructure exposed to the threat of hazards and susceptible to damage due to their own vulnerability; and (3) The Disaster-Inducing Environment System, which comprises the background conditions in a specific time and space that modulate the interaction between hazards and exposure systems. This includes the natural environment (e.g., topography, hydrology), which can amplify or mitigate the disaster’s impact15.

The construction of the cascading events based on Disaster System Theory primarily involves two main steps: node category mapping and relationship type definition. This entails mapping the nodes within the cascading event network to one of the three constituent systems based on their semantic meaning and defining the relationship types between nodes to represent the nature and direction of their interaction. Given that this study focuses on rainstorm-flood disaster event chains, anthropogenic factors are excluded from the Hazard System. Entity types for this system are selected from the Classification and Code of Natural Disasters (GB/T 28921-2012). Entity types for the Exposure System are selected from the Classification and Code of Natural Disaster Exposure (GB/T 32572-2016). The Disaster-Inducing Environment System primarily considers two major categories: the natural environment and the human environment. Entity and relation extraction based on UIE

The UIE framework is an architecture jointly developed by the Institute of Software, Chinese Academy of Sciences, and Baidu Inc. It is designed for text-to-structure generation. The core idea of this architecture is to unify different tasks, such as entity extraction, relation extraction, and event extraction, by modeling them as a single text-to-structure sequence generation problem. The comprehensive framework of the UIE model, depicted in Fig. 3, contains input layers, encoding layers, and output layers.Fig. 3UIE extraction model.

UIE extraction model.

The input layer of the UIE model processes raw, unstructured text by appending prompts to each input sample. These prompts.

are designed to facilitate the extraction of relevant information by providing context and structure. This study employs an automated prompt generation method on predefined schemas16. For example, in relation extraction tasks, the schema is defined as “schema = [entity-type, [entity-type:[relation-type]]”, where “entity-type” represents entities, and “relation-type” denotes 4 types of relationships. This schema allows the model to generate prompts automatically, such as “Heavy rainfall triggers”, “Typhoon triggers”, etc.

The output layer decodes the hidden semantic representations to extract and structure the relevant entities and their relationships using multi-layer label pointer networks. These networks, comprising fully connected layers, predict the start and end positions of entities within the text. The model is trained using the Binary Cross-Entropy Loss (BCELoss) function, which optimizes the accuracy of these predictions by minimizing the loss between the predicted and true positions.

This prompt-based unified modeling paradigm endows UIE with high flexibility and robust zero-shot and few-shot learning capabilities. This allows it to be conveniently applied to domain-specific extraction tasks without the need to design complex model structures. Its application has also been validated in the disaster domain17.

Semantic alignment based on DBSCAN

In the entity extraction results, a single entity category often contains multiple semantically distinct event types. For example, the category “infrastructure” may refer to water supply systems, power systems, or transportation systems. Without further normalization, such semantic heterogeneity would hinder the accurate construction of disaster event chains. To address this issue, this study performs semantic alignment using the DBSCAN(Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise) , which is a density-based clustering algorithm capable of discovering clusters of arbitrary shapes and automatically filtering noise18, thereby enabling the unification and normalization of event expressions by quantifying semantic distances between entities.

First, the BGE-large-zh model is employed to generate vector representations of entity texts. The model maps discrete text into a 1024-dimensional dense semantic space and applies L2 normalization to project vectors onto a unit hypersphere, allowing semantic similarity to be directly measured using cosine distance and ensuring comparability across texts of varying lengths19.

The performance of DBSCAN primarily depends on two parameters: the minimum number of samples (minPts) and the neighborhood radius (ε). In this study, minPts is set to 3 to ensure basic cluster stability, while ε is determined adaptively using the K-distance graph combined with knee detection. Evaluation of event chain construction results based on FCE

Disaster investigation is a highly specialized and practice‑oriented task, and the effectiveness of any analytical framework often depends on the judgment and experience of investigators. Therefore, to assess the applicability and reliability of the proposed disaster event‑chain construction method in real investigation scenarios, this study develops a subjective evaluation system and employs the FCE method for quantitative validation.

FCE is a comprehensive evaluation approach based on fuzzy mathematics. By using membership functions, it converts qualitative expert judgments into quantitative scores and has been widely applied in risk assessment20 and system reliability analysis21. Incorporating FCE enables the integration of multiple investigators’ subjective assessments, thereby providing a holistic reflection of the method’s performance in practical applications.

Results and discussion

Model performance evaluation

In this study, the experimental environment was configured using the PaddlePaddle distributed deep learning framework (version 3.0.0‑beta) together with PaddleNLP 3.0.0‑b2. The “uie‑base” pre-trained model was fine‑tuned for the extraction tasks. The training was conducted on a laptop equipped with a GeForce RTX 4060. After multiple rounds of experimentation, the final hyperparameters were set as follows: a learning rate of 1 × 10–5, batch size of 16, 20 training epochs, and a random seed of 42. The training session lasts about 30 min. Model performance was evaluated in terms of precision, recall, and F1‑score. To further validate the effectiveness of the proposed UIE based entity and relation extraction framework, comparative experiments were conducted using baseline models built on BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) and RoBERTa (Robustly Optimized BERT Approach). BERT is a bidirectional pre-trained language model based on the Transformer architecture, whereas RoBERTa is an enhanced variant of BERT that incorporates dynamic masking, larger‑scale corpora, and longer training sequences to improve extraction performance. Both baseline models adopt the same architectural design: for the entity recognition task, a token‑classification structure is used, where a linear classification head is applied to the output of the multi‑layer Transformer encoder to perform BIO (Begin, Inside, Outside) sequence labeling, where tokens are classified to indicate the boundary and type of each named entity; for the relation extraction task, a sequence classification structure is employed, in which the identified entity pair is encoded together with the sentence, and the [CLS] (Classification Token) representation, which serves as the aggregated semantic embedding of the entire sequence, is passed through a sentence‑level classification head to determine the relation type.

To ensure the fairness of the comparison, the UIE model and all baseline models were trained and evaluated using the same dataset, identical hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, number of epochs, and random seed), and the same evaluation metrics. The average performance of each model is reported in Table 4. The results show that UIE outperforms both BERT and RoBERTa across all three evaluation metrics. This advantage can be attributed to the fact that UIE is built upon the ERNIE 3.0 pre-trained model with hundreds of billions of parameters, trained jointly on large‑scale textual corpora and knowledge‑graph data. Events chain construction and visualization

Taking infrastructure‑related entities as an example, the extracted entities from the two scenarios were clustered using the DBSCAN algorithm, and their high‑dimensional semantic embeddings were visualized through t‑SNE dimensionality reduction. The visualization results are presented in Fig. 4.Fig. 4DBSCAN clustering results of infrastructure‑related entities: (a) Scenario 1; (b) Scenario 2.

DBSCAN clustering results of infrastructure‑related entities: (a) Scenario 1; (b) Scenario 2.

Based on the formula 7 , the DBI values corresponding to Fig. 4a, b are 1.03 and 0.64, respectively. As shown in Fig. 4, a lower DBI value indicates higher intra‑cluster compactness and greater inter‑cluster separation, meaning that entities form well‑defined clusters in the semantic vector space. This demonstrates that the DBSCAN algorithm can effectively identify semantically coherent entity groups, thereby providing a more reliable foundation for subsequent entity normalization and event‑chain construction.

After completing the clustering analysis of the remaining entities, the normalized entities and their relationships were further used to construct disaster‑event chain networks for the two scenarios. The entities and their relational links were visualized using Gephi, resulting in the complete disaster‑event networks shown in Figs. 5 and 6. In these networks, nodes are categorized according to their roles within the disaster chain—such as hazards, exposures, and disaster‑inducing environments—with different colors representing different categories. The edges correspond to various types of relationships, including “trigger,” “modulation,” “impact,” and “cascade.”Fig. 5Disaster event network of the Scenario 1.Fig. 6Disaster event network of the Scenario 2.

The process of constructing a disaster event chain—from data acquisition to final visualization—is fully automated via scripts, requiring very little time. When similar incidents occur, invoking the pre-trained UIE model allows for visualization and analysis to be completed within hours, providing guidance for subsequent disaster investigation efforts.

A comparison between Fig. 5 (Scenario 1) and the official investigation report shows that urban inundation serves as the core hazard source, triggering a series of cascading failures such as subway flooding, traffic paralysis, and communication system outages, ultimately leading to people being trapped and casualties. These key events are highly consistent with the conclusions of the official investigation.

Figure 6 (Scenario 2) presents a more complex disaster chain: intense rainfall induces multiple geological hazards, including landslides, slope failures, and debris flows, which subsequently cause bridge collapses, road damage, and water‑supply system failures. These cascading effects eventually result in traffic disruption, population stranding, and casualties, highlighting the significant influence of complex terrain conditions on disaster evolution in Scenario 2. All these events are documented in the official investigation report of the Miyun rainstorm disaster.

The qualitative analysis demonstrates that the results generated by the proposed framework align closely with the findings of official investigation reports, indicating that the framework can provide meaningful preliminary guidance for disaster‑investigation efforts.

Conclusion

Between the occurrence of a disaster and the initiation of formal investigation and assessment, information is often highly fragmented, incomplete, and lacking clarity regarding the disaster evolution process and its critical links. These information limitations significantly hinder the progress of disaster investigation. To address this issue, this study proposes a news driven framework for reconstructing and analyzing cascading events, using rainstorm induced disaster chains as the research object.

The main findings of this study are as follows:A disaster‑event chain construction method is proposed, which extracts event nodes and relationships from news texts using the UIE model, performs semantic normalization and entity standardization through DBSCAN clustering, and visualizes the reconstructed event chain using Gephi;Two representative cases—the Zhengzhou “7·20” rainstorm‑induced flooding and the Beijing “7·25” rainstorm event—were analyzed, and the corresponding disaster‑event chains were successfully reconstructed, revealing key evolutionary pathways and cascading patterns;FCE was conducted to assess the applicability of the proposed framework in disaster investigation and assessment. The results indicate that the method provides preliminary guidance for data collection and evidence gathering in subsequent formal investigation stages.
