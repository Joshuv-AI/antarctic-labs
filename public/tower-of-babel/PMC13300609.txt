Superselective Craniofacial Nerve Decompression in a Pediatric Patient: A Case Report
Authors: Ruben Sanchez Eligio, Antonio Torres, Julia R. Fedorova, Angelo A. Leto Barone
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Summary:Peripheral nerve release has emerged as a viable treatment option for pharmacologically refractory migraines secondary to craniofacial compression neuropathies. Although occipital nerve decompression is well described, management of supraorbital and zygomaticotemporal nerve compression is less characterized despite its relevance in patients with multi-nerve compression. Conventional techniques rely on long, blepharoplasty-like incisions to address both supraorbital and temporal compression sites. However, extensive areas of dissection may increase complication rates and reduce aesthetic outcomes. We present a pediatric case in which a super-selective supraorbital and temporal nerve release was implemented as part of a staged surgical approach. The technique was applied in a 15-year-old girl with posttraumatic chronic occipital neuralgia refractory to medical management. Following conventional occipital nerve decompression, additional trigger points became apparent, warranting a minimally invasive supraorbital nerve decompression. Targeted release of the bilateral supraorbital nerve was performed using 1 cm incisions along the inferior border of the brow. Immediate and sustained resolution of frontal migraine was achieved, without sensorimotor dysfunction and with minimal scar burden. Residual temporal pain was later addressed with decompression of the zygomaticotemporal nerve, utilizing bilateral pretrichial 1 cm incisions, preserving muscle integrity and limiting iatrogenic facial nerve injury. One year postoperatively, the patient demonstrated complete resolution of occipital and frontal symptoms with marked improvement in temporal migraines. This case illustrates the application of a clinically guided, staged surgical approach for frontotemporal compression neuropathies in a pediatric patient, offering targeted neural release, concealed incisions, and minimal risk of iatrogenic nerve injury.

Takeaways

Question: Can a superselective supraorbital and temporal nerve release be used as part of a staged surgical approach in a pediatric patient?

Findings: This Case Report describes a staged, minimally invasive approach using superselective supraorbital and zygomaticotemporal nerve release in a pediatric patient with refractory frontotemporal trigger site symptoms.

Meaning: This case illustrates a targeted peripheral nerve decompression strategy that may offer a safe and effective surgical option for refractory frontotemporal migraines.

INTRODUCTION

Peripheral craniofacial nerve compression can trigger migraine attacks at defined trigger sites, including the supraorbital, zygomaticotemporal (ZT), and occipital nerves, with surgical decompression associated with durable symptom relief.1–6 Although occipital nerve decompression is well described, supraorbital and ZT nerve release remains less routinely performed. Conventional techniques employing blepharoplasty-like incisions involve extensive dissection and may increase morbidity and scar burden.1,2 We present a super-selective approach for supraorbital and ZT nerve release in a pediatric patient using 1-2 cm incisions.

CASE REPORT

A 15-year-old girl presented with posttraumatic occipital neuralgia causing daily, debilitating headaches impairing school performance. Following a skull fracture at age 6, headaches developed at age 11 and persisted for 4.5 years. Pain was severe (10 out of 10), lasted several hours daily, originated in the suboccipital region with anterior radiation to the supraorbital and temporal areas, and was described as burning and stabbing.

Examination revealed Tinel sign and tenderness over the bilateral greater occipital nerves, supraorbital notches, and ZT regions. Symptoms were refractory to medical therapy, including sumatriptan injections, topiramate, and gabapentin. Trigger sites were evaluated through preoperative pain mapping utilizing pain drawings and photographs, as described by others.2,3,7 Diagnostic bilateral greater occipital nerve blocks using 10 mL of 1% lidocaine with epinephrine per side provided temporary relief. The patient underwent conventional bilateral occipital nerve decompression using two 4 cm, horizontal paramedian incisions over the areas of maximum tenderness, with complete resolution of occipital symptoms.

After 11 months, she developed persistent frontal headaches localized to the supraorbital region. Diagnostic bilateral supraorbital nerve (SON) blocks confirmed SON compression. Using a superselective approach, 1 cm incisions were placed at the inferior brow border along the midpupillary line at the points of maximal tenderness (Fig. 1). Bilateral SON compression with right-sided osseous foramen entrapment was identified. The SONs were approached directly through this incision and decompressed through circumferential neurolysis. The right supraorbital foramen was converted to a notch to release the nerve (Fig. 2A). Dense perineural fibrosis was released on the left (Fig. 2B). Nerve continuity was preserved bilaterally. Incisions within the brow crease left well-concealed scars (Fig. 3). Frontal pain resolved immediately and remained absent at 13-month follow-up.

Six months later, the patient reported new-onset, persistent bilateral temporal pain localized to the ZT nerve distribution. Diagnostic ZT nerve block confirmed a third trigger site. Bilateral 2 cm pretrichial incisions were placed in the temporal hairline. ZT nerves were exposed using a minimally invasive approach. Blunt dissection was performed in the interfascial plane, and nerve stimulation was used throughout the procedure to avoid the temporal branch of the facial nerve. The right ZT nerve was identified and was found to be compressed by dense fibrotic tissue and tethered beneath an arterial loop. The nerve underwent careful external neurolysis with vascular release. The same procedure was then performed on the left, where severe scarring and poor nerve quality and color prompted decompression with intentional neurectomy, with the proximal stump being implanted into the temporalis muscle. The pretrichial incisions were closed in layers. No complications occurred.

Postoperative care included nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs-based analgesia, topical antibiotic ointment followed by petrolatum, cortical reeducation therapy, silicone gel scar massage, and avoidance of strenuous activity and constrictive headwear. At 25 months following occipital nerve decompression, occipital neuralgia remained resolved. The patient was symptom-free at 13 months after supraorbital decompression and 7 months after ZT nerve release. Following ZT nerve release, the patient reported right parietal burning and a small area of left ZT sensory anesthesia without recurrence of migraine symptoms.

DISCUSSION

Given ongoing controversy surrounding the term “migraine surgery,” this case is presented within the context of craniofacial compression neuropathies with intervention directed at peripheral trigger sites. Gfrerer et al outlined principles emphasizing patient selection, trigger-site mapping, and refined dissection techniques, with similar principles described in subsequent studies.2,7 Growing evidence supports fascial preservation and microdissection to reduce postoperative dysesthesia.3,8

Multiple operative strategies exist for frontal nerve release. Endoscopic corrugator resection addresses muscular compression but requires broad dissection and may cause transient frontalis weakness.1,2,8 The transpalpebral approach provides direct exposure; however, more extensive dissection carries the risk of injury to the musculature and facial nerve branches.1,2

We believe a sizable blepharoplasty-like incision is less favorable, as it may lead to unnecessary scarring of the upper eyelid and increased risk of iatrogenic injury to eyelid musculature. In this case, direct inferior brow incisions permitted targeted neurolysis, and selective notch widening or foraminotomy under direct visualization, preserving nerve continuity while minimizing tissue dissection and yielding inconspicuous scars. Extensive subperiosteal elevation was avoided, reducing postoperative edema, and preserving brow animation and lid position.

Conventionally, supraorbital and ZT trigger sites are addressed simultaneously through a single blepharoplasty-like incision.1,2 We opted for two separate procedures. A pretrichial approach was used to access the ZT nerve, with incision localization guided by established anatomic landmarks.3,9,10 Compared with more extensive temporal dissections, this approach was chosen to limit soft tissue disruption and reduce the risk of injury to the temporal branch of the facial nerve.1–3 Although no aneurysmal branches of the temporal artery were encountered in this case, these can be observed during temporal dissection.10 Consistent with a staged approach, the ZT site was addressed only after resolution of dominant occipital and frontal triggers to isolate residual pain generators while limiting surgical morbidity. As symptoms resolved, previously obscured trigger points became apparent, suggesting a sequential unmasking phenomenon.

Our report has inherent limitations; as a single case, its applicability to broader patient populations remains limited, and anatomic variability may preclude this approach. Long-term durability remains to be established.

CONCLUSIONS

This case describes a superselective decompression of the supraorbital and ZT nerves for refractory migraine symptoms secondary to extracranial nerve entrapment. Focused neurolysis and selective foraminotomy through 1–2 cm incisions minimized soft tissue disruption and scar burden. This staged approach, guided by pain mapping and diagnostic blocks, allowed for the targeted treatment of dominant trigger sites. Although limited to a single case, this approach suggests a potential role for superselective, minimally invasive direct nerve decompression of the supraorbital and zygomaticotemporal nerves.
