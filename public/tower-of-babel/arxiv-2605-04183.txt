Nearly-Tight Bounds for Zonotope Containment and Beyond
Friedrich Eisenbrand, Thomas Rothvoss, Matteo Russo, Ruben Skorupinski

Abstract
We investigate the convex-body containment problem max ⁡ { s > 0 : s ​ Z ⊆ Q } \max\{s>0:sZ\subseteq Q\} , where the outer body Q ⊆ ℝ d Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d} is described by a membership oracle and the inner body Z ⊆ ℝ d Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d} is a zonotope. Our main result is a sampling-based O ⁡ ( d ) O(\sqrt{d}) -approximation algorithm for this problem that almost matches the lower bound of Ω ⁡ ( d / log ⁡ d ) \Omega(\sqrt{\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}}}) by Khot and Naor in the oracle model. Assuming zonotopes can be sparsified by a linear number of generators, which is referred to as Talagrand conjecture , our approach attains the optimal approximation factor of Θ ⁡ ( d / log ⁡ d ) \Theta(\sqrt{\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}}}) . Our second main result is a proof of Talagrand’s conjecture for Δ \Delta -modular zonotopes whenever Δ \Delta is constant. Those zonotopes are of the form Z = { W ​ x : ‖ x ‖ ∞ ≤ 1 } Z=\{Wx\colon\|x\|_{\infty}\leq 1\} where the non-zero d × d d\times d sub-determinants of W W are between 1 1 and Δ \Delta . This result establishes a connection between zonoid sparsification and spectral sparsification of Batson, Spielman and Srivastava. We complement these results with a universal Ω ⁡ ( d / log ⁡ d ) \Omega(\sqrt{\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}}}) lower bound holding for all zonotopes. Finally, we consider containment problems max ⁡ { s > 0 : s ​ K ⊆ Q } \max\{s>0:sK\subseteq Q\} , for general convex bodies K ⊆ ℝ d K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d} . A result of Naszódi on approximating K ⊆ ℝ d K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d} by a polytope implies a Θ ⁡ ( d / log ⁡ d ) \Theta(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}}) approximation algorithm in polynomial time. We show the tightness of this approximation factor in the oracle model via a reduction to the circumradius computation. Our lower bound holds for centrally symmetric convex sets, implying that Barvinok’s optimal O ⁡ ( d ) O(\sqrt{d}) -approximation of a centrally symmetric convex body by a polytope with a polynomial number of vertices cannot be computed in polynomial time.

Introduction

The
containment problem
for convex bodies is as follows.
Given two centered convex bodies
K,Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e., containing the origin in their relative interior, determine by how much
K
can be scaled while still being contained in
Q
.
Formally, it is the following optimization problem:

(K,Q)\textsc{-Opt-Containment}:\max\{\alpha>0\mid\alpha\cdot K\subseteq Q\}.

(1)

If the outer body
Q
is a polytope given in its inequality representation, then
(K,Q)\textsc{-Opt-Containment}
can be solved in polynomial time simply by optimizing all normal vectors of
Q
over
K
.
However, already when both bodies are polytopes, and the inner polytope is
described by inequalities
while the outer one is described by its vertices, this problem is NP-hard
[
25
]
. Here, we focus on approximating the optimal value
\alpha^{\star}
of (
1
) within a factor
s(d)\geq 1
that depends on the dimension
d
. Thus our task is to find
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\alpha\leq\alpha^{\star}\leq s(d)\cdot\alpha
holds.
Via binary search, this approximation problem is polynomial-time equivalent to the following
gap decision
version of the problem:

\displaystyle(K,Q)\textsc{-Gap-Containment}:\begin{cases}\textsc{Yes,}&K\subseteq Q\\
\textsc{No,}&s(d)\cdot K\nsubseteq Q\end{cases}.

(2)

An algorithm
solves
the instance
(K,Q)
of
Gap-Containment
if it correctly asserts whether it is a
Yes
or a
No
instance. It is not required to assert that both conditions hold, if this is the case.

Containment is closely related to classical geometric approximation tasks such as estimating radii, widths, and diameters of convex bodies, and to the general theme of approximating geometric functionals using oracle access, see, e.g.,
[
15
,
56
]
.
We study the containment problem with a particular focus on the case in which the inner body
K=Z
is a zonotope and
Q
is given by a membership oracle. A
zonotope
is a set of the form

Z(W)=\{Wx:x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\|x\|_{\infty}\leq 1\},

(3)

where
W\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
is the
generator matrix
of the zonotope. The columns
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
of
W
are the
generators
of
Z(W)
.
Zonotopes are a well-studied class of convex polytopes with rich structure and connections to functional analysis and convex geometry, see, e.g.
[
13
]
.
The
zonotope containment
problem
Z\subseteq Q
has received considerable attention in the recent literature of fields such as control theory, neural networks or complexity theory
[
1
,
39
,
41
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
57
,
8
,
9
,
34
]
.

1.1
Results of This Paper

We present approximation and hardness results for the
zonotope as well as for the general containment problem. In passing, we conclude optimality and impossibility of two well known schemes to approximate a convex body by a polytope respectively and we show that
\Delta
-modular zonotopes have linear-size sparsifiers whenever
\Delta
is a constant. The
computational model
that we use is the classical oracle model for accessing convex bodies, i.e., closed convex sets with non-empty interior
[
32
]
. In particular, we assume the bodies are centered.
Moreover, since we need to sample from the inner body
K
efficiently, we also assume
K
is well-rounded, i.e.,
r\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}\subseteq K\subseteq R\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}
, where
0<r\leq R
(with
R\in\textup{poly}(d)\cdot r
), and
\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}
is the
d
-dimensional
\ell_{2}
-ball. Our algorithms operate in the
membership
oracle which answers
Yes
or
No
to the query “
x\in K
” for a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Zonotope Containment.

We provide a randomized polynomial-time algorithm that decides the
Gap-Containment
problem for
(Z,Q)
with a factor of
s(d)=O(\sqrt{d})
, where
Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a zonotope, (
Theorem

2.5
).
The algorithm is based on sampling. If
Z
is generated by the matrix
W\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
and if
Z\nsubseteq Q
holds, we show that the probability of the event

2\sqrt{\frac{n}{\log n}}\cdot Wy\not\in Q,

is at least
\nicefrac{{1}}{{\textup{poly}(n)}}
if
y\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
is uniformly chosen at random.
This, together with a fundamental result of
[
58
]
and its algorithmic version by
[
17
]
concerning
zonotope sparsification
show the result.
If zonotopes can be sparsified up to constant factors using only
\Theta(d)
many generators (see Talagrand conjecture:
2.3
), then our technique yields
\Theta(\sqrt{\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}}})
-approximation for the zonotope containment problem. This is would be optimal as
[
37
]
provided a corresponding lower bound that holds for the special case in which

is the hypercube.

\Delta
-Modular Zonotopes.

A zonotope

is

-modular

W\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}

, see, e.g.
[

[

,
43
,
49
,
24
]
. Whether integer programming can be solved in polynomial time if
\Delta
is a

is a prominent mystery.
Our main result in this context is a proof of the
Talagrand conjecture
, if
\Delta
is a constant.
This result is proved by establishing a connection between the spectral sparsification of
[
7
]
and the facet structure of
\Delta
-modular zonotopes and it implies an optimal
O(\Delta\sqrt{\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}}})

Universal Hardness for Zonotopes.

[
37
]
have shown a lower bound of
s(d)=\Omega(\sqrt{\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}}})
for a randomized polynomial-time algorithm that decides
Gap-Containment
, where the inner body is the hypercube.
We show a

lower bound of
s(d)=\Omega(\sqrt{\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}}})
for the

problem, where the inner body is

zonotope. To achieve this result, we exploit the structural and volumetric properties of

General Convex Bodies and Polytope Approximation.

[
48
]
provided a sampling based algorithm to approximate any convex body
K
with a polytope
P
spanned by a polynomial number of vertices, such that, if
P
is scaled by
O(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}})
, then it contains

(see also
[
47
]
). One can further note that, for
s(d)=O(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}})
,

is a

-instance if all vertices of

-instance. Since sampling from a convex body can be done in polynomial time
[

,

,
19
]
, then for

A recent result by
[

]
shows that this

that holds even for

For symmetric convex bodies

,

6

showed that there exists a polytope

Matrix Norms.

Given two convex symmetric bodies
K
and
Q
, the
(K,Q)\textsc{-Opt-Containment}
problem can be interpreted via a polar view: given two norms
\|\cdot\|_{K}
and
\|\cdot\|_{Q}

K,Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we seek to find the minimal scalar
s>0
so that
\|x\|_{Q}\leq s\|x\|_{K}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\forall x\in K:~\|x\|_{Q}\leq s\|x\|_{K}\iff\forall x\in K:~\|x\|_{Q}\leq\|x\|_{K/s}\iff\frac{1}{s}K\subseteq Q.

Given the connection between approximating norms and the containment problem, we highlight that, when
K=A\mathbb{B}^{n}_{q}
and
Q=\mathbb{B}^{d}_{p}

\max\{s>0:sK\subseteq Q\}=\nicefrac{{1}}{{\|A\|_{q\to p}}}
, where the

norm of a matrix

is defined by

\|A\|_{q\to p}=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|Ax\|_{p}}{\|x\|_{q}}.

Hence, approximating
(K,Q)\textsc{-Opt-Containment}
for the specific bodies
K=A\mathbb{B}^{n}_{q}
and
Q=\mathbb{B}^{d}_{p}
is equivalent to approximating the
q\to p
norm of the matrix
A
.
It is a well-known fact that, for all
p\geq 2
,
\ell_{p}
balls
\mathbb{B}^{d}_{p}
are
zonoids

[
10

[
13
]
.

Containment of a Zonotope in a Convex Body

In this section we prove our first main result on the containment problem where the inner body
Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a zonotope. We show that we can efficiently identify a point in
\Theta(\sqrt{d}\cdot Z)
that is not contained in the outer body
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
in the case where
Z
itself is not contained in
Q
already. We do this by first looking at the special case where
Z
is the hypercube, then generalize to zonotopes.

2.1
Hypercube Containment

[
37
]
have provided tight bounds on the containment problem for the special case of the hypercube
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{d}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\|x\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}
. The authors show that one cannot identify a point in
o(\sqrt{\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}}}\cdot Z)\setminus Q
with a (randomized) polynomial number of queries to the membership oracle, describing
Q
. The authors also present a matching upper bound in that setting. Their result is phrased in the
dual setting
of approximating the
\ell_{1}
-diameter of the
polar

Q^{\circ}
of
Q

Central to the result of
[
37
]
is the following lemma related to anti-concentration of measure in the hypercube. Recall that a hyperplane
H=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon a^{\top}x=\beta\}
is supporting for
Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
if
\beta=\max\{a^{\top}x\colon x\in Z\}
. In particular, for the hypercube that is
\beta=\|a\|_{1}
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
37
]
)
.

Let
H=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon a^{\top}x=\|a\|_{1}\}
be a supporting hyperplane of the
d
-dimensional cube
[-1,1]^{d}
. There exists a constant
C\in\mathbb{N}
such that, at least
(\nicefrac{{1}}{{d^{C}}})\cdot 2^{d}
vertices of
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{d}=[-1,1]^{d}
satisfy the inequality

a^{\top}x\geq\|a\|_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}.

In our setting the lemma can then be used as follows. Suppose that
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{d}\nsubseteq Q
. The goal is to identify a point in the set

\sqrt{\frac{d}{\log d}}\cdot\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{d}\setminus Q.

Since
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{d}\nsubseteq Q
, there exists a supporting hyperplane
H=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon a^{\top}x=\beta\}
of
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{d}
such that
a^{\top}x\leq\beta
is valid for
Q
. Sample a vertex
x\in\{-1,1\}^{d}
of
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{d}
uniformly at random. The probability that

\sqrt{\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}}}\cdot x\not\in Q
is at least the probability of
\sqrt{\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}}}\cdot x
lying
above

H
. This is at least
\nicefrac{{1}}{{d^{C}}}
. Thus, if
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{d}\nsubseteq Q

\sqrt{\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}}}\cdot\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{d}\setminus Q
.

Our algorithm is structured as follows: in the first step of the algorithm we apply Talagrand’s sparsification to approximate the zonotope

n=O(d\log d)

H=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\mathbf{1}^{\top}x=\|\mathbf{1}\|_{1}\}
. Using Hoeffding’s inequality we can find an upper bound on the probability that a point sampled from the
n
-dimensional hypercube is mapped above the scaled hyperplane:

\displaystyle\underset{x\sim\{-1,1\}^{n}}{\mathbb{P}}\left[\left\langle Wx,\mathbf{1}\right\rangle\geq\frac{d}{s}\right]=\underset{}{\mathbb{P}}\left[x^{\top}W^{\top}\mathbf{1}\geq\frac{d}{s}\right]=\underset{}{\mathbb{P}}\left[\frac{d}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\geq\frac{d}{s}\right]\leq{\textup{exp}}\left(-\frac{n}{2s^{2}}\right).

Hence, for every scaling factor
s=o(\sqrt{\nicefrac{{n}}{{\log n}}})
this probability is exponentially small. This means that, unless the number of generators
n
is sparsified to
O(d)
, this algorithm yields suboptimal bounds even for the
d
-dimensional hypercube with artificially split generators.

3.1
Linear-size Sparsification

Our main result of this section is a proof of the Talagrand conjecture (

\Delta
-modular
zonotopes, in the case where

\Delta
is a constant. Note that the lower bound deduced in

2.1
from

still holds in this setting since the hypercube

The theorem below is stated in terms of the support function of a zonotope

along direction

Theorem 3.1

(Sparsification of
\Delta
-modular matrices)
.

Let

be a
\Delta
-modular matrix.
For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)

, such that for all

Moreover, the columns of

are a subset of positively scaled columns of

and

can be found in polynomial time.

.

Let
W\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
be a
\Delta

Z
generated by
W
. Then, with high probability,
Algorithm

1
solves
(Z,Q)
-Gap-Containment

\displaystyle s\in O\left(\Delta^{2}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{d}{\log d}}\right).

Before proving the sparsification result in

Proof of

.

Recall from the proof of

\displaystyle\underset{}{\mathbb{P}}\left[\sqrt{\frac{n}{\log n}}\cdot Wy\notin Q\right]=\underset{}{\mathbb{P}}\left[\sqrt{\frac{n}{\log n}}\cdot y\notin f_{W}^{-1}(Q)\right]\geq\frac{1}{\textup{poly}(n)}.

Remark
.

3.2
Proof of
Theorem

3.1

In the remainder of this section we thus need to establish
Theorem

3.1
. The proof relies on a well-known result in spectral sparsification of matrices:

Theorem 3.3

(Spectral sparsification
[
7
]
)
.

Let
B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
be an arbitrary matrix with
n\geq d
and suppose
0<\varepsilon<1
is given. Then, one can find a nonnegative diagonal matrix
D=\mathrm{diag}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
with at most
O(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\varepsilon^{2}}})
nonzero entries for which, with high probability,

(1-\varepsilon)^{2}BB^{\top}\preceq BDB^{\top}\preceq(1+\varepsilon)^{2}BB^{\top}.

Moreover, the diagonal matrix
D
can be found in polynomial time.
2
2

2

As it is customary the symbol
\preceq
denotes the Löwner order, i.e., for every pair of symmetric matrices
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,
A\preceq B
means that
B-A
is a positive semidefinite matrix.

The main ingredient to prove
Theorem

3.1
is
Lemma

3.4
, which characterizes the inner product between a facet-defining unit vector and a generator of a
\Delta
-modular zonotope:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
W\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
be a
\Delta
-modular matrix. Moreover, let
w_{1},\ldots,w_{d-1}
be linearly independent
columns of
W
, and let
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|v\|_{2}=1\}
satisfy
u^{\top}w_{i}=0
for all
i\leq d-1
.
Then, there exist
\alpha,\beta>0
, depending only on
u
and
w_{1},\ldots,w_{d-1}
and satisfying
\nicefrac{{\beta}}{{\alpha}}\leq\Delta
, such that for every
column
w
of
W
,

|u^{\top}w|\in\{0\}\cup[\alpha,\beta].

Proof.

Let us set
\tilde{U}=[w_{1},\dots,w_{d-1},u]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
.
Since
w_{1},\dots,w_{d-1}
are linearly independent and
u\perp\mathrm{span}\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{d-1}\}
with
u\neq 0
,
the columns of
\tilde{U}
are independent, hence
\det(\tilde U)\neq 0
.

Let us now fix any column
w
of
W
and write
w=\tilde{U}x
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Taking the inner product with
u
and using
u^{\top}w_{i}=0
gives

u^{\top}w=u^{\top}(\tilde{U}z)=x_{d}u^{\top}u=x_{d}\|u\|_{2}^{2}=x_{d},

so
u^{\top}w=0
if and only if
x_{d}=0
. Otherwise, by Cramer’s rule,

x_{d}=\frac{\det([w_1, \cdots, w_{d-1}, w])}{\det([w_1, \cdots, w_{d-1}, u])}\Longrightarrow\frac{1}{\left|\det(\tilde U)\right|}\leq|x_{d}|\leq\frac{{\Delta}}{\left|\det(\tilde U)\right|}.

The implication above follows since the numerator is the determinant of a
d\times d
submatrix of
W
, which, by assumption, is bounded by
1
from below and
{\Delta}
from above. Therefore, defining
\alpha=\nicefrac{{1}}{{|\det(\tilde U)|}}
and
\beta=\nicefrac{{{\Delta}}}{{|\det(\tilde U)|}}
, we obtain that
|u^{\top}w|\in[\alpha,\beta]
, as claimed.
∎

Furthermore, we recall the following result on the facet structure of zonotopes:

Lemma 3.5

(Section 7.3 in
[
59
]
)
.

Let
Z(W)
be the zonotope generated by
W=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, assuming
\rank(W)=d
. We have that:

i)

For any direction
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, we have that the face
F_{Z}(u)
is the translation of a lower-dimensional zonotope, that is
F_{Z}(u)=v(u)+\sum_{i:w_{i}^{\top}u=0}[-w_{i},w_{i}]
, where
v(u)=\sum_{w_{i}^{\top}u\neq 0}\mathrm{sign}\left(w_{i}^{\top}u\right)\cdot w_{i}
. Consequently,
\dim(F_{Z}(u))=\rank(\{w_i\}_{i: w_i^\top u = 0})
, and, in particular,
u
defines a facet of
Z
if and only if
\rank(\{w_i\}_{i: w_i^\top u = 0})=d-1
.

ii)

Consider
W^{\prime}
obtained from
W
by deleting some columns and rescaling remaining columns by nonzero scalars. Then, every facet normal vector of
Z(W^{\prime})
is also a facet normal vector of
Z(W)
.

With the above results, we now prove
Theorem

3.1
:

Proof of
Theorem

3.1
.

Let
D=\mathrm{diag}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
be as in
Theorem

3.3
, let

I=\{i:c_{i}>0\}
so that
|I|\leq O(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\varepsilon^{2}}})
, and let
W^{\prime}=(c_{i}w_{i})_{i\in I}
. We stress that
W^{\prime}
is found in polynomial time by the spectral sparsification routine
[
7
]
.

We first compare support functions on facet normals. Specifically, let
u\neq 0
be any facet normal vector of
Z(W)
.
By
Lemma

3.5
(i), the set
\{w_{i}:w_{i}^{\top}u=0\}
has rank
d-1
,
so we can choose linearly independent columns
w_{1},\ldots,w_{d-1}
of
W
with
w_{j}^{\top}u=0
.

We, thus, apply
Lemma

3.4
to obtain
\alpha,\beta>0
such that

|w_{i}^{\top}u|\in\{0\}\cup[\alpha,\beta]
for all columns
w_{i}
of
W
, and so
(w_{i}^{\top}u)^{2}\in[\alpha,\beta]\cdot|w_{i}^{\top}u|
.
Using
Theorem

3.3
, and observing that
\nicefrac{{\beta}}{{\alpha}}=\Delta
, gives

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\|W^{\top}u\|_{1}

\displaystyle=(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|w_{i}^{\top}u|\leq\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}{\alpha}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{n}(w_{i}^{\top}u)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}(w_{i}^{\top}u)^{2}\leq\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\cdot\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}|w_{i}^{\top}u|=\Delta\cdot\|(W^{\prime})^{\top}u\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Delta}{\alpha}\cdot(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(w_{i}^{\top}u)^{2}\leq\frac{\Delta\beta}{\alpha}\cdot(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|w_{i}^{\top}u|=\Delta^{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\|W^{\top}u\|_{1}.

To conclude, we recall that for a full-dimensional polytope
Q
containing the origin, a convex body
K
is contained in
Q
if and only if
h_{K}(a)\leq h_{Q}(a)
for all outward facet normals
a
of
Q
.
Since, we have just shown that for all facet-defining vectors
u
of
Z(W)
and thus, by
Lemma

3.5
(ii), also of
Z(W^{\prime})
it holds that
(1-\varepsilon)^{2}h_{Z(W)}(u)\leq h_{Z(W^{\prime})}(u)\leq\Delta^{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}h_{Z(W)}(u)
, this suffices to deduce

(1-\varepsilon)^{2}Z(W)\subseteq Z(W^{\prime})\subseteq\Delta^{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}Z(W),

as desired.
∎

4
Universal Lower Bounds for Zonotope Containment

In this section, we show that, in the oracle model,
for all
zonotopes, one cannot improve on the
\Omega(\sqrt{\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}}})
factor for containment. We emphasize that our result is a
universal
lower bound and, as such, it is not implied by the lower bound in
[
37
]
, where the authors show that there
exists
a zonotope—namely, the hypercube—for which this factor is tight. We do, however, apply a similar strategy to deduce our lower bound. In particular, this means relating the containment problem to approximating
Z
by a polytope
P
.

To this end, we first recall that the
(Z,Q)
-Opt-Containment
problem is equivalent to computing the
\|\cdot\|_{Z}
-inradius of a convex body
Q
. Then, using the same arguments as
[
16
, Section 3.E]
, we observe that any algorithm approximating the
\|\cdot\|_{Z}
-inradius of a convex body
Q
up to a factor of
s
implicitly constructs a polytope
P\subseteq Z\subseteq sP
. Moreover, given a polytope
P\subseteq Z\subseteq sP
, we could simply check whether or not its vertices are inside
Q
to compute its
\|\cdot\|_{Z}
-inradius. Hence, finding a polytope
P
such that
P\subseteq Z\subseteq sP
is equivalent to the
(Z,Q)
-Opt-Containment
problem.

To prove this universal lower bound for the polytope approximation of
Z
, we first perform a preprocessing step in which the zonotope becomes
normalized
as introduced in
[
14
]
. We say that a matrix
W=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
(and the generated zonotope
Z
) is
normalized
if (i) its rows form an orthonormal basis, i.e.,
WW^{\top}=I_{d}
, and (ii) its columns have length
\|w_{i}\|_{2}\leq 2\sqrt{\nicefrac{{d}}{{n}}}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. The following lemma states that any zonotope can be made normalized up to a constant factor:

Lemma 4.1

(
[
14
]
)
.

For any zonotope
\tilde{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exists an invertible linear transformation
T
and a normalized zonotope
Z
such that

\frac{4}{5}Z\subseteq T(\tilde{Z})\subseteq Z.

The next lemma states that every normalized zonotope enjoys the property of being in approximate John’s position and to have large volume. In fact, while
Z\subseteq\sqrt{n}\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}
,
Z
has the same volume as a Euclidean ball of radius
\Omega(\sqrt{n})
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
Z
be a normalized zonotope. Then, the following properties hold:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{n}{d}}\,\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}\subseteq Z\subseteq\sqrt{n}\,\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}

(i)

\displaystyle\textup{Vol}(Z)\geq\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)^{d/2}.

(ii)

Proof.

Let
W\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
be the generating matrix of
Z
. (i) We first argue about the inclusion
Z\subseteq\sqrt{n}\mathbb{B}^{d}_{2}
: after rescaling we may assume that
\|a\|_{2}=1
. Let
Wx
with
x\in[-1,1]^{n}
be the extreme point in
Z
that maximizes the inner product with
a
. Then,

h_{Z}(a)=\left<Wx,a\right>\leq\underbrace{\|W\|_{\textrm{op}}}_{=1}\cdot\|x\|_{2}\cdot\underbrace{\|a\|_{2}}_{=1}\leq\sqrt{n}\cdot\underbrace{\|x\|_{\infty}}_{\leq 1}\leq\sqrt{n}

Here we use that
\|W\|_{\textrm{op}}=\|WW^{\top}\|_{\textrm{op}}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}=1
. For the other inclusion, we need to show that for every
a\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, it holds that
\|W^{\top}a\|_{1}\geq\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}\sqrt{\nicefrac{{n}}{{d}}}
. We know that
\|W^{\top}a\|^{2}_{2}=a^{\top}WW^{\top}a=\|a\|^{2}_{2}=1
and that
\|W^{\top}a\|_{\infty}=\max_{i=1}^{n}|\left\langle a,w_{i}\right\rangle|\leq\|a\|_{2}\cdot\max_{i=1}^{n}\|w_{i}\|_{2}\leq 2\sqrt{\nicefrac{{d}}{{n}}}
.
Hence, by Hölder’s inequality, i.e.,
\|v\|^{2}_{2}\leq\|v\|_{\infty}\cdot\|v\|_{1}
,

\|W^{\top}a\|_{1}\geq\frac{\|W^{\top}a\|^{2}_{2}}{\|W^{\top}a\|_{\infty}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{n}{d}},

which concludes the proof of (i).

For the proof of (ii), we recall
[
5
, Lemma 4]
, which states that for any sequence of nonnegative scalars
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
, and unit vectors
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}u_{i}u_{i}^{\top}=I_{d}
, every zonotope that can be expressed as
Z=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}[-u_{i},u_{i}]
satisfies

\displaystyle\textup{Vol}(Z)\geq 2^{d}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{a_{i}}{c_{i}}\right)^{c_{i}}.

(5)

Suppose that
w_{i}
are the generators of
Z
, then let us choose
a_{i}=\sqrt{c_{i}}=\|w_{i}\|_{2}
, and
u_{i}=\nicefrac{{w_{i}}}{{\|w_{i}\|_{2}}}
so that

Z=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[-w_{i},w_{i}]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|w_{i}\|_{2}\,\left[-\frac{w_{i}}{\|w_{i}\|_{2}},\frac{w_{i}}{\|w_{i}\|_{2}}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}[-u_{i},u_{i}],

and also
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}u_{i}u_{i}^{\top}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}w_{i}^{\top}=WW^{\top}=I_{d}
, since
Z
is normalized. We can apply (
5
) and obtain

\displaystyle\textup{Vol}(Z)\geq 2^{d}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{i}}}\right)^{c_{i}}=2^{d}{\textup{exp}}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\ln{c_i}\right),

which we seek to minimize in terms of the
c_{i}
’s under the constraints
WW^{\top}=I_{d}
and
\|w_{i}\|_{2}\leq 2\sqrt{\nicefrac{{d}}{{n}}}
which are again given by normalization. The minimizer
c^{\star}=(c^{\star}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
of the above lower bound on volume subject to the mentioned constraints is the same as the maximizer
c^{\star}=(c^{\star}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
of the next expression:

\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\ln{c_i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}=d,0\leq c_{i}\leq\frac{4d}{n}\ \forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\right\}.

Since the objective function is convex and we need to maximize it over a convex polytope, then we know that the maximizer
c^{\star}
lies at one of the vertices of said polytope. This means that we need to set as many variables as possible to
\nicefrac{{4d}}{{n}}
. Without loss of generality, assume that
\nicefrac{{n}}{{4}}\in\mathbb{N}
, and the optimizer
c^{\star}
is such that
c^{\star}_{i}=\nicefrac{{4d}}{{n}}
for all
i\leq\nicefrac{{n}}{{4}}
, and
c^{\star}_{i}=0
otherwise. Then, this implies

\displaystyle\textup{Vol}(Z)\geq 2^{d}\left(\frac{n}{4d}\right)^{d/2}=\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)^{d/2},

which concludes the proof of (ii).
∎

In virtue of
Lemma

4.1
and
Lemma

4.2
, we have the following sharp estimate of the mean width of a zonotope:

Corollary 4.3
.

For any zonotope
\tilde{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exists an invertible linear transformation
T
such that
\nicefrac{{2}}{{5}}\sqrt{\nicefrac{{n}}{{d}}}\,\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}\subseteq T(\tilde{Z})\subseteq\sqrt{n}\,\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}
and

\frac{8}{5\sqrt{2\pi e}}\,\sqrt{n}\leq\mathbf{w}(T(\tilde{Z}))\leq\sqrt{n}.

Proof.

First, recall that the mean width a convex body
K
is
\mathbf{w}(K):=\underset{u\sim\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[h_{K}(u)+h_{K}(-u)\right]
. Now, by
Lemma

4.1
, we know that there exists an invertible linear transformation
T
and a normalized zonotope
Z
such that

\frac{4}{5}Z\subseteq T(\tilde{Z})\subseteq Z.

By
Lemma

4.2
(i), we also have that

\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{n}{d}}\,\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}\subseteq Z\subseteq\sqrt{n}\,\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}.

The upper bound on the mean-width follows directly from the containment relation
T(\tilde{Z})\subseteq Z\subseteq\sqrt{n}\,\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}
. For the lower bound, Urysohn’s Inequality
[
50
, Theorem 1.28]
says that

\mathbf{w}(T(\tilde{Z}))\geq 2\left(\frac{\textup{Vol}(T(\tilde{Z}))}{\textup{Vol}(\mathbb{B}^{d}_{2})}\right)^{1/d}\geq\frac{8}{5}\left(\frac{\textup{Vol}(Z)}{\textup{Vol}(\mathbb{B}^{d}_{2})}\right)^{1/d}\geq\frac{8}{5\sqrt{2\pi e}}\,\sqrt{n},

where the second inequality follows by
\nicefrac{{4}}{{5}}Z\subseteq T(\tilde{Z})
and the third by
\textup{Vol}(\mathbb{B}^{d}_{2})\leq(\nicefrac{{2\pi e}}{{d}})^{d/2}
and
Lemma

4.2
(ii).
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a zonotope and let
P=\textup{conv}\{x_{1},...,x_{N}\}\subseteq Z\subseteq sP
be a polytope on
N=\textup{poly}(d)
vertices approximating
Z
up to a factor
s=s(d)
. Then,

\displaystyle s\in\Omega\left(\sqrt{\frac{d}{\log d}}\right).

Proof.

The proof follows a similar line of thought as
[
37
]
. First, note that we can assume without loss of generality that
Z
is normalized, since approximating the normalized zonotope
Z^{\prime}
satisfying
\nicefrac{{4}}{{5}}Z^{\prime}\subseteq T(Z)\subseteq Z^{\prime}
by a polytope
P^{\prime}
and considering
P=T^{-1}(P^{\prime})
gives us the desired approximation of
Z
.

Let
P
be a polytope with
N=\textup{poly}(d)
vertices such that
P\subseteq Z\subseteq sP
. By
Lemma

4.2
we know that
(\sqrt{\nicefrac{{n}}{{4d}}})\,\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}\subseteq Z\subseteq\sqrt{n}\,\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}
and therefore,
(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{n}}})P\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}
. It is well-known (see e.g.
[
37
,
56
,
30
]
) that the volume ratio of a polytope
(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{n})}}P
that is fully contained in
\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}
is upper bounded by

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\textup{Vol}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}P)}{\textup{Vol}(\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d})}\right)^{1/d}\leq O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(\frac{N}{d})}{d}}\right)\leq O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\right),

where the second inequality follows since
N=\textup{poly}(d)
. With this at hand, we can apply
Lemma

4.2
(ii) together with the fact that
\textup{Vol}(\mathbb{B}^{d}_{2})\leq(\nicefrac{{2\pi e}}{{d}})^{d/2}
to find

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\textup{Vol}(P)}{\textup{Vol}(Z)}\right)^{1/d}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{n^{d/2}\textup{Vol}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}P)}{\textup{Vol}(Z)}\right)^{1/d}=\left(\frac{n^{d/2}\textup{Vol}(\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d})}{\textup{Vol}(Z)}\right)^{1/d}\left(\frac{\textup{Vol}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}P)}{\textup{Vol}(\mathbb{B}^{2}_{d})}\right)^{1/d}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{n^{d/2}\textup{Vol}(\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d})}{(n/d)^{d/2}}\right)^{1/d}\cdot O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\right)=\left({d^{d/2}\textup{Vol}(\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d})}\right)^{1/d}\cdot O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\right).

To finish up, note that since
P\subseteq Z\subseteq sP
we also have that
\textup{Vol}(P)\leq\textup{Vol}(Z)\leq s^{d}\textup{Vol}(P)
and thus with the above we get

s\geq\left(\frac{\textup{Vol}(Z)}{\textup{Vol}(P)}\right)^{1/d}\geq\Omega\left(\sqrt{\frac{\ d}{\log d}}\right).\qed

Remark
.

Note that under
2.3
, the above lower bound is tight for all zonotopes since then, all zonotopes can be approximated up to a factor of
O(\sqrt{\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}}})
by polynomially many vertices. In turn, this would imply that approximating the volume of
Z
by a polytope
P\subseteq Z
is exactly as difficult as approximating
Z
by a polytope
P\subseteq Z\subseteq sP
.
On the contrapositive, this would imply that if there exists a zonotope
Z
which is more difficult to approximate in a containment sense than in a volume sense,
2.3
cannot be true.

Remark
.

We remark that, for every isotropic convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and every
\Omega(d)\leq N\leq O({\textup{exp}}\left(d\right))
, the polytope
P=\textup{conv}(\pm x_{1},\ldots,\pm x_{N})
, obtained by sampling
x_{i}\sim K
uniformly and independently has the following volumetric approximation guarantee in expectation:

\left(\frac{\textup{Vol}(P)}{\textup{Vol}(K)}\right)^{1/d}\in\Theta\left(L_{K}\sqrt{\frac{\log\left(\frac{N}{d}\right)}{d}}\right)=\Theta\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log\left(\frac{N}{d}\right)}{d}}\right),

where
L_{K}:=\frac{\det(\mathrm{Cov}(K))^{1/2d}}{\textup{Vol}(K)^{1/d}}
is the isotropic constant of
K
. The inclusion follows from the work of
[
22
]
[Equations (1.7)-(1.8)]. The equality follows from the recent breakthrough affirmative resolution of Bourgain’s Slicing Conjecture
[
11
,
12
]
by
[
33
]
and
[
38
]
, which asserts that
L_{K}\in\Theta(1)
for all isotropic convex bodies
K
.

If we plug in
N\in\textup{poly}(d)
and recall
Theorem

4.4
, we observe that, with a polynomial number of vertices, this approximation error is best possible, and thus tight, for zonotopes. This means that an optimal polytope that approximates a zonotope in a volumetric sense can be found by sampling points from it uniformly at random. Note that this is false for general convex bodies, for example the cross-polytope
\mathbb{B}_{1}^{d}
, where uniform sampling achieves the same
\Theta(\sqrt{\nicefrac{{\log d}}{{d}}})
guarantee which is clearly not optimal since
\mathbb{B}_{1}^{d}
is itself a polytope on
2d
vertices.

5
Containment of General Convex Bodies

We now consider
(K,Q)\textsc{-Gap-Containment}
for general convex bodies
K,Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For the general convex body containment problem in the oracle model, we establish in this section that the optimal approximation guarantee is tightly bounded by
s\in\Theta(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}})
. Specifically, a result by
[
48
]
shows that, given a polynomial number of samples, one can approximate the containment problem within a factor
s\in O(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}})
with high probability. Our main contribution in this section is to prove a matching lower bound: any algorithm that accesses the oracle through a polynomial number of queries must incur an approximation error of at least
s\in\Omega(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}})
with at least constant probability. This establishes the tightness of the approximation in this oracle setting and further indicates that certain existential results on polytopal approximations of convex bodies cannot be made algorithmically feasible.

To achieve the desired approximation guarantee for containment in general convex bodies, we first recall a result of
[
48
]
regarding polytopal approximation of convex bodies.

Theorem 5.1

(Theorem 1.2 in
[
48
]
)
.

Given a centered convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, sampling points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{T}
independently and uniformly from
K
, where
T\in\Theta\left(d\left(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{s}}\right)^{-d}\log\left(\left(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{s}}\right)^{-1}\right)\right)
, yields that the convex hull
P=\mathrm{conv}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{T})
satisfies
P\subseteq K\subseteq sP
with high probability.

The containment guarantee now directly follows by approximating
K
by
P
in the sense of
Theorem

5.1
with
s(d)\in\Theta(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}})
. The random samples can be found in polynomial time
[
23
]
and the number of samples are polynomial in
d
. Then one checks whether at least one of the vertices of
s(d)\cdot P
lies outside
Q
.
If this is the case, then certainly
sK\nsubseteq Q
. Otherwise, one has
K\subseteq Q
.

Corollary 5.2
.

For convex bodies
K,Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s\in\Theta(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}})
,
(K,Q)
-Gap-Containment
can be solved by a randomized polynomial time algorithm with high probability.

5.1
A Matching Lower Bound

We now prove that the
\Omega(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}})
scaling for the containment problem of general oracle-access convex bodies is tight.

Theorem 5.3
.

For
s\in o(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}})
, there does not exist a randomized polynomial time algorithm that decides
(K,Q)
-Gap-Containment
for each
K,Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with high probability.

To prove the above theorem, we first perform a reduction from hardness of computing radius of a general convex body represented by an oracle. Below, we denote by
\textsc{OutRad}_{2}(K)
the
\ell_{2}
-circumradius of convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Fix
r>1
and suppose that, for a symmetric convex body
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one cannot distinguish

\textup{{OutRad}}_{2}(P)\leq 1\qquad\text{from}\qquad\textup{{OutRad}}_{2}(P)>r,

in a polynomial number of oracle calls to the oracle. Then, there are symmetric convex bodies
K,Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
so that one cannot distinguish
K\subseteq Q
from
K\not\subseteq r^{2}Q
in a polynomial number of oracle calls to the oracle.

Proof.

Given a symmetric convex body
P
, set
K=P
and
Q=P^{\circ}
be its polar body.
We want to prove that

\textsc{OutRad}_{2}(P)\leq r\Longleftrightarrow K\subseteq r^{2}Q.

We split this proof into two directions: first we argue that if
\textsc{OutRad}_{2}(P)\leq r
, then
K\subseteq r^{2}Q
. Indeed, we simply have that
\textsc{OutRad}_{2}(P)\leq r
is equivalent to saying that
P\subseteq r\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}
, which in turn implies that

P^{\circ}\supseteq(r\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d})^{\circ}=\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}.

Therefore,

K=P\subseteq r\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}\subseteq r^{2}P^{\circ}=r^{2}Q

which gives the first direction.

For the other direction, we would like to show that if
K\subseteq r^{2}Q
, then
\textsc{OutRad}_{2}(P)\leq r
. To that end, consider a vector
a
such that
a\in P=K\subseteq r^{2}Q=r^{2}P^{\circ}
. Then, it holds that
\nicefrac{{a}}{{r^{2}}}\in P^{\circ}
. By definition of the polar, this means that
\left\langle\nicefrac{{a}}{{r^{2}}},x\right\rangle\leq 1
is a feasible inequality for the primal body
P
. In particular,

\frac{\|a\|_{2}^{2}}{r^{2}}=\left\langle\frac{a}{r^{2}},a\right\rangle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a\in P}}{{\leq}}1,

which can be rearranged to
\|a\|_{2}\leq r
.
Hence if one could distinguish
K\subseteq Q
from
K\not\subseteq r^{2}Q
, then one could also
distinguish
P\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}
from
P\not\subseteq r\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d}
.
∎

We now show
Theorem

5.3
:

Proof of
Theorem

5.3
.

In Section 3 of
[
15
]
, the authors show that there exists a distribution over centrally symmetric convex bodies
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that one cannot distinguish,

\textup{{OutRad}}_{2}(K)\leq 1\qquad\text{from}\qquad\textup{{OutRad}}_{2}(K)\in\Omega\left(\sqrt{\frac{d}{\log d}}\right),

in a polynomial number of oracle calls to the oracle, with success probability at least constant. Hence, the theorem statement follows directly from
Lemma

5.4
.
∎

5.2
Implications for Approximation by Polytopes

We turn to the negative implications of
Theorem

5.3
for algorithmically approximating convex bodies with polytopes. Before discussing them, we first review key results on polytopal approximations of convex bodies. Beyond the algorithmic
O(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}})
approximation for centered convex bodies
[
48
]
(
Theorem

5.1
), a recent information-theoretic lower bound establishes this factor as optimal
[
35
]
. If we restrict our attention to centrally symmetric convex bodies, a renowned result of
[
6
]
shows that the convex hull of
\textup{poly}(d)
points from
K
approximates
K
up to an
O(\sqrt{d})
factor.

Theorem 5.5

(
[
6
]
)
.

For any centrally symmetric convex body
K
, there exists a polytope
P
with at most
\textup{poly}(d)
many vertices such that
\nicefrac{{1}}{{s}}P\subseteq K\subseteq P
, for
s\in O(\sqrt{d})
.

We now highlight the key negative implication of
Theorem

5.3
. Upon examining its proof, we observe that the tight
\Theta(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}})
containment result arises from two symmetric convex bodies—implying that symmetry alone does not mitigate hardness for containment of centered bodies in the oracle model. Crucially, this construction shows that Barvinok’s existential result (
Theorem

5.5
) cannot be made efficiently algorithmic:
Corollary

5.6
proves that any such algorithm would contradict the
\Omega(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}})
hardness for containment.

Corollary 5.6
.

For
s\in o(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}})
, there does not exist a randomized polynomial time algorithm that computes a polytope
P
such that
\nicefrac{{1}}{{s}}P\subseteq K\subseteq P
for each
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with higher than constant probability. In particular, this rules out an efficient algorithm for finding the polytope in
Theorem

5.5
.

Proof.

Assume for the sake of contradiction that we could find, with at least constant probability, a polytope
P
with
\textup{poly}(d)
many vertices that approximates the symmetric convex body
K
in the proof of
Theorem

5.3
as

\frac{1}{s}P\subseteq K\subseteq P,

for
s\in o(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}})
. We could then check whether or not
P\subseteq Q
exactly by exhaustive search on all of its polynomially many vertices. If
P\subseteq Q
, it follows that
K\subseteq Q
, and otherwise it follows that
sK\not\subseteq Q
. Then, this implies that we would be able to distinguish
K\subseteq Q
from
sK\not\subseteq Q
, for
s\in o(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}})
, contradicting
Theorem

5.3
.
Therefore the existence result in
Theorem

5.5
cannot be made efficiently constructive unless
s\in\Omega(\nicefrac{{d}}{{\log d}})
.
∎

We note that the result from
Corollary

5.6
is related to the fact that on one hand for a symmetric convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there is an ellipsoid
E
— called the
Löwner-John ellipsoid
— so that
E\subseteq K\subseteq\sqrt{d}E
. However, if
K
is only given by an oracle, then a factor
O(d)
is best possible in polynomial time, for example by computing an approximate inertia ellipsoid or by a modified ellipsoid method
[
31
, Theorem 4.6.3]
. In contrast, if
K
is given in inequality description, then for any
\varepsilon>0
, an ellipsoid with a
(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{d}
factor can be computed in polynomial time
[
18
]
.
